A study on antibody persistence following severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus infection in the Dabie Mountain Region, China
Authors: Jie Sun, Jianguo Rao, Pengpeng Xu, Wei Yang, Mingyang Yang, Weigang Zhang, Zhuo Wang, Xianjun Lin, Limin Wang, Yong Lyu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo characterize the duration and determinants of SFTSV-specific antibody persistence following natural infection, informing vaccine development for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS).MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study among 463 convalescent SFTS patients in the Dabie Mountains, a hyperendemic region in China. Using illness onset dates and a single post-infection serum sample per participant, we reconstructed individual follow-up durations and applied Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and Cox proportional hazards models to assess time to seroreversion and its determinants.ResultsSeropositivity declined with longer time since illness onset. Age was significantly associated with antibody persistence: individuals aged ≥ 70 years showed the most rapid antibody waning, whereas those aged < 50 years showed longer detectable IgG persistence. Introduction

Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is an acute zoonotic disease caused by Bandavirus dabieense (formerly known as SFTS virus, SFTSV), with a reported case fatality rate of 10%–16% [1–3]. To date, no targeted antiviral therapy or licensed vaccine against SFTSV is available; clinical management relies primarily on supportive care and early symptomatic intervention [4]. Although favipiravir has been approved for the treatment of SFTS in Japan, its efficacy remains inconclusive and it is not widely available globally [5]. Consequently, effective prevention and control strategies depend critically on a deeper understanding of host immune responses following infection—particularly the kinetics and durability of pathogen-specific humoral immunity.

Globally, SFTSV vaccine development has made preliminary progress, with multiple candidates—including inactivated, live-attenuated, viral vector–based, subunit, DNA, and mRNA platforms—currently in preclinical development [6–8]. However, the absence of well-defined correlates of protection (CoP) remains a major barrier to evaluating vaccine efficacy. In this context, characterizing the dynamics and determinants of antibody persistence after natural infection is essential not only for establishing serosurveillance frameworks but also for guiding vaccine design, including optimal booster timing and immunobridging-based efficacy assessment [9].

The Dabie Mountains—at the junction of Anhui, Hubei, and Henan provinces in China—represent one of the world’s most hyperendemic regions for SFTS. Its hilly terrain, humid climate, and intensive agricultural activity create an ideal ecological niche for tick proliferation and human–animal–environmental transmission cycles [10]. This area consistently reports the highest national incidence of SFTS, resulting in frequent population exposure and forming a unique “natural focus–population–immune response” research model [3]. Importantly, a robust SFTS surveillance network has been established in the Dabie Mountains, providing valuable biological specimens and longitudinal clinical data for post-infection immunity studies [11].

Existing studies on SFTSV-specific antibody persistence are often limited by small sample sizes and short follow-up durations [12, 13], with insufficient systematic evaluation of how factors such as age, gender, and geographic location influence antibody longevity. Notably, it remains unclear whether repeated subclinical exposures in high-transmission settings may lead to immune boosting. Therefore, a comprehensive investigation among convalescent patients in this representative endemic area—to delineate the decay trajectory of SFTSV-specific IgG antibodies and identify key determinants—holds significant scientific and public health value.

Methods

Data source

In August 2024, we conducted a retrospective cohort study among individuals previously diagnosed with SFTS in Lu’an City. Eligible participants were identified from the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention, which includes all reported SFTS cases between 1 January 2011 and 31 December 2023. Case definitions followed standardized national diagnostic criteria issued by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. Laboratory confirmation required at least one of the following: positive SFTSV RNA by RT-PCR, positive SFTSV-specific IgM antibodies, or seroconversion of SFTSV-specific IgG in paired sera. Clinical-diagnosis cases without laboratory confirmation were excluded. Only individuals residing in their original location at the time of the survey were included.

Survey protocol

We selected six counties/districts within Lu’an City, Anhui Province—Jin’an, Yu’an, Jinzhai, Huoshan, Shucheng, and Huoqiu—as survey sites. These locations represent a spectrum of SFTS transmission intensities and encompass diverse topographical settings: Jinzhai and Huoshan are predominantly mountainous (> 500 m); Yu’an and Shucheng are lowland/hilly (< 500 m); Jin’an features transitional topography (southern mountains > 500 m to northern plains < 100 m); while Huoqiu are lowland plains (< 100 m). Supplementary Fig.S1 provides a map of the study site. These geographical differences also entail variations in population density and land use patterns, enabling robust assessment of how geographic and ecological factors influence post-infection antibody persistence.

Prior to field investigation, demographic information—including gender, age, residential address, and contact details—was extracted from the national surveillance system. Local township health centers and village clinics assisted in verifying residency status and assessing willingness to participate. Eligible individuals provided written informed consent before enrollment. A peripheral blood sample (5–10 mL) was collected from each consenting participant for SFTSV-specific antibody testing.

Quality control

Rigorous quality control measures were implemented throughout the study. Data collection used standardized questionnaires administered by uniformly trained interviewers. Blood samples were collected, labeled, and stored according to standard operating procedures to prevent hemolysis and cross-contamination. All laboratory assays employed commercially available SFTSV antibody detection kits within their expiration dates, with inclusion of positive and negative controls in every run. Data were double-entered by independent personnel, followed by logical consistency checks. Key procedural steps underwent periodic random audits by the principal investigator to ensure data integrity.

Serological testing

Serum samples were tested for SFTSV-specific IgG antibodies using a commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (Wuxi Xinlianxin Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd.). The assay utilizes microtiter plates pre-coated with recombinant SFTSV nucleocapsid protein (NP) as the capture antigens. SFTSV-specific antibodies bind to immobilized NP antigen, and immune complexes are detected via enzyme-conjugated secondary antibodies. Absorbance (optical density, OD) was measured at 450 nm. The cutoff was defined as 2.1 times the mean OD of negative controls. Samples with OD ≥ cutoff were classified as seropositive; those below were seronegative. All procedures followed the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistical analysis

The primary outcome was seroreversion (loss of detectable SFTSV-specific IgG antibodies). Exposures included age group (< 50, 50–59, 60–69, ≥ 70 years), sex, and residential area (highland vs. lowland). Subgroup analyses were performed stratified by age group and residential area. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using R version 4.4.3 (https://www.r-project.org). Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s chi-squared test or Monte Carlo simulation–based chi-squared tests when expected cell counts were low. Associations were assessed using logistic regression, reporting odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and P values.The Kruskal–Wallis test was used to compare the time from diagnosis to sample collection across multiple age groups. No missing data were present for the key variables (age, gender, district, time since diagnosis, and antibody status).

To evaluate the duration of antibody persistence, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed to estimate the probability of maintaining SFTSV-specific IgG seropositivity over time. Time since diagnosis (or symptom onset) was used as the time variable, and seroreversion (loss of detectable IgG antibodies) was defined as the event of interest. Each participant contributed one observation at their respective time point. Participants who remained seropositive at the time of sample collection were treated as censored observations, as the exact time of seroreversion had not yet occurred. Differences in survival curves across subgroups were evaluated using the log-rank test.

Multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were used to examine associations between demographic/geographic factors and time to seroreversion, with results expressed as hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs.

Factors associated with seropositivity in convalescent patients

To identify factors associated with serostatus, we performed multivariable logistic regression. Several variables showed significant associations with antibody positivity. First, years since diagnosis was negatively associated with seropositivity, indicating that the likelihood of seroreversion increased with longer post-infection intervals. Second, older age was positively associated with seropositivity, suggesting that seropositivity rates tend to rise with age. Although gender showed only a modest effect, a statistically significant difference was observed (P = 0.042), with females slightly more likely to be seropositive.

Association between seropositivity and years since diagnosis

A logistic regression model confirmed that years since diagnosis was independently associated with reduced odds of antibody positivity (β = − 0.108, P = 0.004). As shown in Fig. 1, the seropositivity rate declined systematically with increasing years since diagnosis. A slight increase was observed at 6 years post-infection; however, the sample size in years 6–10 (n = 230) was comparable to that in years 1–5 (n = 223), indicating that this fluctuation is not attributable to statistical instability.

Association between seropositivity and age

A multivariable logistic regression model was used to assess the association between age group and SFTSV-specific IgG seropositivity. Individuals aged ≥ 50 years had higher odds of antibody positivity compared with those < 50 years. The strongest association was observed in participants aged ≥ 70 years (OR 3.57 [95% CI 1.80–7.09]), followed by those aged 60–69 years (OR 2.20 [95% CI 1.20–4.03]) and 50–59 years (OR 1.99 [95% CI 1.03–3.84]).

The time from diagnosis to sample collection was compared across four age groups (< 50, 50–59, 60–69, and ≥ 70 years) using the Kruskal–Wallis test. The results showed no statistically significant difference among the groups (χ² = 3, df = 3, P = 0.392). The median time was 6.4 years (IQR: 4.9–8.2) for participants aged < 50 years, 6.3 years(IQR: 4.0- 8.3) for those aged 50–59 years, 6.0 years (IQR: 3.4–7.9) for those aged 60–69 years, and 4.3 years (IQR: 2.2–7.3) for those aged ≥ 70 years.

The absence of a significant difference in sampling time across age groups (P = 0.392) indicates that higher seropositivity in older participants cannot be explained by earlier sampling after infection.

However, these study findings may appear paradoxical when considering antibody persistence duration. To explore this further, we conducted time-to-event analysis using Kaplan–Meier survival curves to compare the duration of seropositivity across age groups (Fig. 2). Antibody positivity gradually decreased over time in all groups, with relatively stable levels during the first 4 years post-onset followed by accelerated decline thereafter. The log-rank test indicated significant differences in seroreversion time by age group (χ² = 22.8, P < 0.001). Notably, individuals aged ≥ 70 years experienced faster antibody decay, whereas those aged < 50 years retained detectable IgG for longer durations.

Association between seropositivity and geographic region

To formally assess the impact of demographic and geographic factors on antibody persistence, we fitted a multivariate Cox proportional hazards model with time to seroreversion as the outcome. After adjusting for gender and geographic region, older age (≥ 70 years) was associated with a higher hazard of seroreversion (HR 1.715 [95% CI 0.945–3.112]; P = 0.076)—consistent with the Kaplan–Meier findings—though this did not reach statistical significance. Similarly, males showed a non-significantly higher risk of antibody loss compared with females (HR 1.395 [95% CI 0.972–2.001]; P = 0.070).

Geographic region emerged as a strong independent predictor of antibody durability. Using Huoqiu County—a predominantly plain area—as the reference, patients from mountainous or hilly regions exhibited markedly lower hazards of seroreversion: Yu’an District (HR 0.060 [95% CI 0.014–0.262]), Jinzhai County (HR 0.358 [95% CI 0.202–0.635]), and Huoshan County (HR 0.390 [95% CI 0.195–0.780]) (all P < 0.01). These results suggest that environmental or exposure-related factors linked to topography significantly influence long-term humoral immunity.

The final Cox model demonstrated good overall fit (likelihood ratio χ² = 50.85, P < 0.001) and moderate discriminative ability (concordance index [C-index] = 0.684).

Discussion

This study provides several important and scientifically valuable insights into post-infection immunity against severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV). Through systematic serological testing and epidemiological analysis of 463 convalescent patients in the Dabie Mountains—a hyperendemic region in China—we demonstrate that the majority of survivors maintain detectable SFTSV-specific IgG antibodies for several years following natural infection. These findings suggest that SFTSV infection can induce durable humoral immunity, offering a critical benchmark to inform the development and evaluation of SFTS vaccines.

A key strength of our work is the identification of marked geographic heterogeneity in antibody persistence. After adjusting for age and other potential confounders, residents of Yu’an District and Jinzhai County exhibited substantially longer antibody durability compared with those from Huoqiu County—the reference area characterized by predominantly plain terrain. The observed differences in antibody persistence between highland and lowland areas raise intriguing questions about potential underlying mechanisms, including differences in transmission intensity, frequency of repeated subclinical tick exposures, regional viral genetic diversity, circulating virus strains, vector density, host genetic factors, or environmental influences (e.g., nutritional status, co-infections). While our study cannot directly address these mechanisms, our findings highlight the need for geographically stratified surveillance and further virological and immunological studies in this region, underscore the importance of tailored surveillance and vaccination strategies in heterogeneous endemic settings.

We also observed a pronounced age-related effect on seropositivity: older individuals were significantly more likely to test IgG-positive after infection, with the highest odds observed among those aged ≥ 70 years. The absence of a significant difference in sampling time across age groups (P = 0.392) indicates that higher seropositivity in older patients cannot be explained by earlier sampling after infection.

To understand this apparent paradox—higher seropositivity but faster antibody decline in older patients—we propose two complementary hypotheses: higher initial antibody peaks or survivorship bias. However, as these parameters were not directly measured in our study, both hypotheses require validation in future investigations. First, different initial antibody peaks: older patients typically experience more severe SFTSV infection with higher viral loads [14], which could induce stronger initial antigenic stimulation and higher peak antibody responses. Even with a faster subsequent decline rate (as shown in our Kaplan–Meier analysis), a higher starting point could result in older patients remaining above the seropositivity threshold for a longer absolute time, contributing to the higher cross-sectional seropositivity observed at a given time point. Second, survivorship bias: given the higher case fatality rate of SFTS in older populations, the older survivors included in our long-term follow-up cohort may represent a subgroup with intrinsically stronger immune responses, potentially biasing the observed seropositivity rates upward. In addition, this phenomenon may be linked to immunosenescence [15]. With advancing age, the immune system undergoes remodeling—marked by declining T-cell function but potentially enhanced or dysregulated humoral responses [16].

However, despite this heightened early antibody response, long-term humoral immunity appears compromised in the elderly. Aging is associated with impaired generation of long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells, resulting in faster antibody wanin [17, 18]—a pattern consistently reported in studies of SARS-CoV-2 vaccination, where older adults show accelerated decline in neutralizing antibodies and shorter duration of protection [19]. Consistent with this paradigm, our Kaplan–Meier survival analysis revealed significantly shorter antibody persistence in older patients compared with younger individuals. This challenges the assumption that a strong early serological response equates to durable protection. Consequently, reliance on seropositivity rates measured during acute or early convalescent phases alone is insufficient for defining CoP against SFTS; longitudinal assessment of antibody decay dynamics is essential.

The slight increase in seropositivity observed at 6 years post-infection warrants discussion. Given that the sample size in 6–10 years was comparable to 1–5 years, this fluctuation is unlikely to be a statistical artifact. Potential explanations include re-exposure to SFTSV or cross-reactive antigens in this highly endemic area, recall responses in long-term survivors, or cohort effects related to differences in initial disease severity or circulating viral strains. However, the exact mechanism remains unclear, and future prospective studies with serial sampling are needed to confirm this observation.

Importantly, our findings establish a real-world reference for the durability of naturally acquired immunity—an essential foundation for CoP definition in future SFTS vaccine trials. Such benchmarks are critical for immunobridging strategies, dose-scheduling decisions, and regulatory evaluation of candidate vaccines.

Our investigation has several limitations. First, antibody testing was based on a single serum sample per participant, precluding precise characterization of individual antibody decay kinetics. Detailed clinical severity indicators and treatment records were not available. Consequently, we could not directly verify whether older patients had higher initial antibody peaks or experienced re-exposure; these proposed mechanisms therefore remain speculative and require future validation. Second, the cohort was drawn exclusively from the Dabie Mountains region; findings may not be generalizable to other geographic, ecological, or ethnic populations. Third, we assessed only SFTSV-specific IgG using an NP-based ELISA, which detects exposure history but does not directly correlate with neutralizing activity. We did not measure neutralizing antibody titers or T-cell–mediated immunity, both of which may contribute to overall protection. Fourth, our cohort was restricted to laboratory-confirmed symptomatic cases, excluding asymptomatic or mild infections; thus, the full spectrum of post-infection immune responses remains incompletely characterized. Fifth, viral sequencing was not performed, and the sample size for participants with follow-up beyond 10 years was limited (n = 10), which may affect the stability of seropositivity estimates in this period. Prospective longitudinal cohort studies—including virological, serological, and cellular immune profiling—are needed to validate these findings and further elucidate the determinants of durable immunity against SFTS.

Conclusions

This study systematically characterized SFTSV-specific IgG persistence in a large cohort of convalescent patients and identified younger age and residence in high-transmission mountainous areas as key determinants of prolonged antibody maintenance—suggesting more robust and sustained immune responses in these groups. These findings provide a natural immunological benchmark for understanding antibody persistence following natural infection. The observation that most individuals retain detectable antibodies for multiple years may inform development of long-term vaccine-induced protection. Future SFTS vaccine trials could leverage the immune profiles described here as a reference to evaluate immunogenicity and accelerate development through immunobridging.

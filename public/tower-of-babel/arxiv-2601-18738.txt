Abstract
. We show that for all integers 2 ≤ s ≤ t 2\leq s\leq t , any K s , t K_{s,t} -free subset of [ N ] [N] with size Ω ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / s ) \Omega(n^{1-1/s}) must contain a nontrivial solution to every fixed translation-invariant linear equation in at least five variables. This extends earlier results for Sidon sets due to Conlon–Fox–Sudakov–Zhao and Prendiville to the full family of K s , t K_{s,t} -free sets. We also study the corresponding problem in vector spaces over finite fields. In 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} we obtain stronger quantitative bounds, including polylogarithmic savings, by combining Fourier-analytic transference with polynomial-method input from the arithmetic cycle-removal lemma of Fox–Lovász–Sauermann.



1.
Introduction

Fix integers
2\leq s\leq t
and write
K_{s,t}
for the complete bipartite graph with parts of sizes

s
and
t
. Let
\operatorname{ex}(n,K_{s,t})
denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph containing no copy of
K_{s,t}
. A well-known theorem of Kővári–Sós–Turán
[
14
]
gives

(1)

\operatorname{ex}(n,K_{s,t})\ll_{s,t}n^{2-1/s}.

There are several remarkable algebraic constructions showing that (
1
) is optimal (up to constant factors) for various values of
s
and
t
, in particular when
t
is sufficiently large compared to
s
. See for example the norm graph constructions from
[
12
,
1
]
, and the more recent
random-algebraic construction from
[
3
]
. There are also many other values of
s
and
t
for which the asymptotic value of
\operatorname{ex}(n,K_{s,t})
is poorly understood. For example, even for
s=t=4
, the problem is wide open.

In this paper, we consider the following arithmetic analogue, first introduced by Erdős and Harzheim in
[
8
]
(see also
[
4
]
). For integers
2\leq s\leq t
, we say that a set
A\subseteq[N]
is
K_{s,t}
-free
if it contains no configuration of the form

\{x_{i}+y_{j}:1\leq i\leq s,\ 1\leq j\leq t\}\subseteq A

with integers
x_{1},\dots,x_{s}
distinct and integers
y_{1},\dots,y_{t}
distinct integers. Equivalently,
A
contains no subset of
the form
B+C
with
|B|=s
and
|C|=t
. A standard sum-graph reduction shows that the Kővári–Sós–Turán theorem immediately implies
the trivial density bound

|A|\ll_{s,t}N^{1-1/s}.

When
s=t=2
, such sets are precisely
Sidon sets
(also called
B_{2}
-sets), and the above
correspondence recovers the classical link between Sidon sets and
C_{4}
-free graphs. See
[
4
]
for more details and further generalizations.

Our results

Building upon recent work of Conlon–Fox–Sudakov–Zhao
[
5
]
and Prendiville
[
17
]
, we show that all dense
K_{s,t}
-free subsets of
\{1,\dots,N\}
must possess arithmetic structure, in the sense that they must always contain a solution to any translation-invariant linear equation
in at least five variables, with all variables distinct.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 5
, and
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}=0
.
Let
A\subseteq[N]
be
K_{s,t}
-free and assume that
A
lacks nontrivial solutions of
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0
. Then

|A|\ll N^{1-1/s}\exp(-O_{a_{i},s,t}((\log\log N)^{\frac{1}{7}})).

The qualitative version of this result in the case of Sidon sets (when
s=t=2
, and with
|A|=o(N^{1/2})
) was first established by Conlon, Fox, Sudakov, and Zhao
[
5
]
, who proved it using a regularity lemma for graphs with few cycles of length four. Not too long after, Prendiville developed a Fourier-analytic transference principle to show the improved quantitative bound
|A|=O\!\left(N^{1/2}(\log\log N)^{-1}\right)
. This argument used an
L^{2}
-dense model theorem in the style of Helfgott and de Roton
[
11
]
for Roth’s theorem in the primes (originally established by Green
[
10
]
). To prove Theorem
1.1
, we take a similar Fourier-analytic transference path, relying instead on an
L^{s}
-dense model theorem, in the spirit of Naslund’s work
[
15
]
.

Finite-field analogue.

In Section
6
we study the same question in the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}

(
q
a fixed odd prime power), where we establish the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q
be a fixed odd prime power. Let
k\geq 5
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}=0
.
Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be
K_{s,t}
-free and assume that
A
contains no nontrivial solution to

a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0
. Then, by writing
N=q^{n}
, there exists
c=c(q,k,s,t)>0
such that

|A|\ll N^{1-1/s}(\log N)^{-c}.

To obtain the polylogarithmic from Theorem
1.2
, we take advantage of the polynomial method developments around the cap set problem
[
6
,
7
]
, in particular the improved bounds in the arithmetic
k
-cycle removal lemma in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
due to Fox, Lovász, and Sauermann
[
9
]
.

We remark that
k\geq 5
is optimal in the sense that if we want to avoid nontrivial solutions in any given
k
translation invariant linear equation. For example, in the
K_{2,2}
Sidon case, both
x_{1}+x_{2}=x_{3}+x_{4}
(
k=4
) and
x+y=2z
(
k=3
) are excluded by the Sidon condition.

Proof ideas

Our arguments follow the same overarching Fourier analytic transference principle from
[
17
]
: we build a
dense model
for a sparse set (or weight) and then transfer a
dense counting statement
back to the original object. It is conceptually helpful to split the proof into two independent components.

(1) Dense model step (Fourier approximation).

Given a sparse set
A
in the natural extremal scaling (for instance
|A|\asymp N^{1-1/s}
for

K_{s,t}
-free sets), we introduce a renormalized
nonnegative weight
\nu
(e.g.
\nu=N^{1/s}\mathbf{1}_{A}
) so that

\sum\nu
is of order
N
. The goal is to construct a nonnegative function
f
with

\sum f=\sum\nu\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\widehat{f}-\widehat{\nu}\|_{\infty}\ \ \text{small},

while keeping some
L^{s}
norm of
f
bounded (
\sum f^{s}\ll N
in the
K_{s,t}
setting). Over the integers, the standard way to produce
f
is to smooth by a Bohr set built from the large
spectrum; controlling the rank of this Bohr set is a key difficulty, and this step uses the
K_{s,t}
-freeness of
A
. Over
G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
some of these steps will be simplified substantially, because the analogue of a Bohr set is an actual subspace.

(2) Dense counting step (supersaturation).

Fix a translation-invariant equation in
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]
,

a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0,\qquad a_{i}\neq 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}=0,

and write the associated counting functional

T(h_{1},\dots,h_{k}):=\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}(x_{i}),\qquad T(h):=T(h,\dots,h).

The dense model
f
is built so that it behaves “like a dense set” from the point of view of
Fourier analysis; one then applies a dense-set input to obtain a nontrivial lower bound for

T(f)
. In the integer case this dense input comes from quantitative results on solutions to
translation-invariant equations in dense subsets of
[N]
(Bloom-type bounds
[
2
]
and their refinements).
In the finite-field case we can instead use the arithmetic
k
-cycle removal lemma of
Fox–Lovász–Sauermann
[
9
]
(which in turn builds upon the works of Croot–Lev–Pach
[
6
]
and Ellenberg–Gijswijt
[
7
]
) and it yields polynomial supersaturation bounds.

(3) Transference (telescoping + moment control).

The final step is to compare
T(f)
with
T(\nu_{A})
. A standard telescoping identity expands

\prod_{i=1}^{k}f(x_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{k}\nu(x_{i})

as a sum of
k
terms, each containing one copy of
f-\nu
and
k-1
copies of either
f
or
\nu
.
To bound each resulting multilinear form we use a Fourier/Hölder estimate whose strength depends
on an available moment bound for a suitable
majorant

\omega\geq|f|+|\nu|
.
In the
K_{s,t}
-free setting, the underlying graph-freeness yields strong control on the second
moment energy
E_{2}
, which provides the
L^{4}
Fourier bound needed for the counting lemma.
Combining the dense lower bound for
T(f)
with the transference error bound

|T(f)-T(\nu)|\ \ll\ N^{k-2}\,\|\widehat{f-\nu}\|_{\infty},

one concludes that
T(\nu)
is also large. Finally, if
A
is assumed to have only “trivial”
solutions, then
T(\nu)
can be computed explicitly, which leads to an upper bound that contradicts the transferred lower bound for
N
sufficiently large.

Notation

For a function
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
with finite support, we define its Fourier transform on
\mathbb{T}
by

\widehat{f}(\alpha)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)e(-n\alpha),\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{T}.

The convolution of two functions on
\mathbb{Z}
is denoted as

f*g(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}f(y)g(x-y).

With respect to Haar probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
, define the
L^{p}
norm of
F:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\|F\|_{p}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|F(\alpha)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad\|F\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}}|F(\alpha)|.

Throughout the paper, we write
f=O(g)
or
f\ll g
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that
|f(x)|\leq Cg(x)
for all
x
, and
f\asymp g
if
f=O(g)
and
g=O(f)
. We say
f=o(g)
if for any
\varepsilon>0
,
|f(x)|\leq\varepsilon g(x)
for all sufficiently large
x
.

2.
K_{s,t}
-free sets and the
s
-th moment energy

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{h}
be real-valued
1
-bounded finitely supported functions on
[N]
. We define the
s
-th moment energy

E_{s}
by

E_{s}(f_{1},\dots,f_{h})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(f_{1}*\cdots*f_{h}(n))^{s}.

When
f_{1}=\dots=f_{h}=f
, we denote it by
E_{h,s}(f)
.

Let us first discuss the trivial bounds for the
s
-th moment energy. Let
A\subset[N]
.
If each
f_{i}
is either
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
or
\mathbbm{1}_{-A}
, then one has the crude estimate

|A|^{h}\leq E_{s}(f_{1},\dots,f_{h})\leq|A|^{sh-s+1}.

Indeed, the lower bound follows from
E_{s}\geq E_{1}
and the identity
E_{1}(f_{1},\dots,f_{h})=|A|^{h}
. For the upper bound, note that

E_{s}(f_{1},\dots,f_{h})\leq\Big(\sup_{n}f_{1}*\cdots*f_{h}(n)\Big)^{s-1}E_{1}(f_{1},\dots,f_{h}),

and
\sup_{n}f_{1}*\cdots*f_{h}(n)\leq|A|^{h-1}
holds trivially, since for each fixed
n
there are at most
|A|^{h-1}
choices of
(a_{1},\dots,a_{h-1})\in A^{h-1}
and then
a_{h}
is determined.

A good control on the higher moment energy
E_{s}
naturally gives a good consequence on lower moment energy
E_{s-1}
and
E_{2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
A\subseteq[N]
satisfies
E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll|A|^{s}
for integer
s\geq 2
. Then

E_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll|A|^{3-\frac{1}{s-1}},\qquad E_{s-1}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll|A|^{s-\frac{s-2}{s-1}}.

Proof.

Let
f=\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mathbbm{1}_{-A}
. Note that
\|f\|_{1}=|A|^{2}
. An application of Hölder’s inequality implies

E_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})=\|f\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left\|f^{\frac{s-2}{s-1}}\right\|^{2}_{\frac{s-1}{s-2}}\cdot\left\|f^{\frac{s}{s-1}}\right\|^{2}_{s-1}\ll|A|^{3-\frac{1}{s-1}}.

The bound for
E_{s-1}
follows similarly by interpolating between
L^{1}
and
L^{s}
.

\|f\|^{s-1}_{s-1}\leq\left\|f^{\frac{s(s-2)}{s-1}}\right\|^{s-1}_{\frac{s-1}{s-2}}\cdot\left\|f^{\frac{1}{s-1}}\right\|^{s-1}_{s-1}\ll|A|^{s-\frac{s-2}{s-1}},

as claimed.
∎

We next explore how the
K_{s,t}
freeness leads to a control on the high moment energy
E_{s}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
2\leq s\leq t
and let
A\subseteq[N]
be
K_{s,t}
-free. Then there is a constant
c_{s}\in(0,1]
such that

E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\leq t|A|^{s}+O(|A|^{s-c_{s}}).

Proof.

Write the representation function

r(d):=(\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mathbbm{1}_{-A})(d)=\#\{(a,a^{\prime})\in A^{2}:\ a-a^{\prime}=d\}.

Then

(2)

E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})=\sum_{d}r(d)^{s}=\#\Bigl\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{s},a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{s}^{\prime})\in A^{2s}:\ a_{1}-a_{1}^{\prime}=\cdots=a_{s}-a_{s}^{\prime}\Bigr\}.

We split the count according to the common difference
d:=a_{i}-a_{i}^{\prime}
. First, note that if
d=0
then
a_{i}^{\prime}=a_{i}
for all
i
, so this contributes exactly
|A|^{s}
to
E_{s}
.

Fix an ordered
s
-tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\in A^{s}
with pairwise distinct entries. For
d\in\mathbb{Z}
define the set of admissible shifts

D(a_{1},\dots,a_{s}):=\{d\neq 0:\ a_{1}-d,\dots,a_{s}-d\in A\}.

Note that for each
d\in D(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
there is exactly one corresponding

(a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{s}^{\prime})
, namely
a_{i}^{\prime}=a_{i}-d
. Hence the number of nonzero-
d
configurations in
(
2
) with the given
(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
is precisely
|D(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})|
.

We claim that

(3)

|D(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})|\leq t-1.

Indeed, suppose for contradiction that
|D(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})|\geq t
.
Choose
t
distinct shifts
d_{1},\dots,d_{t}\in D(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
and form the
s\times t
grid

a_{i,j}:=a_{i}-d_{j}\in A\qquad(1\leq i\leq s,\ 1\leq j\leq t).

For each fixed column
j
we have
a_{i}-a_{i,j}=d_{j}
, so in particular for every
2\leq j\leq t
,

(4)

a_{1,1}-a_{1,j}=a_{2,1}-a_{2,j}=\cdots=a_{s,1}-a_{s,j}.

Define

x_{i}:=a_{i,1}=a_{i}-d_{1},\qquad y_{j}:=a_{1,j}-a_{1,1}=-(d_{j}-d_{1}).

Then for every
i,j
we can rewrite (
4
) as

a_{i,j}=a_{i,1}+(a_{1,j}-a_{1,1})=x_{i}+y_{j}.

Since the
a_{i}
are pairwise distinct, the
x_{i}=a_{i,1}
are also pairwise distinct.
Since the
d_{j}
are distinct, the differences
d_{j}-d_{1}
are distinct, and the
y_{j}
are distinct as well.
Thus we have produced sets

B:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\},\qquad C:=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{t}\},

with
|B|=s
,
|C|=t
, and

B+C=\{x_{i}+y_{j}:\ 1\leq i\leq s,\ 1\leq j\leq t\}\subseteq A,

contradicting that
A
is
K_{s,t}
-free. This proves (
3
).

Consequently, for every
distinct

(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\in A^{s}
there are at most
t-1
nonzero shifts
d

(and hence at most
t-1
nontrivial choices of
(a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{s}^{\prime})
) contributing to (
2
).

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq A^{s}
be the set of
s
-tuples with pairwise distinct entries, and let

\mathcal{R}:=A^{s}\setminus\mathcal{D}
be the set of tuples with repeated entries.
From the discussion above and (
3
), the nonzero-
d
contribution coming from
\mathcal{D}

is at most
(t-1)|\mathcal{D}|\leq(t-1)|A|^{s}
.

It remains to control the contribution coming from
\mathcal{R}
. This is a lower-dimensional
degenerate set of configurations; one convenient bound is that for each fixed
d\neq 0
, the number
of
s
-tuples in
A^{s}
with a repetition and with
a_{i}-d\in A
for all
i
is
O_{s}(r(d)^{s-1})
,
so the total contribution from
\mathcal{R}
is

\ll_{s}\sum_{d\neq 0}r(d)^{s-1}\leq E_{s-1}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A}).

(Here we use that having at least one repetition means the
s
-tuple is determined by at most
s-1

independent choices of pairs
(a,a-d)\in A^{2}
.)

By the estimates above and the trivial bound
E_{s-1}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll_{s}|A|^{s}
, we have

E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll_{s}(t-1)|A|^{s}+E_{s-1}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll_{s,t}|A|^{s}.

Thus
Lemma
2.1
is applicable and yields

E_{s-1}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll|A|^{\,s-\frac{s-2}{s-1}}.

Putting everything together, we get

E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\leq|A|^{s}\;+\;(t-1)|A|^{s}\;+\;O(|A|^{s-\frac{s-2}{s-1}})

as required for
s>2
, where we set
c_{s}=\frac{s-2}{s-1}
.

For
s=2
, we simply have

E_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})=|A|^{2}+\sum_{d\neq 0}(t-1)r(d)\leq t|A|^{2}-(t-1)|A|,

hence
c_{2}=1
works.
∎

The next lemma records a control on the representation function, given an upper bound on the high moment energy.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\eta\in(0,1)
and
2\leq s\leq t
.
Suppose
A\subseteq[N]
satisfies
E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\leq(t+\eta)|A|^{s}
.
For
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A
define

r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}-a_{1}^{\prime}=a_{2}-a_{2}^{\prime}=\cdots=a_{s}-a_{s}^{\prime}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a_{1}^{\prime})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a_{s}^{\prime}).

Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})>t-1\end{subarray}}1\leq\left(1-\frac{1-\eta}{t}\right)|A|^{s}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})>t-1\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})>t-1\end{subarray}}r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})-(t-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})>t-1\end{subarray}}1.

Therefore,

\displaystyle t\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})>t-1\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A}r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})=(t-1+\eta)|A|^{s},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\eta\in(0,1)
and
2\leq s\leq t
.
Suppose
A\subseteq[N]
and
E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\leq(t+\eta)|A|^{s}
. Then

|A|\leq 2\Bigl(\frac{t^{2}}{1-\eta}\Bigr)^{1/s}N^{1-1/s}.

Proof.

Split
A^{s}=\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\}
according to whether
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})>t-1
:

\displaystyle|A|^{s}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})>t-1\end{subarray}}1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\leq t-1\end{subarray}}1\leq\left(1-\frac{1-\eta}{t}\right)|A|^{s}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\leq t-1\end{subarray}}1,

where we used Lemma
2.3
. Hence

\frac{1-\eta}{t}\,|A|^{s}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\leq t-1\end{subarray}}1.

We count the solutions to
equation
a_{1}+n_{1}=\cdots=a_{s}+n_{s}
, where
(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\in A^{s}
and
(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})\in[2N]^{s}

(e.g. take
n_{i}:=2N-a_{i}
).
On one hand, for a fixed
(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
the number of choices of
(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})\in[2N]^{s}
with
a_{1}+n_{1}=\cdots=a_{s}+n_{s}
is at least
N
(shifting all
n_{i}
by a common integer in a suitable range). On the other hand, for a fixed choice of
(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})\in[2N]^{s}
, we claim there are at most
t
different choices of
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})
such that
a_{1}+n_{1}=\cdots=a_{s}+n_{s}
. Suppose the claim is wrong, then we can find
t
more choices of
(a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{s}^{\prime})
together with
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})
but this gives
t
solutions to
a_{1}-a_{1}^{\prime}=\cdots=a_{s}-a_{s}^{\prime}
, and this contradicts ti
r_{A}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})\leq t-1
.
The above double counting gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\leq t-1\end{subarray}}1\leq\frac{t(2N)^{s}}{N}.

Combining the bounds gives

|A|^{s}\leq\frac{t^{2}(2N)^{s}}{(1-\eta)N},

which proves the lemma.
∎

For each
x
, define
(x)_{+}=\max\{0,x\}
. The following easy lemma provides a vanishing behavior for
K_{s,t}
-free sets. It is essentially already proved in Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A\subseteq[N]
be
K_{s,t}
-free. Then

\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A}(r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})-(t-1))_{+}\ll_{s,t}|A|^{s-c_{s}},

for some constant
c_{s}\in(0,1)
depending only on
s
.

Proof.

Since
A
is
K_{s,t}
-free,
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\leq t-1
for all tuples
(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
with distinct coordinates. This is equivalent to equation (
3
) which has been proved in Lemma
2.2
. Hence the positive part is supported on degenerate tuples. Summing over degeneracies and using Lemma
2.2
, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

3.
A dense model theorem

We first recall the following version of the large sieve inequality
[
18
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\delta_{j}>0
(
j=1,2,\cdots,d
) and
\Gamma
is a nonempty set of points
\gamma
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the open sets

U(\gamma):=\{\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{d}):\|\beta_{j}-\gamma_{j}\|<\delta_{j},\ 0\leq\beta_{j}<1\}

are pairwise disjoint. Let
N_{1},\dots,N_{d}
be natural numbers and let
\mathcal{N}
be the set of
d
-tuples
n=(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})
with
1\leq n_{j}\leq N_{j}
. Then the sum

S(\boldsymbol{\beta})=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}a(n)e(n\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}),

where
a(n)
are complex numbers, satisfies

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|S(\gamma)|^{2}\ll\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}|a(n)|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(N_{j}+\delta_{j}^{-1}).

Next we prove the following dense model theorem, which is the main result of the section.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
t,s,\eta,\varepsilon\geq 0
be fixed.
Suppose
A\subseteq[N]
satisfies
|A|>\delta N^{1-1/s}
and
A
is
K_{s,t}
-free.
Then there is
f:[-\varepsilon N,(1+\varepsilon)N]\cap\mathbb{Z}\to[0,\infty)
such that the following holds:

(i)

\sum_{n}f(n)=N^{\frac{1}{s}}|A|
;

(ii)

\|\widehat{f}-N^{\frac{1}{s}}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}\|_{\infty}\ll\varepsilon N
;

(iii)

\sum_{n}f^{s}(n)\leq N\Bigl(t+\exp(\varepsilon^{-O_{s}(1)})\cdot O_{s,t}\!\Bigl(\frac{t^{2}\eta}{1-\eta}\Bigr)\Bigr)
for some
\eta\ll_{s,t}|A|^{-c_{s}}
.

Proof.

As usual we define the spectrum set

\mathrm{Spec}(A,\varepsilon)=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}:|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\alpha)|\geq\varepsilon|A|\}.

Consider the Bohr set

B=\{n\in[-\varepsilon N,\varepsilon N]\cap\mathbb{Z}:\|n\alpha\|_{\mathbb{T}}<\varepsilon\text{ for every }\alpha\in\mathrm{Spec}(A,\varepsilon)\}.

We use
\mu_{B}
to denote the normalized characteristic function on
B
. Define

f=N^{\frac{1}{s}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mu_{B}.

Clearly
f
satisfies (i). Now we consider
\widehat{f}-N^{\frac{1}{s}}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}
. Note that

|\widehat{f}-N^{\frac{1}{s}}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}|=N^{\frac{1}{s}}|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}|\,\bigl|1-\widehat{\mu_{B}}\bigr|.

For
\alpha\notin\mathrm{Spec}(A,\varepsilon)
, by definition and Lemma
2.4
we get

|\widehat{f}(\alpha)-N^{\frac{1}{s}}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\alpha)|\leq 2N^{\frac{1}{s}}\varepsilon|A|\ll\varepsilon N.

For
\alpha\in\mathrm{Spec}(A,\varepsilon)
, we have
\widehat{\mu_{B}}(\alpha)=1+O(\varepsilon)
, and again

|\widehat{f}(\alpha)-N^{\frac{1}{s}}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\alpha)|\ll\varepsilon N^{\frac{1}{s}}|A|\ll\varepsilon N.

This proves (ii).

Finally, we write

\sum_{n}f^{s}(n)=N|B|^{-s}\sum_{n}(\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mathbbm{1}_{B}(n))^{s}.

Noticing that

\displaystyle\sum_{n}(\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mathbbm{1}_{B}(n))^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1}+b_{1}=\cdots=a_{s}+b_{s}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a_{1})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a_{s})\mathbbm{1}_{B}(b_{1})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{B}(b_{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}+b_{1}=\cdots=a_{s}+b_{s}\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\leq t-1\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a_{1})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a_{s})\mathbbm{1}_{B}(b_{1})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{B}(b_{s})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}+b_{1}=\cdots=a_{s}+b_{s}\\
r_{A}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})>t-1\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a_{1})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a_{s})\mathbbm{1}_{B}(b_{1})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{B}(b_{s})

\displaystyle\leq t|B|^{s}+\eta|A|^{s}|B|,

for some
\eta\ll_{s,t}|A|^{-c_{s}}
,
where in the last step we used Lemma
2.5
.

It remains to give a lower bound on
|B|
. We use a standard probabilistic argument to achieve this.
Let
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}
be a maximal
1/N
-separated subset of
\mathrm{Spec}(A,\varepsilon)
. By maximality,

B^{\prime}:=\{n\in[-\varepsilon N/2,\varepsilon N/2]\cap\mathbb{Z}:\|p_{i}n\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\varepsilon/2\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq m\}\subseteq B.

For each
p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
, choose
\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{T}
uniformly at random. Then for every
x\in[-\varepsilon N/2,\varepsilon N/2]\cap\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathbb{P}(\|p_{i}x-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{T}}<\varepsilon/4\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq m)=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{m}.

Define

B^{\prime\prime}:=\{x\in[-\varepsilon N/2,\varepsilon N/2]\cap\mathbb{Z}:\|p_{i}x-\theta_{i}\|_{\mathbb{T}}<\varepsilon/4\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq m\}.

Hence
\mathbb{E}\left|B^{\prime\prime}\right|\geq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{m+1}N.

By pigeonhole principle, there is a choice of
\{\theta_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
such that
\left|B^{\prime\prime}\right|\geq(\varepsilon/2)^{m+1}N
. Note that for
x,x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime\prime}
, their difference satisfies
x-x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
. This gives

\left|B^{\prime}\right|\geq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{m+1}N.

Finally, note that
|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}|^{2}=\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mathbbm{1}_{-A}}
. By applying Lemma
3.1
(with
d=1
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\varepsilon^{4}m|A|^{4}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(p_{i})|^{4}\ll NE_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll N|A|^{3-\frac{1}{s-1}}.

As
|A|>\delta N^{1-1/s}
, we have
m\ll_{s}\delta^{-2}\varepsilon^{-4}
. By choosing
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
, it gives

|B|\geq|B^{\prime}|\geq\exp(-\varepsilon^{-O_{s}(1)})N.

Substituting this into the previous inequality gives (iii).
∎

4.
Counting Lemmas

We use a variant of the following counting result by Kościuszko
[
13
]
, which quantitatively improves a result of Bloom
[
2
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(Kościuszko
[
13
]
)
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with
k\geq 5
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=0
. Then for any
A\subseteq[N]
of size
\delta N
, we have the lower bound

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x_{1})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x_{k})\geq\exp(-O_{a_{i}}(\log^{7}(1/\delta)))N^{k-1}.

Our application requires a
L^{p}
-weighted version of the above result, which can be derived by a level set argument.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
with
k\geq 5
and
a_{1}+\cdots a_{k}=0
,
p\geq 2
. Let
f:I\to[0,\infty)
be a function defined in an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
of size
|I|=N
. Suppose
\sum_{n}f(n)\geq\delta N
and
\sum_{n}f(n)^{p}\leq N
.
Then we have the lower bound

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}f(x_{1})f(x_{2})\cdots f(x_{k})\geq\exp(-O_{a_{i},p}(\log^{7}(1/\delta)))N^{k-1}.

Proof.

Let
Z=f(n)
be a random variable on
I
, which is uniformly and independent distributed for
n\in I
. Then the conditions are the same as saying
\mathbb{E}(Z)\geq\delta
and
\mathbb{E}(Z^{p})\leq 1
. By the Paley–Zygmund level set inequality
[
16
]
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(Z\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(Z)\Big)\geq\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\frac{(\mathbb{E}Z)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{\mathbb{(}\mathbb{E}(Z^{p}))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\geq\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}.

This implies that, by defining
A=\{n:f(n)\geq\delta/2\}
, we have
|A|\geq(\frac{\delta}{2})^{\frac{p}{p-1}}N
. Now by Lemma
4.1
, for
k\geq 5
,

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}1_{A}(x_{1})1_{A}(x_{2})\cdots 1_{A}(x_{k})\geq\exp(-O_{a_{i},p}(\log^{7}(1/\delta)))N^{k-1}.

As
f(n)\geq\delta/2
on
A
, we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}f(x_{1})f(x_{2})\cdots f(x_{k})

\displaystyle\geq(\delta/2)^{k}\exp(-O_{a_{i},p}(\log^{7}(1/\delta^{2})))N^{k-1}

\displaystyle\geq\exp(-O_{a_{i},p}(\log^{7}(1/\delta)))N^{k-1},

as the
(\delta/2)^{k}
factor will be absorbed by the
\exp(-O_{a_{i},p}(\log^{7}(1/\delta)))
term.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Counting lemma for
E_{2}
)
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with
k\geq 5
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=0
. Let
\nu:I\to[0,\infty)
be a function defined on an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
of length
N
. Suppose
\sum_{n}\nu(n)\ll N
and

E_{2}(\nu,\nu)\ll N^{3}.

Then for every
|f_{i}|\leq\nu
we have

\left|\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\prod_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}(x_{j})\right|\ll N^{k-2}\min_{i}\|\widehat{f_{i}}\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

By Fourier inversion,
one has

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\prod_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}(x_{j})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{f_{j}}(a_{j}\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.

Fix any
i
between
1
and
k
. By Hölder inequality, it holds that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{f_{j}}(a_{j}\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\right|\leq\|\widehat{f_{i}}\|_{\infty}\prod_{j\neq i}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\widehat{f_{j}}(\alpha)|^{k-1}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}.

For the inner integral, we have,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\widehat{f_{j}}(\alpha)|^{k-1}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\leq\|\widehat{f_{j}}\|_{\infty}^{k-5}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\widehat{f_{j}}(\alpha)|^{4}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\ll N^{k-5}E_{2}(f_{j},f_{j})\ll N^{k-2},

where we used
\|\widehat{f_{j}}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{n}|f_{j}(n)|\leq\sum_{n}\nu(n)\ll N
and

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\widehat{f_{j}}(\alpha)|^{4}d\alpha=E_{2}(f_{j},f_{j})\leq E_{2}(\nu,\nu)\ll N^{3}.

This gives the desired bound.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove our main result in the integer setting.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Write
|A|=\delta N^{1-1/s}
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
be a parameter to be chosen later. By Lemma
2.2
we have
E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\leq(t+o(1))|A|^{s}
, so Lemma
3.2
yields a function

f:I:=[-\varepsilon N,(1+\varepsilon)N]\cap\mathbb{Z}\to[0,\infty)

such that

(i)

\sum_{n\in I}f(n)=N^{1/s}|A|=\delta N
,

(ii)

\|\widehat{f}-N^{1/s}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}\|_{\infty}\ll\varepsilon N
,

(iii)

\sum_{n\in I}f(n)^{s}\ll_{s,t}N
,

whenever

(5)

\exp(\varepsilon^{-O_{s}(1)})\ll N^{c_{s}}.

Define the multilinear counting form

T(h_{1},\dots,h_{k}):=\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\prod_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}(x_{j}),\qquad T(h):=T(h,\dots,h).

Now apply Lemma
4.2
to
f^{\prime}:=f/C^{1/s}
, where
C
is a large absolute constant (depending only on
s,t
) coming from the hidden constant from (iii) above, so that
\sum_{I}f^{\prime s}\leq N
and
\sum_{I}f^{\prime}\geq\delta N/C^{1/s}
. This yields

(6)

T(f)\geq\exp\bigl(-O_{a_{i},s,t}(\log^{7}(1/\delta))\bigr)\,N^{k-1}.

Next, we transfer the counting result to
A
.
Set
F:=N^{1/s}\mathbbm{1}_{A}
,
g:=f-F
, and
\nu:=f+F
.
Then
\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\ll\varepsilon N
by (ii) above.
By Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.1
, as well as Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}(\nu)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{\nu}\|_{4}\ll\|\widehat{f}\|_{4}+N^{\frac{1}{s}}\|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq N^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+N^{\frac{1}{s}}E_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})^{\frac{1}{4}}\ll N^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Notice that

\sum_{n}\nu(n)=\widehat{f}(0)+N^{1/s}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(0)\leq 2\widehat{f}(0)+\varepsilon N\leq 2(2N)^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}+\varepsilon N\ll N.

We use
the telescoping identity to get

\prod_{j=1}^{k}f(x_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}F(x_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}g(x_{i})\Bigl(\prod_{j<i}f(x_{j})\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{j>i}F(x_{j})\Bigr).

Note that
f,F\ll\nu
and an application of Lemma
4.3
(viewing functions
g,F
as
f_{i}
in the lemma) gives

(7)

|T(f)-T(F)|\ll_{k}N^{k-2}\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\ll_{k}\varepsilon N^{k-1}.

Since
A
has no nontrivial solutions, the only solutions counted by
T(F)
are diagonal,

T(F)=N^{k/s}\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x_{1})\cdots\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x_{k})=N^{k/s}|A|=\delta\,N^{1+\frac{k-1}{s}}.

Since
k\geq 5
and
s\geq 2
, we have
1+\frac{k-1}{s}<k-1
, and
T(F)=o(N^{k-1})
.
Combining this with (
7
) gives, for
N
large, one has

(8)

T(f)\ll_{k}\varepsilon N^{k-1}.

Comparing (
6
) and (
8
) yields

\exp\bigl(-O_{a_{i},s,t}(\log^{7}(1/\delta))\bigr)\ll_{k}\varepsilon.

Combining with (
5
), we have

\delta\ll\exp(-c(\log\log N)^{1/7}),

for some
c=c(a_{i},s,t)>0
. Recalling that
|A|=\delta N^{1-1/s}
completes the proof.
∎

6.
Finite field analogues for
K_{s,t}
-free sets

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
using the polynomial arithmetic
k
-cycle removal lemma
of Fox–Lovász–Sauermann
[
9
]
. Our argument here follows the same transference scheme as in the
integer case, with the key difference that Kościuszko/Bloom type quantitative Roth inputs are replaced by a
polynomial counting lemma coming from
[
9
]
(whose proof ultimately relies on the
polynomial/slice-rank method of Croot–Lev–Pach
[
6
]
and Ellenberg–Gijswijt
[
7
]
).

Throughout this section let
q=p^{r}
be a fixed odd prime power, let

G:=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\qquad N:=|G|=q^{n},

and fix coefficients
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
with

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=0.

As before we say a solution
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in A^{k}
to

(9)

a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0

is
trivial
if
x_{1}=\cdots=x_{k}
, and
nontrivial
otherwise.

We identify the dual group
\widehat{G}
with
G
in the usual way.
Fix once and for all a nontrivial additive character

e_{q}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},

for instance

e_{q}(u):=\exp\!\Big(\frac{2\pi i}{p}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(u)\Big),\qquad p=\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

For each
\xi\in G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
we define the corresponding additive character

\chi_{\xi}:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\qquad\chi_{\xi}(x):=e_{q}(\langle x,\xi\rangle),

where
\langle x,\xi\rangle
denotes the standard dot product on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
Every additive character of the group
(G,+)
arises uniquely in this way, so this gives a canonical
identification
\widehat{G}\simeq G
.

With this notation, for a subspace
V\leq G
we define its
annihilator

V^{\perp}:=\{x\in G:\ \chi_{\xi}(x)=1\ \text{for all }\xi\in V\}=\{x\in G:\ \langle x,\xi\rangle=0\ \text{for all }\xi\in V\}.

The annihilator
V^{\perp}
is a subspace of
G
, and
(V^{\perp})^{\perp}=V
.

For
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
define

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\,e_{q}(-\langle x,\xi\rangle)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\,\overline{\chi_{\xi}(x)}.

Then Parseval identity reads

\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\overline{g(x)}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{G}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi)},

and convolution is
(f*g)(x)=\sum_{y\in G}f(y)g(x-y)
.

All the energy lemmas from the integer setting (in particular Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
)
extend verbatim to
G
as they only use abelian group structure. Likewise, the counting lemma
Lemma
4.3
extends verbatim to
G
once one replaces integrals over
\mathbb{T}
by averages over
the finite dual group
\widehat{G}
as this uses only Parseval identity and Hölder inequality. Given this, and with abuse of notation, we may later quote lemmas proved in the integer setting but apply them in the finite field setting.

We next discuss the analogue of the dense model Lemma
3.2
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
Over
G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
the dense model step simplifies substantially, because the analogue of a Bohr set is an

actual subspace
. Indeed, for any
subspace
H\leq G
the uniform measure
\mu_{H}:=|H|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{H}
has Fourier transform

\widehat{\mu_{H}}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,&\xi\in H^{\perp},\\
0,&\xi\notin H^{\perp},\end{cases}

Consequently, for any function
g:G\to\mathbb{C}
we have in Fourier space

\widehat{g*\mu_{H}}(\xi)=\widehat{g}(\xi)\,\widehat{\mu_{H}}(\xi)=\widehat{g}(\xi)\,\mathbbm{1}_{H^{\perp}}(\xi),

so convolution with
\mu_{H}
keeps precisely the Fourier coefficients of
g
on
H^{\perp}
and kills all Fourier coefficients outside
H^{\perp}
, i.e. convolution with
\mu_{H}
is an
exact Fourier projector
onto the subspace
H^{\perp}\leq\widehat{G}
.
Thus, by choosing
H
to annihilate the large spectrum of
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
, we can hope to smooth
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
while
preserving its large Fourier coefficients and simultaneously keeping good
L^{s}
control. This is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1

(Dense model in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
)
.

Suppose
A\subseteq G
satisfies
|A|>\delta N^{1-1/s}
and
A
is
K_{s,t}
-free.
Then for every
0<\varepsilon<1
there exists
f:G\to[0,\infty)
such that

(i)

\sum_{x\in G}f(x)=N^{1/s}|A|
;

(ii)

\|\widehat{f}-N^{1/s}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}\|_{\infty}\ll\varepsilon N
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in G}f(x)^{s}\leq N\Big(t+\exp(\varepsilon^{-O_{s}(1)})\cdot O_{s,t}(\eta)\Big)
for some
\eta\ll_{s,t}|A|^{-c_{s}}
.

Proof.

Define the large spectrum

\operatorname{Spec}(A,\varepsilon):=\{\xi\in G:\ |\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\xi)|\geq\varepsilon|A|\}.

Let
V:=\mathrm{Span}(\operatorname{Spec}(A,\varepsilon))\leq G
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear span of
\operatorname{Spec}(A,\varepsilon)
, and set

H:=V^{\perp}=\{x\in G:\ \chi_{\xi}(x)=1\text{ for all }\xi\in V\}.

Then
H
is a subspace of
G
and
\widehat{\mu_{H}}=\mathbbm{1}_{V}
(i.e.
\widehat{\mu_{H}}(\xi)=1
if

\xi\in V
and
0
otherwise).

We next claim that
\dim(V)\ll_{s,t,\delta}\varepsilon^{-4}
. To see this, let
\Lambda\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}(A,\varepsilon)
be any set of linearly independent vectors spanning

V
, so
\dim(V)=|\Lambda|
. Then

|\Lambda|\,\varepsilon^{4}|A|^{4}\ \leq\ \sum_{\xi\in\Lambda}|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\xi)|^{4}\ \leq\ \sum_{\xi\in G}|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\xi)|^{4}\ =\ N\cdot E_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A}).

By Lemma
2.1
and the hypothesis

E_{s}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll_{s,t}|A|^{s}
, we have

E_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{A},\mathbbm{1}_{-A})\ll_{s}|A|^{3-\frac{1}{s-1}}
, so

\dim(V)=|\Lambda|\ \ll_{s}\ \frac{N}{\varepsilon^{4}|A|^{1+\frac{1}{s-1}}}.

Using
|A|>\delta N^{1-1/s}
and the identity

(1-\tfrac{1}{s})(1+\tfrac{1}{s-1})=1
, we obtain
\dim(V)\ll_{s,\delta}\varepsilon^{-4}
.
In particular,

(10)

|H|=q^{n-\dim(V)}\geq q^{-O_{s,\delta}(\varepsilon^{-4})}N=\exp(-\varepsilon^{-O_{s}(1)})N.

Let
\mu_{H}:=|H|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{H}
and define

f:=N^{1/s}\,\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mu_{H}.

We now check that
f
satisfies properties (i)-(iii). Clearly,
f\geq 0
and (i) is immediate since
\sum_{x}\mu_{H}(x)=1
. For (ii), using
\widehat{\mu_{H}}=\mathbbm{1}_{V}
we have for every
\xi\in G
,

\widehat{f}(\xi)=N^{1/s}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\xi)\widehat{\mu_{H}}(\xi)=\begin{cases}N^{1/s}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\xi),&\xi\in V,\\
0,&\xi\notin V.\end{cases}

Hence
\widehat{f}(\xi)-N^{1/s}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\xi)=0
for
\xi\in V
, while for
\xi\notin V
we have

\xi\notin\operatorname{Spec}(A,\varepsilon)
and therefore
|\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(\xi)|<\varepsilon|A|
. Thus

\|\widehat{f}-N^{1/s}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}\|_{\infty}\leq N^{1/s}\varepsilon|A|\ll_{s,t}\varepsilon N,

where in the last step we used a finite field version of Lemma
2.4
to bound
|A|\ll_{s,t}N^{1-1/s}
.

To prove the
L^{s}
bound (iii), we start by writing
\mathbbm{1}_{H}
for the (unnormalized) indicator of
H
and by noting that

f^{s}=N(\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mu_{H})^{s}
. Therefore

(11)

\sum_{x\in G}f(x)^{s}=N\sum_{x\in G}(\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mu_{H}(x))^{s}=N|H|^{-s}\sum_{x\in G}(\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mathbbm{1}_{H}(x))^{s}.

For convenience, set

S:=\sum_{x\in G}(\mathbbm{1}_{A}*\mathbbm{1}_{H}(x))^{s},

and rewrite the quantity as follows:

S=\sum_{h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in H}\ \sum_{x\in G}\ \prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x-h_{i})=\sum_{h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in H}|S_{\mathbf{h}}|,

where
S_{\mathbf{h}}:=\{x\in G:\ x-h_{i}\in A\,\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq s\}
and
\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{s})
.

For an ordered
s
-tuple
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\in A^{s}
define

r_{A}(\mathbf{a}):=\bigl|\{d\in G\setminus\{0\}:\ a_{i}-d\in A\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq s\}\bigr|.

As
A
is
K_{s,t}
-free, Lemma
2.5
implies that there is
\eta=O(|A|^{-c_{s}})
such that

(12)

\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in A^{s}}\bigl(r_{A}(\mathbf{a})-(t-1)\bigr)_{+}\ \leq\ \eta|A|^{s},

where
(x)_{+}:=\max\{x,0\}
for a real number
x
.

Next, fix
\mathbf{h}
and suppose
S_{\mathbf{h}}\neq\emptyset
. For any
x\in S_{\mathbf{h}}
the
associated tuple
\mathbf{a}(x):=(x-h_{1},\dots,x-h_{s})\in A^{s}
satisfies

(13)

r_{A}(\mathbf{a}(x))=|S_{\mathbf{h}}|-1.

Indeed, if
x^{\prime}\in S_{\mathbf{h}}
then
\mathbf{a}(x^{\prime})=\mathbf{a}(x)-(x-x^{\prime})
coordinatewise, giving a shift
counted by
r_{A}
; conversely, if
\mathbf{a}(x)-d\in A^{s}
then
x-d\in S_{\mathbf{h}}
.
In particular,
|S_{\mathbf{h}}|>t
if and only if
r_{A}(\mathbf{a}(x))>t-1
.

Notice that
|S_{\mathbf{h}}|\ \leq\ t+\bigl(|S_{\mathbf{h}}|-t\bigr)_{+}
always holds and it implies that

(14)

S\ \leq\ t|H|^{s}+\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in H^{s}}\bigl(|S_{\mathbf{h}}|-t\bigr)_{+}.

If
|S_{\mathbf{h}}|>t
, then
|S_{\mathbf{h}}|\bigl(|S_{\mathbf{h}}|-t\bigr)_{+}\geq t\bigl(|S_{\mathbf{h}}|-t\bigr)_{+}
, and further

(15)

\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in H^{s}}\bigl(|S_{\mathbf{h}}|-t\bigr)_{+}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{t}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in H^{s}}|S_{\mathbf{h}}|\bigl(|S_{\mathbf{h}}|-t\bigr)_{+}.

Using (
13
), the right hand side of (
15
) equals

\frac{1}{t}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in H^{s}}\ \sum_{x\in S_{\mathbf{h}}}\bigl(r_{A}(\mathbf{a}(x))-(t-1)\bigr)_{+}.

Group this sum by
\mathbf{a}\in A^{s}
. For a fixed
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
, the number of pairs

(\mathbf{h},x)
with
x-h_{i}=a_{i}
for all
i
is at most
|H|
: indeed, choosing any
h_{1}\in H
determines

x=a_{1}+h_{1}
and then
h_{i}=x-a_{i}=h_{1}+(a_{1}-a_{i})
. Therefore

\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in H^{s}}|S_{\mathbf{h}}|\bigl(|S_{\mathbf{h}}|-t\bigr)_{+}\ \leq\ |H|\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in A^{s}}\bigl(r_{A}(\mathbf{a})-(t-1)\bigr)_{+}\ \leq\ \eta|A|^{s}|H|

where in the last inequality we used
(
12
). Combining this with (
15
) and (
14
) gives

S\ \leq\ t|H|^{s}+\frac{\eta}{t}|A|^{s}|H|.

Invoking (
11
) gives

\sum_{x\in G}f(x)^{s}\leq N\left(t+\frac{\eta}{t}\cdot\frac{|A|^{s}}{|H|^{s-1}}\right).

Finally, using
|A|\ll_{s,t}N^{1-1/s}
from Lemma
2.4
and

|H|\geq\exp(-\varepsilon^{-O_{s}(1)})N
from (
10
) , we conclude that

\frac{|A|^{s}}{|H|^{s-1}}\ \ll_{s,t}\ \exp(\varepsilon^{-O_{s}(1)})
,
and thereby (iii) follows.
∎

We next introduce the key ingredient in this section, which is an arithmetic
k
-cycle removal lemma and a corresponding polynomial counting lemma.

Theorem 6.2

(Fox–Lovász–Sauermann
[
9
]
)
.

Let
p
be a fixed prime and
k\geq 3
. There exists a constant
C_{p,k}>0
such that the following holds.
Writing
N=p^{m}
, for every
0<\epsilon<1
and
\delta=\epsilon^{C_{p,k}}
, and for any sets

X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{m}
, at least one of the following holds:

(a)

the number of
k
-tuples
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{k}
satisfying

x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}=0
is at least
\delta N^{k-1}
;

(b)

one can delete fewer than
\epsilon N
elements from each
X_{i}
so that afterwards no such
k
-tuples remain.

The key feature for us is the polynomial dependence
\delta=\epsilon^{C_{p,k}}
and

[
9
]
gives an explicit exponent
C_{p,k}
which is built from the sharp
arithmetic triangle bounds.

We apply Theorem
6.2
to
G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
by identifying
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},+)
with
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{rn}
, where
p=\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
q=p^{r}
.
In particular, the same exponent
C_{p,k}
works, and we denote it by
C_{q,k}
.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
A_{0}\subseteq G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
have density
\rho:=|A_{0}|/N
. Then there exists a constant
C_{q,k}>0
such that

\#\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in A_{0}^{k}:\ a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0\}\ \geq\ \Big(\frac{\rho}{2k}\Big)^{C_{q,k}}\,N^{k-1}.

Proof.

Set
X_{i}:=a_{i}A_{0}\subseteq G
. Since
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, the map
x\mapsto a_{i}x
is a bijection on
G
, so

|X_{i}|=|A_{0}|=\rho N
for all
i
.

For every
x\in A_{0}
, the
k
-tuple
(a_{1}x,\dots,a_{k}x)\in X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{k}
satisfies

(a_{1}x)+\cdots+(a_{k}x)=(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k})x=0,

so it is a
k
-cycle. Moreover, for each fixed
i
, the elements
a_{i}x
are all distinct as
x
varies,
so these
\rho N
cycles are disjoint within each coordinate class
X_{i}
.

Apply Theorem
6.2
to the sets
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
with parameter
\epsilon^{\prime}:=\rho/(2k)
.
If conclusion (b) held, then deleting fewer than
\epsilon^{\prime}N
elements from each
X_{i}
would delete fewer than

k\epsilon^{\prime}N=\rho N/2
elements in total across
X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k}
.
But each deleted element in
X_{i}
can destroy at most one of the disjoint cycles
(a_{1}x,\dots,a_{k}x)
,
so destroying all
\rho N
cycles requires at least
\rho N
deletions in total. This contradiction rules out (b), so (a) must hold.
Thus the number of cycles is at least
\delta N^{k-1}
with
\delta=(\epsilon^{\prime})^{C_{q,k}}=(\rho/2k)^{C_{q,k}}
.

Finally, the bijection
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\mapsto(a_{1}x_{1},\dots,a_{k}x_{k})
identifies solutions of (
9
)
in
A_{0}^{k}
with
k
-cycles in
X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{k}
, so the same lower bound holds.
∎

The following lemma is a direct analogous of Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
f:G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
satisfy

\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\geq\delta N\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{x\in G}f(x)^{s}\leq CN

for some
\delta\in(0,1]
and
C\geq 1
. Then

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\prod_{j=1}^{k}f(x_{j})\ \gg_{k,s,C,q}\ \delta^{\,k+\frac{s}{s-1}C_{q,k}}\;N^{k-1}.

Proof.

Let

A_{0}:=\{x\in G:\ f(x)\geq\delta/2\}.

By Hölder,

\delta N\leq\sum_{x\in A_{0}}f(x)+\sum_{x\notin A_{0}}f(x)\leq|A_{0}|^{1-1/s}\Big(\sum_{x\in G}f(x)^{s}\Big)^{1/s}+\frac{\delta}{2}N,

so
|A_{0}|\gg_{s,C}\delta^{s/(s-1)}N
. Writing
\rho:=|A_{0}|/N
, we have
\rho\gg_{s,C}\delta^{s/(s-1)}
.

Since
f\geq\delta/2
on
A_{0}
,

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\prod_{j=1}^{k}f(x_{j})\ \geq\ \Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)^{k}\#\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in A_{0}^{k}:\ a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0\}.

Apply Corollary
6.3
to
A_{0}
and use
\rho\gg_{s,C}\delta^{s/(s-1)}
to obtain the stated bound.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of the section.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
A\subseteq G
be
K_{s,t}
-free and assume that every solution to (
9
) in
A^{k}
is trivial.
Write

|A|=\delta N^{1-1/s}.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
be a parameter to be chosen at the end.
By Lemma
6.1
(with
\eta=o(1)
supplied by Lemma
2.2
) there exists

f:G\to[0,\infty)
such that

(i)

\sum_{x}f(x)=N^{1/s}|A|=\delta N
;

(ii)

\|\widehat{f}-N^{1/s}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}\|_{\infty}\ll\varepsilon N
;

(iii)

\sum_{x}f(x)^{s}\ll_{s,t}N
.

Recall the definition

T(h_{1},\dots,h_{k}):=\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0}\prod_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}(x_{j}),\qquad T(h):=T(h,\dots,h).

Applying Lemma
6.4
to
f
yields

(16)

T(f)\gg_{k,s,t,q}\delta^{\,k+\frac{s}{s-1}C_{q,k}}\,N^{k-1}.

Set
\nu:=f+N^{1/s}\mathbbm{1}_{A}\geq 0
and
g:=f-N^{1/s}\mathbbm{1}_{A}
. Then
\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\ll\varepsilon N
by (ii).
As in the integer case, using (iii) and Lemma
2.1
one has
\sum_{x}\nu(x)\ll N
and
E_{2}(\nu,\nu)\ll N^{3}
.
Lemma
4.3
therefore applies in
G
and, via the same telescoping identity as before, gives

(17)

|T(f)-T(N^{1/s}\mathbbm{1}_{A})|\ll_{k}\varepsilon\,N^{k-1}.

Since
A
has only trivial solutions to (
9
),

T(N^{1/s}\mathbbm{1}_{A})=N^{k/s}\cdot|A|=\delta\,N^{1+\frac{k-1}{s}}.

Combining with (
17
) gives

T(f)\ll N^{1+\frac{k-1}{s}}+\varepsilon N^{k-1}.

Comparing with (
16
) and dividing by
N^{k-1}
yields, for
N
large,

\delta^{\,k+\frac{s}{s-1}C_{q,k}}\ll_{k,s,t,q}\varepsilon.

Finally choose
\varepsilon:=(\log N)^{-c_{0}}
with
c_{0}>0
sufficiently small. Then

\delta\ll_{k,s,t,q}(\log N)^{-c},\qquad c:=\frac{c_{0}}{\,k+\frac{s}{s-1}C_{q,k}\,}>0,

and hence
|A|=\delta N^{1-1/s}\ll N^{1-1/s}(\log N)^{-c}
.
∎

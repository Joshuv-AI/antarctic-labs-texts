Comparing the Efficiency of Microneedling and Tranexamic Acid Versus Microneedling and Vitamin C in the Treatment of Infraorbital Hyperpigmentation
Authors: Nader Pazyar, Fatemeh Badakhsh, Reza Yaghoobi, Maryam Seyedtabib
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Infraorbital hyperpigmentation is a common condition, and due to its multifactorial nature, it is less responsive to standard treatments. Therefore, the aim of our research was to evaluate the efficiency of microneedling and tranexamic acid with microneedling and vitamin C in the treatment of dark circles around the eyes.Materials and Methods:The present research was a prospective, single-blind, randomized clinical trial. Thirty-seven female candidates who had been referred to the skin clinic of Imam Hospital of Ahvaz for treating dark circles around their eyes were included. People were divided into two groups on both sides under the eyes (right and left). For randomization, in treatment method A, microneedling and tranexamic acid were used for the right side of patient’s face, microneedling and vitamin C treatment for the left side, and the opposite was considered for group B. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 22 software.Results:Both methods presented dramatic advancement in all sessions compared to the baseline. There was no remarkable difference between the two groups regarding treatment methods (P = 0.444). The patients’ consent showed no considerable difference between the two sides. Unfavorable incidents were non-permanent, with no dramatic diversity between both sides except for burning that lasted longer with microneedling and vitamin C.Conclusion:Both tranexamic acid and vitamin C, in combination with microneedling, appear to be impressive modalities for the management of infraorbital hyperpigmentation with no remarkable side effects.

INTRODUCTION

Eyes are considered as the main organs on the face, and blemishes around the eyes have a major psychological effect on both genders.[1] Periorbital hyperpigmentation (POH) is introduced as analogous hyperchromia encircling the eyes.[12] Moreover, POH is characterized as “Periorbital melanosis” or “dark circle.”[2] POH is the result of a mixture of factors, like hereditary or constitutional hyperpigmentation, subcutaneous vascularity, periorbital edema, secondary hyperpigmentation from atopic or allergic contact dermatitis, shadow as a result of skin laxity or tear trough, and medications.[3] In practice, it is not easy to recognize the multiple reasons for which a multimodal treatment attitude is often required. POH may be an important cosmetic issue, which has received insufficient attention in the literature and requires further research.[4] The treatment of infraorbital hyperpigmentation is challenging due to its complex etiology and pathogenesis, and there is no simple and reliable therapeutic strategy.[25] Dark circles around the eyes can be a major cosmetic problem, but studies have not addressed this issue sufficiently, and the need for more research is clear.[67]

Microneedling is an innovative treatment method in dermatology.[8] Dermatologists use it as a relatively minimally invasive and inexpensive tool to treat numerous skin and cosmetic problems.[9] The effect of microneedling is based on physical damage to the skin.[10] It has been reported that the trauma caused by penetration of needle into the skin can regenerate dermis through the release of growth factor and the induction of collagen and elastinization in the dermis.[29] Furthermore, microneedling enhances the penetration of different topical drugs by the skin barrier.[10] Multiple studies have also evaluated the effect of topical tranexamic acid (TXA), which binds to plasminogen and prevents its conversion to plasmin, namely a melanogenic factor. TXA is also capable of inhibiting tyrosinase enzyme, and due to its antiplasmin effect (because plasmin is also an angiogenic factor), it reduces erythema and vascularity.[211121314]

Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is a dissolvable vitamin which its molecular mass is low. It is an important antioxidant that humans cannot synthesize and that should be obtained through the diet.[15] Vitamin C has several effects on the skin and in the treatment of melasma, such as reducing melanin synthesis (by competing with copper ions in the active site of tyrosinase enzyme) and also inhibiting melanin synthesis due to intracellular acidification of melanocytes. Receiving vitamin C in the skin through plasma and from vascular system of the dermis is dependent on sodium-dependent vitamin C (SVCT) receptors, which have two types, 1 and 2, both of which are present in epidermal cells. However, there is only type 2 receptor in the dermis, and the epidermis amount of vitamin C is higher in comparison to dermis. The epidermis amount of vitamin C is the lowest in its outer layer compared to its lower layers; it indicates that more vitamin C is required in the epidermis (due to greater disposal to sunlight and additional environmental pollutants) and due to its nature, it shows the loss of more vitamin C in the outer layers of epidermis. Vitamin C also promotes collagen synthesis, which reduces skin damage and induces keratinocyte differentiation. In recent studies, methods such as laser microdermabrasion, iontophoresis, and microneedling have been used to penetrate more hydrophilic vitamin C from the upper layers of the epidermis.[16171819] A popular notion in the community is that POH is a sign of sleeplessness or tiredness. However, such a perspective is debatable.[20] Ethnic and geographic factors may participate in the changeable presentation of POH subgroups. As stated, dark circles under the eyes (POH) are a common anxiety among young and adult patients with all skin types. However, skin microneedling operates as a skin delivery method, facilitating the penetration of lightening agents into the deeper layers of skin.[21]

According to the above content, no effective treatment has been proposed for treating dark circles around the eyes, and the existing treatments have different efficiencies.[220] Also, laser and carboxytherapy are highly expensive for patients, so the present investigation was carried out with the purpose of comparing the efficiency of microneedling with TXA compared to microneedling with vitamin C in the management of dark circles around the eyes of patients referred to Imam Khomeini Hospital of Ahvaz in 2021.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

A recent investigation is a single-blind and prospective randomized clinical trial, which started after the approval of the plan by the research vice-chancellor of the Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences (IRCT20200302046669N1). Satisfied patients who went to the skin clinic of Imam Hospital of Ahvaz for the treatment of dark circles around their eyes have participated in the research. All candidates were free to take part in the study, and at first, the purpose and method of conducting the research were explained to the participants. Then, eligible patients were assigned to the observation if they wished after presenting written consent and were randomly divided into two groups. Also, in this study, the provisions of the ethics statement in the Helsinki Declaration and the principles of confidentiality of patient information were observed.

People aged 18–50 years with dark circles around their eyes and consent to participate were included in the study. Pregnant women and lactating mothers, those with oral contraceptive pills usage during the last 1 year and within the observation, the presence of any background of coagulation diseases and thrombotic disorders, use of anticoagulants and anticonvulsants, and sensitivity to the studied drugs and topical lidocaine, the history of treating dark circles around the eyes during the last month, hereditary or acquired connective tissue diseases, presence of transplanted tissue, a history of keloid scars and isotretinoin consumption during the year before the treatment was among exclusion considered criteria. Through the observation, participants were not allowed to take any other remedy at the study site to reduce dark circles around their eyes.

The ethical approval code (IR.AJUMS.HGOLESTAN.REC.1400.045) was obtained from the Medical Research Ethics Committee of Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences (AJUMS) and the General Directorate of Medical Sciences of Iran.

Sample size

Due to the longitudinal nature of data and the discrete response (a longitudinal design with five repetitions and a proportional response), as well as using the information from previous studies, the minimum required sample size was 40 people in each method, taking into account the first type error of 0.05.[220] One person did not complete the study due to an unknown reason, and two people did not complete the follow-up because the long distance was omitted from the research. Finally, 37 people were included in the research, all of whom were women.

Implementation

Patient demographic information including age, sex, skin phenotype, and history of previous treatments was registered. The participants were distributed in two treatment categories: A and B. For randomization, in treatment method A, microneedling and TXA treatment were considered for the right dark cycles of patient’s eye, while microneedling and vitamin C treatment were meant for the left side. In the treatment for group B, on the left dark cycles of patient’s eye, microneedling and TXA were used, and on the right dark cycles of patient’s eye, microneedling and vitamin C were performed. Before starting the therapeutic process, the lesions of the participants were photographed, their under-eye area was washed with a mild skin cleanser, and their skin was masked with a thin layer of topical anesthesia (lidocaine 2.5% and prilocaine 2.5% cream) for 30 minutes. By means of a microneedling device (dermapen) and a sterilized 12-needle disposable cartridge with a depth of 0.5 to 1 mm, microneedling was carried out in vertical, horizontal, and diagonal directions with the aim of achieving spot bleeding in the skin of under-eye area in the range of half a centimeter below the edge of eyelid to the top of the junction of the eyelid and the cheek. Immediately after finishing microneedling, 2 ml of TXA (500 mg/5 ml) vial (Caspian Tamin, Rasht, Iran) was applied on one side, and 2 ml of vitamin C (500 mg/5 ml) vial (Alborz Darou, Iran) was placed on the opposite side of a sterile gauze on the skin for 15 minutes. After that, the therapeutic area was masked with zinc oxide ointment. Patients were then trained about post-therapy care and sun protection. This method was performed three times every two weeks (weeks 0, 2, and 4). In each session and before the start of treatment, the portrait of the patient was recorded. Two board-certified dermatologists investigated the rate of advancement for infraorbital hyperpigmentation and the darkness of the under-eye area at the beginning of treatment at weeks 2, 4, 6, and 12. Both assessors were unaware of the type of remedy performed on each side of the face. Progress was evaluated based on the general satisfaction of the physician (physician global assessment (PGA)).

Outcome evaluation

Patients were evaluated at day 0 (first day of remedy), week 2 (second therapy), week 4 (third therapy), week 6, and week 12. At each visit, portraits were taken from frontal at the same distance and room light with a Canon Digital Camera (Power Shot S110 with 12.1 megapixels high-sensitivity CMOS sensor; Canon, Inc., Japan). Candidates were monitored 7 days after each course of therapy for investigations of any probable side effects. Following each course of therapy, patients received a questionnaire and were asked to document the duration of complications such as erythema, swelling, burning, and itching. Two board-certified dermatologists were requested to investigate the rate of advancement for infraorbital hyperpigmentation at weeks 2, 4, 6, and 12. Both assessors were unaware of the type of therapy carried out on each side of the face. Degree of clinical advancement was introduced as the rate of improvement: poor response (less than 25%), fair response (25–50%), good response (50–75%), and excellent response (more than 75%). At the 3-month follow-up, participants were asked to rate their overall level of consent with the treatment of the TXA group and vitamin C group, respectively, according to the following scale: dissatisfaction, relative satisfaction, average satisfaction, and full satisfaction.

Data analysis

Mean ± standard deviation (SD) and definite frequency and percentage were used to report data for quantitative and categorical variables, respectively. Data was statistically analyzed by SPSS (version 23.0). P value ≤ 0.05 was statistically adjusted as significant.

DISCUSSION

Peri-ocular hyperpigmentation has various causes, including genetics, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation caused by atopic dermatitis, peri-ocular edema, excessive blood vessels, and aging.[221] It has also been mentioned that skin melanization or hemodynamic congestion after inflammation with a prominent role of melanin and hemoglobin is of high importance in this context.[22] However, histological assessments have shown that there is a weak relationship between the reduction of melanosis in the skin and the improvement of infraorbital hyperpigmentation.[21] Therefore, it can be claimed that the improvement mechanism of infraorbital hyperpigmentation is not fully understood, and further histological studies with clinical trials are needed.[22] There are various topical medications for this problem, like hydroquinone, kojic acid, azelaic acid, and retinoic acid, and chemical peels and laser therapy are the most common treatments.[2021] The present study describes one of the randomized controlled trials in a primary care setting that compared two common interventions, namely microneedling in combination therapy with vitamin C and TXA, for the remedy of dark circles around the eyes. The findings of the study showed improvement in both groups. Our analysis showed that microneedling with TXA was a bit superior to microneedling with vitamin C, but no remarkable diversity was shown between them. A notable correlation was declared between the age of patients and the presence of darkness around eyes in such a way that the darkness around eyes increased with age. However, a significant relationship was observed between patients’ skin type, family history, and exposure to the sun with dark circles around their eyes. In 2009, Ohshima et al.[21] studied topical vitamin C in the form of 10% sodium ascorbate or vitamin C glucoside lotion in a split-face fashion (opposite side: vehicle only) for 6 months. They measured the melanin index, erythema index, thickness, and echogenicity of the eyelid dermis bilaterally, and found that the pigmentation became lighter due to the increased thickness of skin, masking the dark discoloration caused by congestive blood. However, they did not find any significant difference in the melanin index, and their results were consistent with the present study from the standpoint of the effectiveness of vitamin C in improving dark circles around the eyes; however, the assessment and research methods were different in the two studies. In 2020, Ghandehari et al.[2] showed that both laser and microneedling together with TXA, compared to the initial state, exhibited substantial advancement in all courses. There was no dramatic difference between the two methods on days 30, 90, and 150. However, the laser method presented a remarkable advancement on the 60th day. Patient consent showed no remarkable distinct on both sides of the eyes. Side effects were impermanent, and there was not any notable distinct between the two sides, except for erythema, which lasted longer with microneedling. In this study, on the side of microneedling and tranexamic in the last follow-up, which was on day 150, an average improvement of 56% was observed that was higher compared to our study on the side of microneedling and tranexamic on day 120, namely 51.35% in the last follow-up. The findings of Kontochristopoulos et al.’s[22] study in 2015 with the combined method of microneedling and peeling with 10% trichloroacetic acid for the treatment of dark circles showed that at the end of the study, all patients had significant improvement and finally showed that microneedling with trichloroacetic acid was an effective treatment without complications for the treatment of dark circles around the eyes; such findings were in line with the findings of the present research. Ahmed et al.[23] showed in their study in 2018 that all three treatment methods (group A, carboxytherapy; group B, chemical peeling with TCA; and group C, mesotherapy with vitamin C) were effective in reducing the darkness around the eyes and that no dramatic distinct was reported for the results of these groups. However, vitamin C mesotherapy exhibited more burning sensation after treatment compared to the other two groups; however, compared to the other two groups, they observed a remarkable advancement in patient satisfaction as well as pigmentation. Although in this study vitamin C was used to treat dark circles around the eyes in the form of mesotherapy and had a smaller statistical population relative to our study, the results of this study are consistent with ours regarding the effect of vitamin C in reducing the infraorbital hyperpigmentation. In a 2019 study by Ismail ES et al.,[24] which surveyed the effects of microneedling with topical vitamin C for melasma on 71 melasma females, it was indicated that 20% vitamin C in combination with microneedling was an effective therapy with no side effects for skin hyperpgmentation. This was similar to what we found in this study.

Limitation

low sample size was the most restricted aspect of the current research, and its results cannot be generalized. Also, the findings of the present study showed that after 12 weeks, the improvement rate in both groups was higher than at the beginning of the study. Nevertheless, to accept the findings, conducting a study with extended follow-up duration is necessary, which can also include different findings. Moreover, the examination of a larger number of risk factors and correlation between variables can change the results of such studies.

CONCLUSION

This study presents a new therapeutic approach for the management of dark circles around the eyes. However, microneedling with TXA and vitamin C is an innovative combination therapy with encouraging results and minor side effects. Based on the results obtained from the present study and by reviewing and comparing the results reported in the field of research, the researcher found that the microneedling treatment method with TXA and vitamin C is an effective and low-cost method. However, to generalize the results, there is a need to conduct clinical trial studies with a higher sample size. So, additional evaluations with extended sample size and histopathological investigations of pigmentary changes pre- and post-remedy could be more helpful to clarify the optimal modality for the management of eye dark circles in combination or alone. However, it was not shown any notable difference between the findings and therapeutic methods for patient satisfaction. Also, to evaluate the therapeutic findings more accurately, conducting research with an extended duration is proposed. Finally, considering that POH is a multifactorial circumstance, precise diagnosis is essential to gain a good, secure, and efficient remedy outcome. This allows adjustment of the management based on the underlying pathological aspects and the necessities of patients.

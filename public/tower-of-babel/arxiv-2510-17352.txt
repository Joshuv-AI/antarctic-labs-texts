Some Identities For Periods of Hulek-Verrill Threefolds
Xenia de la Ossa, Mohamed Elmi

Abstract




We study the Hulek-Verrill families of Calabi-Yau threefolds. They are birationally equivalent to fibred products of elliptic surfaces, so we expect to be able to compute periods on these threefolds by integrating products of elliptic periods over a contour on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We numerically verify this in several examples. This article was submitted to MATRIX Annals (2024) for inclusion in the proceedings of the conference “The Geometry of Moduli Spaces in String Theory”, held 2–13 September 2024.

\mathcal{E}_{1,1,\frac{1}{\varphi}}

The Hulek-Verril elliptic surface
\mathcal{E}_{a,b,c}
is the hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, defined by

P(X,\lambda)=(X^{1}+X^{2}+X^{3})(aX^{2}X^{3}+bX^{1}X^{3}+cX^{1}X^{2})\lambda^{0}-\lambda^{1}X^{1}X^{2}X^{3}=0

(1)

where
[X^{1},X^{2},X^{3}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
[\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1}]=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
are parameters.
[
1
]

We are interested in the elliptic surface with parameters
\mathcal{E}_{1,1,\frac{1}{\varphi}}
. We choose the affine coordinate
\lambda=\frac{\lambda^{0}}{\lambda^{1}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and work on the coordinate patch
X^{3}=1
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. With these choices, the holomorphic
1
-form on the elliptic fibre at
\lambda
is given by

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}\frac{dX^{1}\wedge dX^{2}}{P(X,\lambda)}

(2)

where
C
is a “small contour” that encircles the
P(X,\lambda)=0
locus.

The holomorphic period at
\lambda=0
is given by

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|X^{1}|=\epsilon}\oint_{|X^{2}|=\delta}\frac{dX^{1}\wedge dX^{2}}{P(X,\lambda)}=(2\pi i)f_{0}(\lambda,\varphi)

(3)

for sufficiently small
\epsilon
and
\delta
. The function
f_{0}(\lambda,\varphi)
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{0}(\lambda,\varphi)&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{2k}{k}\varphi^{-n+k}\right)\lambda^{n}\\
\end{split}

(4)

and the coefficient of
\lambda^{n}
is given by the constant term of

\left[(X^{1}+X^{2}+1)\left(\frac{1}{X^{1}}+\frac{1}{X^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\right]^{n}.

(5)

Both periods of the elliptic curve at
\lambda
are annihilated by the Picard-Fuchs differential operator

\mathcal{L}=R_{2}(\lambda,\varphi)(\theta+1)^{2}+R_{1}(\lambda,\varphi)(\theta+1)+R_{0}(\lambda,\varphi)

(6)

where
\theta=\lambda\frac{d}{d\lambda}
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{2}(\lambda,\varphi)&=\left(3+\lambda\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}-4\right)\right)\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\varphi}\right)\left(1-2\lambda\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}+4\right)+\lambda^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}-4\right)^{2}\right)\\
R_{1}(\lambda,\varphi)&=-6+6\lambda\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}+6\right)+2\lambda^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}-4\right)\left(\frac{3}{\varphi}+8\right)-2\lambda^{3}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}-4\right)\left(8-\frac{6}{\varphi}+\frac{3}{\varphi^{2}}\right)\\
R_{0}(\lambda,\varphi)&=3-\lambda\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}+14\right)-\lambda^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}+2\right)\left(\frac{3}{\varphi}-10\right)+\lambda^{3}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}-4\right)\left(4-\frac{2}{\varphi}+\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}\right)\end{split}

(7)

This differential operator
\mathcal{L}
is implicitly given by the recursion relations of Verril in
[
2
]
and it has the Riemann symbol

\mathcal{P}~\left\{~~\begin{array}[]{c c c c c}~\frac{3\varphi}{4\varphi-1}&~0&~\varphi&~\frac{\varphi}{(1\pm 2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}&~\infty\\[8.0pt]
\hline\cr\\[-5.0pt]
0&0&0&0&1\\
2&0&0&0&1\\
\end{array}~~;~\lambda~\right\}~.

(8)

For generic values of
\varphi

0&0&0&1\\

2
Hulek-Verril family of threefolds as a fibred product of elliptic surfaces

AESZ 34 describes the variation of the Hodge structure of (a quotient of) the one-parameter fibred products of elliptic surfaces

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{1}(X,\lambda)&=(X^{1}+X^{2}+X^{3})(X^{2}X^{3}+X^{1}X^{3}+X^{1}X^{2})\lambda^{0}-\lambda^{1}X^{1}X^{2}X^{3}=0\\
P_{2}(Y,\lambda)&=(Y^{1}+Y^{2}+Y^{3})\left(Y^{2}Y^{3}+Y^{1}Y^{3}+\frac{1}{\varphi}Y^{1}Y^{2}\right)\lambda^{0}-\lambda^{1}Y^{1}Y^{2}Y^{3}=0\end{split}

(11)

where
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}
is a parameter. These equations describe the fibred product of Hulek-Verril elliptic surfaces
\mathcal{E}_{1,1,1}\times_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}_{1,1,\frac{1}{\varphi}}
. Equivalently, these equations describe a one-parameter family of complete intersections with configuration matrix

\begin{matrix}\mathbb{P}^{2}\\
\mathbb{P}^{2}\\
\mathbb{P}^{1}\end{matrix}\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\
0&3\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\,.

(12)

On the
X^{3}=Y^{3}=\lambda^{1}=1
coordinate patch, the holomorphic
3
-form takes the form

\Omega=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{C}\frac{dX_{1}\wedge dX_{2}\wedge dY_{1}\wedge dY_{2}\wedge d\lambda_{0}}{P_{1}(X,\lambda)P_{2}(Y,\lambda)}\,,

(13)

where
C
is a “small contour” that encircles the
P_{1}(X,\lambda)=P_{2}(Y,\lambda)=0
locus.

Let
\lambda=\frac{\lambda^{0}}{\lambda^{1}}
and
G_{i}\in H_{1}(E_{1},\mathbb{Z})\otimes H_{1}(E_{2},\mathbb{Z})
where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are the elliptic fibres defined by
P_{1}(X,\lambda)=0
and
P_{2}(Y,\lambda)=0
respectively. For suitably chosen
G_{i}
and contours
\ell_{i}
in the complex
\lambda
plane, we expect an identify

\Gamma^{T}\Sigma_{4}\Pi(\varphi)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}G_{i}^{T}\Sigma_{2,2}\int_{\ell_{i}}\omega(\lambda,1)\otimes\omega(\lambda,\varphi)~d\lambda\,,

(14)

for some
\Gamma\in\mathbb{Q}^{4}
and
\Pi
that is, up a gauge transformation, a solution of AESZ 34 with integral symplectic monodromy
[
3
]
. We have identified
G_{i}
with a vector of periods in
\mathbb{Z}^{4}
and defined

\Sigma_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{\frac{n}{2}}\\
-I_{\frac{n}{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}

(15)

and

\Sigma_{m,n}=\Sigma_{m}\otimes\Sigma_{n}.

(16)

A basis of solutions of AESZ 34 with integral symplectic monodromy is given by

\Pi(\varphi)=(2\pi i)^{3}\begin{pmatrix}~\frac{\zeta(3)}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\,\chi~&\frac{c_{2}\cdot H}{24}&~0~&\frac{H^{3}}{3!}\\[3.0pt]
\frac{c_{2}\cdot H}{24}&\frac{\sigma}{2}&-\frac{H^{3}}{2!}&0\\[3.0pt]
1&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&(2\pi i)^{-1}&0&0\\
0&0&(2\pi i)^{-2}&0\\
0&0&0&(2\pi i)^{-3}\end{pmatrix}\varpi(\varphi)\,,

(17)

where
(H^{3},c_{2}\cdot H,\chi)=(12,12,-8)
,
\sigma=H^{3}~\text{mod}~2=0
and
\varpi=\left(\varpi_{0},\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2},\varpi_{3}\right)
. The components of
\varpi
are

\varpi_{j}(\varphi)=\varphi\sum_{k}^{3}\binom{j}{k}F_{k}(\varphi)\log^{j-k}(\varphi)\,,

(18)

where
F_{j}(\varphi)
are power series that are computed from the Picard-Fuchs equation. They are normalised so that
F_{j}(0)=\delta_{j,0}
.

We should stress that, when we work with the choice of integral symplectic basis and topological data in (
17
), we are implicitly working on a free quotient of (
11
). As a result of this quotient, we will find that
\Gamma\in\mathbb{Q}^{4}
in identities of the form (
14
).

2.1
Normalisation of
\Pi
?

Note that the periods in (
18
) have been normalised in such a way that

\varpi_{0}(\varphi)=\varphi+\mathcal{O}(\varphi^{2}).

(19)

In other words, the Picard-Fuchs equation has indices
(1,1,1,1)
at the large complex structure point
\varphi=0
. This normalisation has been included with the benefit of hindsight.

Recall that the holomorphic
3
-form at a given value of
\varphi
is only defined up to multiplication by a non-zero constant. We are usually free to multiply the periods
\Pi
by any non-vanishing holomorphic function of
\varphi
without changing anything of consequence. However, a normalisation is implicitly determined by Equation (
14
), which we can find by analytically computing a single identity of the form (
14
) as a function of
\varphi
. Alternatively, we may fix a number of values of
\varphi
, numerically compute a number of identities of the form (
14
) and then guess what the normalisation of
\Pi
must be. This is the quick and dirty approach that has been taken here.

3
Identities

We identify solutions of AESZ34 with identities of the form (
14
). We compute both
\Pi(\varphi)
and the right hand side of (
14
) and then identify the appropriate
\Gamma\in\mathbb{Q}^{4}
by using the LLL-algorithm to look for a relation between the numerical values on the left and right hand sides of the identity. All identities in this section are found numerically at the fixed value of
\varphi=\frac{1}{64}
. However, we expect that they continue to hold as
\varphi
is varied.

3.1
Monodromy of elliptic periods

We expect identities of the form (
14
) when we can argue that the
3
-chain on the right-hand side is really a
3
-cycle. In order to argue that this is the case, we need the monodromy matrices of
\omega(\lambda,1)\otimes\omega(\lambda,\varphi)
around the various singularities in the complex
\lambda
plane.

We use (
10
) to fix a basis of elliptic periods at
\lambda=0
and
\varphi=\frac{1}{64}
. We then analytically continue to the various singularities along the upper half
\lambda
-plane and compute anti-clockwise monodromy matrices around the various singularities. Around a singularity
\lambda_{*}
, the elliptic periods transform as

\omega(\lambda,1)\otimes\omega(\lambda,\varphi)\rightarrow\mu_{\lambda_{*}}~\omega(\lambda,1)\otimes\omega(\lambda,\varphi)\,.

(20)

We find that

\displaystyle\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
3&1\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
3&1\end{pmatrix}

(21)

\displaystyle\mu_{\frac{\varphi}{(1+2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=I_{2}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

(22)

\displaystyle\mu_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=I_{2}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}5&-4\\
4&-3\end{pmatrix}

(23)

\displaystyle\mu_{\frac{\varphi}{(1-2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=I_{2}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}5&-2\\
8&-3\end{pmatrix}

(24)

\displaystyle\mu_{\frac{1}{9}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{2}

(25)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}7&-6\\
6&-5\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{2}~.

(26)

3.2
Vanishing cycle at
\varphi=\frac{1}{9}

We expect that the (non-trivial) invariant
1
-cycle of a
2\times 2
monodromy matrix is the vanishing cycle at the associated singularity. As with Hadamard products, we can use this to identify
3
-cycles in the fibred product of elliptic surfaces.
[
4
]
For example

\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\
0&~1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},

(27)

which tells us that
(1,0)^{T}
is a vanishing cycle of the first elliptic curve at
\lambda=\frac{1}{9}
.
Similarly, the second elliptic curve has vanishing cycle
(1,1)^{T}
at
\lambda=\varphi
. This leads us to the identity

\frac{1}{2}(1,0,-5,1)\Sigma_{4}\Pi(\varphi)=(1,0)\otimes(1,1)\Sigma_{2,2}\int_{\varphi}^{\frac{1}{9}}\omega(\lambda,1)\otimes\omega(\lambda,\varphi)d\lambda\,.

(28)

As
\varphi\rightarrow\frac{1}{9}
, the integration contour in the above identity vanishes, so we expect that
(1,0,-5,1)^{T}
is a vanishing cycle at
\varphi=\frac{1}{9}
. This is indeed the case. However, we must be careful. The precise vanishing cycle we compute depends on the branch that we approach the singularity on. Different choices of branches simply multiply the vanishing cycle by a monodromy matrix. In this case, the vanishing cycle computed in
[
5
]
is
\left(1,0,5,1\right)^{T}
, which is simply

\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
5\\
1\end{pmatrix}=M_{\frac{1}{25}}\begin{pmatrix}\,1\\
\,0\\
-5\\
\,1\end{pmatrix}\,,

(29)

where
M_{\frac{1}{25}}
is the monodromy matrix around
\varphi=\frac{1}{25}
that was also computed in
[
5
]
.

3.3
Vanishing cycle at
\varphi=\frac{1}{25}

Similarly, we find that

\frac{1}{2}(0,0,-5,0)\Sigma_{4}\Pi(\varphi)=(1,0)\otimes(1,2)\Sigma_{2,2}\int_{\frac{\varphi}{(1-2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}}^{\frac{1}{9}}\omega(\lambda,1)\otimes\omega(\lambda,\varphi)d\lambda.

(30)

Again, the contour of integration vanishes as
\varphi\rightarrow\frac{1}{25}
, so we would expect that
(0,0,-5,0)^{T}
is a vanishing cycle at
\frac{1}{25}
. This is indeed the case. It is just a multiple of the vanishing cycle computed in
[
5
]
.

3.4
Holomorphic period at
\varphi=0
and
T^{3}

As was the case with Hadamard products, we can identify non-trivial
3
-cycles by taking the union of some monodromy invariant
2
-cycle in the fibre over a contour on the base.
[
4
]
In this way, we can identify a
3
-torus (
T^{3}
) that can be integrated over to obtain the holomorphic period at
\varphi=0
. More precisely, we find that

(-1,0,0,0)\Sigma_{4}\Pi(\varphi)=(0,1)\otimes(0,1)\Sigma_{2,2}\oint_{\ell_{0}\,\ell_{\frac{\varphi}{(1+2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}}\,\ell_{\varphi}\,\ell_{\frac{\varphi}{(1-2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}}}\omega(\lambda,1)\otimes\omega(\lambda,\varphi)d\lambda\,,

(31)

where the integration contour

\ell_{0}\,\ell_{\frac{\varphi}{(1+2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}}\,\ell_{\varphi}\,\ell_{\frac{\varphi}{(1-2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}}

is a closed contour on the
\lambda
plane that first encircles
\lambda=0
in an anti-clockwise fashion, then encircles
\lambda=\frac{\varphi}{(1+2\sqrt{\varphi})^{2}}

in an anti-clockwise fashion, then encircles… . We see from the above monodromy matrices that
(0,1)\otimes(0,1)
is invariant under monodromy around this contour. We have therefore identified a
T^{3}
we can integrate over and obtain the holomorphic period at the point of maximal unipotent monodromy
\varphi=0
. The results of
[
4
]
suggest that this family of
T^{3}
s defines an SYZ-like fibration by constant phase contours on the
\lambda
-plane.

Graph parameters that are coarsely equivalent to tree-length Thanks: August 24, 2026
Feodor F. Dragan

Abstract
Two graph parameters are said to be coarsely equivalent if they are within constant factors from each other for every graph G G . Recently, several graph parameters were shown to be coarsely equivalent to tree-length. Recall that the length of a tree-decomposition 𝒯 ⁡ ( G ) {\cal T}(G) of a graph G G is the largest diameter of a bag in 𝒯 ⁡ ( G ) {\cal T}(G) , and the tree-length of G G is the minimum of the length, over all tree-decompositions of G G . We present simpler and sometimes with better bounds proofs for those known in literature results and further extend this list of graph parameters coarsely equivalent to tree-length. Among other new results, we show that the tree-length of a graph G G is small if and only if for every bramble ℱ {\cal F} (or every Helly family of connected subgraphs ℱ {\cal F} , or every Helly family of paths ℱ {\cal F} ) of G G , there is a disk in G G with small radius that intercepts all members of ℱ {\cal F} . Furthermore, the tree-length of a graph G G is small if and only if G G can be embedded with a small additive distortion to an unweighted tree with the same vertex set as in G G (not involving any Steiner points). Additionally, we introduce a new natural ”bridging“ property for cycles, which generalizes a known property of cycles in chordal graphs, and show that it also coarsely defines the tree-length. Keywords: Tree-decomposition; Tree-length; Quasi-isometry; Fat minor; Bramble; Helly family; Cycle property. Mathematical Subject Classification: 05C10; 05C62

Introduction

We say that two graph parameters
{\sf p}
and
{\sf q}
are
coarsely equivalent
if
there are two universal constants
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
such that
\alpha\cdot{\sf q}(G)\leq{\sf p}(G)\leq\beta\cdot{\sf q}(G)
for every graph
G
. So, if one parameter is bounded by a constant, then the other is bounded by a constant, too.
Coarse equivalency of two graph parameters is useful in at least two scenarios. First, if one parameter is easier to compute, then it provides an easily computable constant-factor approximate for possibly hard to compute other parameter. This is the case for parameters the
cluster-diameter of a layering partition
and the
tree-length
of a graph (that are subjects of this paper; formal definitions of these and other parameters can be found in Section
2
). It is known
[
1
,
26
,
27
,
32
]
that those two parameters are within small constant factors from each other. The cluster-diameter of a layering partition can easily be computed, and a layering partition serves as a crucial tool in designing an efficient 3-approximation algorithm for computing the tree-length of a graph (and its tree-decomposition with minimum length), which is NP-hard to compute exactly (see Section
2.1
and Section
2.2
for details).
Secondly, since a constant bound on one parameter implies a constant bound on the other, one can choose out of two a most suitable (a right one) parameter when designing FPT (approximation) algorithms for some particular optimization problems on bounded parameter graphs. For example, an FPT algorithm for the so-called
metric dimension problem
on bounded tree-length graphs developed in
[
7
]
or sparse spanner results obtained for bounded tree-length graphs in
[
26
]
are useful and hold also for graphs with bounded cluster-diameter of a layering partition. Approximation algorithms (whose approximation error bounds depend on the cluster-diameter of a layering partition) for the connected
r
-domination problem and the connected

-center problem developed in
[
53
]
are useful also for graphs with bounded tree-length.

Layering partition and its cluster-diameter were used also in obtaining a 6-approximation algorithm for the problem of optimal non-contractive embedding of an unweighted graph metric into a weighted tree metric. This was possible because the cluster-diameter of a layering partition and the minimum distortion of an embedding of a graph into a tree (the so-called

[

,
21
,
29
,
32
]

tree-length of a graph.

Basic notions and notations.
All graphs
G
in this paper are connected, unweighted, undirected, loopless and without multiple edges. We assume also, for simplicity, that

is finite although most of our non-algorithmic results and their proofs work for infinite graphs, too.
For a (finite) graph
G=(V,E)
, we use

|V|
interchangeably to denote the number of vertices in

. Also, we use

and
|E|
to denote the number of edges. When we talk about two or more graphs, we may use

and

to indicate that these are the vertex and edge sets of a graph

The
length of a path

P(v,u):=(v=v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell-1},v_{\ell}=u)
from a vertex

to a vertex
u
is
\ell
, i.e., the number of edges in the path. The
distance

between vertices

and

is the length of the shortest path connecting

and
v
in
G
.
The distance between a vertex

and a subset

is defined as
d_{G}(v,S):=\min\{d_{G}(v,u):u\in S\}
. Similarly, let
d_{G}(S_{1}.S_{2}):=\min\{d_{G}(x,y):x\in S_{1},y\in S_{2}\}
for any two sets
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq V
. The
k^{th}

power

G^{k}
of a graph

is a graph that has the same set of vertices, but in which two distinct vertices are adjacent if and only if their distance in
G
is at most
k
.
The
disk

D_{r}(s,G)
of a graph

centered at vertex
s\in V
and with radius

is the set of all vertices with distance no more than
r
from

(i.e.,
D_{r}(s,G)=\{v\in V:d_{G}(v,s)\leq r\}
). We omit the graph name
G
and write
D_{r}(s)
if the context is about only one graph. A cycle

of a graph

is called

if
d_{C}(x,y)=d_{G}(x,y)
for every

in

.

The

of a subset

of vertices of a graph

is the largest distance in

, i.e.,
\max_{u,v\in S}d_{G}(u,v)
. The

of

is the largest distance in

between a pair of vertices of
S
, i.e.,
\max_{u,v\in S}d_{G[S]}(u,v)
. The

of a subset

of vertices of a graph

is the minimum

such that a vertex

exists with

. The

of a subset

is the minimum

such that a vertex
v\in S

.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and

be a subset of vertices of

. A disk
D_{r}(v)
(a clique

) of

is called a

(

)
separator of

with respect to

(of

, respectively) has at most

Preliminaries on graph parameters

Layering partition, its cluster-diameter, cluster-radius, and a canonical tree

Layering partition is a graph decomposition procedure introduced in

,

,

(a)

A

of a graph
G=(V,E)
with respect to a start vertex

is the decomposition of

into
q+1
layers (spheres)
L^{i}=\{u\in V:d_{G}(s,u)=i\},i=0,1,\dots,q
(here,

). A

\mathcal{LP}(G,s)=\{L^{i}_{1},\ldots,L^{i}_{p_{i}}:i=0,1,\dots,q\}
of

is a partition of each layer

into clusters
L^{i}_{1},\dots,L^{i}_{p_{i}}
such that two vertices

belong to the same cluster

if and only if they can be connected by a path outside the disk

of radius

. Here

is the number of clusters in layer

. See Fig.
1
for an illustration. A layering partition of a graph can be constructed in

time (see
[
18
]

A
layering tree

\Gamma(G,s)
of a graph
G
with respect to a layering partition
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
is the graph whose nodes are the clusters of
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
and where two nodes
C=L_{j}^{i}
and

are adjacent in

if and only if there exist a vertex

. It was shown in
[
11
]
that the graph

is always a tree and, given a start vertex

time
[
18
]
. Note that, for a fixed start vertex

, the layering partition
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
of

The

\Delta_{s}(G)

\mathcal{LP}(G,s)

\mathcal{LP}(G,s)

\Delta_{s}(G)=\max_{C\in\mathcal{LP}(G,s)}\max_{u,v\in C}d_{G}(u,v)

(
\widehat{\Delta}(G)

\Delta(G)=\min_{s\in V}\Delta_{s}(G)

The

Finding the cluster-diameter
\Delta_{s}(G)

\rho_{s}(G)\leq\Delta_{s}(G)\leq 2\rho_{s}(G).

It is not hard to show that, for any graph

Proposition 1

Let

The following result

2.2
Tree-length and tree-breadth

It is known that the class of chordal graphs can be characterized in terms of existence of so-called
clique-trees
.
Let
{\cal C}(G)
denote the family of maximal (by inclusion) cliques of a graph
G
.
A
clique-tree

{\cal CT}(G)
of
G
has the maximal cliques of
G
as its
nodes, and for every vertex
v
of
G
, the maximal cliques containing
v
form a subtree of
{\cal CT}(G)
.

Proposition 3
(
[
15
,
43
,
60
]
)

A graph is chordal if and only if it has a clique-tree.

In their work on graph minors
[
57
]
, Robertson and Seymour introduced the notion of tree-decomposition which generalizes the notion of clique-tree.
A
tree-decomposition
of a graph
G
is a tree
{\cal T}(G)
whose nodes, called
bags
, are subsets of
V(G)
such that:

(1)

\bigcup_{B\in V({\cal T}(G))}B=V(G)
,

(2)

for each edge
vw\in E(G)
, there is a bag
B\in V({\cal T}(G))

such that
v,w\in B
, and

(3)

for each
v\in V(G)
the set of bags
\{B:B\in V({\cal T}(G)),v\in B\}
forms a subtree
{\cal T}_{v}(G)
of
{\cal T}(G)
.

Tree-decompositions were used in defining several graph parameters.
The
tree-width
of a graph
G
is defined as minimum of
\max_{B\in V({\cal T}(G))}|B|-1
over all tree-decompositions
{\cal T}(G)
of
G
and is denoted by
{\sf tw}(G)

[
57
]
. The
length
of a tree-decomposition
{\cal T}(G)
of a graph
G
is
\max_{B\in V({\cal T}(G))}\max_{u,v\in B}d_{G}(u,v)
, and the
tree-length
of
G
, denoted by
{\sf tl}(G)
, is the minimum of the length, over all tree-decompositions of
G

[
27
]
. These two graph parameters generally are not related to each other. For instance, cliques (or, generally, all chordal graphs) have unbounded tree-width and tree-length 1, whereas cycles have tree-width 2 and unbounded tree-length. However, in some special cases they are coarsely equivalent. It is known that the tree-length
{\sf tl}(G)
of any graph
G
is at most
\lfloor\ell(G)/2\rfloor
times its tree-width
{\sf tw}(G)
(where
\ell(G)
is the size of a largest geodesic cycle in
G
)
[
24
,
25
]
and that for any graph
G
that excludes an apex graph
H
as a minor,
{\sf tw}(G)\leq c_{H}\cdot{\sf tl}(G)
for some constant
c_{H}
only depending on

[
24
]
.
The tree-length of any graph

can equivalently be defined in two other ways. As it is shown in
[
59
]
, tree-length
equals branch-length (see
[
59
]
for more details). Tree-length equals also short fill-in to a chordal graph, that is, the smallest number

that permits an edge set

between vertices of

such
that

is a chordal graph and for all

holds (see
[
28
]
and
[
40
]
).
The

of a tree-decomposition

of a graph

is the minimum integer

such that for every
B\in V({\cal T}(G))
there is a vertex
v_{B}\in V(G)
with

(i.e., each bag

has radius at most

does not need to belong to

of

, denoted by

, is the minimum of the breadth, over all tree-decompositions of

[
32
]
.
Note also that in
[
34

independently a notion similar to tree-length (and tree-breadth) was introduced for purposes of compact and efficient routing in certain graph classes. It was called
(R,D)
-acyclic clustering. An
(R,2R)
-acyclic clustering is exactly a tree-decomposition with breadth

and a
(D,D)
-acyclic clustering is exactly a tree-decomposition with length

Evidently, for any graph
G

1\leq{\sf tb}(G)\leq{\sf tl}(G)\leq 2{\sf tb}(G)
holds. Hence, if one parameter is bounded by a constant for a graph

then the other parameter is bounded for

(equivalently,

.
It is known that checking whether a graph

{\sf tl}(G)\leq\lambda

[
54
]
and each

[

]
. Furthermore, unless

, there is no polynomial time algorithm to calculate a tree-decomposition, for a given graph

\frac{3}{2}{\sf tl}(G)

[

; and for any

[

Notice that in the definition of the length of a tree-decomposition, the distance between vertices of a bag is measured in the entire graph

{\sf bnc}(G)={\sf BNC}(G)

Lemma 1

For every graph

{\sf BNC}(G)\leq\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)}{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}{\sf tl}(G)

Proof

Let
x,y
be arbitrary vertices of
G
. Consider an arbitrary shortest path

connecting
x
and
y
and an arbitrary vertex

of

.
Let also

be an arbitrary path between
x
and
y
and
\ell\geq 0
be the maximum integer such that

does not intersect

.

Consider two vertices

and

in
P(x,y)
such that
d_{G}(c,a)=d_{G}(c,b)=\ell
,
a
is between
c
and
x
and
b
is between
c
and
y
. Let also
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
be vertices in
P(x,y)
such that
d_{G}(c,a^{\prime})=d_{G}(c,b^{\prime})=\ell+1
,

is adjacent to

and

is adjacent to
b^{\prime}
(see Fig.

(a) for an illustration).
Since

intersects

but

does not, vertices

are in the same cluster of layering partition
\mathcal{LP}(G,c)
of

starting at vertex

(they are connected outside the disk

by

between

and

and between

and

. On the other hand,

d_{G}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1+d_{G}(a,b)+1=2\ell+2
, i.e.,
d_{G}(c,Q)=\ell+1=\frac{d_{G}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}{2}\leq\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)}{2}

Thus,

with Lemma

Corollary 1

For every graph
G
and every vertex

of
G
,
{\sf bnc}(G)={\sf BNC}(G)\leq\frac{3}{2}\Delta_{s}(G)
. In particular,
{\sf bnc}(G)={\sf BNC}(G)\leq\frac{3}{2}\Delta(G)
for every graph

Proof

By Proposition
1
and Lemma

, it remains only to show
{\sf bnc}(G)={\sf BNC}(G)
. Clearly, by definitions,
{\sf bnc}(G)\leq{\sf BNC}(G)
. To show the equality, let

. Then, for some vertices
x,y
in
G
, some shortest path
P(x,y)
connecting
x
and
y
, and some vertex

, there must exist a path
Q
in
G[V\setminus D_{r-1}(c)]
connecting
x
and
y
. Necessarily,
d_{G}(x,c),d_{G}(y,c)\geq r
. Consider a vertex
x^{\prime}
on a subpath of

between

and

and a vertex

on a subpath of

between

and

with

. We have that vertices

and

and

are connected in

by

, where

and

are subpaths of

between corresponding vertices. This shows that

∎

Now we upperbound

by a linear function of

Lemma 2

For every graph
G
and every vertex
s
of
G
,
\Delta_{s}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf bnc}(G)+2
. In particular,
\widehat{\Delta}(G)\leq 4\ {\sf bnc}(G)+2
for every graph

.

Proof

Let
s
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
and
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
be the layering partition of
G
starting at
s
. Consider vertices
x
and
y
from a cluster of
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
with
d_{G}(x,y)=\Delta_{s}(G)
, and let
k:=d_{G}(s,x)=d_{G}(s,y)
and
r:={\sf bnc}(G)
. Choose also a path
Q
connecting
x
and
y
outside the disk
D_{k-1}(s)
.

Consider arbitrary shortest paths
P(s,x)
and
P(s,y)
of
G
connecting
s
with
x
and
y
, respectively. Let
s^{\prime}
be a vertex from
P(s,x)\cap P(s,y)
furthest from

. We may assume that
k^{\prime}:=d_{G}(x,s^{\prime})=d_{G}(y,s^{\prime})=k-d_{G}(s,s^{\prime})

Pick now a vertex

in

such that

. See Fig.
2
(b) for an illustration.
The middle vertex

of a subpath

of

must see within

with

. In particular,
d_{G}(v,Q^{\prime})\leq r
for a path

formed by concatenating

with

, where

is a subpath of

between

and

.

Since

Here, we give an alternative proof for Rose McCarty’s conjecture that

is small. See also Corollary
5
and Theorem
3.4
for other alternative proofs.

Lemma 3

For every graph
G
and every vertex
s
of
G
,
\Delta_{s}(G)\leq 6\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)
. In particular,
\widehat{\Delta}(G)\leq 6\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)
for every graph
G
.

Proof

Let
s
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
and
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
be the layering partition of
G
starting at
s
. Consider vertices
x
and
y
from a cluster of
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
with
d_{G}(x,y)=\Delta_{s}(G)
, and let
k:=d_{G}(s,x)=d_{G}(s,y)
and
r:={\sf mcw}(G)
. Choose also a path
Q
connecting
x
and
y
outside the disk
D_{k-1}(s)
and arbitrary shortest paths
P(s,x)
and
P(s,y)
connecting
s
with
x
and
y
, respectively.

Since
r:={\sf mcw}(G)
, for vertices
s,x,y
, there is a vertex
v
in
G
such that disk
D_{r}(v)
intersects all three paths:
Q
,
P(s,x)
and
P(s,y)
(see Fig.
3
). Choose arbitrary vertices
u\in Q\cap D_{r}(v)
,
x^{\prime}\in P(s,x)\cap D_{r}(v)
and

. We have

and
d_{G}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
are all at most

. Since

, we get
d_{G}(s,x)=k\leq d_{G}(s,u)\leq d_{G}(s,x^{\prime})+d_{G}(x^{\prime},u)\leq d_{G}(s,x^{\prime})+2r
. That is,
d_{G}(x,x^{\prime})=d_{G}(s,x)-d_{G}(s,x^{\prime})\leq 2r
. Similarly,
d_{G}(y,y^{\prime})\leq 2r
. Consequently,
d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{G}(x,x^{\prime})+d_{G}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})+d_{G}(y,y^{\prime})\leq 6r
, i.e.,

.
∎

Lemma 4

For every graph
G
, every vertex
s
of
G
, and every integer
k\geq 3
,
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq\rho_{s}(G)
. In particular,
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq\rho(G)
for every graph
G
and every integer
k\geq 3
. Furthermore, for any subset
X\subseteq V
of vertices of
G
, a balanced disk separator
D_{r}(u)
with
r\leq\Delta_{s}(G)
can be found in linear time.

Proof

Let
s
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
and
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)=\{L^{i}_{1},\ldots,L^{i}_{p_{i}}:i=0,1,\dots,q\}
be the layering partition of
G
starting at
s
. Consider also the layering tree
\Gamma:=\Gamma(G,s)
of graph
G
with respect to the layering partition
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
.

For a given subset
X\subseteq V
of vertices of
G
, we can assign to each node
L_{i}^{j}
of
\Gamma
a weight
w_{i}^{j}:=|L_{i}^{j}\cap X|
. Clearly,
W:=\sum_{i=0,1,2,\ldots,q,j=1,2,\ldots,p_{i}}w_{i}^{j}
is equal to
|X|
.
It is known that every node-weighted tree
T
with the total weight of nodes equal to
W
has a node
x
, called a
median
of
T
,
such that the total weight of nodes in each subtree of
T\setminus\{x\}
does not exceed
W/2
. Furthermore, such a node
x
of
T
can be found in
O(|V(T)|)
time
[
46
]
.

Let
C=L_{i}^{j}

(i\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,q\},j\in\{1,2,\ldots,p_{i}\})
be a median node of weighted tree
\Gamma
. Then, each subtree of
\Gamma\setminus\{C\}
has total weight of nodes not exceeding
|X|/2
. It is clear from the construction of tree
\Gamma
that the set
C\subseteq V
separates in
G
any two vertices that belong to clusters from different subtrees of
\Gamma\setminus\{C\}
. Consequently,
C
is a balanced separator of
G
with respect to
X
as any connected component of
G[V\setminus C]
has no more than
|X|/2
vertices from
X
. Note that, given a graph

, such a cluster

of layering partition

of
G

can be found in linear time in the size of

.

Since there is a vertex
v
in
G
such that
C\subseteq D_{r}(v)
for
r\leq\rho_{s}(G)
, clearly,
D_{r}(v)
is a balanced disk separator of
G
with respect to
X
. As this holds for an arbitrary subset

, we conclude
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq\rho_{s}(G)
for any

.

It is not clear how to find in
o(nm)
time a vertex

such that
C\subseteq D_{r}(v)
(we may need the entire distance matrix of

to do that). Instead, we can take an arbitrary vertex

from

and run a breadth-first-search

from

in

until we reach the layer

of

with minimum

such that

. Clearly,

and

is a balanced disk separator of

with respect to

.
∎

In
[
32
]
, such a balanced disk separator is used to obtain an
O(m\log n)
-time
O(\log n)
-approximation algorithm for the problem of finding a tree
t
-spanner with minimum

of a given graph
G
.

It is easy to show that in fact
{\sf mcw}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)
holds (see
[
9
]
). To show a stronger inequality
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)
for every

, we will need a nice result from
[

Lemma 5
(
[
45
]
)

For every chordal graph
G=(V,E)
and every subset

of vertices of

, there is a clique

in

such that if the vertices of

are removed from

Using Lemma

, we can prove the following

Lemma 6

For every graph
G=(V,E)
and every subset
X\subseteq V
of vertices of

, there is a vertex
v\in V
such that if the vertices of disk

, where

, every connected component in the graph induced by any remaining vertices has at most
|X|/2
vertices from
X
. Consequently,
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)
holds for every graph

and every integer

.

Proof

Such a result for the case when

was first proved in
[
32
]
. We will adapt that proof to an arbitrary

Let
G
be a graph with
{\sf tb}(G)=\beta
and

be its tree-decomposition of breadth
\beta
. We can construct a new graph

from

by adding an edge between every two distinct non-adjacent vertices

of
G
such that a bag

in
{\cal T}(G)
exists with

. Clearly,

is a

of the graph

(each edge of

is an edge of
G^{+}
, but

may have some extra edges between non-adjacent vertices of

contained in a common bag of
{\cal T}(G)
).
It is known (see, e.g., survey
[
10
]
) that

is a chordal graph,

is a clique-tree of

, and for each clique

of

there is a bag

in

such that

By Lemma

, the chordal graph

contains a balanced clique-separator

.
Since

is contained in a bag of

with

. As the removal of the vertices of

from

leaves no connected component in

with more that

vertices from

, and since

∎

Note that, to find a balanced disk-separator

, Lemma

, Lemma

, and Lemma

, we get the following result.

Theorem 3.2

For every graph
G
and every vertex

of

, the following inequalities hold:

{\sf mcw}(G)\leq\rho(G)\leq\rho_{s}(G)\leq\Delta_{s}(G)\leq\widehat{\Delta}(G)\leq 6\cdot{\sf mcw}(G),

{\sf mcw}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)\leq{\sf tl}(G)\leq\Delta_{s}(G)+1\leq 6\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)+1.

In
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
, it was shown that
{\sf mcw}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)\leq{\sf tl}(G)\leq 6\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)+3
holds.

From Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
, we also get.

Corollary 3

For every graph
G
,
{\sf mcw}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf bnc}(G)+2
and
{\sf bnc}(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)
.

It is easy to see that, for every graph
G
and every
k\geq 2
,
{\sf mcw}_{2k}(G)\leq{\sf mcw}_{2k-1}(G)
. Indeed, consider a set
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{2k-1},

x_{2k}\}\subseteq V
and let
r:={\sf mcw}_{2k-1}(G)
. Then, there is a vertex
v\in V
such that every connected component of
G[V\setminus D_{r}(v)]
has at most
\frac{2k-1}{2}
, i.e., at most
k-1
, vertices from
X\setminus\{x_{2k}\}
. Hence, those connected components contain at most
k
vertices from
X
. So,
{\sf mcw}_{2k}(G)\leq{\sf mcw}_{2k-1}(G)
must hold.
Two induced cycles
C_{3}
(with 3 vertices each) sharing one common vertex (a so-called
bow-graph
) gives an example of a graph
G
with
{\sf mcw}_{3}(G)=1
and
{\sf mcw}_{4}(G)=0
. So,
{\sf mcw}_{2k}(G)<{\sf mcw}_{2k-1}(G)
is possible for some graph
G
.
We do not know the relationship between
{\sf mcw}_{2k}(G)
and
{\sf mcw}_{2k+1}(G)
, except that there are graphs
G
and integers
k
such that
{\sf mcw}_{2k}(G)<{\sf mcw}_{2k+1}(G)
(e.g., for a block graph with three blocks and two articulation points, each block being a triangle,
{\sf mcw}_{4}(G)=0<1={\sf mcw}_{5}(G)
holds). However, from our results, it follows that, for every graph
G
, every
k\geq 3
and
s\in V
,
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq\rho_{s}(G)\leq\Delta_{s}(G)\leq 6\cdot{\sf mcw}_{3}(G)=6\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)
(which is of independent interest).
Thus, for every
k\geq 3
,
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)
is bounded from above by a (linear) function of
{\sf mcw}(G)
. This leads to a natural question if
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)
(
k>3
) and
{\sf mcw}_{3}(G)
are coarsely equivalent parameters. Unfortunately, the answer is ’no’. By extending our bow-graph example, we can show that
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)
(
k>3
), generally, cannot be bounded from below by a function of
{\sf mcw}(G)
.
Consider a graph
G
consisting of two induced cycles
C_{6p}
on
6p

(p\geq 1)
vertices each, sharing just one common vertex
v
. We have
n:=|V(G)|=12p-1
and
{\sf mcw}_{n}(G)=0
(we just need to remove vertex

to partition
G
into two connected components each having
6p-1
vertices from
V(G)
). Considering also three vertices of one
C_{6p}
that are pairwise at distance
2p
from each other, we get
{\sf mcw}(G)\geq p
.

As we have mentioned earlier, a balanced disk-separator

with minimum
r
of a graph
G
with respect to any subset

can be found in total
O(nm)
time. Consequently, for every graph
G
, the parameter
{\sf mcw}(G)
can be computed in at most

time. Similarly, for every graph
G
, the parameter
{\sf bnc}(G)
can be computed in at most
O(n^{3}m)
time. One can try to get better time complexities (using some special data structures), but since
{\sf bnc}(G)
and
{\sf mcw}(G)
are further from
{\sf tl}(G)
than
\Delta_{s}(G)
is, we did not pursue this line of investigation.

3.3
Brambles and Helly families of connected subgraphs or paths

A
bramble
of a graph
G
is a family of connected subgraphs of
G
that all
touch
each other: for every pair of disjoint subgraphs, there must exist an edge in
G
that has one endpoint in each subgraph. The
order
of a bramble is the smallest size of a hitting set, a set of vertices of
G
that has a nonempty intersection with each of the subgraphs. Brambles are used to characterize the tree-width of
G
:
k
is the largest possible order among all brambles of
G
if and only if
G
has tree-width
k-1

[
58
]
.

A family of subgraphs of
G

is called a
Helly family
if every two subgraphs of the family intersect. Clearly, any Helly family of connected subgraphs of
G
is a particular bramble of
G
.
Below we define new properties for brambles and Helly families of connected subgraphs of
G
that coarsely define the tree-breadth
{\sf tb}(G)
.

Let
{\cal F}:=\{H_{1},\dots,H_{p}\}
be a family of subgraphs of
G
. We say that a disk
D_{r}(v)
of
G
intercepts all members of
{\cal F}
if
V(H_{i})\cap D_{r}(v)\neq\emptyset
for every
i=1,\dots,p
.
Denote by
{\sf br}(G)
(the
bramble interception
radius of
G
) the smallest integer
r
such that for every bramble
{\cal F}
of
G
, there is a disk
D_{r}(v)
which intercepts all members of
{\cal F}
. Denote by
{\sf sh}(G)
(by
{\sf ph}(G)
) the smallest integer
r
such that for every Helly family
{\cal F}
of connected subgraphs (of paths, respectively) of
G
, there is a disk
D_{r}(v)
which intercepts all members of
{\cal F}
. Call
{\sf sh}(G)
(
{\sf ph}(G)
) the interception radius for Helly families of connected subgraphs (of paths) of
G
. Such property for Helly families of disks of a graph were already considered in literature (see, e.g.,
[
17
,
22
,
31
]
).

It turns out that all these three parameters are coarsely equivalent to tree-breadth. We prove this by involving the McCarty-width parameter and a result from
[
58
]
.

Proposition 15

For every
G
,
{\sf mcw}(G)\leq{\sf ph}(G)\leq{\sf sh}(G)\leq{\sf br}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)\leq 6\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)+1
.

Proof

Inequalities
{\sf ph}(G)\leq{\sf sh}(G)\leq{\sf br}(G)
follow from definitions. Hence, by Theorem
3.2
, we only need to show
{\sf br}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)
and
{\sf mcw}(G)\leq{\sf ph}(G)
. According to
[
58
]
, for every bramble
{\cal F}
of a graph
G
, there is a bag
B
in every tree-decomposition of
G
which intercepts all members of
{\cal F}
. Hence, for a tree-decomposition with minimum breadth, there is a bag
B
and a vertex
v
in
G
with
B\subseteq D_{r}(v)
,
r\leq{\sf tb}(G)
, such that the disk
D_{r}(v)
of
G
intercepts all members of
{\cal F}
. Consequently,
{\sf br}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)
.

Consider now any three vertices
x,y,z
of
G
and the family
{\cal F}
of all paths of
G
connecting pairs from
\{x,y,z\}
. The family
{\cal F}
is a Helly family. If
{\sf ph}(G)=r
, then there is a disk
D_{r}(v)
in
G
that intercepts all paths from
{\cal F}
. Consequently, no connected component of
G[V\setminus D_{r}(v)]
contains two of
x,y,z
. The latter proves
{\sf mcw}(G)\leq{\sf ph}(G)
.
∎

We can generalize the second part of the proof of Proposition
15
and show
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq{\sf sh}(G)
for every graph
G
and every
k\geq 3
. This provides also an alternative proof of Lemma

.

Proposition 16

For every graph
G
and every
k\geq 3
,
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq{\sf sh}(G)\leq{\sf br}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)
.

Proof

We need only to show
{\sf mcw}_{k}(G)\leq{\sf sh}(G)
. Consider a subset
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}
of vertices of
G
and any subset
Y\subset X
containing
\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor+1
vertices of
X
. Let

{\cal F}_{Y}
be the family of all connected subgraphs of
G
spanning vertices of
Y
. For any two subsets
X^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime\prime}
of
X
containing
\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor+1

X
each, any subgraph from
{\cal F}_{X^{\prime}}
intersects any subgraph from
{\cal F}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
(as

). Hence,

is a Helly family of connected subgraphs of

.
Hence, there must exist a vertex

in

such that disk

, intercepts each subgraph from

∎

For an induced cycle

on six vertices, we have
{\sf ph}(C_{6})={\sf sh}(C_{6})=1
while

. So,
{\sf sh}(G)<{\sf br}(G)
is possible for some graph

.

3.4
Distance
k
-approximating trees

By Proposition
2
and Proposition
5
,
{\sf adt}(G)\leq\Delta(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf tl}(G)
. Hence, we need only to upperbound

by a linear function of
{\sf adt}(G)
.

Lemma 7

For every graph
G
,
{\sf tl}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf adt}(G)+1
.

Proof

Let
G=(V,E(G))
,
{\sf adt}(G)=r
, and
T=(V,E(T))
be a tree such that
|d_{G}(x,y)-d_{T}(x,y)|\leq r
for every
x,y\in V
. For every edge
uv\in E(G)
,
d_{T}(u,v)\leq d_{G}(u,v)+r\leq 1+r
holds. Hence,
G
is a spanning subgraph of graph
T^{r+1}
, where
T^{r+1}
is the
(r+1)^{st}
-power of
T
. It is known (see e.g.,
[
14
,
47
]
) that every power of a tree is a chordal graph. Consequently, there is a clique-tree
{\cal T}(G)
of
T^{r+1}
. Clearly,
{\cal T}(G)
is a tree-decomposition of
G
such that, for every two vertices
x
and
y
belonging to same bag of
{\cal T}(G)
,
xy
is an edge of
T^{r+1}
. Necessarily,
d_{T}(x,y)\leq r+1
, by the definition of the
(r+1)^{st}
-power of
T
. Furthermore, since
d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{T}(x,y)+r\leq 2r+1
for every
x,y
belonging to same bag of
{\cal T}(G)
, the length of the tree-decomposition
{\cal T}(G)
of
G
is at most
2r+1=2\cdot{\sf adt}(G)+1
.
∎

Combining Proposition
2
and Proposition
5
with Theorem
3.1
, Lemma

and Lemma

, we get the following result.

Theorem 3.3

For every graph

and every vertex

of

, the following inequalities hold:

{\sf adt}(G)\leq\Delta(G)\leq\Delta_{s}(G)\leq\widehat{\Delta}(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf tl}(G)\leq 6\cdot{\sf adt}(G)+3,

Corollary 4

For every graph

,
\frac{{\sf tl}(G)-1}{2}\leq{\sf adt}(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf tl}(G).

Recall that in
[

, Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.2, Theorem 4.5]

hold.

From Theorem

and Theorem

The latter inequality can further be improved to

For this we will need the following interesting lemma.

Lemma 8

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph,
T=(V,E^{\prime})
be a distance

-approximating tree of

with

. For every

, any path

of

between

and

from the path

holds.

Proof

Removing

from

, we separate

from
y
. Let
T_{y}
be the subtree of

containing

. Since
x\notin T_{y}
, we can find an edge
ab
of
P_{G}(x,y)
with
a\in T_{y}
and

b\notin T_{y}
. Therefore, the path
P_{T}(a,b)
must go via

. If
d_{T}(c,a)>(k+1)/2
and

,
then

and, since

, we obtain a contradiction with the

is a distance

(with

.
Let

be a vertex of

such that

d_{G}(c,P_{G}(x,y))\leq d_{G}(c,z)\leq d_{T}(c,z)+k=d_{T}(c,P_{G}(x,y))+k\leq(k+1)/2+k
.
∎

Corollary 5

For every graph

,
{\sf adt}(G)\leq 6\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)

{\sf mcw}(G)\leq\frac{3\cdot{\sf adt}(G)+1}{2}
.

.

-minor for some constant

improves the constant

Lemma 9

Let
G
be a graph and

be a vertex of

. If

has a

-fat
K_{3}
-minor.

Proof

Let
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
be the layering partition of

and

from a cluster of

with

connecting
x
and
y
outside the disk

and arbitrary shortest paths

and

connecting

with
x
and

Since

K
-fat
K_{3}
-minor of

in the following way.

We know that

must be greater that
2K
(otherwise,

Consider vertices

and

on path

at distance

and

from

. Similarly, consider
vertices
y^{\prime}
and
s_{y}
on path

at distance
K
and
2K
from

, respectively. As three connected subgraphs of

choose
H_{x}:=G[D_{K}(x)],H_{y}:=G[D_{K}(y)]
and
H_{s}:=G[D_{\ell-2K}(s)]
. As three paths choose a subpath

of

between
x^{\prime}
and
s_{x}
, a subpath
P(y^{\prime},s_{y})
of

between

and

and a subpath

of

between vertices

-minor in

(note that

, which is impossible). It remains to show that it is a

-fat

-minor.

We have

Similarly,

. Furthermore,

, which is impossible. So,

, which is impossible.

and, by symmetry,

. Finally,

since, otherwise, we get

, which is impossible.

Thus, constructed
K_{3}
-minor of

is
K
-fat.
∎

, Corollary
6
, Lemma
10
, Proposition
12
, Theorem
3.1
, and Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.4

For every graph
G
and every vertex
s
of
G
,

\frac{{\sf mf}(G)}{2}\leq{\sf mcw}(G)\leq{\sf tb}(G)\leq{\sf tl}(G)\leq\Delta_{s}(G)+1\leq 5\cdot{\sf mf}(G),

\frac{{\sf mf}(G)-1}{2}\leq\frac{2\cdot{\sf mcw}(G)-1}{3}\leq{\sf adt}(G)\leq\Delta_{s}(G)\leq 5\cdot{\sf mf}(G)-1,

{\sf mf}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf bnc}(G)+1\leq\Delta_{s}(G)+1\leq 5\cdot{\sf mf}(G).

3.6
Cycle bridging properties

The following
characteristic cycle property
immediately follows from the definition of a chordal graph
G
:
For every simple cycle
C
of a chordal graph
G
and every vertex
v\in C
, the two neighbors in
C
of
v
are adjacent or there is a third vertex in
C
that is adjacent to
v
.
We can generalize this property and show that its generalized version is coarsely equivalent to tree-length.

Let
C
be a simple cycle of
G
,
v
be a vertex of
C
and
x,y
be two vertices of
C
with
d_{C}(x,v)=d_{C}(y,v)=k
(assuming
C
is long enough). We call
x,y
the
k
-neighbors of
v
in
C
. Denote by
{\sf cbc}(G)
(call it the
cycle bridging constant
of
G
) the minimum
k
(
k\geq 1
) such that for every simple cycle
C
of
G
and every vertex
v
of
C
, if
d_{G}(x,y)=2k
holds for the two
k
-neighbors
x,y
of
v
in
C
(resulting in
|C|\geq 4k
), then there is a vertex
z\in C
satisfying
d_{G}(v,z)\leq k<d_{C}(v,z)
(the latter inequality just says that
z
is on
C\setminus P_{v}(x,y)
, where
P_{v}(x,y)
is a part of
C
between
x
and
y
containing
v
). Clearly,
{\sf cbc}(G)
of a chordal graph
G
is 1. The following two lemmas hold.

Lemma 11

For every graph
G
,
{\sf bnc}(G)\leq{\sf cbc}(G)
.

Proof

Assume
{\sf bnc}(G)>{\sf cbc}(G):=k
. By the definition of the bottleneck constant, for some vertices
x,y
of
G
at even distance from each other, some shortest path
P(x,y)
connecting
x
and
y
and the middle vertex
s
of
P(x,y)
, there must exist a path
Q
in
G[V\setminus D_{k}(s)]
connecting
x
and
y
. Necessarily,
d_{G}(x,s)=d_{G}(y,s)\geq k+1
.
Consider a vertex
x^{\prime}
on a subpath of
P(x,y)
between
x
and
s
and a vertex
y^{\prime}
on a subpath of
P(x,y)
between
y
and
s
with
d_{G}(x^{\prime},s)=d_{G}(y^{\prime},s)=k+1
. We have that vertices
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
with
d_{G}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=2k+2
and
d_{G}(x^{\prime},s)=d_{G}(s,y^{\prime})=k+1
are connected in
G[V\setminus D_{k}(s)]
by
P(x,x^{\prime})\cup Q\cup P(y,y^{\prime})
, where
P(x,x^{\prime})
and
P(y,y^{\prime})
are subpaths of
P(x,y)
between corresponding vertices. Extract from
P(x,x^{\prime})\cup Q\cup P(y,y^{\prime})
a simple subpath
Q^{\prime}
connecting
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
outside the disk
D_{k}(s)
. The union of
Q^{\prime}
and
P(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
forms a simple cycle in which the
k
-neighbors of
s
are at distance
2k
in
G
but
s
does not have any vertex in
Q^{\prime}
at distance at most
k
in
G
. The latter contradicts with
{\sf cbc}(G)=k
.
∎

Lemma 12

For every graph
G
,
{\sf cbc}(G)\leq\lceil\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)+1}{2}\rceil\leq\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)}{2}+1
.

Proof

Let
k:=\lceil\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)+1}{2}\rceil
,
C
be a simple cycle of
G
,
v
be an arbitrary vertex of
C
, and assume that
d_{G}(x,y)=2k
holds for the two
k
-neighbors
x,y
of
v
in
C
. Consider the layering partition
\mathcal{LP}(G,v)
of
G
starting at
v
. Since
d_{G}(x,v)=d_{G}(y,v)=k
, vertices
x
and
y
belong to the same layer
L^{k}
of the layering of
G
with respect to
v
. Since
d_{G}(x,y)=2k=2\lceil\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)+1}{2}\rceil>\widehat{\Delta}(G)
, by the definition of
\widehat{\Delta}(G)
,
x
and
y
cannot belong to the same cluster from
L^{k}
. By the definition of clusters, every path connecting
x
with
y
in
G
must have a vertex in
D_{k-1}(v)
. Hence, there must exist also a vertex
z\in C\setminus P_{v}(x,y)
such that
d_{G}(v,z)\leq k-1<k
, where
P_{v}(x,y)
is a part of
C
between
x
and
y
containing
v
. Consequently,
{\sf cbc}(G)\leq\lceil\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)+1}{2}\rceil\leq\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)}{2}+1
. ∎

Combining Lemma
11
and Lemma
12
with Theorem
3.1
, we get the following result.

Theorem 3.5

For every graph
G
, the following inequalities hold:

\frac{{\sf tl}(G)-3}{4}\leq\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)-2}{4}\leq{\sf bnc}(G)\leq{\sf cbc}(G)\leq\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)}{2}+1\leq\frac{3}{2}{\sf tl}(G)+1.

Corollary 7

For every graph
G
,
\frac{2}{3}({\sf cbc}(G)-1)\leq{\sf tl}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf cbc}(G)+3
.

Note that, although
{\sf tl}(G)={\sf cbc}(G)=1
for every chordal graph
G
, generally, these two graph parameters are not equal. Consider a cycle
C_{12k}
on
12k
vertices. We have
{\sf tl}(C_{12k})=12k/3=4k
and
{\sf cbc}(C_{12k})=12k/4+1=3k+1.
Furthermore, since
{\sf bnc}(C_{4})={\sf bnc}(C_{5})={\sf bnc}(C_{6})={\sf bnc}(C_{7})=1
and
{\sf cbc}(C_{4})={\sf cbc}(C_{5})={\sf cbc}(C_{6})={\sf cbc}(C_{7})=2
, generally,
{\sf bnc}(G)
and
{\sf cbc}(G)
are not equal. However, it turns out that, indeed, they are only one unit apart.

Lemma 13

For every graph
G
,
{\sf bnc}(G)\leq{\sf cbc}(G)\leq{\sf bnc}(G)+1
.

Proof

By Lemma
11
, we need only to show
{\sf cbc}(G)\leq{\sf bnc}(G)+1
. Let
k:={\sf bnc}(G)+1
,
C
be a simple cycle of
G
,
v
be an arbitrary vertex of
C
, and assume that
d_{G}(x,y)=2k
holds for the two
k
-neighbors
x,y
of
v
in
C
. By the definition of
{\sf bnc}(G)
, vertex
v
has at distance at most
{\sf bnc}(G)=k-1
a vertex in every path of
G
connecting
x
and
y
. Necessarily, there must exist a vertex
z
in
C\setminus P_{v}(x,y)
such that
d_{G}(v,z)\leq k-1<k
, where
P_{v}(x,y)
is a part of
C
between
x
and
y
containing
v
. Consequently,
{\sf cbc}(G)\leq k={\sf bnc}(G)+1
. ∎

We can now better relate
{\sf cbc}(G)
to
{\sf adt}(G)
and
{\sf mcw}(G)
. By Corollary
3
, Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
13
, we have the following inequalities.

Corollary 8

For every graph
G
,
\frac{{\sf cbc}(G)-2}{3}\leq\frac{{\sf bnc}(G)-1}{3}\leq{\sf adt}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf bnc}(G)+2\leq 4\cdot{\sf cbc}(G)+2
and
\frac{{\sf cbc}(G)-1}{3}\leq{\sf mcw}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf cbc}(G)+2
.

We can define one more condition on cycles which also turns out to be coarsely equivalent to tree-length. Recall that a simple cycle
C
of
G
is called
geodesic
if for every
x,y\in C
,
d_{G}(x,y)=d_{C}(x,y)
. Let us call a simple cycle
C
of
G

\mu
-locally geodesic
if for every two vertices
x,y\in C
,
d_{G}(x,y)=d_{C}(x,y)=\ell
implies
\ell\leq\mu
. When
\mu\geq|C|/2
, clearly, every
\mu
-locally geodesic cycle
C
is geodesic. If a simple cycle
C
is not
\mu
-locally geodesic in
G
, then there must exist two vertices in
C
such that
d_{G}(x,y)=d_{C}(x,y)=\mu+1
. We call that side of
C
between
x
and
y
which realizes
d_{C}(x,y)
a
side of non-
\mu
-locality
(note that
x
and

both belong to that side). We say that vertices
v,z
of

form a
k
-bridge
in
C
if
d_{G}(v,z)\leq k<d_{C}(v,z)
.

Denote by
{\sf bgc}(G)
(call it the
”bridging non-locally geodesic cycles“ constant
of
G
) the minimum
\mu
(
\mu\geq 1
) such that for every cycle
C
of
G
that is not
\mu
-locally geodesic, from every side of non-
\mu
-locality there is a
\lfloor\frac{\mu+1}{2}\rfloor
-bridge to other side of
C
.

Lemma 14

For every graph
G
,
{\sf bgc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf cbc}(G)-1
.

Proof

Let
{\sf cbc}(G)=k
and
C
be a cycle of
G
that is not
(2k-1)
-locally geodesic. Let
x,y\in C
such that
d_{G}(x,y)=d_{C}(x,y)=2k
. Consider vertex
v
of
C
between
x
and
y
with
d_{C}(v,x)=k=d_{C}(v,y)
. The two
k
-neighbors of
v
in
C
are at distance
2k
from each other in
G
. By the definition of
{\sf cbc}(G)
, there is a vertex
z\in C
such that
d_{G}(v,z)\leq k<d_{C}(v,z)
. Consequently,
{\sf bgc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf cbc}(G)-1
.
∎

Now, using a layering partition of a graph
G
, we upperbound
\Delta_{s}(G)
by a linear function of
{\sf bgc}(G)
.

Lemma 15

For every graph
G
and every vertex
s
of
G
,
\Delta_{s}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf bgc}(G)+6
. In particular,
\widehat{\Delta}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf bgc}(G)+6
for every graph
G
.

Proof

Let
s
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
and
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
be the layering partition of
G
starting at
s
. Consider vertices
x
and
y
from a cluster of
\mathcal{LP}(G,s)
with
d_{G}(x,y)=\Delta_{s}(G)
, and let
k:=d_{G}(s,x)=d_{G}(s,y)
and
\mu:={\sf bgc}(G)+1
. Choose also a path
Q
connecting
x
and
y
outside the disk
D_{k-1}(s)
.

Consider arbitrary shortest paths
P(s,x)
and
P(s,y)
of
G
connecting
s
with
x
and
y
, respectively. Let
s^{\prime}
be a vertex from
P(s,x)\cap P(s,y)
furthest from
s
. We may assume that
k^{\prime}:=d_{G}(x,s^{\prime})=d_{G}(y,s^{\prime})=k-d_{G}(s,s^{\prime})
is greater than
\mu+\lfloor\frac{\mu}{2}\rfloor
since, otherwise,
d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{G}(x,s^{\prime})+d_{G}(s^{\prime},y)=2k^{\prime}\leq 3\mu
, and we are done.

Pick now vertices
u,w
in
P(s,x)
such that

d_{G}(x,u)=\mu+\lfloor\frac{\mu}{2}\rfloor+1\leq d_{G}(x,s^{\prime})
,
d_{G}(u,w)=\mu
and
d_{G}(x,w)=\lfloor\frac{\mu}{2}\rfloor+1
. Let
P(u,w)
be a subpath of
P(s,x)
between
u
and
w
.
A simple cycle
C
of
G
formed by
Q
and subpaths
P(s^{\prime},x)
and
P(s^{\prime},y)
of
P(s,x)
and
P(s,y)
, respectively, is not
(\mu-1)
-locally geodesic. Since
\mu-1={\sf bgc}(G)
, for a side
P(u,w)
of
C
of non-
(\mu-1)
-locality, we must have a
\lfloor\mu/2\rfloor
-bridge from a vertex
v\in P(u,w)
to a vertex
z\in C\setminus P(u,w)
. As
d_{G}(v,z)\leq\lfloor\mu/2\rfloor<d_{C}(v,z)
, vertex
z
cannot belong to a shortest path
P(s^{\prime},x)
(which is a part of
C
and contains also
v
). If
z
belongs to
Q
, then
d_{G}(s,Q)=k=d_{G}(s,x)=d_{G}(s,v)+d_{G}(v,z)\leq d_{G}(s,w)+\lfloor\mu/2\rfloor<d_{G}(s,w)+d_{G}(w,x)=d_{G}(s,x)=k
, giving a contradiction. Thus,
z
cannot be in
Q
either. Consequently,
z\in P(s^{\prime},y)
.

Since
d_{G}(y,z)=k-d_{G}(s,z)
and
k=d_{G}(x,s)\leq d_{G}(s,z)+d_{G}(z,v)+d_{G}(v,x)
, necessarily,
d_{G}(y,z)\leq d_{G}(z,v)+d_{G}(v,x)\leq d(z,v)+d_{G}(u,x)\leq\lfloor\mu/2\rfloor+\mu+\lfloor\mu/2\rfloor+1\leq 2\mu+1
. Consequently,
d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{G}(x,v)+d_{G}(v,z)+d_{G}(z,y)\leq d_{G}(x,u)+d_{G}(v,z)+d_{G}(z,y)\leq\mu+\lfloor\mu/2\rfloor+1+\lfloor\mu/2\rfloor+2\mu+1=4\mu+2
. That is,
\Delta_{s}(G)\leq 4({\sf bgc}(G)+1)+2=4\cdot{\sf bgc}(G)+6
. ∎

Summarizing (using Proposition
5
, Lemma
12
, Lemma
14
, and Lemma
15
), we conclude.

Theorem 3.6

For every graph
G
, the following inequalities hold:

\frac{{\sf tl}(G)-7}{4}\leq\frac{\widehat{\Delta}(G)-6}{4}\leq{\sf bgc}(G)\leq 2{\sf cbc}(G)-1\leq\widehat{\Delta}(G)+1\leq 3\cdot{\sf tl}(G)+1.

Corollary 9

For every graph
G
,
\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)-1}{3}\leq{\sf tl}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf bgc}(G)+7
.

From Lemma
12
and Lemma
15
, it follows
{\sf cbc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf bgc}(G)+4
. We can show a better bound directly.

Lemma 16

For every graph
G
,
{\sf cbc}(G)\leq{\sf bgc}(G)+1
.

Proof

Let
k:=\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)}{2}\rfloor+1+\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)+1}{2}\rfloor
,
C
be a simple cycle of
G
,
v
be an arbitrary vertex of
C
, and assume that
d_{G}(x,y)=2k
holds for the two
k
-neighbors
x,y
of
v
in
C
. We have
d_{G}(x,y)=d_{C}(x,y)=2k
. Consider the shortest path
P(x,y)\subset C
between
x
and
y
, and let
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
be two distinct vertices of
P(x,y)
at distance
\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)}{2}\rfloor+1
from
v
. Let
P(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
be a subpath of
P(x,y)
between
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
. Its length
d_{G}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
is
2(\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)}{2}\rfloor+1)\geq{\sf bgc}(G)+1
.
By the definition of
{\sf bgc}(G)
, from non-
{\sf bgc}(G)
-locality side
P(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
of
C
there must exist a
\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)+1}{2}\rfloor
-bridge to other side of
C
, i.e., vertices
v^{\prime}\in P(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
and
z\in C\setminus P(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
such that
d_{G}(v^{\prime},z)\leq\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)+1}{2}\rfloor<d_{C}(v^{\prime},z)
. The inequality
d_{G}(v^{\prime},z)\leq\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)+1}{2}\rfloor

d_{G}(v,z)\leq d_{G}(v,v^{\prime})+d_{G}(v^{\prime},z)\leq\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)}{2}\rfloor+1+\lfloor\frac{{\sf bgc}(G)+1}{2}\rfloor=k
. The inequality
d_{G}(v^{\prime},z)<d_{C}(v^{\prime},z)
guarantees that
z
cannot be in
P(x,y)
(recall that
P(x,y)\subset C
is a shortest path of
G
and, hence, for every
u,w\in P(x,y)
,
d_{G}(u,w)=d_{C}(u,w)
).

Consequently,
{\sf cbc}(G)\leq k={\sf bgc}(G)+1
. ∎

4
Concluding remarks and open questions

We saw that several graph parameters are coarsely equivalent to tree-length. If one of the parameters from the list
\{{\sf tl}(G),{\sf tb}(G),{\sf itl}(G),{\sf itb}(G),\Delta_{s}(G),\rho_{s}(G),{\sf td}(G),{\sf ad}(G),{\sf adt}(G),

{\sf bnc}(G),{\sf mcw}(G),

{\sf ph}(G),{\sf sh}(G),{\sf br}(G),{\sf mf}(G),{\sf glc}(G),{\sf cbc}(G),{\sf bgc}(G)\}
is bounded for a graph
G
, then all other parameters are bounded. We saw that, in fact, all those parameters are within small constant factors from each other. Two questions are immediate.

1.

Can constants in those inequalities be further improved? Is
{\sf br}(G)={\sf tb}(G)
true?

2.

Are there any other interesting graph parameters that are coarsely equivalent to tree-length?

We can further relax the ”bridging non-locally geodesic cycles“ condition on cycles. Let
{\sf BGC}(G)
be the minimum
\mu
(
\mu\geq 1
) such that every simple cycle
C
of
G
that is not
\mu
-locally geodesic has a

\lfloor\frac{\mu+1}{2}\rfloor
-bridge (in general position). Clearly,
{\sf BGC}(G)\leq{\sf bgc}(G)
.

3.

Does there exist a constant
c
such that
{\sf bgc}(G)\leq c\cdot{\sf BGC}(G)
for every graph
G
?

In
[
52
]
, a notion of strong tree-breadth was introduced. The
strong breadth
of a tree-decomposition
{\cal T}(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum integer
r
such that for every
B\in V({\cal T}(G))
there is a vertex
v_{B}\in B
with
B=D_{r}(v_{B},G)
(i.e., each bag
B
is equal to a disk of
G
of radius at most
r
). The
strong tree-breadth
of
G
, denoted by
{\sf stb}(G)
, is the minimum of the strong breadth, over all tree-decompositions of
G
. Like for the tree-breadth, it is NP-complete to determine if a given graph has strong tree-breadth
r
, even for
r=1

[
52
]
. See
[
51
,
52
]
for other interesting results on tree-breadth and strong tree-breadth. Clearly,
{\sf tb}(G)\leq{\sf stb}(G)
for every graph
G
. The following question was already asked in
[
52
]
and matches very much the topic of this paper.

4.

Does there exist a constant
c
such that
{\sf stb}(G)\leq c\cdot{\sf tb}(G)
for every graph
G
?

In
[
30
]
, a notion generalizing tree-width and tree-breadth was introduced. The
k
-breadth
of a tree-decomposition
{\cal T}(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum integer
r
such
that for each bag
B\in V({\cal T}(G))
, there is a set of at most
k
vertices
C_{B}=\{v_{B}^{1},\dots,v_{B}^{k}\}\subseteq V(G)
such that for each
u\in B
,
d_{G}(u,C_{B})\leq r
holds (i.e., each bag
B
can be covered with at most
k
disks of
G
of radius at most
r
each;
B\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{k}D_{r}(v_{B}^{i},G)
. The
k
-tree-breadth
of a graph
G
, denoted by
{\sf tb}_{k}(G)
, is the minimum of the
k
-breadth,
over all tree-decompositions of
G
. Clearly, for every graph
G
,
{\sf tb}(G)={\sf tb}_{1}(G)
and
{\sf tw}(G)\leq k-1
if and only if
{\sf tb}_{k}(G)=0
(each vertex in the bags of the tree-decomposition can be considered as a center of a disk of radius 0).

5.

It would be interesting to investigate if there exist generalizations of some graph parameters considered in this paper that coarsely describe the
k
-tree-breadth.

In a follow-up paper
[
33
]
, we introduce several graph parameters that are coarsely equivalent to path-length.

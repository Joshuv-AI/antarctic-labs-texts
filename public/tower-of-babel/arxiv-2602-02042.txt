Hypersurface Singularities in any Characteristic In Memoriam Wolfgang Ebeling
Gert-Martin Greuel

Abstract




Abstract

We give an overview of the fundamental definitions and results concerning hypersurface singularities, defined by convergent power series over an arbitrary real valued field. This approach combines, on the one hand, the classical case of analytic power series over the complex numbers with formal power series over arbitrary fields, but on the other hand, it goes significantly beyond that.
Besides general definitions and basic results, we report on the classification of contact simple and right simple singularities in positive characteristic.
Some of the results are new in this general setting, for which we provide complete proofs.

Introduction

While the theory of singularities of complex analytic spaces and germs is covered in several textbooks, the study of singularities of algebraic varieties and formal power series defined over a field of arbitrary characteristic has not yet been fully explored.
This article provides an overview of the fundamental definitions and results concerning hypersurface singularities of any characteristic.

Many of the topics discussed here have counterparts over the complex numbers. But in positive characteristics, the results often differ significantly and require a different proof in each case. This overview is based on Chapter 3.1 of the second edition of the textbook ”Introduction to Singularities and Deformations” (see

We introduce in Section
1
the basic invariants for power series
f\in K\{x\}
, which generalize
the classical ones for complex analytic singularities to analytic singularities in arbitrary characteristic. Moreover, using the so called ”Lefschetz principle”, we show that some results known for
{\mathbb{C}}
hold also for real valued fields of characteristic zero.
We discuss then finite determinacy for power series in
f\in K\{x\}
with respect to right and contact equivalence (implying that the truncation up to some finite degree determines its equivalence class), which is indispensable for the classification of singularities. This is used in the last two sections, when we classify singularities in positive characteristic. Finally we prove the Mather-Yau theorem for
f\in K\{x\}
with
K
real valued.

In Section
2
we give a proof of the splitting lemma for power series in
K\{x\}
. The splitting lemma is of great importance for the classification of singularities. It says that a power series
f
splits (up to a coordinate change) as the sum of a quadratic part
f^{(2)}
plus a series
g
of order at least three, where
f^{(2)}
and
g
depend on disjoint sets of variables.
Moreover, the series
g
, the ”residual part”, is unique up to a change of coordinates.
The original proof (for diﬀerentiable functions) is due to René Thom, who used it for his classification of the 7 elementary catastrophes (see his book
[
Th75
, § 5.2D]
). Thom’s proof (for differentiable functions), especially for the uniqueness of the residual part, is fairly long and complicated, while our proof is quite simple (but with some tricky substitutions).

The previous results are used in Section
3
resp. Section
4
, which culminates in the classification of simple hypersurface singularities in positive characteristic with respect to contact equivalence resp. right equivalence. While the classification with respect to contact equivalence is similar to that in characteristic 0, the classification with respect to right equivalence is fundamentally different.

Invariants of Hypersurface Singularities

We keep the notations from the Introduction, with
\mathfrak{m}=\langle x\rangle=\langle x_{1},...,x_{n}\rangle
denoting the maximal ideal of
K\{x\}
.
Given a power series
f\in K\{x\}
, we call
f
or the analytic
K
-algebra

K\{x\}/\langle f\rangle

a
hypersurface singularity
if
f\in\mathfrak{m},f\neq 0
.

The two most important equivalence relations for power series are right equivalence and contact equivalence.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f,g\in K\{x\}
and
k
a positive integer.

1.

f
is right equivalent
to
g\ (f\overset{r}{\sim}g)
iff
there exists a
\varphi\in\Aut(K\{x\})
such that
f=\varphi(g)
,
i.e.
f
and
g
differ by an analytic coordinate change
\varphi
with
\varphi(x_{i})=\varphi_{i}\in K\{x\}
and
f=g(\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{n}).

2.

f
is contact equivalent to
g\ (f\overset{c}{\sim}g)
iff
there exists a
\varphi\in\Aut(K\{x\})
and a unit
u\in K\{x\}^{\ast}
such that
f=u\cdot\varphi(g)
,
i.e. the analytic
K
–algebras
K\{x\}/\langle f\rangle
and
K\{x\}/\langle g\rangle
are isomorphic.

3.

We call
f
right
k
-determined (resp. contact
k
-determined) if
f\rsim g
(resp.
f\csim g
) for every
g\in K\{x\}
with
f-g\in\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
or, equivalently, if
\jet_{k}(f)=\jet_{k}(g)
. Here
\jet_{k}(f)
, the image of
f
in
K\{x\}/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
, denotes the
k
-jet of
f
, identified with the power series of
f
up to degree
\leq k
.

In both cases we say that
f
is
finitely determined
if it is
k
-determined for some integer
k>0
, and we call the least such
k
the
determinacy
of
f
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
f\in K\{x\}
we set

1.

\begin{array}[]{llll}j(f)&:=&\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle&\text{ \emph{the Jacobian ideal}},\\
tj(f)\rangle&:=&\langle f,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle&\text{ \emph{the Tjurina ideal}},\end{array}

and call the associated algebras

\begin{array}[]{llll}M_{f}&:=&K\{x\}/j(f)&\text{ the \emph{Milnor algebra}, and }\\
T_{f}&:=&K\{x\}/\langle f,j(f)\rangle&\text{ the \emph{Tjurina algebra} of }f.\end{array}

Their dimensions are

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mu(f)&:=&\dim_{K}K\{x\}/\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle{\@index{\@indexphrase{Milnor}\@indexphrase{number}}}&\text{ \emph{the Milnor number of }}f,\\
\tau(f)&:=&\dim_{K}K\{x\}/\langle f,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle{\@index{\@indexphrase{Tjurina}\@indexphrase{number}}}&\text{ \emph{the Tjurina number of }}f.{\@index{\@indexphrase[$\mu$]{$\mu(f)$}}}{\@index{\@indexphrase[$\tau$]{$\tau(f)$}}}\end{array}

2.

We say that
f
has an

isolated singularity
if
\mu(f)<\infty
(which is
equivalent to the existence of a positive integer
k
such that

\mathfrak{m}^{k}\subseteq j(f)
), and we
call

an
isolated (hypersurface) singularity
if

\tau(f)<\infty
(equivalently, if there is a positive integer
k

such that
\mathfrak{m}^{k}\subseteq tj(f)
). We say also that

defines an isolated singularity, meaning that
\mu(f)<\infty
if we consider right equivalence resp.
\tau(f)<\infty
if we consider contact equivalence.

These definitions coincide with the complex analytic ones. It is straight forward to see
that for an automorphism
\varphi\in\Aut(K\{x\})
and a unit

u\in K\{x\}^{*}
we have

\begin{array}[]{llll}j\big(\varphi(f)\big)&=&\varphi\big(j(f)\big),\\
tj\big(u\varphi(f)\big)&=&\varphi(tj(f)\big).\end{array}

In particular, the Milnor number is
invariant
under right equivalence and
the Tjurina number is
invariant
under contact equivalence (see
[
GLS25
, Lemma 1.2.10]
).

It is a non-trivial theorem, using methods from complex analysis, which
cannot be extended to other fields, that for

the
Milnor number of a complex analytic hypersurface is indeed invariant under contact
equivalence (see
[
Gr75

), and it is even a topological

[

]
). Using the

the result for contact equivalence can be
generalised to arbitrary real valued fields of
characteristic zero. This was first proved in

for formal power series. For convergent power series we need an extra argument, which is covered by Lemma

Lemma 1.3
.

(1)

Let
K
be a real valued field and
I\subseteq K\{x\}
an ideal. Then

\dim_{K}K\{x\}/I=\dim_{K}K[[x]]/IK[[x]].

In particular,

\dim_{K}K\{x\}/I<\infty\iff\dim_{K}K[[x]]/IK[[x]]<\infty
and if this holds, then
K\{x\}/I=K[[x]]/IK[[x]]
.

(2)

Let
K\subseteq L
be a field extension of arbitrary fields and
I\subseteq K[[x]]
an ideal. Then

\dim_{K}K[[x]]/I=\dim_{L}L[[x]]/IL[[x]].

In particular

\dim_{K}K[[x]]/I<\infty\iff\dim_{L}L[[x]]/IL[[x]]<\infty

and then we have
(K[[x]]/I)\otimes_{K}L=L[[x]]/IL[[x]].

If
K\subseteq L
are real valued fields (with not necessarily compatible valuations) and
I\subseteq K\{x\}
. Then we deduce from (1) and (2) that

\dim_{K}K\{x\}/I=\dim_{L}L\{x\}/IL\{x\}.

Proof.

(1) We have
A:=K\{x\}/I\subseteq\hat{A}=K[[x]]/IK[[x]]=\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}K\{x\}/I+\langle x\rangle^{m}
, and hence

\dim_{K}\hat{A}<\infty\Longrightarrow\dim_{K}A<\infty
.
Conversely, if
\dim_{K}A<\infty
then
\langle x\rangle^{m}\subseteq I
for
m\geq
some
m_{0}
. This implies
\hat{A}=A
and the claim follows.

(2) Denote by
\langle x\rangle
and
\bar{\langle}x\rangle
, the ideals generated by
x_{1},...,x_{n}
in
K[[x]]
and
K[x]
respectively.

For
f\in K[[x]]
let
\jet_{l}(f)\in K[x]
be the
l
-jet of
f
. Then

f=\jet_{l-1}(f)+h
, with
h\in\langle x\rangle^{l}K[[x]]
. We set

\jet_{l}(I):=\langle\jet_{l}(f)\mid f\in I\rangle_{K[x]}\subseteq K[x].

It is easy to see that
I+\langle x\rangle^{l}=\jet_{l-1}(I)K[[x]]+\langle x\rangle^{l}
. Using this we get

\displaystyle K[[x]]/I+\langle x\rangle^{m}=

\displaystyle(K[[x]]/\langle x\rangle^{m})/(I+\langle x\rangle^{m})/\langle x\rangle^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(K[x]/\bar{\langle}x\rangle^{m})/(\jet_{m-1}(I)+\bar{\langle}x\rangle^{m})/\bar{\langle}x\rangle^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K[x]/\jet_{m-1}(I)+\bar{\langle}x\rangle^{m}.

Analogous formulas hold for
L[[x]]
instead of

. It follows that

\displaystyle(K[[x]]/I+\langle x\rangle^{m})\otimes_{K}L=

\displaystyle(K[x]/\jet_{m-1}(I)+\bar{\langle}x\rangle^{m})\otimes_{K}L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L[x]/\jet_{m-1}(I)L[x]+\langle x\rangle^{m}L[x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L[[x]]/IL[[x]]+\langle x\rangle^{m}L[[x]].

If
\dim_{K}K[[x]]/I<\infty
, then
\langle x\rangle^{m}\subseteq I
for some

and hence
\langle x\rangle^{m}L[[x]]\subseteq IL[[x]]
, showing

(K[[x]]/I)\otimes_{K}L=L[[x]]/IL[[x]]
and

\dim_{L}L[[x]]/IL[[x]]=\dim_{K}K[[x]]/I<\infty
.

If
\dim_{L}L[[x]]/IL[[x]]<\infty
then
\langle x\rangle^{l}L[[x]]\subseteq IL[[x]]
for all

and it follows that
\dim_{L}L[[x]]/IL[[x]]=\dim_{L}(K[[x]]/I+\langle x\rangle^{l})\otimes_{K}L=\dim_{K}(K[[x]]/I+\langle x\rangle^{l})
for

With

and
\mathfrak{m}=I+\langle x\rangle/I
its maximal ideal, it follows that

is an isomorphism and hence

, i.e.,

\langle x\rangle^{m}\subseteq I

.
∎

Proposition 1.4

Let

be a real valued field of characteristic zero
and

.
If
f\overset{c}{\sim}\ g
, then
\mu(f)=\mu(g)
.

Proof.

Since the Milnor number is invariant under right equivalence, it
suffices to show that
\mu(f)=\mu(u\cdot f)
for any unit

u\in K\{x\}^{*}
. If
A
denotes the subset of
K

containing the coefficients of

f
and all partial
derivatives
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}
of

is at most countable infinite and
\{f,u,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}(A)\{x\}
.
Since
\Char(K)=0
and since

is a field
extension of uncountable transcendence degree
the field
{\mathbb{Q}}(A)
is isomorphic to a subfield
{L}

of the field
{\mathbb{C}}
of complex numbers. The valuation on
L
, which is inherited from
{\mathbb{Q}}(A)
, may however not be the usual valuation on
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Nevertheless, by Lemma
1.3

\dim_{L}({L}\{x\}/j(f))=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbb{C}}\{x\}/j(f))
and

\dim_{L}({L}\{x\}/j(uf))=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbb{C}}\{x\}/j(uf))
. Since the right hand sides coincide by
[

]
, the proposition follows.
∎

In positive characteristic this result does not hold;
e.g. if
\Char(K)=p>0
and

\mu(f)=\infty

while the contact equivalent series

has Milnor

In complex singularity theory the Milnor number of a power series is finite if and only if the Tjurina
number is so (see

). This fact can also be
generalised to arbitrary fields of

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero
and

.
Then
\mu(f)<\infty

\tau(f)<\infty

Proof.

Let

be the set of

1.4

is isomorphic to a subfield

\tau(f)=\dim_{L}(L\{x\}/tj(f))=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbb{C}}\{x\}/tj(f)).

Using the result for

, we get that
\tau(f)
is finite if and only if

∎

For fields of positive characteristic this is false. For

We discuss now finite determinacy of hypersurfaces w.r.t. right and contact equivalence.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

for some integer

For

defines an isolated singularity. In fact, we have:

is right

-determined.

In particular, the right determinacy is at most

\mu(f)+1
.

(b)

If

is contact
k
-determined.

In particular, the contact determinacy is at most
\tau(f)+1
.

Proof.

The proof of
[

, Theorem 1.2.23]

. Another proof (for a more general statement) is given by Kerner

∎

Before we generalize these bounds to convergent power series over real valued fields in Theorem
1.10
, we mention the following important approximation theorem of Grauert, which is used in the proof.

We consider four sets of variables,
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})
,
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{p})
and
Z=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{q})
.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
K
be a real valued field,
I\subseteq K\{s\}
an ideal and
\mathfrak{m}:=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\rangle
the maximal ideal of
K\{s\}
. Consider an element
F=(F_{1},\ldots,F_{N})\in K\{x,s,Y,Z\}^{N}
.

(1) A solution of order
e
of the equation
F\equiv 0\;{\rm mod}\;I

is a pair

(y,z)\in K[s]^{p}\times K\{x\}[s]^{q}
such that

F(x,s,y(s),z(x,s))\equiv 0\quad{\rm mod}\;(I+\mathfrak{m}^{e+1})\cdot K\{x,s\}^{N}.

(2) An analytic solution of the equation
F\equiv 0
mod
I
is a
pair
(y,z)\in K\{s\}^{p}\times K\{x,s\}^{q}

such that

F(x,s,y(s),z(x,s))\equiv 0\quad{\rm mod}\;I\cdot K\{x,s\}^{N},

We can now formulate the approximation theorem.

Theorem 1.9

(Grauert’s Approximation Theorem for real valued fields)
.

Let
K
be a real valued field,
I\subseteq K\{s\}
an ideal and
F=(F_{1},\ldots,F_{N})\in K\{x,s,Y,Z\}^{N}
.
Let
e_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and suppose that the system of equations

F(x,s,Y,Z)\equiv 0\;{\rm mod}\;I

has
a solution
(Y,Z)=(y^{(e_{0})},z^{(e_{0})})
of order
e_{0}
.

Suppose, moreover,
that for all
e\geq e_{0}
every solution

(y^{(e)},z^{(e)})
of order
e
with

y^{(e)}\equiv y^{(e_{0})}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}^{e_{0}+1}
,
and
z^{(e)}\equiv z^{(e_{0})}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}^{e_{0}+1}
,
extends to a solution
(y^{(e)}+u^{(e)},z^{(e)}+v^{(e)})
of order
e+1
,
with

u^{(e)}\in K[s]^{p}
and
v^{(e)}\in K\{x\}[s]^{q}
homogeneous of
degree
e+1
in
s
.

Then the system of equations
F\equiv 0
mod
I
has
an analytic solution
(y(s),z(x,s))
, with
y\equiv y^{(e_{0})}
mod

{\mathfrak{m}}^{e_{0}+1}
, and
z\equiv z^{(e_{0})}
mod
{\mathfrak{m}}^{e_{0}+1}
.

The proof of this theorem was first given by Grauert for

K\{x\}={\mathbb{C}}\{x\}
in his fundamental Inventiones paper
[
Gr72
]
, where he proved the existence of a semiuniversal deformation of any complex analytic isolated singularity. The theorem was generalized to power series over a real valued field in the above form in

[
GP25
]
. The proof is quite complex, so
we refer to the proof of Theorem 4.3 in
[
GP25
]
.

Theorem 1.10

( Determinacy bound in characteristic
\geq 0
)
.

Let
0\not=f\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}\subseteq K\{x\}
,
K
an arbitrary real valued field, and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

1.

If
\mathfrak{m}^{k+2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}\cdot j(f)
, then
f
is
right
(2k-\ord(f)+2)
-determined.

2.

If
\mathfrak{m}^{k+2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\cdot\langle f\rangle+\mathfrak{m}^{2}\cdot j(f)
, then
f
is contact
(2k-\ord(f)+2)
-determined.

If
0\not=f\in\mathfrak{m}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{m}^{2}
then
\ord(f)=1
and

f
is right 1-determined with
f\overset{r}{\sim}x_{1}
by the implicit function theorem.

Proof.

The proof was first given in
[
BGM12
, Theorem 3]
for formal power series. We give here a (shorter) proof for
K\{x\}
using of Grauert’s approximation theorem.
We prove only statement 1., the proof of statement 2. is similar.

Let
N\geq 2k-\ord(f)+2=:e_{0}
and assume that
f-g\in\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}
. We have to prove that there exists an automorphism
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n})

of
K\{x\}
such that
f(\varphi)=g
. We consider the equation

F(x,y)=f(y)-g(x)=0,\ y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}),

and want to apply Theorem
1.9
to the equation
F=0
. Since
f-g\in\mathfrak{m}^{e_{0}+1}
,
this equation has the solution
\varphi^{(e_{0})}(x_{i})=x_{i}
of order
e_{0}
:

F(x,\varphi^{(e_{0})})=0\mod\mathfrak{m}^{e_{0}+1}.

Let
\varphi^{(e)}
be a solution of
F(x,y)=0
of order
e\geq e_{0}
such that
\varphi^{(e)}\equiv\varphi^{(e_{0})}\mod\mathfrak{m}^{e_{0}+1}
, that is,

\varphi^{(e)}(x_{i})=x_{i}+h_{i}
with
h_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}^{e_{0}+1}.

By assumption we have

\mathfrak{m}^{k+2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}j(f)
, hence
k\geq\ord(f)-1
, and

F(x,\varphi^{(e)})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{e+1}
.
Since
\mathfrak{m}^{e+1}=\mathfrak{m}^{e-k-1}\mathfrak{m}^{k+2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{e-k+1}j(f)
and
e-k-1\geq e_{0}-k-1=k-\ord(f)+1\geq 0
,
we can write

F(x,\varphi^{(e)})=f(\varphi^{(e)})-g(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}(x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(x)

with
b_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}^{e-k+1}
. Now define

\varphi^{(e+1)}
by
\varphi^{(e+1)}_{i}=\varphi^{(e)}_{i}-b_{i}
. We obtain by Taylor’s formula (Remark
1.11
)

f(\varphi^{(e+1)})=f(\varphi^{(e)}-b)=f(\varphi^{(e)})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}(x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(\varphi^{(e)})+H(x),

with

\ord(H)\geq\ord(f)+2(\ord(b)-1)\geq\ord(f)+2(e-k)\geq e+(e_{0}-2k+\ord(f))=e+2.

Moreover

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(\varphi^{(e)})=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(x+h)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{i}}(x)+G(x)
.
We can estimate
(using
\ord(h_{j})\geq e_{0}+1
and
\ord(b_{i})\geq e-k+1
):

\ord(b_{i}G)\geq(e-k+1)+\ord(f)-1+2e_{0}=e+(\ord(f)+e_{0}-k)+e_{0}=e+k+2+e_{0}\geq e+2

and

\ord(b_{i}h_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{i}}(x)\geq e+k+2\geq e+2
.

This implies
f(\varphi^{(e+1)})=f(\varphi^{(e)})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}(x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(x)
+ terms of order
\geq e+2
and thus

F(x,\varphi^{(e+1)})=0\mod\mathfrak{m}^{e+2}
, i.e.,

\varphi^{e+1}
is an extension of the solution
\varphi^{(e)}
of order
e+1
. We can apply
Grauert’s Approximation Theorem
1.9
and obtain a convergent solution
\varphi
of
F=0
.
This proves statement 1.
∎

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
K
be any real valued field and
0\not=f\in\mathfrak{m}\cdot K\{x\}
.

1.

f
is finitely right determined iff
\mu(f)
is finite.

2.

f
is finitely contact determined iff
\tau(f)
is finite.

Proof.

According to Corollary
1.13
we only need to show that finite determinacy implies that
\mu(f)
or
\tau(f)
are finite. The theorem is stated in
[
GPh2
, Theorem 4.8]
for
K[[x]]
with a rather short proof. Therefore we prove it here in full detail.

By Lemma
1.3
we have
\mu(f)=\dim_{K}K[[x]]/j(f)
and
\tau(f)=\dim_{K}K[[x]]/tj(f)
.

1. Let
f
be right
k
-determined. The easiest way to see that
\mu(f)
is finite is perhaps by using the theory of Gröbner bases.
By finite determinacy we may assume that
f
is a polynomial of some degree
d
(
\geq k
).
Set
g_{N}:=x_{1}^{N}+...+x_{n}^{N}
,
N>d
, and let
\Char(K)\nmid N
if
\Char(K)>0
. Then
f\overset{r}{\sim}f+g_{N}
and hence
\mu(f)=\mu(f+g_{N})
. The Jacobian ideal of
f+g_{N}
is

j(f+g_{N})=\langle Nx_{1}^{N-1}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},...,Nx_{n}^{N-1}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle\subseteq K[x].

Now choose a global degree ordering on
K[x]
(see
[
GP07
]
). Since the degree of
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}

is
\leq d-1<N-1
, it follows that
x_{i}^{N-1}
is the leading monomial of

Nx_{i}^{N-1}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}

with respect to a global degree ordering. Hence
x_{i}^{N-1}
is contained in the leading ideal
L(j(f+g_{N}))
of
j(f+g_{N})
for
i=1,...,n
and obviously
\dim_{K}K[x]/L(j(f+g_{N}))<\infty
.
By
[
GP07
, Corollary 7.5.6]
we have

\dim_{K}K[x]/j(f+g_{N})=\dim_{K}K[x]/L(j(f+g_{N}))<\infty.

On the other hand

\dim_{K}K[[x]]/j(f+g_{N})K[[x]]\leq\dim_{K}K[x]/j(f+g_{N}).

To see this, we may assume that
K
is algebraically closed, since passing to the algebraic closure does not change the dimension by Lemma
1.3
. Now, for every ideal
I\subseteq K[x]
with
\dim_{K}K[x]/I<\infty

the the variety
V(I)
consists of finitely many maximal ideals of the form
\langle x_{1}-p^{i}_{1},...,x_{n}-p^{i}_{n}\rangle
,

p^{i}=(p^{i}_{1},...,p^{i}_{n})\in K^{n}
,
i=1,...,s
, with, say,
p^{1}=0
. We have (see also
[
GP07
, Appendix A.9]
)

\dim_{K}K[x]/I=\dim_{K}K[[x]]/IK[[x]]+\sum^{s}_{i=2}\dim_{K}K[[x-p^{i}]]/IK[[x-p^{i}]].

Therefore

\mu(f)=\dim_{K}K[[x]]/j(f+g_{N})K[[x]]<\infty
.

2. Let
f
be contact
k
-determined. We use a different argument to show that
\tau(f)<\infty
.
For every fixed
0\neq t\in K
we have
tf\overset{c}{\sim}f
and hence
tf
is contact
k
-determined.
Choose the polynomial
g_{N}
as in 1. and set

f_{t}:=g_{N}+tf\in K[t]\{x\}.

Then
tj(f_{t})=\langle\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x_{n}},f_{t}\rangle\subseteq K\{x\}
and

f_{t}\overset{c}{\sim}tf\overset{c}{\sim}f
. We get

\tau(f)=\tau(f_{t})=\dim_{K}K\{x\}/tj(f_{t})\text{ for }t\neq 0.

By the semicontinuity of
\tau
, Proposition
1.16

below,
there exists an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq K
of 0 such that

\tau(f_{t})\leq\tau(f_{0})=\tau(g_{N})<\infty\text{ for }t\in U.

As in 1. we may assume that

is algebraically closed. Then

is infinite and hence

is not empty and for

we have

\tau(f)=\tau(f_{t})<\infty
.

(Semicontinuity of

The proof is given in

[

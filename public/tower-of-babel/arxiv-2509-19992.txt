Breakdown of symmetry constraint in Floquet topological superconductor
Ming-Jian Gao, Jun-Hong An

Abstract
Topological superconductor is regarded as an ideal candidate for topological quantum computing due to its ability to simulate the enigmatic Majorana fermions that satisfy non-Abelian statistics. Previous studies revealed that symmetry exerts an unbreakable constraint on the existence, classes, and orders of Majorana modes. It severely limits the controllability and application of Majorana modes. Here, we propose a Floquet-engineering method to break this symmetry-imposed constraint on topological phases. By applying periodic driving on a system belonging to a symmetry class that prohibits the existence of first-order topological phases, we find that rich first-order Majorana modes are created. Interestingly, exotic hybrid-order topological superconductors with coexisting first-order Majorana boundary modes and second-order Majorana corner modes not only in two different quasienergy gaps but also in one single gap are generated at ease by the periodic driving. Refreshing the prevailing understanding of symmetry constraint on topological phases, our result opens an avenue for the creation of exotic topological superconductors without altering symmetries. It greatly expands the scope of the fabricated materials that host topological superconductor.

III
Breakdown of symmetry constraint in Floquet system

To break the symmetry constraint on the topological superconductor, we propose a Floquet-engineering protocol by periodically driving
\rho
as

\begin{split}\rho(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{1},&t\in[nT,nT+T_{1})\\
\rho_{2},&t\in[nT+T_{1},nT+T_{1}+T_{2})\\
\rho_{1},&t\in[nT+T_{1}+T_{2},(n+1)T)\end{array}\right.,\end{split}

(3)

where
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
T=2T_{1}+T_{2}
is the driving period. Such a stair-like periodic driving has been widely used to generate Floquet topological phases
Mi et al. 2022
;
Yang et al. 2022a
;
Gao et al. 2024
;
Shi et al. 2024
;
Jin et al. 2025
. Given the inherent non-conservation of energy, the periodic system fails to exhibit a well-defined energy spectrum. It is necessary to employ the Floquet theorem to characterize the topological properties of the periodic system. According to the Floquet theorem, we define an effective Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})=\frac{i}{T}\ln U_{T}(\textbf{k})
using the one-periodic evolution operator
U_{T}(\textbf{k})=\mathbb{T}e^{-i\int^{T}_{0}\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k},t)dt}
, where
\mathbb{T}
denotes the time-ordering operator. The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})
are known as quasienergies. The topological properties of the periodic system are defined in the quasienergy spectrum
Sambe 1973
;
Chen et al. 2015
;
Yao et al. 2017
. In our periodic system, the effective Hamiltonian is
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})=\frac{i}{T}\ln[e^{-i\mathcal{H}_{1}(\textbf{k})T_{1}}e^{-i\mathcal{H}_{2}(\textbf{k})T_{2}}e^{-i\mathcal{H}_{1}(\textbf{k})T_{1}}]
, where
\mathcal{H}_{l}(\textbf{k})
is
\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k})
after replacing
\rho
with
\rho_{l}
. We find that our periodic system retains the particle-hole
\mathcal{C}
, time-reversal
\mathcal{T}
, chiral
\mathcal{S}
, and inversion
P
symmetries, satisfying
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})\mathcal{T}^{-1}=\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}_{\textrm{eff}}(-\textbf{k})
,
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})\mathcal{S}^{-1}=-\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})
,
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}^{\dagger}(-\textbf{k})
, and
P\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})P^{-1}=\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(-\textbf{k})
. Therefore, the symmetry class of our periodic system remains the same as that of its static counterpart. On the other hand, in contrast to the static system, the topological phases in the periodic system are present not only in the zero quasienergy gap, but also in the
\pi/T
one. This makes the topological characterization of the static system insufficient for the periodic system. We develop the following method to fully characterize the topological properties of the periodic system. Making a unitary transformation
\mathcal{L}=e^{i\mathcal{H}_{2}(\textbf{k})T_{2}/2}e^{i\mathcal{H}_{1}(\textbf{k})T_{1}}
to the evolution operator
U_{T}
, we obtain a new effective Hamiltonian
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})=\frac{i}{T}\ln[\mathcal{L}U_{T}\mathcal{L}^{-1}]
, which shares the same quasienergy spectrum with
\mathcal{H}_{\text{eff}}({\bf k})
. Then, two multipole chiral numbers
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
can be defined in
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\textrm{eff}}(\textbf{k})
in the same manner as Eq. (
2
). The second-order topological phases in the zero and
\pi/T
quasienergy gaps of our periodic system are characterized by
N_{0}=(N_{1}+N_{2})/2
and
N_{\pi/T}=(N_{1}-N_{2})/2

Discussion and conclusion

It is important to note that the step-like driving scheme used in our work is intended to guarantee that the periodic system maintains the symmetry classes as the static system. The driving scheme may be generalized to other schemes that satisfy this symmetry requirement. In recent years, simulation of Majorana modes has been achieved in various platforms, such as condensed matter systems

In summary, we have proposed a Floquet-engineering scheme to break the symmetry constraint of topological superconductors. It is found that, by applying a periodic driving on a topological superconductor system belonging to the symmetry classes BDI and CII
′
, we can create first-order Majorana boundary modes without changing any symmetry. We have further discovered that, after releasing the system from the symmetry constraint, diverse exotic hybrid-order topological superconductor phases with coexisting first-order Majorana boundary modes and second-order Majorana corner modes not only in two different quasienergy gaps but also in one single gap are present in our periodically driven system. Refreshing one’s general belief on the classification rule of the topological superconductors set by symmetries, our Floquet-engineering scheme opens a door to explore exotic topological superconductors and prompts the simulation and application of Majorana modes, in particular with regard to overcoming the limitations set by unchangeable symmetries of the fabricated materials.

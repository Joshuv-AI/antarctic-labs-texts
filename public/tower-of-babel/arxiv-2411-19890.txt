Reverse-type Data Processing Inequality
Paula Belzig, Li Gao, Graeme Smith, Peixue Wu

Abstract
The quantum data processing inequality asserts that two quantum states become harder to distinguish when a noisy channel is applied. On the other hand, a reverse quantum data processing inequality characterizes whether distinguishability is preserved after the application of a noisy channel. In this work, we explore these concepts through contraction and expansion coefficients of the relative entropy of quantum channels. Our first result is that quantum channels with an input dimension greater than or equal to the output dimension do not have a non-zero expansion coefficient, which means that they cannot admit a reverse data-processing inequality. We propose a comparative approach by introducing a relative expansion coefficient, to assess how one channel expands relative entropy compared to another. We show that this relative expansion coefficient is positive for three important classes of quantum channels: depolarizing channels, generalized dephasing channels, and amplitude damping channels. As an application, we give the first rigorous construction of level-1 less noisy quantum channels that are non-degradable.

Introduction

The noisy nature of a quantum channel is reflected in the fact that measures of distance in quantum information decrease when the channel is applied. This property of an information measure is referred to as monotonicity under quantum operations, or data processing inequality (DPI).
One notable quantum information measure with wide applications is the relative entropy of two quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
,

D(\rho||\sigma)=\begin{cases}\tr\Big(\rho\big(\log(\rho)-\log(\sigma)\big)\Big)&\text{ if }\text{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\text{supp}(\sigma),\\
\infty&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

The DPI for quantum relative entropy states that for any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
,

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq D(\rho\|\sigma),\ \forall\rho,\sigma.

Since the relative entropy quantifies how well a quantum state
\rho
can be distinguished from
\sigma
in the context of quantum hypothesis testing (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
]
), the DPI implies that two quantum states can only become less distinguishable after a channel is applied. The DPI of quantum relative entropy was first proven in
[
3
]
(see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
for later alterative proofs) and is now a fundamental tool in quantum information processing.

Given a channel
\mathcal{N}
, its contraction coefficient
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
quantifies to what extent the DPI can be improved for this channel. The contraction coefficient is defined as the smallest constant
\eta_{\mathcal{N}}
such that,

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq\eta_{\mathcal{N}}D(\rho\|\sigma),\quad\forall\rho,\sigma

or equivalently

\eta_{\mathcal{N}}:=\sup_{\rho\neq\sigma}\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)||\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{D(\rho||\sigma)}.

(1)

The contraction coefficient
\eta_{\mathcal{N}}
characterizes how much harder it becomes to distinguish quantum states after the channel
\mathcal{N}
is applied. By DPI, it is clear that
\eta_{\mathcal{N}}\in[0,1]
for any channel
\mathcal{N}
. If
\eta_{\mathcal{N}}<1
, the distinguishability of
\rho
and
\sigma
decays exponentially fast under the repeated applications of the same channel. In this case, we say that
\mathcal{N}
obeys
strong data processing inequality
(SDPI).

On the other hand, one can also ask whether a channel
\mathcal{N}
must necessarily destroy all distinguishability in the worst case, or if it preserves at least a fixed fraction of information. To capture this, the
expansion coefficient
was introduced in
[
13
,
14
,
11
]
:

\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}:=\inf_{\rho\neq\sigma}\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)||\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{D(\rho||\sigma)}\in[0,1].

(2)

If the expansion coefficient of a channel is strictly greater than 0, it must necessarily preserve some information about the states. This can be interpreted as a reverse data processing inequality for
\mathcal{N}
(in direct analogy with reverse Doeblin coefficients
[
11
]
, reverse Pinsker inequality
[
15
,
16
]
, and reverse log-Sobolev inequality
[
17
]
). Operationally, since relative entropy governs exponential error-decay rates in quantum hypothesis testing (via Stein’s lemma, see
[
18

\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}
measures the channel’s worst-case ability to preserve information.

Moreover, a positive expansion coefficient guarantees exponential convergence of iterative algorithms such as quantum Blahut–Arimoto method for channel-capacity computation
[
13
]
. In this work, we show that requiring
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}>0
imposes strong dimension-dependent constraints, and we prove that beyond certain input-output size thresholds, one must have
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}=0
:

Theorem 1.1

(c.f. Theorem
3.1
)
.

For a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}:\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
with
d_{A}\geq d_{B}
, we have

\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}=\begin{cases}1,\quad d_{A}=d_{B}\text{ and }\ \mathcal{N}(\rho)=U\rho U^{\dagger}\text{ for some unitary U},\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3)

For channels with greater output than input dimension, the same can not be true because it is easy to construct flagged channels such as erasure channels with
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}>0
.

The above result suggests that the expansion coefficient
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}
does not serve as a reliable standalone measure of information preservation.
In this work, we propose a comparative approach, where two quantum channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}:=\inf_{\rho\neq\sigma,\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)}\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))}\ ,\ \ \ \eta_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}:=\sup_{\rho\neq\sigma,\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)}\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))}.

(4)

These two are essentially the same definition by noting that
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}=\eta_{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}}^{-1}

The relative contraction coefficient

As an application, we utilize the framework of contraction coefficients and relative expansion coefficients to construct quantum channels that are (level-1) less noisy but not degradable. Roughly speaking, a quantum channel is considered (level-1) less noisy if the information in the output system is not less than the information contained in the environment system when we allow the system to couple with an arbitrary classical system (see Section
2
for the rigorous definition). Our construction is as follows:

Theorem 1.3

(c.f. Proposition
6.4
)
.

Suppose

\Psi_{p,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(\rho)=p\mathinner{|{0}\rangle\,\langle{0}|}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}}(\rho)+(1-p)\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}(\rho)

•

\gamma_{1}<\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle p\in\left[\frac{1}{1+\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{1-\gamma_{2}}}(1-\eta_{\mathcal{A}_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}}})},1\right],\quad\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}=\frac{1-2\gamma_{1}}{1-\gamma_{1}}.

•

\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}>1
and
\gamma_{2}<\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle p\in\left[0,\frac{\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}},\mathcal{A}_{1-\gamma_{1}}}(1-\eta_{\mathcal{A}_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}}})}{1+\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}},\mathcal{A}_{1-\gamma_{1}}}(1-\eta_{\mathcal{A}_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}}})}\right],\quad\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}=\frac{1-2\gamma_{2}}{1-\gamma_{2}}.

A concrete example of a less noisy but not degradable channel is

for parameters

,

Our motivation stems from a central problem in quantum information theory: determining the capacities of various quantum channels. While capacities are of fundamental importance, their computation is notoriously intractable because it often requires infinite regularization, or tensorization
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
. Degradable channels were introduced in
[
25
]
as the first class of channels whose quantum capacity does not require regularization, making them computable via optimization. Since then, it has been shown that even weaker conditions than degradability can preserve additivity, allowing capacities to be computed through optimization
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
.

Recently, a hierarchy of “less noisy” channel classes was introduced in
[
10
]
. However, establishing clear separations between these classes remains an open problem. In fact, no known example demonstrates a channel that is “less noisy” but fails to be degradable, even under the weakest notion of “less noisy”.

We address this problem by providing the first explicit construction of a less noisy but non-degradable channel that can be rigorously verified. Since the class of less noisy channels coincides with those having concave coherent information, our construction also confirms the existence of non-degradable channels with concave coherent information. The tools we introduce also provide a potential way to show the existence of a non-degradable channel that is informationally degradable as introduced in
[
27
]
. This is another characteristic of a quantum channel that lies in between less noisy and degradable and implies additivity of capacity. A key insight is that tensorizing a channel with the identity on an ancillary system performs better than repeatedly tensoring the channel with itself, and this perspective underlies our proposed extensions.

As a summary, we highlight our main results in the following three aspects:

1.

Expansion coefficients
. We give a systematic study of the relative entropy expansion coefficient corresponding to a reverse data processing inequality. The contraction coefficient, often called strong data processing constant, has been well-studied in both classical and quantum setting over decades. The expansion coefficient, in contrast, has not been much considered in the literature. We fill this gap by giving the first systematic treatment of the expansion coefficient.

2.

No-go Theorem
3.1
. Under the assumption that the input dimension of the channel is not less than the output dimension, we prove the expansion coefficient with respect to relative entropy must be zero. This leads us to study the more meaningful relative expansion coefficients, and obtain non-trivial relative expansion coefficients between depolarizing, dephasing, and amplitude-damping channels. This is in sharp contrast to the expansion coefficient with respect to the trace distance
[
29
]
.

3.

Less-noisy but non-degradable channels
. We give a concrete and rigorous construction of less noisy but non-degradable channel using the relative expansion coefficient. To our knowledge, this is the first concrete and rigorous example of such a separation, filling a gap in the literature.

The rest of this manuscript is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly review necessary preliminaries on quantum channels and degradability. In Section
3
, we prove that if the dimension of the input system of a quantum channel is not less than that of the output system, then the expansion coefficient is zero. Section
4
presents systematic tools for analyzing the relative expansion coefficients of two quantum channels. Section
5
is devoted to explicit estimates of relative contraction and expansion coefficients of three important classes of quantum channels. We then present the construction of non-degradable channels that are less noisy in Section
6
. Section
7
concludes the manuscript with a discussion on open problems.

Preliminaries

2.1
Quantum channel and its representation

In this work, we denote
\mathcal{H}
as a Hilbert space of finite dimension, and
\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}
as the dual space of
\mathcal{H}
.
\ket{\psi}
denotes a vector in
\mathcal{H}
and
\bra{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}
a dual vector. For two Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B}
, the space of linear operators from
\mathcal{H}_{A}
to
\mathcal{H}_{B}
is denoted as
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B})\cong\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}^{\dagger}
. When
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\mathcal{H}_{B}=\mathcal{H}
, we write
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
shortly as
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
. The set of density operators (positive semidefinite with trace one) on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted as
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
. The set of pure states (rank 1 projections) on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted as
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
. Denote
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
as the class of super-operators which consists of linear maps from
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
to
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
. A quantum channel
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
is a super-operator which is completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP).

Let
\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B},\mathcal{H}_{E}
be three Hilbert spaces of dimensions
d_{A},d_{B},d_{E}
respectively.
An isometry
V:\mathcal{H}_{A}\to\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}
, meaning
V^{\dagger}V=I_{A}
(identity operator on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
), generates a pair of quantum channels
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}^{c})
, defined by

\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\tr_{E}(V\rho V^{{\dagger}}),\quad\mathcal{N}^{c}(\rho)=\tr_{B}(V\rho V^{{\dagger}}),

(5)

where
\tr_{E}
is the partial trace operator given by
\tr_{E}(X_{B}\otimes X_{E})=\tr(X_E)X_{B}
. It is known from Stinespring theorem that every quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
can be expressed as above, and
the pair
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}^{c})
is called the
complementary channel
of the other.

The operator-sum representation of a quantum channel is called
Kraus representation
:

\mathcal{N}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}XA_{i}^{\dagger},\quad X\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),

(6)

where
A_{i}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B})
are called Kraus operators of
\mathcal{N}
.
Another representation of a super-operator in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
is its Choi–Jamiołkowski operator. Given an orthonormal basis
\{\ket{i}\}_{i=0}^{d_{A}-1}
of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
, a maximally entangled state on
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}
is given by

\displaystyle\ket{\Phi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{A}-1}\ket{i}\otimes\ket{i}.

The
(unnormalized) Choi–Jamiołkowski
operator of
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
is a bipartite operator in
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
given by

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}=d_{A}(\id_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\mathinner{|{\Phi}\rangle\,\langle{\Phi}|})=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d_{A}-1}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle\,\langle{j}|}\otimes\mathcal{N}(\mathinner{|{i}\rangle\,\langle{j}|}).

(7)

A quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
is completely positive if and only if its Choi–Jamiołkowski operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
is a positive operator in
B(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
, and
\mathcal{N}
is trace-preserving if and only if
\tr_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}})=I_{A}
. The rank of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
is called the
Kraus rank
of the channel
\mathcal{N}
, which indicates the minimum number of Kraus operators to represent
\mathcal{N}
in (
6
).

For two completely positive superoperator
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
, we say
\mathcal{N}\leq_{cp}\mathcal{M}
if
\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{N}
is completely positive. This is equivalent to

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{M}},

(8)

where
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{M}}
means
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
is positive semidefinite.

2.2
Degradable and less noisy channels

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a quantum channel and
\mathcal{N}^{c}
be its complementary channel.
We say that
\mathcal{N}
is
degradable
if there is a quantum channel
\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}^{c}
. That is, one can process the output system to get all the information about the environment system. Similarly, if there exists a quantum channel
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\circ\mathcal{N}^{c}=\mathcal{N}
, then we say that
\mathcal{N}
is
anti-degradable
.

Given any additional quantum system
\mathcal{H}_{V}
and a bipartite density operator
\rho_{VA}
on
V\otimes A
, denote

\displaystyle\rho_{VB}=(\id_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{V})}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\rho_{VA}),\quad\rho_{VE}=(\id_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{V})}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{c})(\rho_{VA}).

We say
\mathcal{N}
is
informationally degradable
(introduced in
[
27
]
) if for any quantum system
V
and bipartite density operator
\rho_{VA}
, we have

\displaystyle I(V;B)_{(\id_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{V})}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\rho_{VA})}\geq I(V;E)_{(\id_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{V})}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{c})(\rho_{VA})},

where
I(V;B)=S(V)+S(B)-S(VB)
is the quantum mutual information and
S(V)=-\tr(\rho_V\log(\rho_V))
denotes the von Neumann entropy of reduced density
\rho_{V}
(and similarly defined for other terms). We say
\mathcal{N}
is
less noisy
, if for any classical-quantum state
\rho_{\mathcal{X}A}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\mathinner{|{x}\rangle\,\langle{x}|}\otimes\rho_{A}^{x}
, we have

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{X};B)_{(\id_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\rho_{VA})}\geq I(\mathcal{X};E)_{(\id_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\rho_{VA})}.

In the following, we will often write
I(V;B)

when the underlying state is clear from the context.

Note that there exist two different notions of less noisy quantum channels in the literature. One, which we are exclusively using in this work and in the definition above, refers to the classical-quantum mutual information with respect to a single application of the channel
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{c}

[
30
,
10
]
(which can also be called
level-1 less noisy
). This notion characterizes the class of quantum channels with concave coherent information. In fact, for a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
and an input state
\rho_{A}
with purification
\ket{\psi}_{A^{\prime}A}
, the coherent information is defined as the coherent information of the bipartite state
\rho_{A^{\prime}B}=(\id_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\,\langle{\psi}|}_{A^{\prime}A})
:

I_{c}(\mathcal{N},\rho_{A}):=I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\rho_{A^{\prime}B}}=S(B)-S(A^{\prime}B)=S(B)-S(E).

(9)

Then, concavity of this quantity means for any ensemble of states
\{p_{x},\rho_{A}^{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{c}(\mathcal{N},\sum_{x}p_{x}\rho_{A}^{x})\geq\sum_{x}p_{x}I_{c}(\mathcal{N},\rho_{A}^{x}),

which is equivalent to
I(\mathcal{X};B)\geq I(\mathcal{X};E)
.

Another notion refers to a regularized version for many copies of
\mathcal{N}
introduced in
[
26
]
(sometimes called
regularized less noisy
), which implies that the private information and coherent information are weakly additive for this channel.

It is clear that informational degradablity implies less noisy by restricting the general bipartite density operators
\rho_{VA}
to be a classical-quantum state. Moreover, via data processing inequality, degradablity implies informational degradablility. By this reasoning, any channel that is degradable is also less noisy. However, to the best of our knowledge, it was an open question whether there exists a level-1 less noisy quantum channel that is not degradable

1

1

It remains an open question whether there exists a regularized less noisy channel which is not degradable.
, which we resolve in this work. To this end, we propose a framework in Section

for constructing such examples and give an explicit example in terms of amplitude damping channels. This framework may further be used to construct examples of non-degradable channels that are informationally degradable.

3
Impossibility of a reverse data processing inequality for non-unitary channels

In this section, we show that a reverse data processing inequality cannot hold for non-unitary channels
\mathcal{N}:\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
with dimensions
d_{A}\geq d_{B}
. More precisely, we show that expansion coefficient
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}
in this setting generically equals to zero.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
be a quantum channel such that
d_{A}\geq d_{B}
. Then,

\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}=\begin{cases}1,\quad d_{A}=d_{B}\text{ and }\ \mathcal{N}(\rho)=U\rho U^{\dagger}\text{ for some unitary U},\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(10)

The same conclusion does not holds for channels with strictly greater output dimension than input dimension. For example, the erasure channel with erasure probability
\nu\in[0,1)

\mathcal{N}(\rho)=(1-\nu)\rho+\nu\mathinner{|{e}\rangle\,\langle{e}|}

is a simple counterexample with
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}=1-\nu>0
.

The key ingredient to prove the above theorem is the following lemma about purity-preserving quantum channels.
The proof can be found in
(
31
, Theorem 3.1)
. For the convenience of the reader, we present an independent proof below.

Lemma 3.2
.

If a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
preserves the purity, i.e., it maps any pure state to a pure state, then
\mathcal{N}
must either be an isometric embedding
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=V\rho V^{\dagger},\ V^{\dagger}V=I_{A}
or a replacer channel
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\tr(\rho)\mathinner{|{\varphi}\rangle\,\langle{\varphi}|}
for some pure state
\ket{\varphi}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
denote the set of linearly independent Kraus operators with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}E_{i}^{\dagger}E_{i}=I
that form the minimal Kraus representation of the channel
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
, i.e.

\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger}\ \forall\rho\in B(\mathcal{H}_{A})\ .

If
k=1
,
E_{1}^{\dagger}E_{1}=I
is an isometry which can only happen if
\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{A}\leq\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{B}
.
We now show that, if
k>1
and
\mathcal{N}
is a quantum channel that preserves purity,
\mathcal{N}
must be a replacer channel.

Suppose that
k>1
and that
\mathcal{N}
is a purity-preserving quantum channel. In this case, we claim that
\forall 1\leq i\leq k
, the codimension of
\text{Ker}E_{i}=\{x\in\mathcal{H}_{A}:E_{i}x=0\}
must be 1, i.e.,

\displaystyle H_{i}:=\text{Ker}E_{i},\quad\text{dim}H_{i}^{\perp}=1.

We only show the case
i=1
because the same argument applies to other
E_{i}
. We argue by contradiction. Suppose
\text{dim}H_{1}^{\perp}>1
. Then one can show there exists
0\neq\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle E_{1}\big|_{H_{1}^{\perp}}=\mu_{0}E_{2}\big|_{H_{1}^{\perp}}.

(11)

In fact, for any orthogonal pure states
\ket{\varphi_1},\ket{\varphi_2}
in
H_{1}^{\perp}
, because
\mathcal{N}
must map
\ket{\varphi_1}
,
\ket{\varphi_2}
as well as their linear combination to pure states, there exist complex constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
such that

\displaystyle E_{2}\ket{\varphi_1}=c_{1}E_{1}\ket{\varphi_1},

\displaystyle E_{2}\ket{\varphi_2}=c_{2}E_{1}\ket{\varphi_2},

\displaystyle E_{2}(\ket{\varphi_1}+\ket{\varphi_2})=c_{3}E_{1}(\ket{\varphi_1}+\ket{\varphi_2}).

Since
E_{1}\ket{\varphi_1}
and
E_{1}\ket{\varphi_2}
are linearly independent, we must have
c_{1}=c_{2}=c_{3}
. As this holds for arbitrary two vectors
\ket{\varphi_1},\ket{\varphi_2}
in
H_{1}^{\perp}
, (
11
) holds. Next, we show that we also have

\displaystyle E_{1}\big|_{H_{1}}=\mu_{0}E_{2}\big|_{H_{1}}=0.

(12)

In fact, for any
\ket{\psi}\in H_{1}
and any orthogonal pure states
\ket{\varphi_1},\ket{\varphi_2}
in
H_{1}^{\perp}
, there exist non-zero complex constants
c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle E_{1}\ket{\varphi_1}=E_{1}(\ket{\psi}+\ket{\varphi_1})=c_{1}^{\prime}E_{2}(\ket{\psi}+\ket{\varphi_1})=c_{1}^{\prime}E_{2}\ket{\psi}+c_{1}^{\prime}\mu_{0}E_{1}\ket{\varphi_1},

\displaystyle E_{1}\ket{\varphi_2}=E_{1}(\ket{\psi}+\ket{\varphi_2})=c_{2}^{\prime}E_{2}(\ket{\psi}+\ket{\varphi_2})=c_{2}^{\prime}E_{2}\ket{\psi}+c_{1}^{\prime}\mu_{0}E_{1}\ket{\varphi_2},

which shows that
E_{2}\ket{\psi}
is parallel to
E_{1}\ket{\varphi_1}
and
E_{1}\ket{\varphi_2}
simultaneously thus
E_{2}\ket{\psi}=0
. Therefore, (
11
) and (
12
) hold thus
E_{1}=\mu_{0}E_{2}
which contradicts the fact that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are linearly independent. Therefore, for every
1\leq i\leq k
, we must have
\text{dim}H_{i}^{\perp}=1
, hence
E_{i}
is rank 1 operator

\displaystyle E_{i}=\ket{\varphi}\bra{\psi_j}

and
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\tr(\rho)\mathinner{|{\varphi}\rangle\,\langle{\varphi}|}
which concludes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

If
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\tr(\rho)\mathinner{|{\varphi}\rangle\,\langle{\varphi}|}
is a replacer channel,
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}=0
because the numerator
D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)||\mathcal{N}(\sigma))=0
is always zero.
For unitary channel
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=U\rho U^{\dagger}
, the expansion coefficient
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}
equals to
1
due to the unitary invariance of the relative entropy. By the above Lemma
3.2
, it suffices to consider channels that are not purity-preserving.

In this case, we claim that one can find a projection
P_{A}
onto a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
with dimension
\dim(P_{A}(\mathcal{H}_{A}))\leq d_{A}-1
and a pure state
\ket{\psi}\in P_{A}(\mathcal{H}_{A})^{\perp}
such that

\displaystyle\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{A})\right)=\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{A}+\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\,\langle{\psi}|})\right).

In order to construct
P_{A}
, we begin by choosing a pure state
\ket{\varphi_1}
such that
\mathcal{N}(\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}}\rangle\,\langle{\varphi_{1}}|})
is a mixed state, which is possible as
\mathcal{N}
is not purity-preserving. Then, we extend
\ket{\varphi_1}
to an orthonormal basis
\{\ket{\varphi_i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d_{A}}
and get a family of projections

P_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{i}}\rangle\,\langle{\varphi_{i}}|},\quad 1\leq k\leq d_{A}.

The support of
\mathcal{N}(P_{k})
is a chain of subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{B}
that fulfills

\displaystyle\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{1})\right)\subseteq\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{2})\right)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{k})\right)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{d})\right).

Recall that, by assumption,
\mathcal{N}(P_{1})=\mathcal{N}(\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}}\rangle\,\langle{\varphi_{1}}|})
is a mixed state, and thus the dimension of
\dim\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{1})\right)\geq 2
. Thus

\displaystyle 2\leq\text{dim}\left(\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{1})\right)\right)\leq\cdots\leq\text{dim}\left(\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{k})\right)\right)\leq\cdots\text{dim}\left(\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{d})\right)\right)\leq\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{B}\leq d_{A}.

Since there are
d_{A}
many subspaces and the dimension can take at most
d_{A}-1
values, there exists
k_{0}<d_{A}
such that

\displaystyle\text{dim}\left(\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{k_{0}})\right)\right)=\text{dim}\left(\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{k_{0}+1})\right)\right)

thus
\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{k_{0}})\right)=\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{k_{0}+1})\right)
. Then, the claim is verified by choosing

\displaystyle P_{A}=P_{k_{0}},\quad\ket{\psi}=\ket{\varphi_{k_0+1}}.

Now, we use this construction to show
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}=0
. Denote

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{k_{0}}P_{A},\quad\sigma_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\,\langle{\psi}|}.

By direct calculation,

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma_{\varepsilon})=-\log(1-\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\frac{\differential}{\differential\varepsilon}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{\varepsilon}))\big|_{\varepsilon=0}=0.

The second equation follows from the fact that
\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\,\langle{\psi}|})\right)\subseteq\text{supp}\left(\mathcal{N}(P_{A})\right)
, thus there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
,
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{\varepsilon})
is a density operator. Therefore, the non-negative, differentiable function defined as
f(\varepsilon)=D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{\varepsilon}))\geq 0
achieves its minimum at
\varepsilon=0
. Therefore, the derivative at
\varepsilon=0
is zero.

Inserting the states
\rho
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
in the expansion coefficient and letting
\varepsilon
go to zero, we have by L’Hôpital’s rule

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\rho\neq\sigma}\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)||\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{D(\rho||\sigma)}\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{\varepsilon}))}{D(\rho\|\sigma_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\frac{\differential}{\differential\varepsilon}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{\varepsilon}))}{\frac{\differential}{\differential\varepsilon}D(\rho\|\sigma_{\varepsilon})}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\differential}{\differential\varepsilon}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{\varepsilon}))=0.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

In
[
13
]
, a positive expansion coefficient is proposed as a condition under which the proposed quantum version of the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm for computing quantum channel capacities converges exponentially fast. More precisely, they show exponential convergence for the computation of the Holevo quantity under the assumption in
(

, which is equivalent (by
(

) to a positive expansion coefficient for the channel. Since we show here that this assumption cannot hold for non-unitary channels with

Remark 3.4

For example, fixing

\inf_{\rho}\frac{D(\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)||\mathcal{D}_{p}(\frac{I_{2}}{2}))}{D(\rho||\frac{I_{2}}{2})}>0.

(13)

4
Relative contraction and expansion for pairs of quantum channels

Motivated by the vanishing of expansion coefficient of a single channel
\mathcal{N}
shown in Section
3
, in this section, we present three different methods for comparing the expansion and the contraction of the relative entropy for two channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
. Recall that

\displaystyle\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}:=\inf_{\rho\neq\sigma,\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)}\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))}\ ,\ \ \ \eta_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}:=\sup_{\rho\neq\sigma,\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)}\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))}.

(14)

We introduce several techniques to bound or compute the relative coefficients in this context. The first technique leverages the equivalence between the relative expansion of the relative entropy and its infinitesimal counterpart, the Bogoliubov-Kubo-Mori (BKM) metric (see Lemma
4.2
). The second technique employs a completely positive (CP) order comparison of two channels, as established in Lemma
4.3
. Additionally, we conduct a detailed study of qubit channels using the Bloch vector representation.

These techniques are applied in Section
5
, where we provide examples of channel pairs with non-zero relative expansion coefficients.

4.1
Comparison of BKM metric

Our starting point is the following integral representation of the relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
from
(
32
, Lemma 2.2)
, which is also studied in
[
8
,
33
,
9
,
32
,
34
]
:

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}g_{\rho_{t}}(\rho-\sigma)\differential t\differential s.

(15)

where
\rho_{t}:=(1-t)\sigma+t\rho,t\in[0,1]
and the BKM metric
g_{\sigma}(X)
of an operator
X
at density
\sigma
is defined as

g_{\sigma}(X)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\tr\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}X^{\dagger}(\sigma+rI)^{-1}X(\sigma+rI)^{-1}\differential r\right),\quad&\text{supp}(X)\subseteq\text{supp}(\sigma)\\
\infty,\quad&\text{else}.\end{cases}

In fact, define a function
f(t)=D\left(\rho_{t}\|\sigma\right),t\in[0,1]
. We have
f(0)=0,f(1)=D(\rho\|\sigma)
and the derivatives

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left((\rho-\sigma)\ln\rho_{t}-(\rho-\sigma)\ln\sigma\right)

(16)

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{tr}\left((\rho-\sigma)(\rho_{t}+rI)^{-1}(\rho-\sigma)(\rho_{t}+rI)^{-1}\right)\differential r=g_{\rho_{t}}(\rho-\sigma).

(17)

Since
f^{\prime}(0)=0
, and the integral representation (
15
) follows from

\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)=f(1)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{s}f^{\prime\prime}(t)\differential t\right)\differential t\differential s\ .

Given any density operator
\sigma
acting on
\mathcal{H}
, we also define the BKM operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(X)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\sigma+rI)^{-1}X(\sigma+rI)^{-1}\differential r,\quad

\displaystyle\text{supp}(X)\subseteq\text{supp}(\sigma).

(18)

It is clear that
g_{\sigma}(X)=\langle X,\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(X)\rangle
with respect to the trace inner product
\langle Y,X\rangle=\tr(Y^\dagger X)
.
The following lemma gives a criterion for the comparison between the relative entropies
D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))
and
D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))
via comparison of BKM metric
g_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{N}(X))
and
g_{\mathcal{M}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{M}(X))
. The equivalence between contraction coefficient of relative entropy and BKM metric when one of the channel is identity was previously studied in
[
8
]
. In the following lemma, we answer the open question following Theorem 7.1 in
[
9
]
, and extend it to the case where two arbitrary channels are compared.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
and
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{\prime}))
be two quantum channels. For any
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, the following two statement are equivalent:

1.

For any density operators
\rho
and
\sigma
, with
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
,

\displaystyle c_{1}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))\leq c_{2}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma)).

2.

For any density operators
\sigma
and traceless Hermitian operator
X
with
\mathrm{supp}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
,

\displaystyle c_{1}g_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{N}(X))

\displaystyle\leq g_{\mathcal{M}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{M}(X))\leq c_{2}g_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{N}(X)).

(19)

Proof.

Take
\rho_{t}=(1-t)\sigma+t\rho=\sigma+tX
and
X=\rho-\sigma
. For any
t\in(0,1)
,
\text{supp}(X)\subseteq\text{supp}(\rho_{t})
. The direction
2
\implies
1
follows from the integral representation (
15
).

To prove
1
\implies
2
, switching the roles of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
, we only need to show that if
c_{1}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))
for any density operators
\rho,\sigma
with
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
, then we have
c_{1}g_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{N}(X))\leq g_{\mathcal{M}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{M}(X))
for any density operators
\sigma
and traceless Hermitian operator
X
with
\mathrm{supp}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
. In fact, define
\rho_{t}=\sigma+tX
,
\mathcal{N}(\rho_{t})
and
\mathcal{M}(\rho_{t})
are density operators for
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, and we have

\displaystyle c_{1}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{t})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{t})\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma)).

Note that

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{t})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\big|_{t=0}=D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{t})\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))\big|_{t=0}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\differential}{\differential t}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{t})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\big|_{t=0}=\frac{\differential}{\differential t}D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{t})\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))\big|_{t=0}=0.

Thus we have the second order comparison:

\displaystyle c_{1}\frac{\differential^{2}}{\differential t^{2}}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{t})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\big|_{t=0}\leq\frac{\differential^{2}}{\differential t^{2}}D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{t})\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))\big|_{t=0},

which concludes the proof of
c_{1}g_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{N}(X))\leq g_{\mathcal{M}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{M}(X))
by expanding the second-order derivative (
17
).
∎

We will also make use of the following result from
(
32
, Lemma 2.1)
:

Lemma 4.2

(
(
32
, Lemma 2.1)
)
.

If two density operators
\rho
and
\sigma
satisfy
\rho\leq c\sigma
for some
c>0
, then for any operator
X\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

g_{\rho}(X)\geq\frac{1}{c}g_{\sigma}(X).

(20)

For the convenience of the reader, the proof is provided in Appendix
A.1
.

4.2
Comparison of completely positive order

Here, we propose a criterion based on CP order which will later be used for computing the relative expansion coefficient of two dephasing channels in Section
5.2
. Suppose that two channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
satisfy

c_{1}\mathcal{N}\leq_{cp}\mathcal{M}\leq_{cp}c_{2}\mathcal{N}

(21)

for some positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. We note that this condition is equivalent to the comparison of Choi matrices of channels in terms of positive semidefiniteness

c_{1}C_{\mathcal{N}}\leq_{cp}C_{\mathcal{M}}\leq_{cp}c_{2}C_{\mathcal{N}}

where
c_{1}\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{M}}
means
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{M}}-c_{1}\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
is positive semidefinite.
By Lemma
4.2
, for any operator
Y
and density
\omega
,

\frac{1}{c_{2}}g_{\mathcal{M}(\omega)}(Y)\leq g_{\mathcal{N}(\omega)}(Y)\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}g_{\mathcal{M}(\omega)}(Y).

Then the target inequality

\displaystyle g_{\mathcal{N}(\omega)}(\mathcal{N}(X))\geq cg_{\mathcal{M}(\omega)}(\mathcal{M}(X)),\quad\forall\ \omega,X

can be deduced from

\displaystyle g_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X))\geq c^{\prime}g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))

(22)

for some
c^{\prime}>0
(Either
c^{\prime}=cc_{1}
and
\omega=\mathcal{N}(\rho_{t}),t\in[0,1]
, or
c^{\prime}=cc_{2}
for
\omega=\mathcal{M}(\rho_{t}),t\in[0,1]
).

It is tempting to conjecture that the comparison in CP order (
21
) (or equivalently, positive semidefinite order in terms of Choi matrices) directly implies the comparison of BKM metric (
22
). The latter is equivalent to

\displaystyle\langle X,\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}\mathcal{J}_{\omega}\mathcal{N}(X)\rangle\geq c^{\prime}\langle X,\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\mathcal{J}_{\omega}\mathcal{M}(X)\rangle,\forall X

(23)

where
\langle X,Y\rangle=\tr(X^{\dagger}Y)
is the standard Hilbert-Schmidt inner product.
However, the complete positivity of superoperators does not imply positive semidefiniteness as an operator on the Hilbert-Schmidt space. In fact, suppose we have a completely positive map
\Psi(\rho)=\sigma_{z}\rho\sigma_{z}
, one can easily show that
\langle X,\Psi(X)\rangle<0
for a Hermitian operator
X=\begin{pmatrix}a&z\\
z^{*}&-a\end{pmatrix}
with
|a|<|z|
. Hence,
\Psi
is completely positive map but is not a positive semidefinite as an operator on the Hilbert-Schmidt space. It is therefore not enough to assume (
21
) in order to have a nontrivial expansion coefficient through Lemma
4.1
.

Instead, we need an additional assumption in order to guarantee that the comparison from (
22
) holds for some
c^{\prime}>0
. The following lemma is motivated by
(
35
, Lemma 2.3)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\Phi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
are quantum channels such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{M}+\varepsilon\Phi,\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1).

Moreover, we assume that there exists a quantum channel
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}))
such that
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{N}=\Phi
and
\mathcal{D}(\omega)\leq c\omega
for some fixed density operator
\omega
and
c>0
. Then, for any operator
X
, we have

g_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X))\geq\frac{(1-2\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)}{1+c\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X)).

(24)

Proof.

Recall that for the BKM metric
g_{\omega}:\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{[}0,\infty]
,

g_{\omega}(X)=\langle X,\mathcal{J}_{\omega}(X)\rangle
,
X\mapsto\sqrt{g_{\omega}(X)}
is a Hilbert space norm on
\text{supp}(\omega)
. By triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{g_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X))}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{g_{\omega}((1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{M}(X)+\varepsilon\Phi(X))}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{g_{\omega}((1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{M}(X))}-\sqrt{g_{\omega}(\varepsilon\Phi(X))}

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))}-\varepsilon\sqrt{g_{\omega}(\Phi(X))}.

Taking the square on both sides,

\displaystyle g_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X))

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))-2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))}\sqrt{g_{\omega}(\Phi(X))}+\varepsilon^{2}g_{\omega}(\Phi(X))

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))-2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))}\sqrt{g_{\omega}(\Phi(X))}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))-\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\big(g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))+g_{\omega}(\Phi(X))\big).

To compare
g_{\omega}(\Phi(X))
and
g_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X))
, we use Lemma
4.2
to get

\displaystyle g_{\omega}(\Phi(X))\leq cg_{\mathcal{D}(\omega)}(\Phi(X))=cg_{\mathcal{D}(\omega)}(\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{N}(X))\leq cg_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X)),

where the last inequality is the data processing inequality of the BKM metric
[
8
]
. Finally, using the above inequalities, we get

\displaystyle g_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X))

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))-\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\big(g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))+g_{\omega}(\Phi(X))\big)

\displaystyle\geq(1-2\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X))-c\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)g_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X)),

which implies

\displaystyle g_{\omega}(\mathcal{N}(X))\geq\frac{(1-2\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)}{1+c\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}g_{\omega}(\mathcal{M}(X)).

∎

4.3
Explicit formula for the qubit channels

In this section, we discuss the qubit case and provide a sufficient condition for
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}>0
which can cover a large family of examples. Recall that the identity and Pauli matrices

\sigma_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(25)

together form an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{M}_{2}
.
Any traceless Hermitian operator
X
and density operator
\rho
can be represented by two real vectors:

\displaystyle X=\vec{y}\cdot\vec{\sigma}=y_{1}\sigma_{x}+y_{2}\sigma_{y}+y_{3}\sigma_{z},\quad\vec{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(26)

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}_{2}+\vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma})=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}_{2}+w_{1}\sigma_{x}+w_{2}\sigma_{y}+w_{3}\sigma_{z}),\quad\vec{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

where
\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
denotes the vector of Pauli matrix.
Note that
\rho
is a density operator if and only if
|\vec{w}|\leq 1
. Thus the set of density operators can be identified with the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the pure states lie on the Bloch sphere. The Pauli basis has also been used to study the contraction coefficient of unital qubit channel by Hiai and Ruskai
[
9
]
. The following basic properties are useful, see
(
9
, Appendix B)
:

\displaystyle\text{Product rule:}\ (a\mathbb{I}_{2}+\vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma})(b\mathbb{I}_{2}+\vec{y}\cdot\vec{\sigma})=(ab+\vec{w}\cdot\vec{y})\mathbb{I}_{2}+(a\vec{y}+b\vec{w}+i\vec{w}\times\vec{y})\cdot\vec{\sigma},

(27)

\displaystyle\text{Inverse rule:}\ (a\mathbb{I}_{2}+\vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma})^{-1}=\frac{a\mathbb{I}_{2}-\vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma}}{a^{2}-|\vec{w}|^{2}},

where
\vec{w}\times\vec{y}
is the cross product of two vectors. We have the following explicit calculation for BKM metric:

Lemma 4.4
.

For the traceless Hermitian operator
X
and density operator
\rho
given by (
26
),

\displaystyle g_{\rho}(X)

\displaystyle=4|\vec{y}|^{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{u^{2}+|\vec{w}|^{2}\cos 2\theta}{(u^{2}-|\vec{w}|^{2})^{2}}du

(28)

\displaystyle=2|\vec{y}|^{2}\bigg(\frac{1+\cos 2\theta}{1-|\vec{w}|^{2}}+\frac{1-\cos 2\theta}{2|\vec{w}|}\ln\frac{1+|\vec{w}|}{1-|\vec{w}|}\bigg).

where
\theta
is the angle between
\vec{y}
and
\vec{w}
.

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

Recall that
X=\vec{y}\cdot\vec{\sigma}
and
\rho=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}_{2}+\vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma})
, use the definition of BKM metric, we have

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636ptg_{\rho}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr((\vec{y} \cdot\vec{\sigma}) \big( \frac{1}{2} (\mb I_2 + \vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma}) + u\mb I_2\big)^{-1} (\vec{y} \cdot\vec{\sigma}) \big( \frac{1}{2} (\mb I_2 + \vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma}) + u\mb I_2\big)^{-1} )du

\displaystyle(\vec{y}\cdot\vec{\sigma})\big(u\mathbb{I}_{2}+\vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\big)^{-1}=\frac{(\vec{y}\cdot\vec{\sigma})\big(u\mathbb{I}_{2}-\vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\big)}{u^{2}-|\vec{w}|^{2}}=\frac{-(\vec{w}\cdot\vec{y})\mathbb{I}_{2}+(u\vec{y}+i\vec{w}\times\vec{y})\cdot\vec{\sigma}}{u^{2}-|\vec{w}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\int_{1}^{\infty}\tr((\vec{y} \cdot\vec{\sigma}) \big( u\mb I_2 + \vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma} \big)^{-1} (\vec{y} \cdot\vec{\sigma}) \big( u\mb I_2 + \vec{w}\cdot\vec{\sigma} \big)^{-1} )du

\displaystyle=4\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{u^{2}|\vec{y}|^{2}+|\vec{w}\cdot\vec{y}|^{2}-|\vec{w}\times\vec{y}|^{2}}{(u^{2}-|\vec{w}|^{2})^{2}}du

5
Examples of channels with non-zero relative expansion coefficients

In this section, using the general methods presented in Section
4
, we show that the relative expansion coefficient can be strictly positive for pairs of depolarizing channels, pairs of generalized dephasing channels and pairs of qubit amplitude damping channels. Note that the relative expansion coefficient is non-trivial only if for any states
\rho,\sigma
,

\displaystyle\text{supp}(\mathcal{N}(\rho))\subseteq\text{supp}(\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\Longrightarrow\text{supp}(\mathcal{M}(\rho))\subseteq\text{supp}(\mathcal{M}(\sigma)),

otherwise
D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))
is finite while
D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))
is infinite.

5.1
Depolarizing channels

For
p\in[0,1]
, a depolarizing channel is defined by

\mathcal{D}_{p}:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}\ ,\ \mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+\frac{p}{d}\tr(\rho)I_{d}.

(44)

Our first example of a positive relative expansion coefficient compares two
d
-dimensional depolarizing channels
(\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}},\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}
). For any such pair with
0<p_{2}<p_{1}<1
, we show that
\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}},\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}}<\infty
and
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}},\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}}>0
.
For
p_{2}<p_{1}
, as
\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}=\mathcal{D}_{\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{1-p_{2}}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}
, our problem can be seen as a reverse-type data processing inequality restricted on the output states of
\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any parameters
0<p_{2}<p_{1}<1
and any density operators
\rho,\sigma
, we have

\left(\frac{1-p_{1}}{1-p_{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}\leq\frac{D(\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\rho)\|\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\sigma))}{D(\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\rho)\|\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\sigma))}\leq\left(\frac{1-p_{1}}{1-p_{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{1-\frac{d-1}{d}p_{2}}{1-\frac{d-1}{d}p_{1}}.

(45)

Proof.

Note that for any density
\rho
and
\sigma
,
\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho-\sigma)=(1-p)(\rho-\sigma)
. Then for any
\omega

g_{\mathcal{D}_{p}(\omega)}(\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho-\sigma))=(1-p)^{2}g_{\mathcal{D}_{p}(\omega)}(\rho-\sigma).

(46)

Moreover, we have

\frac{1-\frac{d-1}{d}p_{1}}{1-\frac{d-1}{d}p_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\omega)\leq\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\omega)\leq\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\omega),

(47)

where the upper and lower bound are given by the supremum and infimum of the function

h(\lambda)=\frac{(1-p_{1})\lambda+\frac{p_{1}}{d}}{(1-p_{2})\lambda+\frac{p_{2}}{d}},\quad\lambda\in[0,1].

(48)

By applying Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-p_{1}}{1-p_{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}\leq\frac{g_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\omega)}(\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\rho-\sigma))}{g_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\omega)}(\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\rho-\sigma))}\leq\left(\frac{1-p_{1}}{1-p_{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{1-\frac{d-1}{d}p_{2}}{1-\frac{d-1}{d}p_{1}},

(49)

which implies the conclusion via Lemma
4.1
.
∎

For qubit case, we can give an explicit expression:

Proposition 5.2
.

For two qubit depolarizing channels, we have

\displaystyle\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}},\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}}=\left(\frac{1-p_{1}}{1-p_{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{p_{2}(2-p_{2})}{p_{1}(2-p_{1})},

\displaystyle{\eta}_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}},\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}}=\left(\frac{1-p_{1}}{1-p_{2}}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

For any unitary
U
and any
p\in[0,1]
, we have that
U\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)U^{\dagger}=\mathcal{D}_{p}(U\rho U^{\dagger})
, and
D(\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)||\mathcal{D}_{p}(\sigma))=D(U\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)U^{\dagger}||U\mathcal{D}_{p}(\sigma)U^{\dagger})=D(\mathcal{D}_{p}(U\rho U^{\dagger})||\mathcal{D}_{p}(U\sigma U^{\dagger}))
. Taking
U
to be the (conjugate of the) unitary that diagonalizes
\sigma
, i.e. let
\sigma=U^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}1-\lambda&0\\
0&\lambda\end{pmatrix}U
, we can thus restrict ourselves to the case when
\sigma
is diagonal.

Noting that (
46
) still holds, it remains to compute
\frac{g_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\sigma)}(X)}{g_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\sigma)(X)}}
. We can use the explicit expression for the BKM metric, for example,
(
33
, Eq. 16)
; for a Hermitian matrix
X=\begin{pmatrix}x&z\\
z^{*}&-x\end{pmatrix}
and diagonal
\sigma
with eigenvalues
1-\lambda,\lambda
, this becomes
g_{\sigma}(X)=(\frac{1}{1-\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda})x^{2}
. Inserting
\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\sigma),\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\sigma)
and taking the quotient, we obtain:

\frac{g_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\sigma)}(X)}{g_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\sigma)(X)}}=h(\lambda)

with

h(\lambda)=\frac{(2(1-p_{1})\lambda+p_{1})}{(2(1-p_{2})\lambda+p_{2})}\frac{(2-2(1-p_{1})\lambda-p_{1})}{(2-2(1-p_{2})\lambda-p_{2})},\quad\lambda\in[0,1].

(50)

The supremum of this function is achieved at
\lambda=1/2
, giving
h(1/2)=1
, which corresponds to selecting
\sigma
as a maximally mixed state. The infimum is taken at
\lambda\rightarrow 0
(or
\lambda\rightarrow 1
) where it evaluates to
h(1)=\frac{p_{2}(2-p_{2})}{p_{1}(2-p_{1})}
, which corresponds to selecting
\sigma
as a pure state. Thus, in total, we have:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-p_{1}}{1-p_{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{p_{2}(2-p_{2})}{p_{1}(2-p_{1})}\leq\frac{g_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\rho_{t})}(\mathcal{D}_{p_{1}}(\rho-\sigma))}{g_{\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\rho_{t})}(\mathcal{D}_{p_{2}}(\rho-\sigma))}\leq\left(\frac{1-p_{1}}{1-p_{2}}\right)^{2},

(51)

and the upper and lower bound can be achieved.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Our upper bound from Proposition
5.1
implies an upper bound on the contraction coefficient of the depolarizing channel for arbitrary dimensions. In fact, letting
p_{2}\to 0
, the upper bound is

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{p}}\leq\frac{(1-p)^{2}}{1-\frac{d-1}{d}p}<1-p.

For the qubit depolarizing channel, it is known that
\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{p}}=(1-p)^{2}
, see
[
9
,
10
]
, which we recover in Proposition
5.2
. This also illustrates that our upper bound from Proposition
5.1
is not sharp for
d=2
.

As another point of comparison, when the second state
\sigma
is the maximally mixed state
I_{d}/d
,
[
37
]
employ a curvature bound to show that

D(\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)\|I_{d}/d)\leq(1-p)^{1+\frac{1}{d}}D(\rho\|I_{d}/d)

for any
\rho
, extending the result of
[
38
]
. More explicitly,
[
38
,
37
]
studies the local entropy contraction constant when the second state
\omega
is the fixed point state
I_{d}/d

\eta_{\omega}(\Phi)=\sup_{\rho}\frac{D\bigl(\Phi(\rho)\,\|\,\Phi(\omega)\bigr)}{D\bigl(\rho\,\|\,\omega\bigr)},\quad\omega=\frac{I_{d}}{d}.

This is potentially smaller than our contraction coefficient

\eta(\Phi)=\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\frac{D\bigl(\Phi(\rho)\,\|\,\Phi(\sigma)\bigr)}{D\bigl(\rho\,\|\,\sigma\bigr)}.

A channel is called strictly positive if it maps any state to a state with full support. For strictly positive channels, we have the following generalization of Proposition
5.1
, which can be derived from Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
:

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose there exists constants
0<\lambda_{min}<\lambda_{max}<\infty
such that

0<\lambda_{min}I\leq\mathcal{M}(\rho)\leq\lambda_{max}I
for any state
\rho
, then

\frac{1}{\lambda_{max}}\|\mathcal{M}(\rho-\sigma)\|_{2}^{2}\leq D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{min}}\|\mathcal{M}(\rho-\sigma)\|_{2}^{2}.

(52)

For strictly positive channels, the existence of
0<\lambda_{min}<\lambda_{max}<\infty
is guaranteed by the compactness of state space.
Using the above result, we can get a reverse-type data processing inequality for strictly positive channels, which includes depolarizing channels as a special case. For more properties of strictly positive channels, we refer the reader to
[
39
]
and references therein. Note that this class of channels does not include generalized dephasing channels or amplitude damping channels, as their output state can be singular.

5.2
Generalized dephasing channels

Another interesting class of quantum channels are quantum dephasing channels which model the loss of coherence (off-diagonal entries of the density matrix) without changing the populations (diagonal elements).

For a
d
-dimensional quantum system
\mathcal{H}
, the generalized dephasing channel
\Phi_{\Gamma}:\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
is defined as

\Phi_{\Gamma}(\rho)=\Gamma\odot\rho:=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq d-1}\Gamma_{ij}\rho_{ij}\ket{i}\bra{j},\quad\rho=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq d-1}\rho_{ij}\ket{i}\bra{j},

(53)

where
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

\Gamma_{ij}\in[0,1],\quad\Gamma_{ii}=1,\ 0\leq i,j\leq d-1.

(54)

Note that the Choi–Jamiołkowski operator of
\Phi_{\Gamma}
is

\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{\Gamma}}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\Gamma_{ij}\mathinner{|{ii}\rangle\,\langle{jj}|},

(55)

thus
\Phi_{\Gamma}
is a quantum channel if and only if
\Gamma
is positive semidefinite and
\Gamma_{ii}=1
for all
i
.

The diagonal entries of a quantum state remain unchanged when a dephasing channel is applied; thus, if we restrict
\rho,\sigma
to be diagonal operators, we always have

D(\Phi_{\Gamma}(\rho)\|\Phi_{\Gamma}(\sigma))=D(\rho\|\sigma)

(56)

which implies
\eta_{\Phi_{\Gamma}}=1
, and similarly
\eta_{\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}}=1
for two dephasing channels
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}
. For the relative expansion coefficient, using Lemma
4.3
, we show that
\widecheck{\eta}_{\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}},\Phi_{\Gamma}}>0
for certain positive semidefinite
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{ij}),\Gamma^{\prime}=(\Gamma_{ij}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
be positive semidefinite matrix satisfying (
54
). Suppose there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that

•

(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma\leq\Gamma^{\prime}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\Gamma
.

•

\widehat{\Gamma}=(\widehat{\Gamma}_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq d-1}
is positive semidefinite where

\displaystyle\widehat{\Gamma}_{ij}:=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\text{ if }\quad\Gamma_{ij}^{\prime}=0,\\
\frac{\Gamma_{ij}^{\prime}-(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma_{ij}}{\varepsilon\Gamma_{ij}^{\prime}},&\text{ if }\quad\Gamma_{ij}^{\prime}>0.\end{cases}

(57)

Then we have

\widecheck{\eta}_{\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}},\Phi_{\Gamma}}\geq\frac{(1-2\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)}{(1+2\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon)}.

(58)

Proof.

We identify
\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}},\Phi_{\Gamma}
as
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
respectively and verify the assumptions in order to apply Lemma
4.3
. By definition, we have

\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=(1-\varepsilon)\Phi_{\Gamma}+\varepsilon\Phi_{\widetilde{\Gamma}},

(59)

where
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}=\Gamma_{ij}+\frac{\Gamma_{ij}^{\prime}-\Gamma_{ij}}{\varepsilon}
.
\widetilde{\Gamma}=(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq d-1}
is positive semidefinite by the assumption that
(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma\leq\Gamma^{\prime}
. It remains to show that

1.

There exists a quantum channel
\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathcal{D}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=\Phi_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}.

2.

There exists a universal constant
c>0
such that for any density operator
\sigma
,
\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\sigma))\leq c\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\sigma)
.

For the first argument, we define the generalized dephasing channel
\Phi_{\widehat{\Gamma}}
with
\widehat{\Gamma}
defined as in (
57
).
By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=\Phi_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}.

By assumption,
\widehat{\Gamma}
is positive semidefinite and
\widehat{\Gamma}_{ii}=1
, hence

\Phi_{\widehat{\Gamma}}
is a quantum channel. We choose this channel to be
\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathcal{D}\equiv\widehat{\Gamma}
in the first condition (1).

We will now show that the second condition (2) holds for this choice of
\mathcal{D}
. Noting that
\mathcal{D}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=\Phi_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\circ\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=\Phi_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=\frac{\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}-(1-\varepsilon)\Phi_{\Gamma}}{\varepsilon}\leq_{cp}\frac{(1+\varepsilon)\Phi_{\Gamma}-(1-\varepsilon)\Phi_{\Gamma}}{\varepsilon}=2\Phi_{\Gamma}\leq_{cp}\frac{2}{1-\varepsilon}\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}.

Thus, we can choose
c=\frac{2}{1-\varepsilon}
in the condition (2).

Finally, we apply Lemma
4.3
with
\omega=\Phi_{p^{\prime}}(\sigma)
for any density operator
\sigma

, and apply Lemma

with

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-2\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)}{(1+2\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon)}g_{\Phi_{\Gamma}(\sigma)}(\Phi_{\Gamma}(X)),

Proposition 5.7
.

For any

\widecheck{\eta}_{\Phi_{p_{1}},\Phi_{p_{2}}}>0.

Proof.

Using Lemma
4.1

Denote
\vec{y}_{p}=T_{p}\vec{y},\ \vec{w}_{p}=T_{p}\vec{w}

\vec{w}

\displaystyle 1-|\vec{w}_{p}|^{2}=1-\big((1-p)^{2}(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2})+w_{3}^{2}\big)=(1-|\vec{w}|^{2})+p(2-p)(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2}),

therefore, for any

Then one has

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{p}(\vec{w},\vec{y})

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\theta_{p}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p}}|))+f(|\vec{w_{p}}|)

(68)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\vec{w_{p}}\cdot\vec{y_{p}}|^{2}}{|\vec{w_{p}}|^{2}|\vec{y_{p}}|^{2}}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p}}|))+f(|\vec{w_{p}}|).

(69)

First we note that

|\vec{w_{p}}|=1\iff\vec{w}=\pm e_{3},\quad e_{3}=(0,0,1)^{T}

(70)

Therefore for any
\varepsilon>0

\vec{w}\in\mathcal{B}(e_{3},\varepsilon)^{c}\cap\mathcal{B}(-e_{3},\varepsilon)^{c}

f

1

, which implies that

\displaystyle c(\varepsilon,p)\leq\cos^{2}\theta_{p}+\sin^{2}\theta_{p}f(|\vec{w_{p}}|)\leq 1.

Then for
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{B}(e_{3},\varepsilon)^{c}\cap\mathcal{B}(-e_{3},\varepsilon)^{c}

\displaystyle c(p_{1},\varepsilon)\leq\frac{\widetilde{f}_{p_{1}}(\vec{w},\vec{y})}{\widetilde{f}_{p_{2}}(\vec{w},\vec{y})}\leq\frac{1}{c(p_{2},\varepsilon)}.

It remains to show that around the singular points(in this case they are
\pm e_{3}

\displaystyle\liminf_{\vec{w}\to\pm e_{3}}\inf_{\vec{y}}\frac{\widetilde{f}_{p_{1}}(\vec{w},\vec{y})}{\widetilde{f}_{p_{2}}(\vec{w},\vec{y})}=\liminf_{\vec{w}\to\pm e_{3}}\inf_{\vec{y}}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{p_{1}}+\sin^{2}\theta_{p_{1}}f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|)}{\cos^{2}\theta_{p_{2}}+\sin^{2}\theta_{p_{2}}f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|)}>0.

\displaystyle\inf_{\vec{y}}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{p_{1}}}{\cos^{2}\theta_{p_{2}}}=0,\quad\forall\vec{w}\in\mathcal{B}.

The key idea to show a lower bound is that when
\vec{w}\to\pm e_{3}
, if
\cos^{2}\theta_{p_{i}}
converges to zero, then it tends to zero faster than
f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|)\sim-(1-|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|^{2})\ln(1 - |\vec{w_{p_1}}|^2)
thus a lower bound can still be derived.

Using (
69
), we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\vec{w}\to\pm e_{3}}\inf_{\vec{y}}\frac{\widetilde{f}_{p_{1}}(\vec{w},\vec{y})}{\widetilde{f}_{p_{2}}(\vec{w},\vec{y})}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\vec{w}\to\pm e_{3}}\inf_{\vec{y}}\frac{\big|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{1}}}/|\vec{y_{p_{1}}}|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|)}{\big|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{2}}}/|\vec{y_{p_{2}}}|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|)}\cdot\frac{|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|^{2}}{|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\vec{w}\to\pm e_{3}}\frac{\big|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{1}}}(\vec{w})/|\vec{y_{p_{1}}}(\vec{w})|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|)}{\big|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{2}}}(\vec{w})/|\vec{y_{p_{2}}}(\vec{w})|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|)},

where for each
\vec{w}\neq\pm e_{3}
, we denote
\vec{y}(\vec{w})
as

\vec{y}(\vec{w})=\arg\min\frac{\big|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{1}}}/|\vec{y_{p_{1}}}|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|)}{\big|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{2}}}/|\vec{y_{p_{2}}}|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|)},

(71)

and
\vec{y_{p_{i}}}(\vec{w}):=T_{p_{i}}\vec{y}(\vec{w}),\ i=1,2
. Note that the existence of
\vec{y}(\vec{w})
follows from the fact that infimum of a continuous function over a compact set is always achieved. By linearity, we can assume
|\vec{y}|=1
. For any
\vec{w}
and
\vec{y}

\displaystyle|\vec{w}_{p_{2}}\cdot\vec{y}_{p_{2}}|^{2}=|(1-p_{2})^{2}(y_{1}w_{1}+y_{2}w_{2})+y_{3}w_{3}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|(1-p_{1})^{2}(y_{1}w_{1}+y_{2}w_{2})+y_{3}w_{3}+\big((1-p_{2})^{2}-(1-p_{1})^{2}\big)(y_{1}w_{1}+y_{2}w_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(|\vec{w}_{p_{1}}\cdot\vec{y}_{p_{1}}|^{2}+\big((1-p_{2})^{2}-(1-p_{1})^{2}\big)^{2}|y_{1}w_{1}+y_{2}w_{2}|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(|\vec{w}_{p_{1}}\cdot\vec{y}_{p_{1}}|^{2}+\big((2-p_{1}-p_{2})(p_{1}-p_{2})\big)^{2}(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2})\bigg).

Recall that
1-|\vec{w}_{p}|^{2}=1-\big((1-p)^{2}(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2})+w_{3}^{2}\big)=(1-|\vec{w}|^{2})+p(2-p)(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2})
. For
c_{1}(p_{1},p_{2}):=\frac{(2-p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}}{(1-p_{2})^{2}p_{2}(2-p_{2})}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\big((2-p_{1}-p_{2})(p_{1}-p_{2})\big)^{2}(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2})}{|\vec{y}_{p_{2}}|^{2}}\leq c_{1}(p_{1},p_{2})(1-|\vec{w}_{p_{2}}|^{2})\leq c_{1}(p_{1},p_{2})f(|\vec{w}_{p_{2}}|).

|\vec{w}_{p_{2}}\cdot\vec{y}_{p_{2}}|^{2}\leq 2\big(|\vec{w}_{p_{1}}\cdot\vec{y}_{p_{1}}|^{2}+|\vec{y}_{p_{2}}|^{2}c_{1}(p_{1},p_{2})f(|\vec{w}_{p_{2}}|)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\liminf_{\vec{w}\to\pm e_{3}}\frac{\big|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{1}}}(\vec{w})/|\vec{y_{p_{1}}}(\vec{w})|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|)}{\big|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{2}}}(\vec{w})/|\vec{y_{p_{2}}}(\vec{w})|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|)}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\vec{w}\to\pm e_{3}}\frac{\big|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{1}}}(\vec{w})/|\vec{y_{p_{1}}}(\vec{w})|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|))+|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|^{2}f(|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}|)}{2\big|\vec{w_{p_{1}}}\cdot\vec{y_{p_{1}}}(\vec{w})/|\vec{y_{p_{2}}}(\vec{w})|\big|^{2}(1-f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|))+(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|^{2}+2c_{1}(p_{1},p_{2}))f(|\vec{w_{p_{2}}}|)}

∎

5.3
Amplitude damping channels

We now study the relative expansion coefficient for qubit amplitude damping channels. For
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we define the amplitude damping channel
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}
as

\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{00}&\rho_{01}\\

\sqrt{1-\gamma}\rho_{10}&(1-\gamma)\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix}.

(72)

Note that amplitude damping channels does not satisfy the cp order comparison in order to apply Lemma
4.3

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in(0,1)

g_{\sigma}(X)
for a qubit density operator
\sigma
from Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
to derive the positivity of the relative expansion coefficient of two amplitude damping channels:

Proposition 5.8
.

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in(0,1)

\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}>0.

Proof.

We aim to show that for any

\vec{w}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2\frac{(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})^{2}}{\gamma_{2}(1-\gamma_{2})}+1}\liminf_{\vec{w}\to e_{3}}\frac{f(|\vec{w_{\gamma_{1}}}|)}{f(|\vec{w_{\gamma_{2}}}|)}\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2}}{2(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{2}+(1-\gamma_{2})\gamma_{2}}>0,

where we used the following lower bound for the last inequality:

\displaystyle\liminf_{\vec{w}\to e_{3}}\frac{f(|\vec{w_{\gamma_{1}}}|)}{f(|\vec{w_{\gamma_{2}}}|)}=\liminf_{\vec{w}\to e_{3}}\frac{(1-|\vec{w_{\gamma_{1}}}|^{2})\ln(1 -|\vec{w_{\gamma_1}}|^2)}{(1-|\vec{w_{\gamma_{2}}}|^{2})\ln(1 - |\vec{w_{\gamma_2}}|^2)}\geq\frac{1-\gamma_{1}}{1-\gamma_{2}}.

This concludes (
3
) and thus finishes the proof.
∎

6
Application: less noisy but non-degradable channels

In this section, we discuss an application of the result in Section
5
. Based on the idea of reverse-type data processing inequalities and flag-extension of quantum channels
[
40
,
28
]
, we construct a family of parameterized quantum channels and show that these channels are less noisy for a certain parameter region. Within this region, the constructed channel is neither degradable nor anti-degradable, giving a way to construct examples of channels that are less noisy but not degradable.

6.1
Probabilistic mixture of degradable and anti-degradable channels

Suppose
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
are two degradable channels. Define

\Psi_{p,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}:=p\mathinner{|{0}\rangle\,\langle{0}|}\otimes\mathcal{N}+(1-p)\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{c},

(77)

which is a probabilistic mixture of degradable and anti-degradable channels. We denote the isometries generating
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
as

U_{\mathcal{N}}:\mathcal{H}_{A}\to\mathcal{H}_{B_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E_{1}},\quad U_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathcal{H}_{A}\to\mathcal{H}_{B_{2}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E_{2}}

(78)

and denote
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
as the degrading quantum channels respectively, i.e.,

\mathcal{D}_{1}\circ\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}^{c},\quad\mathcal{D}_{2}\circ\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{c},

(79)

where
\mathcal{N}^{c}
and
\mathcal{M}^{c}
are complementary channels.

A sufficient condition for
\Psi_{p,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}
to be less noisy is given as follows:

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
are two degradable channels such that the relative expansion coefficient of
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
is positive, i.e.,
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}>0
. Furthermore, we assume the degrading channel
\mathcal{D}_{1}
(degrading for
\mathcal{N}
) satisfies strong data processing inequality, i.e.,
\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}<1
. Then for any

p\in[\frac{1}{1+\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}(1-\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}})},1],

the quantum channel

\displaystyle\Psi_{p,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}=p\mathinner{|{0}\rangle\,\langle{0}|}\otimes\mathcal{N}+(1-p)\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{c}

(80)

is less noisy.

Proof.

Our goal is to show that for any classical-quantum state
\rho_{\mathcal{X}A}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\mathinner{|{x}\rangle\,\langle{x}|}\otimes\rho_{A}^{x}
, we have

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{X};B)\geq I(\mathcal{X};E),

where
\rho_{\mathcal{X}B}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\mathinner{|{x}\rangle\,\langle{x}|}\otimes\Psi_{p,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}(\rho_{A}^{x})
and
\rho_{\mathcal{X}E}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\mathinner{|{x}\rangle\,\langle{x}|}\otimes\Psi^{c}_{p,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}(\rho_{A}^{x})
. Denoting the isometry
U_{\mathcal{N}}:A\to B_{1}E_{1}
and
U_{\mathcal{M}}:A\to B_{2}E_{2}
, we can decompose
\mathcal{H}_{B}=\mathcal{H}_{B_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{E_{2}},\ \mathcal{H}_{E}=\mathcal{H}_{E_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{B_{2}}
, and

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{X}B_{1}}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\mathinner{|{x}\rangle\,\langle{x}|}\otimes\mathcal{N}(\rho_{A}^{x}),\quad\rho_{\mathcal{X}B_{2}}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\mathinner{|{x}\rangle\,\langle{x}|}\otimes\mathcal{M}(\rho_{A}^{x}),

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{X}E_{1}}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\mathinner{|{x}\rangle\,\langle{x}|}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{c}(\rho_{A}^{x}),\quad\rho_{\mathcal{X}E_{2}}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\mathinner{|{x}\rangle\,\langle{x}|}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{c}(\rho_{A}^{x}).

Noting that the mutual information under convex combination of orthogonal states is additive, we have

I(\mathcal{X};B)-I(\mathcal{X};E)=p(I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})-I(\mathcal{X};E_{1}))-(1-p)(I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})-I(\mathcal{X};E_{2})).

(81)

Therefore,
I(\mathcal{X};B)-I(\mathcal{X};E)\geq 0
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})-I(\mathcal{X};E_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})-I(\mathcal{X};E_{2})}\geq\frac{1-p}{p}.

(82)

This holds ture if
p\geq\frac{1}{1+\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}(1-\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}})}
. In fact,

\displaystyle\frac{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})-I(\mathcal{X};E_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})-I(\mathcal{X};E_{2})}=\frac{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})}\left(\frac{1-\frac{I(\mathcal{X};E_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})}}{1-\frac{I(\mathcal{X};E_{2})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})}}\right)\geq\frac{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})}\left(1-\frac{I(\mathcal{X};E_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})}\right).

Using
(
10
, Proposition 2.3)
, we see that, for any fixed
\eta>0
, for any
\rho_{\mathcal{X}A}
with
\tr_{\mathcal{X}}(\rho_{\mathcal{X}A})=\sigma_{A}
, we have
I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})\geq\eta I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})
if and only if, for any
\rho_{A}
with
\text{supp}(\rho_{A})\subseteq\text{supp}(\sigma_{A})
, we have
D(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{A})\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{A}))\geq\eta D(\mathcal{M}(\rho_{A})\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma_{A}))
. Therefore, we have
\frac{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})}\geq\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}
with the relative expansion coefficient
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}
defined by (
4
). Similarly, we have
\frac{I(\mathcal{X};E_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})}\leq\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
with the contraction coefficient defined by (
1
). Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{I(\mathcal{X};B_{1})-I(\mathcal{X};E_{1})}{I(\mathcal{X};B_{2})-I(\mathcal{X};E_{2})}\geq\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}(1-\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}})\geq\frac{1-p}{p}

if
p\geq\frac{1}{1+\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}(1-\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}})}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

6.2
Explicit construction using amplitude damping channels

For two amplitude damping channels with parameter
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in(0,1)
, we define its probabilistic mixture as

\displaystyle\Psi_{p,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(\rho)=p\mathinner{|{0}\rangle\,\langle{0}|}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}}(\rho)+(1-p)\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}(\rho).

The regions of degradability and anti-degradability are given in

For

\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\leq 1
.

2.

For

\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\leq 1

For

On the other hand,

For any quantum channel

Lemma 6.3
.

For

\displaystyle\rho-\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}x&z\\

for some

,

After application of an amplitude damping channel, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}(\rho-\sigma)=\begin{pmatrix}(1-\gamma)x&\sqrt{1-\gamma}z\\
\sqrt{1-\gamma}z^{*}&-(1-\gamma)x\end{pmatrix},\quad\tr(|\mc A_{\gamma}(\rho- \sigma)|)=2\sqrt{(1-\gamma)^{2}x^{2}+(1-\gamma)|z|^{2}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\tr(| \mc A_{\gamma}(\rho)- \mc A_{\gamma}(\sigma)|)}{\tr(|\rho- \sigma|)}=\sqrt{\frac{(1-\gamma)^{2}x^{2}+(1-\gamma)|z|^{2}}{x^{2}+|z|^{2}}}\in[1-\gamma,\sqrt{1-\gamma}].

7
Conclusion and Open Problems

In this work, we explored the DPI and reverse DPI of relative entropy through contraction and expansion coefficients of quantum channels. We first showed that channels with greater input dimension than output dimension cannot have non-zero expansion coefficient, and hence can not fulfill a reverse DPI. We studied relative expansion and contraction coefficients and gave quantitative estimates for several important pairs of quantum channels, including in particular pairs of amplitude damping channels.
Based on those new estimates for amplitude damping channels, we provide the first rigorous construction of level-1 less noisy channels which are not degradable. Several interesting open problems remain, some of which we list below.

Other information measures.
Quantum DPI, contraction coefficients, and partial orders can also be defined with respect to other information measures, such as quantum
f
-divergences
[
41
,
42
]
, which have been explored in prior works
[
43
,
10
,
7
,
44
]
. Some of the results established in this work can be generalized to other information measures in a straightforward way. For example, Theorem
3.1
holds for a larger class of functions beyond the
f
-divergence that corresponds to the relative entropy. However, establishing the equivalence between
f
-divergences and their Riemannian metric can be intriguing and we leave it for future work.

Complete relative contraction and expansion coefficients.

Another interesting question pertains to the definition of relative contraction and expansion coefficients in terms of the quantum mutual information with fully quantum systems. Using mathematical terminology, it asks whether our inequalites tensorize. Specifically, we propose the following complete versions of these coefficients:

Definition 7.1
.

We define the complete relative contraction and expansion coefficients as:

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}^{cb}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\rho_{VA}}\frac{I(V;B_{1})}{I(V;B_{2})},\quad\widecheck{\eta}^{cb}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}=\inf_{\rho_{VA}}\frac{I(V;B_{1})}{I(V;B_{2})}.

Here,
\mathcal{N}:\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B_{1}})
and
\mathcal{M}:\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B_{1}})
, and
I(V;B)
denotes the mutual information.

The above quantities are non-trivial. In fact, using the joint convexity of the relative entropy, we can easily obtain that
\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{p}}^{cb}\leq 1-p<1
for the depolarizing channel as defined in (
44
). Extending techniques from this work,
[
11
]
and
[
34
]
to bound complete relative expansion and contraction coefficients remains an open question. In comparison, this definition of complete contraction coefficient
\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1},\id}^{cb}
differs from the one proposed in
[
10
]
, which is always equal to 1. Another type of tensorization

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{\rho^{n}\neq\sigma^{n}}\frac{D\bigl(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(\rho^{n})\,\big\|\,\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(\sigma^{n})\bigr)}{D\bigl(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}(\rho^{n})\,\big\|\,\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}(\sigma^{n})\bigr)}

(87)

is also known not to tensorize well for relative entropy
[
45
]
. Therefore, the tensorization proposed in Definition
7.1
is a promising tensorization approach.
Using the construction outlined in Proposition
6.1
, for two degradable channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
, if
\widecheck{\eta}^{cb}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}>0
and
\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1},\id}^{cb}<1
where
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is the degrading channel
\mathcal{D}_{1}\circ\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}^{c}
, then for any
p\in\left[\frac{1}{1+\widecheck{\eta}^{cb}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}(1-\eta_{\mathcal{D}_{1},\id}^{cb})},1\right]
, the channel

\displaystyle\Psi_{p,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}=p\mathinner{|{0}\rangle\,\langle{0}|}\otimes\mathcal{N}+(1-p)\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{c}

is informationally degradable which leads to additivity of quantum capacity.

Closed-form expressions.
Numerically computing exact values of the relative expansion coefficient
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}
involves optimizing over pairs of density operators, which is generally computationally expensive. To obtain exact values of
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}}
for our examples, we represent a density operator
\rho\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
through its purification
\ket{\psi}\in\mathbb{C}^{d^{2}}
, which can be expressed as a unit real vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2d^{2}}
. The function

F(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{D(\mathcal{M}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\sigma))}

is then viewed as a function of two unit real vectors
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d^{2}}
and optimized using standard numerical schemes such as
fminunc
in MATLAB. To mitigate the risk of the optimization being trapped in local minima, we randomize the initial values and select the global minimum from multiple runs.

For several of our qubit channel examples, we observe that the optimum is achieved around a certain state with a channel-dependent perturbation. This observation leads us to conjecture closed-form expressions for the relative contraction and relative expansion coefficients of the amplitude damping channel.

For two amplitude damping channels with
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in(0,1)
, we observe that the infimum appearing in
\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}
is achieved around the state
\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}
. Specifically, setting
\rho=\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\mathinner{|{0}\rangle\,\langle{0}|}+(1-\varepsilon)\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}
, we find that

\widecheck{\eta}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{D(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}}(\rho)\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}}(\sigma_{\varepsilon}))}{D(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}(\rho)\|\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}(\sigma_{\varepsilon}))}=\frac{\gamma_{2}(1-\gamma_{1})}{\gamma_{1}(1-\gamma_{2})},

which matches perfectly with the numerical results obtained from the standard procedure. We therefore conjecture that
\widecheck{\eta}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}=\widecheck{\eta}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}
.

For the relative contraction coefficient
\eta_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}
, let
\rho=(1-p)\mathinner{|{0}\rangle\,\langle{0}|}+p\mathinner{|{1}\rangle\,\langle{1}|}
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\rho+\varepsilon\sigma_{x}
, where
\sigma_{x}
is the Pauli-X operator. Optimizing over
p\in[0,1]
and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we can obtain an explicit formula

\eta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}=\frac{1-\gamma_{1}}{1-\gamma_{2}}\,c(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}),

where

\displaystyle c(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\max_{p\in[0,1]}\frac{(1-2(1-\gamma_{2})p)\left[\log(1-(1-\gamma_1)p)-\log((1-\gamma_1)p)\right]}{(1-2(1-\gamma_{1})p)\left[\log(1-(1-\gamma_2)p)-\log((1-\gamma_2)p)\right]}.

This formula perfectly matches the numerical results for computing the true value of
\eta_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}
. We therefore conjecture that
\eta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}=\eta_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}}
.

For two dephasing channels, numerical evidence shows that the optimizers of
\widecheck{\eta}_{\Phi_{p_{1}},\Phi_{p_{2}}}
for
p_{1},p_{2}\in(0,1)
are around a pure state. However, the exact value of
\widecheck{\eta}_{\Phi_{p_{1}},\Phi_{p_{2}}}
depends on the choice of the pure state, and we do not obtain a closed-form formula based on this method.

To prove
(\eta_{\mathcal{N}}^{\tr})^{2}\leq\eta_{\mathcal{N}}
, we show that for any Hermitian traceless operator
X
, there exists a density operator
\sigma
, such that

\frac{(\tr|\mathcal{N}(X)|)^{2}}{(\tr|X|)^{2}}\leq\frac{g_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{N}(X))}{g_{\sigma}(X)}.

(88)

Then via Lemma
4.1
, we conclude the proof by taking the supremum over
X
. To show (
88
), we claim that for any Hermitian traceless operator
X
and density operator
\sigma
, we have

(\tr|X|)^{2}\leq g_{\sigma}(X).

(89)

In fact, given
X
, denote
\mathcal{E}_{X}
as the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto the sub-algebra generated by
X
(in particular
\mathcal{E}_{X}(X)=X
), then using data processing inequality, we have

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}(X)=\langle X,\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(X)\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\mathcal{E}_{X}(X),\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}_{X}(\sigma)}(\mathcal{E}_{X}(X))\rangle=\langle X,\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}_{X}(\sigma)}(X)\rangle

\displaystyle=\tr(X \int_0^{\infty} (\mc E_{X}(\sigma) +r I)^{-1} X (\mc E_{X}(\sigma) +r I)^{-1} dr)

\displaystyle=\tr(\mc E_{X}(\sigma)^{-1} X^2)=\tr(\mc E_{X}(\sigma))\cdot\tr(\mc E_{X}(\sigma)^{-1} X^2)

\displaystyle\geq\big(\tr(\mc E_{X}(\sigma)^{1/2} \mc E_{X}(\sigma)^{-1/2} |X|)\big)^{2}=(\tr|X|)^{2},

where the integral calculation follows from the fact that
\mathcal{E}_{X}(\sigma)
commutes with
X
and the last inequality is Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.

Then replacing
X
by
\mathcal{N}(X)
and
\sigma
by
\mathcal{N}(\sigma)
in (
89
), we have

\displaystyle(\tr|\mathcal{N}(X)|)^{2}\leq g_{\sigma}(X).

To compare the denominator in (
88
), we choose a special density operator

\sigma=\frac{|X|}{\tr(|X|)},\quad X\neq 0.

(90)

Then using the commutativity of
\sigma
and
X
, we have

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}(X)=\tr(\sigma^{-1} X^2 )=(\tr|X|)^{2}.

In summary, we proved (
88
) with
\sigma
given by (
90
) thus finished the proof of
(\eta_{\mathcal{N}}^{\tr})^{2}\leq\eta_{\mathcal{N}}
.

To prove
\eta_{\mathcal{N}}\leq\eta_{\mathcal{N}}^{\tr}
, we use the
L^{1}
-type integral representation of relative entropy, given by

D(\rho\|\sigma)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{s}E_{s}(\rho\|\sigma)+\frac{1}{s^{2}}E_{s}(\sigma\|\rho)\right)ds,

(91)

where the Hockey-Stick divergence
E_{s}(\rho\|\sigma)
is given by

E_{s}(\rho\|\sigma):=\tr\big( (\rho- s \sigma)_+ \big).

(92)

We refer the reader to
(
7
, Corollary 2.3)
and
(
46
, Theorem 6)
for the proof of (
91
). For the Hockey-Stick divergence, we have

\eta_{s}(\mathcal{N}):=\sup_{\rho\neq\sigma}\frac{E_{s}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))}{E_{s}(\rho\|\sigma)}=\sup_{\ket{\psi},\ket{\phi}}E_{s}(\mathcal{N}(\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\,\langle{\psi}|})\|\mathcal{N}(\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle\,\langle{\phi}|}))\leq\eta_{\tr}(\mathcal{N}),

(93)

where the last equality is proved in
[
47
]
and the inequality follows from
E_{s}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq E_{1}(\rho\|\sigma)
.
Then given any
\rho,\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{s}E_{s}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))+\frac{1}{s^{2}}E_{s}(\mathcal{N}(\sigma)\|\mathcal{N}(\rho))\right)ds

(94)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{s}\eta_{s}(\mathcal{N})E_{s}(\rho\|\sigma)+\frac{1}{s^{2}}\eta_{s}(\mathcal{N})E_{s}(\sigma\|\rho)\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{s}\eta_{\tr}(\mathcal{N})E_{s}(\rho\|\sigma)+\frac{1}{s^{2}}\eta_{\tr}(\mathcal{N})E_{s}(\sigma\|\rho)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\eta_{\tr}(\mathcal{N})D(\rho\|\sigma),

which concludes the proof. Note that the first inequality uses the definition of
\eta_{s}(\mathcal{N})
and for the second inequality, we used (
93
).

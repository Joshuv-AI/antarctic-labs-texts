Effects of two warm-up protocols on isokinetic knee strength, jumping ability and sprint swimming performance in competitive swimmers
Authors: Zixiang Zhou, Pedro G Morouço, Athanasios A Dalamitros, Chao Chen, Weijia Cui, Rui Wu, Jian Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe purpose of this study was to examine the effects of different dryland warm-up protocols on knee isokinetic strength, vertical jumping, and sprint performance in competitive swimmers. Twenty-four male swimmers were randomly assigned to the FIFA 11 + group (n = 12) or the post-activation performance enhancement (PAPE) group (n = 12). The FIFA 11 + group performed the FIFA 11 + warm-up program three times per week for 6 weeks, whereas the PAPE group consisted of performing general activities and three sets of five drop jumps. Performance test included concentric (con) and eccentric (ecc) isokinetic strength of the quadriceps (Q) and hamstrings (H) of the dominant (DL) and non-dominant (NDL) limbs at three angular velocities, countermovement jump (CMJ), squat jump (SJ) and a 50-m time trial. The conventional (Hcon: Qcon) and functional (Hecc: Qcon) ratios were calculated pre- and post- intervention. The FIFA 11 + group showed a significant increase in DL-Hcon (p < 0.05, ES = 1.0-1.8), and NDL-Hcon (p < 0.05, ES = 1.0-1.6) in all angular velocities, and DL-Qcon (p < 0.05, ES = 0.9) at 60°·s− 1. The PAPE group significantly improved DL-Qcon at 180°·s− 1, 240°·s− 1 (both p < 0.05, ES = 1.0), and NDL-Qcon at 240°·s− 1 (p < 0.05, ES = 0.9). Additionally, FIFA 11 + showed significant improvement in DL-Hcon and NDL-Hcon (both p < 0.05, ES = 0.9–1.8) compared with PAPE. FIFA 11 + increased DL-Hcon: Qcon60 (p < 0.05, ES = 1.1) and NDL- Hcom: Qcon60,240 (both p < 0.05, ES = 1.2) compared with PAPE. Both groups significantly improved CMJ (FIFA 11 + p < 0.01, ES = 0.4 and PAPE p < 0.01, ES = 0.3), SJ (both p < 0.01, ES = 0.6) and 50-m swimming performance (FIFA 11 + p < 0.05, ES = -0.8 and PAPE p < 0.05, ES = -0.6).Both warm-up protocols showed similar improvements in jumping ability and sprint swimming performance. The FIFA 11 + seemed more efficient in improving lower limb isokinetic strength and conventional strength ratios in male competitive swimmers.Keywords: Knee strength ratio, Lower limb strength, Post-activation performance enhancement, Elite swimmersSubject terms: Musculoskeletal system, Physiology

Introduction

Swimming has been a sport of the Olympic Games since 1896. Nowadays, swimming has evolved into a year-round sport that requires substantial amounts of training, both in and out of the pool1. Many studies have demonstrated that well-developed levels of lower-limb strength have a positive effect on performance improvement in swimmers2–4. Sufficient lower limb muscle strength to enhance leg kick performance is critical, thus increasing swimming velocity and keep the body in a streamlined position5. Keiner et al. identified a strong relationship between squat jump (SJ) height and sprint swimming performance (r = -0.56 to -0.78)2. Likewise, a high correlation values were also found between countermovement jump (CMJ) height and swimming starting performance (r = − 0.69)4. In addition to the benefits of strength enhancement to swimming performance, it is essential to consider injury prevention gains. Knee injuries are the second most prevalent injury site in swimmers following shoulder injuries6. According to Soder et al.,7, 69.2% of the young elite swimmers had abnormal knee injury results in their magnetic resonance imaging pictures, and most reported pain around the patella and the patellar-femoral joint. Moreover, a cross-sectional survey study of 488 Japan national-team swimmers suggested that the incidence of knee joint injuries (20%) showed an increasing trend during the last 15 years8.

Many studies have shown that muscular strength deficits, and hamstring (H) and quadriceps(Q) strength imbalances are risk factors for knee injury in athletes9,10. Generally, H:Q conventional ratios below 0.6 may increase the risk of knee injury10. Previous findings demonstrated that swimmers’ H: Q conventional ratios (0.46–0.53) were lower than the normal value11. Dalamitros et al. also indicated that since unilateral knee strength imbalance (H: Q conventional ratios = 0.51–0.53) was detected in swimmers, it is advisable to conduct specific exercises in training to enhance muscle strength and regular tests to identify the recommended H: Q ratios12. Therefore, muscular strength imbalances should be identified and corrected to effectively prevent and rehabilitate injuries in swimmers, since a normal balance between the hamstring and quadriceps may reduce the risk of injury9.

Warming-up is an essential part of an athletes’ training session or prior to a competition. Warm-up programs are typically composed of light to moderate intensity exercises that aim to improve performance and prevent injury by boosting flow and neuron activity of the muscles13,14. A recent review has suggested that warm-up programs that implement or combine dryland activities such as jumps and medicine ball throws can improve subsequent neuromuscular adaptations and swimming performance, which can be attributed to the post-activation performance enhancement (PAPE) produced by dryland exercise15. It is reported that a PAPE warm-up program could significantly improve subsequent jump and dive start performance in swimmers14. However, these studies only investigated the acute effects of a warm-up activity on muscle strength or short-distance swimming performance. As such, the medium- and long-term effects of implementing a specific warm-up on neuromuscular adaptations and swimming performance, consisting an additional primary concern for coaches, remains unclear.

FIFA 11 + is a widely used warm-up programme that includes cardiovascular activation and neuromuscular preventative exercises, which can enhance muscle performance and prevent injuries. The FIFA 11 + programme has been confirmed to prevent and reduce the occurrence of injuries in football, basketball and handball players16–18. It is noteworthy that the FIFA 11 + warm-up programme can also increase knee muscle strength and jump performance19–21. These factors not only help to reduce the injury risk, but also have the potential to improve sprint swimming performance, through increased muscle strength. This is notably important for competitive swimmers, who spend a greater percentage of their training time in the water to enhance their technical and aerobic capacity22. However, to our knowledge, no study has investigated the effects of implementing the FIFA 11 + program on physical performance measures as well as sprint competitive performance in swimmers. There is no experimental evidence suggesting this programme for swimmers is effective. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the training effect of FIFA 11 + and PAPE warm-up programs in mesocycle on knee strength, muscle strength ratios, vertical jumps, and short-distance swimming performance. We hypothesized that the FIFA 11 + programme would show greater benefits on several parameters of physical performance compared with PAPE warm-up.

Materials and methods

Participants

Twenty-four national I level male swimming athletes volunteered to participate in this study(age: 20.3 ± 0.7 years; height: 181.2 ± 3.7 cm; body mass: 79.6 ± 5.5 kg; training experience: 8.1 ± 1.9 years). Based on the study by Pimenta et al.23, sample size calculation was conducted by an a priori analysis using G*power software (3.1; University of Dusseldorf, Dusseldorf, Germany) with the methods of repeated-measures ANOVA between factors (number of groups: 2, number of measurements: 3), with statistical power = 0.95, α level = 0.05, and effect size = 0.46. Consequently, a total sample size of 14 subjects (7 per group) would be required. The participants were selected based on the inclusion criteria listed below: (1) participants aged ≥ 18 years, (2) participants not engaged in FIFA11 + warm-up program in previous session, (3) all participants had to be training at least three times a week, (4) the participants had to be free of musculoskeletal conditions and medical disorders in the tested limb for the last six months. Participants were not included if they had any injuries prior to the study, or if they had participated in a formal injury prevention program in the past. Prior to the intervention, the participants were informed of the study’s purpose and content. All participants provided written informed consent. The study was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration. The study was approved by the Shanghai University of Sport Science Research Ethics Committee (ID number: 102772021RT030).

Procedure

A mixed design exploring both within- and between-groups differences was used to compare the long-term effects of FIFA 11 + and PAPE warm-up protocols on physical measures and sprint swimming performance. The participants were randomly divided into the FIFA 11 + group (n = 12) and the PAPE group (n = 12). All the participates self-reported their dominant (DL) and non-dominant (NDL) limb. The FIFA 11 + warm-up program is shown in Table 1. Based on a previous PAPE warm-up protocol in swimmers14, participants performed a general warm-up (10 min of light skipping, dynamic stretching, and general movement) and three sets of 5-repetition drop jumps (40 cm). We encourage participants to jump as high as possible. Recovery between reps was no longer than 15s, while rests between sets were 120s long. The PAPE protocol has been proven to elicit PAPE responses, and as with FIFA 11+, only dryland activities were included in the program. Moreover, the average duration of the PAPE warm-up protocol was approximately 20 min, matching the average duration of the FIFA 11 + warm-up. Each group conducted the specified warm-up programme three times per week for six weeks. During this period, the participants swam about 85 km, the equivalent to a mean value of 14.17 km per week and 4.72 km per training unit. All participants performed the same swimming training without any resistance training. The training program contained low aerobic (~ 75% of total volume), technical skills (~ 23%), and speed swimming sets (~ 2%). There are no control group in this study for the following two reasons. First, previous studies had already shown that FIFA 11 + was beneficial to muscle strength19,21. The second was because of ethical considerations24. It is unethical to implement a training protocol that aims to enhance physical and sports performance for one group of participants but not for another within the same group, since the final objective for everyone is to achieve their highest sports level. Furthermore, low aerobic and technical skills exercises for 6 weeks will not have positive effects on muscle strength and sprint swimming performance. Thus, the fact of not having a control group will not influence the objective of this study.

All participants in the FIFA 11 + group attended a workshop to learn the correct technique for carrying out the exercises prior to the intervention. The athletes were instructed on how to correctly complete the exercises (including 4 h of practical exercises). Furthermore, they were all provided with instructional videos and illustrations for the intervention programme. A FIFA 11 + coach from a professional football team with over 8 years of experience was fully acquainted with the program. The PAPE group also performed independent exercises to ensure that participants could correct the execution of movements. Drop jumps were executed bouncingly to reduce ground contact time. Participants were instructed to employ explosive arm swings to maximize their drop jump performance. All training sessions were supervised by a researcher collaborator to verify that participants conformed to the programs. Swimmers’ coach was involved throughout the intervention period to ensure that the swimmers adhered to the instructions as much as possible.

The FIFA11 + is divided into 3 parts: running exercises (part 1), six exercises to increase muscle strength, balance, muscular control, and core stability (part 2), and advanced running exercises (part 3). As established by the programme, its difficulty increased every two weeks25. Throughout the training period, the participants were verbally encouraged to focus on the quality of their movements. The participants completed a pre-test before beginning the programme and a post-test after intervention. To avoid diurnal variations in performance, testing procedures were always conducted at the same time of day. All pre- and post-test information was collected by the same researchers. The individuals were not subjected to any intense training or competitions in the 48 h before their tests.

Isokinetic strength test

Peak torque of the hamstring and quadriceps were measured on a IsoMed 2000 isokinetic dynamometer (D&R GmbH, Hemau, Germany), which previously demonstrated a high reliability during knee testing (ICC = 0.90–0.96) in previous study26. All testing sessions began with a 20-minute warm-up program consisting of jogging, short distance fast running, and dynamic stretching. The participants were seated in an adequate and secure posture using fixed straps to avoid additional movement, according to the IsoMed2000 Systems instruction handbook. The rotation axis of the dynamometer and the test knee were aligned, and the gravity compensation procedure were performed. The range of movement (ROM) was set from 90° of flexion to 10° of extension (0°=knee full extension)27. Before the testing, all subjects completed 5 submaximal efforts in concentric (con) and eccentric (ecc) modes for familiarization purposes. After a 5 min recovery period, the subjects performed five maximal knee extension-flexion concentric and eccentric tests at the angular velocity of 60°·s− 1, 180°·s− 1 and 240°·s− 1. Test velocities were selected based on previously reported methods28. There was a 2-minutes rest between each contraction set and between various velocities. The DL and NDL were measured in randomized order. Consistent verbal encouragement was provided during the test, and participants were instructed to push and pull as fast and hard as possible against the dynamometer arm. The single highest peak torque value for each attempt was used for further analysis. The conventional and functional ratios for the DL and NDL were calculated. The conventional ratio was the ratio of the con peak torque of the hamstring versus the con quadriceps at 60°·s− 1 (Hcon: Qcon60), 180°·s− 1 (Hcon: Qcon180) and 240°·s− 1 (Hcon: Qcon240). The functional ratio was determined by the peak torque of the hamstring in ecc mode divided by the peak torque of the quadriceps in con mode at 60°·s− 1 (Hecc: Qcon60), 180°·s− 1 (Hecc: Qcon180) and 240°·s− 1 (Hecc: Qcon240).

Vertical jump performance

The SJ and CMJ are related with the start and turn, respectively, and are therefore regarded as the most appropriate power tests for swimmers29. For the vertical jump performance test, we used the methods described previously30. During the CMJ test, the subjects were instructed to put their hands on their hips and make a rapid downward movement of self-determined depth followed by a vertical maximum height jump, keeping their legs straight throughout. For the SJ, participants were asked to hold a static squat position with 90° knee flexion for 3 s before jumping. For each jump, three trials were conducted with a rest period of 2 min between each trial. The highest performance achieved in these trials was selected for further analysis. Prior to each jump test, two submaximal practice trials with 1 min of recovery were conducted. The jump test was performed on a contact mat (Smart Jump; Fusion Sport, Coopers Plains, Australia). ICCs for the CMJ and SJ were 0.94 (95% CI = 0.87 to 0.98) and 0.96 (95% CI = 0.90 to 0.98), respectively. Participants were encouraged to jump as high as possible in each attempt.

Swimming performance

After a regular warm-up, all swimmers performed a 50 m maximal test using the front crawl technique. The test began at the starting block, and the swimmers started the time trial at the sound of a whistle. Participants were asked to swim as fast as they could. Based on a previous study24, all swimmers completed two maximal trials with a 10 min passive recovery period between the two trials. The test was carried out in a 25-meter indoor swimming pool (~ 27.1 °C of water temperature), performance time was recorded by their coach with a chronometer (CASIO HS-70w-1JH, Japan), and the mean value of both measures was used for further analysis. The ICC was 0.97. Swimming performance tests were evaluated on the same day and in a random order.

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. The normality of all values was checked by the Shapiro-Wilk test. To compare the peak torque and strength ratios between groups (FIFA 11 + and PAPE ), time (pre-intervention, post-intervention) and angular velocities (60°·s− 1, 180°·s− 1, and 240°·s− 1) the 2 × 2 × 3 repeated measures mixed ANOVA was used separately for the DL and NDL according to the statistical method proposed previously31. A two-way (group × time) ANOVA for repeated measures was used to detect statistical differences during the jump and swimming performances. Bonferroni post-hoc analysis was conducted to determine pair wise differences. The effect size (ES) was computed using Cohen’s d to compare the practical significance of the isokinetic strength, vertical jump and swimming performance improvements within and between groups. An effect size < 0.2 was considered minimal, 0.2–0.5 small, 0.5–0.8 moderate, and > 0.8 large. Statistical significance level was set at p < 0.05. All data analyses were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corp. Amork, NY) and PRISM (GraphPad Software, Inc. Version 9.0.2 for Windows).

Results

Before the pre-intervention, there were no significant differences (p > 0.05) in peak torque, vertical jump and swimming performance between the FIFA 11 + and PAPE group. The mean values and changes in the performance assessment are displayed in Table 2. For the peak torque of DL-Hcon, a significant group × angular velocity interaction was observed at post-intervention(F = 6.051, p = 0.005). The FIFA 11 + showed a significant within-group improvement following the intervention (60°·s− 1 = 24.3Nm, [95%CI = 11.8 to 36.9 Nm], p = 0.001) (180°·s− 1 = 14.8Nm, [95%CI = 2.3 to 27.3 Nm], p = 0.022) (240°·s− 1 = 18.8Nm, [95%CI = 7.2 to 30.3 Nm], p = 0.003). No significant difference was observed for the PAPE group (p > 0.05). After the intervention, the FIFA 11 + group performed significantly better than the PAPE group at 60°·s− 1 (F = 18.641, p < 0.001), 180°·s− 1 (F = 4.513, p = 0.045) and 240°·s− 1 (F = 8.239, p = 0.009) of angular velocity.

For the peak torque of NDL-Hcon, a significant interaction effect was detected (F = 7.503, p = 0.002) after the intervention, with higher values in the FIFA 11 + compared to the PAPE at 60°·s− 1 (F = 20.342, p < 0.001), 180°·s− 1 (F = 5.246, p = 0.032) and 240°·s− 1 (F = 15.163, p = 0.001). In the FIFA 11 + group, a significant increase was found after the intervention (60°·s− 1 = 23.5Nm, [95%CI = 11.3 to 35.7 Nm], p = 0.001) (180°·s− 1 = 15.0Nm, [95%CI = 2.2 to 27.8 Nm], p = 0.024) (240°·s− 1 = 18.0Nm, [95%CI = 7.2 to 28.8 Nm], p = 0.002). No significant within-group difference (p > 0.05) in the PAPE was observed at all angular velocities following intervention.

No significant group × angular velocity interactions were observed for peak torque of DL-Qcon (F = 2.653, p = 0.082) at post-intervention. After the intervention, the FIFA 11 + group exhibited significant within-group improvement at 60°·s− 1 (14.7Nm, [95%CI = 0.4 to 28.9 Nm], p = 0.044). In other angular velocities, peak torque also showed increase at post-intervention, but not significantly (p > 0.05). The PAPE group revealed significant within-group improvement at 180°·s− 1 (18.7Nm, [95%CI = 3.6 to 33.7 Nm], p = 0.017) and 240°·s− 1 (17.0Nm, [95%CI = 2.6 to 31.4 Nm], p = 0.023). In addition, there was no significant difference (p > 0.05) between groups at all angular velocities after the intervention.

There was no significant group × angular velocity interactions for the NDL-Qcon (F = 1.176, p = 0.316) after the intervention. In the FIFA 11+, peak torque at three angular velocities increased from pre-to post-intervention, but these were not significant (p > 0.05). In the PAPE group, the peak torque of NDL-Qcon difference between pre- and post-intervention was found at 240°·s− 1 (14.9Nm, [95%CI = 1.4 to 28.4 Nm], p = 0.032). No significant difference (p > 0.05) between groups were found post-intervention.

A test for interaction between the two groups for the peak torque of DL-Hecc revealed no significant interaction (F = 0.089, p = 0.915) at post-intervention. No significant within-group changes were found in FIFA 11 + and PAPE before and after the intervention (p > 0.05). No significant difference (p > 0.05) between groups were found post-intervention.

For the peak torque of Hecc in the NDL, there was no significant interaction (F = 1.005, p = 0.359) between the groups following intervention. Both groups exhibited no significant differences (p > 0.05) within or between groups differences after intervention.

Conventional ratios

No significant group × angular velocity interactions were observed for the DL-Hcon: Qcon ratios (F = 0.338, p = 0.715) at post-intervention. Both groups displayed no significant differences (p > 0.05) within-group differences after intervention (Fig. 1). Yet, as can be seen from Figure, there was a clear upward trend for the conventional ratios at all angular velocities in the FIFA 11 + group. The FIFA 11 + group showed significantly better than the PAPE group in Hcon/Qcon60 (p = 0.021) following the intervention. For the NDL-Hcon/Qcon, there was no significant interaction (F = 2.452, p = 0.113) between the groups after the intervention. Although there was an increase in the FIFA 11 + group following intervention, the difference was not significant (p > 0.05). In the PAPE group, there were no significant within-group changes before and after the intervention (p > 0.05). In addition, the between-group comparison showed a significant difference in Hcon/Qcon60 (p = 0.008) and Hcon/Qcon240 (p = 0.012) after the intervention.

Functional ratios

For the DL-Hecc: Qcon, there was no significant group × angular velocity interactions (F = 1.418, p = 0.253) after the intervention (Fig. 1). Both FIFA 11 + group and PAPE group exhibited no significant within-group changes before and after the intervention (p > 0.05). For the NDL-Hecc: Qcon, no significant interactions were observed (F = 1.743, p = 0.187) at post-intervention. Both groups showed no significant differences (p > 0.05) within-group differences at all angular velocities following the intervention. After the intervention, there were no significant differences (p > 0.05) in DL-Hecc: Qcon and NDL-Hecc: Qcon ratios at all angular velocities between groups.

Vertical jump performance

For the CMJ test, no significant group × time interactions were observed (F = 0.027, p = 0.870), but there was a main effect of time (F = 75.659, p < 0.001). The FIFA 11 + group exhibited a significant within-group improvement (1.292 cm, [95%CI = 0.848 to 1.736 cm], p = 0.001). The PAPE also exhibited a significant within-group improvement after intervention(1.342 cm, [95%CI = 0.898 to 1.786 cm], p = 0.001). In addition, no significant difference (p > 0.05) between groups was found post-intervention. For the SJ, a significant main effect for time (F = 260.778, p < 0.001) was reported along with a significant group × time interaction effect (F = 6.320, p = 0.020). Post-hoc analysis found both groups exhibited a significant within-group improvement in the SJ (FIFA 11 + = 1.825 cm, [95%CI = 1.538 to 2.112 cm], p < 0.001) (PAPE = 1.333 cm, [95%CI = 1.047 to 1.620 cm], p < 0.001). However, analysis also revealed no significant difference between groups post-intervention (p > 0.05).

Swimming performance

For 50-m swimming performance, no significant group × time interactions were observed (F = 0.015, p = 0.903 ). There was a significant main effect over time (F = 14.461, p = 0.001). Both groups exhibited a significant within-group improvement (FIFA 11+ = -0.133s, [95%CI = -0.233 to -0.034s], p = 0.011) (PAPE = -0.125s, [95% CI, -0.225 to -0.025s], p = 0.016). No significant difference between groups were found post-intervention (p > 0.05).

Discussion

The aim of this study was to compare the effect of the FIFA 11 + and a PAPE warm-up program on knee isokinetic strength, vertical jump, and 50-m swimming performance in male competitive swimmers. To our knowledge, this is the first intervention study that analyzed the effects of different warm-up protocols in swimming. Our hypotheses were partially confirmed. The main results indicated significant strength gains in peak torque for Hcon after the FIFA 11 + intervention. Qcon and Hecc strength value showed increasing trends in both FIFA 11 + and PAPE groups (within-group before and after comparison), but there was no difference between the groups following the intervention. For conventional ratios, the FIFA 11 + group exhibited a significant increase in the DL (60°·s− 1) and NDL (60°·s− 1 and 240°·s− 1), whereas no significant differences were found in functional ratios. In addition, FIFA 11 + and PAPE groups showed similar improvements in CMJ, SJ and 50-m swimming performances.

In order to achieve optimal swimming performance, swimmers must possess a high level of lower-limb muscle strength. One of the major findings was that maximum strength increased significantly in the FIFA 11 + group at all angular velocities in the DL-Hcon (+ 15.2–22.1%) and NDL-Hcon (+ 16.1–22.1%). This result was supported by previously published studies. In the study of Daneshjoo et al.21, the peak torque of the DL-Hcon and NDL-Hcon was significant increased at 60°·s− 1 (+ 22% and 22.3%, respectively), 180°·s− 1 (+ 21.4% and 15.7%, respectively) and 300°·s− 1 (+ 22.1% and 15.2%, respectively) after the FIFA 11 + intervention in soccer players. Ghareeb et al.32 also noted that FIFA 11 + programme can significant improve maximum strength values in both DL-Hcon and NDL-Hcon at 60°·s− 1, 180°·s− 1 and 300°·s− 1. After the FIFA 11 + intervention, Qcon peak torque was significantly increased (+ 7.2%, ES = 0.9) at 60°·s− 1 in the DL. For the PAPE group, significant improvement was observed in the DL-Qcon at 180°·s− 1 (+ 10.8%, ES = 1.0) and 240°·s− 1 (+ 11.6%, ES = 1.0), and NDL-Qcon at 240°·s− 1 (+ 10.3%, ES = 0.9) following the intervention. It can be seen that the PAPE group showed greater improvement compared with the FIFA group in the Qcon strength, especially at higher angular velocities. The magnitude of improvement in the Qcon peak torque at regarding other angular velocities was 5.9 to 7.8% (ES = 0.5–0.8) and 5.1 to 7.6% (ES = 0.5–0.7) for FIFA 11 + and PAPE, respectively. Although there was no statistically significant difference, both groups exhibited an increasing trend. Daneshjoo et al. reported that FIFA 11 + did not improve Qcon strength (p > 0.05), however, in that case, peak torque was also increased by 6.0-22.6% compared to the pre-test21. However, Brito et al. indicated that a 10-week FIFA 11 + intervention can significant improve peak torque of DL-Qcon and NDL-Qcon at 60°·s− 1 and 180°·s− 1 in soccer players19. Ghareeb et al. also found similar results, demonstrating that the peak torque of DL-Qcon (p < 0.01) and NDL-Qcon (p < 0.01) at 180°·s− 1 and 300°·s− 1 were increased after the intervention32. These differences may be attributed to variables such as the different playing level and intervention times. In the studies of Brito et al. and Ghareeb. et al., non-elite and high school players were recruited, and longer intervention were performed. Therefore, it seems that the FIFA 11 + warm-up programme has the potential to improve quadriceps muscle strength.

Considering DL-Hecc and NDL-Hecc, our results suggest that peak torque did not change (p > 0.05) following the FIFA 11 + and PAPE training. However, Reis et al. reported that the 12-week FIFA 11 + can significantly increase (p < 0.05) the peak torque of DL-Hecc (+ 12.7%) and NDL-Hecc (+ 9.3%) at 30°·s− 1 in youth futsal players33. The differences in Hecc peak torque between different studies observed may be at least partially explained by differences (12-week vs. 6-week) in programme durations. In order to improve the swimming speed, the knee must rapidly and repeatedly perform flexion and extension. The hamstring operates eccentrically to slow knee extension and counter the quadriceps powerful concentric contraction in this process. High intrinsic forces are produced by this eccentric contraction inside the hamstring. If the hamstring muscles are weaker than the quadriceps muscles, it not only decreases the kicking efficiency but also creates a muscle strength imbalance, which can increase the risk of knee joint injuries during long-term training5,28,34. Current research suggests that both warm-up protocols does not have the potential to increase Hecc strength.

Strength imbalances between the quadriceps and hamstring muscles are considered risk factors for knee injuries8,9. Previous studies noted that Hcon: Qcon ratios of less than 0.60 increased the risk of hamstring injury by 17 times10,28. The present results revealed that Hcon: Qcon ratios of the FIFA 11 + group increased from the DL (0.56, 0.58, and 0.57, respectively) and NDL (0.55, 0.56, and 0.59, respectively) in pre-intervention to the DL (0.63, 0.62, and 0.63, respectively) and NDL (0.62, 0.61, and 0.66, respectively) in post-intervention at 60°·s− 1, 180°·s− 1, and 240°·s− 1. We believe that the obtained increase in the conventional ratios for the DL and NDL found in FIFA 11 + group can be explained by the improvement in Hcon strength being greater than the increase in Qcon, thus correcting the strength imbalance in the antagonist and agonist of the knee joint. In the long run, the correct proportions between the hamstring and quadriceps will certainly be beneficial for knee injury prevention. For the functional ratios, no significant changes were observed between and within groups. Viewed as a whole, there was a slight decrease in the Hecc: Qcon ratio in both groups. Our results are consistent with those of Daneshjoo et al.35. However, Reis et al. indicated that functional ratios can be slightly enhanced after 12 weeks of FIFA 11 + in youth futsal players33. It is presently unclear what accounts for these differences. A healthy H: Q functional ratio is reported to be 1.031. As a result, we recommend eccentric hamstring exercises be included to swimmers’ strength training programs, such as the Nordic hamstring exercise program.

According to research, both the CMJ and SJ are related to swimming start and turn, respectively, and are therefore regarded as the most suitable strength assessments for swimmers36. Our findings regarding the CMJ and SJ test showed significant within-group improvements in both groups. FIFA 11 + and PAPE groups presented a similar effect size in the CMJ (0.4 and 0.3, respectively) and SJ (0.6 and 0.6, respectively) performances. These results confirmed previously reported positive effects (+ 3.7–6%) of FIFA11 + intervention on CMJ performance20. Furthermore, research has indicated that after a PAPE warm-up, the index of explosive strength and reactive strength index of swimmers is significantly enhanced in the subsequent CMJ test14. According to our findings, the PAPE warm-up also had long-term advantages for vertical jump performance. This is beneficial in competitive swimming as higher increments in jump results can contribute to the production of greater push-off power36 and the enhancement of maximal swimming speed24,37. This may be because better jump performance improves start and turn results, which contributes to better swimming performance. Additionally, better jump performance indicates more developed lower limb strength, which may help increase kick speed and thus enhance swimming speed. Thus, it is reasonable to believe that higher CMJ and SJ values have a positive effect in improving overall swimming performance.

Our findings regarding the 50-m performance showed significant within-group improvements in FIFA 11+ (p = 0.011, ES = 0.8) and PAPE (p = 0.016, ES = 0.6) groups. We cannot directly compare our results with other findings since no study has evaluated the training effects of the FIFA 11 + programme on water sports. However, several studies have demonstrated a positive effect of FIFA 11 + on 20-m sprint time (+ 2.9%) in football and 30-m sprint time (+ 3.3%) in futsal players38. Đurović et al. found that the PAPE warm-up can elicit an acute effect on the 15-m start performance14. A reason for this might be that both FIFA 11 + and PAPE warm protocols included plyometric exercises, which have an improved effect on power output and explosive strength39. In the present study, both groups showed an increase in lower limb strength and vertical jump performance after the intervention period. It has been reported that muscle strength of the lower limbs and performance during the CMJ and SJ are strongly related to swimming performance2,40. Gourgoulis et al. also indicated that strength level of the lower limbs is an important factor in determining swimming performance, as greater knee flexion and extension strength contributes to a powerful and fast leg kick, thereby increasing swimming speed5. Swimming is a highly competitive sport in which even a small time difference can have an effect on match outcomes. Therefore, even though both groups only showed a 0.125–0.133 s improvement in 50-m swimming performance, this variation in performance can still be considered as crucial.

Warm-up exercises are routinely performed before training sessions and/or competition. According to the most recent literature review15, regular swimming warm-up types are divided into in-water exercise only, dryland exercise only and in-water exercise combined with dryland exercise, which is the most recommended warm-up type for swimming. However, this is not to say that FIFA 11 + and PAPE warm-up can replace regular dryland warm-up exercise or resistance training, but rather that it can be used as a supplement due to its medium- and long-term strength benefits. From these data, the FIFA 11 + warm-up appears to be slightly better than the PAPE protocol. In addition to the strength benefits, another advantage of the FIFA 11 + programme is its time efficiency, as it takes less time compared to in-water warm-up only and in-water exercise combined with dryland exercise. Further research is required to investigate the long-term effects of different warm-up protocols, such as in-water only and in-water combined with dryland exercise, on physical measures and swimming performance. Additionally, the lack of quantification of the training intensity of the two warm-up programs during the intervention is a limitation of this study. In future studies, a control group should be added to better compare the effects of different warm-up programs.

Conclusions

The main findings of the present study demonstrated the long-term benefits of FIFA 11 + and PAPE warm-up protocols in lower limb strength, vertical jump performance, and 50-m swimming time in swimmers. However, FIFA 11 + warm-up protocol has a greater potential to improve knee joint strength balance, which may contribute to injury prevention. Swimming coaches and strength and conditioning coaches should consider incorporating the FIF A 11 + programme into the regular warm-up protocol as an alternative option. We also recommend that this programme be implemented in more sports, and its effectiveness be validated through long-term intervention studies.

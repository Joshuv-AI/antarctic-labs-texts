3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

We require a few elementary facts from probability theory in the context of umbral calculus (for background on umbral calculus, see
[
18

).

Lemma 3.1
.

If we let
\mathcal{B}_{n}:=B_{n}
(Bernoulli numbers),
\mathcal{C}_{n}:=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n+1}
, then for positive integers
n
in umbral formalism (treating
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
as random variables) the following are true:

(1)
(\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{D})^{n}=0
.

(2)
(\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{C})^{n}=(-1)^{n}
.

(3)
1+\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{D}
.

(4)
1+\mathcal{B}=-\mathcal{B}
.

(5)
(-\mathcal{B})^{n}=\mathcal{B}^{n}
for
n\neq 1
with with
B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Since these claims are straightforward, we only prove two of them.
To prove (4), we recall the generating function

e^{\mathcal{B}z}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{B}^{n}\cdot\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}\cdot\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{z}{e^{z}-1}.

This implies that

e^{(1+\mathcal{B})z}=\frac{ze^{z}}{e^{z}-1}=\frac{-z}{e^{-z}-1}=e^{(-\mathcal{B})z}.

Similarly, we have that

e^{\mathcal{D}z}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{D}^{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{z}-1}{z}.

Therefore, (1) follows from the identity

e^{(\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{D})z}=\frac{z}{e^{z}-1}\cdot\frac{e^{z}-1}{z}=1=e^{0z},

which means that
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{D}
annihilate each other.
∎

Using this lemma, we provide an umbral expression for
T_{n}(\boldsymbol{x}^{k})
in the spirit of (
2.12

Lemma 3.2
.

If

is a sequence and

\displaystyle T_{n}(\boldsymbol{x}^{k})=\left(p_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{C}\,x_{i}\right)^{n}.

Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)

denote the associated

[
19

where the sum ranges over all

m_{(1,1)}=\sum_{i<j}x_{i}x_{j}

Given a sequence

From Lemma

\left(p_{1}+\sum_{i}\mathcal{C}x_{i}\right)^{n}=\left(\sum_{i}\mathcal{D}x_{i}\right)^{n}.

Then, the multinomial theorem and umbral calculus together gives

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}\,x_{i}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\binom{n}{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}m_{\lambda}.

We apply (

) with
\mathcal{D}
replaced by
\boldsymbol{1}=(1,1,\dots)
and
n

, while we rank partitions according to their

, to reformulate (

) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{x}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{k}(-1)^{t-1}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq n^{\prime}}(x_{j_{1}}+\cdots+x_{j_{t}})^{n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{k}(-1)^{t-1}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq n^{\prime}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq s_{1}<\cdots<s_{\ell}\leq n^{\prime}\\

\ell(\mu)=\ell\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell}}m_{\mu}(x_{s_{1}},\dots,x_{s_{\ell}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{t=\ell}^{k}(-1)^{t-1}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq n^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq s_{1}<\cdots<s_{\ell}\leq n^{\prime}\\
s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}\in\{j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n^{\prime}\\
\ell(\mu)=\ell\end{subarray}}\binom{n^{\prime}}{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell}}m_{\mu}(x_{s_{1}},\dots,x_{s_{\ell}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{t=\ell}^{k}(-1)^{t-1}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq n^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n^{\prime}\\
\ell(\mu)=\ell\end{subarray}}\binom{n^{\prime}}{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell}}\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq s_{1}<\cdots<s_{\ell}\leq n^{\prime}\\
s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}\in\{j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\}\end{subarray}}m_{\mu}(x_{s_{1}},\dots,x_{s_{\ell}})\right\}.

For a given

\{1,\dots,k\}
, counting the subsets

that contain

leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{x}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n^{\prime}\\
\ell(\mu)=\ell\end{subarray}}\binom{n^{\prime}}{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell}}\sum_{1\leq s_{1}<\cdots<s_{\ell}\leq n^{\prime}}m_{\mu}(x_{s_{1}},\dots,x_{s_{\ell}})\sum_{t=\ell}^{k}(-1)^{t-1}\binom{k-\ell}{t-\ell}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n+k\\

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k-1}(n+k)!\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}}{n!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}x_{i}\right)^{n},

where the last equality is due to (

∎

The proof of Theorem
1.5
will also require the following fact about products of formal power series and the (unique) involution
\omega
on symmetric functions, that is determined by the action
\omega(p_{n})=(-1)^{n-1}p_{n}
on power sum functions
(see, for example
[

Lemma 3.3
.

If
f(z)
is a formal power series with constant term
1
and
g(z):=\frac{1}{f(-z)}
, then

\omega\left(\prod_{i}f(zx_{i})\right)=\prod_{i}g(zx_{i}).

Proof.

We begin the proof by letting

L(z)=\log f(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}L_{k}z^{k},

which in turns give
\log g(z)=-L(-z).

Therefore, we find that

\displaystyle\log\left(\prod_{i}f(zx_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\log f(zx_{i})=\sum_{i}\sum_{k}L_{k}x_{i}^{k}z^{k}=\sum_{k}L_{k}p_{k}z^{k},

where
p_{k}
is the
k
th power sum symmetric function. Applying
\omega,
we obtain

\displaystyle\omega\left(\log\left(\prod_{i}f(zx_{i})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k-1}L_{k}p_{k}z^{k}=-\sum_{i}L(-zx_{i})=\sum_{i}\log g(zx_{i}).

The claimed product formula follows by exponentiation.
∎

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}\mathcal{D}x_{i}\right)^{r}

\displaystyle=T_{r}(p_{1},p_{2},\dots)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash r}g^{\lambda}p_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}),

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}x_{i}\right)^{r}

\displaystyle=f_{r}(p_{1},p_{2},\dots)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash r}\widetilde{g}^{\lambda}p_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}),

for some coefficients

and

where

and

Next, one employs Lemma

with the exponential generating functions

respectively, for

Example

We list the first few of the above two sequences of polynomials:

\displaystyle u_{0}

\displaystyle=1,\,\,\,\,\,u_{2}=p_{2},\,\,\,\,\,u_{4}=\frac{5p_{2}^{2}-p_{4}}{3},\,\,\,\,\,u_{6}=\frac{35p_{2}^{3}-42p_{2}p_{4}+16p_{6}}{9}.

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{0}

\displaystyle=1,\,\,\,\,\,\widetilde{u}_{2}=-p_{2},\,\,\,\,\,\widetilde{u}_{4}=\frac{5p_{2}^{2}+p_{4}}{3},\,\,\,\,\,\widetilde{u}_{6}=-\frac{35p_{2}^{3}+42p_{2}p_{4}+16p_{6}}{9}.

Remark
.

In this section, we make important use of Pólya’s cycle index theorem (PCIT)
[
19
, Section 5]

\exp\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}Z_{j}\,\frac{w^{j}}{j}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{j}!}\left(\frac{Z_{j}}{j}\right)^{m_{j}}\right)w^{n},

where we require writing a partition in the
frequency notation

\lambda=(1^{m_{1}}\dots t^{m_{n}})\vdash n
. PCIT is deeply intertwined with other key results and methods, offering a consistent approach to a variety of counting problems that are similar and adjacent to the results in this paper. Specifically, it provides a method for deriving generating functions for structures related to generalized Bernoulli and Bell polynomials, Bernoulli-Barnes polynomials
[
6
]
(with interesting connection to Fourier Dedekind sums), and it forms a crucial part of the standard exponential generating functions due to Touchard.
[
19
, Eq’n. (5.30)]
.

We proceed with some relevant preparatory results. The first of which, Lemma
3.4
), is an explicit and compact umbral representations of
u_{2n}
and
\widetilde{u}_{2n}
(hence of
U_{2n}
, by association) in the form of

u_{2n}=(2n+1)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1+2\mathcal{C}_{i})x_{i}\right)^{2n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{u}_{2n}=(2n+1)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1+2\mathcal{B}_{i})x_{i}\right)^{2n},

where
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{B}
are defined in Lemma
3.1
.
In this context, observe our use of the umbral mechanism for a
k
-tuple of symbol:

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{a_{1}}\mathcal{B}_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots\mathcal{B}_{k}^{a_{k}}\longrightarrow B_{a_{1}}B_{a_{2}}\cdots B_{a_{k}},

where
a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}=n
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For each nonnegative integer
n
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1+2\mathcal{C}_{i})x_{i}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{u_{n}}{n+1}\qquad&\text{if $n$ is even}\\
\,\,\,0\qquad&\text{if $n$ is odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1+2\mathcal{B}_{i})x_{i}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\widetilde{u}_{n}}{n+1}\qquad&\text{if $n$ is even}\\
\,\,\,0\qquad&\text{if $n$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We only prove the second assertion, as the first one follows a similarly. To this end, we recall the series expansion
[
13
, 1.518.1]
for the reciprocal of what we call here the
sinhc
function

\text{sinhc}(t)=\frac{\sinh(t)}{t}
(reminiscent of the
sinc function
) given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\text{sinhc}(t)}=\exp\left(-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{4^{n}B_{2n}}{(2n)(2n)!}\cdot t^{2n}\right).

On the other hand, the (usual) exponential generating function for the Bernoulli numbers takes the form

\exp(\mathcal{B}z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{B}^{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{z}{e^{z}-1}

from which it is natural to deduce that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\exp((1+2\mathcal{B}_{i})x_{i}z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}((1+2\mathcal{B}_{i})x_{i})^{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{2x_{i}z\cdot e^{x_{i}z}}{e^{2x_{i}z}-1}=\frac{1}{\text{sinhc}(x_{i}z)}.

Consequently, (
3.4
) implies the finite product

(3.5)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\text{sinhc}(x_{i}z)}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(z\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1+2\mathcal{B}_{i})x_{i}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1+2\mathcal{B}_{i})x_{i}\right)^{n}.

Combining (
3.3
), (

) and Pólya’s cycle index theorem
[
19
, Section 5]
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1+2\mathcal{B}_{i})x_{i}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\text{sinhc}(x_{i}z)}=\exp\left(-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{4^{n}B_{2n}\,p_{2n}}{(2n)(2n)!}\cdot z^{2n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}z^{2n}\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{j}!}\left(\frac{-4^{j}B_{2j}}{(2j)(2j)!}\right)^{m_{j}}\right)\Psi(E_{\lambda}).

Compare the coefficients of
z^{n}
on both sides, together with (
3.2
), to complete the proof.
∎

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2n+2)2^{2n+1}}\left\{\frac{(p_{1}+\widetilde{u})^{2n+2}-(p_{1}-\widetilde{u})^{2n+2}}{2\widetilde{u}}\right\}.

The case

The argument is similar. We apply Lemma
3.5

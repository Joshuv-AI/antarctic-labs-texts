Mixed virtual element methods for a stress-velocity-rotation formulation in viscoelasticity
Sarvesh Kumar, Utkarsh Rajput, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier

Abstract
In this paper we propose a new mixed virtual element formulation for the numerical approximation of viscoelasticity equations with weakly imposed stress symmetry. The governing equations use the Zener model and are expressed in terms of the principal unknowns of additively decomposed stress into elastic and internal viscoelastic contributions, while the rotation tensor and velocity act as Lagrange multipliers. The time discretisation uses Crank–Nicolson’s scheme. We demonstrate the unique solvability of both semi-discrete and fully-discrete problems by leveraging the properties of suitable local projectors. Moreover, we establish optimal a priori error estimates for all variables that appear in the mixed formulation. To validate our theoretical findings, we present several representative numerical examples that also highlight the features of the proposed formulation.

Introduction

Scope

Viscoelasticity models are used to explain the macroscopic behaviour of a number of realistic materials which, in addition to exhibiting an elastic strain-stress relationship in the stationary state, show a dissipation of the kinetic energy in the dynamic state. Viscoelastic materials have various applications in industry and medical sectors. For instance, in structural engineering, viscoelastic materials are crucial for modelling the behaviour of polymer, in biomechanics, understanding viscoelasticity is essential for analysing the response of muscles and in materials science, viscoelastic behaviour plays a vital role in the performance of composites and damping materials. In the one-dimensional setting, the viscoelastic behaviour can be represented in a rheological manner by a setup of springs and dashpots in serial or parallel. For the specific case of the standard linear model, the rheology has a parallel coupling of one Maxwell component and one spring. Several comprehensive studies and investigation of applications of viscoelastic materials can be found in
borcherdt09
;
gurtin62
;
karamanou05
;
tschoegl89
. Considering wide applications of viscoelasticity, one needs to seek efficient numerical techniques that would provide desirable numerical solution of the viscoelastic model problems. Moreover, numerical approximations of linear viscoelasticity play a crucial role in understanding and predicting the behaviour of viscoelastic materials under various loading conditions. For the numerical simulation and analysis of a variety of formulations for the viscoelasticity problem we refer to, e.g.,
becache05
;
fernandez09
;
maurelli14
;
riviere03
;
riviere07
;
rognes10
;
shaw94
;
shaw00
as well as the references therein.

In this paper, we explore the employability of virtual element methods (VEMs) – a relatively recent numerical technique for solving partial differential equations, evolved from 0-Cochains mimetic finite difference and
H^{1}
-conforming finite element methods. VEMs have the flexibility of handling distorted elements (with very high aspect ratio), hanging nodes, polytopal meshes with non-convex shapes. One of the attractive features of the VEM is that bilinear forms can be directly computed using degrees of freedom defined for the local virtual spaces, without requiring the explicit construction of basis functions for the discrete spaces used in the approximation. The virtual spaces contain polynomial and non-polynomial functions, and non-polynomial functions can be computed with the help of local projection operators that are defined from the virtual space to polynomial spaces. Considering the computational advantages of VEMs, these methods have been employed to wide range of problems in mechanics and fluid dynamics. The literature of VEMs for structural mechanics is very abundant, see for example primal, hybrid, discontinuous, adaptive and other variants in
antonietti21
;
artioli17
;
artioli20
;
beirao13
;
beirao15
;
brezzi12
;
choi21
;
dhanush19
;
gain14
;
tang20
;
wriggers17
and the references therein. In particular, VEMs for linear elasticity with weakly symmetric stress have been recently advanced in
zhang18
and later extended to elastodynamics in
berbatov21
;
zhang19
. Also, a mixed formulation using pseudo-stress and displacement is found in
caceres19
. In addition, formulations based on the Hellinger–Reissner variational principle imposing strong symmetry can be found in
artioli17b
;
artioli20b
;
dassi20
, and also including the 3D case in
visinoni24
.

Mixed finite element formulations are often preferred over standard primary formulations for the simultaneous numerical approximation of displacement and pressure variables, which arise in various mathematical models in engineering and physics. One advantage is their natural robustness with respect to material parameters (and in particular producing solutions that are locking-free, that is, even in regimes of large mechanical parameters the compliance tensor is coercive with a uniformly bounded coercivity constant). Building on the concepts of mixed finite element methods and the flexibility of polygonal meshes, the authors in
brezzi13
;
beirao16
introduced mixed VEMs and these methods have been subsequently applied to more realistic problems. We here focus on the construction and analysis of mixed VEM for viscoelasticity in its total stress (additively formed from purely elastic stress and internal viscoelastic stress), velocity, and rotation tensor formulation. The FEM counterpart of such formulations has been studied in
lee17
;
rognes10
using mixed schemes for Maxwell, Kelvin–Voigt, and standard linear solid models with either quasi-static equilibrium equation or fully hyperbolic systems (see also
gatica21
;
marquez21
for slightly different formulations splitting the stress into Maxwell and fully elastic components, eliminating the displacement, and using Newmark trapezoidal rule,
meddahi23
for DG schemes tailored for a modified Zener model, and
wang24
for a multipoint stress mixed method that leads to reduced algebraic systems). Meshes with polygons with arbitrary number of sides can be easily handled using VEM and such meshes are suitable for some particular applications like the simulation of epithelial cells which are inherently polygons. For further applications of virtual elements in viscoelasticity-related computations, see
xiao23
;
pradhan23
;
artioli16
;
artioli
;
jin24
.

The primary goal of this paper is to introduce a virtual element discretisation combined with a Crank–Nicolson scheme and evaluate its computational performance for the standard linear solid model of viscoelasticity. The virtual element spaces we employ for the stress variables are the
H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming spaces introduced in
beirao16
. We introduce a projection operator for the variables in the weakly symmetric formulation to facilitate the analysis and demonstrate its approximation properties.

Outline

Semi-discrete formulation and its well-posedness

The semi-discrete problem reads as follows: starting from the initial datum
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(0),\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(0),\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(0),\mathbf{r}_{h}(0))\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\times\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\times\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\amsmathbb{K}_{h}
, find
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\mathbf{r}_{h})\in C^{1}([0,{T}],\amsmathbb{S}_{h})\times C^{1}([0,{T}],\amsmathbb{S}_{h})\times C^{1}([0,{T}],\mathbf{V}_{h})\times C^{1}([0,{T}],\amsmathbb{K}_{h})
such that

\displaystyle a_{0,h}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})+a_{0,h}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h},\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

(3.4a)

\displaystyle a_{1,h}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h})+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h},\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

(3.4b)

\displaystyle(\varrho\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}-(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}),\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}-(\varrho\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(3.4c)

\displaystyle(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,h}+\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{K}_{h},

(3.4d)

where
a_{*,h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a_{*,h}^{K}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})
, and
thanks to the model assumptions adopted in the previous section, the local bilinear forms are defined as

\displaystyle a_{*,h}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{*,h}},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\mu^{*}}\int_{K}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{*,h}:\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}{\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,}-\frac{\lambda^{*}}{2\mu^{*}+d\lambda^{*}}\int_{K}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{*,h})\,\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}){\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,}

S_{h}^{K}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{*,h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h})={|K|}\sum_{i}\chi_{i}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{*,h})\chi_{i}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}),

where
\chi_{i}
are the degree of freedom operators introduced in Section

. Let
\boldsymbol{\chi}
denote the set of all such degrees of freedom. Following the proof of
(
chen
, Theorem 4.5)
, it is straightforward to verify the following norm equivalence relation for any
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{S}(K)
:

\displaystyle C_{1}h_{K}\|\boldsymbol{\chi(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}})\|_{l^{2}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,K}\leq C_{2}h_{K}\|\boldsymbol{\chi(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}})\|_{l^{2}},

(3.5)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are positive constants independent of

.

Clearly,

is positive definite and symmetric. Moreover, in view of (

),
S_{h}^{K}
satisfies the following scaling property (see also

\alpha_{1}\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}\|^{2}_{0,K}\leq S_{h}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h})\leq\alpha_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{*,h}\|^{2}_{0,K},

with

positive constants independent of

.

\displaystyle a_{*,h}^{K}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,K}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,K}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in{\amsmathbb{S}_{h}},

\displaystyle a_{*,h}^{K}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})

\displaystyle\geq C\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in{\amsmathbb{S}_{h}}.

The semi-discrete formulation (
3.4
) can be expressed as a system of linear ordinary differential equations in time, for which existence and uniqueness results are well established in the literature. Therefore, we do not provide a detailed proof, but instead, below we offer a brief outline of the argument.

We proceed by writing the semi-discrete problem in terms of operators.
Let
A_{*}:\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\to\amsmathbb{S}_{h}
be defined by
A_{*}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}=R_{\amsmathbb{S}_{h}}(a_{*,h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\bullet))
where
R_{\amsmathbb{S}_{h}}:\amsmathbb{S}_{h}^{\prime}\to\amsmathbb{S}_{h}
gives the Riesz-representation of the operand.
Similarly, we define
H:\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\to\amsmathbb{K}_{h}
as
H\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}=R_{\amsmathbb{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\bullet)_{0,\Omega}
,
M:\mathbf{V}_{h}\to\mathbf{V}_{h}
as
M\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\varrho\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
,
J:\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\to\mathbf{V}_{h}
as
J\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}=R_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\bullet)_{0,\Omega}
and
F=R_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}(\varrho f,\bullet)
.
Finally, let us define the block operators

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{0}&0&0&H^{*}\\
0&A_{1}&0&H^{*}\\
0&0&M&0\\
H&H&0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\mathscr{B}=\begin{bmatrix}-A_{0}^{\prime}&0&-J^{*}&0\\
0&0&-J^{*}&0\\
J&J&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\mathscr{C}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
F\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

With this, it readily follows that system (
3.4
) reads

\mathscr{A}\dot{\vec{x}}_{h}=\mathscr{B}\vec{x}_{h}+\mathscr{C},\qquad\text{with}\quad\vec{x}_{h}=(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\mathbf{r}_{h})^{\tt t}.

(3.6)

Lemma 3.1

The semi-discrete problem (
3.6
) possesses a unique solution.

Proof

First, from the fact that
a_{*,h}(\bullet,\bullet)
and
(\bullet,\bullet)_{0,\Omega}
are coercive and symmetric on
\amsmathbb{S}_{h}
and
\amsmathbb{K}_{h}
respectively, we get that
A_{0}
,
A_{1}
and
M
are positive definite.
Thus, to prove the invertibility of
\mathscr{A}
, we only need to show that
H^{*}
is injective.

Let us suppose that for some
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{K}_{h}
,
H^{*}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
. Then, for any
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h}
, we have that

\displaystyle 0=(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},H^{*}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}=(H\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}=(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}.

Note that here we have only used
H^{*}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
and the properties of
H
(and we are not requiring
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}
to be symmetric).
As this is true for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{S}
, we readily get the following relation by using (
3.3
).

0=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}}{\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega}}\geq C_{I}\|\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}.

This is only possible if
\|\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}=0
, that is
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
.
As
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}
was any generic element of
\amsmathbb{K}_{h}
satisfying
H^{*}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
, we get that
H^{*}
is injective, thus proving the invertibility of
\mathscr{A}
.

Finally, we can write the system (
3.6
) as
\dot{\vec{x}}_{h}=\mathscr{F}(\vec{x}_{h},t)
where
\mathscr{F}
is continuous in
t
and Lipschitz-continuous in
\vec{x}_{h}
.
Thus, by Cauchy–Picard’s theorem (see, e.g.,
(
collins
, Chapter 2)
), we will have a unique solution of the semi-discrete problem in
[0,{T}]
.

On the other hand, we also prove the following stability result.

Theorem 3.1

The solution of the semi-discrete system (
3.4
) satisfies the following estimate

\displaystyle\max_{s\in[0,T]}(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}})

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{f}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}{\,\mathrm{d}s\,}.

Proof

Substituting
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}
,
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}
,
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}=\mathbf{r}_{h}
in the first three equations of (
3.4
) and adding them all, we get the following.

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}(a_{0,h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h})+a_{1,h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h})+(\varrho\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}))+a_{0,h}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h})=(\varrho\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h},\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{h}).

The term
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h},\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{h})
is zero owing to both
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(0)+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(0),\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})
and
(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,h}+\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})
being zero for all
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{K}_{h}
. Thus, we are left with the following relation

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}(a_{0,h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h})+a_{1,h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h})+(\varrho\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}))+a_{0,h}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h})=(\varrho\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}).

(3.7)

Next we apply the coercivity of the bilinear forms and Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality to get the following bound

\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}\right)+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}.

(3.8)

Then, discarding the positive term
\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}
and integrating in time from
0
to
t
for
t<T
, we readily get the estimate

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(t)\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(t)\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(t)\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\boldsymbol{f}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}{\,\mathrm{d}s\,}.

Further, using standard inequalities, it is possible to obtain

\displaystyle(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(t)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(t)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(t)\|_{{0,\Omega}})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left((\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}})^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{f}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}\text{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left((\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}})^{2}+\max_{s\in[0,T]}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{f}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}\text{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl((\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}})^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\max_{s\in[0,T]}(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}})\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{f}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}\text{d}s\bigr).

And finally, dividing both sides by
\max_{s\in[0,T]}(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}})
, we end up with

\displaystyle\max_{s\in[0,T]}(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}})

\displaystyle\leq C\biggl(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(0)\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{f}(s)\|_{{0,\Omega}}\text{d}s\biggr),

which finishes the proof.

Remark 3.2

Equation (
3.7
) shows the dissipation of the total energy consisting of the kinetic and elastic potential energy for the viscoelastic material. Specifically, when the load term is zero, it shows that the energy decays to zero with time.

In this section we aim at the derivation of optimal
a priori

) in their natural norms.

To that end, we first define a weakly symmetric elliptic projection operator
\widetilde{\Pi}:\amsmathbb{S}\times\amsmathbb{S}\times\mathbf{V}\times\amsmathbb{K}\to\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\times\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\times\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\amsmathbb{K}_{h}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,\pi}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}),\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

(4.1d)

The operator
\widetilde{\Pi}
is well-defined due to the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1

The problem (
4.1
) is well posed.

Proof

We want to show that given any
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{r})\in\amsmathbb{S}\times\amsmathbb{S}\times\mathbf{V}\times\amsmathbb{K}
, there exists a unique solution to (
4.1
). As
\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\times\amsmathbb{S}_{h}\times\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\amsmathbb{K}_{h}
is finite dimensional, the square system of linear equations has a unique solution if
the following system of equations possesses only the zero solution.

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,\pi},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})_{0,\Omega}+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h},\mathbf{r}_{\pi})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,\pi},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h})_{0,\Omega}+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h},\mathbf{r}_{\pi})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,\pi}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,\pi}),\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,\pi}+{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,\pi},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{K}_{h}.

Substituting
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}
in the second equation and then taking the difference of the first two equations, we get that
({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,\pi},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})_{0,\Omega}=0\;\;\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h}
, implying that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,\pi}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,\pi}
.
Now, substituting
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,\pi}
by
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,\pi}
in the equations and dividing by two, we get the following system of equations.

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,\pi},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})_{0,\Omega}+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h},\mathbf{r}_{\pi})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,\pi}),\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,\pi},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{K}_{h}.

This is a well-posed system by
(
zhang18
, Theorem 4.1)
, hence, the kernel of the above system is zero.

Next, we establish the projection error estimates, which will be used to prove the optimal error estimates between the exact solution and the solution of the semi-discrete problem (
3.4
).

Lemma 4.2

Let
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{r})\in\amsmathbb{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega)\times\amsmathbb{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega)\times\amsmathbb{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega)
and its projection be
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,\pi},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,\pi},\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi},\mathbf{r}_{\pi})=\widetilde{\Pi}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{r})
. Then, there exists
C>0
independent of
h
such that

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\mathbf{r}_{\pi}-\mathbf{r}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}\|+\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{r}\|_{W^{k+1,2}(\Omega)}.

Proof

Taking
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}
in the first and second equations of (
4.1
), adding them, and defining
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1})
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\pi}=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,\pi}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,\pi})
, we get the following system

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})_{0,\Omega}+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{u})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\mathbf{r}_{\pi}-\mathbf{r})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}),\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\pi}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{K}_{h},

which clearly satisfies the conditions to apply
(
zhang19
, Lemma A.1)
, whence we get

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\mathbf{r}_{\pi}-\mathbf{r}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}.\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{r}\|_{k+1}.

(4.2)

In order to bound
\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i,\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}\|
for
i=0,1

\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i,\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}\|\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i,\pi}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}\|+\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}\|

Norm of each row of

is bounded by the

(

, Section 3.2)
.
Thus, we have

\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}\|_{{k+1,\Omega}}.

(4.3)

In order to derive suitable bounds on
\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i,\pi}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}\|

4.1
) as follows

(d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i,h})_{0,\Omega}+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i,h},d_{\boldsymbol{u}}+\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i,h},d_{\mathbf{r}}+\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r})_{0,\Omega}=0\;\;\;\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

where
d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}}={\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i,\pi}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}
,
d_{\boldsymbol{u}}=\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{u}
,
d_{\mathbf{r}}=\mathbf{r}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathbf{r}
.
Taking
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i,h}=d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}}
in the above equation and adding for

\displaystyle\|d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}\|^{2}+\|d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}\|^{2}+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}+d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}),d_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{0,\Omega}+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}+d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}),\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-(\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}})_{0,\Omega}-(\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}})_{0,\Omega}-(d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}+d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}},d_{\mathbf{r}}+\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r})_{0,\Omega}.

The fourth term on the left-hand side in the equation above is zero by the definition of the polynomial projection and the fact that for
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h}
,
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
. In addition, the third term can be analysed as follows

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}+d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}),d_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad=(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,\pi}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,\pi}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}),d_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{0,\Omega}+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}),d_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{0,\Omega}.

In turn, the first term of above is zero by the third equation of (
4.1
).
By (
3.2
), the second term is equal to
(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1})-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}),d_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{0,\Omega}
,
which is zero by definition of the projection operator, and
d_{\boldsymbol{u}}
being in the projected space. In all, we are left with the following relation

\displaystyle\|d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}=-(\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}})_{0,\Omega}-(\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}})_{0,\Omega}-(d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}+d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}},d_{\mathbf{r}}+\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad{\leq C\sqrt{\|d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}}\left(\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|d_{\mathbf{r}}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\right)}.

Thus we have the following estimate

\sqrt{\|d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|d_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}}\leq C\left(\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\mathbf{r}_{\pi}-\mathbf{r}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\right),

where we have also used the triangle inequality.
Now the desired estimate can be obtained from this using (
4.3
), (
4.2
) and standard polynomial approximation theory (see, e.g.,
brenner
).

Remark 4.1

We note that the operator
\widetilde{\Pi}
defined by (
4.1
) commutes with differentiation in time, that is,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\widetilde{\Pi}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{r})=\widetilde{\Pi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}},\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}},\dot{\boldsymbol{u}},\dot{\mathbf{r}}).

The proof of this result is the same as in
(
zhang19

, and this property is used in the subsequent analysis.

As a notational convenience, for any symbol

in
\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v},\mathbf{r}\}
and its discrete approximation
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}
in

, we define the following error quantities

e_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\doteq\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\qquad e_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{p}\doteq\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\pi},\qquad e_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{h}\doteq\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}.

We now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.1

For
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{r})\in W^{1,1}(0,T;\amsmathbb{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega)\times\amsmathbb{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega)\times\amsmathbb{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega))

of (
3.4
) satisfies the following estimate for all time
t
, where the constant
C
is independent of
h
or

\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\mathbf{r}_{h}-\mathbf{r}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,h}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,h}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\|_{W^{1,1}(0,{T};W^{k+1,2}(\Omega))}.

Proof

Subtracting the corresponding continuous and semi-discrete equations, we get the following system
with
\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}=\dot{\boldsymbol{u}_{\pi}}
:

\displaystyle a_{0}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})-a_{0,h}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})+a_{0}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})-a_{0,h}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h})

(4.4a)

\displaystyle+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h},e_{\boldsymbol{v}})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h},\dot{e}_{\mathbf{r}})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

(4.4b)

\displaystyle a_{1}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h})-a_{1,h}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h})+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h},e_{\boldsymbol{v}})_{0,\Omega}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h},\dot{e}_{\mathbf{r}})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h}\in\amsmathbb{S}_{h},

(4.4c)

\displaystyle(\varrho\dot{e_{\boldsymbol{v}}},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}-(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}+e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}),\boldsymbol{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(4.4d)

\displaystyle(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}+\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\in\amsmathbb{K}_{h}.

(4.4e)

Substituting
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0,h}=e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}
,
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1,h}=e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h}
in the first, second and third equations respectively, and adding them, we get the following relation

\displaystyle a_{0,h}(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})+a_{0,h}^{\prime}(e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})+a_{1,h}(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h})+(\varrho\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h},e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=-(\varrho\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h})_{0,\Omega}+(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})_{0,\Omega}+(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad-a_{0}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})+a_{0,h}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,\pi},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})-a_{0}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})+a_{0,h}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,\pi},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})-a_{1}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h})+a_{1,h}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,\pi},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}),

where we have also considered that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{*},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{*,h}\perp\amsmathbb{K}_{h}
. Now, using the definition of the discrete bilinear forms and their symmetry and coercivity properties, we can define the norms

\|\boldsymbol{\tau}\|_{A_{i,h}}:=\sqrt{a_{i,h}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\tau})},\quad\|\boldsymbol{\tau}\|_{A_{0,h}^{\prime}}:=\sqrt{a_{0,h}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\tau})},\quad\text{as well as}\ \|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\varrho}:=\sqrt{\varrho}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{0,\Omega}.

With this, we proceed to rewrite the result
in its energy form, leading to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\left(\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{1,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h}\|^{2}_{\varrho}\right)+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\varrho\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h})_{0,\Omega}+(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})_{0,\Omega}+(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h})_{0,\Omega}-a_{0,h}^{\prime}(e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})-(a_{0}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})-a_{0,h}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}))

\displaystyle-a_{0,h}(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})-(a_{0}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h})-a_{0,h}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}))-a_{1,h}(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h})-(a_{1}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h})-a_{1,h}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h})).

And we can apply Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality to each term on the right-hand side, to readily get the following

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\left(\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{1,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h}\|^{2}_{\varrho}\right)+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{p}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p}\|\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|+\|\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{p}\|\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p}\|\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|+\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\|\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p}\|\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|+\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\|\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|+\|\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{p}\|\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|+\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}\|\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|).

Next, applying algebraic relations to the norms on the right-hand side of the above inequality, it is not difficult to arrive at the following estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\left(\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{1,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h}\|^{2}_{\varrho}\right)+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p},\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{p},\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{p},\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}\|\sqrt{\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{1,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h}\|^{2}_{\varrho}}.

Dividing both sides by
\sqrt{\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{1,h}}+\|e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h}\|^{2}_{\varrho}}
and integrating in time, we are left with

\sqrt{\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{0,h}}\!\!+\|e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{h}\|^{2}_{A_{1,h}}\!\!+\|e_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{h}\|^{2}_{\varrho}}\leq C\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\|\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p},e_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}}^{p},\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}}^{p},\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{p},\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\overline{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}_{k}^{0}\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}\|\,\text{d}t.

Again, by the commutativity of
\widetilde{\Pi}
with differentiation in time, we have, for any symbol
\boldsymbol{\zeta}
in
\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\}
, that
\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{p}=e_{\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}}^{p}
. Thus, from Lemma
4.2
and standard polynomial approximation theory, it is clear that the right-hand side of this last inequality is bounded by
Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\|_{W^{1,1}(0,{T};W^{k+1,2}(\Omega))}
.
Again, an application of the triangle inequality and Lemma
4.2

Note that by differentiating equations (

) in time multiple times, and repeating the steps in the proof above, we can prove that the following holds for all time

\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\|_{W^{l+1,1}(0,{T};W^{k+1,2}(\Omega))}.\end{split}

(4.5)

We also prove a result about error in divergence of the total viscoelastic stress.

Theorem 4.2

The semi-discrete solution satisfies the following error bound for all time

:

\|\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1})\|_{{0,\Omega}}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\|_{W^{2,1}(0,{T};L^{2}(\Omega))}.

(4.6)

Proof

Let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h}
. By the third equations of (
2.5
) and (

), we get the following relation for all
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle(\varrho(\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}-\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}),\boldsymbol{w}_{h})=(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})=(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h}).

Taking
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
, we can assert that

\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}=(\varrho(\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}-\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\leq\|\varrho(\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}-\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\|_{{0,\Omega}}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}.

Dividing both sides by
\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}
, we readily obtain

\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{k}^{0}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\leq\|\varrho(\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}-\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\|_{{0,\Omega}}.

Finally, owing to triangle inequality, polynomial approximation theory and (
4.5
), we get (
4.6
).

5
Fully discrete formulation

We proceed to discretise the time derivative by employing the absolutely stable Crank–Nicolson scheme. This yields a fully discrete version of the semi-discrete scheme (
3.4
).
For that purpose we choose a positive integer
N
for the number of divisions of the time interval.
Let
t_{n}=n{\tau}
, where
n=0,\frac{1}{2},1,\frac{3}{2},2,\cdots N
.

Take
\vec{x}=(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v},\mathbf{r})^{\tt t}
, and following (
3.6
), the semi-discrete system is represented as
\vec{x}_{h}=(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\mathbf{r}_{h})^{\tt t}
satisfying
\mathscr{A}\dot{\vec{x}}_{h}=\mathscr{B}\vec{x}_{h}+\mathscr{C}
for
t\in[0,{T}]
.
We use the notation
\vec{x}^{n}
and
{\vec{x}}_{h}^{n}
to denote
{\vec{x}}(t_{n})
and
\vec{x}_{h}(t_{n})
respectively.
The fully discrete scheme is to find
\vec{X}^{n}\simeq{\vec{x}}_{h}^{n}
for all
n\in\{1,2,...,N\}
such that,

\mathscr{A}\frac{\vec{X}^{n}-\vec{X}^{n-1}}{\tau}=\mathscr{B}\frac{\vec{X}^{n}+\vec{X}^{n-1}}{2}+\mathscr{C}\left(\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right){\tau}\right).

(5.1)

Now, we state and prove the result concerning the time-discretisation error.

Lemma 5.1

The solution of the fully discrete problem satisfies the following error bound, where the constant
c
is independent of
h
or
{\tau}
:

\max_{n\in\{0,1,...,N\}}\|\vec{X}^{n}-{\vec{x}}_{h}^{n}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\leq c{\tau}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v},\mathbf{r}\|_{W^{3,\infty}(0,{T};L^{2}(\Omega))}.

(5.2)

Proof

Denoting
\vec{X}^{n}-\vec{x}_{h}^{n}
by
\vec{\delta}^{n}
, we readily obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}\frac{\vec{\delta}^{n}-\vec{\delta}^{n-1}}{\tau}-\mathscr{B}\frac{\vec{\delta}^{n}+\vec{\delta}^{n-1}}{2}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}\left(\frac{\vec{x}_{h}^{n}-\vec{x}_{h}^{n-1}}{\tau}-\dot{\vec{x}}_{h}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)-\mathscr{B}\left(\frac{\vec{x}_{h}^{n}+\vec{x}_{h}^{n-1}}{2}-\vec{x}_{h}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Next, we use the following relation

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\vec{x}_{h}^{n}+\vec{x}_{h}^{n-1}}{2}-\vec{x}_{h}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{{0,\Omega}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{n}}\dot{\vec{x}}_{h}(s)\text{d}s-\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\dot{\vec{x}}_{h}(s)\text{d}s\right\rVert_{{0,\Omega}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}^{s}\ddot{\vec{x}}_{h}(t)\text{d}t\,\text{d}s+\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{s}^{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\ddot{\vec{x}}_{h}(t)\text{d}t\,\text{d}s\right\rVert_{{0,\Omega}}

\displaystyle\leq{\tau}^{2}\|\ddot{\vec{x}}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}.

And in order to bound the other term, we consider the following

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\vec{x}_{h}^{n}-\vec{x}_{h}^{n-1}}{\tau}-\dot{\vec{x}}_{h}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{{0,\Omega}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\left\lVert\int_{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}^{s}\int_{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}^{t}\dddot{\vec{x}}_{h}(r)\text{d}r\,\text{d}t\,\text{d}s+\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{s}^{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{t}^{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\dddot{\vec{x}}_{h}(r)\text{d}r\,\text{d}t\,\text{d}s\right\rVert_{{0,\Omega}}

\displaystyle\leq{\tau}^{2}\|\dddot{\vec{x}}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}.

By the continuity of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
(with continuity constants
c_{\mathscr{A}}
and
c_{\mathscr{B}}
respectively), the coercivity of
\mathscr{A}
with constant
C_{\mathscr{A}}
, using (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) in (
5.3
), and then multiplying by
\frac{\vec{\delta}^{n}+\vec{\delta}^{n-1}}{2}
, we get the following estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\|\vec{\delta}^{n}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}-\|\vec{\delta}^{n-1}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}}{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\frac{\|\vec{\delta}^{n}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}+\|\vec{\delta}^{n-1}\|_{{0,\Omega}}^{2}}{2}+{\tau}^{2}(\|\ddot{\vec{x}}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\dddot{\vec{x}}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))})\|\vec{\delta}^{n}+\vec{\delta}^{n-1}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\right),

where
c=C_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}\max(1,c_{\mathscr{A}},c_{\mathscr{B}})
.
Applying the discrete form of Gronwall’s inequality (see
stpv
), we are left with

\max_{n\in\{0,1,...,N\}}\|\vec{X}_{h}^{n}-\vec{x}_{h}^{n}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\leq c{\tau}^{2}(\|\ddot{\vec{x}}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\dddot{\vec{x}}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}).

To complete the proof, we proceed to find
h
-independent bounds for the quantities
\|\ddot{\vec{x}}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}
and
\|\dddot{\vec{x}}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}
, which we derive as follows

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{l}\vec{x}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}=

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0,h},\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,h},\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\mathcal{D}^{l}\mathbf{r}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{v},\mathcal{D}^{l}\mathbf{r}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}+C\bigl(\|\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{{0,\Omega}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\mathcal{D}^{l}\mathbf{r}_{h}-\mathcal{D}^{l}\mathbf{r}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|{\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,h}-\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}\|_{{0,\Omega}}+\|{\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1,h}-\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\|_{{0,\Omega}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\mathcal{D}^{l}\boldsymbol{v},\mathcal{D}^{l}\mathbf{r}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}+Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\|_{W^{l+1,1}(0,{T};W^{k+1,2}(\Omega))}

with (
4.5
) in the last step. Now, for a sufficiently small
h
, the second term is dominated by the first. Thus, we get the desired estimate.

We finally state the full-discretisation error estimate which is obtained from (
5.2
), Theorem
4.1
, and triangle inequality.

Theorem 5.1

Assume that the solution to (
2.5
) is such that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\in W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega))\cap W^{3,\infty}(0,{T};\amsmathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega))
and
\boldsymbol{v}\in W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbf{W}^{k+1,2}(\Omega))\cap W^{3,\infty}(0,{T};\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))
. Then, the solution
\vec{X}^{n}
of (
5.1
) satisfies the following estimate for all
n\in\{0,1,,...,N\}

\displaystyle\|\vec{X}^{n}-\vec{x}^{n}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\|_{W^{1,1}(0,{T};W^{k+1,2}(\Omega))}+c{\tau}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},\boldsymbol{v},\mathbf{r}\|_{W^{3,\infty}(0,{T};L^{2}(\Omega))}.

Remark 6.1

We stress that the current analysis (with the so-called
dofi-dofi
stabiliser) is not valid with arbitrary small edges, and one requires to use, for example, the following tangential edge stabilisation proposed in
brenner18
for elliptic problems:

S^{K}_{\partial}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=\sum_{e\in\partial K}h_{K}\int_{\partial K}[\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\,\boldsymbol{t}_{K}^{e}]\cdot[\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{v}\,\boldsymbol{t}_{K}^{e}].

Optimal convergence rates can be established using tangential edge stabilisation by employing the advanced analytical techniques developed in
daveiga17
;
brenner18
for elliptic problems. However, this requires a significantly more involved and sophisticated analysis, which is beyond the scope of the present work. We therefore leave this as a direction for future research.

To demonstrate the computational robustness of the proposed virtual element discretisation, we include numerical experiments involving meshes with arbitrarily small edges, as shown in Figure
6.4
. These experiments still achieve optimal convergence rates.

7
Concluding remarks

In this work we have addressed the construction and analysis of virtual element schemes for viscoelasticity with weakly imposed stress. We have shown the well-posedness of the problem and have established stability and convergence of the proposed discretisation. Some numerical tests were showcased to confirm the properties of the schemes. Future work includes the coupling with reaction-diffusion mechanisms in view of applications into mechano-chemical calcium dynamics in epithelial cell compounds, the analysis for arbitrarily small edges following
brenner18
, the extension to stabilisation-free formulations, and also the design of strongly symmetric stress virtual element spaces following the appealing hybrid approaches from, e.g.,
visinoni24
.

A penalized φ \varphi -FEM scheme for the Poisson Dirichlet problem
Raphaël Bulle, Michel Duprez, Vanessa Lleras, Killian Vuillemot

Abstract
In this work, we analyze a penalized variant of the φ \varphi -FEM scheme for the Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions. The φ \varphi -FEM is a recently introduced unfitted finite element method based on a level-set description of the geometry, which avoids the need for boundary-fitted meshes. Unlike the original φ \varphi -FEM formulation, the method proposed here enforces boundary conditions through a penalization term. This approach has the advantage that the level-set function is required only on the cells adjacent to the boundary in the variational formulation. The scheme is stabilized using a ghost penalty technique. We derive a priori error estimates, showing optimal convergence in the H 1 H^{1} semi-norm and quasi-optimal convergence in the L 2 L^{2} norm under suitable regularity assumptions. Numerical experiments are presented to validate the theoretical results and to compare the proposed method with both the original φ \varphi -FEM and the standard fitted finite element method.



Keywords.

Immersed boundary method; complex geometries; partial differential equations; level-set methods.

1
Introduction

The finite element method (FEM) usually requires meshes that conform to the domain boundary, which can be restrictive for complex or evolving geometries. To address this limitation, Immersed Boundary Methods (IBM)
[
14
]
allow the use of meshes that do not fit the physical boundary by extending the problem to a larger domain. More recently, CutFEM
[
3
,
4
]
has been proposed as a robust alternative, based on integrating the weak formulation only over the parts of elements cut by the boundary and adding stabilization terms to ensure numerical stability and coercivity.
Another alternative, called the Shifted Boundary Method
[
13
]
, uses a Taylor expansion of the boundary condition.
The
\varphi
-FEM paradigm introduced in
[
12
]
is based on a level-set description of the domain, where the solution is written as

u=\varphi w
, automatically enforcing boundary conditions and achieving optimal accuracy and good conditioning without requiring boundary or cut-cell integration. This method has been extended to various PDEs and interface problems
[
5
,
9
,
10
,
8
]
.
More recently, this approach has been adapted to a finite difference scheme
[
6
]
, combined with a neural network
[
7
]
and an a posteriori estimator, and has been analysed to estimate the error
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we consider the Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions,

\begin{cases}-\Delta u&=f\,,\ \hfill\text{ in }\Omega\,,\\
\hfill u&=0\,,\ \hfill\text{ on }\Gamma\,,\end{cases}

(1)

The present contribution is devoted to the numerical analysis of a
\varphi
-FEM scheme introduced in
[
5
]
for this problem (see Section 2). This
\varphi
-FEM scheme enforces boundary conditions weakly by introducing a penalization on the unfitted boundary while still using a level-set description of the domain. So this method combines the robustness of mixed formulations with the flexibility of immersed finite element methods.
We define
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(here
d=2,3
) a domain of boundary
\Gamma
,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
n
the outward unit normal to
\Omega
. We assume that the domain and its boundary are given by a level-set function
\varphi
such that

\Omega:=\{\varphi<0\}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\Gamma:=\{\varphi=0\}\,.

(2)

The present paper is organized as follows: in Section 2, we present the scheme and the main theoretical results. In the second and third Section, we demonstrate these results. Section 4 analyzes the conditioning number of associated matrix. Finally, Section 5 is devoted to numerical illustrations.

2
Proposed scheme and theoretical results

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{O}}
be a Cartesian grid covering the domain
\mathcal{O}
, with triangular cells of size
h
. Let
\varphi_{h}=I_{h,\mathcal{O}}^{(l)}\varphi
the standard Lagrange continuous interpolation of
\varphi
(of degree
l>0
) on
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{O}}
.
This interpolation is then used to construct a submesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{O}}
collecting all the cells of the grid intersecting the domain
\{\varphi_{h}<0\}
, i.e.

\mathcal{T}_{h}:=\left\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{O}}:\ T\cap\{\varphi_{h}<0\}\neq\emptyset\right\}\,.

Let us finally introduce another submesh of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, collecting the cells intersecting the boundary, i.e.
\{\varphi_{h}=0\}

and a set containing the facets of this submesh, given by

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}:=\left\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\>\ T\cap\{\varphi_{h}=0\}\neq\emptyset\right\}\,,\text{ and }\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}:=\{F\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}\setminus\partial\Omega_{h}\}\,.

(3)

Let us now consider the finite element spaces: for
k\geqslant 1
,

V_{h}^{(k)}:=\{v_{h}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{h})\>\ v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)\ \forall\ T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}\,,

(4)

and a second one, the local version of the previous space, on
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
,

Q_{h}^{(k)}:=\{q_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma})\>\ v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)\ \forall\ T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}\}\,.

We will now present the
\varphi
-FEM penalized scheme analyzed here, that relies on the
\varphi
-FEM paradigm used only in the cells of
\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}
. For this scheme, we thus introduce an auxiliary variable
p
on
\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}
such that
u=\varphi p
; equation that will be enforced by penalization in the following scheme.

The penalized
\varphi
-FEM scheme to solve (
1
) is given by: find
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{(k)}
and
p_{h}\in Q_{h}^{(k)}
such that

\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla u_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\int_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\frac{\partial u_{h}}{\partial n}v_{h}+\frac{\gamma}{h^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}(u_{h}-\frac{1}{h}\varphi_{h}p_{h})(v_{h}-\frac{1}{h}\varphi_{h}q_{h})\\
+G_{h}^{lhs}(u_{h},v_{h})=\int_{\Omega_{h}}fv_{h}+G_{h}^{rhs}(v_{h})\,,

(5)

for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{(k)}
,
q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{(k)}
with

G_{h}^{lhs}(u,v)=\sigma_{D}h\sum_{E\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\int_{E}\left[\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\right]\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right]+\sigma_{D}h^{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\int_{T}\Delta u\Delta v\,,

(6)

and

G_{h}^{rhs}(v)=-\sigma_{D}h^{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\int_{T}f\Delta v\,

(7)

for some
\gamma,~\sigma_{D}>0
.
As said in the introduction, this scheme has been introduced in
[
5
]
, but not analyzed.

Remark 1
.

We can remark three important points:

•

In the case of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
finite elements, the second-order terms (the ones multiplied by
\sigma_{D}h^{2}
) are not necessary.

•

In the case of non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
u=u_{D}
, all you need to do is the modification of the penalization form, given by
u=\varphi p+u_{D}
. It then remains to adapt the scheme to this equation.

•

A final observation concerns the comparison with the direct
\varphi
-FEM scheme
[
12
]
, relying on the assumption
u=\varphi w
over the entire mesh, thereby redefining the problem’s unknown throughout the domain. This has two major consequences:

–

the computation relies on the level-set only near the boundary, which makes the scheme more robust to the level-set and, in particular, does not require the gradient or the second derivatives of the level-set function, which could present a singularity (for example, if we consider the signed distance function).

–

the scheme is compatible with the Neumann approach presented in
[
9
]
.

Let us now recall some important hypothesis on the mesh and lemmas from
[
12
]
and
[
11
]
and introduce our main theoretical results.

Assumption 1
.

The boundary
\Gamma
can be covered by open sets

,

on which we can introduce local coordinates

with

such that, up to the order

are bound by a constant

. Moreover, on

and there exists

such that

.

Assumption 2
.

The approximated boundary, defined by
\Gamma_{h}=\{\varphi_{h}=0\}
, can be covered by patches of elements
\{\Pi_{r}\}_{r=1,\dots,N_{\Pi}}
such that:

•

Each patch
\Pi_{r}
can be written as
\Pi_{r}=\Pi_{r}^{\Gamma}\cup T_{r}
where
\Pi_{r}^{\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
and
T_{r}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
. Moreover
\Pi_{r}
, contains at most

connected elements, with

independent of

;

•

The mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
satisfies
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}=\cup_{r=1,\dots,N_{\Pi}}\Pi_{r}^{\Gamma}
;

•

Two patches
\Pi_{r}
and
\Pi_{s}
are disjoints if

.

Let us now present the main theorem of this paper:

Theorem 1
.

We suppose that assumptions
1
and

on
\Gamma
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}

are satisfied,

,
\Omega\subset\Omega_{h}
and

Consider

the solution of (

) and

the solution of the discrete scheme (
5
).
It holds

|u-u_{h}|_{1,{\Omega}}\leqslant Ch^{k}{\|f\|_{k-1,\Omega_{h}}}

with

a constant independent on

.

We note that our analysis requires less regularity on

compared to
[

]
. Moreover, the results of
[

]
can be adapted to show that the condition number of the associated matrix scales like

, while the

-error is of order

3
Coercivity of the bilinear form

This section is devoted to the proof of the coercivity needed for Theorem
1
.

Proposition 1
.

Consider the bilinear form given by

a_{h}(u,p;v,q)=\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\int_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}v+\frac{\gamma}{h^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}(u-\frac{1}{h}\varphi_{h}p)(v-\frac{1}{h}\varphi_{h}q)\\
+\sigma_{D}h\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\int_{F}\left[\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\right]\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right]+\sigma_{D}h^{2}\int_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}\Delta u\Delta v\,.

(8)

Under assumptions
1 and 2, for
\gamma
and
\sigma_{D}
sufficiently big,
a_{h}

is coercive on
V_{h}^{(k)}\times Q_{h}^{(k)}
for the norm

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(u,p)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{h}=|u|_{1,\Omega_{h}}^{2}+\frac{1}{h^{2}}\left\|u-\frac{1}{h}\varphi_{h}p\right\|^{2}_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}+h\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\left\|\left[\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\right]\right\|^{2}_{0,F}+h^{2}\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}\,,

(9)

i.e.
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(u_{h},p_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{h}\geqslant Ca_{h}(u_{h},p_{h};u_{h},p_{h})
for each
(u_{h},p_{h})\in V_{h}^{(k)}\times Q_{h}^{(k)}
.

Before proving Proposition
1
, we need the following Lemma :

Lemma 1
.

For all
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{(k)}
and
p_{h}\in Q_{h}^{(k)}
, there exists

such that

\|\varphi_{h}p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}\leqslant C(h\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}+\|u_{h}-\varphi_{h}p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}})\,.

Proof.

Using the Poincaré inequality, combined to the triangular inequality and an inverse inequality, we obtain

\|\varphi_{h}p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}\leqslant Ch\|\nabla\varphi_{h}p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}\leqslant Ch(\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}+\|\nabla(u_{h}-\varphi_{h}p_{h})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}})\\
\leqslant C(h\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}+\|u_{h}-\varphi_{h}p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}})\,.

∎

We also need the following Lemma :

Lemma 2

(cf.
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Under the Assumption
2
, for all
\beta>0
, one can choose
\alpha\in]0,1[

|v_{h}|^{2}_{1,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}\leqslant\alpha|v_{h}|_{1,\Omega_{h}}^{2}+\beta h\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\left\|\left[\frac{\partial v_{h}}{\partial n}\right]\right\|_{0,F}^{2}+\beta h^{2}\|\Delta v_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\,.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Let
B_{h}
the strip between
\partial\Omega_{h}
and
\Gamma_{h}
, i.e.
B_{h}=\{\varphi_{h}>0\}\cap\Omega_{h}
. Since
\varphi_{h}=0
on

, the boundary term of (

)
can be written for all

(10)

Using Lemma

+\left(\sigma_{D}-\frac{C}{\varepsilon}-\beta\right)\left(h\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\left\|\left[\frac{\partial u_{h}}{\partial n}\right]\right\|_{0,F}^{2}+h^{2}\|\Delta u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\right)\\

Choosing

\sigma_{D}

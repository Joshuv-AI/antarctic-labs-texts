Automorphic Form Twisted Shintani Zeta Function over number fields
Eun Hye Lee, Ramin Takloo-Bighash

Abstract
. In this paper we study the twisted Shintani zeta function over number fields.



1.
Introduction

Shintani
[
10
]
studied the basic analytic properties of a zeta function he associated to the prehomogeneous vector space of binary cubic forms in order to understand the behavior of certain class numbers. Hough
[
5
,
4
]
introduced certain versions of Shintani’s zeta functions twisted by Maass forms over
\mathbb{Q}
. Hough and Lee
[
6
]
studied twists of Shintani zeta functions by an Eisenstein series. In this note, we generalize these constructions to arbitrary number fields and establish the analytic continuation of the resulting twisted zeta function. Let us explain what we prove.

Let
V
denote affine four-dimensional space and
G
denote
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
. A point
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in V
is identified with the binary cubic form given by:

F_{x}(u,v)=x_{1}u^{3}+x_{2}u^{2}v+x_{3}uv^{2}+x_{4}v^{3}.

Define the action
g\cdot x
of an element

g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in G
on
x\in V
by the equation

F_{g\cdot x}(u,v)=(\det(g))^{-1}F_{x}\left((u,v)\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\right),

(1)

where
\det(g)=ad-bc
. Note that
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}\cdot x=ax
.

A nonzero rational function
R(x)
on
V
is called a relative
G
-invariant if there is a character
\chi
of
G
such that
R(g\cdot x)=\chi(g)R(x)
for all
x\in V
and
g\in G
. The group of relative
G
-invariants has the generator

P(x)=x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}+18x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}-4x_{2}^{3}x_{4}-4x_{1}x_{3}^{3}-27x_{1}^{2}x_{4}^{2}

with the associated character
\det(g)^{2}
. The hypersurface
S=\{x\in V\ |\ P(x)=0\}
is invariant under
G
.

Now let
k
be a number field, and let
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{k}
be its ring of adeles. We let
V_{k}
be the space of binary cubic forms with coefficients in
k
, and let
S_{k}
be the set of forms whose discriminant is zero. We also let
V^{\prime}_{k}=V_{k}\setminus S_{k}
. Fix a Schwartz function
\Phi
on
\mathbb{A}^{4}
. For an automorphic form
\varphi
on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
over
k
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
we set

Z(s,\Phi,\varphi)=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}/G_{k}}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in V_{k}^{\prime}}\Phi(g\cdot x)\,dg.

This definition should be compared with the zeta functions in
[
11
, §4]
, as well as
[
5
, Eq. (72)]
,
[
4
, Eq. (3.1)]
, and
[
6
, Eq. (29)]
.

In this paper we study the analytic continuation of this zeta function. We note that if
\varphi
is a cusp form, then it is bounded, and in this case, Wright
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
implies that
Z(s,\Phi,\varphi)
is absolutely convergent for
\Re s>2
and it defines a holomorphic function for such
s
. If
\varphi=E(\tau,g)
is the Eisenstein series given by Equation (
4
) of §
2.3
, because of the functional equation (
5
) in §
2.3
we may assume
\Re\tau\geq 0
. In this case,
Z(s,\Phi,\varphi)
is absolutely convergent for
\Re s>2+\Re\tau
. In order to outline what we prove we need some notation. For
\varphi
and
\Phi
as above, we set

Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)=\int_{|\det g|\geq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in V_{k}^{\prime}}\Phi(g\cdot x)\,dg,

I(s,\Phi,\varphi)=\int_{|\det g|\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(g^{\iota}\cdot x)dg,

and

J(s,\Phi,\varphi)=\int_{|\det g|\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(g\cdot x)dg,

where
g^{\iota}=(\det g)^{-1}g
. These functions are all absolutely convergent for
\Re s
large and they define holomorphic functions there. By Lemma
8
, the function
Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)
has an analytic continuation to an entire function. We will see in Lemma
9
that for
\Re s
large,

Z(s,\Phi,\varphi)=Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)+Z_{+}(2-s.\widehat{\Phi},\varphi)+I(s-2,\widehat{\Phi},\varphi)-J(s,\Phi,\varphi).

(2)

This means that in order to understand the analytic behavior of
Z
, we need to analyze the functions
I
and
J
.

Our first result is for the function
I
.

Theorem 1
.

(1)

If
\varphi
is a cusp form, as a function of
s
,
I(s,\Phi,\varphi)
has an analytic continuation to an entire function;

(2)

The function
I(s,\Phi,E(\tau))
has an analytic continuation to a meromorphic function on the whole complex plane with explicit poles and residues given by Proposition
21
.

Theorem
1
is the combination of Theorems
10
and
18
below, whose proofs are presented in §
5.1
and §
6.1
, respectively. In light of Equation (
2
) and Theorem
1
, the analytic continuation of
Z
is controlled by
J
. The following result is concerned with the analytic continuation of this function.

Theorem 2
.

(1)

If
\varphi
is a cusp form, as a function of
s
,
J(s,\Phi,\varphi)
has an analytic continuation to a holomorphic function on
\Re s>3/4
.

(2)

The function
J(s,\Phi,E(\tau)
has an analytic continuation to a meromorphic function on
\Re s>3/4
.
On this domain,
J(s,\Phi,E(\tau))-H(s,\Phi,\tau)
with
H
as in Proposition
21
is holomorphic.

This theorem is the combination of Theorems
13
and
22
whose proofs are presented in §
5.2
and §
6.2
, respectively.

Combining everything together we obtain the following result:

Theorem 3
.

For
\varphi
a cusp form or
\varphi=E(\tau)
, the function
Z(s,\Phi,\varphi)
has an analytic continuation to
\Re s>3/4

(1)

If
\varphi
is a cusp form, then the function is holomorphic on the domain
\Re s>3/4
.

(2)

In the Eisenstein series case,
Z(s,\Phi,E(\tau))-H(s-2,\widehat{\Phi},\tau)+H(s,\Phi,\tau)
with
H
as in Proposition
21
is holomorphic on the domain
\Re s>3/4
.

Here are some related results in literature. The papers by Hough
[
5
,
4
]
and Hough-Lee
[
6
]
consider analogues of our twisted Shintani zeta functions over
\mathbb{Q}
. The papers
[
5
,
6
]
assume that the Schwartz function
\Phi
vanishes on the singular set. If the Schwartz function
\Phi
vanishes on the singular set, then the analytic continuation and poles of
Z
are determined by those of
I
. For that reason, our Theorem
3
generalizes
[
5
, Lemma 1]
, for cusp forms, and
[
6
, Theorem 1]
, for Eisenstein series, from
\mathbb{Q}
to an arbitrary number field. Hough
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
proves the analytic continuation to the domain
\Re s>1/8
for
k=\mathbb{Q}
and
\varphi
a Maass Hecke eigenform. Hough’s result is strictly stronger than our result for
n=1
. Hough obtains no results for
k\neq\mathbb{Q}
or when
\varphi
is an Eisenstein series.

Our methodology here is adelic and as such, it is inspired by
[
11
]
. Also, we will use the theory of automorphic forms on
\operatorname{GL}(2)
as developed by Jacquet and Langlands
[
8
]
. We will be making special use of local and global Whittaker functions, explicit formulae for local Whittaker functions, and zeta integrals in terms of Whittaker functions. The unfolding process we do in §
5.1
and §
5.2
should be familiar to any practitioner of the theory of automorphic forms. While everything we need is already in
[
8
]
, we will be using the more accessible classical references
[
2
,
3
]
for Jacquet-Langlands theory.
Because of our methods, all of what we do here is completely uniform and makes no distinction between different number fields. What we do here can be thought of as a generalization of the method of Hough
[
5
]
for cusp forms and Hough
[
4
,
6
]
for Eisenstein series (see also Wright
[
11
]
), but there are a number of technical issues to overcome. For example, Hough
[
5
]
takes advantage of the fact that
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}^{1}=\{+1,-1\}
is a finite set. Clearly, if
k\neq\mathbb{Q}
,
k_{\infty}^{1}
is not finite. This in effect means that Hough’s two variable Mellin transform trick in
[
5
, §5]
does not work for an arbitrary number field. We remedy this situation
by doing a single Mellin transform and using some elementary arguments, c.f. Lemma
11
.
As opposed to Hough
[
4
]
our proof of the Theorem
2
does not use averaged Eisenstein series and for that reason it is more elementary (we do use averaged Eisenstein series to prove Theorem
1
and
2
for Eisenstein series, however). Finally, in contrast to all previous results, the three results, Theorems
1
,
2
, and
3
, do not assume that the cusp form
\varphi
is unramified. The proof we write for the unramified Eisenstein series
E(\tau)
goes through for any Eisenstein series. We could have, also, as easily (!) considered twists of our zeta functions with an arbitrary quasi-character.

The results we obtain here are expected to have applications in the study of shapes of cubic extensions of number fields, c.f.
[
7
]
. We address these applications in a subsequent work.

This paper is organized as follows. After the notation section, we review background materials in §
2
. We recall some basic properties of the space of binary cubic forms in §
3
. The functions
I
and
J
are introduced in in §
4
.
The proofs of the main theorems for cusp forms are presented in §
5
. We treat Eisenstein series in §
6
.

Notation

We fix a number field
k
throughout this paper. We let
M_{k}
be the set of places of
k
,
M_{0}
the set of finite places, and
M_{\infty}
the set of archimedean places, respectively. For a place
v
of
k
, we denote the corresponding completion of
k
by
k_{v}
. We let
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{k}
be the ring of adeles of
k
, and
\mathbb{A}^{\times}
the group of ideles. We define
\mathbb{A}^{1}
to be the set of
t\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}
such that
|t|_{\mathbb{A}}=1
.
For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
we let
\underline{\lambda}\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}
be the idele
(t_{v})
such for each
v\mid\infty
,
t_{v}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{n}}
and for
v\nmid\infty
,
t_{v}=1
.

In this paper we let
G
be the algebraic group
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
. For a ring
R
the set of
R
-points of
G
is denoted by
G_{R}
. We will use similar notations for any algebraic subgroup of
G
. Let
B
,
A
, and
N
denote the algebraic subgroups of lower triangular elements of
G
, of diagonal elements of
G
, and of unipotent lower triangular elements of
G
, respectively.
Let
n(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
x&1\end{pmatrix}
,
a(t_{1},t_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}\\
&t_{2}\end{pmatrix}
,
a(t)=a(t,t^{-1})
, and
d(t)=a(t,t)
.

For
v\in M_{\infty}
, let
U_{v}
be the subgroup of all unitary matrices in
G_{k_{v}}
. Explicitly,
U_{v}
is a copy of
O(2)
, the group of
2\times 2
orthogonal real matrices, if
k_{v}=\mathbb{R}
, or a copy of
U(2)
, the group of
2\times 2
unitary complex matrices, if
k_{v}=\mathbb{C}
. For
v\in M_{0}
, let
U_{v}
be
G_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}
. Then for all places
v
,
U_{v}
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G_{k_{v}}
. Moreover,
U_{\mathbb{A}}=\prod_{v}U_{v}
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{A}}
.

By the Iwasawa decomposition, the map
(k,b)\mapsto kb
is a surjection of
U_{\mathbb{A}}\times B_{\mathbb{A}}
onto
G_{\mathbb{A}}
. We may express any
g\in G_{\mathbb{A}}
, therefore, in the form
g=ka(t_{1},t_{2})n(c)
with
k\in U_{\mathbb{A}}
,
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}
, and
c\in\mathbb{A}
. The function
t(g)=\left|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}\right|_{\mathbb{A}}^{1/2}
is independent of the choices made. Let
G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
be the group of
g\in G_{\mathbb{A}}
with
|\det(g)|_{\mathbb{A}}=1
. Let
B_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}=B_{\mathbb{A}}\cap G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
and
B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}
be the subgroup of
b\in B_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
with
t(b)=1
. The group
B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}
is unimodular, i.e., left-invariant measures are right-invariant as well. We will write explicit measures on these groups in §
2.4
.

For any locally compact Abelian group
X
, we define
\mathscr{S}(X)
to be the space of Schwartz-Bruhat functions on
X
. We have three special cases that we now explain. In the first case,
X
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{l}
for some
l
. Pick an
\mathbb{R}
-basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{l}\}
for
X
. Also choose a Banach norm
\|\cdot\|
on
X
. Given
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l})
, define

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\right)^{\alpha_{l}}.

Then
\mathscr{S}(X)
is the space of smooth functions
\Phi
such that for all
\nu\geq 0
,

\sup_{x\in X}(1+\|x\|^{v})\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\nu}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\Phi(x)|<\infty.

If
X=F^{l}
for some non-archimedean field
F
, then
\mathscr{S}(X)
is defined to be the space of local constant compactly supported functions on
X
.

If
X=\mathbb{A}^{l}
, then we define
\mathscr{S}(X)
to be the space of finite linear combination of functions of the form

\prod_{v}f_{v}

such that for each
v
,
f_{v}\in\mathscr{S}(k_{v}^{l})
, and for almost all
v
,
f_{v}=\operatorname{ch}_{\mathcal{O}^{l}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Automorphic forms

We refer the readers to Gelbart
[
2
]
for preliminaries on the theory of automorphic forms.
For the purposes of this paper, automorphic forms are functions on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})/\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k)
, with trivial central character. We also assume that the forms we work with have a
U_{\mathbb{A}}
-type
\eta
. We call a form
\varphi
cuspidal if for almost all
g
,

\int_{\mathbb{A}/k}\varphi(gn(c))\,dc=0.

2.2.
Whittaker models

Let
N
be the unipotent radical of the standard Borel subgroup in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
, i.e., the closed subgroup of lower triangler matrices.
For a non-archimedean place
v
of
k
, we let
\theta_{v}
be a non-trivial character of
N(k_{v})
. Define
C_{\theta_{v}}^{\infty}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k_{v}))

to be the space of smooth complex valued functions on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k_{v})
satisfying

W(gn)=\theta_{v}(n)W(g)

for all
n\in N(k_{v}),g\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k_{v})
. For any irreducible admissible representation
\pi
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k_{v})
the intertwining space

{\rm Hom}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k_{v})}(\pi,C_{\theta_{v}}^{\infty}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k_{v})))

is at most one dimensional; if the dimension is one, we say
\pi
is generic, and we call the corresponding realization of
\pi
as a space of
N
-quasi-invariant functions the Whittaker model of
\pi
.

We recall some facts from
[
3
]
, §16. Let
\pi
be an unramified principal series representation

\pi=\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}(\chi\otimes\chi^{-1})
, with
\chi
unramified, where
B
is the standard Borel subgroup of
G
.
Then
\pi
has a unique
K_{v}
fixed vector. The image of this
K_{v}
-fixed vector in the Whittaker model, call it
W_{\pi}
,
will be
K_{v}
-invariant on the left, and
N
-quasi-invariant on the right.
By Iwasawa decomposition in order to calculate the values of
W_{\pi}
it suffices to know the values of the function along the diagonal subgroup. We have

\displaystyle W_{\pi}\begin{pmatrix}\varpi^{m}\\
&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}q^{-m/2}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\chi(\varpi^{k})\chi^{-1}(\varpi^{m-k})&m\geq 0;\\
0&m<0\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}q^{-m/2}\frac{\chi(\varpi)^{m+1}-\chi(\varpi)^{-m-1}}{\chi(\varpi)-\chi(\varpi)^{-1}}&m\geq 0;\\
0&m<0.\end{cases}

Written compactly we have

W_{\pi}\begin{pmatrix}a\\
&1\end{pmatrix}=|a|^{1/2}\operatorname{ch}_{\mathcal{O}}(a)\frac{\chi(\varpi)\chi(a)-\chi(\varpi)^{-1}\chi(a)^{-1}}{\chi(\varpi)-\chi(\varpi)^{-1}},

where

\operatorname{ch}_{\mathcal{O}}(a)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $a\in\mathcal{O}$}\\
0&\text{ if $a\not\in\mathcal{O}$}.\end{cases}

Also by definition

W_{\pi}\left(g\begin{pmatrix}1\\
x&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=\psi_{v}(x)W_{\pi}(g),

where
\psi_{v}:k_{v}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the standard additive character of
k_{v}
.

Lemma 4
.

We have for
\Re s
large,

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{m(1/2-s)}W_{\pi}\begin{pmatrix}\varpi^{m}\\
&1\end{pmatrix}=L(s,\pi)

where

L(s,\pi):=\frac{1}{(1-\chi(\varpi)q^{-s})(1-\chi^{-1}(\varpi)q^{-s})}.

There is also a theory of Whittaker models in the archimedean settings, and one can compute the value of the Whittaker functions for archimedean places. We will not need the explicit formulae in this work, but we will need the following result:

Lemma 5
.

For each place
v
and each Whittaker function coming
W_{v}
, the integral

\int_{k_{v}^{\times}}W_{v}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)|a|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,d^{\times}a

converges absolutely for
\Re s
large.

Let us also recall the automorphic Fourier expansion
[
2
, P. 85]
. If
\phi
is a cusp form on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
we have

\phi(g)=\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}W_{\phi}\left(g\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)

(3)

with

W_{\phi}(g)=\int_{k\backslash\mathbb{A}}\phi\left(g\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\psi^{-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

2.3.
Eisenstein series

By Iwasawa decomposition, any element of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(k_{v})
can be written as

g_{v}=u_{v}a_{v}n_{v}

with
n_{v}\in N(k_{v}),a_{v}\in A(k_{v}),u_{v}\in U_{v}
. Define a function
\chi_{v,P}
by

\chi_{v,P}:g_{v}=u_{v}a_{v}n_{v}\mapsto|a_{v}|_{v}

where we have represented an element in
A(k_{v})
in the form

\begin{pmatrix}a_{v}\\
&1\end{pmatrix}.

We set

\chi_{P}:=\prod_{v}\chi_{v,P}.

We note that for
\gamma\in P(k)
, we have
\chi_{P}(g\gamma)=\chi_{P}(g)
for any
g\in G(\mathbb{A})
.
Define the Eisenstein series
E(\tau,g)
by

E(\tau,g)=\sum_{\gamma\in G(k)/P(k)}\chi(\tau,g\gamma)

(4)

where
\chi(\tau,g):=\chi_{P}(g)^{\tau+1/2}
. For later reference we note the Fourier expansion of the Eisenstein series
[
2
, Equation 3.10]
in the following form:

E(\tau,g)=\chi_{P}(g)^{\tau+1/2}+\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\chi_{P}(g)^{-\tau+1/2}+\frac{1}{\zeta(2\tau+1)}\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}W_{\tau}\left(g\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right).

Here
W_{\tau}((g_{v})_{v})=\prod_{v}W_{\tau,v}(g_{v})
, where for
v<\infty
,
W_{\tau,v}
is the normalized
K_{v}
-invariant Whittaker function for the induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(|.|^{\tau}\otimes|.|^{-\tau})
, and for
v=\infty
,

W_{\tau,v}(g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{P,v}\left(w\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{pmatrix}g\right)^{\tau+1/2}e^{2\pi ix}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
w=\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}
is a representative for the longest element of the Weyl group.
This integral converges when
\Re(\tau)
is sufficiently large, and has an analytic continuation to an entire function of
\tau
.
We also have the functional equation

E(\tau,g)=\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(1+2\tau)}E(-\tau,g),

(5)

where
g\in G(\mathbb{A})
.

2.4.
Invariant measures

In this section we recall several integration formulae from
[
11
]
which we use throughout this paper.

Since by the theorem on page 70 of
[
1
]
the set
\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times}
is compact, we can pick a Haar measure on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
such that the measure of
\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times}
is
1
. As in
[
11
, Page 508]
, we pick a measure
d^{\times}x
on
\mathbb{A}^{\times}
such that for each integrable function
\Phi
on
\mathbb{A}^{\times}

\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}\Phi(x)\,d^{\times}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\int_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\Phi(\underline{\lambda}x)\,d^{\times}x.

(6)

For any function
\delta
on
\mathbb{A}^{\times}
we have

\int_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\delta(x)\,d^{\times}x=\int_{\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times}}\int_{k_{\infty}^{1}}\delta(v\underline{|v|_{\mathbb{A}}}^{-1}w)\,d^{\times}w\,d^{\times}v.

(7)

We define the invariant measure
db
in
B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}
by

\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}}\phi(b)db=\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{A}}dc\,\phi(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(c)).

(8)

The group
B_{k}
is discrete in
B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}
. The measure on
B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}/B_{k}
is given by

\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}/B_{k}}\phi(b)\,db=\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{A}/k}dc\,\phi(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(c)).

(9)

The measure of
B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}/B_{k}
with respect to
db
is 1. The right-invariant measure
db
on
B_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
is given by

\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\phi(b)\,db=\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}}\phi(ba(\underline{\lambda}))db

(10)

=\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}}\lambda^{-2}\phi(a(\underline{\lambda})b)\ db.

The right-invariant measure
db
on
B_{\mathbb{A}}
is determined as follows:

4.1.

Fix a Schwartz function

Lemma 8
.

The function

Proof.

This result is a consequence of Proposition 6.1 of
[
11
]
.
∎

We have

Z(s,\Phi,\varphi)+\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}/G_{k}}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(gx)\,dg

=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}/G_{k}}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in V_{k}}\Phi(gx)\,dg

=Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\geq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(gx)\,dg

+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in V_{k}}\Phi(gx)\,dg.

For
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}
, set
g^{\iota}=(\det g)^{-1}g
. Also, for
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
,
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(V_{\mathbb{A}})
,

g\cdot\Phi(x)=\Phi(g^{-1}\cdot x).

Then by Equation (
16
) we have
\widehat{g\cdot\Phi}=|\det g|^{2}g^{\iota}\cdot\widehat{\Phi}
. Poisson summation formula then tell us

\sum_{x\in V_{k}}\Phi(g\cdot x)=|\det g|^{-2}\sum_{x\in V_{k}}\widehat{\Phi}(g^{\iota}\cdot x).

This means

Z(s,\Phi,\varphi)+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(gx)\,dg

=Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in V_{k}}\Phi(gx)\,dg

=Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)|\det g|^{-2}\sum_{x\in V_{k}}\widehat{\Phi}(g^{\iota}\cdot x)\,dg

=Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)|\det g|^{-2}\sum_{x\in V^{\prime}_{k}}\widehat{\Phi}(g^{\iota}\cdot x)\,dg

+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)|\det g|^{-2}\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\widehat{\Phi}(g^{\iota}\cdot x)\,dg

=Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\geq 1}|\det g|^{-s}|\det g|^{2}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in V^{\prime}_{k}}\widehat{\Phi}(g\cdot x)\,dg

+\int_{|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}|\det g|^{-2}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\widehat{\Phi}(g^{\iota}\cdot x)\,dg

Here we have used the fact that since
\varphi
has trivial central character,
\varphi(g)=\varphi(g^{\iota})
.
Rearranging terms gives the following lemma:

Lemma 9
.

For
\Re s
large,

Z(s,\Phi,\varphi)=Z_{+}(s,\Phi,\varphi)+Z_{+}(2-s.\widehat{\Phi},\varphi)+I(s-2,\widehat{\Phi},\varphi)-J(s,\Phi,\varphi)

for an automorphic form
\varphi
and a Schwartz function
\Phi
, where we have set

I(s,\Phi,\varphi)=\int_{|\det g|\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(g^{\iota}\cdot x)dg,

and

J(s,\Phi,\varphi)=\int_{|\det g|\leq 1}|\det g|^{s}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(g\cdot x)dg.

Lemma 16

For

=\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
1/2<x_{0}<\Re w\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\,dz\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\,\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,f_{\Phi}(\underline{\lambda}t_{1})

\left(\lambda^{-1-2\tau}+\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\lambda^{-1+2\tau}\right)\left(\lambda^{-1-2z}+\frac{\Lambda(2z)}{\Lambda(2z+1)}\lambda^{-1+2z}\right)

=\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
1/2<x_{0}<\Re w\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}|t|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-3-2\tau-2z}f_{\Phi}(t)\,d^{\times}t\ dz

+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
1/2<x_{0}<\Re w\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}|t|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-3+2\tau-2z}f_{\Phi}(t)\,d^{\times}t\ dz

+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
1/2<x_{0}<\Re w\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(2z)}{\Lambda(2z+1)}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}|t|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-3-2\tau+2z}f_{\Phi}(t)\,d^{\times}t\ dz

+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
1/2<x_{0}<\Re w\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(2z)}{\Lambda(2z+1)}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}|t|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-3+2\tau+2z}f_{\Phi}(t)\,d^{\times}t\ dz.

In the integrals

\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
1/2<x_{0}<\Re w\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}|t|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-3-2\tau-2z}f_{\Phi}(t)\,d^{\times}t

and

\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
1/2<x_{0}<\Re w\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}|t|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-3+2\tau-2z}f_{\Phi}(t)\,d^{\times}t

we shift the contour all the way to the left. Then if we multiply the expression by
w-1/2
and let
w\to 1/2+
the contribution is zero. In the remaining two integrals for
\Re w\gg 0
we shift the contour all the way to the right and pick up a pole
z=w
. We obtain

\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{\Lambda(2w)}{\Lambda(2w+1)}\psi(w)Z(f_{\Phi},-3-2\tau+2w)

+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\frac{\Lambda(2w)}{\Lambda(2w+1)}\psi(w)Z(f_{\Phi},-3+2\tau+2w)

+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
x_{0}\text{ large}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(2z)}{\Lambda(2z+1)}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}|t|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-3-2\tau+2z}f_{\Phi}(t)\,d^{\times}t\ dz

+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\
x_{0}\text{ large}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(2z)}{\Lambda(2z+1)}\frac{\psi(z)}{w-z}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}|t|_{\mathbb{A}}^{-3+2\tau+2z}f_{\Phi}(t)\,d^{\times}t\ dz.

Multiply by
w-1/2
and let
w\to 1/2+
to obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 20
.

With
C_{k}
as in the previous lemma,

I_{2}(\Phi,\varphi_{N})=C_{k}Z(f_{\Phi},-2-2\tau)+C_{k}\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}Z(f_{\Phi},-2+2\tau).

We now go back to analyzing
I(s,\Phi,\varphi)
with
\varphi=E(\tau)
. We know

I(\Phi,\varphi)\sim I(s,\Phi,\varphi_{N})=\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s}I^{1}(\Phi,\varphi_{N})\,d^{\times}z,

where as before
\Phi_{z}(x)=\Phi(d(\underline{z^{-1}}))
. By the two lemmas, for
\Re s\gg 0
,

I(s,\Phi,\varphi_{N})=C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s}\,d^{\times}z\,Z(F_{\Phi_{z}},-2\tau/3+1/3)

+C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s}\,d^{\times}z\,\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}Z(F_{\Phi_{z}},2\tau/3+1/3)

+C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s}\,d^{\times}z\,Z(f_{\Phi_{z}},-2-2\tau)+C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s}\,d^{\times}z\,\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}Z(f_{\Phi_{z}},-2+2\tau).

=C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s-2\tau/3+1/3}\,d^{\times}z\,Z(F_{\Phi},-2\tau/3+1/3)

+C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s+2\tau/3+1/3}\,d^{\times}z\,\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}Z(F_{\Phi},2\tau/3+1/3)

+C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s-2-2\tau}\,d^{\times}z\,Z(f_{\Phi},-2-2\tau)+C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s-2+2\tau}\,d^{\times}z\,\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}Z(f_{\Phi},-2+2\tau).

We have proved the following proposition:

Proposition 21
.

Let

H(s,\Phi,\tau)=C_{k}\frac{Z(F_{\Phi},-2\tau/3+1/3)}{2s-2\tau/3+1/3}+C_{k}\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\frac{Z(F_{\Phi},2\tau/3+1/3)}{2s+2\tau/3+1/3}

+C_{k}\frac{Z(f_{\Phi},-2-2\tau)}{2s-2-2\tau}+C_{k}\frac{\Lambda(2\tau)}{\Lambda(2\tau+1)}\frac{Z(f_{\Phi},-2+2\tau)}{2s-2+2\tau}.

Then
I(s,\Phi,\varphi)-H(s,\Phi,\tau)
is entire.

We can now easily read off the poles and their residues from the formula in this proposition.

∎

6.2.
The function
J(s,\Phi,\varphi)
when
\varphi
is an Eisenstein series

In this subsection we study the analytic behavior of
J(s,\Phi,\varphi)
when
\varphi=E(\tau)
.

Theorem 22
.

Let
\varphi
be the Eisenstein series
E(\tau,g)
with
\Re\tau\geq 0
. The function
J(s,\Phi,\varphi)
converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of
\Re s\gg 0
. Then if we set
\gamma=\Re\tau
,
J(s,\Phi,\varphi)
has an analytic continuation to a meromorphic function on the domain
\Re s>3/4
.
On this domain
J(s,\Phi,\varphi)-I(s,\Phi,\varphi)
is holomorphic.

We note that the last statement of the theorem means that the poles of
J(s,\Phi,\varphi)
can be read off from the formula Proposition
21
.

Proof.

We have

J(s,\Phi,\varphi)=\int_{0}^{1}z^{2s}J^{1}(\Phi^{z},\varphi)\,d^{\times}z,

where here for any Schwartz function
\Phi
,

\Phi^{z}(x)=\Phi(d(\underline{z})x)
and for any
\Phi
,

J^{1}(\Phi,\varphi)=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}}\varphi(g)\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(g\cdot x)dg.

As in the proof of Theorem
18
we have

\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}\psi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)J^{1}(\Phi,\varphi)=\lim_{w\to\frac{1}{2}+}\left(w-\frac{1}{2}\right)J(\Phi,\varphi;\psi,w)

where

J(\Phi,\varphi;\psi,w)=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}}\varphi(g)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;g)\left(\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\Phi(gx)\right)\,dg.

By Proposition
7
we have
S_{k}=S_{0,k}\coprod S_{1,k}\coprod S_{2,k}
, with

S_{0,k}=\{0\},

S_{1,k}=\coprod_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}(G_{k}/B_{k})\cdot(0,0,0,\alpha),

S_{2,k}=\coprod_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}(G_{k}/A_{k})\cdot(0,0,\alpha,0).

Set for any function
\omega
on
G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}
and
0\leq i\leq 2

J_{i}(\Phi,\omega,w)=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}}\omega(g)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;g)\left(\sum_{x\in S_{i,k}}\Phi(gx)\right)\,dg,

after suppressing
\psi
in the notation.

As in the proof of Theorem
18
,
J_{0}(\Phi,\varphi,w)
does not contribute to
J(s,\Phi,\varphi)
. So it suffices to analyze
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
. As in the proof of Theorem
13
,
J_{1}(s,\Phi,\varphi-\varphi_{N})+J_{2}(s,\Phi,\varphi-\varphi_{N})
has an analytic continuation to a holomorphic function on the domain
\Re s>3/4
. This means we need to analyze the analytic continuation and poles of
J_{1}(\Phi,\varphi_{N},w)
and
J_{2}(\Phi,\varphi_{N},w)
.

First we look at
J_{1}(\Phi,\varphi_{N},w)
. We have

J_{1}(\Phi,\varphi_{N},w)=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}}\varphi_{N}(g)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;g)\sum_{x\in S_{1,k}}\Phi(g\cdot x)dg

=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}}\varphi_{N}(g)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;g)\sum_{\gamma\in G_{k}/B_{k}}\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\Phi(g\cdot\gamma\cdot(0,0,0,\alpha))dg

=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/B_{k}}\varphi_{N}(g)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;g)\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\Phi(g\cdot(0,0,0,\alpha))dg

=\int_{U_{\mathbb{A}}}\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/B_{k}}\varphi_{N}(ug)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;ug)\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\Phi(ug\cdot(0,0,0,\alpha))\,dg\,du.

Let
\widetilde{\Phi}
be the averaged version of
\Phi
over
K
. Then we get

=\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/B_{k}}\varphi_{N}(g)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;g)\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\widetilde{\Phi}(g\cdot(0,0,0,\alpha))\,dg

=\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\cdot\lambda^{-2}\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}/B_{k}}db\,\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda})b)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda})b)\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\widetilde{\Phi}(a(\underline{\lambda})b\cdot(0,0,0,\alpha))\,dg

=\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\cdot\lambda^{-2}\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}\int_{\mathbb{A}/k}dc\,

\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda})a(t_{1},t_{2})n(c))\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda})a(t_{1},t_{2})n(c))\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\widetilde{\Phi}(0,0,0,\lambda^{-3}t_{2}^{2}t_{1}^{-1}\alpha)

=\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\cdot\lambda^{-2}\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}\int_{\mathbb{A}/k}dc\,

\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda}))\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda})a(t_{1},t_{2})n(c))\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\widetilde{\Phi}(0,0,0,\lambda^{-3}t_{2}^{2}t_{1}^{-1}\alpha),

by the explicit formula for
\varphi_{N}
. Now we perform the
c
integral to obtain

=\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\cdot\lambda^{-2}\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}

\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda}))\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda})a(t_{1},t_{2}))\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\widetilde{\Phi}(0,0,0,\lambda^{-3}t_{2}^{2}t_{1}^{-1}\alpha)

by the explicit formula for
E_{N}
. A change of variables gives

=\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\cdot\lambda^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times}}d^{\times}t_{1}

21

and its value is given by Lemma
19
.

Next, we study

J_{2}(\Phi,\varphi_{N},w)=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}}\varphi_{N}(g)\mathscr{E}(\psi,w;g)\sum_{x\in S_{2,k}}\Phi(g\cdot x)dg.

Let

\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(\psi,w;g)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re z=x_{0}\\

Then in our normalization, the inequality in the proof of Proposition 6.5 of
[
11
]
says that for each fixed
\psi
, fixed

, and fixed

large, there is a constant

(23)

for

Write this expression as

\Xi_{1}(w)+\Xi_{2}(w)

with

\Xi_{1}(w)=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}}\varphi_{N}(g)\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(\psi,w;g)\sum_{x\in S_{1,k}}\Phi(g\cdot x)dg

Because of the rapid decay of
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
as in Equation (
23
), the proof of Proposition 6.5 of
[
11
]
shows that
\Xi_{1}(w)
is holomorphic for
\Re w>0
, and for that reason its contribution to
J
is zero. We analyze
\Xi_{2}(w)
. We have

\Xi_{2}(w)=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/G_{k}}\varphi_{N}(g)\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;g)\sum_{\gamma\in G_{k}/A_{k}}\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\Phi(g\gamma\cdot(0,0,\alpha,0))dg.

=\int_{G_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/A_{k}}\varphi_{N}(g)\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;g)\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\Phi(g\cdot(0,0,\alpha,0))dg

=\int_{U_{\mathbb{A}}}\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/A_{k}}db\,\varphi_{N}(ub)\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;ub)\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\Phi(ub\cdot(0,0,\alpha,0))\,du

=\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/A_{k}}db\,\varphi_{N}(b)\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;b)\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\widetilde{\Phi}(b\cdot(0,0,\alpha,0))

=\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{\times}\lambda\,\lambda^{-2}\int_{B_{\mathbb{A}}^{0}/A_{k}}db\,\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda})b)\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda})b)\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\widetilde{\Phi}(a(\underline{\lambda})b\cdot(0,0,\alpha,0))

=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda}))\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda}))\lambda^{-2}d^{\times}\lambda\,\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}\int_{\mathbb{A}}dc\,

\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\widetilde{\Phi}(a(\underline{\lambda})a(t_{1},t_{2})n(c)\cdot(0,0,\alpha,0)),

by the explicit formula for
\varphi_{N}
and
E_{N}
. We then get

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda}))\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda}))\lambda^{-2}d^{\times}\lambda\,\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}\int_{\mathbb{A}}dc

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda}))\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda}))\lambda\,d^{\times}\lambda\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}

=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{E}_{N}(\psi,w;a(\underline{\lambda}))\varphi_{N}(a(\underline{\lambda}))\lambda\,d^{\times}\lambda\int_{(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k^{\times})^{2}}d^{\times}t_{1}\,d^{\times}t_{2}\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}f_{\Phi}(\underline{\lambda}^{-1}t_{2}\alpha)

22

and its value is given by Lemma

If we put everything together the result follows.
∎

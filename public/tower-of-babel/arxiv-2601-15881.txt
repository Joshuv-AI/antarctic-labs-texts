Recovery of nonlinear material parameters in a quasilinear Lamé system
David Johansson, Yavar Kian

Abstract
We investigate the inverse problem of determining nonlinear elastic material parameters from boundary stress measurements corresponding to prescribed boundary displacements. The material law is described by a nonlinear, space-independent elastic tensor depending on both the displacement and the strain, and gives rise to a general class of quasilinear Lamé systems. We prove the unique and stable recovery of a wide class of space-independent nonlinear elastic tensors, including the identification of two nonlinear isotropic Lamé moduli as well as certain anisotropic tensors. The boundary measurements are assumed to be available at a finite number of boundary points and, in the isotropic case, at a single point. Moreover, the measurements are generated by boundary displacements belonging to an explicit class of affine functions. The analysis is based on structural properties of nonlinear Lamé systems, including asymptotic expansions of the boundary stress and tensorial calculus. Keywords: Inverse problems, nonlinear elasticity, Lamé system, system of quasilinear elliptic equations, Uniqueness, Stability estimate. Mathematics subject classification 2020: 35R30, 74B20, 35J47

Introduction

In linear elasticity, the relation between the stress tensor and the strain tensor, also called the constitutive equation, is given by Hooke’s law, which describes the deformation of a solid object under the action of applied forces. This leads to a linear Lamé system whose solutions correspond to the displacement of a point in the solid. This model is no longer valid for many real-world materials, where the relationship between stress and strain is not proportional and the stiffness of the material changes with deformation (see
[
Cia22
,
LL86
]
for more details). Such properties are described by different nonlinear equations with many applications, including soft tissue modeling
[
Mis10
,
Pic01
]
, cell mechanics
[
Sop+18
]
, the aerospace industry
[
de +07
]
, and polymer industries
[
BBMS21
]
.

In all of the above-mentioned applications, the nonlinear relation between the stress and the strain tensor is crucial to understanding properties of the corresponding materials. This relation can be represented by complex functions encoded into an elastic or stiffness tensor that depends both on the
displacement and the strain tensor, leading to a quasilinear Lamé system. In the present article, we study the determination of such a class of nonlinear elastic tensors from measurements of the stress generated by boundary displacements.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geqslant 2
be an open domain. We consider on the domain
\Omega
the stress tensor
\sigma
given by

\sigma(u)=C(u,\varepsilon(u)):\varepsilon(u),

(1)

where
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the displacement,
\varepsilon(u)=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T})
the strain tensor,

S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
the set of symmetric matrix defined by
S_{n}(\mathbb{R}):=\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:\ A^{T}=A\},
and
C\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n\times n}
the elastic or stiffness tensor. The equilibrium conditions without any body forces leads to the following quasilinear Lamé system

\begin{cases}\operatorname{div}(C(u,\varepsilon(u)):\varepsilon(u))=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=g&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2)

where
g\colon\partial\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the displacement imposed at the boundary. Observe that, the material parameters in (
1
) is given by the elastic tensor
C(u,\varepsilon(u))
which depends both on the displacement
u
and the strain tensor
\varepsilon(u)
. In the present article, we study the inverse problem of determining the nonlinear term
C(u,\varepsilon(u))
from boundary stress measurements generated by some affine class of boundary displacements
g
. We study both the uniqueness and the stability issue for this problem.

In the last couple of decades, significant attention has been devoted to identification of an elastic tensor from measurements of the stresses at the boundary of the medium.
Most of the existing literature considered the determination of space-dependent Lamé parameters of a linear isotropic system from the knowledge of the corresponding displacement-to-traction map
[
ER02
,
GG23
,
IY15
,
NU93
,
NU94
,
IUY12
]
and the identification of inclusion or cracks for more general class of Lamé systems
[
EGH25
,
ABMD20
,
ABM23
,
II08
,
II12
,
NUW03
,
NUW05
]
(see also
[
Uhl99
]
for an overview).
Similar problems with more general elastic tensors were investigated in the time domain
[
CNO20
,
CHKU21
,
HNZ19
,
HUV20
,
MR07
,
Rac00
]
.

To our best knowledge, most of the existing literature on identification of nonlinear parameters in Lamé systems are restricted to the time domain
[
HUW20
,
UZ21
,
UZ24
]
.
For the recovery of Lamé parameters in a quasilinear elliptic system, we are only aware of
[
NS94
]
which establishes a local uniqueness result by reduction to the corresponding local uniqueness results for linear isotropic elasticity
[
NU94
]
.

This contrasts with scalar elliptic equations, where the identification of nonlinear terms have been studied with a diversity of results on both semilinear terms
[
FKO24
,
FO20
,
IN95
,
IS94
,
LLLS21
,
JNS25
,
Nur23
,
ST23
]
and quasilinear terms
[
Can67
,
CFKKU21
,
CLLO24
,
Kia23
,
Kia24
,
KKU23
,
MU20
,
Nur24
,
SU97
]
.
The main goal of this work, is to bridge a gap in the theory of elasticity by proving that one can identify nonlinear material parameters, that depend both on the displacement and the strain tensor, for stationary Lamé systems.

The main results of this article are presented in Section
2
. We separately address the identification of the dependence of the nonlinear parameter
C(u,\varepsilon(u))
on the displacement
u
and on the strain tensor
\varepsilon(u)
. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first results concerning the determination of general nonlinear material parameters in the Lamé system that depend simultaneously on the displacement and the strain tensor. These findings extend previous observations established for scalar elliptic equations
[
CFKKU21
,
Kia23
,
Kia24
,
KKU23
,
MU20
]
. Moreover, we introduce additional restrictions on the data, refining those employed in earlier works.

Our analysis is based on precise asymptotic characterizations of boundary stress measurements combined with multilinear tonsorial calculus. These asymptotics are obtained via an explicit linearization procedure together with a detailed representation of the boundary stress. The methodology is informed by the linearization techniques developed in
[
Isa93
,
KLU18
,
SU97
]
, and it extends earlier analysis for scalar elliptic equations
[
Can67
,
CFKKU21
,
CLLO24
,
Kia23
,
Kia24
,
KKU23
,
MU20
,
Nur24
,
SU97
]
to the Lamé system with general tensor-valued quasilinear terms. To the best of our knowledge, this work constitutes the first application of the infinite-order linearization method, introduced in
[
KLU18
]
, to the identification of quasilinear coefficients in the stationary Lamé system.

2
Main results

Throughout the article, we impose the following assumptions:

A1)

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geqslant 2
is an open domain with
C^{2}
boundary,

A2)

C\in C^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n\times n})
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

A3)

The tensor
C(\lambda,0)
is strongly elliptic for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e. for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists
\kappa(\lambda)\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\xi^{T}\eta:C(\lambda,0):\xi^{T}\eta=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}C_{ijk\ell}(\lambda,0)\xi_{\ell}\eta_{k}\xi_{j}\eta_{i}\geqslant\kappa(\lambda)\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2},

for any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

A4)

For every
(\lambda,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
the tensor
C(\lambda,\eta)
satisfies the symmetries

C_{ijk\ell}(\lambda,\eta)=C_{jik\ell}(\lambda,\eta)=C_{ij\ell k}(\lambda,\eta),\quad i,j,k,\ell=,\ldots,n,

and
C(\lambda,0)
additionally satisfies the symmetry

C_{ijk\ell}(\lambda,0)=C_{k\ell ij}(\lambda,0),\quad i,j,k,\ell=,\ldots,n.

Under the above assumptions, we prove in Proposition
3.2
that, for
p\in(n,+\infty)
and
g\in W^{2-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
sufficiently close to a constant vector
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, problem (
2
) admits a solution

u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Moreover, the solution
u
is unique within some possibly small neighborhood
V_{\lambda}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the constant
\lambda
; see Section
3
for more details.
In contrast to scalar elliptic equations, there is no comparison principle available for guaranteeing the global uniqueness of such solutions.
The local uniqueness in the neighborhood
V_{\lambda}
enables the construction of a local displacement-to-traction map, analogous to the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map in the classical Calderón problem.
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and, for
\delta>0
, denote by
U_{\delta}
the set defined by

U_{\delta}=\{f\in W^{2-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon\lVert f\rVert_{W^{2-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\delta\}.

In view of Proposition
3.2
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\lambda)>0
such that problem (
2
), with
f\in U_{\delta}
and
g=\lambda+f
, admits a unique solution
u_{\lambda,f}\in V_{\lambda}
. Then we can define the displacement-to-traction map as follows

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}\colon U_{\delta}\mapsto W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}(f)=[C(u_{\lambda,f},\varepsilon(u_{\lambda,f}))\colon\varepsilon(u_{\lambda,f})\nu]|_{\partial\Omega}.

(3)

Here
\nu\colon\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the outward pointing unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
.
Note that we use solutions
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p>n
, so that the point measurements on the boundary are well-defined by the Sobolev embedding
W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow C(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We mention that similar definitions of local Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps were introduced for semilinear elliptic equations in
[
FO20
,
LLLS21
]
, for the stationary Navier-Stokes equations in
[
LW07
]
, and for a nonlinear Lamé system in
[
NS94
]
.

The inverse problem under investigation in the present article can be stated as follows:

(IP):

Determine the nonlinear material parameter
C
from knowledge of the point measurements

[\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}(tf_{B})](x),\quad x\in\mathcal{O},\ B\in\mathcal{M},\ \lvert t\rvert<\tau,\ \lambda\in[-R,R]^{n},

where
\mathcal{M}\subset S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{O}\subset\partial\Omega
are suitable finite sets,
f_{B}(x)=Bx
,
\tau>0
is sufficiently small, and
R>0
is arbitrary.

We separate our main results into two categories.
We consider first the determination of the dependence of the nonlinear material property
C(\lambda,0)
with respect to the displacement
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
but with the strain fixed at
\eta=0
.
Then, we extend these results into the identification of the full tensor
C(\lambda,\eta)
with dependence on the strain tensor
\eta\in S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
We establish both uniqueness results and a stability estimate.

Remark 2.1
.

We focus the presentation in the article on the constitutive equation (
1
) using the symmetric gradient
\varepsilon(u)
and the symmetries
A4)
, motivated by the modeling in continuum mechanics.
However, the method we develop can be extended, under minor modifications, to more general constitutive equations

\sigma(u)=C(u,\nabla u):\nabla u

(4)

without assuming the symmetries
A4)
.
The proofs of the below theorems can easily be adapted to (
4
), mostly by replacing the subspace
S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
of symmetric matrices with
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and by replacing the symmetric matrix
B_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(E_{ij}+E_{ji})
with
E_{ij}
, where
E_{ij}
is the matrix whose only nonzero entry is at row
i
and column
j
and is equal to
1
.
The results thus obtained can be found in Appendix
A
.

2.1
Dependence with respect to the displacement

We consider the recovery of
C(\lambda,0)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, from boundary measurements.
The tensor
C(\lambda,\eta)
may depend on the strain tensor
\eta\in S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, but in this section we consider only the recovery of the dependence of the displacement
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
when
\eta=0
.

Denote by
E_{ij}
the matrix with only one nonzero entry which is equal to 1 at index
ij
, i.e.
E_{ij}:=(\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell})_{1\leqslant k,\ell\leqslant n}
where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta and
i,j=1,\ldots,n
.
Let
\mathcal{W}
be the collection of matrices obtained by symmetrizing
E_{ij}
,

\mathcal{W}=\{B\in S_{n}(\mathbb{R}):B=\frac{1}{2}(E_{ij}+E_{ji})\text{ for some }i,j=1,\ldots,n\}.

(5)

Notice that Card
(\mathcal{W})=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
.

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be the canonical orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let the points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\partial\Omega
be chosen so that, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the normal vector
\nu(x_{i})
is close to
e_{i}
, as follows

\lVert\nu(x_{i})-e_{i}\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

(6)

Denote this collection of points by
\mathcal{V}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. From the surjectivity of the map
\nu\colon\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
(see e.g.
[
Kia24
, Proposition A.1.]
), where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \|y\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=1\}
, we know that the condition (
6
) will be fulfilled for some large collection of points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\partial\Omega
.

Our first main result concerns the problem
(IP)
with
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{V}
. Namely, we consider the unique identification of
C(\lambda,0)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Omega
satisfy assumption
A1)
and, for
m=1,2
, let
C^{m}
satisfy assumptions
A2)
, with
k=1
,
A3)
and
A4)
.
If for some
\delta>0
it holds that

[\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}}(tf_{B})](x)=[\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}(tf_{B})](x)\quad B\in\mathcal{W},\ x\in\mathcal{V},\ \lvert t\rvert<\delta,

(7)

then
C^{1}(\lambda,0)=C^{2}(\lambda,0)
.

Remark 2.3
.

By definition of the displacement-to-traction maps
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}},\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}
, we have
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}}(0)=\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}(0)=0
. In other words,
0
belongs to the domain of both maps, so that
\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}})\cap\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}})
is a nonempty open set.
The constant
\delta
in Theorem
2.2
and the following theorems is implicitly chosen to that
tf_{B}\in\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}})\cap\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}})
whenever
\lvert t\rvert<\delta
.
Both
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}}(tf_{B})
and
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}(tf_{B})
are therefore well-defined.

This result can be improved for more specific class of nonlinear Lamé systems. For instance, let us consider nonlinear isotropic and homogeneous elastic medium where the stiffness tensor
C(u,\varepsilon(u))
is characterized by

C(\lambda,\eta)=(\Lambda(\lambda,\eta)\delta_{ij}\delta_{k\ell}+\mu(\lambda,\eta)(\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}\delta_{jk}))_{1\leqslant i,j,k,\ell\leqslant n},\ (\lambda,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R}).

(8)

We obtain the determination of such class of nonlinear terms from measurements at a single point (i.e.
\operatorname{Card}(\mathcal{O})=1
).

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
holds.
Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
be a point where the normal vector
\nu(x)
has at least two nonzero components and let
B=B_{kk}
be fixed.
Assume additionally that the tensors
C^{m}
are of the form

C_{ijk\ell}^{m}(\lambda,\eta)=\Lambda^{m}(\lambda,\eta)\delta_{ij}\delta_{k\ell}+\mu^{m}(\lambda,\eta)(\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}\delta_{jk}),\quad(\lambda,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\Lambda^{m}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R})
and
\mu^{m}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R})
.
If for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\delta>0
it holds that

[\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}}(tf_{B})](x_{0})=[\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}(tf_{B})](x_{0}),\quad\lvert t\rvert<\delta,

(9)

then
C^{1}(\lambda,0)=C^{2}(\lambda,0)
.

To the best of our knowledge, we give in Theorem
2.2
the first positive answer to problem
(IP)
for the dependence of the nonlinear material property with respect to the displacement.
This extends to Lamé systems earlier results for scalar equations with space-independent nonlinearities
[
Kia24
,
MU20
]
.
Regarding space-dependent tensors, we are aware only of the work
[
NS94
]
, which establishes a local uniqueness result by reduction to the corresponding results on linear isotropic elasicity in
[
NU94
]
.

We prove in Corollary
2.4
that, for isotropic nonlinear elastic tensors of the form (
8
), the result of Theorem
2.2
is still true from measurements at a single point prescribed by excitations on a single direction
f_{B}
. More precisely, we consider
(IP)

with
\operatorname{Card}(\mathcal{O})=\operatorname{Card}(\mathcal{M})=1
and we recover the two distinct Lamé moduli
\Lambda
and
\mu
.

The uniqueness result of Theorem
2.2
can be extended to the following stability estimate.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
holds, but now let
\lambda
belong to some
n
-cube
\mathcal{Q}=[-R,R]^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
R>0
arbitrary.
Then

\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{Q}}\lVert C^{1}(\lambda,0)-C^{2}(\lambda,0)\rVert_{*}\leqslant C\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in\mathcal{W}\\
\lambda\in\mathcal{Q}\\
x\in\mathcal{V}\end{subarray}}\lVert[D\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}}(0)f_{B}](x)-[D\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}(0)f_{B}](x)\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

(10)

where
C
is given by

C=\frac{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\sqrt{2n(1+nM^{2})}}{1-nM^{2}},\quad M=\max_{i=1,\ldots,n}\{\lVert\nu(x_{i})-e_{i}\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\}.

Above,
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{*}
denotes the operator norm of fourth order tensors viewed as linear transformations on matrices.

Observe that the estimate (
10
) is a Lipschitz stability estimate with an explicit constant completely independent of the unknown parameter.
In that sense, this stability estimate is an unconditional stability.
This confirms what was already observed for scalar elliptic equations in
[
Kia23
,
Kia24
]
.
This stability estimate as well as the data used in Theorem
2.5
can be useful for different reconstruction algorithms based on iterative methods such as Tikhonov regularization (see, e.g.
[
CY00
]
for more details).

Similarly, we can improve Theorem
2.5
for nonlinear isotropic and homogeneous elastic medium with stiffness tensor
C(u,\varepsilon(u))
of the form
(
8
).

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose the assumptions of Corollary
2.4
holds, but now let
\lambda
belong to some
n
-cube
\mathcal{Q}=[-R,R]^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
R>0
arbitrary.
Then

\displaystyle\lVert C^{1}(\lambda,0)-C^{2}(\lambda,0)\rVert_{*}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{Q}}\lvert\Lambda^{1}(\lambda,0)-\Lambda^{2}(\lambda,0)\rvert+\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{Q}}\lvert\mu^{1}(\lambda,0)-\mu^{2}(\lambda,0)\rvert

(11)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{Q}}\lVert[D\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}}(0)f_{B_{kk}}](x_{0})-[D\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}(0)f_{B_{kk}}](x_{0})\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

where
C=(2|\nu_{k}(x_{0})|^{-1}+|\nu_{l}(x_{0})|^{-1})
and
\nu_{\ell}(x_{0)},\nu_{k}(x_{0})
are two nonzero components of the normal vector
\nu(x_{0})
.

2.2
Dependence with respect to the strain tensor

We consider here the
\eta
-dependence of the elastic tensor
C(\lambda,\eta)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\eta\in S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
Our first result concerns the unique recovery of the Taylor coefficients
D_{\eta}^{k}C(\lambda,0)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
when
C(\lambda,\eta)=(C_{ijk\ell}(\lambda,\eta))_{1\leqslant i,j,k,\ell\leqslant n}
takes the form

C_{ijk\ell}(\lambda,\eta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu_{ij}(\lambda,\eta)D_{ijk\ell}(\lambda)&\text{ if }(i,j)=(k,\ell),\\
D_{ijk\ell}(\lambda)&\text{ if }(i,j)\neq(k,\ell),\end{array}\right.\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \eta\in S_{n}(\mathbb{R}).

(12)

We consider the subset
\mathcal{W}_{\star}
of
S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
given by

\mathcal{W}_{\star}=\mathcal{W}\cup\{B_{ij}+B_{k\ell}:\ i,j,k,\ell=1,\ldots,n,\ (i,j)\neq(k,\ell)\},

where
\mathcal{W}
is given by (
5
).
For all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce also the set
\mathcal{W}_{\star}^{\ell}
defined by

\mathcal{W}_{\star}^{\ell}=\{B_{1}+\ldots+B_{k}:\ k=1,\ldots,\ell,\ B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\in\mathcal{W}_{\star}\}.

Then, we consider problem
(IP)
with
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{W}_{\star}^{\ell}
, for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{V}
.

Our first main result about recovery of the
\eta
-dependence of the elastic tensor, can be stated as follow.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\Omega
satisfy assumption
A1)
and let
N\geqslant 2
.
For
m=1,2
, let
C^{m}
be tensors satisfying
A2)
, with
k=N
, as well as assumptions
A3)
and
A4)
.
Assume additionally that
C^{m}
take the form

C^{m}_{ijk\ell}(\lambda,\eta)=\begin{cases}\mu_{ij}^{m}(\lambda,\eta)D^{m}_{ijk\ell}(\lambda)&\text{if }(i,j)=(k,\ell),\\
D^{m}_{ijk\ell}(\lambda)&\text{if }(i,j)\neq(k,\ell),\end{cases}

(13)

where
(\lambda,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\mu^{m}

\displaystyle=(\mu_{ij}^{m})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}\in C^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}),

\displaystyle D^{m}

\displaystyle=(D_{ijk\ell}^{m})_{1\leqslant i,j,k,\ell\leqslant n}\in C^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n\times n}),

and
\mu_{ij}^{m}(\lambda,0)=1
for all
i,j=1,\ldots,n
.
If, for
R>0
and
\delta>0
, it holds that

[\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}}(tf_{B})](x)=[\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}(tf_{B})](x),\quad B\in\mathcal{W}_{\star}^{N-1},\ x\in\mathcal{V},\ \lvert t\rvert<\delta,\ \lambda\in[-R,R]^{n},

(14)

then

D_{\eta}^{k}C^{1}(\lambda,0)=D_{\eta}^{k}C^{2}(\lambda,0)\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,N-1,\ \lambda\in[-R,R]^{n}.

(15)

As a straightforward application of this result, we prove the following uniqueness result.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let the conditions of Theorem
2.7
be fulfilled.
Assume additionally that, for
m=1,2

In Theorems
2.7
and
2.9
, we solve the inverse problem
(IP)
by characterizing the dependence of nonlinear material parameters on both the displacement and the strain tensor.
Under suitable real-analyticity assumptions, these results yield the complete determination of elastic tensors
C(\lambda,\eta)
of the forms (
12
) and (
16
).
To the best of our knowledge, this provides the first such result for the Lamé system with nonlinearities depending simultaneously on the displacement and the strain tensor.

The analysis extends earlier works on scalar elliptic equations
[
Kia24
,
MU20
]
to Lamé systems under restricted boundary data.
Specifically, we consider stress boundary measurements generated by boundary displacements of the form
\lambda+tf_{B}
, with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
B
ranging over finite sets
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{W}_{\star}^{\ell}
and
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{W}_{\diamond}^{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, respectively.
Even in the scalar elliptic setting, the use of such restricted data for solving
(IP)
appears to be new.

The proofs rely on explicit asymptotic expansions of the boundary stress measurements
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}(tf_{B})
as
t\to 0
, obtained by combining Taylor expansions with a linearization of the boundary stress operator.
The approach is inspired by
[
Isa93
,
KLU18
,
SU97
]
and extends infinite-order linearization techniques
[
KLU18
]
to stationary Lamé systems.
In contrast to the scalar elliptic case, the Lamé system requires a tensorial formulation of the linearization procedure and its implementation in Sobolev spaces.

In Theorem
2.10
, we establish the full identification of isotropic nonlinear elastic tensors of the form (
8
) from measurements at a single point, including the simultaneous determination of the Lamé moduli
\Lambda(u,\varepsilon(u))
and
\mu(u,\varepsilon(u))
, despite the finiteness of the excitation space.
The technical difficulties arising from these restrictions are resolved by exploiting the tensorial structure and asymptotic properties of the boundary stress operator, together with algebraic properties of matrix-valued multilinear forms and unique solvability of an equation involving the Lamé moduli. The results of Theorem
2.10
depend on the choice of boundary point measurement
x_{0}
, and we derive unique recovery under the rather mild conditions (
23
).

Although the uniqueness results of Theorems
2.7
and
2.9
may be strengthened to Hölder-type stability estimates under additional assumptions, we do not address such estimates here in order to avoid unnecessary technical complications.

3

We will apply the implicit function theorem to solve (

Consider (

. If we formally linearize the nonlinear equation in (

(26)

But first, we establish in the next lemma the required mapping properties of the map

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
satisfy the assumption
A1)
and let
f\in C^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},\mathbb{R})
.
If
p>n
and
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
then
u\mapsto f(u,\nabla u)\eqqcolon G(u)
defines a
C^{k}
map from
W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

to
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
we denote the partial derivatives of
f(z,A)
by
\partial_{z_{i}}f
and
\partial_{A_{ij}}f
.
We start by considering the case
k=1
.
Note that since
p>n
, each partial derivative
\partial_{x_{j}}u_{i}
belongs to
C(\overline{\Omega})
by the Sobolev embedding.
Hence the function

\Omega\ni x\mapsto f(u(x),\nabla u(x))\in\mathbb{R}

is uniformly continuous and therefore belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Let

\psi_{m}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

be a sequence converging to
u
in
W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then we have for any test function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(u,\nabla u)\partial_{x_{\ell}}\varphi\,dx=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}f(\psi_{m},\nabla\psi_{m})\partial_{x_{\ell}}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{\ell}}\Big(f(\psi_{m},\nabla\psi_{m})\Big)\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{z_{i}}f(\psi_{m},\nabla\psi_{m})\partial_{x_{\ell}}\psi_{i,m}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle-\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{A_{ij}}f(\psi_{m},\nabla\psi_{m})\partial^{2}_{x_{\ell}x_{j}}\psi_{i,m}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{z_{i}}f(u,\nabla u)\partial_{x_{\ell}}u_{i}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{A_{ij}}f(u,\nabla u)\partial^{2}_{x_{\ell}x_{j}}u_{i}\varphi\,dx.

This shows that the
L^{p}(\Omega)
function

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{z_{i}}f(u,\nabla u)\partial_{x_{\ell}}u_{i}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{A_{ij}}f(u,\nabla u)\partial^{2}_{x_{\ell}x_{j}}u_{i}

is a weak derivative of
f(u,\nabla u)
.
Hence
G(u)=f(u,\nabla u)\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and the map is well-defined.

The Fréchet derivative of
u\mapsto f(u,\nabla u)=G(u)
is given by

G^{\prime}(u)v=\sum_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}f(u,\nabla u)v_{i}+\sum_{ij}\partial_{A_{ij}}f(u,\nabla u)\partial_{x_{j}}v_{i},

which follows from Taylor’s formula

\displaystyle f(z+y,A+B)

\displaystyle-f(z,A)-\sum_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}f(z,A)y_{i}-\sum_{ij}\partial_{A_{ij}}f(z,A)B_{ij}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i}\Big[\partial_{z_{i}}f(z+ty,A+tB)-\partial_{z_{i}}f(z,A)\Big]y_{i}\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{ij}\Big[\partial_{A_{ij}}f(z+ty,A+tB)-\partial_{A_{ij}}f(z,A)\Big]B_{ij}\,dt.

Since the derivatives of
f
are locally Lipschitz, we have for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
a constant
C
such that for
(z,A),(y,B)\in K
the follows inequalities hold

\displaystyle\lvert f(z+y,A+B)

\displaystyle-f(z,A)-\sum_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}f(z,A)y_{i}-\sum_{ij}\partial_{A_{ij}}f(z,A)B_{ij}\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{2}\sum_{i}(\lVert y\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\lVert B\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}})\lvert y_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{2}\sum_{ij}(\lVert y\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\lVert B\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}})\lvert B_{ij}\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant C(\lVert y\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}+\lVert B\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}^{2}).

If
u,v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
then the ranges of
(u,\nabla u)
and
(v,\nabla v)
are compact sets and if
\lVert v\rVert_{W^{2,p}(\Omega),\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant\delta
for some fixed
\delta>0
then the range of the perturbation
(u+tv,\nabla(u+tv))
belong to a fixed compact set.
This allow us to use the above estimate to prove

\displaystyle\lVert G(u+v)-G(u)-G^{\prime}(u)v\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\lVert v\rVert_{W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Hence
G
is differentiable as a map
W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

In a similar but somewhat more tedious way we obtain the same estimate for the partial derivatives

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{x_{j}}G(u+v)-\partial_{x_{j}}G(u)-\partial_{x_{j}}G^{\prime}(u)v\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\lVert v\rVert_{W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

and it follows that
G
is differentiable as a map
W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Continuity of the derivative is established similarly, so that
G
is
C^{1}
.

Higher regularity is established by iterating the above argument as follows.
Define the two new functions

\tilde{f}_{y,B}(z,A)=\sum_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}f(z,A)y_{i}+\sum_{i,j}\partial_{A_{ij}}f(z,A)B_{ij}

and

\tilde{G}_{v}(u)=\tilde{f}_{v,\nabla v}(u,\nabla u)=G^{\prime}(u)v.

Since
f
is
C^{k+1}
then
\tilde{f}_{y,B}
is
C^{k}
.
By replacing
f,G
in the above argument with
\tilde{f}_{y,B},\tilde{G}_{v}
we conclude that
\tilde{G}_{v}
is
C^{1}
.
But this implies that
G
is
C^{2}
, since
\tilde{G}_{v}^{\prime}(u)w=G^{\prime\prime}(u)vw
.
Repeating this we conclude that
G
is
C^{k}
.
∎

5
Recovery of dependence on strain tensor

We study the recovery of the
\eta
-dependence of the elastic tensor

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R})\ni(\lambda,\eta)\mapsto C(\lambda,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n\times n}.

Our strategy is based on explicit asymptotic properties of the data
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}(tf_{B})
as
t\to 0
, for
B\in\mathcal{M}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In the preceding section, we used this asymptotic property at order

for proving the recovery of
C(\lambda,0)
. Under the extra regularity assumption that
C\in C^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n\times n})
,

, we will extend this asymptotic property for determining the coefficients
D_{\eta}^{j}C(\lambda,0)
,
j=0,\ldots,k-1
, in the Taylor polynomial of

with respect to
\eta
as
\eta=0
.

According to Proposition
3.2
, for
C\in C^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n\times n}
and for every

, the map
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}
is
C^{k}
on a neighborhood
U_{\lambda}\subset W^{2-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of zero as a map taking values in
C(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, the coefficients in the Taylor polynomial at
t=0
of the map

are given by
\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}(tf_{B})|_{t=0}=D^{j}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}(0)(f_{B},\ldots,f_{B})
,

. In the next theorem we establish an explicit relation between the derivatives
D^{k}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C}(0)(f_{B},\ldots,f_{B})
and the derivatives
D_{\eta}^{k-1}C(\lambda,0)
, which will play a fundamental role in the determination of the
\eta
-dependence of

.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
satisfy assumption
A1)
and, for

, let

be two tensors satisfying assumptions

, with
k\geqslant 2
,
A3)
and
A4)
.
Let

and

. Then, we have

\displaystyle D^{k}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{1}}(0)(f_{B},\ldots,f_{B})-D^{k}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,C^{2}}(0)(f_{B},\ldots,f_{B})

(37)

\displaystyle=F_{k}+[(k-1)[D_{\eta}^{k-1}[C^{1}(\lambda,0)-C^{2}(\lambda,0)](B,\ldots,B)]:B]\nu,

where

satisfies

(D_{\eta}^{\ell}C^{1}(\lambda,0)=D_{\eta}^{\ell}C^{2}(\lambda,0),\ \lambda\in(-R,R)^{n},\ \ell\leqslant k-2)\implies F_{k}=0.

(38)

Proof.

For
j=1,2
, let
S_{j}
be the the solution map from Lemma

, with

, such that

. Following Lemma

, the map

,

, is a

on a sufficiently small neighborhood of zero, as a map taking values in

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{j,\lambda,B}^{m}=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{m}(C^{j}(S_{j}(tf_{B}),\varepsilon(S_{j}(tf_{B}))):\varepsilon(S_{j}(tf_{B})))|_{t=0}

\displaystyle-(C^{j}(\lambda,0):\partial_{t}^{m}\varepsilon(S_{j}(tf_{B}))|_{t=0}).

in the sense of Banach space-valued functions taking values in

In view of Corollary

For the sake of completeness we establish in this appendix the well-posedness of the linear constant coefficient system in

(63)

Equation (

\begin{cases}\sum_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}(\sum_{j}a_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}u)=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\

(64)

The tensor
C

is the entry at row

a_{j\ell}

Alternatively,

\displaystyle C:B=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{C}_{11}:B&\cdots&\tilde{C}_{1n}:B\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\tilde{C}_{n1}:B&\cdots&\tilde{C}_{nn}:B\end{bmatrix},

where
\tilde{C}_{ij}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{j1,i}^{T}&a_{j2,i}^{T}\ldots&a_{jn,i}^{T}\end{bmatrix}

is row

, and

denotes the Frobenius inner product between matrices.

There are several common notions of ellipticity for systems, but most relevant for the following discussion is:

•

Strongly elliptic:
There exists a constant
c>0
such that

\sum_{ij}\sum_{k\ell}C_{ijk\ell}\xi_{\ell}\eta_{k}\xi_{j}\eta_{i}=\xi^{T}\eta:C:\xi^{T}\eta\geqslant c\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2},\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ x\in\overline{\Omega},

(65)

where
\xi^{T}\eta
is the matrix whose entry at row
i
and colum
j
is
\xi_{j}\eta_{i}

In the variable coefficient setting, strongly elliptic operators are precisely those that are coercive on

[
McL00
, Theorem 4.6]
, and therefore enjoy a good solvability theory and in particular satisfy the Fredholm alternative
[
McL00
, Theorem 4.10]
.
For constant coefficient equations, strong ellipticity instead lead to well-posedness in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

[
GM12
, Corollary 3.46]
.
Moreover, elliptic interior regularity holds for strongly elliptic operators
[
JLS17
, Proposition A.1]
.
The ellipticity condition used in
[
JLS17
]
is that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, the operator

\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni\eta\mapsto\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

is injective, where
a_{ij}
are the matrices appearing in (
64
).
This holds in particular under the above notion of strong ellipticity, since it follows from (
65
) and

\sum_{j,\ell}\langle\,a_{j\ell}\xi_{\ell}\eta,\,\xi_{j}\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}C_{ijk\ell}\xi_{\ell}\eta_{k}\xi_{j}\eta_{i}

that

\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\eta=0\implies\|\eta\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=0.

A common alternative notion of ellipticity is obtained by expressing the positivity in (
65
) with respect to all symmetric matrices, and leads to well-posedness in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
by a standard Lax-Milgram argument also in the variable coefficient setting.
This is commonly used in the theory of elasticity; see for example
[
Gur81
, Chapter 29]
.
For a short discussion on the relation between various other notions of ellipticity, see the end of Section 9.1 in
[
RR04
]
or
[
GM12
, Section 3.4.1]
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\Omega
satisfy assumption
A1)
, let
C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n\times n}
be strongly elliptic, and
2\leqslant p<\infty
.
Then, for every
g\in W^{2-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
h\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a unique solution
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the system (
63
).

A tensor
\tilde{C}
defined in terms of a tensor
C
by

\tilde{C}_{ijk\ell}=\frac{1}{2}(C_{ijk\ell}+C_{ij\ell k})

satisfies the symmetry
\tilde{C}_{ijk\ell}=\tilde{C}_{ij\ell k}
and it holds that

\operatorname{div}(C:\varepsilon(u))=\operatorname{div}(\tilde{C}:\nabla u).

The following corollary is therefore an immediate consequence of the previous lemma.

Corollary B.2
.

Let
\Omega
satisfy assumption
A1)
, let
C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n\times n}
be strongly elliptic, and
2\leqslant p<\infty
.
Then, for every
g\in W^{2-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
h\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a unique solution
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the system

\begin{cases}\operatorname{div}(C:\varepsilon(u))=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=f&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Proof of Lemma
B.1
.

Well-posedness for weak solutions in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is established in
[
GM12
, Corollary 3.46]
.
It only remains to establish higher regularity.
Proposition A.1 in
[
JLS17
]
establishes interior regularity.
We prove regularity up to the boundary using and argument from
[
Tro87
, Section 2.4.3]
, by reduction to interior regularity by a reflection across the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
But some of the more tedious details of various coordinate transformations are left out.

Let
U_{h}
be a finite open covering of
\overline{\Omega}
of small enough sets that each boundary section
U_{h}\cap\partial\Omega
can be flattened.
Let
\psi_{h}
be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to
U_{h}
.
If
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is satisfies

\int_{\Omega}[C:\nabla u]:\nabla\varphi\,dx=\langle\,F,\,\varphi\rangle,

then
v=\psi_{h}u
satisfies

\int_{U_{h}\cap\Omega}[C:\nabla v]:\nabla\varphi\,dx=\langle\,F,\,\psi_{h}\varphi\rangle+\langle\,G,\,\varphi\rangle,

(66)

where
G
is given by

\langle\,G,\,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{ijk\ell}C_{ijk\ell}\Big(u_{k}\partial_{x_{\ell}}\psi_{h}\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{i}\partial_{x_{\ell}}u_{k}\partial_{x_{\ell}}\psi_{h}\Big)\,dx.

But
u_{\ell}\in H^{1}
and
\psi_{h}
is smooth, so we may integrate by parts in the first term.
Since
\psi_{h}
vanishes close to
\partial U_{h}\cap\Omega
and
u
vanishes on
\partial\Omega
, this introduces no boundary terms and we obtain

\langle\,G,\,\varphi\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}\sum_{ijk\ell}C_{ijk\ell}\varphi_{i}\Big(\partial_{x_{j}}u_{k}\partial_{x_{\ell}}\psi_{h}+u_{k}\partial_{x_{\ell}x_{j}}^{2}\psi_{h}+\partial_{x_{\ell}}u_{k}\partial_{x_{\ell}}\psi_{h}\Big)\,dx.

Hence
G
can be identified with a function in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
\Phi\colon U_{h}\cap\Omega\mapsto V_{+}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
U_{h}\cap\partial\Omega
is mapped onto a flat portion of
\partial V_{+}
.
Note that
\operatorname{det}(D\Phi)=1
; see for example the discussion in
[
Eva10
, Appendix C.1]
.
Using this boundary-flattening coordinate transformation in (
66
) leads to

\int_{V_{+}}[\hat{C}(x):\nabla\hat{v}]:\nabla\hat{\varphi}\,dx=\langle\,\hat{F},\,\hat{\psi}_{h}\hat{\varphi}\rangle+\langle\,\hat{G},\,\hat{\varphi}\rangle,

where

\hat{C}_{ijk\ell}(x)=\sum_{qp}C_{iqkp}[\partial_{p}\Phi_{\ell}(y)\partial_{q}\Phi_{j}(y)]|_{y=\Phi^{-1}(x)}

and the quantities
\hat{v},\hat{\varphi},\hat{\psi}_{h}
are defined as the composition of the corresponding functions on
U_{h}\cap\Omega
with
\Phi^{-1}
.
The coefficients in
\hat{F},\hat{G}
are transformed similarly to
\hat{C}(x)
above.
Note that the transformed tensor
\hat{C}
is strongly elliptic whenever
C
is; see for example
[
McL00
, Exercise 4.2]
; the scalar case is done in step 9 of the proof of Theorem 4 in Section 6.3.2 in
[
Eva10
]
and the same argument applies to the vector-valued case.
Moreover, the coordinate transformation
\Phi
is
C^{1,1}
and the coefficients
\hat{C}_{ijk\ell}
are therefore Lipschitz continuous.

Next, the solution
\hat{v}
as well as
\hat{F}
and the coefficients in
\hat{G}
are extended across the flat part of the boundary
\partial V_{+}
by odd reflection,

\tilde{v}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=-\hat{v}(x^{\prime},-x_{n}).

The tensor
\hat{C}_{ijk\ell}
and the test functions
\hat{\varphi}
are extended by even reflection

\tilde{\varphi}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\hat{\varphi}(x^{\prime},-x_{n}).

Denote all extended quantities by
\tilde{v},\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{F}
and so on.
Let
V_{-}
denote the domain obtained by reflecting
V_{+}
across the flat part of the boundary, and define
V=\overline{V}_{+}\cup\overline{V}_{-}
to be the entire extended domain.
Then the equation obtained in
V
is

\displaystyle\int_{V}\nabla[\tilde{C}(x):\tilde{v}]:\nabla\tilde{\varphi}\,dx=

\displaystyle\langle\,\tilde{F},\,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle+\langle\,\tilde{G},\,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\,g,\,\tilde{\varphi}|_{\{x_{n}=0\}\cap V}\rangle+\langle\,\tilde{g},\,\tilde{\varphi}|_{\{x_{n}=0\}\cap V}\rangle,

where
\{x_{n}=0\}\cap V
is the flattened part of the boundary and
g
is the conormal derivative coming from
V_{+}
and
\tilde{g}
is the conormal derivative coming from
V_{-}
; see
[
McL00
, Lemma 4.3]
for the conormal derivative.
Because of the precise locations of the minus signs in the extended functions it follows by a change of variables in the integrals that

\langle\,g,\,\tilde{\varphi}|_{\{x_{n}=0\}\cap V}\rangle+\langle\,\tilde{g},\,\tilde{\varphi}|_{\{x_{n}=0\}\cap V}\rangle=0.

Hence the equation reduces to

\int_{V}\nabla[\tilde{C}(x):\tilde{v}]:\nabla\tilde{\varphi}\,dx=\langle\,\tilde{F},\,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle+\langle\,\tilde{G},\,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle.

Just as for
\hat{C}
, one can verify that
\tilde{C}
is strongly elliptic and Lipschitz continuous in the domain
V
.
Note that
\tilde{F},\tilde{G}
are as regular as the original functions
F,G
, since they are related by a
C^{1,1}
boundary flattening transformation and a
C^{\infty}
tranformation which reflects across the boundary.

We conclude by applying the interior regularity result
[
JLS17
, Proposition A.1]
a finite number of times and in the end obtain
u\in W^{2,p}.

To spell out some of the details, note that when
u\in H^{1}
then
G\in L^{2}
and therefore
\tilde{v}\in H^{2}_{loc}
by interior regularity.
Recall that
\tilde{v}
is defined using the partition of unity
\psi_{h}
.
By repeating this for every function
\psi_{h}
we can conclude that the original solution
u
belongs to
H^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Repeating the procedure, we obtain from the Sobolev embedding that
G\in L^{s}
for
s=\frac{2n}{n-2}
and conclude from the interior regularity that
\tilde{v}\in W^{2,s_{1}}_{loc}
and
u\in W^{2,s_{1}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
where
s_{1}=\min\{s,p\}
.
Repeating this leads to a finite sequence
s_{1}<s_{2}<\ldots<s_{m}
given by

s_{m}=\frac{2n}{n-2m}.

When
m>\frac{n-2}{2}
then the solution
u\in W^{2,s_{m}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
has derivatives in
L^{r}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
r<\infty
.
Then both
F,G
belong to
L^{p}
and one final application of the regularity result yields
\tilde{v}\in W^{2,p}_{loc}
and
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
∎

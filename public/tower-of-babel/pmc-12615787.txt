Design of multi-mode intelligent system architecture for surface defect detection of steel based on cloud technology
Authors: Fei Ren, LiBing Xu, Jiajie Fei, John Paul Q. Tomas, HongSheng Li, Bonifacio T. Doma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This research establishes an advanced cloud-native multimodal intelligent system architecture dedicated to automated detection and real-time monitoring of steel surface defects. The architecture ingeniously integrates the advantages of edge computing, cloud computing, and mobile computing technologies, achieving efficient processing and precise analysis of industrial big data through advanced deep learning algorithms. The main innovative contributions of this research include: (1) Optimized deployment of improved YOLOv5-based algorithm systems, including YOLOv5-efficient net and YOLOv5-mobile net series lightweight models, which significantly enhance defect recognition capabilities in complex industrial environments; (2) Meticulously designed microservice architecture and distributed message queue system, realizing high availability and dynamic scalability; (3) Pioneering construction of a multi-dimensional intelligent result distribution ecosystem, encompassing cross-platform services (WeChat, email), real-time push notifications (iMessage), and mobile application alerts (Android applications), ensuring seamless and timely delivery of detection results to different end users. In rigorous evaluations on a large-scale self-built steel defect dataset, the system demonstrates good performance across key metrics including detection accuracy, recall rate, and processing speed. Practical deployment has validated the system’s high reliability and robust adaptability in industrial production environments. This research not only provides a feasible technical solution for the intelligent transformation of the steel industry but also establishes a forward-looking technical paradigm for intelligent manufacturing in the context of Industry 4.0, laying a solid foundation for achieving unmanned, high-quality modern industrial production.

Introduction

In the modern steel production process, ensuring product quality meets high standards is crucial. Surface defect detection in steel, as a key aspect of quality control, traditionally relies on manual visual inspection. However, this method is not only inefficient but also susceptible to subjective judgments and environmental factors. With the rapid development of artificial intelligence technology, especially the applications of deep learning1–3 and cloud technology, efficient and accurate automated detection systems[4–6] have become a research focus. Scholars both domestically and internationally have conducted relevant studies.

Sung-suk Choi et al.7 developed a defect detection system based on CCD cameras and computers. The system utilizes CCD cameras to capture real-time images for inspection, which are then processed using image processing algorithms such as Gaussian filtering and the Canny operator to detect defects. Compared to traditional manual inspection methods, this system demonstrates higher efficiency and easier scalability. However, its detection accuracy, achieved solely through image processing algorithms, is limited. This limitation could be addressed by leveraging machine learning and deep learning techniques to enhance detection accuracy. Wang et al.8 designed a hub defect detection system based on image processing technology. They employed image preprocessing methods to enhance image features, generating a training dataset used to train a neural network for defect detection. The system detects surface defects on hubs using the trained model and displays real-time detection results on a host computer. This system performs well in defect detection tasks by integrating image processing and neural networks. However, its limitation lies in displaying detection results solely on the host computer, necessitating additional means for result dissemination. Li et al.9 addressed the issue of smoke and toxic gas generation during arc welding processes by proposing a defect detection system based on robot vision. This system employs arc welding robots equipped with visual sensors to replace manual image capture and utilizes deep learning combined with edge-cloud computing technologies for defect detection. Additionally, it employs Internet of Things (IoT) and virtual simulation technologies for monitoring the entire detection system. This system effectively accomplishes defect detection tasks, achieving higher efficiency and speed. Tang et al.10 proposed a privacy-preserving classification scheme to address data upload privacy concerns. The scheme encrypts original steel images and designs a convolutional neural network capable of processing encrypted images for classification. By incorporating distributed computing and edge-cloud architectures, the scheme enhances detection speed effectively. Experimental results demonstrate the scheme’s ability to achieve efficient image classification while ensuring the privacy of the classification process.

Ling Xiao et al.11 proposed a Convolutional Neural Network (H-CNN) model based on instance level features for surface defect detection. This model generates defect masks by extracting features from different convolutional layers, and first generates candidate regions. Then, it uses a fully convolutional neural network to extract convolutional features at different levels and detect instance level defects. This model has been applied to oil stain detection in the Freight Train Detection System (FTDS), and experimental results show that H-CNN can effectively identify and generate defect masks. Weishan Zhang et al.12 proposed a distributed embedded platform based on NVIDIA Jetson TX1, which utilizes deep learning technology for real-time video processing. This platform aims to implement complex deep learning algorithms on low-power devices. Compared with high-end GPUs such as NVIDIA TITAN X, this platform significantly reduces power consumption at the same processing speed, at the same time, it has good scalability and fault tolerance, and is suitable for intelligent mobile devices such as unmanned aerial vehicles or autonomous vehicle.

Han et al.13 introduced an intelligent detection system for membrane fouling in membrane bioreactors (MBR), aimed at predicting membrane permeability, evaluating MBR performance, and reducing fouling. The system is based on recursive fuzzy neural networks and combines the least squares method to develop a new platform that connects sensors and software to facilitate the execution of complex algorithms. The experimental results show that the system can monitor membrane permeability in real-time and detect fouling early, optimize MBR operation, and improve wastewater treatment efficiency. Liu et al.14 studied a paper based triboelectric nanogenerator (BP-TENG) technology and integrated machine learning techniques, A multifunctional and multi scenario intelligent detection system has been implemented, capable of real-time handwriting to speech conversion, personalized jump prediction, and smart home automation, with a recognition accuracy rate of up to 99.8%. The system has high precision, environmental sustainability, and cost-effectiveness, making it suitable for applications in communication security, sports and health monitoring, and intelligent building management, thereby enhancing the potential of frictional electric sensing technology in intelligent systems.

In this research, ‘multimodal’ (multi-mode) carries a dual meaning: it refers to the system’s support for multiple data input modalities, including images, video streams, and real-time camera acquisition, enabling flexible adaptation to various detection scenarios; it also indicates the system’s implementation of multiple result output modalities, delivering detection results to users across different platforms through various channels such as WeChat, email, iMessage, and Android application notifications. This multimodal design demonstrates significant adaptability and practicality in diverse industrial environments, effectively meeting the complex requirements for defect detection in modern steel production processes.

This research pioneered the construction of a cloud-native multimodal intelligent system architecture, bringing breakthrough innovations to the field of steel surface defect detection: meticulously optimizing a series of advanced lightweight detection algorithms based on YOLOv5, significantly enhancing intelligent recognition capabilities in edge computing environments; elegantly implementing the integration of PyQt framework with deep learning algorithms, creating a multidimensional data input ecosystem; adopting microservice architecture and cloud technologies with foresight, building a distributed intelligent platform with exceptional resilience and scalability; innovatively designing a local-cloud-mobile collaborative push mechanism, achieving seamless cross-platform delivery of detection results; and confirming the system’s outstanding reliability and adaptability through the validation in industrial environments. The “multimodal” concept in this research is manifested both in the diversified data acquisition channels and in the comprehensive result distribution strategies, effectively addressing the complex and variable defect detection requirements in modern steel production. It establishes a forward-looking technical paradigm for the intelligent transformation of the steel industry, promoting innovative development of intelligent manufacturing in the Industry 4.0 era.

Steel surface defect dataset detection based on deep learning

Data serves as the foundation for artificial intelligence and machine learning models, directly impacting the training effectiveness and prediction accuracy of models. This study is based on the steel surface defect dataset from Northeastern University for training purposes24. Additionally, to ensure the effectiveness and practicality of the system, this study focuses on real-world datasets. A specialized company was commissioned to collect and create the steel surface defect dataset, guaranteeing that the system can make effective predictions in real production environments. Through this specialized data collection and creation process, this study obtained a representative steel surface defect dataset, which includes six types of defects: crazing, inclusion, patches, pitted surface, rolled-in scale, and scratches, totaling 472 defects.

The research utilizes the LabelImg open-source image annotation tool for annotating steel surface defect data, which supports bounding box annotation in YOLO format, facilitating direct use in object detection model training. The annotation process was completed by multiple professionals with steel industry backgrounds and underwent cross-validation to ensure annotation quality.

Steel surface defect detection based on deep learning

The core breakthrough in this research regarding the YOLOv5 model architecture lies in the strategic reconstruction of the backbone network. This paper meticulously designs alternatives based on EfficientNet-B1/B2 and MobileNet series, successfully replacing the original CSPDarknet53 backbone network structure of YOLOv5. EfficientNet achieves a balance between feature expression capability and computational efficiency through its compound uniform scaling mechanism and optimized convolutional block design; the MobileNet series, with its innovative depth-wise separable convolution and inverted residual structure, significantly reduces parameter count and floating-point operations while maintaining feature extraction capabilities. This backbone network reconstruction not only optimizes the information flow transmission efficiency of the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) but also enhances the model’s sensitivity to minute defects and textural features on steel surfaces. Quantitative evaluation demonstrates that this architectural innovation achieves a trade-off between computational complexity and detection performance in the field of steel surface defect detection, providing an efficient and reliable algorithmic foundation for industrial-grade edge intelligence applications. Regarding the availability of the self-built large-scale steel defect dataset, this dataset was collected and annotated by professional companies and is currently only available for internal research use under confidentiality agreements. However, researchers can obtain usage permissions after review by contacting the corresponding author, or use the Northeastern University open-source dataset mentioned in this paper for comparative experiments.

To improve the accuracy and efficiency of detection, this study uses a deep learning approach, specifically employing the improved YOLOv5 algorithm for steel surface defect detection. Algorithms such as YOLOv5, YOLOv5-efficient net-b1-YOLOv5-efficient net-b8, YOLOv5-mobile net-large, and YOLOv5-mobile net-small25 were trained on the dataset collected by the team of Song Kechen from Northeastern University. Additionally, to further verify the system’s generalization ability, we tested it on a self-built real steel defect dataset. The test results are shown in Fig. 10. The experimental results show that this detection system has good versatility, and the improved algorithm has achieved good results.

Conclusion

This study proposes and implements a multi-modal intelligent system architecture based on cloud-native technology for automatic detection and monitoring of steel surface defects. The system integrates edge computing, cloud computing, and mobile computing technologies to achieve efficient processing and analysis of industrial big data. The main innovations and achievements of the research are as follows:

Detection algorithm optimization: Based on the improved YOLOv5 algorithm, various lightweight models were introduced, significantly enhancing detection performance in complex industrial environments.System architecture innovation: Adopting microservice architecture and distributed message queues, the system achieved high availability and scalability. Experimental results show that on a self-built large-scale steel defect dataset, this system outperforms existing methods in key indicators such as detection accuracy, recall rate, and processing speed. Long-term deployment results further confirm the system’s reliability and scalability in actual production environments.

However, this study still has some limitations. As the scale and complexity of datasets increase, the system’s generalization ability may face challenges.With the rapid development of artificial intelligence technology, the following trends may emerge in the field of intelligent surface defect detection: (1) The application of self-supervised learning and few-shot learning methods to reduce dependence on large amounts of annotated data; (2) The development of multimodal fusion technologies, such as combining visual, acoustic, and thermal imaging data to provide more comprehensive defect information; (3) The integration of explainable AI technologies to make detection results more interpretable and increase industrial experts’ trust in the system; (4) The maturation of edge AI technologies, enabling online detection with lower latency and higher energy efficiency; (5) The deep integration of digital twin technology with defect detection, facilitating a transition from detection to prediction and supporting preventive maintenance. These developments will further advance the level of intelligent manufacturing in the steel industry, achieving production goals of higher quality and lower costs.

In conclusion, this study provides an implementable solution for the intelligent transformation of the steel industry, while also offering a valuable technical paradigm for intelligent manufacturing in the context of Industry 4.0. Through continuous optimization and innovation, we believe that this multi-modal intelligent system will play an important role in promoting the digital transformation of manufacturing and improving product quality, making positive contributions to increasing production efficiency, reducing costs, and enhancing enterprise competitiveness.

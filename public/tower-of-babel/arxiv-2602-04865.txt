Characterizing ( d , h ) (d,h) -elliptic stable irreducible curves
Juliana Coelho, Renata Costa

Abstract
We use admissible covers to characterize irreducible stable curves that are ( d , h ) (d,h) -elliptic, that is, that are limits of smooth curves admiting finite maps of degree- d d to smooth curves of genus h ≥ 1 h\geq 1 .



1
Introduction

A smooth curve
C
is said to be
d
-gonal if it admits a degree-
d
map to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
The gonality, that is, the minimum
d
such that
C
is
d
-gonal, is an important numerical invariant in the study of algebraic curves.
More generally, we say that
C
is
(d,h)
-elliptic if it admits a degree-
d
morphism to a smooth curve of genus
h
.
It follows from de Franchi’s theorem that, if
d\geq 2
, then a general curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
is not
(d,h)
-elliptic for any
h\geq 1
(see
[
3autores
, Corollary XXI.8.32]
).
It is thus an interesting problem to try to understand how such a smooth curve can degenerate, that is, what are the possible stable limits of
(d,h)
-elliptic smooth curves for
h\geq 1
.

In this paper, we deal with this issue using
the theory of admissible covers, introduced in
[
HarrMum
]
, and greatly generalized by many authors such as in
[
abracortivistoli
]
and
[
mochizuki
]
.
This approach has already been considered
for instance in
[
faberpaganini
]
, where the authors determine the class of the so-called bielliptic locus for genus-3 stable curves, that is, the locus of
(2,1)
-elliptic curves in
\overline{M}_{3}
.
The case
h=1

is related to the classical study of elliptic integrals
and was also studied in
[
ddelliptic2018
]
, albeit without the use of admissible covers, where the authors considered stable curves of genus 2 admiting two morphisms to a smooth curve of genus
1
.

1.1
Main Results

In this paper we work over
\mathbb{C}
.
As we mentioned, we consider a stable curve
C
to be
(d,h)
-elliptic if it is a limit of smooth ones or, equivalently, if there exists a
d
-sheeted admissible cover from a curve stably equivalent to
C
to a curve of genus
h
.
Rougly speaking, an admissible cover is a finite map of nodal curves satisfying some conditions, one of which is that the target is a pointed stable curve, when considered with the smooth branch points of the map.

On Section
3
we define pseudo-admissible covers by switching this condition to the condition that every node of the target curve lies in the intersection of two components.
On Proposition
5
we present a gluing for pseudo-admissible covers in the style of those introduced in
[
1
]
, and
on Proposition
6
we show that it is possible to characterize
(d,h)
-ellipticity using pseudo-admissible covers.

On Section
4
we focus on irreducible curves.
In Theorem
10
we characterize irreducible stable curves that are
(d,h)
-elliptic for
h\geq 1
, in terms of its normalization.
This result is similar to those of
[
2
]
, where the case
h=0
was addressed for stable curves with two components.
As a consequence, we examine in Corollaries
11
and
12
the case of irreducible stable curves with only one node.

2
Technical background

In this paper we always work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

A
curve

C
is a connected, projective and reduced scheme of dimension
1
over
\mathbb{C}
.
We denote by
C^{\text{sing}}
the singular locus of
C
, and by
C^{\text{sm}}
the smooth locus of
C
.
The
genus
of
C
is the arithmetic genus
g(C):=h^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
.
A
subcurve

Y
of
C
is a reduced subscheme of pure dimension
1
, or equivalently, a reduced union of irreducible components of
C
.
If
Y\subseteq C
is a subcurve, we say
Y^{c}:=\overline{C\smallsetminus Y}
is the
complement
of
Y
in
C
.

A
nodal curve

C
is a curve with at most ordinary double points, called
nodes
.
A node is said to be
separating
if there is a subcurve
Y
of
C
such that
Y\cap Y^{c}=\{n\}
.
Let
C
be a nodal curve and
\nu:{C}_{\nu}\rightarrow C
be its normalization. Let

n
be a node of
C
. The points
n_{1},n_{2}\in{C}_{\nu}
such that
\nu(n_{1})=\nu(n_{2})=n
are said to be
branches
of
n
.

A
rational chain
is a nodal curve of genus 0.
We remark that a curve is a rational chain if and only if its nodes are all separating and its components are all rational.
A
cycle
is a nodal curve
E
whose dual graph is a cycle, that is, up to reordering, the components
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}
of
E
are such that
E_{i}
meets
E_{i+1}
at a single point, for
i=1,\ldots,s-1
, and
E_{s}
meets
E_{1}
at a single point. We denote the cycle
E
as the ordered
s
-uple
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{s})
.
A
rational cycle
is a cycle where all components are rational. Note that a rational cycle is a curve of genus 1.

Let
g
and
n
be non negative integers such that
2g-2+n>0
. A
n
-pointed stable curve of genus
g
is a curve
C
of genus
g
together with
n
distinct
marked points

p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in C
such that for every smooth rational component
E
of
C
,
the number of points in the intersection
E\cap E^{c}
plus the number of indices
i
such that
p_{i}
lies on
E
is at least three.
A
stable curve
is a 0-pointed stable curve.

Let
\pi:C\rightarrow B
be a finite map between curves and let
p\in C
and
q\in B
be smooth points such that
\pi(p)=q
. Let
x
be a local parameter of
C
around
p
and
t
be a local parameter of
B
around
q
. Then, locally around
p
, the map
\pi
is given by
t=x^{e}
, where
e
is the
ramification index
of
\pi
at
p
, denoted by
e_{\pi}(p)
. Note that
1\leq e_{\pi}(p)\leq d
, where
d
is the degree of
\pi
.
We say that
\pi
is
ramified
at
p
, or that
p
is a
ramification point
of
\pi
, if
e_{\pi}(p)\geq 2.

Moreover, we say
\pi
is
totally ramified
at
p
if
e_{\pi}(p)=d
.
Finally, we say that
q
is a
branch point
of
\pi
if
e_{\pi}(p^{\prime})\geq 2
for some
p^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(q)
.

3
Admissible Covers

A smooth curve
C

is
(d,h)
-elliptic
if it admits a degree-
d
morphism to a smooth curve of genus
h
.
In this work we define a stable curve
C
to be
(d,h)
-elliptic
if it is a limit of smooth
(d,h)
-elliptic curves in the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of stable curves. More precisely, this occurs if there is a smoothing
f:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow S
whose generic fiber is a smooth
(d,h)
-elliptic curve and the special fiber is isomorphic to
C
.

Alternativelly,
(d,h)
-ellipticity can be characterized in terms of admissible covers.
A
d
-sheeted admissible cover
consists of a finite morphism
\pi:C\rightarrow B
of degree
d
, such that
B
and
C
are nodal curves and:

1.

\pi^{-1}(B^{\text{sing}})=C^{\text{sing}};

2.

\pi
is simply branched away from
C^{\text{sing}}
, that is, over each smooth point of
B
there exists at most one point of
C
where
\pi
is ramified and this point has ramification index 2;

3.

B
is a stable pointed curve, when considered with the smooth branch points of
\pi
;

4.

for every node
q
of
B
and every node
n
of
C
lying over it, the two branches of
C
over
n
map to the branches of
B
over
q
with the same ramification index.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be nodal curves. We say that
C^{\prime}
is
stably equivalent
to
C

if
C
can be obtained from
C^{\prime}
by contracting to a point some of the smooth rational components of
C^{\prime}
meeting the other components of
C^{\prime}
in only one or two points.
We remark that stable equivalence is not an equivalence relation, but rather a partial order
and thus we write
C^{\prime}\succ_{stab}C
to mean that
C^{\prime}
is stably equivalent to
C
.
Note moreover that, in this case,
g(C)=g(C^{\prime})
and there exists a
contraction map

\tau:C^{\prime}\rightarrow C
.
We say that a point
p^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}

lies over
a point
p\in C
if
\tau(p^{\prime})=p
.

Theorem 1
.

A stable curve
C
is
(d,h)
-elliptic if and only if there exists a
d
-sheeted admissible cover
{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow B
, where
{C}^{\prime}\succ_{stab}C
and
g(B)=h
.

Proof.

Follows from
[
mochizuki
]
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
C
be a nodal curve and
\pi:{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow B
be a

. Then for each irreducible component

\tau({C}^{\prime}_{r})=C_{r}

As the components of

4

In this section, our goal is to characterize irreducible nodal

-elliptic curves in terms of their normalizations.
First, we need a lemma.

Lemma 7
.

Let
C
be a nodal curve and let
{C}^{\prime}
be a nodal curve stably equivalent to
C
with contraction map
\tau:{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow C
.

(a)

If
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are two connected subcurves of
{C}^{\prime}
such that
\tau(Z_{1})=\tau(Z_{2})
is a subcurve of
C
, then
\tau(Z_{1})=\tau(Z_{2})=\tau(Z_{1}\cap Z_{2})
.

(b)

If
C
is irreducible then no two subcurves of
{C}^{\prime}
of positive genus have finite intersection.

(c)

Let
\pi:{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow B
be a
d
-sheeted pseudo-admissible cover.
Assume that
C
is irreducible and let
C_{n}
be the normalization of
C
and let
B_{0}=\pi(C_{n})
. Then every subcurve
Z
of
B
of positive genus must contain
B_{0}
.

In particular, if there exists a component
W
of
B
such that
g(W)\geq 1
, then
W=B_{0}
.

Proof.

First we prove (a). Set
Z:=\tau(Z_{1})=\tau(Z_{2})
and assume
Z
is a subcurve of
C
.
Recall from Lemma
2
that, if
W
is a component of
C
,
then there exists an unique component

of

such that
\tau(W^{\prime})=W
. Hence if

, then we must have
W^{\prime}\subset Z_{1}
and also

.
Thus
W^{\prime}\subset Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}
showing that

.

To show (b), let
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
be two subcurves of
{C}^{\prime}
of positive genus
so that
\tau(Z_{1})
and
\tau(Z_{2})
are subcurves of

.
Since

is irreducible, we must have

\tau(Z_{1})=\tau(Z_{2})
which, by (a), implies that

cannot be finite.

Lastly we show (c).
Assume there exists a subcurve

of

Then

is not contained in

Let

Since

q_{1},p_{1},\ldots,p_{s-1},q_{2}\in B
be such that

E_{1}\cap B_{0}={q_{1}},\quad E_{s}\cap B_{0}={q_{2}},\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{i}\cap E_{i+1}={p_{i}},\quad\text{for }i=1,\ldots,s-1.

Let
q^{1}_{1}\in C_{n}
and
q_{1}^{2}\in Z
such that

\pi(q_{1}^{1})=\pi(q_{1}^{2})=q_{1}

Since
q_{1}
is a node and
\pi
is pseudo-admissible, then
q_{1}^{1}
and
q_{1}^{2}
are also nodes. Therefore, there exist rational components
E_{1}^{1}
and
E_{1}^{2}
of
C^{\prime}
such that

q_{1}^{1}=C_{n}\cap E_{1}^{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad q_{1}^{2}=Z\cap E_{1}^{2}.

Note that we must have
\pi(E_{1}^{1})=\pi(E_{1}^{2})=E_{1}
.
Furthermore, since
E_{1}
has another node, namely
p_{1}
, then
E_{1}^{1}
and
E_{1}^{2}
also have nodes, say
p_{1}^{1}
and
p_{1}^{2}
, respectively, such that

\pi(p_{1}^{1})=\pi(p_{1}^{2})=p_{1}.

Consequently, there exist rational components
E_{2}^{1}
and
E_{2}^{2}
of
C^{\prime}
such that

p_{1}^{1}=E_{1}^{1}\cap E_{2}^{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad p_{1}^{2}=E_{1}^{2}\cap E_{2}^{2}

and again
we have,
\pi(E_{2}^{1})=\pi(E_{2}^{2})=E_{2}
.

Prceeding in this manner, for
j=1,2
we obtain rational components

E_{1}^{j},E_{2}^{j},\ldots,E_{s}^{j}
of
C^{\prime}

such that
\pi(E_{i}^{j})=E_{i}

and

E^{j}_{i}
meet
E^{j}_{i+1}
at
p^{j}_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,s-1
.
Lastly, since
E_{s}
meets
B_{0}
at the node
q_{2}
, then

E_{s}^{1}
meets
C_{n}
and

E_{s}^{2}
meets
Z
.
Hence

C_{n}\cup E_{1}^{1}\cup\ldots\cup E_{s}^{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad Z\cup E_{1}^{2}\cup\ldots\cup E_{s}^{2}

are two subcurves of
{C}^{\prime}
of positive genus and having no common component, contradicting Lemma
7
, thus showing that
e=d
.
∎

We remark that the previous result does not hold for admissible or pseudo-admissible covers to a curve of genus 0, as attested by
[
1
, Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.5]
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
C
be an irreducible nodal curve and
C_{n}
be its normalization.
Let
\pi:{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow B
be
a
d
-sheeted pseudo-admissible cover,
where
{C}^{\prime}\succ_{stab}C
. Assume that
g(B)\geq 1
. Let
n\in C
be a node and denote by
n^{1},n^{2}\in C_{n}
its branches.
If
\pi(n^{1})\neq\pi(n^{2})
, then there exists a cycle
Z
in
B
such that
\pi(n^{1})
and
\pi(n^{2})
are nodes of
Z
.
Moreover, we have
e_{\pi}(n^{1})=e_{\pi}(n^{2})
.

Proof.

Assume there is no such cycle of
B
.
Then every connected subcurve of
B
containing
q_{1}:=\pi(n^{1})
and
q_{2}:=\pi(n^{2})
must contain
B_{0}:=\pi(C_{n})
.
Now, since
C^{\prime}
is stably equivalent to
C
,
there is a rational chain
Y^{\prime}\subset{C}^{\prime}
not containing
C_{n}
and connecting
n^{1}
to
n^{2}
.
Hence, since
Y^{\prime}
is connected then so is
\pi(Y^{\prime})
and, since
\pi(Y^{\prime})
contains
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
,
then

\pi(Y^{\prime})
must contain
B_{0}
.
Thus there is a component
L
of
Y^{\prime}
such that
\pi(L)=B_{0}
.
Since
B_{0}=\pi(C_{n})
, then the degree of the restriction

\pi|_{C_{n}}

must be less than

d
, contradicting Proposition
8
.

Now we show the last assertion.
Let
Z=(B_{0},E_{1},\ldots,E_{s})\subset B
be the stated cycle, such that
q_{1}\in E_{1}
and
q_{2}\in E_{s}
.
Set
Y=E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{s}
, that is,
Y:=Z\setminus B_{0}
.
Moreover,
set
\widetilde{Y}\subset Y^{\prime}
such that
C_{n}\cup\widetilde{Y}
is a cycle in
C^{\prime}
through
n^{1}
and
n^{2}
,
say

C_{n}\cup\widetilde{Y}=(C_{n},\widetilde{E}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{E}_{r})
, with
n^{1}\in\widetilde{E}_{1}
and
n^{2}\in\widetilde{E}_{r}
.
Note that
\pi(\widetilde{Y})\subset Y
.

Assume, without lack of generality, that
e_{\pi}(n^{1})<e_{\pi}(n^{2})
.
Then there exists a point
m_{1}\in\widetilde{Y}
distinct from
n^{1}
such that
\pi(m_{1})=q_{1}
.
Since
q_{1}
is a node, then so is
m_{1}
and there is a rational component
E_{1}^{\prime}
of
C^{\prime}

distinct from
\widetilde{E}_{1}
containing
m_{1}
.
Note that

\pi(E_{1}^{\prime})=B_{0}
and so
q_{2}\in\pi(E_{1}^{\prime})
. Hence there exists
m_{2}\in E_{1}^{\prime}
such that
\pi(m_{2})=q_{2}
.
Since
q_{2}
is a node, so is
m_{2}
and there exists a rational component
E_{2}^{\prime}
of
C^{\prime}

distinct from
E_{1}^{\prime}
passing through
m_{2}
.
Then
E_{2}^{\prime}
maps to the component of
B^{\prime}
through
q_{2}
distinct from
B_{0}
.
Applying an argument similar to that used in the proof of
Proposition
8
,
we obtain a chain in
C^{\prime}
not containing
E_{1}^{\prime}
and connecting
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
.
But this implies that
m_{1}
is a triple point, a contradiction.
∎

Finally, we reach the central result of the section, which characterizes the
(d,h)
-ellipticity of stable irreducible curves in terms of that of its normalization and the configuration of its nodes.

Theorem 10
.

Let
C
be an irreducible nodal curve and let
C_{n}
be its normalization.
Let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{\delta}
be the nodes of
C
and
let
n_{1}^{i},n_{2}^{i}\in C_{n}
be the branches of
n_{i}
,
for each
i=1,\ldots,\delta
. Let
d,h\in\mathbb{Z}
with
d\geq 2
and
h\geq 1
.

The curve C is
(d,h)
-elliptic if and only if
C_{n}
is a smooth
(d,{h^{\prime}})
-elliptic curve for some
{h^{\prime}}\leq h
, with a finite map
\pi:C_{n}\rightarrow B_{0}
of degree
d
and
g(B_{0})={h}^{\prime}
,
such that
there exist integers

0\leq\delta_{0}<\delta_{1}<\ldots<\delta_{s-1}<\delta_{s}=\delta

and distinct points

q_{\delta_{1}}^{1},q_{\delta_{1}}^{2},\ldots,q_{\delta_{s}}^{1},q_{\delta_{s}}^{2}\in B_{0}

where
s=h-h^{\prime}
and, up to reordering, we have

(a)

\pi(n_{i}^{1})=\pi(n_{i}^{2})
for
i=1,\ldots,\delta_{0}
;

(b)

\pi^{-1}(q_{\delta_{k}}^{1})=\{n_{\delta_{k-1}+1}^{1},\ldots,n_{\delta_{k}}^{1}\}

and

\pi^{-1}(q_{\delta_{k}}^{2})=\{n_{\delta_{k-1}+1}^{2},\ldots,n_{\delta_{k}}^{2}\}

for
k=1,\ldots,s
;

(c)

e_{\pi}(n_{i}^{1})=e_{\pi}(n_{i}^{2}),

for each
i=\delta_{0}+1,\ldots,\delta
.

Proof.

Assume first that
C
is an
(d,h)
-elliptic curve and let
\pi\colon C^{\prime}\rightarrow B
be a pseudo-admissible cover of degree
d
, where
C^{\prime}\succ_{stab}C
and
g(B)=h
.
Then, by Proposition
8
, the map
\pi|_{C_{n}}
has degree
d
.

Let
\delta_{0}
be the number of nodes
n_{i}
such that
n_{i}^{1}
and
n_{i}^{2}
have the same image under
\pi
.
If
\delta_{0}=\delta
, there is nothing to prove.
Now assume
\delta_{0}<\delta
.
Then there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,\delta\}
such that

\pi(n_{i}^{1})\neq\pi(n_{i}^{2})
.
Set
q^{1}:=\pi(n_{i}^{1})
and
q^{2}:=\pi(n_{i}^{2})
.

If, for some
i^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,\delta\}
we have
\pi(n_{{i}^{\prime}}^{1})=q^{1}
, then we must have
\pi(n_{{i}^{\prime}}^{2})=q^{2}
.
Indeed, if
\pi(n_{{i}^{\prime}}^{2})\neq q^{2}
, then there exists
q\in B_{0}
, with
q\neq q^{2}
such that
\pi(n_{{i}^{\prime}}^{2})=q
.
Since
C^{\prime}
is stably equivalent to
C

there is a rational chain in
C^{\prime}
not containing
C_{n}
connecting
n_{i}^{1}
and
n_{i}^{2}
and,
by Lemma
9
,
this chain maps
to a rational chain in
B
not containing
B_{0}
passing through
q^{1}
and
q^{2}
.
Likewise,
there is a rational chain in
C^{\prime}
not containing
C_{n}
connecting
n_{i^{\prime}}^{1}
and
n_{i^{\prime}}^{2}
mapping
to a rational chain in
B
not containing
B_{0}
passing through
q^{1}
and
q
.
Since
q^{1}
is also a point in
B_{0}
, then in this case
q^{1}
would be a triple point of
B
contradicting the fact that
B
is nodal.

Let
t_{1}
be the number of those
i\in\{1,\ldots,\delta\}
such that
\pi(n_{i}^{1})=q^{1}

and set
\delta_{1}:=\delta_{0}+t_{1}
.
Setting
q_{\delta_{1}}^{1}:=q^{1}
and
q_{\delta_{1}}^{2}:=q^{2}
,
we showed that, reordering the nodes if necessary,

\pi^{-1}(q^{1}_{\delta_{1}})=\{n_{{i}}^{1}\ |\ i=\delta_{0}+1,\ldots,\delta_{1}\}

and

\pi^{-1}(q^{2}_{\delta_{1}})=\{n_{i}^{2}\ |\ i=\delta_{0}+1,\ldots,\delta_{1}\}.

If
\delta_{1}=\delta
, we are done. If not, then there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,\delta\}
such that

\pi(n_{i}^{1})\neq\pi(n_{i}^{2})
and
\{\pi(n_{i}^{1}),\,\pi(n_{i}^{2})\}\neq\{q_{\delta_{1}}^{1},\,q_{\delta_{1}}^{2}\}
. In this case, proceeding as above, we obtain distinct points
q_{\delta_{2}}^{1},q_{\delta_{2}}^{2}\in B_{0}
whose pre-images under
\pi
are of the form above.
Proceeding in this way we obtain

0\leq\delta_{0}<\delta_{1}<\ldots<\delta_{s}=\delta

and distinct points
q_{\delta_{1}}^{1},q_{\delta_{1}}^{2},\ldots,q_{\delta_{s}}^{1},q_{\delta_{s}}^{2},\in B_{0}

as stated, for some
s\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Lemma
9
, each pair
q_{\delta_{j}}^{1}
and
q_{\delta_{j}}^{2}
is associated to a cycle
Z_{j}
of
B
containing
B_{0}
and having
q_{\delta_{j}}^{1}
and
q_{\delta_{j}}^{2}
as nodes.
Hence

g(B)\geq g(B_{0})+s
.

Moreover, there are no other cycles in
B
. Indeed, by Lemma
7
, any cycle
Z
of
B
must contain
B_{0}
. If
q^{1},q^{2}\in B_{0}
are the points in the intersection

B_{0}\cap(\overline{Z\setminus B_{0}})
, then the points in

(\pi|_{C_{n}})^{-1}(q^{1})
and
(\pi|_{C_{n}})^{-1}(q^{1})

are branches of nodes of
C
which would imply that
q^{1}
and
q^{2}
would be
q^{1}_{\delta_{j}}

and
q^{2}_{\delta_{j}}
for some
j
.
Therefore, we get the equality
g(B)=g(B_{0})+s
.
Finally, by Lemma
9
, we have

e_{\pi}(n_{i}^{1})=e_{\pi}(n_{i}^{2})

for every
i=\delta_{0}+1,\ldots,\delta
.

Conversely, assume there exists a map
\pi
as stated. Then applying Proposition
5
successively
s
times followed by Proposition
4
,
the result follows.
∎

As an example, one can classify the
(d,h)
-elliptic irreducible curves having only one node, for
h\geq 1
.

Corollary 11
.

Let
C
be an irreducible nodal curve with only one node
n
.
Let
C_{n}
be the normalization of
C
and let
n_{1},n_{2}\in C_{n}
be the branches of the nodes.
Let
d,h\in\mathbb{Z}
with
d\geq 2
and
h\geq 1
.
Then
C
is
(d,h)
-elliptic if and only if
C_{n}
satisfies one of the following conditions

(a)

C_{n}
is
(d,h)
-elliptic with
a finite map
\pi:C_{n}\rightarrow B_{0}
of degree
d
and
g(B_{0})={h}
, and
\pi(n_{1})=\pi(n_{2})
; or

(b)

C_{n}
is
(d,h-1)
-elliptic with
a finite map
\pi:C_{n}\rightarrow B_{0}
of degree
d
and
g(B_{0})={h-1}
, and
\pi(n_{1})\neq\pi(n_{2})
and
\pi
is totally ramified at
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
10
.
∎

Corollary 12
.

Let
C
be an irreducible nodal curve with only one node
n
and let
C_{n}
be it normalization.
If
C_{n}
is hyperelliptic and has genus at least 2, then
C
is either hyperelliptic or bielliptic, but not both.

Proof.

Follows Theorem
10
, since the hyperelliptic map for
C_{n}
is unique.
∎

On post-Lie structures for free Lie algebras
Annika Burmester, Ulf Kühn

Abstract
We study post-Lie structures on free Lie algebras, the Grossman-Larson product on their enveloping algebras, and provide an abstract formula for its dual coproduct. This might be of interest for the general theory of post-Hopf algebras. Using a magmatic approach, we explore post-Lie algebras connected to multiple zeta values and their q q -analogues. For multiple zeta values, this framework yields an algebraic interpretation of the Goncharov coproduct. Assuming that the Bernoulli numbers satisfy the so called threshold shuffle identities, we present a post-Lie structure, whose induced Lie bracket we expect to restrict to the dual of indecomposables of multiple q q -zeta values. Our post-Lie algebras align with Ecalle’s theory of bimoulds: we explicitly identify the ari bracket with a post-Lie structure on a free Lie algebra, and conjecture a correspondence for the uri bracket.

3

The Ihara bracket

defines another Lie bracket on the free Lie algebra

algebra

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-m-1}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j}-1}\binom{r+m-1}{b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{s}-1}.

Let
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{l})
. With (
50
) and (
52
), we get that the left hand side of the claimed formula is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}=0}^{d_{1}}\cdots\sum_{r_{l}=0}^{d_{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{d_{i}-r_{i}}\binom{d_{i}-1}{r_{i}-1}\Bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-m-1}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j-1}-1}\binom{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{l}+m-1}{b_{j}+\cdots+b_{s}-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 162.18062pt-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-m-1}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j}-1}\binom{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{l}+m-1}{b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{s}-1}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-m-1}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j-1}-1}\sum_{r_{1}=0}^{d_{1}}\cdots\sum_{r_{l}=0}^{d_{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{d_{i}-r_{i}}\binom{d_{i}-1}{r_{i}-1}\binom{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{l}+m-1}{b_{j}+\cdots+b_{s}-1}

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-m-1}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j}-1}\sum_{r_{1}=0}^{d_{1}}\cdots\sum_{r_{l}=0}^{d_{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{d_{i}-r_{i}}\binom{d_{i}-1}{r_{i}-1}\binom{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{l}+m-1}{b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{s}-1}.

(53)

For all
k,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},k\leq d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l}+m
, we have the identity

\sum_{r_{1}=0}^{d_{1}}\cdots\sum_{r_{l}=0}^{d_{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{r_{i}}\binom{d_{i}-1}{r_{i}-1}\binom{m+d_{1}-r_{1}+\cdots+d_{l}-r_{l}}{k-r_{1}-\cdots-r_{l}}=\binom{m}{m-k},

which can be checked via generating series
[
Bur23
, Lemma 3.23]
. This identity applied to the expression in (
53
) yields (choose
k=m+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l}-b_{j}-\cdots-b_{s}
resp.
k=m+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l}-b_{j+1}-\cdots-b_{s}
)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-m-1}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j-1}-1}\binom{m-1}{b_{j}+\cdots+b_{s}-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{l}-1}

(54)

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-m-1}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j}-1}\binom{m-1}{b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{s}-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{l}-1}.

Next, observe that

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert}(\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=\min\{t\mid b_{1}+\cdots+b_{t}\geq b_{1}+\cdots+b_{s}-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{l}\}

\displaystyle=\min\{t\mid b_{t+1}+\cdots+b_{s}\leq d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l}\}.

We compute (
54
) by considering the three cases
j>\operatorname{ind}_{\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert}(\mathbf{b})
,
j<\operatorname{ind}_{\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert}(\mathbf{b})
and
j=\operatorname{ind}_{\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert}(\mathbf{b})
.

If
j>\operatorname{ind}_{\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert}(\mathbf{b})
, then we must have

b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{s}\leq d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l},\quad b_{j}+\cdots+b_{s}\leq d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l}.

Therefore, both latter binomial coefficients in (
54
) vanish, and thus the whole expression equals
0
.

If
j<\operatorname{ind}_{\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert}(\mathbf{b})
, then we must have

b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{s}>d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l},\quad b_{j}+\cdots+b_{s}>d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l}.

With (
51
) in the case
s=2
, we can reduce (
54
) to

\displaystyle\binom{n-1}{(b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j-1})+(b_{j}+\cdots+b_{s}-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{l})-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\binom{n-1}{(b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j})+(b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{s}-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{l})-1}

\displaystyle=0.

Finally, if
j=\operatorname{ind}_{\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert}(\mathbf{b})
, then we must have

b_{j+1}+\cdots+b_{s}\leq d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l},\quad b_{j}+\cdots+b_{s}>d_{1}+\cdots+d_{l}.

Therefore, the latter binomial coefficient in the second row of (
54
) vanishes. So, with (
51
) the expression in (
54
) reduces to

\binom{n-1}{(b_{1}+\cdots+b_{j-1})+(b_{j}+\cdots+b_{s}-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{l})-1}=\binom{n-1}{\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert-1}.

∎

As a special case of Theorem
A.3
, we derive an identity used in Section
4
.

Corollary A.4
.

We have the binomial identity

\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}(-1)^{r}\binom{d}{r}\binom{n+d-r-1}{b-1}=\binom{n-1}{b-d-1},\qquad d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ b,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Set
s=l=1
in Theorem
A.3
. Then, the formula follows from the substitution
r\mapsto d-r
and the observation that
\binom{d}{r}=0
for
r>d
.
∎

A.2
Identities for Bernoulli numbers

Recall that
\mathcal{C}
is the set of all compositions. For
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})\in\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\ell(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=s
the length. We have a map

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}\times\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle(a,\boldsymbol{\alpha})

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(\,\ell(\boldsymbol{\alpha}),\,\operatorname{ind}_{a}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\,\big),

where the second component is the threshold indicator function from (
49
).
Given any formal power series

\mathbf{B}(x,y)=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}B(m,n)x^{m}y^{n},

with the normalizing condition
B(1,1)=1
, we can define multiplicities by

\displaystyle\mu_{a,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{B}=B\left(\ell(\boldsymbol{\alpha}),\operatorname{ind}_{a}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\right),\qquad a\in\mathbb{N},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}.

(55)

Here, we set
\mu_{a,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{B}=0
if
\operatorname{ind}_{a}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=0
.

We extend the multiplicity
\mu_{a,\_}^{B}
linearly in the second argument, i.e., for a formal sum of indices
n_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}+n_{2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}
we set

\mu_{a,n_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}+n_{2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}}^{B}=n_{1}\,\mu_{a,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1}}^{B}+n_{2}\,\mu_{a,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2}}^{B}.

The concatenation of two indices
\mathbf{k},\mathbf{l}
is given by the index
(k_{1},...,k_{\ell(\mathbf{k})},l_{1},...,l_{\ell(\mathbf{l})})
.
We denote it by
(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{l})
and we extend this pairing bilinear.
We combine these convention with the shuffle product of indices, for example

\mu_{2,(4,(3,2)\shuffle(2))}^{B}=\mu_{2,(4,2\,(3,2,2)+(2,3,2))}^{B}=2\mu_{2,(4,3,2,2)}^{B}+\mu_{2,(4,2,3,2)}^{B}.

By a decomposition of an index

\mathbf{k}
we understand a pair of indices
\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}
such that
\mathbf{k}=(\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2})
, where we allow either
\mathbf{k}_{1}
or
\mathbf{k}_{2}
to be the empty index, i.e.,

\mathbf{k}=(\emptyset,\mathbf{k})=(\mathbf{k},\emptyset)
is allowed in this context.
Given an index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{d})
its reversed index is given by
\overline{\mathbf{k}}=(-1)^{d}\,(k_{d},\ldots,k_{2},k_{1})
.

Definition A.5
.

We say a multiplicity
\mu_{a,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{B}
given by (
55
) satisfies the
threshold shuffle identities
if the following equalities hold

(i)

For all
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}
,
{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell({\boldsymbol{\vartheta}})}

and all
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
we have
that

\displaystyle\mu_{t,{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\shuffle{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}}^{B}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2})={\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\\
({\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{2})={\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\end{subarray}}\mu_{\lvert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\rvert-d_{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}}^{B}\,\mu_{t,({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}\shuffle{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{1},\lvert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\rvert-d_{1},{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{2})}^{B}+\mu_{\lvert{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{2}\rvert-d_{2},{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{2}}^{B}\,\mu_{t,({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}\shuffle{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{1},\lvert{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{2}\rvert-d_{2},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2})}^{B},

where
t=\lvert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rvert+\lvert{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\rvert-d_{1}-d_{2}
,

(ii)

For all
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}
,
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}
,
{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell({\boldsymbol{\vartheta}})}

and all
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
we have
that

\displaystyle 0=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2})={\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\\
({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{2})={\boldsymbol{\tau}}\\
({\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{2})={\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\mu_{\lvert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\rvert+\lvert{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{1}\rvert-d_{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\shuffle\overline{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{1}}}^{B}\,\mu_{t,({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}\shuffle\overline{{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{2}},\lvert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\rvert+\lvert{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{1}\rvert-d_{1},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{2}\shuffle\overline{{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{1}})}^{B}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\mu_{\lvert{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{2}\rvert+\lvert{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{1}\rvert-d_{2},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{2}\shuffle\overline{{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{1}}}^{B}\,\mu_{t,({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}\shuffle\overline{{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{2}},\lvert{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{2}\rvert+\lvert{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}_{1}\rvert-d_{2},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\shuffle\overline{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{1}})}^{B},

where
t=\lvert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rvert+\lvert{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\rvert+\lvert{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\rvert-d_{1}-d_{2}
.

Observe that the identities in (i) and (ii) are homogeneous with respect to scaling of each
B(\ell(\boldsymbol{\alpha}),\operatorname{ind}_{a}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}))
by a factor

t^{\ell(\boldsymbol{\alpha})-1}
.

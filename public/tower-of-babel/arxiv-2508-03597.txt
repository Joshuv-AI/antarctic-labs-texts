Optimal Quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -Locally Repairable Codes From Matrix-Product Codes
Meng Cao, Kun Zhou*

Abstract
This paper studies optimal quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs from matrix-product (MP) codes. We establish a necessary and sufficient condition for an MP code to be an optimal ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRC. Based on this, we present a characterization for optimal quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs from MP codes with nested constituent codes, and also study optimal quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs constructed from MP codes with non-nested constituent codes. Through Hermitian dual-containing and Euclidean dual-containing MP codes, we present five infinite families of optimal quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs with flexible parameters.



I
Introduction

A
linear code
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
n
, dimension
k
and minimum (Hamming) distance
d
, denoted as
[n,k,d]_{q}
, is a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
According to the
Singleton bound
, the minimum distance satisfies
d\leq n-k+1
, and if
d=n-k+1
, the code is called a
maximum distance separable (MDS) code
.

Classical locally repairable codes (LRCs) are essential for efficient data recovery in distributed storage systems. Optimal LRCs achieve the theoretical limit described by the Singleton-like bound, which establishes the theoretical limit for balancing redundancy and fault tolerance. Since their formalization in foundational works

[
39
]
and
[
41
]
, these codes have attracted significant research attention due to their practical value in large-scale storage applications.

The quantum counterpart emerged with Golowich and Guruswami’s pioneering quantum LRC framework
[
37
]
, opening new possibilities for quantum data storage. Subsequent research established fundamental bounds and construction methods
[
24
,
10
]
, while Galindo et al.
[
21
]
extended these to quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs. They established equivalence between classical and quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs under specific conditions when using Hermitian dual-containing or Euclidean dual-containing codes.

Matrix-product (MP) codes, introduced in
[
2
]
, can produce long classical codes by combining short constituent codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{N}
with a defining matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}^{N\times r}
. While MP codes efficiently build classical optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRCs (see, e.g.,
[
31
,
29
]
), adapting them to quantum optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRCs faced two critical difficult points:

•

Constituent codes and matrices selection: No systematic/general method existed for choosing suitable constituent codes or defining matrices;

•

Overly Restrictive Conditions: Prior approaches imposed impractical constraints.

This paper aims to study optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs from matrix-product (MP) codes. The main contributions in this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We establish a necessary and sufficient condition for an MP code to be an optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRC.

•

We characterize optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs from MP codes with nested constituent code.

•

We study optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs constructed from MP codes with non-nested constituent codes.

•

We construct three infinite families of optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRCs involving Hermitian or Euclidean duality relation.

•

Through MP codes, we present five infinite families of optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs with flexible parameters.

This paper is organized as follows. Section II reviews fundamental prerequisites, including matrix-product codes, optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs. Section III characterizes optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs from matrix-product (MP) codes. Section IV constructs three infinite families of optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRCs involving Hermitian or Euclidean duality relation. Section V presents three new infinite families of optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs via Hermitian dual-containing MP codes. Section VI presents two new infinite families of optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs via Euclidean dual-containing MP codes. Section VII concludes this paper.

\displaystyle G:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1,1}G_{1}&a_{1,2}G_{1}&\cdots&a_{1,M}G_{1}\\

(

II-C

(r,\delta)
-LRCs

For an
[n,k,d]_{q}
linear code
\mathcal{C}
, we say the

-th symbol

(where

) of

has

-locality
if there exists a subset

containing

and a punctured code

such that the length
|S_{i}|\leq r+\delta-1
and the distance

. Here,
\mathcal{C}|_{S_{i}}
denotes the code

by deleting the components indexed by

in each codeword of
\mathcal{C}
. We call

an

-LRC
if every symbol has

-locality.

In
[
39
]
, the Singleton-type bound for the parameters

,

,

,

and

-LRC is established as

\displaystyle d\leq n-k+1-\bigg(\bigg\lceil\frac{k}{r}\bigg\rceil-1\bigg)(\delta-1).

(10)

An

-LRC meeting this bound with equality is called an

(r,\delta)
-LRC
, or simply called optimal.

Suppose

is an

matrix, where
\mathbf{h}_{i}
is a column vector of length

for every

.
The

\displaystyle\text{Supp}(H):=\{i\in[n]:\;\mathbf{h}_{i}\neq\mathbf{0}\},

where

is the zero column vector of length

[

]
.

.

(
[

, Lemma 2]
)

Let

be an

be positive integers, with

. The
\alpha
-th code symbol of

has

-locality if and only if there exists an

with the following properties:

(1)

;

(2)

II-D

Quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs

As usual, we use the notation
[\mspace{-2mu}[n,k,d]\mspace{-2mu}]_{q}
to denote a
q
-ary quantum code of length
n
, dimension
q^{k}
, and minimum distance
d
. Such a code is a
q^{k}
-dimensional subspace of the
q^{n}
-dimensional complex Hilbert space
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{q^{n}}
. It can detect up to
d-1
quantum errors and correct up to
\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor
quantum errors.

In a recent study
[
21
]
, Galindo et al. established a fundamental connection between classical codes satisfying the Hermitian (resp. Euclidean) dual-containing property and quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs.
Their work effectively characterizes optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs induced by classical codes, as formally defined below.

Definition 3
.

(
[
21
, Definition 31]
)
Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
) be a linear code. Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is Hermitian (resp. Euclidean) dual-containing,
\dim(\mathcal{C})=\frac{n+k}{2}
and
\mathcal{C}
is an
(r,\delta)
-LRC. If
\delta\leq d(\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{H}}})
(resp.
\delta\leq d(\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{E}}})
) and the induced quantum code with parameters
[\mspace{-2mu}[n,k,\geq d(\mathcal{C})]\mspace{-2mu}]_{q}
satisfies

\displaystyle k+2d(\mathcal{C})+2\bigg(\bigg\lceil\frac{n+k}{2r}\bigg\rceil-1\bigg)(\delta-1)=n+2,

(11)

then the induced quantum code with parameters
[\mspace{-2mu}[n,k,d(\mathcal{C})]\mspace{-2mu}]_{q}
is said to be an
optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRC
.

Remark 1
.

In Definition
3
, since
k=2\dim(\mathcal{C})-n
, it is straightforward to verify that Eq. (
11
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{C})=n-\dim(\mathcal{C})+1-\bigg(\bigg\lceil\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{r}\bigg\rceil-1\bigg)(\delta-1).

This is equivalent to stating that
\mathcal{C}
is an optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRC. Therefore, within the framework of Definition
3
, an
(r,\delta)
-LRC with Hermitian (resp. Euclidean) dual-containing property is optimal if and only if its induced quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRC is optimal. This conclusion will be applied in the following sections.

To investigate optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs, we need to revisit the following result.

Lemma 7
.

(
[
22
, Theorem 4.1]
)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRC with parameters
[n,k,d]_{q^{2}}
(resp.
[n,k,d]_{q}
). Then, the following two statements hold:

(1)

n-k\geq\lceil\frac{k}{r}\rceil(\delta-1)
, i.e.,
d\geq\delta
;

(2)

If
\mathcal{C}
is Hermitian (resp. Euclidean) dual-containing, then the induced quantum code is an optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRC.

III-A

Criterion for optimal

-LRCs from MP codes

In the following theorem, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for an MP code to be an optimal

-LRC.

Theorem 1

Let
\mathcal{C}(A):=[\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{N}]\cdot A
, where
A
is an
N\times N
matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
(resp.
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathcal{C}_{i}
is an optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRC with parameters
[n,k_{i},d_{i}]_{q^{2}}
(resp.
[n,k_{i},d_{i}]_{q}
) for every

. If

for some
j_{0}\in[N]
, and

\mathcal{C}(A)
is an
(r,\delta)
-LRC, then
\mathcal{C}(A)
is an optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRC if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

for every

(2)

\Big\lceil\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}}{r}\Big\rceil=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big\lceil\frac{k_{i}}{r}\big\rceil
.

Proof:

Theorem 5

The third family of optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs from Hermitian dual-containing MP codes

In the following theorem, we give an infinite family of optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs from MP codes with non-nested constituent codes.

Theorem 10
.

Let
q\geq 5
be an odd prime power. Let
u,t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
v\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
v\leq u
and
u+v\leq\frac{q-3}{2}
.
Then, there exists an infinite family of optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs with parameters

\displaystyle\big[\mspace{-3mu}\big[2t(q-1),2t(q-1)-4tu-2v,u+v+1\big]\mspace{-3mu}\big]_{q},

(41)

where
(r,\delta)=(q-1-u,u+1)
.

Proof:

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}(u,0,t)
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathcal{C}(u,v,t)
. Then,

•

\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a
(q-1-u,u+1)
-LRC with parameters
[t(q-1),t(q-1)-tu,u+1]_{q^{2}}
;

•

\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a
(q-1-u,u+1)
-LRC with parameters
[t(q-1),t(q-1)-tu-v,u+v+1]_{q^{2}}
.

Define
\mathcal{C}(A):=[\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}]\cdot A
, where

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Since
u+v\leq\lfloor\frac{q-1}{2}\rfloor-1
, both
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
are Hermitian dual-containing.
Note that
d_{1}=\delta=u+1
. Besides,
v\leq u
ensures
d_{2}\leq 2d_{1}
. The condition
u+v\leq\big\lfloor\frac{q-1}{2}\big\rfloor-1
implies

\displaystyle\bigg\lceil\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{r}\bigg\rceil=\bigg\lceil\frac{2t(q-1-u)-v}{q-1-u}\bigg\rceil=2t

and

\displaystyle\bigg\lceil\frac{k_{1}}{r}\bigg\rceil+\bigg\lceil\frac{k_{2}}{r}\bigg\rceil=2t.

Therefore,
\lceil\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{r}\rceil=\lceil\frac{k_{1}}{r}\rceil+\lceil\frac{k_{2}}{r}\rceil=2t
.

By Theorem
4
,
\mathcal{C}(A)
induces an optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRC with parameters in Eq. (
41
).
∎

VI-A

Two infinite families of type I
\tau
-OD matrices

Theorem 11
.

Let
q=p^{e}
be an odd prime power. Let
q-1=(N-1)s
with
N\geq 3
and
s\geq 2
. Then, the following two statements hold.

(1)

If
p\mid N
, then there exists an infinite family of
N\times N
type I
\tau
-OD matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where

\displaystyle\tau=\prod_{i=1}^{\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil}(i,N+1-i).

(2)

If
p\nmid N
, then there exists an infinite family of
N\times N
type I
\tau
-OD matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where

\displaystyle\tau=\prod_{i=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil}(i,N+1-i).

Proof:

Let
g
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Define an
N\times N
matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
as follows:

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
0&1&g^{s}&g^{2s}&\cdots&g^{(N-2)s}\\
0&1&g^{2s}&g^{4s}&\cdots&g^{2(N-2)s}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&1&g^{(N-2)s}&g^{2(N-2)s}&\cdots&g^{(N-2)^{2}s}\\
0&1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(42)

It is straightforward to verify that
A
is NSC, and

\displaystyle AA^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}N&0&\cdots&0&N-1\\
0&0&\cdots&N-1&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\begin{rotate}$\ddots$\end{rotate}&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&N-1&\cdots&0&0\\
N-1&0&\cdots&0&N-1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

By Lemma
4
and Theorem
5
, statements (1) and (2) hold. Therefore, we complete the whole proof.
∎

Remark 7
.

The construction of infinite families of

-OD matrices is an interesting and challenging problem, as proposed in
[

, Sect. 5]

illustrates that there exist two infinite families of type I
\tau

(r,\delta)

Theorem 12
.

Let
q-1=(N-1)s
, where

,

(43)

Proof:

Let
A
be an
N\times N
type I
\tau
-OD matrix from Theorem
11
(1) for

3

(r,\delta)

VII
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we studied optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs from matrix-product (MP) codes.
We established a necessary and sufficient condition for an MP code to be an optimal
(r,\delta)
-LRC.
Based on this, we presented a characterization for optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs from MP codes with nested constituent codes, and also studied optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs constructed from MP codes with non-nested constituent codes.
Through Hermitian dual-containing and Euclidean dual-containing MP codes, we presented five infinite families of optimal quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs with flexible parameters.

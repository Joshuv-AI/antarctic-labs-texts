Abstract
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) in monostatic in-band full-duplex (IBFD) systems encounters significant challenges due to self-interference (SI) at the radar receiver during concurrent communication and radar operations. This paper proposes a novel waveform-domain self-interference cancellation (SIC) technique that leverages the unique properties of orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) and affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM) signals. The proposed approach designs the integrated dual-functionality frame to utilize OFDM for communication and AFDM for radar sensing, both generated using the same modulator block. Then, we establish the conditions under which a wide sense stationary (WSS) process in the time domain appears as WSS in the affine domain and demonstrate that the interfering OFDM signal behaves as an additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) in this domain. Exploiting this property, the received signal is projected into the affine domain, where the SI appears as AWGN, enabling its subtraction with minimal residual interference. To further mitigate the residual SI, an iterative low-complexity windowing scheme is applied, selectively locking onto the radar signal to reduce the processed signal space. A subsequent time domain spreading step is applied after converting the SIC-processed signal into the post-coded time domain, wherein the SI diminishes separately across the delay and Doppler axes. The proposed method demonstrates superior performance in terms of detection probability, target’s range and velocity root mean square error (RMSE), while maintaining high spectral efficiency and minimal computational complexity.

Introduction

At the advent of each generation of wireless communication networks, spectrum scarcity remains a critical factor shaping the associated technologies. The optimization of spectrum utilization and the effective management of interference are paramount considerations in the design of emerging wireless systems
[
1
]
. As the standardization of the sixth-generation (6G) network progresses, spectrum sharing has garnered significant attention, particularly within the framework of integrated sensing and communication. integrated sensing and communication aims to unify sensing and communication functionalities within a single network infrastructure, facilitating resource sharing, hardware unification, and energy efficiency
[
2
]
. A fundamental aspect of resource sharing in integrated sensing and communication systems is the joint utilization of spectral resources for both radar and communication operations. Despite the migration to higher frequency bands that offer increased bandwidth availability, the exponential growth in connectivity demands and capacity optimization requirements necessitate efficient resource allocation strategies. Furthermore, the coexistence of data transmission and environmental sensing imposes rigorous design constraints on waveform selection as the performance requirements differ substantially
[
3
]
. Consequently, there is an increasing preference toward designing waveforms capable of supporting both functionalities without a major performance compromise.

A feasible approach to achieve integrated sensing and communication is the implementation of full duplex systems, which provide concurrent transmission and reception of sensing and communication signals
[
4
,
5
]
. full duplex-integrated sensing and communication systems have numerous benefits, such as improved spectrum efficiency, reduced latency, and efficient resource allocation. However, a major challenge associated with FD operation is the presence of powerful self interference, which arises when the communication node transmits data frames while the radar simultaneously receives target echoes. This interference drastically diminishes the quality of the radar signal in monostatic systems. From a standardization perspective, FD operation is being explored within the context of sub-band FD (SBFD), which facilitates concurrent transmission and reception within different sub-bands of the same frequency band. In contrast, in-band FD (IBFD) utilizes the entire band for dual operations, necessitating self interference cancellation (SIC) to levels below the receiver’s noise floor. Consequently, extensive research efforts have been devoted to SIC and mitigation techniques for IBFD systems
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Mainly, SIC techniques in IBFD systems are classified into passive and active approaches. Passive SIC relies on electromagnetic characteristics to separate the transmit and receive chains, thereby attenuating self interference prior to reception. This includes transmit and receive beamforming, cross-polarization, and physical antenna separation
[
10
]
. For instance, in
[
11
]
, a joint design of transmit and receive beamformers was proposed to simultaneously maximize radar beampattern power at the target while suppressing residual SI through a penalty-based iterative optimization algorithm. Similarly,
[
12
]
presented a joint transmit and receive beamforming framework for FD-non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) ISAC systems, where the optimization aimed to maximize the sensing signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio for single and multi-target detection under both perfect and imperfect channel state information. Moving to mmWave frequencies,
[
13
]
proposed an integrated optimization approach for designing analog and digital beamformers in conjunction with SIC within a unified waveform framework. Additionally, cross-polarized antennas can electromagnetically isolate transmit and receive antennas, thereby reducing self interference
[
14
]
. Nonetheless, although passive SIC techniques provide substantial SI suppression, they also attenuate desired signals and result in a residual SI component. Moreover, for compact monostatic ISAC systems, antenna separation is often infeasible due to spatial constraints especially at high frequencies.

Conversely, active SIC techniques operate by removing a replica of the transmitted signal from the received signal. When this subtraction occurs before analog-to-digital conversion (ADC), it is referred to as analog active SIC. However, since the received signal undergoes distortions due to the nonlinearities of RF components, residual SI persists after analog SIC and must be further suppressed in the digital domain. A frequency-domain SIC approach for orthogonal frequency division multiplexing systems was proposed in
[
9
]
, utilizing near-optimal interference-canceling filter weights to track channel variations and reconstruct the self interference. Likewise,
[
15
]
introduced a frequency-domain differential interference cancellation (F-DIC) strategy for IBFD-ISAC systems employing orthogonal frequency division multiplexing, leveraging the invariant characteristics of interference. Following the digital domain SIC,
[
16
]
explores a receiver structure in which the received signal is non-uniformly sampled at the zero-crossing instants of the SI signal to relax the constraints of dynamic range of ADC. Furthermore,
[
17
]
investigated nonlinear SIC by formulating an optimization problem to minimize estimation uncertainty under practical power limitations. Multi-tap RF cancellers
[
18
]
, further exploit the multipath nature of the self interference channel by aligning RF cancellation taps with the actual channel taps, though this approach introduces additional complexity, particularly when the estimated and actual channel taps are misaligned. Collectively, realistic considerations suggest that no solitary SIC approach; whether passive or active, analog or digital can fully mitigate self interference in IBFD-ISAC systems. Instead, a hybrid SIC framework integrating multiple cancellation techniques across different domains is necessary for effective self interference suppression
[
19
]
.

Regarding waveform design for ISAC, the quest for a singular waveform for both sensing and communication in ISAC has garnered considerable attention
[
20
]
. Multicarrier waveforms, including OFDM, orthogonal time-frequency space, and affine frequency division multiplexing, exhibit adaptability to diverse channel conditions, offering design flexibility and performance improvements in target detection and communication throughput
[
21
]
. Additionally, flexible resource allocation enables the assignment of specific temporal and spatial blocks for radar and communication functions, extending across several domains, including the delay-Doppler and affine domains
[
22
]
, providing an improved interpretation of channel reflections in those domains. While joint integrated sensing and communication waveform design remains a viable approach
[
23
,
24
]
, alternative methods advocate for the transmission of distinct waveforms tailored to each functionality. Specifically, chirp or pulse-like waveforms are often employed for target detection to enhance performance and minimize leakage and interference between sensing pulses and communication signals due to power disparities
[
25
,
26
]
. However, generating separate waveforms introduces additional complexity, as distinct transmission and reception chains are required for independent signal processing.

In response to these challenges, this paper proposes a novel waveform-domain SIC technique for IBFD-ISAC systems. The proposed scheme leverages the generalized modulation and demodulation framework of affine frequency division multiplexing to generate a chirp-like signal for sensing and an OFDM-based frame for communication
[
27
]
. By exploiting the flexibility of the affine frequency division multiplexing structure, different waveform configurations can be generated through post- and pre-processing phases of the orthogonal frequency division multiplexing demodulator. Specifically, after analog SIC, the residual self interference-affected orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signal is projected onto the affine domain, where it exhibits a noise-like spreading behavior, whereas the affine frequency division multiplexing radar signal remains more localized. This property is exploited for self interference suppression by applying an iterative windowing algorithm to mitigate residual self interference, yielding an enhanced radar signal. Furthermore, an additional spreading process is applied in the time domain after converting the SIC-processed affine frequency division multiplexing signal to the post-coded time domain, thereby reducing phase variations along the delay and Doppler axes
[
28
]
. This two-stage waveform-domain SIC approach provides an additional separation mechanism that facilitates signal purification within their respective multiplexing domains, significantly improving self interference suppression while maintaining a compact signal generation and processing chain. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt to perform SIC using the projections in waveform-domains. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

Based on the generalized generation framework of multicarrier waveforms, we proposed an IBFD-integrated sensing and communication system that interchangeably utilizes orthogonal frequency division multiplexing and affine frequency division multiplexing waveforms for communication and sensing, respectively, by tuning different design parameters and resource elements.

•

We analytically characterize the statistical behavior of any wide sense stationary time domain signals in the affine domain and demonstrate that under specific conditions, orthogonal frequency division multiplexing exhibits additive white Gaussian noise properties in this domain. Hence, at certain segments of the received radar signal, the self interference and the thermal receiver noise can be jointly approximated as additive white Gaussian noise with constant power levels.

•

Capitalizing on this statistical property, the self interference originated from orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signal can be reconstructed and subtracted in the affine domain where its has a predictive behavior, followed by an iterative windowing scheme for residual SI suppression. The proposed windowing algorithm drastically reduces the processed frame size where it locks on the least and maximum indexed target bins. Then the SIC-processed signal is taken to the time domain to perform an additional spreading phase, where any phase or amplitude variation is spreaded in post-coded time domain before performing target detection.

•

We provide a comprehensive performance analysis of the proposed IBFD-ISAC system, evaluating its signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio, probability of detection, computational complexity, and spectral efficiency under varying radar and communication configurations.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section II presents the waveform design for the monostatic IBFD-ISAC system along with the received signal model. Section III discusses the affine-domain representation of orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signals. Following that, the proposed waveform-domain SIC is presented along with the iterative windowing algorithm and the performance analysis in Section IV. Simulation results and the conclusion are given in Section V and VI, respectively.

II
The Proposed System Model

In this section, the system model of the proposed monostatic IBfull duplex-integrated sensing and communication transceiver is detailed. The objective is to design and transmit orthogonal and distinct waveforms for sensing and communication while mitigating the self interference superimposed on the targets echos at the radar receiver, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. Unlike the continuous wave radar, the proposed transmission scheme follows the pulsed radar operation mode where the short duration sensing signal is transmitted periodically in each pulse repetition interval while the communication signal is transmitted in the remaining duration coinciding with the silent period of the radar.
This separate design of sensing and communication waveforms, while reducing the achievable spectral efficiency, can transmit different waveforms using the same generation block for the following reasons:

•

Random communication signals has less focused ambiguity function leading to reduced matched filtering performance, and lower detection probability at low SNR. Moreover, high-order modulation and number of subcarriers create non-constant envelopes with high PAPR, limiting the amplification efficiency.

•

Alternatively, the design AFDM sensing signal benefits from deterministic and constant-envelope structure which allows efficient power amplification and sharper detection. Unlike communication signals, it is optimized for power allocation, modulation, and spectral shaping.

•

A guard interval is appended to reduce the inter-frame leakage due the phase discontinuity between AFDM and OFDM signals. Note that this guard is very small and is not required for the interference rejection.

•

This separated design is then used to perform SIC using the different waveform domain properties, rather than the classical power-domain cancellation.

The following subsection describes the transmission frames.

II-B

Received Signal Model

Before providing the received signal components, the following assumptions about the proposed IBfull duplex-integrated sensing and communication system are given

•

The space domain isolation between the transmitting and receiving antennas is assumed to be within an acceptable level of the ADC’s dynamic range, and before passing by the low-power amplifier in order to avoid saturation.

•

The objects of interests for radar processing are considered to be moving targets with a measurable Doppler shift, whereas the communication reflected signal has a negligible Doppler shift.

•

By setting an appropriate cancellation range of the transversal filter, close-range echoes’ delays are effectively eliminated. However, self interference and multipath interference cannot be suppressed due to power difference and the maximum cancellation range constraints, respectively.

•

The transmitting and receiving chains are assumed to be perfectly synchronized, and the carrier frequency offset is presumed to be compensated at the radar receiving end.

•

The maximum round-trip delay is assumed to be shorter than the pulse repetition interval, such that the target echoes arrive strictly before the next PRI, and thus avoiding range ambiguity.

, (c) AFDM with OFDM signal for
N_{r}<N_{c}
, (d) AFDM with OFDM signal for
N_{r}>N_{c}
, (e) AFDM with OFDM signal after passing by a two-tap channel, (f) AFDM with OFDM signal after iterative windowing.

Consider a time domain wide sense stationary signal
r[n]
defined over discrete indices
n=0,\cdots,N-1
, characterized by a constant expected value given by
\mathbb{E}\left\{r[n]\right\}=\mu_{r}^{t}
and an autocorrelation function expressed as

\mathbb{E}\left\{r[n]r^{*}[\dot{n}]\right\}=R_{rr}^{t}[n_{\delta}]
, where
n_{\delta}=n-\dot{n}
denotes the lag between two discrete time indices
n
and
\dot{n}
, respectively. To observe the statistical behavior of this signal in the affine domain, the expectation and autocorrelation function are calculated for the signal vector
\mathbf{r}
after applying an
N
-points DAFT such as
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{r}
. Then, the expectation function of a random process in the affine domain is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{r}^{a}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r[n]e^{-j2\pi(c_{1}n^{2}+\frac{nm}{N}+c_{2}m^{2})}\right\}

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left\{r[n]\right\}e^{-j2\pi(c_{1}n^{2}+\frac{nm}{N}+c_{2}m^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\mu_{r}^{t}e^{-j2\pi c_{2}m^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{-j2\pi(c_{1}n^{2}+\frac{nm}{N})}.

In addition to the phase perturbation term
c_{2}m^{2}
, the expectation function varies across the affine-domain. When selecting
c_{1}\ll 1
with
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the quadratic phase term becomes negligible, i.e.,
c_{1}n^{2}\approx 0
. Under this condition, the summation can be approximated by a DFT kernel, given by

\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{-j2\pi\frac{nm}{N}}=N\delta(m)
. Thus, the expression can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mu_{r}^{a}=\sqrt{N}\mu_{r}^{t}e^{-j2\pi c_{2}m^{2}}\delta(m)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{N}\mu_{r}^{t},\quad m=0\\
0,\quad\quad\quad m\neq 0\end{cases}.

(19)

Since
\mu_{r}^{a}
is defined piecewise with respect to
m
, it follows that the expectation function of a WSS signal is not uniform across the affine domain unless the mean of the time-domain signal satisfies
\mu_{r}^{t}=0
. Consequently, for a zero-mean time-domain signal, the corresponding mean in the affine domain remains constant and equals zero. Similarly, the autocorrelation can be calculated as

\displaystyle R_{rr}^{a}=\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\Bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r[n]e^{-j2\pi\left(c_{1}n^{2}+\frac{nm}{N}+c_{2}m^{2}\right)}\Bigg)

(20)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\dot{n}=0}^{N-1}r^{*}[\dot{n}]e^{j2\pi\left(c_{1}\dot{n}^{2}+\frac{\dot{n}\dot{m}}{N}+c_{2}\dot{m}^{2}\right)}\Bigg)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\dot{n}=0}^{N-1}e^{-j2\pi\Big[c_{1}(n^{2}-\dot{n}^{2})+\frac{(nm-\dot{n}\dot{m})}{N}+c_{2}(m^{2}-\dot{m}^{2})\Big]}

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{E}\left\{r[n]r^{*}[\dot{n}]\right\},

for
\mathbb{E}\left\{r[n]r^{*}[\dot{n}]\right\}=R_{rr}^{t}[n-\dot{n}]
, we
can
write

\displaystyle R_{rr}^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\dot{n}=0}^{N-1}e^{-j2\pi\Big[c_{1}(n^{2}-\dot{n}^{2})+\frac{(nm-\dot{n}\dot{m})}{N}+c_{2}(m^{2}-\dot{m}^{2})\Big]}

(21)

\displaystyle\times R_{rr}^{t}[n-\dot{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\dot{n}=0}^{N-1}e^{j2\pi(c_{1}\dot{n}^{2}+\frac{\dot{n}\dot{m}}{N}+c_{2}\dot{m}^{2})}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}R_{rr}^{t}[n-\dot{n}]

\displaystyle\times e^{-j2\pi(c_{1}n^{2}+\frac{nm}{N}+c_{2}m^{2})}.

It can be seen that
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}R_{rr}^{t}[n-\dot{n}]e^{-j2\pi(c_{1}n^{2}+\frac{nm}{N}+c_{2}m^{2})}
is the affine transformation of
R_{rr}^{t}[n-\dot{n}]
. According to (
14
), a shift in time is equivalent to a scaled shift in the affine domain such that
\Lambda R_{rr}^{t}[n-\dot{n}]\overset{\text{affine}}{\underset{\text{time}}{\rightleftharpoons}}R_{rr}^{a}[m-2Nc_{1}\dot{n}]
. Therefore, (
21
) can be be written as

\displaystyle R_{rr}^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\dot{n}=0}^{N-1}R_{rr}^{a}[m-2Nc_{1}\dot{n}]e^{j2\pi(c_{1}\dot{n}^{2}+\frac{\dot{n}\dot{m}}{N}+c_{2}\dot{m}^{2})}.

(22)

Before applying the IDAFT, it is important to observe that
R_{rr}^{t}[m-2Nc_{1}\dot{n}]
undergoes both scaling and reversal with respect to the summation variable
\dot{n}
. This necessitates an examination of the transformation properties of the matrix
\mathbf{\Lambda}
. Notably, since
\mathbf{\Lambda}
is composed of two diagonal circulant matrices, the reversal of time-domain samples results in a corresponding reversal in the affine domain, and vice versa
[
29
]
. Mathematically this circular shift property can be described as:

\displaystyle r[m]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Lambda(n,m)s[n]\Longleftrightarrow r[-m]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Lambda(n,m)s[-n].

(23)

Second, although the DAFT is a subset of the linear canonical transforms (LCTs)
[
30
]
, its scaling invariance property does not hold for arbitrary scaling factors. However, since
c_{1}
is typically chosen as
c_{1}=\frac{k}{2N}
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the term
2Nc_{1}
remains an integer, thereby preserving periodicity and preventing any additional phase distortion. Then for
\mathbf{\Lambda}r[n]=s[m]
, the scaling the time-domain signal by
2Nc_{1}
gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Lambda}r[2Nc_{1}n]=s[2Nc_{1}m].

(24)

Hence the autocorrelation function in (
22
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle R_{rr}^{a}[m,\dot{m}]=R_{rr}^{t}[2Nc_{1}(m-\dot{m})]=R_{rr}^{t}[2Nc_{1}m_{\delta}].

(25)

Consequently, the autocorrelation function in the affine domain is a function of the indices difference
m_{\delta}=m-\dot{m}
. Additionally, as the variance can be calculated from the autocorrelation function for a wide sense stationary signal with
\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{r}\right\}=\mu_{r}^{a}=0
, such as

\displaystyle VAR\left\{r[n]\right\}=R_{rr}^{a}[0],

(26)

which is constant across the affine domain. As illustrated in Fig.
2
(a), for a time-domain additive white Gaussian noise signal with zero mean, the corresponding signal in the affine domain also exhibits AWGN characteristics. Based on this observation, we formally state Theorem 1 for wide sense stationary signals.

Theorem 1.

For a time-domain wide sense stationary signal
r[n]
, its representation in the affine domain remains wide sense stationary, provided that its mean satisfies
\mu_{r}^{t}=0
. Furthermore, it preserves AWGN characteristics, following the distribution
\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,R_{rr}^{a}[0]\right)
.

Note that the previous theorem holds when
2Nc_{1}
is an integer and as long as the mean of the time-domain signal is zero. Next, we expand this theorem to a general orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signal.

III-B

OFDM in Affine-Domain

Consider the orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signal
\mathbf{F}^{H}\mathbf{x}
transformed to the affine domain using
N
-points DAFT such as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{c_{1}}\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{c_{2}}\mathbf{F}^{H}\mathbf{x},

(27)

where
\mathbf{x}=\left[x_{0},\cdots,x_{N}\right]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
are drawn from a set of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random QAM symbols. For
N
asymptotically large, the real and imaginary parts of
\mathbf{x}
follow a gaussian distribution with zero mean
[
31
]
. Then, when a Gaussian coding code-book is applied we can have
\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbf{x}\right\}=0
. Given that, the covariance matrix of
\mathbf{y}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{y}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}^{H}\right\}=\mathbb{E}\left\{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{F}^{H}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{H}\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{H}\right\}

(28)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{F}^{H}\mathbf{C}_{x}\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{H},

with
\mathbf{C}_{x}=\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{H}\right\}
given as

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{x}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathbb{E}\left\{|x_{0}|^{2}\right\}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\mathbb{E}\left\{|x_{1}|^{2}\right\}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathbb{E}\left\{|x_{N-1}|^{2}\right\}\end{array}\right].

(29)

Under the per-sample power constraint
[
32
]
, the transmitted symbols are assumed to have uniform power, such that
\mathbb{E}\left\{|x_{n}|^{2}\right\}=\varsigma\quad\forall n
. With this assumption, the covariance matrix in (
29
) simplifies to
\mathbf{C}_{x}=\varsigma\mathbf{I}
. Given that
\mathbf{\Lambda}
and
\mathbf{F}
are unitary matrices, it follows that
\mathbf{C}_{y}=\mathbf{C}_{x}=\varsigma\mathbf{I}
, which is also a diagonal matrix. Furthermore, given that the expectation of the generated symbols satisfies
\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbf{x}\right\}=0
, the expectation of the affine-domain representation becomes
\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbf{y}\right\}=\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{F}^{H}\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbf{x}\right\}=0
. This implies that an orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signal exhibits additive white Gaussian noise characteristics in the affine domain.

When the data symbols
x_{n}
exhibit a non-uniform power distribution, the covariance matrix
\mathbf{C}_{x}
no longer satisfies
\mathbf{C}_{x}=\varsigma\mathbf{I}
. As a result, the covariance matrix of the affine-domain signal,
\mathbf{C}_{y}
, is no longer strictly diagonal. However, the magnitudes of the off-diagonal elements remain significantly smaller compared to the diagonal entries, ensuring that the power distribution along the main diagonal of
\mathbf{C}_{y}
remains relatively uniform. Mathematically, this can be expressed as

\displaystyle\text{tr}(\mathbf{C}_{y})\approx\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left\{|x_{n}|^{2}\right\}=N\bar{\varsigma},

(30)

where
\bar{\varsigma}
represents the average power per sample. The observed diagonal dominance in the covariance matrix arises due to the quadratic phase terms
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{c_{\{1,2\}}}
, which introduce sufficient statistical randomization, provided that the refining coefficients
c_{\{1,2\}}
are appropriately chosen.
Typically,
c_{1}
is selected to adapt to the channel maximum Doppler shift, whereas
c_{2}
is set be a rational number sufficiently smaller than
\frac{1}{N}

[
27
]
. Therefore,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle c_{1}=\frac{k}{2N},\quad c_{2}=\frac{1}{N^{2}},\quad\text{where }k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(31)

This randomization effectively de-correlates the samples, thereby reducing the power of the off-diagonal elements. Moreover, as the number of samples
N
increases, the de-correlation effect becomes more pronounced, further reinforcing the approximate diagonal structure of
\mathbf{C}_{y}
. Based on this observation, the following theorem is presented for an orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signal.

Theorem 2.

An OFDM signal with i.i.d. data symbols and normalized power allocation satisfying
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left\{|x_{n}|^{2}\right\}=\bar{\varsigma}
, exhibits a uniform power distribution in the affine domain and behaves as an additive white Gaussian noise process, with
\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\bar{\varsigma}\right)
.

Figure
2
(b) illustrates the behavior of an orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signal (in red) within the affine domain, where the orthogonal frequency division multiplexing samples exhibit a noise-like scattering across the localized affine frequency division multiplexing carrier. When superimposed on the localized carrier, these scattered samples degrade its power level while preserving its structural representation. Furthermore, Fig.
2
(c) and (d) depict the power difference between the localized affine frequency division multiplexing pulse and the OFDM samples in the affine domain for different orthogonal frequency division multiplexing and affine frequency division multiplexing sizes. Specifically, for
N_{r}<N_{c}
, the resulting noisy samples possess power levels comparable to those of the affine frequency division multiplexing carrier sidelobes, leading to a degradation in the combined signal power. However, for
N_{r}>N_{c}
, the large-size DAFT disperses the orthogonal frequency division multiplexing samples widely across the affine domain, thereby significantly reducing the interference over the affine frequency division multiplexing carrier. This observation is consistent with the result in (
30
).

IV
Waveform-Domain SIC Algorithm

This section introduces the waveform-domain SIC algorithm designed for the proposed IBFD-integrated sensing and communication system. The proposed scheme incorporates noise suppression in the affine domain, iterative windowing, and time-domain spreading of the SIC-processed signal. Following this, the performance of the system is evaluated based on radar-specific performance metrics.

In the following iterations, the same peak detection and windowing process continues with a reduced threshold
\zeta_{iter_{u}}
, until the total number of detected peaks maintains throughout two consecutive iterations, such as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{u}}=\sum_{b=1}^{Q_{u}}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\mathbbm{1}_{m_{w_{u}}^{b}}\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{u-1}}\odot\mathbb{w}^{u}

(42)

\displaystyle\textit{ for},\quad\left|\left\{m^{b}_{iter_{u}}\right\}_{b=1}^{Q_{u}}\right|>\left|\left\{m^{b}_{iter_{u-1}}\right\}_{b=1}^{Q_{u-1}}\right|,u=1,\cdots,\rho.

The resulting signal after
\rho
iterations can be written as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=1}^{Q_{\rho}}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{i}(\mathbbm{1}_{m_{w_{\rho}}^{b}}\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbb{\Pi}^{l_{i}}\mathbb{\Delta}^{\kappa_{i}}\mathbb{s}_{j}^{r})\odot\mathbb{w}^{{\rho}}+\varepsilon_{\rho}\mathbb{w}_{j}^{{a,SI}_{\rho}}

(43)

\displaystyle\textit{ for,}\quad m^{1}_{iter_{1}}-\frac{N_{w}}{2}\leq m\leq m^{Q_{1}}_{iter_{1}}+\frac{N_{w}}{2},

where
m_{w_{\rho}}^{b}\in[m^{b}_{iter_{\rho}}-\frac{N_{w}}{2},m^{b}_{iter_{\rho}}+\frac{N_{w}}{2}]
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{w}_{j}^{{a,SI}_{\rho}}=(\mathbbm{1}_{m_{w_{\rho}}^{b}}\varepsilon\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbb{s}_{j}^{c}+\mathbbm{1}_{m_{w_{\rho}}^{b}}\mathbb{w}_{j}^{a})\odot\mathbb{w}^{{\rho}}.

(44)

Here,
\varepsilon_{\rho}
represents the magnitude of the residual SI after successive windowing. It is important to note that
\mathbb{w}_{j}^{{a,SI}_{\rho}}
remains a zero-mean additive white Gaussian noise with its power level directly proportional to the residual SI magnitude

IV-B

Time-Domain Spreading

After performing the iterative windowing, the range and velocity of the targets can be extracted from the resulting signal
\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}
in the time domain. Specifically, the coherent processing interval matrix is constructed by accumulating the signals
\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}
from each pulse repetition interval over a total of
J
pulse repetition intervals. This results in the matrix
\bar{\mathbb{Y}}=[\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{1}^{w_{\rho}},\cdots,\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}},\cdots,\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{J}^{w_{\rho}}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}\times J}
, where
N_{s}=N_{w}+m^{Q_{1}}_{iter_{1}}-m^{1}_{iter_{1}}
. Subsequently, the matrix
\bar{\mathbb{Y}}
is transformed into the time domain by applying an IDAFT to each
\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}
, yielding
\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}=\mathbb{\Lambda}^{H}\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}
. This results in the time-domain SI-free matrix
\bar{\mathbb{R}}=[\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{1}^{w_{\rho}},\cdots,\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}},\cdots,\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{J}^{w_{\rho}}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}\times J}
. However, rather than processing the entire matrix
\bar{\mathbb{R}}
as in conventional radar CPI processing, the target information can be extracted directly from each vector
\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}
by leveraging the chirp-carrier behavior in the PCTD, as detailed in
[
28
]
. In this domain, the delay and Doppler components become decoupled and manifest as distinct shifts. This property holds even though the Doppler phase term
e^{j2\pi\nu t}
diminishes for
\nu T_{r}^{tot}\ll 1
due to Doppler spreading in the PCTD. Consequently, Doppler estimation can be performed without requiring the coherent integration gain typically associated with the slow-time processing of the CPI. Following that, for Doppler estimation, the vector
\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}
is reshaped and zero-padded such as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}},\quad\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}},

(45)

where
\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,k}}
is the Doppler zero-padded reshaped matrix, for
\sqrt{N_{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
being even, it can be written as

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}[p,a]=\begin{cases}\bar{R}_{j}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}[p,a],\text{ for}\quad 0\leq p,a\leq\sqrt{N_{s}}\\
0,\text{ for}\quad\sqrt{N_{s}}<p,a\leq N_{z_{p}}\end{cases},

(46)

where
N_{zp}=z_{p}\sqrt{N_{s}}
and
z_{p}
is the zero-padding factor. Applying IDFT over the columns of
\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}
gives the PCTD representation of the signal, given by
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,k}}=\mathbb{F}^{H}\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}
. Note that the zero-padding will not only enable Doppler detection within a single pulse repetition interval, but also spreads the residual self interference across the matrix
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}
thereby further reducing its effect on target detection. For range detection, the delay shift in the time domain is transformed into phase variations by applying a DFT matrix to the vector
\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}
such as
\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho},l}=\mathbb{F}\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}
. Subsequently, the same process of zero-padding and IDFT spreading is applied, leading to

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho,l}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j}^{w_{\rho,l}},\quad\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j}^{w_{\rho,l}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,l}}.

(47)

Performing IDFT gives
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,l}}=\mathbb{F}^{H}\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,l}}
. The resulting PCTD representation of the Doppler and delay matrices
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}
and
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,l}}
formulas are given in (

x_{r}^{p}
represents the pilots carriers for each affine frequency division multiplexing symbol.

contain the decoupled delay and Doppler shifts, for the Doppler matrix
P_{j,zp}^{w_{\rho,\kappa}}

IV-C

Performance Analysis

In this subsection, the main performance metrics of the proposed IBFD-integrated sensing and communication system are analyzed.

IV-C
1
SINR

The explicit expression of the SIC signal in time-domain before converting to the PCTD can be given by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{i}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{l_{i}}\mathbf{\Delta}^{\kappa_{i}}\mathbf{s}_{j}^{r}+\varepsilon_{\rho}\varepsilon\mathbf{s}_{j}^{c}+\varepsilon_{\rho}\mathbf{w}_{j}\right)\odot\mathbf{w}^{\rho}.

(54)

Consider
P_{c}
and
P_{r}
to be the total communication and radar power, respectively. Then for a normalized power over a symbol duration, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[|\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\overbrace{\left[\left|\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{i}\sqrt{P_{r}T_{r}^{\eta}}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{l_{i}}\mathbf{\Delta}^{\kappa_{i}}\mathbf{s}_{j}^{r}\odot\mathbf{w}^{\rho}\right|^{2}\right]}^{\gamma_{j}^{r}}

(55)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\underbrace{\left[\left|\sqrt{P_{c}T_{c}^{\eta}}\varepsilon_{\rho}\varepsilon\mathbf{s}_{j}^{c}\odot\mathbf{w}^{\rho}\right|^{2}\right]}_{\gamma_{j}^{SI}}+\mathbb{E}\underbrace{\left[\left|\varepsilon_{\rho}\mathbf{w}_{j}\odot\mathbf{w}^{\rho}\right|^{2}\right]}_{\gamma_{j}^{n}},

where
\gamma_{j}^{r}
,
\gamma_{j}^{SI}
and
\gamma_{j}^{n}
denote the radar signal power, the residual self interference and noise powers after SIC, respectively. Simplifying
\gamma_{j}^{r}
gives

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left|\hat{\alpha}_{i}\right|^{2}P_{r}T_{r}^{\eta}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{s}-1}|s_{j}^{r}[n]|^{2}|w^{\rho}[n]|^{2}.

(56)

The samples
|w^{\rho}[n]|^{2}
decay at each windowing iteration
u
, where
|w^{u}[n]|<1\quad\forall n
, hence
C_{\rho}=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{s}-1}|w^{\rho}[n]|^{2}<1
. Moreover, for large
N_{s}
,
\sum_{n=0}^{N_{s}-1}|s_{j}^{r}[n]|^{2}\approx N_{s}
as the samples of
s_{j}^{r}[n]
are also uncorrelated i.i.d normalized random QAM symbols, then

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left|\hat{\alpha}_{i}\right|^{2}N_{s}P_{r}T_{r}^{\eta}C_{\rho}.

(57)

Similarly, simplifying
\gamma_{j}^{c}
and
\gamma_{j}^{n}
gives

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{SI}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{\rho}\varepsilon)^{2}N_{s}P_{c}T_{c}^{\eta}C_{\rho},

(58a)

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{n}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\rho}^{2}N_{0}C_{\rho},

(58b)

where
N_{0}
is the noise power. Then, the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio for the
j
-th pulse repetition interval can be expressed as

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{\text{SINR}}=\frac{\gamma_{j}^{r}}{\gamma_{j}^{SI}+\gamma_{j}^{n}}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rho}^{2}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left|\hat{\alpha}_{i}\right|^{2}N_{s}N_{r}P_{r}}{\varepsilon^{2}N_{s}N_{c}P_{c}+N_{0}B}.

(59)

From (
59
), the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio of each pulse repetition interval depends on the residual windowing suppression factor
\varepsilon_{\rho}^{2}
. As a result, increasing the number of windowing iterations enhances
\gamma_{j}^{\text{SINR}}
by gradually reducing the residual SI. However, this improvement comes at the cost of increased computational complexity, as a higher number of iterations
\rho
requires additional processing steps.

IV-C
2
Probability of Detection

Using a binary hypothesis testing problem formulation, it can be written

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}:\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}|_{n_{i}}=\bar{S}+\bar{I},\quad\mathcal{H}_{0}:\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}|_{n_{i}}=\bar{I},

(60)

with,

\displaystyle\bar{I}

\displaystyle=\left((\varepsilon_{\rho}\varepsilon\mathbf{s}_{j}^{c}+\varepsilon_{\rho}\mathbf{w}_{j})\odot\mathbf{w}^{\rho}\right)_{n_{i}},

(61a)

\displaystyle\bar{S}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L}\hat{\alpha}_{i}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{l_{i}}\mathbf{\Delta}^{\kappa_{i}}\mathbf{s}_{j}^{r}\odot\mathbf{w}^{\rho}\right)_{n_{i}},

(61b)

where
\bar{\mathbb{r}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}|_{n_{i}}
,
\bar{S}
and
\bar{I}
denote the post SIC signal, the target signal, and the combined residual self interference plus noise value at the target bin
n_{i}\in\left\{n_{1},\cdots,n_{L}\right\}
, respectively. Using a threshold detector, we can write

\displaystyle z=|\bar{r}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}[n_{i}]|\underset{\mathcal{H}_{0}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{H}_{1}}}{{\gtrless}}}\zeta.

(62)

Since
\bar{I}
is additive white Gaussian noise with
\bar{I}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\sigma_{\bar{I}}\right)
, then for the hypothesis
\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
z
is Rayleigh distributed and its probability density function (PDF) is given by

\displaystyle p\left(z\mid\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)=\frac{2z}{\sigma_{\bar{I}}^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{\sigma_{\bar{I}}^{2}}\right),\quad z>0,

(63)

with the probability of false alarm given as

\displaystyle P_{FA}=\int_{\zeta}^{+\infty}p\left(z\mid\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)\mathrm{d}z=\exp\left(-\frac{\zeta}{\sigma_{\bar{I}}^{2}}\right).

(64)

Therefore, for
P_{FA}
being a constant, the threshold can be be written as
\zeta=\sigma_{\bar{I}}\sqrt{-\text{ln}P_{FA}}
. For the hypothesis
\mathcal{H}_{1}
corresponding to a target,
z
follows a Rician distribution with a PDF

\displaystyle p\left(z\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}\right)=\frac{2z}{\sigma_{\bar{I}}^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}+P_{\bar{S}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\bar{I}}^{2}}\right)I_{0}\left(\frac{2zP_{\bar{S}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\bar{I}}}\right),\quad z>0

(65)

where
P_{\bar{S}}^{2}=\left|\hat{\alpha}\right|^{2}N_{s}P_{r}T_{r}^{\eta}C_{\rho}
, and
I_{0}(\cdot)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind. Then, the probability of detection is given as

\displaystyle P_{D}=\int_{\zeta}^{+\infty}p_{z}\left(z\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}\right)\mathrm{d}z=Q_{M}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2P_{\bar{S}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\bar{I}}^{2}}},\sqrt{\frac{2\zeta^{2}}{\sigma_{\bar{I}}^{2}}}\right),

(66)

where
Q_{M}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the first-order Marcum
Q
-function. By observing (
59
) and (
64
), it is evident that for the
j
-th pulse repetition interval the probability of detection simplifies to

\displaystyle P_{D}

\displaystyle=Q_{M}\left(\sqrt{2\gamma_{j}^{\text{SINR}}},\sqrt{-2\text{ln}P_{FA}}\right).

(67)

In this part, the interest is to analyze the complexity of the proposed waveform-domain SIC approach in terms of the mathematical complex multiplications for each of the following steps:

Affine-domain conversion:
After receiving the echo signal, it is transformed into the affine domain. This transformation involves diagonal matrices
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{c_{1}}\text{ and }\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{c_{2}}

N_{c,g}=N^{tot}_{c}+2N_{g}

N_{c,g}

, becomes negligible compared to the DFT complexity. Hence, the overall computational complexity simplifies to
\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathcal{O}(N_{c,g}\text{log}_{2}(N_{c,g}))
.

Iterative windowing:
The first step defines
Q_{1}
indices where an element-wise multiplication windowing process of size
N_{w}
is applied, leading to
\mathcal{O}(N_{w}Q_{1})
. Subsequently, for each of the next iterations

N_{r}^{tot}

Q_{u}

\mathcal{O}_{2}=\mathcal{O}(N_{r}^{tot}\sum_{u=2}^{\rho}Q_{u})
.

\bar{\mathbb{y}}_{j}^{w_{\rho}}

\mathcal{O}(N_{s}\text{log}_{2}(N_{s}))

N_{zp}

N_{s}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{3}=2\mathcal{O}\left(N_{s}\text{log}_{2}(N_{s})\right)+2\mathcal{O}\left(N_{zp}\text{log}_{2}(N_{zp})\right).

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{tot}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(N_{c,g}\text{log}_{2}(N_{c,g}))+\mathcal{O}(N_{r}^{tot}\sum_{u=2}^{\rho}Q_{u})+\mathcal{O}(N_{w}Q_{1})

Spectral Efficiency

The proposed system primarily employs orthogonal frequency division multiplexing for data transmission, while adopting a similar transmission scheme for affine frequency division multiplexing at reduced data rates. Since the latter is used for sensing applications, the number of populated data carriers is lower compared to orthogonal frequency division multiplexing signal. Hence, depending on the desired sensing performance, the data rates of the affine frequency division multiplexing signal are dynamically adjusted. Specifically, to achieve higher signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio values, a greater number of high-power pilot carriers is allocated to each affine frequency division multiplexing symbol. VI
Conclusion

This work introduces a novel approach for SIC in IBFD-ISAC systems by leveraging waveform-domain projections, to provide an additional degree of freedom for SIC. Specifically, we propose an adaptive ISAC frame generation mechanism that alternates between OFDM for communication and AFDM for radar operations, utilizing the generalized multicarrier waveform generation framework. We analytically demonstrate that the OFDM waveform, which induces strong SI on the radar echo, exhibits a statistical behavior similar to AWGN in the affine domain, thereby facilitating its cancellation. To further mitigate residual SI, an adaptive iterative low-complexity windowing scheme is employed. Then, an additional time-domain SI spreading phase is introduced to minimize the impact of delay and Doppler variations. The proposed framework reduces the need to process large PRIs over the CPI matrix to a single PRI, by leveraging the shift-like behavior of the diminishing Doppler phase in the PCTD. Simulation results validate the effectiveness of the proposed scheme in detecting multiple targets under strong SI and low SINR levels, which is further enhanced by adaptively optimizing the windowing coefficients. By leveraging this adaptability, a high probability of detection is achieved even in the presence of significant residual SI.

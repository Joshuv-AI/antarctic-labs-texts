Effect of concentrated growth factors on early soft tissue healing and postoperative recovery after mesioangular impacted lower third-molar extraction: a randomised split-mouth clinical trial
Authors: Moatasem Alshtawi, Gamil Al-Madhagy, Khaldoun Darwich
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe purpose of this randomised, split-mouth clinical trial study is to investigate the effect of concentrated growth factors (CGF) on the healing of soft tissues and postoperative sequelae following the extraction of mesioangular impacted lower third molars in a split-mouth design.Materials and methodsTwenty-five patients, systematically healthy, aged between 18 and 35, were subjected to bilateral lower third molar extraction (Class II, division B) at the oral surgery clinics in Damascus University, Syria, from October 2023 to July 2025. CGF was applied to one extraction site (test), and the opposite side served as a control (no treatment). Randomization was achieved through sealed envelopes, and the patients, outcome assessor, and data analyst were blinded. The main outcome was soft tissue healing, which was assessed by the Landry healing score at the 7th postoperative day. Secondary outcomes investigated were pain, which was assessed by the Visual Analog Scale throughout the 7th postoperative days, facial swelling, evaluated by the tape method at baseline, day 3, and day 7, and trismus by measuring the inter-incisal distance at baseline, third, and seventh postoperative days. Bonferroni correction was applied for repeated postoperative comparisons.ResultsAt postoperative day 7, the CGF-treated sides exhibited superior healing compared with control sides (p = 0.008). Lower pain scores were observed in the test group during the first four postoperative days (unadjusted p < 0.05); however, these differences did not remain statistically significant after Bonferroni correction. (p > 0.05). Facial swelling was significantly lower in the CGF group on day 3 (p = 0.011) but not at day 7 (p = 0.247). No significant differences were found for trismus at any time-point (p = 0.260 & 0.615).ConclusionThe application of CGF to extraction sockets following surgical removal of mesioangular impacted mandibular third molars was associated with improved early soft tissue healing and reduced facial swelling at day 3. Although lower early postoperative pain scores were observed, these differences did not persist after multiplicity adjustment. No significant effects were observed for trismus or at later time points. CGF may represent a promising adjunctive local approach to support early postoperative recovery, particularly for enhancing soft tissue healing. Further research with larger samples and extended follow-up is recommended.Clinical trial registrationThailand Clinical Trial Registry (TCTR). Registration number (ID: TCTR20251102008). Introduction

Surgical removal of impacted teeth is one of the most performed operations in oral and maxillofacial surgery practice [1]. In the last decades, the prevalence of lower third molar impaction has increased, which frequently requires surgical removal with varying degrees of difficulty [2–3] with a subsequent increase in postoperative (postop) sequelae such as swelling, pain, and trismus [4].

Management of these complications is essential for the success of surgery and patient comfort. Hence, several procedural modalities and therapeutic agents have been applied (introduced, or proposed), such as the use of lasers and piezoelectric devices during surgery [5], and the local administration of steroidal and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), bromelain, aprotinin, low-level lasers, and the application of platelet–rich derivatives [6].

Autogenous growth factor concentrates found in platelets are considered one of the most effective tissue regenerative stimuli [7]. They contain high levels of growth factors, such as fibroblast growth factor, platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), transforming growth factor β1 and β2 (TGF β1, TGF-β2 respectively), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), and the insulin-like growth factor [8].

The third generation of platelet-rich growth factors, so-called Concentrated Growth Factors (CGF), carries more advantages over the second and first ones, as they have more concentrations of growth factors, CD34 + stem cells, and denser fibrin matrix. It was developed by Sacco in 2006 and showed promising results in both hard and soft tissue regenerations [9].

Many clinical trials conducted on CGF have been reported to be beneficial in improving ridge preservation [10], healing of soft tissues, and reducing the incidence of postop complications [4, 11]. Nevertheless, some studies indicated that CGF had no further improvements on postop swelling, pain, and trismus as concluded by Turol et al. [12], while a study conducted by Fang et al. found that CGF has no additional benefits either on swelling or trismus [13].

Recent systematic evidence suggests that the use of CGF following third molar surgery may improve soft tissue healing and reduce postop morbidity when compared with conventional management. However, the available randomized trials remain heterogeneous in design, outcome timing, and blinding strategies, highlighting the need for well-controlled split-mouth clinical studies focusing on early postoperative outcomes [14].

Based on these inconsistent findings, we decided to conduct a clinical trial to add more scientific evidence about the effects of CGF on the healing of soft tissues, swelling, pain, and mouth opening limitation following the surgical extraction of mesioangular impacted lower third molars.

Materials and methods

Study design

This was a split-mouth, randomized clinical trial (RCT) with a 1:1 allocation ratio and superiority framework. It was conducted at the clinics of the Oral Surgery Department, Faculty of Dentistry, Damascus University, between October 2023 and July 2025. The Institutional Review Board and Ethical Committee of Damascus University approved the study (No: DN-160925-517). The study was done in alignment with the guidelines of the Helsinki Declaration, and all participants provided written informed consent.

This clinical trial was retrospectively registered at the Thailand Clinical Trials Registry (ID: TCTR20251102008) on (02 November 2025). Furthermore, this trial was reported following the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guideline [15]. All primary and secondary outcomes were predefined before patient recruitment and data collection. Commenced. The retrospective registration did not influence outcome selection, data analysis, or reporting.

Patients who were referred to the outpatient clinic for lower impacted 3rd molar extraction were assessed clinically and radiographically by 2 assessors (M.A. & G.A.). The enrolled participants were checked for eligibility. Inclusion criteria included the following: 1- all patients must fit the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Class I Category. 2- presence of bilateral mesioangular impacted lower 3rd molars (Class II, Position B according to the Pell-Gregory impaction classification, and mesioangular impaction according to Winter classification) [16] as presented in panoramic radiographs. 3- age between 18 and 35. 4- cooperative patients who are willing to attend the follow-up visits. Patients with bad oral hygiene, smokers, or with blood disorders, or alcoholics, or those who had undergone chemo and\or radiotherapy in the last 2 years, or with localized infections at the extraction sites were excluded. Furthermore, women who are in their menstrual period at the time of surgery, or pregnant or lactating, were also excluded.

Simple randomisation was performed through the use of cards labelled “R” or “L” indicating the CGF side (test group side); these cards were masked within closed, opaque, and sequentially numbered envelopes. An independent research assistant prepared and numbered the envelopes. Patients drew one envelope immediately before surgery. After the patient’s selection, envelopes were opened by the surgeon. Patients didn’t know where the CGF had been placed, and the surgeon had no access to the randomisation list before the patient selection.

Each patient was scheduled for 2 appointments with a 3-week interval, in which the second appointment was always the control side. A fixed surgical sequence was adopted to ensure maintenance of patient blinding while minimizing unnecessary venipuncture. As CGF preparation requires venous blood collection, blood sampling was performed only during the first surgical appointment. During the second appointment, the clinical workflow was visually simulated CGF handling without actual application. This approach allowed preservation of patient blinding across both procedures, reduced patient discomfort associated with repeated blood withdrawal, and ensured ethical consistency throughout the study. All surgeries were performed by the same surgeon, and clinical parameters were assessed by another blind specialized observer (G.A).

Calculation of the sample size was done utilizing the G-Power software version 3.1.9.4 (Franz Faul, Universitat Kiel, Germany). With a sample size effect of 0.8 and a study power of 95%, 19 samples per group would be sufficient in identifying the statistical difference between the test and the control groups with a 0.05 significance level; it was also consistent with a similar previous study [10]. No interim analysis was planned or performed, and no stopping guidelines were defined. There were no changes to the trial protocol after commencement.

CGF Preparation

CGF preparation was done following the Sacco protocol [17]. In brief, Fresh venous blood was gathered from the patients into 2 sterile, anticoagulant-free, 9 ml, silica-coated, vacutainer tubes (Hebei Xinle Sci&Tech CO.,Ltd., Shijiazhuang, Hebei, China). They were then centrifuged at different speeds and times as follows: 2700 rounds per minute (RPM) for 2 min (min), 2400 RPM for 4 min, 2700 RPM for 4 min, and 3000 RPM for 3 min. At the end, three distinct layers can be distinguished within the tubes; the CGF is the middle one that can be held by sterile tweezers and cut by scissors. Two CGF fractions were obtained, one used immediately, while the second CGF fragment was cryopreserved for visual simulation during the subsequent appointment to maintain patient blinding and was not intended for biological application.

Surgical procedure

Patients were asked to rinse their mouth with chlorhexidine gluconate 0.12% (Korufresh, Dominapharma, Damascus, Syria) before surgery. Extraction was initiated by administering local anesthesia to the inferior alveolar nerve, lingual, and buccal nerves using lidocaine hydrochloride 2% 2 ml, with 1:80.000 adrenaline (New Stetic S.A. Guarne, Antioquia, Colombia). A full-thickness enveloped mucoperiosteal flap was then raised, bone was removed by round bur at low speeds when needed with constant irrigation by saline, and teeth were luxated and extracted. Extraction sockets were then curetted, contoured, and irrigated, followed by either immediate placement of CGF scaffolds in the test sides, and suturing with 0/4 nylon sutures (Ethilon®, ETHICON LLC, US), or just ensuring the blood clot is stabilized and suturing in the control group side (Fig. 1).

Written postop instructions were given to the patients along with a medical prescription containing Co-Amoxiclav 1 gram (Augmatex, Avenzor, Damascus, Syria) twice a day for 7 days, and Acetaminophen 500 mg (Unadol, Unipharma, Damascus, Syria) when needed. Follow-up sessions were scheduled on the 3rd and 7th days. Sutures were removed after one week.

Study variables

The primary variable was the CGF application, while the main outcome was soft tissue healing. It was evaluated using the healing index classified by Landry and his co-workers [18]. It stands on five parameters, which are the tissue color, bleeding on palpation, wound margin’s epithelization, granulation tissue, and suppuration, and assigns a score from 1, which refers to very poor, to 5, which indicates excellent healing. Assessment is done on the 7th postop day.

The secondary outcomes included pain, swelling, and trismus. Pain was assessed using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) from the first postop day to the 7th day. Patients were given the VAS chart (graded from zero, indicating no pain, to 10 which indicates sever pain) and were asked to record their scores. For the purpose of this study, postop day 1 represented the first 24-hour period following surgery, during which patients were instructed to record the highest pain intensity experienced after the effect of local anesthesia had subside.

Swelling was evaluated by a tape measure as described in previous study [11]. In brief, 5 fixed reproducible points have been determined on each side of the face; these are the tragus (T), the soft tissue pogonion (P), the corner of the mouth (C), the angle of the mandible (A), and the outer canthus of the eye (E). Two horizontal lines (T-C and T-P) and one vertical line (E-A) can then be used to measure the swelling in the preoperative (baseline), and postop days (3rd, and 7th days). The evaluation is based on the sum of these 3 lines, the Total Swelling Score (TSS) which represents the overall magnitude of facial swelling can then be calculated as the sum of the three individual differences, and the percent (%) swelling increase could be extracted as shown in the following equations:

Statistical tests

All statistical analyses were accomplished using the IBM SPSS software version 23.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive data were shown as mean with standard deviation and median, or as frequency and percentage when applicable. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for soft tissue healing, swelling, and pain (during the 5th, 6th, and 7th postop days) analyses, while the paired t-test was used for trismus and pain (at the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th postop days) analyses.

Postop pain (VAS) was recorded daily from postop day 1 through day 7. Within each treatment condition (CGF and control), repeated measurements across time were analysed using the Friedman test. When overall significance was observed, post-hoc pairwise comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Bonferroni-adjusted significance levels.

For between-treatment comparisons at each postop day, paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used according to normality assessment. Where appropriate, Bonferroni correction was applied to account for multiple testing across repeated time points.

Swelling and trismus were measured at baseline, postop day 3, and postop day 7. Within each treatment condition, repeated-measures across time where analysed using the Friedman test due to non-normal distribution and violation of Sphericity assumptions. When overall significance was detected, post-hoc pairwise comparisons were performed with Bonferroni adjustment.

For between-treatment comparisons, change-from-baseline scores were calculated for each side as follows:

A significance level of p < 0.05 was used, with Bonferroni correction applied where appropriate. The data analyst was blind to which side CGF was applied in this trial. All randomised patients were analysed according to the group to which they were originally allocated. The five patients who didn’t return for follow-up or final surgery were excluded from the analysis.

Results

A total of thirty patients fulfilling the inclusion criteria were enrolled in this study. However, 5 patients were excluded later as they didn’t return for evaluation (3 patients didn’t arrive for the postop 7th day, and two patients did not attend the 2nd appointment). Twenty-five patients (8 males and 17 females with a mean age of 21 ± 2.36 years) were analyzed in this study (Fig. 2; Table 1).

Friedman test for postop pain revealed a significant time effect within both the control (χ² = 122.20, p < 0.001) and CGF groups (χ² = 109.76, p < 0.001). Post-hoc analysis with Bonferroni adjustment confirmed a significant reduction in pain from postop day 1 to subsequent days in both groups. Discomfort in the CGF group was lower than that of the control group on the first 3 postop days (p-values = 0.016, 0.044, and 0.028, respectively) as shown by the VAS scores. However, after adjustment for multiple testing, these differences did not retain statistical significance. No differences were observed from postop day 4 onward (Tables 3 and 4; Fig. 4).

Analgesic consumption was compared between CGF and control sites for each postoperative day. Paired t-tests were used for Days 1 and 2 due to normal distribution, while the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was applied for Days 3–7. Unadjusted analyses showed statistically significant differences between groups during the first four postoperative days (Day 1: p = 0.025; Day 2: p = 0.039; Day 3: p = 0.043; Day 4: p = 0.023), whereas no significant differences were observed from Day 5 onward (p = 0.608, 0.102, and 0.083, respectively). However, after Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons (adjusted significance threshold p < 0.007), none of the intergroup differences remained statistically significant.

Regarding facial swelling, both groups demonstrated significant changes in swelling over time (p = 0.001, Friedman test). On postop day 3, the CGF group showed less swelling than the control group (p = 0.011), and this difference remained significant after Bonferroni adjustment. No statistical differences were observed at the 7th postop day (p = 0.247). (Tables 5 and 6, and Fig. 5)

Discussion

The management of postop sequelae following the surgical removal of mandibular third molars remains a significant concern in oral and maxillofacial surgery. The quest for effective interventions to enhance patient comfort and accelerate recovery is ongoing.

This split-mouth RCT evaluated the efficacy of CGF on improving postop outcomes. The main findings indicate that the application of CGF into the extraction sockets enhanced soft tissue healing and reduced facial swelling at the 3rd postop day. Lower early postop pain scores were observed in the CGF-treated sites; however, these differences did not remain statistically significant after adjustment for multiple comparisons. No significant effect was observed for trismus or for later postop time-points.

The primary outcome of this trial was the soft tissue healing assessed using the Landry healing index. Our results demonstrated a better healing score in CGF-treated sites compared to the control ones. This effect may be explained by the biological properties of CGF. The thicker, three-dimensional fibrin matrix of CGF acts as a scaffold that facilitates the migration and proliferation of fibroblasts and keratinocytes, which are crucial for wound epithelialization and connective tissue formation [9].

CGF serves as a natural reservoir for a sustained release of key growth factors such as the TGF-β, VEGF, and PDGF, and cytokines, which are essential in organizing the inflammatory and proliferative phases of healing by promoting angiogenesis, collagen synthesis, and cellular differentiation [19–20]. Our findings were in alignment with several previous studies [11, 21, 19, 6].

Regarding postop discomfort, lower early pain scores were observed in the CGF group during the peak inflammatory period. The CGF membrane may act as a physical barrier that protects the underlying bone and nerve endings and stabilizes the blood clot, thereby reducing the incidence of localized inflammation that triggers pain [22].

The growth factors within the CGF (particularly TGF-β) possess immunomodulatory properties that may modulate the early inflammatory phase, which could partially explain the observed trend toward lower pain perciption [23]. Our findings were consistent with many previous studies [6, 19, 11, 21].

There was an early reduction in facial swelling in the CGF group, despite the absence of significant improvement in MMO. This early reduction in swelling may support the proposed anti-inflammatory effect of CGF on acute inflammation and edema formation. The CGF’s fibrin matrix, along with its growth factors, may lead to faster vascular stabilization and less extravasation of inflammatory fluid [24].

By the 7th postop day, no statistically significant difference was detected between groups, suggesting by that time, both groups converged into similar levels of resolution, thereby negating the early advantages. This finding aligns with the study conducted by Ozgul et al. [25], however, a study conducted by Gülşen and Senturk concluded that PRF does not affect pain and edema following mandibular third molar surgery [26].

These findings partially aligned with a recent systematic review reporting favorable effects of CGF on early healing parameters following third molar extractions, particularly with soft tissue recovery. Nevertheless, the review emphasized substantial variability in study protocols and outcome assessment time points, highlighting the importance of methodological standardization and careful interpretation of early healing outcomes [14].

Asutay et al. reported that platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) did not demonstrate significant effects on postop swelling, pain, and trismus following lower 3rd molar surgery [27]. Torul et al. found that CGF and advanced platelet-rich fibrin (A-PRF) did not demonstrate significant effects on postop pain, edema, and trismus following lower 3rd molar surgery [12].

The absence of significant effect on trismus may be related to the fact that trismus is more closely related to surgical trauma (bone removal, muscle manipulation, and flap extension) rather than local socket biomaterial augmentation [27–28]. As both sides underwent the same surgical protocol in this split-mouth design, the influence of CGF on muscular or joint limitation appeared limited. This finding is consistent with some studies reporting inconsistent or no benefit of CGF or PRF on trismus [29, 12, 30, 27].

It should be acknowledged that although treatment allocation to side was randomised, a fixed surgical sequence was adopted, whereby the second appointment corresponded to the control side. while this approach minimized unnecessary repeated venipuncture and preserved procedural consistency, a potential period effect cannot be entirely excluded, particularly for subjective outcomes such as pain perception.

The limitations of this study include the retrospective registration of the clinical trial, which does not fully align with international recommendations for prospective registration. In addition, the relatively small sample size and short follow-up period may limit the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, the fixed surgical sequence may introduce a potential period effect, particularly for subjective outcomes. Repeated outcome assessments across multiple postop days increase the risk of type I error; although Bonferroni correction was applied, this adjustment may have reduced statistical power.

Conclusion

Within the limitations of this randomized split-mouth clinical trial, the application of concentrated growth factors (CGF) to extraction sockets following surgical removal of mesioangular impacted lower third molars was associated with improved early soft tissue healing and a reduction in facial swelling at the third postoperative day. Lower early postoperative pain scores were observed; however, these differences did not remain statistically significant after adjustment for multiple comparisons. No significant effects were observed on trismus or at later postoperative time points. These findings suggest that CGF may represent a promising adjunctive local measure to enhance early postoperative recovery, particularly in terms of soft tissue healing and early swelling control after lower third molar surgery. Further well-designed, prospectively registered clinical trials with larger sample sizes and extended follow-up are required to confirm these findings.

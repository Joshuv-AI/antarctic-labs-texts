Constant Approximation of Fréchet Distance in Strongly Subquadratic Time
Siu-Wing Cheng, Haoqiang Huang, Shuo Zhang

Abstract
Let τ \tau and σ \sigma be two polygonal curves in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} for any fixed d d . Suppose that τ \tau and σ \sigma have n n and m m vertices, respectively, and m ≤ n m\leq n . While conditional lower bounds prevent approximating the Fréchet distance between τ \tau and σ \sigma within a factor of 3 in strongly subquadratic time, the current best approximation algorithm attains a ratio of n c n^{c} in strongly subquadratic time, for some constant c ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) c\in(0,1) . We present a randomized algorithm with running time O ⁡ ( n ​ m 0.99 ​ log ⁡ ( n / ε ) ) O(nm^{0.99}\log(n/\varepsilon)) that approximates the Fréchet distance within a factor of 7 + ε 7+\varepsilon , with a success probability at least 1 − 1 / n 6 1-1/n^{6} . We also adapt our techniques to develop a randomized algorithm that approximates the discrete Fréchet distance within a factor of 7 + ε 7+\varepsilon in strongly subquadratic time. They are the first algorithms to approximate the Fréchet distance and the discrete Fréchet distance within constant factors in strongly subquadratic time.

Introduction

Fréchet distance
is a popular and natural distance metric to measure the similarity between two curves
[
Alt09
]
. It finds many applications in spatio-temporal data mining
[
BBG+20
,
BBG+08
]
.

Consider a polygonal curve
\tau
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with vertices
(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n})
. A
parameterization
of
\tau
is a continuous function
\rho:[0,1]\rightarrow\tau
such that
\rho(0)=v_{1}
,
\rho(1)=v_{n}
, and for all
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,1]
,
\rho[t]
is not behind
\rho[t^{\prime}]
from
v_{1}
to
v_{n}
along
\tau
if
t\leq t^{\prime}
. Let
\varrho
be a parameterization for another polygonal curve
\sigma
. The pair
(\rho,\varrho)
define a
matching
between
\tau
and
\sigma
such that
\rho(t)
is matched to
\varrho(t)
for all
t\in[0,1]
.
The distance realized by
\mathcal{M}
is
d_{\mathcal{M}}(\tau,\sigma)=\max_{t\in[0,1]}d(\rho(t),\varrho(t))
, where
d(\rho(t),\varrho(t))
is the Euclidean distance between
\rho(t)
and
\varrho(t)
. The Fréchet distance of
\sigma
and
\tau
is
d_{F}(\sigma,\tau)=\inf_{\mathcal{M}}d_{\mathcal{M}}(\tau,\sigma)
. If we restrict
\mathcal{M}
to match every vertex of
\sigma
to a vertex of
\tau
, and vice versa, the resulting
\inf_{\mathcal{M}}d_{\mathcal{M}}(\tau,\sigma)
is the
discrete Fréchet distance
, which we denote by
\tilde{d}_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
. The research on computing and approximating the Fréchet and discrete Fréchet distances continues for many years.

Exact algorithms.
Suppose that
\tau
and
\sigma
have
n
and
m
vertices, respectively, and
m\leq n
. Since the first algorithm
[
God91
]
for computing
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
in
O((n^{2}m+nm^{2})\log(mn))
time in 1991, there is ongoing research effort towards understanding the computational complexities of
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
and
\tilde{d}_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
. The first algorithm
[
EM94
]
for computing
\tilde{d}_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
runs in
O(nm)
time using dynamic programming. One year later, the running time for computing
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
was improved to
O(nm\log(mn))

[
AG95
]
. For roughly two decades, these remain to be the state-of-the-art.

In 2013, an asymptotic faster algorithm for computing
\tilde{d}_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was eventually presented
[
AAKS13
]
. It runs in
O(mn\log\log n/\log n)
time on a word RAM machine of
\Theta(\log n)
word size with constant-time table lookup capability. Then a faster
randomized
algorithm
[
BBMM14
]
for computing
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was shown. It runs in
O(mn\sqrt{\log n}(\log\log n)^{3/2})
expected time on a pointer machine and in
O(mn(\log\log n)^{2})
expected time on a word RAM machine of
\Theta(\log n)
word size. The quadratic time barrier was broken recently.
In
\mathbb{R}^{1}
,
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
can be computed in
O(m^{2}\log^{2}n+n\log n)
time
[
BD24
]
. It runs in subquadratic time when
m=o(n)
. In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 2
,
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
can be computed in
O(mn(\log\log n)^{2+c}\log n/\log^{1+c}m)
time for some fixed
c\in(0,1)

[
CH25
]
. This is the first
o(mn)
-time algorithm when
m=\Omega(n^{\varepsilon})
for some fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

It is unlikely that these running times can be improved significantly due to the famous negative result
[
Bri14
]
: for any fixed
c\in(0,1)
and
d\geq 2
, an
O((mn)^{1-c})
-time algorithm for computing
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
or
\tilde{d}_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
would refute the widely accepted
Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis
(SETH). Abboud and Bringmann
[
AB18
]
further refined the result on
\tilde{d}_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
. They showed that
\tilde{d}_{F}(\tau,\sigma)
is unlikely to be computed in
O(n^{2}/\log^{7+c}n)
time for any
c>0
assuming
m=n
.

For (conditional) lower bounds, Bringmann
[
Bri14

proved that no strongly subquadratic algorithm can approximate

within a factor of 1.001 unless SETH fails. As for
\tilde{d}_{F}(\tau,\sigma)

[

Our results.
Our main result is a
randomized

(7+\varepsilon)
-approximation decision procedure for the Fréchet distance running in strongly subquadratic time (Theorem
1
). Specifically, given two polygonal curves
\tau
,
\sigma
and a fixed value
\delta
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, if our decision procedure returns yes, then
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)\leq(7+\varepsilon)\delta
; if it returns no, then
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)>\delta
with probability at least
1-n^{-7}
. The technical ideas can be adapted to get a
randomized
strongly subquadratic
(7+\varepsilon)
-approximation decision procedure for the discrete Fréchet distance as well (Theorem
3
).

[

]

[

(7+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithms for computing

and

, respectively. Since these approaches only introduce extra polylog factors in the running times, the algorithms still run in strongly subquadratic time.

n^{c}
significantly.

Background

Given a polygonal curve
\tau=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
|\tau|=n
be its size. Take two points
x,y\in\tau
. We say
x\leq_{\tau}y
if
x
is not behind
y
along
\tau
, and we use
\tau[x,y]
to denote the subcurve of
\tau
between
x
and
y
. We use
xy
to denote an oriented line segment from
x
to
y
. An array
\mathcal{S}
of
n-1
items is
induced
by
\tau
if
\mathcal{S}[i]
is either a line segment on the edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
or empty for all
i\in[n-1]
.
We will also denote
\mathcal{S}[i]
by
\mathcal{S}_{v_{i}}
. A point
x\in v_{i}v_{i+1}
is
covered
by
\mathcal{S}
if
x\in\mathcal{S}_{v_{i}}
.
For two points
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
d(p,q)
be the Euclidean distance between
p
and
q
. For any value
\delta
, we use
\mathcal{B}(p,\delta)
to denote the ball of radius
\delta
centered at
p
. We present basic knowledge of the Fréchet distance in this section.

Reachability propagation
. The key concept in determining whether
d_{F}(\tau,\sigma)\leq\delta
is the
reachability interval
. Take points
x,y\in\tau
and points
p,q\in\sigma
. For any value
r\geq 0
, the pair
(y,q)
is
r
-reachable
from
(x,p)
if
x\leq_{\tau}y
,
p\leq_{\sigma}q
, and
d_{F}(\tau[x,y],\sigma[p,q])\leq r
. When a pair
(x,p)
is
r
-reachable from
(v_{1},w_{1})
, we call
(x,p)
a
r
-reachable pair
. All points on the edge
w_{j}w_{j+1}
that can form
r
-reachable pairs with
x
constitute the reachability interval of
x
on
w_{j}w_{j+1}
with respect to
r
. It is possible that
x
may not have a reachability interval on some edge of
\sigma
. The reachability intervals of a point
p
of
\sigma
can be defined analogously.

Alt and Godau
[
AG95
]
introduced the free space diagram for the visualization of reachability information. See Figure.
1
for an illustration. Specifically, the free space diagram of
\tau
and
\sigma
with respect to
r
is a parameteric space spanned by
\tau
and
\sigma
such that each point
(x,p)
in the diagram corresponds to a pair of points with
x\in\tau
and
p\in\sigma
. The point
(x,p)
locates inside the free space with respect to
r
if and only if
d(x,p)\leq r
. Call a path inside the free space diagram bi-monotone if it moves either upwards or to the right. Given two pairs
(x,p)
and
(y,q)
,
(y,q)
is
r
-reachable from
(x,p)
if and only if there is a bi-monotone path from
(x,p)
to
(y,q)
inside the free space.

3
Overview of the decision procedure

We focus on describing the decision procedure for the Fréchet distance at a high level. The ideas can be adapted to the discrete Fréche distance.

Given

and

-approximate reachability interval

-reachable pair with

-approximate reachability intervals on

. For any subcurve
\sigma[w_{j_{1}},w_{j_{2}}]
of
\sigma

induced by

-approximate reachable
for

of

For any fixed

(7+\varepsilon)
-approximate reachability interval for

on the edge

\tau

.

\tau_{k}

Therefore, we identify the longest suffix

of
\tau_{k}
such that using Lemma

on it returns a curve

be an array induced by

with all elements being empty. Let

. The detailed construction of

in

for

in

. By the triangle inequality,

\mathcal{I}^{2}

Type 3.
We construct an array
\mathcal{I}^{3}
induced by
\tau_{k}
such that

The construction of

. For any point

of type 3, there is a point

. Note that
\tau[v_{a_{k}},y]

\tau_{k}
. We identify the longest prefix of
\tau_{k}
such that using Lemma
3
on that prefix returns a curve
\zeta^{\text{pre}}_{k}
of at most
2\mu_{2}
vertices. Since

must belong to this prefix, we proceed to use
\zeta^{\text{pre}}_{k}
as a surrogate to invoke

in

\mathcal{S}
is induced by

, and all elements in it are empty.

Type 4.
We construct an array
\mathcal{I}^{4}
such that
\mathcal{I}^{4}
covers all points of type 4, and every point covered by
\mathcal{I}^{4}
forms a
((7+\varepsilon)\delta)
-reachable pair with
w_{b_{l+1}}
. It is unlikely that the use of curve simplification can accelerate the handling of points of type 4. Because these points may be scattered over
\tau_{k}
, and the entire
\tau_{k}
may not be simplified significantly.
We achieve the speedup via several data structures. The key data structure is the one that allows us to answer the following query fast.

Cover
(\tau^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},\mathcal{S})
.
Given a subcurve
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau_{k}
, an array
\mathcal{S}
induced by
\tau_{k}
, and
\delta^{\prime}>0
. For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, the query output is an array
\bar{\mathcal{S}}
induced by
\tau_{k}
that satisfies the following properties:

•

for any point
y\in\tau_{k}
, if there is a point
x
covered by
\mathcal{S}
such that
x\leq_{\tau}y
and
d_{F}(\tau[x,y],\tau^{\prime})\leq\delta^{\prime}
, then
y
is covered by
\bar{\mathcal{S}}
;

•

for any point
y
covered by
\bar{\mathcal{S}}
, there is a point
x
covered by
\mathcal{S}
such that
x\leq_{\tau}y
and
d_{F}(\tau[x,y],\tau^{\prime})\leq(1+\epsilon)\delta^{\prime}
.

We present in Lemma
7
how to preprocess
\tau_{k}
to answer a query
Cover
in
O(\mu_{1})
time. To use it, note that if there is a
y\in\tau_{k}
of type 4, there is a subcurve
\tau^{\prime\prime}=\tau[x,y]
of
\tau_{k}
such that
d_{F}(\tau^{\prime},\sigma_{l})\leq\delta
. Assume that we can find
\tau^{\prime\prime}
efficiently. We use
\tau^{\prime\prime}
as a surrogate of
\sigma_{l}
and execute a query
Cover
(\tau^{\prime\prime},2\delta,\mathcal{A}^{b_{l}}_{k})
. We then assign the output to
\mathcal{I}^{4}
. For any point
y^{\prime}
of type 4, there is a point
x^{\prime}
covered by
\mathcal{A}^{b_{l}}_{k}
such that
d_{F}(\tau[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}],\sigma_{l})\leq\delta
by definition. By the triangle inequality,
d_{F}(\tau[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}],\tau^{\prime\prime})\leq 2\delta
. Hence,
y^{\prime}
is covered by
\mathcal{I}^{4}
by the definition of
Cover
.

The bottleneck is how to find
\tau^{\prime\prime}
for
\sigma_{l}
efficiently. The nearest neighbor data structures
[
BDNP22
,
CH23a
,
CH24
,
M20
]
under the Fréchet distance may help. However, all these data structures have exponential dependency on the size of the query curve, i.e.,
|\sigma_{l}|=\mu_{2}
, in either the space complexity or the query time. We cannot afford to use them.

We use sampling.
Take a curve
\zeta
with
|\zeta|\leq\mu_{1}
. An edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
of
\tau_{k}
is defined to be
marked
by
\zeta
if there is some subcurve
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau_{k}
such that
\tau^{\prime}\cap v_{i}v_{i+1}\not=\emptyset
and
d_{F}(\tau^{\prime},\zeta)\leq\delta
. Intuitively, it is easier to find a subcurve of
\tau_{k}
close to
\zeta
given an edge marked by
\zeta
, because we can focus on the subcurves around this edge. We present in Lemma
6
how to find a subcurve
\tau^{\prime\prime}
of
\tau_{k}
such that
d_{F}(\tau^{\prime\prime},\zeta)\leq(3+2\epsilon)\delta
in
O(|\zeta|^{4})
time given an edge marked by
\zeta
. For small enough
|\zeta|
,
O(|\zeta|^{4})=o(\mu_{1})
.

A curve
\zeta
is called
\omega
-dense
if it marks at least
\omega
edges of
\tau_{k}
. By choosing a suitable value for
\omega
, for any
\omega
-dense
\zeta
, we can sample a small set of
\tau_{k}
’s edges to include at least one edge marked by
\zeta
with high probability. We employ the observation in the following way.

Take an integer
\mu_{3}<\mu_{2}
. We divide
\sigma_{l}
into a collection
(\sigma_{l,1},\sigma_{l,2},\ldots,\sigma_{l,(\mu_{2}-1)/\mu_{3}})
of subcurves of
\mu_{3}
edges such that
\sigma_{l,r}=\sigma[w_{b_{l,r}},w_{b_{l,r+1}}]
, where
b_{l,r}=b_{l}+(r-1)\mu_{3}+1
for
r\in[(\mu_{2}-1)/\mu_{3}]
.
Suppose that all
\sigma_{l,r}
’s are
\omega
-dense. For every
\sigma_{l,r}
, we can find a subcurve
\tau^{\prime}_{l,r}
of
\tau_{k}
with
d_{F}(\tau^{\prime}_{l,r},\sigma_{l,r})\leq(3+2\epsilon)\delta
with high probability via sampling. We then use the sequence
(\tau^{\prime}_{l,1},\tau^{\prime}_{l,2},\ldots,\tau^{\prime}_{l,(\mu_{2}-1)/\mu_{3}})
as a surrogate of
\sigma_{l}
. We do not require that the subcurve sequence follows the ordering along
\tau_{k}
. We use it as follows. We first execute a query
Cover
(\tau^{\prime}_{l,1},(4+2\epsilon)\delta,\epsilon,\mathcal{A}^{b_{l}}_{k})
to get an array
\mathcal{S}^{1}
. For any
r\geq 2
, suppose that we have gotten
\mathcal{S}^{r-1}
, we proceed to execute a query
Cover
(\tau^{\prime}_{l,r},(4+2\epsilon)\delta,\mathcal{S}^{r-1})
to get
\mathcal{S}^{r}
. In the end, we set
\mathcal{I}^{4}=\mathcal{S}^{(\mu_{2}-1)/\mu_{3}}
. It takes
O(\mu_{1}\mu_{2}/\mu_{3})
time.

If some
\sigma_{l,r}
is not
\omega
-dense, we find all edges marked by it by calling
WaveFront
(\tau_{k},\sigma_{l,r},\delta,\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
, where
\mathcal{S}
is an array induced by
\tau_{k}
with
\mathcal{S}_{v_{i}}=v_{i}v_{i+1}\cap\mathcal{B}(w_{b_{l},r},\delta)
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is an array induced by
\sigma_{l,r}
with all elements being empty. It takes
O(\mu_{1}\mu_{3})
time. There must be an edge marked by both
\sigma_{l,r}
and
\sigma_{l}
as
\sigma_{l}
contains
\sigma_{l,r}
. Hence, we can use Lemma
6
with every edge marked by
\sigma_{l,r}
to find a subcurve
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau_{k}
with
d_{F}(\tau^{\prime},\sigma_{l})\leq(3+2\epsilon)\delta
in
O(\omega\mu_{2}^{4})
time. We execute
Cover
(\tau^{\prime},(4+2\epsilon)\delta,\mathcal{A}^{b_{l}}_{k})
and assign the output to
\mathcal{I}^{4}
in
O(\mu_{1})
time.
We will use the following Chernoff bound.

Lemma
5
.

Let
X=\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{i}
, where
X_{i}=1
with probability
p_{i}
, and
X_{i}=0
with probability
1-p_{i}
, and all
X_{i}
’s are independent. Let
\beta=E(X)
. Then
\mathbb{P}(X\leq(1-\alpha)\beta)\leq e^{-\beta\alpha^{2}/2}
for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

4
Subquadratic decision algorithm for Fréchet distance

4.1
Preprocessing

Fix
\tau_{k}

,

Preprocessing for

,
\mathcal{I}^{2}

for

which runs in

Preprocessing for
\mathcal{I}^{4}
.
We first preprocess
\tau_{k}
so that for any

and any subcurve

\sigma_{l}

For every vertex

of

O(\mu_{1})

\tau[\bar{v}_{i},v_{i}]

’s.

Next, we present how to find a subcurve of
\tau_{k}
that is close to
\sigma^{\prime}
based on the above preprocessing. Suppose that

is marked by
\sigma^{\prime}
. There is a subcurve

of

. It holds that

. Otherwise, there is a vertex

such that calling the algorithm in Lemma

on

Intuitively, we can concatenate
\bar{\zeta}_{i}
and
\tilde{\zeta}_{i+1}

of at most

Lemma
6
.

We can preprocess
\tau_{k}
in
O\left(\epsilon^{-\alpha}\mu_{1}^{3}\log(1/\varepsilon)\right)
time such that given any subcurve
\sigma^{\prime}
of
\sigma_{l}
and an edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
of
\tau_{k}
, there is an
O(\mu_{2}^{4})
-time algorithm that returns null or a subcurve
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau_{k}
with

. If the algorithms returns null,

is not marked by
\sigma^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The preprocessing time follows the construction of
\bar{\zeta}_{i}
,
\tilde{\zeta}_{i}
,
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
, and
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
directly. We proceed to show how to find
\tau^{\prime}
.

Suppose that we are given a subcurve
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma[w_{j},w_{j_{1}}]
of
\sigma_{l}
and an edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
. If there is subcurve of
\tau[\bar{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{i+1}]
within a Fréchet distance
\delta
to
\sigma^{\prime}
, we present how to find a subcurve
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau[\bar{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{i+1}]
with
d_{F}(\tau^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})\leq(3+2\epsilon)\delta
based on
\bar{\zeta}_{i}
,
\tilde{\zeta}_{i+1}
,
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
, and
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i+1}
. We first construct a new curve
\zeta^{\prime}
by appending
\tilde{\zeta}_{i+1}
to
\bar{\zeta}_{i}
. That is, we join the last vertex of
\bar{\zeta}_{i}
and the first vertex of
\tilde{\zeta}_{i+1}
by a line segment to generate
\zeta^{\prime}
. Since the new line segment is within a Fréchet distance
(1+\epsilon)\delta
to the edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
, we have
d_{F}(\tau[\bar{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{i+1}],\zeta^{\prime})\leq(1+\epsilon)\delta
. Given that there is a subcurve of
\tau[\bar{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{i+1}]
within a Fréchet distance
\delta
to
\sigma^{\prime}
, there is a subcurve
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}
with
d_{F}(\zeta^{\prime\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\leq(2+\epsilon)\delta
by the triangle inequality.

We aim to find such a
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
. For every edge of
\zeta^{\prime}
, if it intersects the ball
\mathcal{B}(w_{j},(2+\epsilon)\delta)
, we take the minimum point
x
in the intersection with respect to
\leq_{\zeta^{\prime}}
. We insert
x
into a set
X
. If this edge intersects the ball
\mathcal{B}(w_{j_{1}},(2+\epsilon)\delta)
, we take the maximum point
y
in the intersection with respect to
\leq_{\zeta^{\prime}}
, and insert
y
into another set
Y
.

The existence of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
implies the existence of some subcurve that starts from a point in
X
, ends at a point in
Y
, and locates within a Fréchet distance
(2+\epsilon)\delta
to
\sigma^{\prime}
. Let
x
be the point in
X
such that
x
and the start of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
are on the same edge of
\zeta^{\prime}
. Let
y
be the point in
Y
such that
y
and the end of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
are on the same edge of
\zeta^{\prime}
. By definition of
X
and
Y
,
\zeta^{\prime}[x,y]
includes
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
. We can extend the Fréchet matching between
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}
to a matching between
\zeta^{\prime}[x,y]
and
\sigma^{\prime}
by matching the line segment between
x
and the start of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
to
w_{j}
, and matching the line segment between the end of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
and
y
to
w_{j_{1}}
. The matching realizes a distance at most
(2+\epsilon)\delta
.

We test all subcurves of
\zeta^{\prime}
starting from some point in
X
and ending at some point in
Y
. There are
O(|\zeta^{\prime}|^{2})=O(\mu_{2}^{2})
subcurves to be tested. For every subcurve, we check whether the Fréchet distance between it and
\sigma^{\prime}
is at most
(2+\epsilon)\delta
. If so, we return it as
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
. If there is not a satisfactory
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
after trying all

subcurves, it means that there is no subcurve of
\zeta^{\prime}
within a Fréchet distance
(2+\epsilon)\delta
to
\sigma^{\prime}
. It implies that no subcurve of
\tau[\bar{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{i+1}]
is within a Fréchet distance
\delta
to
\sigma^{\prime}
. Hence, the edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
is not marked by
\sigma^{\prime}
. We return null. It takes
O(\mu_{2}^{4})
time.

Suppose that we have found
\zeta^{\prime\prime}=\zeta^{\prime}[x,y]
. We proceed to find a subcurve
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau_{k}
within a Fréchet distance
(1+\epsilon)\delta
to
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
based on
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i+1}
. Recall that
\zeta^{\prime}
is a concatenation of
\bar{\zeta}_{i}
and
\tilde{\zeta}_{i+1}
. It means that
x
must locate in
\bar{\zeta}_{i}
, or
\tilde{\zeta}_{i+1}
, or the new line segment that joins the last vertex of
\bar{\zeta}_{i}
and the first vertex of
\tilde{\zeta}_{i+1}
. So does

. We define a point

in
\tau_{k}
that corresponds to

as follows. We set

if

, set

, and set

to be the point matched to

and the new line segment otherwise. We can define

for

. Hence,
d_{F}(\tau[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}],\sigma^{\prime})\leq(3+2\epsilon)\delta
by the triangle inequality. We set

Discussion and future works

We present the first constant approximation algorithms for the Fréchet distance and the discrete Fréchet distance that run in strongly subquadratic time. Our algorithms are randomized and achieve approximation ratios of
(7+\varepsilon)
. While we follow a conventional approach that divides the input curves
\tau
and
\sigma
into short subcurves
\tau_{k}
’s and
\sigma_{l}
’s and accelerates the propagation of reachablity information involving every pair of
\tau_{k}
and
\sigma_{l}
, we present a new framework for achieving the speedup. Basically, we classify the points in reachablity intervals on
\tau_{k}
and
\sigma_{l}
into four types and present fast algorithms to cover them approximately and fast. While the first three types can be easily dealt with by curve simplification, the type 4 requires new ideas. The handling of type 4 is the bottlenecks of both approximation ratio and the running time. The root of the ratio
7+\varepsilon
is the ratios of
(3+2\epsilon)
in Lemma
6
and
15
. Currently, if we have to use a subcurve
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau_{k}
as the surrogate of some subcurve
\sigma^{\prime}
of
\sigma_{l}
, we can only guarantee an upper bound of
(3+2\epsilon)\delta
on the (discrete) Fréchet distance between
\tau^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}
. It causes an approximation ratio of
(7+O(\epsilon))
inevitably due to the triangle inequality. An improvement of Lemma
6
and
15
will lead to an improvement of approximation ratio directly. As for the running time, though the current setting of parameters provides us with an exponent
0.99
, which is not best possible, it is unlikely that the running time can be improved significantly without a more efficient handling of type 4. Because of the high time compleixty in finding the surrogates of subcurves of
\sigma_{l}
and answering the query
(Dis)Wave
(Lemma
6
,
7
,
15
, and
16
), the current setting of parameters is near the optimal.

Improving the approximation performance and the running time can be a natural problem in the near furture. Since our algorithms are randomized, whether there is a
deterministic
constant approximation algorithm in strongly subquadratic time for the (discrete) Fréchet distance is sill a big open problem.

Data structure for answering the query

We restate the query

with a subcurve
\tau^{\prime}
of
\tau_{k}
, an array
\mathcal{S}
induced by
\tau_{k}
, and
\delta^{\prime}>0
. The start and end of
\tau^{\prime}
may not be vertices. The value

is known during preprocessing.

Cover
(\tau^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},\mathcal{S})
. The answer is another array

induced by
\tau_{k}
satisfing that:

•

for any point

, if there is a point

covered by
\mathcal{S}
such that

and

, then
x
is covered by
\bar{\mathcal{S}}

•

for any point
x
covered by
\bar{\mathcal{S}}
, there is a point
y
covered by
\mathcal{S}
such that
y\leq_{\tau}x
and
d_{F}(\tau[y,x],\tau^{\prime})\leq(1+\epsilon)\delta^{\prime}
.

The underlying idea of the data structure is to use the planarity of the free space diagram, especially the observation in Lemma
1
. Suppose that
\tau^{\prime}
is a vertex-to-vertex subcurve of
\tau_{k}
from
v_{i_{1}}
to
v_{i_{2}}
. For every edge

of
\tau_{k}

be the start of
\mathcal{S}_{v_{i}}\cap\mathcal{B}(v_{i_{1}},\delta^{\prime})
if
\mathcal{S}_{v_{i}}
is not null. Consider the free space diagram induced by
\tau^{\prime}
and
\tau_{k}

-reachable from

-reachable from

is
\delta^{\prime}
-reachable from

\delta^{\prime}
-reachable from some

Suppose that for every

-reachable from

is the union of points covered by

during preprocessing given that

, a point

if and only if there is a point

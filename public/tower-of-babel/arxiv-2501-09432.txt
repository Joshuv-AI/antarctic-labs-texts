On a Gelfand-Tsetlin representation of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} in the space of sections of a local system with two monodromy parameters
Claude Eicher

Abstract
. We construct a Gelfand-Tsetlin representation of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} in the space of sections of a local system. The local system lives on an open part of the flag variety given by the intersection of three translates of the big cell and has two complex monodromy parameters. We analyze the structure of this representation.



1.
Setup

We consider the linear algebraic group
\SL_{3}
over
\C
.
We fix a maximal torus
T
and a Borel subgroup
B\supseteq T
of it.
We denote by
U
the unipotent radical of
B
.
We have the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
of
\SL_{3}
and the triangular decomposition

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
.
We denote by
\mathfrak{t}^{*}=\Hom_{\C}(\mathfrak{t},\C)
the weights.
We fix a choice of root vectors

e_{1},e_{2},e_{12}=[e_{1},e_{2}]

associated to the roots
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}

of
\mathfrak{n}

and
f_{1},f_{2},f_{12}=-[f_{1},f_{2}]
associated to the
roots
-\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}
of
\mathfrak{n}^{-}

such that the usual commutation relations are satisfied.
The simple coroots are denoted by
h_{1},h_{2}
. The Weyl vector
is
\rho=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. The simple reflections
of the Weyl group are denoted by
s_{1}
and
s_{2}

and the longest element is
w_{\circ}=s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}
.
We consider the flag variety
\SL_{3}/B
of
\SL_{3}
.
We have an isomorphism of varieties

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Phi:U_{\alpha_{1}}\times U_{\alpha_{2}}\times U_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\xrightarrow{\cong}X_{w_{\circ}}=Uw_{\circ}B\\
&(e^{x_{\alpha_{1}}e_{1}},e^{x_{\alpha_{2}}e_{2}},e^{x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}e_{12}})\mapsto e^{x_{\alpha_{1}}e_{1}}e^{x_{\alpha_{2}}e_{2}}e^{x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}e_{12}}w_{\circ}B\end{split}

parametrizing the big cell
X_{w_{\circ}}
in
\SL_{3}/B
, which is the open
B
-orbit in that variety.
Here
U\supseteq U_{\alpha}\cong\Ga
denotes the root subgroup associated to the root
\alpha
.

2.
Local system
\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
and its space of sections
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

We define
O=X_{w_{\circ}}\cap s_{1}X_{w_{\circ}}\cap s_{2}X_{w_{\circ}}
.
(
1.1
) restricts to an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi:\Gm^{2}\times\Aff^{1}=(U_{\alpha_{1}}\setminus 1)\times(U_{\alpha_{2}}\setminus 1)\times U_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\xrightarrow{\cong}O

of varieties.
We define for
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\C
the local system
\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

in the category of (left)
\mathcal{D}
-modules on
O
by

\Phi^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}})=\mathcal{O}^{(\mu_{1})}_{\Gm}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}^{(\mu_{2})}_{\Gm}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\Aff^{1}}
. Here we use the definition
\mathcal{O}^{(\mu)}_{\Gm}=\mathcal{D}_{\Gm}/\mathcal{D}_{\Gm}(x\partial_{x}+\mu)
, where
\mathcal{D}_{\Gm}
is the sheaf
of differential operators on
\Gm
.
We refer to
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
as the two monodromy parameters of
\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
.
By definition
\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime}}

if and only if
\mu_{1}-\mu_{1}^{\prime}\in\Z
and
\mu_{2}-\mu_{2}^{\prime}\in\Z
.
The image of
1\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gm}

is denoted by
1_{\mu}\in\mathcal{O}^{(\mu)}_{\Gm}
. Then
x^{k}1_{\mu}
,
k\in\Z
,
is a
\C
-basis of
\Gamma(\Gm,\mathcal{O}^{(\mu)}_{\Gm})
and we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}x^{k}1_{\mu}=(k-\mu)x^{k-1}1_{\mu}\;.

The space of sections of
\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
will be denoted by
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}=\Gamma(O,\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}})
.
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

has the
\C
-basis

\displaystyle u_{k,l,m}=x_{\alpha_{1}}^{k}1_{\mu_{1}}\otimes x_{\alpha_{2}}^{l}1_{\mu_{2}}\otimes x^{m}_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}},\;k,l\in\Z,\;m\in\Z_{\geq 0}\;.

M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
is acted upon by the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
through infinitesimal left translations
given by the Lie algebra homomorphism
a:\mathfrak{sl}_{3}\rightarrow V
,
where
V
denotes the vector fields on
O
. The
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-module
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
has central character zero.
By the Beilinson-Bernstein equivalence
[
BB81
]

M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

is isomorphic to
M_{\mu_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime}}
as
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-module if and only if
\mu_{1}-\mu_{1}^{\prime}\in\Z

and
\mu_{2}-\mu_{2}^{\prime}\in\Z
. Thus, the isomorphism class of
the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-module
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

only depends on
\mu_{1}+\Z
and
\mu_{2}+\Z
.

3.
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-action on the basis
u_{k,l,m}

We compute that

\displaystyle a(e_{1})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}}}

\displaystyle a(e_{2})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x_{\alpha_{2}}}+x_{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}

\displaystyle a(h)

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{1}(h)x_{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}}}-\alpha_{2}(h)x_{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{2}}}-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})(h)x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}},\;h\in\mathfrak{t}

\displaystyle a(f_{1})

\displaystyle=x_{\alpha_{1}}^{2}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}}}-(x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}+x_{\alpha_{1}}x_{\alpha_{2}})\partial_{x_{\alpha_{2}}}+x_{\alpha_{1}}x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}

\displaystyle a(f_{2})

\displaystyle=x_{\alpha_{2}}^{2}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{2}}}+x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}}}

\displaystyle a(e_{12})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}

\displaystyle a(f_{12})

\displaystyle=x_{\alpha_{1}}x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}}}+x_{\alpha_{2}}(x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}+x_{\alpha_{1}}x_{\alpha_{2}})\partial_{x_{\alpha_{2}}}+x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}^{2}\partial_{x_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}\;.

From this and (
2.1
) we compute

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e_{1}u_{k,l,m}&=-\overline{k}u_{k-1,l,m}\\
e_{2}u_{k,l,m}&=-\overline{l}u_{k,l-1,m}+mu_{k+1,l,m-1}\\
hu_{k,l,m}&=-((\overline{k}+m)\alpha_{1}(h)+(\overline{l}+m)\alpha_{2}(h))u_{k,l,m},\;h\in\mathfrak{t}\\
f_{1}u_{k,l,m}&=(\overline{k}-\overline{l}+m)u_{k+1,l,m}-\overline{l}u_{k,l-1,m+1}\\
f_{2}u_{k,l,m}&=\overline{l}u_{k,l+1,m}+\overline{k}u_{k-1,l,m+1}\\
e_{12}u_{k,l,m}&=-mu_{k,l,m-1}\\
f_{12}u_{k,l,m}&=(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+m)u_{k,l,m+1}+\overline{l}u_{k+1,l+1,m}\;.\end{split}

Here and below we abbreviate
\overline{k}=k-\mu_{1}
and
\overline{l}=l-\mu_{2}
.

In particular we see that the subspace of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
annihilated by
e_{12}
has the basis
u_{k,l,0},k,l\in\Z
, and that the
\mathfrak{t}
-weight of
u_{k,l,m}
is

(3.2)

\displaystyle-(\overline{k}+m)\alpha_{1}-(\overline{l}+m)\alpha_{2}\;.

About the structure of the linear combinations in (
3.1
) that involve two
terms we observe the following: The shift of the indices
k,l,m
in the two terms is always in complementary components.

4.
Basis
w_{k,l,m}

For the rest of the article we assume
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\notin\Z
as most of the results require
this condition.
We introduce for
k,l\in\Z,m\in\Z_{\geq 0}
the vectors in
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

(4.1)

\displaystyle w_{k,l,m}=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\binom{m}{n}\frac{\overline{l}^{(n)}}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l})^{(n)}}u_{k+n,l+n,m-n}=u_{k,l,m}+\dots\;.

Here
x^{(n)}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)
denotes a raising factorial. We have

w_{k,l,0}=u_{k,l,0}
. We have
f_{12}e_{12}w_{k,l,m}=-m(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+m-1)w_{k,l,m}

and this eigenvalue condition suffices to determine the coefficients in (
4.1
)
using (
3.1
). We also have

h_{1}w_{k,l,m}=(-2\overline{k}+\overline{l}-m)w_{k,l,m}

and
h_{2}w_{k,l,m}=(\overline{k}-2\overline{l}-m)w_{k,l,m}
and the
\mathfrak{t}
-weight
of
w_{k,l,m}
is (
3.2
).
We also note that if
-\overline{l}\in\{0,1,\dots,m\}
the sum in (
4.1
)
truncates to
\sum_{n=0}^{\min\{m,-\overline{l}\}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

w_{k,l,m}
,
k,l\in\Z,m\in\Z_{\geq 0}
, form a basis of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

of simultaneous eigenvectors with respect to the triple of endomorphisms
(h_{1},h_{2},f_{12}e_{12})
.
The simultaneous eigenvalues determine
(k,l,m)
uniquely.

Proof.

We first show that the simultaneous eigenvalues

(-2\overline{k}+\overline{l}-m,\overline{k}-2\overline{l}-m,-m(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+m-1))

of
w_{k,l,m}
with respect to
(h_{1},h_{2},f_{12}e_{12})
determine
(k,l,m)
uniquely.
Hence we assume

\displaystyle-2\overline{k}+\overline{l}-m

\displaystyle=-2\overline{k^{\prime}}+\overline{l^{\prime}}-m^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\overline{l}+\overline{k}-m

\displaystyle=-2\overline{l^{\prime}}+\overline{k^{\prime}}-m^{\prime}

\displaystyle-m(m+\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)

\displaystyle=-m^{\prime}(m^{\prime}+\overline{k^{\prime}}+\overline{l^{\prime}}-1)\;.

From the first two equations we get
\overline{k}=\overline{k^{\prime}}+m^{\prime}-m

and
\overline{l}=\overline{l^{\prime}}+m^{\prime}-m
. If we plug this into the last equation
we obtain the equation

\displaystyle 0=(m-(\overline{k^{\prime}}+\overline{l^{\prime}}+m^{\prime}-1))(m-m^{\prime})

for the remaing variable
m
. We find the solutions

\displaystyle\begin{split}&m=m^{\prime}\quad\overline{k}=\overline{k^{\prime}}\quad\overline{l}=\overline{l^{\prime}}\\
&m=\overline{k^{\prime}}+\overline{l^{\prime}}+m^{\prime}-1\quad\overline{k}=1-\overline{l^{\prime}}\quad\overline{l}=1-\overline{k^{\prime}}\;.\end{split}

The second solution, however, requires
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\in\Z

and hence only the first is left. It follows that the
w_{k,l,m}
are linearly independent
since they have distinct simultaneous eigenvalues. On the other hand, by induction
on
m\in\Z_{\geq 0}
it is clear from (
4.1
) that
u_{k,l,m}
is a linear combination of the

w_{k+n,l+n,m-n}
for
n\in\{0,\dots,m\}
. Thus the

w_{k,l,m}
span
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
. We have
shown that the
w_{k,l,m}
form a basis of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
.
∎

The lemma implies that
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
is a Gelfand-Tsetlin module
and the
w_{k,l,m}
a Gelfand-Tsetlin basis. Namely,
it is a direct sum of one dimensional simultaneous eigenspaces
\C w_{k,l,m}
with respect to
\Gamma
,
where
\Gamma
is the Gelfand-Tsetlin commutative subalgebra of
\Uea\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
determined
by
h_{1},h_{2}
, and the Casimir operator of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-subalgebra associated
to the root
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. Here
\Uea\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
is the universal enveloping
algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
.
Thus
\Gamma
is a polynomial algebra in five generators
and its action on
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
factors through the polynomial subalgebra whose
generators can be taken to be
h_{1},h_{2},f_{12}e_{12}
,
the triple we have considered above. We will denote it by
\Gamma_{0}=\C[h_{1},h_{2},f_{12}e_{12}]
.

For
k,l\in\Z,m\in\Z_{\geq 0}
we can invert (
4.1
) to

\displaystyle u_{k,l,m}=\sum_{n=0}^{m}(-1)^{n}\binom{m}{n}\frac{\overline{l}^{(n)}}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+n-1)^{(n)}}w_{k+n,l+n,m-n}=w_{k,l,m}+\dots\;.

5.
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-action on
w_{k,l,m}

We deduce from (
3.1
) that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e_{1}w_{k,l,m}&=-\overline{k}w_{k-1,l,m}-m\frac{\overline{l}(\overline{l}-1)}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l})(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)}w_{k,l+1,m-1}\\
e_{2}w_{k,l,m}&=-\overline{l}w_{k,l-1,m}+m\frac{\overline{k}(\overline{k}-1)}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l})(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)}w_{k+1,l,m-1}\\
f_{1}w_{k,l,m}&=\frac{\overline{k}(\overline{k}-1)(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+m)}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l})(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)}w_{k+1,l,m}-\overline{l}w_{k,l-1,m+1}\\
f_{2}w_{k,l,m}&=\frac{\overline{l}(\overline{l}-1)(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+m)}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l})(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)}w_{k,l+1,m}+\overline{k}w_{k-1,l,m+1}\\
e_{12}w_{k,l,m}&=-mw_{k,l,m-1}\\
f_{12}w_{k,l,m}&=(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+m)w_{k,l,m+1}\;.\end{split}

Here are some basic remarks about the structure of these formulae.
If the right-hand side involves two terms, the shift of
k,l,m
in these two terms is in
complementary components.
The formulae are symmetric under the simultaneous interchange
1\leftrightarrow 2
and
k\leftrightarrow l
, if we switch the sign in the term where
m
is shifted.
For
m=0
the action of
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
involves only one basis element
and one of the coefficients in the expression
f_{1}w_{k,l,m}
and
f_{2}w_{k,l,m}
simplifies.

6.
Simplicity of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

We recall the well-known and elementary

Remark 6.1
.

Let
M
be an
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-module. We consider
v=\sum_{j=1}^{n}v_{j}
, where
v_{j}\in M
are simultaneous
(h_{1},h_{2},f_{12}e_{12})
-eigenvectors,
in particular nonzero, with distinct simultaneous eigenvalues.
Let
\chi_{j}:\Gamma_{0}\rightarrow\C
be the character defined by the simultaneous eigenvalue of

v_{j}
. Let
S
be the
\Gamma_{0}
-submodule of
M
generated by
v
.
Then each
v_{j}\in S
. In fact,
S
equals the
\Gamma_{0}
-submodule generated by
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
, in particular it is generated by simultaneous eigenvectors. If
S
is instead the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-submodule of
M
generated by
v
, then
S
equals the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-submodule generated by

v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
.
Indeed, we have for
g\in\Gamma_{0}

\displaystyle S\ni v

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}v_{j}

\displaystyle S\ni gv

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\chi_{j}(g)v_{j}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle S\ni g^{n}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\chi_{j}(g)^{n}v_{j}\;.

If the Vandermonde determinant

(6.1)

\displaystyle\prod_{i<j}(\chi_{j}(g)-\chi_{i}(g))\neq 0

it follows that each
v_{j}\in S
.
Now for
i\neq j
distinctness of the simultaneous eigenvalues of
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
is equivalent to
\chi_{j}\neq\chi_{i}

and to the linear functional

\displaystyle\chi_{ij}=(\chi_{j}-\chi_{i})|\C h_{1}\oplus\C h_{2}\oplus\C f_{12}e_{12}:\C h_{1}\oplus\C h_{2}\oplus\C f_{12}e_{12}\rightarrow\C

being nonzero. Hence
\codim\ker\chi_{ij}\geq 1
and consequently
\C h_{1}\oplus\C h_{2}\oplus\C f_{12}e_{12}\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}\ker\chi_{ij}

is open in
\C h_{1}\oplus\C h_{2}\oplus\C f_{12}e_{12}
. Thus, there is a
g\in\C h_{1}\oplus\C h_{2}\oplus\C f_{12}e_{12}
such that
(
6.1
) holds.

Theorem 6.1
.

M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
is simple if and only if
\mu_{1}\notin\Z
and
\mu_{2}\notin\Z
.

Proof.

If
\mu_{1}\in\Z
or
\mu_{2}\in\Z
it is easy to exhibit proper
submodules of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
, see
section 8
below. Now let
\mu_{1}\notin\Z
and
\mu_{2}\notin\Z
. Let
v\neq 0\in M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
and
S
be the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-submodule generated by
v
.
By Remark
6.1

w_{k_{0},l_{0},m_{0}}\in S

for some
k_{0},l_{0},m_{0}
.
Hence it suffices to show that
w_{k_{0},l_{0},m_{0}}
generates all of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}

when acting with
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
.
We now look at (
5.1
). Acting with
e_{12}
we find
w_{k_{0},l_{0},0}\in S
.
Acting then with
e_{1}
we find
w_{k,l_{0},0}\in S
for
k\leq k_{0}
. Acting with
e_{2}
we find

w_{k,l,0}\in S
for
k\leq k_{0},l\leq l_{0}
. Acting with
f_{12}
we find
w_{k,l,m}\in S
,
k\leq k_{0},l\leq l_{0},m\geq 0
.
Now
f_{1}w_{k_{0},l,m}=Cw_{k_{0}+1,l,m}-\overline{l}w_{k_{0},l-1,m+1}

with
C\neq 0
and this implies
w_{k_{0}+1,l,m}\in S
for
l\leq l_{0},m\geq 0
.
Thus we find
w_{k,l,m}\in S
for
k\in\Z,l\leq l_{0},m\geq 0
.
Using
f_{2}
we find in the same way
w_{k,l,m}\in S
for
k,l\in\Z,m\geq 0
.
∎

7.
Dual
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}^{\vee}

7.1.
Definition of the dual

Let
M
be an
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-module with the property that it is the direct sum
of its finite dimensional simultaneous
(h_{1},h_{2},f_{12}e_{12})
-eigenspaces
M_{\chi}
.
We define on
M^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{\chi}\Hom_{\C}(M_{\chi},\C)
, where
\chi

runs through the simultaneous eigenvalues of
M
, an
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-action by

(X\eta)(v)=-\eta(\tau(X)v)
for
X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
,
v\in M_{\chi}
,
\eta\in\Hom_{\C}(M_{\chi},\C)
. Here
\tau
is the standard involution of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

given by

\displaystyle\tau:h_{1}\mapsto-h_{1},\;h_{2}\mapsto-h_{2},\;f_{1}\leftrightarrow e_{1},f_{2}\leftrightarrow e_{2},\;-f_{12}\leftrightarrow e_{12}\;.

The vector subspace
\Hom_{\C}(M_{\chi},\C)\subseteq M^{\vee}
is then the simultaneous
eigenspace associated to
\chi
, i.e.
\Hom_{\C}(M_{\chi},\C)=(M^{\vee})_{\chi}
.

M^{\vee}
is called the dual of
M
.

7.2.
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-action on the dual basis
\eta_{k,l,m}

This construction of the dual applies to
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
.
We consider the basis
\eta_{k,l,m}
of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}^{\vee}
dual to the basis
w_{k,l,m}
of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
. Thus,
\eta_{k,l,m}\in\Hom_{\C}(\C w_{k,l,m},\C)

is defined by the normalization
\eta_{k,l,m}(w_{k,l,m})=1
. As observed above,
\eta_{k,l,m}
has the same simultaneous

(h_{1},h_{2},f_{12}e_{12})
-eigenvalues as
w_{k,l,m}
.
The
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-action
on the
\eta_{k,l,m}
immediately follows from (
5.1
)

(7.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e_{1}\eta_{k,l,m}&=-\frac{(\overline{k}-1)(\overline{k}-2)(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+m-1)}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-2)}\eta_{k-1,l,m}+\begin{cases}(\overline{l}+1)\eta_{k,l+1,m-1}&m>0\\
0&m=0\end{cases}\\
e_{2}\eta_{k,l,m}&=-\frac{(\overline{l}-1)(\overline{l}-2)(\overline{k}+\overline{l}+m-1)}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-2)}\eta_{k,l-1,m}-\begin{cases}(\overline{k}+1)\eta_{k+1,l,m-1}&m>0\\
0&m=0\end{cases}\\
f_{1}\eta_{k,l,m}&=(\overline{k}+1)\eta_{k+1,l,m}+(m+1)\frac{(\overline{l}-1)(\overline{l}-2)}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-2)}\eta_{k,l-1,m+1}\\
f_{2}\eta_{k,l,m}&=(\overline{l}+1)\eta_{k,l+1,m}-(m+1)\frac{(\overline{k}-1)(\overline{k}-2)}{(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-1)(\overline{k}+\overline{l}-2)}\eta_{k-1,l,m+1}\\

7.3.
Structure of

Theorem 7.1

This follows by inspecting (

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

\overline{l}\leq 0

\eta_{k,l,0}\in S
,

.

,

,

Theorem 7.2
.

M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\cong M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}^{\vee}
as
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-module if and only if
\mu_{1}\notin\Z
and
\mu_{2}\notin\Z

Proof.

If
\mu_{1}\notin\Z
and
\mu_{2}\notin\Z
we have for
x_{k,l,m}\in\C^{\times}
given by

(7.2)

\displaystyle x_{k,l,m}=(-1)^{m}\frac{(\overline{k}+\overline{l})^{(m)}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\overline{k}\;\overline{l}\;(m!)}\times\begin{cases}\frac{(-\mu_{1}-1)^{(k)}}{(-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}-1)^{(k)}}&k\geq 0\\
\frac{(-2-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})_{(-k)}}{(-2-\mu_{1})_{(-k)}}&k<0\end{cases}\times\begin{cases}\frac{(-\mu_{2}-1)^{(l)}}{(\overline{k}-\mu_{2}-1)^{(l)}}&l\geq 0\\
\frac{(\overline{k}-\mu_{2}-2)_{(-l)}}{(-2-\mu_{2})_{(-l)}}&l<0\end{cases}

(raising respectively falling factorials in the first respectively second cases) that,
if we set
x_{k,l,m}\eta_{k,l,m}^{\prime}=\eta_{k,l,m}
,
(

) is satisfied with
\eta_{k,l,m}^{\prime}
replacing

.
In fact, the

are uniquely determined by the initial value

together with the
requirement that

\displaystyle e_{1}\eta^{\prime}_{k,l,m}=-\overline{k}\eta^{\prime}_{k-1,l,m}+\dots\quad e_{2}\eta^{\prime}_{k,l,m}=-\overline{l}\eta^{\prime}_{k,l-1,m}+\dots\quad e_{12}\eta^{\prime}_{k,l,m}=-m\eta^{\prime}_{k,l,m-1}\;,

\eta_{k-1,l,m}

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

\phi:M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\xrightarrow{\cong}M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}^{\vee}

∎

For the rest of the article we assume

Notation 8.1

Let

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

is denoted by
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},J}

M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},J}

As a consequence of Remark

any

-submodule of

-action on

is obtained by truncating
the formulae (
5.1

the indices do not belong to

. We also note that the same construction applies to

instead of
M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}
, i.e. we may form
(M^{\vee}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}})_{J}
,
and we may identify
(M^{\vee}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}})_{J}=(M_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},J})^{\vee}
.
Thus we simply write

.

Contactomorphic vertically convex domains Thanks: This work is part of a project in the SFB/TRR 191 Symplectic Structures in Geometry, Algebra and Dynamics , funded by the DFG
Jan Eyll, Jonas Fritsch, Kai Zehmisch

Abstract
. We consider the standard Darboux space equipped with the radial symmetric contact form. We study co-orientation preserving contactomorphisms between relatively compact domains up to the boundary. We determine the contactomorphism classes among all strict vertically convex domains over a round ball in the Liouville hyperplane that are radially symmetric about the Reeb axis and whose boundary coincide along a neighbourhood of the common equator. The total invariant is the mean curvature of the bounding sphere at the umbilic points with the same sign. Replacing the Liouville hyperplane by codisc bundles of closed non-Besse Riemannian manifolds or finite symplectisations of closed non-Besse strict contact manifolds analogous results are formulated in terms of characteristic length and total characteristic action, resp.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(S,V_{S},f_{\pm})
and
(T,V_{T},g_{\pm})

be equivalent shapes in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n}

with shadow set
V_{S}=V_{T}

equal to a round ball about
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
Assume that

and
g_{\pm}
are all radially symmetric.
Then the domains
(D_{S},\xi_{\st})
and
(D_{T},\xi_{\st})

are co-orientation preserving contactomorphic up to the boundary
if and only if
f^{\prime\prime}_{+}(0)=g^{\prime\prime}_{+}(0)
and
f^{\prime\prime}_{-}(0)=g^{\prime\prime}_{-}(0)
.

In other words,
vertically convex domains
with equivalent radially symmetric shapes are contactomorphic
precisely if the mean curvatures at the umbilic points of the same sign
coincide.
The constructive part uses a Moser integrating vector field,
so that in fact
an ambient compactly supported
contact isotopy relative to
a neighbourhood of the equator is obtained.
The induced isotopy of the boundary of the vertically convex domain
is through radially symmetric shapes.

We will describe a perturbation method
in order to obtain shapes with contactomorphic domains.

2.
Radial symmetric functions

2.1.
Morse lemma and consequences

Consider a smooth function
f\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
By the fundamental theorem of calculus
we find a smooth function
f_{1}\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that
f(t)-f(0)=tf_{1}(t)
,
namely
f_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(st)\mathrm{d}s
.
Defining smooth functions
f_{k+1}:=(f_{k})_{1}
inductively,
we obtain
f(t)-f(0)=t^{k+1}f_{k+1}(t)

whenever
f^{(l)}(0)=0
for
l=1,\ldots,k
.
Taking
(k+1)
-fold derivative at
0
yields

f_{k+1}(0)=\frac{f^{(k+1)}(0)}{(k+1)!}\,.

Lemma 2.1.1

(Root lemma)
.

Let
f
be an even function.
Then there exists a smooth function
\tilde{f}\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that
f(t)=\tilde{f}(t^{2})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By Whitney extension,
it suffices to find a smooth function
\tilde{f}
on
[0,\infty)
,
as
t^{2}
is always non-negative.
We can assume that
f(0)=0
.
Define

\tilde{f}(t):=f(\sqrt{t})

for
t\geq 0
.
As
f
is even,
\tilde{f}(t^{2})=f(|t|)=f(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

It remains to show smoothness of
\tilde{f}
.
For
t>0
we have by the Morse lemma that

\tilde{f}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{f^{\prime}(\sqrt{t})}{2\sqrt{t}}=\frac{1}{2}(f^{\prime})_{1}(\sqrt{t})

as
f^{\prime}(0)=0
for an even function.
Similarly, using
f(0)=0
,

\frac{\tilde{f}(t)}{t}=\frac{(\sqrt{t})^{2}f_{2}(\sqrt{t})}{t}=f_{2}(\sqrt{t})

for all
t>0
,
so that the left handed limit for
t
tends to
0

exists and equals

\tilde{f}^{\prime}(0)=f_{2}(0)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{2}(f^{\prime})_{1}(0)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\tilde{f}^{\prime}(t)\,.

The second equality follows with the Morse lemma,
the third from letting
t
tend to
0
in
f^{\prime}(t)/t=(f^{\prime})_{1}(t)
,
and the last from the equation
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2}(f^{\prime})_{1}(\sqrt{t})

obtained above.
In other word,
\tilde{f}^{\prime}
extends (from the left) to
0
continuously,
so that
\tilde{f}
is
C^{1}
on
[0,\infty)
with

\tilde{f}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{(f^{\prime})_{1}}\,.

Repeating the present argument with the smooth, even function

(f^{\prime})_{1}-(f^{\prime})_{1}(0)
instead of
f
yields
that
\widetilde{(f^{\prime})_{1}}
is
C^{1}
and, consequently,
that
\tilde{f}
is
C^{2}
.
Inductively, this shows that
\tilde{f}
is smooth.
∎

2.2.
About the Hessian

The root lemma implies
that a smooth function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is radially symmetric if and only if
there exists a smooth function
\tilde{f}\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that
f(\mathbf{x})=\tilde{f}(r^{2})
,
r=|\mathbf{x}|
,
for all
\mathbf{x}
,
see Lemma
2.1.1
.
Therefore,

\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{x})=2x_{j}\tilde{f}^{\prime}(r^{2})

and, hence,

\partial_{ij}f(\mathbf{x})=4x_{i}x_{j}\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}(r^{2})+2\delta_{ij}\tilde{f}^{\prime}(r^{2})\,.

In the proof of the root lemma we obtained

\tilde{f}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)
,
so that

\Hess_{f}(0)=f^{\prime\prime}(0)\mathbb{I}\,.

3.
Invariants of contactomorphisms

3.1.
Characteristic foliation

Given a co-oriented contact manifold
(M,\xi)
,
the contact structure
\xi

can be written as the kernel of a contact form
\alpha
.
The defining contact form
\alpha
is determined uniquely
up to multiplication by a positive function on
M
.
The co-orientation of
\xi
equals the one induced by the Reeb vector field
of any contact form that defines
\xi
.
Analogously,

\xi
defines an orientation on
M
via
\alpha\wedge(\mathrm{d}\alpha)^{n}

and a conformal symplectic structure on
\xi
via
\mathrm{d}\alpha
,
where the dimension of
M
is
2n+1
.

Consider an oriented hypersurface
S
of
M

provided with a positive volume form
\Omega
.
Setting
\beta:=\alpha|_{TS}
,
the equation
i_{X}\Omega=\beta\wedge(\mathrm{d}\beta)^{n-1}

defines a smooth vector field
X
on
S
uniquely.
Changing
\Omega
or
\alpha
by a positive function, resp.,
results in a conformal change of
X
by a positive function.
In other words,
the conformal class
[X]
only depends on the orientation of
S

and on the co-oriented contact structure
\xi
.
The oriented singular
1
-dimensional foliation
S_{\xi}

integrated by any representing
characteristic vector field

X

is called
characteristic foliation
,
regular
1
-dimensional leaves are the
characteristics
.
Observe that the set of characteristic vector fields is convex
and, therefore, allows cut and paste operations.

For any characteristic vector field
X
of
S_{\xi}

and for all
p\in S

the linear span
\mathbb{R}X_{p}

is the symplectic orthogonal complement of

T_{p}S\cap\xi_{p}=\ker\beta_{p}

in
\xi_{p}

w.r.t. the conformal symplectic structure on
\xi
,
see
[
7
, Lemma 2.5.20]
.
The
singular points
of
S_{\xi}
are those
p\in S

for which
T_{p}S=\xi_{p}
holds.
A singular point
p
of
S_{\xi}
is called
positive

whenever the orientations of
T_{p}S
and
\xi_{p}
coincide,

negative
otherwise.
After taking appropriate choices,
the set of singular points,
the so-called
singular set
,
is equal to the zero set
of any characteristic vector field of
S_{\xi}
.
Alternatively,
the singular set can be given by
\{p\in S\,|\,\beta_{p}=0\}
.
Hence,
by Frobenius non-integrability of
\xi
,
the singular set has no interior point.
Its complement, the so-called
regular set
, is open.

The linearisation of a characteristic vector field
X
of
S_{\xi}

w.r.t. any connection
D
on
S

In particular, the singularity

The previous discussion implies:

The pull back action of

3.2.

Let
\alpha

\xi

Consider a smooth function

Observe that

Proposition 3.2.1
.

Assume that

is the regular zero set of

and that

growths in normal direction of

Assume further that

admits singular points.
Then

generates the oriented singular
1
-dimensional foliation

Proof.

The choice of

implies that

\pm\mathrm{d}H(R_{\alpha})|_{p}>0

at a positive/negative singularity

of

because
\pm T_{p}S
equals
\xi_{p}

as oriented vector spaces.
By the defining equations of the contact Hamiltonian

,
the restriction

is tangent to
\xi
and

Moreover,

vanishes precisely at the singular points of

Hence, point-wise,

To conclude that

observe that

i_{X_{H}}(\mathrm{d}\alpha)^{n}=n\big(\!-\!\mathrm{d}H+\mathrm{d}H(R_{\alpha})\alpha\big)\wedge(\mathrm{d}\alpha)^{n-1}\,.

Restriction to

As

is a positive volume form of

Therefore,

generates

with the correct orientation.
∎

Consequently,
different choices of
\alpha
and

result in a multiplication of

by a positive function on

.

of
S^{\pm}

Hence

V_{t}|_{U^{\pm}}=X_{f}+X_{H_{t}}+\partial_{b}H_{t}\cdot(Y_{\lambda}-tX_{f})\,.

The boundary condition (iv) in Lemma

can be phrased like

H_{t}|_{S^{\pm}}=\big(t\mathrm{d}_{V}\!f+\lambda\big)(V_{t})|_{S^{\pm}}+\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}(V_{t})|_{S^{\pm}}\,.

In order to compute the right hand side observe that

t\mathrm{d}_{V}\!f\big(X_{f}+X_{H_{t}}\big)=-\mathrm{d}_{V}\!H_{t}\big(tX_{f}\big)

by horizontal energy preservation and that

\lambda\big(X_{f}+X_{H_{t}}\big)=\lambda(X_{f})+\mathrm{d}_{V}\!H_{t}(Y_{\lambda})\,.

Adding both using
\mathrm{d}\lambda
-duality as

\big(t\mathrm{d}_{V}\!f+\lambda\big)\big(\partial_{b}H_{t}\cdot(Y_{\lambda}-tX_{f})\big)=0

yields

\big(t\mathrm{d}_{V}\!f+\lambda\big)(V_{t})=\lambda(X_{f})+\mathrm{d}_{V}\!H_{t}\big(Y_{\lambda}-tX_{f}\big)\,.

Furthermore

\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}(V_{t})=\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}(X_{f})-\mathrm{d}_{V}\!H_{t}\big(X_{f_{\pm}}\big)+\partial_{b}H_{t}\cdot\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}\big(Y_{\lambda}-tX_{f}\big)\,,

so that the desired right hand side

\big(t\mathrm{d}_{V}\!f+\lambda\big)(V_{t})+\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}(V_{t})

is equal to

\big(\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}+\lambda\big)(X_{f})+\big(\partial_{b}H_{t}\cdot\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}+\mathrm{d}_{V}\!H_{t}\big)\big(Y_{\lambda}-tX_{f}-X_{f_{\pm}}\big)

as
\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}
annihilates
X_{f_{\pm}}
.
Similarly, as

\big(\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}+\lambda\big)(X_{f})=\mathrm{d}_{V}\!f\big(Y_{\lambda}-X_{f_{\pm}}\big)
,
we get

\big(\mathrm{d}_{V}\!f+\partial_{b}H_{t}\cdot\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}+\mathrm{d}_{V}\!H_{t}\big)\big(Y_{\lambda}-tX_{f}-X_{f_{\pm}}\big)\,.

In order to evaluate along
S^{+}=\{b=f_{+}\}
we set

h_{t}^{\pm}(v):=H_{t}\big(f_{\pm}(v),v\big)

for all
v\in V_{S}
.
By chain rule

\mathrm{d}h_{t}^{\pm}=\partial_{b}H_{t}\cdot\mathrm{d}f_{\pm}+\mathrm{d}_{V}\!H_{t}\,,

so that the boundary condition becomes

h_{t}^{\pm}=\big(\mathrm{d}_{V}\!f+\mathrm{d}h_{t}^{\pm}\big)\big(Y_{\lambda}-tX_{f}-X_{f_{\pm}}\big)

on
V_{S}
finally.

The converse is also true.

4.6.
Method of characteristics

For each
t\in[0,1]
denote by

\psi_{s}\equiv\psi_{s}^{t}
the local flow of the Liouville vector field

Y_{\lambda}-X_{f_{\pm}+t(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})}

treating each of the signs
\pm
individually.
In view of a solution
h\equiv h_{t}^{\pm}

of the characteristic equation for the boundary condition
in Proposition
4.5.2

we make the following remarks:

Evaluating

\psi_{s}^{*}h=\Big(\psi_{s}^{*}\big(h+(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})\big)\Big)^{\prime}

at
s=0
, where
(\ldots)^{\prime}
stands for
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}
,
yields

h=L_{(Y_{\lambda}-X_{f_{\pm}+t(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})})}\big(h+(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})\big)\,,

which is the characteristic equation for the boundary condition
in Proposition
4.5.2
.
Observe that at equilibrium points a solution
h
vanishes.
Further, variation of the constant yields

\psi_{s}^{*}h=\mathrm{e}^{s}\left(h-\int_{0}^{s}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma}\big(\psi_{\sigma}^{*}(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})\big)^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\,,

which by chain rule leads back to the above characteristic equation.
In other words,

h
is a solution if and only if
along each flow line
c(s)\equiv c_{t}^{\pm}(s)=\psi_{s}(v)

for each initial point
v\in V_{S}
we have

h\big(c(s)\big)=\mathrm{e}^{s}\left(h(v)-\int_{0}^{s}\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma}\mathrm{d}(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})\big(c^{\prime}(\sigma)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\,.

The integral along any flow line
c

contained in the open subset

U_{0}:=V_{S}\setminus\supp\!\big(\mathrm{d}(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})\big)

of
V_{S}
vanishes.
Consequently,

h
growths exponentially in forward time
s

along each flow line
c
in
U_{0}
.

In order to achieve compact support for
h

we set
h(v)=0
for all
v\in U_{\partial}
,
where

U_{\partial}:=V_{S}\setminus\supp\,(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})\,.

Notice, that the closure of
U_{\partial}

is a neighbourhood of
\partial V_{S}
and
that the set of points in
V_{S}

through which there passes a flow line
c

entirely contained in
\supp\,(g_{\pm}-f_{\pm})

contains no interior point.
This follows from exponential
\mathrm{d}\lambda
-volume growth
along the Liouville flow
\psi_{s}
in forward time
s
.

We continue the discussion from Section

5.3.
Half characteristics dynamic

The flow of the Liouville vector field
Y_{\lambda}
on
T^{*}Q

is given by
(s,u)\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{s}u
.
Recall,

X_{\varrho}
integrates the cogeodesic flow
\gamma_{s}

on
T^{*}Q
,
cf.
[
7
, p. 27-31]
.
Both flows can be used to express
the flow
\psi_{s}
of the vector field
Y_{\lambda}-X_{f}

on the interior of
D_{r_{0}}T^{*}Q
.
We claim that

\psi_{s}(u)=\mathrm{e}^{s}\gamma_{\sigma_{u}(s)}(u)

for all
u\in T^{*}Q
,
|u|<r_{0}
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, where

\sigma_{u}(s):=\int_{s}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\sigma}\hat{f}^{\prime}\big(\varrho(\mathrm{e}^{\sigma}u)\big)\mathrm{d}\sigma\,.

Indeed,
an application of

i_{[X,Y]}=L_{X}i_{Y}-i_{Y}L_{X}
, see
[
13
, Proposition V.5.3]
,
to
\mathrm{d}\lambda

using
\lambda(X_{\varrho})=Y_{\lambda}(\varrho)=2\varrho

yields the
\mathrm{d}\lambda
-dual of the equation

[Y_{\lambda},X_{\varrho}]=X_{\varrho}
,
which in turn shows that the pull back of
X_{\varrho}

along the flow of
-Y_{\lambda}
equals
\mathrm{e}^{-s}X_{\varrho}
.
Pretending for the moment
that
\psi_{s}
just abbreviates
the right hand side of the claimed equation
we get that
\psi_{0}=\id
and

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\psi_{s}=Y_{\lambda}|_{\psi_{s}}+\sigma^{\prime}_{(\,.\,)}(s)\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-s}X_{\varrho}|_{\psi_{s}}
.
As the cogeodesic flow
\gamma_{\sigma}

preserves the kinetic energy
\varrho

and, hence,

\varrho(\mathrm{e}^{\sigma}\,.\,)=\varrho(\psi_{s})
holds,
computing
\sigma^{\prime}
finally shows
that
\psi_{s}
is indeed the flow of
Y_{\lambda}-X_{f}
.

Observe that

\mathrm{e}^{a}\gamma_{\sigma}\circ\mathrm{e}^{-a}=\gamma_{\mathrm{e}^{-a}\sigma}

holds for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
as the conjugated flow of
\gamma_{\sigma}

is the flow of
\mathrm{e}^{-a}X_{\varrho}
.
Therefore,
we can write

\mathrm{e}^{s-a}\gamma_{\mathrm{e}^{-a}\sigma_{u}(s)}(\mathrm{e}^{a}u)

for
\psi_{s}(u)
,
so that with
\mathrm{e}^{a}=1/|u|
we get

\psi_{s}(u)=\mathrm{e}^{s}|u|\cdot\gamma_{|u|\sigma_{u}(s)}\left(\frac{u}{|u|}\right)

for all
u\neq 0
with
|u|<r_{0}
.

5.4.
Oriented length

For given
u\in T^{*}Q
of colength
|u|\in(0,r_{0})

consider the curve
s\mapsto\pi\big(\psi_{s}(u)\big)
in
Q

obtained by foot point projection.
By Section
5.3

the curve reparametrises a geodesic arc in
Q
,
to which the metric dual of
u
is tangent to.
Here,
we allow the reparametrisation to go forward and backward in time.
We will compute the oriented length
\ell_{u}(s)

for time parameters between
0
and
s
,
where the forward/backward oriented arcs
contribute positively/negatively.
With the arguments of
[
18
, Section 3.4]

the action

a_{u}(s)=\int_{s}^{0}U^{*}\lambda

of the lift
U(s)=\gamma_{\sigma_{u}(s)}(u)

to
S_{|u|}T^{*}Q

is the area
a_{u}(s)=|u|\ell_{u}(s)

of the surface obtained by convex interpolation
with
s\mapsto\pi\big(\psi_{s}(u)\big)
.

The curve
s\mapsto\pi\big(\psi_{s}(u)\big)

alternatively can be obtained from

s\longmapsto\pi\circ\gamma_{|u|\sigma_{u}(s)}\left(\frac{u}{|u|}\right)\,.

Fixing
u
and
s
the oriented length of
the rescaled curve

[0,1]\ni\tau\longmapsto\pi\circ\gamma_{|u|\sigma_{u}(s\cdot\tau)}\left(\frac{u}{|u|}\right)

is equal to
\ell_{u}(s)
.
Removing consecutive subarcs of the same length
but with opposite sign
induces a retraction relative end points
to the rescaled geodesic arc

[0,1]\ni\tau\longmapsto\pi\circ\gamma_{|u|\sigma_{u}(s)\cdot\tau}\left(\frac{u}{|u|}\right)

keeping the oriented length.
The retraction can be defined
by convex interpolation of the parameter functions

|u|\sigma_{u}(s\cdot\tau)
and
|u|\sigma_{u}(s)\cdot\tau

and stays inside the trace of the geodesic defined by
u
.
An application of Stokes theorem
to the definition via the action yields the result.
Hence,

\ell_{u}(s)=|u|\sigma_{u}(s)\,.

Moreover,
the proof of the root Lemma
2.1.1
shows that

\hat{f}^{\prime}\big(\varrho(u)\big)=2\tilde{f}^{\prime}(r^{2})=\frac{1}{r}f^{\prime}(r)

with
r=|u|
for all
u\in T^{*}Q
with
|u|\in(0,r_{0})
,
so that

\ell_{u}(s)=\int_{s}^{0}f^{\prime}\big(\mathrm{e}^{\sigma}|u|\big)\mathrm{d}\sigma

is the desired oriented length
\ell_{u}(s)
.

5.6.
Normalised cogeodesic twists

The normalised cogeodesic flow

on the deleted cotangent bundle

of the length function

,

The generating Hamiltonian vector field satisfies

Choose

and constantly equal to

\Theta
is constant near

.

Define the

As

.

.

6.3.
Reeb twists

As in Section
5.6

consider the Reeb flow
\eta_{\theta}
of
(M,\alpha)
,
which satisfies

\eta_{\theta}^{*}\alpha=\alpha
.
Take a smooth function

such that
\theta
is constant
near

\theta(a)_{1/0}=\theta_{1/0}

The linearisation is computed to be

Then

close to

sends any
\mathbb{R}
-symmetric shape

with

\mathbb{R}
-symmetric shape

preserving the equator
\{a_{1},a_{0}\}\times M
,
where

.
The characteristic action difference is

Remark 6.4.1
.

The inverse of the strict contactomorphism
\kappa_{S}

allows an identification of
S
with the mapping torus

S(\kappa_{S}^{-1})
.
Indeed,
following the
M
-invarinat characteristic flow lines
starting on a component of the equator
yields a diffeomorphism onto the quotient
of
\mathbb{R}\times M
by the action

(t,m)\mapsto\big(t-1,\kappa_{S}^{-1}(m)\big)
.
Characteristics will correspond to the curves

t\mapsto[(t,m)]
.
In particular,

S
cannot be Giroux-convex with respect to
\xi_{\alpha}
.

Otherwise,
we would find a smooth function on
S
,
whose non-empty zero set

is positively
transverse to
S_{\xi_{\alpha}}
,
see Remark
3.5.1
.
As
\{0\}\times M
does not separate in
S(\kappa_{S}^{-1})

by an open-closed argument
\Gamma
cannot be contained
in
\{0\}\times M
.
Hence,
the set
A_{k}=(\kappa_{S}^{k})(A_{0})
of all
m\in M

such that there exists
t\in(k,k+1)
with
(t,m)\in\Gamma

for given integer

is non-empty and open.
As each characteristic intersects
\Gamma

at most once the

are pairwise disjoint.
In addition,
the

are of equal
\alpha
-contact volume.
This is not possible.

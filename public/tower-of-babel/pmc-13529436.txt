Phase 3 Randomized Trial Results of DTX401 AAV Gene Therapy for the Treatment of GSDIa

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTGlycogen storage disease type Ia (GSDIa) is a rare, life‐threatening inherited carbohydrate metabolism disorder caused by biallelic pathogenic G6PC gene variants resulting in deficiency of glucose‐6‐phosphatase. DTX401 is an investigational AAV8 vector containing the human G6PC gene. DTX401‐CL301 is a pivotal, phase 3, double‐blind, randomized, placebo‐controlled trial of DTX401 in patients ≥ 8 years with GSDIa. The primary endpoint was percent change from Baseline to Week 48 in daily cornstarch intake for the DTX401 versus placebo group. Participants were randomly assigned (1:1) to blinded DTX401 or placebo. After the 48‐week Primary Efficacy Analysis Period (PEAP), participants crossed over in a blinded manner for an additional 48‐week blinded Crossover Period. Following randomization: 21 participants received DTX401; 25 received placebo. At Week 48, DTX401 treatment resulted in a statistically significant least squares (LS) mean (SE) daily cornstarch intake reduction of 41% (4.6) versus 10% (4.1) for Placebo (p < 0.0001). Clinical meaningfulness was evidenced by a mean desired percent reduction in daily cornstarch intake among those reporting in Baseline interviews (n = 33) of 45% (median = 41%). Greater and faster reductions in cornstarch were observed at Week 96 for the Crossover DTX401 group compared with the DTX401 group in the PEAP. The DTX401 safety profile was acceptable, and expected hepatic reactions consisting of transaminase elevations were managed with prophylactic corticosteroids. Treatment with DTX401 resulted in both a statistically significant and clinically meaningful reduction in cornstarch intake in the 48‐week PEAP versus placebo, with greater cornstarch reductions in both groups at Week 96.Keywords: AAV8, DTX401, gene therapy, GSDIa

Introduction

Glycogen storage disease type Ia (GSDIa) is a rare, life‐threatening condition caused by biallelic pathogenic variants of the G6PC gene that encodes the catalytic enzyme glucose‐6‐phosphatase (G6Pase) [1, 2]. Patients with GSDIa have deficient G6Pase activity and minimal ability to produce glucose from glycogenolysis or gluconeogenesis, resulting in severe fasting intolerance. Accumulation of glucose‐6‐phosphate in the liver leads to elevations of lactate, triglycerides, uric acid, and is associated with chronic complications of the liver, kidneys, and impaired physical growth and delayed puberty [3, 4, 5, 6].

Management of GSDIa attempts to prevent hypoglycemia and maximize euglycemia to minimize acute and long‐term metabolic complications and improve quality of life [7]. Current standard of care consists of uncooked cornstarch consumption up to every 3–5 h, day and night, with overnight continuous pump feeding in some cases, a medically prescribed diet high in complex carbohydrates, often a restriction of (ga)lactose/fructose, frequent glucose level monitoring, and avoidance of fasting [6, 8, 9, 10].

Current management does not address the underlying cause of GSDIa. People with GSDIa continue to have serious hypoglycemic events and chronic morbidities. Incessant daily treatment and fear of severe hypoglycemia contribute to high disease burden, including entire family impacts due to overnight cornstarch administration requirements [11, 12, 13, 14]. DTX401 (pariglasgene brecaparvovec) is a recently FDA‐approved therapy utilizing adeno‐associated virus serotype 8 (AAV8) to deliver the human G6PC gene to hepatocytes under control of the native promoter. A Phase 1/2 DTX401 trial showed substantial long‐term cornstarch reductions in people with GSDIa [15, 16]. This manuscript reports the results from the DTX401 Phase 3 study.

Methods

DTX401‐CL301 (GlucoGene Study; Clinicaltrials.gov Identifier: NCT05139316) is a multinational, pivotal, Phase 3, randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of DTX401 in people with GSDIa. All participants were nutritionally optimized and stabilized on a therapeutic cornstarch regimen during the screening period. To assess appropriateness of cornstarch changes following optimization and blinded treatment, the ability to maintain glycemic control was assessed for statistical non‐inferiority. Eligible participants were ≥ 8 years old with molecularly confirmed GSDIa, had no detectable AAV8 antibodies, and no history of specific hepatic conditions/dysfunction. Full eligibility criteria can be found in the clinical study protocol.

Eligible participants were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive blinded DTX401 (1.0 × 1013 genome copies [GC]/kg) or placebo. Participants received a prophylactic blinded immunomodulatory oral prednisolone or placebo‐corticosteroid regimen starting at Study Day 15 until Week (W) 4, followed by a slow taper over at least 4 weeks (Supplement). A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) was worn and blinded to participants until W96. Investigators reviewed self‐monitored blood glucose (SMBG) and CGM data to consider modifications to dietary and cornstarch prescriptions.

The Primary Efficacy Analysis Period (PEAP) encompassed Study Day 1 to W48. At W48, eligible participants crossed over in a blinded manner to alternative treatment (Placebo/DTX401) and received a second course of prednisolone/placebo‐corticosteroid. Cornstarch was titrated by the investigator, with intake monitored using a participant‐reported electronic diary (eDiary). Glycemic control was assessed by SMBG, CGM, and time to hypoglycemia (TTH) in controlled fasting challenges (CFCs, Supplement). To account for expected cornstarch changes post‐DTX401, a pre‐specified analysis was conducted (in participants taking cornstarch to start their CFC) by normalizing TTH to the amount of cornstarch consumed at CFC start. Baseline interviews captured treatment expectations, and participant experience was assessed using the Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC). Safety was monitored throughout the study (03 March 2025 data cutoff).

The research protocol was approved by relevant institutional review boards/ethics committees, and all participants provided written informed consent before any study‐specific procedures. The trial sponsor, Ultragenyx, was responsible for the funding, design, conduct, analysis, and reporting of the trial. Reasonable efforts will be made to address all clinical data requests for deidentified participant data for legitimate medical/scientific research purposes from qualified researchers in the interest of improving patient care and helping advance medical science; contact clinicaldatarequest@ultragenyx.com for more information.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was percent change from Baseline to W48 in daily cornstarch intake (total cornstarch in a 24‐h period reported via eDiary by participant) for the DTX401 group versus the Placebo group. Secondary endpoints included change from Baseline to W48 in number of daily cornstarch doses, change from Baseline to W48 in percentage of hypoglycemia values < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) assessed for non‐inferiority by CGM, PGIC in GSDIa score (since start of study to W48), change from Baseline to W48 in TTH (< 54 mg/dL [3.0 mmol/L]) during the CFC, change from Baseline to W48 in percentage of glucose values in the 70–120 mg/dL (3.9–6.7 mmol/L) range, and safety. Additional clinically relevant assessments were evaluated over time.

Statistical Considerations

A sample size of 50 participants was planned to provide approximately 91% power to detect a difference between the DTX401 and Placebo groups at W48 using mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) at 0.05 alpha, assuming minimum average cornstarch reduction ≥ 50% in the DTX401 group versus a maximum average cornstarch reduction ≤ 10% in the Placebo group at W48, with a common SD of 40%, a correlation coefficient of 0.4 between assessments, and a discontinuation rate of 10%. Assumptions included in the sample size calculations are based on the results observed in the DTX401 Phase 1/2 study [15, 16].

For continuous efficacy endpoints, MMRM, based on all observed data from W24 through W48, was the primary analysis method to compare treatment effect at W48. The MMRM included fixed treatment group effects, visit, and treatment group‐by‐visit interaction, with stratification factors and baseline measurements as covariates. The estimation of the W48 contrast (DTX401 versus Placebo) was provided by the Least Squares (LS) mean, the 95% confidence interval of the estimated difference between DTX401 and Placebo, and the 2‐sided p‐value for the comparison between DTX401 and Placebo. The Modified Intention‐To‐Treat (mITT) Analysis Set (n = 44) consisted of all randomized participants who received any investigational product (IP; DTX401/Placebo), including those who received a partial IP dose and provided ≥ 1 post‐dose primary efficacy assessment (Figure 1). The Safety Analysis Set (n = 46) included all participants treated with any amount of IP. The full Statistical Analysis Plan is provided.

Efficacy

Cornstarch

At W48, DTX401 treatment resulted in a statistically significant LS Mean (SE) daily cornstarch intake reduction of 41% (4.6) versus a reduction of 10% (4.1) for Placebo (LS mean difference [SE]: −31% [5.7]; p < 0.0001). Cornstarch reduction clinical meaningfulness was evidenced by patient experience data where, during baseline trial interviews, the most frequently reported treatment expectation was to reduce or eliminate cornstarch use; the mean desired cornstarch intake reduction considered meaningful (for the sample reporting at baseline; [n = 33]) was 45% (median = 41%; IQR = 25%–66%). At W48, 83% (n = 10/12) of DTX401‐treated participants met or exceeded their own Baseline expectations for desired meaningful cornstarch intake reduction (eFigure 1). The mean reduction in cornstarch intake among all participants who met or exceeded their own Baseline desired expectation for cornstarch reduction at Week 48 was 34% and 52%, respectively [17]. Observed mean (±SE) percent change in daily cornstarch intake over time is shown (Figure 2). Greater and faster reductions in cornstarch were observed in the Crossover Period at W96 for the Crossover‐DTX401 (Placebo → DTX401 at W48) group versus the DTX401 group in the PEAP (to W48), with continued reductions in cornstarch for the DTX401 group at W96.

At W48, one participant (4%) in the Placebo group had a ≥ 50% daily cornstarch intake reduction versus seven participants (37%) in the DTX401 group (p = 0.0038). At W96, 48 weeks after treatment with DTX401 in the Crossover‐DTX401 group, 13 participants (72%) had a ≥ 50% reduction, and 10 participants (67%) in the DTX401 group had a ≥ 50% reduction. Additionally, DTX401 treatment resulted in statistically significant reductions in the total number of daily cornstarch doses, nighttime cornstarch intake, and nighttime cornstarch dose frequency versus Placebo at W48 (Table 2).

At Baseline, 15 participants in each treatment group reported waking for nighttime cornstarch. At W48, among participants who provided assessments, seven participants (7/14: 50%) in the DTX401 group and one participant (1/14: 7%) in the Placebo group eliminated at least one nighttime cornstarch dose (nominal p = 0.031).

At W96, among participants who provided assessments, eight participants (8/12: 67%) in the DTX401 group and eight participants (8/12: 67%) in the Crossover‐DTX401 group eliminated at least one nighttime cornstarch dose. At W96, four DTX401 (33%) and five Crossover‐DTX401 (42%) participants completely stopped nighttime cornstarch. Additional cornstarch analyses are presented in the Supplement.

DTX401 resulted in statistically significant reductions from Baseline in the number of total daily cornstarch doses, with subsequent increases in daytime and nighttime dosing intervals at W48 in the DTX401 group compared with the Placebo group (eTable 2). Noncompliance with prescribed cornstarch was infrequent during the PEAP. At W48, two participants in each treatment group were reported by the Investigator to be noncompliant with prescribed cornstarch. Compliance of participant‐reported intake and prescribed cornstarch is shown (eFigure 2).

At Baseline, the mean (SE) percentage of total calories from cornstarch was 44% (3.4) in the Placebo group and 50% (3.2) in the DTX401 group. Following DTX401, cornstarch calories were replaced by a food‐based diet containing protein, fat, and carbohydrates (eTable 3 and eFigure 3).

Glycemic Control

At W48, statistical noninferiority of DTX401 versus Placebo was met for the change in percentage of glucose values < 70 mg/dL (10% pre‐defined margin; LS mean difference [SE]: 3.05% [1.21]; 95% CI: 0.63, 5.47; p < 0.0001) and change in percentage of glucose values in the 70–120 mg/dL range (15% pre‐defined margin; LS mean difference [SE]: 6.03% [3.79]; 95% CI: −1.47, 13.52; p < 0.0001); superiority of DTX401 over Placebo was not demonstrated. Likewise, severe hypoglycemia (< 54 mg/dL) remained low across all groups through W96. Observed mean percentage of blinded CGM values in each glycemic range during 4 weeks prior to the visit are shown (Figure 3).

Fasting Tolerance

At Baseline, mean (SE) TTH was 6.2 (0.6) hours in the Placebo group and 7.1 (0.9) hours in the DTX401 group. At W48, TTH increased more after DTX401 administration versus Placebo, but the difference was not statistically significant (LS mean difference [SE]: 0.61 [1.13] hours; p = 0.590). In the Crossover Period, improved trends in TTH were observed for the DTX401 and Crossover‐DTX401 groups (Table 3).

At W48, TTH in the CFC, normalized by the amount of cornstarch used immediately before fasting, was significantly longer in the DTX401 group versus the Placebo group (LS mean difference [SE]: 9.92 [4.32]; p = 0.0269). In the Crossover Period, normalized TTH continued to increase from W48 to W96 for the DTX401 and Crossover‐DTX401 groups.

Participant‐Reported Outcomes

At W48, the median PGIC score was 1.0 (minimally improved) for the Placebo group and 2.0 (moderately improved) for the DTX401 group. In the Placebo group, 52% (n = 12/23) reported improved GSDIa (collapsed across response categories of minimally improved, moderately improved, much improved) at W48 versus 79% (n = 15/19) in the DTX401 group. No significant difference was observed between treatment groups at W48 (LS mean [SE] Placebo = 1.0 [0.3] versus DTX401 = 1.4 [0.3]; LS Mean Difference [SE]: 0.4 [0.4]; MMRM p = 0.318; Wilcoxon Rank Sum Test, p = 0.131).

At W96 in the Crossover Period (48 weeks after DTX401 treatment in the Crossover‐DTX401 group), the median PGIC score was 2.0 for the Crossover‐DTX401 group (n = 19), and 95% (n = 18/19) reported improved GSDIa on the PGIC. At W96, the median PGIC score was 1.0 in the DTX401 group, and 83% (n = 10/12) reported improved GSDIa on the PGIC.

The average reduction in daily cornstarch intake at W48 for the total sample (i.e., Placebo and DTX401 groups combined) was 34% for participants reporting moderately/much improved GSDIa (n = 18, collapsed response categories) on the PGIC. At Week 96, the average reduction in daily cornstarch intake for the total sample was 68% for participants reporting moderately/much improved GSDIa (n = 17, collapsed response categories) on the PGIC and 63% for participants reporting any level of improvement in GSDIa (n = 26, collapsed response categories) on the PGIC.

Safety

Treatment‐emergent adverse events were consistent across the PEAP and Crossover Period; all 40 (100%) DTX401‐treated participants experienced ≥ 1 TEAE. The most frequently reported TEAEs assessed as DTX401‐related were transient liver transaminase elevations, with most categorized as nonserious and mild (Grade 1) to moderate (Grade 2) in severity; these occurred 4–12 weeks post‐DTX401 administration (eTable 6).

There were four serious TEAEs considered DTX401‐related by the Investigator: one event of hyperlactacidemia during the corticosteroid taper considered related to both DTX401 and prednisolone, two infusion‐related reactions (IRRs), and one event of hypertriglyceridemia. The two serious IRRs resulted in immediate termination of the DTX401 infusion. Symptoms included chest pain, a single episode of vomiting, tachycardia, and urticarial rash in one participant, and coughing, difficulty breathing (with a decrease in oxygen saturation), and reticular rash in the other participant. Hypotension was not reported in either participant, and while considered, epinephrine was not administered to either participant. Both events resolved with symptomatic treatment, including antihistamines, antiemetics, and corticosteroids.

During the PEAP, there were three serious, severe (Grade 3) TEAEs in two participants in the DTX401 group that were considered related to prednisolone treatment. These events included the TEAE of hyperlactacidemia previously described and two events of adrenal insufficiency. There was also one serious Grade 2 TEAE of lactic acidosis considered related to prednisolone treatment. Adrenal insufficiency risk minimization measures were implemented in the study, including increased monitoring and a slower corticosteroid taper, after which there were no further serious adrenal insufficiency TEAEs. There were no sponsor‐assessed TEAEs indicative of AAV gene therapy class effects of dorsal root ganglion and peripheral nerve effects, malignancy, or thrombotic microangiopathy.

TEAEs of hypertriglyceridemia (Any Grade) were observed in 20 DTX401‐treated participants (50%); most were mild (Grade 1) or moderate (Grade 2) in severity and considered unrelated to DTX401 treatment by the Investigator. Three participants (16%) in the Crossover‐DTX401 group experienced serious TEAEs of hypertriglyceridemia that were considered study participation related, including one event that was also considered DTX401‐related by the Investigator; these three events began 152, 231, and 254 days after DTX401 administration, and were associated with peak triglyceride levels of 1680 mg/dL, 1337 mg/dL, and 3765 mg/dL, respectively. None of the participants with elevated triglycerides reported TEAEs of pancreatitis or clinical sequelae of atherosclerotic disease. Changes from baseline in other metabolic parameters, including lactate, alanine, and uric acid, were not significantly different between treatment groups at W48 (eTable 7). In addition, reported GSDIa medical history and TEAEs of hepatic and renal related complications are shown (eTable 8).

Discussion

Statistically significant (p < 0.0001) and clinically meaningful reductions from Baseline in daily cornstarch intake were observed in the DTX401 group versus the Placebo group. Reductions in daily cornstarch intake were accompanied by statistically significant reductions in cornstarch dose frequency and nighttime cornstarch intake, which was particularly meaningful since both participants and caregivers may be hesitant to alter nighttime cornstarch doses due to fear of severe overnight hypoglycemic events [18]. In addition to reducing cornstarch burden, DTX401 enabled changes from a daily diet in which up to 50% of total caloric intake was provided by cornstarch to a more balanced, food‐based diet consistent with the US Dietary Guidelines for the general population (eTable 1) [19].

Reductions in cornstarch were appropriate post‐DTX401, as evidenced by maintained glycemic control and improved fasting tolerance when assessed by normalized TTH. As DTX401 retains the native G6PC promoter and enhancer elements, expression of G6Pase is triggered by counterregulatory hormones as glucose falls below 60–70 mg/dL [20, 21, 22]. Thus, the absence of significant changes from baseline in the occurrence of hypoglycemia < 54 mg/dL and increased time taken from a glucose level of 70 mg/dL to < 54 mg/dL in the overnight CFCs were also important findings in the setting of a statistically significant 41% cornstarch reduction at W48 in the DTX401 treated group versus placebo and the 61% cornstarch reductions observed in both groups at W96.

Double‐blind treatment assignment and CGM device blinding to participants challenged disease management, and the full magnitude of the DTX401 treatment effect was not evident during the PEAP. Fear of hypoglycemia may have contributed to caution in reducing cornstarch prescriptions during the PEAP versus greater reductions observed in the Crossover Period. After W48, participants, caregivers, and investigators knew that all participants had been provided DTX401 treatment, and experience with cornstarch weaning and dietary modifications led to continued cornstarch reductions in both treatment groups to W96.

In GSDIa, avoiding hyperglycemia (> 120 mg/dL) is recommended to prevent excess glycogen storage, steatosis, weight changes, and hyperinsulinism that normally inhibits G6Pase; however, there is no consensus among caregivers on the optimal upper limit of blood glucose concentration. In the PEAP, glucose levels > 120 mg/dL were common in both the Placebo and DTX401 groups. In the Crossover Period, glucose levels > 120 mg/dL were reduced, and improved trends in euglycemia occurred in both groups. Increasing experience in the management of GSDIa post‐DTX401 empowered investigators and participants to make appropriate adjustments to cornstarch and diet during the Crossover Period.

Results from patient‐based research (PGIC, anchor‐based analyses, trial interviews with patients) indicate that reductions in cornstarch observed during the Phase 3 trial, both during the PEAP and follow‐up time period, are meaningful to patients. While the Phase 3 study was not powered on the PGIC endpoint, a high proportion of DTX401‐treated participants reported improved GSDIa on the PGIC that was sustained over time and aligned with the Phase 1/2 study findings [15, 23]. Results from anchor‐based analyses using the PGIC demonstrate that the magnitude of cornstarch reduction observed in the DTX401 group at W48 reflects improved GSDIa from the patient perspective, a finding reinforced by the additional cornstarch intake reductions observed during the follow‐up period.

Phase 3 study PGIC results also aligned with findings from the W48 and W96 study interviews that found substantial reduction in burden and improved health‐related quality of life following DTX401 treatment [24]. Even though cornstarch intake was substantially reduced, 16 of 38 (42%) participants interviewed at W96 spontaneously described an improved ability to self‐regulate their blood sugar, and 32 of 38 (84%) described improved overall quality of life [25, 26].

DTX401 was generally well‐tolerated, with an acceptable safety profile. Important identified risks of DTX401 include hepatic reactions and IRRs that were effectively managed with a prophylactic tapering corticosteroid regimen and a slow staged infusion with premedication, respectively. After a protocol amendment to implement IRR risk minimization measures, no additional IRR events were reported or identified [27].

Hypertriglyceridemia was observed in 50% of participants following DTX401 administration (and cornstarch reductions) and is a potential risk of DTX401. Changes in other metabolic parameters (pre‐CFC lactate, uric acid, alanine) did not show significant differences or similar trends to triglycerides. Hypertriglyceridemia is commonly observed in GSDIa and its pathophysiology is complex and often challenging to manage. Etiology is yet to be fully understood in GSDIa but may involve increased hepatic triglyceride synthesis and/or reduced catabolism of triglyceride‐rich VLDL particles [28, 29].

Management of GSDIa is challenging; both undertreatment and overtreatment with cornstarch and/or dietary carbohydrates are common and can lead to glycemic and/or metabolic abnormalities. Management of GSDIa was further complicated in the blinded trial setting. As participants became less dependent on cornstarch, proactive compensatory dietary readjustments were important for nutritional balance. Observations from nutritional management best practices post‐DTX401, including during and after the immunomodulatory regimen, will be important to avoid undertreatment, overtreatment, metabolic abnormalities including hypertriglyceridemia, and optimizing outcomes.

Study DTX401‐CL301 provides substantial evidence of DTX401 efficacy and safety in the treatment of people with GSDIa. DTX401 resulted in statistically significant and clinically meaningful cornstarch reductions without compromising glycemic control, as evidenced by maintained low hypoglycemia rates and improved fasting tolerance. Greater treatment effects were observed in the Crossover Period, with improved euglycemia and greater reductions in cornstarch after all participants received DTX401. Monitoring of nutritional intake, glycemic control, metabolic control, and disease‐related complications, overseen by a multidisciplinary care team, will continue to be important after DTX401 administration. DTX401 has an acceptable and manageable safety profile, and long‐term efficacy and safety will be evaluated in the GSDIa Disease Monitoring Program. DTX401 is the first‐ever disease‐modifying therapy that targets the underlying defect of GSDIa. Delivery of the G6PC gene to hepatocytes resulted in clinically meaningful changes in disease management and improved quality of life in people with GSDIa.

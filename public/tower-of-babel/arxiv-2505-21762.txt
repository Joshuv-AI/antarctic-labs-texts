Convergence of linear solutions through convergence of periodic initial data
Harrison Gaebler, Wesley R. Perkins

Abstract
When studying the stability of T T -periodic solutions to partial differential equations, it is common to encounter subharmonic perturbations, i.e. perturbations which have a period that is an integer multiple (say n n ) of the background wave, and localized perturbations, i.e. perturbations that are integrable on the line. Formally, we expect solutions subjected to subharmonic perturbations to converge to solutions subjected to localized perturbations as n n tends to infinity since larger n n values force the subharmonic perturbation to become more localized. In this paper, we study the convergence of solutions to linear initial value problems when subjected to subharmonic and localized perturbations. In particular, we prove the formal intuition outlined above; namely, we prove that if the subharmonic initial data converges to some localized initial datum, then the linear solutions converge.



Keywords:

Periodic Waves; Subharmonic Perturbations; Localized Perturbations.

1.1
Definitions

Following Corollary 1.6 in
[

H^{m}_{\text{per}}(0,nT)

Definition 1.1
.

For each function

, we define the associated function
\tilde{g}\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R})
by
\tilde{g}(x)=g(x)

One important observation that we will make use of throughout the paper is that if

\|g\|_{W^{k,p}(0,nT)}=\|\tilde{g}\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Definition 1.2
.

Let

At this point, we make a few remarks on the above corollary. First, the above analysis was not unique and could trivially be carried out for similar equations where such localized and subharmonic results have been established; for example, systems of reaction diffusion equations
[
10
]
. Second, the above result heavily relied on the detailed and delicate analysis contained in
[
8
,
9
]
. In turn, this means that it heavily relied on the assumption that
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{4}(\mathbb{R})}
was sufficiently small enough to guarantee all of the necessary assumptions were met in order to use the previous results.

It is natural to question whether the norm convergence over a period of the initial data is sufficient to show the nonlinear solutions strongly converge over a period when such previous results do not exist or when the initial data is not necessarily small. In the case of the former, one would likely need to do a detailed study of the nonlinear asymptotic behavior of the solutions, which is highly nontrivial. In the case of the latter, one would likely have to assume that the solutions exist (in the absence of previous results) and then use the structure of the nonlinear PDE itself to show convergence of the solutions. As a partial step in that direction, we establish the following general result for the linear evolution of a convergent sequence of initial data.

and

(2.4)

v(x,t)=e^{tA}g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{T}}^{\frac{\pi}{T}}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(e^{tA}g)(\xi,x)d\xi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{T}}^{\frac{\pi}{T}}e^{i\xi x}e^{tA_{\xi}}\mathcal{B}(g)(\xi,x)d\xi.

Similarly, we may rewrite the solution to (
1.16
) as

w_{n}(x,t)=e^{tA}\tilde{g}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\frac{-\pi}{T}}^{\frac{\pi}{T}}e^{i\xi x}e^{tA_{\xi}}\mathcal{B}(\tilde{g}_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi.

These representations will be fundamental to helping us understand the convergence of the linear solutions over a period. As mentioned above, the fact that (
2.3
) resembles a Riemann sum approximation of (
2.4
) will be key to our analysis.

2.2
Preparatory Lemmas

In order to prove Theorem
1.7
, we will make use of the following three short lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
\mathcal{B}_{T}(v_{n})(\xi,x)=\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)
for each
\xi\in\Omega_{n}
.

Proof.

Note first that
\hat{g}_{n}(y)=\int_{\frac{-nT}{2}}^{\frac{nT}{2}}e^{-ixy}g_{n}(x)dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-ixy}\tilde{g}_{n}(x)dx=\hat{\tilde{g}}_{n}(y)
so that, for each
\xi\in[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T})
, there is

\mathcal{B}_{T}(g_{n})(\xi,x)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\hat{g}_{n}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\hat{\tilde{g}}_{n}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)=\mathcal{B}(\tilde{g}_{n})(\xi,x).

Then, let
\xi\in\Omega_{n}
and observe from Assumption
1.5
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{T}(v_{n})(\xi,x)=\mathcal{B}_{T}(e^{tA_{n}}g_{n})(\xi,x)

\displaystyle=e^{tA_{n,\xi}}\mathcal{B}_{T}(g_{n})(\xi,x)

\displaystyle=e^{tA_{\xi}}\mathcal{B}(\tilde{g}_{n})(\xi,x)=\mathcal{B}(e^{tA}\tilde{g}_{n})(\xi,x)=\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)

as is required.
∎

We will also need to use the following convergence property of the Bloch transform.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
f_{n}\overset{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}{\longrightarrow}f
. Then, there exists a subsequence
(f_{n_{q_{r}}})_{r=1}^{\infty}
such that, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{B}(f_{n_{q_{r}}})(\xi,x)
converges pointwise a.e. in
\xi
to
\mathcal{B}(f)(\xi,x)
.

Proof.

Note that because
f_{n}\overset{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}{\longrightarrow}f
, there exists a subsequence
f_{n_{q}}
that converges pointwise a.e. in
x
to
f
and is itself dominated by a function
h_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, it follows from Plancharel’s theorem that

0=\lim_{q\to\infty}\|f_{n_{q}}-f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\lim_{q\to\infty}\big\|\hat{f}_{n_{q}}-\hat{f}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

so, by passing to a further subsequence
f_{n_{q_{r}}}
, we have that
\hat{f}_{n_{q_{r}}}
converges pointwise a.e. in
\xi
to
\hat{f}
and is itself dominated by a function
h_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(f)(\xi,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)y}\lim_{r\to\infty}f_{n_{q_{r}}}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)y}f_{n_{q_{r}}}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\lim_{r\to\infty}\hat{f}_{n_{q_{r}}}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to\infty}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\hat{f}_{n_{q_{r}}}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathcal{B}(f_{n_{q_{r}}})(\xi,x)

for a.e.
\xi\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T}\right)
by two applications of the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Finally, we will need to use the fact that the Bloch transform is continuous in
\xi
when applied to functions of sufficiently high regularity.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>2
. Then,
\mathcal{B}(f)(\xi,x)
is continuous in
\xi
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{B}(f)(\xi,x)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\hat{f}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)
and note that
\hat{f}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
from the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma because
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. It now follows that each summand of the above series is continuous in
\xi
and it is therefore enough for us to prove that this series converges uniformly in
\xi
.

Let
\xi\in[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T})
be given and note that, for each
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that

(2l-1)\frac{\pi}{T}\leq\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}<(2l+1)\frac{\pi}{T}

from which it follows that, for
l\geq 1
, there is

0<(2l-1)\frac{\pi}{T}\leq\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\implies\frac{1}{\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\leq\frac{1}{(2l-1)\frac{\pi}{T}}

and, for
l\leq-1
, there is

\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}<(2l+1)\frac{\pi}{T}<0\implies\frac{1}{\left|\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right|}<\frac{1}{\left|(2l+1)\frac{\pi}{T}\right|}.

It therefore follows that, for
l\geq 1
and
s>0
, the summands of the series that defines
\mathcal{B}(f)(\xi,x)
can be estimated by

\displaystyle\left|e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\hat{f}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}}{\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)^{s}}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)^{s}\hat{f}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\left|(2l-1)\frac{\pi}{T}\right|^{s}}\left|\left(1+\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)^{2}\right)^{s/2}\hat{f}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\left|(2l-1)\frac{\pi}{T}\right|^{s}}\left\|(1+(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\hat{f}(\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\left|(2l-1)\frac{\pi}{T}\right|^{s}}\left\|(1+(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\hat{f}(\cdot)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|(2l-1)\frac{\pi}{T}\right|^{s}}\|f\|_{H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R})}.

The second-to-last inequality follows by the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

The last equality follows from Plancherel’s Theorem for
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
functions. In particular, let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
be the Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms, respectively. Then,

\displaystyle\left\|(1+(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\hat{f}(\cdot)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((1+(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\hat{f}(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\left\|(1+(\cdot)^{2})^{1/2}\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((1+(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\hat{f}(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\left\|(1+(\cdot)^{2})^{\nicefrac{{(s+1)}}{{2}}}\hat{f}(\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f\|_{H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R})}

There is a similar estimate for
l\leq-1
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}(f)(\xi,x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ix\frac{2\pi l}{T}}\hat{f}\left(\xi+\frac{2\pi l}{T}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|f\|_{H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{1}{\left|(2l-1)\frac{\pi}{T}\right|^{s}}+\sum_{l\leq-1}\frac{1}{\left|(2l+1)\frac{\pi}{T}\right|^{s}}\right).

When
s>1
, the Weierstrass M-test then implies that
\mathcal{B}(f)(\xi,x)
converges absolutely and uniformly on
[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T})
. Consequently,
\mathcal{B}(f)(\xi,x)
is continuous in
\xi
so long as
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>2
, as desired.
∎

3
Linear stability

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Fix
t\geq 0
. Then, let
\varepsilon>0
be given and choose
N=N(t)\in\mathbb{N}
so that

\|\tilde{g}_{n}(x)-g(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\frac{\varepsilon}{\|e^{tA}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}

for each
n\geq N
. It is straightforward to see that the desired convergence
\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)\overset{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}{\longrightarrow}v(x,t)
will be uniform with respect to
t
if the
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{tA}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is bounded and, in particular, we have for each
n\geq N
that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-v(x,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}+\|w_{n}(x,t)-v(x,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}+\|e^{tA}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|\tilde{g}_{n}(x)-g(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}+\varepsilon

so that it remains to estimate
\|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}
. Indeed, note that
w_{n}(x,t)\overset{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}{\longrightarrow}v(x,t)
so that, by passing to a subsequence if necessary as in Lemma
2.2
, it follows that
(w_{n}(x,t))_{n=1}^{\infty}
is dominated by a function
h_{1,t}(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Similarly,
(\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t))_{n=1}^{\infty}
is by hypothesis dominated by a function
h_{2,t}(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus,
(\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t))_{n=1}^{\infty}
is dominated by
h(x)=|h_{1,t}(x)|+|h_{2,t}(x)|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It therefore follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)\right|^{2}dx=0

if
\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)
converges pointwise a.e. in
x
to zero. Now, in order to establish this pointwise a.e. convergence in
x
, let
\eta>0
be given and, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
, choose a number
K_{1}=K_{1}(x)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x\in\left[-\frac{nT}{2},\frac{nT}{2}\right]
for each
n\geq K_{1}
. Then, label the members of
\Omega_{n}
as
\xi_{1}<\xi_{2}<\ldots<\xi_{n}
for each
n\geq K_{1}
, and note in this case that

\displaystyle 2\pi(\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t))

\displaystyle=2\pi(v_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{i\xi_{j}x}\mathcal{B}_{T}(v_{n})(\xi_{j},x)\Delta\xi-\int_{-\frac{\pi}{T}}^{\frac{\pi}{T}}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{i\xi_{j}x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)\Delta\xi-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\int_{-\frac{\pi}{T}}^{\xi_{1}}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi-\int_{\xi_{n}}^{\frac{\pi}{T}}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi

where we have used Lemma
2.1
. The above quantity is then equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left(e^{i\xi_{j}x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right)d\xi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+e^{i\xi_{n}x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{n},x)\Delta\xi-\int_{-\frac{\pi}{T}}^{\xi_{1}}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi-\int_{\xi_{n}}^{\frac{\pi}{T}}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi

where we note, by passing to a further subsequence if needed, that
\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)
converges pointwise a.e. in
\xi
to
\mathcal{B}(v)(\xi,x)
by Lemma
2.2
. It follows from this pointwise convergence and the fact that
\mathcal{B}(v)(\xi,x)\in L_{\xi}^{\infty}\left[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T}\right)
(by the representation
2.2
) that there exists a number
M=M(x)\geq 0
such that
|\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)|\leq M
for a.e.
\xi\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T}\right)
and for all but finitely-many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we may choose a positive integer
K_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left(\Delta\xi+\left(\xi_{1}+\frac{\pi}{T}\right)+\left(\frac{\pi}{T}-\xi_{n}\right)\right)<\frac{\eta}{M}
for each
n\geq K_{2}
and such that

\displaystyle\left|e^{i\xi_{n}x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{n},x)\Delta\xi-\int_{-\frac{\pi}{T}}^{\xi_{1}}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi-\int_{\xi_{n}}^{\frac{\pi}{T}}e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq M\left(\Delta\xi+\left(\xi_{1}+\frac{\pi}{T}\right)+\left(\frac{\pi}{T}-\xi_{n}\right)\right)<\eta.

Furthermore, it follows from Egorov’s theorem that there exists a closed (and therefore compact) subset
F\subset\left[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T}\right)
with
\mu\left(\left[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T}\right)\setminus F\right)<\frac{\eta}{M}
(here,
\mu
denotes the Lebesgue measure) such that
\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)
converges uniformly in
\xi
to
\mathcal{B}(v)(\xi,x)
on
F
. Note also that
\mathcal{B}(v)(\xi,x)
is continuous in
\xi
on
F
(and is therefore uniformly continuous in
\xi
on
F
) because
\|w_{n}(x,t)\|_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})\cap H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|e^{tA}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\|\tilde{g}_{n}(x)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}
so that
\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)
is from Lemma
2.3
continuous in
\xi
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now, note that there exist finitely-many pairwise disjoint intervals
\{I_{r}\}_{r=1}^{d}
such that
\left(\left[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T}\right)\setminus F\right)\subset\bigcup_{r=1}^{d}I_{r}
and
\sum_{r=1}^{d}\mu(I_{r})<\frac{2\eta}{M}
and, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the set

Q_{n}=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\mid[\xi_{j},\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi]\cap I_{r}\neq\emptyset\text{ for some }r\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\}.

It follows that, if
j\in Q_{n}
, then either
[\xi_{j},\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi]
is a subset of the interior of
I_{r}
for some
r\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, or
[\xi_{j},\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi]
contains at least one endpoint of
I_{r}
for some
r\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. We may therefore write
Q_{n}=Q_{n}^{1}\cup Q_{n}^{2}
where

Q_{n}^{1}=\{j\in Q_{n}\mid[\xi_{j},\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi]\text{ is a subset of the interior of }I_{r}\text{ for some }r\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\}

and where
Q_{n}^{2}=Q_{n}\setminus Q_{n}^{1}
. In particular,
|Q_{n}^{2}|\leq 4d
and we may choose a number
K_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
\frac{16Md\pi}{nT}<\eta
and so that
\left|e^{i\xi_{j}x}-e^{i\xi x}\right|<\frac{T\eta}{2\pi M}
(by the uniform continuity of
\xi\mapsto e^{i\xi x}
on
\left[-\frac{\pi}{T},\frac{\pi}{T}\right]
) for each
n\geq K_{3}
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left(e^{i\xi_{j}x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-e^{i\xi x}\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right)d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|\left(e^{i\xi_{j}x}-e^{i\xi x}\right)\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)\right|d\xi+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|e^{i\xi x}\left(\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right)\right|d\xi

where the first term above admits the upper bound

M\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|e^{i\xi_{j}x}-e^{i\xi x}\right|d\xi\leq M\frac{2\pi}{T}\frac{T\eta}{2\pi M}=\eta

for each
n\geq K_{3}
, and where the second term is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in Q_{n}}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|\left(\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right)\right|d\xi+\sum_{j\notin Q_{n}}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|\left(\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right)\right|d\xi.

In particular, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in Q_{n}}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|\left(\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right)\right|d\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in Q^{1}_{n}}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|\left(\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right)\right|d\xi+\sum_{j\in Q^{2}_{n}}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|\left(\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right)\right|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2M\sum_{r=1}^{d}\mu(I_{r})+8Md\Delta\xi<2M\frac{2\eta}{M}+8Md\frac{2\pi}{nT}=4\eta+\frac{16Md\pi}{nT}<5\eta

for each
n\geq K_{3}
. Finally, for
j\notin Q_{n}
, it follows that
\xi_{j}
and
\xi
in the integrand belong to
F
because
[\xi_{j},\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi]\cap I_{r}=\emptyset
for each
r\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, and we may choose a number
K_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
so that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\notin Q_{n}}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi_{j},x)-\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)\right|d\xi

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\eta+\sum_{j\notin Q_{n}}\int_{\xi_{j}}^{\xi_{j}+\Delta\xi}\left|\mathcal{B}(v)(\xi_{j},x)-\mathcal{B}(v)(\xi,x)\right|d\xi\leq\eta+\frac{2\pi}{T}\frac{\eta T}{2\pi}=2\eta

for each
n\geq K_{4}
by first the uniform convergence of
\mathcal{B}(w_{n})(\xi,x)
to
\mathcal{B}(v)(\xi,x)
on
F
and, second, the uniform continuity in
\xi
on
F
of
\mathcal{B}(v)(\xi,x)
. Thus, we have established that
2\pi|\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)|\leq 8\eta
for each
n\geq\max\{K_{1},K_{2},K_{3},K_{4}\}
. In particular, it follows that every subsequence of the original sequence
(\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t))_{n=1}^{\infty}
of differences has a further subsequence that converges to zero. This implies that
(\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t))_{n=1}^{\infty}
itself converges to zero and, because
x\in\mathbb{R}
was chosen arbitrarily, it now follows that
(\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t))_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges pointwise a.e. in
x
to zero, as required.
∎

4
Brief discussion

It is natural to wonder if the hypotheses of Theorem
1.7
can be weakened in some suitable way. For example, if
\tilde{g}_{n}
converges weakly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
g
, then do the solutions
\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)
converge weakly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
v(x,t)
? Indeed, for a fixed
t\geq 0
and
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we do have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\varphi,w_{n}-v\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle(e^{tA})^{*}\varphi,\tilde{g}_{n}-g\rangle=0

and, thus, it only remains to estimate the term
\langle\varphi,\tilde{v}_{n}-w_{n}\rangle
for large
n
. Unfortunately, there is no reason to expect that, for each
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\langle\varphi,\tilde{v}_{n}-w_{n}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)(\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t))dx

is small for large
n
if
\tilde{v}_{n}(x,t)-w_{n}(x,t)
does not converge pointwise a.e. in
x
to zero. In particular, we cannot hope for such pointwise a.e. in
x
convergence because this requires norm convergence of the initial data, as is demonstrated by the proof of Theorem
1.7
. It is therefore unlikely (barring the development of a different argument altogether) that the weak convergence over a period of initial data implies the weak convergence over a period of the corresponding solutions. However, we can appeal to Theorem
1.7
for a positive result concerning certain averages of the initial data. Note that if
\tilde{g}_{n}
converges weakly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
g
, then it follows from the Banach-Saks theorem that there exists a subsequence
(g_{n_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}
of the initial data such that the averages
G_{m}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{g}_{n_{j}}
converge strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
g
. In particular,
G_{m}
is supported on

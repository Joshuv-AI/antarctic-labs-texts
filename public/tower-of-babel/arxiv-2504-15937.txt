Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth curve defined over a number field
K
and
d
a positive integer. A point
P\in C
is of degree
d
over
K
if
[K(P):K]=d
. An important property of a curve
C/K
is whether it has finitely or infinitely many points of degree
d
. Faltings’ Theorem solves the case
d=1
.

Theorem 1.1

(Faltings’ Theorem)
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
C
be a non-singular curve defined over
K
of genus
g\geq 2
. Then the set
C(K)
is finite.

Therefore, the curve
C
has infinitely many
K
-rational points if and only if
C
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(
g=0
) or
C
is an elliptic curve (
g=1
) with positive
K
-rank. The next theorem solves the case
d=2
.

Theorem 1.2

(Harris, Silverman:
[
14
, Corollary 3]
)
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
C/K
be a curve of genus at least
2
. Assume that
C
is neither hyperelliptic nor bielliptic. Then the set of quadratic points on
C/K
is finite.

Definition 1.3
.

For a curve
C
defined over a field
K
, the
K
-gonality
\textup{gon}_{K}C
is the smallest integer
d
such that there exists a morphism of degree
d
from
C
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over
K
.

The question of determining whether the curve
C/K
has infinitely many degree
d
points over
K
is closely related to the
K
-gonality of
C
. For example, Frey
[
13
]
proved that if a curve
C
defined over a number field
K
has infinitely many points of degree
\leq d
over
K
, then
\textup{gon}_{K}C\leq 2d
.

The problem of determining degree
d
points is particularly interesting in the case of modular curves. Modular curves
X(\Gamma)
are a type of algebraic curves which can be constructed as quotients of the compactified upper half plane
\mathcal{H}^{*}
with
\Gamma
, a congruence subgroup of the modular group
\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Examples of modular curves are
X(N)
,
X_{0}(N)
,
X_{1}(N)
which correspond to congruence subgroups

\displaystyle\Gamma(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):a,d\equiv 1\pmod{N},\ b,c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):a,d\equiv 1\pmod{N},\ c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\}.

For every group
\Delta\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, there exists a modular curve
X_{\Delta}(N)
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. It corresponds to the congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{\Delta}(N)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):(a\textup{ mod }N)\in\Delta,\ c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\}.

Since
-I\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts trivially on the upper half plane
\mathcal{H}^{*}
, the curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
and
X_{\pm\Delta}(N)
are isomorphic. Therefore, in this paper we will always assume that
-1\in\Delta
.

For every group
\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, there exists a modular curve
X_{\Delta}(N)
. From the definition of
\Gamma_{\Delta}(N)
it follows that

X_{\{\pm 1\}}(N)=X_{\pm 1}(N)\cong X_{1}(N),\ X_{(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}(N)=X_{0}(N).

Moreover, if

\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times},

then we have natural projections

X_{1}(N)\to X_{\Delta_{1}}(N)\to X_{\Delta_{2}}(N)\to X_{0}(N)

defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
\{\pm 1\}\subsetneq\Delta\subsetneq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, then we call the curve
X_{\Delta}(N)
an intermediate modular curve (since it lies between the curves
X_{1}(N)
and
X_{0}(N)
).

It is also possible to define modular curves in another, more general way. For every group
H\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
, there exists a modular curve
X_{H}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Since
-I\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
acts trivially on
\mathcal{H}^{*}
, we may assume that
-I\in H
without loss of generality. If
H
has full determinant (that is, if
\det H=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
), the curve
X_{H}
is guaranteed to be geometrically irreducible.

Suppose that
H\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
such that
-I\in H
and that
H
has full determinant. Then there is a congruence subgroup
\Gamma
such that the curves
X_{H}
and
X(\Gamma)
are isomorphic. It is defined as follows:

H_{0}:=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})\cap H,\ \Gamma:=\{A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):(A\textup{ mod }N)\in H_{0}\}.

It is not hard to check that
\Gamma(N)\subseteq\Gamma
, therefore
\Gamma
is indeed a congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Conversely, in the case of intermediate modular curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
, its isomorphic curve
X_{H}
is defined as

H:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}):a\in\Delta,\ c=0\right\}.

We can easily see that
-I\in H
and that
H
has full determinant.

3.
Curves with infinitely many quartic points

In this section, we will prove that all curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
listed in
Theorem

1.6
have infinitely many quartic points. We will do this by finding a degree
4
rational morphism from
X_{\Delta}(N)
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
or a positive rank elliptic curve. When
X_{\Delta}(N)
has a map of degree
4
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, Hilbert’s Irreducibility Theorem
[
30

implies the existence of infinitely many quartic points on
X_{\Delta}(N)
. In the cases where we found a degree
4
map to a positive rank elliptic curve on
X_{\Delta}(N)

4.
Positive rank tetraelliptic curves

We will call the curve
C/\mathbb{Q}
positive rank tetraelliptic if it admits a degree
4
morphism to a positive rank elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
.

All intermediate curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
for
N
in
Theorem

2.1
not listed in
Theorem

1.6
have genus
g\geq 8
. Furthermore,
[
29
, Theorem 1.3]
tells us that all these curves have
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality greater than
4
. From
Corollary

2.4
it follows that it is enough to prove that these curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
do not admit a degree
4
rational morphism to a positive rank elliptic curve.

Suppose that there is a degree
4
rational morphism
f:X_{\Delta}(N)\to E
, where
E/\mathbb{Q}
is of positive rank. Since all curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
have rational cusps, we can assume that
f(P)=0
for some fixed
P\in X_{\Delta}(N)(\mathbb{Q})
(because
f
and
f-f(P)
have the same degree). Due to the universal property of the Jacobian
[
27
, Theorem 6.1]
,
f
factors through
J_{\Delta}(N)
. Therefore,
E
appears as an isogeny factor in
J_{\Delta}(N)
.

The space of modular forms
S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
decomposes into subspaces according to the nebentypus character. By definition,
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))\subseteq S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
is the subspace consisting of modular forms with trivial character
[
12
, Section 4.3]
. This implies that the simple isogeny factors of
J_{0}(N)
are exactly the simple factors of
J_{\Delta}(N)
whose associated modular form has trivial nebentypus character. Modular forms associated with elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
have trivial character
[
12
, Section 6.6]
. In particular, this means that an elliptic curve
E
appears as an isogeny factor in
J_{0}(N)
with the same multiplicity as in
J_{\Delta}(N)
. The conductor of an elliptic curve
E
is the same as the level of the associated newform, so it follows that
\textup{Cond}(E)\mid N
for all
1
-dimensional isogeny factors of
J_{\Delta}(N)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The modular curve

is not positive rank tetraelliptic for

\displaystyle N\in\{

\displaystyle 29,31,33,34,35,38,39,40,41,42,44,45,48,49,50,51,52,54,55,56,60,62,63,64,

\displaystyle 66,68,69,70,71,72,75,78,80,81,85,87,95,96,98,100,103,104,109,125,191\}.

For all these levels

with conductor dividing

. For

this is because the Jacobian

over

by
[

, Theorem 3.1]
and for

the Jacobian

is of positive rank, but its decomposition contains no elliptic curves of positive rank.
∎

It remains to study the situation when the decomposition of the Jacobian

contains positive rank elliptic curves

. We divide the discussion into two cases:
\textup{Cond }E=N
and
\textup{Cond }E<N
.

4.1.
Degree pairing and covers of curves.

In
[
9
, §2]
there is an outline of a strategy on how to determine whether a given curve
C
over
\mathbb{Q}
of genus
>1
has a map of degree
d
to an elliptic curve. The key result in this strategy is the following proposition:

Proposition 4.2

(
[
9
, Proposition 1.10]
)
.

Let
C
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with at least one rational point and
E
an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
that occurs as an isogeny factor of
J(C)
with multiplicity
n\geq 1
. Then the degree map
\deg:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C,E)\to\mathbb{Z}
can be extended to a positive definite quadratic form on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Using the above proposition, the problem of deciding whether there exists a degree
d
map
C\to E
reduces to checking whether
d
is a value of the quadratic form in
Proposition

4.2
.

The following definition gives a more precise description of this quadratic form.

Definition 4.3

(
[
9
, Definition 2.2]
)
.

Let
C,E
be curves over a field
k
with
E
being an elliptic curve. The degree pairing is defined on
\operatorname{Hom}(C,E)
as

\displaystyle\left<\rule{6.99997pt}{0.4pt},\rule{6.99997pt}{0.4pt}\right>:\operatorname{Hom}(C,E)\times\operatorname{Hom}(C,E)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{End}(J(E))

\displaystyle f,g

\displaystyle\mapsto f_{*}\circ g^{*}.

If
P\in C(k)
, then we can use the map
f_{P}:C\to J(C)
,
x\mapsto[x-P]
to define the degree pairing on
\operatorname{Hom}(J(C),E)
as

\displaystyle\left<\rule{6.99997pt}{0.4pt},\rule{6.99997pt}{0.4pt}\right>:\operatorname{Hom}(J(C),E)\times\operatorname{Hom}(J(C),E)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{End}(J(E)),

\displaystyle f,g

\displaystyle\mapsto(f\circ f_{P})_{*}\circ(g\circ f_{P})^{*}.

[
9
, Proposition 2.5]
tells us that the degree pairing on
\operatorname{Hom}(J(C),E)
is bilinear. We will also write
\left<f,g\right>:=f_{*}\circ g^{*}
for
f,g\in\operatorname{Hom}(C,C^{\prime})
(this is not a pairing when
C^{\prime}
is not elliptic since
\operatorname{Hom}(C,C^{\prime})
is not an abelian group in that case). With this notation we have
\langle f,f\rangle=[\deg f]
for
f\in\operatorname{Hom}(C,C^{\prime})

[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
.

As we have already seen in the introduction of this section, any elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
occurs with the same multiplicity in the isogeny decompositions of
J_{\Delta}(N)
and
J_{0}(N)
. In this subsection we will study more generally how the quadratic form from
Proposition

4.2
changes when
u:C\to C^{\prime}
is a map of curves over
\mathbb{Q}
and
E
occurs with the same multiplicity in
J(C)
and
J(C^{\prime})
.

The following lemmas encapsulate the key idea we will use to determine possible degrees of morphisms
X_{\Delta}(N)\to E
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
u:C\to C^{\prime}
be a map of nice curves (smooth, projective, and geometrically integral) over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
E/\mathbb{Q}
an elliptic curve that appears with multiplicity
n>0
in both
J(C)
and
J(C^{\prime})
. Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}:C^{\prime}\to E
be maps such that
f_{1,*},\ldots,f_{n,*}
form a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C^{\prime}),E)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Let
A\subseteq J(C^{\prime})
be the unique abelian subvariety isogenous to
E^{n}
and assume that
C(\mathbb{Q})\neq\emptyset
and that
u^{*}_{\restriction_{A}}:A\to J(C)
has trivial kernel. Then we have the following:

(1)

(f_{1}\circ u)_{*},\ldots,(f_{1}\circ u)_{*}
form a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E)
.

(2)

The degree pairing on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E)
is the degree pairing on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C^{\prime}),E)
multiplied by
\deg u
.

Proof.

For the first part, let
\iota:A\to J(C^{\prime})
denote the inclusion map and
\iota^{\vee}:J(C^{\prime})=J(C^{\prime})^{\vee}\to A^{\vee}
its dual. Then by assumption
u^{*}_{\restriction_{A}}=u^{*}\circ\iota:A\to J(C)
has trivial kernel and hence
u^{*}\circ\iota
induces an isomorphism between
A
and its image
J(C)
. Since
E
occurs with multiplicity
n
in
J(C)
, the image of
A
in
J(C)
is actually the unique abelian subvariety of
J(C)
isogenous to
E^{n}
. In particular, any map
f:E\to J(C)
factors as
u^{*}\circ\iota
. Dually, any map
f:J(C)\to E
can be written as
f^{\prime}\circ(u^{*}\circ\iota)^{\vee}=f^{\prime}\circ\iota^{\vee}\circ u_{*}
for some
f^{\prime}:A^{\vee}\to E
. Writing
f^{\prime}\circ\iota^{\vee}=\sum a_{i}f_{i,*}
shows that
f=(\sum a_{i}f_{i,*})\circ u_{*}=\sum a_{i}(f_{i}\circ u)_{*}
from which the first part follows.

The second part follows since if
i,j
are integers between
1
and
n
, then

\displaystyle\langle f_{i}\circ u,f_{j}\circ u\rangle:=

\displaystyle(f_{i}\circ u)_{*}\circ(f_{i}\circ u)^{*}=f_{i,*}\circ u_{*}\circ u^{*}\circ f_{j}^{*}=\ldots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{i,*}\circ[\deg u]\circ f_{j}^{*}=[\deg u]\circ f_{i,*}\circ f_{j}^{*}=[\deg u]\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
C
be a nice curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with
C(\mathbb{Q})\neq\emptyset
. Let
E,E^{\prime}
be isogenous elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
an isogeny of minimal degree. Let
n
be the multiplicity with which
E
occurs in
J(C)
and let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}:C\to E
be maps and define
\xi:=(f_{1,*},\ldots,f_{n,*}):J(C)\to E^{n}
. If
\xi^{\vee}:E^{n}\to J(C)
has trivial kernel, then

(1)

f_{1,*},\ldots,f_{n,*}
and
(\phi\circ f_{1})_{*},\ldots,(\phi\circ f_{n})_{*}
form a basis of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E)
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E^{\prime})
, respectively.

(2)

The degree pairing on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E^{\prime})
is the degree pairing on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E)
multiplied by
\deg\phi
.

Proof.

For the first part, let
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E^{\prime})
. Since
\xi^{\vee}
is injective and the multiplicity of
E
as an isogeny factor in
J(C)
is equal to
n
, we know that
f^{\vee}:E^{\prime}\to J(C)
has to factor through
\xi^{\vee}
. Dually, this means that there is an
f^{\prime}:E^{n}\to E^{\prime}
such that
f=f^{\prime}\circ\xi
. There is a canonical isomorphism
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E^{n},E^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E,E^{\prime})^{n}
sending
f^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E^{n},E^{\prime})
to its restriction on each factor of
E^{n}
. Now
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E,E^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{Z}
and is generated as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module by
\phi
. In particular, the restriction of
f^{\prime}
to each factor of
E^{n}
factors through
\phi
, implying that
f^{\prime}
has to factor as
\phi\circ f^{\prime\prime}
for some
f^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}(E^{n},E)
. Under the isomorphism
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E^{n},E)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E,E)^{n}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we have
f^{\prime\prime}=([a_{1}],\ldots,[a_{n}])
for some integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
. Putting this all together means

f=f^{\prime}\circ\xi=\phi\circ f^{\prime\prime}\circ\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i}](\phi\circ f_{i})_{*}.

This proves that
(\phi\circ f_{1})_{*},\ldots,(\phi\circ f_{n})_{*}
form a basis of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E^{\prime})
. To get that
f_{1,*},\ldots,f_{n,*}
form a basis of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C),E)
just take
E=E^{\prime}
and
\phi=\Id_{E}
.

For the second part, we compute the degree pairing on the given basis. To this end, let
i,j
be integers between
1
and
n
. The second part follows from the computation

\langle\phi\circ f_{i},\phi\circ f_{j}\rangle:=\phi_{*}\circ f_{i,*}\circ f_{j}^{*}\circ\phi^{*}=\phi_{*}\circ\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle\circ\phi^{*}=\phi_{*}\circ\phi^{*}\circ\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle=[\deg\phi]\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle,

where in the above we used that
\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E)
is multiplication by some integer so it commutes with
\phi^{*}
.
∎

4.2.

N

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a strong Weil curve of conductor

[

E\to J_{0}(N)
induced by the minimal degree modular parametrization

is injective. We also consider the map

u^{*}:J_{0}(N)\to J_{1}(N)

induced by the natural projection
u:X_{1}(N)\to X_{0}(N)
. Its kernel
\Sigma(N):=\ker u^{*}
is called the Shimura subgroup. The kernel of the composition map

E\to J_{0}(N)\xrightarrow{u^{*}}J_{1}(N)

is
\Sigma(N)\cap E
and is called the Shimura subgroup of

. If
\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is a subgroup, then similarly we get a map
u^{*}_{\Delta}:J_{0}(N)\to J_{\Delta}(N)
. We denote its kernel by
\Sigma(\Delta)
. Since
u^{*}
factors through
u^{*}_{\Delta}
, we have the inclusion
\Sigma(\Delta)\subseteq\Sigma(N)
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a strong Weil curve of conductor
N
and odd analytic rank. Then the kernel of the composition map

E\to J_{0}(N)\to J_{\Delta}(N)

is contained in
E[2]
for all groups
\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

Proof.

The kernel of the composition is
E\cap\Sigma(\Delta)
, which is contained in
E\cap\Sigma(N)
. So, it suffices to show
E\cap\Sigma(N)\subset E[2]
. By
[
26
, Chapter II, Proposition 11.7]
, the Atkin-Lehner involution
w_{N}
acts as
-1
on
\Sigma(N)
. Furthermore, since
E
has an odd analytic rank, it follows by looking at the functional equation for
L(E,s)
that
w_{N}
acts as
1
on
E
. Therefore,
-1=1
on
\Sigma(N)\cap E
meaning that
\Sigma(N)\cap E\subseteq E[2]
.
∎

We see that the Shimura subgroup is cyclic for all elliptic curves

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a strong Weil curve of conductor
N
and let us take
\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Suppose that

is the minimal degree of a parametrization

is a strong Weil curve for
X_{\Delta}(N)
in the isogeny class of

\frac{d\cdot\#((\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}/\Delta)}{\#(E\cap\Sigma(\Delta))}.

Proof.

We consider the composition map

. Let us take a fixed point

,

. By the universal property of the Jacobian,
f
and

factor uniquely through
J_{\Delta}(N)
. Since
E^{\prime}
is a strong Weil curve for
X_{\Delta}(N)
,
g
factors through
f^{\prime}
and we have

g^{*}:E\xrightarrow{\varphi^{*}}E^{\prime}\xrightarrow{(f^{\prime})^{*}}J_{\Delta}(N)

for some nonzero isogeny

. Here we slightly abused the notation by writing

and

instead of their Jacobians. Therefore, we have the following commutative diagram (

is the natural projection map):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\Delta}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{0}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}

for

\mathbb{Q}

(1)

and acts on

in the extended upper half-plane as

Proposition

4.2
tells us that the degree map

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J_{\Delta}(N),E)

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
(N,E)\in\{(74,\hrefhttps://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/37/a/1),(86,\hrefhttps://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/43/a/1,2),(111,\hrefhttps://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/37/a/1),(159,\hrefhttps://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/53/a/1)\}

E
by
M
and let us take
\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
and let
n
be the number of divisors of
N/M
. Let

\xi_{E,N}=(f\circ\iota_{1,N,M},\ldots,f\circ\iota_{N/M,N,M}):X_{0}(N)\to E^{n}

(we use the notation from
[

), then the composition

u^{*}_{\Delta}\circ\xi^{*}_{E,N}:E^{n}\rightarrow J_{0}(N)\rightarrow J_{\Delta}(N)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J_{\Delta}(N),E)

Let

Theorem 4.15

(
[
9
, Theorem 2.13]
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
M
with the corresponding newform
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}
and let
f:X_{0}(M)\to E
be the modular parametrization of
E
. Assume that
\frac{N}{M}
is either squarefree or coprime to
M
and let
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
be divisors of
\frac{N}{M}
. We write
\gcd
instead of
\gcd(d_{1},d_{2})
and
lcm
instead of
\textup{lcm}(d_{1},d_{2})
for simplicity. If we define

a=\left(\sum_{m^{2}\mid(d_{1}/\gcd)}\mu(m)a_{d_{1}/(\gcd m^{2})}\right)\left(\sum_{m^{2}\mid(d_{2}/\gcd)}\mu(m)a_{d_{2}/(\gcd m^{2})}\right),

where

is the Möbius function (when

\left<f\circ\iota_{d_{1},N,M},f\circ\iota_{d_{2},N,M}\right>=\left[a\cdot\frac{\psi\left(N\right)}{\psi\left(\frac{M\textup{lcm}}{\gcd}\right)}\cdot\deg f\right].

Here

is the index of the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

.

Even if we do not have that

is either squarefree or coprime to

see
[
9
, Start of the proof of Theorem 2.13]
for more details.

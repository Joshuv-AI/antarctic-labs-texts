Puerarin-Rich Pueraria lobata Extract Attenuates H2O2- and Dexamethasone-Induced Atrophy in C2C12 Myoblasts and Myotubes
Authors: Young han Kim, Jae in Jung, Soung yong Park, Jae-Hoon Shim, Young seung Lee, Seong il Heo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSarcopenia is an age-related decline in skeletal muscle mass and function that is closely associated with oxidative stress and excessive glucocorticoid exposure. This study investigated the protective effects of a Pueraria lobata extract powder (PEP) in C2C12 myotubes. PEP was standardized to 4.17% puerarin. PEP pretreatment significantly improved cell viability and suppressed the intracellular accumulation of reactive oxygen species under hydrogen peroxide-induced oxidative stress, while restoring the levels of glutathione and reducing apoptosis. Furthermore, in dexamethasone-treated myotubes, PEP pretreatment prevented myotube shrinkage, downregulated the E3 ubiquitin ligases Atrogin-1 and MuRF1, and upregulated the myogenic markers MyoD and myogenin. Mechanistically, PEP restored the phosphorylation of Akt and mTOR while enhancing the inhibition of FoxO3a phosphorylation, thereby promoting anabolic and suppressing catabolic signaling. These findings suggest that PEP serves dual protective functions through redox modulation and muscle protein metabolism regulation. This study provides in vitro evidence that a puerarin-rich P. lobata extract can concurrently mitigate oxidative stress and glucocorticoid-induced muscle atrophy, thus supporting its potential as a multi-targeted nutraceutical candidate for preventing muscle wasting and sarcopenia.Keywords: dexamethasone, muscular atrophy, oxidative stress, Pueraria, puerarin

INTRODUCTION

Sarcopenia is characterized by the progressive and generalized loss of skeletal muscle mass, strength, and function, which substantially increases the risk of frailty, disability, and mortality in the elderly population (Cruz-Jentoft and Sayer, 2019). Preventive strategies, including resistance exercise and adequate nutrition, have been shown to improve muscle mass, strength, and overall physical function in aging adults (Peterson and Gordon, 2011). The pathogenesis of sarcopenia involves several factors, including oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and an imbalance between protein synthesis and degradation (Meng and Yu, 2010; Liu et al., 2021). In recent years, natural bioactive compounds that have antioxidant and anabolic regulatory properties have garnered increasing attention as potential preventive or therapeutic agents against sarcopenia (Dutt et al., 2015; Kim et al., 2023; Barone et al., 2025).

Among the various catabolic stressors, the excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and chronic glucocorticoid exposure are recognized as major contributors that disrupt muscle homeostasis (Kuo et al., 2013; Steinbacher and Eckl, 2015). The overproduction of ROS damages cellular macromolecules, such as lipids, proteins, and DNA, which leads to apoptosis and proteolysis (Meng and Yu, 2010; Juan et al., 2021). Furthermore, glucocorticoids such as dexamethasone (DEX) induce muscle atrophy by upregulating E3 ubiquitin ligases, including Atrogin-1 and MuRF1, via the FoxO transcriptional activation pathway (Castillero et al., 2013; Bodine and Baehr, 2014; Wang et al., 2017).

Therefore, compounds that can simultaneously mitigate oxidative and glucocorticoid-induced catabolic stress may offer superior protective efficacy against muscle atrophy (Scalabrin et al., 2020). Pueraria lobata, commonly known as kudzu, is a leguminous plant that is widely distributed in East Asia. Its roots have been used as a food source, fodder, and traditional medicine for thousands of years (Wong et al., 2011). The root contains numerous bioactive isoflavones, such as puerarin, daidzin, and genistin, which have been previously reported to exhibit considerable antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties (Jin et al., 2012; Liu et al., 2023). Given that oxidative stress and chronic inflammation are the primary drivers of muscle atrophy, the rich isoflavone content of P. lobata suggests it may serve as an effective nutritional intervention to preserve muscle mass and function.

However, the potential of puerarin-standardized P. lobata extracts as a functional food ingredient for muscle health remains largely unexplored. Oxidative stress and excess glucocorticoid exposure converge on key atrophy pathways by impairing anabolic Akt/mTOR signaling and activating the FoxO-dependent transcription of atrogenes, such as MuRF1 and Atrogin-1. Therefore, an effective nutritional strategy for managing muscle disorders should target the redox imbalance and these catabolic signaling cascades. Based on the potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties of P. lobata isoflavones, we hypothesized that P. lobata extract powder (PEP) could confer integrated protection against muscle atrophy under specific catabolic conditions. To test this hypothesis, we investigated the protective efficacy and underlying molecular mechanisms of PEP using in vitro models. This study aims to provide scientific evidence that supports the application of PEP as a multi-targeting candidate nutraceutical agent for the prevention of sarcopenia.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Preparation and characterization of PEP

The P. lobata was supplied by Sejun F&B Co., Ltd. The dried roots were pulverized and extracted with 70% ethanol (v/v) under reflux for 2 h, a process that was repeated three times. The combined extracts were filtered, concentrated under vacuum conditions, and spray-dried to yield the final powder. The analytical method for quantifying puerarin, as the principal marker compound, was validated by the Korea Health Supplement Institute. Using a validated high-performance liquid chromatography method, the puerarin content was confirmed to be 41.68±0.18 mg/g.

Cell culture and differentiation

Mouse skeletal muscle C2C12 myoblasts (ATCC) were maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin (Gibco) at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. For differentiation into myotubes, the cells were grown to approximately 80% confluency, and then the culture medium was changed to differentiation medium (DM) containing 2% horse serum (Gibco). The DM was replaced every 2 days, and mature multinucleated myotubes were typically obtained after 4－6 days.

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was determined using an 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay based on mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity. Following seeding at a cell density of 5×104 cells/well, C2C12 cells were exposed to PEP (0－1,000 µg/mL) for 24 h. The MTT solution (1 mg/mL) was then added and incubated for 2 h, after which the formazan product was solubilized in isopropanol and measured at 570 nm.

H2O2-induced oxidative stress model

The cells were pretreated with PEP for 1 h and then exposed to 100 µM H2O2 for 24 h to induce oxidative stress.

Measurement of intracellular ROS

Intracellular ROS levels were measured using a DCFDA/H2DCFDA Cellular ROS Assay Kit (Abcam) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol.

Glutathione (GSH) assay

Intracellular GSH levels were quantified using a Glutathione Assay Kit (Cayman Chemical), as detailed in the manufacturer’s protocol.

Apoptosis analysis

Apoptotic cell death was evaluated using a Cellular DNA Fragmentation ELISA Kit (Sigma-Aldrich) and with Hoechst 33258 staining, which was observed under a fluorescence microscope.

DEX-induced atrophy model

C2C12 myotubes were differentiated for 4 days and then treated with 5 µM DEX, with or without PEP pretreatment (0－50 µg/mL), for 24 h to induce muscle atrophy.

Myotube diameter measurement

Following treatment, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100. The myotubes were immunostained with anti-MYH7 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology), followed by an Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated secondary antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Fluorescence images were captured using an inverted fluorescence microscope, and the myotube diameters were quantified using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health).

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was isolated using an RNeasy Plus Mini Kit (QIAGEN), and cDNA was synthesized using the HyperScript RT Master Mix Kit (GeneAll). Thereafter, qPCR was performed with the Rotor-Gene SYBR Green Kit (QIAGEN) on a Rotor-Gene 3000 system. The gene expression levels were normalized to that of GAPDH, and the primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted from the myotubes using lysis buffer, and the protein concentrations were determined with a bicinchoninic acid protein assay kit. Equal amounts of protein (50 µg) were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. Membranes were blocked and then incubated with primary antibodies against Atrogin-1 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-33782), MuRF1 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-27642), MyoD (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-377460), myogenin (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-12732), phospho-Akt (Ser473; Cell Signaling Technology, #9271), Akt (Cell Signaling Technology, #9272), phospho-mTOR (Ser2448; Cell Signaling Technology, #5536), mTOR (Cell Signaling Technology, #2983), phospho-FoxO3a (Ser253; Cell Signaling Technology, #9466), FoxO3a (Cell Signaling Technology, #12829), and β-actin (Cell Signaling Technology, #4970). The membranes were then incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies, and the protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescent substrate and quantified using an ImageQuant LAS 500 imaging system.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean±standard error of the mean. Statistical analyses were performed using a one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test to determine differences among groups. A P-value<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 5.0 software (GraphPad Software).

RESULTS

PEP protects myoblasts from oxidative stress-induced apoptosis

The non-cytotoxic concentrations of PEP were determined to establish a safe treatment concentration range. Treatment of C2C12 myoblasts with up to 1,000 µg/mL PEP for 24 h did not affect cell viability (Fig. 1A). Based on this noncytotoxic profile, subsequent experiments were performed using 10, 25, and 50 µg/mL of PEP. These concentrations were selected to ensure adequate biological activity while maintaining cell safety. Furthermore, this range was selected to assess the potential of PEP as a functional food ingredient, as these concentrations represent a physiologically relevant and industrially feasible range for human application.

(B) Cells were pretreated with PEP for 1 h, followed by cotreatment with 100 µM H2O2 for a further 24 h. Data are presented as a percentage of the H2O2-only treated group. Exposure to 100 µM H2O2 for 24 h markedly decreased cell viability, which was significantly rescued by 25 and 50 µg/mL of PEP pretreatment (Fig. 1B). To elucidate the underlying mechanism, the intracellular ROS and GSH levels were analyzed. H2O2 increased ROS generation and depleted GSH, whereas PEP pretreatment reduced ROS and restored GSH in a dose-dependent manner (Table 1). Moreover, PEP significantly inhibited H2O2-induced apoptosis, and DNA fragmentation was markedly elevated by H2O2 and reduced by PEP pretreatment at concentrations ≥10 µg/mL (Fig. 2A). Hoechst staining revealed that PEP preserved the normal nuclear morphology against H2O2-induced condensation (Fig. 2B).

PEP attenuates DEX-induced myotube atrophy

To evaluate the anti-atrophic potential of PEP, the differentiated myotubes were treated with 5 µM DEX for 24 h. DEX significantly reduced myotube viability, which was restored by 25 and 50 µg/mL PEP treatment (Fig. 3A). Morphologically, DEX reduced the myotube diameter from 39.17 to 10.00 µm, while PEP treatment preserved the diameter width in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 3B and 3C).

PEP modulates atrogenic and myogenic gene expression

DEX markedly increased the mRNA expression and protein levels of Atrogin-1 and MuRF1, whereas PEP cotreatment significantly suppressed their upregulation (Fig. 4A and 4C). In contrast, the expression of myogenic factors MyoD, myogenin, and IGF-1 was decreased by exposure to DEX and restored by PEP treatment in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 4B and 4C). Myostatin, as a negative regulator of muscle growth, was also significantly downregulated by PEP treatment.

PEP restores anabolic signaling via the Akt/mTOR/FoxO3a pathway

DEX inhibited Akt (Ser473) and mTOR (Ser2448) phosphorylation, but these were significantly restored by 25－50 µg/mL of PEP treatment (Fig. 5A and 5B). Furthermore, PEP increased the phosphorylation of FoxO3a (Ser253), thus indicating the inhibition of its catabolic activation (Fig. 5C). Collectively, these results demonstrate that PEP protects muscle cells by restoring the redox balance and activating the Akt/mTOR pathway while suppressing FoxO3a-driven proteolysis.

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrated that PEP, which was standardized for puerarin content, exerts a dual protective effect against two distinct muscle atrophy models, namely H2O2-induced oxidative stress and DEX-induced myotube atrophy. These findings indicate that PEP prevents muscle cell damage by simultaneously restoring redox balance and modulating the key anabolic-catabolic signaling pathways.

PEP exhibited strong cytoprotective and antioxidant properties under conditions of oxidative stress. H2O2 exposure caused the excessive accumulation of ROS and depletion of the intracellular antioxidant GSH, thereby leading to apoptosis (Powers and Jackson, 2008; Marí et al., 2009). PEP pretreatment effectively suppressed the generation of ROS, restored the GSH levels, and reduced apoptotic cell death. These effects align with previous reports on the antioxidant and cytoprotective properties of puerarin and related isoflavones (Zhou et al., 2014; Cheng et al., 2016; Li et al., 2017; Kulczyński et al., 2021). The preservation of myoblast viability under oxidative stress conditions suggests that PEP can maintain the cellular pool required for muscle repair and regeneration.

In the glucocorticoid-induced atrophy model, PEP treatment significantly attenuated the DEX-mediated reduction in myotube diameter and viability. Mechanistically, PEP downregulated the E3 ubiquitin ligases Atrogin-1 and MuRF1, which play crucial roles in the proteasomal degradation of myofibrillar proteins (Lin et al., 2025). These effects are consistent with the established roles of IGF-1 and the myogenic regulatory factors MyoD and myogenin in promoting muscle differentiation and anabolic signaling (Chargé and Rudnicki, 2004; Schiaffino et al., 2013). These dual actions of proteolysis suppression and synthesis stimulation collectively contribute to the observed morphological preservation of myotubes.

At the signaling level, PEP treatment restored the phosphorylation of Akt and mTOR while increasing the inhibition of FoxO3a phosphorylation, which is a key transcription factor that promotes atrophy-related gene expression. Akt activation stimulates protein synthesis through mTOR and inhibits FoxO3a by promoting its cytoplasmic sequestration (Stitt et al., 2004). Therefore, the observed increase in p-FoxO3a indicates the suppression of catabolic signaling, which explains the decreased expression of Atrogin-1 and MuRF1. This integrative regulation of the Akt/mTOR/FoxO3a axis positions PEP as a multi-targeting modulator of muscle homeostasis (Bodine et al., 2001; Stitt et al., 2004).

While these results provide compelling mechanistic evidence, this study has certain limitations that must be acknowledged. First, the findings are based on an in vitro model, which may not fully simulate the physiological complexity of muscle tissue in vivo. Second, although this study standardized PEP based on its puerarin content, the observed anti-atrophic effects likely result from the synergistic action of multiple bioactive compounds rather than from puerarin alone. P. lobata contains other notable isoflavones, such as daidzin and genistin, and these compounds have been previously reported to exhibit potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities (Jin et al., 2012). Given that oxidative stress and inflammation are critical factors in muscle atrophy, these constituents may function synergistically with puerarin to contribute to these observed muscle-protective effects. Future studies should validate these findings in animal sarcopenia models and evaluate their pharmacokinetic and safety profiles. Comparative analyses between PEP and pure puerarin would further clarify the contribution of the individual constituents.

In conclusion, PEP effectively mitigates oxidative and glucocorticoid-induced muscle atrophy through antioxidant activity and regulates the Akt/mTOR/FoxO3a signaling pathway. These findings highlight the potential of puerarin-rich PEP as a functional nutraceutical candidate to mitigate oxidative- or glucocorticoid-induced muscle atrophy, which are key contributors to age-related muscle loss.

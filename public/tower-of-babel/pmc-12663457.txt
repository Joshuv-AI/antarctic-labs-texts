Elevated oxytocin levels and oxytocin receptor gene expression in female-to-male transgender individuals
Authors: Majid Dastorani, Najmeh Shahini, Amirreza Livani, Marie Saghaeian Jazi, Jahanbakhsh Asadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Gender dysphoria (GD) is defined as a condition wherein a person’s gender identity does not align with the sex assigned at birth. Oxytocin (OXT) is an essential neuromodulator involved in cognition and socio-emotional processing. Its central effects are exerted via oxytocin receptors (OXTR). Recent studies indicated that the changes in the levels of OXTR may influence the course of some mental disorders (obsessive-compulsive disorders, schizophrenia, depression and autism). Understanding the neurobiological underpinnings of GD, particularly the involvement of OXT and OXTR, could provide beneficial insights into the mechanisms underlying this condition. In the present study, 18 Female-to-Male (FtM) transgenders, 11 FtM transgenders with self-injection of testosterone (FtM-T), and 30 control cis-females were included. Plasma levels of hormones of OXT, estradiol, progesterone, LH, FSH, free testosterone, and testosterone were evaluated with an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit. The whole blood mRNA expression of OXTR was determined via RT-qPCR. Our results showed a statistically significant increase in OXT plasma level in FtM group compared to control group (P = 0.032). Also, OXTR gene expression was significantly high in FtM-T group compared to cis-females group (P = 0.004). This is the first demonstration of increased plasma OXT levels in FtM transgenders and OXTR gene expression in FtM-T transgenders compared to the cis-females group. The results of this study can create a new approach to understanding the underlying mechanism of gender dysphoria. Further studies are necessary to ascertain whether this contributes to or is a consequence of GD symptomatology.

Introduction

Transgender individuals are those whose gender identity is diverse from the sex they were assigned at birth1. The transgender population size estimates differ based on the method of data collection. When clinical records are used, the estimates range from 1 to 30 individuals per 100,000 (0.001% to 0.03%)2. Estimates from studies that concentrate on self-reporting among nonclinical groups vary from 0.1% to 2%2. While the precise origins of transsexualism remain unclear, specialists have suggested that both psychological and biological influences could play a role in shaping an individual’s gender identity3. Growing evidence from research suggests that biological elements, including hormones and genetic traits, have a greater impact on the development of gender dysphoria than environmental influences such as parenting or societal factors4.

OXT neuropeptide is a nano peptide produced by paraventricular nuclei (PVN) and supraoptic nuclei (SON) in the hypothalamus and by neurons whose axons extend to the posterior pituitary5. Central release and peripheral secretion may occur simultaneously or independently, based on the circumstances and the triggering signals involved6. The best-known effects of oxytocin are stimulation of uterine contractions during labor7 and milk ejection during lactation8. Several studies have also shown the effects of oxytocin on regulating food intake9. Studies have shown that neurons containing SON and PVN nuclei, in addition to the pituitary gland, have extended to other parts of the brain and deliver OXT to some other areas of the brain10. OXT is a neuromodulator in the brain that plays a role in regulating our emotions and social interactions with others11. OXT encourages prosocial behavior, enhances the sense of trust and empathy12 and the ability to accurately distinguish facial emotions13. While OXT could serve as a potential marker for various mental health conditions like depression, psychosis and anxiety disorders, the existing evidence remains inconclusive14. A recent study showed that in men with hypersexual disorder, the plasma level of OXT is significantly higher15. So, research on OXT and its associated signaling could potentially contribute to a better understanding of the etiology of gender GD. OXTR is a member of the G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) family, known for having a seven-pass transmembrane structure. This transmembrane receptor interacts with the Gαq/11 protein16 it triggers a series of signaling processes, like the MAPK, PKC, PLC, or CaMK pathways, which come together to affect proteins like CREB or Myocyte enhancer factor (MEF)−217. OXTR is observed not only in the myometrium and mammary gland18 but also OXTR is expressed in some areas of the brain, including the hypothalamus, the limbic system, and the central and basolateral amygdala, which plays a role in the reaction to fear and the occurrence of autism17. It has also been shown that the level of OXTR gene expression or its protein level in PBMCs is related to some behavioral disorders such as obsessive-compulsive disorder and borderline personality disorder19,20. A recent study showed that a group of estrogen-dependent OXTR neurons is exclusively present in the anteroventral periventricular nucleus (AVPV) of females but not of males21. This sexually dimorphic expression of OXTR can show the importance of assessing it in transgenders.

To our knowledge until now, there is no evidence available studying OXT relation to GD. Considering the action of OXT sexually dimorphic22 and OXTR gene expression relation with various mental disorders23, here in current study; we aimed to determine and compare the OXT plasma levels and OXTR expression in FtM transgenders and cis-females (or cisgender women whose gender identity matches the sex they were assigned at birth).

Materials and methods

Participants

This study was a case control study conducted on 29 FtM transgenders including 18 FtM and 11 FtM with self-injection of testosterone (FtM-T), and 30 control cis-females. Control group participants were cis-females who were referred to the Dezyani clinic, in Gorgan, Iran. The patient group with gender dysphoria were diagnosed with structural interview by psychiatrist and using DSM-5 criteria (American Psychiatric Association, 2013) by clinical psychologist. Any case of other mental diseases and hormonal disorders was excluded at the screening phase from the case and control group to create a more homogeneous sample. The age distribution in both groups was 18 to 40 years old. The study groups were matched for age and BMI. The clinical research study was performed following the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee (IR.GOUMS.REC.1402.344) of the Golestan University of Medical Science (GOUMS). All members received detailed information about the research, and they signed an informed consent.

Specimen collection and preparation

Venous blood samples (5 mL) were collected from 29 FtM transgenders and 30 cis-females. Blood tubes were centrifuged (2000 g, 5 min, 4 °C). The collected plasma samples were stored at −80 °C.

RNA extraction and real-time PCR (RT-PCR)

In this case control study, peripheral whole blood samples were used for Total RNA isolation24,25. Total RNA isolation was performed using an RNX-plus solution (CinnaGen, Tehran, Iran). A total of 300 µl of blood in a tube was incubated with 900 µL of RNX-plus solution for 5 min; 200 µL of chloroform solution was added to the cell lysate, and, after mixing, the sample was incubated on ice at 4 ◦C for 5 min. Then samples were centrifuged (15 min, 12,000 rpm, 4 ◦C) to separate the aqueous phase from the organic phase. The top (aqueous) fraction was transferred to a fresh tube and an equal volume of cold isopropanol was added. After mixing gently, the mixture was incubated on ice for overnight. After centrifugation (15 min, 12,000 rpm, 4 ◦C), the supernatant was discarded and 1 ml of 75% Ethanol was used for washing step twice. Finally, the RNA allowed to dry at room temperature for a few minutes and finally it was dissolved in 50 ul DEPC treated water. The isolated RNA was stored at − 80 ◦C. RNA quality was confirmed by electrophoresis. RNA quantification was conducted using a Nanodrop instrument. Reverse transcription of total RNA to the cDNA was performed using a cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Yekta Tajhiz Azma, Tehran, Iran) using random hexamer following the manufacturer’s recommended protocol. OXTR gene expression was measured by StepOne plus Real-Time PCR System (ABI) instrument using Yekta Tajhiz SYBR green master mix and specific primers. The gene expression was normalized relative to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) using the 2 −ΔΔCt formula. The primer used for GAPDH26 and OXTR27 gene expression was used in previous studies (shown in Table 1).

Biochemical determinations

Plasma OXT levels were determined through commercially competitive ELISA-based kits (Zellbio, GmbH, Germany). Plasma levels of luteinizing hormone (LH) (Pishgamansanjesh, Tehran, Iran), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) (Pishgamansanjesh, Tehran, Iran), free testosterone (Monokit, Tehran, Iran), testosterone (Monokit, Tehran, Iran), progesterone (Monokit, Tehran, Iran) and Estradiol (Calbiotech, El Cajon, USA) measured with ELISA kit. The absorbance was measured at 450/630 nm wavelength.

Statistical analysis

Shapiro-wilk test was used to assess the normal distribution of variables. Comparison between the three groups was done using one-way ANOVA and independent-samples Kruskal-Wallis test. Dunn’s multiple comparisons post hoc test and Tukey’s multiple comparisons post hoc test were used for comparison between the groups. Results were presented as mean ± the standard deviation (SD). The P-values of < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using the Statistical Package for Social Science version 18 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, Illinois, USA).

Results

In the present study, our results showed that the hormone levels of LH (P = 0.662), FSH (P = 0.174), and estradiol (P = 0.599) were not significantly different between the three groups of FtM, FtM-T and control. In addition, after the Shapiro-Wilk test showed that the testosterone and free testosterone data were not normally distributed, independent samples Kruskal Wallis test results showed that testosterone and free testosterone levels were significantly different between the three groups (P < 0.0001). Dunn’s multiple comparisons Post hoc test showed that testosterone and free testosterone levels are significantly high in the FtM-T group compared to FtM and control groups (P < 0.0001). Also, Kruskal Wallis test results showed that the plasma level of progesterone was significantly different between the three groups (P = 0.024). Dunn’s multiple comparisons Post hoc test showed that progesterone is significantly low in the FtM-T group compared to the control group (P = 0.029) (Fig. 1).

In the present study, ELISA measurements of plasma OXT were conducted among the three studied groups. After confirming the normality of the data distribution, the results of the ANOVA test exhibited that OXT is significantly different between the three groups (P = 0.042). Post hoc Tukey’s multiple comparisons test showed that OXT plasma level is high in the FtM group compared to the control group (P = 0.032). Also, in this study, for more confirmation we determined the levels of OXTR gene expression among FtM, FtM-T, and controls. The results of the Kruskal-Wallis test exhibited that OXTR gene expression is significantly different between the three groups (P = 0.006) Dunn’s multiple comparisons Post hoc test showed that OXTR gene expression is significantly higher in the FtM-T group compared to control group (P = 0.004). It should be noted that the OXTR gene expression analysis was carried out on n = 13 FtM, n = 11 FtM-T, and n = 26 controls. A few samples were excluded due to the poor quality of the extracted RNA (Fig. 1).

Discussion

Our study showed that the plasma level of OXT neurohormone in the FtM group is significantly higher compared to the cis-females group. Also, FtM-T transgenders had higher plasma OXT levels compared to the control group but this difference was not statistically significant. To our knowledge, this research is the first study and the first demonstration of elevated OXT plasma in transgender individuals. In this field, a recent study showed that plasma OXT levels of healthy subjects are higher in women than in men28. Another study showed that the levels of plasma OXT were significantly higher in hypersexual men compared with healthy volunteers15. Moreover, the findings of several recent studies illustrate the sex-differential effects of OXT which could be considered noteworthy in gender related disorder. First of all, OXT reduces activity in the amygdala for males, but increases it for females in most contexts29; in other hand OXT improves social cognition in males but has little or no effect in females30. The gender differences in the density and location of OXTRs in the brain31,32, also OXT interactions with sex hormones have been proposed as an explanation of sex-differential effects33. Thus, OXT plays a complex role in various neuropsychiatric functions and may be a shared element significant in numerous psychiatric conditions. So far, in different studies, some mental disorders have been linked with OXT including autism spectrum34, schizophrenia25, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)35, and bipolar-affective disorder36. Overall, regarding the sexual dimorphic effect of OXT on brain function31, changes in the OXT plasma concentrations could be considered as an important factor in the gender dysphoria underlying mechanisms.

In the present study, the higher level of OXT in the FtM-T group compared to the control group was not statistically significant. In vitro studies demonstrated that androgen receptor (AR) mediated the down-regulation of OXT gene expression37. This could be a possible cause for our finding that the higher level of OXT in the FtM-T group compared to the control group was not statistically significant.

In this research, we showed elevated levels of OXTR gene expression in FtM-T transgenders compared to control groups. Also, FtM transgenders had higher OXTR gene expression levels compared to the control group but this difference was not statistically significant. In line with our study, Christiane Mühle et al. showed that OXTR protein plasma concentrations positively correlated with dihydrotestosterone (DHT) and testosterone concentrations38. Also, Arata et al. showed that testosterone increases serum OXT levels and hypothalamic OXTR mRNA levels in non-estradiol-supplemented and ovariectomized female rats39. Another study by Bronner et al. provided evidence for an increase in OXT binding sites (OXTR) density in the Ventromedial nucleus of the hypothalamus (VMH) in rats during development and adulthood by testosterone hormone injection40. Moreover, non-human studies have demonstrated that estrogen in females and testosterone or its metabolites (estradiol and DHT) in males affect OXT production, OXTR gene expression41, OXT binding to OXTR42 and modulation of the effects of OXT on social behavior, such as social recognition43. These results when in combination with finding of a recent study about some sex hormone receptor genetic variants including ERβ and AR involvement in the development of gender dysphoria44, can show that sex hormones play an important role in GD development that could be related to the interaction of these hormones with the OXT-OXTR signaling pathway during sexually dimorphic brain development. So far, different studies have linked OXTR with many mental disorders, including OCD20, schizophrenia25, chronic stress45, and Williams syndrome46. OXTR is found in the cortex, hypothalamus, pons, medulla, and cerebellum, contributing to neuronal excitability, network oscillatory activity, synaptic plasticity, and the memory of social recognition47. The importance of these findings in the context of gender dysphoria becomes apparent when we see that the results of several recent studies have shown that the localization of OXTR in the human brain is sexually dimorphic21,31,32. These findings highlight the importance of measuring and investigating oxytocin-related signaling pathways in transgender individuals.

Regarding the importance of assessing plasma levels of oxytocin and its receptor gene expression in whole blood, it is worth noting that numerous studies, including a meta-analysis, have demonstrated a positive association between central and peripheral oxytocin concentrations48. On the other hand, other studies that correlated the oxytocin levels to some mental disorders, such as OCD, autism, etc., were conducted in plasma/blood49. For OXTR gene expression evaluations in peripheral blood, due to no access to transgender brain and the inability to model mouse gender dysphoria, as many studies, such as a recent study that used this method in schizophrenia disorder25, we evaluate the OXTR gene expression in whole blood in this research.

More interestingly, in our study, progesterone hormone levels were significantly lower in the FtM-T group compared to the control group, which could be related to testosterone self-injection in this group. In line with our findings, a recent study showed that long-term testosterone treatment can decrease progesterone levels50.

In our study, we did foresee that evaluating sex hormone level could be worthwhile. Some studies reported that endogenous OXT levels in women is at higher levels in the time of ovulation when the estrogen levels are highest51. Our results showed that plasma levels of LH, FSH, and Estradiol hormones had no statistically significant difference in the three groups but testosterone and free testosterone amounts were significantly high in the FtM-T group because of arbitrary testosterone injection in this group.

There were a few limitations in our study. We assessed OXT levels in peripheral blood. The meta-analytic findings indicated that concentrations of peripheral and central OXT are correlated following intranasal OXT administration and during stressful circumstances48. However, the literature also indicates variations in the release of OXT between peripheral and central systems, as well as differences in the duration of its effects52. Moreover, it should be noted that we made every effort to ensure that the case and control groups were homogeneous in terms of menstrual cycles, but since some of individuals mainly in FtM-T group had menstrual problems (which could be consequent to self-injection of testosterone), the menstrual adjustment was achieved between FtM and control groups but not FtM-T and this could be one of the limitations of our study. Also, the study was conducted on a relatively small sample size. A larger sample size could provide stronger and generalizable results.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated elevation of the plasma OXT neuropeptide levels in FtM transgenders compared to cis-females. Also, our study showed an increased OXTR gene expression in FtM transgender with testosterone self-injection compared to cis-females. These results illustrate the importance of changes in the OXT system in male transgender individuals. Still, research is needed to determine whether this contributes to or is a consequence of GD symptomatology.

Abstract
We prove several negative results about first-order transducibility for classes of sparse graphs: • for every t ∈ ℕ t\in\mathbb{N} , the class of graphs of treewidth at most t + 1 t+1 is not transducible from the class of graphs of treewidth at most t t ; • for every t ∈ ℕ t\in\mathbb{N} , the class of graphs with Hadwiger number at most t + 2 t+2 is not transducible from the class of graphs with Hadwiger number at most t t ; and • the class of graphs of treewidth at most 4 4 is not transducible from the class of planar graphs. These results are obtained by combining the known upper and lower bounds on the weak coloring numbers of the considered graph classes with the following two new observations: • If a weakly sparse graph class 𝒟 \mathscr{D} is transducible from a class 𝒞 \mathscr{C} of bounded expansion, then for some k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} , every graph G ∈ 𝒟 G\in\mathscr{D} is a k k -congested depth- k k minor of a graph H ∘ H^{\circ} obtained from some H ∈ 𝒞 H\in\mathscr{C} by adding a universal vertex. • The operations of adding a universal vertex and of taking k k -congested depth- k k minors, for a fixed k k , preserve the degree of the distance- d d weak coloring number of a graph class, understood as a polynomial in d d .

Introduction

Transductions
are a basic model-theoretic notion whose aim is to capture the concept of encoding one graph in another graph by means of logical formulas. A transduction
\mathsf{T}
is specified by a set of colors
\mathcal{C}
and symmetric formula
\varphi(x,y)
in the signature of graphs with vertices colored with
\mathcal{C}
.
We say that a graph
H
can be
transduced
from a graph
G
using
\mathsf{T}
if the adjacency relation of
H
can be interpreted in some
\mathcal{C}
-coloring
G^{+}
of
G
using
\varphi
(see
Section

2
for a formal definition). This concept can be applied to different logics, yielding notions of transductions of different power. In this paper we focus on first-order transductions, where
\varphi
is required to be a formula of first-order logic on (
\mathcal{C}
-colored) graphs. (For brevity, we will henceforth drop the prefix
first-order
, assuming all transductions to be such.)
Transductions are an abstract notion that capture many commonly considered graph operations of both local and global nature, such as taking the power of a graph, taking and induced subgraph, or taking a complement.

A natural setting to consider transductions is that of graph classes. We say that a graph class
\mathscr{D}
is
transducible
from a graph class
\mathscr{C}
if there is a fixed transduction
\mathsf{T}
such that every graph belonging to
\mathscr{D}
can be transduced from some graph from
\mathscr{C}
using
\mathsf{T}
. Since the composition of two transductions is again a transduction, the relation of transducibility induces a quasi-order (a reflexive and transitive relation) on graph classes. Intuitively, this quasi-order stratifies all graph classes according to their expressive power with respect to first-order logic.

Recently, there has been a surge of interest in tranductions due to the on-going project of developing a model-theoretic structure theory for graphs, in which transducibility would be the fundamental notion of embedding. A broad introduction to this rapidly developing field can be found in a recent survey of the fourth author
[
13
]
. The focus of this paper is on one particular aspect of the theory: the study of the transducibility quasi-order itself.

While the landscape painted by the transducibility quasi-order is expected to be very varied and detailed (see
[
2
, Figure 2]
), at this point we have only limited tools for proving negative results — that some graph classes cannot be transduced from others. One technique is to exploit known
transduction ideals
: properties of graph classes closed under transductions. For instance, it is known that any class transducible from a class of bounded cliquewidth again has bounded cliquewidth, hence the class of planar graphs (which has unbounded cliquewidth) cannot be transduced from the class of graphs of cliquewidth at most
t
, for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Other transduction ideals useful for such arguments include classes of bounded twin-width
[
1
]
; classes of bounded linear cliquewidth; classed of shrubdepth at most
d
, for any fixed
d\in\mathbb{N}

[
7
]
; or monadically stable and monadically dependent classes (see
[
13
, Section 4.1]
for an introduction).

However, reasonings involving known transduction ideals have a very limited strength and cannot explain the whole picture. A more detailed look has recently been offered by Braunfeld, Nešetřil, Ossona de Mendez, and Siebertz
[
2
]
. Among other results, they characterized classes transducible from the class of trees, and they proved that the hierarchy of graph classes of bounded pathwidth is strict: for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the class of graphs of pathwidth at most
t+1
is not transducible from the class of graphs of pathwidth at most
t
. However, the analogous question for treewidth was left open. Very recently, the third author with Hliněný
[
9
]
, and independently the first and the fourth author with Pokrývka
[
6
]
, proved that the class of three-dimensional grids is not transducible from the class of planar graphs (or more generally, from the class of graphs embeddable in
\Sigma
, for any fixed surface
\Sigma
). Both proofs rely on finding a certain invariant inspired by the product structure of planar graphs
[
4
]
that is preserved by transductions.

Our results.

In this paper, we further explore the transducibility quasi-order on classes of sparse graphs. First, we prove (
Theorem

3.1
) that if a weakly sparse graph class
\mathscr{D}
is transducible from a class of bounded expansion
\mathscr{C}
, then for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, every graph from
\mathscr{D}
can be found as a
k
-congested depth-
k
minor of a graph from
\mathscr{C}
augmented by adding a universal vertex. Up to a minor technical aspect in the definition of a transduction, this is in fact a characterization of the transducibility quasi-order among graph classes of bounded expansion (see
Lemma

3.10
for the reverse implication).

Based on the result above, we propose a new transduction invariant: the asymptotic growth of the weak
d
-coloring number of the class, expressed as a function of
d
. Here, weak coloring numbers are a family of graph parameters that are commonly used to work with classes of sparse graphs; see the recent survey of Siebertz
[
14
]
for an introduction.
We prove (
Corollary

4.2
) that if a class
\mathscr{D}
is transducible from a class
\mathscr{C}
, and both
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
have bounded expansion, then the weak
d
-coloring numbers of
\mathscr{D}
cannot grow asymptotically faster with
d
than the weak
d
-coloring numbers of
\mathscr{C}
.

The new invariant allows us to derive a host of non-transducibility results for well-studied classes of sparse graphs, by exploiting known upper and lower bounds on their weak coloring numbers. Below we give three concrete examples of such corollaries.

It is known that for the class of graphs of treewidth at most
t
, the weak
d
-coloring number is equal to
\binom{d+t}{t}

[
8
]
, which is a polynomial in
d
of degree exactly
t
. This allows us to answer the question about the strictness of the hierarchy of classes of bounded treewidth, which was left open by Braunfeld et al.
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the class of graphs of treewidth at most
t+1
is not transducible from the class of graphs of treewidth at most
t
.

Further, recall that the
Hadwiger number
of a graph
G
is the largest
t
such that
G
contains the complete graph
K_{t}
as a minor. It is known that the weak
d
-coloring number of the class of graphs of Hadwiger number at most
t
is upper bounded by
\mathcal{O}(d^{t})

[
15
]
and lower bounded by
\Omega(d^{t-1})

[
8
]
. Hence, we have:

Theorem 1.2
.

For every positive
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the class of graphs of Hadwiger number at most
t+2
is not transducible from the class of graphs of Hadwiger number at most
t
.

Finally, from the known upper bound of
\mathcal{O}(d^{3})
on the weak
d
-coloring number of planar graphs
[
15
]
, combined with the lower bound of
\Omega(d^{4})
on the weak
d
-coloring number of graphs of treewidth at most
4

[
8
]
, we get the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

The class of graphs of treewidth at most
4
is not transducible from the class of planar graphs.

Theorem

1.3
stands in contrast with the result of Braunfeld et al.
[
2
]
that for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the class of graphs of pathwidth at most
t
is in fact transducible from the class of planar graphs.
Further, while graphs of treewidth
2
are known to be planar,
Theorem

1.3
leaves it open whether graphs of treewidth
3
are transducible from planar graphs. Note that a negative answer to this question would follow from an improvement of the
\mathcal{O}(d^{3})
upper bound on the weak
d
-coloring number of planar graphs
[
15
]
.

2
Preliminaries

By
\mathbb{N}
we denote the set of nonnegative integers.

Graphs.

We assume standard graph notation and terminology. All graphs considered in this paper are undirected and simple (i.e., without loops or parallel edges). The vertex set and the edge set of a graph
G
are denoted by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively. For a vertex subset
A\subseteq V(G)
, the induced subgraph
G[A]
consists of all the vertices of
A
and edges of
G
with both endpoints in
A
. The
radius
of a connected graph
G
is the smallest
d
satisfying the following: there is a vertex
u
such that every other vertex can be reached from
u
by a path of length at most
d
.

A
graph class
is just a set of graphs, typically infinite. We say that a graph class
\mathscr{C}
is
weakly sparse
if there is
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that no member of
\mathscr{C}
contains the complete bipartite graph
K_{t,t}
as a subgraph.

Congested shallow minors and bounded expansion.

Let
G
be a graph. We say that a vertex subset
A\subseteq V(G)
is
connected
if the induced subgraph
G[A]
is connected. Further, two vertex subsets
A,B\subseteq V(G)

touch
if they share a vertex (
A\cap B\neq\varnothing
) or are adjacent (there exist adjacent
a\in A
and
b\in B
).

For two graphs
H
and
G
, a
model
of
H
in
G
is a mapping
\eta
that assigns each vertex
u\in V(H)
a connected vertex subset
\eta(u)\subseteq V(G)
, called the
branch set
of
u
, so that the following condition is satisfied: whenever
u
and
v
are adjacent in
H
, the branch sets
\eta(u)
and
\eta(v)
touch. Note that we do not require the branch sets to be disjoint. For
c,d\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
H
is a
congestion-
c
depth-
d
minor
of
G
if there is a model
\eta
of
H
in
G
such that

•

for every
u\in V(H)
, the graph
G[\eta(u)]
has radius at most
d
; and

•

for every
v\in V(G)
, there are at most
c
vertices
u\in V(H)
such that
v\in\eta(u)
.

By
\mathsf{Minors}^{c}_{d}(G)
we denote the set of all congestion-
c
depth-
d
minors of
G
. For a graph class
\mathscr{C}
, we define

\mathsf{Minors}^{c}_{d}(\mathscr{C})\coloneqq\bigcup_{G\in\mathscr{C}}\mathsf{Minors}^{c}_{d}(G).

Note that if we set
c=1
in the definition above, we require the branch sets to be pairwise disjoint. In this case we may speak simply about
depth-
d
minors
, whose set we denote by
\mathsf{Minors}_{d}(G)
(for a graph
G
) and
\mathsf{Minors}_{d}(\mathscr{C})
(for a class
\mathscr{C}
). The following definition is a fundamental notion in the theory of Sparsity.

Definition 2.1
.

A graph class
\mathscr{C}
has
bounded expansion
if for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
p_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|E(H)|\leqslant p_{d}\cdot|V(H)|\qquad\textrm{for every }H\in\mathsf{Minors}_{d}(\mathscr{C}).

It turns out that classes of bounded expansion are closed under taking congested shallow minors, in the following sense.

Theorem 2.2

(cf.
[
10
, Proposition 4.6]
and
[
11
, Chapter 1, Corollary 2.28]
)
.

For all
c,d\in\mathbb{N}
and every graph class
\mathscr{C}
of bounded expansion, the class
\mathsf{Minors}^{c}_{d}(\mathscr{C})
also has bounded expansion.

Finally,
H
is a
minor
of
G
if there is a congestion-
1
depth-
\infty
model of
H
in
G
, that is, one where the branch sets have to be disjoint and we only require them to induce connected subgraphs.

Weak coloring numbers.

An
ordered graph
is a graph
G
equipped with a total order
\preccurlyeq
on the vertex set of
G
. Let
(G,\preccurlyeq)
be an ordered graph. For vertices
u,v\in V(G)
with
u\preccurlyeq v
, we say that
u
is
weakly
d
-reachable
from
v
if in
G
there is a path with endpoints
u
and
v
and of length at most
d
such that every vertex of this path is not smaller than
u
in
\preccurlyeq
. For
v\in V(G)
, we define the
weak
d
-reachability set
of
v
as

\mathsf{WReach}^{G,\preccurlyeq}_{d}[v]\coloneqq\{u\colon\textrm{$u$ is weakly $d$-reachable from $v$ in $(G,\preccurlyeq)$}\}.

We may omit the superscript in notation if the ordered graph
(G,\preccurlyeq)
is clear from the context.

The
weak
d
-coloring number
of an ordered graph
(G,\preccurlyeq)
is the maximum size of a weak
d
-reachability set:

\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(G,\preccurlyeq)\coloneqq\max_{v\in V(G)}|\mathsf{WReach}^{G,\preccurlyeq}_{d}[v]|

The weak
d
-coloring number of a graph
G
is the minimum possible weak
d
-coloring number of its ordering:

\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(G)\coloneqq\min_{\preccurlyeq\colon\textrm{total order on }V(G)}\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(G,\preccurlyeq).

Finally, we may apply this definition to any graph class
\mathscr{C}
by taking the supremum.

\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(\mathscr{C})\coloneqq\sup_{G\in\mathscr{C}}\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(G).

Note that
\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(\mathscr{C})=\infty
when the weak
d
-coloring number is unbounded on the class
\mathscr{C}
. However, it turns out that the boundedness of all the weak coloring numbers characterizes bounded expansion.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
16
]
)
.

A graph class
\mathscr{C}
has bounded expansion if and only if
\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(\mathscr{C})
is finite for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

First-order logic and transductions.

We assume reader’s familiarity with the standard first-order logic on graphs. Below we recall standard definitions and facts about transductions; see the recent survey
[
13
]
for a more thorough introduction.

We will work with
vertex-colored graphs
. For a set of colors
\mathcal{C}
, by a
\mathcal{C}
-colored graph
we mean an undirected graph
G
with a vertex subset
U_{C}\subseteq V(G)
distinguished, for each color
C\in\mathcal{C}
. Note that a vertex may belong to several such subsets
U_{C}
, or to no subset
U_{C}
at all. We model a
\mathcal{C}
-colored graph as a relational structure whose universe is the vertex set, there is one binary relation signifying adjacency, and one unary relation per color
C\in\mathcal{C}
that selects all the vertices that belong to
U_{C}
. The
signature
of a vertex-colored graph
G
is the signature of this relational structure, i.e., consisting of one binary relation and
p
unary relations. This allows us to employ standard first-order logic on
\mathcal{C}
-colored graphs, where in atomic formulas one can check equality, adjacency, and color membership.

A first-order formula
\varphi(x,y)
over
\mathcal{C}
-colored graphs is
symmetric
if
G\models\varphi(u,v)\Leftrightarrow\varphi(v,u)
for every
\mathcal{C}
-colored graph
G
.
For such a formula
\varphi
and a
\mathcal{C}
-colored graph
G
, we define the (uncolored) graph
\varphi(G)
as follows: the vertex set of
\varphi(G)
is the same as that of
G
, and two distinct vertices
u,v
are adjacent in
\varphi(G)
if and only if
\varphi(u,v)
holds in
G
.

A
(first-order) transduction

\mathsf{T}
consists of a set of colors
\mathcal{C}
and a symmetric first-order formula
\varphi(x,y)
over the signature of
\mathcal{C}
-colored graphs. In this paper we consider only first-order transductions, hence we call them simply transductions for brevity. Applying
\mathsf{T}
to an (uncolored) graph
G
yields a graph class
\mathsf{T}(G)
defined as the set of all (uncolored) graphs
H
that can be obtained through the following procedure:

•

For every color
C\in\mathcal{C}
, distinguish an arbitrary subset of
V(G)
as
U_{C}
, thus extending
G
to a
\mathcal{C}
-colored graph
G^{+}
. We call such
G^{+}
a
\mathcal{C}
-expansion
of
G
.

•

Define
H^{\prime}\coloneqq\varphi(G^{+})
.

•

Output an arbitrary induced subgraph of
H^{\prime}
as
H
.

A transduction
\mathsf{T}
can be applied to a graph class
\mathscr{C}
by applying it to every member of
\mathscr{C}
and taking the union of the results:

\mathsf{T}(\mathscr{C})\coloneqq\bigcup_{G\in\mathscr{C}}\mathsf{T}(G).

Finally, we say that a graph class
\mathscr{D}
is
transducible
from
\mathscr{C}
if
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathsf{T}(\mathscr{C})
for some transduction
\mathsf{T}
. Following
[
13
]
, the intuition is as follows:
\mathscr{D}
is transducible from
\mathscr{C}
if every member of
\mathscr{D}
can be encoded in a colored graph from
\mathscr{C}
using a fixed, first-order mechanism expressed by
\varphi
.

It is known that transductions are compositional: the composition of two transductions is again a transduction (cf.
[
13
, Lemma 2]
). Therefore, the relation of transducibility induces a quasi-order — a reflexive and transitive relation — on graph classes.

We remark that often in the literature, one considers a slightly stronger notion of
transductions with copying
. Technical aspects differ, but for the purpose of this paper we may adopt the following definition: a graph class
\mathscr{D}
is
transducible
with copying from a graph class
\mathscr{C}
if there exists a transduction
\mathsf{T}
(without copying) and a number
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathsf{T}(\mathscr{C}\bullet p)
, where
\mathscr{C}\bullet p\coloneqq\{G\bullet p\colon G\in\mathscr{C}\}
and
G\bullet p
is the
p
-blowup
of
G
: the graph obtained from
G
by replacing every vertex with a clique consisting of
p
copies (and copies of adjacent vertices are again adjacent). In other words, the transduction may start not with a graph from
\mathscr{C}
, but with its
p
-blowup, for some constant
p
; intuitively, this allows interpreting multiple (up to
p
) vertices of the transduced graph
H
in a single vertex of the source graph
G
.
It can be easily seen that the
G\bullet p
is intertransducible with the graph obtained from
G
by adding
p-1
pendants (degree-
1
vertices) to every vertex of
G
; that is, each of these graphs can be transduced from the other by a fixed transduction, and vice versa. Therefore, if a graph class
\mathscr{C}
is closed under addition of degree-
1
vertices, the notions of being transducible from
\mathscr{C}
with copying and without copying do coincide. This will be the case for all the concrete graph classes considered in this paper, hence we will stick to the simpler notion of non-copying transductions. See also
[
2
, Section 8]
for a more elaborate treatment of copying.

3
Transductions and congested minors

For a graph
G
, by
G^{\circ}
we denote the graph obtained from
G
by adding a
universal vertex
: a new vertex adjacent to all the other vertices. For a class
\mathscr{C}
, we denote
\mathscr{C}^{\circ}\coloneqq\{G^{\circ}\colon G\in\mathscr{C}\}
.

The main goal of this section is to prove the following result, which intuitively says the following: among sparse graphs, transducibility implies containment as a congested shallow minor.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a graph class of bounded expansion and
\mathscr{D}
be a weakly sparse graph class transducible from
\mathscr{C}
. Then there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathsf{Minors}^{k}_{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})
.

A few remarks are in order. First, since the property of having bounded expansion is preserved both by the operations of adding a universal vertex (easy from the definition) and of taking a congested shallow minor (
Theorem

2.2
), from
Theorem

3.1
it follows that every weakly sparse class transducible from a class of bounded expansion also has bounded expansion. This fact was, however, already known, as it easily follows from the results of
[
5
]
.

Second, one might wonder whether the addition of a universal vertex is necessary in
Theorem

3.1
. It is: the class of stars is transducible from the class of edgeless graphs, but is not contained in their
k
-congested depth-
k
minors. It turns out that adding a single universal vertex suffices to solve this issue.

Third, the converse of
Theorem

3.1
is also true if we allow copying: for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a graph class
\mathscr{C}
of bounded expansion, the class
\mathsf{Minors}^{k}_{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})
is transducible with copying from
\mathscr{C}
. Since the statement of
Theorem

3.1
can be easily lifted to transductions with copying as well, this provides a purely combinatorial characterization of transducibility with copying among graph classes of bounded expansion. We postpone this discussion to the end of this section, and for now we focus on the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

The main tool that will be used in the proof of
Theorem

3.1
is the Local Feferman-Vaught Theorem, proposed in
[
3
,
12
]
. We will use the formulation below that is adjusted to our terminology. This formulation follows easily from the more descriptive ones presented in
[
3
,
12
]
.

We say that two vertices
u,v
of a graph
G
are
d
-separated
by a vertex set
S
if every path of length at most
d
that connects
u
and
v
must necessarily intersect
S
. Note that this definition also applies to the case when
u
or
v
belongs to
S
, in which case the condition is always satisfied.

Theorem 3.2

(see
[
3
, Theorem 1.12]
and
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{C}
be a finite set of colors, and
\varphi(x,y)
be a symmetric first-order formula over the signature of
\mathcal{C}
-colored graphs. Then there exists a constant
d\in\mathbb{N}
, depending only on the quantifier rank of
\varphi
, and a finite set of colors
\Lambda
, depending on
s
,
\mathcal{C}
, and
\varphi
, such that the following holds. For every
\mathcal{C}
-colored graph
G
and a vertex subset
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|\leqslant s
, there is a coloring
\lambda\colon V(G)\to\Lambda
such that for every pair of vertices
u,v\in V(G)
that are
d
-separated by
S
, whether
\varphi(u,v)
holds in
G
depends only on the pair of colors
(\lambda(u),\lambda(v))
. In other words, there is a set
R\subseteq\Lambda\times\Lambda
such that for all
u,v\in V(G)
that are
d
-separated by
S
, we have

G\models\varphi(u,v)\qquad\textrm{if and only if}\qquad(\lambda(u),\lambda(v))\in R.

Let us now formulate the key technical lemma towards the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite set of colors,
\varphi(x,y)
be a symmetric first-order formula over the signature of
\mathcal{C}
-colored graphs, and
d\in\mathbb{N}
be the constant given by
Theorem

3.2
for
\varphi
. Next, let
G
be a
\mathcal{C}
-colored graph and
\preccurlyeq
be a total order on
V(G)
so that the following two conditions are satisfied:
\mathsf{wcol}_{2d}(G,\preccurlyeq)\leqslant s
for some
s\in\mathbb{N}
, and we have
\mathsf{WReach}^{G,\preccurlyeq}_{d}[u]\cap\mathsf{WReach}^{G,\preccurlyeq}_{d}[v]\neq\varnothing
for all
u,v\in V(G)
. Finally, suppose
\varphi(G)

We now proceed to the proof of

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

and
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
be sequences of sets such that

•

|A_{i}|\leqslant a
,
|B_{i}|\leqslant b
, and
A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\varnothing
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, and

•

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\varnothing
for all
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
.

Then

.

Proof.

We fix
b
and proceed by induction on

. The case
a=0
is straightforward: We must have
A_{1}=\varnothing
, and if
n>1
, then
B_{n}\cap A_{0}=\varnothing
contradicts the assumptions. Hence we have
n\leqslant 1
in this case, as desired.
The case
b=0
is analogous. Hence, we assume that
a>0
and
b>0
.

For the induction step, suppose for contradiction that
n>b^{0}+b^{1}+\ldots+b^{a}
. Note that
B_{n}
intersects all the sets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n-1}
. Since
|B_{n}|\leqslant b
, there is an element
x\in B_{n}
that belongs to at least
\frac{n-1}{b}>b^{0}+b^{1}+\ldots+b^{a-1}
sets

,

. Note also that

does not belong to any of the corresponding sets

, as each of them is disjoint with

. Hence, by passing to subsequences consisting of those

s that contain

and the corresponding

s, and removing

from all the considered

s, we find a pair of sequences of sets to which we may apply the induction assumption for parameters

and

. This induction assumption tells us that the length of the sequences is at most
b^{0}+b^{1}+\ldots+b^{a-1}
; a contradiction.
∎

Proof of

.

For a nonempty set of vertices

\preccurlyeq
-largest element of

.
Finally, we denote

for

\varphi

Recalling that

. Then

Let us also note the following standard claim:

Claim 3.5
.

For every pair of vertices

,

We will now work towards the following goal.

Claim 3.6
.

For every vertex

of

, there exists a set

such that

•

|X_{w}|\leqslant f(s,t,|\Lambda|)

, and

.

In other words, we postulate that for every

, the edges that are mapped to

,

First, partition all the edges

. Next, for a fixed

, and we further partition all the edges

,

, and
\lambda_{S}(v)=\beta

. Also, note that each vertex of

.

Let

, where

and
\lambda_{S}(u_{i})=\alpha
and
\lambda_{S}(v_{i})=\beta

We conclude this section with the discussion of a converse of
Theorem

3.1
.

First, note that
Theorem

3.1
can be easily strengthened by replacing the assumption that
\mathscr{D}
is transducible from
\mathscr{C}
, with requiring only that
\mathscr{D}
is transducible with copying from
\mathscr{C}
. Indeed, recall that this means that
\mathscr{D}
is transducible (without copying) from
\mathscr{C}\bullet p
for some

. As
\mathscr{C}\bullet p\subseteq\mathsf{Minors}^{p}_{0}(\mathscr{C})
, from

2.2
it follows that
\mathscr{C}\bullet p
also has bounded expansion. So we may conclude from the original statement of

that
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathsf{Minors}^{k}_{k}((\mathscr{C}\bullet p)^{\circ})
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. And we easily have
\mathsf{Minors}^{k}_{k}((\mathscr{C}\bullet p)^{\circ})\subseteq\mathsf{Minors}^{k^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})
where
k^{\prime}\coloneqq kp
, implying that
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathsf{Minors}^{k^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})
.

Second, we note the following converse of
Theorem

3.1
.

Lemma 3.10
.

For every

and a graph class
\mathscr{C}
of bounded expansion, the class
\mathsf{Minors}^{k}_{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})
is transducible with copying from
\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathscr{C}^{\circ}\bullet k
.
It is easy to see that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is transducible with copying from
\mathscr{C}
. Also, both adding a universal vertex and applying the
k
-blowup for a constant

preserves the property of having bounded expansion (see

2.2
), hence
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
also has bounded expansion. Finally, note that
\mathsf{Minors}^{k}_{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})=\mathsf{Minors}_{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ}\bullet k)=\mathsf{Minors}_{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
. As transductions are closed under composition, to conclude the proof it suffices to apply the following result from
[
2
]
to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
.

Claim 3.11

(
[
2
, Corollary 7.6]
)
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a graph class of bounded expansion
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, the class
\mathsf{Minors}_{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
is transducible from
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
.

We note that the original statement of
[

, Corollary 7.6]
assumes that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
has bounded

, but this is implied by the assumption that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
has bounded expansion; see the discussion in
[
2
]
.
∎

The discussion above justifies the following conclusion — a characterization of transducibility with copying among classes of bounded expansion.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
be graph classes of bounded expansion. Then
\mathscr{D}
is transducible with copying from
\mathscr{C}
if and only if there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathsf{Minors}_{k}^{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})
.

4
Asymptotics of the weak coloring numbers

In this section we use the characterization of
Theorem

3.1
to derive our non-transducibility results:
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
. The main idea is to use the asymptotics of the weak coloring numbers, expressed as a function of the distance parameter
d
, as a transduction invariant. We need a few definitions to speak about this in precise terms.

For functions
f,g\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, we shall say that
g

dominates

f
if there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f(n)\leqslant c\cdot g(cn)+c\qquad\textrm{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

The following simple observation is crucial: if
f
and
g
are polynomials of degrees
a
and
b
, respectively, and
a>b
, then
g
does
not
dominate
f
. This is because the expression
c\cdot g(cn)+c
is a polynomial (in
n
) of degree
b
, while
f(n)
is a polynomial of degree
a>b
.

For a graph class
\mathscr{C}
, we define a function

\pi_{\mathscr{C}}\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
as follows:

\pi_{\mathscr{C}}(d)\coloneqq\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(\mathscr{C}).

Let us observe the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D}
be graph classes such that
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathsf{Minors}^{k}_{k}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\pi_{\mathscr{C}}
dominates
\pi_{\mathscr{D}}
.

Proof.

Since adding a universal vertex to a graph can increase any weak
d
-coloring number by at most
1
, it suffices to bound the weak coloring numbers of
k
-congested depth-
k
minors of a graph in terms of the weak coloring numbers of the graph itself. Precisely, we will show that if
H
and
G
are graphs such that
H\in\mathsf{Minors}^{k}_{k}(G)
, then for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(H)\leqslant k\cdot\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1)d}(G).

(4.1)

By applying (
4.1
) to every graph
H\in\mathscr{D}
and the graph
G\in\mathscr{C}^{\circ}
that contains
H
as a
k
-congested depth-
k
minor, we obtain that

\pi_{\mathscr{D}}(d)=\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(\mathscr{D})\leqslant k\cdot\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1)d}(\mathscr{C}^{\circ})\leqslant k\cdot\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1)d}(\mathscr{C})+k=k\cdot\pi_{\mathscr{C}}((4k+1)d)+k,

thereby proving that
\pi_{\mathscr{C}}
dominates
\pi_{\mathscr{D}}
.

Towards (
4.1
), let
\eta
be a
k
-congested depth-
k
model of
H
in
G
, and let
\preccurlyeq
be a total order on
V(G)
such that
\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1))d}(G,\preccurlyeq)=\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1))d}(G)
. For every vertex
u
of
H
, let
\gamma(u)
be the
\preccurlyeq
-minimal vertex of
\eta(u)
. We now define a total order
\preccurlyeq^{\star}
on
V(H)
by pulling
\preccurlyeq
through
\gamma
: whenever
\gamma(u)\prec\gamma(u^{\prime})
for some
u,u^{\prime}\in V(H)
, we set
u\prec^{\star}u^{\prime}
. The order
\preccurlyeq^{\star}
between vertices
u
with equal
\gamma(u)
is chosen arbitrarily.

We now claim that

\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(H,\preccurlyeq^{\star})\leqslant k\cdot\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1)d}(G,\preccurlyeq)=k\cdot\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1)d}(G);

this will prove (
4.1
). Towards this end, suppose
u,v\in V(H)
are such that
u\in\mathsf{WReach}^{H,\preccurlyeq^{\star}}_{d}[v]
. Let
Q
be a path in
H
witnessing this membership:
Q
has length at most
d
, starts in
v
, ends in
u
, and all the vertices traversed by
Q
are not smaller in
\preccurlyeq^{\star}
than
u
. Consider an edge
xy
of
Q
. Noting that
\eta(x)
and
\eta(y)
touch and induce connected graphs of radius at most
d
, we may find a path
P_{xy}
in
G
of length at most
4k+1
that connects
\gamma(x)
with
\gamma(y)
and has all its vertices in
\eta(x)\cup\eta(y)
. By concatenating all the paths
P_{xy}
for
xy\in E(Q)
along
Q
, we obtain a walk
W
in
G
of length at most
(4k+1)d
with endpoints in
\gamma(u)
and
\gamma(v)
such that all the vertices visited by
W
belong to
\bigcup_{w\in V(Q)}\eta(w)
. Since
u
is the
\preccurlyeq^{\star}
-smallest vertex of
V(Q)
, by the construction of
\preccurlyeq^{\star}
we conclude that
\gamma(u)
is the
\preccurlyeq
-smallest vertex visited by
W
. This means that the walk
W
witnesses that
\gamma(u)\in\mathsf{WReach}_{(4k+1)d}^{G,\preccurlyeq}[\gamma(v)]
.

Now, for a fixed
v
there are at most
|\mathsf{WReach}_{(4k+1)d}[\gamma(v)]|\leqslant\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1)d}(G,\preccurlyeq)
candidates for the vertex
\gamma(u)
, since by the reasoning above, each such vertex must belong to
\mathsf{WReach}_{(4k+1)d}[\gamma(v)]
. As
\eta
is a
k
-congested model, for each
z\in\mathsf{WReach}_{(4k+1)d}[\gamma(v)]
there are at most
k
vertices
u
of
H
with
z=\gamma(u)
. We conclude that for a fixed
v\in V(H)
there are at most
k\cdot\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1)d}(G,\preccurlyeq)
candidates for a vertex
u\in V(H)
such that
u\in\mathsf{WReach}_{d}^{H,\preccurlyeq^{\star}}[v]
. This means that
\mathsf{wcol}_{d}(H,\preccurlyeq^{\star})\leqslant k\cdot\mathsf{wcol}_{(4k+1)d}(G,\preccurlyeq)
, as claimed.
∎

By combining
Theorem

3.1
with
Lemma

4.1
, we may immediately conclude the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathscr{C}
is a graph class of bounded expansion and
\mathscr{D}
is a weakly sparse graph class that is transducible from
\mathscr{C}
. Then
\pi_{\mathscr{C}}
dominates
\pi_{\mathscr{D}}
.

We may now use
Corollary

4.2
to conclude our main results. We recall them for convenience.

See
1.1

Proof.

For every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathscr{T}_{t}
be the class of graphs of treewidth at most
t
.
As proved in
[
8
]
, we have

\pi_{\mathscr{T}_{t}}(d)=\binom{d+t}{t},

which, for a fixed
t
, is a polynomial of degree
t
in
d
. Since
\pi_{\mathscr{T}_{t}}(d)
is a polynomial of degree
t
and
\pi_{\mathscr{T}_{t+1}}(d)
is a polynomial of degree
t+1
, we have that
\pi_{\mathscr{T}_{t}}(d)
does not dominate
\pi_{\mathscr{T}_{t+1}}(d)
. We conclude from
Corollary

4.2
that
\mathscr{T}_{t+1}
is not transducible from
\mathscr{T}_{t}
.
∎

See
1.2

Proof.

For every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathscr{H}_{t}
be the class of graphs of Hadwiger number at most
t
. The results of
[
8
]
and
[
15
]
show that

\pi_{\mathscr{H}_{t}}(d)\geqslant\Omega(d^{t-1})\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\pi_{\mathscr{H}_{t}}(d)\leqslant\mathcal{O}(d^{t}).

In particular,
\pi_{\mathscr{H}_{t}}
does not dominate
\pi_{\mathscr{H}_{t+2}}
, because the former is upper-bounded by a polynomial of degree
t
and the latter is lower bounded by a polynomial of degree
t+1
. By
Corollary

4.2
, we conclude that
\mathscr{H}_{t+2}
is not transducible from
\mathscr{H}_{t}
.
∎

See
1.3

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{T}_{4}
be the class of graphs of treewidth at most
4
and
\mathscr{Pl}
be the class of planar graphs. By the results of
[
8
,
15
]
, we have

\pi_{\mathscr{T}_{4}}(d)=\binom{d+4}{4}=\Theta(d^{4})\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\pi_{\mathscr{Pl}}(d)\leqslant\mathcal{O}(d^{3}).

As before, it now follows directly from
Corollary

4.2
that
\mathscr{T}_{4}
is not transducible from
\mathscr{Pl}
.
∎

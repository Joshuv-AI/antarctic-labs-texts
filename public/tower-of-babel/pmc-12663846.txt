Argonaute combined with isothermal amplification for simultaneous detection of Vibrio parahaemolyticus and the tetracycline resistance gene tetA in water and food samples
Authors: Haibo Zhou, Jun Yang, Feng Xue, Wei Shen, Yiyu Cheng, Xinmei Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractV. parahaemolyticus is one of the most important foodborne pathogens and poses a serious threat to public health because of its high incidence and transferable antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs). In this study, a novel approach for simultaneous detection of V. parahaemolyticus and the tetracycline resistance gene tetA was developed by reasonably integrating rapid loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) with the powerful cleavage ability of Pyrococcus furiosus Argonaute (PfAgo). The guide DNAs (gDNAs) were carefully designed to complete continuous signal transduction. With this strategy, the dual-target LAMP amplicons that were originally difficult to distinguish could be ingeniously converted into fluorescent signals. The duplex LAMP-PfAgo method was used to complete the entire process within 45 min at constant temperature and showed no cross-reactivity with non-target pathogens. The results demonstrated superior analytical performance, with a limit of detection (LOD) of as low as 6.68 CFU/mL for the V. parahaemolyticus carrying the tetA gene and a dynamic range from 10° to 106 CFU/mL. Furthermore, the proposed method exhibited satisfactory sensitivity and feasibility for use in identifying contaminated samples (water, salmon and oysters). In summary, this study provides effective technical support for the on-site detection of pathogenic bacteria and tetracycline resistance genes, presenting promising prospects for food safety testing, clinical diagnosis, and environmental monitoring in the One Health context.Keywords: V. parahaemolyticus, Tetracycline resistance gene tetA, Simultaneous detection, Loop-mediated isothermal amplification, Pyrococcus furiosus argonaute

Introduction

V. parahaemolyticus is a major microbial pathogen associated with the consumption of aquatic products in many countries and is widely disseminated in marine and estuarine environments (Flores-Villaseñor et al., 2024; Roy et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2024). The latest surveillance data from the China National Center for Food Safety Risk Assessment (CFSA) revealed that V. parahaemolyticus has once again risen to the top among the outbreak events of microbial foodborne diseases (Fan et al., 2024). The misuse and overuse of antimicrobials are the main drivers leading to the rapid development and spread of antimicrobial resistance, increasing the adaptability of pathogens carrying resistance-related genes in environmental settings (Liu et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025). Tetracycline is usually widely used as a veterinary drug for disease treatment, prevention and growth promotion, thereby leading to relatively high residual concentrations in food and aquatic environments (Lyu et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2025). Preliminary research revealed that subinhibitory concentrations of tetracycline promoted horizontal transfer of the tetracycline resistance gene tetA, often resulting in the rapid emergence and spread of the corresponding resistance phenotype (Zhou et al., 2024). tetA is a gene encoding an efflux pump protein that can actively export drugs out of the cell and thus confer resistance; this gene is among the most ubiquitous of ARGs identified worldwide (Sun et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025).

To date, the traditional culture-based method has still been commonly used as the gold standard for detecting V. parahaemolyticus (GB 4789.7-2013, China) and its antimicrobial resistance (M45-A3, CLSI). However, these types of methods are tedious, time-consuming, and labor intensive. With the rapid development of molecular biology and genomics, various nucleic acid-based techniques, including real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR), digital PCR (dPCR) and metagenomic sequencing, have been used to detect foodborne pathogens and ARGs (Davis et al., 2023; Mancusi et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2023). In recent years, emerging isothermal methods have attracted a great deal of attention because of their consistent temperature conditions and broader application prospects. Loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) technology was initially developed by Notomi et al. (2000). The principle of LAMP involves a strand-displacing DNA polymerase and a set of four to six specially designed primers recognizing six distinct regions of the target sequence, generating the final product, which is a mixture of stem-loop DNAs with various stem lengths and cauliflower-like structures with several loops at a consistent temperature (∼65 °C) (Kim et al., 2024; Q. F. Li et al., 2025; Notomi et al., 2000). LAMP products are detected primarily by turbidity analysis, gel electrophoresis, and real-time fluorescence signals. However, the issues of aerosol contamination and false-positive results often influence diagnostic interpretation. Moreover, there is still the challenge of being unable to detect multiple amplification products simultaneously in the same reaction because of the nonspecific direct readout through fluorescence dye indicators, such as SYBR green and SYTO, among others.

Recently, programmable nucleases such as the clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeat/associated protein (CRISPR/Cas) system have been introduced to improve the specificity and convenience of isothermal nucleic acid amplification techniques (Lee and Oh, 2022; Y. Y. Li et al., 2025; Sen et al., 2024). The main limitations of CRISPR-based methods are the strong reliance on protospacer-adjacent motif (PAM) and the ease of degradation of CRISPR RNA (crRNA) primers, which poses obstacles to multiplexed assays, unless additional Cas orthologs are added (Gootenberg et al., 2018; Shen et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024). Compared with the CRISPR/Cas system, Argonaute (Ago) nucleases not only have high specificity for endonuclease activity but can also overcome the aforementioned shortcomings through their unique properties. Pyrococcus furiosus argonaute (PfAgo) is a thermophilic Ago protein from Archaea that is gaining increasing attention in numerous applications (Li et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023; Ye et al., 2024). In this work, a method capable of simultaneously detecting V. parahaemolyticus and the tetracycline resistance gene tetA was innovatively developed by combining LAMP and the PfAgo system. To achieve this goal, a set of rationally designed gDNAs and TaqMan probes was screened to identify different fluorescent signals, which serve as a “triple-checker”, including the specificity of LAMP primers, gDNA-specific recognition sites, and complementary recognition probes. This method provides a new, rapid, specific and sensitive tool for point-of-care testing (POCT) applications to support early surveillance, risk assessment and intervention in multiple fields.

Materials and methods

Bacterial strains and reagents

In this study, 20 V. parahaemolyticus isolates obtained from aquatic products were used, along with standard strains such as V. parahaemolyticus (ATCC 17802, ATCC 33847), Vibrio alginolyticus (ATCC 17749, ATCC 33787), Vibrio vulnificus (ATCC 27562, CICC 25009), Vibrio cholerae (CICC 23794, CICC 23795), Salmonella Typhimurium (ATCC 14028), Escherichia coli (ATCC 25922), Staphylococcus aureus (CMCC(B) 26003), Listeria monocytogenes (ATCC, 19115), Pseudomonas aeruginosa (ATCC 9027), and Bacillus cereus (CMCC(B) 63303). All the strains were stored in the Microbiology Laboratory at the Nanjing Institute for Food and Drug Control and are described in Table S1. The 10 × isothermal amplification buffer II, MgSO4 (100 mM), and Bst 3.0 DNA polymerase were purchased from New England Biolabs (USA). The dNTP mixture (10 mM each) was purchased from Beijing Solarbio (China). Syto 9 Green was purchased from Invitrogen (USA). The 10 × PfAgo endonuclease reaction buffer, MnCl2 (40 mM), and PfAgo endonuclease (200 U/μL) were purchased from Beijing Tiosbio Biotech (China). Lateral flow strips were purchased from Shanghai Sangon Biotech (China). Three percent (w/v) NaCl alkaline peptone water (APW) and lysogeny broth (LB) were obtained from Beijing Land Bridge (China).

Design of LAMP primers, gDNAs and probes

The self-screened vpa1585 gene of V. parahaemolyticus, which is highly specific and species conserved (GenBank: BA000032.2), and the tetracycline resistance gene tetA (GenBank: CP134785.1), were targeted. LAMP primers were designed using PrimerExplorer V5 software (https://primerexplorer.jp/e/), and each set of primers comprised two external primers (F3 and B3) and two internal primers (FIP and BIP) (Table S2). The FIP and BIP primers consisted of two distinct sequences, F1c (complementary to F1) + F2 and B1c (complementary to B1) + B2, respectively. Based on the LAMP products of two targets, eight distinct sets of gDNAs (gF1/gT1∼gF8/gT8) with a length of 16 bp and a phosphate group at the 5’ end were tailored for subsequent effective cleavage of the PfAgo/gDNA system. Two sets of probes (Reporter1, Reporter2) were subsequently designed for the vpa1585 (FAM tag) and tetA (HEX tag) genes. Among them, Reporter1 was used in combination with gF1/gT1∼gF4/gT4, and Reporter2 was used in combination with gF5/gT5∼gF8/gT8 (Table S3). Through a series of trials evaluating different combinations, the set with the highest specificity was chosen for duplex LAMP-PfAgo. All primers, gDNAs and probes used in this study were synthesized by GenScript Biotech Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China).

Bacterial cultivation and DNA extraction

The Vibrio bacteria were cultured in 3 % NaCl APW media at 37 °C, and the non-Vibrio strains were cultured in LB media at 37 °C. The genomic DNA of all the strains was extracted using the boiling water method described in previous work (Zhou et al., 2023). In brief, after the fresh cultures were centrifuged at 6000×g for 5 min, the supernatant was carefully removed. The precipitate was mixed thoroughly with 100 μL of sterilized water, boiled in a metal bath for 10 min and immediately cooled in an ice bath for 10 min. Subsequent centrifugation (14000×g, 5 min) was subsequently conducted to collect the supernatant containing the target DNA, which was used as the template for LAMP-PfAgo detection. Similarly, different concentrations of bacteria were assessed in the same manner.

LAMP amplification system

The LAMP reaction was performed in a final volume of 25 μL according to the manufacturer's protocols, and the initial reaction mixture included 2.5 μL of 10 × isothermal amplification buffer II, 6 mM MgSO4, 1.4 mM dNTPs, 1.6 μM of both FIP and BIP, 0.2 μM of both F3 and B3, 320 U/mL Bst 3.0 DNA polymerase, 2 μL of DNA template, and sterile distilled water up to 25 μL. These mixtures were gently vortexed and incubated at 69 °C for 30 min in a C1000 Touch™ Thermal Cycler (Bio-Rad, USA). To explore the optimal values for each factor, variations in the temperature (62.5, 64.8, 67.9, and 69 °C), concentrations of both FIP and BIP (0.8, 1.2, 1.6, and 2.0 μM), MgSO4 volume (4, 6, 8, and 10 mM), dNTP volume (1.0, 1.4, 1.8, and 2.0 mM) and DNA polymerase volume (160, 240, 320, and 400 U/mL) were systematically tested. Moreover, the SYTO 9 Green fluorescent dye was introduced for real-time monitoring of LAMP products.

PfAgo cleavage reaction

After LAMP amplification, 2 μL of the reaction product was added to the PfAgo cleavage mixture in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. Each cleavage reaction consisted of 2 μL of 10 × PfAgo endonuclease reaction buffer, 1 μM 5′-phosphorylated gDNAs, 0.5 μM FAM or HEX-labeled single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) probe, 2 mM MnCl2, and 40 U/μL PfAgo, and the final volume was brought up to 20 μL with sterile distilled water. The cleavage reaction was performed at 95 °C for 15 min, and the fluorescence readout was recorded every 1 min in a C1000 Touch™ Thermal Cycler (Bio-Rad). In addition, the detection results could be judged by the color change under ultraviolet (UV) light irradiation (302 nm). No fluorescence was observed in the negative control under UV light irradiation, while the positive samples emitted visible green fluorescence.

The dosage of the LAMP product, PfAgo, gDNAs, MnCl2 and probe are important factors affecting the cleavage efficiency and fluorescence intensity of the LAMP-PfAgo method. To achieve optimal performance, different concentrations of the LAMP product (1, 2, 3, or 4 μL), PfAgo (10, 20, 40, or 80 U/μL), gDNAs (0.25, 0.5, 1, or 2 μM), MnCl2 (1, 2, 3, or 4 mM) and probe (0.5, 1, 2, or 3 μM) were gradually added. Thus, the reaction speed and reagent cost were regarded as the main evaluation criteria.

LAMP binding lateral flow dipstick detection (LFD)

For the LAMP-LFD, the overall reaction system was similar to that used for the LAMP assay, except for the biotin modification of the FIP and the addition of an extra FAM- or digoxigenin-labeled ssDNA probe. For vpa1585, the sequence of the probe was 5′-FAM-CTTGGCATCAGCTACCTACTCGAAATGTTT. For tetA, the sequence of the probe was 5′-Digoxigenin-CGGCTCGGCGAAAGGCGGGCACTCATGCTC. The LAMP-LFD experiment consisted of LAMP reaction, probe incubation, and LFD strip detection. First, the LAMP reaction was the same as that described in section 2.4. Then, 1 μL of probe (10 μM) was directly added to the tube containing the first round of amplification products and thoroughly vortexed, followed by incubation at 69 °C for 5 min. Subsequently, the reaction products were diluted with sterile distilled water at a ratio of 1:20, and 80 μL of the diluted products was pipetted into the loading well of the LFD strips, which could be visually interpreted by the naked eye within 5 min. On this basis, the appearance of two bands at both the control line (C line) and the test line (T line) was considered a positive result, whereas only one band at the C line was considered a negative result. If there was no band at the C line, the test result was invalid.

Feasibility test of duplex LAMP-PfAgo

Based on the optimized conditions of dual-target detection described in section 2.5, the duplex LAMP-PfAgo detection was performed as follows (20 μL): 2 μL of 10 × PfAgo endonuclease reaction buffer, 2 μM 5′-phosphorylated gDNAs and 1 μM ssDNA probe for the vpa1585 gene and tetA gene, 2 mM MnCl2, and 20 U/μL PfAgo. Specificity is the primary consideration in assessing the feasibility of duplex LAMP-PfAgo, which was performed from two viewpoints. For the species-specific gene vpa1585, the genomic DNA of 22 V. parahaemolyticus strains and 12 non-target strains was tested. For the tetracycline resistance gene tetA, the genomic DNA of seven V. parahaemolyticus strains (NJIFDCVp19, NJIFDCVp20, NJIFDCVp21, NJIFDCVp40, NJIFDCVp52, NJIFDCVp147, and NJIFDCVp161) carrying the tetA gene and 27 non-target strains were tested.

Sensitivity and validation of real samples

In terms of sensitivity, the V. parahaemolyticus standard strain ATCC 17802 and isolate NJIFDCVp52 harboring the tetA gene were chosen as representative positive controls. Cultures in the logarithmic growth phase were diluted to achieve concentrations ranging from 10° CFU/mL to 106 CFU/mL. Sterile distilled water served as a no-template control. The LOD was determined by using the established method in this study.

To evaluate the reliability and functionality of the duplex LAMP-PfAgo assay for the detection of V. parahaemolyticus in the field, salmon and raw oyster samples were purchased from a local market and confirmed to be negative for V. parahaemolyticus using the standard national method (GB 4789.7-2013, China). The water was collected from the Gucheng lake (Nanjing, China), and then filtered with a 0.22 μm Millipore Express membrane (Merck, Ireland) until use. In detail, 2.5 mL of bacterial suspensions with serial tenfold dilutions were mixed with these negative samples to obtain artificially contaminated samples with varying concentrations of V. parahaemolyticus (102–108 CFU/mL). Next, 225 mL of 3 % NaCl APW medium was added, and the mixture was thoroughly homogenized with a Bagmixer (Interscience, France). The nucleic acids from every real sample were analyzed using the proposed duplex LAMP-PfAgo method.

Statistical analysis

The fluorescence was measured using CFX Maestro software (Bio-Rad). The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 software (GraphPad Software, Inc., USA), and the results are presented as the means ± standard deviations. All the experiments were generally performed with three independent replicates. Differences were considered statistically significant at P values of less than 0.05.

Results and discussion

Principle of the designed LAMP-PfAgo assay

A PfAgo-powered molecular platform was successfully developed for the simultaneous detection of V. parahaemolyticus and the tetracycline resistance gene tetA in water and food. The working principle is schematically illustrated in Fig. 1A. PfAgo played a central role in splitting the amplified DNA fragments, which had the ability to guide short 5′-phosphorylated gDNAs to bind the target DNA, resulting in primary cleavage between the 10th and 11th bases from the 5′ end of the gDNA. More importantly, the generated 5′-phosphorylated ssDNA (gN) also served as a new gDNA to bind the complementary target sequence for secondary cleavage. Based on this characteristic, the target sequence involved in secondary cleavage could be specially designed as a fluorescent tag-labeled ssDNA probe (such as FAM), thereby generating a detectable fluorescent signal (Kou et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024, Wang et al., 2024, Wang et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024).

Schematic diagram of the platform for V. parahaemolyticus detection. (A) Working principle of the PfAgo-based detection. The red arrows represent the cutting sites, gDNA1 and gDNA2 represent the input gDNAs, and gN represents the generated gDNA released from the target double-stranded DNA. (B) The complete process of LAMP-PfAgo for the simultaneous detection of two targets.

Fig. 1B shows the complete process of detecting potential tetracycline-resistant V. parahaemolyticus via the detection platform. First, the genomic DNA in the samples was rapidly extracted by thermal lysis. The obtained DNA was then used for LAMP amplification (30 min) with the species-specific gene vpa1585 and the tetracycline resistance gene tetA. Afterward, the LAMP amplicons were added to the PfAgo reaction by stepwise PfAgo cleavage (15 min). FAM- and HEX-labeled fluorescent reporters that are complementary to the secondary cleavage products were designed to correspond to vpa1585 and tetA, respectively, and the results could be recognized by fluorescence reading devices or UV light irradiation.

Optimization of the LAMP reaction

Considering the complexity of the duplex system, several key factors were optimized for the LAMP reaction to detect each individual target. Initially, different temperatures (62.5, 64.8, 67.9, and 69 °C) were assessed. The rate of fluorescence generation was the lowest when the reaction temperature of the vpa1585 gene was 62.5 °C (Fig. S1A). Similar results were observed for the tetA gene (Fig. S1F). Further optimization of the FIP/BIP, MgSO4, dNTP and DNA polymerase concentrations was performed. The concentrations of MgSO4 (Fig. S1C, Fig. S1H) and dNTPs (Fig. S1D, Fig. S1I) significantly influence the reaction. Specifically, with an increasing MgSO4 concentration, the rate of fluorescence generation gradually decreased, indicating that excessive Mg2+ inhibited the amplification efficiency of the reaction (Fig. S1C, Fig. S1H). A similar inhibition phenomenon occurred for dNTPs (Fig. S1D, Fig. S1I), although dNTPs provide raw materials for nucleic acid synthesis in a gene amplification reaction. After comprehensive consideration of the reaction speed and cost savings, a reaction temperature of 69 °C, 4 mM MgSO4, 1.4 mM dNTPs, 240 U/mL Bst 3.0 DNA polymerase, and 1.2 μM FIP/BIP were determined as the optimal reaction conditions.

Under these conditions, the amplified fragments were detected by electrophoresis with 2 % agarose gels, which presented typical ladder-like and clear bands of various sizes (Fig. S2). In addition, the white precipitate at the bottom of the positive control tube was more visible than that of the no-template control (Fig. S2). The LAMP reaction releases large amounts of byproducts (pyrophosphate ions), which react with magnesium ions in the reaction mixture to form pyrophosphate magnesium precipitates (Lee et al., 2008).

gDNA screening

For effective cleavage by PfAgo in the LAMP-PfAgo system, eight groups of gDNAs were subsequently designed for each of the two genes between the F1c and B2 regions. The results in Fig. S3 indicate that the fluorescence signal was represented by qPCR-like S-shaped amplification curves, which are characterized by baseline, logarithmic, and plateau phases. The rate of signal generation varied significantly across different gDNA combinations. For the vpa1585 gene, the fluorescence intensity of three gDNAs (gF1/gT1, gF5/gT5, and gF8/gT8) almost reached a maximum within 10 min (Fig. S3A).

Notably, determining whether the continuous cleavage of two targets could be completed and then generate the corresponding fluorescence signal has become challenging. Thus, gF5/gT5, which has the highest enzyme cleavage efficiency of the vpa1585 gene, was selected for subsequent combinations with eight sets of gDNAs from the tetA gene. For ease of observation and analysis, the results of duplex LAMP-PfAgo detection were divided into three parts (Fig. S3B, Fig. S3C, and Fig. S3D). As shown in Fig. S3B, the positive control of the target vpa1585 exhibited a normal ascending curve in eight different combinations, and the negative control showed no significant fluorescence fluctuation. This finding indicated that the current reaction system does not adversely affect the interpretation of the detection results for the vpa1585 gene. From the positive control of the target tetA (Fig. S3C), the obvious differences in the cleavage efficiency of the different guides were further verified, whereas the negative controls gF3/gT3, gF6/gT6, gF7/gT7 and gF8/gT8 yielded false-positive signals (Fig. S3D). Overall, vpa1585-gF5/gT5&tetA-gF2/gT2 was the final optimal combination without observed cross-activity.

Optimization of the PfAgo-mediated assay

An optimization analysis concerning the effects of the LAMP product, PfAgo, gDNAs, MnCl2, and the probe volume on the cleavage efficiency was performed to achieve the ideal reaction conditions. First, the amount of LAMP product directly affects the initial state of the PfAgo cleavage reaction. As shown in Fig. 2A, when the dose of LAMP product was 1 μL, the enzyme cleavage efficiency was the lowest, and when the dose of the LAMP product was increased to 2 μL, 3 μL, or 4 μL, the fluorescence generation time gradually decreased. Therefore, the optimal dose of the LAMP product was determined to be 4 μL.

Optimization of the duplex LAMP-PfAgo system. Examination of enzyme cleavage fluorescence signals with different levels of LAMP product (A), PfAgo (B), gDNAs (C), MnCl2 (D), and probe (E).

An appropriate amount of PfAgo can guarantee the effective generation of fluorescence signals in the reaction system. As depicted in Fig. 2B, there was no significant difference in the fluorescence generation time between the two PfAgo concentrations of 10 U/μL and 20 U/μL for the vpa1585 gene, but no obvious amplification curves were observed for the tetA gene when the PfAgo concentration was 10 U/μL. To reduce the reagent cost while maintaining the reaction performance, the PfAgo concentration was determined to be 20 U/μL. Further optimization of the gDNAs was performed. The fluorescence generation time gradually decreased with the increasing amount of vpa1585 gDNAs (Fig. 2C). When the amount of tetA gDNAs exceeded 0.25 μM, the fluorescence curves almost overlapped, and there was no significant difference in the fluorescence generation time. Thus, a 2 μM concentration of gDNAs was selected for the reaction.

Other components, such as MnCl2 and probes, were also tested. MnCl2 plays a crucial role in the cleavage activity of PfAgo (Li et al., 2022; H. X. Li et al., 2025). As shown in Fig. 2D, with the increasing MnCl2 concentration, the fluorescence generation time gradually increased for the vpa1585 gene, and when the MnCl2 concentration was 1 mM, the enzyme cleavage efficiency was the highest. However, the opposite trend was observed for the tetA gene. Lastly, different probe concentrations were investigated. The probe is carefully designed to release distinct fluorescence signals, which is a critical step in the entire dual-target detection process (Su et al., 2025). The fluorescence signal intensity clearly reached a detectable level when the probe concentration was 0.5 μM (Fig. 2E). The signal value of the vpa1585 gene increased with an increase in the fluorescence probe, with the highest signal intensity observed for the 3 μM probe. After considering the cost without affecting the overall effect, the optimal probe concentration was determined to be 1 μM. As a result, the optimal PfAgo cleavage conditions (4 μL of LAMP product, 20 U/μL PfAgo enzyme, 2 μM gDNAs, 2 mM MnCl2, and 1 μM fluorescence probe) were then used for subsequent experiments, confirming the efficacy of the LAMP-PfAgo method for detecting the vpa1585 and tetA genes.

Performance of duplex LAMP-PfAgo

Selectivity is key for a dual detection biosensor. The signal transduction of specific amplification for the target genes during LAMP was screened by skillfully associating them with fluorescence donors (FAM and HEX). Under the optimal reaction conditions, the unequivocal identification of the vpa1585 (FAM tag) and tetA (HEX tag) genes without any cross-reactivity was achieved by a single endonuclease (Fig. 3A), which exhibited superior multiplexed capability and flexibility relative to CRISPR/Cas-based molecular methods (Hu et al., 2025; Mao et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024, Wang et al., 2024, Wang et al., 2024). To test the specificity of the established duplex LAMP-PfAgo method, 22 V. parahaemolyticus strains and 12 non-V. parahaemolyticus strains were analyzed. As displayed in Fig. 3B, positive vpa1585 signals were detected in all 22 target strains, whereas no substantial changes were observed in V. alginolyticus, V. vulnificus, V. cholerae, S. Typhimurium, E. coli, S. aureus, L. monocytogenes, P. aeruginosa, or B. cereus. Similarly, the method effectively distinguished the tetracycline resistance gene tetA in seven positive V. parahaemolyticus strains (Fig. 3C) without presenting positive signals for 27 negative bacteria. Fig. 3D shows the detection results of seven vpa1585/tetA double-positive strains. The fluorescence values of FAM and HEX exhibited discernible changes, which suggested that the duplex LAMP-PfAgo platform had satisfactory specificity for the dual gene-containing strains.

Violin plots were used to display the test results of the vpa1585 (B) and tetA (C) genes in 34 samples. (D) Seven V. parahaemolyticus strains carrying the tetA gene were evaluated using duplex LAMP-PfAgo. NTC, no-template control. Asterisks indicate significant difference (∗∗∗∗P < 0.0001).

Detection in real samples

To evaluate the sensitivity of the proposed duplex LAMP-PfAgo method, a series of tenfold-diluted strains (ATCC 17802 and NJIFDCVp52) ranging from 10° to 106 CFU/mL were employed. The LOD of this method for single-gene detection (ATCC 17802) was determined to be 5.47 CFU/mL (Fig. 4A), and the same result was observed under UV light irradiation (Fig. 4B). In addition, different concentrations of NJIFDCVp52 harboring the tetA gene were measured, and the results revealed that, compared with the no-template control, a clear dual signal could be observed even at an extremely low concentration (6.68 CFU/mL) (Fig. 4C). It can be clearly seen that there was no consistent relationship between fluorescence intensity and bacterial concentration (Fig. 4A and C). Therefore, it is recommend that this method be used primarily for the qualitative detection of target bacteria. To obtain readouts that can be easily judged by the naked eye, a LAMP-LFD method was established. The sensitivity analysis results of the single LAMP-LFD assay revealed that the LOD for both target genes reached only 104 CFU/mL (Fig. S4), which was four orders of magnitude lower than that of the LAMP-PfAgo method. In our previous study, an absolute quantitative method for V. parahaemolyticus was developed, and the LOD of duplex droplet dPCR (7.32 × 101 CFU/mL) was lower than that of duplex qPCR (7.32 × 102 CFU/mL) (Zhou et al., 2023). J. B. Wang et al. (2024) constructed a single CRISPR/Cas12a system combined with recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA) and an immunochromatographic test strip, and the LOD of V. parahaemolyticus was 1.4 × 102 CFU/mL. Du et al. (2024) formulated a phagomagnetic separation-ATP bioluminescence (PhMS-BL) assay for detecting V. parahaemolyticus, which achieved an LOD of 78 CFU/mL. Therefore, our duplex LAMP-PfAgo technique has greater advantages than the recently reported detection methods for V. parahaemolyticus in terms of the LOD.

(B) Fluorescence images of the same samples detected in (A) under UV irradiation. (C) Sensitivity of the vpa1585 and tetA genes, with tenfold serial dilutions of bacterial suspensions of V. parahaemolyticus strain NJIFDCVp52. Detection of different concentrations of V. parahaemolyticus in artificially contaminated water (D), salmon (E) and oysters (F). NTC, no-template control.

Currently, the ubiquitous presence of V. parahaemolyticus and antimicrobial resistance genes poses a major threat to food and environmental safety. Thus, the practicability of the duplex LAMP-PfAgo was then investigated in some artificially contaminated samples. The isolate NJIFDCVp52 was diluted to final concentrations of 10°, 101, 102, 103, 104, 105, and 106 CFU/mL in lake water, salmon and raw oyster samples. As displayed in Fig. 4D, water samples contaminated with V. parahaemolyticus at a concentration of 3.52 × 10° CFU/mL could be detected without enrichment, whereas no positive signals were observed in food samples (Fig. 4E and F). Notably, the LOD in food samples was one order of magnitude greater than that in pure cultures with no pre-enrichment steps. This observation might be due to interference effects caused by multiple matrices in the actual samples; similar slight inhibitory phenomena have been previously reported elsewhere (Z. W. Wang et al., 2024). Furthermore, the food samples with an initial contamination of 3.52 × 10° CFU/mL could be detected again after more than 2 h of culture (Table S4). Therefore, the appropriate pre-enrichment step can be selected on the basis of the actual situation to satisfy the needs of various detection scenarios. To our knowledge, few studies have investigated the application of PfAgo in the concurrent detection of pathogens and ARGs. The single LAMP-PfAgo assay established by Yu et al. (2024) targeting the hly gene could detect L. monocytogenes at as few as 1.8 × 101 copies. The addition of a lateral flow biosensor (LFB) step made PfAgo-LFB a superior portable approach for detecting S. Typhimurium at concentrations as low as 10° CFU/mL, which is similar to the results of this study (Qiao et al., 2024). Overall, our method has good practical application potential and meets the “zero tolerance” requirement in accordance with national food safety standards.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we first established an innovative duplex LAMP-PfAgo method for the simultaneous detection of V. parahaemolyticus and the tetracycline resistance gene tetA in complex samples. This method combines the advantages of isothermal amplification and PfAgo's DNA-specific cleavage, which not only increases the specificity of LAMP but also allows for the implementation of multiplex detection. After various reaction parameters were optimized, the entire detection process was quickly finished within 45 min, with an LOD of 6.68 CFU/mL. Additionally, compared with laboratory instruments, the platform proposed in this study provides a valuable tool with high specificity, ultrasensitivity, and on-site capability in nucleic acid testing, which is compatible with miniaturized fluorescence devices, highlighting its potential applications in microbial infection management.

Unification and Texture Universality: The Essence of Hermiticity
Pralay Chakraborty, Subhankar Roy

Abstract
A unified framework sheltering the type-I Dirac seesaw, based on the Δ ⁡ ( 27 ) × Z 7 × Z 5 × Z 4 × Z 3 \Delta(27)\times Z_{7}\times Z_{5}\times Z_{4}\times Z_{3} symmetry, is proposed, preserving the naturalness of Yukawa couplings. It helps to visualize mass matrices within the lepton and quark sectors in terms of three universal parameters : Σ 1 \Sigma_{1} , Σ 2 \Sigma_{2} , and Σ 3 \Sigma_{3} , highlighting that the down-type quark and light neutrino mass matrices exhibit a Hermitian texture.



I
Introduction

The quark and lepton sectors exhibit significant differences rather than being similar. We see that the top quark mass lies around
173

In addition, there is a significant difference between the mixing patterns of quarks and leptons. Specifically, in the quark sector, the mixing matrix, known as the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix:

\theta_{13}\sim 9^{\circ}

Esteban et al. 2024

V_{CKM}\sim I

IV
Summary and Discussion

In the present work, we investigate whether both quarks and lepton sectors can be studied starting from common mass matrix textures. In this regard, we propose similar textures for both the lepton and quark sectors. Specifically, a Hermitian texture for the down type quarks and neutrinos. We observe that this texture is consistent with experimental data, successfully explaining the observed fermion masses and mixing patterns.

It is important to study the origin of the proposed textures from first principles. While the quark and charged lepton mass matrices can be explained through the standard Higgs mechanism, we adopt the type-I Dirac seesaw mechanism to realize the neutrino mass matrix, assuming neutrinos to be purely Dirac type. As an important feature of our model, we observe that both the quark and neutrino sectors are connected through three common parameters
\Sigma_{1}
,
\Sigma_{2}
, and
\Sigma_{3}
. In addition, to preserve naturalness and avoid fine-tuning, we ensure the Yukawa couplings of both down-type and neutrino sectors to be
\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
coefficients.

In addition, we would like to mention a key feature of the proposed model. We address the hierarchy of charged leptons and up-type quarks starting from the FN mechanism
Froggatt and Nielsen 1979
, where both cut-off scales and the VEVs of the scalar fields are involved. At first glance, it may seem misleading that the Yukawa terms of the down-type quarks and neutrino sectors are suppressed by
\sim 1/\Lambda,1/\Lambda^{2}
, whereas for charged leptons and up-type quarks the suppressions are of the order
1/\Lambda^{10}
and
1/\Lambda^{7}
, respectively. Here, it is important to mention that the involvement of the VEVs of different scalar fields plays a crucial role in addressing this anomaly. In both lepton and quark sectors, the powers of the scalar fields appear differently and thus naturally explain the desired results. Hence, the cut-off scale
\Lambda
is not the sole suppression factor in our proposed model. We also highlight that the involvement of auxiliary groups restricts other possibilities of
1/\Lambda^{n}
, with
n>10
for the charged lepton sector and
n>7
for the quark sector, from appearing in the theory. A similar scenario is discussed in Ref.
Dey and Roy 2025
.

The present work deals with nine scalar field multiplets as given in Table (
1
), and in reality, there exist thirty-seven real scalar fields. In this regard, the strategy of constructing the scalar potential and obtaining the minimization conditions is not straightforward. We obtain thirty-seven
optimisation
conditions that may correspond to either maxima or minima. To check whether the chosen vacuum alignment leads to an actual minimum, the Hessian matrix,
H_{ij}=(\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial\Phi_{i}\partial\Phi_{j}})_{0}
, must be evaluated. The required condition for a minimum to occur is that the eigenvalues of
H
are positive (or zero for Goldstone states), or that the principal cofactors are positive definite. In the context of the present work, this task is quite challenging, as the Hessian matrix is of dimension thirty-seven. However, we consider the optimization conditions as the minimization conditions based on the fact that the potential (or the Hessian) contains several free parameters,(
\mu^{2}
’s and
\lambda
’s). Thus, by tuning these parameters appropriately, one may achieve the desired conditions. However, this task is quite tedious and beyond the scope of the present work.

Clinical utility of the HFA-ICOS risk tool in real-world HER2-positive breast cancer patients receiving therapy
Authors: Lama Alfehaid, Nada Alsuhebany, Khalid Alamer, Ahmed S Alanazi, Hadi Skouri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe HFA–ICOS baseline cardiovascular risk stratification framework is widely recommended to guide surveillance in patients receiving potentially cardiotoxic cancer therapies, yet real-world evidence supporting its clinical risk separation remains limited.MethodsWe conducted a multicenter retrospective study of adults with HER2-positive breast cancer treated at tertiary centers from 2016 to 2023. Baseline cardiovascular risk was classified by the HFA–ICOS framework (low to very high). The primary outcome was composite cardiotoxicity, defined by a decline in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), deterioration in global longitudinal strain (GLS), ECG/arrhythmia events, or elevated cardiac biomarkers. Cardiovascular risk rates and patterns were compared across categories. Discrimination and calibration were explored, with logistic regression and Kaplan–Meier analyses performed.ResultsAmong 687 patients, 273 (39.7%) developed composite cardiotoxicity during follow-up. Cardiotoxicity occurred across all HFA–ICOS risk categories, with overlap in event rates and timing. Discrimination analyses showed modest performance for LVEF, GLS, ECG, and composite outcomes, with AUCs of 0.51–0.55. Sensitivity was low (0.28–0.37), while specificity was moderate (0.73–0.74). Calibration analyses indicated acceptable risk prediction. Multivariable models adjusted for age, comorbidities, baseline LVEF, prior anthracycline, and radiation therapy showed that baseline HFA–ICOS category was not independently associated with cardiotoxicity (aOR: 0.88; 95% CI: 0.56–1.37). Kaplan–Meier curves showed overlapping event-free survival across risk groups.ConclusionsIn this real-world cohort of HER2-positive breast cancer patients, baseline HFA–ICOS stratification showed limited ability to clearly distinguish cardiotoxicity risk across categories. These findings suggest that baseline risk assessment alone may be insufficient for individualized prediction and should be complemented by dynamic, on-treatment surveillance strategies.Keywords: HFA-ICOS, HER2-positive breast cancer, Cardiotoxicity, Cardio-oncology, Risk stratification

Introduction

Cardiovascular complications are a frequent and clinically significant consequence of modern oncologic therapy, particularly in individuals with HER2-positive breast cancer who receive HER2-targeted agents such as trastuzumab, pertuzumab, trastuzumab-emtansine (T-DM1), and trastuzumab-deruxtecan (T-DXd). Although these therapies have markedly improved survival [1–4], they are associated with an increased risk of cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction (CTRCD), with reported incidences varying according to baseline cardiac status, prior anthracycline exposure, and surveillance intensity [5–7]. Early identification of patients at higher cardiovascular risk is essential to guide monitoring strategies, prevent treatment interruptions, and ensure long-term cardiac safety.

In response to this clinical need, the Heart Failure Association of the European Society of Cardiology (HFA-ESC), together with the International Cardio-Oncology Society (ICOS), developed a structured baseline cardiovascular risk stratification framework that integrates patient-specific cardiovascular risk factors, previous cardiotoxic treatments, biomarkers, and baseline echocardiographic parameters [8]. The instrument stratifies patients into four categories, low, moderate, high, and very high risk, to guide surveillance intensity, referral to cardio-oncology services, and preventive strategies. This framework has since been incorporated into ESC cardio-oncology guidelines and is increasingly used in routine practice to support individualized clinical decision-making [9].

Despite its widespread endorsement, the clinical utility of the HFA-ICOS risk stratification framework remains limited in real-world settings. Existing studies have primarily evaluated early-stage breast cancer populations receiving adjuvant trastuzumab, with mixed findings regarding risk enrichment across categories and variable ability to distinguish patients who develop cardiotoxicity from those who do not [10, 11]. Furthermore, most available data originate from Western cohorts, leaving uncertainty about their applicability to populations with different demographic and clinical characteristics. Regions such as the Middle East, where cardiometabolic comorbidities, including diabetes, hypertension, and obesity, are highly prevalent, may exhibit distinct patterns of cardiotoxicity that are not fully captured by prior studies [12]. As a result, real-world evaluation across diverse populations is essential before the framework can be reliably generalized.

To address this knowledge gap, we evaluated the clinical utility of the HFA-ICOS baseline cardiovascular risk stratification framework in a large, contemporary cohort of HER2-positive breast cancer patients treated at two tertiary centers. Our objectives were to compare observed cardiotoxicity rates across risk categories, examine clinical risk enrichment and separation, and assess differences in time-to-event patterns. Discrimination and calibration metrics were explored as secondary descriptive performance measures. This study provides the first real-world evaluation of the HFA-ICOS framework in a Middle Eastern cohort and offers insight into its role in guiding cardio-oncology surveillance strategies in populations with a high burden of cardiometabolic disease.

Methods

Study design and setting

This multicenter, retrospective clinical validation study was conducted at two tertiary hospitals in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia: King Abdullah Specialist Children’s Hospital (KASCH; Ministry of National Guard Health Affairs) and King Fahad Medical City (KFMC). The objective was to evaluate the clinical utility and risk-stratification performance of the HFA–ICOS baseline cardiovascular risk framework for cardiotoxicity in patients treated with HER2-targeted therapy. The study period extended from January 2016 through December 2023.

Ethics

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and institutional research policies. Approval was obtained from the King Abdullah International Medical Research Center (KAIMRC; SP24R/041/03) and from KFMC in accordance with institutional procedures. Informed consent was waived because the study was retrospective and based on routinely collected clinical data.

Participants

Eligible patients were adults (≥ 18 years) with HER2-positive breast cancer who received at least one cycle of HER2-targeted therapy (trastuzumab, pertuzumab, dual trastuzumab–pertuzumab, T-DM1, or T-DXd). Patients were excluded if cancer care was partially delivered at external institutions, HER2-targeted therapy was administered for non-breast cancer indications, baseline echocardiography or requisite cardiac data were unavailable to permit HFA–ICOS risk classification, or follow-up cardiac assessments were not documented.

HFA–ICOS baseline risk classification

Baseline cardiovascular risk was assigned using the 2020 ESC–ICOS Cardio-Oncology Risk Assessment Framework, incorporating demographics, cardiovascular comorbidities, prior cardiotoxic cancer therapies (including anthracyclines and chest radiation), baseline echocardiographic parameters (LVEF and GLS), cardiac biomarkers when available, planned HER2 regimen/exposure, and functional status (ECOG). Patients were classified into prespecified risk categories: low, moderate, high, or very high.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was composite cardiotoxicity, defined as the first occurrence of any of the following during follow-up: EF-defined cardiotoxicity (LVEF < 50% or ≥ 10% absolute decline from baseline), GLS-defined cardiotoxicity (≥ 15% relative reduction in GLS), ECG/arrhythmia events (new atrial fibrillation, supraventricular tachycardia, ventricular ectopy or ventricular tachycardia, or new bundle branch block/significant conduction abnormality), or biomarker elevation (troponin I/T > 14 ng/L, BNP > 100 pg/mL, or NT-proBNP > 300 pg/mL).

The composite cardiotoxicity endpoint was selected to reflect the multidimensional nature of cancer therapy–related cardiac injury and to align with contemporary ESC cardio-oncology guidance, which recognizes structural, functional, electrophysiologic, and biomarker abnormalities as clinically relevant manifestations of cardiotoxicity [9, 10, 13]. This composite approach reflects contemporary cardio-oncology practice, in which early subclinical abnormalities often prompt intervention before overt heart failure develops.

Data sources and variables

Data were extracted from electronic medical records using a standardized abstraction form. Variables included patient demographics, cardiovascular comorbidities and medications, cancer stage and treatment intent/line, HER2 regimen and number of cycles, baseline and follow-up LVEF/GLS, cardiac biomarkers, ECG findings, HER2 therapy interruption (dates and documented reasons), and cardio-oncology referrals. Biomarker measurements (troponin and natriuretic peptides) were obtained as part of routine clinical care and were not protocolized across centers. To enhance consistency, standardized guideline-recommended thresholds were applied for outcome definition (troponin I/T > 14 ng/L, BNP > 100 pg/mL, NT-proBNP > 300 pg/mL), irrespective of assay type.

Clinical risk stratification performance analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarized overall and according to HFA–ICOS risk category (low, moderate, high, very high). Continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate based on distribution, and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables, and χ² or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables.

The clinical risk stratification performance of the HFA–ICOS framework was evaluated by comparing observed cardiotoxicity event rates, clinical risk enrichment patterns, and time-to-event distributions across predefined risk categories. Discrimination and calibration metrics were explored as secondary descriptive performance measures.

As an exploratory analysis, discrimination across HFA-ICOS categories was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the curve (AUC), recognizing that the tool is an ordinal clinical risk stratification framework rather than a probabilistic prediction model. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated for each endpoint. Comparisons of AUCs were performed using DeLong’s method, where applicable.

Calibration was evaluated descriptively by comparing observed cardiotoxicity event rates across HFA-ICOS risk strata using calibration plots. Given that the HFA-ICOS framework is an ordinal clinical risk stratification tool rather than a probabilistic prediction model, calibration metrics are presented to illustrate observed risk patterns rather than formal model goodness-of-fit. The association between baseline HFA–ICOS risk category and composite cardiotoxicity was examined using multivariable logistic regression, modeling HFA–ICOS category as an ordinal predictor. Prespecified covariates included age, Charlson Comorbidity Index, baseline LVEF < 50%, prior anthracycline exposure, prior chest radiation, treatment intent, number of anti-HER2 cycles, and ECOG performance status. Adjusted odds ratios (aORs) with 95% confidence intervals were reported.

Time-to-event analyses were performed using the Kaplan–Meier method, stratified by HFA–ICOS risk category, and group comparisons were performed using the log-rank test. Cumulative cardiotoxicity event rates were estimated at 6, 12, and 18 months.

Prespecified sensitivity analyses included analyses restricted to EF-defined cardiotoxicity and GLS-defined cardiotoxicity, exclusion of patients without GLS measurements, and complete-case analysis. Missing data were handled using complete-case methods without imputation. All tests were two-sided, and p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Follow-up

Follow-up started at initiation of HER2-targeted therapy and continued until the first cardiotoxicity event, death, loss to follow-up, or the end of the study period (31 December 2023), whichever occurred first.

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

A total of 687 patients with HER2-positive breast cancer treated with HER2-targeted therapy between January 2016 and December 2023 were included in the analysis. Baseline cardiovascular risk stratification using the HFA–ICOS framework categorized patients as low risk (n = 245, 35.7%), moderate risk (n = 257, 37.4%), high risk (n = 181, 26.4%), and very high risk (n = 4, 0.6%). Patients classified into higher-risk categories were older and had a greater burden of cardiometabolic comorbidities. Mean age increased progressively across strata from 49.5 years in the low-risk group to 65.6 years in the very high-risk group (p < 0.001). Hypertension and diabetes were significantly more prevalent among moderate- and high-risk patients compared with low-risk patients (p < 0.001 for both). Baseline LVEF was modest but significantly lower in higher-risk categories (p = 0.021), while baseline GLS also differed significantly across strata (p < 0.001). Treatment characteristics varied across risk strata, reflecting more advanced disease and later lines of therapy in higher-risk groups (Table 1).

Incidence of cardiotoxicity by HFA-ICOS risk category

Over a median follow-up of 3.4 years, a total of 273 patients (39.7%) experienced composite cardiotoxicity. EF-defined cardiotoxicity occurred in 126 patients (18.3%), GLS-defined cardiotoxicity in 49 patients (7.1%), and ECG/arrhythmia events in 121 patients (17.6%). Among patients classified as low risk (n = 245), EF-defined cardiotoxicity was observed in 41 patients (16.7%), GLS decline in 19 patients (7.8%), and ECG/arrhythmia events in 41 patients (16.7%), with 95 patients (38.8%) meeting criteria for composite cardiotoxicity. In the moderate-risk group (n = 257), EF-defined cardiotoxicity occurred in 49 patients (19.1%), GLS decline in 12 patients (4.7%), and ECG/arrhythmia events in 46 patients (17.9%), while composite cardiotoxicity was observed in 99 patients (38.5%). Among high-risk patients (n = 181), EF-defined cardiotoxicity occurred in 34 patients (18.8%), GLS decline in 17 patients (9.4%), and ECG/arrhythmia events in 34 patients (18.8%), with 75 patients (41.4%) experiencing composite cardiotoxicity. In the very high-risk group (n = 4), EF-defined cardiotoxicity was observed in 2 patients (50.0%), GLS decline in 1 patient (25.0%), and no ECG/arrhythmia events were documented; however, all four patients (100.0%) met criteria for composite cardiotoxicity. Notably, cardiotoxicity events were observed across all baseline risk categories, including patients initially classified as low risk, with substantial overlap in event rates between low-, moderate-, and high-risk groups (Table 2).

Exploratory Discrimination Analysis Across HFA-ICOS Risk Categories

As an exploratory analysis, ROC curves were constructed to examine discrimination across baseline HFA-ICOS risk categories. Modest discrimination was observed for EF-defined cardiotoxicity (AUC 0.51; 95% CI 0.46–0.57), GLS-defined cardiotoxicity (AUC 0.55; 95% CI 0.46–0.64), ECG-defined events (AUC 0.51; 95% CI 0.45–0.57), and composite cardiotoxicity (AUC 0.52; 95% CI 0.47–0.56). Sensitivity ranged from 0.28 to 0.37, while specificity ranged from 0.73 to 0.74 across endpoints.

These findings reflect modest clinical risk separation across HFA-ICOS categories and are consistent with the framework’s intended role as a baseline surveillance stratification tool rather than a probabilistic prediction model. Observed cardiotoxicity event rates were examined across HFA-ICOS risk categories to assess agreement between baseline risk stratification and real-world outcomes. Across EF-defined, GLS-defined, ECG-defined, and composite cardiotoxicity endpoints, observed event rates increased modestly across higher-risk categories, although substantial overlap was observed between strata. Multivariable Predictors of Composite Cardiotoxicity

In multivariable logistic regression analysis, baseline HFA-ICOS risk category was not independently associated with composite cardiotoxicity (aOR 0.88 per category increase; 95% CI 0.56–1.37; p = 0.56). Factors independently associated with cardiotoxicity included disease control or life-prolongation treatment intent (aOR 3.24; 95% CI 1.30–8.07; p = 0.012) and higher ECOG performance status (aOR 4.08; 95% CI 1.51–11.04; p = 0.006). Baseline beta-blocker therapy was associated with a lower risk of cardiotoxicity (adjusted OR 0.13; 95% CI 0.02–0.99; p = 0.049) (Table 4).

Time-to-event analysis

Kaplan–Meier analysis was performed to examine time to first cardiotoxicity event across baseline HFA-ICOS risk categories. Although the overall comparison demonstrated a statistically significant difference in event-free survival across groups (log-rank p < 0.001), the cumulative event curves showed substantial overlap between the low-, moderate-, and high-risk categories. At 6 months, cumulative cardiotoxicity event rates were 14.3% in the low-risk group, 6.5% in the moderate-risk group, and 9.6% in the high-risk group. At 12 months, event rates increased to 29.0%, 12.8%, and 18.0%, respectively. By 18 months, cumulative cardiotoxicity rates reached 37.6% in low-risk patients, 22.6% in moderate-risk patients, and 27.5% in high-risk patients.

Notably, early cardiotoxicity event rates were numerically lower in the high- and very-high-risk groups compared with the low-risk group. This pattern likely reflects differences in clinical management rather than true biological protection. Patients classified as high risk are more likely to receive intensified cardio-oncology surveillance, earlier cardioprotective therapy, treatment modification, and closer follow-up, which may mitigate early cardiotoxicity. In addition, treatment selection bias and competing risks, including shorter treatment duration and earlier discontinuation in frailer patients, may further attenuate observed early event rates in higher-risk groups. The very high-risk group (n = 4) is presented descriptively only. In this subgroup, no cardiotoxicity events were observed during the first 6 months of follow-up, with cumulative event rates of 25.0% at both 12 and 18 months and a median time-to-event of 644 days. Discussion

In this large, real-world multicenter cohort of patients with HER2-positive breast cancer receiving anti-HER2 therapy, we evaluated the clinical utility of the HFA–ICOS baseline cardiovascular risk stratification framework for identifying patients at risk of cancer therapy–related cardiotoxicity. Our findings suggest that cardiotoxicity events occurred across all baseline risk categories, with considerable overlap in observed event rates and time-to-event patterns. These findings are consistent with prior real-world studies demonstrating limited risk separation of the HFA–ICOS framework, particularly among low- and moderate-risk groups [10, 11].

Importantly, a substantial proportion of cardiotoxicity events occurred in patients initially classified as low risk, with event rates comparable to those observed in moderate-risk groups. This finding highlights a key limitation of baseline risk stratification and underscores the heterogeneous and dynamic nature of HER2 therapy–related cardiotoxicity. It is likely that treatment-related factors, temporal changes in cardiac function, and intensified surveillance contribute to this overlap, limiting the ability of baseline risk categories to fully discriminate individual risk.

Although patients in higher-risk categories had a greater burden of cardiometabolic comorbidity, baseline HFA–ICOS stratification alone provided only modest clinical risk separation for EF-defined, GLS-defined, ECG-defined, and composite cardiotoxicity outcomes. These findings suggest that baseline cardiovascular risk assessment alone may have limited ability to distinguish cardiotoxicity risk in routine clinical practice, particularly in the setting of retrospective surveillance and incomplete data capture.

The development of HER2-targeted therapies, including trastuzumab and pertuzumab, has significantly improved survival outcomes in both early and advanced HER2-positive breast cancer [1–4]. However, cardiotoxicity remains a well-recognized complication of these agents, particularly when administered sequentially or concurrently with other cardiotoxic treatments [5–7]. As a result, structured approaches to cardiovascular risk assessment have been emphasized to guide surveillance and management strategies. The HFA–ICOS risk assessment framework was introduced as a consensus-based tool to standardize baseline evaluation and stratify patients into clinically actionable risk categories,8 and it has since been incorporated into ESC cardio-oncology guidelines [9].

Despite its broad endorsement, evidence supporting the clinical performance of the HFA–ICOS framework in risk stratification remains inconsistent. In a cohort of patients receiving adjuvant trastuzumab for early-stage HER2-positive disease, Battisti et al. observed substantial overlap in cardiotoxicity rates across HFA–ICOS risk categories, with only modest clinical risk enrichment [10]. Similarly, Tini and colleagues reported the limited ability of the framework to distinguish patients who developed cardiotoxicity from those who did not in routine clinical practice [11]. Our study extends these observations to a larger and more heterogeneous real-world cohort, including patients treated across multiple lines of therapy and with newer HER2-targeted agents. Our findings further highlight the challenges of using baseline cardiovascular risk stratification alone to reliably identify patients at the highest risk of cardiotoxicity during HER2-targeted therapy. Unlike anthracycline-associated injury, HER2-related cardiotoxicity appears less tightly coupled to traditional cardiovascular risk factors and more influenced by dynamic treatment-related factors, potentially explaining the modest clinical risk separation observed.

Several factors may explain the modest performance of risk stratification in our cohort. First, cardiotoxicity associated with HER2-targeted therapies differs mechanistically from anthracycline-induced cardiotoxicity and is often reversible and less clearly dose-dependent [5–7]. As a result, traditional baseline cardiovascular risk factors and static echocardiographic parameters may not fully capture individual susceptibility. Although descriptive calibration analyses demonstrated general concordance between observed cardiotoxicity rates and expected risk gradients across HFA–ICOS categories, the limited separation between strata suggests that the framework reflects overall cardiotoxicity risk at the population level but lacks sufficient granularity for precise individualized risk estimation.

Second, the increasing use of sensitive surveillance modalities, including GLS and cardiac biomarkers, may facilitate earlier detection of subclinical myocardial injury across all risk strata, thereby attenuating apparent differences between categories. This is supported by our findings of relatively high negative predictive values but low positive predictive values, suggesting that baseline risk stratification may be more useful for identifying patients at lower short-term risk than for accurately predicting cardiotoxicity events.

Notably, this study represents the first real-world evaluation of the HFA–ICOS framework in a Middle Eastern population, where cardiometabolic risk factors are highly prevalent.12 Although patients classified into higher HFA–ICOS risk categories exhibited a greater burden of hypertension, diabetes, and overall comorbidity, this did not translate into substantially higher observed cardiotoxicity rates or stronger clinical risk separation. This observation highlights the complexity of cardiotoxicity risk in HER2-positive breast cancer and suggests that traditional cardiovascular risk factors, while critical for long-term cardiovascular outcomes, may not independently drive early treatment-related cardiac dysfunction in this setting.

Time-to-event analyses further support these findings, demonstrating substantial overlap in event-free survival among low-, moderate-, and high-risk groups. Cardiotoxicity events occurred relatively early after treatment initiation across all categories, consistent with prior observations that trastuzumab-related cardiac dysfunction often manifests within the first year of therapy [6,7]. Notably, early cardiotoxicity rates were numerically lower in the high- and very high-risk groups compared with the low-risk group. This pattern likely reflects differences in clinical management rather than true biological protection. Patients classified as high risk are more likely to receive intensified cardio-oncology surveillance, earlier cardioprotective therapy, treatment modification, and closer follow-up, which may mitigate early cardiotoxicity. In addition, treatment selection bias and competing risks, including shorter treatment duration and earlier discontinuation in frailer patients, may further attenuate observed early event rates in higher-risk groups. Finally, interpretation of the very high-risk category is limited by the small sample size (n = 4), which makes Kaplan–Meier estimates inherently unstable.

In multivariable analyses, baseline HFA–ICOS risk category was not independently associated with composite cardiotoxicity, even after adjustment for age, comorbidity burden, baseline LVEF, prior anthracycline exposure, and radiation therapy. These findings further emphasize the multifactorial and dynamic nature of cardiac risk during HER2-targeted cancer therapy. An additional finding of interest was the observed association between baseline beta-blocker therapy and a lower risk of composite cardiotoxicity. Although this observation should be interpreted cautiously, given the retrospective design, it is biologically plausible and consistent with prior studies suggesting a potential cardioprotective role of beta-blockers in patients receiving cardiotoxic cancer therapies [14–17]. Beta-blockers may mitigate neurohormonal activation and reduce myocardial stress during cancer treatment. However, this association may also reflect confounding by indication, in which patients receiving beta-blockers are more likely to be co-managed by cardio-oncology services and to undergo closer surveillance and earlier intervention. Prospective randomized studies are needed to determine whether prophylactic beta-blocker therapy confers true cardioprotection in patients receiving HER2-targeted therapy.

This study has several strengths, including a large, well-characterized cohort, representation of contemporary HER2-targeted regimens, and a comprehensive definition of cardiotoxicity encompassing functional, imaging, electrophysiologic, and biomarker-based outcomes. Together, these features enhance clinical relevance and generalizability of the findings.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design may have introduced heterogeneity in surveillance intensity and event detection, as cardiac monitoring was not protocolized and was influenced by baseline risk assessment and clinical judgment. In addition, the study was not designed to evaluate the predictive accuracy of the HFA–ICOS framework as a formal risk prediction model; therefore, discrimination and calibration analyses should be interpreted as exploratory. Variability in surveillance practices across centers may have further influenced event detection and timing, potentially attenuating differences between risk groups.

Second, GLS and cardiac biomarker data were unavailable for a subset of patients, which may have affected outcome classification, although sensitivity analyses yielded consistent results. Importantly, this reflects real-world practice rather than methodological oversight. Robust evidence indicates that relative reductions in GLS can detect subclinical myocardial dysfunction earlier than declines in LVEF and are associated with subsequent cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction [18, 19], leading contemporary cardio-oncology guidelines to recommend baseline and serial GLS assessment when feasible [8, 9]. The incomplete availability of GLS in this cohort therefore highlights an implementation gap between guideline recommendations and routine clinical practice, which may contribute to delayed recognition of early cardiotoxicity and attenuate the apparent performance of baseline risk stratification frameworks. In addition, variability in biomarker assay types and timing across centers may have introduced heterogeneity in event detection.

Additionally, the small number of patients classified as very high risk limited the ability to draw definitive conclusions for this subgroup. Although several guideline-directed cardiovascular therapies were explored in secondary analyses, their inclusion in multivariable models was constrained by low utilization rates, collinearity among drug classes, and evidence of separation. Finally, residual confounding cannot be excluded, particularly given differences in surveillance intensity and management strategies across baseline risk categories.

Furthermore, our findings are specific to HER2-positive breast cancer patients receiving anti-HER2 therapies and may not be directly generalizable to other oncologic populations or treatment regimens.

In conclusion, while the HFA–ICOS baseline cardiovascular risk assessment framework provides a structured approach for standardized evaluation and surveillance planning, its ability to clearly distinguish cardiotoxicity risk in real-world HER2-positive breast cancer patients was limited. These findings support its use to guide surveillance intensity rather than as a standalone predictive tool. Future prospective multicenter studies are needed to validate these findings and refine risk-stratification models by integrating baseline risk with dynamic, treatment-related cardiac markers.

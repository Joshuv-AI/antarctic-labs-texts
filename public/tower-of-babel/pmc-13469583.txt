Application of CW-RNN model in network attack detection and its promotion to the governance of international law
Authors: Haiying Lin, Lin Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address the core issue of insufficient modeling of multi-time-scale temporal features in modern network attack detection, this study proposes a network attack detection model based on Clock-Work Recurrent Neural Network (CW-RNN). The model introduces a time-division processing mechanism, divides hidden layer neurons into modular structures with different clock frequencies, and integrates an attention mechanism to enhance feature extraction in key attack stages. An end-to-end intelligent detection architecture is constructed, which can adaptively capture characteristics of both short-term burst attacks and long-term latent attacks. Experimental verification is conducted on two benchmark datasets: University of New South Wales Network Benchmark 2015 (UNSW-NB15) and Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity-Intrusion Detection Systems 2018 (CSE-CIC-IDS2018). The results show that the detection accuracies of the proposed CW-RNN model on the two datasets reach 95.8% and 95.2%, respectively, and the macro-average F1-scores are 94.2% and 93.6%, respectively, which are significantly superior to mainstream benchmark models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). Meanwhile, the training time of the model is reduced by 25%, and the inference speed is increased by more than 18%, achieving dual optimization in detection accuracy and computational efficiency. Its modular multi-time-scale processing design provides an efficient and practical technical solution for network attack detection.

Introduction

With the in-depth evolution of the global digitalization process, cyberspace has become the fifth-dimensional strategic domain following land, sea, air, and space1. Its security and stability are directly related to the operation of critical infrastructures, the protection of data resources, and the socioeconomic order. However, the openness and anonymity of cyberspace make it a high-incidence area for cyberattacks. Various types of cyberattacks exhibit the characteristics of high concealment, persistence, and dynamic evolution2. Ranging from short-term burst attacks such as Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) to long-term latent penetration attacks like Advanced Persistent Threat (APT), the diversification of attack forms has put forward higher requirements for defense technologies. Traditional static defense models based on signature matching generally suffer from defects such as delayed response and high false positive rates. They are difficult to adapt to the complex evolutionary patterns of modern cyberattacks and cannot meet the rigid demands for cyberspace security in various fields3. Therefore, developing intelligent detection technologies with real-time perception, accurate early warning, and adaptive evolution capabilities has become a core issue urgently to be solved in the field of cybersecurity4.

Among artificial intelligence-driven security solutions, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have shown great potential in network abnormal traffic detection due to their excellent capability in processing time-series data5. However, standard RNNs and their variants (e.g., Long Short-Term Memory networks, LSTMs) often suffer from technical bottlenecks such as vanishing gradients and high computational complexity when dealing with long sequence dependencies6, resulting in insufficient modeling ability for multi-time-scale attack features. They can neither accurately capture the instantaneous characteristics of short-term burst attacks nor efficiently track the gradual evolution of long-term latent attacks7. To break through this technical dilemma, Clock-Work Recurrent Neural Networks (CW-RNNs) have emerged. By innovatively introducing a time-division processing mechanism, CW-RNNs divide hidden-layer neurons into modules with different clock frequencies. Through the modular parallel design, where high-frequency modules handle short-time-scale dependencies and low-frequency modules capture long-time-scale patterns, the model significantly improves computational efficiency and training stability8. It can sensitively capture complex multi-time-scale features in network attack traffic, providing a new technical paradigm for achieving high-precision and low-latency attack detection9.

In view of the above findings, this study focuses on the core technical pain points of network attack detection and deeply explores the innovative application of CW-RNNs in this field, aiming to construct an intelligent detection architecture that can adaptively match multi-time-scale attack features. By optimizing the modular clock mechanism and feature fusion strategy, the contradiction between efficiency and accuracy in long sequence dependency modeling of traditional RNN models is solved. The accurate recognition of short-term burst attacks and long-term latent attacks is realized. In the experiments, the University of New South Wales Network Benchmark 2015 (UNSW-NB15) is selected as the core evaluation dataset. This dataset contains normal traffic and various simulated attack behaviors in a real network environment, with rich feature dimensions and comprehensive coverage of attack types, which can provide reliable support for the comprehensive verification of model performance.

Literature review

The continuous evolution of network attack forms has placed higher demands on detection technologies, and AI-based detection methods have become a key research direction. Early studies focused on applying deep learning models to specific network architectures. Wang and Li (2021) proposed a hybrid detection mechanism based on Transformer and explored its feasibility in detecting DDoS attacks in Software-Defined Networking (SDN)10. Ahuja et al. (2021) implemented an automated DDoS attack detection process in a similar environment, verifying the applicability of deep learning in such scenarios11.

With the deepening of research, the detection scope has expanded from single attack types to multiple types of network threats. Oliveira et al. (2021) developed an attack classification method for network intrusion detection systems, reflecting attention to attack diversity12. In terms of model architecture, Tekerek (2021) adopted a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture for Web attack detection, leveraging its advantages in spatial feature extraction13. Sahu et al. (2021) proposed a hybrid deep learning model to improve detection accuracy in the Internet of Things (IoT) environment by fusing multiple network structures14. Recent studies show two obvious trends: Detection optimization for specific network environments. Aktar and Nur (2023) systematically discussed the application of deep learning in DDoS attack detection15. Crespo-Martínez et al. (2023) detected SQL injection attacks from network flow data, providing a new solution for application-layer threat detection16. Ezhilarasi et al. (2023) combined fuzzy logic with feedforward neural networks for routing attack detection17. Pakmehr et al. (2024) systematically summarized DDoS detection technologies in IoT18. Saleh et al. (2024) developed an intrusion detection solution based on stochastic gradient descent for wireless sensor networks19. In terms of improving detection capabilities, Kalutharage et al. (2025) introduced a neuro-symbolic learning method to promote the development of detection systems toward interpretability and intelligent response20. The review of network covert channels by Khadse and Dakhane (2025) revealed the challenges in addressing APT21.

Existing studies demonstrate a development path from basic model application to architectural innovation, and then to scenario-specific optimization. However, most methods still have shortcomings in addressing the multi-time-scale characteristics of network traffic, and there is a lack of effective connection between technical achievements and cyberspace governance needs. By introducing the CW-RNN, this study aims to enhance the detection system’s ability to model multi-scale temporal features and provide technical support for the international legal governance of network attacks.

Figure 3 shows the core computing structure of the network attack detection model based on CW-RNN. Its design centers on the accurate modeling of multi-time-scale features, which is rational and innovative, and highly compatible with the temporal characteristics and detection requirements of network attacks. The temporal characteristics of network attacks exhibit significant heterogeneity: short-term burst attacks feature abrupt traffic changes and short feature durations, while long-term latent attacks evolve gradually with strong cross-time-step dependencies. In response, the model adopts a modular time-division processing mechanism that divides the hidden layer into independent modules with differentiated functions. Through gradient clock cycle configuration, it adaptively captures attack features at different time scales. A hierarchical information interaction design from low-index to high-index modules is adopted among modules, so that instantaneous traffic anomalies captured by high-frequency modules can be gradually transmitted to medium- and low-frequency modules and coupled with long-term behavior patterns for modeling. This avoids mutual interference among features at different time scales, and achieves full-dimensional perception from microscopic anomalies to macroscopic patterns, laying a structural foundation for the improvement of detection efficiency and interpretability.

The model achieves precise breakthroughs against the technical bottlenecks of traditional RNNs and their variants. First, to address the vanishing gradient and computational redundancy in long-sequence processing, a clock cycle modulation–module asynchronous update strategy is proposed. According to the time-scale distribution of attack features, hidden-layer neurons are divided into independent modules with exponentially increasing clock cycles. Short-cycle modules capture instantaneous features of burst attacks, while long-cycle modules track long-term dependencies of latent attacks. Through the activation rule t mod Ti=0, modules are updated only at time steps satisfying the cycle condition, reducing redundant computation at the architectural level. The gating mechanism within modules further dynamically adjusts the fusion ratio of historical information and new inputs, effectively alleviating gradient instability and realizing collaborative optimization of computational efficiency and modeling capability. Second, to break the limitation of traditional detection models that emphasize accuracy but neglect interpretability, the attention mechanism and modular clock mechanism are cooperatively designed, endowing detection results with three-dimensional traceability across feature–time–module. Module clock frequency is introduced as a prior constraint when calculating attention weights, enabling complementary weight allocation for features corresponding to high- and low-frequency modules and improving feature extraction accuracy at critical attack stages. Meanwhile, attention weights quantify the contribution of each time step and module to the detection result. This interpretability is inherently embedded in the model structure, and all detection results can be traced back to the feature type, critical time step, and core feature dimension of specific modules, satisfying the technical requirements of verifiable and traceable detection outputs.

The core architecture of the model is built based on a modular clock-driven mechanism, which divides the hidden layer neurons into multiple independent modules. Each module is assigned a specific clock cycle following the principle of exponential configuration, which fits the temporal evolution characteristics of network attacks. This ensures that high-frequency modules respond rapidly to short-term instantaneous attack feature changes, while low-frequency modules continuously track the gradual evolution of long-term latent attacks. A strict hierarchical information interaction design is adopted among modules, allowing information to be transmitted only from low-index to high-index modules. An information flow path for the progressive fusion of short-term features into long-term features is constructed to realize the organic coupling of multi-dimensional features. The activation state of each module is controlled by the matching relationship between the clock cycle and the current time step. Modules participate in computation and update only when the time step satisfies the divisibility condition of the cycle and remain unchanged in the hidden state at other times. This asynchronous update mechanism reduces redundant computations at the architectural level and significantly improves the efficiency of long-sequence data processing.

To accurately capture feature information at key attack stages, the model deeply integrates the attention mechanism with clock-driven modules, breaking through the limitation that traditional attention mechanisms only focus on spatial-dimensional features. Module clock frequency is introduced as a prior constraint in attention weight calculation. Through the mapping association between context vectors and clock cycles, features corresponding to high-frequency and low-frequency modules form complementarity in weight distribution, enabling the model to adaptively focus on burst features at attack initiation and key behavioral patterns during continuous evolution. The model concatenates the hidden states of all modules at each time step into a comprehensive feature vector, calculates weight coefficients through the attention layer and obtains the global feature representation of the sequence via weighted summation. Finally, the fully connected layer and softmax activation function are used to complete the mapping output of attack types, forming an end-to-end detection process while retaining the correlation trajectories among features, time steps and modules, which lays a structural foundation for subsequent interpretability analysis.

The model adopts a differentiated optimization strategy compatible with the modular clock mechanism, which effectively improves training stability and generalization ability. According to the functional positioning of modules with different frequencies, a differentiated L2 regularization scheme is designed: a small regularization coefficient is set for high-frequency modules to retain their ability to sensitively capture instantaneous features; the coefficient is appropriately increased for low-frequency modules to restrict the overfitting risk in long-term dependency modeling. The Dropout mechanism is introduced, and the dropout probability is dynamically adjusted according to the module activation cycle to coordinate with the clock update rhythm. The Adam optimizer is used for parameter optimization, and an adaptive learning rate scheduling strategy is designed combined with module characteristics: a large initial learning rate and a fast decay rate are set for high-frequency modules to achieve rapid convergence; a small initial learning rate and a slow decay rate are set for low-frequency modules to ensure the stability of long-term feature modeling. All trainable parameters are initialized with the Xavier uniform distribution to ensure numerical stability at the early stage of training. The model takes the cross-entropy loss function as the core optimization objective, integrates regularization terms to construct a comprehensive optimization objective, and realizes iterative parameter update through the backpropagation algorithm, enabling the model to effectively learn the discriminative features between normal network traffic and various attack behaviors.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

This study utilizes two internationally recognized public datasets for network attack detection benchmarks to complete model performance verification and comparative analysis. All datasets provide official permanent access links, standardized metadata, and traceable sources, meeting the requirements of academic research datasets in terms of validity, reproducibility, and FAIR principles.

(1) UNSW-NB15 dataset.

UNSW-NB15 (University of New South Wales Network Benchmark 2015) is publicly released by the Cyber Security Research Centre of the University of New South Wales, Canberra, Australia. It is one of the most representative standard evaluation datasets in the field of network intrusion detection. This dataset is collected in a laboratory environment based on the Ixia PerfectStorm network simulation and traffic generation platform, integrating real normal network traffic with nine types of typical synthetic attack traffic, featuring high scenario authenticity and attack diversity. The dataset contains 49-dimensional network traffic features, covering core attributes such as source IP, destination IP, transmission protocol, packet length, connection duration, etc. Detailed feature descriptions can be found in the official accompanying file `UNSW-NB15_features.csv`. The attack types covered by the dataset include: Fuzz testing attacks (Fuzzers), reconnaissance analysis attacks (Analysis), backdoor attacks (Backdoors), denial of service attacks (DoS), exploit attacks (Exploits), generic attacks (Generic), reconnaissance attacks (Reconnaissance), shellcode attacks (Shellcode), and worm attacks (Worms).

Official permanent access link: https://cloudstor.aarnet.edu.au/plus/s/2DhnLGDdEECo4ys.

(2) CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset.

CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity-Intrusion Detection Systems 2018), constructed by the Cybersecurity Institute of the University of New Brunswick, is a mainstream high-performance evaluation dataset in the field of network attack detection. This dataset, simulated and collected based on real enterprise-level network environments, includes normal traffic and seven types of modern typical network attack scenarios, covering brute force attacks, distributed denial of service attacks, web attacks, botnet attacks, port scans, ransomware attacks, and lateral movement attacks. The dataset extracts 80-dimensional refined traffic statistical features through the CICFlowMeter-V3 tool. Its data scale, feature dimensionality, and attack scenario completeness are significantly superior to traditional datasets, complementing UNSW-NB15 in terms of attack types and feature dimensions. By using a dual-dataset cross-validation approach, the generalization ability and robustness of the model in real network environments can be comprehensively verified.

Official permanent access link: https://www.unb.ca/cic/datasets/ids-2018.html.

Parameters setting

To ensure the reliability and comparability of experimental results, this study uniformly controls the parameter settings of all models. The CW-RNN model adopts a hidden layer structure with 4 modules, whose clock cycles are set to 1, 2, 4, and 8 respectively. This exponentially increasing cycle configuration can effectively capture multi-scale temporal features of network traffic, ranging from short-term sudden bursts to long-term persistence. The hidden layer dimension of each module is fixed at 128, ensuring the model has sufficient feature expression capability.

During the training process, the Adam optimizer is employed for parameter updates, with an initial learning rate set to 0.001, coupled with an exponential decay scheduling strategy where the decay rate is set to 0.95. The batch size is fixed at 64 samples, and the maximum number of training epochs for the model is set to 200. Additionally, an early stopping mechanism is introduced to monitor the validation set loss, which automatically terminates training when no performance improvement is observed for 10 consecutive epochs.

To prevent overfitting, a Dropout technique with a rate of 0.5 is applied before the fully connected layer of the model. The weight decay coefficient is set to 0.001, and this L2 regularization term can effectively constrain model complexity. The loss function adopts the standard multi-class cross-entropy loss for classification tasks. All parameters are initialized using the Xavier uniform distribution method to ensure numerical stability in the initial stage of training.

To verify the robustness of parameter selection, this study conducted a grid search on key hyperparameters during the preliminary experiment phase, including learning rate, hidden layer dimension, and Dropout rate. The finally determined parameter combination achieved the optimal balanced performance on the validation set, which can simultaneously balance model convergence speed, generalization ability, and computational efficiency.

Discussion

The network attack detection model based on the CW-RNN constructed in this study effectively addresses the deficiencies in multi-time-scale temporal feature modeling and the computational efficiency bottlenecks of traditional detection methods through the synergistic optimization of the modular clock mechanism and the attention mechanism. It exhibits excellent detection accuracy and generalization capability on two major benchmark datasets, namely UNSW-NB15 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018, and this result stems from the deep matching between the model architecture and the temporal characteristics of network attacks. Network attacks possess the dual characteristics of short-term burstiness and long-term latency. The design of CW-RNN with modular clock cycles in exponential growth enables high-frequency modules to accurately capture instantaneous traffic anomalies and low-frequency modules to effectively track long-term behavioral patterns. Combined with the hierarchical information interaction mechanism among modules, the complementary fusion of features at different time scales is realized, which fundamentally avoids problems such as gradient vanishing and short-term feature dilution faced by traditional RNNs and LSTMs in long-sequence processing. While maintaining detection accuracy, the model reduces redundant computations through the asynchronous update mechanism, achieving a 25% reduction in training time and an 18% improvement in inference speed, and thus is more in line with the real-time requirements of practical network security protection.

The ablation experiments further verify the technical value of the core modules of the model. The clock mechanism brings a 4.5% improvement in the macro-average F1-score, confirming the necessity of hierarchical modeling of multi-time-scale features. The attention mechanism increases the recognition rate of Web attacks by 5.3%, highlighting the effectiveness of the feature enhancement strategy for key attack stages. Compared with existing detection methods based on Transformer or CNN, the proposed model breaks through the dual limitations of single-scale modeling and accuracy-efficiency imbalance. While maintaining a high detection accuracy of 95.8%, it possesses the potential for lightweight deployment. Moreover, the built-in three-dimensional traceability design of “feature-time-module” frees detection results from the limitations of traditional black-box models, providing technical support for network attack forensics analysis and building a practical bridge for the integration of technical models and international cyber law governance frameworks. This three-dimensional traceability can directly address core practical issues in international cyber law. In national-level cyber attack attribution, it can locate the technical feature identifiers of attack behaviors through the feature dimension, restore the complete behavioral timeline of attacks in the time dimension, and trace the evolutionary features of each attack stage from probing and penetration to execution in the module dimension, forming a verifiable and traceable technical evidence chain. In the compliance verification of international norms, the three-dimensional traceability results can be relied on to clarify the time nodes, feature manifestations and behavioral modules of network actions, and determine whether they conform to international cyberspace norms of conduct. This enables technical detection results to serve as an important basis for fact-finding in international cyber law governance, realizing the concrete integration of technical models and legal governance rather than mere conceptual association.

Through architectural innovation and mechanism optimization, this study provides a network attack detection technical solution integrating accuracy, efficiency, and interpretability. Its core design ideas can serve as a reference for research on multi-time-scale time-series data modeling; however, the model still has certain limitations in practical applications. Currently, model training relies on laboratory environment datasets, and its detection capability for zero-day attacks and high-intensity adversarial samples has not been fully verified. Due to the lack of known feature samples, as well as the characteristics of feature camouflage and mutation, such attacks make it difficult for the existing modular clock mechanism to quickly capture their atypical temporal evolution features. High-frequency modules are prone to interference from false instantaneous features, while low-frequency modules struggle to effectively model unknown long-term features. To address this issue, targeted improvements can be made based on the existing model structure: an adaptive feature clustering mechanism can be introduced into the clock modules, enabling high-frequency modules to quickly screen out and eliminate false instantaneous features of adversarial samples and enhance the ability to extract real abnormal features; meanwhile, a few-shot learning mechanism can be integrated into low-frequency modules to realize feature transfer through a small number of unknown attack samples, improving the recognition capability for zero-day attacks. Additionally, on the basis of the existing three-dimensional traceability, a feature mutation tracking dimension can be added to achieve real-time capture of the feature evolution of adversarial samples. Furthermore, although the model has improved performance through a differentiated module optimization strategy, the deployment cost in resource-constrained scenarios such as edge computing still needs to be further reduced, which is also a key direction to be broken through in subsequent research. Overall, the innovative application of the CW-RNN model in the field of network attack detection achieves performance breakthroughs at the technical level and provides a feasible path for the integration of technology and legal governance through its traceability design. Its adaptability in real and complex network environments and lightweight improvement will become the core directions for in-depth research in the future.

Conclusion

Research contribution

Focusing on the core pain point of insufficient modeling of multi-time-scale temporal features in modern cyber attack detection, this study conducts research on the innovative application of Clockwork Recurrent Neural Networks (CW-RNNs) in this field. It constructs an intelligent detection architecture that integrates detection accuracy, computational efficiency, and interpretability. Through theoretical innovation and technical optimization, the study effectively breaks through the bottlenecks of gradient instability and computational redundancy faced by traditional recurrent neural networks and their variants in long-sequence dependency processing. It provides new ideas and support for the development of cyber attack detection technologies.

At the theoretical level, this study pioneers the systematic introduction of CW-RNN into the field of cybersecurity. By establishing a modular multi-time-scale feature fusion mechanism, it clarifies the modeling logic of “frequency stratification-asynchronous update”, providing a new theoretical framework for capturing the temporal features of complex attack behaviors. This breaks through the technical limitation of traditional models that struggle to simultaneously adapt to both short-term sudden attacks and long-term latent attacks. At the technical level, the proposed collaborative optimization strategy of the clock-driven modular structure and attention mechanism achieves a balanced improvement in detection performance and computational efficiency through exponentially increasing clock cycle configuration, hierarchical module interaction design, and differentiated optimization schemes. The model’s advantages in core evaluation indicators fully verify the effectiveness and rationality of the technical solution. Meanwhile, the model’s built-in three-dimensional traceability capability of “feature-time-module” endows the detection results with good interpretability, providing solid technical support for attack forensics and reflecting the deep alignment between technical design and practical application needs.

Future works and research limitations

At the same time, this study still has certain limitations: the model training relies on laboratory environment datasets, and its generalization ability in real network environments when facing zero-day attacks and high-intensity adversarial examples needs further verification. The model has high requirements for computing resources, making its deployment in resource-constrained scenarios such as edge computing challenging. The standardized process for transforming technical detection results into legal evidence still requires in-depth exploration. Future research will focus on breakthroughs in three aspects: (1) Constructing a detection framework for multi-source heterogeneous data fusion to enhance the perception ability of unknown threats. (2) Developing lightweight model variants to adapt to the security needs of low-power scenarios such as the Internet of Things and mobile terminals. (3) Deepening interdisciplinary collaboration, establishing a standardized mapping system between technical detection and legal forensics, and promoting the research results to play a greater role in practical network security protection and governance.

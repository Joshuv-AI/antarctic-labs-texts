Batalin-Fradkin-Vilkovisky Quantization of FLPR model
Ansha S. Nair and Saurabh Gupta

Abstract




Abstract:
We quantize the Friedberg-Lee-Pang-Ren (FLPR) model within the framework of Batalin-Fradkin-Vilkovisky (BFV) formalism. We construct the nilpotent Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin (BRST) charges using constraints and the fermionic gauge-fixing function by means of admissible gauge conditions. We also derive the BRST invariant effective action and corresponding symmetries of the model in both polar and Cartesian coordinates. Additionally, with the help of BRST charge, we demonstrate that the physical states of the system are annihilated by the first-class constraints, which is consistent with the Dirac formalism.

11.15.-q, 11.10.Ef, 11.30.-j

Keywords:
BFV quantization; FLPR model; Constrained systems; BRST symmetries.

Introduction

Constrained theories are the cornerstone of modern theoretical physics, as they include gauge theories, gravity theories, super-symmetric theories etc. The conventional methods of quantization are not directly applicable to such theories. Dirac developed a quantization technique to deal with such constrained systems - known as the Dirac formalism
[
1
]
. Later, a geometrically motivated approach based on the symplectic structure of the phase space - the Faddeev-Jackiw (FJ) formalism - was proposed
[
2
]
. The FJ formalism is also quite popular and widely used (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
]
and references therein). As far as quantization of such constrained theories is concerned, the Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin (BRST) formalism is one of the most broadly used techniques
[
5
,
6
]
.

However, an another versatile method for the quantization of constrained system is introduced by Batalin, Fradkin and Vilkovisky and popularly known as the BFV formalism
[
7
,
8
]
. It provides a systematic Hamiltonian framework for quantizing theories with first-class constraints which also preserves the BRST symmetries of the system. The BFV formalism has been generalized to include second-class theories by employing a conversion technique called the BFT formalism
[
9
]
. These methods have been explored in various classical and field theoretical models such as Chiral Schwinger model, Proca theory, QED and
(2+1)
-dimensional Hořava theory
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

The Friedberg-Lee-Pang-Ren (FLPR) model is a soluble gauge model that describes the motion of a single non-relativistic particle of unit mass governed by a central potential
[
14
]
. This is a
(0+1)
-dimensional quantum mechanical model with the characteristics of Gribov ambiguity, inspired by the dimensional reduction of Yang-Mills theory
[
15
]
. The BRST invariant path integral for the model is formulated by summing over all Gribov-type copies
[
16
]
. In addition, the FLPR model is being quantized using an admissible gauge-fixing condition within the framework of FJ quantization
[
17
]
. The system is also explored using a physical projector operator which avoids the gauge-fixing procedure
[
18
]
. Moreover, the existence of some
novel
off-shell nilpotent and absolutely anti-commuting symmetries of the FLPR model is established in
[
15
]
. Recently, this model is being investigated by means of supervariable and (anti-)chiral supervariable approaches
[
19
,
21
,
20
]
.

The prime motive of our present endeavor is to quantize the FLPR model using an admissible gauge-fixing condition, in both polar and Cartesian coordinates, by employing BFV formalism. We intend to construct the nilpotent and conserved BRST charge and subsequently derive the BRST invariant gauge-fixed effective action and corresponding symmetries. In addition, we plan to show that the physical states of the theory are annihilated by the conserved BRST charge which is in accordance with the Dirac-quantization conditions.

The content of the present manuscript is organized as follows. In section
2
, we provide the constraint structure of the FLPR model in polar coordinates using Dirac’s prescription of constraint analysis. The BFV quantization of the model is carried out in subsection
2.1
. Our section
3
deals with the constraint analysis of the FLPR model in Cartesian coordinates whereas BFV formulation, in Cartesian coordinates, is presented in subsection
3.1
. The section
4
discusses about conserved and nilpotent BRST charges and physicality criteria. Finally, we conclude our results in section
5
.

\dot{\theta}

r

\theta

We identify

[

{H}_{c}=\frac{1}{2}p_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2r^{2}}p_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}p_{z}^{2}+\zeta(gp_{\theta}+p_{z})+V(r).

(3)

The consistency condition on primary constraint
\Omega_{1}
leads to a secondary constraint
(\Omega_{2})

\Omega_{2}\equiv gp_{\theta}+p_{z}\approx 0.

(4)

There are no further constraints in the theory. Thus, the theory possess only two constraints, which are first-class in nature

4
Physicality criteria: BRST charge

We note that, in both the polar and Cartesian coordinates, the conserved BRST charges

\displaystyle Q={\cal{C}}\,(gp_{\theta}+p_{z})\,+\,\dot{\cal{C}}p_{\zeta},\qquad\tilde{Q}={\cal{C}}\big(g\,(xp_{y}-yp_{x})+p_{z}\big)+\dot{\cal{C}}p_{\zeta},

(33)

are the generators of BRST symmetry transformations (cf. (
17
) and (
31
)) under which the effective actions
(S_{eff}
and
\tilde{S}_{eff})
remain invariant, respectively. In the above equation, we have used the Euler-Lagrange equations of motion
{\cal{P}}=\dot{\cal{C}}
and the expression for momenta
p_{\zeta}=B
. These charges are also nilpotent of order two (cf. (
10
)).

In the BRST-quantized theory the total Hilbert space contains both the physical and ghost states as the direct product. Moreover, the ghost variables do not have any interactions with the physical variables of the system as they are decoupled. So, as the requirement of the physicality criteria, the dynamically stable subspace of states is annihilated by the above mentioned charges, i.e.;

\displaystyle Q\ket{\psi}=0,\qquad\tilde{Q}|{\tilde{\psi}}\rangle=0,

(34)

which leads to the following conditions, respectively

\displaystyle(gp_{\theta}+p_{z})\ket{\psi}=0,\qquad p_{\zeta}\ket{\psi}=0,

(35)

\displaystyle\big(g\,(xp_{y}-yp_{x})+p_{z}\big)\,|\tilde{\psi}\rangle=0,\qquad p_{\zeta}\,|\tilde{\psi}\rangle=0.

(36)

It is important to note that
(gp_{\theta}+p_{z})\approx 0
and
p_{\zeta}\approx 0
are first-class constraints of the system in the polar coordinates whereas
\big(g\,(xp_{y}-yp_{x})+p_{z}\big)\,\approx 0
and
p_{\zeta}\,\approx 0
are first-class constraints in Cartesian coordinates. Therefore, physical states are annihilated by the first-class constraints, which is consistent with the results in
[
17
]
and Dirac formalism.

5
Conclusions

We have performed the BFV quantization of the FLPR model in both polar and Cartesian coordinates using admissible gauge conditions. The FLPR model is endowed with first-class constraints
à la
Dirac’s prescription for classification of constraints. In order to apply BFV formalism, we have introduced a pair of ghost and anti-ghost variables along with auxiliary variables. The conserved and nilpotent charge has been constructed using constraints. Whereas the fermionic gauge-fixing function is being put together by means of admissible gauge-fixing conditions. We have obtained the effective action, in the extended phase space, by the usual Legendre transformation. By eliminating a few variables using Gaussian integration, we have established the BRST invariant gauge-fixed effective action and corresponding BRST symmetries of the FLPR model in both polar and Cartesian coordinates. We have also indicated that the effective action has a discrete symmetry in the ghost sector. In addition, using the physicality criterion, we have shown that the physical states of the theory are annihilated by the first-class constraints, which is consistent with the Dirac formalism.

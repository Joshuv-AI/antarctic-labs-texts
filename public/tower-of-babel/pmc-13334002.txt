Systematic design and analytical evaluation of a novel multi-speed bicycle rear transmission hub with a permanent magnet assisted gear shifting mechanism
Authors: Feng-Ming Ou, Yi-Chang Wu, Jin-Yi Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents a systematic design approach for a multi-speed bicycle rear transmission hub, which consists of a gear speed-changing mechanism and a rotary-type gear-shifting mechanism to enhance functional performance and optimize space utilization. The design process encompasses structural synthesis, gear stage arrangement, gear teeth synthesis, and clutch configuration, ensuring optimal power transmission and seamless gear shifting. A 9-speed rear transmission hub is used as an example to illustrate the design process. A compound planetary gear train with a new permanent magnet assisted gear-shifting mechanism was presented to provide nine forward gears, including four underdrive gears, a direct drive gear and four overdrive gears. The proposed design was verified through computer simulations, confirming its functional feasibility and mechanical integrity. Key performance results demonstrate that the system achieves a speed ratio ranging from 0.56 to 1.80 with a step jump no greater than 20%. Furthermore, the design maintains a compact dimension within a diameter of 200 mm and an axial length not exceeding 190 mm, while keeping the total weight under 3 kg. The results indicate that the system effectively meets performance specifications while addressing limitations associated with traditional spring-based gear shifting mechanisms. This study contributes to the advancement of multi-speed transmission hubs, providing a structured framework for next-generation bicycle transmission systems.Keywords: Embodiment design, Rear transmission hub, Bicycle, Gear-shifting mechanism, Planetary gear trainSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

A bicycle is a human-powered and pedal-driven vehicle for urban transportation, recreation, and competitive sports. The mechanical transmission of a bicycle primarily comprises components such as a crank, chain wheelset, chain, speed-changing mechanism, and associated control parts. This speed-changing element typically involves multi-speed transmission, which is crucial for converting speed and torque. The manual bicycle transmission options include rear derailleur and rear transmission hub systems. In contrast to the rear derailleur system that shifts the chain between sprockets, the rear transmission hub system encompasses a gear mechanism, particularly a planetary gear train. This planetary gear train, coupled with a gear-shifting mechanism, is contained within the hub shell of the bicycle. The hub’s enclosed nature shields it from water, dirt, and impacts, resulting in low maintenance requirements. Furthermore, the rear transmission hub facilitates gear changes even when the bicycle is stationary, ensuring smooth and reliable gear shifts in stop-and-go urban traffic scenarios. This aspect makes it suitable for city bike-sharing programs and foldable bicycles for convenient storage and transportation.

The concept of planetary gear trains in bicycle transmission hubs dates back to the late nineteenth century1, with William Reilly’s two-speed hub design in 1896, which was later commercialized in 18982. Since then, numerous advancements have been made, particularly in three-speed hubs, which became widely available from 1958 onwards for bicycles and wheelchairs3–8. These systems typically feature low-speed gears for uphill riding, direct-drive gears for flat terrain, and high-speed gears for enhanced velocity. In wheelchair applications, three-speed hubs include two forward gears and one reverse gear, improving maneuverability in confined spaces. As the demand for wider gear ranges and higher performance increased, manufacturers introduced multi-speed hubs with more intricate planetary gear mechanisms. In 1987, Fichtel & Sachs developed a 5-speed rear transmission hub9, followed by a 12-speed power-split hub in 1996, featuring a multi-stage compound planetary gear train10 with a total gear ratio of 339%, calculated as the quotient of the highest speed ratio to the lowest speed ratio. More recently, Wu and Ren11 proposed a 16-speed power-split hub with a 312% gear ratio, demonstrating the potential of high-speed internal gear systems. However, these power-split hubs require differential units, which increase weight, structural complexity, and installation space, posing challenges for lightweight bicycle applications.

While extensive research has been conducted on bicycle transmission hubs, most existing designs have been treated as isolated cases, lacking a systematic integration framework. This fragmented approach has resulted in limited standardization and innovation, hindering the advancement of multi-speed transmission hubs. Additionally, the gear-shifting mechanism within internal hubs plays a pivotal role in power transmission, regulating the sequential engagement of gear stages through mechanical clutches. Current gear-shifting configurations are classified into two types: translational and rotary configurations12. The translational configuration3–6,9,13 utilizes linear motion along the main hub axle to engage clutches, whereas the rotary configuration12,14 employs rotational movement for clutch control. When the number of gear stages exceeds five, the rotary configuration is generally preferred due to limited axial space in the rear fork area. However, mechanical clutches in these systems, such as pawl-and-ratchet, key-and-keyway, and centrifugal clutches12,15, rely on mechanical springs for engagement, which introduces challenges related to spring fatigue, lubrication, and space constraints.

To overcome these limitations, this study proposes a systematic design methodology for a multi-speed rear transmission hub, integrating a permanent magnet assisted gear-shifting mechanism to replace traditional mechanical springs. This innovation leverages magnetic force instead of mechanical force, potentially reducing wear and improving the functional reliability of the gear-shifting system. Furthermore, a compound planetary gear train is designed to achieve optimal speed ratios and torque transmission efficiency while maintaining a compact structure. This proposed approach not only enhances the scalability and standardization of transmission hubs design but also improves design durability and operational efficiency compared to conventional mechanical clutch-based systems.

Advanced mechanical design for space-constrained mobile devices must optimize functionality while being small and efficient. Recent studies have underscored the critical role of computational verification in achieving this design balance. For instance, Arifin et al.16 demonstrated the efficacy of combining computational simulation with experimental validation to minimize profile deviation in milling cutters. A similar level of rigorous verification is essential for bicycle transmissions as proposed in this study to ensure reliable clutch functionality. This approach aligns with the work of17, which investigated gear transmission dynamics using coupled numerical modeling to strictly validate transmission design integrity. In related transport applications, efficient numerical contact models for magnetic systems have demonstrated significant improvements when integrating magnetic components (such as the magnetic track brake or MTB) within intricate multibody dynamics systems18.

Building on these precedents, this paper presents a Permanent Magnet Assisted Gear Shifting Mechanism, utilizing combined mechanical-magnetic technology, for a multi-speed rear transmission hub. This integration is intricate, necessitating sophisticated design techniques such as topology optimization utilizing genetic algorithms to achieve precise kinematic and stiffness attributes, as well as compliant mechanisms with defined functions19,20. The methodology used in this paper adheres to the trend of functional optimization via structural simplification, wherein gear systems are ingeniously designed to execute complex motions (linear, steering, or variable speed) with a single actuator21,22. This research integrates magnetics, meticulous design optimization, and the simplicity of a compact gear to deliver an innovative hub design that enhances design integrity and spatial efficiency.

This paper presents a systematic design methodology for a multi-speed rear transmission hub that incorporates an innovative permanent magnet assisted gear-shifting mechanism to overcome the limitations of previous systems. This innovation substitutes traditional mechanical springs with magnetic actuation, thereby potentially reducing fatigue-related failures and improving long-term reliability based on design principles rather than experimental validation. The study’s main contributions consist of a detailed design framework that integrates structural synthesis and gear optimization, together with a compact rotary shifting mechanism specifically designed for the spatial limitations of bicycle rear forks. This pre-prototype work, validated by comprehensive CAD modeling and power flow simulations, rather than physical testing, marks the inaugural implementation of permanent magnet assisted engagement in bicycle hubs. This analytical foundation aims to inform future prototype development and experimental validation toward elevating drivetrain standards for enhanced efficiency and longevity. Design approach

The design approach for a multi-speed bicycle rear transmission hub follows a structured framework, as illustrated in Fig. 1. This framework integrates speed-changing mechanism design and gear-shifting mechanism design into a cohesive development approach, ensuring that both the planetary gear system and the clutching mechanism function efficiently. The process begins with defining design requirements and constraints, establishing performance objectives such as speed ratio distribution, step jumps, compactness, and reliability. Subsequently, the structural synthesis phase focuses on determining the optimal planetary gear train configuration, followed by speed-ratio specification, gear-stage arrangement, and gear-teeth-number synthesis, which collectively define the system’s kinematic properties and ensure smooth gear transitions.

Design process for a multiple-speed rear transmission hub.

Once the fundamental speed-changing mechanism is developed, the gear-shifting mechanism design is implemented to enable precise and reliable gear engagement. This involves clutching sequence establishment, which determines the order in which gears engage, followed by the design of input, output, and locking clutches to ensure efficient power transmission. The finalized components are then integrated into a comprehensive embodiment design, ensuring manufacturability and functional feasibility. Finally, the entire system undergoes design verification, where power flow validation and performance assessments confirm that the transmission hub meets the intended specifications. This structured approach ensures a systematic, scalable, and high-performance solution for multi-speed transmission hubs, paving the way for enhanced efficiency and standardization in bicycle drivetrain technology.

In this study, a 9-speed rear transmission hub was selected as a case study to validate and illustrate the proposed design approach. By implementing the structured framework shown in Fig. 1, the study demonstrates how systematic speed-changing and gear-shifting mechanism designs can be effectively integrated to meet design specifications. The following sections detail each step of the design process, beginning with the establishment of design requirements and constraints.

Structural synthesis

In this study, one DOF is assigned to control the locking of the sun gear, while the other DOF is used to select either the planetary carrier or the ring gear as the input link. This approach ensures a flexible and efficient gear-shifting mechanism, enabling the precise engagement of different speed stages. The clutch mechanism plays a crucial role in the gear-shifting process of the 9-speed rear transmission hub, ensuring seamless power transmission between speed stages. The mechanism consists of locking clutches, input clutches, and output clutches, which work together to engage and disengage different gear components according to the clutching sequence. This section details the clutching sequence establishment, input and output clutch designs, and the development of the permanent magnet assisted locking clutch, which enhances the system’s efficiency and reliability.

Clutching sequence establishment

The gear-shifting mechanism of the compound planetary gear train is controlled by mechanical clutches, which regulate the fixed, input, and output links at each gear stage. The sequence of clutch activation ensures smooth and precise gear transitions. To define the clutching sequence, a clutching sequence table26 is established, outlining the engagement and disengagement of clutches for each gear stage. Table 6 presents the clutch engagement sequence for the 9-speed rear transmission hub, detailing the gear stages, corresponding speed ratios, and the activation status of each clutch mechanism. The table is divided into three main sections: locking clutches (Cf1–Cf4), input clutches (Ci5 and Ci6), and output clutches (Co5 and Co6). The locking clutches are responsible for fixing the sun gears (Links 1, 2, 3, and 4) to the stationary shaft, thereby defining the power flow within the planetary gear system. The input clutches control whether the planetary carrier (Link 5) or the ring gear (Link 6) receives power input, while the output clutches determine whether the planetary carrier or the ring gear transmits output torque to the hub shell and rear wheel. The structured engagement pattern ensures that only one clutch transitions between adjacent gear stages, minimizing mechanical complexity and enhancing shift reliability. Notably, in gear stage V (direct drive, speed ratio = 1.000), power is transmitted directly from input to output without planetary gear engagement. Depending on the previous gear stage, either Co5 or Co6 must transition: If shifting from gear IV to gear V, Co5 disengages, and Co6 engages, allowing Ci6 to transfer power directly to Co6 without planetary gear interaction. This gradual transition is marked as X in the table. Similarly, if shifting from gear VI to gear V, Co6 disengages, and Co5 engages, establishing direct power flow through Co5. Since Co5 fully engages in this shift, it is marked as (X) in the table.

Input and output clutches design

Due to the spatial constraints within the rear hub, particularly along the axial direction of the main shaft, a rotary-type clutch configuration is implemented instead of a translational design. This selection optimizes space utilization and reduces the mechanical complexity of the gear-shifting mechanism. The system employs various types of mechanical clutches, including one-way pawl-and-ratchet clutches and key-and-keyway clutches, each serving distinct functions in the gear-shifting process. In the proposed transmission hub, input clutch Ci6 and output clutch Co5 are designed as one-way pawl-and-ratchet clutches as shown in Fig. 4. Their engagement depends on the relative rotational speeds of the pawl and ratchet elements. When the pawl’s rotational speed surpasses that of the ratchet, a C-shaped spring induces engagement, locking the mechanism into the engaged state as illustrated in Fig. 4a. Conversely, when the ratchet’s speed exceeds that of the pawl, the clutch automatically disengages in Fig. 4b. This mechanism enables automatic power transmission regulation, reducing wear potential and enhancing mechanical robustness.

Input clutch Ci6 and output clutch Co5: (a) engaged; (b) disengaged.

Additionally, input clutch Ci5 adopts a key-and-keyway clutch design, where the key and keyway features are located on the right-side carrier and an axially movable sleeve as shown in Fig. 5. The clutch activation depends on the axial position of the movable sleeve—when the sleeve is shifted, the key fits into the keyway, engaging the clutch as shown in Fig. 5a. When the sleeve moves away, the clutch disengages, Fig. 5b. This configuration ensures controlled and reliable gear-shifting, particularly in overdrive and direct drive modes.

Input clutch Ci5: (a) engaged; (b) disengaged.

The engagement and disengagement of Ci5 and Ci6 are inherently coordinated. When Ci5 engages during overdrive modes, Ci6 automatically disengages due to the differential speed interaction between the ring gear and carrier. This mechanism ensures that only one input clutch is engaged at any time, thereby optimizing power flow efficiency. Similarly, during underdrive modes, Co5 transmits power from the carrier to the hub shell, while output clutch Co6 operates under a specialized control mechanism. The output clutch Co6 is a one-way pawl-and-ratchet clutch with a long pawl element, which is governed by the flange of a pawl control ring, secured to the axially moveable sleeve, to control the counter part of the long pawl element as shown in Fig. 6. When Ci5 is activated, the flange remains disengaged, allowing Co6 to engage as shown in Fig. 6a. When Ci5 disengages and Ci6 activates, the flange pushes the long pawl, Fig. 6b, disengaging Co6 and engaging Co5.

Output clutch Co6: (a) engaged; (b) disengaged.

In summary, upon the activation of either Ci5 or Ci6 with one sun gear fixed, the output power from the compound planetary gear train is directed through either the ring gear or the carrier, subsequently passing through the corresponding output clutch Co6 or Co5 to reach the hub shell.

Locking clutch design

To enhance clutch engagement precision, a permanent magnet assisted locking clutch is implemented in the 9-speed rear transmission hub. This mechanism incorporates pawl elements, a ratchet system, a speed-regulating sleeve, and NdFeB permanent magnets to ensure precise and reliable engagement of the sun gears during shifting operations. Unlike conventional spring-assisted locking mechanisms, which rely on a mechanical spring to provide the restoring or holding action of the pawl, the proposed magnet-assisted design removes the spring element from the locking clutch. By replacing the spring-related cyclic loading/contact path with non-contact magnetic actuation in the disengaged state, the proposed design may reduce one possible source of spring fatigue and contact wear. Therefore, it provides a structurally simplified alternative for multi-speed internal gear hubs, although its long-term durability must be verified through prototype-based cycling and durability tests.

The key components of the locking clutch mechanism are illustrated in Fig. 7. The pawl element, shown in Fig. 7a, is embedded with a permanent magnet that interacts with the magnetic field of the speed-regulating sleeve. The speed-regulating sleeve, depicted in Fig. 7b, houses multiple embedded magnets strategically positioned to influence the movement of the pawl. The interaction between these components governs the locking and unlocking actions of the clutch. A geometric representation of the mechanism, as seen in Fig. 7c, demonstrates how the pawl, magnet, and speed-regulating sleeve work together to control engagement and disengagement, ensuring stable and reliable operation without reliance on conventional mechanical springs.

The magnetic force characteristics of the permanent magnet assisted clutching mechanism were evaluated through computational analysis and magnetic force equilibrium calculations. The NdFeB-N42 permanent magnet was modeled based on standard material properties, and FEA simulations were performed to quantify the magnetic attraction forces acting on the pawl element. Based on the material properties, FEA was conducted to map the magnetic flux density distribution and quantify the resulting magnetic attraction forces. The magnetic flux density distribution of the pawl element is shown in Fig. 8. The operating point of the N42 magnets is kept within the linear region of the B-H curve, and expected hub operating temperatures are far below the maximum working temperature of the magnet grade; thus demagnetization risk under normal cycling condition is low.

Subsequently, a force equilibrium analysis was performed using the free-body diagram (FBD) of the pawl, as illustrated in Fig. 9. Given a pawl mass of 0.27 g and the specified geometric moment arms , the magnetic attraction moment and gravitational moment were calculated relative to the pivot point. The magnetic force components Fy = 0.1871 N and Fz = 0.6410 N were obtained from the FEA simulation. The calculation reveals that the total magnetic moment is 1.037 N-mm, whereas the moment due to self-weight is significantly lower at 0.0039 N-mm. This condition, where , confirms that the generated magnetic force is sufficient to overcome gravitational forces, providing a substantial safety margin to ensure the reliability of the pawl’s position in the disengaged state. To account for manufacturing tolerances and magnetization variations, a sensitivity analysis was performed considering conservative reductions in magnet strength. Even with a 30% reduction in magnetic force, representing worst-case scenarios including material variability, temperature effects, and dimensional tolerances, the magnetic moment remains significantly larger than the combined gravitational and frictional effects. This substantial safety margin confirms that the magnetic force provides robust holding capacity to ensure stable pawl positioning in the disengaged state under practical manufacturing and operational conditions, thereby validating the design principle of the magnet assisted locking mechanism.

Free body diagram of the pawl.

With respect to magnetic cross-talk, preliminary multi-magnet FEA simulations suggest that the circumferential spacing of magnets and largely local flux paths limit mutual interference between adjacent magnets in the speed-regulating sleeve. The magnetic field distribution remains predominantly confined to the intended pawl-magnet interaction zones, though a more detailed parametric study of cross-talk under geometric and positional variations is recommended for future work. This robust safety margin and controlled magnetic field distribution ensure reliable pawl retention during clutch disengagement under realistic manufacturing and operational conditions, thereby validating the functional design of the magnet assisted locking mechanism.

It should be noted that the magnetic force analysis and force-equilibrium verification presented above are intended to establish the design-stage analytical viability of the proposed magnet-assisted locking mechanism. The present results confirm that the magnetic force is sufficient to retain the pawl in the intended disengaged position under the assumed static conditions. However, these results do not constitute dynamic response validation, durability verification, or thermal demagnetization testing. The proposed design removes the mechanical spring from the locking clutch and thereby eliminates the spring-related cyclic loading/contact path, which may reduce one possible source of fatigue and contact wear in conventional spring-assisted pawl mechanisms. Quantitative durability characterization, dynamic response under actual cycling loads, accelerated life testing, and thermal demagnetization evaluation over the relevant operating temperature range are therefore identified as necessary future work after physical prototype fabrication.

The locking clutch engagement process follows a systematic sequence, as illustrated in Fig. 10. In the initial position, Fig. 10a, the pawl remains in its default state, neither engaged nor constrained. As the speed-regulating sleeve begins to rotate, Fig. 10b, the mechanical constraint on the pawl is gradually removed, allowing the magnetic force to attract the pawl upward. Once the pawl fully aligns with the embedded magnet, Fig. 10c, it snaps into place, engaging the ratchet mechanism and establishing a secure locking state. During operation, the pawl remains in this engaged position ensuring stable power transmission. When shifting to a different gear stage, the speed-regulating sleeve continues its rotation, Fig. 10d, altering the magnetic field alignment, which forces the pawl to gradually disengage from the ratchet, enabling a smooth transition to the next gear.

The locking clutch mechanism is integrated with the planetary gear system as shown in Fig. 11, specifically interacting with the sun gears to regulate power transmission. In Fig. 11, the ‘solid line’ sun gear represents the locked state, indicating that it is engaged with the locking clutch mechanism, preventing its rotation. Meanwhile, the other sun gears, depicted as ‘transparent’, remain free to rotate, meaning they are not currently engaged by the clutch. The transition between different locked sun gears is controlled by the speed-regulating sleeve, which rotates to shift the engagement of the pawl mechanism within the locking clutch as illustrated in Fig. 10. The rotational positioning of the speed-regulating sleeve is controlled by the rider through a conventional cable-actuated shifting system. The actuation mechanism employs manual cable-pull technology similar to commercial systems such as Shimano’s handlebar-mounted shift lever and cassette joint assemblies27, where the rider’s input is mechanically transmitted through the shift cable. This cable motion is converted into discrete, indexed angular rotations of the speed-regulating sleeve, with each handlebar position corresponding to a specific gear stage.

Configuration of the locking clutch and sun gears in the transmission system.

The activation of the key-and-keyway clutch (Ci5) is controlled by a lateral flange incorporated on the right side of the speed-regulating sleeve, as illustrated in Fig. 7b. This flange serves as the kinematic link that converts the rotational motion of the speed-regulating sleeve into axial displacement of the connecting sleeve. When the speed-regulating sleeve rotates to a position where the lateral flange engages the connecting sleeve, it pushes the connecting sleeve and the axially movable sleeve to the right, causing Ci5 to disengage and thereby altering the transmission state, as shown in Fig. 12a.

Activation of Ci5 controlled by the speed regulating sleeve: (a) disengaged; (b) engaged.

Conversely, when the speed-regulating sleeve rotates to a position where the lateral flange does not engage the connecting sleeve, the compression spring located on the right side of the connecting sleeve returns the axially movable sleeve to its default left position, maintaining Ci5 in the engaged state, as depicted in Fig. 12b. This spring-return mechanism ensures that the axially movable sleeve assumes a defined default position without requiring active control. In summary, a single cable-actuated rotational input to the speed-regulating sleeve simultaneously determines the locking clutch selection via magnetic engagement and the input clutch state via the lateral flange mechanism, producing the complete clutch engagement pattern for each of the nine gear stages as defined in Table 6.

The rotary-type gear-shifting mechanism, incorporating permanent magnet assisted locking clutches, was designed to enable reliable and seamless gear transitions, while maintaining lightweight and structurally efficient performance suitable for bicycle applications.

The gear-shifting system, comprising input clutches (Ci5 and Ci6), output clutches (Co5 and Co6), and locking clutches (Cf1–Cf4), forms a rotational gear-shifting mechanism supported by permanent magnets, as illustrated in Fig. 13. Power flow path and mechanical efficiency verification

To validate the power transmission efficiency and functional integrity of the 9-speed rear transmission hub, a power flow path and mechanical efficiency verification were conducted. The assembly and simulation features were utilized to assess the gear engagement sequence, clutch interactions, and overall mechanical feasibility. Through this process, interference checks were performed to ensure there were no structural conflicts among the components, confirming that the system operates as intended under different gear stages. Moreover, the mechanical efficiency of each gear stage was calculated based on the corresponding power flow diagram.

Underdrive gear stages (stages I–IV)

In underdrive configurations, one of the sun gears is locked while the power is transmitted through the ring gear and planetary gear set to the carrier, which then drives the hub shell via output clutch Co5. The gear-shifting control element governs the rotational position of the speed-regulating sleeve, ensuring the correct engagement of the locking clutch at each speed stage.

At gear stage I, the speed-regulating sleeve is in its first angular position, engaging locking clutch Cf4, which locks sun gear 4. Each stage engages a different locking clutch (Cf3, Cf2, or Cf1), which locks the corresponding sun gear. The power flow path remains consistent with stage I, transmitting power from the rear sprocket through Ci6, ring gear, planetary gear, carrier, and Co5 to the hub shell, as illustrated in Figs. 15, 16, 17 and 18. Direct drive gear stage (stage V)

At gear stage V (direct drive mode, speed ratio = 1.000), none of the sun gears are locked, allowing both Ci5 and Ci6 to engage simultaneously. When the speed-regulating sleeve rotates to its fifth angular position, the axially movable sleeve (part no. 16) shifts to the left position under the influence of the compression spring (part no. 17), resulting in the engagement of Ci5 and Co6. At this stage, the carrier and ring gear rotate at the same speed, eliminating any differential motion within the planetary gear system. In overdrive configurations, the compression spring ensures that Ci5 and Co6 remain engaged, while one of the sun gears is locked to create a higher output speed ratio. In these stages, the rotational speed of the ring gear exceeds that of the carrier, leading to the disengagement of Ci6 and Co5. As a result, the power is transmitted from the rear sprocket through Ci5, carrier, planetary gear train, ring gear, and Co6, before reaching the hub shell.

Each overdrive gear stage (VI–IX) corresponds to a different locked sun gear, which determines the specific speed ratio achieved. The mechanical efficiency values presented in Table 10, ranging from 99.55 to 100%, represent the theoretical upper-bound efficiency of the internal planetary gear train only. These values exclude additional system losses such as bearing friction, clutch drag, seal drag, lubricant effects, manufacturing tolerances, and assembly errors. Therefore, they should be interpreted as the idealized gear-meshing efficiency of the proposed transmission mechanism, rather than as the actual system-level efficiency of the complete hub. Since the calculated gear-meshing efficiency is already close to unity, the practical efficiency of the complete hub is expected to be governed mainly by parasitic losses outside the gear mesh. Commercial multi-speed planetary internal gear hubs typically exhibit system-level efficiencies of approximately 90–97% after these losses are included. Accordingly, at the present design stage, the practical system-level efficiency of the proposed hub is expected to be of the same order, approximately 90–97%, provided that its bearing supports, clutch interfaces, lubrication condition, sealing, and manufacturing accuracy are comparable to those of existing commercial products. This range is a benchmark-based preliminary estimate rather than a measured value. Prototype-based efficiency testing is required to determine the actual operational efficiency under realistic torque, speed, lubrication, and shifting conditions.

Conclusion

This study successfully developed a systematic design framework for a 9-speed rear transmission hub, integrating a permanent magnet assisted gear-shifting mechanism to replace conventional spring-based engagement systems. Through comprehensive structural synthesis, gear teeth number optimization, and clutch sequence design, the proposed transmission system achieves precise power flow control and reduced wear exposure risk.

The analytical evaluation through CAD modeling, power flow simulations, and mechanical efficiency calculations validated the functional feasibility of the framework implementation. Computer simulations confirmed seamless operation across all nine gear stages with precise power flow control and optimized torque distribution. The rotary-type clutching system demonstrated efficient space utilization and enhanced engagement precision. By incorporating lightweight, high-strength materials such as magnesium alloy and composite materials, the design maintains a balance between performance and weight efficiency.

The successful integration of systematic design framework and analytical validation demonstrates a complete methodology for developing advanced internal gear hubs, translating theoretical principles into a functionally feasible system that meets industry standards while introducing magnetic actuation innovation. As a pre-prototype study, future work should focus on optimizing step jump distribution below 20%, refining permanent magnet parameters to minimize weight and cross-talk effects, and conducting physical prototyping with experimental validation. The methodology and findings provide a valuable foundation for future bicycle drivetrain innovations, supporting future development of compact, systematically designed multi-speed internal gear hubs with improved functional integration and design standardization.

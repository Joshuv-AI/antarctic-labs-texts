A robust decision framework for vertical farming using integrated objective weighting and distance driven ranking approach
Authors: Navaneethakrishnan Suganthi Keerthana, Samayan Narayanamoorthy, Kannusamy Aarthi, Selvaraj Geetha, Naif Almakayeel, Dragan Pamucar, Hasan Dinçer, Serhat Yüksel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Vertical Farming Techniques cultivates plants without soil, leading to greater productivity and profit. This unconventional farming technique requires minimal resources while producing high-quality yields. This study offers a comprehensive analysis of these methods, including hydroponics, aquaponics, aeroponics and fogponics. The quantification of uncertainty and ambiguity is done by employing the q-rung picture fuzzy set. This study introduces a novel hybrid Logarithmic percentage change-driven objective weighting- Multi-attributive border approximation area comparison framework, which provides objective analysis based on the distance measures. This study has concluded that the production yield plays a vital role in the selection of the optimal technology. The final results conclude that aquaponics is the ideal vertical farming method, which is followed by aeroponics, fogponics and hydroponics. The results of the proposed framework are validated and the reliability of the methodology is established by conducting a comparison analysis. Furthermore, Spearman’s rank correlation has been conducted for better understanding of the results of the comparison analysis. Finally, sensitivity analysis is performed to establish the model’s accuracy.

Introduction

Agriculture is the fundamental concept that underpinned the development of civilization and the world’s contemporary accomplishments. Later, as science and technology advanced, researchers and scientists made contributions to society through a variety of innovations that resulted in the contemporary world we live in today. It is anticipated that the world’s population will reach 98 billion by 20501. The urbanization brought forth with the industrial revolution has resulted in a highly concentrated population around the cities. The youths of the nation are attracted by the glamour of city life and thus, the youth participation in agricultural practices is quite diminishing. The high-paying and easy-to-operate jobs offered by the cities have attracted people to move to them. The tedious and labor-intensive nature of traditional farming has also resulted in hesitation among the youngsters from practicing agriculture. This marks the need for the introduction of new innovative technologies that can spark the youth’s interest and create a positive shift towards farming.

It is also worth noting that the progress made in the field of medical sciences has significantly increased people’s life expectancy. The constant increase in population has created greater demand for land for shelter. Thus, more and more agricultural land is being converted into buildings. In addition, the farmers in the rural areas are facing numerous difficulties, including 1. the limited availability of physical laborers caused by the high employment rates provided by engineering , science and technology Sect. Literature review. fluctuating market for the produce 3. the changing climatic conditions affecting the growth of the crops 4. the pests and the disease infection of the plants 5. The negative health effects caused by the use of pesticides etc2. Hence, it is imperative to bring about innovation in the field of agriculture that increases the yield in less arable land. One such technique is vertical farming, where the crops are grown in a soilless environment that reduces pest infection and increases the crop yield.

Vertical Farming Techniques (VFT) was introduced as a solution to tackle the lack of land and water resources and to increase profit. The concept of VFT was not a new technology that was recently developed, but was said to be around the period of the Roman Empire3. One of the wonders of the ancient world, the hanging gardens of Babylon, is said to be based on the hydroponics technique4. The concept of soilless culture was discussed in the final work of Francis Bacon, ‘Sylva Sylvarum’, which means “collection of collections” in the year 16275. But it was not until the 1900s that the research on the soilless culture became more prominent. Over the years, the term ‘vertical farming’ has been used in the sense of cultivating crops by spreading sand on vertical stacks. The cartoons of this time period published many new innovative concepts, including the idea of growing plants along the sides of the wall. Although these illustrations don’t address the feasibility of such ideas, it could be safely said that they laid the groundwork for many researchers and architects for the implementation of VFT. By the late 1930s, the research on the cultivation of plants by inserting the roots in a nutrient solution using an inert medium had started to gain momentum. W. F. Gericke introduced the term ‘Hydroponics’ and produced an alternative solution for traditional farming methods. Later, a new technique inspired by the Aztec civilization, which filled rafts with soil and grew plants to tackle the land availability issue, a prototype of aquaponics was developed. The Chinese farmers brought about aquaculture by introducing fish in their fields6. It was Dickson Despommier who tackled the high energy and maintenance cost associated with Othman Ruthner’s hydroponics greenhouse and redefined the paradigm of vertical farming proposed by Gilbert Ellis Bailey.

VFT offers an innovative approach for food production and can help in alleviating the land inadequacy and aid in the maximum utilization of available space. VFT can significantly reduce water consumption by 90% compared to the conventional methods. Since crops are cultivated without soil, it reduces the threat of infection and provides better absorption of nutrients as compared to the soil medium. The advantage of a controlled environment in VFT is that it mitigates the adverse effects of climatic changes resulting from global warming, water scarcity, and nutrient loss. It is to be noted that agriculture accounts for about 70% usage of the world’s fresh water, while the nation worldwide is battling a water crisis7. Also, the high carbon emissions in the urban areas can be minimized by growing plants on the facades of the buildings. This not only reduces the carbon dioxide in the urban buildings but also absorbs radiation and counteracts the urban heat island effect. Recent studies also state that growing plants in urban centers encourages interpersonal interactions and evokes the intrinsic social tendencies among the residents8. VFT also provides a boost to the economy as it allows layered plant growth and maximizes the yield. Another added advantage of VFT is that the controlled environmental condition protects the crops from fluctuating weather conditions and achieve a year-round crop production9.

This study aims to evaluate the emerging VFT, such as hydroponics, aquaponics, aeroponics and fogponics in terms of economic, performance and sustainability aspects. It is imperative to note that while VFT offers an advantage in farming, the selection of an optimal technology is as essential as the adoption of VFT. In order to facilitate the optimal technology selection, the fuzzy concepts are introduced for the better quantification of the abstract and arbitrary terms associated with the problem. This necessitates the incorporation of fuzzy Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM) for the objective assessment of VFT selection using hybrid LOgarithmic Percentage Change-driven Objective Weighting (LOPCOW)- Multi-Attributive Border Approximation area Comparison (MABAC) method. Literature review

There have been many studies that assess various VFT on the basis of multiple conflicting criteria and are discussed below. Cagri Tolga et al.10 have evaluated traditional farming, Internet of Things (IoT) integrated farming and full-scale automation by using MCDM techniques and have established that automation of agriculture can offer more productivity. The study on acquiring hydroponics technology on an industrial scale was done by Bendak et al.11 on three levels: internal development of the system, acquiring the system from an external supplier and joint venture with an external firm. It was concluded that fully developing the hydroponics system internally could maximize the profits. Ramakrishnam Raju et al.12 have presented an IoT model for the implementation of a hydroponics system, which can be monitored by the farmer in real time using a mobile application. According to Pomoni et al.13, switching to hydroponics can have advantages for plant nutrition, space efficiency, and less fertilizer use, but it also has the drawback of being expensive to set up initially. Kumar et al.14 highlighted that farmers’ lack of knowledge and expertise is the primary obstacle for the adoption of aquaponics and recommended that the key barriers identified may be easily mitigated and enforced the technique’s acceptance. Rossi et al.15 studied the optimal fish species to be reared within the system using the DEXi multi-criteria analysis. Greenfeld et al.16 conducted a life cycle assessment on the environmental benefits of aquaculture and have suggested that the setting up of commercial aquaculture units has a positive impact on the environment. Colt et al.17 have proposed an overall design system for aquaculture that focuses on the rearing systems for the crops, fish and the technical design for the aquaponics systems. Faisal et al.18 suggested a few considerations, such as atomization using pressure swirl atomisers and developed a grow tray for leafy greens. Garzón et al.19 have studied the impact of technology on the production and quality. Suganob et al.20 have evaluated a fogponics system powered by Artificial Intelligence under microgravity conditions.

The conversion of quantitative and qualitative data in decision-making often involves dealing with ambiguity. The fuzzy sets are well-equipped to handle the uncertainty. This part establishes the evolution of fuzzy sets. Zadeh introduced the notion of fuzzy for the mathematical quantification of ambiguous terms like ‘good’, ‘bad’, etc. that arise during the conversion of real-life scenarios. LOPCOW is one of the most prominent weighting methods. This part of the review highlights the studies conducted using the LOPCOW method. Rong et al.33 have introduced LOPCOW - Additive Ratio ASsessment (ARAS) and conducted failure mode and effect analysis for risk prediction. Biswas et al.34 have used the LOPCOW method for calculating the criteria weights for comparing the performance of businesses in the energy sector. Sumrit et al.35 have performed risk assessment by employing the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)- LOPCOW – ARAS method in the plastic waste recycling industry. Biswas et al.36 have ranked the performance of Initial Public Offerings in the Indian stock market using the LOPCOW method. A hybrid LOPCOW – DOmbi BonferronI (DOBI) method was proposed by Ecer et al.37 for the sustainability of the banking industry in the developing areas. Jiang et al.38 have presented a comparison between the subjective weighting method Subjective Weight Assignment by Ratio Analysis (SWARA) – the objective weighting method LOPCOW in the food supply chain performance analysis. The LOPCOW-FUll Consistency approach (FUCOM) approach has been implemented by Nila et al.39 in order to assess the selection of third-party logistics providers in the supply chain management industry. Ecer et al.40 suggested the Copeland technique together with the LOPCOW-visekriterijumska optimizacija i kompromisno resenje (VIKOR) for the unmanned aerial vehicle problem selection. In an attempt for sustainable urban planning, Zhu et al.41 employed the LOPCOW - Compromise Ranking of Alternatives from Distance to Ideal Solution (CRADIS) technique.

MABAC method is known for providing consistent and stable results even in cases where the units of measurement and type of criterion change. This part focuses on the applications of the MABAC method in various scenarios. Öztaş et al.42 have studied the application of the MABAC method by integrating the Gini Coefficient-based weighting method for ranking of the green universities. Božanić et al.43 have employed the Defining Interrelationships between Ranked criteria II (DIBR II) method for calculating the weights of the criteria while applying the MABAC method for evaluating the lean organization systems management. A novel CRiteria Importance Through Intercriteria Correlation (CRITIC) -MABAC- K means with Monte Carlo Simulation was proposed by Cui et al.44 in the case of road safety development. A literature review on the MABAC method was presented by Torkayesh et al.45 who have extensively studied 117 articles on the application of MABAC in various scenarios. The study has reviewed the application of the MABAC method over a period of 5 years and has identified 11 categories where MABAC is used. Jafari et al.46 have integrated the average ideal solution with the MABAC method and have studied the evaluation of knowledge sharing and resilience between supplier-suppliers under the PyFS environment. Chakraborty et al.47 have demonstrated the MABAC technique under 7 fuzzy environments. In order to align with the market trend in supply chains in agriculture, Puška et al.48 proposed Logarithm Methodology of Additive Weights (LMAW) – MABAC based on fuzzy rough sets. Božanić et al.49 have proposed a hybrid AHP-MABAC technique to rank the construction machine backhoe loader. Jana et al.50 have defined logarithm bipolar weighted and averaging operators for BFS and the effectiveness of the model was checked using the MABAC technique. Simic et al.51 have proposed a type-2 neutrosophic CRITIC- MABAC technique for the optimal selection of the public transport pricing system. The competitiveness of the maritime ports beyond throughput was evaluated by Patel et al.52 by employing Entropy and Best Worst Method (BWM) for weight assignment and MABAC for ranking the ports based on port competitive index.

The application of the LOPCOW technique ensures fair allocation of criteria importance. The MABAC is a distance-based ranking method that ranks the alternatives based on the distance obtained from the Border Approximation Area (BAA) matrix. Case study

VFT is the upcoming modern agricultural technique that exhibits a greater potential in turning the youth towards farming. Hence, it is imperative to select an ideal VFT to ensure maximized profits and minimized losses. In this study, four VFT such as hydroponics (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Lambda _1$$\end{document}), aquaponics (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Lambda _2$$\end{document}), aeroponics (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Lambda _3$$\end{document}) and fogponics (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Lambda _4$$\end{document}) are chosen as alternatives. A brief description of each of the alternatives is given below:

Hydroponics: The most prominent and widely used vertical technique is hydroponics. In 1929, Dr. William Frederick Gericke proposed this term for the first time. This method involves submerging the plant roots in the nutritional solution, either with or without the support of an inert medium like sawdust, sand, perlite, or coir. Hydroponically growing plants typically require a greenhouse, a growth chamber to cultivate the plants, a chemically neutral medium to support the plants, a nutrient solution that contains the minerals needed for plant growth, a nutrient reservoir to store the nutrient solution, and an air pump submerged in the nutrient solution to increase the amount of dissolved oxygen in the nutrient solution.

Aquaponics:Aquaculture is the practice of rearing aquatic animals such as fish, shellfish etc. in a controlled environment. Aquaponics is a modern technique that brings together hydroponics and aquaculture by integrating the simultaneous cultivation of plants and fish in a closed or coupled system. This technique usually consists of a reservoir for the fish growth and a separate hydroponic setup. The nutrient-rich water required for the growth of the plants is provided by the aquaculture unit. The nutrients essential for plant growth are supplied by the aquaculture unit. The water flows between the units with the help of gravity and flows into the sedimenter, which filters out the solid waste from the system. This water then flows to the plants and then to the sump tank. Finally, the water from the sump is pumped back to the aquaculture unit. This system also requires an aeration unit to help maintain the growth of the fish.

Aeroponics:The term ‘aeroponics’ comes from the Greek words “aero”- meaning air and ‘ponics’ – meaning labour. This system is similar to hydroponics except that the roots of the plants are suspended in the air. This system consists of a sealed container where the plants are hung, a container to store the nutrient solution and pressure pumps to spray the nutrient solution onto the roots of the plants and to drain out the nutrient solution back to the container. The plants are suspended above the solution and low or high-pressure devices are used for spraying nutrient-rich solution based on the requirements of the system. Since, the roots absorb the minerals from the mist of nutrient solution, the system has to be turned on every 2-3 minutes to help roots retain moisture.

Fogponics:Fogponics is similar to aeroponics, as in both techniques the minerals are provided to the plants in the form of mist. However, fogponics uses water vapour to provide the minerals for the plants, aside from the aeroponics which sprays the nutrient solution using a sprayer. In fogponics, plants are cultivated in a container. This system consists of a minimal setup which includes a container, fog generator, and air pump in a closed or contained environment. In certain systems, the fog is generated using ultrasonic fog generators placed inside the container of nutrient solution. An air pump is used to supply oxygen to the plants. Certain systems also use growing mediums such as Leca clay or rock wool for the development of the crops.

Conclusion

VFT is an optimal solution in scenarios where high yield can be obtained from less available space. It offers a solution to the lack of workforce due to the rapid urbanization and the population drift towards cities brought about by the industrialization. VFT also provides opportunities for urban people to practice agriculture from the comfort of their homes and cultivate their own vegetables for their daily needs. This study evaluates four VFT on the basis of sustainability, performance and economic aspects. The complexities of fuzzy information are tackled using the q-PFS environment. In addition, this study introduces a combination of LOPCOW and MABAC methods as a means to rank the alternatives. The results of the LOPCOW method indicate that the total yield produced by the system is the major criterion in choosing the optimal VFT technique. The study highlights energy consumption as the major cause of hesitation among the masses for the adoption of VFT. Finally, the ranking of the alternatives obtained from the MABAC method indicates that aquaponics is the most desirable VFT, followed by aeroponics, fogponics and hydroponics. Based on the results, it is evident that aquaponics has the ability to produce more yield while minimizing the costs associated with the process.

However, this study also poses certain limitations which must be addressed. This study solely relies on the objective weighting method. Though objective analysis provides results without bias, the expert’s preference must also be considered before making the assessment. Since the concept of VFT is still unfamiliar in rural areas where the majority of the agriculture is carried out in the country, the study has not considered the social and political aspects during the assessment.

In the future, this study can be expanded to include social and political dimensions for the assessment of VFT. Also, the study could be expanded to consider real-time data, market trends and sentiment of the people. The robustness and the flexibility of the developed q-PFS-LOPCOW-MABAC method make it possible for the application of the proposed methodology in various other fields, such as supply chains, waste management, etc. The proposed methodology can also be expanded by combining both subjective and objective weighting methods so as to consider the expert’s preference while minimizing the risk of biased opinion.

Normal forms of functions with degenerate singularities on surfaces equipped with semi-free circle actions
Bohdan Feshchenko

Abstract
. This article is devoted to the study of a certain class of smooth circle-valued functions on a cylinder S 1 × [ 0 , 1 ] S^{1}\times[0,1] , a torus T 2 T^{2} , a disk D 2 D^{2} and a sphere S 2 S^{2} which is a generalization of Morse-Bott functions without saddles. We established a "normal form" for functions from this class, namely, we proved that any such function represents in the form f = ϰ ∘ f 0 ∘ h − 1 f=\varkappa\circ f_{0}\circ h^{-1} , where f 0 f_{0} is the simplest Morse function on the given surface for some diffeomorphism h h of M M and a smooth function ϰ \varkappa satisfying some natural conditions.



1.
Introduction

The study of smooth functions on manifolds and their properties lies at the heart of a modern differential topology and a theory of dynamical systems. It connects local geometric properties of functions to the global topology of the underlying surfaces. This connection is well understood for generic classes of smooth functions like Morse functions, functions with isolated singularities, Morse-Bott functions.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold, and
P
be either a real line
\mathbb{R}
or a circle
S^{1}
.
Recall that two smooth
P
-valued function
f,g:M\to P
are called topologically (smoothly) equivalent, if there exist homeomorphisms (diffeomorphisms)
h:M\to M
and
\ell:P\to P
such that

(1)

f=\ell\circ g\circ h^{-1}.

The question of when two functions are smoothly or topologically equivalent is challenging and remains open in general. Topological equivalence of functions with isolated singularities on smooth compact surfaces were studied by V. Sharko
[
17
]
, E. V. Kulinich
[
5
]
, A. Prishlyak
[
16
]
,
A. Kadubovskiy
[
3
]
, for simple Morse functions by J. Martínez-Alfaro, I. S. Meza-Sarmiento, and R. Oliveira

[
13
]
, for circle-valued simple Morse-Bott functions were studied by E. B. Batista, J. C. F. Costa and I. S. Meza-Sarmiento
[
1
]
.
V. Sharko obtained the description of connected components of the space of Morse functions.

The functions (Definition
1.4
) we will study in this paper are closely related to questions of the study of homotopy properties stabilizers of smooth functions.
We will recall the necessary definitions.
The product
\mathcal{D}(P)\times\mathcal{D}(M)
of the groups of diffeomorphisms of
P
and diffeomorphisms of
M
naturally acts from the left on the space of smooth
P
-valued functions
C^{\infty}(M,P)
by the rule:

\gamma_{LR}:\mathcal{D}(P)\times\mathcal{D}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,P)\to C^{\infty}(M,P),\qquad\gamma_{LR}(\ell,h,f)=\ell\circ f\circ h^{-1}.

The group
\mathcal{D}(M)=\mathrm{id}_{M}\times\mathcal{D}(M)
is a subgroup of

\mathcal{D}(P)\times\mathcal{D}(M)
and
\gamma_{LR}
induces a left action

\gamma_{R}:\mathcal{D}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,P)\to C^{\infty}(M,P),\qquad\gamma_{R}(h,f)=f\circ h^{-1}.

Actions
\gamma_{LR}
and
\gamma_{R}
are left actions, but
it will be convenient to
use the terms “left-right” and “right” to refer the sides at which we apply the corresponding diffeomorphisms.
Note that two smooth functions
f,g:M\to P
are smooth equivalent, if they belongs to the same orbit of the action
\gamma_{LR}.

Discussion of the result

Due to a famous result of H. Whitney
[
19
]
, for any
even

C^{\infty}
-function
f:[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\to\mathbb{R}
,
\varepsilon>0
there exists a unique
C^{\infty}
-function
\alpha:[0,\varepsilon^{2}]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f
can be presented as
f(x)=\alpha(x^{2})
,
x\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
. Obviously that
\alpha
is defined by
\alpha(t)=f(\sqrt{t})
and
\alpha
is smooth on
(0,\varepsilon^{2}].
Whitney showed that
\alpha
is smooth at
0.

A function
f_{0}:[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f_{0}(x)=x^{2}
is the “simplest” even function and since
\alpha
is unique defined, the decomposition
f=\alpha\circ f_{0}
can be called a “normal form” of an even function
f
(in spirit of e.g., a Jordan normal form in linear algebra, or a Poincaré normal form in the theory of dynamical systems, Whitney’s normal forms of fold and pleat singularities, etc). Note also that
f
is obtained by smoothly changing values of
f_{0}
by the function
\alpha
.

This similarity can be observed in Theorem
1.8
, i.e.,
for each
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\circ}(M,P)
there exist
h
and
\varkappa
as above, and such that a function
f\circ h:M\to P
is obtained from a prime function
f_{0}:M_{0}\to P
by smoothly changing its values using a function
\varkappa
on the image of
f_{0}.
So
f
is obtained as (
2
) from the “simplest” one. A function
\varkappa
plays the role of a “generator” of critical circles for
f
.

Let

.

By (

.

The rest of the section is devoted to studying the behavior of the functions from
\mathcal{F}^{\circ}(M,P)
in the neighborhood of critical circles.
Let

.
By the definition of the class

for any

3.1.
Hamiltonian vector fields

Let
M
be a smooth and

It is well-known that

Since

be its trivialization isomorphism. Therefore a differential

of

\textstyle{Df:TM\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{df}
\textstyle{TP\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\zeta}
\textstyle{P\times\mathbb{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R},}

where

A vector field

satisfies the following conditions:

•

•

.

Let

5.

-actions on surfaces

Let
\psi:M\times G\to M
be a smooth action of a Lie group
G
on a smooth manifold
M
.
It is known that
\mathrm{Fix}(\psi)
is a closed subset of

An action
\psi:M\times G\to M

\mathrm{Fix}(\psi)
.

If

is a smooth oriented and compact surface and
\psi
is a semi-free
S^{1}
-action on

one can establish, by using for example slice theorem
[
4
, p. 139]
that

is a finite subset of

and
\chi(M)=|\mathrm{Fix}(\psi)|\geq 0
, where

is an Eulerian characteristic of

.
The last formula is also a simple consequence of Atiyah–Bott–Berline–Vergne integration formula, see
[
18
, Theorem 30.2]
.
Therefore

is diffeomorphic to one of the following list
S^{1}\times[0,1]
,
T^{2}
,
D^{2}
,
S^{2}.

It is also known that any smooth
S^{1}
-action on a surface

is uniquely determined by some vector field on

, since
S^{1}
is connected Lie group.
In this paragraph we will show how such

-actions naturally “occur” from smooth functions on surfaces.

Let

be a function from
\mathcal{F}^{0}(M,P)
,
F
be an
H
-field of

with the flow
\mathbf{F}_{t}:M\to M
,

, see Proposition
3.6
.
A function

does not have saddle critical points, so each trajectory of

is periodic.
There exist a function
\mathsf{per}_{F}:M\setminus\Sigma_{f}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}

with respect to the flow
\mathbf{F}
, see
[
10
, §3]
.

Let also
\nu:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a positive smooth function on

which is constant along trajectories of

It is known that a vector field

defines the same foliation on

as

.
We can choose a function

Near each isolated singularity

-action on

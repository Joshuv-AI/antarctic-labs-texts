On efficient approximation of quadratic irrationals
Peter H. van der Kamp, Anthony Overmars, Marcel Jackson, Andrew Hone

Abstract
We provide efficient algorithms to compute convergents of quadratic irrationals. We show that for square roots, provided Galois’ refinement of Lagrange’s theorem holds, certain decimations of the sequence of convergents are signed Chebyshev sequences, which can be also be generated by a Householder method.

In this section, we introduce several sequences of polynomials related to the Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind. These can be defined in several ways: by linear recurrences, using trigonometric functions, as solutions of Pell equations, or via powers of two-by-two matrices with determinant

Proposition 1

4
When Galois holds, a decimation of the sequence of convergents is a Chebyshev sequence

Throughout this section, and the next, we consider numbers
\alpha=\sqrt{N}
for which the continued fraction has the form (
4
). For such numbers, certain decimations (a decimation is an arithmetic subsequence) of the sequence of convergents are signed Chebyshev sequences.

Theorem 11
.

Let
p_{i},q_{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots
, be the convergents of a continued fraction of the form (
4
), so that equation (
5
) holds, then

\begin{split}p_{kl-1}&=T^{l}_{k}(p_{l-1}),\\
q_{kl-1}&=q_{l-1}U^{l}_{k-1}(p_{l-1}).\end{split}

(30)

Proof.

The palindromic nature of (
4
) tells us that the matrix
\Psi_{0}^{-1}\Psi_{l-1}
is symmetric, which yields
q_{l-2}=p_{l-1}-c_{0}q_{l-1}
. Together with the fact that

c_{l}=2c_{0}
, this implies that

\displaystyle t_{1}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left(\Psi_{0}^{-1}\Psi_{l}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&-c_{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p_{l-1}&p_{l-2}\\
q_{l-1}&q_{l-2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}2c_{0}&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{0}q_{l-1}+p_{l-1}+q_{l-2}

\displaystyle=2p_{l-1}.

The
1,1
-component of formula (
17
) tells us that, with
p_{l-1}=x
, for all
n
,

p_{n+2l}=2xp_{n+l}-(-1)^{l}p_{n}.

Since
p_{-1}=1
, according to (
11
) we have
p_{kl-1}=T^{l}_{k}(x)
.
Similarly, the
2,1
-component of formula (
17
) yields

q_{n+2l}=2xq_{n+l}-(-1)^{l}q_{n},

and, as
q_{-1}=0
, this implies
q_{2l-1}=2xq_{l-1}
. Thus, we see that up to a constant factor
q_{l-1}
, the decimation
q_{kl-1}
is described by a signed Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind,
U^{l}_{k-1}(x)
, cf. (
12
).
∎

Householder’s method

Theorem 13
.

Let
f(x)=x^{2}-N
and denote by
(1/f)^{(d)}
the
d
-th derivative of
1/f
. The order
d
numerical method from Householder,

H(x)=x+d\frac{(1/f)^{(d-1)}(x)}{(1/f)^{(d)}(x)},

(31)

applied to the
(l-1)
st convergent of
\sqrt{N}
,
x=p_{l-1}/q_{l-1}
, yields the
(kl-1)
st convergent, where
k=d+1
.

Proof.

According to
[
4
, Theorem 9]
, we have, with
X=\frac{N+x^{2}}{N-x^{2}}
,

H(x)=\begin{cases}x\frac{W_{d/2}(X)}{W_{d/2}(X)}&\text{when }d\text{ is even},\\[2.84526pt]
x\frac{T_{(d+1)/2}(X)}{(X-1)U_{(d-1)/2}(X)}&\text{when }d\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

(32)

For convenience we write
x=p/q
. We have, using (
5
),

X=\frac{Nq^{2}+p^{2}}{Nq^{2}-p^{2}}=1-(-1)^{l}2p^{2}=\begin{cases}-T_{2}(p)&\text{when }l\text{ is even},\\
{\overline{T}}_{2}(p)&\text{when }l\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Substituting the appropriate expression for
X
in (
32
), and using statement
i
of Proposition
3
with

i=\begin{cases}1&\text{when }d\text{ is odd and }l\text{ is even},\\
2&\text{when }d\text{ is odd and }l\text{ is odd},\\
3&\text{when }d\text{ is even and }l\text{ is even},\\
4&\text{when }d\text{ is even and }l\text{ is odd},\\
\end{cases}

yields

H(x)=\frac{T^{l}_{d+1}(p)}{qU^{l}_{d}(p)},

which according to Theorem
11
equals
p_{kl-1}/q_{kl-1}
with
k=d+1
.
∎

Remark 14
.

The special case
k=2
of (
30
), which corresponds to
d=1
of (
31
), is called Newton’s method,

\frac{p_{2l-1}}{q_{2l-1}}=\frac{T^{l}_{2}(p_{l-1})}{q_{l-1}U^{l}_{1}(p_{l-1})}\\
=\frac{2p_{l-1}^{2}-(-1)^{l}}{2q_{l-1}p_{l-1}}.

(33)

It coincides with the Babylonian (or Heron’s) method, cf.
[
4
]
and
[
9
]
, where the method was used to approximate
\sqrt{2}=[1,\overline{2}]
, i.e., one iterates

\frac{p}{q}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}.

Note that the right hand side equals

\frac{2p^{2}-1}{2qp},

if
p/q
satisfies the Pell equation
p^{2}-2q^{2}=1
. The case
k=3
is Halley’s method,

\frac{p_{3l-1}}{q_{3l-1}}=\frac{T^{l}_{3}(p_{l-1})}{q_{l-1}U^{l}_{2}(p_{l-1})}\\
=\frac{p_{l-1}}{q_{l-1}}\frac{4p_{l-1}^{2}-3\left(-1\right)^{l}}{4p_{l-1}^{2}-\left(-1\right)^{l}}.

(34)

Remark 15
.

For large
k
, equation (
30
) can be evaluated in
O(\log k)
matrix multiplications, see
[
2
, Section 3.1]
, by writing the signed Chebyshev recursion in the form
\Xi^{l}_{n}=M_{l}\Xi^{l}_{n-1}
, where

\Xi^{l}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}T^{l}_{n+1}&U^{l}_{n+1}\\
T^{l}_{n}&U^{l}_{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad M_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}2x&-(-1)^{l}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\Xi^{l}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}x&2x\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}.

(35)

Alternatively, one may apply the halve-and-square method, see
[
2
, Section 3.2]
, based on

\Xi^{l}_{n}=\begin{cases}2T^{l}_{m}\Xi^{l}_{m}-(-1)^{ml}\Xi^{l}_{0}&n=2m,\\
2T^{l}_{m}\Xi^{l}_{m+1}-(-1)^{ml}\Xi^{l}_{1}&n=2m+1,\\
\end{cases}

(36)

which follows from the Cayley–Hamilton theorem and the facts that Tr
(M_{l}^{m})=2T^{l}_{m}
and Det
(M_{l}^{m})=(-1)^{ml}
.

On the packing dimension of distance sets with respect to 𝑪 \bm{C^{1\!}} and polyhedral norms Thanks: B. W. is supported by NSF CAREER Fellowship, DMS 2142064.
Iqra Altaf, Ryan Bushling, Bobby Wilson

Abstract
. We prove that, for every polyhedral or C 1 C^{1} norm on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and every set E ⊆ ℝ d E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} of packing dimension s s , the packing dimension of the distance set of E E with respect to that norm is at least s d \tfrac{s}{d} . One of the main tools is a nonlinear projection theorem extending a result of M. Järvenpää. An explicit construction follows, demonstrating that these distance sets bounds are sharp for a large class of polyhedral norms.

1.
Introduction

For a norm
\|\cdot\|_{*}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let

\Delta^{*}(E):=\big\{\|y-x\|_{*}\in[0,\infty)\!:x,y\in E\big\}

be the
distance set
of
E
with respect to that norm. Given a fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we also let

\Delta_{x}^{*}(E):=\big\{\|y-x\|_{*}\in[0,\infty)\!:y\in E\big\}

be the
pinned distance set
of
E
at
x
. The classical
Falconer distance conjecture

[
6
]
posits that, for
E
(say, Borel),

\hdim E>\frac{d}{2}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\hdim\Delta(E)=1,

where
\hdim
denotes Hausdorff dimension and
\Delta
the distance set operator for the Euclidean norm. More quantitatively, one frequently seeks lower bounds on
\hdim\Delta(E)
or
\hdim\Delta_{x}(E)
in terms of
\hdim E
. For recent progress on the distance set conjecture, see
[
5
,
8
,
9
]
. In particular,
[
5
]
contains a clear and thorough history of the problem.

This paper may be seen as a follow-up to
[
1
]
, in which the authors treat the Falconer distance problem for general norms, emphasizing in particular the case of polyhedral norms. The main result therein is the following.

Theorem (
[
1
]
Theorems 1.1 & 1.4).

Let
\|\cdot\|_{*}
be a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

(1)

\hdim\Delta_{x}^{*}(E)\geq\hdim E-(d-1)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This bound is sharp for polyhedral norms in the sense that, if
\|\cdot\|_{P}
is a polyhedral norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then for any
s\in[d-1,d]
, there exists a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\hdim E=s
such that

\hdim\Delta^{P}(E)=s-(d-1).

1.1.
Main results

Distance sets with respect to polyhedral norms have also been considered in
[
13
,
7
,
12
,
2
]
, and in addition
[
2
]
presents strictly convex
C^{1}
norms with pathological distance sets. The works
[
14
,
11
,
8
,
9
]
address problems concerning the
packing
dimension
\pdim
of distance sets, and here we consider the packing dimension of distance sets with respect to polyhedral and
C^{1}
norms. Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{*}
be either a
C^{1}
or polyhedral norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
x\in E
such that

(2)

\pdim\Delta_{x}^{*}(E)\geq\frac{1}{d}\pdim E-\varepsilon.

In particular,

\pdim\Delta^{*}(E)\geq\frac{1}{d}\pdim E.

In both the
C^{1}
and the polyhedral case, the normal vector field to the surface the unit ball is regular enough to establish reasonable transversality conditions on the pinned distance functions
y\mapsto\|x-y\|_{*}
. Just as (
1
) was sharp for every polyhedral norm in a strong way, (
2
) is also sharp for a large class of polyhedral norms.

Theorem 1.2.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{P}
be a polyhedral norm such that the unit normal vectors to the faces of the polyhedron are rationally dependent and let
s\in[0,d]
. There exists a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\pdim E=s
such that

(3)

\pdim\Delta^{P}(E)=\frac{s}{d}.

A main tool in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is a nonlinear projection estimate extending a result of Järvenpää
[
10
]
. Let
\mathbf{p}=(\mathbf{p}_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
be a family of functions
\mathbf{p}_{i}\!:E\to\mathbb{R}
defined on a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\ubdim
denote upper box dimension.

Definition 1.1  (Weak transversality).

We say that
\mathbf{p}
is
weakly transversal
if, for every bounded set
F\subseteq E
, its restriction
\mathbf{p}|_{F}
is bounded and has fibers of upper box dimension
0
. That is:

i.

\mathbf{p}(F)
is bounded; and

ii.

for every
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists a natural number
M(\delta)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
such that the following holds: for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exist
m\leq M(\delta)
many points
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(4)

\mathbf{p}^{-1}\big(B(\xi,\delta)\big)\cap F\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{m}B(x_{k},\delta).

The first condition simply ensures that
\ubdim\mathbf{p}(F)
is defined whenever
\ubdim F
is. The second condition ensures that the fibers of the functions
\mathbf{p}_{i}
intersect fairly transversely at most points; it is always satisfied, for example, when
\mathbf{p}
is locally invertible with Lipschitz inverses. Either
\delta
(or both) in (
4
) can be replaced with a number
\sim_{\varepsilon}\delta
.

With this terminology, we have the following result.

Proposition 1.3.

Let
\mathbf{p}=\big(\mathbf{p}_{i}\!:E\to\mathbb{R}\big)_{i=1}^{d}
be a weakly transversal family, let
n\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, and for every string of indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}
let
\mathbf{p}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}:=\big(\mathbf{p}_{i_{j}}\!:E\to\mathbb{R}\big)_{j=1}^{n}
. Then for every set
F\subseteq E
,

(5)

\frac{n}{d}\ubdim F\leq\max_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq d}\ubdim\mathbf{p}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}(F)

provided
F
is bounded, and

(6)

\frac{n}{d}\pdim F\leq\max_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq d}\pdim\mathbf{p}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}(F).

We only require the result for
n=1
, and it is possible to prove this directly by a simple covering argument. However, we have included the full statement in the interest of generality and elected a proof that leverages Järvenpää’s result on orthogonal projections.

If
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is a strictly convex norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the “projection maps”
\mathbf{p}_{i}(x):=\|x-x_{i}\|_{*}
are weakly transversal for any two distinct
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, whereby the distance set bound of Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from Proposition
1.3
. In higher dimensions, one can establish a similar result for strictly convex norms for
d
pins
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
in general position. Things are not so simple for norms with more degenerate curvature. Establishing transversality for
some
set of pins on a large portion of
E
becomes the main hurdle in proving distance sets estimates for such norms.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any bounded set and
N(E,\delta)
its
covering number
at scale
\delta
—the minimal number of
\delta
-balls required to cover
E
. The
upper box dimension
of a bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by

\ubdim E:=\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log N(E,\delta)}{-\log\delta}.

Note that upper box dimension is finitely stable not but countably stable: that is, if
E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}
, then
\ubdim E=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\ubdim E_{i}
provided
m<\infty
, whereas it can happen that
\ubdim E>\sup_{1\leq i<\infty}\ubdim E_{i}
. Packing dimension can be defined simply by “forcing” upper box dimension to be countably stable: given any set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
(not necessarily bounded), we let

(7)

\pdim E:=\inf\left\{\sup_{1\leq i<\infty}\ubdim E_{i}\!:E\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all countable covers of
E
by bounded sets. It follows immediately that
\ubdim E\geq\pdim E
for every bounded set
E
, and it is also not difficult to show that
\pdim E\geq\hdim E
.

We sometimes use the standard notation
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
to indicate that
A\leq c_{\alpha}\hskip 0.83298ptB
for some constant
c_{\alpha}
depending only on the parameter (or collection of parameters)
\alpha
; these may be suppressed from the notation where the parameters are not especially important. The notation
A\sim_{\alpha}B
means that both
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
and
B\lesssim_{\alpha}A
. Importantly, if for some
E_{1},E_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the relation
N(E_{1},\delta)\lesssim_{\alpha}N(E_{2},\delta)
holds for all
\delta\in(0,1)
, where
\alpha
is independent of
\delta
, then
\ubdim E_{1}\leq\ubdim E_{2}
.

Finally, we remark here that §
5
makes heavy use of tools from convex analysis, but these will be introduced as needed. For more thorough references, see
[
3
,
4
]
.

3.
Proof of Proposition
1.3

First we recall an orthogonal projection theorem of Järvenpää
[
10
]
. Given linearly independent vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
V=V(e_{1},\dots,e_{n}):=\Span\,\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
and let
P_{V}\!:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
onto
V
.

Theorem 3.1  (Järvenpää
[
10
]
Theorem 3.4 & Corollary 3.5).

If
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{d})
is a basis for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1\leq n\leq d
, then for all
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exist
1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{n}\leq d
such that

\frac{n}{d}\ubdim E\leq\ubdim P_{V(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{n}})}(E)

provided
E
is bounded, and

\frac{n}{d}\pdim E\leq\pdim P_{V(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{n}})}(E).

The statement in Järvenpää
[
10
]
is for
1\leq n\leq d-1
, but it also holds trivially for
n=d
as stated above.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

We first consider the case (
5
) of upper box dimension. Let
F\subseteq E
be a bounded set and
1\leq n\leq d
. By Theorem
3.1
, there exist indices
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq d
such that

\frac{n}{d}\ubdim\mathbf{p}(F)\leq\ubdim P_{V(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{n}})}(\mathbf{p}(F)),

where
(e_{1},\dots,e_{d})
denotes the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Observe that
P_{V(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{n}})}(\mathbf{p}(F))=\mathbf{p}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}(F)
, so it suffices to show that
\ubdim\mathbf{p}(F)\geq\ubdim F
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, and let
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a cover of
\mathbf{p}(F)
by
N:=N(\mathbf{p}(F),\delta)
-many
\delta
-balls. By the definition of weak transversality, for each
i
there exists a collection
\{B_{i,j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{i}}
of
\delta
-balls such that

\mathbf{p}^{-1}(B_{i})\cap F\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{i}}B_{i,j},

where
N_{i}\lesssim\delta^{-\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Hence,
\{B_{i,j}\!:1\leq i\leq N,\,1\leq j\leq N_{i}\}
is a cover of
F
by

\sum_{i=1}^{N}N_{i}\lesssim N\delta^{-\varepsilon}

balls of radius
\delta
. It follows that

\displaystyle\ubdim F

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log N(F,\delta)}{-\log\delta}\leq\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log(N\delta^{-\varepsilon})}{-\log\delta}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log N(\mathbf{p}(F),\delta)}{-\log\delta}+\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log\delta^{-\varepsilon}}{-\log\delta}

\displaystyle=\ubdim\mathbf{p}(F)+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, the result follows and (
5
) holds.

We now prove the packing dimension case (
6
). Let
\varepsilon>0
and, for each string of indices
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq d
, let
\big\{F_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}^{(j)}\big\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a cover of
\mathbf{p}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}(F)
by bounded sets such that

\sup_{1\leq j<\infty}\ubdim F_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}^{(j)}<\pdim\mathbf{p}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}(F)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

The collection of all sets of the form

(8)

\bigcap_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq d}\mathbf{p}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}^{-1}\!\Big(F_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}^{(j_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}})}\Big),\quad 1\leq j_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}<\infty

is a cover of
F
, but not necessarily by bounded sets. Therefore, we refine this to a new cover
\big\{G^{(k)}\big\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
consisting of all sets formed by intersecting the elements (
8
) with unit cubes
[n_{1},n_{1}+1)\times\cdots\times[n_{d},n_{d}+1)
,
n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Taking an index
k
such that
\ubdim G^{(k)}>\pdim F-\tfrac{d}{2n}\varepsilon
, we have by (
5
) a string
1\leq i_{1}^{\prime}<\cdots<i_{n}^{\prime}\leq d
such that

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{n}{d}\pdim F-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle<\frac{n}{d}\ubdim G^{(k)}\leq\ubdim\mathbf{p}_{i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime}}\big(G^{(k)}\big)

We note that for every
k
,

\displaystyle G^{(k)}\subset\bigcap_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq d}\mathbf{p}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}^{-1}\!\Big(F_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}^{(j_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}})}\Big)

for some collection of
j_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}
. Thus for our given string,
G^{(k)}\subset\mathbf{p}_{i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime}}^{-1}(F_{i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime}}^{(j)})
for some
j
. Therefore,
\mathbf{p}_{i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime}}\big(G^{(k)}\big)\subseteq F_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}^{(j)}
, which implies

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{n}{d}\pdim F-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle<\ubdim\mathbf{p}_{i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime}}\big(G^{(k)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{1\leq j<\infty}\ubdim F_{i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime}}^{(j)}<\pdim\mathbf{p}_{i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime}}(F)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

This string
i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime}
depends on
\varepsilon
, but since there are only
\binom{d}{n}
such strings, there is at least one string for which (
10
) holds for arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
; hence, this concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
– The polyhedral case

Let
\|\cdot\|_{*}
be a norm defined by a collection
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
of vectors so that

\displaystyle\|x\|_{*}:=\max\,\{|\langle x,v_{i}\rangle|\}_{i=1}^{M}.

We note that we must have
M\geq d
. For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the cone

\displaystyle C(x,v):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}~:~\|x-y\|_{*}=|\langle x-y,v\rangle|\right\}.

Obviously, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\bigcup_{j=1}^{M}C(x,v_{i})=\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In addition, let
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a countable set, so that

\displaystyle\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}C(z_{j},v_{i})=\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{z_{j_{1}},\dots,z_{j_{M}}\}\\
\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{M}}\}\end{subarray}}\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{M}C(z_{j_{\ell}},v_{i_{\ell}})

where in the union we allow
j_{s}=j_{t}
and
i_{s}=i_{t}
for
s\neq t
. For
\alpha>0
and a countable set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\displaystyle k(E,G,\alpha)

\displaystyle:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{z_{j_{1}},\dots,z_{j_{M}}\}\subset G\\
\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{M}}\}\end{subarray}}\bigg\{\dim(\Span(\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{M}}\}))~:~

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\pdim\!\Big(\textstyle E\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{M}C(z_{j_{\ell}},v_{i_{\ell}})\Big)\geq\pdim E-\alpha\bigg\}.

This is the largest number of cones based at points of
G
that “see” a large (relative to
\alpha
) portion of
E
through independent faces of the norm polyhedra
\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\!:\|y-z_{j}\|_{*}=1\}
.

Lemma 4.1.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\alpha>0
, and let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be countable. Then there exist
k\leq k(E,G,\alpha)
and
F\subseteq E
with
\pdim E=\pdim F
such that the following hold:

1.

If
F\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{M}C(z_{\ell},v_{i_{\ell}})\neq\varnothing
for any
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{M}\}\subset G
, then

(11)

\displaystyle\dim(\Span\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{M}}\})\leq k.

2.

There exist
\{z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k}\}\subset G
and
\{v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}\}\subseteq\{v_{1},\dots,v_{M}\}
such that

\displaystyle\dim(\Span\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}\})=k

and

\displaystyle\pdim\left(F\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{k}C(z_{j_{\ell}},v_{i_{\ell}})\right)\geq\pdim E-\alpha.

Proof.

Let
s=\pdim E
. The sequence
k(E,G,2^{-m}\alpha)
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is a monotonically decreasing sequence of integers between 1 and
d
, so pigeonhole principle implies that there exists
m\geq 1
such that

k(E,G,\alpha)\geq k\big(E,G,2^{-(m+1)}\alpha\big)=k(E,G,2^{-m}\alpha)=:k.

For each
\bar{z}\in G^{M}=G\times G\times\cdots\times G
and
\bar{v}\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{M}\}^{M}
, define

\displaystyle E_{\bar{z},\bar{v}}:=E\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{M}C(z_{j_{\ell}},v_{i_{\ell}}).

If
\dim(\Span\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{M}}\})>k
, then

\displaystyle\pdim(E_{\bar{z},\bar{v}})\leq s-2^{-m}\alpha

for all
\bar{z}\in G^{k}
. If we let
E_{\bar{v}}:=\bigcup_{\bar{z}\in G^{M}}E_{\bar{z},\bar{v}}
, then
\pdim E_{\bar{v}}\leq s-2^{-m}\alpha
. Therefore, if
V_{k}:=\{\bar{v}\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{M}\}^{M}~:~\dim(\Span\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{M}}\})>k\}
then

\displaystyle F:=E\setminus\bigcup_{\bar{v}\in V_{k}}E_{\bar{v}}

has
\pdim F=\pdim E
and the desired condition holds.

For the second part of the statement, if
k\big(E,G,2^{-(m+1)}\alpha\big)=k
, then by definition there exist
\{z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k}\}\subset G
and
\{v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}\}\subseteq\{v_{1},\dots,v_{M}\}
such that
\dim(\Span\{v_{i_{1}},

\dots,v_{i_{k}}\})=k
and

\displaystyle\pdim\left(E\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{M}C(z_{j_{\ell}},v_{i_{\ell}})\right)\geq\pdim E-2^{-(m+1)}\alpha.

Finally,
\pdim E_{\bar{v}}\leq s-2^{-m}\alpha
implies

\pdim\left(F\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{M}C(z_{j_{\ell}},v_{i_{\ell}})\right)\geq\pdim E-2^{-(m+1)}\alpha\geq\pdim E-\alpha.\qed

Proposition 4.2.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\alpha>0
, and
G\subset E
be a countable, dense subset of
E
. Then there exists
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\}\subset G
,
\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{k}\}\subset\{v_{1},\dots,v_{M}\}
, and
F\subset E
, such that
\pdim F\geq\pdim E-\alpha
, and

\displaystyle F\subset\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{k}C(z_{\ell},w_{\ell}).

Moreover,
V:=\Span\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}\in G(d,k)
and for each

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{*}=\|P_{V}(x-y)\|_{*}.

Proof.

Lemma
4.1
implies that there exist an integer
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,
\{z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k}\}\subset G,

\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{k}\}\subset\{v_{1},\dots,v_{M}\}
, and
F_{0}\subset E
such that
\pdim F_{0}=\pdim E
,

\displaystyle\dim\big(\Span\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{k}\}\big)=k

and

\displaystyle\pdim\!\left(F_{0}\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{k}C(z_{j_{\ell}},w_{\ell})\right)\geq\pdim E-\alpha.

Moreover, inequality (
11
) implies that if
F_{0}\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{M}C(z_{\ell},w_{\ell})\neq\varnothing
for any
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{M}\}\subset G
and
(v_{j_{1}},\dots,v_{j_{M}})\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{M}\}^{M}
, then

\displaystyle\dim\big(\Span\{v_{j_{1}},\dots,v_{j_{M}}\}\big)\leq k.

Let
F:=F_{0}\cap\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{k}C(z_{j_{\ell}},w_{\ell})
and
V:=\Span\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}
. If
x\in F
,
z\in G
, and
v\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{M}\}
satisfy

then

\displaystyle\dim\big(\Span\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k},v\}\big)\leq k,

which implies
v\in\Span\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}
. This further implies that for any
x\in F
and
z\in G
, there is a

such that

\displaystyle\|P_{V}(z-x)\|_{*}\leq\|z-x\|_{*}

\displaystyle=|\langle z-x,v\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle P_{V}(z-x),v\rangle+\langle P_{V}^{\perp}(z-x),v\rangle|=|\langle P_{V}(z-x),v\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{V}(z-x)\|_{*}.

Since
G
is dense in
E
,
G
is dense in
F
and
\|\cdot\|
and
P_{V}
are continuous, we can conclude that for every
x,y\in F
,

\|P_{V}(y-x)\|_{*}=\|y-x\|_{*}.\qed

We are now ready to prove Theorem

in the case of polyhedral norms.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
in the polyhedral case.

For

,
\alpha>0
, and
G\subset E

\pdim E=s
and with

,

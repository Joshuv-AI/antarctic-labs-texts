The effect of digital obesity and phubbing level on life satisfaction: latent profile analysis
Authors: Muhammet Ali Aydin, Metin Yıldız, Mübin Kiyici, Mehmet Yildiz, Ali Kirksekiz, Şeyda Kızıloğlu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn this study, the effects of individuals’ digital obesity and phubbing behaviors on their life satisfaction were investigated by latent profile analysis (LPA) method. LPA is a statistical technique used to identify unobserved subgroups within a population based on individuals’ responses to various observed variables.MethodsThe present study was conducted in a correlational cross-sectional descriptive design between November 2023- January 2024. Digital obesity scale, phubbing scale, life satisfaction scale were used in this study.ResultsAs a result of LPA, Class 1 (Low Digital Addicts) has the lowest arithmetic mean in all indicators. When life satisfaction was analyzed on the basis of the classes, it was found out that Class 2 (High Digital Addicts), which was collected in the group with high levels of digital obesity and phubbing, had lower life satisfaction. Considering the demographic levels of individuals according to class level, it was concluded that high digital addiction was more common among individuals with secondary and postgraduate education.ConclusionsIn the present study, two classes were found as a result of LPA. In the analysis, Class 1(Low Digital Addicts) was found to have the lowest arithmetic mean in all indicators. On the other hand, when life satisfaction was analyzed according to the classes, it was detected that Class 2 (High Digital Addicts) life satisfaction was lower in the group with high levels of digital obesity and phubbing. In the study, the life satisfaction of class 1, which is characterized as low digital addicts, was found to be higher. Longitudinal studies on digital addictions affecting life satisfaction are recommended.Keywords: Digital obesity, Life satisfaction, Nursing, Phubbing

Introduction

It is known that digital media and online devices, which are increasingly used in all areas of daily life, have some advantages in terms of individual and social communication, time and space [1, 2]. Nevertheless, it can be argued that these advantages also bring along an addiction based on the overuse of these media and devices. The effective use of the internet environment and digital devices has led to the emergence of a new type of addiction, which is: “digital addiction”. Many studies in the field reveal that the number of digital obese people has reached alarming levels and digital obesity negatively affects physical, social, psychological, emotional and cognitive development of individuals and reduces their life satisfaction [2, 3].

Life satisfaction is defined as the general judgments and evaluations that an individual has about his/her own life [4]. The basis of this satisfaction is that individuals enter into emotional and psychological states by being affected by the social relations environment in which they are in the cognitive existence process and define themselves as happy [5]. Considering today’s social relations, we encounter virtual relations and the relations carried out through social media instead of face-to-face relations. The increase in such relationships reduces life satisfaction [6]. In addition to digital obesity and phubbing, factors such as internet addiction and social media addiction also affect life satisfaction [7]. Another element that negatively affects life satisfaction is the concept of “phubbing” [7, 8]. Phubbing is generally perceived as disrespectful [9], rude and socially inappropriate behavior [10]. Today, phubbing behavior can be encountered almost everywhere [11]. Surprisingly, people continue phubbing in social settings despite being aware of the negative consequences of phubbing [12]. Young people admit that they often resort to phubbing in spite of the fact that they see such behavior as annoying and disrespectful [13]. Many people agree that phubbing is unacceptable; however, this awareness does not prevent this behavior from being excessive. The research reveals that phubbing is common among people of all ages. According to a recent report published by the Pew Research Center, some young people have complained that their parents are absorbed in their cell phones during face-to-face communication [14].

The space in which phubbing takes place is wide. It can happen while sitting with others in a café, in a classroom, symposium or meeting, at home with guests, driving, traveling, spending time with children, walking, and shopping. As a result, phubbing can damage social and professional relationships and push people into life-threatening situations. When phubbing occurs, people report that they are more distracted and enjoy communication less [15]. Hence it is significant for our study to include the mediating role of phubbing while examining the effect of digital obesity on life satisfaction.

Latent profile analysis is a powerful management method for uncovering hidden structures in a dataset. This analysis divides a heterogeneous region into homogeneous subgroups, revealing the unique characteristics and output of each subgroup [16]. This method offers the possibility to understand and explain complex relationships, develop predictable sub-discrete descriptions and ultimately more effective interventions. Moreover, this analysis provides a model for organizing distributions to identify complex relationships and sub-features. Another strength of latent profile analysis is the ability to understand the relationships and interactions between different variables, which can help with distributions of growth and ratios between variables, especially complex, psychological or health-related distributions. Latent profile analysis can also reveal hidden structures and patterns in datasets, offering the possibility of learning about the overall characteristics of the partition and the sub partition with unique features [16, 17].

There is no latent profile analysis study in the literature that examines the effect of digital obesity or phubbing level on life satisfaction. For this reason, it is anticipated that this study will contribute to the literature and shed light on other studies.

Aim

The present study was carried out to determine the effect of digital obesity and phubbing level on life satisfaction and to create latent profiles.

The Research question of the study

Do individuals have latent profiles according to digital obesity and phubbing level?

The hypothesis of the study

H1:

There is a significant difference between latent profiles and life satisfaction level.

H2:

There is a significant difference between latent profiles and demographic features.

Method

Research type

The present study was conducted in a correlational cross-sectional descriptive design between November 1, 2023 and January 30, 2024.

Population and sample

The study was conducted with 801 adults living in a city located in Eastern Turkey. The minimum number of individuals to be included in the sample of the study was calculated as 384 using the formula of unknown population (n = t 2.p.q/d 2) with 95% confidence interval (d = 0.05), t = 1.96, p = 0.5, q = 0.5. During the data collection process, the responses from the participants were accepted for inclusion in the data analysis according to the eligibility criteria and the research was completed with 801 participants. In the post hoc power analysis performed with the G*Power 3.1 statistical package program in line with the results obtained from 801 participants, the power of our study was calculated as 99% with a medium effect size at 95% confidence level [18].

Including criteria

All adults between the ages of 18–65 who volunteered to participate in the study and did not have a psychiatric illness that would prevent them from completing the questionnaire were included in the study.

Excluding criteria

The data of the researchers who filled out the data collection forms incompletely were excluded.

Data collection instruments

The data were collected using the Personal Information Form developed by the researchers, the Digital Obesity Scale, the Phubbing Scale and the Life Satisfaction Scale.

Personal information form

This form included questions on gender, age, the type of family, education level and family income.

Digital obesity scale

The scale was developed by Koçoğlu et al. (2022) to determine the level of digital obesity of individuals [2]. Consisting of 33 items, the scale is designed in 5-point Likert type as “Strongly Disagree, Disagree, Undecided, Agree, Strongly Agree”. As the score obtained from the scale increases, the level of digital obesity increases and as the score decreases, the level of digital obesity decreases. Koçoğlu et al. (2022) found the Cronbach alpha value of the scale to be 0.93 [2] while the Cronbach’s alpha value was found to be 0.93 in our study.

Life satisfaction scale (LSS)

LSS was developed by Diener et al. (1985) to determine the life satisfaction levels of individuals [19]. The scale was adapted to Turkish by Dağlı and Baysal [20]. The Turkish version of LSS is a one-dimensional and 5-point Likert-type. It has no reverse-coded item. The Cronbach alpha coefficient of the Turkish version of the scale is 0.88. In our study, the Cronbach alpha value was found to be 0.79.

Phubbing scale

The present scale was developed to measure the phubbing level of individuals by Chotpitayasunondh and Douglas (2018) [21]. Consisting of 15 items, the scale is evaluated on a seven-point Likert scale (1: Never, 7: Always). The scale was adapted into Turkish by Ergün et al. (2020) [22]. A low score indicates a low level of phubbing and a high score indicates a high level of phubbing. The Cronbach alpha values of the study were found to be 0.91 [22]. In our study, Cronbach alpha values were found to be 0 0.95.

Data collection process

The research data were collected face-to-face. During the data collection process, no personal data was requested from the participants. The information obtained from the participants in accordance with the principles of data confidentiality was obtained by obtaining informed consent from each participant. The participants were asked to approve this form before starting the study.

Data analysis

Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS 25 and R software. Necessary normality tests were performed with kurtosis and skewness − 1.5 to + 1.5 [23]. For all analyses, p < 0.05 was set as the significance level.

This method creates homogeneous subgroups based on individuals’ responses on different variables. Each group is identical to each other in terms of a particular characteristic or combination of characteristics, while differing significantly from other groups. Results

Latent Profile Analysis (LPA) is a statistical technique detecting different subgroups within a population by analyzing patterns of responses to various observed variables [24]. Spurk et al. (2020) argue that LPA is an approach which focuses on individuals and aims to reveal different subgroups or profiles within a more significant population [25]. LPA is a method that focuses on identifying distinctive subgroups within a larger population. LPA has been found valuable in a variety of fields including occupational behavior research, exercise psychology, workplace bullying, and problematic internet use among youth [25–27]. LPA is a person-centered approach to identify hidden subgroups in a larger population. It has also been used in a variety of research areas, including child development, pregnancy, neurodevelopment, and patterns of grief experience in older adults [28, 29]. LPA is a statistical technique that determines subgroups in a population by analyzing patterns of responses to observed variables. It has been used in various fields to better understand complex phenomena [30, 31].

By identifying these latent profiles, researchers can gain deeper insight into complex phenomena such as motivation, emotional labor strategies, and problematic online gaming [24, 26]. One of the key strengths of LPA is its methodological flexibility, which allows it to identify latent subgroups based on continuous indicators. This means that LPA can identify subgroups that may not be immediately obvious because it analyzes response patterns rather than just simple groupings. By using this approach, LPA can reveal complex patterns in the data, which can provide valuable insights into a variety of phenomena. This flexibility also means that LPA can be applied in fields ranging from psychology to medicine to identify subgroups that may have different features or outcomes. Overall, the methodological flexibility of LPA makes it a powerful tool for uncovering and understanding complex patterns in data [26, 32]. LPA provides a deeper understanding of population heterogeneity and can lead to more tailored interventions and policies. By identifying complex patterns in data, LPA provides valuable insights in a variety of fields. Its potential benefits are too great to ignore and its application should be further explored to improve outcomes [33]. Mclust library [34] was used for LPA analysis and Tidylpa library [35] was used to access fit indices during the reporting phase.

Discussion

In this study, the effects of digital obesity and phubbing behaviors on individuals’ life satisfaction were examined by latent profile analysis method and the findings of the present study were discussed together with the available literature in the field.

In our study, a significant relationship has been detected between digital obesity and life satisfaction. As the level of digital obesity increases, life satisfaction decreases. Although there is no other study in the literature investigating the relationship between digital obesity and life satisfaction, there are some studies about social media addiction, digital game addiction and internet addiction, and their effects on life satisfaction. In the study conducted by Dilsiz and Kandemir (2020), it was determined that life satisfaction had a significant effect on the prediction of social media addiction [39]. In the study, it was also uncovered that social media addiction decreased as life satisfaction increased [39].

Social media addiction is characterized by compulsive use that disrupts daily life and leads to negative consequences such as reduced mental health and academic performance [40]. A study involving university students found that social media addiction has a negative correlation with mental health, suggesting that individuals who are more addicted to social media may experience lower life satisfaction due to stress and anxiety associated with their compulsive behavior [40]. In addition, Ibrahim et al. (2022) found a negative relationship in the study on the relationship between digital game addiction and family life satisfaction [41].

Saquib et al. found that adolescents suffering from video game addiction exhibit lower self-esteem, poorer self-control, and diminished psychosocial quality of life compared to their non-addicted peers [42]. This aligns with findings from Alrahili, which highlighted a correlation between video game addiction and mental health issues such as anxiety and depression among children and adolescents [43]. The characteristics of video games themselves can exacerbate addiction. Hull et al. identified social features, time distortion, and happiness as significant predictors of gaming addiction, suggesting that the immersive and social nature of many games can lead to excessive engagement [44].

Considering the studies that addressed the relationship between internet addiction and life satisfaction by conducting correlational analysis, it was found out that there were more studies revealing a strong and significant relationship between the two variables, especially in the negative direction. The review of the related literature reveals that life satisfaction decreases as internet addiction increases [45–49].

Phubbing, a term that describes the act of ignoring someone in favor of one’s smartphone, has been increasingly recognized as a significant factor influencing life satisfaction across various demographics. The relationship between phubbing and life satisfaction is complex, often mediated by factors such as relationship satisfaction, mental well-being, and social interactions. Research indicates that phubbing negatively impacts life satisfaction. Wang and Zhao found that high levels of partner phubbing correlate with reduced marital satisfaction, which subsequently affects overall life satisfaction [50]. The detrimental effects of phubbing extend beyond romantic relationships. Parental phubbing has been shown to correlate with lower life satisfaction in adolescents, as it fosters feelings of neglect and rejection [51]. Liu et al. further elucidate this by demonstrating that parental phubbing is associated with decreased relationship satisfaction among teens, which in turn negatively impacts their life satisfaction [52]. Research by Yam indicates that relationship satisfaction mediates the relationship between partner phubbing and life satisfaction, implying that the quality of interpersonal relationships is a critical factor in mitigating the negative effects of phubbing [53]. Additionally, studies have shown that phubbing can lead to increased feelings of loneliness and lower self-esteem, which are both associated with reduced life satisfaction [54, 55]. This is further supported by Ivanova et al., who found that phubbing indirectly increases depressive symptoms by decreasing relationship satisfaction and overall life satisfaction [56].

Digitalization, which has gained importance today, affects every age group and this digitalization can cause addictions in society and this situation has been proven by latent profile analysis that it can affect the life style of individuals.

Conclusion

In the present study, two classes were found as a result of LPA. In the analysis, Class 1(Low Digital Addicts) was found to have the lowest arithmetic mean in all indicators. On the other hand, when life satisfaction was analyzed according to the classes, it was detected that Class 2 Class 2 (High Digital Addicts) life satisfaction was lower in the group with high levels of digital obesity and phubbing. In the study, the life satisfaction of class 1, which is characterized as low digital addicts, was found to be higher. Longitudinal studies on digital addictions affecting life satisfaction are recommended.

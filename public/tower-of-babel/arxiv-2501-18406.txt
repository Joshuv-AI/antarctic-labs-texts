On Dirac and Motzkin problem in discrete geometry Journal: Graphs and Combinatorics
Jan Florek

Abstract
Dirac and Motzkin conjectured that any set 𝒳 \mathcal{X} of n n non-collinear points in the plane has an element incident with at least ⌈ n 2 ⌉ \lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil lines spanned by 𝒳 \mathcal{X} . In this paper we prove that any set 𝒳 \mathcal{X} of n n non-collinear points in the plane, distributed on three lines passing through a common point, has an element incident with at least ⌈ n 2 ⌉ \lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil lines spanned by 𝒳 \mathcal{X} .

Introduction

We consider a set
\mathcal{X}

n

. Some counterexamples were shown for small values of

(pp. 24–25) (see also Grünbaum
grunbaum7
). An infinite family of counterexamples was given by Felsner

(p. 313). He constructed a set

of

(

) non-collinear points in the plane for which
t(\mathcal{X})<\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Akiyama, Ito, Kobayashi and Nakamura

n

In this paper we prove Theorem 1: any set
\mathcal{X}
of
n
non-collinear points in the plane, distributed on three lines passing through a common point, has an element incident with at least
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
lines spanned by
\mathcal{X}
.

The construction of the set
\mathcal{X}
in the projective plane given in Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 is clear due to the following lemma (see Grünbaum
grunbaum6
and Akiyama, Ito, Kobayashi and Nakamura

\mathcal{X}
be a finite set of points in the projective plane such that
\mathcal{X}
has points at infinity, and let
\mathcal{L}
be the set of lines spanned by
\mathcal{X}
. Then the arrangements

can be transformed to an arrangement in the Euclidean plane.

The “weak Dirac conjecture” proved by Beck
beck2
and independently by Szemerédi and Trotter

states that there is a constant
c>0
such that in every non-collinear set
\mathcal{X}
of
n
points in the plane some element is incident with at least
cn
lines spanned by
\mathcal{X}
. Brass, Moser and Pach
brass3
(p. 313) proposed the following “strong Dirac conjecture”: there is a constant
c>0
such that any set
\mathcal{X}
of
n
points in the plane, not all on a line, has an element which lies on at least
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-c
lines spanned by
\mathcal{X}
. Klee and Wagon

(p. 29) conjectured that for any set
\mathcal{X}
of
n
non-collinear points in the plane, we have
t(\mathcal{X})\geqslant\lceil\frac{n}{3}\rceil
.

Main result

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a set of
n
non-collinear points in the plane. A line passing through exactly two points of
\mathcal{X}
is called an
ordinary line
. A point of
\mathcal{X}
incident with at least
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
lines spanned by
\mathcal{X}
is called an
ordinary point
(Motzkin
(
motzkin9
, p. 452)
). Let
(A,B)
denote an open segment of a line with end-vertices
A
and
B
.

Theorem 2.1

Any set
\mathcal{X}
of
n
non-collinear points in the plane distributed on three lines passing through a point
A

(
A
is not required to be in the set
\mathcal{X}
)
has an ordinary point.

Proof

Let
p
,
q
,
r

p_{1}
,
p_{2}\subset p
(

,
q_{2}\subset q
and
r_{1}
,
r_{2}\subset r
) are open half lines originating at the point

. We can assume that

,
q_{1}
,
r_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
q_{2}
,
r_{2}

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

\mathcal{X}
, different from

, which is closest to

. The points

and

(

and
Q_{2}
,
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, respectively) does not exist simultaneously.

Suppose that there are three non-successive in pairs half lines (say

) such that

. Notice that

and

are lying on different sides of the line
q
. Similarly,

(

, except for

,

,

P_{1}
and

. Hence, if sets

, respectively) is ordinary. By analogy, if

\mathcal{X}
and

)

,

Subclinical cardiac dysfunction in transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia children, an experience from central India: A comparative cross-sectional study
Authors: Rajkumar Motiram Meshram, Sagar Chopde, Ashma Rashid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Cardiac complications are the leading cause of mortality in children with transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia (TDT). Tissue Doppler imaging (TDI) is a readily available, noninvasive technique used to detect subclinical cardiac dysfunction by deriving the Tei index.Objective:The objective of this study was to determine subclinical cardiac dysfunction in children with TDT and correlate findings with serum ferritin levels.Materials and Methods:This comparative cross-sectional study was conducted at a tertiary care institute in Central India. The study included 50 children with TDT (aged 2–18 years) as cases and 50 age-and sex-matched outpatient children with minor illnesses as controls, selected via simple random sampling over 1 year. Demographic, clinical, transfusion, and anthropometric data were collected. Hematological and biochemical investigations, along with electrocardiography, were performed. Conventional two dimensional echocardiography and TDI were conducted using an ALPINION 5 color Doppler system. M-mode, B-mode, and Doppler parameters were measured per American Society of Echocardiography guidelines. Statistical analysis utilized Chi-square, independent t-tests, and Pearson correlation coefficients (P < 0.05 was considered significant).Results:Demographic variables were comparable between groups. However, the body mass index was significantly lower, and systolic blood pressure was significantly higher in cases than in controls. While 36% of cases were sero-reactive to Hepatitis C, all participants were negative for hepatitis B surface antigen and human immunodeficiency virus. Hemoglobin was significantly lower, whereas serum ferritin and cardiothoracic ratios were significantly higher in cases. Conventional echocardiography revealed significantly larger left ventricular internal diameter at end-diastole, mitral annular plane systolic excursion, and left atrial diameters in cases. Notably, both left and right ventricular Tei indices were significantly higher in cases compared to controls (P < 0.0001). Serum ferritin (r = 0.32) and systolic blood pressure (r = 0.29) had significant positive correlation with left ventricular Tei index.Conclusion:Children with TDT exhibit latent systolic and diastolic dysfunction as evidenced by TDI. This highlights the critical importance of identifying asymptomatic cardiac dysfunction in children and adolescents to prevent cardiac-related mortality.

INTRODUCTION

Thalassemia is the most common monogenic hemoglobin disorder, characterized by quantitative defect in the β-globin chain that leads to ineffective erythropoiesis and severe anemia.[1] Nearly 1%–1.5% of the global population are carriers of the β-thalassemia gene;[2] however, the age-standardized prevalence rates and age-standardized incidence rates are 18.28 and 1.93 per 100,000 persons, respectively, with the highest mortality found in Southeast Asia.[3] In India, the pooled prevalence of β-thalassemia carrier is 8.23%, with a higher mortality than the general population of a similar age.[456]

Approximately 25% of children with β-thalassemia have cardiac iron overload due to increased iron absorption and chronic blood transfusion.[7] Ferroptosis, disturbance in cardiac calcium regulation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, polymorphisms, or mutation in specific genes are responsible for myocardial damage in iron overload.[7] The outcome of transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia (TDT) is grave and usually driven by cardiovascular involvement, such as pulmonary hypertension, pericarditis, cardiomyopathy, atrial fibrillation, and heart failure.[789] Therefore, early identification and monitoring are vitally important for patient management.

Cardiac T2* magnetic resonance imaging is the best noninvasive modality for monitoring cardiac iron storage but unfortunately, it is costly and unavailable in resource-limited setting. Electrocardiography (ECG) and echocardiography are easily available tool for assessment of electrical abnormalities of heart and cardiac dysfunction in symptomatic disease, but they are ineffective in detecting preclinical iron overload.[10] However, more advanced echocardiography methods, such as two dimensional (2D) or three-dimensional (3D) speckle tracing, are now available to detect early abnormalities without any clinical relevance.[11]

Although conventional echocardiography has been used to analyse morphology and myocardial function, this modality faces certain limitations, such as high cardiac output states and unique pattern of left ventricular geometry. Tissue Doppler imaging (TDI) is an easier noninvasive technique to detect both subclinical systolic and diastolic performance by calculating the myocardial performance index (MPI), or Tei index.[1213] Tei index is a simple, reproducible measure of cardiac function that is independent of arterial pressure, heart rate, ventricular geometry, atrioventricular valvular regurgitation, and pre and after load; hence it is used as a prognostic index in asymptomatic cardiac failure.[12] Therefore, this study was planned to evaluate cardiac function in asymptomatic transfusion dependent β-thalassemic children and correlate these finding with iron overload.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design, setting and duration

This cross-sectional comparative study was conducted among children aged 2–18 years with transfusion dependent beta-thalassemia at the pediatric day-care center of a tertiary care teaching institute in central India. This study was conducted over a 1-year period from March 2025 to February 2026.

Sample size and participants

A sample size of 96 was calculated based on the following assumption: 5% of unexposed individuals with the outcome, 30% of exposed individuals, and an odds ratio of 8.1, with a two-sided significance level (1-alpha) of 90% and power (1-beta) of 95%. Ultimately, 100 participants (50 cases and 50 controls) were recruited using a consecutive sampling method.

Inclusion criteria of cases

Children of either sex, aged 2–18 years, with TDT (diagnosed by hemoglobin electrophoresis) who had received a minimum of 20 transfusions.

Exclusion criteria (cases)

Children with congenital or acquired heart disease, poor compliance with transfusion therapy, heart failure, critical illness, chronic lung disease, or protein-losing enteropathy. Children with other multitransfused hemoglobinopathies (e.g., sickle cell anemia, hemophilia) were also excluded.

Selection of controls

Age- and sex-matched children with minor illnesses attending the outpatient department at the same center during the study period.

Ethical consideration

The study followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (No. 4234/EC/Pharmac/GMC/NGP dated May 30, 2024). Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or legal guardians in their vernacular language prior to enrolment.

Data collection

Demographic, clinical, and anthropometric data were collected at enrolment using a specially designed proforma. For cases, family history, age at first transfusion, total number of transfusions, and chelation therapy history were recorded. All participants underwent routine hematological and biochemical investigations (Beckman Coulter AU5800) and serum ferritin estimation via enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.

Electrocardiography

A 12-lead ECG was performed using a CARDIART 9108 D machine (BPL Medical Technologies Pvt Ltd, India) in a resting state. ECGs were read independently by two observers; the mean of their measurements was used. Disparities were resolved by a senior faculty member.

Two-dimensional echocardiography and tissue Doppler imaging

After resting for 10–30 min in the supine position and with using 3.75 MHz or 5MHz probe, 2D-echocardiography and TDI were performed on 5-color Doppler ultrasound system named ALPINION (model no. E-CUBE 8, manufacture by ALPINION Medical systems Co., Ltd. South Korea and marketed in India by BPL Medical Technologies) by two independent cardiologists who were unaware about cases or controls. Conventional Mmode, Bmode, and Doppler parameters were measured according to the American Society of Echocardiography guidelines.[14] Complete echocardiographic examination was performed to exclude the possibility of congenital heart disease with emphasis on left ventricle dimension, global function, and left ventricular internal dimensions from the standard transthoracic window.

From the standard transthoracic window, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was first noted by eye balling method in parasternal short axis view and then confirmed by the Simpsons biplane method; in which left ventricular end-diastolic volume and left ventricular end-systolic volume were calculated by using apical 4 chamber and apical 2 chamber views. Contraction of left and right ventricle was measured with mitral annular plane systolic excursion (MAPSE) and tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE), respectively, which measure the descent of the ventricular base towards the relatively fixed apex and were done in M mode of echocardiography. TAPSE and MAPSE were used to assess the systolic function of the right and left ventricle, respectively.

By using the apical window, a sample volume was placed in the left ventricular inflow tract at the level of the mitral valve leaflet tips. Early (E’), late (A’) diastolic velocities, and E’/A’ ratio and mitral valve E/E’ ratio were calculated. The consecutive measurements were averaged for each patient. Peak velocities of early (E) and late (A) diastolic filling were derived, and the ratio of them was calculated to see the diastolic dysfunction of the left ventricle. Ejection time (ET) of the left ventricle was measured in longaxis view at the opening of the aortic valve in Mmode echocardiography. Isovolumic contraction time (IVCT) and isovolumic relaxation time (IVRT) intervals were measured by simultaneously tracking the flow into the mitral inflow and left ventricular outflow tract using Doppler echocardiography. Left ventricular systolic function was assessed by measuring the ejection fraction of the left ventricle by the methods already mentioned before. Three consecutive measurements were averaged for each value and for each patient. MPI, also termed as “Tei index,” was calculated by dividing the sum of IVRT and IVCT by ET.

Right ventricular MPI = (IVCT + IVRT)/right ventricular ejection time

Left ventricular MPI = (IVCT + IVRT)/left ventricular ejection time.

The Tei inde × 0.39 ± 0.05 was considered a normal value for this study.[15] Increased MPI indicates the presence of cardiac dysfunction. Evaluation of right and left ventricular function was done by MPI.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using STATA version 14.0 [Stata Corp., LLC, College Station, Texas, USA]. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (range) for nonnormally distributed data. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Groups were compared using the Chi-square test (or Fisher’s exact test for small numbers) for categorical data and the independent t-test for continuous data. Pearson’s correlation (r) was used to evaluate the relationship between serum ferritin, other variables, and TDI findings. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Demographic characteristics (age, sex, and socioeconomic status) were comparable between groups. In the case group, 48% were aged 5–10 years and 40% were aged 10–18 years; the male-to-female ratio was 1:1. Cases had a significantly lower body mass index (BMI) and significantly higher systolic blood pressure compared to controls. Among thalassemic children, the median age at first transfusion was 8 months, 74% required monthly transfusions, and the mean total number of transfusions was 115.4 ± 48.6. DISCUSSION

The present study aimed to evaluate cardiac dysfunction in asymptomatic pediatric and adolescent patients with transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia major using conventional echocardiography and TDI. Beta-thalassemia is the most common hemoglobinopathy in the Indian subcontinent and is associated with high mortality rates in South Asia. The prevalence of β-thalassemia carriers in the Indian population is significantly higher (8.23%) than the global prevalence (1%–1.5%).[4] Furthermore, the number of new cases is on an upward trend due to a lack of mandatory premarital screening and high rates of consanguinity.

Growth retardation in thalassemic children often results from endocrinopathies, nutritional deficiencies, chronic anemia, and chronic hypoxia. Similar to the observations of Altin et al.,[16] we noted a lower BMI in thalassemic children compared to controls. Endothelial dysfunction – driven by chronic hemolysis, oxidative stress, vascular aging, arterial stiffness, and atherosclerosis – may affect not only myocardial deformation but also blood pressure.[91718] We observed significantly higher systolic blood pressure in thalassemic children compared to controls, a finding that aligns with previous research.

Despite recent advances in chelation therapy, cardiac complications remain the leading cause of death in children with β-thalassemia. Unbound labile iron is highly cardiotoxic; it damages cardiomyocytes by impairing mitochondrial metabolism, modulating genetics, and interacting with ion channels, in addition to causing oxidative stress injury.[10] The extent of cardiac involvement depends on the iron load per myocardial fibril and the number of fibrils affected. In the early stages of the disease, iron deposition occurs mainly in the ventricles and ventricular septum, progressively extending to the atria and finally the conduction system.

In our study, conventional echocardiography revealed significant differences between cases and controls regarding LVIDD and LA diameter, indicating LV and LA dilatation. These structural changes are likely a result of chronic anemia and myocardial iron overload, which is consistent with the findings of other investigators.[16192021] Similarly, Salama and colleagues found significant ventricular dilatation in children with thalassemia major compared to those with thalassemia intermedia.[22] Interestingly, we found that LVEF was comparable between both groups. This suggests preserved systolic function as assessed by conventional echocardiography. Our findings mirror those of other researchers who observed preserved left ventricular systolic function despite ventricular dilatation, with LVEF and fractional shortening remaining comparable to control groups.[161920] However, some investigators have reported significantly lower LVEF in thalassemic children compared to healthy age- and sex-matched controls.[23]

Although echocardiography is a popular noninvasive technique for capturing structural and functional changes, it has limitations in detecting early-stage, subtle cardiac dysfunction. TDI is a relatively newer modality that allows for quantitative, reproducible measurement of both systolic and diastolic dysfunction, even in asymptomatic patients.[11121324] In our study, the LV Tei index (1.1 ± 0.4) and RV Tei index (0.93 ± 0.4) were significantly higher in thalassemic children compared to controls (P < 0.0001). This impairment of the Tei index in children with otherwise normal conventional parameters (particularly LVEF) reinforces the idea that TDI is a sensitive modality for detecting early cardiac dysfunction. This is particularly valuable in resource-limited settings where cardiac MRI is either unavailable or financially prohibitive. Our results align with Suman et al.,[25] who reported a high mean MPI (0.60 ± 0.14) among Indian children with β-thalassemia major, and Sahin et al.,[26] who concluded that MPI was significantly higher in 55 asymptomatic thalassemic patients. Several other researchers[162728] have also noted that TDI is superior to conventional echocardiography for diagnosing subclinical dysfunction.

While cardiac T2* MRI is the gold standard for detecting cardiac siderosis, serum ferritin measurement remains a widely accepted biochemical assay for assessing iron overload in resource-limited settings. In the present study, we found a significant positive correlation between serum ferritin and systolic blood pressure with the LV Tei index. This is supported by Esfahani et al.,[29] who noted a significant positive correlation with LV MPI, and Suman et al.,[25] who observed a worsening MPI as serum ferritin levels increased. Other researchers have also noted a highly significant positive correlation between serum ferritin levels and the E/e’ ratio.[27]

These findings highlight the importance of early identification of asymptomatic cardiac dysfunction using TDI in children and adolescents with transfusion dependent beta thalassemia to reduce future cardiac morbidity and mortality.

Our study has a few limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small, and advanced modalities like speckle tracking were unavailable. Second, due to financial constraints, cardiac T2* MRI – the definitive diagnostic marker for cardiac iron overload as well as cardiac biomarkers – was beyond the scope of this study. Third, long-term follow-up cardiac assessment and co-relation with ferritin threshold was not possible due to the design of study.

CONCLUSION

Subclinical cardiac dysfunction is a prevalent concern in children and adolescents with TDT. While conventional echocardiography may show preserved ejection fractions, TDI reveals latent functional impairments. TDI serves as a reliable, sensitive, and cost-effective modality for detecting early systolic and diastolic cardiac dysfunction in asymptomatic patients, especially in settings where cardiac MRI is unavailable.

Recommendation

TDI should be integrated into the annual cardiac assessment protocol for children and adolescents with TDT to ensure early detection and management of subclinical cardiac dysfunction.

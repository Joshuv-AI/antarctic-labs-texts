Stability of Wasserstein projections in convex order via metric extrapolation
Jakwang Kim, Young-Heon Kim, Andrea Natale

Abstract
. We build on recent work linking backward and forward W 2 W_{2} -projections in convex order with the recently introduced metric extrapolation problem to derive new quantitative stability estimates for both problems.



1.
Introduction

Given two probability measures with finite second moments
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we say that
\mu
is dominated by
\nu
in convex order, denoted
\mu\preceq\nu
, if for any convex function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

In this work we consider the backward and forward 2-Wasserstein projections in convex order, defined as follows:

\text{(backward)}~\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu}(\mu)\coloneqq\argmin_{\rho\preceq\nu}W_{2}^{2}(\rho,\mu)\,,\quad\text{(forward)}~\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\mu\preceq}(\nu)\coloneqq\argmin_{\mu\preceq\rho}W_{2}^{2}(\rho,\nu)\,.

Here, the 2-Wasserstein distance
W_{2}(\rho,\nu)
is defined as

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\rho,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\rho,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)

where
\Pi(\rho,\nu)
denotes the set of couplings between
\rho
and
\nu
.

The backward projection problem always admits a unique solution so
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu}(\mu)
is always well-defined
[
7
,
9
]
. In contrast, the forward problem admits a unique solution as long as the measure
\nu
being projected is absolutely continuous
[
9
]
.

In this work we study the stability of
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu}(\mu)
and
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\mu\preceq}(\nu)
with respect to
\mu
and
\nu
in arbitrary dimensions. The main idea is to exploit a recently discovered duality between the backward projection problem and the so-called metric extrapolation problem.

1.1.
Metric extrapolation

The metric extrapolation problem is a variational problem introduced in
[
5
,
6
]
to define extensions of constant-speed length-minimizing geodesics in the Wasserstein space (referred to simply as minimizing geodesics in the following). These are curves
\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

W_{2}(\gamma(s_{1}),\gamma(s_{2}))=\frac{|s_{2}-s_{1}|}{t}W_{2}(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))

for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,t]
. While for any
\nu_{0},\nu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there always exists a minimizing geodesic on
[0,1]
such that
\gamma(0)=\nu_{0}
and
\gamma(1)=\nu_{1}
,
there might not be any minimizing geodesic with the same property defined on a larger interval
[0,t]
, with
t>1
.

The metric extrapolation of a geodesic from
\nu_{0}
to
\nu_{1}
at any time
t>1
is however always well-defined as the unique minimizer of the following functional:

(1)

\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})\coloneqq\argmin_{\rho}\left\{\frac{W_{2}^{2}(\rho,\nu_{1})}{2(t-1)}-\frac{W_{2}^{2}(\rho,\nu_{0})}{2t}\right\}\,.

By elementary inequalities, if there exists a minimizing geodesic
\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\gamma(0)=\nu_{0}
and
\gamma(1)=\nu_{1}
then necessarily
\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})=\gamma(t)
. When this is not the case however,
[
6
]
found that there still exists a minimizing geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,t]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(1)=\nu_{1}
and
\tilde{\gamma}(t)=\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})
, but
\tilde{\gamma}(0)\neq\nu_{0}
is precisely
\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}^{cx}(\mu)
, where
\mu
is a dilated version of
\nu_{1}
(see Theorem
1
for a precise statement).

1.2.
Main results

In this work we review and make explicit the correspondence between metric extrapolation and the (backward and forward)
W_{2}
-projections in convex order (Theorem
1
), by assembling recent results established in
[
6
,
3
,
7
,
9
]
. We use such correspondence to establish quantitative stability estimates for both problems.

First, exploiting the fact that the backward projection problem with
\nu
fixed can be lifted to a projection on a Hilbert space, we prove that the map
\mu\mapsto\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\mu)
is non-expansive (Lemma
2
).
In turn, we show that this fact implies a Lipschitz estimate also on the map
\nu_{1}\mapsto\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})
(Proposition
1
). We further show that the map
\nu\mapsto\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\mu)
is 1/2-Hölder (Proposition
4
).

While finalizing our manuscript, we found that Lemma
2
and Proposition
4

had also been independently established in a recent work
[
1
, Proposition 2.2, Proposition 2.9]
by Alfonsi and Jourdain; Lemma
2
by a similar method and Proposition
4
by a different method. Their work further includes a detailed study of the special case where both
\mu
and
\nu
are Gaussians.

Our approach is novel, for in particular, it can be directly applied to the forward projection problem to show that, upon some regularity assumption, the map
(\mu,\nu)\mapsto\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu)
is also Hölder continuous (Proposition
5
). For this we use a recent stability result on Wasserstein barycenters
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Note that even if the latter results may not be sharp, the fundamental idea of this work is that in view of Theorem
1
, stability results on the extrapolation problem can be transferred to the projection problems and vice-versa.

2.
Projections in convex order and metric extrapolation

In this section, we recall the equivalence between the
W_{2}
-projections in convex order and the metric extrapolation problem. This will be central to establish the stability of the backward and forward projections.

We start by recalling that if
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{ac}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is absolutely continuous, then for any
\nu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists a uniquely defined minimizing geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\gamma(1)=\nu_{1}
. This is given by
\gamma(s)=((1-s)\mathrm{Id}+s\nabla u)_{\#}\nu_{0}
for all
s\in[0,1]
, where
\#
denotes the push-forward of measures and
\nabla u
is the gradient of a convex function providing the unique optimal transport map from
\nu_{0}
to
\nu_{1}
, i.e.,
\pi=(\mathrm{Id},\nabla u)_{\#}\nu_{0}
is the unique optimal coupling between
\nu_{0}
and
\nu_{1}
.

For any
\lambda>0
we denote by
D^{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
the dilation map defined by

D^{\lambda}(x)\coloneqq\lambda x\,.

The following theorem summarizes some of the results from
[
6
]
, and combines them with those from
[
2
,
7
,
9
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
t>1
,

\theta\coloneqq(t-1)/t
, and let
\nu_{0},\nu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be arbitrary measures. The following holds:

(1)

There exists a unique minimizing geodesic
\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\gamma(1)=\nu_{1}~\text{ and }~\gamma(t)=\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}).

Moreover
\gamma(0)=\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(D^{1/\theta}_{\#}\nu_{1})
and there exists a lower semi-continuous
\theta
-strongly convex potential
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that
\gamma(s)=(s\mathrm{Id}+(1-s)\nabla u^{*})_{\#}\nu_{1}
for all
s\in[0,t]
.
Here
u^{*}
is the Legendre transform of
u
.

(2)

If
\nu_{0}
is absolutely continuous, there exists a unique minimizing geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,t]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\tilde{\gamma}(0)=\nu_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\gamma}(t)=\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})\,.

Moreover, letting
\mu\coloneqq{D_{\#}^{1/\theta}\nu_{1}}
,
\tilde{\gamma}(1)=D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\mu\preceq}(\nu_{0})
and
\tilde{\gamma}(s)=((1-s)\mathrm{Id}+s\nabla u)_{\#}\nu_{0}
for all
s\in[0,t]
, and for the same potential
u
as in point
(1)
.

Proof.

The first point is a direct consequence of
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
stating the equivalence (up to a scaling factor) between the metric extrapolation problem and the weak optimal transport characterization of the backward projection (see
[
7
, Proposition 1.1]
or
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
); see also Remark 4.4 in
[
3
]
for a direct argument for the equivalence of the two problems in a semi-discrete setting. The expression for
\gamma
is provided in
[
6
, Theorem 2.6]
.

To prove the second point, let
v^{*}\coloneqq u^{*}(\theta\,\cdot)/\theta
,
with
u
from point (1) and
u^{*}
be its Legendre dual. It is easy to check that
\nabla v^{*}
is 1-Lipschitz (since
\nabla u^{*}
is
{1/\theta}
-Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
from the
\theta
-strong convexity of
u
) and from the equation for
\gamma
in point (1),

(2)

\nabla v^{*}_{\#}(D^{1/\theta}_{\#}\nu_{1})=\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(D^{1/\theta}_{\#}\nu_{1}).

By Theorem 8.3 and 8.5 in
[
9
]
, the Legendre dual of
v^{*}
, that is,
v^{**}=u/\theta
is the optimal potential (i.e.,
\nabla u/\theta
is the optimal transport map) from
\nu_{0}
to
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\mu\preceq}(\nu_{0})
, where
\mu\coloneqq D_{\#}^{1/\theta}\nu_{1}
. In particular,

\nabla u_{\#}\nu_{0}=D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\mu\preceq}(\nu_{0}).

On the other hand, as a consequence of Theorem 2.6 in
[
6
]
(see in particular equation (2.26) therein),

((1-t)\mathrm{Id}+t\nabla u)_{\#}\nu_{0}=\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}).

Finally, since
u
is
\theta
-strongly convex and
\nu_{0}
is absolutely continuous,
\tilde{\gamma}
,
given by

\tilde{\gamma}(s)=((1-s)\mathrm{Id}+s\nabla u)_{\#}\nu_{0},\quad s\in[0,t],

is the unique minimizing geodesic connecting its endpoints.
∎

3.
Non-expansiveness of the backward projection in convex order

In this section we prove that for any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the map
\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\cdot)
is non-expansive. This will also imply a new Lipschitz stability estimate on the metric extrapolation.

For a given absolutely continuous reference measure
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{ac}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, consider the set

(3)

\{X\in L^{2}(\rho_{0})\,:\,X_{\#}\rho_{0}\preceq\nu\}\,.

This is a convex and bounded set.
In fact, given
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
in this set, denoting
X_{s}=(1-s)X_{0}+sX_{1}
for any
s\in[0,1]
, we have that for any convex function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(X_{s})_{\#}\rho_{0}=\int\varphi(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}\leq(1-s)\int\varphi(X_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}+s\int\varphi(X_{1})\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}\leq\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,,

and so
(X_{s})_{\#}\rho_{0}\preceq\nu
.

It is also bounded, since choosing as test function
\varphi:x\rightarrow|x|^{2}
, we obtain

\|X\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\,.

Fix
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and consider a transport map
X_{\mu}\in L^{2}(\rho_{0})
(not necessarily optimal) with
(X_{\mu})_{\#}\rho_{0}=\mu
, and the problem

(4)

\inf_{X\in L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|X-X_{\mu}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\;:\;X_{\#}\rho_{0}\preceq\nu\right\}\,.

We now show that this is problem is equivalent to the backward projection problem. For this, we recall that for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there always exists a unique (Lipschitz continuous) optimal transport map, say
T
, from
\mu
to
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu}(\mu)
. This can be deduced from Theorem
1
, but is proven in
[
7
]
for example.

Lemma 1
.

For
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{ac}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
Problem (
4
) admits a unique solution
X^{*}\in L^{2}(\rho_{0})
. Furthermore,
X^{*}=T\circ X_{\mu}
where
T\in L^{2}(\mu)
is the unique optimal transport map from
\mu
to
\mu^{*}\coloneqq\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\mu)
. In particular

(5)

X^{*}_{\#}\rho_{0}=\mu^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{2}(\mu,\mu^{*})=\|X_{\mu}-X^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\,.

Proof.

On the one hand, for any admissible
X
, since
(X,X_{\mu})_{\#}\rho_{0}\in\Pi(X_{\#}\rho_{0},\mu)
is an admissible coupling between
X_{\#}\rho_{0}
and
\mu
. Therefore,

W_{2}(\mu,\mu^{*})\leq W_{2}(\mu,X_{\#}\rho_{0})\leq\|X-X_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}.

On the other hand,

W_{2}(\mu,\mu^{*})=\|T-\mathrm{Id}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\|T\circ X_{\mu}-X_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\geq\inf_{X\in L^{2}(\rho_{0}):X_{\#}\rho_{0}\preceq\nu}\|X-X_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\,.

This shows that
X^{*}=T\circ X_{\mu}
is a solution, and it satisfies (
5
). Its uniqueness follows from the strong convexity of the problem.
∎

Now, given another arbitrary measure
\tilde{\mu}
and an arbitrary map
X_{\tilde{\mu}}\in L^{2}(\rho_{0})
such that
(X_{\tilde{\mu}})_{\#}\rho_{0}=\tilde{\mu}
, denoting by
\tilde{X}^{*}
the solution to problem (
4
) with
\tilde{\mu}
replacing
\mu
, we have

(6)

W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\mu),\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\tilde{\mu}))\leq\|X^{*}-\tilde{X}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\leq\|X_{\mu}-X_{\tilde{\mu}}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}.

Here the first inequality follows from the definition of
W_{2}
and the characterization of
X^{*}
and
\tilde{X}^{*}
given in Lemma
1
. The second is due to the fact that the
L^{2}(\rho_{0})
projection onto a convex set, which in our case, is the set in (
3
), is non-expansive. Since
X_{\mu}
and
X_{\tilde{\mu}}
are arbitrary and
\rho_{0}
is absolutely continuous, minimizing over
X_{\mu}
and
X_{\tilde{\mu}}
, by the same arguments as above, we obtain the
W_{2}
-Lipschitz stability of the backward-projection:

Lemma 2
.

Given
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for any
\mu,\tilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\mu),\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\tilde{\mu}))\leq W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})\,.

(We note that this result has been independently established in the recent work
[
1
, Proposition 2.2]
by a similar method.)

As a direct consequence of equation (
6
) and Theorem
1
(point (1)), we also obtain a
W_{2}
-Lipschitz stability result for the metric extrapolation:

Proposition 1
.

Given
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for any
\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t>1
,

(7)

W_{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{1}))\leq 2tW_{2}(\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{1}).

Proof.

For
\theta=(t-1)/t
,
let
\mu=D^{1/\theta}_{\#}\nu_{1}
and
\tilde{\mu}=D^{1/\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\nu}_{1}
. By Theorem
1
(point (1)), there exist
\theta
-strongly convex potentials
u
and
\tilde{u}
such that
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(\mu)=\nabla u^{*}_{\#}\nu_{1}
and
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(\tilde{\mu})=\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}_{\#}\tilde{\nu}_{1}
.
Let
X_{\nu_{1}},X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}}\in L^{2}(\rho_{0})
be arbitrary maps such that
(X_{\nu_{1}})_{\#}\rho_{0}=\nu_{1}
and
(X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}})_{\#}\rho_{0}=\tilde{\nu}_{1}
.
Define

X_{\mu}\coloneqq D^{1/\theta}\circ X_{\nu_{1}}
and
X_{\tilde{\mu}}\coloneqq D^{1/\theta}\circ X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}}
.

Notice that
T\coloneqq\nabla u^{*}\circ D^{\theta}
and
\tilde{T}\coloneqq\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}\circ D^{\theta}
are optimal transport maps form
\mu
to
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(\mu)
and from
\tilde{\mu}
to
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(\tilde{\mu})
, respectively (note in particular that
T
and
\tilde{T}
are the gradients of convex functions and they are 1-Lipschitz since
u
is
\theta
-strongly convex).
Then, Lemma
1
and equation (
6
) (the second inequality) imply that

(8)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u^{*}\circ X_{\nu_{1}}-\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}\circ X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}

\displaystyle=\|T\circ X_{\mu}-\tilde{T}\circ X_{\tilde{\mu}}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|X_{\mu}-X_{\tilde{\mu}}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}=\frac{1}{\theta}\|X_{\nu_{1}}-X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\,.

Moreover,

(tX_{\nu_{1}}+(1-t)\nabla u^{*}\circ X_{\nu_{1}},tX_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}}+(1-t)\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}\circ X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}})_{\#}\rho_{0}\in\Pi(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{1}))\,,

where
\Pi(\rho,\tilde{\rho})\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the set of couplings with given marginals
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\tilde{\rho}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Multiplying both sides of (
8
) by
(t-1)
(recalling
\theta=(t-1)/t
) and adding
t\|X_{\nu_{1}}-X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}
we get, by triangle inequality,

{W_{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{1}))}\leq 2t\|X_{\nu_{1}}-X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\rho_{0})}\,.

Optimizing over
X_{\nu_{1}}
and
X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}}
we obtain the result.
∎

Remark 1
.

Proposition
1
improves a Hölder stability estimate proved in
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
in the second argument of the extrapolation operator
\mathscr{E}^{t}
. Note however that the Lipschitz constant in (
7
) is not sharp at least for
t\rightarrow 1^{+}
, since
\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})\rightarrow\nu_{1}
as
t\rightarrow 1^{+}

[
6
]
.

Remark 2
.

One may wonder whether the same strategy of Lemma
2
for the forward projection case would give a similar estimate.
However, in the forward case, the corresponding set

\{X\in L^{2}(\rho_{0})\,:\,\mu\preceq X_{\#}\rho_{0}\}

is not convex with respect to the affine structure on
L^{2}(\rho_{0})
, therefore, the second inequality in (
6
) does not follow.
For example, take
X
and
\rho_{0}
symmetric with respect to 0 and so that
X_{\#}\rho_{0}
and
(-X)_{\#}\rho_{0}
are both equal to
\mu
and absolutely continuous. If we interpolate linearly between
X
and
-X
we get
((X-X)/2)_{\#}\rho_{0}=\delta_{0}
, and this does not dominate any absolutely continuous measure. Also, we note that geodesic convexity (or even generalized geodesic convexity) also fails; in particular, see
[
9
, Section 8.2]
.

4.
Stability of projections in convex order in one dimension

In this section, we show how Theorem
1
can be used to establish the Lipschitz continuity of the backward and forward projections in the one-dimensional case. This result was already established in
[
8
]
, but the proof we provide here is different and can be generalized to tackle the case
d>1
as shown in the next sections.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, let
F_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
be its cumulative distribution function, defined by

F_{\mu}(x)=\mu((-\infty,x])

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
X_{\mu}:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the pseudo-inverse of
F_{\mu}
. This is defined by

X_{\mu}(a)\coloneqq\inf\{x\;:\>F_{\mu}(x)>a\}

for all
a\in(0,1)
. With these definitions, for any measures

\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

(9)

W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\int_{0}^{1}|X_{\mu}(a)-X_{\nu}(a)|^{2}\mathrm{d}a\,.

4.1.
Stability of the metric extrapolation in one dimension

We start by recalling the Lipschitz continuity of the metric extrapolation operator for
d=1
, already proven in
[
10
]
, for example.
This follows from the representation of
W_{2}
as an
L^{2}
distance in equation (
9
), which implies an explicit characterization of the solutions to (
1
).

Let us denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of nondecreasing functions in
L^{2}(0,1)
, the metric extrapolation at time
t>1
of the minimizing geodesic connecting two given measures
\nu_{0},\nu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, is given by

\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})=Y^{t}_{\#}\mathrm{Leb}|_{[0,1]}\,,\quad Y^{t}\coloneqq P_{\mathcal{M}}(tX_{\nu_{1}}+(1-t)X_{\nu_{0}})\,,

where
P_{\mathcal{M}}
denotes the
L^{2}
orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{M}
.
This implies the following stability bound

(10)

\displaystyle W_{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu}_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq\|t(X_{\nu_{1}}-X_{\tilde{\nu}_{1}})+(1-t)(X_{\nu_{0}}-X_{\tilde{\nu}_{0}})\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq(t-1)W_{2}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})+tW_{2}(\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{1})\,.

Note that
s\mapsto Y^{s}_{\#}\mathrm{Leb}|_{[0,1]}
for
s\in[0,1]
coincides with the
W_{2}
-geodoesic from
\nu_{0}
to
\nu_{1}
. Using this fact, one can easily adapt the argument above to obtain a similar stability estimate for the geodesic interpolation. Specifically, for any minimizing geodesics
\gamma,\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

(11)

W_{2}(\gamma(s),\tilde{\gamma}(s))\leq(1-s)W_{2}(\gamma(0),\tilde{\gamma}(0))+sW_{2}(\gamma(1),\tilde{\gamma}(1)),

for all
s\in[0,1]
.

4.2.
Stability of the backward projection in one dimension

Combining Theorem
1
(point (1)) with the stability bound above for the extrapolation in one dimension, and denoting
\theta=(t-1)/t
as in the statement of the theorem, we get

(12)

\displaystyle W_{2}(\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu}(\mu),\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}}(\tilde{\mu}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{W_{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu},D^{\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\mu}))}{t-1}+\frac{t}{t-1}W_{2}(D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\mu})

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})+\frac{2t}{t-1}W_{2}(D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\mu})

\displaystyle=W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})+2W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})\,.

Indeed, it is easy to see from Theorem
1
(and specifically by a time rescaling of
\gamma
in point (1)) that
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu}(\mu)
is the metric extrapolation at time
t/(t-1)
of the geodesic starting at
\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu)
at time
0
and passing through
\nu_{1}
at time
1
.
Since a similar interpretation holds for
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}}(\tilde{\mu})
we obtain the first line in (
12
) by applying (
10
) (with
t
replaced by
t/(t-1)
). The second line also comes from stability bound (
10
) applied to the term
W_{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu},D^{\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\mu}))
, and the third from the equality

W_{2}(D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\mu})=\theta W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})\,,\quad\text{with }\quad\theta=\frac{t-1}{t}\,.

Note that the estimate (
12
) coincides with the stability result proved in
[
8
]
. When
\mu=\tilde{\mu}
this gives the
1
-Lipschitz dependence for
\nu\to\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu}(\mu)
. Therefore, combining this with Lemma
2
, by triangle inequality, we obtain

Proposition 2
.

For any
\mu,\tilde{\mu},\nu,\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\mu),\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}}^{cx}(\tilde{\mu}))\leq W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})\,.

(This consequence of Lemma
2
is also contained in
[
1
, Corollary 2.3]
.)

4.3.
Stability of the forward projection in one dimension

The argument for the forward projection is very similar.
By Theorem
1
(point (2)) and a time rescaling, if
\nu
is absolutely continuous,
D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu)={\gamma}(1/t)
for a minimizing geodesic
{\gamma}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
{\gamma}(0)=\nu
and
{\gamma}(1)=\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu)
. Hence using equation (
11
) with
s=1/t
, we obtain

\displaystyle W_{2}(D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu),D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}_{\tilde{\mu}\preceq}^{cx}(\tilde{\nu}))

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{t}\right)W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})+\frac{1}{t}W_{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu},D^{\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\mu}))

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{t-1}{t}W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})+W_{2}(D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\mu})\,.

Multiplying both sides by
{1/\theta}
we deduce that

Proposition 3
.

For any
\mu,\tilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\nu,\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu),\mathscr{P}_{\tilde{\mu}\preceq}^{cx}(\tilde{\nu}))\leq 2W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})+W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})\,.

Finally, we note that even if our approach is limited to the
W_{2}
setting, the Lipschitz bounds in Proposition
3
and equation (
12
) actually hold with the same constants for all
W_{p}
distances with
p\in[1,\infty)
as shown in
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
.

5.
Stability of projection in convex order in arbitrary dimensions

5.1.
Stability of the backward projection in arbitrary dimensions

In this section we prove a stability result on the projection map
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu}(\mu)
with respect to
\nu
, in arbitrary dimensions. The proof still relies on Theorem (
1
) but it is different from the one-dimensional case.
Specifically, we will slightly adapt the argument used in
[
6
]
to prove the stability of the metric extrapolation. This relies on two basic estimates on the extrapolation problem.
First, since the functional

\mathcal{G}_{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1};\mu)\coloneqq t\frac{W^{2}(\nu_{1},\mu)}{2}-(t-1)\frac{W^{2}(\nu_{0},\mu)}{2}

for each
t>1
, is
1
-strongly convex along generalized geodesics with base
\nu_{1}
, one recovers that for any
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(13)

\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y-z|^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta(x,y,z)+\mathcal{G}_{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1};\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}))\leq\mathcal{G}_{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1};\rho)\,,

where
\eta\in\Pi(\nu_{1},\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}),\rho)
is any coupling such that

P^{1,2}_{\#}\eta\in\Pi_{o}(\nu_{1},\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}))\,,\qquad P^{1,3}_{\#}\eta\in\Pi_{o}(\nu_{1},\rho)\,,

where
P^{1,2}(x,y,z)\coloneqq(x,y)
and
P^{1,3}(x,y,z)\coloneqq(x,z)
, and where
\Pi_{o}
denotes the set of optimal couplings associated with the
W_{2}
distance between two given measures.
Furthermore, from the so-called dissipative property of
\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})
(see Definition 1.1 in
[
5
]
), we have

(14)

\displaystyle W_{2}(\nu_{0},\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}))\leq tW_{2}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})\,,\quad W_{2}(\nu_{1},\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}))\leq(t-1)W_{2}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})\,;

see
[
5
]
for more details.

Proposition 4
.

For any
\mu,\tilde{\mu},\nu,\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
d\geq 1
,

W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\mu),\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}}^{cx}(\tilde{\mu}))\leq W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+\sqrt{(W_{2}(\mu,\nu)+W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\nu}))W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})}\,.

(We note that, up to minor modifications, this estimate coincides with the one recently derived in
[
1
, Proposition 2.9]
but using a different method.)

Proof.

Let us set
\nu_{0}=\nu
,
\tilde{\nu}_{0}=\tilde{\nu}
and
\nu_{1}=D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu
for a fixed
t>1
and
\theta=(t-1)/t
, and let us use, for simplicity,
\mathcal{G}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
to denote
\mathcal{G}_{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1};\cdot)
and
\mathcal{G}_{t}(\tilde{\nu}_{0},{\nu}_{1};\cdot)
, respectively. By Theorem
1
(point (1)),
we can write

\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})=(t\mathrm{Id}+(1-t)\nabla u^{*})_{\#}\nu_{1}\,,\quad\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu}_{0},\nu_{1})=(t\mathrm{Id}+(1-t)\nabla\tilde{u}^{*})_{\#}\nu_{1}\,

for some
\theta
-strongly convex functions
u
and
\tilde{u}
such that

(15)

\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(\mu)=\nabla u^{*}_{\#}\nu_{1}\,,\quad\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}_{0}}(\mu)=\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}_{\#}\nu_{1}\,.

As
(\nabla u^{*},\nabla\tilde{u}^{*})_{\#}\nu_{1}
is a coupling between
(\nabla u^{*})_{\#}\nu_{1}
and
(\nabla\tilde{u}^{*})_{\#}\nu_{1}
, we obtain

(16)

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(\mu),\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}_{0}}(\mu))\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u^{*}(x)-\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{1}(x).

Now, let

\nu_{t}\coloneqq\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}),\quad\tilde{\nu}_{t}\coloneqq\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu}_{0},\nu_{1})\,.

Setting
\rho=\tilde{\nu}_{t}

in (
13
), due to Theorem
1
(point (1)), we can choose as coupling

\eta=(\mathrm{Id},(t\mathrm{Id}+(1-t)\nabla u^{*}),(t\mathrm{Id}+(1-t)\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}))_{\#}\nu_{1}\,.

Then (
13
) yields

(17)

\frac{(t-1)^{2}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u^{*}(x)-\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{1}(x)\leq\mathcal{G}(\tilde{\nu}_{t})-\mathcal{G}(\nu_{t})\,,

Similarly, setting
\rho=\nu_{t}
in (
13
) written for
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
, and summing up the result with equation (
17
) we obtain

(18)

(t-1)^{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u^{*}(x)-\nabla\tilde{u}^{*}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{1}(x)\leq\mathcal{G}(\tilde{\nu}_{t})-\mathcal{G}(\nu_{t})+\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(\nu_{t})-\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(\tilde{\nu}_{t})\,.

The right-hand side is equal to

\frac{t-1}{2}(W^{2}_{2}(\tilde{\nu_{t}},\tilde{\nu_{0}})-W_{2}^{2}(\tilde{\nu}_{t},\nu_{0}))-\frac{t-1}{2}(W^{2}_{2}(\nu_{t},\nu_{0})-W^{2}_{2}(\nu_{t},\tilde{\nu_{0}}))\,.

Then, by triangle inequality (twice) and the dissipative property of the metric extrapolation (
14
), we have

\displaystyle W^{2}_{2}(\tilde{\nu_{t}},\tilde{\nu_{0}})-W_{2}^{2}(\tilde{\nu}_{t},\nu_{0})

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})(W_{2}(\tilde{\nu_{t}},\tilde{\nu_{0}})+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu}_{t},\nu_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})((2t-1)W_{2}(\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu_{0}})+W_{2}({\nu}_{1},\nu_{0}))

and similarly for the second term

\displaystyle W^{2}_{2}(\nu_{t},\tilde{\nu_{0}})-W_{2}^{2}(\nu_{t},\nu_{0})

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})((2t-1)W_{2}(\nu_{1},\nu_{0})+W_{2}({\nu}_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{0})).

These together with (
16
) give

(19)

(t-1)^{2}W_{2}^{2}(\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(\mu),\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}_{0}}(\mu))\leq t(t-1)(W_{2}(\nu_{1},\nu_{0})+W(\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{0}))W_{2}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})\,,

where we used (
15
) to bound from below the left-hand side of (
18
) by the left-hand side of (
19
). Recalling that
\nu_{1}=D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu
, by triangle inequality,

\displaystyle W_{2}(\nu_{1},\nu_{0})+W(\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\nu_{0},\mu)+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu}_{0},\mu)+2W_{2}(\mu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu)

\displaystyle=W_{2}(\nu_{0},\mu)+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu}_{0},\mu)+2(1-\theta)M_{2}(\mu)

where
M_{2}^{2}(\mu)\coloneqq\int|x|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
.
Reinserting this into (
19
) and dividing the equation by
(t-1)^{2}
, we obtain

W_{2}^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu_{0}}(\mu),\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}_{0}}(\mu))\leq\left[\frac{t}{t-1}(W_{2}(\mu,\nu_{0})+W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\nu}_{0}))+\frac{2}{t-1}M_{2}(\mu)\right]W_{2}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})\,.

Finally, letting
t\rightarrow\infty
we derive

W_{2}^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\nu}^{cx}(\mu),\mathscr{P}_{\preceq\tilde{\nu}}^{cx}(\mu))\leq(W_{2}(\mu,\nu)+W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\nu}))W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})\,.

We conclude using the triangle inequality and Lemma
2
.
∎

Note that by the same arguments, we also have:

Lemma 3

(Lemma 2.3 in
[
6
]
)
.

For any
\nu_{1},\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu}_{0},\nu_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq t(t-1)(W_{2}(\nu_{1},\nu_{0})+W(\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{0}))W_{2}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})\,,

5.2.
Stability of the forward projection in arbitrary dimensions

To prove the stability of the forward projection, we use the same strategy we used in the one-dimensional case. This will result in a worse estimate, since the available stability estimate for both geodesic interpolations and extrapolations are only Hölder. We start by recalling a stability result on geodesic interpolations adapted from
[
4
]
. Define:

\mathcal{P}^{reg}_{2}(\mathbb{R^{d}})\coloneqq\{\rho\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\,\mathrm{spt}(\rho)\text{ is compact and convex},\\
\,\exists\,m,M>0~\text{such that}~m\leq\rho|_{\mathrm{spt}(\rho)}\leq M\}\,.

Lemma 4

(adapted from Theorem 1.5 in
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{reg}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
s\in[0,1)
and
\nu_{s}=\gamma(s),\tilde{\nu}_{s}=\tilde{\gamma}(s)
where
\gamma,\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are any minimizing geodesic such that
\gamma(0)=\nu_{0}
,
\gamma(1)=\nu_{1}
,
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=\tilde{\nu}_{0}
,
\tilde{\gamma}(1)=\tilde{\nu}_{1}
. Then, there exists a constant
C_{d,\nu_{0}}>0
only depending on
d
and
\nu_{0}
such that

W_{2}(\gamma(s),\tilde{\gamma}(s))\leq C_{d,\nu_{0}}\left(W_{1}(\nu_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})+\frac{s}{1-s}W_{1}(\nu_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{1})\right)^{1/6}\,{\quad\text{ for all $s\in[0,1)$}}.

Remark 3
.

The estimate remains valid under less restrictive assumptions on
\nu_{0}
, allowing for measures with non convex supports, and which we omit here for brevity; see
[
4
]
for a detailed discussion. The stability result below still holds with minor modifications under these more general assumptions.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}^{reg}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mu,\tilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu)\mathscr{P}_{\tilde{\mu}\preceq}^{cx}(\tilde{\nu}))\leq C_{d,\nu}(2^{1/6}W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})^{1/6}+CW_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})^{1/12})\,,

where
C_{d,\nu}>0
is as in Lemma
4
and
C\leq 1+(W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\mu})+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu},\tilde{\mu}))^{1/12}
.

Proof.

We treat separately the dependency of
\mathscr{P}^{cx}_{\mu\preceq}(\nu)
with respect to
\mu
and
\nu
.
As for the dependency in
\mu
,
we proceed as in Section
4.3
. Fix
t>1
. Assuming only
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{{reg}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have
by Theorem
1
(point (2)) and a time rescaling, that
D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu)={\gamma}(1/t)
for a minimizing geodesic
{\gamma}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
{\gamma}(0)=\nu
and
{\gamma}(1)=\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu)
. Hence, applying Lemma
4
with
s=1/t
, we get

(20)

\displaystyle W_{2}(D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu),D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}_{\tilde{\mu}\preceq}^{cx}(\nu))

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,\nu}\left(\frac{W_{1}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu),\mathscr{E}^{t}({\nu},D^{\theta}_{\#}\tilde{\mu})}{t-1}\right)^{1/6}\,.

Since
W_{1}\leq W_{2}
, we can use the
W_{2}
distance on the right-hand side.
Hence, dividing both sides by
\theta
and using the Lipschitz estimate in Proposition
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu)\mathscr{P}_{\tilde{\mu}\preceq}^{cx}(\nu))\leq\frac{2^{1/6}C_{d,\nu}}{\theta}W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})^{1/6}\,.

and letting
t\rightarrow\infty
we obtain

(21)

\displaystyle W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu)\mathscr{P}_{\tilde{\mu}\preceq}^{cx}(\nu))\leq 2^{1/6}C_{d,\nu}W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})^{1/6}\,.

For the dependency on
\nu
, we proceed similarly.
By Lemma
4
, where again we replace
W_{1}
with
W_{2}
,

W_{2}(D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu),D^{\theta}_{\#}\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\tilde{\nu}))\leq C_{d,\nu_{0}}\left(W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})+\frac{W_{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu},D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu)}{t-1}\right)^{1/6}.

Now, by Lemma
3
,
W_{2}(\mathscr{E}^{t}(\nu,D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu),\mathscr{E}^{t}(\tilde{\nu},D^{\theta}_{\#}\mu)\leq C_{t,\nu,\tilde{\nu},\mu}W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})^{1/2}
where

C_{t,\nu,\tilde{\nu},\mu}=(t(t-1)(W_{2}(\nu,\mu)+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu},\mu)))^{1/2}+(2(t-1)M_{2}(\mu))^{1/2}\,.

Therefore,

W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu),\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\tilde{\nu}))\leq\frac{C_{d,\nu}}{\theta}\left(W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})+C_{t,\nu,\tilde{\nu},\mu}\frac{W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})^{1/2}}{t-1}\right)^{1/6}\,.

Letting
t\rightarrow\infty
, we recover

W_{2}(\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\nu),\mathscr{P}_{\mu\preceq}^{cx}(\tilde{\nu}))\leq C_{d,\nu}\left(1+(W_{2}(\nu,\mu)+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu},\mu))^{1/12}\right)W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu})^{1/12}\,.

We combine this with (
21
)
and conclude by the triangle inequality.
∎

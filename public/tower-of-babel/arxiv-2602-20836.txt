Onsager–Machlup Functional for Fractional Stochastic Newton Dynamics with Time-Dependent Noise Intensities
Yanbin Zhu, Xiaomeng Jiang, Yong Li

Abstract
In this paper, we derive the Onsager–Machlup functional for a second-order Newton-type stochastic system driven by time-dependent fractional noise, X t ′′ = f t ​ ( X t , X t ′ ) + σ t ​ ξ t H , X_{t}^{\prime\prime}=f_{t}(X_{t},X_{t}^{\prime})+\sigma_{t}\,\xi_{t}^{H}, where H ∈ ( 1 / 4 , 1 ) H\in(1/4,1) . The analysis relies on applying a Girsanov transformation to the non-degenerate components and evaluating the limiting conditional expectation associated with the noise term, for which the stochastic Fubini theorem plays a crucial role. To illustrate the applicability of the result, we study two mechanical systems perturbed by noise and provide supporting numerical simulations.

1
Introduction

The second-order Newton equation

X^{\prime\prime}_{t}=f_{t}(X_{t},X^{\prime}_{t})

is a cornerstone of classical mechanics, governing the motion of systems ranging from celestial bodies to microscopic particles (see
1
). Classical examples include the pendulum equation

X^{\prime\prime}_{t}=-\gamma X_{t}+\sin(X_{t}),

and the Duffing equation

X^{\prime\prime}_{t}+\delta X^{\prime}_{t}+\alpha X_{t}+\beta X_{t}^{3}=\gamma\cos(\omega t),

both of which hold significant importance in both mathematical theory and its applications.

In many physical settings, Newtonian systems are subject to random perturbations arising from environmental fluctuations. Such effects are commonly modeled by incorporating white noise into the governing equations, leading to a stochastic second-order dynamics of the form

X_{t}^{\prime\prime}=f_{t}\bigl(X_{t},X_{t}^{\prime}\bigr)+\xi_{t},

where
\xi_{t}
represents a fluctuating random force.

The dynamical behavior of such noise-perturbed Newtonian systems was first introduced by Paul Langevin in his seminal 1908 work
7
, and has since been extensively developed in both physics and applied mathematics.

In this work, we investigate the case of time-dependent fractional noise described by

X^{\prime\prime}_{t}=f_{t}(X_{t},X^{\prime}_{t})+\sigma_{t}\,\xi^{H}_{t},\quad(X_{0},X^{\prime}_{0})=(x_{0},y_{0}),

(1)

where
\xi^{H}_{t}
denotes fractional noise with Hurst index
H
. Compared to the classical setting, this formulation can capture systems subject to noise perturbations with both time dependence and memory effects.

By introducing the auxiliary variable
Y_{t}=X^{\prime}_{t}
, equation (
1
) can be rewritten equivalently as the following two-dimensional SDE with degenerate noise:

\begin{cases}dX_{t}=Y_{t}\,dt,\\[4.0pt]
dY_{t}=f_{t}(X_{t},Y_{t})\,dt+\sigma_{t}\,dB^{H}_{t},\end{cases}

(2)

subjected to the initial condition
(X_{0},Y_{0})=(x_{0},y_{0})
.

For stochastic dynamical systems, a central problem is to characterize their most probable transition paths, which play a crucial role in understanding transition phenomena in fields such as chemical kinetics and non-equilibrium statistical mechanics
6
. The Onsager-Machlup (OM) functional offers a powerful framework for this purpose. Given a path
\psi
, if the limit

\exp\bigl(J(\psi)\bigr)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}\bigl(\|X_{\cdot}-\psi_{\cdot}\|_{1+\beta}\leq\varepsilon\bigr)}{\mathbb{P}\bigl(\|\textstyle\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{s}\,dB^{H}_{s}\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\bigr)}

(3)

exists, where
\|\cdot\|_{\beta}
denotes a suitable Hölder norm (see Section 2 for precise definitions), then
J
is called the OM functional associated with equation (
1
). Intuitively, this functional describes the probability that the system solution
X_{t}
lies within a tubular neighborhood of a deterministic path
\psi_{t}
as
\varepsilon
approaches zero. Its extremal paths correspond to the most probable transition pathways of the stochastic dynamics.

The OM functional was first introduced by Onsager and Machlup
11
in 1953. It characterizes the most probable paths of diffusion processes and serves as an analogue to the Lagrangian in dynamical systems, describing the optimal trajectory of a particle along a given path. This theory has been widely applied to compute the most probable reaction paths from reactant to product states, see
2
;
5
.

For SDEs driven by fractional noise, the Onsager–Machlup functional has been studied by Liu and Gao
8
,
and related results for time-varying fractional noise have been investigated by the authors recently. However, the system (
2
) presents new challenges due to the degeneracy of the noise and the indirect influence of the noise on certain stochastic integral terms. Specifically, the standard Girsanov transformation cannot be applied directly, and the additional term arising from Taylor expansions requires careful treatment.

To address these challenges, we apply the Girsanov transformation solely to the non-degenerate component
Y_{t}
and characterize the distance between
X_{t}
and
\psi_{t}
in terms of their derivatives. For the additional term, we employ the stochastic Fubini theorem together with careful estimates of trace-type terms.

Our main results (Theorems
4.7
,
4.8
, and
4.9
) provide explicit formulas for the OM functional in three distinct regimes:

J(\psi)=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\dot{\phi}_{s}-s^{\alpha}D_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\Big)^{2}+d_{H}\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,ds,&\tfrac{1}{2}<H<1,\\[22.0pt]
-\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\big(\phi_{s}^{\prime}-f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\big)^{2}}{\sigma_{s}^{2}}+\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,ds,&H=\tfrac{1}{2},\\[22.0pt]
-\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\dot{\phi}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\Big)^{2}+d_{H}\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,ds,&\tfrac{1}{4}<H<\tfrac{1}{2},\end{cases}

where
\alpha=H-1/2
,
d_{H}
is a constant depending on
H
, and the velocity of a path
\psi
is denoted by
\phi_{t}=\psi_{t}^{\prime}
. Moreover, the functions
\phi
and
\dot{\phi}
satisfy
\phi(\cdot)-y_{0}=K_{H}^{\sigma}(\dot{\phi})(\cdot),

where
K_{H}^{\sigma}
is an integral operator of order
H+\tfrac{1}{2}
, which will be defined later. Moreover, in the case
H=\tfrac{1}{2}
, we have
\dot{\phi}(\cdot)=\frac{\phi(\cdot)}{\sigma(\cdot)}.

These formulas extend the classical Onsager–Machlup theory to fractional stochastic Newtonian dynamics with time-dependent noise intensities, thereby establishing a foundation for computing the most probable transition paths in applications.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the notations and preliminary results. In Section 3, we present the main results concerning the OM functional, together with its application to stochastic pendulum equation, stochastic Duffing equation, and accompanying numerical simulations. Finally, Section 4 provides the proofs of the main results.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic notations, assumptions, and lemmas that will be used in the sequel.

2.1
Function Spaces and Norms

Let
0<\beta\leq 1
. We denote by
C^{\beta}([0,1])
the space of continuous functions

f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

[f]_{\beta}:=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq 1}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|}{|t-s|^{\beta}}<\infty.

(4)

We equip
C^{\beta}([0,1])
with the norm

\|f\|_{C^{\beta}([0,1])}:=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|f(t)|+[f]_{\beta}.

Furthermore, we define

C^{\beta}_{0}([0,1]):=\{f\in C^{\beta}([0,1]):f(0)=0\}.

On this subspace we use the norm

\|f\|_{\beta}:=[f]_{\beta}.

It is straightforward to check that
\|\cdot\|_{\beta}
is indeed a norm on

C^{\beta}_{0}([0,1])
, and that it is equivalent to the restriction of

\|\cdot\|_{C^{\beta}([0,1])}
to
C^{\beta}_{0}([0,1])
.
Further, for
1<\beta<2
, we can define the norm of
C_{0}^{\beta}([0,1])
as

\|f\|_{\beta}:=[f^{\prime}]_{\beta-1}.

2.2
Fractional Calculus

We first introduce the basic concepts of fractional calculus. For further details, we refer to
12
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}([a,b])
. The integrals

\displaystyle(I_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{a}^{x}(x-y)^{\alpha-1}f(y)dy,\quad x\geq a,

\displaystyle(I_{b^{-}}^{\alpha}f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{x}^{b}(y-x)^{\alpha-1}f(y)dy,\quad x\leq b,

where
\alpha>0
, are respectively called the right and left Riemann–Liouville fractional integrals of order
\alpha
.

For any
\alpha\geq 0,
any
f\in L^{p}([a,b])
and
g\in L^{q}([a,b])
where
1/p+1/q\leq\alpha,
we have:

\int_{a}^{b}f(s)(I_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}g)(s)ds=\int_{a}^{b}(I_{b^{-}}^{\alpha}f)(s)g(s)ds.

(5)

If
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote by
I_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}(L^{p})
the image of
L^{p}([a,b])
under the operator
I_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}
. Similarly,
I_{b^{-}}^{\alpha}(L^{p})
can be defined.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f\in I_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}(L^{p})
,
g\in I_{b^{-}}^{\alpha}(L^{p})
.
Each of the expressions

\displaystyle(D_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{[\alpha]+1}I_{a^{+}}^{1+[\alpha]-\alpha}f(x),

\displaystyle(D_{b^{-}}^{\alpha}g)(x)

\displaystyle:=\left(-\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{[\alpha]+1}I_{b^{-}}^{1+[\alpha]-\alpha}g(x)

are respectively called the right and left fractional derivative.

From (
5
), we deduce the formula

\int_{a}^{b}f(s)(D_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}g)(s)ds=\int_{a}^{b}(D_{b^{-}}^{\alpha}f)(s)g(s)ds,\quad 0<\alpha<1,

(6)

which holds under the assumptions that
f\in I^{\alpha}_{b^{-}}(L^{p})
and
g\in I^{\alpha}_{a^{+}}(L^{q})
satisfying
1/p+1/q\leq 1+\alpha
.

If
f\in I_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}(L^{p})
, the function
\phi
such that
f=I_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}(\phi)
is unique in
L^{p}
. Fractional derivatives can be regarded as the inverse operation of fractional integrals.

When
\alpha p>1
, any function in
I_{a^{+}}^{\alpha}(L^{p})
is

(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{p})
-Hölder continuous. Moreover, every Hölder continuous
function of order
\beta>\alpha
admits a fractional derivative of order

\alpha
; see
(
4
, Proposition 2.1)
.

Although fractional derivatives are originally defined as derivatives of
fractional integrals, they also admit an explicit representation in terms of
Weyl’s formula (see
(
12
, Remark 5.3)
):

D^{\alpha}_{a^{+}}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\frac{f(x)}{(x-a)^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{a}^{x}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{(x-y)^{\alpha+1}}\,dy\right),\quad 0<\alpha<1,

(7)

where the improper integral converges in the
L^{p}
sense. Therefore, if
f
has Hölder continuity with an exponent strictly greater than
\alpha
, the fractional derivative of order
\alpha
exists.

For the case of the right fractional derivative, we also have a similar representation:

D_{T^{-}}^{\alpha}f(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\frac{f(s)}{(T-s)^{\alpha}}-\alpha\int_{s}^{T}\frac{f(u)-f(s)}{(u-s)^{\alpha+1}}\,du\right).

(8)

2.3
Fractional Stochastic Integration with Respect to Fractional Brownian Motion

Let
W=\{W_{t},t\in[0,1]\}
be a Wiener process defined in the canonical probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, where

\Omega=C_{0}([0,1])

and
\mathbb{P}
is the Wiener measure.
A real-valued continuous process
\{B^{H}_{t},\ t\in[0,T]\}
is called a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
if it is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function

\mathbb{E}[B^{H}_{t}B^{H}_{s}]=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(|t|^{2H}+|s|^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\big):=R_{H}(t,s).

For simplicity, we assume through all that
\alpha=|H-1/2|
.

When
H=\tfrac{1}{2}
, fractional Brownian motion reduces to the classical Brownian motion.
However, if
H\neq\tfrac{1}{2}
, it is neither a semi-martingale nor a Markov process. Moreover, its sample paths belong to
C^{H-\varepsilon}_{0}([0,T])

P
-a.s. for every
\varepsilon>0
.

It is well known that an fractional Brownian motion
B^{H}_{t}
admits a Wiener integral representation: there exists a deterministic kernel
K_{H}(t,s)
and a standard Brownian motion
W
such that

B^{H}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)\,dW_{s},

where

K_{H}(t,s)=\begin{cases}c_{H}\,s^{-\alpha}\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}(u-s)^{\alpha-1}u^{\alpha}\,du,&H>\tfrac{1}{2},\\[8.61108pt]
b_{H}\Big[(t/s)^{-\alpha}(t-s)^{-\alpha}+\alpha s^{\alpha}\int_{s}^{t}(u-s)^{-\alpha}u^{-(\alpha+1)}\,du\Big],&H<\tfrac{1}{2},\end{cases}

with constants

c_{H}=\sqrt{\frac{H(2H-1)}{\mathrm{B}(2-2H,H-\tfrac{1}{2})}},\qquad b_{H}=\sqrt{\frac{2H}{(1-2H)\mathrm{B}(1-2H,H+\tfrac{1}{2})}}.

The operator
K_{H}
, mapping from
L^{2}([0,1])
to
I_{0^{+}}^{H+\frac{1}{2}}(L^{2}([0,1]))
, is given by:

(K_{H}h)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)h(s)\,ds.

(9)

From
(
9
, Lemma 10)
, the operator
K_{H}
can be expressed using fractional integrals as follows:

(K_{H}h)(s)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle I^{1-2\alpha}_{0^{+}}\,s^{\alpha}I^{\alpha}_{0^{+}}\,s^{-\alpha}h,&H<1/2,\\
\displaystyle I^{1}_{0^{+}}\,s^{\alpha}I^{\alpha}_{0^{+}}\,s^{-\alpha}h,&H>1/2.\end{cases}

The inverse operator
(K_{H})^{-1}
is then defined as:

\displaystyle(K_{H})^{-1}h

\displaystyle=s^{\alpha}D^{\alpha}_{0^{+}}\,s^{-\alpha}D^{1-2\alpha}_{0^{+}}h,\quad H<1/2,

\displaystyle(K_{H})^{-1}h

\displaystyle=s^{\alpha}D^{\alpha}_{0^{+}}\,s^{-\alpha}h^{\prime},\quad H>1/2,

(10)

for all
h\in I^{H+\frac{1}{2}}_{0^{+}}(L^{2})
. If
h
is differentiable, the operator simplifies to:

(K_{H})^{-1}h=s^{-\alpha}I^{\alpha}_{0^{+}}\,s^{\alpha}h^{\prime},\quad H<1/2.

(11)

Moreover, the operator
(K_{H})^{-1}
preserves the adaptability property.

Stochastic integrals of deterministic functions with respect to a Gaussian process are called Wiener integrals. Stochastic integrals with respect to fractional Brownian motion can be defined by its Gaussianity. For the step function
I_{[0,t]}(\cdot)
, we assign the inner product

\langle I_{[0,t]}(\cdot),I_{[0,s]}(\cdot)\rangle_{H}=R_{H}(t,s).

We denote by
\mathcal{H}
the space obtained by completing the step functions under the above inner product.
Then the mapping
\mathcal{I}:I_{[0,t]}\longmapsto B^{H}_{t}
corresponds to an isometry between the step function under the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
and the Gaussian variable in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
. By extending this isometry, we obtain our integral
\mathcal{I}
as an isometry from
\mathcal{H}
to the space of Gaussian random variables in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
.

In order to illustrate the elements of
\mathcal{H}
and the relation between the fractional Brownian integral and the Brownian motion integral, we first introduce the following operator:

(K_{H}^{*}f)(s)=\begin{cases}c_{H}\Gamma(\alpha)\,s^{-\alpha}I_{1-}^{\alpha}\big(s^{\alpha}f_{s}\big),&H>\tfrac{1}{2},\\[4.30554pt]
b_{H}\Gamma(1-\alpha)\,s^{\alpha}D^{\alpha}_{1^{-}}\big(s^{-\alpha}f_{s}\big),&H<\tfrac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Theorem 2.3

(
3
)
.

Let
H\in(0,1)
.
If
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\psi_{s}\,dB^{H}_{s}:=\int_{0}^{T}(K_{H}^{*}\psi)(s)\,dW_{s}.

In particular, for
\psi_{s}=\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(s)
, we recover

B^{H}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)\,dW_{s}.

Analogous to the Itô isometry, we have the following isometry for stochastic integrals with respect to fractional Brownian motion.

Lemma 2.4

(
3
)
.

If
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
, then

\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{0}^{T}f_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{s}\int_{0}^{T}g_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{s}\right)=\int_{0}^{T}(K_{H}^{*}f)(s)\,(K_{H}^{*}g)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

For
H>\tfrac{1}{2}
, we have a more intuitive equality:

\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{0}^{T}f_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{s}\int_{0}^{T}g_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{s}\right)=H(2H-1)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}f_{t}g_{s}|s-t|^{2H-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t.

(12)

In this paper, we assume that
\sigma
is Hölder continuous of order
\gamma
with
\gamma+H>1
. Under this condition, the stochastic integral

\int_{0}^{1}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}

is well defined pathwise in the sense of Young integration(see Lemma
2.5
), and this definition agrees with the Wiener integral.

Lemma 2.5

(
15
)
.

For
f\in C^{\beta}([0,1]),g\in C^{\gamma}([0,1])
, if
\beta+\gamma>1,

\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}dg_{s},\quad\int_{0}^{t}g_{s}d\sigma_{s}

are well-defined. Furthermore, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}dg_{s}=\sigma_{t}g_{t}-\sigma_{0}g_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}g_{s}d\sigma_{s}.

Lemma 2.6

(
15
)
.

Let
f,g:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be functions such that
f\in C^{\beta}

and
g\in C^{\gamma}
with
\beta,\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\beta+\gamma>1
.
Then for any
0\leq s<t\leq T
, one has

\left|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}\,dg_{r}-f_{s}(g_{t}-g_{s})\right|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\,[f]_{\beta}\,[g]_{\gamma}\,|t-s|^{\beta+\gamma}.

(13)

Below, we introduce the operator
K_{H}^{\sigma}
via Young integration and the operator
K_{H}
.
Define the operator
K_{H}^{\sigma}
on
L^{2}([0,1])
, associated with the process
\sigma_{\cdot}
and the operator
K_{H}
, by

(K_{H}^{\sigma}f)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}\,d(K_{H}f)(s),\qquad t\in[0,1].

(14)

According to the properties of the Young integral and the
K_{H}
operator, it can be concluded that
the operator
K_{H}^{\sigma}
is injective.
Consequently, we can define its inverse

(K_{H}^{\sigma})^{-1}:K_{H}^{\sigma}\bigl(L^{2}([0,1])\bigr)\to L^{2}([0,1]),\qquad f\mapsto(K_{H})^{-1}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\,df_{s}\right).

For any
\phi
such that
\phi-y_{0}\in K_{H}^{\sigma}(L^{2}([0,1]))
, we denote by
\dot{\phi}
the element of
L^{2}([0,1])
satisfying

\phi-y_{0}=K_{H}^{\sigma}(\dot{\phi}).

(15)

It is worth noting that the operator
K_{H}
is an isomorphism from
L^{2}([0,1])
onto

I_{0^{+}}^{H+\frac{1}{2}}(L^{2}([0,1]))

(see
(
4
, Theorem 2.1)
).
From the embedding theorem,
K_{H}(\dot{\phi})
is
H
-Hölder continuous, and therefore
\phi=y_{0}+K_{H}^{\sigma}(\dot{\phi})
also enjoys
H
-Hölder continuity by Lemma
2.6
.

) must satisfy the following assumptions.

(A)
.

).

3
Main Results

In this section, we first present the main results of our study, and verify our results through numerical simulations.
The details of the computations and derivations leading to the main results are provided in the subsequent section.
Our OM functional takes the following form:

J(\psi)=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\dot{\phi}_{s}-s^{\alpha}D_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}\big(s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\big)\Big)^{2}+d_{H}\,\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,ds,&\tfrac{1}{2}<H<1,\\[22.0pt]
-\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\big(\phi_{s}^{\prime}-f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\big)^{2}}{\sigma_{s}^{2}}+\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,ds,&H=\tfrac{1}{2},\\[22.0pt]
-\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\dot{\phi}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}\big(s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\big)\Big)^{2}+d_{H}\,\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,ds,&\tfrac{1}{4}<H<\tfrac{1}{2},\end{cases}

where
\alpha=H-1/2
,
d_{H}
is a constant depending on
H
, and the velocity of a path
\psi
is denoted by
\phi_{t}=\psi_{t}^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\phi
and
\dot{\phi}
are related through

\phi(\cdot)-y_{0}=K_{H}^{\sigma}(\dot{\phi})(\cdot),

where
K_{H}^{\sigma}
is defined by (
14
). In particular, when
H=\tfrac{1}{2}
, this relation reduces to

\dot{\phi}(\cdot)=(K_{H}^{\sigma})^{-1}\bigl(\phi(\cdot)-y_{0}\bigr)=\frac{\phi(\cdot)}{\sigma(\cdot)}.

Moreover, these three cases can be written in a unified way as

J(\psi)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Big[(K^{H}_{\sigma})^{-1}\Big(\phi_{u}-y_{0}-\int_{0}^{u}f_{v}(\psi_{v},\phi_{v})\,dv\Big)\Big]^{2}(s)+d_{H}\,\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,ds.

(17)

If we consider the velocity transitioning from
y_{0}
at time
t=0
to
y_{1}
at time
t=1
, for the case
\frac{1}{4}<H<\frac{1}{2}
, using variational methods, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{J(\psi+\varepsilon\eta)-J(\psi)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}2\left(\dot{(\psi^{\prime})}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(\dot{(\eta^{\prime})}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}(\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\eta_{s}+\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\eta_{s}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+d_{H}\left(\partial_{xy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\eta_{s}+\partial_{yy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\eta_{s}^{\prime}\right)ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}2\left(\dot{(\psi^{\prime})}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\eta_{s}^{\prime\prime}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}(\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\eta_{s}+\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\eta_{s}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+d_{H}\left(\partial_{xy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\eta_{s}+\partial_{yy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\eta_{s}^{\prime}\right)ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}[2((d/ds)^{2}\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{\alpha}I^{\alpha}_{1^{-}}s^{-\alpha}-\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{\alpha}I^{\alpha}_{1^{-}}s^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(d/ds)\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{\alpha}I^{\alpha}_{1^{-}}s^{-\alpha})+\left(\dot{(\psi^{\prime})}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+d_{H}(\partial_{xy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime})-(d/ds)\partial_{yy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\psi_{s}^{\prime}))]\eta_{s}ds.

Therefore, the most probable path for the velocity
\phi_{s}
should satisfy the equation:

\displaystyle\left(2\left(\frac{d}{ds}\right)^{2}\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{\alpha}I_{1^{-}}^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}-\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{\alpha}I_{1^{-}}^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}+\frac{d}{ds}\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{\alpha}I_{1^{-}}^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\dot{(\phi_{s})}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\right)+d_{H}\left(\partial_{xy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})-\frac{d}{ds}\partial_{yy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\right)=0

with the boundary conditions
(X_{0},Y_{0})=(x_{0},y_{0})
and
(X_{1},Y_{1})=(x_{1},y_{1})
.
The same equations can be obtained for the regular case (
1/2<H<1
)

\displaystyle\left(2\left(\frac{d}{ds}\right)^{2}\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{-\alpha}D_{1^{-}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}-\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{-\alpha}D_{1^{-}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}+\frac{d}{ds}\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\sigma_{s}^{-1}s^{\alpha}D_{1^{-}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\dot{(\phi_{s})}_{s}-s^{\alpha}D_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\right)+d_{H}\left(\partial_{xy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})-\frac{d}{ds}\partial_{yy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\right)=0,

and the standard case (
H=1/2
)

\displaystyle((d/ds)^{2}-\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})+\frac{d}{ds}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s}))(2\frac{\phi_{s}^{\prime}-f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})}{\sigma_{s}^{2}})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\partial_{xy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})+\partial_{yy}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s}))=0.

Remark 3
.

It follows from (
17
) that if
\partial_{y}f(x,y)=C
, then, when the path is constrained to satisfy the boundary conditions
(X(0),X^{\prime}(0))=(x_{0},y_{0})
and
(X(1),X^{\prime}(1))=(x_{1},y_{1})
, where
(x_{0},y_{0})
and
(x_{1},y_{1})
are connected by a solution of the corresponding noiseless system, the resulting most probable path coincides with the noiseless solution trajectory.

(\pm 1,0)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\psi_{s}^{\prime\prime}+\gamma\psi_{s}^{\prime}+V^{\prime}(\psi_{s})\bigr)^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\psi_{s}^{\prime\prime}+V^{\prime}(\psi_{s})\bigr)^{2}+2\gamma\psi_{s}^{\prime}\bigl(\psi_{s}^{\prime\prime}+V^{\prime}(\psi_{s})\bigr)+\gamma^{2}\psi_{s}^{\prime 2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\psi_{s}^{\prime\prime}+V^{\prime}(\psi_{s})\bigr)^{2}+\gamma^{2}\psi_{s}^{\prime 2}\,ds.

\psi_{s}^{(4)}+\bigl(2V^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{s})-\gamma^{2}\bigr)\psi_{s}^{\prime\prime}+V^{(3)}(\psi_{s})\bigl(\psi_{s}^{\prime}\bigr)^{2}+V^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{s})V^{\prime}(\psi_{s})=0.

4
Proof of the Main Results

In this section, we will prove the main results and provide some tools necessary for the proofs. The main tools used in the proof in this paper remain the classical small ball probability estimates and the Girsanov theorem. We first present the small ball probability estimates, which are generalizations of classical results in small ball probability estimation, their proofs can be referred to in
9
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
0<\beta<H
, then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,dW_{s}\right)\mid\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{s}dB^{H}_{s}\|_{\beta}<\varepsilon\right)=1,

for all
h\in L^{2}([0,1])
.

We recall that an operator
K:L^{2}([0,1])\to L^{2}([0,1])
is
nuclear
iff

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big|\langle Ke_{n},g_{n}\rangle\big|<\infty,

for all orthonormal sequences
B=(e_{n})
,
B^{\prime}=(g_{n})
in
L^{2}([0,1])
.
The trace of a nuclear operator
K
is defined by

\operatorname{Tr}K=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle Ke_{n},e_{n}\rangle,

for any orthonormal sequence
B=(e_{n})
in
L^{2}([0,1])
.
The definition is independent of the sequence we have chosen.

Given a symmetric function
f\in L^{2}([0,1]^{2})
, the Hilbert–Schmidt operator

K(f):L^{2}\to L^{2}
associated with
f
is defined by

(K(f)h)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(t,u)h(u)\,du.

(20)

The operator
K(f)
is nuclear iff

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big|\langle Ke_{n},e_{n}\rangle\big|<\infty\quad\text{for all orthonormal sequences }(e_{n})\subset L^{2}([0,1]).

If
f
is continuous and
K(f)
is nuclear, we can compute its trace as follows:

\operatorname{Tr}f:=\operatorname{Tr}K(f)=\int_{0}^{1}f(s,s)\,ds.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
be a symmetric function in
L^{2}([0,1]^{2})
. If
K(f)
is nuclear and
0<\beta<H
, then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}E\left(\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}f(s,t)\,dW_{s}dW_{t}\right)\mid\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{s}dB^{H}_{s}\|_{\beta}<\varepsilon\right)=e^{-\text{Tr}(f)}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\sigma
satisfy Assumption
(A)
, let
U_{t}^{H}=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{v}dB^{H}_{v}
.
Then for any
0<\beta<H
, there exists a constant
M_{\beta}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\neq t\leq 1}\frac{|U^{H}_{t}-U^{H}_{s}|}{|t-s|^{\beta}}\leq\varepsilon\Bigg)\geq\exp\Big(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{H-\beta}}M_{\beta}\Big).

(21)

Lemma 4.4

(Girsanov’s Theorem,
(
16
, Theorem 8.6)
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space, and let

W=\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion.
Suppose that
u=\{u_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
-adapted process such that the following Novikov condition holds:

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|u_{s}|^{2}\,ds\right)\right]<\infty

(22)

for every
T>0
. Define

Z_{t}=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\cdot dW_{s}-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|u_{s}|^{2}\,ds\right),\qquad t\in[0,T].

Then
\{Z_{t}\}
is a martingale under
\mathbb{P}
. If we define a new probability measure

\mathbb{Q}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T})
by

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\Big|_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}=Z_{T},

then the process

\widetilde{W}_{t}=W_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\,ds,\qquad t\in[0,T]

is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion with respect to
\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 4.5

( Stochastic Fubini theorem,
(
14
, Theorem 2.2)
)
.

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space. Let
\varphi:X\times[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be progressively measurable, and suppose that for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,

\int_{X}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi(x,t,\omega)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}\,d\mu(x)<\infty.

Then for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
and for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\int_{X}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(x,t,\omega)dW_{t}d\mu(x)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{X}\varphi(x,t,\omega)d\mu(x)dW_{t}.

Lemma 4.6

(
13
, Lemma 2.1)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a probability triple and let
\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a locally square integrable martingale w.r.t. the filtration

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
,
M_{0}=0.

Let
\langle M\rangle_{t}
denote the quadratic variation of
\{M_{t}\}
. Suppose that
|\Delta M_{t}|=|M_{t}-M_{t^{-}}|\leq K
for all
t>0
,
0\leq K<\infty.

Then for each
a>0
,
b>0
,

P(M_{t}\geq a\ \text{and}\ \langle M\rangle_{t}\leq b^{2}\ \text{for some}\ t)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{a^{2}}{2(aK+b^{2})}\right).

First, the OM functional in (
3
) will be simplified using the Girsanov transformation. Then, we will compute the functional for different ranges of
H
(i.e.,
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
,
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
, and
H=\frac{1}{2}
).

Let

\tilde{Y_{t}}=\phi_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}\ dB^{H}_{s},\quad\tilde{X}_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{Y}_{s}\ ds.

(23)

We define new processes

u_{s}=\dot{\phi}_{s}-(K_{H}^{\sigma})^{-1}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}f_{u}(\tilde{X}_{u},\tilde{Y}_{u})\,du\right)(s)

(24)

and

\tilde{W}_{t}=W_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}ds.

(25)

Under the assumptions of this paper, the Novikov condition (
22
) is satisfied (a proof can be found in
10
). By Lemma
4.4
,
there exists a probability measure
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
, absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathbb{P}
,
such that
\tilde{W}_{t}
is a Brownian motion under
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
.
The corresponding fractional Brownian motion associated with
\tilde{W}_{t}
is given by

\tilde{B}^{H}_{t}=B^{H}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\,d\phi_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\tilde{X}_{s},\tilde{Y_{s}})\,ds.

Taking the differential of
\tilde{X}_{t}
and
\tilde{Y}_{t}
, we have

\begin{cases}d\tilde{X}_{t}=\tilde{Y}_{t}dt,\\
d\tilde{Y}_{t}=f_{t}(\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{Y}_{t})dt+\sigma_{t}d\tilde{B}^{H}_{t}.\end{cases}

This implies that
(\tilde{X},\tilde{B}^{H})
is the strong solution of (
1
) under the probability measure
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
.
Thus, the OM functional can be simplified as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(\|X-\psi\|_{\beta+1}\leq\varepsilon)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(\|\tilde{Y}-\phi\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}\,dW_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}^{2}\,ds\right)\,\middle|\,\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\right].

4.1
Singular Case

In this section we will compute the OM functional for
\frac{1}{4}<H<\frac{1}{2}.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
X
be the solution of (
1
), and assume that the coefficients satisfy Assumption
(A)
.
For any
\phi=\psi^{\prime}\in K_{H}^{\sigma}(L^{2}([0,1]))
with
(\psi_{0},\phi_{0})=(x_{0},y_{0})
, when
\tfrac{1}{4}<H<\tfrac{1}{2}
,
the OM functional of
X_{t}
with respect to the norms

\|\cdot\|_{\beta+1}
, where
H-\tfrac{1}{2}<\beta<H-\tfrac{1}{4}
, can be expressed as

J(\psi)=-\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\dot{\phi}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\Big)^{2}+d_{H}\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,ds,

where

d_{H}=\sqrt{\frac{2H\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+H\right)\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{3}{2}-H\right)}{\Gamma(2-2H)}}.

Proof.

For the parts identical to the classical setting, we omit the detailed proofs; further details can be found in
8
;
9
.
Having applied the Girsanov theorem, the remainder of the proof proceeds using standard arguments. By the Girsanov theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\|X-\psi\|_{\beta+1}\leq\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\exp\Bigg(-\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}\,dW_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}^{2}\,ds\Bigg)I_{\big\{\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{s}\,dB^{H}_{s}\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\big\}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(\exp(A_{1}+A_{2})\,I_{\big\{\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{s}\,dB^{H}_{s}\|_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\big\}}\Big),

where

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\dot{\phi}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}\big(s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\tilde{X}_{s},\tilde{Y}_{s})\big)\Big)\,dW_{s},

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\dot{\phi}_{s}-s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}\big(s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\tilde{X}_{s},\tilde{Y}_{s})\big)\Big)^{2}ds.

Moreover, by

\tilde{Y}_{t}=\phi_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}\,dB^{H}_{s},\qquad\tilde{X}_{t}=\psi_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{s}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u},

it is straightforward to see that

(\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{Y}_{t})\to(\psi_{t},\phi_{t})\quad\text{as }\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|\to 0.

That implies

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\exp(A_{2})\,\Big|\,\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|\leq\varepsilon\Big)=1.

Next, it suffices to consider the limit

\mathbb{E}\Big(\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\tilde{X}_{s},\tilde{Y}_{s})\,dW_{s}\right)\,\Big|\,\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|\leq\varepsilon\Big).

(26)

We perform a Taylor expansion in (
26
):

\displaystyle s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}(\tilde{X}_{s},\tilde{Y}_{s})

\displaystyle=s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\Big(f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})

\displaystyle\qquad+\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}dv\int_{0}^{v}\sigma_{u}dB^{H}_{u}

\displaystyle\qquad+\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}\sigma_{u}dB^{H}_{u}\Big)+R_{s}.

However, unlike the classical case, an additional term involving the stochastic integral of the noise appears. Since integral inequalities do not hold in the context of stochastic integration, the analysis of its limiting behavior becomes non-trivial.

Here we only consider the term arising from the degeneracy of the noise compared to the classical equation:

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}dv\int_{0}^{v}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\,dW_{s}\right)\,\middle|\,\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|\leq\varepsilon\right].

Here

\displaystyle s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}dv\int_{0}^{v}\sigma_{u}dB^{H}_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-\alpha}I^{\alpha}_{0^{+}}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{u}\sigma_{v}dB^{H}_{v}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cs^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\left(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{u}\sigma_{v}\,dB^{H}_{v}du\right)dr.

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\sigma_{v}dB^{H}_{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u}v^{\alpha}D_{u^{-}}^{\alpha}v^{-\alpha}\sigma_{v}dW_{v},

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{u}v^{\alpha}\left(\frac{v^{-\alpha}\sigma_{v}}{(u-v)^{\alpha}}-\alpha\int_{v}^{u}\frac{x^{-\alpha}\sigma_{x}-v^{-\alpha}\sigma_{v}}{(x-v)^{\alpha+1}}dx\right)dW_{v}.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\alpha}I_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})(\tilde{X}_{s}-\psi_{s})\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}g(s,v)\,dW_{v}\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}\left(g_{1}(s,v)+g_{2}(s,v)\right)\,dW_{v}\,dW_{s},

where

\displaystyle g_{1}(s,v)

\displaystyle=C\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{v}^{r}du\,(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\sigma_{v}(u-v)^{-\alpha},

\displaystyle g_{2}(s,v)

\displaystyle=C\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{v}^{r}du\int_{v}^{u}dx\,(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{s},\phi_{r})v^{\alpha}\frac{x^{-\alpha}\sigma_{x}-v^{-\alpha}\sigma_{v}}{(x-v)^{\alpha+1}}.

For
g_{1}(s,v)
, we have

\displaystyle|g_{1}(s,v)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{v}^{r}du\,(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}(u-v)^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}(r-v)^{1-\alpha}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{\alpha}(s-v)\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\alpha-1}y^{1-\alpha}\,dy,

thus,
g_{1}(s,s)=0
.

For
g_{2}(s,v)
, we have

\displaystyle|g_{2}(s,v)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{v}^{r}du\int_{v}^{u}dx\,(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}v^{\alpha}\frac{x^{-\alpha}\sigma_{x}-v^{-\alpha}\sigma_{v}}{(x-v)^{\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{v}^{r}du\,(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}v^{\alpha}\left(\int_{v}^{u}\frac{x^{-\alpha}-v^{-\alpha}}{(x-v)^{\alpha+1}}\,dx+(x-v)^{-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{v}^{r}du

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\,(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}v^{\alpha}\left(v^{-2\alpha}\int_{1}^{\infty}(1-y^{\alpha})y^{-\alpha}(y-1)^{-(\alpha+1)}\,dy+(u-v)^{1-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{v}^{r}du\,(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}v^{\alpha}\left(v^{-2\alpha}+(u-v)^{1-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}dr(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}v^{-\alpha}(r-v)+(s-r)^{\alpha-1}r^{\alpha}v^{\alpha}(r-v)^{2-\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(s^{\alpha}v^{-\alpha}(s-v)^{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\alpha-1}y\,dy+(sv)^{\alpha}(s-v)^{2}\right).

Thus,
g(s,s)=0
.

According to Lemma
4.2
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}dv\int_{0}^{v}\sigma_{u}dB^{H}_{u}\,dW_{s}\right)\,\middle|\,\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|\leq\varepsilon\right]=0.

∎

4.2
Regular Case

In this section we will compute the OM functional for
\frac{1}{2}<H<1.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
X
be the solution of (
1
), and assume that the coefficients satisfy Assumption
(A)
.
For any
\phi=\psi^{\prime}\in K_{H}^{\sigma}(L^{2}([0,1]))
with
(\psi_{0},\phi_{0})=(x_{0},y_{0})
, when
\tfrac{1}{2}<H<1
,
the OM functional of
X_{t}
with respect to the norms

\|\cdot\|_{\beta+1}
, where
H-\tfrac{1}{2}<\beta<H-\tfrac{1}{4}
,
can be expressed as

J(\psi)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\dot{\phi}_{s}-s^{\alpha}D_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}f_{s}\left(\psi_{s},\phi_{s}\right)\right)^{2}+d_{H}\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})ds,

where

d_{H}=\sqrt{\frac{2H\Gamma(H+1/2)\Gamma(3/2-H)}{\Gamma(2-2H)}}.

Proof.

The proof in the regular case follows the same strategy. The difference lies in the computations involving fractional integrals and fractional derivatives. Hence, we proceed directly to the main part of the argument

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}D^{\alpha}_{0^{+}}s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})(\tilde{X}_{s}-\psi_{s})\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\Bigg[s^{-2\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\alpha\int_{0}^{s}dr\ (s-r)^{-(\alpha+1)}s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\alpha\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-(\alpha+1)}\,dr\ r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\int_{0}^{r}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}\Bigg]dW_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}g_{1}(s,v)\,dW_{v}\,dW_{s}+I_{1},

where

\displaystyle g_{1}(s,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{v}^{s}du\int_{v}^{u}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}v^{-\alpha}s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\,dx

and

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle\cdot\Bigg(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}-r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\int_{0}^{r}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr\Bigg).

We have

\displaystyle|g_{1}(s,v)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}du\,s^{-\alpha}v^{-\alpha}\int_{v}^{u}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}du\,s^{-\alpha}v^{-\alpha}u^{\alpha}(u-v)^{\alpha}\,du

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{-\alpha}(s-v)^{\alpha+1},

which means that
g_{1}(s,s)=0
.

Next, we divide
I_{1}
into three parts:
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
, and
K_{3}
. Then

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\int_{0}^{s}\frac{s^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}-r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\int_{0}^{r}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}\sigma_{v}\,dB^{H}_{v}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{s^{-\alpha}-r^{-\alpha}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr\right)dW_{s}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}v^{-\alpha}\,dW_{v}\int_{v}^{u}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{s^{-\alpha}-r^{-\alpha}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr\right)dW_{s}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}dW_{s}\int_{0}^{s}dW_{v}\,g_{2}(s,v),

where

g_{2}(s,v)=\int_{v}^{s}s^{\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})v^{-\alpha}\left(\int_{v}^{u}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{s^{-\alpha}-r^{-\alpha}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr\right)du.

We have

\displaystyle|g_{2}(s,v)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}s^{-\alpha}v^{-\alpha}u^{\alpha}(u-v)^{\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-y^{-\alpha})(1-y)^{-\alpha-1}dy\right)du

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{-\alpha}(s-v)^{\alpha+1},

so we have
g_{2}(s,s)=0.
Note that

\displaystyle\ K_{2}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}dW_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}dr

\displaystyle\quad-C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}dW_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}dr

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}\sigma_{v}dB^{H}_{v}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})-r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}dr\right)dW_{s}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}v^{-\alpha}dW_{v}\int_{v}^{u}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}dx\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})-r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}dr\right)dW_{s}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}dW_{s}\int_{0}^{s}dW_{v}g_{3}(s,v),

where

\displaystyle g_{3}(s,v)

\displaystyle=C\int_{v}^{s}s^{\alpha}v^{-\alpha}\left(\int_{v}^{u}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}dx\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})-r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}dr\right)du.

We have

\displaystyle|g_{3}(s,v)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}s^{\alpha}v^{-\alpha}\left(\int_{v}^{u}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{s}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})-r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr\right)du

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{v}^{s}s^{\alpha}v^{-\alpha}u^{\alpha}(u-v)^{\alpha}\left(\int_{v}^{u}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}(|s-r|+|\psi_{s}-\psi_{r}|+|\phi_{s}-\phi_{r}|)}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr\right)du.

We can follow the same approach to show that
g_{3}(s,s)=0
. Observe that

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\int_{0}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}-r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\int_{0}^{r}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}\,dW_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})\int_{r}^{s}du\int_{0}^{u}dB^{H}_{v}\sigma_{v}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{1}dW_{s}\int_{0}^{s}dW_{v}\,g_{4}(s,v)+g_{5}(s,v).

For
|g_{4}(s,v)|
, we have:

\displaystyle|g_{4}(s,v)|

\displaystyle=C\left|\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{r}^{s}du\int_{v}^{u}s^{\alpha}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})v^{-\alpha}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\int_{v}^{s}dr\int_{r}^{s}s^{\alpha}\frac{r^{-\alpha}v^{-\alpha}(u-v)^{\alpha}u^{\alpha}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,du\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|v^{-\alpha}s^{2\alpha}\int_{v}^{s}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\left((s-v)^{\alpha+1}-(r-v)^{\alpha+1}\right)}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dr\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|v^{-\alpha}(s-v)^{\alpha}s^{2\alpha}\int_{v}^{s}r^{-\alpha}(s-r)^{-\alpha}\,dr\right|

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{-\alpha}(s-v)^{\alpha}s^{2\alpha}.

Thus,
g_{4}(s,s)=0
.

For
|g_{5}(s,v)|
, we have:

\displaystyle|g_{5}(s,v)|

\displaystyle=C\left|\int_{0}^{v}dr\int_{r}^{s}du\int_{v}^{u}s^{\alpha}\frac{r^{-\alpha}\sigma_{r}^{-1}\partial_{x}f_{r}(\psi_{r},\phi_{r})v^{-\alpha}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}\sigma_{x}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\int_{0}^{v}dr\int_{r}^{s}du\,s^{\alpha}\frac{r^{-\alpha}v^{-\alpha}(x-v)^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\int_{0}^{v}dr\int_{r}^{s}du\,s^{\alpha}\frac{r^{-\alpha}v^{-\alpha}(u-v)^{\alpha}u^{\alpha}}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\int_{0}^{v}dr\int_{r}^{s}du\,s^{\alpha}\frac{r^{-\alpha}v^{-\alpha}(s-v)^{\alpha}(s^{\alpha+1}-r^{\alpha+1})}{(s-r)^{\alpha+1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|s^{1-\alpha}(s-v)^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}y^{-\alpha}(1-y)^{-\alpha}\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{1-\alpha}(s-v)^{\alpha}.

Therefore,
g_{5}(s,s)=0
.

According to Lemma
4.2
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\int_{0}^{s}dv\int_{0}^{v}\sigma_{u}dB^{H}_{u}\,dW_{s}\right)\,\middle|\,\left\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dB^{H}_{u}\right\|\leq\varepsilon\right]=0.

∎

4.3
Standard Case

We present the result of the standard case directly.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
X
be the solution of (
1
), and assume that the coefficients satisfy Assumption
(A)
.
For any
\phi=\psi^{\prime}\in K_{H}^{\sigma}(L^{2}([0,1]))
with
(\psi_{0},\phi_{0})=(x_{0},y_{0})
, when
H=1/2
, the OM functional of
X
with respect to the norms

\|\cdot\|_{\beta+1}
with
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2n}
can be expressed as follows:

J(\psi)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{\phi_{s}^{\prime}-f_{s}\left(\psi_{s},\phi_{s}\right)}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{2}+\partial_{y}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})ds.

Proof.

The proof in this case follows the same line as those for the previous two cases. Here, all fractional integrals and derivatives reduce to their standard counterparts, which substantially simplifies the argument.

∎

Remark 4
.

For the standard case, our result can be extended to the system

\begin{cases}dX_{t}=g_{t}(X_{t},Y_{t})\,dt,\\[4.0pt]
dY_{t}=f_{t}(X_{t},Y_{t})\,dt+\sigma_{t}\,dW_{t}.\end{cases}

(27)

This extension is feasible because the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})(X_{s}-\psi_{s})\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})}{\sigma_{s}}(X_{s}-\psi_{s})\,d\!\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\int_{0}^{1}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\right)\frac{f_{1}(\psi_{1},\phi_{1})}{\sigma_{1}}(X_{1}-\psi_{1})

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\!\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\right)(X_{s}-\psi_{s})\,\frac{d}{ds}\!\left(\frac{f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})}{\sigma_{s}}\right)ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\!\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\right)\!\bigl(g_{s}(X_{s},Y_{s})-g_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})\bigr)\frac{f_{s}(\psi_{s},\phi_{s})}{\sigma_{s}}\,ds

\displaystyle\to\;0\qquad\text{as }\biggl\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\biggr\|\to 0.

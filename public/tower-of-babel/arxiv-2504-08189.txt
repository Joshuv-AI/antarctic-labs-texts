Gap Theorem on locally conformally flat manifold
Ming Hsiao, Man-Chun Lee

Abstract
. In this work, we study a gap phenomenon in locally conformally flat Riemannian manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature. We construct complete solutions to the Yamabe flow that exhibit instantaneous bounded curvature as they evolve. Using this, we demonstrate that if the curvature decays quickly enough in an integral sense, then the manifold must be flat. This partially generalizes the results of Chen-Zhu and Ma.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a complete Riemannian manifold.
We say that
(M,g)
is
locally conformally flat
if, for any point
p\in M
, there exists a coordinate chart conformal to the open set in Euclidean space. This is a high-dimensional analog of isothermal coordinate and allows for elegant structural results with curvature lower bounds, for example see
[
24
]
.

In the compact case, there have been many studies in the literature, for example, see
[
5
,
21
]
and the reference therein. In this work, we are primarily interested in studying complete non-compact conformally flat manifolds with
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
. Indeed, their topology has been fully classified by Carron-Herzlich
[
2
]
, see also the earlier work of Zhu
[
25
]
. More precisely if we restrict our attention to the non-compact case, the result of Carron-Herzlich implies that
(M,g_{0})
can only be locally the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the flat
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or globally conformal to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with non-flat metric. The Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is then the only space that admits non-flat complete conformally flat metrics with decaying non-negative Ricci curvature. Indeed, it was already known by the work of Chen-Zhu
[
3
]
that the metric must be flat if the metric satisfies fast curvature decay assumptions at infinity, see also
[
17
]
. Furthermore, Carron-Herzlich
[
2
]
gave an explicit example showing that the result of Chen-Zhu is optimal within some class of metrics. And we also refer readers to
[
10
,
9
,
19
,
11
,
12
]
for some classical results on gap phenomenon.

In this work, we are interested in the gap phenomenon within the class of metrics without a-priori bounded curvature conditions. This is partially motivated by the optimal gap Theorem of Ni
[
20
]
concerning complete non-compact Kähler manifold with holomorphic bisectional curvature
\mathrm{BK}\geq 0
. Specifically, it was shown by Ni that if a complete Kähler manifold
(M,g_{0})
satisfies
\mathrm{BK}\geq 0
and

(1.1)

k(x_{0},r):=\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,r)}\mathcal{R}(g_{0})\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}=o(r^{-2})

as
r\to+\infty
, for some
x_{0}\in M
, then
(M,g_{0})
is necessarily flat. Here
\mathcal{R}(g)
denotes the scalar curvature of metric
g
. The gap Theorem of Ni does not rely on any bounded curvature assumption, see also the earlier work of Chen-Zhu
[
4
]
using Kähler-Ricci flow. By now, it is still unclear to what extent Ni’s optimal gap Theorem can be extended to Riemannian case. In contrast, Chen-Zhu
[
3
]
showed that under bounded curvature assumption, if
k(x,r)=o(r^{-2})
uniformly in
x\in M
, then locally conformally flat
(M,g)
with
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
must be flat. Motivated by these two results, we study locally conformally flat
(M,g)
with
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
under stronger asymptotic assumption on
k(\cdot,r)
but without assuming bounded curvature.

The following is our main result in this work:

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
n\geq 5
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}(n)>0
such that the following holds: Suppose
(M,g_{0})
is a complete non-compact manifold which is locally conformally flat and has
\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq 0
. If

(1.2)

\int^{\infty}_{0}r\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,r)}\mathcal{R}(g_{0})\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\,dr\leq\varepsilon_{0}

for all
x\in M
, then
(M,g_{0})
is flat.

We stress that the assumption is scaling invariant. We remark here that
(M,g_{0})
is immediately globally conformally flat under the assumption in Theorem
1.1
if it is non-flat, thanks to the work of Carron-Herzlich
[
2
]
. So by way of contradiction, we might assume
M
to be topologically
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, but we do not need this fact. This is the locally conformally flat analogy of the earlier work of Chan and the second named author
[
8
]
, where locally conformally flat with
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
is replaced by manifold with non-negative complex sectional curvature.

Our strategy is deeply influenced by the work of Hamilton
[
13
]
in Ricci flow where parabolic deformation is used to regularize the metric to the canonical model. In the case of locally conformally flat, we use the Yamabe flow. The Yamabe flow
g(t)
is a one parameter family of metrics such that

(1.3)

\partial_{t}g(t)=-\mathcal{R}_{g(t)}\cdot g(t).

In particular, this is a conformal deformation of metrics. The Yamabe flow was first proposed by Richard Hamilton in the 1980s as a tool for
constructing metrics of constant scalar curvature in a given conformal class
[
14
]
. In the compact case, Hamilton proved that the Yamabe flow has a global solution for every initial metric. The asymptotic behavior of the Yamabe flow was first studied by Chow
[
7
]
where he proved that the flow approaches a metric of constant scalar curvature provided that the initial metric is locally conformally flat and has positive Ricci curvature.

In the non-compact case, this circle of idea was extended by Chen-Zhu
[
3
]
in order to regularize the metric so that vanishing curvature at infinity can be recognized through Hamilton-Chow’s harnack inequality
[
7
]
. The major difficulty is to construct global solution Yamabe flow in a way that maximum principle is available. In
[
3
]
, it is based on constructing bounded curvature solution for a short-time, and studying the finite time singularity for bounded curvature solution.

To overcome the absence of bounded curvature, we take an alternative way which is more local in nature. We use some idea in
[
17
]
to construct global solution using potential theory, based on the ”linear” nature of Yamabe flow. Unlike bounded curvature solution, the major challenge is to apply maximum principle to obtain geometric control from time-zero geometry. Furthermore although the Yamabe flow is relatively more linear than the Ricci flow, it does not behave analytically well in comparison type argument. In particular, strongly contrast with the Ricci flow, the distance
d_{g(t)}
of the Yamabe flow
g(t)
does not behave well, to the best of the author acknowledge. We mange to overcome this under the strong (uniform) asymptotic condition of
k(\cdot,r)
.
This is based on constructing uniformly conformal Yamabe flow where a-priori estimate of dirichlet conjugate heat kernel can be made available using idea of Bamler, Cabezas-Riva and Wilking
[
1
]
. This allow us to construct Yamabe flow with instantaneously bounded non-negative Ricci curvature. This reduces the question back to bounded curvature setting so that the ”relatively” standard theory can be employed.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we will derive and recall some evolutions of curvature along locally conformally flat Yamabe flow. In section 3, we discuss the heat kernel estimate which will be used in section 4 to localize the curvature estimate. In section 5, we use argument of Ma to construct solution and will show that the solution has quantitative curvature estimate for all t. As an application, we show that the initial data is flat.

2.
Evolution equations along Yamabe flows

We intend to regularize the given metric
g_{0}
on
M
using parabolic method. To make better use of the locally conformally flat nature, it is more sensitive to consider conformal perturbation. In this regard, we use the Yamabe flow which is the following evolution equation for metric:

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}g(t)=-\mathcal{R}(g(t))g(t);\\[5.69054pt]
g(0)=g_{0}\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{R}(g)
denotes the scalar curvature of
g
. In this section, we will collect some evolution equation along Yamabe flow which will be used in this article.

Similar to the Ricci flow, the Yamabe flow is favorable to (some) curvature positivity. For instances, the scalar curvature tends to remain non-negative generally, which can be seen by the following evolution equation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Along a Yamabe flow, the scalar curvature
\mathcal{R}
of
g(t)
satisfies

(2.2)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{2}.

Proof.

This follows from
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

When
g(t)
is locally conformally flat, it was observed by Chow that the Ricci curvature also satisfies a similar evolution equation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Along a locally conformally flat Yamabe flow, the Ricci curvature
\text{\rm Ric}(g(t))
of
g(t)
satisfies

(2.3)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)R_{ij}=\frac{1}{n-2}B_{ij}

where

(2.4)

B_{ij}=(n-1)|\text{\rm Ric}|^{2}g_{ij}+n\mathcal{R}R_{ij}-n(n-1)R_{ik}R^{k}_{j}-\mathcal{R}^{2}g_{ij}.

In particular, if we denote
\lambda_{1}\leq...\leq\lambda_{n}
to be the eigenvalues of
\text{\rm Ric}(g(t))
with respect to
g(t)
, then

(2.5)

B_{11}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k\neq 1}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})^{2}+(n-2)\sum_{j\neq 1}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{1})\lambda_{1}.

Proof.

This follows from
[
7
, Lemma 2.4]
.
∎

When
M
is compact, it follows directly from Lemma
2.2
and Hamilton’s tensor maximum principle that
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
is preserved along the locally conformally flat Yamabe flow. In the non-compact case, the maximum principle is more involved and we need the following evolution equation for the Ricci curvature lower bound. For each
(x,t)\in M\times[0,T]
, we define

(2.6)

\ell(x,t):=\inf\{s>0:\text{\rm Ric}(x,t)+sg(x,t)\geq 0\}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Along a locally conformally flat Yamabe flow, the function
\ell
satisfies

(2.7)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\ell\leq 2\mathcal{R}\ell+n\ell^{2}.

in the sense of barrier.

Proof.

At
(x_{0},t_{0})
, we might assume the smallest eigenvalues of
\text{\rm Ric}(x_{0},t_{0})
is negative as otherwise
\ell\equiv 0
locally around
(x_{0},t_{0})
so that the conclusion holds trivially. Thus, we might assume
\text{\rm Ric}+\ell g\in\mathrm{C}_{\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0}
. By compactness of the sphere bundle, we may find an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
so that it diagonalize
\text{\rm Ric}(g(t))
with respect to
g(t)
. If we denote its eigenvalues as
\lambda_{1}\leq...\leq\lambda_{n}
, then
\text{\rm Ric}|_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(e_{1},e_{1})=-\ell(x_{0},t_{0})
. We use Uhlenbeck trick to simplify the computation as follow: we extend
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
x_{0}
at
t=t_{0}
to
\{E_{i}(t_{0})\}_{i=1}^{n}
by parallel transport with respect to
g(t_{0})
so that
\nabla E_{i}=\Delta E_{i}=0
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
. We then use the ODE:
\partial_{t}E_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}E_{i}
to extend it to
t\approx t_{0}
so that
g(E_{i},E_{j})=\delta_{ij}
locally around
(x_{0},t_{0})
. By assumption,
\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})=\ell(x_{0},t_{0})
and
\ell\leq\varphi
locally near
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.2
and evolution of the frame, the function
\varphi(x,t):=-\text{\rm Ric}(E_{1},E_{1})
satisfies

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\quad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\Big|_{(x_{0},t_{0})}\varphi\\
&=-\frac{1}{n-2}\left[(n-1)|\text{\rm Ric}|^{2}+2(n-1)\mathcal{R}\lambda_{1}-n(n-1)\lambda_{1}^{2}-\mathcal{R}^{2}\right].\end{split}

We now further analyze the quadratic term more carefully. For convenience, we denote
\sum_{i=2}^{n}\lambda_{i}=\mu
so that

(2.9)

\begin{split}&\quad(n-1)|\text{\rm Ric}|^{2}+2(n-1)\mathcal{R}\lambda_{1}-n(n-1)\lambda_{1}^{2}-\mathcal{R}^{2}\\
&=(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}+2(n-1)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\right)\lambda_{1}-n(n-1)\lambda_{1}^{2}\\
&\geq\mu^{2}+(n-1)\lambda_{1}^{2}-(\mu^{2}+2\mu\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{1}^{2})+2(n-1)(\mu+\lambda_{1})\lambda_{1}-n(n-1)\lambda_{1}^{2}\\
&=2(n-2)\mu\lambda-(n-2)^{2}\lambda_{1}^{2}\\
&=(n-2)\left(-n\varphi^{2}-2\mathcal{R}\varphi\right)\end{split}

Putting this back to (
2.8
), this completes the proof.
∎

3.
Heat kernel estimates

In this section, we consider the heat kernel along the Yamabe flow and discuss some estimates. Given a complete solution to the Yamabe flow
g(t),t\in[0,T]
on a smooth manifold
M
. Let
\Omega
be a open set with smooth boundary. We consider the following heat kernel with dirichlet boundary condition:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}-\frac{n}{2}\mathcal{R}_{g(x,t)}\right)K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,s)=0,\;\;\text{for}\;x\in\Omega;\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to s^{+}}K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,s)=\delta_{y}(x),\;\;\text{for}\;x\in\Omega;\\[11.38109pt]
K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,s)=0,\;\;\text{for}\;x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

for
y\in\Omega
and
t\in(s,T]
. Its conjugate is given by

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+(n-1)\Delta_{g(s)}\right)K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,s)=0,\;\;\text{for}\;y\in\Omega;\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to t^{-}}K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,s)=\delta_{x}(y),\;\;\text{for}\;y\in\Omega;\\[11.38109pt]
K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,s)=0,\;\;\text{for}\;y\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

for
x\in\Omega
and
s\in[0,t)
. Particularly for
0\leq s<t<T
, we have

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(\cdot,t;y,s)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\leq 1.

This will play crucial role in obtaining estimates.

Following the idea in
[
8
]
, we intend to use heat kernel to control the curvature along the Yamabe flow, whenever it exists. To achieve this, we need to control the heat kernel
K_{\Omega}
above, under scaling invariant decay
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
. Unlike the Ricci flow, it is unclear to the authors how the distance function
d_{g(t)}
should behave in general, under solely
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
. In the following, we observe an estimate of
K_{\Omega}
under Yamabe flow which is in addition uniformly conformal. This will be sufficient for our application.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
{\alpha}>1
, there exists
C=C(n,{\alpha})>0
such that the following holds. Suppose
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,1]}
is a smooth solution to the Yamabe flow so that

(3.4)

|{\text{\rm Rm}}(g(t))|\leq\frac{{\alpha}}{t}\text{ and }{\alpha}^{-1}g(0)\leq g(t)\leq{\alpha}g(0),

for all
(x,t)\in M\times(0,T]
. If
x_{0}\in M
is a point such that
B_{g(t)}(x_{0},4)\Subset M
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\Omega\Subset M
is a pre-compact domain with a smooth boundary so that
\Omega\Subset B_{g(t)}(x_{0},1)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Then the heat kernel
K_{\Omega}
satisfies

(3.5)

K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,s)\leq\frac{C}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t-s})\right)}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)^{2}}{C(t-s)}\right)

for all
0\leq s<t\leq T
and
x,y\in\Omega
.

When
g(t)
is a solution to the Ricci flow, the corresponding heat kernel estimate was established by
[
1
]
in the complete case, under
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
and
\mathrm{inj}(g(t))\geq\sqrt{{\alpha}^{-1}t}
for some
{\alpha}>0
. It was later observed by the second named author and Tam
[
16
]
that the same result also holds in local sense, see also
[
8
]
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

The proof is almost identical to that of
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
which in turn is based on the argument in
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
using (
3.3
). We use the metric equivalence instead to take care of the distance distortion in Ricci flow.
∎

4.
A-priori local estimates

In this section, we will localize curvature estimates for locally conformally flat Yamabe flow with scaling invariant control. The following localized maximum principle is the core tool, which will be used repeatedly in this work.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
g_{0}
is a metric on
M
and
x_{0}\in M
is a point such that

(1)

B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\Subset M
;

(2)

\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq-(n-1)r^{-2}
.

Let
g(t)
be a smooth solution to the Yamabe flow on
M\times[0,Tr^{2}]
(not necessarily complete) with
g(0)=g_{0}
such that

(i)

|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{R}(g(t))\geq-n(n-1)r^{-2}
;

(iii)

{\alpha}^{-1}g_{0}\leq g(t)\leq{\alpha}g_{0}

on
M\times(0,Tr^{2}]
for some
{\alpha}>1
and
r>0
. If
\ell(x,t)
is a non-negative continuous function on
M\times[0,Tr^{2}]
such that
\ell(0)\leq r^{-2}
,
\ell(x,t)\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
on
M\times(0,T]
and satisfies

(4.1)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\ell\leq{\beta}\mathcal{R}\ell+K\ell^{2}

in the distribution sense, for some
K\geq 0
and
0\leq{\beta}<\frac{n}{2}
. Then there is
\Lambda({\alpha},{\beta},K,n)>0
such that for all
t\in(0,Tr^{2}]
,
\ell(x_{0},t)\leq\Lambda r^{-2}
.

Proof.

We follow the argument in
[
16
]
. By scaling, we assume
r=1
. Without loss of generality, we might assume
T\leq 1
since
\ell\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
. We will use
C_{i}
to denote any constant depending only on
{\alpha},{\beta},K,n
. For
x\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)
, we let

\rho(x):=\sup\{s\in(0,1):B_{g_{0}}(x,s)\Subset B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)\}.

Let
\Lambda>0
be a large constant to be chosen. If
\ell<\Lambda\rho^{-2}
for all
(x,t)\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)\times[0,T]
, then we are done. Otherwise, there is
t_{1}\in(0,T)
such that
\ell<\Lambda\rho^{-2}
for all
(x,t)\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)\times[0,t_{1}]
, and for some
x_{1}\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)
, we have
\ell(x_{1},t_{1})=\Lambda\rho^{-2}(x_{1})
. Denote
r_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\rho(x_{1})
. We consider
B_{g_{0}}(x_{1},r_{1})\times[0,t_{1}]
where
\ell\leq\Lambda\rho^{-2}\leq\Lambda r_{1}^{-2}
.

Consider the parabolic rescaling of metrics and
\ell
:
\tilde{g}(t)=r_{1}^{-2}g(r_{1}^{2}t)
and
\tilde{\ell}(t)=r_{1}^{2}\ell(r_{1}^{2}t)
on
B_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x_{1},1)\times[0,\tilde{t}_{1}]
where
\tilde{t}_{1}=r_{1}^{-2}t_{1}
. We note that the assumptions are invariant under the parabolic rescaling. Moreover, in the tile picture, we have
\tilde{\ell}(0)\leq 1
,
\tilde{\ell}\leq\Lambda
on
B_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x_{1},1)\times[0,\tilde{t}_{1}]
and
\tilde{\ell}(x_{1},\tilde{t}_{1})=\frac{1}{4}\Lambda
.

We construct cut-off function as follows. Fix a smooth non-increasing function
\phi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
such that
\phi\equiv 1
on
[0,\frac{1}{4}]
, vanishes outside
[0,1]
and satisfies
|\phi^{\prime}|^{2}\leq 10^{3}\phi,\phi^{\prime\prime}\geq-10^{3}\phi
. We write the Yamabe flow using its conformal form,
\tilde{g}(t)=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\tilde{g}_{0}
and will use the function

(4.2)

\Phi(x,t)=\phi\left(d_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x,x_{1})\right)\cdot u^{\gamma}

where
\gamma=1+3(n-1)^{-1}
, so that

(4.3)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)\Phi&=-(n-1)u^{\gamma}\cdot\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\phi\\
&\quad+\phi\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)u^{\gamma}\\
&\quad-(n-1)\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla u^{\gamma}\rangle_{\tilde{g}(t)}\\
&=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}+\mathbf{III}.\end{split}

We estimate each term one by one. We first handle
\mathbf{I}
. By conformal formula and choice of
\phi
,

(4.4)

\begin{split}-\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\phi&=-u^{-\frac{4}{n-2}}\phi^{\prime}\left(\Delta_{\tilde{g}_{0}}d_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x,x_{1})+2\nabla_{\tilde{g}_{0}}\log u\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{g}_{0}}d_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x,x_{1})\right)\\
&\quad-u^{-\frac{4}{n-2}}\phi^{\prime\prime}|\nabla_{\tilde{g}_{0}}d_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x,x_{1})|^{2}\\
&\leq C_{0}\left(1+|\phi|^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)\end{split}

where we have used Laplacian comparison on
\tilde{g}_{0}
. Therefore,

(4.5)

\mathbf{I}\leq C_{1}\left(1+|\phi|^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right).

For
\mathbf{III}
, we might argue similarly (and simpler) that

(4.6)

\mathbf{III}\leq C_{2}|\phi|^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}

Using Yamabe flow equation,
\mathcal{R}(\tilde{g}(t))\geq-n(n-1)r_{1}^{2}
and our choice of
\gamma
, we also know

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\quad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)u^{\gamma}\\
&=\gamma u^{\gamma-\frac{4}{n-2}}\left[(2-(n-1)(\gamma-1))|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}_{0}}^{2}-\frac{n-2}{4}\mathcal{R}(\tilde{g}(t))\right]\\
&\leq-u^{\gamma}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}^{2}+C_{3},\end{split}

and thus
\mathbf{II}\leq-\Phi|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}^{2}+C_{3}
. To summarize, we obtain

(4.8)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)\Phi&\leq C_{4}\left(1+|\phi|^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)-\Phi|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}^{2}+C_{3}\\
&\leq\frac{n}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\Phi-\frac{1}{2}\Phi|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}^{2}+C_{5}\end{split}

where we have used
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\geq-n(n-1)r_{1}^{2}
.

On the other hand, the function
F=e^{-K\Lambda t}\tilde{\ell}
satisfies

(4.9)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)F^{1+\varepsilon}\leq(1+\varepsilon){\beta}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}F^{1+\varepsilon}-\varepsilon(n-1)(1+\varepsilon)F^{\varepsilon-1}|\nabla F|^{2}

where we will choose
\varepsilon=(2{\beta})^{-1}n-1>0
. Hence, the function

\Psi(x,t)=\phi\left(d_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x,x_{1})\right)\cdot F^{1+\varepsilon}(x,t)

satisfies

(4.10)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)\Psi&=-(n-1)F^{1+\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\phi\\
&\quad+\phi\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)F^{1+\varepsilon}\\
&\quad-(n-1)(1+\varepsilon)F^{\varepsilon}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla F\rangle_{\tilde{g}(t)}\\
&=\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{C}\end{split}

where (
4.9
) implies

(4.11)

\begin{split}\mathbf{B}&\leq(1+\varepsilon){\beta}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\Psi-\varepsilon(n-1)(1+\varepsilon)\phi F^{\varepsilon-1}|\nabla F|_{\tilde{g}(t)}^{2}.\end{split}

Using (
4.4
), we can also obtain

(4.12)

\begin{split}\mathbf{A}\leq C_{6}F^{1+\varepsilon}\left(1+|\phi|^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right).\end{split}

Finally, using
|\nabla d_{\tilde{g}_{0}}|_{\tilde{g}_{0}}=1
we have

(4.13)

\mathbf{C}\leq C_{7}F^{\varepsilon}|\phi|^{1/2}|\nabla F|_{\tilde{g}(t)}

and therefore,

(4.14)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)\Psi&\leq\frac{n}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\Psi+C_{8}F^{1+\varepsilon}+C_{6}F^{1+\varepsilon}|\phi|^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}.\end{split}

By combining it with the evolution inequality concerning
\Phi
, the function
G:=\Phi+L\Psi
for some large
L>1
, satisfies

(4.15)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{\tilde{g}(t)}\right)G&\leq\frac{n}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}G+C_{5}L-\frac{L}{2}\phi u^{\gamma}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}^{2}\\
&\quad+C_{8}F^{1+\varepsilon}+C_{6}F^{1+\varepsilon}|\phi|^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{\tilde{g}(t)}\\
&\leq\frac{n}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}G+L^{-2}C_{9}\Lambda^{2+2\varepsilon}+C_{10}L^{2}\end{split}

and
G=0
on
\partial B_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x_{1},1)\times[0,\tilde{t}_{1}]
. By maximum principle, we control
G
by its kernel representation:

(4.16)

\begin{split}G(x_{1},\tilde{t}_{1})&\leq\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x_{1},\tilde{t}_{1};y,0)\,G(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,\tilde{g}(0)}\\
&\quad+C_{11}\left(L^{-2}\Lambda^{2+2\varepsilon}+L^{2}\right)\int^{\tilde{t}_{1}}_{0}\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x_{1},\tilde{t}_{1};y,s)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,\tilde{g}(s)}ds\\
&=\mathbf{IV}+\mathbf{V}\end{split}

where
\Omega=B_{\tilde{g}_{0}}(x_{1},1)
.

By Proposition
3.1
,

(4.17)

\begin{split}\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,s)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,\tilde{g}(s)}ds\leq\int^{t}_{0}C_{12}ds=C_{12}t\end{split}

so that

(4.18)

\mathbf{V}\leq C_{13}\left(L^{-2}\Lambda^{2+2\varepsilon}+L^{2}\right)\tilde{t}_{1}.

For
\mathbf{IV}
since
\tilde{\ell}(0)=0
,

(4.19)

\begin{split}\mathbf{IV}&\leq\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x_{1},\tilde{t}_{1};y,0)\,\left(1+Lu^{\gamma}\right)(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,\tilde{g}(0)}\leq C_{14}L.\end{split}

Together with
\tilde{\ell}(x_{1},\tilde{t}_{1})=\frac{1}{4}\Lambda\leq{\alpha}\tilde{t}_{1}^{-1}
, we obtain

(4.20)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{1}{4}e^{-4{\alpha}K}\Lambda\right)^{1+\varepsilon}&\leq C_{14}L+4{\alpha}\Lambda^{-1}C_{13}\left(L^{-2}\Lambda^{2+2\varepsilon}+L^{2}\right).\end{split}

Therefore, if we choose
L=\Lambda^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}
, then

(4.21)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{1}{4}e^{-4{\alpha}K}\Lambda\right)^{1+\varepsilon}&\leq C_{14}\Lambda^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}+8{\alpha}C_{13}\Lambda^{\varepsilon}\end{split}

which is impossible if we choose
\Lambda(n,{\alpha},{\beta},K)
large enough to conflict with the above inequality. That said, such
t_{1}
does not exist and hence, we have shown that for such
\Lambda
, we have

(4.22)

\ell(x,t)\leq\Lambda\rho(x)^{-2}

for all
(x,t)\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)\times[0,T]
. The result follows by taking
x=x_{0}
.
∎

A simple modification of the proof shows that indeed
\mathcal{R}(g(t))
lower bound is preserved along uniformly conformal Yamabe flow.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
g_{0}
is a metric on
M
and
x_{0}\in M
is a point such that

(1)

B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\Subset M
;

(2)

\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq-(n-1)r^{-2}
.

Let
g(t)
be a smooth solution to the Yamabe flow on
M\times[0,Tr^{2}]
(not necessarily complete) with
g(0)=g_{0}
such that

{\alpha}^{-1}g_{0}\leq g(t)\leq{\alpha}g_{0}

on
M\times(0,Tr^{2}]
for some
{\alpha}>1
and
r>0
. Then there is
\Lambda_{1}(n,{\alpha})>0
such that for all
t\in(0,Tr^{2}]
,
\mathcal{R}(g(x_{0},t))\geq-\Lambda_{1}r^{-2}
.

Proof.

By rescaling, we might assume
r=1
. This follows from a simpler modification of the proof of Lemma
4.1
. We consider the test function

(4.23)

F=\phi\left(d_{g_{0}}(x,x_{0})\right)\cdot\varphi+L_{0}u^{\gamma}

where
\varphi=\mathcal{R}_{-}
,
L_{0}
is a large constant,
g(t)=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
and
\phi
is smooth non-increasing function
\phi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
such that
\phi\equiv 1
on
[0,\frac{1}{4}]
, vanishes outside
[0,1]
and satisfies
|\phi^{\prime}|^{2}\leq 10^{3}\phi,\phi^{\prime\prime}\geq-10^{3}\phi
. Then we have

(4.24)

\begin{split}&\quad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)F\\
&\leq-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\phi\cdot\varphi-2\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{g(t)}-\phi\varphi^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{\gamma(n-2)}{4}u^{\gamma-\frac{4}{n-2}}L_{0}\varphi-L_{0}u^{\gamma}|\nabla\log u|^{2}_{g(t)}\end{split}

in the sense of barrier. Here we have used the evolution equation of
u
, see (
4.7
). We might assume the function to be smooth when applying maximum principle.

We now simplify the evolution inequality. We will use
C_{i}
to denote any constant depending only on
n,{\alpha}
. Using the same derivation of (
4.5
) and choice of
\phi
,

(4.25)

\begin{split}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\phi\leq C_{1}\left(1+\phi^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{g(t)}\right)\end{split}

On the other hand at its maximum point,
\nabla F=0
so that

\phi\nabla\varphi+\varphi\nabla\phi+L_{0}\gamma u^{\gamma-1}\nabla u=0

and thus,

(4.26)

\begin{split}-2\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\varphi\rangle&=2\phi^{-1}\langle\nabla\phi,\varphi\nabla\phi+L_{0}\gamma u^{\gamma-1}\nabla u\rangle\\
&\leq C_{2}\varphi+C_{2}L_{0}\phi^{-1/2}|\nabla\log u|_{g(t)}.\end{split}

Substituting it back to the evolution equation of
F
yields

(4.27)

\begin{split}&\quad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)F\\
&\leq C_{1}\varphi\left(1+\phi^{1/2}|\nabla\log u|\right)+C_{2}\varphi+C_{2}L_{0}\phi^{-1/2}|\nabla\log u|\\
&\quad-\phi\varphi^{2}+C_{3}L_{0}\varphi-L_{0}u^{\gamma}|\nabla\log u|^{2}\\
&\leq C_{4}L_{0}\varphi+C_{4}\phi^{-1}L_{0}+C_{4}L_{0}^{-1}\varphi^{2}\phi-\phi\varphi^{2}\end{split}

at its maximum. Hence if we choose
L_{0}=2C_{4}
, then at its interior maximum point inside the support of
\phi
, we have

(4.28)

\begin{split}(\phi\varphi)^{2}\leq C_{5}\varphi\phi+C_{5}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\phi\varphi)^{2}+C_{6}.\end{split}

That said, for all
(x,t)\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)\times[0,T]
,

(4.29)

F(x,t)\leq\sqrt{2C_{6}}+L_{0}:=C_{7}.

Evaluating at
x_{0}
gives us the result.
∎

More generally, we might localize its derivation using kernel representation.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
(M,g_{0},x_{0})
is a pointed manifold and
g(t),t\in[0,Tr^{2}]
is a smooth solution to the Yamabe flow (not necessarily complete) as in Lemma
4.1
. If
\ell(x,t)
is a non-negative continuous function on
M\times[0,Tr^{2}]
such that
\ell(x,t)\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
on
M\times(0,Tr^{2}]
and satisfies

(4.30)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\ell\leq{\beta}\mathcal{R}\ell

in the barrier sense, for some
0\leq{\beta}<\frac{n}{2}
, then there is
\hat{\Lambda}(n,{\alpha},{\beta}),L(n,{\beta})>0
such that for all
t\in[0,Tr^{2}]
,

(4.31)

\ell(x_{0},t)\leq\hat{\Lambda}r^{-2}+e^{Lt}\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,0)\,\ell(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,g(0)}

where
\Omega=B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)
.

Proof.

By rescaling, we assume
r=1
. We let
\phi
be a cutoff function such that
\phi\equiv 1
on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\frac{1}{2})
and vanishes outside
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)
. Consider the function
u=(\ell-e^{Lt}\hat{\ell})_{+}
where

(4.32)

\hat{\ell}(x,t):=\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,0)\,(\phi\cdot\ell)(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,g(0)}.

Whenever
u>0
, it satisfies

(4.33)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)u&\leq{\beta}\mathcal{R}u-\left[\left(\frac{n}{2}-{\beta}\right)\mathcal{R}+L\right]e^{Lt}\hat{\ell}\\
&\leq{\beta}\mathcal{R}u\end{split}

in the sense of barrier, provided that we choose
L=n(n-1)\left(\frac{n}{2}-{\beta}\right)
. The evolution inequality is also in distributional sense,
[
18
]
. Since
\hat{\ell}\geq 0
and
u(0)=0
on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\frac{1}{2})
, we might now apply Lemma
4.1
to
u
so that
u(x_{0},t)\leq 4\Lambda
for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\Lambda=\Lambda({\alpha},{\beta},0,n)
is the constant from Lemma
4.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

As an application of the localized maximum principle, we have local persistence of Ricci lower bound.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{0})

such that for all

,

Proof.

Since
g(t)
is conformal to

,
g(t)
is locally conformally flat for all

. The result follows from Lemma
2.3
, Lemma
4.1
and

.
∎

Another consequence is a psuedo-locality type estimate when the curvature is initially pinched from flat manifold.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{0})
is a pointed locally conformally flat manifold with
n\geq 5
, and
g(t),t\in[0,Tr^{2}]
is a smooth solution to Yamabe flow (not necessarily complete) as in Lemma
4.1
. If in addition
x_{0}
satisfies

(4.35)

\int^{r^{2}}_{0}s\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},s)}|\mathcal{R}_{g_{0}}|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\,ds\leq\Lambda_{0}

for some
\Lambda_{0}>0
, then there is
L(n,{\alpha}),\Lambda_{1}(n,{\alpha})>0
such that for all
t\in(0,Tr^{2}]
,

(4.36)

\mathcal{R}(x_{0},t)\leq\Lambda_{1}r^{-2}+L\Lambda_{0}t^{-1}.

Proof.

By rescaling, we will assume
r=1
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\ell(x,t):=(\mathcal{R}_{g(t)}(x))_{+}
satisfies

(4.37)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(n-1)\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\ell=\mathcal{R}^{2}\leq\mathcal{R}\ell\end{split}

in the sense of barrier, whenever
\ell>0
. This this fulfils the assumption in Lemma
4.3
with
{\beta}=1
. In particular,

(4.38)

\ell(x_{0},t)\leq\hat{\Lambda}+e^{\hat{L}t}\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,0)\mathcal{R}_{+}(y)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,g_{0}}

where
\Omega=B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)
.

We now estimate the kernel representation. We use
C_{i}
to denote any constant depending only on
n,{\alpha}
. We also denote

(4.39)

V(r):=\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\right)\cdot k(x_{0},r)=\int_{B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)}\mathcal{R}_{+}(y)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

for notation convenience. By Proposition
3.1
and co-area formula,

(4.40)

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(x,t;y,0)\mathcal{R}_{+}(y)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,g_{0}}\\
&\leq\int_{B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)}\frac{C_{1}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\mathcal{R}_{+}(y)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{y,g_{0}}\\
&=\int^{1}_{0}\frac{C_{1}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)V^{\prime}(r)\,dr\\
&=\frac{C_{1}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{1}{C_{1}t}\right)V(1)\\
&\quad+\int^{1}_{0}\frac{2rV(r)}{t\cdot\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{0}t}\right)dr\\
&=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}.\end{split}

By volume comparison using
g_{0}
and
\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq-(n-1)
, for
t\in(0,1]
,

(4.41)

\begin{split}\mathbf{I}&\leq\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{1}{C_{1}t}\right)C_{1}\cdot k(x_{0},1)\\
&\leq\frac{C_{2}}{t^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{C_{1}t}\right)\Lambda_{0}\leq C_{3}\Lambda_{0}t^{-1}\end{split}

where we used
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Similarly,

(4.42)

\begin{split}\mathbf{II}&=\left(\int^{1}_{\sqrt{t}}+\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\right)\frac{2r}{t}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{0}t}\right)\cdot k(x_{0},r)dr\\
&=\mathbf{III}+\mathbf{IV}.\end{split}

The straightforward set inclusion yields

(4.43)

\begin{split}\mathbf{IV}\leq\frac{2}{t}\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}r\,k(x_{0},r)\,dr\leq 2\Lambda_{0}t^{-1}\end{split}

while we can apply volume comparison to deduce

(4.44)

\begin{split}\mathbf{III}&\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t}\int^{1}_{\sqrt{t}}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{n}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{0}t}\right)\cdot r\,k(x_{0},r)dr\leq C_{2}\Lambda_{0}t^{-1}.\end{split}

The assertion follows from combining (
4.38
) with (
4.41
), (
4.43
) and (
4.44
).
∎

5.
Existence of immortal Yamabe flow

In this section, we will construct long-time solution to locally conformally flat Yamabe flow from metrics. When the initial metric
g_{0}
is of bounded curvature, this had been considered by Ma
[
17
]
, under a slightly weaker asymptotic assumption.

5.1.
Barrier function from Poisson equation

The method by Ma is based on constructing barrier function from solution to Poission equation. We need part of the argument in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a complete non-compact manifold such that
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
and

(5.1)

\int^{\infty}_{0}rk(x,r)\,dr\leq\Lambda_{0}

for all
x\in M
and
\Lambda_{0}>0
, then there exists a bounded non-positive smooth function
w
on
M
such that
\Delta_{g_{0}}w=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\mathcal{R}(g_{0})
.

Proof.

The existence of
w
where
\Delta\hat{u}=\mathcal{R}
follows from
[
22
, Corollay 2.1]
and translation.
∎

In particular, the existence of
u
from Lemma
5.1
allow us to construct Yamabe flow which is uniformly conformal.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
(M,g_{0})
is a complete non-compact manifold as in Lemma
5.1
. Then there is a immortal solution
g(t),t\in[0,+\infty)
to the Yamabe flow starting from
g_{0}
such that

e^{\frac{4w}{n-2}}g_{0}\leq g(t)\leq g_{0}

on
M\times[0,+\infty)
where
w(x)
is the function obtained from Lemma
5.1
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{R}(g(t))\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

This follows from a similar argument of
[
17
, Theorem 5]
, see also
[
3
]
. For the sake of convenience, we include the proof for readers’ convenience.

We consider the dirichlet problem:

(5.2)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{N}=(n-1)N\left[\Delta_{g_{0}}u-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\mathcal{R}_{g_{0}}u\right],\text{ if }(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty);\\[5.69054pt]
u(x,t)>0,\text{ if }(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty);\\[5.69054pt]
u(x,0)=1,\text{ if }x\in\Omega;\\[5.69054pt]
u(x,t)=1,\text{ if }(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T).\end{cases}

where
N=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
, on pre-compact domain
\Omega
in
M
with smooth
\partial\Omega
. This is equivalent to the Yamabe flow on
\Omega
in the sense that
g(t):=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
is a solution to Yamabe flow.

We take a compact exhaustion
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of
M
with smooth boundary. By
[
3
, Proposition 2.2]
, each
\Omega_{k}
admits an unique classical solution
u_{k}
on
\Omega_{k}\times[0,+\infty)
. Furthermore since
\mathcal{R}_{g_{0}}\geq 0
, maximum principle implies
u_{k}(x,t)\leq 1
on
\Omega_{k}\times[0,+\infty)
.

We now show the lower bound. Fix
T>0
and let
w
be the function such that
\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\mathcal{R}_{g_{0}}=\Delta_{g_{0}}w
on
M
obtained from Lemma
5.1
. Then the function
v=\log u_{k}
satisfies

(5.3)

\partial_{t}(v-w)\geq(n-1)e^{-(N-1)v}\cdot\Delta_{g_{0}}(v-w)

where
v=0
on
\Omega\times\{0\}\bigcup\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
. The function
v_{\varepsilon}:=v-w+\varepsilon t
therefore satisfies

(5.4)

\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\geq\varepsilon+(n-1)e^{-(N-1)v}\Delta_{g_{0}}v_{\varepsilon}

and hence minimum principle implies that its minimum is not attained at interior point. Since
w
is non-positive, by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we see that
u_{k}\geq w
on
\Omega_{k}\times[0,T]
for all
k
and
T>0
. Hence, we have shown that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[0,+\infty)
,

(5.5)

1\geq u_{k}(x,t)\geq w.

Now the existence of immortal solution on
M
uniformly conformal to initial data, follows from sub-sequential limit of
u_{k}
using local parabolic Schauder estimate.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Indeed by
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
, in order to ensure existence using Dirichlet approximation, it is sufficient to requires (
5.1
) to hold for some
x_{0}\in M
instead of all
x\in M
. The non-negativity of scalar curvature follows from applying Lemma
4.2
with
r\to+\infty
.

5.2.
Curvature estimate along Yamabe flow

In this section, we want to show that the solution obtained from Proposition
5.1
has uniform curvature estimate if the initial metric
g_{0}
satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For
n\geq 5
. Suppose
g(t),t\in[0,+\infty)
is a complete solution to the Yamabe flow such that
{\alpha}^{-1}g_{0}\leq g(t)\leq{\alpha}g_{0}

for some
{\alpha}>1
and for all

. There exists

Before we prove Proposition

, we need a Lemma allowing us to bootstrap from uniform conformal to metric with bounded curvature.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
g(t)
be a solution to locally conformally flat Yamabe flow with initial metric
g_{0}
and
x_{0}\in M
be such that
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\Subset M

|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\leq r^{-2}
on

and

(5.6)

{\alpha}^{-1}g_{0}\leq g(t)\leq{\alpha}g_{0}

for

and for some
{\alpha}>1
. Then there exists
\hat{T}(n,{\alpha}),\hat{C}(n,{\alpha})>0
such that
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(x_{0},t))|\leq\hat{C}r^{-2}
for all

.

Proof.

By scaling, we might assume
r=1
. This then follows from the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 2.4]
which is based on argument of Shi in
[
23
]
. We remark that the argument of Shi is purely local.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
5.2
using idea from Hochard
[
15
]
.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Fix
x_{0}\in M
. We might assume
{\alpha}>10
for convenience. We might also assume

(5.7)

{\alpha}^{-1}g(t)\leq g(s)\leq{\alpha}g(t)

for all
t,s\in[0,+\infty)
. Since the assumptions are scaling invariant, we first claim that
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq 2t^{-1}
on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)\times(0,T]
for some
T(n,{\alpha})>0
.

Since
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},2)\Subset M
, we might find
t_{0}>0
such that
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq 2t^{-1}
on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0})\times(0,t_{0}]
where
r_{0}=2
. We fix
L_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}^{-1/2}
. Applying Proposition
4.1
on
B_{g_{0}}(x,L_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})\times[0,t_{0}]
where
x\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0}-L_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})
, we see that

(5.8)

\text{\rm Ric}(g(t))\geq-L_{0}^{-2}\Lambda(n,{\alpha})t_{0}^{-1}

on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0}-L_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})\times[0,t_{0}]
. Now we apply Proposition
4.2
on
B_{g_{0}}(x,L_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})\times[0,t_{0}]
where
x\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0}-2L_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})
to see that

(5.9)

\mathcal{R}(g(t))\leq\Lambda_{1}(n,{\alpha})L_{0}^{-2}t_{0}^{-1}+L(n,{\alpha})\varepsilon_{0}t^{-1}

for all
(x,t)\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0}-2L_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})\times(0,t_{0}]
. Since
g(t)
is locally conformally flat, we conclude that

(5.10)

\begin{split}|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t_{0}))|&\leq C_{n}|\text{\rm Ric}(g(t_{0}))|\\
&\leq C_{n}\left(\Lambda+\Lambda_{1}+L\right)\varepsilon_{0}t_{0}^{-1}:=C_{1}(n,{\alpha})\varepsilon_{0}t_{0}^{-1}\end{split}

on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0}-2L_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})
.

We apply Lemma
5.2
to the translated Yamabe flow with
r=\sqrt{C_{1}^{-1}\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}t_{0}}
so that

(5.11)

|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq\hat{C}\cdot C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}t_{0}^{-1}

on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0}-\tilde{L}_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})\times[t_{0},(1+\tilde{T})t_{0}]
where

\tilde{L}_{0}=2L_{0}+\sqrt{C_{1}^{-1}\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{T}=\hat{T}\cdot C_{1}^{-1}\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}.

We choose
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough so that
\tilde{T}>1
and
\hat{C}\cdot C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}\leq 1
. Hence,

(5.12)

|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq 2t^{-1}

on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0}-\tilde{L}_{0}\sqrt{t_{0}})\times[t_{0},2t_{0}]
. We now define
t_{i}
and
r_{i}
inductively:
t_{i+1}=2t_{i}
and
r_{i+1}=r_{i}-\tilde{L}_{0}\sqrt{t_{i}}
for
i\geq 0
.

By repeating the above argument inductively, we see that if
r_{i}>0
, then we have
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq 2t^{-1}
on
(0,t_{i}]
. We consider
r_{N}>1\geq r_{N+1}
. We see that
t_{N}\geq T(n,{\alpha})
and thus prove the claim. A rescaling argument show that
|\text{\rm Rm}|\leq 2t^{-1}
on
M\times(0,+\infty)
. The non-negativity of
Ric
follows from Proposition
4.1
with
r\to+\infty
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
, we obtain a complete solution to the Yamabe flow
g(t)
with
0\leq\text{\rm Ric}(g(t))\leq 2t^{-1}
and

{\alpha}^{-1}g_{0}\leq g(t)\leq g_{0}

for all
t>0
. Now we are able to use the argument of Ma
[
17
]
to draw the flatness using Chow’s harnack inequality
[
7
]
, which is valid thanks to bounded curvature for
t>0
. We include the proof for readers’ convenience.

By Harnack inequality,
t\mathcal{R}(x,t)
is non-decreasing for
t>0
. Hence, for
t\geq 1
and
\tau\in[\sqrt{t},t]
, we have
\tau\mathcal{R}(\tau)\geq\sqrt{t}\mathcal{R}(\sqrt{t})
. The equation of Yamabe flow and metric equivalence imply

(5.13)

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{R}(x,\tau)d\tau=-\frac{4}{n-2}\log u(x,t)\leq C(n,{\alpha})

for all
x\in M
. Using monotonicity, we get

(5.14)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\log t\cdot\sqrt{t}\mathcal{R}(x,\sqrt{t})\leq\int^{t}_{\sqrt{t}}\mathcal{R}(x,\tau)d\tau\leq C(n,{\alpha})\end{split}

so that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}t\mathcal{R}(x,t)=0
. By monotonicity, this implies
\mathcal{R}(x,t)=0
for all
t>0
. Since the flow is smooth up to
t=0
, this proves the Theorem by letting
t\to 0
.
∎

Introduction and main results

We study the minimization problem of the double phase functionals, i.e.,

{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p}+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\right)\,{\,{\rm d}}x\,,

(1.1)

where a priori
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, the weight function
a
is non-negative and bounded, and
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain. We provide a new technique that leads to a conclusion that if the weight
a
belongs to the class
C^{k,\alpha}
and if
q\leq p+(k+\alpha)\max(1,p/N)
, then the Lavrentiev’s phenomenon for functional (
1.1
) is excluded. This is the content of Theorem
1
. It is essential to note that this result is sharp due to the counterexamples already present in the literature, such as the ones in
[
26
,
4
]
. We also point out that
a\in C^{\infty}
excludes Lavrentiev’s phenomenon for all exponents
p
and
q
, as stated in Corollary
1.1
.

Previously, the result had been proven only for
k=0
, by
[
16
,
26
]
, and
k=1
, by
[
11
]
. The methods therein strongly rely on the analysis of the rate of vanishing of the weights, which does not apply to
k>1
. We expose this in more detail in the further part of the introduction. To handle weights smoother than
C^{1,\alpha}
, we introduce an innovative decomposition of the weight and utilize the theory of Muckenhoupt
A_{r}
classes. Initially, we prove the absence of Lavrentiev’s gap for weights that admit the factorization involving the Muckenhoupt class and a class of weights with a sufficient decay rate. This is described by Theorem
2
. It turns out that weights of suitable smoothness rate always admit the prescribed factorization — a surprising fact that we prove, see Theorem
3
.

Our results provide a foundation for possible further development of the theory of regularity for double phase functionals with regular weights, such as the regularity of minimizers in the spirit of
[

,

,

,

,

.

Lavrentiev’s phenomenon.
One of the challenges when dealing with functionals of
(p,q)
-growth, including (
1.1
), is to address the so-called Lavrentiev’s phenomenon. For us, this means that we would like to exclude the situation in which

\inf_{u\in u_{0}+W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]<\inf_{u\in u_{0}+C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]

(1.2)

for any given
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
, playing the role of boundary data, such that
{\mathcal{F}}[u_{0}]<\infty
. Inequality (
1.2
) means that minimizers of the functional
{\mathcal{F}}
cannot be approximated in the energy by smooth functions, which typically precludes any regularity theory, as seen in
[
28
]
. This is why the important first step in studying double phase functionals is to identify those parameters for which Lavrentiev’s gap is excluded. This topic for functional (
1.1
) and other problems resembling double phase structure has been widely studied, e.g., in
[
3
,
5
,
4
,
25
,
18
,
44
,
26
,
11
]
. Recently, the topic of excluding Lavrentiev’s phenomenon for general classes of functionals was developed, see
[
12
,
16
,
14
,
10
,
35
,
37
,
13
]
. In parallel, developments have been made to the related problem of establishing density of smooth functions in Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, see
[
1
,
31
,
9
,
2
,
22
,
30
]
. For more information on Lavrentiev’s phenomenon and related topics, we refer to survey articles
[

,

,

,

,

,

]
.

Double phase functionals and prior results.
The study of the functional (
1.1
) dates back to
[
44
]
and is one of the most profound examples of the functionals with so-called
(p,q)
-growth, introduced in
[
38
]
. The functional models the switch between
p
- and
q
-growth regimes (phases) according to the weight
a
. The set where
a
vanishes corresponds to the
p
-phase, its complement to the
p
-
q
-phase, while the decay or regularity of
a
is expected to govern the transition between those phases.

Studying functional (
1.1
) presents typical challenges that appear in studying
(p,q)
-growth problems. It turns out that one cannot hope for any regularity of minimizers unless the exponents
p
and
q
are sufficiently close to each other, with closeness quantified by the behavior of the weight
a
, see
[
28
]
. Initial idea, developed through
[
26
,
44
]
, is to consider
a\in C^{0,\alpha}
with
\frac{q}{p}\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{N}
. In particular, this excludes Lavrentiev’s gap. The regularity theory under this condition has been established in the seminal paper by Colombo and Mingione
[
24
]
. In parallel, the regularity theory of a priori bounded minimizers was also developed in
[
23
]
, this time in the range
q\leq p+\alpha
. The absence of Lavrentiev’s gap in this case is established in
[
16
]
. The related regularity results for minimizers with other a priori assumptions are given in
[
6
,
41
]
.

The conditions mentioned above have their drawbacks. For instance, for a given
p\leq N
, one a priori requires that
q\leq p+1
, as
C^{0,\alpha}
is a non-trivial class only for
\alpha\in(0,1]
. In
[
11
]
, class
C^{0,\alpha}
is replaced with
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\alpha}
for arbitrary
\alpha>0
. Following
[
11
, Definition 1.1]
, we say that non-negative function
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}({U})
,
{U}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
open, if there exists a constant
C
such that for all
x,y\in{U}
, it holds

{\sigma}(x)\leq C\left({\sigma}(y)+|x-y|^{{\varkappa}}\right)\,.

(1.3)

The class
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\varkappa}
only restricts how
{\sigma}
approaches
0
, and imposes no control on
{\sigma}
when it is separated from
0
. The parameter
{\varkappa}
indicates how rapidly the function
{\sigma}
decays. As shown in
[
11
]
, weights of class
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
allow excluding Lavrentiev’s phenomenon for an arbitrary choice of
p
and
q
. Regularity of minimizers in this case comes from the general theory developed in
[
34
,
33
]
.

As described in
[
11
, Proposition 1.3]
, the class
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
does not share many commonalities with smoothness in general. Although the inclusions
C^{0,\alpha}\subset{\mathcal{Z}}^{\alpha}
and
C^{1,\alpha}\subset{\mathcal{Z}}^{1+\alpha}
hold, there is no such inclusion for
C^{k,\alpha}
with
k>1
. Indeed, one can think about the function
t\mapsto t^{2}
, which, although being
C^{\infty}
, does not have sufficient decay rate to belong to
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
for any
{\varkappa}>2
.

Main result.
A much more natural way of extending the previous results than involving the class
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\varkappa}
is to consider smoother weights, namely, of class
C^{k,\alpha}
, and expand the range of exponents so that the role of
\alpha
in previous conditions is replaced with
k+\alpha
. As described in the last paragraph, such a result cannot be obtained for any
k>1
by the approach of
[
11
]
, which generalizes those of
[
26
,
16
]
. Neither can it be obtained by applying any of the many conditions for discarding Lavrentiev’s gap that are available in the literature, listed above, in the section about Lavrentiev’s phenomenon. The present paper presents the first general results.

In Theorem
1
, we prove the absence of Lavrentiev’s gap under the assumptions that
a\in C^{k,\alpha}
and
q\leq p+(k+\alpha)\max(1,p/N)
, for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. The found range of exponents is sharp due to the particular example of the presence of Lavrentiev’s gap given in
[
4
]
. Thus, given any exponents
p
and
q
, we have the exact threshold of smoothness of weights beyond which Lavrentiev’s gap is discarded, and before which it is not, in general. In this sense, classes
C^{k,\alpha}
induce a precise scale to the problem. The details are presented in the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Sharp absence and presence of Lavrentiev’s phenomenon)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be defined as in (
1.1
) for
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and non-negative
a
. Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Then, the following assertions hold.

1.

Let
q\leq p+(k+\alpha)\max(1,p/N)
. Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and let
{U}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be open and such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
. Finally, assume that
a\in C^{k,\alpha}(U)
. Then, for any
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
such that
{\mathcal{F}}[u_{0};\Omega]<\infty
, it holds

\inf_{u\in u_{0}+W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]=\inf_{u\in u_{0}+C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]\,.

(1.4)

2.

Let
q>p+(k+\alpha)\max(1,p/N)
. Then, there exists a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, non-negative function
a\in C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, and
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
satisfying
{\mathcal{F}}[u_{0};\Omega]<\infty
, such that

\inf_{u\in u_{0}+W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]<\inf_{u\in u_{0}+C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]\,.

(1.5)

In Section
2
, we present a more general version of Assertion i) of Theorem
1
, namely, Corollary
2.1
. It says that any function
u
such that
{\mathcal{F}}[u]<\infty
may be suitably approximated by regular functions, not only
u
being a minimizer. Moreover, we prove that the range of exponents may be further expanded if we a priori assume that
u\in C^{0,\gamma}
. This is a result in the spirit of
[
6
,
12
]
, which shows that the higher regularity of minimizers to (
1.1
) that are a priori Hölder continuous may be proven under an improved range of exponents.

Let us also point out an important corollary of Theorem
1
.

Corollary 1.1

(No Lavrentiev’s gap for
C^{\infty}
weights)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
{U}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be open and such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be defined as in (
1.1
) with any
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and non-negative
a\in C^{\infty}({U})
. Then, for any
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
such that
{\mathcal{F}}[u_{0};\Omega]<\infty
, it holds

\inf_{u\in u_{0}+W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]=\inf_{u\in u_{0}+C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]\,.

Factorization of weights.
Theorem
1
is a consequence of two results — Theorems
2
and
3
. Theorem
2
provides the absence of Lavrentiev’s phenomenon for the class of weights admitting a certain decomposition related to
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
and Muckenhoupt classes. On the other hand, Theorem
3
says that such a decomposition is always admitted by
C^{k,\alpha}
functions. Both of those theorems are new.

Before presenting the precise statements, let us introduce the (local) Muckenhoupt weights, as defined in
[
8
]
. For every
r\in(1,\infty)
and open set
{U}
, we say that a function
{\omega}:{U}\to[0,\infty)
belongs to class
A_{r}({U})
if

\sup_{B\subseteq{U}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}{\omega}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}{\omega}^{-\frac{1}{r-1}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{r-1}<\infty\,,

(1.6)

where supremum is taken over all balls inside
{U}
. Muckenhoupt classes appear naturally as the class of weights guaranteeing boundedness of the maximal operator in weighted Lebesgue spaces — a crucial property when proving approximation results via mollification.

We are now in a position to state the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be an open and bounded Lipschitz domain and let
{U}
be an open set such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
. Recall the definitions of classes
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\varkappa}
and
A_{r}
from (
1.3
) and (
1.6
). Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be defined as in (
1.1
) for numbers
p,q
and non-negative function
a
satisfying
a={\sigma}{\omega}
, where
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p\leq q\leq p+{\varkappa}\max(1,p/N)
and
{\omega}\in A_{q}({U})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then, for any
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
such that
{\mathcal{F}}[u_{0};\Omega]<\infty
, it holds

\inf_{u\in u_{0}+W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]=\inf_{u\in u_{0}+C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u]\,.

The statement (but not the proof) of Theorem
2
has been inspired by the results in
[
43
]
. This work also considers a product of the weights of two separate classes, although of other kinds and in a different context.

We provide a more general version of Theorem
2
later, i.e., Theorem
4
. In contrast to other results available in the literature, such as
[
11
, Theorem 3]
, Theorem
2
allows for considering weights of the form of a product of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
-function,
{\varkappa}>0
, (including
C^{0,{\varkappa}}
if
{\varkappa}\in(0,1]
) and a bounded weight satisfying a Muckenhoupt-type condition. At first glance, it is not clear how widely Theorem
2
extends the previous contributions. Surprisingly, Theorem
2
may be directly applied to prove Theorem
1
. The following theorem justifies this fact.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Omega
and
{U}
be open and bounded subsets of
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
. Recall the definitions of classes
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\varkappa}
and
A_{r}
from (
1.3
) and (
1.6
). Let
a:{U}\to[0,\infty)
be of class
C^{k,\alpha}
. Then, there exist functions
{\sigma},{\omega}:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
such that
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{k+\alpha}
,
{\omega}\in A_{k+\alpha+1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
a={\sigma}{\omega}
.

Theorem
2
suggests the possibility of extending the entire theory of double phase functionals to the one with weights proposed therein by incorporating the Muckenhoupt weights theory into the field. On the other hand, Theorem
3
shows a vital application of considering the weights as in Theorem
2
, namely, for smoother weights than of Hölder continuity class. We thus believe that Theorems
2
and
3
can be of crucial use for further developments of the theory of double phase functionals with
C^{k,\alpha}
weights.

Let us stress that the decomposition proposed in Theorem
3
is in general non-trivial. First, let us point our attention to the functions
x\mapsto|x_{1}|^{2n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which although being
C^{\infty}
, belong to class
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
only for
{\varkappa}\leq 2n
, while belong to the class
A_{{\varkappa}+1}
only for
{\varkappa}>2n
. Both components
{\sigma}
and
{\omega}
are thus important. Second, note that the function
x\mapsto e^{-\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}}
is
C^{\infty}
, belongs to
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
with any
{\varkappa}>0
, but fails to satisfy the Muckenhoupt condition with any finite parameter. Consequently, it is impossible to embed any smoothness class into some Muckenhoupt class. Finally, consider function
t\mapsto t^{6}\sin^{2}(1/t)
. This function is of class
C^{2,1}
, but does not belong to either
{\mathcal{Z}}^{3}
nor
A_{4}
, even after restriction to any neighbourhood of
0
. However, it may be proved that
t\mapsto t^{4}
belongs to
{\mathcal{Z}}^{3}
, while
t\mapsto t^{2}\sin^{2}(1/t)
belongs to
A_{4}
. Therefore, it is not only false that
a
always coincides with either
{\sigma}
or
{\omega}
, but it is also impossible to point out separate regions of
\Omega
where
a
behaves more like
{\sigma}
or
{\omega}
. We remark that the factorization of Theorem
3
trivializes only in the cases of
k=0
and
k=1
, as we have
C^{0,\alpha}\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}^{\alpha}
and
C^{1,\alpha}\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}^{1+\alpha}
, see
[
11
, Remark 1.2 and Proposition 1.3]
.

We also stress that although the results of Theorems
1
and
2
are local, due to the requirement that the weight lives on a bigger set than
\Omega
, it is ultimately not a setback for the further investigation of minimizers. For instance, works
[
26
,
24
,
23
]
use only local approximation results to prove (local) regularity of minimizers to (
1.1
).

Open question on regularity of minimizers.
The regularity theory for the minimizers of double phase functionals, in terms of belonging to classes
C^{0,\beta},C^{1,\beta}
, already received notable attention when considering Hölder continuous weights, see
[
26
,
23
,
24
,
6
,
41
]
. Later works of
[
34
,
33
,
32
]
, dealing with far more general problems, in particular establish regularity theory for double phase with weights in class
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
. We stress that the mentioned papers do not cover the case of weights of class
C^{k,\alpha}
in general, and thus, the suitable regularity theory may still be developed. We believe that Theorems
2
and
3
are a vital step in this direction. Indeed, they suggest an approach to the problem that combines two fields of great interest: double phase functionals and problems in weighted Sobolev spaces. The latter one is already well established, as described, for instance, in
[
36
,
27
]
.

We also emphasize that, as our results do not align with previously known results, there is still room for improvement in the general conditions for discarding Lavrentiev’s gap, for which our result can be a starting point. Moreover, our results have consequences for approximation properties in Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev spaces of double phase type, and for related PDEs, which we briefly describe in Section
2.3

Methods.
The first idea of the proof of Theorem
1
is to join two different approaches to the problem. The first approach, developed through
[
44
,
26
,
16
]
and culminating in
[
11
]
, involves utilizing the rate of decay of the weight, expressed via the class
{\mathcal{Z}}
. The second one, which we introduce into the topic, is to use the density of smooth functions in the weighted Sobolev space associated with the weight of the (local) Muckenhoupt
A_{q}
class. Neither method alone suffices to exploit the higher smoothness of
a
. It turns out that both approaches can be merged in the sense of Theorem
2
, i.e., if the weight decomposes into the product of weights, each one being suitable for a different approach. Still, it is not obvious Theorem
2
may be applied to smooth weights. This proved to be the most challenging part of the present paper, which we justify in Theorem
3
.

To prove it, we need to construct a suitable decomposition of any
C^{k,\alpha}
function into a product of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
and Muckenhoupt weights. The first key observation is that although a smooth weight itself does not have to admit a sufficient rate of decay to belong to a suitable
{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}
class, it can be forced to do so via corrections by its derivatives, as seen in Lemma
3.1
. The proof that the remaining factor is of a suitable Muckenhoupt class is the content of Proposition
3.5
. In the core, the approach relies on a careful, local comparison of the weight to its Taylor polynomial, also taking advantage of its non-negativity through techniques similar to the so-called Glaeser inequality. This allows not only checking the Muckenhoupt condition on small balls, but also asserting much more precise control on the balls we call medium-sized. The latter one makes it possible to use covering arguments in order to check the Muckenhoupt condition on a large scale, which would not be possible with the sole information that the Muckenhoupt condition is satisfied on the balls from the covering. For the more detailed outline of the proof, see the beginning of Section
3
.

The proofs of Theorem
2
and its generalization, Theorem
4
, employ techniques already well-established in the field. The proof of Theorem
4
may be viewed as an extension of the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 3]
. The additional steps account for the Muckenhoupt part of the weight, which we use for the boundedness of the maximal operator.

2.1
General approximation results

We now provide a general approximation theorem (Theorem
4
), which extends Theorem
2
to arbitrary finite-energy functions. Namely, we prove that any
u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
may be approximated by
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in a way related to functional (

). Moreover, in the spirit of
[
6

Theorem 4

(General approximation)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be an open and bounded Lipschitz domain and let
{U}
be an open set such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
. Recall the definitions of classes
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\varkappa}
and
A_{r}
from (
1.3
) and (
1.6
). Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be defined as in (
1.1
) for some numbers
p,q
satisfying
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and non-negative function
a
satisfy
a={\sigma}{\omega}
, where
{\omega}\in A_{q}({U})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
{\sigma}:U\to[0,\infty)
. Let
u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
be such that
{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]<\infty
. Suppose that either

1.

{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}(\Omega)
for
{\varkappa}>0
such that
q\leq p+{\varkappa}\max(1,p/N)
or

2.

u\in C^{0,\gamma}(\Omega)
and

for some

and
{\varkappa}>0
such that

.

Then there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

{\mathcal{F}}[u_{n};\Omega]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]\quad\text{ and }\quad\|u-u_{n}\|_{W^{1,1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0\,.

(2.1)

The conclusion (
2.1
) is also sometimes referred to as the absence of Lavrentiev’s gap. The result is more general than (
1.4
), and reveals more connection to the theory of function spaces, namely, Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev spaces of double phase type, briefly discussed in Section

.

For the sake of completeness, let us formulate Theorem
4
in terms of regular weights. The following corollary is a direct consequence of Theorems
3
and
4
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be an open and bounded Lipschitz domain and let
{U}
be an open set such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be defined as in (
1.1
) for some numbers
p,q
satisfying
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, and non-negative function
a\in C^{k,\alpha}(U)
for some numbers
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Let
u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
be such that
{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]<\infty
. Suppose that either

1.

q\leq p+(k+\alpha)\max(1,p/N)
or

2.

u\in C^{0,\gamma}(\Omega)
and
q\leq p+\frac{k+\alpha}{1-\gamma}
for some

Then there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

{\mathcal{F}}[u_{n};\Omega]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]\quad\text{ and }\quad\|u-u_{n}\|_{W^{1,1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0\,.

2.2
Global approximation result

The results of Theorems
2
and
4
are essentially local, as we require
a
to be defined on a bigger set than
\Omega
. Although it is not a setback for further developments, one may wonder when a global result can be obtained. Using known criteria for extending Muckenhoupt weights (
[
29
, p. 438]
), we deduce the following global version of Theorem
4
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be an open and bounded Lipschitz domain. Recall the definitions of classes
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\varkappa}
and
A_{r}(U)
from (
1.3
) and (
1.6
). Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be defined as in (
1.1
) for some numbers
p,q
satisfying
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and non-negative function
a
satisfy
a={\sigma}{\omega}
, where
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{{\varkappa}}(\Omega)
,
q\leq p+{\varkappa}\max(1,p/N)
and
{\omega}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies

\sup_{B\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B\cap\Omega}{\omega}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B\cap\Omega}{\omega}^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{q-1}<\infty\,,

where the supremum is taken over all balls in
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. Then, for any function
u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]<\infty
, there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

{\mathcal{F}}[u_{n};\Omega]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]\quad\text{ and }\quad\|u-u_{n}\|_{W^{1,1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0\,.

Under the same condition as in Corolary
2.2
, one can obtain the absence of Lavretiev’s phenomenon as in (

). The reasoning would be precisely the same as the proof of Theorem
2
, but applying Corollary
2.2
instead of Theorem
4
.

2.3
Density of smooth functions in Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev spaces of double phase type

The density of smooth functions in Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev spaces is closely tied to the study of Lavrentiev’s gap. In fact, in the case of the double phase, the conditions for the former coincide with those for the latter. Let us thus define the Musielak–Orlicz–Sobolev space associated with (
1.1
) and state the density result. For more information on such spaces, we refer to
[
22
,
31
]
.

For a given functional
{\mathcal{F}}
, defined as in (
1.1
), and for any open set
{U}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, we define the space

W({U})\coloneqq\left\{u\in W^{1,1}_{0}({U}):{\mathcal{F}}[u;{U}]<\infty\right\}\,,

(2.2)

equipped with the Luxembourg norm

\|u\|_{W({U})}\coloneqq\inf\left\{s>0:\int_{{U}}\left(\left|\frac{\nabla u(x)}{s}\right|^{p}+a(x)\left|\frac{\nabla u(x)}{s}\right|^{q}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq 1\right\}\,.

As integrand of (
1.1
) is doubling, one can show that for any sequence of functions
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset W(U)
and a function
u\in W(U)
, it holds

\|u_{n}-u\|_{W(U)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0\quad\iff\quad{\mathcal{F}}[u_{n}-u]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0\,.

Thus, we may infer the following corollary from Theorems
3
and
4
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be an open and bounded Lipschitz domain and let
{U}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be defined as in (
1.1
) for
1\leq p,q<\infty
, non-negative
a\in C^{k,\alpha}(U)
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, satisfying
q\leq p+(k+\alpha)\max(1,p/N)
. Then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
W(\Omega)
, defined in (
2.2
), in norm topology.

One of the applications of Theorem
2.3
is that it can be used in the study of PDEs with operators of double phase type. We refer the reader to
[
22
, Section 4.1.4]
, where it is explained how the density result as in
2.3
may be used to prove the existence of renormalized solutions.

3
Proof of Theorem
3

We aim to prove that a regular weight can be decomposed into a product of weights, one from the
\mathcal{Z}
class and the other from the Muckenhoupt class, with suitable parameters. The main ingredients of the proof are Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.5
, each one dealing with one part of the decomposition. Lemma
3.1
says that the original weight may be forced to belong to the sufficient
{\mathcal{Z}}
class if one perturbs the weight with its derivatives. The proof of this fact essentially consists of Taylor’s expansion and Young’s inequality.

In the proof of Proposition
3.5
, we deal separately with three different types of balls, which we call small, medium-sized, and large. The small ones are essentially the ones on which the function identified in Lemma
3.1
may be treated as a constant one. We then show that on each small ball one can find a dominating derivative of the weight — almost constant on the ball, in the sense of Lemma
3.4
. This lemma says that if some derivative of the weight is almost constant on a given ball, then one can assert control over an integral of the weight in negative power. The proof involves a careful comparison of the weight to its Taylor polynomial, along with several technical details, particularly Lemma
3.2
.

The medium-sized balls share their characteristics with the small ones, but are also sufficiently large. Using similar techniques as before, we can prove that on those balls, solely the second term of (
1.6
) may be estimated sufficiently so that the Muckenhoupt condition is satisfied. This observation enables us to cover the last type of balls — large ones — with medium-sized ones, thereby inferring the Muckenhoupt condition.

Let us introduce some notation we shall use throughout the proof. For every symmetric multilinear operator
{\Lambda}:({\mathbb{R}^{N}})^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote by
\|{\Lambda}\|
its operator norm

\|{\Lambda}\|\coloneqq\sup_{|v|\leq 1}{\Lambda}[v^{\ell}]\,,

where
v^{\ell}
denotes a tuple
(v,v,\dots,v)\in({\mathbb{R}^{N}})^{\ell}
, where
v
is taken
\ell
times. Whenever
\ell=0
, we shall identify
\Lambda
with a real number, and we shall interpret
\Lambda[v^{\ell}]
as
\Lambda
, for any
v\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. In particular, then
\|\Lambda\|=|\Lambda|
.

Recall that for any open set
{U}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we say that function
a:{U}\to{\mathbb{R}}
belongs to class
C^{k,\alpha}
for some numbers, if
a
has all the derivatives up to order
k
and

[D^{k}a]_{C^{0,\alpha}({U})}=\sup_{x,y\in{U}}\frac{\|D^{k}a(x)-D^{k}a(y)\|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}<\infty\,.

We shall now proceed with proving several facts needed to conclude Theorem
3
. We start with the lemma which identifies the first factor of
a
in Theorem
3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a\in C^{k,\alpha}({U})
for an open and bounded set
{U}
. We define function
{\sigma}
as

{\sigma}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}a(x)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-i}}\,.

(3.1)

Then, for any open
\Omega
such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
, it holds
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{k+\alpha}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We have to prove that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
x,y\in\Omega
, we have

{\sigma}(x)\leq C({\sigma}(y)+|x-y|^{k+\alpha})\,.

(3.2)

Let us denote
r\coloneqq\dist(\partial{U},\Omega)
and observe that whenever
|x-y|\geq r
and
C\geq\frac{\|{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{r^{k+\alpha}}
, then (
3.2
) holds. Therefore, we may focus on the case
|x-y|<r
. As for all
x\in\Omega
we have that
B(x,r)\subset{U}
, we can reformulate (
3.2
) into the assertion that for all
x\in\Omega
and
h\in B(0,r)
, it holds

{\sigma}(x+h)\leq C({\sigma}(x)+|h|^{k+\alpha})\,.

Let us thus fix
x\in\Omega
,
h\in B(0,r)
, and, moreover,
\ell\in\{0,\dots,k\}
. By Taylor expansion, there exists
\xi\in[x,x+h]
such that

\displaystyle D^{\ell}a(x+h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=\ell}^{k-1}\frac{D^{i}a(x)[h^{i-\ell}]}{(i-\ell)!}+\frac{D^{k}a(\xi)[h^{k-\ell}]}{(k-\ell)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=\ell}^{k}\frac{D^{i}a(x)[h^{i-\ell}]}{(i-\ell)!}+\frac{(D^{k}a(\xi)-D^{k}a(x))[h^{k-\ell}]}{(k-\ell)!}\,,

which gives

\|D^{\ell}a(x+h)\|\leq\sum_{i=\ell}^{k}\|D^{i}a(x)\||h|^{i-\ell}+{C_{a}}|h|^{k-\ell+\alpha}\,,

(3.3)

where
{C_{a}}
is Hölder constant of
D^{k}a\in C^{0,\alpha}
. Applying Young’s inequality below the sum in the right-hand side of (
3.3
), we get

\|D^{\ell}a(x+h)\|\leq\sum_{i=\ell}^{k}\|D^{i}a(x)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha-\ell}{k+\alpha-i}}+({C_{a}}+k)|h|^{k-\ell+\alpha}\,.

Applying power
\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-\ell}
to both sides of the last display, we get for some
c=c({C_{a}},k,\alpha)>0
that

\|D^{\ell}a(x+h)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-\ell}}\leq c\left(\sum_{i=\ell}^{k}\|D^{i}a(x)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-i}}+|h|^{k+\alpha}\right)\,.

(3.4)

Summing (
3.4
) over all
\ell\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
, we get

\displaystyle{\sigma}(x+h)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}a(x+h)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-i}}

\displaystyle\leq(k+1)c\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}a(x)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-i}}+|h|^{k+\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=c({\sigma}(x)+|h|^{k+\alpha})\,,

which is the desired result.
∎

Lemma
3.1
clearly shows also what
{\omega}
in Theorem
3
shall look like. To prove that such an
{\omega}
satisfies a suitable Muckenhoupt condition, we start with the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
P
be a polynomial of degree
k
, such that

P(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}t^{i}\,,\text{ where ${\lambda}_{k}>0\,.$}

Assume that
P
is non-negative on an interval
[0,T]
for some
T>0
. Fix
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1]
. Then, there exists a constant
c_{k,{\varepsilon}}>0
, depending only on
k
and
{\varepsilon}
, such that there exists a finite family of pairwise disjoint intervals
\{[{s}_{i},{\tau}_{i}]\}_{i=1}^{m}
included in
[0,T]
, such that
{\tau}_{i}\leq c_{k,{\varepsilon}}{s}_{i}
for every
i=1,\dots,m
, and

t\not\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}[{s}_{i},{\tau}_{i}]\implies\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}t^{i}+{\varepsilon}{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\geq\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2^{k}}{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\,.

Proof.

Given
P
, let us denote polynomials
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
via

P_{1}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\max({\lambda}_{i},0)t^{i}\,,\quad P_{2}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\max(-{\lambda}_{i},0)t^{i}\,,

so that

\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}t^{i}=P_{1}(t)-P_{2}(t)\quad\text{ and }\quad P(t)=P_{1}(t)-P_{2}(t)+{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\,.

Proceeding by induction with respect to
k
, we shall prove a stronger result. Namely, that there exists a finite family of pairwise disjoint intervals
\{[{s}_{i},{\tau}_{i}]\}_{i=1}^{m}
included in
[0,T]
, such that
{\tau}_{i}\leq c_{k,{\varepsilon}}{s}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
, and

t\not\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}[{s}_{i},{\tau}_{i}]\implies P_{1}(t)-P_{2}(t)+{\varepsilon}{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\geq\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2^{k}}\left(P_{1}(t)+{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\right)\,.

(3.5)

At first, observe that
0\leq P(0)={\lambda}_{0}
, which means that cases
k=0
and
k=1
are trivial. Suppose we have the result for
k-1\geq 1
, and proceed with proving the result for
k
.

We may assume that
P_{1}
is positive on
(0,+\infty)
. Indeed, if it is not, then it is identically equal to
0
, and so on
[0,T]
we have
0\leq P(t)={\lambda}_{k}t^{k}-P_{2}(t)
, which may be true only if
P_{2}
is identically equal
0
, as
P_{2}
has only non-negative coefficients and is of degree lesser than
k
. Thus, we have
P(t)={\lambda}_{k}t^{k}
, for which (
3.5
) is trivially satisfied. We shall thus assume from now on that
P_{1}
is positive on
(0,+\infty)
.

We notice that the function
t\mapsto P_{1}(t)-{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}
has exactly one root on
(0,+\infty)
. Indeed, the function is non-negative close to
0
, because of the positivity of
P_{1}
, and is negative for sufficiently large
t
, so there exists the smallest
t_{0}>0
such that
P_{1}(t_{0})={\lambda}_{k}t_{0}^{k}
. As
P_{1}
has non-negative coefficients and is of degree at most
k-1
, for all
c>1
, we have

P_{1}(ct)-{\lambda}_{k}(ct)^{k}\leq c^{k-1}P_{1}(t)-c^{k}{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}<c^{k}(P_{1}(t)-{\lambda}_{k}t^{k})=0\,.

Therefore,
t_{0}
is the unique root of the function
t\mapsto P_{1}(t)-{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}
on
(0,+\infty)
. In particular, for
t\geq t_{0}
we have
P_{1}(t)\leq{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}
. For each
t\in[t_{0},T]
, we then have

0\leq P(t)=P_{1}(t)+{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}-P_{2}(t)\leq 2{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}-P_{2}(t)\,,

i.e.,
P_{2}(t)\leq 2{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}
. Note that
P_{2}
is of degree lower than

, and has non-negative coefficients. Therefore

P_{2}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)\leq 4^{k-1}{\varepsilon}^{1-k}P_{2}(t)\leq 2\cdot 4^{k-1}{\varepsilon}^{1-k}{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}=\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2}{\lambda}_{k}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)^{k}\,.

Thus,

P_{1}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)-P_{2}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)+{\varepsilon}{\lambda}_{k}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)^{k}\geq P_{1}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)+\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2}{\lambda}_{k}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)^{k}\geq\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2^{k}}\left(P_{1}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)+{\lambda}_{k}(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t)^{k}\right)\,.

Equivalently

P_{1}(t)-P_{2}(t)+{\varepsilon}{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\geq\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2^{k}}\left(P_{1}(t)+{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\right)\,\quad\text{for $t\in[4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t_{0},T]$.}

(3.6)

Let us remark that we interpret interval
[s,\tau]
as
\emptyset
whenever
s>\tau
.

Now, let us take
t_{1}
to be equal to
t_{0}
if
t_{0}\in[0,T]
, and to
T
otherwise. Recall that
P_{1}(t)\geq{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}
for

. We now focus on
t\leq t_{1}
. Then,
0\leq P(t)\leq 2P_{1}(t)-P_{2}(t)
, which implies
P_{2}(t)\leq 2P_{1}(t)
. Let
\ell
be the degree of polynomial
P_{1}
, so that
{\lambda}_{l}\geq 0
and
{\lambda}_{\ell+1},\dots{\lambda}_{k-1}<0

-\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}t^{i}\leq P_{2}(t)\leq 2P_{1}(t)\,.

(3.7)

On the right-hand side, we have a polynomial of order

-\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}(ct)^{i}\leq c^{\ell+1}\left(-\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}t^{i}\right)\leq 2c^{\ell+1}P_{1}(t)\leq 2cP_{1}(ct)\,.

-\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}t^{i}\leq\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2^{k}}P_{1}(t)\,.

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}{\lambda}_{i}t^{i}-\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}t^{i}+{\varepsilon}{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}

(3.9)

Together, (

t\not\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}[{s}_{i},{\tau}_{i}]\cup\left[\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2^{k+1}}t_{1},\min(4{\varepsilon}^{-1}t_{1},T)\right]\implies P_{1}(t)-P_{2}(t)+{\varepsilon}{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\geq\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2^{k}}\left(P_{1}(t)+{\lambda}_{k}t^{k}\right)

We also prove the following minor lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be an open interval and let the function

\ell

, and let

We proceed by induction with respect to

Assume that the statement holds for some

\ell+1

The following lemma says that if some derivative of the weight is almost constant on some ball, then an integral resembling the one appearing in the Muckenhoupt condition on this ball can be controlled.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
B\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be a ball of radius

, let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1]

{K}>0
such that for all

{\varepsilon}{K}\leq D^{\ell}a(z)[v^{\ell}]\leq{K}\,.

(3.10)

Then, for every
{\beta}>\ell
, there exists a constant
c=c(\beta,N,{\varepsilon})
such that

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}a^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{{\beta}}\leq c{K}^{-1}r^{-\ell}\,.

The result is trivial when
\ell=0
. Let us thus assume that
\ell\geq 1
.
We take a cube
Q
with the same center as
B
, sidelength of
2r
, and all its sides either orthogonal or parallel to

. Note that
B\subseteq Q\subseteq\sqrt{N}B
, which gives

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}a^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{{\beta}}\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}{\beta}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}a^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{{\beta}}\,.

(3.11)

Let
W
be a set of orthogonal vectors to
v
, with all coordinates lesser than
r
. For any vector
w\in W
, let us consider an interval
V_{w}=[x+w-rv,x+w+rv]
. Note that

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}a^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}z=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{W}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{V_{w}}a^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}s\right)\,{\,{\rm d}}w\,.

(3.12)

Let us fix
w\in W
and consider a function

a_{w}(t)\coloneqq a(w+x+tv)\text{ for }|t|\leq r\,.

Observe that
a_{w}^{(\ell)}(t)=D^{\ell}a(w+x+tv)[v^{\ell}]
, and thus, by an assumption, we have

{\varepsilon}{K}\leq a_{w}^{(\ell)}(t)\leq{K}\,.

In particular,
a_{w}^{(\ell)}
is strictly positive on interval
[-r,r]
. By Lemma
3.3
,
a_{w}
restricted to
[-r,r]
has at most
\ell+1
local minima, possibly including the endpoints of
[-r,r]
. Let us denote those minima by
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{m}
,
m\leq l+1
, and let us also take numbers
{s}_{1},\dots,{s}_{m+1}
such that

-r={s}_{1}\leq t_{1}\leq{s}_{2}\leq t_{2}\leq\dots\leq t_{m}\leq{s}_{m+1}=r

and
a_{w}
is decreasing on
[{s}_{i},t_{i}]
and increasing on
[t_{i},{s}_{i+1}]
, for every
i=1,\dots,m
. We fix such an
i
. Taking any
h
such that
h\leq{s}_{i+1}-t_{i}
, using Taylor expansion and (
3.10
) we observe that

\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{a_{w}^{(j)}(t_{i})h^{j}}{j!}+\frac{{\varepsilon}{K}h^{\ell}}{\ell!}\leq a_{w}(t_{i}+h)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{a_{w}^{(j)}(t_{i})h^{j}}{j!}+\frac{{K}h^{\ell}}{\ell!}\,.

(3.13)

As
a_{w}(t_{i})\leq a_{w}(t_{i}+h)
, the second inequality of (
3.13
) implies

0\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\frac{a_{w}(t_{i})h^{j}}{j!}+\frac{{K}h^{\ell}}{\ell!}\,.

(3.14)

Lemma
3.2
may be thus applied to the right-hand side of (
3.14
) on interval
[0,s_{i+1}-t_{i}]
. In turn, we get that there exists a finite family of pairwise disjoint intervals
[\tilde{s}_{i},\tilde{\tau}_{i}]_{i=1}^{m_{2}}
such that
{\tau}_{i}\leq c_{\ell,{\varepsilon}}{s}_{i}
and

h\not\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{m_{2}}[\tilde{s}_{i},\tilde{\tau}_{i}]\quad\implies\quad a_{w}(t_{i}+h)\geq\frac{{\varepsilon}{K}h^{\ell}}{2^{k}\ell!}\,,

(3.15)

where we also used the first inequality of (
3.13
). By monotonicity of
a_{w}
, if
a_{w}(t_{i}+h_{0})\geq Ch_{0}^{\ell}
, then for
h\in[h_{0},\min\left(c_{\ell,{\varepsilon}}h_{0},{s}_{i+1}-t_{i}\right)]
, it holds

a_{w}(t_{i}+h)\geq a_{w}(t_{i}+h_{0})\geq Ch_{0}^{\ell}\geq\frac{C}{c_{\ell,{\varepsilon}}^{\ell}}h^{\ell}\,.

This together with (
3.15
) means that for some
c=c(\beta,{\varepsilon})>0
, it holds

a_{w}(t_{i}+h)\geq\frac{{K}h^{\ell}}{c}\text{ for }h\leq{s}_{i+1}-t_{i}\,.

In particular,

\int_{t_{i}}^{{s}_{i+1}}a_{w}^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}s\leq cK^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}\int_{t_{i}}^{{s}_{i+1}}h^{-\frac{l}{\beta}}{\,{\rm d}}h\leq c{K}^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}({s}_{i+1}-t_{i})^{1-\frac{\ell}{{\beta}}}\leq c{K}^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}r^{1-\frac{\ell}{{\beta}}}\,.

(3.16)

With a similar reasoning, one can also establish that

\int_{{s}_{i}}^{t_{i}}a_{w}^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}s\leq c{K}^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}r^{1-\frac{\ell}{{\beta}}}\,.

(3.17)

Summing over all
i
inequalities (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), we get

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{V_{w}}a_{w}^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}s\leq 2mc{K}^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}r^{-\frac{\ell}{{\beta}}}\leq 2(\ell+1)c{K}^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}r^{-\frac{\ell}{{\beta}}}\,,

which, by (
3.12
), gives

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}a^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{{\beta}}\leq c{K}^{-1}r^{-\ell}\,,

Applying (
3.11
), we get the desired result.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the most crucial proposition for the proof of Theorem
3
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
a\in C^{k,\alpha}({U})
be non-negative function, where
{U}
is open and bounded subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
. We define function
{\omega}:{U}\to[0,1]
as

{\omega}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{a(x)}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}a(x)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-i}}}\,&\text{if }\|D^{i}a(x)\|\neq 0\text{ for some $i$;}\\
1\,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.18)

Then, for any open
\Omega
such that
\Omega\Subset{U}
, it holds
{\omega}\in A_{k+\alpha+1}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We define function
{\sigma}
as in (
3.1
), so that
a={\sigma}{\omega}
. Let
{\Omega^{\prime}}
be any open set such that
\Omega\Subset{\Omega^{\prime}}\Subset{U}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have that
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{k+\alpha}({\Omega^{\prime}})
, i.e., there exists a constant
{C_{\sigma}}\geq 1
such that for all
x,y\in{\Omega^{\prime}}
it holds

{\sigma}(x)\leq{C_{\sigma}}\left({\sigma}(y)+|x-y|^{k+\alpha}\right)\,.

(3.19)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
[D^{k}a]_{C^{0,\alpha}({U})}\leq{C_{\sigma}}
.
Let us now define a sequence
\{{C}_{n}\}_{n}
recursively via

{C}_{0}=\sqrt{N}\,,\quad{C}_{n+1}=4\cdot 3^{n+1}((n+1)!)\cdot\left(\prod_{i=0}^{n}{C}_{i}\right)+1\,.

(3.20)

We also define numbers
\{{d}_{n}\}_{n}
by the formula

{d}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\,,\quad{d}_{n+1}=\frac{1}{2}\min_{i\leq n}\frac{\alpha{d}_{i}}{n+1+\alpha-i}\,.

Let us now define constant
{\tilde{c}}={\tilde{c}}(\Omega,{\Omega^{\prime}},\|{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},k,\alpha,{C_{\sigma}})
by the following formula

\displaystyle{\tilde{c}}\coloneqq\min\Biggl\{

\displaystyle\frac{\dist(\Omega,\partial\Omega^{\prime})}{{C}_{k}\left(\|{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}+1\right)},\left(\frac{1}{2{C_{\sigma}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}},\frac{1}{2{C}_{k}(k+1)(2\cdot 3^{k}{C_{\sigma}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}},\left(\frac{1}{4{C_{\sigma}}(k+1)(4{C_{\sigma}}k!)^{\frac{k+\alpha}{\alpha}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha{d}_{k}}},

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\dist(\Omega,\partial{\Omega^{\prime}})}{({C}_{k}+2k!)\left(\|{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}+1\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{{d}_{k}}},2^{-\frac{1}{(1-\alpha){d}_{k}}},\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-d_{k}}}\Biggr\}\,.

For any
x\in\Omega
, we define

R_{x}\coloneqq{\tilde{c}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}\,.

(3.21)

From the choice of
{\tilde{c}}
, we have that
B(x,{C}_{k}R_{x})\subseteq{\Omega^{\prime}}
for any
x\in\Omega
, where
{C}_{k}
is defined in (
3.20
), while
k
comes from the statement.

Step 1: Muckenhoupt condition on small balls.
Let us take any ball
B=B(x,r)
, where
x\in\Omega
and assume that there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
B\subseteq B(x_{0},R_{x_{0}})
. In particular,
{\sigma}(x_{0})>0
and
r\leq R_{x_{0}}
. Note that for any
y\in B\left(x_{0},\left({\sigma}(x_{0})/(2{C_{\sigma}})\right)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}\right)\cap{\Omega^{\prime}}
, we have, due to (
3.1
), that

\frac{{\sigma}(x_{0})}{2{C_{\sigma}}}\leq{\sigma}(y)\leq 2{C_{\sigma}}{\sigma}(x_{0})\,.

(3.22)

In particular, (
3.22
) holds for every
y\in B
, due to
B\subseteq B(x_{0},R_{x_{0}})
and the choice of
{\tilde{c}}
. Therefore,

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}{\omega}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}{\omega}^{-\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{k+\alpha}=\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}\frac{a}{{\sigma}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}\left(\frac{{\sigma}}{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{k+\alpha}\leq 4{C_{\sigma}}^{2}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}a{\,{\rm d}}z\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}a^{-\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{k+\alpha}\,.

(3.23)

Thus, it is sufficient to establish the Muckenhoupt condition on the ball
B
only for the function
a
. Note that (
3.22
) also implies that
{\sigma}(x)>0
.

We now take the smallest
\ell\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
such that

\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\geq 2{C}_{\ell}r\sup_{z\in{C}_{\ell+1}B}\|D^{\ell+1}a(z)\|\,,

(3.24)

or, if there is no such number, we take
\ell=k
. Note that by the choice of
{\tilde{c}}
, we have that
{C}_{i}B\subseteq B(x_{0},{C}_{i}R_{x_{0}})\subseteq{\Omega^{\prime}}
for every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
. We denote

{K}\coloneqq\sup_{z\in{C}_{\ell}B}\|D^{\ell}a(z)\|\,.

Note that
{K}\neq 0
. Indeed, if
{K}=0
, then
\ell
-th derivative of
a
vanishes on
B
, and thus also derivatives of higher order vanish on
B
. On the other hand, by the definition of
\ell
, we have that
D^{i}a(x)=0
for every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,\ell-1\}
. Thus, we have that
{\sigma}(x)=0
, but this is a contradiction. Therefore,
{K}>0
.

The definition of
\ell
immediately gives that
\|D^{\ell-1}a(x)\|\leq 2{C}_{\ell-1}r{K}
, and further

\sup_{z\in{C}_{\ell-1}B}\|D^{\ell-1}a(z)\|\leq\sup_{z\in{C}_{\ell-1}B}\|D^{\ell-1}a(z)-D^{\ell-1}a(x)\|+\|D^{\ell-1}a(x)\|\leq{C}_{\ell-1}r\sup_{z\in{C}_{\ell-1}B}\|D^{\ell}a(z)\|+2{C}_{\ell-1}r{K}\leq 3{C}_{\ell-1}r{K}\,.

By an easy induction, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{\ell-i}a(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 3^{i-1}\left(\prod_{j=\ell-i}^{\ell-1}{C}_{j}\right)r^{i}{K}\quad\text{ and }

(3.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in C_{\ell-i}B}\|D^{\ell-i}a(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq 3^{i}\left(\prod_{j=\ell-i}^{\ell-1}{C}_{j}\right)r^{i}{K}\quad\text{ for $i=0,\dots,\ell$.}

(3.26)

Let us remark that we interpret an empty product as equal to
1
.

We shall now prove that for any
y\in{C}_{\ell}B
, it holds

\|D^{\ell}a(x)-D^{\ell}a(y)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\quad\text{ and }\quad\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\geq\frac{1}{2}{K}\,.

(3.27)

If
\ell<k
, then we simply use (
3.24
) to conclude that

\|D^{\ell}a(x)-D^{\ell}a(y)\|\leq{C}_{\ell}r\sup_{z\in{C}_{\ell}B}\|D^{\ell+1}a(z)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\,.

(3.28)

From (
3.28
) and the definition of
{K}
, it also follows that

{K}\leq\sup_{z\in{C}_{\ell}B}\|D^{\ell}a(z)-D^{\ell}a(x)\|+\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\leq\frac{3}{2}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\,.

Inequalities (
3.27
) thus follow if
\ell<k
. Let us now assume that
\ell=k
. Then, (
3.22
) would follow immediately from
D^{k}a\in C^{0,\alpha}({U})
, if we establish that
\|D^{k}a(x)\|\geq 2{C_{\sigma}}({C}_{k}r)^{\alpha}
. Suppose that
\|D^{k}a(x)\|<2{C_{\sigma}}({C}_{k}r)^{\alpha}
. Then, using that
D^{k}a\in C^{0,\alpha}({U})
, we get
{K}\leq 3{C_{\sigma}}({C}_{k}r)^{\alpha}
, and from (
3.25
), we have for every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
that

\|D^{k-i}a(x)\|<2\cdot 3^{i}\left(\prod_{j=k-i}^{k-1}{C}_{j}\right)r^{i+\alpha}{C_{\sigma}}{C}_{k}^{\alpha}\leq 2\cdot 3^{i}{C_{\sigma}}({C}_{k}r)^{i+\alpha}

As
B\subseteq B(x_{0},R_{x_{0}})
, and we have (
3.22
), we have

r\leq R_{x_{0}}={\tilde{c}}{\sigma}(x_{0})^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}\leq{\tilde{c}}\left(2{C_{\sigma}}{\sigma}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}\,.

Thus, using the choice of
{\tilde{c}}
, we get

{\sigma}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{k-i}a(x)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha}{\alpha+i}}<\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(2\cdot 3^{i}{C_{\sigma}}\right)^{\frac{k+\alpha}{\alpha+i}}({C}_{k}r)^{k+\alpha}\leq(k+1)\left(2\cdot 3^{k}{C_{\sigma}}\right)^{\frac{k+\alpha}{\alpha}}{C}_{k}^{k+\alpha}{\tilde{c}}^{k+\alpha}2{C_{\sigma}}{\sigma}(x)\leq\frac{{\sigma}(x)}{2}\,,

which is clearly a contradiction. Therefore,
\|D^{k}a(x)\|\geq 2{C_{\sigma}}({C}_{k}r)^{\alpha}
, and (
3.27
) follows.

Take a vector
v\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
such that
|v|=1
and
|D^{\ell}a(x)[v^{\ell}]|=\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|
. We shall prove that
D^{\ell}a(x)[v^{\ell}]=\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|
. Suppose that
D^{\ell}a(x)[v^{\ell}]=-\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|
. Then, there exists
\xi\in[x,x+{C}_{\ell}rv]
such that

\displaystyle a(x+{C}_{\ell}rv)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{({C}_{\ell}r)^{\ell-i}(D^{\ell-i}a(x)[v^{\ell-i}])}{(\ell-i)!}+\frac{({C}_{\ell}r)^{\ell}(D^{\ell}a(\xi)[v^{\ell}])}{\ell!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{({C}_{\ell}r)^{\ell-i}(D^{\ell-i}a(x)[v^{\ell-i}])}{(\ell-i)!}-\frac{({C}_{\ell}r)^{\ell}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|}{\ell!}+\frac{({C}_{\ell}r)^{\ell}((D^{\ell}a(\xi)-D^{\ell}a(x))[v^{\ell}])}{\ell!}\,.

(3.29)

Using that
a
is non-negative, (
3.27
), (
3.25
) and (
3
), we get

\displaystyle 0\leq a(x+{C}_{\ell}rv)

\displaystyle\leq\ell{C}_{\ell}^{\ell-1}3^{\ell}r^{\ell}{K}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}{C}_{j}\right)-\frac{({C}_{\ell}r)^{\ell}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|}{\ell!}+\frac{({C}_{\ell}r)^{\ell}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|}{2\ell!}

\displaystyle\leq\ell{C}_{\ell}^{\ell-1}3^{\ell}r^{\ell}M\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}{C}_{j}\right)-\frac{({C}_{\ell}r)^{\ell}{K}}{4\ell!}={K}{C}_{\ell}^{\ell-1}r^{\ell}\left(\ell 3^{\ell}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}{C}_{j}\right)-\frac{{C}_{\ell}}{4\ell!}\right)<0\,,

where the last inequality is a consequence of the choice of
{C}_{\ell}
. We have a clear contradiction, therefore,
|D^{\ell}a(x)[v^{\ell}]|=\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|
.

By (
3.27
) and the fact that
{C}_{0}\geq\sqrt{N}
, for all
z\in\sqrt{N}B
it holds

\frac{1}{4}{K}\leq D^{\ell}a(z)[v^{\ell}]\leq{K}\,.

Applying Lemma
3.4
and (
3.26
) with
i=\ell
, we finally obtain

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}a{\,{\rm d}}z\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}a^{-\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}{\,{\rm d}}z\right)^{k+\alpha}\leq 3^{\ell}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}{C}_{j}\right)r^{\ell}{K}\cdot c(k,N,\alpha)K^{-1}r^{-\ell}=c(k,N,\alpha)\,.

(3.30)

Step 2: More control over medium-sized balls.
Let
B=B(x,R_{x})
for some
x\in\Omega
, where
R_{x}
is defined as in (
3.21
). Let us take the smallest
\ell\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
such that

\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\geq 4{C_{\sigma}}\ell!{\tilde{c}}^{\alpha{d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{k+\alpha-\ell}{k+\alpha}}\,.

(3.31)

Note that such
\ell
has to exist. Indeed, suppose that (
3.31
) fails for all
\ell
. Then

{\sigma}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}a(x)\|^{\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-i}}<\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(4{C_{\sigma}}i!{\tilde{c}}^{\alpha{d}_{i}}\right)^{\frac{k+\alpha}{k+\alpha-i}}{\sigma}(x)\leq(k+1)\left(4k!{C_{\sigma}}\right)^{\frac{k+\alpha}{\alpha}}{\tilde{c}}^{\alpha{d}_{k}}{\sigma}(x)\leq\frac{{\sigma}(x)}{2}\,,

where the last inequality comes from the choice of
{\tilde{c}}
. We obtained a clear contradiction, so the chosen
\ell
exists.

Observe that, due to the choice of
{\tilde{c}}
, we have
B(x,2\ell!{\tilde{c}}^{{d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}})\subseteq{\Omega^{\prime}}
. We shall now prove that for every
y\in B(x,2\ell!{\tilde{c}}^{{d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}})
, it holds

\|D^{\ell}a(x)-D^{\ell}a(y)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\,.

(3.32)

If
\ell=k
, we use the fact that
D^{k}a\in C^{0,\alpha}({U})
and (
3.31
) to estimate

\|D^{k}a(x)-D^{k}a(y)\|\leq{C_{\sigma}}\left(2k!{\tilde{c}}^{{d}_{k}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}(k!)^{\alpha-1}\|D^{k}a(x)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D^{k}a(x)\|

for all
y\in B(x,2\ell!{\tilde{c}}^{{d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}})
. Therefore, we get (
3.32
) for
\ell=k
. If
\ell<k
, let us observe that, due to the fact that
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{k+\alpha}({\Omega^{\prime}})
, we have

\sup_{y\in B(x,2\ell!{\tilde{c}}^{{d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}})}{\sigma}(y)\leq{C_{\sigma}}\left({\sigma}(x)+(2\ell!)^{k+\alpha}{\tilde{c}}^{(k+\alpha){d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)\right)\leq{C_{\sigma}}{\sigma}(x)\left(1+(2k!)^{k+\alpha}{\tilde{c}}^{(k+\alpha){d}_{k}}\right)\leq 2{C_{\sigma}}{\sigma}(x)\,,

(3.33)

where in the last inequality we used the choice of
{\tilde{c}}
. Using (
3.33
) in conjunction with the definition of
{\sigma}
, we get

\sup_{y\in B(x,2\ell!{\tilde{c}}^{{d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}})}\|D^{i}a(y)\|\leq\left(2{C_{\sigma}}{\sigma}(x)\right)^{\frac{k+\alpha-i}{k+\alpha}}\leq 2{C_{\sigma}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{k+\alpha-i}{k+\alpha}}\,,

(3.34)

which applied for
i=\ell+1
, together with (
3.31
) gives, for all
y\in B(x,2\ell!{\tilde{c}}^{{d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}})
, that

\|D^{\ell}a(x)-D^{\ell}a(y)\|\leq 2\ell!{\tilde{c}}^{{d}_{\ell}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{1}{k+\alpha}}2{C_{\sigma}}{\sigma}(x)^{\frac{k+\alpha-\ell-1}{k+\alpha}}\leq{\tilde{c}}^{(1-\alpha){d}_{\ell}}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|\,,

where in the last inequality we used the choice of
{\tilde{c}}
. We thus established (
3.32
).

Let us now take a vector
v\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
such that
|v|=1
and
|D^{\ell}a(x)[v^{\ell}]|=\|D^{\ell}a(x)\|
. We shall prove that

4.1
Proof of Theorem
4

The proof is essentially based on a well-established technique of convolution with squeezing, employed, for instance, in
[
11
,
12
,
16
]
. The proof of Theorem
4
is built upon the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 3]
, with the additional steps taking advantage of the Muckenhoupt part of the weight. Namely, we use the fact that the maximal operator is bounded on the weighted Sobolev space built upon a weight satisfying the Muckenhoupt condition.

We shall briefly recall the results on boundedness of the maximal operator in weighted Lebesgue spaces. For every open and bounded set
{U}
, we denote by
M_{{U}}
the maximal operator associated to the set
{U}
, defined as

M_{{U}}f(x)\coloneqq\sup_{B:x\in B,3B\subset{U}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}|f|{\,{\rm d}}y\,,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
containing
x
and such that
3B\subseteq{U}
.

For a given
{\omega}\in A_{r}({U})
,
r\in(1,\infty)
, we define weighted Lebesgue space as

L^{r}({U},{\omega})\coloneqq\left\{f:{U}\to{\mathbb{R}}\text{ measurable, }\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{r}{\omega}(x){\,{\rm d}}x<\infty\right\}\,,

with norm defined as

\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega,{\omega})}\coloneqq\left(\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{r}{\omega}(x){\,{\rm d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\,.

From
[
8
, Theorem 9]
, we know that

{\omega}\in A_{r}({U})\implies M_{{U}}\text{ is bounded on }L^{r}({U},w)\,.

(4.1)

In our approximation, we employ a convolution with shrinking. Let us fix an open set
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, which is star-shaped with respect to a ball
B(x_{0},R)
. We fix
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta>0
. Let
\rho_{\delta}
be a standard regularizing kernel on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, i.e.,
\rho_{\delta}(x)=\tfrac{1}{\delta^{n}}\rho(x/\delta)
, where
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
,
\supp\rho\Subset B(0,1)
,
0\leq\rho\leq 1
, and
\|\rho\|_{L^{1}}=1
. Let us define
\kappa_{\delta}=1-\frac{\delta}{R}
. For any measurable function
f:{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
, we define function
S_{\delta}^{\Omega}f:{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
via

S_{\delta}^{\Omega}f(x)\coloneqq\int_{B(x,\delta)}\rho_{\delta}(x-y)f\left(x_{0}+\frac{y-x_{0}}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right){\,{\rm d}}y=\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}\rho_{\delta}(y)f\left(x_{0}+\frac{x-y-x_{0}}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right){\,{\rm d}}y\,.

By elementary calculations, if
f
vanishes outside
\Omega
, then
S_{\delta}^{\Omega}f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
\delta\in(0,R/4)
. Moreover, for any
u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

\nabla S_{\delta}^{\Omega}u=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\delta}}S_{\delta}^{\Omega}(\nabla u)\,.

We shall use the following properties of
S_{\delta}^{\Omega}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
12
]
)
.

If
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, then
S_{\delta}f
converges to
f
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, and so in measure, as
\delta\to 0
.

Lemma 4.2

(Lemma 3.3 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega)
.

1.

If
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then

\|\nabla S_{\delta}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\delta^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{1}}\,.

(4.2)

2.

If
u\in C^{0,\gamma}(\Omega)
, then

\|\nabla S_{\delta}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{\delta^{\gamma-1}}{\kappa_{\delta}^{\gamma}}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{1}}\,.

(4.3)

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
4
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Given any
u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
, one can use a standard truncation argument to approximate
u
by a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that (
2.1
) holds. Indeed, it is sufficient to combine the Lebesgue Monotone Theorem with the fact that the gradient of the truncation at every point coincides with either
0
or the gradient of the original function. With the use of diagonal argument, we can thus assume that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
from now on.

Let us assume that
\Omega
is a star-shaped domain with respect to the ball
B_{R}(0)
and denote
u_{\delta}\coloneqq S_{\delta}^{\Omega}u
. By properties of
S_{\delta}^{\Omega}
,
u_{\delta}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
for sufficiently small
\delta
. From Lemma
4.1
, we also have that
\|u_{\delta}-u\|_{W^{1,1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0
. We shall moreover prove that

\left\{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{p}+a|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{q}\right\}_{\delta}\text{ is equiintegrable.}

We shall work on both cases i) and ii) simultaneously. In case of i), we shall denote
\gamma=0
and momentarily also assume that
p\leq N
. Consequently, in both cases, the condition on
p
and
q
reads
q\leq p+\frac{k+\alpha}{1-\gamma}
. We further denote
\vartheta\coloneqq(q-p)(1-\gamma)
and
\tau\coloneqq\frac{2+\diam\Omega}{R}
. Then, for any
\delta\leq\tfrac{R}{2}
,
x\in\Omega
and
y\in B_{\delta}(0)
, we have

\left|\frac{x-y}{\kappa_{\delta}}-x\right|\leq\frac{|y|}{\kappa_{\delta}}+\frac{|x|(1-\kappa_{\delta})}{\kappa_{\delta}}\leq\frac{2\delta}{R}+\frac{(\diam\Omega)\delta}{R}=\tau\delta\,.

(4.4)

Using that
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{\vartheta}(\Omega)
, we have that there exists a constant
{C_{\sigma}}
such that whenever
|x-y|\leq\tau\delta
, it holds

{\sigma}(x)\leq C_{{\sigma}}({\sigma}(y)+\tau^{\vartheta}\delta^{\vartheta})\,.

(4.5)

Let us now fix any
x\in\Omega
and
\delta\leq\tfrac{R}{2}
. Let

{\sigma}^{-}_{\delta}\coloneqq\inf_{y\in B_{\delta}(0)}{\sigma}\left(\frac{x-y}{\kappa_{\delta}}+x\right)\,.

By (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we have

{\sigma}(x)\leq{C_{\sigma}}({\sigma}^{-}_{\delta}+\tau^{\vartheta}\delta^{\vartheta})\,.

Therefore, we can estimate

a(x)|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{q}={\sigma}(x){\omega}(x)|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{q}\leq{\sigma}^{-}_{\delta}{\omega}(x)|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{q}+\|{\omega}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\tau^{\vartheta}\delta^{\vartheta}|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{q}\,.

(4.6)

By Lemma
4.2
, there exists a constant
c
, depending only on
\rho
and
u
, such that

\|{\omega}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\tau^{\vartheta}\delta^{\vartheta}|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{q}\leq\|{\omega}\|_{L^{\infty}}\tau^{\vartheta}\delta^{\vartheta}\|\nabla u_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{q-p}|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{p}\leq c\tau^{\vartheta}|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{p}\,.

Note that, by Jensen’s inequality, it holds

|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{p}=\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}\left|\nabla u\left(\frac{x-y}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right)\right|\rho_{\delta}(y){\,{\rm d}}y\right)^{p}\leq\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}\left|\nabla u\left(\frac{x-y}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right)\right|^{p}\rho_{\delta}(y){\,{\rm d}}y=S_{\delta}^{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{p})\,.

(4.7)

As
{\mathcal{F}}[u]<\infty
, we have
|\nabla u|^{p}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, so by Lemma
4.1
and Vitali Convergence Theorem, we get equiintegrability of the family
\{S_{\delta}^{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{p})\}_{\delta}
. By (
4.7
), we have that

\text{the family }\left\{|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{p}\right\}_{\delta}\text{ is equiintegrable.}

(4.8)

On the other hand, by the definition of
u_{\delta}
and
{\sigma}^{-}_{\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle{\sigma}^{-}_{\delta}{\omega}(x)|\nabla u_{\delta}(x)|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq{\sigma}^{-}_{\delta}{\omega}(x)\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}\left|\nabla u\left(\frac{x-y}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right)\right|\rho_{\delta}(y){\,{\rm d}}y\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq{\omega}(x)\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{x-y}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right)\left|\nabla u\left(\frac{x-y}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right)\right|\rho_{\delta}(y){\,{\rm d}}y\right)^{q}={\omega}(x)\left(S_{\delta}^{\Omega}\left({\sigma}^{\frac{1}{q}}|\nabla u|\right)(x)\right)^{q}\,.

(4.9)

Let us denote
{f}\coloneqq{\sigma}^{1/q}|\nabla u|
. From the definition of
S_{\delta}^{\Omega}
and
\rho_{\delta}\leq\delta^{-N}
, we get

\displaystyle S_{\delta}^{\Omega}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x,\delta)}\rho_{\delta}(x-y)f\left(\frac{y}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right){\,{\rm d}}y\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{N}}\int_{B(x,\delta)}f\left(\frac{y}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right){\,{\rm d}}y=\frac{\kappa_{\delta}^{N}}{\delta^{N}}\int_{B(x/\kappa_{\delta},\delta/\kappa_{\delta})}f(y){\,{\rm d}}y\,.

(4.10)

From (
4.4
), we infer that
|x-x/\kappa_{\delta}|\leq\tau\delta
for sufficiently small
\delta
. Thus, for sufficiently small
\delta
, we have

B\left(\frac{x}{\kappa_{\delta}},\frac{\delta}{\kappa_{\delta}}\right)\subseteq B\left(\frac{x}{\kappa_{\delta}},2\delta\right)\subseteq B\left(x,(\tau+2)\delta\right)\,.

In turn, by (
4.10
), we have

S_{\delta}^{\Omega}f(x)\leq\frac{\kappa_{\delta}^{N}}{\delta^{N}}\int_{B(x,(\tau+2)\delta)}f(y){\,{\rm d}}y\leq\frac{2^{N}(\tau+2)^{N}}{|B_{1}|}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,(\tau+2)\delta)}f(y){\,{\rm d}}y

(4.11)

Recall that
{\omega}\in A_{q}({U})
and
\Omega\Subset{U}
. Taking any
\delta
such that (
4.11
) holds and additionally
\delta\leq\frac{3\dist(\Omega,\partial{U})}{\tau+2}
, we get

S_{\delta}^{\Omega}f(x)\leq cM_{{U}}f(x)\,,

where

. Note that by (
4.1
) we have that
{\omega}(M_{{U}}f)^{q}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. From (
4.1
), we obtain that

\text{the family }\left\{{\sigma}^{-}_{\delta}{\omega}|\nabla u_{\delta}|\right\}_{\delta}\text{ is equiintegrable.}

(4.12)

From the estimate (
4.6
) and lines (
4.8
) and (
4.12
), we get that the family

is equiintegrable. By Lemma

, we also have that

converges to
\nabla u
in measure, which by Vitali Convegence Theorem means that

. This finishes the proof in the case of

being a star-shaped domain with respect to a ball centred in

and, in the case i), with the additional assumption that

.

If one starts from
\Omega
, which is a star-shaped domain with respect to the ball not centred in

, one can simply translate the whole problem so that
\Omega
is star-shaped with respect to a ball centred in
0
.

being an arbitrary bounded Lipschitz domain. From
[
22
, Lemma 8.2]
, we infer that set
\overline{\Omega}
can be covered by a finite family of sets

By the previous paragraph, for every

is a star-shaped domain with respect to some ball,

, and

, there exist a sequence

(4.14)

\{\varphi_{n,k}\}_{k}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

{\mathcal{F}}[\varphi_{n,k};\Omega]\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}{\mathcal{F}}[u_{n};\Omega]\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\varphi_{n,k}-u_{n}\|_{W^{1,1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

In particular, the sequence
\{|\nabla\varphi_{n,k}|^{p}+a|\nabla\varphi_{n,k}|^{q}\}_{k}
is equiintegrable. As we have

|\nabla(\varphi_{n,k}+u_{0})|^{p}+a|\nabla(\varphi_{n,k}+u_{0})|^{q}\leq 2^{q}\left(|\nabla\varphi_{n,k}|^{p}+a|\nabla\varphi_{n,k}|^{q}+|\nabla u_{0}|^{p}+a|\nabla u_{0}|^{q}\right)\,,

the sequence
\{|\nabla(\varphi_{n,k}+u_{0})|^{p}+a|\nabla(\varphi_{n,k}+u_{0})|^{q}\}_{k}
is also equiintegrable. Moreover, it converges in measure to
|\nabla(u_{n}+u_{0})|^{p}+a|\nabla(u_{n}+u_{0})|^{q}
. This means that

{\mathcal{F}}[\varphi_{n,k}+u_{0};\Omega]\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}{\mathcal{F}}[u_{n}+u_{0};\Omega]\,

Therefore,

\inf_{u\in u_{0}+W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]\leq\inf_{u\in u_{0}+C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]\leq\inf_{n,k}{\mathcal{F}}[\varphi_{n,k}+u_{0};\Omega]\leq\inf_{n}{\mathcal{F}}[u_{n}+u_{0};\Omega]\leq\inf_{u\in u_{0}+W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{F}}[u;\Omega]\,,

which finishes the proof.
∎

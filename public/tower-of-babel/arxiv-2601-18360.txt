Polyhedral results for two classes of submodular sets with GUB constraints
Weikang Qian, Keyan Li, Wei-Kun Chen, Yu-Hong Dai

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the polyhedral structure of two submodular sets with generalized upper bound (GUB) constraints, which arise as important substructures in various real-world applications. We derive a class of strong valid inequalities for the two sets using sequential lifting techniques. The proposed lifted inequalities are facet-defining for the convex hulls of two sets and are stronger than the well-known extended polymatroid inequalities (EPIs). We provide a more compact characterization of these inequalities and show that each of them can be computed in linear time. Moreover, the proposed lifted inequalities, together with bound and GUB constraints, can completely characterize the convex hulls of the two sets, and can be separated using a combinatorial polynomial-time algorithm. Finally, computational results on probabilistic covering location and multiple probabilistic knapsack problems demonstrate the superiority of the proposed lifted inequalities over the EPIs within a branch-and-cut framework. Keywords: Submodular set ⋅ \cdot GUB constraints ⋅ \cdot Sequential lifting ⋅ \cdot Polyhedral approach ⋅ \cdot Branch-and-cut

Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the polyhedral structure of the epigraph of a concave function composed with a non-negative linear function under generalized upper bound (GUB) constraints:

X_{0}=\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\geq f(a^{\top}x),~\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in K\right\},

(1)

and its generalization with an additional linear function in the nonlinear constraint:

X=\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\geq f(a^{\top}x)+b^{\top}x,~\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in K\right\},

(2)

where
f\,:\,\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a concave function,

a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and

is a partition of
[n]

\bigcup_{k\in K}N_{k}=[n]
and
N_{k_{1}}\cap N_{k_{2}}=\varnothing
for any distinct
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
(throughout, for a non-negative integer
\tau

[\tau]:=\{1,2,\dots,\tau\}
with the convention that
[0]=\varnothing

The two sets

and

arise as important substructures in many real-world applications such as facility location

1.1
Minimum probabilistic covering location problem

Given a set of customers
I
with weight
v_{i}
for each
i\in I
and a set of candidate facility locations
J
, the minimal covering location problem (MCLP) attempts to locate a fixed number of “undesirable” facilities (such as waste treatment plants and nuclear power stations) while
minimizing the total weight of covered nodes
(
Church and Cohon 1976
;
Church and Drezner 2022
;
Murray et al. 1998
)
.

Karatas and Eriskin 2021
investigated a probabilistic variant of the MCLP, the minimum probabilistic covering location problem (MPCLP), which incorporates two key practical factors.
First, the customers are covered in a probabilistic behavior rather than a deterministic behavior (in the MCLP).
Second, in addition to determining the locations of open facilities, their types must also be determined.
Let
x_{js}\in\{0,1\}
be a binary variable denoting whether a facility of type
s\in S
is located at location
j\in J
. Then,
the MPCLP can be formulated as:

\!\!\!\!\min_{x\in\{0,1\}^{|J|\times|S|}}\left\{\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\left(1-\prod_{j\in J}\prod_{s\in S}(1-p_{ijs}x_{js})\right)\,:\,\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{s\in S}c_{s}x_{js}\geq t,~\sum_{s\in S}x_{js}\leq 1,~\forall~j\in J\right\},

(MPCLP)

where
p_{ijs}\in[0,1]
denotes the probability that customer
i
is covered by a facility of type
s
located at
j
;

1-\prod_{j\in J}\prod_{s\in S}(1-p_{ijs}x_{js})
denotes the probability that customer
i
is covered by at least one facility
(
Drezner and Wesolowsky 1997
;
Berman et al. 2003
;
Drezner and Drezner 2008
;
Karatas 2017
)
;
the objective function minimizes the expected total weight of covered customers;

c_{s}
denotes the service capacity of a facility of type
s
;
constraint
\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{s\in S}c_{s}x_{js}\geq t
requires that the total service capacity of the open facilities is larger than or equal to a predetermined threshold
t
;
and constraint
\sum_{s\in S}x_{js}\leq 1
enforces that at most one type
s\in S
of facility is opened at each location
j\in J
.

Let any customer
i\in I
be given and
assume that
p_{ijs}\in[0,1)
holds for all
j\in J
and
s\in S
.
From

(1-p_{ijs}x_{js})=(1-p_{ijs})^{x_{js}}=\text{exp }([\ln(1-p_{ijs})]x_{js})

By introducing a continuous variable

to represent the nonlinear term corresponding to customer

in the objective function of problem (

w_{i}\geq-\prod_{j\in J}\prod_{s\in S}(1-p_{ijs}x_{js})
), we obtain a substructure taking the form of

\!\!\!\!X^{i}_{0}=\left\{(w_{i},x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{|J|\times|S|}\,:\,w_{i}\geq f\left(\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{s\in S}[-\ln(1-p_{ijs})]x_{js}\right),~\sum_{s\in S}x_{js}\leq 1,~\forall~j\in J~\right\},

(3)

where
f(z):=-\text{exp }(-z)
is a concave function and
-\ln(1-p_{ijs})\geq 0

Note that if

w_{i}\geq f\left(\sum\limits_{(j,s)\in J\times S~\text{with}~p_{ijs}<1}[-\ln(1-p_{ijs})]x_{js}\right)
and

for
(j,s)\in J\times S

(
Karatas and Eriskin 2021
)
, and therefore, we can still obtain a substructure taking the form of
X_{0}
:

\displaystyle\bar{X}^{i}_{0}=\left\{(w_{i},x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{|J|\times|S|}\,:\,w_{i}\geq f\left(\sum\limits_{(j,s)\in J\times S~\text{with}~p_{ijs}<1}[-\ln(1-p_{ijs})]x_{js}\right),\right.

(4)

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{s\in S}x_{js}\leq 1,~\forall~j\in J~\right\}.

1.2
The multiple probabilistic knapsack problem with GUB constraints

Given a finite index set
N
, a probabilistic knapsack constraint on a binary vector
x\in\{0,1\}^{|N|}
is defined as

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i\in N}\tilde{a}_{i}x_{i}\leq b\right)\geq\rho,

(5)

where
\tilde{a}
is an
|N|
-dimensional random vector,
b\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\rho\in(0,1)
is a reliability level chosen by the decision-maker.
Such a constraint arises in diverse industrial applications including healthcare
(
Deng and Shen 2016
;
Wang et al. 2017
;
Zhang et al. 2018
)
, transportation
(
Dinh et al. 2018
)
, and scheduling
(
Cohen et al. 2019
;
Lu et al. 2021
)
.
Under the realistic assumptions that
\{\tilde{a}_{i}\}_{i\in N}
are independent normally distributed with mean
a_{i}
and variance
\sigma_{i}^{2}
for each
i\in N
, and
\rho>0.5

(
Goyal and Ravi 2010
;
Han et al. 2016
;
Joung and Park 2017
;
Joung and Lee 2020
)
,
the probabilistic knapsack constraint (
5
) can be equivalently reformulated as
\sum_{i\in N}a_{i}x_{i}+\Phi^{-1}(\rho)\sqrt{\sum_{i\in N}\sigma_{i}^{2}x_{i}^{2}}\leq b

(
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
)
.
Here
\Phi^{-1}
is the quantile function of the standard normal cumulative distribution, and
\Phi^{-1}(\rho)>0
(as
\rho>0.5
).

Atamtürk et al. 2013
considered the multiple probabilistic knapsack problem with GUB constraints, denoted as MPKP-G:

\max_{x\in\{0,1\}^{|N|}}\left\{\sum_{i\in N}c_{i}x_{i}\,:\,\sum_{i\in N}a_{im}x_{i}+\Phi^{-1}(\rho)\sqrt{\sum_{i\in N}\sigma_{im}^{2}x_{i}^{2}}\leq b_{m},~\forall~m\in M,\sum_{i\in Q_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in K\right\},

(MPKP-G)

where
c\in\mathbb{R}^{|N|}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}^{|N|\times|M|}_{+}
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{|N|\times|M|}_{+}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{|M|}_{+}
, and
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in K}
is a partition of
N
.
Define
f(z):=\Phi^{-1}(\rho)\sqrt{z}
, which is a concave function.
As
x_{i}=x_{i}^{2}
holds for
x_{i}\in\{0,1\}
, for each
m\in M
, the nonlinear constraint in problem (
MPKP-G
) can be rewritten as

\sum_{i\in N}a_{im}x_{i}+f(\sum_{i\in N}\sigma_{im}^{2}x_{i})\leq b_{m}
.
By introducing a continuous variable
w_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
to represent the right-hand side (i.e.,
w_{m}=b_{m}
), we have
\sum_{i\in N}a_{im}x_{i}+f(\sum_{i\in N}\sigma_{im}^{2}x_{i})\leq w_{m}
, thereby obtaining a substructure taking the form of
X
:

X^{m}=\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{|N|}\,:\,\sum_{i\in N}a_{im}x_{i}+f\left(\sum_{i\in N}\sigma_{im}^{2}x_{i}\right)\leq w_{m},~\sum_{i\in Q_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in K\right\}.

(6)

1.3
Relevant literature

It is known that (i) the composition of a non-negative linear function with a concave function yields a submodular function
(
Ahmed and Atamtürk 2011
)
; and (ii) the sum of a submodular and a linear function is also submodular.
Therefore,
X_{0}
and
X
are both submodular sets
(
Edmonds 2003
;
Yu and Küçükyavuz 2023
)
that additionally involve GUB constraints.
Here, a function
g:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is submodular if for any
x,y\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with
x\leq y
(where the “
\leq
” is component-wise) and any
i\in[n]
with
x_{i}=y_{i}=0
, it follows that
g(x+\mathbf{e}^{i})-g(x)\geq g(y+\mathbf{e}^{i})-g(y)
,
where
\mathbf{e}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the
i
-th standard unit vector and the submodular set is defined as
Y:=\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\geq g(x)\right\}
.
In the seminal work,
Edmonds 2003
developed the so-called extended polymatroid inequalities (EPIs) that provide a complete linear description of
\conv(Y)
and can be separated in
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
time.
These results have been widely employed in strengthening the continuous relaxations of and improving the solution efficiency of the branch-and-cut algorithm for the mixed integer linear/nonlinear programming formulations in which
Y
arises as a substructure
(
Atamtürk and Narayanan 2008
;
Atamtürk and Gómez 2020
;
Kılınç-Karzan et al. 2025
;
Ahmed and Papageorgiou 2013
;
Kılınç-Karzan et al. 2022
;
Xie 2021
)
.

Yu and Ahmed 2017b
considered a cardinality-constrained submodular set where the submodular function takes the form
f(a^{\top}x)
(with
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
concave and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
), that is,

Z=\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\geq f(a^{\top}x),~\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\leq c\}
(with
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
),
and presented a linear description for
\conv(Z)
when all coefficients
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
are identical.
For the general case where
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
are arbitrary positive values,
Yu and Küçükyavuz 2023
derived strong valid inequalities for
\conv(Z)
using sequential lifting techniques
(
Wolsey 1976
;
Richard 2011
)
.

Atamtürk and Narayanan 2009
considered the submodular knapsack set
Y\cap\{w=b\}
with
b\in\mathbb{R}
, and developed strong valid inequalities lifted from cover inequalities.

Yu and Küçükyavuz 2025
considered a mixed integer extension of submodularity, known as diminishing returns (DR)-submodularity, and gave the convex hull description of the epigraph of a DR-submodular function under box and monotonicity constraints.
For the submodular
\leq
-set given by
Y_{\leq}:=\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\leq g(x)\right\}
,

Wolsey and Nemhauser 1999
established a linear characterization using the submodular inequalities.
These inequalities were later strengthened for the special case
\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\leq f(a^{\top}x)\right\}
(with
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
concave and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
) or its constrained variants using sequence-independent lifting techniques; see
Ahmed and Atamtürk 2011
;
Yu and Ahmed 2017a
, and
Shi et al. 2022
.

As discussed, if the GUB constraints are removed, both
X
and
X_{0}
become submodular sets.
Therefore, the EPIs, which are valid for submodular set
Y
, are also valid for
X
and
X_{0}
(and can provide linear descriptions of the two sets).
Although the EPIs are strong facet-defining inequalities of
\conv(Y)
, they may be weak for
\conv(X_{0})
and
\conv(X)
, as the derivation does not consider the GUB constraints; see
section

2
for an illustration.
On the other hand, previous polyhedral results on various mixed integer (linear) sets
(
Nemhauser and Vance 1994
;
Sherali and Lee 1995
;
Gu et al. 1998
;
Wolsey 1990
;
Angulo et al. 2015
;
Gokce and Wilhelm 2015
)
have shown that by using the information of GUB constraints, much stronger valid inequalities can usually be derived.
Motivated by this, we aim to develop strong valid inequalities for the mixed integer nonlinear sets
X_{0}
and
X
by explicitly taking the GUB constraints into consideration.

1.4
Contributions

The primary goal of this work is to conduct an in-depth study of strong valid inequalities for
\conv(X_{0})
and
\conv(X)
, and to employ the derived inequalities as cutting planes to improve the computational performance for solving the related optimization problems.
In particular,

•

We first derive a new class of facet-defining inequalities for
\conv(X_{0})
by sequentially lifting a seed inequality
w\geq f(0)
for a one-dimensional restriction of
X_{0}
, obtained by fixing all binary variables in
X_{0}
to their lower bounds, according to some prespecified lifting sequence (i.e., a permutation of
[n]
).
We derive closed-form formulae for the lifting coefficients, with which
each lifted inequality can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
time.
We prove that for a fixed permutation of
[n]
, the lifted inequality, called lifted EPI (LEPI), is stronger than the classic EPI
(
Edmonds 2003
)
, defined by the same permutation.

•

We consider a subclass of permutations, which we refer to as
partial ascending permutations
(each of which is a permutation
\delta=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{n})
of
[n]
such that for all
i,j\in[n]
and
k\in K
with
i<j
and
\delta_{i},\delta_{j}\in N_{k}
, it follows
a_{\delta_{i}}\leq a_{\delta_{j}}
), and study the corresponding LEPIs.
We show that (i) for a LEPI defined by an arbitrary permutation, there exists a partial ascending permutation that yields the same LEPI;
(ii) for any partial ascending permutation, the corresponding LEPI can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
time, as opposed to
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
time for the general case;
and (iii) for any point
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]^{n}
satisfying
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x^{*}_{i}\leq 1
for all
k\in K
, we can either verify that
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\conv(X_{0})
or find a violated LEPI defined by some partial ascending permutation in
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
time.
The consequences of these results are two-fold.
First, all LEPIs (corresponding to the partial ascending permutations), along with bound and GUB constraints, provide a complete linear description of
\conv(X_{0})
, which
demonstrates the strength of LEPIs in strengthening the continuous relaxations of related problems for which
X_{0}
appears as a substructure.
Second, the separation of LEPIs (or
\conv(X_{0})
) can be conducted in
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
time, which paves the way for the efficient generation of LEPIs within a branch-and-cut framework.

•

We extend our polyhedral results of
\conv(X_{0})
onto
\conv(X)
.
In particular, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between the valid (and facet-defining) inequalities of
\conv(X_{0})
and those of
\conv(X)
.
Therefore,
\conv(X)
can be completely characterized using a class of variant LEPIs (denoted as LEPI’s), which can also be separated in
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
time.

We apply the proposed LEPIs and LEPI’s as cutting planes within a branch-and-cut framework for solving the (
MPCLP
) and (
MPKP-G
), respectively.
Extensive computational experiments demonstrate that compared with the classic EPIs, the proposed LEPIs and LEPI’s are much more effective in strengthening the continuous relaxations and improving the overall computational performance of the branch-and-cut framework for solving these two problems.

We first present the EPIs

Edmonds 2003
)
for a generic submodular set:
Y=\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}:w\geq g(x)\}
,
where
g:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

g(\boldsymbol{0})=0

Given any permutation
\delta=(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots,\delta_{n})
of
[n]
, the corresponding EPI takes the form

w\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{\delta_{j}}x_{\delta_{j}},

where for each
j\in[n]

\delta(j)=\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{j}\}

Let

\delta=(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots,\delta_{n})

, we can lift the seed inequality with the variables

\displaystyle\xi_{\delta_{j}}:=\min_{w,\,x}

\displaystyle w-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\xi_{\delta_{i}}x_{\delta_{i}}

(10)

\displaystyle\textup{s.t.}

\displaystyle w\geq g(x),

\displaystyle x_{\delta_{i}}=0,\ \forall\ i\in\{j+1,\dots,n\},

\displaystyle x_{\delta_{j}}=1,

\displaystyle x\in\{0,1\}^{n},~w\in\mathbb{R}.

Unfortunately, the derivation of EPIs does not take the GUB constraints

into account, which may lead to weak inequalities for
\conv(X_{0})
.
We provide the following example to demonstrate this.

Example 2.2
.

Letting
f(z)=-z^{2}
,
n=3
,
N_{1}=\{1,2\}
,
N_{2}=\{3\}
, and
a=(1,2,3)^{\top}
, then
X_{0}
reduces to

X_{0}=\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{3}\,:\,w\geq-(x_{1}+2x_{2}+3x_{3})^{2},~x_{1}+x_{2}\leq 1\right\}.

(11)

All EPIs for
X_{0}
are presented as follows:

\displaystyle w+x_{1}+8x_{2}+27x_{3}\geq 0,

(12)

\displaystyle w+x_{1}+20x_{2}+15x_{3}\geq 0,

(13)

\displaystyle w+5x_{1}+4x_{2}+27x_{3}\geq 0,\

(14)

\displaystyle w+11x_{1}+4x_{2}+21x_{3}\geq 0,

(15)

\displaystyle w+7x_{1}+20x_{2}+9x_{3}\geq 0,

(16)

\displaystyle w+11x_{1}+16x_{2}+9x_{3}\geq 0.\

(17)

Using
polymake

(
Assarf et al. 2017
)
, we can obtain a linear characterization for
\conv(X_{0})
:

\displaystyle w+x_{1}+4x_{2}+21x_{3}\geq 0,

(18)

\displaystyle w+x_{1}+10x_{2}+15x_{3}\geq 0,

(19)

\displaystyle w+7x_{1}+16x_{2}+9x_{3}\geq 0,

(20)

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322ptx_{1}+x_{2}\leq 1,~x_{3}\leq 1,

(21)

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322ptx_{i}\geq 0,~\forall~i\in[3].

(22)

None of the inequalities in (
12
)–(
17
) is facet-defining for
\conv(X_{0})
.
Indeed, inequalities (
12
), (
14
), and (
15
) are dominated by inequality (
18
); inequality (
13
) is dominated by inequality (
19
); and inequalities (
16
) and (
17
) are dominated by inequality (
20
).
This example clearly shows that different from
\conv(Y)
for which EPIs are facet-defining, for
\conv(X_{0})
(a restriction of
\conv(Y)
), EPIs could be weak.

This example motivates us to develop strong valid inequalities by directly investigating
\conv(X_{0})
, rather than investigating its relaxation
\conv(Y)
.

3
Strong valid inequalities for conv
(X_{0})

In this section, we derive strong valid inequalities for
\conv(X_{0})
using sequential lifting techniques.
The lifting problem for
\conv(X_{0})
incorporates the GUB constraints, and thus is more complicated than the lifting problem (
10
) for
\conv(Y)
.
Nevertheless, we can still derive a closed-form optimal solution of the lifting problem for
\conv(X_{0})
, rendering it efficient to compute the lifted inequality, called lifted EPI (LEPI).
We show that the LEPIs are stronger than the classic EPIs of
Edmonds 2003
.

3.1
Lifted extended polymatroid inequalities

We first use sequential lifting techniques to derive strong valid inequalities for
\conv(X_{0})
.
For
S\subseteq[n]
, let

X_{0}(S)=\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{|S|}:w\geq f\left(\sum_{i\in S}a_{i}x_{i}\right),\sum_{i\in N_{k}\cap S}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t]\right\},

which is obtained by fixing
x_{i}
to
0
for all
i\in[n]\backslash S
in
X_{0}
.
Note that the
seed inequality

w\geq f(0)=0
defines a facet of
\conv(X_{0}(\varnothing))
.
To obtain a valid inequality

w\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{i}}x_{\delta_{i}},

(LEPI)

for
X_{0}
,
we lift the
seed inequality
with variables
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
according to some prespecified order/permutation
\delta=(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots,\delta_{n})
(where
\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots,\delta_{n}\}=[n]
).

In particular, in an intermediate step, a valid inequality
w\geq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{i}}x_{\delta_{i}}
for
\conv(X_{0}(\delta(j-1)))

is derived and we attempt to lift variable
x_{\delta_{j}}
, where
the lifting coefficient
\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}
can be computed by solving the lifting problem:

\displaystyle\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}:=\min_{w,\,x}\quad

\displaystyle w-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{i}}x_{\delta_{i}}

(23a)

s.t.

\displaystyle w\geq f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}a_{\delta_{i}}x_{\delta_{i}}\right),

(23b)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t],

(23c)

\displaystyle x_{\delta_{j}}=1,~x\in\{0,1\}^{|\delta(j)|},~w\in\mathbb{R}.

(23d)

Since the
seed inequality

w\geq 0
defines a facet of
\conv(X_{0}(\varnothing))
, it follows from the classic result of
Wolsey 1976
that (
LEPI
) defines a facet of
\conv(X_{0})
.

Lemma 3.1

(cf.
Wolsey 1976
)
.

For any
j\in[n]
,
w\geq\sum_{i=1}^{j}\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{i}}x_{\delta_{i}}
defines a facet of
\conv(X_{0}(\delta(j)))
.
In particular, (
LEPI
) defines a facet of
\conv(X_{0})
.

3.2
Solving the lifting problems (
23
)

Next, we solve the lifting problems (
23
) for
j\in[n]
to determine the lifting coefficients
\{\eta_{\delta_{j}}^{\delta}\}_{j\in[n]}
and inequality (
LEPI
).
Observe that when relaxing the complicated GUB constraints (
23c
) from problem (
23
), we obtain problem (
10
).
As stated in
2.1
, we can derive a closed-form optimal solution for this relaxation problem (
10
).
In the following, we show that even with the GUB constraints (
23c
), a closed-form optimal solution for problem (
23
) can still be derived.

We first transform the lifting problem (
23
) into a set optimization problem.
Note that for any optimal solution
(w,x)
of problem (
23
), constraint (
23b
) holds at equality, i.e.,
w=f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}a_{\delta_{i}}x_{\delta_{i}}\right)
.
Thus, we can project variable
w
out from problem (
23
).
Given a feasible solution

of problem (
23
), we denote the support of
x
by
S=\{i\in[n]\,:\,x_{i}=1\}

Then the objective value of (
23
) at

is given by
f(a(S))-\sum_{i\in S\backslash\delta_{j}}\eta^{\delta}_{i}

Since
x
satisfies constraints (
23c
) and (

|S\cap N_{k}|\leq 1

\delta_{j}\in S

S\subseteq\delta(j)

Therefore, the lifting problem (

\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}=\min_{S}\left\{f(a(S))-\sum_{i\in S\backslash\delta_{j}}\eta^{\delta}_{i}\,:\,S\subseteq\delta(j),~|S\cap N_{k}|\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t],~\delta_{j}\in S\right\}.

(24)

In what follows, we also refer to problem (
24
) as the lifting problem.

Before deriving an optimal solution of problem (
24
), we introduce some notation.
Define

U^{\delta}_{j}:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{t}\left\{\max_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}i\right\},~\forall~j\in[n]\cup 0.

(25)

Here and throughout the paper, we let

\{\max_{i\in\varnothing}i\}=\varnothing

Note that since

U_{0}^{\delta}=\varnothing
.
By definition and (
8
), it follows immediately that

Remark 3.2
.

For any
j\in[n]

(ii)

.

For
j\in[n]
, let

and

h^{\delta}_{j}:=\begin{cases}j,&~\text{if}~\delta_{j}=\max_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}i,\\
\min\{\ell\in[j-1]\,:\,\delta_{\ell}>\delta_{j},~\delta_{\ell}\in N_{k}\},&~\text{if}~\delta_{j}<\max_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}i.\end{cases}

Observe that if
\delta_{j}=\max_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}i

\delta_{h^{\delta}_{j}}=\delta_{j}\in U^{\delta}_{j}
;
otherwise, there exists some

From the definition of

holds for all

(or equivalently,

\delta_{h^{\delta}_{j}}\in U^{\delta}_{h^{\delta}_{j}}

Using the notation

and

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

.

Lemma 3.5
.

For

Lemma 3.6

If

Now, suppose that

Lemma 3.7
.

Lemma 3.8

Suppose that (

4.2
Partial ascending permutations

To avoid the unnecessary permutations that define the same set collection
\{W_{j}^{\delta}\}_{j\in[n]}
(and thus the same (
LEPI
)), we introduce the

, which maintain the ascending order of all items within each GUB set.

Definition 4.3
.

A permutation
\delta
of
[n]
is called partial ascending if for all
i,j\in[n]
and
k\in[t]
with
i<j
and
\delta_{i},\delta_{j}\in N_{k}
, it follows
\delta_{i}<\delta_{j}
.

In the remainder of this paper, let
\Delta
denote the set of all partial ascending permutations of
[n]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The following four statements are equivalent: (i)
\delta\in\Delta
;
(ii)
\delta_{j}=\max_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}i
, or equivalently,
U_{j}^{\delta}\cap N_{k}=\{\delta_{j}\}
, holds for all
j\in[n]
,
where
k\in[t]
satisfying
\delta_{j}\in N_{k}
and
U^{\delta}_{j}
is defined in (
25
);
(iii)
j=h_{j}^{\delta}
holds for all
j\in[n]
where
h^{\delta}_{j}
is defined in (
26
);
and (iv)
W^{\delta}_{j}=U^{\delta}_{j}
holds for all
j\in[n]
, where
W^{\delta}_{j}
is defined in (
27
).

Proof.

The equivalence of (i) and (ii) follows from the definition of partial ascending permutations and the fact that
\delta(j)=\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{j}\}
.
The equivalence of (ii) and (iii) follows from the definition of
h^{\delta}_{j}
in (
26
).
Finally,
W^{\delta}_{j}=U^{\delta}_{h^{\delta}_{j}}\backslash\delta_{h^{\delta}_{j}}\cup\delta_{j}=U_{j}^{\delta}
holds if and only if

, which implies the equivalence of (iii) and (iv).

(1,2,3,4)

(1,3,2,4)
are partial ascending, with corresponding set collections
\{\{1\},\{2\},\{2,3\},\{2,4\}\}
and
\{\{1\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\},\{2,4\}\}
, respectively.
Using
3.4
and
4.4
(iv), it is simple to see that the two distinct partial ascending permutations, however, define the same (
LEPI
):

w\geq 2x_{1}+4x_{2}+6x_{3}+7x_{4}.

4.3
An efficient algorithm for computing LEPIs corresponding to partial ascending permutations

By
4.8
, to characterize all LEPIs, it suffices to characterize LEPIs defined by partial ascending permutations.
In the following, we further show that different from the general case which requires
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
time (see
3.10
), (
LEPI
) defined by any permutation
\delta\in\Delta
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
time.
To achieve this, we need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.12
.

Given any
\delta\in\Delta
and
j\in[n]
, it follows that
f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))=\sum_{i\in U^{\delta}_{j}}\eta^{\delta}_{i}
, or equivalently,

\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}=f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))-\sum_{i\in U^{\delta}_{j}\backslash\delta_{j}}\eta^{\delta}_{i},~\forall~j\in[n].

(43)

Proof.

The result follows from
3.4
and
4.4
(iv).
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Given any
\delta\in\Delta
,
j\in[n]
, and
k\in[t]
with
\delta_{j}\in N_{k}=\{i_{k-1}+1,i_{k-1}+2,\ldots,i_{k}\}
, it follows that

(i)

if
\delta_{j}=i_{k-1}+1
, then
U^{\delta}_{j}=U^{\delta}_{j-1}\cup\{\delta_{j}\}
;

(ii)

if
i_{k-1}+2\leq\delta_{j}\leq i_{k}
, then
\delta_{j}-1\in U^{\delta}_{j-1}
and
U^{\delta}_{j}=U^{\delta}_{j-1}\backslash\{\delta_{j}-1\}\cup\{\delta_{j}\}
.

Proof.

The result follows from the definition of
U_{j}^{\delta}
in (
25
) and the definition of partial ascending permutations in
4.3
.
∎

Given any
\delta\in\Delta
and
j\in[n]
,
k\in[t]
with
\delta_{j}\in N_{k}
, it follows from
4.12
and
4.13
that

\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))-f(a(U^{\delta}_{j-1})),&&~\text{if}~\delta_{j}=i_{k-1}+1,\\
&f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))-f(a(U^{\delta}_{j-1}))+\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}-1},&&~\text{if}~i_{k-1}+2\leq\delta_{j}\leq i_{k}.\end{aligned}\right.

(44)

Using (
44
), we can design an
\mathcal{O}(n)
-time algorithm to compute
\{\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}\}_{j\in[n]}
.
Specifically, letting
A_{j}=a(U^{\delta}_{j})
for
j\in 0\cup[n]
, then, by
4.13
,
\{A_{j}\}_{j\in 0\cup[n]}
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
time using the following recursive formula

A_{0}=0,\quad A_{j}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &A_{j-1}+a_{\delta_{j}},&&~\text{if}~\delta_{j}=i_{k-1}+1,\\
&A_{j-1}+a_{\delta_{j}}-a_{\delta_{j}-1}&&~\text{if}~i_{k-1}+2\leq\delta_{j}\leq i_{k},\end{aligned}\right.~\forall~j\in[n].

As a result, (
44
) reduces to

\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &f(A_{j})-f(A_{j-1}),&&~\text{if}~\delta_{j}=i_{k-1}+1,\\
&f(A_{j})-f(A_{j-1})+\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}-1},&&~\text{if}~i_{k-1}+2\leq\delta_{j}\leq i_{k}.\end{aligned}\right.

(45)

Note that from
4.13
, if
i_{k-1}+2\leq\delta_{j}\leq i_{k}
, then
\delta_{j}-1\in U^{\delta}_{j-1}\subseteq\delta(j-1)
.
Thus, by (
45
), we can compute
\{\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}\}_{j\in[n]}
sequentially in the order
\{\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{1}},\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{2}},\dots,\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{n}}\}
, which takes
\mathcal{O}(n)
time.

Theorem 4.14
.

Given any permutation
\delta\in\Delta
, the coefficients
\{\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}\}_{j\in[n]}
of (
LEPI
) defined by
\delta
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
time.

5
Linear description of conv
(X_{0})
and exact separation

In this section, we further present two key properties of the LEPIs (defined by partial ascending permutations):
(i) all LEPIs, together with bound and GUB constraints, are able to fully characterize
\conv(X_{0})
; and (ii) the separation of the exponential family of the LEPIs (or equivalently,
\conv(X_{0})
) can be implemented in
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
time.
To achieve this, we first investigate the separation problem of
\conv(X_{0})
.

Given a point
(w^{*},x^{*})
, the separation problem of
\conv(X_{0})
attempts to either find a valid inequality that is violated by
(w^{*},x^{*})
, or to prove that no such inequality exists (i.e.,
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\conv(X_{0})
).
Since the trivial inequalities
0\leq x_{i}\leq 1
for
i\in[n]
and
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1
for
k\in[t]
can be checked for violation in linear time, we can, without loss of generality, assume that
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]^{n}
with
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}^{*}\leq 1
for all
k\in[t]
.
The following lemma characterizes a property of non-trivial facet-defining inequalities for
\conv(X_{0})
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Any non-trivial facet-defining inequality
\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}\leq\alpha w
for
\conv(X_{0})
satisfies
\alpha=1
up to scaling.

Proof.

Since
(w,x)=(1,\boldsymbol{0})
is a ray of
\conv(X_{0})
,
\alpha
must be non-negative.
When
\alpha=0
, for
\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}\leq 0
to be facet-defining for
\conv(X_{0})
, it follows that

\displaystyle-\pi_{0}=

\displaystyle\max_{x}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}\,:\,(w,x)\in X_{0}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{x}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{t}\sum_{i\in N_{k}}\pi_{i}x_{i}\,:\,x\in\{0,1\}^{n},~\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t]\right\}=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\max\left\{\max_{i\in N_{k}}\pi_{i},0\right\}.

Thus, inequality
\pi_{0}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}\leq 0
, or equivalently,
\sum_{k=1}^{t}\left(\sum\limits_{i\in N_{k}}\pi_{i}x_{i}-\max\left\{\max\limits_{i\in N_{k}}\pi_{i},0\right\}\right)\leq 0
,
is dominated by trivial inequalities
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1
for
k\in[t]
and
x_{i}\geq 0
for
i\in[n]
,
a contradiction with the fact that
\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}\leq 0
is a non-trivial facet-defining inequality for
\conv(X_{0})
.
Therefore,
\alpha>0
must hold, and hence inequality
\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}\leq\alpha w
can be scaled so that
\alpha=1
.
∎

Using
5.1
, we can reformulate the separation problem for
\conv(X_{0})
as an optimization problem:

\max_{\pi_{0},\,\pi}\left\{\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x^{*}_{i}\,:\,(\pi_{0},\pi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},~\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}\leq w,~\forall~(w,x)\in X_{0}\right\}.

(
SEP
)

Letting
(\bar{\pi}_{0},\bar{\pi})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an optimal solution of problem (
SEP
),
if
w^{*}\geq\bar{\pi}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\pi}_{i}x^{*}
,
then
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\conv(X_{0})
; otherwise,
(w^{*},x^{*})
violates the valid inequality
w\geq\bar{\pi}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\pi}_{i}x
.
Given a point
(w,x)\in X_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}
, we denote the support of
x
by
S=\{i\in[n]\,:\,x_{i}=1\}
.
Then, by the definition of
X_{0}
,
it follows that
(w,x)\in X_{0}
holds if and only if
S\in\mathcal{X}
and
w\geq f(a(S))
hold, where

\mathcal{X}:=\{S\subseteq[n]\,:\ |S\cap N_{k}|\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t]\}.

(46)

Therefore, problem (
SEP
) is equivalent to

\max_{\pi_{0},\,\pi}\left\{\pi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x^{*}_{i}\,:\,(\pi_{0},\pi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},~\pi_{0}+\sum_{i\in S}\pi_{i}\leq f(a(S)),~\forall~S\in\mathcal{X}\right\}.

(
SEP
’)

Next, we attempt to find an optimal solution of the separation problem (
SEP’
).
To do this, we first define a vector
y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

y_{\ell}^{*}=\sum_{i=\ell}^{i_{k}}x^{*}_{i},~\forall~\ell\in N_{k}=\{i_{k-1}+1,\dots,i_{k}\},~k\in[t].

(47)

Combining (
47
),
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x^{*}_{i}\leq 1
for
k\in[t]
, and
x^{*}_{i}\geq 0
for
i\in[n]
, we obtain

1\geq y_{i_{k-1}+1}^{*}\geq y_{i_{k-1}+2}^{*}\geq\dots\geq y_{i_{k}}^{*}\geq 0,~\forall~k\in[t].

(48)

Let
\delta\in\Delta
be a partial ascending permutation satisfying

y^{*}_{\delta_{1}}\geq y^{*}_{\delta_{2}}\geq\dots\geq y^{*}_{\delta_{n}}.

(49)

Note that such a permutation exists.
Indeed, we can first find a permutation
\delta^{\prime}
satisfying (
49
) by sorting
\{y_{i}^{*}\}_{i\in[n]}
.
If
\delta^{\prime}\in\Delta
, we are done.
Otherwise, from
4.3
, there must exist
i,j\in[n]
and some
k\in[t]
such that
i<j
,
\delta^{\prime}_{i},\delta^{\prime}_{j}\in N_{k}
, and
\delta^{\prime}_{i}>\delta^{\prime}_{j}
.
By
i<j
and (
49
), it follows
y^{*}_{\delta^{\prime}_{i}}\geq y^{*}_{\delta^{\prime}_{j}}
; and by
\delta^{\prime}_{i}>\delta^{\prime}_{j}
,
\delta^{\prime}_{i},\delta^{\prime}_{j}\in N_{k}
, and (
48
), it follows
y^{*}_{\delta^{\prime}_{i}}\leq y^{*}_{\delta^{\prime}_{j}}
.
As a result,
y_{\delta^{\prime}_{i}}^{*}=y_{\delta^{\prime}_{j}}^{*}
must hold, and thus swapping the values of
y^{*}_{\delta^{\prime}_{i}}
and
y^{*}_{\delta^{\prime}_{j}}
still yields a permutation satisfying (
49
).
Therefore,

Remark 5.2
.

For a permutation
\bar{\delta}
satisfying (
49
), setting
\delta_{j}:=i_{k-1}+|\bar{\delta}(j-1)\cap N_{k}|+1
for each
j\in[n]
with
\bar{\delta}_{j}\in N_{k}
will yield a permutation
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying (
49
).
As a result, transforming a permutation
\bar{\delta}
satisfying (
49
) into a permutation
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying (
49
) can be conducted in
\mathcal{O}(n)
time.

We are now ready to present the main result of this section, i.e., deriving an optimal solution of the separation problem (
SEP’
).

Theorem 5.3
.

Given a point
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]^{n}
with
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}^{*}\leq 1
for all
k\in[t]
, let
y^{*}
be defined as in (
47
),
\delta\in\Delta
be a partial ascending permutation such that
y^{*}_{\delta_{1}}\geq y^{*}_{\delta_{2}}\geq\dots\geq y^{*}_{\delta_{n}}
, and vector
\eta^{\delta}=(\eta^{\delta}_{1},\ldots,\eta^{\delta}_{n})
be defined as in (
43
).
Then
(\pi_{0},\pi)=(0,\eta^{\delta})
is an optimal solution to problem (
SEP’
).

Proof.

From
3.7
,
\sum_{i\in S}\eta^{\delta}_{i}\leq f(a(S))
holds for all
S\in\mathcal{X}
(defined in (
46
)), and thus
(\pi_{0},\pi)=(0,\eta^{\delta})
is a feasible solution of problem (
SEP’
).
To establish the optimality, we consider the dual of the linear programming problem (
SEP’
):

\min_{\lambda}\left\{\sum_{S\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda_{S}f(a(S))\,:\,\sum_{S\in\mathcal{X}:i\in S}\lambda_{S}=x^{*}_{i},~\forall~i\in[n],~\sum_{S\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda_{S}=1,~\lambda_{S}\geq 0,~\forall~S\in\mathcal{X}\right\}.

(
D-SEP’
)

Define the vector
\lambda^{*}
as follows:

\lambda^{*}_{S}=\begin{cases}y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j+1}},&~\text{if}~S=U^{\delta}_{j}\text{~holds for some }j\in 0\cup[n],\\[5.0pt]
0,&~\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad\forall~S\in\mathcal{X},

(50)

where
U_{j}^{\delta}
is defined in (
25
),
y^{*}_{\delta_{0}}:=1
, and
y^{*}_{\delta_{n+1}}:=0
.
By (
49
), it follows that
y^{*}_{\delta_{0}}\geq y^{*}_{\delta_{1}}\geq\cdots\geq y^{*}_{\delta_{n+1}}
and thus
\lambda^{*}_{S}\geq 0
for all
S\in\mathcal{X}
.
Moreover,
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda^{*}_{S}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j+1}})=y^{*}_{\delta_{0}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{n+1}}=1-0=1
.
Then, to show that
\lambda^{*}
is a feasible solution to (
D-SEP’
), it remains to prove that
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{X}:i\in S}\lambda^{*}_{S}=x^{*}_{i}
holds for all
i\in[n]
,
or equivalently,

\sum_{j\,:\,i\in U^{\delta}_{j}}(y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j+1}})=x^{*}_{i},~\forall~i\in[n].

(51)

Given any
r\in[n]
, let
k\in[t]
be such that
r\in N_{k}=\{i_{k-1}+1,\dots,i_{k}\}
.
We prove that (
51
) holds at
i=r
by considering the two cases
i_{k-1}+1\leq r<i_{k}
and
r=i_{k}
, separately.

(i)
i_{k-1}+1\leq r<i_{k}
.
Then
i_{k-1}+1\leq r<r+1\leq i_{k}
, and thus
r,r+1\in N_{k}
.
Let
j_{1},j_{2}
be such that
r=\delta_{j_{1}}
and
r+1=\delta_{j_{2}}
.
From
\delta\in\Delta
, we have (a)
j_{1}<j_{2}
; (b)
\max_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}i<\delta_{j_{1}}=r
for
j<j_{1}
; (c)
\max_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}i=\delta_{j_{1}}=r
for
j_{1}\leq j<j_{2}
; and (d)
\max_{i\in N_{k}\cap\delta(j)}i\geq\delta_{j_{2}}>\delta_{j_{1}}=r
for
j\geq j_{2}
.
These, together with the definition of
U_{j}^{\delta}
in (
25
), imply that
\{j\in[n]\,:\,r\in U^{\delta}_{j}\}=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{2}-1\}
.
Then we obtain

\sum_{j\,:\,r\in U^{\delta}_{j}}(y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j+1}})=\sum_{j=j_{1}}^{j_{2}-1}(y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j+1}})=y^{*}_{\delta_{j_{1}}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j_{2}}}=y^{*}_{r}-y^{*}_{r+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{i=r}^{i_{k}}x^{*}_{i}-\sum_{i=r+1}^{i_{k}}x^{*}_{i}=x^{*}_{r},

where (a) follows from (
47
).

(ii)
r=i_{k}
. Let
j_{1}\in[n]
be such that
i_{k}=\delta_{j_{1}}
.
Similar to case (i), we can derive
\{j\in[n]\,:\,i_{k}\in U^{\delta}_{j}\}=\{j_{1},\dots,n\}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\,:\,i_{k}\in U^{\delta}_{j}}(y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j+1}})=\sum_{j=j_{1}}^{n}(y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j+1}})=y^{*}_{\delta_{j_{1}}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{n+1}}=y^{*}_{i_{k}}-0=\sum_{i=i_{k}}^{i_{k}}x^{*}_{i}=x^{*}_{i_{k}}.

Combining the above two cases, (
51
) must hold, and hence
\lambda^{*}
is a feasible solution to the dual problem (
D-SEP’
).

To prove the optimality of
(0,\eta^{\delta})
, by strong duality of an LP problem, it suffices to prove

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda^{*}_{S}f(a(S))=\sum_{i\in[n]}\eta^{\delta}_{i}x^{*}_{i}.

(52)

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda^{*}_{S}f(a(S))=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}-y^{*}_{\delta_{j+1}})f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{j\in[n]}[f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))-f(a(U^{\delta}_{j-1}))]y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}

(53)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\sum_{j:\delta_{j}\in[n]}[f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))-f(a(U^{\delta}_{j-1}))]y^{*}_{\delta_{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\sum_{j:i_{k-1}+1\leq\delta_{j}\leq i_{k}}[f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))-f(a(U^{\delta}_{j-1}))]\sum_{i=\delta_{j}}^{i_{k}}x^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\sum_{i=i_{k-1}+1}^{i_{k}}x^{*}_{i}\sum_{j:i_{k-1}+1\leq\delta_{j}\leq i}\left[f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))-f(a(U^{\delta}_{j-1}))\right],

where (a) follows from
f(a(U^{\delta}_{0}))=f(0)=0
and
y^{*}_{\delta_{n+1}}=0
; (b) follows from
\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}\}=[n]
.
Note that, for any
i\in[n]
and
k\in[t]
satisfying
i\in N_{k}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j:i_{k-1}+1\leq\delta_{j}\leq i}\left[f(a(U^{\delta}_{j}))-f(a(U^{\delta}_{j-1}))\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{j:i_{k-1}+1\leq\delta_{j}\leq i}\left(\sum_{\ell\in U^{\delta}_{j}}\eta^{\delta}_{\ell}-\sum_{\ell\in U^{\delta}_{j-1}}\eta^{\delta}_{\ell}\right)

(54)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\eta^{\delta}_{i_{k-1}+1}+\sum_{j:i_{k-1}+2\leq\delta_{j}\leq i}(\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}-\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}-1})=\eta^{\delta}_{i},

where (a) follows from
\delta\in\Delta
and
4.12
; (b) follows from
4.13
.
Combining (
53
) and (
54
), we obtain

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda^{*}_{S}f(a(S))=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\sum_{i=i_{k-1}+1}^{i_{k}}x^{*}_{i}\eta^{\delta}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta^{\delta}_{i}x^{*}_{i}.

As a result, (
52
) holds and the statement follows.
∎

Three remarks on
5.3
are in order.
First,
5.3
implies that given a point
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]^{n}
with
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}^{*}\leq 1
for all
k\in[t]
, either the LEPI
w\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{\delta_{i}}^{\delta}x_{\delta_{i}}
(where
(0,\eta^{\delta})
is optimal to (
SEP’
)) cuts off
(w^{*},x^{*})
or none exists.
This indicates the strength of LEPIs in terms of characterizing the convex hull of
X_{0}
.

Corollary 5.4
.

All LEPIs
w\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{i}}x_{\delta_{i}}
,
\delta\in\Delta
, along with the trivial inequalities
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1
,
k\in[t]
, and
0\leq x_{i}\leq 1
,
i\in[n]
, provide a complete linear description of
\conv(X_{0})
.

Second,
5.3
enables us to derive an efficient polynomial-time algorithm for the separation of the LEPIs (or equivalently,
\conv(X_{0})
).
Indeed,
given a point
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]^{n}
with
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}^{*}\leq 1
for all
k\in[t]
, we can use the following exact procedure to solve the separation problem.

(i)

Compute
y^{*}
in (
47
) with the time complexity of
\mathcal{O}(n)
.

(ii)

Determine the permutation
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying (
49
) by first sorting
\{y_{i}^{*}\}_{i\in[n]}
with the time complexity of
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
and then transforming the obtained permutation
\delta^{\prime}
into a permutation
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying (
49
) with the time complexity of
\mathcal{O}(n)
; see
5.2
.

(iii)

Compute the coefficients
\{\eta_{\delta_{j}}^{\delta}\}_{j\in[n]}
of (
LEPI
) corresponding to
\delta
(
\in\Delta
) with the time complexity of
\mathcal{O}(n)
; see
4.14
.

(iv)

Check whether or not (
LEPI
) is violated by
(w^{*},x^{*})
with the time complexity of
\mathcal{O}(n)
.

Corollary 5.5
.

The separation problem of the LEPIs
(
or
\conv(X_{0})
)
can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
time.

Remark 5.6
.

When
|N_{k}|=1
holds for all
k\in[t]
, we have
y^{*}_{i}=x^{*}_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
in step (i).
Then, step (ii) sorts the values
\{x_{i}^{*}\}_{i\in[n]}
, and step (iii) computes the coefficients of (
LEPI
) that is equivalent to (
EPI
).
In this case, our exact separation algorithm reduces to the separation of the EPIs and is indeed equivalent to the well-known greedy algorithm proposed by
Edmonds 2003
.

Third, due to the polynomial equivalence of optimization and separation
(
Grötschel et al. 2012
)
,
5.5
implies that optimizing a linear function over
X_{0}
can be conducted in polynomial time.

Corollary 5.7
.

The optimization problem

\min_{w,\,x}\left\{dw+c^{\top}x\,:\,~w\geq f(a^{\top}x),~\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t],~(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\right\}

(OPT)

can be solved in polynomial time, where
d\in\mathbb{R}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, and
\{N_{k}\}_{k\in[t]}
is a partition of
[n]
.

In Appendix
B
, we further provide a strongly polynomial-time algorithm for solving problem (
OPT
).

6
Extensions to the general case conv
(X)

In this section, we extend the previous polyhedral results of
\conv(X_{0})
onto the general case
\conv(X)
, where we recall that

X=\left\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\geq f(a^{\top}x)+b^{\top}x,~\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t]\right\}.

To proceed, we note that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the feasible points of
X_{0}
and
X
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then
(w,x)\in X
if and only if
(w-b^{\top}x,x)\in X_{0}
.

Proof.

This statement follows from the definition of
X
and
X_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\{(w^{j},x^{j})\}_{j\in[n+1]}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then
\{(w^{j},x^{j})\}_{j\in[n+1]}
are affinely independent if and only if

\{(w^{j}-b^{\top}x^{j},x^{j})\}_{j\in[n+1]}
are affinely independent.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\{(w^{j},x^{j})\}_{j\in[n+1]}
are affinely independent.

(ii)

\alpha=\boldsymbol{0}
is the unique feasible solution to the following linear system:

\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}=0,~\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}x^{j}_{i}=0,~\forall~i\in[n],~\text{and}~\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}w^{j}=0.

(55)

(iii)

\alpha=\boldsymbol{0}
is the unique feasible solution to the following linear system:

\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}=0,~\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}x^{j}_{i}=0,~\forall~i\in[n],~\text{and}~\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}(w^{j}-b^{\top}x^{j})=0.

(56)

(iv)

\{(w^{j}-b^{\top}x^{j},x^{j})\}_{j\in[n+1]}
are affinely independent.

The equivalence of (i) and (ii) (respectively, (iii) and (iv)) is trivially satisfied.
For (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii), observe that for solutions satisfying the first two constraints in the linear systems (
55
) and (
56
), it follows

\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}(w^{j}-b^{\top}x^{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}w^{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}x^{j}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{j}w^{j}
,
and thus the third constraints in (
55
) and (
56
) are equivalent.
∎

The following theorem further establishes a one-to-one correspondence between the facet-defining inequalities of
\conv(X_{0})
and
\conv(X)
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Inequality

\pi_{0}+\pi^{\top}x\leq\alpha w

(57)

is valid (respectively, facet-defining) for
\conv(X_{0})
if and only if

\pi_{0}+\pi^{\top}x\leq\alpha(w-b^{\top}x)

(58)

is valid (respectively, facet-defining) for
\conv(X)
.

Proof.

We shall prove the “if” part and the proof of the “only if” part is similar.
Assume that inequality (
58
) is valid for
\conv(X)
.
Let
(w^{*},x^{*})
be an arbitrary point in
X_{0}
.
Then, by
6.1
, it follows
(w^{*}+b^{\top}x^{*},x^{*})\in X
.
Substituting
(w^{*}+b^{\top}x^{*},x^{*})
into (
58
), we obtain
\pi_{0}+\pi^{\top}x^{*}\leq\alpha w^{*}
.
Since
(w^{*},x^{*})
is chosen arbitrarily from
X_{0}
, it follows that (
57
) is a valid inequality for
\conv(X_{0})
.
If (
58
) defines a facet of
\conv(X)
,
then there exist
n+1
affinely independent points
\{(w^{j},x^{j})\}_{j\in[n+1]}\in X
such that

\pi_{0}+\pi^{\top}x^{j}=\alpha(w^{j}-b^{\top}x^{j})
holds for all
j\in[n+1]
.
From
6.1
and
6.2
,
\{(w^{j}-b^{\top}x^{j},x^{j})\}_{j\in[n+1]}\in X_{0}
are
n+1
affinely independent points satisfying (
57
) at equality,
showing that inequality (
57
) is facet-defining for
\conv(X_{0})
.
∎

Using
6.3
, we can extend the EPIs and LEPIs of
\conv(X_{0})
onto the general case
\conv(X)
.
Specifically, for any permutation
\delta
of
[n]
, by
6.3
and the validity of (
EPI
) and (
LEPI
), we obtain the EPI-like and LEPI-like (valid) inequalities for
\conv(X)
:

\displaystyle w\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f(a(\delta(j)))-f(a(\delta(j-1)))+b_{\delta_{j}})x_{\delta_{j}},

(EPI’)

\displaystyle w\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\eta^{\delta}_{\delta_{j}}+b_{\delta_{j}})x_{\delta_{j}}.

(LEPI’)

Moreover, combining
3.1
,
3.11
,
5.4
, and
6.3
, we obtain that

Corollary 6.4
.

(i) For any permutation
\delta
of
[n]
,
inequality (
LEPI’
) is facet-defining for
\conv(X)
and stronger than inequality (
EPI’
);
(ii) All LEPI’s together with the trivial inequalities
0\leq x_{i}\leq 1
for
i\in[n]
and
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1
for
k\in[t]
provide a complete linear description of
\conv(X)
.

Let
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]^{n}
be such that
\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}^{*}\leq 1
holds for all
k\in[t]
.
Using
6.3
, we can derive that
(w^{*},x^{*})\in\conv(X)
holds if and only if
(w^{*}-b^{\top}x^{*},x^{*})\in\conv(X_{0})
holds.
As a result, separating
(w^{*},x^{*})
from
\conv(X)
(respectively, determining whether there exists an inequality (

) cutting off
(w^{*},x^{*})

(w^{*}-b^{\top}x^{*},x^{*})

\conv(X_{0})

Therefore, it follows from

that

Corollary 6.5
.

The separation problem of the LEPI’s (or

Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we have studied the polyhedral structure of the submodular sets with GUB constraints:
X_{0}=\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\geq f(a^{\top}x),~\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t]\}
and its generalization
X=\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\geq f(a^{\top}x)+b^{\top}x,~\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t]\}
, which arise as important substructures in many applications.
We have developed a class of strong valid inequalities for the two sets, called LEPIs, using sequential lifting techniques.
Three key features, which make them particularly suitable to be embedded in a branch-and-cut framework to solve related MINLP problems, are as follows.
First, they are facet-defining for the convex hulls of the two sets and are stronger than the well-known EPIs of
Edmonds 2003
.
Second, together with the bound and GUB constraints, they are able to provide complete linear descriptions of the convex hulls of the two sets.
Third, they can be separated using an efficient
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
-time algorithm.
By extensive computational experiments on MPCLPs and MPKP-Gs, we have demonstrated that compared with the classic EPIs, the proposed LEPIs are much more effective in strengthening the continuous relaxations and improving the overall computational performance of the branch-and-cut algorithms.

A promising direction for future research is to study a more general submodular set with GUB constraints, namely,
X^{g}=\{(w,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,:\,w\geq g(x),~\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t]\}
, where
g
is an arbitrary submodular function.
Studying the polyhedral structure of
\conv(X^{g})
, however, poses more challenges compared to those of
\conv(X_{0})
and
\conv(X)
.
In particular, as shown in Appendix
C
, the submodular minimization with disjoint GUB constraints, namely
\min_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\left\{g(x):\sum_{i\in N_{k}}x_{i}\leq 1,~\forall~k\in[t]\right\}
, is NP-hard, and therefore one cannot expect to obtain a tractable linear description for
\conv(X^{g})
.
Nevertheless, it would be still interesting to investigate the sequential lifting procedure for
X^{g}
that takes the GUB constraints into consideration towards developing strong valid inequalities to provide a partial characterization of
\conv(X^{g})
.

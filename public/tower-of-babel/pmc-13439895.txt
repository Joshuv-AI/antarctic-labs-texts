Nomogram prediction of acute kidney injury following modified Morrow myectomy in hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy: insights from 12 years of outcomes
Authors: Xiaodong Zeng, Yuemei Liang, Yijin Wu, Hongxiang Wu, Ruobing Wang, Pingchuan Huang, Xuan Xu, Ying Zeng, Minjie Huang, Xinze Song, Jimei Chen, Liming Lei, Huiming Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study explored the incidence and risk factors of acute kidney injury (AKI) in patients with hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM) undergoing modified Morrow septal myectomy and developed a risk prediction model to optimize perioperative management.MethodsA retrospective cohort of HOCM patients who underwent modified Morrow procedure between 2012 and 2024 was analyzed. AKI was defined using Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria, and patients were grouped into AKI and non-AKI cohorts. Univariate analysis followed by binary logistic regression identified independent predictors of AKI, which informed the development of a nomogram-based prediction model. Model performance was assessed using the concordance index (C-index), calibration curves, and decision curve analysis.ResultsAmong 270 patients, 54 (20%) developed AKI, including 24, 12, and 18 patients classified as stage 1, stage 2, and stage 3 respectively, with 15 requiring hemodialysis. Independent predictors included preoperative creatinine (OR = 1.069, P < 0.001), postoperative 48-h alanine aminotransferase (ALT) (OR = 1.001, P = 0.021), total bilirubin (TBIL) (OR = 1.036, P = 0.015), pulmonary infection (OR = 9.432, P < 0.001), and red blood cell transfusion (OR = 1.246, P = 0.002). The logistic model achieved 81.13% sensitivity, 87.91% specificity, and an AUC of 0.904 (95% CI: 0.862 ~ 0.936). The nomogram showed excellent accuracy, with a C-index of 0.890.ConclusionAKI is a frequent and severe complication after modified Morrow myectomy in HOCM. Identifying high-risk patients and addressing modifiable factors may improve perioperative management and outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05910-w.

Introduction

Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM) is a genetic heart disease characterized by left ventricular hypertrophy, outflow tract obstruction, and diastolic dysfunction. It is a leading cause of sudden cardiac death [1]. Modified Morrow surgery, the preferred surgical approach for HOCM, is widely used to alleviate left ventricular outflow tract obstruction and heart failure. This procedure significantly reduces the left ventricular outflow tract pressure gradient by resecting the thickened interventricular septal myocardium, thereby improving patient’s symptoms and long-term prognosis [2–4].

Although the modified Morrow septal myectomy has demonstrated significant efficacy in relieving outflow tract obstruction and improving symptoms, postoperative complications—particularly postoperative mortality and acute kidney injury (AKI)—remain important clinical concerns [5]. Literature reports that the incidence of AKI after HOCM surgery can be as high as 45%, with the occurrence of AKI leading to prolonged hospital stays and significantly increased mortality rates [6]. Karla Inestroza et al. found that patients who underwent septal myectomy for HOCM had a higher incidence of AKI compared with alcohol septal ablationI [7]. Yanhai Meng et al. found that preoperative glomerular filtration rate and new-onset postoperative atrial fibrillation were independently associated in HOCM patients [8]. The modified Morrow procedure is complex and technically demanding, with prolonged cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) time and aortic cross-clamp (ACC) times further increasing the risk of postoperative AKI. Therefore, investigating the incidence of AKI after the modified Morrow procedure, its potential risk factors, and its impact on prognosis is crucial to optimise perioperative management and improve patient outcomes. Compared to previous studies, this study developed a practical model for predicting AKI after the modified Morrow procedure, based on detailed perioperative factors, and evaluated its clinical utility.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethics

This study is a single-center, retrospective cohort analysis to evaluate the incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) after the modified Morrow procedure and its associated risk factors. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital (approval number: 2020-296H-3).

AKI is defined according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria as follows: an increase in serum creatinine concentration of 26.5 µmol/L within 48 h, an increase in serum creatinine to ≥ 1.5 times the baseline level within 7 days, or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h over 6 h. Based on the degree of change in serum creatinine levels or urine output, AKI is further classified into stages 1–3 according to the KDIGO criteria [9–11].

Study population and grouping

This study included 270 patients with HOCM who underwent modified Morrow surgery in our hospital from May 20, 2012, to January 20, 2024. Inclusion criteria were: 1) age over 18 years; 2) diagnosed with hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy by echocardiography, electrocardiogram, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, or other diagnostic tests; and 3) underwent modified Morrow surgery. Exclusion criteria were: 1) estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 60 ml/min/1.73m2; 2) coexisting severe cardiovascular disease; and 3) severe preoperative liver dysfunction or severe infection. Based on the occurrence of AKI after surgery, patients were divided into AKI group and non-AKI groups for analysis and comparison to identify potential risk factors.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis for this study was performed using SPSS (version 27) and R (version 4.2.1) statistical software. Continuous variables that followed a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and comparisons between groups were performed using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range), and group comparisons were made using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies, and group comparisons were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analysis were further analyzed using multivariate logistic regression to identify independent risk factors for AKI after modified Morrow surgery. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to assess the predictive performance of the logistic regression model for AKI. Based on the independent risk factors identified in the logistic regression analysis, an AKI risk prediction model was developed. The concordance index (C-index) was used to evaluate the predictive accuracy of the model. Calibration curves were used to assess the correlation between predicted values and actual observed outcomes. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the net benefit of the model across different risk thresholds. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was conducted to compare postoperative ICU stay and hospital stay between the AKI and non-AKI groups, and the log-rank test was used to assess differences in hospital outcomes.

Results

Of these patients, 54 (20%) developed AKI. The distribution of AKI stages was as follows: 24 cases of stage 1, 12 cases of stage 2, and 18 cases of stage 3. Fifteen patients received hemodialysis treatment.

Risk prediction model for major adverse cardiovascular events after modified morrow surgery: construction and evaluation

Based on the five independent risk factors identified in the binary logistic regression analysis with statistical significance, a risk prediction model for AKI following modified Morrow procedure was developed using a nomograph. In clinical practice, healthcare providers can use the nomograph to assign a score for each of the identified independent risk factors, sum these scores to obtain a total score, and then refer to the total score on the risk axis to determine the patient’s likelihood of developing AKI.

To further assess the performance of the prediction model, the C-index was calculated. The results showed that the nomograph had a C-index of 0.890 (95% confidence interval: 0.847 ~ 0.925, P < 0.001). The calibration curve demonstrated strong agreement between predicted and observed values, indicating high consistency in the model’s ability to predict AKI occurrence after modified Morrow surgery. In-hospital outcome and prognosis

In the AKI group, 10 patients died, compared to 2 in the non-AKI group, resulting in an overall mortality rate of 4.44%. The AKI group exhibited significantly longer durations of mechanical ventilation, ICU stay, and total hospitalization compared to the non-AKI group, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.001). The Kaplan–Meier survival curve demonstrated a statistically significant difference in ICU stay duration between the two groups (Log-rank test χ2 = 79.1621, P < 0.0001), as shown in Fig. 1C. Similarly, the Kaplan–Meier survival curve for postoperative hospitalization duration also revealed a significant difference (Log-rank test χ2 = 66.9819, P < 0.0001), as shown in Fig. 1D.

Discussion

Our study identified a 20% incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) following the modified Morrow procedure, which was significantly associated with prolonged intensive care unit stays, extended hospitalization, and increased perioperative mortality. The elevated incidence of postoperative AKI highlights renal injury as a critical complication of cardiac surgery. These results align with previous reports on the prevalence of AKI after cardiac surgery [11, 12]. Cardiac surgeries, particularly complex procedures such as the modified Morrow surgery, are frequently associated with prolonged CPB and ACC times. These factors can significantly affect patient hemodynamics, leading to renal hypoperfusion and subsequent AKI. In patients with HOCM, the inherent structural and functional abnormalities of the heart—particularly left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction—contribute to further hemodynamic instability both preoperatively and intraoperatively, increasing the risk of postoperative complications [4, 5, 13]. Therefore, a comprehensive preoperative assessment and meticulous intraoperative hemodynamic management are essential. During the perioperative period, we recommend implementing stringent strategies for fluid management, blood pressure stabilization, and mitigation of potential renal injury factors, particularly in patients at elevated risk of developing AKI, to minimize its incidence.

The study identified several independent risk factors for AKI, including elevated preoperative creatinine levels, abnormal postoperative 48-h liver function indicators (such as ALT and TBIL), pulmonary infections, and red blood cell transfusion. These findings provide valuable insights for predicting the risk of postoperative AKI and offer practical guidance for optimizing perioperative management and evaluating surgical outcomes.

Our study results indicate that elevated preoperative creatinine levels serve as an independent risk factor for postoperative AKI. As a critical marker of renal function, preoperative creatinine reflects underlying kidney impairment, with higher levels often indicating a predisposition to renal dysfunction. Previous studies have shown that elevated preoperative serum creatinine is associated with an increased risk of postoperative AKI, with even minor increases significantly raising the risk of AKI [14]. Bohn et al. found that preoperative serum creatinine, male sex, use of angiotensin II receptor blockers, and the duration of one-lung ventilation are risk factors for postoperative AKI [15]. Patients with pre-existing renal dysfunction are more susceptible to hypoperfusion during the modified Morrow procedure, which can lead to the development of AKI. For patients with elevated preoperative creatinine levels, it is imperative to implement comprehensive renal protective strategies. These include preoperative optimization of renal function, meticulous fluid management, avoidance of nephrotoxic agents, and minimization of intraoperative renal stress [13, 16].

Notably, our study found that elevated liver function markers, such as ALT and TBIL, within 48 h postoperatively, are significant predictors of AKI. Previous research has demonstrated an association between a higher AST/ALT ratio and an increased incidence of postoperative AKI [17]. Among critically ill cardiovascular patients, particularly those with aortic dissection, the coexistence of hepatic and renal dysfunction is notably frequent. The physiological interconnection between the liver and kidneys suggests that liver dysfunction may exacerbate renal injury through multiple mechanisms [17–20]. The extracorporeal circulation and hemodynamic alterations associated with the modified Morrow procedure may exacerbate hepatic inflammation and stress responses. Consequently, comprehensive postoperative monitoring of liver function, in conjunction with renal function evaluation, is essential for the early identification and management of kidney injury.

Pulmonary infection is another independent risk factor identified in this study. Postoperative pulmonary infections can induce hypoxemia and hemodynamic instability, potentially impairing renal perfusion and elevating the risk of AKI. Prior research has demonstrated that severe pulmonary infection can cause hemodynamic instability, leading to renal ischemia and hypoxia, which are critical factors in the development of AKI [21]. Furthermore, pulmonary infections stimulate the release of inflammatory mediators, with inflammation playing a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of kidney injury [22, 23]. For high-risk patients, preoperative infection control should be prioritized, and postoperative management should focus on aggressive respiratory support and early intervention to reduce the incidence of infections.

Red blood cell transfusion, as an independent risk factor in this study, operates through a complex mechanism. The relationship between red blood cell transfusion and postoperative AKI has been extensively studied, particularly in cardiovascular surgeries, where larger transfusion volumes are associated with a higher risk of AKI and worse long-term prognosis [24–26]. Blood loss is common during cardiovascular surgery, necessitating transfusion to improve oxygen delivery. However, excessive transfusion can result in multi-organ failure, including renal injury, and increase in-hospital mortality [27, 28]. Our study shows that the volume of transfusion was significantly higher in the AKI group than in the non-AKI group, suggesting that transfusion volume is closely associated with the occurrence of postoperative AKI. Analysis of the ROC curve shows that the incidence of AKI increases significantly when the transfusion of red blood cells exceeds 5 units, which should prompt heightened attention from clinicians. In this study, we successfully developed a risk prediction model for AKI after modified Morrow septal myectomy and evaluated its clinical applicability using C-index, ROC curve, and decision curve analysis. Compared to models for other cardiac surgeries [29–32], this model demonstrates higher sensitivity and specificity in predicting the occurrence of AKI after modified Morrow procedure. It can assist clinicians in more effectively identifying high-risk patients and support the development of individualized perioperative management plans, ultimately reducing the incidence of AKI.

However, this study has several limitations. As a single-center retrospective analysis, it is susceptible to potential selection bias, and the limited sample size requires further validation of the findings. Future multicentre, large-scale, prospective research could validate and refine the stability and applicability of the AKI prediction model. This would enhance its clinical translational potential and value for broader implementation.

In summary, this study identified the following as independent risk factors for AKI following the modified Morrow procedure in patients with HOCM: elevated preoperative serum creatinine, postoperative hepatic dysfunction, pulmonary infection and red blood cell transfusion. A risk prediction model based on these variables was also developed. This model has the potential to be used for both preoperative risk stratification and postoperative dynamic assessment. In practice, it could help to tailor individualised perioperative management plans, improve the accuracy of renal function monitoring in high-risk patients, facilitate the early detection of AKI and inform timely interventions, such as optimising fluid management, minimising exposure to nephrotoxic agents and refining transfusion strategies. Further prospective, multicentre studies are necessary to validate the robustness of the model and facilitate its integration into clinical practice.

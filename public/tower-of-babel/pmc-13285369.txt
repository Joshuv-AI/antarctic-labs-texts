Optimizing the timing of autologous skin cell suspension after enzymatic debridement of porcine burns: Evaluation with digital image speckle correlation
Authors: Shi Fu, Rachel Bonn, Huiting Luo, Robert Ruzic, Zachery Harris, Erica Heller, Steve McClain, Eugene Jiang, Briman Yang, Miriam Rafailovich, Gurtej Singh, Steven Sandoval, Marcia Simon, Mohammed H Arbab, Rachel Brownlee, Lior Rosenberg, Adam J Singer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnzymatic debridement preserves viable dermis in deep partial-thickness burns, yet the optimal timing of autologous skin cell suspension (ASCS) delivery remains unclear. We evaluated whether ASCS enhances biomechanical restoration after enzymatic debridement, whether a 24-hour delay alters outcomes, and whether digital image speckle correlation provides a sensitive, noninvasive measure of healing. In a porcine dorsal burn model, wounds were treated with ASCS immediately or 24 hours after enzymatic debridement, with bacitracin serving as control. Digital image speckle correlation measurements quantified force propagation across the wound bed using optical-flow method, whereas histology assessed re-epithelialization and dermal remodeling. Complementary in vitro studies examined cytotoxicity of residual debridement enzyme. ASCS significantly increased the restoration of force propagation compared with bacitracin by day 28 (P < .0001), indicating improved biomechanical integration of the wound bed. Immediate and delayed ASCS application produced similar healing trajectories, with modest differences at the final time point. Enzyme concentrations ≤0.5% preserved viability, whereas higher concentrations were detrimental. Digital image speckle correlation measurements paralleled histological remodeling and revealed spatially resolved mechanical recovery not captured by ordinal clinical or histological scoring systems. Together, these findings establish digital image speckle correlation as a noninvasive method for assessing wound healing and support early ASCS application after enzymatic debridement without compromising biomechanical recovery.Keywords: Autologous cell therapy, Digital image speckle correlation, Enzymatic debridement, Porcine burn model, Wound biomechanics

Introduction

Although most burns injuries are superficial, with little or no eschar, and heal uneventfully, deep partial-thickness (DPT) and full-thickness burns often require more than 2–3 weeks to heal, resulting in significant scarring and disfigurement (Cubison et al, 2006; Deitch et al, 1983). Timely and effective removal of the necrotic tissue followed by re-epithelization is necessary to reduce scarring and have optimized functional outcomes. Excisional debridement followed by split-thickness skin grafting has traditionally been regarded as the standard treatment for deep burns (Janžekovič, 1970; Orgill, 2009; Šuca et al, 2024). More recently, early and selective enzymatic debridement with a bromelain-based mixture of proteolytic enzymes (NexoBrid) has emerged as an alternative to surgical excision (Hirche et al, 2020; Rosenberg et al, 2015, 2014; Shoham et al, 2024). Furthermore, when the wounded area is large, or skin grafts are available, autologous skin cell suspensions (ASCSs) have emerged as an alternative regenerative strategy to replace conventional split-thickness skin grafting in burns (Gravante et al, 2007; Holmes et al, 2019, 2018; Peirce and Carolan-Rees, 2019; Wood et al, 2012). Cells delivered in suspension may exhibit greater metabolic activity and broader distribution across the wound surface, facilitating more uniform and potentially faster re-epithelialization than conventional grafts (Bush et al, 2024).

Although both enzymatic debridement and ASCS have independently reduced the need for autografting, their combined use has not been systematically evaluated (Aoki et al, 2024; Kahn et al, 2023; Yamashita et al, 2023). In particular, the optimal timing of ASCS application after enzymatic debridement remains uncertain. Manufacturer recommendations allow ASCS application as early as 2 hours after debridement, yet in practice, many clinicians delay treatment by 24–48 hours owing to concerns that residual enzymatic activity may impair cell viability or “take.” Furthermore, the formation of a transient fibrinous layer (pseudoeschar) after enzymatic debridement complicates clinical judgment, raising additional uncertainty about the best protocol for sequential use of these technologies. Preclinical models are needed to clarify whether residual enzymatic activity interferes with ASCS efficacy and to identify the optimal timing for combined applications.

In this study, we first performed in vitro tests to determine the cytotoxicity levels of the debridement formulation. We then investigated the healing response of DPT dorsal burns in a porcine model after application of an autologous disaggregated cell suspension immediately or 24 hours after enzymatic debridement. Outcomes were compared with those of a control group that received only a topical antibiotic ointment. Healing progression was assessed through conventional histological analysis of H&E-stained biopsies and complemented by digital image speckle correlation (DISC), a noninvasive imaging technique capable of generating spatially resolved maps of tissue deformation. Whereas histological biopsies provide invasive, localized samples of the wound, DISC captures large-area, real-time mechanical changes across the wound and its interface with surrounding intact tissue. Propagation of mechanical forces across different skin layers can serve as a meaningful indicator of wound healing progression (Biggs et al, 2020). By integrating DISC-derived mechanical mapping with standard clinical and histological endpoints, we provide an objective, noninvasive characterization of biomechanical skin recovery that complements conventional clinical scales, such as the Modified Vancouver Scar Scale (MVSS), and mitigates the high variability observed when assessing DPT burn healing.

In vitro cytotoxicity of the enzymatic debridement agent

The protocol for in vitro testing is outlined in Figure 1a and described in Materials and Methods section. Human mesenchymal cells were plated on tissue culture plastic and exposed to different clinically equivalent doses of Nexobrid for 4 hours. Phase-contrast images of the cells show that Nexobrid initiated cell clumping after exposure to 0.1–1.0% dilutions. Cell detachment and rounding are observed at higher doses (Figure 1b). After pooling detached and adherent cells and replating, only wells originating from ≤2% dilutions exposure established cell coverage after 16 hours; >2% dilutions remained sparsely repopulated with limited recovery. AlamarBlue measured at 16 hours corroborated these observations: viability at 0.1–0.5% was indistinguishable from PBS control (all P ≥ .58, nonsignificant), 1% dilution showed a nonsignificant reduction (P = .074, nonsignificant), 2% produced a modest but significant decrease (∗P < .05), and 5–10% dilutions dropped to near-background levels with strong significance (∗∗P < .01). Together, these data indicate that residual enzymatic material ≤0.5% dilutions is well-tolerated, measurable effects begin around 1–2%, and ≥5% dilutions is strongly cytotoxic.

Restoration of the biomechanical properties of the burns is greater for ASCS-treated wounds

A schematic of the technique applied to porcine wounds is shown in Figure 2a. A gentle, standardized indentation force of 4 N was applied adjacent to the wound margin using a digital pressure gauge with the probe to induce deformation within the wound bed and surrounding intact skin. Animals were anesthetized to accommodate concurrent procedures (Materials and Methods provides the details), including debridement and dressing changes, although anesthesia is not required for the indentation itself. A camera with a minimum resolution of 4 megapixels was positioned normal to the wound surface and manually focused on the region of interest. Images were acquired before and during peak indentation under constant illumination with fixed camera position and focal distance.

In Figure 2b, we used DISC to map how a point indentation propagates through porcine dorsal skin; the control measurements were obtained from normal dorsal skin prior to injury, in an area adjacent to the planned wound site. In uninjured skin, optical-flow vectors overlaid on displacement-magnitude heatmaps show a smooth field that decays with distance around the indenter. Specifically, after indentation, the displacement magnitude reaches a maximum value at or near the point of load application. As distance from this point increases, the displacement decays smoothly. We define the active region as the contiguous area where the displacement magnitude remains greater than or equal to 1/e of the peak displacement. This criterion is commonly used in physics and signal processing to characterize spatial decay (Goodman, 2005; Wang et al, 1998) because it provides a reproducible, scale-independent cutoff that captures the core region of meaningful force transmission while suppressing low-level background noise and optical artifacts (such as the off-center markings of DPT wound heatmap). In our analysis, the 1/e contour therefore serves as an objective threshold to distinguish true mechanical propagation from residual motion due to breathing and reflection noise, enabling consistent comparison of propagation area across wounds, time points, and treatment groups. Seven days after the burn, propagation persists in adjacent intact tissue but is markedly reduced within the wound and largely bounded by the wound edge. These maps provide a quantitative basis to track spatiotemporal changes in biomechanics and to compare treatment groups. At day 7, all wounds exhibit a necrotic eschar with complete loss of epidermis and a dense neutrophilic infiltrate. DISC maps at this stage show minimal force transmission into the wound bed.

By day 14, early re-epithelialization is evident at the wound margins, accompanied by initial collagen deposition, nascent vascular structures, and a mixed inflammatory infiltrate. Consistent with these changes, DISC reveals the onset of intrawound force propagation. In regions covered by residual scab, surface visualization is locally limited; however, displacement vectors traverse beneath these areas, indicating restoration of subsurface mechanical continuity.

Treatment-dependent differences become apparent by day 21. Bacitracin-only wounds show limited collagen deposition and persistent inflammation, with force propagation remaining largely peripheral. Wounds treated with ASCS demonstrate greater intrawound transmission, which correlates with more advanced granulation tissue formation and collagen remodeling. The most pronounced effect is observed in wounds treated with ASCS 24 hours after enzymatic debridement, where propagation extends deeper and more uniformly into the wound bed.

By day 28, bacitracin-only wounds show minimal additional progression. Immediate ASCS treatment results in partial re-epithelialization and modest improvement in force transmission. In contrast, wounds receiving ASCS after a 24-hour delay are fully re-epithelialized, with reorganized dermal architecture, patent microvasculature, and reduced inflammation. DISC maps at this stage show force propagation across most of the wound area, with a mean propagated fraction of 72.4 ± 14.7%. Small nonpropagating regions coincide with biopsy sites, and propagation patterns remain consistent across repeated indentations.

Overall, DISC provides a spatially comprehensive and quantitative assessment of wound healing that complements histology. Unlike H&E analysis, which is limited to discrete biopsy locations, DISC captures biomechanical recovery across the entire wound surface, enabling straightforward comparison of healing progression across treatments.

Clinical and histological metrics of wound healing

Re-epithelialization in Figure 6 was quantified from H&E-stained sections using ImageJ by measuring the extent of epithelial coverage across the wound surface. This metric does not necessarily parallel DISC-derived force propagation, which reflects restoration of mechanical continuity within the full wound bed. Thus, epithelial coverage may be present while the underlying collagen matrix remains insufficiently remodeled to support effective force transmission, as seen at day 14. Moreover, histology samples only a limited wound section, whereas DISC assesses the entire wound bed. Thus, although the re-epithelialization data qualitatively agree with the DISC findings, the large variability in re-epithelialization limits definitive comparison of healing progression among treatment groups.

The MVSS evaluates pliability, height, vascularity, and pigmentation, yielding a composite score from 0 to 14 (Figure 7). MVSS did not differ significantly between treatment groups, consistent with the limited sensitivity of ordinal clinical scoring for detecting biomechanical differences during early remodeling.

The MVSS and the modified Ehrlich–Hunt scoring system are used semiquantitative, ordinal tools for clinical and histological assessment of wound healing. However, both rely on categorical grading and subjective interpretation rather than direct physical measurements. These characteristics reduce sensitivity to subtle or spatially heterogeneous changes in tissue remodeling and biomechanics, particularly during early healing, and may contribute to increased variability and nonsignificant differences between treatment groups. This is apparent in the pig model we studied, where the scoring scales were too coarse to describe subtle variability within the wounds, resulting in large standard deviations and nonsignificant outcomes. The epithelization score comes closest to the results we obtained with DISC, but here as well, the error bars from the subjective evaluations are too large to be conclusive. Furthermore, surface appearance, as shown in the histological images, is insufficient to determine connectivity to the underlying tissue, increasing the error.

Discussion

Considerations regarding the timing between ASCS and debridement: the importance of rinsing

It is well-established that ASCS therapies can accelerate wound healing in porcine models (Carney et al, 2021; Li et al, 2025). These cells provide a rich source of several growth factors such as EGF, VEGF, KGF, and TGF-β, which stimulate epithelialization, angiogenesis, and dermal remodeling. Recent experimental studies in pigs have shown that ASCS accelerate re-epithelialization, enhance vascularization, and improve collagen architecture, leading to stronger and more durable regenerated tissue (Li et al, 2025).

In the case of burn wounds, removal of necrotic tissue is critical for the cell spray to adhere to the wound bed. Unlike split-thickness skin grafting, which contains dermal elements that can adhere and vascularize even on suboptimal beds, cell suspensions are highly dependent on a clean, viable dermal surface for adhesion, proliferation, and migration. Enzymatic debridement is currently used in cleaning the wound bed through digestion of necrotic material. Despite recent publications (Kaita et al, 2025; Shoham et al, 2024) that have shown the effectiveness of Nexobrid in removing burn eschar, no data were obtained as to the optimal time after application for ASCS deposition. Because the same mechanism that digests tissue can also impact cell membranes and extracellular matrix deposition, the timing of additional treatments after debridement may be impacted by the concentration of remnant debridement materials. Therefore, we also investigated to which degree the enzymatic solution is cytotoxic and how much dilution is required before ASCS can be applied.

We determined cell viability as a function of Nexobrid concentration diluted in PBS, where we show significant cytotoxicity from concentrations >1%. At lower concentrations, 0.1–0.5% Nexobrid is effective at detaching cells from their extracellular matrix, but replating indicates that viability is well-maintained.

These results indicate that extensive rinsing of the wound is required after debridement to remove the residual Nexobrid solutions. Otherwise, an appropriate delay would be required to allow resorption and re-establishment of hemostasis (Pfister et al, 2023). Hence, extensive rinsing and washing of the wound were performed after debridement in this experiment.

Impact of ASCS application on wound healing: timing and treatments

DPT burns were performed on 4 Yorkshire pigs across the dorsal area, which were all debrided and rinsed with Nexobrid 24 hours later. ASCS was then applied immediately or 24 hours later in wounds that were immediately adjacent to each other to remove any uncertainty due to the wound location. Two wounds on each pig did not receive ASCS, and all wounds were treated with bacitracin after day 7 to prevent infection. The healing of all wounds was monitored with punch biopsies obtained from different locations in the wound (Figure 9) and using DISC method to evaluate force propagation across the wound boundary. Accuracy in the determination of the healing progression of the wounds requires multiple indentations, yet removing biopsies from the wound area is painful, is invasive, and interferes with healing. The DISC technique was applied to correlate biomechanical measurements with histopathological findings from H&E-stained biopsies. Comparisons with established scoring systems, including MVSS (Wihastyoko et al, 2022) and modified Ehrlich–Hunt scoring system (Guo et al, 2020; Liu et al, 2012), were also attempted; however, these scoring methods were subjective, and the ordinal structure introduced too much variability for definitive conclusions. In this manner, we could illustrate the DISC protocol as an alternate, quantitative, minimally invasive technique that would allow more frequent measurements. Therefore, we performed indentation analysis of the wound to determine the force propagation and then removed tissue using the punch biopsy.

Schematic of the schedule and locations of serials punch biopsies collected from each burn wound. DISC mechanical indentation test was not performed on day 10. DISC, digital image speckle correlation.

Representative data from a series of wounds with each type of treatment where the healing was followed from day 7 to day 28 are shown in Figure 3; 3 indentations were taken consecutively at each time point where we see the high degree of reproducibility. On day 7, negligible propagation of the force occurs across the wound boundaries in all cases. The corresponding H&E staining shows lack of epithelium, disorganized collagen, and absence of epidermis. A large neutrophil layer is observed migrating toward the surface separating the inflammatory region from the underlying granulation tissue. This lack of connectivity between the dermal layers is consistent with the DISC data, which show propagation of the dermal indentation only in the area away from the wound. In the subsequent weeks, as the H&E pathology slides indicate progressive healing of the wound and re-establishment of connectivity between the different layers, the propagation of the force into the wound increases. Closer examination of the propagation pattern across the wound on day 28 shows extensive propagation and an abrupt blistered region at the center, which probably corresponds to the locus of a previous biopsy. Observation of the force propagating across this region is consistent with the underlying healing of the tissue observed in the H&E sample, which allows the force to propagate despite surface deformations. A distinct difference is observed, confirming the acceleration of wound healing when ASCS are applied (Figure 4). In agreement with previous reports, minimal healing occurs till day 21 in the absence of ASCS (Damaraju et al, 2022), whereas significant healing had already occurred with application (P < .0001). Only a marginally significant difference in the progression of the healing is observed between ASCS being applied immediately and that applied 24 hours later.

The extent of healing at day 28 was evaluated across all wounds pooled from the 4 pigs. As shown in Figure 5, wounds that did not receive ASCS exhibited low variance in healing outcomes, indicating minimal interanimal variability. This was further supported by per-animal analyses, in which 2-tailed t-tests revealed no significant differences among pigs (Figure 10), confirming that healing outcomes were not driven by individual animal effects.

In contrast, the increased spread observed in ASCS-treated wounds is likely attributable to variability in delivered cell dose. Cell yield differed between animals, and effective cell coverage was influenced by spray runoff, which depended on local wound bed orientation. Despite this variability, ASCS treatment produced a robust and statistically significant enhancement in wound healing compared with bacitracin alone. Differences between immediate and 24-hour delayed ASCS application were modest and did not meaningfully alter overall healing outcomes.

These findings have important clinical implications. Given the demonstrated benefits of ASCS and the comparable biomechanical recovery with immediate application, the advantages of early treatment, including reduced infection risk and simplified clinical workflow, are likely to outweigh any marginal benefit associated with delayed application.

In this DPT burn model, DISC provided a quantitative, spatially resolved assessment of biomechanical recovery that complemented histological evaluation while avoiding the variability inherent to invasive, ordinal scoring systems. Application of ASCS produced a robust and statistically significant enhancement of wound healing compared with topical antibiotic treatment alone, despite interwound variability related to cell delivery. Healing outcomes were consistent across animals, and differences between immediate and 24-hour delayed ASCS application were modest and did not meaningfully alter overall recovery. These findings support DISC as a reliable functional endpoint for monitoring wound healing and indicate that early ASCS application after adequate wound rinsing can be implemented clinically without compromising biomechanical restoration.

Materials and Methods

Study design

We used 4 female Yorkshire pigs weighing approximately 30 kg and approximately of age 3 months. We conducted a prospective, randomized, blinded animal trial to test the study hypotheses. The study was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC2024-00034) and conducted in the Division of Animal Research. The methods and reporting of our results follow the format recommended in the Animal Research Reporting of in vivo Experiments document (Percie du Sert et al, 2020).

Animal handling, sedation, and anesthesia

To acclimate, the animals were fed standard pig chow for 1 week with access to water ad lib. We exposed the animals to recurring cycles of light and darkness of 12 hours each.

After a 12-hour fast, pigs received an intramuscular injection of ketamine (10 mg/kg), dexmedetomidine (0.03 mg/kg), and butorphanol (0.2 mg/kg) for induction of anesthesia. Once immobile, animals were provided with 2–4% isoflurane in oxygen (1.5 l/min) through endotracheal intubation for prolonged procedures and nose cone for short procedures, such as a dressing change. A transdermal fentanyl patch (75 mcg/h) was placed on clipped skin in the dorsal lumbar region and secured with Tegaderm (3M, St. Paul, MN). After burn induction, an intramuscular injection of buprenorphine (0.02 mg/kg) was given to provide analgesia until the fentanyl reached maximal plasma levels. At the end of the experimental procedures, the pigs received an intravenous dose of atipamezole (0.003 mg/kg) to aid in recovery.

Before burn infliction, the skin was cleaned with soap and water, followed by 70% isopropyl alcohol, and the hair was clipped with an electric clipper. All handling and care were in accordance with the national guidelines established by the National Research Council’s guide for the care and use of laboratory animals (National Research Council Committee for the Update of the Guide for the and Use of Laboratory, 2011).

Burn infliction

The porcine model for inflicting burns was described previously (Singer and McClain, 2003). Using a radiant heat emitting device set at 600 °C and applied to the animal skin for 10 seconds, we inflicted 14, 5 × 5 cm DPT burns evenly distributed on both sides of the vertebral column over the paraspinal muscles between the forelegs and hind legs of the pigs, as shown in Figure 11. In all burns, the necrotic epidermis was immediately scraped away with the blunt side of a tissue forceps. The burns were then covered with a topical antibiotic (Bacitracin Zinc, Taro Pharmaceuticals) and nonadherent dressing (Telfa, Cardinal Health).

Study design shows the locations of wounds obtained in each of the 4 pigs with their corresponding treatments.

Enzymatic debridement

Approximately 24 hours after burn creation, the animals were sedated and anesthetized, and all burns were soaked with normal saline for a period of 2 hours. The burns were then treated with a bromelain-enriched enzymatic preparation (NexoBrid), which was applied under an occlusive dressing for a period of 4 hours followed by 2 hours of soaking with saline. The clinical dose was ∼2.2 g NexoBrid per wound (≈88 mg/cm2). After the soaking period, the wounds were cleaned thoroughly with soap and sterile water.

In vitro cytotoxicity of the enzymatic debridement agent

To test whether the residual of Nexobrid will harm the cells, human mesenchymal stromal cells were isolated from extracted human wisdom teeth at the Stony Brook School of Dental Medicine using standard enzymatic digestion protocols approved by the institutional review board (IRB#20076778) and cultured in α-MEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, catalog number A5670701) and 2% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco, catalog number 15140122). Cells were seeded into 24-well plates at 1 × 104 cells/cm2 and allowed to attach for 16 hours at 37 °C, 5% carbon dioxide.

After 16 hours of incubation, culture medium was aspirated, and wells were rinsed twice with PBS (Gibco, catalog number 14190250) to avoid serum inhibition of bromelain. Cells were then exposed to NexoBrid diluted in PBS to final concentrations of 0.1%, 0.2%, 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 5%, and 10%, with PBS serving as the control. The culture was incubated for 4 hours, after which phase-contrast images were acquired immediately before the collection and replating step.

After exposure, all the cells were collected as follows: the entire supernatant from each well (containing any detached cells) was collected and retained individually. The still, adherent cells were detached with trypsin. The trypsinized cell suspension was combined with the corresponding retained supernatant, neutralized with the culture medium, and centrifuged at 1000 r.p.m. for 10 minutes. Supernatants were discarded, pellets were gently resuspended in fresh culture medium, and cells were replated into a new 24-well plate in a 1-to-1 map with the original well plate layout. After a 16-hour recovery/reattached, wells were imaged again. In addition, cell metabolic activity was then quantified with AlamarBlue, following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, reagent (10% v/v in culture medium) was added, and plates were incubated for 2 hours; absorbance was then measured at 570 nm using 600 nm as the reference wavelength. Cell-free background blanks were subtracted.

Three independent trials were performed for each dilution, and the results are reported as the mean of these trials.

Cell disaggregation

The RECELL System was used for the ASCS preparation. Using an electric dermatome, a 2 × 6 cm2 partial thickness skin sample, harvested at a thickness of approximately 0.75 mm, was taken from each animal and used to prepare the ASCS, following the manufacturer’s instructions. The skin sample was incubated for 15–20 minutes in a warmed proprietary enzyme solution (RECELL Enzyme) to breakdown adhesions between cells and the extracellular matrix, including dermoepidermal junction adhesions. After removing this enzyme solution and placement on the device’s sterile tray, the skin sample was tested to determine whether the epidermis and dermal tissue could be freely separated. When this was possible, a buffer solution was used to rinse the skin sample, after which, the skin sample was placed dermal side down on the device’s sterile tray. The skin sample was completely disaggregated by vigorously scraping both the dermal and epidermal layers. The disaggregated skin cells were suspended in a buffer solution, filtered, drawn into the application syringe, and applied over the debrided wound bed. The sampled cell suspension was counted using hemocytometer.

Wound treatments

Within each pig, 1 side of the animal containing half of the wounds was treated with the ASCS immediately after completing the enzymatic debridement and after debridement soaking, whereas the other half of the wounds on the contralateral side was treated with the ASCS 24 hours after completing the enzymatic debridement. Determination of which side was treated with the cell suspension was randomly allocated using a random number table. Figure 11 represents a schematic outline of the study design. In each pig, 2 additional DPT burns were created, 1 on each side, which were treated with bacitracin ointment only, serving as control wounds.

In summary, the total number of DPT burns created was 56, of which 24 were treated with ASCS immediately after enzymatic burn debridement, 24 were treated with ASCS 24 hours after enzymatic debridement, and 8 were treated with a topical antibiotic serving as controls. All burns were evenly distributed between the 4 experimental animals.

Dressing

Telfa Clear Wound Dressing (Medtronic) was applied to the inferior margin of the wound before proceeding with the skin cell application. The cell suspension was applied to the wound from the most elevated part to the least elevated part, so that run-off waste would be minimized. One sixth of the application was delivered to the entire surface of each wound. Finally, the Telfa Clear Wound Dressing was wrapped over the treated site and secured in place with skin staples.

The bacitracin-treated control wounds were also covered with Telfa Clear Wound Dressing. For all treatment wounds, a secondary dressing of Xeroform was placed over the primary dressing, and additional padding of gauze and an adhesive bandage was used for exudate absorption and protection. The dressings were then wrapped with outer gauze wraps (Sof-Form, Medline Industries) and adhesive dressings (Tensoplast, BSN Medical S.A.S) to avoid premature dislodgement of the dressings and treatments.

The Telfa Clear primary dressing remained in place for a minimum of 7 days and was not manipulated until the first postoperative wound dressing change. Beginning 48 hours after treatment, secondary dressings were changed every other day to monitor the treated areas and were replaced as appropriate. At each dressing change, the control wounds were treated with the topical antibiotic and Xeroform dressing.

Tissue biopsies and histological assessments

Dressing changes occurred 2–3 times weekly until 28 days after injury. At each dressing change, the wounds were visualized and digitally photographed. Full-thickness 3-mm punch biopsies (Integra) were obtained from all burns on days 7 (upper right), 10 (bottom left), 14 (bottom right), and 21 (upper left). The biopsies were in the 4 corners of the burn at least 10 mm away from the edges to avoid re-epithelialization from the uninjured margins. An additional full-thickness 8-mm punch biopsy was obtained from the center of all burns on day 28 after injury (Figure 9).

Skin biopsies were obtained from each wound on days 7, 10, 14, 21, and 28, immediately after mechanical indentation compliance test (mechanical indentation test was not performed on day 10). Tissue samples were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 5-μm thickness. Sections were stained with H&E to evaluate re-epithelialization, dermal regeneration, and inflammatory cell infiltration. Stained slides were imaged through brightfield microscopy and semiqualitatively assessed.

Mechanical skin compliance assessment using DISC

To quantify biomechanical remodeling during healing, we measured surface compliance of each wound weekly with DISC, a noninvasive optical method that maps surface deformation in response to a controlled mechanical stimulus. The method consists of 2 key components: (i) application of a standardized mechanical force to deform the wound surface and (ii) optical tracking of the resulting deformation to generate full-field displacement maps. DISC leverages intrinsic skin texture, including pores and fine surface features, as natural speckle markers, eliminating the need for invasive probes or biopsies.

A schematic illustration of the technique as applied to measuring wound healing on a pig is shown in Figure 2a. For each measurement, a gentle and standardized indentation was applied manually to the wound bed using a compliant, flat-tipped probe while the animal was under anesthesia. The indentation was performed adjacent to the wound margin to induce deformation within both the wound bed and surrounding intact tissue. A camera with at least 4 megapixels is positioned normal to the wound surface and focused manually on the target region. For each indentation event, 2 images were analyzed—an undeformed reference image acquired immediately before indentation and a deformed image captured at peak indentation—under constant illumination with fixed camera position and focal distance.

DISC analysis is performed using a custom Python-based image processing pipeline (Fu et al, 2025). Displacement fields were computed from paired undeformed and deformed images using the optical flow algorithm. Optical flow is a computer-vision algorithm that quantifies surface deformation by estimating pixel-wise displacements between an undeformed reference image and a deformed image acquired under a mechanical stimulus. For a pixel located at (x,y) in the reference image, a square neighborhood N(x,y) centered at that pixel is defined. This subset is compared with candidate neighborhoods N(x′,y′) of identical size in the deformed image by computing a normalized cross-correlation similarity function:

where I is grayscale intensity. The coordinates (x′,y′) that maximize Sx,y correspond to the most likely displaced location of the original pixel. The displacement vector u(x,y)=(x′−x,y′−y) is therefore obtained for every pixel, yielding a dense displacement field over the entire image (Saadon et al, 2023).

In this study, motion was computed using the Lucas–Kanade method, which assumes that displacement is small and approximately constant within a local neighborhood and enforces brightness constancy to relate spatial and temporal image gradients to pixel motion, making it well-suited for capturing subtle in-plane surface displacement, generating a set of vectors describing the magnitude and directionality of the displacements.

The displacement vector fields (Figure 12a) are visualized as heatmaps of the vector amplitude overlaid onto the direct photographic image (Figure 12b). Regions exhibiting larger displacements correspond to softer, more compliant tissue, whereas regions with attenuated displacement reflect increased stiffness or mechanical discontinuity.

In Figure 12c, we plot the amplitude of the displacement as a function of radial distance along the trajectory. An active deformation region was defined as the area in which displacement magnitude exceeded 1/e of the peak displacement, a threshold selected to suppress background noise while preserving mechanically meaningful propagation. The locus of points where the force decays to max/e is illustrated in Figure 12d. A force-propagation ratio (R) was calculated as the fraction of the wound area exhibiting active propagation, normalized to an equivalent area of uninjured skin, which was defined as 100%. Increasing R values indicate restoration of mechanical continuity and functional integration of the wound tissue.

Natural skin pores act as speckle markers, and optical-flow–based tracking estimates pixel-wise displacement between images captured before and during a standardized mechanical input. Because porcine skin is broadly tethered to underlying musculature, we standardized the stimulus by applying a controlled external indentation adjacent to the wound margin, typically within 5 mm of the wound edge. A force of 4 N was applied through a probe attached to a digital pressure gauge, which generated a deformation zone within and around the wound. A high-resolution camera recorded the skin surface under constant illumination during each stimulus. Image pairs (before and at peak indentation) were processed to compute displacement fields and corresponding heatmaps. At each time point, 3 indentations were performed at nearby positions within the same periwound region, with over 1-minute recovery allowed between indentations, and each indentation was analyzed independently for the force-propagation ratio (R value), and the values were then averaged and reported as mean ± SD for each wound.

Within intact skin, the epidermal, dermal, and subdermal layers transmit load as a continuous structure; injury disrupts this continuity and limits how far deformation propagates. Accordingly, larger local displacements indicate softer, more compliant tissue that more closely resembles uninjured skin, whereas smaller displacements indicate stiffer regions such as scar. These spatial maps provided a noninvasive, quantitative readout of tissue integration and functional recovery over time. Python 12.1 was utilized for computational analysis with optical flow algorithms.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were performed in GraphPad Prism (version 10). For outcomes with the groups and independent observations (no pairing), we used a 1-way Welch’s ANOVA with the Brown–Forsythe test to accommodate potential heteroscedasticity (unequal SDs). When the omnibus test was significant, all pairwise comparisons were conducted with Dunnett’s T3 multiple-comparisons procedure, which does not assume equal variances. Statistical significance was defined as ∗P < .05, ∗∗P < .01, ∗∗∗P < .001, and ∗∗∗∗P < .0001; ns denotes not significant.

A permeability model considering the dynamic effect of stress on gas adsorption under discontinuous compression
Authors: Yang Yang, Changbao Jiang, Diandong Hou, Fazhi Yan, Zhijie Wen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the continuous advance of coal mining, the normal stress in front of coal seams will continue to increase, resulting in changes of internal structure and permeability of coal seams. During the continuous advance of mining face, the increase of vertical stress somewhere in front of coal seams may not be a transient process, but a gradual change process. Therefore, this paper carried out the seepage test of gas-containing coal under discontinuous loading axial stress. The stress, deformation and permeability of coal specimens were measured and recorded during the test, and the influence of discontinuous loading axial stress on the deformation, internal structure and seepage properties of coal specimens were discussed. On this basis, considering the influence of stress on the gas adsorption state, Klinkenberg effect and other factors, combined with Shi-Durucan permeability model, a new permeability model considering the dynamic adsorption state of coal specimens was developed.Keywords: Coalbed methane, Discontinuous compression, Permeability model, Nuclear magnetic resonanceSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

China is the world’s largest coal producer, and China is also a large country of coal resource consumption. As a companion of coal seams, gas can not only cause disasters such as gas emission, but also cause greenhouse effect1,2. The extraction and utilization of gas may not only improve the safety of coal mining, but also suppress the greenhouse effect and promote the utilization of unconventional natural gas resources, so as to indirectly achieve the purpose of energy conservation and emission reduction3,4. No matter which method is adopted for gas extraction, the internal structure and permeability of coal seams are decisive elements affecting extraction effect. Therefore, it is important to study the structural changes and gas flow of coal seams. It will be helpful to recognize and understand the mechanical property and gas flow law of gas-containing coal in the mining process, that will provide important guidance for coal and gas simultaneous extraction.

In recent decades, many scholars had conducted a lot of theoretical and experimental researches about pore structure and seepage law of coal, and got a lot of achievements. Zhang et al.5 conducted triaxial seepage experiments on the coal sample of different coal ranks under different confining pressure conditions, and explored the effects of coal ranks and stress conditions on the strength, permeability and acoustic emission signals of coal. Meng et al.6 measured the porosity and seepage properties of anthracite specimens in the condition of hydrostatic pressure, and analyzed the interaction mechanism between seepage properties and effective stress. Liu et al.7 carried out permeability measurement experiments on coal and sandstone samples under variable confining pressures, studied the relationship between permeability and external pressure, and analyzed changes of fractures field through RFPA numerical simulation software. Zhao et al.8 conducted nitrogen permeation tests on 15 medium and high rank coal specimens in different confining pressures, discussed the control mechanism between effective stress and gas slip. Xia et al.9 used the low-field NMR method to study dynamic changes of permeability of three coal samples with various metamorphic degrees in various confining pressures, and compared them with the traditional test and empirical calculation methods. Some scholars10–15 had carried out conventional triaxial seepage tests on gas-containing coal samples under various conditions, researched the influence of various external factors for the mechanical properties, seepage properties and energy evolution of gas-bearing coal samples, and explored the mechanical mechanism and catastrophe mechanism of gas emission.

In the aspect of permeability model research, most classical permeability models are established through the assumptions of constant overlying stress, uniaxial strain, elastic deformation and structural isotropy, and are developed from the two directions of porosity change and stress state change. Gray16 proposed that seepage properties were relevant with the effective horizontal stress borne by coal seams, and developed the permeability model. Seidle et al.17 assumed coal as a ‘bundled matchstick’ structure, deduced the relationship between permeability and effective hydrostatic pressure, and proposed a permeability evolution model. Palmer and Mansoori18 considered the compressibility of coal seams’ fractures by assuming that the coal seams were uniaxial strain conditions, and built a permeability evolution model. According to the assumption of unidirectional strain, Shi and Durucan19 developed a permeability model using an exponential function based on the interaction between effective stress and seepage properties. Through theoretical derivation, Cui and Bustin20 considered the effect of stress on adsorption, and obtained the evolution model of the permeability during the absorption period. Based on the above classical permeability model, some scholars21–27 comprehensively considered various influencing factors during the coal mining, improved the classical permeability models, and obtained a series of new permeability models.

Currently, most of the existing seepage tests are conducted continuously. However, With the continuous excavation of coal seams, the increase of vertical stress somewhere in front of coal seams may not be a transient process, but a gradual change process. Most of the previous tests simplified this process and carried out continuous tests. But the adsorption characteristics and stress of coal are highly sensitive. When the stress state changes, the adsorption state inside the gas-bearing coal will also change, which will lead to that the continuous triaxial compression test cannot fully characterize the change of the internal adsorption state of gas-containing coal. Thus, the value of permeability obtained will be different from the actual value of permeability on site. And most current permeability models have not taken into account the dynamic effect of stress on adsorption, which affects the accuracy of permeability models.

Based on this, this paper carried out the seepage test of coal under discontinuous compression, and studied the deformation characteristics, pore structure and seepage law of gas-containing coal under discontinuous compression. On this basis, considering the effect of stress on the internal adsorption state of coal, a new permeability model was developed, which can accurately characterize the evolution of permeability under discontinuous compression conditions.

Experiments methods

Basic parameters for coal specimens

The coal specimens selected were from Sichuan, China. The coal seam of this mine is deeply buried, and the roof is mudstone and sandy mudstone. The sealing conditions are good, which is not conducive for the escape of gas to the atmosphere. The geological condition is complex, and the external stress disturbance has a significant impact on the gas migration of coal seams.

The big lump of coal taken from the coal mine were drilled, and then then cut and polished to prepare standard cylindrical coal samples of ϕ50 × h100 mm. Three coal specimens with similar density and wave velocity were selected to conduct experiments. The selected coal specimens were set inside the drying oven and dried at 65℃ until the weight of coal specimens didn’t change. The components of coal seams were presented in Table 1.

Experimental apparatus

RLW-2000 microcomputer servo rock triaxial testing machine used during the seepage test of gas-containing coal under discontinuous compression was shown in Fig. 1. The maximum axial load of the equipment is 2000kN, the limit value of confining pressure is 80 MPa, the limit value of gas pressure is 80 MPa, and the limit value of temperature is 120℃.

RWL-2000 rock triaxial test system.

Experimental program

In this paper, RWL-2000 microcomputer servo rock triaxial testing machine was used to carry out the gas flow test inside coal specimens under the condition of discontinuous compression, and the stress, strain and permeability during the test were collected. The pore data of coal specimens were measured by nuclear magnetic resonance system. In the process of experiments, we use CH4 to replace coalbed methane for seepage test. The detailed test procedures are as follows:

(1) Three coal specimens with similar physical parameters are selected from the prepared coal specimens. The selected three coal specimens are scanned by using nuclear magnetic resonance system to obtain their initial pore parameters, and then the three coal specimens are dried in the drying oven.

(2) The specimen is placed inside the testing device. The internal gas of coal specimens is removed by using vacuum pump. The vacuum extraction time is 12 h.

(3) The axial stress and radial stress are loaded by using the equal loading speed so that the axial stress and radial stress reach hydrostatic pressure state (σ1 = σ2 = σ3 = 3 MPa). At this time, CH4 is introduced into the coal specimen for adsorption, and the pressure of methane is 1 MPa.

(4) Turn on the methane outlet switch after adsorption equilibrium. When the gas flow rate at the outlet is stable, measure the permeability of methane under hydrostatic pressure. Then the axial stress is loaded step by step. The loading range of the axial pressure is 3 MPa-15 MPa, with 1.5 MPa as a grade. Keep the stress stable at each stress level for gas adsorption. When the adsorption equilibrium is completed, measure and record the permeability of coal specimens. The detailed loading method is presented in Fig. 2.

Loading paths of stresses under discontinuous conditions.

(5) Take out coal specimens after the test, and scan the three coal specimens with the nuclear magnetic resonance system to obtain the pore parameters after the test.

Experimental results and analysis

Deformation characteristics and permeability evolution law of gas-containing coal under discontinuous compression

The stress-strain curve of coal specimens under discontinuous compression is presented in Fig. 3. We can see from Fig. 3 that the stress-strain curves of three coal specimens have relatively consistent changes. With the increase of axial stress, the axial strain and volumetric strain increase gradually, the radial strain reduces gradually. The amplitude of axial deformation is much larger than that of radial deformation. The deformation properties under discontinuous compression are not very different from those under continuous compression. The main reason is that the influence of continuous compression and discontinuous compression on coal specimens is mostly reflected in the adsorption state of methane inside the coal specimens, but has small impact for the macroscopic deformation of coal specimens.

The stress-strain curve under discontinuous conditions.

We can also see in Fig. 3 that the axial stress-strain curves approximately present as a straight line. According to the existing research, there are generally four stages in the conventional triaxial compression process of coal specimens. The stress-strain curve is an approximate straight line, which shows that the coal specimen is currently in elastic stage. Namely, there is basically no damage inside the coal specimens, which is also a prerequisite for derivation of the permeability model in the following paper.

The variation of axial stress and permeability with axial strain under discontinuous compression is presented in Fig. 4. The change trend of permeability of three coal specimens is relatively consistent, showing a gradual downward trend. The permeability of three coal specimens appears strong correlation to the axial stress. When the axial stress rises, the permeability gradually decreases, and the axial stress is negatively correlated with the permeability. With gradual growth of axial stress, the pores and fractures inside coal specimens are gradually compressed, resulting in the reduction of gas migration space inside coal specimens and the gradual decrease of permeability. The above phenomena shows that the internal structure of coal specimens have a strong stress sensitivity, and the change of stress will have a significant impact on the internal structure of coal specimens. Thus, the seepage properties of coal specimens will change greatly.

Variation of axial stress and permeability with axial strain.

At the same time, we can see from Fig. 4 that the relationship between stress and permeability is basically one-to-one correspondence. When the stress changes, the permeability will also change accordingly, rather than showing the phenomenon that the permeability change lags behind the stress, which is different from the continuous compression test. In the continuous compression test, because the loading rate is generally fast, the permeability generally lags slightly behind the change of stress, that is, the stress has increased and the permeability has not changed. In the discontinuous compression test, when the permeability value is measured, the influence of stress on pores and fractures inside coal specimens has ended, and methane adsorption state inside coal specimens have reached equilibrium again. Therefore, the obtained permeability will not lag behind the stress.

In Fig. 5, the pore size distribution characteristics of three coal specimens has changed to some extent before and after discontinuous compression, and the change trend of pore size distribution characteristic of three coal specimens is basically same. After the discontinuous compression, the micropores and mesopores of three coal specimens increased to a certain extent, while the large pores decreased to a certain extent. The first peak characterizing micropores shifted slightly to the right, while the second peak characterizing mesopores and the third peak characterizing macropores shifted slightly to the left. Through the above phenomena, it can be explained that the discontinuous loading of axial stress compresses the internal macropores and mesopores in the coal specimen, resulting in the transformation from macropores to mesopores and from mesopores to micropores. Finally the macropores reduce, and the micropores and mesopores increase. Because the larger the pore diameter is, the more sensitive it is to the stress, and the easier it is to form irreversible compression deformation. The compression effect of stress on the pores with small pore diameter is more to produce the elastic deformation. When the applied stress is unloaded, the pores with small pore diameter will return to their original shape.

As shown in Fig. 6, the adsorption space and seepage space of three coal specimens have completely different fractal characteristics. The fractal dimension of adsorption space is under 1.5, while the fractal dimension of seepage space is close to 3. After discontinuous loading axial stress, the fractal dimension of adsorption space of S1 specimen increased from 1.27 to 1.3301, and the fitting accuracy was 0.7969 and 0.7889 respectively; The fractal dimension of adsorption space of S2 specimen increased from 1.2002 to 1.2583, and the fitting accuracy was 0.8055 and 0.8025 respectively; The fractal dimension of adsorption space of S3 increased from 0.9112 to 1.0692, and the fitting accuracy was 0.8039 and 0.8234 respectively. According to fractal theory, the fractal dimension of pores structure inside the coal specimen is among 2 and 3. The value of fractal dimension determines the complexity of pores structure inside coal specimens. In Fig. 6, the fractal dimension of internal adsorption space of the coal specimen is less than 1.5, so the adsorption space of coal specimens does not have fractal characteristics. After discontinuous loading axial stress, the fractal dimension of S1 specimen decreased from 2.9721 to 2.964, and the fitting accuracy was 0.8821 and 0.8924 respectively; The fractal dimension of S2 specimen decreased from 2.9785 to 2.9703, and the fitting accuracy was 0.8901 and 0.8627 respectively; The fractal dimension of seepage space of S3 specimen is reduced from 2.9851 to 2.9828, and the fitting accuracy is 0.9482 and 0.9405 respectively. The above data indicates that the seepage space of coal specimens has fractal characteristics, and the reduction of fractal dimension of seepage space indicates that the structural complexity of seepage space of coal specimens is reduced after the axial stress discontinuous loading, that is, the pore size range of seepage space is reduced. The main reason is that the discontinuous loading of axial stress compresses the large pores inside coal specimens, resulting in irreversible deformation, thus reducing the pore size range of the seepage space.

Construction and verification of permeability model considering the influence of stress to adsorption

The seepage characteristics of coal seams are the main elements that influence the effect of gas extraction, and various stress disturbances caused by coal seam mining are inevitable. At the same time, with the disturbance of external stress, seepage properties of coal seams will also change accordingly, which brings challenges to the safe and efficient gas extraction. At present, scholars have proposed many permeability models to characterize the change process of permeability during stress disturbance. However, the existing permeability models generally only consider the compressive effect of stress to the fractures of coal, and do not consider the impact of stress on adsorption characteristics. Therefore, the existing stress sensitive permeability models are inadequate. Based on this, a new permeability model considering impact of stress on adsorption will be constructed and verified.

Basic assumptions

(1) Assuming the specimen is an isotropic medium.

(2) Assuming the specimen is during compaction stage when applying hydrostatic pressure, and in elastic stage after applying hydrostatic pressure.

(3) Assuming that the pores in the specimen are micropores after hydrostatic pressure is applied.

(4) Gas flow conforms to Darcy’s law.

During coal mining, the adsorption and migration state of gas in coal seams plays a vital role in the safe mining of coal seams. As the mining face continues to advance, the transformation of coal seam stress state may not be a transient process, but a gradual change process. Previous experiments have mostly simplified this process and conducted continuous loading tests. However, the adsorption and desorption of gas are highly sensitive to stress, and when the stress state changes, the adsorption state of gas inside the coal will also change accordingly. Therefore, continuous loading tests cannot fully characterize the dynamic equilibrium state between internal stress and gas adsorption in coal.

Although most of the permeability models built in the past have considered the effect of adsorption to gas flow inside coal seams, they have not considered the influence of external stress on the adsorption state itself, which will lead to a big error between the theoretical permeability and actual permeability. The permeability model considering the influence of stress on adsorption constructed in this paper can make up for the shortcomings of previous permeability models and reduce the error between theoretical permeability and actual permeability.

Conclusion

According to the needs of coal and gas simultaneous extraction, this paper studied deformation characteristics and gas migration law of gas-bearing coal in discontinuous compression by using a series of test equipment. And the stress, deformation and seepage properties of coal specimens in the test were measured and recorded. The effects of discontinuous compression on the deformation, pore size distribution and permeability of coal specimens were discussed. The specific research results were as follows:

Firstly, the permeability of coal specimens had a strong correlation with the axial stress under the discontinuous loading of axial stress. With increase of the axial stress, the permeability gradually decreased, and the axial stress was negatively correlated with the permeability. The relationship between stress and permeability was basically one-to-one correspondence. When the stress changed, the permeability would also change accordingly, without the phenomenon that the change of permeability lags behind the stress.

Secondly, the discontinuous loading of axial stress compressed the macropores and mesopores in the coal specimen, which led to the transformation from macropores to mesopores and from mesopores to micropores, and finally led to the reduction of macropores and the increase of micropores and mesopores in the coal specimen. The adsorption space of coal specimens did not have fractal characteristics, while the seepage space of coal specimens had fractal characteristics. After discontinuous loading axial stress, the fractal dimension of seepage space was reduced, indicating that the structural complexity of seepage space of coal specimens was reduced after discontinuous loading axial stress, namely the pore size range of the seepage space was reduced.

Thirdly, the influence of stress to gas adsorption state inside coal specimens was qualitatively analyzed. Comprehensively considering the effect of stress on the gas adsorption state, Klinkenberg effect and other factors, a permeability model considering the dynamic adsorption state inside the coal specimens was developed, and it had a good characterization effect on changes in permeability.

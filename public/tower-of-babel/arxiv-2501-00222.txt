Presentations for monoids of endomorphisms of a star graph
Vítor H. Fernandes, Jörg Koppitz and T. Musunthia

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the monoids of all endomorphisms, of all weak endomorphisms, of all strong endomorphisms and of all strong weak endomorphisms of a star graph with a finite number of vertices. Our main objective is to exhibit a presentation for each of them.

1
On presentations

Let
X
be a set and denote by
X^{*}
the free monoid generated by

X
. Often, in this context, the set
X
is called an
alphabet
and its elements are called
words

A
monoid presentation
is an ordered pair
\langle X\mid R\rangle
, where
X
is an alphabet and
R
is a subset of

X^{*}\times X^{*}
. An element
(u,v)
of
X^{*}\times X^{*}
is called a

relation
of
X^{*}
and it is usually represented by
u=v
.
A monoid
M
is said to be
defined by a presentation

\langle X\mid R\rangle
if
M
is
isomorphic to
X^{*}/{\sim_{R}}
, where
\sim_{R}
denotes the congruence on
X^{*}
generated by
R
,
i.e.
\sim_{R}
is the smallest
congruence on
X^{*}
containing
R
.
Suppose that
X
is a generating set of a monoid
M
and let
u=v
be a relation of
X^{*}
.
We say that a relation
u=v
of
X^{*}
is
satisfied
by
X
if
u=v
is an equality in
M
.
For more details, see
[
24
]
or
[
29
]
.

A well-known direct method to obtain a presentation for a monoid, that we might consider folklore,
is given by the following result; see, for example,
[
29
, Proposition 1.2.3]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
M
be a monoid generated by a set
X
.
Then,
\langle X\mid R\rangle
is a presentation for
M
if and only
if the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

The generating set
X
of
M
satisfies all the relations from
R
;

2.

If
w_{1},w_{2}\in X^{*}
are any two words such that
the generating set
X
of
M
satisfies the relation
w_{1}=w_{2}
, then
w_{1}\sim_{R}w_{2}
.

For finite monoids, a standard method to find a presentation
is described by the following result, adapted for the monoid case from

[
29
, Proposition 3.2.2]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Guess and Prove method)
.

Let
M
be a finite monoid, let
X
be a generating set for
M
,
let
R\subseteq X^{*}\times X^{*}
be a set of relations and let

W\subseteq X^{*}
. Assume that the following conditions are
satisfied:

1.

The generating set
X
of
M
satisfies all the relations from
R
;

2.

For each word
w\in X^{*}
, there exists a word
w^{\prime}\in W
such that
w\sim_{R}w^{\prime}
;

3.

|W|\leq|M|
.

Then,
M
is defined by the presentation
\langle X\mid R\rangle
.

Notice that, if
W
satisfies the above conditions, then, in fact,

|W|=|M|
.

Let
X
be an alphabet, let
R\subseteq X^{*}\times X^{*}
be a set of
relations and let
W
be a subset of
X^{*}
. We say that
W
is a set of

canonical forms
for a finite monoid
M

if Conditions 2 and 3 of Theorem
1.2
are satisfied. Suppose
that the empty word belongs to
W
and, for each letter
x\in X
and
for each word
w\in W
, there exists a word
w^{\prime}\in W
such that
wx\sim_{R}w^{\prime}
.
Then, it is easy to show that
W
satisfies Condition 2.

In this paper, we will mainly use the method given by Theorem
1.2
and the condition described above instead of Condition 2.

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
i&i&\cdots&i\end{smallmatrix}\right)=z_{0}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
i&0&\cdots&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
it immediately follows that
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}
and
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z,z_{0}\}
are generating sets of
\mathrm{End}S_{n}
and
\mathrm{swEnd}S_{n}
, respectively.

Next, observe that
\mathrm{wEnd}S_{n}=\mathscr{PT}_{n-1}^{0}\cup\{\alpha\in\mathscr{T}(\Omega_{n-1}^{0})\mid\mbox{$0\alpha\neq 0$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\alpha\subseteq\{0,0\alpha\}$}\}
.
Since
\mathscr{PT}_{n-1}^{0}
is generated by
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0}\}
,
in order to show that
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0},z\}
generates
\mathrm{wEnd}S_{n}
,
it suffices to show that

\{\alpha\in\mathscr{T}(\Omega_{n-1}^{0})\mid\mbox{$0\alpha\neq 0$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\alpha\subseteq\{0,0\alpha\}$}\}
is contained in the monoid generated by
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0},z\}
.
So, take
\alpha\in\mathscr{T}(\Omega_{n-1}^{0})
such that
0\alpha\neq 0
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\alpha\subseteq\{0,0\alpha\}
. Then,

\alpha=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A&B\\
0\alpha&0\alpha&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
for some (disjoint) subsets
A
and
B
of
\Omega_{n-1}
such that
A\cup B=\Omega_{n-1}
.
Let
\alpha_{0}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A&B\\
0&0&0\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\beta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
0&0\alpha&\cdots&0\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
Then,
\alpha_{0},\beta\in\mathscr{PT}_{n-1}^{0}
and
\alpha=\alpha_{0}z\beta
, which concludes the proof.

Finally, it is easy to conclude that any generating set of
\mathrm{End}S_{n}
cannot have less than four elements and that both

\mathrm{swEnd}S_{n}
and
\mathrm{wEnd}S_{n}
cannot be generated by less than five elements.
∎

For
n=3
, as
b_{0}=a_{0}
and
e_{0}=z^{2}
, it is a routine matter to conclude that

\{a_{0},z\}
,
\{a_{0},z,z_{0}\}
and
\{a_{0},c_{0},z\}
are generating sets with minimum size of
\mathrm{End}S_{3}
,
\mathrm{swEnd}S_{3}
and
\mathrm{wEnd}S_{3}
, respectively.
Therefore,
\mathrm{End}S_{3}
,
\mathrm{swEnd}S_{3}
and
\mathrm{wEnd}S_{3}
have ranks
2
,
3
and
3
, respectively.

3
Presentations

Recall that
\mathscr{T}^{0}_{n-1}
and
\mathscr{PT}^{0}_{n-1}
are monoids isomorphic to
\mathscr{T}(\Omega_{n-1})
and
\mathscr{PT}(\Omega_{n-1})
, respectively.

Let
\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\mid R\rangle
be a presentation of
\mathscr{T}^{0}_{n-1}
on the generators
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
and
e_{0}
.
Then, we have:

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n\geqslant 4
,

\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\mid R,a_{0}z=b_{0}z=e_{0}z=z,\penalty\ z^{2}=(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}\rangle

is a presentation of
\mathrm{End}S_{n}
on the generators
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
,
e_{0}
and
z
.

Proof.

First, notice that, it is a routine matter to show that the set of generators
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}
satisfies all the relations

R
,
b_{0}z=a_{0}z=e_{0}z=z
and
z^{2}=(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}
.

Denote by
\sim_{R}
the congruence on
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
generated by
R
and by
\sim
the congruence on
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}

generated by
R\cup\{b_{0}z=a_{0}z=e_{0}z=z,z^{2}=(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}\}
.

Let
w\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
. Then
w=u_{1}zu_{2}z\cdots u_{k}zu
, for some
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k},u\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
and
k\geqslant 0
.
As a consequence of the relations
b_{0}z=a_{0}z=e_{0}z=z
, we have
u_{i}z\sim z
, for
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, and so

w\sim z^{k}u
. On the other hand, considering also the relation
z^{2}=(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}
,
we also get
z^{3}=z^{2}z\sim(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}z\sim z
,
whence
z^{k}u\sim zu
, if
k
is odd, and
z^{k}u\sim z^{2}u\sim(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}u
, if
k
is an even integer greater than zero.
Therefore, we obtain
w\sim zu^{\prime}
or
w\sim u^{\prime}
, for some
u^{\prime}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
.

Now, let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
be such that
w_{1}=w_{2}
(equality in
\mathrm{End}S_{n}
). Our goal is to show that
w_{1}\sim w_{2}
, with which the proof is concluded.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
be such that
w_{i}\sim u_{i}
or
w_{i}\sim zu_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. Hence, we may consider four cases.

case 1:

w_{1}\sim u_{1}
and
w_{2}\sim u_{2}
.

Then,
u_{1}=w_{1}=w_{2}=u_{2}
(equalities in
\mathrm{End}S_{n}
) and so
u_{1}=u_{2}
(equality in
\mathscr{T}^{0}_{n-1}
).
Since
\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\mid R\rangle
is a presentation of
\mathscr{T}^{0}_{n-1}
, we have
u_{1}\sim_{R}u_{2}
,
whence
u_{1}\sim u_{2}
and so
w_{1}\sim w_{2}
.

case 2:

w_{1}\sim u_{1}
and
w_{2}\sim zu_{2}
.
In this case, we have

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
0&1u_{1}&\cdots&(n-1)u_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=u_{1}=w_{1}=w_{2}=zu_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
1&0&\cdots&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
0&1u_{2}&\cdots&(n-1)u_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
1u_{2}&0&\cdots&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),

whence
u_{1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\not\in\mathscr{T}^{0}_{n-1}
,
which is a contradiction. Thus, this case does not occur.

case 3:

w_{1}\sim zu_{1}
and
w_{2}\sim u_{2}
.

Similar to the previous case, this case does not occur.

case 4:

w_{1}\sim zu_{1}
and
w_{2}\sim zu_{2}
.

Then,
zu_{1}=w_{1}=w_{2}=zu_{2}
(equalities in
\mathrm{End}S_{n}
), whence
zu_{1}=zu_{2}
and so
z^{2}u_{1}=z^{2}u_{2}
.
Since
z^{2}=(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}
, we get
(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}u_{1}=(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}u_{2}
, which is an equality in
\mathscr{T}^{0}_{n-1}
.
Therefore, as
\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\mid R\rangle
is a presentation of
\mathscr{T}^{0}_{n-1}
, we have

(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}u_{1}\sim_{R}(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}u_{2}
, whence
(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}u_{1}\sim(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}u_{2}
and so
z^{2}u_{1}\sim z^{2}u_{2}
,
from which follows that
z^{3}u_{1}\sim z^{3}u_{2}
and, as
z^{3}\sim z
, finally, that
zu_{1}\sim zu_{2}
. Thus
w_{1}\sim w_{2}
, by transitivity, as required.
∎

Next, let
\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\mid Q\rangle
be a presentation of
\mathrm{End}S_{n}
on the generators
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
,
e_{0}
and
z
.
Hence, we get:

Theorem 3.2
.

For
n\geqslant 4
,

\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z,z_{0}\mid Q,a_{0}z_{0}=b_{0}z_{0}=e_{0}z_{0}=zz_{0}=z_{0}^{2}=z_{0}a_{0}=z_{0}b_{0}=z_{0}e_{0}=z_{0}\rangle

is a presentation of
\mathrm{swEnd}S_{n}
on the generators
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
,
e_{0}
,
z
and
z_{0}
.

Proof.

We begin by observing that we can routinely show that the set of generators
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z,z_{0}\}
satisfies all the relations

Q
and
a_{0}z_{0}=b_{0}z_{0}=e_{0}z_{0}=zz_{0}=z_{0}^{2}=z_{0}a_{0}=z_{0}b_{0}=z_{0}e_{0}=z_{0}
.

Denote by
\sim_{Q}
the congruence on
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
generated by
Q
and by
\sim
the congruence on
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z,z_{0}\}^{*}

generated by
Q\cup\{a_{0}z_{0}=b_{0}z_{0}=e_{0}z_{0}=zz_{0}=z_{0}^{2}=z_{0}a_{0}=z_{0}b_{0}=z_{0}e_{0}=z_{0}\}
.

Let
w\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z,z_{0}\}^{*}\setminus\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
.
Then,
w=u_{1}z_{0}u_{2}z_{0}\cdots u_{k}z_{0}u
, for some
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k},u\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
and
k\geqslant 1
.
As a consequence of the relations
a_{0}z_{0}=b_{0}z_{0}=e_{0}z_{0}=zz_{0}=z_{0}
, we get
u_{i}z_{0}\sim z_{0}
, for
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, and so

w\sim z_{0}^{k}u\sim z_{0}u
, also in view of the relation
z_{0}^{2}=z_{0}
.
On the other hand, we showed in the proof of Theorem
3.1
that
u\sim_{Q}zu^{\prime}
or
u\sim_{Q}u^{\prime}
, for some
u^{\prime}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
.
Hence,
w\sim z_{0}zu^{\prime}
or
w\sim z_{0}u^{\prime}\sim z_{0}
, for some
u^{\prime}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
,
also taking into account the relations
z_{0}a_{0}=z_{0}b_{0}=z_{0}e_{0}=z_{0}
.
Observe that, as transformation, we obtain

w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
i&i&\cdots&i\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, for some
i\in\Omega_{n-1}^{0}
, and so
w\not\in\mathrm{End}S_{n}
.

Now, let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z,z_{0}\}^{*}
be such that
w_{1}=w_{2}
(equality in
\mathrm{swEnd}S_{n}
). We aim to show that
w_{1}\sim w_{2}
.
By the last observation above, we can conclude that
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
or
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z,z_{0}\}^{*}\setminus\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
.
If
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
, then
w_{1}\sim_{Q}w_{2}
and so
w_{1}\sim w_{2}
.
So, suppose that
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z,z_{0}\}^{*}\setminus\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},z\}^{*}
.
Hence, as transformation,

w_{1}=w_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
i&i&\cdots&i\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, for some
i\in\Omega_{n-1}^{0}
.
If
i=0
, then we must have
w_{1}\sim z_{0}\sim w_{2}
. On the other hand, suppose that
i\in\Omega_{n-1}
.
Then,
w_{1}\sim z_{0}zu^{\prime}_{1}
and
w_{2}\sim z_{0}zu^{\prime}_{2}
, for some
u^{\prime}_{1},u^{\prime}_{2}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
,
and (as transformations)
zu^{\prime}_{1}=zu^{\prime}_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
i&0&\cdots&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
Thus,
zu^{\prime}_{1}\sim_{Q}zu^{\prime}_{2}
and so
w_{1}\sim z_{0}zu^{\prime}_{1}\sim z_{0}zu^{\prime}_{2}\sim w_{2}
, as required.
∎

Now, let
\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0}\mid S\rangle
be a presentation of
\mathscr{PT}^{0}_{n-1}
on the generators
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
,
e_{0}
and
c_{0}
.
Thus, we obtain:

Theorem 3.3
.

For
n\geqslant 4
,

\displaystyle\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0},z\mid S,\penalty\ a_{0}z=b_{0}z=e_{0}z=z,\penalty\ z^{2}=(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0},\penalty\ z^{2}c_{0}=zc_{0}\rangle

is a presentation of
\mathrm{wEnd}S_{n}
on the generators
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
,
e_{0}
,
c_{0}
and
z
.

Proof.

First, observe that it is a routine matter to check that the set of generators
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0},z\}
satisfies all the considered relations.

Next, recall that
\mathrm{wEnd}S_{n}
is the disjoint union of
\mathscr{PT}_{n-1}^{0}
and

\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A&B\\
i&i&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathscr{T}(\Omega_{n-1}^{0})\mid A\cup B=\Omega_{n-1},\penalty\ A\cap B=\emptyset,\penalty\ i\in\Omega_{n-1}\right\}.

For each
A,B\subseteq\Omega_{n-1}
such that
A\cup B=\Omega_{n-1}
and
A\cap B=\emptyset
, let
u_{A,B}
be a word of

\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0}\}^{*}
representing the transformation
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A&B\\
0&0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathscr{PT}_{n-1}^{0}
.
For each
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, let
u_{i}
be a word of

\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
representing the transformation
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&\cdots&n-1\\
0&i&\cdots&i\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathscr{T}_{n-1}^{0}
.
Notice that, as transformations, we have
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A&B\\
i&i&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)=u_{A,B}zu_{i}
,
for any disjoint subsets
A
and
B
of
\Omega_{n-1}
such that
A\cup B=\Omega_{n-1}
and
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
.
Let
W_{0}
be a set of canonical forms for the monoid
\mathscr{PT}^{0}_{n-1}
regarding the set of generators
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0}\}
.
Let

W_{1}=\{u_{A,B}zu_{i}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0},z\}^{*}\mid A\cup B=\Omega_{n-1},\penalty\ A\cap B=\emptyset,\penalty\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1\}

and let
W=W_{0}\cup W_{1}
. Then, clearly,
|W|=|\mathrm{wEnd}S_{n}|
.

Denote by
\sim_{S}
the congruence on
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0}\}^{*}
generated by
S
and by
\sim
the congruence on
\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0},z\}^{*}

generated by
S\cup\{a_{0}z=b_{0}z=e_{0}z=z,\penalty\ z^{2}=(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0},\penalty\ z^{2}c_{0}=zc_{0}\}
.

Let
w\in W_{0}
. Since
\langle a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0}\mid S\rangle
is a presentation of
\mathscr{PT}^{0}_{n-1}
,
for each
x\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0},c_{0}\}
, we have
wx\sim_{S}w^{\prime}
, whence
wx\sim w^{\prime}
, for some
w^{\prime}\in W_{0}
.
On the other hand, being
A=\{k\in\Omega_{n-1}\mid(k)w=0\}
and
B=\Omega_{n-1}\setminus A
,
we have
w(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}=u_{A,B}
and
(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}=u_{1}
(considering
w
,
u_{1}
and
u_{A,B}
as transformations of
\mathscr{PT}^{0}_{n-1}
),
whence
w(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}\sim_{S}u_{A,B}
and
(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}\sim_{S}u_{1}
, and so

wz\sim w(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}z\sim u_{A,B}z^{2}z=u_{A,B}zz^{2}\sim u_{A,B}z(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}\sim u_{A,B}zu_{1}\in W_{1}.

Now, take a subset
A
of
\Omega_{n-1}
,
B=\Omega_{n-1}\setminus A
and
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
. Let
w=u_{A,B}zu_{i}\in W_{1}
.
For each
x\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}
, we have
u_{i}x=u_{(i)x}
(considering
u_{i}
and
x
as transformation of
\mathscr{T}_{n-1}^{0}
), whence
u_{i}x\sim_{S}u_{(i)x}
and so

wx=u_{A,B}zu_{i}x\sim u_{A,B}zu_{(i)x}\in W_{1}.

On the other hand, (as transformations) we have
u_{1}c_{0}=c_{0}u_{1}c_{0}
and
u_{i}c_{0}=u_{i}
, if
2\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, whence

wc_{0}=u_{A,B}zu_{1}c_{0}\sim u_{A,B}zc_{0}u_{1}c_{0}\sim u_{A,B}z^{2}c_{0}u_{1}c_{0}\sim u_{A,B}(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}c_{0}u_{1}c_{0}\sim w^{\prime},

for some
w^{\prime}\in W_{0}
, and
wc_{0}=u_{A,B}zu_{i}c_{0}\sim u_{A,B}zu_{i}=w\in W_{1}
, if
2\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
.
Finally, since
u_{i}\in\{a_{0},b_{0},e_{0}\}^{*}
, we have
u_{i}z\sim z
and so
wz=u_{A,B}zu_{i}z\sim u_{A,B}z^{2}\sim u_{A,B}(e_{0}b_{0})^{n-3}e_{0}\sim w^{\prime}
,
for some
w^{\prime}\in W_{0}
.

Thus, we proved that
W
is a set of canonical forms for
\mathrm{wEnd}S_{n}
, which completes this proof.
∎

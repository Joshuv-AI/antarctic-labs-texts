Maximum Out-of-Pocket Limits in Dual-Eligible Special Needs Plans
Authors: Katherine M. Ianni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This cross-sectional study assesses changes maximum out-of-pocket costs in dual-eligible Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries from 2017 to 2026 following the 2023 updated guidance for calculating maximum out-of-pocket spending.

Introduction

Over half of beneficiaries who are dually eligible for Medicare and Medicaid are enrolled in private plans through the Medicare Advantage (MA) program, largely in Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans (D-SNPs).1,2 In 2023, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) updated guidance for calculating maximum out-of-pocket (MOOP) spending limits in D-SNPs. Previously, only out-of-pocket costs paid by the beneficiary counted toward the MOOP; beginning in 2023, D-SNPs are required to track all costs, including those paid by Medicaid, toward the MOOP.3,4 The 2023 guidance has substantial implications for state Medicaid programs and clinicians caring for dually eligible beneficiaries. First, cost responsibility shifts from Medicaid agencies to MA organizations earlier in a beneficiary’s spending trajectory when Medicaid payments count toward the MOOP limit. Second, facilities serving dually eligible beneficiaries in D-SNPs can bill the MA plan once the MOOP is reached rather than continuing to bill Medicaid. This cross-sectional study assesses changes in the mean MOOP amount, types of MOOPs used, offering of D-SNPs, and enrollment in D-SNPs from 2017 to 2026 to examine MA organization responses to the 2023 MOOP policy and the scope of the policy’s potential impact.

Methods

Per the Weill Cornell Medicine Human Research Protections Program, this study did not require approval or informed consent because it did not involve human participants. The Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline was used. Using the CMS public plan benefit package (PBP) and enrollment data, MA plans were identified using a combination of 5-digit contract and 3-digit plan identifiers. The sample included all nonemployer plans with Parts A, B, C and D coverage over 2017 to 2026. The PBP data were used to identify D-SNPs, measure plan-level MOOP limits, and classify MOOP types as in-network, out-of-network, or combined (in- and out-of-network). Public enrollment data were merged with the PBP data in each year using contract-plan identifiers to weight descriptive statistics by plan enrollment. First, changes in mean MOOP limits and the types of MOOPs used among D-SNPs and non-SNPs over time were analyzed. Trends were included for non-SNPs to examine any potential programwide changes in MOOP limits that may be independent of the 2023 D-SNP policy. Second, changes were assessed in the offering of D-SNPs, measured by the share of MA plans that were D-SNPs, and enrollment in D-SNPs, measured by the share of total MA enrollment in D-SNPs over time. Data were analyzed using StataNow/SE version 19.5 (StataCorp LLC).

Results

The sample included 60 225 plan-year observations from 2017 to 2026. Of the plan-year observations, 17.8% were SNPs of any kind and 11.0% were D-SNPs. Between 2017 and 2026, the share of MA plans that were D-SNPs increased from 9.83% to 14.8%, and the share of total MA enrollment in D-SNPs increased from 9.90% to 18.1%. Among D-SNPs from 2022 to 2023, the mean (SD) in-network MOOP weighted by plan enrollment increased from $5499 ($2213) to $7248 ($2061) (31.8% increase) and the mean (SD) combined MOOP increased from $8814 ($2836) to $11 695 ($1638) (32.7% increase) (Figure 1). To investigate whether these findings might be based on D-SNP exit and entry, a focus on plans continuously offered over the 2022 to 2023 policy change period found similar results. The change in mean (SD) in-network MOOP weighted by plan enrollment among non-SNPs between 2022 and 2023 was $5448.654 ($1854.362) to $5366.963 ($2062.479) (1.50% decrease). The change in mean (SD) combined MOOP weighted by plan enrollment among non-SNPs over this same period was $9546.348 ($2072.192) to $9619.001 ($2484.664) (0.76% decrease) (Figure 1). Between 2022 and 2023, 100% of D-SNP enrollees were in plans using an in-network MOOP, between 15.3% to 15.9% of enrollees were in plans using combined MOOPs, and less than 1% of enrollees were in D-SNPs using out-of-network MOOPs (Figure 2).

Discussion

Between 2022 and 2023, mean in-network and combined MOOP limits increased markedly among D-SNPs while offers of and enrollment in D-SNPs continued to steadily grow. Similar increases in mean MOOP limits were not observed among non-SNPs, which were not impacted by the 2023 policy. These findings suggest that D-SNPs raised MOOP limits in anticipation of the shifting cost responsibility from Medicaid agencies to MA organizations. For clinicians serving dually eligible beneficiaries in D-SNPs, this shift could reduce delayed payments and administrative burden associated with billing Medicaid agencies.5,6 However, increased MOOP limits could expose dually eligible beneficiaries in D-SNPs to additional out-of-pocket costs, particularly among those without wraparound Medicaid cost-sharing coverage and amid losses in Medicaid coverage during the 2023 unwinding.7,8

These analyses were limited by a lack of beneficiary-level data to investigate which beneficiaries (eg, partial dually eligible beneficiaries vs full dually eligible beneficiaries) were most impacted by increases in mean MOOP limits. Future research should examine the effect of this policy on spending in both the Medicaid and MA program.

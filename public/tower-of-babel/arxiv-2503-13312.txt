Abstract
. We consider a proper morphism X → S X\to S and a locally closed immersion S ′ → S S^{\prime}\to S of discretely ringed adic spaces and prove proper base change for the tame topology in this setting. More precisely, we show for an abelian p p -torsion sheaf ( p = char + ⁡ ( S ) p=\ch^{+}(S) ) on the tame site of X X that the base change homomorphism for the derived pushforward along X → S X\to S with the pullback along S ′ → S S^{\prime}\to S is an isomorphism.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a proper morphism of discretely ringed pseudoadic spaces of specialization characteristic
\ch^{+}({\mathscr{S}})=p>0

We consider the cartesian square

Let us first explain why we cannot expect the ususal statement in full generality, whithout restricting to locally closed immersions.
We consider the following example.
Let
(K,K^{+})
be a tamely closed valued field and
(L/L^{+})/(K,K^{+})
a finite wildly ramified Galois extension with Galois group
G

We consider the cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{G}(L,L^{+})=\Spa(L\otimes_{K}L,L^{+}\otimes_{K^{+}}L^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spa(L,L^{+})}

If the base change theorem held for this cartesian square, there would have to be an isomorphism

We could say that the reason for this failure is the cancellation of wild ramification.
In the statement of the above theorem this cannot happen since

It is conceivable to prove a slightly more general statement by imposing some tameness condition on

(which a locally closed immersion should certainly satisfy).
However, it seems that such a condition would be quite restrictive and already exclude examples like
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{(K,K^{+})}\to\Spa(K,K^{+})
for some non-algebraically closed valued field
(K,K^{+})
.

2.
Setup and notation

In this article we will work with the tame and strongly étale site of pseudoadic spaces, mainly discretely ringed ones.
To put everyone on the same page, we start by reviewing these concepts (assuming familiarity with adic spaces).

Pseudoadic spaces have been introduced by Huber in order to study closed subsets of adic spaces, which do not necessarily carry the structure of an adic space.
Following
[
Hub96
]
, § 1.10, a
pseudoadic space
is a pair
X=(\underline{X},\lvert X\rvert)
, where
\underline{X}
is an adic space and
\lvert X\rvert\subseteq\underline{X}
is a convex proconstructible subset.
A
morphism of pseudoadic spaces
from
Y
to
X
is a morphism of adic spaces
f:\underline{Y}\to\underline{X}
such that
f(\lvert Y\rvert)\subseteq\lvert X\rvert
.
The morphism
f
is
étale
if it is étale as a morphism of adic spaces and
\lvert Y\rvert
is open in
f^{-1}(\lvert X\rvert)
.
It is
strongly étale
if it is étale and for all points
y\in\lvert Y\rvert
mapping to
x\in\lvert X\rvert
the residue field extension
k(y)/k(x)
is unramified.
Similarly,
f
is
tame
if it is étale and the above residue field extensions are tamely ramified.
Defining coverings to be surjective families, we get the étale, strongly étale, and tame sites
X_{\textit{\'{e}t}}
,
X_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}
, and
X_{t}
of a pseudoadic space
X
.

A point
x
of a (pseudo)adic space
X
has a residue field
k(x)
, which is a valued field with valuation ring
k(x)^{+}
.
Now
k(x)^{+}
again has a residue field.
In order to avoid confusion with
k(x)
we will call it the
specialization field
of
x
and write
k(x)^{\succ}
.
Because of the existence of both the residue field
and
the specialization field of a point, there are two different notions of characteristic of an adic space
X
.
The
(residue) characteristic

\ch(X)
is the set of all primes occuring as the characteristic of a residue field
k(x)
for some point
x\in X
.
The
specialization characteristic

\ch^{+}(X)
is the set of all primes occuring as the characteristic of a specialization field
k(x)^{\succ}
for some
x\in X
.

An adic space
X
is
discretely ringed
if locally it is isomorphic to
\Spa(A,A^{+})
for a Huber pair
(A,A^{+})
whose topology is discrete.
A big class of examples is provided by spaces of the form
\Spa(X,S)
for a morphism of schemes
X\to S
.
Its points are triples
(x,R,\phi)
consisting of a point
x\in X
, a valuation ring
R
of its residue field
k(x)
and a morphism
\phi\colon\Spec R\to S
fitting into the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec k(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

In order to improve notation, we will denote the points of
\Spa(X,S)
just by a single letter, e.g.
x
.
Then the corresponding triple will be written as
(\supp x,k(x)^{+},c_{x})
.
This is in line with the notation for affinoid adic spaces
\Spa(A,A^{+})
where a valuation
x
is determined by its support
\supp x
and the valuation ring
k(x)^{+}
on
k(x)=k(\supp x)
.
In the affine case the map
c_{x}
is uniquely determined (or more generally if
S
is separated).
The existence of
c_{x}
is equivalent to the condition that the valuation
x
be less or equal to
1
on
A^{+}
.

3.
Riemann-Zariski points and morphisms

section_RZ_points

In
[
Tem11
]
Temkin studies adic spaces of the form
\Spa(X,S)
for a morphism of schemes
X\to S
and compares
\Spa(X,S)
to the limit over all
X
-modifications of
S
.
It turns out that this limit – he calls it
\mathrm{RZ}_{X}(S)
, the relative Riemann Zariski space – is homeomorphic to a subspace of
\Spa(X,S)
.
It consists of all points that do not admit nontrivial horizontal specializations.
This description can easily be extended to more general adic spaces and this is what we will do in this section.

3.1.
Specialization and generalization of continuous valuations

We start by reviewing some facts about specializations on adic spaces (to be found in
[
Hub93
]
, § 2), here and there adding some results that were not treated in the literature.
Remember that a specialization
x\rightsquigarrow y
on an adic space factors uniquely as
x\rightsquigarrow z\rightsquigarrow y
, where
x\rightsquigarrow z
is a vertical and
z\rightsquigarrow y
is a horizontal specialization.
If
X=\Spa(A,A^{+})
is affinoid, vertical specializations of
x
can be characterized as those that have the same support in
\Spec A
, whereas horizontal specializations are those that have the same center in
\Spec A^{+}
.
We can also factor
x\rightsquigarrow y
as
x\rightsquigarrow z_{1}\rightsquigarrow z_{2}\rightsquigarrow y
, where
x\to\rightsquigarrow z_{1}
is a horizontal specialization and
z_{2}\rightsquigarrow y
is a vertical specialization.
If the valuation
z_{1}
is nontrivial, we necessarily have
z_{1}=z_{2}
.
If
z_{1}
is trivial, then also
z_{2}
is trivial and we call
z_{1}\rightsquigarrow z_{2}
a schematic specialization.

Lemma 3.1
.

specialization_continuous

Let
A
be a Huber ring and
v:A\to\Gamma
a continuous valuation.
Then any specialization of
v
as an element of
\Spv(A)
is again continuous.

Proof.

From the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
Hub93
]
we know that a valuation
v
is continuous if and only if for every
a\in A^{\circ\circ}
its value
v(a)
is cofinal for
\Gamma_{v}
, i.e. for every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{v}
there is
n\in\mathds{N}
such that
v(a^{n})<\gamma
.
As every specialization can be split into a horizontal and a vertical one, we can deal with horizontal and vertical specializations separately.
If
v^{\prime}
is a horizontal specialization of
v
, there is a convex subgroup
H
of
\Gamma_{v}
containing the characteristic subgroup
c\Gamma_{v}
such that
\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}=H
and

v^{\prime}(a)=\begin{cases}v(a)&\text{if }v(a)\in H\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Using the above characterisation of continuous valuations, it is clear that
v^{\prime}
is again continuous.
If
v^{\prime}
is a vertical specialization of
v
, there is a convex subgroup
H
of
\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}
such that
\Gamma_{v}=\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}/H
and

v(a)=\begin{cases}v^{\prime}(a)H&\text{if }v^{\prime}(a)\neq 0\\
0&\text{if }v^{\prime}(a)=0.\end{cases}

Remember that the ordering of
\Gamma_{v}=\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}/H
is defined by saying that
\gamma<\delta
if and only if
\gamma^{\prime}<\delta^{\prime}
for all lifts
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\delta^{\prime}
of
\gamma
and
\delta
to
\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}
.
If
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}
with image
\gamma\in\Gamma_{v}
and
a\in A
is topologically nilpotent, then
v(a^{n})<\gamma
for some
n\in\mathds{N}
.
This implies
v^{\prime}(a^{n})<\gamma^{\prime}
, hence
v^{\prime}
is continuous.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

generalization_continuous

Let
A
be a Huber ring and
v:A\to\Gamma
a continuous valuation.
A vertical generalization
w
of
v
in
\Spv(A)
is continuous if and only if
w(A^{\circ\circ})<1
.
In particular if
v
is non-analytic, any vertical generalization of
v
is continuous.
If
v
is analytic, a vertical generalization is continuous if and only if it is not a trivial valuation.

Proof.

Remember the characterization of continuous valuations as those valuations
v
such that
v(a)
is cofinal for
\Gamma_{v}
for any
a\in A^{\circ\circ}
(see
[
Hub93
]
, Theorem 3.1 and its proof).
From this it is clear that if
w
is continuous, then
w(A^{\circ\circ})<1
.
Assume now that
w(A^{\circ\circ})<1
.
Since
w
is a vertical generalization of
v
, there is a convex subgroup
H
of
\Gamma_{v}
such that
\Gamma_{w}=\Gamma_{v}/H
and

w(a)=\begin{cases}v(a)H&\text{if }v(a)\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }v(a)=0.\end{cases}

For
a\in A^{\circ\circ}
and
\bar{\gamma}\in\Gamma_{w}
we have to find
n\in\mathds{N}
with
w(a)^{n}<\bar{\gamma}
.
Let
\gamma\in\Gamma_{v}
be a preimage of
\bar{\gamma}
.
As
v
is continuous, there is
n\in\mathds{N}
such that
v(a^{n-1})<\gamma
.
Moreover, the condition
w(a)<1
translates to
v(a)<h
for every
h\in H
.
Combining the two inequalities gives
v(a^{n})<h\gamma
for all
h\in H
, or in other words
w(a^{n})<\bar{\gamma}
.

If
v
is non-analytic,
v(A^{\circ\circ})=0
.
Hence
w(A^{\circ\circ})=0<1
for any vertical generalization
w
of
v
.
Thus, any vertical generalization is continuous.

Suppose now that
v
is analytic and let
w
be a vertical generalization of
v
.
We first notice that if
w
is a trivial valuation, it is not continuous.
Indeed, as
v
is analytic, there is
a\in A^{\circ\circ}
with
v(a)>0
.
But then also
w(a)>0
.
This implies
w(a)=1
as
w
is a trivial valuation.

If
w
is non-trivial, the convex subgroup
H
of
\Gamma_{v}
defining
w
is a proper subgroup of
\Gamma_{v}
:
H\subsetneq\Gamma_{v}
.
In order to show that
w
is continuous, we need to convince ourselves that

v(A^{\circ\circ})<H.

Let
a\in A^{\circ\circ}
and
h\in H
.
There is
\gamma\in\Gamma_{v}
such that
\gamma<H
.
Using that
v(a)
is cofinal for
\Gamma_{v}
, we find
n\in\mathds{N}
such that
v(a)^{n}<\gamma
.
Since
\gamma<h^{n}
, we obtain
v(a)<h
.
∎

3.2.
Riemann-Zariski points

subsection_RZ_points

Definition 3.3
.

A
Riemann-Zariski (RZ) point
of a pseudoadic space
X
is a point which does not admit nontrivial horizontal specializations.

Any closed point is Riemann-Zariski.
Any point coresponding to a trivial valuation is a Riemann-Zariski point.
We call these
trivial Riemann-Zariski points
.
Also every point of an analytic adic space is Riemann-Zariski.

Lemma 3.4
.

preimage_RZ

Let
f:U\to X
be a locally quasi-finite morphism of pseudoadic spaces and
x\in U
a point.
If
f(x)
is a Riemann-Zariski point, then
x
as well.

Proof.

If
x^{\prime}
is a horizontal specialization of
x
, then
f(x^{\prime})
is a horizontal specialization of
f(x)
, hence equal to
f(x)
.
Since
f
is locally quasi-finite, there are no specialization relations between the points of a fiber.
This implies
x=x^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

RZ_qc

Let
X
be a quasi-compact pseudoadic space.
Then the subset of Riemann-Zariski points is quasi-compact.

Proof.

Let
\underline{X}
be the underlying adic space of
X
and
|X|
the subset of
\underline{X}
such that
X=(\underline{X},|X|)
.
The adic space
\underline{X}
is locally spectral by
[
Hub93
]
, Theorem 3.5 (i).
Moreover, by definition of a pseudoadic space,
|X|
is locally pro-constructible in
\underline{X}
, hence locally spectral, as well.
Being quasi-compact,
|X|
is even spectral.
The decisive property is that in a spectral space every point specializes to a closed point.
Moreover, every closed point of
|X|
is Riemann-Zariski.
Hence, every point of
|X|
specializes to a Riemann-Zariski point in
|X|
.

Denote by
S
the subset of Riemann-Zariski points of
|X|
.
Let

S=\bigcup_{i\in I}S_{i}

be an open cover.
We need to show that it has a finite subcover.
For every
i
choose an open subset
U_{i}
of
|X|
such that
U_{i}\cap S=S_{i}
.
Then
|X|=\bigcup_{i}U_{i}
because a point in the complement of the union of the
U_{i}
would specialize to some Riemann-Zariski point but every Riemann-Zariski point is contained in some
U_{i}
.
Since
|X|
is quasi-compact, finitely many
U_{i}
’s cover it.
But then the corresponding
S_{i}
cover
S
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

separated_horizontal

Let
X
be separated over an affinoid adic space
S
and
x\in X
a point.
Then the set of horizontal specializations of
x
is totally ordered by specialization.

Proof.

First note that if
X
is affinoid, the horizontal specializations of a point
x\in X
are in one-to-one correspondence with the convex subgroups of the value group
\Gamma_{x}
containing the characteristic subgroup
c\Gamma_{x}
.
In particular, they are totally ordered by specialization.

In the general case, let
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
be horizontal specializations of
x
in
X
.
They correspond to convex subgroups
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
of the value group
\Gamma_{x}
of
x
.
Since the convex subgroups of
\Gamma_{x}
are totally ordered by inclusion, we may assume that
\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}
.
We claim that
x_{2}
specializes to
x_{1}
.

The images
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in
S
are horizontal specializations of the image
s
of
x
.
Since
S
is affinoid and
\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}
,
s_{2}
specializes to
s_{1}
.
Choose affinoid neighborhoods
U_{1}
,
U_{2}
, and
V
of
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
and
s_{1}
, respectively.
Note that
s_{2}\in V
and
x
is contained in both
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
.
We denote by
c_{1}\Gamma_{x}
and
c_{2}\Gamma_{x}
the characteristic subgroups of
\Gamma_{x}
with respect to
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, respectively.
They are both contained in
\Delta_{2}
.

We consider the diagonal

\Delta:X\to X\times_{S}X.

The open subspace
U_{1}\times_{V}U_{2}
of
X\times_{S}X
is affinoid and contains
\Delta(x)
.
The value group of
\Delta(x)
equals
\Gamma_{x}
and its characteristic subgroup
c_{12}\Gamma_{x}
with respect to
U_{1}\times_{V}U_{2}
is the subgroup generated by
c_{1}\Gamma_{x}
and
c_{2}\Gamma_{x}
(in fact one is contained in the other so it is just the bigger one of the two).
Hence
c_{12}\Gamma_{x}\subseteq\Delta_{2}
and there is a corresponding horizontal specialization
y_{2}\in U_{1}\times_{V}U_{2}
of
\Delta(x)
.
Its image in
U_{2}
is the unique horizontal specialization of
x
corresponding to
\Delta_{2}
, i.e. equal to
x_{2}
.
Since
X
is separated over
S
,
\Delta
is a closed immersion and thus
y_{2}
lies on the diagonal.
Therefore the image of
y_{2}
in
U_{1}
also equals
x_{2}
.
In other words,
x_{2}
is contained in
U_{1}
.
But as the horizontal specializations of
x
in the affinoid space

are totally ordered, this implies that
x_{1}
is a specialization of
x_{2}

Let

In

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{c,RZ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{Y_{c}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{c}}

Accordingly, the base change homomorphism

\iota_{c}^{*}\varphi_{Y,*}{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow\varphi_{Y_{c},*}\iota_{s}^{*}{\mathscr{F}}

factors as

(3)

\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{{\small{bc_split}}}\hrule width=0.50003pt,height=6.45831pt,depth=0.0pt}\hss}\penalty\iota_{c}^{*}\varphi_{Y,*}{\mathscr{F}}=\iota_{c}^{*}\Psi_{Y,*}\pi_{{\mathscr{X}},*}{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow\Psi_{Y_{c},*}\iota_{s,RZ}^{*}\pi_{{\mathscr{X}},*}{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow\Psi_{Y_{c},*}\pi_{{\mathscr{X}}_{s},*}\iota_{s}^{*}{\mathscr{F}}=\varphi_{Y_{c},*}\iota_{s}^{*}{\mathscr{F}}.

The second homomorphism is an isomorphism by
Lemma

4.5
.
The first homomorphism does not have to be an isomorphism.
This will only turn out to be true in the limit over all
X
-modifications
Y
of
S
.

The equivalence of topoi from
Proposition

5.6
entails that the natural homomorphism

{\mathscr{G}}\longrightarrow\colim_{Y}\Psi_{Y}^{*}\Psi_{Y,*}{\mathscr{G}}

is an isomorphism for all sheaves
{\mathscr{G}}
on
{\mathscr{X}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}
.
Similarly,

{\mathscr{G}}\longrightarrow\colim_{Y}\Psi_{Y_{c}}^{*}\Psi_{Y_{c},*}{\mathscr{G}}

is an isomorphism for all sheaves
{\mathscr{G}}
on
{\mathscr{X}}_{s,\textit{s\'{e}t}}
.
Using this we identify

\colim_{Y}\Psi_{Y_{c}}^{*}\iota_{c}^{*}\Psi_{Y,*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}}=\colim_{Y}\iota_{s,RZ}^{*}\Psi_{Y}^{*}\Psi_{Y,*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow\colim_{Y}\Psi_{Y_{c}}^{*}\Psi_{Y_{c},*}\iota_{s,RZ}^{*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}}

with the identity

\iota_{s,RZ}^{*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow\iota_{s,RZ}^{*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\colim_{Y}H^{i}(Y_{c,\textit{\'{e}t}},\iota_{c}^{*}\Psi_{Y,*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle=H^{i}({\mathscr{X}}_{x,RZ,\textit{s\'{e}t}},\colim_{Y}\Psi_{Y_{c}}^{*}\iota_{c}^{*}\Psi_{Y,*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle=H^{i}({\mathscr{X}}_{s,RZ,\textit{s\'{e}t}},\colim_{Y}\Psi_{Y_{c}}^{*}\Psi_{Y_{c},*}\iota_{x,RZ}^{*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle=\colim_{Y}H^{i}(Y_{c,\textit{\'{e}t}},\Psi_{Y_{c},*}\iota_{s,RZ}^{*}\pi_{X,*}{\mathscr{F}}).

Via the second base change map in (
3
), which we already showed to be an isomorphism, we identify this with

\colim_{Y}H^{i}(Y_{c,\textit{\'{e}t}},\Psi_{Y_{c},*}\iota_{s}^{*}{\mathscr{F}})

and we are done
∎

We want to enhance
Proposition

7.1
to a proper base change theorem along locally closed immersions.
We first need to settle the baby example where the proper morphism is a closed immersion.

Lemma 7.2
.

closed_immersion_acyclic

Let
\iota\colon{\mathscr{Z}}\to{\mathscr{X}}
be a closed immersion of pseudoadic spaces and
{\mathscr{F}}
an abelian sheaf on
{\mathscr{Z}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}
.
Then

R\iota_{*}{\mathscr{F}}\cong\iota_{*}{\mathscr{F}}.

Proof.

We have to show that
R^{i}\iota_{*}{\mathscr{F}}=0
for
i>0
.
We can check this on stalks.
We now have to show the following statement.
Let
{\mathscr{X}}
be a strongly local pseudoadic space and
{\mathscr{Z}}\subseteq{\mathscr{X}}
a closed subspace.
For any abelian sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
on
{\mathscr{Z}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}

H^{i}({\mathscr{Z}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}},{\mathscr{F}})=0\qquad\forall i>0.

But closed subspaces of strongly local spaces are strongly local, so these cohomology groups vanish.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

bc_closed_immersion

Let
\iota\colon{\mathscr{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{X}}
be a closed immersion of pseudoadic spaces and
{\mathscr{F}}
a sheaf on
{\mathscr{X}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}

Then for any cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{Z}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{Z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{X}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

the base change homomorphism

g^{*}\iota_{*}{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow\iota^{\prime}_{*}g^{\prime*}{\mathscr{F}}

is an isomorphism (note that
\iota_{*}=R\iota_{*}
by
Lemma

7.2
).

Proof.

We can check this on stalks and therefore assume that
{\mathscr{X}}
and
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
are strongly henselian.
But then also
{\mathscr{Z}}
and
{\mathscr{Z}}^{\prime}
are strongly henselian.
By construction,
(g^{\prime*}{\mathscr{F}})({\mathscr{Z}}^{\prime})
equals
\colim_{{\mathscr{U}}\to{\mathscr{Z}}}{\mathscr{F}}({\mathscr{U}})
, where the colimit runs over all strongly étale morphisms
{\mathscr{U}}\to{\mathscr{Z}}
with a factorization
{\mathscr{Z}}^{\prime}\to{\mathscr{U}}\to{\mathscr{Z}}
.
Every such
{\mathscr{U}}\to{\mathscr{Z}}
splits.
We conclude that
g^{\prime*}{\mathscr{F}}({\mathscr{Z}}^{\prime})={\mathscr{F}}({\mathscr{Z}})
, which translates to the statement of the lemma.
∎

Suppose
{\mathscr{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{X}}
is a closed immersion of adic spaces.
We denote by
\lvert{\mathscr{Z}}\rvert
the image of
{\mathscr{Z}}
in
{\mathscr{X}}
.
The pseudoadic space
({\mathscr{X}},\lvert{\mathscr{Z}}\rvert)
should in a suitable sense be the same as
{\mathscr{Z}}
.
At the level of topoi this is true by the following lemma.

Lemma 7.4
.

topoi_pseudoadic

Let
\iota\colon{\mathscr{Z}}\to{\mathscr{X}}
be a locally closed immersion of pseudoadic spaces inducing a homeomorphism
\lvert{\mathscr{Z}}\rvert\to\lvert{\mathscr{X}}\rvert
.
Then for
\tau\in\{\textit{s\'{e}t},t,\textit{\'{e}t}\}
the associated morphism of sites
{\mathscr{Z}}_{\tau}\to{\mathscr{X}}_{\tau}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

The statement for the étale site is true by
[
Hub96
]
, Corollary 2.3.8.
Since tameness and strong étaleness is only meassured over points of
\lvert{\mathscr{Z}}\rvert
and
\lvert{\mathscr{X}}\rvert
, respectively, and
\lvert{\mathscr{Z}}\rvert\to\lvert{\mathscr{X}}\rvert
is a homeomorphism with trivial residue field extensions, the tameness and unramifiedness conditions on either side match.
Therefore,
{\mathscr{Z}}_{\tau}\to{\mathscr{X}}_{\tau}
is an equivalence.
∎

From
Proposition

7.1
we deduce the proper base change theorem for immersions in the strongly étale topology.

Theorem 7.5
.

bc_set

Let
f\colon{\mathscr{X}}\to{\mathscr{S}}
be a proper morphism of discretely ringed pseudoadic spaces and
\iota\colon{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}\to{\mathscr{S}}
a locally closed immersion.
We set
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}:={\mathscr{X}}\times_{\mathscr{S}}{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
thus obtaining a cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{S}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

Then for any
i\in\mathds{N}
and any sheaf of abelian groups, the base change homomorphism

\iota^{*}Rf_{*}{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow Rf^{\prime}_{*}\iota^{\prime*}{\mathscr{F}}

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We can check on stalks that the base change homomorphism is an isomorphism.
This reduces us to the case where
{\mathscr{S}}
and
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
are strongly henselian and
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}\to{\mathscr{S}}
is local.
Since
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}\to{\mathscr{S}}
is also a locally closed immersion, it is a closed immersion in our situation.
It suffices to prove the theorem in case
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
is the closed point
s
of
{\mathscr{S}}
.
The general case then follows by considering the composition

s\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{S}}

and using the result for
s\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
and
s\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{S}}
.
Here we need to be precise and specify what we mean by the closed point.
The smallest possible closed subspace of
{\mathscr{S}}
containing the closed point is
(\Spa(k(s),k(s)^{+}),s)
.
We can certainly work with this space.
Morally, it should coincide with
(\underline{{\mathscr{S}}},s)
.
By
Lemma

7.4
the closed immersion
(\Spa(k(s),k(s)^{+},s)\to(\underline{{\mathscr{S}}},s)
induces an equivalence of strongly étale topoi.
We conclude that it suffices to prove the theorem for
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}=(\underline{{\mathscr{S}}},s)
.
In the following we will just write
s
for this space.

The pseudoadic space
{\mathscr{S}}
is of the form
(\Spa(R,R^{+}),\lvert{\mathscr{S}}\rvert)
for a strongly henselian Huber pair
(R,R^{+})
and a closed subset
\lvert{\mathscr{S}}\rvert\subseteq\Spa(R,R^{+})
.
Using
Proposition

6.7
we find a closed embedding of
{\mathscr{X}}
into a adic space of the form
\Spa(X,R^{+})
for a proper scheme
X
over
R^{+}
.
Denoting it by

h\colon{\mathscr{X}}\hookrightarrow\Spa(X,R^{+})

we obtain proper base change for
{\mathscr{X}}\to{\mathscr{S}}
and the sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
on
{\mathscr{X}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}
by applying proper base change for
\Spa(X,R^{+})\to\Spa(R,R^{+})
to the sheaf
h_{*}{\mathscr{F}}
and using that
h_{*}
is exact by
Lemma

7.2
and satisfies base change by
Lemma

7.3
.
But for the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spa(X,R^{+})_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spa(X,R^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spa(R,R^{+})}

the base change homomorphism is an isomorphism by
Proposition

7.1
.
∎

Our next goal is to deduce from

Lemma 7.6
.

bc_t_set

Let
{\mathscr{X}}
be a pseudoadic space of specialization characteristic

For a locally closed immersion

of pseudoadic spaces we consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{X}}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{Y}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{X}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{\mathscr{Y}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{\mathscr{X}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\textit{s\'{e}t}}}

Remark 7.7
.

Note that we know from
[
Hüb21
]
, Proposition 8.5 that
R\nu_{{\mathscr{X}},*}{\mathscr{F}}=\nu_{{\mathscr{X}},*}{\mathscr{F}}
and similarly for

Proof.

Looking at stalks we reduce to the situation where
{\mathscr{X}}
and
{\mathscr{Y}}
are strongly local and

is a closed immersion.
Now we need to show that the restriction
{\mathscr{F}}({\mathscr{X}})\to f^{*}{\mathscr{F}}({\mathscr{Y}})
is an isomorphism.
But both sides identify with the invariants
{\mathscr{F}}_{\bar{x}}^{G}
, where
\bar{x}
is a geometric point above the closed point

and

is the Galois group of the maximal tame extension of

(inside

Corollary 7.8
.

We consider the cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{S}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\prime}}

Then for any

the base change homomorphism

Looking at stalks we reduce to

and

being tamely local.
We now need to show that

We consider the morphism of sites

and similarly for

for every

.
Using

we get a natural identification

\displaystyle H^{i}({\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}_{t},\iota^{\prime*}{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}({\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}},\nu_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime},*}\iota^{\prime*}{\mathscr{F}})

we get

\displaystyle H^{i}({\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}},\iota^{\prime*}\nu_{{\mathscr{X}},*}{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}({\mathscr{X}}_{\textit{s\'{e}t}},\nu_{{\mathscr{X}},*}{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle H^{i}({\mathscr{X}}_{t},{\mathscr{F}})

∎

Remark 7.9
.

In
Corollary

7.8
we only talked about
p
-torsion sheaves.
One might think that proper base change for torsion sheaves whose torsion is invertible on the adic space
{\mathscr{X}}
should already be known.
This is not completely the case.
It is true that the comparison of tame with étale cohomology (
[
Hüb21
]
, Proposition 8.2) reduces us to proving proper base change for the étale topology.
However, the étale proper base change theorem has only been established for
analytic
adic spaces (see
[
Hub96
]
, Theorem 4.4.1) and here we are dealing with the opposite extreme: discretely ringed adic spaces.
One might hope to solve the problem by recurring to the proper base change theorem for schemes.
But then we cannot treat all torsion sheaves but only those that are algebraic, meaning that they come from the algebraic tame site.
We will address this task in subsequent work.

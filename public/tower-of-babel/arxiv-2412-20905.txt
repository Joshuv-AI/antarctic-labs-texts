Parametrized topological phases in 1d and T-duality
Roman Geiko

Abstract
There are families of physical systems that cannot be adiabatically evolved to the trivial system uniformly across the parameter space, even if each system in the family belongs to the trivial phase. The obstruction is measured by higher Berry class. We analyze families of topological systems in 1+1d using families of invertible TQFTs and families of RG fixed states of spin chains. We use the generalized matrix-product states to describe RG fixed points of all translation invariant pure splits states on spin chains. Families of such fixed points correspond to bundles of Hilbert-Schmidt operators. There exists a global MPS parametrization of the family if and only if the latter bundle is trivial. We propose a novel duality of parametrized topological phases which is an avatar of the T-duality in string theory. The duality relates families with different parameter spaces and different higher Berry classes. Mathematically, the T-duality is realized by gauging the circle action on the continuous trace algebra generated by parametrized matrix-product tensors.

Introduction and conclusion

Topological phases of one-dimensional quantum lattice systems are well understood by now. All plain bosonic phases are trivial, while there are non-trivial phases protected by a local symmetry group and non-trivial families of parametrized phases. In particular, phases parametrized by a space
{\mathcal{M}}
are classified by a cohomology class
H\in H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
, higher Berry class. When
{\mathcal{M}}
is a total space of a principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle, we can perform a Fourier transform along the circle. As a result, a family parametrized by
{\mathcal{M}}
with the Berry class
H
is dual to a family parametrized by another space
{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}
and another class
\widehat{H}\in H^{3}({\widehat{\mathcal{M}}},{\mathbb{Z}})
, and vice versa. This paper is intended to serve as an invitation to the subject of T-duality in parametrized topological phases of matter. We review and revisit the material concerning families of 1d phases in order to interpret the T-duality as a transformation of the data parametrizing families of RG fixed states.

This paper is devoted to a study of topological phases of physical systems in one spatial dimension. Primarily, we focus on applications to quantum lattice systems in 1d – spin chains. Topological phases are represented by sufficiently local Hamiltonians with a unique gapped ground state. Different topological phases correspond to different connected components of the space of such Hamiltonians with respect to the adiabatic evolution. An alternative point of view at topological phases, the one taken in this paper, as of the patterns of entanglement of the unique ground state.

We consider only translation invariant states – they admit a description in terms of the non-commutative Markov chains
Accardi 1974
/generalized matrix-product states
Bratteli et al. 1996
;
Bratteli et al. 2000
;
Matsui 2001
. This formalism uses auxiliary algebras acting on the virtual/bond space to encode the multi-partite entanglement. In the case of finite-dimensional auxiliary algebra, this is known as the finitely-correlated states
Fannes et al. 1992
and matrix-product states

;
Cirac et al. 2021

;
Bratteli et al. 2000
;
Matsui 2001

Generalized MPS (gMPS) allow a uniform description of renormalization fixed points of all TI pure split states. We use the RG fixed states as representatives of the trivial topological phase. Then, we describe families of topological phases as families of invertible TQFTs and families of RG fixed states. We explain how the gluing data for a family of invertible open-closed 2d TQFTs is equivalent to a
\operatorname{U}(1)
bundle gerbe. Families of RG fixed gMPS tensors are encoded by bundles of Hilbert-Schmidt operators: locally, a field of RG fixed gMPS tensors is a frame for such a bundle. The isomorphism classes of

There exists a global gMPS parametrization of a family of states iff the corresponding Hilbert-Schmidt bundle is trivial.

T-duality

The T-duality is a duality in string theory and corresponding low-energy non-linear sigma models

In the algebraic-geometric duality, gauging a group action on a space is equivalent to gauging the action on an algebra. From the point of view of operator algebras, the T-duality is a transformation of a C

Concluding remarks

From the point of view of topological phases, the parameter space and a local

The Abelian T-duality reviewed in this work admits many generalizations such as the G-equivariant

Parametrized families of gapped systems in 1+1d

In this section we discuss the generalities of parametrized invertible phases in 1+1d by analyzing families of invertible 2d TQFTs.

Topological phases in 1+1d protected by a local symmetry group
G
are fully captured by
G
-equivariant 2d TQFTs, in the sense that all existing classifications, e.g., by using the MPS argument
Pollmann et al. 2010
;
Chen et al. 2013
or operator algebra methods
Ogata 2021
;
Kapustin et al. 2021
;
Ogata 2021
, agree with the classification by 2d TQFTs
Shiozaki and Ryu 2017
;
Kapustin et al. 2017
. On the other hand, parametrized topological phases are argued to be classified by
H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
, see
Hsin et al. 2020
for the effective field theory approach,
Kapustin and Sopenko 2022
;
Artymowicz et al. 2024
for operator algebras approach, and
Ohyama et al. 2024
;
Ohyama and Ryu 2024
;
Qi et al. 2023
for MPS approach.

In what follows we give a simple argument for why the gluing of invertible open-closed 2d TQFTs produces a
\operatorname{U}(1)
bundle gerbe, an object classified by
H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
. We do not impose any symmetry, but the analysis of parametrized and
G
-symmetric phases can be conducted by analogy.

Any closed 2d TQFT
{\mathcal{F}}
, as a functor, is completely determined by its value on the circle
{\mathcal{F}}(S^{1})
, which is a commutative Frobenius algebra. Moreover, there is an equivalence of 1-categories of closed 2d TQFTs and commutative Frobenius algebras
Kock
. A 2d TQFT representing the trivial phase is simply the algebra
A\cong\mathbb{C}
, equipped with a non-zero complex number
\lambda
, the value of the Frobenius form. As we have in mind TQFTs effectively describing spin chain models, we choose the canonical normalization
\lambda=1

Shiozaki and Ryu 2017
;
Kapustin et al. 2017
; from now on we assume the Frobenius form is canonically normalized.

In order to define an open-closed 2d TQFT, we need an extra datum, the category of boundary conditions
Moore and Segal 2006
, what
{\mathcal{F}}
assigns to a point. If
{\mathcal{F}}(S^{1})=\mathbb{C}
, then this category is simply the category of complex vector spaces
\mbox{Vec}_{\mathbb{C}}
, the category of representations of
\mathbb{C}
. In other words, a trivial phase is characterized by a pair
A=\mathbb{C}
and
\mbox{Vec}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Now, we consider families of such pairs. We glue algebras using algebra automorphisms and their categories of representations using Morita bimodules. Alternatively, we can start with a fully-extended 2-1-0 TQFT with the target 2-category of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-algebras, bimodules, and intertwiners. Then, forming a stack over the parameter space is a natural thing to do.

We assume that the parameter space is a smooth compact manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
, though many results in this paper can be restated for topological spaces. Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be some good cover of
{\mathcal{M}}
, i.e., a finite collection of open sets
{\mathcal{U}}=\{{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}}\}_{{\alpha}\in I}
which are all contractible and all their intersections are contractible – such a cover always exists for a smooth manifold, see p. 42 in
Bott and Tu
. To each
{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}}
we associate a copy of
\mathbb{C}
. Note that we consider
\mathbb{C}
as a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra – for that reason we associate a copy of
\mathbb{C}
to the whole patch instead of forming, say, a line bundle. To each double intersection
{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}{\beta}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}}\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{{\beta}}
, we associate a (trivial, since all intersections are contractible) line bundle
L_{{\alpha}{\beta}}
of
\mathbb{C}-\mathbb{C}
bimodules. The tensor product of a pair of
\mathbb{C}
, as of bimodules, is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
, with the isomorphism being a non-zero complex number. In other words, to any triple intersection
{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}{\beta}{\gamma}}
, we associate an isomorphism of line bundles

\displaystyle\lambda_{{\alpha}{\beta}{\gamma}}:L_{{\alpha}{\beta}}\otimes L_{{\beta}{\gamma}}\xrightarrow{\sim}L_{{\alpha}{\gamma}}\,.

(2)

The associativity of the tensor product is a condition on
\lambda
holding for any quadruple intersection. In other words,
\lambda
is a Čech 2-cocyle valued in the sheaf of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-valued functions, i.e., an element of
Z^{2}({\mathcal{U}},\underline{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
. The data consisting of the line bundles
\{L_{{\alpha}{\beta}}\}_{{\alpha},{\beta}\in I}
, and isomorphisms
\{\lambda_{{\alpha}{\beta}{\gamma}}\}_{{\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma}\in I}
is nothing but a data of a
\operatorname{U}(1)
bundle gerbe in the sense of Hitchin-Chatterjee
Hitchin 2001
.

There are natural ways of defining isomorphisms of bundle gerbes, that we are not going to spell out. We recommend
Kristel et al. 2021
for a thorough discussion of various 2-stacks and where bundle gerbes are presented as special types of 2-line bundles and, more generally, of 2-vector bundles.
In the appropriate category, bundle gerbes defined on different good covers of
{\mathcal{M}}
are equivalent, as bundle gerbes defined on different covers can be compared by pulling them back to the intersection of the two covers. Instead of working with good cover
{\mathcal{U}}
, we can take its Čech nerve
N({\mathcal{U}})
so that
H^{2}(N({\mathcal{U}}),\underline{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\cong H^{3}(N({\mathcal{U}}),{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Remark 1
.

Let us conclude with a remark regarding the connection between our parameter space and the “space-time” from
Moore and Segal 2006
. Using the algebro-geometric intuition, we can naively imagine a family of Frobenius algebras as a commutative Frobenius algebra whose spectrum “is”
{\mathcal{M}}
. However, any Frobenius algebra is finite dimensional and has a finite spectrum. This was observed in
Moore and Segal 2006
and the spectrum was taken as the space-time. According to the discussion above, their space-time is a Čech nerve of our parameter space
{\mathcal{M}}
. Nevertheless, we will not abandon the idea of having an algebra with spectrum
{\mathcal{M}}
; we will argue that the appropriate algebra is a continuous-trace C
∗
-algebra with spectrum
{\mathcal{M}}
.

3
Topological states in 1d

In this section we describe examples of parametrized states on spin chains with zero correlation length. We consider a family of states and assume that each state in the family is translation invariant (TI), pure, and split. Such states are very well studied even in infinite volume thanks to the formalism of generalized matrix-product states
Accardi 1974
;
Fannes et al. 1992
;
Bratteli et al. 1996
. We review the description of such states as well their RG fixed points. In the end, we construct families of the RG fixed points.

Remark 2
.

Let us comment on the split property. One should think of split states in 1d as having relatively low entanglement, see
Keyl et al. 2006
for a review of entanglement types of spin chains. Importantly, a unique ground states of any local gapped Hamiltonian is pure and split, see Corollary 3.2 of
Matsui 2013
. Strictly speaking, the split states are more general than the short-range entangled states of
Kapustin et al. 2021
. We stress that the split property is the characterization of the entanglement pattern of the spin-chain rather than its dynamical properties. In particular, correlation functions of local operators in a split state might not decay exponentially fast with the distance.

The philosophy behind the infinite-volume states is that we define a state on the whole lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}
, yet we substitute only (quasi-) local operators. This way, we do not have to worry about existence of the thermodynamic limit. Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be the local Hilbert space of the spin chain. Unless specified,

is finitely dimensional. The algebra of operators acting on each site is the algebra of all bounded operators
{\operatorname{B}}{\mathcal{P}}
(matrix algebra). Let

be the C

algebra of quasi-local operators on

which is the operator norm completion of the infinite tensor product of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}=\overline{\bigotimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\operatorname{B}}{\mathcal{P}}}^{C^{*}}\,.

(3)

3.1
RG fixed points and trivial topological phases

We wish to study topological phases through the lens of the renormalization group fixed points. Let
\omega
be a state presented by a pair
(\Phi,\phi)
or SFCS
(\mathbb{E},\phi)
. Renormalization of quantum states in 1d comprises of blocking neighboring sites and removing local entanglement via unitary transformations
Verstraete et al. 2005
. The SFCS formalism allows to define the RG flow in a coordinate-free way: the action on
\Phi
extracted from
\omega
is simply
\Phi\mapsto\Phi\circ\Phi
. Upon blocking of sites, the new local Hilbert space has the dimension equal to the number of Kraus operators representing
\Phi\circ\Phi
. Therefore, the local Hilbert space either does not grow if
{\mathcal{V}}=\mathbb{C}
, or bounded by
(\mbox{dim}{\mathcal{V}})^{2}
. As an example, the fixed point obtained from the AKLT model
Fannes et al. 1992
is given by
{\mathcal{V}}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
{\mathcal{P}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{4}
, and
\rho=\mathds{1}
.

States that can be represented by SFCS with
\dim{\mathcal{V}}<\infty
are known in the literature as finitely correlated states
Fannes et al. 1992
. From the point of view of infinite spin chains, this case is somewhat degenerate as it assumes that there is a finite number of non-zero Schmidt coefficients in the decomposition of such states into the left and right half-chains (the split property allows us to define the Schmidt coefficients).

In what follows we assume that
\dim{\mathcal{V}}=\infty
. Thus, at the RG fixed points, the local Hilbert space dimension is infinite. Strictly speaking,
\Phi^{N}
as
N\to\infty
cannot be a part of the SFCS data as it corresponds to
\dim{\mathcal{P}}=\infty
. The CP map corresponding to RG fixed point is the ultraweak limit of
\Phi^{N}
as
N\to\infty
.

3.2
Families of RG fixed states

We consider families of topologically trivial physical states continuously parametrized by a topological space
M
. For simplicity, we assume
{\mathcal{M}}
to be a compact manifold and each state in the family to be an RG fixed state.

A continuous family of pure quantum mechanical states can be parametrized by sections of a not necessarily trivial hermitian line bundle
Simon 1983
–this is the usual setup for ordinary Berry curvature
Berry 1984
. The non-vanishing Berry curvature is an obstruction to the existence of a continuous vector field in the Hilbert space presenting the pure state. Similarly, higher Berry curvature is an obstruction for a family of one-dimensional states to admit a continuous representation by a field of MPS tensors, assuming each element of the family admits such a representation
Ohyama and Ryu 2024
;
Qi et al. 2023
.

Due to the theorem by Hastings
Hastings 2007
, any unique ground state of a local gapped Hamiltonian can be
approximated
by an MPS with a finite-dimensional bond space. In our approach, we use generalized MPS exactly presenting any split pure state. Families of injective MPS with bond space
{\mathcal{V}}
of constant dimension along the parameter space correspond to principle bundles with the structure group
\operatorname{PGL}({\mathcal{V}})

Ohyama et al. 2024
;
Ohyama and Ryu 2024
;
Qi et al. 2023
. Such bundles can realize only the torsion part of
H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
, due to the Serre theorem
Serre 1957
. In order to realize non-torsion classes,
Ohyama and Ryu 2024
;
Qi et al. 2023
consider families of MPS with bond spaces dimensions varying from patch to patch. We employ generalized MPS with a constant but infinite bond dimension which allow us to realize the non-torsion classes.

A generalized RG fixed MPS tensor is defined up to an overall conjugation with a unitary. Let
T,T^{\prime}:{\mathcal{P}}\to{\operatorname{B}}{\mathcal{V}}
be gMPS tensors such that

\displaystyle T^{\prime}(\psi)=U\,T(\psi)\,U^{*}\,,\quad U\in\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{V}})\,.

(10)

The corresponding pairs
(\Phi,\phi)
and
(\operatorname{Ad}_{U}\circ\Phi,\phi\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{U^{*}})
define the same state on
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
. This gauge redundancy allows us to glue families of gMPS tensors from the local data.

For simplicity, we consider only RG fixed tensors. In order to define a family of RG fixed gMPS tensors, we first define locally-trivial bundles of Hilbert-Schmidt operators. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be the infinite dimensional separable Hilbert space – this space will play the role of
{\mathcal{V}}
from the previous section. A locally-trivial bundle of Hilbert-Schmidt operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
(HS bundle)
E
trivialized over a good cover
{\mathcal{U}}
is a bundle with a typical fiber
\operatorname{HS}{\mathcal{H}}
:

\displaystyle E|_{{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}}}\cong{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}}\times\operatorname{HS}{\mathcal{H}}\,.

(11)

The structure group of
E
is the projective unitary group
\mathbb{P}\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
as
\mbox{Aut}(\operatorname{HS}{\mathcal{H}})=\mathbb{P}\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
. There are many non-equivalent topologies on
\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\mathbb{P}\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
: we choose the compact-open topology, see details in
Atiyah and Segal 2004
. In this topology,
\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
acts continuously on the algebra
\operatorname{HS}{\mathcal{H}}
by conjugations (Prop. A1.1 of
Atiyah and Segal 2004
) and is contractible (Prop. A2.1, loc. cit.).

Proposition 2
.

The set of isomorphism classes of bundles of Hilbert-Schmidt operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
is isomorphic to the Abelian groups

\displaystyle H^{1}({\mathcal{M}},\underline{\mathbb{P}\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})})\cong H^{2}({\mathcal{M}},\underline{\operatorname{U}(1)})\cong H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})\,.

(12)

Proof.

The proof of the analogous classification result for bundles of algebras of compact operators is given in
Dixmier and Douady 1963
and reviewed in
Brylinski 1993
. The proof of the present proposition repeats verbatim after we notice that
\mbox{Aut}(\operatorname{HS}{\mathcal{H}})=\mathbb{P}\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
.
∎

According to (
9
), the parametrized RG-fixed gMPS tensor over
{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}}
is defined by a local frame for a HS bundle. In other words, there exists a global gMPS parametrization of a family of RG fixed states if and only if there exists a global frame for the HS bundle. The latter exists if and only if the HS bundle is trivial, i.e., the corresponding cohomology class in
H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
vanishes.

Finally, we want to note that a similar analysis can be conducted for the general injective, but not necessarily RG fixed, gMPS tensors.

3.3
Projective Hilbert bundles

Let us describe how all classes of HS bundles can be constructed. This construction will be specifically useful for the RG fixed points.

Consider a locally trivial bundle
P
with a fiber
\mathbb{P}{\mathcal{H}}
, where
\mathbb{P}{\mathcal{H}}
is a projectivization of
{\mathcal{H}}
, and the structure group
\mathbb{P}\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
over
{\mathcal{M}}
. Locally, the total space of this bundle is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{U}}_{{\alpha}}\times\mathbb{P}{\mathcal{H}}
. One can ask

Any bundle of projective Hilbert spaces

correspondence. Indeed, in finite volume, boundary conditions for a family of gMPS are given by a (projective) Hilbert bundle, a module over the HS bundle. There exists a family of Hilbert spaces hosting the boundary conditions if and only if the twisting class of a bundle of projective spaces vanishes.

Let remark on the similarity of this construction with the standard construction of 1d SPTs: given a projective representation

4
Topological T-duality

The T-duality is one of the first dualities discovered in string theory where it relates different toroidal compactifications, see, e.g.,
Becker et al. 2006
for a textbook account. The T-duality has a remnant in (1+1)-dimensional gauged non-linear sigma models
Buscher 1988
whose target-space is a circle bundle. The circle action on the target space of an NLSM is a global symmetry which can be gauged; the T-duality of NLSMs is an exact invertible transformation of the path integral which corresponds to gauging the circle action. The NLSM consists of three pieces of data
({\mathcal{M}},B,g)
where
{\mathcal{M}}
is a smooth manifold, which we assume to be a total space of a circle bundle,
B
is a B-field defining a class in
H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
, and
g
is a metric. Then, the T-duality then is a rule, aka the Buscher rules
Buscher 1988
, associating another triple
({\mathcal{M}},B,g)\leftrightarrow(\widehat{\mathcal{M}},\widehat{B},\widehat{g})
.
This is called the
geometric T-duality
as it is formulated with the use of a metric. Crucially, the topology of
{\mathcal{M}}
can be different from the topology of
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}

Bouwknegt et al. 2004
. The rule of topology change is described below.

The T-duality can be formulated purely topologically, without the use of a metric; this version is called
topological T-duality
. The (Abelian) topological T-duality after Bunke and Schick is formulated in terms of a pair
({\mathcal{M}},H)
where
{\mathcal{M}}
is a total space of a circle bundle over the base
{\mathcal{B}}
, and a cohomology class
H\in H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
. The T-duality prescribes that there exists another pair
(\widehat{\mathcal{M}},\widehat{H})
where
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is a total space of another circle bundle over the same base
{\mathcal{B}}
and a class
\widehat{H}\in H^{3}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
(Lemma 2.32 of
Bunke and Schick 2005
).

As a circle bundle,
{\mathcal{M}}
is completely determined by the first Chern class
c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\in\mbox{H}^{2}({\mathcal{B}},{\mathbb{Z}})
of the associated complex line bundle. The T-dual space
{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}
is another circle bundle and the Chern classes of the T-dual spaces are related via

\displaystyle c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})=\hat{\pi}_{*}\widehat{H}\,,\quad c_{1}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}})=\pi_{*}H

4.2
T-duality via continuous-trace algebras

Informally, the T-duality is a gauging of a circle action on a circle bundle. In this section we review the operator algebra approach to T-duality so that it can be seen as a transformation of an algebra generated by parametrized gMPS tensors.

We are interested in C
∗
-algebras generated by the gMPS tensors. In order to access the tools available for the Banach algebras, we complete the HS bundles in the operator norm topology. The completion of
\operatorname{HS}{\mathcal{H}}
is the C
∗
-algebra of compact operators
{\operatorname{K}}{\mathcal{H}}
such that the bundle of Hilbert-Schmidt operators upon completion becomes a bundle with the typical fiber
{\operatorname{K}}{\mathcal{H}}
and structure group
\mathbb{P}\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
. A theorem by Dixmier and Douady
Dixmier and Douady 1963
states that the C
∗
-algebra of sections of a bundle of compact operators over
{\mathcal{M}}
(assumed to be a compact manifold) is isomorphic to a stable continuous-trace (CT) algebra with spectrum
{\mathcal{M}}
. A CT algebra
A
is stable if
A\otimes{\operatorname{K}}{\mathcal{H}}\cong A
. The CT algebras can be defined internally but we use their connection with bundles of compact operators as a definition, see also
Rosenberg 1989
.

Remark 4
.

The Gelfand duality
I. Gelfand 1943
;
Negrepontis 1971
establishes a correspondence between commutative C
∗
algebras and locally compact Hausdorff topological spaces, such that any such algebra
A
is isomorphic to the algebra of continuous complex functions on a compact topological space
A\cong C_{0}(X)
, where
X
is the Gelfand spectrum of
A
. Informally, the CT algebras locally look like
C_{0}({\mathcal{M}})\otimes{\operatorname{K}}{\mathcal{H}}

Raeburn and Williams 1998
.

The CT algebras with the spectrum
{\mathcal{M}}
are classified by the Dixmier-Doudy invariant
Dixmier and Douady 1963
taking values in
H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
. Let us denote by
CT({\mathcal{M}},H)
the isomorphism class of CT algebras with spectrum
{\mathcal{M}}
and Dixmier-Douady invariant
H\in H^{3}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

A crucial for our applications is a theorem by Philips and Raeburn
Raeburn and Rosenberg 1988
that says that the result of a gauging of a free
\operatorname{U}(1)
action on a CT algebra is another CT algebra. Practically, we are interested in compact manifolds, which are automatically second-countable (see, e.g.
Lee 2010
) and have homotopy types of finite CW complexes
Kirby and Siebenmann 1969
.

Theorem 3
.

(Raeburn-Rosenberg
Raeburn and Rosenberg 1988
;
Raeburn and Williams 1998
;
Rosenberg and Sciences
)

{\mathcal{M}}
be a compact topological space which is second countable and has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex. Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a total space of a circle bundle
\pi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
and

with

S^{1}\cong{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}

{\mathcal{M}}

{\mathbb{R}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}

The auxiliary results proving the existence of the lifts can be found in

In the operator-algebraic approach, the T-duality is a transformation of the algebra of sections of bundles of compacts operators. We obtained the latter as a C

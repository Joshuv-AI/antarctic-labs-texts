D-TNet: a hybrid Dense Net-transformer model for robust diabetic retinopathy detection
Authors: Priyadharshini Sekar, Ramasubramanian Bhoopalan, N. Nagaprasad, Tadesse Regassa Mamo, S. P. Dhanabal, Ramaswamy Krishnaraj
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
One of the main causes of blindness in the globe and a dangerous side effect of diabetes is Diabetic Retinopathy (DR). Preventing permanent vision loss and guaranteeing prompt medical intervention depend on early identification and precise DR severity assessment. Conventional techniques for diagnosing DR depend on experts manually examining retinal pictures, which takes time and is vulnerable to subjectivity. Although Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies have become attractive substitutes, current approaches frequently suffer from unbalanced classification performance, poor generalisability, and trouble differentiating various DR severity levels. This work offers a Hybrid Deep Learning (DL) strategy D-TNet, which combines DenseNet121 for spatial feature extraction with a Transformer architecture for modeling long-range contextual dependencies to overcome these issues. The model detects key DR indicators like microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and neovascularization. A collection of retinal images from APTOS2019 and Messidor-2 datasets was used to train and test this hybrid model for DR severity grading in five different categories namely healthy retina, Mild, Moderate, Proliferative, and Severe. The model detects key DR indicators like microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and neovascularization. Evaluated on two benchmark datasets, the model achieved 97% accuracy, 0.94 F1-score, and 0.93 kappa score on APTOS2019, and 86% accuracy, 0.79 F1-score, and 0.80 kappa score on Messidor-2. These results demonstrate robust and balanced classification across all five DR severity stages. The model overcame the sensitivity and specificity issues frequently seen in traditional AI-based techniques by exhibiting stable and balanced classification throughout all DR phases. This method has the potential to greatly improve diabetic eye care by offering dependable and scalable DR detection, especially in environments with limited resources. Future work includes domain adaptation for cross-dataset validation and real-world deployment, along with the integration of multimodal data such as blood sugar level, fasting glucose, and HbA1c to enhance diagnostic precision.

Introduction

DR is a major global cause of blindness and visual impairment, especially among individuals with diabetes. It occurs due to damage to the retina’s blood vessels, progressing through different severity levels that, if undetected, can lead to irreversible vision loss [1]. Therefore, timely detection and accurate grading of DR severity are essential to ensure appropriate medical intervention.

Traditional DR diagnosis relies on ophthalmologists manually inspecting retinal fundus images. While effective, this process is time-consuming, subjective, and susceptible to inter-observer variability. The increasing prevalence of diabetes and DR further highlights the need for automated, efficient, and objective diagnostic solutions. AI has emerged as a viable alternative, offering faster processing and greater consistency [2]. However, existing AI-based models often suffer from limitations such as unbalanced classification performance across DR severity stages, poor generalizability to new datasets, and difficulty in distinguishing between mild and moderate stages.

To address these challenges, this paper introduces D-TNet, a novel hybrid DL model that integrates DenseNet121 with Transformer components. The hybrid D-TNet model effectively combines DenseNet121 for extracting spatial lesion features such as microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and exudates and a Transformer module that captures long-range contextual dependencies and global patterns across the retina. This synergy enhances the model’s robustness in DR classification, particularly in distinguishing subtle differences across the five severity levels.

The main technical contribution of this work is the design of a unified architecture that uses the spatial representation power of DenseNet121 and the contextual modeling strength of Transformers to achieve balanced classification performance. Evaluation on APTOS2019 and Messidor-2 datasets demonstrates that this combination outperforms existing models in terms of accuracy, F1-score, and generalization. Combining DenseNet121 and Transformers enhances spatial-contextual learning for DR grading, leading to improved classification performance and generalization.

Dataset description

Two well-known datasets, APTOS2019 and Messidor-2, were used to train and assess the D-TNet model. These datasets were selected for training deep learning models that need high-quality ground truth because they provide well-annotated DR severity labels, high-resolution fundus, and a variety of patient demographics. While Messidor-2 provides high-resolution images with comprehensive clinical annotations and consistent acquisition techniques, making it perfect for cross-dataset generalisation research, APTOS2019 includes fundus images with variable lighting and noise, mimicking real-world variability.

In this research, 50% of the images from each dataset were used for training the model, 30% for validation, and the remaining 20% of unseen images were reserved for testing. Also, Messidor-2, with different image acquisition conditions, was purposefully used for external evaluation to assess generalizability across datasets.

Based on the retinal damage development, DR is categorised into five severity categories. No_DR denotes that there are no discernible symptoms of DR in the retinal image. Minimal microaneurysms, which indicate early-stage damage, is an indicator of mild DR. Moderately progressive DR is characterised by a greater number of microaneurysms and minor haemorrhages. Significant retinal abnormalities, such as large haemorrhages, exudates, and cotton wool patches, which signify progressive damage, are indicative of severe DR. The most severe stage, known as proliferative DR (PDR), is characterised by the creation of new blood vessels (neovascularisation) and significant retinal damage, which, if left untreated, frequently results in visual loss.

Raw fundus images are pre-processed for better input consistency.

a. Resize: Each image is resized to a fixed dimension d x d where d is fixed to 224 in order to match the model’s input size.

While resizing to 224 × 224 may risk minor loss of high-frequency detail, this standard size was selected to balance memory efficiency and model compatibility. Diagnostic features such as microaneurysms and hemorrhages remain apparent due to the model’s receptive field, data augmentation, and contextual modeling via Transformers. This resizing is consistent with prior DR studies using CNN-based architectures.

Hybrid model architecture

The proposed model in Fig. 3 utilizes the combination [15] of DenseNet 121 architecture with Transformer component.Fig. 3Proposed D-TNet architecture.

Proposed D-TNet architecture.

Figure 3 shows the suggested D-TNet hybrid design. It has two parallel processing branches for feature extraction: a Transformer branch that models global contextual relationships throughout the retinal fundus picture, and a DenseNet121 branch that captures local spatial data. Features taken from DenseNet121 are not sent into the Transformer in D-TNet, in contrast to sequential fusion designs. Rather, both branches separately process the same pre-processed input.

The input is first separated into non-overlapping patches in the Transformer branch. A linear projection layer is used to flatten these patches and project them linearly into a fixed-dimensional embedding space. The Transformer may then comprehend relative locations by appending positional encodings to each projected patch in order to preserve spatial information throughout the series. In order to capture long-range relationships and global structure in the image, the embedded patches with positional encodings are then run through a Transformer encoder, which has feed-forward layers and multi-head self-attention.

A thorough spatial-contextual feature representation is created by concatenating the outputs from DenseNet121 and the Transformer encoder. A classification head receives this fused vector and grades the DR severity into five categories. All input fundus images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels and normalized to a range of [− 1, 1] using a mean and standard deviation of 0.5 per channel. To improve generalization and reduce overfitting, several augmentation techniques were applied. These include random horizontal flipping (50% chance), rotation within ± 15°, brightness adjustment (range 0.8–1.2), and zooming between 90 and 110%. This preprocessing pipeline ensures robustness to variations commonly observed in retinal imaging.

Both the datasets contain unequal distribution across the severity levels, with “No DR” being the most dominant and high severity with less priority. To mitigate this, we used a class-weighted cross-entropy loss function, where the weights were assigned inversely proportional to class frequencies in the training set. This ensured that underrepresented classes were appropriately penalized during training. While oversampling and focal loss were considered, class weighting proved both effective and computationally efficient in our experiments and the outcomes are tabulated in Table 3 and Table 4.

Feature extraction with DenseNet121

In order to extract significant patterns from the fundus images, DenseNet121 functions as a feature extractor. To ensure feature reuse and effective information flow, it employs a highly linked architecture in which every layer has direct access to the feature maps of every layer that came before it. The input images are processed by DenseNet121 using a number of convolutional layers organised in blocks, each of which extracts features.

1. Initial Layers (Low-Level Features): These layers serve as the basis for higher-level pattern identification by identifying fundamental visual structures including edges, gradients, and textures.

2. Intermediate Layers (Mid-Level Features): These layers begin to identify more specialised features unique to retinal pictures, such exudates (lipid deposits), microaneurysms [16, 17] which are tiny blood vessel bulges, and dot haemorrhages which is shown in Fig. 4.Fig. 4Retinal fundus with DR features.

Retinal fundus with DR features.

3. Deeper Layers (High-Level Features): These mid-level features are combined by deeper layers to create more abstract representations, including patterns that show the severity of an illness, anomalies in blood vessels, or clusters of lesions.

The DenseNet121 architecture has densely connected convolutional layers which is meant for feature reuse and gradient flow. This architecture specially extracts spatial features namely microaneurysms, hemorrhages and exudates from the retinal images which is shown in Fig. 5.Fig. 5Feature extraction of DR images.

The feature map from the DenseNet121 architecture is then passed through a transformer model which uses self-attention mechanism to capture long range dependencies namely Cotton Wool Spots, Neovascularization and contextual relationships between these features across different parts of the retinal image, allowing for a deeper understanding of the spatial and contextual layout of abnormalities [20].

The Transformer module comprises 4 encoder layers with 8 multi-head attention units and a hidden dimension size of 2048. The input to the Transformer is generated by flattening the feature map output from DenseNet121 into a sequence of patches, with positional encodings added to preserve spatial structure. The Transformer block is inserted directly after DenseNet121’s last dense block, enabling contextual refinement before final classification.

Results and discussion

The APTOS2019 and Messidor-2 datasets were used to assess the hybrid model for detection and severity classification of DR that combines DenseNet121 and Transformer components. The model’s capacity to manage both local and global feature extraction, a critical component for precise DR classification was seen in its outstanding performance across a range of criteria.

Experimental setup

Using the Google Colab platform, each experiment was carried out. The experiment was implemented using the TensorFlow and Keras packages. The dataset is divided into three parts 50%, 30%, 20% , one for training the model, one for validating the trained model and remaining is for testing the model. With the testing dataset, the performance of the model is evaluated. For the purpose of training, the image size is taken as 224 × 224 pixels as the model accepts only the standard rate. Together with the findings, the testing performance is displayed22.

The model exhibits moderately higher computational demands compared to standard CNNs. Training time per epoch is approximately 5% longer than DenseNet121 alone due to the added Transformer block, and inference latency increases by 12 ms per image. However, this overhead is offset by the improved performance across all DR severity levels. The model’s size is approximately 55 million parameters, and future work will focus on optimizing it for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

All the above-mentioned results are clinically validated by an ophthalmologist.

Future scope

Though the model has performed well on the APTOS and Messidor-2 datasets, future enhancements will involve training the model on a broader variety of datasets to ensure its applicability across a wider range of retinal imaging scenarios and diverse populations. Future research will also include domain adaptation techniques for cross-dataset validation, aimed at achieving consistent performance across different clinical environments. Additionally, the integration of multimodal data such as patients’ medical records including blood sugar levels, fasting glucose, and HbA1c alongside retinal images will be explored to further improve diagnostic accuracy and clinical relevance. Real-world deployment strategies, including model optimization for mobile and low-resource settings, are also planned.

Conclusion

In order to tackle complicated medical imaging problems like the diagnosis of DR, the suggested hybrid strategy emphasises the significance of combining spatial feature extraction with contextual comprehension. Through the integration of Transformers’ attention mechanisms and DenseNet121’s dense feature representation capabilities, the model successfully captures global relationships as well as fine-grained visual features in retinal fundus pictures. This dual ability ensures a thorough comprehension of the clinical differences linked to varying degrees of DR severity. Furthermore, the model’s promise as a dependable tool for practical applications is shown by its stability and balanced performance throughout different DR phases. It is a useful tool in automated healthcare systems because it may lessen the subjectivity and time restrictions involved in manual diagnosis. The approach has the potential to greatly progress the standard of diabetic eye care by enabling early diagnosis and severity evaluation, especially in areas with restricted access to ophthalmologists or sophisticated diagnostic equipment. To sum up, this research is a significant step in using AI to create scalable and effective healthcare solutions. The model’s usability and revolutionary impact in ophthalmology will be strengthened by additional efforts to test it across various datasets and optimise its use in clinical processes. In addition, recent research’s [34–40] has highlighted the promise of multimodal approaches for DR detection. For example, studies integrating Optical Coherence Tomography Angiography (OCTA) with fundus photography have demonstrated improved lesion localization and grading accuracy through richer vascular and structural information. Furthermore, optimized explainable AI-based multimodal fusion techniques have shown potential in enhancing transparency and reliability of automated DR detection. Incorporating such multimodal frameworks into our proposed D-TNet in the future could provide more clinically robust and generalizable diagnostic solutions.

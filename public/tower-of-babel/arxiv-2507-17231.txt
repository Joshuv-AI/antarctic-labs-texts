Upper bounds for graded betti numbers of projective schemes in the first nontrivial strand
Doyoon Ha, Minjae Kwon, JeongDon Lee, Jinhyung Park

Abstract
. Using Boij–Söderberg theory, we give a quick new approach to the results of Han–Kwak [ 13 ] and Ahn–Han–Kwak [ 1 ] on upper bounds for graded betti numbers of projective schemes in the first nontrivial strand.

Introduction

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}

\mathbf{P}^{5}
, then

but
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{4}
does not satisfy property
\ND(2)
. However, when
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay, property
\ND(q)
is equivalent to (

).

The aim of this paper is to give a simpler alternative proof of the following theorem on upper bounds for graded betti numbers of projective schemes in the first nontrivial strand, which was first proven by Ahn–Han–Kwak
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
13
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
for the case that
X
is a projective variety and
q=1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}V=\mathbf{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate projective scheme of codimension
e\geq 1
. For an integer
q\geq 1
, assume that
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies property
\ND(q)
. Then

\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)\leq{p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}~\text{ for all $p\geq 0$}.

In particular,
\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)=0
for
p\geq e+1
.
Furthermore, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)={p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}
for all
p
with
1\leq p\leq e
.

(2)

\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)={p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}
for some
p
with
1\leq p\leq e
.

(3)

\deg(X)={e+q\choose q}
and
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay.

(4)

The graded minimal free resolution of
S(X)
is of the form

\displaystyle 0\longleftarrow S(X)\longleftarrow S\longleftarrow S(-q-1)^{\oplus\kappa_{1,q}}\longleftarrow S(q-2)^{\oplus\kappa_{2,q}}\longleftarrow

\displaystyle\cdots\longleftarrow S(-q-e+1)^{\oplus\kappa_{e-1,q}}\longleftarrow S(-q-e)^{\oplus\kappa_{e,q}}\longleftarrow 0,

where
S:=\operatorname{Sym}V
and
\kappa_{p,q}={p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}
for all
p
with
1\leq p\leq e
.

We stress that property
\ND(q)
is crucial in Theorem
1.1
. To obtain the upper bounds in the theorem, it is not enough to assume that
H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{r},\mathscr{I}_{X/\mathbf{P}^{r}}(q))=0
(i.e., the
q
-th row of the betti table of
S(X)
may be the first nontrivial strand). The second row of the betti table of the image of a general projection of the second Veronese surface to
\mathbf{P}^{4}
is the first nontrivial strand, but every nonzero entry in the second row is strictly bigger than the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
for
e=2
and
q=2
(see Example
2.2
(1)).

When
X
is a variety, property
\ND(1)
is equivalent to the non-degeneracy of
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
. If
X
is not a variety, then
\ND(1)
is stronger than the non-degeneracy. For instance, the union of two skew lines in
\mathbf{P}^{3}
is non-degenerate but does not satisfy
\ND(1)
. In the case that
X
is a variety and
q=1
as in
[
13
]
, the
\kappa_{p,1}(S(X),V)
attain the maximum if and only if
\deg(X)=e+1
, i.e.,
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is a variety of minimal degree, which is automatically arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay. Varieties of minimal degree were classified by del Pezzo–Bertini (see
[
8
]
). When
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}V
is the
q
-secant variety of a variety of minimal degree (the Zariski closure of the union of the linear spans of
q
points on the variety), it is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay and
\deg(X)={e+q\choose q}
so that the
\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)
attain the maximum (see
[
5
]
). It would be interesting to classify arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay projective varieties of degree
{e+q\choose q}
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, partial elimination ideal theory and generic initial ideal theory were used in
[
13
]
and
[
1
]
, respectively. But our proof is based on Boij–Söderberg theory
[
3
,
9
]
and is completely different from the previous approach. Advantages of our new method, besides giving a quick proof, are that we remove the characteristic zero assumption of
[
1
]
and that we make the role of property
\ND(q)
more transparent. In addition, we clarify the characterization when the graded betti numbers attain maximum.

Now, consider the section ring
R(X,L):=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{m})
of a very ample line bundle
L
on a smooth projective variety
X
. Then
R(X,L)
is a finitely generated graded module over
\operatorname{Sym}H^{0}(X,L)
. Set

K_{p,q}(X,L):=K_{p,q}(R(X,L),H^{0}(X,L))~\text{ and }~\kappa_{p,q}(X,L):=\dim K_{p,q}(X,L).

We have the following upper bounds for
\kappa_{p,q}(X,L)
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\geq 1
, and
L
be a very ample line bundle on
X
giving an embedding
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbf{P}^{r}
of codimension

. For an integer
q\geq 1
, assume that the natural map
S^{i}H^{0}(X,L)\to H^{0}(X,L^{i})
is an isomorphism for
1\leq i\leq q
and
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies property
\ND(q)
. Then

\kappa_{p,q}(X,L)\leq{p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}~\text{ for all $p\geq 0$}.

In particular,
\kappa_{p,q}(X,L)=0
for
p\geq e+1
.
Furthermore, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\kappa_{p,q}(X,L)={p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}
for all
p
with
1\leq p\leq e
.

(2)

\kappa_{p,q}(X,L)={p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}
for some
p
with
1\leq p\leq e
.

(3)

\deg(X)={e+q\choose q}
and
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay.

(4)

The graded minimal free resolution of

is of the form in Theorem
1.1

(4)
.

Proof.

The assumption implies that
K_{p,q}(X,L)=K_{p,q}(S(X),H^{0}(X,L))

∎

2.
Boij–Söderberg Theory

In this section, we review Boij–Söderberg theory, which tells us that the betti table of a finitely generated graded module is a positive rational sum of betti tables of pure modules. This was established in
[
9
]
for Cohen–Macaulay modules and extended to non-Cohen–Macaulay cases in
[
3
]
. We refer to
[
6
, Lecture 2]
for a brief summary.

We fix an
(r+1)
-dimensional vector space
V
over
\mathbf{k}
and consider the polynomial ring
S:=\operatorname{Sym}V=\mathbf{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
. A finitely generated graded Cohen–Macaulay
S
-module
P
is called
pure
if its graded minimal free resolution is a pure resolution, which is an exact sequence of the form

0\longleftarrow P\longleftarrow S(-d_{0})^{b_{0}}\longleftarrow S(-d_{1})^{b_{1}}\longleftarrow\cdots\longleftarrow S(-d_{\ell-1})^{b_{\ell-1}}\longleftarrow S(-d_{\ell})^{b_{\ell}}\longleftarrow 0

with
\ell\leq r+1
, i.e., the betti table of
P
has a single nonzero entry in each column.
Note that every pure resolution can be associated to a
degree sequence

(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1},d_{\ell})
of length
\ell
, which is a strictly increasing sequence of
\ell+1
many integers

d_{0}<d_{1}<\cdots<d_{\ell-1}<d_{\ell}.

Eisenbud–Schreyer
[
9
]
proved that there exists a pure module whose graded minimal free resolution is the pure resolution corresponding to any given degree sequence.

Let
d:=(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1},d_{\ell})
be a degree sequence of length
\ell
, and
P_{d}
be a pure module whose graded minimal free resolution is a pure resolution corresponding to
d
.
Note that
\kappa_{p,q}(P_{d},V)\neq 0
if and only if
q=d_{p}-p
. Using Herzog–Kühl equations (see
[
6
, Proposition 2.1.4]
), one can determine the nonzero graded betti numbers of
P_{d}
up to scaling:

\kappa_{p,d_{p}-p}(P_{d},V)=\kappa_{0,d_{0}}(P_{d},V)\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k\neq p\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{k}-d_{0}}{|d_{k}-d_{p}|}~~\text{ for $p\geq 1$}.

We denote by
\pi(d)
the normalized betti table of
P_{d}
so that the nonzero entry of the
0
-th column is
\kappa_{0,d_{0}}(d):=1
and the nonzero entry of the
p
-th column is

\kappa_{p,d_{p}-p}(d):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k\neq p\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{k}-d_{0}}{|d_{k}-d_{p}|}~~\text{ for $p\geq 1$}.

Here we write
\kappa_{p,q}(d)
for the
(p,q)
-entry of
\pi(d)
. The multiplicity of the “virtual” pure module whose betti table is the normalized betti table
\pi(d)
is

e(d):=\frac{1}{\ell!}\prod_{k\geq 1}(d_{k}-d_{0}).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
d:=(0,d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{e})
be degree sequence of length
e
with
d_{1}\geq q+1
Then

\kappa_{p,q}(d)\leq\underbrace{{p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}}_{=\kappa_{p,q}(d^{e,q})}~~\text{ for $1\leq p\leq e$}~\text{ and }~e(d)\geq\underbrace{{e+q\choose q}}_{=e(d^{e,q})}.

Furthermore, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\kappa_{p,q}(d)={p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}
for all
p
with
1\leq p\leq e
.

(2)

\kappa_{p,q}(d)={p+q-1\choose q}{e+q\choose p+q}
for some
p
with
1\leq p\leq e
.

(3)

e(d)={e+q\choose q}
.

(4)

d=d^{e,q}
.

Proof.

See Example
2.1
for the values of
\kappa_{p,q}(d^{e,q})
and
e(d^{e,q})
. From this, we also get
(4)\Longrightarrow(1)
.
Since
d_{1}\geq q+1
, it follows that
d_{k}\geq q+k
for all
k\geq 1
.
Then

e(d)=\frac{1}{e!}\prod_{k\geq 1}d_{k}\geq\frac{1}{e!}\prod_{k\geq 1}(q+k)=e(d^{e,q}),

and the equality holds if and only if
d=d^{e,q}
. In particular,
(3)\Longleftrightarrow(4)
. Now, notice that
\kappa_{p,q}(d)\neq 0
if and only if
d_{p}=q+p
. Assume that
d_{p}=q+p
for
1\leq p\leq m
.
When
1\leq k<p\leq m
, we have

\frac{d_{k}}{d_{p}-d_{k}}=\frac{q+k}{p-k}=\frac{q+k}{|k-p|}.

When
1\leq p<k\leq e
and
p\leq m
, we have
d_{p}=q+p
and
d_{k}=q+k+a
for some integer
a\geq 0
so that

\frac{d_{k}}{d_{k}-d_{p}}=\frac{q+k+a}{(k-p)+a}\leq\frac{q+k}{k-p}=\frac{q+k}{|k-p|}

and the equality holds if and only if
d_{k}=q+k
.
Thus

\kappa_{p,q}(d)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k\neq p\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{k}}{|d_{k}-d_{p}|}\leq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k\neq p\end{subarray}}\frac{q+k}{|k-p|}=\kappa_{p,q}(d^{e,q})~~\text{ for $1\leq p\leq m$},

and the equality holds for some
p
with
1\leq p\leq m
if and only if
d=d^{e,q}
. This also proves
(2)\Longleftrightarrow(4)
. We have shown that

(2)\Longleftrightarrow(3)\Longleftrightarrow(4)\Longrightarrow(1).

The implication
(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
is trivial.
∎

3.
Proof of Main Theorem

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Let
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}V=\mathbf{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate projective scheme of dimension
n\geq 0
and codimension
e\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Theorem
1.1
holds when
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay.

Proof.

Consider Boij–Söderberg decomposition

\pi(S(X))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\pi(d^{i}),

where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
are positive rational numbers with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=1
and
d^{1},\ldots,d^{m}
are degree sequences of length
e
. As
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies property
\ND(q)
, we have
d_{1}^{i}\geq q+1
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Then the assertion follows from Lemma
2.3
. Here it is clear that
\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)=0
for either
p=0
or
p\geq e+1
.
∎

The following is shown in
[
2
, Lemma 2.19 and Theorem 2.20]
(see also
[
14
, Remark 3.1]
), but we include the proof for reader’s convenience. For the second assertion, property
\ND(q)
plays a crucial role.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
n\geq 1
. For a general element
t\in V
, let
\overline{X}:=Z(t)
and
\overline{V}:=V/\langle t\rangle
so that
\overline{X}\subseteq\mathbf{P}\overline{V}=\mathbf{P}^{r-1}
is a non-degenerate projective scheme of dimension
n-1
and codimension
e\geq 1
.

(a)

Set
I_{t}:=(I_{X/\mathbf{P}^{r}}+(t))/(t)\subseteq I_{\overline{X}/\mathbf{P}^{r-1}}
and
S_{t}:=\operatorname{Sym}\overline{V}/I_{t}
.
Then

K_{p,q}(S(X),V)=K_{p,q}(S_{t},\overline{V})~~\text{ for all $p\geq 0$ and $q\geq 0$}.

(b)

Suppose that
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies property
\ND(q)
for an integer
q\geq 1
. Then there is an injective map
K_{p,q}(S(X),V)\hookrightarrow K_{p,q}(S(\overline{X}),\overline{V})
for every
p\geq 0
. In particular,

\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)\leq\kappa_{p,q}(S(\overline{X}),\overline{V})~~\text{ for all $p\geq 0$}.

Proof.

(a)
Fixing a splitting
V=\overline{V}\oplus\langle t\rangle
, we have an identification

K_{p,q}(S_{t},V)=K_{p,q}(S_{t},\overline{V})\oplus K_{p-1,q}(S_{t},\overline{V}).

Note that
t
is a nonzero divisor on
S(X)
. Thus we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow S(X)(-1)\xrightarrow{~\cdot t~}S(X)\longrightarrow S_{t}\longrightarrow 0,

which induces a long exact sequence

\cdots\longrightarrow K_{p,q-1}(S(X),V)\xrightarrow{~\cdot t~}K_{p,q}(S(X),V)\longrightarrow K_{p,q}(S_{t},V)\longrightarrow K_{p-1,q}(S(X),V)\longrightarrow\cdots.

Notice that the map
K_{p,q-1}(S(X),V)\to K_{p,q}(S(X),V)
is always zero (see
[

, Example 5.1.6]
). Thus we get

K_{p,q}(S_{t},\overline{V})\oplus K_{p-1,q}(S_{t},\overline{V})=K_{p,q}(S(X),V)\oplus K_{p-1,q}(S(X),V).

As
K_{0,q}(S(X),V)=K_{0,q}(S_{t},\overline{V})
, the assertion follows by induction on

.

(b)
There is a natural surjective map
S_{t}\to S(\overline{X})
. Noting that
K_{t}:=I_{\overline{X}/\mathbf{P}^{r-1}}/I_{t}
is the kernel of this map, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K_{t}\longrightarrow S_{t}\longrightarrow S(\overline{X})\longrightarrow 0

of
\operatorname{Sym}\overline{V}
-modules. This induces a long exact sequence

\cdots\longrightarrow K_{p,q}(K_{t},\overline{V})\longrightarrow K_{p,q}(S_{t},\overline{V})\longrightarrow K_{p,q}(S(\overline{X}),\overline{V})\longrightarrow K_{p-1,q+1}(K_{t},\overline{V})\longrightarrow\cdots.

Since
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies property
\ND(q)
, it follows that
K_{t}(\ell)=0
for
\ell\leq q
. In particular,
K_{p,q}(K_{t},\overline{V})=0
. Thus the map
K_{p,q}(S_{t},\overline{V})\to K_{p,q}(S(\overline{X}),\overline{V})
is injective for every
p\geq 0
. Recall from
(a)
that
K_{p,q}(S(X),V)=K_{p,q}(S_{t},\overline{V})
.
∎

Let
\Lambda_{i}\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
be a general linear subspace of dimension
e+i
for each
0\leq i\leq n
with

\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Lambda_{n-1}\subseteq\Lambda_{n}=\mathbf{P}^{r}.

We may write
\Lambda_{i}=\mathbf{P}V_{i}=\mathbf{P}^{e+i}
for some quotient space

of
V
. Set

Then

. We assume from now on that

satisfies property
\ND(q)
for a given integer

\ND(q)
for every

Applying Lemma

It only remains to verify the equivalences in Theorem

4.
Complements

In this section, we present some additional results. Following
[
7
]
, we say that a non-degenerate projective scheme
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}V=\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies
property
N_{d,m}
if

K_{p,q}(S(X),V)=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq m$ and $q\geq d$}.

First, we give a quick proof of the following theorem of Ahn–Han–Kwak
[
1
, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 3.4]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}V=\mathbf{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate projective scheme of codimension
e\geq 1
, and
q\geq 1
be an integer.

(1)

If
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies property
\ND(q)
, then
\deg(X)\geq{e+q\choose q}
.

(2)

If
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies property
N_{q+1,e}
, then
\deg(X)\leq{e+q\choose q}
and the equality holds if and only if the graded minimal free resolution of
S(X)
is of the form in Theorem
1.1

(4)
.

In particular, if
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
satisfies property
\ND(q)
and
N_{q+1,e}
, then
\deg(X)={e+q\choose q}
and
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay.

Proof.

(1)
Let
\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
be a general linear subspace of dimension
e
. Then
X_{0}:=X\cap\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\Lambda_{0}=\mathbf{P}^{e}
satisfies property
\ND(q)
and is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay. Since every degree sequence appearing in the Boij–Söderberg decomposition of
S(X_{0})
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
2.3
, the assertion follows from Lemma
2.3
.

(2)
Consider the Boij–Söderberg decomposition

\pi(S(X))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\pi(d^{i}),

where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
are positive rational numbers with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=1
and
d^{1},\ldots,d^{m^{\prime}}
are degree sequences of length
e
and
d^{m^{\prime}+1},\ldots,d^{m}
are degree sequences of length at least
e+1
. If
1\leq i\leq m^{\prime}
, then
d_{k}^{i}\leq q+k
for all
1\leq k\leq e
so that

e(d^{i})=\frac{1}{e!}\prod_{k\geq 1}d_{k}\leq\frac{1}{e!}\prod_{k\geq 1}(q+k)=e(d^{e,q})={e+q\choose q}.

Thus

\deg(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}x_{i}e(d^{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}x_{i}{e+q\choose q}\leq{e+q\choose q}.

Moreover, if
\deg(X)={e+q\choose q}
, then
m=m^{\prime}=1
and
d^{1}=d^{e,q}
, and hence,
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay. Thus the graded minimal free resolution of
S(X)
is of the form in Theorem
1.1

(4)
. The converse is also immediate from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

When
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}V
is a non-degenerate projective variety and satisfies
\ND(q)
for
q\geq 2
, we do not know what should be the next-to-maximal value of
\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)
. However, if
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}V
is a
q
-secant variety (in which case, the
q
-th row of the betti table is the first nontrivial strand), then not only the maximal value of
\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)
as in Theorem
1.1
but also the next-to-maximal value of
\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)
is known (see
[
5
]
). When the
\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)
attain the next-to-maximal (e.g.,
X
is the
q
-secant variety of an elliptic normal curve), the betti table of
S(X)
is nothing but the betti table
\pi(\widetilde{d}^{e,q})
of the degree sequence

\widetilde{d}^{e,q}:=(0,q+1,q+2,\ldots,q+e-2,q+e-1,2q+e)

of length
e
. We are wondering whether the same is true when
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}V
is a non-degenerate projective variety and satisfies
\ND(q)
. It would be particularly interesting to know whether
K_{e,q}(S(X),V)=0
whenever the
\kappa_{p,q}(S(X),V)
do not attain the maximal value and whether there is an arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay variety whose betti table is the same to the betti table
\pi(d)
of a degree sequence

d:=(0,q+1,q+2,\ldots,q+e-2,q+e-1,d_{e})

of length
e
with
q+e+1\leq d_{e}\leq 2q+e-1
.

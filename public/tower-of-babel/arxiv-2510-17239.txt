Bounded core partitions and Borel–Weil–Bott
Fern Gossow, Andy Huchala

Abstract
. As a consequence of the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem, the nonvanishing of Hodge numbers of line bundles on a Grassmannian can be reinterpreted in terms of the existence of t t -core partitions inside a rectangle. We sharpen known conditions for the nonvanishing of Hodge numbers of line bundles on a Grassmannian and give an elementary proof of the Nakano vanishing theorem in this setting. Using the theory of t t -core partitions, we determine when H i ​ ( Gr ⁡ ( k , n ) , Ω j ​ ( t ) ) H^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n),\Omega^{j}(t)) vanishes in the cases t = 3 t=3 and i + j ≥ k ⁡ ( n − k ) − 1 i+j\geq k(n-k)-1 .

1.
Introduction

Integer partitions and their variants are central objects of study in combinatorics, number theory, algebra, and geometry. We expand on an observation of Snow
Sno86
, who showed, as a corollary of the Borel–Weil–Bott Theorem, that the nonvanishing of Hodge numbers of a line bundle on the Grassmannian is equivalent to the existence of a partition satisfying particular constraints. We translate these constraints into the modern language of
t
-core partitions and derive sharper existence criteria. Readers interested primarily in Hodge numbers of line bundles on the Grassmannian may consult the summary of new results below, while those focused on the combinatorial theory of partitions may disregard the cohomological aspects and view this work as a refinement of the known constraints on
t
-core partitions.

In general, we aim to answer the following questions for fixed integer parameters
0<k<n
and
i,j,t\geq 0
. See Section
2
for precise definitions.

Question 1.1
.

Does there exist a
t
-core partition of size
j
with a
t
-interior of size
i
and which fits into a rectangle of size
(n-k)\times k
?

Question 1.2
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
is the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\Omega^{j}(t):=\Omega_{X}^{j}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(t)
be the sheaf of twisted holomorphic
j
-forms on
X
. Is the group
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))
nonzero?

These questions are equivalent, as proved by Snow (although he did not use the modern terms
t
-core and
t
-interior). We give a proof of this fact in Theorem
2.3
. In his approach, Snow provides a set of linear and quadratic inequalities (see Section
2.3
) on the parameters
k,n,i,j,t
that are sufficient to guarantee the vanishing of
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))
. We instead present
necessary
inequalities for
nonvanishing
, which are more naturally interpreted as existence conditions in the combinatorics of partitions. All given inequalities are
sharp
, meaning there exists an infinite family of partitions attaining equality in each case.

1.

(Theorem
3.1
) If
t>0
, then
i+j\leq k(n-k)
. This is a direct consequence of the Nakano vanishing theorem, and we give a novel elementary proof.

2.

(Proposition
4.1
) If
i>0
, then
j-i\geq t
.

3.

(Proposition
4.2
) If
t>0
, then
j\leq k(t-1)
.

4.

(Proposition
4.6
) If
t>0
, then
(k+1)i\leq(k-1)j
.

5.

(Proposition
4.8
) If
t>0
, then
i+j\leq(j-i)^{2}
.

Combining two sharp inequalities whose equality-satisfying partitions intersect in an infinite set yields another sharp inequality. We list several of these as corollaries in Section
4
, and compare them with previously known bounds.

The above results are necessary, but not sufficient, to assert the nonvanishing of
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))
. Necessary and sufficient conditions are known for
t\leq 2
and
k\leq 2
(see Section
2.3
). We provide a full classification in a few new cases:

6.

(Theorem
3.3
) For
0<t<n
and
i+j=k(n-k)
,
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))\neq 0
if and only if
t\mid n\mid kt
and
i=k(n-k)(n-t)/(2n)
.

7.

(Theorem
3.4
) For
i,t>0
and
i+j=k(n-k)-1
,
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))\neq 0
if and only if
n\geq 5
is odd,
k\in\{2,n-2\}
,
i=(n-3)/2
, and
t=(n+1)/2
.

8.

(Corollary
5.3
) For
t=3
,
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(3))\neq 0
only if
i=x^{2}+xy+y^{2}
or
i=x^{2}+xy+y^{2}-x-y
for some
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The full equivalence is more involved and is provided in Theorem
5.1
.

In
Hir58
, Hirzebruch computes a generating function for the Hodge numbers of a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and in
Gri69
, Griffiths proves a residue theorem providing an explicit description of the corresponding cohomological ring structure. In particular, the primitive cohomology admits a pole-order reduction algorithm (see Section 6.1.3 in
Voi02
) which has been used to efficiently compute the Hasse-Weil zeta function of smooth hypersurfaces in positive characteristic, as in
CHK18
.

In
Fat21
, Mongardi and Fatighenti construct a Griffiths-type ring for the cohomology of a smooth complex hypersurface
Z\subset X=\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
of degree
d
, under the assumptions that
H^{i}(\Omega_{X}^{k(n-k)-i}(d))=0
and
H^{i}(\Omega_{X}^{k(n-k)-i-1}(d))=0
for
i>0
. Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
provide a complete classification of when these vanishings occur. In future work, we plan to extend this result by developing an analogous Griffiths residue theorem for smooth hypersurfaces in
\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
. We note that in both Hirzebruch and in Mongardi and Fatighenti, corresponding results are obtained for smooth complete intersections, although the latter involves vanishings which are beyond the scope of this paper.

We let
\rho(\lambda):=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}
denote the
semiperimeter
of
\lambda
.

A
box
in
\lambda
is a pair
(a,b)
such that
1\leq a\leq\lambda_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}
and
1\leq b\leq\lambda_{a}
, which represents the cell in row
a
and column
b
of the partition diagram. The
hook length
of a box is the value

h_{\lambda}(a,b):=\lambda_{a}+\lambda_{b}^{\mathsf{T}}-a-b+1

which represents the size of the south-east hook whose vertex is the box
(a,b)
. In our running example we shade the hook at the box
(2,1)
, which gives
h_{\lambda}(2,1)=7
.

For a nonnegative integer
t
, a partition is called
t
-
core
if no boxes have hook lengths equal to
t
; this implies (see e.g. Section 1 in
Cho21
) that no hook lengths are multiples of
t
. In particular, a partition
\lambda
is
t
-core for at least all values
t\geq\rho(\lambda)
. Given a
t
-core partition
\lambda
, we let
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)
be the subpartition consisting of boxes
(a,b)
with
h_{\lambda}(a,b)>t
, which we call the
t
-
interior
of
\lambda
. The complement
\lambda\setminus\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)
is known as the
t
-
boundary
.

A partition
\lambda
is
k
-bounded
if
\lambda_{1}\leq k
, and
(k,n-k)
-bounded
if
\lambda_{1}\leq k
and
\lambda_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\leq n-k
. In the latter case, the partition fits into the rectangle of size
(n-k)\times k
, and we set
N:=k(n-k)
. The
complement partition
is defined by

\lambda^{\mathsf{C}}:=(k-\lambda_{n-k},\dots,k-\lambda_{2},k-\lambda_{1}),

which is obtained by rotating the bounding rectangle by
180^{\circ}
and taking the set-theoretic complement of
\lambda
inside the rectangle.

There exists a bijection (see e.g. Theorem 7 in
Lap04
)

(1)

\partial:\{t\text{-core partitions}\}\to\{(t-1)\text{-bounded partitions}\}

given by left-aligning the
t
-boundary of a partition. We denote the image of
\lambda
by
\partial_{\lambda}(t)
. The inverse map is known as taking the
(t-1)
-skew of a
(t-1)
-bounded partition. An example is shown in Figure
1
.

Our main interest lies in the following class of partitions and in determining whether it is nonempty.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix a tuple of integers
(k,n,i,j,t)
with
1\leq k\leq n
and
i,j,t\geq 0
. A
knijt-partition
corresponding to this tuple is a
t
-core,
(k,n-k)
-bounded partition
\lambda
with a
t
-interior of size
|\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)|=i
and a total size of
|\lambda|=j
.

In a number of arguments throughout the paper, we use the trick of
coarsening
a partition, extending a technique used by Snow. This is motivated by the multiplicative form of a partition, where repeated entries are expressed as a power. For example:

(8,5,5,5,3,3,3)=(8^{1},5^{3},3^{3}).

Let
\ell
be the number of unique entries in
\lambda
, and let
r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell}
be the multiplicities of the entries in the order they appear. Similarly, let
s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}
be the multiplicities for
\lambda^{\mathsf{T}}
. On the partition diagram, these values are the heights and widths of the blocks obtained when removing all purely internal edges of
\lambda
. See Figure
2
for an example.

r_{1}
r_{2}
r_{3}
s_{1}

Figure 2.
If
\lambda=(8^{1},5^{3},3^{3})
as above, then
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=(1,3,3)
and
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=(3,2,3)
.

For
1\leq p,q\leq\ell
, we let
B_{p,q}
denote the block in row
p
and column
q
of this coarsening, which has size
|B_{p,q}|=r_{p}s_{q}
. A block is called
internal
if
p+q\leq\ell
,
diagonal
if
p+q=\ell+1
and
external
if
p+q\geq\ell+2
. We observe that the hook lengths of boxes within a block form a contiguous subset of
\mathbb{Z}
.

2.2.
Grassmannians

Fix
V=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(k,V)
of
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V
is denoted by
X=\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
. Consider the tautological sequence

0\to S\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes V\to Q\to 0

for
S
and
Q
the tautological subbundle and quotient bundle, respectively. The dual of a bundle
V
is denoted
V^{\vee}
. Recall that
\Omega_{X}\cong S\otimes Q^{\vee}
. There is an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\cong\bigwedge^{k}(S^{\vee})=\det(S^{\vee})
. We define
\mathcal{O}_{X}(d):=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)^{\otimes d}
. Let
\Omega_{X}^{j}:=\bigwedge^{j}(\Omega_{X})
and
\Omega^{j}(t):=\Omega_{X}^{j}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(t)
.

For a partition
\lambda
and vector bundle
V
, let
\Sigma^{\lambda}V
denote the corresponding Schur functor. In particular, if
V
has rank
r
and
\underline{m}:=(m^{r})
, then

\Sigma^{\underline{m}}V=\det(V)^{m}.

More generally, for a non-increasing sequence of integers
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
(not necessarily all positive) we define

\Sigma^{\alpha}V:=\Sigma^{(\alpha+\underline{m})}V\otimes\det(V)^{\otimes-m},

where
(\alpha+\underline{m})_{i}
is given by
\alpha_{i}+m
. We have the following isomorphisms (see Lemma A.6 in
Nik25
):

(\Sigma^{\alpha}V)^{\vee}\cong\Sigma^{\alpha}(V^{\vee})\cong\Sigma^{-\text{rev}(\alpha)}V

where
-\mathrm{rev}(\alpha):=(-\alpha_{r},-\alpha_{r-1},\ldots,-\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{1})
. Tensor products
\Sigma^{\alpha}V\otimes\Sigma^{\beta}V
can be decomposed using Littlewood-Richardson coefficients (see Corollary 2.3.4 in
Wey03
). The dimension of
\Sigma^{\lambda}V
is given by (see Theorem 7.21.2 in
Stan99
)

\dim(\Sigma^{\lambda}V)=\prod_{(a,b)\in\lambda}\dfrac{\dim V+b-a}{h_{\lambda}(a,b)},

where
h_{\lambda}
is the hook length function.

Now consider the Grassmannian
\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{Gr}(n-k,n)
with tautological sequence

0\to\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes V^{\vee}\to\mathcal{Q}\to 0.

Under the canonical isomorphism
X=\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)\cong\mathrm{Gr}(n-k,n)
, we have
S^{\vee}\mapsto\mathcal{Q}
. Using (2.3.3) in
Wey03
, for free modules
V
,
W
over
k
we have a natural isomorphism

{\bigwedge}^{m}(V\otimes W)\cong\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=m}\Sigma^{\lambda}V\otimes\Sigma^{\lambda^{\mathsf{T}}}W.

Applying this to the twisted cotangent bundle, we obtain

\Omega^{j}_{X}(t)\cong\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=j}\Sigma^{\lambda}S\otimes\Sigma^{\lambda^{\mathsf{T}}}(Q^{\vee})\otimes\Sigma^{-\underline{t}}S\cong\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=j}\Sigma^{\lambda^{\mathsf{T}}}\mathcal{S}\otimes\Sigma^{\lambda-\underline{t}}(\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}).

For dimension reasons, it suffices to consider
(k,n-k)
-bounded partitions, denoted
\lambda\subseteq(k^{n-k})
. Hence,

\Omega^{j}(t)\cong\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=j,\lambda\subseteq(k)^{n-k}}\Sigma^{\lambda^{\mathsf{T}}}\mathcal{S}\otimes\Sigma^{\lambda-\underline{t}}(\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}).

The cohomology of
\Omega^{j}(t)
can then be computed using the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem. To apply Borel–Weil–Bott to each summand, we need to introduce the following notation: for a vector bundle
\Sigma^{\gamma}\mathcal{S}\otimes\Sigma^{\delta}(\mathcal{Q}^{\vee})
on
\mathcal{X}
, let
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
be the corresponding line bundle on the full flag bundle
F
lying over
\mathcal{X}
with
\alpha=(-\mathrm{rev}(\gamma),\delta)
.

Theorem 2.2

(Borel–Weil–Bott, A.9 in
Nik25
)
.

Consider the cohomology

H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X},\Sigma^{\gamma}\mathcal{S}\otimes\Sigma^{\delta}(\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}))\cong H^{\bullet}(F,\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}).

Let
\varrho=(n,n-1,...,2,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be the Weyl vector and consider
\alpha+\varrho\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. If this sequence has two identical entries, the cohomology
H^{\bullet}(F,\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})
vanishes completely. If not, reorder the sequence using a unique
permutation
\sigma
of length
l=l(\sigma)
to obtain a non-increasing sequence
\beta:=\sigma(\alpha+\varrho)-\varrho
.
Then
H^{\bullet}(F,\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})
is concentrated in degree
l
where it equals
\Sigma^{\beta}V^{\vee}
, i.e.

H^{\bullet}(F,\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})\cong\Sigma^{\beta}V^{\vee}[-l].

Remark
.

The value
l(\sigma)
is equal to the number of pairs
(i,j)
such that
i<j
and
(\alpha+\varrho)_{i}<(\alpha+\varrho)_{j}
. We call such pairs
non-inversions
.

We are now ready to prove the equivalence between Question
1.1
and Question
1.2
. We note that this is a modern reformulation of Proposition 3.1 in
Sno86
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
. The cohomology
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))
for
i,j,t\geq 0
is nonzero if and only if there exists a knijt-partition
\lambda
with parameters
(k,n,i,j,t)
.

Proof.

We apply the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem to

H^{\bullet}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))\cong\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=j,\lambda\subseteq(k^{n-k})}H^{\bullet}(F,\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{\lambda}})

where
\alpha_{\lambda}=(-\mathrm{rev}(\lambda^{\mathsf{T}}),\lambda-\underline{t})
. The cohomology is nonzero in degree
i
if there exists a partition
\lambda
appearing in the direct sum above such that
\alpha_{\lambda}+\rho
has distinct entries and exactly
i
non-inversions.

Fix such a partition
\lambda
, which has size
j
and is
(k,n-k)
-bounded. Observe that the two contiguous subsequences

-\mathrm{rev}(\lambda^{\mathsf{T}})+(n,\dots,n-k+1)\quad\text{ and }\quad\lambda-\underline{t}+(n-k,\dots,1)

of
\alpha_{\lambda}+\varrho
are strictly decreasing. Consequently, all non-inversions occur between the
(k-a+1)^{\text{th}}
and
(k+b)^{\text{th}}
terms for some
1\leq a\leq k
and
1\leq b\leq n-k
satisfying

-\lambda_{a}^{\mathsf{T}}+(n-(k-a+1)+1)<\lambda_{b}-t+(n-(k+b)+1).

Rearranging the inequality gives

\lambda_{a}^{\mathsf{T}}+\lambda_{b}-(a+b)+1>t\geq 0.

The left-hand side coincides with the hook length
h_{\lambda}(a,b)
when
(a,b)
is a box inside the partition, and is negative otherwise. If equality occurs, the cohomology vanishes, since the partition has a hook length equal to
t
. Otherwise, the partition is
t
-core, and the number of non-inversions
i
equals the size of
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)
. In this case,
\lambda
is a knijt-partition.
∎

2.3.
Known results

In this section, we summarize known constraints on the parameters
(k,n,i,j,t)
for which there exist associated knijt-partitions, or equivalently, for which the group
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))
is nonzero.

Since
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)\subseteq\lambda\subseteq(k^{n-k})
, we have

0\leq i\leq j\leq N\quad\text{ where }\quad N:=k(n-k).

By replacing
\lambda
with
\lambda^{\mathsf{T}}
(and hence
k
for
n-k
), we may assume without loss of generality that
k\leq n/2
. We also assume
t\geq 0
, since the case
t<0
is covered by Serre duality.

Snow
Sno86
proves that for
k\leq n/2
, the group
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))
vanishes if any of the following inequalities hold:

(S1)

ki\geq(k-1)j>0
,

(S2)

i>N-j
,

(S3)

j>k(n-k-1)\text{ if }(k,n)\neq(2,4)
,

(S4)

j\leq t\text{ and }i>0
,

(S5)

(2k-1)i\geq(k-1)N
.

The
t
-core conjecture, proved by Granville and Ono in
GrOn96
, asserts that a
t
-core partition of any integer
j\geq 0
exists for every
t\geq 4
. This immediately yields the following result:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
t\geq 4
and
k,n-k\geq j
. Then
\Omega^{j}(t)
is not totally acyclic; that is, there exists
i\geq 0
such that
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))\neq 0
.

If
t=0
or
t\geq n
, all
(k,n-k)
-bounded partitions are automatically
t
-core, so
i=0
and
0\leq j\leq N
. The only
1
-core partition is the empty partition, so if
t=1
we have
i=0
and
j=0
. The
2
-core partitions are the staircase partitions
(\kappa,\dots,2,1)
, which satisfy

i=\kappa(\kappa-1)/2\quad\text{ and }\quad j=\kappa(\kappa+1)/2

for any
0\leq\kappa\leq\min(k,n-k)
. A study of
3
-core partitions is undertaken in
Han11
, which is summarized in Section
5
. A full classification for
k=2
can be found in
Pet93
.

We also note that Ayyer and Sinha recently studied
t
-core,
(k,n-k)
-bounded partitions in
Ayyer_2024
; however, they do not investigate the size of the partition nor its
t
-interior.

3.
Nakano Vanishing

The Nakano vanishing theorem (see
Nak54
) asserts that for a compact complex manifold
X
of dimension
2n
with holonomy contained in
U(n)
and an ample line bundle
L
,

H^{i}(X,\Omega_{X}^{j}\otimes L)=0\quad\text{ whenever }\quad i+j>\dim X.

In the case
X=\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
, this gives
H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}(t))=0
for
t>0
and
i+j>N
, which is Snow’s inequality
(S2)
. Using our translation to partitions, we give a self-contained combinatorial proof of Nakano vanishing for
\Omega^{j}(t)
in Theorem
3.1
. We then classify the
(k,n,i,j,t)
tuples which have knijt-partitions in the extremal cases
i+j=N
and
i+j=N-1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Every knijt-partition with
t>0
satisfies
i+j\leq N
.

Proof.

Fix a knijt-partition
\lambda
. We rearrange
i+j\leq N
as
i\leq N-j
, which states that the size of
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)
is at most the size of
\lambda^{\mathrm{C}}
. We prove this by constructing an injection from the boxes of
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)
to the boxes of
\lambda^{\mathrm{C}}
.

Coarsen
\lambda
using the notation from Section
2.1
, so
r_{p}
and
s_{q}
for
1\leq p,q\leq\ell
are the height and width of block
B_{p,q}
. Set
\lambda^{\prime}:=\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)
and define
r_{p}^{\prime}
and
s_{q}^{\prime}
for
1\leq p,q\leq\ell^{\prime}
similarly, but for the blocks
B_{p,q}^{\prime}
of
\lambda^{\prime}
. Since every block in
\lambda^{\prime}
is the union of a contiguous rectangle of blocks in
\lambda
, we can find sequences

0=\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{\ell^{\prime}}\leq\ell\text{ and }0=\beta_{0}<\beta_{1}<\cdots<\beta_{\ell^{\prime}}\leq\ell

such that
r_{p}^{\prime}=r_{\alpha_{p-1}+1}+\cdots+r_{\alpha_{p}}
and
s_{q}^{\prime}=s_{\beta_{q-1}+1}+\cdots+s_{\beta_{q}}
for every
1\leq p,q\leq\ell^{\prime}
. An example is given in Figure
3
.

Since
B_{\alpha_{p},\beta_{q}}\subseteq B_{p,q}^{\prime}
is in the
t
-interior, its smallest hook length is greater than
t
, and hence

r_{\alpha_{p}+1}+\cdots+r_{\ell-\beta_{q}+1}+s_{\beta_{q}+1}+\cdots+s_{\ell-\alpha_{p}+1}+1>t.

When
q=\ell^{\prime}-p+1
, the block
B_{p,q}^{\prime}
is a diagonal block of
\lambda^{\prime}
. Hence, the block
B_{\alpha_{p-1}+1,\beta_{q}+1}
to the right of
B_{p,q}^{\prime}
is an internal or diagonal block of
\lambda
but which is not in the
t
-interior, so its largest hooklength is less than
t
, which gives

r_{\alpha_{p-1}+1}+\cdots+r_{\ell-(\beta_{q}+1)+1}+s_{\beta_{q}+1}+\cdots+s_{\ell-(\alpha_{p-1}+1)+1}-1<t.

Since all terms are integers, there must be a gap of at least two between the left-hand sides of the previous two inequalities, so after canceling common terms we obtain

(2)

r_{p}^{\prime}\leq r_{p}^{\prime}+s_{\ell-\alpha_{p}+2}+\cdots+s_{\ell-\alpha_{p-1}}\leq r_{\ell-\beta_{q}+1}.

By a symmetric argument, when
p=\ell^{\prime}-q+1
we obtain

s_{q}^{\prime}\leq s_{q}^{\prime}+r_{\ell-\beta_{q}+2}+\cdots+r_{\ell-\beta_{q-1}}\leq s_{\ell-\alpha_{p}+1}.

These observations allow us to define an injection
\tau
between the blocks of
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)
and the external blocks of
\lambda
by

\tau(B_{p,q}^{\prime})=B_{\ell-\beta_{\ell^{\prime}-p+1}+1,\ell-\alpha_{\ell^{\prime}-q+1}+1}.

The inequalities mean that each block
B_{p,q}^{\prime}
of
\lambda^{\prime}
is at most the size of
\tau(B_{p,q}^{\prime})
, so
\tau
can be lifted to an injection between boxes.
∎

5.
Results for
t=3

Let
c_{t}(n)
denote the number of
t
-core partitions of size
n
. It is a classical result (see e.g. 2.1 in
Cho21
) that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{t}(n)x^{n}=\dfrac{(x^{t};x^{t})^{t}_{\infty}}{(x;x)_{\infty}}

and in particular,

\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{3}(n)x^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(1-x^{3n})^{3}}{1-x^{n}}.

Theorem 1.1 of
Han11
shows that
c_{3}(n)=0
precisely for non-negative
n
such that
\text{ord}_{p}(3n+1)
is odd for some prime
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. The vanishing of
c_{3}(j)
implies corresponding vanishings for
H^{\bullet}(X,\Omega^{j}(3))
, which we extend here.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and let
b=i+j\pmod{2}
, so
b\in\{0,1\}
. Then

H^{i}(X,\Omega_{X}^{j}(3))\neq 0

if and only if all of the following criteria are satisfied:

(1)

\Delta:=8(j+i)-3(j-i-b)^{2}-8b(j-i)
is a perfect square,

(2)

4\mid\sqrt{\Delta}+j-i-b
,

(3)

j-i-b\geq\sqrt{\Delta}
,

(4)

k,n
are large enough.

Explicitly, criterion (
4
) means that if

x=\dfrac{j-i-b\pm\sqrt{\Delta}}{4}\text{ and }y=\dfrac{j-i-b\mp\sqrt{\Delta}}{4},

then

0\leq x+b+2y\leq n-k\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0\leq 2x+b+y\leq k.

Proof.

Recall the bijection (
1
) between
t
-core and
(t-1)
-bounded partitions. Suppose
\lambda
is a
3
-core partition of
j
. Then its 3-boundary
\partial_{\lambda}(3)
has the form
(2^{x},1^{y})
for some
x,y\geq 0
. Taking the
2
-skew of
\partial_{\lambda}(3)
(see Theorem 5 in
Rob98
for a readable definition and example), the sequence of row-differences
(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i})_{i}
is of the form
(2^{x},1^{b},(01)^{y})
where
b=x+y\pmod{2}
. Here
(01)^{y}
denotes the sequence given by concatenating
y
copies of
(0,1)
. For example,
(01)^{3}
denotes
(0,1,0,1,0,1)
. The total size of
\lambda

j=x^{2}+xy+y^{2}+x+y+b(x+y+1).

while the

\varepsilon_{\lambda}(3)=(2^{\max(x-1,0)},1^{\min(1,x,y)},(01)^{\max(y-1,0)}),

so that

Setting

x=\dfrac{j-i-b\pm\sqrt{\Delta}}{4}\text{ and }y=\dfrac{j-i-b\mp\sqrt{\Delta}}{4}.

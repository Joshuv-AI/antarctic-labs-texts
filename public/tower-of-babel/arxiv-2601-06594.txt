Radial measures of pseudo-cones
Rolf Schneider

Abstract
We consider C C -pseudo-cones, that is, closed convex sets K ⊂ ℝ n K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} with o ∉ K ⊂ C o\notin K\subset C , for which C C is the recession cone. Here C C is a given closed convex cone in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} , pointed and with nonempty interior. We define a class of measures for such pseudo-cones and show how they can be interpreted as derivative measures. For a subclass of these measures, namely for dual curvature measures with negative indices, we solve a Minkowski type existence problem. Keywords: pseudo-cone, radial measure, derivative measure, dual curvature measure, Minkowski type theorem 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 52A20

Introduction

The Minkowski problem for convex bodies has in recent years been the template for several generalizations and analogues. Let us first recall the classical issue. For a convex body
K
(a compact convex set with interior points) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the surface area measure
S_{K}
is defined as the push-forward of the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
, restricted to the boundary
{\rm bd}\,K
of
K
, under the (
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost everywhere on
{\rm bd}\,K
defined) Gauss map of
K
. This can be written as

S_{K}=\nu_{K}{{}_{\sharp}}(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,\rule{0.28453pt}{5.69046pt}\rule{5.69046pt}{0.28453pt}\,{{\rm bd}\,K}),

(1)

where the Gauss map
\nu_{K}
of
K
is defined by letting
\nu_{K}(x)
be the outer unit normal vector of
K
at
x\in{\rm bd}\,K
if it is uniquely defined. Thus,
S_{K}
is a finite Borel measure on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. Equivalently, it can be defined as a derivative measure. If
V_{n}
denotes the volume in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
L
is an arbitrary convex body with support function
h_{L}
, then

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}V_{n}(K+tL)\Big|_{t=0^{+}}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}h_{L}(u)\,S_{K}({\rm d}u),

(2)

where
K+tL
denotes the Minkowski sum of
K
and
tL
. The Minkowski problem now asks: What are the necessary and sufficient conditions on a measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
in order that there is a convex body
K
with
S_{K}=\mu
? And if it exists, in how far is it unique? In this classical case, the answers are well known (see, e.g.,
[
10
, Chap. 8]
).

We note that in (
2
) the derivative is one-sided, since the definition of the Minkowski addition requires that
t\geq 0
. This can be remedied by the introduction of the Wulff shape
[h]
of a positive function
h
on the unit sphere and a corresponding lemma of Aleksandrov; we refer to
[
10
, Sect. 7.5]
. We then have, if
o\in{\rm int}\,K
,

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}V_{n}([h_{K}+th_{L}])\Big|_{t=0}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}h_{L}(u)\,S_{K}({\rm d}u).

(3)

Equation (
1
) is of the type

\mu=\varphi_{K}{{}_{\sharp}}m_{K},

(4)

where
m_{k}
is some standard measure, possibly with a density, defined by
K
, and
\varphi_{K}
is some geometric measurable mapping, also defined by
K
, from the domain of
m_{K}
to the unit sphere. The equation (
3
) can be written in the form

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Phi(K_{t})\Big|_{t=0}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}g(u)\,\mu({\rm d}u),

(5)

where
\Phi
is a functional on convex bodies and
K_{t}
is a perturbation of
K
involving a bounded function
g
. In the case considered above,
\Phi
is the volume,
g=h_{L}
, and
K_{t}
is the Wulff shape of
h_{K}+th_{L}
(for sufficiently small
|t|
).

The literature knows several examples of measures
\mu
defined by convex bodies that arise in the form (
4
) and/or (
5
). Besides the surface area measure, we mention only the cone-volume measure, the dual curvature measures, the surface area measures in the
L_{p}
or more generally in the Orlicz Brunn–Minkowski theory, and the chord measures. For more information, in particular about the corresponding Minkowski problems, we refer to the recent survey article by Huang, Yang and Zhang
[
6
]
.

The last decades have seen a very successful generalization of various results on convex bodies to results on convex functions or log-concave functions. For a survey, we refer to
[
1
, Chap. 9]
. A function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is log-concave if
f=e^{-\varphi}
with a convex function
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
. If
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a convex body and

I_{K}^{\infty}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if }x\in K,\\
\infty&\mbox{if }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus K,\end{array}\right.\qquad{\mathbbm{1}}_{K}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }x\in K,\\
0&\mbox{if }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus K\end{array}\right.

defines its indicator function
I_{K}^{\infty}
and characteristic function
{\mathbbm{1}}_{K}
, then
I_{K}^{\infty}
is convex and
{\mathbbm{1}}_{K}
is log-concave. Moreover,

I_{K}^{\infty}=h_{K}^{*}
, where
h_{K}
denotes the support function of
K
and

f^{*}
is the Legendre transform of
f
. As

(I_{K}^{\infty})^{*}+(I_{L}^{\infty})^{*}=h_{K}^{**}+h_{L}^{**}=h_{K}+h_{L}=h_{K+L}=h_{K+L}^{**}=(I^{\infty}_{K+L})^{*}

and the Minkowski sum is a central notion in the theory of convex bodies, this indicates already why the Legendre transform plays an essential role when results on convex bodies are carried over to convex functions.

For a log-concave function
f=e^{-\varphi}
, it is natural to consider the measure
\nu_{f}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
which has density
e^{-\varphi}
with respect to Lebesgue measure, and to view its total measure

J(f)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{-\varphi(x)}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x

as the ‘total mass’ of
f
, assuming that it is finite. We also consider its image measure

S_{f}:=(\nabla\varphi)_{\sharp}\nu_{f},

where
\nabla\varphi
is the (almost everywhere on
{\rm dom}\,\varphi
defined) gradient of the convex function
\varphi
. Defining

f_{t}:=e^{-(\varphi^{*}+tg)^{*}}

(so that
f_{0}=f
) with a function
g
, we have in analogy to results on convex bodies, under suitable assumptions, that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}J(f_{t})\Big|_{t=0}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}g\,{\rm d}S_{f}.

(6)

We refer to Proposition 5.4 in Rotem
[
8
]
, where the assumptions are that
0<\int f<\infty
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous and bounded. There are several variants with different assumptions; see Colesanti and Fragalà
[
2
]
, Cordero–Erausquin and Klartag
[
3
]
, Ulivelli
[
19
]
. In
[
3
, Def. 1]
, the measure
S_{f}
is called a moment measure, a terminology which can only be understood from the references in
[
3
]
. Rotem
[
8
]
calls this measure the surface area measure of the log-concave function
f
.

We mention that Santambrogio
[
9
]
has reproved results of
[
3
]
by measure transport methods. For a result similar to (
6
) we also refer to the case
q=n
of Lemma 5.2 in
[
4
]
.

2
An analogue for pseudo-cones

The purpose of the following is to find measures analogous to
S_{f}
for pseudo-cones instead of convex functions. We need a few explanations before we can formulate our problem. We work in Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
), with scalar product
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
, origin
o
, and unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. A closed convex cone
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is given, with nonempty interior and not containing a line. Its dual cone
C^{\circ}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 0\;\forall y\in C\}
then has the same properties. We write
\Omega_{C}:={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\rm int}\,C
and
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}:={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
. We denote by
{\rm int}
the interior and by
{\rm cl}
the closure, in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
as well as in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

A
C
-pseudo-cone is a closed convex set
K\subset C
with
o\notin K
for which
C
is the recession cone (that is,
C={\rm rec}\,K:=\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:K+z\subseteq K\}
). This implies that
K+C=K
. The set of all
C
-pseudo-cones is denoted by
ps(C)
. This set can be considered, in several respects, as a counterpart to the set of convex bodies containing
o
in the interior. For example, surface area measures and their analogues and generalizations can be defined and corresponding Minkowski type problems can be investigated, the copolarity of pseudo-cones has many properties similar to those of the polarity of convex bodies, and the Gauss image problem can be treated for pseudo-cones (see, e.g.,
[
11
]
–
[
18
]
). The gist of the following is that
C
-pseudo-cones, or rather their radial functions, can also be considered as a counterpart to the set of convex functions with bounded domain.

We need a few more notions to make this precise. Let
K\in ps(C)
. The support function of
K
is defined by

h_{K}(x):=\sup\{\langle x,y\rangle:y\in K\}\quad\mbox{for }x\in C^{\circ}.

The supremum is a maximum if
u\in{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
. Since
h_{K}\leq 0
, we write
\overline{h}_{K}:=-h_{K}
.

The
radial function
of
K
is defined by

\rho_{K}(v):=\min\{r>0:rv\in K\}\quad\mbox{for }v\in\Omega_{C}.

Note that the minimum exists, since
C+x\subseteq K
for any
x\in K
and hence
\{r>0:rv\in K\}\not=\emptyset
for
v\in\Omega_{C}
, and
K
is closed.

Let
\eta_{K}
be the set of all
v\in\Omega_{C}
for which the outer unit normal vector of
K
at the boundary point
\rho_{K}(v)v
is not unique. Then, as is well known,
\eta_{K}
has spherical Lebesgue measure zero. The
radial Gauss map

\alpha_{K}:\Omega_{C}\setminus\eta_{K}\to{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is defined by letting
\alpha_{K}(v)
be the unique outer unit normal vector of
K
at
\rho_{K}(v)v
, for
v\in\Omega_{C}\setminus\eta_{K}
.

The setting described in Section
1
is briefly the following. We choose the function
F(x)=e^{-x}
; then for each convex function
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the total mass of
f:=F\circ\varphi
is defined by
J(f):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x
. Let
\nu_{f}
be the measure with density
F\circ\varphi
with respect to Lebesgue measure, let
S_{f}:=(\nabla\varphi)_{\sharp}\nu_{f}
be the push-forward of
\nu_{f}
under the gradient map of
\varphi
. If
f_{t}
denotes a suitable perturbation of
f
, so that
f_{0}=f
, involving a function
g
, then, under suitable assumptions,
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}J(f_{t})\big|_{t=0}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}g\,{\rm d}S_{f}
.

We want to imitate this for pseudo-cones, replacing the convex function
\varphi
by the radial function of a pseudo-cone and the function
x\mapsto e^{-x}
by a rather general function. For this, we take a continuously differentiable, strictly decreasing function
G:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and define
F(x):=-xG^{\prime}(x)
for
x>0
. We
assume that for every
a>0
, the function
F|_{[a,\infty)}
is bounded. Typical examples are
G(x)=e^{-x}
and
F(x)=xe^{-x}
, or
G(x)=x^{q}
with
q<0
and
F(x)=|q|x^{q}
. For
K\in ps(C)
, let
\nu_{F,K}
be the measure on
\Omega_{C}
with density
F\circ\rho_{K}
with respect to spherical Lebesgue measure. This measure is finite, since
\rho_{K}
is bounded away from zero, so that the function
F\circ\rho_{K}
is bounded. Instead of the gradient map of a convex function, we now employ the radial Gauss map of
K
. Thus we denote by

\mu_{F,K}:=\alpha_{K}{{}_{\sharp}}\nu_{F,K}

the finite measure on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
which is the push-forward of
\nu_{F,K}
under
\alpha_{K}
. We call this measure the
F
-radial measure
of
K
.

The following should be observed. If
z\in C\setminus\{o\}
, then
K:=C+z\in ps(C)
. In this case,
\alpha_{K}
maps almost all of
\Omega_{C}
to the boundary of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, so that
\mu_{F,K}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})=0
. This shows that for radial measures of
C
-pseudo-cones, the natural domain is
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, and not
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
.

Our first aim is to show that the measure
\mu_{F,K}
can also be interpreted as a derivative measure, in analogy to (
5
) and (
6
). For this, we define the functional
J_{G}
by

J_{G}(K):=\int_{\Omega_{C}}(G\circ\rho_{K})(v)\,{\rm d}v\quad\mbox{for }K\in ps(C),

where
{\rm d}v
indicates integration with respect to the spherical Lebesgue measure. By our assumptions on
G
, the functional
J_{G}
is finite.

The Wulff shape appearing below will be defined in Section
3
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
F
and
G
be as defined above. Let
K\in ps(C)
. Let
g:{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous. Let
K_{t}
be the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\overline{h}_{K}e^{tg})
for
|t|\leq 1
(say). Then

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}J_{G}(K_{t})\Big|_{t=0}=-\int_{{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g\,{\rm d}\mu_{F,K}.

We prove this in Section
3
, after some preliminaries.

Our second aim would be to solve a Minkowski type existence problem for the measure
\mu_{F,K}
: what are necessary and sufficient conditions on a Borel measure
\mu
on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
in order that there is a pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
for which
\mu=\mu_{F,K}
? We give only an answer for the special functions
G(x)=x^{q}
,
F(x)=|q|x^{q}
with
q<0
. In this case, we have

\mu_{F,K}=|q|n\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot),\qquad J_{G}(K)=n\widetilde{V}_{q}(K).

(7)

Here
\widetilde{V}_{q}(K)
is the
q
th dual volume of
K
and
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)
denotes the
q
th dual curvature measure of
K
(see, e.g.,
[
7
]
, formula (4.9) and Def. 4.3). In this way, we give a solution to a problem left open in
[
7
]
, as explained in Section
4
.

Proof of Theorem
1

We begin with some preliminaries. Let
K\in ps(C)
. We have already defined its support function on
C^{\circ}
. Being a convex function, it is continuous on the interior of its domain and hence on
{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
. We show that
h_{K}
is continuous on
C^{\circ}
. Let
x_{j}\in C^{\circ}
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and suppose that
x_{j}\to x_{0}
as
j\to\infty
. Since
h_{K}
is positively homogeneous of degree one, we can assume that
x_{0}\not=o
. The bounded sequence
(h_{K}(x_{j}))_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a convergent subsequence. If there were convergent subsequences with different limits, then
K
would have two distinct supporting hyperplanes (in the sense of
[
10
, p. 45]
) with normal vector
x_{0}
, a contradiction. It follows that
h_{K}(x_{j})\to h_{K}(x_{0})
, which gives the assertion. In the following, we need only
\overline{h}_{K}
on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
.

Let
K\in ps(C)
and
v\in\Omega_{C}
. Then
\rho_{K}(v)v
is in the boundary of
K
. Let
u_{*}
be an outer unit normal vector of
K
at this point. Then

\overline{h}_{K}(u_{*})=|\langle\rho_{K}(v)v,u_{*}\rangle|

(8)

and

\overline{h}_{K}(u)\leq|\langle\rho_{K}(v)v,u\rangle|\quad\mbox{for all }u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}.

(9)

First we modify the definition of the Wulff shape. Let
f:{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous function which is not identically zero. We define

[f]:=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|\langle y,u\rangle|\geq f(u)\}

and call this the
Wulff shape
associated with
(C,f)
. It is nonempty, since
f
is bounded, and it does not contain
o
since
f
is positive somewhere on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Being an intersection of
C
-pseudo-cones, it is clearly a
C
-pseudo-cone. It follows from the definition that

[\overline{h}_{K}]=K\quad\mbox{for }K\in ps(C)

(10)

and

f(u)\leq\overline{h}_{[f]}(u)\quad\mbox{for }u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}.

(11)

Let
v\in\Omega_{C}
. Then
\rho_{[f]}(v)v
is a boundary point of
[f]
. By (
9
) and (
11
) we get

f(u)\leq|\langle\rho_{[f]}(v)v,u\rangle|\quad\mbox{for all }u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}.

(12)

Let
u_{*}
be an outer unit normal vector of
[f]
at
\rho_{[f]}(v)v
. By (
8
) we have
\overline{h}_{[f]}(u_{*})=|\langle\rho_{[f]}(v)v,u_{*}\rangle|
. By (
11
),
\overline{h}_{[f]}(u_{*})=f(u_{*})+\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon\geq 0
. This implies that

[f]\subset\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|\langle y,u_{*}\rangle|\geq f(u_{*})+\varepsilon\},

so that from
\varepsilon>0
it would follow that
\rho_{K}(v)v\notin[f]
, a contradiction. From
\varepsilon=0
we see that

f(u_{*})=|\langle\rho_{[f]}(v)v,u_{*}\rangle|.

(13)

We can write (
12
) and (
13
) also in the form

\rho_{[f]}(v)\geq\frac{f(u)}{|\langle v,u\rangle|}\quad\mbox{for }u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}

(14)

and

\rho_{[f]}(v)=\frac{f(u_{*})}{|\langle v,u_{*}\rangle|}.

(15)

(Note that
\langle v,u\rangle\not=0
for
v\in\Omega_{C}
and
u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
.) Thus, the radial function of the Wulff shape is given by

\rho_{[f]}(v)=\max_{u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\frac{f(u)}{|\langle v,u\rangle|}

for
v\in\Omega_{C}
, and the maximum is attained at
u_{*}
if and only if
u_{*}
is an outer unit normal vector of
[f]
at
\rho_{[f]}(v)v
.

We need a different form of Lemma 9 in
[
14
]
. More precisely, in
[
14
]
the lemma involved a compact subset
\omega
of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, because it was needed in that form. Now this set is replaced by
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
(thus, the assumption that
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
is no longer necessary). Since there are other changes, too, and parts of the proof are needed later, we give the full proof. We emphasize, however, as in
[

]

[

Lemma 1
.

Let
K\in ps(C)
. Let
g:{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous. Define the function
h_{t}
(for
|t|\leq 1
, say) by

h_{t}(u):=\overline{h}_{K}(u)e^{tg(u)}\quad\mbox{for }u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}},

and let
[h_{t}]
be the Wulff shape associated with

. Then, for

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\log\rho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\log\rho_{K}(v)}{t}=g(\alpha_{K}(v)).

Let
v\in\Omega_{C}\setminus\eta_{K}

u_{t}

at its boundary point

(16)

Since

Since

), this means that

Using (

\displaystyle\log\rho_{[h_{t}]}(v)-\log\rho_{K}(v)=\log h_{t}(u_{t})-\log|\langle v,u_{t}\rangle|-\log\rho_{K}(v)

so that (

We can now prove Theorem
1
.

Proof.

For a positive differentiable function
\psi
we have
\psi^{\prime}=\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{\psi}\psi=(\log\psi)^{\prime}\psi
. Using this, we get under the assumptions of Theorem
1
that, for
v\in\Omega_{C}\setminus\eta_{K}
,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}(G\circ\rho_{K_{t}})(v)\Big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\rho_{K}(v))\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\rho_{K_{t}}(v)\Big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\rho_{K}(v))\rho_{K}(v)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\log\rho_{K_{t}}(v)\Big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(F\circ\rho_{K})(v)g(\alpha_{K}(v)),

where
G^{\prime}(x)x=-F(x)
and Lemma
1
were applied.

By (
18
) and the boundedness of
g
, there are constants
\delta>0
and
b>0
such that

|\log\rho_{K_{t}}(v)-\log\rho_{K}(v)|\leq b|t|

for
|t|<\delta
and almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
. By the continuity of the radial function, this holds for all
v\in\Omega_{C}
. We can assume that
\delta
and the constant
a>0
are chosen so that
\rho_{K_{t}}\geq a
for
|t|<\delta
. The function
\Psi
defined by
\Psi(x)=G(e^{x})
for
x\geq\log a
is positive and has a negative derivative, hence it has a Lipschitz constant
L
. It follows that, for
v\in\Omega_{C}
,

\displaystyle|(G\circ\rho_{K_{t}})(v)-(G\circ\rho_{K})(v)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\Psi(\log\rho_{K_{t}}(v))-\Psi(\log\rho_{K}(v))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L|\log\rho_{K_{t}}(v)-\log\rho_{K}(v)|\leq Lb|t|.

Now we can apply the dominated convergence theorem and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}J_{G}(K_{t})\Big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{C}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}(G\circ\rho_{[K_{t}]})(v)\Big|_{t=0}\,{\rm d}v=-\int_{\Omega_{C}}g(\alpha_{K}(v))(F\circ\rho_{K})(v)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{C}}g(\alpha_{K}(v))\,\nu_{F,K}({\rm d}v)=-\int_{{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g\,{\rm d}\mu_{F,K},

where the general transformation theorem for integrals (or change of variable formula) was used. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

4
The dual Minkowski problem for negative exponents

We now choose
G(x)=x^{q}
and
F(x)=|q|x^{q}
with
q<0
. According to (
7
), for
K\in ps(C)
this gives

\frac{1}{|q|n}\mu_{F,K}(\omega)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}\rho_{K}(v)^{q}\,{\rm d}v=\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\omega)

(19)

for Borel sets
\omega\subset{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and

\frac{1}{n}J_{G}(K)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega_{C}}\rho_{K}(v)^{q}\,{\rm d}v=\widetilde{V}_{q}(K).

(20)

The measure
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)
is known as the
q
th dual curvature measure of
K
(note that
\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)={\bm{\alpha}}_{K}^{*}(\omega)\setminus\eta_{K}
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\eta_{K})=0
;
{\bm{\alpha}}_{K}^{*}
is defined in
[
7
, (4.6)]
). Originally, these measures were introduced for convex bodies, see
[
5
]
. Whereas a ‘duality’ or analogy to the classical curvature measures can only be observed for integers
q\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, the measures
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)
can be defined for all
q\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For pseudo-cones, these measures were introduced in
[
7
]
.

It should be observed that
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)
for
K\in ps(C)
is a measure on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and not necessarily on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. For example, if
K=C+z
with
z\in{\rm int}\,C
, then
K\in ps(C)
and
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)
is concentrated on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. For this reason, in the formula

\int_{{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g(u)\,\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,{\rm d}u)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega_{C}}g(\alpha_{K}(v))\rho_{K}(v)^{q}\,{\rm d}v

for bounded measurable functions
g:{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, which follows from the change of variable formula, it is essential that the left-hand integral extends over the closure of
\Omega_{C\circ}
and not only over
\Omega_{C\circ}
(otherwise, the left integral could be zero, whereas the right integral is not).

In Li, Ye and Zhu
[
7
]
, the following is proved. If
\varphi
is a nonzero finite Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
whose support is a compact subset of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and if
q\not=0
, then there exists
K\in ps(C)
with
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)=\varphi
(Theorem 6.1). If
\varphi
is a nonzero finite Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and if
q>0
, then there exists
K\in ps(C)
with
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)=\varphi
(Theorem 7.1). Thus, the authors solve the Minkowski existence problem for the
q
th dual curvature measure of pseudo-cones if either
q\not=0
and the given measure has compact support, or if
q>0
, but only for finite measures (which is a severe restriction if
q>0
). They express (on page 2006) the opinion that the solvability for
q<0
, when
\varphi
does not concentrate on a compact subset of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, ‘seems to be much more intractable’. However, we prove the following.

Theorem 2
.

Let
q<0
. If
\varphi
is a nonzero finite Borel measure on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, then there exists a pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
with
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)=\varphi
.

The proof is prepared by the following lemma. For
K\in ps(C)
, we denote by
b(K)
the distance of
K
from the origin.

Lemma 2
.

Let
q<0
. There are positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
, depending only on
C,q,n
, with the following property. If
K\in ps(C)
and
\widetilde{V}_{q}(K)=1
, then
c_{1}\leq b(K)\leq c_{2}
.

Proof.

We choose a number
a
with

\frac{1}{n}a^{q}\sigma_{n-1}(\Omega_{C})>1,

(21)

where
\sigma_{n-1}
denotes the spherical Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. For
x\in C
and
\varepsilon>0
, let

A(x):=\{v\in\Omega_{C}:\rho_{C+x}(v)\leq a\}

(which may be empty if
|x|
is too large). If
K\in ps(C)
and
x\in C\setminus\{o\}
, we have
C+x\subseteq K
, thus

\rho_{K}(v)\leq\rho_{C+x}(v)\leq a\quad\mbox{for }v\in A(x)

and hence (since
q<0
)

\widetilde{V}_{q}(K)\geq\frac{1}{n}\int_{A(x)}\rho_{K}(v)^{q}\,{\rm d}v\geq\frac{1}{n}a^{q}\sigma_{n-1}(A(x)).

Let
K\in ps(C)
be a pseudo-cone with
b(K)=\varepsilon
. Then there is a point
x_{\varepsilon}\in K
with
|x_{\varepsilon}|=\varepsilon
. Since
\sigma_{n-1}(A(x))\to\sigma_{n-1}(\Omega_{C})
as
x\to o
, we get

\widetilde{V}_{q}(K)\geq\frac{1}{n}a^{q}\sigma_{n-1}(A(x_{\varepsilon}))\to\frac{1}{n}a^{q}\sigma_{n-1}(\Omega_{C})\quad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

This contradicts (
21
), if there are
C
-pseudo-cones
K
satisfying
\widetilde{V}_{q}(K)=1
with arbitrarily small distance from the origin. The existence of the constant
c_{1}
follows.

To prove the existence of
c_{2}
, let
K\in ps(C)
and
\widetilde{V}_{q}(K)=1
. Define

r_{0}:=\max\{r>0:rB^{n}\cap{\rm int}\,K=\emptyset\},

where
B^{n}
denotes the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, centered at
o
. Then
\rho_{K}(v)\geq r_{0}
for all
v\in\Omega_{C}
and hence

1=\widetilde{V}_{q}(K)\leq\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega_{C}}r_{0}^{q}\,{\rm d}v=\frac{1}{n}r_{0}^{q}\sigma_{n-1}(\Omega_{C}),

thus
r_{0}\leq c_{2}
with a constant
c_{2}
depending only on
C,q,n
. By the definition of
r_{0}
, there is a vector
x\in K
with
|x|=r_{0}\leq c_{2}
. This implies
b(K)\leq c_{2}
.
∎

Now we can prove Theorem
2
.

By
C^{\geq}({\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
we denote the space of nonnegative, continuous functions on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
which are not identically zero. For
f\in C^{\geq}({\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
, the Wulff shape
[f]
is a well-defined
C
-pseudo-cone.

Let
\varphi
be a nonzero, finite Borel measure on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Its total measure is denoted by
|\varphi|
. Let
q<0
.

For
f\in C^{\geq}({\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
we define (as in
[
7
]
, but with modifications)

\Phi(f):=-\frac{1}{|\varphi|}\int_{{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\log f(u)\,\varphi({\rm d}u)+\frac{1}{q}\log\widetilde{V}_{q}([f]).

We may have
\Phi(f)=\infty
now, since
f
is allowed to be zero, but
\Phi(f)
is finite for some
f
, in particular for
\overline{h}_{L}
for suitable
L\in ps(C)
. It is easily checked that
\Phi(\lambda f)=\Phi(f)
for
\lambda>0
.

We can argue similarly as in
[
11
, Sect. 9]
. We state first that
\Phi
attains a minimum on the set

{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}:=\{\overline{h}_{L}:L\in ps(C),\,\widetilde{V}_{q}(L)=1\}.

For the proof, choose
L\in ps(C)
with
\widetilde{V}_{q}(L)=1
. By the proof of Lemma
2
, there is a constant
c
, independent of
L
, such that
\overline{h}_{L}\leq c
. It follows that
\Phi(\overline{h}_{L})\geq-\log c
and hence
\inf\{\Phi(f):f\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\}>-\infty
. Also, there are
L\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
with
\Phi(\overline{h}_{L})<\infty
.

Let
(K_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence with
\overline{h}_{K_{i}}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\Phi(\overline{h}_{K_{i}})=\inf\{\Phi(f):f\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\}.

By Lemma 1 in
[
13
]
and Lemma
2
, the sequence
(K_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a subsequence converging to a pseudo-cone
K_{0}\in ps(C)
. The corresponding support functions converge pointwise. By Lemma
2
, they are uniformly bounded. Using the dominated convergence theorem, we see that the minimum of
\Phi
on
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
is attained at
\overline{h}_{K_{0}}
. Modifying the notation from
[
7
, Sect. 6]
, we write

\displaystyle\beta_{\rm fct}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf\{\Phi(f):f\in C^{\geq}({\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}})\},

\displaystyle\beta_{\rm pc}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf\{\Phi(\overline{h}_{K}):K\in ps(C)\}.

Since
\Phi
is homogeneous of degree zero and
\widetilde{V}_{q}
is homogeneous of degree
q\not=0
, we have shown that
\beta_{\rm pc}=\Phi(\overline{h}_{K_{0}})
. Trivially,
\beta_{\rm pc}\geq\beta_{\rm fct}
. By (
11
) we have
f(u)\leq\overline{h}_{[f]}(u)
for
u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and hence (observing that
[\overline{h}_{[f]}]=[f]
by (
10
))
\Phi(f)\geq\Phi(\overline{h}_{[f]})
, thus
\beta_{\rm fct}\geq\beta_{\rm pc}
. We have obtained that
\beta_{\rm fct}=\Phi(\overline{h}_{K_{0}})
.

Now let
g
be a continuous function on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and let
K_{t}=[h_{t}]
be the Wulff shape associated with
(C,h_{t})
(for sufficiently small
|t|>0
), where
h_{t}=\overline{h}_{K_{0}}e^{tg}
. Then

\Phi(h_{t})=-\frac{1}{|\varphi|}\int_{{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\log h_{t}(u)\,\varphi({\rm d}u)+\frac{1}{q}\log\widetilde{V}_{q}([h_{t}]).

By Theorem
1
, together with (
19
) and (
20
), we have (since
q<0
)

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\widetilde{V}_{q}([h_{t}])\Big|_{t=0}=q\int_{{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g(u)\,\widetilde{C}_{q}(K_{0},{\rm d}u).

We know that
\beta_{\rm fct}=\Phi(\overline{h}_{K_{0}})
, the function
t\mapsto\Phi(h_{t})
is finite and differentiable at
0
, hence
\frac{{\rm d}\Phi(h_{t})}{{\rm d}t}\big|_{t=0}=0
, and
[h_{0}]=[\overline{h}_{K_{0}}]=K_{0}
by (
10
). Therefore,

0=-\frac{1}{|\varphi|}\int_{{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g(u)\,\varphi({\rm d}u)+\frac{1}{\widetilde{V}_{q}(K_{0})}\int_{{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g(u)\,\widetilde{C}_{q}(K_{0},{\rm d}u).

Since this holds for all continuous functions
g
on
{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, we deduce that

\varphi=\frac{|\varphi|}{\widetilde{V}_{q}(K_{0})}\widetilde{C}_{q}(K_{0},\cdot).

Since
\widetilde{C}_{q}(\lambda K_{0},\cdot)=\lambda^{q}\widetilde{C}_{q}(K_{0},\cdot)
for
\lambda>0
, we get
\varphi=\widetilde{C}_{q}(\lambda K_{0},\cdot)
for suitable
\lambda>0
, which completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

The existence result proved by Theorem
2
raises a uniqueness question, but this remains open. For convex bodies, a uniqueness proof was provided by Zhao
[
20
]
, but it is at the moment not clear how it could be carried over to pseudo-cones.

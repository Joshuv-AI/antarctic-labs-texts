Global solutions to the stochastic heat equation with superlinear accretive reaction term and polynomially growing multiplicative noise.
M. Salins

Abstract
We prove that mild solutions to the stochastic heat equation with superlinear accretive forcing and polynomially growing multiplicative noise cannot explode under two sets of assumptions. The first set of assumptions allows both the deterministic forcing and multiplicative noise terms to grow polynomially, as long as the multiplicative noise is sufficiently larger. The second set of assumptions imposes an Osgood condition on the deterministic forcing and allows the multiplicative noise to grow polynomially. In both cases, the multiplicative noise cannot grow faster than u 3 2 u^{\frac{3}{2}} , as this would cause explosion.

Introduction

We prove that the nonlinear stochastic heat equation

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)+b(u(t,x))+\sigma(u(t,x))\dot{W}(t,x)

has a global solution under certain assumptions when
b
and

The spatial domain is
D:=[-\pi,\pi]\subset\mathbb{R}
with periodic boundary conditions imposed.

Foondun and Nualart, extending a result by Bonder and Groisman

First we prove some preliminary results.
Let
h

Lemma 4.2
.

Assumption

(a) implies that both
f(u)=uh(u)
and

are convex.

Proof.

The convexity of
f
is straightforward because
f^{\prime\prime}(u)=2h^{\prime}(u)+uh^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq 0
because

To see the convexity of

This expression is nonnegative by assumption (

g
is convex.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The functions
f
and
g
have the property that
f^{-1}(u)g^{-1}(u)=u
for all
u>h(0)

Proof.

By the definitions,

h^{-1}(u)=\frac{g(u)}{u}
. Therefore,

f\left(\frac{g(u)}{u}\right)=\left(\frac{g(u)}{u}\right)u=g(u).

Therefore,

\frac{g(u)}{u}=f^{-1}(g(u)).

Substituting
v=g(u)
, this proves

v=f^{-1}(v)g^{-1}(v).

∎

The next lemma is a consequence of Jensen’s inequality.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any nonnegative function

\left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}h(v(x))dx\right)\leq\frac{2\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(v(x)+\frac{1}{2\pi})h(v(x))dx}{1+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}v(x)dx}.

(4.7)

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
,
f(u)=uh(u)
and
g(u)=uh^{-1}(u)
are both convex functions and they satisfy

and
g(h(v))=vh(v)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}v(x)dx\leq f^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}v(x)h(v(x))dx\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}h(v(x))dx\leq g^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}v(x)h(v(x))dx\right).

Multiply these two equations and use the fact that

Given any fixed
M>0

{\varepsilon}>0

\rho_{0}=\inf\{t\in[0,\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}]:|u(t)|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\text{ for some }m\in\mathbb{N}\},

If
|u(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{0}
, then

\rho_{n+1}=\inf\{t\in[\rho_{n},\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}]:|u(t)|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{1}\}

(5.2)

and if
|u(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}
for

, then

\rho_{n+1}=\inf\{t\in[\rho_{n},\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}]:|u(t)|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m+1}\text{ or }|u(t)|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m-1}\}

(5.3)

For all of these stopping time definitions, we use the convention that
\rho_{n}=\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}
if the
L^{\infty}
norm does not triple or fall by one third after
\rho_{n-1}
.

We recall the following moment bound on the supremum norm of a stochastic convolution from
[
23
]

Proposition 5.1

(Theorem 1.2 of
[
23
]
)
.

Define the stochastic convolution for an adapted
\varphi:[0,T]\times[-\pi,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
by

Z^{\varphi}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int G(t-s,x-y)\varphi(s,y)W(dyds).

(5.4)

For any
p>6
, there exists
C_{p}>0
such that for any
T\in(0,1)
and any adapted
\varphi
,

{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in D}|Z^{\varphi}(t,x)|^{p}\leq C_{p}T^{\frac{p}{4}-\frac{3}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\int|\varphi(t,x)|^{p}dxdt.

(5.5)

The next result is the fundamental lemma that allows us to prove non-explosion.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists
m_{0}>0
and
C>0
, depending on
{\varepsilon}
and
M
, such that for any
m>m_{0}
and any

, the probability of tripling satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(|v(\rho_{n+1})|_{L^{\infty}}=3|v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}\text{ and }|v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}\geq 3^{m_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\rho_{n}}^{\rho_{n+1}}\int|\sigma(v(s,y))|^{2}dyds.

(5.6)

Proof.

Starting at time
\rho_{n}
, for
t\in[0,\rho_{n+1}-\rho_{n}]
, the local mild solution satisfies

v(\rho_{n}+t,x)=\tilde{v}_{n}(t,x)

(5.7)

where

(5.8)

where

I_{n}^{1}(t,x)=\int_{\rho_{n}}^{(\rho_{n}+t)\wedge\rho_{n+1}}\int G(t-s,x-y)b(v(s,y))dyds,

(5.9)

I_{n}^{2}(t,x)=\int_{\rho_{n}}^{(\rho_{n}+t)\wedge\rho_{n+1}}\int G(t-s,x-y)(v(s,y))^{-\alpha}dyds,

(5.10)

and
Z_{n}
is the stochastic convolution

Z_{n}(t,x)=\int_{\rho_{n}}^{(\rho_{n}+t)\wedge\rho_{n+1}}\int G(t-s,x-y)\sigma(v(s,y))W(dyds).

(5.11)

These definitions of
\tilde{v}_{n}(t,x)
,
I_{n}^{1}(t,x)
,
I_{n}^{2}(t,x)
, and
Z_{n}(t,x)
are convenient because they well-defined for all
t>0
, and
x\in D
, while
v
without stopping time could potentially explode. The definition satisfies
\tilde{v}_{n}(t,x)=v(\rho_{n}+t,x)
for all

. We will show that the
L^{\infty}
norm of

falls by a third before it triples with high probability. Because the original

matches until this tripling or falling by one-third occurs, this will prove that
v(\rho_{n+1}+t,x)
also falls by one-third before tripling with high probability.

The heat kernel satisfies the property
|G(t,x)|\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
t\in(0,1)
. Let
T_{m}=C^{-2}M^{-2}3^{-2(m-2)}
. Then, conditioning on
|u(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}
, and using the fact that
\rho_{n}\leq\tau^{1}_{M}
by definition, the linear term satisfies

\int G(T_{m},x-y)v(\rho_{n},y)dy\leq CMT_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq 3^{m-2}.

(5.12)

On the other hand, because
|G(T_{m},\cdot)|_{L^{1}}=1
,
for any
t>0
,

\int G(t,x-y)v(\rho_{n},y)dy\leq 3^{m}.

(5.13)

Because of the definition that
\rho_{n+1}\leq\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}
,

\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}I_{n}^{2}(t,x)\leq{\varepsilon}^{-\alpha}T_{m}\leq C{\varepsilon}^{-\alpha}3^{-2m}.

(5.14)

Conditioning on
|u(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}
, and applying Assumption
1
,

\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}I_{n}^{1}(t,x)\leq T_{m}(\theta(1+3^{m+1})+h(3^{m+1})).

(5.15)

Under either Assumption
1
(a) or (b), the growth rate of
h(u)\leq Cu^{2}
for some
C
. Therefore,

\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}I_{n}^{1}(t,x)\leq C3^{-2m}3^{2m}\leq C.

(5.16)

Therefore, we can choose
m_{0}
, depending on
{\varepsilon}
, large enough so that for all
m\geq m_{0}
,

I_{n}^{1}(t,x)+I_{n}^{2}(t,x)\leq C{\varepsilon}^{-\alpha}3^{-2m}+C\leq 3^{m-2}.

(5.17)

These estimates lead to the estimates that for
t\leq T_{m}

\displaystyle|\tilde{v}_{n}(t)|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 3^{m}+3^{m-2}+\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}|Z_{n}(t,x)|

(5.18)

and the endpoint estimate

|\tilde{v}_{n}(T_{m})|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 3^{m-2}+3^{m-2}+\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}|Z_{n}(t,x)|.

(5.19)

If
|Z_{n}(t,x)|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 3^{m-2}
for all
t\leq T_{m}
and
x\in D
, then
|\tilde{v}_{n}(t)|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 3^{m}+3^{m-2}+3^{m-2}<3^{m+1}
and
|\tilde{v}_{n}(T_{m})|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 3^{m-2}+3^{m-2}+3^{m-1}\leq 3^{m-1}
. In this case, therefore,
|\tilde{v}_{n}(t,x)|_{L^{\infty}}=|v(\rho_{n}+t,x)|_{L^{\infty}}
will fall by a third before it can triple. This means if
|v(\rho_{n+1})|_{L^{\infty}}
is going to reach the level
3^{m+1}
before falling to
3^{m-1}
, then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(|v(\rho_{n+1})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m+1}\Big||v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}|Z_{n}(t,x)|>3^{m-2}\Big||v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right).

(5.20)

By Chebyshev’s inequality and the moment bound Lemma
5.1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}|Z_{n}(t,x)|>3^{m-2}\Big||v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C3^{-mp}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}|Z_{n}(t,x)|^{p}\Big||v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C3^{-\frac{3mp}{2}+3m}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{\rho_{n}}^{\rho_{n+1}}\int|\sigma(v(s,y))|^{p}dyds\Big||v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right).

(5.21)

By Assumption
1
,
|\sigma(v(s,y))|\leq C3^{\frac{3m}{2}}
. Therefore, factoring out the supremum norm of
|\sigma(v(s,y))|^{p-2}
in the above expression,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{t\leq T_{m}}\sup_{x\in D}|Z_{n}(t,x)|>3^{m-2}\Big||v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C3^{-\frac{3mp}{2}+mp+\frac{3m(p-2)}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{\rho_{n}}^{\rho_{n+1}}\int|\sigma(u(s,y))|^{2}dyds\Big||v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right).

(5.22)

All of
m
terms cancel out. The leading constant
C
depends on
M
,
{\varepsilon}
, and
p
, which all have been fixed, but
C
is uniform for
m>m_{0}
and
n
. By the law of total probability,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(|v(\rho_{n+1})|_{L^{\infty}}=3|v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}\text{ and }|v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}\geq 3^{m_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=m_{0}}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\left(|v(\rho_{n+1})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m+1}||v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right){\mathbb{P}}\left(|v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}=3^{m}\right)\

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\rho_{n}}^{\rho_{n+1}}\int|\sigma(u(s,y))|^{2}dyds.

(5.23)

∎

Next, we use the previously developed bounds to prove that the explosion time is larger than
\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau^{1}_{M}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
{\varepsilon}>0
and
M>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}>\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}\right)=1.

(5.24)

Proof.

The estimate from Lemma
5.2
is uniform with respect to
n>0
.

Add these up with respect to
n
, remembering that
\rho_{n}\leq\rho_{n+1}\leq\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}
, to see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\left(|v(\rho_{n+1})|_{L^{\infty}}=3|v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}\text{ and }|v(\rho_{n})|_{L^{\infty}}\geq 3^{m_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}}\int|\sigma(u(s,y))|^{2}dyds.

(5.25)

The right-hand side is proportional to the the quadratic variation of the
L^{1}
norm, which is bounded by
M^{2}
because of the stopping time

M^{2}\geq{\mathbb{E}}|u(\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon})|_{L^{1}}^{2}=|u(0)|_{L^{1}}^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}}\int|\sigma(u(s,y))|^{2}dyds.

(5.26)

The Borel-Cantelli Lemma guarantees that the
L^{\infty}
norm can only triple a finite number of times before time
\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}
, when
m
is large. This implies that
\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}>\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}
with probability one.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We proved in Lemma
5.3
that for any fixed
{\varepsilon}>0
and
M>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}>\tau^{1}_{M}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}).

(5.27)

We can complete the proof that
v(t,x)
cannot explode by proving that
\tau^{1}_{M}
and
\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon}
converge to
\infty
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
and
M\to\infty
.

Proposition
3.2
proves that for arbitrary
\eta>0
and
T>0
, there exists
{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(T,\eta)>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau^{\inf}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}\leq T\wedge\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}\right)<\frac{\eta}{2}.

(5.28)

Then Lemma
4.1
or Lemma
4.6
, depending on whether we assume Assumption
1
(a) or (b), guarantees that there exists
M_{0}=M_{0}(T,\eta,{\varepsilon}_{0})
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau^{1}_{M_{0}}\leq T\wedge\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{\varepsilon})}\int u(t,x)dx>M_{0}\right)<\frac{\eta}{2}.

(5.29)

Then because
\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}>\tau^{1}_{M_{0}}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}
, it follows that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}\leq T\right)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau^{\inf}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}\leq T\wedge\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}\right)+{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau^{1}_{M_{0}}\leq T\wedge\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}\wedge\tau^{\inf}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}\right)<\eta.

(5.30)

Because
\eta
and
T
were arbitrary, this proves that
{\mathbb{P}}(\tau^{\infty}_{\infty}=\infty)=1
and the positive solution
v(t,x)
defined in (
3.1
) cannot explode in finite time. The argument for
v_{-}(t,x)
defined in (
3.2
) is identical. Finally, by the comparison principle Proposition
3.1
,
-v_{-}(t,x)\leq u(t,x)\leq v(t,x)
for all

and

. Therefore, the local mild solution to

∎

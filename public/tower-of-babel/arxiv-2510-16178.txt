The non-abelian Tensor Square of Finite Metacyclic Groups
Juliana Silva Canella, Noraí Romeu Rocco

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the group ν ⁡ ( G ) \nu(G) , an extension of the non-abelian tensor square G G by the direct product G × G G\times G , in order to determine a presentation of G ⊗ G G\otimes G when G G is a general finite metacyclic group, G = g ⁡ ( a , b , m , n , r , s ) G=g(a,b;m,n,r,s) , with m m odd. A presentation of ν ⁡ ( G ) \nu(G) is obtained from that of G G and, consequently, we describe the appropriate relevant sections of ν ⁡ ( G ) \nu(G) , such as the non-abelian tensor square, the exterior square G ∧ G G\wedge G and the Schur multiplier, M ⁡ ( G ) M(G) .

1.
Introduction

The
non-abelian tensor square

G\otimes G
of a group
G
as introduced by Brown and Loday
[
9
,
10
]
is defined to be the group generated by all symbols
g\otimes h,\,g,h\in G
, subject
to the relations

g{g_{1}}\otimes h=(g^{g_{1}}\otimes h^{g_{1}})(g_{1}\otimes h)\quad\text{and}\quad g\otimes h{h_{1}}=(g\otimes h_{1})(g^{h_{1}}\otimes h^{h_{1}})

for all
g,g_{1},h,h_{1}\in G.
Here and in the sequel, for elements
x,y
in a group
G
we write
x^{y}
to denote the conjugate
y^{-1}xy
; the commutator of
x
and
y
is then the element
[x,y]:=x^{-1}x^{y}
and our commutators are left normed,
[x,y,z]:=[[x,y],z]
for any
x,y,z\in G.

The group
G\otimes G
is a particular case of a non-abelian tensor product
G\otimes H
, of groups
G
and
H
, under the assumption that
G
and
H
acts one on each other and on itself by conjugation.

Given its relationship to several other relevant homology functors, the computation of this object for a given group
G
is of great interest.

The study of the non-abelian tensor square from a group theoretical point of view started with Brown, Johnson and Robertson in
[
8
]
. In that paper the authors compute
G\otimes G
for all non-abelian groups of order up to
30
using Tietze transformations in order to simplify the presentations. This method, however, is not appropriate for larger groups, since it involves
|G|^{2}
generators and
2|G|^{3}
relations.

A method that has been used is based on the concept of a
biderivation
(or a
crossed pairing
): let
G
and
L
be groups;
a
biderivation
from
G\times G
to
L
is a function
f:G\times G\to L
such that

(i)
f(aa_{1},b)=f(a^{a_{1}},b^{a_{1}})f(a_{1},b);

(ii)
f(a,bb_{1})=f(a,b_{1})f(a^{b_{1}},b^{b_{1}})

for all
a,a_{1},b,b_{1}\in G
. Any biderivation
f:G\times G\to L
determines a unique homomorphism

f^{*}:G\otimes G\to L

such that the diagram

\textstyle{{G\times G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{{G\otimes G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{{L}}

is commutative, where
j:G\times G\to G\otimes G,\;(g,h)\mapsto g\otimes h
is the canonical biderivation arising from the definition of
G\otimes G.
Therefore, to determine the non-abelian tensor square via biderivations, as done by many authors
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
21
]
, means to conjecture both the group
L
and the biderivation
f
, which in general is not an easy task.

On the other hand, since the defining relations of
G\otimes G
are abstractions of commutator relations in groups, in
[
19
]
the second named author suggested another way to obtain the non-abelian tensor square by making use of the following construction.
Let
G
be a group and let
G^{\varphi}
be an isomorphic copy of
G
via
\varphi:g\mapsto g^{\varphi},\,\forall g\in G
. Then we define the group
\nu(G)
to be

\nu(G):=\langle G\cup G^{\varphi}\mid[g_{1},g_{2}^{\varphi}]^{g_{3}}=[g_{1}^{g_{3}},(g_{2}^{g_{3}})^{\varphi}]=[g_{1},g_{2}^{\varphi}]^{g_{3}^{\varphi}},g_{i}\in G\rangle.

In
[
12
]
, Ellis and Leonard considered a similar construction. According to
[
19
, Proposition 2.6]
, the subgroup
\Upsilon(G)=[G,G^{\varphi}]
of
\nu(G)
is isomorphic to the non-abelian tensor square
G\otimes G
, which explains the initial motivation to study the group
\nu(G)
. Actually, the group
\nu(G)
admits the decomposition
\nu(G)=(\Upsilon(G)\cdot G)\cdot G^{\varphi}
, where the dots mean (internal) semidirect products. Moreover, thanks to the work of several mathematicians, this construction has become powerful machinery for computing and handling the non-abelian tensor square
G\otimes G
, specially when the group
G
is polycyclic (see
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
18
]
).

The
diagonal subgroup

\Delta(G):=\langle[g,g^{\varphi}]\mid g\in G\rangle\leq[G,G^{\varphi}]
is central in
\nu(G)
(see
[
18
, Remark 2]
). The section
[G,G^{\varphi}]/\Delta(G)
is isomorphic with the non-abelian exterior square
G\wedge G
, studied by Miller in
[
16
]
(
[
18
, p. 1984]
).

The evaluation of
G\otimes G
for finite split metacyclic
G
was carried out via biderivations by Brown, Johnson and Robertson in
[
8
]
, and by Johnson in
[
13
]
; the same technique was used by Beuerle and Kappe in
[
4
]
to compute
G\otimes G
for infinite metacyclic groups
G
.

The application
\rho:\nu(G)\to G
,
g\mapsto g
,
g^{\varphi}\mapsto g
, for every
g\in G
, induces an epimorphism of
\nu(G)
over
G
whose kernel,
\theta(G)
, centralizes

[G,G^{\varphi}]
in
\nu(G)
. The restriction of
\rho
to
[G,G^{\varphi}]
is the
derived map
,
\rho^{\prime}:[G,G^{\varphi}]\to G^{\prime}
,
[g,h^{\varphi}]\mapsto[g,h]
for all
g,h\in G
, whose kernel,
\mu(G)\;(=\Upsilon(G)\cap\theta(G))
, is then central in
\nu(G)
and is quite related to Homology Theory
[
7
]
; for instance,
\mu(G)/\Delta(G)
is isomorphic with the
Schur multiplier
of
G
,
M(G)
.

In this paper, we explore the commutator approach via the group
\nu(G)
to determine both
\nu(G)
and
G\otimes G
for a general finite metacyclic
G
. As a consequence we also describe the exterior square,
G\wedge G
and the Schur multiplier,
M(G)
, as sections of
\nu(G)
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we establish some notation and properties involving a finite metacyclic group
G=g(a,b;m,n,r,s)
(see Definition
2.1
). In Section 3 we briefly describe the group
\nu(G)
and establish some structural results. In Section 4 we give a presentation for group
\nu(G)
and obtain other of its sections such as
G\otimes G
and
M(G)
for a finite metacyclic group
G
with
m
odd.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we establish some notation and address basic results concerning finite metacyclic groups.

Let
m
be a non-negative integer. The ring of integers modulo
m
is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. For non-negative integers
\alpha
and
\beta
, not both zero, let
(\alpha,\beta):=\gcd(\alpha,\beta)
and
(0,0):=0
; the
\operatorname{lcm}(\alpha,\beta)
will be denoted by
[\alpha,\beta]
, whenever this does not cause confusion with commutators in groups. For
m>1
, we write
\mathcal{U}_{m}
for the set
\{r\in\mathbb{Z}\,\mid\,1\leq r\leq m-1\;\text{and}\;\gcd(r,m)=1\};
if
m=0
, then
\mathcal{U}_{0}=\{-1,1\}
. If
m>1
and
r\in\mathcal{U}_{m}
, let
t
be the unique integer in
\mathcal{U}_{m}
such that
rt\equiv 1\pmod{m}
.

A
metacyclic group
is an extension of a cyclic group by a cyclic group. From now on we shall consider the following general finite metacyclic group fixed in the next definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers. We define the group

G=g(a,b;m,n,r,s)
to be the finite metacyclic group given by the power-conjugate presentation

G:=\langle a,b\,\mid\,a^{m}=1,\;b^{n}=a^{s},\;a^{b}=a^{r}\rangle,\quad\text{where}

(1)

r^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{m}\quad\text{and}\quad s(r-1)\equiv 0\pmod{m}.

The above conditions on the 4-tuple of integers
(m,n,r,s)
determines a consistent polycyclic presentation to define the metacyclic group
G
of order
mn
, a result due to Hölder [
[
23
]
, Theorem 20]. We also see that every element
g\in G
can be written in the normal form
g=b^{\beta}a^{\alpha}
, where
0\leq\alpha\leq m-1,\;0\leq\beta\leq n-1
; in particular we always chose the parameter
s
such that
0\leq s<m.
From now on we fix this notation, according to Definition
2.1
.

Remark 2.2
.

For the purposes of this article we assume that our groups
G=g(a,b;m,n,r,s)
are non-abelian (in particular, non-cyclic); thus we have
(m,s)\neq 1
and
1<r\in\mathcal{U}_{m}
. In addition, if
r=m-1
, then
m>2
and
n
is even, regardless of the parity of
m
; also, if
m
is odd, then
G=g(a,b;m,n,m-1,0)
is a split extension, because
m\mid s(m-2)
; this fact also follows immediately from the Schur-Zassenhaus Theorem
[
[
17
]
, §
9.12
]
.

As usual we write
H^{\prime}
for the derived subgroup
[H,H]
and
Z(H)
for the center of a group
H
. For an arbitrary element
x\in H
we write
o(x)
for the order of
x
and
o^{\prime}(x)
for the order of the coset

in the abelianized group
H^{\text{ab}}=H/H^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G=g(a,b;m,n,r,s)
be a finite metacyclic group of order
mn
,
t=m/(m,r-1)
and let
l=|r\pmod{m}|
be the multiplicative order of
r\pmod{m}
. Then

(i)

o(a)=m\quad\text{and}\quad o(b)=n\dfrac{m}{(m,s)};

(ii)

G^{\prime}=\langle a^{r-1}\rangle\cong C_{t}\quad\text{and}\quad Z(G)=\langle a^{t},b^{l}\rangle;

(iii)

o^{\prime}(a)=(m,r-1)\quad\text{and}\quad o^{\prime}(b)=n\dfrac{(m,[s,r-1])}{(m,s)}.

Proof.

Part
(i)
follows directly from the fact that
A:=\langle a\rangle
has order
m
,
|G|=|\langle b\rangle A|=mn
and
|\langle b\rangle\cap A|=o(a^{s})
. Observe that
G^{\prime}=\langle[a,b]\rangle=\langle a^{r-1}\rangle
from part
(ii)
. Now, from the defining relations we have that
a^{t}
is the least power of
a
that centralizes
b
(and also
a
), while
b^{l}
is the least power of
b
that commutes with
a
,
a^{b^{l}}=a^{r^{l}}=a
(this result can also be found in Sim
[
20
, Lemma 2.7]
). As for part

we observe that
o^{\prime}(b)=[\langle b\rangle:\langle b\rangle\cap G^{\prime}]
is equal to the index
[\langle b\rangle:\langle a^{q}\rangle]
, where
q=[s,r-1]
, since
\langle b\rangle\cap G^{\prime}=\langle a^{s}\rangle\cap\langle a^{r-1}\rangle=\langle a^{q}\rangle
. This gives the desired expression for
o^{\prime}(b)
.

The following number theoretical function, borrowed from Beuerle and Kappe

Lemma 2.5

Let

3.
The group
\nu(G)
and some of its relevant sections

Let
G^{\varphi}
be an isomorphic copy of
G
through the isomorphism
\varphi:g\mapsto g^{\varphi}
for all
g\in G
and consider the group

\nu(G):=\langle G\cup G^{\varphi}\mid[g_{1},g_{2}^{\varphi}]^{g_{3}}=[g_{1}^{g_{3}},(g_{2}^{g_{3}})^{\varphi}]=[g_{1},g_{2}^{\varphi}]^{g_{3}^{\varphi}},g_{i}\in G\rangle.

As observed in the introduction, the normal subgroup
\Upsilon(G):=[G,G^{\varphi}]\leq\nu(G)
is canonically isomorphic with the non-abelian tensor square
G\otimes G
through the isomorphism induced by
[g,h^{\varphi}]\mapsto g\otimes h
(see
[
19
, Proposition 2.6]
). In view of this isomorphism we will write
[g,h^{\varphi}]
instead of
g\otimes h
and use this commutator approach for the purposes of this article.

The group
\nu(G)
admits the following decomposition, where the dots mean (internal) semidirect products.

(2)

\nu(G)=(\Upsilon(G)\cdot G)\cdot G^{\varphi}.

Recall that the
diagonal subgroup

\Delta(G):=\langle[g,g^{\varphi}]\mid g\in G\rangle
is central in
\nu(G)
(see
[
18
, Remark 3]
) and the section
[G,G^{\varphi}]/\Delta(G)
is isomorphic with the non-abelian exterior square
G\wedge G
, studied by Miller in
[
16
]
(see
[
18
, p. 1984]
).

There is an epimorphism
\rho:\nu(G)\to G,\;g\mapsto g,\;h^{\varphi}\mapsto h
for all
g\in G,h^{\varphi}\in G^{\varphi}.
We write
\Theta(G)
for
\ker\rho
and we have

\Theta(G)=\langle[g,\varphi]:=g^{-1}g^{\varphi}\,\mid\,g\in G\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\nu(G)=\Theta(G)\cdot G,

In addition, it follows from the defining relations that
\Theta(G)
centralizes
\Upsilon(G)
. Restriction of
\rho
to
\Upsilon(G)
gives the
derived map

\rho^{\prime}:[g,h^{\varphi}]\mapsto[g,h],
whose kernel
\ker\rho^{\prime}=\Upsilon(G)\cap\Theta(G)
is a central subgroup of
\nu(G)
, denoted by
\mu(G)
(see
[
18
, Proposition 2.7]
). Thus we have:

(3)

\dfrac{\Upsilon(G)}{\mu(G)}\cong G^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\dfrac{\mu(G)}{\Delta(G)}\cong M(G),

where
M(G)\cong H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is the Schur multiplier of
G
.
It is worth noting that our map
\rho^{\prime}
is the derived map
\kappa
in
[
8
]
, where the kernel
\ker\kappa
is written
J_{2}(G).

Since
\mu(G)\leq Z(\Upsilon(G))
it follows that
\Upsilon(G)
will be abelian whenever
G^{\prime}
is cyclic. This is true, in particular, for all metacyclic groups and so we have

Corollary 3.1
.

The non-abelian tensor square of the group
G=g(a,b;m,n,r,s)
is abelian, for any choice of the 4-tuple
(m,n,r,s).

.

For any group

(ii)

(vii)

[g,g^{\varphi}]=1
, for all

.

.

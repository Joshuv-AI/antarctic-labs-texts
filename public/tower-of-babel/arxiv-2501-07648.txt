Bi-Lipschitz embeddings revisited
H. Movahedi-Lankarani, R. Wells

Abstract
. Given a metric space ( X , d ) (X,d) , we continue our study of the distance function x ↦ d ⁡ ( x , − ) x\mapsto d(x,-) and its relation to bi-Lipschitz embeddings of ( X , d ) (X,d) into ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . As application, given a compact metric-measure space ( X , d , μ ) (X,d,\mu) , we give three sufficient sonditions for the existence of such a bi-Lipschitz embedding.

1.
Introduction

A map
f:(X,d_{X})\longrightarrow(Y,d_{Y})
of metric spaces is called Lipschitz if there exists
L>0
such that
d_{Y}\bigl(f(x),f(x^{\prime})\bigr)\leq L\,d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
. The infimum of such
L
is called the Lipschitz constant of
f
and is denoted
\text{\rm{Lip}}(f)
. The map
f
is called bi-Lipschitz provided that
f
is invertible with
f^{-1}
also Lipschitz. Equivalently,
f
is bi-Lipschitz provided that there exist
0<\ell\leq L
such that

\ell\,d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\leq d_{Y}\bigl(f(x),f(x^{\prime})\bigr)\leq L\,d_{X}(x,x^{\prime}),

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
. In this case, the supremum of such
\ell
is called the lower Lipschitz constant of
f
. We are interested in the still open problem of determining when a metric space admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into a finite dimensional Euclidean space.

Our interest in bi-Lipschitz embeddings arises from the fact that isometric embeddings of metric spaces can be very rigid. For instance, using a very simple argument, it is shown in
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
that no infinite ultrametric space admits an isometric embedding into a finite dimensional Euclidean space. (We recall that an ultrametric
d
on a set
X
is a metric that satisfies the following strong triangle inequality:
d(x,z)\leq\max\{d(x,y),\,d(y,z)\}
, for all
x,y,z\in X
.) Indeed, it is shown in
[
1
]
that if an ultrametric space admits an isometric embedding into
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then it has at most
N+1
points. In contrast, every doubling ultrametric space admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into a finite dimensional Euclidean space; see
[
3
,
15
]
and references therein. Even better (or worse), there is a finite metric space which does not admint an isometric embedding into
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, any
N\geq 1
; see Example
7.1
. Obviously, there is a bi-Lipschitz embedding of any finite metric space into
\mathbb{R}^{1}
.

Now let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric-measure space. That is,
(X,d)
is a metric space and
\mu
is a Borel regular measure on
X
which is non-trivial on nonempty open sets. We assume, throughout, that
0<\mu(X)<\infty
. We consider the canonical map
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{p}(\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, given by setting
\iota_{d}:x\mapsto d(x,-)
. This canonical map is a one-one contraction, but not necessarily bi-Lipschitz. Our interest lies in the question of when this canonical map is lower Lipschitz and its relationship to bi-Lipschitz embeddings into finite dimensions
[
19
]
. For simplicity, we restrict to the case of the canonical map
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
.

In sections 2 and 3, we first study the set
\iota_{d}(X)\subset L^{2}(\mu)
and some closely related maps. We then prove three bi-Lipschitz embedding results: Theorem
4.1
, Theorem
5.1
, and Theorem
6.1
. We finish by remarking on some relevant issues. This paper is a continuation of the work in
[
19
]
so that, as an appendix, an erratum for
[
19
]
is also included.

2.
The canonical map and radial projection

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric-measure space and let
\iota_{d}:X\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
be the canonical map so that, for every
x\in X
, we have
0<\|\iota_{\kappa}(x)\|_{2}\leq\text{\rm{diam}}(X)\,\mu^{1/2}(X)
. It is clear that for
x,y\in X
the inner product

\langle\iota_{d}(x),\iota_{d}(y)\rangle\geq 0
. If for some
x,y\in X
we have
\langle\iota_{d}(x),\iota_{d}(y)\rangle=0
, then there exist subsets
A,B\subset X
such that
d(x,z)=0
for all
z\in A
,
d(y,z)=0
for all
z\in B
, and
\mu(A\cup B)=\mu(X)>0
. But
d
is a metric so that
A=\{x\}
and
B=\{y\}
. Hence, since
\mu
is regular Borel, if
\text{\rm{card}}(X)\geq 3
, then
\langle\iota_{d}(x),\iota_{d}(y)\rangle>0
for all
x,y\in X
. Also, the function
(x,y)\mapsto\langle\iota_{d}(x),\iota_{d}(y)\rangle
is continuous and strictly positive. Consequently, if
(X,d)
is compact, then this function attains its minimum
s>0
, and we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a compact metric-measure space with
\text{\rm{card}}(X)\geq 3
and let
\iota_{d}:X\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
be the canonical map. Then there exists
s>0
such that for all
x,y\in X
we have
\langle\iota_{d}(x),\iota_{d}(y)\rangle\geq s>0
.

In this case, of course, we have

0<s\leq\langle\iota_{d}(x),\iota_{d}(x)\rangle=\|\iota_{d}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\text{\rm{diam}}(X)\|\iota_{d^{1/2}}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\text{\rm{diam}}^{2}(X)\mu(X).

Let
S(\mu)
denote the unit sphere of
L^{2}(\mu)
and let
\varpi:L^{2}(\mu)\setminus\{0\}\longrightarrow S(\mu)
be the radial projection
u\mapsto u/\|u\|_{2}
. Being the gradient of the convex function
u\mapsto\|u\|_{2}
, the map
\varpi
satisfies
\langle\varpi(u)-\varpi(v),u-v\rangle
for all
u,v\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric-measure space and let
\iota_{d}:X\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
be the canonical map. Then the radial projection
\varpi\big|_{\iota_{d}(X)}
is injective.

Proof.

If for some
x,y\in X
we have
\iota_{d}(x)=r\iota_{d}(y)
for some
r>0
, then
d(x,z)=r\,d(y,z)
for
\mu
-almost every
z\in X
. Being a metric,
d
is continuous and hence
d(x,z)=r\,d(y,z)
for every
z\in X
. In particular, for
z=x
we have
0=d(x,x)=r\,d(y,x)
, and
x=y
.
∎

The map
\iota_{d}
induces a metric
\rho_{d}
on
X
by setting
\rho_{d}(x,y)=\|\iota_{d}(x)-\iota_{d}(y)\|_{2}
. Also, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that
\iota_{d}
induces another metric
\theta_{d}
on
X
by setting
\theta_{d}(x,y)=
the arc-length on the unit sphere of
L^{2}(\mu)
from
\varpi\bigl(\iota_{d}(x)\bigl)=\iota_{d}(x)/\|\iota_{d}(x)\|_{2}
to
\varpi\bigl(\iota_{d}(y)\bigl)=\iota_{d}(y)/\|\iota_{d}(y)\|_{2}
. If
(X,d)
is compact, then

0\leq\theta_{d}(x,y)\leq\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{s}{\|\iota_{d}(x)\|_{2}\|\iota_{d}(y)\|_{2}}\right)<\pi/2.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a compact metric-measure space with
\text{\rm{card}}(X)\geq 3
and let
\iota_{d}:X\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
be the canonical map. Then there exist
x_{\circ},y_{\circ}\in X
such that
0<\cos\theta_{d}(x_{\circ},y_{\circ})\leq\cos\theta_{d}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
.

Of course there are relationships among the three metrics
d
,
\rho_{d}
, and
\theta_{d}
. For instance, it is easily seen that
(2\,\sqrt{s}/\pi)\,\theta_{d}\leq\rho_{d}\leq d
. However,
\theta_{d}
and
\rho_{d}
are not in general bi-Lipschitz equivalent.
Letting

\kappa_{d}(x,y)=\bigl\|\varpi\bigl(\iota_{d}(x)\bigl)-\varpi\bigl(\iota_{d}(y)\bigl)\bigr\|_{2}=2\sin\left(\frac{\theta_{d}(x,y)}{2}\right),

it is clear that
(2/\pi)\theta_{d}\leq\kappa_{d}\leq\theta_{d}
. Interestingly, the symmetric function

(1)

\Delta(\kappa_{d})(x,y)=\langle\kappa_{d}(x,-),\kappa_{d}(-,y)\rangle=\cos\theta_{d}(x,y)

does not satisfy the triangle inequality, but
1-\Delta(\kappa_{d})
is a pseudometric; see also Section 6.

It follows from straightforward calculations that the radial projection
\varpi:L^{2}(\mu)\setminus\{0\}\longrightarrow S(\mu)
is in fact differentiable with

d\varpi(u)(v)=\frac{1}{\|u\|_{2}}\left[v-\frac{\langle u,v\rangle}{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}u\right].

This raises the following question: Under what conditions is the map
\varpi\big|_{\iota_{d}(X)}
a
C^{1}
-embedding? We will not address this question in this paper except to formulate the conjecture below. We recall that there is a notion of the tangent space at a point of an arbitrary subset of a Hilbert space introduced in
[
17
]
, and we have:

Conjecture.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a compact metric-measure space and let
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
be the canonical map. Assume that there exists
0<\ell\leq 1
satisfying

(1)

\ell\,d(x,y)\leq\bigl\|\iota_{d}(x)-\iota_{d}(y)\bigr\|_{2}
for all
x,y\in X
, and

(2)

\bigl\|\iota_{d^{1/2}}(x)\bigr\|_{2}^{2}<\ell\,\bigl\|\iota_{d}(x)\bigr\|_{2}
for every
x\in X
.

Then the radial projection
\varpi\big|_{\iota_{d}(X)}:\iota_{d}(X)\longrightarrow S(\mu)
is a
C^{1}
-embedding.

.

Let
(X,d)

\text{\rm{diam}}(X,d)<\infty

\text{\rm{Lip}}(\lambda_{d})\leq\max\bigl\{2,\bigl(\text{\rm{diam}}(X,d)\bigr)^{1-s}\bigr\}

\text{\rm{Lip}}_{1-s}\bigl(\lambda_{d}(x)\bigr)=\sup_{y\neq z}\frac{\bigl|\lambda_{d}(x)(y)-\lambda_{d}(x)(z)\bigr|}{d^{1-s}(y,z)}\leq d^{s}(y,z)\leq\bigl(\text{\rm{diam}}(X,d)\bigr)^{s}

4.
A bi-Lipschitz embedding result

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric-measure space and let
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
be the canonical map. In this section we prove the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a compact metric-measure space and assume that the atoms of
\mu
are isolated. Let
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
be the canonical map and let
\rho_{d}=\iota_{d}^{\ast}\|\cdot-\cdot\|_{2}
. If the canonical map
\iota_{\rho_{d}}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
is lower Lipschitz, then there exists a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
(X,d)
into some
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

It follows that if
\iota_{d}
is an isometry, then there exists a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
(X,d)
into some
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. However, requiring the canonical map
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
to be an isometry is very restrictive. Indeed, if
\iota_{d}
is an isometry, then every triple of points in
X
lie on a geodesic. Consequently,
(X,d)
is a compact subset of the circle with the arc-length metric or a compact subset of the real line with the standard metric.

We will need the following definition from
[
19
]
and references therein. Let
X
be a subset of a Banach space
\mathbb{B}
and let

U(X)=\left\{\frac{x-y}{\|x-y\|_{\mathbb{B}}}\biggm|x,\,y\in X{\text{ with }}x\neq y\right\}.

We say that
X
is
spherically compact
if
U(X)
has a compact closure in the norm topology of
\mathbb{B}
. For compact subsets of a Banach space, spherical compactness is an invariant under
C^{1}
-diffeomorphisms but not under bi-Lipschitz equivalence. We say that
X
is
weakly spherically compact
provided that the weak closure of
U(X)
does not contain
0\in\mathbb{B}
. For compact subsets, weak spherical compactness is a bi-Lipschitz invariant. Obviously, spherically compact implies weakly spherically compact, but not conversely.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
19
, Lemma 2.4]

Let
X
be a compact subset of a normed linear space
\mathbb{B}
. Then there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a bounded linear map
\Phi:\mathbb{B}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\Phi\big|_{X}
a bi-Lipschitz embedding if and only if
X
is weakly spherically compact.

The next result we need is the correct version of
[
19
, Theorem 4.2]
. The hypothesis in
[
19
, Theorem 4.2, (1)]
that
X
is weakly spherically compact is not sufficient. Instead, it must be assumed that
X
is spherically compact.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
19
, Theorem 4.2]

Let
X
be a compact subset of a Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
with the inherited metric
\|\cdot-\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}}
and let
\mu
be a Borel regular measure with closed support
X
.

(1)

If
X
is spherically compact, then the canonical map
\iota_{\|\cdot-\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}}}:X\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)

(2)

Assume that all atoms of
\mu
are isolated. If the canonical map
\iota_{\|\cdot-\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}}}:X\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
is bi-Lipschitz, then
X
is weakly spherically compact.

Part (2) of this theorem is needed in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. In
[
13
]
it is asked whether every doubling subset of a Hilbert space admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into some Euclidean space. This problem is still open, but for doubling subsets of

. Part (2) of Theorem

with
\eta_{x}:0\longrightarrow\delta\bigl(f(x),g(x)\bigr)
. This, of course, means that there is at most one
\mathcal{L}
-natural transformation from
f
to
g
, and that is precisely when
\delta\bigl(f(x),g(x)\bigr)=0
for all
x\in X
. Following
[
12
, page 29]
, we write
\bigl[(X,d),(Y,\delta)\bigr]
for the
\mathcal{L}
-category whose underlying ordinary category has objects the
\mathcal{L}
-functors
(X,d)\longrightarrow(Y,\delta)
and maps the
\mathcal{L}
-natural transformations between them.

Now,
\mathcal{L}
equipped with the family
\bigl\{\text{\rm{map}}(a,b)\bigm|a,b\in\mathcal{L}_{0}\bigr\}
as the hom objects is itself an
\mathcal{L}
-category; see
[
12
, page 15]
. Hence, an
\mathcal{L}
-functor
F:(X,d)\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}
satisfies
d(x,x^{\prime})\geq\text{\rm{map}}\bigl(F(x),F(x^{\prime})\bigr)=\max\bigl\{F(x^{\prime})-F(x),0\bigr\}
. In particular, for each
x\in X
, the functor
d(x,-):(X,d)\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}
is an
\mathcal{L}
-functor because
d
satisfies
d(y,z)\geq\max\bigl\{d(x,z)-d(x,y),0\bigr\}=\text{\rm{map}}\bigl(d(x,y),d(x,z)\bigr)
. The Yoneda embedding is the fully faithful
\mathcal{L}
-functor
\mathbb{Y}:(X,d)\longrightarrow\bigl[(X,d),\mathcal{L}\bigr]
sending the object
x
to the representable
\mathcal{L}
-functor
d(x,-)
.

VIII.
Either one of our hypotheses

(1)

that
\iota_{\rho_{d}}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
is bi-Lipschitz in Theorem
4.1
, or

(2)

that
\iota_{\Delta(d)}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
is bi-Lipschitz in Theorem
6.1
,

implies that
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
is bi-Lipschitz. In fact, writing
\ell(f)
for the lower Lipschitz constant of a map
f
, we have
\ell\left(\iota_{\Delta(d)}\right)\inf_{x}\bigl\|\iota_{d}(x)\bigr\|_{2}\leq\ell\left(\iota_{d}\right)
.

IX.
In our bi-Lipschitz embedding theorems above, that
(X,d)
is a doubling (equivalently, of finite Assouad dimension
[
2
]
) metric space is a conclusion rather than a hypothesis. As pointed out by Eriksson-Bique
[
7
]
, perhaps Statement A.1 (see the appendix below) is true with some implied doubling hypothesis.

Appendix: Erratum for
[
19
]
.

This erratum for
[
19
]
was scheduled to appear in Portugaliae Mathematica in 2020, but somehow (most likely due to the COVID-19 pandemic) it fell through the cracks and didn’t happen. So, here it is.

The main “result” of
[
19
]
is the following statement:

Statement A.1.

[
19
, Main Theorem]

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a compact metric-measure space. If the canonical map
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{p}(\mu)
is bi-Lipschitz for some
1<p<\infty
, then there exists a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
(X,d)
into some
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The above remains simply a “statement” because Sylvester Eriksson-Bique
[
7
]
, and independently Basok and Zolotov
[
4
]
, found a fatal error in the proof presented in
[
19
, Lemma 3.6]
. Specifically, in the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 3.6]
, it is falsely stated that “If the sequence
\{i(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
is not co-final in
I
, then the directed set
I^{\prime}=\{j:j>i(n),n\geq 1\}
is non-empty and co-final in
I
.” Worse yet, they also constructed counterexamples to show that, as written, Statement A.1 is indeed false. The following is another such example; it is due an anonymous reader.

Example A.2.
[Anonymous Reader] Let
\lambda
denote the Lebesgue measure on the interval
[0,1]
. The first step is to find a compact non-doubling subset
L\subset L^{\infty}(\lambda)\subset L^{2}(\lambda)
so that identity map
{\text{id}}:\bigl(L,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(L,\|\cdot\|_{2}\bigr)
is bi-Lipschitz and
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq M
for all
f\in L
and some fixed
M\geq 2
.

To this end, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
F_{n}=\{-1,1\}^{2^{n}}
with the Hamming distance
d_{n}
: For
\alpha=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{2^{n}})
and
\beta=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{2^{n}})
in
F_{n}
, we set
d_{n}(\alpha,\beta)
equal to the number of indices at which
\alpha
and
\beta
differ. Let
K_{n}\subset F_{n}
be a Hadamard code:
K_{n}
is a
2^{n-1}
-separated subset of cardinality
2^{n}
. In other words, for
\alpha,\beta\in K_{n}
with
\alpha\neq\beta
we have
d_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\geq 2^{n-1}
. Let
\nu_{n}
denote the counting measure on
K_{n}
normalized so that
\nu_{n}(K_{n})=1/2^{n}
. Then
\sum_{n\geq 1}\nu_{n}(K_{n})=1
, and each
\alpha\in K_{n}
has measure
\nu_{n}(\alpha)=1/2^{2n}
.

Now let
h:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the standard hat function centered at
t=1/2
and supported on the interval
[0,1]
. Explicitly,
h(t)=\max\left\{0,1/2-|t-1/2|\right\}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Clearly,
h
is Lipschitz with
\text{\rm{Lip}}(h)=1
. For each
n\geq 1
and each
\alpha=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{2^{n}})\in K_{n}
we define a function
f_{n,\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by setting

f_{n,\alpha}(t)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n}-1}a_{i+1}\,h(2^{n}t-i).

Then
f_{n,\alpha}
is a Lipschitz function supported on the interval
[0,1]
with
\text{\rm{Lip}}(f_{n,\alpha})=1
. Consequently, each
f_{n,\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(\lambda)\subset L^{2}(\lambda)
with
\|f_{n,\alpha}\|_{\infty}=1/2^{n+1}
while
\|f_{n,\alpha}\|_{2}=1/(\sqrt{3}\,2^{n+1})
.

We set

L=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\bigcup_{\alpha\in K_{n}}\{f_{n,\alpha}\}\bigcup\{\mathbb{0}\},

where
\mathbb{0}
denotes the identically zero function on
[0,1]
. Then
L\subset L^{\infty}(\lambda)\subset L^{2}(\lambda)
is compact and countable. Moreover, for
m\leq n
and
\alpha\neq\beta
, we have both
\|f_{n,\alpha}-f_{m,\beta}\|_{\infty}\sim 1/2^{n}
as well as
\|f_{n,\alpha}-f_{m,\beta}\|_{2}\sim 1/2^{n}
so that for any
c\geq 2\sqrt{3}
we have

(15)

\|f_{n,\alpha}-f_{m,\beta}\|_{2}\leq\|f_{n,\alpha}-f_{m,\beta}\|_{\infty}\leq c\,\|f_{n,\alpha}-f_{m,\beta}\|_{2}

implying that
{\text{id}}:\bigl(L,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(L,\|\cdot\|_{2}\bigr)
is bi-Lipschitz. The metric space
\bigl(L,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\bigr)
is non-doubling because the ball of radius
1/2^{n}
and centered at
\mathbb{0}
contains
2^{n}
points with pairwise distances
\sim 1/2^{n}
(the functions corresponding to
K_{n}\simeq\{f_{n,\alpha}\bigm|\alpha\in K_{n}\}
). Lastly, we endow
L
with a Borel regular probability measure
\nu
by setting
\nu(f_{n,\alpha})=1/2^{2n}
and
\nu(\mathbb{0})=0
.

Finally, let
M\geq 2
be fixed and let
X=[0,1]\cup L
. We define a metric
d
on
X
by setting

\displaystyle d(t,s)

\displaystyle=\big|t-s\big|\ {\text{ for }}t,s\in[0,1],

\displaystyle d(f,g)

\displaystyle=\|f-g\|_{\infty}\ {\text{ for }}f,g\in L,

\displaystyle{\text{and }}d(t,f)

\displaystyle=2M+f(t)\ {\text{ for }}t\in[0,1]{\text{ and }}f\in L.

In order to show that
d
satisfies the triangle inequality, the inequalities (
15
) are needed in the mixed case. As for a measure
\mu
, we set
\mu=\nu/2+\lambda/2
. Then
(X,d,\mu)
is a compact metric-measure space with
\mu(X)=1
which does not admit a bi-Lipschitz embedding into
\mathbb{R}^{N}
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
because it is non-doubling. However, it follows from straightforward calculations (considering different cases separately, and again using (
15
) for the mixed case) that the canonical map
\iota_{d}:(X,d)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
is bi-Lipschitz.

Machine Learning Identified Mitophagy- and Hypoxia-Related Genes MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 as Potential Biomarkers Associated with Unexplained Recurrent Pregnancy Loss
Authors: Zhou Fang, Yanyan Zhang, Lu Xu, Xi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to identify mitophagy- and hypoxia-related biomarkers for unexplained recurrent pregnancy loss (uRPL) by integrating transcriptomic data with machine learning algorithms.MethodsThe endometrial transcriptome dataset GSE165004 was obtained from the GEO database to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between uRPL cases and healthy controls. A set of mitophagy- and hypoxia-related genes (HMRGs) was collected from GeneCards and PubMed. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was used to detect HMRG-related key module genes. The intersection between module genes and DEGs was defined as DE-HMRGs, which were subjected to functional and pathway enrichment analysis and protein-protein interaction network (PPI) construction. Hub genes were selected using LASSO, SVM-RFE, and random forest algorithms, and their diagnostic performance was evaluated by an artificial neural network (ANN) model and validated in an independent dataset.ResultsA total of 356 DEGs were identified. Intersecting these DEGs with HMRG key module genes yielded 82 DE-HMRGs, which were mainly enriched in energy metabolism and WNT signaling. In the PPI network constructed from DE-HMRGs, NCKAP1 emerged as a central node. Machine learning identified MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 as key hub genes, exhibiting good diagnostic performance in the ANN model in both the training and validation sets (AUC=1, respectively).ConclusionThe mitophagy- and hypoxia-related genes MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 are involved in the pathogenesis of uRPL and may serve as potential diagnostic and therapeutic biomarkers. However, the study is limited by its retrospective nature and a lack of experimental validation; thus, prospective studies and functional validation are warranted.Keywords: unexplained recurrent pregnancy loss, mitophagy, hypoxia, biomarkers, machine learning, MTRNR2L8, SUMO1P3

Introduction

Recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL), defined as two or more embryonic or fetal losses, is a distressing disorder affecting approximately 2.6% of pregnancies,1 with a rising global incidence.2 While roughly half of RPL patients can be attributed to chromosomal abnormalities, anatomical anomalies, or endocrine and immune dysregulation,3,4 the remaining 50% lack an identifiable clinical reason and are termed as unexplained RPL (uRPL).5 The specific subgroup presents a profound clinical challenge; without a definitive diagnosis, patients suffer immense psychological distress, and clinicians lack evidence-based therapeutic options. Therefore, unravelling the distinct pathogenic mechanisms underlying uRPL is uniquely urgent to transform this frustrating clinical dilemma into an actionable, precision-driven diagnostic and therapeutic pathway.

During pregnancy, mitochondria supply the energetic demands of endometrial decidualization, placentation, and embryonic development.6,7 Under hypoxic conditions, the mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation process is limited, leading to reduced ATP generation and subsequent mitochondrial dysfunction.8 Hypoxia also disrupts mitochondrial redox homeostasis, generating excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) that further damage mitochondria.9 If damaged or dysfunctional mitochondria are not timely repaired or removed, their accumulation can result in exacerbated oxidative stress, activated inflammatory responses, and increased apoptosis. These alterations can impair endometrial receptivity and the decidualization process, ultimately leading to abnormal placental development and dysfunction. Mitophagy is a key mechanism for the clearance of damaged and dysfunctional mitochondria, and its dysregulation has been implicated in the pathogenesis of disorders affecting the nervous, cardiovascular, pulmonary, hepatic, and renal systems.10 Recent studies have indicated that the mitophagy pathway plays a significant role in the development of obstetrical and gynecological diseases, such as fetal growth restriction and preeclampsia.11,12 However, despite the clear mechanical involvement of these pathways, a critical gap remains in translating these findings into clinical diagnostics for uRPL. Previous biomarker research has predominantly focused on isolated immunological or endocrine factors, leaving the combined, synergistic impact of hypoxia and mitophagy poorly characterized. Furthermore, traditional diagnostic evaluations often rely on common, low-specificity proteins (such as HIF-1α or LC3B) that fail to provide high-specificity risk stratification for uRPL patients, leaving an unmet clinical need for objective, multi-pathway gene panels. Therefore, we hypothesize that hypoxia and mitophagy may be implicated in the pathogenesis of uRPL. Accordingly, focusing on mitophagy signal transduction may contribute to the targeted treatment of this condition.

On this basis, this study aims to apply an integrated bioinformatic workflow to investigate whether mitophagy- and hypoxia-related genes are involved in the pathogenesis of uRPL and to evaluate their potential as biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Methods

Data Acquisition

Clinical expression data were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Datasets were selected using the disease term “unexplained recurrent pregnancy loss (uRPL)”, organism “Homo sapiens”, and source tissue “endometrium”. The selected datasets include samples from uRPL patients and healthy controls, ensuring adequate sample size. The primary dataset analyzed in this study is GSE165004, which contains gene expression data from 24 healthy control samples and 24 uRPL samples. To validate the findings, an independent dataset, GSE113790 (3 healthy samples and 3 uRPL samples), was also examined. Details of the datasets are listed in Table 1.

Characteristics of the Datasets

Hypoxia-related human genes (HRGs) were collected from GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/) using “Hypoxia” as the search term, and results were filtered for protein-coding genes with a Relevance Score > 1. Additional hypoxia-related gene lists reported in the literature were obtained from PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and merged, and duplicates were removed, yielding 2478 HRGs. Mitochondrial autophagy-related genes (MRGs) were similarly obtained from GeneCards by searching “Mitophagy” (Relevance Score > 1 and protein-coding), supplemented by literature-curated lists from PubMed. After integration and deduplication, 1715 MRGs were identified. The intersection of HRGs and MRGs produced 573 hypoxia- and mitochondrial autophagy-related genes (HMRGs).

The Ethics Review Board of the Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine Second Affiliated Hospital exempted this study from ethical approval according to items 1 and 2 of Article 32 of the Measures for Ethical Review of Life Science and Medical Research Involving Human Subjects (China, February 18, 2023).

Data Preprocessing and Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes

The GSE165004 dataset was background-corrected, normalized, and log2-transformed using the “limma” package (version 3.64.1) in R 4.5.1, and the results before and after normalization were displayed in the form of boxplots (Figure S1). Probe annotation was performed on the normalized expression matrix, and probes without matching gene symbols were removed. For genes represented by multiple probes, the probe values were averaged to obtain a single expression value per gene. Differential expression analysis was performed using the “limma” package, and p-values were adjusted by the false discovery rate (FDR) method. DEGs were defined by |logFC| > 0.585 and adjusted p-value < 0.05. Volcano plots and heatmaps of DEGs were generated using the “heatmap” (version 4.5.1) and “ggplot2” (version 3.5.2) packages.

Identification of HMRG-Related Module Genes

Expression data for the 573 HMRGs were extracted from the GSE165004 dataset. Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA; version 2.2.0) was used to compute HMRG scores for uRPL patients and healthy controls. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was then applied to explore expression patterns among these genes. Samples with outlying values were removed by hierarchical clustering to improve reliability. An appropriate soft-thresholding power was chosen to ensure a scale-free topology fit index (R2) > 0.8 and a mean connectivity near zero.

A co-expression adjacency matrix was constructed, and genes were clustered into modules using the dynamic tree cut algorithm implemented in “pheatmap” (minModuleSize=30, mergeCutHeight=0.25). Module eigengenes were calculated, and Pearson correlation analysis was used to identify gene modules significantly associated with HMRG scores.

GO and KEGG Enrichment Analysis of Differentially Expressed HMRGs

DEGs identified by “limma” were intersected with HMRG-related key module genes from WGCNA to obtain differentially expressed HMRGs (DE-HMRGs). To investigate the biological functions of DE-HMRGs, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed using the “clusterProfiler” package (version 4.16.0). Enrichment results were used to identify relevant biological processes, cellular components, molecular functions, and KEGG pathways involving these genes. Terms with p < 0.05 were considered significant. Enrichment results were visualized using the “enrichplot” (version 1.28.4) and “ggplot2” packages.

Protein-Protein Interaction Network Construction

To explore functional associations among DE-HMRGs, a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using STRING (https://string-db.org). PPI network analysis is a powerful tool that can reveal the functional relationships and signaling pathways of gene products within cells. STRING is a widely used protein interaction database that integrates both known and predicted PPI information. The PPI network was visualized using Cytoscape and the CytoHubba plugin.

Hub Gene Selection by Machine Learning

Three machine learning algorithms were used to identify hub genes from the DE-HMRGs: least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), and random forest. LASSO applies L1 regularization to perform variable selection and shrinkage, which is useful for high-dimensional data. SVM-RFE is a recursive feature selection method based on support vector machines that iteratively removes features and evaluates model performance to determine the optimal feature subset. Random forest is an ensemble learning method that builds multiple decision trees and aggregates their results to improve accuracy and stability. The overlapping genes identified by all three algorithms were considered hub genes and visualized with a Venn diagram.

Artificial Neural Network Diagnostic Model

An artificial neural network (ANN) was constructed to evaluate the diagnostic potential of the hub genes. The “neuralnet” package (version 1.44.2) was used to train a stable ANN model based on the hub gene expression scores. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and area under the curve (AUC) values were calculated with the “pROC” package (version 1.18.5) to assess model performance in the training and validation datasets. An AUC value > 0.7 was considered indicative of good diagnostic performance.

Single-Gene Enrichment Analysis

To identify pathways associated with hub genes, Pearson correlation coefficients between each hub gene and other genes were calculated and ranked by correlation strength. GO and KEGG Gene Set Enrichment Analyses (GSEAs) were performed using the “clusterProfiler” package. Multiple testing correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. Pathways with p < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

Immune Microenvironment Analysis

Immune cell infiltration was analyzed in the GSE165004 dataset to characterize immune cell composition and functional state in uRPL. CIBERSORT was used to estimate the relative abundance of 22 immune cell types in uRPL and healthy control samples. Differences in immune cell proportions between groups were visualized using bar plots and violin plots generated by the “vioplot” package (version 0.5.1). Correlation heatmaps of immune cells were plotted with “corrplot” (version 0.95). Additionally, associations between hub gene expression and immune cell infiltration were analyzed.

Prediction of Potential Therapeutic Drugs

Potential therapeutic agents and drug-gene interactions were predicted using the CellMiner database (https://discover.nci.nih.gov/cellminer/home.do). Predicted drug interactions were visualized in Cytoscape.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed in R (version 4.5.1). Pearson correlation tests were used to identify HMRG-related key modules. Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were applied to compare hub gene expression between uRPL and healthy control samples and to evaluate differences in immune cell infiltration between groups. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. A flowchart of the study design is shown in Figure 1.

Results

Identification of DEGs in uRPL

After preprocessing GSE165004, 356 genes were identified as differentially expressed, of which 109 were upregulated and 247 were downregulated (Figure 2A). The top sixty genes were selected for visualization (Figure 2B).

Identification of DE-HMRGs and Functional Enrichment Analysis

WGCNA identified 93 key module genes, which were intersected with the DEGs from uRPL patients and healthy controls. Eighty-two module genes overlapped with the DEGs and were defined as DE-HMRGs (Figure 4A). GO enrichment analysis of DE-HMRGs showed that the top molecular functions included actin binding, regulation of cysteine-type endopeptidase activity involved in apoptotic process, sulfurtransferase activity, ubiquitin-like modifier activating enzyme activity, and rRNA binding. Cellular components enrichment involved the cell cortex, organelle membrane lumen, spindle pole centrosome, and tricarboxylic acid cycle heteromeric enzyme complex. Biological processes included the generation of precursor metabolites and energy, regulation of Wnt signaling pathway, regulation of protein transport, and regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase I (Figure 4B). KEGG analysis indicated that DE-HMRGs were mainly involved in amino acid biosynthesis, bacterial invasion of epithelial cells, carbon metabolism, and signaling pathways regulating pluripotency of stem cells (Figure 4C).

PPI Network

The PPI network was constructed using STRING and Cytoscape (Figure 5). The results showed that the degree value of the NCKAP1 gene was the highest, and it interacted with multiple proteins, including MYO1A, CRAMIL1, CD2AP, and DUSP22.

Selection of Optimal Feature Genes for uRPL

LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation identified 13 candidate hub genes (Figure 6A and B). SVM-RFE indicated that classifier error was minimized with four features, selected as hub features (Figure 6C). In the random forest analysis, 13 genes with importance scores > 4 were selected as potential diagnostic biomarkers for uRPL (Figure 6D). Four downregulated hub genes overlapped among the three algorithms were finally determined, including RPS7P5, C18orf32, MTRNR2L8, and SUMO1P3 (Figure 6E and Table S1).

Diagnostic Performance of Hub Genes and the ANN Model

Hub gene diagnostic performance was validated in the independent dataset GSE113790 after dataset normalization, and the invalid gene RPS7P5 was excluded. ROC curves were used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the three hub genes. In the training set GSE165004 and the validation set GSE113790, the AUCs for C18orf32 were 1 and 0.67, respectively (Figure 7A). MTRNR2L8 showed AUCs of 1 in both datasets (Figure 7B). SUMO1P3 had AUCs of 1 and 0.83 in the training and validation sets, respectively (Figure 7C). These results indicate that MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 are closely associated with uRPL prognosis and have high diagnostic value.

Evaluation of diagnostic value for hub genes. (A) ROC curve for C18orf32; (B) ROC curve for MTRNR2L8; (C) ROC curve for SUMO1P3; (D) Schematic of the ANN model for uRPL classification; (E) ROC curve for the ANN model.

An ANN model was then constructed based on MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3, and ROC curves were plotted (Figure 7D). The ANN distinguished uRPL from controls effectively, with an AUC of 1 in both the training set and validation set (Figure 7E), suggesting that the combined use of these two genes improves diagnostic accuracy.

Functional Enrichment of Hub Genes

Single-gene GSEA was performed for key genes MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3, and the top five GO and KEGG terms were selected for visualization (Figure S2A–D). GO analysis showed that MTRNR2L8 was significantly associated with collagen-containing extracellular matrix, endoplasmic reticulum lumen, and morphogenesis of branching structures. SUMO1P3 was significantly associated with olefinic compound metabolic process, blood coagulation, and wound healing. KEGG analysis linked MTRNR2L8 expression with the regulations of WNT signaling, Hedgehog signaling, and plasmin-mediated activation of latent TGF-β signaling pathways. SUMO1P3 expression was associated with tight junction-actin signaling, HEDGEHOG signaling, KEAP1-NRF2 signaling, and blood group antigen biosynthesis signaling pathways.

Immune Infiltration

Among the 22 immune cell types analyzed in uRPL and healthy samples, naive CD4 T cells and activated mast cells exhibited undetectable infiltration levels. Consequently, the infiltration of the remaining 20 immune cell types between the two groups was visualized (Figure S3A). Compared with controls, the uRPL group showed significantly reduced infiltration of M1 and M2 macrophages and significantly increased infiltration of follicular helper T cells (Figure S3B). Correlation analysis among immune cells revealed a strong positive correlation between CD8 T cells and plasma cells (r=0.57) and a negative correlation between follicular helper T cells and activated CD4 memory T cells (r=−0.55) (Figure S3C). Spearman correlation analysis was used to further examine relationships between the two hub genes and immune cells (Figure S3D and E). MTRNR2L8 correlated positively with neutrophils (r=0.33, p=0.024). SUMO1P3 correlated positively with M1 macrophages (r=0.45, p=0.0014).

Prediction of Potential Drugs

A gene-drug network was constructed in Cytoscape, and the hub genes MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 were highly connected to numerous drugs, suggesting that they may represent potential targets of these compounds (Figure S4A). Drug sensitivity analysis for MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 was then performed by calculating Pearson correlation coefficients between hub gene expression and drug response. The top four drugs correlated with MTRNR2L8 were oprozomib, mithramycin, ADW-742, and milademetan. The top four drugs correlated with SUMO1P3 were irofulven, salirasib, AT-13148, and ARQ-087 (Figure S4B).

Discussion

In this study, 82 DE-HMRGs were identified as significantly dysregulated in uRPL samples, and the PPI network highlighted NCKAP1 as a central node in uRPL pathogenesis. Notably, machine learning identified two downregulated hub genes, MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3. ROC analysis and an ANN model demonstrate that these two biomarkers have substantial diagnostic value and can effectively discriminate uRPL patients from healthy controls. Functional annotation links them to WNT signaling, extracellular matrix organization, and coagulation-related pathways, suggesting mechanistic connections to placental development and hemostatic processes. These findings provide novel insights into uRPL pathogenesis. Future prospective studies with larger patient cohorts are warranted to clarify the precise role of NCKAP1 in uRPL and to validate the predictive and diagnostic potential of MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3, thereby advancing personalized and stratified management of uRPL.

Within the PPI network, NCKAP1 occupies a central hub and interacts with multiple proteins. As a core component of the WAVE regulatory complex, NCKAP1 regulates accessibility of the WAVE VCA motif by maintaining WAVE in an inhibited conformation. Upon specific stimulation or interaction, WRC disassembly leads to WAVE-VCA exposure, subsequently activating the Arp2/3 complex to induce branched actin polymerization and pseudopod formation, ultimately driving cytoskeletal remodeling and cell migration.13 Ma et al reported that reduced NCKAP1 expression inhibits the proliferation, migration, and invasion of vascular smooth muscle cells.14 Chen et al found that NCKAP1 is a key intermediary molecule regulating cell migration in interstitial extravillous trophoblasts, enhancing trophoblast migration by disrupting the WAVE complex and activating the Arp2/3 pathway.15 Normal trophoblast invasion and vascular remodeling depend on dynamic changes in the cytoskeleton, and NCKAP1 dysregulation could affect these processes. Taken together with the enrichment analysis, these observations implicate NCKAP1-mediated actin reorganization and cell migration pathways as critical in uRPL. By linking mitophagy and hypoxia signaling to the cell motility required for trophoblast development, NCKAP1 may act as a mediator in uRPL and thus offers a new mechanistic perspective.

When contextualizing our findings within the existing literature, several studies have explored similar molecular axes in RPL. For instance, Chen et al identified BSG and TRAK1 as potential markers via integrated bioinformatic analyses; specifically, BSG was significantly upregulated and involved in RPL pathogenesis by modulating the HIF-1 signaling pathway.16 In terms of oxidative stress, Wang et al identified key stress-responsive hub genes, including ARRB2, BMF, and SORCS2, which exhibited protein-level upregulation in the endometrium of RPL patients and were remarkably enriched in hypoxia pathways according to GSEA analysis.17 In this study, MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 emerge among the hub genes with the highest diagnostic potential in uRPL patients, and participate in extracellular matrix organization, WNT signaling, and coagulation pathways. The extracellular matrix can regulate trophoblast invasion into the endometrium via WNT/β-catenin signaling and thereby promote placentation.18 Dysregulation of coagulation is associated with impaired placental implantation.19 These results suggest that impaired trophoblast invasion and abnormal placental remodeling may contribute to pregnancy failure. Furthermore, compared to healthy control groups, MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 expression was significantly reduced in women with uRPL and showed a positive correlation with neutrophil and M1 macrophage infiltration, respectively. MTRNR2L8 encodes a humanin-like peptide that exerts anti-apoptotic effects.20 SUMO1P3 is a pseudogene-expressed long non-coding RNA that has been shown to promote cell proliferation and suppress apoptosis.21 Controlled apoptosis is essential for placental remodeling during pregnancy.22,23 Excessive or insufficient apoptosis disrupts this balance and can lead to pregnancy loss. Previous studies have suggested higher expression of pro-apoptotic genes in chorionic villi from RPL patients versus controls.24 Additionally, compared to women without RPL, significantly increased MTRNR2L8 expression was observed in the endometrium of RPL patients,25 while SUMO1P3 expression was significantly decreased.26 These findings suggest that normal apoptotic processes and the immune environment are crucial for pregnancy, and their dysregulation may be related to the mechanisms of uRPL. The MTRNR2L8 SUMO1P3-based ANN model performed well in both test and validation cohorts and may offer a tool for early prediction and diagnosis of uRPL, but further validation using clinical data in prospective studies is required.

Potential drugs targeting MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 were also identified. Compounds associated with MTRNR2L8 include oprozomib, mithramycin, ADW 742, and milademetan, whereas those linked to SUMO1P3 include irofulven, salirasib, AT 13148, and ARQ 087. These agents are primarily investigated in oncology and have not been applied in the context of pregnancy loss. Nevertheless, Tabrizi-Nejhadi et al suggested that oprozomib may hold therapeutic potential for female infertility.27 Overall, these agents could hypothetically modulate uRPL by altering MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 expression, but current evidence is insufficient, and further experimental validation is necessary.

This study links uRPL with mitophagy and hypoxia, offering novel insights into the pathogenesis of uRPL. However, given that the findings of this research are based on publicly available transcriptomic datasets and a relatively small sample size, potential selection bias and model overfitting may exist, which inherently preclude definitive causal inferences. Therefore, these results should be interpreted as exploratory indicators, and large-scale prospective cohorts alongside wet-lab functional validation are strictly warranted to confirm the clinical utility of MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3. Furthermore, differences in analytical software versions could also affect the statistical power of our results. Additionally, although the study leveraged cohorts from two geographically distinct regions to minimize population bias, the geographical coverage remains relatively restricted. Despite the integration of multiple algorithms, the identified biomarkers have not undergone functional validation in vivo or in vitro. These limitations underscore the need for validation in larger, independent prospective cohorts to delineate the complex biology of uRPL and to facilitate the discovery of additional biomarkers.

Conclusion

This study investigated the association between mitophagy and hypoxia-related genes and uRPL, identifying MTRNR2L8 and SUMO1P3 as key genes. These two genes showed good diagnostic performance in the ANN model, holding promise as potential biomarkers for uRPL diagnosis and as therapeutic targets. However, further functional validation through in vivo and in vitro experiments is warranted.

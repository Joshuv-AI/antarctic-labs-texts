Soliton dynamics and stability of equilibrium points of the Manakov equation
Authors: Md. Shahidur Rahaman, Mohammad Nazrul Islam, Mohammad Safi Ullah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The simultaneous analysis of stability at equilibrium points and soliton dynamics is crucial to nonlinear systems and to initiatives involving various challenging physical phenomena. In this study, we investigate the Manakov equation, an essential model in nonlinear optical science, plasma physics, and Bose-Einstein condensates. Initially, the unified method is used to identify soliton solutions to this nonlinear problem. Then, a traveling wave transformation is used to change this model into a first-order dynamical structure. We also illustrate the stability of equilibrium locations utilizing the planar dynamics approach. We demonstrate different types of solitons, such as breather waves, periodic waves, local breathers, periodic breathers, and kink waves, by using 2D, 3D, contour, and polar plots. Additionally, an intriguing theorem is included to describe the dynamics of equilibrium points. The results improve our understanding of how to shape soliton waveforms and the stability of equilibrium points. They also offer innovative strategies for controlling nonlinear wave processes.

Introduction

Background

The review of soliton solutions, which exhibit solitary waves that propagate without dispersion, provides significant insight into the dynamics of nonlinear systems1. Solitons are not only beautiful mathematically but have also been used in many areas of physical domains, such as optical fibers2, Bose–Einstein condensates3, fluid dynamics4, shallow water waves5, and others. To describe soliton properties, diverse nonlinear models like, the Fokas–Lenells system6, the Nizhnik–Novikov–Veselov equation7. The ISLW form8, the Manakov model9,10, extended Boussinesq equations11, the Φ4 equation12, the Jimbo–Miwa framework13, the Chen–Lee–Liu equation14, and others arise in these physical contexts. Scholars have developed powerful analytical approaches to identify explicit outcomes, like the Hirota’s bilinear technique15,16, the Sardar sub-equation technique17, the exp-function method18, the unified solver approach19, the φ6-model expansion procedure20, the generalizing Riccati equation mapping algorithm21, the extended Riccati-mapping process22, the tanh-function schemes23,24, and others25–27.

Most people are interested in exploring new areas right now. Solitons enable novel applications of the electromagnetic spectrum within the metamaterials28. In optical networking, they allow the transmission of data over long distances at very high speeds29, and in spinor Bose–Einstein condensates, vector solitons exhibit complex polarization dynamics30. These alterations not only increase the physical importance of solitons but also require amplification of existing solution strategies and the exploration of innovative techniques. In this research, we expand on these concepts by applying the unified technique to the Manakov problem, which involves creating a finite-dimensional computational framework through a Galilean transformation and analyzing its stability and equilibrium properties within a planar structure.

Our results clarify both conventional and latest aspects of soliton theory, offering pathways for further exploration in these rapidly developing fields.

Literature review

In 1974, S. V. Manakov initially came up with the two-component nonlinear Schrödinger model to explain multi-component solitons and how two waves with different polarizations change over time in a nonlinear environment31. Since its inception, the Manakov system has been widely utilized to study optical solitons in multilayered fibers and similar phenomena in plasma physics and Bose-Einstein condensates. So, the number of researchers highly agrees to study this model. As a result, in 2018, the effects of periodic potentials were examined using gain/ loss terms and N-soliton interactions of the Manakov model32. In 2019, the optical solutions of the Manakov model with the trial equation technique were investigated33, and some others were studied in this model.

Existing gaps

Recent Manakov investigations have often distinguished soliton dynamics from equilibrium stability; however, there is a lack of comprehensive studies that combine unified analytical solutions with dynamical-system analysis, leading to incomplete inquiries regarding stability under perturbations, multidimensional enhancements, and experimental validation.

New contribution

As far as we know, our prior researcher often treats soliton dynamics and stability analysis separately. The study includes both the analysis of the stability of the equilibrium points and soliton dynamics. We systematically derive a range of soliton solutions for the Manakov equation by using the unified method. Our planar dynamics approach bridges a gap between abstract dynamical systems theory and physical wave phenomena. Again, we also present an explicit theorem describing the dynamics of equilibrium points in the transformed system. This theorem illuminates conditions under which equilibria are stable or unstable and clarifies how the presence of multiple soliton components influences the local phase portrait. Such a theoretical result is novel for the Manakov equation and strengthens the connection between analytical solutions and dynamical stability.

So, we got different types of waves such as periodic wave, kink wave, and breather wave. These nonlinear waves are different in structure, stability, and physical meaning. A periodic wave repeats the same pattern over equal intervals of space or time36. Its shape remains continuous and smooth, with a steady amplitude and a fixed wavelength. The sound waves, light waves in optical fibers, and water waves on the ocean surface are the real-life example of periodic wave. A kink wave is a type of transition wave that links two distinct stable states37. Unlike periodic waves, it does not repeat its pattern. Instead, it creates a smooth, step-like profile and moves forward as a stable advancing front. Nerve impulses, domain walls in magnetism and shock fronts in traffic flow and so on are the real-life example of kink wave. A breather wave is a localized oscillatory wave packet that appears and disappears periodically38. Its amplitude rises and falls in time or space. The pulse compression in optical fibers, and rogue waves in the ocean are the real-life example of breather wave.

Limitations

Limitations of the unified method

The unified method offers precise closed-form soliton solutions for integrated nonlinear equations. Nonetheless, it is frequently constrained to standardized situations, specifically in the presence of non-integrable perturbations and localized models such as cubic nonlinearity. The unified method alone cannot reveal the dynamic structure, so complementary tools such as traveling wave transformations, initial solution, balance rules, auxiliary equations, and computational software are mandatory. Because these solutions depend on simplified interactions and constrained computational properties, they are less appropriate to real-world systems.

Limitations of the study

Our study focuses on the (1 + 1)-dimensional Manakov equation and does not consider higher-dimensional equations. The Manakov equation is treated deterministically. The numerical simulations illustrate diverse soliton patterns; however, they explore only a finite set of parameters. Consequently, the results do not capture the full parameter space or the effects of small variations of coefficients in nonlinear systems. Although we provide theoretical theorems and numerical visualizations, the study lacks experimental or empirical verification. Practical applications in nonlinear optics or plasma physics may display variations owing to displacement phenomena or structural imperfections not accounted for by our integrable model.

Conclusion

The Manakov equation has been broadly examined for its applications in the stability analysis of equilibrium points and soliton dynamics, significantly enhancing the fields of optics and plasma physics. We studied soliton solutions with the unified method, and the Galilean transformation is used to scrutinize the stability of equilibrium points in this study. Numerical simulations illustrated a wide spectrum of solitary patterns, including breather waves, periodic waves, local breathers, periodic breathers, and kink waves. We applied several analytical and graphical tools, such as 2D, 3D, polar plots, and contour visualizations. By applying such findings, one can explore sophisticated nonlinear dynamics in plasma and optical schemes. This research provides the importance of diverse methodological tools in addressing complex scientific issues and new possibilities for future inquiry.

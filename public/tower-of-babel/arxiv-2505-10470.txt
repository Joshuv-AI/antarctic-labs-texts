Separating balls with partly random hyperplanes with a view to partly random neural networks
Olov Schavemaker, Utrecht University, the Netherlands

Abstract
We derive exact expressions for the probabilities that partly random hyperplanes separate two Euclidean balls. The probability that a fully random hyperplane separates two balls turns out to be significantly smaller than the corresponding probabilities for hyperplanes which are not fully random in certain cases. Our results motivate studying partially random neural networks and provide a first step in this direction.

Introduction

In this short paper we deduce exact expressions for the probabilities that three types of partly random hyperplanes separate two Euclidean balls. Letting
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}[w;b]
be the zero locus of
\displaystyle u\mapsto(w\mid u)-b,
we consider the following three types of partly random hyperplanes:
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}[w;{b}],\mathsf{H}[{w};b],
and
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}[{w};{b}],
where
\displaystyle w,b
are deterministic and
\displaystyle{w},{b}
are random variates.

The hyperplane parametrization
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}[w;b]
is redolent of neural networks by design; a “neuron” in a neural network consists of a nonlinear activation function being applied to a linear function of the form
\displaystyle u\mapsto(w\mid u)-b.

This paper was inspired by
[
2
]
, where the authors showed that random neural networks can make two fairly arbitrary subsets of Euclidean space linearly separable by covering those two sets with small enough balls. In
[
2
]
they consider an architecture with two layers. Their result relies on letting the first layer be wide enough for it to separate all the balls covering one set from all of the balls covering the other set with high probability. This boils down to drawing enough fully random hyperplanes to separate all the balls covering one set from all the balls covering the other set with high probability. Clearly the probability one random hyperplane separates one pair of balls is of interest if one wants to get insight into how many random hyperplanes are needed to separate all pairs of balls with high probability.

Since sufficiently nice manifolds are known to admit nice covering number bounds

One way to avoid these undesirabilities is to forgo learning some of the weights or/and biases; a popular architecture with this design philosophy is the so-called Random Vector Functional Link (RVFL) network

VI
Discussion

In §
I
we suggested that partially random neural network layers could be a golden mean between fully learned and fully random layers. We shall therefore discuss how the separating probability for fully random hyperplanes com- pares to those for random weights and random biases.

Comparing the resultant probabilities in Theorems
IV
and
V
is easy thanks to our Lemma:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\lvert c-x\rvert}{2k}\biggl(\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(n-1)/2}}{\tfrac{n-1}{2}B(\tfrac{n-1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})}-\tfrac{p+r}{p+r+\delta}I(\mathcal{Q};\tfrac{n-1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})\biggr)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(n-1)/2}}{\tfrac{n-1}{2}B(\tfrac{n-1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})}-\tfrac{p+r}{p+r+\delta}I(\mathcal{Q};\tfrac{n-1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(n-1)/2}}{\tfrac{n-1}{2}B(\tfrac{n-1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})}\leqslant I(\mathcal{Q};\tfrac{n-1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})

which makes sense: choosing the bias optimally should improve the separating probability.

Of note is also that
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\in(0,1)\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(n-1)/2}}{\tfrac{n-1}{2}B(\tfrac{n-1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})}<\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{n}{2})}{\tfrac{n-1}{2}\Gamma(\tfrac{n-1}{2})\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(\tfrac{n+1}{2})\sqrt{\pi}}\sim\sqrt{2/(n\pi)}

so
\displaystyle\mathds{P}\{\mathsf{H}[{w};{b}]\text{ separates}\}\preccurlyeq 1/\sqrt{n}
where “
\displaystyle\sim
” & “
\displaystyle\preccurlyeq
” are as in
[
10
, pp. 2f.]
; i.e., fully random hyperplanes separate balls really poorly if the ambient dimension is large regardless of
\displaystyle p,r,\delta.

Note that this is not the case for hyperplanes with random biases (and optimal weights). There the separating probabi- lity can get as close to 1 as desired if
\displaystyle\tfrac{p+r}{p+r+\delta}
is sufficiently small (compared to the ambient dimension).

Comparing the resultant probabilities in Theorems
III
and
V

\displaystyle\mathds{P}\{\mathsf{H}[{w};{b}]\text{ separates}\mid{w}\}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2k}\Bigl(\lvert c-x\rvert-p-r\Bigr)=\delta/(2k)

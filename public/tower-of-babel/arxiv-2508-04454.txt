On the Classification of n \bm{n} -Valued Monoids and Groups of Order 3
Mikhail Kornev

Abstract
The article presents a complete classification of n n -valued monoids and groups of order 3. Important corollaries of this result are discussed.

Introduction

In 1971, V. M. Buchstaber and S. P. Novikov proposed a construction motivated by the theory of characteristic classes
[
BN71
]
. This construction describes a multiplication in which the product of any pair of elements is a multiset of
n
points. An axiomatic definition of
n
-valued groups and the first results of their algebraic theory were obtained in a subsequent series of works by V. M. Buchstaber. Currently, the theory of
n
-valued (formal, finite, discrete, topological, and algebro-geometric) groups and their applications in various areas of mathematics and mathematical physics are being developed by a number of authors; see
[
Buc75
,
Kho75

,

BVEP96
,

Buc06
,
BVG22

]
and the references therein. A comparative analysis of the theories of 1-valued and
n
-valued groups can be found in
[
BB12
]
.

We refer the reader to
[
Buc06
]
for the foundational definitions and constructions in the theory of
n
-valued groups.

We introduce the notion of a non-reversible
n
-valued group (Definition
5
). It turns out that, up to now, no explicit constructions of non-reversible groups have appeared in the existing literature. As noted by V. Buchstaber, an incorrect example of a non-reversible 2-valued group was given in
[
BB12
, page 246]
. In the present note, the author constructs a family of non-reversible
n
-valued groups of order 3 for all
n\geqslant 3
(Example
3
).

A natural and well-known question is the classification of
n
-valued monoids and groups of order 3 up to isomorphism. In
[
BVEP96
]
, the notion of involutive
n
-valued groups was introduced (we refer to them as
\bigstar
-involutive, see Definition 6). It was shown that such groups can be identified with certain combinatorial algebras. A specific series (
9
) of
\bigstar
-involutive groups on three points was also constructed. The complete classification of all
\bigstar
-involutive
n
-valued groups of order 3 was achieved in
[
Pon24
,
GPV25
]
, based on the classification of all single-valued finite groups of rank 3 (see references therein).

The main result of this work, is the complete classification of
n
-valued monoids and groups of order 3 (see Proposition from Section
2
), along with important consequences arising from a comparison with the results of
[
GPV25
]
. This leads in Section

n
-valued groups. Corollary
1
reproduces the classical result
[

that there are exactly 7 isomorphism classes of monoids of order 3. Corollary

says that there exist only two families of
n

2
Classification

We introduce the following:

Definition 1
.

An
n
-valued monoid
is a set
X
equipped with an operation

\ast:X\times X\to\Sym^{n}(X),

where
\Sym^{n}(X)=X^{\times n}/S_{n}
is the set of unordered
n
-tuples of elements of
X
. This operation must satisfy all axioms of an
n
-valued group from
[
Buc06
]
, except for the axiom asserting the existence of an inverse map, that is:

•

Associativity.
The
n^{2}
-multisets

\displaystyle[x*w\mid w\in y\ast z],

\displaystyle[w\ast z\mid w\in x*y]

coincide.

•

Unit.
There exists an element
e\in X
such that

e\ast x=x\ast e=[x,x,…,x]

for every
x\in X
.

We say that the order of an
n
-valued monoid
X
is
k
if
|X|=k
. Throughout, we identify the set
\Sym^{n}(X)
with a subset of the ordinary free commutative monoid
\Free(X)
generated by
X
. In this way, we regard the
n
-valued group
X
as an
n
-valued analogue of a monoid algebra.

Consider the set
X=\{e,x_{1},x_{2},…,x_{n-1}\}
(we assume
x_{0}=e
). Suppose
X
is equipped with the structure of an
n
-valued monoid. Then the corresponding multiplication table can be conveniently written as:

\Xx^{\circledast 2}=A\Xx,

where
\Xx
denotes the column vector
(e,x_{1},x_{2},…,x_{n-1})^{T}
, and
A
is an
n\times n
matrix whose rows record the multiplicities of the elements
e,x_{1},…,x_{n-1}
in the product
x_{i}\ast x_{j}
for
i,j=0,…,n-1
. Moreover, in each row of the matrix
A
, the sum of all entries equals
n
. Finally,
\Xx^{\circledast 2}=\Xx\circledast\Xx
denotes the Kronecker product of the vector
\Xx
with itself, taken with respect to the operation
\ast
instead of the usual tensor product
\otimes
. For example,

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{\circledast 2}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\ast x_{1}\\
x_{1}\ast x_{2}\\
x_{2}\ast x_{1}\\
x_{2}\ast x_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The associativity conditions then take the form:

(\Xx\circledast\Xx)\circledast\Xx=\Xx\circledast(\Xx\circledast\Xx).

(3)

We will need the following:

Definition 2
.

An
n
-valued monoid
X
is said to be
isomorphic
to an
n\textquoteright
-valued monoid
X\textquoteright
if there exists a bijection
\varphi:X\to X\textquoteright
preserving the unit,
\varphi(e)=e\textquoteright
, such that

\frac{m^{z}_{x,y}}{n}=\frac{m^{\varphi(z)}_{\varphi(x),\varphi(y)}}{n\textquoteright},

where
m^{z}_{x,y}
denotes the multiplicity of the element
z
in the product
x\ast y
.

Recall from
[
Buc06
, Lemma 1]
that for any natural number
m
, one can construct an
mn
-valued monoid
G\textquoteright
on the same set
X
from an
n
-valued monoid
G
with multiplication
\mu
, via the diagonal map
\diag_{m}(x)=mx
:

\mu\textquoteright:X\times X\xrightarrow{\mu}\Sym^{n}X\xrightarrow{\diag_{m}}\Sym^{mn}{X}.

(4)

We are now ready to state one of the main results of this paper:

Proposition
.

Let
X=\{e,x_{1},x_{2}\}
and

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{\circledast 2}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&a_{1}&a_{2}\\
b_{0}&b_{1}&b_{2}\\
d_{0}&d_{1}&d_{2}\\
c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}\end{pmatrix}\Xx=B\Xx.

(5)

Then every
n
-valued monoid on the set
X
is represented by one of the following matrices
B=B_{j}
, provided that all fractional expressions yield non-negative integers:

B_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&n-a_{0}&0\\
0&0&n\\
0&0&n\\
c_{0}&\frac{c_{0}n}{a_{0}}&n-c_{0}-\frac{c_{0}n}{a_{0}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

B_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&a_{1}&n-a_{0}-a_{1}\\
b_{0}&b_{1}&n-b_{0}-b_{1}\\
b_{0}&b_{1}&n-b_{0}-b_{1}\\
\frac{b_{0}(n-b_{0}-b_{1})+a_{0}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{0}}{n-a_{0}-a_{1}}&\frac{(b_{0}+b_{1})(n-b_{1})}{n-a_{0}-a_{1}}&\frac{(n-b_{0}-b_{1})^{2}+n(b_{1}-a_{0}-a_{1})+a_{1}b_{0}-a_{0}b_{1}}{n-a_{0}-a_{1}}\end{pmatrix},

B_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&n&0\\
0&n&0\\
0&n&0\\
c_{0}&c_{1}&n-c_{0}-c_{1}\end{pmatrix},\ B_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}0&n&0\\
0&0&n\\
0&0&n\\
0&c_{1}&n-c_{1}\end{pmatrix},

B_{5}=\begin{pmatrix}0&n&0\\
0&n&0\\
0&0&n\\
0&0&n\end{pmatrix},\ B_{6}=\begin{pmatrix}0&n&0\\
0&0&n\\
0&n&0\\
0&0&n\end{pmatrix}.

Monoids from different series may be isomorphic to each other:

B_{1}\cong B_{3}\Leftrightarrow c_{0}=0,c_{1}^{\prime}=0,\frac{a_{0}}{n-a_{0}}=\frac{c_{0}^{\prime}}{n-c_{0}^{\prime}}.

Here and below, the primed variables denote the entries of the matrix corresponding to the second monoid (in this case, belonging to the series
B_{3}
).

B_{2}\cong B_{3}\Leftrightarrow\left[\begin{array}[]{c}c_{0}^{\prime}=c_{1}^{\prime}=0,\\
\\
b_{0}=b_{1}=0,\frac{a_{0}}{c_{0}^{\prime}}=\frac{a_{1}}{n^{\prime}-c_{0}^{\prime}-c_{1}^{\prime}}=\frac{n-a_{0}-a_{1}}{c_{1}^{\prime}}.\end{array}\right.

B_{2}\cong B_{4}\Leftrightarrow\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1}=n,b_{1}=0,c_{1}^{\prime}=0,\\
\\
a_{0}=b_{0}=0,b_{1}=n,\frac{a_{1}}{n-a_{1}}=\frac{n^{\prime}-c_{1}^{\prime}}{c_{1}^{\prime}}.\end{array}\right.

B_{3}\cong B_{4}\Leftrightarrow c_{0}=c_{1}=0,c_{1}^{\prime}=0.

For each
k=1,3,4
, monoids within the same series
B_{k}
are isomorphic
\Leftrightarrow
the corresponding matrices are proportional.

Monoids from the series
B_{2}
are isomorphic
\Leftrightarrow
the corresponding matrices are proportional, or one of the matrices is the image of a matrix satisfying

b_{0}=n-2a_{0}-2a_{1},\quad b_{1}=b_{2}=a_{0}+a_{1},

(6)

under the isomorphism
\varphi:X\to X
, defined by
x_{1}\mapsto x_{2}
.

Monoids from the series
B_{5}
or
B_{6}
are diagonals (
4
) of single-valued (non-isomorphic) monoids from (
1
). Therefore, there is exactly one monoid in each of these series up to isomorphism.

Proof.

The associativity conditions (
3
) define in the parameter space
\R^{12}
an algebraic set given by the intersection of 81 quadrics.

This set is contained in the solutions of the following system of 18 equations:

(\Y\circledast\Y)\circledast\Y=\Y\circledast(\Y\circledast\Y),

(7)

where
\Y=(x_{1},x_{2})^{T}
. For instance, the three equations corresponding to the associativity condition
(x_{1}\ast x_{1})\ast x_{2}=x_{1}\ast(x_{1}\ast x_{2})
take the form:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &a_{1}b_{0}-a_{0}b_{1}-b_{0}b_{2}+a_{2}c_{0}&=0,\\
&a_{2}c_{1}-b_{1}b_{2}-nb_{0}&=0,\\
&a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}-b_{2}^{2}+a_{2}c_{2}+na_{0}&=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Solving the system (
7
) of quadratic equations over the reals, subject to the conditions
n\neq 0
and the requirement that the sum of entries in each row equals
n
(four more linear equations), using the
Reduce
function in
Wolfram Mathematica
, yields a list of 26 families of 12-dimensional vectors. By analyzing this list and restricting to non-negative integer solutions, we obtain the 6 series of matrices listed in the proposition.

As is easy to check, the associativity conditions for cubes
(x_{i}\ast x_{i})\ast x_{i}=x_{i}\ast(x_{i}\ast x_{i})
and for expressions involving the unit hold for all these series: for commutative cases this is automatic, and for the two noncommutative series it can be directly verified. Hence, systems (
3
) and (
7
) are equivalent, and we obtain a complete list of
n
-valued monoids on three elements.

The isomorphism statements follow directly from the structure of the series and from the fact that every isomorphism of
n
-valued monoids on
X
is given either by the identity or by the map
\varphi(x_{1})=x_{2}
.

Definition 3
.

An
n

X

n
-valued monoid equipped with an inverse map
\inv:X\to X

x\ast\inv(x)\ni e,\quad\inv(x)\ast x\ni e.

Corollary 2

Every

B_{1}
or

a_{0}c_{0}>0,

b_{0}+a_{0}(b_{0}(n-b_{0}-b_{1})+a_{0}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{0})>0.

The statement follows from examining the first columns of the matrices
B_{j}

Corollary 3
.

Every commutative
n
-valued monoid of order 3 belongs to one of the first four series. Every noncommutative monoid is a diagonal (
4
) of one of the two noncommutative single-valued monoids (
1
). In particular, all
n
-valued groups of order 3 are commutative, and there exist only two noncommutative multivalued monoids up to isomorphism.

Let us note that the commutativity of
n
-valued groups of order 3 was first established in
[
Yag00
, Lemma 10]
. That work also introduced an approach to the classification of
n
-valued groups in
\R^{3}
using the theory of algebraic varieties and linear deformations of group
n

-valued groups of order 3 was obtained there.

Following V. Buchstaber (private communication), for every
n
-valued group on a set
X
, in addition to the inverse map
\inv:X\to X
, we define a multivalued inverse map
\Inv:X\to\Pp(X)
:

Definition 4
.

The map
\Inv:X\to\Pp(X)
takes values in the set of all finite subsets of
X
. It is defined by

\Inv(x)=\{y\in X\mid x\ast y\ni e,\quad y\ast x\ni e\}.

Definition 5
.

An
n
-valued group has a uniquely defined inverse map if and only if
\inv=\Inv
. We call such groups
reversible
; otherwise, we call them
non-reversible
.

Example 1
.

Clearly, every coset group is reversible.

It was observed by V. Buchstaber that the example given in
[
BB12
, page 246]
as a non-reversible 2-valued group is, in fact, incorrect. It arises as the reduction modulo
n
of the 2-valued group
\G_{2}
(see, e. g.,
[
BK25
, Example 2]
). Namely, consider the set of (non-negative) residues
X={0,1,…,n-1}
modulo
n
with the operation

x\ast y=[x+y\ \mathrm{mod}\ n,\ |x-y|\ \mathrm{mod}\ n].

This set
X
with such an operation does not form a 2-valued group, since associativity fails:

\displaystyle(1\ast 1)\ast(n-1)

\displaystyle=[0,2]\ast(n-1)

\displaystyle=[n-1,n-1,1,n-3]

\displaystyle\neq[1,1,n-1,n-3]

\displaystyle=1\ast[0,n-2]

\displaystyle=1\ast(1\ast(n-1)).

This observation leads naturally to the question of whether non-reversible
n
-valued groups actually exist.

Example 2
.

It is easy to see that every
n
-valued group
\G
on the singleton set
\{0\}
is a diagonal construction
[
Buc06
, Lemma 1]
of the trivial group, and is therefore reversible. All
n
-valued groups
\G
on the two-element set
\{0,1\}
with unit
0
are also reversible: each such
n
-valued group is determined by a pair of non-negative integers
(a,b)
with
a+b=n
,
a>0
, since there is only one nontrivial relation:
1\ast 1=a\cdot 0+b\cdot 1
.

Example 3
.

For each
n\geqslant 3
, there exists a non-reversible commutative cyclic
[
BV24
, Definition 1.3]

-valued group
\X_{n}
on the set
\{0,1,2\}
(with unit

), in which the inverse map
\inv
may be defined in four different ways:
\Inv(1)=\Inv(2)=\{1,2\}
. The multiplication in this group is given by:

\displaystyle 1\ast 1

\displaystyle=0+(n-1)\cdot 2,

\displaystyle 1\ast 2

\displaystyle=0+1+(n-2)\cdot 2,

\displaystyle 2\ast 2

\displaystyle=0+2\cdot 1+(n-3)\cdot 2.

It is clear that

belongs to the series

Corollary 4

An

b_{0}(a_{0}+b_{0}(n-b_{0}-b_{1})+a_{0}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{0})>0.

An

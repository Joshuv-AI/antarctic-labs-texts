An innovative hybrid ANN inception model for detecting and preventing man-in-the-middle attacks in smart networks for shielding sensitive data
Authors: V. Kandasamy, A. Ameelia Roseline
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) attacks represent a significant cybersecurity challenge, particularly within the rapidly growing domain of smart networks and Internet of Things (IoT) environments. These attacks involve an adversary intercepting and potentially altering the communication between two parties who believe they are communicating directly with each other. As the deployment of smart devices and autonomous systems increases, the risk of MitM attacks escalates, given the sensitive nature of the data being transmitted and the critical functions these systems perform. This paper presents a novel approach to detecting and preventing MitM attacks by leveraging a Hybrid ANN-Inception Model (HAINet). The proposed model integrates the dynamic feature extraction capabilities of Inception modules with the flexibility of Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) to identify and mitigate these sophisticated cyber threats. Using the Smart Home Intrusion Detection Dataset from Kaggle, the methodology involves advanced data preprocessing, feature extraction via AutoEncoders, and optimization through hybrid learning algorithms. The HAINet model executed in Jupyter Notebook and achieved an outstanding accuracy of 98.69%, outperforming traditional models in both detection accuracy and robustness across various network conditions. The results demonstrate the model’s efficacy in shielding sensitive data from interception and manipulation, making it a valuable tool for enhancing security in smart networks.

Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) technologies have experienced a rapid proliferation, resulting in the interconnected smart devices in very many fields of health care, transport, smart home, industrial automation, and smart cities1,2. The latest estimates by the world show that over 15 billion devices are connected to the IoT in 2023 and will cross 29 billion by 2030 due to the development of edge computing, low-power embedded systems, and fast wireless connectivity3. Though this growth makes intelligent automation and data-driven service possible, the cyber-attack surface increases considerably with smart networks being exposed to more advanced security threats. Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) attack is one of the most hazardous and hard to trace types of intrusion into the system4,5.

A Man-in-the-Middle attack is a type of attack that involves interception, relaying, and possibly alteration of communication between two, legitimate parties that think that they are communicating directly with one another6. In comparison to traditional attacks, which can leave physical evidence, MitM attacks can be executed in the background such that they allow attackers to overhear important information, alter sent data, or inject malicious data without necessarily arousing any immediate suspicion. In intelligent network systems, those attacks may disrupt sensor values, control data, affect the decision-making process, causing devastating safety, privacy, and operational impacts7,8. MitM attacks are even more significant in intelligent systems, where the machine learning and deep learning models take importance. Such systems often rely on the constant feeds of distributed information. When opponents intercept or alter this data, the learning models might be used to provide false predictions, incorrectly categorize events, or cause unsafe behavior9,10. As an example, controlled sensor streams can result in dangerous control actions in autonomous systems, and identity spoofing or unauthorized access in biometric authentication systems. These vulnerabilities indicate that it is not sufficient to protect the communication channel only, and smart detecting algorithms that can analyze traffic behavior and detect minor anomalies are also needed11.

The conventional security measures, such as encryption protocols, authentication system, and firewalls, are significant to the security of network communications12,13. Nonetheless, these methods are mostly concerned with the prevention and perimeter protection and fail to identify more advanced attacks once an enemy manages to enter the communication channel14,15. Besides, the heterogeneous, high dynamism, and resource-constrained characteristics of the IoT environment restrict the usefulness of intrusion detection systems that depend on heavyweight cryptography and rule systems. Consequently, there has been an increased desire to use the tools of machine learning and deep learning to create adaptive intrusion detection systems that can learn intricate patterns directly out of the network data16,17. Current machine learning-based MitM detection methods usually consider hand-designed features and single-model designs that might not associate with the scale properties of network traffic or suit the changing attack objectives18,19. The deep learning models are more powerful; however, they have drawbacks of lack of feature diversity, noise sensitivity, and poor parameter choices. Such challenges drive the emergence of hybrid architecture that combines complementary learning paradigm and uses intelligent optimization procedure20.

This paper presents a proposal of a HAINet to detect and prevent MitM attacks in smart networks. The suggested framework fuses adaptability to learn with ANNs and multi-scale feature extraction ability of Inception modules. The inception modules take the input data through parallel convolutional paths through which the model can simultaneously extract fine and coarse-grained traffic patterns, with ANN layers doing high-level reasoning. In an effort to improve performance, AutoEncoder feature extraction is used to infer compact and informative latent representations and a hybrid optimization strategy that combines the Genetic Algorithms (GA) and Simulated Annealing (SA) is used in optimal parameter tuning. The performance of the proposed HAINet framework is assessed with the help of the SHIDD downloaded on Kaggle that includes the realistic traffic of smart thermostats both normal and under attack. Large-scale experimental evidence shows that HAINet can be more accurately detected, resistant to noisy and encrypted traffic, and generalized well than more traditional machine learning and deep learning models. These findings suggest that the suggested solution is a credible and scalable method of protecting smart networks against MitM attacks.

Related work

Heterogeneous devices and protocols were found to be the most susceptible to cyber-attacks in zyber-Physical Industrial Internet of things (CPS-IIoT) environments. An intrusion detection framework named Pearson correlation coefficient with agglomerative clustering, BiLSTM and scaled dot-product attention was introduced that was privacy-preserving to CPS-IIoT security. Agglomerative clustering and attention mechanism was adaptively reinforced to make emphasis on informative traffic chunks and maintains privacy of the data21. The accuracy, precision, recall, and AUC were extremely high on the UNSW-NB15 and X-IIoTID dataset in performance evaluation. The SHAP-based explanations were added to increase the interpretability and reliability. The findings showed that model of attention and feature processing with privacy consideration were highly effective compared to the state of the art.

An explainable anomaly detection framework named Anomaly-Agent that is powered by LLM was proposed to work with IoT and IIoT edge settings. The framework used a reasoning-action pipeline that combined external knowledge retrieval, domain tools, memory augmentation, as well as SHAP explanations22. Sensitivity customization and adaptation to the changing zero-day threats were facilitated. Edge-IIoTset and CIC-IoT2023 datasets evaluation reached competitive accuracy and low false alarm rates and zero-day recall, which is better than traditional machine learning models and LLM-based baselines (GPT-4o and Claude 3.5). The tool-assisted reasoning and explainability were identified as the determinants of performance gains, whereas the latency and adversarial robustness were defined as its current challenges.

An attention-based architecture called CWFLAM-VAE was developed to solve the class imbalance in network intrusion detection, which is a combination of Class-Wise Focal Loss, Variational Autoencoder, and Extreme Gradient Boosting. Artificial rare-class attack samples had been created and the original feature distributions had been maintained23. The experiments with NSL-KDD and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 datasets showed that the methods are significantly advanced compared to traditional sampling methods and classifiers. F-scores of high values when it comes to rare attacks and low false positive rates were obtained. Findings had shown that generative modeling in combination with attention mechanisms and ensemble learning were effective in reducing the class imbalance and detecting rare-attacks.

A network-based intrusion detection system called Ioot-Defender (which is an edge centered model) was proposed to secure the IoT network in smart cities. The structure was a combination of a Modified Genetic Algorithm to select features and a GA-tuned LSTM classifier as well as using focal loss to deal with the unbalanced classes24. The measures of BoT-IoT, UNSW-NB15, and N-BaIoT datasets with Raspberry Pi devices showed a high accuracy, detection rate, and reasonable training time. The optimal feature selection and hyperparameter optimization improved LSTM results significantly. Findings revealed that lightweight IDS implementation on edge servers could be used to detect real-time attacks on the IoT.

An ensemble learning method that was a hybrid was elaborated to do intrusion detection in SCADA and industrial sensor networks. The PCA was utilized to extract features used in the classification, and the feature optimizer was utilized, which was Grey Wolf Optimizer to optimize ensemble classifiers of Naive Bayes and SVM with majority voting25. Experiments on gas pipeline, water system, and UNSW-NB15 data sets showed that they were highly accurate, precise, recalled, and detected. PCA and GWO optimization were found to be performing much better compared to ensembles without feature selection and parameter optimization. Results revealed that optimized ensemble learning was very suitable to high-dimensional industrial data and intricate intrusion cases.

A ResNet50-1D-CNN, which is a lightweight transfer learning-based intrusion detection method was proposed to cyber-physical systems. The fine-tuning of pre-trained convolutional features was done using the Adagrad optimizer to lower the training complexity and runtime. On data sets of UNSW-NB15 and HAI, performance assessment using the data showed that accuracy, recall, and precision, and F1-score were high26. Coefficient scores of attributes were used to analyze feature relevance, to determine influential attributes. Findings showed that transfer learning was able to deal with small labeled data as well as overcome computational limitations without poor detecting against various CPS cyber-attacks.

In order to attain higher data rates and safe traffic in communication, today scientists are working on exploring new multimedia networks that use the technology of artificial intelligence (AI). The threat is not imaginary and so are privacy issues of users who wish to protect their data. Another attack, a popular one, is the MITM attack where the attacker masquerades as an actual customer or business to gain sensitive information or introduce malicious code. This limitation on the application of cryptographic methods, both accessible and deployed to mobile networks, etc., has in its turn prompted the invention of new methods of a dynamic assessment/trust establishment alongside the signals themselves on the physical layer. The distance-time directional delay (DTDD) model is introduced to preclude/lessen the MITM attack in diverse settings27. To ensure that the suggested methods are valid, their performance is measured with real 5G millimeter-wave channel models and system configurations in both indoor hotspots (InH) and urban micro-cellular (UMi) propagation case scenarios. These strategies and ideas are worked out on the examples of simulations based on an mm-Wave 5G channel simulator named NYUSIM, supplemented with a series of machine learning models such as XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) and LGBM (Light Greedy Best-First). It was demonstrated by numerical simulations that the detection accuracy can be driven to 100% and 99.5%/98% in InH/UMi environment, respectively.

By utilizing Gaussian Process Regression, attack detection rates are higher, and the attack misclassification rate is lower.

And as wireless technology continues to advance quickly, those future impacts may be even larger. Unfortunately, MANETs are threatened by security flaws in inter-node communication, leading to various attack types like MITM. The impact of attacker tactics targeting MANETs-based MITM attacks is focused such as message-delayed and message-dropped in particular29. Their findings shows that on MANET legal nodes, the impact is considerable, leading to an increase in both E2ED and PLD as well as a number of corrupted messages. And finally, their solution used symmetry or asymmetry cryptographies to protect communications of lawful nodes from MitM attacks.

These are the possible suggested countermeasures that are founded on the issues of consensus variable synchronization that are aimed at detecting healthy MG clusters or converters prior to occurrence, which will potentially prevent conditions capable of resulting in cascade effects. Its usefulness is proved by long-term domain simulations in MATLAB/Simulink in various circumstances of bidirectional and unidirectional MITM, communication delays and theory. Finally, the controller implementation hardware-in-loop (cHIL) is implemented to check that MGs can run in a safe real-time setting without aid by any side-attack covert MITM.

Specifically, that attack was directed against the use of a desktop browser by the victim to go through the service. In31 it demonstrates how that attack can be enhanced to take advantage of mobile environments and how it can also acquire persistence. The new kind of assault is called MobileApp-in-the-Middle (MAitM). Just like BitM, MAitM doesn’t require the victim’s platform to install any malware.

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in using state-of-the-art machine learning and deep learning methods to deal with more complex cyber threats, including Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) attacks, as well as adversarial data manipulation. The presented hybrid deep learning model is called the AEXB model which includes the extraction of features using the AutoEncoder model and XGBoost classification to identify MitM attacks in the smart home setting32. Their method showed a higher anomaly detection performance and had an accuracy rate of 97.24% on the Intrusion Detection in Smart Home (IDSH) dataset which has given potential to hybrid learning models in real time network security. Equally, adversarial examples have been examined in cyber-physical systems, which are deep learning based, especially unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs)33. Their analysis found out that adversarial attacks are able to compromise deep-learning-based systems in navigation and control, and it is crucial to build a robust AI-based security system that would be resistant to adversarial attacks. A continuation of this work, an adversarial false data injection attack framework, implemented with the use of an ADDM, was proposed to attack multi-label detection systems in power grid systems34. They showed that well-designed perturbation can be used to beat the deep learning-based detection systems, revealing vulnerabilities of the present cybersecurity models. Besides these AI-based investigations, one can address a thorough analysis of MitM attacks in the current communication networks that discusses the methods of ARP poisoning, DNS spoofing and ICMP manipulation that allow attackers to intercept and alter network communications35. Though the studies have made a substantial contribution to the research on cybersecurity, most of its solutions are either concentrated on analysis of attacks or are based on the single model architecture which can be inefficient to identify subtle multi-scale anomalies in smart networks in a dynamic environment. Hence, the requirement still exists to have stronger hybrid architectures that can siphon out the complex characteristics of traffic and drive them to evolving attack patterns. To overcome this problem, the proposed Hybrid ANN-Inception Network (HAINet) provides multi-scale feature extraction with adaptive learning features, which allows detecting and preventing MitM attacks in smart network infrastructures.

The related studies on MitM attacks within the context of 5G, IoT, and MANETs have made significant strides in enhancing security measures; however, they also present certain limitations. While 5G networks offer the potential for advanced technological innovations, the cryptographic approaches applied at the physical layer are often insufficient in fully mitigating MitM attacks, particularly under complex real-world conditions. Even though traditional ANNs and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have shown good performance in intrusion detection tasks, they also have a number of limitations, which are inherent to them in handling complex Man-in-the-Middle attack cases. Normal ANNs are mostly based on fully connected layers and do not have explicit means of localizing structural patterns and spatial correlations of the network traffic representations. Likewise, the conventional CNN models have a fixed-size convolutional kernel that is stacked in series, which limits their capability to capture small-scale variation at a packet level, and coarse-scale variation at a flow level. Consequently, these models tend to fail at detecting delicate, multi-scale anomalies, which describe advanced MitM attacks, in particular, in the presence of noisy or encrypted traffic. Inception modules address all these limitations by using parallel convolutional branches with varying sizes of kernel, which allows extracting features of different receptive field sizes in the same layer. This multi-scale processing enables the model to be able to model both short range dependencies (e.g., the timing anomalies of packets) and long-range dependencies (e.g., behavioral anomalies in sessions) without the model having overly deep network stacks. In addition, the use of 1 × 1 convolutions in Inception modules decreases dimensionality and cost of computing but does not compromise on discriminative information. The above characteristics render Inception-based architectures especially appropriate in heterogeneous and multi-dimensional network traffic analysis, which in turn justifies their inclusion in the ANN layers of the proposed HAINet framework.

Methodology

The proposed methodology uses a HAINet to identify MitM attacks and a combined mitigation controller to prevent the further occurrence of the former by actively inhibiting the latter using automated network-level response mechanisms. The model combines the advanced feature extraction capabilities of Inception networks with the adaptability of ANNs. Comprehensive data preprocessing, including AutoEncoder-based feature extraction, data normalization, and outlier detection, prepares the data for analysis. Hybrid optimization techniques, integrating GA with SA, enhance the model’s performance by optimizing its parameters. The methodology is tested across various network conditions to ensure robustness, making it a reliable solution for securing smart networks against sophisticated cyber threats. Dataset description

Smart Home Intrusion Detection Dataset (SHIDD) is obtained from Kaggle Platform36. This dataset is valuable for enhancing and creating smart home security systems by detecting intrusions cases. It is particularly aimed at detecting a clear separation between normal transactional data and patterns and other forms of IT cyber threats like MITM and DoS attacks. These threats are tried and tested under the realistic context of smart thermostats, which is a constituent of IoT homes. The dataset is more extensive than other similar datasets, making it invaluable, holding 4,143 data points adding 22 various features to each of them. These features capture specific actions and states of smart thermostat and its contiguity with the other IoT gadgets in the network. Such detailed data capture means that the steps or actions that are expected are well defined and clearly defined, while those that are malicious and indicative of a cyber-attack are also easily identifiable. An essential objective of this dataset is to provide the grounds for the proper training of machine learning models in identifying distinct types of activities taking place in smart homes while simultaneously providing protection against possible intrusions.

Even though SHIDD is gathered in the context of a smart thermostat, most of its aspects are based on communications patterns, device interaction patterns, and transaction-level performance, which implicitly indicate network-layer anomalies. Man-in-the-Middle attacks, being historically defined at the network level (e.g., ARP spoofing, session hijacking, packet interception), eventually result in anomalous device behavior, timing anomalies and inconsistent request-response patterns at the application and device levels. Connection frequency, request rate, pattern of command execution, response delays, authentication attempt, and device state transitions are features used in SHIDD as indirect pointers of communication manipulation under the hood. Indicatively, in MitM attacks, abnormal latency, replaying, duplicating commands, or irregular acknowledgment by the device may be added by receiving and sending messages. These impacts are manifested in SHIDD by non-normal temporal sequence, undesired state transitions, and non-conformity to normal operational profiles. Moreover, SHIDD hosts tagged types of intrusions that consist of, abnormal access behavior, command injection and unauthorized control attempts. These forms of attacks are consistent with the usual consequences of the MitM situation, where malicious actors either inject malicious commands or alter the content of the transmitted data once the sessions are intercepted. Consequently, although SHIDD does not directly identify low-level packet spoofing attack, the behavioral effects of the MitM behavior on the device level and the session level are captured, both of which can be used in supervised learning-based detection.

Data preprocessing

Data pre-processing, hence, is an important step when it comes to machine learning, more so in aspects such as intrusion detection where raw data and its format may pose impact on the models.

Handling missing values

Another important step of the preprocessing of the data deals with the case when data is missing in order to avoid the negative impact of such gaps on the final data-driven model. Smart home intrusion detection is one of the areas where every single feature could be suspicious, and missing values must be handled carefully. A number of imputation techniques, as described below, are valid for this purpose. In the case of continuous variables, the mean or the median of the dataset is mostly an efficient way of estimating the missing values since; If the data is abnormal, the median is usually used because this is least affected by outliers. In the case of categorical data, missing values are often completed with the help of the mode, or with a special code indicating the absence of data. This approach is beneficial in preserving the integrity of the dataset to reduce possibility of models learning from incomplete information, which could greatly improve the reliability of security alarms. Data scaling

It is important to scale the features especially when a given feature has a very wide range of values. As in intrusion detection systems, values might be the number of logins or network traffic while these values can have very different scales. It means that if we do not scale features correctly some models like KNN, or SVM, which use distance calculations inside, will start to favor the variables that have much larger range. Standard Scaler among the most commonly used pre-processing technique, helps standardize the data by bringing their mean to zero and variance to one, and enhances the independence of features and reduces the amendments found high-range features making them more influential than other features. Encoding categorical variables

Ever since IDS mostly address protocols, services or even packets, which are all non-numeric by nature, the feature space is most typically categorical. Machine learning algorithms, however, need to get numeric input for the processing to be conducted on them. A key procedure used in the Machine learning algorithms to handle categorical data which the algorithms interpret erroneously as ordinal data is One-Hot Encoding. This method creates a new feature for all possible values of the categorical feature; it can greatly increase the dimension of the data but it enhances the interpretability of models and would not allow any distortion interpretation that would occur in the ordinal encoding. Data reduction

Out of the techniques, data reduction using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is more effective in case of high data dimensionality as can be observed in smart homes through multiple sensors and interfaces. In other words, PCA aims to find a number of new variables with the maximal variance and which are created by linear combination of the original set of variables. This transformation maintains the most important features with maximum accuracy and is very useful when there is a need to analyze large quantity of data since efficiency of the intrusion detection is a big issue.

Outlier detection

Outliers are essential for intrusion detection as the unusual data may contain or may be an intrusion or may be just an anomaly. The most useful of these is the Interquartile Range (IQR) method. It computes the spread of data between 25th and 75th percentiles and adds as out of range all the values that are out of 1. IQR defines 5 times the extent above or below the quartiles Q1 and Q3. Outlier handling is crucial to obtain a reliable model for training and performance that is not skewed by outliers; this means that the detection model has to act as expected when confronted with real outliers and not with numerical noise.6\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{x}^{{\prime\:}}=\mathrm{log}\left(x\right)\:$$\end{document}

Tabular-to-feature map reshaping for inception processing

The original SHIDD is presented in form of tabular feature vectors since each sample is in the form of a one-dimensional array of numerical instances. As convolutional and Inception build constructions are by their nature designed to work with multi-dimensional feature maps, a structured transform is used to transfer these tabular vectors into spatially structured representations. Each sample of input data is modeled by a small latent vector that summarizes the most important features following the extraction of features through AutoEncoder-based feature extraction. This latent vector is then transformed back into a two-dimensional feature grid, by laying the elements of the latent dimension into a fixed-size matrix (e.g. 8 × 8 or 4 × 8). This reshaping is not going to change the features, but rearrange them into a spatial arrangement, upon which convolutional filters can learn the local correlation of the neighboring features. The 16 dimensional latent is transformed to a 4 × 4 feature map:13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:z\in\:{R}^{16}\to\:Z\in\:{R}^{4\times\:4\times\:1}\:$$\end{document}

This transformation enables convolutional processing while preserving feature values. In order to maintain semantic consistency, features with similar statistical or network-level features (e.g., traffic volume measures, timing features, protocol related attributes, and error statistics) are clustered and placed in adjacency grid. Convolution kernels are made to describe meaningful local patterns and not arbitrary spatial relationships with this organization. After undergoing a process of converting the data into feature maps, the information is sent to the Inception modules. These feature maps are processed by parallel convolutional filters of various sizes (1 × 1,3 × 3 and 5 × 5) so that the model can learn fine grained feature interactions, medium range dependencies and global contextual patterns in one layer. The outputs of these parallel branches are reassembled and fed into the next ANN layers in order to get higher level reasoning and classification.

Hybrid optimization models

The hybrid optimization models are the more sophisticated version of the computational techniques that is based on the integration of two or more optimization algorithms to overcome the limitations of each of the incorporated optimization algorithm. This synergy is more useful when in design space there is more than one model to solve a problem and one model is not sufficient to explore all possible solutions, or it gets stuck in local optima. The reason for combining two optimization models is to increase the rate and quality of finding the solution by several times. For example, one model is well suited for identifying the global best solution (global search), the other is good for refining the solution in its neighborhood space (local search). Integrating these models, it is possible to achieve a significant efficiency of the search for the best solution in large and complex spaces, preserving the accuracy of the final stages of the refinement of the solution. The GA is well known for its ability to provide stability in searching for a variety of solution spaces according to mechanism such as selection, crossover and mutation alluding to the process of natural selection. This model is rather successful in general search conditions but may be not accurate enough in the conditions of local search. Automated MitM attack prevention and response layer

In addition to proper detection, the proposed framework also has an automated prevention and response layer that converts the output of the HAINet classifier into actual security measures. When HAINet decides a network flow or session is malicious, a mitigation controller is activated to reduce the number of attacks and further exploitation. In the first place, there is immediate isolation of the session. Malicious connection is blocked by terminating the connection at the gateway or edge node. This will ensure additional leakage of data is avoided and any effort by the hacker to interfere with running communication streams is stopped. At the same time, the origin and destination address of the bad path are blacklisted temporarily to limit the number of connection attempts.

Second, legitimate communicating endpoints are subject to dynamic traffic rerouting. The system creates a different secure flow of communication using trusted routing nodes with the help of software-defined networking (SDN) rules. The framework avoids compromised routes to maintain continuity of service as well as removing the point of interception by the attacker. The operational methods used in this rerouting are transparent and do not need any manual operations by the end users. Third, secure-channel re-establishment in the form of adaptive is instituted. In the case of sessions that are considered suspicious, the framework will insist on renegotiation of the cryptographic parameters such as new session keys and more resistant cipher suites. To prevent downgrade attacks and impersonation attacks, transport Layer Security (TLS) handshakes are restarted and validating the certificate is revalidated. This is because even in case an attacker in the past had part of the session information, he will be unable to use it. Fourth, the framework updates the behavior access control. Devices with a high-risk score are those with a history of suspicious activity and this is indicated by an increased risk score and results in tightened authentication policies like multi-factor authentication or communication restrictions. In the long run, such risk conscious policy improvement makes the network more robust against the continuous enemies. Lastly, the mitigation layer produces prevention logs that are returned into the training repository. The dataset is updated using these logs to train HAINet periodically to be able to learn novel variants of attacks and keep the effectiveness of both detection and prevention processes rising.

Novelty of this work

The novelty of this work is embodied in the development and application of the HAINet to tackle the critical issue of MitM attacks in smart networks. This model uniquely combines the strengths of Inception networks, known for their advanced feature extraction capabilities across multiple scales, with the flexibility and adaptability of ANNs, enabling it to effectively detect complex and subtle patterns indicative of cyber threats. Furthermore, the incorporation of hybrid optimization techniques, which merge GA with SA, enhances the model’s ability to efficiently search for and refine optimal solutions within complex data spaces. This approach not only improves the model’s detection capabilities but also ensures its robustness and reliability across diverse network conditions, including noisy and highly encrypted environments. By integrating these advanced techniques, the work significantly advances the field of cybersecurity, offering a powerful and resilient solution for protecting smart networks.

Edge vs. cloud deployment analysis and inference time per packet

Smart network environments are usually made of resource-constrained IoT devices, moderately capable edge gateways and even high-performance cloud servers. Although the proposed HAINet model does not focus on the direct implementation on low-power IoT sensors, it is expected to be used in the aggregation area, like in edge gateways or fog nodes where the network traffic is inherently focused and where more resources in the form of computing capabilities are available. Based on Table 3, the experimental platform-based measurement of the inference time of HAINet is about 45 milliseconds per input sample. In the suggested construction, every input sample will be matched to a feature sequence of a brief packet window or flow block instead of the separate raw packet. This design option minimizes the computing cost and still provides adequate behavioral context on which to detect MitM.

Assuming that a single feature vector represents a window of 50 packets, the effective inference time per packet can be approximated as:

The given processing time of under one millisecond shows that the suggested framework can host the near real-time network edge intrusion detection. In simple applications of smart home gateways with a few thousand packets and even less, the above delay can tolerate continuous monitoring without adding visible communication latency. In addition, when several feature windows are batched it allows further throughput improvements on the multi-core processors typically found in current gateways. Cloud-based execution is a more desirable alternative in large-scale applications, e.g., smart cities or industrial IoT settings, where parallelism and scalability are required. In this case, edge nodes may extract lightweight features and send them to cloud servers to perform centralized inferencing and correlation of threats, as well as to centralize features through HAINet.

Conclusion and future work

In this study, we have developed and evaluated a HAINet aimed at detecting and preventing MitM attacks in smart networks. The model’s architecture, which combines the robust feature extraction of Inception networks with the adaptability of ANNs, has proven highly effective in identifying complex patterns associated with MitM attacks. The HAINet model significantly outperformed traditional machine learning models, achieving an impressive accuracy of 98.69%. This high level of accuracy, coupled with the model’s resilience under different network conditions, underscores its potential as a robust solution for securing smart networks against MitM attacks. Our findings suggest that the integration of deep learning techniques into cybersecurity frameworks can substantially improve the detection and prevention of sophisticated cyber threats. While the HAINet has demonstrated superior performance in detecting and preventing MitM attacks, there are several avenues for future research. One potential direction is the enhancement of the model’s efficiency by exploring more advanced optimization algorithms, which could reduce computational overhead while maintaining or even improving accuracy. Additionally, expanding the model’s applicability to other types of cyber-attacks, such as Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) or Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs), could further solidify its role in comprehensive cybersecurity solutions. Future work could also involve real-time implementation and testing in diverse smart network environments, such as smart cities or IoT ecosystems, to validate the model’s scalability and adaptability. Moreover, integrating the HAINet model with other emerging technologies, such as blockchain or quantum computing, could offer new dimensions in secure communication frameworks. The ongoing refinement of the model’s accuracy and robustness will be crucial in keeping pace with the evolving landscape of cyber threats.

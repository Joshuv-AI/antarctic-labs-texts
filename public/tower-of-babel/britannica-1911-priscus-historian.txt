PRISCUS, of Panium in Thrace, Greek sophist and historian, lived during the 5th century A.D. He accompanied Maximin
the ambassador of Theodosius the Younger, to the court of
Attila (448). During the reign of Marcian (450–457) he also took
part in missions to Arabia and the Egyptian Thebaid. Priscus
was the author of an historical work in eight books ( Βυζαντινικὴ Ἱστορία ),
probably from the accession of Attila to that of Zeno
(433–474). Only fragments of the work remain, but the description
of Attila and his court and the account of the reception
of the Roman ambassadors is a most valuable piece of contemporary
history. Priscus’s style is pure, and his impartiality
and trustworthiness entitle him to an honourable place among
the writers of his time.
Fragments and life in C. W. Müller, Fragmenta historicorum
graecorum, iv. 69–110; v. 24–26, ed. B. G. Niebuhr in Bonn, Corpus
Scriptorum hist. byzantinae (1829), vol. vi., and L. Dindorf in Historici
graeci minores (1870), vol. i. For the embassy to Attila see Gibbon,
Decline and Fall, ch. 34.

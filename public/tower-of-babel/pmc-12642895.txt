Characterization of the complete chloroplast genome of Cladopus doianus Koriba
Authors: Huanxi Yu, Jianliang Zhang, Zhedong Qian, Yingying Lv, Ye Wang, Wanggu Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCladopus doianus Koriba 1928, a species within the Cladopus genus, possesses remarkable aerial shoots and an extensive underground root system, making it an exceptional model for investigating the evolutionary aspects of angiosperm morphology. In this study, we employed Illumina sequencing to assemble and annotate the complete chloroplast (cp) genome of C. doianus. The cp genome is 133,017 base pairs in length, containing 107 unique genes: 73 protein-coding genes, 30 tRNA genes, and four rRNA genes. Notably, 17 of these genes are duplicated within the inverted repeat (IR) regions, comprising four rRNA genes, seven tRNA genes, and six protein-coding genes. Additionally, we constructed a phylogenetic tree of selected species within the Podostemaceae family, and the results were consistent with previous studies, indicating that C. doianus is closely related to P. chiangmaiensis. In conclusion, our study presents the first complete chloroplast genome of C. doianus, providing valuable insights for future research on the Podostemaceae family.

Introduction

Podostemaceae, the largest family of strictly aquatic flowering plants globally, forms subaquatic meadows contributing to autochthonous carbon production and serving as a vital food source for associated fauna (Suzuki et al. 2002). It is classified into three subfamilies: Podostemoideae, Weddellinoideae, and Tristichoideae (Koi and Kato 2015). They play a key role in ecological restoration due to their unique ecological niche and water purification functions within ecosystems.

Furthermore, the extreme environmental conditions encountered by Podostemaceae can lead to significant modifications in their vegetative and reproductive morphologies (Rutishauser 2016). These unique adaptations pose taxonomic challenges due to limited morphological traits, impeding studies on the biology and evolution of this group. Cladopus doianus, distinguished by its flattened roots spreading along rock surfaces, with leaves and flowers emerging from them (Xue et al. 2020). Due to its remarkable aerial shoots and extensive underground root system, C. doianus serves as an exceptional model for investigating the evolutionary aspects of angiosperm morphology (Rutishauser and Pfeifer 2002). Additionally, C. doianus exhibits strict requirements for water quality and thrives in streams with pristine conditions, making it a valuable bioindicator of river health.

However, the distinctiveness of both its habitat and morphology has led to varying scholarly perspectives regarding the classification status of this plant (Xue et al. 2020). Further clarification regarding the origin and phylogeny of C. doianus is needed, particularly through the utilization of genomic data. Currently, the availability of chloroplast genome data for Podostemaceae remains insufficient. Therefore, it is urgent and necessary to assemble and sequence the complete chloroplast genome of C. doianus, which may be crucial in addressing accurate species identification and advancing phylogenetic research.

Materials and methods

The healthy and fresh leaves of C. doianus were collected from Xinqiao Hukou Village in Changting County, Longyan City, Fujian Province (25°51′03″ N, 116°25′10″ E, Figure 1). Plant specimens were deposited at the Nanjing Institute of Environmental Sciences under accession number ctctc2020101201 (Huanxi Yu, 2280328008@qq.com). Total DNA was isolated from leaf tissues following the manufacturer’s protocol using an E.Z.N.A.® Tissue DNA Kit (Omega, Norcross, GA, USA). High-throughput paired-end sequencing (read length: 150 bp) was conducted using the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform, yielding approximately 8 GB of raw sequencing reads for downstream analysis. Raw reads were trimmed and subjected to quality control using Fastp with default parameters (Chen et al. 2018). The chloroplast genome of C. doianus was assembled using GetOrganelle with k-mer sizes of 21, 77 and 121 (Jin et al. 2020). The complete chloroplast genomes of Apinagia fucoides (MN165812.1), Marathrum capillaceum (NC060291), and Paracladopus chiangmaiensis (NC061663.1) were utilized as reference genomes (Bedoya et al. 2019). To ensure the accuracy of genome assembly and annotation, read depth as well as cis- and trans-splicing genes were examined using CPStools and CPGview (Huang et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2023). The complete chloroplast genome map of C. doianus was then visualized using OGDRAW (Greiner et al. 2019).

To determine the phylogenetic position of C. doianus within the Podostemaceae family, phylogenetic trees were constructed using 13 representative species. Three species (Hirtella racemosa, Passiflora edulis, and Garcinia mangostana), were chosen as outgroups. Shared protein-coding genes were extracted using CPStools, and multiple sequence alignments were performed with MAFFT using default parameters (Katoh et al. 2019). The optimal nucleotide substitution models were identified using ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017). Subsequently, Bayesian Inference (BI) and maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenies were reconstructed based on the shared protein-coding genes (PCGs) in PhyloSuite (Zhang et al. 2020). The resulting phylogenetic trees were visualized using FigTree.

Results

The complete chloroplast genome of Cladopus doianus spans 133,017 base pairs (bp) and consists of a large single copy (LSC) region of 78,968 bp, a small single copy (SSC) region of 12,331 bp, and a pair of inverted repeats (IRa and IRb) each 20,859 bp in length (Figure 2). Each base pair depth, as well as cis- and trans-splicing genes, were examined and validated (Figures S1 and S2), confirming the reliability of the assembled genome. The overall GC content is 34.86%, with varying distributions: 31.92% in the LSC region, 27.99% in the SSC region, and 42.46% in the IR regions. A total of 107 unique genes were identified in the C. doianus chloroplast genome, including 73 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 30 tRNA genes, and four rRNA genes. Among these, 17 genes are duplicated within the IR regions, comprising four rRNA genes, seven tRNA genes, and six protein-coding genes. Additionally, 16 genes in the C. doianus chloroplast genome contain introns, including 10 protein-coding genes and six tRNA genes. Notably, the trnK-UUU gene possesses the longest intron, which includes the matK gene within its intron region.

Annotated chloroplast gene map of Cladopus doianus. Genes depicted on the inside of the circle are transcribed clockwise while genes shown on the outside of the circle are transcribed counterclockwise. The dark grey area within the circle represents the GC content across the chloroplast genome.

Bayesian inference and maximum likelihood phylogenetic analyses based on 70 shared PCGs yielded identical topologies, with all nodes receiving strong support (Bayesian posterior probability = 1; bootstrap support value = 100). The resulting phylogenetic tree revealed that all species clustered into two major clades (Figure 3). The first clade comprised five genera from the subfamily Podostemoideae: Clapodus, Paraclapodus, Polypleurum, Apinagia, and Marathrum. Within this clade, Clapodus was most closely related to Paraclapodus. C. doianus was sister to C. yinggelingensis, and together they formed a sister group with C. pierrei and C. fukienensis. The second clade consisted solely of T. yongtaiensis from the subfamily Tristichoideae. Overall, our phylogenetic results are consistent with previous studies (Kita and Kato 2001; Koi and Kato 2015).

Discussion and conclusions

The results demonstrate that the chloroplast genome structure and gene content of C. doianus are largely conserved and closely resemble those of seven other Podostemaceae species, indicating strong evolutionary conservation within the family (Bedoya et al. 2019). Similar to other terrestrial plants, the GC content in the eight Podostemaceae species was lower than the AT content, ranging from 34.8% to 37.0% (Graham et al. 2000; Cheng et al. 2017). In C. doianus, 16 genes possess introns, and the lengths of their exons are comparable to those in other Podostemaceae species (Bedoya et al. 2019). Notably, the ycf1 and ycf2 genes were absent in the C. doianus chloroplast genome, with only Paracladopus chiangmaiensis retaining the ycf1 gene among the Podostemaceae species. The loss of these genes is a common phenomenon in chloroplast genomes and has been previously documented in species such as Panax ginseng and Cucumis sativus (Pląder et al. 2007; Zhao et al. 2014). The deletion of the ycf1 gene in the chloroplast genome may be due to its transfer to the nuclear genome. Since the C. doianus genome has been published, further studies on gene transfer between the chloroplast and nuclear genomes are needed (Xue et al. 2020).

Taxonomic classification within Podostemaceae has been identified as a challenge (Bedoya et al. 2019). The high phenotypic plasticity exhibited by wild Podostemaceae species complicates their identification, and new species within this family continue to be discovered. Phylogenetic studies of the Podostemaceae family have primarily relied on chloroplast genes and nuclear gene fragments. However, these approaches have not provided robust support for the relationships among Podostemaceae species (Bedoya et al. 2019; Xue et al. 2020). Whole chloroplast genomes have been shown to be more effective and accurate for phylogenetic analysis than single or a few genes (Shen et al. 2022; Yu et al. 2022). In this study, consensus trees constructed from the complete genome and protein-coding genes were identical, with all nodes receiving 100% bootstrap support values. Given that the Podostemaceae family comprises approximately 300 species across 54 genera, there is a need to explore a much larger number of species.

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of the complete chloroplast genome of C. doianus alongside other Podostemaceae species, providing a valuable genomic database for further investigations of this family. The genetic resources obtained from this study will greatly facilitate future endeavors in population genetics, species identification, and conservation biology related to Podostemaceae.

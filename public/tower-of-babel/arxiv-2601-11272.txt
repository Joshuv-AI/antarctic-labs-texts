Suppression of Fast Flavor Conversion by Red Turbulence in Supernovae
Yiwei Bao, Andrea Addazi, Shuai Zha

Abstract
Fast flavor conversions (FFCs) in supernovae, driven by neutrino-neutrino refraction, can catastrophically equilibrate flavors and potentially affect the neutrino-driven explosion. We present a pivotal insight: matter density fluctuations characterized by extremely red spectra ( ν < 0 \nu<0 , where | ν | ≡ − ν > 0 |\nu|\equiv-\nu>0 ) naturally arising in stratified supernova environments, can suppress such instabilities by inducing accelerated decoherence. By deriving exact analytical solutions for two-flavor evolution in red turbulent matter—where correlations grow as t | ν | t^{|\nu|} —we uncover a novel acceleration of coherence loss . This dynamical decoherence mechanism raises an effective energy barrier against the collective growth of flavor instabilities. Translating our master-equation results into an effective damping rate for FFC linear analysis, we find that realistic red turbulence ( ν ∼ − 1 \nu\sim-1 , fluctuation strength ξ ∼ 0.1 \xi\sim 0.1 ) can elevate the FFC threshold by a factor of ∼ 3 − 5 \sim 3-5 , potentially stabilizing regions that would otherwise undergo explosion-killing flavor equilibration (or vice versa). Our work provides the first analytical criterion for FFC suppression in turbulent media and identifies red turbulence as a critical, physics-grounded ingredient missing from current supernova models.



Introduction.
—Core-collapse supernova explosions are powered by a delicate balance: neutrino heating in the gain region must narrowly exceed cooling to revive the stalled shock. Modern simulations reveal this balance is often marginal, with net heating surpassing cooling by mere percentages
5
;
11
. This fine-tuning renders the explosion mechanism exquisitely sensitive to microphysical processes that alter neutrino fluxes, particularly flavor transformations.

While matter-induced (MSW) oscillations occur predominantly outside the gain region, the collective oscillations driven by neutrino-neutrino interactions present a more immediate and disruptive threat. Fast flavor conversions (FFCs), which can develop on centimeter scales within nanoseconds, rapidly equilibrate
\nu_{e}
and
\nu_{x}
populations. This equilibration can reduce the neutrino heating efficiency by 20–30%, thereby quenching explosions that would otherwise be marginally successful
3
. The impact of FFCs on supernova dynamics, however, remains an active area of investigation, with recent studies suggesting outcomes that range from strongly suppressive to modestly supportive depending on the progenitor and modeling details
9
;
15
;
16
. Linear stability analyses suggest FFCs may be ubiquitous in supernova cores
8
, raising a critical question: what physical mechanisms can suppress these instabilities?

Turbulence, generated by convection and hydrodynamical instabilities, permeates supernovae and affects neutrino propagation. Beginning with Loreti and Balantekin’s master-equation approach
13
, studies have examined white-noise and Kolmogorov-type spectra. Intriguingly, some turbulent spectra may even enhance FFC growth
2
, deepening the theoretical challenge.

However, supernova turbulence in stratified regions—like the proto-neutron star atmosphere or convective shells—differs fundamentally. It exhibits ”red spectra” with negative spectral exponents
\nu<0
, where temporal correlations grow algebraically (
K(t)\sim t^{|\nu|}
) at short times. This red turbulence reflects persistent, coherent structures rather than a disordered cascade and is a natural outcome in density-stratified environments
6
. Its impact on neutrino flavor evolution has remained unexplored.

In this Letter, we present exact analytical solutions for neutrino evolution in red turbulent matter. Solving the non-Markovian master equation with kernel
K(t)=\xi(t/\tau_{c})^{|\nu|}e^{-t/\tau_{c}}
(
\nu<0
), we discover that red turbulence generates
fractional integral operators
of order
1+|\nu|
, contrasting with the fractional derivatives of Kolmogorov turbulence. This leads to a key physical effect: accelerated decoherence—slower damping of flavor coherence compared to white noise of equivalent fluctuation strength.

We translate this microphysical insight to FFCs: the derived decoherence rate
\Gamma_{\text{dec}}
acts as an effective damping term in the linear stability analysis. For realistic supernova parameters (
\nu\sim-1
,
\xi\sim 0.1
,
\tau_{c}\sim 0.1
km), the turbulence can raise the FFC threshold by a factor of 3–5, potentially stabilizing regions vulnerable to explosion-killing flavor equilibration or vice versa. Our work provides both a novel analytical framework for neutrinos in correlated media and a physics-based mechanism to resolve the tension between predicted FFC instabilities and the requirement of sustained neutrino heating for successful explosions.

Physical Implications for Supernova Heating.
—Our analytical framework provides quantitative predictions for how red turbulence affects neutrino flavor evolution in supernovae. The key quantity is the effective decoherence rate
\Gamma_{\text{dec}}
derived from our master equation solution, which modifies the FFC linear stability criterion to:

\imaginary[\omega_{\text{FFC}}]>\Gamma_{\text{dec}}(\nu,\xi,\tau_{c}),

(12)

where
\imaginary[\omega_{\text{FFC}}]
is the FFC growth rate in static matter.

To quantify the potential suppression, we evaluate
\Gamma_{\text{dec}}
using turbulence parameters from supernova simulations. Multi-dimensional studies show significant density fluctuations in the gain region, with relative amplitudes
\delta n_{e}/\bar{n}_{e}\sim 40\%
yielding
\xi\sim 0.16

\tau_{c}\sim 0.1
km in natural units

\nu

-1.0

-0.5

The implications for explosion dynamics stem from the delicate heating-cooling balance. Simulations show that successful explosions typically require net positive heating with
Q^{+}>Q^{-}
by only a few percent
5
. Turbulence itself can aid explosion by enhancing neutrino heating
7
, but FFCs threaten to counteract this by reducing the effective
\nu_{e}
and
\bar{\nu}_{e}

While quantifying the exact impact on explosion outcomes requires incorporating our suppression criterion into full simulations, the magnitude of the threshold enhancement suggests it could be significant. In marginal cases where small changes in heating determine success or failure, suppressing FFCs could tip the balance toward explosion. This is particularly relevant given that FFCs are predicted to be most prevalent in regions where red turbulence naturally occurs
6
.

For practical implementation, we propose that future supernova simulations extract turbulence parameters (
\nu
,
\xi
,
\tau_{c}
) along neutrino trajectories and apply the criterion
\imaginary[\omega_{\text{FFC}}]>\Gamma_{\text{dec}}
to determine FFC viability region-by-region. This provides a physics-based approach to assess whether and where FFCs might develop in realistic turbulent environments.

Conclusions.
—We have developed the first analytical framework for neutrino flavor evolution in matter with red turbulent spectra, characterized by negative spectral exponents
\nu<0
. Our exact solutions to the non-Markovian master equation reveal that red turbulence generates fractional integral operators of order
1+|\nu|
, fundamentally distinguishing it from conventional turbulence models that produce fractional derivatives.

The key physical discovery is that red turbulence induces accelerated decoherence: for the same integrated fluctuation strength
\xi
, flavor coherence persists longer than in white-noise environments but decays differently than in Kolmogorov-type turbulence. This decoherence behavior emerges from the algebraically growing temporal correlations
K(t)\sim t^{|\nu|}
characteristic of stratified astrophysical plasmas.

Applying our results to the supernova fast flavor conversion problem, we find that the derived decoherence rate
\Gamma_{\text{dec}}(\nu,\xi,\tau_{c})
can act as an effective damping mechanism in the linear stability analysis. For realistic parameters in supernova gain regions and proto-neutron star atmospheres (
\nu\sim-1
,
\xi\sim 0.1
,
\tau_{c}\sim 0.1
km), this damping rate is
\sim 10
–100 km
-1
—comparable to typical FFC growth rates. Consequently, red turbulence can raise the FFC instability threshold by a factor of 3-5, potentially suppressing explosion-killing flavor equilibration in critical regions.

Our work establishes several important advances:
1. Mathematical foundation: Exact solutions for neutrino propagation in red turbulent media, expressed through Mittag-Leffler functions and fractional calculus.
2. Physical mechanism: Identification of accelerated decoherence as a novel effect of long-range temporal correlations.
3. Astrophysical application: A quantitative criterion for FFC suppression that can be implemented in supernova simulations.
4. Paradigm shift: Red turbulence as a self-consistent suppressor of flavor instabilities, rather than merely a source of noise.

These results suggest that the stratified turbulence naturally present in supernovae may protect the neutrino heating mechanism from disruptive fast flavor conversions. This could provide a resolution to the tension between the linear stability prediction of ubiquitous FFCs and the requirement of sustained
\nu_{e}
dominance for successful explosions, although it is also possible that it exacerbates
9
;
15
;
16
the problem.

Looking forward, our analytical framework provides essential benchmarks for numerical simulations and opens new directions for studying quantum systems in correlated astrophysical environments. The connection between negative spectral exponents, fractional integrals, and decoherence dynamics represents a fundamental advance with applications beyond supernovae to neutron star mergers, accretion disks, and early universe cosmology.

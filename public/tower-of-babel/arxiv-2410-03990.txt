Some Fixed Point Results for Interpolative Contractive Mappings in C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebra valued metric spaces
Hafida Massit 1 , Mohamed Rossafi 2 and Choonkil Park 3∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we present a new concept of interpolative contraction mappings in C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebra valued complete metric space and we prove the existence of fixed points and common fixed points for Kannan-Riech type contractions.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

In
1968
Kannan introduced a new result that there is a discontinuous mapping that satisfies certain conditions and admits a fixed point in a complete metric spaces. This result stills one of the most important results that generalizes the famous Banach contraction principle.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a metric space space. A self-mapping
T:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
is said to be a Kannan contraction if there exists
\tau\in[0,\dfrac{1}{2}[
such that

d(Tx,Ty)\leq\tau(d(x,Tx)+d(y,Ty));\forall x,y\in\mathcal{U}

In 1969, Kannan
[
5
]
proved that if
(\mathcal{U},d)
is complete metric space, then every Kannan contraction on
\mathcal{U}
has a unique fixed point. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a metric space. A self-mapping
T
on
\mathcal{U}
is called Reich contraction if there exists
r\in\left[0,\frac{1}{3}\right[
such that

d(Tu,Tv)\leq rd(u,v)+rd(u,Tu)+rd(v,Tv),\forall u,v\in\mathcal{U}.

In 1972, Reich
[
12
]
proved that if
(\mathcal{U},d)
is complete metric space, then every Reich contraction on
\mathcal{U}
has a unique fixed point. It is interesting that Riech’s theorem is generalized of the Banach contraction principle
[
2
]
and Kannan contraction mapping.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a metric space. A self-mapping
T
on
\mathcal{U}
is called weak contraction if there exists
\varphi:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R^{+}}
be a lower semicontinuous function and
\phi:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R^{+}}
be an altering distance function such that

\phi(d(Tu,Tv))\leq\phi(d(u,v))-\varphi(d(u,v)).

Weak contraction principle was first introduced by Alber et al in Hilbert spaces
[
1
]
.

Recently, Errai et al
[
3
]
announced the notion of interpolative
Kannan Contraction on
b
-metric space, they also proved a
some fixed point results of Interpolative Kannan contraction theorem. Moreover, the notion of Interpolative Kannan contraction are studied by many authors in the field of fixed point theory (see
[
10
,
6
]
and references therein).

Theorem 1.2
.

If
(\mathcal{U},d)
is a complete metric space, then
every Kannan contraction on
\mathcal{U}
has a unique fixed point.

In 2018, Karapinar published a new type of contraction
obtained from the definition of the Kannan contraction by
interpolation as follows.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},d)
be a metric space. A self-mapping
T:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
is said to be an interpolative Kannan type contraction if there are two constants
\tau,\beta\in]0,1[
such that

d(Tx,Ty)\leq\tau(d(x,Tx)^{\beta})(d(y,Ty))^{1-\beta}

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{U}
with
x\not=Tx
and
y\not=Ty
.

Karapinar obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},d)
be a complete metric space and
T:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
be an interpolative Kannan-type contraction mapping. Then
T
has a unique fixed point.

Definition 1.5
.

[
11
]
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a non-empty set
Suppose the mapping

satisfies:

.

(ii)

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}
be a sequence in a
C^{\ast}-
algebra-valued metric space
\left(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{A},d\right)
such that

d(x_{n},x_{n+1})\preceq\delta d(x_{n-1},x_{n})

(1.1)

for some
\delta\in[0,1[
and
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then,
\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\left(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{A},d\right)

Proof.

First we denote that

d(x_{n+1},x_{n})\preceq\delta^{n}d(x_{1},x_{0}),\;\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

For
m\geq 1
and
p\geq 1

\displaystyle d(x_{m},x_{m+p})

\displaystyle\preceq b\left(d(x_{m},x_{m+1})+d(x_{m+1},x_{m+p})\right)

\displaystyle\preceq d(x_{m},x_{m+1})+d(x_{m+1},x_{m+2}+...+d(x_{m+p-2},x_{m+p-1})

\displaystyle+d(x_{m+p-1},x_{m+p})

\displaystyle\preceq\delta^{m}d(x_{0},x_{1})+\delta^{m+1}d(x_{0},x_{1})+\delta^{m+2}d(x_{0},x_{1})+\delta^{m+3}d(x_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle+......+\delta^{m+p-1}d(x_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle=\delta^{m}+\delta^{m+1}+...+\delta^{m+p-1})d(x_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\delta^{m+k}d(x_{0},x_{1}).

Since

\|d(x_{m},x_{m+p})\|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(\delta)^{m+k}\|d(x_{0},x_{1})\|\rightarrow 0\;\text{quand}\;m\rightarrow\infty
.

Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{n,m\rightarrow\infty}d({x_{n}},{x_{m}})=\theta.

This implies that

is a Cauchy sequence.

∎

2.
Main result

inspired by the notion of interpolative Kannan in complete metric space, we present a new concepts of interpolative Kannan-Reich weak type contraction mappings.
we discuss the existence of fixed point results for these new contractive mappings in
C^{\ast}
-algebra valued complete metric space.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{A},d)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra-valued metric space. A self-mapping
T:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
is called an interpolative Kannan type contraction if there exist
\tau,\beta\in]0,1[
such that

d(Tx,Ty)\preceq\tau d(x,Tx)^{\beta}d(y,Ty)^{1-\beta};\forall x,y\in\mathcal{U}

(2.1)

with
Tx\not=x
.

C^{\ast}-
algebra-valued metric space and

has a fixed point in
\mathcal{U}

Proof.

Let

x_{n+1}=Tx_{n}

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

x_{n}\not=Tx_{n}
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},x_{n})=d(Tx_{n},Tx_{n-1})

\displaystyle\preceq\tau d(x_{n},Tx_{n})^{\beta}d(x_{n-1},Tx_{n-1})^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle=\tau d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{1-\beta}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})^{\beta}

this implies that

d(x_{n},x_{n+1})^{1-\beta}\preceq\tau d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{1-\beta}

(2.2)

Thus, we have that the sequence
\{d(x_{n},x_{n-1})\}
is non-increasing and non-negative.
From
2.2
,
we deduce that

d(x_{n},x_{n+1})\preceq\tau d(x_{n-1},x_{n})\preceq\tau^{n}d(x_{0},x_{1}).

(2.3)

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in the inequality
2.6
, we obtain
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})=\theta
.

For
m\geq 1
and

\displaystyle d(x_{m},x_{m+r})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x_{m},x_{m+1})+d(x_{m+1},x_{m+2})+...+d(x_{m+r-1},x_{m+r})

is a Cauchy sequence in

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},z)=\theta
.

Now, we shall show that
z
is fixed point of
T
.
we get

d(Tx_{n},Tz)\preceq\tau d(x_{n},Tx_{n})^{\beta}d(z,Tz)^{1-\beta}

For uniqueness, Let

\displaystyle d(z,y)

\displaystyle=d(Tz,Ty)

\displaystyle=0

∎

\displaystyle d(x_{m},x_{m+r})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x_{m},x_{m+1})+d(x_{m+1},x_{m+2})+...+d(x_{m+r-1},x_{m+r})

\displaystyle\preceq\tau^{\frac{m}{1-\eta}}+...+\tau^{\frac{m+r-1}{1-\eta}}

\displaystyle\preceq\tau^{\frac{m}{1-\eta}}\dfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}\rightarrow\theta\;\;as\;\;m\rightarrow\infty.

Hence
\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{A},d)
. Hence there exist
z\in\mathcal{U}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},z)=\theta

Now, we shall show that
z
is fixed point of
T
.
we get

d(Tx_{n},Tz)\preceq\tau d(x_{n},z)^{\alpha}d(x_{n},Tz)^{\beta}d(z,Tz)^{\eta}

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
and using the continuity of
T
.

We have
d(z,Tz)=\theta
.
∎

Definition 2.12
.

A function
\psi:\mathcal{A}^{+}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{+}
is called an alterning distance function if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\psi
is continuous,

(ii)

\psi
is nondecreasing,

(iii)

\psi(x)=\theta
if and only if
x=\theta
.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{A},d)
be a complete
C^{\ast}-
algebra-valued metric space and
T:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
is an interpolative weakly contractive mapping of the Reich type,
\forall x,y\in\mathcal{U}
with
x\not=Tx
and
y\not=Ty
. Let
\alpha,\beta,\eta\in]0,1[
such that
\alpha+\beta+\eta=1
.
If there exists a point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
satisfying
d(x_{0},Tx_{0})\preceq I
then,
T
has a fixed point in
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
and define a sequence
\{x_{n}\}\in\mathcal{U}
by
x_{0}=x
and
x_{n+1}=Tx_{n}
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
x_{n}\not=Tx_{n}
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We have

\displaystyle\phi(d(x_{n},x_{n+1}))

\displaystyle\preceq\phi(d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{\alpha}d(x_{n-1},Tx_{n-1})^{\beta}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})^{\eta})

\displaystyle-\psi(d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{\alpha+\beta}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})^{\eta})

\displaystyle\preceq\phi(d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{\alpha+\beta}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})^{\eta})

This implies that

\displaystyle d(x_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{\alpha+\beta}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})^{\eta}

\displaystyle\preceq d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\eta}}

\displaystyle\preceq d(x_{n-1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\preceq d(x_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle\preceq I.

Then, there exists
\gamma\succeq\theta
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})=\gamma.

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in

\phi(d(x_{n},x_{n+1}))\preceq\phi(d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{\alpha+\beta}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})^{\eta})-\psi(d(x_{n-1},x_{n})^{\alpha+\beta}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})^{\eta})

we obtain

\phi(\gamma)\preceq\phi(\gamma^{\alpha+\beta}\gamma^{\eta})-\psi(\gamma^{\alpha+\beta}\gamma^{\eta})\preceq\phi(\gamma)-\psi(\gamma).

This implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})=\theta.

For
m\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\phi(d(x_{m+1},x_{p+1}))

\displaystyle\preceq\phi(d(x_{m},x_{p})^{\alpha}d(x_{m},x_{m+1})^{\beta}d(x_{p},x_{p+1})^{\eta})-\psi(d(x_{m},x_{p})^{\alpha}d(x_{m},x_{m+1})^{\beta}d(x_{p},x_{p+1})^{\eta})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\theta\;\;as\;\;m,p\rightarrow\infty.

Then,
\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence, and there exists
z\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},z)=\theta
.

We have

d(x_{n},z)^{\alpha}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})^{\beta}d(z,Tz)^{\eta}\rightarrow\theta.

We get

\phi(d(z,Tz))\preceq\phi(d(x_{n},z)^{\alpha}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})^{\beta}d(z,Tz)^{\eta})-\psi(d(x_{n},z)^{\alpha}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})^{\beta}d(z,Tz)^{\eta}).

Hence
d(z,Tz)=\theta
.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{A},d)
be a complete
C^{\ast}-
algebra-valued metric space and
T:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
a mapping such that there exists
k\in]0,1[

d(Tx,Ty)\preceq k[d(x,y)^{\alpha}d(x,Tx)^{\beta}d(y,Ty)^{\eta}]

\alpha+\beta+\eta=1
.

If there exists a point

satisfying

then,
T
has a fixed point in

.

Proof.

It is sufficient to apply
\phi(x)=x
and
\psi(x)=(I-k)x
in Theorem
2.15
.
∎

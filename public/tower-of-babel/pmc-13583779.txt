Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy with afatinib for locally advanced head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (neoCHANCE-2): an open-label, single-arm, phase 2 study
Authors: Zhigong Wei, Huijiao Chen, Dejuan Wang, Leyu Li, Yiyan Pei, Huaju Yang, Feng Wang, Jing Jin, Yiqiao Luo, Fei Chen, Jun Liu, Xingchen Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile immune checkpoint inhibitor-based neoadjuvant therapy shows promise in locally advanced head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (LA-HNSCC), the optimal regimen has yet to be established. We report, for the first time, a neoadjuvant treatment that combines chemotherapy with dual blockade of the programmed cell death protein 1 and epidermal growth factor receptor pathways in LA-HNSCC.MethodsIn this single-arm phase 2 trial (NCT05516589) conducted at a single academic center, patients with LA-HNSCC were enrolled and received two cycles of tislelizumab and TP chemotherapy (nab-paclitaxel and cisplatin), administered on day 1 of each 3-week cycle, along with afatinib during the interval between chemoimmunotherapy cycles, followed by surgical resection. The primary endpoint was the complete pathologic response (pCR) rate. Secondary endpoints included the major pathologic response (MPR) rate, objective response rate (ORR), event-free survival, overall survival (OS), and safety.ResultsA total of 40 patients were enrolled and received neoadjuvant treatment, 32 of whom proceeded to surgical resection; among these patients, the pCR rate was 40.6% (95% CI 23.7% to 59.4%). The ORR was 82.5% (95% CI 67.2% to 92.7%). The 1-year OS rate was 97.3% (95% CI 92.2% to 100%). The most common treatment-related adverse event (TRAE) of grade 3−4 was lymphopenia (5/40, 12.5%). No grade 5 TRAEs leading to death occurred, and no treatment-related surgical delays were observed. In the prespecified exploratory biomarker analysis, increased cytotoxic T lymphocytes and B cells in the tumor immune microenvironment may be associated with pCR/MPR. Neoadjuvant treatment induced a significant increase in the proportion of peripheral CD8+T cells, along with a reduction in B cells.ConclusionsWe report, for the first time, the promising efficacy and acceptable safety profile of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy combined with afatinib in patients with LA-HNSCC. Neoadjuvant treatment potentially induced an immune-enhanced tumor microenvironment. Further evaluation in large-scale clinical trials with longer follow-up periods is needed.Trial registration numberNCT05516589.

Introduction

Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) accounts for over 90% of head and neck malignancies and is the sixth most common cancer worldwide.1 2 Treatment for locally advanced disease (LA-HNSCC) typically requires a multidisciplinary approach, combining surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. Despite aggressive interventions, patients with LA-HNSCC remain at high risk for local recurrence (up to 40%), and the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate remains low at just 50%.3 These challenges highlight the urgent need for therapeutic innovations to enhance long-term disease control.

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy offers several appealing advantages, including downsizing tumors, enhancing eligibility for organ preservation, and eradicating micrometastases. However, whether it provides superior overall benefits in LA-HNSCC remains unclear.4 Chemotherapeutic agents have been shown to modulate the tumor microenvironment and induce programmed cell death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) overexpression, prompting widespread exploration of chemoimmunotherapy in advanced cancer.5 6 Recently, this combined regimen has gained increasing interest in the neoadjuvant setting.7–9 Previous studies reported that two cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy achieved a major pathologic response (MPR) rate of 44% (including complete pathologic response (pCR) rate of 17%) in patients with LA-HNSCC.8 Although this combination shows promise, the optimal immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based neoadjuvant regimen that aims to achieve higher pathologic response rates without increasing toxicity requires further investigation.

In HNSCC, over 90% of patients exhibit epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) overexpression, which can drive the continuous activation of its downstream signaling pathway, thereby promoting the initiation and progression of cancer.10 11 Targeting EGFR has become an important strategy for the treatment of HNSCC.12 In the era of immunotherapy, altered EGFR signaling may influence the tumor microenvironment by modulating levels of immunosuppressive cytokines and upregulating the expression of PD-L1, suggesting the potential benefit of combining ICIs with EGFR-targeted inhibitors.11 Recently, a phase 2 ALPHA study reported an objective response rate (ORR) of 41% and manageable toxicities with combination therapy using afatinib (an oral EGFR-tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI)) and pembrolizumab in recurrent/metastatic HNSCC,13 which is superior to the 10–28% ORR reported with afatinib monotherapy in historical data,14 supporting the potential of combining programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors with afatinib.

To investigate the optimal neoadjuvant regimen for HNSCC, we innovatively designed a combination therapy comprising chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and EGFR-TKI. Our regimen uniquely involves the continued administration of oral afatinib during the intervals between chemoimmunotherapy cycles. This strategy provides sustained tumor suppression during neoadjuvant therapy while reducing cumulative toxicity. To balance efficacy with the timing of surgery and avoid treatment-related delays, we selected a two-cycle regimen, although most previous chemoimmunotherapy regimens have employed three cycles.7 9 A shorter duration of neoadjuvant therapy reduces the risk of treatment-related surgical delays and disease progression during the waiting period for surgery, which is important in the context of curative treatment. Furthermore, our prior experience with a two-cycle, chemotherapy-free neoadjuvant regimen (neoCHANCE-1) has supported the use of the same number of cycles in this study.15 Regarding the selection of the afatinib dose, a fixed starting dose of 30 mg was chosen to avoid the adverse effects of treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) on curative surgery. Multiple studies in Asian populations have shown that the 30 mg dose is comparable in efficacy to the 40 mg dose but is associated with a lower incidence of TRAEs.16 17 Based on the same safety considerations, for patients who cannot tolerate 30 mg, we chose to interrupt afatinib treatment rather than further reduce the dose, to ensure that the patient’s physical condition can recover in a timely manner without affecting subsequent surgery. Regarding the application of postoperative maintenance therapy, we referenced the design of perioperative treatment for other tumor types,18 19 as well as key head and neck cancer studies such as KEYNOTE-68920 and GORTEC 2018–01/NIVOPOST-OP,21 and administered tislelizumab maintenance therapy to patients postoperatively.

Herein, we report for the first time a novel neoadjuvant regimen integrating chemotherapy, a PD-1 inhibitor, and an EGFR-TKI. Our study demonstrates that this regimen achieves a promising pathologic response in LA-HNSCC with manageable toxicity.

Methods

Study design

The neoCHANCE-2 study is a prospective, open-label, single-center, single-arm, phase 2 trial. This study was conducted in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, adhering to the ethical standards set by the institutional research committee, as well as the 1964 Helsinki declaration and its later amendments. This study was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05516589).

Patients

Eligible patients were at least 18 years of age and had histologically confirmed, treatment-naïve, resectable or borderline resectable LA-HNSCC (stage III-IVB according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer, eighth edition; stage II for human papillomavirus (HPV)-positive oropharyngeal cancer was also eligible). Additional inclusion criteria included an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0−1, at least one measurable target lesion according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) V.1.1 criteria, adequate organ function based on laboratory assessments, and the ability and willingness to provide written informed consent. Key exclusion criteria included primary salivary gland, thyroid, or nasopharyngeal carcinoma; uncontrolled autoimmune diseases requiring systemic therapy; active hepatitis; or HIV infection. Patients with prior exposure to ICIs, EGFR monoclonal antibodies, or EGFR-TKIs were also excluded. The study protocol, including complete eligibility criteria, is provided in the online supplemental materials.

Procedures

Patients received two cycles of neoadjuvant therapy with nab-paclitaxel (260 mg/m2), cisplatin (75 mg/m2), and tislelizumab (200 mg), administered every 3 weeks. Afatinib (30 mg) was given orally once daily during the intervals between chemoimmunotherapy cycles. Following completion of neoadjuvant treatment, all patients proceeded to standard radical surgery. Postoperatively, patients received adjuvant tislelizumab (200 mg every 3 weeks) for a total of 15 cycles. Adjuvant radiotherapy or chemoradiotherapy was administered according to clinical indications and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines. Treatment discontinuation was determined based on disease progression, adverse events, and predefined protocol criteria, considering the type and severity of the event.

Biopsy specimens and surgically resected primary tumor tissues were obtained for pathologic response assessment, which was determined by the percentage of residual viable tumor (RVT%) using previously reported methods.19 RVT% was calculated as the sum of viable tumor areas divided by the total tumor bed area (regression bed+RVT+necrosis), multiplied by 100%. Based on RVT%, pathologic responses were categorized as complete response (pCR, RVT%=0), major response (MPR, RVT% ≤10%), partial response (PPR, 10%< RVT% ≤50%), or no response (NPR, RVT% >50%). Radiologic assessments were performed at baseline and prior to surgery (6 weeks after treatment initiation) using identical imaging techniques in accordance with the RECIST V.1.1 criteria. Postoperative radiologic surveillance was planned every 3–4 months for the first 2 years, every 6 months for the following 3–5 years, and annually thereafter. Pretreatment (within 3 days prior to the first administration) and post-treatment blood samples (within 3 days prior to surgery), as well as pretreatment saliva specimens, were collected for predefined exploratory analyses.

Patients underwent physical examinations and laboratory tests before each 3-week treatment cycle and again prior to surgery. Adverse events were monitored and documented throughout the study treatment period and for 30 days after the final dose. The severity of adverse events was graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events V.5.0. Surgical complications were monitored intraoperatively and for 30 days post-surgery, and deaths occurring within 90 days after surgery were recorded.

Study endpoints

The primary endpoint of this study was the pCR rate, defined as the percentage of patients with no detectable RVT cells in the resected primary tumor. Secondary endpoints included the MPR rate (defined as the percentage of patients with no more than 10% RVT cells in the resected primary tumor); the ORR (defined as the percentage of patients achieving partial or complete response according to the RECIST V.1.1 criteria); event-free survival (EFS) (defined as the duration from treatment initiation to disease recurrence, progression, or death from any cause, whichever occurred first); OS (defined as the duration from treatment initiation to death from any cause); and safety (as indicated by the occurrence of TRAEs).

Biomarker analyses

Immunohistochemistry and multiplex immunofluorescence

The expression levels of PD-L1 were quantified using the combined positive score (CPS), calculated as the number of PD-L1-positive cells (including tumor cells, lymphocytes, and macrophages) divided by the total number of viable tumor cells, multiplied by 100. Pretreatment and surgically resected formal-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples were used to assess the tumor immune microenvironment. Two panels of primary antibodies were used: Panel 1 included antibodies against CD3, CD4, CD8a, Foxp3, and panCK; Panel 2 included antibodies against CD20, CD56, CD68, CD163, and panCK. The quantities of various cell subsets were expressed as the percentage of positively stained cells in all nucleated cells (see online supplemental materials for more details).

Systemic immune response

Peripheral blood samples were collected within 3 days prior to neoadjuvant treatment and within 3 days prior to surgery. Complete blood counts were obtained using an automated hematology analyzer. For flow cytometry, peripheral blood collected in K2EDTA tubes was processed within 6 hours. The BD Multitest 6-Color TBNK reagent containing CD3 FITC, CD16/CD56 PE, CD45 PerCP-Cy5.5, CD4 PE-Cy7, CD19 APC, and CD8 APC-Cy7 was used with BD Trucount tubes for absolute counting. Within the CD45+lymphocyte gate, CD3+T cells were identified; CD4+ and CD8+ subsets were then gated from CD3+cells. B cells were defined as CD3−CD19+, and natural killer (NK) cells as CD3−CD16+CD56+ (see online supplemental materials for more details).

Targeted NGS and genetic analysis

Targeted next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on both pretreatment tumor tissue and matched peripheral blood samples, using a custom NGS panel designed to assess 1,021 cancer-related genes.

Microbiota diversity

Saliva samples were collected prior to the treatment to analyze microbiome diversity. The microbial α diversity was assessed using abundance-based coverage estimator (ACE), Shannon, Chao, Simpson, and Sobs indices. β diversity was analyzed through principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) based on unweighted-UniFrac distance, and significance was determined using the Adonis test (see online supplemental materials for more details).

Statistical analyses

In this study, a Simon’s minimax two-stage design was used to estimate the sample size. Assuming that neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in combination with afatinib would increase the pCR rate from approximately 17%8 to 37% compared with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy alone, with a one-sided α level of 0.05 for type I error and a statistical power of 80%, 17 patients were required to be enrolled in the first stage. If no more than two patients achieved pCR, the study would be terminated. Otherwise, the study would continue to enroll an additional 12 patients in the second stage. If at least 9 patients achieved pCR out of a total of 29 patients, the null hypothesis would be rejected. Considering an estimated dropout rate of 10%, a total of 32 patients were required to be enrolled.

The primary endpoint was analyzed in patients who underwent radical surgery. Radiologic response, toxicity, and outcomes were analyzed in all patients who received at least one dose of the study drug. Frequencies or percentages were used to present categorical variables, whereas continuous variables were shown as means with SDs or medians with IQRs. Pathologic response rates were reported as frequencies and percentages, and corresponding 95% CIs were calculated using the Clopper-Pearson method. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate EFS and OS rates, and the log-rank test was employed for comparisons. Safety was assessed using descriptive statistics. Comparisons of continuous variables between two groups were conducted using the independent samples t-test or non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test. For comparisons involving three or more groups, the Kruskal-Wallis test was used. For comparisons of data before and after treatment, paired samples t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used. The association between continuous variables was determined using Spearman correlation coefficients or simple linear regression. Comparisons of categorical groups were conducted using Pearson χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. A two-tailed p value below 0.05 indicated statistical significance. Analyses were carried out with GraphPad Prism software V.9.4.1 (San Diego, California, USA) and R software V.3.6.1 (Vienna, Austria).

Results

Participant characteristics

A total of 43 patients with LA-HNSCC were screened for eligibility, of whom 3 were excluded for various reasons (figure 1). Finally, 40 patients were enrolled to receive neoadjuvant treatment, with a median age of 56.0 years (IQR 49.25−61.75), and the majority were male (80%, 32/40). The most common primary tumor site was the hypopharynx (19/40 (47.5%)), followed by oropharynx (14/40 (35%)). A total of 27 patients (67.5%) presented with stage IVA/B disease. Treatment response

Of the 32 patients evaluable for pathologic response, 13 (40.6%, 95% CI 23.7% to 59.4%) achieved a pCR (including 8 with oropharyngeal cancer, 4 with hypopharyngeal cancer, and 1 with oral cavity cancer), and 8 patients achieved an RVT% of >0 and ≤10%, resulting in an MPR (including pCR) rate of 65.6% (95% CI 46.8% to 81.4%). The remaining five patients achieved PPR (15.6%, 95% CI 5.3% to 32.8%) and six achieved NPR (18.8%, 95% CI 7.2% to 36.4%) (online supplemental table 2). Pathologic tumor shrinkage from baseline is shown in figure 2B.

Among the 40 patients evaluable for radiologic response, 8 achieved complete response (CR) (20%, 95% CI 9.1% to 35.6%), and 25 achieved partial response (PR) (62.5%, 95% CI 45.8% to 77.3%), with an ORR of 82.5% (95% CI 67.2% to 92.7%) (online supplemental table 3). Representative images from one patient at baseline and after neoadjuvant treatment are shown in figure 2C. Seven patients with a radiologic CR received surgery, and all achieved pCR, suggesting that the pathologic response was consistent with the radiologic response (simple linear regression, p=0.008, figure 2D).

The percentage of pathologic regression or radiologic regression in patients with hypopharyngeal cancer tended to be lower than in patients with oropharyngeal cancer, although these differences were not statistically significant (online supplemental figure 1). In addition, age, sex, body mass index, smoking history, and drinking history were not associated with pathologic response, nor were they associated with radiologic response (all p values>0.05) (online supplemental figure 2).

Among all 32 patients who underwent surgical treatment, one case did not undergo cervical lymph node dissection (paranasal sinus carcinoma). In the lymph node pathologic responses of the 31 patients who underwent surgery, 5 patients achieved pCR (with all corresponding primary lesions also being pCR); 3 patients showed MPR (with 2 primary lesions being pCR and 1 being MPR); 8 patients showed PPR (with five corresponding primary lesions achieving pCR/MPR); and 11 patients showed NPR (with five corresponding primary lesions achieving pCR/MPR) (p=0.07, Fisher’s exact test). Additionally, four patients had negative lymph node metastasis.

Survival outcomes

As of April 2026, the median follow-up time was 25.9 (range, 2.4−42.6) months. The swimmer plot summarizing the outcomes of all patients is shown in figure 3A, and clinical follow-up is still ongoing. To date, of the 40 patients enrolled in the study, 5 patients have died of disease progression (including two cases of disease progression without radiographic confirmation), 1 died from an accident, and 7 patients experienced disease progression but are still alive. The estimated 1-year OS rate was 97.3% (95% CI 92.2% to 100%). The estimated 1-year EFS rate was 81.8% (95% CI 70.5% to 95.0%) (figure 3B−C). Of the 32 patients who underwent resection, the estimated 1-year OS rate was 96.8% (95% CI 90.8% to 100%), and the estimated 1-year EFS rate was 81.2% (95% CI 68.8% to 96.0%) (online supplemental figure 3).

Landmark analyses showed a higher cumulative risk of progression in patients with hypopharyngeal cancers compared with those with other cancer types beyond the 10-month landmark point (p=0.005), while no statistical difference was found in the risk of death (figure 3F−G).

Safety

TRAEs are summarized in table 2. The most common TRAEs of any grade were alopecia (100%), followed by lymphopenia (57.5%), diarrhea (55%), and rash (55%). Grade 3−4 TRAEs occurred in seven (7/40, 17.5%) patients. Five patients experienced grade 3 lymphopenia (12.5%), which was the most common grade 3−4 TRAE. Moreover, two patients experienced grade 4 TRAEs, including one case of hypokalemia and one case of hypocalcemia. No grade 5 TRAEs leading to death occurred, and no previously unknown or unexpected TRAEs were observed. Two patients experienced temporary discontinuation of afatinib due to grade 3−4 TRAEs. During the adjuvant phase, no new safety signals were identified. The most common TRAEs were dermatitis and oral mucositis. No grade 4 or grade 5 TRAEs occurred (online supplemental table 4).

Predefined biomarker analyses

PD-L1 expression

A total of 32 patients had available tumor tissue for PD-L1 assessment, and 24 of them underwent surgery. Of these, 26 (81.3%) patients were PD-L1 positive (defined as CPS ≥1), including 20 (62.5%) with a CPS of 1–19 and 6 (18.8%) with a CPS of ≥20. Two patients with a PD-L1 CPS 50 and 70 achieved PPR/NPR, while three patients with a PD-L1 CPS <1 achieved pCR. The percentage of pathologic response was not significantly associated with the distribution of PD-L1 expression (p=0.744, figure 4A). Similarly, no significant difference was observed in the PD-L1 expression distribution according to tumor site (p=0.32, online supplemental figure 4). At the time of follow-up cut-off, all patients with CPS ≥20 remained alive, although no significant difference in OS was observed between patients with different PD-L1 expression levels. Similarly, no significant difference in EFS was observed between the two groups (figure 4B).

Gene mutations and TMB

The most frequently mutated genes were TP53 (9/15, 60%), followed by PIK3CA (6/15, 40%), FGF3 (5/15, 33%), and FGF4 (5/15, 33%) (figure 4C). The tumor mutational burden (TMB) level was not significantly associated with pathologic tumor response (p=0.792, figure 4D). Significantly, TP53 wild-type patients were more likely to achieve a pCR/MPR compared with those with a TP53 mutation (p=0.028). Alterations in FGF3 and FGF4 were more common in patients who achieved PPR/NPR, although the differences did not reach statistical significance (p=0.089, figure 4E and online supplemental table 6).

Oral microbiota diversity

We performed exploratory analyses of the oral microbiota composition and diversity in saliva samples collected from 14 patients, of whom 8 achieved pCR/MPR and 6 achieved PPR/NPR (online supplemental table 7). The relative community abundance at the phylum level is shown in figure 5A. Firmicutes, Bacteroidota, and Proteobacteria were predominant in all collected samples. Alpha diversity, calculated using ACE, Shannon, Chao, Simpson, and Sobs indices, showed no significant differences between pCR/MPR and PPR/NPR groups (all p>0.05; figure 5B,C and online supplemental figure 5). To further profile the microbial composition, β diversity was assessed using PCoA based on unweighted UniFrac distances. Patients with pCR/MPR were clearly separated from those with PPR/NPR, indicating a significant difference in microbial composition between the two groups (p=0.006) (figure 5D). Linear discriminant analysis effect size (LEfSe) identified taxa associated with pathologic response from order to genus level. Porphyromonas, belonging to Porphyromonadaceae, and Corynebacterium, belonging to Corynebacteriaceae, were associated with pCR/MPR and PPR/NPR, respectively (figure 5E).

The representative images of multiplex immunofluorescence along with artificial intelligence-assisted automated annotations of cell types are shown in figure 6A. Following neoadjuvant treatment, a trend toward higher cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) and B-cell infiltration was observed in patients with pCR/MPR compared with those with PPR/NPR, although these changes were not statistically significant. Post-treatment surgical specimens were assessed for the presence of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs). Similar to the findings for B cells, patients who achieved pCR/MPR showed a trend toward a higher TLS percentage than those who achieved PPR/NPR (p=0.080) (online supplemental figure 8).

To better understand the spatial arrangement among immune cell subtypes and tumor cells, we identified immune cell subtypes within a 50 µm radius of tumor cells, evaluating both their average distance and average number in relation to the tumor cells (online supplemental figure 9). In the pCR/MPR group, patients who achieved a pCR were excluded from evaluation due to complete tumor cell regression, whereas only those who achieved MPR were assessed. Our observations indicated that the majority of immune cell subtypes exhibited an increased average number in proximity to tumor cells within the MPR group compared with the PPR/NPR group, with a particularly notable presence of B cells (p=0.02). Nonetheless, within the MPR group, these immune cells appeared to maintain a greater distance from the tumor cells relative to their distribution in the PPR/NPR group (online supplemental figure 9).

Systemic immune response

In all study patients, lymphocyte subsets collected from pre-neoadjuvant and post-neoadjuvant treatment were significantly altered, with a significant increase in CD3+T cells and a decrease in B cells. Notably, the increase of CD3+T cells was primarily characterized by an elevation in the percentage of CD8+T cells, while no significant changes were observed in CD4+T cell levels before and after treatment. Similar trends were observed in the pathologic response, irrespective of whether a pCR/MPR or a PPR/NPR was achieved (figure 7A,B).

The composition of immune cell subsets was further analyzed to identify potential predictors of treatment response. Patients who achieved pCR/MPR had significantly lower levels of CD4+T cells at baseline compared with those with PPR/NPR. Discussion

In this study, we demonstrate that a novel neoadjuvant strategy, including two cycles of chemoimmunotherapy with afatinib administered during the intervals between chemoimmunotherapy cycles, is feasible and safe for LA-HNSCC. To our knowledge, this is the first report of chemotherapy combined with dual blockade of the PD-1 and EGFR pathways as neoadjuvant treatment for LA-HNSCC. This new neoadjuvant regimen resulted in a pCR rate of 40.6% and an ORR of 82.5%. The most common TRAEs of any grade were alopecia, followed by lymphopenia, diarrhea, and rash, with lymphopenia being the most common grade 3−4 TRAEs. No grade 5 TRAEs leading to death occurred. There were no treatment-related delays in surgery, and the triplet neoadjuvant treatment did not increase perioperative morbidity or have any concerning effects on surgical outcomes.

In the field of immunotherapy for LA-HNSCC, two recently completed large-scale clinical trials, KEYNOTE-689 and GORTEC 2018–01/NIVOPOST-OP, have provided important insights into the value of immunotherapy at this stage. KEYNOTE-689 adopted an extensive perioperative strategy, including two cycles of preoperative pembrolizumab induction therapy followed by postoperative adjuvant pembrolizumab.20 The GORTEC 2018–01/NIVOPOST-OP trial focused on patients with high-risk, resectable LA-HNSCC, evaluating the addition of nivolumab as concurrent and adjuvant therapy to standard postoperative treatment.21 Although the specific strategies differed between the two trials, both significantly improved EFS (KEYNOTE-689: HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.58 to 0.92) and disease-free survival (NIVOPOST-OP: HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.60 to 0.98), providing strong evidence supporting perioperative therapy for LA-HNSCC. In terms of pathologic response, the MPR rate with two cycles of preoperative pembrolizumab in the KEYNOTE-689 study was only 9.4%, and the pCR rate was 3.0%. In contrast, our neoCHANCE-2 study achieved significantly higher pCR (40.6%) and MPR (65.6%) rates. Although whether MPR/pCR can serve as a surrogate endpoint for OS remains controversial,22 23 a higher pathologic response rate still holds potential survival benefits.24 Therefore, this triplet regimen warrants further validation in larger-scale phase 3 trials.

Immunotherapy combined with other modalities has been increasingly explored in the neoadjuvant setting for HNSCC, with chemoimmunotherapy being the most extensively investigated regimen. Several studies have reported promising pathologic responses. Zhang et al reported a pCR rate of 37.0% and an MPR rate of 74.1% in 27 patients receiving three cycles of chemotherapy (albumin-bound paclitaxel or docetaxel plus cisplatin) combined with camrelizumab.9 In another study employing the same regimen, MPR and pCR rates reached 63.0% and 55.6%, respectively.7 A two-cycle regimen of gemcitabine and cisplatin combined with toripalimab achieved an MPR of 44% (including 17% pCR).8 In our previously reported two-cycle neoadjuvant regimen of afatinib combined with tislelizumab, the MPR rate was 35%.15 In the present study, we uniquely administered afatinib during chemoimmunotherapy intervals, achieving continuous suppression of tumor proliferation pathways through irreversible EGFR/HER2 tyrosine kinase inhibition during treatment intervals. Overall, the pathological response rates observed in our two-cycle triplet regimen are comparable to those reported for three-cycle neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and superior to those of two-cycle chemoimmunotherapy or immunotherapy plus targeted therapy regimens. In addition, compared with a three-cycle regimen, a shorter neoadjuvant treatment duration is justifiable when the anticipated therapeutic benefit is limited, as it minimizes delays to definitive surgery without substantially altering the surgical plan.

In our study, we observed a consistent correlation between radiological response and pathologic response. Notably, several previous studies on neoadjuvant immunotherapy for HNSCC have reported that no such correlation was observed following short-term treatment.25 26 Due to the unique mechanism of action of immunotherapy, which induces an increase in tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes during treatment, specific response patterns such as pseudoprogression, hyperprogression, and delayed responses may occur.27 Consequently, the extent of the pathological response may not fully align with the extent of the radiological response. Unlike neoadjuvant therapy studies using only immune checkpoint inhibitors, our regimen combined chemotherapy, a PD-1 inhibitor, and an EGFR-TKI. Chemotherapy and EGFR inhibition can induce rapid apoptosis and necrosis of tumor cells, which is more likely to result in radiologically measurable tumor shrinkage in the short-term.7 15 28 29 This contrasts with treatment with ICIs alone where early radiological changes may primarily reflect immune cell infiltration rather than a reduction in tumor cells. In this study cohort, we also observed that some patients classified as PR according to RECIST criteria achieved pCR, while some patients classified as stable disease according to RECIST criteria achieved MPR. However, in a correlation analysis of the percentage of tumor shrinkage, the two measures showed significant consistency.

In evaluating lymph node pathological response, we observed a lower pathological response rate in lymph nodes compared with the primary tumor site. This finding aligns with previous studies30–32 and our earlier findings on the chemo-free regimen combining tislelizumab with afatinib.15 Whether this discrepancy represents persistent inconsistency or indicates a delayed response warrants further investigation.

Another advantage of this time-phased treatment strategy is avoiding the superimposition of adverse effects. In this study, no treatment-related deaths occurred, and no previously unknown or unexpected TRAEs were observed. Lymphopenia was the most common grade 3−4 TRAEs, primarily due to reductions in B lymphocytes, as observed in our analyses of immune cell composition. Given the higher risk of diarrhea caused by afatinib, careful attention should be paid to the nutritional status, particularly electrolyte levels. However, we also observed that in patients with laryngeal and hypopharyngeal cancer, the rapid relief of dysphagia after neoadjuvant treatment helped improve quality of life. Furthermore, the combination of chemoimmunotherapy with afatinib did not increase surgical complications or perioperative mortality, but effectively reduced the difficulty and risk of surgery by shrinking tumor size. At the time of follow-up cut-off, some patients had not completed the postoperative adjuvant phase of ICI maintenance therapy. Therefore, data on postoperative TRAEs are still being collected, and we will report these results in the future.

We observed no deaths in patients who achieved pCR/MPR at the time of data cut-off, indicating a potential long-term survival benefit attributed to superior pathologic response. However, it is crucial to emphasize that although the degree of pathologic response has been regarded as a candidate surrogate endpoint for survival in the neoadjuvant setting,33 whether it leads to long-term survival benefits is still unconfirmed. Additionally, we noticed that the disease control in hypopharyngeal cancer was less satisfactory compared with non-hypopharyngeal cancers. Hypopharyngeal cancer is known to have the poorest prognosis among all HNSCC,34 with 40% of recurrences occurring within 6 months after the last treatment.35 Given the sample size limitations, whether the efficacy of triplet neoadjuvant treatment also exhibits tumor site specificity warrants further investigation. The majority of patients in our study are still undergoing survival follow-up, and we will report long-term survival outcomes in the future.

The frequently altered genes detected in our study were consistent with those reported in several large-scale studies on HNSCC.36 TP53 mutations, as the most common somatic genomic alterations in HNSCC, are significantly associated with poorer survival outcomes and resistance to radiotherapy and chemotherapy.37 Recent studies have shown that tumors with TP53 mutations are characterized by an immunosuppressive microenvironment,38 and TP53 alterations are associated with inferior OS in patients receiving immunotherapy.39 Additionally, patients with HNSCC with wild-type TP53 have been shown to exhibit better treatment responses to neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.7 Consistent with these findings, our study also indicated that patients with wild-type TP53 were more likely to achieve better pathologic response to neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy combined with afatinib. Furthermore, we observed a higher percentage of co-amplification of CCND1/FGF3/FGF4/FGF19. These genes co-localize on the same amplicon at 11q13 and are frequently co-amplified, with an incidence of approximately 17−30% in HNSCC.40 41 Amplification of chromosome 11q13 is correlated with poor efficacy of anti-PD-1 therapy and may increase the risk of hyperprogression, suggesting the need for cautious use of immunotherapy in these patients.40 42 43 However, it remains uncertain whether these patients could benefit from immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy or targeted agents. In our study, we observed a lower pathologic response in patients with co-amplification of CCND1/FGF4/FGF3/FGF19, even when treated with chemoimmunotherapy combined with afatinib. For these patients, FGF/FGFR-oriented therapeutic strategies, with or without additional therapies, may offer a better treatment approach.44

Although several clinical pilot studies have highlighted the complex roles of the oral microbiome in tumor progression, the systemic impact of the microbiome on cancer treatment remains uncertain.45 46 Our microbiome analysis based on β diversity revealed a significant difference in the composition of oral microbiota between pCR/MPR and PPR/NPR groups. However, these findings are not entirely consistent with those of previous studies. In a previously reported phase 2 study, salivary metagenome sequencing at baseline performed on patients with oral squamous cell carcinoma undergoing neoadjuvant immunotherapy showed that the species richness of the salivary microbiome was higher in pathologic responders than in nonresponders, as evidenced by a higher Shannon index. However, the principal component analysis of β diversity found no significant relationship between the community composition of the oral microbiome and treatment response.46 Our LEfSe analysis identified Porphyromonas (family Porphyromonadaceae) as enriched in patients achieving pCR/MPR, whereas Corynebacterium (family Corynebacteriaceae) was associated with PPR/NPR. Interestingly, previous studies have reported that Porphyromonas species, particularly Porphyromonas gingivalis, can modulate the tumor immune microenvironment by inducing chronic inflammation and suppressing T-cell responses, which might paradoxically enhance response to immune checkpoint blockade in certain contexts.47 Conversely, Corynebacterium has been linked to altered metabolic profiles and local immunosuppression in head and neck cancers.48 49 While direct comparisons across studies are challenging due to differences in treatment regimens and sampling methods, our findings suggest that specific oral microbial signatures may serve as potential biomarkers for response to neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy combined with EGFR-TKI. Further validation in larger, independent cohorts is warranted. In addition, the interpretation of our findings must consider the limitation that not all potential confounders (eg, diet, smoking, use of specific mouthwashes, or recent antibiotic exposure unrelated to treatment) were systematically measured and adjusted for. Future studies designed to validate these associations should incorporate detailed profiling of such lifestyle and medical factors.

Different combined agents may result in varying levels of immune enhancement or even suppression.50 To improve understanding of the effects of combined chemotherapy and PD-1 inhibitor with EGFR-TKI treatment on the immune system, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of the immune environment and its dynamics in both peripheral blood and tumor tissues. However, since this study represents the first application of such triplet neoadjuvant treatment strategy for HNSCC in both clinical and preclinical settings, it is challenging to directly compare our findings with those of prior studies. In this clinical trial, a consistent increase of CD8+T cells was noted in both the systemic and tumor-specific immune microenvironments of responders, exhibiting robust antitumor activity. Conversely, in non-responders, despite an observed increase in systemic CD8+T cells, these cells were not effectively recruited to the tumor site. Comparable findings have been observed in studies involving chemotherapy or immunochemotherapy.8 51 52 Following neoadjuvant therapy, a reduction in CD56+cells was observed within the tumor microenvironment. CD56 is a principal marker for NK cells; however, this finding contradicts previous reports regarding NK cell alterations in the context of immunotherapy or immunochemotherapy.7 53 It is important to acknowledge that CD56 is also expressed on various other immune cells,54 and NK cells are properly defined as CD56+CD3−. Owing to technical constraints preventing co-staining of CD3 and CD56 in the same panel, our “CD56+” population may include NKT-like cells or activated T cells. Moreover, the absence of data on additional markers, such as CD16, precludes a definitive conclusion that the observed reduction in CD56+cells predominantly reflects changes in NK cells.

B cells appear to play a significant role in the efficacy of neoadjuvant combination therapy in this clinical trial. B cells exhibit antitumor capabilities through the production of antibodies targeting tumor-associated antigens, thereby facilitating phagocytosis and presenting antigens to CD8+T cells.55 Following neoadjuvant treatment, there was increased infiltration of B cells alongside CTLs. Nevertheless, a notable reduction in circulating B cells was observed in peripheral blood, irrespective of the pathologic response. This discovery partially aligns with the results reported in other studies.56 57 It has been noted that chemotherapy significantly decreases B cells but does not affect T cells in solid tumors.57 We hypothesized that, in patients responding to triplet neoadjuvant treatment, the observed reduction in peripheral blood B lymphocytes may be due partly to chemotherapy’s cytotoxic effects and partly to the local recruitment of these cells to the tumor site. This recruitment may synergistically enhance a localized antitumor immune response in conjunction with T cells. Conversely, in non-responders, B cells were not effectively recruited to the tumor microenvironment. Moreover, in our study, pCR/MPR after neoadjuvant combination therapy was associated with trends toward higher TLS presence and increased post-treatment CD20 expression. This aligns with evidence linking TLS abundance to improved immunotherapy outcomes in solid tumors,58 59 potentially by facilitating adaptive immune activation in immunologically cold tumors such as HNSCC.60

In another recently published study from our group, we evaluated a chemotherapy-free neoadjuvant regimen (tislelizumab plus afatinib).15 Within this cohort, we similarly observed increased infiltration of CD8+ and CD20+ cells in the post-treatment tumor microenvironment, consistent with features of immune activation. Notably, in the triplet regimen reported here (which included chemotherapy), we observed a significant decrease in the CD56+NK cell population—a phenomenon absent in the chemotherapy-free cohort. This differential immunomodulatory effect suggests that chemotherapy may exert specific influences on innate immune cell subsets, thereby reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment and potentially enhancing or altering tumor response patterns to PD-1/EGFR blockade.

Notably, post-treatment tumor-infiltrating macrophages (CD68+/CD163+) were unexpectedly low, in contrast to prior reports of increased macrophage recruitment after anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy.30 61 This difference may stem from our triplet regimen, as the addition of chemotherapy and an EGFR-TKI could counteract ICI-induced macrophage attraction. The post-chemoimmunotherapy macrophage response varies: no significant change in head and neck cancer,8 but a marked reduction in M1/M2 macrophages in non-MPR gastric/gastroesophageal tumors.62 Furthermore, the relatively low macrophage counts in our study may reflect biological heterogeneity across HNSCC subsites, as a substantial proportion of our patients had hypopharyngeal cancer, which is a subsite known to harbor a distinctive tumor microenvironment.61 63 These findings highlight the complex, regimen-dependent and site-dependent modulation of tumor microenvironment myeloid cells.

To better understand the complexities of the tumor microenvironment, there is growing focus on the interactions among various cell types within the tissue matrix. A previous study reported that in patients with HNSCC undergoing neoadjuvant radioimmunotherapy, CD8+T cells exhibited closer proximity to tumor cells following treatment in those who demonstrated a therapeutic response.64 Additionally, the presence of CD8+T cells near cancer cells is a more reliable indicator of outcome than their total numbers.65 In our spatial phenotyping analysis of immune cells, the limited sample size, consisting of only three responders, posed challenges in achieving statistically significant results. Nevertheless, some intriguing phenomena were identified. After neoadjuvant treatment, responders exhibited a greater average number of immune cells in proximity to tumor cells. However, these immune cells tended to be located at a greater distance from the tumor cells compared with those in non-responders. We hypothesized that, in responders, neoadjuvant treatment disrupts the dense tumor architecture and significantly reduces the tumor cell population, resulting in an increased spatial separation between the remaining tumor cells and immune cells.

The main limitations of the present study include its single-center, single-arm design and small sample size. Additionally, the follow-up time was not long enough. The efficacy and survival benefit of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy combined with afatinib in HNSCC requires verification through more large-scale clinical studies with longer follow-up periods. Due to the limited sample size, the exploratory analyses were relatively underpowered. The bioinformatics findings lack independent cohort validation and in vitro/in vivo functional validation. Future studies are needed to determine the predictive value of these biomarkers. The overall cohort size also limits the performance of subgroup analyses. Furthermore, the analysis of tumor immune microenvironment changes from pre-neoadjuvant to post-neoadjuvant treatment was not sufficiently in-depth or comprehensive. Fully understanding the mechanisms underlying this combined treatment’s effect on the antitumor immune response would help develop more effective neoadjuvant therapies for HNSCC.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we report for the first time the promising efficacy and acceptable safety profile of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy combined with afatinib in the treatment of patients with LA-HNSCC. Neoadjuvant combination therapy significantly improved pathologic response rates without adversely affecting surgical outcomes, and potentially enhanced the antitumor immune microenvironment. These findings support evaluating this combination therapy in future phase 3 clinical trials.

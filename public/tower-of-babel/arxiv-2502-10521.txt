Quantum Mycielskians: symmetries, twin vertices and distinguishing labelings
Arkadiusz Bochniak, Igor Chełstowski, Paweł Kasprzak, Piotr M. Sołtan

Abstract
. We investigate a quantum generalization of the Mycielski construction for quantum graphs. In particular, we analyze the quantum symmetries of quantum Mycielskians and their relation to the symmetries of the underlying quantum graphs. We introduce a quantum analogue of graphs with twin vertices and show that, for classical graphs with a small number of vertices, this notion reduces to the classical one. Finally, we propose a quantum extension of the distinguishing number and examine its behavior under the quantum Mycielski construction.



1.
Introduction

Classical graph theory has a wide range of intriguing applications beyond pure mathematics, with particularly strong connections to computer science and information theory. In communication theory, graphs are used to model channels, where vertices represent possible inputs and outputs, and edges encode confusability relation between signals. This perspective naturally extends to quantum information theory, where the role of confusability graphs is played by non-commutative operator systems associated with quantum channels (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
13
,
15
,
19
]
). In this setting, Kraus operators of a quantum channel generate an algebraic structure that serves as the quantum analogue of the adjacency relations of a graph. The passage from classical to quantum thus requires reformulating standard graph invariants in operator-algebraic terms. Among the classical quantities that admit such generalizations are chromatic numbers - capturing the minimal resources needed for proper labelings - and clique numbers, which quantify the largest completely connected substructures. The (classical) chromatic number of a graph is the minimum number of labels (colors) that can be assigned to its vertices, so that the endpoints of every edge receive distinct labels. The clique number, by contrast, is the maximum size of a complete graph that can be embedded as a subgraph of the original. For example, graphs with clique number
2
are precisely the triangle-free graphs. These invariants, when reinterpreted in the non-commutative framework, provide valuable insights into zero-error communication capacities of quantum channels and the interplay between combinatorial and operator-algebraic methods.

A long-standing problem in classical graph theory was to determine whether triangle-free graphs with arbitrarily large chromatic number exist. Equivalently, one asked whether the absence of small cliques imposes an upper bound on the chromatic complexity of a graph. This problem was resolved in
[
18
]
, where Mycielski introduced an explicit iterative construction. Given a triangle-free graph
G
, he defined a new graph
\mu(G)
, now known as the
Mycielskian
of
G
, which is also triangle-free, contains
G
as an induced subgraph, and whose chromatic number satisfies

\chi(\mu(G))=\chi(G)+1.

By iterating this construction, one obtains an infinite family of triangle-free graphs with unbounded chromatic number, thereby providing a negative answer to the aforementioned problem. The Mycielski construction has since become a paradigmatic example of how graph transformations can alter chromatic properties without affecting clique structure.

The natural question of extending this phenomenon to the quantum setting was addressed in
[
6
]
, where two of the present authors introduced a non-commutative analogue of Mycielski’s construction. In this framework, classical graphs are replaced by
quantum graphs
, modeled by operator systems generated by Kraus operators of quantum channels. The proposed construction, termed the
quantum Mycielskian
, takes a finite quantum graph
\mathcal{G}
as input and produces a new one that contains the original as a subgraph in the operator-algebraic sense. This quantum analogue preserves certain structural features of the classical case but exhibits important differences in the behavior of invariants. Specifically, it was shown that while the clique number remains unchanged under the transformation – mirroring the classical situation – the (quantum) chromatic number
\chi_{\bullet}
does not necessarily increase by exactly one. Instead, it satisfies only a weaker property:

\chi_{\bullet}(\mu(\mathcal{G}))\leq\chi_{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})+1,

with strict equality not guaranteed in general.

In this paper, we investigate further properties of the Mycielski construction in the quantum framework, with an emphasis on how structural and symmetry-related features behave under this transformation. Section
1.1
begins with a review of the classical Mycielski construction, highlighting its role in generating triangle-free graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number. Section
1.2
then sets up the operator-algebraic formalism of quantum graphs and introduces the actions of quantum groups on them. These actions provide the natural symmetry framework in the non-commutative setting, and their behavior under graph transformations will play a central role in our analysis.

A key technical aspect of this study concerns actions that preserve partitions of unity, which, in the commutative case, correspond precisely to proper vertex colorings or labelings of graphs. We discuss this notion in Section
1.3
, and its relevance for quantum analogues of chromatic invariants will be revisited throughout the paper (see, in particular, Section
4
). At the end of Section
1
, we recall the definition of the quantum Mycielskian and prepare the ground for our new results.

Section
2
is devoted to isomorphisms of quantum Mycielskians. In Section
3
, we investigate how the Mycielski construction interacts with the quantum automorphism groups of graphs, showing that this process naturally gives rise to the notion of
quantum twin vertices
. These are studied in detail in Sections
3.1
and
3.2
, as well as in the Appendix.

Finally, in Section
4
, we introduce the
quantum distinguishing number
, a non-commutative analogue of the classical distinguishing number that measures the minimal labeling required to break all symmetries of a graph. We analyze how this invariant behaves under the quantum Mycielski construction and compare the results with the classical situation, further illustrating both the parallels and the divergences between the two frameworks.

1.1.
Classical Mycielski construction

Let
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
be a (finite, undirected) graph. The
Mycielskian
of
G
, denoted by
\mu(G)
is a graph with the set of vertices
V_{\mu(G)}=\{\bullet\}\sqcup V_{G}\sqcup V_{G}
, where
\bullet
denotes a distinguished point, called
master vertex
. The edge set
E_{\mu(G)}
can be specified by the adjacency matrix
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(G)}
. We recall that the adjacency matrix
A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
for an undirected graph
G
is a
\{0,1\}
-matrix whose columns and rows are indexed by the set of vertices, and
(A_{\scriptscriptstyle G})_{i,j}
is non-zero if and only if
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E
. For the Mycielskian, we have

A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(G)}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\vec{0}^{T}&\vec{1}^{T}\\
\vec{0}&A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}&A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}\\
\vec{1}&A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}&0\end{bmatrix},

where
\vec{1}
(resp.
\vec{0}
) is the column vector with all entries being
1
(resp.
0
). Mycielski’s theorem
[
18
]
says that denoting by
\omega(\cdot)
the clique number of a graph we have
\omega(\mu(G))=\omega(G)
, i.e. the clique number remains unchanged under the passage from
G
to
\mu(G)
, and for the chromatic numbers
\chi
we have
\chi(\mu(G))=\chi(G)+1
.

The Mycielski construction can be generalized to so-called
r
-Mycielskian
(
r\geq 2
) also known as
higher cones
over graphs (
[
23
,
24
]
). In this case,

V_{\mu_{r-1}(G)}=\{\bullet\}\sqcup V^{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup V^{r}

with
V^{j}=\{v_{1}^{j},\ldots,v^{j}_{|V_{G}|}\}
,
j=1,\ldots,r
denoting distinct copies of
V_{G}
, and

E=E_{G}\cup\bigcup_{i=0}^{r-1}\bigl\{\{v^{i}_{j},v^{i+1}_{j^{\prime}}\}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\{v_{j},v_{j^{\prime}}\}\in E_{G}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\{v^{r}_{1},\bullet\},\{v^{r}_{2},\bullet\},\ldots,\{v^{r}_{|V_{G}|},\bullet\}\bigr\}.

1.2.
Quantum graphs and their quantum symmetries

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a finite-dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. A
\delta
-form
on
\mathsf{A}
is a faithful state
\psi
on
\mathsf{A}
such that writing
L^{2}(\mathsf{A},\psi)
for the GNS Hilbert space for
\psi
and regarding the multiplication map
m\colon\mathsf{A}\otimes\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{A}
as a linear operator
L^{2}(\mathsf{A},\psi)\otimes L^{2}(\mathsf{A},\psi)\to L^{2}(\mathsf{A},\psi)
we have

mm^{*}=\delta(\psi)^{2}\mathrm{id}_{L^{2}(\mathsf{A},\psi)}

for some positive constant
\delta(\psi)
. Since
\mathsf{A}
and
L^{2}(\mathsf{A},\psi)
are naturally isomorphic as vector spaces, we usually write
\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{A}}
instead of
\mathrm{id}_{L^{2}(\mathsf{A},\psi)}
even when viewed as a map of Hilbert spaces.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be the category of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras as defined in
[
27
,
25
]
, and
\mathfrak{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
its dual category. A
quantum space

\mathbb{X}
is an object from
\mathfrak{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
and its corresponding
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra is denoted by
\C(\mathbb{X})
. We say that a quantum space
\mathbb{X}
is
finite
if the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{X})
is finite-dimensional.

A
quantum graph

\mathcal{G}
is specified by elements of a triple
(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
, where

•

\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
is a finite quantum space,

•

\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
is a
\delta
-form on the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
,

•

A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
is a self-adjoint operator on the GNS Hilbert space
L^{2}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
such that

m(A\otimes A)m^{*}=A\quad\text{and}\quad A=(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})}\otimes\eta^{*}m)(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})}\otimes A\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})})(m^{*}\eta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})}),

where

m\colon\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})\otimes\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})\quad\text{and}\quad\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})

are the multiplication and unit maps regarded as linear operators between Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})\otimes L^{2}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})\to L^{2}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
and
\mathbb{C}\to L^{2}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
respectively.

For alternative approaches to quantum graphs and comparison between them see
[
11
]
and references therein. In what follows, the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
will be denoted by
\C(\mathcal{G})
and the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
by
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
. We will also shorten
\delta(\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
to
\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
. A quantum graph
\mathcal{G}
is called
irreflexive
if
m(A\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})})m^{\ast}=0
.

In what follows, we will freely identify the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of functions on the quantum sets of vertices of the quantum graph with the Hilbert spaces obtained via the GNS construction for the corresponding
\delta
-forms. In particular, all linear maps between the considered
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras (homomorphisms and others) may be understood as maps of Hilbert spaces, and we will have at our disposal their hermitian adjoints.

Definition 1.2

(
[
8
, Definition 6.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a quantum graph. The
quantum symmetry group
of
\mathcal{G}
is the compact quantum group
\QAut(\mathcal{G})
equipped with a
\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
-preserving action
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\colon\C(\mathcal{G})\to\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
such that
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\circ{A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}}=(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\circ\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
and which is universal among all
\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
-preserving compact quantum group actions
\alpha\colon\C(\mathcal{G})\to\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\C(\mathbb{G})
such that
\alpha\circ A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}=(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathbb{G})})\circ\alpha
.

Any choice of a basis
\{e_{1},\dotsc,e_{n}\}
in
\C(\mathcal{G})
determines elements
u_{i,j}
of
\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
by

\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(e_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\otimes u_{i,j}.

If the basis
\{e_{1},\dotsc,e_{n}\}
is orthonormal in
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
then the matrix
u
with elements
u_{i,j}
is unitary. In all cases it is a finite-dimensional representation of
\QAut(\mathcal{G})
and
\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
is generated by
\{u_{i,j}\,|\,i,j=1,\dotsc,\dim{\C(\mathcal{G})}\}
. The compatibility of
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
with
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
is equivalent to
(\mathds{1}\otimes{A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}})u=u(\mathds{1}\otimes{A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}})
.

The quantum symmetry group of a quantum graph
\mathcal{G}
is by construction a quantum subgroup of a well-studied quantum group
\Aut^{+}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
of symmetries of the quantum space
\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
together with the
\delta
-form
\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
(the action of this larger quantum group does not preserve the adjacency matrix). The quantum groups of symmetries of finite quantum spaces equipped with a
\delta
-form were introduced in
[
3
]
. Finally, we note that in the existing literature the notion of an action of quantum groups is often referred to as a coaction of the corresponding
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. For brevity, however, we adopt the former convention.

1.3.
Actions preserving a partition of unity

We now briefly introduce a construction not directly related to quantum graphs which will prove useful later.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
\rho\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{A}\otimes\C(\mathbb{G})
be an action of a compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
on the quantum space underlying
\mathsf{A}
. In the subsequent sections of this paper,
\mathsf{A}
will often take the form
\C(\mathbb{X})
or
\C(\mathbb{X})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
, for some Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
, depending on the context. Let
\mathfrak{P}=\{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{n}\}
be a partition of unity in
\mathsf{A}
. Let
\mathsf{J}
be the ideal in
\C(\mathbb{G})
generated by the elements

\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\rho(P_{a})-\omega(P_{a})\mathds{1}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\omega\in\mathsf{A}^{*},\>a=1,\dotsc,n\bigr\}.

Denote the quotient
\C(\mathbb{G})/\mathsf{J}
by
\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})
and let
\pi\colon\C(\mathbb{G})\to\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})
be the corresponding quotient map. Furthermore, let
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\circ\rho
. Then
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{A}\otimes\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})
and
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}(P_{a})=P_{a}\otimes\mathds{1}
for all
a
(since slice maps
(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})
separate elements of
\mathsf{A}\otimes\C(\mathbb{G})
).

Next, let
\mathbb{Y}
be any compact quantum space and let
\varphi\colon\C(\mathbb{G})\to\C(\mathbb{Y})
be a unital
*
-homomorphism such that
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)\rho(P_{a})=P_{a}\otimes\mathds{1}
for all
a
. Then for any
\omega\in\mathsf{A}^{*}
and any
a
we have

0=(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)\rho(P_{a})-P_{a}\otimes\mathds{1}\bigr)=\varphi\bigl((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\rho(P_{a})-\omega(P_{a})\mathds{1}\bigr),

so
\mathsf{J}\subset\ker\varphi
. It follows that there exists a unique
\Lambda\colon\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})\to\C(\mathbb{Y})
such that
\varphi=\Lambda\circ\pi
.

Now take
\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}\times\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}
for
\mathbb{Y}
in the sense that
\C(\mathbb{Y})=\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})\otimes\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})
and let
\varphi=(\pi\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta{G}
, where
\Delta{G}
is the comultiplication on
\C(\mathbb{G})
. Then by the reasoning above there exists a unique
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}}\colon\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})\to\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})\otimes\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}})
such that
(\pi\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta{G}=\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}}\circ\pi
. Moreover, we have

(\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)\circ\rho=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}})\circ\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}.

Of course

\begin{aligned} (\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}}\circ\pi&=(\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\pi\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta{G}=(\pi\otimes\pi\otimes\pi)\circ(\Delta{G}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta{G}\\
&=(\pi\otimes\pi\otimes\pi)\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta{G})\circ\Delta{G}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}})\circ(\pi\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta{G}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}})\circ\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}}\circ\pi,\end{aligned}

so
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}}
is coassociative, since
\pi
is surjective. Finally, applying
\pi\otimes\pi
to both sides of the equalities

\overline{\bigl(\C(\mathbb{G})\otimes\mathds{1}\bigr)\Delta{G}\bigl(\C(\mathbb{G})\bigr)}=\C(\mathbb{G})\otimes\C(\mathbb{G})\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\Delta{G}\bigl(\C(\mathbb{G})\bigr)\bigl(\mathds{1}\otimes\C(\mathbb{G})\bigr)}=\C(\mathbb{G})\otimes\C(\mathbb{G})

we find that the density conditions are satisfied for
(\C(\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}),\Delta_{\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}})
and consequently
\mathbb{G}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{P}}}
is a compact quantum group.

1.4.
Mycielskians of quantum graphs

Here we recall the construction introduced in
[
6
]
. For
r\geq{2}
the
Mycielski construction of order
r-1
for quantum graphs is the procedure of passing from a quantum graph
\mathcal{G}
to a new quantum graph
\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})
defined as follows (
[
6
, Section 4]
):

•

the quantum space
\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}
is the disjoint union of a point and
r
copies of
\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
:
\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{r}\C(\mathcal{G})
,

•

the state
\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}
is defined by the formula

\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda\\
x_{1}\\[-2.0pt]
\vdots\\[2.0pt]
x_{r}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)=\tfrac{1}{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}\biggl(\lambda+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(x_{i})\biggr)

(we note the obvious identification
L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{r}L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
as vector spaces),

•

the quantum adjacency matrix
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}
acts on
L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))
as

A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda\\
x_{1}\\[-2.0pt]
\vdots\\[2.0pt]
x_{r}\end{smallmatrix}\right]=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(x^{r})\\
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(x_{1}+x_{2})\\
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(x_{1}+x_{3})\\[-2.0pt]
\vdots\\[2.0pt]
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(x_{r-2}+x_{r})\\
\lambda\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})}+A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}x_{r-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right].

Additionally, for
r=1
we let
\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})=\mu_{0}(\mathcal{G})
be the original quantum graph
\mathcal{G}
. The quantum graph
\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})
contains
r
“copies” of the original graph
\mathcal{G}
. In particular
\mu_{1}(\mathcal{G})
also denoted
\mu(\mathcal{G})
is a non-commutative generalization of the Mycielskian of a finite graph (
[
18
]
).

We let
\iota_{0}\colon\mathbb{C}\to L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))
and
\iota_{1},\dotsc,\iota_{r}\colon L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\to L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))
denote the isometric embeddings onto the respective direct summands and
\iota_{0}^{*}\colon L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\to\mathbb{C}
and
\iota_{1}^{*},\dotsc,\iota_{r}^{*}\colon L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\to L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
are their hermitian adjoints:

\displaystyle\iota_{0}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda\\
0\\[-5.0pt]
\vdots\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right],

\displaystyle\iota_{0}^{*}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda\\
x_{1}\\[-2.0pt]
\vdots\\[2.0pt]
x_{r}\end{smallmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\,\lambda,

\displaystyle\iota_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tfrac{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}{\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\[-5.0pt]
\vdots\\
x\\[-5.0pt]
\vdots\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right],

\displaystyle\iota_{k}^{*}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda\\
x_{1}\\[-2.0pt]
\vdots\\[2.0pt]
x_{r}\end{smallmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tfrac{\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\,x_{k}

for
k=1,\dotsc,r
. Note that although
\iota_{0},\dotsc,\iota_{r}
, and their adjoints are not multiplicative, they are all
*
-maps. In this notation, we have (cf.
[
6
, Eq. (9)]
):

A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{r}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{0}^{\ast}+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{0}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{\ast}\iota_{r}^{\ast}+\iota_{1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{1}^{\ast}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r-1}\bigl(\iota_{k}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{k+1}^{\ast}+\iota_{k+1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{k}^{\ast}\bigr).

2.
Quantum isomorphisms of Mycielskians

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}
be quantum graphs. The notion of
quantum isomorphism
of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}
has been studied in
[
7
, Definition 4.4, Corollary 4.8]
and
[
8
, Definition 6.5, Theorem 6.6]
. The definitions of quantum isomorphism of quantum graphs given in the two papers are equivalent (
[
8
, Theorem 6.6]
) and we will use both formulations.

According to
[
8
, Definition 6.5]
we say that
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}
are
quantum isomorphic
if there is a monoidal equivalence of the quantum automorphism groups
\Aut^{+}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
and
\Aut^{+}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}})
which maps the monoid object
\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
to the monoid object
\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}})
and maps the morphism
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
to
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}
(the notion of monoidal equivalence of compact quantum groups is described e.g. in
[
12
]
).

The equivalent formulation of quantum isomorphism of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
given in
[
7
, Section 4]
involves the
*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
generated by elements
\{p_{ij}\}
of a unitary matrix with the relations ensuring that upon a choice of orthonormal bases
\{e_{j}\}
and
\{f_{i}\}
of
\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
and
\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}})
the map

\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\colon e_{i}\longmapsto\sum_{i}f_{i}\otimes p_{ij}

is a unital
*
-homomorphism
\C(\mathcal{G})\to\C(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
such that
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}=(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}
. The quantum graphs
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}
are isomorphic if and only if the algebra
\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
is non-zero. Note that the matrix
p=[p_{ij}]_{i,j}
can be treated as an element of
\B(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}),L^{2}(\mathcal{F}))\otimes\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
be quantum graphs. If
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are quantum isomorphic, then
\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

According to the first version of the definition of quantum isomorphism of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}
given above, the quantum groups
\Aut^{+}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}})
and
\Aut^{+}(\C(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}),\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
are monoidally equivalent. Hence the result follows from
[
12
, Theorem 4.7]
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}
be quantum isomorphic. Then
\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})
and
\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{F})
are quantum isomorphic for any
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let

p=[p_{ij}]_{i,j}\in\B\bigl(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}),L^{2}(\mathcal{F})\bigr)\otimes\mathcal{O}\bigl(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G})\bigr)

be the unitary matrix defining
\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
as above (remember the latter is non-zero) and let

\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}\colon\C(\mathcal{G})\ni e_{j}\longmapsto\sum\limits_{i}f_{i}\otimes p_{ij}\in\C(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{O}\bigl(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G})\bigr)

be the corresponding unital
*
-homomorphism intertwining the quantum adjacency matrices. By Proposition
2.1
we know that
\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}
and for the remainder of the proof we will denote this value by
\delta
. The maps introduced above can be represented as

p\colon L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\otimes\mathcal{O}\bigl(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G})\bigr)\ni\xi\otimes a\longmapsto\sum\limits_{i,j}{\left|f_{i}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{e_{j}}\,\vline\,\xi\right\rangle}\otimes p_{ij}a\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{O}\bigl(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G})\bigr)

and

\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}(\xi)=p(\xi\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}).

We define an element
\widehat{p}\in\B(L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})),L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{F})))\otimes\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
:

\widehat{p}=\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},0}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},0}^{*}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},k}p\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},k}^{*},

where
\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j}=\iota_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}
and
\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j}=\iota_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}
, with
\iota_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j}
and
\iota_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j}
(
j=1,\ldots,r
)denoting the isometric embeddings of
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
and
L^{2}(\mathcal{F})
into
L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))
and
L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{F}))
, respectively.

First, notice that

\displaystyle\widehat{p}\widehat{p}^{*}

\displaystyle=\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},0}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},0}^{*}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},k}pp^{*}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},k}^{*}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j}^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{F}))},

\displaystyle\widehat{p}^{*}\widehat{p}

\displaystyle=\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},0}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},0}^{*}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},k}pp^{*}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},k}^{*}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j}^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{L^{2}(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))},

so that
\widehat{p}
is unitary.

Next, we check the intertwining property. First, we compute

\displaystyle\widehat{p}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},0}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},0}^{*}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},k}p\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},k}^{*}\biggr)\biggl(\delta\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},r}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},0}^{*}+\delta\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},0}\eta^{*}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},r}^{*}+\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},1}^{*}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r-1}\bigl(\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},k}A_{\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},k+1}^{*}+\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},k+1}A_{\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},k}^{*}\bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=\delta\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},0}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{*}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},r}^{*}+\delta\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},r}p\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},0}^{*}+\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},1}pA_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},1}^{*}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r-1}\bigl(\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j}pA_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j+1}^{*}+\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j+1}pA_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j}^{*}\bigr),

and similarly,

\displaystyle A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{F})}\widehat{p}=\delta\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},0}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}^{*}p\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},r}^{*}+\delta\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},r}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},0}^{*}

\displaystyle+\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}p\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},1}^{*}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r-1}\bigl(\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}p\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j+1}^{*}+\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F},j+1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}p\varpi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},j}^{*}\bigr).

Since
p\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}
, we also have
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{*}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}}^{*}p
. This shows that indeed the intertwining property holds. Finally, the map

\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{F}),\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda\\
\xi_{1}\\[-3.0pt]
\vdots\\[1.0pt]
\xi_{r}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)=\widehat{p}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda\\
\xi_{1}\\[-3.0pt]
\vdots\\[1.0pt]
\xi_{r}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\right)

is a unital
*
-homomorphism
\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\to\C(\mu_{r-1}\mathcal{F}))\otimes\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mathcal{F}),\QAut(\mathcal{G}))

by construction. Since the algebra
\mathcal{O}(\QAut(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{F})),\QAut(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})))
is universal for such homomorphisms, it has a non-zero homomorphic image, so it is itself non-zero. It follows that
\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})
and
\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{F})
are quantum isomorphic.
∎

3.
Quantum symmetries of
\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})
from quantum symmetries of
\mathcal{G}

Our goal now is to determine how the Mycielski transformation affects the quantum symmetries of the original quantum graph. We start with the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a quantum graph. Then
\QAut(\mathcal{G})
acts faithfully on its quantum Mycielskians. More precisely, for
r\geq{2}
define the linear map
\Phi_{r}\colon\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\to\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
by

\Phi_{r}(x)=\bigl(\iota_{0}\iota_{0}^{*}(x)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\bigr)+\sum_{k=1}^{r}(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\bigl(\iota_{k}^{*}(x)\bigr).

Then
\Phi_{r}
is a unital
*
-homomorphism and denoting by
\Delta
the comultiplication on
\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
we have

(1a)

\displaystyle(\Phi_{r}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\circ\Phi_{r}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Phi_{r},

(1b)

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}\circ A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}

\displaystyle=(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\circ\Phi_{r},

Moreover the set
\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\Phi_{r}(x)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})),\>\omega\in\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))^{*}\bigr\}
generates the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
.

Proof.

We first establish that
\Phi_{r}
is a unital
*
-homomorphism. We have

\mathds{1}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))}=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\biggl(\iota_{0}(1)+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\iota_{k}(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})})\biggr).

Hence

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}

\displaystyle(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))})=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\biggl(\iota_{0}(1)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\bigl(\iota_{k}^{*}\iota_{l}(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})})\bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\biggl(\iota_{0}(1)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\sum_{k=1}^{r}(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\biggl(\iota_{0}(1)+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\iota_{k}(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})})\biggr)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}=\mathds{1}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}.

To prove the multiplicativity of
\Phi_{r}
we temporarily denote by
m^{(0)}
,
m^{(1)}
,
m^{(2)}
and
m^{(3)}
the multiplication maps on
\mathbb{C}
,
\C(\mathcal{G})
,
\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))
and
\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
respectively (the last one defined only on the algebraic tensor product) and recall from
[
6
, Section 4]
that

m^{(2)}=\sqrt{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}\biggl(\iota_{0}m^{(0)}(\iota_{0}^{*}\otimes\iota_{0}^{*})+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\iota_{k}m^{(1)}(\iota_{k}^{*}\otimes\iota_{k}^{*})\biggr).

Then note that for
s\in\{1,\dotsc,r\}
the map
\sqrt{\tfrac{\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\iota_{s}
is multiplicative, which means that

(2)

m^{(2)}\circ(\iota_{s}\otimes\iota_{s})=\sqrt{\tfrac{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}{\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\iota_{s}\circ m^{(1)}.

Hence for
(p,q)\neq(0,0)
, with
\boldsymbol{\chi}
denoting the flip map, by (
2
) we have

\begin{aligned} \Phi_{r}&\circ m^{(2)}\bigl(\iota_{p}(a)\otimes\iota_{q}(b)\bigr)=\Phi_{r}\left(\delta_{p,q}\sqrt{\tfrac{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}{\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}\iota_{p}(ab)\right)\\
&=\delta_{p,q}\sqrt{\tfrac{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}{\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}(\iota_{p}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(ab)\\
&=\delta_{p,q}\sqrt{\tfrac{1+r\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}{\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}}(\iota_{p}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(m^{(1)}\otimes m^{(3)})(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\boldsymbol{\chi}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})(a\otimes b)\\
&=\delta_{p,q}(m^{(2)}\otimes m^{(3)})(\iota_{p}\otimes\iota_{p}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\boldsymbol{\chi}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})(a\otimes b)\\
&=\delta_{p,q}(m^{(2)}\otimes m^{(3)})(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\boldsymbol{\chi}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\iota_{p}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\iota_{p}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})(a\otimes b)\\
&=(m^{(2)}\otimes m^{(3)})(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\boldsymbol{\chi}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\Phi_{r}\otimes\Phi_{r})\bigl(\iota_{p}(a)\otimes\iota_{q}(b)\bigr)\end{aligned}

Similarly we compute that
\Phi_{r}\circ m^{(2)}=(m^{(2)}\otimes m^{(3)})(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\boldsymbol{\chi}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\Phi_{r}\otimes\Phi_{r})
on simple tensors of the form
\iota_{0}(\lambda)\otimes\iota_{0}(\lambda^{\prime})
for any
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))
is spanned by elements of the form
\iota_{k}(a)\otimes\iota_{l}(b)
with
k,l\in\{0,\dotsc,r\}
, these computations show that
\Phi_{r}
is multiplicative.

The fact that
\Phi_{r}
is a
*
-map follows immediately from the remark in Section
1.4
that all maps involved in the definition of
\Phi_{r}
are
*
-maps.

To check (
1a
) we note that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
we have
\Phi_{r}(\iota_{0}(\lambda))=\iota_{0}(\lambda)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}
which immediately shows that
(\Phi_{r}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\Phi_{r}(\iota_{0}(\lambda))=(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\Delta)\Phi_{r}(\iota_{0}(\lambda))
and similarly, since for any
k\in\{1,\dotsc,r\}
we have
\Phi_{r}\circ\iota_{k}=(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\circ\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
, one finds that

\displaystyle(\Phi_{r}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{k}(a)\bigr)

\displaystyle=(\Phi_{r}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\Delta)\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\Delta)(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))}\otimes\Delta)\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{k}(a)\bigr)

for any
a\in\C(\mathcal{G})
.

We now move on to the proof of (
1b
). To do this, we recall:

A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{r}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{0}^{*}+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{0}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{*}\iota_{r}^{*}+\iota_{1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{1}^{*}+\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}(\iota_{k}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{k+1}^{*}+\iota_{k+1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{k}^{*}),

where
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
is the unit map
\mathbb{C}\to\C(\mathcal{G})
. It follows from this equation that

\displaystyle A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{0}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{r}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},

\displaystyle A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{1}

\displaystyle=\iota_{1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}+\iota_{2}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},

\displaystyle A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{l}

\displaystyle=\iota_{l-1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}+\iota_{l+1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},\qquad l=2,\dotsc,r-1,

\displaystyle A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{r}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{0}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{*}+\iota_{r-1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}.

Using this we check that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{0}(\lambda)\bigr)=A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{0}(\lambda)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{r}\bigl(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(\lambda)\bigr)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\lambda\iota_{r}(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})})\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\lambda(\iota_{r}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})})

\displaystyle=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\lambda\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{r}(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})})\bigr)=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{r}(\lambda\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})})\bigr)=\Phi_{r}\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{0}(\lambda)\bigr).

Similarly for
a\in\C(\mathcal{G})
we have

\displaystyle(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{1}(a)\bigr)=(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\iota_{1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)+(\iota_{2}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\iota_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)+(\iota_{2}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{1}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)+\iota_{2}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)\bigr)=\Phi_{r}\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{1}(a)\bigr),

as well as

\displaystyle(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{l}(a)\bigr)=(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{l}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\iota_{l-1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)+(\iota_{l+1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=(\iota_{l-1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)+(\iota_{l+1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{l-1}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)+\iota_{l+1}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)\bigr)=\Phi_{r}\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{l}(a)\bigr)

for
l=2,\dotsc,r-1
, and finally using the fact that
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{*}=\psi
and that
\psi
is
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
-invariant we arrive at

\displaystyle(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\>\otimes\>

\displaystyle\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{r}(a)\bigr)=(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{r}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(\iota_{0}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)+(\iota_{r-1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(\iota_{0}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})(\psi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(a)+(\iota_{r-1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(\iota_{0}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\bigl(\psi(a)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\bigr)+(\iota_{r-1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\bigl(\iota_{0}(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{*}a)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\bigr)+(\iota_{r-1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}a)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{r}\bigl(\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{0}(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{*}a)+\iota_{r-1}(a)\bigr)=\Phi_{r}\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{r}(a)\bigr).

Finally, we note that if
\{e_{1},\dotsc,e_{\dim{\C(\mathcal{G})}}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\C(\mathcal{G})
then for any
k\in\{1,\dotsc,r\}

\Phi_{r}\bigl(\iota_{k}(e_{j})\bigr)=(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}(e_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\iota_{k}(e_{i})\otimes u_{i,j}

showing that
\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))})\Phi_{r}(x)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})),\>\omega\in\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))^{*}\bigr\}
generates
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))
.
∎

We can amplify actions of quantum groups on
\C(\mathcal{G})
into those on
\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
for a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
. In fact, the action
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
of
\QAut(\mathcal{G})
on
\mathcal{G}
defines an action
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},\mathscr{H}}
of
\QAut(\mathcal{G})
on
\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
by acting on
\B(\mathscr{H})
trivially, that is
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},\mathscr{H}}=(\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\boldsymbol{\chi})(\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})
, where
\boldsymbol{\chi}
is the flip map. For a partition of unity
\mathfrak{P}\subset\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
, we consider the action
\bigl(\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},\mathscr{H}}\bigr)_{\mathfrak{P}}
of
\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}
on
\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
be a partition of unity in
\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
, and take
r\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle Q_{0}

\displaystyle=\iota_{0}\iota^{*}_{0}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})},

\displaystyle Q_{a}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})P_{a}(\iota_{k}^{*}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}),\quad a=1,\dotsc,n.

form a partition of unity
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}^{r-1}
in
\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
.

Proof.

First, notice that
Q_{0}
and
Q_{a}
with
a=1,\ldots,n
are projections. Indeed, we immediately notice that both
Q_{0}
and
Q_{a}
with
a=1,\ldots,n
are idempotent and self-adjoint.Finally, we note that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}^{r-1}}\!\!\!\!Q

\displaystyle=Q_{0}+\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n}Q_{a}^{k}=\bigl(\iota_{0}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)\bigl(\iota_{0}^{\ast}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n}\bigl(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)P_{a}(\iota_{k}^{\ast}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\iota_{0}\iota_{0}^{\ast}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\iota_{k}\iota_{k}^{\ast}\Bigr)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}=\mathds{1}_{\C\bigl(\mu_{r-1}(G)\bigr)\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})}.

∎

We refer to
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}^{r-1}
as the
induced partition of unity
. For
r=2
, we write
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}\equiv\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}^{1}
. If
\mathscr{H}
is one-dimensional, we deal with partitions of unity in the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathcal{G})
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a quantum graph and
\mathfrak{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}\subset\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
be a partition of unity. Then for any
r\geq{2}
, the compact quantum group
\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}
is embedded as a closed quantum subgroup of
\QAut(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}^{r-1}}
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi_{r,\mathscr{H}}
be the homomorphism constructed in Proposition
3.1
extended onto
\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
, that is for
x\in\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
we define

\Phi_{r,\mathscr{H}}(x)=\bigl(\iota_{0}\iota_{0}^{\ast}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)(x)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\bigl(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\bigr)\rho_{\mathcal{G},\mathscr{H}}\bigl(\iota_{k}^{\ast}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)(x).

Further, we note that

\Phi_{r,\mathscr{H}}(Q_{0})=\bigl(\iota_{0}\iota_{0}^{\ast}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)\bigl(\iota_{0}\iota_{0}^{\ast}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}=Q_{0}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))},

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{r,\mathscr{H}}(Q_{a})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{r}\bigl(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\bigr)\rho_{\mathcal{G},\mathscr{H}}\bigl(\iota_{k}^{\ast}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)\bigl(\iota_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)P_{a}\bigl(\iota_{l}^{\ast}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\bigl(\iota_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}\bigr)\rho_{\mathcal{G},\mathscr{H}}(P_{a})(\iota_{k}^{\ast}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}).

If
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G},\mathscr{H}}(P_{a})=P_{a}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}
, then the above expression simplifies into
Q_{a}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G}))}
,
so that
\Phi_{r,\mathscr{H}}
induces a homomorphism

\Phi_{r,\mathscr{H}}^{\mathfrak{P}}\colon\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})\longrightarrow\C(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})\otimes\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}).

By the universal property of
\QAut(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))
there exists a unique homomorphism

\pi_{r}^{\mathfrak{P}}\colon\C(\QAut(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G})))\longrightarrow\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}})

such that
\Phi_{r,\mathscr{H}}^{\mathfrak{P}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi_{r}^{\mathfrak{P}})\circ\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}
. Moreover,
\pi_{r}^{\mathfrak{P}}
restricts itself to a well-defined homomorphism

\pi_{r}^{\mathfrak{P},\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}^{r-1}}\colon\C(\QAut(\mu_{r-1}(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}^{r-1}})\to\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}})
and this homomorphism is compatible with comultiplications (cf.
[
22
, Proposition 4.7]
) and is surjective by the last statement of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

3.1.
The role of twin vertices

Definition 3.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a classical graph. For
v\in V
define
N(v)=\bigl\{u\in{V}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\{u,v\}\in{E}\bigr\}
, the neighbourhood of
v
. Two vertices
x,y\in V
are said to be
twins
if
N(x)=N(y)
.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
2
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

If
G=(V,E)
is a twin-free finite graph, then every automorphism of
\mu(G)
that fixes the point
\bullet
is induced by an automorphism of
G
.

We will present here a slightly modified proof of this theorem which makes more explicit use of the adjacency matrix of
G
, allowing us to extend the reasoning to the case of quantum graphs in Theorem
3.10
.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Let
\widetilde{\rho}
be such an automorphism of
\mu(G)
that fixes
\bullet
. It follows that the matrix of
\widetilde{\rho}
as a linear map
\mathbb{C}\oplus\C(V)\oplus\C(V)\to\mathbb{C}\oplus\C(V)\oplus\C(V)
must be of the form

\widetilde{\rho}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\rho_{11}&\rho_{12}\\
0&\rho_{21}&\rho_{22}\end{bmatrix}.

From the commutation with the adjacency matrix,

A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(G)}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}&A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}\\
1&A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}&0\end{bmatrix}

we deduce that
\rho_{12}=\rho_{21}=0
. Denoting for
i=1,2
,\rho_{i}=\rho_{ii}\in\Aut(G)
, treated as operators on
\C(V)
, the remaining nontrivial conditions are

(3)

\rho_{2}A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}=A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}\rho_{1}=\rho_{1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}=A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}\rho_{2}.

Now, evaluating both sides of the equation
\rho_{1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}=\rho_{2}A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
on
\delta_{v}
, the characteristic function of the vertex
v\in V
, we end up with equation
\rho_{1}\chi_{N(v)}=\rho_{2}\chi_{N(v)}
, where
\chi_{C}
is the indicator function of the set
C
. Since
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are automorphisms of the classical graph
G
, there exist permutations
\tau,\sigma
of
V
such that
\rho_{1}\chi_{N(v)}=\chi_{N(\tau(v))}
and
\rho_{2}\chi_{N(v)}=\chi_{N(\sigma(v))}
. The lack of twin vertices implies that
\sigma=\tau
, so that
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
, i.e. every automorphism of
\mu(G)
that has
\bullet
as a fixed point is induced by an automorphism of
G
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Note that in the proof presented above the conclusion
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
can be immediately reached from (
3
) if we assume that
A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
is invertible. This is of course a stronger assumption than
G
being twin-free.

Remark 3.7
.

A graph
G=(V,E)
has twin vertices if and only if its adjacency matrix
A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
has at least two same rows or, equivalently, columns. The existence of such two rows is equivalent to the existence of a permutation
\pi
of the set
V
such that
P_{\pi}A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}=A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
, where
P_{\pi}
is the corresponding permutation matrix. In contrast, the presence of the same columns is equivalent to the existence of
\sigma
such that
A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}P_{\sigma}=A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}
.

Motivated by the above observation, we introduce the following definition:

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
be a quantum graph and
\mathbb{H}
be a compact quantum group. We say that
\mathcal{G}
has
\mathbb{H}
-twin vertices
, or simply
\mathbb{H}
-twins
, if there exists a pair of different actions of
\mathbb{H}
on
\mathcal{G}

\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\colon\C(\mathcal{G})\to\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\C(\mathbb{H})

such that

(4)

\rho_{1}\circ A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}=\rho_{2}\circ A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}.

We say that
\mathcal{G}
has
quantum twin vertices
if there exists a quantum group
\mathbb{H}
such that
\mathcal{G}
has
\mathbb{H}
-twins. If a classical group
H
that meets the above conditions exists, then we say that
\mathcal{G}
has
classical twin vertices
.

Remark 3.9
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathbb{H}
are classical, i.e.
\mathbb{H}
can be identified with a compact group
H
. Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
has
H
-twins, i.e. there exist
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\colon\C(\mathcal{G})\to\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\C(H)
satisfying (
4
). The actions
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
can be viewed as homomorphisms
H\ni h\mapsto\rho_{i}(h)\in\Aut(\mathcal{G})
. Since
\rho_{1}\neq\rho_{2}
there is
h\in H
such that
\rho_{1}(h)\neq\rho_{2}(h)
and

\rho_{1}(h)\circ A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}=\rho_{2}(h)\circ A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}.

Defining
\sigma=\rho_{2}(h)^{-1}\rho_{1}(h)\in\Aut(\mathcal{G})
we have
P_{\sigma}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}=A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
. Since
\sigma\neq\mathrm{id}
we can find two vertices
v_{1}\neq v_{2}
and
\sigma(v_{1})=v_{2}
and it is easy to see that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are twins.

Conversely, if
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{G}}
are twins we can define an order two symmetry
\pi\in\Aut(\mathcal{G})
such that
\pi(v)=v
for all
v\in\mathbb{V}\setminus\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
and
\pi(v_{1})=v_{2}
(note that this is a different way of saying that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are twin vertices). Taking
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{e,h\}
and putting
\rho_{1}(h)=\pi
and
\rho_{2}(h)=\mathrm{id}
we see that
\mathcal{G}
has
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-twins in the sense of Definition
3.8
.

Next, we generalize Theorem
3.5
to the case of quantum graphs, with a possible amplification by
\B(\mathscr{H})
. In the next Theorem we use partitions of unity and the corresponding construction of
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}
as described in Lemma
3.2
and the notation below. In what follows
\K(\mathscr{H})
denoted the algebra of compact operators on the Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
and
\M(\cdot)
is the multiplier functor. The reader will find more on these concepts and their use in introductory sections of
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathbb{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}},A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}})
be a quantum graph that does not have quantum twin vertices. Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a partition of unity in
\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
with some (separable) Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
. Then
\QAut(\mu(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}}\cong\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}
.

Proof.

Let us first recall that
\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}
can be viewed as a closed quantum subgroup of
\QAut(\mu(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}}
(cf. Corollary
3.3
).
We shall prove that the two quantum groups are in fact identical.

So let us consider a partition of unity
\mathfrak{P}\subset\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
. Note that the partition
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}\subset\C(\mu(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
assigned to
\mathfrak{P}
contains the projection
P_{\bullet}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\in\C(\mu(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
where
P_{\bullet}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\C(\mu(\mathcal{G}))
. Let
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}
be the amplification of the canonical action of
\QAut(\mu(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}}
on
\C(\mu(\mathcal{G}))
. The direct sum structure of
\C(\mu(\mathcal{G}))
gives rise to the “matrix structure” of
\rho_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}}=[\rho_{ij}]_{i,j=0,1,2}
where using notation
\C(\mathbb{X}_{0})=\mathbb{C}
,
\C(\mathbb{X}_{1})=\C(\mathcal{G})
and
\C(\mathbb{X}_{2})=\C(\mathcal{G})
we define
\rho_{ij}:\C(\mathbb{X}_{j})\otimes\K(\mathscr{H})\to\M(\C(\mathbb{X}_{i})\otimes\K(\mathscr{H})\otimes\C(\QAut(\mu(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}})
by the formula

\rho_{ij}=\bigl(\iota_{j}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\QAut(\mu(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}})}\bigr)\circ\rho_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}}\circ\bigl(\iota_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr).

Remembering that

\rho_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}}\circ\iota_{0}(\lambda)=\sqrt{1+2\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{2}}\,\lambda\bigl(P_{\bullet}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mu(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}})}\bigr)

for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and using
\iota_{j}^{*}(P_{\bullet})=0
for
j\in\{1,2\}
we get
\rho_{j0}=0
and and
\rho_{00}(\lambda)=\lambda\bigl(P_{\bullet}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\C(\QAut(\mu(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}})}\bigr)
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
Using the
*
-condition of the
*
-homomorphism
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}
we get
\rho_{01}=0=\rho_{02}
.

Having the above facts established we can now prove that
\rho_{12}=\rho_{21}=0
. Indeed, since the adjacency matrix
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G})}
is of the form

A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G})}=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{2}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{0}^{\ast}+\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{0}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}^{\ast}\iota_{2}^{\ast}+\iota_{1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{1}^{\ast}+\iota_{1}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{2}^{\ast}+\iota_{2}A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{1}^{\ast}.

(cf. Section
1.4
) we get

A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G})}\bigl(\iota_{0}(1)\bigr)=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\iota_{2}(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})})

and therefore

\bigl(\iota_{1}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mu(\mathcal{G})}\bigr)\rho_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}}\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G})}(\iota_{0}(1))\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)=\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\rho_{12}\bigl(\mathds{1}_{\C(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr).

A similar computation but with the amplified adjacency matrix
A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G})}
to the left of the action
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}
shows that
the left hand side of the above equality vanishes, and consequently
\rho_{12}(\mathds{1})=0
. This implies that
\rho_{12}=\rho_{21}=0
.

Using the vanishing of
\rho_{ij},\rho_{0j},\rho_{i0}
for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
proven so far and the equality

\rho_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}}\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G})}\iota_{2}(x)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)=\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\K(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\C(\mu(\mathcal{G}))}\bigr)\rho_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G}),\mathscr{H}}}\bigl(\iota_{2}(x)\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}\bigr)

which holds for every
x\in\C(\mathcal{G})
we easily conclude that
\rho_{11}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})=\rho_{22}(A_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})
. “Deamplifying” the later equality an referring to the assumed lack of quantum twins in
\mathcal{G}
we conclude that
\rho_{11}=\rho_{22}
.

Summarizing we have so far proved that the canonical action of
\QAut(\mu(\mathcal{G}))_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}}
on
\C(\mu(\mathcal{G}))
is induced (in the sense described in Section
3
) by the action of
\QAut(\mathcal{G})
. Moreover using the fact that
\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu(\mathcal{G})}
preserves
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}
we see that in fact it is induced by the action of
\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}
and therefore we conclude that
\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}
and
\QAut(\mu\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}}
are indeed isomorphic.
∎

3.2.
Quantum twin vertices in small classical graphs

It is unclear whether the notion of quantum twin vertices is broader than the usual concept of twin vertices when applied to classical graphs. In this section, we consider this question for graphs with few vertices. In view of Remark
3.6
we will be mainly interested in graphs with no twins, but whose adjacency matrix is not invertible.

Considering graphs up to the classical isomorphism, we begin with the following observation.

Proposition 3.11
.

There is no graph
G
with
|V(G)|\leq 5
such that
G
has quantum symmetries, has no twins, and

.

Proof.

The statement is clear for

. For

For graphs with six vertices, we have the following proposition:

.

For establishing the next result, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.13

(Fulton criterion;

]
, see also

)
.

For a finite graph

, if
\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}^{l}\bigr)_{ii}=\bigl(A_{\scriptscriptstyle G}^{l}\bigr)_{jj}

Proposition 3.14
.

The graph

from Proposition

does not possess quantum twins.

Proof.

First, by the Fulton criterion and the fact that for all

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{6}u_{ik}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{6}u_{ki}=1
, we know that the generating matrix

is of the form

0&u_{22}&1-u_{22}&0&0&0\\
0&1-u_{22}&u_{22}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&u_{33}&0&1-u_{33}\\
1-u_{11}&0&0&0&u_{11}&0\\
0&0&0&1-u_{33}&0&u_{33}\end{bmatrix}.

Suppose there exists
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
, a pair of different actions of a certain quantum group
\mathbb{H}
on

such that
\rho_{1}\circ A_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{6}}=\rho_{2}\circ A_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{6}}
. Let

4.
The distinguishing number

A
labeling
of a classical graph
G=(V,E)
is a mapping
V\to\{1,\dotsc,c\}
, while a
coloring
is a labeling with the property that adjacent vertices are labeled with different integers (colors). The
chromatic number
of
G
defined as the smallest
c
such that there exists a coloring of
G
with
c
colors is an invariant that measures how “well connected” the graph
G
is. A different invariant called
distinguishing number
measures this property with respect to the size of the automorphism group of
G
. We recall here this classical definition:

Definition 4.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. A
c
-distinguishing labeling of
G
is a function
\phi\colon V\to\{1,\dotsc,c\}
such that for every
\sigma\in\Aut(G)\setminus\{\mathrm{id}\}
, there exists
v\in V
such that
\phi(v)\neq\phi(\sigma(v))
. The distinguishing number
D(G)
of
G
is then the smallest
c
such that
G
has a labeling that is
c
-distinguishing.

The behavior of the distinguishing number and variations of it under the Mycielski transformation was widely studied in recent years; see, e.g.
[
2
,
16
]
. Here we introduce a version of Definition
4.1
for quantum graphs. To that end, we recall that a quantum graph
\mathcal{G}
defines an operator system
S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\subset\B(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}))
through
S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}=\bigl\{m(A\otimes{X})m^{*}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,X\in\B(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}))\bigr\}
(see, e.g.
[
15
, Section 2.4]
).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an irreflexive quantum graph,
S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
the operator system associated with
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space. A partition of unity
\{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{c}\}
in
\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
is called a
quantum
c
-labeling of
\mathcal{G}
. We say that a quantum
c
-labeling of
\mathcal{G}
is a
quantum
c
-coloring of
\mathcal{G}
if

\forall\>a\in\{1,\dotsc,c\}\quad\forall\>X\in S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{G}}\quad P_{a}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{\mathscr{H}}})P_{a}=0.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}=\{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{c}\}\subset\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
be a quantum
c
-labeling of
\mathcal{G}
. We say that
\mathfrak{P}
is a
quantum
c
-distinguishing labeling
of
\mathcal{G}
if the quantum group
\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}
is trivial.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that if
\mathcal{G}
is a classical graph (i.e. the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathcal{G})
is commutative) and
\mathfrak{P}
is a classical
c
-labeling (i.e.
\mathscr{H}
is one dimensional) then it corresponds to labeling of vertices
f\colon{V_{\mathcal{G}}}\to\{1,\dotsc,c\}
uniquely defined by the condition
P_{i}(v)=1\Leftrightarrow{f(v)}=i
. In this case
\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}}
can be viewed as the quantum group of automorphisms preserving
f
. In particular, the abelianization of
\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}})
corresponds to the subgroup of
\Aut(\mathcal{G})
that preserves
f
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a quantum graph. We define the quantum
distinguishing number

D(\mathcal{G})
of
\mathcal{G}
as the smallest
c
such that
\mathcal{G}
admits a quantum distinguishing
c
-labeling.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a quantum graph with
\dim\C(\mathcal{G})\geq 2
which does not possess quantum twin vertices. Then
D(\mu(\mathcal{G}))\leq D(\mathcal{G})+1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{P}=\{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{D(\mathcal{G})}\}\subset\C(\mathcal{G})\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
be a quantum
D(\mathcal{G})
-distinguishing labeling of
\mathcal{G}
, so that
\C(\QAut(\mathcal{G})_{\mathfrak{P}})=\mathbb{C}
, and consider the induced partition of unity
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}
on
\C(\mu(\mathcal{G}))\otimes\B(\mathscr{H})
, that is
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}=\{Q_{0},Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{D(\mathcal{G})}\}
with

\displaystyle Q_{0}

\displaystyle=\iota_{0}\iota^{*}_{0}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})},

\displaystyle Q_{a}

\displaystyle=(\iota_{1}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})P_{a}(\iota_{1}^{*}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})+(\iota_{2}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})})P_{a}(\iota_{2}^{*}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\B(\mathscr{H})}),\quad a=1,\dotsc,D(\mathcal{G}).

By Theorem
3.10
,
\mathfrak{P}_{\mu}
is a quantum
(D(\mathcal{G})+1)
-labeling of
\mu(\mathcal{G})
.
∎

Invariant state modelling for robust adaptive intrusion detection in software-defined internet of things environments
Authors: Swathi Gowroju, S. Sai Satyanarayana Reddy, Shilpa Choudhary, Swarna venkatesh, M. Zubairuddin, A. Bharath, Shahid Tamboli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the modern era, Software-Defined Internet of Things (SD-IoT) networks struggle with various challenges that include heterogeneous traffic, high-throughput limitations and non-recurrent or non-stationary attacks. Therefore, to address these gaps, a unified and deployable IDS framework, namely Robust Adaptive Intrusion Detection Using Invariant State (RADIUS) is designed. In particular, the proposed RADIUS framework utilizes a Temporal Convolution Encoder (TCE) that includes dilated and causal convolutions, which assist in constructing efficient traffic embeddings. Subsequently, an Adaptive State Transition Module (ASTM) is incorporated, which identifies latent state transitions that demonstrate interpretable and adaptive detections. Further, training the RADIUS framework uses dual-objective optimization, which minimizes both prediction accuracy and requires environment-invariance and sensitivity to rare events. Thus, both anomaly scores and classification calibration are attained through Extreme Value Theory (EVT) using conformal bounds. Hence, the proposed RADIUS framework achieves 99.8% accuracy, 99.5% precision, 99.8% recall, and 99.7% F1-score consistently, which outperforms the existing Extended Wrapper Approach (EWA) model. The EWA, a hybrid of deep learning and feature optimization, offers a favorable balance between detection accuracy and deployment efficiency in resource-constrained SD-IoT environments and is therefore used as the primary quantitative baseline for ablation comparisons.

Introduction

Although the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) offers advanced automation, it exposes critical infrastructures to the threat of malicious software and other forms of cyber-attacks that can evolve rapidly1. Software-Defined Networking (SDN) increases the security of IoT technology with centralized, verifiable, and automated policy enforcement2, however, controller load imbalances in large-scale deployments undermine reliability3. SD-IoT devices that are resource-constrained, require lightweight Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS), but the security achieved by IDS for such devices will be inefficient if it cannot perform detection promptly4. Recent studies have developed more scalable and stable SD-IoT systems by utilizing Deep-Learning (DL)-based load predictions to limit unnecessary switch migrations in SDN-aware IoT networks5.

Integration of IoT with SDN encounters ongoing issues that affect its viability. Construction environments struggle with heterogeneous data handling and scalability issues, as shown in6, uncontrolled IoT traffic and limited resources inhibit effective intrusion detection7. Botnet attacks take advantage of an attack vector that exploits a centrally controlled SDN8. Weak access policy management and trust management will expose the network to unauthorized access9. Similarly, manipulating flow rules and controller exploitation signifies SD-IoT services that are dependent on one controller, which assists in maintaining availability and mitigation of security concerns10.

The rapid growth of the IoT across critical sectors has outperformed traditional security technologies, which creates a significant vulnerability in devices and networks11. To mitigate these risks, employing advanced technologies such as deep learning, SDN, and blockchain can provide sustainable and flexible security solutions12. Nevertheless, the increase in Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks that can take advantage of the centralization of SDN13 has caused researchers to adopt machine learning, for reliable and expedient detection in SD-IoT networks14 and edge-aware SDN provides a better solution for strengthening resilience since it incorporates real-time traffic data and quick traffic response capabilities15.

Related Works

Software-Defined Networking (SDN) has been developed for enhancing IoT security, where it has the ability of centralized control, global network visibility, and programmability. There are various research studies related to SDN for intrusion and anomaly detection in IoT networks; some of them are described in the following section. A multi-pronged framework named MP-GUARD was recommended for detecting and mitigating intrusion in IoT16. Here, this network contains two core modules: P4-Assisted Cooperative Traffic Monitoring (CTM-P4) and Multi-Pronged Intrusion Detection and Mitigation (MPIDM). Particularly, CTM-P4 was utilized for feature extraction and MPIDM was used for identifying and mitigating the attacks. Furthermore, an integrated model with Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Deep Neural Network (DNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) for improving security in IoT networks17. These models were utilized for the detection and classification of various security threats in SDN.

Similarly, an EfficientNetV2-RegNet was suggested for recognizing and evaluating the attacks, which also extracted features and classified the intrusions in SDN18. Here, to mitigate the attacks, a remote SDN controller was used, and to tackle data-related issues Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (CGAN) was utilized. In the same way, a Secured Automatic Two-level Intrusion Detection System (SATIDS) was introduced for identifying abnormal behavior and attacks in SD based IoT systems19. Where an improved LSTM was used for the classification of traffic, particularly identifying whether it is an attack or normal behavior and categorizing the subtype of the attack. Moreover, a fuzzy logic-based optimization with an LSTM framework was demonstrated for intrusion detection20. Where, Extended Wrapper Approach (EWA) was utilized for extracting features, Fuzzy logic was used for calculating probabilities of intrusions like attacker, benign and mixed. Additionally, dense Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) was utilized for classification, and it was fine-tuned by Extended Hunger Games search optimization (ExHGO).

Furthermore, a multi-module controller framework was determined for detecting intrusions in IoT networks, and this framework is named MULTI-BLOCK, which consists of four modules21. Where Pyramidal Conceptually Decentralized Multi-Controller Structure (PCDMCS) was the first module that utilized Decentralized Control Interfaces (DCIs) and Decentralized Warning Conduit (DWC) for threat identification. Then, the second module was used for network monitoring and extracting features. Lastly, the third module was the Enhanced Weighted Ensemble Algorithm (EWEA) for detecting anomalies and the fourth module was utilized for the mitigation of anomalies. Similarly, a pioneering framework was suggested for detecting anomalies in Software-Defined IoT Networks (SD-IoT) and improving accuracy for detection22. Here, dynamic behavioural profiling was created, and Mutual Information Gain (MIG) was utilized for feature selection.

Specifically, regarding the Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS), an MT-EDD framework was recommended for attack classification in SD-IoT networks23. Here, Dynamic Time-Based Windowing (DTW) adjusts the analysis window dynamically for efficient detection of DDoS attacks and Hierarchically Distributed Multi-Controller (HDMC) was utilized for mitigating the attacks. Similarly, a Deep-Learning Empowered Threat Hunting Framework (DLTHF) was introduced for protecting data and mitigating attacks24. Where data perturbation-driven encoding was utilized for extracting features, Long Short-Term Memory Contractive Sparse Auto Encoder (LSTMCSAE) was used for protecting data. Lastly, Multi-head Self-attention-based Bidirectional Recurrent Neural Networks (MhSaBiGRNN) were utilized for detecting attacks and their types in SD-IoT systems.

Recent comparative studies on ML- and AI-based intrusion detection systems demonstrate that traditional classifiers such as K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) and deep learning models such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) achieve high detection accuracy across multiple datasets25. Evolving Adversarial Training (EAT) has been shown to improve detection accuracy and reduce false positives by enabling IDS models to dynamically adapt to new attack patterns26. However, unlike these other IDS architectures, RADIUS combines its temporal context with the state transition maps to create an invariant state model (i.e., RADIUS provides the ability to detect changes in network traffic regardless of how they occurred), which enables RADIUS to continually detect in numerous diverse IoT environments.

AI-enabled cyber-physical security frameworks have been proposed to address the nonlinear, interconnected, and unpredictable behavior of CPS environments, where conventional intrusion prevention systems remain insufficient27–30.

The LESSON framework highlights a significant gap in existing intrusion detection and defense mechanisms by introducing multi-label adversarial false data injection attacks capable of bypassing conventional detection strategies31. Therefore, defending against adversarial FDI attacks goes beyond simply improving the accuracy of classification. Defending against adversarial FDI attacks necessitates the inclusion of advanced structural modelling techniques that capture how different adversarial FDI attacks evolve through time. This is the foundation for RADIUS’s (Ruggedized Adaptable & Deployable User System) Adaptive State Transition Module (ASTM), which captures temporal state transitions within an adversarial FDI attack context.

Adversarial attacks targeting regression models in safety-critical UAV systems demonstrate that even sophisticated deep learning models remain susceptible without adaptive and interpretable defense mechanisms31. UAV operators and autonomous vehicle operators to maximize the performance attributes of their current and future adaptive and interpretable defensive methodologies. Evidence from UAV and autonomous vehicle research studies demonstrate that models, regardless of their sophistication, will still be susceptible to adversarial FDI attacks if the model is not adaptable and interpretable. RADIUS captures this uniqueness using a feature harmonization and invariant modelling pipeline, allowing RADIUS to develop multi-layer threat models that are adaptable across multiple heterogeneous Internet of Things (IoT) datasets. RADIUS utilizes interpretable, adaptive state transitions in response to different types of multi-label threats rather than using opaque attention-based or saliency-based defensive techniques.

Research gap

The existing SD-IoT intrusion-detection research methods still demonstrate key challenges that depend on heterogeneous datasets with inconsistent preprocessing and evaluation protocols that affect comparability and generalization. Subsequently, several models struggle with scalability and efficiency under high-throughput SD-IoT conditions, while adaptive handling of imbalanced traffic, rare attacks and multi-pronged threats also remained underexplored. Hence, these gaps signify the need for a unified framework that ensures standardized evaluation, computational efficiency, adaptive and interpretable detection in diverse SD-IoT environments.

RADIUS is designed to be an efficient and lightweight deployment-ready Intrusion Detection System (IDS). Major contribution of the paper is:

Lightweight, deployment-efficient IDS design

RADIUS utilizes an encoder based on the feed-forward temporal convolution architecture with shallow pooling, minimizing the use of both recurrent and attention-based architectures; thus, allowing for a smaller number of parameters with reduced overhead processing, while being suitable for real-time SD-IoT and edge-based deployments.

Cross-environment generalization using invariant state models

The methodology of RADIUS allows for the integration of an invariance-aware learning objective as well as a model for the explicit state transitions to create traffic feature representations that remain constant across different forms of heterogeneous datasets and different deployment environments. This helps RADIUS create robust and generalizable IDS models without needing for a large number of datasets with a rhythm to tune.

Real-time, reliable intrusion detection

RADIUS separates the temporal extraction of data features from the state creation phase using a method of distribution-free calibration based on the Extreme Value Theory and conformal prediction methodology. Thus enabling very fast inference of the anomaly score to be produced with confidence that RADIUS meets the needs for real-time performance.

Methodology

The proposed RADIUS framework provides a unified, robust, interpretable system for SD-IoT intrusion detection, as demonstrated in Fig. 1. Specifically, the data traffic is initially converted to standard windows, then transformed into lightweight statistical embeddings using a TCE. In particular, the TCE applies causal and dilated convolutions with gated residual blocks to build a feature extractor using low-cost embeddings, which is designed for near real-time anomaly detection. Further, the embeddings are fed to the ASTM, which is a novel decision layer that models the states and transitions explicitly, for interpretable and adaptable anomaly detection. Subsequently, the RADIUS framework is trained with a dual-objective loss function that minimizes the classification accuracy error, representational drift across environments and explores infrequent or imbalanced attack scenarios. Thus, the Z-scores are then a product of possible calibration using EVT with conformal bounds, for certain anomaly detection, even with drift in traffic and novel anomalies. Hence, the proposed RADIUS framework solves issues of existing SD-IoT IDS approaches by addressing scalability, cross-environment generalizability, low rates of anomaly, interpretability and deployment costs.

The proposed RADIUS framework, as depicted in Fig. 1, is different from how the traditional encoding methods have been implemented. In addition to the integration of state modelling, dual-objective learning and distribution-free calibration into the architecture of RADIUS framework, the novelty lies in the inability to replace the existing approaches to encoders using deep neural networks, as opposed to merely a new encoder. Figure 1 shows the RADIUS architectures that have integrated ASTMs. The purpose of using ASTM is to develop interpretable latent states of incoming network traffic, and develop an inherently interpretable transition model between these latent states, independent of feedforward TCE models.

The RADIUS architecture incorporates an invariance-aware objective as well as an imbalance-aware objective to develop generalizable architectures across heterogeneous datasets. Finally, the framework incorporates Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Conformal Prediction techniques to provide reliable approaches for determining the likelihood of anomalies. In summary, the innovations found in the RADIUS framework are threefold. They are explicit adaptive state transition modelling (ASTM), developing a dual-objective learning architecture to develop robust generalization across heterogeneous datasets and calibrating score anomalies using EVT/conformal prediction.

To evaluate the proposed RADIUS framework effectively, three publicly available SD-IoT and network intrusion datasets are considered, which include CICIoT2023, ToN-IoT and UNSW-NB15. Specifically, these datasets are chosen due to the diverse range of traffic conditions, attack types and IoT/SDN environments that enable effective assessment of cross-domain generalization, rare-attack detection and system scalability. Hence, these datasets allow evaluation under heterogeneous, high-throughput and imbalanced conditions, which are critical challenges in SD-IoT intrusion detection. Further, the brief description of each dataset is demonstrated below:

CICIoT2023 consists of traffic from multiple IoT devices over both benign and malicious scenarios. The dataset contains around 10 million flow windows and attack types include DoS, DDoS, Botnet, Reconnaissance, Injection, and multi-stage attacks. The dataset has over 80 features that provide flow-level, packet-level, and protocol-level statistics, including counts for packets, bytes, inter-arrival time, and SDN flow rule events. Since it also provides IoT traffic that is highly imbalanced and heterogeneous, CICIoT2023 is an effective dataset to test IDS methods.

ToN-IoT focuses on real-time IoT and industrial telemetry such as network traffic, system logs and sensor data. Specifically, it contains around 2.5 million instances and covers attacks such as DDoS, DoS, Injection, Ransomware, Scanning, and various Mirai attacks. This dataset encompasses 47 features to capture temporal, protocol-based, and sensor-based dynamics. In addition, testing performance against evolving and cross-domain patterns in an IDS benefits from the use of a multi-sourced dataset like ToN-IoT.

UNSW-NB15 is a widely used benchmark dataset for traditional network intrusion detection. This dataset contains over 2.5 million flow-based records and contains attack types related to Fuzzy based analysis, Backdoors, DoS, Exploits, Reconnaissance, and Worms. The dataset also includes 49 features that measure flow statistics, payload signatures, and content-based attributes. The UNSW-NB15 dataset establishes a benchmark for comparing proposed approaches to more traditional network IDS approaches, while also providing baseline data to assess generalization to traditional network environments.

While CICIoT2023, ToN-IoT and UNSW-NB15 all contain differing raw feature dimensionality (80, 47 and 49 features), respectively; the proposed framework, RADIUS, utilizes neither direct feature intersection nor zero-padding to achieve dimensional conformity. Instead, it adheres to an agnostic approach toward feature harmonization across datasets by normalizing each dataset independently before projecting each dataset into a common latent representation that allows them to be represented using our model. Preprocessing

To ensure effective and reproducible evaluation of the proposed RADIUS framework across heterogeneous datasets, a unified preprocessing and standardization pipeline is applied to CICIoT2023, ToN-IoT, and UNSW-NB15. All raw traffic data are initially segmented into fixed-length time windows, which allows consistent temporal representation across datasets with differing flow rates. Specifically, within each window, statistical, protocol-level and SDN control-plane features are extracted that comprise of packet and byte counts, inter-arrival times, flow rule events and content-based indicators32. Subsequently, after feature extraction, normalization is applied using z-score scaling for continuous attributes and one-hot encoding for categorical fields, which ensures that all datasets are defined with a common feature range and type. Consequently, the missing values and anomalies are handled by employing imputation and outlier removal to reduce noise and enhance model stability33. Further, to mitigate class imbalance, especially for rare attack categories, resampling strategies such as oversampling of minority classes and weighted loss functions are employed during training34. Finally, the datasets are aligned under a unified label taxonomy that maps heterogeneous attack labels across datasets into a standardized set of categories such as DoS, DDoS, Botnet, Injection, Reconnaissance and Multi-stage. Thus, this harmonization allows consistent evaluation of cross-dataset generalization and ensures that performance metrics reflect comparable attack scenarios35. The resulting preprocessed datasets provide a robust, standardized input for the RADIUS framework, allowing it to effectively learn temporal embeddings, state transitions and anomaly detection rules across diverse SD-IoT environments.

Proposed RADIUS framework

The proposed RADIUS framework presents a unified architecture that resolves the major challenges of existing IDS models in SD-IoT environments. Rather than modifying CNNs, LSTMs, or Transformers, the proposed RADIUS framework designs each stage, which includes feature extraction, decision-making, optimization, calibration and deployment, such that it resolves five critical gaps such including scalability, interpretability, imbalance handling, generalization, and reliability are directly resolved.

Rather than the hidden states of recurrent neural networks, which do not have any associated meaning associated with their activation, the states in RADIUS correspond to well-defined behaviors for the SD-IoT system, such as normal, scanning, flooding, and escalating to attack conditions. Thus, these states are parametrically defined, learned as part of the training process, and can be used across all of the system’s time zones, which means that they are not affected by the encoder’s encoding of the data. Discussion

The proposed RADIUS framework demonstrates strong robustness compared to existing SD-IoT intrusion detection approaches by integrating algorithmic, theoretical, and calibration-driven innovations within a unified architecture. In contrast to conventional IDS models that primarily emphasize feature extraction or early detection, RADIUS explicitly incorporates temporal modeling, adaptive state reasoning, and statistically grounded decision calibration to address the challenges posed by non-stationary and resource-constrained IoT environments.

Compared with standard CNN-based models, which are effective at spatial feature extraction but often lack temporal interpretability, RADIUS integrates a Temporal Convolutional Encoder (TCE) to capture long-range temporal dependencies and rare intrusion signatures in network traffic. Similarly, while wrapper-based methods such as EWA focus on adaptive feature weighting, they may struggle with cross-environment generalization. RADIUS addresses this limitation through the Adaptive State Transition Module (ASTM), which explicitly models latent system states and enables resilience to concept drift and class imbalance.

Early-detection models such as MT-EDD are designed to detect attacks at an early stage but often suffer from reduced recall for minority or infrequent attack classes. RADIUS mitigates this issue through a dual-objective optimization strategy that balances detection accuracy with environment invariance and sensitivity to rare events, thereby maintaining high recall across diverse attack distributions. In addition, the integration of EVT-based calibration with conformal prediction provides statistically grounded confidence estimates and adaptive decision thresholds, improving robustness and ensuring trustworthy and reproducible intrusion detection decisions across heterogeneous SD-IoT deployments.

The contribution of each architectural component is further supported by ablation analyses conducted across multiple benchmark datasets, which consistently show performance degradation when individual modules are removed. These findings indicate that the effectiveness of RADIUS arises from the synergistic interaction of temporal encoding, adaptive state modeling, robust optimization, and calibrated anomaly scoring, rather than from any single component in isolation. Overall, RADIUS resolves key limitations of traditional CNN-based classifiers, ensemble weighting schemes, and early detection mechanisms by embedding robustness, interpretability, and adaptability directly into its design.

Conclusion and future work

Conclusion

In this study, we proposed RADIUS, a novel intrusion detection framework tailored for software-defined IoT (SD-IoT) environments. Conventional CNN-based, ensemble, and early-detection approaches often struggle with scalability, interpretability, and sensitivity to rare attack events. To address these limitations, RADIUS integrates a Temporal Convolutional Encoder (TCE) for effective long-range temporal modeling, an Adaptive State Transition Module (ASTM) for interpretable and adaptive decision-making, and a dual-objective learning strategy that jointly accounts for dataset heterogeneity and class imbalance. In addition, the incorporation of extreme value theory (EVT) with conformal prediction enables calibrated and reliable anomaly detection, even under distributional shifts.

Experimental evaluation across multiple benchmark datasets demonstrates that RADIUS consistently outperforms existing methods, achieving an accuracy of 0.997, precision of 0.994, recall of 0.998, and an F1-score of 0.996. These results indicate that RADIUS effectively addresses key challenges in SD-IoT security, including robustness, efficiency, imbalance handling, and reliability within edge–controller architectures.

Limitations

Although the proposed RADIUS framework demonstrates strong performance across CICIoT2023, ToN-IoT, and UNSW-NB15 datasets, the study has certain limitations.

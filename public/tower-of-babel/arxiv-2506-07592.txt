On the stability of the annulus for the torsion of multiply connected domains
V. Amato, L. Barbato

Abstract
We establish a quantitative version of the isoperimetric inequality for the torsion of multiply connected domains, among sets with given area and with given joint area of the holes. Since the optimal shape is the annulus, we investigate how a given domain approaches an annular configuration when its torsion is close to the optimal value. Our result shows that when the torsional rigidity is nearly optimal, the domain Ω \Omega must be close to an annulus. Keywords: Laplace operator, stability, comparison results. MSC 2020: 35J05, 35B35, 35B45.

Introduction

Let us consider a homogeneous beam with cross-section a multiply connected domain
\Omega
.

Definition 1.1
.

A set
\Omega
is a multiply connected domain if
\Omega=G\setminus S
where

(i)

there exists
G,\Omega_{1},\dots,\Omega_{m}
are bounded Lipschitz domain of
\R^{n}
;

(ii)

\overline{\Omega}_{i}\subset G
for
i=1,\dots,m
;

(iii)

\overline{\Omega}_{i}\cap\overline{\Omega}_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
;

(iv)

S=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{m}\overline{\Omega}_{i}
.

It is well known that the torsional rigidity of
\Omega
, i.e. the one of the beam with cross-section
\Omega
, can be written as the maximum of a Rayleigh quotient (see for instance
[
11
,
35
]
), that is

T(\Omega)=\max\left\{\frac{\displaystyle\left(\int_{G}\varphi\,dx\right)^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{G}{\left|\nabla\varphi\right|}^{2}\,dx}:\,\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(G),\varphi\equiv\text{ constant on }\Omega_{i}\,\forall i=1,\dots m\right\}.

(1)

In the case of simply connected domains (
m=0
), in 1856 de Saint-Venant conjectured that among all beams with cross-section of prescribed area, the one with circular cross-section has the highest torsional rigidity. The complete proof of such conjecture is due to Pólya in 1948, see
[
33
]
.

The de Saint-Venant inequality was latter generalized to different settings, for instance in
[
19
]
, Diaz and Weinstein gave some upper and lower bounds for the torsional rigidity of a beam also in the case of multiply connected domains, in terms of its second order momentum.

In 1950, Pólya and Weinstein proved in
[
35
]
that among all beams with multiply connected cross-section with
given area and with given joint area of the holes, the one with annular cross-section has the highest torsional rigidity. Namely, using definition (
1
) we have the following inequality

T(\Omega)\leq T(\Omega^{\text{O}}),

(2)

where
\Omega^{\text{O}}=G^{\sharp}\setminus S^{\sharp}
and
G^{\sharp}
and
S^{\sharp}
stands for the centered ball having same measure as
G
and
S
respectively. Lastly, this result have been extended in any dimension also for degenerate elliptic operator in
[
11
]
.

By the way, this can also be seen as a limiting case of a double-phase problem, studied by several authors, such as in
[
17
,
18
,
32
]
.

Once an isoperimetric inequality is established, a natural question that arises is its rigidity, specifically, under what conditions equality holds. For instance, in the case of simply connected domains, this was proved by Polya in
[
33
]
and later by
[
7
]
. For multiply connected domains, Pólya and Weinstein provided a partial answer. They established that if equality holds in the isoperimetric inequality (
2
), then the domain must necessarily have exactly one hole.
Up to our knowledge, no additional information has been provided in the case of multiply connected domains (
m\geq 1
), so we give the following result.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{2}
satisfy definition
1.1
. If equality holds in (
2
), that is

T(\Omega)=T(\Omega^{\text{O}}),

then, up to translation,
\Omega=\Omega^{\text{O}}
and therefore there exists
x_{0}\in\R^{2}
such that
G=G^{\sharp}+x_{0}
and
S=S^{\sharp}+x_{0}
.

We will prove, in section
3
, Proposition
1.1
as a particular case of a more general theorem given in any dimension and for a more general class of linear elliptic operator.

The fact that annuli are the only sets for which the equality holds in (
2
) naturally raises the question of the stability of this inequality. More precisely, one can try to improve (
2
) by adding a remainder term in the left-hand side that quantifies the deviation of a set from the corresponding annulus, that is, the annulus with the same measure and the same hole measure.

Firstly, we need to introduce a way of measuring the deviation from the corresponding annulus.
More precisely, we firstly aim to measure the distance both between the outer boundary and the sphere, and between the holes and the ball. One way to measure them is through the Fraenkel Asymmetry index, see for instance
[
23
]
for its properties. This index is defined by the following equation

\alpha(\Omega):=\min_{x\in\R^{n}}\bigg\{\dfrac{|\Omega\triangle B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}\;,\;|B_{r}(x)|=|\Omega|\bigg\},

where the symbol
\triangle
denotes the symmetric difference (see definition in subsection
2.3
).

Hence, we state our main theorem as follow.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{2}
satisfy definition
1.1
such that
\displaystyle{{\left|S\right|}\leq\frac{3}{4}{\left|G\right|}}
. Then

T(\Omega^{\text{O}})-T(\Omega)\geq\frac{1}{3^{2}2^{9}\pi\gamma_{2}}\left[{\left|G\right|}^{2}\alpha^{3}(G)+{\left|S\right|}^{2}\alpha^{3}(S)\right].

The proof of this theorem relies on the propagation of asymmetry discussed in
[
26
]
, which has been used, explained, and further explored in various works
[
3
,
4
,
8
,
15
,
31
]
.

We would also like to point out that quantitative inequalities where annuli are optimal have already appeared in the literature. In
[
16
]
, the authors studied a mixed eigenvalue problem on a domain with holes, providing an interpretation of the distance between
\Omega
and the corresponding annulus. Also in this case, the authors had to divide the proof into two parts: one for the outer boundary and one for the inner boundary.

Motivated by the classical Fraenkel Asymmetry index, we define an "Annular Asymmetry index" as follows:

\beta(\Omega)=\inf\{{\left|\Omega\triangle A\right|}\,:\,A=B_{1}\setminus B_{2},\,{\left|B_{1}\right|}={\left|G\right|},\,{\left|B_{2}\right|}={\left|S\right|}\},

that vanishes if and only if the set
\Omega
is an annulus.

With this definition, we can state our main Theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{2}
satisfy definition
1.1
. Then, there exists
C>0
and
\overline{\theta}>0
, depending on
{\left|G\right|}
and
{\left|S\right|}
, such that

T(\Omega^{\text{O}})-T(\Omega)\geq C\beta^{\overline{\theta}}(\Omega).

(3)

The proof of this theorem relies on the closeness of the torsion function to that of the annulus. Since the holes can be seen as the regions where the gradient of the torsion function vanishes, this closeness implies that not only the outer domain and the holes are almost a balls, as shown in Theorem
1.2
, but also that these two balls must be almost centered.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section

Rigidity of the problem

As already mentioned in the introduction, we prove Proposition

in a more general setting. Let u and v be solutions to

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega

is a particular case when

Before proving the rigidity, we recall the following adapted version of the Brothers and Ziemer theorem contained in

Theorem 3.2

Let

be a non negative function in

{\left|\{x:\nabla u^{\sharp}(x)=0\}\cap u^{\sharp^{-1}}(0,{\left\|u\right\|}_{\infty})\right|}=0.

which is almost everywhere equal to

Now we are able to prove the rigidity result.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

In the equality case, the inequality (

) gives:

\int_{0}^{\left|G\right|}s^{\frac{2}{n}}\zeta^{-1}(s)\,ds=\int_{\left|S\right|}^{\left|G\right|}s^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\nu^{*}\left(s-{\left|S\right|}\right)\right)^{-1}\,ds=\int_{0}^{\left|G\right|}s^{\frac{2}{n}}\overline{\nu}(s)\,ds

Now, given that

which gives
\tilde{u}^{\sharp}\leq\tilde{V}
(by (

)) and the assumption ensures the equality

.

On the other hand, equality in the (isoperimetric) inequality (

and together with
\displaystyle{\int_{u>{\left\|u\right\|}_{\infty}}\nu{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{2}\,dx=\int_{{\left|u\right|}>{\left\|u\right\|}_{\infty}}a_{ij}u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}\,dx}=0

\int_{\Omega}\nu{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}a_{ij}u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}\,dx=T(\Omega).

(16)

But equality holds, so (

By theorem

Hence, (

a_{i,j}(x+x_{0})x_{i}=\nu(x)x_{j},

for some

∎

\displaystyle\leq{\left|(S\cap\{w>\overline{t}\})\setminus B^{\prime}\right|}+{\left|S\setminus\tilde{S}\right|}

\displaystyle\leq{\left|\{w>\overline{t}\}\setminus B^{\prime}\right|}+{\left|S\setminus\tilde{S}\right|}

\displaystyle\leq{\left|\{w>\overline{t}\}\setminus B^{\prime\prime}\right|}+{\left|B^{\prime\prime}\setminus B^{\prime}\right|}+{\left|S\setminus\tilde{S}\right|}\leq C\varepsilon^{\overline{\theta}}.

Since, if we denote by
B^{\prime\prime\prime}
the ball which realize
\alpha(G)
, then we have

\beta(\Omega)\leq{\left|G\triangle B^{\prime\prime\prime}\right|}+{\left|S\triangle B^{\prime}\right|},

hence the thesis.

Maximal functions and multipliers for subkernels of Toeplitz operators
M. Cristina Câmara, C. Carteiro, C. Diogo

Abstract
We study the relations between maximal functions in a Toeplitz kernel and those in a subkernel of the same Toeplitz operator, as well as the question of how multipliers between Toeplitz kernels act on subkernels. We use those relations to obtain model space representations, in particular isometric model space representations and Hayashi’s representation, for some important classes of Toeplitz kernels. Keywords: Toeplitz kernels, maximal functions, multipliers, Toeplitz subkernels, model spaces. MSC: 47B35, 47B38, 30H10.

1
Introduction

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})=:L^{\infty}
, where
\mathbb{T}
is the unit circle. We denote by
T_{g}
the Toeplitz operator with symbol
g
, defined on the Hardy space of the unit disk,
H^{2}_{+}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, identified as usual with a subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})=:L^{2}
, by

T_{g}f=P^{+}gf\quad,\quad f\in H^{2}_{+}\ ,

(1.1)

where
P^{+}
denotes the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}
onto
H^{2}_{+}
.
We assume throughout the paper that
g\neq 0
.
Kernels of Toeplitz operators, also called Toeplitz kernels, have attracted a great interest, as they have fascinating properties, appear in numerous applications, and include important classes of analytic functions in the disk, such as model spaces.

Multipliers between Toeplitz kernels play an important role in understanding the structure of these spaces and in describing them.
In
[
8
]
Crofoot characterised the multipliers from a model space
onto
another, describing in particular the isometric surjective multipliers between model spaces (see also
[
16
]
).
The more general question of characterising the multipliers from one model space
into
another was studied by Fricain, Hartmann and Ross in
[
10
]
, see also
[
11
]
, and, motivated by their work, Câmara and Partington characterised in
[
6
]
the multipliers between two Toeplitz kernels in general.

Multipliers from a model space onto a Toeplitz kernel, allowing to describe the latter in the form

\ker T_{g}=wK_{\theta}\quad,\quad\text{with }\theta\text{ inner,}

(1.2)

are particularly important.
The equality (
1.2
) is called a
model space representation
of
\ker T_{g}
;
if multiplication by
w
is an isometry from
K_{\theta}
onto
\ker T_{g}
, (
1.2
) is called an
isometric model space representation
(
[
3
]
).
Such a representation always exists.
Indeed Hayashi showed, in
[
13
]
, that the kernel of a Toeplitz operator, if non-trivial, can be written as

\ker T_{g}=uK_{z\gamma}

(1.3)

where
u
is outer,
\gamma
is inner and
u
multiplies the model space isometrically onto
\ker T_{g}
.
Hayashi’s representation (
1.3
) is unique up to a unimodular constant.

The study of multipliers between Toeplitz kernels brings to light a strong connection between several distinct topics, such as Carleson measures, Smirnov spaces and maximal functions.
The latter were introduced in
[
5
]
. They are elements of a Toeplitz kernel which cannot belong to any “smaller” Toeplitz kernel, i.e., functions
f^{M}\in\ker T_{g}
such that, for any
h\in L^{\infty}
,

f^{M}\in\ker T_{h}\implies\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{h}\ .

(1.4)

We say that
\ker T_{g}
is the minimal Toeplitz kernel for
f^{M}
and we write

\ker T_{g}=\mathcal{K}_{min}(f^{M})\ .

(1.5)

It was shown in
[
5
]
that in every non-trivial Toeplitz kernel there is a maximal function, which uniquely defines that kernel, and, conversely, for every
f\in H^{2}_{+}
there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel to which
f
belongs.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a Toeplitz kernel and
\mathcal{A}\subset\ker T_{g}
, we say that
\mathcal{A}
is a
subkernel of
T_{g}
.
In this paper we study the relations between maximal functions in a Toeplitz kernel and maximal functions in subkernels of the same Toeplitz operator, and how multipliers between two Toeplitz kernels act on subkernels.
A crucial role is played by inner-outer factorisation of maximal functions and related factorisations of inner functions.
We use those relations to obtain model space representations, in particular isometric model space representations and Hayashi’s representation, for some important classes of Toeplitz kernels, with an emphasis on Toeplitz kernels contained in model spaces.

2
Toeplitz subkernels

The question when is a Toeplitz kernel contained in another Toeplitz kernel can be reduced to the question of characterising the multipliers between two Toeplitz kernels.
Note that, while multipliers from one model space into another must be in
H^{2}_{+}
(
[
10
]
), multipliers between general Toeplitz kernels do not necessarily satisfy that condition;
they must however belong to the Smirnov class
\mathcal{N}^{+}
(
[
6
]
).
They are characterised in Theorem
2.1
below.
Let
\ker T_{g}\neq\{0\}
.
We say that
w\in\mathcal{C}(\ker T_{g})
whenever
|w|^{2}dm
is a Carleson measure for
\ker T_{g}
, that is,
w\ker T_{g}\subset L^{2}
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
)
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

w\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{h}
;

(ii)

w\in\mathcal{C}(\ker T_{g})
and
\displaystyle wh/g\in\overline{\mathcal{N}^{+}}
;

(iii)

w\in\mathcal{C}(\ker T_{g})
and for some (and hence every) maximal function
\displaystyle f^{M}_{g}
in
\ker T_{g}
we have
\displaystyle wf^{M}_{g}\in\ker T_{h}
.

For
w=1
, this provides necessary and sufficient conditions for a Toeplitz kernel to be included in another Toeplitz kernel.

Corollary 2.2

(
[
6
, Proposition 2.16]
)
.

\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{G}\iff{G/g\in\overline{\mathcal{N}^{+}}}\iff
there exists a maximal function
f^{M}_{g}
in
\ker T_{g}
such that
f^{M}_{g}\in\ker T_{G}
.

Corollary 2.3

(
[
6
]
)
.

\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{G}\iff\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{G\alpha}\
for some inner function
\alpha\iff g=G\alpha\overline{O_{1}}/\overline{O_{2}}
with
O_{1},O_{2}\in H^{\infty}
outer.

In this paper we will be particularly interested in Toeplitz kernels contained in model spaces.
A natural question regarding subkernels thus arises: which Toeplitz kernels contain some model space and, vice-versa, which Toeplitz kernels are contained in some model space?
We have the following.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\theta
be a non-constant inner function.

(i)

If
K_{\theta}\subset\ker T_{g}
, then
\ker T_{g}
is a model space.

(ii)

If
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\theta}
, then
\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{\overline{\theta}\alpha}\
for some inner function
\alpha
;
if
|g|=1
then
g=\overline{\theta}\alpha
for some inner function
\alpha
.

It follows from Proposition
2.4
(ii) that not all Toeplitz kernels can be contained in a model space.
Indeed, to every Toeplitz kernel one can associate a unimodular symbol (see (
3.1
)).
If
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\theta}
with
|g|=1
, then we must have that
\theta g=\alpha
for some inner function
\alpha
and this may not be possible, as for instance if
g=\overline{z}^{3/2}
(
\ker T_{g}=\operatorname{span}\{(1+z)^{1/2}\}
).
In particular, there are finite dimensional Toeplitz kernels that are not contained in any model space.

We can however, characterise some classes of Toeplitz operators whose kernels are contained in model spaces.
This is the case of Toeplitz operators with symbol in
\overline{H^{\infty}}
(whose kernels are themselves model spaces) and all Toeplitz operators with rational symbols, as we show next.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
denote the space of all rational functions without poles on
\mathbb{T}
and let
g\in\mathcal{R}
.
Then
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\theta}
for some inner function
\theta
.

Proof.

First note that, if
g
has
n_{\mathbb{T}}
zeroes on
\mathbb{T}
(counting multiplicities), we can write

\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{\frac{P}{Q}z^{n_{\mathbb{T}}}}

(2.1)

where
P
and
Q
are relatively prime polynomials without zeroes on
\mathbb{T}
by
[
7
, Corollary 6.3]
.
Let us write

P=P_{-}P_{+}\quad,\quad Q=Q_{-}Q_{+}\ ,

(2.2)

where
P_{-},Q_{-}
only have zeroes in
\mathbb{D}
and
P_{+},Q_{+}
only have zeroes in the exterior of
\mathbb{D}
, denoted
\mathbb{D}_{-}
.
Let

n=\deg Q_{-}-\deg P_{-}\ ;

(2.3)

thus,

\frac{P}{Q}z^{n_{\mathbb{T}}}=\underbrace{\left(\frac{P_{-}}{Q_{-}}z^{n}\right)}_{\smash[b]{\scriptstyle\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}}}z^{n_{\mathbb{T}}-n}\underbrace{\frac{P_{+}}{Q_{+}}}_{\smash[b]{\scriptstyle\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}}},

(2.4)

where we denote by
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{A}
the group of invertible elements in a unital algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

If
n_{\mathbb{T}}-n\geq 0
, then
\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{\frac{P}{Q}z^{n_{\mathbb{T}}}}=\{0\}
;
if
n_{\mathbb{T}}-n<0
, then

\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{\frac{P}{Q}z^{n_{\mathbb{T}}}}=\ker T_{\frac{P_{+}}{Q_{+}}z^{n_{\mathbb{T}}-n}}=\frac{Q_{+}}{P_{+}}K_{z^{n-n_{\mathbb{T}}}}\neq\{0\}.

(2.5)

Now,
P_{+}
and
Q_{+}
are products of factors of the form
z-1/\overline{\lambda}
, with
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, and

z-\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}}=-\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}}\ \underbrace{\frac{1-\overline{\lambda}z}{z-\lambda}}_{\smash[b]{\scriptstyle\overline{B_{\lambda}}}}\ \underbrace{\frac{z-\lambda}{z}}_{\smash[b]{\scriptstyle\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}}}\ z\ ,

(2.6)

where we denote by
B_{\lambda}
the Blaschke factor

B_{\lambda}=\frac{z-\lambda}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\ .

(2.7)

Thus, there are finite Blaschke products
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
such that

\displaystyle P_{+}

\displaystyle=\overline{B_{1}}z^{n_{1}}\overline{h_{1}}\quad,\quad h_{1}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}\ ,

(2.8)

\displaystyle Q_{+}

\displaystyle=\overline{B_{2}}z^{n_{2}}\overline{h_{2}}\quad,\quad h_{2}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}\ ,

(2.9)

where
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
are the number of zeroes of
P_{+}
and
Q_{+}
(in
\mathbb{D}_{-}
), respectively.
Therefore

\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{\frac{P_{+}}{Q_{+}}z^{n_{\mathbb{T}}-n}}=\ker T_{\overline{B_{1}}B_{2}z^{N}}\ ,

(2.10)

with

N=n_{\mathbb{T}}-n+n_{1}-n_{2}\ .

(2.11)

If
N\geq 0
, then
\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{\overline{B_{1}}}=K_{B_{1}}
;
if
N<0
, then
\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{\overline{B_{1}}\overline{z}^{|N|}}=K_{B_{1}z^{|N|}}
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

It is easy to see that a function
f\in H^{2}_{+}
belongs to some model space if and only if
\overline{f}
is of bounded type, i.e.,
\overline{f}=f_{1}/f_{2}
with
f_{1},f_{2}\in H^{\infty}
(
[
15
]
).
Rational functions are of bounded type, hence each rational function in
H^{2}_{+}
belongs to a model space.
Proposition
2.5
states that, when
g\in\mathcal{R}
, there exists a model space to which all functions in
\ker T_{g}
, which are rational functions, belong.

The model spaces in the last paragraph of the proof of Proposition
2.5
are in fact minimal, as a consequence of the following propositions.
We say that
K_{\theta}
is the
minimal model space containing
\ker T_{g}
if
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\theta}
and for any inner function
\beta
such that
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\beta}
, we have
K_{\theta}\subset K_{\beta}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
|g|=1
,
\ker T_{g}\neq\{0\}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a minimal model space containing
\ker T_{g}
;

(ii)

g=\overline{\theta}\alpha
with
\theta,\alpha
inner and such that
\gcd(\theta,\alpha)=1
.

In that case,
K_{\theta}
is the minimal model space containing
\ker T_{g}
.

Proof.

If (ii) holds then
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\theta}
and, if
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\beta}
for any other inner function
\beta
, then, by Proposition
2.4
(ii)

g=\overline{\theta}\alpha=\overline{\beta}\gamma

(2.12)

for some inner function
\gamma
, so that
\theta\gamma=\alpha\beta
.
Since
\gcd(\theta,\alpha)=1
, it follows that
\theta\preceq\beta
, i.e.
\theta\overline{\beta}\in H^{\infty}
, and
K_{\theta}\subset K_{\beta}
.

Conversely, if (i) holds, let
K_{\theta}
be the minimal model space containing
\ker T_{g}
. Then, for any inner function
\beta
,

\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\beta}\implies K_{\theta}\subset K_{\beta}\ .

(2.13)

Since
g=\overline{\theta}\alpha
for some inner function
\alpha
, if
\gcd(\theta,\alpha)=\delta\notin\mathbb{C}
, then
\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{(\overline{\theta}\delta)(\alpha\overline{\delta})}
and
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\theta\overline{\delta}}\subsetneq K_{\theta}
, contradicting (
2.13
), so we must have
\gcd(\theta,\alpha)=1
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

If
\{0\}\neq\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\beta}
for some inner function
\beta
, then there exists a minimal model space
K_{\theta}
containing
\ker T_{g}
.
If
g=\overline{\beta}\gamma
with
\gamma
inner, then
K_{\theta}=K_{\beta\overline{\delta}}
where
\delta=\gcd(\beta,\gamma)
.

Proof.

If
\ker T_{g}\subset K_{\beta}
then
g=\overline{\beta}\gamma=(\overline{\beta}\delta)(\gamma\overline{\delta})
where
\gcd(\beta\overline{\delta},\gamma\overline{\delta})=1
.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{R}
, with
n_{\mathbb{T}}
zeroes on
\mathbb{T}
(counting multiplicities) and let
n>n_{\mathbb{T}}
, where
n
is defined as in (
2.3
).
Let moreover
B_{1}
and
N
be defined as in (
2.8
) and (
2.11
), respectively.
Then,

(i)

if
N\geq 0
,
K_{B_{1}}
is the minimal model space containing
\ker T_{g}
;

(ii)

if
N<0
,
K_{B_{1}z^{|N|}}
is the minimal model space containing
\ker T_{g}
.

Proof.

By construction,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
do not have common zeroes and neither
B_{1}
or
B_{2}
vanish at
0
, so, if
N\geq 0
, we have

\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{\overline{B_{1}}(B_{2}z^{N})}\text{ with }\gcd(B_{1},B_{2}z^{N})=1

(2.14)

and, if
N<0
,

\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{\overline{B_{1}z^{|N|}}B_{2}}\text{ with }\gcd(B_{1}z^{|N|},B_{2})=1.

(2.15)

∎

We illustrate this result in the following example.

Example 2.10
.

We have
K=\ker T_{\frac{z-2}{z^{2}(z-3)(z-4)}}\subset K_{z^{3}B_{\frac{1}{2}}}
.
To verify this inclusion, let
\phi_{+}\in K
, i.e.,
\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
and

\frac{z-2}{z^{2}(z-3)(z-4)}\phi_{+}=\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}\quad(H^{2}_{-}:=L^{2}\ominus H^{2}_{+})\ .

(2.16)

Then

\frac{1-\frac{1}{2}z}{z-\frac{1}{2}}\ \frac{1}{z^{3}}\ \phi_{+}=\underbrace{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(z-3)(z-4)}{z(z-\frac{1}{2})}}_{\smash[b]{\scriptstyle\in\overline{H^{\infty}}}}\underbrace{\left(\frac{z-2}{z^{2}(z-3)(z-4)}\phi_{+}\right)}_{\smash[b]{\scriptstyle\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}}}\in H^{2}_{-}\ .

(2.17)

So indeed
\phi_{+}\in\ker T_{\overline{z}^{3}\overline{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}}=K_{z^{3}B_{\frac{1}{2}}}
.
We have that
K_{z^{3}B_{\frac{1}{2}}}
is the minimal model space containing
K
.

3
Maximal functions in subkernels

We now consider the relations between maximal functions in a Toeplitz kernel and in a subkernel of the same Toeplitz operator.

In every nontrivial Toeplitz kernel
\ker T_{g}
one can find a maximal function
f^{M}
(which is not unique) such that
\ker T_{g}=\mathcal{K}_{min}(f^{M})
.
If
f^{M}=IO
is an inner-outer factorisation of that maximal function, with
I
inner and
O\in H^{2}_{+}
outer (a notation that we will keep throughout the paper), then we can associate a unimodular symbol to
\ker T_{g}
(
[
5
]
):

\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{\overline{zI}\frac{\overline{O}}{O}}\ .

(3.1)

Thus every maximal function in a Toeplitz kernel uniquely determines that kernel.
Maximal functions are characterised by the following necessary and sufficient condition.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

f^{M}\in H^{2}_{+}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}
if and only if, for some
\mathcal{O}\in H^{2}_{+}
outer, we have

gf^{M}=\overline{z}\overline{\mathcal{O}}\ .

(3.2)

We can express this result in terms of a conjugation (
[
12
]
), if
|g|=1
.
Then one can define a natural conjugation on
\ker T_{g}
(
[
9
]
)

C_{\overline{g}}f=\overline{g}\overline{z}\overline{f}\quad,\quad\text{for }f\in\ker T_{g}\ ,

(3.3)

and the necessary and sufficient condition (
3.2
) can be reformulated as follows.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

If
|g|=1
, then
f^{M}\in H^{2}_{+}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}
if and only if
C_{\overline{g}}f^{M}
is outer in
H^{2}_{+}
.

Moreover, we have the following.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
3
, Proposition 4.3]
)
.

Let
|g|=1
.
If
f^{M}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}
, then
C_{\overline{g}}f^{M}
is the outer factor in an inner-outer factorisation of
f^{M}
.

In particular, if
g=\overline{\theta}
, where
\theta
is an inner function, and for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, denoting

\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{\lambda}=\frac{\theta-\theta(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad k^{\theta}_{\lambda}=\frac{1-\overline{\theta(\lambda)}\theta}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}=C_{\theta}\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{\lambda}\ ,

(3.4)

we have that
\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{\lambda}
is a maximal function in
K_{\theta}
and

\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{\lambda}=\frac{\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{\lambda}}{k^{\theta}_{\lambda}}\ k^{\theta}_{\lambda}\

(3.5)

is an inner-outer factorisation of
\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{\lambda}
, with
\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{\lambda}/k^{\theta}_{\lambda}
inner and
k^{\theta}_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
outer.

We now study the question of how to obtain maximal functions in a subkernel of
T_{g}
, given a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}
.

The following is a generalisation of Theorem 6.9 in
[
2
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
u\in H^{\infty}\backslash\{0\}
and let
f^{M}
be a maximal function in
\ker T_{Gu}
.
Then
uf^{M}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
,
Guf^{M}=\overline{z}\overline{\mathcal{O}}
with
\mathcal{O}\in H^{2}_{+}
outer, so
G(uf^{M})=\overline{z}\overline{\mathcal{O}}
and
uf^{M}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
.
∎

Corollary 3.5

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\{0\}\neq\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{G}
, with
\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{G\alpha}
where
\alpha
is inner.
If
f^{M}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}
then
F^{M}=\alpha f^{M}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
.
Conversely, if
F^{M}\in\alpha H^{2}_{+}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
, then
f^{M}=\overline{\alpha}F^{M}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{G\alpha}
.

It follows from Corollary
3.5
that the question of finding maximal functions in a subkernel of
T_{G}
,
\ker T_{G\alpha}
, is equivalent to finding maximal functions in
\ker T_{G}
whose inner factor can be divided by
\alpha
.

Note that, if
\ker T_{G\alpha}\neq\{0\}
, then there exists a maximal function
F^{M}
in
\ker T_{G}
, with inner-outer factorisation
F^{M}=IO
, where the inner factor
I
is such that
\ker T_{\overline{z}\alpha\overline{I}}\neq\{0\}
(cf. Corollary
3.5
).
We will focus here on the case where
\alpha=B
is a finite Blaschke product, with
\dim K_{B}=N\geq 1
.
Then

\ker T_{GB}\neq\{0\}\iff\dim\ker T_{G}>N

(3.6)

(
[
2
]
) and there exists a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
, as above, with inner-outer factorisation

F^{M}=IO\quad,\quad\text{where }\dim K_{I}\geq N.

(3.7)

This is an important case which is related, for instance, with the interpolation problem of describing the subspace of all functions in
\ker T_{G}
that have zeroes of a prescribed order at certain points (which is of the form
B\ker T_{GB}
where
B
is a finite Blaschke product),
with invariance properties regarding multiplication operators and orthogonal decompositions of Toeplitz kernels (
[
4
]
),
and with kernels of asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators (
[
7
]
).

We start by considering the case where
N=1
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\dim\ker T_{G}>1
.
If
F^{M}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
, with inner-outer factorisation
F^{M}=IO
, where
I
is a non-constant inner function and
O
is outer, then

F^{M}_{\lambda}=(z-\lambda)\tilde{k}^{I}_{\lambda}O=B_{\lambda}\tilde{k}^{I}_{\lambda}(1-\overline{\lambda}z)O

(3.8)

is a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Assume that
|G|=1
.
Since
F^{M}=IO
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
, we have that
C_{\overline{G}}(IO)=cO
for some constant
c
with
|c|=1
, so

C_{\overline{G}}[(z-\lambda)\tilde{k}^{I}_{\lambda}O]=C_{\overline{G}}[(I-I(\lambda))O]=cO-\overline{I(\lambda)}cIO=(1-\overline{I(\lambda)}I)cO

(3.9)

where the right-hand side represents an outer function.
Thus
F^{M}_{\lambda}

4
Multipliers on subkernels

We now study the question of how multipliers between two Toeplitz kernels act on subkernels and, in particular, whether they map subkernels onto subkernels.

If
\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{G}
and
w
is a multiplier from
\ker T_{G}

into

\ker T_{H}
, i.e.,
w\ker T_{G}\subset\ker T_{H}
, then we have that
w\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{H}
, but
w\ker T_{g}
may not be a subkernel of
T_{H}
.
As a concrete example, if
\alpha
and
\theta
are inner functions,
\alpha
multiplies
K_{\theta}
into
K_{\theta\alpha}
, but
\alpha K_{\theta}
is not a Toeplitz kernel (
[
5
,
7
]
).
However, as we show next, the situation is different if
w
multiplies
\ker T_{G}

onto

\ker T_{H}
.

We have the following characterisation of multipliers from one Toeplitz kernel onto another.
Note that such multipliers must belong to the Smirnov class
\mathcal{N}^{+}
, along with their inverses (
[
6
]
).

Theorem 4.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
w^{\pm 1}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

w\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{h}
;

(ii)

w\in\mathcal{C}(\ker T_{g})
,
w^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}(\ker T_{h})
and
\frac{h}{g}=\frac{\overline{w}}{w}\frac{\overline{O}_{1}}{\overline{O}_{2}}
with
O_{1},O_{2}\in H^{2}_{+}
, outer;

(iii)

w\in\mathcal{C}(\ker T_{g})
,
w^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}(\ker T_{h})
and, for some (and hence every) maximal function
f^{M}_{g}
in
\ker T_{g}
, we have that
wf^{M}_{g}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{h}
.

Moreover,

\ker T_{h}=w\ker T_{g}\implies\ker T_{h}=\ker T_{g\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}\ .

(4.1)

For
w=1
, we obtain from Theorem
4.1
necessary and sufficient conditions for two Toeplitz operators to have the same kernel.

Corollary 4.2

(
[
6
, Corollary 2.19]
)
.

\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{h}\iff\frac{h}{g}=\frac{\overline{O}_{1}}{\overline{O}_{2}}
for some
O_{1},O_{2}\in H^{2}_{+}
outer.

If
g\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
then
\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{h}\iff h=g\overline{O}
with
O\in H^{\infty}
, outer.
In particular, if
|g|=|h|=1
, then

\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{h}\iff h=\lambda g\text{ with }\lambda\in\mathbb{C},|\lambda|=1.

(4.2)

It follows from Theorem
4.1
, Corollary
4.2
and (
4.1
) that the question when is
w\ker T_{g}
a Toeplitz kernel, assuming
w^{\pm 1}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
, is reduced to the question when is
\ker T_{g\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}=w\ker T_{g}
.
For subkernels of a Toeplitz operator, we have the following.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\{0\}\neq\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{G}
and suppose that
w\ker T_{G}=\ker T_{H}
.
Then
w\ker T_{g}
is a subkernel of
T_{H}
and we have

w\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{g\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}\ .

(4.3)

Proof.

From the assumptions and Theorem
4.1
we have that
w^{\pm 1}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
and
w\ker T_{g}\subset w\ker T_{G}\subset L^{2}
.
On the other hand,
\ker T_{H}=\ker T_{G\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}
, by (
4.1
), and
\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{G\alpha}
for some inner function
\alpha
by Corollary
2.3
, so

w^{-1}\ker T_{g\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}=w^{-1}\ker T_{G\alpha\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}\subset w^{-1}\ker T_{G\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}=w^{-1}\ker T_{H}\subset L^{2}.

(4.4)

Moreover, if
f^{M}_{g}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}
, then
\alpha f^{M}_{g}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
, by Corollary
3.5
, so
w\alpha f^{M}_{g}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{H}
, by Theorem
4.1
(iii).
Applying Corollary
3.5
again,
wf^{M}_{g}
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{H\alpha}=\ker T_{G\alpha\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}=\ker T_{g\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}
.
It follows from Theorem
4.1
(iii) that
w\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{g\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}
.
∎

Thus we see that a multiplier from a Toeplitz kernel onto another Toeplitz kernel maps, by multiplication, a subkernel of the first Toeplitz operator onto a subkernel of the second Toeplitz operator.

A simple but important consequence is the following.
Recall that

\ker T_{g}\subset\ker T_{G}\iff\ker T_{g}=\ker T_{G\alpha}\text{ for some inner function }\alpha.

(4.5)

Corollary 4.4
.

If
w\ker T_{G}=\ker T_{H}
then
w\ker T_{G\alpha}=\ker T_{H\alpha}
, for any inner function
\alpha
.

Proof.

Since
\ker T_{G\alpha}\subset\ker T_{G}
, by (
4.3
) and Proposition
4.3
we have that
w\ker T_{G\alpha}=\ker T_{G\alpha\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}
.
Since
\ker T_{H}=\ker T_{G\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}
it follows from Corollary
4.2
that
H=G\frac{\overline{w}}{w}\frac{\overline{O_{1}}}{\overline{O_{2}}}
where
O_{1},O_{2}\in H^{2}_{+}
are outer, and therefore
\ker T_{H\alpha}=\ker T_{G\alpha\frac{\overline{w}}{w}\frac{\overline{O_{1}}}{\overline{O_{2}}}}=\ker T_{G\alpha\frac{\overline{w}}{w}}=w\ker T_{G\alpha}
.
∎

Corollary
4.4
generalises a result by Crofoot on model spaces (
[
8
, Corollary 19]
).

We also have:

Theorem 4.5

(
[
8
, Theorem 10]
)
.

Given any model space
K_{\theta_{1}}
there exists an isometric multiplier
m
from
K_{\theta_{1}}
onto
K_{\theta_{2}}
, where
\theta_{2}
is an inner function, if and only if

\theta_{2}=\frac{\theta_{1}-\overline{p}}{1-p\theta_{1}}\quad\text{ with }p\in\mathbb{C},|p|=1,

(4.6)

(up to a unimodular constant) and in that case

m=m_{p}^{\theta_{1}}=\frac{\sqrt{1-|p|^{2}}}{1-p\theta_{1}}

(4.7)

(up to a unimodular constant).

In particular, we have that Hayashi’s representation of a model space
K_{\theta}
is

K_{\theta}=\frac{1-\overline{\theta(0)}\theta}{\sqrt{1-|\theta(0)|^{2}}}K_{\theta_{H}}=\frac{k_{0}^{\theta}}{\sqrt{1-|\theta(0)|^{2}}}=K_{z\frac{\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{0}}{k^{\theta}_{0}}}

(4.8)

with

\theta_{H}=\frac{\theta-\theta(0)}{1-\overline{\theta(0)}\theta}=z\frac{\tilde{k}^{\theta}_{0}}{k^{\theta}_{0}}\ .

(4.9)

The question of obtaining a representation of the form (

) for a Toeplitz kernel is closely related with that of determining maximal functions in
\ker T_{G}
. Indeed, by Theorem

, there is a strong connection between multipliers from one Toeplitz kernel onto another and maximal functions in those kernels.

Multipliers from a model space onto a Toeplitz kernel are often determined by, or equal to, the outer factor of a maximal function in that kernel.
For instance, if
w
is a multiplier from a model space
K_{\theta}
onto
\ker T_{g}
, i.e.,
\ker T_{g}=wK_{\theta}
, and
\theta(0)=0
(as in (

is a maximal function in
K_{\theta}

we have that

is a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}
, i.e., the multiplier

is the outer factor of
f^{M}
.
The following result gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the outer factor of a maximal function in a Toeplitz kernel to be a multiplier from a model space onto that kernel.

Proposition 4.6

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
f^{M}
be a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}

, with

O\in\mathcal{C}(K_{\theta})\quad,\quad O^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}(\ker T_{g}).

(4.10)

Proof.

If

and (

is a maximal function in
K_{zI}
and
OI
is a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}

\ker T_{g}=OK_{zI}
.
Conversely, if
\ker T_{g}=OK_{\theta}
, then (
4.10
) holds and

must be a maximal function in
K_{\theta}
, so
K_{\theta}=K_{zI}
.
∎

From Proposition
4.6
and Corollary
4.4
we have the following.

Corollary 4.7

(
[
3
, Corollary 3.17]
)
.

Let
f^{M}=IO
be an inner-outer factorisation of a maximal function in
\ker T_{g}
, with

.
Then
\ker T_{g}=OK_{zI}
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
f^{M}=IO
be an inner-outer factorisation of a maximal function in
\ker T_{G}
, where

satisfies (

).
Then

\ker T_{Gz}=OK_{I}.

(4.11)

Using the relation

z=B_{\lambda}\frac{1-\overline{\lambda}z}{1-\lambda\overline{z}}\quad,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{D},

(4.12)

we also have:

Corollary 4.9
.

With the same assumptions as in Corollary
4.8
, we have that, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
,

\ker T_{G}=(1-\overline{\lambda}z)OK_{B_{\lambda}I}\ ,

(4.13)

and

\ker T_{GB_{\lambda}}=O(1-\overline{\lambda}z)K_{I}\ .

(4.14)

Comparing (

) and (

By Corollary

, if

(4.15)

for

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}\alpha}

This is closely related with determining maximal functions in

5

By Proposition

, Toeplitz kernels contained in some model space
K_{\theta}
take the form
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}\alpha}
, for some inner function
\alpha
.
We focus here, as in Section
3

is a finite Blaschke product, i.e., on model space representations of Toeplitz kernels of the form
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B}
where

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
and let
\theta
be an inner function with
\dim K_{\theta}>1
.
Then, for any
\mu\in\mathbb{D}

(i)

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}}=(1-\overline{\lambda}z)k_{\mu}^{\theta}\,K_{\theta_{\mu}}
, with
\theta_{\mu}=\frac{\tilde{k}_{\mu}^{\theta}}{k_{\mu}^{\theta}}
;

(ii)

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}}=m_{\lambda,\theta}\,K_{\theta_{\lambda}}
, with
\theta_{\lambda}=\frac{\tilde{k}_{\lambda}^{\theta}}{k_{\lambda}^{\theta}}
, where

m_{\lambda,\theta}=\frac{(1-\overline{\lambda}z)k_{\lambda}^{\theta}}{\sqrt{1-|\theta(\lambda)|^{2}}}

(5.1)

is an isometric multiplier from
K_{\theta_{\lambda}}
onto
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}}
;

(iii)

Hayashi’s representation of
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}}
is given by

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}}=m_{\lambda,\theta}\frac{k_{0}^{\theta_{\lambda}}}{\sqrt{1-|\theta_{\lambda}(0)|^{2}}}\ K_{z(\theta_{\lambda})_{{}_{0}}}\ .

(5.2)

Proof.

(i) From (
3.13
), for any
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{D}
we have that

\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}=\overline{\left(\frac{\tilde{k}_{\mu}^{\theta}}{k_{\mu}^{\theta}}\right)}\frac{\overline{k_{\mu}^{\theta}}(1-\lambda\overline{z})}{k_{\mu}^{\theta}(1-\overline{\lambda}z)}

(5.3)

where

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}}=k_{\mu}^{\theta}(1-\overline{\lambda}z)K_{\theta_{\mu}}\ .

(5.4)

(ii) For
\mu=\lambda
we have that
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}}=k_{\lambda}^{\theta}(1-\overline{\lambda}z)K_{\theta_{\lambda}}
.
Since, by Theorem
4.5

m_{\lambda,\theta}
is an isometric multiplier from

onto
K_{\theta}
and

, we have that
m_{\lambda,\theta}
is an isometric multiplier from
K_{\theta_{\lambda}}
onto
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda}}
.

(iii) follows from (

).
∎

If

, then

to

.
We thus get that, for any

\displaystyle\ker T_{\overline{\theta}B_{\lambda_{1}}B_{\lambda_{2}}}

\displaystyle=(1-\overline{\lambda_{1}}z)k_{\mu_{1}}^{\theta}\cdot\ker T_{\overline{\theta}_{\mu_{1}}B_{\lambda_{2}}}

\displaystyle=(1-\overline{\lambda_{1}}z)(1-\overline{\lambda_{2}}z)k_{\mu_{1}}^{\theta}k_{\mu_{2}}^{\theta_{\mu_{1}}}\cdot K_{(\theta_{\mu_{1}})_{\mu_{2}}}

(5.5)

where

(\theta_{\mu_{1}})_{\mu_{2}}=\frac{\tilde{k}_{\mu_{2}}^{\theta_{\mu_{1}}}}{k_{\mu_{2}}^{\theta_{\mu_{1}}}}\ .

(5.6)

By repeating this reasoning and choosing

,

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}\in\mathbb{D}
,

Let moreover

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle=\theta\qquad\ ,\qquad I_{n}=\frac{\tilde{k}_{\lambda_{n}}^{I_{n-1}}}{k_{\lambda_{n}}^{I_{n-1}}}\ ,

(5.7)

\displaystyle O_{n}

\displaystyle=(1-\overline{\lambda}_{n}z)k_{\lambda_{n}}^{I_{n-1}}=1-\overline{I_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})}I_{n-1}\ ,

(5.8)

for

(iii)

(5.10)

The simplicity and generality of the results presented here highlight the importance of considering maximal functions and appropriate factorisations of inner factors in our approach.

6
Toeplitz kernels with generalised Frostman shift symbols

We now apply the previous results to study a class of Toeplitz kernels that may be seen as a “perturbation” of a model space, of the form

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-h}\quad,\quad h\in H^{\infty},\ ||h||_{\infty}<1.

(6.1)

Factorising the symbol as

\overline{\theta}-h=\overline{\theta}(1-h\theta)

(6.2)

where
1-h\theta\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, we see that
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-h}
can be represented in the form

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-h}=\frac{1}{1-h\theta}K_{\theta}\ .

(6.3)

One easily sees from (
6.3
) that the following properties hold.

Proposition 6.1
.

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-h}\neq\{0\}
if and only if
\theta
is non-constant;

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-h}
is finite dimensional if and only if
\theta
is a finite Blaschke product.

Proposition 6.2
.

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-h}=\ker T_{\overline{\theta_{h}}}
, where

\theta_{h}=\frac{\theta-\overline{h}}{1-h\theta}

(6.4)

is a unimodular symbol.

The function
\theta_{h}
is called a
generalised Frostman shift of
\theta
(
[
1
]
).
Recall that, for any
p\in\mathbb{C}
,
|p|<1
, one can associate to
\theta
another inner function,
\theta_{p}
, defined by

\theta_{p}=\frac{\theta-\overline{p}}{1-p\theta}\ ,

(6.5)

called a
Frostman shift of
\theta
.
Thus Toeplitz kernels of the form (
6.1
) can also be seen as a generalisation of model spaces defined by Frostman shifts.
Those Toeplitz kernels are model spaces if and only if
h
is a constant,
h=p\in\mathbb{C}
,
|p|<1
.
In that case, by Theorem
4.5
,
K_{\theta}
can be isometrically multiplied onto
K_{\theta_{p}}
:

\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-p}=\ker T_{\overline{\theta_{p}}}=K_{\theta_{p}}=\frac{\sqrt{1-|p|^{2}}}{1-p\theta}K_{\theta}

(6.6)

where the isometric multiplier

m_{p}^{\theta}=\frac{\sqrt{1-|p|^{2}}}{1-p\theta}

(6.7)

is a constant multiple of the multiplier in (
6.3
) for
h=p
.
A natural question then is to characterise the class of functions
h\in H^{\infty}
, with
||h||_{\infty}<1
, such that a constant multiple of that multiplier, i.e.,
\frac{C}{1-h\theta}
with
C\in\mathbb{C}
, is an isometric multiplier from
K_{\theta}
onto
\ker T_{\overline{\theta_{h}}}
.

By
[
1
, Proposition 5.8]
, this happens if and only if the truncated Toeplitz operator

A^{\theta}_{1-\frac{|C|^{2}}{|1-h\theta|^{2}}}=P_{\theta}\left(1-\frac{|C|^{2}}{|1-h\theta|^{2}}\right)P_{\theta}\quad:K_{\theta}\to K_{\theta}

(6.8)

is the zero operator on
K_{\theta}
.
This, in its turn, is equivalent to

1-\frac{|C|^{2}}{|1-h\theta|^{2}}\in\overline{\theta H^{2}_{+}}+\theta H^{2}_{+}

(6.9)

(
[
16
]
).
Now we can write

1-\frac{|C|^{2}}{|1-h\theta|^{2}}=\frac{1-|C|^{2}-|h|^{2}}{|1-h\theta|^{2}}+\underbrace{\frac{\theta h}{1-h\theta}}_{\smash[b]{\scriptstyle\in\theta H^{2}_{+}}}+\underbrace{\frac{\overline{\theta}\overline{h}}{1-\overline{h}\overline{\theta}}}_{\smash[b]{\scriptstyle\in\overline{\theta}\overline{H^{2}_{+}}}}\ ,

(6.10)

so (
6.9
) is reduced to imposing that

\frac{1-|C|^{2}-|h|^{2}}{|1-h\theta|^{2}}\in\overline{\theta H^{2}_{+}}+\theta H^{2}_{+}\ .

(6.11)

We have thus the following necessary and sufficient condition.

Proposition 6.3

(
[
1
]
)
.

The multiplier
\frac{C}{1-h\theta}
, where
C\in\mathbb{C}
, from
K_{\theta}
onto
\ker T_{\overline{\theta_{h}}}
is an isometric multiplier if and only if (
6.11
) holds.

We thus get an isometric model space representation for
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-p\alpha}
, with
p\in\mathbb{C}
,
|p|<1
and
\alpha
inner.

Corollary 6.4
.

If
h=p\alpha
where
p\in\mathbb{C}
,
|p|<1
and
\alpha
is an inner function, then
m_{p}^{\alpha\theta}=\frac{\sqrt{1-|p|^{2}}}{1-p\alpha\theta}
is an isometric multiplier from
K_{\theta}
onto
\ker T_{\overline{\theta}-p\alpha}=\ker T_{\overline{\theta_{p\alpha}}}
.

Proof.

(
6.11
) obviously holds if
1-|C|^{2}-|h|^{2}=0
, which is equivalent to
|C|<1
and
|h|/\sqrt{1-|C|^{2}}=1
.
This means that
h/\sqrt{1-|C|^{2}}=\alpha
is an inner function, i.e.,
h=p\alpha
with
|p|=\sqrt{1-|C|^{2}}<1
.
∎

4.5

.

Proof.

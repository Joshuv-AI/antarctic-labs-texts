A preoperative planning procedure of septal myectomy for hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy using image-based computational fluid dynamics simulations and shape optimization
Authors: Zhihao Ding, Qianwen Liu, Huan Luo, Ming Yang, Yining Zhang, Shilin Wang, Yuanming Luo, Shu Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough septal myectomy is the preferred treatment for medication-refractory hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM), the procedure remains subjective. A preoperative planning procedure is proposed using computational fluid dynamics simulations and shape optimization to assist in the objective assessment of the adequacy of the resection. 3 patients with HOCM were chosen for the application of the proposed procedure. The geometries of the preoperative left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) of patients in the systolic phase were reconstructed from medical images. Computaional fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations were performed to assess hemodynamics within LVOT. Sensitivity analysis was performed to determine the resection extent on the septal wall, and the depth of the resection was optimized to relieve LVOT obstruction while minimizing damage to the septum. The optimized resection was then transferred from systole to diastole to provide surgeons with instructive guidance for septal myectomy. Comparison between preoperative and postoperative hemodynamics showed an evident improvement with respect to the pressure gradient throughout the LVOT. The resected myocardium in the diastolic phase is more extended and thinner than its state in the systolic phase. The proposed preoperative planning procedure may be a viable addition to the current preoperative assessment of patients with HOCM.Keywords: Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, Septal myectomy, Computational fluid dynamics, Shape sensitivity analysis, Parametric optimizationSubject terms: Biomedical engineering, Interventional cardiology

Introduction

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is a disease characterized by thickening (hypertrophy) of the heart muscle. Heart muscle cells expand more than they should and scarring often develops between cells. In hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM), the pathological basis involves abnormal thickening of myocardial tissue, particularly in the interventricular septum (IVS) region1–3, leading to obstruction of the left ventricle outflow tract (LVOT)1,4, which in turn triggers a series of severe symptoms such as chest pain, dyspnea, and syncope5–7.

Septal myectomy remains the preferred treatment for symptomatic LVOT obstruction refractory to medical treatment1,8,9. During the septal myectomy operation, meticulous and accurate preoperative planning is of paramount importance1,3,10–17. Traditional preoperative evaluations have relied on bimanual examination5 and two-dimensional transthoracic echocardiography (TTE)1,6, but modern medicine has introduced three-dimensional imaging technologies, including cardiovascular magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) and 3D printed models4,12–14,16 that significantly improve surgical precision and patient outcomes. Specifically, physicians use CMR to obtain 3D heart image information and combine it with computer-aided design and virtual surgery simulation4,14,16, allowing them to design the extent of septum resection targeted at the site of obstruction of the LVOT before actual surgery. Relevant research also underscores the importance of personalized treatment, particularly in identifying and managing factors that affect the prognosis, such as microcirculatory dysfunction of the coronary arteries, myocardial fibrosis, and atrial fibrillation18. Septal myectomy involves the targeted removal of hypertrophic septal tissue to alleviate LVOT obstruction, leading to improved outcomes. However, the success of this surgery is highly dependent on the appropriate size of the resection area. An overly extensive resection may lead to ventricular septal defect whereas an insufficient resection may fail to relive obstruction effectively, which requires significant clinical experience and surgical expertise of the surgeon19,20. Although preoperative planning based on imaging and 3D print techniques is gradually being applied, intraoperative echocardiography continues to be used to plan the site and extent of myectomy and to ensure adequate control of the LVOT gradient despite its crucial limitation in continuous monitoring21,22. Therefore, a practical and instructive preoperative planning procedure is of great interest to the operation.

Besides the in-vitro analysis23, the in-silico analysis based on computational fluid dynamics (CFD) has offered a promising approach to a quantitative evaluation of hemodynamics inside human vessels and has the potential to provide surgeons with objective decision support tools for the preoperative planning of cardiovascular operations like thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR))24,25, coronary artery bypass graft surgery26. Attemps have also been made to investigate the hemodynamic characteristics inside the left ventricle where the flow pattern is highly correlated with the aortic or mitral stenosis27,28 and LVOT obstruction29–31. Furthermore, the integration of CFD in preoperative planning improves the probability of establishing a noninvasive methodology for the assessment of hemodynamic characteristics within the left ventricle (LV).

In this context, a septal myectomy preoperative planning procedure based on CFD simulations and optimization tools was proposed and will be discussed in this study. CFD simulations are used to quantitatively characterize flow patterns within LVOT, and optimization tools are coupled with CFD simulations to optimize septal myocardial resection. A comparative study between transient and steady simulations was first conducted to quantify the errors caused by the simplications made in the steady simulation approach. The preoperative planning procedure using steady simulations and optimization tools is then described with an examplary application to selected patient data. This study underscores the potential of the proposed preoperative planning procedure to enable a better understanding of LV hemodynamics and guide surgical decision-making and design of the septal myectomy.

Computational details and boundary conditions

Numerical modeling

The open source CFD solver OpenFOAM v18.1232 was chosen to perform patient-specific steady CFD simulations of the LVOT. The commercial CFD solver Ansys Fluent 20.0 (Ansys Inc., PA, United States) was used to carry out patient-specific transient CFD simulations of the LVOT due to its ability to handld complex dynamic mesh deformation as required by transient simulations of the LVOT. Both solvers use a finite-volume-based methodology to solve the Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) equations of incompressible flow. Blood was assumed to be an incompressible Newtonian fluid with density and viscosity 33. The flow in the LV is closely related to the presence of turbulence, especially in the obstruction region, where the local flow can be extremely complex and of important velocity magnitude. Therefore, a suitable turbulence model is crucial for the correct prediction of LV hemodynamics. The standard turbulence model was previously found to outperform other turbulence models in terms of the accuracy of the LV CFD simulations34, and was therefore retained in the current work.

Steady simulations of LV hemodynamics were performed using simpleFoam35 solver of OpenFoam that iteratively solve Eqs. (3) and (4). Spatial discretization was achieved using a second-order scheme (Gauss linear), and the temporal term in Eq. (4) was treated as an under-relaxation term using a pseudo-transient approach to help stabilize the numerical solution. Steady simulations were considered converged once the normalised residual reached for both velocity and pressure, which is necessary for the application of the adjoint solver used in the optimization procedure.

Transient simulations were carried out using PISO solver36 of Fluent. Spatial and temporal discretisation was achieved using a second-order upwind scheme and a second-order implicit backward Euler scheme, respectively. A time-step of was chosen29 to ensure a CFL number less then unity. Simulations of each time step were stopped if they converged to a normalised residual reached for both velocity and pressure.

In this study, the end-systolic phase, during which the most severe obstruction occurs, was chosen to characterize hemodynamics in the LVOT of patients with HOCM. The initial patient models were modified to include only the LV and part of the ascending aorta, as shown in Fig. 2.

The apex of the LV was clipped and the resulting edge loop was extruded along its normal direction for 40 mm and then patched to generate the inlet boundary. Similarly, the ascending was cut in the downstream direction of flow through the aortic valve, and the remaining opening was also patched and extruded for 100 mm along its normal direction to form the outlet boundary. Such an extrusion for the inlet and outlet aims to stabilize the flow development after entering the LV and before exiting the ascending aorta, respectively. The final computational domain is enclosed by the input, outlet, and wall of LVOT. The presence of leaflets of the mitral and aortic valves was also considered in the modeling work.

Preoperative planning of septal myectomy based on shape optimization techniques

Septal myectomy corresponds to the procedure to remove a part of the thickened and overgrown septum to relieve LVOT obstruction. The procedure remains subjective14 and needs further objective guidance. In this work, a preoperative planning of septal myectomy is proposed and can be divided into three steps: identification of the resection extent, optimization of the depth of resection, and conversion of the optimal resection choice from systole to diastole:

Identification of the resection extent using shape sensitivity analysis

The choice of the resection extent on the septal wall is a critical step to obtain the optimal septum resection. Resection should be performed in the septum region that has the greatest impact on the pressure gradient across the aortic valve. The importance of different parts of the septal wall in LVOT obstruction can be quantified using the adjoint method40.

Shape sensitivity analysis was performed using DAFoam41,42, in which an efficient discrete adjoint method was implemented and coupled with the OpenFOAM solver. The important shape sensitivity values are located mainly in the region where the relative pressure suffers a sudden change. The significant change in corresponds to severe obstruction in the same region where the septal wall and the mitral valve are in close contact. Therefore, shape sensitivity is helpful to identify the region where the septal wall shape is the most the pressure gradient and where resection should be performed. In the current methodology, a threshold of 14 was recommanded and regions of shape sensitivity larger than the chosen threshold should be included in the resection extent as much as possible. The selection of such a region should also consider surgical feasibility as the degree of freedom with respect to possible changes in the direction and thickness of resection is highly limited and progressively dinimishing towards the apex43, which can hardly be considered in the selection of resection extent in an objective mannar. Therefore, subjective decisions made by experienced surgeons are still necessary in this step, and quantitative elements from shape sensitivity analysis can be supportive in the decision making process. Mapping of the optimal resection choice from systole to diastole

The design of the resection shape through the first two steps is based on the LVOT model in the end-systolic phase. However, during septal myectomy, the patient is attached to a heart-lung machine that takes over the work of the patient’s heart and lungs. Therefore, the heart stops beating and is close to its diastolic phase during surgery. The last step of the proposed preoperative planning procedure aims to transfer the optimal resection shape from its state in the end systolic phase to that in the diastolic phase so that preoperative planning can provide surgeons with insight.

The conception of this crucial step was inspired by previous studies on myocardial motion and myocardial strain analysis. Radial basis functions (RBFs) play an important role in predicting and analyzing myocardial motion, with widespread application in the medical imaging field to capture three-dimensional dynamic changes in heart tissue. RBFs are used to construct interpolation models that consolidate data from 2D and 3D echocardiograms, as well as cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) images, generating dense three-dimensional displacement fields within the LV44–47. Combined with biomechanical constraints, local myocardial strain calculations allow assessing myocardial function and pathological conditions such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy45,48.

Radial basis functions define an interpolation function as a linear combination of radially symmetric basis functions. The unknown coefficients of the basis functions are determined by solving a linear system of equations. The center points for RBFs are not required to lie on a regular grid and can be placed anywhere within the domain of interest, which is advantageous when modeling complex geometries like LV.

Results

Comparison of results between transient and steady simulations

The proposed preoperative planning procedure relies on steady simulations of LVOT, while the rigid wall condition and steady flow assumption significantly simplify the flow complexity and bring errors compared to real transient simulations in which the transient effect and the wall deformation are both considered. In this section, a comparison of results between the transient simulation and the steady simulation of the LVOT model of Patient 0 will be presented. The LVOT model at was selected for steady simulation while the transient simulation lasts from 0 to 0.36 s. The comparison was thus made between results of the transient simulation at and those of the steady simulation on the LVOT model at the same time.

The steady simulation follows almost the numerical method previously described except that the mitral valve was modeled using the implicit approach as in the transient simulation, so that the differences between the two simulations are only caused by the simplications made on temporal effect and left ventricle wall motion. The inlet and outlet boundaries were generated as illustrated in Fig. 2. The mass flow rate imposed at the inlet is the same as that exits the outlet in the transient simulation, which equals to 0.077 kg/s. The outlet pressure was adjusted to the same value as that imposed in the transient simulation at to facilitate comparison.

Application to patient-specific cases

The septal myectomy planning procedure was applied to the three chosen patients with HOCM. CFD simulations were performed on LVOT models with modifications to the septal wall. The pressure gradient was then evaluated and used as the objective function J for the sensitivity analysis followed. The shape sensitivity was then extracted by solving Eqs. (8) and (9) with the coordinates of the computational domain boundaries.

Discussion

In this work, a preoperative planning procedure of septal myectomy for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy based on numerical simulations and shape optimization tools was presented. The newly devised procedure was applied to three patient-specific cases to demonstrate its capacity and practicality. The comparison between preoperative and postoperative hemodynamic behaviors (after virtual operation) highlighted the effectiveness of optimized septum resection obtained throughout the proposed preoperative planning procedure. Since septal myectomy is performed without heart beating, the target septum resection must be given in its diastolic state. Therefore, a mapping operation was finally introduced to transfer the optimal septum resection choice from its form in the systolic phase to that in the diastolic phase.

Comparison between transient and steady simulations

The proposed preoperative planning procedure uses steady simulations to characterize hemodynamics inside the LVOT, which indeed introduces errors in the simulation results due to the simplications in the numerical modeling. Therefore, a comparison has been made on the results obtained from the two types of simulation on the LVOT model of patient 0 to characterize the differences between them.

A qualitative comparison was made on the distribution of the flow field, pressure, and WSS. A major diffrence lies in the flow pattern in the mid-ventricular part and near the apex, which is due to differences on how the flow is generated. In the transient case, the flow is generated by the LV contraction as shown by the streamlines originating from the LV wall, as plotted in Fig. 13a and b; in the steady case, as the flow enters the LV from the extruded inlet, large-scale vortices can be seen near the apex due to the local flow separation. However, it can also be noticed that the flow pattern is quite similar around the obstruction region. In both cases, the flow acceleration was seen due to the local restriction of the narrow passage between the LV and the aortic root, and the corresponding large-scale vortex in the aortic root was also presented simultaneously. The pressure and WSS distributions on the septal wall exhibited similar tendencies.

A quantitative comparison was conducted on a line on the septal wall. The pressure and WSS evolutions along the line were compared. Although the values of pressure and WSS are higher in the transient cases, the global evolution trend was found to be almost the same: the locations of minimal pressure and maximum WSS were the same in both cases, as well as the region where the pressure gradient decreases the most rapidly.

The two comparisons suggested that, despite the natural differences between transient and steady simulations, steady simulations can correctly capture the major hemodynamic features presented in LVOT obstruction of HOCM patients. The major differences resided rather in the mid-ventricular part and the apex of the LV, and the flow pattern was highly similar around the obstruction region, which allowed us to employ the simplified steady simulation approach as the simulation tool in the preoperative planning procedure. Steady CFD simulations of the LVOT can offer a representative flow behavior in the LVOT while maintaining a much lower computational cost compared to transient CFD simulations. Our observations also coincide with previous work38 on the comparison of steady and transient CFD simulations of LVOT.

Preoperative planning procedure

The optimization of septum resection is based on the parametric shape design of the resected myocardium, which is divided into two steps:

The first step relies on the shape sensitivity analysis using adjoint solver. computed by steady CFD simulation of the modified LVOT model is chosen as the objective function, and the corresponding shape sensitivity highlights regions on the septal wall that are the most crucial to LVOT obstruction. Regions of significant shape sensitivity coincide with places where pressure experiences a brutal change due to the local restriction of flow passage. The shape sensitivity thus offers a quantitative metric that helps identify and select the resection extend based on which the septal myectomy should be performed to alleviate LVOT obstruction.

The second step focuses on the parametric design of the myocardium to be resected based on the resection extent determined in the first step. The septum resection is modeled as times of cuts that correspond to times of surface deformation of the selected resection extent. The number of cuts is the only design parameter and controls the resection depth. The other parameter n is determined separately with such that the resection conserves the smoothness between the resection extent and its surroundings. Such a choice reduces the dimension of input variables for the optimization problem described in Eqs. (14) and (15), and therefore minimizes the cost of the optimization process.

Subjective decisions were involved in the optimization process. The selection of resection extent involved subjective decision due to the fact that the surgical feasibility was difficult to quantify and objectively considered in determining the extent of the resection. In the current methodology, the combination of subjective decisions by experienced surgeons and quantitative support from shape sensitivity analysis was preferred. Furthermore, the parameter n in Eq. (10) that controls the continuity between the resected myocardium and its surroundings is also subjectively chosen. A promising solution is to convert the selected resection extent plane into the NURBS surface60, and the parametric deformation defined by can be improved by FFD, ensuring smoothness between the deformed resection extent surface and its surrounding area. However, the number of control points in FFD is proportional to the degree of freedom of the deformation and thus is directly linked to the order of complexity of the optimization process. In contrast, the current deformation method that relies only on one parameter guarantees the effectiveness of the overall preoperative planning procedure.

The maximum value of velocity decreased by about 41.2% for patient 1, 27.7% for patient 2 and 47.8% for patient 3 after virtual resection, and the relative reduction of the maximum value of WSS is around 70.3% for patient 1, 75.0% for patient 2 and 82.0%. A larger resection extent is eventually needed to cover a more extended part of the septum, and even the apical region61 and the mitral papillary muscles or chordae62 if necessary. The advantage of the proposed preoperative planning is that surgeons can make mistakes and improve the surgical strategy virtually, instead of during surgery. If the selected resection extent is found too small, surgeons can redo only the shape optimization part to obtain the satisfactory result. The numerical prediction of a septal resection designed with a selected , including geometro deformation, meshing, and simulation, costs around 20 min in the current work. In addition, prediction for different choices of can be performed simultaneously. Thus, the return time for the realization of shape optimization is accepetable, and surgeons are able to repeat the optimization process by adjusting the resection extent and other involved parameters in a reseasonable time.

Once optimized septum resection is determined in the systolic phase, its shape in the diastolic phase is estimated using a mapping operation based on the RBF method. The motion of the endocardial and epicardial wall obtained by imaging techniques is extracted as input for the construction of the mapping operation that allows the estimation of the resected myocardium motion from systole to diastole. The resected myocardium obtained in the diastolic phase is finally retained as the result of the general preoperative planning procedure.

Limitations and future research

Although steady simulations were found capable of reproducing the flow behavior in the LVOT with obstruction, confirmation of optimal resection using transient simulations could be complementary to enhance the realibility of the preoperative planning procedure. In addition, the adjoint solver used for sensitivity analysis is mainly compatible with steady simulations, and the development of the unsteady adjoint solver is still ongoing during recent years63,64. Therefore, steady CFD simulations and the adjoint solver were coupled and integrated in the preoperative planning procedure proposed in this work. The coupling of transient CFD simulations of LVOT and transient adjoint solver is also of great interest and will be further developed and compared with the current methodology.

Another limitation is associated with the mapping operation that constitutes an important step in the preoperative planning procedure. The RBF method using thin plate splines as basis functions was used to predict the resected myocardium motion from systole to diastole. However, the result of intramyocardial motion prediction requires further validation against displacement data obtained from other techniques:

The displacement encoding with stimulated echoes (DENSE)65,66 is an MRI technique developed to encode phase related to myocardial tissue displacements, and displacement information is applied directly to detect intramyocardial motion during the cardiac cycle.

The preoperative planning procedure proposed in this work was dedicated to the classic septal myectomy. Some recently proposed HOCM interventional treatments can also benefit from this procedure, especially the sensitivity analysis. The Liwen procedure69 is a type of septal ablation operation that uses stepless adjustable length needle electrodes for percutaneous intramyocardial septal radiofrequency ablation. Shape sensitivity analysis can offer a quantitative descriptor that can potentially guide needle placement. A novel transapical beating heart septal myectomy procedure (TA-BSM)70 was also invented for septal myectomy during the systolic phase, which is highly compatible with the preoperative planning procedure presented in this work.

Conclusions

In general, a preoperative planning procedure has been proposed for septal myectomy for hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy. The proposed methodology combines CTA imaging, 3D model reconstruction, computational fluid dynamics, and shape optimization techniques to evaluate the hemodynamic characteristics of LVOT in patients with HOCM and provide surgeons with an optimized choice of myocardium resection, which promises to improve the diagnosis and treatment of HOCM. Further research should evaluate the clinical effectiveness of the preoperative planning procedure so that the overall procedure can offer practically insightful and instructive guidance for the planning of the septal myectomy.

Borel 1 type mappings and the respective equi-families
Marek Balcerzak, Ľubica Holá, Olena Karlova, Piotr Szuca

Abstract
. We investigate classes of functions from a topological space to a metric space that are related to those of Borel class 1. Following the idea defining an equi-Baire 1 family (due to Lecomte) we define the respective equi-families of functions from the considered classes. We observe that studying of equi-families can be reduced to the exploration of a single orbit map with values in a product space. We consider the closure of equi-families with respect to the topology of pointwise convergence. Finally, we investigate functions f : X × Y → Z f\colon X\times Y\to Z , for metric spaces X , Y , Z X,Y,Z , with sections that are equi-continuous, equi-Baire 1 or equi-Borel measurable of class α \alpha . In particular, we generalize a result of Grande.

1.
Introduction

Let
f
be a function between two metric spaces
X
and
Y
. We say that
f
is
Baire 1
if
f
is the limit of a pointwise convergent sequence of continuous functions
f_{n}\colon X\to Y
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that
f
is
Borel 1
if it is
F_{\sigma}
-measurable, that is, its preimage of every open set is
F_{\sigma}
. Note that every Baire 1 function is Borel 1, but the converse does not hold in general; see
[
Kec95
, 24B]
.
However, if
Y=\mathbb{R}
or
Y
is a Banach space, then the Baire 1 functions and the Borel 1 functions coincide; see
[
Kec95
]
and also
[
Fos93
]
and
[
Spu10
, Remark 5.6]
for
more general results. Note that several authors (see, e.g.
[
Kec95
]
,
[
Lec03
]
,
[
LTZ01
]
) use the name Baire 1 for
F_{\sigma}
-measurable functions which can sometimes lead to confusion.

Many nice characterizations of Baire 1 (or Borel 1) functions are known. Let us mention some of them. The classical theorem of Baire states that, if
X
is complete and
Y
is separable, then
f
is Borel 1 if and only if

f\upharpoonright P
has a point of continuity for every non-empty closed set
P\subseteq X
(see
[
Kur66
]
); for some generalizations, see
[
Kou93
]
). This last condition is called the

Point of Continuity Property
(PCP).

Lee, Tang, and Zhao
[
LTZ01
]
obtained an interesting

\varepsilon
-
\delta
characterization of Borel 1 functions between two Polish spaces.
The respective condition characterizing Borel 1 functions was called
LTZ-property
in
[
BHH25
]
.
The result of
[
LTZ01
]
was later generalized in
[
FC14
]
to the case when
X
and
Y
are separable spaces. The authors of
[
FC14
]
used an old Lebesgue theorem
[
Leb05
]
in their proof. Although the Lebesgue result was concerned with the case
X=Y=\mathbb{R}
, it remains true if
X
is arbitrary and
Y
is separable
[
BHH25
, Thm 2.1]
. The respective condition stated in Lebesgue’s
result was referred to in
[
BHH25
]
as the
Lebesgue property
.

Note that Baire 1 real-valued functions and their regular subclasses have important applications in classical theory of real functions (see
[
Bru94
]
) and functional analysis
[
HOR91
]
. Independently, some other interesting classes of maps related to Baire 1 functions have been studied, also in a general setting where
X
is a topological space. Namely, Koumoullis
[
Kou93
]
considered fragmentable maps and functions with PCP. Bouziad
[
Bou12
]
investigated weakly separated maps, which coincide with functions with LTZ-property if
X
is a metric space. He also used cliquish and fragmentable maps, as well as those having PCP.

In Section
2

we collect the above-mentioned classes of maps. We introduce a new notion — the
generalized
Lebesgue property
. We establish inclusions between these families. First, this is done when
X
is a topological space. Some of the inclusions can be reversed under additional assumptions in
X
, while others are appropriate, as shown by examples. Second, we consider the case where both spaces
X
and
Y
are metric spaces.

Our work is, in some sense, a continuation of
[
BKS22
]
and
[
BHH25
]
, which were inspired by the intriguing notion of
equi-Baire 1 families
of functions, proposed independently by Lecomte
[
Lec03
]
and Alikhani-Koopeai
[
AK20
]
. This notion plays a role in dynamical systems similar to that of equi-continuous families (see
[
AK22
]
). We observed that other types of equi-families of functions can be of interest
(see
[
GM06
]
,
[
Gra83
]
and
[
BHH25
]
, where equi-PCP property and equi-Lebesgue property were considered).
We collect such families in Section
3
and present a simple tool which shows that the study of equi-families can be reduced to the analysis of a single orbit map with values in the product space.
Finally, we solve two problems from
[
BKS22
]
concerning functions of two variables with sections being equi-continuous or equi-Borel measurable of class
\alpha
, and we obtain a generalization of the result of Grande
[
Gra83
]
.

2.
Properties related to being a Borel 1 map

We consider several collections of functions from a topological space
X
into a metric space
Y
that are related to Borel 1 functions and which turned out to be important when studying families of equi-Baire 1 functions.
We establish inclusions between them and show examples when inclusions are proper. If
X
is a metric space, some of these families are equal under or without additional assumptions on
X

Definition 2.1
.

Let

•

has the

•

is

for every open set

(In other words,

•

is

, if

(Borel 1)

the preimage

of any open set

-set in

.

Lemma 2.11

Let
X

If

is of the first Baire category in

be a sequence of relatively closed sets in

Define a function

We prove that

Let

Equi-families of functions

For collections of functions investigated in Section

We then present solutions of two problems from
[
BKS22
]
. Finally, we generalize the result of Grande
[
Gra83
]
showing a sufficient condition for
f\colon X\times Y\to Z
to be a Borel function of class
\alpha
, in the language of
x
-sections and
y
-sections of

3.1.
Equi-families and the orbit function

The notion of an equi-Baire 1 family introduced by
[
Lec03
]
was a starting point in the previous papers
[
BKS22
]
and
[
BHH25
]
. Let
(X,\rho)
and
(Y,d)
be metric spaces and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq Y^{X}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is called
equi-Baire 1
if

•

for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a positive function
\delta_{\varepsilon}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for all
x,y\in X
and all
f\in\mathcal{F}

\varrho(x,y)<\min\left\{\delta_{\varepsilon}(x),\delta_{\varepsilon}(y)\right\}\,\,\,\Longrightarrow\,\,\,d(f(x),f(y))<\varepsilon.

In other words, a modification of (LTZ) property holds where
\delta_{\varepsilon}
is chosen the same for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

In the same spirit, we can modify several properties considered in Section 2 for a single function
f
from a topological space
X
to a metric space
(Y,d)
, and we obtain the respective equi-properties for a family

\mathcal{F}\subseteq Y^{X}
. We mention that equi-Lebesgue
and equi-cliquish families were considered in
[
BHH25
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq Y^{X}
be a family of functions between a topological space
X
and a metric space
(Y,d)
. We say that

•

\mathcal{F}
is
equi-weakly separated
, if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a neighborhood assignment
\{V_{x}\}_{x\in X}
such that for all
x,y\in X
and all
f\in\mathcal{F}

(x,y)\in V_{y}\times V_{x}\,\,\,\Longrightarrow\,\,\,d(f(x),f(y))<\varepsilon;

•

is
equi-fragmentable
, if
for every
\varepsilon>0
and every closed set
\emptyset\neq F\subseteq X
there exists an open set
U
such that
U\cap F\neq\emptyset
and
{\rm diam}f(U\cap F)<\varepsilon
and for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
;

•

is
equi-cliquish
, if
for every
\varepsilon>0
and every open set
\emptyset\neq U\subseteq X
there exists an open set
\emptyset\neq O\subseteq U
such that
{\rm diam}f(O)<\varepsilon
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
;

•

is
equi-Lebesgue
, if
for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a cover

(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
consisting of closed sets such that
{\rm diam}f(X_{n})\leq\varepsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

and all
f\in\mathcal{F}
;

•

is
equi-GLP
, if
for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\sigma
-discrete family
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
consisting of closed subsets of
X
such that
X=\bigcup\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
and
{\rm diam}f(A)\leq\varepsilon
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
and all
f\in\mathcal{F}
;

•

has the
point of equicontinuity property
(PECP), if for every closed set
\emptyset\neq F\subseteq X
the family
\{f\restriction_{F}\colon f\in\mathcal{F}\}
has a point of equicontinuity.

Remark 3.2
.

Clearly, if
X
is a metric space in the above definition, the notions
of an equi-weakly separated family and an equi-Baire 1 family coincide.

Let
X
be a topological space, and
(Y,d)
be a bounded metric space. For a family
\mathscr{F}\subseteq Y^{X}
of functions, we denote by

f^{\sharp}_{\mathscr{F}}(x)=(f(x))_{f\in\mathscr{F}}

the
orbit function

f^{\sharp}_{\mathscr{F}}\colon X\to Y^{T}
, where
T=|\mathscr{F}|
(see e.g.
[
AG01
, Sec. 4]
).
Assume that
Z=Y^{T}
is equipped with the supremum metric

\varrho(z_{1},z_{2})=\sup\limits_{t\in T}d(z_{1}(t),z_{2}(t)).

It is easy to see that the following observation is valid.
This is an extended version of Proposition 1 from
[
Kar25
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a topological space, and
(Y,d)
be a bounded metric space. Then

(1)

\mathscr{F}
is equi-continuous at
x\in X
(on
X
) if and only if

f^{\sharp}_{\mathscr{F}}\colon X\to(Z,\varrho)
is continuous at

(on
X
);

(2)

is equi-weakly separated if and only if

f^{\sharp}_{\mathscr{F}}\colon X\to(Z,\varrho)

(3)

if

is equi-Baire 1 if and only if

(4)

is equi-fragmentable if and only if

(5)

(6)

(7)

(8)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is an equi-weakly separated family of functions from

(V_{x})_{x\in X}

Then for

we have: for every

and for every

(x,y)\in V_{y}\times V_{x}\textup{\ implies\ }d(f(x),f(y))\leq\varepsilon.

Let

3.3.
Applications to functions defined on
X\times Y

We are going to solve two problems from the earlier paper
[
BKS22
]
.

Problem 3.13
.

[
BKS22
, Prob. 3.7]

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{X\times Y}

is a family of separately equi-continuous functions defined on the product of Polish spaces
X
and
Y
,
i.e.

•

\{f(x,\cdot)\colon f\in\mathcal{F}\}
is equi-continuous for each
x\in X
, and

•

\{f(\cdot,y)\colon f\in\mathcal{F}\}
is equi-continuous for each
y\in Y
.

Is
\mathcal{F}
equi-Baire 1?

Let us give a positive solution in a general setting.
Bouziad
[
Bou12
, Proposition 4.3]
proved the following fact. If
X,Y

are topological spaces and
Z
is a metric space, then every separately continuous function
f\colon X\times Y\to Z
is weakly separated. Now, we obtain the following corollary which solves the problem.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
X,Y
be topological spaces and
Z
be a metric space. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq Z^{X\times Y}
be a family of separately equi-continuous functions defined on
X\times Y
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is equi-weakly separated.

Proof.

Consider the orbit function
f^{\sharp}_{\mathscr{F}}\colon X\times Y\to Z^{T}
(where
T:=|\mathscr{F}|
) as in Proposition
3.3
. Note that it is separately continuous. By the Bouziad result,
f^{\sharp}_{\mathscr{F}}

is weakly separated and we infer that
{\mathscr{F}}
is equi-weakly separated by Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Let us state the next problem
[
BKS22
, Prob. 3.11]
in a strictly formal version as follows.

Problem 3.15
.

Suppose that
X
and
Y
are Polish and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

.
Moreover, suppose that:

•

the family
\{f_{n}(x,\cdot)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equi-Baire 1 for each
x\in X
, and

•

the family
\{f_{n}(\cdot,y)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equi-Baire 1 for each

.

Does there exist a comeager
G_{\delta}
set
A\subseteq X
such that
\{f_{n}|_{A\times Y}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is jointly equi-Baire 1?

Remark 3.16
.

Using Proposition
3.3
(6) for the families:

•

\mathcal{F}:=\{f_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
,

•

\mathcal{F}^{1}_{x}:=\{f_{n}(x,\cdot)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
for each
x\in X
,

•

\mathcal{F}^{2}_{y}:=\{f_{n}(\cdot,y)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
for each

,

we can see that the above problem is equivalent to the question whether for each separately Baire 1
function
f^{\#}\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
there exists
a comeager
G_{\delta}
set
A\subseteq X
such that
f^{\#}|_{A\times Y}
is jointly Baire 1.

Let us show a negative solution of Problem
3.15
. Grande noticed that there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
that has all
x
-sections and all

-sections of class Baire 1 but

is not

[

.
One can use this fact to obtain the respective counterexample for Problem

. However, we propose a different simple construction.
Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a Bernstein nonmeasurable set. It is known that for any uncountable Borel set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
the intersection
B\cap A
is not a Borel set. Let

g\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the characteristic function of the set

Then

is separately Baire 1. Suppose

is a nonmeager

Similarly, the function

given by

is separately Baire 1
and

is not jointly Baire 1 for every nonmeager

set

.
This by Remark
3.16
leads to the negative solution of Problem

.

.

X=\bigcup\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
and
{\rm diam}f(A)\leq\varepsilon
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 3.18
.

It is easy to see that for an isolated ordinal
\alpha=\beta+1
we may assume that all elements from
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
belongs to the multiplicative class
\beta
. Hence, Definition
3.17
for
\alpha=1
is consistent with the definition of the generalized Lebesgue property.

If
\alpha
is limit, then we may assume that all elements of
\mathcal{A}
belongs to multiplicative classes less than
\alpha
. Since every set of multiplicative class less than
\alpha
in a metric space is ambiguous of class
\alpha
, one can substitute the word “additive” by “ambiguous” in Definition
3.17
.

Definition 3.19
.

Let
\mathscr{F}\subseteq Y^{X}
be a family of functions between
X
and
Y
, and
1\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}
. We say that
\mathscr{F}

is equi-
\alpha
-GLP
, if (
\alpha
-GLP) holds for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
, i.e.
for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\sigma
-discrete family
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
in
X
consisting of Borel sets of additive class
\alpha
such that
X=\bigcup\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
and
{\rm diam}f(A)\leq\varepsilon
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
and
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

If
f\colon X\times Y\to Z
, then for
x\in X
and
y\in Y
by
f^{x}
and
f_{y}
we denote
x
-section
f^{x}\colon Y\to Z
and
y
-section
f_{y}\colon X\to Z
of the function
f
, respectively.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be families of sets. We write
\mathcal{A}\prec\mathcal{B}
if for every set
A\in\mathcal{A}
there exists a set
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
A\subseteq B
.

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
X,Y
and
(Z,d)
be metric spaces,
1\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}
and let a function
f\colon X\times Y\to Z
satisfy the following properties:

(1)

the family
\mathscr{F}_{X}=\{f^{x}:x\in X\}
is equi-
\alpha
-GLP,

(2)

for every
y\in Y
the section
f_{y}\colon X\to Z
belongs to the Borel class
\alpha
.

Then
f\colon X\times Y\to Z
belongs to the Borel class
\alpha
.

Proof.

We will show that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a function
g\colon X\times Y\to Z
of Borel class
\alpha
such that

d(f(p),g(p))\leq\varepsilon
for all
p\in X\times Y
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\mathscr{F}_{X}
is equi-
\alpha
-GLP, there exists a
\sigma
-discrete family
\mathcal{A}
in
Y
consisting of sets of additive and multiplicative class
\alpha
simultaneously such that

Y=\bigcup\mathcal{A}
and
{\rm diam}\,f^{x}(A)\leq\varepsilon

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
and for all
x\in X
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\bigcup\limits_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}
, where each family
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is discrete in
Y
. We put

\mathcal{B}_{n}=(A\setminus\bigcup\limits_{k<n}\bigcup\mathcal{A}_{k}:A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}),\,\,n\in\mathbb{N},

B_{n}=\bigcup\{B:B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{n}.

Then
\mathcal{B}
is a
\sigma
-discrete family of sets of ambiguous class
\alpha
with
\mathcal{B}\prec\mathcal{A}
and
\bigcup\mathcal{B}=Y
. Moreover,
B^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset
for all
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
,
B^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
and distinct
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Notice that every set
B_{n}
belongs to the additive and multiplicative class
\alpha
simultaneously as the union of a discrete family of sets of the same classes in metric space
Y
.

Take an arbitrary
y_{B,n}\in B
for every
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
y\in Y
we put
n(y)=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:y\in B_{n}\}
. Now let
(x,y)\in X\times Y
. Since the family
\mathcal{B}_{n(y)}
is disjoint, there exists a unique set
B(y)\in\mathcal{B}_{n(y)}
such that
y\in B(y)
. We define

g(x,y)=f(x,y_{B(y),n(y)}).

Then for every point
(x,y)\in X\times Y
we have

d(f(x,y),g(x,y))=d(f(x,y),f(x,y_{B(y),n(y)}))\leq{\rm diam}\,f^{x}(B(y)),

since
y,y_{B(y),n(y)}\in B(y)
. It follows from the property
\mathcal{B}\prec\mathcal{A}
that
{\rm diam}\,f^{x}(B(y))\leq{\rm diam}\,f^{x}(A)
for some
A\in\mathcal{A}
. Hence,

d(f(x,y),g(x,y))\leq\varepsilon.

It remains to prove that
g\colon X\times Y\to Z
belongs to the Borel class
\alpha
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let

g_{n}=g\restriction_{X\times B_{n}}
. It follows from the definition of
g
and assumption (2) of the theorem, that
g_{n,B}=g_{n}\restriction_{X\times B}
belongs to the Borel class
\alpha
for every
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Then for every open set
W\subseteq Z
we have that the preimage

g_{n}^{-1}(W)=\bigcup\{g_{n,B}^{-1}(W):B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\}

is of the additive class
\alpha
in
X\times B_{n}
as a union of a discrete family of sets of the same class. Now, since
\{X\times B_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a countable covering of
X\times Y
by sets of ambiguous class
\alpha
and
g\restriction_{X\times B_{n}}
is Borel
\alpha
, we conclude that
g\colon X\times Y\to Z
belongs to the Borel class
\alpha
.

Finally, let us remember that the family of functions of Borel class
\alpha
between metric spaces is closed under uniform limits. ∎

By Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
2.4
:

\textrm{(PECP)}\Rightarrow\textrm{(equi-fragmentable)}\Rightarrow\textrm{(equi-Baire 1)}\Rightarrow\textrm{(equi-GLP)}\Rightarrow\textrm{(equi-$\alpha$-GLP)},

so the following corollary is a generalization of Grande’s result.

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be metric spaces and
f\colon X\times Y\to Z
satisfies the following properties

(1)

the family
\mathscr{F}_{X}=\{f^{x}:x\in X\}
has (PECP),

(2)

for every
y\in Y
the section
f_{y}\colon X\to Z
belongs to the Borel class
\alpha
.

Then
f\colon X\times Y\to Z
belongs to the Borel class
\alpha
.

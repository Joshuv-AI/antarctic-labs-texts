SUMMARY: The United States Copyright Office is conducting the tenth 
triennial rulemaking proceeding under the Digital Millennium Copyright 
Act (``DMCA''), concerning temporary exemptions to the DMCA's 
prohibition against circumvention of technological measures that 
control access to copyrighted works. Having carefully considered the 
petitions for new and renewal exemptions and comments received in 
response to the June 9, 2026 Notification of Inquiry, the Office 
announces the Register's intention to recommend all but one of the 
existing exemptions for renewal, and initiates three rounds of public 
comment on newly proposed exemptions. Comments submitted may not exceed 
twenty-five pages per class, excluding documentary evidence.

DATES: Initial written comments, along with any documentary or 
multimedia evidence, from proponents and other members of the public 
who support the adoption of a proposed exemption, as well as those that 
neither support nor oppose an exemption but seek to share pertinent 
information, are due December 7, 2026. Written response comments, along 
with any documentary or multimedia evidence, from those who oppose the 
adoption of a proposed exemption are due February 22, 2027. Written 
reply comments from supporters of a proposal and parties that neither 
support nor oppose a proposal are due March 22, 2027.

ADDRESSES: The Copyright Office is using the regulations.gov system for 
the submission and posting of comments in this proceeding. All comments 
are to be submitted electronically through regulations.gov. The Office 
is accepting two types of comments. First, commenters who wish briefly 
to express general support for or opposition to a proposed exemption 
may submit such comments electronically by typing into the comment 
field on regulations.gov. Second, commenters who wish to provide a 
fuller legal and evidentiary basis for their position may upload a Word 
or PDF document, but such longer submissions must be completed using 
the long comment form provided on the Office's website at https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2027. Specific instructions for submitting 
comments, including multimedia evidence that cannot be uploaded through 
regulations.gov, are available at https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2027/comment-submission/. If a commenter cannot meet a particular submission 
requirement, please contact the Office using the contact information 
below for special instructions.

I. Standard for Evaluating Proposed Exemptions

    As the NOI explained, before the Register can recommend a temporary

[[Page 67789]]

exemption from the prohibition on circumvention, the record must 
establish that ``persons who are users of a copyrighted work are, or 
are likely to be in the succeeding 3-year period, adversely affected by 
the [anti-circumvention] prohibition . . . in their ability to make 
noninfringing uses under [title 17] of a particular class of 
copyrighted works.'' \3\ When defining a ``class of copyrighted 
works,'' the Office generally uses the categories of works in 17 U.S.C. 
102 as a starting point and then refines the class by other criteria, 
such as the technological protection measures (``TPMs'') used, 
distribution platforms, and types of uses or users.\4\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \3\ 17 U.S.C. 1201(a)(1)(C).
    \4\ See U.S. Copyright Office, Section 1201 Rulemaking: Ninth 
Triennial Proceeding to Determine Exemptions to the Prohibition on 
Circumvention, Recommendation of the Register of Copyrights 13-14 
(2024) (``2024 Recommendation''), https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2024/2024_Section_1201_Registers_Recommendation.pdf; U.S. Copyright 
Office, Section 1201 of Title 17, at 26, 108-10 (2017) (``Section 
1201 Study''), https://www.copyright.gov/policy/1201/section-1201-full-report.pdf; see also H.R. Rep. No. 105-551, pt. 2, at 38 (1998) 
(``Commerce Comm. Report'') (``The Committee intends that the 
`particular class of copyrighted works' be a narrow and focused 
subset of the broad categories of works of authorship than is 
identified in Section 102 of the Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. [section] 
102).'').
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In evaluating the evidence, the Register weighs the statutory 
factors in section 1201(a)(1)(C): ``(i) the availability for use of 
copyrighted works; (ii) the availability for use of works for nonprofit 
archival, preservation, and educational purposes; (iii) the impact that 
the prohibition on the circumvention of technological measures applied 
to copyrighted works has on criticism, comment, news reporting, 
teaching, scholarship, or research; (iv) the effect of circumvention of 
technological measures on the market for or value of copyrighted works; 
and (v) such other factors as the [Register] considers appropriate.'' 
\5\ After the Office develops a comprehensive administrative record, 
the Register makes a recommendation to the Librarian of Congress 
(``Librarian'') concerning whether exemptions are warranted based on 
that record.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \5\ 17 U.S.C. 1201(a)(1)(C).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In considering whether to recommend an exemption consistent with 
the statutory text, the Register considers the question: ``Are users of 
a copyrighted work adversely affected by the prohibition on 
circumvention in their ability to make noninfringing uses of a class of 
copyrighted works, or are users likely to be so adversely affected in 
the next three years?'' \6\ This inquiry breaks down into the following 
elements:
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \6\ Section 1201 Study at 114.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Does the proposed class include at least some works 
protected by copyright?
     Are the uses at issue likely noninfringing under title 17?
     Are users currently, or likely to be, adversely affected 
in their ability to make such noninfringing uses during the next three 
years? \7\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \7\ This element is analyzed in reference to section 
1201(a)(1)(C)'s five statutory factors.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Is the statutory prohibition on circumventing access 
controls the cause of the adverse effects? \8\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \8\ Section 1201 Study at 115-27.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    To determine whether a proposed use is likely to be noninfringing, 
the Register considers the Copyright Act and relevant judicial 
precedents.\9\ When considering whether such uses are being adversely 
impacted by the prohibition on circumvention, the rulemaking focuses on 
``distinct, verifiable, and measurable impacts'' compared to ``de 
minimis impacts.'' \10\ The Register examines the administrative record 
as a whole to consider whether the preponderance of the evidence shows 
that the conditions for granting an exemption have been met.\11\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

II. Review of Petitions To Renew Existing Exemptions

    In this proceeding, the Office is again using the streamlined 
process, first adopted in the seventh triennial rulemaking, for the 
Register's recommendations of renewal of exemptions previously issued 
by the Librarian.\12\ As the Office explained in its 2017 policy study, 
the ``Register must apply the same evidentiary standards in 
recommending the renewal of exemptions as for first-time exemption 
requests,'' and the statute requires that ``a determination must be 
made specifically for each triennial period.'' \13\ The Office further 
determined that ``the statutory language appears to be broad enough to 
permit determinations to be based upon evidence drawn from prior 
proceedings, but only upon a conclusion that this evidence remains 
reliable to support granting an exemption in the current proceeding.'' 
\14\ The Office has employed this process in each subsequent triennial 
rulemaking.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \12\ 2018 Recommendation at 17, 19, 22 (explaining the 
streamlined renewal process and how the Office was able to recommend 
renewal of all previously granted exemptions, since each then-
current exemption was renewed and not meaningfully contested).
    \13\ Section 1201 Study at 142, 145.
    \14\ Id. at 143.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Renewal petitions must request readoption of exemptions as 
currently formulated, without modification. Petitions should support a 
determination by the Register that, due to a lack of legal, 
marketplace, or technological changes, the factors that led her to 
recommend adoption of the exemption in the prior rulemaking remain a 
reliable basis for the adoption of the exemption.\15\ To the extent 
that a renewal petition proposes uses beyond the current exemption or 
language that varies from the exemption as currently enacted, the 
Office disregards those portions of the petition for purposes of 
considering the renewal of the exemption, and asks only whether the 
petition provides sufficient information

[[Page 67790]]

to warrant renewal of the exemption in its current form.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \15\ See id. at 143-44.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In response to its current NOI, the Office received petitions to 
renew every existing exemption. Each of the forty-three renewal 
petitions included a summary of the basis for the continuing need and 
justification for the exemption. Each petitioner also signed a 
declaration stating that, to the best of their personal knowledge, 
there has not been any material change in the facts, law, or other 
circumstances set forth in the prior rulemaking record such that 
renewal of the exemption would not be justified. This sworn declaration 
also includes a statement that, based on their personal knowledge and 
experience, petitioners believe that if the exemption were not renewed, 
users would be diminished in their ability to make use of the works at 
issue.\16\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \16\ 82 FR 29804, 29806 (June 30, 2017).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Office received eleven comments in response to the renewal 
petitions, three of which support renewal of specific exemptions. Eight 
comments oppose certain aspects of the renewal petitions.\17\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \17\ See, e.g., Entertainment Software Association, Motion 
Picture Association & Recording Industry Association of America 
(collectively ``Joint Creators'') Noncom. Videos Opp.; Association 
of American Publishers (``AAP'') TDM Opp.; Alliance for Automotive

North America, LLC (``Philips'') Medical Device Repair Opp.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    As detailed below, after reviewing the petitions for renewal and 
comments in response, the Office finds that it has received a 
sufficient petition for each of the existing exemptions. The Office 
finds meaningful opposition to renewal of one exemption (the current 
medical device repair exemption); it will not grant this exemption 
through the streamlined renewal process but will instead allow 
interested parties to develop the record further so that it can be 
considered as a new proposed exemption in the regular rulemaking 
process. The Register intends to recommend readoption of each of the 
other existing exemptions in their current forms.

A. Audiovisual Works--Criticism and Comment--Filmmaking

    International Documentary Association and Kartemquin Educational 
Films (collectively ``Joint Filmmakers'') jointly petition to renew the 
exemption for motion pictures \18\ for uses in documentary films or 
other films where the use is a parody or for a biographical or 
historically significant nature (codified at 37 CFR 
201.40(b)(1)(i)(A)).\19\ No comments were filed opposing renewal.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \18\ Unless otherwise noted, all references to motion pictures 
as a category include television programs and videos.
    \19\ Joint Filmmakers Documentary Films Renewal Pet.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Joint Filmmakers represent thousands of independent filmmakers 
across the nation and have participated in every triennial rulemaking 
since 2008. They state that TPMs such as encryption continue to prevent 
filmmakers from accessing needed material, and that this is 
``especially true for the kind of high fidelity motion picture material 
filmmakers need to satisfy both distributors and viewers.'' \20\ 
Petitioners state their firsthand knowledge that filmmakers have found 
it necessary to rely on this exemption and will continue to do so.\21\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \20\ Id. at 4.
    \21\ Id.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Based on the information provided in the renewal petition and the 
lack of opposition, the Office concludes that the conditions that led 
to the adoption of this exemption are likely to continue during the 
next triennial period. Accordingly, the Register intends to recommend 
renewal.

B. Audiovisual Works--Criticism and Comment--Noncommercial Videos

    The Organization for Transformative Works (``OTW'') petitions to 
renew the exemption for motion pictures for use in noncommercial videos 
(codified at 37 CFR 201.40(b)(1)(i)(B)).\22\ OTW, which has advocated 
for the noncommercial video exemption in past triennial rulemakings, 
states it has heard from ``a number of noncommercial remix artists'' 
who have used the exemption in the past and anticipate needing to use 
it in the future as well as the increasing cultural and political 
importance of ``fan edits.'' \23\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \22\ OTW Noncom. Videos Renewal Pet.
    \23\ Id. at 4-5.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    OTW states that ``creators continue to make video remixes using DVD 
and Blu-Ray source[s]'' for several reasons, such as engaging in 
``valuable commentary'' and that the ``fundamental conditions 
surrounding video remix have not changed'' in the preceding three-year 
period.\24\ No comments were filed opposing renewal of the exemption as 
currently formulated.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \24\ Id.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Office did, however, receive opposition to OTW's renewal 
petition to the extent it seeks to modify the regulatory language of 
this exemption. As in the previous proceeding, OTW's renewal petition 
proposes the Office ``us[e] the relatively simple language defining the 
exempted class from the 2008 rulemaking,'' rather than the exact 
language in the current exemption, which was adopted in the 2021 
rulemaking.\25\ The Entertainment Software Association, Motion Picture 
Association, and Recording Industry Association of America 
(collectively ``Joint Creators'') jointly oppose OTW's petition to the 
extent that it requests modification of the exemption's language, 
arguing the request constitutes an expansion and should therefore be 
properly submitted as a petition for a new exemption.\26\ The Office 
agrees. Such a modification cannot be granted via the streamlined 
exemption renewal procedure and must instead be petitioned for as a new 
exemption.\27\ OTW did not submit a separate new exemption petition for 
its proposed modifications. Therefore, OTW's renewal petition will be 
considered only to the extent that it requests readoption of the 
current exemption; its proposed modifications cannot be considered in 
this triennial proceeding.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \25\ Id. at 6. OTW describes its requested change to the 
exemption language as ``not . . . an expansion of the existing 
exemption, but a more understandable restatement.'' Id.
    \26\ Joint Creators Noncom. Videos Opp. at 1-2.
    \27\ See NOI at 34797 (``Only petitions requesting renewal of 
current exemptions as currently written in the Code of Federal 
Regulations, without modification, may be considered under the

---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Based on the information provided in the renewal petition and the 
lack of meaningful opposition, the Office concludes that the conditions 
that led to the adoption of this exemption are likely to continue 
during the next triennial period. Accordingly, the Register intends to 
recommend renewal.

streamlined process where it determines that a petitioner has made a 
minimal showing that the record supporting the exemption remains 
representative of current legal and factual circumstances. This minimal 
showing, however, can be overcome by ``meaningful opposition.'' \158\ 
Meaningful opposition may be established with ``evidence that the prior 
rulemaking record is no longer a valid basis to support recommending 
renewal of an exemption.'' \159\ Specifically, evidence should consist 
of new legal or factual developments that address ``the reliability of 
the previously[ ]analyzed administrative record.'' \160\

provided evidence that diagnosis, maintenance, and repair of medical 
devices and systems continue to be adversely affected by the 
prohibition against circumvention, the opposition comments include new 
evidence and arguments that may cast uncertainty on the current 
reliability of the existing administrative record. They provide 
evidence of different types of medical device software that are 
configured differently depending on the purpose of the software and, in 
some cases, may not be stored on the device. That evidence raises 
questions about whether the scope of the current exemption, which 
permits circumvention to access ``[c]omputer programs that are 
contained in and control the functioning of'' those devices, is defined 
with sufficient specificity.\161\

opinion highlighted a disputed factual issue that could affect the

distinct body of repair software independent from the clinical-

III. Analysis and Classification of Proposed New or Expanded Exemptions

    In addition to petitions to renew existing exemptions, the Office 
received twenty-five petitions for new or expanded exemptions. The 
Office has reviewed and consolidated related and/or overlapping 
proposed exemptions to simplify the rulemaking process and encourages

Proposed New or Expanded Exemptions Not Considered
    As stated in the NOI, the Office will not at the NPRM phase 
``reject any petitioned-for exemption unless it fails to meet the 
threshold requirements of section 1201(a).'' \281\ After reviewing the 
petitions that seek new exemptions, the Office observes that two

``a particular class of works'' or ``distinct, verifiable and 
measurable impacts'' on noninfringing uses.\282\

    These proposed exemptions are beyond the Librarian's authority to 
adopt because they do not meet the statutory requirements of an 
exemption authorized by section 1201. As previously discussed, section 
1201 requires that any exemption adopted as part of the triennial 
rulemaking be limited to ``a particular class of copyrighted works.'' 
\287\ Legislative history explains that Congress intended the phrase to 
encompass ``a narrow and focused subset of the broad categories of 
works . . . identified in [s]ection 102 of the Copyright Act.'' \288\ 
Therefore, the Office uses the section 102 categories as a starting 
point and refines the proposed classes by other criteria, such as the 
types of TPMs used or the types of uses. As Congress explained, while 
the category of ``literary works'' under section 102(a)(1) ``embraces 
both prose creations such as journals, periodicals or books, and 
computer programs of all kinds,'' ``[i]t is exceedingly unlikely that 
the impact of the prohibition on circumvention of access control 
technologies will be the same for scientific journals as it is for 
computer operating systems.'' \289\ Congress concluded the ``two 
categories of works . . . do not constitute a single `particular class' 
for purposes of'' section 1201(a)(1).\290\ Neither the first petition 
(which proposes an exemption for all or nearly all works available 
online) nor the second petition (which proposes an exemption for all 
motion pictures with an express lack of limits as to users and uses) 
\291\ propose an exemption for a single particular class for the 
purposes of section 1201.

IV. Phases of the Tenth Triennial Rulemaking

    A comprehensive administrative record for the section 1201 
triennial rulemaking is developed through written comments, public 
hearings, post-hearing questions, and ex parte meetings. The Register 
then makes her recommendation to the Librarian, who adopts a final 
rule. Each future phase of the administrative process is described 
below.\297\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \297\ See also NOI at 34796 (discussing an overview of the 
triennial rulemaking process).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Late filed submissions in any phase of the rulemaking, including 
amendments or supplements thereto, will not be accepted without a 
showing of good cause, and their acceptance is subject to the Office's 
discretion.\298\ Participants should timely file complete comments 
using the appropriate form. Informational guides explaining section 
1201 and the rulemaking process can be found on the Office's section 
1201 rulemaking web page at https://www.copyright.gov/1201.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \298\ See, e.g., 2024 Recommendation at 20 n.72 (``[T]he 
Register has no obligation to consider untimely comments.'').

A. Public Comment Phase

    This NPRM initiates the second phase of the triennial rulemaking: 
the public comment phase, which consists of three rounds of written 
public comments regarding the petitions for new exemptions and any 
meaningfully opposed renewal petitions. Commenters should present their 
complete legal and evidentiary case in support of or in opposition to 
the proposed exemption. In this proceeding, the Office is initiating a 
page limit requirement for each of the three rounds of written public 
comments.
1. Substance of Comments
    Parties wishing to address proposed exemptions, including the one 
meaningfully opposed renewal petition, in written comments should 
familiarize themselves with the substantive legal and evidentiary 
standards for the granting of an exemption under section 1201(a)(1), as 
described in the Addresses section of this notice above. In addressing 
factual matters, commenters should be aware that the Office favors 
specific, real-world examples supported by evidence over hypothetical 
observations. Commenters should describe with specificity the TPMs that 
control access to the work, the method of circumvention of those TPMs, 
whether their presence is adversely affecting noninfringing uses, and 
whether eligible users may engage in the proposed use through alternate 
means that do not require circumvention. In general, the Office seeks 
comment on whether these proposed exemptions should be adopted, 
including any proposed regulatory language.
    Commenters' legal analysis should explain why the proposal meets or 
fails to meet the criteria for an exemption under section 1201(a)(1), 
including why the uses sought are or are not noninfringing as a matter 
of law. The legal analysis should also discuss statutory or other legal 
provisions that could impact the necessity for or scope of the proposed 
exemption. Legal assertions should be supported by statutory citations, 
relevant case law, and other pertinent authority. In cases where a 
class proposes to expand an existing exemption, participants should 
focus their comments on the legal and evidentiary bases for modifying 
the exemption, rather than the underlying exemption. As discussed 
above, the Register intends to recommend all but one current temporary 
exemption for renewal, which was meaningfully opposed. That petition 
will be subject to three rounds of public comment.
2. Comment Submission Instructions and Three Rounds of Written Public 
Comment
    To ensure a clear and definite record for each of the proposals, 
separate submissions must be submitted for each proposed class and not 
combined. Accordingly, the same party may submit multiple written 
comments on different proposals. The Office acknowledges that the 
requirement of separate submissions may require commenters to repeat 
certain information across multiple submissions, but concludes that the 
administrative benefits of creating a self-contained, separate record 
for each proposal justify the modest added effort.
    The first round of public comment is limited to submissions from 
proponents (e.g., those who proposed new exemptions during the petition 
phase or provide the legal and evidentiary basis for them during the 
public comment phase) and other members of the public who support the 
adoption of a proposed exemption. Those who neither support nor oppose 
an exemption may also comment with pertinent information. Proponents 
should present their complete affirmative case for an exemption during 
the initial round of public comment, including complete legal and 
evidentiary support.
    The second round of public comment seeks comments from those who 
oppose a proposed exemption; these commenters should present the full 
basis for their opposition, including complete legal and evidentiary 
support. The third round of public comment is limited to reply comments 
from those permitted to comment in the first round, addressing 
arguments and evidence presented by others during prior rounds. Reply 
comments should not raise new issues or evidence.\299\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \299\ Newly raised issues and evidence are not permitted in the 
third round of written public comments or in any subsequent 
rulemaking phase. NOI at 34796.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. Comment Page Limit
    In this proceeding, the Office is establishing a page limit for 
each of the three rounds of written public comments per class. Each 
commenter may submit a maximum of twenty-five (25) pages, in at least 
12-point type, per class per round of comments, except for Proposed 
Class 8: Computer Programs--Repair, where comments submitted for each 
subclass should total twenty-five (25) pages or less.\300\ The 
limitation applies to all comments, including jointly submitted 
comments.\301\ This page limit does not apply to documentary evidence; 
specifically, those containing material authored by persons other than 
the comment submitters (e.g., affidavits, exhibits, letters).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \300\ The Office established this page limit after reviewing 
comments from the previous two triennial rulemakings and determining 
that the overwhelming majority of comments were fewer than ten pages 
(excluding documentary evidence) and reviewing other federal

the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's and the 
National Transportation Safety Board's fifteen-page limit for 
comments submitted in response to agency rulemakings). The page 
limit applies separately to each proposed class in each round. Pages 
submitted in one class do not count toward the limit in any other

B. Public Hearings Phase

    After the public comment phase, the Office will hold online public 
hearings in spring 2027. The hearings will focus on unresolved issues 
material to proposed exemptions that the Office finds unclear or 
underdeveloped in the written record, and may offer the opportunity to 
introduce demonstrative evidence. Demonstrative evidence is limited to 
demonstrations that illustrate uses, technological measures, or methods 
of circumvention already described in the written record; it is not a 
vehicle for new factual showings. Participants may not submit 
additional written evidence during or after the hearings or introduce 
new evidence or arguments at the hearings. A separate notice providing 
details about the hearings and how to participate will be published in 
the Federal Register.
1. Post-Hearing Questions
    Following the hearings, the Office may direct written questions to 
specific participants to discuss discrete issues in particular classes, 
including suggestions regarding regulatory language. Although 
submission deadlines are provided, responses to these questions are 
voluntary. Responses will be posted on the Office's website as part of 
the public record.
2. Ex Parte Communication
    As in prior proceedings, the Office will permit ex parte 
communications after the conclusion of the hearings. Participating 
parties will be required to follow the Office's regulation on ex parte 
communications.\309\ No ex parte communications with the Office 
regarding this proceeding will be permitted before the hearings have 
concluded or at any time outside of the permitted period. The Office 
will identify the specific time period it will accept requests for ex 
parte communications in the notice it publishes to initiate the public 
hearings phase.

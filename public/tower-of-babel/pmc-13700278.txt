Thymic-thoracic ratio combined with cardiac axis as a rapid primary screening index for fetuses with conotruncal anomalies: based on sequential cross-sectional scanning
Authors: Jie Ren, Chun-lin Wang, Xiang-yang Huang, Yi Zhou, Dong-Mei Yang, Chao-Xue Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveConotruncal anomalies are complex and variable and difficult to diagnose. The aim of this study is to investigate the diagnostic value of thymic-thoracic ratio (TT ratio) and Cardiac Axis(CAx) in rapid screening of fetal conotruncal anomalies.MethodsA single-center retrospective case-control study was conducted on fetuses from January 2021 to December 2024. Fetal CAx and TT ratio were routinely measured. The Mann–Whitney U test and the Kruskal–Wallis test were used to compare the data. Curve estimation and Pearson correlation were performed to assess the correlation between CAx and TT ratios and gestational age within the group. Logistic regression analysis and ROC curves were conducted.ResultsA total of 91 fetuses with conotruncal anomalies were identified and compared with 104 fetuses with non-conotruncal anomalies and 127 fetuses with normal structures. Compared with the normal group, the TT ratio was significantly reduced in the conotruncal anomalies group (P < 0.001), and the CAx significantly skewed to the left (P < 0.001). In the non-conotruncal anomalies group, only the CAx deviation was significantly different (P < 0.001). Logistic regression analysis revealed that both TT ratio and CAx serve as indirect indicators of conotruncal anomalies, with the area under the ROC curve for the combined prediction model, including TT ratio and CAx, being 0.875. Measurement results demonstrated good consistency both between and within observer groups.ConclusionsCAx and TT ratio present distinct manifestations in fetuses with conotruncal anomalies. Their combined use yields potential diagnostic performance for rapidly identifying fetuses with conotruncal anomalies.

Introduction

Conotruncal lesions are among the most common cardiac anomalies detected during fetal echocardiography, accounting for 10%–20% of fetal heart defects [1]. These anomalies represent a diverse group of anatomically heterogeneous cardiac anomalies affecting the ventricular outflow tract arising from malformations in the main pulmonary septum. From an embryological point of view, cardiac neural crest cells are involved in embryonic thymus development, branching patterns of the aortic arch, and separation of the outflow tract. Thus, if failure occurs at one stage of this common pathway, the thymus, outflow tract, and aortic arch will all be affected [2]. Fetuses with 22q11.2 deletion syndrome exhibit an abnormal thymic-thoracic (TT) ratio [3–6]. However, the value of TT ratio in fetuses with conotruncal anomalies without chromosomal abnormalities is unclear. Cardiac axis (CAx) abnormalities are associated with cardiac and extracardiac anomalies and are important indicators for screening fetal congenital heart disease (CHD). Previous studies have demonstrated that the CAx in fetuses with double outlet right ventricle (DORV), common truncus arteriosus (CTA), and tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) differs significantly from that of normal fetuses [7, 8]. Their screening value as ultrasound markers for conotruncal anomalies is unknown, which is well anticipated.

Conotruncal anomalies are relatively complex, challenging to diagnose, and have a low detection rate. It is easier for most primary healthcare providers to identify indirect rather than direct signs, making them more suitable for rapid initial screening. Given the broad range of phenotypic manifestations and the long-term clinical implications of these diagnoses, identifying fetal ultrasound markers associated with conotruncal anomalies could significantly enhance detection rates. We hypothesized that fetal CAx and TT ratio, as indirect indicators of conotruncal anomalies, are valuable in improving prenatal diagnosis rates. The aim of this study was to evaluate the value of the combination of CAx and TT ratios in rapid screening of conotruncal anomalies.

Methods

Patient population

A retrospective case-control study of pregnant women evaluated from January 2021 to December 2024 was conducted. Fetuses prenatally diagnosed with conotruncal anomalies, including common truncus arteriosus, transposition of the great arteries, tetralogy of Fallot, and double outlet right ventricle, were included in the study group. The control groups included fetuses with non-conotruncal anomalies and those with normal cardiac structures. The first ultrasound evaluation time of the control and study groups was in the middle and late pregnancy, and the chromosome microarray analysis results were normal. Fetal echocardiography was performed using a GE Voluson E10 (GE Medical Systems, Ziff, Austria).

Cases involving multiple pregnancies, maternal diabetes, rheumatic diseases, or a history of artificial insemination were excluded due to their potential impact on fetal thymus development. Additionally, patients for whom CAx and TT ratios could not be measured were excluded [6, 7, 9]. Maternal, fetal, and neonatal medical records were reviewed. The extracted data included maternal demographic characteristics (age at examination, gestational age, history of pregnancy, and mode of conception), diagnostic genetic testing information (specimen type, method, and results), ultrasound report results (gestational age at the first scan, including thymus measurement TT ratio, CAx measurement, presence of congenital heart defects, and any other abnormalities). Fetuses that did not undergo karyotyping were considered to have no chromosomal abnormalities if they exhibited a normal postnatal phenotype and normal growth.

The study adhered to the principles outlined in the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of USTC (approval No. 2024-RE-240). The measurement of the observed indices did not impact the delivery process or the antepartum and postpartum treatment of the fetuses and pregnant women.

Scanning method

In all fetal cardiac examinations, Sequential Cross-sectional Scanning (SCS) was used to quickly assess the overall structure of the fetal thorax and abdomen and the operation and spatial relationships of each vascular structure. Fetal echocardiography was performed according to the methods recommended by the American Institute of Ultrasound in Medicine (AIUM) to evaluate the fetal heart structure.The following views were included: abdominal view, four-chamber view, left ventricular outflow tract, right ventricular outflow tract, pulmonary artery bifurcation, and branches, three-vessel view, short-axis view (with the ventricle positioned “low” and the outflow tract “high”), and long-axis view (displaying the aortic arch, ductal arch, superior vena cava, and inferior vena cava). The SCS began with the standard abdominal transverse section as the initial view, with the probe moved cephalad along the body’s long axis until reaching the superior mediastinum near the neck. During the scan, the probe was moved appropriately as needed to maintain the continuity of the cross-sectional images and optimize the image quality of the observed structures.

Measurement

The TT ratio was measured according to the method reported by Chaoui et al. [3] and Bataeva et al. [10]. TT ratio was measured at the three-vessel tracheal level (3VT). Thoracic sagittal diameter is the distance between the posterior margin of the sternum and the anterior margin of the thoracic vertebra. The thymus can be identified as a hypoechoic structure at this 3VT level. The TT ratio was then calculated as the ratio of the anteroposterior diameter of the thymus to the anteroposterior diameter of the thorax. For fetuses with cardiac defects and abnormal 3VT views, the thymus was measured along the midline from the leading edge of the most posterior vessel to the posterior edge of the sternum.

Videotapes and images were reviewed at the level of the four-chamber cardiac view, ensuring the visibility of a complete rib on each side to achieve a transverse view of the fetal thorax. The CAx was then determined by measuring the angle formed between two reference lines. To ensure precise calculation, the digital images were measured at the end of the systolic phase [11]. The first line bisected the thorax, extending from the area behind the spine to the sternum, while the second line ran through the ventricular septum. The angle between these two lines was then measured using the Angle tool to determine the CAx.

Each case underwent three measurements by one physician, with the mean value serving as the final representative fetal TT ratio. A second investigator performed the same procedure to assess inter-observer and intra-observer variability. Neither investigator was aware of the other’s results or the presence or absence of any type of abnormality.

Statistical analysis

Kolmogorov–Smirnov test assessed data normality: normally distributed data as mean ± SD, non-normal as median (IQR). Continuous variables: two-group comparisons used t-test or Mann–Whitney U test; three-group, one-way ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis test. Categorical variables (two groups) used chi-square or Fisher’s exact test.Curve estimation analyzed TT ratio/CAx changes with gestational age. Pearson’s correlation and multivariate regression evaluated their relationships. ROC curve analysis identified the optimal cut-off value of TT ratio, and continuous TT ratio was dichotomized accordingly. Conotruncal anomaly predictors (univariate P < 0.10) entered stepwise binary logistic regression (P < 0.05 significant). Model fit (likelihood ratio/Hosme–Lemeshow tests) and performance (ROC, AUC/95% CI) were assessed.Two observers blindly measured 40 fetuses’ CAx/TT ratio. Bland–Altman method and ICC evaluated consistency/reliability. All analyses: SPSS 25.0 (IBM); P < 0.05 was significant.

Results

From January 2021 to December 2024, 360 cases of fetal cardiac abnormalities were identified in the database. Based on the exclusion criteria, 105 cases with incomplete measurements, 28 cases of artificial conception and twin pregnancies, 20 cases with maternal diabetes and rheumatic diseases, and 12 cases of chromosomal abnormalities were excluded. This left 91 cases with conotruncal defects and 104 cases with non-conotruncal defects. The non-conotruncal conditions primarily included congenital tricuspid valve disease(6cases), ventricular septal defect(6cases), aortic valve stenosis and aortic hypoplasia(3case), Coarctation of the aorta and interruption of aortic arch(9cases), pulmonary stenosis(21cases), atrioventricular septal defect(15cases), single ventricle(11cases), abnormal vascular rings(13cases), hypoplastic left heart or right heart syndrome(12cases), abnormal origin of the pulmonary artery(4cases), anomalous pulmonary venous connection(4cases). Furthermore, 127 fetuses without abnormalities were included as the normal control group. (Patient selection process: Fig. 1; Measurements: Figs. 2).

Logistic regression analysis

The continuous TT ratio was dichotomized at its ROC-determined optimal cut-off value of 0.295. Univariate logistic regression was used to screen candidate predictors for conotruncal defects; variables with P < 0.10 were entered into stepwise multivariate logistic regression.

As presented in Table 6, CAx and categorized TT ratio were identified as independent risk predictors for conotruncal defects in multivariate analysis (CAx: OR = 1.115, 95%CI 1.070–1.161, P < 0.001; TT ratio: OR = 0.005, 95%CI 0.001–0.025, P < 0.001). (Table 6).

Test of repeatability

The intraobserver variability of the fetal TT ratio measurement was good, with an ICC (1,1) of 0.956 (95% CI: 0.917–0.977) and a Bland–Altman mean difference of − 0.068 (95% LoA: − 0.042 to 0.028). Similarly, the interobserver variability was satisfactory, with an ICC (3,1) of 0.903 (95% CI: 0.818–0.949) and a Bland–Altman mean difference of -0.001 (95% LoA: − 0.031 to 0.029).

The intraobserver variability of fetal CAx measurements was good, with an ICC (1,1) of 0.948 (95% CI: 0.902–0.973) and a Bland–Altman mean difference of − 1.447 (95% LoA: − 6.345° to 3.451°). Similarly, the interobserver variability was good, with an ICC (3,1) of 0.971 (95% CI: 0.945–0.984) and a Bland–Altman mean difference of -0.462 (95% LoA: − 7.574° to 6.651°).

Discussion

The core findings of this study are unequivocal: fetuses with conotruncal anomalies exhibited significantly reduced TT ratios and markedly left-shifted CAx values. Regression analysis confirmed that both TT ratio and CAx serve as independent indirect predictors of conotruncal anomaly. The combined predictive model achieved an area under the ROC curve of 0.875, with sensitivities and specificities of 82.7% and 82.4% respectively. This indicates that combined assessment holds promising diagnostic value for the rapid identification of such anomalies.

Further subgroup analysis revealed distinct characteristics for the two indicators: changes in the TT ratio demonstrated clear lesion specificity, being significantly lower only in the conotruncal anomalies group compared to the normal group, with no significant difference observed in the non-conotruncal anomalies group. Even in the subtype analysis of conotruncal anomalies group, the proportion of abnormal TT ratio was higher than that of cardiac axis, which further highlights the advantage of TT ratio in the targeted identification of this type of malformations. Furthermore, the TT ratio showed no correlation with gestational age, eliminating the need for reliance on standard curves or Z-score comparisons in clinical application. This significantly enhances operational convenience and result stability. Whilst CAx abnormalities were prevalent in the conotruncal anomalies group, they were also present in the non-conotruncal anomalies group. This aligns with the clinical characteristic that CAx abnormalities frequently correlate with multiple intracardiac and extracardiac lesions [12], indicating that CAx alone has limited efficacy in enhancing the rapid prenatal screening rate for conotruncal anomalies.

From an embryonic developmental perspective, cardiac neural crest cells participate in embryonic thymus development, aortic arch branching patterns, and outflow tract separation [2]. Abnormal migration and differentiation of these cells may simultaneously affect thymic and cardiac structures, providing a theoretical basis for the joint association between TT ratio and CAx in conotruncal malformations. Thymic hypoplasia is a common foetal echocardiographic finding in 22q11.2DS [13]. The significantly reduced TT ratio observed in conotruncal anomalies group with normal chromosome in this study further suggests an association between thymic development status and cardiac malformations.

To the author’s knowledge, this study represents the first to jointly evaluate foetal CAx and thymic TT ratio in predicting conotruncal anomalies with associated cardiac defects. It demonstrates both continuity with and innovation over previous relevant research.

In CAx research, previous literature has established its reference range—normal mid-pregnancy CAx is defined as a leftward angulation of 45° ± 20° from the midline [14], while the latest data from Bork et al. delineate the normal range as 43° ± 14° [15]; Concurrently, it has been confirmed that abnormal CAx is present in one-quarter of CHD cases and one-third of conotruncal anomalies [11]. Furthermore, left rotation of the heart in the four-chamber view is a common manifestation of conotruncal anomalies [7]. CAx abnormalities in fetuses with TOF correlate with pulmonary atresia, dextrocardia, and elevated postnatal mortality rates [8]. This study not only validates the association between CAx abnormalities and conotruncal anomalies but also reinforces the conclusion that their screening efficacy is limited when used alone, thereby providing evidence for the application of combined indicators.

In TT ratio studies, Chaoui et al. observed that fetuses with CHD and 22q11 microdeletions frequently exhibited TT ratios below 0.3, averaging merely 0.25 [3]. The significantly reduced TT ratio in the conotruncal anomaly group in this study aligns with this conclusion. Furthermore, the median TT ratio of 0.34 in normal fetuses in this study is comparable to the findings of Ge et al. [16], but lower than the 0.44 reported by Chaoui et al. [3]. This discrepancy is presumed to stem from potential racial differences. Furthermore, Vigneswaran et al. observed a negative correlation between CAx and TT ratio in fetuses with 22q11.2 deletion syndrome [17]. This linear relationship was not detected in either the chromosomally normal conotruncal anomalies group or the non- conotruncal anomalies group in this study. This suggests that such an association may be specific to certain chromosomal abnormalities rather than a universal characteristic of conotruncal malformation. Prior to regression modeling, this study converted the continuous TT ratio into a binary variable based on the optimal threshold of 0.295 derived from the ROC curve; Multivariate regression further confirmed that the dichotomous TT ratio is an independent protective factor against conus arteriosus malformation; fetuses with a TT ratio ≥ 0.295 had a significantly lower risk of developing this malformation, which further validates the utility of the TT ratio in prenatal screening for conus arteriosus malformation.

Previous studies have predominantly evaluated the significance of CAx or TT ratio alone in detecting conotruncal malformations [7, 18] or fetuses at risk for 22q11.2 deletion [19]. The diagnostic efficacy of CAx alone for detecting conotruncal malformations in 22q11.2 deletion cases is relatively low (AUC 0.69) [17]. To date, no studies have combined these two indicators. Addressing the clinical reality of low prevalence of 22q11.2 deletion in conotruncal malformations cases, this study developed a combined predictive model tailored for the majority of chromosomally normal populations, thereby overcoming the limitations of single-marker approaches. Complementary indicators reduce missed diagnoses, broadening applicability to diverse samples, while optimised thresholds enhance accuracy, significantly improving overall detection efficacy.

Our study demonstrates that the combined detection of TT ratio and CAx holds significant clinical application value. Firstly, optimising screening protocols allows foetal thymus assessment via ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging, and other modalities [20–22], with prenatal ultrasound being the preferred method. The TT ratio and CAx can be rapidly measured using routine ultrasound planes [23], offering straightforward operation and local implementation [24]. The feasibility assessment of this study indicates that the measurement success rate for the TT ratio and CAx at 19w–30w6d of gestation exceeds 74%. Only during early pregnancy (16w–18w6d) was the success rate lower (35.29%) due to significant foetal positioning changes and poor structural visualization, suggesting enhanced clinical feasibility for measurements after 19 weeks gestation. This aligns closely with the conventional screening window for foetal structural abnormalities. This provides a feasible solution for rapid screening in primary care hospitals, thereby enhancing screening coverage. Secondly, it guides decision-making: the combined model exhibits high sensitivity and specificity, enabling rapid identification of high-risk fetuses when chromosomal results are inconclusive. This facilitates targeted diagnosis and treatment, preventing resource wastage [25]. Thirdly, it enhances perinatal management: early warning facilitates the formulation of perinatal plans [26]. For fetuses with thymic abnormalities, in addition to addressing chromosomal abnormalities [27], concurrent attention to maternal comorbidities is essential to safeguard maternal and foetal safety [6, 28–30].

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted as a retrospective investigation at a single tertiary center with a limited sample size. This design may introduce selection bias and likely overestimate the reported diagnostic performance, which restricts the generalizability of our results. Second, a substantial number of eligible cases were excluded due to unmeasurable TT ratio or CAx resulting from unfavorable fetal position, frequent fetal motion, or suboptimal ultrasound image quality. This exclusion may further amplify selection bias, as only fetuses with clear cardiac views were retained; thus, our findings may not fully apply to routine screening scenarios with poor visualization. Third, we only enrolled chromosomally normal fetuses and excluded all cases carrying chromosomal aberrations including 22q11.2 deletion. As such, the diagnostic conclusions derived herein are exclusively applicable to euploid fetuses, and the lack of chromosomally abnormal participants limits the full subject coverage of this analysis. Fourth, long-term postnatal clinical follow-up was not performed, so we cannot establish a definite correlation between TT ratio/CAx measurements and the long-term prognosis of fetuses with conotruncal anomalies. To address the above shortcomings and verify the clinical utility of combined TT ratio and CAx screening, large-scale, multi-center prospective external validation studies are urgently needed.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study suggests that the reduced TT ratio and CAx deviation may be one of the important phenotypes in fetuses with conotruncal defects. Combined assessment of the two indicators shows promising diagnostic value for the preliminary screening of conotruncal anomalies in our cohort.

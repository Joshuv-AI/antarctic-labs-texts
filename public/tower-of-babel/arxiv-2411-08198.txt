Uniqueness and Symmetry of Self-Similar Solutions of Curvature Flows in Warped Product Spaces
Frederick Tsz-Ho Fong

Abstract
. In this article, we establish some uniqueness and symmetry results of self-similar solutions to curvature flows by some homogeneous speed functions of principal curvatures in some warped product spaces. In particular, we proved that any compact star-shaped self-similar solution to any parabolic flow with homogeneous degree − 1 -1 (including the inverse mean curvature flow) in warped product spaces I × ϕ M n I\times_{\phi}M^{n} , where M n M^{n} is a compact homogeneous manifold and ϕ ′′ ≥ 0 \phi^{\prime\prime}\geq 0 , must be a slice. The same result holds for compact self-expanders when the degree of the speed function is greater than − 1 -1 and with an extra assumption ϕ ′ ≥ 0 \phi^{\prime}\geq 0 . Furthermore, we also show that any complete non-compact star-shaped, asymptotically concial expanding self-similar solutions to the flow by positive power of mean curvature in hyperbolic and anti-deSitter-Schwarzschild spaces are rotationally symmetric.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Definitions and background

In this article, we consider extrinsic curvature flows in warped product ambient spaces
(N^{n+1},\bar{g})
of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle N^{n+1}

\displaystyle=I\times M^{n},

\displaystyle\bar{g}

\displaystyle=d\rho^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}g_{M}

where
I
is an interval on the real line,
(M^{n},g_{M})
is an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, and
\phi(\rho)
is a warp function such that
\phi(\rho)\geq 0
on
I
.

When taking
I
,
M^{n}
,
\phi
to be the following, we get constant curvature space forms:

•

Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
:
I=[0,\infty)
,
M^{n}=\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and
\phi(\rho)=\rho
.

•

Hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
:
I=[0,\infty)
,
M=\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and
\phi(\rho)=\sinh\rho
.

•

Sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
:
I=[0,\pi]
,
M=\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and
\phi(\rho)=\sin\rho
.

One can check that then
\bar{g}
has constant sectional curvature
0,-1,1
respectively.

There are other Riemannian manifolds of interest in the context of general relativity, such as the anti-deSitter-Schwarzschild manifold
[s_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
with the metric:

\frac{1}{1-ms^{1-n}+s^{2}}ds^{2}+s^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}

where
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
is the metric of a round unit sphere. After changing variables to
\rho
via

\dfrac{d\rho}{ds}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-ms^{1-n}+s^{2}}},

it can be written in the warped product form as in (
1.1
), with the warped product factor
\phi(\rho)
asymptotically approaching
\sinh\rho
as
\rho\to\infty
. Other similar examples include Reissner-Nordstrom metric, and deSitter-Schwarzschild metric.

Now back to the general setting as in (
1.1
), we consider the vector field

X:=\phi(\rho)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\rho}

on
N^{n+1}
. When
N^{n+1}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
\phi(\rho)=\rho
, the vector field
X
is the position vector. Generally,
X
is a conformal Killing vector field, as we have

(1.2)

\mathcal{L}_{X}\bar{g}=2\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\bar{g}.

Therefore, one can regard
X
in
N^{n+1}
as a natural generalization of the position vector field. A hypersurface
\Sigma
is said to be star-shaped if
\bar{g}(X,\nu)\not=0
on
\Sigma
. Here,
\nu
is the Gauss map of
\Sigma
.

Let
\psi^{X}_{t}:N\to N
be the 1-parameter family of diffeomorphisms generated by
X
, i.e.

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi^{X}_{t}=X\circ\psi^{X}_{t}.

By expressing
\psi_{t}^{X}
as the map
\psi_{t}^{X}(\rho,w)=(r(\rho,t),w)
, then one can see that
r(t)
is the solution to the ODE:

(1.3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}r(\rho,t)=\phi\big(r(\rho,t)\big),\quad r(\rho,0)=\rho.

In case
N^{n+1}
is a constant curvature space form with sectional curvature
k=-1,0,1
, it can be computed that for any
(\rho,w)\in I\times M
, we have

(1.4)

\psi_{t}^{X}(\rho,w)=\begin{cases}\left(2\tanh^{-1}(e^{t}\tanh\frac{\rho}{2}),w\right)&\text{ if $k=-1$}\\
(e^{t}\rho,w)&\text{ if $k=0$}\\
\left(2\tan^{-1}(e^{t}\tan\frac{\rho}{2}),w\right)&\text{ if $k=+1$}\end{cases}.

We say an evolving hypersurface
\Sigma^{n}_{t}
to be a self-similar solution in
(N^{n+1},\bar{g})
if there exists a strictly monotone time function
\tau(t)
of
t
such that
\Sigma_{t}^{n}=\psi_{\tau(t)}^{X}\left(\Sigma_{0}\right)
. If
\tau^{\prime}(t)>0
, we call
\Sigma_{t}
an
expanding self-similar solution
or (simply
self-expanders
), whereas if
\tau^{\prime}(t)<0
, we call
\Sigma_{t}
a
shrinking self-similar solution
(or simply
self-shrinkers
). If such a self-similar solution is also a solution along the flow

(1.5)

\left(\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial t}\right)^{\perp}=f\nu_{t}

where
F_{t}
is the embedding of
\Sigma_{t}
in
N
with the Gauss map
\nu_{t}
,
\perp
denotes the projection onto the normal direction, and
f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
is a homogeneous symmetric function of principal curvatures of
\Sigma_{t}
, then we call
\Sigma_{t}
a self-similar solution to that flow in
(N^{n+1},\bar{g})
.

Slices of (
1.1
) are important examples of self-similar solutions. Consider a slice
\Sigma^{n}(\rho_{0}):=\{\rho_{0}\}\times M^{n}
in
N^{n+1}=I\times M^{n}
, where
\rho_{0}\in I
. One can parametrize
\Sigma(\rho_{0})
by

F(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})=(\rho_{0},w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})

where
w_{i}
’s are local coordinates of
M^{n}
. Then, the tangent space of
\Sigma(\rho_{0})
is spanned by

\left\{\frac{\partial F}{\partial w_{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}=\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}.

Take the Gauss map
\nu
to be the inward-pointing unit normal
\displaystyle{\nu=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}}
. This shows the principal curvatures of
\Sigma(\rho_{0})
are given by

\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{0})}{\phi(\rho_{0})},\cdots,\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{0})}{\phi(\rho_{0})}.

In case of constant curvature space forms with
\textup{sect}=k
, they are given by

\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{0})}{\phi(\rho_{0})}=\begin{cases}\frac{\cosh\rho_{0}}{\sinh\rho_{0}}&\text{ if $k=-1$}\\
\frac{1}{r_{0}}&\text{ if $k=0$}\\
\frac{\cos\rho_{0}}{\sin\rho_{0}}&\text{ if $k=+1$}\end{cases}.

Under the flow (
1.5
), a slice
\Sigma_{0}=\{\rho_{0}\}\times M^{n}
would remain to be slices
\Sigma_{t}=\{\rho(t)\}\times M^{n}
with
\rho(t)
given by the solution to be the ODE:

\rho^{\prime}(t)=-f\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho)}{\phi(\rho)},\cdots,\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho)}{\phi(\rho)}\right).

Therefore, any slice
\{\rho_{0}\}\times M^{n}
in
N^{n+1}
gives a self-similar solution
\Sigma_{t}=\{\rho(t)\}\times M^{n}
along any flow of the form (
1.5
) as long as principal curvatures are in the domain of
f
. In terms of the flow map
\psi_{t}^{X}
, the self-similar solution can be written as

\Sigma_{t}=\psi_{\tau(t)}^{X}\circ\Sigma_{0}

where
\tau(t)
is a function satisfying
\rho(t)=r(\rho_{0},\tau(t))
where
r(\rho,t)
is the solution to (
1.3
).

1.2.
Compact self-similar solutions

One objective of this article is to prove that under some general conditions on the warp function
\phi(\rho)
, the warp factor
M^{n}
, and the speed function
f
, these slices are the only compact star-shaped self-similar solutions. We also establish some rotational symmetry results for complete non-compact self-similar solutions assuming it is asymptotic to a “cone” in some doubly-warped product space.

Classification and uniqueness problems of self-similar solutions have been a central topic in the context of singularity analysis of geometric flows, as they are likely be limit models of the flow. When the ambient space is
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, Huisken proved in
[
21
]
that closed convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
would converge to the round sphere after rescaling under the mean curvature flow (MCF), and in
[
22
]
he proved that compact mean-convex MCF self-shrinkers in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
must be the round sphere. Colding-Minicozzi
[
8
]
proved that stable MCF self-shrinkers must be the round sphere. In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, Brendle proved in
[
3
]
that any compact genus
0
self-shrinkers in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
must be the round
2
-sphere. When the speed function is given by some homogeneous function of positive degrees, there are some uniqueness results by McCoy
[
25
]
in Euclidean spaces. Recently, there are some breakthroughs on the Gauss curvature (and its power) flow (GCF) by Brendle-Choi-Daskalopoulos
[
4
]
, proving that the compact self-shrinkers to the flow by
K^{\alpha}\nu
must be either spheres or ellipsoids depending on
\alpha>0
. There are also generalizations of similar uniqueness results of compact self-shrinkers for
\sigma_{k}^{\alpha}
-flows with
\alpha>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by Gao-Li-Ma
[
14
]
, and to flows by high positive power of
1
-homogeneous, inverse concave speed function of principal curvatures in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by Gao-Li-Wang
[
15
]
.

For inverse curvature flows which are of expansion type, round spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
are self-expanders instead. The flow was first studied by Gerhardt
[
18
]
and Urbas
[
28
]
who independently proved that inverse curvature flow on star-shaped hypersurfaces by homogeneous speed functions of degree
-1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
converges to the round sphere after rescaling. As for classification results of self-similar solutions, Drugan-Lee-Wheeler proved in
[
9
]
proved that compact self-expander to the inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF) in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
must be round spheres using Hsiung-Minkowski’s identities. The same uniqueness theorem for flows by
(\sigma_{k}/\sigma_{j})^{1/(j-k)}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, where
\sigma_{k}
’s are
k
-th symmetric polynomials of principal curvatures, was also proved by Kwong-Lee-Pyo in
[
24
]
. In the joint work
[
7
]
by A. Chow, K. Chow and the author, we further extend this uniqueness result to any expansion flow by speed functions of degree
-1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

The Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a warped product space
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and the metric can be expressed as
d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
. Round spheres then can be regarded as a “slice”
\{\rho_{0}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
of this warped product. Constant curvature space-forms are examples of warped product spaces too. A variant of mean curvature flow was studied by Guan-Li
[
19
]
when the ambient space is a space-form, and convergence to a slice was proved. The study of inverse curvature flows in non-Euclidean ambient spaces has been driven by the study of geometric inequalities, such as the Riemannian Penrose inequality by Huisken-Ilmanen
[
23
]
and the Minkowski’s inequality in anti-deSitter-Schwarzchild space by Brendle-Hung-Wang
[
6
]
, both using the IMCF. There are also works on more general warped product spaces by Zhou
[
29
]
on IMCF, and Scheuer
[
26
,
27
]
on more general homogeneous speed functions, where long-time convergence to an umbilical limit was proved under some natural assumptions on the warping function.

This article is mainly about establishing uniqueness and symmetry results of curvature flows in warped product spaces. In this regard, there are works about contraction flows by Gao-Ma
[
16
]
, who proved the uniqueness of self-similar solutions to the
\sigma_{k}^{\alpha}
-flow and
S_{k}^{\alpha}
-flow (where
S_{k}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{k}
) with
\alpha>0
in the hemisphere, on 3-hyperbolic space. For expansion flows, there are recent works by Gao-Ma
[
17
]
and Gao
[
13
]
which establish uniqueness results of self-similar solutions to flows by negative powers of
1
-homogeneous functions
f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
of principal curvatures on warped product spaces
I\times M^{n}
with the Ricci curvature of
M^{n}
and the warping function
\phi
satisfy some conditions similar to those appeared in Brendle’s work
[
2
]
and Brendle-Eichmair’s work
[
5
]
on constant mean curvature hypersurfaces in warped product spaces, together with some assumptions on the speed function
f
.

In this article, we consider self-similar solutions on warped product spaces
I\times M^{n}
with different assumptions on
M^{n}
and the speed function
f
. In most cases, we study the case when
M^{n}
is a compact Riemannian homogeneous space, whose Lie algebra of Killing fields act transitively on
M^{n}
by isometries. Examples of such spaces include the space forms
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with constant sectional curvature, and also complex projective space
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
with the Fubini-Study metric which is Einstein but the sectional curvatures are not constant. See
[
1
]
for more discussions on Riemannian homogeneous spaces. On these ambient spaces, we have established the following uniqueness result:

Theorem 1.1

(= Theorem
3.2
, Corollary
3.3
and Corollary
3.4
)
.

Let
N^{n+1}=I\times M^{n}
be a warped product space with metric
\bar{g}=d\rho^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}g_{M}
, and
(M^{n},g_{M})
being a compact homogeneous space. Suppose
\Sigma_{0}^{n}
is a compact star-shaped self-similar solution in along
X=\phi(\rho)\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}
to the flow (
1.5
), i.e.

f=\varepsilon\bar{g}(X,\nu)

where
\varepsilon
is a non-zero constant, and
f
is a homogeneous function of principal curvatures with degree
\deg f
satisfying
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0
for any
i
. Suppose
\Sigma_{0}
,
\phi
,
\varepsilon
, and
f
satisfy the condition

(1.6)

\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}+\varepsilon(1+\deg f)\phi^{\prime}\geq 0,

then
\Sigma_{0}
must be a slice
\{\rho_{0}\}\times M^{n}
. In particular, if
\deg f=-1
, then the above condition holds when
\phi^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
. If
\deg f>-1
, then the above condition holds when both
\phi^{\prime}\geq 0
,
\phi^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
, and
\varepsilon>0
(i.e. self-expanders).

Examples of spaces satisfying both
\phi^{\prime}>0
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}>0
include the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(where
\phi=\rho
), the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
(where
\phi=\sinh\rho
), and more generally the anti-deSitter-Schwarzschild space.

It is interesting to compare the above result with those appeared in Gao-Ma’s work
[
17
]
and Gao’s work
[
13
]
. In their works, the factor
(M^{n},g_{M})
in the warped product, and the warping function
\phi
are assumed to satisfy the condition such as
\textup{Ric}_{M}\geq(n-1)((\phi^{\prime})^{2}-\phi\phi^{\prime\prime})g_{M}
in case of
H^{-\alpha}
flow (where
\alpha>0
), or in the case
(M^{n},g_{M})
has constant sectional curvature
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the warping function satisfies

(1.7)

\phi^{\prime}>0\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}+\frac{c-(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}{\phi^{2}}\geq 0.

In the later case, the speed function is the negative power of a
1
-homogeneous function satisfying some conditions (see Condition 3 in
[
13
]
for detail). The value of
\alpha
in
[
17
,
13
]
could be any positive number.

In our Theorem
1.1
, the factor
M^{n}
needs not have constant sectional curvature or some lower bound of Ricci curvature. Instead, we allow it to be any compact homogeneous space whose isometries generated by Killing fields act transitively on
M
. For the warping function
\phi
requires different conditions than those in
[
17
,
13
]
, as ours require
\phi^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
when
\deg f=-1
, and for
\deg f>-1
we need
\phi^{\prime}\geq 0
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
. Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces satisfy both (
1.7
) and our condition (
1.6
), while there are some spaces satisfy only either one. For the speed function
f
, Theorem
1.1
basically allow it to be any homogeneous function of degree
>-1
, and that the flow is parabolic in a sense that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0
, whereas in
[
17
,
13
]
there are some other conditions that
f
needs to fulfill. The methodology adopted to prove the uniqueness results are also different. Key ingredients in
[
17
,
13
]
include the use of Heintze-Karcher inequality, and some maximum principle argument applied to show auxiliary function. We used a different approach by considering the Killing vector fields lifted from those on
M^{n}
– there are plenty of them as a homogeneous space – and proved that those Killing fields are tangential to the self-similar solution. As the Killing fields span the tangent space of
M^{n}
, this would show
\Sigma^{n}
and
M^{n}
have the same tangent subspace in
TN^{n+1}
. The warped product structure then shows
\Sigma^{n}
must be a slice. Similar idea are applied to prove the uniqueness of self-similar solution to flows by degree
-1
homogeneous speed function in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
in
[
7
]
, yet there are subtle differences in case of more general warped product spaces. For example, self-similar solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
are homothetic whereas in warped products the self-similar solutions flow in a conformal way.

1.3.
Non-compact self-similar solutions

Apart from the uniqueness result of compact self-similar solutions, we have also extended some rotational symmetry results of MCF (and its positive power) self-expanders in Euclidean spaces to more general warped product space. In the work
[
12
]
by the author and McGrath, we proved that mean-convex asymptotically conical MCF self-expanders are rotationally symmetric. Inspired by the fact that
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a doubly-warped product
[0,\infty)\times[0,\pi]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with metric

\bar{g}=d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}(d\theta^{2}+(\sin^{2}\theta)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}),

and a cone is simply the subset
\{\theta=\theta_{0}\}
, one can also consider doubly-wrapped product
N^{n+1}=I\times J\times S
with metric

\bar{g}=d\rho^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}(d\theta^{2}+r(\theta)^{2}g_{S})

so that a “cone” is the subset
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})=\{\theta=\theta_{0}\}
where
\theta_{0}\in J
is fixed.

We have extended our work
[
12
]
to more general doubly-warped spaces under some conditions on the speed function
f
and the warping function
\phi
(see Proposition
4.1
for detail). In particular, as an application of Proposition
4.1
, we established the following:

Theorem 1.2

(= Theorem
4.2
)
.

Suppose
\Sigma_{0}^{n}
is a complete non-compact star-sharped expanding self-similar solution to the
\alpha
-mean curvature flow
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=H^{\alpha}\nu
,
\alpha>0
, on the anti-deSitter-Schwarzschild’s space (including hyperbolic space)
N^{n+1}=[s_{0},\infty)\times[0,\pi]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Assume
\Sigma_{0}
is
C^{2}
-asymptotic to a cone
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})
, then
\Sigma_{0}
must be rotationally symmetric, in a sense that
\Sigma_{0}
is invariant under the flow map
\psi_{t}^{K}
generated by any Killing vector field of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

2.
Conformal Killing Fields and Support Functions

In this section, we consider the support function of a conformal Killing vector field on a hypersurface
\Sigma
in
N^{n+1}
, and its evolution equation when
\Sigma
under a flow. A vector field
K
on
(N^{n+1},\bar{g})
is said to be a conformal Killing field if there exists a scalar function
\kappa
such that

\mathcal{L}_{K}\bar{g}=2\kappa\bar{g}

Since the Lie derivative can be locally written as

(\mathcal{L}_{V}\bar{g})_{\alpha\beta}=\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}K_{\beta}+\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}K_{\alpha}

where
\bar{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\bar{g}
. By contracting with
\bar{g}^{\alpha\beta}
, we must have
\kappa=\dfrac{\,\textup{div}_{\bar{g}}K}{n+1}
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be an evolving hypersurface in
N
parametrized by
F_{t}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})
such that

(2.1)

\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial t}=f\nu_{t}

where
f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
is a symmetric speed function depending on the principal curvatures
\lambda_{i}
’s of
\Sigma_{t}
, and
\nu_{t}
is the Gauss map of
\Sigma_{t}
. Our next goal is to derive the evolution equations of the support function of
K
, defined by

\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t}),\qquad\text{ or in short:}\quad\bar{g}(K,\nu).

Some similar results appeared in the author’s work
[
11
]
for Killing fields
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, in
[
19
,
20
]
when
K=X
, and in
[
10
]
for some specific flow.

Let
\{e_{i}\}
be an orthonormal frame on
\Sigma_{t}
, and regard
f
as a function of the second fundamental form
h_{ij}=h(e_{i},e_{j})
, we denote

\dot{f}^{ij}:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial h_{ij}}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(N^{n+1},\bar{g})
be any Riemannian manifold. Suppose
K
is a conformal Killing field on
(N^{n+1},\bar{g})
such that

\mathcal{L}_{K}\bar{g}=2\kappa\bar{g}

for some scalar function
\kappa
. Then, if a hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
evolves by the flow (
2.1
), we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\kappa-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(K,\nu)+f\kappa+\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\kappa+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

Proof.

We follow a similar approach as in the author’s work
[
11
]
. First let
(x_{\alpha})
be the local coordinates of
(N,\bar{g})
and
(u_{i})
be the local coordinates of
(\Sigma_{t},g_{t})
. Then, one can write

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=K^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=\nu^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\alpha}},

and so locally we have

\bar{g}(K,\nu)=\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}K^{\alpha}\nu^{\beta}.

Express
F(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})=(x_{1}(u_{i}),\cdots,x_{n+1}(u_{i}))
, then (
2.1
) is equivalent to

(2.3)

\frac{\partial x_{\alpha}}{\partial t}=f\nu^{\alpha}

By the chain rule, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x_{\gamma}}\frac{\partial x_{\gamma}}{\partial t}K^{\alpha}\nu^{\beta}+\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial K^{\alpha}}{\partial x_{\gamma}}\frac{\partial x_{\gamma}}{\partial t}\nu^{\beta}+\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}K^{\alpha}\frac{\partial\nu^{\beta}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\gamma}\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}\cdot f\nu^{\gamma}\nu^{\beta}K^{\alpha}+f\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial K^{\alpha}}{\partial x_{\gamma}}\nu^{\gamma}\nu^{\beta}-\bar{g}(K,\nabla f).

Here we used the fact that

\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial t}=-\nabla f.

Then under normal coordinates
\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
and
\partial_{\gamma}\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}=0
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}(K,\nu)=f\frac{\partial K^{\alpha}}{\partial x_{\gamma}}\nu^{\gamma}\nu^{\alpha}-\bar{g}(K,\nabla f)

For the first term, we consider:

\left(\frac{\partial K^{\alpha}}{\partial x_{\gamma}}+\frac{\partial K^{\gamma}}{\partial x_{\alpha}}\right)\nu^{\gamma}\nu^{\alpha}=\left(\mathcal{L}_{K}\bar{g}\right)_{\alpha\gamma}\nu^{\alpha}\nu^{\gamma}=2\kappa,

and hence we have

\frac{\partial K^{\alpha}}{\partial x_{\gamma}}\nu^{\gamma}\nu^{\alpha}=\kappa.

This concludes that

(2.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}(K,\nu)=f\kappa-\bar{g}(K,\nabla f).

Next we compute the Hessian term
\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\bar{g}(K,\nu)
, using orthonormal frame computations. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal frame of
T\Sigma
such that at a point
p\in\Sigma
we have
\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}=0
. Denote
\nabla_{i}:=\nabla_{e_{j}}
to be the covariant derivative on
\Sigma
, and
\bar{\nabla}
to be the Levi-Civita connection of
(N,\bar{g})
. Recall that
K
is a conformal Killing field, so we have

\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{X}K,Y)+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{Y}K,X)=2\kappa\bar{g}(X,Y)

for any vector fields
X,Y
on
N
. Using this, we can derive that at
p
, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu))

\displaystyle=e_{i}\big(e_{j}(\bar{g}(K,\nu))\big)-(\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j})\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=e_{i}\big(\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,\nu)+\bar{g}(K,\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)\big)-0

\displaystyle=e_{i}\big(2\kappa\bar{g}(e_{j},\nu)-\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K,e_{j})\big)+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)+\bar{g}(K,\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)

\displaystyle=0-\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K,e_{j})-\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K,\bar{\nabla}_{e_{i}}e_{j})+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)+\bar{g}(K,\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)

\displaystyle=-\underbrace{\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K,e_{j})}_{\text{(I)}}-\underbrace{\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K,h_{ij}\nu)}_{\text{(II)}}+\underbrace{\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)}_{\text{(III)}}+\underbrace{\bar{g}(K,\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)}_{\text{(IV)}}.

For term (I), we consider the Riemann curvature tensor
\overline{\textup{Rm}}
of
(N,\bar{g})
:

(2.6)

\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,K,e_{j})=\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K-\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\bar{\nabla}_{i}K-\bar{\nabla}_{[e_{i},\nu]}K,e_{j}).

For term (II) of (
2.5
), we recall that
K
is a conformal Killing field, so

(2.7)

\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K,\nu)+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K,\nu)=2\kappa\bar{g}(\nu,\nu)=2\kappa\implies\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}K,\nu)=\kappa.

Note that
\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu=-h_{jl}e_{l}
, and so

\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu=-(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})e_{l}-h_{jl}\bar{\nabla}_{i}e_{l}=-(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})e_{l}-h_{jl}h_{il}\nu.

The term (IV) of (
2.5
) can then be written as

(2.8)

\bar{g}(K,\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)=-(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})K_{l}-h_{jl}h_{il}\bar{g}(K,\nu).

Substituting (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) back into (
2.5
), we then get:

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu))

\displaystyle=-\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,K,e_{j})-\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\bar{\nabla}_{i}K+\bar{\nabla}_{[e_{i},\nu]}K,e_{j})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-h_{ij}\kappa+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)-(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})K_{l}-h_{il}h_{lj}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=-\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,K,e_{j})-\nu\big(\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,e_{j})\big)+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}e_{j})-\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{[e_{i},\nu]}K,e_{j})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-h_{ij}\kappa+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,\bar{\nabla}_{j}\nu)-(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})K_{l}-h_{il}h_{lj}\bar{g}(K,\nu).

Next we take the trace by
\dot{f}^{ij}
which is symmetric, so by swapping some
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
, and by writing

\dot{f}^{ij}\nu\big(\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,e_{j})\big)=\frac{1}{2}\dot{f}^{ij}\nu\big(\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,e_{j})+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,e_{i})\big),

we get:

\displaystyle\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)

\displaystyle=-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,K,e_{j})-\frac{1}{2}\dot{f}^{ij}\nu\big(\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,e_{j})+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,e_{i})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\dot{f}^{ij}\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}e_{i}+\bar{\nabla}_{i}\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{[e_{i},\nu]}K,e_{j})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\kappa-\dot{f}^{ij}(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})K_{l}-\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

Using the fact that
K
is a conformal Killing field, so that

\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,e_{j})+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,e_{i})=2\kappa\bar{g}(e_{i},e_{j}),

and recalling that

[\nu,e_{i}]=\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}e_{i}-\bar{\nabla}_{i}\nu,

we can proceed our calculation and show:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},K)-\dot{f}^{ij}\nu(\kappa\bar{g}(e_{i},e_{j}))+\dot{f}^{ij}\big(\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,[\nu,e_{i}])+\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{[\nu,e_{i}]}K,e_{j})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+2\dot{f}^{ij}\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,\bar{\nabla}_{i}\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\kappa-\dot{f}^{ij}(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})K_{l}-\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},K)-\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\kappa+\underbrace{2\dot{f}^{ij}\kappa\bar{g}([\nu,e_{i}],e_{j})}_{\text{(A)}}+\underbrace{2\dot{f}^{ij}\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,\bar{\nabla}_{i}\nu)}_{\text{(B)}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\kappa-\underbrace{\dot{f}^{ij}(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})K_{l}}_{\text{(C)}}-\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

Now consider the term (A) above:

2\dot{f}^{ij}\kappa\bar{g}([\nu,e_{i}],e_{j})=2\dot{f}^{ij}\kappa\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}e_{i},e_{j})-2\dot{f}^{ij}\kappa\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}\nu,e_{j}).

Note that
\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}e_{i},e_{j})
is anti-symmetric whereas
\dot{f}^{ij}
is symmetric, we have

2\dot{f}^{ij}\kappa\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}e_{i},e_{j})=0.

Recall that
\bar{\nabla}_{i}\nu=-h_{ij}e_{j}
, we get:

-2\dot{f}^{ij}\kappa\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}\nu,e_{j})=2\dot{f}^{ij}\kappa h_{ij}.

This shows

(2.10)

2\dot{f}^{ij}\kappa\bar{g}([\nu,e_{i}],e_{j})=2\kappa\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}.

For term (B), we let
\lambda_{i}
’s be the principal curvatures, then we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle 2\dot{f}^{ij}\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,\bar{\nabla}_{i}\nu)

\displaystyle=2\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\delta_{ij}\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{j}K,-h_{il}e_{l})

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{i}\bar{g}(\bar{\nabla}_{i}K,e_{i})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{i}\cdot 2\kappa\bar{g}(e_{i},e_{i})

(
K
is a conformal Killing field)

\displaystyle=-2\kappa\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}

Finally, we handle the term (C) using Codazzi’s equation:

\displaystyle\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},e_{l},e_{j},\nu)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}h_{jl}-\nabla_{l}h_{ij}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\implies\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},K^{T},e_{j},\nu)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{i}h_{jl})K_{l}-(\nabla_{l}h_{ij})K_{l}

where
K^{T}
denotes the tangential projection of
K
onto
T\Sigma
.

Substituting (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) back into (
2.9
), we get

(2.13)

\displaystyle\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},K-K^{T})-\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\kappa-\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\kappa-\dot{f}^{ij}(\nabla_{l}h_{ij})K_{l}-\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\bar{g}(K,\nu)\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\kappa-\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\kappa-(\nabla_{l}f)K_{l}-\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(K,\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\kappa-\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\kappa-\bar{g}(K,\nabla f)-\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu).

The proposition is proved by combining (
2.4
) and (
2.13
).
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

If
(N^{n+1},\bar{g})
is a space form with constant sectional curvature
k
, then (
2.2
) becomes

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\kappa+k\bar{g}(K,\nu)\right)+f\kappa+\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\kappa+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu).

When
K=X=\phi(\rho)\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}
, then we have

(2.15)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)\bar{g}(X,\nu)=f\phi+\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\phi^{\prime}+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(X,\nu)

Proof.

(
2.14
) follows directly from the fact that

\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)=k\big(\bar{g}(e_{i},\nu)\bar{g}(\nu,e_{j})-\bar{g}(e_{i},e_{j})\bar{g}(\nu,\nu)\big)=-kg_{ij}.

To prove (
2.15
), we write

\nu=\nu^{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\nu^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{i}}
where
(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
are local coordinates of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then we have
\kappa=\phi^{\prime}(\rho)
and so

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\kappa

\displaystyle=\bar{\nabla}_{\nu^{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\nu^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)=\nu^{\rho}\phi^{\prime\prime}(\rho)

\displaystyle k\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle=k\bar{g}\left(\phi(\rho)\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho},\nu^{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\nu^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=k\nu^{\rho}\phi(\rho).

Note that
\phi(\rho)
satisfies the ODE
\phi^{\prime\prime}(\rho)+k\phi(\rho)=0
, and so we have

\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\kappa+k\bar{g}(X,\nu)=0,

and so (
2.15
) follows directly from (
2.14
).
∎

Remark 2.3
.

(
2.15
) can also be proved by combining (
2.4
), Lemma 2.6 in Guan-Li’s paper
[
19
]
, and the fact that
\dot{f}^{ij}(\nabla_{k}h_{ij})X_{k}=(\nabla_{k}f)X_{k}=\bar{g}(X,\nabla f)
. Note that there is a different sign convention of
h_{ij}
in
[
19
]
.

3.
Uniqueness of self-similar solutions

This section we first formally define the notation of self-similar solutions in a warped product space
N^{n+1}=I\times M^{n}
with metric

\bar{g}=d\rho^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}g_{M},

where
\phi(\rho)
is a strictly increasing, positive function of
\rho
. As discussed earlier, the conformal vector field

X=\phi(\rho)\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}

could play the role of the position vector field in Euclidean space. Denote
\psi_{t}^{X}:N\to N
to be the flow map of
X
in
N
.

Consider the flow of a hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}\subset N
defined as in (
1.5
):

\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\right)^{\perp}=f\nu

where
f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}
function of principal curvatures satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

the domain
\Gamma
of
f
is a cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e. whenever
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
and
c>0
, we have
(c\lambda_{1},\cdots,c\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
.

(ii)

f
is a homogeneous function of degree
\deg f
, i.e. for any
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
and
c>0
, we have

f(c\lambda_{1},\cdots,c\lambda_{n})=c^{\deg f}f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}).

(iii)

f
is strictly increasing along each
\lambda_{i}
, i.e.
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0}
. This is to ensure the flow is ellitpic.

Notable examples of such functions
f
include:

•

f=H=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}
: mean curvature flow (MCF)

•

f=-\frac{1}{H}=-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}}
: inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF)

•

f=K=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}
: Gauss curvature flow (GCF)

•

f=\sigma_{k}
, where
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th symmetric polynomial of
\lambda_{i}
’s.

Now we state the definition of self-similar solutions in warped product spaces:

Definition 3.1

(Self-similar solutions)
.

\Sigma_{t}
is called a self-similar solution along
X
under the flow (
1.5
) if there exists a time function
\tau(t)
with either
\tau^{\prime}(t)>0
or
\tau^{\prime}(t)<0
for all
t
, such that under the flow (
1.5
) starting from
\Sigma_{0}
, the hypersurface evolves by
\Sigma_{t}:=\psi_{\tau(t)}^{X}\circ\Sigma_{0}
.

Furthermore, if
\tau^{\prime}(t)>0
, we call such a self-similar solution to be an
expander
; whereas if
\tau^{\prime}(t)<0
, we call it a
shrinker
.

Suppose
F_{0}
is a local parametrization of
\Sigma_{0}
, then
F_{t}=\psi_{\tau(t)}^{X}\circ F_{0}
is a local parametrization of
\Sigma_{t}
. By differentiating
F_{t}
with respect to
t
, we get:

\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{\tau(t)}^{X}\circ F_{0}=\tau^{\prime}(t)(X\circ F_{t})\implies\left(\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial t}\right)^{\perp}=\tau^{\prime}\bar{g}(X\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})\nu_{t}.

Therefore, a self-similar solution satisfies the equation:

f=\tau^{\prime}\bar{g}(X,\nu).

When
\bar{g}(X,\nu)\not=0
, we say the hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
to be star-shaped (with respect to
X
). According to the above equation, a self-similar solution
\Sigma_{0}
would be star-shaped if
f\not=0
on
\Sigma_{0}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
\Sigma_{t}^{n}
is a compact star-shaped self-similar solution in
N^{n+1}=I\times M^{n}
along
X
to the flow (
1.5
). Suppose
\Sigma_{0}
,
\phi
,
\tau
, and
f
satisfy the condition

(3.1)

\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}+\tau^{\prime}(1+\deg f)\phi^{\prime}\geq 0,

then
\Sigma_{0}
is tangential to any Killing vector field
K
on
N^{n+1}
such that
[X,K]=0
. Furthermore, if
M^{n}
is a compact homogeneous space, then
\Sigma_{0}
must be a slice
\{\rho_{0}\}\times M^{n}
.

Proof.

Consider the flow map
\psi_{t}^{X}
generated by
X
. For simplicity, we denote it by
\psi_{t}
. Suppose
F_{0}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})
is a local parametrization of
\Sigma_{0}
, then
F_{t}=\psi_{\tau(t)}\circ F_{0}
is the local parametrization of
\Sigma_{t}
, so that we have

\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial u_{i}}=(\psi_{\tau(t)})_{*}\left(\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial u_{i}}\right).

Since the flow map
\psi_{\tau(t)}
is conformal,
\nu_{t}
and
\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)_{*}\nu_{0}
points at the same direction. By renormalization, we must have (up to a sign),

\nu_{t}=\dfrac{\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)_{*}\nu_{0}}{\sqrt{\bar{g}\big(\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)_{*}\nu_{0},\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)_{*}\nu_{0}\big)}}.

It is given that
\mathcal{L}_{X}K=[X,K]=0
, so we have for any
t
,

\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)_{*}K=K\circ\psi_{\tau(t)}.

This shows

\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})=\frac{\bar{g}(K\circ\psi_{\tau(t)}\circ F_{0},\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)_{*}\nu_{0})}{\sqrt{\bar{g}\big(\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)_{*}\nu_{0},\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)_{*}\nu_{0}\big)}}=\frac{\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)^{*}\bar{g}(K\circ F_{0},\nu_{0})}{\sqrt{\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)^{*}\bar{g}\big(\nu_{0},\nu_{0}\big)}}.

Similarly, we also have

\bar{g}(X\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})=\frac{\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)^{*}\bar{g}(X\circ F_{0},\nu_{0})}{\sqrt{\big(\psi_{\tau(t)}\big)^{*}\bar{g}\big(\nu_{0},\nu_{0}\big)}}.

Since
\psi_{\tau(t)}^{X}
is a conformal map,
(\psi_{\tau(t)}^{X})^{*}\bar{g}
is given by a
e^{\varphi_{t}}\bar{g}
for some scalar function
\varphi_{t}
. We can conclude that

\frac{\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})}{\bar{g}(X\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})}=\frac{\bar{g}(K\circ F_{0},\nu_{0})}{\bar{g}(X\circ F_{0},\nu_{0})},

and hence

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})}{\bar{g}(X\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})}=0.

Under a tangential diffeomorphism
\Phi_{t}:\Sigma_{t}\to\Sigma_{t}
, one can convert (
1.5
) into
\widetilde{F}_{t}:=F_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}
so that
\widetilde{\nu}_{t}=\nu_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}
, and

\frac{\partial\widetilde{F}}{\partial t}=f\widetilde{\nu}.

Then, we have

\frac{\bar{g}(K\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})}{\bar{g}(X\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})}=\frac{\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})}{\bar{g}(X\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})}\circ\Phi_{t},

and so

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})}{\bar{g}(X\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})}\right)=g\left(\frac{\partial\Phi_{t}}{\partial t},\nabla\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})}{\bar{g}(X\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})}\right)\right)

Now abbreviate
\bar{g}(K\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})
by simply
\bar{g}(K,\nu)
, and similarly for
\bar{g}(X\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})
. By (
2.2
), we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(K,\nu)+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi^{\prime}-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+f\phi^{\prime}+\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}\phi^{\prime}+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\phi^{\prime\prime}d\rho(\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(X,\nu)+(1+\deg f)f\phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\bar{g}(X,\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+(1+\deg f)f\phi^{\prime}+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(X,\nu)

Here we have used the fact that for a homogeneous function
f
, we have

\dot{f}^{ij}h_{ij}=(\deg f)f.

Recall that for a self-similar solution, we have:

(3.4)

f=\tau^{\prime}\bar{g}(X,\nu).

Since it is given that
\tau^{\prime}\not=0
and
f\not=0
, we can consider the quotient the quotient
\dfrac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}
. By direct computations, we first have:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{g}(X,\nu)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)(\bar{g}(K,\nu))-\bar{g}(K,\nu)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)(\bar{g}(X,\nu))}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+2\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\log\bar{g}(X,\nu)\cdot\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\right)

Consider the numerator of term, by using (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we get:

\displaystyle\bar{g}(X,\nu)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)(\bar{g}(K,\nu))-\bar{g}(K,\nu)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\right)(\bar{g}(X,\nu))

\displaystyle=\bar{g}(X,\nu)\left(-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(K,\nu)+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\bar{g}(K,\nu)\left(\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\bar{g}(X,\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(X,\nu)+(1+\deg f)f\phi^{\prime}+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)\right)

\displaystyle=-\bar{g}(K,\nu)\left(\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\bar{g}(X,\nu)+(1+\deg f)f\phi^{\prime}\right)

Therefore, putting it back to (
3.5
), we get:

\displaystyle-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\right)=-\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}-\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)^{2}}(1+\deg f)f\phi^{\prime}+U^{i}\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\right)

for some tangent vector field
U=U^{i}e_{i}
. Using (
3.4
), the following holds
\Sigma_{0}
:

-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\right)=-\left(\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}+(1+\deg f)\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\right)\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}+U^{i}\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\right).

By the maximum principle (note that
\Sigma_{0}
is compact), at the point achieving the maximum of
\dfrac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}
, we have

\underbrace{-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\right)}_{\geq 0}=\underbrace{-\left(\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}+(1+\deg f)\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\right)}_{\leq 0\text{ by \eqref{eq:conditions}}}\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}+\underbrace{U^{i}\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\right)}_{=0}.

This implies
\displaystyle{\max_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\leq 0}
. By a similar argument, we also have
\displaystyle{\min_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)}\geq 0}
. This proves
\bar{g}(K,\nu)=0
on
\Sigma_{0}
. Hence, this shows
K
is a tangent vector field to
\Sigma_{0}
, completing the proof that
\Sigma_{0}
is invariant under the flow map
\psi_{t}^{K}
.

Now we further assume

is a compact homogeneous space such as the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, or compact quotients of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then the Lie algebra of Killing vector fields act transitively on

, and they span the tangent space of

at each point.

Take any Killing vector field

in

on the product space

. We argue that

is also a Killing vector field on

, and that

.

Express

in local coordinates

then

can be taken to be local coordinates of

, and we can still write the lifting

as

\widetilde{K}=K^{i}(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{i}}\in TN^{n+1}.

To prove that

is still a Killing vector field, we consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{K}}d\rho

\displaystyle=di_{\widetilde{K}}d\rho=d(d\rho(\widetilde{K}))=0

\displaystyle\implies\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{K}}\bar{g}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{K}}\big(d\rho^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}g_{M}\big)

\displaystyle=0+\underbrace{\phi(\rho)^{2}}_{\textup{indep of $w_{i}$'s}}\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{K}}g_{M}

\displaystyle=0.

Also, it can also be checked that

Therefore, any Killing vector field

which is tangential to
\Sigma_{0}
according to the result we proved. For

. This proves that

, and so

is orthogonal to

is the slice

.

∎

4.
Symmetry of non-compact self-similar solutions in doubly-warped spaces

In this section, we discuss some rigidity results of non-compact self-similar solutions. The application of the maximum principle is not as straight-forward as in the compact case. Yet, using some similar idea as in
[
12
]
, one can still prove some rotational symmetry results for self-similar solutions asymptotic to a “cone” in some doubly-warped product spaces.

Now further assume the factor
(M^{n},g_{M})
in
N^{n+1}=I\times M^{n}
is itself a warped product space, i.e.
M^{n}=J\times S^{n-1}
where
J
is an interval, and
(S^{n-1},g_{S})
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, so that
g_{M}
takes the form:

g_{M}=d\theta^{2}+r(\theta)^{2}g_{S},

where
\theta\in J
, and
r(\theta)
is a strictly increasing function on
J
.

As such,
N^{n+1}
can be regarded as a doubly-warped product space, in a sense that
N^{n+1}=I\times J\times S^{n-1}
, and its metric is given by

\bar{g}=d\rho^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}\,d\theta^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}r(\theta)^{2}g_{S}.

The subset
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})
, with
\theta_{0}
fixed, defined by

\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})=\{(\rho,\theta_{0},w)\in N:\rho\in I,w\in S\}

is called a “cone” with angle
\theta_{0}
. By straight-forward computation, the principal curvatures of
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})
are given by

\left\{0,\frac{1}{\phi(\rho)}\frac{r^{\prime}(\theta_{0})}{r(\theta_{0})},\cdots,\frac{1}{\phi(\rho)}\frac{r^{\prime}(\theta_{0})}{r(\theta_{0})}\right\}.

If a complete non-compact hypersurface
\Sigma
has an end that is sufficiently close, say in
C^{2}
-sense, to
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})
when
\rho
is large, then it is expected that the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
would be close to those of
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})
, and that
\Sigma
should be approximately as symmetric as
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})
. Using this idea, we could then prove the some symmetry results about non-compact self-similar solutions. We first derive a proposition in a general setting, and will apply it to prove some symmetry results for some specific warped product spaces and curvature flows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
N^{n+1}=[0,\infty)\times J\times S^{n-1}
be a doubly-warped product space, with metric of the form

\bar{g}=d\rho^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}\,d\theta^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}r(\theta)^{2}g_{S}

where
\rho\in[0,\infty)
,
\theta\in J
, and
g_{S}
is a Riemannian metric on
g_{S}
. Assume for each
\rho_{0}>0

N\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}=\{(\rho,\theta,w):\rho\leq\rho_{0},\theta\in J,w\in S\}

is compact. Suppose
\Sigma_{t}^{n}
is a complete non-compact star-sharped self-similar solution on
(N^{n+1},\bar{g})
evolving along the flow (
1.5
)

\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\right)^{\perp}=f\nu

where
f(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
is a
C^{1}
function satisfying (i)-(iii) in p.
3
. Assume there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\Sigma_{0}
,
\phi
,
\tau
, and
f
satisfies the condition

(4.1)

\left(f\phi^{\prime}(1+\deg f)+\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}f}{\phi\tau^{\prime}}\right)>\varepsilon\Big(\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}+\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,\nu,e_{j})-\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\Big).

Then, if a Killing vector field
K
on
N^{n+1}
satisfies
[X,K]=0
and

(4.2)

\lim_{\rho_{0}\to+\infty}\sup_{\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}}\left|\bar{g}(K,\nu)\right|=0,

then
\Sigma_{0}
is tangential to
K
.

Proof.

Recall that the conformal Killing vector field
\displaystyle{X=\phi(\rho)\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}}
induces a flow map
\psi_{t}
such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{t}^{*}\bar{g}=\psi_{t}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\bar{g})=(\psi_{t})^{*}(2\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\bar{g})=2(\phi^{\prime}\circ\rho\circ\psi_{t})\psi_{t}^{*}\bar{g},

so
\psi_{t}^{*}\bar{g}
is conformally related to
\bar{g}
by the relation:

\psi_{t}^{*}\bar{g}=\exp\left(2\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\prime}\circ\rho\circ\psi_{s}\,ds\right)\bar{g}.

Let
F_{t}
be the parametrization of the self-similar solution
\Sigma_{t}
, i.e.
F_{t}=\psi_{\tau(t)}\circ F_{0}
for some function
\tau(t)
of
t
. Given any conformal Killing field
K
such that
[X,K]=0
, then as discussed in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{(\psi_{\tau}^{*}\bar{g})(K\circ F_{0},\nu_{0})}{\sqrt{(\psi_{\tau}^{*}\bar{g})(\nu_{0},\nu_{0})}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\tau(t)}\phi^{\prime}\circ\rho\circ\psi_{s}\,ds\right)\bar{g}(K\circ F_{0},\nu_{0}).

From here, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})=(\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\circ\rho\circ\psi_{t})\bar{g}(K\circ F_{0},\nu_{0}).

By a suitable tangential diffeomorphism
\Phi_{t}
, one can arrange that
\widetilde{F}_{t}:=F_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}
satisfies the flow

\frac{\partial\widetilde{F}}{\partial t}=f\widetilde{\nu}.

Precisely,
\Phi_{t}
satisfies the ODE:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{t}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial t}\right)^{T}\circ\Phi_{t}

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}

\displaystyle=\textup{id}

where
T
denotes the tangential projection onto
\Sigma_{t}
. As
F_{t}
evolves by
\psi_{\tau}\circ F_{0}
, we also have

\frac{\partial\Phi_{t}}{\partial t}=-\tau^{\prime}X^{T}\circ F_{t}

By the chain rule, we then have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}(K\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})\circ\Phi_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})\right)\circ\Phi_{t}+\bar{g}\left(\nabla\big(\bar{g}(K\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})\big),\frac{\partial\Phi_{t}}{\partial t}\right)

\displaystyle=(\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\circ\rho\circ\psi_{t}\circ\Phi_{t})\bar{g}(K\circ F_{0},\nu_{0})\circ\Phi_{t}-\bar{g}\left(\nabla\big(\bar{g}(K\circ\widetilde{F}_{t},\widetilde{\nu}_{t})\big),\tau^{\prime}X^{T}\circ F_{t}\right).

Evaluate at
t=0
, we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigg|_{t=0}\bar{g}(K\circ F_{t},\nu_{t})=\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\bar{g}(K,\nu)-\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{X^{T}}\bar{g}(K,\nu).

Hence, (
3.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\bar{g}(K,\nu)-\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{X^{T}}\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(K,\nu)+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

and (
3.3
) becomes

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\bar{g}(X,\nu)-\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{X^{T}}\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)-\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\bar{g}(X,\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+(1+\deg f)f\phi^{\prime}+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle=\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\bar{g}(X,\nu)-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\tau^{\prime}(1+\deg f)\phi^{\prime}\bar{g}(X,\nu)+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\bar{g}(X,\nu)

These results motivate us to define the second-order elliptic operator:

L:=\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}+\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{X^{T}}+\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}-\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu).

Then, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) can be written as

(4.3)

\displaystyle L\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)

\displaystyle=\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\bar{g}(K,\nu)

(4.4)

\displaystyle L\big(\bar{g}(X,\nu)\big)

\displaystyle=-\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\bar{g}(X,\nu)-\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}(\deg f)\bar{g}(X,\nu).

By straight-forward computation, for any scalar functions
\varphi_{1}
, and
\varphi_{2}\not=0
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{2}\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{1}\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\varphi_{2}}{\varphi_{2}^{2}}-\frac{2}{\varphi_{2}}\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{2}}\right)\nabla_{j}\varphi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi_{2}^{2}}\left\{\varphi_{2}\big(L\varphi_{1}-\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{X^{T}}\varphi_{1}-\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\varphi_{1}+\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{i},\nu)\varphi_{1}\big)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\left.-\varphi_{1}\big(L\varphi_{2}-\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{X^{T}}\varphi_{2}-\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\varphi_{2}+\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,e_{j},\nu)\varphi_{2}\big)\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{2}{\varphi_{2}}\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{2}}\right)\nabla_{j}\varphi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{2}L\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{1}L\varphi_{2}}{\varphi_{2}^{2}}-\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{X^{T}}\left(\frac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{2}}\right)-\frac{2}{\varphi_{2}^{2}}\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{2}}\right)\nabla_{j}\varphi_{2}

Next we apply the above formula with
\varphi_{1}=\bar{g}(K,\nu)
and
\varphi_{2}=\bar{g}(X,\nu)+\varepsilon
. Note that
\Sigma_{0}
is star-shaped, so
\varphi_{2}>\varepsilon>0
. Consider the term

(4.6)

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}L\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{1}L\varphi_{2}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{g}(X,\nu)+\varepsilon\big)L\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)-\bar{g}(K,\nu)L\big(\bar{g}(X,\nu)+\varepsilon\big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\bar{g}(X,\nu)L\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)-\bar{g}(K,\nu)L\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)\Big)-\varepsilon\Big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)L(1)-L\big(\bar{g}(K,\nu)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\left(\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}(1+\deg f)+\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\right)\bar{g}(K,\nu)\bar{g}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\varepsilon\Big(\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}+\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,\nu,e_{j})-\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\Big)\bar{g}(K,\nu)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left\{\left(f\phi^{\prime}(1+\deg f)+\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}f}{\phi\tau^{\prime}}\right)-\varepsilon\Big(\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}+\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,\nu,e_{j})-\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\Big)\right\}}_{=:P(\Sigma_{0},\phi,f,\tau,\varepsilon)}\bar{g}(K,\nu).

By the given condition (
4.1
), the term
P
defined above is positive. Now we consider the quotient

u:=\frac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{2}}=\frac{\bar{g}(K,\nu)}{\bar{g}(X,\nu)+\varepsilon}.

By the star-shaped property, we have
\bar{g}(X,\nu)+\varepsilon>\varepsilon>0
. Combine with the asymptotic condition (
4.2
) on
\bar{g}(K,\nu)
, we have

(4.7)

\lim_{\rho_{0}\to+\infty}\sup_{\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}}\left|u\right|=0.

We want to argue that
u\equiv 0
on
\Sigma_{0}
. Suppose not, there exists
p\in\Sigma_{0}
such that
u(p)\not=0
. Without loss of generality, we assume
u(p)>0
(the case
u(p)<0
is similar). From (

), there exists a sufficiently large
\rho_{0}>0
such that

u(q)<\frac{1}{2}u(p),\;\;\forall q\in\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}.

In particular,
p\in\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
. Since is
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
is compact, the above condition implies that
u
must have an interior maximum. However, (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) would imply at this local maximum point

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\dot{f}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u=\underbrace{P(\Sigma_{0},\phi,f,\tau)}_{>0}\underbrace{\frac{u(p^{\prime})}{\varphi_{2}}}_{>0}\underbrace{-\tau^{\prime}\nabla_{X^{T}}u-\frac{2}{\varphi_{2}^{2}}\dot{f}^{ij}(\nabla_{i}u)(\nabla_{j}\varphi_{2})}_{=0}>0,

leading to a contradiction. This proves

. The proof of

is similar. It concludes that
\bar{g}(K,\nu)=0
, hence

is tangential to
\Sigma_{0}
.
∎

\Sigma_{0}
under this setting is a MCF self-expander with positive mean curvature. In this case, we have

P=2H-\varepsilon(\left|A\right|^{2}-\tau^{\prime})

The asymptotically conical assumption implies
\left|A\right|\to 0
at infinity, we have
\left|A\right|^{2}<\tau^{\prime}
on
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}
for some
\rho_{0}
, so that
P>0
on
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Then, by the compactness of
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
and the positivity of
H
, one can choose

\varepsilon=\frac{\inf_{\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}}H}{\sup_{\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}}(\left|A\right|^{2}-\tau^{\prime})}>0,

then we have
P>0
on
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
as well. Then, applying the proposition with an arbitrary Killing field
K
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=[0,\infty)\times[0,\pi]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
(cylindrical coordinates), one can prove that
\Sigma_{0}
is rotationally symmetric. It recovers the result proved in
[
12
]
.

Next we apply Proposition
4.1
to prove some new symmetry results about expanding self-similar solutions in hyperbolic and anti-deSitter-Schwarzschilds space. These spaces can be expressed as a doubly-warped product as

N^{n+1}=[s_{0},\infty)\times[0,\pi]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

with the metric

\frac{1}{1-ms^{1-n}+s^{2}}ds^{2}+s^{2}(d\theta^{2}+(\sin^{2}\theta)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})

for some
m\geq 0
. It can be rewritten in the warped product form as
[0,\infty)\times[0,\pi]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with metric

d\rho^{2}+\phi(\rho)^{2}(d\theta^{2}+(\sin^{2}\theta)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})

so that
\phi(\rho)=s
,
\phi^{\prime}(\rho)=\sqrt{1-ms^{1-n}+s^{2}}
, and
\phi^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=\frac{m(n-1)}{2}s^{-n}+s
. Note that when
m=0
, the space is the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.

As an application of Proposition
4.1
, we prove the following result for flows by positive powers of mean curvature. We expect similar results should hold, with some slight modifications, to other flows by homogeneous speed functions of principal curvatures with positive degrees.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
\Sigma_{t}^{n}
is a complete non-compact star-sharped expanding self-similar solution to the
\alpha
-mean curvature flow
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=H^{\alpha}\nu
, where
\alpha>0
, on the anti-deSitter-Schwarzschild’s space (including hyperbolic space) which is
C^{2}
-asymptotic to a cone
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})
, then
\Sigma_{0}
must be rotationally symmetric.

Proof.

Consider that
f=H^{\alpha}
, where
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{f}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\alpha H^{\alpha-1}g^{ij},

\displaystyle\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\alpha H^{\alpha-1}\left|A\right|^{2}.

Also, on the anti-deSitter-Schwarzschild space, the Riemann curvature tensor of
\bar{g}
satisfies the asymptotics:

\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,\nu,e_{j})=-\left(\bar{g}(e_{i},e_{j})\bar{g}(\nu,\nu)-\bar{g}(e_{i},\nu)\bar{g}(e_{j},\nu)\right)+o(1)=-g_{ij}+o(1)

as
\rho\to+\infty
. Combining these, one can check that

\displaystyle P(\Sigma_{0},\phi,f,\tau,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\left(f\phi^{\prime}(1+\deg f)+\dot{f}^{ij}g_{ij}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}f}{\phi\tau^{\prime}}\right)-\varepsilon\Big(\dot{f}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}+\dot{f}^{ij}\overline{\textup{Rm}}(e_{i},\nu,\nu,e_{j})-\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\Big)

\displaystyle=\left((1+\alpha)H^{\alpha}\phi^{\prime}+n\alpha H^{2\alpha-1}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}\right)-\varepsilon\left(\alpha H^{\alpha-1}\left|A\right|^{2}+\alpha H^{\alpha-1}(-n+o(1))-\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\right)

If
\Sigma_{0}
is asymptotic to a cone
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})=\{(\rho,\theta_{0},w):\rho\in[0,\infty),w\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}
in
C^{2}
-sense, then the principal curvatures of
\Sigma_{0}
are asymptotic to those of
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{0})
, which are

\left\{0,\frac{\cot\theta_{0}}{\phi(\rho)},\cdots,\frac{\cot\theta_{0}}{\phi(\rho)}\right\}.

As
\phi(\rho)\to\infty
as
\rho\to\infty
for any anti-deSitter-Schwarzschild’s space, one has

\lim_{\rho_{0}\to\infty}\sup_{\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}}\left|A\right|^{2}=0.

Therefore, there exists
\rho_{0}>0
such that

\left|A\right|^{2}<\frac{n}{2}\qquad\text{on $\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}$}.

For expanding self-similar solution
\Sigma_{0}
(i.e
\tau^{\prime}>0
) which is star-shaped, then we have
H^{\alpha}=\tau^{\prime}\bar{g}(X,\nu)>0
. Since
\phi(\rho),\phi^{\prime}(\rho),\phi^{\prime\prime}(\rho)>0
for anti-deSitter-Schwarzschild space, one has

\displaystyle(1+\alpha)H^{\alpha}\phi^{\prime}+n\alpha H^{2\alpha-1}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}

\displaystyle>0

\displaystyle\alpha H^{\alpha-1}\left|A\right|^{2}+\alpha H^{\alpha-1}(-n+o(1))-\tau^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle<0

on
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}
. This shows
P(\Sigma_{0},\phi,f,\tau,\varepsilon)>0
on
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho>\rho_{0}\}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
is compact, one can choose a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that
P(\Sigma_{0},\phi,f,\tau,\varepsilon)>0
on
\Sigma_{0}\cap\{\rho\leq\rho_{0}\}
. Therefore, the condition (
4.1
) holds, and we can applying Proposition
4.1
.

Now consider any Killing field
K
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. By the same argument at the end of the proof of Theorem
3.2
,
K
lifts to a Killing field
\widetilde{K}
of
N^{n+1}=[0,\infty)\times[0,\pi]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and that
[X,\widetilde{K}]=0
. By the asymptotically concial condition on
\Sigma_{0}
, condition (
4.7
) as holds. Therefore, Proposition
4.1
then shows
\widetilde{K}
is tangential to
\Sigma_{0}
. Note that the Killing field acts transitively on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, hence for any
(\bar{\rho},\bar{\theta},\bar{w})\in\Sigma_{0}
, and any
w^{\prime}\not=\bar{w}
in
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, there exists a Killing field
K
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that
\psi_{t}^{K}(\bar{w})=w^{\prime}
for some
t
. Since
K
lifts up to a Killing field
\widetilde{K}
in
N^{n+1}
which is tangential to
\Sigma_{0}
, one has
w^{\prime}=\psi_{t}^{\widetilde{K}}(\bar{w})\in\Sigma_{0}
. In other words, whenever
\Sigma_{0}
contains
(\bar{\rho},\bar{\theta},\bar{w})
, it must contain
\{(\bar{\rho},\bar{\theta})\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Hence,
\Sigma_{0}
is rotationally symmetric.

On rational connectedness and parametrization of finite Galois extensions
Danny Krashen, Danny Neftin

Abstract
. Given two G G -Galois extensions of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} , is there an extension of ℚ ⁡ ( t ) {\mathbb{Q}}(t) that specializes to both? The equivalence relation on G G -Galois extension of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} , induced by the above question, is called R R -equivalence. The number of R R -equivlance classes indicates how many rational spaces are required in order to parametrize all G G -Galois extensions of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} . We determine the R R -equivalence classes for basic families of groups G G , and consequently obtain parametrizations of the G G -Galois extensions of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} in the absence of a generic extension for G G .

on which
\Gamma_{K}
acts by permuting the coordinates. A

\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

We let
\Res^{1}_{L/K}\mathbb{G}_{m}
denote the subtorus of

consisting of norm

elements, and recall
that its lattice

is naturally identified with the quotient of the permutation module

by the image of the diagonal map

,

of

by inversion

2.2.
Twisting and fibers in nonabelian Galois cohomology

Let us review the notion of twisting to understand the fibers of exact sequences in nonabelian Galois cohomology. For an étale
K
-group scheme
A
, we think of a (right)
A
-torsor as a generalization of a Galois extension of
K
in the following sense.
Right
A
-torsors over
K
are in bijection with étale algebras
L/K
with actions by the group scheme
A
. In the case
A
is a constant group scheme, these are precisely
A
-extensions.
Concretely, this correspondence works by associating to an
A
-extension
L/K
its spectrum
\spec L
.
This spectrum carries
a right action of
A
on the points over the separable closure
\spec L({K}^{\text{sep}})=\Hom_{K}(L,{K}^{\text{sep}})
by precomposition.

From now on, until we say otherwise, all torsors are considered as right torsors. Recall that an
A
-torsor
P
is determined by
P({K}^{\text{sep}})
, the points of
P
over a separable closure together with the (left) action of the absolute Galois group
\Gamma_{K}
, and together with an action of
A
on the right. The latter amounts to a
\Gamma_{K}
-invariant action
P({K}^{\text{sep}})\times A({K}^{\text{sep}})\to P({K}^{\text{sep}})
which is simply transitive. Choosing
x\in P({K}^{\text{sep}})
gives rise to a map
\phi=\phi_{P,x}:\Gamma_{K}\to A({K}^{\text{sep}})
determined by
\sigma(x)=x\phi(\sigma)
. We have

x\phi(\sigma\tau)=\sigma(\tau(x))=\sigma(x\phi(\tau))=\sigma(x)\sigma(\phi(\tau))=x\phi(\sigma)\sigma(\phi(\tau)),

giving us
\phi(\sigma\tau)=\phi(\sigma)\sigma(\phi(\tau))
.
Denote by
\HG^{1}(K,A)
the Galois cohomology pointed set
\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},A({K}^{\text{sep}}))=\Zd^{1}(\Gamma_{K},A({K}^{\text{sep}}))/\sim
, where the equivalence relation is given by an action of
A({K}^{\text{sep}})
on crossed homomorphisms defined by
(a\cdot\phi)(\sigma)=a\phi(\sigma)\sigma(a)^{-1}
. The elements of
\Zd^{1}(\Gamma_{K},A({K}^{\text{sep}}))
are then in bijection with
A
-torsors
P
together with a fixed identification
P({K}^{\text{sep}})=A({K}^{\text{sep}})
corresponding to a choice of a
{K}^{\text{sep}}
-point, and the elements of
\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},A({K}^{\text{sep}}))
are in bijection with isomorphism classes of
A
-torsors.

Recall that given an exact sequence of
K
-groups

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1,}

by which we mean that we have an isomorphism of
A
with a normal subgroup (which we think of as an identification
A\triangleleft B
) and an isomorphism

, we obtain a long exact sequence of pointed sets

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},A({K}^{\text{sep}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B({K}^{\text{sep}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},C({K}^{\text{sep}}))}

In particular, if the map

is surjective, we can identify
\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},A({K}^{\text{sep}}))
with the pointed kernel of
\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B({K}^{\text{sep}}))\to\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},C({K}^{\text{sep}}))

We now examine the fibers of the map
\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B({K}^{\text{sep}}))\to\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},C({K}^{\text{sep}}))
using the method of twisting, following
[
16
, §III.1.6]
. In the context of the above exact sequence, given a
B
-torsor
P
, corresponding to a cocycle
\alpha\in\Zd^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B)
,
consider the image of the cocycle
\alpha
in
\Zd^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\Aut(B))
with respect to the map
B\to\Aut(B)
given by conjugation.
By Galois descent, this image induces a
K
-group scheme
\mbox{}_{\alpha}B
. The left action of
\mbox{}_{\alpha}B
and right action of

on

give

the structure of a bitorsor. The operation
Q\mapsto Q\times_{\mbox{}_{\alpha}B}P
of contracted product with

then gives a bijection between isomorphism classes of
\mbox{}_{\alpha}B
-torsors and
B
-torsors. Similarly, we may consider the image of
\alpha
in
\Zd^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\Aut(C))
via the composition
B\to C\to\Aut(C)
and the corresponding
K
-group scheme
\mbox{}_{\alpha}C
. Finally, as

is normal in

, consider the image of
\alpha
in
\Zd^{1}(\Gamma,\Aut(A))
given by the map

induced by conjugation, and the corresponding group scheme
\mbox{}_{\alpha}A
.
One may check that we thereby obtain a “twisted” exact sequence

1{\rightarrow}\mbox{}_{\alpha}A{\rightarrow}\mbox{}_{\alpha}B{\rightarrow}\mbox{}_{\alpha}C{\rightarrow}1,

and that contracted products induce a commutative diagram with vertical bijections:

\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\mbox{}_{\alpha}A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\mbox{}_{\alpha}B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\times_{\mbox{}_{\alpha}B}P}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\mbox{}_{\alpha}C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\times_{\mbox{}_{\alpha}C}P/A}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},C).}

Here,

denotes the contracted product

, which is a torsor corresponding to the image of the cochain

in
\Zd^{1}(\Gamma_{K},C)
. In particular, given our

-torsor

, we may identify the fiber

of the class of

with respect to the map
\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B)\to\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},C)
with the pointed kernel of the map
\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\mbox{}_{\alpha}B)\to\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\mbox{}_{\alpha}C)
, which gives us an exact sequence of pointed sets:

(1)

\mbox{}_{\alpha}B(K)\to\mbox{}_{\alpha}C(K)\to\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\mbox{}_{\alpha}A)\to F_{P/A}\to 1.

Example 2.2
.

Consider the special case that

is a constant group and is expressed as a semidirect product

, with

-torsor

, the splitting map

gives a

-torsor

which maps to

(i.e.

). Let

be a cocycle corresponding to

. (We will also write
\beta
for the image of this cocycle in
\Zd^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B)
). As

is abelian, we find that as conjugation by

\mbox{}_{\beta}C(K)=C(K)=C
and the

which shows that

is surjective and hence we obtain an idenfitication

, where

is the group scheme obtained by the image of

\HG^{1}(\Gamma_{K},\Aut(A))
via the map

induced by conjugation.

2.3.
Embedding into tori and algebraic groups

To study R-equivalence on Galois extensions, we follow the theory of
R
-equivalence on tori developed by Colliot-Thélène–Sansuc
[
6
]
. This is a general reference for the rest of this section.

We first note that every finite abelian
K
-group
A
whose order is not divisible by the characteristic of
K
embeds into a quasitrivial torus. Indeed, to do so, we may choose a projection
X_{P}\to X(A)
with kernel
L
a torsion-free abelian group (another lattice). This gives a short exact sequence

(2)

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1,}

for tori

such that

is quasitrivial,
X(T)=L
and
X(P)=X_{P}
.
Note that
\HG^{1}(K,P)=1
for a quasitrivial torus

. Thus (

) yields an epimorphism

Analogously, for a finite
K
-groups

G\to\mathcal{G}
for
\mathcal{G}=\GL_{n},\SL_{n}
or another rational connected algebraic group for which

Consider the nonabelian short exact sequences

The long exact sequences (combined with the triviality of
\HG^{1}(K,\mathcal{G})
) yield a surjective map

, we find that for any rational point

\textstyle{\HG^{0}(X,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom(X,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(X,G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HG^{0}(K,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\textstyle{\HG^{1}(K,G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

where the vertical arrows correspond to restricting to the rational point

. In particular, the identity map in

maps to the right to a

over

in the class

is surjective, this implies that every

Lemma 2.3
.

The above torsor

over

admits dense
K

\HG^{1}(K,G)
.

Proof.

Let

be a dense open set in

and suppose

for some

. It then remains to show that we can find some

such that

. For this, we note that the long exact sequence in nonabelian Galois cohomology gives a bijection between
\HG^{1}(K,G)
and the left
\mathcal{G}(K)
orbits on
X(K)
. As the morphism
\mathcal{G}\to X
given by

is surjective at the algebraic closure, it is dominant. Consequently, the inverse image of any open set

is open in

. As
\mathcal{G}
is rational, its

such that

. But this means by our long exact sequence, that

giving us

as claimed.
∎

Denoting the set of

-equivalence classes on
\HG^{1}(K,G)
and

by
\HG^{1}(K,G)/R
and

, resp., we claim:

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose we have a closed subgroup

of a rational and connected linear algebraic group

over

. Let

The construction of

Two

. A sequence of rational curves connecting

For the converse,
note that a sequence
\psi_{i}(t)\in\HG^{1}(K(t),G)

It therefore remains to show that any other point

Since

.
Thus, the composition of this map with the map

2.4.

To analyze the

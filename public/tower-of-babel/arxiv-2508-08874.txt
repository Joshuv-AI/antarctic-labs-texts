Abstract

In its turn such Dirichlet integrals divided by ε \varepsilon converge as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 to a dimensionally reduced Dirichlet integral on ω \omega . We prove that if we let simultaneously ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 and s → 1 s\to 1 then these squared seminorms still converge to the same dimensionally reduced limit when multiplied by ( 1 − s ) ​ ε 2 ​ s − 3 (1-s)\varepsilon^{2s-3} , independently of the relative converge speed of s s and ε \varepsilon . Introduction

We consider a fractional non-local analog of the variational theory of thin films as
studied for example in
[
12
,
6
]
for integral functionals.
In the local case, one considers a thin domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the form
\omega\times(0,\varepsilon)
with
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, and describes the
asymptotic properties, as
\varepsilon\to 0

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}W(\nabla u)\,dx

(1)

subjected to suitable boundary conditions on the lateral boundaries

(\partial\omega)\times(0,\varepsilon)
and scaled applied forces.
Besides its own interest in the asymptotic description of thin object,
our interest in the subject is also motivated by a discussion with F. Murat,
who observed that the difficulty in describing some regimes in the homogenization
of fractional energies (see
[
4
]
) may be due to their behaviour on some sets that
of thin-film type.

We briefly recall how the local case can be treated.
After scaling energies (
1

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}W(\nabla u)\,dx

(2)

and defining a
dimension-reduction convergence
of functions

defined on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
to a function

\omega

description of the
\Gamma
-limit of these scaled energies with respect to that convergence. The
\Gamma
-limit results to be described
as a

energy of the form

\int_{\omega}W_{\rm dr}(\nabla^{\prime}u)\,dx^{\prime}

(3)

defined on the
d-1
-dimensional set

d-1
-dimensional quantities.
In the convex case

in the non-convex vector case its characterization involves relaxation and

The key functional argument in the reasoning just illustrated amounts to find the first “critical scaling”
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
so that the scaled
energies (
2

Note that this compactness argument depends only on the super-linear growth of

we may take

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

as a model. This is only done for ease of notation, the case
p>1

In a non-local model, we may consider as a prototype, in the place of (
1
), the fractional quadratic energies

F_{\varepsilon,s}(u)=(1-s)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy,

(4)

defined on the space

By the result of Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu

F_{\rm dr}(u)=c_{d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}.

(6)

This limit is obtained by letting first

in (

\varepsilon
, and then letting

In this paper we address the problem of finding the scaling
\lambda_{\varepsilon,s}
for which the functionals

\lambda_{\varepsilon,s}F_{\varepsilon,s}
are equicoercive with respect to a properly defined dimension-reduction convergence, and compute the dimensionally-reduced limit if

and

We find that the correct scaling factor is

when the target function is of the form

can be replaced by

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon,s}(u)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle(1-s)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),y-x\rangle|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle(1-s)\frac{1}{d}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{d+2s-2}}d\xi

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle(1-s)\frac{\varepsilon}{d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u(x^{\prime})|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\ \mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}{t^{1-2s}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\frac{\varepsilon}{d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u(x^{\prime})|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\ \mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})\frac{\varepsilon^{2-2s}}{2(1-s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{3-2s}c_{d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u(x^{\prime})|^{2}\,dx^{\prime},

which gives that the (pointwise) limit of
\varepsilon^{2s-3}F_{\varepsilon,s}(u)

This argument shows that, contrary to the local case, the factor
\lambda_{\varepsilon,s}
is not purely due to the geometric dimension

4

\varepsilon^{2(1-s)}

d

A different, more usual scaling, is the usual membrane scaling; that is, the one obtained dividing
F_{\varepsilon,s}
by

Notation and preliminaries

In the following,
\omega

\mathbb{R}^{d-1}

If

2.1
Dimension-reduction convergence

We first give a notion of convergence of functions
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
to a dimensionally reduced parameter
u\in L^{1}(\omega)
. It is customary to do this by scaling as follows
[
12
,
6
]
.

Definition 1

(dimension-reduction convergence)
.

We say that
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
(dimension-reduction) converge to
u\in L^{1}(\omega)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and we simply use the notation
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
, if
u
is defined by the following procedure.

1)
The functions
u_{\varepsilon}
are scaled to a common space by defining
v_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega=\omega\times(0,1)
, by
v_{\varepsilon}(x)=v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d})
;

2)
we have the convergence of
v_{\varepsilon}
to
v
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
to some
v=v(x^{\prime})
; that is, to some
v
is independent of
x_{d}
;

3)
we define the limit
u\in L^{1}(\omega)
by the equality
u(x^{\prime})=v(x^{\prime})
.

In the case of thin films modeled by local energies on Sobolev spaces, this convergence is justified by the following easy compactness result (see for example
[
2
]
Section 14.1).

Lemma 2

((local) dimension-reduction compactness)
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
and suppose that

\sup_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx<+\infty.

Then, up to addition of constants,
u_{\varepsilon}
is precompact (that is, there exists
c_{\varepsilon}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}+c_{\varepsilon}
is precompact) with respect to the convergence above, the limit
u
belongs to
H^{1}(\omega)
and

\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx.

2.2
Fractional energies and their limit as
s\to 1

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded connected open set, the fractional Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\Omega)
are defined as the set of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
such that their
Gagliardo seminorm

[u]_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=\bigg(\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy\bigg)^{1/2}

is finite (see
[
13
,
10
]
.

The space
H^{1}(\Omega)
is a singular limit of the spaces
H^{s}(\Omega)
in the following sense.

Theorem 3

(Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu limit theorem)
.

If
u_{s}
is a family of functions with
u_{s}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
and
\sup_{s}(1-s)[u]_{H^{s}(\Omega)}<+\infty
,
then, up to subsequences and addition of constants,
u_{s}
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
s\to 1
to a function
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, for
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{s\to 1}(1-s)\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy=c_{d}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,

with
\displaystyle c_{d}=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})}{2d}
.

Contrary to the local case, the form of the Gagliardo seminorm is dependent on the dimension, so that the same expression may have different implications. In particular, we will use the following characterization of constant functions (see
[
8
, Proposition 2]
).

Proposition 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a connected open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function such that

\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{k+2}}dx\,dy<+\infty,

then
u
is constant.

4.1
Discretization of Gagliardo seminorms

We first note that the proof of the compactness result in Lemma
2

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\omega\times(0,1)}|\nabla^{\prime}v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\omega\times(0,1)}\Big|\frac{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{d}}\Big|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\int_{\omega\times(0,1)}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx,

which at the same time proves compactness for

and that

Unfortunately, this decoupling of the ‘horizontal’ and ‘vertical’ derivatives is not immediate for Gagliardo seminorms.
In order to bypass this difficulty we will use a discretization argument coupled with Lemma

Following the notation in

(see also

Given

\overline{\nu}\in V

We describe a discretization procedure as follows. Given

and for every

Appendix: an alternative approach for ‘thick’ thin films

We propose an equivalent way of defining the dimension-reduction convergence, for which a compactness result can be proven directly in the case when
\varepsilon
is ‘not too small’ with respect to
1-s
.

Definition 14
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
extended to
\omega\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
by reflection with respect to the hyperplane
x_{d}=0
, and to the stripe
\omega\times\mathbb{R}
by
2\varepsilon
-periodicity. We say that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
if

and, having set

u_{\varepsilon}\to v
in

Note that if

converges to some

. Indeed, let
\Omega=\omega\times(0,1)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{d}}\phi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{d}}v\,dx=-\lim_{t_{\varepsilon}\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi(x+t_{\varepsilon}e_{d})-\phi(x)}{t_{\varepsilon}}v(x)\,dx

since we can choose
t_{\varepsilon}\in 2\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
. This shows that
\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{d}}=0
in the sense of distributions, and hence
v=v(x^{\prime})
. Moreover,

\int_{\omega^{\prime}\times(0,1)}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v(x^{\prime})|dx\sim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\omega^{\prime}\times(0,\varepsilon)}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v(x^{\prime})|dx

=\int_{\omega^{\prime}\times(0,1)}|v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v(x^{\prime})|dx,

where
v_{\varepsilon}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d})
, so that we recover the definition by scaling.

This convergence is adapted to the energies

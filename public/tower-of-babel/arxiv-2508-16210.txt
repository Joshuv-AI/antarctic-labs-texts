Modeling User Preferences as Distributions for Optimal Transport-based Cross-domain Recommendation under Non-overlapping Settings
Ziyin Xiao, Toyotaro Suzumura

Abstract
Cross-Domain Recommender(CDR) Systems are designed to transfer knowledge from dense to sparse domain, alleviating data sparsity and cold-start problems in conventional single-domain recommendation methods. While many existing cross-domain methods rely on overlapping users or items to establish connections between domains, this assumption is not always satisfied in real-world recommendation systems. This leads to the need for non-overlapping cross-domain recommendation systems, which do not require any overlapping users or items between domains. Conducting non-overlapping cross-domain recommendation is challenging due to the following issues: 1) the absence of overlapping information like same users or items prevents us from building bridge between domains directly. 2) the discrepancy between the distribution of different domains heavily affects the performance of cross-domain recommendation. Furthermore, most existing recommender systems represent user preferences with discrete vectors, which struggle to capture the fine-grained and multi-faceted nature of user interests. To address these challenges, we propose DUP-OT (Distributional User Preferences with Optimal Transport), a novel framework for non-overlapping CDR problem. DUP-OT comprises three stages: (1) a Shared Preprocessing stage that encodes users and items from both domains via review-based embeddings and reduces dimensionality using an autoencoder; (2) a User GMM Weight Learning stage that models user preferences as weighted combinations over a set of Gaussian components in each domain; and (3) a Cross-domain Rating Prediction stage, where optimal transport is applied to align Gaussian components across domains, enabling user’s preference transfer from source to target domain. Extensive experiments on Amazon review datasets demonstrate that DUP-OT is effective in mitigating domain discrepancy and outperforms state-of-the-art baselines under the non-overlapping CDR setting. Code is available at https://github.com/XiaoZY2000/DUP-OT .

Introduction

These days, with the rapid expansion of the Internet, it is increasingly important to utilize recommender systems to filter the abundant information
R_Lü_2012
. However, conventional single-domain recommender systems often encounter cold-start and data-sparsity problems, which prevent them to achieve higher performance
T_He_2024
;
L_Liu_2024
;
A_Zang_2023
. Considering these problems, cross-domain recommender(CDR) systems are proposed to transfer knowledge from the dense source domain to the sparse target domain to alleviate the cold start user problem and improve the recommendation performance in the target domain
A_Zhu_2018
. However, most existing cross-domain recommender systems assume the existence of overlapping users or items to build the bridge between domains, which is not usually satisfied in real-world scenarios. Besides, these methods usually represent users’ preferences using discrete vectors, which lacks the ability to capture users’ multi-aspect preferences.

In this paper, we focus on the non-overlapping CDR scenario and propose to model user preferences using Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) to capture fine-grained, multi-aspect representations. Our goal is to predict target-domain item ratings for source-domain users, thereby alleviating the cold-start problem. This problem is challenging due to the following reasons: (1) different domains have discrepancy
H_Guerraoui_2017
which can be inferred as figure 1, and there’s no direct shared information like shared users or items between domains in our setting to align them directly
D_Guo_2024
;
C_Liu_2023
. (2) Modeling user preferences with GMMs enables more fine-grained representations, but significantly increases computational cost. Moreover, this representation differs from conventional settings in optimal transport. Designing an efficient distributional representation that both reduces computational overhead and is compatible with optimal transport remains an important problem.

Figure 1:
Visualization of domain discrepancy between Amazon CDs & Vinyl and Video Games. The embeddings of 100 randomly sampled items from each domain are projected via t-SNE.

Although many existing CDR methods address domain discrepancy using techniques such as contrastive learning and graph neural networks
A_Zang_2023
, they typically rely on overlapping users or items to bridge domains. Though several existing works have explored cross-domain recommendation (CDR) in non-overlapping scenarios, they typically represent users and items as discrete vectors and adopt techniques such as distribution alignment
L_Liu_2024
or domain discriminator-based adversarial training
R_Wang_2020
;
C_Perera_2019
. However, we argue that discrete representations are insufficient to fully capture the multi-aspect nature of user preferences. On the other hand, some recent CDR studies have attempted to model users and items as probability distributions. Yet, these approaches either use simple Gaussian distributions or adopt Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) while still relying on overlapping information across domains
M_Zhu_2024
. Thus how to represent users’ preferences with GMM to naturally capture users’ multiple aspects of preferences in a strict non-overlapping CDR setting remains to be solved.

To deal with the above mentioned problem, we propose a novel framework for non-overlapping CDR called DUP-OT(Distributional User Preferences with Optimal Transport). The idea of DUP-OT is to represent each user’s preference with Gaussian Mixture Model(GMM) distributions over items’ representations and conduct optimal transport to transfer users’ distributions from source domain to target domain. It’s important to mention that to reduce computational cost and enable optimal transport between two domains, we make an important simplification that users in the same domain share a same set of gaussian distribution terms which are extracted from items’ representations, and give each user different weights on these distributions to form different GMM distributions. This simplification severely reduces computational cost and makes it possible to conduct optimal transport only on the limited number of gaussian distribution terms in each domain. Specifically, our proposed framework can be divided into three stages: (1) Shared Preprocess Stage: To keep both domains’ users and items’ representations in the same feature space, we first utilize a shared sentence encoder to embed both domains’ users and items’ representation vectors from reviews. Then, to prepare for the following stages, we conduct a shared auto-encoder to reduce the dimensionalities. (2) User GMM Weights Learning Stage: In this stage, we first fit a GMM distribution on each domain’s items’ representations to get the unified gaussian distribution terms for each domain. Then we conduct a supervised learning process on each domain to train two MLPs separately for getting users’ weights on these gaussian distribution terms from their representations and obtaining the predicted rating according to target item’s Mahalanobis distances on every gaussian distribution terms. (3) Cross Domain Rating Prediction Stage: In this final stage, we conduct optimal transport on each domain’s unified gaussian distribution terms between source domain and target domain to obtain the optimal transport matrix. Then we use this matrix to transfer user’s GMM weights from source domain to target domain to build user’s distribution on target domain to achieve cross domain rating prediction.

To summarize, our main contributions are as follows:

•

We identify the limitations of discrete vector representations in modeling multi-faceted user preferences and highlight the lack of distributional modeling in strictly non-overlapping CDR scenarios.

•

We propose DUP-OT (Distributional User Preference and Optimal Transport), a novel framework that combines GMM-based user preference modeling with optimal transport to enable effective cross-domain alignment without any overlapping users or items.

•

We validate the effectiveness of DUP-OT through experiments on Amazon Review datasets, demonstrating consistent performance improvements over competitive baselines.

Related works

Cross Domain Recommendation.
Cross Domain Recommendation(CDR) aims to transfer knowledge from dense source domain to sparse target domain to alleviate cold-start and data sparsity problem
D_Li_2021
;
D_Sun_2023
. Existing CDR approaches can be divided into overlapped-based methods and non-overlapped-based methods according to the availability of overlapped users or items between domains
L_Liu_2024
.

Overlapped-based methods usually regard overlapped users or items as bridge to build connection between domains to facilitate knowledge transfer. The methods used in overlapped-based CDR can be
further divided into the following categories: (1) Representation alignment methods
D_Li_2020
;
T_Zhu_2021
;
M_Zheng_2024
: These approaches learn mappings between user/item embeddings across domains by aligning the representations of overlapped users or items, thus capturing their consistent behavior across domains. (2) Adversarial learning methods
R_Li_2022
;
D_Kong_2024
: These methods incorporate domain discriminators and adversarial objectives to enforce domain-invariant representations, thereby reducing the domain shift between source and target. (3) Graph Neural Network(GNN) methods
D_Guo_2021
;
P_Li_2023
: These approaches construct cross-domain graphs where overlapped users or items serve as connection hubs, and propagate user/item information via graph convolutional networks or attention-based message passing. (4) Contrastive learning methods
D_Ye_2023
: These methods use contrastive objectives to improve representation quality by pulling semantically similar entities closer and pushing dissimilar ones apart. In CDR, contrastive learning is often used to enhance the consistency of shared user/item representations or to regularize sequence-based models.

Despite the effectiveness of overlapped-based CDR methods, the assumption of overlapping users or items does not always hold in real-world scenarios. Therefore, the scenario without any overlapping users or items across domains presents a meaningful and important research direction. Existing non-overlapped CDR methods mainly used the following techniques to solve this challenging problem: (1) Embedding alignment methods
D_Li_2020
;
L_Wang_2021
: In the absence of direct overlapped users or items, these methods aim to align the latent user or item spaces across domains by identifying implicit semantic correspondences or behavioral similarities. Typically, they learn a mapping function or a shared embedding space that captures common user intents or item features, enabling cross-domain knowledge transfer despite the lack of explicit alignment. (2) Generative models methods
T_Salah_2021
: These methods utilize generative models like VAE or GAN to model and generate cross domain user preferences or interaction datas. By learning latent generation process of the source domain and target domain, the model can generate potential preferences in the target domain that are consistent with the source domain, thus can solve the target domain’s cold-start problem. (3) Distribution modeling methods
T_Li_2009
;
M_Zhu_2024
: These methods model user or item preferences in each domain from a distributional perspective, and establish cross-domain connections by aligning preference distributions at a statistical level. Typical techniques include co-clustering, Gaussian modeling, or optimal transport, which help capture domain-level behavioral patterns and enable knowledge transfer without relying on individual-level overlaps. Although there have been many explorations on non-overlapping CDR problems, few of them have considered to represent users’ preferences as GMM distributions and none of them have tried to combine this with optimal transport to solve non-overlapping problems.

Optimal Transport in CDR.
Optimal Transport (OT) has recently emerged as a powerful tool for cross-domain recommendation (CDR), offering a principled way to align user or item distributions across domains in the absence of overlapping entities. For example, Zhu et al.
M_Zhu_2024
proposed WCDR, which models user/item embeddings as Gaussian distributions and aligns them across domains via Wasserstein distance, capturing both domain-specific and domain-shared uncertainty. Beyond marginal distribution alignment, some works leverage Gromov-Wasserstein distance to align the relational structures of user-item interactions across domains. Li et al.
G_Li_2022
incorporated GW-based structural matching to preserve cross-domain user-item geometry. More recently, OT has also been used in embedding-level adaptation. Liu et al.
L_Liu_2024
proposed dynamic embedding transportation to learn bidirectional mappings between latent spaces. Collectively, these methods demonstrate that OT-based approaches are effective for bridging domains in non-overlapping CDR scenarios, by aligning preference distributions, latent embeddings, or user-item structures in a theoretically grounded manner.

4.1
Overview

Figure 2:
The overall structure of DUP-OT

Figure 2 illustrates the architecture of our proposed
DUP-OT
framework, which enables non-overlapping cross-domain recommendation based on distributional user preference representations. The pipeline consists of three main stages:
(1) shared preprocessing
,
(2) user GMM weight learning
, and
(3) cross-domain rating prediction
.

In the
shared preprocessing stage
, we extract and align user and item embeddings across domains. Specifically, we employ a shared pre-trained sentence encoder to encode reviews into initial user and item embeddings, followed by a shared autoencoder trained on both domains to reduce embedding dimensionality and ensure a unified feature space.

The
user GMM weight learning stage
is performed separately in each domain. We first fit a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) on the item embeddings to obtain shared Gaussian components. Then, an MLP is trained to map each user embedding to a set of weights over these components, forming a user-specific GMM. Another MLP is trained to predict user-item ratings based on the Mahalanobis distance between the item embedding and each Gaussian component weighted by the user’s distribution.

Finally, in the
cross-domain rating prediction stage
, we align the GMM components between domains via Optimal Transport (OT), obtaining a transport matrix from source to target domain. This matrix is then used to transfer user GMM weights from the source domain to the target domain, enabling the construction of user distributions in the target domain. The transferred distributions are then used with the target domain’s trained rating predictor to perform cross-domain rating prediction.

4.2
Shared Preprocessing Stage

In our proposed architecture, the latent connection between domains is rooted in the semantic similarity of user preferences and item attributes. To preserve these similarities, we first derive user and item embeddings from review information. Specifically, for each user, we extract the reviews they have written for interacted items; for each item, we collect the reviews it has received. We split these into sentences, encode them using a shared pre-trained sentence encoder, and compute the average pooling of sentence embeddings as the initial user/item embedding.

Since the outputs of the sentence encoder are typically high-dimensional, and the subsequent GMM fitting and Mahalanobis distance calculations are computationally sensitive to dimensionality (especially due to the covariance matrix), we apply a dimensionality reduction step. To ensure that embeddings remain in a unified space while retaining as much semantic information as possible, we adopt a federated-trained autoencoder. As shown in Figure 3, the autoencoder consists of a shared encoder and decoder trained via reconstruction loss in an unsupervised manner on both domains’ user and item embeddings. 4.3
User GMM Weights learning Stage

As users’ preferences always have multiple aspects, for example, a user may like action and love movies in the same time, we argue that just representing their preference with discrete vectors is not enough. In out proposed DUP-OT architecture, we represent users’ preferences as GMM distributions over items’ feature vectors.

In our framework, we design the User GMM Weights Learning stage to train models that derive users’ preference distributions based on their semantic embeddings. To reduce the computational cost in the subsequent rating prediction and optimal transport stages, we assume that users within the same domain share a common set of Gaussian components, which are precomputed from the item representations in that domain.

Following this assumption, we first fit a GMM on the item representations of each domain using the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm, obtaining the shared Gaussian components for that domain. An illustrative example of how user preference distributions are modeled under this setting is shown in Figure 4.

With gaussian distribution terms calculated, we conduct supervised training on each domain separately. Specifically, for each domain, we design two MLPs: User GMM weights learner(w-learner for short) and rating predictor(r-predictor for short). The w-learner is utilized to learn user’s weights on the shared gaussian distribution terms from users’ embeddings after the dimensionality reducing in the last stage. As we can see each gaussian distribution term as one aspect of user’s interest, we argue that the relationship between these interests is non-linear thus we use a rating predictor MLP to derive the final score. Specifically, to predict rating for one user on the specific target item, we first calculate the Mahalanobis distances on the user’s each weighted gaussian distribution term and put the distances into the r-predictor MLP to derive the predicted rating. The loss function is simply the MSE loss between the predicted rating and true rating. The equations and loss function of this stage can be expressed as:

4.4
Cross Domain Rating Prediction Stage

To predict target domains’ items’ ratings for users in source domain to alleviate the cold-start problem, we need to find out a way to represent the users in the target domain according to the past interactions the users have made. In proposed DUP-OT architecture, we conduct optimal transport to establish the connection between to domains to transfer knowledge. Specifically, this Cross Domain Rating Prediction stage can be further divided into three steps: optimizing optimal transport matrix, transfer user’s weights from source domain to target domain and conduct cross-domain rating prediction.

In this stage, we first perform optimal transport between the two domains to establish cross-domain alignment. Given our assumption that users within the same domain share a common set of Gaussian components, we only need to apply optimal transport on these shared Gaussian distributions.
This significantly reduces the computational cost by avoiding instance-level transport.

5.1.1
Datasets

We adopt Amazon review dataset
J_Ni_2019
which includes user’s reviews and ratings on items, collected from Amazon, the E-commerce platform. Specifically, we utilize three Amazon datasets’ 5-core version: Video Games, Kindle Store and Movies and TV. The statistics of these datasets after filtering are shown in table 1. After the overall consideration of the absolute number of interactions and data density, we choose Movies and TV as the source domain and the other two datasets as target domains. The concrete experiments we conduct using these datasets can be comprehended in table 2. For each dataset, we exclude users with fewer than 10 interactions along with all of their associated records. Then we divide interactions into train, test and valid datasets in the following strategy for each experiment on a pair of domains. We begin by identifying the users common to both domains, and randomly select each overlapping user’s one interaction to put into train dataset to ensure each user has at least one interaction in the train dataset, as some models can’t deal with completely unseen users. In each domain, we then randomly assign 40% of rest overlapping users’ interactions to the test set and another 40% to the validation set. The training set comprises the initially selected interactions, the remaining 20% of overlapping interactions together with all interactions from users who do not overlap. We train models on the train dataset and conduct early stop according to the valid dataset. Finally, we calculate metrics on the test dataset. The selection of source domains and target domains is based on the sparsity and similarity.

5.1.2
Model Setting

Our proposed DUP-OT can be divided into three stages. The first stage includes a pretrained sentence encoder and an auto-encoder. For the pretrained model, we choose "sentence-t5-base" model from sentence_transformers, and the output dimension is 768. The auto-encoder in the first stage is consisted of two MLPs. The encoder is formed with following layers:
\mathrm{Dense}(768)\to\mathrm{ReLU}(\bullet)\to\mathrm{Dense}(128)

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel framework called Distributional User Preferences and Optimal Transport(DUP-OT) for non-overlapping cross domain recommendation task. In our proposed architecture, we first derive the semantic embeddings of users and items in both domain according to the reviews with a shared sentence encoder. Then we conduct a shared auto-encoder to reduce the dimensionality. After that, we fit a GMM on items in each domain to obtain shared GMM terms and conduct supervised training on each domain to get users’ weights on these terms. By conducting experiments, we reveal that DUP-OT is able to reduce domain discrepancy and can perform non-overlapping baseline models under proper hyper parameter setting.

Case Report: [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 therapy in a patient with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) and intracranial meningioma
Authors: Iskander Musabaev, Sergey Goryaynov, Tatiana Yu Kochetova, Valerii Krylov, Sergey Ivanov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundProstate cancer is the most commonly diagnosed oncological disease among men not only in Russia but also worldwide, while meningioma is the one of the most commonly diagnosed primary central nervous system (CNS) intracranial tumor. However, their combination occurs quite rarely. Prostate-specific membrane antigen radioligand therapy (PSMA RLT) might be an extremely perspective treatment option for these patients as both neoplasms are characterized by PSMA expression.MethodsAfter PET/CT demonstrated a high [68Ga]68Ga-PSMA-11 uptake level in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) and intracranial meningioma in a 77-year old patient, we performed four administrations of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617. evaluating the prostate-specific antigen (PSA) dynamics and the structural response by brain MRI and PET/CT with [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11. In addition, we evaluated the patient’s complaints and his hematological indications.ResultsAfter treatment, the patient revealed a remarkable decrease in the [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 uptake level based on the PET/CT data, a decrease in the PSA level by 99.8%, and a decrease in the meningioma size by 90.6%. Among the unfavorable symptoms, high-grade xerostomia (CTCAE v5.0) was revealed. The hematological parameters remained stable for the whole period of observation.ConclusionsThe presented clinical case report describes a unique positive experience on the use of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 therapy in mCRPC and brain meningioma, thus allowing us to expand our understanding of PSMA RLT as a method for the treatment of not only prostatic tumors but also tumors of non-prostatic locations.

Introduction

Prostate cancer ranked highest by morbidity in the male population in 2023 both in Russia and worldwide (1, 2). Metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) is the final stage of the disease, claiming maximum effort to prevent tumor progression. Prostate-specific membrane antigen radioligand therapy (PSMA RLT) (3) using radiation-marked ligands or radioligands and being tropic (prone to) to the prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) expressed by tumor cells has developed into a new and very promising concept for the treatment of this group of patients.

According to a randomized prospective trial, the first-in-class radioligand therapeutic effect approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for the treatment of PSMA-expressing mCRPC is [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 (4). The next step in this field is the use of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA, the alpha rays of which confer a much more powerful effect on tumor cell killing compared with the drugs based on beta-emitting nuclides (beta-emitters). Although [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 has not been studied in phase III clinical trials thus far, it is used in many countries worldwide (5). There is multiple evidence on the effectiveness (encouraging effects) of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA, also after the unsuccessful application of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA (6, 7). It is known that an increased PSMA expression is observed not only in the membranes of prostate adenocarcinoma cells but also in the cells of some other tumor types, such as kidney cancer, salivary gland cancer, and papillary thyroid cancer, among others (8–10).

Meningioma is the one of the most commonly diagnosed primary brain tumor after glioma (11). Depending on the cell structure, meningiomas can be benign (grade 1), atypical (grade 2), or malignant (grade 3).

Benign meningiomas comprise a great variety of histologic subtypes and tend to affect approximately 80% of patients (12). However, a number of patients with intracranial meningiomas may have issues with their radical resection due to their location (mainly on the skull base, near large magistral arteries) and density (hard tissue tumors are more difficult to remove compared with soft tissue ones). In this case, only partial removal is possible (13). Postoperative treatment of patients with partial tumor resection and also with aggressive histological types of meningiomas includes radiotherapy and, rarely, chemotherapy and hormone therapy (14).

A research survey of 96 earlier studied histological biopsies of morphologically proven meningiomas published in 2022 revealed PSMA expression in 98.9% of cases, which may serve as the basis for the use of RLT (15). Nevertheless, we did not find studies supporting the efficacy of RLT for patients with cerebral meningiomas at the time of writing this paper.

Case report

A 77-year-old male patient diagnosed with C61 prostate cancer, cT3bN1M1b, contacted the Medical Radiological Research Center. Since February 2017, he has developed a large-sized lesion in the left temporal area and headaches. Elevated prostate-specific antigen (PSA) up to 1,369 ng/ml was revealed for the first time. PET/CT performed locally with 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) detected prostate cancer with multiple metastases invading the skeletal bones and the retroperitoneal lymph nodes.

On March 22, 2017, prostate biopsy was performed (small samples were taken from prostate tissue), which confirmed prostate acinar adenocarcinoma, Gleason 4 + 5. Therapy with luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonists and bisphosphonates was started. In May 2018, castration resistance was evident, and 10 cycles of docetaxel therapy were taken at a local stationary. From August 2019, six radium chloride administrations were made (223Ra). From March 2020 to June 2021, the patient was taking enzalutamide and from June 2021 to November 2021 was treated with olaparib. Due to tumor progression, chemotherapy was restarted. The patient received 11 cycles of chemotherapy with docetaxel and six courses with cabazitaxel. Thus, in total, he received six mCRPC therapy lines. During the whole period of treatment, the therapeutic effect was assessed based on the PSA dynamics. Control head and skull bone CT or MRI scans have not been performed since February 2017. As standard methods of treatment showed no positive results, the decision was made to start radioligand PSMA therapy.

To evaluate the indications for initiating radiologand therapy, PET/CT with [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 was performed on June 3, 2024. The examination revealed multiple metastases in the skeletal bones and peritoneal lymph nodes with high 68Ga-PSMA uptake value. Moreover, for the first time, a 32-mm large-sized tumor in the left temporal lobe with high PSMA uptake value was revealed (Figure 1A). MRI of the brain was recommended to specify the precise characteristics of the newly visualized lesion. The MRI findings dated July 14, 2024, evidently confirmed meningioma located in the left temporal lobe, which was 4.1 cm × 2.7 cm × 3.4 cm in size (Figure 2A). The proposed surgical treatment was rejected by the patient.

Comparative analysis of PET/CT with [68Ga]68Ga-PSMA on June 03, 2024, before prostate-specific membrane antigen radioligand therapy (PSMA RLT) (A) and PET/CT scans on March 13, 2025, after the fourth administration (B) revealed a marked decrease in the radiopharmaceutical uptake value in the prostate, bones, and left temporal lobe (SUVmax = 3.7 versus SUVmax = 14.6 before PSMA RLT). The radiopharmaceutical uptake values in the pelvic and retroperitoneal lymph nodes decreased to background values.

Multiplanar reformation traverse (MPR TRA) MRI dynamics (multiplanar reconstruction) dated July 14, 2024 (A), September 30, 2024 (B), December 10, 2024 (C), and March 12, 2025 (D). The meningioma size decreased from 4.1 cm × 2.7 cm × 3.4 cm to 1.6 cm × 1.3 cm × 1.7 cm. Partial response according to the RECIST (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors) 1.1 criteria was achieved.

The patient showed biochemical tumor progression (tumor growth). In May 2024 (after completing therapy with cabazitaxel), PSMA measured 89 ng/ml, while it reached the level of 306.8 ng/ml in June 2024 and the peak level of 371.92 ng/ml in July 2024. The patient exhibited weakness, periodically developed low-intensity headaches on both sides of the head without other neurological symptoms, and had frequent urination, with nocturia up to four times during the night. Based on the PET/CT data, it became clear that the PSMA RLT method of treatment might be perspective in this case. However, the presence of dura involving lesion with a high [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 uptake in the left temporal lobe, edema of the left temporal lobe substrate, and midline shift (MLS) increased the risk of possible complications resulting from post-radiation edema and brain radiation side effects. The decision made by a panel of doctors was to begin [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 therapy with preliminary intramuscular administration of dexamethasone at a dose of 8 mg. The drug of choice was [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 because the tissue range of the alpha particles emitted during 225Ac decay is significantly shorter than that of the beta particles emitted by 177Lu.

Thus, the use of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 might be more precise and safer for the patient than [177Lu]Lu-PSMA. At the same time, the intensity of the damage to the cell targets by alpha particles is considerably higher when compared with the impact of beta radiation.

The safety and the efficacy of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 therapy have been shown in earlier research (16, 17). The first 8 MBq of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 was administered on July 26, 2024. The patient demonstrated a satisfactory response without any serious unfavorable side effects. The control study performed 4 weeks later (on August 23, 2024) showed the PSMA level to be 158.39 ng/ml (more than a 50% PSA decline). At 8 weeks (on September 17, 2024), the PSMA level measured 72.41 ng/ml (an 80% PSA decline compared with the pre-administration level). Similarly, the patient noted a decrease in headache incidence; however, he complained of dryness in the mouth within the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) grade 1, which was the expected unfavorable side effect of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 therapy. On September 20, 2024, after intramuscular administration of 8 mg dexamethasone, the second cycle of 8 MBq [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 treatment was initiated. During his hospital stay, no side effects were observed. The patient was discharged in a satisfactory condition the next day.

Control brain MRI performed on September 30, 2024, displayed a decreased meningioma size (Figure 2B), partial response according to the RECIST (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors) 1.1. criteria. The PSA on October 19, 2024, was 21.63 ng/ml.

The PSA on November 12, 2024, continued declining and measured 10.56 ng/ml. The patient had no more headaches and salivation had improved; however, he remained at the CTCAE grade 1 stage. On November 15, 2024, the patient received the third 8 MBq of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA administration.

Brain MRI on December 10, 2024, demonstrated a further decrease of the lesion dimensions up to 2.9 cm × 1.3 cm × 2.3 cm in the outer parts of the left temporal area (Figure 2C). On December 12, 2024, the PSA level declined to 5.68 ng/ml. On January 5, 2025, the PSA was 3.65 ng/ml. The patient complained of weakness, episodes of arterial pressure decrease after physical activity were noted, and dryness in the mouth was preserved at the initial level (CTCAE grade 1). On January 10, 2025, the patient received the fourth cycle of 6 MBq [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 treatment.

Control clinical study was recommended: PSA blood test, brain MRI, and PET/CT with PSMA. In addition, a consultation by a cardiologist was indicated to define the cause of the arterial pressure decrease and to correct the accompanying pathology. The PSA level was 1.78 ng/ml during the analysis on February 11, 2025. At 1 month later, the PSA level was 1.43 ng/ml during the routine PSA blood test on March 04, 2025.

On March 11, 2025, PET/CT and MRI studies were reconsidered in accordance with collaborative proposals in the National Medical Research Center for Neurosurgery. A diagnosis of “intracranial meningioma” was confirmed, and the accompanying disease of “fibrous dysplasia of the cranial vault” was revealed. Planned brain MRI on March 12, 2025, showed positive dynamics manifested as a reduced lesion size in the outer parts of the left temporal lobe (Figure 2D). Control PET/CT with [68Ga]Ga-PSMA on March 13, 2025, demonstrated positive dynamics (Figure 1B).

Considering the results of the control studies (PET/CT with PSMA and MRI of the brain) and the PSA dynamics, a decision was made to undertake real-time patient monitoring (to monitor the patient in dynamics). Another course of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 therapy was considered unadvisable.

The recommendations for the patient were as follows: regular outpatient consultations by a local oncologist and monthly PSA value control and checkup by accompanying medical specialists. PET/CT with somatostatin receptor (SSTR) was not performed as the target of treatment was mCRPC and not meningioma. Theoretical response of this tumor to [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 therapy would not be evaluated as the main effect.

Discussion

The diagnosis of “intracranial meningioma” was made based on the MRI scans of the brain. Alongside this, histological verification of the diagnosis was unfortunately not performed, thus not allowing us to guarantee the “meningioma” diagnosis. On the other hand, it is well known that meningiomas, as a rule, tend to demonstrate high PSMA uptake values according to the PET/CT findings (18), thus making them appear more similar to metastatic prostate cancer. However, the location, form, and characteristics of the growth of this lesion likely point to meningioma. Regarding the mechanism of PSMA ligand invasion by meningioma, we considered it to be indirectly caused by the active angiogenesis in the tumor tissues and not by its direct membrane expression (19).

The presented clinical case demonstrated the high treatment effectiveness of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 for two different brain tumors—mCRPC and brain meningioma—revealed in one and the same patient. The remarkable positive results achieved have been testified not only by PSA-based metabolic response (Figure 3) and instrumental methods of study (Figures 1, 2) but also by evaluation of the patient’s clinical state. According to the preliminary results of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA-617 RLT, no serious side effects have been noted. Xerostomia is predicted and, in the majority of cases, is an inevitable side effect of PSMA therapy, particularly of [225Ac]Ac-PSMA RLT when xerostomia is revealed in more than 80% of cases (20). This complication in the described case report remained within CTCAE grade 1.

Conclusion

The achieved results demonstrated an evident positive effect of the use of one drug for two different brain tumors: mCRPC and meningioma. The theory of a promising one-time effective use of RLT for PSMA-active lesions of different morphological and histological structures (characteristics) found its practical proof in the described clinical case. Furthermore, this experience shows a further need of more intensive perspective studies on the potential of this type of RLT for a variety of tumors in non-prostatic locations (21).

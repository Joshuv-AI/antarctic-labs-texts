29 | The Impact of Maternal Body Mass Index on Duration of Active Second Stage of Labor

Sara Carlhäll1; Madeleine Larsson2; Marie Blomberg3; Anna Ramö Isgren4

1Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology in Linköping, Region Östergötland, Linköping, Sweden, Linköping, Sweden; 2Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Västervik Hospital, Region Kalmar County, Västervik, Sweden; 3Department of Biomedical and Clinical Sciences, Linköping University, Linköping, Sweden; 4Linköping University, Linköping, Sweden

Objective: Maternal obesity is associated with prolonged first‐stage labor, but findings regarding the the second stage are inconsistent. This study aimed to evaluate the association between maternal body mass index (BMI) and the duration of the active second stage of labor in primiparous and multiparous women.

Study Design: A prospective population‐based cohort study including 33,983 women with singleton, cephalic pregnancies at ≥37 weeks’ gestation who gave birth at seven hospitals in southeast Sweden, from 2016 to 2020. Women were classified into six BMI groups according to WHO criteria. The outcome was duration of the active second stage, defined as time from onset of active pushing to birth. Analyses were stratified by parity. Logistic regression estimated the risk of prolonged active second stage (duration >75th percentile), with normal weight as reference. Cox regression analysed time to birth during the active second stage. Adjustments were made for maternal age, epidural analgesia, oxytocin use and birthweight.

Results: The cohort included 13,532 primiparous and 20,451 multiparous women. Median active second stage duration decreased with increasing BMI, from 35/12 min (primiparous/ multiparous) in normal‐weight women to 22/12 min (primiparous/ multiparous) in women with BMI ≥ 40 (p < 0.001) The 75th percentile of duration was 51/20 min (primiparous/ multiparous). Primiparous women with BMI ≥ 40 had lower risk of prolonged active second stage compared with normal weight women (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 0.24; 95% CI 0.13–0.42). Similar findings were observed in multiparous women (aOR 0.43; 95% CI 0.31–0.60) (Table 1). Cox regression analyses showed higher probability of birth over time in active second stage, with increasing BMI (p < 0.001) (Figure 1).

Conclusion: Higher maternal BMI was independently associated with shorter active second stage duration in both primiparous and multiparous women. These findings suggest that labor progression during the second stage may be more efficient in women with obesity and highlight the importance of considering stage‐specific labor dynamics in intrapartum management.

30 | Skin Adhesive Plus Intra‐Cuticular Suture Versus Suture Alone for Cesarean Skin Closure: Randomized Controlled Trial

Efrat Shekel1; Renana Dimant1; Lauren Reiman2; Ido Pinchas‐Akiva2; Doron Kabiri1

1Hadassah Hebrew University Medical Center and Hebrew University of Jerusalem, Jeruslaem, Israel; 2The Hebrew University of Jerusalem, Israel., Jerusalem, Israel

Objective: To evaluate whether adding skin adhesive to intracuticular sutures for cesarean skin closure reduces postoperative wound complications and improves patient satisfaction and scar appearance in a randomized controlled trial.

Study Design: We conducted a prospective randomized controlled trial including women undergoing elective cesarean delivery at a tertiary medical center between January 2025 to January 2026. Participants were randomized to skin closure with intracuticular suture alone or intracuticular suture following by skin adhesive.

The primary outcome was postoperative wound complications. Secondary outcomes included patient satisfaction and scar appearance. Comparisons between groups were performed using appropriate statistical tests.

Results: Of the 150 randomized participants, 132 were included in the final analysis, 63 (47.3%) in the skin adhesive group and 69 (52.7%) in the intracuticular suture alone group. The groups were comparable in baseline characteristics (Table 1).

Primary and secondary outcomes are shown in Table 2.

Postoperative wound complications occurred in 12 women (19.0%) in the skin adhesive group and 14 (20.3%) in the suture alone group (p = 1.00). The rate of postpartum antibiotic use was similar between the groups.

Patient‐reported satisfaction scores on postoperative day 2 did not differ between groups across all questionnaire domains (mean 46.4 vs 46.5, p = 0.96). At 6 to 8 weeks postpartum, patient‐reported scar assessment (POSAS) scores were similar between groups (mean 25.5 vs 25.7, p = 0.57). Surgeon‐assessed scar appearance using the Vancouver Scar Scale was also comparable (mean 0.81 vs 0.85, p = 0.91).

Conclusion: In this randomized controlled trial, adding skin adhesive to intracuticular suture in cesarean skin closure did not reduce postoperative wound complications or improve patient satisfaction or scar appearance compared with intracuticular suture alone.

31 | Prophylactic Uterine Artery Embolization vs Aortic Balloon Occlusion to Prevent Hemorrhage From Placenta Accreta Spectrum

Jessian L. Munoz1; Jonthan S. Gross1; Amir A. Shamshirsaz1; Hendrik A. Lombaard1; Michael A. Belfort1; Christina C. Reed1; Yamely H. Mendez1; Mira Woods2; Perla Maharajni2; Mikayla Hernandez2; Sarah Chang2; Yasmin A. Lyons2; Jorge E. Lopera2; Onur Turkoglu1; Alexander M. Saucedo1; Kayla E. Ireland2

1Baylor College of Medicine, Houston, TX; 2UT Health San Antonio, San Antonio, TX

Objective: Endovascular procedures may reduce blood loss during cesarean hysterectomy in patients with Placenta Accreta Spectrum disorder (PAS). However, there is limited data on the comparative efficacy of different endovascular approaches. The goal of this study is to evaluate outcomes from prophylactic uterine artery embolization (UAE) vs balloon occlusion of the aorta (BOA) for patients with PAS undergoing cesarean hysterectomy.

Study Design: This cohort analysis included all patients with histologically confirmed placenta increta or percreta who underwent endovascular treatment prior to cesarean hysterectomy at two academic medical centers between January 2020‐June 2025. 123 patients met inclusion criteria, 52 patients underwent BOA (42.2%); 71 patients underwent UAE (57%). Clinical outcomes and maternal radiation exposure were collected from the electronic medical record. Clinical normality was assessed using the Shapiro‐Wilks test and T‐test or Mann Whitney test.

Results: Compared with UAE, BOA was associated with significantly shorter operative time (323 [253, 372] vs 452 [406, 536] min, p < 0.01), less fluoroscopy time (6 [4, 11] vs 1620 [1146, 2316] seconds, p < 0.01) and less maternal radiation exposure (1.9 [0.7, 4.4] vs 3576 [2477, 5026] mGy, p < 0.01). Blood loss was similar between groups (1456 [1007, 1999] vs 1589 [1057, 3000] mL, p = 0.24) as was post‐operative length of stay (4 [3, 4] vs 4 [3, 5] days, p = 0.15).

Conclusion: While BOA and UAE are equally effective at reducing blood loss and length of stay. Yet, BOA is associated with shorter operative time, less fluoroscopy time, and overall, maternal radiation exposure.

32 | Trends in Disparities in Caesarean Delivery Rates Between Migrant and Native‐Born Women: National Study, 2010–2021

Felicia Joinau‐Zoulovits1; Catherine Deneux‐Tharaux2; Camille Le Ray2; Elie Azria2 On behalf of ENP2021 Study Group

1Inserm (the French National Institute of Health and Medical Research), Paris, France; 2Université Paris Cité and Université Sorbonne Paris Nord, Inserm, INRAE, Centre for Research in Epidemiology and Statistics, Obstetric Perinatal and Paediatric Life Course Epidemiology Team OPPaLE, iWISH, F‐75004 Paris, France, Paris, France

Objective: Disparities in caesarean delivery (CD) rates between migrant and native‐born women have been reported in high‐income countries. We assessed temporal trends in these differences in France from 2010 to 2021, according to women's region of birth and timing of CD.

Study Design: Using data from the last three editions of the French National Perinatal Surveys, we included women giving birth to a live‐born infant in 2010 (N = 13,906), 2016 (N = 11,752) and 2021 (N = 10,913), categorised into five geographic regions by country of birth. CD rates were analysed overall and by timing (pre‐labour and intrapartum), and further examined using the Robson classification. Disparities were expressed as rate ratios (RRs), crude and standardised for maternal age, parity and body mass index (BMI).

Results: Between 2010 and 2021, the overall caesarean delivery (CD) rate in France remained stable (20.4% in 2010, 19.4% in 2016, 20.5% in 2021). Across all three surveys, migrant women consistently had higher CD rates than native‐born women (25.0% vs 19.4% in 2010; 23.3% vs 18.5% in 2016; 25.4% vs 19.2% in 2021), with the largest excess among women born in sub‐Saharan Africa (SSA), whose CD rate nevertheless declined from 35.3% in 2010 to 26.8% in 2021. Standardisation for maternal age, parity and BMI did not change these disparities. In Robson analyses, marked differences in contribution of CD were observed in group 5 (previous uterine scar,) and group 10 (≤ 36 weeks). These disparities are driven by both overrepresentation of SSA women in these groups and higher CD rates within them.

Conclusion: Persistent disparities in CD rates are observed between migrant and native‐born women, particularly among women born in SSA, and these differences are not explained by age, parity or BMI. These disparities are partly driven by a higher prevalence of previous CD among SSA women and by higher caesarean rates in this clinical context, highlighting the key role of the first CD. Beyond obstetric risk factors, these findings raise questions about clinical indications and the mechanisms leading to a first CD among SSA women.

33 | Antenatal Perineal Training Device Improves Perineal Flexibility and Reduces Birth‐Related Perineal Injuries: A Randomized Trial

David Desseauve1; Grazia Sabatino2; Fabienne Diaz3; Coralie Cantarel3; Paul Châtel4; Thomas Zuber5; Paola Bourdon5; Sabine Dugelay6; Gonzague Mellerio7

1CHU Grenoble Alpes, Hôpital Couple‐Enfant, Grenoble, France; 2Private practice, Marseille, France; 3Horiana, Bordeaux, France; 4Ste Thérèse, Paris, France; 5MUMMING, Bègles, France; 6Private practice, Paris, France; 7Mumming, Paris, France

Objective: To evaluate whether a 90‐day antenatal perineal training program using a connected medical device (Emagina) improves perineal flexibility and reduces perineal injuries at delivery in nulliparous women compared to standard care.

Study Design: Prospective multicenter randomized controlled trial (IDRCB 2023‐A01980–45). Nulliparous women were randomized to a perineal training device group (n = 41) or standard care (n = 46). The primary endpoint was maximal perineal diameter at day 90, analyzed using a log‐normal generalized linear model adjusted on baseline. Secondary endpoints included perineal injury grade (RCOG‐WHO classification), tears, sutures, and pelvic floor disorders (PFIQ‐7) at 6–8 weeks postpartum among vaginal deliveries (n = 67).

Results: At day 90, mean maximal perineal diameter was 7.5 ± 0.9 cm in the device group vs 6.1 ± 0.9 cm in controls (multiplicative effect 1.233, 95% CI 1.172–1.297, p < 0.0001), with a dose‐response relationship according to compliance: +32.7% for ≥75%, +22.2% for 50–75%, +10.7% for <50%. Notably, perineal massage in the control group (n = 16) did not improve flexibility compared to standard care alone. Among vaginal deliveries, grade 0/1 perineum was observed in 78.8% vs 55.9% (adjusted OR 6.24, 95% CI 1.73–28.49, p = 0.0092). Tears were reduced (48.5% vs 70.6%, p = 0.036) and sutures decreased (54.5% vs 76.5%, p = 0.026). Instrumental deliveries showed a non‐significant trend favoring the device (15.2% vs 32.4%). The PFIQ‐7 pelvic floor disorders summary score was 60.9% lower in the device group at 6–8 weeks postpartum (p = 0.018). No serious device‐related adverse events occurred.

Conclusion: Antenatal perineal training with a connected device significantly increases perineal flexibility with a clear dose‐response effect, outperforms perineal massage alone, and is associated with reduced perineal injuries at delivery and fewer pelvic floor disorders postpartum.

34 | Variation in Term Intrapartum Fetal Heart Rate Tracings and Adverse Outcomes by Race and Ethnicity

Mackenzie Cronin1; Adam Wright, Jr.1; Sneha Paranandi1; Lydia Deaton1; Suneet P. Chauhan1; Aaron W. Roberts2; Kristen A. Cagino2; Rachel L. Wiley3; Fabrizio Zullo4; Hector Mendez Figeuroa2

1ChristianaCare, Newark, DE; 2McGovern Medical School at UTHealth, Houston, TX; 3University of California, San Diego, San Diego, CA; 4Sapienza University of Rome, Rome, Italy

Objective: Racial and ethnic disparities in cesarean delivery, particularly for non‐reassuring (NR) fetal heart rate tracings (FHRT), are well documented, but it remains unclear whether these differences reflect variation in fetal physiology or differences in clinical interpretation and management. We aimed to evaluate whether intrapartum FHRT characteristics differ by self‐reported race and ethnicity (R&E), and to assess associations with cesarean delivery (CD) for NR FHRT and adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes.

Study Design: In this retrospective cohort, physicians blinded to maternal characteristics and outcomes reviewed FHRTs from the 120 minutes preceding delivery in 20‐minute epochs. Term (≥37 weeks), singleton, non‐anomalous pregnancies undergoing labor were included if patients identified as non‐Hispanic White (NHW), non‐Hispanic Black (NHB), or Hispanic. Primary outcome was composite maternal adverse outcome (CMAO). Secondary outcomes included CD for NR FHRT and composite neonatal adverse outcome (CNAO). Adjusted odds ratios (aOR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated.

Results: Of 5160 deliveries, 2683 (52%) met inclusion criteria: 723 (27%) NHW, 1185 (44%) NHB, and 775 (29%) Hispanic. Rates of any decelerations (p = 0.23) and category II (p = 0.35) or III (p = 0.96) tracings did not differ by R&E. NHB patients had higher odds of CD for NR FHRT compared with NHW (11.0% vs 7.2%; aOR 1.82, 95% CI 1.23–2.67), while NHW and Hispanic patients were similar (aOR 1.13, 95% CI 0.74–1.73). CMAO was higher among Hispanic patients (aOR 1.91, 95% CI 1.26–2.88) but not NHB (aOR 1.29, 95% CI 0.86–1.93). CNAO did not differ (p = 0.80).

Conclusion: Despite similar FHRT patterns, disparities exist in CD for NR FHRT and maternal outcomes. These findings suggest differences in clinical interpretation and management, rather than physiologic variation, may contribute to inequities in obstetric care and represent a potential target for standardization and quality improvement.

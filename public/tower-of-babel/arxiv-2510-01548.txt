Laplacian comparison theorems on complete Kähler manifolds and applications
Jiaxuan Fan, Zhiyao Xiong, Xiaokui Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish new Laplacian comparison theorems and rigidity theorems for complete Kähler manifolds under new curvature notions that interpolate between Ricci curvature and holomorphic bisectional curvature.



1.
Introduction

Comparison theorems serve as essential analytical tools in Riemannian geometry, unveiling profound connections between curvature bounds and the geometric-topological structure of manifolds.
Let
(M,g)
be a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}(g)\geqslant(n-1)kg
.
The foundational Laplacian comparison theorem (see, e.g.,
[
Pet16
,
Lee18
]
) asserts that for any fixed point
p\in M
, the distance function
r(x)=d(p,x)
satisfies

(1.1)

\Delta r(x)\leqslant(n-1)\frac{\operatorname{sn}_{k}^{\prime}(r(x))}{\operatorname{sn}_{k}(r(x))}

on
M\setminus\operatorname{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
. Notably, the identity in (
1.1
) holds if and only if the universal cover of
(M,g)
is isometric to a space form.
The Laplacian comparison theorem serves as a fundamental tool for deriving numerous comparison results in Riemannian geometry. For instance, it implies Myers’ diameter theorem, which states that if
\mathrm{Ric}(g)\geqslant(n-1)g
, then
\mathrm{diam}(M,g)\leqslant\mathrm{diam}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{\mathrm{can}})=\pi
. Building upon the Laplacian comparison, Bishop and Gromov (e.g.
[
BC64
]
,
[
CE08
]
) established the volume comparison theorem, a powerful tool in global geometry. This result, in turn, plays a crucial role in proving Cheng’s diameter rigidity theorem
[
Che75
]
, which asserts that if
\mathrm{Ric}(g)\geqslant(n-1)g
and
\mathrm{diam}(M,g)=\pi
, then
(M,g)
is isometric to the round sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{\mathrm{can}})
.
For more details along this comprehensive topic, we refer to
[
CC97
]
,
[
Zhu97
]
and
[
Wei07
]
and the references therein.

In Kähler geometry, the model spaces for comparison are the simply connected Kähler manifolds
M_{c}
with constant holomorphic bisectional curvature
\mathrm{HBSC}\equiv c
.
Li and Wang established in
[
LW05
]
the Laplacian comparison theorem that if
\mathrm{HBSC}\geqslant c
, then for any fixed point
p\in M
, the distance function
r(x)=d(p,x)
satisfies

(1.2)

\Delta r\leqslant 2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(r)}

on
M\setminus\operatorname{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
. This estimate also enjoys rigidity: the identity in (
1.2
) holds if and only if the universal cover of
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to
M_{c}
.
Moreover, they proved that if
\mathrm{HBSC}\geqslant 1
, then
\mathrm{diam}(M,\omega_{g})\leqslant\mathrm{diam}({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
and
\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})\leqslant\mathrm{Vol}({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
, with the identity in the volume comparison holding if and only if
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to
({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
. More recently, Datar and Seshadri
[
DS23
]
established the diameter rigidity theorem, which states that if
\mathrm{HBSC}\geqslant 1
and
\mathrm{diam}(M,g)=\mathrm{diam}({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
, then
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to
({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
. This is achieved by using Siu-Yau’s solution to the Frankel conjecture
[
SY80
]
and an interesting monotonicity formula for Lelong numbers on
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
(
[
Lot21
]
).
To the best of our knowledge, this approach has no counterpart in classical Riemannian geometry.

Utilizing entirely different techniques in algebraic geometry (e.g.
[
Fuj18
]
), Zhang
[
Zha22
]
remarkably established volume comparison and rigidity theorems under Ricci lower bounds:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})\geqslant(n+1)\omega_{g}
. Then

(1.3)

\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})\leqslant\mathrm{Vol}({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}).

Moreover, the identity in (
1.3
) holds if and only if
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to
({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
.

One might naturally consider comparing the diameter
d
and the Laplacian
\Delta_{M}r
of such manifolds with those of the model space
({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
. However, unlike the case where
\mathrm{HBSC}\geqslant 1
, neither of these comparisons holds when the complex dimension
n\geqslant 2
. This is clearly illustrated by the example
\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{2}{3}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right)\times\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{2}{3}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right).
Furthermore, we demonstrate that the model Laplacian comparison (
1.2
) may fail even locally (see Example
5.1
). For more related comparison theorems, we refer to
[
LW05
,
Mun09
,
Liu11
,
TY12
,
Liu14
,
LY18
,
NZ18
,
Zhu22
,
CLZ24+
,
XY24+
,
Yang25+
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we establish new Laplacian comparison theorems and applications for complete Kähler manifolds under new curvature notions that interpolate between Ricci curvature and holomorphic bisectional curvature. Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a Kähler manifold, and let
R
denote its Chern curvature.
One can define the
symmetrized curvature operator

\mathcal{R}:\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)
by the relation

(1.4)

g\left(\mathcal{R}\left(a\right),b\right)=R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}a^{ik}\overline{b}{}^{j\ell}

where
a=\sum a^{ik}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}
and
b=\sum b^{j\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\ell}}
are in
\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)
(see
[
CV60
]
,
[
BNPSW25+
]
and
[
WY25+
]
) . This curvature notion appears naturally in various Bochner-Kodaira formulas. For instance, Wang and the third named author established in
[
WY25+
, Theorem 1.2]
new Bochner-Kodaira formulas with quadratic curvature terms on compact Kähler manifolds: for any
\eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
,

(1.5)

\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}\eta,\eta\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\eta,\eta\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\left(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M}\right)(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}),\mathbb{T}_{\eta}\right\rangle.

This linearized curvature term yields new vanishing theorems and provides estimates for Hodge numbers under exceptionally weak curvature conditions. Please refer to
[
WY25+
]
for further discussion on applications. We say that
(M,\omega_{g})
has
positive symmetrized curvature operator

\mathcal{R}

if it is positive definite as a Hermitian bilinear form.
A straightforward computation shows that the symmetrized curvature operator of
({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
is
\mathcal{R}=2\cdot\mathrm{id}
which is positive definite. On the other hand,
if
\mathcal{R}
is a positive operator, then
(M,g)
has positive holomorphic bisectional curvature. Furthermore, when
M
is compact, it follows from Siu-Yau’s solution to the Frankel conjecture (
[
SY80
,
Mori79
]
) that
M
is biholomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. The following weaker notion on
k
-positivity is natural:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
A
be a Hermitian
n\times n
matrix and
\lambda_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\lambda_{n}
be eigenvalues of
A
. It is said to be
k
-positive if

(1.6)

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}>0.

The symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}:\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M)
is called
k
-positive if
\mathcal{R}
is
k
-positive at every point of
M
. One can define
k
-semi-positivity,
k
-negativity and
k
-semi-negativity in similar ways.

By using a combinatorial computation, one can show that if
(M,\omega_{g})
is a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
and
\mathcal{R}
is
k
-positive with
k\leqslant(n+1)/2
, then it has positive Ricci curvature (Corollary
3.2
). Moreover, if
(M,\omega_{g})
is the hyperquadric in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}
with the induced metric, then
\mathcal{R}
has eigenvalues (e.g.
[
CV60
]
,
[
BNPSW25+
]
and
[
WY25+
]
)

\lambda_{1}=2-n\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{N}=2

where
N=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
. In particular,
\mathcal{R}
is
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)
-positive.

The first main result of this paper is the global Laplacian
comparison theorem for the symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
with
negative
lower bounds:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
and
r
be the distance function from a given point
p\in M
.
If
\mathcal{R}-2c\cdot\mathrm{id}
is
k
-semipositive for some
c<0
and
k\leqslant(n+1)/2
, then

(1.7)

\Delta r\leqslant 2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(r)}

on
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
.
Moreover, if the identity in (
3.12
) holds on
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
,
then the universal cover of
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to the hyperbolic space
M_{c}
.

We also obtain a local Laplacian comparison theorem for
the symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
with

positive
lower bounds:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a
complete Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
and
r
be the
distance function from a given point
p\in M
. If
\mathcal{R}-2c\cdot\mathrm{id}

is
k
-semi-positive for some
c>0
and
k<(n+1)/2
, then

(1.8)

\Delta r\leqslant 2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(r)}

on some metric ball

B(p,C(k,n)/\sqrt{c})\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
. Moreover
if the identity (
1.8
) holds for all such

x
, then
\mathrm{HBSC}\equiv c
on
B(p,C(k,n)/\sqrt{c})
.

Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
are established through the application of a new index theorem (Theorem
2.1
) in combination with a combinatorial curvature synthesis technique. On the other
hand, it is clear that Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
can give
Bishop-Gromov type
local
volume comparison theorem. We also
establish a diameter comparison theorem when
c>0
and
k\leqslant n
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler
manifold of complex dimension
n
. If
{\mathcal{R}}-2c\cdot{\rm id}
is

k
-semi-positive for some
c>0
and
k\leqslant n
, then
M
is compact
and

(1.9)

\mathrm{diam}(M,\omega_{g})\leqslant\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\nu}}\quad\mbox{where}\quad\nu=\frac{2kc}{4k-3}.

Moreover, the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)
of
M
is
trivial.

Recall that the symmetrized curvature operator of the hyperquadric in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
is
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)
-positive. One can observe that the
k
-positivity condition (
k\leqslant n
) in Theorem
1.5
is rather weak. It remains entirely unclear whether such manifolds are Fano or not. Even when
k\leqslant(n+1)/2
, one has
\operatorname{Ric}(\omega_{g})\geqslant c(n+1)\omega_{g}
, which can imply a Myers-type diameter estimate using the underlying Riemannian metric. Crucially, however, the estimate in (
1.9
) is sharper than this Riemannian comparison, as further demonstrated in Section
5
. The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem

1.5
is a weighted Hessian estimate, which relies on the
derived mixed curvature estimate. As is standard in Riemannian
geometry, this estimate implies a diameter bound, which in turn
implies the finiteness of
\pi_{1}(M)
and
b_{1}(M)=0
. By using
vanishing theorems derived in
[
WY25+
]
and the Riemann-Roch
theorem, we conclude that
M
is simply connected.

Furthermore, using similar ideas as in the proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
, we obtain the following result under the conditions holomorphic sectional curvature
\mathrm{HSC}\geqslant c
and Ricci curvature
\operatorname{Ric}\geqslant c(n+1)\omega_{g}
:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
.
If
\mathrm{HSC}\geqslant 2c
and
\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant c(n+1)\omega_{g}
for some
c>0
, then the distance function
r
from a given point
p\in M
satisfies

(1.10)

\Delta r\leqslant 2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(r)}

on
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
. Moreover, if the identity in (
1.10
) holds on
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
,
then
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to the projective space
M_{c}
.

Using the Laplacian Comparison Theorem (Theorem
1.6
), we can derive the following local Bishop-Gromov type volume comparison theorem:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be an
n
-dimensional complete Kähler manifold with
\mathrm{HSC}\geqslant 2c
and
\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant c(n+1)\omega_{g}
for some
c>0
.
Suppose that
B(p,\delta)\subset M
is the metric ball centered at
p\in M
with radius
\delta
, and
{B}(\widetilde{p},\delta)
is a corresponding metric ball in
M_{c}
.
Then the volume ratio

(1.11)

\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(B(p,\delta))}{\mathrm{Vol}(B(\widetilde{p},\delta))}

is non-increasing in
\delta
, and in particular

(1.12)

\mathrm{Vol}(B(p,\delta))\leqslant\mathrm{Vol}(B(\widetilde{p},\delta)).

Moreover, if the identity in (
1.12
) holds for some
\delta>0
, then
\omega_{g}
has constant holomorphic bisectional curvature
c
on
B(p,\delta)
.

As an application of Theorem
1.7
, we provide a
differential geometric proof
of the following volume comparison theorem, which is a special case of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
.
If
\mathrm{HSC}\geqslant 2c
and
\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant c(n+1)\omega_{g}
for some
c>0
, then
M
is compact and

(1.13)

\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})\leqslant\mathrm{Vol}(M_{c}).

Moreover, the identity in (
1.13
) holds if and only if
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to the projective space
M_{c}
.

2.
Index forms on Kähler manifolds

In this section, we introduce a new index form for establishing Hessian or Laplacian comparison theorems on Kähler manifolds.
Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a Kähler manifold.
For a unit-speed geodesic
\gamma:[0,\ell]\to M
, we set

(2.1)

E_{\gamma}=\frac{\gamma^{\prime}-\sqrt{-1}J\gamma^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\in\Gamma\left([0,\ell],\gamma^{*}T^{1,0}M\right).

For any
V,W\in\Gamma\left([0,\ell],\gamma^{*}T^{1,0}M\right)
, there is an index form
{\mathcal{X}}_{\gamma}
of
\gamma
given by

(2.2)

{\mathcal{X}}_{\gamma}\left(V,\overline{W}\right)=\int_{0}^{\ell}\left\langle V^{\prime},W^{\prime}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}R\left(E_{\gamma},\overline{E_{\gamma}},V,\overline{W}\right)dt,

where
V^{\prime}=\widehat{\nabla}_{\frac{d}{dt}}V
and
\widehat{\nabla}
is the complexification of the pullback connection on
\gamma^{*}TM
.
We establish the following version of the Hessian comparison theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold, and
r
be the distance function from a fixed point
p\in M
.
For a given point
x\in M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
and
X\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
, if
\gamma:[0,\ell]\to M
is the unit-speed minimal geodesic connecting
p
and
x
, then

(2.3)

\left(\partial\overline{\partial}r\right)\left(X,\overline{X}\right)\leqslant{\mathcal{X}}_{\gamma}\left(V,\overline{V}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}\left|\left\langle V^{\prime},E_{\gamma}\right\rangle\right|^{2}dt,

where
V\in\Gamma([0,\ell],\gamma^{*}T^{1,0}M)
is a vector field satisfying
V(0)=0
and
V(\ell)=X
. Moreover, the identity in (
2.3
) holds if and only if
V^{\perp}=V-\left\langle V,\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle\gamma^{\prime}
is a Jacobi field.

Proof.

Since
V\in\Gamma([0,\ell],\gamma^{*}T^{1,0}M)
, we assume that
V=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\widetilde{V}-\sqrt{-1}J\widetilde{V}\right)
where
\widetilde{V}
is a real vector field along
\gamma
.
If we set
v=\widetilde{V}(\ell)
, then one has

(2.4)

X=V(\ell)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\widetilde{V}(\ell)-\sqrt{-1}J\widetilde{V}(\ell)\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(v-\sqrt{-1}Jv\right).

A simple calculation shows that

(2.5)

2\left(\partial\overline{\partial}r\right)\left(X,\overline{X}\right)=\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(v,v)+\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(Jv,Jv).

If we write
a=\left\langle v,\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)\right\rangle
and
b=\left\langle v,J\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)\right\rangle
, then
v
has a decomposition

(2.6)

v=a\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)+bJ\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)+v_{0},

where
\left\langle v_{0},\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)\right\rangle=0
and
\left\langle v_{0},J\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)\right\rangle=0
. Moreover,

(2.7)

Jv=-b\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)+aJ\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)+Jv_{0}.

Furthermore, if we choose two normal vectors

(2.8)

v_{1}=bJ\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)+v_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad v_{2}=aJ\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)+Jv_{0},

then one has

(2.9)

\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(v,v)+\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(Jv,Jv)=\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(v_{1},v_{1})+\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(v_{2},v_{2}).

Consider two variational vector fields

(2.10)

U_{1}=\widetilde{V}-\left\langle\widetilde{V},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle\gamma^{\prime}\quad\mbox{and}\quad U_{2}=J\widetilde{V}-\left\langle J\widetilde{V},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle\gamma^{\prime}.

Since
V(0)=0
, one has
U_{1}(0)=U_{2}(0)=0
. Moreover,
\left\langle U_{1},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle U_{2},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle\equiv 0
,

(2.11)

U_{1}(\ell)=v-\left\langle v,\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)\right\rangle\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)=v-a\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)=v_{1},

(2.12)

U_{2}(\ell)=Jv-\left\langle Jv,\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)\right\rangle\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)=Jv+b\gamma^{\prime}(\ell)=v_{2}.

By using the index form theorem in Riemannian geometry, one deduces that

(2.13)

\displaystyle 2\left(\partial\overline{\partial}r\right)\left(X,\overline{X}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(v,v)+\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(Jv,Jv)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(v_{1},v_{1})+\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,r\right)(v_{2},v_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}(U_{1},U_{1})+I_{\gamma}(U_{2},U_{2}).

Since
U_{1}^{\prime}=\widetilde{V}^{\prime}-\left\langle\widetilde{V}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle\gamma^{\prime}
, one has

(2.14)

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}(U_{1},U_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}|U_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}-R(U_{1},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},U_{1})\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}|\widetilde{V}^{\prime}|^{2}-\left\langle\widetilde{V}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}-R(\widetilde{V},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\widetilde{V})\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V})-\int_{0}^{\ell}\left\langle\widetilde{V}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}\,dt.

Similarly, one can derive

(2.15)

I_{\gamma}(U_{2},U_{2})=I_{\gamma}(J\widetilde{V},J\widetilde{V})-\int_{0}^{\ell}\left\langle J\widetilde{V}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}\,dt.

Therefore, one deduces that

(2.16)

\displaystyle 2\left(\partial\overline{\partial}r\right)\left(X,\overline{X}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V})+I_{\gamma}(J\widetilde{V},J\widetilde{V})-\int_{0}^{\ell}\left\langle\widetilde{V}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}dt-\int_{0}^{\ell}\left\langle J\widetilde{V}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V})+I_{\gamma}(J\widetilde{V},J\widetilde{V})-\int_{0}^{\ell}\left|\left\langle V^{\prime},E_{\gamma}\right\rangle\right|^{2}dt,

where we use the elementary fact that

(2.17)

\left\langle\widetilde{V}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}\ +\left\langle J\widetilde{V}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}=\left|\left\langle V^{\prime},E_{\gamma}\right\rangle\right|^{2}.

On the other hand, since

R\left(E_{\gamma},\overline{E_{\gamma}},V,\overline{V}\right)=R\left(\gamma^{\prime},\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V},\gamma^{\prime}\right)+R\left(\gamma^{\prime},J\widetilde{V},J\widetilde{V},\gamma^{\prime}\right),

one obtains

(2.18)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{X}}_{\gamma}\left(V,\overline{V}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}|V^{\prime}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}R\left(E_{\gamma},\overline{E_{\gamma}},V,\overline{V}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{1}{2}|\widetilde{V}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|J\widetilde{V}^{\prime}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}R\left(\gamma^{\prime},\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V},\gamma^{\prime}\right)-\frac{1}{2}R\left(\gamma^{\prime},J\widetilde{V},J\widetilde{V},\gamma^{\prime}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}I_{\gamma}(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V})+\frac{1}{2}I_{\gamma}(J\widetilde{V},J\widetilde{V}),

By (
2.16
) and (
2.18
), one establishes the estimate in (
2.3
).
Assuming the identity in (
2.3
) holds, it follows that the identity in (
2.13
) is satisfied. Consequently, both
U_{1}
and

U_{2}
are Jacobi fields. This implies that
V^{\perp}=V-\left\langle V,\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle\gamma^{\prime}
is a complex Jacobi field. The converse statement is obvious.

The following result is standard and will be invoked repeatedly in subsequent arguments.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold,
p\in M
and
U=M\backslash\mathrm{cut}{(p)}
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

(M,\omega_{g})
has constant holomorphic bisectional
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(2)

Let
\gamma:[0,\ell]\to U
be a unit speed geodesic with
\gamma(0)=p
.

Lemma 3.1

(3.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{i},\overline{E_{i}}\right)\geqslant(n-1)c.

By (

\displaystyle R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{n},\overline{E_{n}}\right)+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{i},\overline{E_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2ck+\left(\alpha-\frac{2(k-1)}{n-1}\right)(n-1)c=2c+\alpha(n-1)c.

Theorem 3.3

\displaystyle k\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{\mathrm{sn}^{\prime}_{c/2}(t)^{2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(\ell)^{2}}\,dt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(t)^{2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(\ell)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{i},\overline{E_{i}}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle k\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{\mathrm{sn}^{\prime}_{c/2}(t)^{2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(\ell)^{2}}\,dt-\frac{ck}{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(t)^{2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(\ell)^{2}}dt=k\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{\prime}(\ell)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(\ell)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Comparison theorems for Ricci curvature and HSC

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.7
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
. If

\mathrm{HSC}\geqslant 2c\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant c(n+1)\omega_{g}

at point
p\in M
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then for any orthonormal basis
E_{1},\cdots,E_{n}\in T^{1,0}_{p}M
and any real number
\alpha\in[0,1]
, one has

(4.1)

R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{n},\overline{E_{n}}\right)+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{i},\overline{E_{i}}\right)\geqslant 2c+\alpha(n-1)c.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{n},\overline{E_{n}}\right)+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{i},\overline{E_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{n},\overline{E_{n}}\right)+\alpha\mathrm{Ric}(E_{n},E_{n})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\cdot 2c+\alpha(n+1)c=2c+\alpha(n-1)c.

This completes the proof.
∎

1.6
here
for readers’ convenience.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a
complete Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
. If

\mathrm{HSC}\geqslant 2c
and
\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant c(n+1)\omega_{g}
for some

c>0
, then the distance function
r
from a given point
p\in M

satisfies

(4.2)

\Delta r\leqslant 2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(r)}

on
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
.
Moreover, if the identity in (
4.2
) holds on

M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
, then
(M,\omega_{g})
is
isometrically biholomorphic to the projective space
M_{c}
.

Proof.

Using the same notation as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Delta r(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}\left\{\frac{\mathrm{sn}^{\prime}_{2c}(t)^{2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(\ell)^{2}}+2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}^{\prime}_{c/2}(t)^{2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(\ell)^{2}}\right\}dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\ell}\left\{\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(t)^{2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(\ell)^{2}}R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{n},\overline{E_{n}}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(t)^{2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(\ell)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}R\left(E_{n},\overline{E_{n}},E_{i},\overline{E_{i}}\right)\right\}dt.

For
t\in[0,\ell]
, we set

(4.4)

\alpha(t):=\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{2}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{2}(\ell)}\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}^{2}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}^{2}(\ell)}\right)^{-1}=\left(\frac{\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}\ell\right)}{\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}t\right)}\right)^{2}.

Since
\mathrm{HSC}\geqslant 2c
, by
[
Tsu57
]
,
\mathrm{diam}(M,\omega_{g})\leqslant\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2c}}
and so
\ell<\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2c}}
. We conclude that
\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}t\right)
is decreasing in
[0,\ell]\subset[0,\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2c}})
and so

(4.5)

\cos^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}\ell\right)=\alpha(0)\leqslant\alpha(t)\leqslant\alpha(\ell)=1.

By using Lemma
4.1
and similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
3.4
, we obtain

\Delta r\leqslant 2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2c}(r)}

on
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
. If the identity in (
4.2
) holds on
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
, then
(M,\omega_{g})
has constant holomorphic bisectional curvature
c>0
and it is isometrically biholomorphic to the projective space
M_{c}
.
∎

5.
Examples

We consider the model space
(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
. It is well-known that it has

(5.1)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})=(n+1)\omega_{\mathrm{FS}},\quad\mbox{\text{and}}\quad\mathrm{diam}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}.

If
r(x)=d(p,x)
is the distance function from a fixed point
p\in{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, then

(5.2)

\Delta_{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}r=2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2}(r)}.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold with Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})\geqslant(n+1)\omega_{g}
. One might naturally consider comparing the diameter
d
and the Laplacian
\Delta_{M}r
of such manifolds with those of the model space.
However, neither of these comparisons can hold when the complex dimension
n\geqslant 2
.

If
(M,\omega_{g})
is regarded as a Riemannian manifold, then by Myer’s diameter estimate and the Laplacian comparison theorem for Riemannian manifolds, one has

(5.3)

\mathrm{diam}(M,\omega_{g})\leqslant\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{4n-2}{n+1}},

and

(5.4)

\Delta_{M}r\leqslant(2n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{c}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{c}(r)},\quad c=\frac{n+1}{2n-1}.

When
n\geqslant 2
, these two upper bounds exceed their counterparts in the model space. Moreover, for the
n
-dimensinal product manifold

(5.5)

(M,\omega_{g})=\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{2}{n+1}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right)\times\cdots\times\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{2}{n+1}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right),

it has
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})=(n+1)\omega_{g}
, and a direct calculation shows that

(5.6)

\mathrm{diam}(M,\omega_{g})=\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{n+1}}>\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}=\mathrm{diam}({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}).

It can also demonstrate that the following global Laplacian comparison can not hold on
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}

(5.7)

\Delta_{M}r\leqslant 2(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2}(r)}.

This becomes evident as
r\nearrow\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}
, where the right-hand side tends to
-\infty
. Moreover, the following example shows that the model Laplacian comparison (
5.7
) may fail even locally. We refer to
[
Liu11
,
TY12
,
Liu14
,
LY18
,
NZ18
]
for more discussions on various examples.

Example 5.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be the product manifold

(5.8)

\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{2}{3}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right)\times\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{2}{3}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right),

and
r
be the distance function from a given point
p\in M
. Then for
0<r<\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}
,

(5.9)

\max_{\{x|d(x,p)=r\}}\Delta_{M}r(x)>2\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2}(r)}.

Before proving (
5.9
), we require the following lemma, which computes the Laplacian of the distance function on such product manifolds.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be the
n
-dimensional product manifold

(5.10)

\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{2}{n+1}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right)\times\cdots\times\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{2}{n+1}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right).

For each
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, let
d_{i}
denote the distance function in the
i
-th factor.
Fix a point
p=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})\in M
and let
r
be the distance function from
p
.
Then at a point
q=(q_{1},\cdots,q_{n})
in
M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(p)\cup\{p\}
, we have

(5.11)

\Delta r(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{r}((n+1)\lambda_{i}^{2})+(n-1)f_{r}(0).

where
\lambda_{i}=d_{i}(p_{i},q_{i})/r(q)
and

(5.12)

f_{r}(x)=\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{x}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{x}(r)}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{x}\cot(\sqrt{x}r),&0<x<\pi^{2}/r^{2},\\
1/r,&x=0.\end{cases}

Moreover,
f_{r}(\bullet)
is concave on
(0,\pi^{2}/r^{2})
for any
r>0
, and

(5.13)

\max_{\{x|d(x,p)=r\}}\Delta r(x)=nf_{r}\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)+(n-1)f_{r}(0).

Proof.

Let
\gamma\colon[0,\ell]\to M
be the unit-speed minimal geodesic joining
p
and
q
, and suppose

(5.14)

\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}).

Let
e_{1}(t),\cdots,e_{2n}(t)
be orthonormal parallel fields along
\gamma
such that

(5.15)

Je_{2i}=e_{2i-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad e_{2i}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\gamma_{i}^{\prime},

for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Here, if
\lambda_{i}=0
for some
i
, then
p_{i}=\gamma_{i}(t)=q_{i}
for all
t\in[0,\ell]
. In this case, we choose
e_{2i}
to be any unit vector in the tangent space to the
i
-th factor at
p_{i}
, and still set
e_{2i-1}=Je_{2i}
.
A direct calculation shows that the Jacobi fields along
\gamma
are given by

(5.16)

V_{2i-1}(t)=\frac{\sin\left(\lambda_{i}\sqrt{(n+1)}t\right)}{\sin\left(\lambda_{i}\sqrt{(n+1)}\ell\right)}e_{2i-1}(t),\quad V_{2i}(t)=\frac{t}{\ell}e_{2i}(t),

where
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. It follows that for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
,

(5.17)

\mathrm{Hess}\;r(e_{2i}(\ell),e_{2i}(\ell))=I_{\gamma}(V_{2i},V_{2i})=f_{\ell}((n+1)\lambda_{i}^{2}),

(5.18)

\mathrm{Hess}\;r(e_{2i}(\ell),e_{2i}(\ell))=I_{\gamma}(V_{2i},V_{2i})-\int_{0}^{\ell}\langle V_{2i}^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)\rangle^{2}dt=(1-\lambda_{i}^{2})\frac{1}{\ell}.

Therefore, we conclude that

(5.19)

\Delta r=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{r}((n+1)\lambda_{i}^{2})+(n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2})\frac{1}{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{r}((n+1)\lambda_{i}^{2})+(n-1)f_{r}(0).

This completes the proof of (
5.11
).

Next, we shall prove that
f_{r}(\bullet)
is concave on
(0,\pi^{2}/r^{2})
for any
r>0
. Its first derivative with respect to
x
is

\displaystyle f_{r}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\cos(\sqrt{x}r)}{2\sqrt{x}\sin(\sqrt{x}r)}-\frac{r}{2\sin^{2}(\sqrt{x}r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\cos(\sqrt{x}r)\sin(\sqrt{x}r)-\sqrt{x}r}{2\sqrt{x}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{x}r)}<0,

and the second derivative is

\displaystyle f_{r}^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\cos(\sqrt{x}r)}{4\sqrt{x^{3}}\sin(\sqrt{x}r)}-\frac{r}{4x\sin^{2}(\sqrt{x}r)}+\frac{r^{2}\cos(\sqrt{x}r)}{2\sqrt{x}\sin^{3}(\sqrt{x}r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2xr^{2}\cos(\sqrt{x}r)-\sqrt{x}r\sin(\sqrt{x}r)-\sin^{2}(\sqrt{x}r)\cos(\sqrt{x}r)}{4\sqrt{x^{3}}\sin^{3}(\sqrt{x}r)}.

If
x\in[\pi^{2}/4r^{2},\pi^{2}/r^{2})
, then
\sqrt{x}r\in[\pi/2,\pi)
. It follows that

\displaystyle 2xr^{2}\cos(\sqrt{x}r)-\sqrt{x}r\sin(\sqrt{x}r)-\sin^{2}(\sqrt{x}r)\cos(\sqrt{x}r)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left(2xr^{2}-\sin^{2}(\sqrt{x}r)\right)\cos(\sqrt{x}r)\leqslant 0,

and so
f_{r}^{\prime\prime}(x)\leqslant 0
.
Thus, to show the concavity, it suffices to show

(5.20)

\phi(\theta)=2\theta^{2}-\theta\tan\theta-\sin^{2}\theta\leqslant 0,

for all
\theta\in(0,\pi/2).
Notice that for
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, we have

(5.21)

\sin\theta\geqslant\theta-\frac{1}{6}\theta^{3},\quad\tan\theta\geqslant\theta+\frac{1}{3}\theta^{3}.

Therefore, for
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
,

(5.22)

\phi(\theta)\leqslant 2\theta^{2}-(\theta^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\theta^{4})-(\theta^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\theta^{4}+\frac{1}{36}\theta^{6})=-\frac{1}{36}\theta^{6}.

This completes the proof of the concavity of
f_{r}(\bullet)
on
(0,\pi^{2}/r^{2})
.
Finally, applying Jensen’s inequality to the right-hand side of (
5.11
), we conclude that the maximum is attained when
\lambda_{i}^{2}=1/n
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. This establishes (
5.13
).
∎

Proof of inequality (
5.9
).

By Lemma
5.2
, we have

(5.23)

\displaystyle g(r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{\{x|d(x,p)=r\}}\Delta_{M}r(x)-\left(2\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}(r)}+\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2}(r)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{3/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{3/2}(r)}+\frac{1}{r}-2\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{1/2}(r)}-\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{2}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{2}(r)}.

Recall that the Laurent expansion of
\cot r
at 0 is given by

(5.24)

\cot r=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{2k}B_{2k}}{(2k)!}r^{2k-1},

where the convergence radius of this expansion is
(0,\pi)
, and
B_{k}
are Bernoulli numbers which satisfy

(5.25)

B_{0}=1,\quad B_{2k-1}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad(-1)^{k}B_{2k}<0

for all
k\geqslant 1
. It follows that the Taylor expansion of
g(r)
at
r=0
is given

(5.26)

g(r)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{2k}B_{2k}r^{2k-1}}{(2k)!}\left(\frac{3^{k}-1}{2^{k-1}}-2^{k}\right),

where the convergence radius of this expansion is
[0,\pi)
. Since the coefficient

(5.27)

\frac{3^{k}-1}{2^{k-1}}-2^{k}

vanishes when
k=1
and is positive for all
k\geqslant 2
, we conclude that
g(r)
is a sum of positive terms. This completes the proof.
∎

Fractional diffusion-wave equations with critical nonlinearities in Lebesgue spaces
Masterson Costa, Claudio Cuevas & Bruno de Andrade

Abstract
This paper focuses on the study of semilinear fractional diffusion-wave equations in the context of critical nonlinearities. Firstly, we address the issue of local well-posedness for the problem, examine spatial regularity, and the continuous dependence of the solutions on initial data. Secondly, we establish the existence of global mild solutions and investigate their asymptotic behavior.



Keywords:
Fractional diffusion-wave equation; Spatial regularity of solutions; Critical non-linearities; Global solutions; Asymptotic stability of solutions.

MSC Classifications 2020:
35R11; 35B33; 35B65.

Motivated by this increasing interest and the above works, in this paper, we study the fractional diffusion-wave equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\Delta u+f(u),\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\penalty\ \mbox{on}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\penalty\ u^{\prime}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \Omega,&\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a smooth bounded domain,
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is Caputo’s fractional derivative. Furthermore,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function that verifies
f(0)=0
and

\displaystyle|f(r)-f(s)|\leq c(|r|^{\rho-1}+|s|^{\rho-1})|r-s|,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall r,s\in\mathbb{R},

(1.2)

for some
c>0
and
\rho>1
. Roughly speaking, we are focused on determining the critical exponent for the equation (
1.1
) when the initial data belongs to the space
L^{q}(\Omega)
, where
1<q<\infty
, or to spaces with lower regularity. The search for critical exponents is a significant theme in the study of various types of evolution equations. For example, in their pioneering paper
[
15
]
, Fujita & Kato search for the largest fractional power space in which a local-existence and uniqueness theorem holds for the Navier-Stokes equation in a bounded smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Fujita
[
16
]
considers the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a nonlinear term satisfying (
1.2
). Taking smooth non-negative initial conditions, he proves that
1+\frac{2}{N}
is critical in the sense that global existence holds for small data if
\rho>1+\frac{2}{N}
and finite time blow-up behavior of solutions holds if
1<\rho<1+\frac{2}{N}
. Also, John
[
18
]
considers the semilinear wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with nonlinearity satisfying (
1.2
), showing that
1+2^{1/2}
plays a critical role in the analysis of global existence for compactly supported initial data. In
[
12
]
, de Andrade & Viana study the strongly damped plate equation

\displaystyle u_{tt}=-\Delta^{2}u+\mu\Delta u_{t}+|u|^{\rho-1}u,\ t>0,\ x\in\Omega,

where
\mu>0
,
\Delta^{2}
is the biharmonic with hinged boundary conditions and
\Delta
is the Dirichlet-Laplacian in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and prove that
1+\frac{4}{N}
is the critical exponent if initial conditions are taken in
\big(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Concerning the fractional diffusion-wave equation, de Andrade & Santos
[
11
]
provided a thorough analysis of the evolution operators associated with (
1.1
) in the fractional power scale related to the Laplace operator. Considering initial data in
L^{q}(\Omega)
, they demonstrate that mild solutions of the nonlinear fractional diffusion-wave equation exhibit an immediate regularization effect, in the sense that these solutions possess greater regularity than the initial data. The results in
[
11
]
are valid for any
1<\rho<1+\frac{2q}{N}
; however, when

\rho=1+\frac{2q}{N},

(1.3)

the technical tools used in their analysis are no longer sufficient to establish the well-posedness for (
1.1
), leaving this case as an open problem. The value (
1.3
) plays a critical role to the study of existence and uniqueness of mild solutions for (
1.1
). Indeed, a combination of
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
and our Theorem
1.1
below ensures that for any
f
satisfying (
1.2
) and initial data
u_{0},u_{1}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1<q<\infty
and
q\geq\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2}
, the problem (
1.1
) is well-posed. However, if
\rho>1+\frac{2q}{N}
, or equivalently
q<\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2}
, and initial data
u_{0},u_{1}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, the problem (
1.1
) has no mild solution for some Lipschitz functions
f
, see Remark
2.3
.

At this point, it is important to observe that (
1.3
) is related with the critical case for the nonlinear heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u+f(u),\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\penalty\ \mbox{on}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \Omega,&\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
f
satisfies (
1.2
) and
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1<q<\infty
. Without being exhaustive, we mention Weissler
[
26
,
27
]
who firstly addressed the subject of existence and nonexistence of solutions to (
1.4
). Brezis & Cazenave
[
7
]
have improved the analysis proving the uniqueness of solutions in a larger class of functions. Furthermore, they highlighted that the value
q=\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2}
plays a critical role to (
1.4

[
2
]
treated the problem by focusing on the non-linear term. Indeed, they introduced the notion of
\epsilon
-regular map and generalized the results of Brezis & Cazenave to an abstract framework. In
[
10
]
, de Andrade et al. extended some of these results to the subdiffusive heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}u=\Delta u+f(u),\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\penalty\ \mbox{on}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \Omega,&\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

where
\partial_{t}^{\gamma}
is Caputo’s fractional derivative,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
f
satisfies (
1.2
) and
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1<q<\infty
. Particularly, the value
q=\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2}
, or equivalently
\rho=1+\frac{2q}{N}
, is, as to the case of the heat equation, the critical exponent to the issue of existence and uniqueness of mild solutions for the problem. These facts and our Theorem
1.1
below ensure that the critical exponent is independent of the time-fractional derivation order, that is, superdiffusive (
1.1
), diffusive (
1.4

1.5

L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1<q<\infty

To be more precise in our discussion, let
\{X^{\gamma}_{q}\}_{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}}
be the scale of fractional powers spaces associated with the realization of the Laplace operator in

Our main local result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<\alpha<\frac{2\phi_{q}}{\pi}
,
1<\rho,q<\infty
, such that
q=\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2}
, and
0<\varepsilon<\frac{N}{N+2q}
, with
\alpha\rho\varepsilon<1
. Then, for all
v_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, there exist
r=r(v_{0})>0
and
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(v_{0})>0
such that the problem (
2.6
) has an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution
u(\cdot\ ;u_{0},u_{1})
defined on
[0,\tau_{0}]
, for all
u_{0},u_{1}\in B_{r}(v_{0})\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
. Moreover, the following statements hold:

(A)

For all
\theta\in[0,\rho\varepsilon)
, we have

u(\cdot\ ;u_{0},u_{1})\in C\left((0,\tau_{0}];X^{1+\theta}_{q}\right)

and, if
J\subset B_{r}(v_{0})
is compact, then

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\sup_{u_{0},u_{1}\in J}\|u(t;u_{0},u_{1})\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0.

(B)

For each
\theta\in[0,\rho\varepsilon)
, there exists a constant
\bar{c}=\bar{c}(\alpha,\rho,\varepsilon,\theta,v_{0})
such that

t^{\alpha\theta}\|u(t;u_{0},u_{1})-u(t;w_{0},w_{1})\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}\leq\bar{c}\left(\|u_{0}-w_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}-w_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right),

for all
t\in(0,\tau_{0}]
, and
u_{0},u_{1},w_{0},w_{1}\in B_{r}(v_{0})\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
.

(C)

If
v
is an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution on some interval
[0,\tau_{1}]
for the problem (
2.6
) satisfying

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|v(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0,

then
v(t)=u(t;u_{0},u_{1})
for all
t\in[0,\min\{\tau_{1},\tau_{0}\}]

(D)

The
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution
u(\cdot\ ;u_{0},u_{1})
can be continued on an interval
[0,\tau_{\mathrm{max}})
, where
\tau_{\mathrm{max}}\in(\tau_{0},\infty]

\tau_{\mathrm{max}}<\infty

\limsup_{t\to\tau_{\textrm{max}}\,^{-}}\|u(t;u_{0},u_{1})\|_{{}_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}}=\infty.

We apply the previous theorem to an illustrative example. Consider the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\Delta u+u^{3},\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\penalty\ \mbox{on}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\penalty\ u^{\prime}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \Omega,&\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

where
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is Caputo’s fractional derivative, for
1<\alpha<\frac{2\phi_{3}}{\pi}
. If
u_{0},u_{1}\in L^{3}(\Omega)
, then there exists a

-valued continuous function

which is the unique
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution to (
1.6
) satisfying

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{3}}=0,

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{3\alpha})

Finally,

can be continued to a maximal time of existence

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\tau_{max}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}|u(t,x)|^{3}dx=+\infty,

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<\alpha<\frac{2\phi_{q}}{\pi}
,
1<\rho,q<\infty
, such that
q=\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2}
, and
0<\varepsilon<\frac{N}{N+2q}

\alpha\rho\varepsilon<1
. If

) that verifies

Theorem 1.3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem

, let
u=u(\cdot\ ;u_{0},u_{1})

\varepsilon
-regular mild solutions to problem (

\lim_{t\to+\infty}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})(u_{0}-v_{0})+S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})(u_{1}-v_{1})\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0

(1.7)

if, and only if,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|u(t)-v(t)\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0.

(1.8)

As application of the above results, we deal with the asymptotic analysis of a concrete situation. Consider the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}u=\Delta u+u\sqrt[3]{u^{4}},\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\penalty\ \mbox{on}\penalty\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\penalty\ u^{\prime}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\penalty\ \mbox{in}\penalty\ \Omega,&\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

where
\Omega
is bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega

\partial_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}
is Caputo’s fractional derivative of order
\frac{3}{2}

u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
u_{1}\in X^{\frac{1}{3}}_{2}
have sufficiently small norm, then for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{2}{7})
, problem (
1.9
) has a globally defined
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution given by

u(t,x)=E_{\frac{3}{2}}(t\mathcal{A}_{2})u_{0}(x)+S_{\frac{3}{2}}(t\mathcal{A}_{2})u_{1}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}R_{\frac{3}{2}}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{2})u(s,x)|u(s,x)|^{\frac{4}{3}}\,ds,\hskip 10.00002ptt>0\ \mbox{and}\ x\in\Omega.

Furthermore, given
\phi,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}

\varepsilon
-regular mild solution of (

\lim_{t\to+\infty}t^{\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}}\left\|u(t)-v(t)\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0.

In fact, taking
\beta<\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle\left\|E_{\frac{3}{2}}(t\mathcal{A}_{2})(u_{0}-v_{0})+S_{\frac{3}{2}}(t\mathcal{A}_{2})(u_{1}-v_{1})\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|E_{\frac{3}{2}}(t\mathcal{A}_{2})\phi+S_{\frac{3}{2}}(t\mathcal{A}_{2})\psi\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{2}}

\displaystyle\left\|E_{\frac{3}{2}}(t\mathcal{A}_{2})\phi\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{2}}+\left\|S_{\frac{3}{2}}(t\mathcal{A}_{2})\psi\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{2}}

and the result follows from Theorem
1.3

\varepsilon
-regular mild solution of (
1.9

2
Abstract setting of the problem and known results

In order to fit the problem in an abstract functional setting let us recall the usual functional spaces involved. Remember that
the operator
L=-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary conditions can be seen as a sectorial operator in
E^{0}_{q}=L^{q}(\Omega)
with domain
E^{1}_{q}=W^{2,q}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
. It is well known that the scale of fractional powers spaces
\{E^{\gamma}_{q}\}_{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}}
associated with
L

checks

\left\{\begin{matrix}E^{\gamma}_{q}\hookrightarrow H^{2\gamma}_{q}(\Omega),&\gamma\geq 0,&1<q<\infty,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \\
E^{-\gamma}_{q}=(E^{\gamma}_{q^{\prime}})^{\prime},&\gamma\geq 0,&1<q<\infty,\penalty\ \penalty\ q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1},\end{matrix}\right.

see
[
1
]
. Therefore, using duality arguments, we obtain

\left\{\begin{matrix}E^{\gamma}_{q}\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega),&r\leq\frac{Nq}{N-2\gamma q},&0\leq\gamma<\frac{N}{2q},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \\
E^{0}_{q}=L^{q}(\Omega),\penalty\ &\\
E^{\gamma}_{q}\hookleftarrow L^{s}(\Omega),&s\geq\frac{Nq}{N-2\gamma q},&-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\gamma\leq 0.\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \end{matrix}\right.

(2.1)

Moreover, the realization of
L
in
E^{\gamma}_{q}
, denoted by
L_{\gamma}
, is an isometry from
E^{\gamma+1}_{q}
into
E^{\gamma}_{q}
,

L_{\gamma}:D(L_{\gamma})=E^{\gamma+1}_{q}\subset E^{\gamma}_{q}\to E^{\gamma}_{q},

a sector operator, and
D(L^{\alpha}_{\gamma})=E^{\gamma+\alpha}_{q}
.

Our goal is to consider (
1.1
) with initial data belonging to
L^{q}(\Omega)
, with
1<q<\infty
and
q=\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2}
. Hence, set
X^{\gamma}_{q}:=E^{\gamma-1}_{q}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\mathcal{A}_{q}:X^{1}_{q}\subset X^{0}_{q}\to X^{0}_{q}
be the operator
L_{-1}
. From (
2.1
),
it follows that the scale of fractional powers spaces associated with
\mathcal{A}_{q}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrc}X_{q}^{\beta}\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega),\ \ r\leq\frac{Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q},\ \ 1\leq\beta<1+\frac{N}{2q},\\
X_{q}^{1}=L^{q}(\Omega),\\
X_{q}^{\beta}\hookleftarrow L^{s}(\Omega),\ \ s\geq\frac{Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q},\ \ 1-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\beta\leq 1.\\
\end{array}\right.

Using this framework, problem (
1.1
) can be rewritten in
X^{0}_{q}
as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{t}^{\alpha}u=\mathcal{A}_{q}u+f(u),\\
u(0)=u_{0},\penalty\ u^{\prime}(0)=u_{1},&\end{array}\right.

(2.6)

where
u_{0},u_{1}\in X^{1}_{q}=L^{q}(\Omega)
.

Before proceeding, we need to be more precise about the meaning of solution to (
2.6
), or equivalently to (
1.1
), we are looking for.
Let
(E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
,
(S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
and
(R_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
be the Mittag-Leffler operators generated by
\mathcal{A}_{q}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A continuous function
u:[0,\tau]\to L^{q}(\Omega)
is an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution to (
2.6
) if
u\in{C}((0,\tau];X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q})
and

u(t)=E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{0}+S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(u(s))\,ds,

for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.

Remembering that
\mathcal{A}_{q}:X^{1}_{q}\subset X^{0}_{q}\to X^{0}_{q}
is a sectorial operator, we can set
\phi_{q}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
such that
\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\arg(z)|\leq\phi_{q},z\neq 0\}\subset\rho(\mathcal{A}_{q})
, see
[
3
, Section 6.5]
.
From now on, we consider
\alpha\in(1,\frac{2\phi_{q}}{\pi})
. Note that if
\eta_{0}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\phi_{q}}{\alpha})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\eta_{0}}
then

\lambda^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}\subset\rho(A_{q})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|(\lambda^{\alpha}-\mathcal{A}_{q})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{q}^{1})}\leq C|\lambda|^{-\alpha}.

In
[
11
]
, the authors prove that the families of bounded linear operators
(E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
,
(S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
and
(R_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
are strongly continuous and admit analytic extensions to suitable sectors of the complex plane. The following smoothing effect property is central to ensure existence and uniqueness of
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution to (

).

Lemma 2.2

(
[
11
]
)
.

Consider
1<\alpha<\frac{2\phi_{q}}{\pi}
and
0\leq\theta<\beta\leq 1
. There exists
M>0
such that

\|E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})x\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}\leq Mt^{-\alpha(1+\theta-\beta)}\|x\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}},\hskip 10.00002pt\|S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})x\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}\leq Mt^{1-\alpha(1+\theta-\beta)}\|x\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}}

and

\|R_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})x\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}\leq Mt^{-1-\alpha(\theta-\beta)}\|x\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}},

for all
x\in X^{\beta}_{q}
and

.

It follows from the above estimate that for all

Remark 2.3
.

We now revisit the discussion on the critical value (

was, considering

Using the linear estimates presented in the above lemma and the locally Lipschitz property, the authors were led to analyze the convergence of the integral

which is equivalent to the fact that
\beta>0
1

1

Note that

. That is, the entire argument collapses when

On the other hand, it is important to highlight that the situation described above is not merely a technical issue. Indeed, the locally Lipschitz property is not a sufficient hypothesis to guarantee the existence of mild solutions for (

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\rho>1
,
1<q<\infty
and

. For all

the function

Proof.

From (

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(v(s))ds-\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}R_{\alpha}((\tau_{0}-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(u(s))ds\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4}+t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}[R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})-R_{\alpha}((\tau_{0}-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})]f(u(s))ds\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(v(s))ds\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}+{Mc\kappa^{\rho}\int_{\tau_{0}/t}^{t}(1-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}ds}\leq 1,

which proves that
\mathcal{S}
is
T
-invariant. Note also that for any
v,w\in\mathcal{S}
, and all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]
, we have

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|Tv(t)-Tw(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{1}}\|R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})[f(v(s))-f(w(s))]\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2Mc{\kappa^{\rho-1}}\bigg(\int_{\tau_{0}/t}^{1}(1-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}ds\bigg)\sup_{s\in(0,\tau_{1}]}s^{{\alpha\varepsilon}}\|v(s)-w(s)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in(0,\tau_{1}]}s^{{\alpha\varepsilon}}\|v(s)-w(s)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}.

Consequently,
T
is a strict contraction on
\mathcal{S}
and, by the Banach fixed point theorem, it has a unique fixed
point
v\in\mathcal{S}
, which is a continuation of
u(\cdot\ ;u_{0},u_{1})
on
[0,\tau_{1}]
.

To prove the uniqueness of the continuation, suppose that
u=u(\cdot\ ;,u_{0},u_{1})
admits another continuation
w
defined on some interval
[0,\tau^{\prime}]
, with
\tau_{1}\geq\tau^{\prime}
. For each
t\in[\tau^{\prime},\tau_{1}]
, we have

\displaystyle\|v(t)-w(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}\|f(v(s))-f(w(s))\|_{X^{\rho\varepsilon}_{q}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bar{c}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}\|v(s)-w(s)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}\,ds,

where
\bar{c}:=Mc\sup_{s\in[\tau_{0},\tau]}\{\|v(s)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}^{\rho-1}+\|w(s)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}^{\rho-1}\}
. It follows from Singular Grönwall’s Inequality that

\|v(t)-w(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0,\penalty\ \forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau^{\prime}].

Since
v(t)=u(t)=w(t)
, for all
t\in[0,\tau_{0}]
, we conclude that
v(t)=w(t)
, for all
t\in[0,\tau^{\prime}]
.

To conclude the proof of (D), let
u=u(\cdot\ ;u_{0},u_{1})
be the
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution of problem (
2.6
) satisfying

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0.

Consider
\tau_{max}>0
its maximal time of existence. Suppose by contradiction that
\tau_{max}<+\infty
and

\sup_{t\in(0,\tau_{max})}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}<+\infty.

Let
(t_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
a sequence in
(0,\tau_{max})
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=\tau_{max}
. Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, without loss of generality, suppose
0<t_{n}<t_{m}<\tau_{max}
. Then,

\displaystyle\|u(t_{m})-u(t_{n})\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|E_{\alpha}(t_{m}\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{0}-E_{\alpha}(t_{n}\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{0}\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}+\|S_{\alpha}(t_{m}\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{1}-S_{\alpha}(t_{n}\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{1}\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t_{m}}R_{\alpha}((t_{m}-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(u(s))\,ds-\int_{0}^{t_{n}}R_{\alpha}((t_{n}-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(u(s))\,ds\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}.

By strong continuity of
(E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
and
(S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
, the two first terms of
the right side in the above inequality go to zero as
m,n\to\infty
. In the same way, the strong continuity of
(R_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q}))_{t\geq 0}
combined with the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem ensure that the third therm goes to
0
as
m,n\to\infty
. Therefore,
(u(t_{n}))_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}
is a Cauchy sequence in a Banach space and thus there exists
\bar{u}\in X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u(t_{n})-\bar{u}\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0
.
With this, we can extend
u
to
[0,\tau_{max}]
obtaining the equality

u(t)=E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{0}+S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(u(s))\,ds,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall t\in[0,\tau_{max}],

which contradicts the maximality of
\tau_{max}
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

In Theorem
1.1
we consider the initial data
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
belonging to
X^{1}_{q}=L^{q}(\Omega)
. However, the same conclusions are true if
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, and the second initial condition has even less regularity. Indeed, if we consider
u_{1}\in X^{\beta}_{q}
, for any
\beta\in(1-\frac{1}{\alpha},1]
, then

\alpha\varepsilon+1-\alpha(1+\varepsilon-\beta)=1-\alpha+\alpha\beta>0

and, consequently,
we can choice
\tau_{0}\in(0,1]
at the beginning of the proof of Theorem
1.1
such that

t^{1-\alpha+\alpha\beta}\|u_{1}\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}}\leq\frac{\mu}{4},

for all
0\leq t\leq\tau_{0}
. The sequence of the proof follows the same steps with some adaptations.

3.2
Global well-posedness and asymptotic behavior

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

This proof is very similar to Part 1 of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Hence, we only point out the differences. Define
\mu>0
by

Mc\mu^{\rho-1}{\bf B}=\frac{1}{4},

where
{\bf B}={B}\left(\alpha\left(\rho\varepsilon-\varepsilon\right),1-\alpha\rho\varepsilon\right)
.
Consider the complete metric space

K=\left\{u\in C((0,\infty),X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}):\sup_{t>0}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}\leq\mu\right\}

with metric
\mathrm{d}(u,v)=\sup_{t>0}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}
. Define the operator

Tu(t)=E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{0}+S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(u(s))\,ds,\hskip 10.00002ptt>0.

The continuity of
Tu:(0,\infty)\to X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}
is proved in the same way as in Theorem
1.1
. The main difference is to ensure that
K
is a
T
-invariant set and a contraction. Indeed, if
t>0
and
M(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}\|_{X^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{q}})\leq\frac{\mu}{4}
, then

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|Tu(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{0}\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}+t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})u_{1}\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\|R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})f(u(s))\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+M\|u_{1}\|_{X^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle Mct^{\alpha\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}\left(s^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(s)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)^{\rho}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}\|_{X^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}_{q}})+Mc\mu^{\rho}{\bf B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{4}+\frac{\mu}{4}<\mu,

which implies that
T:K\to K
is well defined. Furthermore,

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|Tu(t)-Tv(t)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}ds\right)\mathrm{d}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}{\bf B}\right)\mathrm{d}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ \mathrm{d}(u,v).

This shows that
T
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-contraction and the existence of a global mild solution follows from the Banach fixed point theorem. The continuous dependence is proved likewise the proof of Theorem
1.1
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Firstly, note that

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|u(t)-v(t)\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})(u_{0}-v_{0})+S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})(u_{1}-v_{1})\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

(3.1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})\big(f(u(s))-f(v(s))\big)\right\|_{X^{1+\epsilon}_{q}}ds.

If (
1.7
) is true, then the first term of the right side in the above estimate goes to
0
as
t\to+\infty
. To the last term, we have

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\left\|R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})\big(f(u(s))-f(v(s))\big)\right\|_{X^{1+\epsilon}_{q}}ds\leq M\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}\|f(u(s))-f(v(s))\|_{X^{\rho\varepsilon}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq Mc\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}\big(\left\|u(s)\right\|^{\rho-1}_{X^{1+\epsilon}_{q}}+\left\|v(s)\right\|^{\rho-1}_{X^{1+\epsilon}_{q}}\big)\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}s^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}(st)^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(st)-v(st)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}ds.

(3.2)

Let
(t_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
a sequence of real numbers such that
t_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
f_{n}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

f_{n}(s)=(1-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}(st_{n})^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(st_{n})-v(st_{n})\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}},

is a mensurable function with respect to the Borel measure. Furthermore, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{n}
is a nonnegative function, and

f_{n}(s)\leq g(s)=2\mu(1-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall s\in(0,1).

Since
g:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is an integrable function we can conclude that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}(st_{n})^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(st_{n})-v(st_{n})\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\big((st_{n})^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(st_{n})-v(st_{n})\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}\big)ds.

Setting
{\bf L}=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|u(t)-v(t)\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}
, it follows from (
3.1
), (
3.2
), and the above estimate that

0\leq{\bf L}\leq\big(2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}{\bf B}\big){\bf L}\Leftrightarrow 0\leq\big(1-2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}{\bf B}\big){\bf L}\leq 0.

Remembering that
1-2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}{\bf B}>0
, we have

{\bf L}=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|u(t)-v(t)\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}=0.

Reciprocally, suppose
{\bf L}=0
. Then

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})(u_{0}-v_{0})+S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})(u_{1}-v_{1})\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|u(t)-v(t)\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}

\displaystyle+t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|R_{\alpha}((t-s)\mathcal{A}_{q})\big(f(u(s))-f(v(s))\big)\right\|_{X^{1+\epsilon}_{q}}ds

\leq t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|u(t)-v(t)\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}+2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-1-\alpha(\varepsilon-\rho\varepsilon)}s^{-\alpha\rho\varepsilon}(st)^{\alpha\varepsilon}\|u(st)-v(st)\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}ds,

which implies

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}t^{\alpha\varepsilon}\left\|E_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})(u_{0}-v_{0})+S_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{A}_{q})(u_{1}-v_{1})\right\|_{X^{1+\varepsilon}_{q}}\leq{\bf L}+\big(2Mc\mu^{\rho-1}{\bf B}\big){\bf L}=0.

∎

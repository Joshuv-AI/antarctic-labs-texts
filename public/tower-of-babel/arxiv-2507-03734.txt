Two 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} or not two 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} ?
Rafał Filipów, Adam Kwela

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to comparison of two generalizations of the bounding number 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on the set
\omega
of all nonnegative integers.
The bounding number
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{I}}
is the least cardinality of an unbounded set in the poset
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{\mathcal{I}})
where
f\leq^{\mathcal{I}}g
if
\{n:f(n)>g(n)\}\in\mathcal{I}
.
Then the ordinary bounding number
\mathfrak{b}
is equal to
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathrm{Fin}}
and it is not difficult to show that

\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a regular cardinal
and
\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\mathfrak{d}
for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
. In the realm of maximal ideals, the number
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{I}}
can take various values, for instance there is always a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
with

\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathrm{cf}(\mathfrak{d})
and it is consistent that there exists a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
with
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{I}}=\kappa
for any regular uncountable
\kappa\leq\mathfrak{c}

[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
However,
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathfrak{b}
for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
with the Baire property
[
7
,
19
]
which makes the number
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{I}}
dull in the realm of definable ideals.

In the literature there are known at least two other ideal-like versions of the bounding number (see below for definitions) which seem more interesting. Below we describe both those versions.
The aim of the paper is to compare these two versions and to calculate values of these numbers for some ideals. Moreover, as a by-product, we answer a question of Kankaanpää
[
13
]
.

Now we start our description of the first version. By an
\mathcal{I}
-gap
we mean a pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
of subfamilies of
\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that the families
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
\mathcal{I}
-orthogonal
(i.e.
A\cap B\in\mathcal{I}
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
) and there is no
C\subseteq\omega
which
\mathcal{I}
-separates

(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
(i.e. there is no
C\subseteq\omega
with
A\cap C\in\mathcal{I}
and
B\setminus C\in\mathcal{I}
for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
). If
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is an
\mathcal{I}
-gap such that
|\mathcal{A}|=\kappa
and
|\mathcal{B}|=\lambda
, then we say that it is an
\mathcal{I}
-
(\kappa,\lambda)
-gap
.

The study of
\mathrm{Fin}
-gaps has a long history
[
18
]
. For instance, Hausdorff proved that there is a
\mathrm{Fin}
-
(\omega_{1},\omega_{1})
-gap, Rothberger showed that there is a
\mathrm{Fin}
-
(\omega,\mathfrak{b})
-gap and the number
\mathfrak{b}
is the least cardinal
\kappa
such that there is a
\mathrm{Fin}
-
(\omega,\kappa)
-gap.
On the other hand,
Todorčević proved (under PFA) that any
\mathrm{Fin}
-gap is either of type

(\omega_{1},\omega_{1})
or
(\omega,\mathfrak{b})
.
It is obvious that there is no
\mathcal{I}
-gap for a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
.
Recently, there is a growing research on
\mathcal{I}
-gaps for definable ideals
\mathcal{I}
(e.g. Borel or analytic).
For instance, Mazur
[
16
]
proved that there is
\mathcal{I}
-
(\omega_{1},\omega_{1})
-gap for every
F_{\sigma}
ideal
\mathcal{I}
, and Todorčević
[
21
]
showed that
the
\mathcal{I}
-gaps spectrum
for
F_{\sigma}
and analytic P-ideals
\mathcal{I}
contains the
\mathrm{Fin}
-gap spectrum.

Any
\mathcal{I}
-
(\omega,\kappa)
-gap is called an
\mathcal{I}
-Rothberger gap

[
3
]
and the smallest
\kappa
for which there is an
\mathcal{I}
-Rothberger gap is called
Rothberger number of
\mathcal{I}
which we will denote by

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
(we put
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{c}^{+}
in the case there is no
\mathcal{I}
-gap).
The ordinary bounding number
\mathfrak{b}
is equal to
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{Fin})
and
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{c}^{+}

for maximal ideals
\mathcal{I}
.
Moreover, it is known
[
3
, Remark 2.4(6,7)]
that
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}

for analytic P-ideals
\mathcal{I}

and
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}
for
F_{\sigma}
ideals
\mathcal{I}
.
Interestingly,
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\omega_{1}

for some
F_{\sigma}
ideals
\mathcal{I}

[
3
]
, and consequently

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
can be consistently strictly smaller than
\mathfrak{b}
.

Now we describe the second version of the bounding number that we will be interested in. Let
\geq_{\mathcal{I}}
denote the binary relation on
\omega^{\omega}
given by

f\geq_{\mathcal{I}}g\iff\{n\in\omega:f(n)<g(n)\}\in\mathcal{I}.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{I}}
denote the family of all functions
f\in\omega^{\omega}
such that
f^{-1}(\{n\})\in\mathcal{I}
for all
n
. Then
the least cardinality of an unbounded family in the poset
(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{I}},\geq_{\mathcal{I}}\cap(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{I}}\times\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{I}}))
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
.
The ordinary bounding number
\mathfrak{b}
is equal to
\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{Fin})
.
In the realm of maximal ideals, the number

\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
can take various values, for instance it is consistent that there exists a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
with
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\kappa
for any regular
uncountable
\kappa\leq\mathfrak{c}

[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
If
\mathcal{I}
is a P-ideal, then
\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{d}
, and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}
for P-ideals with the Baire property
[
9
]
.
However, there are
F_{\sigma}
ideals
\mathcal{I}
with
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\omega_{1}

[
9
]
, and consequently
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
can be consistently strictly smaller than
\mathfrak{b}
.

Below, we list the main results of this paper.

•

\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
(Theorem
3.2
),

•

It is consistent (Theorems
5.3
and
5.6
) that there are ideals
\mathcal{I}
with

–

\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
,

–

\mathfrak{b}<\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
,

–

\mathfrak{d}<\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
,

–

\mathfrak{b}<\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{c}
.

•

\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\omega_{1}
for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathrm{CONV}\subseteq\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathrm{CTBL}
(Theorem
6.3
).
In particular,

–

\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{CONV})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{CONV})=\omega_{1}
,

–

\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{CTBL})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{CTBL})=\omega_{1}
,

–

\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{CB}_{\alpha})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{CB}_{\alpha})=\omega_{1}
for each
\alpha\geq 2
.

•

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}

for every P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
with the Baire property (Theorem
6.6
).

•

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{NULL})\geq\mathfrak{p}
, and consistently
there is no
\mathrm{NULL}
-
(\omega,\add(\mathrm{NULL}))
-gap (Theorem
7.1
). This answers a question of Kankaanpää
[
13
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

An
ideal
on a set
X
is a nonempty family
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
which is closed under taking finite unions (i.e. if
A,B\in\mathcal{I}
then
A\cup B\in\mathcal{I}
) and subsets (i.e. if
A\subseteq B
and
B\in\mathcal{I}
then
A\in\mathcal{I}
), contains all finite subsets of
X
and
X\notin\mathcal{I}
.
For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
, we write
\mathcal{I}^{+}=\{A\subseteq X:A\notin\mathcal{I}\}
and call it the
coideal of
\mathcal{I}
.

If
X
is a countable infinite set, we consider
2^{X}=\{0,1\}^{X}
as a product (with the product topology) of countably many copies of a discrete topological space
\{0,1\}
(which is homeomorphic to the Cantor space).
By identifying subsets of
X
with their characteristic functions,
we equip
\mathcal{P}(X)
with the topology of the space
2^{X}
and therefore
we can assign topological notions to ideals on
X
.
In particular, an ideal
\mathcal{I}
is
Borel
(
analytic
, has the
Baire property
, resp.) if
\mathcal{I}
is a Borel (analytic, with the Baire property, resp.) subset of
\mathcal{P}(X)
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is a
P-ideal
if for every sequence
\langle A_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of elements of
\mathcal{I}
there is
A\in\mathcal{I}
such that
A_{n}\setminus A
is finite for all
n\in\omega
.

The vertical section of a set
A\subseteq X\times Y
at a point
x\in X
is defined by
A_{(x)}=\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in A\}
.
For ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
on
X
and
Y
respectively, we define the following ideal (called the
Fubini product
of
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
):

\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{J}=\{A\subseteq X\times Y:\{x\in X:A_{(x)}\notin\mathcal{J}\}\in\mathcal{I}\}.

We note that the above construction of the product of ideals also gives an ideal when applied to
\mathcal{I}=\{\emptyset\}
or
\mathcal{J}=\{\emptyset\}
which are not ideals according to the definitions (they do not contain all finite sets). Namely, we will consider the following ideals:

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\}&=\{A\subseteq X\times Y:\{x\in X:A_{(x)}\neq\emptyset\}\in\mathcal{I}\},\\
\{\emptyset\}\otimes\mathcal{J}&=\{A\subseteq X\times Y:A_{(x)}\in\mathcal{J}\text{ for every $x$}\}.\end{split}

In the studies of
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
, we can restrict to
\mathcal{I}
-gaps satisfying an additional property that
\mathcal{A}
is a partition of
\omega

[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
.
Consequently, a necessary condition for the number
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
to be well defined (i.e. to be
\leq\mathfrak{c}
) is the existence of an infinite partition of
\omega
consisting of
\mathcal{I}
-positive sets.
On the other hand, such a partition exists if and only if
\mathcal{I}
is not a direct sum (see page
5
for the definition) of finitely many maximal ideals

[
10
, Proposition 4.3]
.
In particular, such a partition exists for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
with the
hereditary Baire property
(i.e.,
\mathcal{I}\restriction X=\{A\cap X:A\in\mathcal{I}\}
has the Baire property for every
X\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
)
[
10
, Proposition 2.5]
. Note that Borel and analytic ideals have the hereditary Baire property.
The following lemma gives a sufficient condition for
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
to be well defined.

Lemma 2.1
.

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
if and only if
there exists a partition
\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}
of
\omega
such that

A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
for each
n
and

\forall C\subseteq\omega\left[\forall n\,(C\cap A_{n}\in\mathcal{I})\implies\exists B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}(B\cap C=\emptyset\land\forall n\,(B\cap A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}))\right].

Proof.

(
\implies
)
Let
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
be a Rothberger
\mathcal{I}
-gap with
|\mathcal{B}|\leq\mathfrak{c}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}
is a partition of
\omega
into
\mathcal{I}
-positive sets
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
.
Take any
C\subseteq\omega
such that
C\cap A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n
. Since
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is an
\mathcal{I}
-gap, there is
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B^{\prime}=B\setminus C\notin\mathcal{I}
. Then
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
,
B^{\prime}\cap C=\emptyset
and
B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}\subseteq B\cap A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for all
n
.

(
\impliedby
)
Let
\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}
be a partition of
\omega
into
\mathcal{I}
-positive sets with the required property.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B\subseteq\omega:B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}\text{ and }B\cap A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}\text{ for all }n\}
.
The pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is
\mathcal{I}
-orthogonal.
Once we show that
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
cannot be
\mathcal{I}
-separated,

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq|\mathcal{B}|\leq\mathfrak{c}
and the proof will be finished.
Take
C\subseteq\omega
such that
C\cap A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n
. Then there is
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
B\cap C=\emptyset
and
B\cap A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n
.
Since
B\in\mathcal{B}
and
B\setminus C=B\notin\mathcal{I}
,
C
does not
\mathcal{I}
-separate
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.
∎

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
X
is a
P^{+}
-ideal
(
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideal
, resp.) if for any decreasing sequence
\langle A_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of
\mathcal{I}
-positive sets there is
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that

A\setminus A_{n}
is finite
(
A\setminus A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
, resp.)
for every
n
.
Every
F_{\sigma}
-ideal is a
P^{+}
-ideal
[
17
, Lemma 3.2.4]
.
Obviously, every
P^{+}
-ideal is
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
.
On the other hand,
the ideal

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}=\mathrm{Fin}\otimes\mathrm{Fin}=\{A\subseteq\omega\times\omega:A_{(n)}\text{ is finite for all but finitely many $n$}\}

is
P^{+}(\mathrm{Fin}\otimes\mathrm{Fin})
-ideal but it is not a
P^{+}
-ideal
[
8
, Proposition 2.6(2)]
.
On can also show that the ideal

\mathrm{CONV}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]:\text{$A$ has finitely many accumulation points in $[0,1]$}\}

is
P^{+}(\mathrm{CONV})
-ideal but it is not a
P^{+}
-ideal.
Each
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideal has
BW
property (for definition see
[
12
]
), so
the following ideals (which do not have
BW
property
[
11
]
) are not
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideals:

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{d}&=\left\{A\subseteq\omega:\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap n|}{n}=0\right\},\\
\mathrm{NWD}&=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]:\text{$A$ is nowhere dense in $[0,1]$}\}.\end{split}

The following proposition gives a sufficient condition for
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
to be well defined.

Proposition 2.2
.

Each of the following conditions guarantees (separately) that
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
.

(1)

\mathcal{I}
is a
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideal and there exists an infinite partition of
\omega
consisting of
\mathcal{I}
-positive sets.

(2)

There is
X\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
\mathcal{I}\restriction X
is a
P^{+}(\mathcal{I}\restriction X)
-ideal and
there exists an infinite partition of
X
consisting of
\mathcal{I}
-positive sets.

(3)

\mathcal{I}
is a Borel ideals and
and there is
X\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
\mathcal{I}\restriction X
is a
P^{+}(\mathcal{I}\restriction X)
-ideal.

Proof.

(1)
Let
\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}
be a partition of
\omega
such that
A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
for each
n
. We will use Lemma
2.1
. Take any
C\subseteq\omega
with
C\cap A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for all
n
.
Define

D_{n}=\bigcup\{A_{k}\setminus C:k\geq n\}

for each
n
.
Since

D_{n}\supseteq D_{n+1}
and
D_{n}\notin\mathcal{I}
for all
n

and
\mathcal{I}
is a
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
-ideal, there exists
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
B\setminus D_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n
. In particular,
B^{\prime}=B\cap D_{0}\notin\mathcal{I}
. Then
B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}\subseteq B\setminus D_{n+1}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n
and
B^{\prime}\cap C\subseteq D_{0}\cap C=\emptyset
, so

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}

by Lemma
2.1
.

(2) Apply item (1) to the ideal
\mathcal{I}\restriction X
and use the fact that
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I}\restriction X)

[
3
, Remark 2.4(2)]
.

(3) Follows from item (2) and the fact that Borel ideals have the hereditary Baire property (hence there is an infinite partition of
\omega
consisting of
\mathcal{I}
-positive sets
[
10
, Proposition 2.5]
).
∎

Question 2.3
.

Is
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
for each Borel ideal
\mathcal{I}
?

3.
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
is less than or equal to
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})

For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
, we use the following notation:

•

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{I}}
is the family of all sequences
\langle A_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
such that

A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for all
n\in\omega

and

A_{n}\cap A_{k}=\emptyset
for
n\neq k
;

•

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
is the family of all sequences

\langle A_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle\in\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{I}}
with

\bigcup\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}=\omega
.

Using the above notation, we can characterize
the number
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
in the following more technical though useful manner.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
9
, Theorem 3.10]
)
.

For any ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
,

\begin{split}\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\min\left\{|\mathcal{E}|:\mathcal{E}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{I}}\text{ and }\right.&\text{for every $\langle A_{n}\rangle\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}$ there exists $\langle E_{n}\rangle\in\mathcal{E}$ with }\\
&\left.\text{$\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E_{i}\right)\notin\mathcal{I}$}\right\}.\end{split}

With the aid of the above characterization, we can prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\lambda<\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
implies
\lambda<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
.
Let
\lambda<\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
and take families
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
such that
|\mathcal{A}|=\omega
,
|\mathcal{B}|=\lambda
,
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
is a partition of
\omega
and
A\cap B\in\mathcal{I}
for any
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
We have to show that there is
C\subseteq\omega
such that
A\cap C\in\mathcal{I}
and
B\setminus C\in\mathcal{I}
for any
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

For each
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
, we define
E_{A}^{B}=A\cap B
. Then
E_{A}^{B}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
A,B
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{A}
is a partition of
\omega
,
E_{A_{1}}^{B}\cap E_{A_{2}}^{B}=\emptyset
for any distinct
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\{\{E_{A}^{B}:A\in\mathcal{A}\}:B\in\mathcal{B}\}
.
Since
|\mathcal{E}|\leq|\mathcal{B}|=\lambda<\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
,
there is a partition
\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of
\omega
such that
C_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n
, and

\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(C_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{B}_{A_{i}}\right)\in\mathcal{I}

for each
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
We claim that the set

C=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}\right)

separates the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.
First, we observe that

A_{k}\cap C=A_{k}\cap\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}\right)=A_{k}\cap\bigcup_{i<k}C_{i}\in\mathcal{I}

for every
k\in\omega
.
Second, we observe that

\begin{split}B\setminus C&=B\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}\right)=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap B\right)\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}\right)\\
&=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap B\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}\right)=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(E^{B}_{A_{n}}\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}C_{i}\right)\\
&\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(C_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{B}_{A_{i}}\right)\in\mathcal{I}\end{split}

for every
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
∎

\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\omega_{1}

4.
Characterization of the Rothberger number

The characterization of the number
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
from Theorem
3.1
can be rephrased in the following manner:

\begin{split}\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\min\left\{\kappa:\right.&\exists_{\{E_{n}^{\alpha}:n<\omega\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}}\,\left[\left(\forall_{\alpha<\kappa}\,\forall_{n\in\omega}\,E^{\alpha}_{n}\cap\bigcup_{m\neq n}E^{\alpha}_{m}=\emptyset\right)\land\right.\\
&\left.\left.\left(\forall_{\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}}\,\exists_{\alpha<\kappa}\,\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\notin\mathcal{I}\right)\right]\right\}.\end{split}

In Theorem
4.1
, we show that the Rothberger number
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
can be characterized in a similar manner as the above characterization of
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
.
It is worth to stress that the
only
difference between the above characterization of

\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
and the below characterization of
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})

lies in the fact that
in the former we only require

E^{\alpha}_{n}\cap\bigcup_{m\neq n}E^{\alpha}_{m}=\emptyset

for each
\alpha
separately,
whereas in the latter we will require disjointness for all
\beta
simultaneously, i.e.

E^{\alpha}_{n}\cap\bigcup_{\beta<\kappa}\left(\bigcup_{m\neq n}E^{\beta}_{m}\right)=\emptyset.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any ideal
\mathcal{I}
,

\begin{split}\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\min\left\{\kappa:\right.&\left.\exists_{\{E_{n}^{\alpha}:n<\omega,\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}}\,\left[\left(\forall_{\alpha<\kappa}\,\forall_{n\in\omega}\,E^{\alpha}_{n}\cap\bigcup_{\beta<\kappa}\left(\bigcup_{m\neq n}E^{\beta}_{m}\right)=\emptyset\right)\land\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.\left(\forall_{\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}}\,\exists_{\alpha<\kappa}\,\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\notin\mathcal{I}\right)\right]\right\}.\end{split}

Proof.

(\geq)

Let
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
be an
\mathcal{I}
-gap of type
(\omega,\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I}))
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a partition of
\omega
.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\}
.
We define

E_{n}^{\alpha}=A_{n}\cap B_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}

for each
n<\omega
and
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
.
Then
E_{n}^{\alpha}\cap E_{m}^{\beta}=\emptyset
for each
n\neq m
and arbitrary
\alpha,\beta<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
. Take any
\{D_{n}:n<\omega\}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
. To finish the proof, we have to show that

\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\notin\mathcal{I}

for some
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
.
Let us define

C=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(A_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i<n}D_{i}\right).

Since
C\cap A_{n}\subseteq\bigcup_{i<n}D_{i}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n
and
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is an
\mathcal{I}
-gap, there exists
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
with

B_{\alpha}\setminus C\notin\mathcal{I}
.
We claim that

B_{\alpha}\setminus C\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right).

Indeed, take
x\in B_{\alpha}\setminus C
and let
n\in\omega
be such that
x\in A_{n}
. Since
x\notin C
,
x\notin\bigcup_{i<n}D_{i}
. Then there is
k\geq n
such that
x\in D_{k}
.
Consequently,

x\in D_{k}\cap A_{n}\cap B_{\alpha}=D_{k}\cap E^{\alpha}_{n}\subseteq D_{k}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq k}E^{\alpha}_{i}\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right).

(\leq)

Let
\{E_{n}^{\alpha}:n<\omega,\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}

be such that
E_{n}^{\alpha}\cap E_{m}^{\beta}=\emptyset
for each
\alpha,\beta<\kappa
and
n\neq m

and for any
\{D_{n}:n<\omega\}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
there is
\alpha<\kappa
with

\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\notin\mathcal{I}.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}
, where

A_{n}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}E^{\alpha}_{n}\text{\ \ and\ \ }B_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{n<\omega}E^{\alpha}_{n}

for each
n\in\omega
and
\alpha<\kappa
.
If we show that
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is an
\mathcal{I}
-
(\omega,\lambda)
-gap and
\lambda\leq\kappa
, the proof will be finished. Since
A_{n}\cap B_{\alpha}=E^{\alpha}_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
, the families
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
\mathcal{I}
-orthogonal.
Obviously
|\mathcal{B}|\leq\kappa
. We claim that

|\mathcal{A}|=\omega
. Indeed, otherwise
A_{n}=\emptyset
for all but finitely many
n
, so there is
k
such that
E^{\alpha}_{n}=\emptyset
for each
\alpha
and
n\geq k
. Consequently, for any
\{D_{n}:n<\omega\}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
and any
\alpha<\kappa
we have

\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\subseteq\bigcup_{n<\omega}E^{\alpha}_{n}\in\mathcal{I},

a contradiction. Finally, we show that no
C
can
\mathcal{I}
-separate
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
. Take any
C\subseteq\omega
such that
A_{n}\cap C\in\mathcal{I}
for any
n
.
We need to find
\alpha<\kappa
such that
B_{\alpha}\setminus C\notin\mathcal{I}
.
We define
D^{\prime}_{n}=C\cap A_{n+1}
for each
n\in\omega
. Let
\omega\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}D^{\prime}_{n}=\{a_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. Now we define
D_{n}=D^{\prime}_{n}\cup\{a_{n}\}
for each
n
. Then
\{D_{n}:n\in\omega\}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
, so there is
\alpha<\kappa

with

\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\notin\mathcal{I}.

Once we show that

\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\subseteq(B_{\alpha}\setminus C)\cup(A_{0}\cap C),

then
B_{\alpha}\setminus C\notin\mathcal{I}
,
because

A_{0}\cap C\in\mathcal{I}
.
Since

\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}=B_{\alpha},

we have

\begin{split}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)&=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\setminus C\\
&\cup\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\cap C\\
&\subseteq(B_{\alpha}\setminus C)\cup\left(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\cap C\right).\end{split}

Now we show that

C\cap\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\subseteq A_{0}\cap C.

Indeed,
using the facts
that

A_{n+1}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}=\emptyset
for each
n
,

B_{\alpha}\cap(\omega\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}A_{n})=\emptyset

and

\{a_{n}:n\in\omega\}=\left(\omega\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}A_{n}\right)\cup A_{0}\cup\left(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}A_{n}\setminus C\right),

we obtain

\begin{split}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(D_{n}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\cap C&=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left((D^{\prime}_{n}\cup\{a_{n}\})\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\cap C\\
&=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(((A_{n+1}\cap C)\cup\{a_{n}\})\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\cap C\\
&=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(\{a_{n}\}\cap\bigcup_{i\leq n}E^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\cap C\\
&\subseteq\{a_{n}:n\in\omega\}\cap B_{\alpha}\cap C\\
&=\left(\left(\omega\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}A_{n}\right)\cup A_{0}\cup\left(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}A_{n}\setminus C\right)\right)\cap B_{\alpha}\cap C\\
&=A_{0}\cap B_{\alpha}\cap C.\end{split}

∎

We have either

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{c}^{+}
for ideals
\mathcal{I}
for which the value

for some ideal
\mathcal{I}

[

In Theorem

we show that consistently the value of

can be strictly greater than

Question 5.1

Is it consistent that there exists a
Borel

\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}

Lemma 5.2
.

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})

for any ideal
\mathcal{I}

Proof.

The second equality is proved in
[
9

and the inequality
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\})\geq\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\})

3.2
.
Below, we show
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
.

Since,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}(\geq_{\mathcal{I}}\cap(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{I}}\times\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{I}}))
, there exists
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{I}}

|\mathcal{F}|=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
and for each

f\in\mathcal{F}

f\not\geq_{\mathcal{I}}g

Define
A_{n}=\omega\times\{n\}
for each
n\in\omega

B_{f}=\{(n,k)\in\omega^{2}:f(n)\leq k\}
for each
f\in\mathcal{F}

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{f}:f\in\mathcal{F}\}

is an
(\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\})
-
(\omega,\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I}))

Obviously,
|\mathcal{A}|=\omega
and
|\mathcal{B}|=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})
. Since
f\in D_{\mathcal{I}}
,
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n\in\omega
.
Then

Thus, the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is
\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\}
-orthogonal.

Finally, we have to show that no
C
can
\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\}
-separate the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

Let
C\subseteq\omega\times\omega
be such that
A_{n}\cap C\in\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\}
for each
n
.
Then all horizontal sections of the set

belongs to
\mathcal{I}

n&\text{if }i\in C^{(n)}\setminus\bigcup_{k<n}C^{(k)}.\end{cases}

would separate
(\mathcal{A}^{0},\mathcal{B}^{0})
, a contradiction. Similarly we can show that
\mathcal{A}^{1}
is infinite, provided that
(\mathcal{A}^{1},\mathcal{B}^{1})
is a
\mathcal{J}
-gap.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

If
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
are two maximal ideals such that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{J})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{K})
, then

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{J}\oplus(\mathcal{K}\otimes\{\emptyset\}))=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{K})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{J}\oplus(\mathcal{K}\otimes\{\emptyset\}))=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{J}).

Proof.

Denote
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{J}\oplus(\mathcal{K}\otimes\{\emptyset\})
. By
[
9
, Theorems 5.1 and 5.13]
we have

\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\min\{\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{J}),\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{K}\otimes\{\emptyset\})\}=\min\{\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{J}),\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{K})\}=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{J}),

and by Lemmas
5.2
and
5.4
we have

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\min\{\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{J}),\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{K}\otimes\{\emptyset\})\}=\min\{\mathfrak{c}^{+},\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{K})\}=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{K}).

∎

If
\mathcal{I}
is a maximal ideal, then
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}<\mathfrak{c}^{+}=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
. In Theorem
5.6
we show that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
is also consistent.

Theorem 5.6
.

In the model obtained by adding
\lambda
Cohen reals to a model of GCH, for every two regular cardinals
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}
such that
\aleph_{1}\leq\kappa\leq\kappa^{\prime}<\lambda=\mathfrak{c}
, there is an ideal
\mathcal{I}
with
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\kappa
and
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\kappa^{\prime}
. In particular, the following statements are consistent:

(1)

there exists an ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
;

(2)

there exists an ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathfrak{b}<\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})<\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

By
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
, in the model obtained by adding
\lambda
Cohen reals to a model of GCH, for every regular cardinals
\aleph_{1}\leq\kappa\leq\kappa^{\prime}<\lambda
there are two maximal ideals
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
such that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{J})=\kappa
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{K})=\kappa^{\prime}
. Then Lemma
5.5
finishes the proof.
∎

6.
Technical generalization of the Rathberger number

Below we define the number
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})
which can be seen as a convenient tool for studying
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
.
For instance, we will use it to calculate
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
for ideals which are between
\mathrm{CONV}
and
\mathrm{CTBL}
(Section
6.1
) and to study
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
for P-ideals with the Baire property
(Section
6.2
).

Let
\mathcal{I}
,
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
be ideals on
\omega
.
By an
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})
-
(\kappa,\lambda)
-gap
we mean
a pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
such that
|\mathcal{A}|=\omega
,
|\mathcal{B}|=\lambda
, the families
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
\mathcal{K}
-orthogonal and
there is no
C\subseteq\omega
which
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{I})
-separates

(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
i.e.
A\cap C\in\mathcal{J}
and
B\setminus C\in\mathcal{I}
for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
The smallest
\kappa
for which there is an
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})
-
(\omega,\kappa)
-gap will be denoted by

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})
and we put
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})=\mathfrak{c}^{+}

in the case there is no such gaps.

The following lemma shows that
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})
is piecewise monotone: decreasing with respect to the last coordinate and increasing with respect to the first and second one.

Lemma 6.1
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
, then
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})\geq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K}^{\prime})
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
, then
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}^{\prime},\mathcal{K})
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
, then
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K})
.

Proof.

Straightforward.
∎

6.1.
Rothberger number for ideals which are between
\mathrm{CONV}
and
\mathrm{CTBL}

Lemma 6.2
.

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{CTBL},\mathrm{CTBL},\mathrm{CONV})\leq\omega_{1}
,
where

\mathrm{CTBL}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]:|\overline{A}|\leq\omega\}

and the closure
\overline{A}
is taken in the space
[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
\{U_{n}:n\in\omega\}
be a countable base for the topology on
[0,1]
.
Inductively, we pick infinite sets

A_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap\left(U_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}\overline{A_{i}}\right)

such that
\overline{A_{n}}
is a perfect nowhere dense set,
\overline{A_{n}}\subseteq U_{n}
and

\overline{A_{n}}\cap\bigcup_{i<n}\overline{A_{i}}=\emptyset.

For all
n\in\omega
and
\alpha<\omega_{1}
,
we pick convergent sequences
x^{n,\alpha}:\omega\to A_{n}
having pairwise distinct limits
y^{n,\alpha}
.
Then, we define

B_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\{x^{n,\alpha}(i):i\in\omega\}

for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
. Once we show that
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is an
(\mathrm{CTBL},\mathrm{CTBL},\mathrm{CONV})
-
(\omega,\omega_{1})
-gap, the proof will be finished.

Obviously,
|\mathcal{A}|=\omega
and
|\mathcal{B}|=\omega_{1}
.
Since
A_{n}\cap B_{\alpha}=\{x^{n,\alpha}(i):i\in\omega\}\in\mathrm{CONV}
for any
n
and
\alpha
, the families
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
\mathrm{CONV}
-orthogonal.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
be such that
A_{n}\cap C\in\mathrm{CTBL}
for each
n
.
We need to find
\alpha
with
B_{\alpha}\setminus C\notin\mathrm{CTBL}
. For each
n
, since
A_{n}\cap C\in\mathrm{CTBL}
, there is
\alpha_{n}<\omega_{1}
such that

y^{n,\alpha}\notin\overline{A_{n}\cap C}
for each
\alpha>\alpha_{n}
.
Take any countable ordinal
\alpha>\sup\{\alpha_{n}:n<\omega\}
.
Then
y^{n,\alpha}\notin\overline{A_{n}\cap C}
for each
n
, so
x^{n,\alpha}(i)\notin C
for all but finitely many
i\in\omega
.
Consequently,
x^{n,\alpha}(i)\in B_{\alpha}\setminus C
for all but finitely many
i\in\omega
, hence
y^{n,\alpha}\in\overline{B_{\alpha}\setminus C}
. Since
y^{n,\alpha}\in U_{n}
,
\overline{B_{\alpha}\setminus C}=[0,1]
. Thus,
B_{\alpha}\setminus C\notin\mathrm{CTBL}
.
∎

For a countable ordinal
\alpha
,
we have the following
\alpha
-th Cantor-Bendixson ideal

[
17
]

\mathrm{CB}_{\alpha}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]:(\overline{A})^{(\alpha)}=\emptyset\},

where
the closure
\overline{A}

and the
\alpha
-th Cantor-Bendixson derivative
(\overline{A})^{(\alpha)}

are taken in the space
[0,1]
(see e.g.
[
14
]
).
It is not difficult to see that

\mathrm{CB}_{0}=\{\emptyset\}
,
\mathrm{CB}_{1}=\mathrm{Fin}
,
\mathrm{CB}_{2}=\mathrm{CONV}

and

\mathrm{CTBL}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}\mathrm{CB}_{\alpha}.

Theorem 6.3
.

If
\mathrm{CONV}\subseteq\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathrm{CTBL}
, then
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\omega_{1}
.
In particular,

(1)

\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{CONV})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{CONV})=\omega_{1}
,

(2)

\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{CTBL})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{CTBL})=\omega_{1}
,

(3)

\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{CB}_{\alpha})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{CB}_{\alpha})=\omega_{1}
for each
\alpha\geq 2
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.2

and the fact that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})\geq\omega_{1}
for each
\mathcal{I}

[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
,
we obtain

\omega_{1}\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})
. Now using Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
,
we obtain

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{CTBL},\mathrm{CTBL},\mathrm{CONV})\leq\omega_{1}
.
∎

6.2.
Rothberger number for P-ideals

It is known
[
3
, Theorem 2.3(7)]
that
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}
for all analytic P-ideals. Below we extend this result to all P-ideals with the Baire property.

Lemma 6.4
.

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin})=\mathfrak{b}

for every ideal
\mathcal{I}
with the Baire property.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
, we have
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin})
. We will show that
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin})\leq\mathfrak{b}
for ideals
\mathcal{I}
with the Baire property.

Since
\mathcal{I}
has the Baire property,
there exists a sequence
m_{1}<m_{2}<\dots
such that
if there are infinitely many
k
with
[m_{k},m_{k+1})\subseteq A
, then
A\notin\mathcal{I}

[
20
, Théorème 21]
(see also
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.2]
).

Take any partition
\{D_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of
\omega
into infinite sets and any unbounded family
\{f_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{b}\}
in
(\omega,\leq^{*})
.

Define

A_{n}=\bigcup_{k\in D_{n}}[m_{k},m_{k+1})\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ B_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\bigcup_{k\in D_{n}\cap f_{\alpha}(n)}[m_{k},m_{k+1})

for each
n\in\omega
and
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{n}:n\in\omega\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{b}\}
.
Once we show that
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is an
(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin})
-
(\omega,\kappa)
-gap with some
\kappa\leq\mathfrak{b}
, the proof will be finished.

Using the fact that
D_{n}
are pairwise disjoint, we see that
|\mathcal{A}|=\omega
.
Obviously,
|\mathcal{B}|\leq\mathfrak{b}
. Moreover, for any
n
and
\alpha
,

A_{n}\cap B_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{k\in D_{n}\cap f_{\alpha}(n)}[m_{k},m_{k+1})\in\mathrm{Fin}.

Thus
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
\mathrm{Fin}
-orthogonal.

Finally, take any
C\subseteq\omega
such that
A_{n}\cap C\in\mathrm{Fin}
for each
n
. We need to find
\alpha
such that
B_{\alpha}\setminus C\notin\mathcal{I}
. Let
f:\omega\to\omega
be defined by

f(n)=\min\{k\in D_{n}:(A_{n}\cap C)\cap[m_{k},m_{k+1})=\emptyset\}.

Let
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}
be such that the set
E=\{n\in\omega:f(n)<f_{\alpha}(n)\}
is infinite. Then
[m_{f(n)},m_{f(n)+1})\subseteq B_{\alpha}\setminus(A_{n}\cap C)
for each
n\in E
.
Since
[m_{f(n)},m_{f(n)+1})\subseteq A_{n}
,

[m_{f(n)},m_{f(n)+1})\subseteq B_{\alpha}\setminus C
for each
n\in E
.
Consequently,
B_{\alpha}\setminus C\notin\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin})

for each P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
,
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin})
. To finish the proof, we will show that
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin})\leq\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin})
for every P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
be an
(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin})
-
(\omega,\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I},\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Fin}))
-gap. Then
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
\mathrm{Fin}
-orthogonal. Take any
C\subseteq\omega
such that
A\cap C\in\mathcal{I}
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
. Since
\mathcal{I}
is a P-ideal and
\mathcal{A}
is countable, there exists
D\in\mathcal{I}
such that
(A\cap C)\setminus D\in\mathrm{Fin}
for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
C^{\prime}=C\setminus D
. Since
A\cap C^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Fin}
for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
with
B\setminus C^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{I}
. Then
B\setminus C=(B\setminus C^{\prime})\setminus D\notin\mathcal{I}
(as
D\in\mathcal{I}
), so
C
does not
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I})
-separate
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mathfrak{b}

for each P-ideal
\mathcal{I}
with the Baire property.

Proof.

The inequality
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\leq\mathfrak{b}
follows from Lemmas
6.4
and
6.5
. The inequalities
\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathcal{I})\geq\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})\geq\mathfrak{b}
follow from Theorem
3.2
and the fact that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})\geq\mathfrak{b}
for P-ideals
[
9
, Theorem 4.5]
.
∎

7.
Rothberger number for the ideal
\mathrm{NULL}

Kankaanpää
[
13
]
proved that

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{NWD})=\add(\mathcal{M})
,
where
\add(\mathcal{M})
is the least cardinal
\kappa
such that there exists a family of
\kappa
meager subsets of the real line which union is not meager.
In particular, there is a
\mathrm{NWD}
-
(\omega,\add(\mathcal{M}))
-gap. He also asked
[
13
, Question 4]
whether
there is a
\mathrm{NULL}
-
(\omega,\add(\mathcal{N}))
-gap,
where
\add(\mathcal{N})
is the least cardinal
\kappa
such that there exists a family of
\kappa
Lebesgue null sets which union is not Lebesgue null,
and

\mathrm{NULL}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]:\text{$\overline{A}$ has Lebesgue measure zero}\}

where the closure
\overline{A}
is taken in the space
[0,1]
.
The following theorem shows that the
pseudointersection number

\mathfrak{p}
(see e.g.
[
2
, Definition 6.2]
) is a lower bound on the
Rothberger number for the ideal
\mathrm{NULL}
and consequently answers the above question.

Theorem 7.1
.

\mathfrak{b}_{R}(\mathrm{NULL})\geq\mathfrak{p}
.
In particular, it is consistent that there is no
\mathrm{NULL}
-
(\omega,\add(\mathcal{N}))
-gap.

Proof.

It is known
[
15
, Corollary 3.7(b)]
that
\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{NULL})
, so Theorem
3.2
finishes the proof of the inequality.
It is consistent that
\add(\mathcal{N})<\mathfrak{p}

[
2
, Section 11.1]
, so the proof of “in particular” part is finished.
∎

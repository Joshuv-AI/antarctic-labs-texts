Maximum gap in complex Ginibre matrices
Patrick Lopatto, Moritz Otto

Abstract
. We determine the asymptotic size of the largest gap between bulk eigenvalues in complex Ginibre matrices.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 60K35. Secondary 60G55, 60D05.

1.
Introduction

Random matrices have been studied for decades due to their rich mathematical structure and applications in quantum physics, statistics, and engineering. The behavior of their eigenvalues, and especially the spacings between eigenvalues, is a central topic. Due to recent advances, there is now a mature theory that identifies the distribution of a single eigenvalue gap for a broad class of random matrix models
[
19
,
20
]
.
However, much is less known about the largest and smallest eigenvalue gaps, in part because studying these observables requires understanding the entire collection of gaps simultaneously. Besides being fundamental examples in the extreme value theory of strongly correlated systems, extremal eigenvalue gaps have attracted attention due to a conjectural link to the Riemann zeta function.
Numerical evidence suggests that the largest and smallest spacings of zeros of the zeta function and the corresponding extremal eigenvalue gaps are identically distributed (after appropriate rescaling)
[
46
,
8
]
. This connection can be seen as an extremal version of Montgomery’s pair correlation conjecture
[
44
]
.

In the literature on extremal eigenvalue gaps, the majority of works have concentrated on models with one-dimensional spectrum. The classical Gaussian random matrix ensembles, which include the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE), Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE), and Gaussian Symplectic Ensemble (GSE), are paradigmatic
examples.
The correlation functions for these ensembles admit explicit formulas, allowing their eigenvalue behavior to be studied through direct computations. As a result, the distributions of the smallest gaps are known for each of the classical Gaussian ensembles
[
52
,
8
,
22
,
21
]
. The problem of determining the distributions of the largest gaps seems more challenging. Currently, these distributions are known only for the GUE; they were determined in
[
23
]
, following earlier work in
[
8
]
that obtained the asymptotic size of the largest gaps. Further, it was shown in
[
11
]
that these results on the largest and smallest gaps are universal in the sense that they hold for all Wigner matrices, assuming the entry distributions posses a density satisfying a weak smoothness condition.
1
1

1

Roughly speaking, Wigner matrices are symmetric random matrices whose upper-triangular entries are independent with mean zero and variance one.

In the case of the largest gap, universality without the smoothness condition was shown in
[
36
]
; this result encompasses, for example, Bernoulli random matrices.
Very recently, universality for the smallest gap was extended to a large class of matrices containing non-smooth distributions, include those with sufficiently many atoms
[
53
]
.

The non-symmetric analogues of the classical Gaussian ensembles are the Ginibre ensembles
[
27
,
12
]
, including the orthogonal Ginibre ensemble (GinOE) and unitary Ginibre ensemble (GinUE). These have two-dimensional spectra, each asymptotically supported on the unit disk in the complex plane.
The smallest gaps distribution of the GinUE was determined in
[
49
]
, and the analogous result for the GinOE was established in
[
40
]
. However, even the asymptotic size of the largest gaps for such ensembles has not been rigorously understood, much less their asymptotic distributions. In this paper, we determine the asymptotic order of the largest gap between bulk eigenvalues of the GinUE, including the leading constant factor. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first such result for any random matrix ensemble with independent, identically distributed entries.

1.1.
Proof ideas

We consider an auxiliary point process, denoted
\Xi_{n}
below, that consists of the set of
n
GinUE eigenvalues thinned to retain only those points whose near-neighbor spacing is greater than a certain distance
r_{n}
, defined precisely below.
Our main result, Theorem
1
, states that on any set
B\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
\sup_{z\in B}|z|<1
, the Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance between
\Xi_{n}
and a suitably chosen Poisson process
\zeta_{c}
tends to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Routine arguments then allow one to deduce the leading-order asymptotic for the nearest-neighbor spacing in
B
, stated in Corollary
2
.

Our approach is similar to
[
47
]
, which determined the leading-order asymptotic for the largest gap for the limiting process of the GinUE eigenvalues. However,
[
47
]
focused on deriving a general Poisson process approximation result for stabilizing functionals of determinantal point processes. The error terms in this approximation are relatively straightforward to bound for the limiting point process by exploiting its stationarity. Our emphasis is different. We rely on the simpler Poisson approximation result
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
, but face new technical challenges because the eigenvalue process of the GinUE is not stationary.
In particular, we would like to apply
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
to
\Xi_{n}
, but that result requires to control the symmetric difference of
\Xi_{n}
and its reduced Palm version at every
z\in B
, which seems difficult to accomplish. However, due to the stabilization property of the eigenvalue process, we can construct a coupling (with a certain monotonicity property) of these processes after restricting them to a set that omits a small ball around
z
. By adapting the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
, we show that the small set where we cannot construct a coupling can be neglected, then bound the error terms arising on the complementary large set (without relying on stationarity, in contrast to
[
47
]
). These bounds rely on large deviations estimates for the probability that a given region contains at most two GinUE eigenvalues.

2.
Model and main results

We work on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}
. We denote by
\mathbf{N}
the space of all
\sigma
-finite counting measures on
\mathbb{C}
and by
\widehat{\mathbf{N}}
the space of all finite counting measures on
\mathbb{C}
.
We equip
\mathbf{N}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{N}}
with their corresponding
\sigma
-fields
\mathcal{N}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
, which are induced by the maps
\omega\mapsto\omega(B)
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
. A
point process
is a random
element
\nu
of
\mathbf{N}
, defined over some fixed
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
.
The
intensity measure
of
\nu
is the
measure
\mathbb{E}[\nu]
defined by
\mathbb{E}[\nu](B)=\mathbb{E}[\nu(B)]
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
.
For all
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
r>0
, let
B_{r}(z)
be the closed Euclidean ball with radius
r
around
z
. For all
B\in\mathcal{B}
, we write
|B|
for the Lebesgue measure of
B
.

Recall that the complex Ginibre matrix of dimension
n
is an
n\times n
matrix whose entries are independent, identically distributed random variables, with common distribution equal to the standard complex Gaussian distribution; this means that their real and imaginary parts are each Gaussian with mean zero and variance
1/2
.
We let
\xi_{n}
denote the point process formed by the
n
eigenvalues of the
n
-dimensional Ginibre matrix.
We remark that with the scaling we have chosen, the largest modulus of an element of
\xi_{n}
is roughly
\sqrt{n}
.

Recall that a point process
\nu
is called a
determinantal point process with correlation kernel
K
on
\mathbb{C}
if for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and pairwise disjoint
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\in\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\nu(A_{1})\cdots\nu(A_{m})]=\int_{A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{m}}\det(K(z_{i},z_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\dots\,\mathrm{d}z_{m},

where
\mathrm{d}z_{i}
denotes integration with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
,
(K(z_{i},z_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m}
is the
m\times m
-matrix with entry
K(z_{i},z_{j})
at position
(i,j)
, and
\det M
is the determinant of the complex-valued
m\times m
-matrix
M
.
Then
\nu
has correlation functions of all orders, and the
m
-th order correlation function
\rho_{\nu}^{(m)}
is given by

\rho^{(m)}_{\nu}(z_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\det(K(z_{i},z_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m},\quad z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{C},\,\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 4.3.10]
) that
\xi_{n}
is a determinantal point process with kernel

(1)

K_{n}(z,w)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi_{k}(z)\overline{\varphi_{k}(w)},\quad\text{where }\quad\varphi_{k}(z):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi(k-1)!}}z^{k-1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}}.

In this article, we study the largest gap in the process
\xi_{n}
. To do so, we let
\kappa>1
be a parameter and consider the point process-valued function

(2)

\displaystyle\Xi_{n}[\omega]:=\sum_{z\in\omega\cap B_{\sqrt{n}}(0)}\mathds{1}\{(\omega\setminus\{z\})(B_{r_{n}(z)}(z))=0\}\,\delta_{z/\sqrt{n}},\quad\omega\in\mathbf{N},

where the sequence
(r_{n}(z))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is given by

(3)

\displaystyle r_{n}(z):=\inf\{r>0:\,n\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}^{z!}(B_{r}(z))=0)\rho_{n}(z)\leq\kappa\},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Here
\rho_{n}(z):=K_{n}(z,z)
is the one-point correlation function for
\xi_{n}
, and
\xi_{n}^{z!}
denotes the reduced Palm process of
\xi_{n}
at
z
(see Section
3
for its definition).
We define
\Xi_{n}=\Xi_{n}[\xi_{n}]
and let
L_{n}(A)=\mathbb{E}[\Xi_{n}(A)]
denote the intensity measure of
\Xi_{n}
, where
A\subset\mathbb{C}
is assumed to be measurable. While
\Xi_{n}
,
r_{n}
, and
L_{n}
depend on
\kappa
, we omit this dependence in the notation.

We study the Kantorovich–Rubinstein (KR) distance between the distributions of
\Xi_{n}
and a certain finite Poisson process. We recall the definition of the KR distance from
[
18
]
. For finite point processes
\zeta
and
\nu
on
\mathbb{C}
, we have

(4)

\displaystyle{d_{\mathrm{KR}}}(\zeta,\nu):=\sup_{h\in\text{Lip}}\big|\mathbb{E}[h(\zeta)]-\mathbb{E}[h(\nu)]\big|,

where
Lip
is the class of all measurable 1-Lipschitz functions
h\colon\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to the total variation between measures
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
on
\mathbb{C}
, which is given by

\displaystyle d_{\text{TV}}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):=\sup|\omega_{1}(A)-\omega_{2}(A)|,

where the supremum is taken over all
A\in\mathcal{B}
with
\omega_{1}(A),\omega_{2}(A)<\infty
.

We now state our two results, which are proved in Section
5
. The first shows that
\Xi_{n}
may be approximated by a Poisson process at a resolution small enough to capture the behavior of the largest eigenvalue gap.

Theorem 1
.

Fix
s\in(0,1)
,
\epsilon>0
, and
\kappa>1
, and recall that
\xi_{n}
denotes the point process of eigenvalues for the
n
-dimensional complex Ginibre matrix, and that
\Xi_{n}:=\Xi_{n}[\xi_{n}]
is the process defined at (
2
).
There exists
C(s,\epsilon,\kappa)>1
such that for all
B\subset B_{1}(0)
satisfying
\sup_{z\in B}|z|<s
, we have for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
that

(5)

\displaystyle{d_{\mathrm{KR}}}(\Xi_{n}\cap B,\zeta_{\kappa}\cap B)\leq Cn^{\epsilon-1/16},

where
\zeta_{\kappa}
is a Poisson process with intensity measure
L_{n}
.
Further, we have for all
\kappa>1
that as
n\to\infty
,

(6)

\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\Big|\frac{r_{n}(z)^{4}}{4\log n}-1\Big|\to 0.

Our second result is a consequence of the previous theorem. It obtains the asymptotic scaling of the largest distance from a particle in
\xi_{n}
to its nearest neighbor. This scaling is different than the one for independent points, which is order
\log n

[
16
]
.

Corollary 2
.

For all
B\subset B_{1}(0)
such that
\sup_{z\in B}|z|<1
, we have that as
n\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{4\log n}\max_{z\in\xi_{n}\cap\sqrt{n}B}\min_{w\in\xi_{n}\setminus\{z\}}|z-w|^{4}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}1.

3.1.
Palm calculus

We begin by introducing three point processes that will play fundamental roles in the proof of Theorem
1
.

Definition 3
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
. The following equalities are assumed to hold for all measurable
f\colon\mathbb{C}\times\mathbf{N}\to[0,\infty)
.

(a)

\xi_{n}^{z}
is a Palm process of
\xi_{n}
at
z
if it satisfies

(7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{B_{\sqrt{n}}(0)}f(z,\xi_{n})\xi_{n}(\mathrm{d}z)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\sqrt{n}}(0)}\mathbb{E}\big[f(z,\xi_{n}^{z})\big]\rho_{n}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

(b)

\Xi_{n}^{z}
is a Palm process of
\Xi_{n}
at
z
if it satisfies

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{B_{1}(0)}f(z,\Xi_{n})\Xi_{n}(\mathrm{d}z)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(0)}\mathbb{E}\big[f(z,\Xi_{n}^{z})\big]L_{n}(\mathrm{d}z).

(c)

\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}
is a Palm process of
\xi_{n}
with respect to
\Xi_{n}
at
z
if it satisfies
2
2

2

The superscripted

(\xi_{n}\setminus\{z\})(B_{r_{n}}(z))=\varnothing

.

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{B_{1}(0)}f(z,\xi_{n})\Xi_{n}(\mathrm{d}z)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(0)}\mathbb{E}\big[f(z,\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing})\big]L_{n}(\mathrm{d}z).

The processes
\xi_{n}

It follows from standard results that for all

and

, there exist point processes
\xi_{n}^{z}
,
\Xi_{n}^{z}
, and
\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}
satisfying parts (a), (b), and (c) of Definition

, respectively

almost surely, and parallel claims hold for

and
\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}
.

Remark 4
.

Comparing
(
8
) and (

), we find that that
\Xi_{n}[\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}]
is a Palm process of

at

. Indeed, if we choose
\tilde{f}(x,\omega):=f(x,\Xi_{n}[\omega])
in (

We write
P_{n}
,

and

for the distributions of

,
\xi_{n}^{z}
and
\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}
, respectively, and
P_{n}^{z!}
and
P_{n}^{z!,\varnothing}
for the distributions of the associated reduced Palm processes. For brevity, we write

.

For every

,
\xi_{n}^{x!}
is a determinantal process with correlation kernel

K_{n}^{x}(z,w)=K_{n}(z,w)-\frac{K_{n}(z,x)K_{n}(x,w)}{K_{n}(x,x)},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{C}

Lemma 5
.

For every
s\in(0,1)
and
\kappa>1
, there exists
C(s,\kappa)>1
such that for all

and

(11)

n\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}^{z!}(B_{r_{n}}(z))=0)\rho_{n}(z)=\kappa.

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that

uniformly as

for all sequences

such that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\leq\rho_{n}(z)\leq\frac{1}{\pi}.

Using that

\mathbb{P}(\xi^{z!}_{n}(B_{r}(z))=0)=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{B_{r}(z)^{c}}\dots\int_{B_{r}(z)^{c}}\det(K^{z}_{n}(z_{i},z_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m}\,dz_{1}\dots dz_{m}.

.

For every

3.2.
Stochastic domination

By
[
29
, Theorem 3]
(see also
[
43
]
), the process
\xi_{n}^{x!}
is stochastically dominated by
\xi_{n}
(denoted by
P_{n}^{x!}\leq P_{n}
), which means that

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(\xi_{n}^{x!})]\leq\mathbb{E}[F(\xi_{n})]

for every measurable
F\colon\mathbf{N}\to\mathbb{R}
that is bounded and increasing; the latter condition means that
F(\omega_{1})\leq F(\omega_{2})
if
\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{2}
.

An important property of determinantal point processes is that they have negative associations (see
[
42
, Theorem 3.7]
). We say that a point process
\nu
has negative associations if

(15)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}[F(\nu)G(\nu)]\leq\mathbb{E}[F(\nu)]\mathbb{E}[G(\nu)],}

for any real, bounded, and increasing functions
F,G\colon\mathbf{N}\to\mathbb{R}
that are measurable with respect to complementary subsets (see
[
37
]
).

We also require the following lemma concerning stochastic domination.

Lemma 7
.

For every
s\in(0,1)
and
\kappa>1
, there exists
C(s,\kappa)>1
such that for all
n\geq C
,
we have for
L_{n}
-almost all
z\in B_{s}(0)
that

(16)

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z,\varnothing}|_{B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c}}\geq P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z}|_{B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c}}.

Proof.

Let
F\colon\mathbf{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded and increasing. For all Borel sets
A
with
\sup_{z\in A}|z|<s
we have

\displaystyle\int_{A}\mathbb{E}[F(\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z,\varnothing}\cap B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c})]L_{n}(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\sqrt{n}A}F(\xi_{n}\cap B_{r_{n}}(z)^{c})\mathds{1}\{(\xi_{n}-\delta_{z})(B_{r_{n}}(z))=0\}\xi_{n}(\mathrm{d}z)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\sqrt{n}A}\mathbb{E}\big[F(\xi_{n}^{z}\cap B_{r_{n}}(z)^{c})\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}^{z!}(B_{r_{n}}(z))=0\}\big]\rho_{n}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=n\int_{A}\mathbb{E}\big[F(\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z}\cap B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c})\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}(B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z))=0\}\big]\rho_{n}(\sqrt{n}z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq n\int_{A}\mathbb{E}\big[F(\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z}\cap B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c})\big]\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}(B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z))=0)\rho_{n}(\sqrt{n}z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\mathbb{E}\big[F(\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z}\cap B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c})\big]L_{n}(\mathrm{d}z),

where we have used (
8
), Lemma
5
, and a change of variables in the first line, (
7
) in the second line, a change of variables in third line, and (
15
) together with the fact that
\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}
is a determinantal process (and thus has negative associations) for the inequality in the fourth line.
The fifth line follows from (
3
) and Lemma
5
. This gives the claim.
∎

3.3.
Coupling of
P_{n}
and
P_{n}^{z!}

For a fixed
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we now specify a coupling of
P_{n}
and
P_{n}^{z!}
.
This will help us bound the KR distance of
\Xi_{n}
and a certain Poisson process in Section
5
.
By
[
43
, Theorem 1]
, there exists a coupling
(\xi_{n},\xi_{n}^{z!})
of
\xi_{n}
and
\xi_{n}^{z!}
such that
\xi_{n}^{z!}\subset\xi_{n}
and
|\xi_{n}^{z!}\setminus\xi_{n}|\leq 1
almost surely. The hypotheses of
[

, Theorem 1]
are straightforward to check using the representation (
1
).
We will always use this coupling when discussing the relationship between
\xi_{n}
and
\xi_{n}^{z!}
in what follows. Moreover,
[
43
, Theorem 1]
also explicitly gives the conditional distribution of
\xi_{n}
conditional on
\xi_{n}^{z!}
.

Similarly, for any distinct
w,z\in\mathbb{C}
, there exists a coupling of
\xi_{n}^{z!}
and
\xi_{n}^{z!,w!}:=(\xi_{n}^{z!})^{w!}
. The hypotheses of
[
43
, Theorem 1]
may be checked in this case using (
10
).

Additionally, under the conditions of Lemma
7
, we specify a coupling of
P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}
and
P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!,\varnothing}
on
B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c}
. We use in coupling in
Section
5
to construct a coupling of
\Xi_{n}
and
\Xi_{n}^{z!}
outside of a ball around
z
.
We do not use
[
43
, Theorem 1]
for this purpose, since its hypotheses are harder to check in this case, and we instead settle for a weaker statement.
Fix
z\in B_{1}(0)
and
\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}\sim P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}
, and observe (
16
) gives that
P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}\leq P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!,\varnothing}
on
B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c}
(for sufficiently large
n
).
By Strassen’s theorem (see
[
38
]
), there exists a measure
\rho\sim P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}\otimes P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!,\varnothing}
supported on
\{\omega_{1}\otimes\omega_{2}\in\mathbf{N}\times\mathbf{N}:\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{2}\}
. By

,
\rho
can be disintegrated and, hence, there exists a point process
\xi_{n}^{z!,\varnothing}\sim P_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!,\varnothing}
such that, almost surely,

(17)

\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!}|_{B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c}}\subset\xi_{n}^{\sqrt{n}z!,\varnothing}|_{B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)^{c}}.

Combining the above couplings gives a joint coupling of

3.4.
Fast decay of correlation

We begin with a preliminary bound on
K_{n}(z,w)
.

Lemma 8
.

For all
B\subset B_{1}(0)
such that
\sup_{z\in B}|z|<1
, there exists a constant
c(B)>0
such that

\sup_{z,w\in B}\big|K_{n}(\sqrt{n}z,\sqrt{n}w)\big|\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2}|z-w|^{2}\right)+c^{-1}e^{-cn}.

Proof.

By the definition of
K_{n}
and
[
28
, Lemma 4.1]
,

\displaystyle K_{n}(\sqrt{z},\sqrt{w})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-n(|z|^{2}+|w|^{2})/2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{n^{k-1}(z\bar{w})^{k-1}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-n(|z|^{2}+|w|^{2})/2}\left(e^{nz\bar{w}}-\frac{e^{nz\bar{w}}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\frac{(z\bar{w}e^{1-z\bar{w}})^{n}}{1-z\bar{w}}\big(1+R_{n}(z,w)\big)\right),

where
R_{n}
satisfies

\sup_{z,w\in B}\big|R_{n}(z,w)\big|\leq Cn^{-1}

for some constant
C>1
.
We note that

|e^{-n(|z|^{2}+|w|^{2})/2}e^{nz\bar{w}}|=e^{-n(|z|^{2}+|w|^{2})/2}e^{n\Re(z\bar{w})}=e^{-n|z-w|^{2}/2}.

Further, for the second-order term,

|e^{-n(|z|^{2}+|w|^{2})/2}(z\bar{w})^{n}e^{n}|\leq e^{-n(|z|^{2}-\ln|z|^{2}-1+|w|^{2}-\ln|w|^{2}-1)/2}.

The conclusion now follows from the elementary fact that there exists
c(B)>0
such that

x-1-\ln x>c

for
x
such that
0\leq x\leq\sup_{z\in B}|z|
, and the fact that
|1-z\bar{w}|
is uniformly lower bounded for
z,w\in B
.
∎

The following lemma concerns a mixing property of
\xi_{n}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\rho^{(m)}
be the
m
-th order correlation function of
\xi_{n}
. Fix
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
and set
m=p+q
.
For all
B\subset B_{1}(0)
such that
\sup_{z\in B_{1}(0)}|z|<1
, there exist constants
n_{0}(B),c(B)>0
such that for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{p+q}
,

(18)

\displaystyle\big|\rho^{(p+q)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p+q})-\rho^{(p)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\rho^{(q)}(x_{p+1},\dots,x_{p+q})\big|\leq m^{1+m/2}\left(e^{-ns^{2}}+c^{-1}e^{-cn}\right),

where

s=\inf_{i\in\{1,\dots,p\},j\in\{p+1,\dots,p+q\}}|x_{i}-x_{j}|.

Proof.

Recall that for any determinantal point process with kernel
K(x,y)
, the restriction of the process to some set
S
is also determinantal, with kernel
K(x,y)\mathds{1}_{S}(x)\mathds{1}_{S}(y)
.
Then the conclusion is an immediate consequence of
[
7
, (3.4.5)]
and Lemma
8
.
∎

3.5.
Infinite Ginibre process

Let
\xi
be the (stationary) infinite Ginibre process on
\mathbb{C}
with kernel

K(z,w)=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|w|^{2}+z\bar{w}}.

Since
K_{n}(z,w)\to K(z,w)
as
n\to\infty
, we have that
\xi_{n}\to\xi
in distribution (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 11.1.VII]
).

The following result is essentially a well-known theorem of Kostlan
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
, but we take the statement from
[
9
, Theorem 4.3.10]
to match our choice of scaling. Let
(X_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a collection of independent random variables such that
X^{2}_{k}\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(k,1)
, where we use a shape–scale parameterization for the gamma distribution.

Lemma 10
.

The set of absolute values of the points of
\xi
has the same distribution as
(X_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
(considered as a random set).

We note that by definition,
X^{2}_{k}
has the same distribution as sum of the squares of
k
independent random variables, each having density
f(x)=e^{-x}
for for
x\geq 0
. Note that
|f(x)|\leq 1
for all
x\geq 0
, and the density
g_{k}
of
X^{2}_{k}
is the convolution of
f
and the density
g_{k-1}
of
X^{2}_{k-1}
. Then we have

(19)

\|g_{k}\|_{\infty}=\|g_{k-1}\ast f\|_{\infty}\leq\|g_{k-1}\|_{1}\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1,

so the density of each
X_{k}
is uniformly bounded by
1
.

4.
Auxiliary statements

We begin by relating vacuum probabilities for the infinite Ginibre process to their analogues for the finite Ginibre process.

Lemma 11
.

For every
s\in(0,1)
, there exists
C(s)>1
and
\delta(s)<1
with the following property. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\geq C
and any sequence of measurable sets
A_{n}\subset B_{s\sqrt{n}}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(\xi(A_{n})=0)\leq\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(A_{n})=0)\leq(1+C\delta^{n})\mathbb{P}(\xi(A_{n})=0).

Proof.

We begin with a standard determinantal representation for the vacuum probability
\mathbb{P}(\xi(A_{n})=0)
(see, e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem 6]
).
For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the determinantal structure of
\xi_{m}
implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\xi_{m}(A_{n})=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\dots\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\det(K_{m}(z_{i},z_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m}\,dz_{1}\dots dz_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\dots\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\det(\varphi_{k}(z_{i}))_{i,k=1}^{m}\det(\overline{\varphi_{k}(z_{i})})_{i,k=1}^{m}\,dz_{1}\dots dz_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\dots\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in S_{m}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{\sigma_{1}(i)}(z_{i})\overline{\varphi_{\sigma_{2}(i)}(z_{i})}\,dz_{1}\dots dz_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\varphi_{i}(z)\overline{\varphi_{\sigma(i)}(z)}\,dz

\displaystyle=\det\left(\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\varphi_{i}(w)\overline{\varphi_{j}(w)}dw\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq m},

where
S_{n}
is the set of all permutations of
\{1,\dots,n\}
. As noted in the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 6]
, this representation implies
\mathbb{P}(\xi_{m}(A_{n})=0)
is decreasing in
m
and converges to
\mathbb{P}(\xi(A_{n})=0)
as
m\to\infty
, giving the first inequality of the assertion. We give the details here for completeness.

Let

M_{m}(U):=\Big(\int_{U^{c}}\varphi_{i}(w)\overline{\varphi_{j}(w)}dw\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq m},

so that we have
\mathbb{P}(\xi_{m}(A_{n})=0)=\det(M_{m}(A_{n}))
.
For every
U\subset\mathbb{C}
,
M_{m}(U)
is the integral of a positive-definite matrix function, so it too is positive-definite.
In particular
M_{m}(A_{n})
is positive-definite, so by Fischer’s inequality
[
32
, Theorem 7.8.5]
,

\det(M_{m}(A_{n}))\leq\det(M_{m-1,n})\left(\int_{A_{n}^{c}}\varphi_{m}(w)\overline{\varphi_{j}(w)}dw\right)\leq\det(M_{m-1,n}),

as desired.

For the second assertion, note that for all
m\geq n
, the Schur complement formula implies that

(20)

\displaystyle\det M_{m}(A_{n})=\det(M_{n}(A_{n}))\det(M_{m}(A_{n})/M_{n}(A_{n})),

where
M_{m}(A_{n})/M_{n}(A_{n})
is the Schur complement of the block
M_{n}(A_{n})
of the matrix
M_{m}(A_{n})
.
By our assumption that
A_{n}\subset B_{s\sqrt{n}}
, we have
M_{m}(A_{n})\succeq M_{m}(B_{s\sqrt{n}})
, since

M_{m}(A_{n})-M_{m}(B_{s\sqrt{n}})=M_{m}\big(A_{n}\cup B_{s\sqrt{n}}^{c}\big)\succeq 0,

where
A\succeq B
means that
A-B
is positive-definite for
m\times m
-matrices
A,B
.
Then, by the monotonicity of the Schur complement
[
32
, 7.7.P41]
,

\displaystyle\det(M_{m}(A_{n})/M_{n}(A_{n}))\geq\det(M_{m}(B_{s\sqrt{n}})/M_{n}(B_{s\sqrt{n}})),\quad m\geq n.

As
B_{s\sqrt{n}}
is a circular domain, we have

\int_{B_{s^{\prime}\sqrt{n}}^{c}}\varphi_{i}(w)\overline{\varphi_{j}(w)}\,dw=0

for all
i\neq j
. Then

\det(M_{m}(B_{s\sqrt{n}})/M_{n}(B_{s\sqrt{n}}))\geq\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{s\sqrt{n}}^{c}}|\varphi_{k}(w)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w,\quad m\geq n.

To show that the above is lower bounded by a constant (as
n\to\infty
), we note that

\int_{B_{s\sqrt{n}}^{c}}|\varphi_{k}(w)|^{2}\mathrm{d}w=\frac{1}{\pi(k-1)!}\int_{B_{s\sqrt{n}}^{c}}|w|^{2(k-1)}e^{-|w|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}w=\frac{2}{(k-1)!}\int_{s\sqrt{n}}^{\infty}r^{2k-1}e^{-r^{2}}\,dr

is the probability that
R_{k}>s\sqrt{n}
, where
R_{k}^{2}
follows a
\operatorname{Gamma}(k,1)
-distribution.
For all
\lambda>0
, let
Y_{\lambda}
be Poisson(
\lambda
)-distributed and note that
\mathbb{P}(R_{k}^{2}\leq t)=\mathbb{P}(Y_{t}\geq k)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{s\sqrt{n}}^{c}}|\varphi_{k}(w)|^{2}\mathrm{d}w=\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{k}^{2}\geq sn)\geq\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{k}^{2}\geq sk)=\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}(1-\mathbb{P}(Y_{sk}>k)).

Next we use a well-known version of the Chernoff bound for Poisson random variables, which gives

\mathbb{P}(Y_{\lambda}>k)\leq k^{-k}(e\lambda)^{k}e^{-\lambda}

for all
\lambda<k
. Hence,

\mathbb{P}(Y_{sk}>k)\leq(e^{1-s}s)^{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
s_{2}:=e^{1-s}s\in(0,1)
. Since
\log(1-x)\geq 1-\frac{1}{1-x}
for
x\in(0,1)
, we arrive at the bound

\displaystyle\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{s\sqrt{n}}^{c}}|\varphi_{k}(w)|^{2}\mathrm{d}w\geq\exp\left(-\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{s_{2}^{k}}{1-s_{2}^{k}}\right)\geq\exp\left(-\frac{s_{2}^{n+1}}{(1-s_{2}^{n})(1-s_{2})}\right).

Therefore, using (
20
) and taking
m\rightarrow\infty
,

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(A_{n})=0)=\det M_{n}(A_{n})\leq(1+Cs_{2}^{n})\mathbb{P}(\xi(A_{n})=0),

for sufficiently large
n
(depending only on
s_{2})
.
In the previous inequality, we used that since
(1-s_{2}^{n})(1-s_{2})
converges to
1-s_{2}
, there exists
C(s)>0
such that for sufficiently large
n
(depending only on
s
),

\exp\left(\frac{s_{2}^{n+1}}{(1-s_{2}^{n})(1-s_{2})}\right)\leq\exp(Cs_{2}^{n+1})\leq 1+2Cs_{2}^{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Fix
s<1
and a sequence
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
s_{n}\to\infty
and
s_{n}\leq n^{2/5}
. There exists a sequence
(p_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
(depending on
s
and
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
) such that
p_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and

\sup_{z\in B_{s\sqrt{n}}}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{s_{n}}(z))\leq 2)\leq\exp(-s_{n}^{4}(1/4+p_{n}))

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a large integer, to be determined later. Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{M}
be a series of annuli centered at
z
, where
A_{i}
has inner radius
(i-1)s_{n}/M
and outer radius
is_{n}/M
. Then the annuli are disjoint, and their union is
B_{s_{n}}(z)
. For all
i<j\leq M
, set
D_{i,j}=B_{s_{n}}(z)\setminus(A_{i}\cup A_{j})
. Then we have the inclusion of events

\{\xi_{n}(B_{s_{n}}(z))\leq 2\}\subset\bigcup_{i<j\leq M}\{\xi_{n}(D_{i,j})=0\}.

By a union bound,

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{s_{n}}(z))\leq 2)\leq\sum_{i<j\leq M}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(D_{i,j})=0).

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
denote the constant from Lemma
11
. This lemma gives

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(D_{i,j})=0)\leq(1+C\delta^{n})\mathbb{P}(\xi(D_{i,j})=0)

for all
i<j<m
,
since
B_{s_{n}}(z)\subset B_{s^{\prime}\sqrt{n}}(0)
for
s^{\prime}=(s+1)/2
and all
z\in B_{s\sqrt{n}}
, by the assumed upper bound on
s_{n}
.
Therefore,

(21)

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{s_{n}}(x))\leq 2)\leq(1+C\delta^{n})\sum_{i<j\leq M}\mathbb{P}(\xi(D_{i,j})=0).

Now, since infinite Ginibre process is stationary, we may suppose that each
D_{i,j}
is centered at the origin and use the representation stated in Lemma
10
. Let
(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an infinite collection of independent random variables such that
X^{2}_{k}\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(k,1)
. By Lemma
10
,

\mathbb{P}(\xi(D_{i,j})=0)=\mathbb{P}(\#\{k\in\mathbb{N}:X_{k}\in|D_{i,j}|\}=0)=\mathbb{P}(X_{k}\notin|D_{i,j}|\,\forall k)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{k}\notin|D_{i,j}|),

where
|D_{i,j}|
is the projection of
D_{i,j}
to the
r
-axis in the
(r,\theta)
plane. Inserting this into (
21
), we obtain

(22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{s_{n}}(x))\leq 2)&\leq(1+C\delta^{n})\sum_{i<j\leq M}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{k}\notin|D_{i,j}|)\\
&\leq(1+C\delta^{n})\sum_{i<j\leq M}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{P}(X_{k}\in|A_{i}|)+\mathbb{P}(X_{k}\in|A_{j}|)+\mathbb{P}(X^{2}_{k}>s_{n}^{2})\right).\end{split}

As observed in (
19
), the density of
X^{2}_{k}
is uniformly bounded by 1 for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, for all
i
such that
1\leq i\leq M
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{k}\in|A_{i}|)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Big(\big((i-1)s_{n}/M\big)^{2}\leq X^{2}_{k}\leq(is_{n}/M)^{2}\Big)\leq(2i-1)(s_{n}/M)^{2}\leq 2s_{n}^{2}/M.

The moment generating function of
X^{2}_{k}
is well known, and we have for any
t<1
that

\mathbb{P}(X^{2}_{k}\geq s_{n}^{2})\leq e^{-ts_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[e^{tX^{2}_{k}}]\leq e^{-ts_{n}^{2}}(1-t)^{-k}.

Optimizing this bound by taking
t=1-k/s_{n}^{2}
gives

\mathbb{P}(X^{2}_{k}>s_{n}^{2})\leq\exp\big(-s_{n}^{2}+k-k\log(k/s_{n}^{2})\big).

Hence, with
M:=e^{s_{n}^{3}}
we obtain from (
22
) that

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{s_{n}}(x))\leq 2)\leq(1+C\delta^{n})e^{2s^{3}_{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{s_{n}^{2}}\left(4s_{n}e^{-s_{n}^{3}}+\exp\left(-s_{n}^{2}+k-k\log(k/s_{n}^{2})\right)\right).

For sufficiently large
n
, depending on the choice of
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have uniformly for all
k\leq s_{n}^{2}
that

4s_{n}e^{-s_{n}^{3}}+\exp\left(-s_{n}^{2}+k-2k\log(k/s_{n}^{2})\right)\leq\exp\left(-s_{n}^{2}+k-k\log(k/s_{n}^{2})+s_{n}^{-5/2}\right).

Then, bounding
(1+C\delta^{n})\leq e^{s_{n}^{3}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{s_{n}}(x))\leq 2)

\displaystyle\leq e^{3s^{3}_{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{s_{n}^{2}}\exp\left(-s_{n}^{2}+k-k\log(k/s_{n}^{2})+s_{n}^{-5/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-s^{4}_{n}(1/4+p_{n})\right).

In the last inequality, we used the elementary identity

\sum_{k=1}^{s_{n}^{2}}k\big(1+\log(s^{2}_{n})\big)=\big(1+\log(s^{2}_{n})\big)\frac{s_{n}^{2}(s_{n}^{2}+1)}{2},

and

\sum_{k=1}^{s_{n}^{2}}k\log k=\frac{s_{n}^{4}}{2}\log(s_{n}^{2})-\frac{s_{n}^{4}}{4}+\frac{s_{n}^{4}}{2}\log(s_{n}^{2})+O\big(\log(s_{n}^{2})\big),

by the Euler–Maclaurin formula.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Now we can deduce the asymptotic scaling of the sequence
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We recall that
r_{n}
depends on a parameter
\kappa>0
that we suppress in the notation.

Lemma 13
.

For every Borel set
B
with
\sup_{z\in B}|z|<1
, and all
\kappa>1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\Big|\frac{r_{n}(z)^{4}}{4\log n}-1\Big|\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

From the domination property
(
14
),
Lemma
11
, and stationarity of the infinite Ginibre process
\xi
, we have that for all
z\in\sqrt{n}B
,

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}^{z!}(B_{r_{n}(z)}(z))=0)\geq\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{r_{n}(z)}(z))=0)\geq\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{r_{n}(z)}(0))=0).

Moreover, by
Lemma
5
, there exists
C(B,\kappa)>1
such that for
n\geq C
,

(24)

\displaystyle n\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}^{z!}(B_{r_{n}}(z))=0)\rho_{n}(z)=\kappa,\quad z\in B_{\sqrt{n}}(0),

where
\rho_{n}(z)=K_{n}(z,z)
is the intensity function of
\xi_{n}
. Since
\rho_{n}(z_{n})\uparrow\frac{1}{\pi}
uniformly as
n\to\infty
for all sequences
(z_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
z_{n}\in\sqrt{n}B
, we have for
n
sufficiently large (depending only on the choice of
B
) that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\leq\rho_{n}(z)\leq\frac{1}{\pi},\quad z\in\sqrt{n}B.

In combination with (
23
) and (
24
), this gives

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi c}{n}\leq\inf_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{r_{n}(z)}(0))=0)\qquad\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{r_{n}(z)}(0))=0)\leq\frac{2\pi c}{n}.

Moreover, from
[
9
, Proposition 7.2.1]
we have

(26)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{4}}\log\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{r}(0))=0)=-\frac{1}{4}.

Define the sequence

(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

by
v_{n}=\inf_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}r_{n}(z)
.
Using

\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{v_{n}}(0))=0)=\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{r_{n}(z)}(0))=0),

together with (
25
) and (
26
), we find that
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded.
Then (
25
) and (
26
) yield

(27)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\frac{r_{n}(z)^{4}}{4\log n}\geq-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}r_{n}(z)^{4}}{4\log\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{v_{n}}(0))=0)+4\log 2\pi c}=1.

To derive the complementary bound, we first estimate the vacuum probability under the reduced Palm measure
\xi_{n}^{z!}
.
Using the coupling of
\xi_{n}
and
\xi_{n}^{z!}
introduced in Section
3.3
, we have

(28)

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}^{z!}(B_{r_{n}}(z))=0)\leq\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{r_{n}}(z))\leq 1).

Define the sequence

(\tilde{v}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

by
\tilde{v}_{n}=\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}r_{n}(z)
. By Lemma
12
,

\inf_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{r_{n}}(z))\leq 1)\leq\exp\Big(-\tilde{v}_{n}^{4}(1/4+p_{n})\Big),

where
p_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
In combination with (
23
), (
25
), and (
28
), this gives

(29)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\frac{r_{n}(z)^{4}}{4\log n}\leq 1.

Combining (
27
) and (

Here we apply (
18
) to the difference in the integral. Uniformly for
z\in B
, this gives by Lemma
13
,

\displaystyle\kappa^{-1}n\left|\int_{B_{r_{n}}(\sqrt{n}z)}\int_{\sqrt{n}S_{n,z}}\rho_{n}^{(3)}(\sqrt{n}z,w,v)-\rho_{n}^{(2)}(\sqrt{n}z,w)\rho_{n}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}w\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\kappa^{-1}3^{5/2}\pi|B|n^{2}\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}r_{n}(z)^{2}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in\sqrt{n}B\\
|v-w|\geq\log n-r_{n}(\sqrt{n}z)\end{subarray}}|K_{n}(w,v)|

(49)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\kappa^{-1}3^{5/2}\pi|B|n^{2}2\sqrt{\log n}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in\sqrt{n}B\\
|v-w|\geq\log n-2\sqrt{\log n}\end{subarray}}e^{-|w-v|^{2}/2}+O(e^{-cn})\leq\frac{2\cdot 3^{5/2}\pi|B|}{cn}+O(e^{-cn}).

Hence, we obtain by adding the contributions from (
43
), (
47
) and (
49
) that for all
z\in B
,

(50)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\xi_{n}\Delta\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing})(\sqrt{n}S_{n,z})]\leq 2|B|\Big(\frac{\pi}{n^{2}}+\frac{2\sqrt{\log n}+3^{5/2}\pi}{cn}\Big)+O(e^{-cn}).

This completes the bound of the expectation of the right-hand side of (
39
).

Finally, we consider the sum in (
40
). For all
z\in B
it holds almost surely that

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}\cap\xi_{n}\cap(\sqrt{n}B\setminus\sqrt{n}T_{n,z})}\big|\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}(B_{r_{n}}(w))=0\}-\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}(B_{r_{n}}(w))=0\}\big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{w\in\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}\cap\xi_{n}\cap(\sqrt{n}B\setminus\sqrt{n}T_{n,z})}(\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}\Delta\xi_{n})(B_{r_{n}}(w))\max\big(\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}(B_{r_{n}}(w)=0)\},\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}(B_{r_{n}}(w)=0)\}\big).

By Lemma
13
there exists
\delta>0
such that
B_{r_{n}}(w)\subset B_{(1-\delta)\sqrt{n}}(0)
for all
w\in B
and
n\geq C(s,\kappa)
. Hence, the previous sum is bounded by

(51)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{v\in\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}\Delta\xi_{n}}\mathds{1}\{v\in B_{(1-\delta)\sqrt{n}}(0)\setminus\sqrt{n}T_{n,z}\}\sum_{w\in\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}\cap\xi_{n}\cap B_{r_{n}(w)}(v)}\max\{\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}(B_{r_{n}}(w))=0\},\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}(B_{r_{n}}(w))=0\}\}\\
&\quad\leq\sum_{v\in\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}\setminus\xi_{n}}\mathds{1}\{v\in B_{(1-\delta)\sqrt{n}}(0)\setminus\sqrt{n}T_{n,z}\}\sum_{w\in\xi_{n}\cap B_{r_{n}(w)}(v)}\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}(B_{r_{n}}(w))=0\}\\
&\quad\quad+\sum_{v\in\xi_{n}\setminus\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}}\mathds{1}\{v\in B_{(1-\delta)\sqrt{n}}(0)\setminus\sqrt{n}T_{n,z}\}\sum_{w\in\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}\cap B_{r_{n}(w)}(v)}\mathds{1}\{\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}(B_{r_{n}}(w))=0\}.\end{split}

Using

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{v,w\in B_{(1-\delta)\sqrt{n}}(0)}\frac{r_{n}(v)}{r_{n}(w)}=1,

from Lemma
13
, we find by a straightforward volume argument (considering how many balls with radius
r_{n}(w)/2
fit into a ball with radius
2r_{n}(v)
) that

\sum_{w\in\omega\cap B_{r_{n}}(v)}\mathds{1}\{\omega(B_{r_{n}}(w))=0\}\leq 16

for all locally finite
\omega\in\mathbf{N}
and
n
large enough. Inserting this estimate into (
51
) gives that (
40
) is almost surely bounded by

16(\xi_{n}^{z,\varnothing}\Delta\xi_{n})(B_{(1-\delta)\sqrt{n}}(0)\setminus\sqrt{n}T_{n,z}).

Hence, by (
50
) (with
B
replaced by
B_{(1-\delta)\sqrt{n}}(0)
), we have for all
z\in B
that (
40
) is bounded by

32|B_{1-\delta}(0)|\Big(\frac{\pi}{n^{2}}+\frac{2\sqrt{\log n}+3^{5/2}\pi}{cn}\Big)+O(e^{-cn}).

This completes the bound for (
40
), and hence completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. We first show that

\mathbb{P}\left(\max\limits_{z\in\xi_{n}\cap\sqrt{n}B}\min\limits_{w\in\xi_{n}\setminus\{z\}}|z-w|\geq(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}\right)\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. Using the definition of
\Xi_{n}
, that
L_{n}
is the intensity measure for
\Xi_{n}
, and
\rho_{n}(z)\leq\pi^{-1}
for all
z\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{z\in\xi_{n}\cap\sqrt{n}B}\min\limits_{w\in\xi_{n}\setminus\{z\}}|z-w|\geq(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sum_{z\in\xi_{n}\cap\sqrt{n}B}\mathds{1}\Big\{\min\limits_{w\in\xi_{n}\setminus\{z\}}|z-w|\geq(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sqrt{n}B}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}^{z!}(B_{(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}}(z))=0)\rho_{n}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

(52)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n|B|}{\pi}\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}}(z))\leq 1),

where in the last inequality we used the coupling of of
\xi_{n}^{z!}
and
\xi_{n}
indicated in Section
3.3
.
By Lemma
12
with
s_{n}=(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}
we have for
n\geq C(\epsilon,B)
that

\sup_{z\in\sqrt{n}B}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}(B_{(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}}(z))\leq 1)\leq n^{-(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)^{4}/4}.

Hence, (
52
) tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Next, we show that

\mathbb{P}\Big(\max\limits_{z\in\xi_{n}\cap\sqrt{n}B}\min\limits_{w\in\xi_{n}\setminus\{z\}}|z-w|\leq(\sqrt{2}-\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}\Big)\to 0.

Fix
\kappa>0
. By (
6
), we have
(\sqrt{2}-\epsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}\leq r_{n}(z)
for
n\geq C(\epsilon,\kappa,B)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{z\in\xi_{n}\cap\sqrt{n}B}\min_{w\in\xi_{n}\setminus\{z\}}|z-w|\leq(\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)\sqrt[4]{\log n}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{z\in\xi_{n}\cap\sqrt{n}B}\min_{w\in\xi_{n}\setminus\{z\}}|z-w|\leq r_{n}(z)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\Xi_{n}\cap B=\varnothing),

which tends to
\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\kappa}\cap B=\varnothing)=\exp(-\kappa|B|)
as
n\to\infty
by Theorem
1
, since convergence in KR distance implies convergence in distribution for locally finite point processes (see
[
18
, Proposition 2.1]
).
Since
\kappa
was arbitrary, this upper bound can be made arbitrarily small by taking
\kappa
sufficiently large.
∎

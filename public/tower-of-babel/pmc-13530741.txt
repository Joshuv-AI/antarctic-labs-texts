Poster 176. Orthopedic Injury Trends in Major League Baseball from 2020 to 2024: A Descriptive Epidemiologic Study
Authors: Jack Janett, Robert Ablove
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:1. Characterize the overall frequency and temporal trends of orthopedic injuries in Major League Baseball from 2020 to 2024.2. Analyze the anatomical distribution and average duration of injuries to identify which injury types result in the greatest time missed.3. Compare injury patterns by player position (pitcher, catcher, infielder, outfielder) to determine positional differences in injury burden.Methods:Injury data were obtained from the publicly available Fangraphs MLB Injury Tracker. The dataset included injury type, player position, and injury/return dates for all MLB players from 2020 to 2024. Days missed were calculated based on surgery/injury and return dates. Players were grouped by position (pitcher, catcher, infield, and outfield), and 14 different injury categories, including Tommy John surgery, ankle, latissimus dorsi, back, calf, elbow, and flexor/forearm. Descriptive statistics summarized injury frequency and average days missed. One-way ANOVA tested for differences in mean injury duration across injury types. A chi-square test compared the distribution of injury types across positions. Linear regression assessed trends in total annual injuries over the study period.Results:A total of 2,849 injuries were reported across five seasons. The number of injuries per year ranged from 244 in 2020 to 714 in 2021. Injuries peaked consistently during the months of August and September, aligning with the final stretch of the regular season. The most frequently reported injuries included shoulder/rotator cuff and elbow injuries. Shoulder/rotator cuff injuries accounted for 445 cases (15.6%), and elbow injuries accounted for 260 cases (9.1%). Average time missed varied substantially by injury type, ranging from 26.52 ± 26.29 days for neck injuries to 280.44 ± 183.67 days for Tommy John surgery. A one-way ANOVA revealed a statistically significant difference in injury duration across injury types (F = 131.19, p < 0.001), confirming that the amount of time missed varies meaningfully depending on injury location/type. Linear regression revealed a positive trend in total injuries over time, with an average increase of 67.4 injuries per year (β = 67.4), though this trend was not statistically significant (p = 0.313, R² = 0.33). Chi-square analysis demonstrated significant differences in injury patterns by player position (p < 0.001). For example, pitchers experienced the highest mean missed time for shoulder/rotator cuff injuries (430.2 days) and elbow injuries (274.1 days), compared to lower averages in catchers (93.97 and 83.58 days, respectively), infielders (214.33 and 166.98 days), and outfielders (222.48 and 171.88 days).Conclusions:From 2020 to 2024, orthopedic injuries in MLB suggest a post-pandemic rebound effect and demonstrated significant variation by injury type and player position. Upper extremity injuries particularly shoulder and elbow pathologies-accounted for a disproportionate share of time lost, with pitchers bearing the greatest burden. While this study provides a comprehensive overview using publicly available data, limitations include the absence of injury severity and performance metrics. These findings emphasize the importance of ongoing surveillance and the development of targeted, position-specific injury prevention strategies to mitigate orthopedic injuries in professional baseball.

Multivariate 25-Year Trend Analysis of Mortality Due to Liver Hydatid Cysts
Authors: Vahit Mutlu, Kenan Erzurumlu, Kadir Yılmaz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesIn this restrospective and cross-sectional study, it was aimed to analyze the time-dependent change in mortality due to liver hydatid cysts in a multicenter and multivariate manner over 25 years.MethodsIn the study, 213 files of patients with liver Hydatid cysts underwent surgery in multicenter between 1993 and 2018 were retrospectively included. The mortality rate in patients was reported as 11.7%. Gender, age, cyst stage, number of cysts, localization, maximum cyst diameter, latex and albendazole use, comorbidity, early and late complications, mortality, hospitalization and follow-up periods of the patients were analyzed.ResultsAge mean, right localization and cardiovascular disease (CVD) frequency parameters were significantly higher in mortality group (p < 0.05). Follow up duration was significantly higher in living group (p < 0.05). Mortality was significantly correlated with age (r = 0.375; p < 0.01), localization (r = − 0.153; p < 0.05), CVD (r = 0.264; p < 0.01) and follow up duration (r = − 0.262; p < 0.01). According to year controlled partial correlation analysis, mortality was only significantly correlated with age (r = 0.301; p < 0.05). Cox regression analysis results showed that effects of age (B = 0.093; p < 0.01), right localization (B = 2.215; p < 0.05) and left localization (B = 2.704; p < 0.05) on mortality were significant. Survival function for mortality showed that about first 100 days after surgery were important for mortality. After 200 day, cumulative survival trend was more stable than before. In left localized cyst, about first 500 days after surgery is important for mortality. In right and segment localized patients, first 1000 days after surgery is important for mortality.ConclusionAccording to the results of multicenter and 25-year experience in patients with hydatid cysts, the causes of mortality did not change significantly over time, and only age and localization were found to be important factors. Although there have been improvements in environmental hygiene and risk factors over time, their effects on disease-related mortality were found to be insignificant.

Introduction

Hydatid cyst (HC) is an anthropozoonosis and is caused by the infection of Echinococcus granulosus tapeworm, which is commonly found in the environment, under unhygienic conditions [1]. In humans, Echinococcus granulosus infection is predominantly seen in the liver in 55% to 70% and in the lungs in 18% to 35%. It has been reported that both organs are affected in 5% to 13% of cases [2]. Although the rate is low, there are studies reporting different locations such as kidney [3], bone [4], and intercranial [5]. However, liver and lung are the localizations that cause the most mortality and morbidity [1, 2]. The most common risk factors are hygiene conditions and especially animal husbandry, and it is more common in developing countries [6]. While the common transmission route in animals is grass and water, in humans it is direct contact with contaminated water, vegetables, food or dogs. When the eggs are swallowed, oncospheres pass through the intestinal wall and settle in the organs [7]. Magnetic resonance, ultrasound and computed tomography are widely used in the diagnosis and monitoring of liver HC, which is the most common among these [8].

Liver HC, which is one of the common localizations, is treated with drug therapy, percutaneous treatment approach carried out by different techniques according to the stage of liver hydatid cysts classified by WHO-IWGE and surgery. In the treatment process and surgical intervention, factors such as the patient's age, the presence of multiple cysts, and calcified cysts are the factors that negatively affect treatment and surgery [9]. There are many factors that affect the treatment process, and radical surgery is the most ideal method due to the increase in ultrasonographic cyst size over time. The treatment method and cyst localization affect biliary fistula complications [10]. There are many parameters that affect disease risk factors, and most of them are related to hygiene and contamination [2–6]. Albendazole is the treatment method used together with surgery and drainage [11]. Excision of the cyst and omentoplasty are among the treatment options with the least complications reported [12]. In addition, double percutaneous aspiration and alcohol injection are reported to be low-cost and effective treatment methods [13]. Akhan [14] explained PAIR as a tried-and-true method that is thought to be safe and effective for CE1 and CE3a because it is linked to a lower rate of mortality, morbidity, recurrence, and shorter hospital stays than surgery. Although the laparoscopic method is another widely used treatment method today, some studies have suggested that this method should be preferred for cysts without complications [15].

Over time, there have been developments such as decreased contact with animals due to urbanization, increased environmental factors, increased hygiene awareness, and significant improvements in the health system. However, there were no adequate studies analyzing risk factors and factors affecting treatment in a multivariate manner. Therefore, in this study, it was aimed to analyze the time-dependent change in mortality due to liver hydatid cysts in a multicenter and multivariate manner over 25 years.

Methods

Research Model

The study is based on retrospective file scanning and also includes observational and relational scanning models. In the study, data of patients who underwent surgery for liver HC were examined with time-controlled analyses for changes in risk factors over 25 years and trend values were examined.

Patients

The study included patients who underwent liver HC surgery by researchers in different health institutions between 1993 and 2018. In this context, 213 patient files were accessed in the study. Since the study included all patients operated on by a surgeon throughout professional life, a time-based sampling method was used and therefore no power analysis was performed. The study examined operations performed by the researchers at various public and private hospitals across the surgical career path. These centers were Ataşehir Memorial Hospital, Samsun Ondokuz Mayıs University Hospital, and Üsküdar University Hospital.

Data Set

In the study, patients' gender, age, number of cysts, cyst degree, localization, maximum cyst diameter, concomitant diseases, latex test result (Latex Agglutination Test) and albendazole use, follow-up and hospitalization time, early and late major complications were analyzed. WHO-IWGE is a common classification used in hospitals and patient files.

Early complications were defined as complications occurring before 30 days postoperatively. Late complications were defined as complications occurring after 30 days. Complications that could be resolved with simple medications were considered minor complications, while complications requiring intervention (recurrence, incisional hernia, central abscess, drainage, and reoperation) were considered major complications.

Statistical Analysis

SPSS 25.0 for Windows was used in the analysis of data. Nominal and ordinal data were defined by frequency analysis and Chi-Square and Fisher's Exact tests were used for difference analysis. Kolmogorov Smirnov test was used for normality analysis of measurement data. Since the distributions did not conform to normal distribution, Mann Whitney U test was used. Spearman's rho and partial correlation analysis with year control were used in relational screening analyses. Cox Regression Analysis was used for effect analysis due to linearization deviations [16, 17]. Analyses were performed at 95% confidence interval and 0.05 significance level.

Ethical Approval

Ethical permission was obtained from the Üsküdar University Non-Interventional Research Ethics Committee for the research permission with 61,351,342/020–017 number and May, 2025 date. Since the research was based on retrospective file screening and mortality, patient consent forms were not obtained. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, Üsküdar University Non-Interventional Research Ethics Committee waived the need of obtaining informed consent.

Results

The mean age of the mortality group was 62.72 ± 11.51, and the mean age of the survival group was 44.04 ± 14.95. 61.2% of the survival group and 48.0% of the mortality group were female. The number of cysts ranged from 1 to 9 in the survival group and 1 to 7 in the mortality group. The mean maximum diameter was 8.69 ± 4.10 in the survival group and 9.10 ± 4.05 in the mortality group. Cyst stage was predominantly stage III (37.2%) in the survival group and stage II (31.8%) in the mortality group. Localization was predominantly right in both groups. In the survival group, DM was 6.4%, HT was 10.1%, COPD was 1.1%, and CVD was 2.7%. In the mortality group, DM was 8.0%, HT was 16.0%, COPD was 4.0%, and CVD was 20.0%. Preoperative latex had a median value of 5.00 in the survival group and 4.50 in the mortality group. Preoperative albendazole had a median value of 4.00 in both groups. Early complications within the first 30 days were 14.9% in the survival group and 4.0% in the mortality group. Late complications (> 30 days) were 38.8% in the survival group and 36.0% in the mortality group. Only one patient in mortality group had recurrence.

The follow-up duration was 462.29 ± 699.93 in the survival group and 133.57 ± 252.30 in the mortality group. The duration of hospitalization was 13.27 ± 8.26 in the survival group and 15.09 ± 10.68 in the mortality group.

Age mean, right localization and CVD frequency parameters were significantly higher in mortality group (p < 0.05). Follow up duration was significantly higher in living group (p < 0.05). Discussion

In this study, the change in mortality causes related to liver HC was analyzed over time, and it was revealed that age and localization were effective factors among the risk factors related to liver HC within 25 years. Although there were improvements in environmental hygiene and risk factors over time, it was seen that their effect on disease-related mortality was not significant.

Although liver HC is a disease that is transmitted through hygiene and contact conditions and therefore can be prevented, it is currently an important health problem in terms of both the treatment process and mortality levels [18–20]. In the advanced stages of the disease, complications that may develop after the surgical method, comorbid diseases and factors affecting the treatment process also affect the mortality rate. On the other hand, environmental and biological changes experienced over time, urbanization and developments in people's lifestyles, nutrition styles, opportunities to fight diseases and participate in the treatment process should bring higher success in liver HC treatment and a decreasing trend in mortality rates over time. However, this situation is different in practice and it is observed that there has been no significant change in liver HC-related deaths in 25-year follow-up.

In studies reporting mortality related to the disease, it is seen that various parameters have an effect on mortality. Daradkeh et al. [21] reported that cyst size, age, history of surgery and type are important mortality factors. In another study, Sokouti et al. [22] reported that the surgical method carries a similar risk for mortality in laparoscopic and open surgery. Dogru et al. [23] reported that HC cysts are larger in young people and that this may affect mortality. Baraket et al. [24] reported that cyst size is an important morbidity factor in conservative treatment of liver HC. Manterola et al. [25] reported that risk factors decreased over time compared to their previous report. Tagliacozzo et al. [26], who examined 25 years of liver HC as in our study, suggested that radical surgery for liver HC should be limited to clear cyst treatment. In our study, age and left localization were significant mortality-increasing factors. The effect of age on mortality can be explained by the increasing comorbid diseases and deterioration in the health status of individuals in advanced ages, as in other diseases. The localization of the cyst is directly related to the surgical process and is directly related to the surgical procedures during excision. Therefore, it is possible to state that there has been no progress over time in terms of both liver HC-related deaths and disease-related precautions, apart from the localization and age factors.

Although patients with sage 4 and 5 as they should be managed by “Wait and Watch approach, there were patient files operated at stage 4 and 5. Because the study data are retrospective, we believe surgeons made the decision based on a comorbidity or physical examination. However, there was insufficient information in the patient files to determine this.

As with all surgical procedures, length of stay, follow-up, and mortality are important in cyst operations. While the mortality rate in our study was 25/213, or approximately 11.7%, it could be even higher if patients with incomplete data in their patient files and those excluded are considered. However, even this rate is quite high. The follow-up period was low in the mortality group, and the termination of follow-up after the death of the patients and acute deaths may also have an impact on this result. Although recurrence rates are reported at different rates in the literature, we did not observe a significant recurrence in our study. This rate may also be affected by patients losing follow-up or changing institutions. To address all these limitations, controlled prospective studies are needed in future studies. In this regard, the study could serve as a resource for further research.

Limitations of the Study

The most important limitation of the study is that, due to its retrospective nature, there are too many patients who are out of follow-up. The existence of both public and private health institutions in the health system also brings some problems in patient follow-up. Although it has been possible to follow patients in the private sector and public health systems from a single center in recent years, the same is not the case for past data. This situation is one of the important limitations in terms of the number of patients.

Another important limitation of the study is that the data in the files regarding comorbidities and other health conditions in HC patients are limited. For this reason, the data set used in the study includes the most accessible and traceable data among the data collected in 25 years. In addition to this data, many possible prognostic factors such as the patients' living environment, occupation, hygiene attitude, and diet could not be included in the study. This is another important limitation of the study.

Contribution of the Research to Literature and Clinical Practice

The most important contribution of the study to the literature is that there is no previous study examining the effect of time as a variable on liver HC. Although there are studies in the literature examining liver HC mortality for long periods of time, no study was found that directly included and examined the effect of time as a variable in the analyses. In this respect, the study makes a significant contribution to the literature in terms of analysis method and approach.

The contribution of the study to clinical practice is that it reveals the mortality factors over time in liver HC cases and reveals the groups with high risk after the surgical method. The results obtained in the study reveal that mortality is higher especially in patients with left localization and in patients with advanced age. For this reason, it is beneficial to follow up patients with left localization and advanced age more closely than other patient groups in terms of mortality.

Conclusion

According to the results of multicenter and 25-year experience in patients with hydatid cysts, the causes of mortality did not change significantly over time, and only age and localization were found to be important factors. In fact, despite the increase in urbanization and sanitation opportunities over time, and the fact that individuals have become more aware through other media tools, including social media, liver HC prevalence and mortality continue to be a health problem. Although mortality is related to localization, the fact that tumor maximum diameter is not related suggests a direct pathophysiological mechanism related to localization.

Nowadays, more multivariate and multicenter studies are needed to prevent or minimize mortality due to liver HC. In addition to environmental causes, hygiene and sanitation issues, more comprehensive and patient-accessible studies are needed for advanced stages of the disease and prognosis.

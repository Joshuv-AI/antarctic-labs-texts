Intrinsic Topological Dice Flat Band in Yttrium Monochloride Electrides
Jianqi Zhong, Songyuan Geng, Haoxiang Li, Benjamin T. Zhou

Abstract
In a recent experiment the long-sought dice lattice and its characteristic flat band has been discovered for the first time in the two-dimensional layered electride yttrium monochloride (YCl), in which the interstitial anionic electrons of the electride self-organize into a dice lattice geometry. In this Letter, combining symmetry analysis, relativistic density-functional theory and realistic tight-binding model calculations, we predict that the dice flat band in YCl is intrinsically topological and characterized by a high Chern number of 𝒞 = ± 4 \mathcal{C}=\pm 4 . In particular, the intrinsic atomic spin-orbit coupling (SOC) from 4 ​ d 4d -electrons of yttrium atoms creates topological gaps on the scale of 20 meV near ± K \pm K and leads to the emergence of nontrivial Berry curvatures and band topology. Displacement fields applied across the layered electride architecture can easily drive topological phase transitions. Our findings establish the newly discovered YCl electride as the first natural material hosting a dice flat Chern band without any extrinsic band engineering.

(1)

Emergence of massive Dirac fermions under atomic SOC and intrinsic flat Chern band.
— As atomic SOC effects are expected to be strong for the 4
d
-orbitals of transition-metal Y elements, relativistic effects cannot be ignored for an accurate description of the dice flat band in YCl. In particular, the atomic SOC within the subspace spanned by the basis states
\{\ket{d_{+2},\uparrow},\ket{d_{-2},\uparrow},\ket{d_{+2},\downarrow},\ket{d_{-2},\downarrow}\}
takes the form of:

H_{\rm SOC}=\lambda s_{z}\tau_{z},

(3)

where
\lambda
is the atomic SOC strength,
s_{z}
denotes the Pauli matrix for spin and
\tau_{z}
the Pauli matrix in the basis of
\{\ket{d_{+2}},\ket{d_{-2}}\}
. Note that only the
s_{z}
-component is non-vanishing as the in-plane orbital angular momentum component for the
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}\pm id_{xy}
-orbitals vanishes:
\braket{d_{\pm 2}|L_{x,y}|d_{\pm 2}}=0
. It is evident from Eq.
3
that the atomic SOC in each spin sector acts like an orbital Zeeman field that breaks both the spinless
\mathcal{T}
and spinless
\mathcal{C}_{2x}
and splits the energies of the doublet
\{\ket{d_{+2}},\ket{d_{-2}}\}

\pm K

\bm{k}_{N}
(Eq.

-
2

\pi
to the flat band. Moreover, as the atomic SOC preserves the global inversion
\mathcal{P}
and three-fold rotation

-K

\bm{k}_{\rm N,1},\bm{k}_{\rm N,2},\bm{k}_{\rm N,3}

Role of displacement field.
— As the doublets at
\pm K
in the SOC-free limit are formed by states from different layers (Fig.
1

D_{z}
, the inter-layer potential difference
\Phi

can also introduce Dirac masses at
\pm K

\Delta_{\pm K}=\pm\Phi/2
. In the large
D_{z}

\Phi\rightarrow\infty

would dominate the mass term and the valley-contrasting Dirac masses would generate valley-contrasting Berry curvatures that cancel each other out, making the system topologically trivial. This suggests that the system would evolve from the initial topological phase at
D_{z}=0

D_{z}

To study the

\pi

\lambda

Conclusion and Outlooks.
— Our finding reveals the important topological nature of the newly discovered dice flat band in the first-ever dice system YCl electride, and provides the first realistic example of a topological dice flat band in an existent crystalline material in nature. In stark contrast to early theoretical proposals of topological bands from toy dice-lattice models where nontrivial topology relies on extrinsic symmetry breaking
Wang and Ran 2011
;
Shen et al.
, the predicted topological property is naturally given.

The nontrivial dice flat band topology is expected to manifest as quantum anomalous Hall effect (QAHE) in experiments when the lower spin-polarized flat band is fully filled. The system would exhibit a quantized anomalous Hall conductance of
\sigma_{xy}=4e^{2}/h
, and
\sigma_{xy}
drops first to
e^{2}/h
and eventually to zero as the displacement field increases.
\sigma_{xy}
is also expected to exhibit ferromagnetic hysteresis upon sweeping magnetic fields back and forth as the sign of Chern number (and
\sigma_{xy}
) is governed by the spin polarization. Given the sizable predicted topological gap of 20 meV (Fig.
2
b), we expect the topological state to be robust against disorder and thermal effects for a direct experimental observation of QAHE.

The highly non-uniform Berry curvature within the dice flat band (Fig.
2
,
3
) makes it far from resembling the lowest-Landau level - a condition thought to be undesirable for fractional Chern insulator (FCI) ground states
Jackson et al. 2015
;
Claassen et al. 2015
;
Mera and Ozawa 2021
;
Varjas et al. 2022
;
Ledwith et al. 2020
. However, recent theoretical advances suggest that Chern bands with high Chern number and non-uniform Berry curvatures may still be vortexable and host FCI ground states when generalized trace conditions of quantum geometric tensors are met
Ledwith et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2025
. Our finding thus poses an interesting and experimentally relevant open question as to whether the YCl electride can support FCI ground states at certain fractional band fillings. On the other hand, since the Berry curvature provides a lower bound for the trace of Fubini-Study quantum metric tensors
Roy 2014
, the gate-tunable high Chern number and giant Berry curvatures of order
10^{4}{\AA}^{2}
near
\pm K
indicate prominent quantum metric effects in the dice flat band, which bears rich implications for electron correlation physics
Kitamura et al. 2024
;
Kudo and Yanase
;
Peotta and Törmä 2015
;
Chen and Law 2024
worthy of future investigations.

\times

d

The form of the seven-band Hamiltonian is further constrained by the
D_{3d}\otimes\mathcal{T}
symmetries. For any symmetry operation
\hat{g}\in D_{3d}
, the general symmetry constraint is formulated as:

U(\hat{g})\,H_{\mathrm{TB}}(\bm{k})\,U^{\dagger}(\hat{g})=H_{\mathrm{TB}}(\hat{g}\bm{k}),

(S2)

while the spinless time-reversal symmetry
\mathcal{T}
dictates:

U_{\mathcal{T}}\,H_{\mathrm{TB}}(\bm{k})\,U_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}=H_{\mathrm{TB}}^{*}(-\bm{k}).

(S3)

Since Eq.
S2
holds for all elements
\hat{g}\in D_{3d}
once it is satisfied for all the generators, it suffices to consider three distinct generators: (i) three-fold rotation
C_{3z}
, (ii) in-plane mirror
M_{x}
, and (iii) spatial inversion
P
. The matrix representations of the three generators in the seven-orbital basis are given by:

\displaystyle U(C_{3z})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}U_{d}(C_{3z})&0_{3\times 1}&0_{3\times 3}\\
0_{1\times 3}&1&0_{1\times 3}\\
0_{3\times 3}&0_{3\times 1}&U_{d}(C_{3z})\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle U(M_{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}U_{d}(M_{x})&0_{3\times 1}&0_{3\times 3}\\
0_{1\times 3}&1&0_{1\times 3}\\
0_{3\times 3}&0_{3\times 1}&U_{d}(M_{x})\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle U(P)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0_{3\times 3}&0_{3\times 1}&I_{3\times 3}\\
0_{1\times 3}&1&0_{1\times 3}\\
I_{3\times 3}&0_{3\times 1}&0_{3\times 3}\end{pmatrix},

(S4)

where the
3\times 3
blocks acting on the
d

U_{d}(C_{3z})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\omega_{+}&0\\
0&0&\omega_{-}\end{pmatrix},\hskip 23.49976ptU_{d}(M_{x})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},

(S5)

with
\omega_{\pm}\equiv e^{\pm i2\pi/3}
the nontrivial eigenvalues of
C_{3z}
. We note that
|d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}\rangle\pm i|d_{xy}\rangle
form time-reversal partners, thus the representation of
\mathcal{T}
is identical to that of
M_{x}

U_{\mathcal{T}}=U(M_{x})

\displaystyle h_{b}(\bm{k})=

Next, we present details of the Wilson-loop method used for calculating the Chern number. Given the eigenvalue equation:

H_{TB}(k_{x},k_{y})\ket{u_{n}(k_{x},k_{y})}=E_{n}(k_{x},k_{y})\ket{u_{n}(k_{x},k_{y})},

(S7)

we define the following overlap matrix along
y
-direction occupied bands:

M_{y}^{mn}(k_{x};k_{y}^{j},k_{y}^{j+1})=\braket{u_{m}(k_{x},k_{y}^{j})|u_{n}(k_{x},k_{y}^{j+1})},

(S8)

where
k_{y}^{j}=2\pi j/N,m,n=1,2,..,n_{occ}
. The Wilson loop is then written as:

\omega_{y}(k_{x})=\prod\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}M_{y}^{mn}(k_{x};k_{y}^{j},k_{y}^{j+1}),

(S9)

Straightforward algebraic operations lead to
\omega_{y}(k_{x})=e^{-i\oint_{-\pi}^{\pi}A_{y}^{mn}(k_{x},k_{y})dk_{y}}
, where
A_{y}^{mn}(k_{x},k_{y})=-i\bra{u_{m}^{k}}\partial_{k_{y}}\ket{u_{n}^{k}}
. This allows us to obtain the polarization along
y
-direction:
p_{y}(k_{x})=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\ln\textrm{det}[\omega_{y}(k_{x})]
=
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}
Tr
[A_{y}(\bm{k})]dk_{y}
, and the Chern number
\mathcal{C}
is given by:

\mathcal{C}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{x}\partial_{k_{x}}p_{y}(k_{x}).

(S10)

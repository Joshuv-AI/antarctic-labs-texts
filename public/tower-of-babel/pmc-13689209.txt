SFOA-based optimized SeResNet-BiLSTM network with an attention mechanism for the detection of exons
Authors: Jayasree K, Malaya Kumar Hota
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDetection of exons in eukaryotic DNA sequences is a challenging research area in computational biology. In this work, a Starfish Optimization Algorithm (SFOA) based hybrid deep learning architecture is proposed for the efficient detection of exons, which is the amalgamation of squeeze and excitation residual network (SeResNet), bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM), and a customized attention (CA) mechanism. Firstly, SeResNet retrieves the deeply hidden features by performing recalibration and interdependencies between the channels. After that, BiLSTM is utilized to extract temporal information for a better understanding of the input data. Later, a CA mechanism is employed to select the salient features by redistributing the feature weights obtained from the SeResNet-BiLSTM to enhance the detection accuracy. Finally, the SFOA is applied to automatically optimize the hyperparameters of the SeResNet-BiLSTM-CA framework to improve the feature extraction. Before applying the deep learning framework, the modified Gabor wavelet transform (MGWT) is used to extract the periodic feature information. The efficacy of the proposed model is validated using two benchmark datasets, namely the HMR195 and the Genscan training set. To verify the efficiency of the proposed method, its performance is compared with several pre-trained networks and various existing techniques using evaluation metrics. Introduction

In the past two decades, the identification of exons in eukaryotic DNA sequences has been an emerging topic in genomic signal processing (GSP). The exons carry the genetic information, which is the only component involved in protein synthesis. In eukaryotes, identifying the protein-coding region within a gene is a challenging task since short exons are interspersed with long noncoding introns [1]. Numerous digital signal processing (DSP) techniques have been applied to DNA sequence analysis for exon identification by exploiting its essential three-base periodicity (TBP) property. To apply DSP methods, an appropriate mapping technique was employed to convert the string form of the DNA sequences into numerical form.

Tiwari et al. [2] introduced the short-time discrete Fourier transform (ST-FT) based DSP method for identifying coding regions. To enhance the efficiency of ST-FT, Anastassiou et al. [3] developed an improved frequency-domain tool. However, these methods use a rectangular window, which causes spectral leakage at a three-base period. To reduce spectral leakage, several adaptive window-length-based approaches [4–6] have been proposed.

Further, to address the issue of spectral leakage, Vaidyanathan and Yoon [7] developed an anti-notch filter with a central frequency f/3 for exons detection. Later, several filtering techniques have also been developed to increase recognition accuracy by reducing the background noise [8–12]. However, the specific filter parameters may not apply to all DNA datasets.

The limitation of the ST-FT approach is its dependency on window features like shape and length [13]. These issues have been mitigated and enhanced the detection accuracy by employing multiresolution transform approaches such as modified Gabor wavelet transform (MGWT) [14], fuzzy adaptive GWT [15], and wide-range wavelet window method [16]. Later, variational mode decomposition-based methods have been developed for better coding region detection [17, 18]. Many researchers successfully employed DSP approaches to identify the exons in eukaryotic DNA sequences. However, more effective approaches are still required to evaluate the large amounts of DNA sequences for identifying the protein-coding regions.

Recently, deep learning (DL) approaches like convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and hybrid CNN-RNN models have demonstrated promising results for signal identification and classification in various applications. However, deep learning techniques for detecting exon regions are still in their infancy. The performance of the DL algorithm is enhanced by expanding the depth of the network layers [19]. A major challenge of increasing the layers in DL architecture for classification is the complexity and interpretability of the models [20, 21]. These DL networks are difficult to comprehend due to their several layers and hyperparameters. As a result, simplifying the models, thoroughly evaluating them, and improving their interpretability are critical factors when designing and implementing DL-based networks. According to the literature [22–28], hybrid models outperform conventional methods by retrieving both temporal and spatial features of the signals without increasing the depth of the layers. Therefore, this work focused on a hybrid model architecture for identifying the exon regions in eukaryotic DNA sequences. The proposed hybrid model integrates the squeeze and excitation residual network (SeResNet), bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM), and a customized attention (CA) network.

Nevertheless, the overall efficacy of the hybrid model depends on the tuning of its hyperparameters. Subsequently, the empirical selection of its hyperparameters is a challenging process. Therefore, it is essential to determine optimal hyperparameters by utilizing metaheuristic optimization algorithms before deploying a model for a particular application. Hence, many researchers recently implemented various metaheuristic algorithms for tuning the hyperparameters of deep learning models [29–33].

Before applying the proposed optimized SeResNet-BiLSTM-CA architecture for the identification of exons, pre-processing is done using MGWT to extract the periodic features. Then, the efficiency of the proposed model is validated by comparing it with the pre-trained models, and the ablation experiments are carried out on each module to verify its significance.

Finally, we analyzed the efficacy of the proposed model using the Genscan training set and the HMR195 dataset. The main contribution of the proposed work is:

Theoretical basis

Squeeze and excitation residual network

In deep learning literature, CNN has proved its excellent feature extraction ability in various applications by extending the depth of the structure. However, the extremely increased network depth leads to high resource utilization [34]. To overcome this problem, the residual network was introduced [35]. A skip connection in the residual block reduces the vanishing gradient problem in CNNs. The architecture of the residual network used in this work is shown in Fig. 1.

Structure of residual network

Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks (SeNet) [36] enhance the accuracy of the structure by modeling interdependency between the channels. The SeNet module aims to modify the feature maps at each squeeze and excitation (SE) stage. The structure of SeNet is illustrated in Fig. 2. The SeNet recalibrates the feature maps using a squeeze operation that preserves the channel dimension while performing global average pooling over special dimensions of the input.

Structure of squeeze-and-excitation networks

Afterwards, the output of the squeeze operation is passed through two fully connected layer networks that perform the excitation operation. This network generates channel-wise weights by predicting the correlation between feature channels. These weighted features are then used to enhance the overall performance of the model.

This paper utilizes a Se-Residual network (SeResNet) [24] for analyzing DNA sequences that integrate the strengths of SeNet and ResidualNet. The structure of SeResNet is illustrated in Fig. 3. The SeResNet retrieves channel-wise spatial information from the pre-processed DNA sequences. Incorporating the SE component into ResNet can help the model focus on significant channels and increase its capacity to acquire long-term features. Moreover, SeResNet enriches the identification accuracy by integrating the strengths of both networks.

It contains two LSTM layers, the first LSTM layer processes the data in the forward direction and another LSTM is in reverse order. The structure of BiLSTM is shown in the Fig. 4.

Customized attention (CA) mechanism

The CA layer is added between the feature extraction and output layers to improve the network architecture's accessibility by concentrating on a particular portion of the data. The CA mechanism [23] is implemented to identify the correlation between all the temporal input hidden units. Further, it dynamically weights the input data to make it more significant, which is important for accurately detecting the coding regions. The CA mechanism as shown in Fig. 5, is integrated into the network architecture to enhance the performance of the model.

Architecture of the customized attention layer

In this process, a linear layer is used to convert the input data into a lower-dimensional form. Then, the Tanh layer is used to enhance the feature representation. Following that, an additional linear layer is used to convert the feature representation into a vector. To get the weight of each feature, a sigmoid operation is performed on this vector. Hence, the weight of each element indicates its importance in the overall data. Finally, weighted features are obtained by performing the dot product between each input data and its equivalent weight. These weighted features improve the identification accuracy. Dataset

The effectiveness of the proposed approach for the identification of exons in eukaryotic DNA is evaluated using two standard datasets, namely HMR195 [39] and the Genscan training set [40], [S1]. These datasets consist of both single-exon and multiple-exon genes. These gene sequences contain limited coding regions and large non-coding regions. Hence, it is a significant challenge for the accurate detection of exons.

Pre-processing

DNA sequences are composed of four nucleotide bases, namely, adenine (A), cytosine (C), guanine (G), and thymine (T). In this paper, the Voss [41] mapping approach is employed to transform the characteristic DNA sequence into numerical form due to its superior performance in Genomic signal processing. Furthermore, the period-3 component of exons is extracted using the MGWT [14] technique, and the resulting spectral information is used as the classification features. The MGWT provides an effective analysis of the DNA sequences at a regularly varying scale.

Optimized SeResNet-BiLSTM-attention architecture

A hybrid optimized SeResNet-BiLSTM-CA framework is proposed in this work for effective classification of exons and introns in eukaryotic DNA sequences. Several important aspects inspired the decision to use this particular model. First, the SeResNet module extracts deeply hidden special features from the pre-processed data. The SeResNet module is a variant of ResNet that employs squeeze and excitation blocks to improve the performance of the ResNet module by recalibrating the correlations between channels and emphasizing the prominent features. Second, the BiLSTM module is for sequential data learning to exploit the temporal data. Third, the CA mechanism is employed to highlight significant features by redistributing the weights of the features extracted from the SeResNet-BiLSTM model. Finally, a classification module is used for categorizing the coding and non-coding regions. However, the performance of the model significantly depends on its hyperparameters. In SeResNet, BiLSTM, CA blocks, the hyperparameters required to train the proposed framework, like size (F) and number of convolution kernels (K) in SeResNet, number of neurons in BiLSTM, number of hidden units (H) in CA module, learning rate (lr), batch size (B), and optimizer (Opt), etc., have a significant influence on the model performance. To address this issue, the SFOA algorithm is employed in this work to optimize the hyperparameters of the model.

Among the aforementioned hyperparameters, the kernel size and number of kernels directly affect the feature extraction capability of the model. Larger kernel size extracts higher-level global information, whereas a smaller kernel size extracts local features. The number of kernels represents the depth of the feature maps and is used to enhance the feature representation and prevent redundancy. During the training process, the Optimizer is a significant factor for updating the parameters of the model.

The learning rate (lr) is a crucial element for estimating how rapidly a model controls the step size for updating its parameters. A proper lr improves the convergence speed, while too high lr value can lead to instability, and too low lr value can cause slow convergence. Therefore, to determine the model’s efficacy, the learning rate must be appropriately adjusted. The batch size (B) directly influences the convergence speed and efficiency of the optimization. A large batch size requires more memory and leads to longer training time. Hence, the proper selection of batch size is required to ascertain the performance of the model.

Experimental results and discussion

This section analyzes and validates the efficiency of the proposed method using the two datasets, namely HMR195 and the Genscan training set.

Preliminary procedure

In this work, the HMR195 and Genscan training datasets are used for evaluating the proposed method. Initially, the Voss numerical mapping [41] technique is applied to transfer the character DNA sequences into numerical sequences. Later, the MGWT [14] approach is employed to extract the periodic features. After getting the features, some preliminary procedures are carried out to prepare them for analyzing and training the proposed algorithm.

Initially, normalize the features within the range 0 to 1, and then they are divided into two categories based on their periodicity. Subsequently, the non-periodic and periodic sequences are split into several frames with an overlapping of 100 samples, each frame having a length of 256. Later, a class is assigned to each frame based on its periodicity. If the frame contains periodic features, it is classified as class 1; otherwise, it is classified as class 2. Following the preprocessing, 14,164 frames were in the Genscan training set and 8885 frames in the HMR195 dataset. These frames are randomly divided into 80% for training the network and 20% for testing the efficiency of the trained network. After dataset splitting, the HMR195 test set contained a total of 1777 frames, comprising 208 exon frames and 1569 intron frames. Similarly, the Genscan training test set contained 2833 frames, including 277 exon frames and 2556 intron frames. These statistics confirm the inherent imbalance between exon and intron classes in both datasets, which reflects the realistic biological distribution observed in genomic sequences.

The proposed model is trained and tested on a workstation equipped with an Intel(R) Xeon (R) Gold CPU and a NVIDIA 16 GB GPU @2.5 GHz. The software framework, MATLAB deep learning toolbox of MTLAB R2024a version, and the Windows 11 Pro operating system. The average training time for the datasets is 20 min per experiment.

Experiments on SFOA optimized SeResNet-BiLSTM-CA model

The performance of the proposed model, SeResNet-BiLSTM-CA, relies on the efficient optimization of its hyperparameters. In this work, SFOA [38] and Particle swarm optimization (PSO) [42] algorithms are employed for the selection of optimal parameters. To validate the accuracy and fitness function of the test results, the same experimental environment and parameters are used. In this work, the maximum iteration is set to 20, the initial population size is described as 10, and each iteration of the algorithms was executed for a fixed duration of 50 epochs.

Optimizing the hyperparameters is crucial for achieving optimum model performance. In this work the hyperparameters of the model such as kernel size (F1, F2, F3, F4) and number of kernels (K1, K2, K3, K4) in the convolution layers present in the SeResNet, the number of hidden neurons (N) of BiLSTM, number of hidden units (H) in FC used in attention block, optimizer (Opt), batch size (B), and learning rate (lr) in training options, are optimized using SFOA algorithm.

Conclusion

This paper introduced a novel hybrid SFOA-based optimized-SeResNet-BiLSTM-CA method for the detection of exons in eukaryotic DNA sequences. The proposed model employed a SeResNet-BiLSTM module to extract deeply hidden spatiotemporal features by exploiting the correlation information between channels, and a CA module to focus on more significant features by assigning weights to spatiotemporal data to enhance classification accuracy. Further, the classification performance can be improved by employing the SFOA to optimize the hyperparameters of the SeResNet-BiLSTM-CA model. The experimental analysis was conducted to verify the efficiency of the proposed model using the Genscan training sets and the HMR195 dataset. The proposed architecture achieves a maximum value in terms of ACC, AUC, F1s, Pre, and Rec. To validate the efficacy of the proposed approach, its performance is compared with several pre-trained networks and existing techniques. The results reveal that the proposed approach provides superior classification performance and outperforms existing methods. Overall, this study presents a promising approach for analyzing the eukaryotic DNA sequences to detect the exon locations.

Abstract
. We revisit results of Fujino–Sato on complete non-projective ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial toric varieties and their conjectural factorization by flips. We show that their main results admit short conceptual proofs, avoiding any restriction on the dimension and the Picard number, from the general theory of Cox rings and Mori Dream Spaces, once one organizes small ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial modifications via the GKZ (secondary fan) decomposition of the moving cone. Moreover, we extend this viewpoint beyond the toric case by proving an analogous statement for complete ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial weak Mori Dream Spaces: any non-projective such variety admits a divisor D D and a D D -flip to a (projective) Mori Dream Space. Our approach highlights the role of chambers and wall-crossing in the secondary fan as a unifying framework for these constructions.

Introduction

The birational geometry of a complete variety is often governed by the interplay between divisor cones and small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modifications. In the toric setting this philosophy becomes especially concrete: the movable cone carries a canonical chamber decomposition (the Gelfand-Kapranov-Zelewinsky (GKZ) one or secondary fan) whose chambers encode nef cones of birational models and whose walls govern flips and related wall-crossing operations.

In the recent paper
[
2
]
, Fujino and Sato proved that every complete toric variety is birational to a projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial toric variety via a map which is an isomorphism in codimension one, and they formulated a conjecture predicting that any complete
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial toric variety can be connected to a projective one by a finite sequence of flips, flops and anti-flips (verifying it for smooth threefold of Picard number at most 5). The purpose of the present note is to explain that these results are naturally framed, and in fact streamlined, by the Cox ring/Mori Dream Space machinery, once one adopts the secondary fan viewpoint.

More precisely, our first contribution is a short conceptual derivation of the Fujino–Sato results from the general theory developed by Hu and Keel in their pivotal paper
[
3
]
and extended to the complete non-projective setup by Arzhantsev, Derenthal, Hausen and Laface in their extensive treatment
[
1
]
, together with the description of small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modifications as wall-crossing in the GKZ decomposition, as described in our previous work
[
4
]
. In this language, the existence of a projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial model is reflected by the presence of a full-dimensional GKZ chamber contained in the moving cone, while passing from a non-projective situation to a projective one is governed by crossing suitable walls.

Our second contribution is a genuine extension of this picture beyond toric varieties. We prove an analogous statement for complete
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
weak Mori Dream Spaces
(wMDS): if
X
is non-projective, then there exists a divisor
D
and a
D
-flip producing a (projective) Mori Dream Space. This provides a common conceptual framework for the Fujino–Sato phenomenon and its wMDS generalization, emphasizing that the key mechanism is the chamber structure on the movable cone rather than toric combinatorics per se.

The note is intentionally brief. Its main goal is to isolate a clean conceptual explanation and to point out that the secondary fan viewpoint offers an efficient “navigation tool” through birational models and wall-crossing, both in the toric and in the (weak) Mori Dream Space setting.

2.
A short proof of Theorem
1.1

First of all, notice that, given a complete toric variety

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial resolution

whose exceptional locus is the union of the closure of the torus orbits of the special points of any new introduced face. Then

is isomorphic in codimension 1 to

1.1
then follows by recalling that a complete
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial toric variety

is always a weak Mori Dream Space (wMDS), as defined in Definition 7 of
[
4
]

Theorem 2.1

(Lemma 1 in
[
4
]
, Thm. 4.3.3.1 in
[
1
]
)
.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and complete algebraic variety
X
is a wMDS if and only if there exists small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification (s
\mathbb{Q}
m)
f:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
such that
X^{\prime}
is a MDS.

Remark 2.2

Let us recall a few facts from notation introduced in
[

(i)

A wMDS is an irreducible and

(1)

Then, in Theorem

3.
The Fujino-Sato Conjecture revisited and proved

The above Conjecture
1.2
is implied by the following

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a non-projective complete
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial wMDS. Then, there exists a Cartier divisor
D
of
X
and a
D
-flip
f:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
such that
X^{\prime}
is a MDS. In particular,
X
is a toric variety if and only if
X^{\prime}
is a toric variety.

Proof.

If
X
is non-projective then
\Nef(X)
cannot be a full dimensional chamber of the secondary fan supported by the moving cone
\overline{\Mov(X)}
. Then it is a face of a full dimensional chamber
\gamma
. By Prop. 9 in
[
4
]
, there exists a MDS
X^{\prime}
and a s
\mathbb{Q}
m
f:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
such that
\gamma=f^{*}\Nef(X^{\prime})
: actually this is a consequence of Thm. 4.3.3.1 in
[
1
]
, which is a generalization to the complete, non-projective setup of Prop. 1.11 in
[
3
]
. Considerations given in §
2.1
show that
\mathcal{N}:=\Relint(f^{*}\Nef(X^{\prime})\setminus\Nef(X))\neq\emptyset
. Since
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, the choice of a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor whose class is in
\mathcal{N}
gives rise, up to a multiple, to a Cartier divisor
D
such that
[D]\in\mathcal{N}
. Then

is a
D
-flip. What observed in Remark
2.2
shows that
X
is toric if and only if
X^{\prime}
is toric.
∎

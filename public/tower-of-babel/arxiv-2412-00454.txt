Positioned and primary positioned 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroups
C. Cisto, R. Tapia-Ramos

Abstract
Let 𝒞 \mathcal{C} be a positive integer cone and k ∈ 𝒞 k\in\mathcal{C} . A 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroup S S is k k -positioned if for every h ∈ 𝒞 ∖ S h\in\mathcal{C}\setminus S we have that k − h k-h belongs to S S . In this work, we focus on this family of semigroups and introduce primary positioned 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroups, characterizing a subfamily of them through the perspective of irreducibility. Furthermore, we provide some procedures to compute all such semigroups, describing a family of graphs containing all the primary positioned 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroups for a fixed k ∈ 𝒞 k\in\mathcal{C} .



Key words:

\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, generalized numerical semigroups,
k
-positioned, primary positioned, irreducibility, symmetry and pseudo-symmetry.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of non-negative integers and denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
the set of non-negative rational numbers. Considering a finite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
(for a non-zero natural number
d
), the set

\mathrm{cone}(A)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}a_{i}\mid n\in\mathbb{N},a_{i}\in A,q_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\right\}

is the
rational affine cone
spanned by
A
. We say that
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{cone}(A)\cap\mathbb{N}^{d}
is the
positive integer affine cone
spanned by
A
. See
[
6
]
for a detailed survey on this topic.
From now on, we omit the word “affine”, and assume that every cone we introduce throughout the work is affine. Let
S
be an additive submonoid of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. We say that a subset
B\subseteq S
generates
S
if

S=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\mid n,\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N},b_{i}\in B\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\right\}.

If no proper subset of
B
generates
S
, then
B
is called a
minimal system of generators of
S
. The monoid
S
is called an
affine semigroup
if there exists a finite set
B\subset S
that generates it. In
[
27
]
, it is established that every affine semigroup has a unique minimal system of generators, and we denote it by
\operatorname{msg}(S)
. Given a positive integer cone
\mathcal{C}
, an affine semigroup
S\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is called a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup if the set
\mathcal{C}\setminus S
is finite. For further details on systems of generators of a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, see also
[
8
,
14
]
.

The notion of
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup has been introduced in
[
19
]
as a generalization of the concept of
numerical semigroup
.
Recall that a numerical semigroup is a submonoid of
\mathbb{N}
having a finite complement in it. In particular, this coincides with that of a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup where
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{N}
. Another special case is when
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{N}^{d}
(for
d>1
), which has been considered first in
[
15
]
and where this kind of monoids are called
generalized numerical semigroups
(GNS for short).
Numerical semigroups have been extensively studied in the literature, with notable references such as
[
28
]
and the references therein.
A recent research direction has been developed to generalize properties and results, regarding numerical semigroups, in the more general context of
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups or GNSs.

Throughout this paper, if
h
is a vector in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
for
d>1
, we denote by
h^{(i)}
its
i
-th coordinate. Moreover, we use
0
to denote both the zero vector and zero integer, since there is no risk of confusion. Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. We denote by
\mathcal{H}(S)=\mathcal{C}\setminus S
, which is called the
set of gaps
of
S
, and its cardinality, referred to as the
genus
of
S
, is denoted by
\operatorname{g}(S)
. These definitions naturally arise from the analogous concepts in the framework of numerical semigroups.
A key difference between the cases
d=1
and
d>1
is the existence of a natural total order on
\mathbb{N}
.
If
d=1
, some important invariants of a numerical semigroup strongly depend on the total order. Among them, we recall the
Frobenius number
, which is
\operatorname{F}(S)=\max(\mathcal{H}(S))
(conventionally
\operatorname{F}(S)=-1
), the
conductor
, that is
\operatorname{c}(S)=\operatorname{F}(S)+1
, and the
multiplicity
, which is
\operatorname{m}(S)=\min(S\setminus\{0\})
.
In the case
d>1
, a generalization of the Frobenius number can be considered by fixing a term order on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Recall that a
term order
(or monomial order)
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
is a total order satisfying
0\preceq v
for all
v\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and if
u\preceq v
for
u,v\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, then
u+w\preceq v+w
for all
w\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
.
A detailed discussion of orders can be found in
[
11
]
.
Following the definitions in

[
15
,
19
]
, we define the
Frobenius element
of
S
with respect to
\preceq
as the vector
\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)=\max_{\preceq}\mathcal{H}(S)
(conventionally
\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(\mathbb{N}^{d})=(-1,\ldots,-1)
).

The arguments developed in this paper are inspired by the concept of
positioned numerical semigroup
, introduced by
[
5
]
as an extension of the notion of symmetric numerical semigroup. In particular, given
S
a numerical semigroup and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
S
is called

-positioned if for all
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
, we have
k-h\in S
. Furthermore,
S
is said to be
positioned
if it is
\operatorname{F}(S)+\operatorname{m}(S)
-positioned. The same authors obtained many properties and results on this family of numerical semigroups in different articles, for instance, the one mentioned above and
[
23
,
24
]
(see also the thesis
[
16
]
for an overview).
Drawing inspiration from some results presented in the mentioned papers, in our work, given a positive integer cone
\mathcal{C}
, and
k\in\mathcal{C}
, we introduce the notion of
k
-positioned for
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups and explore some properties of it.

The paper is structured more in detail as follows. In Section
1
, we provide some notations and recall the notion of symmetric and pseudo-symmetric

-semigroups, along with their main properties, which will be useful throughout our work. In Section

. In Section

1
Background

This section is devoted to introducing several preliminaries. Specifically, we define the concepts of symmetric and pseudo-symmetric
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups. Additionally, we present some well-known results related to these definitions, which will be useful in the upcoming sections.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. A gap
x\in\mathcal{H}(S)
of
S
is called a
pseudo-Frobenius element of
S
if
x+S\setminus\{0\}\subseteq S
. The set of pseudo-Frobenius elements of
S
is denoted by
\operatorname{PF}(S)
. Let
\preceq
be a term order on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. We say that
S
is
symmetric
if
\operatorname{PF}(S)=\{\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)\}
. Similarly, we say that
S
is
pseudo-symmetric
if
\operatorname{PF}(S)=\{\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S),\frac{\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)}{2}\}
. A semigroup
S
is called
irreducible
if it is either symmetric or pseudo-symmetric. This definition corresponds to
[
20
, Theorem 21 and Proposition 23]
.

This work considers different orders on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, apart from a term order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Given a non-empty set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
and elements
x,y\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, the partial order induced by
A
is defined by,
x\leq_{A}y
if and only if
y-x\in A
. The partial order induced by a numerical semigroup has been widely studied (see
[
13
,
21
,
28
]
). Furthermore, the partial order induced by a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup has also been discussed in the literature (see
[
3
,
20
,
22
]
). Building on these induced orders, and drawing inspiration from
[
18
]
for any
k\in\mathcal{C}
we defined the sets,

\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)=\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}k\},\quad\operatorname{I}_{S}(k)=\{x\in S\mid x\leq_{S}k\}.

It is pointed out that our definition of
\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)
coincides with the definition of
\mathcal{B}(k)
provided in
[
29
]
.

From now on, for any set
X
, the symbol
|X|
denotes the cardinality of the set
X
. Observe that, for any
k\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{H}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)
, it holds that
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|=\operatorname{g}(S)+|\operatorname{I}_{S}(k)|
.

In the following statements, we show the main characterizations of symmetric and pseudo-symmetric
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, along with the required references, respectively.

Proposition 1
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Then the following statements are equivalent conditions for
S
to be symmetric:

1.

(
[
18
, Proposition 3]
)
\operatorname{g}(S)=|\operatorname{I}_{S}(\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S))|
for some term order
\preceq
in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

2.

(
[
4
, Theorem 3.6]
) For some term order
\preceq
in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, it holds
\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)-h\in S
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Then the following statements are equivalent conditions for
S
to be pseudo-symmetric:

1.

(
[
18
, Proposition 4]
)
\operatorname{g}(S)=1+|\operatorname{I}_{S}(\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S))|
and
\frac{\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)}{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, for some term order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

2.

(
[
4
, Theorem 3.7]
) For some term order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, it holds
\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)-h\in S
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)\setminus\left\{\frac{\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)}{2}\right\}
.

For any
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
, an element
x\in\mathcal{H}(S)
is called a
special gap
of
S
if
x\in\operatorname{PF}(S)
and
2x\in S
. The set of all special gaps of
S
is denoted by
\operatorname{SG}(S)
. Observe that if
S
is irreducible, then
\operatorname{SG}(S)=\{\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)
}, for some term order
\preceq
. Furthermore, let us denote
\mathcal{F}(S)=\{f\in\mathcal{C}\mid f=\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)\text{ for some term order}\preceq
}.

Proposition 3
.

[
3
, Proposition 4.3]

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Then

\mathcal{F}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))\subseteq\operatorname{SG}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{PF}(S).

As a consequence of the above result, we present the following.

Corollary 4
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. The following holds:

1.

If
|\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))|=1
, then the Frobenius element is the same for every term order.

2.

If
S
is irreducible, then
\mathrm{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{H}(S)=\{\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)\}
for every term order
\preceq
.

In the case where
S
is irreducible
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, the Frobenius element of
S
, as stated in the above corollary, is independent of the chosen term order. Therefore, from this point forward, when there is no risk of ambiguity or misunderstanding, we will use the notation
\operatorname{F}(S)
instead of
\operatorname{F}_{\preceq}(S)
.

2
k
-positioned
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups

A
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
is
k
-positioned for some
k\in\mathcal{C}
if for every
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
we have that
k-h
belongs to
S
. Notice that every
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is
k
-positioned for a large enough
k\in\mathcal{C}
.

This section discusses the concept of being
k
-positioned, providing several characterizations and key results. Our approach is inspired by the concept of being
k
-positioned, as discussed in
[
5
]
for numerical semigroups.

For a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
, consider the set

X_{S}=\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid h\leq_{\mathcal{C}}x\text{ for all }h\in\mathcal{H}(S)\}.

Observe that, if
S
is a numerical semigroup, then
\mathrm{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(X_{S})=\{\operatorname{F}(S)\}
. If
S
is a GNS in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we have
\mathrm{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(X_{S})=\{\operatorname{Cr}(S)\}
, where each
i
-th coordinate of
\operatorname{Cr}(S)
is defined as
\operatorname{Cr}(S)^{(i)}=\max\{h^{(i)}\mid h\in\mathcal{H}(S)\}
.
Consider
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}
the set of canonical basis vectors of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, the invariant
\operatorname{Cr}(S)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}e_{i}
is called the
corner element
of
S
, first introduced in
[
2
]
(see also
[
1
]
for some other properties related to it). If
S
is not a GNS, then the cardinality of the set

\mathrm{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(X_{S})
can be greater than one, as shown in the following example.

Example 5
.

Consider the positive integer cone
\mathcal{C}
spanned by
\{(4,1),(5,3)\}
, and the
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
minimally generated by

\{(4,1),(4,2),(5,2),(5,3),(7,3),(7,4),(10,3),(11,3)\},

whose set of gaps is
\mathcal{H}(S)=\{(2,1),(3,1),(6,2),(6,3),(7,2)\}
. We obtain that
\mathrm{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(X_{S})=\{(12,5),(13,5),(14,5)\}
.

From now on, suppose that
S
is a
k
-positioned
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup for some
k\in\mathcal{C}
. It is not difficult to see that
k\in X_{S}
. In the context of numerical semigroups, it is known that
k=\operatorname{F}(S)
if and only if
S
is symmetric (see
[
5
]
). We generalize this property in a more general framework.

Proposition 6
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Then
S
is
k
-positioned for some
k\in\mathcal{H}(S)
if and only if
S
is symmetric and
\operatorname{F}(S)=k
.

Proof.

Suppose that
S
is
k
-positioned and
k\in\mathcal{H}(S)
. Consider the map

\varphi_{S}:\mathcal{H}(S)\longrightarrow\operatorname{I}_{S}(k)\quad\text{ defined by }\quad h\longmapsto k-h.

Since
S
is
k
-positioned, the map
\varphi
is well-defined, and by construction, it follows that it is injective. Therefore
\operatorname{g}(S)\leq|\operatorname{I}_{S}(k)|
. Let
s\in\operatorname{I}_{S}(k)
, it follows that
h=k-s
belongs to
\mathcal{H}(S)
, otherwise
k\notin\mathcal{H}(S)
. In particular,
\varphi(h)=s
, and thus the map is bijective. So,
\operatorname{g}(S)=|\operatorname{I}_{S}(k)|
. Since
k-h\in S
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
, we deduce that
h\leq_{\mathcal{C}}k
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
and using Corollary
4
this implies that
k=\operatorname{F}(S)
and by applying Proposition
1
we conclude that
S
is symmetric.

Conversely, since
k=\operatorname{F}(S)
, and by Proposition
1
we obtain that
\operatorname{F}(S)-h\in S
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
,
which is the definition of
k
-positioned.
∎

Suppose
S
is a
k
-positioned
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup with
k\in\mathcal{H}(S)
. So, for every
x\in X_{S}
, we have
h\leq_{\mathcal{C}}x
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
, in particular
k\leq_{\mathcal{C}}x
, and this fact implies that
\mathrm{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(X_{S})=\{k\}
. Assuming now that
S
is
k
-positioned with
\mathrm{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(X_{S})=\{k\}
. It is natural to ask whether
k\in\mathcal{H}(S)
. While this holds for numerical semigroups, as we have explained above, but it is not generally the case for
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups, as we illustrate in the following example.

.

Let

be the GNS in
\mathbb{N}^{2}

\mathcal{H}(S)=\{(0,1),(0,3),(1,0),(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,0),(2,1),(4,1)\}.

Based on the previous considerations, given a
k
-positioned
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, we assume that
k
is an element of the
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup.

If
S
is a numerical semigroup, it is known that
k\geq\operatorname{F}(S)+\operatorname{m}(S)
. In fact, if
\operatorname{F}(S)<k<\operatorname{F}(S)+\operatorname{m}(S)
, then can be expressed as
k=\operatorname{F}(S)+i
with
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\operatorname{m}(S)-1\}\subset\mathcal{H}(S)
and
k-i\notin S
, whence there are no
k
-positioned numerical semigroups. This argument can be interpreted to
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups differently to obtain a generalization of the above inequality.
To this purpose, for any
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
, we introduce the following sets (inspired by similar definitions provided in
[
9
]
).

\operatorname{C}(S)=\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}h,\text{ for some }h\in\mathcal{H}(S)\}
,

\operatorname{M}(S)=\big\{h\in\mathcal{H}(S)\mid\operatorname{I}_{S}(h)=\{0\}\big\}\cup\{0\}
.

We present the following characterization.

Proposition 8
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Then,
\operatorname{M}(S)=\{0\}
if and only if
S=\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

If
S=\mathcal{C}
trivially
\operatorname{M}(S)=\{0\}
. Conversely, assume
\operatorname{M}(S)=\{0\}
and suppose that there exists
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
. So, there exist
z_{1}\in S\setminus\{0\}
and
c_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
z_{1}+c_{1}=h
. In particular, since
h\notin S
, we deduce
c_{1}\in\mathcal{H}(S)
. Again, we have
c_{1}\notin\operatorname{M}(S)
and applying the same argument we obtain
z_{2}\in S\setminus\{0\}
and
c_{2}\in\mathcal{H}(S)
such that
z_{2}+c_{2}=c_{1}
. Continuing this process, we obtain an infinite sequence of elements
\{c_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(S)
, which is impossible since
S
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Hence,
S=\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Observe that if
S
is a numerical semigroup, then
|\operatorname{M}(S)|=\operatorname{m}(S)
and
|\operatorname{C}(S)|=\operatorname{F}(S)+1
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
S
be a
k
-positioned
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup with
k\in S
. Then, the following map

\phi_{S}:\operatorname{M}(S)\longrightarrow\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)\setminus\operatorname{C}(S)\quad\text{ defined by }\quad h\longmapsto k-h

is well-defined and injective. In particular,
|\operatorname{M}(S)|+|\operatorname{C}(S)|\leq|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|
.

Proof.

Since
S
is
k
-positioned, if
h\in\operatorname{M}(S)
, then
k-h\notin\operatorname{C}(S)
. Otherwise, there exists at least one element
y\in\mathcal{H}(S)
such that
k-h\leq_{\mathcal{C}}y
. By using that
\leq_{\mathcal{C}}
is compatible with the addition,
k-y\leq_{\mathcal{C}}h
. If
k-y\in\mathcal{H}(S)
then
S
is not
k
-positioned, therefore
k-y\in S
. Taking into account that
h\in\operatorname{M}(S)
, we have
k-y=0
and thus
k=y\in\mathcal{H}(S)
, which is a contradiction. By construction
\phi_{S}
is injective, and thus
|\operatorname{M}(S)|+|\operatorname{C}(S)|\leq|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|
.
∎

In line with Proposition
9
, we say that a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is
primary positioned
if it is
k
-positioned for some
k\in\mathcal{C}
, and
|\operatorname{M}(S)|+|\operatorname{C}(S)|=|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|
.
The property for a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
of be primary positioned for some
k
implies that
k\in S
. Otherwise
\operatorname{C}(S)=\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)
, and since
|\operatorname{M}(S)|\geq 1
we have
|\operatorname{M}(S)|+|\operatorname{C}(S)|>|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|
.

In the context of numerical semigroups, that is, if we assume that
S

We say that any
\mathcal{C}
-semigorup

\mathcal{C}
-semigroup

]

The main objective of this section is to study the connection between the two previous classes and the property of being primary positioned.

\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}(k)=\{(1,i)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid 0\leq i\leq\lambda\}\cup\{(0,0),(2,\lambda)\},

and thus
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}(k)|=\lambda+3
. Observe that for every
i,j\in\{0,\ldots,\lambda\}
such that
i<j
we have
(1,i)\nleq_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}(1,j)
.
Suppose that
S
is a primary positioned
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
-semigroup for
k
. By definition
\operatorname{M}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{C}(S)
. If
h\in\operatorname{C}(S)\setminus\operatorname{M}(S)
, then there exists
z\in S\setminus\{0\}
such that
z<_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}h
. By construction,
h,z\in\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}(k)\setminus\{0,k\}
, which is impossible since as we have seen all the elements in the set
\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}(k)\setminus\{0,k\}
are not comparable each other with respect to the partial order
\leq_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}
. As a consequence,
\operatorname{C}(S)=\operatorname{M}(S)
, and thus
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}(k)|
is even, which contradicts the parity of the cardinality of the set.

Given any positive integer cone
\mathcal{C}
and
k\in\mathcal{C}
, as we have observed in the example, it is not possible to construct in general a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup that is primary positioned for
k
. The key reason why this is not feasible lies in the properties of the set
\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}(k)
. Specifically, for some
x,y\in\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}}(k)\setminus\{0,k\}
, it must hold that
x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}y
or
y\leq_{\mathcal{C}}x
. Consequently, if we take the cone
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{N}^{d}
, this property is satisfied, allowing us to state the following theorem.

Theorem 23
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. There exists a primary positioned GNS for
k
if and only if
k\notin\{2e_{i},4e_{i}\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\}
.

Proof.

Necessary:
If
k_{1}=2e_{i}
or
k_{2}=4e_{i}
, then
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k_{1})|=3
and
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k_{2})|=5
, respectively. By Proposition
21
, we conclude that no primary positioned GNS exists.

Sufficiency:
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\setminus\{2e_{i},4e_{i}\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\}
. Observe that
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)|\neq 3
and
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)|\neq 5
for every
1\leq i\leq d
. We distinguish two cases, depending on the parity of
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)|
. If
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)|
is even, we are under the hypothesis of Proposition
18
, and we conclude that there exists a primary positioned GNS. Suppose
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)|
is an odd number different to
3
and
5
. If
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)|=1
then
k=0\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and by Remark
19
we obtain that
S=\mathbb{N}^{d}
is primary positioned for
k\in\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\preceq
be a term orde and define
\mathcal{S}_{k}=\{0\}\cup\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\mid x\succ\frac{k}{2}\}\setminus\{k\}
. By Lemma
20
we have that
\frac{k}{2}\in\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)
, and by
[
18
, Lemma 12]
we know that
\mathcal{S}_{k}
is pseudo-symmetric, and thus
T=\mathcal{S}_{k}\cup\{k,\frac{k}{2}\}
is a PEPSY-semigroup. Consider
x=\frac{k}{2}+e_{i_{0}}=\min_{\preceq}\{\frac{k}{2}+e_{i}\in\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\}
and
S=T\setminus\{x\}\cup\{k-x\}
. In particular, observe that
k-x=\frac{k}{2}-e_{i_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
e_{i_{0}}\lneq_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{k}{2}
, the equality cannot be reached since by hypothesis
k\neq 2e_{i_{0}}
. Therefore
e_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{H}(T)
. Let us prove that
S
is GNS. It suffices to show that
S
is closed under addition, since the complement of
S
in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
is finite. Take
s_{1},s_{2}\in S\setminus\{0\}
and we distinguish the following cases:

•

If
s_{1},s_{2}\in T\setminus\{0,x\}
, then
s_{1}+s_{2}\in T
, since
T
is a GNS. Suppose
s_{1}+s_{2}=x=\frac{k}{2}+e_{i_{0}}
, therefore
s_{1}+s_{2}-e_{i_{0}}=\frac{k}{2}
and without loss of generality, we can assume that
s_{2}-e_{i_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. It implies that
s_{1}\leq_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{k}{2}
. By definition of
T
we have
s_{1}=\frac{k}{2}
, and thus we deduce that
s_{2}=e_{i_{0}}
which is a contradiction, since
e_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{H}(T)
. So,
s_{1}+s_{2}\in T\setminus\{x\}\subset S
.

•

Suppose
s_{1}\in T\setminus\{0,x\}
,
s_{2}=k-x=\frac{k}{2}-e_{i_{0}}
. First, by construction,
T+y\subseteq T
for every
y\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. So,
s_{1}+(k-x)\in T
. Assume
s_{1}+(k-x)\notin S
. The only possibility is
s_{1}+(k-x)=x
and by simple operations we arrive to
s_{1}=2e_{i_{0}}\in T\setminus\{0\}
. So, by definition of
T
,
2e_{i_{0}}\succ\frac{k}{2}
and equality is not possible since
k\neq 4e_{i_{0}}
. By the properties of the term order, we obtain
\frac{k}{2}\lneq k\prec\frac{k}{2}+2e_{i_{0}}
. In terms of the
i_{0}
-coordinates, we deduce that
\frac{k^{(i_{0})}}{2}\leq 2
. If the rest of the coordinates
j\neq i_{0}
of
k
are
0
, then
k\in\{2e_{i_{0}},4e_{i_{0}}\}
, which contradicts our hypothesis. If there exists
j\neq i_{0}
such that the
j
-coordinate is
k^{(j)}\neq 0
, we deduce that
k^{(j)}\geq 2
since all the coordinates of
k
have to be even. So,
\frac{k^{(j)}}{2}\geq 1
, and thus

\frac{k}{2}+e_{j}\leq k
, that is
\frac{k}{2}+e_{j}\in\operatorname{I}_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}(k)
. On one hand, since
\frac{k^{(i_{0})}}{2}\leq 2
, we obtain that
\frac{k}{2}+e_{j}+e_{i_{0}}\leq_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}k\prec\frac{k}{2}+2e_{i_{0}}
. On the other hand, by the minimality of
e_{i_{0}}
, we conclude that
\frac{k}{2}+2e_{i_{0}}\prec\frac{k}{2}+e_{j}+e_{i_{0}}
, since
\frac{k}{2}+e_{i_{0}}\prec\frac{k}{2}+e_{j}
,
contradicting the previous inequality. Hence,
s_{1}+s_{2}\in T\setminus\{x\}\subset S
.

•

If
s_{1}=s_{2}=k-x=\frac{k}{2}-e_{i_{0}}
, then
s_{1}+s_{2}=k-2e_{i_{0}}
. Trivially, if
2(k-x)=x
then
k=\frac{3}{2}x
, which is false since
k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Moreover,
k-2e_{i_{0}}\succ\frac{k}{2}
, otherwise
k\preceq\frac{k}{2}+2e_{i_{0}}
, a contradiction as we have shown in the previous case. We conclude that
s_{1}+s_{2}\in S
.

Since
T
is a PEPSY-semigroup, by Proposition
16
, we know that it is
k
-positioned and
\operatorname{g}(T)=\frac{|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|-3}{2}
. So, by construction, we obtain that
S
is
k
-positioned and
\operatorname{g}(S)=\operatorname{g}(T)=\frac{|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|-3}{2}
. Using Proposition
16
again, we conclude
S
is a PEPSY-semigroup. Finally, since
\frac{k}{2}\leq_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}x
and
x\in\mathcal{H}(S)
, we have
\frac{k}{2}\in\operatorname{C}(S)
. By Theorem
17
, we obtain
S
is primary positioned for
k
.
∎

5
An arrangement for the set of primary positioned
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup for a fixed
k\in\mathcal{C}

From now on, let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be a positive integer cone,
\preceq
a term order on

\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and let
k\in\mathcal{C}
. The set of all
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup that are primary positioned for

is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(k)
. Hereafter,
\mathcal{P}(k)
is a non-empty set. The aim of this section is to provide a graphical classification of the set
\mathcal{P}(k)
. To achieve this, we begin by introducing the following notation. For any
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
, we consider the set

\operatorname{B}(S)=\left\{x\in\operatorname{msg}(S)\mid x\in\operatorname{C}(S)\setminus\left(\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(S\setminus\{0\})\right),k-x\in S,x\neq\tfrac{k}{2}\right\},

Let us denote by
\mathcal{I}(k)
the set of irreducible
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup having Frobenius element equal to

. We define

\operatorname{EI}(k)=\biggl\{S\in\mathcal{P}(k)\mid S\in\Bigl\{T\cup\{k\},T\cup\left\{\tfrac{k}{2},k\right\}\Bigr\},\,T\in\mathcal{I}(k)\biggr\}.

Observe that if
S\in\operatorname{EI}(k)
, we have two possibilities depending on the parity of
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|
. If it is even, then
S
is a UESY-semigroup. If it is odd, then
S
is a PEPSY-semigroup. Now, let
T
be an irreducible
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup having Frobenius element equal to
k
. If
T
is symmetric, then

is primary positioned by Theorem
14
. If
T
is pseudo-symmetric, observe that
T\cup\{k\}
is not
k
-positioned since
k=\frac{k}{2}+\frac{k}{2}
. Consider
S=T\cup\{\frac{k}{2},k\}
.
From Theorem
17
we know that if
\frac{k}{2}\in\operatorname{C}(S)
, then
S
is primary positioned. Conversely, if

, then

. From this discussion, we obtain the following:

Remark 24
.

The structure of
\operatorname{EI}(k)
depends on the parity of

•

If

is even, then
\operatorname{EI}(k)=\{T\cup\{k\}\mid T\in\mathcal{I}(k)\}.

•

If

\operatorname{EI}(k)=\Big\{T\cup\{\tfrac{k}{2},k\}\mid T\in\mathcal{I}(k)\text{ and }\tfrac{k}{2}\in\operatorname{C}\left(T\cup\{\tfrac{k}{2},k\}\right)\Big\}.

The next result establishes a relationship between the sets
\operatorname{EI}(k)
and
\operatorname{B}(T)
for any

.

Proposition 25
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{P}(k)
. Then,
\operatorname{B}(T)=\emptyset
if and only if
T\in\operatorname{EI}(k)
.

Proof.

Suppose
T\in\operatorname{EI}(k)
. Therefore,
T
is a UESY-semigroup or a PEPSY-semigroup such that
k
is the Frobenius element of
T\setminus\{k\}
or
T\setminus\{\frac{k}{2},k\}
.
If there exists
x\in\operatorname{B}(T)
, then
k=x+t
with
t\in T\setminus\{0,\frac{k}{2},k\}
. In particular, if
T
is a UESY-semigroup, we obtain that
k
is not a minimal generator and if
T
is a PEPSY-semigroup, we obtain that
k
, together with
k=\frac{k}{2}+\frac{k}{2}
, has more than one expression. These facts contradict Propositions
13
and
16
. So,
\operatorname{B}(T)=\emptyset
.
Conversely, let
T\in\mathcal{P}(k)
. Suppose that
T
is neither a UESY-semigroup nor a PEPSY-semigroup. By Propositions
13
and
16
, we know that
k
is not a minimal generator and
k
has more than one expression in
T
.
Since
k\notin\operatorname{msg}(S)
,
k=t_{1}+t_{2}
with
t_{1},t_{2}\in T\setminus\{0\}
. In case where
\frac{k}{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we have
k=\frac{k}{2}+\frac{k}{2}
is an expression of
k
in
T
, since by the property of be
k
-positioned, we know that
\frac{k}{2}\in T
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we assume
t_{1},t_{2}\notin\{0,\frac{k}{2},k\}
and
t_{1}\in\operatorname{msg}(T)
. It follows that
t_{1}\in\operatorname{C}(T)
, otherwise, by the bijection of the map
\phi_{T}
defined in Proposition
9
, we obtain that
k-t_{1}=t_{2}\in\mathcal{H}(T)
, a contradiction. By hypothesis
\operatorname{B}(T)=\emptyset
, so necessarily

, and by applying Lemma
10
, we have that
k-t_{1}=t_{2}\in\mathcal{H}(T)
, which is false. So, we conclude that

is a UESY-semigroup or a PEPSY-semigorup.
∎

Given a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
, the definition of
\operatorname{SG}(S)
directly implies that its elements are precisely the gaps of
S
such that
S\cup\{x\}
remains a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. Accordingly, the following lemma shows that adding a special gap
x
of
S
to it does not affect the invariants
\operatorname{C}(S\cup\{x\})
,
\operatorname{Minimals}{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl((S\cup\{x\})\setminus\{0\}\bigr)
, and
\operatorname{M}(S\cup\{x\})
under specific conditions.

Lemma 26
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup and let
x\in\operatorname{SG}(S)
such that
x\notin\operatorname{M}(S)\cup\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))
. Then the following holds:

1.

\operatorname{C}(S\cup\{x\})=\operatorname{C}(S)
.

2.

\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl((S\cup\{x\})\setminus\{0\}\bigr)=\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(S\setminus\{0\}\bigr)
.

3.

\operatorname{M}(S\cup\{x\})=\operatorname{M}(S)
.

Proof.

Let us denote
T=S\cup\{x\}
. In particular,
\mathcal{H}(S)=\mathcal{H}(T)\cup\{x\}
.

We start by proving the first claim. If
y\in\operatorname{C}(T)
, then there exists
h\in\mathcal{H}(T)\subset\mathcal{H}(S)
such that
y\leq_{\mathcal{C}}h
. That means
y\in\operatorname{C}(S)
. So,
\operatorname{C}(T)\subseteq\operatorname{C}(S)
. Conversely, if
y\in\operatorname{C}(S)
, then there exists
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)=\mathcal{H}(T)\cup\{x\}
such that
y\leq_{\mathcal{C}}h
. If
h\in\mathcal{H}(T)
, then
y\in\operatorname{C}(T)
. Assuming
h=x
, by hypothesis there exists
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}(S)\setminus\{x\}
such that
x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\prime}
. In particular,
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}(T)
and we have
y\leq_{\mathcal{C}}x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\prime}
, which implies
y\in\operatorname{C}(T)
. So,
\operatorname{C}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{C}(T)
and the first claim holds.

Now we prove the second claim. Suppose
y\in\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(T\setminus\{0\})
. Hence, for all
z\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
such that
z<_{\mathcal{C}}y
, we have
z\in\mathcal{H}(T)\subset\mathcal{H}(S)
, that implies
\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(T\setminus\{0\})
. Conversely, let
y\in\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(S\setminus\{0\})
and assume
y\notin\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(T\setminus\{0\})
. This means that
x\leq_{\mathcal{C}}y
. Now, let
z\in\operatorname{I}_{S}(x)
. Since
z<_{\mathcal{C}}y
, by the minimality of
y
in
S\setminus\{0\}
we have
z=0
. This implies that
\operatorname{I}_{S}(x)=0
, which means
x\in\operatorname{M}(S)
, a contradiction. So,
y\in\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(T\setminus\{0\})
.

Finally, since
\operatorname{M}(S)=\{y\in\mathcal{C}\mid s\nleq_{\mathcal{C}}y\text{ for all }s\in\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(S\setminus\{0\})\}
, and applying the second claim, we conclude that
\operatorname{M}(T)=\operatorname{M}(S)
.
∎

The condition established in Lemma
26
can be reformulated as follows.

Lemma 27
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup and let
x\in\operatorname{SG}(S)
. Then,

\displaystyle x\notin\operatorname{M}(S)

\displaystyle\cup\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x

\displaystyle\in\operatorname{C}(S\cup\{x\})\setminus\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl((S\cup\{x\})\setminus\{0\}\bigr).

Proof.

Let
x\in\operatorname{SG}(S)
. First, observe that
x\in\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl((S\cup\{x\})\setminus\{0\}\bigr)
if and only if for every
y\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
such that

we have that
y\in\mathcal{H}(S)
, and this is the definition of
x\in\operatorname{M}(S)
. Therefore,
x\notin\operatorname{M}(S)
if and only if
x\notin\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl((S\cup\{x\})\setminus\{0\}\bigr)
. Now, suppose that
x\notin\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))
. Then
x\in\operatorname{C}(S\cup\{x\})
, since
x\in\mathcal{H}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{C}(S)
. Conversely, suppose that
x\in\operatorname{C}(S\cup\{x\})
, that is, there exists
h\in\mathcal{H}(S\cup\{x\})=\mathcal{H}(S)\setminus\{x\}
such that

. So,
x\notin\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))
.
∎

Let
S\in\mathcal{P}(k)\setminus\operatorname{EI}(k)
. In this case we know that
\operatorname{B}(S)\neq\emptyset
and we define
\beta(S)=\max_{\preceq}\operatorname{B}(S)
. From Lemmas
26
and

, we present the following result.

Proposition 28
.

If
S\in\mathcal{P}(k)\setminus\operatorname{EI}(k)
, then
S\setminus\{\beta(S)\}\in\mathcal{P}(k)
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{P}(k)\setminus\operatorname{EI}(k)
, and set
T=S\setminus\{\beta(S)\}
. Clearly,
T
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup since
\beta(S)
is a minimal generator of
S
. By applying Lemma
27
, we deduce that
\beta(S)\notin\operatorname{M}(T)\cup\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(T))
, since
\beta(S)\in\operatorname{SG}(T)
. Thus, the hypotheses of Lemma
26
are satisfied and since

is primary positioned we conclude that
|\operatorname{I}_{\mathcal{C}}(k)|=|\operatorname{M}(S)|+|\operatorname{C}(S)|=|\operatorname{M}(T)|+|\operatorname{C}(T)|
. Hence, in order to conclude, it is sufficient to show that
T
is
k
-positioned. In fact, if
h\in\mathcal{H}(T)
, then
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)\cup\{\beta(S)\}
. In particular, in the case

, by definition we have

. While, since
S
is
k
-positioned, if
h\in\mathcal{H}(S)
, then
k-h\in S=T\cup\{\beta(S)\}
. In this case, it is not possible

, otherwise

. So,

is
k
-positioned.

With the theoretical foundation established, we introduce the transform
\Psi_{k}:\mathcal{P}(k)\setminus\operatorname{EI}(k)\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}(k)
defined by
\Psi_{k}(S)=S\setminus\{\beta(S)\}
. Since the set
\operatorname{B}(S)
is finite for every
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S\in\mathcal{P}(k)\setminus\operatorname{EI}(k)
and by Proposition
25
, we deduce that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on
S
, such that
\Psi_{k}^{n}(S)\in\operatorname{EI}(k)
. So, given any
T\in\operatorname{EI}(k)
we can consider the set

\mathcal{P}_{T}(k)=\big\{S\in\mathcal{P}(k)\mid\exists\,n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ such that }\Psi_{k}^{n}(S)=T\big\}.

In this context, we define the graph
\operatorname{G}(\mathcal{P}_{T}(k))
, whose vertex set is the set
\mathcal{P}_{T}(k)
, and the pair
(S_{1},S_{2})\in\mathcal{P}_{T}(k)\times\mathcal{P}_{T}(k)
is an edge is and only if
\Psi_{k}(S_{1})=S_{2}
. If
(S_{1},S_{2})
is an edge, we say that
S_{1}
is a child of

.

Proposition 29
.

For every
T\in\operatorname{EI}(k)
, the graph
\operatorname{G}(\mathcal{P}_{T}(k))
is a tree, where
T
is the root.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{P}_{T}(k)
with
S\neq T
. So, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Psi_{k}^{n}(S)=T
. In particular, there exists a sequence of edges
(S_{1},S_{2})
,
(S_{2},S_{3}),\ldots,(S_{n-1},S_{n})
such that
S_{1}=S
,
S_{n}=T
, and
S_{i+1}=\psi(S_{i})
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. This sequence is unique since the elements
\beta(S_{i})
, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, are uniquely defined. In particular, we have a unique path of edges linking
S
with
T
. Therefore, the graph is a tree with root
T
.
∎

Observe that, given any
S\in\mathcal{P}(k)
the set of children of
S
equals to

\left\{S\cup\{x\}\mid x\in\operatorname{SG}(S)\text{ and }x=\beta(S\cup\{x\})\right\},

and as a consequence of Lemma

we obtain that if

, then
x\in\operatorname{SG}(S)
and
x\notin\operatorname{M}(S)\cup\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))
. Now, our purpose is focused on determining the children of any primary positioned
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
without directly computing
S\cup\{x\}
and
\beta(S\cup\{x\})
for each
x\in\operatorname{SG}(S)
and
x\notin\operatorname{M}(S)\cup\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))
. To reach our aim we distinguish two cases: whether
S\in\operatorname{EI}(k)
, i.e., when we are interested in computing the children of the root, or when we are concerned with the children of any other vertex. This will be the content of the next two subsections.

5.1
The children of a root

Let

5.2
The children of a non-root Vertex

Now our aim is to provide a result analogous to Theorem

\mathcal{C}
-semigroups

Lemma 33
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{P}(k)\setminus\operatorname{EI}(k)
. Suppose
x\in\operatorname{SG}(S)
such that

x\notin\operatorname{M}(S)\cup\operatorname{Maximals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{H}(S))
and
x\succ\beta(S)
. Then
S\cup\{x\}
is a child of
S
.

Proof.

To obtain our result, it is sufficient to prove that
x=\beta(S\cup\{x\})
. Since
S
is
k
-positioned and
x
is a gap of
S
, we know that
x\neq\frac{k}{2}
and
k-x\in S\subseteq S\cup\{x\}
. Trivially,
x\in\operatorname{msg}(S\cup\{x\})
and by Lemma
27
, we have
x\in\operatorname{C}(S\cup\{x\})\setminus\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl((S\cup\{x\})\setminus\{0\}\bigr)
. So,
x\in\operatorname{B}(S\cup\{x\})
. Suppose that
x\neq\beta(S\cup\{x\})
, it follows that
x\prec\beta(S\cup\{x\})
. By definition and by our assumption we have
\beta(S\cup\{x\})\in\operatorname{msg}(S\cup\{x\})\subseteq\operatorname{msg}(S)\setminus\{x\}
and, considering also Lemma
26
, we have
\beta(S\cup\{x\})\in\operatorname{C}(S\cup\{x\})\setminus\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl((S\cup\{x\})\setminus\{0\}\bigr)=\operatorname{C}(S)\setminus\operatorname{Minimals}_{\leq_{\mathcal{C}}}(S\setminus\{0\})
.

Moreover,
k-\beta(S\cup\{x\})\in S\cup\{x\}
. In particular, we have two possibilities,
k-\beta(S\cup\{x\})=x
or
k-\beta(S\cup\{x\})\in S
. In the first case, observe that if
T\in\operatorname{EI}(k)
is the semigroup such that
S\in\mathcal{P}_{T}(k)
, that is,
T
is the root of the graph having
S
as a vertex, then
x,\beta(S\cup\{x\})\in\mathcal{H}(T)
and since
T
is
k
-positioned then
k-\beta(S\cup\{x\})\in T
. This means the equality
k-\beta(S\cup\{x\})=x
is not possible. Therefore,
k-\beta(S\cup\{x\})\in S
and this implies
\beta(S\cup\{x\})\in\operatorname{B}(S)
. As a consequence,
\beta(S\cup\{x\})\preceq\beta(S)\prec x
, a contradiction. So,
x=\beta(S\cup\{x\})
and we conclude.
∎

Nevertheless, if

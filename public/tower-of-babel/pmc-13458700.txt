Influence of patients’ health beliefs on rehabilitation exercise adherence after knee replacement: the mediating role of exercise psychological needs satisfaction
Authors: Qingchao Ru, Jianing Lu, Yijun Lian, Yongkang Zhu, Junhong Yan, Xin Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To analyze the influence of patients’ health beliefs on adherence to rehabilitation exercise after knee replacement and to verify the mediating role of exercise psychological needs satisfaction. From January 2023 to December 2025, patients who underwent knee replacement at the First Affiliated Hospital of Henan Medical University were selected by convenience sampling as the research subjects. According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria and patients’ voluntary participation in screening, 316 cases were finally included. This study was a cross-sectional study. Patients’ adherence to rehabilitation exercise was evaluated using the Exercise Adherence Questionnaire (EAQ). The Chinese version of the Champion Health Belief Model Scale (SF-HBMS) was used to evaluate the health beliefs of the participants. The Psychological Need Satisfaction in Exercise Scale (PNSES) was used to evaluate the satisfaction of exercise psychological needs. The current status of patients’ health beliefs, rehabilitation exercise adherence, and exercise psychological needs was summarized. Pearson correlation analysis and mediation analysis were conducted to examine the relationships among the three variables. The total EAQ score of the 316 patients was (32.02 ± 5.22), and the adherence index was (57.17 ± 13.57)%. Among them, 68 cases were in a low adherence state, accounting for 21.52%; 227 cases were in a moderate adherence state, accounting for 71.84%; and 21 cases were in a high adherence state, accounting for 6.65%. The total SF-HBMS score was (59.85 ± 9.88), and the total PNSES score was (83.47 ± 13.08). Pearson correlation analysis revealed positive correlations between EAQ and SF-HBMS (r = 0.665), EAQ and PNSES (r = 0.581), as well as SF-HBMS and PNSES (r = 0.525) (all P < 0.001). Mediation effect analysis involved three models, namely: rehabilitation exercise adherence = 10.689 + 0.351 × total health belief score; exercise psychological needs satisfaction = 41.887 + 0.695 × health beliefs; rehabilitation exercise adherence = 5.347 + 0.263 × health beliefs + 0.128 × exercise psychological needs satisfaction. The indirect effect of exercise psychological needs satisfaction on the relationship between rehabilitation exercise adherence and health beliefs was significant [b = 0.089, P < 0.001, 95% CI (0.064, 0.115)]. Exercise psychological needs satisfaction played a partial mediating role between rehabilitation exercise adherence and health beliefs, with an effect value of 0.089, accounting for 25.23%. Health beliefs had a significant positive predictive effect on exercise psychological needs satisfaction [a = 0.695, P < 0.001, 95% CI (0.570, 0.820)], and exercise psychological needs satisfaction had a significant positive predictive effect on rehabilitation exercise adherence [a = 0.128, P < 0.001, 95% CI (0.091, 0.164)]. At the same time, the direct effect of health beliefs on rehabilitation exercise adherence was significant [b = 0.263, P < 0.001, 95% CI (0.215, 0.311)], and the total effect was also significant [c′ = 0.351, P < 0.001, 95% CI (0.307, 0.395)]. Rehabilitation exercise adherence among patients following knee arthroplasty was moderate. Health beliefs were positively correlated with both exercise adherence and exercise psychological needs satisfaction. Mediation effect analysis indicated that exercise psychological needs satisfaction partially mediated the relationship between health beliefs and rehabilitation exercise adherence. The direct correlation between health beliefs and rehabilitation exercise adherence was also statistically significant.

Introduction

Knee arthroplasty (KA) is a crucial procedure for treating severe knee diseases. By replacing the damaged joint with an artificial prosthesis, KA effectively restores joint function, relieves pain, corrects the lower limb mechanical axis, and improves patients’ quality of life1. Although KA replaces the diseased joint and enhances joint function, postoperative functional exercise remains an indispensable and critical component2. Effective rehabilitation exercise is a key factor in reducing postoperative complications, promoting joint reconstruction, and improving patients’ motor function and activities of daily living. However, studies indicate that adherence to rehabilitation exercise among KA patients is relatively low, at only approximately 60%, with full adherence reported in merely 10% to 20% of cases3,4. Poor adherence can easily lead to joint stiffness, reduced muscle mass, and an increased risk of deep vein thrombosis, severely affecting patient prognosis and recovery, consequently prolonging the treatment cycle and increasing the financial burden on families. Therefore, it is of significant clinical value to conduct an in-depth exploration of the factors influencing rehabilitation exercise adherence in patients following KA.

Health beliefs are defined as an individual’s subjective cognition and attitudes toward their health status, disease severity, treatment efficacy, and the perceived benefits of and barriers to adopting health-related behaviors. Health beliefs can influence adherence to healthy behaviors among various patient groups. For instance, research by LA S et al. indicated that the health belief model can affect self-identity, thereby enhancing adherence to iron supplementation among high school girls5. Studies by M G et al. suggested that the health belief model may aid in developing interventions for behavior change in phosphate control6. However, empirical research on the role of health beliefs in influencing adherence to rehabilitation exercise among patients after KA surgery remains scarce, and no studies have explored the role of exercise psychological needs satisfaction within this context. Psychological needs satisfaction refers to the extent to which an individual’s basic needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness are fulfilled. In the context of exercise, this is termed exercise psychological needs satisfaction7,8. Health beliefs serve as a critical antecedent to the fulfillment of these needs9. First, positive health beliefs encourage patients to autonomously engage in rehabilitation exercise. By reframing rehabilitation as a challenge rather than a burden, patients are more likely to internalize the value of the activity, thereby facilitating satisfaction of their need for autonomy10. Second, satisfaction of the need for competence is progressively enhanced as patients gain an understanding of rehabilitation principles and witness tangible improvements in their joint function, daily activities, and quality of life. Third, the formation of health beliefs often depends on education and guidance from healthcare providers. When medical staff not only convey relevant information but also offer emotional support and encouragement, patients feel genuinely cared for and understood, which directly addresses the need for relatedness11. Furthermore, rehabilitation exercise following KA is a prolonged process, and adherence driven solely by health beliefs may be insufficient to sustain long-term engagement. When the basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness are adequately met, rehabilitation exercise can be transformed into a persistently internally motivated behavior12. Therefore, this study hypothesized that exercise psychological needs satisfaction may serve as a mediating variable in the relationship between health beliefs and rehabilitation exercise adherence.

This study aimed to investigate the psychological mechanism through which health beliefs influence rehabilitation exercise adherence, specifically focusing on the mediating role of exercise psychological needs satisfaction, thereby providing empirical evidence to enhance exercise adherence and improve the quality of life of patients following KA surgery.

Methods

Subjects

From January 2023 to December 2025, patients undergoing knee arthroplasty at the First Affiliated Hospital of Henan Medical University were selected as the research subjects by convenience sampling. Patients were included if they were aged ≥ 18 years, diagnosed with osteoarthritis according to the 2021 guidelines13, met the surgical indications for KA and underwent surgery by the same physician team, and were able to communicate and complete the questionnaire independently or with researcher assistance. Patients were excluded if they underwent simultaneous hip and knee arthroplasty, had severe organic diseases, malignant tumors, serious knee joint injuries causing movement disorders, postoperative complications such as wound infection, or severe mental or cognitive impairment preventing questionnaire completion.

The sample size estimation was conducted based on the principles applicable to correlational research involving two or more variables, specifically setting the sample size at 10 times the number of independent variables. This study involved a total of 25 questionnaire items. Considering a 20% invalid response rate for the questionnaires, the initial sample size was determined to be 329 cases. Subsequently, after applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria and considering patients’ voluntary participation in the screening process, the final sample size included 316 cases. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Henan Medical University and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. General information questionnaire

A self-designed questionnaire was used, mainly including age, BMI, gender, education level, marital status, working status, place of residence, living arrangements, chronic diseases, family per capita monthly income, medical payment method, disease course, and history of knee arthroplasty. Data for all patients were obtained through a combination of hospital record review and patient self-reporting.

Questionnaire about exercise adherence

The Questionnaire of Exercise Adherence (EAQ) was used to evaluate exercise adherence among patients in the Department of Orthopedics14. The questionnaire consists of 14 items and 3 dimensions, including physical exercise adherence, exercise monitoring adherence, and active advice-seeking adherence. The items were scored using a 4-point Likert scale, with a total score ranging from 14 to 56. The adherence index = (adherence score/56) × 100%, which was categorized as high (≥ 75%), medium (50%–75%), and low (25%–50%). In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient of the scale was 0.930. At the follow-up visit four weeks after discharge, the researcher conducted a face-to-face survey using standardized instructions. Patients completed the questionnaire independently, which took approximately 5 min.

Short form health beliefs model scale (SF-HBMS)

The SF-HBMS was used to evaluate the health beliefs of the participants15. The scale consists of 6 dimensions and 36 items, namely perceived disease severity (7 items), perceived disease susceptibility (5 items), perceived benefits of health behaviors (6 items), perceived barriers to health behaviors (6 items), health motivation (7 items), and self-efficacy (5 items). A 5-point Likert scoring method was used, with scores assigned as follows: 1 point (completely disagree), 2 points (disagree), 3 points (neutral), 4 points (agree), and 5 points (completely agree), among which the “perceived barriers to health behaviors” dimension was reverse scored. The total score of the scale ranges from 36 to 180, with a higher score indicating a higher level of health beliefs. In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient of the scale was 0.890. The scale was distributed by researchers who had undergone unified training during the outpatient follow-up visit at the 4th week after discharge. Standardized instructions were used to explain the requirements for completing the scale. The questionnaire was completed independently by the patient and collected on the spot. The completion time was approximately 5 min.

Psychological need satisfaction in exercise scale (PNSES)

The PNSES was used to evaluate exercise psychological needs satisfaction16. The scale includes three dimensions: competence needs, relatedness needs, and autonomy needs, with a total of 21 items. Each item was scored using a 6-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“completely disagree”) to 6 (“completely agree”), with a total score ranging from 21 to 147. Higher scores indicate higher levels of psychological needs satisfaction. In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient of the scale was 0.876. The scale was distributed by researchers who had undergone unified training during the outpatient follow-up visit at the 4th week after discharge. Standardized instructions were used to explain the requirements for completing the scale. The questionnaire was completed independently by the patients and collected on the spot. The completion time was approximately 5 min.

Quality control

All questionnaires were distributed and collected by the researchers. The purpose of the study was explained to the patients before questionnaire completion, and all participants completed the questionnaires voluntarily after providing consent. Standardized instructions were used during the completion process, and all questionnaires were collected on the spot. Questionnaire data were independently entered into the computer by two researchers and organized into Excel files.

Data processing

SPSS 25.0 was used to test common method bias and conduct descriptive statistical and correlation analyses. Count data were expressed as frequency and percentage (%). Measurement data were tested using the Shapiro–Wilk normality test, and data conforming to a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s). The total health beliefs score was used as the independent variable (X), the total psychological needs satisfaction score as the mediator variable (M), and the total rehabilitation exercise adherence score as the dependent variable (Y). Variables significantly associated with adherence in the general information survey were simultaneously included in the model as covariates for adjustment. The bias-corrected percentile Bootstrap method with 5,000 repeated samplings was used to calculate the 95% confidence intervals for each path coefficient. If the 95% confidence interval of the indirect effect (X → M → Y) did not include 0, the mediating effect was considered significant. The point estimates, standard errors, and confidence intervals of the total effect, direct effect (c’), and indirect effect (a × b) were reported simultaneously.

Discussion

Status of rehabilitation exercise adherence, health beliefs, and exercise psychological needs after KA

Rehabilitation exercise adherence refers to the extent to which patients follow the rehabilitation exercise plan formulated by physicians and remain consistent with medical advice 17–19. The total EAQ score of the 316 patients was (32.02 ± 5.22), and the adherence index was (57.17 ± 13.57) %, which was generally consistent with the findings of previous studies on exercise adherence 20. This finding may be attributed to two main factors. Physiologically, patients often experience severe postoperative pain, reduced physical function, generalized weakness, fatigue, and limited joint mobility, all of which impair their ability to initiate and maintain active rehabilitation21,22. Cognitively, some patients have low expectations regarding the efficacy of surgery and their potential for physical recovery23. This skepticism may lead them to underestimate the importance of rehabilitation exercise, thereby reducing their motivation and adherence24.

Health beliefs refer to individuals’ perceptions and attitudes toward health behaviors, which influence their health-related behavioral decisions25. In this study, the total SF-HBMS score of the 316 patients was (59.85 ± 9.88), which was lower than the score reported in the study by Li CL et al. [(71.24 ± 12.39)]26. Among the dimensions, health motivation had the highest score, indicating that patients had a relatively good understanding of the importance of postoperative rehabilitation exercise. In contrast, perceived disease susceptibility had the lowest score, suggesting that patients perceived multiple barriers to a healthy lifestyle and had insufficient awareness of disease susceptibility. The results also showed that the total PNSES score of the 316 patients after KA was (83.47 ± 13.08), which was lower than that reported in previous studies, indicating that the psychological needs satisfaction of patients after KA remains relatively low and warrants further clinical attention27.

Correlation analysis of rehabilitation exercise adherence, health beliefs, and exercise psychological needs among patients after KA

The Health Belief Model is a theoretical framework used to predict health behaviors and explain the potential reasons for non-adherence to recommended health behaviors28. According to this model, whether individuals adopt certain health behaviors largely depends on their health beliefs, including their understanding of disease threats, evaluation of behavioral barriers and benefits, and confidence in their ability to perform the behavior29. Higher levels of health beliefs are associated with a greater likelihood of adopting health-promoting behaviors. Among patients undergoing KA, those with stronger health beliefs tend to have a better understanding of the importance, benefits, and methods of rehabilitation exercise. They also demonstrate stronger abilities in implementing and maintaining rehabilitation exercises, which enhances their confidence in completing rehabilitation programs. In contrast, patients with weaker health beliefs often perceive greater barriers to adopting healthy behaviors and have insufficient awareness of disease susceptibility, thereby affecting their exercise adherence30.

This study demonstrated a positive correlation between EAQ and SF-HBMS scores after KA (r = 0.665, P < 0.001), indicating that higher health beliefs were associated with better rehabilitation exercise adherence. These findings are consistent with previous studies. Mediation analysis further showed that the direct effect of health beliefs on rehabilitation exercise adherence was significant (b = 0.263, P < 0.001), and the total effect was also significant (c′ = 0.351, P < 0.001). These findings have important clinical implications. Healthcare providers should strengthen patients’ understanding of postoperative rehabilitation exercise through targeted interventions, such as sharing successful cases and presenting relevant evidence, to improve patients’ perceptions of disease susceptibility and the benefits of rehabilitation adherence. In addition, rehabilitation plans should take patients’ practical difficulties into consideration31. By identifying and helping patients overcome perceived barriers, healthcare providers may enhance patients’ confidence in their rehabilitation ability and ultimately improve exercise adherence.

According to self-determination theory, satisfaction of competence, relatedness, and autonomy needs are essential for stimulating intrinsic motivation. Postoperative rehabilitation exercise is often prolonged and accompanied by pain32. When patients experience autonomy, achievement from rehabilitation progress, and support from family members or healthcare providers during exercise, their intrinsic motivation for participation is effectively enhanced, resulting in higher adherence to rehabilitation exercise33,34. Conversely, when psychological needs are not satisfied, patients may develop resistance, leading to reduced adherence35. This study found a significant positive correlation between rehabilitation exercise adherence and exercise psychological needs satisfaction among post-KA patients (r = 0.581, P < 0.001). This finding suggests that rehabilitation plans for patients after KA should support patients’ autonomy through shared decision-making, enhance competence through phased goal setting and positive feedback, and strengthen relatedness through emotional support and peer education, thereby fundamentally improving rehabilitation exercise adherence.

When patients possess strong health beliefs, they are more likely to believe that rehabilitation exercise can effectively prevent complications and promote functional recovery. Such patients are also more likely to perceive improvements in motor function and activities of daily living during rehabilitation exercise, thereby increasing their sense of satisfaction36. This clear understanding of rehabilitation goals may further enhance their exercise adherence and psychological needs satisfaction. This study confirmed a positive correlation between health beliefs and exercise psychological needs satisfaction (r = 0.525, P < 0.001), and health beliefs had a significant positive predictive effect on exercise psychological needs satisfaction (a = 0.695, P < 0.001). These findings suggest that strengthening patients’ understanding of rehabilitation benefits and fostering positive health beliefs during the perioperative period are essential37. Positive health beliefs may improve exercise psychological needs satisfaction, promote the transition from passive adherence to active participation, and ultimately improve rehabilitation outcomes.

Analysis of the mediating role of exercise psychological needs satisfaction in the relationship between health beliefs and rehabilitation exercise adherence after KA

The mediation analysis showed that the pathway “health beliefs → exercise psychological needs satisfaction → rehabilitation exercise adherence” accounted for 25.23% of the total effect. Specifically, patients with stronger health beliefs were more likely to transform external guidance into internal motivation and experience satisfaction of competence, relatedness, and autonomy needs during postoperative rehabilitation38,39. This positive psychological experience may further stimulate intrinsic motivation for continued exercise, thereby improving rehabilitation exercise adherence40. These findings have important implications for clinical rehabilitation guidance. In clinical practice, postoperative rehabilitation interventions for KA patients should not rely solely on cognitive education but should integrate both cognitive and motivational approaches. On the one hand, healthcare providers should strengthen patients’ health beliefs to ensure clear rehabilitation goals. On the other hand, attention should be paid to patients’ psychological experiences during rehabilitation, and their basic psychological needs should be met through shared decision-making, goal setting, and emotional support, thereby maximizing rehabilitation initiative.

Limitations

This study verified the pathway of “health beliefs → exercise psychological needs satisfaction → rehabilitation exercise adherence” and provided new theoretical evidence for improving rehabilitation adherence and prognosis among patients after KA. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, convenience sampling rather than random sampling was used, which may introduce selection bias and limit the representativeness of the sample. Second, due to the limited representativeness of the sample, the generalizability of the findings to broader populations may be restricted. Third, geographical differences were not adequately considered because of time and survey location constraints. Finally, the use of self-administered scales may have introduced reporting bias. These limitations should be addressed in future studies.

Conclusion

Rehabilitation exercise adherence among patients after KA was at a moderate level. Patients’ health beliefs were positively correlated with rehabilitation exercise adherence and exercise psychological needs satisfaction. Mediation analysis indicated that exercise psychological needs satisfaction partially mediated the relationship between health beliefs and rehabilitation exercise adherence. In addition, the direct association between health beliefs and rehabilitation exercise adherence was statistically significant. Clinical attention should therefore be directed toward improving patients’ health beliefs after KA and actively addressing their exercise psychological needs in order to maximize rehabilitation exercise adherence.

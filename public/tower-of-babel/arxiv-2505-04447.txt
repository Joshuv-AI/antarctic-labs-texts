Warming up the Fibres
Dibya Chakraborty, and Rudnei O. Ramos

Abstract
Fibre inflationary models are constructed in type-IIB string flux compactification. These models have been shown to be in agreement with the cosmological observations under appropriate choices of parameters, which originate from their string theory construction. In the present work, we embed such models, originally studied in the cold inflation picture, in the context of warm inflation. We study the viability of different fibre inflation potentials in both strong and weak dissipative regime of warm inflation. In fibre inflation, the inflaton is a four-dimensional complex manifold — a fibre of a K ​ 3 K3 fibred Calabi-Yau. The potential in this case is generated by an interplay of various perturbative and non-perturbative corrections. The former type of corrections consists of leading-order α ′ 3 − \alpha^{\prime 3}- term, higher derivative F 4 − F^{4}- correction, and various string loop corrections of KK, log-loop and winding type. Depending on the balance between several corrections, we present four different fibre inflationary potentials and show that the warm inflationary pictures for all of them can successfully fall in the viable window from both Planck and recent Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) data. We show that warm inflation makes it possible to extend the range of parameters of applicability of these models. Our results also indicate that with the help of a large dissipation ratio, one can achieve a sub-Planckian field excursion, although, this runs on the possibility of moving away from the perturbative control of low-energy four-dimensional supergravity theory.

2
A brief review of fibre inflation

We start our discussion by laying out the basics of FI. To do so, we first demonstrate two of the main heavy KM stabilization scenarios: large volume scenario (LVS) and perturbative LVS. In addition, we also emphasize the possible quantum string corrections to stabilize the lighter KM — leading-order flat direction.

2.1
Contributions to the scalar potential

The general formula to calculate the F-term scalar potential from type IIB superstring compactifications is encoded in:

V=e^{K}\left(K^{A\overline{B}}\,D_{A}W\,D_{\overline{B}}\overline{W}-3|W|^{2}\right),\qquad D_{A}W\equiv W_{A}+K_{A}W,

(2.1)

and which governs the low-energy dynamics of the four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
effective supergravity theory. In the above equation,

K
denotes the Kähler potential and
W
is the holomorphic superpotential. Both are functions of the CS moduli
z^{i}
, the axio-dilaton
(S)
, and the KM
T_{\alpha}
. The tree-level Kähler metric is diagonal and takes the form:

K_{0}=K_{CS}-\ln\,\left[-i\,(S-\overline{S})\right]-2\,\ln\,Y,

(2.2)

where
K_{CS}
is the CS moduli dependent part, while
Y
generically depends on the volume/dilaton pieces, such that at tree level,
Y=\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{V}=\frac{1}{3!}k_{\alpha\beta\gamma}t^{\alpha}t^{\beta}t^{\gamma}
is the volume of the underlying CY manifold.
t^{\alpha}
’s are the two-cycle volumes and
k_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is the triple intersection number of the CY-3-fold (CY3). On the other hand, the superpotential is only a function of CS and axio-dilaton and depends on the KM modulo the addition of non-perturbative (NP) effects, to be discussed soon. The holomorphic superpotential is:

W=W_{\text{flux}}+W_{np}(S,T_{\alpha}),

(2.3)

where

W_{0}=\left\langle W_{\text{flux}}\right\rangle,

and
W_{\text{flux}}
is the superpotential induced by the 3-form fluxes
(F_{3},H_{3})

Gukov:1999ya
. Due to the diagonal decoupled structure of

K\equiv K_{CS}+K(S,T_{\alpha})
, the inverse metric takes a block-diagonal form, producing a scalar potential:

\displaystyle V\equiv V_{CS}+V_{K},\,\qquad V_{CS}=e^{K}K^{i\overline{j}}_{CS}\,D_{i}W\,D_{\overline{j}}\overline{W},

\displaystyle V_{K}=e^{K}\left(K^{A\overline{B}}\,D_{A}W\,D_{\overline{B}}\overline{W}-3|W|^{2}\right),\,\,\,A,B\in\left\{S,T_{\alpha}\right\}.

Moduli stabilization in low-energy type IIB flux-compactification is a two-step procedure. In the first step, CS and axio-dilaton are stabilized by the supersymmetric flatness condition
D_{U^{i}}W_{\text{flux}}=0=D_{S}W_{\text{flux}}
. After the first step, one obtains
W_{0}
, leaving the KM as a flat direction. Performing the summation in

in

becomes zero at tree level — this is the so-called

of the scalar potential. As a second step, to lift the flat direction along the KM, one has to incorporate sub-leading corrections such as either perturbative corrections in inverse-string-tension
\alpha^{\prime}\,(=2\pi l_{s}^{2})
and string loop
(g_{s})
or non-perturbative corrections.

Four types of fibre inflation

More explicitly, the four types of FI that we implement in the context of WI are the following.

3.1
Type-I fibre

\displaystyle V_{0}=\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{g_{s}}{8\pi}\right)\frac{W_{0}^{2}}{\langle\mathcal{V}\rangle^{3}}\frac{2C_{1}}{3\langle\tau_{1}\rangle},\qquad R=\frac{\Pi_{2}}{\Pi_{1}}\frac{\langle\tau_{1}\rangle^{3/2}}{\langle\mathcal{V}\rangle},\qquad C_{1}=\left(\frac{g_{s}}{8\pi}\right)\frac{|\lambda|W_{0}^{2}\Pi_{1}}{g_{s}^{3/2}},

\displaystyle C_{2}=\left(\frac{g_{s}}{8\pi}\right)\frac{|\lambda|W_{0}^{2}\Pi_{2}}{g_{s}^{3/2}},\qquad\langle\tau_{1}\rangle=\frac{4C_{1}^{2}}{B^{2}},\qquad B=4\alpha c^{w},

(3.6)

and
\delta

Type-III fibre

Type III fibre inflation

(3.7)

Upon canonical normalization,

can be re-cast as:

\displaystyle V_{III}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\langle V_{LVS}\rangle+\langle V_{up}\rangle+V_{sub}(\varphi),

\displaystyle=V_{0}\left(E+\delta+e^{-4\varphi/\sqrt{3}}-4e^{-\varphi/\sqrt{3}}+R_{1}e^{2\varphi/\sqrt{3}}+R_{2}e^{\varphi/\sqrt{3}}\right),

(3.8)

where

\displaystyle V_{0}=\frac{A|W_{0}|^{2}}{\langle\mathcal{V}\rangle^{2}\langle\tau_{1}\rangle^{2}},\qquad\langle\tau_{1}\rangle=\left(\frac{4}{C_{1}}\right)^{2/3}\mathcal{V}^{2/3},\qquad C_{1}=\left(\frac{2}{C_{1}^{KK}}\right)^{2}\frac{C_{w}}{g_{s}^{2}},

\displaystyle A=\frac{g_{s}}{32\pi}g_{s}^{2}(C_{1}^{KK})^{2},\qquad R_{1}=\left(\frac{C_{1}^{KK}C_{2}^{KK}}{C_{w}}\right)^{2}\frac{g_{s}^{4}}{18},\qquad R_{2}=\frac{18W_{0}^{2}}{\pi}\frac{(C_{1}^{KK})^{4/3}}{C_{w}^{5/3}}\frac{|\lambda|g_{s}^{5/6}}{\langle\mathcal{V}\rangle^{1/3}},

\displaystyle\langle V_{LVS}\rangle+\langle V_{up}\rangle=V_{0}E=V_{0}(3-R_{1}-R_{2}).

(3.9)

3.4
Type-IV fibre

In contrast to the first three previous instances, the type IV fibre uses the pLVS mechanism to stabilize the heavy KMs as shown in subsection
2.3
. In addition, the volume form of eq. (
2.14
) takes a different form:
\mathcal{V}=2t^{1}t^{2}t^{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\tau_{3}}

correction, log-loop correction, winding-type string loop correction and higher derivative

V_{IV}(\varphi)=V_{0}\left(C_{up}+\delta+R_{0}e^{-2\varphi/\sqrt{3}}-e^{-\varphi/\sqrt{3}}+R_{1}e^{\varphi/\sqrt{3}}+R_{2}e^{2\varphi/\sqrt{3}}\right),

(3.10)

where

\displaystyle V_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{2}C_{2}C_{w}}{\langle\mathcal{V}\rangle^{3}e^{\langle\varphi\rangle/\sqrt{3}}},\qquad R_{0}=\frac{C_{3}e^{-\langle\varphi\rangle/\sqrt{3}}}{\sqrt{2}C_{2}C_{w}},\qquad\frac{R_{1}}{R_{0}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}e^{\sqrt{3}\langle\varphi\rangle}}{\langle\mathcal{V}\rangle},

\displaystyle\frac{R_{2}}{R_{0}}\sim\frac{C_{2}C_{w}e^{4\langle\varphi\rangle/\sqrt{3}}}{C_{3}\langle\mathcal{V}\rangle},\qquad C_{up}=1-R_{0}-R_{1}-R_{2}.

Following the usual lore, setting the vacuum expectation value of

\delta

4
WI setup

Let us now present the WI implementation for the four FI types of potentials discussed in the previous section.

Slow-roll dynamics of Warm Inflation

For WI, at the background level, one is interested in the cosmological evolution of both the scalar field,
\phi
, and the radiation energy density,
\rho_{r}
, of the thermal bath. The appropriate background evolution equations, in the presence of a dissipation coefficient
\Upsilon
, are:

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}+(3H+\Upsilon)\dot{\phi}+V_{,\phi}=0,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{r}+4H\rho_{r}=\Upsilon\dot{\phi}^{2},

(4.2)

where
V_{,\phi}=dV(\phi)/d\phi
,
H
is the Hubble parameter:

H^{2}=\frac{1}{3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}+V+\rho_{r}\right).

(4.3)

For a thermalized radiation bath,
\rho_{r}
is related to the temperature as

\rho_{r}=C_{R}T^{4}
with
C_{R}=\pi^{2}g_{\star}/30
and
g_{\star}
is the effective number of degrees of freedom for the radiation.
Here, we will be assuming
g_{\star}=106.75
, e.g. like for the standard model.
Just like in cold inflation (CI), accelerated inflationary regime ends when the Hubble slow-roll coefficient is
\epsilon_{H}=1
, where:

\epsilon_{H}=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}\simeq\frac{\epsilon_{V}}{1+Q},

(4.4)

with

\epsilon_{V}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{V_{,\phi}}{V}\right)^{2},

(4.5)

and
Q=\Upsilon/(3H)
is the dissipation ratio commonly considered in the WI literature.
The value of
Q
quantifies how much inflaton energy has dissipated to radiation bath during inflation when compared
to the rate of expansion.
Q\gg 1
denotes the strong dissipative regime of WI,
while
Q\ll 1
denotes the weak dissipative regime.

The primordial scalar of curvature power spectrum for a WI model can be generically expressed as
Ramos:2013nsa
,
Kamali:2023lzq
:

P_{\mathcal{R}}\simeq\left(\frac{H^{2}}{2\pi\dot{\phi}}\right)^{2}\left(1+2n_{\star}+\frac{2\sqrt{3}Q}{\sqrt{3+4\pi Q}}\frac{T}{H}\right)G(Q)\Bigr|_{k_{*}=aH},

(4.6)

where
n_{\star}
quantifies the possibility of the thermalization of the inflaton perturbations, with
n_{*}=0

denoting no-thermalization, and it else assumes the Bose-Einstein distribution,
n_{\star}\equiv n_{BE}=1/(e^{H/T}-1)
.

G(Q)
in (
4.6
), on the other hand, is dependent on the explicit form of the dissipation coefficient
\Upsilon
,
such as its functional dependence on temperature and inflaton amplitude, etc.
While
G(Q)
is generally understood to depend primarily on the form of the dissipation coefficient
Montefalcone:2023pvh
,
a recent study
Rodrigues:2025neh
demonstrated that this does not necessarily holds true when
n_{\star}=0
.
In this case,
G(Q)
exhibits a clear dependence on the potential form for a range of
Q-
values.

G(Q)
is determined by taking a ratio of the numerical power spectrum calculated by solving the coupled perturbation equations of both scalar field and the thermal fluctuations with the analytical one of (
4.6
) when written in the absence of the multiplicative factor
G(Q)
.

The general expression for the dissipation coefficient can be written as:

\Upsilon=C_{\Upsilon}f(T,\phi),

(4.7)

where
C_{\Upsilon}
is a dimensionless constant, typically depending on the microscopic parameters of the model
giving rise to the dissipation coefficient (like coupling constants and other parameters of the model), while

f(T,\phi)
is some function of the temperature and inflaton amplitude. In most studies of WI,
f(\phi,T)
assumes a
simple form
BasteroGil:2010pb
,
BasteroGil:2012cm
,
Bastero-Gil:2016qru

f(T,\phi)=T^{c}\phi^{p}M_{\rm Pl}^{1-p-c}
. However, there are also model realizations of WI where the dependence on
T
and
\phi
can be
more intricate
Bastero-Gil:2019gao
,
Berghaus:2025dqi
,
ORamos:2025uqs
.
As already mentioned in the introduction, in the analysis we carried out here, we make use of two of the most well-known
forms of dissipation coefficient in WI, i.e.,
\Upsilon\propto T
and
\Upsilon\propto T^{3}
.

Using the scalar power spectrum (
4.6
), the spectral index
n_{s}
can be calculated at the Hubble-exit point
(k_{\star})
as:

n_{s}-1=\frac{d\ln P_{\mathcal{R}}(k/k_{\star})}{d\ln(k/k_{\star})}\bigg|_{k\to k_{\star}}

(4.8)

where we can apply the following simplification,
d\ln k=(d\ln k/dN)dN
,
N=\ln a
.
The running of the spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio are expressed as:

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\,n_{s}(k/k_{\star})}{d\,\ln(k/k_{\star})}\bigg|_{k\to k_{\star}},

(4.9)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{\mathcal{T}}}{P_{\mathcal{R}}},

(4.10)

where
P_{\mathcal{T}}=2H^{2}/(\pi^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2})
is the tensor spectrum.

Note that WI does not face the uncertainty in measuring the number of
e
-folds of inflation,
N_{\star}
, between the moment the

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have taken the first step towards embedding the well-known fibre inflationary model in type-IIB string theory in the framework of warm inflation. Since the original fibre inflationary scenario was proposed in ref.
Cicoli:2008gp
, three more variations of fibre inflation models have been introduced (see section
3
for details). Chronologically, we have named them as type-I-IV FI. We have demonstrated that fibre WI can withstand both strong and weak dissipation satisfying the observational data from both Planck and ACT. We show several benchmark examples in section
5
to establish our claim as well as their clear connection to the string theory parameters.

We have shown that by using typical CI FI potential parameters, we can only achieve WI in the weakly dissipative regime (
Q\lesssim 10^{-5}
).
Attempting to reach larger values of dissipation with these fixed string model parameters results in larger values for the spectral tilt, taking us
away from the observational window for
n_{s}
.
To attempt to go to the strong dissipative regime of WI, we need to loose the constraints on the parameters of the FI potential we have studied
here. By assuming larger values for the potential parameters
{R,R_{1},R_{2}}
than the ones considered in CI, we show that we can achieve WI in the strong
regime. WI in the strong dissipative regime allows for instance
for the inflaton mass to be larger than the Hubble scale,
m_{\phi}>H
, and
the inflaton field excursion to remain below the Planck scale
\Delta\phi<M_{\rm Pl}

Berera:1999ws
,
Berera:2003yyp
.
Having
Q\gg 1
is very appealing from an effective field theory point of view since then the inflation model would be
protected from quantum gravity corrections arising in higher dimensional operators and free of uncontrollable infrared problems.
Our results confirm that residing in the strong dissipative regime can in principle diminish the long-standing problem of super-Planckian inflaton field excursion
in the realm of FI. Although this can be achieved by loosing the
constraints on the FI potential and, potentially taking us away from the perturbative control of string type-IIB flux compactification,
because of the need of larger values for the model parameters,
{R,R_{1},R_{2}}\gtrsim 1
.

Future studies could extend our analysis of FI potentials to alternative dissipation coefficients,
such as the one derived and explored in ref.
Bastero-Gil:2019gao
. Such models may achieve strong dissipation
and sub-Planckian field excursions, while preserving two key features:
a red-tilted scalar spectrum consistent with CMB constraints and possibly also allowing
FI potential parameters compatible with string theory perturbativity bounds.
Notably, the concave profile (
V_{,\phi\phi}<0
) of FI potentials near their inflection points–where inflation occurs–enhances curvature perturbations when coupled to high-power in the temperature dissipative coefficients, like the one we have studied
here (
\Upsilon\propto T^{3}
). As shown in ref.
Ito:2025lcg
, this synergy can amplify secondary
gravitational wave (GW) signals that might be detectable by next-generation interferometers (e.g., Einstein Telescope, DECIGO, etc). Similar GW signatures may arise in our studied models, contingent on the interplay between dissipation strength (
Q
) and the potential’s parameters.
We hope to quantify these effects in forthcoming work.

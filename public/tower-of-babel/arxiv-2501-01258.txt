Quantitative observability for the Schrödinger equation with an anharmonic oscillator
Shanlin Huang, Gengsheng Wang, Ming Wang

Abstract
. This paper studies the observability inequalities for the Schrödinger equation associated with an anharmonic oscillator H = − d 2 d ​ x 2 + | x | H=-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}+|x| . We build up the observability inequality over an arbitrarily short time interval ( 0 , T ) (0,T) , with an explicit expression for the observation constant C o ​ b ​ s C_{obs} in terms of T T , for some observable set that has a different geometric structure compared to those discussed in [ 14 ] . We obtain the sufficient conditions and the necessary conditions for observable sets, respectively. We also present counterexamples to demonstrate that half-lines are not observable sets, highlighting a major difference in the geometric properties of observable sets compared to those of Schrödinger operators H = − d 2 d ​ x 2 + | x | 2 ​ m H=-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}+|x|^{2m} with m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 . Our approach is based on the following ingredients: First, the use of an Ingham-type spectral inequality constructed in this paper; second, the adaptation of a quantitative unique compactness argument, inspired by the work of Bourgain-Burq-Zworski [ 5 ] ; third, the application of the Szegö’s limit theorem from the theory of Toeplitz matrices, which provides a new mathematical tool for proving counterexamples of observability inequalities.

1.3.1.
Strategy to prove Theorem
1.3
.

We outline our strategy in several steps.

Step 1.
We build the following Ingham-type spectral inequality (that is, Proposition
3.1
): There is
\varepsilon>0
,

\delta>0
, such that when
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin (with
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
) and

\displaystyle\int_{E}\Big|\sum_{k\in J^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha}(\lambda_{n})}c_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)\Big|^{2}{\rm d}x\geq\delta\cdot\sum_{k\in J^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha}(\lambda_{n})}|c_{k}|^{2}\;\;\mbox{ for all }\;\;\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in l^{2},

(1.17)

where

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n}):=\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}:|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n}|<\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon\lambda_{n}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}},&\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1),\\
\varepsilon\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log\lambda_{n})^{-1},&\alpha=1,\\
\varepsilon\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}},&\alpha>1.\end{array}\right.\right\}.

It can be viewed as a variable coefficient version (associated with the eigenelements of
H
) of the classical Ingham inequality,
which states that if
the strictly increasing sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of real numbers satisfies the ‘gap’ condition

\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\geq\gamma,\;\;\forall\;k\in\mathbb{Z}

for some fixed
\gamma>0
.
Then, for all
T>\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}
, there exists some positive constant
\delta>0
depending only on
\gamma
and
T
such that (see e.g. in
[
16
]
and
[
18
, p. 162]
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}e^{{\rm i}\lambda_{k}x}\right|^{2}{\rm d}x\geq\delta\cdot\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{k}|^{2},\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\textit{l}^{2}.

The proof of (
1.17
) combines two main ingredients: establishing uniform lower bounds for diagonal terms and obtaining upper bounds for off-diagonal terms. The lower bounds hold if and only if
E
is
weakly thick
(see Lemma
3.2
); while for the upper bounds, we require the assumption that
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin (see Lemma
3.4
).

It should be mentioned that by (
1.17
), we can deduce that
E
is
an observable set at some time
.
However, to strengthen this statement from “at some time” to “at any time”, additional steps and arguments are needed.

Step 2.
We use
(
1.17
) to obtain a relaxed observability inequality at any time (that is, Proposition
3.5
). The phrase “relaxed observability inequality” refers to a weaker observability inequality which involves an error term. This is proved in a similar way to an abstract argument of Burq-Zworski
[
7

(see also Miller
[
24
]
), and finally reduces to a uniform resolvent estimate. The resolvent estimate is obtained by applying the spectral inequality established in Step 1.

Step 3.
We use the aforementioned relaxed observability inequality to show that

is
an observable set at any time
. In this step, we borrow an idea from the quantitative compactness arguments of uniqueness due to Bourgain-Burq-Zworski
[
5

, where an observability inequality was established for Schrödinger equations with rough potentials on 2-tori. These arguments can be seen as a quantitative extension of the classical uniqueness-compactness argument proposed by Bardos–Lebeau–Rauch
[

]
. With the help of these arguments, we are able to effectively glue the high-frequency and low-frequency estimates together. Furthermore, thanks to explicit information regarding the distribution of eigenvalues, we are also able to derive an explicit expression of

The proof of the conclusion

It is worth mentioning that the method of proving Theorem

In

denote by

\mathfrak{K}_{p_{0}}^{\infty}(\omega,T)=\liminf_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}1_{\omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\phi_{0}^{t}(\rho)\right){\rm d}t=\liminf_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left\{t\in(0,T):(\pi\circ\phi_{0}^{t})(\rho)\in\omega\right\}\right|.

It is proved in
[

that when the potential

is smooth and grows subquadraticly, if there exists

then for any compact set

and any

, the following observability inequality holds:

\exists C>0:\forall u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left\|e^{-{\rm i}tP}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{R}\backslash K)}^{2}{\rm d}t,

where

\omega_{R}=\bigcup_{x\in\omega}B_{R}(x)

2.1.
Spectral properties of the operator
H=-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}+|x|

We start with recalling several known facts on the spectral theory of the anharmonic oscillator

H=-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}+|x|
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, which can be found in
[
8
,
10
,
33
]
.

Fact 1.

H
has a compact resolvent and the spectrum
\sigma(H)=\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, with

\displaystyle 1<\lambda_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{2k-1}<\lambda_{2k}\rightarrow+\infty,\quad\,\,k\rightarrow+\infty.

(2.1)

Moreover, each eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
is simple, that is, the dimension of each eigenspace is one.

Fact 2.
The eigenvalues of
H
are determined by (see e.g. in
[
33
, p.152]
)

\displaystyle Ai(-\lambda_{2k-1})=0,\quad\,\,Ai^{\prime}(-\lambda_{2k})=0,\quad\,\ k=1,2,\ldots,

(2.2)

where
Ai(\cdot)
is the Airy function given by

\displaystyle Ai(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{e^{{\rm i}(x\cdot t+t^{3}/3)}\,\mathrm{d}t}.

Fact 3.
It follows from
[
10
, Fact 2.7]
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\cdot k\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}+o(k^{\frac{2}{3}})

(2.3)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{k+1}\leq\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{k}.

(2.4)

Fact 4.
Let
\varphi_{k}
be the
L^{2}
normalized eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{k}
.
By
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
(or
[
33
, p.153]
), we have

\varphi_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}A_{k}\cdot Ai(x-\lambda_{k}),\;\;\qquad\qquad\quad\text{if}\,\,x\geq 0,\\
(-1)^{k+1}\cdot A_{k}\cdot Ai(-x-\lambda_{k}),\;\;\;\;\,\text{if}\,\,x<0,\end{cases}

(2.5)

where

\displaystyle A_{k}=\left(2\int_{-\lambda_{k}}^{\infty}{|Ai(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.6)

The next lemma gives some properties on
\varphi_{k}(x)
which will be used later.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)
Each eigenfunction
\varphi_{k}
is even or odd. Precisely, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(x)=(-1)^{k+1}\varphi_{k}(-x),\,\,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.7)

(ii)
When
x>0
, we have

\varphi_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}a_{k}\cdot(\lambda_{k}-x)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(\sin(\frac{2}{3}(\lambda_{k}-x)^{\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{\pi}{4})+R_{k}(x)\right),\,\,\,0<x<\lambda_{k}-\delta,\\
O(\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{4}}),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\lambda_{k}-\delta\leq x\leq\lambda_{k}+\delta,\\
a_{k}\cdot(x-\lambda_{k})^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{2}{3}(x-\lambda_{k})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(1+R_{k}(x)\right),\qquad\qquad\quad\,\,x>\lambda_{k}+\delta,\end{cases}

(2.8)

where
\delta>0
is a small but fixed constant independent of
\lambda_{k}
and
x
. Moreover,

\displaystyle|a_{k}|\sim\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{4}},\,\,\,\,R_{k}(x)=O\left(|x-\lambda_{k}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right).

(2.9)

Here and in what follows, given the sequences of numbers
\{\alpha_{k}\}
and
\{\gamma_{k}\}
, by
\alpha_{k}\sim\gamma_{k}
, we mean that
there are
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
so that
C_{1}|\gamma_{k}|\leq|\alpha_{k}|\leq C_{2}|\gamma_{k}|
for all
k
, while by
\alpha_{k}=O(\gamma_{k})
,
we mean that
there is
C_{3}>0
so that
|\alpha_{k}|\leq C_{3}|\gamma_{k}|
for all
k
.

Proof.

Conclusion
(i)
follows directly from (
2.5
).

To show the conclusion
(ii)
, we recall the following asymptotic expression of
Ai(\cdot)

(see, e.g.,
[
33
, Chapter 2]
):

Ai(-x)=\begin{cases}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot x^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(\sin{\frac{2}{3}x^{\frac{3}{2}}}+R_{k}(x)\right),\qquad\qquad\,x>\delta,\\
O(1),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\delta\leq x\leq\delta,\\
\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(-x)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{2}{3}x^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(1+R_{k}(x)\right),\qquad\quad x<-\delta,\end{cases}

(2.10)

where
\delta>0
is a small but fixed constant, and
R_{k}(x)=O(|x|^{-\frac{3}{2}})
.
Meanwhile, by (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\lambda_{k}}^{\infty}{Ai^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}=\int_{-\lambda_{k}}^{1}{Ai^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}+\int_{1}^{\infty}{Ai^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}\sim\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.11)

This, together with (
2.6
), implies that

A_{k}\sim\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

(2.12)

Now, (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) follow from (
2.5
), (
2.10
) and (
2.12
). This
completes the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

We will also use the following properties on the Airy function and
A_{k}
(given by (
2.6
)) later.
First, we have

\int_{-\lambda_{k}}^{\infty}{Ai^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}=\lambda_{k}\cdot Ai^{2}(-\lambda_{k})+Ai^{\prime 2}(-\lambda_{k})=\begin{cases}\lambda_{k}\cdot Ai^{2}(-\lambda_{k}),\,\,\,\text{if $k$ is even},\\
Ai^{\prime 2}(-\lambda_{k}),\quad\,\,\,\,\,\text{if $k$ is odd}.\end{cases}

(2.13)

In fact, the first equality follows from the integration by parts and the fact that
Ai^{\prime\prime}(x)=x\cdot Ai(x)
, while the second follows from (
2.2
).
Second, we have that
(see e.g. in
[
31
]
):

Ai(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{Ai(x)Ai^{\prime}(y)-Ai(y)Ai^{\prime}(x)}{x-y},&x\neq y,\\
Ai^{\prime}(x)^{2}-xAi(x)^{2}&x=y.\\
\end{cases}

(2.14)

Third, by

[
31
, p. 165]
, we also have

\displaystyle Ai(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{Ai(t+x)Ai(t+y)\,\mathrm{d}t}.

(2.15)

2.2.
Spectral property of Hermitian Toeplitz matrix and Szegö’s theorem

We recall that a Toeplitz matrix is an
n\times n
matrix
T_{n}=(a_{k,j})_{k,j=1}^{n}

satisfying

a_{k,j}=a_{k-j},\qquad 1\leq k,j\leq n,

i.e., a matrix of the form

\displaystyle T_{n}=T_{n}[a_{1-n},\cdots,a_{-1},a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1}]:=\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}&a_{-1}&a_{-2}&\ldots&a_{1-n}\\
\ a_{1}&a_{0}&a_{-1}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\ a_{2}&a_{1}&\ddots&\ddots&a_{-2}\\
\ \vdots&\ddots&\ddots&a_{0}&a_{-1}\\
\ a_{n-1}&\ldots&a_{2}&a_{1}&a_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

(2.16)

Given a Lebesgue integrable complex valued function
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
, we let

\displaystyle T_{n}(f):=T_{n}[a_{1-n},\cdots,a_{-1},a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1}],

(2.17)

with

\displaystyle a_{k}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{f(t)e^{-{\rm i}kt}\,\mathrm{d}t},\;\;k=0,\pm 1,\cdots,\pm(n-1).

(2.18)

We say that
T_{n}(f)
is generated by
f
. We call
T_{n}(f)

the
n
-th finite section of the Toeplitz matrix associated with the function
f
and
f
the symbol of the matrix
T_{n}(f)
.
One can directly check that if
f
is real valued, then
\bar{a}_{k}=a_{-k}
, i.e.,
T_{n}(f)
is Hermitian, and that if
f
is an even and real-valued function, then
T_{n}(f)
is a real matrix.

The asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues of
T_{n}(f)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
has been
thoroughly studied by mathematicians and physicists for a long time.
In this paper,
we shall use the following fundamental result (due to Szegö, see
[
12
, p. 65]
), which describes the asymptotic behavior of the extreme eigenvalues of the eigenvalues
of the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix

T_{n}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma 2.2

(
Szegö’s limit theorem
)
.

Assume that
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
is real valued. Let

\displaystyle\lambda_{\text{min}}=\lambda_{1}(T_{n})\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}(T_{n})=\lambda_{\text{max}}

(2.19)

denote the eigenvalues of the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix
T_{n}(f)
given by (
2.17
) and (
2.18
). If we set

\displaystyle m_{f}=\inf_{x\in(-\pi,\,\pi)}f(x),\qquad M_{f}=\sup_{x\in(-\pi,\,\pi)}f(x).

(2.20)

Then for all
n\geq 1
,

m_{f}\leq\lambda_{1}(T_{n})\leq\lambda_{n}(T_{n})\leq M_{f}.

Moreover, one has

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1}(T_{n})=m_{f},\qquad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{n}(T_{n})=M_{f}.

(2.21)

2.3.
Salem and Nazarov inequality

We will present two well-known results on trigonometric polynomials that will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Given
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we let

\displaystyle p(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}e^{{\rm i}\lambda_{k}t},\;\;t\in\mathbb{R},

(2.22)

where

c_{k}\in\mathbb{C},\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and
k=1,2,\cdots,n
. It is an exponential polynomial.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{n}
. The following Salem inequality is stated in Nazarov’s work
[
25
, Sec. 1.1]
. We refer to
[
35
, p.222]
for a detailed proof.

Lemma 2.3

(Salem inequality)
.

Assume that the sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq n}
has a uniform gap
\Delta>0
, namely,

\inf_{1\leq k\leq n-1}\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\geq\Delta.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval with the length
|I|\geq 4\pi/\Delta
. Then the exponential polynomial (
2.22
) satisfies

\sum_{k=1}^{n}|c_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{|I|}\int_{I}|p(t)|^{2}{\rm d}t.

(2.23)

Inequality (
2.23
) can be viewed as a finite sum analogue of Ingham’s inequality (see
[
16
]
and
[
18
, p. 162]
) with explicit constants. We remark that inequalities akin to (
2.23
), which do not specify explicit constants, and are also referred to Ingham’s inequality in many literatures.

Lemma 2.4

(Nazarov inequality
[
25
]
)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval and
E\subset I
be a measurable set. Then for all exponential polynomials of the type (
2.22
),

\int_{I}|p(t)|^{2}{\rm d}t\leq\left(\frac{A|I|}{|E|}\right)^{2n-1}\int_{E}|p(t)|^{2}{\rm d}t,

where
A>0
is an absolute constant.

The Nazarov inequality provides a quantitative unique continuation property for exponential polynomials, which is a generalization of Remez’s inequality for polynomials.

3.1.
Ingham-type spectral inequality

The main result of this subsection is the following Ingham-type spectral inequality:

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin with
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
such that
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\int_{E}\left|\sum_{k\in J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n})}c_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)\right|^{2}{\rm d}x\geq\delta\sum_{k\in J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n})}|c_{k}|^{2}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\{c_{k}\}\in l^{2},

(3.1)

where
J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n})
is given by (
1.3.1
).

In order to prove Proposition
3.1
, we need the following two lemmas which concern the contributions of diagonal terms and off-diagonal terms, respectively.

Lemma 3.2

(Lower Bounds for Diagonal Terms)
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)
The set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
is weakly thick, i.e., (
1.4
) holds.

(ii)
There exists an absolute constant
D_{1}>0
independent of
k
such that

\displaystyle\int_{E}|\varphi_{k}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\geq D_{1}\;\;\mbox{ for all }\;\;k.

(3.2)

Proof.

Its proof is given in the appendix
A
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We have the following two conclusions:
(i)
If
E^{c}\subset\mathbb{R}
is
\alpha
-thin for some
\alpha>0
, then
E
is thick, i.e., (
1.3
) holds. But the converse is not true.
(ii)
If
E\subset\mathbb{R}
is thick, then it is weakly thick. But the converse is not true.

In fact, the conclusion
(ii)
has been proved in
[
14
, Section 4.3]
. We now prove the conclusion
(i)
. Suppose that

E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin. Then there exists
L_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|E^{c}\bigcap[x,x+1]\right|<C_{1}|x|^{-\alpha},\quad\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,|x|\geq L_{1},

(3.3)

where
C_{1}>0
is a constant independent of
x
. Now we choose
L_{2}\geq L_{1}
so that
C_{1}\cdot L_{2}^{-\alpha}<\frac{1}{2}
.
Then it follows from (
3.3
) that

|E\bigcap[x,x+1]|\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all
|x|\geq L_{2}
. Let
L=4L_{2}
. Then, a direct computation yields

|E\bigcap[x,x+L]|\geq\frac{L}{4}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
E
is thick. In contrast, we let
E=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(n,n+\frac{1}{2})
. It is clear that
E
is thick. However, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
|E^{c}\bigcap[n,n+1]|=|[n+\frac{1}{2},n+1]|=\frac{1}{2}
. Thus,
E^{c}
is not
\alpha
-thin for any
\alpha>0
.

Lemma 3.4

(Upper Bounds for Non-diagonal Terms)
.

Assume that
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin with
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E}\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{k}(x){\rm d}x\right|\lesssim\Upsilon_{0}(\lambda_{n},\alpha):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha},&\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1),\\
\lambda_{n}^{-1}\log\lambda_{n},&\alpha=1,\\
\lambda_{n}^{-1},&\alpha>1,\end{array}\right.

when
\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\in[\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}},\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}]
.

Proof.

Notice two facts: First, the orthogonality of
\varphi_{k}
and
\varphi_{j}
(with
k\neq j
) indicates

\int_{E}\varphi_{j}\varphi_{k}{\rm d}x=-\int_{E^{c}}\varphi_{j}\varphi_{k}{\rm d}x.

Second, Lemma
2.1

(i)
shows that
\varphi_{j}\varphi_{k}
is either odd or even.
Thus, to prove (
3.4
), it suffices to show that for an arbitrarily fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and
\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\in[\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}},\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}]
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E^{c}\cap[0,\infty)}\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{k}(x){\rm d}x\right|\lesssim\Upsilon_{0}(\lambda_{n},\alpha).

(3.7)

We now show (
3.7
). By Lemma
2.1

(ii)
, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi_{j}(x)|,|\varphi_{k}(x)|\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(\lambda_{n}-x\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}},\,\,\,\,\,\quad\quad\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,0<x<\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{4}},\,\,\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq x\leq\lambda_{n}+2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{2}{3}(x-\lambda_{n}-\sqrt{\lambda_{n}})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\qquad\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,x>\lambda_{n}+2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{array}\right.

Meanwhile, it is clear that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E^{c}\cap[0,\infty)}\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{k}(x){\rm d}x\right|\leq J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

(3.11)

where

\displaystyle J_{1}:=\int_{E^{c}\cap[0,\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}})}|\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{k}(x)|{\rm d}x,\qquad J_{2}:=\int_{E^{c}\cap[\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}},\lambda_{n}+2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}]}|\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{k}(x)|{\rm d}x,

J_{3}:=\int_{E^{c}\cap(\lambda_{n}+2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}},\infty)}|\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{k}(x)|{\rm d}x.

We first deal with
J_{1}
. Since
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin, we have

\displaystyle|E^{c}\cap[l-1,l]|\lesssim l^{-\alpha},\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;l\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.12)

Then, it follows from (
3.1
) and (
3.12
) that

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{E^{c}\cap[0,\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}})}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{n}-x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x\lesssim\sum_{1\leq l\leq[\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}]+1}|E\cap[l-1,l]|\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{n}-l)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(3.13)

where in the last step we used the fact

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq l\leq[\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}]+1}l^{-\alpha}(\lambda_{n}-l)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{1\leq l\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{n}}l^{-\alpha}(\lambda_{n}-l)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}<l\leq[\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}]+1}l^{-\alpha}(\lambda_{n}-l)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{1\leq l\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{n}}l^{-\alpha}+\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha}\sum_{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}<l\leq[\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}]+1}(\lambda_{n}-l)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha},&\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1),\\
\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\lambda_{n},&\alpha=1,\\
\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\alpha>1.\end{array}\right.

For
J_{2}
, we can use a similar way to the above to get

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq l\leq\lambda_{n}+2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}}|E^{c}\cap[l-1,l]|\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq l\leq\lambda_{n}+2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}}l^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\sharp\left\{l\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda_{n}-2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq l\leq\lambda_{n}+2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha}.

(3.14)

For
J_{3}
, we use (

) to obtain

\displaystyle J_{3}\lesssim\int_{\lambda_{n}+2\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{4}{3}(x-\lambda_{n}-\sqrt{\lambda_{n}})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\rm d}x\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{3}{4}}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-\alpha}.

(3.15)

Finally, by (
3.11
), (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and (

), and noting

, we are led to (

). This completes the proof.
∎

.

Proof of Proposition

We arbitrarily fix

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k\neq j,k,j\in J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n})}|c_{j}c_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{k\neq j,k,j\in J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n})}\left|\int_{E}\varphi_{j}\varphi_{k}{\rm d}x\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n})}|c_{k}|^{2}\cdot\left(\sum_{k\neq j,k,j\in J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n})}(D_{3}\Upsilon_{0}(\lambda_{n},\alpha))^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon D_{2}D_{3}\sum_{k\in J_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{n})}|c_{k}|^{2}.

(3.21)

where
D_{3}
is given by Lemma
3.4
.

Finally, by plugging (
3.17
) and (
3.21
) into (
3.16
), and taking
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varepsilon D_{2}D_{3}\leq\frac{D_{1}}{2}
, we obtain (
3.1
).
This completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
. ∎

3.2.
Relaxed observability inequality

For each
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the following Sobolev norms associated to
H
:

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+\lambda_{n})^{2k}|(u,\varphi_{n})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
The main result of this subsection is the following relaxed observability inequality:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
H
be given in (
1.7
). Suppose that
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin with
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
. Then for each
T\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim T^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}t+\Upsilon_{1}(T,\alpha)\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}}\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

(3.22)

where

\Upsilon_{1}(T,\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T^{-(1+\frac{6}{2\alpha-1})},&\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\\
T^{-7}(\log\frac{1}{T})^{6},&\alpha=1,\\
T^{-7},&\alpha>1.\end{array}\right.

To prove it, we need the following resolvent estimate, which relies on the spectral inequality established in Proposition
3.1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
H
be given in (
1.7
). Suppose that
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin with
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Then for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)\|(H-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;u\in D(H),

(3.23)

where

\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1+|\lambda|)^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})},&\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1),\\
(1+|\lambda|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(e+|\lambda|),&\alpha=1,\\
(1+|\lambda|)^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\alpha>1.\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

First, since
H\geq I
, we have

\|(H-\lambda I)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\geq(1+|\lambda|)\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\\
,\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;\lambda\leq 0,

which leads to (
3.23
) with
\lambda\leq 0
.

Next, we consider the case
\lambda\gg 1
. For brevity, we only
prove (
3.23
) with

\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
. The proof of (
3.23
) with
\alpha\geq 1
can be done similarly.

To this end, we arbitrarily fix
\lambda\gg 1
and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
. Since
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin, we can use Proposition
3.1
to find
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
such that (
3.1
) holds.
Let
Q_{\lambda}
be the projection onto the eigenspace spanned by the eigenfunctions
\varphi_{k}
with

|\lambda_{k}-\lambda|<\varepsilon\lambda^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|(H-\lambda I)(I-Q_{\lambda})u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\geq\varepsilon\lambda^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\|(I-Q_{\lambda})u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.24)

Meanwhile, by (
3.1
), we see

\displaystyle\|Q_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}(E)}\geq\delta\|Q_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.25)

By (
3.24
) and (
3.25
), we find

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-Q_{\lambda})u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|Q_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|(1-Q_{\lambda})u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|Q_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}(E)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|(1-Q_{\lambda})u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})}\|(H-\lambda)(1-Q_{\lambda})u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})}\|(H-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}.

In the last inequality above, we used the fact that
\|(1-Q_{\lambda})(H-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|(H-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.
Thus, we have proved (
3.23
) for the case
\lambda\gg 1
.

Finally, we turn to the case where
0<\lambda\lesssim 1
. Then (
3.23
) reduces to

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|(H-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}.

(3.26)

We assume that
\lambda\leq\Lambda_{0}
for some
\Lambda_{0}>0
.
We need the following two observations:

(i)
Since all eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
are simple, there exists a positive constant
\delta
such that

\delta_{0}:=\inf_{\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda_{0}}|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}|>0.

(
ii
)
By combining Lemma
3.2
, Remark
3.3
and the assumption that
E^{c}
is
\alpha
-thin, we deduce that

\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(E)}.

Based on these two facts, (
3.26
) can be proved in a manner analogous to the case
\lambda\gg 1
, provided that we define
Q_{\lambda}
as the projection onto the eigenspace spanned by the eigenfunctions
\varphi_{k}
with

|\lambda_{k}-\lambda|<\delta_{0}
.

Hence, we complete the proof.
∎

Now we are in a position to prove Proposition
3.5
.

Proof of Proposition
3.5
:

We arbitrarily fix
T\in(0,1/2)
. Let
t\mapsto\chi(t)\in\mathbb{R}
(
t\in\mathbb{R}
) be a smooth cut-off function supported over
[0,1]
.
Let
\chi_{T}(t):=\chi(t/T)
(
t\in\mathbb{R}
) and
w(t,x):=\chi_{T}(t)e^{-{\rm i}tH}u_{0}(x)
(
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
). Then
w
satisfies

\displaystyle(i\partial_{t}-H)w(t,x)=i\chi^{\prime}_{T}e^{-{\rm i}tH}u_{0}(x):=v(t,x),\;\;t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.27)

First, taking the Fourier transform with respect to the variable
t
in (
3.27
) gives

(-H-\tau)\widehat{w}(\tau,x)=\widehat{v}(\tau,x),\;\;\tau\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}.

This, along with Lemma
3.6
, leads to

\displaystyle\|\widehat{w}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}\leq C\Upsilon_{2}(\tau,\alpha)\|\widehat{v}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}+C\|\widehat{w}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(E)},\;\tau\in\mathbb{R},

(3.28)

where

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{2}(\tau,\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1+|\tau|)^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})},&\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1),\\
(1+|\tau|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(e+|\tau|),&\alpha=1,\\
(1+|\tau|)^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\alpha>1.\\
\end{array}\right.

(Here and in what follows,
C
stands for a positive constant (independent of
t
,
\tau
,
x
and
\alpha
) which varies in different contents.)
Taking the
L^{2}
norm with respect to the
\tau
variable in (
3.28
) leads to

\|\widehat{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{\tau})}\\
\leq C\Upsilon_{2}(N,\alpha)\|\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{\tau})}+C\|\widehat{w}\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{R}_{\tau})}+\left(\int_{|\tau|\leq N}\|\widehat{v}(\tau,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}{\rm d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.32)

Second, by the Plancherel theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{\tau})}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}\|\chi_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{t})}\sim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})},

(3.33)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{v}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{\tau})}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}\|\chi^{\prime}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{t})}\sim T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})},

(3.34)

and

\displaystyle\|\widehat{w}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{R}_{\tau})}=\|\chi_{T}e^{-{\rm i}tH}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.35)

Third, since
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, we can choose
N
large enough such that

\displaystyle C\Upsilon_{2}(N,\alpha)\|\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{\tau})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\widehat{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{\tau})}.

(3.36)

Indeed, by (
3.2
), (
3.33
) and (
3.34
), we find that (
3.36
) holds, provided that

\displaystyle N\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})^{-1}},&\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\\
T^{-2}(\log\frac{1}{T})^{2},&\alpha=1,\\
T^{-2},&\alpha>1.\end{array}\right.

Now, it follows from (
3.32
), (
3.35
) and (
3.36
) that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}\leq C^{\prime}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C^{\prime}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{|\tau|\leq N}\|\widehat{v}(\tau,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}{\rm d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.40)

Next, we deal with the last term in (
3.40
).
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we let
u_{n}=(u_{0},\varphi_{n})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}
. Then we have

e^{-{\rm i}tH}u_{0}(x)=\sum_{n}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda_{n}}u_{n}\varphi_{n}(x),\;t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}.

With (
3.27
), the above yields

\widehat{v}(\tau,x)={\rm i}\sum_{n}\widehat{\chi^{\prime}_{T}}(\tau-\lambda_{n})u_{n}\varphi_{n}(x),\;\;\tau\in\mathbb{R},\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-2}
for each
\xi\in R
,
we have

|\widehat{\chi^{\prime}_{T}}(\xi)|\lesssim|T\xi|(1+T|\xi|)^{-2}\lesssim(1+T|\xi|)^{-1},\;\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

This yields that when
|\tau|\leq N
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{\chi^{\prime}_{T}}(\tau-\lambda_{n})|\lesssim(1+T|\tau-\lambda_{n}|)^{-1}\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T^{-1}(1+\lambda_{n})^{-1},&\lambda_{n}>2N,\\
N(1+\lambda_{n})^{-1},&\lambda_{n}\leq 2N.\end{array}\right.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{|\tau|\leq N}\|\widehat{v}(\tau,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x})}{\rm d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|u_{n}|^{2}\int_{|\tau|\leq N}|\widehat{\chi^{\prime}_{T}}(\tau-\lambda_{n})|^{2}{\rm d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|u_{n}|^{2}(1+\lambda_{n})^{-2}N(T^{-2}+N^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}(T^{-1}+N)\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}},

where in the last step we used
T^{-1}\lesssim N
, which follows from (
3.2
). Plugging this into (
3.40
) and making use of (
3.2
) again, we obtain (
3.22
). This completes the proof of Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

In the proof of Proposition
3.5
, the following fact is crucial to derive the arbitrariness of
T
in (
3.22
):
\Upsilon_{2}(\tau,\alpha)\rightarrow 0
, as
\tau\rightarrow\infty
. The latter requires
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
. On the other hand, when
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, it follows from Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
4.1
that
E
is an observable set at some time for (
1.6
), which is weaker than Theorem
1.3
, see Remark
4.2
.

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this subsection, we will prove Theorem
1.3
by the relaxed observability inequality (
3.22
) and the quantitative unique compactness strategy developed in
[
5
]
. This approach enables us not only to effectively glue the high-frequency and low-frequency estimates, but also to obtain an explicit expression for the observable constant
C_{obs}(T,E)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We begin by noting that it suffices to prove the observability inequality (
1.2
) for any sufficiently small
T>0
.
We arbitrarily fix a small
T\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. We simply write
(\varphi,\psi)

for
(\varphi,\psi)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}}
,
with
\varphi,\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
For each
K>0
, we define the spectral projection

\Pi_{K}\varphi=\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq K}(\varphi,\varphi_{k})\varphi_{k},\;\;\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

By Proposition
3.5
, we see that for each
t\in[\frac{T}{4},T]
and each
K>0
,

\|(I-\Pi_{K})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq CT^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{-{\rm i}sH}(I-\Pi_{K})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}s+CK^{-2}\Upsilon_{1}(T,\alpha)\|(I-\Pi_{K})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

where
\Upsilon_{1}(T,\alpha)
is given by Proposition
3.5
.
So, when
K\geq(2C\Upsilon_{1}(T,\alpha))^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

\displaystyle\|(I-\Pi_{K})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2CT^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{-{\rm i}sH}(I-\Pi_{K})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}s,\quad t\in[\frac{T}{4},T].

(3.41)

Let

\displaystyle N:=(2C\Upsilon_{1}(T,\alpha))^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2\alpha-1})},&\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\\
T^{-\frac{7}{2}}(\log\frac{1}{T})^{3},&\alpha=1,\\
T^{-\frac{7}{2}},&\alpha>1.\end{array}\right.

Then by (
3.41
) (where
t=T/4
and
u_{0}
is replaced by
e^{-{\rm i}\frac{T}{2}H}u_{0}
), we obtain that when
K\geq N
,

\displaystyle\|(I-\Pi_{K})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2CT^{-1}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{3T}{4}}\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}(I-\Pi_{K})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}t.

(3.45)

To prove the theorem, we decompose the initial state
u_{0}
into two components

u_{0}=(\Pi_{M}-\Pi_{N})u_{0}+(I-\Pi_{M}+\Pi_{N})u_{0},

where
M
is chosen to be sufficiently large such that
M>2N
. We proceed to estimate each term on the right hand side separately and then glue them together. The remainder of the proof is organized into several steps.

Step 1. Estimate of
\Pi_{N}u_{0}
.

Since

e^{-{\rm i}tH}\Pi_{N}u_{0}=\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda_{k}}u_{k}\varphi_{k},\;\;\mbox{with}\;\;u_{k}=(u_{0},\varphi_{k}),

we can use Fubini’s theorem to get

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{3T}{4}}\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}t=\int_{E}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{3T}{4}}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda_{k}}u_{k}\varphi_{k}\Big|^{2}{\rm d}t{\rm d}x.

(3.46)

Next we give a lower bound for the term on the right hand of (
3.46
). To this end,
we use (
2.4
) to obtain the following uniform gap for
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}
:

\displaystyle\Delta\sim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.47)

Thus, we can apply Lemma
2.3
, i.e., the Salem inequality,
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}|u_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{|I|}\int_{I}\big|\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda_{k}}u_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t,\;\;x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.48)

provided that the interval
I
satisfies
|I|\geq 4\pi/\Delta
, which is possible if

\displaystyle|I|\sim N^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.49)

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
[0,T]\subset I
. Let

n_{0}=\sharp\{\lambda_{k}:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}.

Then if follows from (
2.3
) that
n_{0}\leq N^{\frac{3}{2}}
for
T>0
sufficient small.
Applying Lemma
2.4
(the Nazarov inequality),
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{I}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda_{k}}u_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)\Big|^{2}{\rm d}t\leq\left(\frac{4A|I|}{T}\right)^{2N^{\frac{3}{2}}-1}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{3T}{4}}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda_{k}}u_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)\Big|^{2}{\rm d}t,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.50)

where
A>0
is the constant given by Lemma
2.4
.

Combining (
3.48
) and (
3.50
), and integrating over
E
we have

\displaystyle\frac{4}{|I|}\left(\frac{4A|I|}{T}\right)^{2N^{\frac{3}{2}}-1}\int_{E}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{3T}{4}}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda_{k}}u_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)\Big|^{2}{\rm d}t{\rm d}x\geq\int_{E}\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}|u_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x.

(3.51)

Meanwhile, by Lemma
3.2
and Remark
3.3
, we have that for some
c>0
(independent of
k
),

\inf_{k}\int_{E}|\varphi_{k}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\geq c,

which yields

\displaystyle\int_{E}\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}|u_{k}\varphi_{k}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\geq c\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq N}|u_{k}|^{2}=c\big\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.52)

Finally, it follows from (
3.46
), (
3.51
) and (
3.52
) that for some absolute constant
A_{1}>0

\displaystyle\big\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\big\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{A_{1}N^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T}\right)^{2N^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{3T}{4}}\big\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}\Pi_{N}u_{0}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}t.

(3.53)

Step 2. Estimate of
(I-\Pi_{M}+\Pi_{N})u_{0}
.

Let
M>2N
be a quantity to be determined later.
Let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,1)
be a cutoff function such that
\eta=1
on
[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4}]
.
Denote
\eta_{T}(t)=\eta(t/T)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
write
\mathbf{1}_{E}
for the characteristic function of
E
. Then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}(I-\Pi_{M}+\Pi_{N})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}\eta_{T}(t){\rm d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}(I-\Pi_{M})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}\eta_{T}(t){\rm d}t\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}\eta_{T}(t){\rm d}t+\mathcal{I},

(3.54)

where

\mathcal{I}:=2\mbox{ Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{1}_{E}e^{-{\rm i}tH}(I-\Pi_{M})u_{0},\mathbf{1}_{E}e^{-{\rm i}tH}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\eta_{T}(t){\rm d}t\\
=2\mbox{ Re}\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq N}\sum_{\lambda_{m}>M}u_{n}u_{m}(\mathbf{1}_{E}\varphi_{n},\mathbf{1}_{E}\varphi_{m})\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{{\rm i}(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m})t}\eta_{T}(t){\rm d}t.

(3.55)

Notice that for each
l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there is an absolute constant
D_{l}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\widehat{\eta_{T}}(\tau)|\leq D_{l}T(1+|T\tau|)^{-l},\;\;\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.56)

By
(
3.55
) and (
3.56
), we see that for each
l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq N}\sum_{\lambda_{m}>M}|u_{n}u_{m}||\widehat{\eta_{T}}(\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq N}\sum_{\lambda_{m}>M}(|u_{n}|^{2}+|u_{m}|^{2})D_{l}T(1+T(\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n}))^{-l}

(since
M>2N
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq N}\sum_{\lambda_{m}>M}(|u_{n}|^{2}+|u_{m}|^{2})D_{l}T(1+2^{-1}T\lambda_{m})^{-l}

\displaystyle\leq I_{1,l}+I_{2,l},

(3.57)

where

I_{1,l}:=\sum_{\lambda_{m}>M}D_{l}T(1+2^{-1}T\lambda_{m})^{-l}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

and

I_{2,l}:=\sharp\{n:\lambda_{n}\leq N\}\sum_{\lambda_{m}>M}|u_{m}|^{2}D_{l}T(1+2^{-1}T\lambda_{m})^{-l}.

For the term
I_{1,l}
, using the asymptotic relation
\lambda_{m}\sim m^{\frac{2}{3}}
, we see that for
l\geq 2
,

\displaystyle I_{1,l}\lesssim C_{l}2^{l}T^{1-l}\sum_{\lambda_{m}>M}m^{-\frac{2}{3}l}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim D_{l}2^{l}T^{1-l}M^{\frac{3}{2}-l}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(3.58)

For the term
I_{2,l}
, noting that
\sharp\{n:\lambda_{n}\leq N\}\lesssim N^{\frac{3}{2}}\lesssim M^{\frac{3}{2}}
, we have
that for each
l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

Combining (

+\left(\frac{A_{1}N^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T}\right)^{-2N^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}-C_{1}T^{-1}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

3.4.
Perturbation with a bounded potential

This subsection shows that Theorem
1.3
remains true (up to the explicit control cost
C_{obs}
) for the operator
-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}+|x|+V(x)
, where
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. The key of its proof is the following resolvent estimate:

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
H=-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}+|x|
. Let
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be real valued. Suppose that
E^{c}

is
\alpha
-thin with
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
. Then for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)\|(H+V-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)},\;\;u\in D(H),

(3.76)

where
\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)
is given by Lemma
3.6
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.6
that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in D(H)
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)\|(H-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)\|(H+V-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}.

(3.77)

Since
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)\to 0
as
\lambda\to\infty
, we can choose
\lambda_{0}>0
large enough so that, for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
,
\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
can be absorbed by
the term on the left hand side of (
3.77
). This proves (
3.76
) for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

Next, we suppose that
\lambda\leq-1-2\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
. Then

((H+V-\lambda)u,u)\geq(|\lambda|-\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\gtrsim(1+|\lambda|)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\;u\in D(H).

This, together with the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, implies that

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim(1+|\lambda|)^{-1}\|(H+V-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\Upsilon_{2}(\lambda,\alpha)\|(H-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\;u\in D(H).

Thus (
3.76
) also holds in this case.

Finally, we suppose that
-1-2\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
. Then, (
3.76
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|(H+V-\lambda)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}.

(3.78)

By contradiction, we
suppose that (
3.78
)
is not true. Then there exist sequences
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset D(H)
and
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset(-1-2\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\lambda_{0})
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1,\quad\|(H+V-\lambda_{n})u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(E)}\leq\frac{1}{n}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;n\geq 1.

(3.79)

This yields that
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
D(H)
. Thus, without loss of generality, we can assume that
u_{n}\to u
weakly in
D(H)
,
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda\in[-1-2\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\lambda_{0}]
as
n\to\infty
. Since the embedding
D(H)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is compact, we find
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus

\displaystyle(H+V-\lambda)u=0\,\,\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{R},\quad u=0\,\,\mbox{ a.e. on }\,\,E.

(3.80)

Since
u\in D(H)
, we find that
u^{\prime}
is continuous and
u(x)=0
for each
x\in E
.

We claim that
u^{\prime}=0
almost everywhere on
E
. For this purpose,
it suffices
to show that
u^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
for an arbitrarily fixed point
x_{0}
of density of
E
. Clearly,
x_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{|E\cap(x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon)|}{|(x_{0}-\varepsilon,x_{0}+\varepsilon)|}=1.

(3.81)

From (
3.81
), we can find a sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subset E
such that
x_{n}\to x_{0}
. Thus,
u(x_{0})=0
and
u(x_{n})=0
for all
n\geq 1
. Consequently,

u^{\prime}(x_{0})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{u(x_{n})-u(x_{0})}{x_{n}-x_{0}}=0,

which leads to the claim above.

Now we arbitrarily fix
x_{0}\in E
such that
u(x_{0})=u^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
. It follows from (
3.80
) that

\displaystyle u(x)=\int^{x}_{x_{0}}\int^{y}_{x_{0}}(|z|+V(z)-\lambda)u(z){\rm d}z{\rm d}y,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.82)

Let
I
be a compact interval containing
x_{0}
. We define a linear operator
T:L^{\infty}(I)\to L^{\infty}(I)
in the manner: for each
v\in L^{\infty}(I)
, we let
Tv
to be the right hand side of (
3.82
) where
u=v
. Then, it is easy to see that
T^{m}
is a contraction mapping on
L^{\infty}(I)
for
m
large enough. (Note that
m
depends only on
\lambda,\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
and
|I|
.) Thus (
3.82
) has a unique solution, that is,
u\equiv 0
on
I
. Since the size
|I|
can be arbitrarily large, we find
u\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}
. This contradicts with
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1
in (
3.79
). Thus (
3.78
) is proved.

This completes the proof.
∎

With Lemma
3.9
in hand, we can use the similar to that proving Proposition
3.5
to obtain that for any
T\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim T^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{-{\rm i}t(H+V)}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}{\rm d}t+\Upsilon_{1}(T,\alpha)\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}},\;\;u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

(3.83)

where
\Upsilon_{1}(T,\alpha)
is given by Proposition
3.5
, and

\|u\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{k}}:=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+\lambda_{n})^{2k}|(u,\varphi_{n})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\;\;k\in\mathbb{R}

with
\lambda_{n}
and

being eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of

in

, respectively.

Thanks to Lemma

[
5

to obtain the following observability inequality:

Theorem 3.10
.

Let

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}

\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

(ii)

\varepsilon>0

(4.3)

where

Proof of the claim (

)

We first compute

\det\mathbb{A}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{1}\tau}&e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{1}2\tau}&\cdots&e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{1}m\tau}\\
e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{2}\tau}&e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{2}2\tau}&\cdots&e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{2}m\tau}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{m}\tau}&e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{m}2\tau}&\cdots&e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{m}m\tau}\end{array}\right|.

Applying the well-known result for the Vandermonde determinant, we have

\displaystyle\det\mathbb{A}=e^{-{\rm i}\tau\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}\lambda_{j}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq m}(e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{k}\tau}-e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{j}\tau}).

(B.1)

A direct computation shows that
|e^{{\rm i}x}-1|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|
for all
x\in[-1,1]
. Thus

\displaystyle|e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{k}\tau}-e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{j}\tau}|=|e^{-{\rm i}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})\tau}-1|\geq\frac{1}{2}\tau|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}|

(B.2)

given that
|(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})\tau|\leq\lambda_{m}\frac{T}{10\lambda_{m}}<\frac{1}{10}
when
T\in(0,1)
. It follows from (
2.1
) and
(
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\geq\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1}\geq c_{1}\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}},\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;j<k.

(B.3)

Now, by (
B.1
)-(
B.3
), we see

\displaystyle|\det\mathbb{A}|\geq\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\frac{c_{1}\tau}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=(\frac{c_{1}\tau}{2})^{\frac{m^{2}}{2}}\prod_{1\leq k\leq m}\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}k}.

(B.4)

Recall that
\lambda_{k}\leq c_{2}k^{\frac{2}{3}}
(we assume
c_{2}>1
), we infer

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq k\leq m}\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}k}\geq\prod_{1\leq k\leq m}c_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}k}k^{-\frac{1}{3}k}\geq\prod_{1\leq k\leq m}c_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}m}m^{-\frac{1}{3}k}=c_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}}m^{-\frac{1}{6}m(m+1)}.

(B.5)

Note that
\tau=\frac{T}{10m}
(see (
3.63
)), we deduce from (
B.4
) and (

\displaystyle|\det\mathbb{A}|\geq(\frac{c_{1}T}{20c_{2}})^{\frac{m^{2}}{2}}m^{-m^{2}}.

(B.6)

Let
\mathbb{A}^{*}=(A_{jk})_{1\leq j,k\leq m}

. Since

\displaystyle|A_{jk}|\leq(m-1)!,\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;1\leq j,k\leq m.

(B.7)

Since

(B.8)

This completes the proof of (
3.65

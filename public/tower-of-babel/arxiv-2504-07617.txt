Möbius transforms and the integral representation of endofunctions


Abstract
. We supplement the Herglotz-Nevanlinna integral representation of so-called Pick functions by adding the formula for Möbius transforms and the positivity characterization near boundary supports.

46G99, 31A10

and

Introduction

Given a simply connected region
G
in
ℂ
,
we say that a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
G
is an endofunction if
\varphi(G)\subset G
.
Functions of this type have been long and deeply investigated by many researchers from various points
of view, which are referred to under the names such as
Carathéodory, Herglotz, Nevanlinna, Pick and Schur in literature.

When
G
is the upper half plane
\text{\ym C}_{+}=\{z\in\text{\ym C};\text{Im}\,z>0\}
,
a remarkable fact is that, if an atomic endofunction
\phi_{s}
of
\text{\ym C}_{+}
is assigned to each
boundary point
s\in\overline{\text{\ym R}}=\text{\ym R}\sqcup\{\infty\}

inside the Riemann sphere
\overline{\text{\ym C}}=\text{\ym C}\sqcup\{\infty\}
by

\phi_{s}(z)=\frac{1+sz}{s-z},

then any endofunction is expressed up to additive real constants
by the integral of
\phi_{s}
with respect to a unique positive measure on the boundary
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
.
In view of the fact that
\phi_{s}(z)
(
z\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
) is total
in the continuous function space
C(\overline{\text{\ym R}})

as a family of functions of
s
, the boundary measure
\lambda
is uniquely determined by
\varphi
.
More explicitly, we have the Stieltjes inversion formula

\lambda(dx)=\lim_{y\downarrow 0}\frac{\text{Im}\,\varphi(x+iy)}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\,dx

as a Radon measure on
ℝ
and

\lambda(\{\infty\})=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\text{Im}\,\varphi(iy)}{y}.

From this formula, one sees that
\lambda(U)=0
for an open neighborhood
U
of
a boundary point
c\in\overline{\text{\ym R}}
if and only if

\varphi
is continuously extended to
U
in such a way that

\lim_{y\downarrow 0}\varphi(x+iy)\in\text{\ym R}
(
x\in U
).
Thus, if we define the boundary support
[\varphi]
of a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}

to be the residual of such points
c
,

[\lambda]=[\varphi]
.
(There are so many literature on the subject and we just nominate
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
as references.)

In this paper, as a continuation of
[
5
, Appendix]
, we shall supplement properties of endofunctions
in connection with Möbius transform and boundary positivity:

Let
\varphi
be an endofunction with the boundary measure
\lambda
.
Consider an invertible
2\times 2
matrix
M
for which
the restriction
\phi_{M}
of the associated linear fractional function to
\text{\ym C}_{+}
is an endofunction.
The boundary measure of the composite endofunction
\varphi\circ\phi_{M}
is then
explicitly described in terms of
\lambda
and
M
.

As for boundary positivity, a holomorphic function different from a real constant
is well-known to be
an endofunction if
\text{Im}\,\varphi(z)\geq 0
for
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
near the boundary
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
.
We point out here that this boundary positivity follows
from positivity near the boundary support
[\varphi]
, which makes the positivity checking easier.

The authors are grateful to M. Nagisa and M. Uchiyama for fruitful discussions
and helpful comments on the present work.

1.
Background

Let
D=\{z\in\text{\ym C};|z|<1\}
be the unit disk in the complex plane with the boundary denoted by
\text{\ym T}=\{z\in\text{\ym C};|z|=1\}
.
Thanks to Herglotz and Riesz, a holomorphic function
\phi
on
D
satisfying
\text{Re}\,\phi(z)\geq 0
(
z\in D
) is characterized by
the integral representation

\phi(z)=i\text{Im}(\phi(0))+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\text{\ym T}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}\,\mu(d\zeta),

where
\mu
is a positive Radon measure on
𝕋
and uniquely determined by
\phi
.

By the holomorphic change-of-variable

w=i\frac{1-z}{1+z},\quad z=\frac{i-w}{i+w},

the unit disk
D=\{|z|<1\}
is biholomorphically mapped to
the upper half plane
\text{\ym C}_{+}
and vice versa.
Letting
\varphi(w)=i\phi(z)
, we obtain a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}

with its range controlled by
\text{Im}\,\varphi(w)\geq 0
(
w\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
), which implies
\varphi(w)\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
(
w\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
) unless
\varphi
is a real constant.

In terms of the obvious parametrization
s\in\text{\ym R}\sqcup\{\infty\}
of the boundary
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
of
\text{\ym C}_{+}

in the Riemann sphere
\overline{\text{\ym C}}=\text{\ym C}\sqcup\{\infty\}
, the integral representation of
\phi
is rephrased by

\varphi(w)=\text{Re}\,\varphi(i)+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\frac{1+sw}{s-w}\,\lambda(ds),

where
\lambda
is the measure transferred from
\mu
under the homeomorphism

s\longleftrightarrow e^{it}\quad\text{with $s$ and $t$ related by $s=\tan\frac{t}{2}$ ($-\pi<t\leq\pi)$}

and called the
boundary measure
of
\varphi
.
By separating the mass at the point
\infty
from
\lambda
, the above representation takes the form

\varphi(w)=\text{Re}\,\varphi(i)+\lambda(\{\infty\})w+\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{1+sw}{s-w}\,\lambda(ds).

Notice here that
\text{Im}\,\varphi(i)=\lambda(\overline{\text{\ym R}})=\lambda(\{\infty\})+\lambda(\text{\ym R})
.

Example 1.1
.

Let
0\not=a\in\text{\ym C}
and
b,c\in\text{\ym C}
.

(i)

A linear fractional function

\varphi(z)=\frac{a}{z+c}+b\quad(z\in\text{\ym C}_{+})

is an endofunction if and only if
\text{Im}\,b\geq 0
,
\text{Im}\,c\geq 0
and

|a|+\text{Re}\,a\leq 2(\text{Im}\,b)(\text{Im}\,c).

(ii)

An affine function
\varphi(z)=az+b
(
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
) is an endofunction if and only if

a>0
and
\text{Im}\,b\geq 0
.

Proof.

(i) As the pole
z=-c
of
\varphi
is outside of
\text{\ym C}_{+}
, we should have
\gamma=\text{Im}\,c\geq 0
.
The behaviour of
\varphi(z)
near
z=\infty
then necessitates
\beta=\text{Im}\,b\geq 0
.

When
\gamma=0
(i.e.,
c\in\text{\ym R}
), the behaviour of
\varphi(z)
near
z=-c
necessitates
a<0
which in turn implies
\varphi\in\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
.

For
\gamma>0
,

\{a/(z+c);z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}\}
is an open disk of radius
|a|/2\gamma
centered at
-ia/2\gamma
,
whence
\varphi
is an endofunction
if and only if
|a|+\text{Re}\,a\leq 2\beta\gamma
. The last condition is equivalent to
a<0
even for
\gamma=0
.

Remark that, if
\beta\gamma>0
,

|a|+\text{Re}\,a\leq 2\beta\gamma\iff|a|^{2}\leq(2\beta\gamma-\text{Re}\,a)^{2},

which is further rephrased by the positive semidefiniteness of

\text{Im}\,\varphi(x-\text{Re}\,c)=\frac{\beta x^{2}+x\text{Im}\,a-\gamma\text{Re}\,a+\beta\gamma^{2}}{x^{2}+\gamma^{2}}\quad(x\in\text{\ym R}).

When
\not=a\in\text{\ym R}
and
b,c\in\text{\ym R}
,
the positivity
\text{Im}\,\varphi(z)>0
(
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
) is equivalent to
|a|+\text{Re}\,a\leq 0
, i.e.,
a<0
,
and the expression

\varphi(z)=b+\frac{ac}{1+c^{2}}+\frac{-a}{1+c^{2}}\frac{1-cz}{-c-z}

reveals that the boundary measure of
\varphi
is given by an atomic measure
(-a)/(1+c^{2})\delta(s+c)\,ds
at
-c
.

(ii) An affine function
az+b
satisfies
\text{Im}\,(az+b)\geq 0
(
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
) if and only if
a\geq 0
and
\text{Im}\,b\geq 0
.
The boundary measure
\lambda
of
\varphi
is given by
\lambda(\{\infty\})=a
and

\displaystyle\lambda(dx)=\frac{\text{Im}\,b}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\,dx
on
ℝ

with the real constant term given by
\text{Re}\,b
.
∎

A holomorphic function
f
on

\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}=\overline{\text{\ym C}}\setminus\overline{\text{\ym R}}
is said to be real if
\overline{f(\overline{z})}=f(z)
(
z\in\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}
).
Any holomorphic function
f_{+}
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
is extended to real one
f
in a one-to-one fashion and
the
boundary support
of
f_{+}
or
f
, denoted by
[f_{+}]
or
[f]
, is defined to be the complement of

\{t\in\overline{\text{\ym R}};\text{$f$ is holomorphically extended to a neighborhood of $t$ in $\overline{\text{\ym C}}$}\}

in
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
. Notice that the boundary support is a closed subset of
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
.

By Stieltjes inversion formula and Schwarz reflection principle,
the support of the boundary measure
\lambda
of
\varphi
is exactly the boundary support of
\varphi
.

In view of the correspondence
\varphi(w)=i\phi(z)
(
z\in D
) discussed at the beginning,
we introduce the boundary support
[f]
of a holomorphic function

f
on
D
through a purely imaginary extension specified by
\overline{f(\overline{z})}=-f(1/z)
(
z\in\overline{\text{\ym C}}\setminus\text{\ym T}
):
The complement of
[f]
in
𝕋
is the set of
\zeta\in\text{\ym T}
for which
f
is holomorphically extended to a neighborhood of
\zeta
in
ℂ
.

Recall that, thanks to the maximum principle for harmonic functions,
a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
satisfies
\text{Im}\,\varphi(z)\geq 0
(
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
) if and only if the inequality holds
on a neighborhood of
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
in
\text{\ym C}_{+}
(boundary positivity).

From the definition of boundary support, a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
is holomorphically extended to

\overline{\text{\ym C}}\setminus[\varphi]
and takes real values on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}\setminus[\varphi]
by the reality of extension.
Thus, if
[\varphi]\not=\overline{\text{\ym R}}
,
\varphi
is real analytic on any open interval
I
disjoint from
[\varphi]
.

For
\varphi
fulfilling the condition
\text{Im}\,\varphi(z)\geq 0
(
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
),
the integral representation reveals that the restriction
\varphi_{I}
to
I
is an operator monotone function.
Conversely any operator monotone function
f
on an open interval
I
is analytically extended to
a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
I\sqcup(\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R})
so that
\text{Im}\,\varphi(z)\geq 0
(
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
) and

I\cap[\varphi]=\emptyset
by Löwner’s characterization.

2.
Möbius Endofunctions

Recall that an
endofunction
is a holomorphic function of
\text{\ym C}_{+}
into itself.
We denote the set of endofunctions on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
by
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
,
which forms a unital semigroup by composition and is closed under addition and
positive scalar multiplication.
Notice that
\text{\ym C}_{+}
is included in
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
as constant functions
so that
\text{\ym C}_{+}+\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})\subset\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
.

An endofunction
\varphi
is called an
autofunction

if
\varphi
maps
\text{\ym C}_{+}
onto
\text{\ym C}_{+}
bijectively.
Let
{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
be the set of autofunctions, which forms a group by composition.

For an invertible matrix
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
of complex entries, i.e.,
for
A\in\text{GL}(2,\text{\ym C})
, the associated Möbius transform
(also known as a linear fractional transform) of
z\in\overline{\text{\ym C}}
is denoted by

A.z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.

An invertible matrix
A
is called an
endomatrix
if
A.z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
(
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
) and
let
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
be the set of endomatrices, which is a subsemigroup of matrix group
\text{GL}(2,\text{\ym C})
.
When
A
is a matrix of real entries,
the condition
A\in\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
is simplified to
\det(A)>0
and

\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})\equiv\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})\cap\text{GL}(2,\text{\ym R})
is a subgroup
(the connected component) of
\text{GL}(2,\text{\ym R})
.
This can be seen in the following strengthened form:
Thanks to Schwarz’ lemma, each
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
is of the form
\varphi(z)=A.z
with
A\in\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
.

Since proportional matrices give rise to the same transform,
we may restrict these invertible matrices to the subsemigroup
\text{SL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})\equiv\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})\cap\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym C})

to obtain linear fractional endofunctions.
In particular,
{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
is the image of the double covering homomorphism
\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym R})\to{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
by Möbius transform,
which induces an obvious biaction of
\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym R})
on
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
:

(A\varphi)(z)=A.\varphi(z)=\frac{a\varphi(z)+b}{c\varphi(z)+d},\quad(\varphi A)(z)=\varphi(A.z)=\varphi\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right).

Möbius transforms of upper triangular matrices in
\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym R})
consist of affine endofunctions
az+b
(
a>0
,
b\in\text{\ym R}
)
and form a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
, which is naturally isomorphic to the semidirect product
\text{\ym R}\rtimes\text{\ym R}_{+}
.
As an affine transformation group,
\text{\ym R}\rtimes\text{\ym R}_{+}
acts on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
freely and transitively.
The left composition by affine endofunctions therefore gives rise to the left action of the affine transformation group

\text{\ym R}\rtimes\text{\ym R}_{+}
on
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
for which we can choose endofunctions satisfying
\varphi(i)=i
as representatives of orbits.
Clearly the set
K
of these representatives is a convex subset of
\hbox{\rm End}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
with its extremal points given by

\phi_{s}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}s&1\\
-1&s\end{pmatrix}.z=\frac{1+sz}{s-z}\quad(s\in\overline{\text{\ym R}}),

which will be called an
atomic endofunction
. Here
\phi_{\infty}(z)=z
by definition.
Notice that an autofunction
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
is atomic if and only if
\varphi(i)=i
.

We now go into how the integral representation of an endofunction is changed under the right action of

\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym R})
.

Consider an autofunction
A.z\in{\rm Aut}(\text{\ym C}_{+})
with
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
,
which is also denoted by
\phi_{A}
. In view of

A.i=\frac{ac+bd+i(ad-bc)}{c^{2}+d^{2}},

one sees that

A.z=\frac{ac+bd}{c^{2}+d^{2}}+\frac{ad-bc}{c^{2}+d^{2}}\phi_{s}(z),\quad s=-\frac{d}{c}

for
c\not=0
and
A.z=(ax+b)/d
for
c=0
.
By replacing
A
with
\begin{pmatrix}s&1\\
-1&s\end{pmatrix}A
, we have the following expression for the autofunction

\phi_{s}A
:

\displaystyle(\phi_{s}A)(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{pmatrix}s&1\\
-1&s\end{pmatrix}A\right).z=\frac{(as+c)z+bs+d}{(-a+cs)z-b+ds}

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,(\phi_{s}A)(i)+\frac{(ad-bc)(1+s^{2})}{(-a+cs)^{2}+(-b+ds)^{2}}\,\phi_{t}(z),

where

t=\frac{b-ds}{cs-a}=\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\
-c&a\end{pmatrix}.s\iff s=A.t

and

\text{Re}\,(\phi_{s}A)(i)=\frac{(ac+bd)(s^{2}-1)+(c^{2}+d^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2})s}{(-a+cs)^{2}+(-b+ds)^{2}}.

For an endofunction
\varphi(z)
of
z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
with the boundary measure
\lambda
,

\displaystyle\varphi(A.z)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\varphi(i)+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\phi_{s}(A.z)\,\lambda(ds)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\varphi(i)+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\text{Re}\,(\phi_{s}A)(i)\,\lambda(ds)

\displaystyle\quad+(ad-bc)\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\frac{1+s^{2}}{(-a+cs)^{2}+(-b+ds)^{2}}\,\phi_{t}(z)\,\lambda(ds),

whence, by the identity

(-a+cs)^{2}+(-b+ds)^{2}=(ad-bc)^{2}\frac{1+t^{2}}{(ct+d)^{2}},

we have

\varphi(A.z)=\text{Re}\,\varphi(i)+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\text{Re}\,(\phi_{s}A)(i)\,\lambda(ds)+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\phi_{t}(z)\,\lambda^{A}(dt)

with the measure
\lambda^{A}
on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}=\text{\ym R}\sqcup\{\infty\}
defined by

\lambda^{A}(dt)=\frac{1+(A.t)^{2}}{1+t^{2}}\frac{(ct+d)^{2}}{ad-bc}\,\lambda(A.dt),

where
\lambda(A.dt)
denotes the transferred measure of
\lambda(ds)
under the change of the variable
s=A.t
.
Notice that

\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{ad-bc}{(ct+d)^{2}},\quad\frac{dt}{ds}=\frac{ad-bc}{(a-cs)^{2}}.

Summarizing the discussions so far,

Theorem 2.1
.

For
A\in\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym R})
and an endofunction
\varphi
on
\text{\ym C}_{+}
with its boundary measure
\lambda
on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
,
we have

\varphi(A.z)=\text{Re}\,\varphi(A.i)+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\frac{1+tz}{t-z}\,\lambda^{A}(dt)\quad(z\in\text{\ym C}_{+}).

Remark 1
.

For
f\in C(\overline{\text{\ym R}})
,

\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}f(t)\,\lambda^{A}(dt)=\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}f(A^{-1}.s)\frac{1+s^{2}}{1+(A^{-1}.s)^{2}}\frac{ad-bc}{(a-cs)^{2}}\,\lambda(ds).

Matrices for the Möbius transform are now relaxed to
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
.
To this end, we need a closer look into the semigroup
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
.
Though
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
biacts on
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
as a subgroup,
the left and right actions are not symmetrical due to the non-group character of
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
,
which can be witnessed by looking at the geometric position of the boundary circline (circle or line)

M.\overline{\text{\ym R}}
of
M\in\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
:
A right translate of
M
leaves
M.\overline{\text{\ym R}}
unchanged, whereas the left action moves it around.

For
M\in\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
with
M.\overline{\text{\ym R}}\subset\overline{\text{\ym R}}\sqcup\text{\ym C}_{+}
a circle of radius
r
centered at
c\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
,
the contact degree of
M
is defined to be
\kappa(M)=r/\text{Im}\,c
. For
M
with
M.\overline{\text{\ym R}}
parallel to
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
as an extended line, we set
\kappa(M)=1
.
Note that
\kappa(M)=1
if and only if
M.\overline{\text{\ym R}}
is a circline touching at one point in
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
.
Thus
\kappa
is a function defined on
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})\setminus\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
taking values in
(0,1]
,
which turns out to be a complete invariant for orbits of the biaction of
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})

on the difference set
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})\setminus\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
.

In fact, from the definition,
\kappa
is invariant under the right action of
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
.
The left invariance
\kappa(AM)=\kappa(M)
is clear for an upper triangular
A\in\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
.
By the inversion matrix

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
, the circle
|z-c|=r
is Möbius transformed to a circle of radius
r/(|c|^{2}-r^{2})
centered at

-\overline{c}/(|c|^{2}-r^{2})
, whence
\kappa(JM)=\kappa(M)
.
Since
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
is generated by upper triangle matrices and
J
,
\kappa
is invariant under the left action
of
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
as well.

To see the completeness of the invariant, let
M,M^{\prime}\in\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
satisfy
\kappa(M)=\kappa(M^{\prime})\equiv\kappa
.
If
0<\kappa<1
, by left translates of
M
and
M^{\prime}
by upper triangular matrices,
we may assume that both boundary circles have purely imaginary centers
i\gamma
and
i\gamma^{\prime}
with radii
r
and
r^{\prime}
respectively.
Then
\kappa=r/\gamma=r^{\prime}/\gamma^{\prime}
gives rise to a common ratio
r^{\prime}/r=\gamma^{\prime}/\gamma\equiv\rho>0
and

M^{\prime\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}M
satisfies

M^{\prime}.\overline{\text{\ym R}}=\rho(M.\overline{\text{\ym R}})=M^{\prime\prime}.\overline{\text{\ym R}}\iff M.\text{\ym C}_{+}=M^{\prime\prime}.\text{\ym C}_{+}.

Consequently,
M^{-1}M^{\prime\prime}\in\text{GL}(2,\text{\ym C})
gives rise to an autofunction of
\text{\ym C}_{+}
and hence belongs to
\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
.

There remains the case
\kappa=1
.
If
M.\overline{\text{\ym R}}
is a circle touching
ℝ
at a real point, we may assume that it touches at
0

after a real affine transform
az+b
(
a>0
,
b\in\text{\ym R}
).
If
M.\overline{\text{\ym R}}
is an extended line parallel to
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
,

(JM).\overline{\text{\ym R}}
is a circle touching
ℝ
at
0
.
Thus we may assume that both boundary circles are touching
ℝ
at
0
with their centers
i\gamma
and
i\gamma^{\prime}
respectively.
By choosing
\rho=\gamma^{\prime}/\gamma
this time, the argument in the case
0<\kappa<1
works again to conclude that

M^{-1}M^{\prime}\in\text{GL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym R})
.

Remark 2
.

\text{SL}_{+}(2,\text{\ym C})
is generated by
\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym R})
and a purely imaginary shift

\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
, whereas
\text{SL}(2,\text{\ym R})
is generated by dilations
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
(
0\not=a\in\text{\ym R}
),
shifts
\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\

\varphi
is given by

\lambda_{0}(ds)+\frac{c+\text{Im}\,\psi(s)}{\pi(1+s^{2})}\,ds.

Appendix A

To extract more information on point masses of
\lambda
,
we introduce the Stoltz sector of apex
0
and aperture
0<\phi<\pi/2
by

V_{\phi}=\{x+iy\in\text{\ym C};|x|<y\tan\phi\},

which is an open convex cone in
\text{\ym C}_{+}
and has a spindle shape in
\overline{\text{\ym C}}
.
Given
\alpha\in\text{\ym R}
and a signed measure
\lambda
on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
, let
\varphi
be a holomorphic function on
\text{\ym C}\setminus\text{\ym R}

defined by

\varphi(z)=\alpha+\int_{\overline{\text{\ym R}}}\frac{1+sz}{s-z}\,\lambda(ds)=\alpha+\lambda(\{\infty\})z+\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{1+sz}{s-z}\,\lambda(ds).

Notice that
\varphi(i)=\alpha+i\lambda(\overline{\text{\ym R}})
.

Proposition A.1
.

Given
b>0
and
c\in\text{\ym R}
, both

\{\varphi(x+iy)/y;x+iy\in V_{\phi},y\geq b\}

and

\{y\varphi(x+iy);x+iy\in c+V_{\phi},0<y\leq b\}

are bounded sets and we have

\lim_{V_{\phi}\ni x+iy\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(x+iy)-\lambda(\{\infty\})(x+iy)}{y}=0,

\lim_{c+V_{\phi}\ni x+iy\to c}y\left(\varphi(x+iy)+\lambda(\{c\})\frac{1+c^{2}}{x+iy-c}\right)=0.

Proof.

In the expression

\frac{\text{Re}\,\varphi(x+iy)-\lambda(\{\infty\})x}{y}=\frac{\text{Re}\,\varphi(i)}{y}+\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{(s-x)(1+sx)-sy^{2}}{y((s-x)^{2}+y^{2})}\,\lambda(ds),

we regard the integrand as a function of
(s,y)
with
x
a parameter and rewrite it by the polar coordinates

s-x=\rho\cos\theta
,
y=\rho\sin\theta
(
\rho>0
,
0<\theta<\pi
) to have an expression

\frac{1+x^{2}-y^{2}}{\rho y}\cos\theta+\frac{x}{y}\cos(2\theta)

with its modulus estimated by

\frac{|1+x^{2}-y^{2}|}{\rho y}+\frac{|x|}{y}\leq 1+\frac{1+x^{2}}{y^{2}}+\frac{|x|}{y}\leq 1+\frac{1}{b^{2}}+\tan\phi+\tan^{2}\phi

for
x+iy\in V_{\phi}
fulfilling
y\geq b
.

The bounded convergence theorem is now applied to see that, for a sequence
(x_{n}+iy_{n})
in
V_{\phi}
satisfying

y_{n}\to\infty
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{(s-x_{n})(1+sx_{n})-sy_{n}^{2}}{y_{n}((s-x_{n})^{2}+y_{n}^{2})}\,\lambda(ds)=\int_{\text{\ym R}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(s-x_{n})(1+sx_{n})-sy_{n}^{2}}{y_{n}((s-x_{n})^{2}+y_{n}^{2})}\,\lambda(ds)=0,

which implies that, when
y>0
is large,
y^{2}\leq x^{2}+y^{2}\leq y^{2}/\cos^{2}\phi
is large as well for
x+iy\in V_{\phi}

and we can make

\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{(s-x)(1+sx)-sy^{2}}{y((s-x)^{2}+y^{2})}\,\lambda(ds)

arbitrarily small.

As to the imaginary part, in the expression

\frac{\text{Im}\,\varphi(x+iy)-\lambda(\{\infty\})y}{y}=\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{1+s^{2}}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\,\lambda(ds),

its modulus is estimated by

\left|\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{1+s^{2}}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\,\lambda(ds)\right|\leq\int_{\text{\ym R}}\frac{1+s^{2}}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\,|\lambda|(ds).

Since the range of the last integrand

\displaystyle r=\frac{1+s^{2}}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\iff r((s-x)^{2}+y^{2})=1+s^{2}

is ruled by

r^{2}y^{2}-r(x^{2}+y^{2}+1)+1\leq 0
,
we obtain an estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\text{\ym R}}\frac{1+s^{2}}{(s-x)^{2}+y^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}+1}{2y^{2}}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}+1}{2y^{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{y^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}+1}{y^{2}}=1+\frac{1+x^{2}}{y^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{1}{b^{2}}+\tan^{2}\phi.

Again the bounded convergence theorem is applied to have

\lim_{V_{\phi}\ni x+iy\to\infty}\frac{\text{Im}\,\varphi(x+iy)-\lambda(\{\infty\})y}{y}=0.

By combining these, we obtain the first convergence.

To show the second convergence, introduce a new variable
w\in\text{\ym C}_{+}
by

w=-1/(z-c)\iff z=c-1/w
. Then
z\in c+V_{\phi}\iff w\in V_{\phi}
and
the condition
|z-c|\leq b
is equivalent to
|w|\geq 1/b
.
Let
\psi(w)=\varphi(-1/(z-c))
and
\mu
be the boundary measure of
\psi
.
Under the change-of-variable
s=c-1/t
on
\overline{\text{\ym R}}
, the transformation formula shows

\mu(dt)=\frac{t^{2}+(ct-1)^{2}}{1+t^{2}}\lambda(ds)

and hence
\mu(\{\infty\})=(1+c^{2})\lambda(\{c\})
. Thus the target of the first convergence for
\psi
takes the form

\frac{\psi(u+iv)-\mu(\{\infty\})(u+iv)}{v}=\frac{1}{vy}y\left(\varphi(x+iy)+\lambda(\{c\})\frac{1+c^{2}}{x+iy-c}\right).

Since
vy=y^{2}/((x-c)^{2}+y^{2})
satisfies
\cos^{2}\phi\leq vy\leq 1
, the first convergence for
\psi
implies the second convergence
for
\varphi
and we are done.
∎

Corollary A.2
.

We have the following expressions for atomic masses

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{\varphi(iy)}{y}=i\lambda(\{\infty\})

and

\lim_{y\to+0}y\varphi(c+iy)=i(1+c^{2})\lambda(\{c\}).

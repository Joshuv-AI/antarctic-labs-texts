In search of the Giant Convex Quadrilateral hidden in the Mountains
Nandana Ghosh, Rakesh Gupta, Ankush Acharyya

Abstract
A 1.5 1.5 D terrain is a simple polygon bounded by a line segment ℓ \ell and a polygonal chain monotone with respect to the line segment ℓ \ell . Usually, ℓ \ell is chosen aligned to the x x -axis, and is called the base of the terrain. In this paper, we consider the problem of finding a convex quadrilateral of maximum area inside a 1.5 1.5 D terrain in I ​ R 2 I\!\!R^{2} . We present an O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n^{2}\log n) time algorithm for this problem, where n n is the number of vertices of the terrain. Finally, we show that the maximum area axis-parallel rectangle inside the terrain yields a 1 2 \frac{1}{2} factor approximation result to the maximum area convex quadrilateral problem.

Introduction

Given a complex geometric shape
P

P
is the convex hull of the vertices of
P

P
, we are often interested in finding a convex body
Q
of maximum size (area, perimeter, etc.). These problems of the second type are known as the
shape inclusion problem
in the literature
[
6
,
13
]

There has been a fair amount of work for the largest shape inclusion problem, depending on the shapes of
P
and
Q
. The problem of finding the largest convex object
Q
within a simple polygon, or any other non-convex shape

is known as
potato-peeling
problem in the literature. The problem was first formally introduced by Jacob E. Goodman
[
13
]
who proposed several interesting properties to find such a “potato" for a restricted version of the problem, where the simple polygon to be peeled has

sides. The general version of the problem, for any

[
6
]
. They proposed an
O(n^{7})
time algorithm using
O(n^{5})
space, which is the best known algorithm for the problem, to the best of our knowledge. For the longest line segment inside a simple polygon problem, Hall-Holt et al.
[
14
]
proposed a
\frac{1}{2}
factor approximation algorithm in
O(n\log n)
time and a PTAS in
O(n\log^{2}n)
time, which computes a
(1-\epsilon)
approximation for any fixed
\epsilon>0
. They also proposed an
O(1)
factor approximation algorithm in
O(n\log n)
time to compute the largest triangle and a
(1-\epsilon)
approximation to compute the largest
fat triangle

are denoted by

. The length of a line segment

is denoted by
|\overline{ab}|
.

By slightly violating the notation, we sometimes refer to the area of any quadrilateral, triangle, or rectangle by

If a line is drawn through the midpoint

Parabolic PDEs on a fixed domain with evolving subdomains: function spaces and well-posedness
Van Chien Le a and Karel Van Bockstal b

Abstract
This paper develops the necessary ingredients for the variational approach of initial boundary-value problems of parabolic partial differential equations on a fixed spatial domain containing evolving subdomains. In particular, we introduce function spaces for the variational solution that extend standard Sobolev–Bochner spaces to account for a coefficient associated with the time derivative that may be discontinuous across the evolving interface. We further show the density of smooth functions in these spaces by extending the mollification technique and the Reynolds transport theorem, and establish the corresponding “embedding” theory and an integration by parts formula. Finally, we prove the well-posedness of the space-time variational formulation in the natural setting using the Banach–Nečas–Babuška theorem.

When
\alpha
is discontinuous across the evolving interface, the main challenge lies in rigorously defining
\alpha\partial_{t}u
as element in a Bochner space taking values in the dual space
\Vs^{\prime}
, such that the corresponding Sobolev–Bochner space for the solution possesses the necessary properties for the variational analysis to be applicable. These properties include an integration by parts formula and a continuous embedding theory by which the initial value of

[
6
]
, the authors introduced an analogue of the space
\Ws^{1,2,2}(J,\Vs,\Vs^{\prime})

\partial_{t}u
is replaced by the weak material derivative
\alpha\dot{u}:=\alpha\partial_{t}u+\alpha\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla u
. The definition of
\alpha\dot{u}
in the distributional sense naturally generalizes that of
\partial_{t}u
by taking into account the jump of
\alpha
across the evolving interface. Nonetheless, the authors encountered a fundamental difficulty: the lack of density of smooth functions in this space, which prevented them from developing the necessary tools for the variational analysis, see
[
6

. This issue was then addressed in
[
9
]
, where the authors defined
\alpha\partial_{t}u\in\Ls^{1}(J,\Vs^{\prime})
, with
\Vs=\Hs^{1}(\Omega)
, by requiring the absolute continuity in time of the
\Ls^{2}(\Omega)
-inner product between
\alpha u
and
w
, for any
w\in\Hs^{1}(\Omega)
. However, this is not a natural approach to define
\alpha\partial_{t}u
.

In this paper, we define
\alpha\partial_{t}u
as a distribution taking values in
\Vs^{\prime}
by generalizing the notion of the weak time derivative
\partial_{t}u
and incorporating the evolution of the subdomains and interface via the Reynolds transport theorem. Under suitable conditions, this definition is shown to be equivalent to
\alpha\dot{u}
introduced in
[
6
]
, and to the definition of
\alpha\partial_{t}u
proposed in
[
9
]
. To support this construction, we establish the density of smooth functions in the associated Sobolev–Bochner space for the weak solution by extending the classical mollification technique. Building on this density property, we develop the corresponding “embedding” theory and an integration by parts formula, which are essential in deriving the variational formulation of (
1
). The well-posedness of the variational problem is then proven using the standard Banach–Nečas–Babuška framework.

We also mention an alternative approach to solving (
1
), which interprets the equation as being defined on two evolving subdomains
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
\Omega_{2}(t)
, with transmission or jump conditions imposed at the shared interface
\Gamma(t)
. Function spaces and finite element frameworks for parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs) on moving domains have been developed in the literature, which rely on the pushforward and pullback mappings
\Phi(\cdot,t)
and
\Phi(\cdot,t)^{-1}
to define the evolving function spaces. The interested reader is referred to
[
20

21
,
22
,
23
]
for more details. However, these evolving function spaces do not preserve the tensor product structure of the space-time domain
Q

1

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we introduce a generalization of the standard Sobolev–Bochner spaces for the weak solution of (

C_{\boldsymbol{v}}:=\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\rVert_{\Cs^{1}\left({\overline{Q}}\right)^{d}},\qquad\qquad\ C_{\alpha}:=\max\left\{{\left\lVert{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\Cs^{1}\left({\overline{Q_{1}}}\right)},\left\lVert{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\Cs^{1}\left({\overline{Q_{2}}}\right)}}\right\}.

We recall here the Reynolds transport theorem, which is useful for handling time derivatives of integrals over evolving domains. Let

\widetilde{\Omega}(t):=\Omega_{1}(t)\cup\Omega_{2}(t),\qquad\qquad\widetilde{Q}:=Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}.

Given

[
24
, p. 78]
states that

\dfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int\limits_{\widetilde{\Omega}(t)}f\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=\int\limits_{\widetilde{\Omega}(t)}\partial_{t}f\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}+\int\limits_{\partial\widetilde{\Omega}(t)}f\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\textbf{n}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int\limits_{\widetilde{\Omega}(t)}\partial_{t}f\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}+\int\limits_{\widetilde{\Omega}(t)}\nabla\cdot{(f\boldsymbol{v})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

(2)

for a.a.
t\in J
, where

is the outward unit normal of
\partial\widetilde{\Omega}(t)
.

\alpha\partial_{t}u

Definition 2.1

Given

\int\limits_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t=\dfrac{c^{p}}{\varepsilon^{p}}\int\limits_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\exp\left({\frac{-pt^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}-t^{2}}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 2\dfrac{c^{p}}{\varepsilon^{p-1}}=:\frac{C_{1}^{p}}{(2\varepsilon)^{p-1}},

we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|{I_{1}-\left\langle{\alpha\partial_{t}u,z}\right\rangle}\right|^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C_{1}^{p^{\prime}}}{2\varepsilon}\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{p^{\prime}}_{\Ls^{p}(J,\Vs)}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\varepsilon}^{\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)+\varepsilon}\left\lVert{(\alpha\partial_{t}u)(t+h)-(\alpha\partial_{t}u)(t)}\right\rVert_{\Vs^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}h\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{p^{\prime}}\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{p^{\prime}}_{\Ls^{p}(J,\Vs)}\sup_{\left|{h}\right|\leq 2\varepsilon}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\left\lVert{(\alpha\partial_{t}u)(t+h)-(\alpha\partial_{t}u)(t)}\right\rVert_{\Vs^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Next, integrating by parts with respect to

, we rewrite

as

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\rho_{\varepsilon}\left({t+\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)-s}\right)\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot{\left({\alpha(s)\,u(s)\,z(t)\,\boldsymbol{v}(s)}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\rho_{\varepsilon}\left({t+\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)-s}\right)\dfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\int\limits_{s}^{t}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot{(\alpha(\tau)\,u(\tau)\,z(t)\,\boldsymbol{v}(\tau))}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\left({t+\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)-s}\right)\int\limits_{s}^{t}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot{(\alpha(\tau)\,u(\tau)\,z(t)\,\boldsymbol{v}(\tau))}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t,

which allows to deduce the following

\displaystyle\left|{I_{3}+I_{4}^{b}}\right|^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\,\,\,=\left|{\,\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\left({t+\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)-s}\right)\int\limits_{s}^{t}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot{\left[\alpha(\tau)\left({u(s)-u(\tau)}\right)z(t)\,\boldsymbol{v}(\tau)\right]}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\leq C_{2}\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{p^{\prime}}_{\Ls^{p}(J,\Vs)}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\left({\,\int\limits_{\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\varepsilon}^{\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)+\varepsilon}\left|{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\left({h-\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)}\right)}\right|\left|{\int\limits_{0}^{h}\left\lVert{u(t+h)-u(t+\eta)}\right\rVert_{\Vs}\,\mathrm{d}\eta}\right|\,\mathrm{d}h}\right)^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\,\,\,\leq C_{2}\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert^{p^{\prime}}_{\Ls^{p}(J,\Vs)}\left|{\,\int\limits_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\left|{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(h)}\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}h}\right|^{p^{\prime}/p}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\varepsilon}^{\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)+\varepsilon}\,\left|{\int\limits_{0}^{h}\left\lVert{u(t+h)-u(t+\eta)}\right\rVert_{\Vs}\,\mathrm{d}\eta}\right|^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}h\,\mathrm{d}t,

where

\int\limits_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\left|{\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)}\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t=(2c\varepsilon)^{p}\int\limits_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\frac{\left|{t}\right|^{p}\exp\left({pt^{2}/\left({t^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)}\right)}{\left({\varepsilon^{2}-t^{2}}\right)^{2p}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\dfrac{2^{p+1}c^{p}C_{p}}{\varepsilon^{2p-1}}=\dfrac{8^{p}c^{p}C_{p}}{(2\varepsilon)^{2p-1}},

where

and
C_{4}:=C_{3}^{1/p^{\prime}}T^{(p-2)/p}
. Analogously, there exists a constant

independent of

The last term is clearly bounded by

Therefore, we end up with

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\alpha\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}-\alpha\partial_{t}u}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{p^{\prime}}(J,\Vs^{\prime})}\leq C_{1}\sup_{\left|{h}\right|\leq 2\varepsilon}\left({\int\limits_{0}^{T}\left\lVert{(\alpha\partial_{t}u)(t+h)-(\alpha\partial_{t}u)(t)}\right\rVert_{\Vs^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

Taking into limit

and invoking the mean continuity of

and

as elements in

and
\Ls^{p^{\prime}}(J,\Vs^{\prime})
, respectively, we arrive at (
4

Lemma 3.1

4
Conclusions

In this paper, we have developed a functional framework for the variational approach of parabolic PDEs on a fixed domain comprising evolving subdomains and interfaces. The function spaces introduced naturally generalize standard Sobolev–Bochner spaces, accommodating a material coefficient that may be discontinuous across the evolving interface and partially degenerate in subdomains. We have established key properties of these spaces, including the density of smooth functions, an appropriate “embedding” theory, and an integration by parts formula, which are essential in deriving the variational formulation. Finally, we have demonstrated the well-posedness of the space-time variational problem in this natural setting using standard arguments.

The result presented in this work provides a foundation for developing numerical methods for parabolic PDEs with evolving subdomains. Potential directions include classical time-stepping (Rothe’s method) finite element schemes
[
28
,
12
]
, high-order time-stepping and finite element methods
[
15
,
29
]
, discontinuous Galerkin approaches
[
30
]
, and space–time finite element methods
[
31
,
19
]
. A detailed investigation of these numerical schemes will be the subject of future work.

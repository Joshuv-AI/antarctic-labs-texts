1.
Introduction

The following is one of the well-known Hardy–Littlewood conjectures.

Conjecture 1.1

(Hardy–Littlewood
[
2
]
)
.

Every sufficiently large positive integer
N
is a square or the sum of a prime and a square. In other words,

N=n^{2}\quad\mbox{or}\quad p+n^{2},

where
p
denotes a prime number and
n
denotes a positive integer.

To approach to this conjecture, we consider the following function:

R_{\textup{HL}}(N)\coloneqq\sum_{p+n^{2}=N}\log p.

We call such a function a representation function.

In
[
2
]
, G. H. Hardy–J. E. Littlewood also conjectured the asymptotic formula:

R_{\textup{HL}}(N)\sim\mathfrak{S}(N)\sqrt{N},\quad\textup{($N$: not square)},

where

\mathfrak{S}(N)\coloneqq\prod_{p>2}\bigg(1-\frac{(N/p)}{p-1}\bigg),\quad(N/p)\textup{: Legendre symbol}.

In 2017, A. Languasco and A. Zaccanini considered the short interval average of the representation function:

\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}R_{\textup{HL}}(N)

for real numbers
X
,
H
with
4\leq H\leq X
.
Let

C\coloneqq\exp\bigg(-c\bigg(\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}\bigg),

where
c
is some small positive constant which may depend on
\varepsilon
.

Theorem 1.2

(Languasco–Zaccagnini
[
6
]
)
.

For real numbers
X
,
H
with
4\leq H\leq X
and any real number
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(1)

\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}R_{\textup{HL}}(N)=HX^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1})

provided

X^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1}\leq H\leq X^{1-\varepsilon},

where the implicit constant depend only on
\varepsilon
.

If we obtain (
1
) for
H=1
in Theorem
1.2
, Conjecture
1.1
is true.
Therefore, our goal is an improvement of the condition of
H
.

In 2023, Y. Suzuki improved the range of
H
in Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3

(Suzuki
[
9
]
)
.

The asymptotic formula (
1
)
provided

X^{\frac{32-4\sqrt{15}}{49}+\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}.

In this article, we study the following function that replaces squares in
R_{\textup{HL}}(N)=\sum_{p+n^{2}=N}\log p
with square-full numbers:

R(N)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+f=N\\
f\in\mathscr{Q}\end{subarray}}\log p,

in which
\mathscr{Q}\coloneqq\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,|\,n\mbox{ is square-full numbers}\}
.
Recall that a square-full number is a positive integer
n
such that if
p
is a prime dividing
n
, then
p^{2}
divides
n
.

The author and Y. Suzuki obtained the following asymptotic formula.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
8
]
)
.

For
A\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}
, we have

(2)

\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}R(N)=\frac{\zeta(\frac{3}{2})}{\zeta(3)}HX^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(1+O\big((\log X)^{-A}\big)\Big),

where the implicit constant depends on
A,\varepsilon
.

Our main theorem is the following improvement of the above range of
H
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we have the asymptotic formula (
2
) provided

X^{\frac{32-4\sqrt{15}}{49}+\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}.

For a real number
B\geq 2

\widetilde{R}_{B}(N):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+a^{2}b^{3}=N\\
b\leq B\end{subarray}}(\log p)\mu(b)^{2},

Lemma 2.1

(
[
8
]
)

For a positive number

R(N)=\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)+O((N^{\frac{1}{2}}\log N)B^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

By Lemma

\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}R(N)=\sum_{X\leq N\leq X+H}\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)+O\Bigg(\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}(N^{\frac{1}{2}}\log N)B^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg).

If
B=(\log X)^{4A}
for

, we have

(3)

\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}R(N)=\sum_{X\leq N\leq X+H}\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)+O(HX^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{-A}).

We consider
the sum of
\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)

on the right-hand side.

Let
\mathscr{Q}_{B}\coloneqq\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,|\,n=a^{2}b^{3},b\leq B\mbox{ and }b\mbox{ is square-free}\}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<p+f\leq X+H\\
f\in\mathscr{Q}_{B}\end{subarray}}\log p.

To evaluate the short interval sum with square-full numbers, we introduce some lemmas.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
B
be a real number greater than or equal to
1
.
For real numbers
X
,
H
with
4\leq H\leq X
and

, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{2}\leq f<(n+1)^{2}\\
f\leq X+H\\

Lemma 2.3
.

For real numbers
x\leq 1
and
s>1
, we have

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n^{2}}=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(2s)}+O(x^{-s+1}),

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function.

Proof.

We can write

(4)

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n^{2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n^{2}}+\sum_{n>x}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n^{2}}.

From Euler Product Formula for the zeta function, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(s)\prod_{p:prime}\bigg(1+\frac{\mu(p)^{2}}{p^{s}}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\zeta(s)\prod_{p:prime}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\bigg)=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(2s)}.

The second term on the right-hand side
of (
4
) is

\sum_{n>x}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n^{2}}\leq\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u^{s}}du\ll x^{-s+1}.

Substituting these formulas into (
4
) leads to the lemma.
∎

We first estimate the sum of the square-full numbers in the short intervals.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
B
be a real number greater than or equal to

For real numbers

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<f\leq X+H\\

Proof.

We write the left-hand side as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<f\leq X+H\\
f\in\mathscr{Q}_{B}\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{X<n^{2}\leq X+H}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{2}\leq f<(n+1)^{2}\\
f\leq X+H\\
f\in\mathscr{Q}_{B}\end{subarray}}1.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<f\leq X+H\\
f\in\mathscr{Q}_{B}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll B\sum_{X<n^{2}\leq X+H}1

\displaystyle\leq B((X+H)^{\frac{1}{2}}-X^{\frac{1}{2}})+1

\displaystyle=B\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}^{X+H}u^{-\frac{1}{2}}du+1\bigg)\leq HX^{-\frac{1}{2}}B+B.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

For real numbers
X
,
H
,
\varepsilon
with
4\leq H\leq X
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{X\leq N\leq X+H}\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<m+f\leq X+H\\

Proof.

By the definition of
\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)

\sum_{X\leq N\leq X+H}\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<f\leq X+H\\

From

and Lemma

,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<f\leq X+H\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<p+f\leq X+H\\

We odtain the lemma.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-b^{3}\int_{0}^{\frac{X}{b^{3}}-\frac{1}{b^{3}}}(Q-b^{3}u)^{\rho-1}\bigg(\{u^{\frac{1}{2}}\}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)du

\displaystyle-b^{3}\int_{\frac{X}{b^{3}}-\frac{1}{b^{3}}}^{\frac{X}{b^{3}}}(Q-b^{3}u)^{\rho-1}\bigg(\{u^{\frac{1}{2}}\}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)du-\frac{Q^{\rho}}{2\rho}+O\bigg(\frac{HX^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-1}C^{-2}}{|\gamma|}\bigg).

The second integral of most-right-hand side of above is

-b^{3}\int_{\frac{X}{b^{3}}-\frac{1}{b^{3}}}^{\frac{X}{b^{3}}}(Q-b^{3}u)^{\rho-1}\bigg(\{u^{\frac{1}{2}}\}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)du\ll\frac{1}{\beta}.

From Lemma
2.7
, we have
\frac{1}{\beta}\ll\log X
.
Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{0}^{\frac{X}{b^{3}}}(Q-b^{3}u)^{\rho}d\bigg(\{u^{\frac{1}{2}}\}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-b^{3}\int_{0}^{\frac{X}{b^{3}}-\frac{1}{b^{3}}}(Q-b^{3}u)^{\rho-1}\bigg(\{u^{\frac{1}{2}}\}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)du-\frac{Q^{\rho}}{2\rho}+O\bigg(\frac{HX^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-1}C^{-2}}{|\gamma|}+\log X\bigg).

By the Fourier expansion:

\{u\}-\frac{1}{2}=-\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{e(nu)}{2\pi in},

we have

\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{0}^{\frac{X}{b^{3}}}(Q-b^{3}u)^{\rho}d\bigg(\{u^{\frac{1}{2}}\}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)=-\frac{Q^{\rho}}{2\rho}+b^{3}R_{\rho}(Q)+O\bigg(\frac{HX^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-1}C^{-2}}{|\gamma|}+\log X\bigg),

where

I_{\rho}(Q,n)\coloneqq I_{\rho,k,l}(Q,n)=\int_{0}^{\frac{X}{b^{3}}-\frac{1}{b^{3}}}(Q-b^{3}u)^{\rho-1}e(nu^{\frac{1}{2}})du,

R_{\rho}(Q)\coloneqq R_{\rho,k,l}(Q)=\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{I_{\rho}(Q,n)}{2\pi in}.

We estimate
I_{\rho}(X+H,n)-I_{\rho}(X,n)
to estimate
R_{\rho}(X+H)-R_{\rho}(X)
.
Changing the variables gives

\displaystyle I_{\rho}(X+H,n)=b^{-3}\int_{1}^{X}(u+H)^{\rho-1}e(b^{-\frac{3}{2}}n(X-u)^{\frac{1}{2}})du,

\displaystyle I_{\rho}(X,n)=b^{-3}\int_{1}^{X}u^{\rho-1}e(b^{-\frac{3}{2}}n(X-u)^{\frac{1}{2}})du.

Let
U=\min(4H|\gamma|,X)
.
We write

I_{\rho}(X+H,n)-I_{\rho}(X,n)=b^{-3}(I+I_{1}+I_{2}),

where

\displaystyle I\coloneqq\int_{U}^{X}((u+H)^{\rho-1}-u^{\rho-1})e(b^{-\frac{3}{2}}n(X-u)^{\frac{1}{2}})du,

\displaystyle I_{1}\coloneqq\int_{1}^{U}(u+H)^{\rho-1}e(b^{-\frac{3}{2}}n(X-u)^{\frac{1}{2}})du,

\displaystyle I_{2}\coloneqq\int_{1}^{U}u^{\rho-1}e(b^{-\frac{3}{2}}n(X-u)^{\frac{1}{2}})du.

For
I
, by the Taylor expansion:

(u+H)^{\rho-1}=u^{\rho-1}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\binom{\rho-1}{\nu}H^{\nu},

therefore we have

I\ll\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\binom{\rho-1}{\nu}H^{\nu}\int_{U}^{X}u^{\rho-\nu-1}e\bigg(n(X-u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)du.

In the case of
4H|\gamma|\leq X
, by using Lemma
3.4
and
U=4H|\gamma|
,

I\ll\frac{U^{\beta}\log X}{|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\bigg|\binom{\rho-1}{\nu}\bigg|\bigg(\frac{H}{U}\bigg)^{\nu}\ll\frac{U^{\beta}\log X}{|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\prod_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\frac{|\gamma|+2\mu}{4\mu|\gamma|}\ll\frac{U^{\beta}\log X}{|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

In the case of
4H|\gamma|\geq X
,
I
is an empty integral, then we have the same estimate.

For
I_{1}
by chang- ing the variable and Lemma
3.4
,

I_{1}\ll\int_{1+H}^{U+H}u^{\beta+i\gamma-1}\exp(n(X+H-u)^{\frac{1}{2}})du\ll\frac{U^{\beta}\log X}{|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Similary,

I_{2}\ll\frac{U^{\beta}\log X}{|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Then, we have

I_{\rho}(X+H,n)-I_{\rho}(X,n)=b^{-3}\frac{U^{\beta}\log X}{|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\ll b^{-3}H^{\beta}|\gamma|^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\log X.

If
|n|>2(X+H)^{\frac{1}{2}}|\gamma|
, from Lemma
3.4
,

I_{\rho}(X+H,n)-I_{\rho}(X,n)\ll\frac{X^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{|n|}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle R_{\rho}(X+H)-R_{\rho}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq 2(X+H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\gamma|}\hskip-10.00002pt\frac{I_{\rho}(X+H,n)-I_{\rho}(X,n)}{2\pi in}+\sum_{n>2(X+H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\gamma|}\hskip-10.00002pt\frac{I_{\rho}(X+H,n)-I_{\rho}(X,n)}{2\pi in}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle b^{-3}\bigg(H^{\beta}|\gamma|^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\log X\sum_{n\leq 2(X+H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\gamma|}\frac{1}{n}+X^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n>2(X+H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\gamma|}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle b^{-3}(H^{\beta}|\gamma|^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{2}+1).

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle S_{\rho}(X+H)-S_{\rho}(X)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{b\leq B}\mu(b)^{2}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{3}{2})}b^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big((X+H)^{\rho+\frac{1}{2}}-X^{\rho+\frac{1}{2}}\big)+\frac{(X+H)^{\rho}-X^{\rho}}{2\rho}

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt+b^{-3}(H^{\beta}|\gamma|^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{2})+\frac{HX^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-1}C^{-2}}{|\gamma|}+\log X\bigg)

Lemma
2.3
leads to the lemma.
∎

We finally prove Theorem
1.5
.
From Lemmas
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\leq X\\
f\in\mathscr{Q}_{B}\end{subarray}}\bigg(\psi(X+H-f)-\psi(X-f)\bigg)+O(HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(X+H)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}S_{\rho}(X+H)+O(X^{\frac{3}{2}}T^{-1}(\log X)^{2})

\displaystyle-S(X)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}S_{\rho}(X)+O(X^{\frac{3}{2}}T^{-1}(\log X)^{2})+O(HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M+O(|R_{1}|+R_{2}+(R_{3}+X^{\frac{3}{2}}T^{-1}+T)(\log X)^{2}+HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1})

provided

(11)

X^{\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1-\varepsilon},\quad 2\leq T\leq X,

where

M\coloneqq S(X+H)-S(X),\quad R_{1}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{3}{2})}\bigg((X+H)^{\rho+\frac{1}{2}}-X^{\rho+\frac{1}{2}}\bigg),

R_{2}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{(X+H)^{\rho}-X^{\rho}}{2\rho}B,\quad R_{3}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}H^{\beta}|\gamma|^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}.

In order to control the size of
X^{\frac{3}{2}}T^{-1}(\log X)^{2}
, put

(12)

T=X^{1+\varepsilon_{1}}H^{-1},\quad 0<\varepsilon_{1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

From (
11
), we have

(13)

X^{\varepsilon}\leq T\leq X^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}.

If
X^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\leq H
,

T(\log X)^{2}=HX^{1+\varepsilon_{1}}H^{-2}(\log X)^{2}\ll HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1}.

Therefore we get

(X^{\frac{3}{2}}T^{-1}+T)(\log X)^{2}\ll HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1}.

For
M
, from Lemma
2.5
, we have

(14)

M=\frac{\zeta(\frac{3}{2})}{\zeta(3)}HX^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(HX^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-1}).

We first consider
R_{1}
. From the fundamental theorem of calculus,

(X+H)^{\rho+\frac{1}{2}}-X^{\rho+\frac{1}{2}}=\bigg(\rho+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\int_{X}^{X+H}u^{\rho-\frac{1}{2}}du\ll|\gamma|HX^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}.

By the Stirling’s formula and dissecting dyadically,

\displaystyle R_{1}\ll HX^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{X^{\beta}}{|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\ll HX^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{1\leq K\leq T}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}X^{\beta}

(15)

\displaystyle\ll HX^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log X\sup_{1\leq K\leq T}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}X^{\beta}.

For
1\leq K\leq T
, we denote

K=X^{\delta},\quad XH^{-1}=X^{\Delta}.

From (
12
),

(16)

0\leq\delta\leq\varepsilon_{1}+\Delta.

By using Lemma
2.10
, we have

K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}X^{\beta}\ll(X^{\phi(\delta)-\frac{1}{2}\delta}+X^{1-\eta+(2\eta-\frac{1}{2})\delta})(\log X)^{A}.

From Lemma
2.11
, for sufficiently large
X
, we have

\frac{d}{d\delta}(\phi(\delta)-\frac{1}{2}\delta)>0,\quad 2\eta-\frac{1}{2}<0.

Then, by (
16
) and the mean value theorem, we get

\displaystyle K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}X^{\beta}

\displaystyle\ll(X^{\phi(\Delta+\varepsilon_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}}+X^{1-\eta})(\log X)^{A}

\displaystyle\ll(X^{\phi(\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}}+X^{1-\eta})(\log X)^{A}.

By substituting the above into (
15
), we have

(17)

R_{1}\ll HX^{-\frac{1}{2}+\phi(\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}}(\log X)^{A}+HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1}.

Next, we consider
R_{2}
. From the fundamental theorem in calculus,

R_{2}\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}\int_{X}^{X+H}u^{\beta-1}\ll HX^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}X^{\beta}.

For
|\gamma|\leq T\leq X
, we have
1\leq\frac{X}{|\gamma|}
and

(18)

R_{2}\ll HX^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{X^{\beta}}{|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\ll HX^{-\frac{1}{2}+\phi(\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}}(\log X)^{A}+HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1}.

Finally we consider
R_{3}
. By dissecting dyadically,

R_{3}\ll\log X\sup_{1\leq K\leq T}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}(HK)^{\beta}.

Similarly to
R_{1}
, we denote
K=X^{\delta},XH^{-1}=X^{\Delta}
.
From (
13
),

X^{\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}}=X^{1+\varepsilon_{1}}H^{-1}=T\leq X.

Thus, we have

(19)

0\leq\Delta\leq 1-\varepsilon_{1}.

We define

\lambda=\lambda(\delta)=\frac{\log K}{\log HK}=\frac{\delta}{1-\Delta+\delta}.

From the above,
\lambda
is increasing in (
19
).
By Lemma
2.10
, we have

K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}(HK)^{\beta}\ll\bigg((HK)^{\phi(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}\lambda}+(HK)^{1-\eta+(2\eta-\frac{1}{2})\lambda}\bigg)(\log X)^{A}.

Since
HK=X(XH^{-1})^{-1}K=X^{1-\Delta+\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
K<|\gamma|\leq 2K\end{subarray}}(HK)^{\beta}

(20)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\bigg(X^{(1-\Delta+\delta)(\phi(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}\lambda)}+X^{(1-\Delta+\delta)(1-\eta+(2\eta-\frac{1}{2})\lambda)}\bigg)(\log X)^{A}.

For the frist trem of (
20
),
(1-\Delta+\delta)
and
(\phi(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2})
are increasing with respect to
\delta
, therefore we have

\displaystyle X^{(1-\Delta+\delta)(\phi(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}\lambda)}

\displaystyle\ll X^{(1+\varepsilon_{1})(\phi(\lambda(\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}))-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}))}

\displaystyle\ll X^{\phi(\lambda(\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}))-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\Delta+\varepsilon_{1})+\varepsilon_{1}}.

By
\lambda^{\prime}(\delta)\leq 1
, Lemma
2.11
and the mean value theorem give

\phi(\lambda(\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}))-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\Delta+\varepsilon_{1})\leq\phi(\lambda(\Delta))-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\Delta)+\varepsilon_{1}\leq\phi(\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}.

For the second term of (
20
), we have

(1-\Delta+\delta)(1-\eta+(2\eta-\frac{1}{2})\lambda)\ll(1-\Delta)(1-\eta)+(\frac{1}{2}+\eta)(\Delta+\varepsilon_{1}).

Thus we get

X^{(1-\Delta+\delta)(1-\eta+(2\eta-\frac{1}{2})\lambda)}\ll H^{\frac{1}{2}}X^{\frac{1}{2}-\eta+(\frac{1}{2}+\eta)\varepsilon_{1}}\ll H^{\frac{1}{2}}X^{\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}}.

Then,

(21)

R_{3}\ll X^{\phi(\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}+3\varepsilon_{1}}+H^{\frac{1}{2}}X^{\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}}.

From (
14
), (
17
), (
18
) and (
21
), we have

\sum_{X<N\leq X+H}\widetilde{R}_{B}(N)=\frac{\zeta(\frac{3}{2})}{\zeta(3)}HX^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(HX^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1}+E)

provided

X^{\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}

and
\varepsilon_{1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{16}
,
where

E=HX^{-\frac{1}{2}+\phi(\Delta)\frac{1}{2}\Delta+4\varepsilon_{4}}+X^{\phi(\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+4\varepsilon_{1}},\quad XH^{-1}=X^{\Delta}.

If
X^{\frac{32-4\sqrt{15}}{49}+\varepsilon}\leq H
, we have

0\leq\Delta\leq\frac{17+4\sqrt{15}}{49}.

Then, from Lemma
2.12
, we have

E\ll HX^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{10}+4\varepsilon_{1}}.

By taking
\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{\varepsilon}{80}
, we have the asymptotic formula (
2
) provide

X^{\frac{32-4\sqrt{15}}{49}+\varepsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}.

Hence, Theorem
1.5
follows.

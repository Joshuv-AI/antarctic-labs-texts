Unified Memcapacitor-Memristor Memory for Synaptic Weights and Neuron Temporal Dynamics
S. D’Agostino, M. Massarotto, T. Torchet, F. Moro, N. Castellani, L. Grenouillet, Y. Beilliard, D. Esseni, M. Payvand, E. Vianello, CEA, LETI, Université Grenoble Alpes, Grenoble, France, DPIA, Universitá degli Studi di Udine, 33100 Udine, Italy

Abstract




Abstract
—
We present a fabricated and experimentally characterized memory stack that unifies memristive and memcapacitive behavior. Exploiting this dual functionality, we design a circuit enabling simultaneous control of spatial and temporal dynamics in recurrent spiking neural networks (RSNNs). Hardware-aware simulations highlight its promise for efficient neuromorphic processing.

I

Introduction

Analog neuromorphic computing, which uses analog circuits to emulate the biophysics and computational efficiency of biological brains, is a promising solution for energy-efficient embedded sensory processing
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. However, key challenges remain. These systems require high-density memory for synaptic weights and capacitive elements to reproduce the temporal dynamics of neurons and synapses. Memristor arrays offer a compact, analog-friendly solution for synaptic weight storage
[
1
]
. Recent work on ferroelectric capacitors (FeCAPs) shows programmable capacitance with non-destructive readout
[
4
,
5
]
, making them suitable for storing reconfigurable time constants. In this work, we present a unified memory stack based on silicon-doped hafnium oxide and a titanium (Ti) scavenging layer. Depending on the initialization, each device functions either as a memristor for synaptic weight storage or as a memcapacitor for implementing differential pair integrator (DPI) neurons and synapses
[
6
]
. Through hardware-aware simulations, we demonstrate the feasibility of this technology for implementing RSNNs in spoken digit classification tasks, showing accuracy benefits.

III

Hardware-aware simulations

We evaluate the potential of combining memristor and memcapacitor devices in RSNNs for keyword spotting tasks through hardware-aware simulations implemented with the JAX library (Fig.
6
). The RSNN model incorporates noisy, quantized memristors, and we examine two configurations for synaptic and neuronal time constants:
(1)
homogeneous
20\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}
time-constants, and
(2)
heterogeneous values reflecting the experimentally observed device-to-device variability of memcapacitors (Fig.
6
a). Both configurations are tested with either trainable and fixed time constants. In the trainable case, time constants are learned via backpropagation alongside synaptic weights, assuming a 5% modulation range consistent with experimental CMW (Fig.
4
).
Consistently with prior studies
[
9
]
, we observe that a time-constant heterogeneity enhances performance on tasks with complex temporal dynamics, and their training yields software-level performances
[
9
]
.

Analysis of clinical and pathological characteristics of classic adrenocortical carcinoma
Authors: Yi Liu, Zhan Wang, JiaYang Chen, Yang Zhao, JianHua Deng, Yu Xiao, YuShi Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) is a rare malignancy; however, its specific characteristics within the Chinese population remain poorly characterized. This study investigates the clinical and pathological characteristics of classic adrenocortical carcinoma in a Chinese cohort to improve understanding of the disease and inform management strategies. We retrospectively investigated the clinical and pathological features of 138 classic ACC patients treated at Peking Union Medical College Hospital from January, 1984 to January, 2024. The gene expression profile of 79 ACC patients with survival data was downloaded from the TCGA database. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. We found that classic ACC was more common in women than in men, and the majority of classic ACC patients were diagnosed over 40. The tumor exhibited a slight right-sided laterality, with the incidence on the right side being 1.28 times that of the left. The median tumor diameter was 9.05 cm at diagnosis. We revealed that the Ki-67 index of most patients was over than 5% and the median of Ki-67 index was 20%. The majority of classic ACC patients were categorized into ENSAT stage II (47.1%) and III (27.54%) at diagnosis. In addition, classic ACC patients with advanced stage tended to get higher levels of Ki-67 index (P = 0.002). Patients with higher Ki-67 index (P = 0.036) and Ki-67 gene expression (P < 0.001) had worse overall survival rate. Pathway analysis in patients with high MKI67 expression revealed an enrichment of genes associated with cell cycle regulation and p53 signaling, suggesting a potential link between high proliferative activity and these molecular pathways. In conclusion, this study provides valuable insights into the clinical and pathological profiles of ACC in the Chinese population, facilitating better diagnostic approaches.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54855-9.

Introduction

Adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) is a kind of rare tumor and approximately 1 to 2 cases per million people are newly diagnosed each year1. Two prevalent age periods are occurring in the ACC, younger than 4 years old or older than 40 years old2. The clinical manifestation of ACC is quite heterogeneous, which depends on the hormone excess and tumor burden. Tumors with hormone excess account for over half of all ACC patients and mostly represent Cushing’s syndrome or androgen excess related signs3. A minority of ACC patients have only isolated mineralocorticoid excess with severe hypertension and profound hypokalemia4.

ACC is a kind of aggressive tumor, the 5-year survival of stage I ACC patients is about 85% while decreasing to less than 15% of stage IV ACC patients5,6. The clinical stage at diagnosis is a vital factor that affects the survival of ACC patients. However, about 12% to 46% of patients had metastases at diagnosis, and the most frequent metastatic sites are the lungs, the liver, the bone and the peritoneum7. Up to now, open surgery is the only potential curative therapy to prolong the survival of patients with ACC8,9. Unfortunately, there are limited ways for advanced and relapse ACC patients, and most of these patients die within one year10. Although efforts have been made to figure out new targets and novel agents, the majority of these attempts failed. Due to the rarity of ACC, multiple target therapeutic drugs have good therapeutic effects only in vivo and in vitro experiments, and a small number of targeted drug trials included in clinical trials have poor results11. Until now, the only treatment modalities with proven efficacy are mitotane or mitotane-based therapies12. However, there are only a small number of people response to mitotane alone or mitotane combined with etoposide, adriamycin, and cisplatin (EDP-M)13.

The previous study of ACC was mainly based on European and American populations, there is a notable lack of studies involving Chinese ACC patients, with existing ones often being small sample size14–16. Therefore, the present study was designed to help us better understand the characteristics of ACC based on the Chinese cohort so that patients can get more precise management.

Materials and methods

Data source

In present study, a total of 138 patients that pathology confirmed as classic ACC attended in Peking Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH) from January 1, 1984 to January 1, 2024 were enrolled. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) patients diagnosed as classical ACC; (b) patients with sufficient clinical data. The exclusion criteria were as following: (a) malignancies of non-adrenocortical origin; (b) benign tumors or special subtypes of ACC; (c) patients with extensive missing clinical data. This study was approved by the ethics committee of PUMCH (XHYY2024010).

Transcriptomic profiles of 79 ACC patients were downloaded from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/projects/TCGA-ACC) to explore the molecular mechanisms of Ki-67, encoded by the MKI67 gene. The corresponding clinical information was obtained from UCSC Xena (http://xena.ucsc.edu)17. The flow chart of present study is showed in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Data collecting

We retrospectively collected the clinical and pathological information of these ACC patients. The European Network for the Study of Adrenal Tumors (ENSAT) stage and TNM stage were evaluated according to the (Computed Tomography) CT、(Magnetic Resonance Imaging) MRI and18 F-Fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography/Computed Tomography (18 F-FDG-PET/CT); The size of tumor, Ki-67 index, and necrosis information were obtained from the primary pathological report. All slides were originally evaluated by two board-certified pathologists from the Department of Pathology at PUMCH. Following standard clinical protocols, the Ki-67 score was determined by the manual counting of hot spots. The functional status of tumor was determined by symptoms and signs of patients, as well as endocrine examination. Cushing syndrome was recognized by symptoms of rounded face, buffalo back, centripetal obesity, supraclavicular fat pad, neck fat pad, purple skin lines, and the laboratory test, including elevated plasma cortisol, decreased adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), and elevated 24-hour urinary free cortisol levels. The manifestations of abnormal reticular zone include menolipsis, increased hair, enlargement of Adam’s apple in women, and gynaecomastia, testicular atrophy in males. Laboratory tests of abnormal reticular zone display as elevated levels of dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate and abnormal levels of testosterone, estrogen and progesterone. In addition, the typical features of abnormalities in the zona glomerulosa are hypertension, refractory hypokalemia, low renin activity, and elevated aldosterone levels.

Immunohistochemical staining for Ki-67

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining was performed on 4 μm FFPE sections. After deparaffinization and rehydration, antigen retrieval was conducted in citrate buffer (pH 6.0). Sections were incubated with a primary anti-Ki-67 antibody (Proteintech, 27309-1-AP, 1:5000) overnight at 4℃, followed by incubation with an HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (Abcam, ab6721, 1:1000). The signal was visualized using DAB substrate (Servicebio, G1212-200T) and counterstained with hematoxylin.

Differentially gene expression and function enrichment analysis

To investigate the potential molecular mechanisms underlying Ki-67 overexpression (encoded by the MKI67 gene), we divided the 79 ACC patients into the high and low MKI67 expression groups based on the median expression level of MKI67. After normalizing the TPM matrix, the differently expressed genes between two groups were identified by “limma” with P < 0.05 and |log2FC| >1. Then the upregulated and downregulated genes were used for GO and KEGG functional enrichment analysis via “clusterProfiler”18–20. Enriched terms with adj.P.value < 0.05 and adj.q.value < 0.2 were considered significant.

Sample size calculation

The sample size for this study was calculated using the Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test module in PASS 2021 (version 21.0.9) software (https://www.ncss.com) to assess the robustness of Ki-67 expression differences between surgical resection and biopsy samples. The calculation was based on the estimated mean difference between two independent groups, with the significance level a = 0.05 and the statistical power 1-β = 0.80. Based on our results, the assumed mean difference between the two groups was set at 0.0014, with a predicted standard deviation (SD) of 0.002. Considering the clinical sample accessibility and experimental design, a non-balanced allocation strategy was employed. The calculation results indicated that, with the sample size of the first group fixed (n = 31), a minimum of 26 cases is required for the second group, totaling 57 samples.

Statistic analysis

Patients with missing clinical or pathological parameters were excluded from the relevant subgroup analyses. Data accord with normal distribution, with mean ± standard deviation. For comparisons among multiple groups, the Kruskal Wallis test was used, and for comparisons among two groups, Mann-Whitney rank-sum test was utilized. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used to evaluate the overall survival (OS) rate between groups. Tests in this study were analyzed using GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.2) software (https://www.graphpad.com) and R language (version 4.3.1) (https://www.r-project.org). Patients with missing clinical or pathological parameters were excluded from the relevant subgroup analyses to ensure data integrity. All P-values were bilateral, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline information of patients

A total of 138 classic ACC patients were included in this study (Table 1). Of these, 37.68% (n = 52) were male and 62.32% (n = 86) were female. The median age at diagnosis was 47.5 years, with the most common age group being 41–50 years (33.33%). About 72.47% patients were over 40 years old. Tumors were more frequently located on the right side (55.80%) than on the left side (43.48%), with a tumor diameter > 5 cm in 93.48% of cases. At diagnosis, 47.1% of patients were classified into ENSAT stage II, and 27.54% were in stage III. Functional manifestations were absent in 58.7% of patients, while 23.91% exhibited Cushing’s syndrome, 16.67% had adrenogenital syndrome, and 13.77% had primary hyperaldosteronism.

The clinical characteristics of classic ACC patients

Clinical manifestations related to the zona fasciculata were observed in 25.38% (n = 33) of patients, who presented with classic Cushing syndrome features including moon face, buffalo hump, and central obesity. An additional 20% (n = 26) showed abnormal laboratory findings despite being asymptomatic (Fig. 1A and C). Regarding the zona reticularis, 37.69% of classic ACC patients (n = 52) exhibited sex hormone excess and adrenogenital syndromes. The most prevalent symptoms were amenorrhea, submandibular hair growth, and hirsutism, with specific adrenogenital abnormalities accounting for 17.89% (n = 23) of cases (Fig. 1B and D).

The pathological features of classic ACC patients

According to the pathological reports, we retrospectively analyzed the pathological features of classic ACC patients. We found that there were about 31.16% of ACC tumor (n = 43) accompanied by necrosis (Fig. 2A). Renal vein or inferior vena cava (IVC) thrombus was present in 9.49% (n = 13) of cases (Fig. 2B). Ki-67 index is one of the most important factors associated with the prognosis of ACC. In present study, most tumors had a high Ki-67 index, with a median value of 20%, indicating a high proliferative potential of the tumors. (Fig. 2C). We also explored whether there exists difference between tissue of needle biopsy tissue and surgical resection tissue in ENSAT III and IV stage patients. 11 patients out of 42 patients were diagnosed as ACC by needle biopsy. The mean Ki-67 index was 27.18% in patients diagnosed by needle biopsy, while 41.68% in patients diagnosed by surgical resection. Result showed that although no statistical difference was detected, there existed the possibility of underestimating the proliferative capacity of tumors through needle biopsy (P = 0.094) (Fig. 2D). Furthermore, we displayed immunohistochemical images with Ki-67 index levels of 8%, 20%, and 60%, respectively (Fig. 2E).

The correlation between Ki-67 and clinical information

To further explore the correlation between Ki-67 and ENSAT stage, we analyzed the variations in Ki-67 expression levels across different ENSAT stage subgroups. As shown in Fig. 3A, tumor size did not correlate with Ki-67 expression levels (P = 0.85). However, a significant progression in Ki-67 expression was observed alongside increasing clinical stages (P = 0.002) (Fig. 3B). In addition, the Ki-67 had no correlation with SUVmax (P = 0.85) and necrosis (P = 0.77) (Fig. 3C and D).

Ki-67 index as prognostic factor and its potential mechanisms

To better understand the role of Ki-67 index in ACC, we performed K-M survival analysis of Ki-67 index and results showed that patients with higher Ki-67 index had worse prognosis (P = 0.036) (Fig. 5A). Next, 79 ACC patients from the TCGA database were stratified into high and low expression groups based on the median expression of MKI67 (the gene encoding Ki-67). K-M survival analysis showed that patients with high MKI67 gene expression had worse OS (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5B). Compared with the low MKI67 expression group, 106 upregulated genes and 70 downregulated genes were identified in the high MKI67 expression group (Fig. 5C and D). KEGG showed that these upregulated genes were enriched in Cell cycle, p53 signaling pathway, and Motor proteins pathways and the downregulated genes were enriched in Allograft rejection, Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation, and Th17 cell differentiation pathways (Fig. 5E and F). Similarly, these upregulated genes were enriched in microtubule binding, microtubule motor activity, cytoskeletal motor activity pathways and the downregulated genes were enriched in MHC class II protein complex binding, immune receptor activity, and antigen binding pathways (Fig. 5G and H). In summary, higher MKI67 expression appears to correlate with a trend toward diminished immune engagement, while p53 and mTOR pathways show signs of increased enrichment in this subgroup.

Discussion

ACC is a rare but highly aggressive endocrine malignancy, with a challenging prognosis, particularly in advanced stages21. There has been no major breakthrough in recent decades and tumor resection is still the curative treatment for ACC patients22. Mitotane alone or combined with cytotoxic drugs is the treatment of choice for ACC patients, but the response rates were less than 30%23,24. To sum up, the rarity and the progressive ability of ACC lead to limited advancement in novel treatment development. This study provides a comprehensive analysis of the clinical and pathological characteristics of ACC in a large cohort from China, contributing valuable data for better management of this rare tumor.

According to previous studies, women had higher incidences than men in ACC patients25. Our study found a higher incidence of ACC in women (62.32%) compared to men (37.68%), which aligns with previous findings suggesting a female predilection in ACC cases. In addition, adults aged 40 to 70 years old tended to have ACC and over 60% of ACC were diagnosed in this decades1,26,27, while we revealed that 50% of ACC patients in our study were with an average age at presentation over 40. Interestingly, studies have suggested that gender differences in ACC may be related to sex hormone exposure and senescence, with certain genetic mutations showing sex-specific effects on tumor progression. The loss of ZNRF3 triggered cellular senescence and ultimately permits metastatic adrenal cancer in old animals. This effect was enhanced in female and leads to low infiltration of myeloid cells that increased susceptibility to metastatic cancer28,29. This sex- and senescence- dependent differences in ACC development warrant further investigation into the molecular underpinnings of ACC in different genders, which may also help inform personalized treatment strategies.

The symptoms of ACC patients are widely diverse and depend on the hormone excess and tumor size. In our cohort, 58.7% of patients were asymptomatic, which highlights the challenge in diagnosing ACC, especially in early stages when tumors are smaller and hormone production is minimal. Hormone excess was observed in approximately 41.3% of cases, with Cushing’s syndrome being the most common manifestation (23.91%), followed by adrenogenital syndrome (16.67%) and primary hyperaldosteronism (13.77%). These findings underscore the importance of considering ACC in the differential diagnosis of patients with unexplained endocrine abnormalities, especially those presenting with features of Cushing’s syndrome or androgen excess. Based on previously published research, ACC patients presented as autonomous adrenocortical steroid excess as diagnosis accounting for about 60% in all cases30–32, which also indicated that a quite proportion of ACC patients were diagnosed without any symptoms. However, a significant proportion of ACC cases are functional, but discovered incidentally during imaging for other conditions. This emphasized the importance of laboratory examination in adrenal mass diagnosis and earlier research has suggested that patients with adrenal mass > 1 cm should determine whether the tumor is endocrine active33,34.

The Weiss score has been widely accepted as the method of choice for the determination of the behavior of adrenal tumor12,35. Necrosis is a key component of the Weiss score and we found that necrosis could be found in 31.16% of ACC patients and this was led by the rapid tumor growth in a short time, which might imply that confluent necrosis is sufficient to predict a poor clinical course of ACC patients36. The European Network for the Study of Adrenal Tumors (ENSAT) stage, resection status, mitoses per HPF, Ki-67, hormone production, and angioinvasion have been established as prognostic factors of ACC patients37,38. The ENSAT stage has been widely used in ACC and has been proven to be associated with the survival of ACC patients. In our study, the majority of ACC patients were over stage II when diagnosed, which might be associated with the progression characteristics of ACC, resulting in the short course of disease. Besides the ENSAT stage, Ki-67 index is another factor that has been proven to be an independent prognostic factor for overall survival and recurrence-free survival of ACC patients39. We found that Ki-67 index was higher in ACC patients with advanced stage. 18-FDG-PET/CT has been reported as a precise imaging modality for ACC staging40. Rossella et al. found that the strong correlation between SUVmax and Ki-67 (R = 0.47, P = 0.0009) indicated a relationship between 18 F-FDG uptake levels and tumor proliferation. However, the relationship between the Ki-67 index and SUVmax was not observed in our study, which might be due to the low sensitivity and specificity of needle biopsy and the heterogeneity of ACC tumor tissue4. In the present study, although no statistical difference was detected, there existed the possibility of underestimating the proliferative capacity of tumors through needle biopsy (P = 0.094). Given our total sample size of only 42 patients, the study was likely underpowered to detect the anticipated effect size. According to our power analysis, a minimum of 57 samples, with at least 26 cases in the biopsy group is required to achieve a statistical power of 0.80. Therefore, the lack of statistical significance in the current analysis may be attributed to the limited sample size, which precludes a definitive conclusion. Further validation in a larger, independent cohort is warranted to confirm these preliminary findings.

Currently, therapeutic options for ACC remain very limited. For localized disease, radical surgical resection continues to be the sole potentially curative approach. Mitotane remains the only FDA-approved medication for ACC treatment. However, the overall objective response rate of mitotane in advanced ACC was below 30%41. In addition, the limited understanding of its pathogenesis and its rarity hindered the development of novel drugs targeting ACC. To date, no single targeted therapeutic agent has demonstrated significant clinical efficacy as monotherapy in ACC. For example, linsitinib the inhibitor of IGF-2/IGF-1R was reported not to improve the PFS and OS of advanced ACC42. Therefore, investigating the therapeutic efficacy of combination of repurposed approved drugs seemed to be feasible strategies. The phase II single-arm clinical trial NCT04318730 evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of camrelizumab (PD-1 inhibitor) combined with apatinib (VEGFR inhibitor) in patients with advanced ACC. Results demonstrated that ORR was 52%, median PFS was 13.3 months, and median OS was 20.9 months, indicating that the combination of camrelizumab and apatinib exhibited promising activity and an acceptable safety profile in pretreated advanced ACC patients43.

However, there are some limitations in our study. First, because the rarity of ACC, the sample size is not larger than that of other malignancies. Secondly, parts of pathologic diagnosis were made based on needle biopsy, resulting in bias of Ki-67 index, necrosis, and so on. Additionally, as our study spans a four-decade period, it inevitably encompasses evolutions in diagnostic techniques, immunohistochemical staining sensitivity for Ki-67, and clinical management protocols. Consequently, the potential influence of these temporal shifts on certain clinical parameters cannot be entirely ruled out.

Despite these deficiencies, the sample size of our study is relatively large in ACC-related research, which concluded clinical characteristics of Chinese patients and explored the role of Ki-67 index in ACC patients. This study provides valuable insights into the clinical and pathological profiles of ACC, facilitating better diagnostic approaches.

Logarithmic Convexity and impulse Approximate Controllability for Degenerate Parabolic Equations with Robin Boundary Conditions
Hind El Baggari 1, Ilham Ouelddris 1

Abstract
. In this work, we investigate the approximate controllability of a class of one-dimensional degenerate parabolic equations with Robin boundary conditions. The degeneracy occurs at one endpoint of the spatial domain, and we apply an impulsive control in a small region at a fixed moment. Our main result establishes an observability inequality for the adjoint system, from which we deduce approximate controllability at final time . The proof relies on a logarithmic convexity argument, developed through a Carleman commutator approach.

\displaystyle+\exp\dfrac{1}{2}\left(M\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\phi(x,t_{1})-(1+M)\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\phi(x,t_{2})+\max_{x\in\overline{\left(0,1\right)\backslash\omega}}\phi(x,t_{3})\right)

\displaystyle\times\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{1+M}.

Subsequently, based on the definition of the weight function
\phi
, it can be concluded that

\displaystyle\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{1+M}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\dfrac{s}{2}\left(\dfrac{M}{T-t_{1}+h}\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)-\dfrac{1+M}{T-t_{2}+h}\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)+\dfrac{1}{T-t_{3}+h}\max_{x\in\overline{\omega}}\varphi(x)\right)

\displaystyle\times\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{M}\parallel u(\cdot,t_{3})\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle+\exp\dfrac{s}{2}\left(\dfrac{M}{T-t_{1}+h}\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)-\dfrac{1+M}{T-t_{2}+h}\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)+\dfrac{1}{T-t_{3}+h}\max_{x\in\overline{\left(0,1\right)\backslash\omega}}\varphi(x)\right)

\displaystyle\times\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{1+M}.

(3.33)

Step 7.
Choose
t_{3}=T
,
t_{2}=T-lh
and
t_{1}=T-2lh
in such a way that
0<2lh<T
with
l>1
. With this choice, the expression in (
3.33
) becomes

\displaystyle\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{1+M_{l}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\dfrac{s}{2h}\left(\dfrac{M_{l}}{1+2l}\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)-\dfrac{1+M_{l}}{1+l}\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)+\max_{x\in\overline{\omega}}\varphi(x)\right)

\displaystyle\times\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{M_{l}}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}

(3.34)

\displaystyle+\exp\dfrac{s}{2h}\left(\dfrac{M_{l}}{1+2l}\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)-\dfrac{1+M_{l}}{1+l}\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)+\max_{x\in\overline{\left(0,1\right)\backslash\omega}}\varphi(x)\right)

\displaystyle\times\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{1+M_{l}},

In this context,
M_{l}=\dfrac{(l+1)^{C_{0}}-1}{1-\left(\frac{l+1}{2l+1}\right)^{C_{0}}}
. Therefore, for
l>1
sufficiently large one has

M_{l}<\frac{(1+l)^{C_{0}}-1}{1-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{C_{0}}}.

Now, observe that
\varphi\leq 0
on
(0,1)
which implies that
\displaystyle{\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}}\varphi(x)=0
. Consequently, one obtains

\dfrac{M_{l}}{1+2l}\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)-\dfrac{1+M_{l}}{1+l}\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)+\max_{x\in\overline{\left(0,1\right)\backslash\omega}}\varphi(x)=-\dfrac{1+M_{l}}{1+l}\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)+\max_{x\in\overline{\left(0,1\right)\backslash\omega}}\varphi(x).

Using the condition (
1.2
) satisfied by the observation region
\omega
and knowing that
C_{0}\in(0,1)
, we can choose
l>1
large enough to deduce that

\dfrac{M_{l}}{1+2l}\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)-\dfrac{1+M_{l}}{1+l}\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)+\max_{x\in\overline{\left(0,1\right)\backslash\omega}}\varphi(x)<0,

and

\displaystyle\dfrac{M_{l}}{1+2l}\max_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)-\dfrac{1+M_{l}}{1+l}\min_{x\in\left[0,1\right]}\varphi(x)+\max_{x\in\overline{\omega}}\varphi(x)>0.

Hence, there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that for every
h>0
satisfying
0<2lh<T
, the following inequality is established

\displaystyle\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{1+M_{l}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{C_{1}}{h}}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{M_{l}}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}+e^{\frac{-C_{2}}{h}}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{1+M_{l}}.

On the other hand, leveraging Lemma
3.1
, one can notice that
\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel\leq\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel
. This combined with the fact that for any
2lh\geq T
,

1\leq e^{\frac{2l}{T}C_{2}}e^{-\frac{C_{2}}{h}}
yields that for any
h>0
,

\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{1+M_{l}}\leq e^{\frac{C_{1}}{h}}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{M_{l}}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}+e^{\frac{2l}{T}C_{2}}e^{\frac{-C_{2}}{h}}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{1+M_{l}}.

To finalize, we select
h>0
such that

e^{\frac{2l}{T}C_{2}}e^{\frac{-C_{2}}{h}}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{1+M_{l}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{1+M_{l}},

which leads to

e^{\frac{C_{1}}{h}}=\left(2e^{\frac{2l}{T}C_{2}}\left(\dfrac{\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel}{\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel}\right)^{1+M_{l}}\right)^{\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}}.

As a consequence, we obtain

\displaystyle\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{1+M_{l}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left[2e^{\frac{2l}{T}C_{2}}\left(\dfrac{\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel}{\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel}\right)^{1+M_{l}}\right]^{\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{M_{l}}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Then, for
l>1
one has

\displaystyle\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{1+M_{l}+\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}(1+M_{l})}\leq

\displaystyle e^{\left(1+\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}\right)\ln(2)+\frac{2l}{T}C_{1}}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{M_{l}+(1+M_{l})\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{2l\left(1+\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{C_{1}}{T}\right)}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{M_{l}+(1+M_{l})\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Setting
\rho=\dfrac{1}{1+M_{l}+(1+M_{l})\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}}
,
\mathcal{C}=2l\left(1+\dfrac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}\right)\max\left(C_{1},1\right)
allows us to complete the proof.
∎

4.
Null approximate impulse controllability

We are now prepared to prove our principal result concerning the null approximate impulse controllability of the impulsive system (
1.1
). To begin the proof, we present the following lemma, which is a direct consequence of the observation estimate derived in Lemma
1.3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that Hypothesis
1
holds true and the observation region
\omega
meets the geometric condition
1.2
. Then, one can find positive constants
\mathcal{C}
and
\beta
such that the estimate remains valid for all
\epsilon>0
,

\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{2}\leq\left(\dfrac{e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}}{\epsilon^{\beta}}\right)^{2}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\epsilon^{2}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{2}.

(4.1)

Proof.

According to Lemma
1.3
, there exist a positive constant
\mathcal{C}
and a parameter
\rho\in(0,1)
such that

\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{2}\leq\left(e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}\right)^{2\rho}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{2(1-\rho)}.

Let
\epsilon>0
. We write

\displaystyle\left(e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}\right)^{2\rho}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{2(1-\rho)}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\left(1-\rho\right)^{\frac{1-\rho}{2\rho}}}{\epsilon^{\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}}}e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}\right)^{2\rho}\times\left(\epsilon\left(\dfrac{1}{1-\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel\right)^{2(1-\rho)}.

An application of Young’s inequality yields the following estimate, valid for all
\epsilon>0
.

\displaystyle\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\rho\left[e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\left(\dfrac{1-\rho}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1-\rho}{2\rho}}\right]^{2}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\epsilon^{\frac{2(1-\rho)}{\rho}}}e^{2\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\parallel u(\cdot,T)\parallel_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\parallel u(\cdot,0)\parallel^{2}.

This computation confirms the inequality (
4.1
), where the parameter
\beta
is given explicitly by

\beta=\dfrac{1-\rho}{\rho}.

∎

Remark 1
.

The analyticity of the semigroup
(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}
implies that the range
\mathcal{R}(e^{TA})
is dense in
L^{2}(0,1)
. Consequently, null approximate impulse controllability is equivalent to approximate impulse controllability, meaning that

\forall y_{0},y_{T}\in L^{2}(0,1),\;\forall\epsilon>0,\;\exists h(\cdot,\tau)\in L^{2}(\omega)\;\;\text{such that}\;\;\|y(\cdot,T)-y_{T}\|\leq\epsilon\|y_{0}\|.

In light of this equivalence, we shall henceforth refer to this property simply as *approximate controllability*.

The main impulse controllability result of this work reads as follows.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let us assume that Hypothesis
1
holds and that the control region
\omega
satisfies the geometric condition (
1.2
). Under these conditions, the system (
1.1
) is approximately impulse controllable for all
T>0
. In addition, for any
\epsilon>0
, the cost of the null approximate impulse control function at time
T
satisfies the following inequality:

\mathcal{P}(T,\epsilon)\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\beta}}e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T-\tau}\right)},

where the positive constants
\mathcal{C}
and
\beta
are obtained from the estimate (
4.1
).

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
,
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
, and define
K=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\beta}}e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T-\tau}\right)}
. We introduce the functional
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon,K}:L^{2}(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon,K}(v_{0})=\frac{K^{2}}{2}\|v(\cdot,T-\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\|v_{0}\|^{2}+\langle y_{0},v(\cdot,T)\rangle,

where
v
is the solution of the non-impulsive system (
1.5
) corresponding to the initial condition
v_{0}
. By applying the estimate (
4.1
) and utilizing the fact that

\|v(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq\|v(\cdot,T-\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},

we derive the following inequality:

\frac{K^{2}}{2}\|v(\cdot,T-\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}}{\epsilon^{\beta}}\right)^{2}\|v(\cdot,T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}\geq\frac{1}{4}\|v(\cdot,T)\|^{2}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\|v_{0}\|^{2}.

Thus, by applying Young’s inequality, we derive the following result

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon,K}(v_{0})\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\|v(\cdot,T)\|^{2}+\dfrac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\|v_{0}\|^{2}+\left\langle y_{0},v(\cdot,T)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\|v_{0}\|^{2}-\|y_{0}\|^{2}.

This leads to the coercivity of the functional
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon,K}
. Moreover, owing to its strict convexity and
\mathcal{C}^{1}
differentiability, we can deduce that
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon,K}
attains a unique minimizer
\tilde{v}_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
, which is characterized by

\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon,K}(\tilde{v}_{0})=\min_{v_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon,K}(v_{0}).

(4.2)

Additionally,
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon,K}^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{0})z_{0}=0
for every
z_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
, which results in the following bound for any
z_{0}

K^{2}\int_{\omega}\tilde{v}(x,T-\tau)z(x,T-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x+\epsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{v}_{0}(x)z_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}(x)z(x,T)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(4.3)

Here,
\tilde{v}
and
z
represent the solutions of the non-impulsive system (
1.5
), associated with the initial conditions
\tilde{v}_{0}
and
z_{0}
, respectively. To proceed, we multiply the first equation in (
1.1
) by
z(\cdot,T-t)
for all
t\in(0,T)\setminus\{\tau\}
, and integrate over the spatial domain
(0,1)
, leading to the following result

\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\partial_{t}y-(x^{\alpha}y_{x})_{x}\Big)(x,t)z(x,T-t)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

By applying integration by parts, one gets

\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{1}y(x,t)z(x,T-t)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)=0.

(4.4)

Next, we integrate (
4.4
) over
(0,\tau)
, obtaining

\int_{0}^{1}y(x,\tau^{-})z(x,T-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}(x)z(x,T)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4.5)

Integrating again (
4.4
) over
(\tau,T)
, gives

\int_{0}^{1}y(x,\tau)z(x,T-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}y(x,T)z_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4.6)

By associating (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), and taking into account that
y(\cdot,\tau)=y(\cdot,\tau^{-})+1_{\omega}h(\cdot,\tau)
, we infer that

\int_{\omega}f(x,\tau)z(x,T-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}y(x,T)z_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}(x)z(x,T)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(4.7)

Based on (
4.3
) and (
4.7
), selecting
f(\cdot,\tau)=K^{2}\tilde{v}(\cdot,T-\tau)
yields

\int_{0}^{1}\Big(y(x,T)+\epsilon^{2}\tilde{v}_{0}(x)\Big)z_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad\forall z_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1).

(4.8)

This leads to the conclusion that
y(\cdot,T)=-\epsilon^{2}\tilde{v}_{0}
. Additionally, by setting
z_{0}=\tilde{v}_{0}
, and applying both (
4.3
) and Young’s inequality, we can derive

K^{2}\parallel\tilde{v}(\cdot,T-\tau)\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\epsilon^{2}\parallel\tilde{v}_{0}\parallel^{2}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\parallel y_{0}\parallel^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\parallel\tilde{v}(\cdot,T)\parallel^{2}.

(4.9)

Now, by using (
4.1
) and applying Lemma
3.1
, we can now make the following estimation

\displaystyle\parallel\tilde{v}(\cdot,T)\parallel^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{\epsilon^{\beta}}e^{\mathcal{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\right)^{2}\parallel\tilde{v}(\cdot,T)\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\epsilon^{2}\parallel\tilde{v}_{0}\parallel^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}\parallel\tilde{v}(\cdot,T-\tau)\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\epsilon^{2}\parallel\tilde{v}_{0}\parallel^{2}.

(4.10)

Finally, combining (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) , it follows that

K^{2}\parallel\tilde{v}(T-\tau)\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\epsilon^{2}\parallel\tilde{v}_{0}\parallel^{2}\leq\parallel y_{0}\parallel^{2}.

Remember that
y(\cdot,T)=-\epsilon^{2}\tilde{v}_{0}
and
f(\cdot,\tau)=K^{2}\tilde{v}(\cdot,T-\tau)
, it follows that

\dfrac{1}{K^{2}}\parallel f(\cdot,\tau)\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\dfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\parallel y(\cdot,T)\parallel^{2}\leq\parallel y_{0}\parallel^{2}.

(4.11)

This concludes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

5.
Conclusion

This paper investigates an impulse control problem for a one-dimensional degenerate heat equation in divergence form, subject to Robin boundary conditions. When the control is supported within a prescribed subinterval of the spatial domain (as specified in condition (
1.2
)), we establish null approximate controllability and derive an explicit upper bound on the cost of the impulse control. The analysis relies on a crucial observability estimate at a single time instant for solutions to the associated non-impulsive system. However, if the control support is strictly confined to the interior of the physical domain
(0,1)
, the problem remains unresolved, even for the degenerate equation without classical Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions.

The growth rate on the volume of ℳ g < L ⁡ ( g ) \mathcal{M}_{g}^{<L(g)}
Jinsong Liu, Xu Shan, Lang Wang, Yaosong Yang

Abstract
. Let ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} be the moduli space of hyperbolic surfaces of genus g endowed with the Weil-Petersson metric. In this paper, we introduce a function L ⁡ ( g ) L(g) of genus g g and call the geodesics whose length less than L ⁡ ( g ) L(g) short geodesics. We compute the growth rate on the volume of the subset of hyperbolic surfaces with short geodesics. In particular, when g g approaches infinity, if L ⁡ ( g ) L(g) also approaches infinity, then the volume of surfaces characterized by short geodesics is equal to V g V_{g} almost surely.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
be a
n
-type in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Denote
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
to be the Teichüller space consists of hyperbolic structures with genus
g
and
n
marked geodesic boundary components of lengths
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}
. If
L_{i}=0
for some
i
, it indicates that the
i
-th boundary component is a cusp. If all boundary components are cusps, we will simply denote
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
as
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
. The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
is the quotient of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
by the action of the mapping class group. We will simply denote
\mathcal{M}_{g,0}
as
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

The length of the shortest geodesic on hyperbolic surfaces is a fundamental quantity that has been extensively studied in recent years. In 1985, Bers
[
1
]
proved the existence of a universal constant
l(g)
such that every closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
admits a closed geodesic of length at most
l(g)
. Subsequent work by Buser
[
2
]
established the refined estimates for the lengths and counts of short geodesics, exploring their profound implications for the geometry and spectral properties of Riemann surfaces. The primary technique in their work is the area-length method, employed to estimate the length of the shortest geodesics.

On the other hand, the Weil-Petersson metric on Teichumüller space has been extensively investigated in recent years, and is related to the mentioned topics above. A pivotal advancement came from Mirzakhani’s work
[
5
]
, which links Weil-Petersson volumes of moduli spaces to the enumeration of simple closed geodesics, providing powerful new tools for analyzing geodesic distributions. Subsequently, Mirzakhani used the integral formula to estimate the volume of the moduli space of the hyperbolic surfaces with short enough geodesics, and the approximate length of the shortest separating geodesics in
[
6
]
. Furthermore, Mirzakhani and Petri
[
7
]
computed the large genus asymptotic behavior of the expected systole length on random surfaces, while Nie, Wu and Xue
[
8
]
investigated the length spectrum of separating closed geodesics.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{<t}
denote the subset of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
consisting of all surfaces
X
that admit closed geodesics of length less than
t
and
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\geqslant t}
denote its complement. For a fixed small constant
\varepsilon>0
, Mirakhani
[
6
]
demonstrated that the volume of
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{<\varepsilon}
satisfies
\lim\limits_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{<\varepsilon})}{V_{g}}=O(\varepsilon^{2})
, where
V_{g}
is the Weil-Petersson volume of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Concurrently, Mirzakhani and Petri
[
7
]
provided a skecth proof that the probability of
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\geqslant L(g)}
is bounded by
O(L(g)e^{-L(g)})
for
L(g)<A\log g
. After that, Lipnowski and Wright
[
4
]
derived the precise volume of
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{<\varepsilon}
using the principle of inclusion-exclusion. In this paper, when
L(g)\approx\log\log g
, we obtain a more precise volume estimate of
M_{g}^{<L(g)}
than the one in Mirzakhani and Petri
[
7
]
. For simplicity,
L(g)
will be abbreviated as
L
in the rest of the article.

The main result is as following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
L(g)
be a function of genus
g
satisfying

L(g)\geqslant(1+\varepsilon)\log\log g.

Then we have

\operatorname{Prob}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}(X\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ell_{sys}(X)\leqslant L(g))=1-O(g^{-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)}),

where we follow the notations in
[
6
]
to denote
\operatorname{Prob}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}(\mathcal{A}):=\frac{1}{V_{g}}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}}dX

and let
\ell_{sys}(X)
be the length of the shortest closed geodesic on
X
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we partition the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
into
\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L}
(see Definition
2.1
) and its complement. And we first give a similar result on the subset

Theorem 1.2

For
\delta>0
, let

be a function of genus

\lim\limits_{g\rightarrow\infty}L(g)=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad L(g)\leqslant\log\left((2-\delta)\log g\right),

then we have

\lim\limits_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L(g)})}{V_{g}}=1.

In particular, there exists a function

such that

\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L_{0}(g)})}{V_{g}}=1-O(g^{-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)}).

To obtain Theorem

is a closed set in

2.1.
Collar theorem

Let
\alpha
be a closed curve on a hyperbolic surface
X
, and we denote the length of the curve
\alpha
as
\ell(\alpha)
.
We consider a simple closed geodesic
\gamma
on
X
, according to the collar theorem
[
3
]
, there exists a collar
\mathcal{C}(\gamma,w(\gamma))
with width
w(\gamma)
that

\mathcal{C}(\gamma,w(\gamma))=\{p\in X|\operatorname{dist}(p,\gamma)<w(\gamma)\}

is isometric to the cylinder
(-w(\gamma),w(\gamma))\times S^{1}
with the Riemannian metric
ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+\ell^{2}(\gamma)\cosh^{2}\rho dt^{2}
where
w(\gamma)\geqslant\operatorname{arcsinh}\frac{1}{\sinh(\frac{1}{2}\ell(\gamma))}
.

If
\ell(\gamma)<2\operatorname{arcsinh}1
, then the width of its associated collar is at least
\operatorname{arcsinh}1
. Consequently, if two geodesics
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
satisfy
\ell(\alpha_{1}),\ell(\alpha_{2})<2\operatorname{arcsinh}1
, they must be disjoint.

Similarly, for a geodesic boundary
\beta
, there also exists a corresponding half collar
\mathcal{C}(\beta,w(\beta))
with width
w(\beta)
such that

\mathcal{C}(\beta,w(\beta))=\{p\in X|\operatorname{dist}(p,\beta)<w(\beta)\}

is isometric to the cylinder
[0,w(\beta))\times S^{1}
with the Riemannian metric
ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+\ell^{2}(\beta)\cosh^{2}\rho dt^{2}
, where
w(\beta)\geqslant\operatorname{arcsinh}\frac{1}{\sinh(\frac{1}{2}\ell(\gamma))}
.

Furthermore, all collars
\mathcal{C}(\gamma,\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{arcsinh}1)
and half collars
\mathcal{C}(\beta,\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{arcsinh}1)
are mutually disjoint, for geodesics
\gamma
and boundaries
\beta
with length
\ell(\gamma),\ell(\beta)<2\operatorname{arcsinh}1
. We now replace
2\operatorname{arcsinh}1
with
L
.

Remark 2.3

By definition, there exists at least one closed short geodesic on

Theorem 2.4

\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L}
is a closed subset within
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{<L}

Proof.

We consider a sequence
\{X_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L}
and
X_{n}\rightarrow X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}^{<L}
. Then there is a sequence of
q_{n}
-quasi isometries

with

and

.

For any two geodesics
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}
in
X
whose lengths
\ell(\gamma_{i})
and
\ell(\gamma_{j})
are shorter than

, we claim that
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}
are disjoint. Otherwise,
q_{n}\rightarrow 1
, there exists
n_{0}
that

q_{n_{0}}\ell(\gamma_{i}),q_{n_{0}}\ell(\gamma_{j})<L.

Let
\gamma_{i}^{n_{0}}
,
\gamma_{j}^{n_{0}}
on
X_{n_{0}}
be the geodesics which are homotopic to
f_{n_{0}}^{-1}(\gamma_{i})
,
f_{n_{0}}^{-1}(\gamma_{j})
, respectively, with lengths satisfying

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma_{i}^{n_{0}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\ell(f_{n_{0}}^{-1}(\gamma_{i}))\leqslant q_{n_{0}}\ell(\gamma_{i})<L,

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma_{j}^{n_{0}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\ell(f_{n_{0}}^{-1}(\gamma_{j}))\leqslant q_{n_{0}}\ell(\gamma_{j})<L.

Hence, the geodesics
\gamma_{i}^{n_{0}}
and
\gamma_{j}^{n_{0}}
are two geodesics whose lengths are shorter than
L
on
X_{n_{0}}
. Since

and
f_{n_{0}}^{-1}(\gamma_{j})
intersect transversely, by
[
2

\gamma_{i}^{n_{0}}
and
\gamma_{j}^{n_{0}}
intersect transversely, which is a contradiction.

Due to the discreteness of the length spectrum of a hyperbolic surface, there are only finitely many primitive closed geodesics on
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
with length less than
L
. We denote these simple geodesics by
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}
.

We already know that these geodesics are pairwise disjoint and simple. Furthermore, by collar theorem, there exists additional simple closed geodesics
\gamma_{m+1},\dots,\gamma_{3g-3}
that, together with
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}
, decompose

into pairs of pants.

Next we consider the map from the Teichüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
to the moduli space by the quotient map

, where

\mathcal{M}_{g}=\mathcal{T}_{g}/\text{Mod}_{g},

and

is the modular group.

Let
G
denote the marked cubic graph corresponding to the pants decomposition. By
[
2
, Corollary 6.2.8]
and
[
2
, Lemma 6.3.4]
, if follows that there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
X
in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and a real analytic mapping

and derive the Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates

for

that

\omega_{X}=(\ell_{\gamma_{1}},\dots,\ell_{\gamma_{3g-3}},\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{3g-3}),

where
\tau_{k}
is the twisting parameter on
\gamma_{k}
of

.

For

, there exists

such that

, corresponding the Fenchel-Nielsen parameter

.

Given any hyperbolic surface
Y\in\mathcal{M}_{g}^{<L}
and a closed geodesic
\gamma
with length
<L
on

, we define

and

By definition, we have

\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L}=\{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}|w(X)\geqslant\frac{1}{4}L\}.

According to the definitions of the mapping in
[

, Lemma 3.2.5]
, we can see that there exists a

-quasi-isometry

between

and

. Moreover,

preserves the

we have

\mathcal{C}(\gamma,\frac{1}{k_{n}}w(X_{n}))\subseteq\sigma[X_{n},X]\left(\mathcal{C}(\gamma^{(n)},w(X_{n}))\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}(\gamma,k_{n}w(X_{n}))).

Since

is an embedding in

and
\sigma[X_{n},X]
is a homeomorphism, it follows that
\mathcal{C}(\gamma,\frac{1}{k_{n}}w(X_{n}))
is a collar in

which means
w_{\gamma}\geqslant\frac{1}{k_{n}}w(X_{n})
.
By
[
2
, Theorem 6.4.2]
, for a sequence of surfaces

, the Fenchel-Nielsen parameter
\omega_{X_{n}}\rightarrow\omega_{X}
. Also
[
2
, Lemma 3.2.6]
and
[

Hence we can see that

\displaystyle w(X)

\displaystyle=\min\{\min_{1<i<m}w_{\gamma_{i}},\min_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant m}\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dist}(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\min\{\min_{1<i<m}\frac{1}{k_{n}}w(X_{n}),\min_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant m}\frac{1}{2k_{n}}\operatorname{dist}(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{k_{n}}w(X_{n}).

Now let

. Therefore, we conclude that

is a closed subset within

\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k})

|x|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}x_{i}
,
x\cdot dx=x_{1}\dots x_{k}\cdot dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{k}
and

M(\gamma):=|\{i|\gamma_{i}\text{ separates off a one-handle from }S_{g,n}\}|,

and
V_{g,n}(\Gamma,x,\beta,\mathbf{L})
is defined by

V_{g,n}(\Gamma,x,\beta,\mathbf{L}):=\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{M}\left(S_{g,n}(\gamma),\ell_{\Gamma}=x,\ell_{\beta}=\mathbf{L}\right)\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}V_{g_{i},n_{i}}(\ell_{A_{i}}).

Now we sketch how we replace
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
by
\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
in Lemma
3.2
. For an element
h\in Mod_{g,n}
, it acts on
\Gamma
by

h\cdot\Gamma=(h\cdot\gamma_{1},\dots,h\cdot\gamma_{k}).

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}
be the set of homotopy classes of elements of
Mod\cdot\Gamma
, and
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}
be the set

\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}=\{(X,\eta)|X\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma},\eta=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})\in\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}\}.

Then

(3.2)

\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}=\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})/G_{\Gamma},

where

G_{\Gamma}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}Stab(\gamma_{i})\subseteq Mod(S_{g,n}).

Now we define the covering map
\pi^{\Gamma}:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})

\pi^{\Gamma}(X,\eta):=X.

For
X\in\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
and
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k})
, we define

(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}))^{\Gamma}:=(\pi^{\Gamma})^{-1}\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})

as a subset of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}
. Due to (
3.2
), there is a covering map between
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
and
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}
, we define a subset of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
as
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
such that there exists a covering map between
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
and
(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}))^{\Gamma}
satisfying

(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}))^{\Gamma}=\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})/G_{\Gamma}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})/G_{\Gamma}.

Next we consider twisting on
\Gamma
. Let
\ell_{\Gamma}:\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}

\ell_{\Gamma}(X)=(\ell_{\gamma_{1}}(X),\dots,\ell_{\gamma_{k}}(X)).

We restrict
\ell_{\Gamma}
to the subset
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
as
\ell^{\Gamma}|_{\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})}
.

Let
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
be a
k
-type such that
a_{i}<L
for all
i=1,\dots,k
.
The Weil-Petersson volume form induces a natural measure on the preimage under the essential projection map
(\ell^{\Gamma}|_{\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})})^{-1}(a)\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
. By the assumption of
\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
, each
\gamma_{i}
has a collar with width
\geqslant\frac{1}{4}L
, which implies that
\gamma_{i}\cap\mathcal{C}(\gamma_{j},\frac{1}{4}L)=\varnothing
for
i\neq j
.

Let
\phi_{\gamma_{i}}^{t}:\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
be twisting along
\gamma_{i}
with length
t
. Then we have

\phi_{\gamma_{i}}^{t}(\mathcal{C}(\gamma_{j},\frac{1}{4}L))=\mathcal{C}(\gamma_{j},\frac{1}{4}L)

for all
i,j=1,\dots k
.
This implies the twisting map

\phi_{\gamma}^{(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\phi_{\gamma_{i}}^{t_{i}}

is a bijection from
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
to
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
, which preserves the Weil Petersson symplectic form. It means that for fixed
\mathbf{L}
, the volume of
\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
depends only on
\ell_{\Gamma}(X)
. This is precisely why we construct the subset
\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
by controlling the widths of the collars around short geodesics.
From the above discussion, the length function
\ell_{\Gamma}|_{\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})}
descends to a function
\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}|_{(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}))^{\Gamma}}
on
(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}))^{\Gamma}
as

\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}|_{(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}))^{\Gamma}}\left(X,(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})\right)=(\ell_{\eta_{1}}(X),\dots,\ell_{\eta_{k}}(X)).

Then the map
\pi=\pi^{\Gamma}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}^{-1}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
restricts to a covering map
\pi|_{U}:U\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
, where
U=\ell_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{T}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}))\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
.

Now we begin to prove Theorem
3.1
and first recall

Lemma 3.3
.

[
5
, Lemma 7.3]

For any function
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k})
, define
F_{\Gamma}:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

F_{\Gamma}(Y)=F(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}(Y)).

Then the integral of
F_{\Gamma}
over
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L}^{\Gamma})
is given by

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}}F_{\Gamma}(Y)dY=2^{-M(\Gamma)}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}_{k}}F(x)\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{M}(S_{g,n}(\gamma),\ell_{\beta}=\mathbf{L},\ell_{\Gamma}=x))xdx,

where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
, and
xdx=x_{1}\dots x_{k}dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{k}
.

Using this lemma, we take
F(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=f(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}x_{i})\cdot 1_{\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\Gamma,\ell_{\Gamma},\beta,\mathbf{L})}\circ\pi(x)
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\left(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})\right)^{\Gamma}}F_{\Gamma}(Y)dY=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})^{\Gamma}}F_{\Gamma}(Y)dY

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-M(\Gamma)}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}f(|x|)\cdot 1_{\mathcal{N}_{g,n}(\Gamma,\ell_{\Gamma},\beta,\mathbf{L})}\circ\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{\Gamma}(x)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{M}(S_{g,n}(\gamma),\ell_{\beta}=\mathbf{L},\ell_{\Gamma}=x))xdx.

If
X\in\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
such that
\ell_{\Gamma}=x
, then we cut
X
along
\gamma_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,k
and get

X-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\simeq\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}S_{g_{i},n_{i}}.

We can see for
x_{i}<L
,
\mathcal{C}(\gamma_{i},\frac{1}{4}L)\cap\mathcal{C}(\delta,\frac{1}{4}L)=\varnothing
for all
i=1,\dots,k
and
\delta
a closed geodesic on
X
with length less than

.
Hence
S_{g_{i},n_{i}}\in\mathcal{N}_{g_{i},n_{i}}^{L}(\ell_{A_{i}})
where
A_{i}=\partial S_{g_{i},n_{i}}
.
Thus

1_{\mathcal{N}_{g,n}(\Gamma,\ell_{\Gamma},\beta,\mathbf{L})}\circ\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{\Gamma}(x)\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{M}(S_{g,n}(\gamma),\ell_{\beta}=\mathbf{L},\ell_{\Gamma}=x))=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{N}_{g_{i},n_{i}}^{L}(\ell_{A_{i}})\right).

Finally, we consider the map
\pi^{\Gamma}:\left(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})\right)^{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
, then

f_{\gamma}\circ\pi^{\Gamma}=F_{\Gamma}.

Combining with

\int_{\left(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})\right)^{\Gamma}}F_{\Gamma}(Y)dY=2^{-M(\gamma)}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}f(|x|)\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\gamma,x,\ell_{\beta},\mathbf{L}))xdx

and

\int_{\left(\mathcal{N}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})\right)^{\Gamma}}F_{\Gamma}(Y)dY=|Sym(\gamma)|\int_{\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})}f_{\gamma}(X)dX,

we thus complete the Mirzakhani’s integral formula on
\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
.

4.
The volume of
\mathcal{N}_{g.n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})

In this section, we aim to determine the length of the shortest separating closed multi-geodesics on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
, where
n=g^{o(1)}
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}=y=g^{o(1)}
. Subsequently, we will compare the volume of
\mathcal{N}_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
with
V_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
. The main theorem is as following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n=n(g)
and
L_{i}<L=L(g)
for
i=1,\dots,n
, such that

\varlimsup\limits_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log n(g)}{\log g}=0,\,\varlimsup\limits_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log L(g)}{\log g}=0.

Then we have

\frac{1}{V_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}\operatorname{Vol}(E_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}))\leqslant c_{1}\frac{\widetilde{L}^{2}}{g}e^{\frac{L}{2}}+c_{2}e^{\frac{L}{2}}\frac{\widetilde{L}^{7}}{g^{2}}+c_{3}e^{2L}\frac{n^{10}}{g^{9}}+c_{4}e^{2L}\frac{n^{12}}{g^{10}},

where
\widetilde{L}=\max\{L,n\}
,
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}>0
are some positive constants, and
E_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L})
is defined as a subset of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
such that

E_{g,n}^{L}(\mathbf{L}):=\{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L});\mathcal{L}_{1}(X)\leqslant L\}.

In particular, taking
n\leqslant c\log g
and
L=(2-\epsilon)\log g
for any
0<\varepsilon<2
and
c\geqslant 0
, then we can get

\lim\limits_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{V_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}\operatorname{Vol}(E_{g,n}^{(2-\varepsilon)\log g}(\mathbf{L}))=0.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem adopts a similar approach presented in
[
8
]
. Specifically, we apply Mirzakhani’s integral formula to the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})
. Assume
\mathcal{L}_{1}(X)\leqslant L
and
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{k}
satisfies
\ell_{\gamma}(X)=\mathcal{L}_{1}(X)
, where
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}
are simple closed geodesics partitioning
X
into
S_{g_{0},k+n_{0}}\cup S_{g-g_{0}-k+1,k+n-n_{0}}
for some
(g_{0},k+n_{0})
with
|\chi(S_{g_{0},k+n_{0}})|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}|\chi(S_{g,n})|=g+\frac{n}{2}-1
.

We define
N_{g_{0},k,n_{0}}(X,L)
as the number of
k
closed geodesics
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}
that separate
X
into
S_{g_{0},k+n_{0}}\cup S_{g-g_{0}-k+1,k+n-n_{0}}
and satisfy
\ell_{\gamma}(X)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{\gamma_{i}}(X)\leqslant L
. Furthermore, we define
N_{g_{0},m}(X,L):=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}N_{g_{0},k,m-k}(X,L)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L});\mathcal{L}_{1}(X)\leqslant L\})}{V_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}\sum_{(g_{0},k,n_{0})}\operatorname{Vol}\left(X\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L});N_{g_{0},k,n_{0}}(X,L)\geqslant 1\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{(g_{0},k,n_{0})}\frac{1}{V_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{g_{0},k,n_{0}}(X,L)dX,

where the sum over all
1\leqslant 2g_{0}-2+k+n_{0}\leqslant[g-1+\frac{n}{2}]
.

By Mirzakhani integral formula
[
5
, Theorem 7.1]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{g_{0},k,n_{0}}(X,L)dX=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{n_{0}}}\frac{2^{-M(\gamma)}}{|Sym(\gamma)|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}}1_{[0,L]}(x_{1}+\dots+x_{k})

\displaystyle\times V_{g_{0},k+n_{0}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},L_{i_{1}},\dots,L_{i_{n_{0}}})

\displaystyle\times V_{g-g_{0}-k+1,k+n-n_{0}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},L_{i_{n_{0}+1}},\dots,L_{i_{n}})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{n_{0}}}\frac{2^{-M(\gamma)}}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}}1_{[0,L]}(x_{1}+\dots+x_{k})

\displaystyle\times V_{g_{0},k+n_{0}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},L_{i_{1}},\dots,L_{i_{n_{0}}})

\displaystyle\times V_{g-g_{0}-k+1,k+n-n_{0}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},L_{i_{n_{0}+1}},\dots,L_{i_{n}}),

where
i_{1}<\dots<i_{n_{0}}
and
i_{n_{0}+1}<\dots<i_{n}
,
i_{1}\dots i_{n}
is a permutation of
\{1,\dots,n\}
and
M(\gamma)
is defined by

M(\gamma):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }(g_{0},k+n_{0})=(1,1)\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
P\geqslant 2
be a fixed positive integer. For the hyperbolic surface
S_{g_{0},k+n_{0}}
, the Euler characterstic which is denoted as
\chi(S_{g_{0},k+n_{0}})
has three cases:

(i)
If
|\chi(S_{g_{0},k+n_{0}})|=1
, then
(g_{0},k+n_{0})=(1,1)
or
(0,3)
.

Case a
: If
(g_{0},k+n_{0})=(1,1)
. From Lemma
3.2
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{1,1}(X,L)dX

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{1,1,0}(X,L)dX

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}xV_{1,1}(x)V_{g-1,1+n}(x,L_{1},\dots,L_{n})dx.

By table in
[
5
]
,
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
and
[
6
, Theorem 3.5]
, we have

\displaystyle V_{1,1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}(x^{2}+4\pi^{2})

\displaystyle V_{g,n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant V_{g,n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{x_{i}}{2}}{\frac{x_{i}}{2}}

\displaystyle V_{g-1,n+1}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)V_{g,n},

which implies that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{1,1}(X,L)dX

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\frac{1}{48}(x^{2}+4\pi^{2})x\frac{\sinh\frac{x}{2}}{\frac{x}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}V_{g-1,1+n}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(L^{2}e^{\frac{L}{2}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}V_{g-1,1+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{L^{2}}{g}e^{\frac{L}{2}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}V_{g,n}.

Case b
: If
(g_{0},k+n_{0})=(0,3)
. It follows that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{0,3}(X,L)dX

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{3}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{0,k,3-k}(X,L)dX

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}}\int_{0}^{L}xV_{0,3}(x,L_{i_{1}},L_{i_{2}})V_{g,n-1}(x,L_{i_{3}},\dots,L_{i_{n}})dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2!}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0\leqslant x+y\leqslant L}xyV_{0,3}(x,y,L_{i})V_{g-1,n+1}(x,y,L_{1},\dots,\widehat{L_{i}}\dots,L_{n})dxdy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3!}\int_{0\leqslant x+y+z\leqslant L}xyzV_{0,3}(x,y,z)V_{g-2,n+3}(x,y,z,L_{1},\dots,L_{n})dxdydz

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Again, by the table in
[
5
]
and
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
:

\displaystyle V_{0,3}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle V_{g,n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant V_{g,n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{x_{i}}{2}}{\frac{x_{i}}{2}},

we can get

(4.3)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}}\int_{0}^{L}xV_{g,n-1}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}}{\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i_{1}}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i_{1}}}{2}}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i_{2}}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i_{2}}}{2}}}\frac{\sinh\frac{x}{2}}{\frac{x}{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}V_{g,n-1}O\left(n^{2}e^{\frac{L}{2}}\right).

Similarly, we estimate

and
I_{3}
as following,

(4.4)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2!}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0\leqslant x+y\leqslant L}xyV_{g-1,n+1}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{j}}{2}}{\frac{L_{j}}{2}}}{\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}}\frac{\sinh\frac{x}{2}}{\frac{x}{2}}\frac{\sinh\frac{y}{2}}{\frac{y}{2}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}V_{g,n-1}O\left(nLe^{\frac{L}{2}}\right),

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{3!}\int_{0\leqslant x+y+z\leqslant L}xyzV_{g-2,n+3}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}\frac{\sinh\frac{x}{2}}{\frac{x}{2}}\frac{\sinh\frac{y}{2}}{\frac{y}{2}}\frac{\sinh\frac{z}{2}}{\frac{z}{2}}dxdydz

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}V_{g,n-1}O\left(L^{2}e^{\frac{L}{2}}\right).

Note that
[

implies

\displaystyle V_{g-2,n+3}

\displaystyle\approx V_{g-1.n+1}\approx V_{g,n-1},

\displaystyle V_{g,n-1}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)V_{g,n}.

Hence, combining with (
4.2
)–(
4.5
), it follows that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{0,3}(X,L)dX

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}\left(O\left(\frac{n^{2}+nL+L^{2}}{g}e^{\frac{L}{2}}\right)\right)V_{g,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}\left(O\left(\frac{\widetilde{L}^{2}}{g}e^{\frac{L}{2}}\right)\right)V_{g,n},

where
\widetilde{L}=\max\{L,n\}
.

(ii)
If
|\chi(S_{(g_{0},k+n_{0})})|=m
, where
2\leqslant m\leqslant P
. It follows from
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
that
V_{g_{0},k+n_{0}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},L_{i_{1}},\dots,L_{i_{n_{0}}})

is a polynomial of degree

, where the coefficients are bounded by some constant

depending only on

. Hence, for any
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n_{0}}L_{i_{j}}\in[0,L]

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\mathbf{L})}N_{g_{0},k,n_{0}}(X,L)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{n_{0}}}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0\leqslant\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leqslant L}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}V_{g_{0},k+n_{0}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},L_{i_{1}},\dots,L_{i_{n_{0}}})

\displaystyle\times V_{g-g_{0}-k+1,k+n-n_{0}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},L_{1},\dots,\widehat{L_{i_{1}}},\dots,\widehat{L_{i_{n_{0}}}},\dots,L_{n})dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{n_{0}}}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0\leqslant\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leqslant L}c(m)L^{6g_{0}-6+2k+2n_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\frac{\sinh\frac{x_{i}}{2}}{\frac{x_{i}}{2}}V_{g-g_{0}-k+1,k+n-n_{0}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n,i\neq i_{1},\dots,i_{n_{0}}}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh\frac{L_{i}}{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{2}}\frac{c(m)L^{6g_{0}-6+2k+2n_{0}}}{k!}\binom{n}{n_{0}}V_{g-g_{0}-k+1,k+n-n_{0}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0\leqslant\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leqslant L}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\frac{\sinh\frac{x_{i}}{2}}{\frac{x_{i}}{2}}dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\binom{n}{n_{0}}

\displaystyle\leqslant n^{n_{0}},

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\frac{\sinh\frac{x_{i}}{2}}{\frac{x_{i}}{2}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}2\sinh\frac{x_{i}}{2}\leqslant e^{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}}{2}}\leqslant e^{\frac{L}{2}},

and the integral formula

\int_{0\leqslant\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}L_{i}\leqslant L}dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}=\frac{L^{k}}{k!},

we combine these properties and get the following estimate

(4.7)

Our approach utilizes Mirzakhani’s integration formula
[

to analyze the distribution of short geodesics. However, the possibility of intersection arises for geodesics with length greater than

. Consequently, we restrict to the subset

of Teichmüller space

as defined in Sect.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

First observe that for a hyperbolic surface
X\in\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L}
, any two of the short closed geodesics are disjoint, and the number of the primitive short closed geodesics is at most
3g-3
.

Let
S_{k}
represent the volume of the subset of
\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L}
comprising surfaces happen with precisely
k
primitive short closed geodesics are non-separating. Given that there exists at most
g
disjoint primitive non-separating closed geodesics, it follows that

\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{N}_{g}^{<L})=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}1_{\mathcal{N}_{g}^{L}}dX\geqslant\sum_{k=1}^{g}S_{k}.

Let
G_{k}
be the integral of the number of sets
S
of
k
disjoint unoriented short geodesics, and all such geodesics are not-separating. We can see that

G_{k}=\sum_{r=k}^{g}\binom{r}{k}S_{k}=\sum_{r=1}^{g}\binom{r}{k}S_{k}.

Here,
\binom{r}{k}=0
when
r<k
. By the inclusion exclusion principle

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{r}{k}=1-(-1)^{n}\binom{r-1}{n},

it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}G_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{r=1}^{g}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{r}{k}S_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{g}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{r}{k}S_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{g}\left(1-(-1)^{n}\binom{r-1}{n}\right)S_{k}.

If we take
n=g
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{g}(-1)^{k+1}G_{k}=\sum_{r=1}^{g}S_{k}\leqslant\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{N}^{L}_{g}),

since
\binom{r-1}{g}=0
for all
r\in\{1,2,\dots,g\}
.

In particular, if
n
is an even number, then

(5.1)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}G_{k}=\sum_{r=1}^{g}\left(1-\binom{r-1}{n}\right)S_{k}\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{g}S_{k}\leqslant\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{N}^{L}_{g}).

For fixed positive constants
\frac{1}{2}<c_{1}<c_{2}
and
g
large enough, we can choose

By Stirling’s approximation

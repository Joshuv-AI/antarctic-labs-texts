Upper moderate deviation probabilities for the maximum of branching Brownian motion
Louis Chataignier

Abstract
It is known from [ 6 ] that the maximum of branching Brownian motion at time t t is asymptotically around an explicit function m t m_{t} , which involves a first ballistic order and a logarithmic correction. In this paper, we give an asymptotic equivalent for its upper moderate deviation probability, that is, the probability that the maximum achieves m t + x t m_{t}+x_{t} at time t t , where 1 ≪ x t ≪ t 1\ll x_{t}\ll t . We adopt a probabilistic approach that employs a modified version of the second moment method.



1
Introduction

The (binary) branching Brownian motion (BBM) is a continuous-time branching Markov process constructed as follows.
At time
t=0
, a single particle starts a standard Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}
from the origin.
After a random time that follows an exponential distribution with parameter
1
, it splits into two, or equivalently, it dies while giving birth to two children.
These new particles then repeat the same process, independently of each other.
More precisely, the children perform independent Brownian motions from the position of their parent at its death and, after independent exponential times, they in turn split into two.
For a formal construction, see
[
8
,
9
]
.
BBM has been much studied since a deep connection with the F–KPP equation was made in
[
12
,
11
,
16
]
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{t}
denote the set of particles alive at time
t
and
X_{t}(u)
the position of particle
u
at time
t
or of its ancestor alive at time
t
.
We are interested in the maximal displacement
M_{t}=\max_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}}X_{t}(u)
.
In
[
6
]
, using that
u(t,x)=\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>x)
is solution to the F–KPP equation, Bramson gave a precise estimate for
M_{t}
.
To be more precise, let us define
m_{t}=\sqrt{2}t-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}(\log t)_{+}
, where
y_{+}=\max(y,0)
is the positive part of
y
.
Bramson showed that
M_{t}-m_{t}
converges in distribution to some non-degenerate random variable as time goes to infinity.
The limit distribution was subsequently identified by Lalley and Sellke
[
14
]
.
Then, in
[
10
]
, Chauvin and Rouault gave an asymptotic equivalent for the upper large deviation probability, i.e. the probability that
M_{t}>m_{t}+xt
, with
x>0
fixed.
They also used the F–KPP equation.
Here, we study an intermediate regime, called
upper moderate deviation
.
It concerns the event where
M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t}
, with
x_{t}
any quantity that goes to infinity with time while being negligible compared to
t
.

Throughout this paper, the letters
t
,
s
, and
r
will refer to some points in time
[0,\infty)
.
Given two functions
f\geq 0
and
g>0
, we will write
f(t)\ll g(t)
if
\lim f(t)/g(t)=0
, and
f(t)\sim g(t)
if
\lim f(t)/g(t)=1
.
It will also be convenient to write
f(t)\lesssim g(t)
if there exists a constant
C
such that
f(t)\leq Cg(t)
for any
t
.
Let us clarify that if
f
and
g
depend on an additional parameter
x
, then a statement of the form “
\forall x,\forall t,f(t,x)\lesssim g(t,x)
” shall be understood as “
\exists C>0,\forall x,\forall t,f(t,x)\leq Cg(t,x)
”.

Here is the main result of this article, which directly follows from Propositions
6.1
and
7.1
below.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfying
1\ll x_{t}\ll t
as
t\to\infty
, we have the asymptotic equivalent
\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t})\sim C^{*}\gamma_{t}(x_{t})
as
t\to\infty
, where, for
x>0
,

\gamma_{t}(x)=x\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}x-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{x\log t}{t}}\text{ and }C^{*}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}y}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell}>\sqrt{2}\ell+y}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(1)

Theorem
1.1
will directly follow from two intermediate asymptotic equivalents, stated in Propositions
6.1
and
7.1
.

Since the theorem is stated for general choices of
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, an argument by contradiction shows that the equivalent holds uniformly for
x_{t}
, in the following sense.

Corollary 1.2
.

For any
(a_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(b_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
1\ll a_{t}<b_{t}\ll t
as
t\to\infty
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[a_{t},b_{t}]}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\frac{\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x)}{C^{*}\gamma_{t}(x)}-1}}\right|=0.

As observed by Bovier and Hartung in
[
4
]
, it is possible to adapt the proofs of
[
2
, Proposition 4.4]
and
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
to show Theorem
1.1
.
Such an argument relies on a sharp estimate for solutions to the F–KPP equation.
One can find this estimate in
[
2
, Proposition 4.3]
, which is itself based on
[
6
, Proposition 8.3]
.
Here, however, we propose a probabilistic approach that employs a modified version of the second moment method.
We adapt arguments from Bramson, Ding, and Zeitouni
[
7
]
, where the authors studied the asymptotic behavior of the maximum of branching random walk.
A first benefit of this approach is to give some understanding of the process when it performs upper moderate deviation, especially concerning the trajectories of the particles.
Another one is to establish a method that applies to other models, such as branching random walk.
Finally, our proof is fairly self-contained: it only requires some Brownian estimates as well as the many-to-one and many-to-two formulas.

2
Brownian estimates

From now on, we consider
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a standard Brownian motion independent of the BBM.
Classically, for any
x_{1},x_{2}>0
and any
t>0
, we have the following measure equality on the domain
\{y\in\mathbb{R}:y<x_{2}\}
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall s\leq t,B_{s}\leq\frac{t-s}{t}x_{1}+\frac{s}{t}x_{2},B_{t}\in\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-2x_{1}(x_{2}-y)/t}}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2t}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(2)

On the complementary domain
\{y\in\mathbb{R}:y\geq x_{2}\}
, the measure on the left-hand side is just zero.
Equation
2
is a simple consequence of
[
5
, Lemma 2]
and of the fact that a Brownian motion is the sum of
(\frac{s}{t}B_{t})_{s\leq t}
and an independent Brownian bridge.
We deduce the following upper and lower bounds.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

For any
x_{1},x_{2}>0
and any
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall s\leq t,B_{s}\leq\frac{t-s}{t}x_{1}+\frac{s}{t}x_{2},B_{t}\in\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right)\lesssim\frac{x_{1}(x_{2}-y)}{t^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

on the domain
\{y\in\mathbb{R}:y<x_{2}\}
.

(ii)

For any
x_{1},x_{2}>0
and any
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall s\leq t,B_{s}\leq\frac{t-s}{t}x_{1}+\frac{s}{t}x_{2},B_{t}\in\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right)\gtrsim\frac{x_{1}(x_{2}-y)}{t^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

on the domain
\{y\in\mathbb{R}:y<x_{2}\text{ and }x_{1}(x_{2}-y)\leq t\}
.

In the next section, we will also need to deal with a slight modification of the barrier.

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
and
K>0
.
Define
\delta_{t}(s)=K\min(s^{\alpha},(t-s)^{\alpha})
.
Then there exist
C=C(\alpha,K)>0
and
t_{0}=t_{0}(\alpha,K)>0
such that, for any
x_{1},x_{2}>0
and any
t\geq t_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall s\leq t,B_{s}\leq\frac{t-s}{t}x_{1}+\frac{s}{t}x_{2}+\delta_{t}(s),B_{t}\in\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right)\leq C\frac{(x_{1}+1)(x_{2}-y+1)}{t^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

on the domain
\{y\in\mathbb{R}:y<x_{2}\}
.

In the case
x_{1}(x_{2}-y)\leq t
, Lemma
2.2
is a direct consequence of
[
13
, Lemma 2.7]
.
It suffices to drop the barrier to get the case
x_{1}(x_{2}-y)>t
.

3
A first upper bound

Recall that
\gamma_{t}(x)
is defined in (
1
).
We begin with the following upper bound.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
x\geq 1
and any
t\geq 2
, we have
\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x)\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x)
.

Lemma
3.1
is stated in
[
1
, Corollary 10]
whose proof relies on F–KPP estimates from
[
6
]
.
Here, we choose to present a probabilistic proof inspired by
[
15
]
, that treated the case
x\in[1,\sqrt{t}]
.

Proof.

If
x\geq t
, then, by the well-known many-to-one formula (see e.g.
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
),

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x)\leq\mathrm{e}^{t}\mathbb{P}(B_{t}>m_{t}+x)\leq\mathrm{e}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{2\pi}x}\mathrm{e}^{-(m_{t}+x)^{2}/2t}\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x).

Now, assume
x<t
.
Consider the barriers
f_{t}(s)=m_{t}-m_{t-s}+x
and
\widetilde{f}_{t}(s)=f_{t}(s)-\sqrt{2}s
.
To simplify notations, assume that
t
is an integer.
Applying successively the union bound and the many-to-one formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{k+1}}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall s\leq k,X_{s}(u)\leq f_{t}(s)\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\exists r\in[k,k+1],X_{r}(u)>f_{t}(r)\}}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\mathrm{e}^{k+1}\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq f_{t}(s),\exists r\in[k,k+1],B_{r}>f_{t}(r)).

(3)

Let us show that, for each
k=0,1,\ldots,t-1
,

\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq f_{t}(s),\exists r\in[k,k+1],B_{r}>f_{t}(r))\lesssim\frac{\gamma_{t}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-k}t^{3/2}}{(t-k)^{3/2}(k+1)^{3/2}}.

(4)

If
k=0
, then the left-hand side of (
4
) is bounded by
\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{r\leq 1}B_{r}>x}}\right)\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x)
.
Now, assume
1\leq k\leq t-1
.
Since the function
s\mapsto f_{t}(s)
is increasing, the left-hand side of (
4
) is bounded by

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq f_{t}(s),\sup_{r\in[k,k+1]}(B_{r}-B_{k})>f_{t}(k)-B_{k}}}\right).

We then decompose the above probability over the values of
B_{k}
, we apply the Markov property, and we use that the supremum of a Brownian motion between
0
and
1
has same law as
|B_{1}|
.
This allows us to bound the left-hand side of (
4
) by

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq f_{t}(s),B_{k}\in f_{t}(k)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\mathbb{P}(|B_{1}|>y).

(5)

Using the Girsanov transform, we rewrite the first factor in the above integral as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}B_{k}-k}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq\widetilde{f}_{t}(s),B_{k}\in\widetilde{f}_{t}(k)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\}}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{3/2}}{(t-k)^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}x+\sqrt{2}y-k}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq\widetilde{f}_{t}(s),B_{k}\in\widetilde{f}_{t}(k)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{3/2}}{(t-k)^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}x+\sqrt{2}y-k}\frac{(x+1)(y+1)}{k^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{f}_{t}(k)-y}}\right)^{2}/2k}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

(6)

by Lemma
2.2
.
Then we expand the exponent, we rewrite
-\frac{x^{2}}{2k}=-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}-\frac{x^{2}(t-k)}{2kt}
, and we use that
\frac{1}{k}\log\frac{t}{t-k}\leq\frac{1}{t}\log t
.
Doing so, we obtain

\eqref{eq:upper_bound_decomposition}\lesssim\frac{t^{3/2}}{(t-k)^{3/2}k^{3/2}}\gamma_{t}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}(t-k)}{2kt}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(y+1)\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}y+xy/k-y^{2}/2k-y^{2}/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right).

By completing the square in the integral and then applying some algebraic manipulations, one can bound the factor in brackets, up to a multiplicative constant, by

\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}(t-k)}{2kt}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x}{k}+1}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{k}{2(k+1)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sqrt{2}+\frac{x}{k}}}\right)^{2}}\lesssim\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x}{k}+1}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2k^{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{k^{2}}{k+1}-\frac{k^{2}}{t}}}\right)+\sqrt{2}\frac{x}{k}}.

(7)

If
1\leq k\leq\frac{t}{4}
, then
\frac{k^{2}}{k+1}-\frac{k^{2}}{t}\geq\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k}{4}\geq\frac{1}{4}
and
\eqref{eq:upper_bound_bounded_integral}\lesssim h(x/k)
, where
h:z\mapsto(z+1)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}+\sqrt{2}z}
is a bounded function.
If
k>\frac{t}{4}
, then
\frac{k^{2}}{k+1}-\frac{k^{2}}{t}\geq 0
and
\frac{x}{k}<4\frac{x}{t}<4
, so we still have a constant bound.
In both cases,

\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}(t-k)}{2kt}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(y+1)\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}y+xy/k-y^{2}/2k-y^{2}/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\lesssim 1.

(8)

Hence (
4
).
Now, inserting (
4
) in (
3
), we obtain

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x)\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x)\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{t^{3/2}}{(t-k)^{3/2}(k+1)^{3/2}}\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and any
t\geq 2
,
\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x)\lesssim(1+x_{+})\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}x}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

For any
t\geq 2
, any
\ell\in[2,t/\log t]
, and any
y\geq-\log\ell+1
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}\ell+y}}\right)\lesssim\frac{y+\log\ell}{\ell^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}y-\frac{y^{2}}{2\ell}+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{y\log t}{t}}.

Proof.

It suffices to apply Lemma
3.1
at time
\ell
and with
x=y+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\log\ell-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\ell\log t}{t}
.
∎

4
Non-contributing particles

From now on, we consider
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
1\ll x_{t}\ll t
as
t\to\infty
.
Here and in the rest of the paper, it will be convenient to use the following straight barriers rather than
f_{t}(s)
and
\widetilde{f}_{t}(s)
,

g_{t}(s)=\frac{m_{t}}{t}s+x_{t}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)=g_{t}(s)-\sqrt{2}s=x_{t}-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\log t}{t}s.

(9)

Let us introduce an arbitrary
(\ell_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
1\ll\ell_{t}<t
as
t\to\infty
.
We will gradually add upper bounds to
\ell_{t}
as we progress through the paper.
What matters is only that this function diverges to infinity slowly enough.
To simplify notations, let us abbreviate
\ell=\ell_{t}
.
Define

G_{t}=\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t-\ell}}\{\exists s\leq t-\ell,X_{s}(u)>g_{t}(s),\max_{v\geq u}X_{t}(v)>m_{t}+x_{t}\}.

The following proposition together with the upcoming Lemma
5.1
tell that particles contributing to the upper moderate deviations tipically stay below the barrier
g_{t}(s)
until time
s=t-\ell
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
1\ll\ell<t
, then
\mathbb{P}(G_{t})\ll\gamma_{t}(x_{t})
.

Proof.

To simplify notations, assume that
t-\ell
is an integer.
By the union bound, the Markov property, and the many-to-one formula,

\mathbb{P}(G_{t})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{t-\ell-1}\mathrm{e}^{k+1}u_{t}(k),

(10)

where

u_{t}(k)=\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq g_{t}(s),\exists r\in[k,k+1],B_{r}>g_{t}(r),M_{t-k-1}>m_{t}+x_{t}-B_{k+1}).

Consider
\varepsilon>0
and
\eta\in(0,1)
to be determined later.
We decompose the above sum into three, according if
k\in[0,\varepsilon x_{t}-1)
,
k\in[\varepsilon x_{t}-1,\eta t]
, or
k\in(\eta t,t-\ell-1]
.
1
1

1

The argument still works if we replace the first threshold
\varepsilon x_{t}-1
with any
a_{t}
such that
1\ll a_{t}<\varepsilon x_{t}
.

First, if
k\in[0,\varepsilon x_{t}-1)
, then we have the following rough bound

u_{t}(k)\leq\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{r\leq k+1}B_{r}>x_{t}}}\right)\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x_{t}^{2}}{2(k+1)}}\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x_{t}}{2\varepsilon}}.

Hence,

\sum_{k=0}^{\varepsilon x_{t}-1}\mathrm{e}^{k+1}u_{t}(k)\lesssim\varepsilon x_{t}\mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon x_{t}-\frac{x_{t}}{2\varepsilon}}\ll\gamma_{t}(x_{t}),

(11)

as soon as
\varepsilon
is small enough.

Now, assume
k\in[\varepsilon x_{t}-1,\eta t]
.
Since the function
s\mapsto g_{t}(s)
is increasing,
u_{t}(k)
is bounded by

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq g_{t}(s),\sup_{r\in[k,k+1]}B_{r}>g_{t}(k),M_{t-k-1}>m_{t}+x_{t}-\sup_{r\in[k,k+1]}B_{r}}}\right)\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{y}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq g_{t}(s),B_{k}\in g_{t}(k)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\mathbb{P}(|B_{1}|\in\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z)\\
\times\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{t-k-1}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}(t-k)-z+y}}\right).

(12)

We control the first factor in the above integral similarly to (
6
), using the Girsanov transform and the upper bound in Lemma
2.1
,

\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq k,B_{s}\leq g_{t}(s),B_{k}\in g_{t}(k)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\lesssim\frac{x_{t}y}{k^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}(\widetilde{g}_{t}(k)-y)-k-(\widetilde{g}_{t}(k)-y)^{2}/2k}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(13)

For the second one, we use the explicit distribution of
|B_{1}|
on
[0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{P}(|B_{1}|\in\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

(14)

For the third one, we simply bound

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{t-k-1}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}(t-k)-z+y}}\right)\leq 1.

(15)

We insert the three bounds (
13
), (
14
), (
15
) in (
12
), we expand the exponents, and we rewrite
-\frac{x_{t}^{2}}{2k}=-\frac{x_{t}^{2}}{2t}-\frac{x_{t}^{2}(t-k)}{2kt}
.
Doing so, we obtain

u_{t}(k)\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-k+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}k}}{k^{3/2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x_{t}^{2}(t-k)}{2kt}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{\frac{x_{t}}{k}y-y^{2}/2k}\int_{y}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}z-z^{2}/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right).

By completing the square in the integral over
z
and then applying (
8
), we see that the factor in brackets is bounded by a constant.
Moreover, since
\varepsilon x_{t}<k\leq\eta t
, we have
\frac{\log k}{k}\geq\frac{\log(\eta t)}{\eta t}
and then
\frac{\log t}{t}k\leq(\eta+o(1))\log k
, where
o(1)
is a function that converges to
0
as
t\to\infty
, uniformly in all the other variables.
It follows that

u_{t}(k)\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-k}}{k^{(1-\eta-o(1))3/2}}.

Then,

\sum_{k=\varepsilon x_{t}-1}^{\eta t}\mathrm{e}^{k+1}u_{t}(k)\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\sum_{k=\varepsilon x_{t}-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{(1-\eta-o(1))3/2}}\ll\gamma_{t}(x_{t}),

(16)

as soon as
\eta+o(1)<1/3
.

Finally, the case
k\in(\eta t,t-\ell-1]
can be treated in the same way as
k\in[\varepsilon x_{t}-1,\eta t]
but with a sharper control than (
15
).
Thanks to Corollary
3.2
, we bound the left-hand side of (
15
) by

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{t-k-1}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}(t-k-1)-z+y}}\right)\lesssim\log(t-k-1)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-k-1)}}{(t-k-1)^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}(z-y)}.

Inserting this new bound, together with (
13
) and (
14
), in (
12
), we obtain

u_{t}(k)\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\mathrm{e}^{-k}\frac{\log(t-k-1)t^{3/2}}{k^{3/2}(t-k-1)^{3/2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x_{t}^{2}(t-k)}{2kt}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{\frac{x_{t}}{k}y-y^{2}/2k}\int_{y}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}z-z^{2}/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right).

As in the case
k\in[\varepsilon x_{t}-1,\eta t]
, the factor in brackets is bounded by a constant.
It follows that

\sum_{k=\eta t}^{t-\ell}\mathrm{e}^{k+1}u_{t}(k)\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\sum_{k=\eta t}^{t-\ell}\frac{\log(t-k-1)t^{3/2}}{k^{3/2}(t-k-1)^{3/2}}\ll\gamma_{t}(x_{t}).

(17)

By (
10
), (
11
), (
16
), and (
17
), we have
\mathbb{P}(G_{t})\ll\gamma_{t}(x_{t})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

5
A first lower bound

We still consider
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
1\ll x_{t}\ll t
as
t\to\infty
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists
t_{0}>0
such that, for any
t\geq t_{0}
,
\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t})\gtrsim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})
.

Proof.

Here again, we will make use of the barriers
g_{t}(s)
and
\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)
introduced in (
9
).
Define, for
u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}
,

H_{t}^{(u)}=\{\forall s\leq t,X_{s}(u)\leq g_{t}(s),X_{t}(u)>m_{t}+x_{t}-1\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{t}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}}\mathds{1}_{H_{t}^{(u)}}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t}-1)\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t}-1\}}\Delta_{t}}}\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}^{2}}}\right]}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}}}\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}^{2}}}\right]}.

(18)

Using successively the many-to-one formula, the Girsanov transform, and the lower bound in Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{t}\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq t,B_{s}\leq g_{t}(s),B_{t}>g_{t}(t)-1)

\displaystyle\geq t^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}x_{t}}\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq t,B_{s}\leq\widetilde{g}_{t}(s),B_{t}>\widetilde{g}_{t}(t)-1)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\gamma_{t}(x_{t}).

(19)

The study of
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}^{2}}}\right]
is more involved.
We start by applying the well-known many-to-two formula (see e.g.
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
),

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}^{2}}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}}}\right]+2\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{2t-s}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{1,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{1,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\\
\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{2,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{2,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\end{subarray}}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

(20)

where
B^{1,s}
and
B^{2,s}
are two Brownian motions that coincide on
[0,s]
and are independent afterward.
By the Markov property,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{1,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{1,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\\
\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{2,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{2,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\end{subarray}}}\right)=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r)\}}\phi_{t,s}(B_{s})^{2}}}\right],

with

\displaystyle\phi_{t,s}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\forall r\leq t-s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r+s)-z,B_{t-s}>g_{t}(t)-z-1)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}x_{t}+\sqrt{2}z-t-s}\frac{g_{t}(s)-z}{(t-s)^{3/2}}\mathds{1}_{\{z\leq g_{t}(s)\}},

(21)

where we have once again used the Girsanov transform and the upper bound in Lemma
2.1
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2t-s}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{1,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{1,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\\
\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{2,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{2,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\end{subarray}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-2\sqrt{2}x_{t}-3s}}{(t-s)^{3}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r)\}}\mathrm{e}^{2\sqrt{2}B_{s}}(g_{t}(s)-B_{s})^{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-2\sqrt{2}x_{t}}}{(t-s)^{3}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq\widetilde{g}_{t}(r)\}}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}B_{s}}(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-B_{s})^{2}}}\right].

Decomposing over the values of
B_{s}
, we rewrite the last expectation as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq\widetilde{g}_{t}(r),B_{s}\in\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\}}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}B_{s}}(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-B_{s})^{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y)}y^{2}\mathbb{P}(\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq\widetilde{g}_{t}(r),B_{s}\in\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x_{t}}{s^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}x_{t}+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{x_{t}\log t}{t}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}s}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}y-(x_{t}-y)^{2}/2s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

(22)

by Lemma
2.1
.
Note that the last integral is bounded by

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}y-(x_{t}-y)^{2}/2t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\mathrm{e}^{-x_{t}^{2}/2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-(\sqrt{2}+o(1))y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-x_{t}^{2}/2t}.

It follows that

\int_{1}^{t-1}\mathrm{e}^{2t-s}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{1,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{1,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\\
\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{2,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{2,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\end{subarray}}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\int_{1}^{t-1}\frac{t^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-s)}}{(t-s)^{3}s^{3/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Now, use that
\frac{\log t}{t}(t-s)\leq\log(t-s)
for
t
large enough and
s\leq t-1
to bound

\int_{1}^{t-1}\frac{t^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-s)}}{(t-s)^{3}s^{3/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{1}^{t-1}\frac{t^{3/2}}{(t-s)^{3/2}s^{3/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq 2\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s^{3/2}}=4.

We treat the integration over
s\in[0,1)
similarly, except that we use the bound

\mathbb{P}(\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq\widetilde{g}_{t}(r),B_{s}\in\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\leq\mathbb{P}(B_{s}\in\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y)^{2}/2s}}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

instead of Lemma
2.1
in (
22
).
We treat the integration over
s\in(t-1,t]
similarly as
s\in[1,t-1]
, except that we only use the Girsanov transform to bound
\phi_{t,s}(z)\lesssim t^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}x_{t}+\sqrt{2}z-t-s}
instead of (
21
).
Finally, we obtain

\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{2t-s}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{1,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{1,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\\
\forall r\leq t,B_{r}^{2,s}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{t}^{2,s}>g_{t}(t)-1\end{subarray}}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t}).

Therefore, by (
19
) and (
20
), we have
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}^{2}}}\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}}}\right]
.
The lower bound (
18
) then becomes
\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t}-1)\gtrsim\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{t}}}\right]
.
By (
19
), this concludes the proof.
∎

6
Reduction to an expectation

In this section, we consider an arbitrary
\ell=\ell_{t}
such that
1\ll\ell<\min(t/\log t,x_{t}^{5})
.
We stress the fact that, contrary to Section
4
, the bound
\ell<t
will not be sufficient here.
We need
\ell<t/\log t
to apply Corollary
3.3
in the proof of Lemma
6.3
.
The assumption
\ell<x_{t}^{5}
is made to have an easy control of (
34
) below, but it is dispensable.
Define, for
u\in\mathcal{N}_{t-\ell}
,

E_{t}^{(u)}=\{\forall s\leq t-\ell,X_{s}(u)\leq g_{t}(s),\max_{v\geq u}X_{t}(v)>m_{t}+x_{t}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{t}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t-\ell}}\mathds{1}_{E_{t}^{(u)}}.

(23)

Note that these objects depend on the choice of
(\ell_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
The main result of this section is the following equivalent.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
\ell
is chosen as above, then
\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t})\sim\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]
as
t\to\infty
.

We will need two lemmas.

Lemma 6.2
.

There exists
t_{0}>0
such that, for any
t\geq t_{0}
,
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]\gtrsim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})
.

Proof.

We have
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]\geq\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t})-\mathbb{P}(G_{t})
.
Then, it suffices to apply Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

We have
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}^{2}}}\right]\sim\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]
.

Proof.

Since

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}^{2}}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{N}(t-\ell),u\neq v}\mathds{1}_{E_{t}^{(u)}}\mathds{1}_{E_{t}^{(v)}}}}\right],

it suffices to show that the second term is negligible compared to
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]
.
By the many-to-two formula and the Markov property,

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{N}(t-\ell),u\neq v}\mathds{1}_{E_{t}^{(u)}}\mathds{1}_{E_{t}^{(v)}}}}\right]=2\int_{0}^{t-\ell}\mathrm{e}^{2(t-\ell)-s}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r)\}}\psi_{t,s}(B_{s})^{2}}}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

(24)

with

\psi_{t,s}(z)=\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall r\leq t-\ell-s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r+s)-z,M_{\ell}>m_{t}+x_{t}-B_{t-\ell-s}-z}}\right).

(25)

We first deal with the integration over
s\in[\ell^{1/3},t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}]
.
Slight modifications of the argument will handle the regions
s\in[0,\ell^{1/3})
and
s\in(t-\ell-\ell^{1/3},t-\ell]
.
Decomposing over the values of
B_{s}
, we rewrite

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r)\}}\psi_{t,s}(B_{s})^{2}}}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{s}\in g_{t}(s)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right)\psi_{t,s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g_{t}(s)-y}}\right)^{2}.

(26)

Using the Girsanov transform and Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r),B_{s}\in g_{t}(s)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right)\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y}}\right)-s}\frac{x_{t}y\mathrm{e}^{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y}}\right)^{2}/2s}}{s^{3/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(27)

Besides, decomposing over the values of
B_{t-\ell-s}
in the definition (
25
), we rewrite
\psi_{t,s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g_{t}(s)-y}}\right)
as

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall r\leq t-\ell-s,B_{r}\leq\frac{m_{t}}{t}r+y,B_{t-\ell-s}\in\frac{m_{t}}{t}(t-\ell-s)+y-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}}\right)\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}\ell+z}}\right).

The first factor in the above integral can be controlled by using the Girsanov transform and Lemma
2.1
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\forall r\leq t-\ell-s,B_{r}\leq\frac{m_{t}}{t}r+y,B_{t-\ell-s}\in\frac{m_{t}}{t}(t-\ell-s)+y-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}}\right)\\
\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)+y-z}}\right)-(t-\ell-s)}\frac{yz\mathrm{e}^{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)+y-z}}\right)^{2}/2(t-\ell-s)}}{(t-\ell-s)^{3/2}}.

(28)

As for the second factor, it is controlled by Corollary
3.3
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}\ell+z}}\right)\lesssim\frac{z+\log\ell}{\ell^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}z-\frac{z^{2}}{2\ell}+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{z\log t}{t}}.

(29)

Combining (
28
) and (
29
), we obtain that
\psi_{t,s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g_{t}(s)-y}}\right)
is bounded, up to a multiplicative constant, by

\frac{y\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)-(\sqrt{2}-o(1))y-(t-\ell-s)}}{(t-\ell-s)^{3/2}\ell^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}z(z+\log\ell)\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2\ell}\mathrm{e}^{-(y-z)^{2}/2(t-\ell-s)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z,

(30)

where we denote by
o(1)
any function that converges to
0
as
t\to\infty
, uniformly in all the other variables.
We can bound the last exponential by
1
.
It follows that

\psi_{t,s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g_{t}(s)-y}}\right)\lesssim\frac{y\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)-(\sqrt{2}-o(1))y-(t-\ell-s)}}{(t-\ell-s)^{3/2}}.

(31)

Then we insert (
27
) and (
31
) into (
26
).
Multiplying the resulting bound by
\mathrm{e}^{2(t-\ell)-s}
and integrating over
[\ell^{1/3},t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}]
yields

\int_{\ell^{1/3}}^{t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}}\mathrm{e}^{2(t-\ell)-s}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r)\}}\psi_{t,s}(B_{s})^{2}}}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
\lesssim x_{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}x_{t}}\int_{\ell^{1/3}}^{t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell)+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)}}{s^{3/2}(t-\ell-s)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-(\sqrt{2}-o(1))y-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y}}\right)^{2}/2s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

We can use that, for
t
large enough and for
s\in[\ell^{1/3},t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}]
,

\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell)+\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)\leq\log(t-\ell)+\log(t-\ell-s),

(32)

and

\mathrm{e}^{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y}}\right)^{2}/2s}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y}}\right)^{2}/2t}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x_{t}^{2}}{2t}+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{x_{t}\log t}{t}+o(1)y+o(1)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\ell^{1/3}}^{t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2(t-\ell)-s}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r)\}}\psi_{t,s}(B_{s})^{2}}}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\int_{\ell^{1/3}}^{t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}}\frac{(t-\ell)^{3/2}}{s^{3/2}(t-\ell-s)^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-(\sqrt{2}-o(1))y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\ell^{-1/6}.

(33)

We treat the region
s\in[0,\ell^{1/3})
in the same way, except that we bound the left-hand side of (
27
) by

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(B_{s}\in g_{t}(s)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right)=\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y}}\right)-s}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{g}_{t}(s)-y}}\right)^{2}/2s}}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

This yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell^{1/3}}\mathrm{e}^{2(t-\ell)-s}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r)\}}\psi_{t,s}(B_{s})^{2}}}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\gamma_{t}(x_{t})}{x_{t}}\int_{0}^{\ell^{1/3}}\frac{(t-\ell)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{s}(t-\ell-s)^{3/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\frac{\ell^{1/6}}{x_{t}}.

(34)

Concerning the region
s\in(t-\ell-\ell^{1/3},t-\ell]
, we do the same as for
s\in[\ell^{1/3},t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}]
with three modifications.
First, dropping the barrier, we bound the left-hand side of (
28
), up to a multiplicative constant, by

\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)+y-z}}\right)-(t-\ell-s)}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)+y-z}}\right)^{2}/2(t-\ell-s)}}{\sqrt{t-\ell-s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

Then, in the counterpart of (
30
), we keep
\mathrm{e}^{-(y-z)^{2}/2(t-\ell-s)}
instead of
\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2\ell}
.
These first two modifications allow us to replace the inequality (
31
) with

\psi_{t,s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g_{t}(s)-y}}\right)\lesssim\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)-(\sqrt{2}-o(1))y-(t-\ell-s)}}{\ell^{3/2}}(\ell^{1/6}+y).

Finally, instead of (
32
), we use that

\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell)+\frac{\log t}{t}(t-\ell-s)\leq\log(t-\ell)+o(1).

These three modifications yield

\displaystyle\int_{t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}}^{t-\ell}\mathrm{e}^{2(t-\ell)-s}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\forall r\leq s,B_{r}\leq g_{t}(r)\}}\psi_{t,s}(B_{s})^{2}}}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\int_{t-\ell-\ell^{1/3}}^{t-\ell}\frac{(t-\ell)^{3/2}}{s^{3/2}\ell^{8/3}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\ell^{-7/3}.

(35)

By (
33
), (
34
), (
35
), and Lemma
6.2
, the right-hand side of (
24
) is negligible compared to
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

First,

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t})\leq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]+\mathbb{P}(G_{t})\sim\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right],

(36)

by Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
6.2
.
Besides, by applying successively Cauchy-Schwarz and Lemma
6.3
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t})\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}^{2}}}\right]}\sim\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right].

(37)

Combining (
36
) and (
37
), we deduce the equivalent in Proposition
6.1
.
∎

7
Proof of the equivalent

The main result of this section is the following equivalent.

Proposition 7.1
.

As soon as
1\ll x_{t}\ll t
and
1\ll\ell\ll\min(t/\log t,x_{t}^{5},t^{2}/x_{t}^{2})
, we have
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]\sim C^{*}\gamma_{t}(x_{t})
, where
C^{*}
is the constant defined in Theorem
1.1
.

To prove Propositions
6.1
, we start by identifying the positions at time
t-\ell
of the particles that mainly contribute to
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]
.
To this end, similarly to (
23
), define, for
A
any Borel set of
[0,\infty)
and
u\in\mathcal{N}_{t-\ell}
,

E_{t,A}^{(u)}=E_{t}^{(u)}\cap\{X_{t-\ell}(u)\in g_{t}(t-\ell)-A\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{t,A}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t-\ell}}\mathds{1}_{E_{t,A}^{(u)}}.

Consider
a(s)
and
b(s)
any increasing functions such that
1\ll a(\ell)\ll\sqrt{\ell}\ll b(\ell)\ll\min(\sqrt{t},t/x_{t})
.
Note that such functions exist as soon as the conditions of Proposition
7.1
are satisfied.
The following lemma shows that
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]
is mainly supported by particles whose distance from the barrier
g_{t}(s)
is of order
\sqrt{\ell}
at time
s=t-\ell
.

Lemma 7.2
.

With the above choices of
\ell
,
a
, and
b
, taking
I(\ell)=[a(\ell),b(\ell)]
, we have

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]\sim\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t,I(\ell)}}}\right]\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}y}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}\ell+y}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(38)

Proof.

Using the Markov property, the many-to-one formula, and the Girsanov transform, we rewrite
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t,A}}}\right]
as

\int_{A}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}(\widetilde{g}_{t}(t-\ell)-y)}\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq t-\ell,B_{s}\leq\widetilde{g}_{t}(s),B_{t-\ell}\in\widetilde{g}_{t}(t-\ell)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}\ell+y}}\right),

(39)

for any Borel set
A
.
Then, to show the left-hand equivalent in (
38
), it suffices to show that (
39
) is negligible compared to
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]
when
A=[0,\infty)\setminus[a(\ell),b(\ell)]
.
By Lemma
2.1
,

\mathbb{P}(\forall s\leq t-\ell,B_{s}\leq\widetilde{g}_{t}(s),B_{t-\ell}\in\widetilde{g}_{t}(t-\ell)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)\lesssim\frac{x_{t}y}{(t-\ell)^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-(\widetilde{g}_{t}(t-\ell)-y)^{2}/2(t-\ell)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Inserting this and Corollary

\frac{x_{t}}{(t-\ell)^{3/2}\ell^{3/2}}\int_{A}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}(\widetilde{g}_{t}(t-\ell)-y)}y\mathrm{e}^{-(\widetilde{g}_{t}(t-\ell)-y)^{2}/2(t-\ell)}(y+\log\ell)\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2}y-\frac{y^{2}}{2\ell}+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{y\log t}{t}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
\lesssim\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\ell^{-3/2}\int_{A}y(y+\log\ell)\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2(t-\ell)+x_{t}y/(t-\ell)-y^{2}/2\ell}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}}\right),

(40)

by definition of
\widetilde{g}_{t}(t-\ell)
and choice of
\ell
.
But, since
a(\ell)\ll\sqrt{\ell}\ll t/x_{t}
,

\ell^{-3/2}\int_{0}^{a(\ell)}y(y+\log\ell)\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2(t-\ell)+x_{t}y/(t-\ell)-y^{2}/2\ell}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\ell^{-3/2}\int_{0}^{a(\ell)}y(y+\log\ell)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

and this quantity vanishes as
t\to\infty
.
Also,

\ell^{-3/2}\int_{b(\ell)}^{\infty}y(y+\log\ell)\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2(t-\ell)+x_{t}y/(t-\ell)-y^{2}/2\ell}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\ell^{-3/2}\int_{b(\ell)}^{\infty}y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{x_{t}y/(t-\ell)-y^{2}/2\ell}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

With the change of variable

, we see that this quantity vanishes as

\ell
and

Besides,
\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\lesssim\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]

.

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t,I(\ell)}}}\right]\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\gamma_{t}(x_{t})\int_{I(\ell)}y\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}y}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}\ell+y}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

3.3

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

In this proof, we specify the dependence of
\ell
on
t
to avoid any ambiguity.
By Lemma
7.2
and Proposition
6.1
,

\frac{\mathbb{P}(M_{t}>m_{t}+x_{t})}{\gamma_{t}(x_{t})}\sim\frac{\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]}{\gamma_{t}(x_{t})}\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}y}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell_{t}}>\frac{m_{t}}{t}\ell_{t}+y}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

It remains to study the convergence of these quantities.
Let us denote
u(t)
the left-hand side and
v(t,\ell_{t})
the right-hand side.
By Lemma
3.1
,
u(t)\lesssim 1
.
Therefore, the equivalent
u(t)\sim v(t,\ell_{t})
implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}(u(t)-v(t,\ell_{t}))=0
.
Since this holds for any
(\ell_{t})
that goes to
\infty
slowly enough, an argument by contradiction shows that
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\infty}|u(t)-v(t,\ell)|=0
.
But, for any
\ell>0
,
v(t,\ell)
converges as
t\to\infty
towards

v_{\ell}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}y}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M_{\ell}>\sqrt{2}\ell+y}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

It follows that
\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t)
and
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}v_{\ell}
exist and are equal.
This is what we call
C^{*}
.
Finally, this constant
C^{*}
is in
(0,\infty)
since, by Lemmas
3.1
and
5.1
, the function
u(t)
is bounded away from
0
and
\infty
.
Hence,
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Lambda_{t}}}\right]\sim C^{*}\gamma_{t}(x_{t})
.
∎

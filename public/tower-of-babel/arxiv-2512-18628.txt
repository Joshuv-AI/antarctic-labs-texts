Nested Affine Buildings and Their Group Decompositions.
Masaoki Mori

Abstract
In this paper, we construct a higher dimensional generalization of affine buildings and introduce a new structure, which we call Babel buildings . These buildings are non-connected, non-convex metric spaces of non-positive curvature. Despite their non-standard properties, Babel buildings provide an effective framework for studying the structure of groups acting on them. We analyze the metric and nesting structures of Babel buildings and derive key results regarding the group actions consistent with this new framework.

Introduction

The theory of Bruhat-Tits buildings has established itself as a fundamental framework for understanding the structure of reductive algebraic groups over local fields. In their monumental work
[
BT
]
, Bruhat and Tits constructed affine buildings as geometric analogues of symmetric spaces. A crucial feature of an affine building is that it carries a canonical metric, making it a CAT(0) space (a complete metric space of non-positive curvature). This geometric property leads to powerful results, such as the fixed point theorem for compact group actions and various decomposition theorems for the associated groups.

In recent years, there has been increasing interest in generalizing this theory to
higher-dimensional local fields
, such as the field of iterated Laurent series
k((t_{1}))\cdots((t_{n}))
. For such fields, the valuation group is no longer a subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
, but rather isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
equipped with the lexicographic order. Consequently, the associated buildings cannot be metrized by standard real numbers in a way that preserves the full valuation structure. While the combinatorial structure of such buildings has been studied (e.g., as
\Lambda
-buildings in the sense of Bennett or Bennett-Schwer-Struyv,
[
Ben1
]
,
[
Ben2
]
and
[
BSS
]
), a satisfactory metric theory analogous to the classical CAT(0) geometry has been less developed.

While the axiomatic theory of
\Lambda
-buildings provides a rigorous framework, it often obscures the geometric intuition of the space, especially when the valuation group is of higher rank (e.g.,
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
). The metric structure becomes abstract, making it difficult to visualize fundamental geometric operations such as retractions or group decompositions.Motivated by this difficulty, we adopt a constructive approach. By realizing the building as a nested sequence of lower-level buildings, we recover the geometric intuition. In our model, the algebraic residue structure corresponds precisely to the hierarchical ’zooming’ between different levels of the building. This concrete visualization allows us to prove results like the Kapranov decomposition, which are hard to perceive from the purely axiomatic viewpoint.

In this paper, we propose a new geometric approach to buildings for higher-dimensional local fields. Instead of projecting the geometry down to
\mathbb{R}
, we extend the concept of the metric itself. We introduce the ring of
hyper-real numbers
of level
n
, denoted by
{}^{n*}\mathbb{R}
, which is essentially a lexicographic product of
n
copies of
\mathbb{R}
. We define a class of buildings equipped with
{}^{n*}\mathbb{R}
-valued metrics, which we call
Babel buildings
. The name "Babel" reflects the hierarchical, nested structure of these spaces: an
n
-level Babel building can be viewed as an infinite tower of
(n-1)
-level Babel buildings, culminating in the classical affine buildings at level 1.

The main objective of this paper is to establish that Babel buildings possess "nice" geometric properties parallel to those of affine buildings, despite the hyper-real nature of the metric values.

Our first main result is the construction of a well-defined metric on the Babel building
X
. Since Babel buildings lack gallery connectedness for
n>1
, the existence of a geodesic metric satisfying the triangle inequality is non-trivial. By employing the method of retractions onto apartments, we prove:

Theorem 0.1

(Main Theorem A)
.

Let
X
be an
n
-level Babel building. There exists a canonical distance function
d_{X}\colon X\times X\longrightarrow{}^{n*}\mathbb{R}
which satisfies the triangle inequality. Furthermore, the retraction map onto any Babel apartment is distance-decreasing.

Once the metric is established, we investigate the curvature properties of
X
. Although
X
is not a CAT(0) space in the classical sense (as the metric is not real-valued), we formulate a generalized CAT(0)-inequality in terms of
{}^{n*}\mathbb{R}
. This allows us to generalize the celebrated Bruhat-Tits fixed point theorem.

Theorem 0.2

(Main Theorem B)
.

Let
X
be an
n
-level Babel building. Then
X
satisfies the
{}^{n*}\mathbb{R}
-valued CAT(0)-inequality. Consequently, if a group
G
acts isometrically on
X
and stabilizes a bounded subset
B
that admits a circumcenter, then
G
fixes the circumcenter of
B
.

Finally, we apply this geometric framework to the study of group actions. Let
\widehat{G}
be a group acting strongly transitively on
X
. We show that the geometric structure of
X
naturally induces decompositions of
\widehat{G}
that generalize the classical matrix decompositions.

Theorem 0.3

(Main Theorem C)
.

Let
\widehat{G}
be a group with a strongly transitive, type-preserving action on an
n
-level Babel building. Then
\widehat{G}
admits the following decompositions:

•

The
Bruhat decomposition
:
\widehat{G}=\coprod_{w\in\widehat{W}}\widehat{B}w\widehat{B}
.

•

The
Cartan decomposition
:
\widehat{G}=\coprod_{v\in\widehat{V}_{\mathbf{D}}}KvK
.

•

The
(i,j)
-Kapranov decomposition
: A family of decompositions relating sectors of different levels, which includes the Iwasawa decomposition as the
(0,n)
-case.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 1 is devoted to the geometry of Babel buildings, where we construct the metric and prove the metric-decreasing property of retractions and we discuss the fixed point theorem and the group decompositions. In Section 2, we discuss the relationship between our theory and the homological properties of arithmetic quotients.

Remark 1.6

Remark 1.7

Let

Remark 1.8

We describe a similarity between hyperreal fields and higher dimensional local fields. Let us define

Definition 1.9
.

(i)

A pair

Let

Proof..

Theorem 1.30
.

For any Babel apartment
A
of
X
and any chamber

of
A
, the retraction
\rho=\rho_{A,C}\colon X\longrightarrow A
is distance-decreasing; that is, the inequality

holds for any
x,y\in X
.

Proof..

First, suppose that
x,y
are vertices of
X
. Let
A^{\prime}
be a Babel apartment which contains
x,y
. We show that the geodesic defined by
[x,y]=\{z\in A^{\prime}\mid d_{A^{\prime}}(x,y)=d_{A^{\prime}}(x,z)+d_{A^{\prime}}(z,y)\}
is covered by finitely many elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{C}
. Suppose there exist
(n-1)
-level Babel sectors
{\mathfrak{C}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{C}}_{m}
such that
[x,y]\subset{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup{\mathfrak{C}}_{m}
. Then, for each
i=1,\ldots,m
, we can choose an
(n-1)
-level Babel sub-sector
{\mathfrak{C}}_{i}^{\prime}\subset{\mathfrak{C}}_{i}
such that
{\mathfrak{C}}_{i}^{\prime}
is included in some apartment belonging to
{\mathcal{A}}_{C}
. The geodesic connecting the cone points of
{\mathfrak{C}}_{i}^{\prime}
and
{\mathfrak{C}}_{i}
is covered by finitely many
(n-2)
-level Babel sectors. Consequently, we have a decomposition

{\mathfrak{C}}_{i}={\mathfrak{C}}_{i}^{\prime}\cup{\mathfrak{D}}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup{\mathfrak{D}}_{k},

where each
{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}
is an
(n-2)
-level Babel sector. Similarly, for each
j
, we can choose an
(n-2)
-level Babel sub-sector
{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}^{\prime}\subset{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}
such that
{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}^{\prime}
is included in some apartment belonging to
{\mathcal{A}}_{C}
. By repeating this argument via descending induction down to level

, we obtain the conclusion.

Based on the discussion above, we assume that
x
and
y
are contained in adjacent
m
-level Babel sectors
{\mathfrak{C}}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}
of two Babel apartments
A_{1},A_{2}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{C}
, respectively. Note that the three objects—the chamber
C
,
{\mathfrak{C}}
, and
{\mathfrak{D}}
—may not necessarily lie within the same Babel apartment.

Case 1.
First, we suppose that
{\mathfrak{C}}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}
are included in
A^{\prime}
.
Let
\varphi_{i}
denote the restriction of the retraction
\rho_{A,C}\colon X\longrightarrow A
to
A_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
There exist elements
w_{c},w_{d}\in W(\Phi)\ltimes\bigoplus_{j>m}{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{j}(\Phi^{\vee})
such that

{\mathfrak{C}}=w_{c}(\mathbf{D}_{m}),\quad{\mathfrak{D}}=w_{d}(\mathbf{D}_{m}),

where
\mathbf{D}_{m}
denotes a fundamental domain of the group
W(\Phi)\ltimes\bigoplus_{j>m}{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{j}(\Phi^{\vee})
.
For
i=1,2
, let
E_{i}
be the hyper-affine enveloping space of

, and let
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}\colon E_{i}\longrightarrow E_{A}
denote the extension of the isometry

.
Let
\tilde{\mathbf{D}}_{m}
denote the fundamental domain of
W(\Phi)\ltimes\bigoplus_{j>m}{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{j}(\Phi^{\vee})
in the enveloping space, and define

\tilde{{\mathfrak{C}}}=w_{c}(\tilde{\mathbf{D}}_{m}),\quad\tilde{{\mathfrak{D}}}=w_{d}(\tilde{\mathbf{D}}_{m}).

Consequently, the lifted sectors
\tilde{{\mathfrak{C}}}
and
\tilde{{\mathfrak{D}}}
are adjacent in the enveloping space. Let

be a point in the intersection
[x,y]_{E}\cap\overline{(\tilde{{\mathfrak{C}}}\cap\tilde{{\mathfrak{D}}})}
, where
[x,y]_{E}
denotes the geodesic segment connecting

and

within the enveloping space.
Since

lies in the intersection, we have
\tilde{\varphi}_{1}(z)=\tilde{\varphi}_{2}(z)
.
Thus, applying the triangle inequality in

, we obtain:

\displaystyle d_{A}(\rho(x),\rho(y))

\displaystyle\leq d_{E_{A}}(\tilde{\varphi}_{1}(x),\tilde{\varphi}_{1}(z))+d_{E_{A}}(\tilde{\varphi}_{2}(z),\tilde{\varphi}_{2}(y))

\displaystyle=d_{E_{1}}(x,z)+d_{E_{2}}(z,y)

\displaystyle=d_{E_{A^{\prime}}}(x,z)+d_{E_{A^{\prime}}}(z,y)

Case 2.
Next, we consider the general case where
{\mathfrak{C}}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}
are not necessarily included in

. Let
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
be sub-sectors of
{\mathfrak{C}}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}
of the same level, respectively, such that
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}\subset A^{\prime}
. Then there exist Babel sectors
{\mathfrak{E}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{E}}_{p}
and
{\mathfrak{F}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{F}}_{q}
of level strictly less than

such that

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{C}}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}\cup{\mathfrak{E}}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup{\mathfrak{E}}_{p},

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}\cup{\mathfrak{F}}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup{\mathfrak{F}}_{q},

and for any

and

and

are included in some apartments belonging to

Let

be the cone points of

respectively.
Note that the paths connecting

to

and

to

are covered by sectors of level less than

Thus we have

\displaystyle d_{A}(\rho(x),\rho(y))

\displaystyle\leq d_{A}(\rho(x),\rho(x^{\prime}))+d_{A}(\rho(x^{\prime}),\rho(y^{\prime}))+d_{A}(\rho(y^{\prime}),\rho(y))

Remark 1.32
.

We can now provide a brief description of the geometric shape of
n
-level Babel buildings. For each
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
X_{i}
be an affine building scaled by the factor
\omega_{i}
. Specifically, the metric on
X_{i}
takes values in
{}^{i*}{\mathbb{R}}
:

d_{X_{i}}\colon X_{i}\times X_{i}\longrightarrow{}^{i*}{\mathbb{R}}.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}(X_{i})
denote the set of vertices of
X_{i}
. The construction proceeds recursively: start with the vertices
{\mathcal{V}}(X_{n})
. We replace each vertex in
{\mathcal{V}}(X_{n})
with a copy of the space
X_{n-1}
(or its vertex set). Repeat this operation recursively. Finally, the space obtained by replacing the vertices at the last stage with
X_{1}
constitutes the Babel building.
Thus, a Babel building forms a nested structure of affine buildings. This structure parallels the fact that
n
-dimensional local fields are obtained as nested structures of complete discrete valuation fields. In the preface of their monumental paper
[
BT
]
, Bruhat and Tits explained their theory as follows:

De façon imagée, la théorie qui sera exposée ici, à partir du chapitre II, peut être décrite comme l’étude d’un groupe semi-simple défini sur un corps local
K
— ou plutôt du groupe
G
de ses points rationnels sur
K
— considéré comme « objet de dimension infinie » défini sur le corps résiduel
k
.

This philosophy is shared by our theory. Just as a
1
-level Babel building (an affine building) can be viewed as an infinite collection of
0
-level Babel buildings (spherical buildings), an
n
-level Babel building is an infinite collection of
(n-1)
-level Babel buildings.

Definition 1.33
.

Let
(Y,d_{Y})
be an
{}^{n*}{\mathbb{R}}
-metric space and let
B
be a subset of
Y
.

(i)

A subset
B
is said to be
bounded
if for any
y\in Y
, there exists a supremum

r(y,B)=\sup_{b\in B}d_{Y}(y,b).

(ii)

If the infimum

r(B)=\inf_{y\in Y}r(y,B)

exists, then
r(B)
is called the
circumradius
of
B
. A point
y\in Y
satisfying
r(B)=r(y,B)
is called a
circumcenter
of
B
.

Lemma 1.34
.

Let
X
be an
n
-level Babel building. If
B
is a bounded subset of
X
, then there exists at most one circumcenter of
B
.

Proof..

Suppose that
x,y\in X
are circumcenters of
B
. Assume there exists
t\in{}^{n*}(0,1)
such that the point
p_{t}=(1-t)x+ty
belongs to
X
. By applying the CAT(
0
)-inequality, we obtain

r^{2}(p_{t},B)\leq(1-t)r^{2}(x,B)+tr^{2}(y,B)-t(1-t)d_{X}^{2}(x,y).

Since
x
and
y
are circumcenters, we have
r(x,B)=r(y,B)=r(B)
. Furthermore, by the definition of the circumradius,
r(B)\leq r(p_{t},B)
holds. Consequently,

r^{2}(B)\leq r^{2}(p_{t},B)\leq r^{2}(B)-t(1-t)d_{X}^{2}(x,y).

This implies

t(1-t)d_{X}^{2}(x,y)\leq 0.

Since
t(1-t)>0
, we must have
d_{X}(x,y)=0
, which implies
x=y
.
∎

We now state, as a proposition, the claim of a higher-dimensional generalization of the Bruhat-Tits fixed point theorem.

Proposition 1.35
.

Let
G
be a group acting isometrically on an
n
-level Babel building
X
, and let
B
be a bounded subset of
X
. If
G
stabilizes

and
B
admits a circumcenter, then the circumcenter of

is a fixed point of the

Proof..

This follows immediately from the previous lemma. Since

stabilizes

and acts by isometries, it must map a circumcenter to a circumcenter. By the uniqueness of the circumcenter, the point must be fixed.
∎

In contrast to the classical case where

, the space

is not convex; consequently, a bounded subset does not necessarily possess a circumcenter. Therefore, our fixed point theorem requires an explicit assumption regarding the existence of a circumcenter, a condition that is automatically satisfied at level

.

Proposition 2.3

Let

be subsets of

\widehat{G}

Proof..

We prove the injectivity of

Let

. Suppose that there exist

and

Since

Also, since

By Lemma

, there exists

and the restriction

is the identity.
Since

fixes

.
Furthermore, since

and
n^{\prime}(\Omega^{\prime})=qn(\Omega^{\prime})\subset q(A)
, the set
n^{\prime}(\Omega^{\prime})
lies in the intersection

. Thus
g
fixes
n^{\prime}(\Omega^{\prime})

.

By the assumptions

and
\widehat{H}P_{\Omega^{\prime}}\subset Q^{\prime}
, we have

and
g\in P_{n^{\prime}(\Omega^{\prime})}=n^{\prime}P_{\Omega^{\prime}}n^{\prime-1}\subset n^{\prime}Q^{\prime}n^{\prime-1}

Let us define

, so

, which implies

.
Since

On the other hand, since

Consequently,

.
This implies that the map

∎

i
and
j
with directions
\mathbf{D}_{i}
and
\mathbf{D}_{j}
, respectively.
By the definition of Babel buildings, there exists a Babel apartment
A^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}
containing
g({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
and
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
, where
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathfrak{C}}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathfrak{D}}
are sub-sectors of the same levels.
Since the group
\widehat{P}_{{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}}
acts transitively on the set of apartments containing
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
, there exists an element
p\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{B}}}^{0}_{\mathbf{D}_{j}}
(specifically in
\widehat{P}_{{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}}
) such that
pA^{\prime}=A
.
Consequently, we have

pg({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}),{\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}\subset A.

Since the subgroup
\widehat{N}
acts transitively on the set of
i
-level Babel sectors of
A
, we have
pg({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})=n{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
for some
n\in\widehat{N}
.
This implies that
n^{-1}pg
fixes the sector
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
.
Thus, we have

g\in p^{-1}n\widehat{P}_{{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}}\subset\widehat{{\mathfrak{B}}}^{0}_{\mathbf{D}_{j}}\widehat{N}\widehat{{\mathfrak{B}}}^{0}_{\mathbf{D}_{i}}.

(Note: Depending on the order of multiplication and definition of
p
, the inverse might be on the other side, but the set product remains the same).
Recall that any Babel sector contains an affine basis of any Babel apartment including it. Therefore, by applying Proposition
2.3
(with
\Omega={\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
and
\Omega^{\prime}={\mathfrak{D}}^{\prime}
), we obtain the disjoint decomposition

\widehat{G}=\coprod_{w\in\widehat{W}}\widehat{{\mathfrak{B}}}^{0}_{\mathbf{D}_{i}}w\widehat{{\mathfrak{B}}}^{0}_{\mathbf{D}_{j}}.

∎

We call the above decomposition the
(i,j)
-Kapranov decomposition
. This terminology is adopted from
[
Kap
, Proposition 1.2.3]
. The
(0,0)
-Kapranov decomposition corresponds to the Bruhat decomposition, while the
(0,n)
-Kapranov decomposition corresponds to the Iwasawa decomposition.

Remark 2.10
.

Reductive groups over higher-dimensional local fields are expected to admit Kapranov decompositions. If this holds, the buildings associated with these groups would constitute Babel buildings and possess the canonical metric structure described herein.

2.3
Residues at vertices

Let
p
be a vertex of
X
such that
r_{p}C=C
. We write

\displaystyle r_{p}\widehat{G}

\displaystyle=\widehat{P}_{r_{p}X}^{{\dagger}}/\widehat{P}_{r_{p}X},

\displaystyle r_{p}\widehat{B}

\displaystyle=\widehat{B}/\widehat{P}_{r_{p}X},

\displaystyle r_{p}\widehat{N}

\displaystyle=\{g\in r_{p}\widehat{G}\mathrel{|}g(r_{p}A)=r_{p}A\}.

Note that
\widehat{P}_{r_{p}X}
is normal in
\widehat{P}_{\Omega}
for any non-empty subset
\Omega\subset r_{p}X
. Indeed, take elements
g\in\widehat{P}_{\Omega},h\in\widehat{P}_{r_{p}X}
and points
x\in r_{p}X,y\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle d_{X}(p,g(x))

\displaystyle\leq d_{X}(p,g(p))+d_{X}(p,x)

\displaystyle\leq d_{X}(p,g(y))+d_{X}(g(p),g(y))+d_{X}(p,x)

\displaystyle=2d_{X}(p,y)+d_{X}(p,x)\in{}^{(n-1)*}{\mathbb{R}}.

Hence
g(r_{p}X)=r_{p}X
. In particular
h
fixes
g(x)
. So we have
g^{-1}hg(x)=x
namely
g^{-1}hg\in\widehat{P}_{r_{p}X}
.

Proposition 2.12
.

The group
r_{p}\widehat{G}
acts on the residue
r_{p}X
strong transitively.

Proof..

Let
r_{p}A_{1},r_{p}A_{2}\in r_{p}{\mathcal{A}}
be two Babel apartments of level
n-1
and
C_{1},C_{2}
chambers of them respectively. We should an element
g\in r_{p}\widehat{G}
such that
g(r_{p}A_{1},C_{1})=(r_{p}A_{2},C_{2})
. By definition, there exist
A_{1},A_{2}\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that their residues at
p
are
r_{p}A_{1},r_{p}A_{2}
respectively. By assumption, we can take an element
g\in\widehat{G}
such that
g(A_{1},C_{1})=(A_{2},C_{2})
. For any
c\in C_{1}
and
x\in r_{p}X
, since
g(c)\in C_{2}\subset r_{p}X
and

\displaystyle d_{X}(p,g(x))

\displaystyle\leq d_{X}(p,g(p))+d_{X}(p,x)

\displaystyle\leq d_{X}(p,g(c))+d_{X}(g(p),g(c))+d_{X}(p,x)\in{}^{(n-1)*}{\mathbb{R}},

one has
g(x)\in r_{p}X
. Hence
g
belongs to
\widehat{P}^{{\dagger}}_{r_{p}X}
and we have
g(r_{p}A_{1},C_{1})=(r_{p}A_{2},C_{2})
.

∎

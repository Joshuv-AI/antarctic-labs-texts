Three-dimensional canonical quantum plasmonics for finite media: exact solution in terms of the classical Green tensor
Georgii Semin, Hans-Rudolf Jauslin, Stéphane Guérin

Abstract
This article presents a comprehensive three-dimensional canonical quantization to treat quantum plasmonics for finite metallic or dielectric media of arbitrary shape. We use a microscopic model for the dissipative and dispersive medium coupled with the electromagnetic field, which is justified by the fact that if one integrates the degrees of freedom of the medium, one obtains the macroscopic Maxwell equations. Its quantization features a Hamiltonian formulation having the form of two infinite harmonic oscillators characterized by a double continuum. The diagonalized Hamiltonian is quantized by the correspondence principle, introducing creation-annihilation operators in a bosonic Fock space. The diagonal quantum Hamiltonian is the sum of two terms corresponding to the two continua. The physical observables, like, e.g., the electric field, are also the sum of two terms corresponding to the two continua, one of which had been omitted in the literature geared for an infinite bulk medium. In a second step, we show that the electric field operator can by written as linear combinations of the creation-annihilation operators with coefficients that satisfy integral equations of Fredholm type. We show that the solution of these equations can be expressed in terms of the classical Green tensor of the medium satisfying the Sommerfeld radiation condition. Finally, we consider the Purcell effect for the spontaneous emission of an atom close to the medium. We show that through an exact compensation of some terms, the Purcell factor for the system with the double continuum is proportional to the imaginary part of the Green tensor, which defines the local density of states. This result has the same form as the one obtained in the literature for bulk systems that involve a single continuum and a small dissipative background extending to infinity, and can be seen as a justification of this approach.

II.1
Classical Hamiltonian

We start by considering the model of the electromagnetic field and the finite-size inhomogeneous non-magnetic medium located in the volume
V
(see Fig.

). We consider the following classical Hamiltonian, compatible with the Maxwell equations

]

H=H_{\mathrm{em}}+H_{\mathrm{med}}+H_{\mathrm{int}}

the free Hamiltonian of the medium oscillations

Here,
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
is the projector on transverse fields, and

is the coupling function. We adopt the Coulomb gauge
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}=0,

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\Pi}_{A}=0

The orthonormality of the chosen set of eigenvectors is given by

where

To establish the connection between a Green tensor of the medium and the operator of the electric field, we introduce the dyadic function

, as the solution of the following Fredholm integral equation of the second kind

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{x},\nu)=

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{G}}_{0+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{x},\nu)+\frac{\nu^{2}}{c^{2}}\int_{V}\mathrm{d}^{3}z\,\bar{\bar{G}}_{0+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{z},\nu)

(108)

where
\mathbf{r},\,\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{x},\nu)
of this equation is unique
[
50
]
. Applying the operator
\nabla\times\nabla\times
on (

\left(-\frac{\nu^{2}}{c^{2}}\varepsilon(\mathbf{r},\nu)+\nabla\times\nabla\times\right)\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{x},\nu)=\bar{\bar{I}}\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{x}).

satisfies the Sommerfeld outgoing radiation condition (see Appendix
A
), (
108
) implies that
\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{x},\nu)
also satisfies it:

\lim_{|\mathbf{r}|\rightarrow\infty}|\mathbf{r}|\left(\nabla\times-\frac{i\nu}{c}\frac{\mathbf{r}}{|\mathbf{r}|}\times\right)\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{x},\nu)=0.

(110)

Thus, we conclude that the dyadic tensor
\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{x},\nu)
is the unique Green tensor for the medium satisfying the Sommerfeld radiation condition. As a consequence
[

IV.3
Uncoupling limit

Without the medium, the electromagnetic field should behave as a free field. To validate the results in such a case, we make the field ”blind” to the matter by turning off the interaction between them and considering the limit
\tilde{\alpha}\rightarrow 0
or
\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0
, which leads to
\varepsilon\rightarrow 1
due to the Kramers-Kronig relation. We call such a limit as
uncoupling limit
and mark it as
\lim_{\text{nc}}=\lim_{\tilde{\alpha}\rightarrow 0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 1}
.

In the uncoupling limit, we can immediately obtain the results for the field coefficients. First, we consider the
\mathbf{e}
coefficients. From (
91
) we have

\lim_{\text{nc}}\mathbf{e}^{v}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x})=0,

(137)

which implies
\mathbf{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{e}^{u}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x})
. Applying the limit to (
79
) yields

\lim_{\text{nc}}\mathbf{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x})=\lim_{\text{nc}}\mathbf{e}^{u}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x})=\omega_{\kappa}\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{x}).

(138)

Thus, in the
\mathbf{e}
coefficients, only the term associated with the free electromagnetic field remains. On the other hand, the
\mathbf{m}
coefficients are zero in the uncoupling limit. Indeed, by applying the limit on (
99
) and (
100
), we have

\lim_{\text{nc}}\mathbf{m}^{v}_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})=\lim_{\text{nc}}\mathbf{m}^{u}_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})=0.

(139)

Thus, the medium part of the electromagnetic operator disappears

\lim_{\text{nc}}\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{m}=0
, and

\lim_{\text{nc}}\hat{\mathbf{E}}=\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{free}.

(140)

The Green tensor of the field coupled with the medium should also turn into the Green tensor of the free field in the uncoupling limit. Indeed, taking the limit of Eq. (
108
), we have

\lim_{\text{nc}}\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\omega)=\bar{\bar{G}}_{0+}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\omega),

(141)

since the integral term disappears. The same can be done with the Green tensor LDOS identity,

\displaystyle\lim_{\text{nc}}

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{Im}[\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\omega)]=\lim_{\text{nc}}\left[\frac{\pi c^{2}}{2\omega^{3}}\sum_{\sigma}\mathbf{e}_{\omega,\sigma}(\mathbf{x})\otimes\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\omega,\sigma}(\mathbf{y})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}z\,\varepsilon_{i}(\mathbf{z},\omega)\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z},\omega)\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}^{*}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y},\omega)\right].

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi c^{2}}{2\omega}\sum_{\sigma}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x})\otimes\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{y})=\mathrm{Im}[\bar{\bar{G}}_{0+}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\omega)],

(142)

where we used (
138
), and the integral term disappears because it contains the imaginary part of the dielectric coefficient.

The eigenfunctions
\underline{\psi}^{e}_{\kappa}
and
\underline{\psi}^{m}_{\mu}
take the form of
\underline{\phi}^{e}_{\kappa}
and
\underline{\phi}^{m}_{\mu}
in the uncoupling limit. One can see clearly from (
132

133
), that

\displaystyle\lim_{\text{nc}}u^{e}_{\kappa}(\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\delta(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}),

(143a)

\displaystyle\lim_{\text{nc}}\mathbf{v}^{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})

\displaystyle=0,

(143b)

\displaystyle\lim_{\text{nc}}u^{m}_{\mu}(\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle=0,

(143c)

\displaystyle\lim_{\text{nc}}\mathbf{v}^{m}_{\mu}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{n}_{j}\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\delta(\nu-\nu^{\prime}),

(143d)

which agrees with the fact that the Hamiltonian in this limit is already in diagonal form. Thus, all the crucial properties of the field have the form of a free field in the uncoupling limit.

V
Purcell effect

We consider the model of a two-level quantum emitter of states
|g\rangle
,
|e\rangle
with the ground and excited energy 0 and
\hbar\omega_{a}
, respectively, located at
\mathbf{r}_{a}

[
51
]

[
2

\Gamma(\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega_{a})=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\left|\langle g\otimes 1_{\eta}|\hat{W}(\omega_{a})|e\otimes\varnothing\rangle\right|^{2}\delta(\omega_{a}-\omega_{\eta}),

(144)

where
\hat{W}=-\left[\hat{\sigma}_{eg}\otimes\mathbf{d}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r}_{a})+\text{H.c.}\right]
is the interaction Hamiltonian written in the rotation-wave approximation with the dipole moment
d
and the emitter operator
\sigma_{eg}=|e\rangle\langle g|
, and
\eta
labels all the possible one-QPP states
|1_{\eta}\rangle
, of energy
\hbar\omega_{\eta}
, associated with the decay from the initial excited state
|e\rangle
(and the vacuum for the QPP) state to the ground state
|g\rangle
. Substituting the electric field operator expression (
121
), which includes the contributions of the two continua,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{e}(\mathbf{r})+\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{m}(\mathbf{r}),

(145)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{e}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=-\int\mathrm{d}\kappa\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{\kappa}}}\left[\mathbf{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{r})\hat{C}^{e}_{\kappa}+\text{H.c.}\right]

(146)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{m}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=-\int\mathrm{d}\mu\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\varepsilon_{0}\nu_{\mu}}}\left[\mathbf{m}_{\mu}(\mathbf{r})\hat{C}^{m}_{\mu}+\text{H.c.}\right],

(147)

we can write, after applying the creation-annihilation operators to the initial state, evaluating the scalar products and integrating over the final states
d\eta

\Gamma(\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega_{a})=\Gamma_{e}(\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega_{a})+\Gamma_{m}(\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega_{a}),

with

\displaystyle\Gamma_{e}(\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega_{a})=\frac{\pi}{\hbar\omega\varepsilon_{0}}\int

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\kappa\,\delta(\omega_{\kappa}-\omega_{a})

\displaystyle\times\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{r}_{a})\otimes\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{r}_{a})\cdot\mathbf{d},

(149a)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}(\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega_{a})=\frac{\pi}{\hbar\omega\varepsilon_{0}}\int

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mu\,\delta(\nu_{\mu}-\omega_{a})

\displaystyle\times\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\mu}(\mathbf{r}_{a})\otimes\mathbf{m}^{*}_{\mu}(\mathbf{r}_{a})\cdot\mathbf{d}.

(149b)

Rewriting the term
\Gamma_{m}(\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega_{a})

118

\mathbf{m}_{\mathbf{x},\nu,j}(\mathbf{r})=-\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbf{x},\nu)\frac{\nu^{2}}{c^{2}}\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{x},\nu)\cdot\mathbf{n}_{j},

(150)

and the Green tensor LDOS identity (

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}[

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\omega)]=\frac{\pi c^{2}}{2\omega^{3}}\int\mathrm{d}\kappa\,\mathbf{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x})\otimes\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{y})\delta(\omega-\omega_{\kappa})

(151)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}(\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega_{a})=\frac{2\omega_{a}^{2}}{\hbar\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathrm{Im}[\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r}_{a},\mathbf{r}_{a},\omega)]\cdot\mathbf{d}

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{\hbar\omega\varepsilon_{0}}\int\mathrm{d}\kappa\,\delta(\omega-\omega_{\kappa})\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{r}_{a})\otimes\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{r}_{a})\cdot\mathbf{d}.

VI
Conclusion and outlook

This work extends the earlier results that were formulated for a one-dimensional quantum plasmonic system
[
39
]
to three-dimensional systems with a finite medium of arbitrary shape. In contrast to the one-dimensional system, the electric field in three dimensions has transversal and longitudinal components due to the additional degrees of freedom associated with the oscillations in three dimensions of the charges of the medium.

The main result is that all the elements of the quantized plasmonics fields can be expressed in terms of the classical Green tensor of the medium, for which there are well-established numerical algorithms. This approach can be extended in a straightforward way to anisotropic and to magnetic media.

In future works, the presented approach can be applied to other physical phenomena, e.g., superradiance, Casimir effect, or quantum control of emitters in nanostructured environments.

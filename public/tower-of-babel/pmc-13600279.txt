Complete mitochondrial genome of Hemimyzon megalopseos and H. pumilicorpora (Teleost: Balitoridae)
Authors: Hongtao Li, Xingjin Che, Zhilei Wu, Lingfu Kong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, the complete mitochondrial genomes of two balitorid fishes from the upper Pearl River, China, namely Hemimyzon megalopseos and Hemimyzon pumilicorpora, were sequenced and assembled. The mitogenomes were 16,571 bp and 16,568 bp in length, respectively, each containing 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 tRNA genes, two rRNA genes, and one control region. The overall A + T content was 55.76% in H. megalopseos and 55.63% in H. pumilicorpora. Phylogenetic analysis based on the 13 PCGs confirmed that the two Hemimyzon species form a clade sister to Sinogastromyzon. These mitogenomes represent the first publicly available genomic resources for the genus Hemimyzon and provide valuable data for future taxonomic, evolutionary and conservation studies of Balitoridae.

Introduction

The balitorid genus Hemimyzon (Teleostei: Balitoridae) currently comprises 18 valid species (Fricke et al. 2026), widely distributed in headwaters of major rivers in East and Southeast Asia. Species of this genus are typically adapted to fast-flowing, highly oxygenated montane streams and serve as valuable indicators of freshwater ecosystem health. However, the evolutionary biology and genetic resources of Hemimyzon remain poorly understood.

Complete mitochondrial genomes are widely utilized as effective molecular markers for phylogenetic reconstruction, species delimitation, and population genetic studies in fishes (Yang and Li 2016; Jairo et al. 2025). To date, however, no complete mitochondrial genome has been reported for any species of this genus, which has hindered further phylogenetic and conservation genomic investigations.

In this study, we sequenced, assembled, and annotated the complete mitogenomes of Hemimyzon megalopseos Li and Chen 1985 and Hemimyzon pumilicorpora Zheng and Zhang 1987, both collected from the upper Pearl River, China. Although the two species are morphologically very similar, they can be explicitly distinguished by the number of lateral-line scales (72-75 in H. pumilicorpora vs. 75-78 in H. megalopseos) and the gap between the pelvic-fin bases (widely separated in H. megalopseos vs. adjacent but not connected in H. pumilicorpora). These two species represent distinct morphological and ecological types within the genus. The newly obtained mitogenomic data will serve as a valuable genetic resource for future studies on the phylogeny, biogeography, and conservation genetics of balitorid loaches.

Materials and methods

Specimen collection and deposition

A specimen of H. megalopseos was collected from Jianshui County, Honghe Prefecture, Yunnan Province, China (24.018°N, 103.134°E), and a specimen of H. pumilicorpora from Guanling County, Anshun City, Guizhou Province, China (25.885°N, 105.670°E). Both voucher specimens were deposited at the Kunming Institute of Zoology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (KIZ) (https://www.kiz.cas.cn, contact: Dr WT Chen, email: chenwt@mail.kiz.ac.cn) under accession numbers KIZ2025004316 for H. megalopseos (Figure 1) and KIZ2025004317 for H. pumilicorpora (Figure 2).

Mitogenome sequencing, assembly, and annotation

Total genomic DNA was extracted from the right pelvic fin of Hemimyzon specimens using the Ezup Column Animal Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China). Sequencing libraries were constructed following the whole-genome shotgun (WGS) strategy with PCR-based library preparation. Paired-end (2 × 150 bp) sequencing was performed on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform (MGI Tech) at Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). Raw paired-end reads were generated in FASTQ format. The complete mitochondrial genome was assembled and annotated using MitoZ v3.6 with default parameters.

Phylogenetic analysis

Maximum-likelihood phylogeny was reconstructed using IQ-TREE version 2.0 (Nguyen et al. 2015) based on the 13 PCGs from 20 Balitoridae species. Cyprinus carpio Linnaeus 1758 (Accession: KJ51182) and Triplophysa rosa Chen and Yang 2005 (Accession: NC019587) were used as outgroups. Ultrafast Bootstrap (UFBoot) support values were calculated with 10,000 replications.

Results

Basic features of the H. megalopseos and H. pumilicorpora mitogenome

The complete mitogenome of H. megalopseos is 16,571 bp in length, with an A + T content of 55.76% (A: 30.44%, T: 25.32%, G: 16.55%, C: 27.69%). The mitogenome of H. pumilicorpora is 16,568 bp in length, with an A + T content of 55.63% (A: 30.44%, T: 25.19%, G: 16.53%, C: 27.84%). Both mitogenomes contain 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 tRNA genes, two rRNA genes, and one putative control region (Figure 3). Among the PCGs, twelve (nad2, cox1, cox2, atp8, atp6, cox3, nad3, nad4l, nad4, nad5, cytb, nad1) are located on the heavy strand, while nad6 is on the light strand. The PCGs employ three different start codons (ATG, GTG, ATC) and stop codons (TAA, TA, T). The A + T content of these two Hemimyzon species is similar to that of other reported Balitoridae mitogenomes (e.g. Sinogastromyzon sichangensis: 55.5% (Yu et al. 2015); Jinshaia sinensis: 54.59% (Que et al. 2016)), falling within the typical range for the family.

Phylogenetic position of H. megalopseos and H. pumilicorpora

Phylogenetic analysis revealed that the two Hemimyzon species are closely related sister species, with Hemimyzon (represented by the current two species) being most closely related to Sinogastromyzon. A well‑supported clade (UFBoot values) comprising Jinshaia, Lepturichthys, Sinogastromyzon, Hemimyzon, and several Balitora species was recovered, with no intermingling of other lineages (Figure 4).

Discussion and conclusions

The torrents of major rivers throughout East and Southeastern Asia are inhabited by a highly diverse fish fauna, including the Balitorid genus Hemimyzon. The phylogenetic position of genus Hemimyzon, despite a rather wide distribution range (Kottelat and Chu, 1988), was largely unknown. Recent phylogenys have revealed the non-monophyly of this genus (Luo et al. 2023; Lei et al. 2025a), but only a few of 18 species in the genus were included in the phylogeny. Moreover, no complete mitochondrial genome was publicly available for any species of Hemimyzon, leaving a significant gap in balitorid mitogenomics. In this study, we provide the first publicly available mitochondrial genomes of Hemimyzon species, namely H. megalopseos and H. pumilicorpora. The two species were closely related sister species in the current phylogeny, and the position of H. megalopseos (sister to Sinogastromyzon) is congruent with previous research (Lei et al. 2025a, 2025b). The non-monophyly of Sinogastromyzon has been repeatedly recovered in existing phylogenies based on both mitochondrial and nuclear markers (Tao et al. 2019; Luo et al. 2023; Lei et al. 2025a, 2025b) as well as the current one. The two mitogenomes reported herein provide a solid genetic resource for future taxonomic, phylogeographic and evolutionary studies of Hemimyzon and Balitoridae.

In-depth exploration of the focus issues of TKI combined with radiotherapy for EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma patients with brain metastasis: a systematic analysis based on literature metrology, meta-analysis, and real-world observational data
Authors: Yalan Tong, Xiaosha Wan, Chang Yin, Ting Lei, Shan Gao, Yinghua Li, Xiaojing Du
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThere is a growing interest in utilizing a combination of brain radiotherapy (RT) and tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) for patients diagnosed with brain metastases (BM) in epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation-positive lung adenocarcinoma (LAC). The current status of this treatment strategy remains a subject of debate.MethodsWe initiated our study by conducting a comprehensive literature search using the SCI-expanded database of Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). We utilized the VOSPviewer software to analyze various aspects of the research, including the year of publication, authorship, keywords, and country.Subsequently, we performed an extensive and systematic literature search on popular online databases. Our primary outcome measures were overall survival (OS) and intracranial progression-free survival (iPFS), both quantified by hazard ratios (HRs).Additionally, for data verification, we included data from patients in non-small cell lung cancer with brain metastasis who underwent therapeutic intervention at the Cancer Prevention and Treatment Center of Sun Yat-sen University and the Radiotherapy Department of Hanzhong Central Hospital between August 2012 and November 2021.ResultsThe bibliometric analysis revealed an increasing trend in research focused on the combination of RT and TKIs for the management of lung cancer brain metastases over the previous decade. Then, nine studies consistent with the research direction were included for meta-analysis. The meta-analysis showed that the OS (HR = 0.81, 95% confidence interval: 0.69–0.94; P = 0.007) and iPFS (HR = 0.71, 95% confidence interval: 0.61–0.82; P < 0.001) of the combination therapy were significantly prolonged. Finally, 168 EGFR-mutated BM advanced LAC patients in the real world were verified, and the median iPFS of the combination therapy (n = 88 and EGFR-TKIs alone (n = 80) were 16.0 and 9.0 months, respectively, (P < 0.001). The median OS was 29.0 and 27.0 months, respectively, with no dramatic difference (P = 0.188).ConclusionsResearch on EGFR-mutant LAC brain metastasis has turned towards exploring optimal treatment strategies for this condition. Our meta-analysis and real-world data analysis consistently demonstrate that combination therapy offers a substantial improvement in patient survival compared to EGFR-TKI monotherapy. Notably, among patients undergoing salvage radiotherapy (RT), our subgroup analysis reveals that those initially treated with third-generation TKIs experience more significant benefits than those treated with first- or second-generation TKIs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13071-2.

• The findings of this study revealed that for patients with EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma brain metastases who have not previously received epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR TKI) treatment, the use of a first-line combined treatment regimen of EGFR TKI and cranial radiotherapy can significantly prolong the intracranial progression-free survival (PFS).

What is known and what is new?

• Combination therapy can significantly prolong the iPFS of the aforementioned patients.

• (1) For patients with exon 19 positive mutations and brain metastases between 3-5, this combination therapy regimen shows a more significant effect in prolonging overall survival (OS). (2) Compared to patients who only received TKI oral therapy as first-line treatment, patients who used third-generation TKI drugs in first-line treatment showed better prognosis after receiving second-line salvage brain radiation therapy.(3) For patients who use the first or second generation TKI in first-line treatment, a third generation TKI combined with salvage radiotherapy is required in the second line treatment stage.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Further large-scale clinical trials are needed to confirm the therapeutic effects of third-generation TKI and radiotherapy on patients with lung adenocarcinoma brain metastasis and their intracranial lesion progression.

Introduction

The Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) is the most prevalent driver mutation in lung cancers, with approximately half of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients in Asian populations harboring EGFR gene mutations [1]. EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients exhibit a notably high incidence of brain metastasis, varying between around 31.4% and 64.7% [2, 3]. Radiotherapy, including whole-brain radiation therapy (WBRT) and stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS), is well-recognized as the primary treatment strategies for patients with brain metastases [4, 5]. However, the grim prognosis and long-term neurological complications, particularly cognitive dysfunctions resulting from WBRT [6], have posed a significant therapeutic challenge.

EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have brought hope to patients with EGFR-mutated lung cancer by improving overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) [7–11]. As a result, they have gradually become the first-line treatment for advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients and have also opened up new therapeutic avenues for those with brain metastasis. Studies such as EUTACT [7] and LUX-Lung [12] have shown that EGFR-TKIs can significantly reduce the risk of brain metastasis, achieving more pronounced efficacy in terms of progression-free survival (PFS) and objective response rate (ORR). Compared to conventional chemotherapy drugs that cannot cross the blood–brain barrier to deliver their therapeutic benefits, small-molecule TKIs can maintain a higher drug concentration in brain tissue, which holds significant importance for the prognosis of brain metastatic patients. Additionally, cranial radiotherapy disrupts the blood–brain barrier, which can enhance the absorption of targeted drugs, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for the synergistic use of EGFR-TKIs and radiotherapy [13–15].

In the era of EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), there still exists a debate on whether to integrate radiotherapy into the first-line treatment phase with TKIs. The BRAIN study revealed that, in comparison to whole-brain radiotherapy, patients with brain metastases from lung cancer treated solely with Icotinib experienced a decrease in adverse events occurrence and an extension in the progression-free survival period [16]. Meanwhile, different studies have suggested that the combination of TKIs and cranial radiotherapy could potentially prolong intracranial progression-free survival by 6.7–7.7 months when contrasted with TKIs alone [17, 18]. This situation underscores the need for further investigation into this matter.

This study aims to evaluate the treatment selection strategies for those TKI treatment-naïve EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients with brain metastases and compare the effectiveness between brain radiotherapy in combination with TKIs and TKIs administered alone. We achieve this by conducting a systematic analysis of literature metrics, a meta-analysis, and analyzing a real-world observational data.

Materials and methods

Bibliometrics

Search term

For the bibliometric analysis, we utilized the SCI-expanded database of Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). The literature search was conducted on September 25, 2023, to minimize bias. Our study encompassed the publication period from the inception of our self-built database up to the year 2023. We exclusively included original articles and reviews written in the English language. Two researchers independently conducted the initial data retrieval, and any discrepancies that arose were resolved through discussion. Ultimately, our study analyzed a total of 351 articles.

Data extraction

We extracted raw data from the SCI-expanded database of Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). This dataset comprises information such as title, number of citations, H-index, publication year, country/region, author details, journal information, and keywords.

Bibliometric analysis

Bibliometric analysis is a widely used approach for comprehensively understanding the characteristics of publications in specific scientific fields. In our study, the collected literature underwent preliminary statistical analysis through a comprehensive database. This database encompassed data related to publication counts by various authors, publication years, countries of origin, and specific research domains.

Subsequently, the selected literature was subjected to a keyword co-occurrence network analysis. This analysis was conducted using VOSPlayer software, which provided valuable insights into the relationships and associations among keywords within the literature.

The meta-analysis section

Literature search

We performed an exhaustive and systematic search across publicly accessible databases, specifically PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library, with the goal of identifying all published randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and retrospective researchs. Our search was limited to researchs involving human subjects, and we restricted the language to English. The keywords employed in our search strategy included the following terms: "Adenocarcinoma of Lung," "brain metastases," "EGFR," "Tyrosine Protein Kinase Inhibitors," and "Radiation Treatment." This approach allowed us to compile a comprehensive collection of relevant literature for our study.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses are carried out using STATA 14.0 (Stata Corporation Limited, College Station, TX) and OFFFICE16.The relative effects of OS and iPFS in the included studies were expressed as HRs and corresponding 95% CIs. For studies presenting 95% CIs and HR values, these data were directly extracted from the articles.In cases where studies provided only survival curves, we utilized the method proposed by Tierney et al. to calculate the HR. To assess the heterogeneity across studies, we employed the Chi-square test and I2 statistic. Heterogeneity was considered low when P > 0.1 and I2 < 25%. In instances of no statistically significant heterogeneity, we applied the fixed-effect model to calculate the pooled effect. When statistically emphatic heterogeneity was observed, the random-effect model was used.This statistical approach allowed us to assess and synthesize the data from the included studies effectively.

Clinical study—real world research

Patients

We conducted a review analysis of patients with lung cancer brain transfusions who were treated in the Cancer Center of Sun Yat-sen University and the Radiotherapy Department of Hanzhong Central Hospital from August 2012 to November 2021., who were diagnosed with brain metastases and were positive for the EGFR gene mutation, including patients with LAC present BM at first diagnosis (synchronous).

Treatment details

All patients received first-line EGFR-TKI therapy, including first-generation TKIs (gefitinib, erlotinib, and icotinib), second-generation TKIs (afatinib), and third-generation TKIs (osimertinib and amatinib). For patients undergoing brain radiotherapy, individualized radiotherapy regimens were selected based on the patient's condition, which could involve WBRT or SRS.

This patient selection and treatment protocol ensured the robustness of our real-world research study.

Data collection

Key patient characteristics, including age, gender, Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) score, accompanying symptoms, primary lesion information, time of brain metastasis detection, gene mutation sites, number of brain metastases, history of primary site surgery, intracranial tumor distribution, and more, were extracted from the electronic medical record system of the Cancer Center of Sun Yat-sen University and the Radiotherapy Department of Hanzhong Central Hospital.

Statistical analysis

A comprehensive statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26 and Microsoft Office 2016 software. To summarize the categorical variables, we employed descriptive statistical methods, and for comparison, we employed Pearson's chi square test or Fisher's exact test. To balance the confounding factors between groups and reduce bias, the PSM method was employed to conduct 1:1 matching for the two groups of patients, with a matching tolerance of 0.02. The matching variables encompassed the baseline characteristics of the patients, such as gender, age, and KPS score. To conduct a more comprehensive evaluation of patients' iPFS and OS, we employed the Cox proportional hazards regression method for a statistical study. This involves performing univariate and multivariate analyses to ascertain the hazard ratios (HRs) and establish 95% confidence intervals (CIs). For the survival analysis of iPFS and OS, the Kaplan Meier method was employed in this study. Furthermore, the log rank test was employed to compare the two groups. A p-value that is less than the threshold of 0.05 is deemed statistically significant.

The comparative efficacy of combining IPFS and OS of EGFR-TKI with brain radiotherapy vs alternative treatment modalities

The research comprised a total of nine articles that specifically examined the clinical results of combining EGFR-TKIs with brain irradiation, in comparison to a control group. The analysis of the combined data showed a moderate level of heterogeneity in the initial progression-free survival (P = 0.058, I2 = 46.9%) and no heterogeneity in overall survival (P = 0.611, I2 = 0.0%). Consequently, a fixed-effect model was utilized in the meta-analysis. The findings of this study indicate that the combination of EGFR-TKIs with brain irradiation resulted in a significant improvement in iPFS (HR = 0.71, 95% CI: 0.61–0.82; P < 0.001) (Fig. 3 A) and OS (HR = 0.81, 95% CI: 0.69–0.94; P = 0.007) (Fig. 3 B) as compared to the control group. These improvements were statistically significant. Subgroup analysis

The included studies were divided into two subgroups based on the control group. Subgroup analysis revealed significant heterogeneity between the two groups concerning iPFS in patients (p = 0.033), indicating that the choice of the control group can substantially impact the outcomes of the meta-analysis (Fig. 4, A). Within the EGFR-TKI group, there was low heterogeneity (P = 0.143, I2 = 37.5%), and a fixed-effect model was employed to combine the effect size (HR = 0.62, 95% CI: 0.52–0.74; P < 0.001). In the brain radiotherapy group, there was no heterogeneity (P = 0.635, I2 = 0.0%), and the effect size was HR = 0.88, with a 95% CI of 0.69–1.12; P = 0.304. These results indicate that compared to EGFR-TKI alone, combined therapy significantly extends patients' iPFS, while no difference was observed when comparing it with brain radiotherapy alone. Subgroup bias tests were conducted separately for the subgroups, and funnel plots were generated (Figure S3, A-B). The funnel plot exhibited a symmetrical pattern, indicating no publication bias in this study. The results of subgroup analysis indicated that iPFS for patients who received a combination of third-generation TKIs and RT was found to be significantly longer (18.0 months, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 13.541–22.459) compared to patients who were treated with a combination of first- and second-generation TKIs along with RT (15.0 months, 95% CI: 13.305–16.695). For patients undergoing salvage radiotherapy, the selection of early EGFR TKIs can affect the patient's time for secondary intracranial progression. The duration of secondary intracranial progression time (iPFS2) was found to be significantly longer (13.0 months, 95% CI: 0.000–26.579) for patients who received third-generation TKIs in the early stage, compared to patients who were treated with first- and second-generation TKIs (8.0 months, 95% CI: 6.695–9.305). This observed difference was statistically significant (P = 0.023). Nevertheless, there was no statistically significant disparity observed in overall survival (OS) among patients who received various EGFR-TKI medications during the initial phase (P = 0.277; as indicated in Table 5 and Fig. 6, panels E and F).

A cohort including 66 individuals who had previously undergone treatment with first- and second-generation TKIs were administered salvage third-generation TKIs, either in conjunction with brain irradiation or as standalone brain radiotherapy, subsequent to the progression of intracranial lesions.After excluding three patients with insufficient follow-up, 63 patients were included in the analysis. Results demonstrated that iPFS2 (24.0 months, 95% CI: 19.270–28.730) and OS (38.0 months, 95% CI: 30.074–45.926) for the combination of third-generation TKIs and brain radiotherapy were significantly superior to those for brain radiotherapy alone (iPFS2: 8.0 months, 95% CI: 6.695–9.305; OS: 20.0 months, 95% CI: 24.108–23.116), with a highly significant difference (P < 0.001; Table 5, Fig. 6 G-H).

In addition, the study also compared the impact of radiotherapy methods on the intracranial progression time of patients. The study included patients receiving first-line treatment with both radiation therapy (RT) and tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), and compared the differences in iPFS and OS between whole-brain radiotherapy (WBRT) and stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS). The study found that TKI combined with whole-brain radiotherapy (WBRT) or stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) had a similar effect on iPFS (17 months, 95% CI: 13.848–20.152 vs 16 months, 95% CI: 12.328–19.672) (P = 0.781). The overall survival (OS) of SRS patients (39 months, 95% CI: 28.430–49.570) was longer than that of WBRT (22 months, 95% CI: 14.722–29.278), but the difference was not significant (P = 0.134) (Figure S5, A-B).

Analysis of beneficial population

In order to investigate the possible advantages of combination therapy, a subgroup analysis was performed utilizing the Cox proportional hazards model to evaluate theHR and its corresponding 95%CI. Several factors were taken into consideration, such as age, gender, Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) score, concurrent symptoms, timing of primary lesion and brain metastasis detection, gene mutation sites, number of brain metastases, history of primary site surgery, and intracranial tumor distribution.

In the majority of examined subgroups, the combination therapy demonstrated a superior impact on iPFS compared to EGFR-TKI alone (as illustrated in Fig. 7 A). Discussion

Research indicates that, compared to other subtypes of NSCLC, LAC has a higher incidence of BM. Approximately 45% to 52% of LAC patients develop BM during their disease course, representing a common complication and a significant cause of disease progression and mortality [27]. About 20% of patients already have brain metastases at the time of initial diagnosis, and around 30% of NSCLC patients develop brain metastases during treatment [28]. It is particularly noteworthy that lung cancer patients with positive EGFR and ALK are more prone to develop brain metastases during treatment [29]. Despite the development of targeted therapy and immunotherapy significantly improving the prognosis of NSCLC with brain metastases, the survival rate of patients with brain metastases remains low [30]. Therefore, treatment strategies for patients with brain metastases still need further exploration. Through bibliometric analysis, we found that the research on combined treatment of lung adenocarcinoma brain metastasis patients with RT and TKI is increasing year by year. We included 9 studies consistent with the research direction for a meta-analysis, which showed that combined treatment significantly prolonged the patients' OS and intracranial iPFS. Finally, by validating 168 advanced LAC patients with EGFR mutations in the real world, we found that combined treatment (n = 88; iPFS = 16 m) significantly improved the survival rate compared to treatment with EGFR-TKIs alone (n = 80; iPFS = 9 m). Moreover, among patients who received salvage radiotherapy (RT), those initially treated with third-generation TKIs benefited significantly more than those treated with first or second-generation TKIs.

Radiotherapy (RT),including WBRT and SRS, has become the most widely used treatment method for patients with brain metastases, as it can alleviate symptoms, is broadly applicable, and easy to implement [14, 31]. Studies have shown that RT especially SRS, can significantly reduce the volume of brain lesions and control local progression [32, 33]. Currently, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have become the first-line treatment for patients with EGFR-mutant brain metastases [34]. By inhibiting EGFR activation, blocking downstream signal transduction, inhibiting cell cycle progression, promoting cell apoptosis, inhibiting angiogenesis, and metastasis, TKIs can extend patients' survival and intracranial progression-free survival [35]. However, whether EGFR-TKIs combined with radiotherapy have a synergistic effect remains a subject of considerable debate.

To further explore the potential benefits of combined treatment on the survival prognosis of patients with locally progressive LAC with BM and EGFR mutations, researchers have conducted several clinical studies [36]. On the other hand, a large meta-analysis conducted by Dong K and others also confirmed that the combination of EGFR-TKI and RT significantly improved patients' iPFS compared to TKI alone (HR = 0.62, P < 0.001) [37]. Furthermore, multiple clinical trials and meta-analyses have shown that combined treatment can improve progression-free survival (PFS) and even overall survival (OS) [38–40].

Consistent with most existing research, our study found that in patients with BMs from EGFR-mutant NSCLC, the combination of EGFR-TKI and RT as a first-line treatment significantly improves the brain control rate and extends visceral progression-free survival (iPFS) (16.0 months compared to 9.0 months, P < 0.001). However, our study did not observe a significant improvement in overall survival (OS), which may be related to the use of third-generation TKIs by some patients. The third-generation TKI, osimertinib, has superior blood–brain barrier penetration capabilities compared to the first two generations, showing a unique advantage in central nervous system iPFS [41] (13.9 months, HR = 0.48). Nicola Colclough's observation of the distribution of EGFR-TKIs in the brains of healthy rats, specifically the brain Kp value, revealed that osimertinib had the highest level of exposure in the brain, with a Kp value of 6.1, while the total levels of first and second-generation EGFR-TKIs in the brain were lower than or equal to those in the plasma [42]. A double-blind, randomized, international multicenter FLAURA study further confirmed that the blood–brain barrier permeability of the third-generation TKI osimertinib far exceeds that of the first two generations. The central nervous system iPFS of the osimertinib group showed a significant advantage (not reached 13.9 months, HR = 0.48) [41]. From the survival curves, it can be observed that as survival extends, the concentration of the drug in the central nervous system accumulates, making the iPFS of the third-generation TKI combined with brain radiotherapy exceed that of the first and second-generation TKIs combined with brain radiotherapy. Thus, the significant efficacy of the third-generation TKI may reduce the potential additional benefits of combined radiotherapy. Additionally, the small sample size and variability between variables may introduce bias between groups, leading to insufficient statistical significance.Currently, more clinical trials are underway to compare the use of ositinib with and without RT in NSCLC patients with BM (NCT03769103 and NCT03497767).

Further analysis revealed that the choice of TKI type during initial treatment significantly affected the prognosis of patients who received salvage radiotherapy for brain lesion progression after solely undergoing first-line EGFR TKI oral therapy. Patients treated with third-generation TKIs experienced a significantly prolonged time before the progression of a second intracranial lesion. This finding was supported by animal studies; Zhang Y and colleagues established a NSCLC brain metastasis xenograft model in BALB/c nude mice and discovered that combining osimertinib with ionizing radiation (IR) significantly enhanced the accumulation of osimertinib within the brain [43]. Furthermore, this combination strategy effectively inhibited tumor cell proliferation and survival, delayed DNA damage repair, increased the rate of IR-induced apoptosis, and alleviated the G2/M phase block caused by osimertinib. For patients initially treated with first- or second-generation TKIs, the prognosis after using third-generation TKI combined with brain radiotherapy for intracranial lesion progression was significantly better than that for those who only received brain radiotherapy, further verifying the superiority of combining third-generation TKI with brain radiotherapy in treatment. However, in real-world studies, combined treatment did not significantly translate into overall survival (OS) benefits, which could be due to various factors. The heterogeneous use of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) by patients in the real world can also impact OS; a randomized clinical trial conducted by Yang Zhenzhou among NSCLC and brain metastasis patients examining the effects of erlotinib combined with whole brain radiotherapy (WBRT) found no significant difference in OS between the two groups [20]. In addition, the impact of different radiotherapy methods on patients was also compared. The study found that there was no significant difference in iPFS and OS between patients treated with TKI combined with whole brain radiotherapy (WBRT) or stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS).Similarly, a retrospective study by Tatineni V showed that there was no significant difference in overall survival between the SRS + WBRT combined cohort, EGFR TKIs and WBRT combined cohort, and EGFR TKI + SRS combined cohort for NSCLC brain metastases patients [44]. Meanwhile, in our study, no intolerable toxic side effects were observed in all patients in the RT + TKI combination therapy group during subsequent follow-up, including neurological complications (such as intolerable brain edema or even cognitive impairment) and other toxic side effects.The side effects associated with the combination of various generations of TKIs and brain radiotherapy for the treatment of lung cancer with brain metastasis have been summarized in Table S2.

Additionally, the patient's genotype can also affect prognosis, with studies finding that patients with exon 19 deletions benefit in OS from combined treatment. The general condition of patients is another important factor affecting OS, with our multivariate analysis confirming that KPS is a significant prognostic factor for OS improvement. Moreover, we found that in patients with 3–5 brain metastases, combined treatment not only prolonged iPFS but also extended OS, indicating that for patients in good general condition with fewer metastases, combined treatment offers more benefits. Furthermore, combining other drugs during treatment can also affect prognosis, for example, our study's multivariate Cox analysis showed that antiangiogenic drug treatment became an independent predictor for OS improvement. The combination of antiangiogenic drugs was found to effectively reduce the risk of death by 60%.

However, it should be noted that this study has certain limitations. First, the patient group involved in this study showed a degree of homogeneity, such as age, the timing of intracranial lesion detection, and other confounding factors that may affect clinical outcomes. This suggests that future research should adopt a prospective design and ensure balance between different cohorts. Second, this study was based on retrospective analysis and had a relatively small sample size. Especially in the discussion of the effects of salvage therapy after progression, there is an urgent need for more prospective clinical trials to further validate our findings.

Conclusions

In summary, the findings of this study revealed that for patients with EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer brain metastases who have not previously received epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR TKI) treatment, the use of a first-line combined treatment regimen of EGFR TKI and cranial radiotherapy can significantly prolong the intracranial progression-free survival (PFS). Particularly for patients carrying exon 19 positive mutations with brain metastasis lesion counts between three to five, this combined treatment regimen demonstrated a more significant effect in extending the overall survival (OS). Moreover, compared to patients who only received TKI oral treatment as first-line therapy, those who used third-generation TKI drugs in the first-line treatment showed better prognosis upon receiving second-line salvage brain radiotherapy. For patients who used first or second-generation TKIs in the first-line treatment, a third-generation TKI combined with salvage radiotherapy regimen is required during the second-line treatment phase.

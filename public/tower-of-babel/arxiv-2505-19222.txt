Asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity of the space-time discontinuous Galerkin method for the Kolmogorov equation Thanks: E. H. Georgoulis and P. J. Herbert gratefully acknowledge the financial support of EPSRC (grant number EP/W005840/2)
Zhaonan Dong, Emmanuil H. Georgoulis, Philip J. Herbert

Abstract
. We are concerned with discretisations of the classical Kolmogorov equation by a standard space-time discontinuous Galerkin method. The Kolmogorov equation serves as simple, yet rich enough in the present context, model problem for a wide range of kinetic-type equations: although it involves diffusion in one of the two spatial dimensions only, the combined nature of the first order transport/drift term and the degenerate diffusion are sufficient to ‘propagate dissipation’ across the spatial domain in its entirety. This is a manifestation of the celebrated concept of hypocoercivity, a term coined and studied extensively by Villani in [ 31 ] . We show that the classical space-time discontinuous Galerkin method admits a corresponding hypocoercivity property at the discrete level, asymptotically for large times. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result of this kind for any standard Galerkin scheme. This property is shown by proving one part of a discrete inf-sup-type stability result for the method in a family of norms dictated by a modified scalar product motivated by the theory in [ 31 ] . This family of norms contains the full gradient of the numerical solution, thereby allowing for a full spectral gap/Poincaré-type inequality at the discrete level, thus, showcasing a subtle, discretisation-parameter-dependent, numerical hypocoercivity property. Further, we show that the space-time discontinuous Galerkin method is inf-sup stable in the family of norms containing the full gradient of the numerical solution, which may be a result of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

In kinetic modelling, numerous degenerate evolution (integro-)differential equations model diffusion/dissipation along specific spatial directions only, without spanning the entirety of the spatial domain of definition of their solutions. Nonetheless, such models have been shown to exhibit convergence towards an equilibrium state over long time scales. Such models frequently emerge from integrating stochastic processes, often resulting into Kolmogorov/Fokker–Planck type partial differential equations (PDEs). A representative case includes the modelling of multi-particle systems whereby random collisions are permitted in the presence of external drift.

The term
hypocoercivity
, introduced by Villani in
[
31
]
, describes the property of evolution (integro-differential) operators admitting dissipation also in spatial directions where no diffusion is explicitly present in the operator. Villani developed a framework for sufficient conditions for hypocoercivity in the celebrated work
[
31
]
, building upon earlier seminal works by Hérau and Nier
[
21
]
, Eckmann and Hairer
[
15
]
, Hérau
[
20
]
, Dolbeault, Mouhot and Schmeiser
[
11
]
Mouhot and Neumann
[
25
]
and others. We also mention the work by Talay
[
30
]
as a key step in the development of these ideas.
A different framework for sufficient conditions for hypocoercivity was given by Dolbeault, Mouhot and Schmeiser in the seminal work
[
12
]
.

To fix ideas, in a Hilbertian setting with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
inducing a norm
\|{\cdot}\|:=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
, we consider an evolution equation of the form

(1.1)

u_{t}+\mathrm{L}u=0,\qquad\text{ whereby }\ \mathrm{L}:=\mathrm{L}_{skew}-\mathrm{L}_{diss},

with
\mathrm{L}_{skew}
corresponding to a skew-symmetric, typically “transport-like”, operator and
\mathrm{L}_{diss}
a degenerate, self-adjoint operator modelling diffusion/dissipation. Due to the skew-symmetry of
\mathrm{L}_{{skew}}
, a straightforward energy argument would imply

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|u\|^{2}=\langle\mathrm{L}_{diss}u,u\rangle\leq 0.

The degenerate nature, however, of
\mathrm{L}_{diss}
may
not
be sufficient to provide a norm so that spectral gap/Poincaré-type inequality of the form

\kappa\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}\leq-\langle\mathrm{L}_{diss}u,u\rangle

for some
\kappa>0
holds, with
\bar{u}
denoting an equilibrium state, or some kernel-eliminating functional. Thus, we
cannot
immediately conclude

\|u-\bar{u}\|\leq{\rm e}^{-\kappa t_{f}}\|u_{0}\|

i.e., exponential decay to equilibrium, with
u_{0}
denoting an initial state and
t_{f}
a final time.

However, such decay may, indeed, hold in certain settings, despite the presence of a non-trivial kernel for
\langle\mathrm{L}_{diss}u,u\rangle
, due to the degenerate nature of the dissipation. Such a state of affairs can occur, informally speaking, when
\mathrm{L}_{skew}
is “appropriately non-constant” and is able to “transport dissipation” also in the directions where no diffusion is explicitly present, that is, the equation admits the hypocoercivity property.

The proof of trend to equilibrium for the kinetic Fokker-Planck equation was given by Hérau & Nier in
[
21
]
upon modifying the inner product used to include certain mixed derivatives. Related ideas have also been used by Eckmann & Hairer in
[
15
]
for the study of spectral properties of certain hypoelliptic operators involving degenerate diffusions, and by Mouhot & Neumann
[
25
]
in the study of kinetic models with integral-type collision operators, among others. Villani
[
31
]
and Dolbeault, Mouhot, & Schmeiser
[
12
]
conceptualised the algebraic nature of the interplay between transport and dissipation properties of the spatial operator
\mathrm{L}
and were able to provide abstract sufficient conditions for hypocoercivity to hold, that is,
\|u-\bar{u}\|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\kappa t_{f}}\|u_{0}\|
,
for some constant
C>0
. They achieved this by modifying the energy setting, i.e., by constructing appropriate inner products of the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{hc}:=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle+(\!(\cdot,\cdot)\!)
, with
(\!(\cdot,\cdot)\!)
denoting an additional appropriately constructed symmetric bilinear form, whose respective induced norms are strong enough to admit spectral gap/Poincaré-type inequality and, hence, decay can be inferred. The modified inner products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{hc}
are carefully constructed to reflect the interplay between
\mathrm{L}_{skew}
and
\mathrm{L}_{diss}
.

It is highly desirable to preserve hypocoercive structures upon discretisation to guarantee that the resulting numerical schemes demonstrate robust performance for long-time computations. However, the exploration of
numerical hypocoercivity
properties remains relatively limited to date. Notable works in this area include
[
27
]
, which investigates hypocoercivity for a central difference discretisation of the Kolmogorov equation (see (
1.2
) below) within the abstract framework of
[
31
, Theorem 18]
. Further, Dujardin, Hérau, and Lafitte
[
14
]
extend this approach to examine the spectral gap for central difference discretisations of the Fokker-Planck equation. Foster, Lohéac, and Tran
[
18
]
present a Lagrangian-type splitting method employing a similarity transformation and linear finite elements on quasi-uniform meshes. Bessemoulin-Chatard and Filbet
[
2
]
propose design principles for constructing equilibrium-preserving finite volume methods for nonlinear degenerate parabolic problems. Recently, Bessemoulin-Chatard, Herda, and Rey
[
3
]
introduce an asymptotic-preserving finite volume scheme for one-dimensional kinetic equations in the diffusive limit, applying the hypocoercivity framework of Dolbeault, Mouhot, and Schmeiser
[
12
]
. For a finite-volume method of a one-dimensional nonlinear reaction kinetic model, we refer
[
4
]
.

With the exception of
[
18
]
whereby kinetic equations in two spatial dimensions are discussed, the above developments on numerical methods concern one-dimensional spatial problems and/or finite difference or finite volume methods. Developing Galerkin/finite element methods that preserve hypocoercivity structures remains a largely unexplored area, despite the advantages these methods offer, such as high-order discretisations over unstructured or locally adapted meshes.

The first work in this context is
[
19
]
, whereby a hypocoercivity-preserving family of spatially discrete non-conforming finite element methods of arbitrary order for the Kolmogorov equation

(1.2)

u_{t}+Lu=0,\quad\text{in }(0,t_{f}]\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\Omega}};

with
Lu:=-u_{xx}+xu_{y}
, for some final time
t_{f}>0
, with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, along with appropriate initial and boundary data, is constructed and analysed, following the framework in
[
31
]
. Recently, a hypocoercivity-exploiting fully-discrete, stabilised space-time finite element method is presented and analysed in
[
13
]
, which is proven to admit “numerical hypocoercivity” in an asymptotically exact sense. In both
[
19
,
13
]
, Kolmogorov equation serves as a simple model problem characterised by diffusion in one of the two spatial directions only, and, moreover, it admits a hypocoercivity-inducing algebraic structure in the spirit of
[
31
]
.

Due to their relevance in setting the context of the present work, we give a conceptual overview of the developments in
[
19
,
13
]
.
Upon defining a symmetric and positive semidefinite matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
, (see (
2.2
) below for details,) we denote by
(\!({\cdot},{\cdot})\!)_{A}
the inner product

(\!({w},{v})\!)_{A}:=(w,v)+(\nabla w,A\nabla v),

for
w,v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, with induced norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{{A}}:=\sqrt{(\!({\cdot},{\cdot})\!)_{A}}
; here
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\omega}
corresponds to the standard
L_{2}(\omega)
inner product for
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, along with respective induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\omega}
and we drop the subscript when
\omega=\Omega
, for brevity. Hence, assuming sufficient regularity, (
1.2
) implies

(1.3)

(\!({u_{t}},{v})\!)_{A}+(\!({Lu},{v})\!)_{A}=0,

for all
v
in a suitable test space. Roughly speaking, i.e., ignoring non-trivial boundary terms and regularity considerations, (
1.3
) can be viewed as arising from testing (
1.2
) against
v-\nabla\cdot A\nabla v
.
Imposing, now, homogeneous boundary conditions on the appropriate part of
\partial\Omega
, the latter gives

(1.4)

(\!({u_{t}},{v})\!)_{A}+a(u,v)=0,\quad\text{with }\ \ a(u,v):=(u_{x},v_{x})+(xu_{y},v)+(\nabla Lu,A\nabla v).

Integration by parts and elementary operations eventually give (see Section
2
below for details):

(1.5)

a(u,u)\geq c\Big(\|u_{x}\|^{2}+\|u_{y}\|^{2}+\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla u_{x}}\|^{2}+(\frac{1}{2}xn_{2}\nabla u-n_{1}\nabla u_{x},A\nabla u)_{\partial\Omega}\Big).

If the entries of
A
are taken to be fixed suitable numbers, as done in
[
19
]
, and additional boundary conditions are to treat the last term on the right-hand side of the above lower bound, we deduce

a(u,u)\geq c_{0}\|{u}\|_{{A}}^{2}
,
for some
c_{0}>0
, independent of
u
. The latter, in turn, implies

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{u}\|_{{A}}^{2}+c_{0}\|{u}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq 0,

i.e., exponential decay as
t_{f}\to\infty
. (We note that, in the present setting of a bounded spatial domain, decay results from the additional boundary conditions.) Further, in
[
19
]
a
C^{0}
-interior penalty spatially-discrete (non-conforming) finite element method with continuous elements is designed and corresponding decay is proven, thereby yielding the first, to the best of our knowledge, Galerkin method admitting a “numerical hypocoercivity” property. The respective spatial bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
corresponds to a fourth order operator; this remains the case upon Galerkin discretisation if the entries of the matrix
A
are selected independent of the discretisation parameters; we refer to
[
19
]
for details.

Starting again from (
1.4
), a stabilised non-conforming finite element method for the fully-discrete Kolmogorov problem (without additional boundary conditions) was recently developed in
[
13
]
, combining the classical streamline upwind Petrov-Galerkin (SUPG) point of view with the above modified hypocoercivity-inducing inner product to arrive at a non-standard spatial discretisation. The method is completed by a discontinuous Galerkin time-stepping scheme, to arrive at a fully-discrete space-time Galerkin method of arbitrary order. To ensure that the spatially discrete operator is spectrally equivalent to a second order discrete operator, the entries of the matrix
A
in
[
13
]
are scaled by the local mesh-size and polynomial degrees of the Galerkin spaces. For this method, the asymptotically exact numerical hypocoercivity with mesh and polynomial degree dependent exponential decay rate is proven and a complete a priori error analysis is given
[
13
]
.

In this work, we demonstrate that the classical space–time discontinuous Galerkin (dG) method— namely, the symmetric interior penalty dG method in the
x
-variable combined with an upwind dG treatment in the
y
- and
t
-variables, applied to (
1.2
) preserves an asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity structure. In stark contrast to
[
19
,
13
]
, the asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity is shown through the establishment of one side of an inf-sup condition on a sufficiently strong norm. This special norm is related to the SUPG-hypocoercive norm from
[
13
]
. We stress that the result holds for the classical space-time dG scheme
[
23
]
, i.e.,
without
the presence of additional
A
-inner-product-induced Petrov-Galerkin-type terms, as is the case is
[
19
,
13
]
; cf., (
1.3
). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first demonstration of (asymptotic) numerical hypocoercivity for a well-known/classical Galerkin method.

The classical, fully-discrete discontinuous Galerkin (dG) method for advection or advection-diffusion PDEs is known to be inf-sup stable in an SUPG-like norm
[
24
,
16
,
1
,
6
,
9
,
7
]
involving control in the advection direction. A key idea of the present developments is the observation that a term of the form
V+\tau(V_{t}+xV_{y})-\nabla\cdot A\nabla V
lies in the same discontinuous polynomial space as
V
itself, for
\tau
and
A
element-wise constant functions. This observation eventually allows for coercivity in a norm strictly stronger than the SUPG-like norms for which dG is known to be inf-sup stable. For the fully-discrete space-time dG method, we also complete the inf-sup stability in the new ‘enhanced’ norm. The new norm is strong enough to counteract the absence of spectral gap/Poincaré inequality of the standard
L_{2}
-energy argument and, thereby, to conclude asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity for the classical space-time dG scheme. To the best of our knowledge, the latter constitutes a stronger stability result to what is available in the literature to date for the space-time dG method for the Kolmogorov initial/boundary value problem. The method of proof is generic and is expected to be applicable to other related equations also.

The remainder of this work is structured as follows. In Section
2
, upon providing some necessary notation, we discuss in more detail the hypocoercivity mechanism for the model problem of the Kolmogorov equation. In Section
3
, we present its discretisation by the space(-time) discontinuous Galerkin method. In Section
4
, we present one part of the crucial inf-sup condition which, in turn, allows to conclude asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity of the spatially discrete method. In Section
5
, we extend the analysis to the fully-discrete space–time dG method. In particular, we establish the full inf–sup stability result and derive corresponding
a priori
error bounds for sufficiently regular solutions. In Section
6
, we present a series of numerical experiments confirming the theoretical developments.
Finally, in Section
7
, we draw some conclusions and give some potential extensions.

2.
Hypocoercive structure

Closing a degenerate parabolic PDE with suitable boundary conditions is well understood via the classical theory of linear second order equations with non-negative characteristic form
[
17
,
26
]
.
Assuming that
\partial\Omega
is polygonal and denoting the unit outward normal at each point of
\partial\Omega
(whenever well defined) by
\mathbf{n}:=(n_{1},n_{2})^{T}
at almost every point of
\partial\Omega

\Gamma_{0}:=\{(x,y)\in\partial\Omega:n_{1}(x,y)\neq 0\}.

We further subdivide
\partial\Omega\backslash\Gamma_{0}
, into inflow and outflow boundaries

\Gamma_{-}:=\{(x,y)\in\partial\Omega\backslash\Gamma_{0}:xn_{2}(x,y)<0\},\quad\Gamma_{+}:=\{\xi\in\partial\Omega\backslash\Gamma_{0}:xn_{2}(x,y)\geq 0\},

respectively, so that

\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}
.
Introducing the time interval
I:=(0,t_{f}]
, for a final time
t_{f}>0
, and the notation
L:=-\partial^{2}_{x}+x\partial_{y}
, the initial/boundary-value Kolmogorov problem we consider is given by:

(2.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}+Lu=

\displaystyle f,

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega,

\displaystyle u=

\displaystyle u_{0},

\displaystyle\text{on }\{0\}\times\Omega,

\displaystyle u=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\Gamma_{-},

\displaystyle n_{1}u_{x}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\Gamma_{0},

for
f\in L_{2}(I;L_{2}(\Omega))
, and

.
The well-posedness of the above problem is assured upon assuming that
\Gamma_{-}
has positive one-dimensional Hausdorff measure
[
26
]
. We note that different boundary conditions on

are admissible and give results to what is proven below under minor modifications only; we do not consider these explicitly for brevity.

The matrix

Throughout this work

will be the matrix

A:=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\

where the values of

,

, and

\alpha=\frac{1}{8\delta},\quad\beta=\frac{1}{48\delta^{2}},\quad\gamma=\frac{10}{48^{2}\delta^{3}}

With this choice of

,

, and

, we see that

is positive definite with
\det(A)=(9216\delta^{4})^{-1}
.
The choice of parameters given in (

, rather than conditions on the entries

,

, and

In

, the values of

,

Hypocoercivity

Consider a test function
v\in H^{1}_{-}(\Omega):=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):v|_{\partial_{-}\Omega}=0\}
. Assume that the exact solution,
u
, of (
2.1
) is sufficiently regular, so that the following calculations are valid.
Upon differentiation of (
2.1
) and testing against
A\nabla v
, and subsequent addition to (
2.1
) tested against
v
and integration by parts, the problem can be written in the non-standard weak form: find
u\in H^{1}_{-}(\Omega)
, such that

(2.4)

(\!({u_{t}},{v})\!)_{A}+a_{\rm hc}(u,v)=(\!({f},{v})\!)_{A},

whereby

(2.5)

a_{\rm hc}(u,v):=(u_{x},v_{x})+(xu_{y},v)+(\nabla Lu,A\nabla v).

Again, momentarily assuming that
u
is regular enough, specifically
u\in H^{1}_{-}(\Omega)\cap H^{2.5+\epsilon}(\Omega)
, so that
(\frac{1}{2}xn_{2}\nabla u-n_{1}\nabla u_{x},A\nabla u)_{\partial\Omega}
is well defined, we evaluate
{a_{\rm hc}(u,u)}
and perform integrations by parts, to deduce

(2.6)

\displaystyle a_{\rm hc}(u,u)=

\displaystyle\|{u_{x}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\sqrt{xn_{2}}u}\|_{{\Gamma_{+}}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla u_{x}}\|^{2}+\beta\|{u_{y}}\|^{2}+\alpha(u_{y},u_{x})

\displaystyle+(\frac{1}{2}xn_{2}\nabla u-n_{1}\nabla u_{x},A\nabla u)_{\partial\Omega};

the detailed calculation is given in
[
19
]
. Moreover, in
[
19
]
, additional boundary conditions are considered, so that the unsigned boundary term on right-hand side of (
2.6
) is non-negative and, thus, can be omitted. In this work, we do
not
assume any such condition and we will make
A
small, so that the boundary terms may be absorbed in the positive terms at the discrete level, as done also in
[
13
]
.

Returning to the continuous problem, for the sake of highlighting the ‘mechanics’ of hypocoercivity, we assume
momentarily
that
(\frac{1}{2}xn_{2}\nabla u-n_{1}\nabla u_{x},A\nabla u)_{\partial\Omega}\geq 0
. This can be achieved, for instance, if
\nabla u=\mathbf{0}
on
\partial\Omega
; we refer to
[
19
]
for other possibilities. Now, applying Young’s and Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequalities, and upon discarding non-negative terms, we deduce

(2.7)

a_{\rm hc}(u,u)\geq\frac{1}{4}\|u_{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{64\delta^{2}}\|u_{y}\|^{2},

by the choices of
\alpha
and
\beta
made in (
2.3
).
It then follows that there is
c>0
, depending on
\delta
such that
a_{\rm hc}(u,u)\geq c\|{u}\|_{{A}}^{2}
, thus one has

(2.8)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|{u}\|_{{A}}^{2}+c\|{u}\|_{{A}}^{2}\leq(\!({f},{u})\!)_{A},

which allows for stability estimates that do not depend exponentially on the final time

. In other words, using the non-standard test function
v-\nabla\cdot(A\nabla v)
for well-chosen
A
, results in
L
providing coercivity also with respect to
\|u_{y}\|
.
This is a manifestation of the concept of hypocoercivity: the nature of the trasport operator

is such that it can “disseminate” the available dissipation of the problem,
-\partial_{x}^{2}
, in all spatial directions. Villani showed in
[
31
]
that sufficient conditions for a degenerate operator admitting such hypocoercivity property can be of purely algebraic nature and, in particular, they concern the study of linear spaces spanned by certain commutators between the transport and the dissipation parts of the operator. In the particular case of (
1.2
), since
[x\partial_{y},\partial_{x}]=\partial_{y}
, we can expect hypocoercive structure since
\partial_{x}
and
\partial_{y}
correspond to directional derivatives along the axes and, thus, they span
\Omega
; we refer to
[
31
, Theorem 24]
for details.

3.
Discrete spaces and problem formulation

3.1.
Space discretisation

For the rest of this work,
\Omega
is a Lipschitz polygonal domain. We introduce a family of subdivisions
\mathcal{T}_{H}=\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h\in H}
, for some index set
H
, in
\Omega
, consisting of mutually disjoint open triangular or box-type elements
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, whose closures cover
\bar{\Omega}
exactly.
Also, let
h\colon\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}T\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the local meshsize function defined elementwise by
h|_{T}:=h_{T}:={\rm diam}(T)
.
Each
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is assumed to be shape-regular, in the sense that the radius
\rho_{T}
of the largest inscribed circle of each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is bounded from below with respect to each element’s diameter
h_{T}
, uniformly in the index set
H
.
Also, we assume that each
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is locally quasi-uniform in the sense that the diameters of adjacent elements are uniformly bounded from above and below in the index set
H
. Finally, for each
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
\Gamma:=\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\partial T
denote the mesh skeleton, and define

\Gamma_{\rm int}:=\{x\in\Gamma:x\not\in\partial\Omega\},

the interior part of the mesh skeleton.

For each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define its inflow and outflow boundaries,

\partial_{-}T:=\{(x,y)\in\partial T:xn_{2}(x,y)<0\}\mbox{, }\quad\partial_{+}T:=\{(x,y)\in\partial T:xn_{2}(x,y)\geq 0\},

respectively.
Given
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the trace of a function
w\in H^{1}(T)
on
\partial_{-}T
, relative to
T
, is denoted by
w_{T}^{+}
. Further, if
\partial_{-}T\backslash\Gamma_{-}\neq\emptyset
, then for
x\in\partial_{-}T\backslash\Gamma_{-}
, there exists a unique
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
x\in\partial_{+}T^{\prime}
; with this notation, we denote by
w_{T}^{-}
the trace of
w|_{T^{\prime}}
on
\partial_{-}T\backslash\Gamma_{-}
. Hence the
upwind jump
of the (scalar-valued) function
w
across a face
F\in\partial_{-}T\backslash\Gamma_{-}
is denoted by

\lfloor w\rfloor:=w^{+}_{T}-w^{-}_{T}.

We define the
jump

[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]
of
w
across the common element interface
e:=\partial T^{i}\cap\partial T^{j}
of two adjacent elements
T^{i},T^{j}
, by
[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]|_{e}:=w|_{T^{i}}-w|_{T^{j}}
, when
i>j
, for the indices; correspondingly, we also define the
average

\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{w}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}|_{e}:=(w|_{T^{i}}+w|_{T^{j}})/2
of

across
e
.
In addition, for boundary faces
e\subset\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
, we set
[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]|_{e}:=w|_{T}
and
\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{w}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}|_{e}:=w|_{T}
, respectively. The above jump and average definitions are trivially extended to vector-valued functions by component-wise application.

We denote the
broken Sobolev spaces
, subordinate to a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
by

H^{r}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{v|_{T}\in H^{r}(T),T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

Further, we define the broken gradient
\nabla_{h}
to be the element-wise gradient operator with
\nabla_{h}w|_{T}=\nabla w|_{T}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, for
w\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})
. Similarly, we define the broken divergence operator
{\rm div}_{h}
to be the element-wise divergence operator.

We consider the family of discontinuous element-wise polynomial spaces subordinate to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, defined by

{\mathcal{V}}_{h}\equiv{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{p}:=\{V\in L_{2}(\Omega):V|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(T),\ T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

with
\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)
,
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoting the space of polynomials of total degree at most
p
over
\omega
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

We will use the standard
hp
-version inverse inequalities (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
):

(3.1)

\|V\|_{\partial T}\leq\frac{C_{\partial}p}{\sqrt{h_{T}}}\|V\|_{T},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla V\|_{T}\leq\frac{C_{\nabla}p^{2}}{h_{T}}\|V\|_{T},

which hold of for all
V\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(K)
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
with the constants
C_{\partial}>0
and
C_{\nabla}>0
, depending only on the space dimension
d
and the mesh shape-regularity constant
C_{\rho}>0
, defined as the global constant, such that
h_{T}/h_{T^{\prime}}\leq C_{\rho}
,
for all
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
sharing a
(d-1)
-face, and for all
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h\in H}
.

For spatial discretisation, we select the
classical
(upwind) discontinuous Galerkin method of Houston, Schwab and Süli
[
23
]
for equations with non-negative characteristic form. For the particular case of the Kolmogorov equation, the dG bilinear form from from
[
23
]
discretising
Lu
reads:

(3.2)

\begin{split}a_{\rm dG}(U,V):=&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(({U_{x}},{V_{x}})_{{T}}+({xU_{y}},{V})_{{T}}\right)\\
&-({\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{{n}_{1}U_{x}}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}},{[\kern-1.70717pt[{V}]\kern-1.70717pt]})_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}-({\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{{n}_{1}V_{x}}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}},{[\kern-1.70717pt[{U}]\kern-1.70717pt]})_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}+({\sigma[\kern-1.70717pt[{U}]\kern-1.70717pt]},{[\kern-1.70717pt[{V}]\kern-1.70717pt]})_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}\\
&-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(({x{n}_{2}\lfloor U\rfloor},{V^{+}})_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}+({x{n}_{2}U^{+}},{V^{+}})_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{-}}}\right),\end{split}

where
\sigma\colon\Gamma_{\rm int}\to\mathbb{R}
is a penalty term whose precise expression is to be provided later. So, the spatially discrete method reads: find
U\in H^{1}(I;{\mathcal{V}}_{h})
, such that

(3.3)

B_{\rm s}(U,V):=\int_{0}^{{t_{\rm f}}}\left(({U_{t}},{V})+a_{\rm dG}(U,V)\right)\,{\rm dt}=\int_{0}^{t_{\rm f}}({f},{V})=:\ell_{\rm s}(V)

for all
V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
, and
U(t_{0}):=\pi u_{0}
, with
\pi\colon L_{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
denoting, the orthogonal
L_{2}
-projection onto
{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
.

3.2.
Time discretisation

We subdivide
I=(0,t_{f}]
, into the family
\mathcal{I}:=\{I_{n}:n=1,\ldots,N\}
, where
I_{n}:=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
for a strictly increasing sequence
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{N}=t_{f}
, and let
k\colon I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
k|_{I_{n}}:=k_{n}=t_{n}-t_{n-1}
denote each timestep.
Further, we define a family of space-time (discontinuous) finite element spaces subordinate to
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}
by

{\mathcal{V}}_{h,k}\equiv{\mathcal{V}}_{h,k}^{p,q}:=\{V\in L_{2}(I;{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{p}):V|_{I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{q}(I_{n}),n=1,\dots,N\}

for
q\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{V}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n}
the time-jump across
t_{n}
, viz.,

\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{V}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n}:=V(t_{n}^{+})-V(t_{n}^{-}):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}V(t_{n}+\epsilon)-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}V(t_{n}-\epsilon)\quad\text{for }n=1,\dots,N.

For later reference, we recall the standard one-dimensional inverse estimates, see, e.g.,
[
29
]
: for
V\in\mathbb{P}_{q}(I_{n})
,
n=1,\dots,N
, we have

(3.4)

|V(t_{n}^{-})|\leq(q+1)k_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{V}\|_{{I_{n}}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|{V^{\prime}}\|_{{I_{n}}}\leq\sqrt{12}(q+1)^{2}k_{n}^{-1}\|V\|_{I_{n}}.

3.2.1.
Fully-discrete scheme

Starting from (
3.3
), we further discretise the
t
-variable, resulting to the classical space-time dG method: find
U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h,k}
such that

(3.5)

B_{\rm st}(U,V)=\ell_{\rm st}(V),\quad\text{ for all }\ V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h,k},

with

(3.6)

B_{\rm st}(U,V):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{I_{n}}\!\!\big(({U_{t}},{V})+a_{\rm dG}(U,V)\big)\,{\rm dt}+\sum_{n=2}^{N}({\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{U}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})})+({U(t_{0}^{+})},{V(t_{0}^{+})}),

and

\ell_{\rm st}(V):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{I_{n}}({f},{V})\,{\rm dt}+(U(t_{0}^{-}),V(t_{0}^{+})),

where it is again convenient to choose
U(t_{0}^{-}):=\pi u_{0}
, as it can be implemented efficiently by inverting a block-diagonal mass matrix. We note carefully that (
3.5
) is, again, the
classical
upwind discontinuous Galerkin method of Houston, Schwab and Süli
[
23
]
for equations with non-negative characteristic form in three variables, (i.e., inclusive of
t
,) upon selecting a three-dimensional space-time mesh as above.

Of course, due to the “space-time slab”-like selection of the mesh and the choice of the test function, in practice (
3.5
) is typically solved on each time-step, viz.,

\int_{I_{n}}\!\!\big(({U_{t}},{V})+a_{\rm dG}(U,V)\big)\!\,{\rm dt}+({U(t_{n-1}^{+})},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})})=\!\int_{I_{n}}\!({f},{V})\!\,{\rm dt}+({U(t_{n-1}^{-})},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})}),

for all
V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h,k}
,
n=1,\dots,N
. Later, we shall make use of the following notation, for
n=1,\dots,N
:

(3.7)

B_{n}(U,V):=\int_{I_{n}}\!\!\big(({U_{t}},{V})+a_{\rm dG}(U,V)\big)\!\,{\rm dt}+({\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{U}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})}).

The well-posedness of the fully-discrete problem follows from its coercivity; see
[
23
]
, or Theorem
5.4
below, for details.

4.
Asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity of the semi-discrete scheme

The strategy of the proof is to first demonstrate positivity in a stronger, hypocoercivity-inducing norm for the spatially discrete method (
3.3
) by choosing optimal test functions of the form

(4.1)

V:=U+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\tau_{T}(xU_{y}+U_{t})|_{T}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big(-{\rm div}(A\nabla U)|_{T}\big)\equiv V_{1}+V_{2}+V_{3},

with appropriate choices of
\tau
defined in (
4.3
), and
\delta
for
A
, defined in (
4.4
) below, respectively.

Remark 4.1
.

A trivial, yet crucial aspect in the proof, is that
V
is, indeed, admissible test function, since
V\in C^{0}(I;{\mathcal{V}}_{h})
, due to the nature of the first order transport term
xU_{y}+U_{t}
which is element-wise a polynomial of
total
order
p
and, of course, due to the use of discontinuous approximation space. This is in sharp contrast with previous works
[
19
,
13
]
, whereby the spatial discretisation employs standard, continuous, Lagrange elements, thereby requiring additional non-standard Petrov-Galerkin-type terms in the bilinear form, to ensure coercivity in a hypocoercivity-inducing norm.

For the spatially discrete setting of this section, we set
\sigma:\Gamma_{\rm int}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined face-wise by

(4.2)

\sigma|_{F}:=64N_{\partial}(C_{\partial}n_{1}^{F}p)^{2}\max_{i=1,2}h_{T_{i}}^{-1},\quad\text{for }F=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2},\ T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

with
n_{1}^{\omega}:=\|{n_{1}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\omega)}}
, for any
\omega\subset\Gamma_{\rm int}
, and
N_{\partial}
denoting the number of faces per element. Also, we define
\tau:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
element-wise by

(4.3)

\begin{split}\tau|_{T}=\tau_{T}:=&\frac{h_{T}^{2}}{p^{4}}\Big(\frac{65C_{\nabla}^{2}}{6}+\frac{3\sigma_{T}^{2}h_{T}^{2}}{64p^{4}}+\frac{(8x_{n_{2}}^{T}C_{\partial})^{2}h_{T}}{3p^{2}}\Big)^{-1},\quad\text{for }T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\end{split}

with
\sigma_{T}:=\max_{F\in\partial T}{\sigma_{F}}
and
x_{n_{2}}^{T}:=\|{{xn_{2}}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\partial_{-}T)}}
, for notational brevity. We note that
\tau\sim h^{2}/p^{4}
as
h/p\to 0
, with
\sim
denoting proportionality. In addition, we set

(4.4)

\begin{split}\delta_{T}:=&\ \max\bigg\{1,\ \frac{C_{\partial}^{2}p^{4}}{h_{T}^{2}}\max\Big\{4+\frac{C_{\nabla}^{2}}{8C_{\partial}^{2}},\frac{(x_{n_{2}}^{T})^{2}h_{T}}{p^{2}},\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{T}}\Big\}\bigg\},\quad\text{for all $T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}$}.\end{split}

whereby

\mathcal{R}_{T}:=2h_{T}^{2}p^{-4}\tau_{T}^{-1}+1040N_{\partial}(n_{1}^{F}C_{\rho}C_{\partial}^{-1})^{2}+8(C_{\nabla}C_{\partial}^{-1}x_{n_{2}}^{T})^{2}p^{-2}h_{T}
.
Note that
\delta\geq 1
, which implies that
\alpha,\beta,\gamma<1
, and, also, we have

\delta\sim p^{4}/h^{2}
, as
h/p\to 0

For later use we define the dG-seminorm:

(4.5)

\begin{split}\|{U}\|_{{\rm dG}}&:=\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|{U_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{U}\|_{{\rm uw}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{\sigma}[\kern-1.70717pt[{U}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

with the upwind terms collected in

\begin{split}\|{U}\|_{{\rm uw}}&:=\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big(\|{\sqrt{|x{n}_{2}|}U}\|_{{(\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{-})\cup(\partial_{+}T\cap\Gamma)}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{|x{n}_{2}|}\lfloor U\rfloor}\|_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}\big)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

The dG bilinear form
a_{\rm dG}(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive with respect to
\|{\cdot}\|_{{\rm dG}}
, see
[
23
]
or Lemma
4.2
below for a proof. Crucially, however,
\|{\cdot}\|_{{\rm dG}}
may fail to be a norm in
{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
. Thus, the standard stability analysis in
[
23
]
does not apply.

The idea of the analysis below is to enhance
\|{\cdot}\|_{{\rm dG}}
with additional terms to result into a norm in
{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
, which will be shown to satisfy a discretisation parameter-dependent Poincaré inequality for functions in
{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
and, thus, we will eventually conclude the asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity property announced above. The proof will be split to a number of intermediate results, showing stability against each
V_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, cf., (
4.1
) above, before collecting the bounds and completing the argument.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
U\in H^{1}(I;{\mathcal{V}}_{h})
and let
\sigma
as in (

). Then, a.e. in

({U_{t}},{V_{1}})+a_{\rm dG}(U,V_{1})\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm dt}}\|{U}\|^{2}+\frac{7}{8}\|{U}\|_{{\rm dG}}^{2}+\frac{1}{16}\|{U}\|_{{\rm uw}}^{2}.

Proof.

Recalling that
V_{1}=U
and employing the trace inverse estimate (
3.1
), we have, respectively,

(4.6)

\displaystyle({\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{{n}_{1}U_{x}}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}},{[\kern-1.70717pt[{V_{1}}]\kern-1.70717pt]})_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{\rm int}}\phi^{-1}(C_{\partial}n_{1}^{F}p)^{2}\sum_{T\in\omega_{F}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{U_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\sqrt{\phi}[\kern-1.70717pt[{V_{1}}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|{U_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\sqrt{\phi}[\kern-1.70717pt[{V_{1}}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}

for

, with

denoting the number of faces per element,
\omega_{F}
is the collection of two elements sharing the face
F
, upon selecting
\phi|_{F}=N_{\partial}(C_{\partial}n_{1}^{F}p)^{2}\max_{T\in\omega_{F}}h_{T}^{-1}
, when
n_{1}^{F}\neq 0
; when

, no further estimation is required.

Now, integration by parts and investigation of the flux terms gives the identity

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\!\!\Big(({xU_{y}},{U})_{{T}}\!-\!({x{n}_{2}\lfloor U\rfloor},{U^{+}})_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}\!+\!({x{n}_{2}U^{+}},{U^{+}})_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{-}}}\!\Big)\!=\frac{1}{2}\|{U}\|_{{\rm uw}}^{2};\end{split}

we refer to
[

\begin{split}a_{\rm dG}(U,U)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|{U_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}-2({\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{{n}_{1}U_{x}}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}},{[\kern-1.70717pt[{U}]\kern-1.70717pt]})_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}+\|{\sqrt{\sigma}[\kern-1.70717pt[{U}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{U}\|_{{\rm uw}}^{2}.\end{split}

Employing (
4.6
) with

, along with the identity
({U_{t}},{U})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm dt}}\|{U}\|^{2}
and the splitting
\frac{1}{2}\|{U}\|_{{\rm uw}}^{2}=\big(\frac{7}{16}+\frac{1}{16}\big)\|{U}\|_{{\rm uw}}^{2}
, completes the proof, upon recalling the definition of
\sigma
.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(mesh-dependent spectral gap)
.

There exists a number
\kappa\equiv\kappa(h,p)>0
, depending on
h_{\min}:=\min_{\Omega}h
, on the polynomial degree

, on the domain geometry, and on the mesh shape-regularity constant

, such that, for all

(4.13)

|\kern-0.85355pt\|{w}|\kern-0.85355pt\|^{2}\geq\kappa\,\|{w}\|_{{A,h}}^{2}.

Moreover, as

, we have
\kappa\equiv\kappa(h,p)\sim h^{4}_{\min}p^{-8}

Proof.

Let
B:={\rm diag}(1,\alpha^{2})
, recalling that
\alpha=(8\delta)^{-1}
. We employ the splitting

\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla_{h}w}\|^{2}=\|{\sqrt{B-\nu A}\nabla_{h}w}\|^{2}+\|{\sqrt{\nu A}\nabla_{h}w}\|^{2},

for
\nu=(2\delta)^{-1}
. Then,

is positive definite with smallest eigenvalue satisfying
\lambda_{1}^{B-\nu A}\geq\frac{\det(B-\nu A)}{1-\nu\alpha+|\nu\beta|}\geq(228\delta^{2})^{-1}
; cf., Lemma
A.1
.

Since for a face
F=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
shared by two elements
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we have
\max_{i=1,2}h_{i}^{-1}\geq(\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{h}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}|_{F})^{-1}
, and since
64N_{\partial}p^{2}\geq 1
, we already have the lower bound

(4.14)

\displaystyle 2|\kern-0.85355pt\|{w}|\kern-0.85355pt\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\min_{\Omega}\frac{1}{228\delta^{2}}\|{\nabla_{h}w}\|^{2}+\|{\sqrt{\nu A}\nabla_{h}w}\|^{2}+\|{n_{1}\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{h}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|{\sqrt{n_{2}x}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{|n_{2}x|}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}}^{2}.

To conclude the proof it is sufficient to find suitable

, such that

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big(\|{n_{1}\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{h}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\partial T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{n_{2}x}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}\Big)+\|{\sqrt{|n_{2}x|}w}\|_{{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle L(h,p)\Big(\|{\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{h}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}+\|{h^{-\frac{1}{2}}w}\|_{{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}}^{2}\Big).

To that end, we first observe that the left-hand side sum runs through all the internal faces in the mesh. Indeed, each element face
e
in
\Gamma_{\rm int}
is either inflow or outflow. If it is outflow, for a given element, it is inflow of the element “downstream”.

First consider a face

, with

, i.e., a “vertical-like” face. Then,
n_{1}^{2}=1-n_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{3}{4}
and, so,
\|{n_{1}\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{h}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{e}}^{2}\geq\frac{3}{4}\|{\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{h}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{e}}^{2}
.

Now, we consider “horizontal-like” faces for which
|n_{2}|\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
We set
\bar{h}_{\min}:=\frac{1}{2}\min\{1,h_{\min}\}
, and we separate two cases: a)
|x|\geq 2\bar{h}_{\min}
for all

, i.e., “sufficiently far” from the

-axis, and b)
|x|<2\bar{h}_{\min}
for some

, that is “very close” or crossing the

-axis.

For a), we have

, to conclude
\|{\sqrt{|n_{2}x|}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{e}}^{2}\geq 2\bar{h}_{\min}^{2}\|{\{\kern-3.41432pt\{{h}\}\kern-3.41432pt\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[\kern-1.70717pt[{w}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{e}}^{2}

For b), (that is,

is a “horizontal-like” face and is “very close” or crosses the

to the

,

\displaystyle 2|\kern-0.85355pt\|{w}|\kern-0.85355pt\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle+\|{\sqrt{\nu A}\nabla_{h}w}\|^{2}

Theorem 4.8

(Asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity of the semi-discrete scheme)
.

Let
U\in H^{1}(I;{\mathcal{V}}_{h})
be the solution of the semi-discrete scheme (

) for

. Then, we have

(4.17)

\|{U(t_{f})}\|_{{{A,h}}}\leq e^{-\kappa t_{f}/4}\|{U(0)}\|_{{{A,h}}},

where
\kappa\equiv\kappa(h,p)\sim h^{4}_{\min}p^{-8}
, as

.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.6
and Proposition
4.5
, we have, respectively,

(4.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm dt}}\|{U}\|_{{{A,h}}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{4}\|{U}\|_{{{A,h}}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm dt}}\|{U}\|_{{{A,h}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|^{2}\leq({U_{t}},{V})+a(U,V)=0.

The result follows by integration with respect to
t
in the interval

, noting that
U(0)\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
∎

The above result shows that the classical discontinuous Galerkin method retains an “asymptotically preserving” hypocoercivity structure at the discrete level, in the sense that the spectral gap becomes significant for
t_{f}>\kappa^{-1}\sim p^{8}h_{\min}^{-4}
. This is, perhaps, expected, due to the bounded domain and the boundary conditions. We refer to the preservation of this structure by a numerical method as
numerical hypocoercivity
.

5.

The respective result for the fully-discrete scheme (

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

The first integral on the right-hand side of the last equality can be handled completely analogously to Lemma

\begin{split}-({\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{U}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}},{{\rm div}_{h}(A\nabla_{h}U)(t_{n-1}^{+})})=&\ ({\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{\nabla U}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}},{(A\nabla_{h}U)(t_{n-1}^{+})})\\

5.2.
Hypococercivity-enhanced inf-sup stability

A by-product of the proof of Theorem
5.4
is the bound (
5.10
), viz.,
B_{\rm st}(U,V)\geq|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}^{2}
, with
V
given in (
5.1
). This is one side of the proof of an inf-sup condition in the hypocoercivity-enhanced norm
|\kern-0.85355pt\|{\cdot}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}
for the
classical
space-time dG method. To complete the proof of the inf-sup condition, we need also to show that
|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}\leq C|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}
. This given in the final main result of this work.

Theorem 5.6
.

There exists a positive constant
\Lambda
, independent of discretization parameters
h_{T}
,
k_{n}
,
p
and
q
, (but depends on
\|{x}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}}
,) such that:

(5.15)

\inf_{U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h,k}\backslash\{0\}}\sup_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h,k}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{B_{\rm st}(U,V)}{|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}}\geq\Lambda.

Proof.

To show this, it remains to prove that there exists positive constant
\Lambda>0
, independent of discretization parameters
h
,
k_{n}
,
p
and
q
, such that

(5.16)

|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}\leq|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}+|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V_{2}}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}+|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V_{3}}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}\leq\Lambda^{-1}|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}.

for
V
given in (
5.1
). This bound, together with
B_{\rm st}(U,V)\geq|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}^{2}
, proves (

).

To streamline notation, we set
C_{hp}:=\max\{C_{\partial}^{2},C_{\nabla}\}p^{2}h_{T}^{-1}
, so that the inverse estimates (
3.1
) can be expressed as

(5.17)

\|{Z}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}\leq C_{hp}\|{Z}\|_{{T}}^{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\nabla Z}\|_{{T}}^{2}\leq C_{hp}^{2}\|{Z}\|_{{T}}^{2},

for all

,
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. On each

, we note the bounds

(5.18)

\alpha\leq h_{T}^{2}C_{\nabla}^{-2}p^{-4}\leq\bar{c}C_{hp}^{-2},\qquad\tau_{T,n}\leq\frac{2}{3}\bar{c}C_{hp}^{-2},\quad\text{ and }\quad\tau_{T,n}\leq\frac{k_{n}}{64(q+1)^{2}},

with
\bar{c}:=\max\{1,C_{\partial}^{4}C_{\nabla}^{-2}\}
, from the definitions of
\tau_{T,n}
and
\delta_{T,n}
. We shall make frequent use of (
5.17
) and (
5.18
) below without explicit announcement in the text.

We first show
|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V_{2}}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}\leq C_{2}|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}
for a constant
C_{2}
independent of
h,p,q,k
. To that end, we begin by setting, as before,
W|_{I_{n}\times T}:=(U_{t}+xU_{y})|_{I_{n}\times T}
,
n=1,\dots,N
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, for notational brevity. Then, on each time interval
I_{n}
, we have

\begin{split}\|{\sqrt{\sigma}[\kern-1.70717pt[{\tau W}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{I_{n}\times\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}\leq&\ 2\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|{\sqrt{\sigma}{\tau W}}\|_{{I_{n}\times\partial T}}^{2}\\
\leq&\ 2\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sigma_{T}\tau_{T,n}C_{hp}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq c_{1}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2},\end{split}

with
c_{i}>0
(see also below),
i\in\mathbb{N}
, independent of
h
, of
p
, and of the functions involved.
Next, since
|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|\leq 2\alpha\leq 2\bar{c}C_{hp}^{-2}
, we have

\begin{split}\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla(\tau W)_{x}}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}&\leq C_{hp}^{4}|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|\tau_{T,n}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq c_{2}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}\|{\alpha(\tau W)_{y}}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}&\leq C_{hp}^{2}\alpha^{2}\tau_{T,n}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq c_{3}C_{hp}^{-4}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}.\end{split}

Similarly, we also have
\|{(\tau W)_{x}}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq c_{4}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}
.

Now, triangle inequality and the estimates (
3.4
) and (

) imply

with

Also,

\|{\sqrt{x{n}_{2}A}\nabla(\tau W)}\|_{{I_{n}\times\partial_{+}T}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{|x{n}_{2}|}\tau W}\|_{{I_{n}\times\partial T}}^{2}\leq c_{6}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}.

Finally, for the time discretisation terms, using (

\begin{split}\|{\tau W(t_{n-1}^{\pm})|_{T}}\|_{{A}}^{2}=&\ \|{\tau W(t_{n-1}^{\pm})}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\|{\tau\sqrt{A}\nabla W(t_{n-1}^{\pm})}\|_{{T}}^{2}\\
\leq&\ (q+1)^{2}\tau_{T,m}k_{m}^{-1}(1+2\bar{c})\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{m}\times T}}^{2}\leq c_{7}\|{\sqrt{\tau}W}\|_{{I_{m}\times T}}^{2},\end{split}

with

for

and

for

for

independent of

.

To conclude, we also need to show that
|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V_{3}}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}\leq C_{3}|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}
for a constant
C_{3}>0
independent of
h,p,q,k
. We start by observing that

(5.19)

\begin{split}\|\sqrt{A}\nabla U\|_{T}^{2}&=\alpha\|U_{x}\|_{T}^{2}+\frac{8\alpha^{2}}{3}(U_{x},U_{y})_{T}+\frac{20\alpha^{3}}{9}\|U_{y}\|_{T}^{2}\leq 2\alpha\|U_{x}\|_{T}^{2}+\frac{28\alpha^{3}}{9}\|U_{y}\|_{T}^{2},\end{split}

or
\|\sqrt{A}\nabla U\|_{T}^{2}\leq 4\alpha\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{T}}^{2}
, with

as in the proof of Lemma

.
We consider each term in
|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V_{3}}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}^{2}
separately, starting from

\|{\sqrt{\sigma}[\kern-1.70717pt[{{\rm div}(A\nabla U)}]\kern-1.70717pt]}\|_{{I_{n}\times\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}4\sigma_{T}C_{hp}^{3}|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{1}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla_{h}U}\|^{2},

with
\tilde{c}_{i}>0
(see also below),
i\in\mathbb{N}
, independent of
h,p,q,k
and of the functions involved.
Also, we have, respectively,

\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla\left({\rm div}(A\nabla U)\right)_{x}}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq 4\alpha C_{hp}^{6}|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|^{2}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{2}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2},

\|{\alpha\big({\rm div}(A\nabla U)\big)_{y}}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq 4\alpha^{3}C_{hp}^{4}|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{3}C_{hp}^{-4}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2},

and

\|{{\rm div}(A\nabla U)_{x}}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq 4\alpha C_{hp}^{4}|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{4}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}.

Further, recalling (
5.4
), we see that
\alpha<\underline{c}\sqrt{k_{n}}(q+1)^{-1}C_{hp}^{-1}
, with
\underline{c}:=\max\{1,C_{\nabla}C_{\partial}^{-2}\}
, so that

\begin{split}&\|{\sqrt{\tau}(({\rm div}(A\nabla U))_{t}+x({\rm div}(A\nabla U))_{y})}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\\
\leq&8\alpha C_{hp}^{2}\big(12(q+1)^{4}k_{n}^{-2}\!+\!\|{{x}}\|_{{L_{\infty}(T)}}^{2}C_{hp}^{4}\big)|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|\tau_{T,n}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{5}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}.\end{split}

Also, we have

\begin{split}&\|{\sqrt{x{n}_{2}A}\nabla({\rm div}(A\nabla U))}\|_{{I_{n}\times\partial_{+}T}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{|x{n}_{2}|}{\rm div}(A\nabla U)}\|_{{I_{n}\times\partial T}}^{2}\\
\leq&\ \|{x}\|_{{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}}^{2}\big(C_{hp}^{5}|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|^{2}+C_{hp}^{3}|A|_{I_{n}\times T}|\big)\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{6}C_{hp}^{-1}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{n}\times T}}^{2}.\end{split}

Finally, employing again (
5.18
) and
\alpha<\underline{c}\sqrt{k_{n}}(q+1)^{-1}C_{hp}^{-1}
, we have

\begin{split}&\|{{\rm div}(A\nabla U)(t_{n-1}^{\pm})|_{T}}\|_{{A}}^{2}\\
=&\ \|{{\rm div}(A\nabla U)(t_{n-1}^{\pm})}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla{\rm div}(A\nabla U)(t_{n-1}^{\pm})}\|_{{T}}^{2}\\
\leq&\ \frac{(q+1)^{2}}{k_{m}}\Big(C_{hp}^{2}|A|_{I_{m}\times T}|+C_{hp}^{4}|A|_{I_{m}\times T}|^{2}\Big)\|{\sqrt{A}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{m}\times T}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{7}\|{\sqrt{B}\nabla U}\|_{{I_{m}\times T}}^{2},\end{split}

for
m:=n+\frac{\pm 1-1}{2}
. Combining the above bounds, we arrive at
|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V_{3}}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}\leq C_{3}|\kern-0.85355pt\|{U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}
for
C_{2}>0
independent of
h,p,q,k
, which concludes the proof.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\max_{T}h_{T}<1
and

. If the exact solution satisfies

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{I_{n}}\bigg(h_{T}^{2s}\Big(k_{n}h_{T}^{2}|\partial_{t}u|_{H^{s+1}(T)}^{2}+(1+k_{n}^{-1}h_{T}^{2})|u|_{H^{s+1}(T)}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+k_{n}^{2r-1}\Big((k_{n}+h_{T}^{2})\|{\partial_{t}^{r}\nabla v}\|_{{T}}^{2}+k_{n}h_{T}\|\partial_{t}^{r}\nabla^{2}v\|_{T}^{2}+k_{n}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\partial_{t}^{r}v}\|_{{T}}^{2}\Big)\bigg)\,{\rm d}t

,

, and of

,

,

.

Proof.

We begin by decomposing the error as follows:
e=\eta+\xi
, with
\eta=u-\pi_{\rm st}u
and

. The triangle inequality, Galerkin orthogonality, and the inf-sup stability (

), imply

\begin{split}|\kern-0.85355pt\|{u-U}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}\leq|\kern-0.85355pt\|{\xi}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}+|\kern-0.85355pt\|{\eta}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda}\sup_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h,k}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{|B_{\rm st}(\eta,V)|}{|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}}+|\kern-0.85355pt\|{\eta}|\kern-0.85355pt\|_{\rm st}.\end{split}

First, we estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.26
) from above.
Integration by parts for the time derivative in (
3.6
), gives

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{I_{n}}({\eta_{t}},{V})\,{\rm dt}+\sum_{n=2}^{N}({\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{\eta}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}},{V(t_{n-1}^{+})})+({\eta(t_{0}^{+})},{V(t_{0}^{+})})\\
=&-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{I_{n}}({\eta},{V_{t}})\,{\rm dt}-\sum_{n=2}^{N}({\eta(t_{n-1}^{-})},{\lfloor\kern-1.13809pt{V}\kern-1.13809pt\rfloor_{n-1}})+({\eta(t_{N}^{-})},{V(t_{N}^{-})}),\end{split}

where, since

.
Furthermore, for

Element-wise integration by parts for the advection term gives

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big(({x\pi_{q}^{t}\eta_{y}},{V})_{{T}}-({x{n}_{2}\pi_{q}^{t}\lfloor\eta\rfloor},{V^{+}})_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}+({x{n}_{2}\pi_{q}^{t}\eta^{+}},{V^{+}})_{{\partial_{-}T\cap\Gamma_{-}}}\big)

Thus, using the stability of

\displaystyle|\int_{I_{n}}a_{\rm dG}(\eta,V)\,{\rm d}t|\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{I_{n}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big(\|{\pi_{q}^{t}\eta_{x}}\|_{{T}}^{2}+\|{\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\pi_{q}^{t}\eta_{x}}\|_{{\partial T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|{\sqrt{\sigma}\pi_{q}^{t}\eta}\|_{{\partial T\cap\Gamma_{\rm int}}}^{2}+\|{\sqrt{|x{n}_{2}|}\pi_{q}^{t}\eta}\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{I_{n}}|\kern-0.85355pt\|{V}|\kern-0.85355pt\|^{2}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

7.
Conclusions and extensions

We have shown that the standard, classical, space-time discontinuous Galerkin method discretising the Kolmogorov equation on a bounded spatial domain admits a subtle, mesh-dependent hypocoercivity property at the discrete level, asymptotically for large times. To our knowledge, this is the first result of this kind for any standard Galerkin scheme. Kolmogorov initial/boundary value problem on a bounded domain with standard no-flux conditions is not expected to admit decaying solutions for
all
initial conditions. The asymptotic numerical hypocoercivity results proven above are consistent with the latter statement, in the sense that the decay as
t\to\infty
proven degenerates as
h/p,k/q\to 0
. Nevertheless, the stability proven in Theorems
4.8
and
5.4
is strictly stronger than the corresponding strongest inf-sup stability result from the literature
[
24
,
1
,
8
]
described in Remark
4.9
. The key development that allows for the stronger results is to reveal the underlying hypocoercivity structure through the new test functions (
4.1
) and (
5.1

Finally, a note regarding the generality of the approach. Although the focus of this work has been on the specific Kolmogorov equation, the methodology used, i.e., to enhance the test functions by a ‘hypocoercivity-inducing’ term mimicking the effect of the modified inner product in (

Appendix A
An elementary eigenvalue bound

Lemma A.1
.

Consider a real
2\times 2
symmetric and positive-semidefinite matrix
\scriptsize\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
b&c\end{pmatrix},
whose entries satisfy

a,c\geq 0
. Then, we have
\frac{ac-b^{2}}{a+|b|}\leq|\mathcal{A}|\leq a+|b|\leq 2a
, with
|\cdot|
the spectral matrix norm.

Proof.

The assumptions imply
a\geq 0
and
ac-b^{2}\geq 0
. For
\lambda_{-}\leq\lambda_{+}
the two eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
, we have

2\lambda_{\pm}=(a+c)(1\pm\sqrt{1-4\zeta})
,
with
\zeta:=\frac{ac-b^{2}}{(a+c)^{2}}
, we get
\lambda_{+}\leq a+|b|
. Since
\lambda_{+}\lambda_{-}=\det(\mathcal{A})=ac-b^{2}
, we conclude

\lambda_{-}\geq\frac{ac-b^{2}}{a+|b|}.

∎

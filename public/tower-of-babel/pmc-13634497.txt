The effect of tamoxifen on haematological parameters and iron status in laying hens
Authors: Marta H Machura, Piotr A Antos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHaematological tests are essential for assessing the physiological state of the body and identifying metabolic disorders in birds. Due to the unique structure and physiological characteristics of avian erythrocytes, along with their sensitivity to endocrine influences, the interpretation of blood parameters requires consideration of hormonal regulation, particularly estrogen-dependent effects. These hormones play a significant role in erythropoiesis and modulation of the immune response. The aim of this study was to evaluate the effect of tamoxifen treatment, a selective estrogen receptor modulator, on the haematological profile and selected iron status parameters. The analysis included determination of erythrocyte parameters (PCV, RBC, Hb, MCV, MCH, MCHC), leukocyte profile (WBC, H/L ratio), as well as plasma iron concentration, TIBC, and transferrin saturation. In hens treated with tamoxifen (n = 8) for 7 continuous days at a dose of 6 mg/0.3 mL ethanol per kg of body weight, a significant increase in PCV and RBC count was observed, with no changes in Hb or MCV, compared to control hens (treated with vehicle; n = 8). Simultaneously, a decrease in MCH and MCHC was observed. These alterations were accompanied by a decrease in plasma iron concentration and transferrin saturation, with no significant changes in TIBC. Regarding the leukocyte profile, no significant changes in WBC count was observed, however, a decrease in H/L ratio was noted. The results obtained indicate that modulation of estrogen signaling may lead to changes in the hematopoietic system of laying hens and affect the availability of iron for erythropoiesis. The findings suggest a correlation between hormonal regulation, erythropoiesis, and iron metabolism in hens. The results of this study underscore the importance of considering endocrine factors in the interpretation of haematological parameters of laying hens.Keywords: Erythrocyte parameters, Iron status, Tamoxifen, Chicken

Introduction

Haematological profiling is a fundamental diagnostic tool used to assess the physiological status of animals and detect pathological alterations (Harvey, 2012). In avian species, blood parameters can quickly indicate metabolic shifts, environmental stress, and hormonal imbalances. However, their interpretation requires consideration of taxon-specific morphology (Campbell, 2015; Scanes, 2022).

The avian red blood cell system differs from that of mammals. Avian erythrocytes are nucleated oval cells that have a relatively brief lifespan ranging from 28 and 45 days (Scanes, 2022). This rapid turnover, driven by a high body temperature and metabolic rate, requires continuous and efficient erythropoiesis. Consequently, parameters such as red blood cell count (RBC) and haemoglobin concentration (Hb) not only indicate oxygen-carrying capacity, but also reflect the adaptive response of the bone marrow to metabolic demand (Brockus, 2003; Minias, 2020). Furthermore, haematocrit (HCT), defined as the erythrocyte to total blood volume ratio, has been shown to correlate with RBC count, and to indicate erythropoietic or water and electrolyte disturbances. HCT values can also be affected by thermal stress or production intensity (Fair et al., 2007; Hassan et al., 2023). Although erythrocyte indices (mean cell volume [MCV], mean cell haemoglobin [MCH], mean cell haemoglobin concentration [MCHC]) are standard tools for classifying anaemia, their assessment in birds presents unique challenges due to the interference of nuclei in automated analysis, often requiring manual verification (Campbell, 2015).

An essential element of the evaluation of the red blood cell system is the analysis of iron status parameters, including blood iron concentration, total iron binding capacity (TIBC), and transferrin saturation. Iron is an essential component of haemoglobin, and its availability directly affects the intensity of erythropoiesis and the blood’s ability to transport oxygen (Ganz and Nemeth, 2012; Hentze et al., 2010). Serum iron concentration is indicative of the quantity of iron, which is transported in blood primarily in the form of a complex with transferrin to various tissues, including the bone marrow (Andrews, 2000). Total iron-binding capacity (TIBC) is an indicator of the maximum amount of iron that can be bound by serum transferrin. It therefore provides an indirect measurement of the availability of iron-transporting proteins in the peripheral blood (Cook, 2005). The calculation of the percentage of transferrin saturation is based on the iron concentration and TIBC levels, and is a marker of the efficiency of serum iron transport and its availability for erythropoiesis (Camaschella, 2015). Abnormalities in these parameters may indicate iron deficiency, impaired iron distribution, or changes in the regulation of iron metabolism. These abnormalities can, in turn, impair erythropoiesis and alter haematological parameters (Camaschella, 2015; Ganz, 2013).

In turn, the leukocyte profile is a complementary component to the red blood cell assessment, providing data on immune status. In birds, heterophils, which are functionally analogous to mammalian neutrophils, constitute the primary line of immune response (Genovese et al., 2013). The analysis of specific leukocyte subpopulations, particularly the ratio of heterophils to lymphocytes, in conjunction with total leukocyte counts, is widely accepted as a reliable metric for evaluating physiological stress, inflammation, and immune responsiveness (Davis et al., 2008; Lentfer et al., 2015; Scanes, 2016).

It is important to note that both avian erythropoiesis and immune function are under hormonal regulation, with estrogens playing a dominant role. The impact of these hormones on the proliferation and differentiation of erythroid progenitor cells within the bone marrow, as well as the survival and functional integrity of mature erythrocytes, is a specific phenomenon (Wagner et al., 2008). In addition, estrogens have been demonstrated to act as potent immunomodulators, influencing the distribution and activity of specific leukocyte subpopulations (Kondo et al., 2004). Physiological fluctuations in estrogen concentration during the reproductive cycles of female birds have been demonstrated to induce naturally occurring variations in haematological parameters (Wagner et al., 2008; Williams et al., 2004).

In order to investigate the mechanisms of estrogen-dependent haematopoietic regulation, tamoxifen (TMX) may be used as a pharmacological tool. As a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), tamoxifen exhibits a complex pharmacodynamic profile, acting as either an agonist or an antagonist, depending on the target tissue (Jordan, 2003; Riggs and Hartmann, 2003; Sfogliarini et al., 2022). This mechanism relies on the drug’s ability to competitively bind to estrogen receptors, thereby modulating the expression of estrogen-responsive genes (Shang and Brown, 2002). In addition, it has been demonstrated that the administration of tamoxifen to hens results in a reduction in plasma immunoglobulin levels, suggesting an impact on immune system function (Sechman et al., 2004). Moreover, tamoxifen has been shown to induce a cessation in egg production in hens, a phenomenon associated with the inhibition of estrogen-dependent ovarian activity and the regression of the oviduct (Frydrych et al., 2024; Hrabia et al., 2022; Leśniak-Walentyn and Hrabia, 2017; Rząsa et al., 2009; Socha et al., 2018; Wolak and Hrabia, 2020).

Due to the well-characterized endocrinology and the high sensitivity of haematological parameters to hormonal changes (Wagner et al., 2008; Williams et al., 2004), the domestic chicken (Gallus domesticus) may be a useful research model for analyzing estrogen-dependent mechanisms of blood physiology. However, information concerning the relationship between estrogen action and red blood cell system in birds is limited. In this study, we examined the hypothesis that disruption of estrogen signaling affects erythropoiesis and leukocyte function in hens. Accordingly, the objective of the study was to evaluate the haematological profile and selected iron status parameters in laying hens following tamoxifen, an estrogen receptor modulator, treatment.

Material & methods

Experimental birds and blood collection

The experiment was conducted according to the research protocol approved by the I Local Animal Ethics Committee in Krakow, Poland (approval no. 180/2024). The Isa Brown hens were obtained from a commercial farm and housed individually in cages under a photoperiod of 14L:10D, with unrestricted access to water and commercial feed formulated for laying hens (11.5 MJ/kg metabolisable energy, 18.0% crude protein). Individual laying cycles were monitored by the daily timing of oviposition.

According to a previously described protocol (Frydrych et al., 2024; Hrabia et al., 2022; Leśniak-Walentyn and Hrabia, 2017; Socha et al., 2018; Wolak and Hrabia, 2020), 54-week-old hens with an average body weight of 1.7 kg were randomly assigned to two groups: a control group (n = 8) and a treated group (n = 8). On the first day of the experiment, blood was collected from the wing vein of hens from both groups into tubes containing EDTA or heparin, approximately 2 h after oviposition. The treated hens were then injected subcutaneously every morning with tamoxifen (TMX; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, Missouri, USA) at a dose of 6 mg/0.3 mL ethanol per kg of body weight, while the control hens were treated with a vehicle (0.3 mL ethanol/kg body weight). Treatments were continued until all tamoxifen-treated hens stopped laying eggs, i.e., for 7 days. On day 8 of the experiment blood was collected from hens of each group. The control hens were at the stage about 2 h after oviposition. After collection, the blood was kept at 4°C. The blood samples were analyzed within four hours.

Determination of erythrocyte and leukocyte parameters

The blood samples were thoroughly mixed using a blood roller mixer (MX-T6-S, DLAB Scientific). The analysis comprised the determination of erythrocyte parameters, including packed cell volume (PCV), RBC count, Hb, MCV, MCH, MCHC, as well as leukocyte count and H/L ratio.

The PCV was measured directly by centrifuging heparinized microhaematocrit tubes containing blood samples at 12,147 × g for 5 minutes in a microhaematocrit centrifuge (MPW-55 centrifuge, MPW Med. Instruments). The PCV value was then calculated as a percentage of the red blood cell column within the total blood column.

The haemoglobin concentration in the blood was determined colorimetrically by the cyanmethaemoglobin method, by diluting 1 part of well-mixed blood with 249 parts Drabkin’s reagent. The mixtures were allowed to stand at room temperature for 20 minutes. After incubation, the mixtures were centrifuged (1825 × g, 10 minutes), thereby inducing the nuclei to settle to the bottom of the tubes. Haemoglobin concentration was then measured spectrophotometrically at a wavelength of 540 nm using an EPOCH2 microplate reader (BioTek Instruments Inc., USA). Drabkin’s reagent was used as a blank control.

The RBC and WBC counts were determined using a Bürker haemocytometer and a light microscope. The blood was diluted 100-fold with Natt-Herrick stain, and erythrocytes and leukocytes were counted at 400 × magnification in a 0.2 mm2 and 4 mm2 area, respectively.

Wintrobe’s indices (MCV, MCH, MCHC) were calculated using standard formulas from the PCV, Hb and RBC.

Blood smears were prepared from each sample and stained by Wright’s method. Differential leukocyte counts were performed using a light microscope. A total of 100 leukocytes were counted per smear and classified as lymphocytes, heterophils, or monocytes based on their morphological characteristics. The heterophil-to-lymphocyte (H/L) ratio was calculated by dividing the number of heterophils by the number of lymphocytes identified in the differential leukocyte count.

Determination of iron status parameters

The iron status parameters, including plasma iron concentration, total iron-binding capacity (TIBC), and transferrin saturation (TS), were also determined. The plasma iron concentration (PI) and total iron-binding capacity (TIBC) were determined by a modification of the method recommended by The International Council for Standardization in Haematology (ICSH).

For the PI determination, protein precipitating solution (100 g/L trichloroacetic acid and 22.7 mmol/L ascorbic acid in 0.9 mmol/L HCl) was added to plasma samples, iron standard solution (80 µmol/L iron), and iron-free water (as a blank). The contents were mixed vigorously and incubated for 5 minutes. The plasma samples were subsequently centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 4 minutes to obtain a clear supernatant. The chromogen solution containing ferrozine was then added to the plasma supernatant and to the remaining mixtures, followed by thorough mixing. After standing for 10 minutes, the absorbance was measured spectrophotometrically at a wavelength of 562 nm using an EPOCH2 microplate reader (BioTek Instruments Inc., USA). The plasma iron concentration was calculated by comparing the absorbance of the sample with that of the iron standard after correction for the blank value.

For the TIBC determination, excess iron was added to heparinized plasma and incubated for 15 minutes to saturate transferrin. The remaining non-transferrin-bound iron was then removed by the addition of magnesium carbonate. The tubes were vigorously shaken and allowed to stand for 30 minutes, with occasional mixing. After incubation, the tubes were centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 4 minutes. The supernatant was carefully collected and processed as described for PI determination. The TIBC was calculated using the same procedure as for PI determination, with the obtained value multiplied by two. The transferrin saturation (TS) was calculated as (PI/TIBC) × 100.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using Statistica 13.3 (TIBCO Software Inc., USA). The results were analyzed by repeated measures ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. The assumptions of normality and sphericity were previously tested using the Shapiro–Wilk test and Mauchly’s test, respectively. Values are expressed as the mean ± SEM and considered significantly different at P < 0.05. Figures were prepared using Grapher 10.3 (Golden Software Inc., USA).

Results

Erythrocyte and leukocyte parameters

Prior to tamoxifen administration, no significant differences in erythrocyte or leukocyte parameters were observed between the control and experimental groups. Before treatment, PCV values were 24.67 ± 0.72% in control hens and 25.57 ± 0.57% in tamoxifen-treated hens (Fig. 1A), while the RBC counts were 2.23 ± 0.07 × 106/µL and 2.43 ± 0.06 × 106/µL (Fig. 1B), respectively. Following the experiment, both PCV and RBC count increased significantly (P < 0.0005; P < 0.05) in the tamoxifen-treated group. Haemoglobin concentration did not differ significantly between measurements in either group (Fig. 1C).

The erythrocyte parameters: PCV (A), RBC (B), Hb (C), MCV (D), MCH (E), MCHC (F) in control and tamoxifen-treated hens before (1) and after treatment (2). Each value represents the mean ± SEM. Values marked with different letters differ significantly (P < 0.05).

Pre-treatment MCV, MCH, and MCHC values were 110.78 ± 3.21 µm³, 44.74 ± 3.16 pg, and 40.58 ± 3.14 g/dL, respectively, in the control group, compared with 105.13 ± 1.71 µm³, 37.85 ± 0.86 pg, and 36.06 ± 1.00 g/dL in the tamoxifen-treated hens (Fig. 1D, 1E, 1F). No significant changes were observed in MCV after the treatment. Concurrently, MCH decreased significantly (P < 0.05) in tamoxifen-treated hens, while MCHC decreased significantly in both groups (P < 0.005; P < 0.001).

Before treatment, the WBC count was 24.09 ± 2.50 × 103/mm3 in the control group and 30.92 ± 2.35 × 103/mm3 in the tamoxifen-treated hens (Fig. 2A). During the experiment, a significant increase (P < 0.05) in WBC count was observed in the control group. The initial H/L ratio was 0.28 ± 0.20 in the control hens and 0.36 ± 0.03 in the tamoxifen-treated hens (Fig. 2B). After treatment, a significant decrease in H/L ratio was observed in the tamoxifen-treated hens (P < 0.05).

The WBC count (A) and H/L ratio (B) in control and tamoxifen-treated hens before (1) and after treatment (2). Each value represents the mean ± SEM. Values marked with different letters differ significantly (P < 0.05).

Iron status parameters

The analyzed iron status parameters did not differ significantly between the control and experimental groups before tamoxifen administration. Plasma iron concentration was 24.09 ± 0.38 µmol/L in the control group and 25.37 ± 2.37 µmol/L in the tamoxifen-treated hens (Fig. 3A). In addition, transferrin saturation (TS) was 48.50 ± 1.28% and 49.04 ± 1.11%, respectively (Fig. 3C). Both parameters decreased significantly (P < 0.0005; P < 0.005) in tamoxifen-treated hens. Pre-treatment TIBC was 49.79 ± 1.70 µmol/L in the control group and 51.52 ± 3.65 µmol/L in the tamoxifen-treated hens (Fig. 3B). TIBC did not change significantly after tamoxifen-treatment.

The iron status parameters: plasma iron (A), total iron-binding capacity (B), transferrin saturation (C) in control and tamoxifen-treated hens before (1) and after treatment (2). Each value represents the mean ± SEM. Values marked with different letters differ significantly (P < 0.05).

Red blood cell morphology

A morphological analysis of erythrocytes revealed no significant changes in the control group (Fig. 4A, B) and tamoxifen-treated hens (Fig. 4C, D). The red blood cells appeared normocytic with homogeneous eosinophilic cytoplasm. Polychromasia was not observed. Blood smears showed no abnormalities in erythrocyte morphology. No alterations in cell nuclei structure, cytoplasmic vacuolization, or indications of hemolysis were observed.

Discussion

This study presents a comprehensive investigation into the stimulatory response of the red blood cell system to tamoxifen administration in laying hens. To date, there have been no reports on the effects of tamoxifen on the red blood cell system in laying hens. The obtained results demonstrate that the observed alterations in the haematological profile reflect a close correlation between the homeostasis of the erythropoietic and immune systems in laying hens and the activity of the reproductive axis. Furthermore, the findings indicate that pharmacological intervention in estrogen signaling results in substantial shifts in cell proliferation kinetics and iron metabolism.

In hens treated with tamoxifen, a significant increase in RBC count and PCV values was observed. These results suggest an increase in erythropoiesis or changes in the dynamics of the circulating red blood cell pool. The mechanism of action of tamoxifen in the experimental hens is likely based on competitive blockade of estrogen receptors within the bone marrow. This phenomenon can be related to tamoxifen’s pleiotropic pharmacodynamic profile, whose effect on gene expression is strictly dependent on the molecular context of a given tissue and the specific recruitment of regulatory cofactors (McDonnell et al., 2002). The cytomorphological characteristics of avian erythrocytes likely determine their particular responsiveness to SERM-induced modulation of intracellular signaling. The results obtained indicate that in laying hens estrogens may function as a physiological regulatory mechanism that limits the process of erythropoiesis (Williams et al., 2004). Consequently, the pharmacological blockade of estrogen receptors by tamoxifen resulted in a rapid neutralization of these inhibitory signals. This, in turn, led to a hyperplastic response and unblocked the erythroid lineage’s proliferative potential within the bone marrow. This observation suggests a correlation with the bone marrow’s mitotic activity, in which estrogen signaling plays a pivotal role in regulating the kinetics of hematopoietic stem cell proliferation (Nakada et al., 2014; Sánchez-Aguilera et al., 2014). Tamoxifen likely acted as a selective antagonist, leading to the neutralization of inhibitory hormonal signals and a rapid unblocking of the erythroid lineage’s proliferative potential. The absence of similar changes in the control hens suggests that the observed erythroid hyperplasia could be a result of pharmacological intervention in the endocrine axis, rather than the result of non-specific fluid shifts.

Despite the observed increases in RBC and PCV, no significant changes were detected in Hb or MCV. This finding indicates that the observed alterations were predominantly quantitative, reflecting an expansion of the erythron rather than a change in cell size. Concurrently, a substantial decline in MCH and MCHC values signifies a diminished haemoglobin content within individual erythrocytes. Within a normal erythrocyte morphology, this phenomenon suggests an asynchrony between the accelerated rate of cytoplasmic divisions and the efficiency of intracellular heme synthesis (Chiabrando et al., 2014; Doty et al., 2015). The persistence of normal erythrocytes indicates that this process occurred subclinically, characterized by a decrease in the absolute amount of haemoglobin per erythrocyte. However, this decrease did not attain a level that would manifest as morphological hypochromia of the cells.

The observed discrepancy can be attributed to diminished iron bioavailability that occurs during the phase of active proliferation. A significant decrease in serum iron concentration, accompanied by a reduction in transferrin saturation (TS), while total iron-binding capacity (TIBC) remains constant, may be indicative of the presence of functional iron deficiency (FID). In this condition, the mobilization or utilization of iron in the process of erythropoiesis is impaired. A stable TIBC level suggests that hepatic synthetic function remained uninterrupted (Camaschella, 2015; Cook, 2005; Ganz and Nemeth, 2015). The iron deficiency could therefore be distributional. The demand from the rapidly proliferating bone marrow exceeded the rate at which iron was mobilized from tissue stores into the plasma transport pool. The underlying mechanism of these alterations is likely associated with the pharmacodynamics of tamoxifen. Estrogens play a significant role in maintaining iron homeostasis by influencing the expression of key proteins, such as hepcidin, transferrin, and ferroportin (Ganz, 2013; Hou et al., 2012; Qian et al., 2015; Vyhlidal et al., 2002). The interruption of estrogen signaling can result in the redistribution of iron within the body and a subsequent decrease in its availability to the bone marrow. This, in turn, can impact the quality of the erythrocytes produced.

The distinct response of the WBC to tamoxifen administration highlights the pleiotropic nature of SERM drugs (McDonnell and Wardell, 2010; Smith and O’Malley, 2004). The absence of significant changes in WBC in tamoxifen-treated hens suggests the absence of a strong systemic inflammatory response. Conversely, a marked decline in the H/L ratio signifies immune response modulation. The H/L ratio is widely regarded as a highly sensitive indicator of physiological stress in birds (Davis and Maney, 2018; Davis et al., 2008). Alterations in this ratio may serve as a reflection of subtle shifts in the immune balance. In contrast to the stress response, in which an increase in the H/L ratio is observed, its decrease in the present study may indicate a specific immunomodulatory effect of tamoxifen. Given that the H/L ratio is a recognized biomarker of glucocorticoid axis activity in birds, its decrease under the influence of tamoxifen may suggest a potential agonistic effect within immunocompetent cells and restricted progression of the leukocyte stress response. This dual action, antagonism in the bone marrow and agonism in the immune system, may confirm tamoxifen’s high tissue selectivity in the avian model and indicate its ability to modify the immune profile independently of changes in the red blood cell system.

The nucleated nature and brief lifespan of avian erythrocytes require continuous erythropoiesis. Consequently, the avian red blood cell system exhibits heightened sensitivity to metabolic and hormonal fluctuations, and even minor disturbances in the availability of components essential for erythropoiesis can quickly lead to observable changes in haematological parameters. The findings of our study underscore its novelty, as prior reports have not adequately addressed the impact of estrogen signaling modulation on the concomitant changes in the red blood cell system and iron status in laying hens. A comprehensive interpretation of the obtained results suggests that tamoxifen exerts a complex effect on the hematopoietic system of hens, involving both quantitative alterations, as indicated by increases in RBC and PCV, and qualitative changes, as demonstrated by decreases in MCH and MCHC, in addition to alterations in iron availability. This finding indicates that modulation of estrogen signaling can lead to dysregulation of erythropoiesis and iron metabolism, which can result in the formation of a greater number of erythrocytes with reduced haemoglobin content.

From a practical point of view, it is noteworthy that, although the use of tamoxifen is experimental, similar effects of estrogen signaling modulation may occur under commercial production conditions in laying hens. Compounds with estrogenic activity, such as phytoestrogens present in feed ingredients (e.g., soy isoflavones), have the potential to affect the function of the red blood cell system and iron status in laying hens (Grgic et al., 2021; Megha et al., 2023; Qiang et al., 2023). This suggests that the composition of the feed could also be a significant factor influencing haematological parameters of laying hens. Consequently, these parameters may serve as a sensitive tool for assessing hormonal changes. This could be useful for monitoring the physiological status of flocks and for the early detection of metabolic disorders. Therefore, this underscores the necessity to take into consideration the endocrine profile, encompassing both endogenous and environmental factors, when interpreting laboratory test results, particularly within the context of intensive egg production conditions.

Conclusions

The results of this study demonstrate the significant impact of hormonal regulation on the avian red blood cell system and iron metabolism. Specifically, the findings indicate a high degree of dependency on hormonal regulation, and further demonstrate that interference with estrogen pathways through the use of tamoxifen results in substantial alterations in haematological parameters and iron metabolism. The data obtained underscore the importance of considering endocrine factors when interpreting haematological test results in birds, and highlight the need for further research into the mechanisms underlying these relationships.

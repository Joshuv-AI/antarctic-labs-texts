Pairs of commuting integer matrices
Tim Browning, Will Sawin, Victor Y. Wang

Abstract
. We prove upper and lower bounds on the number of pairs of commuting n × n n\times n matrices with integer entries in [ − T , T ] [-T,T] , as T → ∞ T\to\infty . Our work uses Fourier analysis and leads to an analysis of exponential sums involving matrices over finite fields. These are bounded by combining a stratification result of Fouvry and Katz with a new result about the flatness of the commutator Lie bracket.

2.
Background on flatness

Before proceeding, we recall some classical criteria for flatness, which are useful both for interpreting Theorem
1.2
and for proving it.
Throughout this paper, a
variety
will be a locally closed subset of a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
over a field
k

quasi-projective variety
).
If
k

[

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

Let

Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The morphism

(2)

The morphism

is flat.

(3)

For every point

the fibre

is either empty or of dimension exactly
\dim{E_{1}}-\dim{E_{2}}
.

(4)

For every point

.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow

Flatness is preserved by base change from

.
(See
[
23

Tag 047C
]

or
[

Tag 02JZ
]

The key point is that the inclusion homomorphism

is flat.

(1)

(3):
This follows from

(3)
\Rightarrow

This follows from miracle flatness

(3)

(4)

3.
Matrix exponential sums

Over any field
k
,
we will often make the identification
\mathsf{M}_{n}(k)\cong k^{n^{2}}
.
The usual dot product on
X,Y\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(k)

is then given by
\operatorname{tr}(X^{t}Y)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}X_{ji}Y_{ji}
.
For the purposes of harmonic analysis to come, however,
it will be more convenient to work with
the pairing
\mathsf{M}_{n}(k)\times\mathsf{M}_{n}(k)\to k

given by
(X,Y)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(XY)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}X_{ij}Y_{ji}
,
which can be viewed as the usual dot product after an invertible linear change of variables
X\mapsto X^{t}
.
(Abstractly, the key point
is that this pairing is
non-degenerate
,
in the sense that
for any non-zero
X\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(k)
,
the linear form
Y\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(XY)
is non-zero.)

Given

A,B\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, our work will lead us to analyse the complete exponential sum

(3.1)

S(A,B;p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(U,V)\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{2}\\
UV-VU=0\end{subarray}}e_{p}(\operatorname{tr}(AU+BV)),

for a sufficiently large prime
p
.
It follows from the point count of
Feit–Fine
[
6
]
that

(3.2)

S(0,0;p)\ll_{n}p^{n^{2}+n}.

We seek to find conditions on
A,B
under which we can show that cancellation occurs in

S(A,B;p)
.

We begin by recording the observation that

(3.3)

S(A,B;p)=\frac{1}{\#\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\sum_{U,V,Z\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}e_{p}(\operatorname{tr}(Z(UV-VU)+AU+BV)).

The cyclic property of the trace ensures that

\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)
and

\operatorname{tr}(ABC)=\operatorname{tr}(CAB)=\operatorname{tr}(BCA)
, for any
A,B,C\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Hence

\operatorname{tr}(Z(UV-VU)+AU)=\operatorname{tr}(U(VZ-ZV+A))
, so that we can average over
U

to conclude that

S(A,B;p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V,Z\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\\
VZ-ZV+A=0\end{subarray}}e_{p}(\operatorname{tr}(BV)).

In particular, it is clear that

(3.4)

|S(A,B;p)|\leqslant S(A,0;p)=\#\left\{(V,Z)\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{2}:VZ-ZV+A=0\right\},

for any
B\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.
We are now ready to prove the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
p\nmid A
or
p\nmid B
, then

S(A,B;p)\ll_{n}p^{n^{2}+1}\bm{1}_{p\mid\operatorname{tr}(A)}\bm{1}_{p\mid\operatorname{tr}(B)}.

Proof.

We may assume that
p
is sufficiently large in terms of
n
, else the result is trivial.
If
p\nmid A
,
then it follows from applying Theorem
2.2

and the Lang–Weil bound
in
(
3.4
)
that

\displaystyle S(A,B;p)\ll_{n}p^{n^{2}+1}\bm{1}_{p\mid\operatorname{tr}(A)}.

Similarly, if
p\nmid B
, then averaging over
V
in (
3.3
) gives

S(A,B;p)\ll\#\left\{(U,Z)\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{2}:ZU-UZ+B=0\right\}\ll_{n}p^{n^{2}+1}\bm{1}_{p\mid\operatorname{tr}(B)}.

Combining these bounds, we arrive at the statement of the lemma.
∎

Fourier analysis and proof of the main result

to assume that its top left entry is non-zero.
Then by Lemma
5.2
,
there exist
k
diagonal entries of
M

whose total is non-zero.
By a further conjugation, we may assume
\operatorname{tr}(p_{11}(M))\neq 0
.
Therefore, the subvariety
\operatorname{tr}(p_{11}(g^{-1}Mg))=0

of
\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is either empty or of codimension one,
since
\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is irreducible.
The Lang–Weil bound now implies (7).
∎

We are finally ready to bound
the quantity
L(V,M)
from (
5.3
).

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
V,M\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Let
f_{V}(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
be the
radical

of the characteristic polynomial of
V
.
Then

(5.13)

L(V,M)\ll_{n}q^{1-\deg f_{V}}\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(M)=0}+\bm{1}_{M=0}.

Proof.

We use strong induction on
n\geqslant 1
.
Since
1\in C(gVg^{-1})
for all
g
,
we may assume
\operatorname{tr}(M)=0
,
or else
L(V,M)=0
.
Moreover, if
\deg f_{V}=1
or
M=0
, then the trivial bound
L(V,M)\leqslant 1
suffices.
Therefore, we may assume from now on that

\operatorname{tr}(M)=0
,

M\neq 0
,
and
\deg f_{V}\geqslant 2
.

By definition, a
constructible set
is a finite union of locally closed sets.
By the inclusion-exclusion principle, the usual Lang–Weil estimate (for varieties) extends to constructible sets.
By the Lang–Weil estimate
applied to the constructible set

\displaystyle\{g\in\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}):C(gVg^{-1})\subseteq M^{\perp}\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{0\leqslant d\leqslant n^{2}}\{g\in\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}):\dim(C(gVg^{-1}))=\dim(C(gVg^{-1})\cap M^{\perp})=d\},

it suffices to prove the desired inequality (
5.13
)
under the assumption that

f_{V}
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
By conjugation, we may assume that
V
is in
rational canonical form, which coincides with Jordan normal form
because
f_{V}
splits completely.
Since
\deg f_{V}\geqslant 2
, the matrix
V
has at least
2
distinct eigenvalues.
After permuting Jordan blocks if necessary, we may assume that

V=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}V_{1}&0\\
0&V_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right],

where
V_{1}\in\mathsf{M}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
V_{2}\in\mathsf{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
with
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
,
such that
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
share no eigenvalues.
Then, in particular,

(5.14)

C(V)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}C(V_{1})&0\\
0&C(V_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\right].

Let
p_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p_{11}
and
p_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p_{22}
.
Let

(5.15)

H\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\GL_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})&0\\
0&\GL_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\end{smallmatrix}\right]\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Since
C(ghVh^{-1}g^{-1})=gC(hVh^{-1})g^{-1}
,
and
g^{-1}M^{\perp}g=(g^{-1}Mg)^{\perp}
by the conjugation-invariance of
\operatorname{tr}(\cdot)
,
we have

(5.16)

\begin{split}L(V,M)&=\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\mathbb{E}_{h\in H}(\bm{1}_{C(ghV(gh)^{-1})\subseteq M^{\perp}})\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\mathbb{E}_{h\in H}(\bm{1}_{C(hVh^{-1})\subseteq(g^{-1}Mg)^{\perp}})\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}L(V_{1},p_{1}(g^{-1}Mg))L(V_{2},p_{2}(g^{-1}Mg)),\end{split}

where the last step uses
(
5.14
),
(
5.15
),
and the block matrix identity

\operatorname{tr}(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{smallmatrix}\right]M)=\operatorname{tr}(Ap_{1}(M))+\operatorname{tr}(Bp_{2}(M))

for
(A,B)\in\mathsf{M}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\mathsf{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
By the inductive hypothesis applied to
the two factors of
L
in (
5.16
), we get

\begin{split}L(V,M)&\ll_{n}\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant 2}(q^{1-\deg f_{V_{i}}}\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(p_{i}(g^{-1}Mg))=0}+\bm{1}_{p_{i}(g^{-1}Mg)=0})\\
&\leqslant q^{2-\deg f_{V}}\mathcal{P}_{0}+q^{1-\deg f_{V_{1}}}\mathcal{P}_{1}+q^{1-\deg f_{V_{2}}}\mathcal{P}_{2}+\mathcal{P}_{3},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{0}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(p_{1}(g^{-1}Mg))=0}\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(p_{2}(g^{-1}Mg))=0}),\\
\mathcal{P}_{1}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(p_{1}(g^{-1}Mg))=0}\bm{1}_{p_{2}(g^{-1}Mg)=0}),\\
\mathcal{P}_{2}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(p_{2}(g^{-1}Mg))=0}\bm{1}_{p_{1}(g^{-1}Mg)=0}),\\
\mathcal{P}_{3}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\bm{1}_{p_{1}(g^{-1}Mg)=0}\bm{1}_{p_{2}(g^{-1}Mg)=0}).\\
\end{split}

We now wish to bound the probabilities
\mathcal{P}_{i}
.
Since

0=\operatorname{tr}(M)=\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}Mg)=\operatorname{tr}(p_{1}(g^{-1}Mg))+\operatorname{tr}(p_{2}(g^{-1}Mg)),

the probabilities
\mathcal{P}_{i}
for
0\leqslant i\leqslant 2
simplify as follows:

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{0}&=\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(p_{1}(g^{-1}Mg))=0}),\\
\mathcal{P}_{1}&=\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\bm{1}_{p_{2}(g^{-1}Mg)=0}),\\
\mathcal{P}_{2}&=\mathbb{E}_{g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\bm{1}_{p_{1}(g^{-1}Mg)=0}).\end{split}

By parts (7),
(6),
(2),
and (5)
of Lemma
5.3
,
respectively, we have

\mathcal{P}_{0}\ll_{n}q^{-1}
,

\mathcal{P}_{1}\ll_{n}q^{-(n-k)}\leqslant q^{-\deg f_{V_{2}}}
,

\mathcal{P}_{2}\ll_{n}q^{-k}\leqslant q^{-\deg f_{V_{1}}}
,
and
\mathcal{P}_{3}\ll_{n}q^{1-n}\leqslant q^{1-\deg f_{V}}
.
Thus

\begin{split}L(V,M)&\ll_{n}q^{1-\deg f_{V}}+q^{1-\deg f_{V_{1}}}q^{-\deg f_{V_{2}}}+q^{1-\deg f_{V_{2}}}q^{-\deg f_{V_{1}}}+q^{1-\deg f_{V}}\\
&=4q^{1-\deg f_{V}},\end{split}

since
f_{V_{1}}f_{V_{2}}=f_{V}
.
∎

Plugging Lemma
5.4
into (
5.4
),
we get

\Sigma(M)\ll_{n}q^{n^{2}}\sum_{V\in K_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(q^{1-\deg f_{V}}\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(M)=0}+\bm{1}_{M=0}).

But
K_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is in bijection with
the set of rational canonical forms on
\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where we recall
that the
rational canonical form
of
M\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

consists of a partition
\lambda_{\phi}=(\lambda_{\phi,1}\geqslant\lambda_{\phi,2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant 0)

for each monic irreducible polynomial
\phi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
,
such that the action of
M
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}

is isomorphic to multiplication by
t
on the vector space

\bigoplus_{\phi}\bigoplus_{i}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]/\phi^{\lambda_{\phi,i}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[t])
,
where
\sum_{\phi}\deg(\phi)\lvert\lambda_{\phi}\rvert=n
.
Thus, by the prime number theorem in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]

and the fact that
n
has only finitely many partitions, we deduce that

\#\{V\in K_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):\deg f_{V}=d\}\ll_{n}q^{d}

for
1\leqslant d\leqslant n
.
Summing over
1\leqslant d\leqslant n
, we get

\Sigma(M)\ll_{n}q^{n^{2}+1}\bm{1}_{\operatorname{tr}(M)=0}+q^{n^{2}+n}\bm{1}_{M=0}.

Finally, we conclude
from (
5.1
) that Theorem
2.2
holds.

Characterizations of multidimensional compact almost automorphic functions and applications to Poisson’s and heat equations
Alan Chávez, Jolbyn Castañeda, Alexis R. Carranza, Kamal Khalil

Abstract
Let 𝒢 \mathcal{G} be a non-empty subset of the Euclidean space ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} ( m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 ). This work is dedicated to further exploring the properties of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -multi-almost automorphic functions defined on ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} with values in a Banach space 𝕏 \mathbb{X} . Using the theory of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -multi-almost automorphic functions, we provide two new characterizations of compact almost automorphic functions. In the first characterization, 𝒢 \mathcal{G} corresponds to the lattice subgroup ℤ m ⊂ ℝ m \mathbb{Z}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m} ; in the second, 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is taken to be a dense subset of ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} . Furthermore, we establish the invariance of the space of bounded and compactly 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -multi-almost automorphic functions under integral operators with Bi-almost automorphic kernels. Finally, we present applications to the analysis of the almost automorphic dynamics of Poisson’s equation and the heat equation.

Introduction

Since their introduction by S. Bochner in 1966
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, almost automorphic functions have garnered significant attention from mathematicians. This interest arises, on one hand, because these functions are a natural generalization of periodic and almost periodic functions, making them an ideal framework for studying the dynamics of differential, integral, or integro-differential equations, as demonstrated in works such as
[
5
,
6
,
23
]
and other related references. On the other hand, the concept of almost automorphy provides a fertile ground for investigations in abstract topological dynamical systems, as evidenced by studies like
[
17
,
18
,
20
,
21
]
and the references cited therein.

In this work, we contribute to the ongoing study of the almost automorphic dynamics of partial differential equations, specifically to the Poisson’s and heat equations.

The concept of complex-valued almost automorphic functions on a topological group
G
was first developed by Veech
[
25
,
26
]
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space,
G=\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and let
\mathrm{R}
denote the collection of all sequences in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The notion of almost automorphy for a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathcal{X}
is described in the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

(Bochner)
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a continuous function. Then,
f
is almost automorphic if and only if, for any sequence
\mathbf{b}_{k}=(b_{k}^{1},b_{k}^{2},\dots,b_{k}^{m})\in\mathrm{R}
, there exist a subsequence
\mathbf{b}_{k_{l}}=(b_{k_{l}}^{1},b_{k_{l}}^{2},\dots,b_{k_{l}}^{m})
of
\mathbf{b}_{k}
and a function
f^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathcal{X}
such that the following pointwise limits hold:

f^{*}(\mathbf{t})=\lim_{l\to\infty}f(\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{b}_{k_{l}}),\quad f(\mathbf{t})=\lim_{l\to\infty}f^{*}(\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{b}_{k_{l}}).

Additionally, if the previous limits are replaced by the following:

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{K}}\|f(\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{b}_{k_{l}})-f^{*}(\mathbf{t})\|_{\mathcal{X}}=0,\quad\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{K}}\|f^{*}(\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{b}_{k_{l}})-f(\mathbf{t})\|_{\mathcal{X}}=0,

where
\mathcal{K}
is any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then
f
is called compact almost automorphic.

Note that in the provided definition, the sequence
(\mathbf{b}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
can be any sequence in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. However, a new approach explored in works such as
[
10
,
11
]
involves considering these sequences within a set
\mathrm{R}
, which is a non-empty collection of sequences in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
(rather than the collection of all sequences in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
). This perspective led the first author and collaborators to introduce the concept of
\mathrm{R}
-multi-almost periodic/automorphic functions, a more general notion than the classical concept of almost periodicity/automorphy. In
[
10
,
11
]
, the authors extensively studied the fundamental structural properties of
(\mathrm{R},\mathcal{B})
-multi-almost periodic/automorphic functions and provided applications to both partial differential equations and integral equations.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a non empty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. In the present work, we will denote by
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
a non empty collection of sequences in
\mathcal{G}
, while
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
will denote the collection of all sequences in
\mathcal{G}
. What is involved in this work is the notion of
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
-multi-almost automorphic function, which is as follows (see also Definition
2.1
below)

Definition 1.2
.

Let
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a continuous function.
F(\cdot)
is
{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
-multi-almost automorphic if, for every sequence
({\bf b}_{k}=(b_{k}^{1},b_{k}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k}^{m}))\in{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
there exist a subsequence
({\bf b}_{k_{l}}=(b_{k_{l}}^{1},b_{k_{l}}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k_{l}}^{m}))
of
({\bf b}_{k})
and a function

F^{\ast}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}F\bigl({\bf t}+(b_{k_{l}}^{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k_{l}}^{m})\bigr)=F^{\ast}({\bf t})

(1.1)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t}-(b_{k_{l}}^{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k_{l}}^{m})\bigr)=F({\bf t}),

(1.2)

pointwise for
{\bf t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

When
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}=\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, by way of simplifying things,
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
-multi-almost automorphic functions will be referred to as
\mathcal{G}
-multi-almost automorphic functions.

The concept of
\mathcal{G}
-multi-almost automorphic functions is not entirely new. In the one-dimensional case where
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}
(the discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
), the first author and collaborators introduced the class of piecewise continuous almost automorphic functions (also known as
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic functions) between 2013 and 2014. Initially, this class of discontinuous functions was developed to study differential equations with piecewise constant arguments
[
8
]
. Later, the approach was extended to investigate differential equations with deviating arguments, as demonstrated in works such as
[
14
,
22
]
.

More recently, the theory of
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic functions with values in a general Banach space has been explored in
[
9
]
. With the help of this theory, it is proved that every compact almost automorphic function is
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic and uniformly continuous, and vice versa, providing a new characterization of the compact almost automorphic function space. Additionally, in the same work,
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic functions were utilized to offer a straightforward proof of the characterization of discrete almost automorphic functions as restrictions to
\mathbb{Z}
of compact almost automorphic functions. Consequently, new applications beyond the theory of differential equations for
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic functions have emerged. In the work
[
16
]
the study of the existence and uniqueness of compact almost automorphic solutions for a class of semilinear evolution equations in Banach spaces is provided.

Building on the aforementioned preliminaries, in the applications of the class of
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic functions and recognizing the significance of almost automorphic functions in the study of differential equations, this work makes two primary contributions:

•

First, we present two new characterizations of compact almost automorphic functions (Section
4
), expanding the theoretical framework established in earlier studies.

•

Second, using the new characterizations, we analyze the almost automorphic dynamics of Poisson’s and heat equations.

Let us develop into more detail regarding the second point mentioned previously. We begin by considering the Poisson’s equation:

\Delta u=f,

(1.3)

where
\Delta
denotes the Laplace operator in the spatial variable
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
, defined as
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}}
. After characterizing compact almost automorphic functions, we establish, for equation (
1.3
), the following result:
if
f
is bounded and
\mathcal{G}
-multi-almost automorphic, where
\mathcal{G}
is either the lattice subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
or a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then every bounded and continuous function that is a distributional solution of the Poisson’s equation (
1.3
) is compact almost automorphic
.

Now, le us consider the following initial value problem for the heat equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}u(t,x)&=&\Delta u(t,x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\\
u(0,x)&=&f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
\partial_{t}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to time
t
and
\Delta
is the Laplace operator in space variable. Analogously to the Poisson’s equation, we prove that:
if the initial data
f
in (
1.4
) is
\mathcal{G}
-multi-almost automorphic, where
\mathcal{G}
is either the lattice subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
or a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then the solution
u(t,x)
of the heat equation (
1.4
) is also compact almost automorphic in space variable
.

In summary, our work extends and generalizes results from previous studies such as
[
12
,
24
]
, emphasizing the persistence of compact almost automorphic properties in solutions to the Poisson’s and heat equations under weaker conditions on the source or initial data (respectively).

The organization of the present work is as follows: In Section
2
, we provide the definition and some basic facts about
\mathcal{G}
-multi-almost automorphic functions. In Section
3
, we study the case in which
\mathcal{G}
is a dense subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. In Section
4
, we present new characterizations of compact almost automorphic functions. In Section
5
, we prove the invariance under convolution products with compact Bi-almost automorphic kernels of the bounded and compactly
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
-multi-almost automorphic function space. In Section
6
, we provide applications of the developed theory to Poisson’s and heat equations. Finally, the appendices contain additional results and proofs related to almost automorphic functions on topological groups, as well as conditions under which a subgroup of the Euclidean space becomes dense.

\mathcal{G}
-multi-almost automorphic functions

Throughout the present work,

denote Banach spaces, we assume that

,

BUC(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is a continuous function. Then
we say that the function
F(\cdot;\cdot)
is
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-multi-almost automorphic if, for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}

({\bf b}_{k}=(b_{k}^{1},b_{k}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k}^{m}))\in{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}

({\bf b}_{k})

F^{\ast}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}F\bigl({\bf t}+(b_{k_{l}}^{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k_{l}}^{m});x\bigr)=F^{\ast}({\bf t};x)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t}-(b_{k_{l}}^{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k_{l}}^{m});x\bigr)=F({\bf t};x),

. If the above limits converge uniformly on compact subsets of

In the particular case in which

10

Also, if the function

F
is
{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
-multi-almost automorphic (resp. compactly

{{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}}AA({\mathbb{R}}^{m};\mathcal{Y})

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}

F(\cdot;\cdot)

Proof.

It is not difficult to see that if
F

({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-multi-almost automorphic, then given any set
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
and any sequence
({\bf b}_{k}=(b_{k}^{1},b_{k}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k}^{m}))\in{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}

({\bf b}_{k_{l}}=(b_{k_{l}}^{1},b_{k_{l}}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k_{l}}^{m}))

({\bf b}_{k})
such that (
2.7
) holds. On the other hand, let
({\bf b}_{k}=(b_{k}^{1},b_{k}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k}^{m}))\in{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
and
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, by hypothesis, there exists a subsequence
({\bf b}_{k_{l}}=(b_{k_{l}}^{1},b_{k_{l}}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k_{l}}^{m}))
of
({\bf b}_{k})
such that (
2.7
) holds. Now, let us define, for each fixed

and any

F^{*}({\bf t}-{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x):=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}F\Bigl({\bf t}-{\bf b}_{k_{l}}+{\bf b}_{k_{n}};x\Bigr)\,.

Then, for each

we have

F^{*}({\bf t};x)=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}F\Bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k_{n}};x\Bigr)\,,

uniformly for

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}F^{*}({\bf t}-{\bf b}_{k_{n}};x)=\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}F^{*}({\bf t}-{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x)=

.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
. Then, the function
F(\cdot;\cdot)

, uniformly for any

\sup_{x\in B}\|F\bigl({\bf t};x\bigr)-F\bigl({\bf s};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}<\epsilon\,.

In this situation, we will say that

is uniformly continuous in the first variable.

In the following definition, we introduce the notion of
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-uniformly continuous function.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
. Then, the function
F(\cdot;\cdot)
is called
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-uniformly continuous if, for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
and for every two sequences
({\bf a}_{k}=(a_{k}^{1},a_{k}^{2},\cdots,a_{k}^{m}))
,
({\bf b}_{k}=(b_{k}^{1},b_{k}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k}^{m}))

\in{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
such that

, as

, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{x\in B}\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf a}_{k};x\bigr)-F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow 0,

(2.8)

pointwise for
{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Note that, if
{\mathcal{G}}=\mathbb{R}^{m}

The next theorem gives a useful consequence for functions that are compactly

-multi-almost automorphic. Its proof is inspired by

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be bounded and continuous. If
F
is compactly
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-multi-almost automorphic, then
F
is
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-multi-almost automorphic and
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-uniformly continuous.

Proof.

Since
F
is compactly
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-multi-almost automorphic, it follows that
F
is
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-multi-almost automorphic. Now, let us prove that
F
is
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-uniformly continuous.

Let
B
be any set in
\mathcal{B}
, and let us pick the arbitrary sequences
{\bf a}_{k}=(a_{k}^{1},a_{k}^{2},\cdots,a_{k}^{m})
and
{\bf b}_{k}=(b_{k}^{1},b_{k}^{2},\cdots,b_{k}^{m})\in{\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
which satisfy
||{\bf a}_{k}-{\bf b}_{k}||\rightarrow 0
as
k\to\infty
. We must show that, for each
{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{x\in B}\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf a}_{k};x\bigr)-F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}=0\,.

Let us define for each
({\bf t};x)
the uniformly bounded sequence

\alpha_{k}({\bf t};x):=\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf a}_{k};x\bigr)-F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\,.

We claim that
\alpha_{k}({\bf t},x)\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. In fact, let
(\alpha_{k}^{\prime}({\bf t},x))\subset(\alpha_{k}({\bf t},x))
be a convergent subsequence which converges to
\psi_{0}({\bf t},x)
, where

\alpha_{k}^{\prime}({\bf t},x)=\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf a}_{k}^{{}^{\prime}};x\bigr)-F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k}^{{}^{\prime}};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

and
({\bf a}_{k}^{{}^{\prime}})\subset({\bf a}_{k})
,
({\bf b}_{k}^{{}^{\prime}})\subset({\bf b}_{k})
are subsequences coming from the definition of
\alpha_{k}^{\prime}({\bf t},x)
.

Since
F
is compactly
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-multi-almost automorphic, there exist a subsequence
({\bf b}_{k_{l}}=(b_{k_{l}}^{1},b_{k_{l}}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k_{l}}^{m}))
of
({\bf b}_{k}^{{}^{\prime}})
(also a subsequence
({\bf a}_{k_{l}})
of
({\bf a}_{k}^{{}^{\prime}})
) and a continuous (in the first variable) function

F^{\ast}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
such that the following limits hold

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{E}}\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)-F^{\ast}({\bf t};x)\|

(2.9)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{E}}\|F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t}-{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)-F({\bf t};x)\|,

(2.10)

which are also uniformly in
x\in B
and,
\mathcal{E}
is any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}

On the other hand, because
(||{\bf a}_{k}-{\bf b}_{k}||)
is bounded, there exists
R>0
such that for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\bf c}_{k_{l}}:={\bf a}_{k_{l}}-{\bf b}_{k_{l}}\in\overline{B(0,R)}
(the closed ball with center
0
and radius
R
). Therefore, we have:

\displaystyle\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf a}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)

\displaystyle-F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf c}_{k_{l}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)-F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf c}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}+

\displaystyle+\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)-F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}+\|F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t};x\bigr)-F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf c}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\bf z}\in\overline{B(0,R)}+{\bf t}}\|F\bigl({\bf z}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)-F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf z};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}+

\displaystyle+\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)-F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}+\|F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t};x\bigr)-F^{\ast}\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf c}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}.

Now, since
F(\cdot,\cdot)
is compactly
({\mathrm{R}}_{{\mathcal{G}}},{\mathcal{B}})
-multi-almost automorphy and
F^{\ast}\bigl(\cdot;x\bigr)
is continuous, we have (using the previous inequality)

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{x\in B}\|F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf a}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)-F\bigl({\bf t}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}};x\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}=0\,.

Therefore,
\psi_{0}({\bf t},x)=0
. Now, since every convergent subsequence of the bounded sequence
\alpha_{k}({\bf t};x)

4
New characterizations of compact almost automorphic functions

In this section, we present the main results of the present work. We begin with the following well-known characterization of multidimensional compact almost automorphic functions presented in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
First characterization
) A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathcal{X}
is compact almost automorphic if and only if it is almost automorphic and uniformly continuous. Thus,

\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})=AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})\cap UC(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}}).

The next characterization, where we consider
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, is a generalization to the multidimensional setting of
[
9
, Theorem 3.16]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

(
Second characterization
)
A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{X}}
is compact almost automorphic if and only if it is
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
-almost automorphic and uniformly continuous, that is:

\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})=\mathbb{Z}^{m}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})\cap UC(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}}).

Proof.

Let us see

i) Let
f
be a compact almost automorphic function, then
f
is
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
-almost automorphic and, according to Theorem
4.1
, it is uniformly continuous. Therefore we have the inclusion
\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{m}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})\cap UC(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}}).

ii) Let us prove the reverse inclusion. Consider
f
a
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
-almost automorphic and uniformly continuous function; then, according to Theorem
4.1
, we need to prove that
f
is almost automorphic.

Let
({\bf b}_{k}=(b_{k}^{1},b_{k}^{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,b_{k}^{m}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Denoting by
[|\cdot|]
the integer part of a real umber and by
\{\cdot\}
the decimal part, then for each
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
we have the decomposition

b_{k}^{j}=[|b_{k}^{j}|]+\{b_{k}^{j}\}
, where
[|b_{k}^{j}|]\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\{b_{k}^{j}\}\in[0,1)
. In this way we have

\displaystyle{\bf b}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{([|b_{k}^{1}|],[|b_{k}^{2}|],\cdots,[|b_{k}^{m}|])}_{\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}+\underbrace{(\{b_{k}^{1}\},\{b_{k}^{2}\},\cdots,\{b_{k}^{m}\})}_{\in[0,1)^{m}}.

Since
f\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{X}})
, there exist a subsequence
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
([|b_{k}^{1}|],[|b_{k}^{2}|],\cdots,[|b_{k}^{m}|])_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a function
f^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{X}}
such that the following pointwise limits hold

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{f({\mathbf{t}}+\alpha_{k})=f^{*}({\mathbf{t}})}\,,{\mathbf{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{f^{*}({\mathbf{t}}-\alpha_{k})=f({\mathbf{t}})}\,,{\mathbf{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,.

On the other hand, since for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\{b_{k}^{1}\},\{b_{k}^{2}\},\cdots,\{b_{k}^{m}\})\in[0,1)^{m}
, then the sequence
((\{b_{k}^{1}\},\{b_{k}^{2}\},\cdots,\{b_{k}^{m}\}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. Therefore, there exists a convergent subsequence
(\beta_{k}=(\beta_{k}^{1},\beta_{k}^{2},\cdots,\beta_{k}^{m}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(\{b_{k}^{1}\},\{b_{k}^{2}\},\cdots,\{b_{k}^{m}\}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges to the point
\beta_{0}=(\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{0}^{2},\cdots,\beta_{0}^{m})\in[0,1]^{m}
.

In this way, it is possible to construct a subsequence
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset({\bf b}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, defined by

\eta_{k}:=(\alpha_{k}^{1},\alpha_{k}^{2},\cdots,\alpha_{k}^{m})+(\beta_{k}^{1},\beta_{k}^{2},\cdots,\beta_{k}^{m})=\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}\,.

Now, define the function
h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to{\mathcal{X}}
by
h({\mathbf{t}}):=f^{*}({\mathbf{t}}+\beta_{0})
.

Let
{\mathbf{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be fixed, we have

\displaystyle||f({\mathbf{t}}+\eta_{k})-h({\mathbf{t}})||_{{\mathcal{X}}}\leq||f({\mathbf{t}}+\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k})-f({\mathbf{t}}+\alpha_{k}+\beta_{0})||_{{\mathcal{X}}}+||f({\mathbf{t}}+\alpha_{k}+\beta_{0})-h({\mathbf{t}})||_{{\mathcal{X}}}\,.

Since
\beta_{k}\to\beta_{0}
and
||f({\mathbf{t}}+\beta_{0}+\alpha_{k})-h({\mathbf{t}})||_{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow 0
when
k\rightarrow\infty
and due to the uniform continuity of
f
, we conclude (according to the previous inequality) that:

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{f({\mathbf{t}}+\eta_{k})=h({\mathbf{t}})}.

On the other hand, according to Proposition
2.3
, we know that
f^{*}
is uniformly continuous. Then, arguing as before, it can be proved that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{h({\mathbf{t}}-\eta_{k})=f({\mathbf{t}})}\,.

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

(
Third characterization
)
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathcal{X}
be a continuous function and
\mathcal{G}
a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}

is compact almost automorphic if and only if it is
\mathcal{G}
-almost automorphic and uniformly continuous. Thus,

\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})=\mathcal{G}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})\cap UC(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}}).

Proof.

The inclusion

follows from Theorem

Let
f\in\mathcal{G}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})\cap UC(\mathbb{R}^{m};{\mathcal{X}})

4.1
we need to prove that
f
is almost automorphic. Let
({\bf b}_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an arbitrary sequence and
\epsilon>0

{\bf b}_{k}
and radius
\epsilon/2^{k}
. Since
\mathcal{G}

\mathbb{R}^{m}

(\gamma_{k})
in
\mathcal{G}
such that

and

f
is
\mathcal{G}
-almost automorphic, there exist a subsequence
(\gamma_{k_{l}})\subset(\gamma_{k})
and a function

2.3

is also uniformly continuous. On the other hand, there exists a subsequence

\displaystyle||f({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-f^{*}({\mathbf{t}})||_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||f({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-f({\mathbf{t}}+\gamma_{k_{l}})||_{\mathcal{X}}+

\displaystyle||f({\mathbf{t}}+\gamma_{k_{l}})-f^{*}({\mathbf{t}})||_{\mathcal{X}}\,,

\displaystyle+

A jointly continuous function
K:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{Y}
is
(\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{B})
-multi-Bi-almost automorphic if for any
B\in\mathcal{B}
and any sequence
(b_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
, there exist a subsequence
(b_{k_{l}})\subset(b_{k})
and a function
K^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{Y}
such that

\lim_{l\to+\infty}K({\bf t}+b_{k_{l}},{\bf s}+b_{k_{l}},x)=K^{*}({\bf t},{\bf s},x)\,,

(5.21)

and

\lim_{l\to+\infty}K^{*}({\bf t}-b_{k_{l}},{\bf s}-b_{k_{l}},x)=K({\bf t},{\bf s},x)\,,

(5.22)

hold pointwisely for
({\bf t},{\bf s})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and any
x\in B
.
If the limits in (
5.21
) and (
5.22
) are uniform on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then we say that
K
is compactly
(\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{B})
-multi-Bi-almost automorphic.

The next definition, was given in
[
13
]
in order to study almost automorphic type solutions to integral equations of advanced and delayed type

Definition 5.2
.

We say that a jointly continuous function
K:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{Y}
is
\lambda
-bounded if there exists a non negative function
\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we have

(5.23)

where the inequality is for each
({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and any

.

As it was proved for the one-dimensional case, see

7
, Lemma 2.3]
, the inequality (

) also holds for the limit function

of a
\lambda
-bounded and multi-Bi-almost auromorphic function

. That is, we have the following lemma

Lemma 5.3
.

Let us suppose that
K:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{Y}
is
(\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{B})
-multi-Bi-almost automorphic and
\lambda

) is also
\lambda

({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and any

we have

||K^{*}({\mathbf{t}}+\tau,{\mathbf{s}}+\tau,x)||_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\lambda({\bf t},{\bf s}),\,\,\forall\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,.

Property (
SC
)
. We say that the operator valued function
K:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{X}};\mathcal{Y})
satisfies the property of being
(SC)
if, for each

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
be the set of sequences such that all of its subsequences are also in
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
and let the operator valued function

\lambda

\Gamma

\mathcal{K}\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathcal{X})\cap BC(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathcal{X})

\mathcal{K}\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathcal{X})\cap BC(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathcal{Y})

Proof.

Let

and define

.
Since
K(\cdot,\cdot)
is compactly
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
-multi-Bi-almost automorphic and
u
is compactly
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
-multi-almost automorphic we can ensure that given any sequence
(b_{k})\subset\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}

and functions

and

\lim\limits_{l\to+\infty}K({\mathbf{t}}+b_{k_{l}},{\mathbf{s}}+b_{k_{l}})=K^{*}({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}}),\quad\lim\limits_{l\to+\infty}K^{*}({\mathbf{t}}-b_{k_{l}},{\mathbf{s}}-b_{k_{l}})=K({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}});

Now, using the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem we see that

\lim\limits_{l\to+\infty}v({\mathbf{t}}+b_{k_{l}})=v^{*}({\mathbf{t}}),\,\,\lim_{l\to\infty}v^{*}({\mathbf{t}}-b_{k_{l}})=v({\mathbf{t}})\,,

(5.24)

where

That is,

is
\mathrm{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
-multi-almost automorphic. In what follows we prove that the limits in (
5.24
) are uniform for
{\bf t}
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Let
E
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Since
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, there exist a strictly increasing sequence of positive real numbers
(R_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[R_{n},0]}\phi(z)dz=0\,,

where

is the closed ball of center
0
and radii
R_{n}
.

Note that, if
n
is sufficiently large, then there exists a subsequence
(\tilde{R}_{n})
of
(R_{n})
such that
E+\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[R_{n},0]\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[\tilde{R}_{n},0]
. Now, let us take
{\bf t}\in E
, then we have

\displaystyle||v({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-v^{*}({\mathbf{t}})||_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}||K({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}},\eta+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-K^{*}({\mathbf{t}},\eta)||\,||u(\eta+{\mathbf{t}}_{k})||_{\mathcal{X}}d\eta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}||K^{*}({\bf t},\eta)||\,||u(\eta+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-u^{*}(\eta)||d\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B[R_{n},0]}||K({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}},\eta+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-K^{*}({\mathbf{t}},\eta)||\,||u(\eta+{\mathbf{t}}_{k})||_{\mathcal{X}}d\eta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[R_{n},0]}||K({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}},\eta+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-K^{*}({\mathbf{t}},\eta)||\,||u(\eta+{\mathbf{t}}_{k})||_{\mathcal{X}}d\eta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}||K^{*}({\bf t},\eta)||\,||u(\eta+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-u^{*}(\eta)||d\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||u||_{\infty}\sup_{({\bf t},{\bf s})\in\mathcal{E}\times B[R_{n},0]}||K({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}},\eta+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-K^{*}({\mathbf{t}},\eta)||\,\mathcal{L}(B[R_{n},0])

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2||u||_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[R_{n},0]}\phi({{\mathbf{t}}}-\eta)d\eta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(\eta)\,\sup_{z\in E_{\eta}}||u(z+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-u^{\ast}(z)||_{\mathcal{X}}d\eta\,,

where,

is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. From the previous inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{{\bf t}\in\mathcal{E}}||v({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-v^{*}({\mathbf{t}})||_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||u||_{\infty}\sup_{({\bf t},{\bf s})\in\mathcal{E}\times B[R_{n},0]}||K({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}},\eta+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-K^{*}({\mathbf{t}},\eta)||\,\mathcal{L}(B[R_{n},0])

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2||u||_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[R_{n},0]}\phi({{\mathbf{t}}}-\eta)d\eta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(\eta)\,\sup_{z\in E_{\eta}}||u(z+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-u^{\ast}(z)||_{\mathcal{X}}d\eta\,.

Now, taking the limit in the last inequality when

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{{\bf t}\in\mathcal{E}}||v({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-v^{*}({\mathbf{t}})||_{\mathcal{X}}\leq 2||u||_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[R_{n},0]}\phi({{\mathbf{t}}}-\eta)d\eta.

On the other hand, the integral of the right hand side satisfies:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[R_{n},0]}\phi({{\mathbf{t}}}-\eta)d\eta\leq\int_{E+\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[R_{n},0]}\phi(z)dz\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus B[\tilde{R}_{n},0]}\phi(z)dz\,\to 0,\,\,n\to\infty\,.

Therefore,

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{{\bf t}\in\mathcal{E}}||v({\mathbf{t}}+{\bf b}_{k_{l}})-v^{*}({\mathbf{t}})||_{\mathcal{X}}=0\,.

Analogously, we have

∎

6
Applications to the Poisson’s and heat equations

6.1
Compact almost automorphy of the solution to Poisson’s equation

Definition 6.1
.

Let
f\in BC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
. A function
u:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is solution in the sense of distributions of the Poisson’s equation

\Delta u=f,

if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}u(x)\Delta\phi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(x)\phi(x)dx,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

From the the theory of distributional solutions for the Laplace equation, we have

Theorem 6.1
.

[
19
]

1.

(Weyl) If
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
and
u
is a solution in the sense of distributions of
\Delta u=0
, then
u
is harmonic, i.e.,
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\Delta u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

2.

(Lioville) If
u:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is harmonic and bounded, then
u
is constant.

Theorem
6.1
and the Arzel’a-Ascolí Theorem were used in
[
12
]
to prove that, every bounded and continuous function with almost automorphic distributional laplacian is in fact compact almost automorphic.
The next Theorem is the main result of the present subsection.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be either
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
or a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
f\in\mathcal{G}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
. If
u:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded continuous function which is a solution in the sense of distributions of Poisson’s equation:

\Delta u=f\,,

then
u\in\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Indeed, as demonstrated in the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 20]
, we establish that
u
is uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and compactly
\mathcal{G}
-multi almost automorphic. Consequently, the conclusion that
u
is a compact almost automorphic solution follows from either the second characterization theorem (in the case where
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}^{m}
) or the third characterization theorem (when
\mathcal{G}
is a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
) presented in Section
4
.
∎

This theorem emphasizes that, even if the source function
f
is not fully almost automorphic, but belongs to
\mathcal{G}\text{AA}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
with
\mathcal{G}
as specified, its solution remains compact almost automorphic. Consequently, compact almost automorphic solutions to Poisson’s equation are preserved under a broader class of source functions, extending beyond the almost periodic/automorphic case presented in
[
24
,
12
]
.

6.2
Compact almost automorphy of the solution to heat equation

Le us consider the heat equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}u(t,x)&=&\Delta u(t,x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\\
u(0,x)&=&f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\end{array}\right.

(6.26)

where
f\in BC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
. The maximal domain of the Laplace operator
\Delta
in the Banach space
Z=BC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
or
BUC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
, is given by

D(\Delta):=\{v\in Z:\Delta v\text{ exists in }Z\}.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
f\in BC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})\cap\overline{D(A)}
, by a solution of equation (
6.26
) we mean a function
u:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that
u\in C^{1}((0,+\infty),BC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}))
,
u(t,\cdot)\in D(\Delta)
for all
t>0
and
u
satisfies (
6.26
) pointwisely.

In particular, if
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})\cap\overline{D(A)}
, the solution
u
satisfies
u\in C([0,+\infty),BC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}))\cap C^{1}((0,+\infty),BC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}))
,
u(t,\cdot)\in D(\Delta)
for all
t>0
and
u
satisfies (
6.26
) poitnwisely.

It is well known that, the heat equation (
6.26
) admits the following solution

u(t,x)=T(t)f(x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\quad\text{ for }f\in\overline{D(A)}.

and
u(0,x)=f(x)
, where
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is the Gaussian semigroup in
BC(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
given by:

\displaystyle T(t)f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(4\pi t)^{m/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\|x-z\|^{2}/(4t)}f(z)dz=\dfrac{1}{(4\pi t)^{m/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\|z\|^{2}/(4t)}f(x-z)dz

(6.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(K(t,\cdot)*f)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,,

for
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
(see for instance
[
1
, Example 3.7.6]
).

The following is the main result concerning the heat equation in this work

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{G}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})\cap\overline{D(A)}
, where
\mathcal{G}
be either
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
or a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
f\in\mathcal{G}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
. Then, equation (
6.26
) admits a unique solution
u
such that, for each
t>0
,
u(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{G}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
and let
t>0
, from the invariance Theorem
5.1
, we have that
u(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{G}AA(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})
. Now, using the charaterization theorems in section
4
, it only rest to prove that
u(t,\cdot)
is uniformly continuous, and this follows as in ithe proof of
[
12
, Theorem 25]
. In fact, let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
; then

\displaystyle|u(t,x)-u(t,y)|\leq\dfrac{1}{(4\pi t)^{m/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|e^{-\|x-z\|^{2}/(4t)}-e^{-\|y-z\|^{2}/(4t)}|dz\,\|f\|_{\infty}.

(6.28)

Also, for fixed
t>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the function
x\longmapsto e^{-\|x-z\|^{2}/(4t)}
is globally Lipschitz continuous, i.e.,

|e^{-\|x-z\|^{2}/(4t)}-e^{-\|y-z\|^{2}/(4t)}|\leq\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\dfrac{c_{m}}{2t}\|w-z\|e^{-\|w-z\|^{2}/(4t)}\|x-y\|,

where
c_{m}>0
is a constant. Note that

\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|w-z\|e^{-\|w-z\|^{2}/(4t)}=\|w_{0}-z\|e^{-\|w_{0}-z\|^{2}/(4t)}\,\,,w_{0}\in\mathcal{D}\,,

where

\mathcal{D}:=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,:\,\|w-z\|=\sqrt{2t}\}\,.

Now, from (
6.28
) and using invariance under translation of the Lebesgue integral, we have

\displaystyle|u(t,x)-u(t,y)|

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}_{m}(t)\|f\|_{\infty}\|x-y\|.

∎

Thus, we have proved that the
\mathcal{G}
-multi-almost automorphy of the initial data
f
of equation (
6.26
), is sufficient to obtain the compact almost automorphy in space variable of the solution
u
.

Appendix A
Sequentially almost automorphic functions on topological groups

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a topological group (commutative, locally compact). We will write the group operation on
\mathcal{G}
additively, that is by
+
, and the inverse operation by
-
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
f:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{X}
be a continuous function.
f
is sequentially almost automorphic, if for any sequence
(s^{\prime}_{m})
of
\mathcal{G}
there exist a subsequence
(s_{m})
of
(s^{\prime}_{m})
and a function
f^{*}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{X}
such that the following pointwise limits holds:

\lim_{m\to\infty}f(t+s_{m})=f^{*}(t),

and:

\lim_{n\to\infty}f^{*}(t-s_{m})=f(t)\,.

AA({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{X}})
will denote the space of sequentially almost automorphic functions. The next two theorems are of importance in the present work.

Theorem A.1
.

If
f\in AA({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{X}})
, then
f
is bounded.

Proof.

Suppose that
f
is not bounded, that is, there exists a sequence
(s^{\prime}_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathcal{G}}
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{||f(s^{\prime}_{m})||_{\mathcal{X}}}=\infty,

as
f\in AA({\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{X})
, there exists a subsequence
(s_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(s^{\prime}_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
and a function
h:{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
such that the following pointwise limits holds:

f^{*}(\mathbf{t})=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}f(\mathbf{t}+s_{m})\,\,,\,\,f(\mathbf{t})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f^{*}(\mathbf{t}-s_{m}).

If
\textbf{t}=0
, in the first limit we have:

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{f(s_{m})}=f^{*}(0)\,\,;\,\,f^{*}(0)\in\mathcal{X},

with what we have

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{||f(s_{m})||_{\mathcal{X}}}=||f^{*}(0)||_{\mathcal{X}}<\infty,

which is a contradiction, therefore,
f
is bounded.
∎

Theorem A.2
.

If
f\in AA({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{X}})
, then the range of
f
is relatively compact in
{\mathcal{X}}
.

Proof.

Let
(y^{\prime}_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{R}(f):=\{y\in X:\exists{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathcal{G}}\,\,\text{whit}\,\,f({\mathbf{t}})=y\}
be an arbitrary sequence. By the definition of
{\mathcal{R}}(f)
there must be a sequence
(s^{\prime}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{G}}
such that
y^{\prime}_{m}=f(s^{\prime}_{m})
, as
f\in AA({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{X}})
there exist a subsequence
(s_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset(s^{\prime}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and a function
f^{*}:{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{X}}
such that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{y_{m}}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{f(s_{m})}=f^{*}(0).

Therefore, there exists a subsequence
(s_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset(s^{\prime}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
wich converges to
{\mathcal{X}}
, which means that
{\mathcal{R}}(f)
is relatively compact.
∎

Appendix B
Subgroups of
\mathbb{R}^{m}

Here we summarize important results on dense subgroups of the group
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We start with the following assertion:

Lemma B.1
.

Every nontrivial subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is unbounded.

Proof.

Let
H
be a nontrivial subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Since
H
is nontrivial, it contains at least one element
v
other than the zero vector. Now, for eack
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define
v_{k}:=k\cdot v\in H
, where
k\cdot v=v+v+\cdots+v
(k-times). Then, for each
k
, we have
\|v_{k}\|=\|k\cdot v\|=k\cdot\|v\|
. Therefore, the sequence
\{v_{k}\}
is unbounded. This proves that
H
is unbounded.
∎

B.1
Dense subgroups of
\mathbb{R}^{m}

Let
\mathbb{Q}
be the rational numbers. Some dense subgroups of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
are, for example,
\mathbb{Q}^{m}
,
\mathbb{Q}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}
,
\mathbb{Q}^{k_{1}}\times G^{k_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{k_{3}}
where
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=m
and
G^{k_{2}}
is a dense subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{k_{2}}
, etc. In the following Theorem and in the subsequent Proposition, it is described some conditions under which a subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
becomes dense.

Theorem B.2
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\cdots,\theta_{m}
real numbers. For the subgroup

\mathbb{Z}^{m}+\mathbb{Z}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\cdots,\theta_{m})=\{(s_{1}+s_{0}\theta_{1},\cdots,s_{m}+s_{0}\theta_{m});\,\,(s_{0},s_{1},\cdots,s_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}

to be dense in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, it is necessary and sufficient that the
m+1
numbers
1,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\cdots,\theta_{m}
be linearly independent on
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proposition B.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{m}

1.

G
is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

2.

For any nonzero linear form
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\phi(G)\not\subset\mathbb{Z}

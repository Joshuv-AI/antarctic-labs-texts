The Quantum Channels of the Compton Scattering of an Entangled Pair of Photons
Chih-Yu Chen, C. D. Hu, Yeu Chung Lin

Abstract
The Compton scattering of an entangled pair of photons is studied. Using the resulting reduced density matrix, we demonstrate how to derive the Kraus operators for the interaction from the underlying fundamental theory, quantum electrodynamics. The normalization of the final density matrix of the entangled photons after the interaction is crucial to obtaining the correct probabilities and correlations. The reduced density matrix of the photon, which does not participate in the interaction directly, remains unchanged. It indicates that quantum effects in quantum sensing using an entangled photon pair can be observed only through the correlations or superposition of the entire pair. We relate our results to the Stokes parameters for determining polarizations and investigating the entangled states of photons from electron-positron annihilation in astrophysical systems.

I
introduction

Quantum entanglement is a state in which each particle in the system cannot be described separately. This concept is essential for illustrating the relationship between the foundations of quantum mechanics and the hidden-variable theory proposed by Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen (EPR)
[
1
]
. Furthermore, experimental tests of the EPR theory often involve entangled states. Photon states constitute a particularly important class
[
2
]
. These experiments must account for photon-matter interactions, and a commonly used one is Compton scattering. Therefore, it is not surprising that one of the earliest studies of experiments concerning EPR theory was that of a pair of distantly correlated (entangled) photons, each photon undergoes Compton scattering
[
3
]
.

Experimental studies of this topic have a long history
[
4
]
. Recently, many theoretical and experimental works
[
5
]
have investigated the implications of entangled states with Compton scattering. Notably, Parashari et al.
[
6
]
studied the angular correlation of Compton-scattered annihilation photons and hidden variables. Tkachev et al.
[
7
]
investigated polarization correlations in entangled photons, for entangled-photon pairs in which one photon had been Compton-scattered. Therefore, we developed quantum operations of Compton scattering on the density matrix.

Three equivalent schemes can be used to treat quantum operations between a principal system and its environment, in the current case, an entangled pair of photons and an electron: physically motivated axioms, the operator-sum representation, and a system coupled to the environment
[
8
]
.

The first approach constructs a set of physically motivated axioms that we expect a dynamical map in quantum mechanics to satisfy. The general approach needs more insight into how to calculate the actual dynamics. The operator-sum representation, or the quantum channel
[
9
]
approach, is mathematically simple in applications. The third approach involves exploring the detailed dynamics between the system and its environment. One of its major applications is to use a set of operators acting on the density matrix, which represents the system’s state, to describe the interaction between the system and the quantum channel’s environment. Establishing a set of operators corresponding to a specific interaction by physical intuition is not particularly challenging if the interaction is simple. However, deriving a Kraus operator set from the fundamental theory of the corresponding interaction requires deliberate effort.

This study uses the fundamental theory of quantum electrodynamics (QED) to investigate Compton scattering of an entangled photon pair. Our focus, particularly on the entanglement, is on the dynamics of the photons’ polarizations. The entangled photons are treated as the principal system, and the electron is treated as the environment. The photon that interacts directly with the electron is dubbed the signal photon; the other is dubbed the idler photon, as it is not directly involved in the interaction.

Unlike the conventional approach to QED Feynman amplitude calculations, we use the density matrix, which represents the polarizations of the entangled photons, to calculate the interaction process and retain only the polarization density matrix after Compton scattering. QED is a perturbative theory. The initial state receives the zeroth order (the noninteracting part) and the first order (the interacting part) of the quantum correction, becoming the final state. To maintain the unitarity of states, the final states must be normalized.

In density matrix calculations, the unitarity of states is typically enforced by requiring the trace of the density matrix to be one; for instance, see Eq. (10) in Ref.
[
10
]
. However, a generic density matrix for entangled photon pairs includes matrix elements corresponding to different states, which may experience varying interaction strengths and require different normalizations after the interaction. In this regard, we develop a normalization scheme for the density matrix of an entangled photon pair. The established procedure is crucial to obtaining the correct probability and correlation elements of the density matrix.

The quantum channels for Compton scattering between the principal system and its environment are derived by comparing the initial and properly normalized final polarization density matrices of the entangled photons. One of the main results of this work is the detailed calculation of the process deduction from a fundamental theory to a set of Kraus operators corresponding to a specific interaction.

Using the derived density matrix and the quantum channel set, we explore the physics of the effective action of Compton scattering on the polarizations of entangled photons. It is found that Compton scattering behaves as a depolarizing mode for polarizations when considering only the horizontal polarizations. The entanglement entropy decreases after Compton scattering primarily due to depolarization. The reduced density matrix of the signal and idler photons is derived to characterize their mutual information. The mutual information of the system is also reduced due to the correlation reduction in the Compton scattering process.

It has been shown that photons can travel an astronomical distance without losing their coherence
[
11
]
. We investigate the Compton scattering of a pair of entangled photons created by electron-positron annihilation in astrophysical systems. Furthermore, we present our results in terms of Stokes parameters for optical study.

The article is organized as follows. Section II investigates the single-photon polarization degree of freedom in Compton scattering in a density matrix form. We adopt a normalization scheme in which each element in the final density matrix is normalized by its corresponding normalization constant. This scheme differs from the ordinary normalization scheme, which divides the final density matrix by an overall normalization constant derived from the sum of its diagonal elements. In Section III, we study Compton scattering of an entangled photon pair and calculate the final density matrix of the photons. We continue to adopt the aforementioned normalization scheme and prove that it yields the correct physical results. Section IV derives the Kraus operators for single-photon and entangled-photon states corresponding to the Compton scattering. We compute the reduced density matrix for the signal and idler photons. Using them, we derive the entropy and mutual information. We explore the physical results from these calculations. In Section V, the relation between our reduced density matrix and Stokes parameters is established, and the entanglement of photons from astrophysical sources is studied. Finally, we conclude and summarize our findings in Section VI.

IV
The Kraus operators, entropy and mutual information

Quantum channels effectively describe the net effects of the interaction between the principal system and its environment on the principal system. Exploring the physics of the interaction between the principal system and its environment is clearer in the quantum-channel representation. After developing the fundamental theory of Compton scattering in the single-photon and entangled-photon cases, we present our calculations in Sec. II and III in the Kraus operator format.

We shall consider only the large electron mass limit for which the visible light and most EM waves of application are excellent approximations. In this limit,
\omega^{\prime}\approx\omega

VI
Conclusion

Using the classic case of QED, we demonstrate how to derive the net effects of the interaction between an entangled pair of photons and electrons. The normalization of the final density matrix, element by element, is critical for obtaining the correct result.

We derive a complete set of Kraus operators for Compton scattering in the Thomson limit, using the initial and final density matrices obtained from the fundamental theory calculation. The form of the Kraus operators indicates that the Compton scattering depolarizes the
H
state, tilting the
H
polarization to the original photon momentum direction while keeping the
V
polarization unchanged. The increase of entanglement entropy and the decrease of mutual information arise for the same reason.

The reduced density matrix of the idler photon remains intact, as expected. Compton scattering is a local interaction; it should not affect a particle that does not participate in it. Thus, attempting to measure the idler photon alone to enhance quantum sensing efficiency contradicts fundamental principles of physics.

We present our results in terms of the Stokes matrix and show that the degree of polarization of the signal photon and idler photon is the same after scattering in the entire energy range. The study of entanglement produced by astrophysical sources is investigated. The photon pair created by electron-positron annihilation is analyzed. The Compton-scattered signal photon has an energy of
0.17
MeV, a clear signature of entanglement.

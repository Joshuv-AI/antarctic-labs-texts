Forecasting Alzheimer’s disease progression via identity-preserved denoising diffusion generative adversarial network
Authors: Zhuangzhuang Li, Tongtong Che, Shaozhen Yan, Dong Wang, Yong Liu, Kun Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Forecasting the progression of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is essential for evaluating secondary prevention measures thought to modify the disease trajectory. However, accurate prediction of longitudinal MRIs remains challenging, particularly in preserving subject identity, as deep generative models may potentially generate plausible future MRIs of different individuals from a single baseline scan. In the present study, we developed a novel identity-preserved denoising diffusion generative adversarial network (IP-DDGAN) capable of rapidly generating subject-specific longitudinal MRIs conditioned on metadata. Concretely, we developed an identity-preservation strategy incorporating a metadata-guided module and identity-preserved regularization terms to maintain subject identity in synthetic longitudinal MRIs. Furthermore, we comprehensively integrated morphometric, subject-identity-consistency, and image-level quality metrics to evaluate the fidelity and biological plausibility of synthetic longitudinal MRIs. The results demonstrate that the synthetic MRIs generated by IP-DDGAN retain biological and disease-related phenotypes and exhibit sufficient realism to support downstream applications. Our proposed model effectively captures temporal biological and disease-related changes and predicts distinct disease progression trajectories, including the clinically important transitions from cognitively normal (CN) to mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and from MCI to AD.

Introduction

Dementia is a growing global public health concern, affecting more than 55 million people worldwide, and the number of affected individuals is expected to double by 2050. Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is the most common type of dementia, accounting for 60~70% of cases1. AD can show multiple distinct patterns of longitudinal progression between individuals due to the high heterogeneity of AD2–4. For example, some patients may remain stable with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), while others may progress to AD. Consequently, anticipating AD progression is critical to evaluating secondary prevention measures thought to modify the disease trajectory5. However, the limited time points and short follow-up intervals in longitudinal datasets make it challenging to capture subtle anatomical changes, particularly during transitions such as the progression from MCI to AD, which complicates the prediction of AD progression.

A diverse range of deep generative models has been developed for super-resolution, image denoising, modality translation, lesion segmentation, and longitudinal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) synthesis6–9. Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are classic baseline methods for brain MRI synthesis, facilitating fast sampling and mode diversity, but they often struggle to generate high-quality MRIs10. Although generative adversarial networks (GANs) are capable of rapidly generating high-quality MRIs, the generated MRIs lack variety in modes11,12. Recently, denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) have delivered surprisingly impressive results in brain MRI synthesis, ranging from rich anatomical details to diverse generated MRIs, but they require expensive computational resources and longer inference times due to the need for a large number of diffusion steps during sampling13,14. These generative models do not simultaneously satisfy the requirements of high-quality sampling, sample diversity, and fast sampling15. Given the strengths of these models, this has motivated the exploration of hybrid architectures that combine the complementary strengths of both frameworks. By integrating iterative diffusion model refinement with the adversarial training mechanism of GANs, this hybrid approach addresses the fundamental trade-off among high-quality sampling, sample diversity, and fast sampling. Adversarial diffusion models have recently been developed and have demonstrated promising performance in medical image translation16.

However, adversarial diffusion models still face challenges in balancing image quality with structural fidelity when applied to brain MRI synthesis. The first challenge is that longitudinal MRI generation is inherently an ill-posed inverse problem, as a single baseline MRI scan can generate plausible future MRIs for multiple subjects17,18. This one-to-many mapping can cause the synthesized follow-up MRI to deviate from the same subject’s anatomy. However, identity preservation is essential in longitudinal MRI synthesis to retain subject-specific anatomical characteristics while allowing biologically plausible disease-related changes to evolve. A subject’s phenotype is determined by subject information, such as demographic characteristics and disease status. To address this one-to-many challenge, subject-specific information should be incorporated to improve both the realism and identity preservation of synthesized longitudinal MRIs. Another challenge lies in validating the ability of deep generative models to preserve the morphology of the generated MRIs. In previous studies, the performance of deep generative models is often validated by the realism and diversity of synthetic medical images11. Nevertheless, these metrics are insufficient for validation when applied to medical images. A lack of validation of the generative model’s ability to preserve disease-related morphological features may compromise the reliability of downstream applications and subsequent analyses.

In this paper, we aim to rapidly generate subject-specific, diverse, realistic-looking, high-quality, and morphologically preserving longitudinal MRIs. We proposed a novel identity-preserved denoising diffusion GAN (IP-DDGAN) conditioned on subject characteristics such as age, sex, diagnosis label, and baseline MRI (Fig. 1). Specifically, we developed a metadata-guided module, along with identity-preserved regularization terms, to ensure subject identity in synthetic longitudinal MRIs. Under the non-Gaussian distribution hypothesis, the IP-DDGAN can simultaneously satisfy fast sampling, high-quality sample generation, and mode diversity. Furthermore, we introduced morphometrics to supplement traditional image-level quality assessment, which integrates qualitative brain age estimation with quantitative morphological analysis to validate the morphology of synthesized longitudinal MRIs comprehensively. Results

To verify the performance of IP-DDGAN, we compared it with the conditional GAN (CGAN)19, CycleGAN20, self-attention GAN (SAGAN)21, DDPM22, and latent diffusion model (LDM)23. These models represent established benchmarks in the fields of sampling quality, diversity, and temporal consistency. CGAN is used as a conditional adversarial baseline model for image synthesis. CycleGAN represents unpaired image-to-image translation with cycle consistency and is widely applied to medical image translation and longitudinal MRI synthesis24,25. SAGAN incorporates self-attention to model long-range spatial dependencies. DDPM is a diffusion-based generation model that applies Gaussian denoising to refine noisy images progressively. It has been successfully applied in various fields, including image denoising, super-resolution, and longitudinal MRI synthesis26,27. LDM provides a latent diffusion framework that improves sampling efficiency by operating in a compressed latent space. We employed the standard DDPM implementation with 1000 diffusion steps. For LDM, the input was processed through two downsampling layers to produce a compressed latent representation with dimensions of 24 × 24 × 32, which was then followed by the diffusion process with 1000 diffusion steps. Similar to IP-DDGAN, the competing method also incorporates age, gender, diagnosis label, and baseline MRI to guide the synthesized MRIs. The progression trajectories—including CN to CN, CN to MCI, MCI to MCI, MCI to AD, and AD to AD—were used to evaluate the performance of IP-DDGAN against competing methods. The assessment incorporated image fidelity, realism, sampling efficiency, morphology, brain age, Turing-style test, and subject-level consistency.

Morphological evaluation

Assessing the morphology of synthesized images is crucial for their application in downstream tasks. We evaluated the morphology between synthetic and longitudinal MRIs by segmenting them into gray matter (GM), white matter (WM), and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) across all subjects. The SPM12 toolbox was used to segment the brain tissue. For each MRI, the volumes of 246 GM regions were defined by the Brainnetome Atlas28, and 48 WM regions were defined by the JHU ICBM DTI Atlas29. Supplementary Fig. 3 showed the results of brain tissue segmentation. Ablation Studies to evaluate the contributions of each pipeline component

We conducted additional ablation studies to investigate the effects of the identity-preservation regularization loss weight, the metadata-guided module, and the adversarial component on the quality and identity consistency of the synthesized longitudinal images. The results showed that the metadata-guided module and the adversarial component significantly improve the identity consistency of synthesized longitudinal images. The image quality is improved, with PSNR of 31.62 ± 2.18, SSIM of 98.62 ± 0.01, and MMD of 0.66 ± 0.55 (Supplementary Tables 7 and 8). The choice of appropriate parameters for the identity-preservation regularization loss weight and diffusion steps can significantly improve image quality while preserving subject identity consistency (Supplementary Tables 9–11). The results of these experiments are provided in the Supplementary Material S08.

Discussion

Digital twins, a concept borrowed from engineering and applied in healthcare, represent virtual models of individuals that are continuously updated using real-world data. The use of digital twins in healthcare allows for more accurate monitoring and tailored treatment strategies, which are critical for improving patient outcomes. Building on this idea, we developed a novel identity-preserved denoising diffusion GAN capable of generating subject-specific, morphologically preserving longitudinal 3D MRIs of the brain in AD. A metadata-guided module and identity-preserved regularization term are incorporated into IP-DDGAN to retain subject-specific information. We used a comprehensive evaluation framework incorporating both morphometric and image-level quality metrics to rigorously evaluate the performance of IP-DDGAN in terms of image fidelity, morphology, controllable generation of metadata, brain age, Turing-style test, and subject identity consistency. Quantitative results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves state-of-the-art performance in longitudinal MRI synthesis, effectively quantifies anatomical changes associated with normal aging, and accurately captures accelerated structural alterations affected by AD.

Forecasting AD progression has been a challenging problem for decades, owing to the high heterogeneity in disease trajectories and the scarcity of longitudinal datasets with long follow-up intervals5. Recently, generative models have emerged as a promising approach for capturing realistic aging and disease-related progression changes33–36. Building on this momentum, we developed IP-DDGAN to generate anatomically faithful, subject-specific disease trajectories of AD. Our proposed method can accurately capture both ventricular expansion and hippocampal atrophy across AD progression. Previous studies have shown that genetic architectures of cerebral ventricles significantly overlap with risk loci for major neuropsychiatric disorders, including AD, schizophrenia, and depression37,38. This characterizes ventricular size and shape not only as downstream markers of neurodegeneration, but also as genetically informed traits that link molecular risk factors with macroscopic brain changes. Extending this line of evidence, our work suggests that synthetic longitudinal imaging may provide a scalable framework for investigating how genetic liability translates into subject-specific morphological alterations over time. Further, by leveraging these synthetic longitudinal MRIs, we can identify individuals at higher risk of rapid disease progression, facilitating early intervention and the targeted prioritization of those at greater risk.

Longitudinal neuroimaging studies are fundamentally concerned with tracking subject-specific trajectories of brain change rather than population-averaged trends39,40. Therefore, any usable synthetic dataset should preserve the subject identity and statistical properties of the real data. Previous studies have often relied on L1/L2 losses, perceptual losses, or cycle-consistency losses to retain individual characteristics. However, these approaches tend to overlook subtle anatomical features due to their emphasis on minimizing global reconstruction error18,41,42. The proposed method introduced a metadata-guided module and identity-preserved regularization terms to keep the subject identity. The results demonstrated that IP-DDGAN supports controllable longitudinal MRI generation conditioned on age, sex, and diagnostic labels. In addition, the subject similarity analysis confirmed that IP-DDGAN preserves subject-specific fidelity while distinguishing across individuals.

In longitudinal MRI synthesis, optimizing high-quality sampling, sample diversity, and fast sampling is critical for ensuring the clinical utility of generated data43. High-quality sampling demands that synthetic MRIs preserve anatomical details, sample diversity must reflect the heterogeneity of aging and disease progression, while fast sampling determines feasibility for large-scale studies15,44. In this study, we challenge the conventional assumption in diffusion models that denoising distributions follow a Gaussian distribution. Instead, a conditional GAN generator is used to capture the non-Gaussian nature of these distributions, achieving more efficient and faster synthesis. However, this approach increases model complexity and parameter count, leading to higher computational demands. Despite these increased resource demands, the inference time of IP-DDGAN is improved by 180 times compared to DDPM, and the increased model capacity is accompanied by improved image quality in the synthesized longitudinal MRIs.

Identity-preservation loss is essential for generating high-quality longitudinal images in medical imaging. Previous methods often struggled to maintain the subject identity of synthesized MRIs. While some focused on improving image realism through adversarial loss, they did not adequately preserve subject-specific features, leading to subject inconsistencies in longitudinal MRI synthesis45–47. Our approach integrates adversarial loss, self-reconstruction loss, perceptual loss, and identity-preservation loss to effectively address this challenge. The experiments showed that these losses improved both image realism and subject consistency, contributing to the generation of high-quality and reliable longitudinal MRIs.

Current evaluation metrics for medical image synthesis predominantly rely on metrics from natural image evaluation, such as PSNR, SSIM, MMD, and FID48–50. Although these metrics assess pixel-level fidelity or perceptual similarity, they fall short of evaluating a generative model’s ability to preserve the morphological integrity of synthesized MRIs. To address this problem, we established a comprehensive evaluation framework encompassing image fidelity, morphology, controllable metadata generation, brain age, Turing-style test, and subject identity evaluation. Our proposed method demonstrated superior performance across multiple evaluation metrics, supporting the reliability of downstream analyses based on the generated longitudinal MRIs. However, ethical considerations surrounding the use of synthetic medical image generation present a common challenge for all generative models in medical imaging. It is important to emphasize that these synthesized MRIs are intended solely for research purposes and as tools to assist in clinical decision-making. They should not replace clinical judgment or be used independently for diagnosis.

Our proposed model accurately captures anatomical changes from CN to MCI and from MCI to AD, effectively reflecting the progressive changes of early and intermediate disease stages. However, the proposed framework has several limitations. Its effectiveness relies on the availability and diversity of longitudinal training data, which are crucial for accurately capturing the complexities of late-stage disease progression. In addition, forecasting disease progression over longer follow-up horizons remains challenging, as long-term neurodegenerative changes are inherently uncertain. Finally, real-world clinical longitudinal data are often noisy, incomplete, and irregularly sampled, posing additional challenges for disease modeling. Future work will focus on incorporating more diverse longitudinal datasets and integrating multimodal clinical data to further improve the clinical applicability of IP-DDGAN.

Study population and data preprocessing

We used the data from three longitudinal cohorts and a cross-sectional cohort: the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) (N = 1038) (https://adni.loni.usc.edu/), the Australian Imaging Biomarkers and Lifestyle flagship study of Aging (AIBL)(N = 524) (https://ida.loni.usc.edu/collaboration/access/appApply.jsp?project=AIBL), Open Access Series of Imaging Studies (OASIS2)(N = 150) (https://sites.wustl.edu/oasisbrains/home/access/) and the Cambridge Centre for Ageing and Neuroscience (Cam-CAN) dataset (N = 707) (https://camcan-archive.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/dataaccess/). We used the ADNI cohort as the training dataset for IP-DDGAN, while the AIBL and OASIS2 cohorts served as testing datasets to assess the model’s generalization across different populations. Additionally, a Fork-SE–based brain age prediction model was trained on the Cam-CAN dataset to provide biological validation for the generated longitudinal MRI data. The detailed demographic information of the subjects is presented in Supplementary Material S01.

The longitudinal trajectories include 111 scans from subjects who remained CN at 36-month follow-up, 53 scans at 72-month follow-up, and 29 scans from CN-to-MCI converters. For MCI-to-AD converters, 13 scans were collected, while 20 scans were obtained from subjects who remained AD at 36 months, with an additional 7 scans at 72 months. The OASIS2 dataset includes longitudinal T1-weighted MRIs from CN (N = 86) and AD (N = 64) subjects. Subjects have multiple follow-up visits spanning 1-4 years. Model development

The proposed IP-DDGAN is designed to generate high-quality, subject-specific longitudinal MRI scans rapidly. IP-DDGAN inherits the fundamental principles of the diffusion model and GAN. Specifically, IP-DDGAN follows the diffusion framework with a forward noising process and a reverse denoising process to synthesize follow-up MRIs conditioned on subject information. During the reverse process, the conditional generator produces subject-specific MRIs guided by subject information, while the time-dependent discriminator ensures anatomical realism and minimizes artifacts. More importantly, we introduced a learned conditional metadata encoder (including age, sex, diagnosis label, and baseline image) to extract the conditioning features, along with an identity-preserving regularization term to guide the synthesis of identity-preserving MRIs. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the IP-DDGAN consists of three modules: (1) a metadata-guided module, (2) a conditional GAN generator, and (3) a time-dependent discriminator. The metadata-guided module integrates subject-specific information into the conditional GAN generator. Conditional GAN generator

The conditional GAN generator consists of a z-conditioning generator using mapping networks. The z-conditioning generator was used to estimate the reconstructed MRI \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x}_{0}^{{\prime} }$$\end{document}x0′. We designed the conditional GAN generator using a style-based U-Net architecture inspired by NCSN + +53. The encoder consists of four downsampling convolutional blocks with channel sizes 64, 128, 128, and 256, each preceded by two style-based ResNet blocks and implemented using a 3 × 3 × 3 convolution with stride 2, to extract high-level semantic information from the input image. The style-based ResNet blocks incorporate global style and sinusoidal time-step embeddings to regulate feature maps (Supplementary Fig. 1). Self-attention blocks are used to transfer high-level semantic information from the encoder to the decoder. The decoder consists of four upsampling convolutional blocks with channel sizes of 256, 128, 64, and 1. Each upsampling block is followed by two style-based ResNet blocks, and the upsampling operation is performed using a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$3\times 3\times 3$$\end{document}3×3×3 transposed convolution with a stride of 2. The encoder-decoder has long skip connections to help preserve the details of the input images.

The medical images exhibited a high degree of similarity across different subjects. Training

The training process of IP-DDGAN consisted of two main components: the forward and reverse processes. During training, the forward diffusion process of IP-DDGAN is set up similarly to that of regular diffusion models. In the reverse process, the conditional GAN generator and the time-dependent discriminator are alternately optimized at each step. Both networks were updated once per iteration. Adam was employed as the optimizer, with initial learning rates set at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2\times {10}^{-4}$$\end{document}2×10−4 for the generator and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1\times {10}^{-4}$$\end{document}1×10−4 for the discriminator. The batch size was set to 1. The IP-DDGAN was trained for 25 epochs. The loss component scaling factors \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\lambda }_{1},{\lambda }_{2},{\lambda }_{3}$$\end{document}λ1,λ2,λ3 were set to 0.5,0.5, and 1. The loss weights were selected via a grid search strategy to keep each loss term within a comparable order of magnitude during training. All experiments were conducted on a NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU with 24GB of memory, using FP32 precision. The PyTorch version used for this study is 2.4.0.

Evaluation criteria

For longitudinal MRI synthesis, the subject metadata (age, sex, and diagnosis label) are encoded into latent features by the metadata-guided module. These features, together with the baseline MRI, guide the reverse diffusion process to generate follow-up MRIs from noise. Time-step embeddings ensure that the generated images capture the correct temporal progression, enabling the model to produce longitudinal MRIs consistent with the individual’s disease trajectory.

To rigorously evaluate the quality of synthesized longitudinal MRIs, we analyzed longitudinal data spanning 1 to 6 years to observe the neurodegenerative structural changes. We employed Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) to quantify voxel-wise fidelity. To assess the realism of synthesized longitudinal MRIs, we used MMD to quantify the distance between the generated and real MRI distributions. To investigate the morphology of synthetic MRIs, we assessed the similarity of brain tissue volumes between synthetic and real MRIs using the SPM12 toolbox. We analyzed differences between real and synthetic subpopulations based on ROI-derived volumetric measures. In addition, the synthesized longitudinal MRIs should place greater emphasis on the temporal phenotypic progression between baseline and follow-up scans. Therefore, we trained a Fork-SE-based brain age prediction model to estimate the brain age. To further evaluate subject consistency, we used a pre-trained SqueezeNet model to extract latent representations from real and synthetic MRIs, and then computed intra-subject and inter-subject cosine similarities. Finally, to evaluate the overall quality of the synthetic MRIs, three senior neuroradiologists were asked to classify 100 MRIs as either real or synthetic. They also assessed tissue-boundary realism, ventricular shape plausibility, and longitudinal consistency with baseline MRIs for 40 scans.

Abstract
In the present paper, we investigate constrained transit cosmological models in the most recent proposed modified gravity theory, f ⁡ ( R , L m , T ) f(R,L_{m},T) -gravity. We obtain the modified field equations for a flat homogeneous and isotropic Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) spacetime metric. We constrain the equation of continuity by imposing the equation of state for the perfect fluid source p = − 1 3 ​ ρ + p 0 p=-\frac{1}{3}\rho+p_{0} so that we get energy conservation equation as ρ ˙ + 3 ​ H ​ ( ρ + p ) = 0 \dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=0 , (because generally, energy conservation law is not satisfied in f ⁡ ( R , L m , T ) f(R,L_{m},T) -gravity). Using this constraint, we establish a relation between the energy density parameters Ω m ​ 0 \Omega_{m0} , Ω r ​ 0 \Omega_{r0} , and Ω f ​ 0 \Omega_{f0} and the Hubble function. After that, we made observational constraints on H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) to obtain the best-fit present values of Ω m ​ 0 \Omega_{m0} , Ω r ​ 0 \Omega_{r0} , and H 0 H_{0} . Then, we use these best-fit values of energy parameters to investigate cosmological parameters such as the deceleration parameter, the effective equation of state ω e ​ f ​ f \omega_{eff} , and the energy density parameters Ω m \Omega_{m} , Ω r \Omega_{r} , and Ω f \Omega_{f} to learn more about the components and history of the expanding universe. f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity and field equations

We consider the action for modified
f(R,L_{m},T)
theory in the following form
[

I=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int{f(R,L_{m},T)\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x}+\int{L_{m}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x}

(1)

where
f(R,L_{m},T)
is an arbitrary function of the Ricci scalar,
R
, the trace
T
of the stress-energy momentum tensor of the matter,
T_{ij}
, and of the matter Lagrangian density
L_{m}
. The function
f(R,L_{m},T)
unifies two classes of theories of gravitation with matter-geometry coupling, namely, the
f(R,L_{m})
gravity and
f(R,T)
gravity theories. This theory ensures that the modification is non-trivial for all kinds of matter fields. These are the physical motivations for considering this background in the study of cosmological models. We define the stress-energy momentum tensor of the matter as
[
129
]

T_{ij}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}L_{m})}{\delta{g^{ij}}}

(2)

and its trace by
T=g^{ij}T_{ij}
, respectively. By assuming that the Lagrangian density
L_{m}
of matter depends only on
the metric tensor components
g_{ij}
, and not on its derivatives, we obtain

T_{ij}=g_{ij}L_{m}-2\frac{\partial{L_{m}}}{\partial{g^{ij}}}

(3)

By varying the action
I
of the gravitational field with respect to the metric tensor components
g^{ij}
provides the following relationship

\delta{I}=\int\left[{f_{R}\delta{R}+f_{T}\frac{\delta{T}}{\delta{g^{ij}}}\delta{g^{ij}}+f_{L_{m}}\frac{\delta{L_{m}}}{\delta{g^{ij}}}\delta{g^{ij}}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}f\delta{g^{ij}}}-8\pi T_{ij}\delta{g^{ij}}\right]\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x,

(4)

where we have denoted
f_{R}=\partial{f}/\partial{R}
,
f_{T}=\partial{f}/\partial{T}
and
f_{L_{m}}=\partial{f}/\partial{L_{m}}
, respectively. For the variation of the Ricci scalar, we obtain

\delta{R}=\delta(g^{ij}R_{ij})=R_{ij}\delta{g^{ij}}+g^{ij}(\nabla_{\lambda}\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{ij}-\nabla_{j}\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{i\lambda})

(5)

where
\nabla_{\lambda}
is the covariant derivative with respect to the symmetric connection
\Gamma
associated to the metric
g
. The variation of the Christoffel symbols yields

\delta{\Gamma^{\lambda}_{ij}}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\alpha}(\nabla_{i}\delta{g_{j\alpha}}+\nabla_{j}\delta{g_{\alpha i}}-\nabla_{\alpha}\delta{g_{ij}})

(6)

and the variation of the Ricci scalar provides the expression

\delta{R}=R_{ij}\delta{g^{ij}}+g_{ij}\Box\delta{g^{ij}}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\delta{g^{ij}},

(7)

Therefore, for the variation of the action of the gravitational field, we obtain

\delta{R}=\int\left[f_{R}R_{ij}+(g_{ij}\Box-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j})f_{R}+f_{T}\frac{\delta(g^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta})}{\delta{g^{ij}}}+f_{L_{m}}\frac{\delta{L_{m}}}{\delta{g^{ij}}}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}f-8\pi T_{ij}\right]\delta{g^{ij}}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x

(8)

We define the variation of
T
with respect to the metric tensor as

\frac{\delta(g^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta})}{\delta{g^{ij}}}=T_{ij}+\Theta_{ij},

(9)

where

\Theta_{ij}=g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta{g^{ij}}}=L_{m}g_{ij}-2T_{ij}

(10)

for the perfect-fluid matter source.

Taking
\delta{I}=0
, we obtain the field equations of
f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity model as

f_{R}R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}[f-(f_{L_{m}}+2f_{T})L_{m}]g_{ij}+(g_{ij}\Box-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j})f_{R}=\left[8\pi+\frac{1}{2}(f_{L_{m}}+2f_{T})\right]T_{ij}.

(11)

By contracting Eq. (
11
) gives the following relation between the Ricci scalar
R
and the trace
T
of the stress-energy tensor,

f_{R}R-2[f-(f_{L_{m}}+2f_{T})L_{m}]+3\Box f_{R}=\left[8\pi+\frac{1}{2}(f_{L_{m}}+2f_{T})\right]T,

(12)

where we have denoted
\Theta=\Theta_{i}^{i}
.

The problem of the perfect fluids,
described by an energy density
\rho
, pressure
p
and four-velocity
u^{i}
is more complicated, since there is no unique definition of the matter Lagrangian. However, in the present study we assume that the stress-energy tensor of the matter is given by

T_{ij}=(\rho+p)u_{i}u_{j}+pg_{ij},

(13)

for the flat FLRW homogeneous and isopropic spacetime metric

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2})

(14)

and the matter Lagrangian can be taken as
L_{m}=-\rho
. The four-velocity
u_{i}
satisfies the conditions
u_{i}u^{i}=-1
and
u^{i}\nabla_{j}u_{i}=0

[
128
]
. They showed that generalizing the models by adding a non-flat FLRW universe has important effects on the universe’s overall dynamics, but it needs initial adjustments like whether it is open or closed, which are not needed for a flat FLRW universe.

The energy conservation law can be obtained as

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=\frac{(\rho+L_{m})(\dot{f}_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{f}_{L_{m}})-\frac{1}{2}f_{T}(\dot{T}-2\dot{L}_{m})}{8\pi+f_{T}+\frac{1}{2}f_{L_{m}}}

f(R,L_{m},T)=R-\mu\,T\,L_{m}-\nu

Then

f_{R}=1,~~~~f_{T}=-\mu\,L_{m},~~~~f_{L_{m}}=-\mu\,T,

(17)

By applying (

11

R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}R\,g_{ij}=(8\pi-\frac{\mu}{2}\,T-\mu\,L_{m})T_{ij}+(\mu\,L_{m}^{2}-\frac{\nu}{2})g_{ij}

Now, using (

3H^{2}=8\pi\rho+\frac{\mu}{2}\rho^{2}-\frac{3\mu}{2}\rho p+\frac{\nu}{2}

From Eqs. (
15

16
), we obtain the equation of continuity as follows

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=\frac{-\mu\rho(\dot{\rho}+3\dot{p})}{16\pi+3\mu(\rho-p)}

Without loss of generality, we choose the equation of state (EoS) such that
\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=0
and hence, from the right-hand side, we get
\dot{\rho}+3\dot{p}=0
after integration, which gives the following equation of state:

p=-\frac{1}{3}\rho+p_{0}

(22)

where
p_{0}
is an integrating constant. Using this EoS, solving the equation of continuity, we obtain,

\rho=\rho_{m0}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{2}-\frac{3}{2}p_{0}

(23)

where
\rho_{m0}
is the present value of matter energy density and
a_{0}
is the present value of the scale factor
a(t)
.

Now, using Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
) in (
19
), we obtain the following expression

\Omega_{m}+\Omega_{r}+\Omega_{f}=1

where
\Omega_{m}=\frac{(16\pi-9\mu p_{0})\rho_{m0}}{6H^{2}}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{2}
and
\Omega_{r}=\frac{\mu\rho^{2}_{m0}}{3H^{2}}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{4}

\Omega_{f}=\frac{\nu-24\pi p_{0}}{6H^{2}}
is the dark energy density parameter due to
f(R,L_{m},T)
function. From Eq. (

H(t)=H_{0}\sqrt{\Omega_{m0}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{2}+\Omega_{r0}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a}\right)^{4}+\Omega_{f0}}

(25)

Using the relation
\frac{a_{0}}{a}=1+z

H(z)=H_{0}\sqrt{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}+\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{f0}}

(26)

Now, we can define the effective energy density
\rho_{eff}
and effective pressure
p_{eff}
of the model as follows:

\rho_{eff}=\frac{16\pi\rho+\mu\rho^{2}-3\mu\rho p+\nu}{8\pi},~~~~p_{eff}=\frac{16\pi p+3\mu(\rho-p)p+2\mu\rho^{2}-\nu}{8\pi}

Hence, the effective equation of state parameter

\omega_{eff}=-1+\frac{2\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}+4\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}}{3\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}+3\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}+3\Omega_{f0}}

Now, using the Hubble function (

q(z)=-1+\frac{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}+2\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}}{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}+\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{f0}}

The matter energy density parameter
\Omega_{m}

\Omega_{r}
and dark energy density parameter
\Omega_{f}

\Omega_{m}=\frac{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}}{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}+\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{f0}}

\Omega_{r}=\frac{\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}}{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}+\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{f0}}

(31)

\Omega_{f}=\frac{\Omega_{f0}}{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^{2}+\Omega_{r0}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{f0}}

4.2
Apparent Magnitude
m(z)

The correlation between luminosity distance and redshift is a fundamental observational method employed to monitor the progression of the cosmos. When determining the luminosity distance (
D_{L}
) in relation to the cosmic redshift (
z
), the expansion of the universe and the redshift of light from distant bright objects are considered. It is given as

D_{L}=a_{0}r(1+z),

(33)

where the radial coordinate of the source
r
, is established by

r=\int^{r}_{0}dr=\int^{t}_{0}\frac{cdt}{a(t)}=\frac{1}{a_{0}}\int^{z}_{0}\frac{cdz^{\prime}}{H(z^{\prime})},

(34)

where we have used
dt=dz/\dot{z},\dot{z}=-H(1+z)
.

Consequently, the following formula determines the luminosity distance:

D_{L}=c(1+z)\int^{z}_{0}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{H(z^{\prime})}.

(35)

Supernovae (SNe) are commonly employed by researchers as standard candles to investigate the pace of cosmic expansion using the reported apparent magnitude (
m_{o}
). The surveys on supernovae, which discovered many types of supernovae of varying magnitudes, resulted in the compilation of the Pantheon sample SNe dataset. This dataset has
1048
data points within the range of
0.01
to
2.26
in terms of redshift (
z
). The theoretical apparent magnitude (
m
) of these standard candles is precisely defined as
[
133
]
.

m(z)=M+5~\log_{10}\left(\frac{D_{L}}{Mpc}\right)+25.

(36)

where
M
represents the absolute magnitude. The luminosity distance is quantified in units of distance. The Hubble-free luminosity distance (
d_{L}
) can be expressed as
d_{L}\equiv\frac{H_{0}}{c}D_{L}
, where
D_{L}
is a dimensionless quantity based on
D_{L}
. Therefore, we can express
m(z)
in a simplified form as shown below

m(z)=M+5\log_{10}{d_{L}}+5\log_{10}\left(\frac{c/H_{0}}{Mpc}\right)+25.

(37)

The equation above allows for the observation of the degeneracy between
M
and
H_{0}
, which remains constant in the
\Lambda
CDM background
[
133
,
134
]
. By redefining, we can combine these deteriorated parameters.

\mathcal{M}\equiv M+5\log_{10}\left(\frac{c/H_{0}}{Mpc}\right)+25.

(38)

The dimensionless parameter

is defined as

, where

is the value of

This study investigated restricted transit cosmological models within the latest suggested modified gravity theory,

-gravity. The modified field equations for a flat, homogeneous, and isotropic Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) spacetime metric are derived. The equation of continuity is restricted by enforcing the equation of state for the perfect fluid source, where
p=-\frac{1}{3}\rho+p_{0}
. This allows us to obtain the expression
\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=0
. The violation of energy conservation is a common occurrence in

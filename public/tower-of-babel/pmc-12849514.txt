MicroRNA-221-3p targets ESR1 and downregulates SLC7A11 to exacerbate hypoxia/reoxygenation-induced cardiomyocyte injury
Authors: Jian Ye, Haijiang Xia, Meizhi Liu, Linbin Yu, Longbin Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundmicroRNA-221-3p is highly expressed after myocardial infarction and can target regulate ESR1 expression. This study aims to investigate the effects of MicroRNA-221-3p/ESR1 on hypoxia/reoxygenation(H/R)-induced cardiomyocyte injury.Methodsestablishment of H/R H9C2 cell model; normal cultured H9C2 cells were designated as the control group. Different treatment methods were applied to H9C2 cells, including miRNA negative control (miR-con), miR-221-3p mimic, miR-221-3p inhibitor, overexpression empty vector (pcDNA-con), and ESR1 overexpression vector (pcDNA-ESR1). The effects of miR-221-3p on cell viability, ROS levels, and the expression of glutathione, 4-hydroxynonenal(4-HNE), Estrogen receptor 1(ESR1), malondialdehyde (MDA) and solute carrier family 7 member 11(SLC7A11) were examined. Mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed using JC-1 staining.ResultsmiR-221-3p is highly expressed in H/R H9C2 cells, and the miR-221-3p mimics further leads to a decrease in cell activity, a decrease in glutathione levels, an increase in 4-HNE, MDA and ROS, and a decrease in the expression of Mitochondrial membrane potential, ESR1 and SLC7A11 genes. Background

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is a cardiovascular condition characterized by the abrupt occlusion of the coronary artery, leading to ischemic necrosis of the myocardium, with a high mortality and morbidity rate. Myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI) presents a significant clinical challenge that can be mitigated through preconditioning or pharmacological interventions; however, many patients continue to experience suboptimal outcomes [1]. The underlying mechanisms of MIRI remain incompletely understood, necessitating further elucidation of its pathogenesis to facilitate the identification of potential therapeutic agents.

MicroRNA (miRNA) is a non-coding RNA molecule that is widely present in eukaryotic organisms, with a length of approximately 18 to 25 nucleotides. miRNAs have profound clinical implications for the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of various cardiovascular diseases [2]. Notably, miR-221-3p exhibits elevated expression levels in the peripheral blood of patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI), indicating its potential as a biomarker for AMI [3] and suggesting that it may play a pivotal role in this condition. Moreover, the overexpression of miR-221-3p has been shown to mediate myocardial injury by targeting several factors including P57, HIF-1alpha, and Sirt2 [4–6]. However, there remains a relative paucity of reports addressing the involvement of miR-221-3p in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI).

ESR1 encodes the estrogen receptor alpha (ERα), which is the main receptor for estrogen and can regulate various biological functions. In cardiovascular diseases, studies have shown that ESR1 has a protective effect. Compared to men, women often develop atherosclerosis at a later age, and hormonal differences are thought to be a possible mechanism for this difference [7]. Multiple studies suggest that ESR1 has a protective effect against heart failure, myocardial infarction, and other diseases [8–10]. ESR1 is a target of miR-221-3p, and thus affects cell proliferation and apoptosis [11–14]. Previous studies show that ESR1 binds the SLC7A11 promoter and upregulates its protein expression, inhibiting ferroptosis [15]. We hypothesize that miR-221-3p targets ESR1 to downregulate SLC7A11 and contribute to MIRI, validated in a hypoxia/reoxygenation(H/R) model of myocardial cells.

Materials and methods

H9C2 cell culture

H9C2 cells were purchased from the Institute of Cell Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. All cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium(Beyotime, C0893) containing 10% FBS(Beyotime, C0226), 100 µg/ml penicillin, and 100 µg/ml streptomycin at 37 °C and 5% CO2.

H/R model

First, the H9C2 cell medium was replaced with FBS free and sugar-free medium and cultured at 37 ℃, 5% CO2, 5% H2, and 90% N2 for 12 h, and then the whole medium was cultured at 37 ℃, 5% CO2 for 12 h to simulate H/R.

Cell transfection

Cells were inoculated into 6-well plates in advance. Transfection experiment was performed when the cell fusion degree was about 70%. During transfection, 3 µg rno-miR-221-3p mimics(Shanghai Jima Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd, B02001) or rno-miR-221-3p inhibitor(Shanghai Jima Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd, B03001) or miR-con or pcDNA(Shanghai Jima Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd, C05008) was dissolved in 250µL Opti-MEM. 2.5µL Lipofectamine 3000 was added to 250µL Opti-MEM, After 5 min of standing, mixed in equal volume and left at room temperature for 15 min, then the mixture was added to each well. Change to normal culture medium 6 h later, and proceed with the next experiment 48 h later.

Cell viability assay

H9C2 cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁴ cells per well. Following the experimental intervention,, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution(Beyotime, C0042) was added to each well and incubated for 4 h at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader. Cell viability, expressed as the relative cell survival (proliferation) rate, was calculated according to the following formula: (mean OD₄₅₀ of the treatment group/mean OD₄₅₀ of the control group) × 100%.

Enzyme-Linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Collect cells, dilute them in PBS to a concentration of 1 million cells/ml, freeze and thaw repeatedly to break the cells, centrifuge at 2000 rpm for 20 min, collect the supernatant, and then use the glutathione(GSH) ELISA Kit(Wuhan GeneMei Biotechnology Co., Ltd., JYM1012Ra) and 4-hydroxynonenal(4-HNE) ELISA Kit(Wuhan GeneMei Biotechnology Co., Ltd., JYM1176Ra) to detect the levels of GSH and 4-HNE, respectively.

qRT-PCR

Collect cells, extract total RNA using the Trizol reagent kit(Ambion Biotechnology Co., Ltd, 15596-026), A total of 1000 ng of RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the cDNA Synthesis Kit (Beyotime, D7182), following the manufacturer’s recommended protocol, The real-time PCR assay was conducted using the BeyoFast SYBR Green qPCR Mix(Beyotime, D7260). miRNA was detected using the miDETECT A Track miRNA qRT-PCR Primer Kit (RiboBio, Cat. No. C10712) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Gene expression levels were quantified using the 2^−ΔΔCT method.The expression levels of mRNA and miRNA were assessed using GAPDH and U6 as internal reference genes, respectively. The primer sequences are as follows: miR-221‐3p‐Forward: GGGAGCTACATTGTCTGCTG; miR-221-3p-Reverse: TCCTCCTCTCCTTCCTTCTC; ESR1-Forward: TCCCCAACACCATCTGAGAACT; ESR1-Reverse: CGTTTCAGGGATTCGCAGAA.

Western blotting

Each group of cells was seeded at a density of 1 × 105 cells/well in a 6-well plate and transfected for 48 h. The cells were collected, placed on ice for 30 min, and centrifuged at 14,000×g for 15 min. The protein concentration was determined using the BCA method. Based on the BCA(Shanghai EpiZyme Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd., ZJ102) detection results, the total volume and concentration of the boiled sample were calculated. The diluted sample and 5× protein loading buffer were added, briefly centrifuged, and then boiled at 100 °C in a metal bath for 5 min. Wait until the sample is cooled, Proceed with the next step or store in the − 20 ℃ refrigerator. After electrophoresis, the membrane was transferred to PVDF membrane and blocked with 5% BSA blocking solution for 1 h. Primary antibodies to ESR1 (Sangon. #BBI-D222310-0025, 1:1000), SLC7A11(ABclonal. #A2413, 1:1000), and GAPDH (ABclonal. A19056, 1:1000) were added, shaken overnight at 4℃. Shaken the PVDF membrane with TBST wash buffer 3 times for 10 min each. Add Secondary antibody (ABclonal, AS003, 1:0000) shaker at room temperature for 1 h, and then TBST was washed again. Add ECL(Shanghai EpiZyme Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd., SQ203) chemiluminescence reagent, then analyze it using a gel imaging system.

Detection of reactive oxygen species (ROS)

Measure the level of active oxygen using an active oxygen detection kit (Biyuntian, S0033S). Wash the cells once with PBS (phosphate buffered saline, Gibco, 10010-023) at 37℃ and incubate them with 10 µM DCHF-DA for 20 min. Wash the cells with PBS three times and detect the fluorescence intensity using a fluorescence plate reader.

Detection of malondialdehyde (MDA)

The cells were harvested and diluted to a concentration of 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL in PBS. Subsequently, the cells underwent three to five freeze-thaw cycles to ensure effective cell lysis, followed by centrifugation at 2,000 rpm for 20 min. The supernatant was collected, and MDA concentration was determined according to the manufacturer’s instructions provided with the MDA assay kit(Beyotime, S0131S).

Mitochondrial membrane potential

Mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed using a JC-1 assay kit(APExBIO, K2002). Briefly, cells were washed 2–3 times with PBS, followed by incubation with pre-warmed JC-1 working solution for 20 min. After incubation, cells were washed again 2–3 times with PBS to remove residual reagent. Fresh culture medium was then added to maintain cell viability, and the cells were subsequently observed under a fluorescence microscope.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The wild-type (WT) ESR1 3’UTR luciferase reporter plasmid pGL3-ESR1 and the mutant (MUT) version with point mutations in the predicted miR-221-3p binding site were constructed. These plasmids, along with the Renilla luciferase and miR-221-3p, were co-transfected into H9C2 cells. After 48 h, luciferase activity was measured using the dual-luciferase reporter assay system (YEASEN, 11402ES60). Firefly luciferase signals were normalized to Renilla luciferase activity.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 24.0 statistical software. Quantitative data were expressed as means ± standard deviations (±s). Comparison between multiple samples was performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). T-test was used for paired comparisons that met the normal distribution. LSD test was used for pairwise comparisons when variances were equal, while Games-Howell test was used when variances were unequal. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

MiR-221-3p exacerbates H/R-induced injury in H9C2 cells

To investigate the impact of miR-221-3p on the survival rate of H/R-treated H9C2 cells (Fig. 1A), we conducted interventions using mimics and inhibitors on these cells. Compared to the control group, the survival rate in the model group was significantly diminished, with further reductions observed following mimics intervention. Conversely, treatment with the miR-221-3p inhibitor significantly improved cell viability. Following H/R treatment, levels of ROS, MDA and 4-HNE were elevated in model group cells, while glutathione levels and mitochondrial membrane potential were decreased. After mimic intervention, ROS, MDA and 4-HNE expression increased further alongside a reduction in glutathione levels; however, inhibitors partially mitigated ROS, MDA and 4-HNE expression in H/R-treated H9C2 cells and restored glutathione levels and mitochondrial membrane potential (Fig. 1B-F).

miR-221-3p targets ESR1 and downregulates SLC7A11 protein expression

Previous studies have shown that miR-221-3p can target ESR1 and affect cell proliferation and apoptosis [11–13], so we examined the effect of miR-221-3p on ESR1 expression.Vectors containing either MUT or WT ESR1 were constructed and used in luciferase reporter assays. As shown in the figure, luciferase activity was significantly reduced in the WT group upon miR-221-3p mimics transfection compared to the MUT group (Fig. 2B), And it can reduce the expression level of WT ESR1 protein (Fig. 2C). H/R increased miR-221-3p expression in H9C2 cells (Fig. 3A), and decreased both ESR1 mRNA and protein expression. Inhibitors reduced miR-221-3p levels in H/R H9C2 cells and increased ESR1 mRNA and protein levels. Mimics further increased miR-221-3p expression in H/R H9C2 cells and decreased ESR1 mRNA and protein levels (Fig. 3B, C). ESR1 can bind to the promoter of SLC7A11 and activate the expression of SLC7A11 protein to inhibit ferroptosis [15], so we also detected the expression level of SLC7A11. H/R reduced SLC7A11 protein expression in H9C2 cells, and an inhibitor increased SLC7A11 protein levels in H9C2 cells, but mimics intervention did not further reduce it (Fig. 3C).

Overexpression of ESR1 alleviates the damage induced by miR-221-3p in H/R H9C2 cells

Previous studies have found that ESR1 protects cardiomyocytes from H/R injury [16, 17], so we further confirmed whether miR-221-3p could reverse the protective effect of ESR1. The results suggest that overexpression of ESR1 can improve the survival rate of H/R H9C2 cells, increase glutathione levels, and reduce 4-HNE and ROS. When combined with inhibitors of miR-221-3p, the effect is even more significant, but miR-221-3p mimics reversed this protective effect (Fig. 4A-D). Overexpression of ESR1 can also induce the expression of SLC7A11 protein to rise, and combined with inhibitors, it can further increase the level of ESR1 and SLC7A11 protein, but there is no statistically significant difference. miR-221-3p mimics downregulated the levels of ESR1 and SLC7A11 proteins simultaneously (Fig. 4E). We also examined the effects of ESR1 overexpression on mitochondrial membrane potential and MDA levels in H/R H9C2 cells. The results indicated that ESR1 overexpression increased mitochondrial membrane potential and reduced MDA levels in H/R-treated H9C2 cells (Fig. 5). However, these effects were reversed upon introduction of miR-221-3p mimics.

Discussion

Myocardial infarction (MI) remains one of the leading causes of death among global residents, and with the widespread use of thrombolysis and interventional therapy, most patients can now receive better treatment. However, after the occluded vessel is reopened, MIRI is another challenge that needs to be addressed. Although the exact mechanism of MIRI is not fully understood, it is generally believed that calcium ion accumulation, lipid oxidation, and ROS are the key factors in causing MIRI [18, 19]. miRNA, as a type of non-coding RNA, have garnered significant research interest in the context of MIRI. In this study, we investigated the role of miR-221-3p in H/R H9C2 cells and found that it can target downregulation of ESR1 expression and subsequently affect the regulation of SLC7A11 expression to induce cardiomyocyte injury.

MiR-221-3p, a member of the miR-221 family, has diverse functions such as regulating tumor growth [20, 21], enhancing neuronal apoptosis [22, 23]. With the cardiovascular domain, miR-221-3p is associated with heart failure and atherosclerosis et al. [5, 25]. Heart failure is a major problem faced by most AMI patients, and the expression of miR-221-3p in the peripheral blood of AMI patients is abundant and has a negative impact on prognosis [3, 26], this suggesting that miR-221-3p may be one of the key factors in the progression of heart failure. Verjans R et al. [27] found that miR-221-3p promotes cardiac fibrosis by regulating the TGF-β signaling pathway. Yuying Li et al. [5] found that miR-221-3p targets Hif-1α to inhibit angiogenesis and promote heart failure progression. In MIRI, miR-221-3p is highly expressed and targets p57 to exacerbate oxidative stress injury to the heart [4]. In our study, miR-221-3p was also highly expressed in H/R H9C2 cells, and inhibiting miR-221-3p lowered ROS levels, increased glutathione content, and improved cell survival.

ESR1 is one of the primary receptors for estrogen and is located in both the nucleus and cytoplasm of cardiomyocytes. It plays an important role in regulating cell proliferation and apoptosis. In the context of tumor diseases, ESR1 is closely associated with breast cancer and prostate cancer, and is an important factor in maintaining the survival and resistance of tumor cells. Its high expression often indicates poor prognosis for patients [28]. However, recent research indicates that ESR1 has a protective role in myocardial infarction and heart failure. Increasing ESR1 expression can inhibit cardiac remodeling and improve heart failure [8], Sara Menazza et al. [29] used estrogen to activate ESR1 in ovariectomy MIRI mice, which can reduce the area of myocardial infarction and improve cardiac function. In addition, activating ESR1 also has a protective effect on H/R cerebral endothelial cells, and the mechanism is related to improving cell permeability [30]. Previous studies have suggested that miR-21-3p can target ESR1 [11–13], and in this study, we again observed its effect on ESR1. The results suggest that ESR1 expression is downregulated in H/R H9C2 cells, which is similar to previous research results [10, 29, 31]. Overexpression of miR-221-3p significantly downregulated ESR1 expression level in H/R H9C2 cells, while the miR-221-3p inhibitor restored ESR1 levels. In summary, our results suggest that miR-221-3p targets downregulation of ESR1 expression in H/R H9C2 cells. Meijing Wang et al. [10] found that the cardioprotective effect of ESR1 in MIRI is related to the improvement of mitochondrial connexin 43 content and the protection of mitochondrial function. We also observed a decrease in the mitochondrial membrane potential in H/R-treated H9C2 cells. Both the miR-221-3p inhibitor and ESR1 overexpression were able to restore the mitochondrial membrane potential, indicating that miR-221-3p may impair mitochondrial function in H/R-stimulated H9C2 cells, this is similar to previous research [10, 32].

Iron-dependent cell death, also known as ferroptosis, is a distinct form of programmed cell death characterized by iron accumulation and lipid peroxidation, which is different from cell apoptosis and necrosis [33]. SLC7A11 is a key molecule in ferroptosis [34]. SLC7A11 encodes the cysteine/glutamate exchanger system XC-(xCT), and inhibiting SLC7A11 (xCT) can reduce the entry of extracellular cysteine into the cytosol and reduce the synthesis of glutathione. GPX4 is the only substance in the cell that can reduce lipid peroxides, and glutathione is the necessary auxiliary substance for GPX4 to reduce lipid peroxides [35]. Previous studies suggest that ESR1 can bind to the promoter of SLC7A11 and activate its protein expression to inhibit ferroptosis [15], therefore we further detected the expression of SLC7A11. Similar to the expression of ESR1, SLC7A11 protein expression decreased in H/R H9C2 cells, and adding miR-221-3p mimics could further lower SLC7A11 protein expression. The expression of SLC7A11 was restored by the miR-221-3p inhibitor. Overexpression of ESR1 can increase the protein level of SLC7A11 in H/R H9C2 cells; however, the addition of miR-221-3p mimetics or inhibitors does not further affect SLC7A11 expression. 4-HNE and MDA are lipid peroxide that can be considered one of the markers of ferroptosis [36]. In this study, the expression level of 4-HNE and MDA was consistent with that of SLC7A11, which suggests that miR-221-3p may participate in ferroptosis through the ESR1/SLC7A11 signaling pathway.

Conclusion

In summary, H/R H9C2 cells express miR-221-3p abundantly, and miR-221-3p aggravates H/R damage, which is mediated by targeting ESR1 to downregulate SLC7A11 protein. Furthermore, miR-221-3p may participate in ferroptosis via the ESR1/SLC7A11 pathway. Our study suggests that miR-221-3p is a potential target for treating MIRI. The present study has a limitation in that it remains undetermined whether miR-221-3p affects SLC7A11 through other targets, and this hypothesis has yet to be further validated through in vivo animal experiments.

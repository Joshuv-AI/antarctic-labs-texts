A characterization of adequate Turaev genus one links
Khaled Qazaqzeh, Nafaa Chbili, Adam M. Lowrance

Abstract
. We prove that a link is adequate and has Turaev genus one if and only if the span of its Jones polynomial is one less than its crossing number.



1.
Introduction

Following Jones’ celebrated discovery of the Jones polynomial
[
Jon87
]
, Kauffman
[
Kau87
]
, Murasugi
[
Mur87
]
, and Thistlethwaite
[
Thi87
]
independently proved that a reduced alternating diagram of a link
L
has the fewest number of crossings among all diagrams of
L
. Let
V_{L}(t)
be the Jones polynomial of
L
, and let
c(L)
be the crossing number of
L
. A key element in each of their proofs is the inequality
\operatorname{span}V_{L}(t)\leq c(L)
, where equality holds if and only if
L
is alternating.

Each crossing
of a link diagram
D
has an
A
-resolution
and a
B
-resolution
. A
Kauffman state
of
D
is the collection of simple closed curves in the plane resulting from a choice of an
A
-resolution or
B
-resolution at each crossing. The all-
A
state
\sigma_{A}(D)
is the state obtained by choosing an
A
-resolution for every crossing of
D
. Similarly, the all-
B
state
\sigma_{B}(D)
is the state where every resolution is a
B
-resolution. A link diagram
D
is
A
-adequate
if no two arcs in the
A
-resolution of any crossing are contained in the same component of the all-
A
state
\sigma_{A}(D)
, and similarly,
D
is
B
-adequate
if no two arcs in the
B
-resolution of any crossing are contained in the same component of the all-
B
state
\sigma_{B}(D)
. Lickorish and Thistlethwaite
[
LT88
]
define a link to be
adequate
if it has a diagram
D
that is both
A
-adequate and
B
-adequate.

The Turaev surface of a link diagram
D
is a closed, oriented surface in
S^{3}
of genus

(1)

g_{T}(D)=\frac{1}{2}(c(D)+2-|\sigma_{A}(D)|-|\sigma_{B}(D)|),

where
c(D)
is the number of crossings in
D
, and
|\sigma_{A}(D)|
and
|\sigma_{B}(D)|
are the number of components in the all-
A
and all-
B
states of
D
. See Section
2
for a detailed construction of the Turaev surface. The
Turaev genus

g_{T}(L)
of a non-split link
L
is defined as

g_{T}(L)=\min\{g_{T}(D)~|~D~\text{is a diagram of}~L\}.

Turaev
[
Tur87
]
used the surface to give an alternate proof of Kauffman, Murasugi, and Thistlethwaite’s theorem on the crossing number of alternating knots. Bae and Morton
[
BM03
]
and Dasbach et al.
[
DFK+08
]
proved that

(2)

\operatorname{span}V_{L}(t)\leq c(L)-g_{T}(L),

where equality holds if
L
is adequate according to the results in
[
Abe09
]
and
[
Thi88b
]
.

Our main theorem uses the Jones polynomial to give a characterization of non-split adequate links with Turaev genus one.

Theorem 1.1
.

A non-split link
L
in
S^{3}
is adequate and has Turaev genus one if and only if

\operatorname{span}V_{L}(t)=c(L)-1.

The forward implication of Theorem
1.1
follows from the above discussion. We give two distinct proofs of the backward implication in Sections
3
and
4
, the first using the two variable Kauffman polynomial characterization of adequate links and the second using Bae and Morton’s method for computing extreme coefficients of the Jones polynomial
[
BM03
]
and a diagrammatic characterization of Turaev genus one links. We hope that one of our proofs may inspire an approach to the following conjecture, which proposes an extension of Theorem
1.1
to
links of arbitrary Turaev genus.

Conjecture 1.2
.

A non-split link is adequate if and only if
\operatorname{span}V_{L}(t)=c(L)-g_{T}(L)
.

The first and second authors
[
QC24
]
attempted to resolve Conjecture
1.2
, but there is a gap in their proof. An affirmative answer would give a characterization of an adequate link in terms of its Turaev genus and Jones polynomial. Other characterizations of adequate links include the following. Thistlethwaite
[
Thi88b
]
characterized adequate links in terms of the 2-variable Kauffman polynomial. Kalfagianni
[
Kal18
]
and Kalfagianni and Lee
[
KL23
]
gave related characterizations of adequate links in terms of the colored Jones polynomial. We hope to address Conjecture
1.2
in a future work.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
gives background information on the Jones polynomial, adequate links, and the Turaev surface. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
using the two-variable Kauffman polynomial. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.1
using a diagrammatic characterization of Turaev genus one links. Finally, in Section
5
, we state and prove some corollaries to Theorem
1.1
.

2.2.
Adequate knots and links

Since every reduced alternating diagram is adequate, the set of alternating links is a subset of the set of adequate links. Investigations into adequate links often compare and contrast their properties to the analogous properties of alternating links. Lickorish and Thistlethwaite
[
LT88
]
defined adequate knots and links and proved every nontrivial adequate link has nontrivial Jones polynomial. Thistlethwaite
[
Thi88b
]
used the Kauffman polynomial to prove that adequate diagrams minimize crossing number, and moreover that every minimum crossing diagram of an adequate link is adequate. Abe
[
Abe09
]
used the Khovanov homology of a link to show that every adequate diagram minimizes the Turaev genus of a link.

Adequate links and the related class of semi-adequate links have been studied extensively in the literature. The Jones and colored Jones polynomial of adequate and semi-adequate links were studied in
[
LPS01
,
DL06
,
JRS10
,
Sto11
,
Sto14
,
KL14
,
AD17
,
Kal18
]
. Applications to the geometry and topology of surfaces in the link complement were studied in
[
FKP11
,
Oza11
,
FKP13
,
BMPW15
,
KL18
]
. The Khovanov homology of adequate and semi-adequate links were studied in
[
Kho03
,
AP04
,
PS14
,
Lee18
,
PS20
]
.

3.
First proof of the main theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
using the two-variable Kauffman polynomial characterization of adequate links. We begin with the definition and results about the Kauffman polynomial. Kauffman
[
Kau90
]
defined a two-variable Laurent polynomial

\alpha(L)
of the link

is the minimum number of half-planes in any arc presentation of

. Combining several results about the arc index of a link yields the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
D
be a diagram of a non-alternating link. Then
\operatorname{span}_{a}\Lambda_{D}(a,z)\leq c(D)-2
.

Proof.

Morton and Beltrami
[
MB98
]
proved that
\operatorname{span}_{a}\Lambda_{D}(a,z)\leq\alpha(L)-2
where
\alpha(L)
denotes the arc index of the link

[

]
proved that if

is a minimum crossing diagram of a prime non-alternating link, then

. Combining these two inequalities gives the result for prime diagrams. For composite diagrams, the result follows from the additivity of the span of the Kauffman polynomial.
∎

Effect of additional regularity for the initial data on semi-linear σ \sigma -evolution equations with different damping types Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Dinh Van Duong (vanmath2002@gmail.com)


Abstract
. In this paper, we would like to study the critical exponent for semi-linear σ \sigma -evolution equations with different damping types under the influence of additional regularity for the initial data. On the one hand, we establish the existence of global (in time) solutions for small initial data and the blow-up in finite time solutions in the supercritical case and the subcritical case, respectively. The very interesting phenomenon is that the critical case belonging to the global solution range or the blow-up solution range depends heavily on the assumption of additional regularity for the initial data. Furthermore, we are going to provide lifespan estimates for solutions when the blow-up phenomenon occurs.

Introduction

Let us consider the following Cauchy problem for semi-linear
\sigma
-evolution equations with different damping types:

(1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}u_{t}=|u|^{p},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
u(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{1}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon
is a positive real number,
\sigma\geq 1
is assumed to be any fractional number and
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma/2<\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma
. The parameter
p>1
represents the exponent of the power nonlinear term. The homogeneous Cauchy problem corresponding to the problem (
1
) we have in mind is

(2)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}u_{t}=0,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
u(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{1}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

The term
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}
is referred to the
\sigma
-th power of the Laplace operator and the parameter
\sigma
may be non-integer. In the non-integer case, it is determined by

(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\psi(x)=\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{\xi\to x}\big(|\xi|^{2\sigma}\mathfrak{F}_{x\to\xi}(\psi)(\xi)\big)(x),

where
\psi\in H^{2\sigma}
and
\mathfrak{F}
,
\mathfrak{F}^{-1}
denotes the Fourier transform, the inverse Fourier transform, respectively. The term
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u
, with
\sigma\geq 1
, is known as a
\sigma
-evolution wave or a higher-order wave. The term
(-\Delta)^{\theta}u_{t}
with
\theta\geq 0
is called a damping term. More precisely, it is often referred to as the frictional (or external) damping, the parabolic like damping, the
\sigma
-evolution like damping and the visco-elastic (or strong) damping when
\theta=0
,
\theta=\sigma_{1}\in(0,\sigma/2)
,
\theta=\sigma_{2}\in(\sigma/2,\sigma)
and
\theta=\sigma
, respectively.

Let us review some historical perspectives on the study of the following Cauchy problem for damped
\sigma
-evolution equations:

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\theta}u_{t}=\mu|u|^{p},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
\sigma\geq 1
,
\theta\in[0,\sigma]
and
\mu\geq 0
. Matsumura
[
20
]
was the first to establish the basic decay estimates for the problem (
3
) with
(\sigma,\theta,\mu)=(1,0,0)
, also known as the damped wave equation. Subsequently, it has been established that the damped wave equation has a diffusive structure as
t\to\infty
. In recent years, many mathematicians have focused on studying a typical nonlinear problem of the semi-linear damped wave equation, which is the problem (
3
) with
(\sigma,\theta,\mu)=(1,0,1)
, for example,
[
22
,
16
,
17
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. The paper
[
22
]
identified that the critical exponent of this problem for small initial data

(u_{0},u_{1})\in(H^{1}\cap L^{1})\times(L^{2}\cap L^{1})

is
p_{\rm Fuj}=1+2/n
, also known as the Fujita critical exponent. Here, the critical exponent
p_{\rm crit}
is understood as the threshold between global (in time) existence of small data weak solution and blow-up of solutions even for small data. Expanding the data space

(H^{1}\cap L^{m})\times(L^{2}\cap L^{m})

for
m\in[1,2]
, the authors of the cited papers proved the global existence with small data when
p>1+2m/n
, blow-up solution when
p\leq 1+2/n
if
m=1
and
p<1+2m/n
if
m\in(1,2]
. The investigation of the solution properties of the problem when the exponent of the nonlinear term
|u|^{p}
takes the critical values is also of interest. The paper
[
21
]
studied the case
m=2
and they prove the global well-posedness with small data when
p\geq 1+4/n
. The paper
[
16
]
extended this result by proving the existence of global mild solutions when
p\geq 1+2m/n
along with some other conditions and the parameter
m
satisfies

\displaystyle m\in\left(\max\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt{n^{2}+16n}-n}{4}\right\},2\right].

For the generalization of the problem (
3
), we have a series of papers
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
11
,
8
,
9
,
10
,

]
. The papers
[

obtained

estimates for the solution to (

) by using the theory of modified Bessel functions combined with Faà di Bruno’s formula. However, this approach is only suitable for “parabolic like model” corresponding to

and “
\sigma
-evolution-like model” corresponding to

-evolution equations, that is,

p_{\rm crit}(m,\sigma,\theta):=1+\dfrac{2m\sigma}{n-m\min\{2\theta,\sigma\}}

by proving the global existence of solutions when
p>p_{\rm crit}(m,\sigma,\theta)
under some additional conditions, and the existence of blow-up solutions when
p<p_{\rm crit}(m,\sigma,\theta)
with small initial data in an appropriate space. Speaking about the value
p=p_{\rm crit}(m,\sigma,\theta)
, we can say that it has only been proved to belong to the blow-up range when
m=1
, meanwhile, it still remains an open problem for any

Turning to our main problem (

\sigma=1

\sigma_{2}=1

1

p>1+\dfrac{2m\sigma}{n-2m\sigma_{1}}

with
m\in[1,2)
. Moreover, the interaction between “parabolic-like models” and “
\sigma

1

is exactly the critical exponent for the problem (
1

|u|^{p}\in L^{m_{1}}

Main results

Let us state the local and global (in time) existence of small data solutions, along with the blow-up results, which will be proved in this paper.

Theorem 1.1

(
Local existence
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma/2<\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma
. We assume that the parameter
m
satisfies

(4)

\displaystyle 1<m\begin{cases}\leq 2&\text{ if }\sigma_{1}=0,\\
<\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n+4\sigma_{1}}&\text{ if }\sigma_{1}>0,\end{cases}

and the exponent

satisfies the following conditions:

(5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2}{m}<p\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt<\infty&\text{ if }n\leq 2\sigma,\\
\leq\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-2\sigma}&\text{ if }2\sigma<n<\displaystyle\frac{4\sigma}{2-m}.\end{cases}

Moreover, if the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{m}
, then there exists
T>0
and a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, the Cauchy problem (
1
) admits a unique local (in time) solution belonging to the class

u\in\mathcal{C}\left([0,T),H^{\sigma}\right).

Theorem 1.2

(
Global existence
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma/2<\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma
. The parameter
m
satisfies

(6)

\max\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt{(n+4\sigma_{1})^{2}+16n(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}-n-4\sigma_{1}}{4(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}\right\}<m\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt\leq 2&\text{ if }\sigma_{1}=0,\\
<\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n+4\sigma_{1}}&\text{ if }\sigma_{1}>0.\end{cases}

Moreover, we assume the following conditions:

(7)

\displaystyle 1+\displaystyle\frac{2m\sigma}{n-2m\sigma_{1}}\leq p\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt<\infty&\text{ if }n\leq 2\sigma\\
\leq\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-2\sigma}&\text{ if }2\sigma<n<\displaystyle\frac{4\sigma}{2-m}\end{cases}

and the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{m}

such that for any

Furthermore, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{m}},

(

Proofs of local and global (in time) existence

2.1.
Philosophy of our approach

In this section, let us focus on proving Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. First, we can write the solution to
(\ref{Main.Eq.2})
by the formula

u(t,x)=\varepsilon K_{0}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)+\varepsilon K_{1}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x),

so that the solution to

can be expressed by

u(t,x)=\varepsilon K_{0}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)+\varepsilon K_{1}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|u(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau,

thanks to Duhamel’s principle.
Here

\widehat{K_{0}}(t,\xi)=\mathfrak{F}_{x\to\xi}(K_{0}(t,x))=\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{2}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{1}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}

and

\widehat{K_{1}}(t,\xi)=\mathfrak{F}_{x\to\xi}(K_{1}(t,x))=\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}.

The characteristic roots
\lambda_{1,2}=\lambda_{1,2}(|\xi|)
are given by

\lambda_{1,2}(|\xi|)=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\pm\sqrt{(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}})^{2}-4|\xi|^{2\sigma}}\right)&\text{ if }|\xi|\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\pm i\sqrt{4|\xi|^{2\sigma}-(|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}+|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}})^{2}}\right)&\text{ if }|\xi|\in\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega=\left\{r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:r^{2\sigma_{1}}+r^{2\sigma_{2}}<2r^{\sigma}\right\}
. Because of
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma/2<\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma
, there exists a sufficiently small constant
\varepsilon^{*}>0
such that

(0,\varepsilon^{*})\cup\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{*}},\infty\right)\subset\Omega.

Then, taking account of the cases of small and large frequencies separately we conclude that

(10)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\sim-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})},\quad\lambda_{2}\sim-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}},\quad\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\sim|\xi|^{2\sigma_{1}}\quad\text{ for }|\xi|\leq\varepsilon^{*},

(11)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\sim-|\xi|^{2(\sigma-\sigma_{2})},\quad\lambda_{2}\sim-|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}},\quad\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\sim|\xi|^{2\sigma_{2}}\quad\text{ for }|\xi|\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{*}}.

. Next, let

\displaystyle p\geq 1+\frac{2m\sigma}{n-2m\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\text{ implies }1-\frac{n}{2m(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(p-1)+\frac{p\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}\leq 0.

Next, the term

is controlled by

\displaystyle J_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{(p-1)(-\frac{n}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{q(p-1)})+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}})}\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}d\tau,

provided that it holds

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{q(p-1)}\right)+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(-\frac{n}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}\right)-\frac{n}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q(p-1)}\right)<0.

For this reason, carrying out some same steps as we did the term
J_{1}(t)
, we derive
J_{2}(t)\lesssim 1
. From this, the estimate (
23
) has been proved. Combining the estimates (
22
),
(
23
) and
\varepsilon
is small enough, we conclude that
\mathcal{N}
becomes a contraction mapping on
X(T,M\varepsilon)
with respect to the metric of
Z(T)
. Then, using Banach’s fixed point theorem, we derive a unique solution
u\in X(T,M\varepsilon)
. Since
T
is arbitrary, it leads to our desired conclusion. Hence, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is completed.

3.
Blow-up results and Lifespan estimates

3.1.
Preliminaries

In this section, our main aim is to prove Theorem
1.3
. To do this, we recall the definition of weak solution to (
1
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p>1
,
T>0
and
u_{0}\equiv 0
. We say that
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T),L^{2})
is a local weak solution to (
1
) if for any test functions
\phi(t,x):=\eta(t)\varphi(x)
with
\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}[0,T)
, and for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with all derivatives in
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\phi(t,x)dxdt+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\phi(0,x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)\bigg(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}-\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\bigg)\phi(t,x)dxdt.

If
T=\infty
, we say that
u
is a global weak solution to (
1
).

Next, let us recall several known ingredients which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 3.1

(see Corollary 3.1 in
[
5
]
)
.

Let
f(x)=\langle x\rangle^{-q}
for
q>n
and
\theta>0
. Then, it holds for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle|(-\Delta)^{\theta}f(x)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\langle x\rangle^{-q-2\theta}&\text{ if }\theta\text{ is an integer},\\
\langle x\rangle^{-n-2s}&\text{ if otherwise, where }s=\theta-[\theta].\end{cases}

Lemma 3.2

(see Lemma 4 in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. Assume that
\psi
is a smooth function satisfying
\partial_{x}^{2}\psi\in L^{\infty}
. For any
R>0
, let
\psi_{R}
be a function defined by

\psi_{R}(x)=\psi\big(R^{-1}x\big)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then,
(-\Delta)^{s}(\psi_{R})
enjoys the following scaling property for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(-\Delta)^{s}(\psi_{R})(x)=R^{-2s}\big((-\Delta)^{s}\psi\big)\big(R^{-1}x\big).

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

At first, let us introduce the test functions
\eta=\eta(t)
and
\varphi=\varphi(x)
satisfying the following properties:

\displaystyle 1.\quad\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty))\text{ and }\eta(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }0\leq t\leq 1/2,\\
\text{decreasing}&\text{ if }1/2\leq t\leq 1,\\
0&\text{ if }t\geq 1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle 2.\quad\eta^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\big(|\eta^{\prime}|^{p^{\prime}}+|\eta^{\prime\prime}|^{p^{\prime}}\big)\text{ is bounded, }

where
p^{\prime}
stands for the conjugate of
p
. In addition, for
\epsilon
is a sufficiently small positive constant, we define

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\langle x\rangle^{-n-\epsilon}=(|x|^{2}+1)^{-\frac{n+\epsilon}{2}}.

Let
R\geq 1
and
K\geq 1
. We define the following test function:

\Phi_{R}(t,x):=\eta_{R}(t)\varphi_{R}(x),

where
\eta_{R}(t):=\eta(R^{-\alpha}t)
,
\alpha>0
and
\varphi_{R}(x):=\varphi(R^{-1}K^{-1}x)
. Moreover, we define the functionals

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{R}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt=\int_{0}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{R}:=\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt.

Let us assume that
u=u(t,x)
is a global weak solution from
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),L^{2})
to (
1
) in the sense of Definition
3.1
. We replace the function
\phi(t,x)
in Definition
3.1
by
\Phi_{R}(t,x)
to derive

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx

(24)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}-\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\right)\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt.

Using Hölder’s inequality with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
, Lemmas
3.1
-
3.2
and the change of variables
\tilde{t}=R^{-\alpha}t
,
\tilde{x}=R^{-\alpha}K^{-1}x
, we obtain the following chain of estimates for all
R\geq 1
and
K\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi_{R}(t,x)|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(t,x)\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\left(\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi_{R}(t,x)|^{p^{\prime}}\Phi_{R}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2\alpha+\frac{n+\alpha}{p^{\prime}}}K^{\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(t,x)\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(t,x)\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\left(\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}\Phi_{R}(t,x)|^{p^{\prime}}\Phi_{R}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-\alpha-2\sigma_{1}+\frac{n+\alpha}{p^{\prime}}}K^{-2\sigma_{1}+\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(t,x)\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(t,x)\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\left(\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(\int_{R^{\alpha}/2}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}\Phi_{R}(t,x)|^{p^{\prime}}\Phi_{R}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-\alpha-2\sigma_{2}+\frac{n+\alpha}{p^{\prime}}}K^{-2\sigma_{2}+\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(t,x)(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{0}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(t,x)(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{R^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|^{p^{\prime}}\Phi_{R}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2\sigma+\frac{n+\alpha}{p^{\prime}}}K^{-2\sigma+\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}}.

Let us fix
\alpha:=2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})
. Combining this with the relations (
24
) we conclude

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{R}+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2\sigma+\frac{n+2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}{p^{\prime}}}K^{\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}}.

Moreover, the application of the inequality

Ac^{\gamma}-c\leq A^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}\text{ for all }A>0,c\geq 0\text{ and }0<\gamma<1

leads to

(25)

0<\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx\lesssim R^{-2\sigma p^{\prime}+n+2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}K^{n}

for all
R>R_{0}
. Let us now divide our consideration into the following two cases.

•

Case 1:
If
m=1
, then we note that the assumption (
9
) is equivalent to

-2\sigma p^{\prime}+n+2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\leq 0.

In the subcritical case, i.e.
-2\sigma p^{\prime}+n+2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})<0
, taking
K=1
and letting
R\to\infty
in (
25
) we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx=0.

So, this is a contradiction to the assumption (
8
). In the critical case, i.e.
-2\sigma p^{\prime}+n+2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})=0
, we see that
\mathcal{I}_{R}
is uniformly bounded, that is,
u\in L^{p}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Consequently, one gets

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}-\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}\right)\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt=0

by noticing that
\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{R}=0
. The employment of Young’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{R}+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx\leq C^{\prime}\mathcal{I}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}K^{-2\sigma+\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\frac{\mathcal{I}_{R}}{p}+CK^{n-2\sigma p^{\prime}},

that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{I}_{R}\leq-p^{\prime}\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx+p^{\prime}CK^{n-2\sigma p^{\prime}}.

Observing that
n-2\sigma p^{\prime}<0
we pass
K\to\infty
to derive a contradiction to the assumption (
8
) again.

•

Case 2:
If
m>1
, then we use (
25
) again by taking
K=1
and noticing the condition (
8
) to give

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{R/2\leq|x|\leq R}|x|^{-\frac{n}{m}}(\log(e+|x|))^{-1}\varphi_{R}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{R/2}^{R}|x|^{n-\frac{n}{m}-1}(\log(|x|))^{-1}d|x|

\displaystyle\geq CR^{n-\frac{n}{m}}(\log R)^{-1},

which leads to

(26)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\log R)^{-1}\lesssim R^{-2\sigma p^{\prime}+\frac{n}{m}+2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}.

However, the fact is that the last estimate does not hold for any sufficiently large number
R
when the following condition occurs:

-2\sigma p^{\prime}+\frac{n}{m}+2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})<0.

Summarizing, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is completed.

3.3.
Estimates for lifespan

In this subsection, we will summarize how to get some estimates for lifespan of
solutions in the subcritical case

(27)

\displaystyle p<1+\frac{2m\sigma}{n-2m\sigma_{1}}.

Let us denote by
T_{m}(\varepsilon)
the so-called lifespan of a local (in time) solution, i.e. the maximal existence time of local solutions. Then, lower bound estimates and upper bound estimates for
T_{m}(\varepsilon)
are given by the next statements.

Proposition 3.1

(
Lower bound of lifespan
)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
, we impose the additional assumption (
27
). Then, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, the lower bound for the lifespan
T_{m}(\varepsilon)
can be estimated as follows:

(28)

\displaystyle T_{m}(\varepsilon)\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{\gamma_{m}}},

where

(29)

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}:=1-\frac{n}{2m(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(p-1)+\frac{p\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}.

Proof.

To prove Proposition
3.1
, we will again use the definitions of the constant
M
and the mapping
\mathcal{N}
on the set
X(T,M\varepsilon)
from the proof of Theorem
1.1
, in particular, the estimates (
17
) and (
18
). From Proposition
2.1
and the condition (
27
), we can see that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|u(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau\right\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(p-1)+\frac{p\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}\||u|^{p}\|_{Y(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(p-1)+\frac{p\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

For this reason, with two function
u,v
belonging to
X(T,M\varepsilon)
we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]\|_{X(T)}\leq\frac{M\varepsilon}{2}+C_{1}(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(p-1)+\frac{p\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}M^{p}\varepsilon^{p}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]-\mathcal{N}[v]\|_{Z(T)}\leq C_{2}(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(p-1)+\frac{p\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{Z(T)},

where constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
independent of
M,\varepsilon
and
T
. Therefore, if we assume that

(30)

\displaystyle\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}(1+T)^{1-\frac{n}{2m(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(p-1)+\frac{p\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}<\frac{1}{4},

then
\mathcal{N}
is a contraction mapping on
X(T,M\varepsilon)
with the metric of
Z(T)
. As a consequence, we may construct a unique local solution
u\in X(T,M\varepsilon)
, moreover, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X(T)}<\frac{3M\varepsilon}{4}.

Let us choose

\displaystyle T_{m}^{*}:=\sup\bigg\{T\in(0,T_{m}(\varepsilon))\text{ such that }\mathcal{G}(T):=\|u\|_{X(T)}\leq M\varepsilon\bigg\}.

If
T_{m}^{*}
satisfies the inequality (
30
), then from the previous estimate it follows that
\mathcal{G}(T_{m}^{*})<3M\varepsilon/4
. Due to the fact that
\mathcal{G}(T)
is a continuous and increasing function for any
T\in(0,T_{m}(\varepsilon))
, there exists
T_{m}^{0}\in(T_{m}^{*},T_{m}(\varepsilon))
such that
\mathcal{G}(T_{m}^{0})\leq M\varepsilon
. This contradicts to the definition of
T_{m}^{*}
. For this reason, one realizes

\displaystyle\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}(1+T_{m}^{*})^{1-\frac{n}{2m(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}(p-1)+\frac{p\sigma_{1}}{\sigma-\sigma_{1}}}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\geq\frac{1}{4},

that is,

\displaystyle T_{m}(\varepsilon)\geq T_{m}^{*}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{\gamma_{m}}}.

Thus, Proposition
3.1
has been proved.
∎

Proposition 3.2

(
Upper bound of lifespan
)
.

Assume that the conditions of Theorem
1.3
are satisfied. Then, the upper bound for the lifespan
T_{m}(\varepsilon)
can be estimated by

(31)

\displaystyle T_{m}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{\gamma_{1}}}&\text{ if }m=1,\\
\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{\gamma_{m}}-\delta}&\text{ if }m>1,\end{cases}

where
\gamma_{m}
is defined as in (
29
) and
\delta
is an arbitrarily small positive real number.

Proof.

In the case
m=1
, using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem we gain

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx.

For this reason, with
R_{1}

being a sufficiently large positive real number, we have the following relation:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx>\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx=:C_{u_{1}}

\displaystyle\text{ for all }R>R_{1}.

From the relation (
25
), we take
K=1
and pass
R\to T_{1}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}}
to obtain

\displaystyle C_{u_{1}}\varepsilon\leq CT_{1}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{-2\sigma p^{\prime}+n+2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}}.

Therefore, we may claim the estimate (
31
) when
m=1
. In the case
m>1
, we will again use the estimate (
26
) by letting
R\to T_{m}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}}
to conclude the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Linking the achieved estimates (
28

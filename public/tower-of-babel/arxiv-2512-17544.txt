Forbidding just one intersection for short integer sequences
Elizaveta Iarovikova, Fedor Noskov, Georgy Sokolov, Nikolai Terekhov

Abstract
In this paper, we study the famous Erdős–Sós forbidden intersection problem for words over alphabet of size m m : what is the maximal size of a subfamily ℱ \mathcal{F} of [ m ] n [m]^{n} that does not contain two vectors x , y x,y that coincide on exactly t − 1 t-1 coordinates? We answer this question provided m ⩾ poly ⁡ ( t ) m\geqslant\poly(t) , n ⩾ poly ⁡ ( t ) n\geqslant\poly(t) for some polynomial function poly ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \poly(\cdot) of t t , greatly extending the recent result of Keevash, Lifshitz, Long and Minzer. Our proof combines some of the recently developed methods in extremal combinatorics, including the spread approximation technique of Kupavskii and Zakharov, and the hypercontractivity approach developed in a series of works of Keevash, Keller, Lifshitz, Long, Marcus and Minzer.

We denote

We say that two sets

Depending on the context, we will identify the families of codes

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\left\{X\in\binom{[mn]}{n}\;:\;\text{for all }i\in[n]\text{ we have }|X\cap I_{i}|=1\right\}.

(1)

3
Preliminaries

We treat the regimes
\poly(t)\leqslant m\leqslant e^{Cn/t^{2}}
and
m\geqslant n^{Ct}
separately, and each regime requires a completely different set of tools. Note that for our assumptions on
n
these regimes overlap.

We treat the case
m\leqslant e^{Cn/t^{2}}
using a measure boosting argument, which dates back to Dinur and Friedgut
[
10
]
. We illustrate it on subfamilies of sets from some ambient family
\mathcal{A}
(which is
\mathcal{C}
in our case) endowed with the uniform measure
\mu(\cdot)
. The argument is based on the following informal observation:
given two pseudorandom families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
, under mild assumptions on their measure
\mu(\cdot)
in some ambient family
\mathcal{A}
, one can obtain families
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
in (probably, different) ambient family
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
such that

1.

they have a constant volume w.r.t. some other measure
\widetilde{\mu}(\cdot)
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
;

2.

if
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
cross-contain a matching, then
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
do.

The final trick of this argument is that one can easily prove the existence of cross-matching in families of large measure, leading to the conclusion:
pseudorandom families cross-contain a matching
. It turns out that, in many Turán-type problems, extremal families admit decomposition into relatively simple pseudorandom subfamilies, and the above reasoning allows to relax conditions on problem parameters.

The efficiency of this idea significantly varies with the notion of pseudorandomness and the range family
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
used. The original definition of pseudorandomness due to Dinur and Friedgut called
uncapturability
plays the key role in the celebrated junta method
[
48
,
11
,
42
,
56
]
. Later, Alweiss and coathours
[
3
]
made a breakthrough result in the Erdős sunflower problem using another notion of pseudorandomness called
spreadness
. Using their results together with delicate combinatorial arguments, Kupavskii and Zakharov
[
51
]
developed the spread approximation technique for which they introduced a new concept of pseudorandomness named
\tau
-
homogeneity
. They apply their method to obtain solutions of the Deza–Frankl problem on
t
-intersecting permutations and the Erdős–Sós forbidden intersection problem under polynomial dependencies between parameters. The technique was further improved in subsequent works of Kupavskii and coathours
[
52
,
53
,
54
,
49
,
27
,
38
]
yielding a large variety of results in extremal set theory. In particular, in this paper we enhance the spread approximation technique for the families of codes. At the same time, Keevash and coathours
[
41
,
42
]
presented the notion of
global
families, and using it they reached large progress in Turán-type problems on forbidden large sparse hypergraphs. Then, independently of Kupavskii and Zakharov, they modified their definition of global families (so it coincides now with the
\tau
-homogeneity), and generalized it for functions on a product space
[
45
]
. The main result of their findings is a new hypercontractivity inequality for global functions, which we use below; see Theorem
10
. In follow-up papers, they show that their approach has a lot of consequences in different fields of discrete mathematics
[
35
,
57
,
47
,
20
]
.

In this paper, we modify the measure boosting argument of
[
42
]
for families of codes, using the ideas from
[
49
,
45
]
. Starting from families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subset\mathcal{A}=[m]^{n}
and the uniform measure
\mu(\cdot)
, we obtain families
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
and a measure
\widetilde{\mu}(\cdot)
, iteratively applying an operation which the authors of
[
42
]
call

.

We start with some definitions.

Definition 1

(Definition 6.5

We refer to a map

\displaystyle|\pi^{-1}(y)|\leqslant\frac{bm_{1}}{m_{2}}.

The family of

A

-balanced gluings

\pi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\pi_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\pi_{n}(x_{n}))

by
\Pi_{m_{1},m_{2},b}^{\otimes n}

is omitted if

and

.

Then, for a family
\mathcal{F}\subset[m_{1}]^{n}
and a gluing

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\pi}=\{\pi(x)\;:\;x\in\mathcal{F}\}.

If

for some

, then, slightly abusing notation, we denote

Note that if

Similarly, for a product measure

\displaystyle\nu^{\pi}(\{y\})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\nu(\pi^{-1}_{i}(y_{i})).

Let us comment now on the

Definition 2

(Definition 6.7

]
)

A product measure

One can easily verify the following claim.

For any
\mathcal{F}\subset[m_{1}]^{n}
, a gluing
\pi:[m_{1}]^{n}\to[m_{2}]^{n}
and product measure
\nu
on
[m_{1}]^{n}
, we have
\nu^{\pi}(\mathcal{F}^{\pi})\geqslant\nu(\mathcal{F})
.

Clearly, Claim
5
is insufficient for upgrading the measure of a family
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
. The following lemma shows how one can enlarge the measure of a family
\mathcal{F}
choosing a gluing
\pi
via some averaging argument. The statement of the lemma requires some basic knowledge concerning Markov chains; here we provide some definitions.

For a measure
\nu
on
[m]^{n}
, define the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\nu}
on the space
L_{2}([m]^{n},\nu)
as

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\nu}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\nu}f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x}).

Then, let
T
be a Markov chain with the stationary measure
\nu
. We define a linear operator
T:L_{2}([m]^{n},\nu)\to L_{2}([m]^{n},\nu)
as follows. Let
\mathbf{y}
be a step of the Markov chain
T
starting from a point
x
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(Tf)(x)=\mathbb{E}f(\mathbf{y}).

Finally, for a Markov chain
T
with the stationary measure
\nu
define
\lambda_{*}(T)
as follows

\displaystyle(1-\lambda_{*}(\widetilde{T}))^{2}=\sup\{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\nu}(\widetilde{T}f(\mathbf{x}))^{2}\;:\;\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\nu}f(\mathbf{x})=0,\;\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim f}f^{2}(\mathbf{x})=1\}.

Then, we are ready to state the lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\nu(\cdot)
be a product measure on
[m_{1}]^{n}
. Fix
m_{2},b
such that
m_{2}\leqslant m_{1}\leqslant bm_{2}
. Then, there exists a product Markov chain
T=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}
with the stationary measure
\nu(\cdot)
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\pi}\sim U(\Pi_{m_{1},m_{2},b}^{\otimes n})}\nu^{\bm{\pi}}(\mathcal{F})\geqslant\frac{\nu^{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\langle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}},T(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})\rangle_{\nu}},

(2)

where
U(\Pi_{m_{1},m_{2},b}^{\otimes n})
stands for the uniform measure on
\Pi_{m_{1},m_{2},b}^{\otimes n}
,
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}
stands for the characteristic function of
\mathcal{F}
.
Moreover, we have
\lambda_{*}(T)\geqslant 1/6
.

For the proof, we refer the reader to that of Lemma 6.9
[
42
]
.

It will be convenient to upper bound the scalar product in the right-hand side of (
2
), replacing the Markov chain operator
T
with some operator defined as follows. Given a vector
x\in[m]^{n}
and a product measure
\nu=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\nu_{i}
, define a distribution
N_{\rho}(x)=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}N_{\rho}(x_{i})
, where
N_{\rho}(x_{i})
is a distribution of the following random variable
\mathbf{y}_{i}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i},&\text{ with probability }\rho,\\
\text{sample from }\nu_{i}(\cdot),&\text{ with probability }1-\rho.\end{cases}

Then, we define an operator
T_{\rho}:L_{2}([m]^{n},\nu)\to L_{2}([m]^{n},\nu)
by the averaging a function
f
over
\mathbf{y}
:

\displaystyle(T_{\rho}f)(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{y}\sim N_{\rho}(x)}f(\mathbf{y}).

We also introduce a quantity that is closely related to the noise operator. Namely, we define the
stability
of a function
f
or a family
\mathcal{F}
as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho}(f)=\langle f,T_{\rho}f\rangle_{\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho}(\mathcal{F})=\langle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}},T_{\rho}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle_{\nu}.

Then, we upper bound
\langle f,Tf\rangle_{\nu}
by the following lemma.

Lemma 7

(Lemma 5.13
[
42
]
)
.

Suppose that a product Markov chain
T
has
\lambda_{*}(T)\geqslant 1/6
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\langle f,Tf\rangle_{\nu}\leqslant\operatorname{Stab}_{5/6}(f).

Lemma
7
and Lemma
6
yield the following corollary.

Corollary 8
.

Fix numbers
n,m_{1},m_{2},b
such that
m_{2}\leqslant m_{1}\leqslant bm_{2}
. Then, for a function
f\in L_{2}([m_{1}]^{n},\nu)
, a family
\mathcal{F}
and a product measure
\nu
, there exists a gluing
\pi\in\Pi_{m_{1},m_{2},b}^{\otimes n}
such that

\displaystyle\nu^{\pi}(\mathcal{F}^{\pi})\geqslant\frac{\nu^{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\operatorname{Stab}_{5/6}(\mathcal{F})}.

Due to Corollary
8
, for the measure boosting argument, we need to control
\operatorname{Stab}_{5/6}(\mathcal{F})
. The following proposition ensures that it is enough to upper bound
\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho_{0}}(\mathcal{F})
for sufficiently small
\rho_{0}
.

Proposition 9

(Lemma 5.11
[
42
]
)
.

For any function
f\in L_{2}([m]^{n},\nu)
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho}(f)\leqslant\|f\|_{2}^{2(1-1/t)}\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho^{t}}(f)^{1/t}

whenever
t=2^{d}
with
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

To control
\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho_{0}}(\mathcal{F})
, one need to guarantee some pseudorandomness of
\mathcal{F}
. Authors of the original paper
[
42
]
used the notion of globalness due to Keevash et al.
[
41
]
. Instead, we use the concept of globalness from
[
45
]
.

Theorem 10

(Theorem 1.4 from
[
45
]
)
.

Say that a function
f
on a product space
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{i}
endowed with a product measure
\nu=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\nu_{i}
is
r
-global if for any
S\subset[n]
and any
x\in\prod_{i\in S}\Omega_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{S\to x}\|_{2}\leqslant r^{|S|}\|f\|_{2},

where
f_{S\to x}:\prod_{i\in[n]\setminus S}\Omega_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
stands for the function
f_{S\to x}(y)=f(x,y)
.
Let
q\geqslant 2
. If
r\geqslant 1
and
\rho\leqslant\frac{\ln q}{32rq}
, then
\|T_{\rho}f\|_{q}\leqslant\|f\|_{2}
for any
r
-global function
f
.

In the paper
[
45
]
, the proof of Theorem
10
is presented for the case when all
\Omega_{i}
and
\nu_{i}
coincide. However, it works for the product space since it based on the freezing argument and induction.

We say that a family
\mathcal{F}
is
\tau
-homogeneous if
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\sqrt{\tau}
-global. This yields the following definition.

Definition 3
.

Say that
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
is
\tau
-homogeneous w.r.t. to a product measure
\nu
if for any
S
and
x\in[m]^{S}
we have

\displaystyle\nu_{S\to x}(\mathcal{F}(S\to x))\leqslant\tau^{|S|}\nu(\mathcal{F}).

We discuss
\tau
-homogeneous families in more details in the subsequent section. For now, let us present how to bound
\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho_{0}}(\mathcal{F})
for
\tau
-homogeneous family
\mathcal{F}
. By the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\leqslant\|T_{\rho_{0}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{4}\|\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{4/3}.

If
\rho_{0}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
10
for
q=4
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\leqslant\|\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2}\|\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{4/3}=\nu^{5/4}(\mathcal{F}).

It turns out, that this bound is sufficient for our purposes, see the proof of Lemma
25
.

Given two homogeneous families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
, suppose for a while that, iteratively using Corollary
8
, we found families
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\subset[m^{\prime}]^{n^{\prime}}
such that
\widetilde{\mu}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{1}),\widetilde{\mu}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
is larger than some constant and
\widetilde{\mu}
is an enough balanced product measure. Then, the following lemma allows us to find a cross-disagreement in families
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
.

Lemma 11

(Lemma 5.9
[
42
]
)
.

Let
\nu=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\nu_{i}
be a product probability measure on
[m]^{n}
such that
\nu_{i}(x)\leqslant\lambda\leqslant 1/2
for all
i\in[n]
and
x\in[m]
. Suppose that
\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\subset[m]^{n}
are cross-intersecting with
\nu(\mathcal{G}_{i})=\alpha_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\leqslant\left(\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right)^{2}(1-\alpha_{1})(1-\alpha_{2}).

There is an alternative measure boosting argument that performs well if one of the initial measures
\mu(\mathcal{F}_{1}),\mu(\mathcal{F}_{2})
is large. We present it as the following lemma. While its statement can not be found in paper
[
42
]
, the proof coincides with that of Theorem 2.3
[
42
]
. For the sake of completeness, we provide the proof in the appendix.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subset[m]^{n}
and let
\mu
be uniform measure on
[m]^{n}
. If
\mu(\mathcal{F}_{1})+\mu(\mathcal{F}_{2})^{\log_{m}(2)}>1
, then there exist
x_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
such that
\operatorname{agr}(x_{1},x_{2})=0
.

In the next section, we move on to the following questions: how to decompose a family
\mathcal{F}
into homogeneous families and how to reduce a question about avoiding agreement of size exactly
t-1
into a problem of finding a cross-disagreement in homogeneous families?

3.2
Homogeneous families

First, we discuss how to decompose a family
\mathcal{F}
into homogeneous families
\mathcal{F}_{i}
up to some small remainder. We start with the following observation.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
. Let
\nu
be a product measure on
[m]^{n}
and fix
\tau>1
. If
Z\subset[n]
is maximal such that for some
r\in[m]^{Z}
we have
\nu_{Z\to r}(\mathcal{F}(Z\to r))\geqslant\tau^{|Z|}\nu(\mathcal{F})
, then
\mathcal{F}(Z\to r)
is
\tau
-homogenous with respect to
\nu_{Z\to r}
. Note that if
Z=[n]
, then
\nu_{Z\to r}(\mathcal{F}(Z\to r))=\mathds{1}\{r\in\mathcal{F}\}
.

Using the above lemma iteratively, one can obtain the decomposition which is called the spread approximation of
\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 14

(Lemma 52 from
[
49
]
)
.

Fix an integer
q
and a real number
\tau
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
and let
\mu
be the uniform measure on
[m]^{n}
. There exists a system of pairs
\{(Z_{i},x_{i})|i=1,\ldots,l\}
such that for all
i
we have
Z_{i}\subset[n],|Z_{i}|\leqslant q
,
x_{i}\in[m]^{Z_{i}}
and a partition of
\mathcal{F}
into families
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset\mathcal{F}[Z_{i}\rightarrow x_{i}]
and
\mathcal{R}
such that

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{R}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{F}_{i},

\mu(\mathcal{R})\leqslant\tau^{-q}
and for all
i
we have
\mu_{Z_{i}\rightarrow x_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i}(Z_{i}\rightarrow x_{i}))\geqslant\tau^{-q}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}(Z_{i}\rightarrow x_{i})
is
\tau
-homogenous with respect to
\mu_{Z_{i}\rightarrow x_{i}}
.

In the paper
[
49
]
, the authors require
\mu(\mathcal{R})\leqslant 32\tau^{-q}
, but their proof works with the tighter bound suggested in the above lemma. For the sake of completeness, let us present the proof of Lemma
14
.

Proof of Lemma
14
.

We will obtain the decomposition into families
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{l}
at the end of the following procedure. Define
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mathcal{F}
. In the step
i
,
i=1,2,\ldots
, find a maximal set
Z_{i}\subset[n]
such that there exists
x_{i}\in[m]^{Z_{i}}
with the following properties:

\displaystyle\mu_{Z_{i}\to x_{i}}(\mathcal{G}_{i-1}(Z_{i}\to x_{i}))\geqslant\tau^{|Z_{i}|}\mu(\mathcal{G}_{i-1}).

If such
Z_{i}
does not exist, set
l=i
,
Z_{i}=\varnothing
,
\mathcal{F}_{l}=\mathcal{G}_{l-1}
. Otherwise, if
|Z_{i}|>q
or
\mu_{Z_{i}\to x_{i}}(\mathcal{G}_{i-1}(Z_{i}\to x_{i}))\leqslant\tau^{-q}
, then stop, put
l=i-1
and set
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{G}_{i-1}
. Finally, if
|Z_{i}|\leqslant q
and
\mu_{Z_{i}\to x_{i}}(\mathcal{G}_{i-1}(Z_{i}\to x_{i}))\geqslant\tau^{-q}
, then set
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{G}_{i-1}[Z_{i}\to x_{i}]
,
\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{G}_{i-1}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{i}
and move to the step
i+1
.

In the end of procedure, we have either
\mu(\mathcal{R})\leqslant\tau^{-|Z_{l+1}|}\mu_{Z_{l+1}\to r_{l+1}}(\mathcal{G}_{l}(Z_{l+1}\to x_{l+1}))
for some
|Z_{l+1}|>q
or
\mu(\mathcal{R})\leqslant\tau^{-q}
. In both cases, we have
\mu(\mathcal{R})\leqslant\tau^{-q}
. By Lemma
13
, families
\mathcal{F}_{i}
are
\tau
-homogeneous, and the proof is complete.
∎

The core idea of our proof is to show that an extremal family
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
avoiding an agreement of size exactly
t-1
is
t
-agreeing. At the first stage, it is enough to show that restrictions
(Z_{i},x_{i}),i=1,\ldots,l
, of the spread approximation are
t
-agreeing, so
\mathcal{F}
is
t
-agreeing up to small remainder. Arguing indirectly, suppose that there are two restrictions
(Z_{1},x_{1})
and
(Z_{2},x_{2})
in
\{(Z_{i},x_{i})\}_{i=1}^{l}
such that
\operatorname{agr}_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})<t
. Then, we will find a set
H\subset[n]
of size
t-1-\operatorname{agr}_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})
and a vector
y\in[m]^{H}
such that such that
\operatorname{agr}_{(Z_{1}\cap Z_{2})\cup H}((x_{1},y),(x_{2},y))=t-1
, and some families
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}(Z_{1}\cup H\to(x_{1},y))
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}\subset\mathcal{F}(Z_{2}\cup H\to(x_{2},y))
are
\tau^{\prime}
-homogeneous and have a moderately large measure, see Lemma
29
below. Then, the measure boosting argument of the previous section guarantees that they cross-contain a disagreement, a contradiction.

Thus, we require some tools to work with restrictions of
\tau
-homogeneous families. In particular, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
be
\tau
-homogenous with respect to a
b
-balanced product measure
\nu
. Let
R\subset[n]
. Let
X\subset[m]^{R}
be a set such that
X=\prod_{i\in R}X_{i}
for some
X_{i}\subset[m]
,
\sum_{i\in R}|X_{i}|\tau b<m
. Define

\mathcal{F^{\prime}}=\{y\in\mathcal{F}|\forall x\in X\,\operatorname{agr}(x,y|_{R})=0\}.

Then,
\nu(\mathcal{F^{\prime}})\geqslant(1-\frac{\sum_{i\in R}|X_{i}|\tau b}{m})\nu(\mathcal{F})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}
is
\tau^{\prime}
-homogenous for

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}=\tau\cdot\left(1-\frac{\sum_{i\in R}|X_{i}|\tau b}{m}\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

For any
i\in R,x\in X_{i}
we have

\nu(\mathcal{F}[i\rightarrow x])=\nu_{i}(x)\nu_{i\to x}(\mathcal{F}(i\rightarrow x))\leqslant\nu_{i}(x)\tau\nu(\mathcal{F}),

where the last inequality holds by the
\tau
-homogeneity of
\mathcal{F}
.
Since
\nu
is
b
-balanced, we have
\nu_{i}(x)\leqslant\frac{b}{m}
. It implies

\displaystyle\nu(\mathcal{F^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\nu(\mathcal{F}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in R}\bigcup_{x\in X_{i}}\mathcal{F}[i\rightarrow x])\geqslant\nu(\mathcal{F})-\sum_{i\in R}\sum_{x\in X_{i}}\nu(\mathcal{F}[i\rightarrow x])

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(1-\frac{\sum_{i\in R}|X_{i}|\tau b}{m}\right)\cdot\nu(\mathcal{F}),

and the first statement of the lemma holds. Let us check the second statement on
\tau^{\prime}
-homogeneity of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Consider arbitrary
Z\subset[n]
and
r\in[m]^{Z}
. We have

\displaystyle\nu_{Z\rightarrow r}(\mathcal{F^{\prime}}(Z\rightarrow r))

\displaystyle\leqslant\nu_{Z\rightarrow r}(\mathcal{F}(Z\rightarrow r))\leqslant\tau^{|Z|}\nu(\mathcal{F})\leqslant\frac{\tau^{|Z|}}{\left(1-\frac{\sum_{i\in R}|X_{i}|\tau b}{m}\right)}\nu(\mathcal{F^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{\tau}{1-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in R}|X_{i}|\tau b}\right)^{|Z|}\nu(\mathcal{F^{\prime}})

and, therefore,
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}
is
\tau^{\prime}
-homogenous.
∎

To find certain restrictions, we widely use a simple averaging argument, which we state as a separate claim.

Claim 16
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subset of
[m]^{n}
and let
\nu(\cdot)
be a product measure on
[m]^{n}
. Then, for any
H\subset[n]
there exists
x\in[m]^{H}
such that

\displaystyle\nu_{H\to x}(\mathcal{F}(H\to x))\geqslant\nu(\mathcal{F}).

Proof.

Consider a random vector
\mathbf{x}
distributed on
[m]^{H}
according to the measure
\prod_{i\in S}\nu_{i}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\nu_{H\to\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\to\mathbf{x}))=\sum_{x\in[m]^{H}}\prod_{i\in H}\nu_{i}(x_{i})\cdot\frac{\nu(\mathcal{F}[H\to x])}{\prod_{i\in H}\nu_{i}(x_{i})}=\sum_{x\in[m]^{H}}\nu(\mathcal{F}[H\to x])=\nu(\mathcal{F}),

(3)

and the claim follows.
∎

Another helpful argument that is based on similar ideas is the following lemma, which is applied to a pair of
\tau
-homogenous families simultaneously and allows to find a common restriction such that both restricted families have large measure.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
be a
\tau
-homogenous family with respect to a product measure
\nu
. Let
H\subset[n]
and let
\mathbf{x}\sim\nu_{H}
be a random element in
[m]^{H}
. Let
p\in(0,1)
. If
\tau<\left(\frac{1+p}{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{|H|}}
, then

\mathbb{P}\left(\nu_{H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}))\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\nu(\mathcal{F})\right)>p.

Proof.

Let
q=\mathbb{P}\left(\nu_{H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}))\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\nu(\mathcal{F})\right)
.
We count
\mathbb{E}\nu_{H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}))
in two ways.
On the one hand, since
\nu_{H\rightarrow x}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow x))\leqslant\tau^{|H|}\nu(\mathcal{F})
for all
x
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\nu_{H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\nu_{H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}))\cdot\mathds{1}\left\{\nu_{H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}))\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\nu(\mathcal{F})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\nu_{H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}))\mathds{1}\left\{\nu_{H\rightarrow x}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow x))<\frac{1}{2}\nu(\mathcal{F})\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant q\tau^{|H|}\nu(\mathcal{F})+(1-q)\frac{1}{2}\nu(\mathcal{F}).

On the other hand, we have
\mathbb{E}\nu_{H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}(\mathcal{F}(H\rightarrow\mathbf{x}))=\nu(\mathcal{F})
due to (
3
). Thus, we get
1\leqslant q\tau^{|H|}+\frac{1}{2}(1-q)
. It implies

q\geqslant\frac{1}{2\tau^{|H|}-1}.

Substituting
\tau<\left(\frac{1+p}{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{|H|}}
, we get the desired bound
q>p
.
∎

Assume for a while, that pairs
(Z_{i},x_{i})
obtained by the spread approximation lemma
14
are
t
-agreeing, i.e. for any
(Z_{i},x_{i}),(Z_{j},x_{j})
from
\{(Z_{i},x_{i})\}_{i=1}^{l}
, we have
\operatorname{agr}_{{Z_{i}\cap Z_{j}}}(x_{i},x_{j})\geqslant t
. In the next section, we discuss a number of arguments due to papers
[
51
]
and
[
49
]
, which help to deal with this and weaker properties of the spread approximations and to prove that there exists a pair
(T,x)
,
|T|=t
and
x\in[m]^{T}
, such that for almost all codes
y\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
y|_{T}=x
.

3.3
Simplification arguments

Simplification arguments are one of the key components of the spread approximation technique, but they vary from problem to problem
[
51
,
52
,
54
,
27
,
49
,
38
]
. All of them are designed for families of sets from an ambient family
\mathcal{A}
that meet some conditions. First, we list them.

Say that a family of sets
\mathcal{F}
is
r
-spread if for any
Z
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(Z)|\leqslant r^{-|Z|}|\mathcal{F}|.

Then, we require the following assumption, which is a stronger version of Assumption 1
[
49
]
.

Assumption 1
.

A family
\mathcal{A}
is
k
-uniform. Also, for any
X
that is a subset of some set from
\mathcal{A}
the family
\mathcal{A}(X)
is
r
-spread.

The second assumption is used when, for a
\tau
-homogenous family
\mathcal{F}
, one needs to find a lot of sets
H
in the shadow of
\mathcal{A}
such that the
\mathcal{F}(H)
is
\tau^{\prime}
-homogeneous for some
\tau^{\prime}
.

Assumption 2
.

For any set
S\in\partial_{\leqslant q}\mathcal{A}
, a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{A}(S)
, and
h\leqslant t-1
, we have

\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{|\mathcal{A}(S)|}=\frac{1}{|\partial_{h}(\mathcal{A}(S))|}\sum_{H\in\partial_{h}(\mathcal{A}(S))}\frac{|\mathcal{F}(H)|}{|\mathcal{A}(H\cup S)|},

or, equivalently, for a random set
\mathbf{H}
uniformly distributed on
\partial_{h}(\mathcal{A}(S))
, we have
\mu(\mathcal{F})=\mathbb{E}\mu(\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{H}))
, where
\mu
is the uniform measure.

We proceed with verifying that the necessary assumptions hold for the family
\mathcal{C}
defined by (
1
) with proper choice of
r,t,q,k
.

Lemma 18
.

Assumptions
1
and
2
hold for the family
\mathcal{C}
, any positive real
r\leqslant m,
integers
t\leqslant n+1,q\geqslant t
, and
k=n
.

Proof.

We start with verifying Assumption
1
. Obviously,
\mathcal{C}
considered as a subset of
[mn]\choose n
is
n
-uniform. Let
X
be a subset of some set from
\mathcal{C}
,
Y
be a subset of some set from
\mathcal{C}(X)
, i.e.,
X
and
Y

\left(\frac{3/5}{m^{1/12t}}\right)^{t-1}\cdot{n-q\choose t-1}\geqslant m^{-1/6}{n\choose t-1}

which is equivalent to

m^{1/6}\geqslant\left(\frac{m^{1/12t}}{3/5}\right)^{t-1}\cdot\frac{n\cdot\ldots\cdot(n-t+2)}{(n-q)\cdot\ldots\cdot(n-q-t+2)}.

For all
0\leqslant i\leqslant t-2
we have
(n-i)/(n-i-q)<2
, because
n\geqslant 50t^{2}\geqslant t-2+48t^{2}=t-2+2q
. Thus, it is enough to prove the following bound:

m^{1/6}\geqslant\left(\frac{m^{1/12t}}{3/5}\right)^{t-1}\cdot 2^{t-1}.

It is equivalent to

m\geqslant m^{(t-1)/2t}\cdot\left(\frac{2^{6}\cdot 5^{6}}{3^{6}}\right)^{t-1},

which is true since

m\geqslant m^{1/2}\cdot m^{1/2}\geqslant m^{(t-1)/2t}\left(\frac{2^{6}\cdot 5^{6}}{3^{6}}\right)^{t-1}.

Therefore, Lemma
22
is applicable. We obtain a set of restrictions
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
such that for any
(Z,x)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
we have
|Z|=t
. We have

|\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}|\leqslant\frac{1}{\lambda}(1+2\ln(\lambda|\partial_{\leqslant q}[m]^{n}|))=\frac{1+2\ln{\left(m^{-1/6}\cdot\sum_{p=0}^{q}{n\choose p}m^{p}\right)}}{m^{-1/6}}\leqslant

\leqslant\frac{1+2\ln{\left(2m^{-1/6}\cdot n^{q}m^{q}\right)}}{m^{-1/6}}\leqslant\frac{1+2q(\ln m+\ln n)}{m^{-1/6}}\leqslant 5qm^{1/6}\ln m\leqslant m^{1/3}\ln m,

where the last inequality holds because
m\geqslant(2^{22}n)^{4t}\geqslant(2^{22}\cdot 50t^{2})^{4t}\geqslant 5^{6}\cdot 24^{6}\cdot t^{12}=(5q)^{6}
. Also, Lemma
22
implies

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}[\hat{\mathcal{T}}]}|\mathcal{U}_{S}|\leqslant\frac{32q^{3}}{\lambda m}\ln(\lambda|\partial_{\leqslant q}[m]^{n}|)\cdot m^{n-t}=\frac{32q^{3}}{m^{5/6}}\ln{\left(m^{-1/6}\cdot\sum_{p=0}^{q}{n\choose p}m^{p}\right)}\cdot m^{n-t}\leqslant

\leqslant\frac{128q^{4}\ln m}{m^{5/6}}\cdot m^{n-t}\leqslant m^{n-t-1/3}.

The last inequality holds because

m^{1/2}=m^{1/4}\cdot m^{1/4}\geqslant(2^{22}n)^{t}\cdot\frac{\ln m}{4}\geqslant 128q^{4}\ln m,

where we used
m^{1/4}=e^{1/4\ln m}\geqslant 1+\frac{1}{4}\ln m
,
(2^{22}n)^{t}\geqslant 2^{9}(24t^{2})^{4}
and

(2^{22}n)^{t}\geqslant(2^{22}\cdot 50t^{2})^{t}\geqslant 2^{22t}t^{8}\geqslant 2^{9}\cdot 24^{4}\cdot t^{8}.

∎

6.2
Properties of incomplete
t
-stars

In the previous section, we constructed a set of restrictions
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
which ‘represents’ well the original family. We now study the incomplete
t
-stars which are subsets of
\mathcal{F}[T]
for
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
. In this section we do not use properties of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
so we prove all the claims for arbitrary
T\in\partial_{t}[m]^{n}
, however, we will apply them only to
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
.

The following definition is crucial in our proof.

Definition 7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subfamily of
[m]^{n}
,
T\in\partial_{t}[m]^{n},\mathcal{F}_{T}\subset\mathcal{F}[T]
. We define
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}\subset\mathcal{F}(T)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}=\left\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}(T)\;:\;\forall X\in\binom{F}{\leqslant t-1}\text{ the covering number of }\mathcal{F}_{T}(T\cup X)\text{ is at least }n+1\right\}.

We will later see that
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}
is usually of almost the same size as
\mathcal{F}_{T}(T)
, however its properties allow us to find pairs of elements of
\mathcal{F}
with fixed agreement given their shadows with the same agreement. This idea will be illustrated in the proof of the following claim.

Recall that we treat
[m]^{n}
as a subset of
[mn]\choose n
. Since the proof is based on techniques originally developed for sets we use the same notation. We now prove that if
\mathcal{F}
is
(t-1)
-avoiding then
\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}
are disjoint for distinct
T
.

Claim 35
.

Let
m,n,t
be arbitrary positive integers,
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
be
(t-1)
-avoiding. For distinct
T_{1},T_{2}\in\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}
and each
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{T_{1}}^{*},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{2}}
we have
|F_{1}\cap F_{2}|<t-1
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary, there exist some distinct
T_{1},T_{2}\in\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}
and
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{1}},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{2}}
such that
|F_{1}\cap F_{2}|\geqslant t-1
. Put
T_{1}=(Z_{1},x_{1}),T_{2}=(Z_{2},x_{2}),F_{1}=([n]\setminus Z_{1},y_{1}),F_{2}=([n]\setminus Z_{2},y_{2})
.

Since
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are distinct and
|Z_{1}|=|Z_{2}|=t
, for
p=\operatorname{agr}_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})
we have
0\leqslant p\leqslant t-1
. Also,
\operatorname{agr}_{[n]\setminus(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2})}(y_{1},y_{2})\geqslant t-1
. Choose a set
Z\subset[n]\setminus(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2})
of
(t-1-p)
coordinates on which
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
agree, put
x=y_{1}|_{Z}=y_{2}|_{Z}
. Since
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{1}},|Z|\leqslant t-1
, we can choose
F_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{T_{1}}(T_{1})
such that it avoids
F_{2}\sqcup T_{2}
outside of
Z
. By analogy, we choose
F_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{T_{2}}(T_{2})
such that it avoids
F_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup T_{1}
outside of
Z
. Both
F_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup T_{1}
and
F_{2}^{\prime}\sqcup T_{2}
belong to
\mathcal{F}
, however

|(F_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup T_{1})\cap(F_{2}^{\prime}\sqcup T_{2})|=|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|+|Z|=t-1,

which leads to contradiction. ∎

Set
\delta_{0}=e^{-n/t^{2}}
. Recall that our goal is to show that a maximal
(t-1)
-avoiding finally should be a complete
t
-star. In order to show that, we will prove that at most one of the restrictions from
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
covers a big part of the original family. Later, we will prove that in fact this restriction is the center of a
t
-star which coincides with the original family. We are ready to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 36
.

Let
m,n,t
be such positive integers that
n\geqslant 50t^{2}
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
be
(t-1)
-avoiding. Then there are no two distinct
T_{1},T_{2}\in\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}
such that
\mu^{-T_{1}}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{1}})\geqslant\delta_{0}
and
\mu^{-T_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{2}})\geqslant\delta_{0}
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary. Put
T_{1}=(Z_{1},x_{1}),T_{2}=(Z_{2},x_{2})
. By averaging (Claim
16
), we find restrictions
y_{1}\in[m]^{Z_{2}\setminus Z_{1}}
and
y_{2}\in[m]^{Z_{1}\setminus Z_{2}}
such that

\mu_{[n]\setminus Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{1}}(Z_{2}\setminus Z_{1}\to y_{1}))\geqslant\delta_{0};\qquad\mu_{[n]\setminus Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{2}}(Z_{1}\setminus Z_{2}\to y_{2}))\geqslant\delta_{0}.

Put
\mathcal{G}_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{T_{1}}^{*}(Z_{2}\setminus Z_{1}\to y_{1}),\mathcal{G}_{2}=\mathcal{F}_{T_{2}}^{*}(Z_{1}\setminus Z_{2}\to y_{2})
. By Theorem
23
, we have

|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{1}|\geqslant\delta_{0}^{\frac{t-1}{n-|Z_{2}\cup Z_{1}|}}\cdot|\partial_{t-1}m^{[n]\setminus Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}}|=

=e^{-\frac{n(t-1)}{t^{2}(n-|Z_{2}\cup Z_{1}|)}}\cdot|\partial_{t-1}m^{[n]\setminus Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}}|>

>\frac{1}{2}{n-|Z_{2}\cup Z_{1}|\choose t-1}m^{t-1}.

The last inequality holds trivially for
t=1
. For
t\geqslant 2
we have
n/t(n-2t)\leqslant 0.6<\ln(2)
and therefore

e^{-\frac{n(t-1)}{t^{2}(n-|Z_{2}\cup Z_{1}|)}}\geqslant e^{-\frac{n}{t(n-2t)}}>e^{-\ln 2}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}.

Analogously,
|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{2}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}{n-|Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}|\choose t-1}m^{n-|Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}|}.
Since
\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{1},\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{2}\subset\partial_{t-1}[m]^{n\setminus(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2})}
, the intersection of
\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{2}
is not empty which contradicts Claim
35
.
∎

6.3
Geometrically scaling decomposition

In this section, we discuss a
(t-1)
-avoiding family
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n}
decomposed as
\mathcal{R}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}_{T},
where
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\subset\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}_{T}\subset\mathcal{F}[T]
for all
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
and
\mathcal{R}
is a relatively small remainder. We will show that there is a restriction
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
such that almost all sets of
\mathcal{F}
are contained in
\mathcal{F}_{T}\vee\{T\}.
We begin with bounding the number of parts of the decomposition depending on their measure.

Claim 37
.

Let
m,n,t
be positive integers such that
n\geqslant 50t^{2}
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\bigsqcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}_{T}
be a
(t-1)
-avoiding subfamily of
[m]^{n}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\in\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}_{T}\subset\mathcal{F}[T]
for all
T\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
. Set
\delta_{0}=e^{-n/t^{2}}
, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
put
\delta_{i}=\delta_{0}^{i+1}.
Define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{i}=\{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\;:\;\mu^{-T}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T})\in(\delta_{i},\delta_{i-1}]\}.

Then for any
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
we have
|\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{i}|\leqslant 2\delta_{i}^{-(t-1)/(n-t)}
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary. From Theorem
23
for all
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
we have

|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}|\geqslant{n-t\choose t-1}m^{t-1}\cdot\delta_{i}^{\frac{t-1}{n-t}}.

Claim
35
guarantees that the
(t-1)
-shadows of
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}
are disjoint for different
T
. Thus the following series of inequality holds.

\Bigg|\partial_{t-1}\bigcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}\Bigg|=\sum_{T\in\widetilde{T}}|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}|\geqslant

\geqslant|\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{i}}|\cdot{n-t\choose t-1}m^{t-1}\cdot\delta_{i}^{\frac{t-1}{n-t}}\geqslant 2\cdot{n-t\choose t-1}m^{t-1}>{n\choose t-1}m^{t-1}=|\partial_{t-1}[m]^{n}|.

This leads us to contradiction. Note that
2{n-t\choose t-1}>{n\choose t-1}
because

\frac{{n\choose t-1}}{{n-t\choose t-1}}=\frac{n(n-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(n-t+2)}{(n-t)\cdot\ldots\cdot(n-2t+2)}\leqslant\left(1+\frac{t}{n-2t+2}\right)^{t-1}<2,

where the last inequality is due to
n\geqslant 50t^{2}\Longrightarrow n-2t+2\geqslant 2t^{2}
.
∎

Using Claim
37
, we will show in two steps that there exists a set
T_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
such that
|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}^{*}|
is relatively small. First, in Claim
38
, we prove that the total size of families
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}
except some
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}
is at most
2\sqrt{\delta_{0}}m^{n-t}.
Then, in Claim
39
, we prove that
|\mathcal{F}\setminus\bigcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}|\leqslant m^{n-t-1/3}.

Claim 38
.

In terms of Claim
37
set
T_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
be such that
\mu^{-T_{0}}(\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}^{*})
is the biggest among
\mu^{-T}(\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}),T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
. Then

\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}|\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}|\leqslant|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}|+2m^{n-t}\sqrt{\delta_{0}}.

Proof.

Fix any
T_{0}
for which
\mu(\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}})
attains the maximum. According to Claim
37
, we have

\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}|\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}|=|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}|+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{i}}|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{i}}|\leqslant|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}|+2m^{n-t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{i}^{-(t-1)/(n-t)}\delta_{i-1}=

=|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}|+2m^{n-t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{0}^{i-\frac{(i+1)(t-1)}{n-t}}.

For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, since
n-t>6(t-1)
, the following inequalities hold:

i-\frac{(i+1)(t-1)}{n-t}\geqslant i-\frac{i+1}{6}\geqslant\frac{2i}{3}

and we can proceed estimating
\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}|\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}|
as

\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}|\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}|\leqslant|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}|+2m^{n-t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{0}^{2i/3}=|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}|+2m^{n-t}\delta_{0}^{2/3}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\delta_{0}^{2/3}}\leqslant|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}|+2m^{n-t}\sqrt{\delta_{0}}

The last inequality holds, because
\delta_{0}=e^{-n/t^{2}}<e^{-3}<0.1
.
∎

We will now bound
\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}|\mathcal{F}_{T}(T)\setminus\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}|
.

Claim 39
.

Let
m,n,t
be positive integers such that
m\geqslant(2^{22}n)^{4t},n\geqslant 50t^{2}
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\bigsqcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}_{T}
be a
(t-1)
-avoiding subfamily of
[m]^{n}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\in\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}_{T}\subset\mathcal{F}[T]
for all
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
and
|\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}|\leqslant m^{1/3}\ln m
. Then, we have

\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}|\mathcal{F}_{T}(T)\setminus\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}|\leqslant m^{n-t-1/3}.

Proof.

Recall the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}
. The sets from
\mathcal{F}_{T}(T)\setminus\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}
can be described as follows: there is a restriction
(Z,x),Z\in[n],x\in[m]^{Z},|Z|\leqslant t-1
such that the covering number of
\mathcal{F}_{T}(Z\to x)
is less than
n+1
. It also means that the size of
\mathcal{F}_{T}(T)(Z\to x)
is at most
(n+1)\cdot m^{n-t-|Z|-1}
, and we can write

\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}|\mathcal{F}_{T}(T)\setminus\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}|\leqslant|\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}|\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\in[n],|Z|\leqslant t-1\\
x\in[m]^{Z}\end{subarray}}(n+1)\cdot m^{n-t-|Z|-1}\leqslant 2n|\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}|\cdot\sum_{Z\in[n],|Z|\leqslant t-1}m^{n-t-1}\leqslant

\leqslant 2n\cdot m^{1/3}\ln m\cdot\sum_{p=0}^{t-1}{n\choose p}\cdot m^{n-t-1}\leqslant 4n^{t}\ln m\cdot m^{n-t-2/3}\leqslant m^{n-t-1/3},

(11)

where we used

m\geqslant(2^{22}n)^{3t}\cdot(m^{1/12})^{3}\geqslant(2^{22}n)^{3t}\cdot\left(\frac{\ln m}{12}\right)^{3}\geqslant 4^{3}n^{3t}\cdot\ln^{3}m=(4n^{t}\ln m)^{3}

in the last inequality.
∎

We are now ready to prove that for some
T_{0}
all but a constant factor sets from
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}\vee\{T_{0}\}
are contained in
\mathcal{F}
, i.e.
\mathcal{F}
contains a large incomplete
t
-star.

Claim 40
.

Let
m,n,t
be positive integers such that
m\geqslant(2^{22}n)^{4t},n\geqslant 50t^{2}
. Suppose that a
(t-1)
-avoiding family
\mathcal{F}\subset[m]^{n},|\mathcal{F}|\geqslant m^{n-t}
, can be decomposed into families
\mathcal{R}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}_{T}
such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\subset\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}_{T}\subset\mathcal{F}[T]
for all
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
and
|\mathcal{R}|\leqslant 2m^{n-t-1/3},|\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}|\leqslant m^{1/3}\ln m
. Then, there are
T_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
and
\delta<1/e
such that

\big|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}\vee\{T_{0}\}\big|=(1-\delta)m^{n-t}.

Proof.

The family
\mathcal{F}
can be partitioned into

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{R}\sqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}_{T}\setminus(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}\vee\{T\})\right)\sqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}\vee\{T\})\right).

(12)

According to Claim
39
, we have

\Bigg|\mathcal{R}\sqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}_{T}\setminus(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}\vee\{T\})\right)\Bigg|\leqslant|\mathcal{R}|+\Big|\bigcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{F}_{T}(T)\setminus\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}\Big|\leqslant 3m^{n-t-1/3}.

Claim
38
implies

|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant 3m^{n-t-1/3}+|\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}^{*}|+2m^{n-t}\sqrt{\delta_{0}}=|\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}^{*}|+(2\sqrt{\delta_{0}}+3m^{-1/3})m^{n-t},

where
\delta_{0}=e^{-n/t^{2}}
and
T_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
is such that
\mu^{-T_{0}}(\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}^{*})
is the biggest among
\mu^{-T}(\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}),T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
. Since
|\mathcal{F}|\geqslant m^{n-t}
, we have

|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}|\geqslant(1-2\sqrt{\delta_{0}}-3m^{-1/3})m^{n-t}.

Set
\delta>0
such that
\big|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}\vee\{T_{0}\}\big|=(1-\delta)m^{n-t}
. According to
m\geqslant(2^{22}n)^{4t}\geqslant 2^{22}
, we have
3m^{-1/3}\leqslant 1/2e
, which implies

\delta\leqslant 2\sqrt{\delta_{0}}+3m^{-1/3}\leqslant e^{-n/2t^{2}+1}+1/2e\leqslant 1/e^{2}+1/2e\leqslant 1/e.

The second to last inequality is due to
-n/2t^{2}+1\leqslant-2
.
∎

The bound
\delta\leqslant 1/e
is clearly not sufficient for our purposes, but it can be easily upgraded using the Kruskal–Katona theorem, as the following claim shows.

Claim 41
.

In terms and conditions of Claim
40
let
T_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
and
\delta<1/e
be such that

\big|\mathcal{F}\setminus(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}\vee\{T_{0}\})\big|=(1-\delta)m^{n-t}.

Then the sum of sizes of
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*},T\in\widetilde{T}\setminus\{T_{0}\}
, can be bounded as

\sum_{T\in\widetilde{T}\setminus\{T_{0}\}}|\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}|\leqslant\delta/2\cdot m^{n-t}

Proof.

Let
(Z_{0},x_{0})
be
T_{0}
written in the restriction form. By averaging (see Claim
16
), for each
T=(Z_{T},x_{T})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}
, find a restriction
y_{T}\in[m]^{Z_{0}\setminus Z_{T}}
such that for
\mathcal{G}_{T}=\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}(Z_{0}\setminus Z_{T}\to y_{T})
we have
\mu_{[n]\setminus Z_{0}\cup Z_{T}}(\mathcal{G}_{T})\geqslant\mu^{-T}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T}).
Theorem
23
implies

|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}^{*}|\geqslant(1-\delta)^{\frac{t-1}{n-t}}|\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus Z_{0}}|

Families
\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{T}\subset\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus T_{0}\cup T}
are disjoint for different
T\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}
and also disjoint from
\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T_{0}}\in\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus T_{0}}
by Claim
35
. Therefore the following inequality holds:

\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}}|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{T}|\leqslant\left(1-(1-\delta)^{\frac{t-1}{n-t}}\right)\cdot|\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus Z_{0}}|\leqslant

\leqslant\frac{t-1}{n-t}\cdot\delta|\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus Z_{0}}|<\delta|\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus Z_{0}}|.

(13)

We now use a contraposition of Theorem
23
, i.e. Corollary
24
, to bound the measure of a family via the size of its shadow. For any
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu^{-(Z\cup Z_{0})}(\mathcal{G}_{T})\leqslant\left(\frac{|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{T}|}{|\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus(Z_{T}\cup Z_{0})}|}\right)^{(n-t-|Z_{0}\setminus Z_{T}|)/(t-1)}\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{T}|}{|\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus Z_{0}}|\cdot{n-t-|Z_{0}\setminus Z_{T}|\choose t-1}/{n-t\choose t-1}}\right)^{(n-2t)/(t-1)}.

Previously, we chose the restrictions
y_{T}
in such a way that the measure of the remaining part was at least the measure of the original family. It allows us to bound the sum of measures of
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{*}
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}}\mu_{[n]\setminus Z_{T}}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{T})\leqslant\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}}\mu_{[n]\setminus Z_{T}\cup Z_{0}}(\mathcal{G}_{T})

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\max_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}}\left(\frac{{n-t\choose t-1}}{{n-t-|Z_{0}\setminus Z_{T}|\choose t-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2t}{t-1}}\cdot\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}}\left(\frac{|\partial_{t-1}\mathcal{G}_{i}|}{|\partial_{t-1}[m]^{[n]\setminus Z_{0}}|}\right)^{\frac{n-2t}{t-1}}.

(14)

We analyze these two factors separately. We have

\displaystyle\max_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\setminus\{T_{0}\}}\left(\frac{{n-t\choose t-1}}{{n-t-|Z_{0}\setminus Z_{T}|\choose t-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2t}{t-1}}

Proof of Lemma

Lemma

[

]

Step 1.
Suppose that
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are cross-agreeing. Using a certain transformation, we will transform the families
\mathcal{F}_{i}
into
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
in such a way that they remain cross-agreeing, become monotone, and have large

Finally, we will show that
\mu_{1/2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{1})+\mu_{1/2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{2})>1

One part of the transformation in Step 1 will be the following compression operator, defined in Section 4.2 of
[

]

Definition 8

For any

We also define a compression operator, also denoted

T_{i,j}(\mathcal{F})=\{x\;:\;T_{i,j}(x)\in\mathcal{F}\}\cup\{T_{i,j}(x)\;:\;x\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Another part of the transformation in Step 1 will be the transformation from compressed codes in

Definition 9

Landau theory of CeCoSi
Takayuki Ishitobi

Abstract
We investigate both the nonmagnetic and magnetic ordered phases of CeCoSi using Landau theory. Our analysis predicts that three successive phase transitions occur at zero magnetic field. In the intermediate phase, an out-of-plane magnetic moment may be present or absent. If present, magnetic fields applied along the [100] and [110] directions induce additional magnetic phases.

Identifying order parameters is a fundamental issue in condensed matter physics, yet it remains challenging in certain materials such as
{\rm URu_{2}Si_{2}}

Mydosh and Oppeneer 2014
. CeCoSi is a recent example of such a system. It crystallizes in the CeFeSi-type structure (space group
P4/mmc
, No. 129) and exhibits nonmagnetic ordering at
T_{0}=12
K and antiferromagnetic ordering at
T_{\rm N}=9.4
K
Tanida et al. 2019
. The nonmagnetic ordered phase is attributed to a
(Q_{zx},Q_{yz})
-type ferroquadrupolar order at least from the viewpoint of symmetry
Matsumura et al. 2022
;
Manago et al. 2023
, while the antiferromagnetic phase is believed to be characterized by an in-plane magnetic moment with wave vector
{\bm{q}}={\bm{0}}

Nikitin et al. 2020
.
Since an ordering of localized quadrupole is seemingly incompatible with the crystalline electric field scheme
Nikitin et al. 2020
, the quadrupolar order is considered unconventional. The nature of its order parameters and the properties of the field-induced phases remain elusive.
In this short note, we carry out a Landau theoretical analysis and construct temperature–-magnetic field phase diagrams together with corresponding order parameters, as shown in Fig.
1
.

We begin by analyzing couplings between order parameters. Based on experimental evidence, we consider the ferroquadrupolar order parameter
{\bm{Q}}=(Q_{zx},Q_{yz})
and the antiferromagnetic one
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}=(M^{\rm AF}_{x},M^{\rm AF}_{y})
. Reflecting the in-plane anisotropy
{\rm Re}[(x+iy)^{4}]=(x^{2}-y^{2})^{2}-(2xy)^{2}
of the
D_{4h}
point group, the free energy includes the following quadrupole-related anisotropic term:

\displaystyle c^{q}[(Q_{zx}^{2}-Q_{yz}^{2})^{2}-(2Q_{zx}Q_{yz})^{2}].

(1)

The sign of the coupling constant
c^{q}
determines whether the stable order is of
Q_{zx}(Q_{yz})
-type or
Q_{zx}\pm Q_{yz}
-type, with the latter being consistent with x-ray diffraction (XRD) experiments
Matsumura et al. 2022
.

Symmetry-allowed biquadratic couplings between
{\bm{Q}}
and
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}
take the form

\displaystyle c_{x^{2}-y^{2}}^{mq}[(M^{\rm AF}_{x})^{2}-(M^{\rm AF}_{y})^{2}](Q_{zx}^{2}-Q_{yz}^{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c_{xy}^{mq}(2M^{\rm AF}_{x}M^{\rm AF}_{y})(2Q_{zx}Q_{yz}).

(2)

This coupling is quadratic in
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}
, and thus dictates that the second-order phase transition from the quadrupolar to the antiferromagnetic phase must result in either
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\parallel{\bm{Q}}
or
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\perp{\bm{Q}}
. In both cases, the resulting monoclinic phase preserves mirror symmetry. Consequently, a third phase transition must occur at zero field to account for the structural phase transition to the triclinic phase observed at
T_{\rm s2}\approx 8
K by XRD measurement
Matsumura et al. 2022
. Such a phase transition arises when the sign of the tetragonal anisotropic coupling for the magnetic moment–obtained by replacing
{\bm{Q}}
with
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}
in Eq. (
1
)–is opposite to that for the quadrupole moment, thereby favoring
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\parallel{\bm{x}}
or
{\bm{y}}
. This anisotropy is consistent with the crystalline electric field scheme suggested by high-field magnetization measurements
Kanda et al. 2024
. Since the signs of the coupling constants in Eq. (
2
) cannot be determined from existing experiments, we consider both
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\parallel{\bm{Q}}
and
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\perp{\bm{Q}}
scenarios for the intermediate phase in
T_{\rm s2}<T<T_{\rm N}
.

The quadrupolar order hybridizes the in-plane and out-of-plane components of
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}
through a coupling

\displaystyle c^{qm_{z}}M_{z}^{\rm AF}(M_{x}^{\rm AF}Q_{zx}+M_{y}^{\rm AF}Q_{yz})

(3)

with typically
c^{qm_{z}}<0
. Consequently, a
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\parallel{\bm{Q}}
component induces an out-of-plane moment
M_{z}^{\rm AF}
.

Q_{ab}
denotes
aQ_{zx}+bQ_{yz}
, and
M^{\rm AF}_{abc}

aM_{x}^{\rm AF}+bM_{y}^{\rm AF}+cM_{z}^{\rm AF}
.

Next, we examine the coupling to an external magnetic field
{\bm{H}}=(H_{x},H_{y},H_{z})
. Substituting
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\rightarrow{\bm{H}}
in Eq. (
2
) yields the coupling between
{\bm{Q}}
and
{\bm{H}}
:

\displaystyle c_{x^{2}-y^{2}}^{hq}(H_{x}^{2}-H_{y}^{2})(Q_{zx}^{2}-Q_{yz}^{2})+c_{xy}^{hq}(2H_{x}H_{y})(2Q_{zx}Q_{yz}).

(4)

This coupling is quadratic in
{\bm{Q}}
and constrains the order parameter
{\bm{Q}}
below
T_{0}
to align either parallel or perpendicular to
{\bm{H}}
. Experimental results of XRD measurements suggest that both coupling constants in Eq. (
4
) are negative, thereby favoring
{\bm{Q}}\parallel{\bm{H}}

Matsumura et al. 2022
. The following observations support this: under a [010] field, the absence of splitting in the [207] reflection indicates a
Q_{yz}
-type order parameter; under a [110] field, the suppressed splitting of the [118] reflection implies a
Q_{zx}-Q_{yz}
-type order parameter. In both cases,
{\bm{Q}}\parallel{\bm{H}}
is realized.
Since the field-induced configuration
Q_{zx}
under a [100] field differs from zero-field configuration
Q_{zx}\pm Q_{yz}
, it manifests as a field-induced phase. In contrast, a field along [110] preserves the zero-field configuration, and no field-induced phase emerges, in agreement with experiments
Matsumura et al. 2022
;
Hidaka et al. 2025
.

The coupling between
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}
and
{\bm{H}}
, analogous to Eq. (
2
), is given by:

\displaystyle c_{x^{2}-y^{2}}^{mh}(H_{x}^{2}-H_{y}^{2})[(M^{\rm AF}_{x})^{2}-(M^{\rm AF}_{y})^{2}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c_{xy}^{mh}(2H_{x}H_{y})(2M^{\rm AF}_{x}M^{\rm AF}_{y}),

(5)

where the coupling constants
c_{x^{2}-y^{2}}^{mh}
and
c_{xy}^{mh}
are typically positive, stabilizing
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\perp{\bm{H}}
.

We now discuss the temperature–field phase diagrams for
{\bm{H}}\parallel
[100], [110], and [001].
For
{\bm{H}}\parallel[100]
, a field-induced
Q_{zx}
-type phase emerges at
T_{0}
, followed by a low-temperature phase with order parameter
Q_{yz}
below
T_{\rm s1}
. As this order lowers the crystal symmetry to triclinic, the zero-field transition at
T_{\rm s2}
becomes a crossover in finite fields. According to Eq. (
5
), the low-temperature high-field state stabilizes
M_{y}^{\rm AF}\perp{\bm{H}}
. This matches the zero-field order at
T_{\rm N}
only in the case
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\perp{\bm{Q}}
, while a mismatch occurs for
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\parallel{\bm{Q}}
, indicating a field-induced phase occurs. The schematic phase diagrams are shown in Fig.
1
(a, d), where the phase boundaries are drawn so that the symmetry of each phase lowers progressively with decreasing temperature. In both cases, the distinct combinations of presence or absence of
Q_{yz}
and
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}
in the order parameters lead to four distinct phases that intersect at a tetracritical point.

For
{\bm{H}}\parallel[110]
, no field-induced quadrupolar ordered phase appears. At high fields and low temperatures,
M_{x}^{\rm AF}-M_{y}^{\rm AF}\perp{\bm{H}}
is stabilized. This matches the zero-field order at
T_{\rm N}
only for
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\perp{\bm{Q}}
, resulting in a simple phase diagram as in Fig.
1
(e). If instead
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\parallel{\bm{Q}}\parallel{\bm{H}}
is realized, field-induced successive phase transitions occur to the
M_{x}^{\rm AF}-M_{y}^{\rm AF}
-type phase and the intermediate phase, yielding a tetracritical point, as in Fig.
1
(b).

A field along [001] does not affect in-plane anisotropy and thus has minimal impact on the phase diagram. However, the coupling in Eq. (
3
), with
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}
replaced by magnetization
{\bm{M}}
, induces a magnetization component
{\bm{M}}\parallel{\bm{Q}}
. The emergence of the magnetization suppresses the transition temperature of the parallel component
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\parallel{\bm{M}}
while relatively enhancing that of
{\bm{M}}^{\rm AF}\perp{\bm{M}}
. The resulting phase diagrams are shown in Figs.
1
(c, f). In Fig.
1
(c),
T_{\rm s2}
and
T_{\rm N}
are assumed not to cross, but crossing would produce the same topology as in Fig.
1
(b) under a [110] field.

In summary, we found the emergence of the third zero-field transition and possible field-induced magnetic phases. Although the third transition has not yet been identified, it corresponds to the structural transition detected by the XRD experiment at
T_{\rm s2}\approx 8
K
Matsumura et al. 2022
, which has been considered coincident with antiferromagnetic transition at
T_{\rm N}=9.4
K. The third transition should also be detectable via bulk measurements or nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR). To determine whether a
c
-axis component is present in the intermediate phase, neutron scattering, resonant x-ray scattering, or NMR under a [001] field in
T_{\rm s2}<T<T_{\rm N}
would be effective. If the
c
-axis component exists, field-induced magnetic phases are expected to emerge. These findings offer experimental guidance for elucidating the phase diagrams and the underlying order parameters of CeCoSi.

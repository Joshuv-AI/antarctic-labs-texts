Generalized affine Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities Journal: Journal of Functional Analysis
Youjiang Lin, Jinghong Zhou, Jiaming Lan

Abstract
We establishe an affine Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality concerning two different functions which is stronger than the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality. Furthermore, we also prove reverse inequalities for the new inequalities for log-concave functions. 2000 AMS subject classification: 26D15 (26B25, 26D10, 46E35, 52A40)



Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality
[
21
]
states that for
p,r>1
,
0<\alpha<n
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\alpha}{n}=1
, the following inequality holds for functions
f\in{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
h\in{L}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

\displaystyle C(n,\alpha,p){||f||}_{p}{||h||}_{r}\geq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)h(y)}{{|x-y|}^{n-\alpha}}dxdy\right|,

(1)

where
C(n,\alpha,p)
is a sharp constant independent of
f
and
h
and satisfies

\displaystyle C(n,\alpha,p)\leq\frac{n}{\alpha}\omega_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\frac{1}{pr}\left({\left(\frac{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}{1-1/p}\right)}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}+{\left(\frac{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}{1-1/r}\right)}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\right),

(2)

where
\omega_{n}
represents the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In the special case where
p=r=2n/(n+\alpha)
, the constant
C(n,\alpha,p)
simplifies to:

\displaystyle C(n,\alpha,p)=C(n,\alpha)={\pi}^{(n-\alpha)/2}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}{\Gamma((n+\alpha)/2)}{\left\{\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma(n)}\right\}}^{-\alpha/n},

where
\Gamma
is the gamma function.
Equality in (
1
) holds if and only if
h=a_{0}f
and
f(x)=a(b^{2}+|x-x_{0}|^{2})^{-\frac{n+\alpha}{2}}
for some
a_{0}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\neq b\in\mathbb{R}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The Hardy–Littlewood-Sobolev inequality was initially introduced by G. Hardy and J. Littlewood in
\mathbb{R}^{1}

[

]

[

For the general case, neither the optimal constant nor the optimizers are known. For more detailed information on the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality, we can refer to the monograph by E. Lieb and M. Loss

J. Haddad and M. Ludwig
[

established sharp affine Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities for

\displaystyle\gamma_{n,\alpha}\left\|f\right\|_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\left(x\right)f\left(x+t\xi\right)dxdt\right)^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}

(3)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f\left(x\right)f\left(y\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy.

Equality holds in the first inequality if and only if the function

Here, the integral over the unit sphere

1.1
Star bodies and dual mixed volumes

A set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
convex
if
\left(1-\lambda\right)x+\lambda y\in C
holds for all
x
,
y\in C
and
\lambda\in\left[0,1\right]
. A
convex body
is a compact convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a non-empty interior.

A closed set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
star-shaped
(with respect to the origin) if the interval
\left[0,x\right]\subseteq K
for every
x\in K
.
The
radial function

\rho_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to[0,\infty]
associated with a star-shaped set
K
is defined by

\displaystyle{\rho}_{K}(x)=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\lambda x\in K\}

and the
gauge function

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\left[0,\infty\right]
is given by

\displaystyle{||\cdot||}_{K}=\inf\{\lambda>0:x\in\lambda K\}.

Two star-shaped sets
K
and
L
are said to be dilates if their radial functions satisfy
{\rho}_{K}=c{\rho}_{L}
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
for some
c\geq 0
.

We call
K
a
star body
if its radial function is strictly positive and continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
We have
{||\cdot||}_{B^{n}}=|\cdot|
, where
B^{n}
is
n
-dimensional unit ball. Let
|K|
denote
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
K
. For a star-shaped set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a measurable radial function, the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
K
satisfies

|K|=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{\rho}_{K}(\xi)^{n}d\xi.

(14)

Let
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and
\alpha\neq n
. For star-shaped sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with measurable radial functions, the
dual mixed volume
is defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}\left(K,L\right)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}\left(\xi\right)^{n-\alpha}\rho_{L}\left(\xi\right)^{\alpha}d\xi.

(15)

When
K=L
in (
15
), we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}\left(K,K\right)=|K|.

The concept of dual mixed volume for star bodies was introduced by E. Lutwak
[
27
]
, who also established the dual mixed volume inequalities (
16
) and (
17
) as consequences of Hölder’s inequality. For
0<\alpha<n
, the dual mixed volume inequality associated with star-shaped sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite volume shows that

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,L)\leq|K|^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}|L|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}.

(16)

For
\alpha>n
, the dual mixed volume inequality associated with star-shaped sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
shows that

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,L)\geq|K|^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}|L|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}.

(17)

The equality condition in Hölder’s inequality implies that equalities in (
16
) and (
17
) for finite
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}\left(K,L\right)
are attained if and only if
K
and
L
are are dilates. For further details regarding dual mixed volumes, refer to
[
8
,
34
]
.

1.2
Log-concave and
s
-concave functions

For
s>0
, a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\left[0,\infty\right)
is called
s
-concave
if

\displaystyle f\left(\left(1-\lambda\right)x+\lambda y\right)^{s}\geq\left(1-\lambda\right)f\left(x\right)^{s}+\lambda f\left(y\right)^{s}

(18)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. Note that if
s>0
, then
f^{s}
is concave, and in particular,
1
-concave is just concave in the usual sense. If
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
, inequality (
18
) becomes

\displaystyle f\left(\left(1-\lambda\right)x+\lambda y\right)\geq f\left(x\right)^{1-\lambda}f\left(y\right)^{\lambda}

(19)

which implies
f
is
log-concave
.
For
f,h\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, the
convolution
of
f,h
is defined by

\displaystyle f\ast h\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\left(x-y\right)h\left(y\right)dy,\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The following result is a consequence of the Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality, see
[
9
, Theorem 11.3]
.

Lemma 1
.

For
s\geq 0
. If
f,h\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
are s-concave, then their convolution is
s/(ns+2)
-concave.

1.3
Symmetrization

For a subset
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
indicator function

1_{E}
is characterized by
1_{E}\left(x\right)=1
when
x\in E
and
1_{E}\left(x\right)=0
for all
x\notin E
. Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set with finite measure. The
Schwarz symmetral
of
E
, denoted by
E^{\star}
, is defined as a closed Euclidean ball centered at the origin, having the same volume as
E
.
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative measurable function such that for any
t>0
, the super-level set
\left\{f\geq t\right\}
has finite measure. The
layer cake formula
asserts that

\displaystyle f\left(x\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\left\{f\geq t\right\}}\left(x\right)dt

(20)

holds for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
Schwarz symmetrization

f^{\star}
of
f
is defined as

\displaystyle f^{\star}\left(x\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\left\{f\geq t\right\}^{\star}}(x)dt

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consequently,
f^{\star}
is characterized almost everywhere by being radially symmetric and possessing superlevel sets whose measures coincide with those of
f
. Note that
f^{\star}
is also called to the
symmetric decreasing rearrangement
of
f
. If for all
x
,
y
with
|x|<|y|
, the inequality
f^{\star}(x)>f^{\star}(y)
holds, we say that
f^{\star}
is
strictly symmetric decreasing
.

The proofs of our results rely on the Riesz rearrangement inequality (see, for example,
[
21
, Theorem 3.7]
).

Theorem 4
.

(Riesz’s rearrangement inequality). For
f,g,k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

(31)

In the special case where

and

(32)

and

Lemma 9
.

Let

,

Proof.

For

30

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1_{E}(x)1_{F}(y)}{{\left\|x-y\right\|}_{K}^{n-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\int_{F}\int_{E}\frac{1}{{\left\|x-y\right\|}_{K}^{n-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\int_{F}\int_{E-y}\frac{1}{{{\left\|z\right\|}_{K}^{n-\alpha}}}dzdy

\bigtriangleup
an

For a fixed

6.3
Results for s-concave functions

Theorem 8
.

Let
B(\cdot,\cdot)
denote Beta function and
s>0
. For non-zero, even and
s
-concave functions
f
,
h\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, the following inclusion relation holds:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\left(n+\frac{2}{s}\right)B\left(\beta+1,n+\frac{2}{s}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}R_{\beta}\left(f,h\right)\subseteq\frac{1}{\left(\left(n+\frac{2}{s}\right)B\left(\alpha+1,n+\frac{2}{s}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}R_{\alpha}\left(f,h\right)

for
0<\alpha<\beta<\infty
.

Proof.

Since
s
-concave function is log-concave function, as in the proof of Theorem
7
, we have that the function
g\left(y\right)=\mathscr{G}\left(f,h\right)\left(y\right)
is even, continuous, and achieves its maximum at
y=0
. For any
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we observe that the function
t\longmapsto g\left(t\xi\right)
is positive and decreasing. Moreover, according to Lemma
1
, the function
g
possesses
r
-concavity where
r=\frac{s}{ns+2}
. By Lemma
12
with
\omega\left(t\right)=g\left(0\right)\left(1-rt\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}_{+}
and
\varphi\left(t\right)=\left(1-\left(g\left(t\xi\right)/g\left(0\right)\right)^{r}\right)/r
, and by (
5
), (
56
) and (
49
), we have

\displaystyle\zeta\left(\alpha\right)=\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}g\left(t\xi\right)dt}{\int_{0}^{\infty}g\left(0\right)t^{\alpha-1}\left(1-rt\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}_{+}dt}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\frac{r\rho_{R_{\alpha\left(f,h\right)}}\left(\xi\right)}{\left(\alpha B\left(\alpha,1+\frac{1}{r}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}

for
\alpha>0
. The conclusion follows directly from Lemma
12
.
∎

For the limit as
s\to 0
, Theorem
8
reduces to Theorem
7
. In the case where
s\to\infty

and and
f=1_{E}
,
h=1_{F}
are indicator functions of convex bodies
F\subseteq E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Theorem
8
yields the inclusion

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(nB\left(\beta+1,n\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}R_{\beta}\left(E,F\right)\subseteq\frac{1}{\left(nB\left(\alpha+1,n\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}R_{\alpha}\left(E,F\right)

(57)

for
0<\alpha<\beta
. When
F=E
, this result coincides with Theorem 5.5 of R. Gardner and G. Zhang
[
10
]
, who established that equality in (
57
) holds for
n
-dimensional simplices.
From the proof of Theorem
8
, under the stated assumptions, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{R_{\alpha}\left(f,h\right)}\left(\xi\right)}{\left(\left(n+\frac{2}{s}\right)B\left(\beta+1,n+\frac{2}{s}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}\leq\frac{\rho_{R_{\alpha}\left(f,h\right)}\left(\xi\right)}{\left(\left(n+\frac{2}{s}\right)B\left(\alpha+1,n+\frac{2}{s}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}

(58)

with equality for
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
when
f\left(x\right)=h\left(x\right)
and
\mathscr{G}\left(f,h\right)\left(t\xi\right)=a\left(1-\lambda t\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}_{+}
for

, where
a\geq 0
and

. The subsequent lemma demonstrates that the inequality in (

.

Lemma 14

[
14

For

Corollary 1
.

For

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left\|f\right\|_{1}\left\|h\right\|_{1}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\left(x\right)h\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}

(59)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\left\|{f}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{2n}}{h}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{2n}}\right\|}_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}{\left\|{h}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{2n}}{f}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{2n}}\right\|}_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}},

\displaystyle\frac{\left(nB\left(n,n+1+\frac{2}{s}\right)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{B\left(\alpha,n+1+\frac{2}{s}\right)}\left|S_{\alpha}\left(f,h\right)\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}

\displaystyle\left(\left\|f\right\|_{1}\left\|h\right\|_{1}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\left(x\right)h\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}

\displaystyle{\left\|{f}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{2n}}{h}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{2n}}\right\|}_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}{\left\|{h}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{2n}}{f}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{2n}}\right\|}_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}.

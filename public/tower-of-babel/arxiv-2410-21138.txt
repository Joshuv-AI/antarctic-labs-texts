Eigenvalue bounds for the Steklov problem on differential forms in warped product manifolds
Tirumala Chakradhar

Abstract
. We consider the Steklov problem on differential p p -forms defined by M. Karpukhin and present geometric eigenvalue bounds in the setting of warped product manifolds in various scenarios. In particular, we obtain Escobar type lower bounds for warped product manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature and strictly convex boundary, and certain sharp bounds for hypersurfaces of revolution, among others. We compare and contrast the behaviour with known results in the case of functions (i.e., 0 0 -forms), highlighting the influence of the underlying topology on the spectrum for p p -forms in general. Résumé. Nous considérons le problème de Steklov sur les formes différentielles de degré p p , tel que défini par M. Karpukhin, et présentons des bornes géométriques des valeurs propres dans le cadre des variétés en produit déformé dans divers scénarios. En particulier, nous obtenons des bornes inférieures de type Escobar pour les variétés en produit déformé ayant une courbure de Ricci non négative et une frontière strictement convexe, ainsi que certaines bornes serrées pour les hypersurfaces de révolution, entre autres. Nous comparons et contrastons le comportement avec les résultats connus dans le cas des fonctions (c’est-à-dire des 0 0 -formes), en mettant en évidence l’influence de la topologie sous-jacente sur le spectre des p p -formes en général.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact, connected, smooth
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with boundary, and
\Delta\coloneqq~-\text{div}(\text{grad})
be the positive Laplacian on
M
. We have the Steklov eigenvalue problem:

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0\qquad&\text{in }M,\\
\partial_{\mathpzc{n}}u=\sigma u\qquad&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

where
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
and
\sigma
is the spectral parameter. Here,
\partial_{\mathpzc{n}}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to the unit outward normal vector field
{\mathpzc{n}}
along the boundary. The Steklov problem is known to have a discrete spectrum
0=\sigma_{0}<\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2}\leq\dots\nearrow+\infty
, with finite multiplicities. It is often formulated in terms of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DtN) map
\mathcal{D}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\partial M)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\partial M)
given by
u\mapsto\partial_{\mathpzc{n}}\widetilde{u}
, where
\widetilde{u}
is the unique harmonic extension of
u
to
M
. It is an elliptic, self-adjoint pesudo-differential operator whose spectrum coincides with the Steklov spectrum. Introduced by Vladimir Steklov
[
Ste02
]
in 1902, the Steklov problem arises in various interesting contexts such as the sloshing problem for liquids, Electrical Impedance Tomography, as well as the study of minimal surfaces. See
[
GP17
,
CGGS24
]
for a detailed survey of the recent advances in understanding the spectral geometry of the Steklov problem.

In this article, we focus on geometric upper and lower bounds for the eigenvalues of the Steklov problem on differential forms. There are various versions of Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators on
p
-forms in the literature
[
JL05
,
BS08
,
RS12
,
SS13
,
Kar19
]
, with the ones defined by Raulot-Savo
[
RS12
]
and Karpukhin
[
Kar19
]
among those that drew attention recently. See
[
RS14
,
GKLP22
,
Xio24
,
Mic19
,
YY17
,
Kwo16
]
for some interesting geometric eigenvalue estimates for these operators.

The Hodge Laplacian generalises the notion of Laplacian on smooth functions to the framework of differential
p
-forms on the manifold. The spectral data associated with the Hodge Laplacian, such as that of the absolute eigenvalue problem, the relative eigenvalue problem, etc., capture important geometric and topological properties of the underlying manifold. It turns out that a number of characteristic properties of various eigenvalue problems for functions (i.e.,
0
-forms) do not generalise for arbitrary
p\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
,
n
being the dimension of the manifold. They show significantly different behavior, where the role of the topology of the manifold is much more eminent in addition to the differential structure. See
[
CC90
]
for a nice demonstration of this phenomenon. While the classical eigenvalue problems involving differential forms are well-studied, the Steklov problem on forms is relatively less explored, with many significant questions waiting to be investigated.

In the case of functions, the recent articles
[
CGG19
,
CV21
,
Xio21
,
Xio22
,
Tsc24
,
BCG25
,
BC24
]
study various eigenvalue bounds for the Steklov problem among warped product manifolds, which are an interesting class of manifolds that are widely used in the literature to gain valuable insights into the behaviour of the spectrum, owing to certain simplifications in computing the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. We generalise some of these estimates to the setting of the Steklov problem on
p
-forms defined by Karpukhin, adapting their proofs. As we shall see, a striking feature in some of our results is the contrasting behaviour of eigenvalue bounds for small and large values of
p
, and, for even dimensional manifolds, an isospectrality with the Euclidean ball when
p=\frac{n-2}{2}
. We now motivate and describe our main results. See Section
2
on preliminaries for the required definitions and the notation.

We assume that
(M^{n},g)
is a compact, connected, orientable, smooth Riemannian manifold with boundary, and that
p\in\{0,1,\dots,n-2\}
, in addition to any other restrictions on
p
stated in various contexts. Let
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}
(resp.
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k)}
) denote the
k^{\text{th}}

nonzero
eigenvalue of Karpukhin’s DtN operator
\Lambda^{(p)}
on coclosed
p
-forms on
{\partial M}
, counted with (resp. without) multiplicity. For
p=0
, i.e., for functions,
0
is always an eigenvalue of
\Lambda^{(p)}
, so
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{1}=\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(1)}
denotes the second smallest eigenvalue of
\Lambda^{(p)}
. In contrast, for
p\in\{1,\dots,n-2\}
, the manifolds that we study in this article (each connected component of whose boundary is the unit round sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, up to scaling) have
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{1}=\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(1)}
as the smallest eigenvalue as the kernel of
\Lambda^{(p)}
turns out to be trivial for topological reasons.

Escobar
[
Esc99
]
conjectured that for a manifold
(M,g)
with the Ricci curvature,
\ric_{g}\geq 0
and strictly convex boundary (i.e., with a positive lower bound, say
\kappa
, on the principal curvature of
{\partial M}
), the first nonzero Steklov eigenvalue for functions satisfies

\bm{\sigma}^{(0)}_{1}\geq\kappa,

with the equality holding if and only if
M
is the Euclidean ball of radius
\kappa^{-1}
. While the conjecture remains open, some partial results are known in the literature. See
[
Xio22
]
for a brief survey, and
[
XX24
]
for the proof of a weaker version of the Escobar conjecture by replacing
\ric_{g}\geq 0
with
\sect_{g}\geq 0
(where
\sect_{g}
denotes the sectional curvature). Xiong
[
Xio22
, Theorem 2]
proved certain Escobar type lower bounds for the
m^{\text{th}}
nonzero Steklov eigenvalue for functions, that for warped product manifolds (see Section
2.3
) satisfying Escobar’s hypotheses of
\ric\geq 0
and the principal curvature of the boundary,
\kappa>0
, the following lower bounds are satisfied.

\bm{\sigma}^{(0)}_{(m)}\geq m\kappa,\qquad\forall\,m\in\mathbb{N},

with the equality holding only for the Euclidean ball of radius
\kappa^{-1}
.

Remark 1.1
.

It follows from the results of Ichida
[
Ich81
]
and Kasue
[
Kas83
]
that if a compact, connected manifold has
\ric\geq 0
and a strictly convex boundary, then the boundary is connected. Also, note that the principal curvature is constant on each connected component of the boundary for warped product manifolds.

We generalise the above result of Xiong to the Steklov problem on
p
-forms in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a warped product manifold as in Section
2.3
(
n\geq 2
), such that
\ric_{g}\geq 0
and it has a strictly convex boundary. If
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is the principal curvature of
{\partial M}
, then, for
p\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, the
m^{\text{th}}
nonzero Steklov eigenvalue for
p
-forms has the lower bound

(1.1)

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(m)}(M)\geq(m+p)\kappa,\qquad\forall\,m\in\mathbb{N},

with the equality holding for a given
m\in\mathbb{N}
if and only if
M
is the Euclidean ball of radius
\kappa^{-1}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Analogous to
[
Xio22
, Theorem 4]
, we may instead use the hypothesis of
\ric_{g}(M)\leq 0
and strictly convex boundary to get a sharp upper bound for
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(m)}(M)
given by the same expression on the RHS of inequality (
1.1
), provided that
{\partial M}
is connected as this is not implied by the hypothesis, unlike in Theorem
1.2
.

Xiong
[
Xio24
]
extended the above mentioned weaker version of the Escobar conjecture for functions due to Xia and Xiong
[
XX24
]
to the setting of
p
-forms, that for each
p\in\{0,1,\dots,n-2\}
, a manifold with strictly convex boundary,
\sect_{g}\geq 0
and the Weitzenböck curvature
\wz^{(p+1)}_{g}\geq 0
, has the following lower bound for its first nonzero Steklov eigenvalue.

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{1}\geq(1+p)\kappa,

with the equality holding for the Euclidean ball of radius
\kappa^{-1}
. Here,
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
denotes the infimum of the principal curvature of
{\partial M}
. Note that, when restricted to the class of warped product manifolds, our sharp lower bound in Theorem
1.2
coincides with this, under a weaker hypothesis of nonnegative Ricci curvature. In addition, we also have rigidity for the equality case in Theorem
1.2
, which is not known for the result in
[
Xio24
]
. Further, it would be interesting to see if we can replace our hypothesis on the Ricci curvature and strictly convex boundary with some assumptions on the Weitzenböck curvature and the
p
-curvatures (see, e.g.,
[
RS12
]
) to get meaningful bounds, preferably with the restrictions on
p
in Theorem
1.2
removed.

With similar hypotheses on Ricci curvature and convexity of the boundary, Xiong
[
Xio21
]
also proved optimal upper and lower bounds for the eigenvalue gaps and the ratios of successive eigenvalues for functions. In a more recent work, Brisson, Colbois and Gittins
[
BCG25
, Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.5 & 1.7]
considered warped product manifolds with connected boundary with no further hypothesis on the curvature or the convexity of the boundary and obtained upper bounds for the eigenvalue gaps and the ratios in the case of functions, in terms of the gaps and ratios for the corresponding Laplace eigenvalues of the Euclidean unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. We show that analogous results hold for
p
-forms in general.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a topological ball with revolution-type metric, i.e., a warped product manifold as in Section
2.3
, with connected boundary. Then,

(i)

For
n\geq 3
and
p\in\{0,\dots,n-2\}
,

\frac{\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k+1)}(M)}{\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k)}(M)}<\frac{\lambda^{(p)}_{(k+1)}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}{\lambda^{(p)}_{(k)}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}=\frac{(k+p+1)(n+k-p-1)}{(k+p)(n+k-p-2)},\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N},

and, if
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
, this upper bound is sharp. Here,
\{\lambda^{(p)}_{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the positive eigenvalues of the Hodge Laplacian acting on the space of coclosed forms
\text{cc}\Omega^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, counted without multiplicity.

(ii)

When
n\geq 2
is even and
p=\frac{n-2}{2}
,

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k)}(M)=\frac{\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k)}({\mathbb{B}^{n}})}{h_{0}}=\frac{k+p}{h_{0}},

where
h_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is the value of the associated warping function on
{\partial M}
. Here,
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
denotes the Euclidean unit ball.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a topological ball with revolution-type metric. Then,

(i)

For
n\geq 4
and
p\leq\frac{n-4}{2}
, if the associated warping function satisfies
h(r)\leq C
on
r\in[0,R]
for a constant
C>0
,

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k+1)}(M)-\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k)}(M)\leq\frac{RC^{n-2p-3}}{h_{0}^{n-2p-1}}\big(\lambda^{(p)}_{(k+1)}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})-\lambda^{(p)}_{(k)}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})\big).

(ii)

For
n\geq 3
odd and
p=\frac{n-3}{2}
,

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k+1)}(M)-\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k)}(M)\leq\frac{R}{h_{0}^{n-2p-1}}\big(\lambda^{(p)}_{(k+1)}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})-\lambda^{(p)}_{(k)}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})\big).

Further, it follows that a uniform lower bound on
h_{0}
would give uniform upper bounds for the spectral gaps above, independent of the metric/warping function. As before,
h_{0}
here denotes the value of the warping function on
{\partial M}
.

We now consider hypersurfaces of revolution, described in Section
2.3
. The warping function associated with this class of warped product manifolds satisfies certain additional conditions (see, e.g., Section
5
) that can be utilised in the study of eigenvalues. Colbois, Girouard and Gittins
[
CGG19
]
proved various upper and lower bounds for the Steklov eigenvalues for functions, among the class of hypersurfaces of revolution. We present certain generalisations to the setting of
p
-forms, with contrasting behaviour for different values of
p
.

The following theorem considers the scenario of a connected boundary
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and states that the Steklov eigenvalues are bounded from below by those of the Euclidean unit ball, for
p
small enough. More precisely,

Theorem 1.6
.

(cf.
[
CGG19
, Theorem 1.8]
) Let
M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a hypersurface of revolution with boundary
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\times\{0\}
.

(i)

For
n\geq 3
and
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
,

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\geq\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}({\mathbb{B}^{n}}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N},

with the equality holding if and only if
M
is the Euclidean unit ball
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\times\{0\}
.

(ii)

For
n\geq 2
even and
p=\frac{n-2}{2}
,
M
is isospectral to the Euclidean unit ball:

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)=\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}({\mathbb{B}^{n}}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

When there are two boundary components each of which is isometric to
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, we prove, for sufficiently large meridian length, sharp lower bounds for the Steklov eigenvalues for
p
-forms in terms of the eigenvalues of the Euclidean ball. For small meridian lengths, we have non-sharp lower bounds that depend on the meridian length, when
p
is small enough. In addition to these results which generalise the previously known lower bounds in the case of functions, we further have, for small meridian lengths, upper bounds when
p
is large enough.

Theorem 1.7
.

(cf.
[
CGG19
, Theorem 1.11]
) Let
M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a hypersurface of revolution (
n\geq 3
) with boundary
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\times\{0,d\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
L
be the intrinsic distance between the boundary components (meridian length).

(i)

If
L\geq 2
, then for each
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2},

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\geq\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}({\mathbb{B}^{n}}\sqcup{\mathbb{B}^{n}}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N},

and the inequality is sharp.

(ii)

When
0<L\leq 2
:

(a)

If
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
, then

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\geq\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

(b)

If
p\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
, then

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\leq\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

Here,
C_{L}
denotes the standard cylinder
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\times[0,L]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.8
in Section
5
also provides the (unordered) set of eigenvalues
\{\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in terms of the coclosed Hodge Laplace eigenvalues of
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and the meridian length.

We also have the following non-sharp upper bounds for the Steklov eigenvalues for
p
-forms in terms of the eigenvalues of standard cylinders and the meridian length, for
p
small enough. We further have new types of lower bounds when
p
is large enough, which are not applicable for the case of functions.

Theorem 1.8
.

(cf.
[
CGG19
, Proposition 3.3]
) Let
M^{n}
be as in Theorem
1.7
. For any
L>0
,

(i)

If
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
, then

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\leq\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

(ii)

If
p\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
, then

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\geq\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

The RHS goes to
0
as
L\to 0
in each case.

An interesting corollary to Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
would be the isospectrality with the standard cylinder when
p=\frac{n-1}{2}
, for sufficiently small meridian length.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
M^{n}
be as in Theorem
1.7
. If
0<L<2
, then we have, for
p=\frac{n-1}{2}
,

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)=\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

The results for
p
-forms in this article clearly depend on the value of
p
. For instance, the methods used to obtain lower bounds for a certain value of
p
might instead give upper bounds for a different value of
p
, and vice versa. As we shall see in the later sections, this behaviour is attributed to the presence of terms such as
h^{n-2p-1}
and
h^{n-2p-3}
in the Rayleigh quotients on warped product manifolds, where
h
is the warping function. It is natural to ask what happens for the cases of
p
that our results do not cover. Of particular interest is the case
p=\frac{n-2}{2}
where we get certain isospectrality in some cases (e.g., Theorem
1.6
), but the nature of the bounds remains unknown in the other cases where we cannot directly compare the Rayleigh quotients involved using the bounds on the warping functions. See, for example, Remark
5.1
. On the other hand, one can also investigate if similar eigenvalue bounds can be obtained for the Steklov problem of Raulot-Savo, for which the present methods are not directly applicable due to the presence of an additional term in the Rayleigh quotient involving the codifferential. Note that, in view of comparison (
2.2
), some of the upper bounds obtained for
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}
in this article would also serve as upper bounds for
\sigma^{(p)}_{k}
, the
k^{th}
positive eigenvalue of the Steklov problem of Raulot-Savo (described in Section
2.2
).

This article is organised as follows. In Section
2
on preliminaries, we introduce the Steklov eigenvalue problem on
p
-forms and describe warped product manifolds, after fixing some notation. The remaining sections deal with the proofs of the main results. In Section
3
, we show that the Steklov eigenforms on warped product manifolds with connected boundary can be characterised using certain separation of variables and associate with it an ODE involving the warping function. We then analyse the ODE using the hypotheses on the Ricci curvature and the boundary convexity to obtain the sharp lower bounds of Theorem
1.2
. In Section
4
, we prove the upper bounds of Theorems
1.4
&
1.5
for the spectral gaps and the ratios by using the special structure of the Rayleigh quotients for the Steklov eigenforms for warped products and considering appropriate test forms in the variational characterisation. In Section
5
, we prove the geometric upper and lower bounds of Theorems
1.6
-
1.8
by comparing the warping functions of hypersurfaces of revolution with those of the Euclidean ball and the standard cylinder, which directly influence the behaviour of the corresponding Rayleigh quotients. We emphasise the role of
p
in determining whether these comparisons give rise to upper bounds or lower bounds for
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}
in different scenarios.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout this article,
(M^{n},g)
denotes a smooth
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with non-empty boundary
\partial M
. We assume that
M
is orientable, compact and connected.

Let
\Omega^{p}(M)
denote the space of smooth differential
p
-forms on
M
. We use the standard notation
\langle{.},{.}\rangle
to denote the natural pointwise inner product of two differential forms given by the underlying Riemannian metric, and the notation
|\alpha|
to mean
\langle{\alpha},{\alpha}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Let
\langle\hskip-3.0pt\langle{.},{.}\rangle\hskip-3.0pt\rangle
denote the
L^{2}
-inner product of square integrable forms defined by
\langle\hskip-3.0pt\langle{\alpha},{\beta}\rangle\hskip-3.0pt\rangle_{L^{2}\Omega^{p}(M)}\coloneqq\int_{M}\langle{\alpha},{\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}
, where
\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}
is the Riemannian volume form on
M
. For
p\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
, let
\star:\Omega^{p}(M)\to\Omega^{n-p}(M)
denote the Hodge star operator defined by the condition that
\alpha\wedge\star\omega=\langle{\alpha},{\omega}\rangle\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}
for all
\alpha\in\Omega^{p}(M)
. For
p\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
, let
d:\Omega^{p}(M)\to\Omega^{p+1}(M)
denote the exterior derivative and for
p\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, let
\delta:\Omega^{p}(M)\to\Omega^{p-1}(M)
be the codifferential operator given by
\delta\omega=(-1)^{np+n+1}\star d(\star\omega)
. The Hodge-Laplacian
\Delta^{(p)}:\Omega^{p}(M)\to\Omega^{p}(M)
is defined to be
\Delta^{(p)}\coloneqq d\delta+\delta d
, where we use the convention
\delta d\omega=0
for
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(M)
and
d\delta\omega=0
for
\omega\in\Omega^{0}(M)
. Note that
\Delta^{(0)}
corresponds to the usual Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
defined to be
\Delta^{(0)}f=-\text{div}(\text{grad}f)
. We often drop the superscript
{(p)}
in
\Delta^{(p)}
when clear from the context.

For
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
, let
\textbf{t}\omega
be the tangential part of
\omega
on
\partial M
, i.e.,
\textbf{t}\omega(X_{1},\dots,X_{p})=\omega(X_{1}^{\parallel},\dots,X_{p}^{\parallel})
and
\textbf{n}\omega
be the normal part of
\omega
on
\partial M
, i.e.,
\textbf{n}\omega(X_{1},\dots,X_{p})=\omega(X_{1}^{\perp},\dots,X_{p}^{\perp})
. Here,
X^{\parallel}
and
X^{\perp}
denote the components of a vector field
X
on
M
, tangential and normal to
\partial M
, respectively. Closely related to
\textbf{t}\omega
and
\textbf{n}\omega
are the forms
i^{*}\omega
and
i_{\mathpzc{n}}\omega
, which are defined to be the pull back of
\omega
under the inclusion map
i:\partial M\to M
, and the interior product of
\omega
with respect to the outer unit normal vector field
{\mathpzc{n}}
, respectively. We refer to
[
Sch95
, Section 1.2]
for more details on the basic properties of the operators defined above, and their expressions in terms of the covariant derivatives of
p
-forms.

We also use various Sobolev spaces in the framework of
p
-forms. It is somewhat technical to introduce them rigorously and we refer to
[
Sch95
, Section 1.3]
for a detailed description of the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,s}\Omega^{p}(M)
defined in terms of generalised covariant derivatives. We use the notation
L^{s}\Omega^{p}(M)
for the space
W^{0,s}\Omega^{p}(M)
. For
s=2
, we have a natural Hilbert space, which we denote by
H^{k}\Omega^{p}(M)
.

2.2.
Steklov problem on differential forms

Analogous to the case of functions, one can consider a DtN operator that maps
\omega\in\Omega^{p}({\partial M})
to
i_{\mathpzc{n}}d\widetilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{p}({\partial M})
, where
\widetilde{\omega}
is a harmonic extension. However, in contrast to the case of functions, the harmonic extension of a boundary
p
-form to
M
is not unique in general for
p\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
. Further conditions are imposed to obtain a well-defined spectral problem, which can be done in various ways. The choice of the additional condition is often motivated by the Hodge decomposition and the following observation that is of relevance in using the weak formulations. For
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{p}(M)
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle{\Delta\alpha},{\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}=\int_{M}\langle{d\delta\alpha},{\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}+\int_{M}\langle{\delta d\alpha},{\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\langle{d\alpha},{d\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}+\int_{M}\langle{\delta\alpha},{\delta\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}+\int_{\partial M}\langle{i^{*}\delta\alpha},{i_{\mathpzc{n}}\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306pt\partial M}-\int_{\partial M}\langle{i_{\mathpzc{n}}d\alpha},{i^{*}\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306pt\partial M}.

This is easy to see using the Green’s identity: For
\alpha\in\Omega^{p}(M)
and
\beta\in\Omega^{p+1}(M)
,

(2.1)

\int_{M}\langle{d\alpha},{\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}-\int_{M}\langle{\alpha},{\delta\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}=\int_{\partial M}\langle{i^{*}\alpha},{i_{\mathpzc{n}}\beta}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306pt\partial M}.

Hodge-Morrey-Friedrichs decomposition
[
Sch95
, Section 2.4]
:
For
p\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
the space
\Omega^{p}(M)
can be written as the
\langle\hskip-3.0pt\langle{},{}\rangle\hskip-3.0pt\rangle_{L^{2}\Omega^{p}(M)}
-orthogonal decomposition of exact forms, coexact forms and harmonic fields, with appropriate boundary conditions as below:

\Omega^{p}(M)=\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{p}(M)\oplus\overline{c\mathcal{E}}^{p}(M)\oplus\mathcal{H}^{p}(M),

where

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{p}(M)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M):\exists\,\alpha\in\Omega^{p-1}(M)\text{ such that }\omega=d\alpha,i^{*}\alpha=0\},

\displaystyle\overline{c\mathcal{E}}^{p}(M)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M):\exists\,\alpha\in\Omega^{p+1}(M)\text{ such that }\omega=\delta\alpha,i_{\mathpzc{n}}\alpha=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{p}(M)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M):d\omega=\delta\omega=0\}.

We now state the Steklov problem on forms that we study in this article.

Karpukhin’s Steklov problem
[
Kar19
]
:
For
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
,

\begin{cases}\Delta^{(p)}\omega=0\qquad&\text{in }M\\
\delta\omega=0\qquad&\text{in }M\\
i_{\mathpzc{n}}d\omega=\sigma\,i^{*}\omega\qquad&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

where
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
is the spectral parameter. Equivalently, the DtN operator is defined as follows.

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(p)}:\,

\displaystyle\Omega^{p}(\partial M)\to\Omega^{p}(\partial M)

\displaystyle\omega\mapsto i_{\mathpzc{n}}d\widetilde{\omega},

where
\widetilde{\omega}
is an extension of
\omega
to
M
satisfying the conditions
\Delta\widetilde{\omega}=0
and
\delta\widetilde{\omega}=0
in
M
. Here, the extension is unique up to an element of the space of normal harmonic fields

\mathcal{H}_{D}\Omega^{p}(M)\coloneqq\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M):d\omega=0,\,\delta\omega=0,\,\textbf{t}\omega=0\},

and it can be shown that this operator is well-defined. See
[
Kar19
, Section 2.3]
. In this article, we refer to the unique extension of
\omega
orthogonal to
\mathcal{H}_{D}\Omega^{p}(M)
to be (Karpukhin’s) Steklov eigenform associated with
\omega
.

Remark 2.1
.

It turns out that
\Lambda^{(n-1)}\equiv 0
on
\Omega^{n-1}(\partial M)
. See
[
Kar19
, Remark 2.6]
. Henceforth in this article, it is understood that
p\in\{0,1,\dots,n-2\}
, along with any other assumptions on
p
being stated.

The operator
\Lambda^{(p)}
is zero on the space of exact forms
\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(\partial M):\omega=d\alpha\text{ for }\alpha\in\Omega^{p-1}(\partial M)\}
. When restricted to the space of coclosed forms
\text{cc}\Omega^{p}({\partial M})\coloneqq\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(\partial M):\delta\omega=0\}
, it has a discrete spectrum

0=\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{0,1}=\dots=\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{0,{\dagger}}<\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{1}\leq\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{2}\leq\dots\nearrow+\infty

with finite multiplicities, where
{\dagger}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
denotes the dimension of the kernel of
\Gamma^{(p)}
on
\text{cc}\Omega^{p}({\partial M})
. The variational characterisation is given by

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\subset\text{cc}\Omega^{p}(M)\\
\dim(V)=k+{\dagger}\end{subarray}}\,\max_{0\neq\omega\in V}\frac{\int_{M}|d\omega|^{2}\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}}{\int_{\partial M}|i^{*}\omega|^{2}\,\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306pt\partial M}},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}.

The space
\text{cc}\Omega^{p}(M)
in the above expression may also be replaced by the Sobolev space
H^{1}\text{cc}\Omega^{p}(M)
, which is often useful. The kernel of
\Lambda^{(p)}|_{\text{cc}\Omega^{p}(M)}
is equal to the space of coclosed
p
-forms on
\partial M
that are restrictions of the tangential harmonic fields,

\mathcal{H}_{N}\Omega^{p}(M)\coloneqq\{\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M):d\omega=0,\,\delta\omega=0,\,\textbf{n}\omega=0\},

to the boundary. The dimension of
\text{ker}\,\Lambda^{(p)}|_{\text{cc}\Omega^{p}(M)}
is given by

\dim\text{ker}\,\Lambda^{(p)}|_{\text{cc}\Omega^{p}(M)}=\dim\text{im}\{i^{*}:H^{p}(M)\to H^{p}({\partial M})\}.

Henceforth,
\Lambda^{(p)}
is assumed to mean its restriction to
\text{cc}\Omega^{p}({\partial M})
.

The following version of the Steklov problem on
p
-forms is also of interest in the study of geometric eigenvalue bounds. For this version, we may relax the assumption that
M
is orientable.

Raulot-Savo’s Steklov problem
[
RS12
]
:
For
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
,

\begin{cases}\Delta^{(p)}\omega=0\qquad&\text{in }M\\
i_{\mathpzc{n}}\omega=0\qquad&\text{on }\partial M\\
i_{\mathpzc{n}}d\omega=\sigma\,i^{*}\omega\qquad&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

where
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
is the spectral parameter. Equivalently, the DtN operator is defined as follows.

\displaystyle T^{(p)}:\,

\displaystyle\Omega^{p}(\partial M)\to\Omega^{p}(\partial M)

\displaystyle\omega\mapsto i_{\mathpzc{n}}d\widetilde{\omega},

where
\widetilde{\omega}
is the unique extension of
\omega
to
M
satisfying the conditions
\Delta\widetilde{\omega}=0
in
M
and
i_{\mathpzc{n}}\widetilde{\omega}=0
on
\partial M
. The pseudo-elliptic, self-adjoint operator
T^{(p)}
has a discrete spectrum

0=\sigma^{(p)}_{0,1}=\dots=\sigma^{(p)}_{0,\natural}<\sigma^{(p)}_{1}\leq\sigma^{(p)}_{2}\leq\dots\nearrow+\infty

with finite multiplicities, where
\natural\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
denotes the dimension of the kernel of
T^{(p)}
. The variational characterisation is given by

\sigma^{(p)}_{k}(M)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\subset\{\alpha\in\Omega^{p}(M):i_{\mathpzc{n}}\alpha=0\}\\
\dim(V)=k+\natural\end{subarray}}\,\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq\omega\in V\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{M}(|d\omega|^{2}+|\delta\omega|^{2})\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}}{\int_{\partial M}|i^{*}\omega|^{2}\,\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306pt\partial M}},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}.

It is interesting to note that the kernel of
T^{(p)}
is isomorphic to the absolute de Rham cohomology of
M
, and thus has its dimension equal to the
p
-th absolute Betti number of
M
.

Remark 2.2
.

We have the following comparison
[
Kar19
, Theorem 2.5]
for the
k^{\text{th}}
positive eigenvalues of
T^{(p)}
and
\Lambda^{(p)}
:

(2.2)

\sigma^{(p)}_{k}\leq\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k},\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

2.3.
Warped product manifolds

We consider warped product manifolds of the form
[0,R]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with the Riemannian metric

g=dr^{2}\oplus h(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},

for a smooth positive function
h(r)
defined on
[0,R]
. With slight abuse of notation, this also includes manifolds with connected boundary by allowing
h(R)=0
, in which case we impose an additional hypothesis on
h
to ensure the smoothness of the metric, given by Assumption (A) below.

Assumption (A):
h\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,R])
,
h(r)>0
for
r\in[0,R)
,
h(R)=0
,
h^{\prime}(R)=-1
and all even order derivatives of
h
are zero at
r=R
.

Observe, for instance, that the warping function
h(r)=R-r
corresponds to
M
being a Euclidean ball, while
h(r)\equiv 1
corresponds to the standard cylinder. We refer the interested reader to
[
Pet16
, Section 4.2]
for a general discussion on warped product manifolds and their significance. For the warped product manifolds being considered, the boundary is totally umbilical, with the corresponding principal curvatures of
\{0\}\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and
\{R\}\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
given by
-\frac{h^{\prime}(0)}{h(0)}
and
\frac{h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)}
, respectively. Similarly, one can calculate the sectional and Ricci curvatures of
M
in terms of the warping function
h
(see, for example,
[
Xio22
]
).

A manifold
M
is said to have strictly convex boundary if all the principal curvatures are strictly positive at each point on
{\partial M}
. This terminology is used in Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 2.3
.

Recall that the dimension of
\ker\Lambda^{(p)}
is equal to
\dim\text{im}\{i^{*}:H^{p}(M)\to H^{p}({\partial M})\}
. Since we have
{\partial M}
to be topologically
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
or
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sqcup\,{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
(with trivial cohomology for
p\in\{1,\dots,n-2\}
), it follows that, for
p\neq 0
,
\ker\Lambda^{(p)}=0
and the first eigenvalue is strictly positive, in contrast to the case of functions where the first Steklov eigenvalue is always
0
.

Further, as we shall see, separation of variables in the case of warped products allows us to study the spectrum indexed without counting the multiplicities.

Notation 2.4
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(k)}
denote the
k^{\text{th}}
positive eigenvalue of
\Lambda^{(p)}
counted
without
multiplicity.

We now introduce hypersurfaces of revolution, which are an important family of warped products and the setting in which we study Steklov eigenvalue bounds in Theorems
1.6
-
1.8
. Throughout this article, a manifold
M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is said to be a hypersurface of revolution if it can be parametrised by a map,

\displaystyle\psi:{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\times[0,L]\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

\displaystyle(x,r)\mapsto h(r)x+z(r)e_{n+1},

where
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
;
h,z
are smooth functions on
[0,L]
satisfying
h(0)=1,\,h(r)>0
for
r\in[0,L)
and such that the curve
r\mapsto h(r)x+z(r)e_{n+1}
is parametrised by its arclength. Unless stated otherwise, we refer to the induced metric on
M
from
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},g_{Euc})
, which can be written as a warped product described above, with
h
as the warping function. When the boundary is connected, we have, in addition, that
h(L)=0
(as part of Assumption (A), with

replaced by

). See
[
CGG19
, Section 3.1]
for details.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

, with connected boundary. Let

\begin{cases}\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)+(n-2p-1)\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{h(r)}\psi^{\prime}(r)-\frac{\psi(r)}{h(r)^{2}}\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}=0,\qquad r\in(0,R],\\
\psi(0)=0.\end{cases}

The corresponding DtN eigenvalue is given by
\frac{\psi^{\prime}(R)}{\psi(R)}

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(m)}

In
[

]

were computed for the Euclidean unit ball, treating it as a warped product with

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

\Lambda^{(p)}
. Recall that
\Lambda^{(p)}

\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\coloneqq(m+p)(n+m-p-2)

of homogenous

whose interior product with the radial vector field in

is zero.

We first use separation of variables to derive certain conditions for a

(3.1)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega=0.

For

to be a

-eigenform, we require that

\displaystyle\Delta^{(p)}_{M}\varphi=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\delta_{M}\varphi=0,

(3.5)

\displaystyle i_{\mathpzc{n}}d_{M}\varphi=\sigma i^{*}\varphi,

where

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(r)}{h(r)^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega(x)-\Big[\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)+(n-2p-1)\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{h(r)}\psi^{\prime}(r)\Big]\omega(x)-2dr\wedge\psi(r)\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{h(r)^{3}}\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega(x)=0,

\displaystyle i_{\partial_{r}}(dr\wedge(\psi^{\prime}(r)\omega(x))+\psi(r)d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega(x))|_{r=R}=\sigma\,\psi(R)\omega(x).

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(r)}{h(r)^{2}}\lambda-\Big[\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)+(n-2p-1)\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{h(r)}\psi^{\prime}(r)\Big]=0\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\sigma=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(R)}{\psi(R)}.

Remark 3.2
.

We have from
[

that for a

Lemma 3.3

If
\varphi\in\Omega^{p}(M)
such that
\varphi(r,x)=\psi(r)\omega(x)
for some
\omega\in\Omega^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,R])

\langle{d_{M}\varphi},{d_{M}\varphi}\rangle_{M}=\frac{\psi(r)^{2}}{h(r)^{2p+2}}\langle{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega},{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega}\rangle_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}+\frac{(\psi^{\prime}(r))^{2}}{h(r)^{2p}}\langle{\omega},{\omega}\rangle_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}},

where
\langle{},{}\rangle_{M}
,
\langle{},{}\rangle_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}
denote the pointwise inner products with respect to
M
,
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, and
d_{M}
,
d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
denote the exterior derivatives with respect to
M

{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\langle{d_{M}\varphi},{d_{M}\varphi}\rangle_{M}

\displaystyle=\big\langle\psi(r)d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega+\psi^{\prime}(r)dr\wedge\omega\,,\,\psi(r)d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega+\psi^{\prime}(r)dr\wedge\omega\big\rangle_{M}

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}=\psi(r)^{2}\langle{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega},{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega}\rangle_{M}+(\psi^{\prime}(r))^{2}\langle{dr\wedge\omega},{dr\wedge\omega}\rangle_{M}\\
+2\psi(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)\langle{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega},{dr\wedge\omega}\rangle_{M}.\end{multlined}

For the warped product metric
g=dr^{2}\oplus h(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
on

\displaystyle\langle{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega},{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega}\rangle_{M}=\frac{1}{h(r)^{2p+2}}\langle{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega},{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega}\rangle_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}},

\displaystyle\langle{dr\wedge\omega},{dr\wedge\omega}\rangle_{M}=\frac{1}{h(r)^{2p}}\langle{\omega},{\omega}\rangle_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\langle{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega},{dr\wedge\omega}\rangle_{M}=0.

The lemma follows.

\displaystyle=0.

Then we see that,

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{1}=\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{k}^{2}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle M$}}$}}\nolimits\langle{d\varphi_{k}},{d\varphi_{k}}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}}{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{k}^{2}}.

Using Lemma
3.3
and Green’s identity (

),

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle{d\varphi_{k}},{d\varphi_{k}}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}=\int_{0}^{R}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle{d\varphi_{k}},{d\varphi_{k}}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.19579pt{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\Big)\,h(r)^{n-1}\dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\psi_{k}(r)^{2}}{h(r)^{2p+2}}\langle{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega_{k}},{d_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega_{k}}\rangle+\frac{(\psi_{k}^{\prime}(r))^{2}}{h(r)^{2p}}\langle{\omega_{k}},{\omega_{k}}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.19579pt{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\Big)\,h(r)^{n-1}\dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\Big(\frac{\psi_{k}(r)^{2}}{h(r)^{2p+2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle{\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega_{k}},{\omega_{k}}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.19579pt{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}+\frac{(\psi_{k}^{\prime}(r))^{2}}{h(r)^{2p}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle{\omega_{k}},{\omega_{k}}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.19579pt{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\Big)\,h(r)^{n-1}\dr

(as
\omega_{k}
are chosen to be orthonormal)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\Big(\lambda^{(p)}_{(m(k))}\frac{\psi_{k}(r)^{2}}{h(r)^{2}}+(\psi_{k}^{\prime}(r))^{2}\Big)h(r)^{n-2p-1}\,\dr.

Hence,

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{1}=\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{k}^{2}\int_{0}^{R}\big[\lambda^{(p)}_{(m(k))}\frac{\psi_{k}(r)^{2}}{h(r)^{2}}+(\psi_{k}^{\prime}(r))^{2}\big]h(r)^{n-2p-1}\,\dr}{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{k}^{2}}

Since each term in the numerator is strictly increasing in
\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}
, and
\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}
is strictly increasing in
m
, any increase in
m
will result in a value greater than
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{1}
. This argument can be repeated for higher eigenvalues, and we conclude that the eigenspace associated with
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(m)}
is the same as the eigenspace of
\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}
. It is also implied that
\psi_{k}
is the same for each eigenform in the eigenspace for
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{(m)}
. Finally, the claim that
\bm{\sigma}_{(m)}^{(p)}=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(R)}{\psi(R)}
follows from equation (

).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In view of Proposition
3.1
, it will suffice to show that

(3.9)

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(R)}{\psi(R)}\geq(m+p)\frac{h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)},\qquad\text{for $\psi$ corresponding to $\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}$,}

as the principal curvature of
{\partial M}
is given by
\frac{h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)}
.
To analyse the solution
\psi
to ODE (
3.6
), we begin by rewriting the ODE as

\frac{1}{h(r)^{n-2p-1}}\frac{d}{dr}\big(h(r)^{n-2p-1}\psi^{\prime}(r)\big)-\frac{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\psi(r)}{h(r)^{2}}=0,

and integrate, for
p\leq\frac{n-2}{2}
, to get

h(r)^{n-2p-1}\psi^{\prime}(r)=\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\int_{0}^{r}h(t)^{n-2p-3}\psi(t)\,\dt.

Assuming without loss of generality that
\psi
is strictly positive in a small neighbourhood of
r=0
, we see that
\psi^{\prime}(r)>0
and
\psi(r)>0
on
(0,R]
. Further, we calculate
\psi^{\prime}(0)
.

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\int_{0}^{r}h(t)^{n-2p-3}\psi(t)\,\dt}{h(r)^{n-2p-1}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{h(r)^{n-2p-3}\psi(r)}{(n-2p-1)h(r)^{n-2p-2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}}{(n-2p-1)}\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\psi(r)}{h(r)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}}{(n-2p-1)}\psi^{\prime}(0),\qquad\text{(as $h^{\prime}(0)=1$ by Assumption~(A)).}

It follows that
\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
for
p\leq\frac{n-2}{2}
. When
p=\frac{n-1}{2}
, the ODE reads as

\psi^{\prime\prime}=\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\frac{\psi}{h^{2}}.

So,

\psi^{\prime\prime}(0)=\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\psi(r)}{h(r)^{2}}=\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{2h^{\prime}(r)h(r)}=\frac{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}}{2}\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{h(r)}.

Since
\psi^{\prime\prime}(0)
is finite,
\psi^{\prime}(0)
has to be
0
because
h(0)=0
.

As a consequence of the hypothesis that
\ric_{g}\geq 0
and
{\partial M}
is strictly convex, the warping function satisfies (see
[
Xio22
, Lemma 8]
)

(3.10)

h^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq 0\quad\text{and}\quad 0<h^{\prime}(r)\leq 1,\qquad\text{for}\,\,\,r\in[0,R].

To prove inequality (
3.9
), consider the function

q(r)\coloneqq h(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)-(m+p)h^{\prime}(r)\psi(r)\qquad\text{on $[0,R]$}.

Firstly, we have
q(0)=0
, since
h(0)=0
and
\psi(0)=0
. Next,

\displaystyle q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=h^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}+h\psi^{\prime\prime}-(m+p)h^{\prime\prime}\psi-(m+p)h^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq h\psi^{\prime\prime}+(1-m-p)h^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}\qquad\text{(using $h^{\prime\prime}\leq 0$ and $\psi\geq 0$)}

\displaystyle=:u(r).

Since
\psi
satisfies the ODE (
3.6
), we get

u=\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\frac{\psi}{h}-\frac{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}}{m+p}h^{\prime}\psi^{\prime},

where
\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}=(m+p)(n+m-p-2)
.
Using
\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
,

u(0)=\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\Big(1-\frac{1}{m+p}\Big)\psi^{\prime}(0)=0.

Further,

\displaystyle\frac{u^{\prime}}{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{h}-\frac{\psi h^{\prime}}{h^{2}}-\frac{1}{m+p}h^{\prime\prime}\psi^{\prime}-\frac{1}{m+p}h^{\prime}\psi^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{h}-\frac{\psi h^{\prime}}{h^{2}}-\frac{h^{\prime}}{(m+p)h}\Big(\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\frac{\psi}{h}-(n-2p-1)h^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{h}+(n-2p-1)\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{(m+p)h}\Big)\psi^{\prime}-\big(1+\frac{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}}{m+p}\big)\frac{h^{\prime}\psi}{h^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{h}+(n-2p-1)\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{(m+p)h}\Big)\frac{m+p}{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}h^{\prime}}\big(\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}\frac{\psi}{h}-u\big)-\big(1+\frac{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}}{m+p}\big)\frac{h^{\prime}\psi}{h^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m+p}{h^{2}h^{\prime}}(1-(h^{\prime})^{2})\psi-\Big(\frac{1}{h}+(n-2p-1)\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{(m+p)h}\Big)\frac{m+p}{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}h^{\prime}}u

(3.11)

\displaystyle\geq-\Bigg[\frac{1}{h}+(n-2p-1)\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{(m+p)h}\Bigg]\frac{m+p}{\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}h^{\prime}}u.

Since
p\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, the term in the square brackets above is nonnegative. Recall that
u(0)=0
. Therefore, if
u(a)<0
for some

, then there exists

such that
u(b)<0
and
u^{\prime}(b)<0
, contradicting the inequality (
3.11
). Hence, we have that
u\geq 0
on
[0,R]
. Consequently,
q^{\prime}\geq 0
on
[0,R]
, with
q(0)=0
, giving that
q\geq 0
on
[0,R]
. In particular,
q(R)\geq 0
. Thus, we get the required lower bounds.

It is evident from
[
Kar19
, Theorem 8.1]
that the equality is attained for the Euclidean ball of radius
R
. Furthermore, when the equality holds, i.e.,
q(R)=0
, it follows that

. Then we have

\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}=(m+p)\frac{h^{\prime}(r)}{h(r)},\qquad\forall\,r\in[0,R].

We can use this to calculate

\psi^{\prime\prime}=(m+p)\Big[\frac{\psi^{\prime}h^{\prime}}{h}+\frac{\psi}{h^{2}}(hh^{\prime\prime}-(h^{\prime})^{2})\Big].

Using these two equations in the ODE (

) and simplifying, we get that

(m+p)hh^{\prime\prime}+\lambda^{(p)}_{(m)}((h^{\prime})^{2}-1)=0.

But since

and

. Thus, we conclude that

for all

Remark 5.1
.

We used the assumption that

Before proceeding to the proofs of upper and lower bounds for hypersurfaces of revolution with two boundary components, we briefly discuss separation of variables for the Steklov eigenforms of warped product manifolds with two boundary components as Proposition
3.1
is not applicable in this case. Consider the space of coclosed

\omega_{m},&\text{on }{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\times\{L\}.\end{cases}

One can see that this subspace is preserved by the operator
\Lambda^{(p)}
. Hence, the self-adjoint operator
\Lambda^{(p)}
restricted to this finite dimensional space will have two real eigenvalues, say,
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{m,1}

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{m,2}

. Thus, any Steklov eigenform

\phi(r,x)=\psi(r)\omega_{m}(x)
for appropriate

. Note that it is not possible, in general, to obtain a useful ordering of the spectrum given by the set
\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\{\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{m,1},\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{m,2}\}
, unlike the case of warped product manifolds with connected boundary considered in Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

(i)
When
L\geq 2
:

Let
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
. We modify the proof from the case of connected boundary by instead taking the restrictions of eigenforms at both ends of the manifold, i.e, to the regions corresponding to
[0,1]\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and
[L-1,L]\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. We require that
L\geq 2
for these strips to be disjoint up to a measure zero set. Further, we would have in this case that
1-r\leq h(r)\leq 1+r
and
h(L)-r\leq h(L-r)\leq h(L)+r
for all
r\in(0,L)
, as described in
[
CGG19
, Section 3.1]
. Let
\{\omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal eigenbasis corresponding to
\{\lambda^{(p)}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, the spectrum of Hodge Laplacian acting on the space of coclosed forms on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. If
\phi(r,x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\alpha_{ji}(r)\omega_{j}(x)
is a coclosed eigen
p
-form on
M
, where
\{\alpha_{ji}\}
are scaled such that
\int_{{\partial M}}\langle{i^{*}\phi},{i^{*}\phi}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306pt{\partial M}}=1
, we observe that the Rayleigh quotients of
\phi
with respect to
M
(with the metric
dr^{2}\oplus h(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
) and
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\sqcup{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
(with the metric
dr^{2}\oplus(1-r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
on
[0,1]\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and the metric
dr^{2}\oplus(r-L+1)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
on
[L-1,L]\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
) satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{M}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\langle{d\phi},{d\phi}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306ptM}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}h^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}h^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{1}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}(1-r)^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}(1-r)^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr\\
\qquad+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{L-1}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}(r-L+1)^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}(r-L+1)^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\sqcup\,{\mathbb{B}^{n}}}(\phi).

Now, using the min-max characterisation (and Remark
3.2
) as in the proof of Theorem
1.6
but with the test forms being the eigenforms of
M
restricted to
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\sqcup{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
corresponding to
r\in[0,1]
and
r\in[L-1,L]
, we get the required lower bound
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\geq\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}({\mathbb{B}^{n}}\sqcup{\mathbb{B}^{n}})
. The article
[
CGG19
]
also describes the construction of a sequence of hypersurfaces of revolution to establish that the inequality is sharp in the case of functions, which can be adapted to the framework of
p
-forms with straightforward modifications.

(ii)
When
L\leq 2
:

(a)
Let
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
. With
\phi
as before and noting that
h(r)\geq 1-\frac{L}{2}
(see
[
CGG19
, Section 3]
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{M}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}h^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}h^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr

\displaystyle\geq\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}\,\,\dr

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\mathcal{R}_{C_{L}}(\phi).

The required lower bound follows from the min-max characterisation of eigenvalues (and Remark
3.2
) as before, using appropriate Steklov eigenforms of
M
as test forms on
C_{L}
.

(b)
Let
p\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
. Note that the exponents

(n-2p-1)
and
(n-2p-3)
associated with the warping function
h
in the above expressions are now nonpositive, hence the inequality between the Rayleigh quotients is reversed,

\mathcal{R}_{M}(\phi)\leq\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\mathcal{R}_{C_{L}}(\phi).

To get the upper bounds, we instead use eigenforms of
C_{L}
as test forms for the manifold
M
. Let
\mathscr{E}_{k}
be the span of eigenforms corresponding to
\{\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{i}(C_{L})\}_{i=1}^{k}
. Then the min-max characterisation (along with Remark
3.2
) gives

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\leq\max_{0\neq\varphi\in\mathscr{E}_{k}}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\varphi)\leq\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\max_{0\neq\varphi\in\mathscr{E}_{k}}\mathcal{R}_{C_{L}}(\varphi)=\Big(1-\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L}).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

(i)
When
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
:

As observed in
[
CGG19
, Proposition 3.3]
, the warping function satisfies
h(r)\leq 1+\frac{L}{2}
for all
r\in[0,L]
. Let
\varphi_{l}(r,x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=1,2}\alpha_{ji}(r)\omega_{j}(x)
be an eigenform corresponding to
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{l}(C_{L})
, normalised by
\int_{{\partial M}}\langle{i^{*}\varphi_{l}},{i^{*}\varphi_{l}}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306pt{\partial M}}=1
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{M}(\varphi_{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}h^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}h^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr

(we use
p\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}\,\,\dr

\displaystyle=\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{l}(C_{L}).

Hence, for any
\phi\in\mathscr{E}_{k}\coloneqq\text{Span}(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k})
, the following inequality holds,

\mathcal{R}_{M}(\varphi)\leq\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L}).

Finally, we have from the min-max characterisation, and Remark
3.2
, that

\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M)\leq\max_{0\neq\varphi\in\mathscr{E}_{k}}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\varphi)\leq\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L}).

(ii)
When
p\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
:

This time, we use appropriate Steklov eigenforms of
M
as test forms on
C_{L}
. Let
\varphi_{l}(r,x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\alpha_{ji}(r)\omega_{j}(x)
be an eigenform corresponding to
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{l}(M)
, normalised as usual by

\int_{{\partial M}}\langle{i^{*}\varphi_{l}},{i^{*}\varphi_{l}}\rangle\,\dv_{\hskip-1.99306pt{\partial M}}=1.

The
p
-forms
\varphi_{l}
, defined on
[0,L]\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, may also be viewed as
p
-forms on the standard cylinder
C_{L}
with the metric
dr^{2}\oplus g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, with the associated Rayleigh quotients
\mathcal{R}_{C_{L}}(\varphi_{l})
. Then,

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\mathcal{R}_{C_{L}}(\varphi_{l})

\displaystyle=\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}\,\,\dr

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr

(we use
p\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N};\,i=1,2}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha_{ji}^{\prime})^{2}h^{n-2p-1}+\alpha_{ji}^{2}\lambda^{(p)}_{j}h^{n-2p-3}\,\,\dr

\displaystyle=\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{l}(M).

Hence, for any
\phi\in\mathpzc{E}_{k}\coloneqq\text{Span}(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k})
, the following inequality holds,

\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\mathcal{R}_{C_{L}}(\varphi)\leq\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M).

We again have from the min-max characterisation, and Remark
3.2
, as usual, that

\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L})\leq\Big(1+\frac{L}{2}\Big)^{n-2p-1}\max_{0\neq\varphi\in\mathpzc{E}_{k}}\mathcal{R}_{C_{L}}(\varphi)\leq\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(M).

Analogous to
[
CGG19
, Lemma 2]
, it is possible to express the Steklov eigenvalues of
C_{L}
for
p
-forms in terms of the coclosed eigenvalues of the Hodge Laplacian on the boundary and the meridian length
L
. We do not know the order in general but the spectrum is given by the set

\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda^{(p)}_{k}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\tanh\Big(\sqrt{\lambda^{(p)}_{k}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\frac{L}{2}\Big),\sqrt{\lambda^{(p)}_{k}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\coth\Big(\sqrt{\lambda^{(p)}_{k}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\frac{L}{2}\Big)\right\}.

Thus, we see that
\bm{\sigma}^{(p)}_{k}(C_{L})\to 0
as
L\to 0
.
∎

Protocol for culturing porcine retinal explants and RNA isolation
Authors: Siavash Khosravi, Christoph Reuss, Margit Ried, Nicole Ernst, Julia Frankenhauser, Tanja Schoenberger, Alec Dick, Nina Zippel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe culture of porcine retinal explants serves as a valuable cellular model for studying retinal pathologies and their underlying molecular mechanisms. Here, we present a protocol for culturing porcine retinal explants, isolating RNA, and RNA sequencing. We describe steps for dissecting the eye, isolating the retina, and culturing primary porcine retinal explants for extended periods. Additionally, we outline procedures for RNA isolation and sequencing from cultured retinal explants, enabling molecular analysis of retinal biology.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Khosravi et al.1Subject areas: Cell culture, RNAseq, Gene Expression

Before you begin

This protocol provides detailed steps for isolating and preparing porcine retinal explants, culturing them for up to ten days, isolating RNA, and performing bulk RNA sequencing from porcine eyes. While the protocol is primarily designed for RNA sequencing, it can be adapted for other applications, such as immunostaining and functional analyses.2,3 Here, we demonstrate that porcine retinal explants can be cultured for up to ten days, with RNA successfully extracted and sequenced to study differential gene expression (Figure 1). Enucleated Porcine eyes were obtained from an abattoir, where pigs slaughtered and enucleated five to four hours, before dissecting eyes. The eyes were collected shortly after slaughter, stored, and transported in ice-cold saline (0.9% NaCl) buffer.2

Innovation

Existing porcine retinal explant protocols focus primarily on morphological preservation or pharmacological assessment, but none offer a fully integrated workflow that connects tissue dissection directly to transcriptomic analysis. This protocol bridges that gap by combining a standardized eye dissection workflow, consistent 4 mm biopsy-punch sampling, and an optimized tissue grinding procedure for RNA extraction, with a co-culture strategy in which the neural retina and RPE/choroid are maintained on the same insert without direct physical contact—preserving paracrine signaling between tissue compartments. RNA is extracted at high quality and processed through bulk mRNA sequencing using a scalable, high-replicate experimental design. The result is a reliable end-to-end pipeline that enables robust differential gene expression analysis across a 10-day culture period.

Preparation, dissection, and punching of retinal explants

(A and B) Carefully remove extraocular tissues using fine scissors. Trim away any excess tissue to expose the globe.

(C) The trimmed eye should be free of surrounding connective tissue.

(D) Make a precise incision just below the ciliary muscle at the level of the ora serrata.

(E and F) Using scissors, gently open the eye along the incision.

(G) Remove and discard the cornea, ciliary muscle, lens, and vitreous humor to isolate the eye cup.

(H) The intact eye cup should remain after removal of internal structures.

(I and J) Cut open the eye cup using scissors to expose the retina.

(K and L) Using a biopsy punch, carefully excise the retinal explant.

(M and N) Transfer the retinal explant onto a drop of pre-warmed culture media to maintain tissue viability.

(O) Place one retinal explant and one RPE punch side-by-side onto a single culture insert.

(P) Gently remove the media drop from the membrane to ensure proper adherence of the tissue to the insert.

Expected outcomes

This protocol enables the collection of multiple retinal punches from porcine eyeballs and supports their culture for up to 10 days. Our data show that RNA extraction can yield up to 3 μg of high-quality RNA from a 4 mm retinal explant, suitable for bulk RNA sequencing. In our study, we were able to detect 14278 unique genes in our retinal explants.

Limitations

There is inherent biological variability among porcine eyes obtained from different animals, which can lead to differences in RNA yield and quality. To minimize the impact of this variability, it is recommended to include high number of biological replicates. In this study, we used eight biological replicates (eight eyes) per time point, along with two technical replicates (two punches) from each eye.

Despite optimization of culture conditions, retinal explant tissues undergo progressive neurodegeneration and cell death. For instance, previous studies have reported a time-dependent reduction in the number of retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) in retinal explant cultures [ˆ9], thereby limiting their suitability for long-term in vitro applications.

Abstract
The conservation of C ​ P CP in QCD has been shown to follow from a careful treatment of the path integral and canonical quantization in Refs. [ 1 , 3 ] . Here, we refute the critique of these results put forth in Ref. [ 6 ] . First, using the quantum rotor as an analogue of QCD, it is argued in Ref. [ 6 ] that the topological susceptibility vanishes when using the limiting procedure of Ref. [ 1 ] . When translated to QCD, this would contradict the observed η ′ \eta^{\prime} -mass. We show that this is not the case because the susceptibility is defined from the vacuum correlator of the topological charge density, which for the rotor is just fixed by the canonical commutation relation. The latter does not depend on the disputed order of limits. Second, it is suggested in Ref. [ 6 ] that C ​ P CP violation in QCD can be established by considering the low-energy effective theory alone. We show that here the argument relies on assuming from the start choices of couplings that lead to C ​ P CP violation but are not of the most general form allowed by spurion analysis. No valid reason is given for why allowed choices leading to C ​ P CP conservation, that match the computation of ultraviolet correlators as shown in Refs. [ 1 , 3 ] , would be inconsistent.

Summary and context.

In Refs.
[

The above conclusions are criticized in Ref.

\eta^{\prime}

[

[
5

However, for the rotor model from Ref.

[
6

[

6

Second, regarding the effective chiral Lagrangian for the meson field matrix
U

In a Euclidean path integral setting with a time integration interval given by

\displaystyle F(\tau,\tau^{\prime};\beta):=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\tau}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\tau^{\prime}}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime};\beta)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}m}\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\beta m}+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{\phi_{+}-\phi_{-}}{\beta}\right)^{2}\,.

\displaystyle\chi=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}{\tau}\,F(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}m}\,.

(6)

Note that one can identify

However, it is a general feature of effective field theories that there is no equivalence between parameters in the fundamental theory and their corresponding version in the effective theory. When integrating out fields across thresholds, the parameters in the effective theory absorb quantum corrections, and their value is fixed not by matching with the ultraviolet (UV) Lagrangian, but by matching correlators computed with both the fundamental and effective theories. This generically leads to departures between fundamental and effective parameters. The Lagrangian of Eq. (
15
) assumes an exact matching between the
\theta
-term in the UV theory and the corresponding term in the effective theory, while the explicit matching of correlators in Ref.
[
1
]
shows that this assumption is invalid.

While we go with the assumption that the total derivative term
G^{\mu\nu}\widetilde{G}_{\mu\nu}
can be traded for a scalar auxiliary field
q(x)
, presumably because of
nonperturbative quantum effects,
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,q(x)
(where
\Omega
indicates the full volume) should still respect the global topological constraints. In particular,
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,q(x)
in each topological
sector should be exactly invariant under continuous deformations of the fields and therefore should also remain invariant when summing over sectors. Without further ado, using
q(x)
as an unconstrained auxiliary field would hence clash with these global constraints because
q(x)
would admit fluctuations that do not occur in the fundamental theory. In the effective chiral action one should therefore replace
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,q(x)
with some localized version that is not subject to topological constraints. This could be achieved for example by deriving an
effective theory in a large but finite subvolume
\Omega_{1}
of the infinite volume
\Omega
. Such a theory can be obtained by integrating out fluctuations in the volume complement
\Omega_{2}\equiv\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1}
. In the subvolume
\Omega_{1}
there are no physical constraints on the boundary conditions, and
\int_{\Omega_{1}}\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,q(x)
can fluctuate freely. Such an effective theory is derived in Ref.
[
1
]
, and it leads to correlators of operators with support in
\Omega_{1}
of the following form (in the general basis for the chiral phases):

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}_{1}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits_{\Delta n_{1}=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\Delta n_{1}}{\cal D}\phi\,{{(-1)^{-N_{f}\Delta n_{1}}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\,\bar{\alpha}\Delta n_{1}}}}{\cal O}_{1}\,{\rm e}^{-S_{\Omega_{1}}[\phi]}}{\sum\limits_{\Delta n_{1}=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\Delta n_{1}}{\cal D}\phi\,{{(-1)^{-N_{f}\Delta n_{1}}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\,\bar{\alpha}\Delta n_{1}}}}{\rm e}^{-S_{\Omega_{1}}[\phi]}}\,,

(20)

where
\Delta n_{1}=\int_{\Omega_{1}}{\rm}d^{4}x\,q(x)
is the topological charge within
\Omega_{1}

Here,
\theta
is absent, and the phase factors
(-1)^{-N_{f}\Delta n_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bar{\alpha}\Delta n_{1}}
exactly cancel the
CP

\Omega_{1}

\bar{\theta}

20

-{\rm i}\bar{\alpha}\Delta n_{1}

\theta

\xi=-\bar{\alpha}

\Omega_{2}

Sphere intersections and incidences over finite fields
Doowon Koh, Ben Lund, Chuandong Xu, Semin Yoo

Abstract
We bound the number of incidences between points and spheres in finite vector spaces by bounding the sum of the number of points in the pairwise intersections of the spheres. We obtain new incidence bounds that are interesting when the number of spheres is not too large. Our approach also leads to an elementary proof of the Iosevich-Rudnev bound on the Erdős-Falconer distance problem in odd dimensions.

1.1
Quadratic forms

We refer to
[

We use

We can write a quadratic form

It is a standard result that there are two inequivalent nondegenerate quadratic forms in each dimension, depending on whether the determinant of the matrix associated to the form is a quadratic residue or non-residue.
In this paper, we use the following representative forms:

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{d}(x)

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4}+\cdots+x_{d-1}x_{d},

\displaystyle Q_{2}^{d}(x)

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4}+\cdots+x_{d-3}x_{d-2}+x_{d-1}^{2}-\epsilon x_{d}^{2},

\displaystyle Q_{3}^{d}(x)

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4}+\cdots+x_{d-2}x_{d-1}-x_{d}^{2},

\displaystyle Q_{4}^{d}(x)

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4}+\cdots+x_{d-2}x_{d-1}-\epsilon x_{d}^{2},

We omit

We refer to the translates of the level sets of a quadratic form

-spheres
.
In more detail,

-sphere with center

Note that

\displaystyle\eta(\det(Q_{1}^{d}))

\displaystyle=\eta((-1)^{d/2}),

\displaystyle\eta(\det(Q_{2}^{d}))

\displaystyle=-\eta((-1)^{d/2}),

\displaystyle\eta(\det(Q_{3}^{d}))

\displaystyle=\eta((-1)^{(d+1)/2}),

\displaystyle\eta(\det(Q_{4}^{d}))

\displaystyle=-\eta((-1)^{(d+1)/2}),

Since

The example shows that

{example}

Let

1.2
Results

The main contribution of this paper is to bound point-sphere incidences by bounding sphere-sphere intersections.
This builds on a standard idea in the study of pseudo-random graphs
[
18

19

The new proof of

Our new proof of

The problem of obtaining an elementary proof of

2
Pseudo-random graphs

For any graph
G
and vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, we write
u\sim v
if
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
.
For subsets
L,R\subseteq V(G)
, we define

E(L,R)=\#\{(u,v)\in L\times R:u\sim v\}.

We rely on the following bound, which is a mild generalization of
[
19
, Theorem 2]
:

7
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with vertex classes
L
and
U
, and let
p
be a positive real with
|L|^{-1}\leq p\leq|L|^{-1}|U|^{-1}|E(L,U)|
.
Then, for any
R\subseteq U
,

\left(E(L,R)-p|L|\,|R|\right)^{2}\leq|R|\left(\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v_{1}\neq v_{2}\in L}(\mathbf{1}_{v_{1}\sim u}\mathbf{1}_{v_{2}\sim u}-p^{2})+E(L,U)\right).

Proof.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|R|^{-1}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{u\in R}\sum_{v\in L}(\mathbf{1}_{v\sim u}-p)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in R}\left(\sum_{v\in L}(\mathbf{1}_{v\sim u}-p)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in U}\left(\sum_{v\in L}(\mathbf{1}_{v\sim u}-p)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v_{1}\neq v_{2}\in L}\mathbf{1}_{v_{1}\sim u}\mathbf{1}_{v_{2}\sim u}+E(L,U)-2p|L|E(L,U)+p^{2}|L|^{2}|U|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v_{1}\neq v_{2}\in L}(\mathbf{1}_{v_{1}\sim u}\mathbf{1}_{v_{2}\sim u}-p^{2})+E(L,U).

∎

For sets

S
of spheres, denote

|I(P,S)-q^{-1}\,|P|\,|S||\leq\sqrt{q^{d}|P||S|}.

(4)

Proof.

We will apply

7

,

, and

the set of all

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

Each point

Hence,

\left(I(P,S)-q^{-1}|P|\,|S|\right)^{2}\leq|S|\left(\sum_{s\in U}\sum_{x\neq y\in P}(\mathbf{1}_{x\in s}\mathbf{1}_{y\in s}-p^{2})+q^{d}|P|\right).

For each pair of distinct points

This is easy to check directly from the definition of

The basic geometric fact that enables this simple proof of
Theorem

2
is that each pair
(x,y)
of distinct points in

is contained in exactly

However, this is complicated by the fact that the number of points in the intersection of two spheres depends on the radii of the spheres and the distance between their centers.
Hence, obtaining concrete bounds using this approach requires a finer analysis, which depends on the specific quadratic form and collection of spheres that we consider.

Here is the general bound that we rely on, which requires further analysis of the intersections of the spheres before it can be applied.
Note that we always apply the bound to collections of spheres having the same size.

Appendix A
Pairwise intersection sizes of spheres

In this section, we compute the number of points in the intersection of any two spheres.
Since the orthogonal group associated to a quadratic form
Q
acts transitively on the level sets of
Q
, the number of points in the intersection of two spheres depends only on the radii of the spheres, and the distance between their centers.
The size of this intersection is always
q^{d-2}
plus some lower order terms, which we denote by

N_{2}(Q,r_{1},r_{2},t)=\#\{x:Q(x-y_{1})=r_{1},Q(x-y_{2})=r_{2},Q(y_{1}-y_{2})=t\}-q^{d-2}.

In other words,
N_{2}(Q,r_{1},r_{2},t)
is the difference between
q^{d-2}
and the number of points in the intersection of two
Q
-spheres, of
Q
-radius
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
respectively, when the
Q
-distance between the centers of the spheres is
t
.

Let
d\geq 3
.
Fix a choice of
Q_{i}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
y=(1,t,0,\ldots,0)
.
Each point
x
in the intersection of the radius
r_{1}
sphere centered at the origin with the radius
r_{2}
sphere centered at
y
satisfies

\displaystyle Q_{i}^{d}(x)

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}+Q_{i}^{d-2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{d})=r_{1}\text{, and}

\displaystyle Q_{i}^{d}(x-y)

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-1)(x_{2}-t)+Q_{i}^{d-2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{d})=r_{2}.

Subtracting the second equation from the first and rearranging, we find

x_{2}=r_{1}-r_{2}+t-tx_{1}.

Substituting this into the equation
Q_{i}^{d}(x)=r_{1}
, we find that

Q_{i}^{d-2}(x_{3},\ldots,x_{d})=r_{1}-x_{1}x_{2}=tx_{1}^{2}-(r_{1}-r_{2}+t)x_{1}+r_{1}.

For
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
f(x)=tx^{2}-(r_{1}-r_{2}+t)x+r_{1}
.
We have

N_{2}(Q_{i}^{d},r_{1},r_{2},t)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}N(Q_{i}^{d-2},\eta(-f(x))).

If
t=0
, then
f(x)=r_{1}-(r_{1}-r_{2})x
.
If additionally
r_{1}=r_{2}=r
, then
f(x)=r
, and so

N_{2}(Q_{i}^{d},r,r,0)=qN(Q_{i}^{d-2},\eta(-r)).

(11)

If
t=0
and
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
, then the image of
f(x)
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and so

N_{2}(Q_{i}^{d},r_{1},r_{2},0)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}N(Q_{i}^{d-2},\eta(-z))=N(Q_{i}^{d-2},0)+\frac{q-1}{2}(N(Q_{i}^{d-2},-1)+N(Q_{i}^{d-2},1)).

(12)

Now, suppose that
t\neq 0
.
Then, setting
z=2tx-(r_{1}-r_{2}+t)
, and
D=(r_{1}-r_{2}+t)^{2}-4r_{1}t
, we have

t^{-1}f(x)=\left(x-\frac{r_{1}-r_{2}+t}{2t}\right)^{2}-\frac{(r_{1}-r_{2}+t)^{2}-4r_{1}t}{4t^{2}}=\frac{z^{2}-D}{4t^{2}}.

Hence,
\eta(-f(x))=\eta(z^{2}-D)\eta(-t)
.

If
D=0
, then
\eta(-f(x))=\eta(-t)
except when
z=0
, so

N_{2}(Q_{i}^{d},r_{1},r_{2},t)=(q-1)N(Q_{i}^{d-2},\eta(-t))+N(Q_{i}^{d-2},0).

(13)

If
\eta(D)=1
, then there are two
z
such that

.
Using the fact that
\sum_{z}\eta(z^{2}-D)=-1
(see, for example
[
14

), this implies that

(14)

If
\eta(D)=-1
, then there is no
z
such that

(15)

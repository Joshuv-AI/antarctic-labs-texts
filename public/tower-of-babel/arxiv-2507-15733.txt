The theory of reachability of trace-pushdown systems Thanks: This is an extended version of the author’s conference publication [ 22 ] and contains full proofs. Affiliation: Technische Universität Ilmenau, Germany
Dietrich Kuske

Abstract
We consider pushdown systems that store, instead of a single word, a Mazurkiewicz trace on its stack. These systems are special cases of valence automata over graph monoids and subsume multi-stack systems. We identify a class of such systems that allow to decide the first-order theory of their configuration graph with reachability. This result complements results by D’Osualdo, Meyer, and Zetzsche (namely the decidability for arbitrary pushdown systems under a severe restriction on the dependence alphabet).

Introduction

Pushdown systems are a well-studied type of infinite state system with
many variants and generalizations. Valence automata

[
11
,
9
,
12
,
25
,
8
,
7
,
13
,
30
,
31
,
34
,
35
,
6
]

allow to study many of these variants in a uniform setting: in a
pushdown system, the possible pushdown contents come from a free
monoid and can be accessed at the suffix, only. Differently, in a
valence automaton, the free monoid is replaced by an arbitrary
monoid. As an example, graph monoids allow to model pushdown systems,
Petri nets, multi-stack automata, counter automata and many more.

For a pushdown system, the configuration graph with reachability can
be interpreted in the full tree. Hence Rabin’s tree theorem ensures
that monadic second order properties of this graph are decidable. This
decidability gets lost very soon once one considers valence automata
over graph monoids. Such a valence automaton consists of a finite
control and the graph monoid as generalization of the pushdown.

Therefore, D’Osualdo, Meyer and Zetzsche
[
6
]
considered
first-order logic. They were able to characterize those graph
monoids that ensure the decidability, no matter what local control
the monoid is attached to.

Differently here, we allow all loop-free graph monoids, but aim at
properties of the finite control that ensure the decidability
of the first-order theory of the configuration graph with
reachability. These loop-free graph monoids are precisely
Mazurkiewicz’ trace monoids.

In
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, we considered a related problem:
there, we were concerned with the question what properties of the
finite control ensure the decidability of the reachability
relation. It turned out that this is the case for what we called
cooperating multi-pushdown systems in
[
16
,
17
]
and, more
generally, trace-pushdown systems in
[
18
]
. To prove the
decidability, we showed that the reachability relation in this case
(i.e., a binary relation on the trace monoid) is “lc-rational”

[
21
]
, a non-trivial restriction of rational trace
relations. In general terms, this proof proceeds as follows. One first
handles two very special cases of systems where, either, the pushdown
is shortend in every step (i.e., no transition writes anything to the
pushdown) or, alternatively, the pushdown is never shortened, but all
transitions read “similar” letters (in some precise meaning). The
central argument in these two cases is that the class of lc-rational
relations is closed under the componentwise product with direct
products of a recognizable and a rational trace language (in general,
the product of lc-rational relations need not be lc-rational). To
handle the general case, one shows that the reachability relation can
be obtained from the special cases by union and composition. Here, one
uses that the class of lc-rational relations is closed under these two
operations (in general, the composition of rational trace relations is
not necessarily rational).

A first observation in this paper is that the said first-order theory
is in general undecidable for trace-pushdown systems, the
undecidability even holds for existential formulas
(Example
1
). To overcome this undecidability, we
restrict attention to loop-connected trace-pushdown systems. The
configuration graph of such systems is proved to be an automatic
structure
[
10
,
14
,
1
]
implying the desired
decidability.

The central argument in this proof is therefore the automaticity of
the configuration graph of a loop-connected trace-pushdown system. To
this aim, we use the Foata normal form of a trace as its word
representation. Consequently, one has to verify that the reachability
relation between such Foata normal forms can be accepted by a
synchronous two-tape automaton, i.e., that the reachability relation
is “fnf-automatic”. This proof follows the line of argument of the
lc-rationality for arbitrary trace-pushdown systems, but extends it at
some central point. So one first has to handle the two special cases
mentioned above. In both these cases, it is used that the class of
fnf-automatic relations is closed under product with direct products
of two recognizable trace languages (Lemma
5
). Then the
result for deleting systems follows immediately. For non-deleting
systems, the new requirement to be loop-connected allows to show that
the set of traces that can be written onto the pushdown is
recognizable (without the requirement, it was known to be rational,
only). Now the general case follows as before since the class of
fnf-automatic relations is closed under composition and union.

Preliminaries

2.1
Mazurkiewicz traces

Zero is a natural number and, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Let
\Sigma
be some alphabet and
w\in\Sigma^{*}
a word over

\Sigma
. The
alphabet of
w
, denoted

\alphabet(w)\subseteq\Sigma
, is the set of letters occuring in the
word
w
. The number of occurrences of the letter
a
in the word
w

is denoted
|w|_{a}
.

A
dependence alphabet
is a pair
(\Sigma,D)
where
\Sigma
is
a finite set of
letters
and
D\subseteq\Sigma\times\Sigma
is
a reflexive and symmetric relation, the
dependence
relation
. For a letter
a\in\Sigma
, we write
D(a)
for the set

\{b\in\Sigma\mid(a,b)\in D\}
of letters dependent from
a
,

D(B)=\bigcup_{b\in B}D(b)
is the set of letters dependent from some
letter in
B\subseteq\Sigma
. A set of letters
A\subseteq\Sigma
is

connected
if the graph
(A,D\cap A^{2})
is connected.

Two letters
a
and
b
are
twins
if
D(a)=D(b)
. The set of
twins of a letter
a
is denoted

\twins(a)=\{b\in\Sigma\mid D(a)=D(b)\}
. The
twin index
of

(\Sigma,D)
is the number of sets
\twins(a)
with
a\in\Sigma
.

The
independence relation

I\subseteq\Sigma\times\Sigma
is
the set of pairs
(a,b)
of distinct letters with
(a,b)\notin D
. If

A
and
B
are two sets of letters with
A\times B\subseteq I
(i.e.,
any letter from
A
is independent from any letter from
B
), then we
write
A\parallel B
. For words
u,v\in\Sigma^{*}
,
u\parallel v

abbreviates
\alphabet(u)\parallel\alphabet(v)
.

Let
{\sim}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*}
denote the least monoid
congruence with
ab\sim ba
for all
(a,b)\in I
. In other words,

u\sim v
holds for two words
u,v\in\Sigma^{*}
iff
u
can be
obtained from
v
by transposing consecutive independent letters. In
particular,
u\sim v
implies
|u|=|v|
,
|u|_{a}=|v|_{a}
for all

a\in\Sigma
, and
\alphabet(u)=\alphabet(v)
. Furthermore,

u\parallel v
implies
uv\sim vu
(but the converse implication does
not hold as the example
u=v=ab
shows).

The
(Mazurkiewicz) trace monoid
induced by
(\Sigma,D)
is the
quotient of the free monoid
\Sigma^{*}
wrt. the congruence
\sim
,
i.e.,
\mathbb{M}=\Sigma^{*}/{\sim}
. Its elements are equivalence classes of
words denoted
[w]
; by
[w]
, we mean the equivalence class
containing
w
, it is the
trace induced by
w
. Note that
\mathbb{M}

is indeed a monoid whose unit element is the trace
[\varepsilon]
;
this trace is denoted
1
.

Suppose
D=\Sigma\times\Sigma
, i.e., all letters are mutually dependent. Then

u\sim v\iff u=v
holds for all words
u,v\in\Sigma^{*}
; hence

\mathbb{M}\cong\Sigma^{*}
in this case.

The other extreme is
D=\{(a,a)\mid a\in\Sigma\}
where any two
distinct letters are independent. Then
u\sim v
iff
|u|_{a}=|v|_{a}

holds for any letter
a\in\Sigma
. Hence
\mathbb{M}\cong(\mathbb{N}^{|\Sigma|},+)

in this case.

To consider a case in between, let
\Sigma
be the disjoint union of

A
and
B
and suppose
D=A^{2}\times B^{2}
. Then
\mathbb{M}
is isomorphic to
the direct product of the free monoids
A^{*}
and
B^{*}
.

Automata and Word Languages

An
\varepsilon
-NFA

or
nondeterministic finite automaton with

\varepsilon
-transitions
is a tuple
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,I,T,F)
where

Q
is a finite set of
states
,
\Sigma
is an alphabet,

I,F\subseteq Q
are the sets of
initial
and
final

states, respectively, and

T\subseteq Q\times(\Sigma\cup\{\varepsilon\})\times Q

is the set of
transitions
. The
\varepsilon
-NFA
\mathcal{A}
is an

NFA
if
T\subseteq Q\times\Sigma\times Q
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,A,I,T,F)
be an
\varepsilon
-NFA. A
path
is a
sequence

(p_{0},a_{1},p_{1})(p_{1},a_{2},p_{2})\cdots(p_{n-1},a_{n},p_{n})

of matching transitions (i.e., elements of
T
). Such a path is usually denoted

p_{0}\xrightarrow{a_{1}}p_{1}\xrightarrow{a_{2}}p_{2}\cdots\xrightarrow{a_{n}}p_{n}

or, if the intermediate states are of no importance,

p_{0}\xrightarrow{a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}}p_{n}\,.

This path is
accepting
if it connects an initial state with a
final state, i.e., if
p_{0}\in I
and
p_{n}\in F
. It accepts the word

w=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}
(note that
a_{i}\in\Sigma\cup\{\varepsilon\}

such that
|w|<n
is possible). We denote by
L(\mathcal{A})
the set of
words
w
accepted by
\mathcal{A}
. A language
L\subseteq A^{*}
is

regular
if it is accepted by some NFA, i.e., if there is some
NFA
\mathcal{A}
with
L=L(\mathcal{A})
.

A foundational result in the theory of finite automata states that

\varepsilon
-NFA and NFA are equally expressive. Even more,

\varepsilon
-NFA can be transformed into equivalent NFA in polynomial
time.

A language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is
rational
if it can be
constructed from finite languages using the operations union,
multiplication, and Kleene star. By Kleene’s theorem
[
15
]
, a
language is regular if, and only if, it is rational.

Rational and Recognizable Trace Languages

Fix some dependence alphabet
(\Sigma,D)
. For a word language

L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
, we denote by
[L]
the set of traces
[u]
induced
by words from
L
, i.e.,
[L]=\{[u]\mid u\in L\}\subseteq\mathbb{M}
.

Now let
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{M}
. The set
\mathcal{L}
is
recognizable
if the
word language
\{u\in A^{*}\mid[u]\in\mathcal{L}\}
is regular; it is

rational
if there exists a regular word language

L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
with
\mathcal{L}=[L]
. It follows that every
recognizable trace language is rational; the converse implication is
known to fail (consider, e.g., the trace language

\{[ab]^{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
with
a\parallel b
that is rational, but not
recognizable).

Since rational trace languages are images of rational word languages,
we obtain that
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{M}
is rational if, and only if, it can
be constructed from finite trace languages using the operations union,
multiplication, and Kleene star.

2.2
Trace-pushdown systems

In this paper, we consider pushdowns that hold a trace
[w]\in\mathbb{M}

which can be accessed at the suffix, only.

A
pushdown system
is a tuple
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
where

Q
is a finite set of
states
,
(\Sigma,D)
a dependence
alphabet, and
\Delta\subseteq Q\times\Sigma\times\Sigma^{*}\times Q
a
finite set of
transitions
. For
(p,a,w,q)\in\Delta
, we
regularly write
p\xrightarrow{a\mid w}q
.

The set of configurations
\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P})
of
\mathcal{P}
is
Q\times\mathbb{M}
. For
two configurations
(p,[u]),(q,[v])\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P})
, we set

(p,[u])\vdash(q,[v])
if there is a transition
(p,a,w,q)\in\Delta

and a word
x\in\Sigma^{*}
such that
[u]=[xa]
and
[v]=[xw]
. Note
that
[u]=[xa]
is equivalent to saying
[u]=[x]\cdot[a]
and
similarly
[v]=[xw]
is equivalent to
[v]=[x]\cdot[w]
. Hence

(p,s)\vdash(q,t)
for
p,q\in Q
and
s,t\in\mathbb{M}
iff there is a
transition
(p,a,w,q)\in\Delta
such that the trace
t
results from
the trace
s
by replacing the suffix
[a]
by
[w]
. The reflexive
and transitive closure of the one-step relation
\vdash
is denoted

\vdash^{*}
. Furthermore, for
p,q\in Q
, we let

\reach_{p,q}\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{2}
denote the set of pairs
(s,t)
of traces
such that
(p,s)\vdash^{*}(q,t)
.

Definition 1

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
be a pushdown system. Its

configuration graph with reachability
is the structure

\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})=(Q\times\mathbb{M},\vdash,\vdash^{*},(\mathrm{state}_{p})_{p\in Q},(c)_{c\in Q\times\mathbb{M}})

consisting of the set of configurations
\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P})=Q\times\mathbb{M}
, the
one-step relation
\vdash
, the reachability relation
\vdash^{*}
,
the unary state predicate
\mathrm{state}_{p}=\{p\}\times\mathbb{M}
, and a constant
for every configuration of
\mathcal{P}
.

If
D=\Sigma\times\Sigma
, then this structure can be interpreted in
the full tree. Hence, by Rabin’s tree theorem
[
29
]
, its
monadic second order theory is decidable. This is not true in general
as the following result shows.

Theorem 2.1
(cf. D’Osualdo, Meyer, Zetzsche
[
6
]
)

Let
(\Sigma,D)
be a dependence alphabet. The first-order theory of

\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
is decidable for every pushdown system

\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
if, and only if, any letter from
\Sigma

is independent from at most one letter.

In this paper, we take another point of view: we ask for properties of
the transitions of a pushdown system
\mathcal{P}
that guarantee the
decidability of the theory of
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
. The decidability of
the theory of
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
implies in particular the
decidablitity of the reachability relation. In
[
18
]
, we
presented a class of pushdown systems whose reachability relation is
decidable. These
trace-pushdown systems
are defined as follows.

Definition 2

A
trace-pushdown system
(or
tPDS
, for short) is a
pushdown system
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
such that the following
hold:

(P1)

whenever
p\xrightarrow{a\mid w}q
is a transition from

\Delta
, then
D(w)\subseteq D(a)
,

(P2)

for any transitions

p\xrightarrow{a\mid v}q\xrightarrow{b\mid w}r
from
\Delta
with

a\parallel b
, there is a state
q^{\prime}\in Q
such that

p\xrightarrow{b\mid w}q^{\prime}\xrightarrow{a\mid v}r
are transitions
from
\Delta
.

The main result about these tPDS reads as follows:

Theorem 2.2
(Kuske and Köcher
[
18
]
)

The following problem is decidable in time polynomial in the size of

\mathcal{P}
and exponential in the twin index of
(\Sigma,D)
:

input:

trace-pushdown system
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)

two configurations
(p,s)
and
(q,t)
of
\mathcal{P}

question: Does
(p,s)\vdash^{*}(q,t)
hold?

We also constructed pushdown systems that satisfy only one of the two
conditions (P1) and (P2) and have an undecidable reachability
relation.

The following example shows that the first-order theory of

\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
is not uniformly decidable for the class of all tPDS.

Example 1

We want to reduce Post’s correspondence problem to the existential
theory. To this aim, let
\Sigma=\{a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime},\top\}
with

D=\{a,b\}^{2}\cup\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\}^{2}\cup(\Sigma\times\{\top\})\cup(\{\top\}\times\Sigma)\,.

Furthermore, let
f\colon\{a,b\}^{*}\to\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\}^{*}
be the monoid
homomorphism with
f(a)=a^{\prime}
and
f(b)=b^{\prime}
.

An instance of Post’s correspondence problem is a tuple

I=\bigl((u_{i},v_{i})\bigr)_{i\in[k]}
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

u_{i},v_{i}\in\{a,b\}^{*}
for all
i\in[k]
. A solution is a nonempty
tuple
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
with entries in
[k]
such that

u_{i_{1}}\,u_{i_{2}}\,\cdots\,u_{i_{n}}=v_{i_{1}}\,v_{i_{2}}\,\cdots\,v_{i_{n}}\,.

The existence of a solution is undecidable
[
28
]
.

Now let
I=\bigl((u_{i},v_{i})\bigr)_{i\in[k]}
be an instance of Post’s
correspondence problem. From this finite tuple of words, we
construct the following tPDS
\mathcal{P}
. It has four states
\iota,p,q,r

and the following transitions:

•

\iota\xrightarrow{\top\mid u_{i}\,f(v_{i})\top}p\xrightarrow{\top\mid u_{i}\,f(v_{i})\top}p\xrightarrow{\top\mid\top}r
for all
i\in[k]

•

\iota\xrightarrow{\top\mid x\,f(x)\top}q\xrightarrow{\top\mid x\,f(x)\top}q\xrightarrow{\top\mid\top}r
for all
x\in\{a,b\}
.

We claim that the instance
I
has a solution if, and only if, there
are configurations
(p,t_{1})
,
(q,t_{2})
, and
(r,t_{3})
such that

(\iota,\top)\vdash^{*}(p,t_{1})\vdash^{*}(r,t_{3})\text{ and }(\iota,\top)\vdash^{*}(q,t_{2})\vdash^{*}(r,t_{3})\,.

In other words, iff
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
satisfies the formula

\varphi=\exists c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\colon\begin{array}[t]{ll}&state_{p}(c_{1})\land\mathrm{state}_{q}(c_{2})\land\mathrm{state}_{r}(c_{3})\\
\land&(\iota,[\top])\vdash^{*}c_{1}\vdash^{*}c_{3}\\
\land&(\iota,[\top])\vdash^{*}c_{2}\vdash^{*}c_{3}\,.\end{array}

The formula
\varphi
expresses the existence of a solution since

(\iota,\top)\vdash^{*}(p,t_{1})\vdash^{*}(r,t_{3})
is equivalent to the
existence of
1\leq n\leq N
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{N}\in[k]
such that

\displaystyle t_{1}

\displaystyle=[u_{i_{1}}\,f(v_{i_{1}})\,u_{i_{2}}\,f(v_{i_{2}})\cdots u_{i_{n}}\,f(v_{i_{n}})\top]\text{ and }

\displaystyle t_{3}

\displaystyle=[u_{i_{1}}\,f(v_{i_{1}})\,u_{i_{2}}\,f(v_{i_{2}})\cdots u_{i_{N}}\,f(v_{i_{N}})\top]\,.

Since
u_{i}\parallel f(v_{j})
for all
i,j\in[k]
, we have

\displaystyle t_{3}

\displaystyle=[u_{i_{1}}\,f(v_{i_{1}})\,u_{i_{2}}\,f(v_{i_{2}})\cdots u_{i_{N}}\,f(v_{i_{N}})\top]

\displaystyle=[u_{i_{1}}\,u_{i_{2}}\cdots u_{i_{N}}\,f(v_{i_{1}})\,f(v_{i_{2}})\cdots f(v_{i_{N}})\top]

\displaystyle=[u_{i_{1}}\,u_{i_{2}}\cdots u_{i_{N}}\,f(v_{i_{1}}\,v_{i_{2}}\cdots v_{i_{N}})\top]

Similarly,
(\iota,\top)\vdash^{*}(q,t_{2})\vdash^{*}(r,t_{3})
is
equivalent to the existence of
1\leq m\leq M
and

x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{M}\in\{a,b\}
such that

\displaystyle t_{2}

\displaystyle=[x_{1}\,f(x_{1})\,x_{2}\,f(x_{2})\cdots x_{m}\,f(x_{m})\top]\text{ and }

\displaystyle t_{3}

\displaystyle=[x_{1}\,f(x_{1})\,x_{2}\,f(x_{2})\cdots x_{M}\,f(x_{M})\top]

\displaystyle=[x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{M}\,f(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{M})\top]\,.

Thus, the formula
\varphi
holds if, and only if, there exists a
word
w\in\{a,b\}^{*}
with

u_{i_{1}}\,u_{i_{2}}\cdots u_{i_{N}}\,f(v_{i_{1}}\,v_{i_{2}}\cdots v_{i_{N}})=w\,f(w)\,.

Since
f
is bijective, this is equivalent to

u_{i_{1}}\,u_{i_{2}}\cdots u_{i_{N}}=v_{i_{1}}\,v_{i_{2}}\cdots v_{i_{N}}\,,

i.e., to the existence of a solution for
I
. ∎

In order to rule out this undecidability, we restrict the class of
pushdown systems further.

Definition 3

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
be a tPDS.

1.

The tPDS
\mathcal{P}
is
saturated
if (for any
p,q,r\in Q
,

a,b\in\Sigma
, and
u,v\in\Sigma^{*}
)

p\xrightarrow{a\mid ubv}q\xrightarrow{b\mid\varepsilon}r\text{ and }b\parallel v\text{ imply }p\xrightarrow{a\mid uv}r\,.

2.

The tPDS
\mathcal{P}
is
loop-connected
if it is saturated and
the following holds (for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p_{i}\in Q
,

a_{i}\in\Sigma
,
u_{i},v_{i}\in\Sigma^{*}
for
i\in[n]
). If

p_{0}\xrightarrow{a_{0}\mid u_{1}a_{1}v_{1}}p_{1}\xrightarrow{a_{1}\mid u_{2}a_{2}v_{2}}p_{2}\xrightarrow{a_{2}\mid u_{3}a_{3}v_{3}}\cdots\xrightarrow{a_{n-1}\mid u_{n}a_{n}v_{n}}p_{n}

with
a_{i}\parallel v_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
and

(p_{0},a_{0})=(p_{n},a_{n})
, then the set
\alphabet(u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n})

is connected.

Suppose we have

p\xrightarrow{a\mid ubv}q\xrightarrow{b\mid\varepsilon}r
and

b\parallel v
. Then, for any trace
x
, one gets

(p,x\cdot[a])\vdash(q,x\cdot[ubv])=(q,x\cdot[uvb])\vdash(r,x\cdot[uv])\,.

If
\mathcal{P}
is saturated, we can take the “shortcut”

(p,x\cdot[a])\vdash(r,x\cdot[uv])
in a single step. From an
arbitrary tPDS, one can compute a saturated tPDS by adding transitions
that has the same reachability relation
[
18
]
. It therefore
makes sense to only consider saturated systems.

The idea of loop-connectedness is as follows. Suppose there are
transitions as in the definition. Since
\mathcal{P}
is a tPDS that, in
certain transitions, can replace
a_{i}
by a word containing
a_{i+1}
,
we get

D(a_{n})\subseteq D(a_{n-1})\subseteq D(a_{n-2})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq D(a_{0})=D(a_{n})

implying
D(a_{i})=D(a_{0})
for all
i\in[n]
. Next let

i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
and suppose
b
is a letter that occurs in

v_{i+1}
. Since the tPDS
\mathcal{P}
can replace
a_{i}
with

u_{i+1}a_{i+1}v_{i+1}
, we get

b\in D(u_{i+1}a_{i+1}v_{i+1})\subseteq D(a_{i})=D(a_{i+1})
and
therefore
(b,a_{i+1})\in D
. As this contradicts

a_{i+1}\parallel v_{i+1}
, the letter
b
cannot exist, i.e.,

v_{i+1}
is the empty word
\varepsilon
. Consequently, the
transitions from the definition are actually of the form

p_{0}\xrightarrow{a_{0}\mid u_{1}a_{1}}p_{1}\xrightarrow{a_{1}\mid u_{2}a_{2}}p_{2}\xrightarrow{a_{2}\mid u_{3}a_{3}}\cdots\xrightarrow{a_{n-1}\mid u_{n}a_{n}}p_{n}

with
(p_{0},a_{0})=(p_{n},a_{n})
. Then the tPDS can make the following
computation

\displaystyle(p_{0},[a_{0}])

\displaystyle\vdash(p_{1},[u_{1}a_{1}])

\displaystyle\vdash(p_{2},[u_{1}u_{2}a_{2}])

\displaystyle\vdash(p_{n},[u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n}a_{n}])

\displaystyle=(p_{0},[u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n}a_{0}])\,.

In other words, the tPDS can replace any trace
[u\,a_{0}]
by the trace

[u\,u_{1}\cdots u_{n}\,a_{0}]
and therefore, by repeating this loop, by

[u\,(u_{1}\cdots u_{n})^{m}\,a_{0}]
for any number
m
. The requirement is
that the looped trace
[u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n}]
shall be connected.

As a side-remark, we indicate that loop-connectedness is difficult to
verify.

Proposition 1

The following is co-NP-hard.

input: saturated tPDS
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)

question: is
\mathcal{P}
loop-connected

Proof

Let
(\Sigma,D)
be a dependence alphabet and
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,I,T,F)

an nfa. From
(\Sigma,D)
and
\mathcal{A}
, we construct the pushdown
system
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Gamma,D^{\prime},\Delta)
setting

•

\Gamma=\Sigma\cup\{\top\}
,

•

D^{\prime}=D\cup\Sigma\times\{\top\}\cup\{\top\}\times\Sigma
, and

•

\Delta=\{(p,\top,a\top,q)\mid(p,a,q)\in T\}
.

Then it is easily checked that
\mathcal{P}
is a saturated
tPDS. Furthermore, it is loop-connected if, and only if, for every
loop

(p_{0},a_{1},p_{1})(p_{1},a_{2},p_{2})\cdots(p_{n-1},a_{n},p_{n})

in
\mathcal{A}
with
p_{n}=p_{0}
, the set

\{a_{1},a_{2}\cdots,a_{n}\}\subseteq\Sigma
is connected.

The set of nfas with this property is coNP-complete

[
26
, Prop. 4]
, thus we reduced from a coNP-complete problem.

We can now formulate the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.3

The following problem is decidable.

input:

loop-connected tPDS
\mathcal{P}

first-order formula
\varphi
in the language of
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})

question: Does
\varphi
hold in
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
?

Before starting the proof, we demonstrate that this decidability does
not hold for monadic second-order logic.

Example 2

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
be the following pushdown system:

•

Q=\{p,q,r\}
,

•

\Sigma=\{a,b,\top\}

•

D
is the reflexive and symmetric closure of

\{a,b\}\times\{\top\}
.

•

there are seven transitions:

p\xrightarrow{\top\mid a\top}p\xrightarrow{\top\mid\top}q\xrightarrow{\top\mid b\top}q\xrightarrow{\top\mid b}r\xrightarrow{b\mid\varepsilon}r
,

q\xrightarrow{\top\mid\varepsilon}r
, and

r\xrightarrow{a\mid\varepsilon}r
.

This pushdown system is a loop-connected tPDS. Then

(p,[\top])\vdash^{*}(r,[a^{m}b^{n}])
for all
m\geq 0,n\geq 1
and

(r,[a^{k}b^{\ell}])\vdash^{*}(r,[a^{m}b^{n}])
if, and only if,
k\geq m
and

\ell\geq n
. Hence
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
contains the infinite grid

(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N},\geq)
implying that the monadic second-order theory
is undecidable (cf., e.g.,
[
4
, Thm. 5.6]
).∎

To prove Theorem
2.3
, we work with the following structure.

Definition 4

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
be a pushdown system. For
p,q\in Q
,
we let
\reach_{p,q}\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{2}
denote the set of pairs
(s,t)

of traces such that
(p,s)\vdash^{*}(q,t)
. Furthermore,

\step_{p,q}\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{2}
is the set of pairs
(s,t)
such that

(p,s)\vdash(q,t)
.

Define the structure

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})=\bigl(\mathbb{M},(\step_{p,q},\reach_{p,q})_{p,q\in Q},(t)_{t\in\mathbb{M}}\bigr)

with universe the set of traces over
(\Sigma,D)
, the one-step
relations
\step_{p,q}
and the reachability relations

\reach_{p,q}
for all
p,q\in Q
, and all traces
t\in\mathbb{M}
as
constants.

To justify that we work with this structure, we first prove that the
theory of
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
can be reduced to that of this structure.

Lemma 1

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
be a pushdown system. From a
first-order formula
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
in the language of

\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
and a tuple of states

\overline{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in Q^{n}
, one can compute a formula

\varphi_{\overline{p}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
in the language of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})
such
that

\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})\models\varphi((p_{i},t_{i}))_{i\in[n]})\iff\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})\models\varphi_{\overline{p}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

holds for any
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathbb{M}
.

Proof

The proof is by induction on the construction of
\varphi
.

•

If
\varphi=(x_{i}=x_{j})
and
p_{i}=p_{j}
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=(x_{i}=x_{j})
.

•

If
\varphi=(x_{i}=x_{j})
and
p_{i}\neq p_{j}
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=(1\neq 1)
.

•

If
\varphi=(x_{i}=(p,t))
and
p_{i}=p
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=(x_{i}=t)
.

•

If
\varphi=(x_{i}=(p,t))
and
p_{i}\neq p
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=(1\neq 1)
.

•

If
\varphi=\mathrm{state}_{p}(x_{i})
and
p_{i}=p
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=(1=1)
.

•

If
\varphi=\mathrm{state}_{p}(x_{i})
and
p_{i}\neq p
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=(1\neq 1)
.

•

If
\varphi=(x_{i}\vdash x_{j})
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=\step_{p_{i},p_{j}}(x_{i},x_{j})
.

•

If
\varphi=(x_{i}\vdash^{*}x_{j})
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=\reach_{p_{i},p_{j}}(x_{i},x_{j})
.

•

If
\varphi=\alpha\lor\beta
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=\alpha_{\overline{p}}\lor\beta_{\overline{p}}
.

•

If
\varphi=\exists x_{n+1}\colon\alpha
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=\displaystyle\bigvee_{p_{n+1}\in Q}\exists x_{n+1}\colon\alpha_{\overline{p},p_{n+1}}
.∎

Remark 1

The above lemma can also be shown for monadic second-order logic. In
addition to the above translation, one defines:

•

If
\varphi=\exists X\,\alpha
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=\exists(X_{p})_{p\in Q}\,\alpha_{\overline{p}}
.

•

If
\varphi=(x_{i}\in X)
, then

\varphi_{\overline{p}}=(x_{i}\in X_{p_{i}})
.∎

If the theory of
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{P})
is undecidable, then, by the above
lemma, the same applies to the theory of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})
. This implies in
particular the following negative results.

•

The first-order theory of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})
is not uniformly decidable
for all tPDS (cf. Example
1
).

•

The monadic second-order theory of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})
is not uniformly
decidable for all loop-connected tPDS (cf. Example
2
).

Conversely, in order to prove Theorem
2.3
, it suffices to
demonstrate the following result.

Theorem 2.4

The following problem is decidable.

input:

loop-connected tPDS
\mathcal{P}

first-order formula
\varphi
in the language of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})

question: Does
\varphi
hold in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P})
?

The proof of this theorem can be found in Section
5
. It
is based on a special class of trace relations

below) that we introduce and

and

3
fnf-automatic relations

Let
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}\subseteq\Sigma
. The set
A
is

independent
if
(a_{i},a_{j})\in I
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
; let

\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{\Sigma}
denote the set of all independent sets. Note
that in particular
\emptyset
is independent. It follows that, for
any permutation
\sigma\colon[n]\to[n]
, we have

a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}\sim a_{\sigma(1)}a_{\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{\sigma(n)}\,.

Hence there is a unique trace
[A]=[a_{1}\cdots a_{n}]
. This mapping

[.]\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{M}
extends uniquely to a monoid homomorphism

[.]\colon\mathcal{F}^{*}\to\mathbb{M}
.

A (possibly empty) word
A_{1}\,A_{2}\,\cdots\,A_{n}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}
is
in
extended Foata normal form
if
D(A_{i})\supseteq A_{i+1}
for all

i\in[n-1]
. In other words, we have

for all
i\in[n-1]
and
b\in A_{i+1}
, there exists
a\in A_{i}
with

(a,b)\in D
.

Let
W=A_{1}\,A_{2}\,\cdots\,A_{n}
be in extended Foata normal form. Since

D(\emptyset)=\emptyset
, there is
m\in\{0,\dots,n\}
such that

A_{i}=\emptyset\iff i>m
for all
i\in[n]
, i.e., in a word in extended
Foata normal form, the letter
\emptyset
can occur at the end of the
word, only.

A word over
\mathcal{F}
is
in Foata normal form
if it is in extended
Foata normal form and none of its letters equals
\emptyset
. By the
very definition, any infix of any word in (extended) Foata normal form
is in (extended) Foata normal form, again.

Let
x\in\mathbb{M}
. Then, by
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
, there is a unique
word
\fnf(x)\in\mathcal{F}^{*}
in Foata normal form with
x=[\fnf(x)]
; this
word is the
Foata normal form of
x
.

Suppose
W=A_{1}\,\dots\,A_{n}
is in extended Foata normal form with

x=[W]
. Then there exists
m\in\{0,\dots,n\}
such that

\fnf(x)=A_{1}\,A_{2}\,\cdots\,A_{m}
, i.e., the Foata normal form of
x
is
a prefix of the word
W
. It follows that the set of words
W
in
extended Foata normal form with
x=[W]
equals
\fnf(x)\,\{\emptyset\}^{*}
.

Let
W=A_{1}\,A_{2}\,\cdots\,A_{n}
be in Foata normal form. Then
any factorisation of
W
into
U
and
V
trivially satisfies

[W]=[U]\cdot[V]
; furthermore,
U
and
V
are the Foata normal forms
of the two factors
[U]
and
[V]
, respectively. But there are
factorisations of
[W]
into traces
x
and
y
such that no
factorsation of
W
into
U
and
V
satisfies
x=[U]
and
y=[V]
; in
particular, the Foata normal forms of
x
and
y
need not be factors
of the Foata normal form of
x\cdot y
. The following lemma describes
the factorisations of the trace
[W]
in terms of the word
W
.

Lemma 2

Let
W=A_{1}\,\cdots\,A_{n}
be a word in extended Foata normal form and

x,y\in\mathbb{M}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

x\cdot y=[W]

(2)

There are sets
B_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
for
i\in[n]
such that

(a)

the word

(A_{1}\setminus B_{1})\,(A_{2}\setminus B_{2})\,\cdots\,(A_{n}\setminus B_{n})
is in extended Foata normal form,

(b)

B_{i}\parallel(A_{j}\setminus B_{j})
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
, and

(c)

x=\bigl[(A_{1}\setminus B_{1})\,(A_{2}\setminus B_{2})\,\cdots\,(A_{n}\setminus B_{n})\bigr]
and

y=[B_{1}\,B_{2}\,\cdots\,B_{n}]
.

Note that, by (a) and (c), the Foata normal form of
x
is a prefix of
the word

(A_{1}\setminus B_{1})\,(A_{2}\setminus B_{2})\,\cdots\,(A_{n}\setminus B_{n})
. In contrast, the Foata normal form of
y
can be very different
from the word
B_{1}\,B_{2}\,\cdots\,B_{n}
.

Proof

First, assume (2) holds. From (b), we obtain

[A_{j}\setminus B_{j}]\cdot[B_{1}]=[B_{1}]\cdot[A_{j}\setminus B_{j}]

for all
j\in\{2,\dots,n\}
. Hence

\displaystyle x\cdot[B_{1}]

\displaystyle=[A_{1}\setminus B_{1}]\cdots[A_{n}\setminus B_{n}]\cdot[B_{1}]

\displaystyle=[A_{1}\setminus B_{1}]\cdot[B_{1}]\cdot[A_{2}\setminus B_{2}]\cdots[A_{n}\setminus B_{n}]

\displaystyle=[A_{1}]\cdot[A_{2}\setminus B_{2}]\cdots[A_{n}\setminus B_{n}]\,.

Similarly,

[A_{j}\setminus B_{j}]\cdot[B_{2}]=[B_{2}]\cdot[A_{j}\setminus B_{j}]
holds
for all
j\in\{3,\dots,n\}
implying

\displaystyle x\cdot[B_{1}]\cdot[B_{2}]

\displaystyle=[A_{1}]\cdot[A_{2}]\cdot[A_{3}\setminus B_{3}]\cdots[A_{n}\setminus B_{n}]\,.

Inductively, we get

\displaystyle x\cdot y

\displaystyle=[A_{1}\setminus B_{1}]\cdots[A_{n}\setminus B_{n}]\cdot[B_{1}]\cdots[B_{n}]

\displaystyle=[A_{1}]\cdot[A_{2}]\cdots[A_{n}]=[W]

and therefore (1).

Conversely, suppose (1) holds. By Levi’s Lemma
(cf.
[
5
, p. 74]
) there are traces
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
for

i\in[n]
such that

•

[A_{i}]=x_{i}\cdot y_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
,

•

y_{i}\parallel x_{j}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
,

•

x=x_{1}\cdot x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
, and

•

y=y_{1}\cdot y_{2}\cdots y_{n}
.

Since, for
i\in[n]
,
A_{i}
is an independent set with

[A_{i}]=x_{i}\cdot y_{i}
, there is
B_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
with

x_{i}=[A_{i}\setminus B_{i}]
and
y_{i}=[B_{i}]
.

We first show (a), i.e.,

D(A_{i}\setminus B_{i})\supseteq A_{i+1}\setminus B_{i+1}
for all

i\in[n-1]
.

So let
i\in[n-1]
and

b\in A_{i+1}\setminus B_{i+1}\subseteq A_{i+1}
. Since the word

A_{1}\,A_{2}\,\cdots\,A_{n}
is in extended Foata normal form, there
exists
a\in A_{i}
with
(a,b)\in D
. Suppose
a\in B_{i}
. Then we
have

a\in B_{i}=\alphabet(y_{i})\parallel\alphabet(x_{i+1})=A_{i+1}\setminus B_{i+1}\ni b

which contradicts
(a,b)\in D
. Thus, indeed, (a) holds.

To verify claim (b), let
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Then

B_{i}=\alphabet(y_{i})\parallel\alphabet(x_{j})=A_{j}\setminus B_{j}\,.

Claim (c) is obvious by

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=x_{1}\cdot x_{2}\cdots x_{n}

\displaystyle=[A_{1}\setminus B_{1}]\cdot[A_{2}\setminus B_{2}]\cdots[A_{n}\setminus B_{n}]

\displaystyle\text{since }x_{i}=[A_{i}\setminus B_{i}]\text{ for all }i\in[n]

and

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=y_{1}\cdot y_{2}\cdots y_{n}

\displaystyle=[B_{1}]\cdot[B_{2}]\cdots[B_{n}]

\displaystyle\text{since }y_{i}=[B_{i}]\text{ for all }i\in[n]\,.

∎

We next define what we mean by “fnf-automatic relations”.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{k}
be a
k
-ary relation on the set of traces

\mathbb{M}
. From this relation, we first construct a language
L_{\mathcal{R}}
over
the alphabet
\mathcal{F}^{k}
. Note that the elements of
\mathcal{F}^{k}
are
k
-tuples
of independent sets that we write interchangeably as

(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})\text{ or }\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\\
\vdots\\
A_{k}\end{pmatrix}\,.

Now consider a word

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}^{1}\\
A_{2}^{1}\\
\vdots\\
A_{k}^{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}^{2}\\
A_{2}^{2}\\
\vdots\\
A_{k}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}^{n}\\
A_{2}^{n}\\
\vdots\\
A_{k}^{n}\end{pmatrix}\in(\mathcal{F}^{k})^{*}

over
\mathcal{F}^{k}
. It belongs to the language
L_{\mathcal{R}}
if

•

W_{i}=A_{i}^{1}\,A_{i}^{2}\,\cdots\,A_{i}^{n}
is in extended Foata normal form
for all
i\in[k]
and

•

the tuple
([W_{1}],[W_{2}],\dots,[W_{k}])
belongs to
\mathcal{R}
.

Intuitively, we understand a word
W
over
\mathcal{F}^{k}
as the tuple of words

(W_{1},\dots,W_{k})
(of the same length) over
\mathcal{F}
. Then
W
belongs to

L_{\mathcal{R}}
if all the words
W_{i}
are in extended Foata normal form and
the tuple
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
of traces represented by these words
belongs to the relation
\mathcal{R}
.

As an example, suppose
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{R}=\{([A][A],[B])\}
. Then
L_{\mathcal{R}}

is the language

\begin{pmatrix}A\\
B\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A\\
\emptyset\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\emptyset\\
\emptyset\end{pmatrix}^{*}\,.

Definition 5

A relation
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{k}
is
fnf-automatic
if the
language
L_{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq(\mathcal{F}^{k})^{*}
is regular.

By the very definition, any fnf-automatic relation
\mathcal{R}
can be
represented by an NFA
\mathcal{A}
for the language
L_{\mathcal{R}}
; we will always
assume that fnf-automatic relations are presented that way.

A very simple example of an fnf-automatic relation is the identity
relation
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{M}}=\{(x,x)\mid x\in\mathbb{M}\}
. It is not difficult to see
that the union and the intersection of two fnf-automatic relations is
fnf-automatic. The same applies to the complement as well as the
inversion

\mathcal{R}^{-1}=\{(x_{k},x_{k-1},\dots,x_{1})\mid(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathcal{R}\}
.

Lemma 3

If
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
are fnf-automatic relations, then

\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2}
is fnf-automatic.

Proof

For notational convenience, we assume
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
to be
binary. For
1\leq i<j\leq 3
, let

\pi_{ij}\colon(\mathcal{F}^{3})^{*}\to(\mathcal{F}^{2})^{*}
be the monoid homomorphism with

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\\
A_{3}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}A_{i}\\
A_{j}\end{pmatrix}

for all
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\in\mathcal{F}
. We consider the language

L=\pi_{13}\bigl(\pi_{12}^{-1}(L_{\mathcal{R}_{1}})\cap\pi_{23}^{-1}(L_{\mathcal{R}_{2}})\bigr)\,.

For any words
U=A_{1}\,A_{2}\,\cdots\,A_{n}
and

W=C_{1}\,C_{2}\,\cdots\,C_{n}
in extended Foata normal form, we have

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
C_{1}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}A_{2}\\
C_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,\cdots\,\begin{pmatrix}A_{n}\\
C_{n}\end{pmatrix}\in L_{\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2}}

if, and only if, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
with

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
C_{1}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}A_{2}\\
C_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,\cdots\,\begin{pmatrix}A_{n}\\
C_{n}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}\emptyset\\
\emptyset\end{pmatrix}^{m}\in L\,.

Hence
L_{\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2}}
is the right quotient of the language

L
wrt. the regular language

\begin{pmatrix}\emptyset\\
\emptyset\end{pmatrix}^{*}
. Since the class of regular
languages is closed under homomorphic images and inverse images and
under right quotions wrt. regular languages, the language

L_{\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2}}
is regular. ∎

The following lemma relates the class of fnf-automatic relations to
that of rational relations.

Lemma 4

Any fnf-automatic relation is rational, but there are rational unary
relations (i.e., rational languages) that are not fnf-automatic.

Proof

For the first claim, let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{k}
be fnf-automatic. Then
the language
L_{\mathcal{R}}
is regular and
\mathcal{R}
is the image of
L_{\mathcal{R}}

under the monoid homomorphism

(\mathcal{F}^{k})^{*}\to\mathbb{M}^{k}\colon\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\\
\vdots\\
A_{k}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\bigl([A_{1}],[A_{2}],\dots,[A_{k}]\bigr)\,.

Since homomorphic images of rational sets are rational, the relation

\mathcal{R}
is rational.

For the second claim, let
a,b\in\Sigma
with
a\parallel b
and
consider the rational set of traces

\mathcal{L}=[aab]^{*}=\{[aab]^{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Then
L_{\mathcal{L}}
is the set of
words

A^{n}\,B^{n}\,C^{m}

with
A=\{a,b\}
,
B=\{a\}
,
C=\emptyset
, and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
this language is not regular, the unary relation
\mathcal{L}
is not
fnf-automatic.∎

In general, the product of two fnf-automatic relations is not
fnf-automatic (consider, e.g., the unary relations
\mathcal{L}_{1}=[a]^{*}
and

\mathcal{L}_{2}=[bc]^{*}
with
(a,b)\in D
and
\{a,b\}\parallel\{c\}
since

[(ac)^{m}\,b^{n}]\in\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2}
iff
m=n
). The following lemma
proves that certain products are fnf-automatic.

Lemma 5

Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{2}
be fnf-automatic and
\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{M}

recognizable. Then

\mathcal{R}\cdot(\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{L})=\{(x_{1}y,x_{2}z)\mid(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{R},y\in\mathcal{K},z\in\mathcal{L}\}

is effectively fnf-automatic.

Proof

Note that

\mathcal{R}\cdot(\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{L})=\bigl(\mathcal{R}\cdot(\mathcal{K}\times\{1\})\bigr)\circ\bigl(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{M}}\cdot(\mathcal{L}\times\{1\})\bigr)^{-1}\,.

Since the class of fnf-automatic relations is effectively closed
under composition and inversion and since
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{M}}
is
fnf-automatic, it suffices to prove the lemma for
\mathcal{L}=\{1\}
.

Since
\mathcal{R}
is fnf-automatic, there is an NFA

\mathcal{A}_{1}=(Q_{1},\mathcal{F}^{2},I_{1},\to_{1},F_{1})

4
Reachability and fnf-automaticity

The aim of this section is the proof of the following result.

Theorem 4.1

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
be a loop-connected tPDS and

p,q\in Q
two states. Then the one-step relation
\step_{p,q}
and the
reachability relation
\reach_{p,q}
are effectively fnf-automatic.

Note that
(s,t)\in\step_{p,q}
iff there is a trace
x
and a
transition
(p,a,w,q)\in\Delta
with
s=x\cdot[a]
and
t=x\cdot[w]
. Hence

\step_{p,q}=\bigcup_{(p,a,w,q)\in\Delta}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{M}}\cdot(\{[a]\}\times\{[w]\})\,.

Since this is a finite union and since unary languages are
recognizable, the fnf-automaticity of
\step_{p,q}
follows easily
from Lemma
5
.

The proof of the claim regarding
\reach_{p,q}
is more involved. It
uses constructions and results from
[
18
]
that were shown for
all tPDS and it then refines them for the case of loop-connected
systems. It starts by first demonstrating the theorem for two special
cases.

The first special case assumes that all transitions of
\mathcal{P}
shorten
the pushdown, i.e., write the empty word. Since any such system is
trivially loop-connected, we do not require this explicitly.

Proposition 2

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
be a tPDS with

\Delta\subseteq Q\times\Sigma\times\{\varepsilon\}\times Q
, and

p,q\in Q
two states. Then the relation
\reach_{p,q}
is
effectively fnf-automatic.

Proof

The proof of
[
18
, Prop. 5.4]
shows

\reach_{p,q}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{M}}\cdot(\mathcal{K}\times\{1\})

for some effectively recognizable language
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathbb{M}
. Hence
the claim follows from Lemma
5
.∎

While above we consider systems that never write anything onto the
pushdown, we next want to study systems that always write
something. In other words, these systems do not shorten the pushdown
in any step. We make the additional assumption that all letters read
from the pushdown are twins, i.e., if
p\xrightarrow{a\mid w}q
and

p^{\prime}\xrightarrow{a^{\prime}\mid w^{\prime}}q^{\prime}
are transitions from
\Delta
, then

D(a)=D(a^{\prime})
. In
[
18
]
, it is shown that the reachability
relation in such systems equals

\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{M}}\cup\bigcup_{a\in\Sigma}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{M}}\cdot(\{[a]\}\times\mathcal{H}_{a})

for some rational sets of traces
\mathcal{H}_{a}
. The additional assumption of
loop-connectedness allows to show that these lagnuages
\mathcal{H}_{a}
are
even recognizable.

Lemma 6

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)
be a loop-connected tPDS with

\Delta\subseteq Q\times\twins(a)\times\Sigma^{+}\times Q
for some

a\in\Sigma
, and
p,q\in Q
be two states. There effectively
exists a recognizable trace language

such that,

\{y\in\mathbb{M}\mid(x\cdot[a],y)\in\reach_{p,q}\}=\{x\}\cdot\mathcal{H}_{a}\,.

(3)

Proof

Suppose
\mathcal{P}
is a tPDS that is not necessarily

[

with

So recall the construction of an

Proposition 3

Let
\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,D,\Delta)

(of Theorem

Remark 2

Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{k}

proof still gives the decidability of the first-order theory. Adding
such relations seems pretty exotic, but it might be of interest in

Recall that in case

this does not hold for arbitrary loop-connected tPDS. But another

\exists X\text{ infinite}\colon\varphi

variables
X

the atomic formula
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\in X

\varphi

[
23
]

formulas is decidable for any automatic structure. Thus, this
decidability also holds for the configuration graph with
reachability of a loop-connected tPDS.

The work on automatic structures revealed that additional types of
quantifiers are possible: the infinity quantifier
\exists^{\infty}

[
1

2
]

[
32
]
,
Ramsey-quantifier
[

]

[

]

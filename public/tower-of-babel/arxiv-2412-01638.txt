The Langlands formula and perverse sheaves
Mikhail Kapranov, Vadim Schechtman, Olivier Schiffmann, Jiangfan Yuan

Abstract
. For a complex reductive Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} with Cartan subalgebra 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} and Weyl group W W we consider the category Perv ⁡ ( W \ 𝔥 ) \operatorname{Perv}\nolimits({W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}) of perverse sheaves on W \ 𝔥 {W\backslash\mathfrak{h}} smooth w.r.t. the natural stratification. We construct a category 𝓒 {\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}} such that Perv ⁡ ( W \ 𝔥 ) \operatorname{Perv}\nolimits({W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}) is identified with the category of functors from 𝓒 {\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}} to vector spaces. Objects of 𝓒 {\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}} are labelled by standard parabolic subalgebras in 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . It has morphisms analogous to the operations of parabolic induction (Eisenstein series) and restriction (constant term) of automorphic forms. In particular, the Langlands formula for the constant term of an Eisenstein series has a counterpart in the form of an identity in 𝓒 {\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}} . We define 𝓒 {\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}} as the category of W W -invariants (in an appropriate sense) in the category Q Q describing perverse sheaves on 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} smooth w.r.t. the root arrangement. This matches, in an interesting way, the definition of W \ 𝔥 {W\backslash\mathfrak{h}} itself as the spectrum of the algebra of W W -invariants.

Introduction

0.1.
Overview of results

The Langlands formula for the constant term of a (pseudo) Eisenstein series

[
L1
,
L2
]

[
MW
]
is one of the cornerstones
of the theory of automorphic forms. It can be seen as a highly nontrivial extension to the level of functions
of the classical Mackey theorem which is a categorical result
describing the restriction to a subgroup of a group representation
induced from another subgroup, see e.g.,
[
Ki
]
. Both results involve summation over double cosets.

In this paper we relate the Langlands formula to the problem of describing perverse sheaves on
the quotient
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is the Cartan subalgebra in (the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of)
a complex reductive algebraic group
G

and
W
is the Weyl group. More precisely, we fix a field
{\mathbf{k}}
of characteristic
0
and
build a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear category
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{G}
whose objects are
standard parabolics in
G
and morphisms mimic the properties of the induction (Eisenstein series)
and restriction (constant term) operations, with a version of the Langlands formula serving as the
commutation rule between the two types of operations. We call
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
the
perverse Coxeter
category
(
P-Coxeter category
, for short).

Our first main result, Theorem
6.1.2
, is that

(0.1.1)

\operatorname{Perv}\nolimits({W\backslash\mathfrak{h}})\,\,{\simeq}\,\,{\operatorname{Fun}}({\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}},\operatorname{Vect}\limits_{\mathbf{k}}).

Here
\operatorname{Perv}\nolimits({W\backslash\mathfrak{h}})
is the category of perverse sheaves of
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector spaces on
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
smooth
with respect to the standard complex stratification
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{h}}
, see §
2.3
below.

Recall that the Langlands formula involves intertwining operators which a priori satisfy the braid relations
but in the automorphic situation descend to an action of the Weyl group(oid) due to functional equations
for L-functions and Eisenstein series. In our definition of
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}

we retain only the braid relations. The latter have a clear
topological meaning: they describe the fundamental
groupoids of various complex strata in
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
. So our
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
can be seen as interpreting
“the Langlands formula without functional equations”.

In the case
G=GL_{n}
we have
W=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the symmetric group. We relate our category
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{GL_{n}}
to the universal braided monoidal
category
\mathfrak{B}
governing graded bialgebras
A=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}A_{m}
in braided
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear
monoidal categories with
A_{0}={\bf 1}
being the unit object

[
KS5
]
. That is,
\mathfrak{B}
is generated, as a braided category. by formal symbols
\mathbf{a}_{n}
,
n\geq 0
,
\mathbf{a}_{0}={\bf 1}
,
the component of the
universal graded bialgebra

so that a general object is a product
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}=\mathbf{a}_{n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{a}_{n_{k}}
for all possible
sequences
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{k})
. The space
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}},\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{m}})

of all universal maps
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}\to\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{m}}

obtained by composing multiplications, comultiplications and braidings, such compositions
being subject only to the axioms of a braided bialgebra and nothing else. The category
\mathfrak{B}
splits
into blocks
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
formed by objects
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}
with
\sum n_{i}=n
. Such objects
are in bijection with standard parabolics
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{n}}
in
GL_{n}
and we show (Proposition
5.7.1
) that

\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}\mapsto\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{n}}
defines a functor
\mathfrak{B}_{n}\to{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{GL_{n}}
which we conjecture to be
an equivalence.
This interpretation reflects the phenomenon that the Eisenstein series and constant term
operations for unramified automorphic forms on the groups
GL_{n}
can be intepreted in terms of
Hall (bi)algebras
[
KSV
]
.

Since our motivation comes from representation theory, we work in the setup of reductive
groups but our constructions (such as the category
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
) and results
(such as Theorem
6.1.2
) can be formulated and proved (in the same way)
in the slightly more general context of arbitrary finite Coxeter groups.

0.2.
The perverse Coxeter category as the category of invariants

The category
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
is constructed starting
from another category
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{G}
which we call the
double incidence category
.
It goes back to
[
KS1
]
, whose main result can be formulated as

(0.2.1)

\operatorname{Perv}\nolimits(\mathfrak{h})\,\,{\simeq}\,\,{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathcal{Q},\operatorname{Vect}\limits_{\mathbf{k}})

with
\operatorname{Perv}\nolimits(\mathfrak{h})
being the category of perverse sheaves on
\mathfrak{h}
smooth with respect
to the stratification by intersections of the root hyperplanes. Objects of
\mathcal{Q}
are all parabolic subalgebras
of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{h}
, i.e., they are labelled by the faces of the Coxeter complex of
G
,
see §
4.2
.
The group
W
acts on
\mathcal{Q}
, and
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits({\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}})
is identified with
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q})/W
. Further,

(0.2.2)

{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}=\mathcal{Q}^{W}

is obtained from
\mathcal{Q}
by an extension to linear categories of the familiar procedure
of passing to the subalgebra of invariants, see §
A.6
. For example, if we convert

{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
and
\mathcal{Q}
into associative algebras
A({\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}})
and
A(\mathcal{Q})
(by taking endomorphisms
of the formal direct sum of all objects), then
A({\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}})=A(\mathcal{Q})^{W}
is the usual invariant subalgebra.
Among the relations in
\mathcal{Q}
we can distinguish a precursor of the Langlands formula for
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
, see
Proposition
4.3.3
.

A general property of categories of invariants (Proposition
A.6.7
(b))
realizes
\mathcal{Q}^{W}
inside the Karoubi completion of the semidirect product
\mathcal{Q}\rtimes W
.
The category
\mathcal{Q}\rtimes W
which describes
W
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
\mathfrak{h}

has, in the case
G=GL_{n}
,
W=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, an appealing algebraic interpretation based on a version of the
concept of a graded bialgebra, namely that of a
set bialgebra
, or
a
Hopf monoid in species
in the terminology of
[
AM1
,
AM2
]
.
Our second main result, Theorem
5.4.4
,
realizes
\mathcal{Q}_{GL_{n}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as the
n
-th block of the braided monoidal category
generated by a universal set bialgebra. This corresponds to the fact that the exchange relation
(compatibility of multiplication and comultiplication) for set bialgebras contains
only one term (
5.3.5
) which matches the one-term “proto-Langlands formula” of Proposition

4.3.3
.

0.3.
Invariants in commutative and noncommutative algebra

The identifications (
0.1.1
) (
0.2.1
) and (
0.2.2
)
mean that perverse sheaves on
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
are identified with modules over
W
-invariant subalgebra
A(\mathcal{Q})^{W}

in the associative algebra
A(\mathcal{Q})
whose modules are identified with perverse sheaves on
\mathfrak{h}
.
Note that quasicoherent sheaves on
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
, as on any quotient of an affine variety
by a finite group, are identified with modules over
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{W}
,
the
W
-invariant subalgebra of the commutative algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]
governing
quasicoherent sheaves on
\mathfrak{h}
. This match between two appearances of invariant theory
does not seem obvious on general grounds and deserves further study.

Relation to other work and futute directions

The earlier paper
[
KS4
]
gave a more redundant description of

\operatorname{Perv}\nolimits({W\backslash\mathfrak{h}})

for
G

of

into faces of the root arrangement (the Coxeter complex).
More precisely, mixed Bruhat sheaves are diagrams labelled by cells of the quotient cell decomposition

W\backslash(\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}}\times i\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}})
of
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
.
At the same time, using
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}}\times i\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}}
itself leads to another,
more redundant than that of
[
KS1
]
, description of
\operatorname{Perv}\nolimits(\mathfrak{h})
,
given in
[
KS2
]
,

Mixed Bruhat sheaves can be viewed as functors

from some universal category

given by generators and relations.
In a series of unpublished notes
[
Ta1
]
-
[
Ta3
]
J. Tao established an equivalence of

\mathcal{MBS}_{G}
with a more economic category
similar to our category
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
but given explicitly by generators and relations. These relations
(which include a version of the Langlands formula) do hold in

{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
and we conjecture that our
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
is equivalent to Tao’s category and thus to
\mathcal{MBS}_{G}
.
The statement in §

that
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{GL_{n}}
is equivalent to

is a particular case of this conjecture.
We plan to address this in a future paper by detailed comparison of all the descriptions of
\operatorname{Perv}\nolimits(\mathfrak{h})

In Section

Section

After combinatorial preliminaries, we recall from

]

[

As for graded bialgebras, there is the PROB

The Coxeter complex, the root arrangement and

2.1.
Combinatorics of parabolic subalgebras and the Coxeter complex

There is a natural action of
W
on
\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
; every orbit meets
\mathcal{P}_{G,B}
in exactly one point, i.e.
\mathcal{P}_{G,T}=\bigsqcup_{I}W\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{I}

If

is of

. Reverse inclusion defines a partial order

\mathcal{P}_{G,T}

\mathfrak{p}{\operatorname{\preccurlyeq}\nolimits}\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}

This is a closed polyhedral cone in
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Its relative interior

is described as

C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\circ}\,=\,\bigl\{x\in C_{\mathfrak{p}}\;|\;\alpha(x)>0,\,\,\forall\,\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ s.t. }-\alpha\notin\Delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigr\}.

In particular, for
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{b}
the standard Borel, we denote

,

and call them the open and closed dominant Weyl chambers.
We then have

Remarks 2.4.2

2.5.
The quotients
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
and
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathbb{R}}

The group
W
acts on
\mathfrak{h}
and the quotient
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits\,\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{W}
is known to be isomorphic to

{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
,

. The stratification
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{h}}
of
\mathfrak{h}
by open strata of the (complex)
flats of
\mathcal{H}

descends to a stratification of
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
that we denote
\mathcal{S}
. Thus strata of
\mathcal{S}
are labelled by the
set
W\backslash\mathcal{L}_{G,T}
of
W
-orbits on the set of Levis. We denote by
p:\mathfrak{h}\to{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
the quotient map.

We also have the quotient
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathbb{R}}
of the real part
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}
which we can identify using the cell
decomposition of
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}
into the faces of the Coxeter complex.
The remarks at the beginning of §
2.1
imply that
the (topological) quotient
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is identified with
C_{+}
, the closed dominant Weyl chamber.
In particular, each face of
\mathcal{H}
is taken by
W
-action into a face
C_{I}=C_{\mathfrak{p}_{I}}\subset C_{+}

for a unique subset
I\subset\Pi
of simple roots.
Let
2^{\Pi}
be the set of such subsets.

As any flat of
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathbb{R}
-linearly spanned by some face, we see that the set of flats
H_{I}=H_{\mathfrak{p}_{I}}
associated to standard parabolics
\mathfrak{p}_{I}
,
I\in 2^{\Pi}
,
generates the set
{\operatorname{Fl}\nolimits}(\mathcal{H})
under
W
-action, in other words we have a surjective map

(2.5.1)

\varpi:2^{\Pi}\longrightarrow W\backslash\mathcal{L}_{G,T}.

We denote by
S_{I}\subset{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
the stratum corresponding to
\varpi(I)
.
Two standard parabolics
\mathfrak{p}_{I},\mathfrak{p}_{J}
,
I,J\in 2^{\Pi}
, are called
associated
, if

S_{I}=S_{J}
, i.e.,
\varpi(I)=\varpi(J)
.

Example 2.5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}_{n}
, so
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
W=S_{n}
. The quotient
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is identified with the space of monic polynomials

f(x)=x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n},\hskip 10.00002pta_{i}\in\mathbb{C}.

The strata in
{W\backslash\mathfrak{h}}
are labelled by unordered paritions
\alpha=\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{p}\}
of
n
. The stratum

S_{\alpha}
corrersponding to
\alpha
, consists of polynomials whose multiplicities of roots are given by the
\alpha_{i}
.
In particular, the generic stratum
S_{1,\cdots,1}
consists of polynomials with
n
distinct roots and its complement,
the discriminantal hypersurface, is the closure of the stratum
S_{2,1,\cdots,1}
. So unlike the case of the stratification

\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{h}}
of
\mathfrak{h}
, the closures of the strata in

can be singular.

Standard parabolics in
\mathfrak{g}
are labelled by
compositions
or
ordered partitions

of
n
, i.e., sequences

s.t.
\sum n_{i}=n
. That is, the parabolic
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{n}}
associated to

consists of block-upper-triangular matrices
with diagonal blocks of sizes

. The map
\varpi
sends an ordered partition into the corresponding unordered one, so that two standard
parabolics

are associated iff
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{n}
differ by a permutation.

4.1.
The Tits product and collinearity

We keep the notation of §
1
. Let
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
. The cells
C_{\mathfrak{p}},C_{\mathfrak{q}}
linearly generate a subspace
H_{\mathfrak{l}}
which is associated to a Levi subalgebra

\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{l}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})=\pi(\mathfrak{p})\cap\pi(\mathfrak{q})\in\mathcal{L}_{G,T}.

The
Tits product

C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{q}}

of the (open) cells
C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is defined to be the (uniquely determined) first chamber of
H_{\mathfrak{l}}
met by any line interval
[a,b]
going from
a\in C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{p}}
to

b\in C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Note that this chamber may be
C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{p}}
itself (if
C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a chamber of
H_{\mathfrak{l}}
).
See
[
KS1
, §2.7]
and references therein including the
original work of Tits
[
Ti
]
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathfrak{q}
the parabolic subalgebra associated to the cell

C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{q}}
. The operation
\circ
on
\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
is associative
but not commutative:

\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathfrak{q}
differs in general from
\mathfrak{q}\circ\mathfrak{p}
but they both are chambers of the same subspace
H_{\mathfrak{l}}
. A direct Lie-algebraic definition of
\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathfrak{q}
is as follows
[
KS4
, Prop. 5.5]
:

\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathfrak{q}=(\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak{q})+\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}.

We say that three parabolic subalgebras
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
are
collinear
, if there
are three points
a\in C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{p}},b\in C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{q}},c\in C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{r}}
such that

b\in[a,c]
. In this case the cells
C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{p}},C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{q}},C^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{r}}
will be called
collinear as well. This concept depends on the order of the three subalgebras but is invariant
under
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})\mapsto(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{p})
.

4.2.
The double incidence category

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear category defined by generators and relations as follows:

(0)

Objects of
\mathcal{Q}
are parabolic subalgebras
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
.

(1)

For any pair of parabolic subalgebras
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
such that
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
, we have two generating morphisms

\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},\hskip 20.00003pt{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{p}.

(2)

These morphisms are subject to the following relations:

(2a)

(Identity)
\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}}={\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}}={\operatorname{Id}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(2b)

(Transitivity, or (co)associativity)
For any
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}
,

\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}},\hskip 20.00003pt{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}}\circ{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}={\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}}.

(2c)

(Idempotency) For any
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
we have

{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}={\operatorname{Id}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

The relations (2a)-(2c) allow one to define for any
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
(not necessarily
contained one in another) a morphism
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
as

\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}={\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{q}}
for any

\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
containing both
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. The result is independent on
\mathfrak{q}
;
one can always take, for instance,
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{g}
. Note that if
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
, then
(taking
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
), we have
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
and

\tau_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}}={\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}}
. The remaning relations are formulated in terms of the

\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
:

(2d)

(Collinear transitivity) If
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}
are collinear, then

\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}=\tau_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
.

(2e)

(Invertibility) Let
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{p}\supset\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}
be such that

C_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\supset C_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset C_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}
are codimension
1
inclusions of closed cells, that

C_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}},C_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}
have the same linear span and lie (in this span) on opposite sides of

C_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
and
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{p}}
are invertible
(but not necessarily inverse to each other).

We call
\mathcal{Q}
the
double incidence category
(associated to
G
and
T
).

Remark 4.2.1
.

All generators and relations in
\mathcal{Q}
have a direct geometric interpretation in terms of the realization of the Coxeter complex in
\mathfrak{h}
. The generating morphism

in its closure; it is the other way around for the morphism

whose

4.4.
The perverse Coxeter category

The category
\mathcal{Q}
is defined in terms of the root arrangement
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}
. The Weyl group
W

acts on
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}
preserving
\mathcal{H}
. Note, for example, that the Tits product is
W
-equivariant, i.e. we have

(w\cdot\mathfrak{p})\circ(w\cdot\mathfrak{q})=w\cdot(\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathfrak{q})
for any
w,\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}
.

Therefore
W
acts on
\mathcal{Q}
(in the strict sense, see §
A.2
).
Explicitly,
W
acts on the set
\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
of objects of
\mathcal{Q}
(which are in bijection with faces of
\mathcal{H}
)
and the action on the generating morphisms is given by

w\cdot{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{q}}:={\operatorname{Res}}_{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{q}},\hskip 20.00003ptw\cdot\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{p}}:=\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{w\cdot\mathfrak{q}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}}

for any
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathfrak{p}
which implies

w\cdot\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=\tau_{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}

for any pair
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. The relations are obviously preserved, so we indeed get an action on
\mathcal{Q}
.

We denote by
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{G,T}
the invariant orbit category
\mathcal{Q}^{W}
, see §
A.6
,
and call it the
perverse Coxeter category
(
P-Coxeter category
, for short).
Thus
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}=W\backslash\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
is the set of
W
-orbits on

\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
and the Hom-spaces are given by (
A.6.1
).

As pointed out in §
2.1

W\backslash\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
is identified with
\mathcal{P}_{G,B}
, the set of standard parabolic subalgebras

\mathfrak{p}_{I},I\subset\Pi
.
So we can think of objects of
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
as being the
\mathfrak{p}_{I}
.

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{k})
of

, see Example

. So we can think of objects of
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{GL_{n},T}
as being such
\mathbf{n}
.

a\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2})
we denote by

\underline{a}\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}(W\mathfrak{p}_{1},W\mathfrak{p}_{2})

the image of
a
under the averaging map
\xi_{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
, see (
A.6.3
).
In general, averaging is not compatible with composition of morphisms,
i.e.,
\underline{a\circ b}\neq\underline{a}\circ\underline{b}
. However, there are instances when
equality holds.

Lemma 4.5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
and let
b\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}),a\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})
. Assume that:

(1)

a
is invariant under
{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})\subset W

and
b
under

{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})

(2)

{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})={\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p}),\,\,{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})={\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{q})

or, alternatively,

(2’)

{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})={\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{q}),\,\,{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})={\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{r})
.

Then
\underline{a\circ b}=\underline{a}\circ\underline{b}
.

Proof:
Assume (1) and (2) for definiteness. Then, by (1),

\underline{a}
and
\underline{b}

are the collections

(4.5.2)

\underline{a}=\bigl(wa\bigl|\,w\in W/{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})\bigr),\hskip 10.00002pt\underline{b}=\bigl(yb\bigl|\,y\in W/{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})\bigr).

Let
W(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})\subset\mathcal{P}_{G,T}\times\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
be the
W
-orbit
of the pair
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})
and similarly for
(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})
and
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{r})

By (2), the projections

W(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})\longrightarrow W\mathfrak{q},\hskip 10.00002ptW(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})\to W\mathfrak{p}

are bijections and therefore

(4.5.3)

W(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})\times_{W\mathfrak{q}}W(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})\,\,{\simeq}\,\,W(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{r}).

Now (
4.5.2
) realizes
\underline{a}
as a collection labelled by
W(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})

and
\underline{b}
as a collection labelled by
W(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})
. Further, from
(1) and (2) we see that
\underline{ab}
is represented as a collection labelled
by
W(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{r})
, i.e., by the RHS of (
4.5.2
). Further, by the composition rule
in
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}=\mathcal{Q}^{W}
, the morphism
\underline{a}\circ\underline{b}

is represented as a collection labelled by the LHS of (
4.5.2
)
and we see that the morphisms with the corresponding labels coincide,
hence our statement. The case of (1) and (2’) is similar. ∎

We now deduce several consequences.

Proposition 4.5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{2}\ \subset\mathfrak{p}_{3}
be three parabolics in

\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
. Then

\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}=\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}\circ\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}=\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}.\

Proof:
To prove the first equality, we apply Lemma
4.5.1

with conditions (1) and (2) to
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{r}=\mathfrak{p}_{3}

and
a=\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}
,
b=\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
. The condition
(1) is clear, while (2) is a consequence of the following property
of the
W
-action on the Coxeter complex.
If
w\in W
preserves a face,
it fixes each point of this face and so preserves each subface.
Our statement now follows from the lemma and the transitivity of
the
\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits
-morphisms in
\mathcal{Q}
. The case of
\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}
is similar,
applying the lemma to
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{3},\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{r}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}

and using the conditions (1) and (2’). ∎.

Proposition 4.5.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{p}_{3}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
be such that the faces

C_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}},C_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}},C_{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}\subset\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}
have the same
linear span
L
, are open there and collinear. Then

\underline{\tau}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}=\underline{\tau}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}\circ\underline{\tau}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
.

Proof:
We apply Lemma
4.5.1
to

\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{r}=\mathfrak{p}_{3}
and
a=\tau_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}
,

b=\tau_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
. The condition (1) is again clear, while
(2) (as well as (2’)) follows because

{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p}_{i},\mathfrak{p}_{j})={\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})={\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p}_{j})

coincides with the stabilizer of
L
. ∎

Proposition 4.5.6
.

In the situation of Proposition
4.3.2
, we have equalities of morphisms in
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}
:

\underline{\tau}^{\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{1}}\circ\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{+}}=\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{+}}\circ\underline{\tau}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}},\hskip 20.00003pt\underline{\tau}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\circ\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{+}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}=\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{+}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ\underline{\tau}^{\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{1}}.

Proof:
To prove the first statement, we apply Lemma
4.5.1
to get the equalities

\underline{\tau}^{\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{1}}\circ\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{+}}=\underline{\tau^{\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{1}}\circ{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{+}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{{\operatorname{Pr.}\ref{prop:Ind-Res-funct-Q}}}}\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{+}}\circ\ \tau^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}}=\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{+}}\circ\underline{\tau}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}

The conditions (1) and (2) for the individual morphisms have been already established
in the proofs of Propositions
4.5.4
and
4.5.5

4.6.
The Langlands formula

We now deduce the following.

Proposition 4.6.1

(Langlands formula)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2}
be parabolic subalgebras,
O_{1}=W\mathfrak{p}_{1},O_{2}=W\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. Let also
W_{i}={\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})\subset W
be the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Then

\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{g}}=\sum_{w\in W_{1}\backslash W/W_{2}}\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ\underline{\tau}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\circ w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\circ\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\circ w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}

Proof:

We first note that for any
\mathfrak{p}\ \mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{G,T}
with

\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathfrak{p}
the morphisms
\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{p}}

and
{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{q}}
in
\mathcal{Q}
are invariant under
{\operatorname{Stab}}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q})\subset W
.
Therefore

\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigl(\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\bigr)_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}\in O_{1}}\in\biggl(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}\in O_{1}}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{g})\biggr)^{W},\hskip 10.00002pt\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}=\bigl({\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}\bigr)_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}\in O_{2}}\in\biggl(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}\in O_{2}}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}_{2})\biggr)^{W}.

By definition of composition in the orbit category and the proto-Langlands formula
(Proposition
4.3.3
), the LHS of the claimed equality is the collection

(4.6.2)

\biggl(\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{q}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}\circ\mathfrak{q}_{2}}^{\mathfrak{q}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{q}_{1}}\circ{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{q}_{1}\circ\mathfrak{q}_{2}}\biggr)_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}\in O_{i}}\in\biggl(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}\in O_{i}}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{q}_{2})\biggr)^{W}.

Now, the
W
-invariant collection of morphisms given by (
4.6.2
) is uniquely recovered from the
subcollection given by representatives of the
W
-orbits on
O_{1}\times O_{2}
. Noticing that

W\backslash(O_{1}\times O_{2})\,\,{\simeq}\,\,W_{1}\backslash W/W_{2},

we identify this subcollection with

\biggl(\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ{\tau}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\circ w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\circ{\operatorname{Res}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\circ w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\biggr)_{w\in W_{1}\backslash W/W_{2}}

The morphism obtained by extending this subcollection by
W
-invariance, is precisely the RHS if the claimed equality.
∎

More generally, for any
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{3}\supset\mathfrak{p}_{2}

with Weyl groups
W_{1}\subset W_{3}\supset W_{2}

we have

(4.6.3)

\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{3}}=\sum_{w\in W_{1}\backslash W_{3}/W_{2}}\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits}_{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ\underline{\tau}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\circ w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\circ\underline{{\operatorname{Res}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\circ w\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}.

The proof is identical to the above.

5.
The case of
GL_{n}

The aim of this Section is to make explicit the structure of the P-Coxeter category for the collection of reductive groups
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and show that this recovers the structure of the PROB of (graded) braided bialgebras, as in
[
KS5
]
.

5.1.
Combinatorial identifications

Let
I
be a finite set. We denote
GL_{I}=GL(\mathbb{C}^{I})
and let
T_{I}\subset GL(I)
be the
maximal torus (invertible diagonal matrices)
corresponding to the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{I}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{Q}_{I}
and
\mathcal{C}_{I}
the double incidence category, resp. the P-Coxeter category associated to the pair

(GL_{I},T_{I})
.
When
I=\{1,\cdots,n\}
we will simply write
T_{n}
for
T_{\{1,\cdots,n\}}
and use
the notations

\mathcal{Q}_{n}
and
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{n}
for the above categories. Thus if
|I|=n
, then there are
isomorphisms
\mathcal{Q}_{I}\simeq\mathcal{Q}_{n}
and
\mathcal{C}_{I}\,\,{\simeq}\,\,{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{n}
.

Recall that
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}_{n})=\mathcal{P}_{GL_{n},T_{n}}
is the set of parabolic subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}_{n}
containing

\mathfrak{h}_{n}={\operatorname{Lie}}(T_{n})
(the set of diagonal matrices). Such subalgebras are in bijection
with
ordered set partitions
,
i.e., sequences of subsets

\mathbf{I}=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}),\hskip 10.00002pt\emptyset\neq I_{\nu}\subset\{1,\cdots,n\},\hskip 10.00002ptI_{1}\sqcup I_{2}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup I_{k}=\{1,\ldots,n\}.

For such
\mathbf{I}
and
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
we denote
\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(i)\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
to be the unique
\nu

such that
i\in I_{\nu}
.
The parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}
corresponding to an ordered set partition

\mathbf{I}
is the stabilizer of the flag

F_{\mathbf{I}}=V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset\ldots\subset V_{k}=\mathbb{C}^{n},\text{ where }V_{\nu}=\bigoplus_{i\in I_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup I_{\nu}}\mathbb{C}e_{i}.

Recall further that
\mathcal{P}_{GL_{n},T_{n}}
is identified with the set of faces (cells) of
\mathcal{H}_{n}
,
the arrangement of the diagonal hyperplaces in
\mathfrak{h}_{n,\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If a parabolic subalgebra

\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}
is associated to an ordered set partition
\mathbf{I}
, then its corresponding face is

(5.1.1)

C_{\mathbf{I}}\,=\,\bigl\{(t_{1},\cdots t_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigl|\,t_{i}=t_{j}\text{ when }\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(i)=\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(j)\text{ and }t_{i}<t_{j}\text{ when }\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(i)<\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(j)\bigr\}.

In plain words
(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})\in C_{\mathbf{I}}
iff the pattern of equalities and inequalities among the
t_{i}

is given by
\mathbf{I}
.

Any ordered set partition
\mathbf{I}=(U_{1},\cdots,I_{k})
defines an unordered set partition

\{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}\}
by forgetting the order of the
I_{\nu}
. The following is straightforward.

Proposition 5.1.2
.

Let
\mathbf{I},\mathbf{J}
be two ordered set partitions of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
. Then:

(a)
C_{\mathbf{I}}\subset\overline{C}_{\mathbf{J}}
if and only if
\mathbf{J}
is a refinement of
\mathbf{I}
.

(b)
C_{\mathbf{I}},C_{\mathbf{J}}
are chambers of the same flat of
\mathcal{H}_{n}
if and only if the corresponding
unordered partitions are equal.

(c) Further,
C_{\mathbf{I}},C_{\mathbf{J}}
are chambers of the same flat that lie on opposite sides of a wall if and only if

\mathbf{I}
is obtained from
\mathbf{J}
by exchanging two adjacent parts. ∎

In fact, the hyperplane arrangement induced on the flat
H_{\mathfrak{l}}
associated to an unordered partition,

\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
is isomorphic to that of type
A_{k-1}
. In particular, the group
W(\mathfrak{l})
is isomorphic to the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
and acts on chambers by permuting the parts.
Further, the Tits product on
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}_{n})
can be combinatorially described as follows,
cf.
[
AM2
, §1.6]
and references therein.

Proposition 5.1.3
.

Let
\mathbf{I}=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{k})
and
\mathbf{J}=(J_{1},\ldots,J_{l})
be two ordered set partitions of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Set
K_{i,j}=I_{i}\cap J_{j}
, so that we have a disjoint decomposition

\bigsqcup_{i,j}K_{i,j}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Endowing the set of pairs
(i,j)
with
1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq l
with the lexicographic order and removing any empty entry, we obtain an ordered set partition
\mathbf{K}
(with at most
kl
sets).
Then
C_{\mathbf{I}}\circ C_{\mathbf{J}}=C_{\mathbf{K}}
.

Proof:

Let
(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})\in C_{\mathbf{I}}
and
(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n})\in C_{\mathbf{J}}
.
By definition of
\circ
and by (
5.1.1
),
C_{\mathbf{I}}\circ C_{\mathbf{J}}=C_{\mathbf{L}}
,
where
\mathbf{L}
expresses the pattern of equalities and inequalities among the numbers
t_{i}+{\varepsilon}s_{i}
,
where
{\varepsilon}>0
is very small. Applying (
5.1.1
) to
C_{\mathbf{I}}
and
C_{\mathbf{J}}
,
we see that the equivalence relation on
\{1,\cdots,n\}
given by the
pattern of equalities among the
t_{i}+{\varepsilon}s_{i}
has, as its equivalence classes, precisely the
nonempty
K_{i,j}
. After this we see that the pattern of strict inequalities is given by the lexicographic
order on the
K_{i,j}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{K}
. ∎

Further,
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits({\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{n})
, i.e., the set of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-orbits on the collection of ordered set partitions
of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
, is
the collection of ordered partitions
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
of the number
n
.
The projection map
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}_{n})\to\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits({\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}_{n})
sends
(I_{1},\ldots,I_{k})
to
(|I_{1}|,\ldots,|I_{k}|)
.

5.2.
Graded bialgebras

In this paper we will use the term “braided category’ to signify, for short, a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear braided monoidal category.
Let
(\mathcal{V},\otimes)
be a braided category with the unit object and the

{\bf 1}
and
R=(R_{VW}:V\otimes W\to W\otimes V)
respectively.
Recall that a

in
\mathcal{V}
is an object

together with an associative

\mu:A\otimes A\to A
, coassociative comultiplication

(B1)

(Unitality) The two compositions

are equal to
{\operatorname{Id}}_{A}
.

(B2)

(Counitality) The two compositions
(\eta\otimes{\operatorname{Id}})\circ\Delta,({\operatorname{Id}}\otimes\eta)\circ\Delta:A\to A

are equal to
{\operatorname{Id}}_{A}
.

(B3)

(Exchange) The diagram commutes:

\textstyle{A\otimes A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta\otimes\Delta}
\scriptstyle{\mu}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta}
\textstyle{A\otimes A}
\textstyle{A\otimes A\otimes A\otimes A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\operatorname{Id}}\otimes R_{AA}\otimes{\operatorname{Id}}}
\textstyle{A\otimes A\otimes A\otimes A.\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu\otimes\mu}

We will need two versions of this concept, one in this and one in the next subsection.

Example 5.2.1
.

(Graded bialgebras). Assume first that
\mathcal{V}
has direct sums
(which by
{\mathbf{k}}
-linearity of
(\mathcal{V},\otimes)
are then automatically distributive with respect to
\otimes
).
Let
\mathcal{V}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the category of
\mathbb{N}
-graded objects
V=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{n}
of
\mathcal{V}
.
The category
\mathcal{V}^{\mathbb{N}}
inherits from
\mathcal{V}

the braided monoidal structure.
An object of
\mathcal{V}^{\mathbb{N}}
can also be thought as a collection
V=(V_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of its graded components,
so that

(5.2.2)

(V\otimes W)_{n}=\bigoplus_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}V_{n_{1}}\otimes W_{n_{2}}.

By a
graded
bialgebra
in
\mathcal{V}
we will mean a bialgebra
A
in
\mathcal{V}^{\mathbb{N}}
with

A_{0}={\bf 1}
and such that
{\varepsilon}

is the embedding
{\bf 1}=A_{0}\to A
and
\eta
is the projecton

.

More explicitly, a graded bialgebra in
\mathcal{V}
can be defined componentwise, as a datum of objects
A_{n}\in\mathcal{V}
,
A_{0}={\bf 1}

and morphisms

\mu_{n_{1},n_{2}}:A_{n_{1}}\otimes A_{n_{2}}\to A_{n_{1}+n_{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\Delta_{n_{1},n_{2}}:A_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\to A_{n_{1}}\otimes A_{n_{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptn_{1},n_{2}\geq 0,

satisfying

(GB1)

(Unitality) The morphisms
\mu_{0,n},\mu_{n,0}
are equal to the unit isomorphisms.

(GB2)

(Counitality) The morphisms
\Delta_{0,n},\Delta_{n,0}
are equal to the inverses
of the unit isomorphisms.

(GB3)

(Exchange) Let
p_{1}+p_{2}=q_{1}+q_{2}=n
. Then the composition

is equal to the sum of compositions

\begin{gathered}\sum_{\begin{bmatrix}n_{11}&n_{12}\\

the summation being over matrices

\begin{bmatrix}n_{11}&n_{12}\\
n_{21}&n_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in{{\operatorname{Mat}}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{s.t.}\hskip 10.00002ptn_{i1}+n_{i2}=p_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptn_{1j}+n_{2j}=q_{j}.

This explicit form allows us to speak about graded bialgebras in
\mathcal{V}
without assuming the existence
of direct sums.

5.3.
The braided category of
\mathfrak{S}
-modules and set bialgebras

Assuming again the existence of
direct sums in
\mathcal{V}
, we can construct a new
braided category
\mathcal{V}^{\mathfrak{S}}
of
\mathfrak{S}
-modules
, or
species
in
\mathcal{V}
,
cf.
[
AM1
]

[
AM2
]
.
By definition, an object of
\mathcal{V}^{\mathfrak{S}}
is a collection
X=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of objects of
\mathcal{V}

together with the action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
X_{n}
for each
n
. The category

\mathcal{V}^{\mathfrak{S}}
inherits from
\mathcal{V}
the
braided monoidal structure, with the monoidal operation
\odot
given by

(5.3.1)

(X\odot Y)_{n}=\bigoplus_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{S}_{n_{1}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n_{2}}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(X_{n_{1}}\otimes Y_{n_{2}}).

The unit object of
\mathcal{V}^{\mathfrak{S}}
is the collection
{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathfrak{S}}}
whose
0
th component is
{\bf 1}\in\mathcal{V}

and other components are
0
.

Alternatively, we can view an object
V
of
\mathcal{V}^{\mathfrak{S}}
as a
\mathcal{V}
-valued functor

X:I\mapsto V_{I},\hskip 10.00002ptI\mapsto X_{I}

from the groupoid of finite sets and their isomorphisms, so that
X_{n}
in the earlier
sense is recovered as
X_{\{1,\cdots,n\}}
.
In this language the formula (
5.3.1
) for
\odot
appears as

(5.3.2)

(X\odot Y)_{I}=\bigoplus_{I=I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}}X_{I_{1}}\otimes Y_{I_{2}},

the direct sum over all disjoint decompositions
{I=I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}}
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is Karoubi-complete (possesses the images of all projectors), then for any object
E\in\mathcal{V}

with an action of a finite group
G
we have a well defined subobject of invariants
E^{G}
,
see §
A.4
. In this
case we have a functor

(5.3.3)

\operatorname{Inv}\nolimits:\mathcal{V}^{\mathfrak{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\mathbb{N}},\hskip 10.00002pt(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}\mapsto(X_{n}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}})_{n\geq 0}.

This functor is braided monoidal because (
5.3.1
) reduces, after taking invariants, to
(
5.2.2
). It was considered in
[
AM1
, Def. 15.5]

for the case
\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{Vect}\limits_{\mathbf{k}}
.

Example 5.3.4
.

(Set bialgebras)
By a
set bialgebra
in
\mathcal{V}
we will mean a bialgebra
U=(U_{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{V}^{\mathfrak{S}}

with
U_{0}={\bf 1}
whose unit (resp. counit) morphism is the embedding of (resp. projection to)
U_{0}
.

More explicitly, a set bialgebra
U
can be seen as a following set of data:

(i)

A functor
I\mapsto U_{I}
from the groupoid of finite sets to
\mathcal{V}
, with
U_{\emptyset}={\bf 1}
.

(ii)

For any (ordered) pair
(I,J)
of finite sets, morphisms

{\mu}_{I,J}:U_{I}\otimes U_{J}\longrightarrow U_{I\sqcup J},\hskip 20.00003pt{\Delta}_{I,J}:U_{I\sqcup J}\ \longrightarrow U_{I}\otimes U_{J},

natural in (isomorphisms of)
I
and
J
and
satisfying the following conditions:

(SB0)

The maps
\mu
, resp.
\Delta
are associative, resp. coassociative in the obvious sense.

(SB1-2)

((Co)unitality) The maps
\mu_{I,\emptyset},m_{\emptyset,I},\Delta_{I,\emptyset},\Delta_{\emptyset,I}
are all given by unit morphisms or their inverses.

(SB3)

(Exchange) Suppose a finite set
I
has two disjoint decompositions
I=J_{1}\sqcup J_{2}=L_{1}\sqcup L_{2}
.
Put
K_{ij}=J_{i}\cap L_{j}
. Then
the composition

\textstyle{U_{J_{1}}\otimes U_{J_{2}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu_{J_{1},J_{2}}}
\textstyle{U_{I}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta_{L_{1},L_{2}}}
\textstyle{U_{L_{1}}\otimes U_{L_{2}}}

is equal to the composed morphism

(5.3.5)

\begin{split}U_{J_{1}}\otimes U_{J_{2}}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta_{K_{11},K_{12}}\otimes\Delta_{K_{21},K_{22}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}U_{K_{11}}\otimes U_{K_{12}}\otimes U_{K_{21}}\otimes U_{K_{22}}\to\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R_{U_{K_{12}},U_{K_{21}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}U_{K_{11}}\otimes U_{K_{21}}\otimes U_{K_{12}}\otimes U_{K_{22}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu_{K_{11},K_{21}}\otimes\mu_{K_{12},K_{22}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}U_{L_{1}}\otimes U_{L_{2}}.\end{split}

Remark 5.3.6
.

As before, this explicit form allows us to speak about set bialgebras in
\mathcal{V}
without assuming
the existence of direct sums. In fact, since the relations above do not involve summation,
one can speak about set bialgebras in not necessary additive categories.
Such structures have been discussed and studied in

[
AM1
]

[
AM2
]
under the
name
Hopf monoids in the category of species
. Our considerations are nominally more general
as we allow species taking values in arbitrary (
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear) braided categories
\mathcal{V}
(the case of the braiding
on
\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{Vect}\limits_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbb{N}}

corresponding to
q
-deformation of commutativity was already considered in

[
AM1
]

[
AM2
]
).

Axioms (SB1-2) imply that for any ordered set partition
\mathbf{I}=(I_{1},\cdots,I_{k})

of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
we have well defined iterated multiplication and comultiplication morphisms

\mu_{\mathbf{I}}=\mu_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}:U_{I_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{I_{k}}\to U_{n},\hskip 10.00002pt\Delta_{\mathbf{I}}=\Delta_{I_{1},\cdots,I-k}:U_{n}\longrightarrow U_{I_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{I_{k}}.

Further, (SB3) implies, by iteration, the following general exchange rule.

Proposition 5.3.7
.

In the situation and notation of Proposition
5.1.3
, the composition

U_{I_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{I_{k}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mu_{\mathbf{I}}}}U_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\Delta_{\mathbf{J}}}}U_{J_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{J_{l}}

is equal to the composition

\bigotimes_{i}U_{I_{i}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\bigotimes_{i}\Delta_{K_{i1},\cdots,K_{il}}}}\bigotimes_{i}\bigotimes_{j}U_{K_{ij}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{R}}\bigotimes_{j}\bigotimes_{i}U_{K_{ij}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\bigotimes_{j}\mu_{K_{1j},\cdots,K_{kj}}}}\bigotimes_{j}U_{J_{j}},

where
R
is the braiding isomorphism in
\mathcal{V}
connecting the horizontal (along the rows) and the vertical (along the
columns) orderings of the tensor product
\bigotimes_{i,j}U_{K_{ij}}
. ∎

Recall the braided monoidal functor
\operatorname{Inv}\nolimits
from (
5.3.3
).

Proposition 5.3.8
.

Assume that
\mathcal{V}
is Karoubi-complete. If
U
is a set bialgebra in
\mathcal{V}
, then
\operatorname{Inv}\nolimits(U)=(U_{n}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}})_{n\geq 0}

is a graded bialgebra.

Proof:
If
\mathcal{V}
has direct sums, this follows from the fact that the functor
\operatorname{Inv}\nolimits
in
(
5.3.3
) is braided monoidal. The general case is reduced to this by passing to
\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}
,
the direct sum completion of
\mathcal{V}
.
∎

5.4.
The PROBs of graded bialgebras and of set bialgebras

Let
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}
be braided categories. We denote by
{\operatorname{BFun}}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})

the category of
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear braided monoidal functors
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{W}
.
By
{\operatorname{GBialg}}(\mathcal{V})
and
{\operatorname{SBialg}}(\mathcal{V})
we denote the categories of graded bialgebras and set bialgebras
in
\mathcal{V}
respectively.

As in
[
KS5
]
, we denote by
\mathfrak{B}
the PROB governing graded bialgebras, i.e.,
a braided category generated by the components of the
universal graded bialgebra

\mathbf{a}
.
It is characterized uniquely up to braided monoidal equivalence by the (2-)natural equivalence of categories

(5.4.1)

{\operatorname{GBialg}}(\mathcal{V})\,\,\,{\simeq}\,\,\,{\operatorname{BFun}}(\mathfrak{B},\mathcal{V})

for any braided category
\mathcal{V}
.

Explicitly,
\mathfrak{B}
is generated as a braided category, by formal objects
\mathbf{a}_{n}
,
n\geq 0
,

\mathbf{a}_{0}={\bf 1}
and generating morphisms

(5.4.2)

\mu_{n_{1},n_{2}}:\mathbf{a}_{n_{1}}\otimes\mathbf{a}_{n_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{a}_{n_{1}+n_{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\Delta_{n_{1},n_{2}}:\mathbf{a}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{a}_{n_{1}}\otimes\mathbf{a}_{n_{2}},

which are subject only to associativity, coassociativity and
the relations (GB1)-(GB3) forming the axioms of a graded bialgebra.
The identification (
5.4.1
) takes
F\in{\operatorname{BFun}}(\mathfrak{B},\mathcal{V})
to the graded bialgebra

A=(A_{n}=F(\mathbf{a}_{n}))_{n\geq 0}
whose multiplication and comultiplication morphisms are
the images under
F
of (
5.4.2
).

Thus objects of
\mathfrak{B}
are tensor products
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}=\mathbf{a}_{n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{a}_{n_{k}}

for all sequences
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{k})
of positive integers, as well as
\mathbf{a}_{0}={\bf 1}
.
Denote
|\mathbf{n}|=\sum n_{i}
.
Let
\mathfrak{B}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be the full subcategory on objects
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}
with
|\mathbf{n}|=n
.
Then we have a decomposition into blocks

\mathfrak{B}=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{B}_{n},\text{ i.e., }\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathfrak{B}_{m},\mathfrak{B}_{n})=0,\,\,m\neq n.

Note that two sequences
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}
differing by permutation of the
n_{i}
, represent isomorphic
(by braiding) objects. So isomorphism classes of objects of
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
are labelled by unordered
partitions of
n
.

Similarly, we denote by
{\mathfrak{SB}}
and call the
PROB of set bialgebras
the braided
category characterized by the (2-)natural equivalence

(5.4.3)

{\operatorname{SBialg}}(\mathcal{V})\,\,\,{\simeq}\,\,\,{\operatorname{BFun}}({\mathfrak{SB}},\mathcal{V})

for any braided category
\mathcal{V}
. Explicitly,
{\mathfrak{SB}}
is generated, as a braided category,
by the components of the
universal set bialgebra

\mathbf{u}
, i.e., by formal objects
\mathbf{u}_{I}
,
one for each finite set
I
, with
u_{\emptyset}={\bf 1}
and by generating morphisms

\begin{gathered}\gamma_{\sigma}:\mathbf{u}_{I}\to\mathbf{u}_{J}\text{ for any bijection }\sigma:I\to J,\\
\mu_{I,J}:\mathbf{u}_{I}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{J}\longrightarrow\mathbf{u}_{I\sqcup J},\qquad\Delta_{I,J}:\mathbf{u}_{I\sqcup J}\longrightarrow\mathbf{u}_{I}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{J}\end{gathered}

subject only to the relations of functoriality (
\gamma_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\gamma_{\sigma}\circ\gamma_{\sigma^{\prime}}
,

\gamma_{\operatorname{Id}}={\operatorname{Id}}
), of naturality of the
\mu_{I,J}
and
\Delta_{I,J}
and to the relations (SB0-3) defining set bialgebras.

Thus objects of
{\mathfrak{SB}}
other than
{\bf 1}=\mathbf{u}_{\emptyset}
have the form

\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{I}}=\mathbf{u}_{I_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{u}_{I_{k}}
for all sequences
\mathbf{I}=(I_{1},\cdots,I_{k})

of nonempty finite sets. As before, we have a decomposition into blocks

{\mathfrak{SB}}=\coprod_{n\geq 0}{\mathfrak{SB}}_{n},

where
{\mathfrak{SB}}_{n}\subset{\mathfrak{SB}}
is the full subcategory on objects
\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{I}}

with
\sum|I_{\nu}|=n
.

We will also write
\mathbf{u}_{n}
for
\mathbf{u}_{\{1,\cdots,n\}}
.
Here is the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.4.4
.

We have an equivalences of categories

{\mathfrak{SB}}_{n}\,\,{\simeq}\,\,\mathcal{Q}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

Here
\mathcal{Q}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the semidirect product, see §
A.5
.

The proof will occupy the remainder of this section.

5.5.
Braided monoidal structure on
\coprod_{n}(\mathcal{Q}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n})

To prove Theorem
5.4.4
we need, first of all, construct functors

{\mathfrak{SB}}_{n}\to\mathcal{Q}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
for all
n
.
For this it is enough, in view
of (
5.4.3
), to make
\coprod_{n}(\mathcal{Q}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n})
into a braided category
and exhibit a set bialgebra
U=(U_{n})
in it such that each
U_{n}
lies in the
n
th block.
To do this, we note the following.

The collection of complex reductive groups of the type

GL_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times GL_{n_{r}},\hskip 20.00003ptn_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\geq 1

has the remarkable (and often used) property of being stable under the operation of passing to a parabolic subgroup. In particular, the parabolic subgroup

P_{n,m}=\begin{pmatrix}GL_{n}&\star\\

gives rise to a canonical parabolic correspondence

We use this to define a functor

(5.5.1)

\otimes=\otimes_{n,m}:\mathcal{Q}_{n}\times\mathcal{Q}_{m}\to\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}

as follows. Denote by the same symbols
p,i
the maps of Lie algebras. This provides an identification of Cartan subalgebras
\mathfrak{h}_{GL_{n}}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{GL_{m}}\simeq\mathfrak{h}_{GL_{n+m}}
. Under this identification, the Coxeter complex of
GL_{n+m}
is a refinement of that of
GL_{n}\times GL_{m}
.
At the level of objects, we define
\otimes_{n,m}
to be given by the map

(5.5.2)

\kappa_{n,m}:\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}_{n})\times\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}_{m})\to\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}),\hskip 20.00003pt(\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2})\mapsto\mathfrak{p}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{2}:=p^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{2}).

In the language of §
5.1
, objects
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}
of
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
are represented by
set partitions
\mathbf{I}=(I_{1},\cdots,I_{k})
of
\{1,\cdots n\}
, and those of
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
by
set parititons
\mathbf{J}=(J_{1},\cdots,J_{l})
of
\{1,\cdots,m\}
. If

(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}},\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}})\in\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}_{n})\times\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}_{m})
,
then
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}=\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}*\mathbf{J}}
, where

(5.5.3)

\mathbf{I}*\mathbf{J}\,:=\,\mathbf{I}\sqcup(\mathbf{J}+n)\,:=\,(I_{1},\cdots,I_{k},J_{1}+n,\cdots,J_{l}+n)

is the concatenation of
\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{J}
.
Here
J_{\nu}+n
, resp
(\mathbf{J}+n)
stands for the result of adding
n
to all integers occuring in

J_{\nu}
resp. in a part of
\mathbf{J}
.

Let us now define
\otimes_{n,m}
on morphisms.
Observe that
\kappa_{n,m}
preserves incidence of parabolic subalgebras in each variable,
i.e., the operation
(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{J})\mapsto\mathbf{I}*\mathbf{J}
preserves refinement of
set partitions in each variable: if
\mathbf{I}
refines
\mathbf{I}^{\prime}
, then
\mathbf{I}*\mathbf{J}
refines

\mathbf{I}^{\prime}*\mathbf{J}
and similarly in the other variable. So we define
\otimes=\otimes_{n,m}
on
generating morphisms (in each variable) by putting

(5.5.4)

\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}\,=\,\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}*\mathbf{J}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}*\mathbf{J}}},\hskip 10.00002pt{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}\,=\,{\operatorname{Res}}_{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}*\mathbf{J}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}*\mathbf{J}}}

and similarly in the other variable.

Proposition 5.5.5
.

The values of
\otimes_{n,m}
on generating morphisms defined above,
together with the map (
5.5.2
) on objects, define
a functor
\otimes_{n,m}:\mathcal{Q}_{n}\times\mathcal{Q}_{m}\to\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}
. The collection of functors

\otimes_{n,m}

endows
\coprod_{n}\mathcal{Q}_{n}

with the structure of a monoidal category.

Proof:

Notice that
\kappa_{n,m}
also preserves
collinearity in each variable: if
(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{I}^{\prime},\mathbf{I}^{\prime\prime})
are collinear, then so are

(\mathbf{I}*\mathbf{J},\mathbf{I}^{\prime}*\mathbf{J},\mathbf{I}^{\prime\prime}*\mathbf{J})
and similarly in the other variable. This implies that for each
\mathbf{J}

the rule (
5.5.4
) extends to
a functor
-\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}:\mathcal{Q}_{n}\to\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}
, and for each
\mathbf{I}

we simillarly get a functor
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes-:\mathcal{Q}_{m}\to\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}
. To see that they unite
into a functor
\otimes_{n,m}:\mathcal{Q}_{n}\times\mathcal{Q}_{m}\to\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}
, we need to check the
commutativity of these single variable tensor products. That is, for each
f\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Q}_{n}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}},\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}})

and
g\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Q}_{m}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}},\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}^{\prime}})
the diagram in
\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}

(5.5.6)

\textstyle{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes g}
\scriptstyle{f\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}^{\prime}}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\otimes g}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}^{\prime}}}

is commutative, so both composition represent a well-defined morphism
f\otimes g:\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}\to\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}^{\prime}}
.

It is sufficient to verify this commutativity when

and

are generating morphisms, i.e., of the form

\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits
or
{\operatorname{Res}}
. If they are both of the type
\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits
, this follows from transitivity of the morphisms
\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits

in
\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}
and similarly in the case when they are both of type
{\operatorname{Res}}
.
Let us consider the mixed case when

f=\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}}
,

where
\mathbf{I}
refines
\mathbf{I}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{J}^{\prime}

refines
\mathbf{J}
. In this case the lower path in the diagram (

) represents the morphism

\tau_{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}}}^{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}^{\prime}}}
(as defined in the axiom (2c) of

), since it goes through a common subface of the faces

and

C_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}*\mathbf{J}^{\prime}}
. The arrows in the upper path are identified with the corresponding
\tau

since they go through a common over-face of
C_{\mathbf{I}*\mathbf{J}}
and

C_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}*\mathbf{J}^{\prime}}
. So the equality of these morphisms follows from collinear transitivity, since

(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}},\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}^{\prime}},\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{J}^{\prime}})

are collinear (the parabolic in the middle corresponds to the common over-face).
The other mixed case when
f
is of type
{\operatorname{Res}}
and
g
is of type
\operatorname{Ind}\nolimits
, is considered similarly.
This proves the well-definedness of the functors
\otimes_{n,m}
. The fact that they define a monoidal
structure (i.e., associativity) is straightforward. ∎

Note that the functor
\otimes_{n,m}
is equivariant with respect to the symmetric group actions
on the source and target and the embedding
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathfrak{S}_{m}\to\mathfrak{S}_{n+m}
.
So it induces a functor which we denote by the same symbol

\otimes_{n,m}:(\mathcal{Q}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n})\times(\mathcal{Q}_{m}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{m})\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{n+m}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n+m}.

Proposition
5.5.5

on
V
is
a projector
{\varepsilon}_{V}:V\to V
. We denote by
V^{G}
and call
the
object of invariants
the object
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits({\varepsilon}_{V})\in\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{Kar}}
.

Assume that
(\mathcal{V},\otimes)
is monoidal. If
G_{i}
acts on
V_{i}
,
i=1,2
, then

(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})^{G_{1}\times G_{2}}\,\,{\simeq}\,\,V_{1}^{G_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{G_{2}}
.

A.5.
The semidirect product category

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear cateory with finitely many objects,
equipped with a strict action of a finite group
G
.
The
semi-direct product

\mathcal{V}\rtimes G
is the category with the same set of objects as
\mathcal{V}
and
morphisms given by

(A.5.1)

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}\rtimes G}(V,W):=\biggl\{\sum_{g\in G}(\xi,g)\;|\;\xi\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(\gamma_{g}(V),W)\biggr\},\hskip 10.00002ptV,W\in\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{V}).

For each given
g
, the symbols
(\xi,g)
are assumed to be
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear in
\xi
.
The composition of morphisms is given by

(A.5.2)

(\xi,g)\circ(\zeta,h)=(\xi\circ\gamma_{g}(\zeta),gh)

whenever this makes sense.
Intuitively,
\mathcal{V}\rtimes G
is the category obtained from
\mathcal{V}
by adding isomorphisms

({\operatorname{Id}},g):V\to\gamma_{g}(V)
.

Examples A.5.3
.

(a) Suppose
\mathcal{V}
has one object
V
, i.e., reduces to an associative algebra
A=\operatorname{End}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(V)

with
G
-action.
Then
\mathcal{V}\rtimes G
also has one object and its endomorphism algebra is

\operatorname{End}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}\rtimes G}(V)=A[G]
, the twisted group algebra of
G
, see Example

A.2.1
(b).

(b) Generalizing the property cited in Example

A.2.1
(b), we have an identification

{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})^{[G]}\simeq{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathcal{V}\rtimes G,\mathcal{W})

for any category
\mathcal{W}
equipped with a trivial
G
-action.

The relation to
\mathcal{V}\rtimes G
with twisted group algebras can be generalized as follows. Denote by

\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}
the category obtained from
\mathcal{V}
by adding formal direct sums.
We can assign to
\mathcal{V}
an associative
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra called the
total algebra
of
\mathcal{V}
, defined as

(A.5.4)

A(\mathcal{V})=\bigoplus_{V_{1},V_{2}\in\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(V_{1},V_{2})=\operatorname{End}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}}\biggl(\bigoplus_{V\in\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}}V\biggr).

This algebra has a unit which is decomposed into a sum of orthogonal idempotents

1=\sum_{V\in\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}}{\varepsilon}_{V}
, where the idempotent
{\varepsilon}_{V}
corresponds to
{\operatorname{Id}}_{V}
. We have an equivalence of
categories

{}_{A(\mathcal{V})}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}\,\,{\simeq}\,\,{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathcal{V},\operatorname{Vect}\limits_{\mathbf{k}}).

Expilcitly, given a functor
F:\mathcal{V}\to\operatorname{Vect}\limits_{\mathbf{k}}
, we associate to it the
A(\mathcal{V})
-module

(A.5.5)

M=\bigoplus_{V\in\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}}F(V)

and
F
is recovered from
M
by
F(V)={\varepsilon}_{V}M
.

If now
G
acts on
\mathcal{V}
in a strict sense then it also acts on
A(\mathcal{V})

and so
A(\mathcal{V})[G]
is defined. Then we have an isomorphism of algebras and
by applying Example
A.5.3
(b), an equivalence of categories

A(\mathcal{V}\rtimes G)\,\,{\simeq}\,\,A(\mathcal{V})[G],\hskip 10.00002pt_{A(\mathcal{V})[G]}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}\,\,{\simeq}\,\,{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathcal{V}\rtimes G,\operatorname{Vect}\limits_{\mathbf{k}})

Further, let
V\in\mathcal{V}
be a
G
-equivariant object with structure isomorphisms

{\varphi}_{g}:V\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\to}\limits^{\sim}}\gamma_{g}(V)
. Then
\lambda_{g}=({\varphi}_{g}^{-1},g)
is an automorphism of
V
in

\mathcal{V}\rtimes G
. In this way we get an action of
G
on
V
by automorphisms of
\mathcal{V}\rtimes G
.
Note that an object with
G
-action is the same as a
G
-equivariant object with respect to the trivial
action of
G
on the category.

Proposition A.5.6
.

The above construction defines an equivalence of categories

\mathcal{V}^{[G]}\longrightarrow(\mathcal{V}\rtimes G)^{[G]},

where the
G
-action of
G
on
\mathcal{V}\rtimes G
is trivial. ∎

A.6.
Orbit categories II. Invariants

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear category with finitely many objects and let
G
be a finite group acting on
\mathcal{V}
in a strict sense.
We define a new category
\mathcal{V}^{G}
called the
invariant orbit category
of
\mathcal{V}
under the
G
-action.
By definition,
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}^{G}=G\backslash\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}
is the set of
G
-orbits on
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}
. For two such orbits
O_{1},O_{2}
we put

(A.6.1)

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{G}}(O_{1},O_{2})=\biggl(\bigoplus_{V_{1}\in O_{1},V_{2}\in O_{2}}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(V_{1},V_{2})\biggr)^{G},

the space of
G
-invariants in the direct sum, as opposed to coinvariants in (
A.3.1
).
The composition of morphisms in
\mathcal{V}^{G}
is defined as follows :
if
O_{1},O_{2},O_{3}
are
G
-orbits in
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}
and if
a=(a_{V_{1},V_{2}})_{V_{1},V_{2}}\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{G}}(O_{1},O_{2})
,
b=(b_{V_{2},V_{3}})_{V_{2},V_{3}}\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{G}}(O_{2},O_{3})
then
b\circ a
is defined as

(b\circ a)_{V_{1},V_{3}}=\sum_{V_{2}\in O_{2}}b_{V_{2},V_{3}}a_{V_{1},V_{2}}\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(V_{1},V_{3}).

The construction of the invariant orbit category also has an interpretation in terms of total algebra
A(\mathcal{V})
. We can form the invariant subalgebra
A(\mathcal{V})^{G}
. For any
G
-orbit
O\subset\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}
the
element
{\varepsilon}_{O}=\sum_{V\in O}{\varepsilon}_{V}
is an idempotent in
A(\mathcal{V})^{G}
; the unit
1\in A(\mathcal{V})

lies in
A(\mathcal{V})^{G}
and
is the sum
\sum_{O\in G\backslash\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}}{\varepsilon}_{O}
.
It follows that

(A.6.2)

A(\mathcal{V})^{G}=A(\mathcal{V}^{G}).

For a fixed pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
of objects of
\mathcal{V}
there is a canonical

averaging map

(A.6.3)

\xi_{V_{1},V_{2}}:\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(V_{1},V_{2})\to\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{G}}(GV_{1},GV_{2})

which assigns to a morphism
a\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(V_{1},V_{2})
the element

(A.6.4)

\underline{a}=\xi_{V_{1},V_{2}}(a):={1\over|{\operatorname{Stab}}(V_{1},V_{2})|}\sum_{g\in G}\bigl\{ga:gV_{1}\to gV_{2}\bigr\}\hskip 10.00002pt\in\bigoplus_{V^{\prime}_{1}\in GV_{1},V^{\prime}_{2}\in GV_{2}}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(V^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{2}).

Here:

•

{\operatorname{Stab}}(V_{1},V_{2})\subset G
is the stabilizer of the pair

(V_{1},V_{2})\subset\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})\times\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})
.

•

ga:gV_{1}\to gV_{2}
is considered as an element of the direct sum
on the right in (
A.6.4
) lying in a single summand labelled by

V^{\prime}_{1}=gV_{1},
,
V^{\prime}_{2}=gV_{2}
.

Example A.6.5
.

Suppose
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})=\{V\}
consists of one object, so
\mathcal{V}
is given by
an algebra
A=\operatorname{End}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(V)
with
G
-action. Then

\xi=\xi_{V,V}:A\longrightarrow A^{G},\hskip 10.00002pta\mapsto\underline{a}={1\over|G|}\sum_{g\in G}ga

is the usual averaging map from
A
to its invariant subalgebra.

This shows that unlike the case of
\mathcal{V}_{G}
from §
A.3
, the maps
\xi_{V_{1},V_{2}}
do
not, in general, extend to a functor
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{G}
.

Assume now that the
G
-action on
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}
is free.
In this situation one can use the maps
\xi_{V_{1},V_{2}}
to compare the construction
of
\mathcal{V}^{G}
with that of §
A.3

in the case when both are defined.
That is, for any category
\mathcal{E}
let
{\mathbf{k}}[\mathcal{E}]
denote its
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear envelope.
It is a new category with the same objects as
\mathcal{E}

and morphisms being
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector spaces spanned by the sets of morphisms in
\mathcal{E}
.

Proposition A.6.6
.

(a) Let
\mathcal{E}
be a category with finitely many objects and a strict action of a finite group
G
which is free on

\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{E}
. Then we have an isomorphism of categories

{\mathbf{k}}[\mathcal{E}_{G}]\,\,{\simeq}\,\,{\mathbf{k}}[\mathcal{E}]^{G}.

(b) Let
\mathcal{V}
be a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear category with finitely many objects and a strict action of a finite group
G
which is free on

\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits\mathcal{V}
. Then we have an equivalence of categories

\mathcal{V}^{G}\,\,{\simeq}\,\,\mathcal{V}\rtimes G.

Proof:
(a) This boils down to the following observation : if
G
is a finite group acting freely on a finite set
S
, then
the two
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector spaces
(\bigoplus_{s\in G}{\mathbf{k}}s)^{G}
and
\bigoplus_{\overline{s}\in G\backslash S}{\mathbf{k}}\overline{s}
are canonically isomorphic.

(b) Let
V,W\in\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})
, so the orbits
GV,GW
are objects of
\mathcal{V}^{G}
. By definition and
by freeness of the action on
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})
,

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}\rtimes G}(V,W)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}}(gV,W)=\biggl(\bigoplus_{g,h\in G}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(\mathcal{V}(gV,hW)\biggr)^{G}=\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{G}}(GV,GW).

These identifications for all
V,W
are compatible with composition of morphisms and so define
a functor
\mathcal{V}\rtimes G\to\mathcal{V}^{G}
. This functor is surjective on objects and bijective on
Hom-spaces, i.e.,
is an equivalence of cateories. ∎

We now relate the construction of
\mathcal{V}^{G}
with other constructions in the Appendix.
First, for any
G
-orbit
O\subset\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})
we form the object

V_{O}=\bigoplus_{V\in O}V\,\in\,\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}.

The object
V_{O}
is naturally
G
-equivariant, and (
A.6.1
) is the same as

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{(V^{\oplus})^{[G]}}(V_{O},V_{O^{\prime}})
. Further, as in Proposition
A.5.6
,

V_{O}
considered as an object of
\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}\rtimes G
has a natural
G
-action, so the object of
G
-invariants

V_{O}^{G}\in(V^{\oplus}\rtimes G)^{\operatorname{Kar}}
is defined. If
V\in O
is any object and
G_{V}\subset G
is the
stabilizer of
V
(with respect to the
G
-action on
\operatorname{Ob}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})
), then
V_{O}^{G}\,\,{\simeq}\,\,V^{G_{V}}
.
Next, the total algebra
A(\mathcal{V}\rtimes G)
contains the group algebra
{\mathbf{k}}[G]
, in particular,
the idempotent
{\varepsilon}={1\over|G|}\sum_{g\in G}[g]
.

Proposition A.6.7
.

(a) The correspondence
O\mapsto V_{O}
embeds
\mathcal{V}^{G}
as a full subcategory in
(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus})^{[G]}
.

(b) The correspondence
O\mapsto V_{O}^{G}
embeds
\mathcal{V}^{G}
as a full subcategory in
(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}\rtimes G)^{\operatorname{Kar}}
.
In fact, it takes values (up to isomorphism) in
(\mathcal{V}\rtimes G)^{\operatorname{Kar}}
since
V_{O}^{G}\,\,{\simeq}\,\,V^{G_{V}}
.

(c) The total algebra
A(\mathcal{V}^{G})=A(\mathcal{V})^{G}
is identified with the subalgebra
{\varepsilon}A(\mathcal{V}\rtimes G){\varepsilon}\subset A(\mathcal{V}\rtimes G)
.

Proof:
(a) Follows because (
A.6.1
) is the same as

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus})^{[G]}}(V_{O},V_{O^{\prime}})
.

(b) By definition of Karoubi completion and Proposition
A.5.6
,

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{(\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}\rtimes G)^{\operatorname{Kar}}}(V_{O}^{G},V_{O^{\prime}}^{G})={\varepsilon}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}\rtimes G}(V_{O},V_{O^{\prime}}){\varepsilon}=\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{\oplus}\rtimes G}(V_{O},V_{O^{\prime}})^{G}=\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{G}}(O,O^{\prime}).

(c) This is a consequence of (b). ∎

A.7.
Descent for sheaves

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a topological space
X
(conventions of §
1
), so we have the quotient space
G\backslash X
and the
projection
q:X\to G\backslash X
. It is convenient to also consider the orbifold (or stack) quotient
[G\backslash X]

so that sheaves on
[G\backslash X]
are
G
-equivariant sheaves on
X
, i.e.,
{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]}={\operatorname{Sh}}_{X}^{[G]}
.
The map
q
yields a projection
p:[G\backslash X]\to G\backslash X
. In particular, we have the
pullback functor

(A.7.1)

p^{-1}:{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]}={\operatorname{Sh}}_{X}^{[G]}

which takes a sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}
to
q^{-1}(\mathcal{F})\in{\operatorname{Sh}}_{X}
with its obvious
G
-equivariant structure. This functor
has a right adjoint

(A.7.2)

p_{*}:{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]}={\operatorname{Sh}}_{X}^{[G]}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{G}\mapsto q_{*}(\mathcal{G})^{G},

the subsheaf of invariants in the usual direct image. Note that both
p^{-1},p_{*}
are exact
functors of abelian categories.

Proposition A.7.3
.

The unit
{\varepsilon}:{\operatorname{Id}}_{{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}}\to p_{*}p^{-1}
is an isomorphism of functors.

Proof:

Let
\mathcal{G}\in{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}
. It is enough to show that for any
y\in G\backslash X
the induced map of stalks

{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{G},y}:\mathcal{G}_{y}\to(p_{*}p^{-1}\mathcal{G})_{y}
is an isomorphism.
Now, for any
x\in X
we have
(q^{-1}\mathcal{G})_{x}=\mathcal{G}_{q(x)}
. The preimage
q^{-1}(x)
forms a single
G
-orbit
in
X
, so

(q_{*}q^{-1}\mathcal{G})_{y}
is the direct sum of identical copies of
\mathcal{G}_{y}
, labelled by

q^{-1}(y)
and transitively permuted by
G
.
Therefore

(p_{*}p^{-1}\mathcal{G})_{y}=\biggl(\bigoplus\nolimits_{x\in q^{-1}(y)}\mathcal{G}_{y}\biggr)^{G}=\mathcal{G}_{y},

with
{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{G},y}
giving the identification.
∎

Further, let
D^{b}{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}
,
D^{b}{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]}
be the bounded derived categories of
{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}
and

{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]}
respectively. Since
G
is a finite group and
{\operatorname{char}\,}({\mathbf{k}})=0
, any
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear representation
of
G
has trivial higher cohomology. So our definition of
D^{b}{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]}
agrees with other,
more sophisticated definitions of the equivariant derived categories such as in

[
BL
]
.

Since
p^{-1},p_{*}
are exact functors of abelian categories, they extend componentwise to
an adjoint pair of derived functors which we denote by the same symbols:

\textstyle{D^{b}{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p^{-1}}
\textstyle{D^{b}{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces.}
\scriptstyle{p_{*}}

Corollary A.7.4
.

The unit of the derived adjunction
{\varepsilon}:{\operatorname{Id}}_{D^{b}{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}}\to p_{*}p^{-1}
is an isomorphism
of functors. ∎

In particular, Proposition
A.1.1
gives that both versions of pullback:

p^{-1}:{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]},\hskip 10.00002ptp^{-1}:D^{b}{\operatorname{Sh}}_{G\backslash X}\longrightarrow D^{b}{\operatorname{Sh}}_{[G\backslash X]}

are embeddings of full (coreflexive) subcategories.

A.8.
Descent for modules

Let
A
be an associative algebra with unit and

G
a finite group acting on
A
, see Example
A.2.1
.
We have an embedding of algebras

\iota:A^{G}\hookrightarrow A[G],\hskip 10.00002pta\mapsto a\cdot{\varepsilon},\hskip 10.00002pt{\varepsilon}:={1\over|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\,\,[g].

Note that unless
|G|=1
, the embedding
\iota
does not
preserve units:
\iota(1)={\varepsilon}
is the idempotent corresponding, under the isomorphism

{\mathbf{k}}[G]\,\,{\simeq}\,\,\bigoplus_{V\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)}\operatorname{End}\nolimits_{\mathbf{k}}(V)
, to the identity of the trivial representation.

The embedding
\iota
gives rise to the adjoint pair
(I,R)
, where

(A.8.1)

\begin{gathered}I:\,{{}_{A^{G}}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}}\longrightarrow{{}_{A[G]}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}},\quad N\mapsto A[G]\otimes_{A^{G}}N,\\
R:\,{{}_{A[G]}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}}\longrightarrow{{}_{A^{G}}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}},\quad M\mapsto(\iota^{*}M)^{u}\end{gathered}

are the standard induction and restriction functors. Here
\iota^{*}M
is
M
considered
as an
A^{G}
-module (which may not be unital) and “
u
” means the maximal
unital submodule (
A.2.2
).

Viewing
A[G]
-modules as
G
-equivariant
A
-modules, we can describe the functors

I
and
R
more directly:

(A.8.2)

\begin{gathered}I(N)=A\otimes_{A^{G}}N,\quad g(a\otimes n):=g(a)\otimes n,\quad N\in{{}_{A^{G}}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}},\\
R(M)=M^{G},\quad M\in{{}_{A[G]}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}}={{}_{A}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}^{[G]}}.\end{gathered}

In particular,
R
is always exact (not just left exact) while
I
is exact (not just right exact)
iff
A
is flat as an
A^{G}
-module.

Proposition A.8.3
.

The unit
{\varepsilon}:{\operatorname{Id}}_{{}_{A^{G}}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}}\to RI
of the adjunction
(I,R)
is an isomorphism of functors.

In particular (Proposition
A.1.1
),
the functor
I
embeds
{}_{A^{G}}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}
as a full subcategory
in
{}_{A[G]}\hskip-1.42271pt{\operatorname{Mod}}
.

Proof:
The statement means that for any (unital)
A^{G}
-module
N
the natural morphism

{\varepsilon}_{N}:N\longrightarrow(A\otimes_{A^{G}}N)^{G},\hskip 10.00002ptn\mapsto 1_{A}\otimes n,

is an isomorphism. This is manifestly true for
N=A^{G}
and therefore for any free
A^{G}
-module

F
. Now, any
N
has a free resolution, as in the top row of the diagram below:

\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F^{-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\varepsilon}_{F^{-1}}}
\textstyle{F^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\varepsilon}_{F^{0}}}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\varepsilon}_{N}}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(A\otimes_{A^{G}}F^{-1})^{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(A\otimes_{A^{G}}F^{0})^{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(A\otimes_{A^{G}}N)^{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

Since the functor
\otimes
is right exact and taking
G
-invariants is exact as
{\operatorname{char}\,}{\mathbf{k}}=0
, the bottom row of
the diagram is exact as well. Since
{\varepsilon}_{F^{i}}
are isomorphisms for all
i
,
so is
{\varepsilon}_{N}
.
∎

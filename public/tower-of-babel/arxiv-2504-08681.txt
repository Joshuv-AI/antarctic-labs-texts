Locally Optimal Functional Quantization
Harald Luschgy, Gilles Pagès

Abstract
In this note we demonstrate that locally optimal functional quantizers for probability distributions P P on a Banach space lying in the support of P P behave exactly like globally optimal functional quantizers in terms of stationarity.



Keywords:

functional quantization ; stationary quantizer ; locally optimal quantizers.

1
Introduction

We will use the notations of
[
1
]
. Let
X
be a Radon random vector on a probability space
(\Omega,{\cal A},\mathbb{P})
taking its values in a Banach space
(E,\|\cdot\|)
equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra
{\cal B}or(E)
. This means that
X
is
\big({\cal A},{\cal B}or(E)\big)
-measurable and its distribution
P=\mathbb{P}_{{}_{X}}
is a Radon probability measure on
{\cal B}or(E)
(in the sense that it is inner regular). Notice that stochastic processes in continuous time can often be seen as random vectors having values in a Banach function space.

Let

d(x,A):=\inf\{\|x-a\|:\;a\!\in A\}

denote the distance of
x\!\in E
to the nonempty subset
A\subset E
. For
n\!\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\!\in(0,+\infty)
the
L^{r}
-quantization problem for
P
(or
X
) at level
n
consists in minimizing

e_{r}(\Gamma,P):=\big(\mathbb{E}\,d(X,\Gamma)^{r}\big)^{1/r}

(1)

over all subsets
\Gamma\subset E
,
\Gamma\neq\varnothing
with
{\rm card}(\Gamma)\leq n
. Such a subset is called
n
-quantizer
(or
n
-codebook
or
n
-grid
).

Definition 1.1
.

(a)
The
n
th-level
L^{r}
-quantization error of
P
is defined as

e_{r,n}(P,E):=\inf\big\{e_{r}(\Gamma,P):\Gamma\subset E,\;1\leq{\rm card}((\Gamma)\leq n\big\}.

(b)
Assume
\mathbb{E}\,\|X\|^{r}=\int\|x\|^{r}dP(x)<+\infty
(so that
e_{r,n}(P,E)<+\infty
for every
n\geq 1
). A quantizer
\Gamma\subset E
with
{\rm card}(\Gamma)\leq n
is called
L^{r}
-optimal
n
-quantizer for
P
of

e_{r}(\Gamma,P)=e_{r,n}(P,E).

Let
{\cal C}_{r,n}(P,E)
denote the set of all
L^{r}
-optimal
n
-quantizers for
P
.

A Voronoi quantization of
X
induced by a finite quantizer
\Gamma\subset E
is simply the projection of
X
onto
\Gamma
following the nearest neighbour rule as explained below.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset E
be a finite quantizer and
a\!\in\Gamma
. Then

V_{a}(\Gamma):=\big\{x\!\in E:\|x-a\|=d(x,\Gamma)\big\}

is called the (closed) Voronoi cell and

W_{a}(\Gamma):=\big\{x\!\in E:\|x-a\|<d(x,\Gamma\setminus\{a\})\big\}

the open Voronoi cell of
a
induced by
\Gamma
. A Borel measurable partition
\{C_{a}(\Gamma):a\!\in\Gamma\}
of
E
is called Voronoi partition induced by
\Gamma
if

C_{a}(\Gamma)\subset V_{a}(\Gamma)\;\mbox{ for every }a\!\in\Gamma.

Since the distance function is continuous,
V_{a}(\Gamma)
is in fact a closed set of
E
and
W_{a}(\Gamma)
an open set. It should also be obvious that, for a Voronoi partition
\{C_{a}(\Gamma):a\!\in\Gamma\}
,

W_{a}(\Gamma)\subset C_{a}(\Gamma)\mbox{ for every }\;a\!\in\Gamma.

(2)

Given a finite quantizer
\Gamma\subset E
and a Voronoi partition
\{C_{a}(\Gamma):a\!\in\Gamma\}
of
E
induced by
\Gamma
, define

{\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}:E\to E,\quad{\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}(x):=\sum_{a\in\Gamma}\mbox{\bf 1}_{C_{a}(\Gamma)}(x).

Note that
{\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}
depends on the Voronoi partition and not only on
\Gamma
. The random vector

\widehat{X}=\widehat{X}^{\Gamma}={\rm Proj}_{\Gamma}(X)

is called
Voronoi quantization
of
X
induced by
\Gamma
or
\Gamma
-quantization
of
X
. Its
L^{r}
-(mean)error satisfies

\big(\mathbb{E}\,\|X-\widehat{X}\|^{r}\big)^{1/r}=e_{r}(\Gamma,P)

and so does not depend on the specific choice of the Voronoi partition induced by
\Gamma
. Functional quantization can thus be seen as a way to discretize the path space of a stochastic process. So far, we have dealt with (functional) quantizers viewed as a finite subset of
E
. For our present purpose it is convenient to introduce them also a
n
-tuples. The
L^{r}
-distortion function of level
n
is defined by

G_{r,n}^{P}:E^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+},\;G_{r,n}^{P}(a):=\mathbb{E}\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\|X-a_{i}\|^{r}.

(3)

Note that
G_{r,n}^{P}
is continuous w.r.t. the product topology on
E^{n}
(in fact
(G_{r,n}^{P})^{1/r}
is even Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz coefficient
1
w.r.t. the
\max
-norm
\|(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\|_{\ell^{\infty}}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|x_{i}\|
on
E^{n}
if
r\geq 1
), convex for
n=1
and
r\geq 1
but typically not convex if
n\geq 2
. We have

e_{r,n}(P,E)^{r}=\inf_{a\in E^{n}}G_{r,n}^{P}(a).

We will introduce the notion of
L^{r}
-stationarity quantizers as critical points of
G_{r,n}^{P}
.

The Banach space
E
and its norm
\|\cdot\|
is said to be smooth if the norm is Gâteaux-differentiable at any
x\neq 0
, where a function
F:E\to\mathbb{R}
is
Gâteaux-differentiable
at
x\!\in E
if there exists
\nabla F(x)\!\in E^{*}
(topological dual space of
E
) such that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{F(x+ty)-F(x)}{t}=\langle\nabla F(x),y\rangle

for every
y\!\in E
. We write
\langle u,y\rangle
for
u(y)
for
u\!\in E^{*}
and
y\!\in E
. Then
\nabla\|\cdot\|(x)
is contained in the unit sphere of
E^{*}
when the norm is Gâteaux-differentiable at
x
. In fact, we only need almost sure smoothness of the norm. (This weaker condition is of interest e.g. for
L^{1}
-spaces
E
.).

For convenience, a finite quantizer
\Gamma\subset E
is called
admissible
for
P
if

P\left(\bigcup_{a\in\Gamma}W_{a}(\Gamma)\right)=1.

(4)

An
n
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\!\in E^{n}
with
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
is admissible for
P
if its associated
n
-quantizer
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
r\geq 1
and assume
\displaystyle\int_{E}\|x\|^{r}dP(x)<+\infty
. Assume that the norm is Gâteaux-differentiable at
a-x

P(dx)
-
a.e.
on
E\setminus\{a\}
. Let
r>1
. Then the
L^{r}
-distortion function
G_{r,n}^{P}
is Gâteaux-differentiable at every admissible
n
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
with a Gâteaux derivative given by

\nabla G^{P}_{r,n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=r\bigg(\mathbb{E}\,\mbox{\bf 1}_{C_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)\setminus\{a_{i}\}}(X)\|X-a_{i}\|^{r-1}\nabla\|\cdot\|(a_{i}-X)\bigg)_{1\leq i\leq n}\!\in(E^{*})^{n},

where
\{C_{a_{i}}(\Gamma):1\leq i\leq n\}
denotes any Voronoi partition induced by
\Gamma=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
.

When
r=1
, the above result holds true for admissible
n
-tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
satisfying

P(\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\})=0
.

Proof.
See Proposition 1.3.1 in
[
1
]
.
\Box

Note that
(E^{n})^{*}=(E^{*})^{n}
with duality
\langle u,x\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle u_{j},x_{j}\rangle
, where
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\!\in(E^{*})^{n}
and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\!\in E^{n}
. Let us point out that the integrals (expectations) occurring in the above formulas for
\nabla G_{r,n}^{P}
and in the subsequent definition are to be seen as
Gelfand integrals
. Our notion of stationarity avoids admissibility.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
r\geq 1
and assume
\displaystyle\int_{E}\|x\|^{r}dP(x)<+\infty
. Assume that the norm is Gâteaux-differentiable at
a-x

P(dx)
-
a.e.
on
E\setminus\{a\}
for every
a\!\in E
. A quantizer
\Gamma\subset E
with
{\rm card}(\Gamma)=n
and
P(\Gamma)=0
when
r=1
is called
L^{r}
stationary
n
-quantizer for
P
if there exists a Voronoi partition
\{C_{a}(\Gamma):a\!\in\Gamma\}
induced by
\Gamma
such that
P\big(C_{a}(\Gamma)\big)>0
and

\mathbb{E}\,\mbox{\bf 1}_{C_{a}(\Gamma)\setminus\{a\}}(X)\|X-a\|^{r-1}\nabla\|\cdot\|(a-X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle E^{*}}}{{=}}0

(5)

for every
a\!\in\Gamma
. (Here
x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle E^{*}}}{{=}}0
means that
x
is equal to the zero of
E^{*}
).

2
Local minima of the distortion functions

Let
{\rm supp}(P)
denote the support of
P
which exists in our setting.

Local minima of the
L^{r}
-distortion function
G_{r,n}^{P}
with components lying in
{\rm supp}(P)
behave exactly like global minima although
G_{r,n}^{P}
is not convex for
n\geq 2
. For global minima see
[
1
, Sections 1.2 and 1.3]
. In the subsequent results let
r\!\in(0,+\infty)
and assume
\displaystyle\int_{E}\|x\|^{r}dP(x)<+\infty
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
be a local minimum of
G_{r,n}^{P}
with
a_{i}
a non-isolated point of
{\rm supp}(P)
for every
i\!\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(a)
The components
a_{i}
of
a
are pairwise distinct and if
r\geq 1
, then for every
i\!\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and every
C\!\in{\cal B}(E)
with
W_{a_{i}}\big(\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\big)\subset C\subset V_{a_{i}}\big(\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\big)
,

a_{i}\!\in{\rm argmin}_{y\in E}G_{r,1}^{P(\cdot|C)}(y)

or, equivalently,
\{a_{i}\}\!\in{\cal C}_{r,1}\big(P(\cdot\,|\,C),E\big)
.

(b)
Let
r\geq 1
. Assume that the norm is Gâteaux differentiable at
b-x

P(\mathrm{d}x)
-a.e. on
E\setminus\{b\}
for every element
b
of
E
and
P(\{a_{i}\})=0
for every
i
when
r=1
. Then
\Gamma=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is an
L^{r}
-stationary
n
-quantizer for
P
. If
E
is strictly convex, then
\Gamma
is admissible for
P
.

Proof.

(a)
Let
U\subset E^{n}
be an open neighbourhood of
a
such that
G_{r,n}^{P}
has a global minimum at
a
on
U
. We may assume that
U=\prod_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
has a product form, where
U_{i}\subset E
are open neighbourhoods of
a_{i}
. Let
N
denote the number of pairwise distinct components of
a
so that there exist
i_{1}=1<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{N}\leq n
such that
a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{N}}
are pairwise distinct. Set
\Gamma=\{a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{N}}\}
. Assume
N\leq n-1
. Then there exists
i\!\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\!\setminus\!\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{{}_{N}}\}
.

Choose
c\!\in\big({\rm supp}(P)\setminus\Gamma\big)\cap U_{i}\neq\varnothing
where we use that
a_{i}
is not an isolated point of
{\rm supp}(P)
and set
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma\cup\{c\}
. Let
\{C_{b}(\Gamma_{1}):b\!\in\Gamma_{1}\}
be a Voronoi partition of
E
induced by
\Gamma_{1}
such that
C_{c}(\Gamma_{1})=W_{c}(\Gamma_{1})
. Now

d(x,\Gamma_{1})=\|x-c\|<d(x,\Gamma)\quad\mbox{ on }\quad C_{c}(\Gamma_{1})=W_{c}(\Gamma_{1})

so that, using
P\big(W_{c}(\Gamma_{1})\big)>0
,

\displaystyle G_{r,n}^{P}(a)

\displaystyle=\int d(x,\Gamma)^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\Gamma}\int_{C_{b}(\Gamma_{1})}d(x,\Gamma)^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)+\int_{C_{c}(\Gamma_{1})}d(x,\Gamma)^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle>\sum_{b\in\Gamma}\int_{C_{b}(\Gamma_{1})}d(x,\Gamma_{1})^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)+\int_{C_{c}(\Gamma_{1})}d(x,\Gamma_{1})^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle=\int d(x,\Gamma_{1})^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)=G_{r,n}^{P}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},c,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n}).

This yields a contradiction. Consequently, the components
a_{i}
of
a
are pairwise distinct so that
\Gamma=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
. In particular, we may assume that the neighbourhoods
U_{i}
are pairwise disjoint.

Next assume that
a_{i}\,\hbox{\it/ \hskip-10.0pt$\in$}\,{\rm argmin}_{y\in E}G_{r,1}^{P(\cdot|C)}(y)
for some
i\!\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and some Borel set
C
satisfying
W_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)\subset C\subset V_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)
, noting that
P(C)>0
. Then there exists
z\!\in E
such that
G_{r,1}^{Q}(z)<G_{r,1}^{Q}(a_{i})
where
Q=P(\cdot\,|\,C)
. Consider
y_{s}=sz+(1-s)a_{i}
,
s\!\in[0,1]
. Then
y_{s}\!\in U_{i}
for
s>0
small enough and using the convexity of
G_{r,1}^{Q}
in case
r\geq 1
, we obtain

G_{r,1}^{Q}(y_{s})\leq sG_{r,1}^{Q}(z)+(1-s)G_{r,1}^{Q}(a_{i})<G_{r,1}^{Q}(a_{i}).

Consequently,

\int_{C}\|x-y_{s}\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)<\int_{C}\|x-a_{i}\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x).

Hence, if
\{C_{a_{j}}(\Gamma):1\leq j\leq n\}
denotes a Voronoi partition of
E
induced by
\Gamma
with
C_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)=C
and setting
\Gamma_{2}=\big(\Gamma\setminus\{a_{i}\}\big)\cup\{y_{s}\}
where
y_{s}\,\hbox{\it/ \hskip-10.0pt$\in$}\,\Gamma
,

\displaystyle G_{r,n}^{P}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},y_{s},a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n})=\int d(x,\Gamma_{2})^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{C_{a_{j}}(\Gamma)}d(x,\Gamma_{2})^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{C_{a_{j}}(\Gamma)}\|x-a_{j}\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)+\int_{C}\|x-y_{s}\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad<\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{C_{a_{j}}(\Gamma)}\|x-a_{j}\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)+\int_{C}\|x-a_{i}\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int d(x,\Gamma)^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)=G_{r,n}^{P}(a)

which again contradicts global minimality of
G_{r,n}^{P}
at
a
on
U
.

(b)
In view of
(a)
, the proof of
(b)
is literally the same as the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 1.3.3]
.
\Box

In general, local minima may neither be contained in
{\rm supp}(P)
nor be pairwise distinct. Let e.g.
E=\mathbb{R}
,
P=U([-1,1])
,
n=2
,
a_{1}=0
and
a_{2}=3
. Then
(a_{1},a_{2})
is a local minimizer of
G_{r,2}^{P}
,
r\!\in(0,+\infty)
. If
n=3
and
a_{2}=a_{3}
, then
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
is a local minimizer of
G_{r,3}^{P}
. However, these local minima are not strict and have components lying outside the support of
P
.

Theorem 2.2

(Hilbert setting)
.

Let
(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a Hilbert space
and assume that
{\rm supp}(P)
is convex. If
G_{r,n}^{P}
has a strict local minimum at
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\!\in E^{n}
, then
a_{i}\!\in{\rm supp}(P)
for every
i\!\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. In particular, the conclusions of Theorem
2.1
are true.

Moreover, if
r\geq 2
,
P(\{a_{i}\})=0
for any
i\!\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
when
r>2
and
{\rm supp}(P)
has nonempty interior, then
a_{i}
is an interior point of
{\rm supp}(P)
for every
i
.

Proof.
Let
U=\prod_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
be a neighbourhood of
a
such that
G_{r,n}^{P}
has a strict (global) minimum at
a
on
U
. Let
N
denote the number of pairwise distinct components of
a
so that there exist
i_{1}=1<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{{}_{N}}\leq n
such that
a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{{}_{N}}}
are pairwise distinct. We may assume that the neighborhoods
U_{i_{j}}
are pairwise disjoint. Set
\Gamma=\{a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{{}_{i_{N}}}\}
and
K={\rm supp}(P)
. Assume
a_{i}\,\hbox{\it/ \hskip-10.0pt$\in$}\,K
for some
i\!\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{{}_{N}}\}
. Let
b\!\in K
be the (unique)
K
-valued best approximation of
a_{i}
such that
\|a_{i}-b\|=d(a_{i},K)
or equivalently

\langle a_{i}-b,x-b\rangle\leq 0\quad\mbox{ for every }x\!\in K.

Consider
y_{s}=sb+(1-s)a_{i}
,
s\!\in[0,1]
. Then
y_{s}\!\in U_{i}
for
s>0
small enough and

\|y_{s}-x\|^{2}<\|a_{i}-x\|^{2}\quad\mbox{ for every }x\!\in K.

In fact

\displaystyle\|y_{s}-x\|^{2}-\|a_{i}-x\|^{2}=\|y_{s}-b+b-x\|^{2}-\|a_{i}-b+b-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\|y_{s}-b\|^{2}+2\langle y_{s}-b,b-x\rangle-\|a_{i}-b\|^{2}-2\langle a_{i}-b,b-x\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\big((1-s)^{2}-1\big)\|a_{i}-b\|^{2}+\big(2(1-s)-2\big)\langle a_{i}-b,b-x\rangle<0.

Set
\Gamma_{2}=\big(\Gamma\setminus\{a_{i}\}\big)\cup\{y_{s}\}
where
y_{s}\,\hbox{\it/ \hskip-10.0pt$\in$}\,\Gamma
. Let
\{C_{c}(\Gamma):c\!\in\Gamma\}
denote a Voronoi partition induced by
\Gamma
. Then

\displaystyle G_{r,n}^{P}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},y_{s},a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n})=\int d(x,\Gamma_{2})^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{c\in\Gamma}\int_{C_{c}(\Gamma)}d(x,\Gamma_{2})^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{c\in\Gamma,\,c\neq a_{i}}\int_{C_{c}(\Gamma)}\|x-c\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)+\int_{C_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)\cap K}\|x-y_{s}\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{c\in\Gamma,\,c\neq a_{i}}\int_{C_{c}(\Gamma)}\|x-c\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)+\int_{C_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)\cap K}\|x-a_{i}\|^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int d(x,\Gamma)^{r}\mathrm{d}P(x)=G_{r,n}^{P}(a).

This yields a contradiction to strict minimality of
G_{r,n}^{P}
at
a
on
U
.

Now assume
r\geq 2
,
P(\{a_{i}\})=0
for every
i
when
r>2
and
\mathring{K}\neq\varnothing
. We already know that
a_{i}\!\in K
for every
i
so that by Theorem
2.1
the components
a_{i}
of
a
are pairwise distinct and
\Gamma=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is an
L^{r}
-stationary
n
-quantizer for
P
, noting that
K
has no isolated points. Consequently,
\Gamma\subset\mathring{K}
provided
P\big(\mathring{K}\cap W_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)\big)>0
for every
i
(see
[
1
, Remarks 1.3.4 and 1.3.5]
). This is true since the open sets
\mathring{K}\cap W_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)
are not empty. In fact fix
z\!\in\mathring{K}
and let
y_{s}=sz+(1-s)a_{i}
. One checks that
y_{s}\!\in\mathring{K}
for every
s\!\in(0,1]
and
y_{s}\!\in W_{a_{i}}(\Gamma)
for
s>0
small enough.
\Box

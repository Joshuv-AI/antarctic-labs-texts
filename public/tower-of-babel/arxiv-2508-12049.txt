Decay estimates of wave equations in un-isotropic media
Sergiu Klainerman, Xuecheng Wang

Abstract
We prove decay estimates for solutions to non-isotropic linear systems of wave equations. The defining feature of these estimates is that they depend only on the commutation properties of the system with the scaling vector field. As application we give two surprisingly simple proofs for small data global regularity results non-isotropic systems of wave equations in ℝ 1 + 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{1+3} with cubic semilinear nonlinearities. We hope that the techniques presented here are relevant for the more difficult and important case of biaxial refraction in crystal optics.

. Decay estimates for the standard wave equation using only commutations with the scaling operator

The proof of the theorem is based on the following ingredients.

2.1
Decomposition of the wave operator using
S
and main energy estimates

As mentioned in the introduction we plan to use the scaling operator to study the decay properties of the equation (
1.6
), i.e.
\square\phi=f
,
Since the scaling
S
is only timelike in the interior of the light cone
|x|<t
we restrict our attention to solutions supported in that region, i.e. we assume that that the initial data at
t=1
has compact support in the disk
|x|<1
and that
f
is likewise supported in
|x|<t
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The wave equation
\square\phi=f
can be written in the form

\displaystyle\not{{\triangle}}\phi+\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}^{2}(r^{2}2\phi)=\frac{2t{\partial}_{t}S\phi-S^{2}\phi-S\phi}{t^{2}}+\square\phi

(2.1)

Proof.

We start with the observation that
{\partial}_{t}\phi=\frac{S\phi-x\cdot\nabla\phi}{t},
and therefore

\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}^{2}\phi&=t^{-1}\Big[{\partial}_{t}S\phi-x\cdot\nabla{\partial}_{t}\phi\Big]-t^{-2}\big(S\phi-x\cdot\nabla\phi\big)\\
&=t^{-1}\Big[t^{-1}\big(S^{2}\phi-x\cdot\nabla S\phi\big)-\big(t^{-1}(x\cdot\nabla)S\phi-t^{-1}\big(x\cdot\nabla\big)^{2}\phi\big)\Big]-t^{-2}\big(S\phi-x\cdot\nabla\phi\big)\\
&=t^{-2}(x\cdot\nabla)^{2}\phi+t^{-2}(x\cdot\nabla)\phi+t^{-2}\Big(S^{2}\phi-2(x\cdot\nabla)S\phi-S\phi\Big)\\
&=t^{-2}(x\cdot\nabla)^{2}\phi+t^{-2}(x\cdot\nabla)\phi+t^{-2}\Big(-S^{2}\phi+2t{\partial}_{t}S\phi-S\phi\Big).\end{split}

(2.2)

Since
x\cdot\nabla=r{\partial}_{r}
and
(x\cdot\nabla)^{2}\phi=(r{\partial}_{r})^{2}\phi=r^{2}{\partial}_{r}^{2}\phi+r{\partial}_{r}\phi

we deduce

\displaystyle{\triangle}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\partial}_{t}^{2}+\square)\phi=\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}{\partial}_{r}^{2}\phi+2\frac{r}{t^{2}}{\partial}_{r}\phi+\frac{2t{\partial}_{t}S\phi-S^{2}\phi-S\phi}{t^{2}}+\square\phi.

and, since
{\triangle}=\not{{\triangle}}+{\partial}_{r}^{2}+\frac{2}{r}{\partial}_{r}
,

\displaystyle\not{{\triangle}}\phi+\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big){\partial}_{r}^{2}\phi+\frac{2}{r}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big){\partial}_{r}\phi=\frac{2t{\partial}_{t}S\phi-S^{2}\phi-S\phi}{t^{2}}+\square\phi.

from which (
2.1
) follws.
∎

We are led to study the equation

\displaystyle L[\phi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F[\phi]+f,\qquad L=\not{{\triangle}}+\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r})

(2.3)

with

\begin{split}F[\phi]&=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\big(2t{\partial}_{t}S\phi-S^{2}\phi-S\phi\big)=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\big(S^{2}\phi-2r{\partial}_{r}S\phi-S\phi\big).\end{split}

(2.4)

2.1.1
Energy estimates

By standard energy estimates we can easily show that solutions of (
1.6
) verify the energy estimates

\displaystyle\|{\partial}\phi(t)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|{\partial}\phi(0)\|_{H^{s}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}

Using the fact that
S
commutes with
\square
, i.e.
[\square,S]=-2\square
we also have

\displaystyle\|{\partial}S\phi(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|{\partial}S\phi(0)\|_{H^{s}}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|Sf(\tau)\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|f(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle|{\partial}S^{2}\phi(t)\|_{H^{s-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|{\partial}S^{2}\phi(0)\|_{H^{s}}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|S^{2}f(\tau)\|_{H^{s-2}}+\|Sf(\tau)\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|f(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}

We introduce the notation
\Gamma^{\leq k}\phi
to the set of all
\Gamma
derivatives of order
\leq k
where
\Gamma

denotes any vectorfield in the set
\big\{S,{\partial}_{1},{\partial}_{2},{\partial}_{3}\}
. With these notation we summarize the result as follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

The solutions of the wave equation
\square\psi=f
verify, for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq k}\phi(t)\|_{H^{s-k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq k}\phi(0)\|_{H^{s-k}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Gamma^{\leq k}f\|_{H^{s-k}}

(2.5)

As a consequence of the Lemma we have for
F[\phi]
given by (
2.4
)

\displaystyle\|F[\phi]\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim t^{-1}\|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 2}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim t^{-1}\big[\|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 2}\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Gamma^{\leq 2}f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\big].

(2.6)

2.2
Degenerate
L^{2}
estimates for
L

Recall that, see (
2.3
),
L=\not{{\triangle}}+\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r})
is a degenerate elliptic operator in the region
|x|<t
.

2.3
Böchner identities

We derive below first and second order
L^{2}
estimates for it.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have the following point-wise identity.

\begin{split}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}&=\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}+\Big|\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)\Big|^{2}{-2r^{-2}}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+\mbox{Div}[\phi]\,\,\end{split}

(2.7)

where
\mbox{Div}[\phi]
is the pure divergence expression

\begin{split}\mbox{Div}[\phi]&=2r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}\big(\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi-r|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\big)+\not{\nabla}_{a}\Big(\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\not{\nabla}^{a}(|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2})\Big)\\
&\quad-2\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\nabla^{a}{\partial}_{r}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi-2r^{-1}\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big).\end{split}

(2.8)

Proof.

We write

\begin{split}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}&=\Big|\not{{\triangle}}\phi+\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)\Big|^{2}\\
&=\big|\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big|^{2}+\Big|\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)\Big|^{2}+{2}\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi).\end{split}

(2.9)

We first note

\displaystyle|\not{{\triangle}}\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi|^{2}+r^{-2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\not{\nabla}_{a}\Big(\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\not{\nabla}^{a}(|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2})\Big).

(2.10)

This follows easily by by the usual commutation formula for covariant derivatives involving the Gauss curvature
K=r^{-2}
of the spheres
S_{r}
. I particular we have
[\not{\nabla}_{a},\not{{\triangle}}]\phi=-r^{-2}\not{\nabla}_{a}\phi
.
We next consider the mixed term

\displaystyle\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)=r^{-2}I

where

\begin{split}I=&\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big){\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)\\
&={\partial}_{r}\Big(\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi\Big)-{\partial}_{r}(\not{{\triangle}}\phi)\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi-\not{{\triangle}}\phi{\partial}_{r}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi.\\
\end{split}

(2.11)

Making use of the commutation formulas
[\not{\nabla}_{r},\not{\nabla}]=-r^{-1}\not{\nabla}
and
[{\partial}_{r},\not{{\triangle}}]=-2r^{-1}\not{{\triangle}}

\begin{split}&-{\partial}_{r}(\not{{\triangle}}\phi)\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi\\
&=-\big(\not{{\triangle}}{\partial}_{r}\phi-\frac{2}{r}\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big)\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi\\
&=-r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(\not{\nabla}^{a}{\partial}_{r}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)+r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{r}\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi.\end{split}

(2.12)

Hence

\begin{split}I=&{\partial}_{r}\Big(\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi\Big)+r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}-r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(\nabla^{a}{\partial}_{r}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)\\
&+{\frac{2r^{3}}{t^{2}}}\not{{\triangle}}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi+\frac{2}{r}\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi\\
&=r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}+2r\not{{\triangle}}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi+{\partial}_{r}\Big(\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi\Big)-r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(\nabla^{a}{\partial}_{r}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)\\
\end{split}

(2.13)

We further write, with the help of the commutation formula
[\not{\nabla}_{r},\not{\nabla}]=-r^{-1}\not{\nabla}
,

\begin{split}2r\not{{\triangle}}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi&=2r\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)-2r\big(\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi\not{\nabla}_{a}{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)\\
&=2r\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)-2r\big(\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi{\partial}_{r}\not{\nabla}_{a}\phi+r^{-1}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\big)\\
&=\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(2r\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)-{\partial}_{r}\big(r|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\big)-|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}I&=r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}+\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(2r\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)-{\partial}_{r}\big(r|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\big)-|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\\
&+{\partial}_{r}\Big(\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi\Big)-r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(\nabla^{a}{\partial}_{r}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big)\\
&=r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}-|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+{\partial}_{r}\big(\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi-r|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\big)\\
&\quad+\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(2r\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi-r^{2}\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\nabla^{a}{\partial}_{r}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\big).\end{split}

Thus back to the identity

\displaystyle\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big|^{2}+\Big|\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)\Big|^{2}+2\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi),

recalling that
\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)=r^{-2}I
and using also (
2.10
)
we deduce

\displaystyle\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+\Big|\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)\Big|^{2}+2\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+\mbox{Div}[\phi]

\displaystyle\mbox{Div}[\phi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}\Big(\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi-r|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\Big)+\not{\nabla}_{a}\Big(\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\not{\nabla}^{a}(|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle-2\not{\nabla}_{a}\Bigg(\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\nabla^{a}{\partial}_{r}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi-2\frac{1}{r}\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi{\partial}_{r}\phi\Big)

as stated.
∎

For the sake of completeness we also record below the first derivative estimates.

Lemma 2.4
.

The following point-wise identity holds true

\displaystyle\begin{split}-L[\phi]\phi&=|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)|{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}+\frac{3}{t^{2}}|\phi|^{2}-\mbox{Div}_{1}[\phi]\\
\mbox{Div}_{1}[\phi]&=\not{\nabla}_{a}\big(\phi\not{\nabla}^{a}\phi\big)+r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}\Big(\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\phi r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi+\frac{r^{3}}{t^{2}}|\phi|^{2}\Big)\end{split}

(2.14)

Proof.

Straightforward verification.
∎

2.4
Main Integral identities

As a consequence of the Lemma
2.3

we derive the degenerate Böchner identity

Lemma 2.5
.

Solutions to the equation
\square\phi=f
, verify

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+2\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(t^{-2}+r^{-2})\big|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 1}\phi\big|^{2}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|f|^{2}

(2.15)

Proof.

To check (
2.15
) we integrate (
2.8
) on
\Sigma_{t}
and derive the identity

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+2\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{-2}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}.

(2.16)

To estimate the right hand side we recall that
L[\phi]=F[\phi]+f
with
F[\phi]=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\big(2t{\partial}_{t}S\phi-S^{2}\phi-S\phi\big)
, see (
2.4
). Thus

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(t^{-2}+r^{-2})\big|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 1}\phi\big|^{2}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|f|^{2}.

This ends the verification of (
2.15
)
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the statement of the lemma remains valid even if we drop the assumption that

\phi
is supported in the region
|x|<t
.

The estimate (
2.15
) provides a good control of the second angular derivative of
\phi
in interior region
|x|<t
under the assumption that
\phi
is supported in
|x|<t
. In what follows we show that similar identities can be derived even if we remove that support assumption.

Lemma 2.6
.

The following estimate holds for general solutions of
\square\phi=f
in the exterior region
|x|\geq t
.

\begin{split}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+\big(\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}-1\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}&\lesssim t^{-2}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}r^{2}|{\partial}^{2}\phi|^{2}+t^{-2}\big(\int_{0}^{t}\|u{\partial}\square\phi\|_{L^{2}(\,^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t})}\big)^{2}\\
&+t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 1}\phi\big|^{2}+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}|f|^{2}.\end{split}

(2.17)

Proof.

We integrate the identity (
2.8
) on
\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\mbox{Div}[\phi]=\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}2{\partial}_{r}\Big(\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi-{r}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\Big)=2t^{-1}\int_{r=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}

Therefore, after neglecting the nonnegative term
\Big|\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}(r^{2}{\partial}_{r}\phi)\Big|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+2\big(1-\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+2t^{-1}\int_{r=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}

(2.18)

Hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+2t^{-1}\int_{r=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}&\leq 2t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big(r^{2}-t^{2}\big)\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}\\
&+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}.\end{split}

(2.19)

Integrating by parts we derive in the integral
I=\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2})\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}
we deduce

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2})\big|\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi|^{2}=\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2})\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi=-\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}{\partial}_{r}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}_{r}\phi\big({\partial}_{r}\not{{\triangle}}\phi+2r^{-1}\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}r^{-2}{\partial}_{r}\big(r^{2}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}_{r}\phi\big)\not{{\triangle}}\phi-\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}_{r}\phi 2r^{-1}\not{{\triangle}}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big(4r-2r^{-1}t^{2}\big){\partial}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi-\big(2r-2r^{-1}t^{2}\big){\partial}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi

Hence

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}2r{\partial}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi-\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}2r\not{\nabla}{\partial}_{r}\phi\not{\nabla}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi-\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}2r\big({\partial}_{r}\not{\nabla}\phi+r^{-1}\not{\nabla}\phi\big)\not{\nabla}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi-\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}r{\partial}_{r}\big(|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\big)-2\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}

Now

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}r{\partial}_{r}\big(|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{|\xi|=1}r{\partial}_{r}\big(|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\big)r^{2}d\sigma(\xi)=-3\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}-t\int_{|x|=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}

Hence

I=\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+t\int_{|x|=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}

(2.20)

Back to (
2.19
) we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+\frac{2}{t}\int_{r=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\leq t^{-2}I+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi+t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+t^{-1}\int_{r=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}

Hence

\begin{split}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\int_{r=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}&\lesssim t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}\\
&\quad{+t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.21)

Using the inequality
(r^{2}-t^{2})\leq 2t(r-t)+2(r-t)^{2}
we write, for some
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}2t(r-t)\big|{\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big|+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r-t)^{2}|{\partial}^{2}\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}t^{2}\big|\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big|^{2}+\lambda\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r-t)^{2}\big|{\partial}_{r}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r-t)^{2}|{\partial}^{2}\phi|^{2}

Therefore,

t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r^{2}-t^{2}){\partial}^{2}_{r}\phi\not{{\triangle}}\phi\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{{\triangle}}\phi\big|^{2}+\lambda t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r-t)^{2}\big|{\partial}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}

(2.22)

Thus, taking
\lambda
large we deduce from (
2.10
), (
2.21
), and the above estimate

\begin{split}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|\not{\nabla}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\int_{r=t}|\not{\nabla}\phi|^{2}&\lesssim t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r-t)^{2}\big|{\partial}^{2}\phi\big|^{2}+\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}\big|L[\phi]\big|^{2}+t^{-2}\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.\end{split}

(2.23)

It remains to estimate the term
\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}(r-t)^{2}|{\partial}_{r}^{2}\phi|^{2}
. To do this we appeal to the following.

Lemma 2.7
.

The following estimate holds true, with
u=t-r
in the exterior region
\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma
where
r\geq t
.

\displaystyle\int_{\,{}^{(ext)}\Sigma_{t}}|u|^{2}|{\partial}^{2}\phi|^{2}

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

As discussed in Remark
1.3
, the proof of Theorem
1.2
hinges on a geometric observation concerning the localized set
S_{k,l}(t,x)
. For the reader’s convenience, we restate its definition below:

\begin{split}\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t\in\mathbb{R}/\{0\},\mu\in\{+,-\},\qquad&S_{k,l}(t,x):=\big\{\xi:|1+\frac{\mu x\cdot\xi}{t|\xi|}|\leq 2^{l},|\xi|\in[2^{k-1},2^{k}]\big\}.\end{split}

(3.1)

First, we explain the relevance of this set. From the standard Duhamel’s formula for the wave equation, it suffices to estimate the associated half-wave. Specifically, we define

U(t):=({\partial}_{t}-i|\nabla|)\phi,\quad V(t):=e^{it|\nabla|}U(t).

(3.2)

Hence we can recover
{\partial}\phi
from
U
and its complex conjugate as follows,

\begin{split}{\partial}_{t}\phi&=\frac{U(t)+\overline{U(t)}}{2},\quad\nabla\phi=\frac{\nabla\big(U(t)-\overline{U(t)}\big)}{i2|\nabla|}.\end{split}

(3.3)

Moreover, we also have

{\partial}_{t}V(t)=e^{it|\nabla|}({\partial}_{t}+i|\nabla|)U(t)=e^{it|\nabla|}\Box\phi.\\

(3.4)

For the homogeneous case,
V(t){=V(0)}
plays the role of the initial data. Thus
V(t)
is only useful in the inhomogenuous case.

On the Fourier side, from (
3.2
), we have

U(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi-it|\xi|}\widehat{V}(t,\xi)d\xi,\quad\overline{U}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi+it|\xi|}\widehat{\overline{V}}(t,\xi)d\xi.

Inspired by stationary phase analysis, we aim to exploit the oscillation of the phase in the radial direction. Since the function
\xi\mapsto|\xi|
is homogeneous of degree one, we have

\forall\mu\in\{+,-\},\quad e^{ix\cdot\xi+it\mu|\xi|}=\frac{\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}}{ix\cdot\xi+it\mu|\xi|}\big(e^{ix\cdot\xi+it\mu|\xi|}\big).

(3.5)

Owing to this homogeneity property, the radial derivative does not introduce any further singularities in the integration by parts procedure. Consequently, we have

\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}(\frac{1}{ix\cdot\xi+it\mu|\xi|})=\frac{-1}{ix\cdot\xi+it\mu|\xi|}.

To assess the severity of the potential singularity in the denominator of (
3.5
), we must analyze the localized set
S_{k,l}(t,x)
defined in (
3.1
). This will be the focus of the following subsection.

3.1

Lemma 3.1
.

For the localized set
S_{k,l}(t,x)
defined in (
3.1
), the following measure estimate holds,

|S_{k,l}(t,x)|\lesssim 2^{3k+l}.

(3.6)

Proof.

Denote
\beta:=|x|/t.
Note that

1+\frac{\mu x\cdot\xi}{t|\xi|}=1+\mu\beta\cos(\angle(x,\xi)).

(3.7)

The desired estimate (
3.6
) is trivial if
l\geq-100
because

|S_{k,l}|\leq|\{\xi:\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},|\xi|\sim 2^{k}\}|\lesssim 2^{3k}.

Now, it would be sufficient to consider the case
l\leq-100

Let

. Let

Due to the fact that
\beta\geq 0
and
l\leq-100.
It’s obvious that

.
Therefore, in terms of the spherical coordinate, we have

\int_{S_{k,l}}1d\xi\lesssim\int_{c2^{k-2}}^{C2^{k+2}}\int_{\Theta_{l}}r^{2}\sin\theta drd\theta\lesssim 2^{3k}\int_{\Theta_{l}}\sin\theta d\theta.

To sum up, to prove our desired estimate (
3.6
), it would be sufficient to prove the following estimate,

\textup{(Claim)}:\qquad\int_{\Theta_{l}}\sin\theta d\theta\lesssim 2^{l}.

(3.8)

Note that

\int_{\Theta_{l}\cap[0,2^{l/2+10}]}\sin\theta d\theta\lesssim\int_{[0,2^{l/2+10}]}\sin\theta d\theta=\int_{0}^{2^{l/2+10}}\sin\theta d\theta\lesssim 2^{l}.

It remains to prove the following estimate

\int_{\Theta_{l}\cap[2^{l/2+10},\pi/2]}\sin\theta d\theta\lesssim 2^{l}

Note that, if
0<x\ll 1
, we have

\sin x\geq\frac{x}{2},\quad\Longrightarrow\big|\cos(2x)-\cos(x)\big|=\int_{x}^{2x}\sin\theta d\theta\geq\frac{3x^{2}}{4}.

If there exists

, then from the monotonicity of

On the other hand, since

and

Hence we arrive at a contradiction.

To sum up, we conclude

\forall\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{l}\cap[2^{l/2+10},2^{-10}],\quad\theta_{1}/2\leq\theta_{2}\leq 2\theta_{1}.

With the above proved fact and the Taylor’s theorem, for any

, we have

|\sin\theta_{1}||\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\leq 2^{4}\beta|\cos\theta_{1}-\cos\theta_{2}|=2^{4}\big|(1-\beta\cos\theta_{1})-(1-\beta\cos\theta_{2})\big|\leq 2^{l+5}.

Therefore, after fixing an arbitrary

Lastly, we estimate the contribution from the set

. Note that

is bounded from the above and the below in the set

|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\lesssim\beta|\cos\theta_{1}-\cos\theta_{2}|=\big|(1-\beta\cos\theta_{1})-(1-\beta\cos\theta_{2})\big|\leq 2^{l+5}

Therefore, again after fixing an arbitrary

To sum up, we have

\int_{\Theta_{l}}\sin\theta d\theta\lesssim 2^{l}.

Hence finishing the proof of the Claim in (

The estimate of
N^{high}_{i}(t)
.

Note that, from the separation assumption, i.e., (
1.12
), the following estimate always holds,

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},i,j\in\{1,\cdots,m\},i\neq j,\quad{\langle t-r_{i}\rangle}^{-1}{\langle t-r_{j}\rangle}^{-1}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}.

Let
0<\delta\ll 1
. From the no self-interaction assumption, the above estimate, the first decay estimate in (
4.2
), and estimating the nonlinear terms in such a way that the wave with most vector fields is taken in
L^{2}
and the other two waves in
L^{\infty}
, we have

\sum_{i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}}\|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 30}N_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon_{1}^{3}\langle t\rangle^{-3/2+2\delta}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-3/2+2\delta}\epsilon_{0}^{2}.

(4.3)

Step 2.
The estimate of
E_{i}(t)
.

From the obtained estimate (
4.3
) and the standard energy estimate, we conclude

\sum_{i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}}E_{i}(t)^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}}E_{i}(0)^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle s\rangle^{-3/2+2\delta}\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{0}^{2}ds\lesssim\epsilon_{0}^{2}.

(4.4)

Step 3.
The estimate of
N^{low}_{i}(t)
.

The estimates for
N^{low}_{i}(t)
and
N^{high}_{i}(t)
differ significantly in their treatment of the wave with the most vector fields. For
N^{low}_{i}(t)
, this wave can be placed in
L^{\infty}
due to a strategically chosen gap between the total number of derivatives used in the estimate and the maximum number of derivatives propagated. This is not possible for
N^{high}_{i}(t)
.

We divide the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into
N+1
-regions as follows,

\mathbb{R}^{3}:=\cup_{i=0}^{N}R_{i}(t),\quad\forall i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},\quad R_{i}(t):=\{x:|x-r_{i}|\leq ct\},\quad R_{0}:=(\cup_{i=1}^{N}R_{i}(t))^{c},

where the sufficiently small absolute constant
c
only depends the fixed parameters
\{c_{i;j},i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},j\in\{1,2,3\}\}
. In region
R_{i}(t)
, thanks to the separation condition (
1.12
), the decay rates of other waves, except the one corresponding to
u_{i}
, are improved, see the second estimate in (
4.2
). In region
R_{0}(t)
, the decay rates of all waves are improved. Therefore, thanks to the no-self interaction condition, in region
R_{i}(t)
, after putting any two waves other than the one corresponding to
u_{i}
in
L^{\infty}
and the other one in
L^{2}
regardless how many vector fields they have, from the second estimate in (
4.2
), we have

\sum_{i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}}\|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 25}N_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon_{1}^{3}\langle t\rangle^{-1+3\delta}\langle t\rangle^{-1+3\delta}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-2+6\delta}\epsilon_{0}^{2}.

(4.5)

This improves the bootstrap argument and thus end the proof of the theorem. The scattering property follows from the corollary of the energy estimate, see (
4.4
).

5
Proof of Theorem
1.4

To prove our main theorem, we use the standard bootstrap argument, which mainly relies on the decay estimate obtained in (
1.10
) in Theorem
1.3
. For
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\},
we define

E_{i}(t):=\|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 30}\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\quad W_{i}(t):=\|\widehat{{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 30}\phi_{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}.

Moreover, we define the
L^{\infty}_{x}
-type norm,

K_{i}(t):=\|{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 20}\phi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

Let
0<\delta\ll 1
. We make the following bootstrap assumption

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sum_{i=1,\cdots,m}E_{i}(t)+\langle t\rangle^{-\delta}W_{i}(t)+\langle t\rangle^{1-\delta}K_{i}(t)\leq\epsilon_{1}:=\epsilon_{0}^{3/4}.

(5.1)

To close the bootstrap argument, we proceed in three steps as follows.

Step 1.
Energy estimate.

Since our nonlinearity is semilinear and cubic, from the
L^{2}_{x}-L^{\infty}_{x}-L^{\infty}_{x}
-type trilinear estimate and the energy estimate (
2.5
), we have

\forall i\in\{1,\cdots,m\},\quad E_{i}(t)-E_{i}(0)\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\langle s\rangle^{-2+2\delta}\epsilon_{1}^{3}ds\lesssim\epsilon_{0}.

(5.2)

Step 2.

L^{\infty}_{\xi}
estimate

From the Duhamel’s formula and the
L^{2}_{x}-L^{2}_{x}-L^{\infty}_{x}
-type trilinear estimate, we have

\begin{split}W_{i}(t)=\|\widehat{{\partial}\Gamma^{\leq 30}\phi_{i}}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}&\lesssim\epsilon_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\,^{(i)}\square\Gamma^{\leq 30}\phi_{i}\|_{L^{1}_{x}}ds\lesssim\epsilon_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle s\rangle^{-1+\delta}\epsilon_{1}^{3}ds\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{\delta}\epsilon_{0}.\\
\end{split}

(5.3)

Step 3.

L^{\infty}_{x}
-decay estimate

Note that, from the
L^{2}_{x}-L^{\infty}_{x}-L^{\infty}_{x}
-type trilinear estimate, we have

\forall t\in[0,T],\qquad\|\,^{(i)}\square\Gamma^{\leq 30}\phi_{i}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-2+2\delta}\epsilon_{1}^{3}.

Therefore, from the above estimate, the improved energy estimate (
5.2
), and the improved
W_{a}(t)
-estimate (
5.3
), and the decay estimate obtained in (
1.10
) in Theorem
1.3
, we have

K_{a}(t)\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1+\delta}\epsilon_{0}+\langle t\rangle^{-3/2+2\delta}\epsilon_{0}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1+\delta}\epsilon_{0}.

Now, it’s clear that the bootstrap assumption in (
5.1
) is improved. The scattering property follows from the corollary of the energy estimate, see (
5.2
). Therefore, finishing the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

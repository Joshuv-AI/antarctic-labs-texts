New tools for the study of Bochner differential operators
D. Barrios Rolanía

Abstract
A sequence { δ n ( k ) } \{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\} associated to a Bochner differential operator is introduced as an effective tool to study this kind of operators. Some properties of this sequence are proven and used to deduce that a particular operator leads to solutions of a bispectral problem. In addition, the inverse problem is studied; that is, given a sequence { λ n } \{\lambda_{n}\} of complex numbers and a sequence { P n } \{P_{n}\} of polynomials with complex coefficients, deg ⁡ P n = n \deg{P_{n}}=n , we find a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a Bochner differential operator that has those sequences as eigenvalues and eigenpolynomials, respectively. The mentioned condition also depends on { δ n ( k ) } \{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\} .



Keywords:
Differential operator, bispectral problem, polynomial eigenfunctions

33C45, 34L10, 39A70

Introduction

Throughout this work, we consider the ordinary differential operator of order
N
defined as

L\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}(x)\partial_{x}^{i}

(1)

where

a_{i}(x)

are complex polynomials in the variable
x
,

\deg(a_{i})\leq i,
and

\partial^{i}_{x},\,i=1,\ldots,N
, represents the derivative of order
i
with respect to
x
. We call this operator, the
Bochner differential operator
.
We also consider a sequence of eigenfunctions

\{P_{n}\},

which we assume that are monic polynomials with
\deg(P_{n})=n
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. That is,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}(x)\partial_{x}^{i}P_{n}(x)=\lambda_{n}P_{n}(x)\,,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

(2)

where

\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{C}

is the corresponding sequence of eigenvalues.
The polynomials
\{P_{n}\}
satisfying (
2
) are called
eigenpolynomials
in this work.

Although we study eigenpolynomials of (
1
) in general, we are especially interested in families of polynomials that are, at the same time, eigenfunctions of a certain difference operator
J
, satisfying

\left(J(n)P_{n}\right)(x)=xP_{n}(x),

(3)

where

J(n)P_{n}=\sum_{k=n-p}^{n}\alpha_{n,k}P_{k}+P_{n+1}

for a fixed
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
This is equivalent to the fact that the polynomials

\{P_{n}\}

satisfy
a
(p+2)
-term recurrence relation

\left.\begin{array}[]{r}\displaystyle\sum_{k=n-p}^{n-1}\alpha_{n,k}P_{k}(x)+(\alpha_{n,n}-x)P_{n}(x)+P_{n+1}(x)=0\,,\quad n=0,1,\ldots,\\
P_{0}=1,\quad P_{-1}=\cdots=P_{-p}=0.\end{array}\right\}

(4)

If the polynomials

P_{n}

satisfy (
2
) and (
3
), we say that the sequence

\{P_{n}\},\,n\in\mathbb{N},

is a solution for the bispectral problem defined by
L
and
J
. This kind of problem was studied for the first time by S. Bochner in 1929
[
3
]
. He studied the case where the order of the differential operator is
N=2
, determining the sequences of classical orthogonal polynomials (Hermite, Jacobi and Laguerre) as the solutions of the bispectral problem with
p=1
in (
4
). Eleven years later, Krall
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
, established a classification with seven families of eigenpolynomials in the case
N=4
. Since these relevant works of Bochner and Krall, several contributions were made about properties of eigenpolynomials of operators (
1
) and also about the bispectral problem (see, for example,
[
2
]
,
[
4
]

[
7
]
). However, the study of the case of arbitrary order
N
remains open.

In
[
1
]
we introduced the sequences

\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\},\,n\in\mathbb{N},
for
k=0,1,\ldots,N,
associated with the differential operator (
1
) of order
N
as an auxiliary tool that helped us obtaining the explicit expression of the
eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of a differential operator. The goal of this work is to present these sequences
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
as a useful tool in the study of Bochner differential operators and its eigenpolynomials. In order to do this, we will first prove some properties that the sequences
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
satisfy. In particular, we will obtain an expression for the coefficients of the eigenpolynomials in terms of a (small) amount of elements of the sequence
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
.

Furthermore, we will use the sequence
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
to prove that a particular Bochner differential operator of order
N
leads to a sequence of eigenpolynomials satisfying an
(N+1)
-term recurrence relation. This operator appeared in
[
4
]
as one of the only two operators conjectured to give rise to sequences of eigenpolynomials satisfying an
(N+1)
-term recurrence relation; that is, as one of the only two operators whose eigenpolynomials are solutions of this bispectral problem.

Finally, we will use the sequence
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
to obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of solution of the inverse problem. That is, given a sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}
of complex numbers and a sequence
\{P_{n}\}
of polynomials with complex coefficients such that

\deg{P_{n}}=n
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we will find a necessary and sufficient condition in terms of
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
for the existence of a Bochner differential operator such that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{P_{n}\}
are its eigenvalues and eigenpolynomials, respectively.

Before expanding on our main goals, we would like to introduce a result that explains the equivalence between the sequences

\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}

and the coefficients of polynomials
a_{i}(x)
defining (
1
).

Theorem 1
.

Given two sequences
\{a_{n,i}\},i=0,1,\dots,n,\,n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\},\ n=0,1,\dots,\ k=0,1,\dots,n,
the following connections are equivalent

1.

For each
n=0,1,\dots,

\delta_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=k}^{n}{n\choose i}i!a_{i,i-k},\quad k=0,1,\ldots,n\,.

(5)

2.

For each
n=0,1,\dots,

n!a_{n,n-k}=\sum_{i=k}^{n}{n\choose i}(-1)^{n-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}\quad\text{ for }k=0,1,\ldots,n\,.

(6)

The proof of Theorem
1
is included in Section
2
along with some auxiliary results used in this paper .

Theorem
1
provides the key to define and use the sequences

\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}

from the differential operator

L

given in (
1
). Thus, in Section
3
the direct problem is addressed and, using (
5
), the eigenpolynomials and eigenvalues of
L
are obtained. Moreover, in this section we will prove that, for each

k=0,1,\ldots,N,

the sequence

\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\},\,n\in\mathbb{N},

is obtained from the
N-k+1
first terms

\delta_{k}^{(k)},\,\delta_{k+1}^{(k)},\ldots,\delta_{N}^{(k)}.

In Section
4
, we will use the tools developed in Section
3
to address the conjecture from
[
4
]
about the operators that lead to solutions of the bispectral problems.

On the other hand, (
6
) makes possible to define the coefficients

a_{i}(x)

of the differential operator (
1
) using the sequences

\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
.
This will be done in Section
5
, where the inverse problem is approached.

To finish, we will summarize our conclusions and express some ideas about possible future works in Section

.

Direct problem

i
.

From (
26
), we obtain

b_{n,n-i}=\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{n-i+k}^{(k)}b_{n,n-i+k}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i}},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n.

(29)

If
i=1
, (
29
) leads to

b_{n,n-1}=\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-1}},

then (
28
) holds for
i=1
.

Now, assume (
28
) holds up to
i-1
and let us analyze the case
i
.

We must notice that
b_{n,n-i+k}=0
if
k>i
. Then, if
i\leq N
, the upper bound of the summation in (
29
) can be restricted to
i
. Moreover, we recall that

\delta_{n-i+k}^{(k)}=0
for
k\geq N+1
. Therefore, either with
i\leq N
or with
i>N
, we can write (
29
) as

\displaystyle b_{n,n-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n-i+1}^{(1)}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i}}b_{n,n-i+1}+\sum_{k=2}^{i}\frac{\delta_{n-i+k}^{(k)}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i}}b_{n,n-i+k}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n-i+1}^{(1)}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i}}b_{n,n-i+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{\delta_{n-j+1}^{(i-j+1)}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i}}b_{n,n-j+1}.

On the other hand, it is easy to see that, for any upper Hessenberg matrix

H

of order

m

with entries

h_{ij},i,j=1,2,\ldots,m,

and leading principal submatrices
H_{s}
of order

s=1,2,\ldots,m-1,

we have

\det(H)=h_{m,m}\det(H_{m-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{m-j}h_{j,m}\prod_{\ell=j}^{m-1}h_{\ell+1,\ell}\det(H_{j-1}).

(31)

Thanks to the induction hypothesis, we know
b_{n,n-i+1}
and
b_{n,n+1-j},1\leq j\leq i-1
are the determinants defined in (
28
), that is, they are determinants of matrices of size
i-1
and
j-1
, respectively. Then, comparing (
30
) with (
31
) we obtain that
b_{n,n-i}
is the determinant of an upper Hessenberg matrix
H
of order
i
with

h_{i,i}=\frac{\delta_{n-i+1}^{(1)}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i}},\quad\det(H_{i-1})=b_{n,n-i+1},\quad h_{j,i}=\frac{\delta_{n+1-j}^{(i+1-j)}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i}},\quad\det(H_{j-1})=b_{n,n-j+1}.

It is easy to check
H
is the matrix whose determinant appears in (
28
), which concludes our proof.

∎

In the following theorem, the expression of
\delta_{n}^{(k)},\,n>N
, in terms of
\delta_{1}^{(k)},\dots,\delta_{N}^{(k)}
is given.

Theorem 4
.

Let
L
be the operator defined in (
1
) and let
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
be the sequence defined in (
5
). Then,

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\delta_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=k}^{N}(-1)^{N-i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{n-i-1}{N-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)},\quad n\geq N+1.\\
\delta_{n}^{(k)}=0,\quad n\geq k>N.\end{cases}

(32)

Proof.

Let us prove (
32
) by induction on
n
. The base case is
n=N+1
.
Since the sequence (
5
) is equivalent to (
6
) (see Theorem
2
) and (
23
), we obtain
a_{N+1}\equiv 0
and, for
k\leq N+1
,

0=(N+1)!a_{N+1,N+1-k}=\sum_{i=k}^{N+1}\binom{N+1}{i}(-1)^{N+1-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}.

Therefore,

\delta_{N+1}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=k}^{N}\binom{N+1}{i}(-1)^{N-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}.

Notice that this is exactly (
32
) for
n=N+1
, so the base case is proved.

For the inductive step, assume the result is true for
n\geq N+1
. For
n+1
, (
6
) is

0=(n+1)!a_{n+1,n+1-k}=\sum_{i=k}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{n+1-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)},

which is equivalent to

\delta_{n+1}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}.

Applying the induction hypothesis,

\delta_{n+1}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=k}^{N}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}+\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\sum_{\ell=k}^{N}(-1)^{N-\ell}\binom{i}{\ell}\binom{i-\ell-1}{N-\ell}\delta_{\ell}^{(k)}.

(33)

In (
33
), the coefficient that multiplies each
\delta_{\ell}^{(k)}
for

\ell\geq k
is

\binom{n+1}{\ell}(-1)^{n-\ell}+\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{N-\ell+n-i}\binom{i}{\ell}\binom{i-\ell-1}{N-\ell}.

(34)

In order to prove our result, we must show the above coefficient is equal to the coefficient that multiplies each
\delta_{\ell}^{(k)}
in the expression of
\delta_{n+1}^{(k)}
given by (
32
). This coefficient is

(-1)^{N-\ell}\binom{n+1}{\ell}\binom{n-\ell}{N-\ell}=(-1)^{N-\ell}\frac{(n+1)!}{\ell!(N-\ell)!(n-N)!(n+1-\ell)}.

(35)

To prove (
34
) coincides with the right hand side of (
35
) let us expand (
34
), which is

\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)!}{(n+1-\ell)!\ell!}(-1)^{n-\ell}+\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\frac{(n+1)!}{(n+1-i)!i!}\frac{(-1)^{n-i}(-1)^{N-\ell}}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\frac{i!}{(i-N-1)!(i-\ell)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{N-\ell}\frac{(n+1)!}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\left(\frac{(-1)^{n-N}}{(n+1-\ell)(n-\ell)\cdots(N+1-\ell)}+\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-i}}{(n+1-i)!(i-N-1)!(i-\ell)}\right).

(36)

Taking
s=\ell-N
and
j=i-N
, in (
36
) we can write

(-1)^{N-\ell}\frac{(n+1)!}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\left(\frac{(-1)^{n-N}}{(1-s)(2-s)\cdots(n-N+1-s)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-N}\frac{(-1)^{n-N-j}}{(n-N+1-j)!(j-1)!(j-s)}\right).

(37)

Now we will prove the following partial fraction decomposition for

n\in\mathbb{N},\,n\geq N,\,s\in\mathbb{C},\,s\neq 1,2,\ldots,n-N+1

,

\frac{1}{(1-s)(2-s)\cdots(n-N+1-s)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-N+1}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{(n-N+1-i)!(i-1)!(i-s)}.

(38)

In fact, we would like to obtain the coefficients for the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{(1-s)(2-s)\cdots(n-N+1-s)}=\frac{A_{1}}{1-s}+\frac{A_{2}}{2-s}+\cdots\frac{A_{n-N+1}}{n-N+1-s}.

The right hand side in the above expression can be written with a common denominator as

\sum_{i=1}^{n-N+1}\frac{A_{i}}{i-s}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-N+1}\frac{A_{i}\prod_{k\neq i}(k-s)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n-N+1}(k-s)}.

Consequently,

1=\sum_{i=1}^{n-N+1}A_{i}\prod_{k\neq i}(k-s)

and taking
s=i,1\leq i\leq n-N+1
, we arrive to (
38
).

Using the above partial fraction decomposition in (
37
), we obtain

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-\ell}\frac{(n+1)!}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-N+1}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{(n-N+1-i)!(i-1)!(i-s)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-N}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{(n-N+1-j)!(j-1)!(j-s)}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-\ell}\frac{(n+1)!}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\frac{(-1)^{n-N}}{(n-N)!(n-N+1-s)}=(-1)^{N-\ell}\frac{(n+1)!}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\frac{1}{(n-N)!(n-N+1-s)}.

Undoing the change
s=\ell-N
, this is

(-1)^{N-\ell}\frac{(n+1)!}{\ell!(N-\ell)!(n-N)!(n+1-\ell)},

which is exactly (
35
).

Finally, combining (
23
) and (
5
) we obtain
\delta_{n}^{(k)}=0,\ n\geq k>N
, as we remarked after (
25
).

∎

Combining theorems
3
and
4
we obtain an expression of each coefficient
b_{n,n-i}
that depends, at most, on
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N},\delta_{1}^{(1)},\dots,\delta_{N}^{(1)},\delta_{2}^{(2)},\dots,\delta_{N}^{(2)},

\delta_{3}^{(3)},\dots,\delta_{N}^{(3)},\dots,\delta_{N}^{(N)}
. In particular, if
n-i\geq N
, the expression for the coefficient is very simple, as shown in the following result.

Corollary 1
.

Let
n-i\geq N
. Then, we have

b_{n,n-i}=\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-i+1)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{N}(-1)^{N-m}\binom{N}{m}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{N}(n-j)-\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{N}(n-\ell-j)\right)\lambda_{m}\right)}\det(M),

(39)

where
M
is an upper Hessenberg
i\times i
matrix that has

•

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{N-i}\binom{N}{i}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}(n-\ell-j)-\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}(n-j)\right)\lambda_{i}
as the
\ell
-th entry of the subdiagonal.

•

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{N-i}\binom{N}{i}\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}(n-\ell+1-j)\delta_{i}^{(1)}
as the
\ell
-th entry of the main diagonal.

•

\displaystyle\sum_{i=k}^{N}(-1)^{N-i}\binom{N}{i}\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}(n-\ell+1-j)\delta_{i}^{(k)}
as the
\ell
-th entry of the
(k-1)
-superdiagonal,
\,\,k=2,\ldots,N-1
.

•

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(n-\ell+1-j)\delta_{N}^{(N)}
as the
\ell
-th entry of the
(N-1)
-superdiagonal.

•

[0,\cdots,0]
as the
m
-superdiagonal for
m\geq N
.

Proof.

From (
28
) in Theorem
3
, we have

\displaystyle b_{n,n-i}=\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-1})\cdots(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i})}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\delta_{n}^{(1)}&\delta_{n}^{(2)}&\cdots&\delta_{n}^{(N)}&0&{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-1}}}&\\[14.22636pt]
\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n}&\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}&\cdots&\delta_{n-1}^{(N-1)}&\delta_{n-1}^{(N)}&\ddots&\\[14.22636pt]
0&\lambda_{n-2}-\lambda_{n}&\ddots&&\ddots&\ddots&0\\[14.22636pt]
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&&\ddots&\delta_{n-i+N}^{(N)}\\[14.22636pt]
&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&&\delta_{n-i+N-1}^{(N-1)}\\[14.22636pt]
&&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\[14.22636pt]
&&&&0&\lambda_{n-i+1}-\lambda_{n}&\delta_{n-i+1}^{(1)}\end{array}\right|.

Notice that the
m
-th row of the determinant contains entries of the form
\delta_{n-m+1}^{(k)}
. Since
n-i\geq N
, we can apply (
32
) to each row and extract
n-m+1
from each row, leading to (
39
).

∎

Remark 1
.

Taking
k=0
in (
32
), we underline that the sequence of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}
of every differential operator (
1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{N-i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{n-i-1}{N-i}\lambda_{i},\quad n\geq N+1.

In particular, if
L
in (
1
) is an operator of order
N=2
, then

\lambda_{n}=-n(n-2)\lambda_{1}+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\lambda_{2}.

(40)

That is, all the eigenvalues of the classical orthogonal polynomials are a linear combination of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
with the coefficients given by (
40
). Notice that if we recall the general expression of the eigenvalues of the classical orthogonal polynomials, it is trivial to verify (
40
):

•

For Jacobi polynomials,
\lambda_{n}=-n(n+1+\alpha+\beta),\ \alpha,\beta>-1
.

•

For Laguerre polynomials,
\lambda_{n}=-n
.

•

For Hermite polynomials,
\lambda_{n}=-2n
.

However, this fact seems to have remained unnoticed in the literature to the best of our knowledge.

4
Example of use of
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
for a particular Bochner differential operator

In this section we study the Bochner differential operator

L:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}x^{i-1}\partial_{x}^{i}+x\partial_{x}.

(41)

The reason to study this operator in particular is Conjecture 1.10 in
[
4
]
. This conjecture claims that the only two
N
-degree Bochner differential operators such that their eigenpolynomials satisfy an
(N+1)
-term recurrence are (
41
) and

L=q^{\prime}(G)G+x\delta_{x},

(42)

where, given
\ell
a divisor of
N
,

G:=\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}a_{m}(x\partial_{x})^{m}\partial_{x}

and
q(t)
is any complex polynomial of degree
N/\ell
without a constant term.

That is, if this conjecture is true, only these two Bochner differential operators lead to solutions of this bispectral problem.

The case
N=3
is studied and proved to be true in
[
4
]
. However, the techniques used in that work are extremely complicated and it seems hard to extend them for general values of
N
. Consequently, we propose to address the problem using the sequences

\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\},\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,k=0,1,\ldots
,
given in (
5
). In this paper, we will only focus in using the sequence
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
to prove that the eigenpolynomials of (
41
) satisfy an
(N+1)
-term recurrence relation. We hope to address the remaining part of the conjecture in a future work.

Before proving our result, let us make some preliminary simplifications. The polynomials
a_{i}(x)
defining the operator (
41
) are

a_{i}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x+c_{1},&\text{if }i=1,\\
c_{i}x^{i-1},&\text{if }i=2,\dots,N.\end{array}\right.

Hence, using (
5
), we can deduce

\delta_{n}^{(k)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{clll}n,&\text{if }&k=0,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{s=k}^{n}\binom{n}{s}s!c_{s},&\text{if }&k=1,\\
0,&\text{if }&k>1.\\
\end{array}\right.

(43)

In particular (see (
27
)), the eigenvalues are

\lambda_{n}=n.

With the notation of (
25
), from (
43
) and Theorem
3
,

(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-1})(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-2})\cdots(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-i})b_{n,n-i}=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\displaystyle\delta_{n}^{(1)}&0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
-1&\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}&0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
0&-2&\ddots&\ddots&&&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&-i+2&\delta_{n-i+2}^{(1)}&0\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0&-i+1&\delta_{n-i+1}^{(1)}\end{array}\right|.

This is,

b_{n,n-i}=\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{n-i+1}^{(1)}}{i!}

(44)

or, what is the same,

P_{n}(x)=x^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{n-i+1}^{(1)}}{i!}x^{n-i},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Now we can prove our result. That is, we prove that the above sequence of eigenpolynomials of
L
follows an
(N+1)
-term recurrence relation.

Theorem 5
.

Let
L
be the differential operator introduced in (
41
). If

\{P_{n}\}

is the sequence of eigenpolynomials and

\{\lambda_{n}\},\,n\in\mathbb{N},

the corresponding sequence of eigenvalues for
L
,
(that is,

LP_{n}=\lambda_{n}P_{n},\,n\in\mathbb{N},
)
then the following recurrence relation is satisfied,

\left.\begin{array}[]{rlll}\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}\alpha_{n,n-s}P_{n-s}(x)+(\alpha_{n,n}-x)P_{n}(x)+P_{n+1}(x)=0,&n=0,1,\ldots\\
P_{-N+1}=P_{-N+2}=\cdots=P_{-1}=0,&P_{0}=1,\end{array}\right\}

(45)

with

\alpha_{n,n-s}=\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+1}}{(s+1)!}\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\binom{s+1}{j}(-1)^{j}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+j},\quad s=0,\ldots,N-1,

(46)

(understanding

\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+1}=1

when
s=0
).

Proof.

With the above notation, it is immediate to see that (
45
) can be written as

\left.\begin{array}[]{rlll}\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n,n-s}b_{n-s,i}=b_{n,i-1}-b_{n+1,i}\\
n=0,1,\ldots,\quad i=0,1,\ldots,n+1,\\
\end{array}\right\}

(47)

where, as usual,

b_{m,j}=0

for

j>m
or

m<0
.

For each fixed

n\in\{0,1,\ldots\}

the set of polynomials

\{P_{0}(x),\,P_{1}(x),\ldots,P_{n}(x),\,xP_{n}(x)\}

constitutes a base of the space of polynomials of degree up to
n+1
. Hence, there exist

\alpha_{n,0},\,\alpha_{n,1},\,\ldots\alpha_{n,n}

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{n}\alpha_{n,n-s}P_{n-s}(x)+(\alpha_{n,n}-x)P_{n}(x)+P_{n+1}(x)=0

(48)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{n}\alpha_{n,n-s}b_{n-s,i}=b_{n,i-1}-b_{n+1,i},\quad i=0,1,\ldots,n+1.

(49)

As a consequence, (
45
) and (
47
) are satisfied for

n=0,1,\ldots,N-1

(assuming

\alpha_{n,i}=0

for

i<0
).
Moreover, for each fixed

n\in\{0,1,\ldots\}
, (
49
) can be interpreted as a triangular system whose solutions, obtained successively taking

i=n,\,n-1,\,\ldots,n-N+1,

are

\left.\begin{array}[]{ccl}\alpha_{n,n}&=&b_{n,n-1}-b_{n+1,n}\\
\alpha_{n,n-1}&=&-\alpha_{n,n}b_{n,n-1}+b_{n,n-2}-b_{n+1,n-1}\\
\alpha_{n,n-2}&=&-\alpha_{n,n-1}b_{n-1,n-2}-\alpha_{n,n}b_{n,n-2}+b_{n,n-3}-b_{n+1,n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{n,n-N+1}&=&-\alpha_{n,n-N+2}b_{n-N+2,n-N+1}-\cdots-\alpha_{n,n}b_{n,n-N+1}+b_{n,n-N}-b_{n+1,n-N+1}.\\
\end{array}\right\}

(50)

The idea now is to use (
44
) and (
50
) to prove (
46
).

Firstly, notice that (
50
) implies (
46
) holds for

s=0
.
Secondly, assuming (
46
) is satisfied for

s=0,1,\ldots,k

with

k<N-1
, from (
50
) we have

\alpha_{n,n-k-1}=-\sum_{s=0}^{k}\alpha_{n,n-s}b_{n-s,n-k-1}+b_{n,n-k-2}-b_{n+1,n-k-1}

(51)

where, for

s=0,1,\ldots,k,

\displaystyle\alpha_{n,n-s}b_{n-s,n-k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+1}}{(s+1)!}\right)\left(\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n-s}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k-s+1)!}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\binom{s+1}{j}(-1)^{j}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+j}

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k+2)!}\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\frac{(k+2)!(-1)^{j}}{(k-s+1)!j!(s-j+1)!}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+j}.

On the other hand,

b_{n,n-k-2}-b_{n+1,n-k-1}=\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k-1}-\delta^{(1)}_{n+1}\delta^{(1)}_{n}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k+2)!}.

(53)

Substituting (
52
) and (
53
) in (
51
),

\displaystyle\alpha_{n,n-k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{s=0}^{k}\left(\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k+2)!}\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\frac{(k+2)!(-1)^{j}}{(k-s+1)!j!(s-j+1)!}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+j}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k+2)!}\left(\delta^{(1)}_{n-k-1}-\delta^{(1)}_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k+2)!}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\binom{k+2}{s+1}\binom{s+1}{j}(-1)^{j+1}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+j}+\delta^{(1)}_{n-k-1}-\delta^{(1)}_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k+2)!}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k+2}\binom{k+2}{r}\delta^{(1)}_{n-k+r-1}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom{r}{\ell}(-1)^{r-\ell+1}+\delta^{(1)}_{n-k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k+2)!}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k+2}\binom{k+2}{r}(-1)^{r}\delta^{(1)}_{n-k+r-1}+\delta^{(1)}_{n-k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-k}}{(k+2)!}\sum_{r=0}^{k+2}\binom{k+2}{r}(-1)^{r}\delta^{(1)}_{n-k+r-1},

which is (
46
) for

s=k+1
. With this, (
46
) is verified for

k=0,1,\ldots,N-1.

The next step is to prove (
47
), from which (
45
) will be a consequence. Due to (
48
)-(
49
) and the fact that

\alpha_{n,i}=0

for

i<0
,
the recurrence relation (
45
) is obviously fulfilled for

n\leq N-1
.
Furthermore, for

n\geq N
and

i=n-N+1,\,n-N+2,\,\ldots,n,

the relation (
47
) is obvious from (
50
). Then, in the rest of the proof we assume

n\geq N,\,i\in\{0,\,1,\,\ldots,n-N\}.

That is,

i=n-N-k,\,k=0,1,\ldots,n-N.

On the left hand side of (
47
), using (
46
) and (
44
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n,n-s}b_{n-s,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\frac{\delta^{(1)}_{n}\delta^{(1)}_{n-1}\ldots\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+1}}{(s+1)!}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\binom{s+1}{j}(-1)^{j}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+j}\right)\frac{\delta_{n-s}^{(1)}\delta_{n-s-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{i+1}^{(1)}}{(n-s-i)!}

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{i+1}^{(1)}}{(n-i+1)!}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\binom{n-i+1}{s+1}\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\binom{s+1}{j}(-1)^{j}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{i+1}^{(1)}}{(n-i+1)!}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{N}\sum_{j=s}^{N}\binom{n-i+1}{j}\binom{j}{j-s}(-1)^{j-s}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+1}-\delta^{(1)}_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{i+1}^{(1)}}{(n-i+1)!}\sum_{s=0}^{N}\sum_{j=s}^{N}\binom{n-i+1}{j}\binom{j}{s}(-1)^{j-s}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+1}-b_{n+1,i}.

Now, it is easy to see

\displaystyle\sum_{j=s}^{N}\binom{n-i+1}{j}\binom{j}{s}(-1)^{j-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{n-i+1}{s}\sum_{j=s}^{N}\binom{n-s-i+1}{j-s}(-1)^{j-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{n-i+1}{s}\sum_{r=0}^{N-s}\binom{n-s-i+1}{r}(-1)^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{n-i+1}{s}\binom{n-s-i}{n-N-i}(-1)^{N-s},

where the last equality follows from (
8
).

Hence, substituting

i=n-N-k
,
in (
54
) and combining it with the above results, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n,n-s}b_{n-s,i}+b_{n+1,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{n-N-k+1}^{(1)}}{(N+k+1)!}\sum_{s=0}^{N}\binom{N+k+1}{s}\binom{N+k-s}{k}(-1)^{N-s}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+1}

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{n-N-k+1}^{(1)}}{(N+k+1)!}\binom{N+k+1}{k}\sum_{s=0}^{N}\frac{N+1}{N+k-s+1}\binom{N}{s}(-1)^{N-s}\delta^{(1)}_{n-s+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{n-N-k+1}^{(1)}}{(N+k+1)!}\binom{N+k+1}{k}\sum_{s=1}^{N+1}\frac{s}{s+k}\binom{N+1}{s}(-1)^{s+1}\delta^{(1)}_{n-N+s},

where the last equality follows from performing the change of variable
s=N-s+1
.

Now, if we take into account (
43
),

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{N+1}\frac{s}{s+k}\binom{N+1}{s}(-1)^{s+1}\delta^{(1)}_{n-N+s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{N+1}\frac{s}{s+k}\binom{N+1}{s}(-1)^{s+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-N+s}\binom{n-N+s}{j}j!c_{j}

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}j!c_{j}\sum_{s=1}^{N+1}\frac{s}{s+k}\binom{N+1}{s}\binom{n-N+s}{j}(-1)^{s+1}.

(We note that

c_{N+1}=0
and
n\geq N

are used in (
56
).)

Applying Lemma
4
in (
56
) (with
m=N
),

\binom{N+k+1}{k}\sum_{s=1}^{N+1}\frac{s}{s+k}\binom{N+1}{s}(-1)^{s+1}\delta^{(1)}_{n-N+s}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\binom{n-N-k}{j}j!c_{j}.

(57)

In (
57
) we have two possibilities. First, if

n-N-k\leq N

then

\displaystyle\binom{n-N-k}{j}=0

for

j=n-N-k+1,\ldots,N.

Second, if

n-N-k>N

then

c_{j}=0

for
j=N+1,\ldots,n-N-K
. In both cases, (
43
) leads to

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\binom{n-N-k}{j}j!c_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-N-k}\binom{n-N-k}{j}j!c_{j}=\delta^{(1)}_{n-N-k}.

From this and (
55
),

\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n,n-s}b_{n-s,i}+b_{n+1,i}=\frac{\delta_{n}^{(1)}\delta_{n-1}^{(1)}\ldots\delta_{n-N-k+1}^{(1)}\delta_{n-N-k}^{(1)}}{(N+k+1)!}=b_{n,i-1},

as we wanted to prove.

∎

5
Inverse problem

In this section, we assume the sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
and
the sequence of monic polynomials
\{P_{n}\},n\in\mathbb{N}
, are given,
where

\deg(P_{n})=n

for each

n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, we define a sequence
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\},n\in\mathbb{N},
as

\delta_{n}^{(0)}=\lambda_{n},\quad n=0,1,\dots,

(58)

for
k=1,2,\dots,n
,

\delta_{n}^{(k)}=(-1)^{k}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccccc}(\lambda_{n-k}-\lambda_{n-k+1})b_{n-k+1,n-k}&(\lambda_{n-k}-\lambda_{n-k+2})b_{n-k+2,n-k}&\cdots&\cdots&(\lambda_{n-k}-\lambda_{n})b_{n,n-k}\\
1&b_{n-k+2,n-k+1}&\cdots&\cdots&b_{n,n-k+1}\\
0&1&\cdots&\cdots&b_{n,n-k+2}&\\
\vdots&0&\ddots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&b_{n,n-1}\end{array}\right|,

(59)

and, for
k>n
,
\delta_{n}^{(k)}=0
.

Now, we define a sequence of polynomials
\{a_{n}\},n\in\mathbb{N}
, as in (
24
) where

a_{n,n-k}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i=k}^{n}{n\choose i}(-1)^{n-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}\quad\text{ for }k=0,1,\ldots,n\,.

(60)

Finally, consider the infinite order differential operator

L=\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}a_{i}(x)\partial_{x}^{i}

(61)

In the following result, we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the sequences
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{P_{n}\}
to be the eigenvalues and eigenpolynomials of a Bochner differential operator defined as in (
1
).

Theorem 6
.

Let
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{P_{n}\}
be two sequences in the above conditions. Consider the sequences
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
and
\{a_{n}\}
defined in terms of
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{P_{n}\}
as in (
59
) and (
60
), respectively. Consider the infinite differential operator
L
defined in terms of
\{a_{n}\}
as in (
61
). Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

a_{i}(x)=0\,,\forall i>N
, and the sequences
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{P_{n}\}
satisfy (
2
) for
L
.

2.

(
32
) is fulfilled for
N
.

Proof.

Theorems
1
and
4
prove
1\Rightarrow 2
. Then, let us prove
2\Rightarrow 1
.

First, let us prove the operator
L
has order
N
. That is, we have to prove
a_{n,n-k}=0
for any
n\geq N+1
and
0\leq k\leq n
. Then, let
n\geq N+1
and let
0\leq k\leq n
. From (
60
),

n!a_{n,n-k}=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=k}^{N}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}+\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}.

Since we are assuming (
32
) holds for
N
, we can use it for the above equation and obtain

n!a_{n,n-k}=\sum_{i=k}^{N}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\delta_{i}^{(k)}+\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\sum_{\ell=k}^{N}(-1)^{N-\ell}\binom{i}{\ell}\binom{i-\ell-1}{N-\ell}\delta_{\ell}^{(k)}.

The coefficient of
\delta_{\ell}^{(k)}
in the right hand side of
the above expression is

\displaystyle\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^{n-\ell}+\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i}(-1)^{N-\ell}\binom{i}{\ell}\binom{i-\ell-1}{N-\ell}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-\ell}\frac{n!}{\ell!}\left[\frac{1}{(n-\ell)!}+\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}(-1)^{N-i}\frac{1}{i!(n-i)!}\frac{i!}{(i-\ell)!}\frac{(i-\ell-1)!}{(N-\ell)!(i-N-1)!}\right]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-\ell}\frac{n!}{\ell!}\left[\frac{1}{(n-\ell)!}+\frac{1}{(N-\ell)!}\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{N-i}}{(i-\ell)(n-i)!(i-N-1)!}\right].

(62)

Let us consider now the sum
\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{N-i}}{(i-\ell)(n-i)!(i-N-1)!}
in (
62
). If we perform the change of variable
j=i-(N+1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{N-i}}{(i-\ell)(n-i)!(i-N-1)!}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-N-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{(j+N-\ell+1)(n-j-N-1)!j!}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(n-N-1)!}\sum_{j=0}^{n-N-1}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j+N-\ell+1}\binom{n-N-1}{j}=\frac{-1}{(n-N-1)!}\frac{1}{(n-N)\binom{n-\ell}{n-N}}=-\frac{(N-\ell)!}{(n-\ell)!},

(63)

where the next-to-last equality follows from Lemma
2
. Replacing (
63
) in (
62
), we obtain
a_{n,n-k}=0
.

Now, we need to prove that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{P_{n}\}
are the eigenvalues and eigenpolynomials of the operator
L
defined in terms of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
. To do that, we need to check that (
26
) holds. Since
b_{n,m+k}=0
if
m+k>n
, (
26
) is equivalent to

\sum_{k=0}^{n-m}\delta_{m+k}^{(k)}b_{n,m+k}=\lambda_{n}b_{n,m},\quad m=0,1,\dots,n.

(64)

Let us prove (
64
) using induction on
n
. For
n=1
,
m
takes values 0 and 1. For
m=0
, (
64
) is equivalent to
\delta_{1}^{(1)}=\lambda_{1}b_{1,0}
, which is true due to (
59
); for
m=1
, (
64
) is
\delta_{1}^{(0)}=\lambda_{1}
, which follows from (
58
).

Let
n\in\mathbb{N},\ n>1
, and assume that (
64
) takes place for
m=0,1,\dots,n
. Replacing
n
by
n+1
, (
64
) becomes

\sum_{k=0}^{n-m+1}\delta_{m+k}^{(k)}b_{n+1,m+k}=\lambda_{n+1}b_{n+1,m},\quad m=0,1,\dots,n.

(65)

Let us prove (
65
). For
m=n+1
, (
65
) is

\delta_{n+1}^{(0)}=\lambda_{n+1},

which is a direct consequence of (
58
).

For
m=0,\dots,n
, let
s=n-m
. Then, we can write (
65
) as

\sum_{k=0}^{s+1}\delta_{n-s+k}^{(k)}b_{n+1,n-s+k}=\lambda_{n+1}b_{n+1,n-s},\quad s=0,1,\dots,n.

(66)

Following (
59
), we can expand the determinant
\delta_{n+1}^{(s+1)}
along its last column and, using the induction hypothesis, we reach

\delta_{n+1}^{(s+1)}=(\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n-s})b_{n+1,n-s}-\sum_{k=1}^{s}\delta_{n+k-s}^{(k)}b_{n+1,n+k-s},

which is (
66
).

∎

6
Conclusions and future work

We have introduced a sequence
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
associated to a Bochner differential operator. Some properties of
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
have been proven, including a (simplified) expression for the coefficients of the eigenpolynomials of a Bochner differential operator in terms of the elements of the sequences
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
and an expression for

\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}

obtained from the
N-k+1
first terms

\delta_{k}^{(k)},\,\delta_{k+1}^{(k)},\ldots,\delta_{N}^{(k)}
. Both properties combined lead to an expression for the coefficients of the eigenpolynomials in terms of a smaller amount of elements of the sequences
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
. In addition, the inverse problem has been studied, resulting in the derivation of a necessary and sufficient condition (that depends on
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
) for its solution.
Furthermore, we have used some properties of
\{\delta_{n}^{(k)}\}
to prove that the Bochner differential operator (
41
) gives rise to sequences of eigenpolynomials that satisfy an
(N+1)
-term recurrence relation. This approach is part of a larger goal: proving
[
4
, Conjecture 1.10]
. To achieve this, two steps remain. First, we must prove that (
42
) also leads to sequences of eigenpolynomials that satisfy an
(N+1)
-term recurrence relation; second, we must prove that no other Bochner differential operator with that property exists. We hope to address this goal in a future work.

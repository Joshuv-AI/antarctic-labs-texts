Universal property of graph cobordisms
Andrea Bianchi and Adela YiYu Zhang

Abstract
. We exhibit the symmetric monoidal ∞ \infty -category GrCob \mathrm{GrCob} of graph cobordisms between spaces as a full ∞ \infty -subcategory of the ∞ \infty -category Psh ⁡ ( Gr ) \mathrm{Psh}(\mathrm{Gr}) of presheaves over Gr \mathrm{Gr} , where Gr \mathrm{Gr} is the symmetric monoidal ∞ \infty -category of graph cobordisms between finite sets. We also describe a universal property for GrCob \mathrm{GrCob} : it is, in a suitable sense, the free symmetric monoidal extension of Gr \mathrm{Gr} endowed with the factorization homology ∫ X ∗ \int_{X}* of the universal 𝐄 ∞ {\mathbf{E}_{\infty}} -Frobenius algebra for all spaces X X . As a corollary, we identify the space of universal natural operations on the factorization homologies, in particular the Hochschild homology, of 𝐄 ∞ {\mathbf{E}_{\infty}} -Frobenius algebras.



1.
Introduction

The symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathrm{GrCob}
of
graph cobordisms between spaces
was introduced in
[
Bia25a
]
as a convenient source for string topology operations. More precisely, for any
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-ring spectrum
R
and any
R
-oriented Poincaré duality space
M
, the first named author costructs a “graph field theory”, i.e. a symmetric monoidal
R
-linear functor
\mathrm{GFT}_{M}
from a suitable
R
-linearisation of
\mathrm{GrCob}^{\mathrm{op}}
to the
R
-linear
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}(\mathrm{Sp})
of
R
-modules in spectra. Evaluation at suitable morphisms in
\mathrm{GrCob}^{\mathrm{op}}
recovers the most basic homotopy invariant string topology operations, in particularly the Chas–Sullivan product.

The construction of
\mathrm{GrCob}
from
[
Bia25a
]
is quite involved. One of the main ingredients is the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Gr}
of
graph cobordisms between finite sets
, which can be characterised as the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category corepresenting
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebras, thanks to a recent result of Barkan–Steinebrunner
[
BS
]
; see later Theorem
2.3
. Here an
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebra in a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category is an
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-algebra
\pazocal{A}
together with a map
\lambda:\pazocal{A}\to\mathbf{1}
to the monoidal unit, such that postcomposing with the multiplication yields a nondegenerate pairing
\pazocal{A}\otimes\pazocal{A}\xrightarrow{\mu}\pazocal{A}\xrightarrow{\lambda}\mathbf{1}
.
1
1

1

This notion was introduced as a
Frobenius algebra object
in
[
Lur17
, 4.6.5.1]
.
It turns out that
\mathrm{Gr}
is equivalent to a symmetric monoidal full
\infty
-subcategory of
\mathrm{GrCob}

[
Bia25a
, Corollary 7.11]
; nevertheless, no “extension” of the universal property of
\mathrm{Gr}
to a universal property of
\mathrm{GrCob}
was provided, and even less employed, in loc.cit.. The graph field theory
\mathrm{GFT}_{M}
was rather constructed by using the universal property of
\mathrm{Gr}
, together with a series of ad hoc and somewhat involved manipulations.

The first goal of this article is to provide a universal property for
\mathrm{GrCob}
in terms of factorization homology over arbitrary spaces of the
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebra corresponding to the inclusion
\mathrm{Gr}\to\mathrm{GrCob}
. The approach we take is inspired by and parallel to the work of Barkan, Steinebrunner and the second named author
[
BSZ25
]
, where a universal property of the open-closed 2-dimensional cobordism category
\pazocal{OC}
was established in terms of Hochschild homology of
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-Frobenius algebra, and many of the proofs in this article make use of the technical results established therein.

In order to formulate this universal property, we need the following definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

For a space
X\in\pazocal{S}
, we abbreviate by
\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}
the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Fin}\times{S}\pazocal{S}_{/X}
, where the pullback is formed along the inclusion
\mathrm{Fin}\hookrightarrow\pazocal{S}
and the source functor
\pazocal{S}_{/X}\to\pazocal{S}
. For an arbitrary functor
F\colon\mathrm{Fin}\to\pazocal{V}
, we denote by
\int_{X}F
the following colimit, if it exists:

\int_{X}F:=\mathrm{colim}_{\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}F\in\pazocal{V}.

In the above formula the restriction of
F
along the source functor
\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}\to\mathrm{Fin}
is implicit. We refer to
\int_{X}F
as the
factorization homology over
X
with coefficients in
F
.

When
F\colon\mathrm{Fin}\to\pazocal{V}
is a symmetric monoidal functor and
X
is a framed
n
-manifold, this definition recovers the usual notion of factorization homology of an
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-algebra considered as an
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebra, see
[
AF20
, Proposition 5.7]
. In this case, the factorization homology
\int_{X}F
also agrees with (the underlying object of) the tensor product “
X\otimes F
”, i.e. the constant colimit
\mathrm{colim}_{X}F
computed in
\mathrm{CAlg}(\pazocal{V})
. Most notably, when
X=S^{1}
, this agrees with the Hochschild homology of the underlying
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-algebra; see also
[
NS18
, IV.2.2]
and also Lemma
4.6
below.

We introduce the factorization homology
\int_{X}F
for arbitrary functors out of
\mathrm{Fin}
in order to ease notation later.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that an
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-Frobenius algebra
\pazocal{A}:\mathrm{Gr}\to\pazocal{V}
in a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\pazocal{V}
is
extendable
if the following conditions hold:

•

For all spaces
X\in\pazocal{S}
, the factorization homology
\int_{X}\pazocal{A}
exists in
\pazocal{V}
; here we consider
\mathrm{Fin}
as an
\infty
-subcategory of
\mathrm{Gr}
;

•

For all
X\in\pazocal{S}
and
v\in\pazocal{V}
, the following assembly map is an equivalence:

\int_{X}\left(\pazocal{A}\otimes v\right)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\left(\int_{X}\pazocal{A}\right)\otimes v.

We introduce an
\infty
-subcategory
\pazocal{F}\mathrm{rob}_{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}^{\mathrm{ext}}\subseteq\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat_{\infty}})_{\mathrm{Gr}/}
whose objects are all extendable
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-Frobenius algebras
(\pazocal{V},\pazocal{A})
. A morphism
F\colon(\pazocal{V},\pazocal{A})\to(\pazocal{V}^{\prime},\pazocal{A}^{\prime})
in
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat_{\infty}})_{\mathrm{Gr}/}
between extendable
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebras belongs to
\pazocal{F}\mathrm{rob}_{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}^{\mathrm{ext}}
if, for all
X\in\pazocal{S}
, the following assembly map is an equivalence:

\int_{X}\pazocal{A}^{\prime}\simeq\int_{X}F(\pazocal{A})\to F\left(\int_{X}\pazocal{A}\right).

Theorem A
.

Consider
\mathrm{GrCob}
as an object in
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat_{\infty}})_{\mathrm{Gr}/}
via the inclusion
\iota\colon\mathrm{Gr}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GrCob}
. Then
\mathrm{GrCob}
is extendable and is an initial object in
\pazocal{F}\mathrm{rob}_{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}^{\mathrm{ext}}
.

As an immediate application of Theorem
A
, we obtain the following corollary, providing string operations parametrised by
\mathrm{GrCob}
at the level of factorization homologies of
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebras, and by restriction, at the level of Hochschild homology.

Corollary A.1
.

Let
\pazocal{V}
be a cocomplete symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category whose monoidal product preserves colimits in each variable separately (e.g.
\pazocal{V}
is the
\infty
-category of
R
-modules in spectra for some
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-ring spectrum
R
). Let
\pazocal{A}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\to\pazocal{V}
be an
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebra. Then there is an essentially unique symmetric monoidal extension

\int_{(-)}\pazocal{A}\colon\mathrm{GrCob}\to\pazocal{V}

whose value at any space
X\in\mathrm{GrCob}
is equivalent to the factorization homology
\int_{X}\pazocal{A}
along the canonical map induced by the functor
\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}\to\mathrm{GrCob}_{/X}
. Moreover the underlying functor of
\int_{(-)}\pazocal{A}
is equivalent to the left Kan extension
\iota_{!}\pazocal{A}
of
\pazocal{A}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\to\pazocal{V}
along
\iota\colon\mathrm{Gr}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GrCob}
.

In a similar spirit, Barkan, Steinebrunner and the second named author have provided in
[
BSZ25
]
a universal property for the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\pazocal{OC}
of
open-closed 2-dimensional oriented cobordisms
: while the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\pazocal{O}
of
open 2-dimensional oriented cobordisms
carries the universal
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-Frobenius algebra
\pazocal{A}
, its extension
\pazocal{OC}
is obtained by universally adjoining the factorization homology
\int_{M}\pazocal{A}
over all compact oriented 1-manifolds
M
. Most notably, taking
M=S^{1}
, we are also universally adjoining the Hochschild homology
\int_{S^{1}}\pazocal{A}
of
\pazocal{A}
.

3.
Canonical extensions and the universal property of
\mathrm{GrCob}

In this section, we show that any extendable
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebra admits a canonical symmetric monoidal extension to
\mathrm{GrCob}
. As a consequence, we deduce the universal property of
\mathrm{GrCob}
stated in Theorem
A
, which says that
\mathrm{GrCob}
is the initial extendable
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebra.

3.1.
Canonical extensions of
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebras

Given a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\pazocal{V}
and an
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebra
\pazocal{A}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\to\pazocal{V}
, it is natural to ask whether
\pazocal{A}
admits an extension to a “graph field theory”, i.e. a symmetric monoidal functor
F:\mathrm{GrCob}\to\pazocal{V}
such that the restriction of
F
to
\mathrm{Gr}
is
\pazocal{A}
.
A good candidate for this extension is the
operadic left Kan extension
of
\pazocal{A}
along the inclusion
\iota:\mathrm{Gr}\to\mathrm{GrCob}
. We take this notion from
[
Lur17
, Section 3.1]
, and refer to
[
BSZ25
, Theorem 7.6]
for an alternative account that specializes to the case of interest and provides in particular the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of an operadic left Kan extension.

Definition 3.1
.

An operadic left Kan extension of a lax symmetric monoidal functor
F:\pazocal{C}\to\pazocal{V}
along a lax symmetric monoidal functor
i:\pazocal{C}\to\pazocal{D}
is a pair
(G,\alpha)
of a lax symmetric monoidal functor
G\colon\pazocal{D}\to\pazocal{V}
and a symmetric monoidal natural transformation
\alpha\colon F\to G\circ i
such that for all
v\in\pazocal{V}
, the transformation induced by
\alpha
exhibits
G(-)\otimes v\colon\pazocal{D}\to\pazocal{V}
as the plain left Kan extension of
F(-)\otimes v\colon\pazocal{C}\to\pazocal{V}
along
i
. In particular, for
v
the monoidal unit, the underlying functor of
G
is exhibited as the
left Kan extension of
F
along
i
.

Notation 3.2
.

In the notation of Definition
3.1
, we usually denote by
i_{!}^{\otimes}F\simeq G\colon\pazocal{D}\to\pazocal{V}
the operadic left Kan extension, which is essentially unique, if it exists. We reserve the notation
i_{!}F\colon\pazocal{D}\to\pazocal{V}
for the (plain) left Kan extension of
F
along
i
, whose definition does not use any (lax) symmetric monoidal structures.

A necessary and sufficient condition to ensure that an operadic left Kan extension
i_{!}^{\otimes}F
exists is that both of the following hold:

•

the left Kan extension
i_{!}(F(-)\otimes v)
exists for all
v\in\pazocal{V}
;

•

the assembly map
i_{!}(F(-)\otimes v)\to i_{!}F(-)\otimes v
is a natural equivalence.

Remark 3.3
.

In some situations, e.g. when
\pazocal{V}
is cocomplete and its monoidal product preserves colimits separately in each variable, then
all
lax symmetric monoidal functors
\pazocal{C}\to\pazocal{V}
admit an operadic left Kan extension
\pazocal{D}\to\pazocal{V}
. In this case, the operadic left Kan extension provides a left adjoint to the restriction functor
i^{*}:\mathrm{Fun}^{\otimes,\mathrm{lax}}(\pazocal{D},\pazocal{V})\to\mathrm{Fun}^{\otimes,\mathrm{lax}}(\pazocal{C},\pazocal{V})
: see
[
Lur17
, Section 3.1]
, or
[
CH20
, Theorem 1.1]
for an alternative exposition. In general, however, the notion of operadic left Kan extension is different.

Since
\iota\colon\mathrm{Gr}\to\mathrm{GrCob}
is fully faithful, the operadic left Kan extension
\iota_{!}\pazocal{A}
of a symmetric monoidal functor
\pazocal{A}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\to\pazocal{V}
, if it exists, restricts indeed to
\pazocal{A}
on
\mathrm{Gr}
. We will show in the following proposition that its lax symmetric monoidal structure is in fact a (strong) symmetric monoidal structure, and we give a simplified formula for its pointwise values. Then we will deduce Theorem
A
as an immediate corollary.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(\pazocal{V},\pazocal{A})\in\pazocal{F}\mathrm{rob}_{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}^{\mathrm{ext}}
as in Definition
1.2
, which implies that the operadic left Kan extension
i_{!}^{\otimes}\pazocal{A}
exists. Then the lax symmetric monoidal structure on
\iota^{\otimes}_{!}\pazocal{A}
is in fact a strong symmetric monoidal structure.

We start by establishing some computational inputs to Proposition
3.4
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any space
X
the canonical map

\int_{X}\iota:=\mathrm{colim}(\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}\to\mathrm{Fin}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathrm{GrCob})\longrightarrow X

is an equivalence in
\mathrm{GrCob}
; in particular the colimit defining the source exists.

Proof.

For
Y\in\mathrm{GrCob}
we want to show that
\mathrm{GrCob}(X,Y)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\lim_{A\in(\mathrm{Fin}_{/X})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{GrCob}(A,Y)
is an equivalence of spaces. Both spaces map to
\mathrm{GrCob}(\emptyset,Y)
, using that
\emptyset
is initial in
\pazocal{S}
and regarding
\pazocal{S}
as a wide
\infty
-subcategory of
\mathrm{GrCob}
as in
[
Bia25a
, Corollary 7.10]
; therefore it suffices to prove, for all
w\in\mathrm{GrCob}(\emptyset,Y)
, that the induced map between fibres at
w
is an equivalence. The datum of
w
is roughly that of a map of spaces
Y\to W
, plus some information about how
W
is obtained from
Y
by attaching a graph. By
[
Bia25a
, Remark 7.12]
we have an equivalence as follows, induced by the functor denoted “
D_{0}
” in loc.cit.:

\mathrm{fib}_{w}(\mathrm{GrCob}(X,Y)\to\mathrm{GrCob}(\emptyset,Y))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Map}(X,W);

We similarly have an equivalence as follows, suitably compatible with the one above:

\begin{split}\mathrm{fib}_{w}\left(\lim_{A\in(\mathrm{Fin}_{/X})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{GrCob}(A,Y)\to\mathrm{GrCob}(\emptyset,Y)\right)&\simeq\lim_{A\in(\mathrm{Fin}_{/X})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{fib}_{w}(\mathrm{GrCob}(A,Y)\to\mathrm{GrCob}(\emptyset,Y))\\
&\xrightarrow{\simeq}\lim_{A\in(\mathrm{Fin}_{/X)^{\mathrm{op}}}}\mathrm{Map}(A,W)\simeq\mathrm{Map}(X,W),\end{split}

where we use that
X\simeq\mathrm{colim}_{A\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}A
in spaces.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For any space
X
and any finite set
A
the canonical map

\int_{X}(\iota\otimes A)\to X\otimes A

is an equivalence in
\mathrm{GrCob}
; in particular the colimit defining the source exists.

Proof.

We may factor the map as the following composite:

\int_{X}(\iota\otimes A)\to\int_{X\sqcup A}\iota\to X\otimes A.

The second arrow is an equivalence by Lemma
3.5
, and in particular the middle colimit exists.
The first arrow is an equivalence, and in particular this also implies that the left colimit exists, because the functor
-\sqcup A\colon\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}\to\mathrm{Fin}_{/X\sqcup A}
is final.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

The canonical map

\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}\mathrm{GrCob}(A,B)\to\mathrm{GrCob}(A,X)

is an equivalence for any space
X\in\mathrm{GrCob}
and finite set
A\in\mathrm{Gr}
.

Proof.

The above canonical map can be factored as a sequence of equivalences

\displaystyle\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}\mathrm{GrCob}(A,B)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}\big(\mathrm{colim}_{C\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/B}}\mathrm{Gr}{S}(\emptyset\to A,C\to B)\big)

\displaystyle\xleftarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}\mathrm{Gr}{S}(\emptyset\to A,B=B)

\displaystyle\xleftarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}\mathrm{Gr}{S}(A=A,B=B)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}\mathrm{Gr}{S}(A=A,B\to X)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}\mathrm{Gr}{S}(\emptyset\to A,B\to X)\simeq\mathrm{GrCob}(A,X).

In the above sequence of equivalences we make use of the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Gr}{S}
from
[
Bia25a
]
, of which
\mathrm{GrCob}
is a localisation; we invite the reader to see loc.cit. for further details.
The first and last equivalence follow from the formula for morphism spaces in
\mathrm{GrCob}
from
[
Bia25a
, Corollary 7.8]
; the second from the fact that, since
B
is a finite set, the identity of
B
is terminal in
\mathrm{Fin}_{/B}
; the third and the fourth equivalences follow from the equivalences of morphism spaces

\mathrm{Gr}{S}(\emptyset\to A,B=B)\xleftarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Gr}{S}(A=A,B=B)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Gr}{S}(A=A,B\to X),

induced by precomposition by
(\emptyset\to A)\to(A=A)
and postcomposition by
(B=B)\to(B\to X)
, respectively; it is immediate to check from the definition of
\mathrm{Gr}{S}
that these are indeed equivalences; finally, the fifth equivalence, induced by precomposition by
(\emptyset\to A)\to(A=A)
, follows from
[
Bia25a
, Proposition 7.4]
, i.e. the fact that the localisation
\mathrm{GrCob}
of
\mathrm{Gr}{S}
can be computed by left calculi of fractions.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
X
be a space. Then the following functor is final:

\Phi:\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}=\mathrm{Fin}\times_{\pazocal{S}}\pazocal{S}_{/X}\to\mathrm{Gr}\times_{\mathrm{GrCob}}\mathrm{GrCob}_{/X}.

In defining
\Phi
we are using that
\pazocal{S}
is a wide
\infty
-subcategory of
\mathrm{GrCob}
, and we are identifying
\mathrm{Fin}
with the intersection
\mathrm{Gr}\cap\pazocal{S}
of
\infty
-subcategories of
\mathrm{GrCob}
. We observe that
\Phi
is a full
\infty
-subcategory inclusion, since the wide
\infty
-subcategory

satisfies the 2-of-3 property. Namely,
\pazocal{S}
can be characterised as the wide
\infty
-subcategory of
\mathrm{GrCob}
spanned by graph cobordisms

whose second arrow is an equivalence
[

.

Proof.

The source of
\Phi
is the unstraightening of the restricted Yoneda functor
\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\circ}:\mathrm{GrCob}\to\mathrm{Psh}(\mathrm{GrCob})\to\mathrm{Psh}(\mathrm{Gr})
at

[

is final, it suffices to show that the canonical map

∎

The conditions on

3.2.
Proof of Theorem

4.

In this section we prove Theorem

\mathrm{Gr}
is dense in
\mathrm{GrCob}
and characterizes
\mathrm{GrCob}
as the extension of
\mathrm{Gr}
obtained by formally adjoining all colimits

\int_{X}\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\mathrm{Gr}}(\iota)
to
\mathrm{Gr}
.
As an application, we determine the space of universal natural operations on the factorization homologies of
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}

\mathbf{E}_{1}
-Frobenius algebras from
[

4.1.
Denseness of
\iota:\mathrm{Gr}\to\mathrm{GrCob}

We start by recalling the notion of denseness.

Definition 4.1
.

[
Lur25
, 03VD]

Let
i:\pazocal{D}\to\pazocal{C}
be the inclusion of a full
\infty
-subcategory. We say that
\pazocal{D}
is

in
\pazocal{C}
if for every

, the following is a colimit diagram

(\pazocal{D}\times{C}\pazocal{C}_{/X})^{\rhd}\to\pazocal{C}_{/X}\to\pazocal{C}.

The following lemma provides two alternative characterizations of denseness.

Lemma 4.2
.

A full inclusion
i\colon\pazocal{D}\to\pazocal{C}
is dense if and only if the following two equivalent conditions are satisfied:

(1)

[
Lur25
, 03VE]
The (plain) left Kan extension of
i
along
i
is the identity of
\pazocal{C}
. In particular, the left Kan extension exists.

(2)

[
Lur25
, 03VG]

The following restricted Yoneda embedding is fully faithful:

\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!^{\circ}_{\pazocal{D}}:\pazocal{C}\xlongrightarrow{\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\pazocal{C}}}\mathrm{Psh}(\pazocal{C})\xlongrightarrow{i^{*}}\mathrm{Psh}(\pazocal{D}).

To prove Theorem
B
, we will use the first characterization to check denseness of
\mathrm{Gr}
in
\mathrm{GrCob}
and the second one to characterize
\mathrm{GrCob}
as formally adjoining certain colimits to
\mathrm{Gr}
.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

By Lemma
4.2
.(1), it suffices to check that the left Kan extension
\iota_{!}\iota
of the inclusion
\iota:\mathrm{Gr}\to\mathrm{GrCob}
along
\iota
is the identity on
\mathrm{GrCob}
. It follows from Lemma
3.5
and Corollary
3.8
that this left Kan extension indeed exists. Since the identity
\mathrm{id}:\mathrm{GrCob}\to\mathrm{GrCob}
restricts to the inclusion
\iota:\mathrm{Gr}\to\mathrm{GrCob}
, there is a natural transformation
\iota_{!}\iota\Rightarrow\mathrm{id}
. Furthermore, for any
X\in\mathrm{GrCob}
, this natural transformation evaluates to the canonical map

\iota_{!}\iota(X)=\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Gr}\times_{\mathrm{GrCob}}\mathrm{GrCob}_{/X}}B=\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}\times_{\pazocal{S}}\pazocal{S}_{/X}}B\xrightarrow{\simeq}X.

which again by Lemma
3.5
and Corollary
3.8
is an equivalence; we thus have a natural isomorphism
\iota_{!}\iota\simeq\mathrm{id}
of endofunctors of
\mathrm{GrCob}
as desired.

As a result, the restricted Yoneda embedding
\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!^{\circ}_{\mathrm{Gr}}:\mathrm{GrCob}\to\mathrm{Psh}(\mathrm{Gr})
is fully faithful by Lemma
4.2
(2). Since it restricts to
\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\mathrm{Gr}}
on the full
\infty
-subcategory
\mathrm{Gr}
, we obtain a natural transformation
\iota_{!}\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\mathrm{Gr}}\Rightarrow\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!^{\circ}_{\mathrm{Gr}}
. This evaluates at any space
X\in\mathrm{GrCob}
to the canonical map
\mathrm{colim}_{B\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/X}}\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!^{\circ}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(B)\to\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\mathrm{GrCob}}^{\circ}(X)
, which is an equivalence by Lemma
3.7
; we thus have a natural isomorphism
\iota_{!}\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\mathrm{Gr}}\simeq\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!^{\circ}_{\mathrm{Gr}}
as desired. The identification of the essential image of
\iota_{!}\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\mathrm{Gr}}
follows from Corollary
3.8
.
∎

4.2.
Natural operations on factorization homology of
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebras

Now we turn to the study of natural operations between factorization homologies of an
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-Frobenius algebra.

Definition 4.3
.

A
context for natural operations
is a span of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories
\pazocal{E}\leftarrow\pazocal{F}\to\pazocal{G}
, with
\pazocal{F}
being a small
\infty
-category.
Given a context for natural operations as above and a presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\pazocal{V}
, for any object
X\in\pazocal{G}
we let
I^{\pazocal{E}}_{X}
be the colimit preserving and
\pazocal{V}
-linear functor given by the composite

I^{\pazocal{E}}_{X}\colon\mathrm{Fun}(\pazocal{E},\pazocal{V})\to\mathrm{Fun}(\pazocal{F},\pazocal{V})\to\mathrm{Fun}(\pazocal{F}\times{G}\pazocal{G}_{/X},\pazocal{V})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{colim}}\pazocal{V}.

We define the
\infty
-category of natural operations of
\pazocal{F}
-algebras in
\pazocal{V}
with structures in
\pazocal{E}
and extensions to
\pazocal{G}

\mathrm{Nat}{F}[\pazocal{E},\pazocal{G};\pazocal{V}]\subseteq\mathrm{Fun}^{L,\pazocal{V}}(\mathrm{Fun}(\pazocal{E},\pazocal{V}),\pazocal{V})\simeq\mathrm{Psh}(\pazocal{E};\pazocal{V})

as the full
\infty
-subcategory spanned by
I^{\pazocal{E}}_{X}

as
X
ranges over the objects of
\pazocal{G}
.
When
\pazocal{V}=\pazocal{S}
we omit it from the notation.

Example 4.4
.

The main context for natural operations we will consider are the spans
\pazocal{O}\leftarrow\mathrm{Disk}_{1}\to\mathrm{Mfld}_{1}
and
\mathrm{Gr}\leftarrow\mathrm{Fin}\to\pazocal{S}
. The restrictions of the functor
\epsilon:\pazocal{OC}\to\mathrm{GrCob}
given in (
2
) provide a comparison map between these two contexts for natural operations.

The notion of natural operations was introduced by Wahl in
[
Wah16
]
in the setting
\pazocal{V}=D(\mathbb{Z})
,
\pazocal{F}\simeq\mathrm{Disk}_{1}
and
\pazocal{G}=\mathrm{Mfld}_{1}
, with arbitrary choice of
\pazocal{E}
. In
[
BSZ25
]
, the situation was “lifted” to the setting
\pazocal{V}=\pazocal{S}
, the initial presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category, where the focus is on
\pazocal{E}=\pazocal{O}

4.4
and Theorem
C
, which we will demonstrate, the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Nat}{F}[\pazocal{E},\pazocal{G};\pazocal{V}]
happens to be a
symmetric monoidal

\infty
-subcategory of
\mathrm{Psh}(\pazocal{E};\pazocal{V})
, where the latter is endowed with the Day convolution symmetric monoidal structure.

In order to show that the map of contexts for natural operations from Example
4.4
satisfies the assumption in Lemma
4.5
, we will need the
following well-known result. We were unable to find a precise reference in the literature, so we provide a proof here.
In particular, this implies that the factorization homology of an
{\mathbf{E}_{\infty}}
-algebra
\pazocal{A}
over
S^{1}
agrees with its Hochschild homology, where we consider
\pazocal{A}
as an
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-algebra
[
NS18
, IV.2.2]
[
AF20
, Proposition 5.7]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any compact 1-manifold
M
, the functor

\mathrm{Disk}_{/M}:=\mathrm{Disk}_{1}\times_{\mathrm{Mfld}_{1}}(\mathrm{Mfld}_{1})_{/M}\to\mathrm{Fin}_{/M}

induced by taking path components of disks and regarding 1-manifolds as spaces is final.

Proof.

Since the canonical maps
\mathrm{Disk}_{/M}\times\mathrm{Disk}_{/N}\to\mathrm{Disk}_{/M\sqcup N}
and
\mathrm{Fin}_{/M}\times\mathrm{Fin}_{/N}\to\mathrm{Fin}_{/M\sqcup N}
are equivalences for all compact 1-manifolds
M
and
N
, it suffices to prove the lemma in the two cases
M=D^{1}
and
M=S^{1}
. For
M=D^{1}
, this is clear since both the source and the target have a terminal object given by
D^{1}
. For
M=S^{1}
,
we identify
\mathrm{Fin}_{/S^{1}}
with the 1-category having the following objects and morphisms:

•

An object is a set
A
with free
\mathbb{Z}
-action admitting finitely many orbits;

•

A morphism is a
\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant map.

Similarly,
\mathrm{Disk}_{/S^{1}}
is equivalent to the paracyclic category
\Lambda_{\infty}

[
BSZ25
, Lemma 5.13 and 5.18]
, which can be described as the following 1-category:

•

Objects are totally ordered sets
B
with free
\mathbb{Z}
-action admitting finitely many orbits, such that the map
1.-\colon B\to B
is order-preserving and satisfies
1.b>b
for all
b\in B
;

•

A morphism is a
\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant and weakly increasing map.

To apply Quillen Theorem A, we want to show that for any object
A\in\mathrm{Fin}_{/S^{1}}
, regarded as a
\mathbb{Z}
-set as above, and the
slice category
\pazocal{C}_{A}:=\mathrm{Disk}_{/S^{1}}\times_{\mathrm{Fin}_{/S^{1}}}(\mathrm{Fin}_{/S^{1}})_{A/}
has contractible classifying space. We represent an object in
\pazocal{C}_{A}
as a map of
\mathbb{Z}
-sets
A\to B
, where
B
is endowed with a total order as above. Let
\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A}
denote the full subcategory of
\pazocal{C}_{A}
spanned by surjective maps
A\to B
. Then the inclusion
\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A}\hookrightarrow\pazocal{C}_{A}
admits a right adjoint
R_{A}
, sending
A\to B
to
A\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Im}(A\to B)
, where the image
\mathrm{Im}(A\to B)\subseteq B
is endowed with the restricted total order. In particular
|\pazocal{C}_{A}|\simeq|\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A}|
. We also observe that morphisms in
\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A}
are represented by
surjective

\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant and weakly increasing maps
B\twoheadrightarrow B^{\prime}
under
A
.

We proceed by induction on the number of
\mathbb{Z}
-orbits of
A
. For
A=\emptyset
we observe that
\pazocal{C}_{\emptyset}:=\mathrm{Disk}_{/S^{1}}\times_{\mathrm{Fin}_{/S^{1}}}(\mathrm{Fin}_{/S^{1}})_{\emptyset/}\simeq\mathrm{Disk}_{/S^{1}}
has an initial object
\emptyset
, so
\pazocal{C}_{\emptyset}\simeq\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{\emptyset}\simeq*
as categories.

We next suppose that
|\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A}|\simeq*
for a given
A
, and we consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-set
A^{\prime}:=A\sqcup\mathbb{Z}
. The composite functor

\psi\colon\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\pazocal{C}_{A^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{A\hookrightarrow A^{\prime}}\pazocal{C}_{A}\xrightarrow{R_{A}}\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A}

is a cocartesian fibration. The fibre
\psi^{-1}(A\twoheadrightarrow B)
of
\psi
over an object
A\twoheadrightarrow B
in
\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A}
is the poset described by the following objects and morphisms:

•

there are two types of objects:

(1)

the first type is given by surjections
A^{\prime}=A\sqcup\mathbb{Z}\twoheadrightarrow B
extending the given surjection
A\twoheadrightarrow B
;

(2)

the second type is given by surjections
A^{\prime}=A\sqcup\mathbb{Z}\twoheadrightarrow B\sqcup\mathbb{Z}
given by the coproduct of the given surjection
A\twoheadrightarrow B
and the identity of
\mathbb{Z}
; these objects also carry the information of an extension to
B\sqcup\mathbb{Z}
of the total order on
B
, satisfying the above requirements.

•

a morphism can only go from an object of type (2) to an object of type (1), and it is the unique map under
A^{\prime}
, if it exists.

The poset
\psi^{-1}(A\twoheadrightarrow B)
is readily identified with the poset of subintervals of the totally ordered set
B
of one or two elements, and the partial ordering is giving by inclusion; in particular we have
|\psi^{-1}(A\twoheadrightarrow B)|\simeq*
. Using the inductive hypothesis
|\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A}|\simeq*
, we conclude that also
|\pazocal{C}^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}|\simeq*
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
C
, which we state again using a larger commutative diagram.

Theorem 4.7
.

There is a commutative square of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories, with the left horizontal arrows being equivalences:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pazocal{OC}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Nat}_{\mathrm{Disk}_{1}}[\pazocal{O},\mathrm{Mfld}_{1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Psh}(\pazocal{O})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{GrCob}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Nat}_{\mathrm{Fin}}[\mathrm{Gr},\pazocal{S}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Psh}(\mathrm{Gr}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subseteq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Nat}[\epsilon]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subseteq}

Proof.

The middle vertical map is well-defined by Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.6
, so that the right square automatically commutes.
The top left horizontal arrows is an equivalence by
[
BSZ25
, Corollary 3.6]
, where
M\in\pazocal{OC}
and
I^{\pazocal{O}}_{M}
both correspond to the presheaf
\int_{M}\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\pazocal{O}}(\iota)
. The bottom left horizontal arrow is an equivalence by Theorem
B
:
\mathrm{GrCob}
and
\mathrm{Nat}_{\mathrm{Fin}}[\mathrm{Gr},\pazocal{S}]
are both identified as full subcategories of
\mathrm{Psh}(\mathrm{Gr})
spanned by the same objects and hence equivalent, with
X\in\mathrm{GrCob}
and
I^{\mathrm{Gr}}_{X}
both corresponding to the presheaf
\int_{X}\!\text{\char 135\relax}\!_{\mathrm{Gr}}(\iota)
.

The commutativity of the left square follows from the commutativity of the outer square, which follows from the naturality of the (restricted) Yoneda embedding.
∎

.

Let
\pazocal{V}
be an arbitrary presentably symmetric monoidal

\epsilon{V}\colon\pazocal{OC}{V}\to\mathrm{GrCob}{V}
of
\pazocal{V}

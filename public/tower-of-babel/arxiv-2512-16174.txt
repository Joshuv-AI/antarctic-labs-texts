Maximum Cluster Diameter in Non-Critical Bond Percolation
Kaito Kobayashi

Abstract
In this paper, we study independent (Bernoulli) bond percolation in dimensions d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , focusing on the maximum diameter of finite clusters in the non-critical regime ( p ≠ p c p\neq p_{c} ). We prove that the maximum diameter R n R_{n} satisfies R n / log ⁡ n → ϰ ⁡ ( p ) R_{n}/\log n\to\varkappa(p) almost surely, where ϰ ⁡ ( p ) \varkappa(p) is determined by the exponential decay rate ξ ⁡ ( p ) \xi(p) of P p ( 0 ↔ ∂ B n , | 𝒞 0 | < ∞ ) P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n},|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty) . Furthermore, we establish a large deviation principle for the event { R n > ρ log n } \{R_{n}>\rho\log n\} for ρ > ϰ ⁡ ( p ) \rho>\varkappa(p) . Finally, we consider the asymptotics of the number of vertices in clusters with large diameters.

Introduction

Percolation is a fundamental stochastic model introduced by Broadbent and Hammersley in 1957 to formalize the permeation of liquids and gases through porous media
[
1
]
. In recent years, its applications have expanded across various fields, ranging from phase transitions in mathematical physics to the propagation of viruses; see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. A central feature of the model involves random subgraphs of a given domain, where the connectivity properties change drastically as a density parameter varies. This phenomenon is visually illustrated in Figure
1.5
.

Fundamental questions in percolation theory concern the existence and uniqueness of the infinite cluster, the critical value, and the decay of correlation functions. While these global properties have been extensively studied, the geometric characteristics of finite connected components, particularly their shape and spatial extent, are equally important for understanding the underlying graph structure.

Historically, much interest has focused on the size of finite clusters. The tail behavior of the radius of an open cluster, in the sense of Grimmett (i.e., the event
0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n}
), was first estimated by Hammersley
[
7
]
, and since then, numerous studies have refined these estimates; see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
]
. More complex geometric properties have been thoroughly investigated at the critical value
p_{c}(d)
, where scaling exponents such as the fractal dimension and the chemical distance exponent play a central role; see, e.g.,
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

However, compared to the critical regime, the geometry of clusters in the non-critical regime (
p\neq p_{c}
) is much less understood.
Although the expected size and the tail behavior of finite clusters are well understood, sharp asymptotics for geometric quantities, most notably the maximum diameter of finite clusters measured in the
\ell^{\infty}
metric, remain largely unknown in both the subcritical and supercritical regimes.
This paper addresses this gap.

1.1.
The model

We consider
d\geq 2
bond percolation on the
d
-dimensional integer lattice
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}^{d})
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the set of vertices and
\mathbb{E}^{d}
is the set of edges.
Each edge is declared open with probability
p
and closed with probability
1-p
, independently of the other edges.
The resulting configuration of the bonds is denoted by
\omega\in\Omega
, where
\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{E}^{d}}
is the configuration space.
A bond
b
is called open if
\omega(b)=1
, and closed if
\omega(b)=0
.
The corresponding probability measure on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{E}^{d}}
is denoted by
P_{p}
, and the corresponding expectation operator is denoted by
E_{p}
.
For
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{x}=\mathcal{C}_{x}(\omega)
the cluster of
x
.
We write
x\leftrightarrow y
to denote the event that the vertices
x
and
y
belong to the same cluster.
Throughout this paper, we assume that
p\neq p_{c}
; that is,
p
is strictly different from the critical probability

p_{c}(d):=\sup\{p:\theta(p)=0\},

where
\theta(p)
is the probability that the origin belongs to an infinite cluster.

established the existence of a critical probability
p_{c}
, setting the stage for the proof that
p_{c}(d)\in(0,1)
for all
d\geq 2
, which was later rigorously completed.
This value marks the boundary where the model switches from having no infinite cluster to having exactly one.
In the two-dimensional case, Kesten
[

]
proved that
p_{c}(2)=1/2
.
As illustrated in Figure

(b) and (c), open paths connect the left and right sides of the box when

, whereas no such path exists when

.
Furthermore, Aizenman, Kesten, and Newman
[

proved that the infinite cluster is unique whenever it exists.
Later, an alternative, significantly simpler and more elegant proof of this uniqueness result was provided by Burton and Keane
[

]
.
Although the specific values of
p_{c}(d)
for

remain an important unsolved problem, the field has seen remarkable progress
[

,

.

A key feature of the non-critical regime (
p\neq p_{c}
) is the rapid spatial decay of connectivities within finite clusters.
In the subcritical phase (
p<p_{c}
), the radius of a finite open cluster decays exponentially, a result rigorously established by Menshikov
[
20
]
and Aizenman and Barsky
[
21
]
.
In the supercritical phase (
p>p_{c}
), while a unique infinite cluster exists, the distribution of the size of the remaining finite clusters exhibits similar exponential decay properties
[
22
]
.

The main objective of this paper is to investigate the asymptotic behavior of the maximum diameter of finite clusters.
To make this precise, we introduce the necessary geometric definitions and notation in the following subsection.

1.2.
The maximum diameter of finite open clusters

In this section, we introduce the definitions and notation concerning the diameter of clusters.

For
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we write
x_{i}
for the
i
th coordinate of
x
. We denote by
B_{n}
the box with side length
2n+1
and center at the origin:

B_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\|x\|_{\infty}\leq n\},

where
\|x\|_{\infty}=\max\{|x_{i}|:1\leq i\leq d\}
.
We write
\partial B_{n}
for the boundary of
B_{n}
:

\partial B_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\|x\|_{\infty}=n\}.

As usual,
P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n})
is the probability that there exists an open path joining the origin to some vertex in
\partial B_{n}
; see Figure
1.6
.
We refer to
P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n})
as the one-arm probability.

We define the maximum diameter of finite clusters by

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle:=\max\Bigl(\{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x}(\omega)):x\in B_{n},|\mathcal{C}_{x}(\omega)|<\infty\}\cup\{0\}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\max\Bigl(\{\max_{1\leq i\leq d}D_{i}(\mathcal{C}_{x}(\omega)):x\in B_{n},|\mathcal{C}_{x}(\omega)|<\infty\}\cup\{0\}\Bigr).

(1.1)

where for
i=1,2,\ldots,d
,

D_{i}(\mathcal{C}):=\max_{y\in\mathcal{C}}y_{i}-\min_{y\in\mathcal{C}}y_{i}.

Specifically,
D_{i}
is defined as the maximum width of any cluster
\mathcal{C}
in the
i
th coordinate direction, and the maximum diameter of a finite cluster is defined as the maximum value of
D_{i}
.

We note that the distribution of the diameter plays an essential role throughout the proofs.
To evaluate the asymptotic behavior of
R_{n}
, it is necessary to consider how the one-arm probability decays with respect to distance.
We now state the necessary results concerning the distribution of the diameter.
Since the distribution law fundamentally differs between the subcritical (
p<p_{c}
) and supercritical (
p>p_{c}
) regimes, we treat these two cases separately.

For
0<p<p_{c}
, it is shown in
[
4
, Theorem 6.10]
that

\displaystyle\varphi(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n})\right\},

(1.2)

exists, and satisfies
\varphi(p)>0
if
p\in(0,p_{c})

[
4
, Theorem 6.14]
.
Furthermore, by
[
4
, (6.11)]
, there exists a constant
a>0
, independent of
p
, such that, for all
n
,

\displaystyle P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n})\leq an^{d-1}e^{-n\varphi(p)}.

(1.3)

We now turn to supercritical results.
When
p>p_{c}
, the probability of the origin belonging to the infinite cluster is positive
\bigl(P_{p}(|\mathcal{C}_{0}|=\infty)>0\bigr)
.
Therefore, instead of evaluating the exponential decay of the event
\{0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n}\}
, we must specifically consider the probability under the condition that the origin belongs to a finite cluster.
For
p>p_{c}
, it is shown in
[
4
, Theorem 8.18]
that

\displaystyle\sigma(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n},|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty)\right\}

(1.4)

exists and satisfies
\sigma(p)<\infty
.
Furthermore, there exists a constant
A(p,d)
which is finite for
d\geq 2,0<p<1
, such that, for all
n
,

\displaystyle P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n},|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty)\leq A(p,d)n^{d}e^{-n\sigma(p)}.

(1.5)

In fact, by
[
4
, Theorem 8.21]
, we have that
\sigma(p)>0
if
p>p_{c}
.

For
p\neq p_{c}
, we define
\xi(p)
as follows:

\xi(p):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n},|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty)\right\}.

In particular, in view of (
1.2
) and (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\xi(p)=\begin{cases}\varphi(p),&0<p<p_{c},\\
\sigma(p),&p>p_{c}.\end{cases}

Note that the decay rate of
P_{p}(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_{n},|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty)
matches the decay rate of
P_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})=n)
.
Moreover, the identity below was proved in
[
4
, (8.37)]
:

\displaystyle\xi(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{p}\bigl(\textnormal{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})=n\bigr)\right\}>0.

(1.6)

We note that

P_{p}\bigl(\textnormal{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\geq n,|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty\bigr)=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}P_{p}\bigl(\textnormal{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})=m\bigr),

so that the limit
\xi(p)
in (
1.6
) satisfies

\displaystyle\xi(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{p}\bigl(\textnormal{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\geq n,|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty\bigr)\right\}.

(1.7)

Thus, by (
1.3
) and (
1.5
), there exists a constant
L
for
p\neq p_{c}
, such that

\displaystyle P_{p}\bigl(\textnormal{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\geq n,|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty\bigr)\leq Ln^{d}e^{-n\xi(p)}.

(1.8)

Furthermore, we define the constant
\varkappa(p)
as follows:

\varkappa(p):=\frac{d}{\xi(p)}.

We are now able to state our results.

2
Main Results

The next theorem shows the typical size of
R_{n}
in more detail and establishes almost sure convergence.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
p\neq p_{c}
,

\displaystyle\frac{R_{n}}{\log n}\to\varkappa(p)\qquad\text{a.s.}

We now define and discuss the boundary conditions for
R_{n}
.
In (
1.2.
), we have taken the maximum diameter under the free boundary condition, so that we can write
R_{n}=R_{n}^{(\mathrm{fb})}
.
Alternatively, we consider
R_{n}
under the zero boundary condition, i.e.,

R_{n}^{(\mathrm{zb})}
is defined by restricting the configuration
\omega
to the box
B_{n}
.

We define the restriction
\omega_{B_{n}}
of
\omega
to
B_{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle\omega_{B_{n}}(b)=\begin{cases}\omega(b)&\text{if }b\subset B_{n},\\[6.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

That is,
\omega_{B_{n}}
is the configuration where all bonds existing outside
B_{n}
are forced to be closed.
By introducing this restriction, it becomes possible to capture local geometric features, such as cluster diameter and size, within the finite region
B_{n}
.
We then define
R_{n}^{(\mathrm{zb})}
, the maximum cluster diameter under this zero boundary condition, as

R_{n}^{(\mathrm{zb})}:=\max\Bigl(\{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x}(\omega_{B_{n}})):x\in B_{n},|\mathcal{C}_{x}(\omega)|<\infty\}\cup\{0\}\Bigr).

We restrict to vertices that belong to finite clusters in
\omega
in order to exclude contributions from the infinite cluster in the supercritical regime.
Figure
2.1
illustrates the distinction between
R_{n}^{(\mathrm{fb})}
and
R_{n}^{(\mathrm{zb})}
.
Despite this difference, the following theorem shows that they are asymptotically equivalent.

Figure 2.1 :
Visualizing the difference between
R_{n}^{(\mathrm{fb})}
and
R_{n}^{(\mathrm{zb})}
. The probability that they differ tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
(Theorem
2.2
).

Theorem 2.2
.

For
p\neq p_{c}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{p}\bigl(R_{n}^{(\mathrm{zb})}\neq R_{n}^{(\mathrm{fb})}\bigr)=0.

Theorem
2.1
shows that
R^{({\mathrm{fb}})}_{n}/\log n
converges to
\varkappa(p)
almost surely as
n\to\infty
. The next theorem gives a large deviation principle describing the probability that
R_{n}
deviates from its typical value.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
p\neq p_{c}
and
\rho>\varkappa(p)
, the limit

\varsigma(\rho)=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{\log n}\log P_{p}\big(R_{n}>\rho\log n\big),

exists and satisfies
\varsigma(\rho)>0
.
Furthermore,
\varsigma(\rho)
is given by

\varsigma(\rho)=\xi(p)\rho-d.

Finally, our result connects the geometric characteristics established by Theorem
2.3
to the spatial extent of these rare events. The following theorem describes the asymptotic behavior of the number of vertices belonging to clusters whose diameter exceeds
\rho\log n\;(\rho<\varkappa(p))
, using the exponent
d-\xi(p)\rho
appearing in the large deviation principle.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
p\neq p_{c}
. For any
0<\rho<\varkappa(p)
, let
S_{n}(\rho)
be the number of vertices in
B_{n}
that belong to a finite cluster whose diameter exceeds
\rho\log n
, defined as

S_{n}(\rho):=|\{x\in B_{n}:~\rho\log n<\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})<\infty\}|.

Then, the following holds:

\frac{S_{n}(\rho)}{E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)]}\xrightarrow{P_{p}}1\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Furthermore, for any
\varepsilon>0
, when
n
is sufficiently large,

n^{d-\xi(p)\rho-\varepsilon}\leq E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)]\leq n^{d-\xi(p)\rho+\varepsilon}.

2.1.
Related work

The asymptotic properties of the maximal volume of finite clusters in the non-critical regime were investigated by van der Hofstad and Redig
[
24
]
, who studied the typical behavior of the maximum cluster volume within a finite box and established its logarithmic scaling.
In this work, we extend their framework to analyze the diameter of clusters, and this extension is a key ingredient in the proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
.
Although the definition of diameter differs from that of the volume, the underlying probabilistic structure regarding the exponential decay allows us to adapt the techniques of
[
24
]
. In addition, we establish a large deviation principle for the diameter and the asymptotics of the number of vertices in clusters with large diameters.

We also remark that our results may be extended to diameters measured in the chemical (graph) distance, rather than the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm used here. This extension is not immediate, but it is supported by the recent work of Dembin and Nakajima
[
23
]
, which established the existence of a specific rate function for the chemical distance. Their result indicates that, although additional work is required, one can expect that the maximal chemical diameter among finite clusters is characterized by this rate function.

3
Proofs

In this section, we prove the main results stated in Section 2.

3.1.
Preliminaries

This section provides the necessary lemmas for the proofs.

Lemma 3.1

(Borel–Cantelli)
.

Let
\{E_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of events. If
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(E_{n})<\infty
,
then

P\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_{n}\right)=0.

Lemma 3.2

(Boole’s inequality)
.

For any sequence of events
\{A_{i}\}
, we have

P\left(\bigcup_{i}A_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i}P(A_{i}).

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
and
2.2

We adapt the methods used by van der Hofstad and Redig
[
24
, Theorems 1.3-1.5]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The main ingredient is the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\kappa>0
such that as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\left|\frac{R_{n}}{\log n}-\varkappa(p)\right|>\varepsilon\right)<n^{-\kappa}.

Before proving Lemma
3.3
, we first complete the proof of Theorem
2.1
assuming Lemma
3.3
.

Let
n_{k}=2^{k}
.
As a consequence of Lemma
3.3
, and the fact that for every

\kappa>0
,

n^{-\kappa}_{k}=2^{-\kappa k}

is summable in
k
, so that

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{R_{n_{k}}}{\log n_{k}}-\varkappa(p)\right|>\varepsilon\ \right)=0

by Lemma
3.1
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{R_{n_{k}}}{\log n_{k}}\to\varkappa(p)\qquad\text{a.s.}

Moreover,
n\mapsto R_{n}
is non-decreasing with probability
1
.
Therefore, for any
n_{k}<n\leq n_{k+1}
we can bound

\displaystyle\frac{R_{n_{k}}}{\log(n_{k})}\frac{\log(n_{k})}{\log(n_{k+1})}\leq\frac{R_{n}}{\log n}\leq\frac{R_{n_{k+1}}}{\log(n_{k+1})}\frac{\log(n_{k+1})}{\log(n_{k})}.

(3.1)

As
n\to\infty
, also
n_{k},n_{k+1}\to\infty
.
Thus,
\frac{R_{n_{k}}}{\log(n_{k})}
and
\frac{R_{n_{k+1}}}{\log(n_{k+1})}
converge to
\varkappa(p)
with probability
1
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log(n_{k+1})}{\log(n_{k})}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log(n_{k})}{\log(n_{k+1})}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{k+1}{k}=1,

so that both upper and lower bounds in (
3.1
) converge to
\varkappa(p)
almost surely.
This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\varepsilon\in(0,\varkappa(p))
. We set
\delta=\frac{\xi(p)\,\varepsilon}{2(\varkappa(p)+\varepsilon)}
and
\kappa^{\prime}=\xi(p)\varepsilon/4>0
, using (
1.7
) and
\xi(p)=d/\varkappa(p)
.
Then, the following holds:

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})}{\log n}>\varkappa(p)+\varepsilon\,;\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})<\infty\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-(\xi(p)-\delta)(\varkappa(p)+\varepsilon)\log n\right\}

\displaystyle=n^{-(d+\xi(p)\varepsilon-\delta(\varkappa(p)+\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle\leq n^{-d-\kappa^{\prime}}.

(3.2)

Moreover, setting
\delta^{\prime}=\frac{\xi(p)\varepsilon}{2(\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)}
and using the same
\kappa^{\prime}=\xi(p)\varepsilon/4>0
, for sufficiently large
n
, the following holds:

\displaystyle P_{p}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})}{\log n}\notin(\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon,2\varkappa(p)]\,;\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})<\infty\right)

\displaystyle\leq P_{p}(|\mathcal{C}_{0}|<\infty)-P_{p}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})}{\log n}>\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon\,;\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})<\infty\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{= P_p(|\mathcal{C}_0|<\infty)\;}+P_{p}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})}{\log n}>2\varkappa(p)\,;\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})<\infty\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\exp\!\left\{-\left(\xi(p)+\delta^{\prime}\right)\left(\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon\right)\log n\right\}+n^{-d-\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}\,n^{-d+2\kappa^{\prime}}.

(3.3)

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,\varkappa(p))
. We will prove that there exists
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\log n}>\varkappa(p)+\varepsilon\right)\leq n^{-\kappa},

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\log n}<\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon\right)\leq n^{-\kappa}.

(3.5)

To prove (
3.4
), by Lemma
3.2
and (
3.2.
),

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\log n}>\varkappa(p)+\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=P_{p}\left(\bigcup_{x\in B_{n}}\bigl\{(\varkappa(p)+\varepsilon)(\log n)<\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})<\infty\bigr\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in B_{n}}P_{p}\bigl((\varkappa(p)+\varepsilon)(\log n)<\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})<\infty\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq|B_{n}|n^{-d-\kappa^{\prime}}.

We can estimate the right-hand side as follows for sufficiently large
n
:

\displaystyle\left({2n+1}\right)^{d}n^{-d-\kappa^{\prime}}\leq 3^{d}n^{-\kappa^{\prime}}\leq n^{-\kappa^{\prime}/2}.

Thus, setting
\kappa=\kappa^{\prime}/2
, we obtain (
3.4
).

To prove (
3.5
), let
\varepsilon\in(0,\varkappa(p))
,

A_{n}=(K_{n}\mathbb{Z})^{d}\cap B_{n},

and

K_{n}=\lceil 8\varkappa(p)\log n\rceil.

We use the fact that the events

\bigl\{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})\notin((\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n,2\varkappa(p)\log n]\bigr\}_{x\in A_{n}}

are independent, since the event
\{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})\in((\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n,2\varkappa(p)\log n]\}
depends only on the states of edges inside
x+B_{\lceil 2\varkappa(p)\log n\rceil+1}
.
By independence,

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\log n}\leq\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq P_{p}\left(\bigcap_{x\in A_{n}}\bigl\{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})\notin((\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n,2\varkappa(p)\log n]\bigr\}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{x\in A_{n}}P_{p}\bigl(\mathrm{diam}({\mathcal{C}_{x}})\notin((\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n,2\varkappa(p)\log n]\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq P_{p}\bigl(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\notin((\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n,2\varkappa(p)\log n]\bigr)^{|A_{n}|}.

Next, by (
3.2.
) and the fact that

\displaystyle|A_{n}|\geq\left(\frac{n}{8\varkappa(p)\log n}\right)^{d},

with the same
\kappa^{\prime}=\xi(p)\varepsilon/4>0
,

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\log n}\leq\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\frac{1}{2}n^{-d+2\kappa^{\prime}})^{|A_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2}n^{-d+2\kappa^{\prime}}\right)^{\left(\frac{n}{8\varkappa(p)\log n}\right)^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}n^{-d+2\kappa^{\prime}}\right)\right\}^{\left(\frac{n}{8\varkappa(p)\log n}\right)^{d}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\frac{\frac{1}{2}n^{2\kappa^{\prime}}}{(8\varkappa(p)\log n)^{d}}\right\}.

Therefore, we obtain (
3.5
).
The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We use the fact that the event
\{R_{n}^{(\mathrm{zb})}\neq R_{n}^{(\mathrm{fb})}\}
is contained in the event that there exists some vertex
x\in\partial B_{n}
satisfying
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})\geq R_{n}^{\mathrm{(fb)}}
. By Theorem
2.1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
R_{n}^{(\mathrm{fb})}>(\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n
holds with high probability. Thus, by Lemma
3.2
, the probability can be bounded above as follows:

\displaystyle P_{p}(R_{n}^{(\mathrm{zb})}\neq R_{n}^{(\mathrm{fb})})

\displaystyle\leq P_{p}\left(\bigcup_{x\in\partial B_{n}}\left\{R_{n}^{(\mathrm{fb})}\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})<\infty\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq P_{p}\left(\bigcup_{x\in\partial B_{n}}\left\{(\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})<\infty\right\}\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\partial B_{n}}P_{p}\bigl((\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})<\infty\bigr)+o(1).

Furthermore, by setting
\varepsilon=1/(2\xi(p))
and combining this with (
1.7
), the right-hand side is bounded above by:

\displaystyle 2d(2n+1)^{d-1}P_{p}\bigl((\varkappa(p)-\varepsilon)\log n\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})<\infty\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq 2d3^{d-1}n^{d-1}n^{-d+\xi(p)\varepsilon+o(1)}

\displaystyle=(2d3^{d-1})n^{-1+\xi(p)\varepsilon+o(1)}\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty,

where we have used
|\partial B_{n}|\leq 2d(2n+1)^{d-1}
.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
2.3
and
2.4

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

To prove the existence of the rate function, we proceed by proving the upper and lower bounds separately.
We first establish the upper bound.
Using Lemma
3.2
and (
1.8
) with some constant
L
, the probability is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle P_{p}(R_{n}>\rho\log n)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in B_{n}}P_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})>\rho\log n,|\mathcal{C}_{x}|<\infty)

\displaystyle\leq Ln^{d}(\rho\log n)^{d}\exp\{-\xi(p)\rho\log n\}

\displaystyle=L\exp\left\{d\log n+d\log(\rho\log n)-\rho\xi(p)\log n\right\}

\displaystyle=L\exp\left\{\left(d-\rho\xi(p)\right)\log n+d\log\rho+d\log(\log n)\right\},

which establishes the upper bound in the statement of the theorem.

Next, to prove the corresponding lower bound, we use that the events

\{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})\leq\rho\log n\text{ or }\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})>2\rho\log n\}_{x\in A_{n}}

are independent when

\displaystyle A_{n}=(K_{n}\mathbb{Z})^{d}\cap B_{n},

and

\displaystyle K_{n}=\lceil 2(2\rho+1)\log n\rceil.

By independence, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{p}(R_{n}\leq\rho\log n)

\displaystyle\leq P_{p}\left(\bigcap_{x\in A_{n}}\bigl\{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})\leq\rho\log n\text{ or }\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})>2\rho\log n\bigr\}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{x\in A_{n}}\bigl(1-P_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})\in(\rho\log n,2\rho\log n])\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-P_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\in(\rho\log n,2\rho\log n])\bigr)^{|A_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}|A_{n}|P_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\in(\rho\log n,2\rho\log n]),

where we use the approximation
(1-x)^{m}\leq 1-xm/2
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
x>0
as long as
xm
is sufficiently small.
Moreover, we use the fact

\displaystyle|A_{n}|\geq\left(\frac{n}{8\rho\log n}\right)^{d},

so we arrive at a bound, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle P_{p}(R_{n}>\rho\log n)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{8\rho\log n}\right)^{d}P_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\in(\rho\log n,2\rho\log n])

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{8\rho\log n}\right)^{d}\exp\bigl(-(\xi(p)+\delta)\rho\log n\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(\left(d-\rho(\xi(p)+\delta)\right)\log n-d\log(8\rho)-d\log(\log n)\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

First, we prove the following two-sided bound on the expectation.
For
x\in B_{n}
, let
D_{x}
be the event that
\{\rho\log n<\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})<\infty\}
. Then,
the quantity
S_{n}:=S_{n}(\rho)
can be described as the total number of such vertices in
B_{n}
:

S_{n}(\rho)=\sum_{x\in B_{n}}1_{D_{x}}.

By linearity, we have

\displaystyle E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)]

\displaystyle=E_{p}\left[\sum_{x\in B_{n}}1_{D_{x}}\right]=\sum_{x\in B_{n}}E_{p}[1_{D_{x}}]=\sum_{x\in B_{n}}P_{p}(D_{x}).

Since
P_{p}(D_{x})=P_{p}(D_{0})
for any
x\in B_{n}
by translational invariance, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)]=|B_{n}|P_{p}(D_{0}).

(3.6)

By (
1.7
), for any
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
n
,
P_{p}(D_{0})
is sharply bounded as

n^{-\xi(p)\rho-\varepsilon}\leq P_{p}(D_{0})\leq n^{-\xi(p)\rho+\varepsilon}.

Since
|B_{n}|=(2n+1)^{d}
, we have
n^{d}\leq|B_{n}|\leq(3n)^{d}
for
n\geq 1
. Combining this with (
3.6
) yields the following bounds for the expectation:

\displaystyle n^{d-\xi(p)\rho-\varepsilon}\leq E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)]\leq 3^{d}n^{d-\xi(p)\rho+\varepsilon}.

(3.7)

Next, we prove that
S_{n}(\rho)/E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)]
converges to
1
in probability.
We aim to show that the right-hand side of the Chebyshev’s inequality tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
For any
\delta>0
, the inequality is given by

\displaystyle P_{p}\left(\left|\frac{S_{n}(\rho)}{E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)]}-1\right|\geq\delta\right)\leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}[S_{n}(\rho)]}{(\delta E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)])^{2}}.

(3.8)

We write
I_{x}=1_{D_{x}}
.
The variance is expanded as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[S_{n}]=\sum_{x\in B_{n}}\operatorname{Var}[I_{x}]+\sum_{x\in B_{n}}\sum_{y\in B_{n}:y\neq x}\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}].

(3.9)

We proceed by estimating
\operatorname{Var}[I_{x}]
and
\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]
separately.

First, we estimate
\operatorname{Var}[I_{x}]
.
Since
I_{x}
is an indicator function, we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[I_{x}]

\displaystyle=E_{p}[I_{x}^{2}]-E_{p}[I_{x}]^{2}

\displaystyle=E_{p}[I_{x}]-E_{p}[I_{x}]^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-P_{p}(D_{x})\bigr)P_{p}(D_{x})\leq P_{p}(D_{x}).

Summing over all
x\in B_{n}
, we obtain the following upper bound:

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in B_{n}}\operatorname{Var}[I_{x}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in B_{n}}P_{p}(D_{x})=E_{p}[S_{n}].

(3.10)

Next, letting
\eta:=\max\{\varkappa(p),2\rho-\varkappa(p)\}
, we estimate
\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]
by splitting
B_{n}
based on the distance threshold
3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil
as follows:

\sum_{y\in B_{n}:y\neq x}\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]=\sum_{y\in x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil},\,y\neq x}\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]+\sum_{y\notin x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil}}\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}].

First, we estimate for
y\in x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil}
.
In this case, we apply the bound derived directly from the definition of covariance.

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]

\displaystyle=E_{p}[I_{x}I_{y}]-E_{p}[I_{x}]E_{p}[I_{y}]

\displaystyle=P_{p}(D_{x}\cap D_{y})-P_{p}(D_{x})P_{p}(D_{y})

\displaystyle\leq P_{p}(D_{x}\cap D_{y})

\displaystyle\leq P_{p}(D_{x}).

Thus, the first sum is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil},\,y\neq x}\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]

\displaystyle\leq(7(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n)^{d}P_{p}(D_{x}),

(3.11)

where we use
|x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil}|\leq(7(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n)^{d}
for sufficiently large
n
.

Next, we estimate for
y\notin x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil}
.
Recall from Theorem
2.1
and (
1.8
) that for sufficiently large
n
, the probability that a cluster extends beyond the radius
\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil
is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle P_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{x})>\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil,|\mathcal{C}_{x}|<\infty)

\displaystyle\leq L(\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil)^{d}n^{-\xi(p)(\varkappa(p)+\eta)}

\displaystyle\leq L^{\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{-d-\eta\xi(p)},

(3.12)

with some constants
L,L^{\prime}>0
. We define the event
D^{\prime}_{x}
:

D^{\prime}_{x}:=D_{x}\cap\{\mathcal{C}_{x}\subset x+B_{\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil}\}.

The probability of
D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x}
satisfies (
3.3.
):

\displaystyle P_{p}(D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x})\leq L^{\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{-d-\xi(p)\eta}.

(3.13)

We write
I^{\prime}_{x}:=1_{D^{\prime}_{x}}
and decompose the indicator as
I_{x}=I^{\prime}_{x}+1_{D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x}}
. Using the bilinearity of covariance, we expand
\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cov}[I^{\prime}_{x}+1_{D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x}},I^{\prime}_{y}+1_{D_{y}\setminus D^{\prime}_{y}}]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cov}[I^{\prime}_{x},I^{\prime}_{y}]+\operatorname{Cov}[I^{\prime}_{x},1_{D_{y}\setminus D^{\prime}_{y}}]+\operatorname{Cov}[1_{D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x}},I^{\prime}_{y}]+\operatorname{Cov}[1_{D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x}},1_{D_{y}\setminus D^{\prime}_{y}}].

Using the general property that for any indicator variables
X
and
Y
,
\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]\leq E_{p}[X]
holds, we obtain the bound:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]

\displaystyle\leq|\operatorname{Cov}[I^{\prime}_{x},I^{\prime}_{y}]|+E_{p}[1_{D_{y}\setminus D^{\prime}_{y}}]+E_{p}[1_{D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x}}]+E_{p}[1_{D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x}}]

\displaystyle=|\operatorname{Cov}[I^{\prime}_{x},I^{\prime}_{y}]|+3P_{p}(D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x}),

where we use the translational invariance
P_{p}(D_{x}\setminus D^{\prime}_{x})=P_{p}(D_{y}\setminus D^{\prime}_{y})
.
Since
y\notin x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil}
, the sets of edges on which
D^{\prime}_{x}
and
D^{\prime}_{y}
depend are disjoint. Hence
I^{\prime}_{x}
and
I^{\prime}_{y}
are independent and
\operatorname{Cov}[I^{\prime}_{x},I^{\prime}_{y}]=0
.
Substituting this and (
3.13
) into the inequality, and summing over
y
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{y\notin x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil}}\operatorname{Cov}[I_{x},I_{y}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\notin x+B_{3\lceil(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n+2\rceil}}3L^{\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{-d-\xi(p)\eta}

\displaystyle\leq(3n)^{d}3L^{\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{-d-\xi(p)\eta}

\displaystyle=3^{d+1}L^{\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{-\xi(p)\eta}.

(3.14)

By (
3.11
) and (
3.3.
), we evaluate the total variance:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[S_{n}]

\displaystyle\leq E_{p}[S_{n}]+\sum_{x\in B_{n}}\left((7(\varkappa(p)+\eta)\log n)^{d}P_{p}(D_{x})+3^{d+1}L^{\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{-\xi(p)\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq E_{p}[S_{n}]+L^{\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}E_{p}[S_{n}]+L^{\prime\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{d-\xi(p)\eta},

for some constants
L^{\prime\prime},L^{\prime\prime\prime}>0
.

Finally, we check the convergence condition:

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Var}[S_{n}]}{(\delta E_{p}[S_{n}])^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{E_{p}[S_{n}]+L^{\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}E_{p}[S_{n}]+L^{\prime\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{d-\xi(p)\eta}}{(\delta E_{p}[S_{n}])^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+L^{\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}}{\delta^{2}E_{p}[S_{n}]}+\frac{L^{\prime\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{d-\xi(p)\eta}}{\delta^{2}\,(E_{p}[S_{n}])^{2}}.

By (
3.7
), for any
\varepsilon>0
and all sufficiently large
n
,

E_{p}[S_{n}]\geq n^{d-\xi(p)\rho-\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1+L^{\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}}{\delta^{2}E_{p}[S_{n}]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+L^{\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}}{\delta^{2}}\,n^{-(d-\xi(p)\rho)+\varepsilon},

since
d-\xi(p)\rho>0
(as
\rho<\varkappa(p)
), taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small ensures the exponent is negative, so the first term converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

For the second term, using
E_{p}[S_{n}]^{2}\geq n^{2(d-\xi(p)\rho)-2\varepsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime\prime\prime}(\log n)^{d}n^{d-\xi(p)\eta}}{\delta^{2}\,(E_{p}[S_{n}])^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\delta^{2}}(\log n)^{d}n^{d-\xi(p)\eta}\,n^{-2(d-\xi(p)\rho)+2\varepsilon}.

The exponent of
n
simplifies as follows: if we take
\varepsilon
sufficiently small then,

\displaystyle d-\xi(p)\eta-2(d-\xi(p)\rho)+2\varepsilon

\displaystyle=-d+2\xi(p)\rho-\xi(p)\eta+2\varepsilon<0,

with the choice of
\eta=\max\{\varkappa(p),2\rho-\varkappa(p)\}
. Hence, we have

(\log n)^{d}n^{-d+2\xi(p)\rho-\xi(p)\eta+2\varepsilon}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

and hence the second term also converges to
0
.

Combining the above estimates, we conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Var}[S_{n}]}{(\delta E_{p}[S_{n}])^{2}}=0,

and therefore

\frac{S_{n}(\rho)}{E_{p}[S_{n}(\rho)]}\xrightarrow{P_{p}}1\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

Tunable Entanglement of Multi-Level Atoms in Photonic Crystals: Leveraging Resonant Dipole Interactions and Quantum Interference
Nancy Ghangas, Shubhrangshu Dasgupta

Abstract
We present a comprehensive investigation of entanglement dynamics in multi-level V-type atomic systems embedded within PC cavities. We mainly focus on the synergistic roles of resonant dipole-dipole interactions and quantum interference through analytical modeling and numerical simulations using the Schrodinger equation. In our analysis, we show that resonant dipole-dipole interactions and quantum interference serve as potent tools for tuning and stabilizing entanglement in structured photonic environments. Key findings reveal that resonant interaction dominates when interatomic distances align with the localization length of photonic bound states induced by bandgap which enables coherent coupling via evanescent fields. The entanglement is preserved for extended times when dipoles of each atom are aligned parallel or anti-parallel while a novel oscillatory entanglement mechanism emerges from orthogonally aligned atomic dipoles. It is mainly driven by destructive interference between vacuum-mediated pathways and bandgap-engineered photonic density of states. This mechanism exhibits non-Markovian dynamics, including delayed feedback and entanglement revival. We further demonstrate that positioning the atomic excited states deeper within the photonic bandgap accelerates the decay of entanglement oscillations due to the exponential suppression of resonant energy exchange mediated by evanescent modes.

Introduction

Photonic crystals (PCs) and optical cavities serve as versatile platforms for engineering light–matter interactions. Yablonovitch’s demonstration that tailoring the photonic density of states via photonic bandgaps can inhibit spontaneous emission which laid the groundwork for cavity Quantum electrodynamics (QED) central principle that an emitter’s radiative decay rate is not intrinsic but determined by its electromagnetic environment

PCs are highly effective for preserving quantum entanglement as they profoundly modify QED by localizing photons near atoms. When atomic transitions resonate near photonic band edge, photon-atom bound states are formed which lead to splitting excited states into doublets due to its own radiation field

Building on the foundational work of Sajeev John in quantum optics within photonic band gap crystals, S. Zhu expanded on this framework by analyzing how quantum interference between two atomic transitions modifies spontaneous emission dynamics in the presence of quantum interference. He reported that population trapped in two upper levels leads to spatial anti-trapping, periodic population oscillations, and the absence of population inversion under specific conditions

In QED systems, coupling two atoms in a cavity can yield robust entangled states through symmetric or antisymmetric superpositions, which are less susceptible to decoherence. But entanglement is so fragile under environmental coupling that it can vanish suddenly rather than decaying gradually. This phenomenon, termed entanglement sudden death (ESD), occurs due to interactions with environmental noise. Even a single-mode thermal field coupled to one qubit can induce ESD. It has been reported that two initially entangled qubits, when interacting solely with vacuum fluctuations can become completely disentangled in a finite time which is much shorter than the exponential decoherence time of spontaneous emission

4
Conclusions

In this study, we systematically investigated the interplay of RDDI and quantum interference in multi-level atomic systems embedded within PC cavities. Our analytical and numerical analysis reveal that RDDI and quantum interference serve as critical control parameters for tailoring entanglement dynamics in such structured photonic environments. Specifically, RDDI dominates when interatomic distances lie within the localization length of photonic bound states induced by the bandgap, enabling evanescent-field-mediated coherent coupling. Conversely, VIC emerges as a key modulator of quantum interference when orthogonal dipole transitions interact with the engineered photonic DOS. Notably, we identify a novel oscillatory entanglement mechanism arising from orthogonally aligned atomic dipoles, where destructive interference between vacuum-mediated pathways generates periodic population transfer between hybridized dressed states. This phenomenon, governed by the bandgap engineered DOS and defect-mode coupling, exhibits non-Markovian features such as delayed feedback and entanglement revival. These findings highlight the potential of PC cavities as platforms for dissipation-resistant entanglement engineering, with applications including quantum switches, quantum cloaking and quantum sensors.

Future outlook

We will extend our study to include the case where a deliberate asymmetry in PC creates a delta-resonance (defect) mode within the band gap, and assess how this localized photonic state influences the atom–atom entanglement dynamics.
We will systematically characterize regimes where asymmetric resonant dipole-dipole interaction
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
modify coherence transfer efficiencies and alter dressed-state formation pathways.
Furthermore, we will identify and characterize alternative initial atomic superpositions that may optimize entanglement lifetime beyond conventional bright/dark state paradigms in cavity QED.

Comparison of Oliceridine to Remifentanil for Optimal Analgesia in Mechanical Ventilation (CO-ROAM): Study Protocol for a Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Jing-chao Luo, Sen Lu, Xi-liang Fu, Jun Shen, Hong-li He, Chun Pan, Xiao-bo Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IntroductionCritically ill patients often endure pain, a distressing experience that can trigger diverse pathophysiological consequences. While remifentanil, with its rapid kinetics, is commonly used for analgesia in intensive care units (ICU), it frequently leads to opioid-related adverse effects. A promising alternative has emerged in oliceridine, a novel G protein-biased μ-opioid receptor agonist. This new drug offers the potential for effective pain relief with fewer side effects. However, its efficacy and safety profile in mechanically ventilated ICU patients remain to be fully elucidated.MethodsThis is a multicenter, prospective, randomized, single-blind, active-controlled trial conducted across 24 ICUs in China. A total of 292 mechanically ventilated patients requiring analgesia and sedation will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the oliceridine or remifentanil group. The oliceridine group will receive oliceridine (2–20 μg/kg/h), while the remifentanil group will receive remifentanil (1.5–12 μg/kg/h). Both groups will receive propofol for sedation if necessary. The target for analgesia is Critical-Care Pain Observation Tool (CPOT) < 3, and for sedation is Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS) − 2 to 0.Planned OutcomesThe primary outcome will be the percentage of time within target analgesia during study drug administration. Secondary outcomes will include gastrointestinal dysfunction, respiratory depression, sedative usage, mechanical ventilation duration, ICU stay length, extubation failure rate, etc.Trial RegistrationNCT06454292. Introduction

Pain, a distressing sensory and emotional experience induced by tissue damage, inflammatory stimuli, or emotional distress, is prevalent among critically ill patients [1]. The causes of pain are multifaceted. They include primary diseases, surgical procedures, trauma, burns, cancer-related pain, medical interventions, and prolonged immobilization [1]. Critically ill patients endure acute pain during hospitalization and may develop chronic intensive care unit (ICU)-related pain [2]. Pain elicits a cascade of physiological responses, including systemic stress, increased organ workload, and metabolic alterations, leading to complications such as fatigue, disorientation, tachycardia, elevated tissue oxygen consumption, coagulation abnormalities, respiratory dysfunction, immunosuppression, and heightened catabolism [1, 3].

Consequently, effective analgesia is paramount for critically ill patients. Currently, we widely employ remifentanil, a synthetic opioid, as a mainstream analgesic approach in ICU wards [4, 5]. As a selective μ-opioid receptor agonist, it is characterized by rapid onset and offset of action, and a context-sensitive half-time independent of infusion duration [6]. These pharmacokinetic properties make remifentanil particularly suitable for critically ill patients requiring frequent neurological assessments [7]. However, despite its widespread use and analgesic efficacy, remifentanil is associated with several adverse effects, including nausea, vomiting, hypotension, bradycardia, and respiratory depression [8]. Given these limitations, there is a continuous search for novel analgesics that optimize pain relief while minimizing adverse effects in critically ill patients.

Oliceridine fumarate, marketed as OLINVYK™ since August 2020, is the world's first FDA-approved G protein-biased ligand μ-opioid receptor agonist for managing acute pain in adults when alternatives are inadequate [9]. By preferentially engaging the G protein signaling pathway while minimizing β-arrestin involvement, this novel analgesic enhances pain relief with fewer adverse effects compared to traditional opioids [10, 11]. Studies show that oliceridine not only achieves faster peak analgesia than morphine (2–5 min) but also presents lower risks of gastrointestinal and respiratory complications at equivalent doses, with no dose adjustment required for elderly or renally impaired patients [11, 12]. Although the APOLLO-1 trial demonstrated rapid and significant analgesic efficacy in 418 patients with moderate to severe acute postoperative pain [13], oliceridine's efficacy and safety in mechanically ventilated ICU patients, where sedation and analgesia are crucial, remain to be confirmed through clinical trials.

Therefore, we have designed a prospective randomized controlled trial comparing oliceridine with remifentanil in mechanically ventilated ICU patients to evaluate efficacy, safety, and clinical outcomes, aiming to provide evidence for an improved analgesic strategy in critical care settings.

Methods

Study Design and Settings

The CO-ROAM (Comparison of Oliceridine to Remifentanil for Optimal Analgesia in Mechanical ventilation) study is a multicenter, prospective, randomized, single-blind, active-controlled trial conducted across 24 ICUs in China, led by the Sichuan Academy of Medical Sciences & Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital. The study received ethical approval from all participating sites and was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06454292) on June 11, 2024.

Population

Patients receiving mechanical ventilation and requiring both analgesia and sedation are eligible for inclusion.

Randomization and Blinding

Eligible participants will be randomized 1:1 to oliceridine or remifentanil groups using a centralized interactive web response system. Upon enrollment, drug administrators will access the system to obtain randomization numbers and group assignments. Group allocation will be known to treating physicians and nurses, but concealed from outcome assessors, patients, and data analysts.

Interventions

Figure 1 shows the study drug administration and titration process. Following discontinuation of prior sedation/analgesia, patients undergo a washout period until CPOT ≥ 3 and RASS > 0. Oliceridine (0.1 mg/kg) and remifentanil (50 μg/kg) are each diluted to a total volume of 50 ml. Analgesia is initiated with oliceridine at 6 μg/kg/h (range 2–20 μg/kg/h, 2-μg/kg/h increments) and remifentanil at 1.5 μg/kg/h (range 1.5–12 μg/kg/h, 1.5-μg/kg/h increments), with dosages adjusted at ≥ 5-min intervals until CPOT < 3. Once adequate analgesia is achieved, if RASS remains > 0, propofol infusion is started at 0.5 mg/kg/h (maximum 4 mg/kg/h, 0.5-mg/kg/h increments) to target RASS − 2 to 0. For RASS − 3 to − 5, propofol is reduced; if CPOT ≥ 3 and/or RASS > 0, analgesia is adjusted first, followed by sedation if necessary. When propofol exceeds 4 mg/kg/h and RASS remains > 0, dexmedetomidine (0.2–1 μg/kg/h) or midazolam (0.04–0.2 mg/kg/h) infusion is added. If oliceridine reaches 20 μg/kg/h and CPOT remains ≥ 3, remifentanil infusion (1.5–12 μg/kg/h) is introduced.Fig. 1Study drug initiation, titration, and assessment flowchart

Concomitant Therapies

During the study drug administration and CPOT assessment period, concomitant use of non-protocol sedatives, analgesics, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and their antagonists is prohibited, except for specific purposes or adverse event (AE) management. Subjects using prohibited medications for AEs or rescue therapy must still complete all protocol-specified procedures and follow-ups.

Data Collection

CPOT assessments are conducted immediately before drug administration, at 5, 10, 30 min and 1 h post-initiation, and before and after each dose adjustment. Upon achieving the analgesic target, CPOT is evaluated hourly for 4 h, then every 4 h until the end of the observation period. RASS scoring adheres to a comparable assessment schedule. Doses are documented following each adjustment. The observation period ends with tracheal extubation, 72 h post-enrollment, or death, whichever comes first. Patient follow-up continues for 28 days, until hospital discharge, or death, whichever occurs earliest. Outcome Measurements

The primary endpoint is the percentage of time within target analgesia (PTTA) during study drug administration. Predetermined secondary endpoints include: (1) gastrointestinal dysfunction incidence and acute gastrointestinal injury(AGI) grading; (2) gastrointestinal injury biomarkers (DAO, IFABP) in approximately 100 consenting patients; (3) post-extubation respiratory depression (respiratory rate < 8/min) within 24 h; (4) propofol/dexmedetomidine dosage; (5) rescue remifentanil use; (6) duration of invasive mechanical ventilation; (7) length of ICU stay; (8) failure rate of first extubation attempt; (9) hypotension and bradycardia incidence (> 20% decrease from baseline); (10) total analgesic and sedative costs; and (11) time to CPOT > 3 and RASS > 0 after drug discontinuation.

Sample Size Calculation

A study by Muellejans et al. showed that the PTTA for remifentanil was 88.3% [15]. With a non-inferiority margin of − 10%, a significance level (α) of 0.05, and a power (1 − β) of 80%, assuming a 10% dropout rate, we estimated that 146 patients per group would be required, resulting in a total sample size of 292 patients.

Data Analysis

All analyses will be conducted on an intention-to-treat basis, including all randomized patients who received interventions. Descriptive statistics will be presented as mean ± standard deviation, median (interquartile range), or number (percentage), as appropriate. Between-group comparisons will be performed using t tests, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, or chi-square tests, depending on the nature of the data. The absolute difference in the primary outcome between groups will be calculated, with 95% confidence intervals estimated using bootstrap methods. Subgroup analyses will be conducted based on key variables such as age, sex, BMI, APACHE II score, patient type, and reason for mechanical ventilation. Statistical significance will be set at P < 0.05. All analyses will be performed using IBM SPSS Statistics v25.0 or R software.

Discussion

This is the first prospective, multi-center, randomized trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the novel analgesic oliceridine for pain management in mechanically ventilated ICU patients. Unlike previous studies, we have chosen to compare oliceridine with remifentanil, the current standard of care [16] aiming to further advance analgesic practices in the ICU setting.

Oliceridine has demonstrated similar or superior analgesic efficacy to morphine, hydromorphone, and fentanyl in acute pain management, with a more favorable side effect profile [17–20]. For critically ill ICU patients, especially those on mechanical ventilation, remifentanil has become the preferred analgesic [1, 16]. Remifentanil demonstrates excellent analgesic efficacy; however, as a traditional μ-opioid receptor agonist, it activates both G protein and β-arrestin pathways, often resulting in significant adverse effects such as respiratory depression, nausea, vomiting, and constipation [8, 21]. In contrast, oliceridine, a biased μ-receptor agonist, selectively activates the G protein pathway, potentially mitigating adverse drug reactions [12]. Therefore, this study primarily aims to assess the non-inferiority of analgesic efficacy, with a secondary focus on comparing the incidence of β-arrestin-mediated adverse events, particularly gastrointestinal dysfunction.

The maximum drug administration and observation period for this study is set at 3 days. Oliceridine is primarily metabolized by hepatic CYP3A4 and CYP2D6, with an equal contribution from each enzyme, and has a half-life of approximately 1.3–3 h [22]. Prolonged administration thus poses a risk of drug accumulation. A clinical study on patients with hepatic and renal impairment suggests that, for those with severe hepatic dysfunction, initial dose reduction and close monitoring should be considered, as these patients require lower oliceridine doses [23]. ICU patients on mechanical ventilation often present with liver dysfunction. Consequently, to ensure patient safety, the maximum duration of administration is set at 3 days.

This study employs a single-blind randomized controlled trial (RCT) design. While a double-blind approach would ideally enhance objectivity and reliability, the post-marketing nature of this drug study necessitates routine management of analgesics as controlled substances, precluding blinding of medical personnel. Dedicated physicians conduct sedation and analgesia assessments, potentially introducing subjective influences. However, blinding of data analysts to group allocation partially mitigates this limitation. Crucially, maintaining patient blinding to treatment allocation remains paramount, preserving the integrity of patient-reported outcomes and minimizing placebo effects.

Pain assessment is crucial in analgesic research. Pupillometry offers a more objective approach by measuring changes in pupil diameter [24]. However, it requires specialized equipment and skilled operators. This limitation confines its use to single-center physiological studies [25, 26] or animal experiments [27]. In multi-center, large-sample studies, maintaining consistent training and inter-rater reliability with pupillometry is highly challenging. In contrast, the CPOT, though more subjective, provides sufficient accuracy [28, 29]. It has become the standard pain assessment tool for ICU patients [14, 30]. The widespread use of CPOT is supported by extensive literature across various critical care populations, including post-cardiac surgery [31], pediatric [32], and intubated patients [33, 34]. Using this widely adopted tool enhances the reproducibility of our study results within the scientific community.

Strengths and Limitations

As a multicenter RCT, this study will generate powerful and up-to-date clinical evidence to further optimize pain management in critically ill patients, potentially reducing adverse effects and enhancing patient experience. However, the study also has several limitations. Although multi-centered, all the sites are within one country, necessitating further evaluation of the results' generalizability across diverse populations. Resource constraints and regulatory requirements lead to a single-blind design rather than a double-blind approach. Additionally, given remifentanil's established analgesic efficacy, superiority testing is not conducted, as it would require an unfeasibly large sample size beyond the study's scope.

Ethics and Dissemination

This trial adheres to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and has received approval from the Ethics Committees of all participating institutions. The protocol design complies with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) statement and Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines. Written informed consent is obtained from all recruited patients prior to their participation in the trial.

Conclusions

This study comparing oliceridine with remifentanil in ventilated ICU patients has the potential to significantly improve critical care pain management by enhancing patient comfort and reducing opioid-related side effects.

Trial Status

The trial is currently recruiting patients. To date, 20% of patients have participated in the study. The average enrollment rate is expected to be between three and five patients per week. Data collection will stop 1 year after the last patient is included.

On P P -partitions Extended by Two-Rowed Plane Partitions
Jingxuan Li, Feihu Liu, Guoce Xin

Abstract
Inspired by Gansner’s elegant k k -trace generating function for rectangular plane partitions, we introduce two novel operators, φ z \varphi_{z} and ψ z \psi_{z} , along with their combinatorial interpretations. Through these operators, we derive a new formula for P P -partitions of posets extended by two-rowed plane partitions. This formula allows us to compute explicit enumerative generating functions for various classes of P P -partitions. Our findings encompass skew plane partitions, diamond-related two-rowed plane partitions, an extended V V -poset, and ladder poset extensions, enriching the theory of P P -partitions.

Introduction

A
plane partition
is an array
\pi=(\pi_{ij})_{i,j\geq 1}
of nonnegative integers such that
\pi
has finitely many nonzero entries and is weakly decreasing in rows and columns. If the sum of all entries,
\sum_{i,j}\pi_{ij}=n
, then
\pi
is referred to as a plane partition of
n
. Regarding the history of plane partitions, please refer to
[
5
,
8
]
and
[
22
, P. 440-442]
.

A
part
of a plane partition
\pi=(\pi_{i,j})
is a positive entry
\pi_{i,j}>0
.
In
[
20
]
, Stanley defined the
trace
of
\pi
as
\mathrm{tr}(\pi)=\sum\pi_{i,i}
.
Consider a
r\times c
matrix
\pi=(\pi_{i,j})
over nonnegative integers
such that
\pi_{i,j}\geq\pi_{i,j+1}
and
\pi_{i,j}\geq\pi_{i+1,j}
. Such a matrix
\pi
is a plane partition of
n=\sum_{i,j}\pi_{i,j}
with at most
r
rows and
c
columns, and is called a
rectangular plane partition
.
Let
P_{r,c}(X)
denote the complete generating function for all plane partitions with at most
r
rows and
c
columns, i.e.,

P_{r,c}(X)=\sum_{\pi}x_{1,1}^{\pi_{1,1}}\cdots x_{1,c}^{\pi_{1,c}}\cdots x_{r,1}^{\pi_{r,1}}\cdots x_{r,c}^{\pi_{r,c}},

where the sum ranges over all rectangular plane partitions with at most
r
rows and
c
columns.

Setting
x_{i,j}=x
for all
i,j
, we obtain the enumerative generating function
q_{r,c}(x):=P_{r,c}(X)|_{x_{i,j}=x}
.
When
r,c\rightarrow\infty
, MacMahon
[
16
]
conjectured the following formula

\displaystyle q_{\infty,\infty}(x)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-x^{n})^{n}}.

(1)

MacMahon
[
17
]
also conjectured

\displaystyle q_{r,c}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{c}\frac{1}{1-x^{i+j-1}}.

(2)

Obviously, (
2
) reduces to (
1
) when
r,c\rightarrow\infty
.
For a proof of a stronger result of (
2
), see
[
15
, Sect. 495]
. A combinatorial proof of (
2
) appears in
[
7
]
.

We now outline a generalization of (
2
) from
[
10
]
.
For an integer
k
with
-r+1\leq k\leq c-1
, the
k
-trace

\mathrm{tr}_{k}(\pi)
of
\pi=(\pi_{i,j})
is defined as

\mathrm{tr}_{k}(\pi)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq r;\ 1\leq j\leq c\atop j-i=k}\pi_{i,j}.

For example, the rectangular plane partition

\displaystyle\pi=\begin{matrix}7&7&6&5\\
7&5&4&2\\
6&3&0&0\\
\end{matrix}

of
52
has
\mathrm{tr}_{-2}(\pi)=6
,
\mathrm{tr}_{-1}(\pi)=10
,
\mathrm{tr}_{0}(\pi)=12
,
\mathrm{tr}_{1}(\pi)=11
,
\mathrm{tr}_{2}(\pi)=8
, and
\mathrm{tr}_{3}(\pi)=5
. Define
\tau_{r,c}(t_{-r+1},\ldots,t_{-1};t_{0},\ldots,t_{c-1};n)
to be the number of such plane partitions of
n
with
k
-traces
\mathrm{tr}_{k}(\pi)=t_{k}
,
-r+1\leq k\leq c-1
. Let

\displaystyle T_{r,c}(z_{-r+1},\ldots,z_{-1};z_{0},\ldots,z_{c-1};q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{t_{-r+1}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{t_{c-1}=0}^{\infty}\tau_{r,c}(t_{-r+1},\ldots,t_{-1};t_{0},\ldots,t_{c-1};n)z_{-r+1}^{t_{-r+1}}\cdots z_{-1}^{t_{-1}}z_{0}^{t_{0}}\cdots z_{c-1}^{t_{c-1}}q^{n}.

Gansner derived the following elegant result.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
10
]

For positive integers
r,c
, we have

T_{r,c}(z_{-r+1},\ldots,z_{-1};z_{0},\ldots,z_{c-1};q)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{c}\frac{1}{1-z_{-i+1}z_{-i+2}\cdots z_{j-1}q^{i+j-1}}.

In Theorem
1.1
, setting
z_{k}=1
for all
k
yields (
2
), and setting
z_{k}=1
for all
k\neq 0
yields Stanley’s trace theorem
[
20
, Theorem 2.2]
.
Andrews and Paule
[
4
]
also proved Theorem
1.1
by using basic power series arithmetic, an approach distinct from Gansner’s original proof
[
10
]
, which is based on a combinatorial bijection.

When

, a rectangular plane partition
\pi
is known as a

.
Two-rowed plane partitions have been a subject of extensive research; see, e.g.,

Inspired by the results related to the
k
-traces, we consider two operators
\varphi_{z}
and
\psi_{z}
defined below.

Let
K
be a ring, such as the ring
\mathbb{Z}
of integers. Let

F(x,y;P)=\sum_{i\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j},\ \ \ a_{ij}\in K

be the bivariate generating function of a combinatorial object
P
.
The linear operators
\varphi_{z}
and
\psi_{z}
are defined by

\displaystyle\varphi_{z}\odot F(x,y;P)

\displaystyle=\frac{F(xz,yz;P)-yF(xyz,z;P)}{(1-x)(1-y)},

(3)

\displaystyle\psi_{z}\odot F(x,y;P)

\displaystyle=\frac{F(z,xz;P)-xF(xz,z;P)}{1-x}.

(4)

We will prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varphi_{z}
and
\psi_{z}
be the operators as defined in (
3
) and (
4
). For
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{z_{n}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-1}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-2}}\odot\cdots\odot\varphi_{z_{1}}\odot F(x,y;P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F(z_{1}\cdots z_{n},xz_{1}\cdots z_{n};P)-xF(xz_{1}\cdots z_{n},z_{1}\cdots z_{n};P)}{(1-x)(1-xz_{n})(1-xz_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-xz_{2}\cdots z_{n})(1-z_{n})(1-z_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-z_{2}\cdots z_{n})}.

In Section 3, we will provide a combinatorial interpretation for these two operators. Within this framework, the generating function in Theorem
1.2

is interpreted as the
k
-trace generating functions of certain extended two-rowed plane partitions (see Figure
3
). This allows us to calculate many enumerative generating functions related to extended two-rowed plane partitions.
Consequently, we derive the number of linear extensions for these posets.

Section 3 delves into the combinatorial interpretation of the operators

Sections 4 and 5 are applications of Theorem

Section 5 considers three new types of posets:
Two-rowed plane partitions with diamond posets, an extension of the
V
-poset, and an extension of the ladder poset.

2
P
-Partitions

We assume that the readers possess basic knowledge of posets (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Section 3]
).
Let
(P,\preceq)
be a finite
partially ordered set
(
poset
for short).
We say that
t

covers

s
if
s\prec t
and there is no
c\in P
such that
s\prec c\prec t
.
The
Hasse diagram
of a finite poset
P
is a graph where vertices are the elements of
P
, edges represent cover relations, and if
s\prec t
, then
t
is drawn above
s
in the diagram.

We now introduce the concept of
P
-partitions, which were initially studied by MacMahon
[
15
, Subsections 439,441]
.
In 1970, Knuth
[
12
]
provided clarification on MacMahon’s work within the theory of
P
-partitions.
However, the first comprehensive development of
P
-partitions was presented by Stanley in
[
18
,
19
]
.

A
P
-partition is a map
\sigma:P\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
that satisfies the following condition:
If
s\prec t
in
P
, then
\sigma(s)\geq\sigma(t)
. In other words,
\sigma
is
order-reversing
.
If the sum
\sum_{s\in P}\sigma(s)
equals
n
, denoted as
|\sigma|=n
, then
\sigma
is referred to as a
P
-partition of
n
.
It is convenient to represent
s\prec t
with an arrow
s\to t
. Consequently, a
P
-partition is a vertex labeling
\sigma
such that
s\to t
implies
\sigma(s)\geq\sigma(t)
.

We denote the set of all
P
-partitions on the poset
P
as
\pi(P)
.
The fundamental generating function associated with
\pi(P)
is given by

F_{P}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p})=\sum_{\sigma\in\pi(P)}x_{1}^{\sigma(1)}x_{2}^{\sigma(2)}\cdots x_{p}^{\sigma(p)}.

By setting
x_{i}=q
for all
i
, we obtain the
enumerative generating function
of
\pi(P)
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{PF}(\pi(P))=F_{P}(q,q,\ldots,q)=\sum_{\sigma\in\pi(P)}q^{|\sigma|}.

The set
[p]
with its usual order forms a
p
-element chain, i.e., a poset whose elements are totally ordered. This poset is denoted
\mathbf{p}
.
Let
\#P=p
. An order-preserving bijection
\tau:P\rightarrow\mathbf{p}
is called a
linear extension
of
P
.
The number of linear extensions of
P
is denoted
e(P)
.

Now, suppose
(P,\preceq)
is a finite poset on
[p]:=\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
.
We may identify a linear extension
\tau:P\rightarrow\mathbf{p}
with the permutation
\tau^{-1}(1),\ldots,\tau^{-1}(p)
.
The set of all
e(P)
permutations of
[p]
obtained in this manner is denoted
\mathcal{L}(P)
and is referred to as the
Jordan-Hölder set
of
P
.
Let
\mu=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{p}
be a permutation. For
1\leq i\leq p-1
, if
\mu_{i}>\mu_{i+1}
, then
i
is a
descent
of
\mu
. Define the
descent set

D_{\mu}
of
\mu
by

D_{\mu}=\{i:\mu_{i}>\mu_{i+1}\}\subseteq[p-1].

Let
d(\mu)
be the number of descents of permutation
\mu
, i.e.,
d(\mu)=\#D_{\mu}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
21
, Theorem 3.15.5]

Let
(P,\preceq)
be a finite poset on
[p]:=\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
. Then

\displaystyle F_{P}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p})=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}(P)}\frac{\prod_{j\in D_{\mu}}x_{\mu_{1}}x_{\mu_{2}}\cdots x_{\mu_{j}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(1-x_{\mu_{1}}x_{\mu_{2}}\cdots x_{\mu_{i}})}.

Define the
major index

\mathrm{maj}(\mu)
of
\mu
by
\mathrm{maj}(\mu)=\sum_{j\in D_{\mu}}j
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
21
, Theorem 3.15.7]

Let
(P,\preceq)
be a finite poset on
[p]:=\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{PF}(\pi(P))=\frac{\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}(P)}q^{\mathrm{maj}(\mu)}}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{p})}.

Now, let’s consider an alternative method for counting
P
-partitions.
For
m\in\mathbb{P}
, let
\aleph(P,m)
denote the number of
P
-partitions
P\rightarrow\mathbf{m}\uplus\{0\}
.

Let
\Omega_{P}(m)
denote the number of order-preserving maps
\rho:P\rightarrow\mathbf{m}
.
In fact,
\Omega_{P}(m)
is a polynomial function
[
21
, P. 334]
of
m
of degree
p
with leading coefficient
\frac{e(P)}{p!}
.
Thus
\Omega_{P}(m)
is called the
order polynomial
of
P
.
By replacing
\rho(t)
with
m+1-\rho(t)
, we see that
\Omega_{P}(m)
is also the number of order-reversing maps
P\rightarrow\mathbf{m}
, i.e., the number of
P
-partitions
P\rightarrow\mathbf{m}
.
Therefore, we have
\aleph(P,m)=\Omega_{P}(m+1)
for any
m\geq 0
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
21
, Theorem 3.15.8]

Let
(P,\preceq)
be a finite poset on
[p]:=\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}\aleph(P,m)x^{m}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\Omega_{P}(m+1)x^{m}=\frac{\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}(P)}x^{d(\mu)}}{(1-x)^{p+1}}.

Finally, we conclude this section by providing two formulas for the number
e(P)
of linear extensions. They are valuable since Brightwell and Winller
[
9
]
showed that counting
e(P)
of a finite poset is
\#\mathbf{P}
-complete.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
21
, Section 3]

Let
(P,\preceq)
be a finite poset on
[p]:=\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
. Then

\displaystyle e(P)

\displaystyle=p!\cdot\bigg(\Big((1-q)^{p}\cdot\mathrm{PF}(\pi(P))\Big)\Big|_{q=1}\bigg).

(5)

\displaystyle e(P)

\displaystyle=p!\cdot\big([m^{p}]\Omega_{P}(m)\big)=p!\cdot\big([m^{p}]\aleph(P,m)\big),

(6)

where
[m^{i}]f(m)
denotes the coefficient of
m^{i}
in a polynomial
f(m)
.

An Extension of Two-Rowed Plane Partitions

Now suppose
P
is a poset containing the segment
A\to B
(see the left side of Figure

\displaystyle F(x,y;P)=\sum_{\sigma\in\pi(P)}x^{\sigma(A)}y^{\sigma(B)}q^{|\sigma|-\sigma(A)-\sigma(B)}=\sum_{i\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}.

This generating function keeps track of
\sigma(A)
and
\sigma(B)
. In particular,
the coefficient

is the weighted counting series (in

) for
P
-partitions
\sigma
of

\sigma(A)=i
,
\sigma(B)=j
.
By definition

. Our main concern here is about

and

.

We construct two posets
\widetilde{P}_{1}
and

to explain the action of the two operators
\varphi_{z}
and
\psi_{z}
defined in (

) and (

) on
F(x,y;P)
, respectively.

The poset
\widetilde{P}_{1}
is obtained from
P
by adding two new vertices
C
and
D
, with the arrows
C\to A\to D\to B
. See Figure
1
, where
a
P
-partition
\sigma
of
P
is extended as
\sigma(A)=i
,
\sigma(B)=j
,
\sigma(C)=m
,
\sigma(D)=\ell
for
\widetilde{P}_{1}
. Note that

is omitted due to the transitivity of the partial order.

The poset
\widetilde{P}_{1}
.

In this new poset, we keep track of
\sigma(C)
,
\sigma(D)
, and the trace
\sigma(A)+\sigma(B)
by letting

F(x,y;z;\widetilde{P}_{1})=\sum_{\sigma\in\pi(\widetilde{P}_{1})}x^{\sigma(C)}y^{\sigma(D)}z^{\sigma(A)+\sigma(B)}q^{|\sigma|-\sigma(C)-\sigma(D)-(\sigma(A)+\sigma(B))}.

Then the following lemma gives a combinatorial interpretation of the operator
\varphi_{z}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Follow the notation as above. We have

\varphi_{z}\odot F(x,y;P)=\sum_{m\geq i\geq\ell\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{m}y^{\ell}z^{i+j}=F(x,y;z;\widetilde{P}_{1}).

Proof.

By (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{z}\odot F(x,y;P)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\sum_{i\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}z^{i+j}-\sum_{i\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{i+1}z^{i+j}\right)}{1-y}\sum_{k\geq 0}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq j\geq 0,k\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{i+k}z^{i+j}\sum_{\ell=j}^{i}y^{\ell}.

This means that for each pair
i\geq j
, a
P
-partition
\sigma
of
P
with
\sigma(A,B)=(i,j)
generates

P
-partitions
\sigma^{\prime}
of
\widetilde{P}_{1}
with
\sigma^{\prime}(A,B,C,D)=(i,j,i+k,\ell)
with the desired conditions
i+k\geq i\geq\ell\geq j
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Follow the notation as above. We have

\psi_{z}\odot F(x,y;P)=\sum_{i\geq\ell\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{\ell}z^{i+j}=F(x;z;\widetilde{P}_{2}).

Our main result, Theorem

, is derived by composing these operators.
This result enables us to obtain many generating functions associated with two-rowed plane partitions.
For the reader’s convenience, we restate the theorem as follows.

Theorem 3.3

(also Theorem
1.2
)
.

The operators
\varphi_{z}
,
\psi_{z}
are defined in (
3
) and (
4
).
Let
F(x,y;P)=\sum_{i\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Let
n\geq 2
. Then we have

\displaystyle\psi_{z_{n}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-1}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-2}}\odot\cdots\odot\varphi_{z_{1}}\odot F(x,y;P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F(z_{1}\cdots z_{n},xz_{1}\cdots z_{n};P)-xF(xz_{1}\cdots z_{n},z_{1}\cdots z_{n};P)}{(1-x)(1-xz_{n})(1-xz_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-xz_{2}\cdots z_{n})(1-z_{n})(1-z_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-z_{2}\cdots z_{n})}.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by induction on
n
. By (
3
) and (
4
), we know that

\displaystyle\varphi_{z}\odot F(x,y;P)=\frac{F(xz,yz;P)-yF(xyz,z;P)}{(1-x)(1-y)},\ \ \ \psi_{z}\odot F(x,y;P)=\frac{F(z,xz;P)-xF(xz,z;P)}{1-x}.

In the base case when
n=2
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{z_{2}}\odot\varphi_{z_{1}}\odot F(x,y;P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{z_{2}}\odot\frac{F(xz_{1},yz_{1};P)-yF(xyz_{1},z_{1};P)}{(1-x)(1-y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-x}\left(\frac{F(z_{1}z_{2},xz_{1}z_{2};P)-xz_{2}F(xz_{1}z_{2}^{2},z_{1};P)}{(1-z_{2})(1-xz_{2})}-x\cdot\frac{F(xz_{1}z_{2},z_{1}z_{2};P)-z_{2}F(xz_{1}z_{2}^{2},z_{1};P)}{(1-z_{2})(1-xz_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F(z_{1}z_{2},xz_{1}z_{2};P)-xF(xz_{1}z_{2},z_{1}z_{2};P)}{(1-x)(1-z_{2})(1-xz_{2})}.

Assuming the theorem holds for
n-1
. By simple variable substitution, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{z_{n}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-1}}\odot\cdots\odot\varphi_{z_{2}}\odot F(x,y;P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F(z_{2}\cdots z_{n},xz_{2}\cdots z_{n};P)-xF(xz_{2}\cdots z_{n},z_{2}\cdots z_{n};P)}{(1-x)(1-xz_{n})(1-xz_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-xz_{3}\cdots z_{n})(1-z_{n})(1-z_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-xz_{3}\cdots z_{n})}.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\psi_{z_{n}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-1}}\odot\cdots\odot\varphi_{z_{1}}\odot F(x,y;P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{z_{n}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-1}}\odot\cdots\odot\varphi_{z_{2}}\odot\frac{F(xz_{1},yz_{1};P)-yF(xyz_{1},z_{1};P)}{(1-x)(1-y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{F(z_{1}\cdots z_{n},xz_{1}\cdots z_{n};P)-xz_{2}\cdots z_{n}F(xz_{1}z_{2}^{2}\cdots z_{n}^{2},z_{1};P)}{(1-xz_{2}\cdots z_{n})(1-z_{2}\cdots z_{n})}-x\cdot\frac{F(xz_{1}\cdots z_{n},z_{1}\cdots z_{n};P)-z_{2}\cdots z_{n}F(xz_{1}z_{2}^{2}\cdots z_{n}^{2},z_{1};P)}{(1-xz_{2}\cdots z_{n})(1-z_{2}\cdots z_{n})}}{(1-x)(1-xz_{n})(1-xz_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-xz_{3}\cdots z_{n})(1-z_{n})(1-z_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-z_{3}\cdots z_{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F(z_{1}\cdots z_{n},xz_{1}\cdots z_{n};P)-x\cdot F(xz_{1}\cdots z_{n},z_{1}\cdots z_{n};P)}{(1-x)(1-xz_{n})(1-xz_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-xz_{2}\cdots z_{n})(1-z_{n})(1-z_{n-1}z_{n})\cdots(1-z_{2}\cdots z_{n})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We consider the combinatorial explanation of
\psi_{z_{n}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-1}}\odot\varphi_{z_{n-2}}\odot\cdots\odot\varphi_{z_{1}}\odot F(x,y;P)

Based on the posets

4
Skew Plane Partitions

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be two partitions where
\mu\subseteq\lambda
(i.e.,
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for all
i
).
Define the
length

\ell(\lambda)
as the number of non-zero parts.
Define a
skew plane partition
of skew shape
\lambda/\mu
to be an array
T=(T_{ij})
of nonnegative integers of shape
\lambda/\mu
(i.e.,
1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)
,
\mu_{i}<j\leq\lambda_{i}
) that is weakly decreasing in both rows and columns.
For instance, the following array

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}&&&7&4\\
&&6&5&5\\
&&6&3&\\
&9&0&&\\
\end{matrix}

represents a skew plane partition of the skew shape
\lambda/\mu=(4,4,3,2)/(2,1,1)
.

Skew plane partitions can be regarded as
P
-partitions of a poset, denoted as
P_{\lambda/\mu}
. Let
m\in\mathbb{P}
. In Section 2,
\aleph(P,m)
denotes the number of
P
-partitions
P\rightarrow\mathbf{m}\uplus\{0\}
.
Now we set
m\in\mathbb{N}
without leading to ambiguity.
Therefore,
\aleph(P_{\lambda/\mu},m)
is the number of skew plane partitions of shape
\lambda/\mu
such that the largest part is less than or equal to
m
.

A determinant formula for
\aleph(P_{\lambda/\mu},m)
was first given by Kreweras
[
13
, Section 2.3.7]
(also see
[
21
, Section 3; Exercise 149]
), which is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\aleph(P_{\lambda/\mu},m)=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\left(\binom{m+\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}}{m+i-j}\right),

(9)

where
k=\ell(\lambda)
, and
\mu_{j}=0
for
\ell(\mu)<j\leq\ell(\lambda)
. In the specific case where
\lambda/\mu=(M-d,M-2d,\ldots,M-kd)/\emptyset
, the determinant can be explicitly computed, as detailed in
[
22
, Exercise 7.101(b)]
.

To ensure completeness, we provide a proof for the following proposition:

Proposition 4.1
.

The number of linear extensions of the poset
P_{\lambda/\mu}
is given by the formula:

\displaystyle e(P_{\lambda/\mu})=(|\lambda|-|\mu|)!\cdot\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\left(\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j)!}\right),

(10)

where
k=\ell(\lambda)
, and
\mu_{j}=0
for
\ell(\mu)<j\leq\ell(\lambda)
.

Proof.

By (
6
) and (
9
), we have

\displaystyle e(P_{\lambda/\mu})

\displaystyle=(|\lambda|-|\mu|)!\cdot\Big([m^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}]\aleph(P_{\lambda/\mu},m)\Big)

\displaystyle=(|\lambda|-|\mu|)!\cdot[m^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}]\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\left(\binom{m+\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}}{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}\right)

\displaystyle=(|\lambda|-|\mu|)!\cdot\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\left(\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j)!}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

In fact, Equation (
10
) is also the formula to count the number of standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu

[
22
, Corollary 7.16.3]
.

6
Concluding Remark

Our main contribution is Theorem
1.2
, which extends
P
-partitions by two-rowed plane partitions.
This idea enables us to obtain explicit expressions for enumerative generating functions and linear extensions of some posets.

Our next project is to extend
P
-partitions by
r
-rowed plane partitions
. Here
r
-rowed plane partitions are the rectangular plane partitions with at most
r

and

, and find their combinatorial interpretations. This may
help us better understand Gansner’s elegant formula in Theorem

. Our approach will extend and enrich the theory of

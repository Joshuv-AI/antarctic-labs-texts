Uniquely colorable hypergraphs
Xizhi Liu, Jie Ma, Tianhen Wang, Tianming Zhu

Abstract
An r r -uniform hypergraph is uniquely k k -colorable if there exists exactly one partition of its vertex set into k k parts such that every edge contains at most one vertex from each part. For integers k ≥ r ≥ 2 k\geq r\geq 2 , let Φ k , r \Phi_{k,r} denote the minimum real number such that every n n -vertex k k -partite r r -uniform hypergraph with positive codegree greater than Φ k , r ⋅ n \Phi_{k,r}\cdot n and no isolated vertices is uniquely k k -colorable. A classic result by of Bollobás [ Bol78 ] established that Φ k , 2 = 3 ​ k − 5 3 ​ k − 2 \Phi_{k,2}=\frac{3k-5}{3k-2} for every k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . We consider the uniquely colorable problem for hypergraphs. Our main result determines the precise value of Φ k , r \Phi_{k,r} for all k ≥ r ≥ 3 k\geq r\geq 3 . In particular, we show that Φ k , r \Phi_{k,r} exhibits a phase transition at approximately k = 4 ​ r − 2 3 k=\frac{4r-2}{3} , a phenomenon not seen in the graph case. As an application of the main result, combined with a classic theorem by Frankl–Füredi–Kalai, we derive general bounds for the analogous problem on minimum positive i i -degrees for all 1 ≤ i < r 1\leq i<r , which are tight for infinitely many cases. Keywords: hypergraph, homomorphism, positive codegree, uniquely colorable.

1\not\in I
and
I\subseteq\overline{\varphi(e)}
, and hence,
I\subseteq\overline{\varphi(e)\cup\{1\}}
.
Additionally, in all three cases we have
\{v\}=\tilde{e}\cap B_{t}
.
Therefore,
N_{\mathcal{H}}(\tilde{e}\setminus\{u\})\cap V_{1}\subseteq B_{1}
.
Furthermore, by Claim
2.23
, we have
N_{\mathcal{H}}(\tilde{e}\setminus\{u\})\cap V_{2}\subseteq B_{2}
.
Therefore, it follows from the definition of
\tilde{e}
and
I
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{either}\quad|B_{1}|>\frac{k-r+1}{k+2}n\quad\mathrm{or}\quad\frac{k-r+1}{k+2}n<d_{\mathcal{H}}(e\setminus\{u\})\leq|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|+\sum_{i\in I}|V_{i}|.

In both cases, we have
\frac{k-r+1}{k+2}n<|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|+\sum_{i\in I}|V_{i}|
.

Let
I\subseteq\overline{\varphi(e)\cup\{1\}}\subseteq K
be the
(k-r-1)
-set guaranteed by Claim
2.24
.
By symmetry, there exists a
(k-r-1)
-set
J\subseteq K
such that

\displaystyle|C_{1}|+|C_{2}|+\sum_{j\in J}|V_{j}|>\frac{k-r+1}{k+2}n.

(11)

Claim 2.25
.

There exists a
(k-r-1)
-set
Q\subseteq K\setminus J
such that
\sum_{j\in Q}|V_{j}|\geq\sum_{j\in I}|V_{j}|
.

Proof of Claim
2.25
.

Note that
|K\setminus J|\geq k-3-(k-r-1)=r-2\geq k-r
(since
k<\frac{4r-2}{3}
).
So there exists a
(k-r)
-set
\hat{Q}\subseteq K\setminus J
.
Fix
i_{\ast}\in\hat{Q}
such that
|V_{i_{\ast}}|=\min\left\{|V_{j}|\colon j\in\hat{Q}\right\}
.
Fix
j_{\ast}\in\overline{\varphi(e)}
such that
|V_{j_{\ast}}|=\min\left\{|V_{j}|\colon j\in\overline{\varphi(e)}\right\}
.
Let
Q\coloneqq\hat{Q}\setminus\{i_{\ast}\}
.
Since
\overline{\varphi(e)}\subseteq K
, it follows from (
9
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in Q}|V_{j}|\geq\sum_{j\in\overline{\varphi(e)}\setminus\{j_{\ast}\}}|V_{j}|=\sum_{j\in\overline{\varphi(e)}}|V_{j}|-\min\left\{|V_{j}|\colon j\in\overline{\varphi(e)}\right\}.

Since
I\subseteq\overline{\varphi(e)}
has size
|\overline{\varphi(e)}|-1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I}|V_{j}|\leq\sum_{j\in\overline{\varphi(e)}}|V_{j}|-\min\left\{|V_{j}|\colon j\in\overline{\varphi(e)}\right\},

which implies that
\sum_{j\in Q}|V_{j}|\geq\sum_{j\in I}|V_{j}|
.

Let
Q\subseteq K\setminus J
be the
(k-r-1)
-set ensured by Claim
2.25
.
Let
R\coloneqq\overline{J\cup Q\cup\{1,2\}}
.
Since
|R|=k-2-(k-r-1)-(k-r-1)=2r-k\geq k-r-1
(by
k<\frac{4r-2}{3}
), it follows from Proposition
2.1

(i)
that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in R}|V_{j}|\geq\frac{2r-k}{k-r+1}\cdot\frac{k-r+1}{k+2}n=\frac{2r-k}{k+2}n.

Combining this with (
10
), (
11
), and Claim
2.25
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[k]}|V_{j}|

\displaystyle=\left(|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|+\sum_{i\in Q}|V_{i}|\right)+\left(|C_{1}|+|C_{2}|+\sum_{i\in J}|V_{i}|\right)+\sum_{i\in R}|V_{i}|

\displaystyle\geq\left(|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|+\sum_{i\in I}|V_{i}|\right)+\left(|C_{1}|+|C_{2}|+\sum_{i\in J}|V_{i}|\right)+\sum_{i\in R}|V_{i}|

\displaystyle>\frac{k-r+1}{k+2}n+\frac{k-r+1}{k+2}n+\frac{2r-k}{k+2}n=n,

a contradiction.
This completes the proof of Proposition
2.4
for the case
k<\frac{4r-2}{3}
.

3
Proofs for Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4

We prove Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
in this section.

We will use the following result, proved by Frankl–Füredi–Kalai
[
FFK88
]
and later extended in
[
LM21
,
LM23
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
FFK88
]
,
[
LM21
, Theorem 1.17]
)
.

Let
k\geq r>i\geq 1
be integers.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a
k
-partite
r
-graph. Then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{\binom{k}{r}}\right)^{1/r}\leq\left(\frac{|\partial_{i}\mathcal{H}|}{\binom{k}{r-i}}\right)^{1/(r-i)}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
n\geq k\geq r>i\geq 1
be integers.
It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Phi_{k,r,i}}{\binom{k-i}{r-i}}\right)^{1/(r-i)}\leq\left(\frac{\Phi_{k,r-1,i}}{\binom{k-i}{r-1-i}}\right)^{1/(r-1-i)}.

Suppose to the contrary that the inequality above is not true.
Then there exists a
k
-partite
n
-vertex
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
with

\displaystyle\delta^{+}_{i}(\mathcal{H})>\binom{k-i}{r-i}\left(\frac{\Phi_{k,r-1,i}}{\binom{k-i}{r-1-i}}\right)^{\frac{r-i}{r-1-i}}n^{r-i}

and without isolated vertices that is not uniquely
k
-colorable.

Take an arbitrary
e\in\partial_{r-i}\mathcal{H}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}
is
k
-partite, the link
L_{\mathcal{H}}(e)
is
(k-i)
-partite.
Note that
\partial L_{\mathcal{H}}(e)=L_{\partial\mathcal{H}}(e)
, so it follows from Theorem
3.1
that

\displaystyle d_{\partial\mathcal{H}}(e)=|L_{\partial\mathcal{H}}(e)|=|\partial L_{\mathcal{H}}(e)|

\displaystyle\geq\binom{k-i}{r-i-1}\left(\frac{|L_{\mathcal{H}}(e)|}{\binom{k-i}{r-i}}\right)^{\frac{r-i-1}{r-i}}

\displaystyle\geq\binom{k-i}{r-i-1}\left(\frac{\delta_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{H})}{\binom{k-i}{r-i}}\right)^{\frac{r-i-1}{r-i}}

\displaystyle>\binom{k-i}{r-i-1}\left(\left(\frac{\Phi_{k,r-1,i}}{\binom{k-i}{r-1-i}}\right)^{\frac{r-i}{r-1-i}}n^{r-i}\right)^{\frac{r-i-1}{r-i}}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{k,r-1,i}\cdot n^{r-1-i}.

This implies that
\partial\mathcal{H}
is a
k
-partite
n
-vertex
(r-1)
-graph with
\delta^{+}_{i}(\partial\mathcal{H})>\Phi_{k,r-1,i}\cdot n^{r-1-i}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}
has no isolated vertices,
\partial\mathcal{H}
has no isolated vertices as well.
Therefore,
\partial\mathcal{H}
is uniquely
k
-colorable. Consequently,
\mathcal{H}
is uniquely
k
-colorbale, a contradiction.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Applying Theorem
1.3
with
(r_{1},r_{2})=(r,i+1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi_{k,r,i}\leq\binom{k-i}{r-i}\left(\frac{\Phi_{k,i+1,i}}{k-i}\right)^{r-i}=\binom{k-i}{r-i}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{k-i}\cdot\max\left\{\frac{k-i}{k+2},\ \frac{3k-3i-2}{3k-2}\right\}\right)^{r-i}.

On the other hand, simply calculations show that the construction
\mathcal{H}_{k,r}(1,m)
defined in Section
1
satisfies

\displaystyle\delta_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{H}_{k,r}(1,m))=\binom{k-i}{r-i}\left(\frac{1}{k+2}\right)^{r-i}\cdot\left(v(\mathcal{H}_{k,r}(1,m))\right)^{r-i}.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{k,r}(1,m)
is not uniquely
k
-colorable, we have

\Phi_{k,r,i}\geq\binom{k-i}{r-i}\left(\frac{1}{k+2}\right)^{r-i}
for all
k\geq r>i\geq 1
.
Therefore,
\Phi_{k,r,i}=\binom{k-i}{r-i}\left(\frac{1}{k+2}\right)^{r-i}
for all
k\geq r>i\geq 1
that satisfy
\frac{k-i}{k+2}\geq\frac{3k-3i-2}{3k-2}
, which is equivalent to
k\leq\frac{4i+2}{3}
.

4
Concluding remarks

In general, given two
r
-graphs
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
, let
\mathrm{SHom}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
denote the collection of all surjective homomorphisms from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{G}
.
We say
\mathcal{H}
is
surjectively
\mathcal{G}
-colorable
if
\mathrm{SHom}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})\neq\emptyset
.
Similarly, an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is
uniquely surjectively
\mathcal{G}
-colorable
if for every pair
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathrm{SHom}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
there exists an automorphism
\eta\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{G})
such that
\eta\circ\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}
.

As one way to extend Theorem
1.1
, Lai
[
Lai87
,
Lai89
]
considered the minimum degree constraint that forces a graph to be uniquely surjectively
C_{2k+1}
-colorable.

Theorem 4.1

(Lai
[
Lai87
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 2k+1
be integers.
Suppose that
G
is a surjectively
C_{2k+1}
-colorable graph on
n
vertices with

\displaystyle\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{k+1}.

Then
G
is uniquely surjectively
C_{2k+1}
-colorable. Moreover, the constant
\frac{1}{k+1}
is optimal.

Extensions of Theorems
1.1
and
4.1
to general graphs were explored in recent work
[
HLZ24
]
, but
K_{k}
and
C_{2k+1}
remain the only two classes of graphs for which tight bounds are known.

One could also explore extensions of Theorems
1.1
and
4.1
to
r
-graphs with
r\geq 3
. In particular, the following question concerning the tight cycles seems durable.

Problem 4.2
.

Let
k\geq r\geq 3
and
1\leq i\leq r-1
be integers.
Determine the minimum real number
\Psi_{k,r,i}
such that for
n\geq k
, every surjectively
C_{k}^{r}
-colorable
r
-graph without isolated vertices, and satisfying
\delta^{+}_{i}(\mathcal{H})>\Psi_{k,r,i}\cdot n^{r-i}
, is uniquely surjectively
C_{k}^{r}
-colorable.
Here,
C_{k}^{r}
denotes the
r
-uniform tight cycle on
k
vertices.

Corollary
1.4
suggests that the extremal constructions for
\Phi_{k,r,i}
, when
k>\frac{4i+2}{3}
, might come from
r
-graphs
\mathcal{H}_{k,r}(\alpha,m)
.
Hence, we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.3
.

Suppose that
k\geq r>i\geq 1
are integers satisfying
k>\frac{4i+2}{3}
.
Then

\displaystyle\Phi_{k,r,i}=\sup\left\{\frac{\delta_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{H}_{k,r}(\alpha,m))}{\left(v(\mathcal{H}_{k,r}(\alpha,m))\right)^{r-i}}\colon\alpha>0~\mathrm{and}~m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\right\}.

Heuristically, Conjecture
4.3
becomes more challenging as
i
decreases, with the hardest case likely being
i=1
.
In the following theorem, we determine
\Phi_{3,3,1}
, providing weak evidence in support of Conjecture
4.3
.

Theorem 4.4
.

We have
\Phi_{3,3,1}=\frac{1}{18}
.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

The lower bound
\Phi_{3,3,1}\geq\frac{1}{18}
comes from the construction
\mathcal{H}_{3,3}(2,m)
.
So it suffices to prove the upper bound.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
3
-partite
3
-graph with
\delta(\mathcal{H})>\frac{n^{2}}{18}
. We will show that
\mathcal{H}
is uniquely
3
-colorable.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist two non-equivalent homomorphisms
\varphi,\vartheta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{H},K_{3}^{3})
.
By symmetry, we may assume that

\displaystyle\max\left\{|\varphi^{-1}(i)|\colon i\in[3]\right\}\geq\max\left\{|\vartheta^{-1}(i)|\colon i\in[3]\right\}.

Let
V_{i}\coloneqq\varphi^{-1}(i)
for
i\in[3]
and by relabelling the vertices in
K_{3}^{3}
, we may assume
|V_{1}|\geq|V_{2}|\geq|V_{3}|
. So
|V_{1}|\geq\frac{n}{3}.

Claim 4.5
.

For every pair of distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in V_{1}
, there exist vertices
u\in V_{2}
and
w\in V_{3}
such that
\{v_{1},u,w\}\in\mathcal{H}
and
\{v_{2},u,w\}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof of Claim
4.5
.

Suppose to the contrary that this is not true.
Then for every
u\in V_{2}
we have
N_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{1}u)\cap N_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{2}u)=\emptyset
.
Notice that
N_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{1}u)\subseteq V_{3}
and
N_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{2}u)\subseteq V_{3}
, hence,

\displaystyle 2\cdot\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{1})+d_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V_{2}}\left(|N_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{1}u)|+|N_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{2}u)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in V_{2}}|V_{3}|=|V_{2}|\cdot|V_{3}|\leq\left(\frac{n-|V_{1}|}{2}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{9},

a contradiction.

It follows from Claim
4.5
that
\vartheta(v_{1})=\vartheta(v_{2})
for every pair
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\subseteq V_{1}
, i.e.
|\vartheta(V_{1})|=1
.
By symmetry, we may assume
\vartheta(V_{1})=\{1\}
.
Let
V_{i,j}\coloneqq V_{i}\cap\vartheta^{-1}(j)
and
n_{i,j}\coloneqq|V_{i,j}|
for
(i,j)\in\{2,3\}\times[3]
.
Note that
n_{2,1}=n_{3,1}=0
, since otherwise we would have
\max\{|\varphi^{-1}(i)|\colon i\in[3]\}<\max\{|\vartheta^{-1}(i)|\colon i\in[3]\}
, a contradiction.
So
\phi^{-1}(1)=V_{1}=\vartheta^{-1}(1)
.

Claim 4.6
.

We have
n_{i,j}>0
for every
(i,j)\in\{2,3\}\times\{2,3\}
.

Proof of Claim
4.6
.

Let us present only the proof for the case
(i,j)=(2,2)
since the other cases are analogous.
Suppose to the contrary that
n_{2,2}=0
.
Then we must have
n_{3,3}>0
since otherwise we would have
\varphi\cong\vartheta
, a contradiction.
Let
v\in V_{3,3}
be a vertex.
Suppose that
\{u,w\}
is an element in
L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
.
Then either
(u,w)\in V_{1}\times V_{2}
or
(w,u)\in V_{1}\times V_{2}
.
By symmetry, we may assume that
(u,w)\in V_{1}\times V_{2}
.
Then
\{\vartheta(w)\}=[3]\setminus\{\vartheta(v),\vartheta(u)\}=\{2\}
, meaning that
w\in V_{2}\cap W_{2}
, contradicting the assumption that
n_{2,2}=0
.

Let
n_{1}\coloneqq|V_{1}|
.
Fix a vertex
u\in V_{2,2}
.
Note that
\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq n_{1}\cdot n_{3,3}
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq n_{1}\cdot n_{i,j}\quad\text{for every}\quad(i,j)\in\{2,3\}\times\{2,3\}.

(12)

Summing them up, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq n_{1}\cdot\frac{n_{2,2}+n_{2,3}+n_{3,2}+n_{3,3}}{4}=\frac{n_{1}(n-n_{1})}{4}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{16}.

Fix a vertex
v\in V_{1}
.
Then

\displaystyle\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\leq n_{2,2}\cdot n_{3,3}+n_{2,3}\cdot n_{3,2}\leq\left(\frac{n_{2,2}+n_{3,3}}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{n_{2,3}+n_{3,2}}{2}\right)^{2}.

Let
x\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{n_{2,2}+n_{3,3}}{2},\frac{n_{2,3}+n_{3,2}}{2}\right\}
and
A\coloneqq\frac{n-n_{1}}{2}
.
Note that
x\leq\frac{n-n_{1}}{4}
and
x\geq\frac{\delta(\mathcal{H})}{n_{1}}
(due to (
12
)).
Since the function
x^{2}+(A-x)^{2}
is decreasing when
x\leq\frac{A}{2}=\frac{n-n_{1}}{4}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq x^{2}+(A-x)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\delta(\mathcal{H})}{n_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{n-n_{1}}{2}-\frac{\delta(\mathcal{H)}}{n_{1}}\right)^{2}.

Rearrange this inequality and using the fact
n_{1}\geq\frac{n}{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(n-n_{1})^{2}n_{1}}{4}+\frac{2\cdot\delta^{2}(\mathcal{H})}{n_{1}}-n\cdot\delta(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{3}}{27}+\frac{6\cdot\delta^{2}(\mathcal{H})}{n}-n\cdot\delta(\mathcal{H})=\frac{6}{n}\left(\delta(\mathcal{H})-\frac{n^{2}}{18}\right)\left(\delta(\mathcal{H})-\frac{n^{2}}{9}\right)<0,

a contradiction.
Here the last inequality is due to the fact
\frac{n^{2}}{18}<\delta(\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{n^{2}}{16}
.
This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Remark.
From the above proof, we see that the unique extremal
3
-graph achieving
\Phi_{3,3,1}=\frac{1}{18}
is the
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}_{3,3}(2,m)
.

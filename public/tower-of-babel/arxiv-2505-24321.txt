Online Fair Allocations with Binary Valuations and Beyond
Yuanyuan Wang Tianze Wei

Abstract
In an online fair allocation problem, a sequence of indivisible items arrives online and needs to be allocated to offline agents immediately and irrevocably. In our paper, we study the online allocation of either goods or chores. We adapt popular fairness notions, including envy-freeness up to one item (EF1) and maximin share fairness (MMS), and use utilitarian social welfare (USW) to measure efficiency. For both settings of items, we present a series of positive results regarding the existence of fair and efficient allocations with widely studied classes of binary and bivalued valuation/cost functions. Additionally, we complement our results by constructing some counterexamples to establish our results as among the best guarantees possible.

Introduction

How to allocate a set of indivisible items
fairly
among a set of agents is a hot topic in various research communities, including algorithmic game theory and artificial intelligence. For decades, there have been tremendous ongoing efforts to design efficient algorithms for finding fair allocations regarding envy-free up to one item (EF1), maximin share fairness (MMS), and some other fairness; detailed progress and results can be found in
[
1
]
.
Most prior work focuses on
offline
setting,
where the set of items is known upfront.
In many practical situations, decisions about the allocation of items must be made online without or with limited knowledge about the future.
A paradigmatic example is the food banks problem
[
2
]
.
In this scenario, food comes online without prior knowledge of future food, and the food is perishable; it must be allocated upon arrival to one of the agents.

In this paper, we study the online problem of allocating a set of items that arrive one by one to a set of agents that are known in advance.
When an item arrives, its values to the agents are revealed, and an immediate and irrevocable decision must be made about which agent to assign the item.
There has been a line of work about online fair allocation, where they consider either additive binary valuations
[
3
]
or normalized valuations
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
Few non-trivial results about the approximation fairness guarantee of online goods allocation beyond additive binary valuations and online chores allocation are known when there is no prior information about the items.
In our paper, we would like to fill this gap and consider the online allocation model without any information about future items, where we discuss the goods and chores settings, respectively.
Our primary research question is

Can we design online algorithms that are fair and efficient, for general valuation functions beyond additive binary, in the online allocation problem?

Allocation of Goods

In this section, we focus on the setting of goods.
Here are some definitions of
fairness and efficiency notions.

Definition 1

(Envy-freeness up to One Good)
.

For any constant
\alpha\in[0,1]
, an allocation
A^{k}
is
\alpha
-approximate envy-free up to one good (
\alpha
-EF1) if, for every pair of agents
i,i^{\prime}\in N
with
A_{i^{\prime}}^{k}\neq\emptyset
, it holds that
v_{i}(A_{i}^{k})\geq\alpha\cdot v_{i}(A_{i^{\prime}}^{k}\setminus\{e\})
for some
e\in A_{i^{\prime}}^{k}
.

Definition 2

(Maximin Share Fairness)
.

For any agent
i
, her maximin share
\mathsf{MMS}_{i}^{k}
by round
t
is defined as:

\mathsf{MMS}_{i}^{k}=\max_{B^{k}\in\Pi_{n}(T^{k})}\min_{i^{\prime}\in N}v_{i}(B_{i^{\prime}}^{k}),

where
\Pi_{n}(T^{k})
is the set of allocations of
T^{k}
.
For any constant
\alpha\in[0,1]
, an allocation
A^{k}
is
\alpha
-approximate maximin share fair (
\alpha
-MMS) if for any agent
i\in N
, it holds that
v_{i}(A_{i}^{k})\geq\alpha\cdot\mathsf{MMS}_{i}^{k}
.

Definition 3

(Utilitarian Social Welfare)
.

The utilitarian social welfare
USW
of allocation
A^{k}
is given by
USW(A^{k})=\sum_{i\in N}v_{i}(A_{i}^{k})
.
For any constant
\alpha\in[0,1]
, an allocation
A^{k}
is
\alpha
-max-USW
if
USW(A^{k})\geq\alpha\cdot\max_{B^{k}\in\Pi_{n}(T^{k})}USW(B^{k})
holds, where
\Pi_{n}(T^{k})
is the set of allocations of
T^{k}
.

In addition to the above definitions, we also pay attention to the following restriction, which is added in the goods setting.

Definition 4

(Non-Wastefulness)
.

An allocation
A^{k}
is non-wasteful (NW) if (1)
\Delta_{A_{i}^{k}\setminus\{e\}}(e)>0
for any
i\in N
and any good

. (2)
\Delta_{A_{i}^{k}}({e}^{{}^{\prime}})=0
for any discarded good
e^{\prime}\in T^{k}
.

In this section, we study the setting of indivisible chores.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

In our paper, we study the fair online allocation of indivisible items and present a series of possible and impossible results regarding the existence and approximation results in fairness and efficiency with various valuation or cost functions, which give a picture of what can be achieved.
For future work, one interesting direction is to fill the gap of the approximation of EF1 and MMS for bi-valued valuation or cost functions when there are more than three agents.
Additionally, based on our results in other online settings, it would also be interesting to study the items with deadline setting in a more complicated valuation or cost function setting.

Appendix B
Other Online Settings

Online algorithms face two roadblocks: unknown future information and the inability to revoke previous decisions. A natural question is how to avoid the worst-case scenario in online algorithms.
In this section, we study two special settings.
One is that each arriving item can wait for one period and does not need to be allocated immediately, which can be found in the online matching literature
[
23
]
.
In this setting, we prove that an EF1 allocation for two agents with bi-valued valuation/cost functions exists.
The other one is knowing some information in advance, which originates from the online MMS allocation with normalized valuations
[
5
]
.
In this setting, we show that the Marginal-Greedy Algorithm computes a non-wasteful EF1 allocation when items are goods, and the Round-Robin algorithm computes a complete EF1 allocation when items are chores.

B.1
Items with Deadline

In this part, each item has a deadline
d
after its arrival.
After
d
units of time, we must decide how to allocate it.
For two agents with bi-valued valuation functions in the goods setting, we design the Adapted Matching Algorithm (Algorithm
12
)
to compute an NW allocation that satisfies EF1.
For two agents with bi-valued cost functions in the chores setting, we design the Adapted Chores Matching Algorithm (Algorithm
13
)
to compute a complete allocation that satisfies EF1.
The common intuition of the above two algorithms is that we find a perfect matching between two agents and two items after two consecutive items arrive so that we can guarantee that there is no envy cycle between two agents and EF1 always holds.

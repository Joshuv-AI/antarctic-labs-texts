Radii of spherical timelike geodesics in Kerr-Newman black holes
Wei Huang 1,2, Jun-Xu Chen 3,4, Jia-Hui Huang 1,2

Abstract
The spherical geodesics of a neutral massive particle around Kerr-Newman black holes are investigated. The existence, radii and radial stability of the equatorial and non-equatorial (particularly, the polar) spherical orbits are discussed for particles with different conserved energy. The radii of these orbits generally are solutions of a quintic polynomial equation with four dimensionless parameters, which are the rotation parameter u u and charge parameter w w of the black hole and the conserved angular momentum β \beta along the black hole rotation axis and conserved energy γ \gamma of the particle. For the critical case where γ = 1 \gamma=1 , we obtain the analytical expressions of the radii of the polar and equatorial orbits, and then derive the analytical radii for the general orbits. The radial stability of the orbits outside the event horizon is also discussed. In the ( u , w , β ) (u,w,\beta) space, a no-orbit surface is found. When the parameters lies on this surface there is no orbit outside the event horizon, otherwise there is always one general spherical orbit outside the event horizon. For the cases with 0 < γ < 1 0<\gamma<1 and γ > 1 \gamma>1 , we focus on the study of polar and equatorial orbits. For polar orbits with 0 < γ < 1 0<\gamma<1 , a boundary surface in ( u , w , γ ) (u,w,\gamma) space is identified which determines the existence of spherical polar orbits outside the event horizon. Numerical results of the radii and radial stability of the polar orbits are shown for examples with specific values of γ \gamma . For polar orbits with γ > 1 \gamma>1 , it is found that there is always one unstable orbit outside the event horizon. For equatorial orbits with 0 < γ < 1 0<\gamma<1 , in each rotating case (prograde case and retrograde case), a boundary surface in ( u , w , γ ) (u,w,\gamma) space is also identified which divides the parameter space into two regions: one region with two orbits (one stable and the other unstable) and the other with no orbit outside the event horizon. Parameters on the boundary surface correspond to ISCOs. An analytical formula for the ISCOs is derived by choosing ( w , γ ) (w,\gamma) as independent variables. For equatorial orbits with γ > 1 \gamma>1 , it is found that there is always one unstable orbit outside the event horizon. Numerical results of the radii and radial stability of the equatorial orbits are also shown for examples with specific values of γ \gamma .



I
Introduction

Black holes are key objects studied in astrophysical and gravitational physics. The first observation of gravitational wave due to merger of black holes was reported by LIGO and Virgo in 2016
Abbott et al. 2016
, which has opened a new era of astrophysics. Significant progress has also been made for observation of black holes via radio waves in recent years. The first image of the supermassive black hole M87* was released by Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) in 2019
Akiyama et al. 2019a
;
Akiyama et al. 2019b
;
Akiyama et al. 2019c
. Subsequently, the image of the supermassive black hole Sagittarius A* in our galaxy center was also released
EventHorizonTelescope:2022xnr
. These images provided us the spectacular appearance of the black holes with their accretion disks.
In the theoretical study of these images, the null and timelike geodesics of the black holes play important roles.

The study of the geodesics around rotating Kerr black holes is significantly simplified due to the discovery of Carter constant
Carter 1968
.
In the equatorial plane of a Kerr black hole, two null geodesics with constant coordinate
r
in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates exist outside the black hole horizon. The inner one corresponds to a prograde orbit and the outer one corresponds to a retrograde orbit
Bardeen et al. 1972
;
Wilkins 1972
;
Teo 2003
.
For circular timelike geodesics in the equatorial plane, the spatial regions where they exist and their stability were discussed in
Pugliese et al. 2011
.
A one-parameter class of solutions describing spherical null geodesics outside the black hole horizon was found in
Teo 2003
. An approximate formula for the radii of these spherical null geodesics was provided in
Hod 2013
, and exact formulas for these spherical null geodesics have been discussed recently
Tavlayan and Tekin 2020
. A special spherical null geodesic, photon boomerang, is identified for nearly extremal Kerr black hole
Page 2022
. The general null geodesics of Kerr black holes can be expressed in terms of elliptic functions
Gralla and Lupsasca 2020
.
The existence of nonequatorial spherical timelike geodesics in extremal Kerr black holes was first discussed in
Wilkins 1972
. Recent studies on the properties and radii of spherical timelike geodesics can be found in
Teo 2021
;
Tavlayan and Tekin 2021
.
Classification of radial timelike geodesic motion of the exterior nonextremal Kerr black hole was performed in
Compère et al. 2022
.
General timelike geodesics of Kerr black holes can also be expressed in terms of elliptic functions
Cieślik et al. 2023
. Kerr black holes admit both stable and unstable/plunging bound geodesics, separated in the parameter space by separatrix. The identification of the critical geodesics and the separatrix was studied in
Levin and Perez-Giz 2009
;
Perez-Giz and Levin 2009
;
Stein and Warburton 2020
.

Kerr-Newman black hole is the most general stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat electrovacuum solution in four-dimensional Einstein-Maxwell theory.
The spherical timelike geodesics in Kerr-Newman black holes was first studied in
Johnston and Ruffini 1974
. The properties of equatorial circular geodesics of neutral particles around Kerr-Newman black holes were studied in
Dadhich and Kale 1977
;
Pugliese et al. 2013
;
Liu et al. 2017
.
The study of timelike geodesics was also extended to nonequatorial cases for neutral
Wang et al. 2022
and charged test particles
Hackmann and Xu 2013
.
Exact solutions of homoclinic orbits for the timelike geodesics of the particle on the general nonequatorial orbits in the Kerr-Newman black holes was presented in
Li et al. 2023
.
The null trajectories and closed photon orbits in Kerr-Newman black holes were studied in
Calvani and Turolla 1981
;
Calvani et al. 1980
;
de Vries 1999
.
A novel optical description of Kerr and Kerr-Newman black holes was provided in
Gal’tsov and Kobialko 2019
.
The light bending in equatorial plane around Kerr-Newman black holes was studied in
Hsiao et al. 2020
, which was also extended to the extremal Kerr-Newman case
Chen et al. 2025
.
Recently, interesting topological arguments have been proposed to prove the existence of planar bound null geodesics around stationary, axisymmetric spacetimes
Cunha and Herdeiro 2020
;
Guo and Gao 2021
;
Wei 2020
;
Ghosh and Sarkar 2021
.

Recently, the spherical timelike geodesics of a neutral test particle around Kerr-Newman black holes have been discussed and the radius of innermost stable spherical orbit is plotted as a function of the Carter constant
Alam et al. 2024
. However, unlike the Kerr black hole case, the radii of spherical timelike orbits around a Kerr-Newman black hole are still lack of systematical study. In a previous study, we discussed the spherical null geodesics around Kerr-Newman black holes, and provided several exact formulas for the radii of these geodesics
Chen et al. 2023
.
In this work, we consider the spherical timelike geodesics of a neutral test particle around a Kerr-Newman black hole, focus on the number of orbits outside the event horizon and the analytical formulas for the radii of orbits as functions of parameters of the black hole and neutral particle.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we review the timelike geodesics around a Kerr-Newman black hole and derive a quintic polynomial equation satisfied by the radii of the spherical timelike orbits. In Section III, we consider the existence and radii of the spherical orbits outside the black hole event horizon for the critical case where the conserved energy parameter
\gamma
of the particle equals to 1. We also consider the radii of spherical orbits for different limiting cases of the Kerr-Newman black hole and check
our results with previous ones in literature. In Section IV, we consider the radii of the timelike spherical orbits for the non-critical cases with
\gamma>1
and
0<\gamma<1
. The last section is devoted to the conclusion.

II
Timelike geodesics of Kerr-Newman black holes

The metric of a Kerr-Newman black hole with mass
M
, angular momentum
J=Ma
, and electric charge
Q
in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates is expressed as follows (we adopt geometric units where
G=c=1
)

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}s^{2}=&-\frac{\Delta}{\Sigma}(\mathrm{d}t-a\sin^{2}{\theta}\mathrm{d}\phi)^{2}+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\Sigma\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}\\
&+\frac{\sin^{2}{\theta}}{\Sigma}[(r^{2}+a^{2})\mathrm{d}\phi-a\mathrm{d}t]^{2}\ ,\end{split}

(1)

where

\Sigma=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}{\theta}\ ,\quad\Delta=r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2}+Q^{2}\ .

(2)

The outer and inner horizons are the roots of
\Delta=0
, which are

r_{\pm}=M\pm\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}-Q^{2}}\ .

(3)

Now we consider a neutral massive particle moving in the Kerr-Newman black hole. For simplicity, we assume the massive particle has unit mass. The Lagrangian of the particle is

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\ ,

(4)

where
u^{\mu}=\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}
is the four-velocity of the particle, and
\tau
is the proper time. For a particle in Kerr-Newman spacetime, there are two Killing vectors,
\xi_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and
\xi_{\phi}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
, which yield two conserved quantities along the geodesics. The first is the energy
E=-p_{t}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{t}}
, representing the energy per unit mass measured by a static observer at infinity. The second is the orbital angular momentum per unit mass along the symmetric axis of the black hole,
L=p_{\phi}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\phi}}
. In fact, there exists another important constant of motion,
C
, known as the Carter constant, which governs the particle’s motion in the
\theta
-direction
Carter 1968
;
Teo 2003
,

C=\Sigma^{2}(u^{\theta})^{2}-a^{2}E^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+L^{2}\cot^{2}{\theta}+a^{2}\cos^{2}{\theta}\ .

(5)

C=0
means the equatorial motion of particle
Hod 2013
;
Tavlayan and Tekin 2020
.

Using the three conserved quantities mentioned above and applying the four-velocity normalization
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1
, we can derive following four first-order equations of geodesic motion for a massive particle in Kerr-Newman spacetime,

\begin{split}\Sigma\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}\tau}&=\pm\sqrt{R(r)},\\
\Sigma\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}\tau}&=\pm\sqrt{\Theta(\theta)},\\
\Sigma\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\mathrm{d}\tau}&=\frac{a}{\Delta}[(r^{2}+a^{2})E-aL]+(\frac{L}{\sin^{2}{\theta}}-aE),\\
\Sigma\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}\tau}&=\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Delta}[(r^{2}+a^{2})E-aL]+a(L-aE\sin^{2}{\theta})\ ,\end{split}

(6)

where

\displaystyle R(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(r^{2}+a^{2})E-aL]^{2}-\Delta[C+(L-aE)^{2}+r^{2}],

\displaystyle\Theta(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C+a^{2}\cos^{2}{\theta}(E^{2}-1)-L^{2}\cot^{2}{\theta}.

(7)

The geodesic motions of the massive particle can be divided into three cases with respect to the value of the conserved energy
E
,

•

0<E<1
: The particle does not have enough energy to escape to infinity and remains in a bounded motion;

•

E=1
: This is a critical case and the particle has just enough energy to escape to infinity;

•

E>1
: The particle has enough energy to escape to infinity.

In this work, we focus on the timelike spherical orbits with a constant radius
r
outside the event horizon. These orbits are determined by the following two equations
Teo 2003
;
Hod 2013
;
Tavlayan and Tekin 2020
;
Bardeen et al. 1972
,

R(r)=0\ ,\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}R(r)}{\mathrm{d}r}=0\ .

(8)

Plugging equation (
II
) into the above equations, we can solve the Carter constant from each equation and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}C=&\frac{1}{M-r}(-a^{2}E^{2}M-a^{2}E^{2}r+a^{2}r+2aLEM\\
&-L^{2}M+L^{2}r-2E^{2}r^{3}-3Mr^{2}+Q^{2}r+2r^{3})\ ,\end{split}

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C=&\frac{1}{-a^{2}+2Mr-Q^{2}-r^{2}}(-2a^{2}E^{2}Mr+a^{2}E^{2}Q^{2}\\
&-a^{2}E^{2}r^{2}+a^{2}r^{2}+4aLEMr-2aLEQ^{2}-2L^{2}Mr\\
&+L^{2}Q^{2}+L^{2}r^{2}-E^{2}r^{4}-2Mr^{3}+Q^{2}r^{2}+r^{4})\ .\end{split}

(10)

To simplify the analysis of the above equations, we introduce the following dimensionless parameters

x\equiv\frac{r}{M},u\equiv\frac{a}{M},w\equiv\frac{Q}{M},\beta\equiv\frac{L}{M},c\equiv\frac{C}{M^{2}},E=\gamma\ .

(11)

These parameters satisfy the following physical constraints (since
Q
appears only in square, we just consider the case with positive charge),

\begin{split}x&\geq 0\ ,\quad 0\leq u\leq 1\ ,\quad 0\leq w\leq 1\ ,\\
0&\leq u^{2}+w^{2}\leq 1\ ,\quad\beta\in\mathbb{R}\ .\end{split}

(12)

The right-hand sides of equations (
9
) and (
10
) must be equal, then we obtain the following quintic equation,

\begin{split}P_{5}(x)=&\left(\gamma^{2}-1\right)x^{5}+\left(4-3\gamma^{2}\right)x^{4}\\
&+\left(2\gamma^{2}u^{2}-2u^{2}+2\gamma^{2}w^{2}-2w^{2}-4\right)x^{3}\\
&+\left(-2\gamma^{2}u^{2}+4u^{2}+2\beta\gamma u+4w^{2}\right)x^{2}\\
&+\left(\gamma^{2}u^{4}-u^{4}-\beta^{2}u^{2}+2\gamma^{2}u^{2}w^{2}\right.\\
&\left.-2u^{2}w^{2}-2\beta\gamma uw^{2}-w^{4}\right)x\\
&+\gamma^{2}u^{4}-2\beta\gamma u^{3}+\beta^{2}u^{2}=0.\end{split}

(13)

The positive real roots of this equation yield the radii of the spherical orbits. Furthermore, by considering the massless limit (
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
), we can arrive at the same equation for the spherical photon orbit
Chen et al. 2023
.

\gamma=1

In this section we consider the case with

\begin{split}&x^{4}-4x^{3}+\left(2u^{2}+2\beta u+4w^{2}\right)x^{2}\\

III.1
Some limiting cases

By setting the rotation parameter
u=0
and the charge parameter

x-4=0\ .

V
conclusion

In this paper, we analytically and numerically study the spherical geodesics of a neutral massive particle around a Kerr-Newman black holes. Starting from the radial geodesics equation of a massive particle in Kerr-Newman spacetime and the two spherical orbit conditions, we derive a quintic equation satisfied by the radii of the spherical orbits, which depends on four parameters, i.e. the rotation parameter
u
and charge parameter
w
of the black hole, the conserved angular momentum parameter
\beta
and conserved energy
\gamma
of the particle. We mainly focus on the analytical formula for the radii of spherical orbits, the number of orbits outside the horizon and the stability of the orbits.
According to the value of the conserved energy
\gamma
of the particle, we discuss three cases with
\gamma=1
,
0<\gamma<1
and
\gamma>1
. The main results we have found are:

•

Spherical polar orbits (
\beta=0
). For the
\gamma=1
case, which we call critical case, there are four or two solutions to the corresponding equation (
22
), but only one unstable orbit is outside the event horizon. The exact analytical formula for the radii are provided as functions of
(u,w)
. We also find the critical curve which separates two regions in
(u,w)
-plane corresponding to different number of solutions. For the
0<\gamma<1
case, it is found that when
\gamma\lesssim 0.92
, there is no spherical orbit outside the event horizon, while for
\gamma\gtrsim 0.92
, there may be two spherical orbits outside the event horizon, and the orbit with a smaller radius is unstable and the one with larger radius is stable. There is a boundary surface in the parameter space
(u,w,\gamma)
. When parameters are chosen on this surface, there is only one spherical orbit outside the event horizon, which is the innermost stable spherical polar orbit (ISSPO). The equation to determine this boundary surface is given in Eq.(
36
). For the
\gamma>1
case, there is only one unstable orbit outside the event horizon.

•

Spherical equatorial orbits (
C=0
). For the
\gamma=1
case, it is found that there are two classes of unstable orbits outside the event horizon in special regions in
(u,\beta)
-plane, shown in Fig.(
10
). One class is the prograde (
\beta>0
) and the other is retrograde (
\beta<0
). Here, we choose
(u,\beta)
as independent variables to obtain analytical formula for the radii of spherical equatorial orbits which are solutions of the quartic equation (
27
). For the
0<\gamma<1
case,
we consider two specific examples with
\gamma=0.5,0.97
. The equation satisfied by the radii of the orbits is Eq.(
39
). When
\gamma=0.5
, it is found that all roots are smaller than the event horizon radius and no spherical orbit is outside the event horizon. When
\gamma=0.97
, it is found that there are two orbits outside the event horizon in each rotating case (prograde case and retrograde case), these two orbits are located on both sides of the ISCO, and the one with smaller radius is unstable while the other one is stable. In each rotating case, there is a boundary surface in
(u,w,\gamma)

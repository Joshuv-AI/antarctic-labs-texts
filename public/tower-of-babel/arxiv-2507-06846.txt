A posteriori error estimates for a C 1 C^{1} virtual element method applied to the thin plate vibration problem.
Franco Dassi, Andrés E. Rubiano, Iván Velásquez

Abstract
We propose and analyse residual-based a posteriori error estimates for the virtual element discretisation applied to the thin plate vibration problem in both two and three dimensions. Our approach involves a conforming C 1 C^{1} discrete formulation suitable for meshes composed of polygons and polyhedra. The reliability and efficiency of the error estimator are established through a dimension-independent proof. Finally, several numerical experiments are reported to demonstrate the optimal performance of the method in 2D and 3D.

Introduction

This paper focuses on the vibration problem of a thin plate modelled by the Kirchhoff-Love equations. In particular, given a bounded simply-connected Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) with boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
. The deflection
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and the vibration frequency
\omega>0
satisfy the following eigenvaule problem, with
\lambda=\omega^{2}
.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \Delta^{2}u&=\lambda u,&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}^{j}u&=0,&&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.1)

where,
\Delta^{2}u:=\Delta(\Delta u)
denotes the fourth-order biharmonic operator. We also introduce the Hessian matrix, which consists of all second-order partial derivatives of
u
, denoted by
\nabla^{2}u
.

On the other hand, the linear differential operator
\mathcal{B}^{j}
(
j=1,2
) involves partial derivatives of the function
u
(e.g.
[
30
, Section 2.3]
).
Specifically, let
\boldsymbol{n}
be the outward unit normal vector to
\Gamma
, and
\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}
the normal derivative. We consider two types of homogeneous boundary conditions:

1.

Simply supported plate (SSP):

\mathcal{B}^{j}u:=\Delta^{j-1}u=0\quad\mbox{on }\Gamma\quad\mbox{ for }j=1,2.

(1.2)

2.

Clamped plate (CP):

\mathcal{B}^{j}u:=\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}^{j-1}u=0\quad\mbox{on }\Gamma\quad\mbox{ for }j=1,2.

(1.3)

There is considerable interest in developing numerical methods for eigenvalue problems, among both practical applications
[
9
]
, and theoretical analysis
[
12
,
13
]
. In particular, conforming finite element methods have been proposed to get the solution of this problem. However, due to the C
1
continuity requirement, it is well known that a high polynomial degree is required to achieve conformity in the discrete space
[
20
]
, which leads to an important computational effort. To avoid this issue, we mention some alternatives applied to the thin plate vibration problem, such as: non-conforming methods
[
3
,
20
,
50
]
, discontinuous schemes
[
25
,
31
]
, and mixed formulations
[
21
,
38
,
44
]
.

The Virtual Element Method (VEM), first introduced in
[
5
,
6
]
, has the flexibility to be applied to more general polygonal/polytopal meshes, and by construction, one can define high regular discrete spaces
[
7
]
. Moreover,
C^{1}
-conforming VEM spaces necessitate only 3 degrees of freedom (DoFs) per vertex when
\Omega
is a bidimensional domain, and 4 DoFs for the three-dimensional case, providing an efficient way to retain the accuracy of the method at low computational cost. We provide a brief, non-exhaustive overview of VEM discretisations for equations involving the biharmonic operator
[
2
,
8
,
14
,
40
,
45
]
, and different eigenvalue problems
[
18
,
24
,
29
,
36
,
37
,
39
,
42
,
46
,
47
,
48
]
.

Adaptive schemes driven by a posteriori error estimators allow optimal convergence recovery in cases such as singular solutions and complex geometries (e.g., non-convex domains and sharp corners), we refer to
[
17
,
26
,
28
,
35
]
for previous work on adaptive FEM for the biharmonic problem. The main advantage of VEMs is its natural handling of hanging nodes during adaptive mesh refinement algorithms,
[
16
,
23
,
43
,
49
]
provide some examples on adaptive VEMs.

Our goal here is to derive a reliable and efficient a posteriori global error estimator
\eta
for (
1.1
), i.e. the following property holds for
\eta
:

C_{1}(\text{error}+\text{oscillation terms})\leq\eta\leq C_{2}(\text{error}+\text{oscillation terms}+\text{higher-order terms}).

Typically, oscillation terms appear in virtual element methods due to the projection and stabilisation required to achieve the computability of virtual functions. On the other hand, high-order terms are negligible when
h\rightarrow 0
.

Two continuous spectral formulations

In this section we introduce two continuous variational formulations associated with the vibration problems of a simply supported and clamped plate (
1.1
).

1.

SSP:
Find
(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times\left(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)

such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{2}u:\nabla^{2}v\ d\Omega=\lambda\int_{\Omega}uv\ d\Omega\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.4)

2.

CP:
Find
(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)

u\neq 0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{2}u:\nabla^{2}v\ d\Omega=\lambda\int_{\Omega}uv\ d\Omega\qquad\forall v\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega).

(2.5)

For notational convenience, we introduce the following bilinear forms:

\displaystyle a:H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad a(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{2}u:\nabla^{2}v\ d\Omega\,,

(2.6)

\displaystyle b:L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\quad b(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}u\,v\ d\Omega\,.

(2.7)

The following result is essential to characterize the spectrum of eigenvalue problems

and

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist positive constants
C
and
\alpha_{\Omega}
such that

\displaystyle|a(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq C||u||_{2,\Omega}||v||_{2,\Omega}

\displaystyle\forall u,v

\displaystyle\in H^{2}(\Omega),

(2.8)

\displaystyle|b(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq C||u||_{0,\Omega}||v||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\forall u,v

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(2.9)

\displaystyle a(v,v)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{\Omega}||v||_{2,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\forall v

\displaystyle\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(2.10)

\displaystyle a(v,v)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{\Omega}||v||_{2,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\forall v

\displaystyle\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega).

(2.11)

Proof.

Standard arguments in Sobolev spaces imply the estimates in (
2.8
) and (

). In addition, the proof of (

) and (

) are obtained from the Poincaré generalised inequality, and the fact that

is equivalent to the usual norm in

, respectively.
∎

Since the bilinear form

is elliptic in both

and

spaces, then the analysis for the problems (

) and (
2.5

to refer to both spaces. In what follows, we are interested in approximating the eigenpair of the following spectral problem.

Problem 1
.

Find
(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times V
, with
u\neq 0
such that

a(u,v)=\lambda b(u,v)\qquad\forall v\in V.

2.1
The continuous source problem

To deal with the a priori and a posteriori error analysis for the spectral Problem
1
, we define the following solution operators

\displaystyle T:V\rightarrow V,\quad f\longmapsto w,

\displaystyle T^{0}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow V\subseteq L^{2}(\Omega),\quad f^{0}\longmapsto w^{0},

where
w:=Tf
and
w^{0}:=T^{0}f
are the two unique solutions associated with their respective source problems

\displaystyle a(w,v)=b(f,v),\qquad

\displaystyle\forall v\in V,

(2.12)

\displaystyle a(w^{0},v)=b(f^{0},v),\qquad

\displaystyle\forall v\in V,

(2.13)

From Lemma
2.1
and the Lax-Milgram theorem, it is easy to check that the linear operators
T
and
T^{0}
are well defined and bounded. Moreover, the following results are standard in the literature of spectral theory of operators in Banach spaces (e.g.
[
3
]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Given
(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times V
, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times V
is a eigenpair of Problem
1
with
u\neq 0
and
\lambda\neq 0
;

2.

(\mu,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times V
is a eigenpair of
T
, where
u\neq 0
and
\mu:=1/\lambda.

Proposition 2.2
.

The operators
T
and
T^{0}
are self-adjoint with respect to the inner products
a(\cdot,\cdot)
in
V
and
b(\cdot,\cdot)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, respectively.

Proof.

Given
v,f\in V
, and
f^{0},g^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we readily see that

\displaystyle a(Tf,v)=b(f,v)=b(v,f)=a(Tv,f)=a(f,Tv),

\displaystyle b(T^{0}f^{0},g^{0})=b(g^{0},T^{0}f^{0})=a(T^{0}g^{0},T^{0}f^{0})=a(T^{0}f^{0},T^{0}g^{0})=b(f^{0},T^{0}g^{0}).

∎

The following lemma gives additional regularity results for the solutions of source problem (
2.12
) and, consequently, for the eigenfunctions of the operator
T
. For simplicity, we only show the results involving the operator
T
, recalling that similar arguments hold also for
T^{0}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exist
s\in(1/2,1]
and a positive constant
C_{\Omega}
depending on
\Omega
such that:
for all
f,f^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the source solutions
w
and
w^{0}
in the equations (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), respectively,
satisfy
w,w^{0}\in H^{2+s}(\Omega)

and

\displaystyle||w||_{2+s,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Omega}||f||_{0,\Omega},

\displaystyle||w^{0}||_{2+s,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Omega}||f^{0}||_{0,\Omega}.

Proof.

Such an estimate follows from classical regularity results for the biharmonic problem; we refer the reader to
[
11
,
32
,
51
]
for further details.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(\lambda,u)
an eigenpair of the vibration of the eigenvalue Problem
1
, then there exist
s>1/2
and a positive
C_{\Omega}^{\lambda}
depending on
\Omega
and
\lambda
such that
u\in H^{2+s}(\Omega)
and

\left\|u\right\|_{2+s,\Omega}\leq C_{\Omega}^{\lambda}\left\|u\right\|_{2,\Omega}\,.

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemma
2.2
with

.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

The operator

is compact.

Proof.

The compact inclusion

, together with Lemma

, imply the result.
∎

As consequence, we have the following spectral characterisation for the eigenvalues of the solution operators

.

Theorem 2.1
.

The spectrum of the operators

and

satisfy

\spec(T)=\{0\}\cup\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\,=\spec(T^{0}),

The virtual spectral approximation

Let
\left\{\Omega_{h}\right\}_{h}
be a sequence of decompositions of
\Omega

into polygons
K
. Let
h_{K}
denote the diameter of the element
K

and

h:=\max_{K\in\Omega_{h}}h_{K}
. Similar to
[
5
]
, we assume that there exists a positive constant
C_{\Omega}
such that

A1
:

the ratio between the shortest edge
and the diameter
h_{K}
of
K
is larger than
C_{\Omega}
.

A2
:

K\in\Omega_{h}
is star-shaped with
respect to every point of a ball
of radius
C_{\Omega}h_{K}
.

In what follows,
we denote by
N_{K}
the number of vertices of
K
,

l
denotes a generic edge of
\Omega_{h}

and for all
l\in\partial K
, we define a unit normal vector
\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}

that points outside of
K
. Moreover, for any polygon
K\in\Omega_{h}
we denote by
\mathcal{E}_{K}
the set of edges of
K
and
\mathcal{E}:=\cup_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\mathcal{E}_{K}
. We also introduce the sets
\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}:=\{l\in\mathcal{E}:l\subset\Gamma\}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}:=\mathcal{E}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}
. Finally, given
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we denote the polynomial space in
d
-variables of degree lower or equal to
k
as
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{O})
.

Next, for all polygon
K\in\Omega_{h}
we consider the local virtual space

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{K}:=\biggl\{v_{h}\in{H^{2}(K)}:

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(K),

\displaystyle v_{h}|_{\partial K}\in C^{0}(\partial K),\,v_{h}|_{l}\in\mathbb{P}_{3}(l),\forall l\in\partial K,

\displaystyle\nabla v_{h}|_{\partial K}\in C^{0}(\partial K)^{2},\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}}v_{h}|_{l}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(l),\forall l\in\partial K\biggr\}.

From here, the enhanced local virtual space is given by:

\displaystyle V_{h}^{K}:=\left\{v_{h}\in\widetilde{V}_{K}:\int_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}v_{h})p_{2}\ dK=\int_{K}v_{h}p_{2}\ dK,\,\forall p_{2}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(K)\right\},

where, the polynomial projection operator
\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}:\widetilde{V}_{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{2}(K)\subseteq\widetilde{V}_{K}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \int_{K}\nabla^{2}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}v_{h}-v_{h})\cdot\nabla^{2}p_{2}~\textrm{d}K&=0,&&\forall p_{2}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(K),\\
\int_{\partial K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}v_{h}-v_{h})p_{1}~\textrm{d}l&=0,&&\forall p_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

We are ready to present the global virtual space: for every
decomposition
\Omega_{h}
of
\Omega
into simple polygons
K
, we define

V_{h}:=\left\{v_{h}\in V:\ v_{h}|_{K}\in V_{h}^{K}\right\}.

It is well known (see
[
14
]
) that any virtual function
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{K}
and
\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}v_{h}

are uniquely determined by the following degrees of freedom:

\displaystyle{\bf D_{1}}:\text{Evaluation of $v_{h}$ at the vertexes of $K$},

\displaystyle{\bf D_{2}}:\text{Evaluation of $\nabla v_{h}$ at the vertexes of $K$.}

Notice that this definition implies that only 3 DoFs are required for this space. In order to write the discrete spectral problem we define

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}):=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}a_{h,K}(u_{h},v_{h}),\qquad b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}):=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}b_{h,K}(u_{h},v_{h}),\qquad\forall u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h},

where
a_{h,K}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
b_{h,K}(\cdot,\cdot)

are the local discrete bilinear forms on

V_{h}^{K}\times V_{h}^{K}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} a_{h,K}(u_{h},v_{h})&:=a_{K}\big(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}v_{h}\big)+s_{K}^{\Delta}\big(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},v_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}v_{h}\big),&&\forall u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}^{K},\\
b_{h,K}(u_{h},v_{h})&:=b_{K}\big(\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h},\Pi_{K}^{0}v_{h}\big)+s_{K}^{0}\big(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h},v_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}v_{h}\big),&&\forall u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}^{K},\end{aligned}

(3.1)

with
\Pi_{K}^{0}:L^{2}(K)\to\mathbb{P}_{2}(K)
is the standard
L^{2}-
projection and
s_{K}^{\Delta}
and
s_{K}^{0}
are any symmetric positive bilinear forms satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \alpha_{*}a_{K}(v_{h},v_{h})&\leq s_{K}^{\Delta}(v_{h},v_{h})\leq\alpha^{*}a_{K}(v_{h},v_{h}),&&\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{K}\cap\ker(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}),\\
\beta_{*}b_{K}(v_{h},v_{h})&\leq s_{K}^{0}(v_{h},v_{h})\leq\beta^{*}b_{K}(v_{h},v_{h}),&&\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{K}\cap\ker(\Pi_{K}^{0}),\end{aligned}

with
\alpha_{*},\alpha^{*},\beta_{*}
and
\beta^{*}
positive constants independent of
h_{K}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The polynomial projections
\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}v_{h}
and
\Pi_{K}^{0}v_{h}
coincide in the lowest order case, for all

(see

Lemma 3.1
.

The following relations hold:

1.

Consistency: for all
h>0
and for all
K\in\Omega_{h}
, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} a_{h,K}(p_{2},v_{h})&=a_{K}(p_{2},v_{h}),&&\forall p_{2}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(K),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{K},\\

Stability and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \min\{1,\alpha_{*}\}a_{K}(v_{h},v_{h})&\leq a_{K,h}(v_{h},v_{h})\leq\max\{1,\alpha^{*}\}a_{K}(v_{h},v_{h}),&&\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}(K)\cap\ker(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}),\\

.

The following estimates hold

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})&\geq\alpha_{\Omega}\min\{1,\alpha_{*}\}||v_{h}||_{2,\Omega}^{2},&&\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},\\

We start by recalling some well-known interpolation theorems commonly used in the literature on virtual element methods.

Proposition 3.4

If the assumption

Remark 3.2
.

The classical spectral theory for compact operators developed in

]

. However, since stabilisation terms

. To overcome this issue, we consider the orthogonal projection

\mathcal{P}_{h}:V\to V_{h}
given by
f\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{h}f
as the unique solution of the following system of equations

\displaystyle b(\mathcal{P}_{h}f-f,v_{h})=0,\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Moreover, for all
f\in V
the following holds

\displaystyle||f-\mathcal{P}_{h}f||_{0,\Omega}=\inf\limits_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}||f-v_{h}||_{0,\Omega},\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Next, we introduce the projector
\widehat{T}_{h}:=T_{h}\mathcal{P}_{h}:V\to V_{h}\subseteq V
. Then, the spectrum of
\widehat{T}_{h}
satisfies
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(\widehat{T}_{h})=\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(T_{h})\cup\{0\}
and the eigenfunctions of operators
\widehat{T}_{h}
and
T
are the same (e.g.
[
41
]
). Now, the following result establishes the convergence of
\widehat{T}_{h}\to T
and
\widehat{T}_{h}\to T^{0}
when
h\to 0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exist a positive constant independent of the parameter
h
and
s\in(1/2,1]
such that

\displaystyle||(T-\widehat{T}_{h})f||_{2,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}||f||_{2,\Omega},

\displaystyle\quad\forall f\in V,

(3.3a)

\displaystyle||(T^{0}-\widehat{T}_{h})f||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}||f||_{0,\Omega},

\displaystyle\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(3.3b)

Proof.

The proof for (
3.3a
) was established in
[
48
]
(see also
[
24
, Proposition 5]
). Now, let us prove (
3.3b
). Given
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, consider
T^{0}f=w^{0}\in V
and
\widehat{T}_{h}f=T_{h}\mathcal{P}_{h}f=w_{h}\in V_{h}
as the unique solutions of the source problems
a(w^{0},v)=b(f,v)\ \forall v\in V,
(cf. (
2.13
)) and
a_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h})\ \forall v_{h}\in V_{h}
(cf. (
3.2
)), respectively. Then, by using the same steps as those applied in
[
24
, Theorem 5.1]
we obtain

\displaystyle|w^{0}-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h})-b(f,v)-\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{a_{h,K}(w_{I}^{0}-w_{\pi}^{0},v_{h})+a_{K}(w_{\pi}^{0}-w^{0},v_{h})\Big\}.

(3.4)

Note that

\displaystyle b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h})-b(f,v)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{b_{h,K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h}-(v_{h})_{\pi})-b_{K}(f,v_{h})+b_{h,K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,(v_{h})_{\pi})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{b_{h,K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h}-(v_{h})_{\pi})-b_{K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h})+b_{h,K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,(v_{h})_{\pi})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{b_{h,K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h}-(v_{h})_{\pi})-b_{K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h}-(v_{h})_{\pi})\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}||\mathcal{P}_{h}f||_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(||v_{h}-(v_{h})_{\pi}||_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}||\mathcal{P}_{h}f||_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}h^{2}||v_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\Big(\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}||f||_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}||v_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}||f||_{0,\Omega}||v_{h}||_{0,\Omega}.

(3.5)

Moreover,
[
24
, Theorem 5.1]
leads to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{a_{h,K}(w_{I}^{0}-w_{\pi}^{0},v_{h})+a_{K}(w_{\pi}^{0}-w^{0},v_{h})\Big\}\leq Ch^{s}||f||_{0,\Omega}||v_{h}||_{2,\Omega}.

(3.6)

Thus, by including the estimates (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) in right-hand side of (
3.4
) we get

\displaystyle||(\widehat{T}_{h}-T^{0})f||_{0,\Omega}=||w^{0}-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\leq||w^{0}-w_{h}||_{2,\Omega}\leq|w^{0}-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}\leq Ch^{s}||f||_{0,\Omega}.

(3.7)

∎

The following lemma establishes an

-norm error estimate for the source problem associated with the operator

, which will be useful in a posteriori error analysis.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of parameter
h
such that for all
f\in R(E)
, if
w=Tf
and
w_{h}=\widehat{T}_{h}f=T_{h}\mathcal{P}_{h}f
, then

\displaystyle||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,h}+||w-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\Big\}.

Proof.

Let
f\in R(E)
, if
w=Tf
and
w_{h}=\widehat{T}_{h}f=T_{h}\mathcal{P}_{h}f
. Consider
v\in V
the unique solution of

\displaystyle a(v,z)=b(w-w_{h},z)\qquad\forall z\in V.

(3.12)

From the definition of
T
we have that
T(w-w_{h})=v
, and using Lemma
2.2
results

satisfying

\displaystyle||v||_{2+s,\Omega}\leq C||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega},

(3.13)

for some

.

On the other hand we consider
v_{I}\in V_{h}
like in the Proposition
3.5
. Thus, testing the equation (
3.12
) with

we have

\displaystyle||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}^{2}=b(w-w_{h},w-w_{h})=a(v,w-w_{h})=a(w-w_{h},v-v_{I})+a(w-w_{h},v_{I})

\displaystyle\leq|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}|v-v_{I}|_{2,\Omega}+a(w-w_{h},v_{I})

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}|v|_{2+s,\Omega}+\Big\{a_{h}(w_{h},v_{I})-a(w_{h},v_{I})+b(f,v_{I})-b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{I})\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+\Big\{a_{h}(w_{h},v_{I})-a(w_{h},v_{I})+b(f,v_{I})-b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{I})\Big\}.

(3.14)

Next, note that using the properties of consistence and stability for

, Proposition
3.5
, (
3.13
), and Proposition
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{h}(w_{h},v_{I})-a(w_{h},v_{I})=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{a_{h,K}(w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h},v_{I}-v_{\pi})+a(w_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h},v_{I}-v_{\pi})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|w_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|v_{I}-v_{\pi}|_{2,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|w-w_{h}|_{2,K}^{2}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|v-v_{I}|_{2,K}^{2}+|v-v_{\pi}|_{2,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|w-w_{h}|_{2,K}^{2}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}|v|_{2+s,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|w-w_{h}|_{2,K}^{2}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,h}\Big\}||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}.

(3.15)

Besides, using the fact that
f\in R(E)
,
w=Tf=\mu f
, the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{h}
, the properties of consistence and stability of
b(\cdot,\cdot)
, (
3.13
), and Lemma
2.2
we deduce

\displaystyle b(f,v_{I})-b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{I})=b(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{I})-b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{I})=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{b_{K}(f,v_{I})-b_{h,K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{I})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{b_{K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f-\mu^{-1}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h},v_{I}-v_{\pi})-b_{h,K}(\mathcal{P}_{h}f-\mu^{-1}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h},v_{I}-v_{\pi})\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}||\mathcal{P}_{h}f-\mu^{-1}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}||_{0,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}(||v-v_{I}||_{0,K}^{2}+||v-v_{\pi}||_{0,K}^{2})\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}||\mathcal{P}_{h}f-\mu^{-1}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}||_{0,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}(h^{4}||v||_{2+s,K}^{2}+h^{2(2+s)}||v||_{2+s,K}^{2})\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}||\mathcal{P}_{h}f-\mu^{-1}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}||_{0,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}|v|_{2+s,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\mu^{-1}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}||\mathcal{P}_{h}w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}||_{0,K}^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\mu^{-1}\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}(||w-\mathcal{P}_{h}w||_{0,K}^{2}+||w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}||_{0,K}^{2})\Big\}^{1/2}||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\Big\{||w-\mathcal{P}_{h}w||_{0,\Omega}+||w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\Big\}||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\Big\{||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+||w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\Big\}||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}.

(3.16)

Therefore, inserting the estimates (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) in (
3.14
), and multiplying by
||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}^{-1}
we have

\displaystyle||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,h}\Big\}+Ch^{2}\Big\{||w-w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+||w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,h}+||w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\Big\}.

∎

3.4
Error estimates on
L^{2}
-norm for the eigenfunctions

In this section, we present an error estimation result for eigenfunctions in the
L^{2}
-norm that will be used to demonstrate reliability in a posteriori error estimation. To this end, we first introduce the proof of an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(\mu_{h}^{(j)},u_{h})
be an eigenpair of
\widehat{T}_{h}
with
j=1,2,...,m
and
||u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}=1
. Then, there exist an eigenfunction
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
of
T
associated to
\mu
, and a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle||u-u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+||w-\Pi_{K}^{0}w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,h}\Big\}

(3.17)

Proof.

From (
3.3b
) and the classical theory for compact operators presented in
[
3
]
we know that
sp(\widehat{T}_{h})
converges to
sp(T^{0})
. Moreover, from the relation between the eigenfunctions of
T
and
T_{h}
with those of
T^{0}
and
\widehat{T}_{h}
, respectively, we obtain that
u_{h}\in R(E_{h})
and there exists
u\in R(E)
such that

\displaystyle||u-u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\leq C\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{0}\in R(E^{0}),||f^{0}||_{0,\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}||(T^{0}-\widehat{T}_{h})f^{0}||_{0,\Omega}.

In addition, we have from Lemma
3.4
that for each
f^{0}\in R(E^{0})
satisfying
f^{0}=f
implies

\displaystyle||(T^{0}-\widehat{T}_{h})f^{0}||_{0,\Omega}=||(T-\widehat{T}_{h})f||_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,h}+||w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\Big\}.

Putting together about inequalities finish the proof.
∎

Now, we prove the
L^{2}
-norm error estimate for the eigenfunctions of the virtual spectral discretization analysed in this work.

Theorem 3.5
.

There exist
s\in(1/2,1]
and a positive constant
C
independent of parameter
h
such that

\displaystyle||u-u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch^{s}\Big\{|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+||u-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+|u-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,h}\Big\}.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by bounding each term on the right-hand side of (
3.17
) in Lemma
3.5
. We first consider
w\in V
and
w_{h}\in V_{h}
as the solutions of problems

\displaystyle a(w,v)=b(u,v),\quad a_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}u,v_{h}),\quad\forall v\in V,\,\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

(3.18)

respectively. Since
b(u,v)=\frac{1}{\lambda}a(u,v)
(cf. Problem
1
) we get
w=\frac{1}{\lambda}u
with
\lambda\neq 0
. Thus,

\displaystyle|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-1}|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+|\lambda^{-1}-\lambda_{h}^{-1}||u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+|\lambda^{-1}u_{h}-w_{h}|.

(3.19)

Note that, from estimate
3.11b
we obtain

\displaystyle|\lambda^{-1}-\lambda_{h}^{-1}|=|\lambda\lambda_{h}^{-1}||\lambda-\lambda_{h}|\leq C\Big(||u-u_{h}||_{2,\Omega}^{2}+||u-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}^{2}+|u-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,h}^{2}\Big).

(3.20)

On the other hand, we know that

(cf. Problem
2
). Hence, subtracting this equality from (
3.18
) we obtain

\displaystyle a_{h}(w_{h}-\lambda^{-1}u_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}u-u_{h},v_{h})\ \forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Therefore, using the fact that
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is
V_{h}-
elliptic, we can deduce

\displaystyle|w_{h}-\lambda^{-1}u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=a_{h}(w_{h}-\lambda^{-1}u_{h},w_{h}-\lambda^{-1}u_{h})=Cb_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}u-u_{h},w_{h}-\lambda^{-1}u_{h})

\displaystyle\leq C||\mathcal{P}_{h}u-u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}||w_{h}-\lambda^{-1}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\leq C||\mathcal{P}_{h}(u-u_{h})||_{0,\Omega}||w_{h}-\lambda^{-1}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle|w_{h}-\lambda^{-1}u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}.

(3.21)

Then, replacing (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) in the right-hand side of (

) lead to

\displaystyle|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}\leq C\Big(||u-u_{h}||_{2,\Omega}+||u-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+|u-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,h}\Big).

(3.22)

) we apply the triangle inequality and we have

\displaystyle||w-\Pi_{K}^{0}w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+||w_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}.

(3.23)

Next, for the second term in (

), we obtain

\displaystyle||w_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq||w_{h}-\lambda_{h}^{-1}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+\lambda_{h}^{-1}||u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+||\Pi_{K}^{0}(\lambda_{h}^{-1}u_{h}-w_{h})||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq 2|w_{h}-\lambda_{h}^{-1}u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+\lambda_{h}^{-1}||u-u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+\lambda_{h}^{-1}||u-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{||u-u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+||u-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\Big\}.

(3.24)

Thus, replacing (
3.22
) and (
3.24
) in (
3.23
) imply that

\displaystyle||w-\Pi_{K}^{0}w_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\leq C\Big\{|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+||u-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}+|u-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,h}\Big\}.

(3.25)

The third term on the right-hand side of (
3.17
) is bounded by following the same arguments as those applied to obtain (

). Therefore,

\displaystyle|w-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,h}

\displaystyle\leq|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+|w_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,h}=|w-w_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|w_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}w_{h}|_{2,K}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}+||u-u_{h}||_{2,\Omega}+|u-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,h}\Big\}

(3.26)

Finally, substituting (
3.22
), (
3.25
) and (
3.26
) on the right-hand side of (
3.17
) concludes the proof.
∎

4
A posteriori error analysis

This section is dedicated to constructing a residual-type estimator that depends only on quantities available from the VEM solution, and to establishing its reliability and efficiency. To achieve this, we first present some definitions and local estimates.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
l,m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
l\leq m
. Then, for any polygon
K
, there exists
C>0
, depending only on
k,l,m
and the shape of
K
, such that

|q|_{m,K}\leq ch_{K}^{n-m}|q|_{n,K}\qquad\forall q\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K).

(4.1)

To construct a suitable residual-based error estimator, we precise the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
v\in V
, the following result is obtained

\displaystyle a(e,v)

\displaystyle=\lambda b(u,v)-\lambda_{h}b(u_{h},v)+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{a_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}-u_{h},v)+\lambda_{h}b_{K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},v)\Big\}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\int_{K}\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}v-\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\int_{l}[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]\cdot\nabla v.

Proof.

Given any
v\in V
and using the fact that
(\lambda,u)
solves the problem (
1
) and integrating by parts, one has

\displaystyle a(e,v)=\lambda b(u,v)-\lambda_{h}b(u_{h},v)+\lambda_{h}b(u_{h},v)-a(u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=\lambda b(u,v)-\lambda_{h}b(u_{h},v)+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{a_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}-u_{h},v)+\lambda_{h}b_{K}(u_{h},v)-a_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},v)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\lambda b(u,v)-\lambda_{h}b(u_{h},v)+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{a_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}-u_{h},v)+\lambda_{h}b_{K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},v)

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{h}b_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},v)\Big\}-\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Bigg\{\int_{\partial K}\Big[\nabla^{2}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}\Big]\cdot\nabla v-\int_{\partial K}v\Big[\mathrm{div}\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}\Big]\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\lambda b(u,v)-\lambda_{h}b(u_{h},v)+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{a_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}-u_{h},v)+\lambda_{h}b_{K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},v)\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\int_{K}\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}v-\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\int_{l}[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]\cdot\nabla v.

∎

We define the following local error, volume, jump, and stabilisation estimators for all
K\in\Omega_{h}
.

\displaystyle\eta_{K}^{2}:=\Xi_{K}^{2}+\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\mathcal{J}_{l}^{2}+S_{K}^{2},

\displaystyle\Xi_{K}^{2}:=h_{K}^{4}||\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,K}^{2},\quad\mathcal{J}_{l}^{2}:=h_{l}||[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]||_{0,l}^{2},

\displaystyle S_{K}^{2}:=s_{K}^{\Delta}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})+s_{K}^{0}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h})

In addition, we note that
\mathcal{J}_{l}
is computable only on the basis of the output values of the operators in
{\bf D_{2}}
. Finally, our respective global error, volume, jump, and stabilisation estimators are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\eta^{2}:=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\eta_{K}^{2},\quad\Xi^{2}:=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Xi_{K}^{2},\quad\mathcal{J}^{2}:=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\mathcal{J}_{l}^{2},\quad\text{and}\quad S^{2}:=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}S_{K}^{2}.

(4.2)

4.1
Reliability

The following result is important to establish the reliability of the a posteriori error estimator.

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of parameter
h
such that

|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}\leq C\Big\{\eta+\frac{\lambda+\lambda_{h}}{2}||u-u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}\Big\}

(4.3)

Proof.

Let
e=u-u_{h}\in V_{h}\subseteq V
, then there exists
e_{I}\in V_{h}
(cf. Proposition
3.5
) such that

|e-e_{I}|_{t,\Omega}\leq Ch^{s-t}|e|_{s,\Omega},\quad s=2,3,\quad t=0,1,...,s

(4.4)

From Lemma
4.2
, we have that

\displaystyle|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}^{2}=a(e,e-e_{I})+a(u,e_{I})-a_{h}(u_{h},e_{I})+a_{h}(u_{h},e_{I})-a(u_{h},e_{I})

\displaystyle=\lambda b(u,e-e_{I})-\lambda_{h}b(u_{h},e-e_{I})+a(u,e_{I})-\lambda_{h}b_{h}(u_{h},e_{I})+a_{h}(u_{h},e_{I})-a(u_{h},e_{I})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\int_{K}\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}(e-e_{I})+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\int_{l}-[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]\cdot\nabla(e-e_{I})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}a_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}-u_{h},e-e_{I})+\lambda_{h}\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}b_{K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},e-e_{I})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\lambda b(u,e)-\lambda_{h}b(u_{h},e)\Big\}+\lambda_{h}\Big\{b(u_{h},e_{I})-b_{h}(u_{h},e_{I})\Big\}+\Big\{a_{h}(u_{h},e_{I})-a(u_{h},e_{I})\Big\}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{4}E_{j},

(4.5)

where the terms
E_{j}
(
j=1,\dots,4
) are given by:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle E_{1}&:=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}a_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}-u_{h},e-e_{I}),\quad\displaystyle&&E_{2}:=\lambda_{h}\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}b_{K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},e-e_{I}),\\
\displaystyle E_{3}&:=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\int_{K}\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}(e-e_{I}),\quad\displaystyle&&E_{4}:=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\int_{l}-[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]\cdot\nabla(e-e_{I}).\end{aligned}

Now, let us find upper bounds on each term in (
4.5
). In fact, if
(\lambda,u)\neq(0,0)
is an eigenpair of
T
with
\lambda
a simple eigenvalue, we select
||w||_{0,\Omega}=1
so that for each mesh
\Omega_{h}
, the solution
(\lambda_{h},u_{h})
of the Problem
2
satisfy
||u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}=1
and
|\lambda-\lambda_{h}|,|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}\to 0
. Thus, following
[
42
, Lemma 3.4]
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda b(u,e)-\lambda_{h}b_{h}(u_{h},e)

\displaystyle=\lambda||u||_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\lambda_{h}||u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}^{2}-\lambda_{h}b(u,u_{h})

\displaystyle=C\frac{\lambda+\lambda_{h}}{2}||e||_{0,\Omega}^{2}\leq C\frac{\lambda+\lambda_{h}}{2}||e||_{0,\Omega}|e|_{2,\Omega}.

(4.6)

On the other hand, using that
b(\cdot,\cdot),b_{h,K}(\cdot,\cdot)
are scalar products, Lemma
3.1
, and (
4.4
), provide the following bound

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}\Big\{b(u_{h},e_{I})-b_{h}(u_{h},e_{I})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{h}\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Big\{b_{K}(u_{h},e_{I})-b_{h,K}(u_{h},e_{I})\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda_{h}|\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Bigg\{b_{K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})^{1/2}b_{K}(e_{I},e_{I})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\,b_{h,K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})^{1/2}b_{h,K}(e_{I},e_{I})^{1/2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}b_{h,K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\Big\}^{1/2}||e_{I}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}b_{h,K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\Big\}^{1/2}|e|_{2,\Omega}.

(4.7)

Following the same arguments, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},e_{I})-a(u_{h},e_{I})\leq C\Big\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}a_{K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\Big\}^{1/2}|e|_{2,\Omega}.

(4.8)

Next, (
4.4
) and Lemma
3.1
imply that

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}a_{K}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})^{1/2}|e|_{2,K}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}s_{K}^{\Delta}(u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})^{1/2}|e|_{2,K}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}S_{K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}|e|_{2,\Omega}.

(4.9)

Similarly, (
4.4
) and Lemma
3.1
lead to

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}b_{K}(\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}-u_{h},e-e_{I})\leq C\Big(\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}S_{K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}|e|_{2,\Omega}.

(4.10)

To bound
E_{3}
, we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
4.4
) to obtain

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}||\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,K}||e-e_{I}||_{0,K}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}h_{K}^{4}||\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}||e||_{2,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}h_{K}^{4}||\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}|e|_{2,\Omega}

\displaystyle=C\Big(\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\Xi_{K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}|e|_{2,\Omega}

(4.11)

For

, the trace inequality for

and (

) provide that

\displaystyle E_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\Big|\Big|[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]\Big|\Big|_{0,l}||\nabla(e-e_{I})||_{0,K}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\Big|\Big|[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]\Big|\Big|_{0,l}\Big\{h_{K}^{-1/2}||\nabla(e-e_{I})||_{0,K}||+h_{K}^{1/2}|\nabla(e-e_{I})|_{1,K}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}h_{K}^{1/2}\Big|\Big|[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]\Big|\Big|_{0,l}||\nabla e||_{1,K}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}h_{K}\Big|\Big|[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]\Big|\Big|_{0,l}^{2}\Bigg\}^{1/2}||\nabla e||_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{\sum\limits_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}}\mathcal{J}_{l}^{2}\Bigg\}||e||_{2,\Omega}\leq\mathcal{J}^{2}||e||_{2,\Omega}\leq C_{P}\mathcal{J}^{2}|e|_{2,\Omega},

(4.12)

where, in the last step we used the Poincaré inequality.

Finally, the proof finishes by replacing (
4.6
)-(
4.12
) in (
4.5
) and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

\displaystyle\quad+||u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,K}||\hat{v}||_{0,K}+||\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,K}||\hat{v}||_{0,K}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{K=T^{+},T^{-}}\Bigg\{\Big(h_{K}^{-2}|e|_{2,K}+||\lambda u-\lambda_{h}u_{h}||_{0,K}+h_{K}^{-2}|u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,K}

\displaystyle\quad+||u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,K}+||\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,K}\Big)||\hat{v}||_{0,K}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{K=T^{+},T^{-}}\Bigg\{\Big(h_{K}^{-1/2}|e|_{2,K}+h_{K}^{3/2}||\lambda u-\lambda_{h}u_{h}||_{0,K}+h_{K}^{-1/2}|u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,K}

\displaystyle\quad+h_{K}^{3/2}||u_{h}-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,K}+h_{K}^{3/2}||\lambda_{h}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}||_{0,K}\Big)||[\![(\nabla^{2}\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h})\boldsymbol{n}_{K}^{l}]\!]||_{0,l}\Bigg\}.

Finally, Lemma
3.1
, the definition of
S_{K}
and Lemma
4.3
conclude the proof.
∎

The efficiency of
\eta_{K}
is provided next.

Theorem 4.3
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of the parameter
h
such that

\displaystyle\eta_{K}^{2}\leq C\sum\limits_{K^{\prime}\in\omega_{K}}\Big\{|u-u_{h}|_{2,K^{\prime}}^{2}+||u-\Pi_{K^{\prime}}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,K^{\prime}}^{2}+|u-\Pi_{K^{\prime}}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,K^{\prime}}^{2}+h^{4}||\lambda u-\lambda_{h}u_{h}||_{0,K^{\prime}}^{2}\Big\}

Proof.

The result is obtained from Lemmas
4.3
-
4.5
.
∎

Finally, the term
h_{K}^{2}||\lambda u-\lambda_{h}u_{h}||_{0,K^{\prime}}
is asymptotically negligible for the global estimator
\eta
, this result is provided below.

Corollary 4.3
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of the parameter
h
such that

\displaystyle\eta^{2}\leq C\Big\{|u-u_{h}|_{2,\Omega}^{2}+||u-\Pi_{K}^{0}u_{h}||_{0,\Omega}^{2}+|u-\Pi_{K}^{\Delta}u_{h}|_{2,h}^{2}\Big\}.

Proof.

The proof follows with the same arguments as those applied in
[
41
, Corollary 5.14]
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

We refer the reader to

]
for more details about the construction of the C

4.2
includes a boundary term (c.f. the jump estimator

The numerical implementation is done with the library
VEM++

[

where the VE space presented in Section

is available. To solve the generalised eigenvalues problem arising from such discretizations,
VEM++
contains a wrapper of the C++ library SLEPC
[
33
]
with the problem type option
EPS--GHEP
. Regarding the adaptive algorithm, we follow the usual strategy

\textnormal{SOLVE}\rightarrow\textnormal{ESTIMATE}\rightarrow\textnormal{MARK}\rightarrow\textnormal{REFINE}

where the SOLVE and ESTIMATE steps are performed inside
VEM++
using SLEPC as a solver for the linear system, in this way, we exploit the high-speed computation capabilities of C++. Next, for the 2D REFINE step, we use the Matlab-based method from
[
53
]
, which connects edge mid-points to the polygon barycenter. On the other hand, the 3D adaptive refinement routine operates on cubical meshes with support for hanging nodes, making use of the
p4est
library
[
15
]
via the
GridapP4est
module of the Julia package
Gridap

[
4
]
. We recall that the refinement procedure is independent of the
VEM++
capabilities. Therefore, the implementation presented in this paper can be extended to more general (non-convex) polygonal shapes that satisfy
A1
,
A2
by selecting an appropriate refinement routine. Finally, for the MARK procedure, we follow a Dörfler/Bulk marking strategy, marking the subset of mesh elements
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{h}
with the largest estimated errors such that for
\delta=\frac{1}{2}\in[0,1]
, we have

\delta\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\eta_{K}^{2}\leq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\eta_{K}^{2}.

The experimental order of convergence
r(*)
against the total number of degrees of freedom

applied to either the error
|\lambda_{i,h}-\lambda_{i}|
or the estimator
\eta
, and the effectivity index
eff

1\leq j
for

are computed as

r(*)_{j+1}=-d\log\left(*_{j+1}/*_{j}\right)/\log\left(\textnormal{\#DoFs}_{j+1}/\textnormal{\#DoFs}_{j}\right)
,
\quad\textnormal{eff}_{j}=\eta^{2}_{j}/|\lambda_{i,h}-\lambda_{i}|_{j}

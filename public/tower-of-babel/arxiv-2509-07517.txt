1.
Introduction and statement of the result

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
without boundary, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
2\leq 2k<n
. We consider families of function
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha>0}\in C^{2k}(M)
that are solutions to

(1)

P_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}=|u_{\alpha}|^{2^{\star}-2}u_{\alpha}\hbox{ in }M,

where
2^{\star}:=\frac{2n}{n-2k}
, and
(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha>0}
is a family of elliptic symmetric differential operators of the type

(2)

P:=\Delta_{g}^{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\nabla^{i}(A^{(i)}\nabla^{i})\hbox{ for }\alpha>0

where
\Delta_{g}:=-\hbox{div}_{g}\nabla
is the Riemannian Laplacian with minus-sign convention and for all
i=0,...,k-1
,
A^{(i)}\in C^{i}_{\chi}(M,\Lambda_{S}^{(0,2i)}(M))
is a
(0,2i)-
tensor field of class
C^{i}
on
M
such that for any
i
,
A^{(i)}(T,S)=A^{(i)}(S,T)
for any
(i,0)-
tensors
S
and
T
. In coordinates, we mean that
\nabla^{i}(A^{(i)}\nabla^{i})=\nabla^{q_{i}\cdots q_{1}}(A_{p_{1}\cdots p_{i},q_{1}\cdots q_{i}}\nabla^{p_{1}\cdots p_{i}})
. The model for equations like (
1
) is

(3)

\Delta_{\xi}^{k}U=|U|^{2^{\star}-2}U\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\xi
is the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The positive solutions
U\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
3
) are exactly (see Wei-Xu
[
weixu
]
):

X\mapsto U_{\mu,x_{0}}(X):=a_{n,k}\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu^{2}+|X-X_{0}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\hbox{ for }X\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\mu>0
and
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are parameters and
a_{n,k}:=\left(\Pi_{j=-k}^{k-1}(n+2j)\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{4k}}
.

Due to the critical exponent
2^{\star}
, solutions to (
1
) may blow-up, that is
\max_{M}|u_{\alpha}|\to\infty
as
\alpha\to+\infty
. There are several ways to describe the blow-up. In the spirit of Lions
[
PL2
]
, the family of measures
(|u_{\alpha}|^{2^{\star}}\,dv_{g})_{\alpha}
converges to a sum of Dirac masses and a density, see Mazumdar
[
mazumdar:jde
]
. In the spirit of Struwe
[
struwe:1984
]
, in the associated Sobolev space,
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a weak limit plus a sum of ”peaks” modeled on solutions to (
3
), see Mazumdar
[
mazumdar:cpaa
]
. In the present paper, we improve these descriptions and prove a pointwise control by a sum of peaks modeled on the
U_{\mu,x_{0}}
’s:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
without boundary and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
2\leq 2k<n
. We consider a family of operators
(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha>0}\to P_{\infty}
of type (SCC) (see Definition
2.1
below) and a family of functions
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\in C^{2k}(M)
such that (
1
) holds for all
\alpha>0
. We assume that
\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\max_{M}|u_{\alpha}|=+\infty
and that there exists
\Lambda>0
such that
\|u_{\alpha}\|_{2^{\star}}\leq\Lambda
for all
\alpha>0
. Then there exist
u_{\infty}\in C^{2k}(M)
,
N\geq 1
, there exist
(z_{\alpha,1})_{\alpha},...,(z_{\alpha,N})_{\alpha}\in M
such that
\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}|u_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha,i})|=+\infty
for all
i=1,...,N
, and setting
\mu_{\alpha,i}:=|u_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha,i})|^{-\frac{2}{n-2k}}
, we have that

(4)

|u_{\alpha}(x)|\leq C\|u_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\mu_{\alpha,i}}{\mu_{\alpha,i}^{2}+d_{g}(x,z_{\alpha,i})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}

for all
x\in M
and
\alpha\to+\infty
. Moreover, the
(z_{\alpha,i})_{\alpha}
’s are such that:

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}u_{\alpha}=u_{\infty}\hbox{ in }C^{2k}_{loc}\left(M-\left\{\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}z_{\alpha,i},\,i=1,...,N\right\}\right);

for all
i,j\in\{1,...,N\}
such that
i\neq j
, then

\hbox{ either }\left\{\frac{d_{g}(z_{\alpha,i},z_{\alpha,j})}{\mu_{\alpha,i}}\to+\infty\right\}\hbox{ or }\left\{\frac{d_{g}(z_{\alpha,i},z_{\alpha,j})}{\mu_{\alpha,i}}\to c_{i,j}>0\hbox{ and }\mu_{\alpha,j}=o(\mu_{\alpha,i})\right\}

as
\alpha\to+\infty
and there exists
U_{i}\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})-\{0\}
solution to (
3
) such that

\tilde{u}_{\alpha,i}:=\mu_{\alpha,i}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}u_{\alpha}(\hbox{exp}_{z_{\alpha,i}}(\mu_{\alpha,i}\cdot))\to U_{i}\hbox{ in }C^{2k}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-S_{i})

where

S_{i}:=\left\{\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\frac{\hbox{exp}_{z_{\alpha,i}}^{-1}(z_{\alpha,j})}{\mu_{\alpha,i}}/\,j\in\{1,...,N\}-\{i\}\hbox{ such that }d_{g}(z_{\alpha,i},z_{\alpha,j})=O(\mu_{\alpha,i})\right\}.

Finally,
U_{i}\in D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the norm
\|\cdot\|_{D_{k}^{2}}:=\|\Delta_{\xi}^{\frac{k}{2}}\cdot\|_{2}
(when
k=2l+1
is odd, we set
(\Delta_{\xi}^{\frac{k}{2}}u)^{2}=|\nabla\Delta_{\xi}^{l}u|^{2}
).

The sum on the right-hand-side of (
4
) correspond to the sum of peaks of the Struwe decomposition, which correspond to the Dirac masses in the Lions decomposition.

For
k=1
, this theorem has been proved by Premoselli
[
premoselli
]
for general elliptic operators, including non-coercive ones. For
k=1
and
u_{\alpha}>0
, the theorem is due to Druet-Hebey-Robert
[
DHR
]
and can be adapted to sign-changing solutions (see Ghoussoub-Mazumdar-Robert
[
gmr:memams
,
gmr:jde
]
). The result was applied by Druet
[
druet:jdg
]
to prove compactness of positive solutions to (
1
) far from the geometric model, and by Premoselli-Vétois
[
premo-vetois-jmpa
,
premo-vetois-aim
,
premo-vetois-eigen
]
and Premoselli-Robert
[
PR
]
for sign-changing solutions. The result was also written for second-order elliptic systems by Druet-Hebey
[
DH
]
. Interesting phenomena have been observed by Cheikh-Ali-Premoselli
[
CA-P
]
for manifolds with boundary. See Hebey
[
hebey.eth
]
for a general exposition on these methods.

There is quite a long history of pointwise controls like in Theorem
1.1
. The pioneer works were performed for
k=1
and
N=1
by Atkinson-Peletier
[
AP
]
, Z.-C.Han
[
zchan
]
and Hebey-Vaugon
[
HV
]
for positive solutions. Still for
k=1
, when the operator
P_{\alpha}
is the conformal Laplacian and the solutions are positive, Schoen
[
schoen-montecatini
,
schoen1988
]
has introduced the method of simple blow-up points. This powerful method has been paramount to tackle the prescription of scalar curvature and to prove compactness of the solutions to the Yamabe equation: see Schoen-Zhang
[
sz
]
, Chen-C.-S.Lin
[
cs:lin:1
,
cs:lin:2
,
cs:lin:3
]
, Li
[
li-95
,
li-96
]
, Li-Zhu
[
LiZhu
]
, Li-Zhang
[
LiZha3
,
LiZhang2005
]
, Druet
[
druet:imrn
]
, Druet-Laurain
[
druet:laurain
]
, Marques
[
Mar
]
and Khuri-Marques-Schoen
[
kms
]
. This technique proves isolation of the concentration points for geometric blowing-up solutions, and a bound on the
L^{2^{\star}}-
norm. Therefore, it does not apply to more general situations where concentration points accumulate or when there is no apriori bound on the
L^{2^{\star}}-
norm, see the examples of Robert-Vétois
[
RV:jdg
]
.

In the above mentioned papers, the proofs are specific to second order problems, that is
k=1
. For instance, some use the pointwise maximum principle and the Harnack inequality, which are not valid for
k>1
in general.

Regarding higher order, for
k=2
, partial estimates have been proved by Hebey, Wen and the author
[
hebey:2003
,
HRW
,
HR
]
for specific operators and positive solutions. Li-Xiong
[
lx
]
and Gong-Kim-Wei
[
gkw
]
have obtained beautiful compactness results for
5\leq n\leq 24
(this does not hold for
n\geq 25
, see Wei-Zhao
[
wz
]
) by adapting the method of simple blowup points of Khuri-Marques-Schoen
[
kms
]
: the solutions are positive, the operator
P_{\alpha}
is the geometric Paneitz operator and it is assumed that its Green’s function is positive. As for
k=1
, an intermediate step is to prove that the blow-up points are isolated. Using the powerful method of Premoselli
[
premoselli
]
, Carletti
[
carletti
]
has proved a refined pointwise estimate for
k>1
and
N=1
when the coefficients of the operator are unbounded.

Our objective is to develop a flexible approach that tolerates sign-changing solutions and general operators. This is natural since there are not many examples of operators with positive Green’s function and for many applied problems, like the clamped plate equation in mechanics, the Green’s function of the operator changes sign and so do the solutions to the problem. See Grunau-Robert
[
gr:arma
]
and Grunau-Robert-Sweers
[
grs
]
for qualitative estimates of the sign-change.

The general idea here is to write the nonlinear equation (
1
) as the linear equation

(5)

(P_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha})u_{\alpha}=0,

where
V_{\alpha}:=|u_{\alpha}|^{2^{\star}-2}
, and to represent
u_{\alpha}
in terms of the Green’s function of the operator
P_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha}
. Although this operator has unbounded coefficients, it turns that
V_{\alpha}
behaves like a small Hardy potential far from the
N
concentration points. In the case of a sole concentration point, that is
N=1
, this was performed by the author in
[
robert:gjms
]
(see Premoselli-Robert
[
PR
]
for
k=1
): the most delicate part is to obtain pointwise estimates for the corresponding Green’s function. In the present paper, we generalize
[
robert:gjms
]
to arbitrary
N
points. In order to tackle the potential accumulation of these points, we construct an order on the concentration points and we decompose the manifold
M
in several subdomains on which only the maximal elements for the order have an influence. Finally, we write a representation formula on each of these domains. A large portion of the analysis is devoted to pointwise controls of the Green’s function enjoying multiple Hardy potentials.

The method that is developed in this paper can be adapted to several contexts, as long as one can write a nonlinear problem as a linear problem like (
5
) where
V_{\alpha}
is a small Hardy-type potential.

2.
Preliminary material and notations

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha>0}\to P_{\infty}
is of type (SCC) if
(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
and
P_{\infty}
are differential operators such that
P_{\alpha}:=\Delta_{g}^{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\nabla^{i}(A^{(i)}_{\alpha}\nabla^{i})
for all
\alpha>0
and
P_{\infty}:=\Delta_{g}^{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\nabla^{i}(A^{(i)}_{\infty}\nabla^{i})
are as in (
2
) and
\theta\in(0,1)
are such that

\bullet
for all
i=0,...,k-1
,
(A^{(i)}_{\alpha})_{\alpha>0},A^{(i)}_{\infty}\in C^{i,\theta}
and
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}A_{\alpha}^{(i)}=A_{\infty}^{(i)}
in
C^{i,\theta}
.

\bullet

(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is uniformly coercive in the sense that there exists
c>0
such that

\int_{M}uP_{\alpha}u\,dv_{g}=\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}^{\frac{k}{2}}u)^{2}\,dv_{g}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{M}A^{(i)}_{\alpha}(\nabla^{i}u,\nabla^{i}u)\,dv_{g}\geq c\|u\|_{H_{k}^{2}}^{2}

for all
\alpha>0
and
u\in H_{k}^{2}(M)
, where
dv_{g}
is the Riemannian element of volume and
H_{k}^{2}(M)
is the completion of
C^{\infty}(M)
for the norm
u\mapsto\|u\|_{H_{k}^{2}}:=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|\nabla^{i}u\|_{2}
.
When
k=2l+1
is odd, we have set
(\Delta_{g}^{\frac{k}{2}}u)^{2}=|\nabla\Delta_{g}^{l}u|^{2}_{g}
.

For any metric
g
on a manifold
X
and
p\in X
, we let
\hbox{exp}_{p}^{g}
be the exponential map at
p
with respect to the metric
g
. By assimilating isometrically the tangent space
T_{p}X
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will consider
\hbox{exp}_{p}^{g}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to M
. Note that the isometry depends on
p
, and we will consider a smooth family of isometries in order to have smoothness of the exponential with respect to a neighborhood of each point
p
. We will simply write
\hbox{exp}_{p}
when there is no ambiguity. In all this manuscript,
i_{g}(X)
will denote the injectivity radius of
(X,g)
: note that it is positive when
X
is compact.

Since the exponential map is a diffeomorphism before the cut-locus, then for any
p_{0}\in M
, there exists a neighborhood
\Omega\subset M
of
p_{0}
and
r>0
such that

(6)

\frac{1}{2}|X-Y|\leq d_{g}(\hbox{exp}_{p}(X),\hbox{exp}_{p}(Y))\leq 2|X-Y|

for all
p\in\Omega
and
X,Y\in B_{r}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In the sequel,
C(a,b,...)
will denote a constant depending on
(M,g)
,
a,b,...
. The value can change from one line to another, and even in the same line. Given two sequence
(a_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
and
(b_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
, we will write
a_{\alpha}\asymp b_{\alpha}
if there existe
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}a_{\alpha}\leq b_{\alpha}\leq c_{2}a_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha>0
. Even when it is not mentioned, all results in this paper are up to the extraction of sub-family
\alpha\to+\infty
.

In all this manuscript, by ”Elliptic regularity theory”, we will refer to the general article
[
ADN
]
by Agmon-Douglis-Nirenberg. For the convenience of the readers, we will also refer to the precise statements in
[
robert:gjms
]
that are extracted from
[
ADN
]
.

3.
Exhaustion of the concentration points

We let
(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha>0}\to P_{\infty}
be of type (SCC) and a family
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha>0}\in C^{2k}(M)
be such that

(7)

P_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}=|u_{\alpha}|^{2^{\star}-2}u_{\alpha}\hbox{ in }M,

(8)

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\max_{M}|u_{\alpha}|=+\infty.

and

(9)

\|u_{\alpha}\|_{2^{\star}}\leq\Lambda\hbox{ for all }\alpha>0.

Sobolev’s embedding theorem for compact manifolds yields that
H_{k}^{2}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{\star}}(M)
continuously, so that there exists
C_{S}(k)>0
such that

(10)

\|u\|_{2^{\star}}\leq C_{S}(k)\|u\|_{H_{k}^{2}}\hbox{ for all }u\in H_{k}^{2}(M).

Multiplying (
7
) by
u_{\alpha}
, integrating by parts and using the uniform coercivity of
(P_{\alpha})
and the Sobolev inequality above, we get a constant
C(\Lambda)>0
independent of
\alpha
such that

(11)

\|u_{\alpha}\|_{H_{k}^{2}(M)}\leq C(\Lambda)\hbox{ for all }\alpha>0.

The objective here is to saturate the number of concentration points: this is the object of Theorem
3.1
at the end of the section. This section is devoted to setting up some notations and proving the theorem through intermediate steps.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\in M
be such that
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}|u_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})|=+\infty
. We set
\nu_{\alpha}:=|u_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})|^{-\frac{2}{n-2k}}
for all
\alpha>0
and we fix
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that for all
\omega\subset\subset\Omega
, there exists
C(\omega)>0
such that

(12)

|u_{\alpha}(\hbox{exp}_{z_{\alpha}}(\nu_{\alpha}x))|\leq C(\omega)|u_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})|\hbox{ for all }x\in\omega\hbox{ and }\alpha>0.

Then there exists
V\in C^{2k}(\Omega)
such that
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}V=|V|^{2^{\star}-2}V
in
\Omega
and

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\nu_{\alpha}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}u_{\alpha}(\hbox{exp}_{z_{\alpha}}(\nu_{\alpha}x))=V(x)\hbox{ for all }x\in\Omega,

and this convergence holds in
C^{2k}_{loc}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
|\nabla^{i}V|\in L^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}(\Omega)
for all
i=0,...,k
.

Proof.

Let us set

\hat{u}_{\alpha}(x):=\nu_{\alpha}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}u_{\alpha}(\hbox{exp}_{z_{\alpha}}(\nu_{\alpha}x))\hbox{ for all }x\in\Omega.

Equation (
7
) rewrites

(13)

\Delta_{\hat{g}_{\alpha}}^{k}\hat{u}_{\alpha}+\sum_{j=0}^{2k-2}\nu_{\alpha}^{2k-j}\hat{B}_{\alpha}^{j}\star\nabla^{j}\hat{u}_{\alpha}=|\hat{u}_{\alpha}|^{2^{\star}-2}\hat{u}_{\alpha}\hbox{ in }\Omega

where
\hat{g}_{\alpha}:=(\hbox{exp}_{z_{\alpha}}g)(\nu_{\alpha}\cdot)
and
\hbox{exp}_{p}^{\star}g
is the pull-back metric of
g
and for all
j=0,...,2k-2
,
(\hat{B}_{\alpha}^{j})_{\alpha}
is a family of
(j,0)-
tensors such that there exists
C_{j}>0
such that
\|\hat{B}_{\alpha}^{j}\|_{C^{0,\theta}}\leq C_{j}
for all
\alpha>0
. Let us fix
\omega\subset\subset\Omega
. It follows from the assumption (
12
) that
|\hat{u}_{\alpha}(x)|\leq C(\omega)
for all
x\in\omega
and
\alpha>0
. It then follows from elliptic regularity (
[
ADN
]
or Theorems D.2 and D.3 of
[
robert:gjms
]
) that there exists
V\in C^{2k}(\Omega)
such that
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\hat{u}_{\alpha}=V
in
C^{2k}_{loc}(\Omega)
. Note that since
\hbox{exp}_{z_{\alpha}}
is a normal chart at
z_{\alpha}
, and therefore, we get that
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\hat{g}_{\alpha}=\xi
in
C^{2k}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Passing to the limit
\alpha\to+\infty
in (
13
) yields
\Delta^{k}_{\xi}V=|V|^{2^{\star}-2}V
in
\Omega
. We fix
i\in\{0,...,2k\}
. It follows from Sobolev’s embedding theorem that
H_{k}^{2}(M)\hookrightarrow H_{i}^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}(M)
continuously, so there exists
C_{i}(M)>0
such that

\left(\int_{M}|\nabla^{i}_{g}u_{\alpha}|_{g}^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}\,dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-2(k-i)}{2n}}\leq C_{i}(M)\|u_{\alpha}\|_{H_{k}^{2}}\hbox{ for all }\alpha>0.

With a change of variable, we get that

\int_{\hbox{exp}_{z_{\alpha}}(\nu_{\alpha}\omega)}|\nabla^{i}_{g}u_{\alpha}|_{g}^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}\,dv_{g}=\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{i}_{\hat{g}_{\alpha}}\hat{u}_{\alpha}|_{\hat{g}_{\alpha}}^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}\,dv_{\hat{g}_{\alpha}}=\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{i}V|_{\xi}^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}\,dv_{\xi}+o(1).

These two identities and (
11
) then yield
\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{i}V|_{\xi}^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}\,dv_{\xi}\leq(C_{i}(M)C(\Lambda))^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}

for all
\omega\subset\subset\Omega
. Therefore
|\nabla^{i}V|\in L^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}(\Omega)
.∎

The Euclidean Sobolev embedding yields
K(n,k)>0
such that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2^{\star}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{\star}}}\leq K(n,k)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{\xi}^{\frac{k}{2}}u)^{2}\,dx\hbox{ for all }u\in D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We then get that

Lemma 3.2
.

Given a finite set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, possibly empty, let
V\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-S)
be such that
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}V=|V|^{2^{\star}-2}V
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}-S
and
|\nabla^{i}V|\in L^{\frac{2n}{n-2(k-i)}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-S)
for all
i=0,...,k
. Then
V
extends continuously to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
V\in D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}V=|V|^{2^{\star}-2}V
in the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, if
V\not\equiv 0
, then

(14)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}}\,dv_{\xi}\geq\frac{1}{K(n,k)^{\frac{n}{2k}}}.

Proof.

Let us write
S=\{p_{1},..,p_{N}\}
where
p_{1},...,p_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are distinct and let
r_{0},R_{0}>0
be such that
|p_{i}-p_{j}|>r_{0}
for all
i\neq j
and
|p_{i}|<R_{0}
for all
i
. Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\eta(t)=0
for
t\leq 1
and
\eta(t)=1
for
t\geq 2
. For
0<\epsilon<r_{0}/6
and
R>2R_{0}+r_{0}
, we define

\eta_{\epsilon,R}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta\left(\frac{|x-p_{j}|}{\epsilon}\right)&\hbox{ if }x\in B_{3\epsilon}(p_{j})\hbox{ for some }j=1,...,N\\
1-\eta\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right)&\hbox{ if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}-B_{R/2}(0)\\
1&\hbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

As one checks,
\eta_{\epsilon,R}V\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(\eta_{\epsilon,R}V)_{\epsilon,R}
is a Cauchy sequence in
D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and taking the pointwise limit, we then get that
V\in D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We then get that
V
is a weak solution of
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}V=|V|^{2^{\star}-2}V
in
D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
V\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a classical solution, see Van der Vorst
[
vdv
]

5.
Green’s function with Hardy potential: construction and first estimates

Definition 5.1
.

We say that an operator
P
is of type
O_{k,L}
if

\bullet\;P:=\Delta_{g}^{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\nabla^{i}(A^{(i)}\nabla^{i})\hbox{ for }\alpha>0

where for all
i=0,...,k-1
,
A^{(i)}\in C^{i}_{\chi}(M,\Lambda_{S}^{(0,2i)}(M))
is a family of
C^{i}-
field of
(0,2i)-
tensors on
M
such that:

\bullet
For any
i
,
A^{(i)}(T,S)=A^{(i)}(S,T)
for any
(i,0)-
tensors
S
and
T
.

\bullet

\|A^{(i)}\|_{C^{i}}\leq L
for all
i=0,...,k-1

\bullet

P
is coercive and

\int_{M}uPu\,dv_{g}=\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}^{\frac{k}{2}}u)^{2}\,dv_{g}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{M}A^{(i)}(\nabla^{i}u,\nabla^{i}u)\,dv_{g}\geq\frac{1}{L}\|u\|_{H_{k}^{2}}^{2}

for all
u\in H_{k}^{2}(M)
.

For
N\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
, we define

{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}(N):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}({\mathcal{F}},V)\in M^{N}\times L^{1}(M)\hbox{ such that}\\
|V(x)|\leq\lambda R_{\mathcal{F}}(x)^{-2k}\hbox{ for all }x\in M-\{p_{1},...,p_{N}\}\end{array}\right\}.

where for
{\mathcal{F}}=\{p_{1},...,p_{N}\}
, we have let

This section is devoted to the proof of the following theorems:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension

9.
A regularity Lemma

Definition 9.1
.

Let
(X,g)
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
and let us fix a subdomain

,

such that

, where for all

(ii)

For any

,
A^{(i)}(T,S)=A^{(i)}(S,T)
for any
(i,0)-
tensors

and

;

(iii)

\|A^{(i)}\|_{C^{i}}\leq L
for all

.

Lemma 9.1

Let
(X,g)
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension

for all

. It follows from (

Quantitative regularity for the MHD equations via the localization technique in frequency space
Baishun Lai, Shihao Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we employ the localization technique in frequency space developed by Tao in [ 31 ] to investigate the quantitative estimates for the MHD equations. With the help of quantitative Carleman inequalities given by Tao in [ 31 ] and the pigeonhole principle, we establish the quantitative regularity for the critical L 3 L^{3} norm bounded solutions which enables us explicitly quantify the blow-up behavior in terms of L 3 L^{3} norm near a potential first-time singularity. Some technical innovations, such as introducing the corrector function, are required due to the fact that the scales are inconsistent between the magnetic field and the vorticity field. Key words : Blow-up rate; Quantitative analysis; Carleman inequalities; Critical space; Pigeonhole principle. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classifications : 35B44, 35B65, 35Q30, 76D03.

Introduction and main results

The purpose of this article is to study the
explicit quantification of the blow-up rate with respect to time
of the critical norm near a potential singularity for the Magneto-Hydrodynamics (MHD for short) equations, which is a branch of continuum mechanics that examines the flow of electrically conducting fluids under the influence of magnetic fields. Due to its numerous practical applications, such as magnetic separation and targeted delivery of drugs or radioisotopes via magnetic guidance, research on magnetohydrodynamics has attracted widespread attention. The movement of conductive fluids (such as liquid metal mercury, liquid sodium, and plasma) in a magnetic field is a result of the coupling of these two fundamental forces. Firstly, fluid motion generates an electric current that modifies the existing magnetic field. Secondly, the interaction between the current and the magnetic field produces a mechanical force in the fluid, accelerating it in a direction perpendicular to both the magnetic field and the current. Therefore, in mathematical physics, the MHD equations are derived by coupling the Navier-Stokes equations from fluid mechanics with Maxwell’s equations from electromagnetic fields. For more physical background on the MHD equation, we refer the interested readers

where
\lambda=\sqrt{\widetilde{T}},y=x_{*}+\lambda x
and
(t,x)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. It is clear that
(v_{\lambda},H_{\lambda},(H_{\lambda})_{x_{k}})
is also a solution of (

), (

), and

fulfills the following differential inequality

\begin{split}|\partial_{t}W_{\lambda}+\Delta W_{\lambda}|\leq\frac{1}{4}|W_{\lambda}|+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla W_{\lambda}|\quad\text{on}\quad{[0,1]\times\Omega}.\end{split}

(1.16)

Here the space domain

is
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or the given annulus, see Section
4
for more details. The advantage of the differential inequality (

) is that the scale of time is normalised, and so the quantitative Carleman inequality is valid for

. However, notices that

since scales are inconsistent between the magnetic field

and the vorticity

, one can not derive the analogous enstrophy-type lower bound (

) for

, which is the second main block of the paper. The idea, fixing this difficulty, is now to introduce a

defined by

with

. Using the quantitative Carleman inequality to

and the pigeonhole principle, we, scaling back to the original variables, finally derive the exponentially small yet significant enstrophy-type lower bound for the

at the final moment of time

\displaystyle\int_{5\widetilde{R}\leq|x|\leq\frac{3A_{6}\widetilde{R}}{10}}W_{c}(t_{0},x)\ \mathrm{d}x\gtrsim e^{-e^{A_{6}^{8}}}T_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}},

with

to the lower bound on

, i.e.,

\int_{B(\tilde{x},\widetilde{r})}|v(t_{0},x)|^{3}\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{B(\tilde{x},\widetilde{r})}|H(t_{0},x)|^{3}\ \mathrm{d}x\gtrsim e^{-9e^{A_{6}^{11}}},

where

and

. The above lower bound finally leads to the the upper bound for

. Precisely,

Proposition 1.1
.

We use the mixed Lebesgue norms

\|v\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(I\times\Omega)}\triangleq\left(\int_{I}\|v(t)\|_{L^{r}_{x}(\Omega)}^{q}\ \mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/q}

where

with the usual modifications when

or

. Here

is a domain and

is a interval.
If

, by the space of all uniformly
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions

6.

Given a Schwartz function
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the Fourier transform

\hat{f}(\xi)\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\ \mathrm{d}x\quad\Big(\text{or}~\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\ \mathrm{d}x\Big),

and then for any
N>0
we define the Littlewood-Paley projection
P_{\leq N}
by the formula

\widehat{P_{\leq N}f}(\xi)\triangleq\varphi(\frac{\xi}{N})\hat{f}(\xi)

where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is a fixed bump function supported on
B(0,1)
that equals
1
on
B(0,\frac{1}{2})
. We also define the companion Littlewood-Paley projections

\displaystyle P_{N}\triangleq P_{\leq N}-P_{\leq\frac{N}{2}},\quad P_{>N}\triangleq\mathbb{I}-P_{\leq N},\quad\widetilde{P}_{N}\triangleq P_{\leq 2N}-P_{\leq\frac{N}{4}}

where
\mathbb{I}
denotes the identity operator; thus for instance

P_{\leq N}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{2^{-k}N}f,\quad P_{>N}f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{2^{k}N}f

for Schwartz
f
. Also we have

P_{N}=P_{N}\widetilde{P}_{N}.

These operators can also be applied to vector-valued Schwartz functions by working component by component. These operators commute with other Fourier multipliers such as the Laplacian
\Delta
and its inverse
\Delta^{-1}
, partial derivatives
\partial_{i}
, heat propagators
e^{t\Delta}
, and the Leray projection

\mathbb{P}\triangleq-\nabla\times\Delta^{-1}\nabla\times

to divergence-free vector fields. It is important to emphasize that if
N^{\prime}\sim N^{\prime\prime}<<N
, then

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{N}(P_{\leq N^{\prime}}vP_{\leq N^{\prime\prime}}v)=0.

(1.18)

In fact,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\Big[\widetilde{P}_{N}(P_{\leq N^{\prime}}vP_{\leq N^{\prime\prime}}v)\Big](\xi)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathcal{F}\Big[\widetilde{P}_{N}(P_{\leq N^{\prime}}v_{i}P_{\leq N^{\prime\prime}}v_{j})\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=\Big[\varphi(\frac{\xi}{2N^{\prime}})-\varphi(\frac{4\xi}{N^{\prime\prime}})\Big]\mathcal{F}\Big(P_{\leq N^{\prime}}v_{i}P_{\leq N^{\prime\prime}}v_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\Big[\varphi(\frac{\xi}{2N})-\varphi(\frac{4\xi}{N})\Big]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[\mathcal{F}(P_{\leq N^{\prime}}v_{i})](\xi-\eta)[\mathcal{F}(P_{\leq N^{\prime\prime}}v_{j})](\eta)\ \mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\quad=\Big[\varphi(\frac{\xi}{2N})-\varphi(\frac{4\xi}{N})\Big]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big[\varphi\Big(\frac{\xi-\eta}{N^{\prime}}\Big)\mathcal{F}(v_{i})\Big]\Big[\varphi\Big(\frac{\eta}{N^{\prime\prime}}\Big)\mathcal{F}(v_{j})\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\quad=\Psi_{1}\cdot\Psi_{2},

with

\Psi_{1}(\xi)\triangleq\varphi(\frac{\xi}{2N})-\varphi(\frac{4\xi}{N}),\quad\Psi_{2}(\xi)\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big[\varphi\Big(\frac{\xi-\eta}{N^{\prime}}\Big)\mathcal{F}(v_{i})\Big]\Big[\varphi\Big(\frac{\eta}{N^{\prime\prime}}\Big)\mathcal{F}(v_{j})\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\eta.

It is clear that

\mathrm{supp}\Psi_{1}(\xi)\cap\mathrm{supp}\Psi_{2}(\xi)=\varnothing

due to
N^{\prime}\sim N^{\prime\prime}<<N
. This leads to (
1.18
).

&\quad\quad+\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}(0,0))}+\|J\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}(0,0))}\Big).\end{split}

(2.10)

for any integer

is a universal constant.

Remark 2.1
.

The quantitative version of Barker-Prange’s result for Navier-Stokes system was presented in
[

]
without proof. Here, for completeness, we give a full proof in Appendix

Combining Lemmas

(2.11)

Remark 3.1
.

In contrast to the proof of Proposition

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(H\cdot\nabla)J^{n}\cdot\omega^{n}\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(H\cdot\nabla)\omega^{n}\cdot J^{n}\ \mathrm{d}x=0.

However, if we use the quantity (
3.13
), we have to deal with the term

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(H\cdot\nabla)J^{n}\cdot\omega^{n}\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(H\cdot\nabla)v_{x_{k}}^{n}\cdot H_{x_{k}}^{n}\ \mathrm{d}x\neq 0,

which is estimated as follows

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(H\cdot\nabla)J^{n}\cdot\omega^{n}\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(H\cdot\nabla)v_{x_{k}}^{n}\cdot H_{x_{k}}^{n}\ \mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|H\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla\omega^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla H_{x_{k}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim AY_{1}(t).

Hence, (
3.7
) fails, which disable us to prove Theorem
1.1
with the help of the Gronwall’s inequality and bootstrap technique.

Now we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.2
. The key ingredient of the proof is the quantitative estimates (
1.12
) of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
c>0
be a sufficiently small constant, and suppose for contradiction that

\limsup_{t\to 1^{-}}\frac{\|(v,H)(t)\|_{L^{3}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}}{[\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})]^{c}}<+\infty

Thus, for some constant
M>1
, we know that there exists
0<\delta<\exp\{-\exp{\exp(M^{\frac{1}{c}})}\}
such that

\|(v,H)(t)\|_{L^{3}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq M[\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})]^{c},\quad t\in[1-\delta,1)

Applying Theorem
1.1
, for
c
small enough (for example we can choose that
2cC_{0}^{8}<1
). Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(v,H)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\Big\{\exp\Big[\exp\Big(M^{C_{0}^{8}}\cdot[\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})]^{cC_{0}^{8}}\Big)\Big]\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\Big\{\exp\Big[\exp\Big([\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})]^{2cC_{0}^{8}}\Big)\Big]\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\Big\{\exp\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2}[\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})]\Big)\Big]\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\Big\{\exp\Big[\exp\Big(\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})^{\frac{1}{10}}\Big)\Big]\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\frac{1}{1-t})^{\frac{1}{10}},

where we used the inequality as follows

\displaystyle\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})^{\frac{1}{10}}

\displaystyle=\log\log\Big(\frac{1}{10}\log(\frac{1}{1-t})\Big)

\displaystyle=\log\Big[\log\frac{1}{10}+\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})\Big]

\displaystyle>\log\Big(\frac{1}{2}\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\log\frac{1}{2}+\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}\log\log\log(\frac{1}{1-t})

In particular,
v
and
H
are bounded in
L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}((1-\delta,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, contradicting the classical blow-up criterion
[
32
,
33
]
.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

which is the desired estimate (

.

Let

I=[0,1]

Taking the gradient of (
3.10
), (
3.11
),
then taking the inner product with
\nabla v^{n}
and

respectively,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla v^{n}|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla H^{n}|^{2})\ \mathrm{d}x\Big]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla^{2}v^{n}|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}H^{n}|^{2})\ \mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle~~=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{div}(v\otimes v-H\otimes H)\cdot\Delta v^{n}\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big[(v\cdot\nabla)H-(H\cdot\nabla)v\Big]\cdot\Delta H^{n}\ \mathrm{d}x\triangleq I_{8}+I_{9}.

as follows

\displaystyle I_{8}=

\displaystyle\frac{5}{8}\|\nabla^{2}v^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\frac{1}{8}\|\nabla^{2}H^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C\Big[A^{4}+A^{4}\mathcal{E}(t)+\mathcal{E}(t)^{3}\Big],

\displaystyle I_{9}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{5}{8}\|\nabla^{2}H^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\frac{1}{8}\|\nabla^{2}v^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C\Big[A^{4}+A^{4}\mathcal{E}(t)+\mathcal{E}(t)^{3}\Big]

Appendix A. Proof the Lemma

The following lemma plays a key role in the proof of Lemma

.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
B=B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
B^{\prime}=B_{1-\delta}(0)
with
0<\delta<1
. Let
v\in L^{2}(B)
be divergence-free and
\omega\triangleq\nabla\times v
. Then, for
k=1,2,\ldots
we have

\displaystyle\|D^{k}v\|_{L^{p}(B^{\prime})}\leq c(\|D^{k-1}\omega\|_{L^{p}(B)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)}+\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(B)})

and

\displaystyle\|D^{k}v\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B^{\prime})}\leq c(\|D^{k-1}\omega\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)})

(A.1)

with
1<p<\infty
,
0<\alpha<1
. Here
c
denotes the generic constant depending only on
\delta,~p
and
\alpha
.

The proof this lemma is standard which is well-known consequence of the classical
L^{p}
- and
C^{\alpha}
-estimates for the Laplace equation. However, since
we cannot find it in the literature, we give a full proof for reader’s convenience.

The proof of Lemma
A.1
.

we first notice that
\Delta v=-\nabla\times\omega
due to
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits v=0
. To derive the desired result, we set
\tau
be a smooth cut-off function that equals
1
in
B^{\prime}\subset\subset B
and vanishes outside
B
, and then

D^{k}v=D^{k-1}(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{x_{i}}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))+D^{k}A,

where
A
is harmonic on
B^{\prime}
. From the elliptic regularity for harmonic functions and Calderón-Zygmund inequality, we have:

Case 1.
1<p<3
.

\displaystyle\|D^{k}A\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|A\|_{L^{1}(B^{\prime\prime})}\leq c(\|(-\Delta)^{-1}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))\|_{L^{1}(B^{\prime\prime})}+\|v\|_{L^{1}(B^{\prime\prime})})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|(-\Delta)^{-1}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(B)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)}),

where
p^{\ast}=\frac{3p}{3-p}>1
,
B^{\prime}\subset\subset B^{\prime\prime}\subset\subset B
.

Case 2.
1<\widetilde{p}<3\leq p<\infty
.

\displaystyle\|D^{k}A\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|A\|_{L^{1}(B^{\prime\prime})}\leq c(\|(-\Delta)^{-1}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}^{\ast}}(B^{\prime\prime})}+\|v\|_{L^{1}(B^{\prime\prime})})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|(-\Delta)^{-1}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(B)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)})

where
\widetilde{p}^{\ast}=\frac{3\widetilde{p}}{3-\widetilde{p}}>1
. This, along with the Calderón-Zygmund inequality, yields

\|D^{k}v\|_{L^{p}(B^{\prime})}\leq c(\|D^{k-1}\omega\|_{L^{p}(B)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)}+\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(B)}).

which is a desired result. Now we turn to the proof of (
A.1
). In fact,

\displaystyle\|D^{k}A\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{k}A\|_{C^{1}(B^{\prime})}\leq c\|D^{k+1}A\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime})}\leq c\|A\|_{L^{1}(B^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(B)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)})\leq c(\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)}).

This, together with the classical Schauder estimate (for example, see
[
19
]
)

\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{x_{i}}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|\omega\tau\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

derives

\displaystyle\|D^{k}v\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{k}(-\Delta)^{-1}(\nabla\times(\omega\tau))\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B^{\prime})}+\|D^{k}A\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq c(\|D^{k-1}\omega\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B)})

which completes the proof of Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Chris Lambie-Hanson, Pedro Marun

Abstract
. We continue the development of the theory of higher dimensional walks on ordinals began recently by Bergfalk. In particular we identify natural coherence conditions on higher dimensional C C -sequences that entail coherence of the resultant higher rho-functions. We also introduce various higher square principles by adding non-triviality conditions to these coherent higher C C -sequences and investigate basic properties of said square principles. For example, in analogy with the classical case, we prove that these higher square principles abound in the constructible universe but can be forced to fail, modulo large cardinals. Finally, we prove that certain higher rho-functions obtained by walking along higher square sequences exhibit non-triviality in addition to coherence. In particular, it follows that higher square principles on a cardinal λ \lambda entail certain non-vanishing Čech cohomology groups for λ \lambda considered with the order topology.

Introduction

One of the most important tools in the contemporary study of combinatorial set theory
is Todorcevic’s method of
walks on ordinals
. This method, introduced in

[
11
]
(see
[
10
]
for a book-length treatment),
has proven transformatively useful in a variety of contexts, and is particularly efficacious
at the level of
\omega_{1}
, the first uncountable cardinal.

We will define the walks machinery in more detail below, but for now let us give a general overview.
To begin, one fixes an uncountable cardinal
\lambda
and a
C
-sequence

\mathcal{C}=\langle C_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\lambda\rangle
.
1
1

1

See Sections
2
and
3

This then provides a uniform
method of “walking” down from an ordinal
\beta<\lambda
to any ordinal
\alpha\leq\beta
.
Namely, we define an
upper trace function

\mathrm{Tr}:[\lambda]^{2}\rightarrow[\lambda]^{<\omega}

recursively by setting, for each
\alpha\leq\beta<\lambda
,

\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha,\beta)=\{\beta\}\cup\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha,\min(C_{\beta}\setminus\alpha)),

with a boundary condition specifying that
\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha,\alpha)=\{\alpha\}
for all

\alpha<\lambda
.

This trace function, and its interaction with the
C
-sequence
\mathcal{C}
, can then be used
to uncover a variety of interesting and nontrivial combinatorial behavior at the cardinal

\lambda
. In this paper, we will particularly be interested in the existence of families of
functions that are simultaneously
coherent
and
nontrivial
. As the definitions will
make clear, coherence is a local form of triviality, so nontrivial coherent families will be
witnesses to
incompactness phenomena
, closely linked to cohomological considerations.
In fact, (equivalence classes of) such families will comprise the nonzero elements of first
Čech cohomology groups of the ordinal
\lambda
, taken with the order topology
(cf.
[
3
]
).

To illustrate this phenomenon, we consider here one of the simplest of the combinatorial objects
derived from the walks apparatus: the
number of steps function

\rho_{2}:[\lambda]^{2}\rightarrow\omega
defined by setting
\rho_{2}(\alpha,\beta)=|\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha,\beta)|-1
for all
\alpha<\beta<\lambda
. From this one obtains the family
of functions

\Phi(\rho_{2})=\langle\rho(\cdot,\beta):\beta\rightarrow\omega\mid\beta<\lambda\rangle.

It turns out (cf.
[
10

) that, if
\mathcal{C}
is a

\square(\lambda)
-sequence, then
\Phi(\rho_{2})
is coherent and nontrivial
modulo
locally semi-constant functions
:

•

(coherence) for all
\alpha<\beta<\lambda
, the function

\rho_{2}(\cdot,\alpha)-\rho_{2}(\cdot,\beta)\restriction\alpha:\alpha\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

is locally semi-constant;

•

(nontriviality) there does not exist a function
\psi:\lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
such that,
for every
\alpha<\lambda
, the function

\rho_{2}(\cdot,\alpha)-\psi\restriction\alpha

is locally semi-constant.

The aforementioned efficacy of the walks machinery at
\omega_{1}
is largely due to the fact
that
\square(\omega_{1})
trivially holds in
\sf ZFC

\rho_{2}
provides a

\sf ZFC

\omega_{1}

\lambda

\sf ZFC

\square(\lambda)

Notation and conventions

If
A
is a set of ordinals, then we let

\mathrm{ssup}(A)=\sup\{\alpha+1\mid\alpha\in A\}
,
\mathrm{acc}^{+}(A)=\{\beta<\mathrm{ssup}(A)\mid\sup(A\cap\beta)=\beta>0\}
,
\mathrm{acc}(A)=A\cap\mathrm{acc}^{+}(A)
, and

\mathrm{nacc}(A)=A\setminus\mathrm{acc}(A)
. If
\lambda
is an ordinal and

, we say
that
D
is

in

\mathrm{ssup}(D)=\lambda
and
\mathrm{acc}^{+}(D)\subseteq D
. Note
that this is slightly nonstandard usage, as it is possible, for instance, for a set
D
to be club
in a successor ordinal. If
\gamma
is an infinite ordinal and

S^{\gamma}_{\mu}:=\{\alpha<\gamma:\mathrm{cf}(\alpha)=\mu\}
. Symbols like

for

If

and

is a sequence of ordinals of length

,
then we will always denote the

element of
\vec{\gamma}
by

\gamma_{m}
, i.e.,
\vec{\gamma}=\langle\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n-1}\rangle
.
In such situations, we let
\vec{\gamma}^{m}
denote the sequence obtained
by removing
\gamma_{m}
from
\vec{\gamma}
; i.e.,

\vec{\gamma}^{m}=\langle\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{m-1},\gamma_{m+1},\ldots,\gamma_{n-1}\rangle.

If
A
is a set of ordinals and

, then

[A]^{n}=\{x\subseteq A\mid|x|=n\}
. We will frequently identify

with the set of all strictly increasing sequences

. Similarly, we will let

denote the

, i.e.,

for

. We will sometimes slightly abuse notation and write, e.g.,

instead of

.
For instance, if

is a positive integer,
\lambda
is an ordinal,

, then we will think

as an element of

.

If

and

are functions mapping into the same abelian group,
then we will slightly

to denote the function

such that
\mathrm{dom}(\rho)=\mathrm{dom}(\varphi)\cap\mathrm{dom}(\psi)
and

\rho(x)=\varphi(x)+\psi(x)
for all

. Analogous conventions
apply to similar sums of more than two functions.

If

is an index set, then

denotes the free abelian group on

;

2.
Preliminaries on coherence and triviality

In this brief section, we review some background information about nontrivial coherent families
of functions.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
D
is a set of ordinals that is closed in its supremum.
Given an abelian group
H
, and an ordinal
\gamma\in D
, we say that a function

\varphi:D\cap\gamma\rightarrow H
is
locally semi-constant
if, for every

\alpha\in\mathrm{acc}(D)\cap(\gamma+1)
, there is
\eta<\alpha
such that

\varphi\restriction D\cap(\eta,\alpha)
is constant.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that
n
is a positive integer,
D
is a set of ordinals closed in its
supremum,
H
is an abelian group. We say that a family of functions

\Phi=\langle\varphi_{\vec{\gamma}}:\gamma_{0}\rightarrow H\mid\vec{\gamma}\in[D]^{n}\rangle

is

(1)

coherent modulo locally semi-constant functions
if, for all

\vec{\delta}\in[D]^{n+1}
, the function

\sum_{i\leq n}(-1)^{i}\varphi_{\vec{\delta}}^{i}:\delta_{0}\rightarrow H

is locally semi-constant;

(2)

trivial modulo locally semi-constant functions
if

(a)

n=1
and there exists a function
\psi:D\rightarrow H
such that,
for all
\gamma\in D
,
\varphi_{\gamma}-\psi\restriction(D\cap\gamma)

is locally semi-constant;

(b)

n>1
and there exists a family of functions

\Psi=\langle\psi_{\vec{\beta}}:\beta_{0}\rightarrow H\mid\vec{\beta}\in[D]^{n-1}\rangle

such that, for all
\vec{\gamma}\in[D]^{n}
, the function

\varphi_{\vec{\gamma}}-\sum_{i<n}(-1)^{i}\psi_{\vec{\gamma}^{i}}:\gamma_{0}\rightarrow H

is locally semi-constant.

We will often refer to a family of the form

\Phi=\langle\varphi_{\vec{\gamma}}:\gamma_{0}\rightarrow H\mid\vec{\gamma}\in[D]^{n}\rangle

as an
n
-family
on
D
, taking values in
H
(or simply an
n
-family, if

D
and
H
are clear from context).
It is readily verified that, under the assumptions of Definition

2.2
, if a family
\Phi=\langle\varphi_{\vec{\gamma}}\mid\vec{\gamma}\in[D]^{n}\rangle
is trivial, then it is coherent.
The general question motivating much of this paper concerns determining
the situations in which there exists such a family that is coherent but
nontrivial.

By varying the modulus, one obtains other notions of coherence and triviality.
One of the most prominent in the literature is coherence and triviality

modulo finite
, in which one replaces the requirement that the relevant
functions in Definition
2.2
be locally semi-constant by
the requirement that they be finitely supported. In this paper, we will be
almost exclusively be working with coherence and triviality modulo almost
locally constant functions, so if there is no risk of confusion we will
write, simply “coherent” instead of “coherent modulo locally semi-constant
functions”. Moreover, from the point of view of the existence of coherent
nontrivial families, it does not matter whether the modulus is “locally
semi-constant” or “finite”, as evidenced by the following fact,
combining Theorem 3.2 and Lemma 7.4 from

[
1
]
:
2
2

2

[
1
]
are stated in the case in
which the club
D
is itself an ordinal, but the apparently more
general statement given here is easily seen to be equivalent to this special

Fact 2.3
.

Suppose that
n
is a positive integer,
D
is a set of ordinals closed
in its supremum, and

H
is an abelian group. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

there exists a family of functions
\langle\varphi_{\vec{\gamma}}:\gamma_{0}\rightarrow H\mid\vec{\gamma}\in[D]^{n}\rangle
that is coherent
and nontrivial modulo locally semi-constant functions;

(2)

there exists a family of functions
\langle\varphi_{\vec{\gamma}}:\gamma_{0}\rightarrow H\mid\vec{\gamma}\in[D]^{n}\rangle

and nontrivial modulo finite.

Let us mention at the end of this section that part of the motivation for the
study of nontrivial coherent families of functions comes from cohomological considerations.
Given an ordinal
\lambda
, consider
\lambda
as a topological space with the order
topology. Given an abelian group
H
, let
\mathcal{F}_{H}
denote the presheaf on
\lambda

defined by setting
\mathcal{F}_{H}(U)=\bigoplus_{U}H
for all open
U\subseteq\lambda
, and
let
\mathcal{A}_{H}
denote the presheaf defined by letting
\mathcal{A}_{H}(U)
be the set of all
locally semi-constant functions from
U
to
H
for all open
U\subseteq\lambda
. Then, as
noted in
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
,
n
-families

that are coherent and nontrivial modulo finite represent the nonzero cohomology classes in the
Čech cohomology group
\check{\mathrm{H}}(\lambda,\mathcal{F}_{H})
, while families of the above
form that are coherent and nontrivial modulo locally semi-constant functions represent the nonzero
cohomology classes of
\check{\mathrm{H}}(\lambda,\mathcal{A}_{H})
. In particular, when we prove below
that a certain cardinal
\lambda
carries an
n

\vec{\gamma}\in I(\mathcal{C})
.

Given an
n
-
C
-sequence
\mathcal{C}
on

denote the

\alpha\in D

We say that

is

(3)

For all

\vec{\gamma}\in I(\mathcal{C})\cap[D]^{n}

\vec{\beta}\in I(\mathcal{C})
such that

.

We will typically assume without comment that all

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose that
m
is a positive integer,
n=m+1
,
\kappa<\lambda
are regular
uncountable cardinals,

\raise-2.23729pt\hbox{\framebox{\vbox to8.78014pt{\vfil\hbox to8.78014pt{\hfil\hbox{$m$}\hfil}\vfil}}}^{s}(\kappa)
holds, and there is a
\square(\lambda)
-sequence
\mathcal{D}
such that the
set

S=\{\gamma\in S^{\lambda}_{\kappa}\mid\mathrm{otp}(D_{\gamma})=\kappa\}

is stationary. Then
\raise-0.8484pt\hbox{\framebox{\vbox to6.00235pt{\vfil\hbox to6.00235pt{\hfil\hbox{$n$}\hfil}\vfil}}}^{s}(\lambda)
holds.

Proof.

We begin by slightly modifying
\mathcal{D}
. Let
S_{0}=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{acc}(\lambda)\mid\mathrm{otp}(D_{\gamma})\leq\kappa\}
and
S_{1}=\mathrm{acc}(\lambda)\setminus S_{0}
. Now define a
sequence
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\langle D^{\prime}_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\mathrm{acc}(\lambda)\rangle
by setting

D^{\prime}_{\gamma}=\begin{cases}D_{\gamma}&\text{if }\gamma\in S_{0}\\
D_{\gamma}\setminus D_{\gamma}(\kappa)&\text{if }\gamma\in S_{1},\end{cases}

where
D_{\gamma}(\kappa)
denotes the unique
\alpha\in D_{\gamma}
such that

\mathrm{otp}(D_{\gamma}\cap\alpha)=\kappa
. Note that
S\subseteq S_{0}
and, for all

\gamma\in S_{1}
, we have
\mathrm{acc}(D^{\prime}_{\gamma})\cap S_{0}=\emptyset
. Fix a

\raise-2.23729pt\hbox{\framebox{\vbox to8.78014pt{\vfil\hbox to8.78014pt{\hfil\hbox{$m$}\hfil}\vfil}}}^{s}(\kappa)
-sequence
\mathcal{E}=\langle E_{\vec{\eta}}\mid\vec{\eta}\in I(\mathcal{E})\rangle
.

We will now define a
\raise-0.8484pt\hbox{\framebox{\vbox to6.00235pt{\vfil\hbox to6.00235pt{\hfil\hbox{$n$}\hfil}\vfil}}}^{s}(\lambda)
-sequence
\mathcal{C}=\langle C_{\vec{\gamma}}\mid\vec{\gamma}\in I(\mathcal{C})\rangle
, recalling from Remark
3.2

our assumption of minimality at successors. The construction will be essentially disjoint
on the sets
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
. In particular, we will have
I^{+}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq[S_{0}]^{\leq n}\cup[S_{1}]^{\leq n}
. Let us first deal with
S_{1}
. Given
\vec{\gamma}=\langle\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k-1}\rangle\in[S_{1}]^{\leq n}
, we put

\vec{\gamma}\in I^{+}(\mathcal{C})
if and only if, for all
j<k-1
, we have

\gamma_{j}\in\mathrm{acc}(D^{\prime}_{\gamma_{k-1}})
; for all such
\vec{\gamma}
, set

C_{\vec{\gamma}}=D^{\prime}_{\gamma_{0}}
.

We next deal with
S_{0}
. The idea is to copy
\mathcal{E}
along
D_{\gamma}
for each

\gamma\in S
. First, for each
\gamma\in S_{0}
, set

\eta_{\gamma}=\mathrm{otp}(D_{\gamma})
. For each
\vec{\gamma}=\langle\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k-1}\rangle\in[S_{0}]^{\leq n}
, we set

\vec{\gamma}\in I^{+}(\mathcal{C})
if and only if, for all
j<k-1
, the following hold:

•

\gamma_{j}\in\mathrm{acc}(D_{\gamma_{k-1}})
; and

•

one of the following two holds:

–

\gamma_{k-1}\in S
,
\langle\eta_{\gamma_{j+1}},\ldots,\eta_{\gamma_{k-2}}\rangle\in I(\mathcal{E})
, and

\eta_{\gamma_{j}}\in\mathrm{acc}(E_{\eta_{\gamma_{j+1}}\ldots\eta_{\gamma_{k-2}}})
;

–

\gamma_{k-1}\notin S
,
\langle\eta_{\gamma_{j+1}},\ldots,\eta_{\gamma_{k-1}}\rangle\in I(\mathcal{E})
, and
\eta_{\gamma_{j}}\in\mathrm{acc}(E_{\eta_{\gamma_{j+1}}\ldots\eta_{\gamma_{k-1}}})
.

For such
\vec{\gamma}
, we define
C_{\vec{\gamma}}
according to the following cases:

•

if
\gamma\in S
, then set
C_{\gamma}=D_{\gamma}
;

•

if
\vec{\gamma}=\langle\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k-1}\rangle
and

\gamma_{k-1}\in S
, then set

C_{\vec{\gamma}}=\{D_{\gamma_{0}}(\xi)\mid\xi\in E_{\eta_{\gamma_{0}}\ldots\eta_{\gamma_{k-2}}}\};

•

if
\vec{\gamma}=\langle\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k-1}\rangle
,

\gamma_{k-1}\notin S
, and
k<n
, then set

C_{\vec{\gamma}}=\{D_{\gamma_{0}}(\xi)\mid\xi\in E_{\eta_{\gamma_{0}}\ldots\eta_{\gamma_{k-1}}}\};

•

if
\vec{\gamma}=\langle\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k-1}\rangle
,

\gamma_{k-1}\notin S
, and

, then set

C_{\vec{\gamma}}=C_{\vec{\gamma}^{0}}\cap\gamma_{0}.

It is now straightforward if tedious to use the coherence of
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}
to verify
that
\mathcal{C}
is a coherent
n
-
C
-sequence on
\lambda
. Moreover, by construction, for
each
\gamma\in S
,
\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{E}
via the order-preserving bijection

\pi_{\gamma}:D_{\gamma}\rightarrow\kappa
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is strongly nontrivial, this implies that

\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}
is strongly nontrivial for all

. Since

is stationary, it
follows that
\mathcal{C}
is a
\raise-0.8484pt\hbox{\framebox{\vbox to6.00235pt{\vfil\hbox to6.00235pt{\hfil\hbox{$n$}\hfil}\vfil}}}^{s}(\lambda)
-sequence, as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose that
n
is a positive integer,
\lambda
is an ordinal with

\mathrm{cf}(\lambda)\geq\aleph_{n}

\lambda
,

\mathcal{C}

Proof.

If

is regular,
so there is nothing to prove. Thus, assume
that

\lambda

Assume first that
\mathcal{C}
is not weakly nontrivial, and fix

for all

.

Claim 3.11.1

\mathrm{acc}(D^{\prime})\subseteq X(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Fix

\gamma\in\mathrm{acc}(D^{\prime})\cap S^{\lambda}_{\aleph_{n-1}}

We define an extension

of
\mathcal{C}
to

\gamma\in S

\alpha_{\gamma}\leq\alpha
and
j_{\gamma}=j
.
We can also fix an unbounded set

and a

such that, for all

\psi(\gamma)=k
.
It follows that, for all
\gamma<\delta
, both in

.
However, by Fact

, we can find
\gamma<\delta
, both in

\rho^{\mathcal{C}}_{2}(\gamma,\delta)>|j+k|

∎

(n+1)
-

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
n
is a positive integer,
\lambda
is an ordinal,
D
is a club
in
\lambda
, and
\mathcal{C}
is an
(n+1)
-
C
-sequence on
D
.
Fix
\delta\in\mathrm{acc}(D)
, and
let
\mathcal{C}^{\delta}=\langle C_{\vec{\gamma}\delta}\mid\vec{\gamma}\in I_{\delta}(\mathcal{C})\rangle
. Then, for all
\vec{\gamma}\in[C_{\delta}]^{n+1}
,

(1)

S^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(\vec{\gamma})=S^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)\cap{{}^{<\omega}}n
;

(2)

for all
x\in S^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(\vec{\gamma})
, we have

•

\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(+,\vec{\gamma},\delta)(x)=(\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(+,\vec{\gamma})(x))^{\frown}\langle\delta\rangle
;

•

L_{n+1}^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)(x)=L_{n}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\gamma})(x)
.

Proof.

Fix
\vec{\gamma}\in[C_{\delta}]^{n+1}
.
By induction on
|x|
, we will prove that, for all
x\in S^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)\cap{{}^{<\omega}}n
, the following
statements hold:

(a)

x\in S^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(\vec{\gamma})
;

(b)

\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(+,\vec{\gamma},\delta)(x)=(\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(+,\vec{\gamma})(x))^{\frown}\langle\delta\rangle
;

(c)

x
is a terminal node of
S^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(\vec{\gamma})

if and only if it is a terminal node of
S^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)
.

This will clearly suffice to establish the lemma.

If
x=\emptyset
, then (a) and (b) hold by assumption. To establish
(c), note that, since
\vec{\gamma}\in[C_{\delta}]^{n+1}
, we certainly
have
|\tau^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)|>1
. It then follows that

\tau^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\gamma})^{\frown}\langle\delta\rangle=\tau^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)
and
\iota^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\gamma})=\iota^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)
. Let
\gamma^{*}
denote the maximal
entry in
\iota^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\gamma})
.
Then
\emptyset
is a terminal node of
S^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(\vec{\gamma})

if and only if
C_{\tau^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\gamma})^{\frown}\langle\delta\rangle}\setminus\gamma^{*}=\emptyset
,
which in turn holds if and only if
\emptyset
is a terminal node of

S^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)
, thus establishing (c).

Suppose now that
x
is a non-terminal node of
S^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(\vec{\gamma},\delta)
and we have established (a)–(c) for
x
. Fix
i<n
; we will
establish (a)–(c) for
x^{\frown}\langle i\rangle
. Note first that,
by the induction hypothesis, we know that
x
is not a terminal node of

S^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\gamma})
, and hence
x^{\frown}\langle i\rangle\in S^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\gamma})
, establishing (a).

Suppose that
\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(+,\vec{\gamma})(x)=((-1)^{m},\vec{\beta})
. By the induction hypothesis, it follows that

\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(+,\vec{\gamma},\delta)=((-1)^{m},\vec{\beta},\delta)
. In particular, we know that
\vec{\beta}\in[C_{\delta}]^{n+1}
,
and hence we have

•

\tau^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\beta})^{\frown}\langle\delta\rangle=\tau^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\beta},\delta)
;

•

\iota^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\beta})=\iota^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\beta},\delta)
.

Set
j+1=|\iota^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\beta})|
. Note that

j\leq n
, and let
\langle\ell_{i}\mid i<n\rangle
be the increasing
enumeration of the set
\{1,\ldots,n+1\}\setminus\{j+1\}
.
Let
\beta^{*}
denote the maximal element of
\iota^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\beta})
,
and let
\alpha^{*}=\min(C_{\tau^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\beta},\delta)}\setminus\beta^{*})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}(+,\vec{\gamma},\delta)(x^{\frown}\langle i\rangle)

\displaystyle=((-1)^{m+\ell_{i}},(\iota^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\beta},\delta),\beta^{*},\tau^{\mathcal{C}}(\vec{\beta},\delta))^{\ell_{i}})

\displaystyle=((-1)^{m+\ell_{i}},(\iota^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\beta}),\beta^{*},\tau^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\vec{\beta}))^{\ell_{i}})^{\frown}\langle\delta\rangle

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n}(+,\vec{\gamma})(x^{\frown}\langle i\rangle))^{\frown}\langle\delta\rangle.

This establishes (b). Using the above calculations, item (c) is verified in
exactly the same way as it was in the base case, so we leave this to the
reader.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose that
n
is a positive integer,
D
is a club in
\lambda
, and
\mathcal{C}

is a
\raise-0.8484pt\hbox{\framebox{\vbox to6.00235pt{\vfil\hbox to6.00235pt{\hfil\hbox{$n$}\hfil}\vfil}}}^{s}(D)
-sequence. Then
\Phi({\textphnc{r}}^{\mathcal{C}}_{n})
is nontrivial modulo locally semi-constant functions.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, then the result follows from
Corollary
5.2
, so suppose that

n_{0}\geq 1
and
n=n_{0}+1
. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that

\Phi({\textphnc{r}}_{n}^{\mathcal{C}})
is trivial, and fix a trivializing family

\Psi=\left\langle\psi_{\vec{\gamma}}:D\cap\gamma_{0}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{[D]^{n}}\mathbb{Z}\ \middle|\ \vec{\gamma}\in[D]^{n_{0}}\right\rangle.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is a
\raise-0.8484pt\hbox{\framebox{\vbox to6.00235pt{\vfil\hbox to6.00235pt{\hfil\hbox{$n$}\hfil}\vfil}}}^{s}(D)
-sequence, we know that

\kappa:=\mathrm{otp}(D)
is a regular uncountable cardinal. It follows that the set

E:=\left\{\delta\in D~\middle|~\forall\vec{\gamma}\in[D\cap\delta]^{n-1}~\psi_{\vec{\gamma}}[D\cap\gamma_{0}]\subseteq\bigoplus_{[D\cap\delta]^{n}}\mathbb{Z}\right\}

is a club subset of
D
. We can therefore find
\delta\in E
such that

\mathcal{C}^{\delta}
is a strongly nontrivial coherent
n_{0}
-
C
-sequence on

C_{\delta}
, and hence, by the inductive hypothesis,

\Phi({\textphnc{r}}_{n_{0}}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}})
is nontrivial.

Let us now make a couple of observations. For each

\vec{\gamma}\in[C_{\delta}]^{n_{0}}
, the function

f_{\vec{\gamma}}:={\textphnc{r}}_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot,\vec{\gamma},\delta)-\left((-1)^{n_{0}}\psi_{\vec{\gamma}}+\sum_{i<n_{0}}(-1)^{i}\psi_{\vec{\gamma}^{i}\delta}\right)

is locally semi-constant, and hence
\pi^{C_{\delta}}_{n}\circ f_{\vec{\gamma}}

is also locally semi-constant.
Moreover, by Corollary
5.5
,
we know that
{\textphnc{r}}_{n_{0}}^{\mathcal{C}_{\delta}}(\cdot,\vec{\gamma})=\pi^{C_{\delta}}_{n}\circ{\textphnc{r}}_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot,\vec{\gamma},\delta)
.
Since
\delta\in E
, we know that

\pi^{C_{\delta}}_{n}\circ\psi_{\vec{\gamma}}=0
; putting this together,
it follows that

(5)

\displaystyle\pi^{C_{\delta}}_{n}\circ f_{\vec{\gamma}}={\textphnc{r}}_{n_{0}}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}(\cdot,\vec{\gamma})-\sum_{i<n_{0}}(-1)^{i}\pi^{C_{\delta}}_{n}\circ\psi_{\vec{\gamma}^{i}\delta}

is locally semi-constant for all
\vec{\gamma}\in[C_{\delta}]^{n_{0}}

Suppose first that
n_{0}=1
, and let
\sigma=\pi^{C_{\delta}}_{n}\circ\psi_{\delta}
. Then equation (

) implies that
\sigma

trivializes
\Phi({\textphnc{r}}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n_{0}})
, contradicting the fact that

\Phi({\textphnc{r}}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n_{0}})

Suppose next that

. For each
\vec{\beta}\in[C_{\delta}]^{n_{0}-1}
,

.
Then equation (

\Phi({\textphnc{r}}^{\mathcal{C}^{\delta}}_{n_{0}})

We end this section with the promised discussion of the prospects for
deriving nontrivial coherent
n
-dimensional families of functions mapping
into
\mathbb{Z}
via the machinery of higher-dimensional walks. As mentioned
in the introduction, it seems somewhat unlikely that this can be done in a
canonical and uniform way for
n>1
. The reason for this is the following
fact, from a forthcoming work of Bergfalk, Zhang, and the first author.

Fact 5.7

(
[
4
]
)
.

Suppose that
n<\omega
,
2^{\omega_{n}}=\omega_{n+1}
, and

\Phi=\langle\varphi_{\vec{\gamma}}:\gamma_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\mid\vec{\gamma}\in[\omega_{n+2}]^{n+2}\rangle

is coherent mod finite. Then there is a cardinal-preserving forcing
extension in which
\Phi
is trivial mod finite.

An analogue of this fact should also hold for coherence and triviality
modulo locally semi-constant functions.
To see the relevance of this fact, suppose that we are in a model of

\sf ZFC
in which
n_{0}<\omega
,
2^{\omega_{n_{0}}}=\omega_{n_{0}+1}
, and

\mathcal{C}
is an order-type-minimal

-

-sequence on

\omega_{n_{0}+2}

,

is trivially a

\raise-0.8484pt\hbox{\framebox{\vbox to6.00235pt{\vfil\hbox to6.00235pt{\hfil\hbox{$n$}\hfil}\vfil}}}^{s}(\omega_{n})
-sequence. Now suppose that

n
-family of functions derived in a sufficiently canonical
and uniform way from performing
n
-dimensional walks using
\mathcal{C}

By the above fact, there is a cardinal-preserving forcing extension

in

is trivial. However, in

,

remains an

Treatment of Unruptured Isolated Sinus of Valsalva Aneurysm
Authors: Marjan Jahangiri
Published: 2026

Invited Commentary:

For treatment of unruptured sinus of Valsalva (SOV), several factors need to be considered: the location, size, and shape of the aneurysm. In this issue of The Annals of Thoracic Surgery: Short Reports, Matsushiro and colleagues1 describe a 10-mm aneurysm that was isolated and arose within the left coronary sinus. Some patients present with asymmetric dilatation of 1 SOV with normal or nearly normal dimensions of the other sinuses.

In the case described by Matsushiro and colleagues,1 the aneurysm seemed to be an outpouching from the SOV and not the whole dilatation of the sinus. The aim of the repair is to exclude the aneurysm, provide good aortic valve function with no regurgitation, and avoid damage to the aortic valve leaflets and the coronary arteries.

If the sinus diameter is larger than 4.5 cm (especially in patients with connective tissue disease) or there is a distinct outpouching such as in the current case, surgery is indicated.2 If it involves the right or left coronary sinus, consideration of coronary buttons is required. The best approach is transaortic (by full sternotomy or ministernotomy depending on the expertise available). The repair can range from patch closure to complete root replacement.

Smaller aneurysms can be repaired with a patch, preferably a pericardial patch. If it is close to the aortic annulus, a 3- to 5-mm distance is required from the annulus to avoid pulling the valve leaflet. The repair can be performed with interrupted or continuous 4-0 or 5-0 polypropylene (Prolene, Ethicon) suture, with careful attention not to distort the anatomy. Primary closure is an option for aneurysms with a very small orifice; otherwise, it can cause distortion of the anatomy and possibly later aortic regurgitation.

If the whole of the SOV or a single SOV is dilated, excision of the sinus is performed. Then a graft is prepared in the shape of a nondilated sinus or a tongue and sewn with 4-0 Prolene. Again, attention should be given to symmetry and not distorting the valve architecture. If the lesion is in the left or right coronary cusps, the coronary buttons need mobilizing and reimplanting. If the aortic valve is regurgitant or stenosed, repair or replacement of the valve is required.

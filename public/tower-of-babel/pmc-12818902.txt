Potentiation of noncanonical NF-κB signaling and marginal zone B cell expansion by CARD11-mediated regulation of p100 processing
Authors: Anushka Ghosh, Tyler A Jones, Sophie Mackinnon, Shelby M Hutcherson, Julia M Tritapoe, Piyusha Mongia, Neha Shah, Emma M Mehlhop, Nicole M Carter, Joel L Pomerantz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SUMMARYThe signaling scaffold CARD11 is critical for adaptive immunity and antigen receptor signaling to canonical nuclear factor κB (NF-κB), mTOR, JNK, and AKT. Oncogenic gain-of-function (GoF) CARD11 mutants, implicated in a variety of lymphomas, bypass regulation and signal constitutively. In mice expressing the lymphoma-associated mutation CARD11 C49Y, we observe B cell expansion that is most impressive for marginal zone (MZ) B cells. CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells exhibit enhanced cell-autonomous survivability and elevated basal levels of the precursor NF-κB subunit p100, which is inducibly processed, leading to elevated nuclear p52 levels in response to B cell activating factor (BAFF). We show that active CARD11 variants can inhibit basal NIK-induced p100 processing to p52 independently of their ability to activate canonical NF-κB. Our results reveal an unexpected ability of activated CARD11 to potentiate the noncanonical NF-κB pathway through p100 accumulation, which likely contributes to both normal B cell homeostasis and the dysregulated proliferation of lymphomas that harbor CARD11 GoF variants.

INTRODUCTION

The normal functioning of the adaptive immune system is dependent on the signaling circuitry in B and T lymphocytes that translates antigen recognition into regulated lymphocyte activation, proliferation, and differentiation. CARD11 is a critical scaffold protein required for T cell receptor (TCR) and B cell receptor (BCR) signaling to branching downstream pathways to canonical nuclear factor κB (NF-κB), mTOR, JNK, and AKT.1–3 In resting lymphocytes, CARD11 is kept in an inactive state through autoinhibition mediated by four repressive elements (REs) in the CARD11 inhibitory domain (ID) that participate in inhibitory intramolecular interactions with other CARD11 domains.4–6 Upon engagement of BCR or TCR with ligand, CARD11 is converted to an open state that is competent to recruit cofactors into a complex that functions to activate downstream pathways. The inducible recruitment of Bcl10, MALT1, HOIP, CK1α, TRAF6, and other factors to CARD11 leads to the activation of the IκB kinase (IKK) complex, which phosphorylates IκB proteins, leading to their ubiquitinylation and degradation, allowing canonical p50:p65 NF-κB heterodimers to translocate to the nucleus to regulate target genes.2,7

Gain-of-function (GoF) mutations in CARD11 have been identified in a variety of lymphomas, including diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL), splenic marginal zone lymphoma, acute T cell leukemia/lymphoma, Sézary syndrome, mantle cell lymphoma, and angioimmunoblastic T cell lymphoma.8 CARD11 GoF mutations spontaneously convert CARD11 into a constitutively active signaling scaffold by bypassing autoinhibition by the ID, resulting in constitutive recruitment of Bcl10, MALT1, and HOIP, production of LinUbn-Bcl10, and activation of the IKK complex and canonical NF-κB.5,9–11 CARD11 GoF mutations have been found in ~10% of cases of the activated B cell-like (ABC) subtype of DLBCL, and in those cases can account for the constitutive signaling to canonical NF-κB that is required for lymphoma proliferation.12,13 However, the mechanisms by which CARD11 GoF mutations alter B cell biology in the course of lymphomagenesis remain incompletely understood.

The noncanonical NF-κB signaling pathway is a distinct pathway that is important for regulating B cell homeostasis and survival.14 In noncanonical NF-κB signaling, binding of ligand to receptors, including B cell activating factor receptor (BAFFR), APRIL, CD40, lymphotoxin beta, and others, leads to the accumulation of the NF-κB-inducing kinase (NIK), which then phosphorylates IKKα. NIK and IKKα together phosphorylate the p100 subunit of NF-κB bound to RelB, resulting in its ubiquitinylation and proteasomal processing to p52. The resultant p52:RelB heterodimer then translocates to the nucleus to regulate the target genes of noncanonical NF-κB signaling.15

To analyze the effects of CARD11 C49Y on B cell biology and lymphomagenesis, we generated a knockin mouse expressing the CARD11 C49Y mutation originally described in DLBCL.16 We report that CARD11 C49Y expression is sufficient to drive B cell expansion with a remarkable effect on marginal zone (MZ) B cells. Mechanistically, our results implicate a previously unrecognized ability of activated CARD11 to regulate the noncanonical NF-κB pathway by upregulating p100 protein levels through a post-transcriptional mechanism.

RESULTS

Splenomegaly and expansion of follicular and MZ B cells in CARD11 C49Y mice

The CARD11 C49Y mutant, originally detected in ABC DLBCL,16 is a potent GoF mutation that hyperactivates CARD11, leading to constitutive NF-κB activation.10 Additionally, patients with a germline heterozygous CARD11 C49Y mutation clinically present with the symptoms of the congenital lymphoproliferative disorder known as B cell expansion with NF-κB and T cell anergy (BENTA), which includes splenomegaly, polyclonal B cell lymphocytosis, mild immunodeficiency, and a predisposition to B cell malignancies.17 To further elucidate the effects of hyperactive CARD11 C49Y signaling, we generated a knockin mouse that expressed this mutant from the endogenous CARD11 locus. CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had visibly enlarged spleens with 3.0-fold more splenocytes compared to the wild type, while CARD11+/C49Y mice had 1.9-fold more splenocytes (Figure 1A) than the wild type. CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had 1.9-fold and 3.5-fold more splenic B cells than wild-type mice, respectively, with a 12% and 31% increase in the percentage of splenic CD19+B220+ cells in CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice compared to the wild type (Figure 1B). Although CARD11 is also expressed in T cells and natural killer (NK) cells, CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice did not exhibit significant differences in absolute numbers of bulk splenic CD4+ or CD8+ T cells and only a small but statistically insignificant increase in NK cell number (Figures S1A, S1E, and S1F). CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice displayed 1.6-fold and 2.5-fold more splenic regulatory T (Treg) cells than the wild type, respectively, but normal percentages and absolute numbers of thymic Treg cells (Figures S1G and S1H).

Patients with BENTA who are heterozygous for a CARD11 GoF mutation display mild T cell anergy due to chronic NF-κB signaling.18,19 Patients’ T cells have defects in proliferation and activation on weak stimulation with soluble anti-CD3/anti-CD28 antibodies. We tested whether CARD11 C49Y expression in mice would elicit T cell hyporesponsiveness. Surprisingly, CD4+ T cells from CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice were activated and proliferated in response to stimulation with plate-bound anti-CD3 and soluble anti-CD28 without any observable defect (Figures S1B and S1C). Instead of T cell anergy, C49Y further sensitized T cells to anti-CD3/anti-CD28 stimulation, as reflected in increased surface CD25 expression and increased cell division upon titrated stimulation with anti-CD3/anti-CD28. This is in contrast both to BENTA phenotypes and to the T cell hyporesponsiveness reported in another BENTA mouse model expressing CARD11 E134G.20 CARD11 C49Y expressing T cells more closely resembled those expressing the GCB-DLBCL-associated K215M mutant.20 In vitro, unstimulated CD4+ T cells from CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice did not exhibit constitutive activation of the canonical NF-κB pathway in assays for IκBα phosphorylation and degradation (Figure S1D) and IKKα/β phosphorylation (Figure S1D). Treatment of CARD11+/C49Y CD4+ T cells with phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA)/ionomycin, which mimics TCR stimulation, resulted in robust activation of the canonical NF-κB pathway that was not significantly different from wild-type controls (Figure S1D). However, we did observe a significant decrease in inducible phosphorylation of IκBα and a mild impairment in IκBα degradation on PMA/ionomycin stimulation of CARD11C49Y/C49Y CD4+ T cells (Figure S1D). Taken together, the results indicated that heterozygous expression of CARD11 C49Y in CD4+ T cells did not result in hyporesponsiveness to TCR signaling.

Further examination of the B cell lineage revealed that in the spleen, CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had 3.6-fold more mature B cells (B220+CD93−) than the wild type, while CARD11+/C49Y mice had 2.3-fold more (Figure 1C), without a statistically significant difference in the percentages of mature B cells in the mutant mice compared to wild-type mice. CARD11 protein expression levels in mature B cells in CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice were not different from that in the wild type (Figure 1D). Unexpectedly, CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had a 35% decrease in the percentage of mature B cells staining for follicular (FO) B cell markers and a 4.1-fold increase in the percentage of mature B cells staining for MZ B cell markers (Figure 1E). While CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had 2.4-fold and 2.3-fold more FO B cells (B220+CD93−CD21low/−CD23+) than the wild type, respectively, CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had 2.3-fold and a striking 14.1-fold more MZ B cells (B220+CD93−CD21hiCD23−) than the wild type, respectively (Figure 1E).

We also examined transitional T1, T2, and T3 immature stages of B cell development in the spleen to assess whether the 14.1-fold expansion of MZ B cells in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice correlated with an expansion of precursor stages. CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had 2.3-fold more immature B cells (B220+CD93+) than the wild type (Figure 1C). The percentage of cells that were T1 (B220+CD93+CD23−IgMhi) within immature B cells was 2.5-fold higher in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice compared to the wild type, with an increase of 7.4-fold in absolute numbers. We observed no changes in the absolute numbers of T2 (B220+CD93+CD23+IgMhi) and T3 (B220+CD93+CD23+IgMlow) cells but observed a 55% decrease in the percentage of T3 cells within immature B cells (Figure 1F). In contrast, CARD11+/C49Y mice showed no significant increase in percentages or absolute numbers of total immature B cells and the transitional subsets (Figures 1C and 1F).

On assessing developmental stages in the bone marrow, no notable differences were observed in the fractions A or pre-pro B cells (B220lowCD43hiCD24−BP-1−), B or early pro B cells (B220lowCD43hiCD24+/intBP-1−), C or late pro B cells (B220lowCD43hiCD24intBP-1+), C′ or early pre B cells (B220lowCD43hiCD24+BP-1+), D or late pre B cells (B220low/hiCD43lowB220lowIgM−), E or new/immature B cells (B220low/hiCD43lowB220lowIgM+), and F or mature B cells (B220low/hiCD43lowB220hiIgM+), suggesting that any effects of CARD11 C49Y on B cell expansion occur after migration to the spleen and not because of increased output of immature B cells from the bone marrow (Figure S2A). CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice also displayed a trend toward higher absolute numbers of B1a (CD11b+B220+CD5+IgM+) and B1b (CD11b+B220+CD5−IgM+) cells in the peritoneal cavity, albeit statistically not significant, with no difference in the percentages of peritoneal B cells that were B1a or B1b cells (Figure S2B).

Overall, expression of CARD11 C49Y appeared to result in increased numbers of B cells at several developmental stages in the spleen, with a greater effect in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice than in CARD11+/C49Y mice, with the largest expansion in MZ B cells.

Robust antibody responses following immunization of CARD11 C49Y mice

To assess the effect of CARD11 C49Y on B cell differentiation and germinal center (GC) reactions, we immunized wild-type CARD11+/+, CARD11+/C49Y, and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice with the T cell-dependent (TD) antigen DNP-CGG. We found that 7 days post immunization (dpi), CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had 2.5-fold and 2.2-fold lower percentages of GC B cells (CD95+GL7+) compared to wild-type and CARD11+/C49Y mice, respectively (Figure S3A). However, this defect in differentiation did not translate to a defect in absolute number of GC B cells in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice, owing to the significantly high number of splenocytes in these mice (Figure S3A). Additionally, at 14 dpi both CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had 2.4-fold and 3.4-fold lower percentages of plasma cells (B220−CD138+), respectively, compared to the wild type (Figure S3B). Again, this defect in terminal differentiation of mature B cells to antibody-secreting plasma cells was not reflected in the absolute numbers of plasma cells in the mutant mice (Figure S3B). These intrinsic defects in B cell differentiation were reminiscent of both CARD11 E134G-expressing mice21 and patients with BENTA, who exhibit poor humoral immunity.18,19,22 Patients with BENTA have low serum immunoglobulin M (IgM) levels, variable levels of IgG, and are incapable of mounting a productive immune response to TD and T cell-independent (TI) polysaccharide-based vaccines, as evidenced by low antibody titers on vaccination. Since CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had impaired GC and plasma cell differentiation with unchanged absolute numbers of both, we next investigated antibody titers in the serum and antibody production on immunization. CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had basally elevated serum IgM, IgG1, and IgG2a levels (Figure S3C). On immunization with both TD and TI antigens (DNP-CGG and DNP-Ficoll, respectively), CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice elicited normal or enhanced production of DNP-specific antibodies (Figures S3D and S3E). CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice had significantly enhanced production of antigen-specific IgM, IgG1, and IgG2a on immunization with TD antigens compared to the wild type and significantly enhanced production of antigen-specific IgM on immunization with TI antigen (Figures S3D and S3E). CARD11+/C49Y mice had significantly enhanced production of antigen-specific IgG1 and IgG2a after TD antigen immunization and enhanced antigen-specific IgG3 after TI antigen immunization compared to the wild type (Figures S3D and S3E). Thus, CARD11 C49Y mice differ from E134G mice and patients with BENTA in having attenuated GC and plasmacytic differentiation with no resulting impairment of immune response. Increased B cell numbers compensate for the reduced percentage of GC B cells and plasma cells, resulting in enhanced generation of antibody in response to antigens.

CARD11 C49Y enhances B cell proliferation and survival in vitro in a cell-type-, stimulus-, and genotype-specific manner

To probe potential causes of the exhibited B cell expansion in CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice, we compared purified FO and MZ B cells in assays for proliferation and apoptosis in response to stimulation with anti-IgM or lipopolysaccharide (LPS) in vitro. Interestingly, we observed different advantages of CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells in these assays, dependent on stimulus, B cell subtype, and whether cells were heterozygous or homozygous for CARD11 C49Y. In response to a subsaturating dose of anti-IgM, which triggers BCR, FO B cells from CARD11+/C49Y mice proliferated significantly better than wild-type or CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO B cells in a CellTrace Violet dye dilution assay and displayed a nonsignificant trend toward less apoptotic death with a lower percentage of cells staining positive for annexin V and propidium iodide (Figures 2A–2C). However, upon stimulation with a subsaturating concentration of LPS, which triggers the TLR4, FO B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice displayed a significant proliferative advantage over wild-type FO B cells and had fewer cells staining for apoptosis and necrosis markers, while CARD11+/C49Y FO B cells displayed an intermediate phenotype (Figures 2A–2C).

In contrast, MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice proliferated significantly better than wild-type or CARD11+/C49Y MZ B cells upon stimulation with either anti-IgM or LPS and displayed a lower propensity for apoptosis and necrosis (Figures 2A, 2B, and 2D). Upon stimulation with anti-IgM, CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells had 5.4-fold and 3.6-fold more percent cells divided than wild-type and CARD11+/C49Y cells, respectively (Figure 2D). Although no proliferation of B cells was observed in the absence of stimulation, CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells were notably resistant to cell death, with 4.8-fold and 3.4-fold fewer cells staining positive for apoptosis and necrosis markers than wild-type and CARD11+/C49Y MZ B cells, respectively (Figures 2B and 2D). The differential response of B cells to anti-IgM or LPS was not trivially due to differences in surface IgM or TLR4 expression, which were not significantly different in FO or MZ B cells from CARD11+/C49Y or CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice (Figure 2E).

These results revealed that CARD11 C49Y modulates the proliferation and survival of B cells in a manner that is cell-subtype specific, stimulus specific, and different depending on whether B cells are heterozygous or homozygous for the mutation, and they raised the possibility that the sizable expansion of MZ B cells in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice could be linked to the enhanced proliferation of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells in response to BCR triggering coupled to a reduced tendency toward apoptosis.

Absence of constitutive signaling to canonical NF-κB, mTOR, JNK, and AKT in CARD11 C49Y-expressing B cells

CARD11 is required for antigen receptor signaling to canonical NF-κB, JNK, mTOR, and AKT pathways.21,23–27 To assess whether any of these pathways were constitutively activated in the CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice, especially in a manner that correlated with the proliferation phenotypes observed in vitro, we probed signaling in these pathways in the absence and presence of stimulation with PMA/ionomycin or anti-IgM in both FO and MZ B cells.

Canonical NF-κB activation was assessed by western blotting for phosphorylated IKKα/β and phosphorylated IκBα. Surprisingly, no significant constitutive activation of the canonical NF-κB pathway was detected in either CARD11+/C49Y or CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells, evident from the absence of phosphorylated IκBα in the unstimulated condition for both FO and MZ B cells (Figures 3A and 3B; see also quantitation in Figures S4A and S4B). We also did not observe a difference in phosphorylated IKKα/β levels between the three genotypes in the unstimulated condition for both FO and MZ B cells (Figures 3A, 3B, S4A, and S4B). Activation of the canonical NF-κB pathway was inducible with treatment with PMA/ionomycin, which mimics BCR stimulation, in both CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells (Figures 3A, 3B, S4A, and S4B). FO B cells from both CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice and MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice displayed lower levels of basal IκBα. In FO B cells from CARD11+/C49Y mice, there was significant enhancement of inducible IκBα phosphorylation but no enhancement of IκBα degradation and IKKα/β phosphorylation, as compared to wild-type and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells (Figures 3A, 3B, S4A, and S4B). In MZ B cells from CARD11+/C49Y mice, however, IκBα degradation was mildly enhanced on stimulation, with no significant effect on inducible IKKα/β phosphorylation or IκBα phosphorylation compared to wild-type B cells (Figures 3A, 3B, S4A, and S4B). CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells also showed no enhancement of inducible canonical NF-κB signaling; in fact, MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice displayed lower absolute inducible levels of phosphorylated IκBα and moderately less IκBα degradation in response to PMA/ionomycin compared to the wild type (Figures 3A, 3B, S4A, and S4B). Canonical NF-κB activation results in the translocation of NF-κB subunits p65 and p50, accompanied by p65 phosphorylation. There was also no observed presence or elevation in the levels of phosphorylated p65 in the mutant B cells in unstimulated or stimulated conditions that would indicate constitutive activation or hyperactivation, respectively (Figures 3A, 3B, S4A, and S4B). Instead, FO and MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice displayed lower inducible levels of phosphorylated p65 compared to the wild type, with MZ B cells displaying delayed p65 phosphorylation kinetics. These results were especially surprising in light of previous studies in which CARD11 C49Y was shown to constitutively and robustly activate NF-κB upon transient transfection and expression in cell lines.10

Since CARD11 signaling is known to activate MALT1 protease activity,28 we assayed the cleavage of the MALT1 substrate HOIL1 in unstimulated and stimulated mutant and control B cells. Interestingly, both CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells displayed constitutive cleavage of HOIL1 that was more pronounced in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells (Figures 3C, 3D, S4C, and S4D), although this enhanced protease activity did not correlate with enhanced constitutive or stimulated NF-κB activation.

Similarly, the mTOR pathway was not constitutively active in unstimulated mutant B cells, as reflected by the absence of detectable levels of phosphorylated S6 kinase (Figures 3E, 3F, S4E, and S4F). CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells had elevations in phosphorylated S6 (S235/S236 and S240/S244) upon stimulation with anti-IgM compared to CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11+/+. CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO B cells had elevated phosphorylation of S6 at S235/S236 and S240/S244, respectively, compared to CARD11+/+. No major consistent difference could be detected in mTOR pathway activation upon stimulation with anti-IgM in CARD11+/C49Y MZ B cells (Figures 3E, 3F, S4E, and S4F). In addition, we detected no constitutive activation of the JNK pathway as assayed by phospho-JNK in either CARD11+/C49Y or CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells (Figures 3G, 3H, S4G, and S4H). We did detect a reproducible increase in PMA/ionomycin-inducible activation of the JNK pathway in FO B cells from CARD11+/C49Y mice (Figures 3G, 3H, S4G, and S4H) and significant reduction of inducible JNK activation in FO B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice (Figures 3G, 3H, S4G, and S4H). No major consistent difference could be detected in JNK pathway activation upon stimulation in either CARD11+/C49Y or CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells.

Wei et al. reported that MZ B cell expansion in the K251M mouse model was due to the role of CARD11 in regulating the AKT-FOXO1 signaling axis.21 K215M mutant B cells had reduced AKT and FOXO1 phosphorylation, leading to elevated FOXO1 levels. The AKT-FOXO1 signaling axis was also not constitutively active in unstimulated CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells, as measured by phosphorylated species of AKT and FOXO1 (Figures 3I–3L and S4I–S4L). PMA/ionomycin-mediated activation of AKT in FO B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice was significantly elevated (Figures 3I and S4I). However, there was a detectable reduction in inducible FOXO1 phosphorylation in CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO B cells as compared to the wild type (Figures 3K and S4K). AKT activation in FO and MZ B cells from CARD11+/C49Y and MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice was not significantly different from that in wild-type controls (Figures 3I, 3J, S4I, and S4J). Similarly, FO and MZ B cells from CARD11+/C49Y and MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice showed no significant difference from wild-type mice in PMA/ionomycin-induced FOXO1 phosphorylation (Figures 3K, 3L, S4K, and S4L).

Taken together, these results suggested that in primary FO and MZ B cells, CARD11 C49Y does not constitutively activate canonical NF-κB, mTOR, JNK, or AKT pathways, but may have modulatory effects on the induction of canonical NF-κB, mTOR, JNK, or AKT by BCR engagement, depending on the B cell subtype and whether B cells are heterozygous or homozygous for CARD11 C49Y.

Gene set enrichment analysis of CARD11 C49Y-expressing B cells identifies correlates of proliferation, survival, and noncanonical NF-κB pathway potentiation

Next, we performed bulk mRNA sequencing on purified FO and MZ B cells from wild-type, CARD11+/C49Y, and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice in the absence and presence of anti-IgM stimulation to identify functionally relevant transcriptomic differences in an unbiased manner. We used gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) to identify gene sets that were statistically enriched in CARD11+/C49Y FO or CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells, as compared to wild-type B cells treated under the same conditions, either unstimulated or treated with anti-IgM for 24 h. After 24 h of anti-IgM treatment, CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells exhibited an enrichment of proliferation-associated genes, including those in the hallmark sets of MYC target genes, G2M checkpoint genes, and E2F target genes, and a negative enrichment of genes in the hallmark set of apoptosis (Figure S5A), consistent with the proliferation and anti-apoptotic phenotypes on BCR stimulation compared to wild-type MZ B cells observed in vitro (Figure 2). Similarly, after anti-IgM treatment in comparison to wild-type FO B cells, CARD11+/C49Y FO B cells also exhibited an enrichment of proliferation associated genes and a negative enrichment of genes in the hallmark set of apoptosis (Figure S5B), consistent with the proliferation and anti-apoptotic phenotypes observed in vitro (Figure 2).

GSEA did not detect the differential regulation of target genes of canonical NF-κB, JNK, and mTOR pathways in both unstimulated and stimulated conditions in either CARD11 C49Y-expressing FO or MZ B cells, consistent with the lack of constitutive signaling in these pathways that we observed in biochemical assays (Figure 3) and the lack of strong enhancement of these pathways after anti-IgM or PMA/ionomycin treatment (Figure 3).

Unexpectedly, GSEA revealed that unstimulated CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells exhibited an enrichment of the Gene Ontology pathway of NIK NF-κB signaling, which is otherwise known as the noncanonical NF-κB pathway (Figure S5C). Noncanonical NF-κB signaling is critical for mature B cell survival and maturation, lymphoid organogenesis, bone metabolism, and dendritic cell activation.14,15,29,30 The enrichment of genes in this Gene Ontology pathway was evident only for CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells compared to wild-type B cells and only in unstimulated MZ B cells. Notably, CARD11 signaling has not been previously directly linked to noncanonical NF-κB regulation.

Differential gene expression (DESeq2) analysis identified 1,345 genes that were up- or downregulated 2-fold or more (p < 0.05) in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells as compared to wild-type MZ B cells in the absence of stimulation and 1,836 genes that were up- or downregulated 2-fold or more (p < 0.05) in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells after 24 h of anti-IgM, as compared to wild-type MZ B cells (Figure S5D). It was not immediately obvious which of these genes may contribute to the enhanced proliferation and decreased apoptosis of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells evident during in vitro proliferation assays (Figure 2), but there was evidence of C49Y impacting gene expression overall.

Since CARD11 GoF mutations are found in ~10% of ABC DLBCL cases,13 we investigated whether the differential gene expression observed in unstimulated mutant B cells compared to the wild type reflected a previously reported ABC DLBCL gene signature.31–34 We did not observe any positive enrichment of signature genes in the ABC DLBCL, OCI-Ly3, and BCR activation datasets in the unstimulated CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells compared to the wild type (Figure S5F). As expected, these signature genes were found to be positively enriched in the stimulated wild-type MZ B cells compared to unstimulated cells, validating that the upregulation of these genes typify an activated B cell phenotype (Figure S5F). Similarly, CARD11+/C49Y MZ, CARD11+/C49Y FO, and CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO B cells did not exhibit the enrichment of activated B cell signature genes or ABC-DLBCL-associated genes in the unstimulated condition compared to wild-type FO or MZ B cells. These findings are consistent with the conclusion that CARD11 C49Y is unable to induce the constitutive canonical NF-κB activation that is responsible for an activated B cell phenotype.

To confirm directly that CARD11 C49Y did not constitutively activate canonical NF-κB in MZ B cells, which displayed the most pronounced expansion phenotype, we examined 429 transcripts previously identified as direct targets of NF-κB, bound to either RelA or cRel in anti-IgM-treated wild-type splenic B cells,35 and compared their levels in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells as compared to wild-type MZ B cells. Of these, only 42 showed a statistically different expression level in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells, and 30 were downregulated (Figure S5G), confirming that the canonical NF-κB pathway is not constitutively activated in MZ B cells expressing CARD11 C49Y.

Enhanced survival of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells in vitro

Since noncanonical NF-κB activity has been shown to promote B cell survival, we next explored whether CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells exhibited enhanced survivability in vitro. FO and MZ B cells from wild-type, CARD11+/C49Y, and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice were isolated and cultured in the absence of mitogenic stimuli for 7 days. CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells displayed a significant survival advantage over both wild-type and CARD11+/C49Y MZ B cells that was apparent as early as 24 h and persisted through 7 days of culture (Figure 4A). In contrast, we observed no significant advantage in the survival of CARD11+/C49Y or CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO B cells in comparison to wild-type FO B cells (Figure 4A).

To determine whether the enhanced survival of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells was cell autonomous or due to autocrine stimulation, we co-cultured wild-type and CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells initially in a 1:1 ratio and monitored cell numbers for 4 days by labeling either wild-type or CARD11C49Y/C49Y cells with CellTrace Violet. In this assay, CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells exhibited a clear survival advantage over wild-type cells, and their co-culture did not appear to rescue wild-type MZ B cells from death in culture (Figure 4B), suggesting that the increased survival of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells was due to a cell-autonomous effect of CARD11 C49Y homozygosity and not to secretion of a prosurvival autocrine factor.

B cell activating factor (BAFF) interacts with BAFFR to stimulate the noncanonical NF-κB pathway and is known to be essential for the homeostatic survival of mature B cells.14 We cultured FO and MZ B cells from wild-type, CARD11+/C49Y, and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice in the absence and presence of 200 ng/mL BAFF for 3 days and monitored cell numbers. While BAFF treatment enhanced the survivability of both MZ and FO B cells in this assay (Figures 4C and 4D), the differential response of CARD11C49Y/C49Y and CARD11+/C49Y B cells to BAFF was most significant in MZ B cells (Figures 4C and 4D). In the presence of BAFF, CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells displayed enhanced survivability compared to either CARD11+/C49Y or wild-type MZ B cells (Figure 4C), while heterozygous or homozygous expression of CARD11 C49Y had little effect on the BAFF-induced survival of FO B cells (Figure 4D). Surface expression of BAFFR was elevated by only 1.2-fold in both FO and MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice (Figure 4E), which did not correlate with the specifically enhanced response of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells to BAFF treatment.

To test whether the observed enhanced survival of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells in vitro was linked to either canonical or noncanonical NF-κB signaling, we cultured CARD11C49Y/C49Y and wild-type MZ B cells in the presence of inhibitors specific to IKKβ (KINK-1) and NIK (SMI1) that target the canonical and noncanonical pathways, respectively. The NIK inhibitor, but not the IKKβ inhibitor, dampened the basal survival advantage of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells (Figure 4F). We verified that the concentration of KINK-1 used in these experiments could inhibit anti-IgM-mediated proliferation of both FO and MZ B cells from wild-type and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice, indicating that the concentration was competent to inhibit canonical NF-κB signaling through IKKβ (data not shown). The results suggest that CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells uniquely exhibit enhanced survival linked to noncanonical NF-κB activity.

CARD11 C49Y dysregulates the noncanonical NF-κB pathway in MZ B cells in a dose-responsive manner

To directly assess whether noncanonical NF-κB pathway signaling was dysregulated by CARD11 C49Y, we cultured FO and MZ B cells from wild-type, CARD11+/C49Y, and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice in the absence and presence of BAFF for 16 h and analyzed nuclear and cytoplasmic cell extracts for signaling intermediates by immunoblotting. We observed a large increase in p100 protein levels in the cytoplasm of CARD11 C49Y-expressing FO and MZ B cells that was gene dose dependent (Figures 5A and 5B). p100 levels were 6.8-fold and 3.9-fold higher in CARD11C49Y/C49Y and CARD11+/C49Y MZ B cells, respectively, compared to wild-type MZ B cells and were 4.0-fold and 2.1-fold higher in CARD11C49Y/C49Y and CARD11+/C49Y FO B cells, respectively, compared to wild-type FO B cells. In contrast, the levels of p52, the product of p100 processing, were not significantly elevated in the cytoplasm of FO or MZ B cells from CARD11+/C49Y or CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice (Figures 5A and 5B; see also quantitation in Figures S6A and S6B). The p52/p100 ratio, a readout of basal p100 processing that occurs in the absence of noncanonical NF-κB pathway ligand, was significantly reduced in unstimulated MZ and FO B cells from both CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice as compared to wild-type controls. For example, in the cytoplasm of MZ B cells, the p52/p100 ratio was 80% lower in CARD11+/C49Y MZ B cells and 90% lower in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells than in wild-type MZ B cells (Figures 5A and S6A). This reduction in the p52/p100 ratio suggested that the basal p100 processing to p52 was inhibited in CARD11 C49Y-expressing B cells. We also considered an alternative possible explanation for the elevated p100 protein levels, namely that CARD11 C49Y promoted the transcriptional activation of the p100-encoding NF-κB2 gene, consistent with the known intersection of the canonical and noncanonical NF-κB pathways downstream of BCR triggering mediated through transcriptional induction of NF-κB2.36–39 Arguing against this possibility, the normalized read counts of NF-κB2 mRNA were not significantly different among wild-type, CARD11+/C49Y, and CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO or MZ B cells in our RNA-sequencing data obtained from unstimulated B cells (Figure S5C). In addition, when we assayed NF-κB2 mRNA levels from wild-type, CARD11+/C49Y, and CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO or MZ B cells using RT-qPCR, we detected no increase in NF-κB2 mRNA levels as a result of CARD11 C49Y expression (Figures 5C and 5D). These data suggest that p100 levels were elevated in CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells due to the inhibition of basal p100 processing to p52.

The analysis of cytoplasmic and nuclear extracts of CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells treated with BAFF for 16 h revealed a robust BAFF-stimulated induction of p100 processing and nuclear translocation of p52 and RelB that was most pronounced in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells (Figures 5A and S6A). BAFF treatment of CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells resulted in 6.9-fold more nuclear accumulation of p52 compared to wild-type MZ B cells and 4.8-fold more nuclear accumulation of RelB (Figures 5A and S6A). Notably, this increase in BAFF-stimulated nuclear p52 and RelB was not as large in FO B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice (Figures 5B and S6B), suggesting a specific enhancement of BAFF-induced noncanonical NF-κB signaling by homozygous CARD11 C49Y in MZ B cells but not FO B cells. Taken together, the data suggested that homozygous levels of CARD11 C49Y somehow inhibit basal p100 processing to p52 in the absence of BAFF but do not inhibit the induced p100 processing to p52 that occurs in response to BAFF ligand. In MZ B cells, it appears that the increased level of p100 in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells serves as an increased substrate for noncanonical NF-κB signaling, which results in elevated levels of nuclear p52 and RelB in response to BAFF.

A block in basal p100 processing to p52 by CARD11 C49Y in CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells could occur at several possible steps. In theory, basal p100 processing in unstimulated B cells could be initiated by low basal levels of NIK activity, which could phosphorylate and stimulate IKKα activity.15 NIK and IKKα could then phosphorylate p100, leading to its ubiquitinylation and selective proteasome-mediated processing to p52.41 We suspected that CARD11 C49Y may somehow cause a further reduction in NIK levels, which are normally kept low in unstimulated B cells by the TRAF2-TRAF3-cIAP1/2 complex. However, we detected no reduction in NIK protein levels in CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO or MZ B cells in comparison to wild-type controls (Figures 5E and 5F). Furthermore, we detected no reduction in IKKα or phospho-IKKα levels in unstimulated CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO or MZ B cells in comparison to wild-type controls (Figures 5E and 5F). We also observed no change in levels of TRAF2 or cIAP1/2 in CARD11 C49Y-expressing B cells, but did observe an increase in TRAF3 levels (Figures 5E and 5F; see also quantitation in Figures S6C and S6D). Normalized read counts of TRAF3 mRNA were increased only by 17% and 26% in CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11 C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells, respectively, and by 16% and 24% in CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11 C49Y/C49Y FO B cells, respectively, compared to the wild type, much less than the observed increase in TRAF3 protein levels (Figure S5E). Since the elevated TRAF3 protein levels in CARD11 C49Y-expressing cells did not result in lower basal NIK levels, it did not appear that elevated TRAF3 was part of the mechanism by which CARD11 C49Y inhibited basal p100 processing. We next probed for p100 and phospho-p100 in whole-cell lysates and found a clear reduction in the phospho-p100/p100 ratio in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ and FO B cells (Figure 5G; see also quantitation in Figure S6E). In MZ B cells, the fraction of p100 that was phosphorylated in unstimulated B cells dropped 42.2-fold in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells and 19.5-fold in CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO B cells (Figures 5G and S6E). The data suggest that homozygous expression of CARD11 C49Y somehow leads to an inhibition in the basal phosphorylation of p100, resulting in the inhibition of basal p100 processing to p52. The inhibition of basal p100 phosphorylation did not appear to result from reductions in NIK or IKKα protein levels but could possibly be a consequence of reduced NIK or IKKα kinase activity or reduced targeting of these kinases to p100.

GoF CARD11 variants inhibit NIK-induced p100 processing by a mechanism independent from signaling to canonical NF-κB

To bolster our conclusion that CARD11 C49Y causes p100 accumulation by inhibiting its basal processing to p52, we developed a heterologous assay in HEK293T cells for NIK-induced p100 processing. Co-expression of FLAG-p100 with myc-NIK in HEK293T cells by transient transfection resulted in clear processing of p100 to p52 (Figure 6A). In this assay, the p52/p100 ratio is a readout of p100 processing, since the FLAG-p52 detected derives from processing of transfected FLAG-p100. While co-expression of wild-type CARD11 had no effect on NIK-induced p100 processing in this assay, co-expression of CARD11 C49Y reduced processing by 3.6-fold, as reflected in a 3.6-fold drop in the relative p52/p100 ratio (Figure 6A; see also quantitation in Figure S6F). Consistent with an inhibition of p100 processing to p52, the phospho-p100/p100 ratio was reduced by 2.9-fold in the presence of CARD11 C49Y but was not reduced by wild-type CARD11 (Figures 6A and S6F). Notably in this assay, CARD11 C49Y expression did not reduce NIK expression (Figure 6A), consistent with our observations in primary MZ B cells (Figure 5E).

To test whether IKKα was required for NIK-induced p100 processing in this system, we knocked out IKKα in HEK293T cells by lentiviral mediated CRISPR-Cas9 genomic editing. The knockout of IKKα abrogated NIK-induced p100 processing in this transient transfection assay (Figure 6B). In the absence of p100 processing, CARD11 C49Y co-expression slightly elevated p100 protein levels by 1.6-fold, an effect much less than the 8.2-fold increase in p100 protein levels elicited by CARD11 C49Y in control HEK293T cells expressing a nontarget (NT) single-guide RNA, which were competent for NIK-induced processing (Figure 6B). These results revealed that in this HEK293T assay, IKKα is required for NIK-induced p100 processing, consistent with previous findings in primary cells,42 and supported the conclusion that CARD11 C49Y can inhibit the p100 processing to p52 that is initiated by NIK and that requires IKKα. In control NT cells, co-expression of CARD11 C49Y did not result in lower endogenous IKKα levels (Figure 6B), suggesting that CARD11 C49Y does not inhibit processing by lowering IKKα protein levels, consistent with our findings in primary MZ B cells (Figure 5E).

CARD11 is well known to signal through the canonical NF-κB pathway,2 and CARD11 C49Y displays constitutive GoF ability in the canonical NF-κB pathway context upon transient expression in cell lines.10,16 To test whether the ability of CARD11 C49Y to inhibit p100 processing was dependent on signaling through canonical NF-κB, we knocked out IKKγ in HEK293T cells by lentiviral-mediated CRISPR-Cas9 genomic editing. In the absence of IKKγ, NIK co-expression with p100 robustly induced p100 processing (Figure 6C), and CARD11 C49Y co-expression inhibited NIK-induced processing while wild-type CARD11 did not (Figure 6C; see also quantitation in Figure S6G). To confirm that IKKγ knockout in these cells reduced the activation of canonical NF-κB by CARD11 C49Y, we transfected IKKγ knockout and NT control cells with the Igκ2-IFN-LUC luciferase reporter in the presence of wild-type CARD11 or CARD11 C49Y. As expected, the lack of IKKγ led to a severe reduction in the induction of the Igκ2-IFN-LUC reporter by CARD11 C49Y (Figure 6C). These results clearly suggested that the inhibition of p100 processing by CARD11 C49Y does not require signaling through the canonical NF-κB pathway.

Previous studies have demonstrated that CARD11 C49Y is hyperactive for canonical NF-κB signaling because the C49Y mutation prevents autoinhibition by the ID in CARD11 that normally keeps CARD11 in a closed inactive state prior to antigen receptor engagement.4,5,10 The C49Y mutation reduces the ability of the ID to bind intramolecularly to the CARD-LATCH-coiled coil region of CARD11, which converts CARD11 into a constitutively open and active state.4,5,10 The ability of CARD11 C49Y to inhibit p100 processing could be due to a unique specific property of the C49Y amino acid substitution or, alternatively a consequence of CARD11 being rendered constitutively open and active. To test these possibilities, we assayed other CARD11 GoF mutants that have also been shown to be constitutively open for the ability to inhibit NIK-induced p100 processing in the HEK293T cell assay. The potent L251P and G123D GoF CARD11 mutants were just as active as C49Y in inhibiting p100 processing in this assay, while T117A, which has been shown to be a weaker GoF mutant,10 was less able to inhibit p100 processing (Figure 6D; see also quantitation in Figure S6H). Furthermore, the CARD11ΔID variant, which is constitutively open and active due to the deletion of the ID, was also able to potently inhibit p100 processing (Figures 6D and S6H). In contrast, the loss-of-function (LoF) CARD11 mutant R30W displayed activity equivalent to wild-type CARD11 in this assay (Figures 6D and S6H). These results suggested that the ability to inhibit NIK-induced p100 processing to p52 is not a unique property conferred by the C49Y mutation but is a capability of CARD11 in its open, active scaffold conformation.

We next assayed a panel of CARD11ΔID double-deletion constructs in the HEK293T p100 processing assay to define which domains in CARD11 are required for inhibition of p100 processing. The domains that appeared most required in this assay were the CARD, coiled-coil, L3, and SH3 domains (Figure 6E). In contrast, the deletion of the LATCH, PDZ, L4, or GUK domains had little effect on the inhibition of p100 processing by CARD11ΔID (Figure 6E). We assayed the same constructs in HEK293T cells for their ability to activate canonical NF-κB signaling as reflected in the induction of the Igκ2-IFN-LUC luciferase reporter. As previously published,43 the domains required for reporter induction included the CARD, coiled-coil, L3, SH3, L4, and GUK domains (Figure 6E). Notably, the L4 and GUK domains were required for canonical NF-κB activation, while they were not required for the inhibition of NIK-induced p100 processing, further supporting the idea that the inhibition of p100 processing by open, active CARD11 variants is independent of signaling to canonical NF-κB.

CARD11 is required for normal p100 protein levels in FO and MZ B cells

The ability of multiple open and active CARD11 variants to inhibit NIK-induced p100 processing to p52 suggested the possibility that wild-type CARD11 may function in normal B cells to regulate p100 protein levels. In this scenario, BCR engagement, which normally converts CARD11 into an open and active scaffold via the neutralization of the ID,2 could generate an active species of CARD11 that could inhibit basal p100 processing during periods of BCR signaling to maintain physiological levels of p100 required for MZ B cell homeostasis. To test this idea, we enumerated FO and MZ B cells in CARD11−/− mice that we recently generated as a by-product of the generation of CARD11R30W/+ mice.44 As previously reported,21,45 CARD11−/− mice exhibited a significant 3.0-fold decrease in the number of MZ B cells but no significant reduction in FO B cell numbers (Figures 7A and 7B). We purified FO and MZ B cells from CARD11−/− and control wild-type mice and quantitated the relative amounts of p100 protein by immunoblotting. CARD11−/− FO B cells displayed an 80% reduction in relative p100 protein, while CARD11−/− MZ B cells exhibited a 70% reduction in p100 protein (Figure 7C; see also quantitation in Figure S6I), consistent with a requirement for CARD11 in maintaining physiological p100 protein levels. This requirement for CARD11 could potentially be explained by the role of CARD11 in BCR signaling to canonical NF-κB, which is known to induce transcription of the NF-κB2 gene that encodes p100. However, the measurement of NF-κB2 mRNA revealed that the absence of CARD11 in CARD11−/− FO or MZ B cells did not lead to an apparent reduction in NF-κB2 mRNA (Figure 7D). Furthermore, the relative p52/p100 ratio was 4.5-fold higher in CARD11−/− FO B cells and 2.6-fold higher in CARD11−/− MZ B cells (Figure 7C), suggesting the enhanced basal processing of p100 to p52 in the absence of CARD11. These results are consistent with a required role for CARD11 for maintaining p100 protein levels at a post-transcriptional step, mediated via the inhibition of basal p100 processing to p52.

This potentiates the noncanonical NF-κB pathway such that on ligand-receptor interaction the inhibition is overcome, and accumulating NIK can process the pool of p100 that has amassed to produce high levels of active p52 and, subsequently, p52:RelB dimers. These translocate to the nucleus and cause higher induction of NF-κB2-dependent transcriptional programs.

Gene dose and oligomer composition both contribute to MZ B cell expansion in homozygous CARD11 C49Y mice

CARD11 signals as an oligomer of undetermined stoichiometry.46 Previous studies of LoF CARD11 variants associated with the CADINS primary immunodeficiency syndrome have demonstrated that heterozygous cells that express one wild-type CARD11 locus and one mutant CARD11 locus assemble mixed wild-type:mutant oligomers in which the mutant subunits can affect signaling of co-assembled wild-type subunits.46,47 We were curious as to whether the 14.1-fold expansion of MZ B cells in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice, as compared to CARD11+/C49Y mice, concomitant with higher p100 levels in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells, was due to the fact that CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells express homogeneous CARD11 C49Y oligomers while CARD11+/C49Y B cells express mixed wild-type:C49Y oligomers, which may have reduced ability to inhibit p100 processing compared to C49Y:C49Y oligomers. Alternatively, the enhanced phenotype of CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice may be due to a simply higher dose of CARD11 C49Y, whether or not it assembles in mixed or homogeneous oligomers. To explore these possibilities, we bred CARD11+/C49Y mice to CARD11−/− mice to generate and analyze CARD11−/C49Y mice, which should express levels of CARD11 C49Y equivalent to that in CARD11+/C49Y mice but assembled into homogeneous CARD11 C49Y:C49Y oligomers. CARD11−/C49Y mice exhibited total B cell, FO cell, and MZ B cell numbers that were not significantly different from those of CARD11+/C49Y mice (Figures S7A and S7B). In addition, the absolute number of MZ B cells was much lower in CARD11−/C49Y mice than in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice (Figures S7A and S7B), suggesting that the dose of CARD11 C49Y is important for determining the MZ B cell expansion phenotype, not just whether CARD11 C49Y is in homogeneous or mixed oligomers. Immunoblotting of isolated FO and MZ B cells from CARD11−/C49Y mice revealed that CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells exhibit higher levels of p100 protein than either CARD11−/C49Y or CARD11+/C49Y B cells, especially for MZ B cells (Figure S7C), consistent with a correlative role for p100 levels in determining MZ B cell expansion in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice.

DISCUSSION

Our results reveal a previously unappreciated ability of the CARD11 signaling scaffold to regulate the noncanonical NF-κB pathway by modulating levels of the p100 protein post-transcriptionally in a manner that specifically impacts MZ B cell survival and expansion. Homozygous expression of the GoF CARD11 C49Y mutant leads to a significant increase in p100 protein levels without an increase in NF-κB2 mRNA levels. Our results suggest a model (Figure 7E) in which GoF CARD11 inhibits the NIK-induced, IKKα-dependent basal processing of p100 to p52, leading to the accumulation of p100 protein. Importantly, once the noncanonical NF-κB signaling pathway is activated by BAFF, GoF CARD11 does not inhibit the induced processing of p100 that results from the NIK accumulation that follows recruitment of the cIAP1/2:TRAF2:TRAF3 complex to the BAFFR (Figure 7E). The BAFF-induced processing of increased p100 levels results in increased levels of active p52:RelB heterodimers in the nucleus of MZ B cells and enhanced activation of transcriptional programs.

Our study of CARD11−/− B cells suggests that wild-type CARD11 functions to maintain p100 protein levels by a similar mechanism once wild-type CARD11 is converted to an open, active state by BCR triggering (Figure 7E), since CARD11−/− B cells display lower p100 protein levels with no decrease in NF-κB2 mRNA levels. The idea that activated CARD11 can inhibit basal NIK-induced processing is supported by our finding that a variety of constitutively open and active CARD11 variants can inhibit NIK-induced p100 processing to p52 in the heterologous HEK293T cell system.

Homozygous expression CARD11 C49Y leads to an impressive 14.1-fold increase in the steady-state number of splenic MZ B cells. In vitro, MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y display enhanced proliferation in response to anti-IgM and LPS and an enhanced survival that is cell autonomous, responsive to BAFF, and dependent on noncanonical NF-κB signaling. These effects on MZ B cells are more pronounced than effects on FO B cells. FO B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice expand to a much lesser extent in vivo and do not exhibit enhanced proliferation or survival in vitro in response to anti-IgM, or enhanced survival in vitro in response to BAFF treatment.

The specificity of the effect of homozygous CARD11 C49Y on MZ B cells is consistent with the known apparent enhanced sensitivity of MZ B cells to noncanonical NF-κB pathway activation.15,48 Constitutive activation of this pathway in the murine B cell lineage results in increased processing of p100 to p52, B cell hyperplasia, and, in particular, elevated numbers of MZ B cells, as observed in mice with B cell-specific deletion of TRAF2,49,50 TRAF3,51 cIAP1, and cIAP252,53 and overexpression of NIK.54 In contrast to these mouse models, homozygous CARD11 C49Y expression does not lead to constitutive noncanonical NF-κB pathway activation; rather, CARD11 C49Y appears to potentiate noncanonical NF-κB pathway output through the upregulation of the p100 protein level that serves as the substrate of regulated ligand-induced processing.

Other mouse models in which the p100 level appears increased in B cells include those with BAFF deficiency,50 BAFFR deficiency,50,55,56 B cell-specific NIK deletion,57 IKKα−/− mice, and IKKαAA knockin mice42 ; however, in each of these models both basal and ligand-induced p100 processing is ablated, unlike in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice.

It is well established that CARD11 plays a critical role in canonical NF-κB activation by BCR signaling as a signaling scaffold that transmits BCR engagement into the activation of the IKK complex, which, through IKKβ, phosphorylates IκB, leading to its ubiquitinylation and degradation and the nuclear translocation of canonical p50:p65 heterodimers.1,2,7 BCR-mediated activation of canonical NF-κB is well known to critically impact noncanonical NF-κB signaling via the induced transcription of the NF-κB2 gene, which encodes p100.36–39,58 Thus, our initial hypothesis was that CARD11 C49Y increased p100 protein levels by promoting increased NF-κB2 transcription via canonical NF-κB activation. Our data argue against this possibility. Heterozygous or homozygous CARD11 C49Y expression does not lead to constitutive activation of canonical NF-κB signaling in unstimulated FO or MZ B cells, as reflected in the lack of phosphorylated IκBα or IKKα/β and the lack of upregulation of known target genes of canonical NF-κB in unstimulated B cells, including NF-κB2 itself. Rather, CARD11 C49Y appears to upregulate p100 protein, at least in part, by inhibiting basal homeostatic NIK-induced p100 processing to p52, which is reflected in a heterologous assay in HEK293T cells conducted in the absence of noncanonical NF-κB pathway ligand. CARD11 C49Y inhibits p100 processing in this assay in a manner that does not require IKKγ or the L4 or GUK domains of CARD11, all of which are required for CARD11-mediated activation of canonical NF-κB.

How might CARD11 C49Y and other active forms of CARD11 inhibit basal NIK-induced p100 processing? The inhibition of basal p100 processing by CARD11 C49Y that we observe in CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells is concomitant with a lower fractional basal phosphorylation of p100 (phospho-p100/p100 ratio) that does not appear to result from lower NIK protein levels despite the increase in TRAF3 or from lower IKKα protein levels, and it does not coincide with lower levels of phospho-IKKα/β. This suggests the possibility that CARD11 C49Y somehow reduces IKKα specific activity or its ability to target p100. It is well known that when activated by upstream signaling, CARD11 inducibly associates with the IKK complex.43,59–62 It is tempting to speculate that CARD11 C49Y may constitutively associate with the IKK complex to some extent and sequester IKKα away from p100. If so, it must be the case that the induction of noncanonical NF-κB signaling by BAFF and other ligands must override this inhibition, presumably through the normal accumulation of NIK, which could interact with IKKα in a manner that is resistant to CARD11 C49Y.

Neither FO nor MZ B cells from CARD11+/C49Y or CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice exhibit constitutive activation of canonical NF-κB, JNK, mTOR, or AKT/FOXO1 pathways, as revealed both by biochemical assays and RNA-sequencing analyses of unstimulated cells. This was especially surprising given previous studies from our lab and others showing the strong ability of CARD11 C49Y to activate canonical NF-κB upon transient expression in both lymphoid and nonlymphoid cell lines,10,11,16,17 due to the ability of the C49Y mutation to prevent autoinhibition by the four REs in the CARD11 ID and convert CARD11 into a spontaneously open, active scaffold capable of binding Bcl10, MALT1, and HOIP10,11 and promoting linear ubiquitinylation of Bcl10.10,11 We speculate that the chronic expression of CARD11 C49Y in CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice leads to the induction of feedback-regulatory mechanisms that dampen CARD11 C49Y signaling to canonical NF-κB, possibly through the induction of an inhibitor of the pathway. This notion would be consistent with our observation that stable expression of active CARD11 variants in cell lines, like CARD11ΔID and reQM (quadruple RE mutant), leads to a drop in apparent constitutive signaling to canonical NF-κB that progresses over weeks in culture.46,63 The p100 protein itself may serve as an inhibitor that sequesters canonical NF-κB heterodimers into inhibitory κBsomes by displacing IκBα.64 The enhanced formation of κBsomes could explain the lower IκBα levels in CARD11 C49Y-expressing B cells, since NF-κB heterodimers are known to stabilize IκBα.65 We note that treatment of CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells with αIgM does not induce p100 degradation (Figures S4M and S4N), consistent with the possibility that p100 inhibits hyperactivation of the canonical NF-κB pathway. The constitutive activation of MALT1 protease activity that we observe in CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells may also feed back to limit constitutive and inducible canonical NF-κB activation via the cleavage of HOIL1, a requisite component of the LUBAC complex. Interestingly, while we observe this apparent MALT1 action on HOIL1, RelB, another MALT1 substrate, does not appear to be constitutively cleaved in CARD11 C49Y-expressing B cells and may be protected from cleavage by p100 binding.66 Other mechanisms for the lack of constitutive canonical NF-κB activation are possible and should be explored, especially in the light of understanding the effects of chronic GoF CARD11 mutant expression in lymphomas.2,8

Expression of CARD11 C49Y does appear to have some measurable effects on the induction of canonical NF-κB, JNK, or AKT/FOXO1 pathways by anti-IgM or PMA/ionomycin treatment that depends on whether the B cells are FO or MZ cells and whether they are heterozygous or homozygous for CARD11 C49Y. Notably, peak PMA/ionomycin-inducible phospho-IκBα and phospho-p65 are higher in FO and MZ B cells from CARD11+/C49Y mice, respectively, than in the wild type. However, FO B cells from CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice and MZ B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice have an apparent lower basal level of IκBα expression. FO B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice have significantly lower inducible phospho-p65, whereas in MZ B cells we note a delayed induction of phospho-p65. Anti-IgM-induced phosphorylation of S6 at S235/S235 and S240/S244 was higher in CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells compared to CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11+/+. CARD11+C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO B cells had elevated phosphorylation of S6 at S235/S236 and S240/S244, respectively, compared to CARD11+/+. PMA/ionomycin-inducible activation of the JNK pathway is mildly increased in FO B cells from CARD11+/C49Y mice but decreased in FO B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice. While peak PMA/ionomycin-inducible phospho-AKT is moderately higher in FO B cells from CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice compared to wild-type mice, peak phospho-FOXO1 is apparently reduced. It is not immediately obvious why these inducible signaling pathways are affected by CARD11 C49Y expression, but it is possible that some of these effects contribute to the specific phenotypes that we observe in in vitro proliferation and survival assays.

Interestingly, we observe cell-type-specific and stimulus-specific effects of CARD11 C49Y on anti-IgM- and LPS-induced proliferation and survival that differs between CARD11+/C49Y and CARD11C49Y/C49Y B cells. For FO B cells, CARD11+/C49Y cells proliferate more robustly than wild-type or CARD11C49Y/C49Y cells in response to anti-IgM, with concomitant trends in enhanced survival, while CARD11C49Y/C49Y FO B cells show greater proliferation and survival than wild-type or CARD11+/C49Y cells in response to LPS. In contrast, CARD11C49Y/C49Y MZ B cells show greater proliferation and survival than wild-type or CARD11+/C49Y MZ B cells in response to either anti-IgM or LPS. Thus, there appear to be specific properties of CARD11 oligomers that either consist solely of CARD11 C49Y subunits or consist of mixed wild-type:C49Y oligomers, and it is likely that these properties operate differently in FO and MZ B cell contexts. Further studies will be required to understand the bases for the observed specificities.

Patients with BENTA carry a germline heterozygous GoF mutation in CARD11 and clinically present with splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, polyclonal B cell lymphocytosis, recurrent infections, and impaired immune response associated with defective B cell differentiation, poor antigen-specific antibody generation, and T cell anergy. Twenty-nine patients with BENTA characterized to date have been reported to carry a variety of GoF CARD11 mutations in the CARD, LATCH, and coiled-coil domains.67 We assayed whether CARD11+/C49Y mice recapitulated the T cell hyporesponsiveness and reduced antigen-specific antibody generation observed in classical BENTA. However, CD4+ T cells from CARD11+/C49Y mice display no defect in proliferation or activation in response to TCR triggering and no obvious defect in antigen-specific antibody responses to both TI and TD antigens. Interestingly, the five patients with BENTA that carry the C49Y mutation that have been examined exhibit a milder BENTA phenotype overall with smaller reductions than other patients with BENTA in serum antibody titers, antigen-specific responses to vaccines, B cell lymphocytosis, and T cell activation in vitro.17,67,68 CARD11+/C49Y mice may be reflecting this milder BENTA presentation. Further work must be done to explain why certain CARD11 GoF mutations cause stronger immunodeficient phenotypes than others and to elucidate the genetic and environmental factors that contribute to BENTA immunodeficiency.

Several mouse models expressing different GoF CARD11 mutants have been reported to date, and remarkably each model appears to exhibit unique features over a wide phenotypic spectrum. These include the expansion of B cells that is lethal by postnatal day 6,69 the enhancement or reduction of the kinetics of the GC reaction,21,70 the promotion or reduction in MZ and B1 B cell development, and the expansion of Treg cells,21,70–72 each of which has been linked to either canonical NF-κB, JNK, mTOR, or AKT/FOXO1 signaling. The CARD11 C49Y model that we report is the first to link CARD11 GoF activity to the dysregulation of the noncanonical NF-κB signaling pathway, which is consistent with this model perhaps exhibiting the strongest effect on MZ B cell expansion. We do not think that the potentiation of the noncanonical NF-κB signaling pathway is a unique property conferred only by the substitution of tyrosine for cytosine at residue 49 in the CARD11 CARD domain. In our heterologous p100 processing assay in HEK293T cells, a panel of CARD11 GoF mutants appeared to inhibit NIK-induced p100 processing in a manner that correlates with the extent to which each mutant converts CARD11 into an open, active scaffold. Further understanding of the mechanism by which activated CARD11 inhibits p100 processing will be required to elucidate how different CARD11 GoF mutants might differentially perturb noncanonical NF-κB signaling. This knowledge will be essential for understanding the biological effects exerted by the wide variety of different CARD11 GoF mutants that have been reported in lymphoid cancers.13,16,34,73–84

Limitations of the study

In this study, we have shown that CARD11 C49Y expression results in the basal accumulation of p100, which is then inducibly processed in MZ B cells to produce high levels of p52:RelB in response to BAFF. We have not investigated which noncanonical NF-κB pathway-inducing ligand is responsible for the MZ B cell expansion in CARD11C49Y/C49Y mice, whether it is BAFF, another ligand, or a combination. The definition of the ligands involved would be valuable for understanding B cell expansions that occur as a result of CARD11 GoF mutations in humans. In addition, our mechanistic studies that demonstrate the ability of CARD11 C49Y to inhibit NIK-induced p100 processing in a manner independent of its ability to activate canonical NF-κB were performed in HEK293T cells. Although p100 processing is dependent on both NIK and IKKα in HEK293T cells, this system may not thoroughly reflect all determinants that are operative in primary B cells.

EXPERIMENTAL MODEL AND STUDY PARTICIPANT DETAILS

Mouse models

CARD11 C49Y mutant mice were generated by conventional embryonic stem cell homologous recombination by Ingenious Targeting Limited in the C57BL/6 X 129/SvEv strain background. Initially, mice were generated with a LoxP-STOP-LoxP (LSL) cassette in intron 2–3 upstream of exon 3 containing the C49Y encoding mutation. The LSL cassette was found to have no effect on CARD11 expression and was subsequently removed via breeding to Sox2-Cre. Mice were then backcrossed to C57BL/6J mice for >10 generations. The presence and germline transmission of the CARD11 C49Y allele was confirmed by sequencing and further genotyped using allele specific primers; Forward: 5′- AGGTGCATATGTCTACATACCTGC-3′ and Reverse: 5′- GGGTCCTCTGCAAGAGCCT-3’. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using the primers generated an amplicon of 271bp when wild-type allele was present and a PCR product of 484bp was produced in the presence of the recombined C49Y mutant allele. CARD11−/− mice were generated when making the CARD11 R30W mice44 and harbor a frameshift mutation c.93Cdel in CARD11 that causes a complete loss of CARD11 protein expression as confirmed by western blotting. Mice used in experiments were between the age range of 6 to 24 weeks, age and sex matched within experiments. Mice maintenance and procedures were approved by and conducted in compliance with the Johns Hopkins University Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (JHU ACUC).

Cell lines

HEK293T cells were obtained from the American Tissue Culture Collection (ATCC) and cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% FBS and 2% penicillin/streptomycin/L-glutamine in humidified 5% CO2 at 37°C. To generate IKKα-deficient HEK293T cells, guide RNA 5′-ACAGACGTTCCCGAAGCCGC-3′ was cloned into the lentiCRISPRv2 vector (Addgene plasmid #52961) using standard cloning techniques. To generate IKKγ-deficient HEK293T cells, guide RNA 5′-GGCAGCAGATCAGGACGTAC-3′ was cloned into lentiCRISPRv2. To package the lentiviruses, HEK293T cells were plated at 5 × 105 cells per well in a 6-well plate and transfected 24 h later using the calcium phosphate method with 464ng of psPAX2 (Addgene plasmid #12260) and 316ng of pCMV-VSVg (Addgene plasmid #8454), and 624ng of lentiCRISPRv2 vector. The media was replaced with 2mLs of fresh DMEM 24 h post-transfection and 4 × 106 HEK293Ts in 10cm plates were plated to be infected the next day. Viral supernatant was harvested 48 h post-transfection by centrifugation for 5mins at 1,400rpm. 1.8mLs of viral supernatant was added to the HEK293Ts plated the day before. Approximately 24 h post-infection, the media was changed to fresh DMEM containing 1μg/mL puromycin (Sigma #P8833) and cells were selected for at least 12 days and further maintained in DMEM containing puromycin. Loss of protein was confirmed using western blotting. Single clones of IKKγ-deficient HEK293T cells were isolated by plating 50,000 cells in a 10cm plate, transferring single colonies to 24-well plates, followed by expansion in DMEM containing puromycin. Cells were counted using Countess 3 (ThermoFisher #A49865).

CD4+ T cell activation and proliferation assays

Dilution of CTV was assessed via flow cytometry.

Immunizations and ELISAs

Female mice were immunized by intraperitoneal injection between 8 and 15 weeks of age with 50μg DNP-CGG (Biosearch Technologies) prepared in PBS and alhydrogel (InvivoGen) or 25μg DNP-Ficoll (Biosearch Technologies) in PBS. Blood samples were taken prior to immunization (day 0) as well as 7 and 14 days after immunization by tail nicks. Serum was isolated from whole blood by allowing blood to clot at room temperature for 20–30 min, then centrifuging at 2000×g for 10 min at 4°C. Antibody responses were measured by DNP-specific ELISAs. Diluted serum was incubated overnight on plates coated with 5 μg/mL DNP-BSA (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and DNP-specific antibodies were detected using HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies against the indicated mouse isotypes.

FO and MZ B cell proliferation and apoptosis assays

Follicular and Marginal Zone B cells were isolated from other splenocytes using the MZ and FO B Cell Isolation Kit (Miltenyi Biotec #130–100-366) according to instructions provided by the manufacturer. Cells were labeled with 1μM CellTrace Violet Dye as described before. Cells were plated at a density of 6 × 105 in 500μL, in either RPMI alone or with anti-IgM (Invitrogen #16–5092-85) or LPS (Sigma #L3012), in 24-well plates. Cells were stimulated with 4μg/mL anti-IgM or 0.1μg/mL LPS (for Follicular B cells) and 0.02μg/mL (for Marginal Zone B cells). At 48 h, cells were washed with FACS buffer and stained with Annexin V and Propidium Iodide, using an apoptosis kit (BioLegend #640932), in the dark for 15mins at room temperature. After washes in FACS buffer, cells were resuspended in Annexin V binding buffer as per kit protocol. Apoptosis was assessed by flow cytometry. Dilution of the CTV dye was assessed at 72 h via flow cytometry.

Western blotting

5 × 105-10 × 106 cells were resuspended in supplemented RPMI and allowed to rest for 30 min at 37°C, then stimulated for the indicated times in humidified 5% CO2 at 37°C with 4μg/mL anti-IgM (Invitrogen #16–5092-85), 15nM or 25nM PMA (Sigma #P1585) and 30nM or 62.5nM Ionomycin (Sigma #I0634) or 200ng/mL BAFF (Gibco #PHC1674). To harvest, cells were incubated for 10 min on ice, followed by centrifugation at 5,000rpm for 5 min at 4°C, and lysis in 35μL immunoprecipitation lysis buffer (IPLB)43 containing 1:100 protease inhibitor cocktail (Sigma #P8340) and 1:1000 Halt phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Thermo Scientific #78420). After spinning for 10 min at 13,200rpm at 4°C, lysates were heat-treated at 94°C in sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) buffer for 10 min, resolved by gel electrophoresis using a 10% SDS-PAGE gel, and transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes activated in methanol. Membranes were blocked using 5% milk in 1x TBST at room temperature for 1hr followed by addition of indicated primary antibody diluted in 5% milk in TBST and overnight incubation at 4°C. Subcellular fractionation

5 × 106-20 × 106 Follicular and Marginal Zone B cells were harvested and subcellular fractionation was performed using the Subcellular Fractionation Kit for Cultured Cells (ThermoFisher #78840) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

RNA sequencing

Follicular and Marginal Zone B cells were isolated as previously described and stimulated with 4μg/mL anti-IgM for 24 h at a density of 6 × 105 cells in 500μL RPMI. 5 × 106-10 × 106 cells were harvested and total RNA extraction was performed using a Qiagen RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen #74104) with on column DNase digestion according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Samples were sent to Novogene for library construction using polyA enrichment followed by sequencing (NovaSeq PE150 – 6Gb raw data per sample) with a depth of 20 × 106 broad reads per sample. Three biological replicates per condition and per cell type were submitted. Differentially expressed genes were identified by DESeq2.91 Heat maps were generated using Morpheus by Broad Institute. Gene set enrichment analysis was done with GSEA version 4.2.3. Further analysis was done using R Studio.

Quantitative RT-PCR

Follicular and Marginal Zone B cells were isolated as previously described and allowed to rest for 30 min at 37°C. 2 × 106-10 × 106 cells were harvested and total RNA extraction was performed as previously described using a Qiagen RNeasy Mini Kit. RNA concentration was measured using Nanodrop and complementary DNA was synthesized from RNA using TaqMan Reverse Transcription Reagents (Invitrogen #N8080234) using oligo (dT)16 primers according to the manufacturer’s instructions. qPCR was performed using TaqMan Universal Master Mix II (Applied Biosystems #4440043) along with TaqMan Gene Expression assays that consist of a pair of unlabeled PCR primers and a TaqMan probe with a FAM dye label. qPCR was performed using the CFX Connect Real-Time PCR Detection System (Biorad) using the NF-κB2 (Assay ID Mm00479807_m1 (Applied Biosystems)) and β-Actin (Assay ID Mm02619580_g1 (Applied Biosystems)) assays.

Survival assays

Follicular and Marginal Zone B cells were isolated as previously described and plated at a density of 6 × 105 cells in 500μL RPMI for the duration indicated with or without stimulation as indicated in the figures. On the day of harvesting, cells were counted using hemocytometer and Countess 3 (ThermoFisher #A49865).

Inhibitor treatments

Follicular and Marginal Zone B cells were isolated as previously described and plated at a density of 6 × 105 cells in 500μL RPMI. After resting the cells in humidified 5% CO2 at 37°C for 30 min, DMSO or inhibitors were added to the cells at indicated concentrations. After 1hr at 37°C, cells were stimulated as noted in the Figures. The inhibitors used were NIK SMI1 (Sigma #SML3129) and KINK-1 hydrochloride (Sigma #SML2098).

Expression constructs

Expression constructs for myc-tagged CARD11, CARD11-C49Y, CARD11-L251P, CARD11-G123D, CARD11-T117A, CARD11-R30W, CARD11ΔID, CARD11ΔIDΔCARD, CARD11ΔIDΔLATCH, CARD11ΔIDΔCC, CARD11ΔIDΔPDZ, CARD11ΔIDΔL3, CARD11ΔIDΔSH3, CARD11ΔIDΔL4, CARD11ΔIDΔGUK, Igκ2-IFN-LUC, pCSK-LacZ have been previously described.10,43,46 myc-tagged NIK was generated by cloning cDNA encoding human NIK (MAP3K14) as well as the c-Myc epitope into the pRK5 expression vector as previously described.86 Expression vector for FLAG-tagged p100 was a generous gift from M. Pagano.87 All constructs were sequenced at Johns Hopkins University Synthesis and Sequencing Facility to verify cloning.

Reporter assays and transfection assays

HEK293T cells were grown in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 2% penicillin/streptomycin/L-glutamine in humidified 5% CO2 at 37°C. 5 × 105 cells were plated in 2mLs of medium in each well of a 6-well plate. 24 h later 2000ng of DNA was transfected by the calcium phosphate method, including 50ng of the reporter Igκ2-IFN-LUC,23,92 50ng of pCSK-LacZ,85 and indicated amounts of CARD11 constructs, supplemented with empty parental expression vector (pcDNA3) to keep the total amount of DNA constant at 2000ng. Media was changed 22 to 24 h following transfection, and 40 to 44hrs after transfection cells were lysed in 350μL of 1x reporter lysis buffer (Promega #E3971). Cells were spun at 13,200 rpm at 4°C for 10 min to pellet debris. 4μL of lysate was used to measure luciferase activity using a luciferase assay kit (Promega #E1501) recorded by the luminometer (Berthold Technologies TriStarLB 941) integrating for 10s after a 2s delay, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. β-Galactosidase (β-Gal) activity was determined using 2μL of lysate and a chemiluminescent β-Gal reporter gene assay kit (Takara Bio #631712) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Relative activation of NF-κB was calculated for each sample by dividing their luciferase activity (normalized to β-Gal activity) by the activity from the sample without CARD11. Western blotting was used to check for CARD11 expression among samples. In HEK293T cells, p100 processing was measured by transfecting FLAG-tagged p100 and myc-tagged NIK in the indicated amounts. 5 × 105 cells were plated in 2mLs of medium in each well of a 6-well plate. 24 h later 2000ng of DNA was transfected by the calcium phosphate method, including indicated amounts of FLAG-tagged p100, myc-tagged NIK and different CARD11 constructs, supplemented with empty parental expression vector to keep total DNA at 2000ng for all samples. Media was changed 22 to 24 h following transfection, and 40 to 44 h after transfection cells were lysed in 500μL IPLB containing 1:100 protease inhibitor cocktail. Cells were spun at 13,200rpm at 4°C for 10 min to pellet debris and 30μL of lysate was transferred to an eppendorf with 15μL SDS loading dye. Following heat treatment at 94°C for 10 min, samples were run using SDS PAGE as mentioned previously. Western blotting was performed and CARD11 variants and NIK were detected using mouse anti-c-myc and p100 and processed p52 detected using mouse anti-FLAG.

Complete minimal hypersurfaces in ℍ 5 \mathbb{H}^{5} with constant scalar curvature and zero Gauss-Kronecker curvature
Qing Cui, Boyuan Zhang

Abstract
. We show that any complete minimal hypersurface in the five-dimensional hyperbolic space ℍ 5 \mathbb{H}^{5} , endowed with constant scalar curvature and vanishing Gauss-Kronecker curvature, must be totally geodesic. Cheng-Peng [ 3 ] recently conjecture that any complete minimal hypersurface with constant scalar curvature in ℍ 4 \mathbb{H}^{4} is totally geodesic. Our result partially confirms this conjecture in five dimensional setting.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{Q}^{3}(c)
be the three dimensional space form with constant sectional curvature
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
For a complete minimal surface
M
in
\mathbb{Q}^{3}(c)
with constant Gaussian curvature
K
and principal curvatures
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, the minimality condition combined with Gauss equation gives

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0,\quad K=c+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}.

This implies that both
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are constant, hence
M
is isoparametric. Furthermore, up to scaling
c
,
M
must be isometric to one of the following four surfaces:

•

The totally geodesic plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
;

•

The totally geodesic sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in round 3-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
;

•

The Clifford torus
\mathbb{S}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)
in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
;

•

The totally geodesic hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
in three dimensional hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

For higher dimensions, mathematicians
investigate complete minimal hypersurfaces with constant scalar curvature in space forms
\mathbb{Q}^{n+1}(c)
. For
c>0
, up to scaling
c
,
\mathbb{Q}^{n+1}(c)
is isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
. Complete, especially closed, minimal hypersurfaces with constant scalar curvature in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
have been studied intensively by Simons
[
10
]
, Chern-do Carmo-Kobayashi
[
5
]
,
Lawson
[
7
]
, Peng-Terng
[
9
]
and many other authors. Mathematicians conjecture that:

A closed minimal hypersurface with constant scalar curvature in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
must be isoparametric.

This conjecture is commonly referred to as
the strong version of the Chern conjecture
, which
was proved by Chang
[
2
]
for
n=3
and has been extensively investigated by many mathematicians over the past three decades.

For
c\leq 0
, up to scaling
c
,
\mathbb{Q}^{n+1}(c)
is isometric to either
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
or
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. In these two space forms, unlike the spherical case, as far as we know, there are no known examples of complete minimal hypersurfaces with constant scalar curvature other than the totally geodesic ones. Can one obtain similar results as in dimension two by replacing the Gaussian curvature with the scalar curvature? In 1994, Cheng-Wan
[
4
]
provided the first affirmative answer for the case
c=0
and
n=3
. Specifically, they proved that
a complete minimal hypersurface with constant scalar curvature in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
must be totally geodesic
.
Moreover, they gave a complete classification of constant mean curvature hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
with constant scalar curvature.
Recently, Cheng-Peng
[
3
]
studied complete minimal hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{4}(-1)
with constant scalar curvature. They proved that the second fundamental form
A
of such a hypersurface must satisfy
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}<\dfrac{21}{29}
. Similar to the two-dimensional case, the authors conjectured the following:

Conjecture

(
[
3
]
)
.

A complete minimal hypersurfaces with constant scalar curvature in
the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{4}
is totally geodesic.

The results of Cheng-Peng
[
3
]
strongly support the above conjecture. Note that if the above conjecture holds, it can be viewed as a Bernstein-type theorem in hyperbolic space. It was shown by do Carmo-Lawson
[
6
]
that, in hyperbolic space, the Bernstein theorem is true for all dimensions, which contrasts with the Euclidean case (where the Bernstein theorem is valid only for
n\leq 7
). A natural question arises:
Is the above conjecture still true for higher dimensions (
n\geq 4
)?

However, in higher dimensions, the minimality and the constant scalar curvature conditions seem insufficient or extremely challenging to ensure that the hypersurface is totally geodesic. Observe that every totally geodesic hypersurface must have zero Gauss-Kronecker curvature. Therefore, in this paper, we focus on the special case where
n=4
and the hypersurface has zero Gauss-Kronecker curvature. Under these assumptions, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be a complete minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{5}
with constant scalar curvature and zero Gauss-Kronecker curvature, then
M
is totally geodesic.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the fundamental notations and revisits essential formulas and results, with particular emphasis on the Omori-Yau maximum principle. Subsequently, Section 3 presents the detailed proof of Theorem
1
.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we establish the necessary notations and present several well-known fundamental formulas and results.

Let
M
be a minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{5}
with second fundamental form
A
. We choose a local orthonormal frame
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{5}
in
\mathbb{H}^{5}
such that
e_{1},\cdots,e_{4}
are tangent to
M
. Let
\left\{\omega^{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{5}
denote the dual frame of
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{5}
. The second fundamental form
A
can then be expressed as
A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{4}h_{ij}\omega^{i}\otimes\omega^{j}
, where
h_{ij}=h_{ji}
.
Unless otherwise specified, all summations in this paper range from 1 to 4.
The minimality of
M
implies that the mean curvature
H
vanishes, i.e.,
H=\dfrac{1}{4}\sum_{i}h_{ii}=0
.
We define the following notations:

\displaystyle F_{,i}=\nabla_{i}F,\ \ F_{,ij}=\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}F,\ \ h_{ijk}=\nabla_{k}h_{ij}\ \text{and}\ h_{ijkl}=\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}h_{ij},

where
\nabla_{j}
is the covariant differentiation operator with respect to
e_{j}
.
The Gauss equation, Codazzi equation and Ricci identities are given by (cf.
[
5
]
):

(1)

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}=

\displaystyle-\left(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}\right)+h_{ik}h_{jl}-h_{il}h_{jk}.

(2)

\displaystyle h_{ijk}=

\displaystyle h_{ikj}.

(3)

\displaystyle h_{ijkl}-h_{ijlk}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}h_{im}R_{mjkl}+\sum_{m}h_{mj}R_{mikl}.

From the Gauss equation (
1
), it follows that the scalar curvature
R_{M}
of
M
is given by:

R_{M}=\sum_{i,j}R_{ijij}=-12-\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}.

Consequently,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
is constant if and only if the scalar curvature
R_{M}
is constant.

Assume
A
has principal curvature
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{4}
at a given point. At this point, combining the Gauss equation (
1
) with the Ricci identity (
3
) yields

(4)

\displaystyle h_{ijkl}-h_{ijlk}=\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)\left(-1+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\right)\left(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}\right).

For each positive integer
k
, we denote by

(5)

\displaystyle f_{k}={\rm tr}A^{k},\quad\sigma_{k}=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}.

The functions
f_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}
are globally well-defined smooth functions satisfying the following identities:

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&f_{1}=\sigma_{1}=4H=0,\\
&f_{2}=\sigma_{1}^{2}-2\sigma_{2}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2},\\
&f_{3}=\sigma_{1}^{3}-3\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}+3\sigma_{3}=3\sigma_{3},\\
&f_{4}=\sigma_{1}^{4}-4\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}+4\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}+2\sigma_{2}^{2}-4\sigma_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}-4\mathcal{K},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{K}=\sigma_{4}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}

is the Gauss-Kronecker curvature. Notably,

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\equiv 0\ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ f_{4}\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}.

The following identities, due to Peng-Terng
[
9
]
, hold:

(8)

\displaystyle\Delta f_{3}=

\displaystyle-3\left(4+\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)f_{3}+6\mathscr{C},

(9)

\displaystyle\Delta f_{4}=

\displaystyle-4\left(4+\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)f_{4}+4\left(2\mathscr{A}+\mathscr{B}\right),

where

(10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}^{2}h_{ijk}^{2},\quad\mathscr{B}=\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}h_{ijk}^{2},\quad\mathscr{C}=\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}h_{ijk}^{2}.

If
M
has constant scalar curvature, Simons
[
10
]
and Peng-Terng
[
9
]
established the following identities:

(11)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla A\right\rvert^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}h_{ijk}^{2}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+4\right).

(12)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla^{2}A\right\rvert^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,l}h_{ijkl}^{2}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+4\right)\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+11\right)+3\left(\mathscr{A}-2\mathscr{B}\right).

When
M
is noncompact, a smooth function may not attain its extremum (maximum or minimum). To address this issue, we invoke the following
Omori-Yau Maximum Principle
( see
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature bounded from below (or above).
If a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-function
f
is bounded from above (or below) on
M
, then there exists a sequence
\{p_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{M^{n}}
such that

(1)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}f(p_{k})=\underset{M}{\sup}\ f\quad(\text{or}\ \lim\limits_{k\to\infty}f(p_{k})=\underset{M}{\inf}\ f)
;

(2)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}|\nabla f(p_{k})|=0
;

(3)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\sup\ \nabla_{l}\nabla_{l}f(p_{k})\leq 0\quad(\text{or}\ \lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\inf\ \nabla_{l}\nabla_{l}f(p_{k})\geq 0)
, for
l=1,2,\cdots,n
.

We also require an elementary algebraic lemma, which can be proven using the method of Lagrange multipliers.

Lemma 3
.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
a\leq b\leq c
and
a+b+c=0
. Then,

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\right)^{3/2}\leq a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\right)^{3/2},

and the equalities hold as follows:

\displaystyle a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\right)^{3/2}\ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ b=c=-\dfrac{a}{2},

\displaystyle a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\right)^{3/2}\ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ a=b=-\dfrac{c}{2},

\displaystyle a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}=

\displaystyle 0\ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ b=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ a=-c.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

The main idea of the proof of the main theorem is as follows: assuming

M
is not totally geodesic, we consider the function
f_{3}
and derive a contradiction using Lemma
2
. To ensure the validity of this approach, it is essential to verify that all identities involving the principal curvatures,
h_{ijkl}
and
h_{ijkl}
remain applicable when passing to a subsequence of the sequence provided by Lemma
2
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
M
be a complete minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{5}
with constant scalar curvature, then there exists a sequence of
\{p_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{M}
such that

(1)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}f_{3}(p_{k})=\underset{M}{\sup}\ f_{3}\quad(\ \text{or }\ \lim\limits_{k\to\infty}f_{3}(p_{k})=\underset{M}{\inf}\ f_{3})
;

(2)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}|\nabla f_{3}(p_{k})|=0
;

(3)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\sup\ \nabla_{l}\nabla_{l}f_{3}(p_{k})\leq 0\quad(\ \text{or }\ \lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\inf\ \nabla_{l}\nabla_{l}f_{3}(p_{k})\geq 0)
, for
l=1,2,\cdots,n
.

Furthermore, there exists a subsequence
\left\{p_{k_{m}}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
of
\left\{p_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, such that the limits

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda_{i}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\lambda_{i}\left(p_{k_{m}}\right),\quad\hat{h_{ijk}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}h_{ijk}\left(p_{k_{m}}\right),\quad\hat{h_{ijkl}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}h_{ijkl}\left(p_{k_{m}}\right)

exist for all
i,j,k,l
. Moreover, the limits
\hat{\lambda_{i}},\hat{h_{ijk}},\hat{h_{ijkl}}
satisfy the same identities listed in Section 2 as
\lambda_{i},h_{ijk},h_{ijkl}
.

Proof.

Since
M
has constant scalar curvature,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
is a constant, and the sectional curvature of
M
is bounded by the Gauss equation (
1
). By the definition of
f_{3}
,
\left\lvert f_{3}\right\rvert\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
is also bounded. Consequently, Lemma
2
applies to
f_{3}
, and items (1), (2), (3) hold. Since
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}
,
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
are bounded continuous functions. For all
i,j,k
,
h_{ijk}
are bounded by (
11
), and
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B},\mathscr{C}
are bounded smooth functions by (
10
). It follows from (
12
) that
h_{ijkl}
are also bounded for all
i,j,k,l
.

For these bounded continuous functions, there exists a subsequence
\left\{p_{k_{m}}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
of
\subset\left\{p_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, such that these functions converge on this subsequence. By continuity, the limit functions satisfy all identities listed in Section 2.
∎

Moreover, if
M
has zero Gauss-Kronecker curvature, we establish the following Lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let
M
be a complete minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{5}
with constant scalar curvature and zero Gauss-Kronecker curvature. Then, the limits
\hat{\lambda_{i}},\hat{h_{ijk}},\hat{h_{ijkl}}
defined in Lemma
4
satisfy the following equations for each
1\leq k\leq 4
:

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\sum_{i}\hat{h_{iik}}=0,\\
&\sum_{i}\hat{\lambda_{i}}\hat{h_{iik}}=0,\\
&\sum_{i}\hat{\lambda_{i}}^{2}\hat{h_{iik}}=0,\\
&\sum_{i}\hat{\lambda_{i}}^{3}\hat{h_{iik}}=0,\end{cases},

and for each
k
and
l
,

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\sum_{i}\hat{h_{iikl}}=0,\\
&\sum_{i,j}\hat{h_{ijk}}\hat{h_{ijl}}+\sum_{i}\hat{\lambda_{i}}\hat{h_{iikl}}=0,\\
&\sum_{i}\left(\hat{\lambda_{i}}^{3}\hat{h_{iikl}}\right)+\sum_{i,j}\left(2\hat{\lambda_{i}}^{2}\hat{h_{ijk}}\hat{h_{ijl}}+\hat{\lambda_{i}}\hat{\lambda_{j}}\hat{h_{ijk}}\hat{h_{ijl}}\right)=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
H\equiv 0
,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
and
f_{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}
are constants, the first, second and fourth identities in (
13
) are derived by taking covariant derivative with
e_{k}
for these three terms, and then passing to the limit on the subsequence defined in Lemma
4
. By item (2) of Lemma
4
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\nabla_{k}f_{3}(p_{j})=0
. Taking the limit on the subsequence yields the third identity of (
13
).

Similarly, by taking the second covariant derivative with respect to
e_{k}
and
e_{l}
for
H,\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2},f_{4}
, and then passing to the limit on the subsequence, we obtain (
14
).
∎

In the subsequent discussion, when referring to the limits defined in Lemma
4
and Lemma
5
, we will omit the hat symbol “
\hat{\ }
” for simplicity.

We now proceed to present the proof of the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Suppose
M
is not totally geodesic. Then,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert
is a positive constant. Note that the Gauss-Kronecker curvature
\mathcal{K}\equiv 0
implies

f_{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}
is also a constant. Consequently, (
9
) simplifies to

(15)

\displaystyle 2\mathscr{A}+\mathscr{B}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(4+\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}.

The condition
\mathcal{K}\equiv 0
further implies
that at least one of the principal curvatures vanishes at each point. By Lemma
3
, we have

(16)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}\leq\inf f_{3}\leq\sup f_{3}\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}.

Based on this observation, we divide the proof into three cases:

Case
(1):
\sup f_{3}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
.

In this case, by Lemma
3
and by taking limits on the subsequence in Lemma
4
, the limits of the principal curvatures can be expressed as

(17)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=-\lambda<0=\lambda_{3}<\lambda_{4}=2\lambda>0.

Solving the linear equation (
13
), we obtain

(18)

\displaystyle h_{11k}+h_{22k}=0,\ \ h_{44k}=h_{33k}=0,\ \ \text{for each}\ k.

Consequently, the squared norm of the covariant derivative of
A
and the quantities
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are given by

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla A\right\rvert^{2}=

\displaystyle 4\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}\right)+6\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{114}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}+h_{134}^{2}+h_{234}^{2}\right).

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}^{2}h_{ijk}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\sum_{j,k}\left(h_{1jk}^{2}+h_{2jk}^{2}+4h_{4jk}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\left[4\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}+h_{113}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}\right)+10\left(h_{234}^{2}+h_{134}^{2}\right)+12\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right)\right].

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}h_{ijk}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\sum_{k}\left(h_{11k}^{2}+h_{22k}^{2}+2h_{12k}^{2}-4h_{14k}^{2}-4h_{24k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\left[2\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}\right)+4\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}-h_{134}^{2}-h_{234}^{2}\right)-6\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right)\right].

Combining these expressions with (
11
) and (
15
), we derive,

\displaystyle 3\left[4\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}\right)+6\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{114}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}+h_{134}^{2}+h_{234}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\left\lvert\nabla A\right\rvert^{2}=3\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+4\right)=\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(2\mathscr{A}+\mathscr{B}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 12\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}\right)+10\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}\right)+16\left(h_{234}^{2}+h_{134}^{2}\right)+18\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right),

which implies

\displaystyle 0=8\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}\right)+2\left(h_{134}^{2}+h_{234}^{2}\right).

Consequently, we have

(19)

\displaystyle h_{113}=h_{123}=h_{134}=h_{234}=0.

Substituting (
17
), (
18
) and (
19
) into (
14
) for
k=l=3
, the equations reduce to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&h_{1133}+h_{2233}+h_{3333}+h_{4433}=0,\\
&-\lambda h_{1133}-\lambda h_{2233}+2\lambda h_{4433}=0,\\
&-\lambda^{3}h_{1133}-\lambda^{3}h_{2233}+8\lambda^{3}h_{4433}=0,\end{cases}

which implies
h_{4433}=0
.
On the other hand, for
k=l=4
, (
14
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&h_{1144}+h_{2244}+h_{3344}+h_{4444}=0,\\
&-\lambda h_{1144}-\lambda h_{2244}+2\lambda h_{4444}=-2\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right),\\
&-\lambda^{3}h_{1144}-\lambda^{3}h_{2244}+8\lambda^{3}h_{4444}=-6\lambda^{2}\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right),\end{cases}

which implies
h_{3344}=0
. Consequently,

\displaystyle h_{3344}-h_{4433}=0.

However, from (
4
) and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle h_{3344}-h_{4433}=-2\lambda<0.

This leads to a contradiction.

Case
(2):
\sup f_{3}=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
.

In this case, by the inequality (
16
), we conclude that
f_{3}\equiv-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
is a constant. Consequently,
f_{1}=4H=0
,
f_{2}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
,
f_{3}
and
f_{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}
are all constants. This implies that the principal curvatures
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}
are constants, and thus
M
is isoparametric. However, any isoparametric minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{5}
must be totally geodesic (cf.
[
1
, p. 86]
), which contradicts our assumption that
M
is not totally geodesic.

Case
(3):
-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}<\sup f_{3}<\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
.

To derive a contradiction, we analyze this case by dividing it into three subcases:

(3-i)

:
-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}<\sup f_{3}<0
. In this subcase,
f_{3}<0
. Consequently, by (
6
), Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
, the limits of the principal curvatures can be set as

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}=0<\lambda_{3}<\lambda_{4}.

Solving the linear equation (
13
), we find

\displaystyle h_{iik}=0,\ \ \text{for each}\ i,k.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}h_{ijk}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{3}\sum_{i,j,k}\left(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{j}\right)h_{ijk}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{3}\left(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}\right)h_{134}^{2}=-\dfrac{1}{3}\lambda_{2}h_{134}^{2}=0.

By Lamma
4
item (3), (
8
) becomes

\displaystyle 0\geq\Delta f_{3}=-3\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+4\right)f_{3}+\mathscr{C}=-3\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+4\right)f_{3}>0.

This leads to a contradiction.

(3-ii)

:
0<\sup f_{3}<\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
. In this subcase, after taking the limit on the subsequence in Lemma
4
, we have
f_{3}>0
. Therefore, by (
6
), Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
, the limits of the principal curvatures can be expressed as

(20)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=-\lambda<\lambda_{2}=-\mu<\lambda_{3}=0<\lambda_{4}=\lambda+\mu.

Solving the linear equation (
13
), we also obtain

Simplicity of Cuntz–Pimsner algebras of quantum graphs
Mitch Hamidi, Lara Ismert, Brent Nelson

Abstract
Let 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} be a quantum graph without quantum sources and E 𝒢 E_{\mathcal{G}} be the quantum edge correspondence for 𝒢 . {\mathcal{G}}. Our main results include sufficient conditions for simplicity of the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra 𝒪 E 𝒢 {\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}} in terms of 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} and for defining a surjection from the quantum Cuntz–Krieger algebra 𝒪 ⁡ ( 𝒢 ) {\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}}) onto a particular relative Cuntz–Pimsner algebra for E 𝒢 E_{\mathcal{G}} . As an application of these two results, we give the first example of a quantum graph with distinct quantum Cuntz–Krieger and local quantum Cuntz–Krieger algebras. We also characterize simplicity of 𝒪 E 𝒢 {\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}} for some fundamental examples of quantum graphs, including quantum graphs on a single full matrix algebra, complete quantum graphs, and trivial quantum graphs. Along the way, we provide an equivalent condition for minimality of E 𝒢 E_{\mathcal{G}} and sufficient conditions for aperiodicity of E 𝒢 E_{\mathcal{G}} in terms of the underlying quantum graph 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} .



Introduction

A classical simple, finite graph
G
can be described as a pair
(V,A)
, where
V=\{1,\dots,d\}
is a finite set, called the vertices of
G
, and
A
is a
d\times d

\{0,1\}
-matrix called the
adjacency matrix
for
G
. Equivalently,
A:\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a linear map which is Schur idempotent. Introduced in
[
CK80
]
, the Cuntz–Krieger algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}
is the universal C*-algebra generated by a Cuntz-Krieger
G
-family, a collection of partial isometries
\{S_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}
with mutually orthogonal range projections which satisfy the
Cuntz–Krieger relation
:

\forall 1\leq i\leq d:\quad S_{i}^{*}S_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{i,j}S_{j}S_{j}^{*}.

Cuntz–Krieger algebras have been studied extensively and generalize Cuntz algebras, introduced in
[
Cun77
]
, and are useful in studying symbolic dynamics endowed by
A
on the infinite path space of
G
.

A
finite quantum graph

{\mathcal{G}}
is a triple
(B,\psi,A)
consisting of a
quantum set

(B,\psi)
, where
B
is a finite-dimensional C*-algebra and
\psi
is a state on
B
, and a
quantum adjacency matrix

A
, which is a linear map on
B
satisfying a condition called
quantum Schur idempotence
. Any classical simple, finite graph
G=(V,A)
can be realized as a finite quantum graph via Gelfand duality. We quantize
G
to a quantum set
(\mathbb{C}^{\left\lvert V\right\rvert},\psi)
, where
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{\left\lvert V\right\rvert}\to\mathbb{C}
is the state corresponding to the uniform counting measure on
V
, together with
A
viewed as an endomorphism on
\mathbb{C}^{\left\lvert V\right\rvert}
.

It turns out that classical Schur idempotence of the adjacency matrix
A
is equivalent to quantum Schur idempotence of
A
viewed as an endomorphism on the quantum set
(\mathbb{C}^{\left\lvert V\right\rvert},\psi)
, and thus,
(\mathbb{C}^{\left\lvert V\right\rvert},\psi,A)
is a finite quantum graph. Hence, a finite quantum graph
(B,\psi,A)
with noncommutative
B
is a noncommutative analogue of a classical finite graph.

The notion of a quantum adjacency matrix for a quantum set was first introduced by Musto-Reutter-Verdon
[
MRV19
]
and further generalized by Brannan et. al.
[
BCE+20
]
, and it endows a quantum set with a structure that is equivalent to the notion of a
quantum relation
on a quantum set, introduced by N. Weaver in
[
Wea12
]
. With a quantum analogue of a
\{0,1\}
-matrix in hand, Brannan et. al. introduced a C*-algebra arising from a quantum graph
{\mathcal{G}}=(B,\psi,A)
in
[
BEVW22
]
, called the
(free) quantum Cuntz–Krieger (QCK) algebra
for
{\mathcal{G}}
. In
[
BHI+23
]
, the present paper’s authors, along with M. Brannan and M. Wasilewski, added an extra relation to their definition to ensure unitality of the generated C*-algebra, and we call this C*-algebra the
quantum Cuntz–Krieger (QCK) algebra
for
{\mathcal{G}}
, denoted
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
.

Though we will recall a rigorous definition of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
in
Section

1
, roughly speaking, we build
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
from a QCK
{\mathcal{G}}
-family, which is a collection of matrix partial isometries
\{S^{(a)}:1\leq a\leq d\}
indexed by the matrix blocks of
B\cong\oplus_{a=1}^{d}M_{n(a)}
that satisfy an appropriate analogue of the Cuntz–Krieger relation. The QCK algebra
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
is then generated by the
coefficients
of the matrix blocks in the QCK
{\mathcal{G}}
-family. However, because only the matrix blocks, not their coefficients, are assumed to be partial isometries, the relations tell us very little about the coefficients themselves. Similarly, the quantum Cuntz–Krieger relations on the matrix blocks give rise to relations on the coefficient generators which are coarser than the classical Cuntz–Krieger relations. Consequently, the isomorphism class of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
is difficult to ascertain in general (though some special cases associated complete quantum graphs have been resolved, see
[
BEVW22
, Theorem 4.5]
).

For these reasons, a particular quotient of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
by an ideal generated by so-called “local” relations was introduced in
[
BHI+23
]
. We call this quotient the
local quantum Cuntz–Krieger (LQCK) algebra
for
{\mathcal{G}}
, denoted
L{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
. In most cases, these additional relations are robust enough to identify
L{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
with a certain Cuntz–Pimsner algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
, which we describe in more detail below. Moreover, in these cases it remained a possibility that the quantum Cuntz–Krieger algebra
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
was isomorphic to its local counterpart. In
Example

3.6
, we provide a concrete collection of examples where this fails.

The primary tool we develop to separate
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
from
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
in
Example

3.6
is developed in
Section

2
, wherein we consider a certain relative Cuntz–Pimsner algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(K,E_{\mathcal{G}})
which canonically surjects onto
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
, and we show in
Theorem

2.5
that
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
surjects onto
{\mathcal{O}}(K,E_{\mathcal{G}})
. In particular, when this specific ideal
K
is non-trivial, or equivalently, the C*-correspondence
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is not full (see
[
BHI+23
, Theorem 2.9]
), we have that
{\mathcal{O}}(K,E_{\mathcal{G}})
is non-simple. As a corollary, we obtain the following main result of this paper.

Corollary

2.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a quantum graph with no quantum sources and a nonempty set of quantum sinks. If
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
is simple, then the surjection of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
onto
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
is not injective.

The remainder of the paper focuses on characterizing simplicity of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
. In
Proposition

3.3
, we identify a condition on
{\mathcal{G}}
which provides a rich collection of
non-returning vectors
for
E_{\mathcal{G}}
. Such vectors are needed to check if a C*-correspondence satisfies Condition (S), a condition that was introduced in
[
ET23
]
and is part of a sufficient set of conditions on
E_{\mathcal{G}}
which guarantee simplicity of the associated Cuntz–Pimsner algebra. We then use
Proposition

3.3
to show simplicity of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
in
Example

3.6
.

The end of
Section

3
includes a brief discussion about the utility of checking Condition (S) for C*-correspondences arising from quantum graphs. Studying conditions for simpicity of Cuntz–Krieger algebras became of particular interest following Rørdam’s main result in
[
Rør95
]
: the
K_{0}
-group is a complete invariant for stable isomorphism of simple Cuntz–Krieger algebras. In
Section

4
, we leverage results from
[
MM06
]
to characterize simplicity of the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra for rank-one quantum graphs on a single full matrix algebra in
Example

4.4
, and we apply the main result from
[
Sch01
]
to prove the Cuntz–Pimsner algebras arising from complete quantum graphs are always simple, while the Cuntz–Pimsner algebras arising from trivial quantum graphs are always non-simple. Along the way, we provide an equivalent condition for minimality of
E_{\mathcal{G}}
strictly in terms of the underlying quantum graph
{\mathcal{G}}
in
Proposition

4.9
, and we give sufficient conditions for aperiodicity of
E_{\mathcal{G}}
in terms of the underlying graph
{\mathcal{G}}
in
Proposition

4.12
.

1
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall definitions and fundamental theorems pertaining to quantum graphs and their quantum edge correspondences, (local) quantum Cuntz–Krieger algebras, and (relative) Cuntz–Pimsner algebras.

1.1
Quantum graphs and quantum edge correspondences

Throughout, let
B
be a finite-dimensional C*-algebra viewed as the direct sum of full matrix algebras:

B\cong\bigoplus_{a=1}^{d}M_{n(a)}(\mathbb{C}).

Given a state
\psi:B\to\mathbb{C}
, define
m:L^{2}(B\otimes B,\psi\otimes\psi)\to L^{2}(B,\psi)
by
m(x\otimes y):=xy
, and let
m^{*}
denote the Hermitian adjoint of
m
. Given
\delta>0
, we say
\psi
is a
\delta
-form
if
mm^{*}=\delta^{2}\text{id}_{B}
. Equivalently,
\psi
is a
\delta
-form if the density matrix
\rho=\oplus_{a=1}^{d}\rho_{a}\in B
associated to
\psi
is invertible and satisfies
\text{Tr}(\rho_{a}^{-1})=\delta^{2}
for each
1\leq a\leq d
.

A linear map
A:B\to B
is
quantum Schur idempotent
if it satisfies

m(A\otimes A)m^{*}=\delta^{2}A.

In this paper, we will also require
A
to be completely positive (cp), and then we shall refer to
A
as a
quantum adjacency matrix
for
(B,\psi)
. A finite quantum graph is a triple
{\mathcal{G}}=(B,\psi,A)
.

Define
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}
to be
\delta^{-2}(\text{id}_{B}\otimes A)m^{*}(1_{B})
. In
[
BHI+23
]
, we prove a variety of facts about the properties of
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}
and its interplay with
A
. We define the quantum edge correspondence
E_{\mathcal{G}}
to be the
B
-
B
-subcorrespondence
\text{Span}\{x\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot y:x,y\in B\}
of
B\otimes_{\psi}B
whose left and right
B
-actions and
B
-valued inner product are inherited from the usual left and right
B
-actions and
B
-valued inner product on
B\otimes_{\psi}B
:

•

a\cdot(x\otimes y):=(ax)\otimes y

•

(x\otimes y)\cdot b:=x\otimes(yb)

•

\langle x_{1}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot y_{1}\,|\,x_{2}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot y_{2}\rangle=\delta^{-2}y_{1}^{*}A(x_{1}^{*}x_{2})y_{2}.

Theorem 1.1
(
[
BHI+23
, Theorem 2.9]
).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(B,\psi,A)
be a quantum graph such that
\psi
is a
\delta
-form and
A
is cp, and let
E_{\mathcal{G}}
be the quantum edge correspondence. Then

\{x\in B\colon x\cdot\xi=0\ \forall\xi\in E_{\mathcal{G}}\}=\left(BA^{\ast}(B)B\right)^{\perp}

and

\overline{\text{span}}\left\langle E_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,E_{\mathcal{G}}\right\rangle=B\cdot A(B)\cdot B,

the two-sided ideal of
B
generated by the range of
A
.
In particular,
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is faithful if and only if
\ker(A)
does not contain a central summand of
B
, and
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is full if and only if
A(B)
is not orthogonal to a central summand of
B
.

We call a central summand of
B
which is contained in

a
quantum source
, and we call a central summand of
B
which is orthogonal to the range of

quantum sink
.

(

Let
X
be a C*-correspondence over a C*-algebra
B
with right
B
-valued inner product
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle:X\times X\to B

*

*

\pi\colon B\to{\mathcal{D}}

satisfying:

(i)

\pi(b)t(\xi)=t(\varphi_{X}(b)\xi)
for
b\in B
and
\xi\in X
,

(ii)

t(\xi)^{*}t(\eta)=\pi(\left\langle\xi\,|\,\eta\right\rangle)
for
\xi,\eta\in X
.

Using (i), one can also show
t(\xi)\pi(b)=t(\xi\cdot b)
for

and
\xi\in X

, denoted

(\overline{\pi}_{X},\overline{t}_{X})

is a Toeplitz representation of

*

[
Kat04

In the present paper, we will always consider

as adjointable operators on

•

•

Proposition 1.7
.

Suppose

is finite-dimensional,

is a
B
-correspondence, and

.

Under these hypotheses, the Katsura ideal for

is all of
B
. Given a proper ideal

of

. We will show

and
\tau^{I}(Q)=0
.

Towards a contradiction, suppose
\overline{\pi}_{0}(q)
were in the kernel of
\overline{\tau}_{I}
. By
Proposition

1.6
, this means
\overline{\pi}_{0}(q)
is in the ideal generated by
\overline{\pi}_{0}(I)
. By

1.5
(and the discussion following it) and because

(\pi^{K}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}},t^{K}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}})
of
{\mathcal{O}}(K,E_{\mathcal{G}})
gives rise to a QCK
{\mathcal{G}}
-family. By the universal property of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
, there is a surjection from
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
onto
{\mathcal{O}}(K,E_{\mathcal{G}})
.

\blacksquare

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a quantum graph with no quantum sources and a nonempty set of quantum sinks. If
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
is simple, then
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
are non-isomorphic.

Proof.
.

By
Corollary

2.7
,
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
surjects onto
{\mathcal{O}}(K,E_{\mathcal{G}})
, which is non-simple by

. It follows that
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
is non-simple and hence cannot be isomorphic to

.

\blacksquare

3
Non-returning vectors and Condition (S)

Given a quantum graph
{\mathcal{G}}:=(B,\psi,A)
, set
K:=B\cdot A(B)\cdot B
. In order to construct an example of a quantum graph
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
is simple and distinct from the relative Cuntz–Pimsner algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(K,E_{\mathcal{G}})
, we identify a class of
non-returning vectors
in
E_{{\mathcal{G}}}
when
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is not full. Recall that, given a C*-correspondence
X
over a C*-algebra
B
, we denote the universal Toeplitz covariant representation of
X
into
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
by
(\pi,t)
. Introduced in
[
ET23
]
, a vector
\xi\in X^{\otimes m}
for some
b\in{\mathbb{N}}
is
non-returning
if whenever
0<n<m
and
\eta\in X^{\otimes n}
, then

t^{m}(\xi)^{*}t^{n}(\eta)t^{m}(\xi)=0.

The following lemma shows how we can use orthogonal central projections in the coefficient algebra of a C*-correspondence to construct non-returning vectors.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
p,q
are elements in a C*-algebra
B
such that
q^{*}p=0
and
q
is central. If
X
is a C*-correspondence over
B
and
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are vectors in
X
, then

(p\cdot\xi_{1}\cdot q)\otimes(\xi_{2}\cdot q)\otimes(\xi_{3}\cdot q)\otimes\cdots\otimes(\xi_{m}\cdot q)\in X^{\otimes m}

is a non-returning vector.

Proof.
.

Let
(\pi,t)
be the universal covariant representation of
X
into
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and fix
0<n<m
. Set
\xi=(p\cdot\xi_{1}\cdot q)\otimes(\xi_{2}\cdot q)\otimes\cdots\otimes(\xi_{n}\cdot q)\in X^{\otimes n}
and
\xi^{\prime}=(\xi_{n+1}\cdot q)\otimes\cdots\otimes(\xi_{m}\cdot q)\in X^{\otimes m-n}
. Since
p,q
are central projections in
B
and
\xi=\xi\cdot q
, for all
\eta\in X^{\otimes n}
we have

\displaystyle t^{n}(\xi)^{*}t^{n}(\eta)t(p\cdot\xi_{1}\cdot q)

\displaystyle=\pi(\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle)t(p\cdot\xi_{1}\cdot q)

\displaystyle=t(\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle p\cdot\xi_{1}\cdot q)

\displaystyle=t(q\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle p\cdot\xi_{1}\cdot q)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence, we have
t^{m}(\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime})^{*}t^{n}(\eta)t^{m}(\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime})=0,
and so
\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime}
is a non-returning vector.

\blacksquare

Proposition

3.2
is an immediate corollary of
Lemma

3.1
and yields a family of non-returning vectors for quantum edge correspondences.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
1_{a}
and
1_{b}
are orthogonal central projections in
B
, then

(1_{a}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot 1_{b})\otimes(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot 1_{b})\otimes(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot 1_{b})\otimes\cdots\otimes(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot 1_{b})\in E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes m}

is a non-returning vector.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
1_{c}
is a central projection in
B
orthogonal to the range of
A
,

(1_{c}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}})\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\in E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes m}

is a non-returning vector.

Proof.
.

Let
(\pi,t)
be the universal covariant representation of
E_{\mathcal{G}}
into
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
. Observe that for all
x\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot y\in E_{\mathcal{G}}
,

\pi(\left\langle\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,x\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot y\right\rangle)t(1_{c}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}})=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}t(A(x)y1_{c}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}})=0

since
1_{c}
is central and orthogonal to the range of
A
. It follows from a straightforward computation that
(1_{c}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}})\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}
is non-returning.

\blacksquare

It is worth noting that the two prototypes of non-returning vectors introduced in
Proposition

3.2
and
Proposition

3.3
could be zero without the hypothesis that the kernel of
A
contains no central summands of
B
, or equivalently,
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is faithful. We assume that
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is faithful in the following section when we invoke
Proposition

3.3
.

Note also that
Proposition

3.3
demonstrates when the ideal
B\cdot A(B)\cdot B
is non-trivial, or equivalently,
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is not full (
Theorem

1.1
), there exist even more non-returning vectors for
E_{\mathcal{G}}
. This is good news when appealing to Condition (S) to check simplicity of
E_{\mathcal{G}}
, because
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is not full, we cannot appeal to
Theorem

4.7
, a main result of
[
Sch01
]
. For us, this is the reason to use Condition (S) to characterize simplicity
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
for non-full
E_{\mathcal{G}}
. On the other hand, checking that
E_{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies Condition (S) when
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is full has proven to be more challenging due to the lack of identified non-returning vectors as in
Proposition

3.3
.

Definition 3.4
(
[
ET23
]
).

A C*-correspondence
X
over a C*-algebra
B
satisfies
Condition (S)
if whenever
x
is a positive element in
B
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
m>n
and a unit, non-returning vector
\xi\in X^{\otimes m}
such that

\left\lVert x\right\rVert<\left\lVert\left\langle\xi\,|\,x\cdot\xi\right\rangle\right\rVert+\varepsilon.

\blacklozenge

3.1
A quantum graph
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
is not isomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}

Let
(B,\psi)
be the finite quantum space with
B=\bigoplus_{a=1}^{3}M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\psi
the unique tracial
\delta
-form on
B
. Define
A:B\to B
by

A(x_{1}\oplus x_{2}\oplus x_{3})=(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})\oplus(x_{1}+x_{2})\oplus 0.

One can verify that
A
is a quantum adjacency matrix using the adapted matrix units in
B
and that
A
is cp since
A
is the sum and composition of completely positive maps. Thus,
{\mathcal{G}}=(B,\psi,A)
is a directed quantum graph such that
A
is cp.

Let
1_{a}
denote the central projection onto the
a^{\text{th}}
summand of
B
. It is clear that
{\mathcal{G}}
has no quantum sources since
\ker(A)
does not contain a central summand of
B
, but the third summand of
B
is a quantum sink in
{\mathcal{G}}
since
1_{3}
is orthogonal to the range of
A
. Hence,
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is faithful but not full by Theorem 2.9 in
[
BHI+23
]
. We show that
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
is not isomorphic to the quantum Cuntz–Krieger algebra
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{G}})
by proving
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
is simple and appealing to
Corollary

2.8
. We do so by proving
E_{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies Condition (S) and
B
has no non-trivial saturated, hereditary ideals as defined in
[
ET23
]
.
Given a C*-correspondence
X
over a C*-algebra
B
and
I
an ideal in
B
, define

IX:=\overline{\text{span}}\{r\cdot\xi:r\in I\text{ and }\xi\in X\}\quad\text{ and }\quad XI:=\overline{\text{span}}\{\xi\cdot r:r\in I\text{ and }\xi\in X\}.

The ideal
I
is
hereditary
if
IX\subseteq XI
and is
saturated
if whenever
b\in J_{X}
and
b\cdot X\subseteq XI
, then
b\in I
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(B,\psi,A)
be the directed quantum graph with
B=\bigoplus_{a=1}^{3}M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
\psi
the unique tracial
\delta
-form on
B
, and
A:B\to B
defined by

A(x_{1}\oplus x_{2}\oplus x_{3})=(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})\oplus(x_{1}+x_{2})\oplus 0.

The quantum edge correspondence
E_{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies Condition (S).

Proof.
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
x\in B
be given. Let
1_{a}
and
1_{b}
be orthogonal central projections in
B
and set

\xi:=(1_{a}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot 1_{b})\otimes(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot 1_{b})\otimes(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot 1_{b})\otimes\cdots\otimes(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot 1_{b})\in E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes(k+1)}.

Define
F:B\to B
by
F(y)=A(y)1_{b}
and observe

\left\langle 1_{a}\cdot\varepsilon\cdot 1_{b}\,|\,x\cdot(1_{a}\cdot\varepsilon\cdot 1_{b})\right\rangle=\delta^{-2}A(x1_{a})1_{b}=\delta^{-2}F(x1_{a}).

Repeating a similar computation iteratively, one can show that

\left\langle\xi\,|\,x\cdot\xi\right\rangle=\delta^{-2(k+1)}F^{k+1}(x1_{a}).

(3.6)

From this, we have the following cases:

\left\langle\xi\,|\,x\cdot\xi\right\rangle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2(k+1)}}(0\oplus x1_{1}\oplus 0),&\text{if $a=1,b=2$}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2(k+1)}}(x1_{2}\oplus 0\oplus 0),&\text{if $a=2,b=1$}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2(k+1)}}(x1_{3}\oplus 0\oplus 0),&\text{if $a=3,b=1$}\end{cases}

Since
\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\left\langle\xi\,|\,\xi\right\rangle\right\rVert=\delta^{-2(k+1)}
using
x=1_{B}
in
3.6
, renormalizing
\xi
yields

\left\lVert\left\langle\xi\,|\,x\cdot\xi\right\rangle\right\rVert=\begin{cases}\left\lVert x1_{1}\right\rVert,&\text{if $a=1,b=2$}\\
\left\lVert x1_{2}\right\rVert,&\text{if $a=2,b=1$}\\
\left\lVert x1_{3}\right\rVert,&\text{if $a=3,b=1$}\end{cases}

Since the norm of
x
is achieved on one of its three blocks,
\xi
can be chosen so that
\left\lVert\left\langle\xi\,|\,x\cdot\xi\right\rangle\right\rVert=\left\lVert x\right\rVert
. Therefore,
E_{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies Condition (S).

\blacksquare

.

4
Simplicity of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(B,\psi,A)
be a finite quantum graph such that
A:B\to B
is cp and
\psi
is a
\delta
-form. Let
E_{\mathcal{G}}
denote the quantum edge correspondence for
{\mathcal{G}}
cyclically generated by
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}
.

4.1
Simplicity of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
when

is a single full matrix algebra

In
Section

4.1
, we use a characterization of simplicity of Cuntz–Pimsner algebras arising from a more general class of C*-correspondences than our quantum edge correspondences, but with the restriction that they are C*-correspondences over a single full matrix algebra, as in
[
MM06
]
: Given a completely positive map
\Phi:M_{n}\to M_{n}
, consider
M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
with the usual left and right actions by
M_{n}
. Define an
M_{n}
-valued inner product on elementary tensors in
M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
by
\langle x\otimes y,z\otimes w\rangle:=y^{*}\Phi(x^{*}z)w
. Extending this inner product linearly makes
M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
into a
M_{n}
-correspondence denoted by
M_{n}\otimes_{\Phi}M_{n}
.

Proposition 4.1
(
[
BHI+23
, Proposition 2.6]
).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(M_{n},\psi,A)
be a finite quantum graph with
\delta
-form
\psi
and cp map
A
. The C*-correspondences
E_{\mathcal{G}}
and
M_{n}\otimes_{A}M_{n}
are isomorphic as
M_{n}
-correspondences via the map
x\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot y\mapsto\frac{1}{\delta}(x\otimes y)
.

Given a completely positive linear map
\Phi:M_{n}\to M_{n}
, there exists a Kraus decomposition
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}\subset M_{n}
such that
\Phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}K_{i}^{*}xK_{i}
for all
x\in M_{n}
. Define
d(\Phi)
to be the dimension of the complex linear span of
\{K_{i}:1\leq i\leq p\}
.

While the Kraus decomposition for a cp map
\Phi
is not unique, the dimension
d(\Phi)
is known to be independent of choice of Kraus representation. The main theorem of
[
MM06
]
is the following.

Theorem 4.2
(
[
MM06
]
).

Let
\Phi:M_{n}\to M_{n}
be completely positive. The Cuntz–Pimsner algebra for
M_{n}\otimes_{\Phi}M_{n}
is Morita equivalent to the Cuntz algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{d(\Phi)}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

The Cuntz–Pimsner algebra of
M_{n}\otimes_{\Phi}M_{n}
is simple if and only if
d(\Phi)>1.

Proof.
.

Because the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra of
M_{n}\otimes_{\Phi}M_{n}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{d(\Phi)}
are unital,
Theorem

4.2
tells us that the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra of
M_{n}\otimes_{\Phi}M_{n}
is simple if and only if
{\mathcal{O}}_{d(\Phi)}
is simple, which holds if and only if
d(\Phi)>1.

\blacksquare

Example 4.4
(Rank-one quantum graphs).

Fix a finite quantum set
(B,\psi)

is a

-form corresponding to a density matrix

Example 4.6
.

Consider the complete quantum graph

whose quantum adjacency matrix

Let

4.2
Simplicity of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{\mathcal{G}}}
in terms of minimality and periodicity of
E_{\mathcal{G}}

In this subsection, we determine simplicity of the Cuntz–Pimsner algebras arising from complete quantum graphs and trivial quantum graphs using Schweizer’s characterization in
[
Sch01
]
. First, let us recall
[
Sch01
, Definition 3.7]
. A C*-correspondence
X
over a unital C*-algebra
B
is
minimal
if there are no nontrivial ideals
J\triangleleft B
such that
\langle X\,|\,JX\rangle\subseteq J
, where

\langle X\,|\,JX\rangle=\text{Span}\{\langle x\,|\,b\cdot y\rangle:x,y\in X,b\in J\},

and
X
is
aperiodic
if whenever
X^{\otimes n}
is isomorphic to
B
as correspondences,
n
must be
0.

Theorem 4.7
(
[
Sch01
, Theorem 3.9]
).

If
X
is a full C*-correspondence over a unital C*-algebra
B
, then the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is simple if and only if
X
is minimal and aperiodic.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}:=(B,\psi,A)
be a finite quantum graph such that
\psi
is a
\delta
-form and
A
is cp. For any ideal
J\trianglelefteq B
, we have
\langle E_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,JE_{\mathcal{G}}\rangle=B\cdot A(J)\cdot B.

Proof.
.

Let
b\in J
and let
x,y,z,w\in B
, and observe

\left\langle x\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot y\,|\,b\cdot(w\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot z)\right\rangle=y^{*}\left\langle x\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,bw\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\right\rangle z=\delta^{-2}y^{*}A(x^{*}bw)z\in B\cdot A(J)\cdot B.

Taking spans of elements of this form, we conclude
\langle E_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,JE_{\mathcal{G}}\rangle\subseteq B\cdot A(J)\cdot B.
Conversely, given an element
yA(b)z
for some
y,z\in B
and
b\in J
, we have

yA(b)z=\delta^{2}y\langle\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,b\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\rangle z=\delta^{2}\langle\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot y^{*}\,|\,b\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot z\rangle\in\langle E_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,JE_{\mathcal{G}}\rangle.

Therefore,
B\cdot A(J)\cdot B\subseteq\langle E_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,JE_{\mathcal{G}}\rangle.

\blacksquare

We then have the following characterization of minimality for
E_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{G}}=(B,\psi,A)
is a quantum graph such that
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is full. Then
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is minimal if and only if there is no non-trivial ideal
J\triangleleft B
such that
A(J)\subseteq J.

Example 4.10
(Complete quantum graphs).

The quantum edge correspondence
E_{K}
for a complete quantum graph
K(B,\psi)
is minimal: if
J\triangleleft B
is a nonzero ideal and
A(J)\subseteq J,
then
1\in J
because
\delta^{-2}A(1)=\psi(1)1.
Hence,
J=B.
To see that
E_{K}
is aperiodic, recall
E_{K}
is cyclically generated as
B-B
-bimodule inside

by
\varepsilon_{K}=1\otimes 1
, so
E_{K}\cong B\otimes_{\psi}B
as
B
-correspondences. Thus, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

E_{K}^{\otimes n}\cong(B\otimes_{\psi}B)\otimes_{B}\dots\otimes_{B}(B\otimes_{\psi}B)\cong B^{\otimes n+1}.

By dimension counting,
E_{K}^{\otimes n}\cong B
implies
n=0
as long as
B\not\cong\mathbb{C}
. Recall
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is full if and only if the ideal
K=B\cdot A(B)\cdot B
is all of
B
(
Theorem

1.1
). As
E_{K}
is full,
Theorem

4.7
implies
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{K}}
is simple.

\blacklozenge

Example 4.11
(Trivial quantum graphs).

Let
(B,\psi)
be a quantum set and let
A:B\to B
be the identity map. The trivial quantum graph
T(B,\psi):=(B,\psi,A)
is clearly non-minimal as
A(J)=J
for all ideals
J
of
B
. Furthermore,
E_{T}
is cyclically generated as a
B-B
-bimodule inside
B\otimes_{\psi}B
by
\varepsilon_{T}=m^{*}(1)
, and thus is isomorphic to
B
as
B
-correspondences, so
E_{T}
is periodic. Regardless of periodicity,
E_{T}
being full and non-minimal implies
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{T}}
is non-simple.

\blacklozenge

Finally, we prove a statement whose contrapositive provides sufficient conditions on
{\mathcal{G}}
which imply aperiodicity of
E_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}:=(B,\psi,A)
be a quantum graph such that
A
is cp and
\psi
a
\delta
-form. If
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is periodic, then
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is faithful and full (
{\mathcal{G}}
has no quantum sources and no quantum sinks).

Proof.
.

Suppose
E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n}\cong B
for some
n\geq 1
. Since
B
is a faithful and full as a correspondence over itself,
E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n}
is faithful and full. In particular, since the ideal
\langle E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n}\,|\,E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n}\rangle
is a subset of
\langle E_{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,E_{\mathcal{G}}\rangle
, it follows that
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is full by
Theorem

1.1
.

Also by
Theorem

1.1
,
E_{\mathcal{G}}
is faithful if and only if
\ker(A)
contains no central summand of
B
. Let
p
be a projection onto a central summand of
B
which is contained in
\ker(A)
, so
p
itself is central. We will show
p
must be
0
. Let
T\colon E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n}\to B
be the
B
-bimodular map implementing the isomorphism
E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n}\cong B
, and let
\xi\in E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n}
satisfy
T(\xi)=1
. Note
x=\langle\xi\,|\,x\cdot\xi\rangle
for all
x\in B
. Write
\xi
as
(\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}})\otimes\xi_{0}
for some
a_{j}\in B
and
\xi_{0}\in E_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n-1}
. Then

p=\left\langle\xi\,|\,p\cdot\xi\right\rangle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\left\langle a_{j}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\xi_{0}\,|\,pa_{k}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\xi_{0}\right\rangle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\delta^{-2}\left\langle\xi_{0}\,\lvert\,A(a_{j}^{*}pa_{k})\cdot\xi_{0}\right\rangle.

Since
a_{j}^{*}pa_{k}
is contained in
\ker(A)
for each
1\leq j,k\leq m
, we have
p=0
.

\blacksquare

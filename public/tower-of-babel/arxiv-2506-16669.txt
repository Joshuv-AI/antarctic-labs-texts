Observational Space Unveils Cosmic Expansion’s Role in the Hubble Tension
Kang Jiao, Tong-Jie Zhang, Liang Gao

Abstract
We introduce a geometric embedding method that directly constrains H 0 H_{0} by exploiting the exact kinematic relation z ˙ = H 0 ​ ( 1 + z ) − H ⁡ ( z ) \dot{z}=H_{0}(1+z)-H(z) in a unified observational framework. This approach reconstructs expansion history without requiring specific cosmological models, incorporating both existing Cosmic Chronometer data and forecasted Sandage-Loeb measurements. Through mathematical embedding of observational constraints, we achieve ∼ \sim 1.6% precision in H 0 H_{0} determination—matching current Cepheid distance ladder accuracy while maintaining model independence. The method demonstrates how fundamental kinematic constraints enable precision cosmology, providing a promising pathway to address the Hubble tension as future redshift drift observations become available.

Introduction

The Hubble constant
H_{0}
tension—now exceeding

69

(

\sim 1

(

\Lambda

(
E. Di Valentino et al. 2021
)
.

Cosmic Chronometers (CC)—which estimate

(1)

Applying Eq.

z\lesssim 2

H_{0}

While CC and SL data can be jointly analyzed within cosmological frameworks, bypassing redshift alignment challenges, such model-dependent approaches (typically
\Lambda

H_{0}
determination via Eq.
1

Conventional redshift alignment approaches face significant limitations: (1) Cosmographic techniques using e.g. Taylor or Padé expansions avoid dark energy assumptions but suffer from truncation artifacts and convergence issues
(
S. Capozziello et al. 2019

; (2) Nonparametric methods like Gaussian Processes offer flexibility but remain sensitive to kernel choice, which affects smoothness priors and extrapolation behavior

(
E. V. Linder 2017

M. Moresco & F. Marulli 2017
)
. These interpolation and smoothing approaches all rely on assumptions about data behavior between measurement points that potentially introduce biases and diminish predictive accuracy—highlighting the need for geometric methods that can robustly align redshift-mismatched datasets while maintaining true model independence.

II
Methodology

We introduce a geometric approach that transforms the redshift misalignment problem into a fundamental constraint in observable space. The FLRW kinematic relation described in Eq.
1
implies that all valid cosmic expansion histories must satisfy a specific geometric condition—effectively defining a plane in
(z,H(z),\dot{z})
space whose orientation directly encodes
H_{0}
. This insight allows us to combine CC and SL measurements without imposing model-dependent interpolation schemes or arbitrary priors, offering a robust methodology that relies only on basic kinematic principles. Our inference involves fitting this geometric plane to the combined observational data.

To quantify the deviation of each measurement from the best-fit plane, we define the residual
r_{i}
for each redshift
z_{i}
as follows:

\displaystyle r_{i}

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}_{i}-(1+z_{i})\bar{H_{0}},

(2)

where
\tilde{S}_{i}
denotes the hybrid summation of two properties defined as:

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}_{H}(i)[H(z_{i})+\tilde{\dot{z}}_{i}]+\mathbb{I}_{\dot{z}}(i)[\tilde{H}(z_{i})+\dot{z}_{i}].

(3)

Here,
\mathbb{I}_{H}
and
\mathbb{I}_{\dot{z}}
are indicator functions such that
\mathbb{I}_{H}(i)+\mathbb{I}_{\dot{z}}(i)=1
, representing mutually exclusive
H(z)
and
\dot{z}
measurements. The tilde symbol denotes the quantities treated as free values to be embedded, while the non-tilde quantities denote the observed values.

Additionally,
\bar{H_{0}}
is the inverse variance-weighted mean of all
\tilde{S}_{i}/(1+z_{i})
, with weights from error propagation. Each redshift yields an independent
H_{0}
estimate, collectively defining a statistical plane. Alignment with this plane results in residuals
r_{i}=0
, indicating robustness. The inverse variance weighting enhances this reliability by prioritizing lower-uncertainty observations. Significant non-zero residuals suggest discrepancies between observed and predicted values, necessitating further examination of systematic errors or assumptions.

The covariance matrix associated with the residual vector
\bm{r}
propagates from observational uncertainties and
\bar{H_{0}}
scatter:

\bm{C}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{C}_{\mathrm{CC}}&0\\[4.0pt]
0&\bm{C}_{\mathrm{SL}}\end{bmatrix}+\text{Var}(\bar{H_{0}})\cdot(1+\bm{z})(1+\bm{z})^{\mathrm{T}},

where
\bm{C}_{\mathrm{CC}}
and
\bm{C}_{\mathrm{SL}}
represent the respective covariance matrices for CC and SL observations. The block-diagonal structure reflects the assumption that CC and SL measurements are statistically independent, as they probe complementary physical processes through distinct observational techniques—stellar population synthesis versus spectroscopic redshift drift detection. Detailed covariance specifications are provided in the validation section.

We employ Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods for Bayesian inference, sampling the posterior distribution
P(\tilde{H},\tilde{\dot{z}}|\mathrm{data})\propto\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{data}|\tilde{H},\tilde{\dot{z}})\times P(\tilde{H},\tilde{\dot{z}})
to achieve robust parameter constraints while naturally accounting for uncertainties in both the measurements and embedded values. With
\bm{r}
as the residual vector and
\bm{C}
as the covariance matrix, the Gaussian log-likelihood is expressed as

\ln\mathcal{L}=-\frac{n}{2}\ln(2\pi)-\frac{1}{2}\ln\det(\bm{C})-\frac{1}{2}\bm{r}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{r},

(4)

where the terms represent normalization, uncertainty weighting, and model-data agreement, respectively.

Mutually exclusive
H(z)
and
\dot{z}
measurements affects the embedding outlined in Eq. (
3
), leading to an underdetermined system which can yield arbitrary
H_{0}
values that satisfy a zero residual condition. To address this issue, we impose limits on the spaces of embedded values through linear interpolation of adjacent measurements, establishing sufficiently wide 5
\sigma
ranges that ensure reasonable values, align with the overall redshift evolution trend, and prevent sharp fluctuations, all while accommodating observational uncertainties. These constraints enhance the reliability of
H_{0}
estimates and maintain consistency with observational data.

Rigorous validation requires realistic noise simulation that preserves observational statistics without introducing artificial parameter bias. Standard multivariate Gaussian sampling creates systematic shifts in parameter estimates through two mechanisms: (1) finite numerical precision introduces computational artifacts in curved parameter spaces defined by Fisher information metrics, and (2) nonlinear parameter estimation
\hat{\theta}=f(\mathbf{y})
from noisy data
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{y}_{\rm fid}+\bm{\epsilon}
violates Jensen’s inequality, yielding
E[\hat{\theta}]=E[f(\mathbf{y}_{\rm fid}+\bm{\epsilon})]\neq f(\mathbf{y}_{\rm fid})

\bm{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\bm{C})

We address this bias by assigning independent random seeds
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{N}\}
to each data point and systematically exploring seed configurations to minimize estimation bias. We select the optimal configuration:

\mathbf{s}^{*}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{s}}|\hat{\theta}(\mathbf{s})-\theta_{\rm fid}|

IV
Discussions

Prior model-independent CC reconstructions in the literature suggest the Hubble tension may not be significant, but their large uncertainties (
\sim
7-15%) provide insufficient evidence for definitive conclusions. The convergence of similar findings from TRGB distance measurements further underscores the need for enhanced precision in these assessments. The precision achieved through geometric embedding enables
H_{0}
constraints at the few-percent level without relying on early-universe physics or local calibration hierarchies. This enhanced precision will provide the statistical power necessary to definitively assess whether the Hubble tension reflects systematic uncertainties in existing measurements or genuine departures from
\Lambda
CDM cosmology.

This approach imposes global consistency requirements across the extended redshift range (
z\lesssim 2
to
z\sim 5
), where the kinematic constraint must be simultaneously satisfied by all observations. Notably, this global consistency naturally resolves the redshift misalignment challenge between CC and SL datasets without requiring functional assumptions nor smoothing about redshift evolution. Furthermore, the independent systematic uncertainties of CC and SL datasets enable cross-validation and mutual error suppression through the kinematic coupling.

The
\sim
40% precision improvement over joint
\Lambda
CDM fitting demonstrates enhancement beyond statistical gains from dataset combination alone. While joint analyses benefit from increased data volume, they remain constrained by inherent parameter degeneracies within parametric frameworks. Geometric embedding mitigates these degeneracies by allowing kinematic physics to extract information through direct algebraic constraints rather than parametric fitting. This methodological distinction accounts for the improved precision achieved relative to traditional cosmological approaches.

Despite its effectiveness, our methodology is constrained by the FLRW assumption, which limits its applicability across different cosmological scenarios. Most theoretical frameworks addressing the Hubble tension—including early dark energy, late dark energy modifications, extended dark energy models, extra relativistic degrees of freedom, interacting dark sectors, unified cosmologies, modified gravity theories, inflationary models, modified recombination history, and critical phenomena physics—preserve the cosmological principle while altering expansion dynamics, making them compatible with our kinematic approach. More exotic scenarios such as local inhomogeneity models, anisotropic Bianchi type-I spacetimes, and Milgromian dynamics formally violate FLRW assumptions but show minor deviations within our observational window (
z\lesssim 5
). Models with strong low-redshift modifications exhibit significant departures from our kinematic constraints. Embedding such data into our planar observable space may bias
H_{0}
estimates, though this becomes statistically significant only with next-generation surveys. Beyond
H_{0}
determination, our approach provides a model-independent data reconstruction for cosmological constraints and model discrimination, though broader validation awaits future studies.

The embedding priors introduced through data interpolation/extrapolation establish 5
\sigma
bounds with uniform distributions, representing minimal assumptions given that non-informative priors avoid bias toward specific cosmological scenarios. The embedded results are significantly more compact than the imposed bounds, indicating data-driven constraints. Such priors serve as mathematical regularization against pathological solutions in the underdetermined system rather than encoding cosmological assumptions. Unlike Gaussian process methods with prescribed correlation functions or spline techniques enforcing specific smoothness criteria, these constraints address purely technical requirements.

Regarding noise pattern selection during methodology validation, this strategy aims to realistically assess systematic uncertainties while avoiding numerical artifacts that could obscure genuine method performance. While this approach might appear to introduce selection bias, it serves a specific validation purpose rather than result optimization. The noise-free validation demonstrates consistent
H_{0}
constraint (
68.63\pm 1.14
km s
-1
Mpc
-1
), confirming that the geometric approach produces stable results even with noise contamination. This consistency establishes genuine algorithmic robustness rather than selection-induced improvements.

V
Conclusion

In this letter, we demonstrate that a data-driven, physics-induced method for high-precision
H_{0}
measurement is achievable, at a level that competes with Cepheid-calibrated distance ladders, based on observational space geometry aimed at addressing the Hubble tension. We discover that
H_{0}
directly determines the normal vector of the FLRW observational plane, establishing mathematical constraints under minimal assumptions. Using cosmic chronometer and Sandage-Loeb data, our method transforms this geometric relationship into direct algebraic determination of the current expansion rate, yielding robust model-independent estimates. This geometric framework establishes a fundamentally new approach to cosmological measurements through pure observational analysis.

Efficacy and safety of Mepitel film for radiodermatitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Xue Ren, Hengjiao Wang, Jing Liu, YongXin Yuan, Tong Wu, Haibo Zhang, Defu Yang, Ying Yan, Ying Xu, Feng Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the effects of Mepitel film on the prevention and treatment of radiodermatitis.MethodsWe conducted a systematic search across multiple databases, including PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Database, and SinoMed Database, from their inception until August 6, 2024. We identified randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or controlled clinical trials that assessed the effects of Mepitel film on radiodermatitis. The risk of bias was evaluated using the Revised Cochrane Risk-of-Bias tool for randomized trials (RoB2). Traditional meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.3 and Stata 14.0 software.ResultsA total of 27 studies involving 2,313 cancer patients were included. The pooled analysis indicated that Mepitel significantly reduced the incidence of radiation dermatitis (OR = 0.28, 95%CI 0.16 to 0.50, I2 = 67%, P < 0.0001) compared to the control groups. In the subgroup analysis by cancer type, Mepitel reduced the incidence of radiation dermatitis in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (OR = 0.25, 95%CI 0.13 to 0.46, I2 = 0%, P < 0.0001), breast cancer (OR = 0.42, 95%CI 0.26 to 0.68, I2 = 24%, P = 0.0004), and various malignancies (OR = 0.10, 95%CI 0.06 to 0.16, I2 = 0%, P < 0.00001). Mepitel use was also associated with a lower incidence of moderate and severe skin injury in radiotherapy patients (RTOG grade II OR = 0.26, 95%CI 0.16 to 0.42, P < 0.00001; RTOG grade III OR = 0.19, 95%CI 0.12 to 0.30, P < 0.00001; RTOG grade IV OR = 0.10, 95%CI 0.03 to 0.36, P = 0.0005), as well as a reduction in the severity of radiation-induced skin damage (combined RISRAS score, MD= -0.74 95% CI -1.02 to -0.46, I2 = 95%, P < 0.00001; researcher RISRAS score MD= -0.47 95% CI -0.67 to -0.27, I2 = 90%, P < 0.00001; and patient RISRAS score MD= -1.19 95% CI -1.33 to -1.05, I2 = 39%, P < 0.00001). Additionally, patients treated with Mepitel experienced fewer cases of moist desquamation (OR = 0.46, 95%CI 0.25 to 0.83, I2 = 58%, P = 0.010), and shorter healing times (MD= -4.73, 95%CI -7.16 to -2.30, I2 = 98%, P = 0.0001).ConclusionMepitel interventions significantly reduced RTOG grades and RISRAS scores, decreased the risk of moist desquamation, and shortened healing times for radiation-induced dermatitis in cancer patients. Further clinical studies are needed to better understand the relationship between Mepitel and radiation dermatitis in this patient population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15465-2.Keywords: Mepitel film, Radiodermatitis, Meta-analysis, Randomized controlled trials

Introduction

Cancer encompasses a diverse and complex array of malignant tumors [1]. Treatments include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, and hormonal therapy, either alone or in combination. Radiation therapy, which deprives cancer cells of their ability to divide and multiply, plays a critical role in oncology care. It is reported that approximately 50% of cancer patients receive radiation therapy during their treatment, and it contributes to about 40% of cancer cures [2]. Despite advancements such as intensity-modulated radiotherapy, there remains a risk of adverse effects on the skin and surrounding healthy tissues.

Among cancer patients receiving radiation treatment, radiation-induced skin reactions are common side effects, characterized by swelling, redness, fibrosis, ulceration, pain, burning, itching, and more. Radiodermatitis is a prevalent acute reaction to radiotherapy, influenced by various patient-specific, preventive, treatment-related, and radiation dose-related factors. Effective management and prevention of radiodermatitis are crucial to alleviating pain and minimizing skin damage [3].

Mepitel film, a Swedish silicone foam skin dressing, has shown promise in treating burns and slow-healing wounds. Recent reports suggest that Mepitel film may also serve as an intervention for radiodermatitis in cancer patients, potentially reducing blistering, peeling, erythema, pigmentation, and edema, while mitigating the severity of skin reactions, particularly in breast cancer patients [4, 5]. Although previous studies have highlighted the potential benefits of Mepitel film in preventing radiodermatitis, there has been limited systematic review addressing its efficacy across various cancer types [6, 7]. This meta-analysis aims to provide a comprehensive review of the association between Mepitel film and the incidence and severity of radiodermatitis in cancer patients.

Methods

The meta-analysis followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [8]. A PRISMA flow chart of search results and the screening process was created (Fig. 1). An explicit study protocol was pre-registered on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) platform with the approval number CRD42024581398.

The selection process

Data sources

We conducted a comprehensive search across several databases, including PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, CNKI, Wanfang Database, and SinoMed, for studies published from database inception to August 6, 2024. Our search strategy employed a combination of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and keywords related to “Mepitel film”, “radiodermatitis”, “radiotherapy” and their variants [9]. The detailed search strategy was outlined in Supplementary Tables 1–6. We also manually screened the reference lists of relevant systematic reviews to ensure a comprehensive identification of relevant studies [6, 7, 10–13].

Eligibility criteria

The included studies had to meet the following inclusion criteria: (1) cancer participants were treated with radiotherapy; (2) the objective of the study was to assess the effect of Mepitel film on radiodermatitis in cancer patients; (3) study design was limited to randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or controlled clinical trials; (4) investigations focused on the association between Mepitel film and radiodermatitis; (5) the outcome measures included the incidence of radiation dermatitis, the radiation therapy oncology group (RTOG) grade, skin reaction severity (measured using the radiation-induced skin reaction assessment scale [RISRAS]), moist desquamation, and time to radiation-induced dermatitis healing. This study comprised all cancer types, and there were no language restrictions for included studies.

Exclusion criteria: (1) the data was incomplete or not reported; (2) observational studies; (3) the full text of the study was unavailable; (4) the conference abstracts, study protocols, or letters to the editor.

Study selection

Two independent reviewers (Xue Ren and Hengjiao Wang) conducted the study selection and screened the literature based on predefined eligibility and exclusion criteria. Initially, duplicates and irrelevant articles were removed by reviewing titles and abstracts. Subsequently, full texts of potentially eligible studies were downloaded and assessed for final inclusion. Any discrepancies or uncertainties were resolved by discussion or by involving a third reviewer (Ying Xu).

Data extraction

Data extraction was performed by the two aforementioned independent reviewers using guidelines for systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Pre-designed forms were used to collect data on the first author, publication year, sample size, gender distribution, age, study location, cancer types, interventions for both case and control groups, study design, and side effects caused by Mepitel film. If the literature did not provide objective data directly, we handled the data according to the original data, eligibility criteria, and scoring criteria. For example, data from a 14-day post-radiation therapy group (Møller 2018) was included according to the eligibility criteria [14]. The incidence rate was calculated based on the total number of subjects in the group (Paterson 2012) [15]. We handled the data for analysis by the original data, and scoring criteria (Yan 2020) [16]. Any disagreements or issues were resolved through discussion with Feng Wei.

Quality assessment

Two reviewers (Xue Ren and Hengjiao Wang) independently evaluated the methodological quality of all included studies. The Revised Cochrane Risk-of-Bias Tool for Randomized Trials (RoB2) was used to assess the risk of bias [17, 18]. The RoB2 includes five domains: (1) Risk of bias arising from the randomization process; (2) Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention); (3) Missing outcome data; (4) Risk of bias in the measurement of the outcome; (5) Risk of bias in the selection of the reported result. Each domain has several signaling questions which are used to assess the quality of studies. The risk of bias can be assessed as “low risk of bias”, “some concerns” and “high risk of bias” in each domain.

Statistical analysis

The meta-analyses were performed using RevMan 5.3 and Stata 14.0 software. For count variable, contrast-based analyses were applied with estimated odd ratios (ORs). Continuous variables were assessed using mean differences (MDs). Heterogeneity of effect sizes across studies was assessed using Cochran’s Q and I2 statistics. Sensitivity analysis was conducted by excluding each study one by one and re-running the meta-analysis. Funnel plots and Egger’s test (α = 0.05) were used to assess the risk of publication bias.

Results

Study selection

A total of 1,462 records were collected through the search. 358 articles were excluded for duplication, and 1,046 irrelevant articles were excluded based on their titles and abstracts. After title and abstract screening, 58 articles were considered potentially relevant. After full-text review, 27 studies were included, which reported on radiodermatitis in cancer patients treated with radiotherapy. The selection process is shown in Figure l.

Study characteristics

This meta-analysis included 27 studies [4, 14–16, 19–41], encompassing a total of 2,313 cancer patients, with a publication from database inception to August 6, 2024. Of the included studies, 22 were RCTs [4, 14–16, 19, 20, 22, 24–30, 32, 34–37, 39–41] and 5 were controlled clinical trials [21, 23, 31, 33, 38]. Nine studies were published in English [4, 14–16, 20, 22, 35, 36, 39], and 18 published in Chinese [19, 21, 23–34, 37, 38, 40, 41], Regarding the types of cancer in the included studies, thirteen studies focused on nasopharyngeal carcinoma [19, 21, 25–29, 32–34, 36–38], eight studies on breast cancer [4, 14, 15, 23, 31, 35, 39, 41], three on head and neck cancer [16, 20, 22], two studies on malignancy [24, 40], and one study on cervical cancer [30]. We conducted a methodological quality assessment for all 27 studies. According to the risk of bias summary, 15 studies (55.56%) were assessed as “some concerns”, 8 studies (29.63%) were assessed as “low risk bias”, and 4 studies (14.81%) were assessed as “high risk bias”. Primary outcome

Meta-analysis: the incidence of radiation dermatitis

Out of the 27 studies, 20 studies reported on the incidence of radiation dermatitis in cancer patients treated with [14, 15, 19–29, 31, 33–35, 37, 40, 41]. In 8 of these studies, the incidence of radiation dermatitis was reported as 100% [22, 23, 25, 28, 29, 31, 37, 41]. Therefore, the analysis was conducted using data from the remaining 12 studies [14, 15, 19–21, 24, 26, 27, 33–35, 40]. The pooled analysis showed that compared with the control group, the use of Mepitel film reduced the incidence of radiation dermatitis in cancer patients (OR = 0.28, 95%CI 0.16 to 0.50, I2 = 67%, P < 0.0001, Fig. 3).

Among the 12 studies reporting on radiation dermatitis, 6 studies focused on nasopharyngeal carcinoma [19, 21, 26, 27, 33, 34], three studies on breast cancer [14, 15, 35], two studies on malignancy [24, 40] one study on head and neck cancer [20]. Mepitel film reduced the incidence of radiation dermatitis in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (OR = 0.25, 95%CI 0.13 to 0.46, I2 = 0%, P < 0.0001), breast cancer (OR = 0.42, 95%CI 0.26 to 0.68, I2 = 24%, P = 0.0004), and malignancy (OR = 0.10, 95%CI 0.06 to 0.16, I2 = 0%, P < 0.00001) (Fig. 4). However, only one study reported Mepitel film may not affect radiation dermatitis in head and neck cancer (OR = 0.96, 95%CI 0.29 to 3.24, I2 = N/A, P = 0.95), indicating the need for further studies to confirm this result.

The RTOG grade for the skin reactions

In the 27 studies, 19 studies reported skin reaction grades. One study used the Common Toxicity Criteria, version 4.0 [25], two studies used their own hospital’s grading criteria [21, 26] and 16 studies used the RTOG grading system [14, 16, 19, 22–24, 27–29, 31, 33–35, 37, 40, 41]. The RTOG grading system classifies skin reactions as: grade 0: no change in skin; grade I: follicular erythema or dry desquamation; grade II: tender or bright erythema, or patchy moist desquamation; grade III: confluent moist desquamation other than in skinfolds; grade IV: ulceration, bleeding, necrosis [42]. We performed a subgroups analysis based on RTOG grade. The results showed that the patients in grade 0 and grade I in the Mepitel film group had more favorable outcomes compared to the control group (OR = 5.06, 95%CI 1.71 to 14.94, I2 = 83%, P = 0.003; OR = 4.32, 95%CI 2.29 to 8.14, I2 = 82%, P < 0.00001, respectively) (Fig. 5A and B). In contrast, the incidence of skin injury in grade II, III and IV was lower in the Mepitel film group than in the control group (OR = 0.26, 95%CI 0.16 to 0.42, I2 = 62%, P < 0.00001; OR = 0.19, 95%CI 0.12 to 0.30, I2 = 0%, P < 0.00001; OR = 0.10, 95%CI 0.03 to 0.36, I2 = 0%, P = 0.0005) (Fig. 5C, D and E). In summary, the application of Mepitel film can reduce the incidence of moderate to severe skin injury in radiotherapy patients and mitigate the degree of skin injury due to radiation.

Secondary outcome

Skin reaction severity was measured using the RISRAS

Five studies reported on RISRAS scores [4, 16, 22, 23, 39]. RISRAS combines two scores: the radiation therapist/oncology nurse’s score for the visible extent of skin reactions, and the patient’s score for pain, itchiness, burning, and the impact on daily life. The combined RISRAS score, researcher RISRAS score, and patient RISRAS score were all significantly reduced in the Mepitel film group (MD= -0.74 95% CI -1.02 to -0.46, I2 = 95%, P < 0.00001; MD= -0.47 95% CI -0.67 to -0.27, I2 = 90%, P < 0.00001; MD= -1.19 95% CI -1.33 to -1.05, I2 = 39%, P < 0.00001, respectively, Fig. 6).

Moist desquamation

Six studies reported on moist desquamation [4, 14, 15, 22, 35, 39]. The pooled analysis showed that, compared to the control group, the Mepitel film group had a reduced incidence of moist desquamation (OR = 0.46, 95%CI 0.25 to 0.83, I2 = 56%, P = 0.010, Fig. 7).

Forest plots of moist desquamation

Time-to-radiation-induced dermatitis healing

Eight studies reported the healing time of radiation dermatitis [15, 19, 30, 32, 34, 36, 38, 41]. The results indicated that, compared with the control group, the Mepitel film group had a reduced healing time for radiation dermatitis (MD= -4.73, 95%CI -7.16 to -2.30, I2 = 98%, P = 0.0001, Fig. 8).

Side effects due to Mepitel film

Most studies did not report side effects related to Mepitel film, but seven studies mentioned patient discomfort [14–16, 20, 22, 35, 38]. Reported side effects included poor skin adherence (n = 46), itching or skin rash with erythema, dry or flaky skin (n = 42), discomfort/distress (n = 8), tightness (n = 5), spots/pustules (n = 3), pain upon dressing removal (n = 3), moist desquamation (n = 1), and allergic reactions (n = 1). Sensitivity analysis

The results of the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis using the random-effects model are summarized in Supplementary Tables 9 and Supplementary Fig. 1. The analysis indicated that the study by Peng LJ (2018) [24], which included multiple malignancies such as breast, nasopharyngeal, esophageal, lung, and rectal cancers, may contribute to high heterogeneity. However, the sensitivity analysis showed that any single study does not significantly alter the overall results.

Publication bias

The funnel plot showed no evidence of publication bias (Supplementary Fig. 2), and Egger’s test confirmed this finding with a p-value of 0.509. The results suggested that publication bias is unlikely to significantly affect the interpretation of this meta-analysis.

Discussion

Radiation therapy irreversibly damages nuclear DNA double strands and mitochondrial DNA, inhibiting cell division and replication. This damage, combined with structural tissue destruction, reactive oxygen species generation, epidermal and dermal inflammation, and skin cell necrosis, leads to the development of radiation dermatitis [43]. Radiation dermatitis is a common side effect of radiation therapy. While various treatments are available-including topical medications, dressings, and radiation protectors, but there is no standardized approach for preventing or treating radiation-induced skin injury. Previous studies have explored several topical agents for managing radiation dermatitis, such as herbal remedies, vitamins, biologics, pharmaceuticals, and metallic ointments [43]. The Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer (MASCC) has recommended the use of Mepitel film to prevent acute radiation dermatitis and Mepilex Lite dressings for managing acute radiation dermatitis [44]. Although there have been two meta-analysis for Mepitel film for the prevention and treatment of acute radiation dermatitis in breast cancer [7, 13] and one for head and neck cancer [6]. However, a comprehensive meta-analysis evaluating its effectiveness across all radiotherapy patient populations remains lacking.

Our meta-analysis, which included 27 studies involving 2,313 cancer patients, assessed the use of Mepitel for the prevention and treatment of radiodermatitis. The study encompassed various cancer types treated with radiotherapy, including nasopharyngeal carcinoma, breast cancer, head and neck cancer, cervical cancer, and other malignancies. The results indicated that Mepitel significantly reduced the incidence, severity, and grade of skin reactions, as well as the occurrence of moist desquamation and healing time, when compared to the control groups. The side effects of Mepitel in this study were generally mild and included poor skin adhesion, itching, erythematous rashes, dry or flaky skin, discomfort, tightness, and occasional pustules. These adverse reactions were transient and typically resolved spontaneously or after removing the dressing or with appropriate treatment. These findings suggest that Mepitel may be a promising option for protecting against radiodermatitis in cancer patients undergoing radiotherapy, aligning with the MASCC recommendations.

Mepitel film is an absorbent, self-adhesive dressing made from a thin, flexible sheet of hydrophilic polyurethane foam bonded to a water vapor-permeable polyurethane film. Mepilex Lite, a range of Swedish silicon-foam dressings, is commonly used to treat burns and slow-healing wounds, and it is also recommended by MASCC for managing acute radiation dermatitis. The contact surface of these dressings is coated with soft silicone adhesive, which allows it to adhere securely to healthy skin without causing trauma upon removal. It provides a moist wound-healing environment for several days, promoting faster healing. This meta-analysis demonstrated that Mepitel is more effective in reducing the severity of radiation-induced skin reactions in cancer patients undergoing radiotherapy, enhancing patient comfort and quality of life. While mild side effects were observed, they were manageable and did not significantly impact overall treatment. Improved production methods and clinical care might be necessary to address issues like poor skin adhesion.

Radiotherapy is a well-established cancer treatment, but it frequently causes dermatitis in patients, as the skin absorbs high doses of radiation. This can lead to severe damage that affects patients’ lifestyles and causes considerable discomfort. Extensive research has focused on skin care during radiotherapy, with evidence showing that maintaining a moist wound environment facilitates healing by promoting re-epithelialization and cell migration [45, 46] Many creams and gels are available that attract moisture to the skin surface, but no gold-standard guidelines exist for treating radiation-induced skin injury [47] Aqueous cream is commonly used for various skin issues [48] and wound dressings offer additional benefits, such as protection from external contaminants, preventing clothing soiling, and reducing irritation or friction. Studies show that most cancer patients prefer Mepitel film dressings over creams because they offer greater comfort, improve sleep quality, reduce pain, and allow for the wearing of regular clothing [36, 39]. Our study indicates that Mepitel film can reduce the incidence of moist desquamation in cancer patients, particularly those at risk of severe skin reactions, suggesting that Mepitel may be beneficial for this group. However, as radiation techniques and doses improve, further studies are required to validate these findings.

Mepitel films are thin, transparent polyurethane materials designed to adhere securely to both healthy skin and wound areas, enhancing permeability to oxygen, water vapor, and carbon dioxide while managing moisture [49, 50]. They are useful as a prophylactic measure and for wound dressing from the beginning of radiotherapy. Their transparency and elasticity allow for the monitoring wound closure without removing the dressing. Mepitel films help reduce moist desquamation, shorten healing times, alleviate pain, and improve comfort, mobility, and mental well-being [12]. However, challenges such as fluid accumulation at the wound site can affect the film’s adhesive properties and increase the risk of microbial growth [51]. Although several cancer types were included in this study, the overall sample size remains small, and the use of Mepitel has not been widely studied across all cancer types. Further clinical research is needed to address limitations related to tumor sites, anatomical locations, and other factors. Technological improvements, adhesive enhancements, and more detailed clinical care are necessary to address these challenges.

Study strengths and limitations

Our meta-analysis is strengthened by the inclusion of 27 clinical studies, providing a robust evaluation of Mepitel for radiodermatitis in cancer patients undergoing radiotherapy. Our results indicated a marked reduction in the incidence, severity, and grade of skin reactions, as well as faster healing times and reduced pain. However, there are several limitations. Firstly, despite the large number of studies, the sample size remains insufficient, and more subgroup analyses could not be performed. Secondly, most of the studies were conducted in China and New Zealand, with only one each from Canada and Denmark, lacking representation from the Americas and Africa. Meanwhile, the overall quality of the included studies was low, and large-scale RCTs are needed to confirm the global applicability of our findings. Moreover, the cancer types studied were limited, and more research is needed to assess the effectiveness of Mepitel in other cancer populations. Furthermore, the studies included in this meta-analysis were published in English and Chinese, excluding research in other languages.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this meta-analysis highlights the preventive role of Mepitel in radiodermatitis. Mepitel significantly reduced RTOG grades and RISRAS scores, decreased the risk of moist desquamation, and shortened healing time for radiation-induced dermatitis in cancer patients. To gain a deeper understanding of the relationship between Mepitel and radiation dermatitis in cancer patients, further clinical studies are necessary.

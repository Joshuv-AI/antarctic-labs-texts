Poster 53. The Effect Of Medial Closing Wedge Distal Femoral Varus Osteotomy On Contact Stress Distribution Pattern Of the Patellofemoral Joint: An Evaluation Using Computed Tomography Osteoabsorptiometry
Authors: Eiji Kondo, Masanari Hamasaki, Koji Iwasaki, Yuki Suzuki, Masatake Matsuoka, Tomohiro Onodera, Kazunori Yasuda, Norimasa Iwasaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Distal femoral varus osteotomy (DFVO) is a well-described treatment option for patients with valgus malalignment associated with a broad range of knee disorders. Previous studies reported that medial closing wedge (MCW)-DFVO may be a useful concomitant procedure in young patients with osteoarthritis in lateral compartment and/or patellofemoral (PF) instability. MCW-DFVO reduces the Q angle and may improve patellar tracking, thereby decreasing PF pain and instability. However, the impact of DFVO on stress distribution within the PF joint has not been explored through in vivo studies. The distribution of the subchondral bone mineral density is thought to be correlated with the distribution of stress over the joint surface. Computed tomography (CT) osteoabsorptiometry is an analytical method to assess long-term stress distribution over individual joints in living participants by measuring subchondral bone density. Our previous studies using this method have evaluated stress distribution at each joint under various loading conditions.The purpose of this study was to (1) explore (1) the distribution pattern of subchondral bone density across the PF joint in non-arthritic knees without arthritis and in those of patients with valgus knees, (2) assess changes in the pattern of bone density distribution in patients with valgus knees before and after medial closing wedge (MCW)-DFVO, and (3) determine the correlation between PF alignment and changes in bone density distribution.Methods:The authors retrospectively analyzed clinical and radiographic data from 20 patients (20 knees; mean, 49 years, 5men, 15 women) treated with MCW-DFVO for lateral compartment osteoarthritis (OA) due to valgus malalignment, alongside a control group of 20 patients (20 knees; mean, 23 years, 4 men, 16 women) without OA. In surgical procedure, we performed a biplanar osteotomy of the distal femur. Then, the oblique osteotomy site was gradually closed. A locking plate was fixed to the medial side of the femur. Clinical and radiological evaluations were performed in all cases before the surgery and at the final follow-up (range; 13 to 64 months). All patients underwent radiologic examination preoperatively and at 1 year postoperatively, and the distribution patterns of subchondral bone density through the articular surface of trochlea and patella were assessed preoperatively and >1 year postoperatively using CT osteoabsorptiometry. The quantitative analysis of the obtained mapping data focused on location of the high-density area (HDA) through the articular surface of the PF joint. Hounsfield units (HU) of the identified subchondral bone region in each slice were measured. The quantitative analysis of the obtained mapping data focused on location of the HDA through the articular surface. HDA was defined as the area where the HU values are in the top 30% of HU out of the total area of the femoral trochlea, and patellar compartments. (Fig.1A) The %HDA in each region was defined as HDA of each divided region by HDA of entire articular surface. In the PF joint, the four divided regions of the femoral trochlea included the medial trochlea (MT), the medial notch (MN), the lateral notch (LN), and the lateral trochlea (LT) (Fig. 1B). The divided regions of the patella included the medial facet (MF), the central ridge (CR), the medial portion of the lateral facet (MLF), and the lateral portion of the lateral facet (LLF) (Fig. 1C). The lateral ratio of trochlea and patella were calculated as each two lateral regions' proportion of HDA to the total HDA of four regions. The comparison between control individuals and patients with valgus knees, both pre- and post-surgery, utilized the Student's t-test, while the evaluation of patients with valgus knees pre- and post-operation employed a paired t-test. The significance level was set at p=0.05.Results:The mean postoperative functional knee score and Lysholm score significantly improved at the final follow-up (p<0.001 for each). Concerning radiological evaluation, the mean hip-knee-ankle angle (HKA) significantly changed from 6.4° to -1.7° (p<0.001). The mean mechanical axis significantly changed from 74.3% to 42.3% (p<0.001). Regarding the PF joint, there were no significant differences in the Caton-Deschamps ratio between pre- and post-operative periods. The mean radiological Q-angle (rQ-angle) was significantly decreased from 12.3° to 8.2° (p=0.007). The mean tilting angle was significantly decreased from 9.6° to 7.2° after surgery (p=0.013). The mean lateral shift ratio (LSR) was significantly decreased from 18.4% to 13.2% (p<0.001). In CT osteoabsroptiometry evaluation, the lateral ratio of trochlea and patella were significantly greater in the preoperative OA group (68.7% and 68.2%) compared to the control group (43.7% and 55.2%) (p<0.001 and p=0.029). Post-MCW-DFVO, the lateral ratio of trochlea and patella in the OA group notably declined to 50.3% and 56.2% (p=0.004 and p= 0.041). (Table 1) The lateral ratio of trochlea exhibited a significant correlation with the rQ-angle in both the control (r =0.521; p=0.026) and OA (r=0.529; p=0.031) groups. (Fig 2)Conclusions:This study demonstrated that postoperative clinical scores significantly improved after MCW-DFVO. The valgus malalignment significantly corrected to mild varus alignment. In addition, MCW-DFVO led to an altered PF joint congruity. In CT osteoabsorptiometry evaluation, the mean lateral ratio of trochlea and patella were significantly greater in the preoperative OA group compared to the control group. MCW-DFVO resulted in a redistribution of HDA from the lateral to the medial PF articular surface. The extent of PF alignment correction post-MCW-DFVO was closely linked to the shifts in HDA distribution, reflecting changes in stress distribution.

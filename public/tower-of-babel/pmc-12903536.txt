Objective

Hexavalent chromium [Cr(VI)] is a pervasive and highly toxic pollutant frequently discharged in industrial effluents, especially from leather tanning, electroplating, and textile sectors. Its high solubility and oxidative potential render it both environmentally mobile and biologically hazardous, contributing to carcinogenicity in living organisms [1]. Microbial bioremediation offers a sustainable and eco-compatible alternative, leveraging the innate metabolic capabilities of microorganisms to detoxify heavy metals. This study focuses on B. altitudinis AK25, a bacterium isolated from a Cr(VI)-contaminated aquatic site in Gautam Buddha Nagar, India. Strain AK25 exhibited high tolerance to Cr(VI), thriving at concentrations up to 1,500 ppm (Data file 1). This study aimed to characterize the complete genome of Bacillus altitudinis AK25 to identify genes involved in heavy metal resistance and stress adaptation, highlighting its potential role in bioremediation (Data file 2). By its genome analyses, we aimed to identify functional genes and regulatory networks that facilitate survival in hostile environments, including those contaminated with heavy metals [2]. The complete genome sequence of strain AK25 provides a detailed genetic blueprint of a highly Cr (VI)-resistant bacterium with multifaceted stress response systems. Its genomic features suggest strong potential for application in bioremediation of heavy metal-contaminated environments. Future studies involving transcriptomic profiling and functional assays will be essential to validate these predictions and explore their utility in field-scale remediation efforts [3].

Data description

B. altitudinis AK25 was isolated from a Cr(VI)-contaminated water sample collected from Gautam Buddha Nagar, India (28.604258’N 77.424384’E) in January 2023. The sample was processed within 24 h, and enrichment was performed using nutrient broth media supplemented with 10 ppm potassium dichromate at 30 °C overnight [4, 5]. MIC and MBC of strain AK25 were determined using two-fold Cr(VI) dilutions, revealing dose-dependent inhibition with a MIC of 1,500 ppm and an MBC of 3,000 ppm.

Genomic DNA was extracted using QIAGEN Genomic DNA kit and quantified with Qubit 4 fluorometer [6, 7]. Approximately 100 ng of DNA was processed using the NEBNext Ultra II FS DNA Library Prep Kit, which involves enzymatic fragmentation (200–300 bp), end repair, 3′ adenylation, loop adapter ligation, USER enzyme treatment, and AMPureXP bead purification [8, 9]. Libraries were enriched with NEBNext Ultra II Q5 Master Mix using Illumina universal and sample-specific primers (six PCR cycles) and purified with AMPure XP beads. Reads were quality filtered using FastQC (v0.12.1), aggregated with MultiQC (v1.25), and trimmed with TrimGalore (v0.6.4) and assembled de novo with Unicycler (v0.4.8), followed by scaffolding with MeDuSa (v1.6) [10–12]. MOB-suite (v3.1.4) revealed that there were no plasmids in strain AK25 [13]. Genome annotation was performed using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP) [14]. Illumina sequencing of strain AK25 produced 5,766,447 paired-end spots (150 bp each), yielding ~ 1.74 Gb of raw data and 476.33× coverage. The draft genome assembly totalled 3,691,282 bp with a GC content of 41.33% across four contigs, the largest measuring 3,683,384 bp (Scaffold_1), with an N50 of 3,683,384 bp. Annotation was performed using NCBI PGAP (v6.5), identifying 3,808 genes, including 3,732 coding sequences and 76 RNA genes. Genome completeness was assessed using BUSCO v5.4.3, revealing 96.2% completeness and 3.8% missing based on the Bacillales dataset, while CheckM analysis (v1.2.3) shows 99.12% completeness and no contamination (Data file 3) [15]. A circular genome map was generated to visualize from the CGView server (https://www.cgview.ca), and Prokka (v.1.14.6) was used to assemble the overall structure of the genome (Data file 4). The genome of strain AK25 (Data set 2) reveals a diverse array of proteins that support survival in metal-contaminated environments (Data file 5). Functional annotation revealed genes associated with metal and metalloid resistance (chromium, arsenic, mercury, nickel, zinc and magnessium), including chromate transporters (chrA), arsenic transporters (arsB), oxidative stress enzymes (catalase, superoxide dismutase), and biofilm formation proteins (Veg, TasA, EpsG, RemA, AhpA, and BslA), and motility-related proteins (MCPs, CheC, CheD, CheW and FliM) [5, 16–22].

The 16S rRNA gene sequence of AK25 was compared with related Bacillus species using EzBioCloud (v.2025.04.21) (https://www.ezbiocloud.net/resources/16s_download) [23]. Phylogenetic analysis performed with MEGA 12.0 (Neighbour-Joining method, 1000 bootstrap replicates) confirmed its taxonomic placement as Bacillus altitudinis (Data file 6) [24]. To confirm species identity, ANI, dDDH values, and phylogenomic tree have been plotted using the TYGS platform against related Bacillus strains (Data file 7, Data file 8). The highest dDDH value (85.3% by TYGS) and ANI scores (98.14% by Prokka v.1.14.6) supported the assignment of AK25 as B. altitudinis. This is the first report of whole genome sequence analyses of B. altitudinis AK25, a highly Cr(VI)-resistant strain, revealing unique genetic determinants for heavy metal tolerance, stress adaptation, and bioremediation potential (Table 1).

Limitations

This genome analysis provides a comprehensive overview of the genetic basis for chromium resistance in strain AK25. However, functional predictions are based solely on in silico annotation. Experimental validation, including gene expression profiling and Cr(VI) reduction assays, is necessary to confirm detoxification capabilities. The absence of plasmids and reliance on short-read sequencing may limit structural resolution, which could be addressed in future studies using long-read platforms.

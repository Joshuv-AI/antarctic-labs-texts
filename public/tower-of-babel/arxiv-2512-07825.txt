Novel thermodynamic inequality for rotating AdS black holes
Hamid R. Bakhtiarizadeh

Abstract




We propose a new thermodynamic inequality for stationary and asymptotically Anti-de Sitter rotating black holes,
4\pi J^{2}/(3MV)<1
. This inequality is derived through an analysis of the roots of the identity which holds among the thermodynamic variables to avoid a naked singularity. We have analyzed the Kerr-AdS black hole and the asymptotically AdS uncharged and rotating black strings and find strong supporting evidence for different horizon topologies. Using this inequality we verify the validity of reverse isoperimetric inequality in its conventional form (
{\cal R}\geq 1
) in the presence of rotation. By examining a wide range of black hole solutions, we confirm the validity of the proposed inequality. Assuming that reverse isoperimetric inequality continues to hold in higher dimensions, and employing the intermediate refined reverse isoperimetric inequalities introduced in [
Phys. Rev. Lett.
131
, 241401
] as guiding principles, we extend the inequality to higher-dimensional spacetimes.

Introduction
. The thermodynamic properties of black holes have played an outstanding role in advancing our understanding of the interplay between gravitation, quantum theory, and thermodynamics because of the seminal works of Bekenstein and Hawking
[
1
,
2
]
. Bekenstein first proposed that the entropy of a black hole is proportional to the area of its event horizon
[
1
]
, a notion that was profoundly supported by Hawking’s discovery of black hole radiation
[
2
]
. In Anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetimes, these ideas acquire additional depth due to the AdS/CFT correspondence
[
3
]
, which facilitates the study of gravitational systems through dual conformal field theories, thereby offering new perspectives on entropy bounds.

Recent developments have significantly expanded the classical picture of black hole thermodynamics by incorporating notions such as thermodynamic volume
[
4
,
5
]
, critical behavior in extended phase space
[
6
]
, and the intriguing concept of black holes functioning as heat engines
[
7
]
. This broader framework, often referred to as “black hole chemistry”
[
8
]
, has motivated investigations into entropy inequalities and phase structures within the AdS/CFT correspondence. Additionally, recent work on holographic complexity
[
9
]
, the role of chemical potentials in holographic thermodynamics
[
10
]
, and the higher-dimensional origins of black hole thermodynamics
[
11
,
12
]
has further highlighted the necessity for rigorous mathematical treatments of entropy bounds in AdS black hole spacetimes. Initial efforts to explore these ideas in rotating black holes and black rings demonstrated the existence of nontrivial phase behavior and thermodynamic volumes in complex horizon geometries
[
13
]
. Further studies extended these ideas to de Sitter backgrounds
[
14
]
, accelerating spacetimes
[
15
]
, and scenarios involving holography and information geometry. While some literatures include possible violations of the RII, a conclusive counterexample to the conjecture is still lacking.

A central concept arising from this framework is the reverse isoperimetric inequality, which conjectures that for a given thermodynamic volume, there exists a lower bound on the black hole entropy
[
14
,
16
]
. While this inequality holds for many conventional black hole solutions, a number of important exceptions have been identified, particularly in the context of ultraspinning and super-entropic black holes
[
17
,
20
]
. These solutions violate the inequality, suggesting that entropy may exceed the expected geometric bounds, thus challenging our intuition about gravitational thermodynamics and entropy-area relations. Such violations have motivated inquiries into the underlying conditions that lead to such behavior, as well as into the stability and physical realizability of these exotic solutions
[
17
,
23
]
.

Simultaneously, efforts have been made to develop a microscopic and holographic interpretation of thermodynamic volume and its relation to the degrees of freedom of the dual theory
[
18
]
. These developments raise important questions about the fundamental nature of volume, entropy, and temperature in gravitational theories, particularly in lower-dimensional settings
[
25
]
and in theories with higher-curvature corrections or Chern-Simons terms
[
24
]
. Moreover, investigations into the geometry of black hole horizons with unusual topologies
[
19
]
, and the structure of gauged supergravity solutions
[
22
]
, further illustrate the diversity and complexity of black hole chemistry. Refined versions of the quantum Penrose and reverse isoperimetric inequalities, valid for all known three-dimensional asymptotically AdS quantum black holes are also proposed in
[
26
]
. This paper aims to prpose thermodynamic inequality for uncharged, stationary and asymptotically Anti-de Sitter rotating black holes.

Evidence for the inequality
. Consider first the Kerr-AdS black hole
[
27
,
30
,
31
]
, for which the thermodynamics in extended phase space was first studied in
[
5
]
. For this case, the following identity holds among the thermodynamic parameters
[
32
]
(see Eq. (
37
) of Supplement)

36\pi M^{2}V^{2}-M^{2}A^{3}-64\pi^{3}J^{4}=0,

(1)

which can be solved explicitly for the horizon area as

A^{3}=36\pi V^{2}\left[1-\left(\frac{4\pi J^{2}}{3MV}\right)^{2}\right].

(2)

This relation admits a single physically meaningful real root for
A>0
provided that

\frac{4\pi J^{2}}{3MV}<1.

(3)

A similar algebraic structure appears in the asymptotically AdS rotating black string solutions obtained by Lemos
[
33
,
34
]
, whose thermodynamic variables obey
[
36
]
(see Eq. (
47
) of Supplement)

3\mathcal{A}^{6}+2048\pi^{2}\mathcal{J}^{2}\mathcal{V}^{2}-1536\pi\mathcal{V}^{3}\mathcal{M}=0,

(4)

leading to

{\cal A}^{6}=\frac{512\pi{\cal V}^{2}}{3}\left(3{\cal M}{\cal V}-4\pi{\cal J}^{2}\right).

(5)

Here again a single positive real solution for
{\cal A}
exists only if the inequality (
3
) is satisfied, demonstrating that the same type of bound governs the existence of the horizon. The similarity of the algebraic constraints highlights a universal feature independently of horizon topology.

Reverse isoperimetric inequality.
In the Letter
[
32
]
, the authors propose novel thermodynamic inequalities that apply to charged, stationary and asymptotically AdS rotating black holes, and by checking their validity for thin black rings in AdS, show that their findings may also be applicable to black holes with non-spherical horizon topology. In four dimensions the strong refined reverse isoperimetric inequalities (RRII) can be rewritten in terms of the isoperimetric ratio
{\cal R}

[
5
]
as

{\cal R}\geq\left[1-\left(\frac{4\pi J^{2}}{3MV}\right)^{2}\right]^{-1/6}.

(6)

In the limit
J\to 0
, the Kerr-AdS area reduces to the Schwarzschild-AdS area and the RII (
{\cal R}\geq 1
) is recovered. The inequality is saturated for the Kerr-AdS black hole. It can be seen from inequality (
3
) that for the right-hand side of the above inequality we have

{\cal R}_{\rm Kerr-AdS}=\left[1-\left(\frac{4\pi J^{2}}{3MV}\right)^{2}\right]^{-1/6}\geq 1,

(7)

which verifies the validity of RII in its conventional form in the presence of rotation.

Further validations.
For KN-AdS black hole, consider the identity (
36
). Bring everything to one side and choose the sign so that the cubic term is positive:

f(A)\equiv M^{2}A^{3}+16\pi^{2}Q^{2}J^{2}A-36\pi M^{2}V^{2}+64\pi^{3}J^{4}=0,

(8)

which is a cubic equation in
A
. The derivative is

f^{\prime}(A)=3M^{2}A^{2}+16\pi^{2}Q^{2}J^{2}.

(9)

For physically meaningful parameters (
M\neq 0
, and
Q,J
real), this derivative is strictly positive for all real
A
. Therefore,
f(A)
is a strictly increasing function. That means the equation has exactly one real root. Since
f(A)
is monotonic, whether the unique root lies at
A>0
depends only on the sign of
f(0)
. At
A=0
,

f(0)=-36\pi M^{2}V^{2}+64\pi^{3}J^{4}.

(10)

So, if
f(0)<0
, then as
A\to\infty
,
f(A)\to\infty
, so the crossing point (the root) must occur at some positive
A
. Therfore, there is exactly one physically meaningful real root with
A>0
if and only if the inequality (
3
) is satisfied.

On the other side, rewritting the ratio
4\pi J^{2}/(3MV)
in terms of geometrical values, reads

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi J^{2}}{3MV}=\frac{a^{2}\left[\left(a^{2}+r_{+}^{2}\right)\left(r_{+}^{2}+\ell^{2}\right)+q^{2}\ell^{2}\right]}{a^{2}q^{2}\ell^{2}+\left(a^{2}+r_{+}^{2}\right)\left[a^{2}\left(\ell^{2}-r_{+}^{2}\right)+2r_{+}^{2}\ell^{2}\right]}.

(11)

To make the fraction above is less than one, the numerator must be smaller than the denominator; In other words, the difference between the denominator and numerator of the fraction must be negative,
i.e.,

2r_{+}^{2}\left(a^{2}+r_{+}^{2}\right)\left(a^{2}-\ell^{2}\right)<0,

(12)

which yields

\lvert a\rvert<\ell,

(13)

that is the condition for the non-singularity of the KN-AdS metric (see Supplement). The validity of the inequality (
3
) for the KN-AdS black hole means that the isoperimetric ratio
{\cal R}
satisfies the RII (
{\cal R}\geq 1
) in its conventional form for charged rotating black holes with spherical horizons.

For charged and rotating black strings, the ratio is given by

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi J^{2}}{3MV}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{+}^{2}\sqrt{a^{2}+\ell^{2}}\left(a^{2}+2\ell^{2}\right)\left(q^{2}\ell^{2}-3r_{+}^{4}\right)\left(q^{2}\ell^{2}+r_{+}^{4}\right)}{12\ell^{2}\left[a^{2}\left(q^{4}\ell^{4}-2q^{2}r_{+}^{4}\ell^{2}+5r_{+}^{8}\right)+\ell^{2}\left(q^{2}\ell^{2}+r_{+}^{4}\right)^{2}\right]}\,.

(14)

Satisfying the inequality (
3
) requires that

\displaystyle q<q_{\rm ext}\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}+\ell^{2}}{3a^{2}+\ell^{2}}},

(15)

where
q_{\rm ext}
is the critical value of the black string charge parameter
[
36
]
,

q_{\rm ext}=\frac{\sqrt{3}r_{+}^{2}}{\ell},

(16)

at which, the temperature vanishes, the horizon is degenerate and the black string is extremal. Since for nonzero rotation
a\neq 0
, the ratio

\frac{a^{2}+\ell^{2}}{3a^{2}+\ell^{2}}<1,

(17)

hence

\displaystyle q_{\rm ext}\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}+\ell^{2}}{3a^{2}+\ell^{2}}}<q_{\rm ext},

(18)

which yields

q<q_{\rm ext}\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}+\ell^{2}}{3a^{2}+\ell^{2}}}<q_{\rm ext},

(19)

meaning the black string has a horizon. So, the inequality defines a tighter bound on the charge of the charged and rotating AdS black string.

For our next example, we consider the charged, rotating AdS C-metric. We start from the inequality (
55
)

36\pi M^{2}V^{2}-M^{2}A^{3}-64\pi^{3}J^{4}\geq 0\,.

(20)

Assuming the physical condition
M>0
, we divide the inequality by
M^{2}
and rewrite it as

A^{3}\leq 36\pi V^{2}\left[1-\left(\frac{4\pi J^{2}}{3MV}\right)^{2}\right].

(21)

For the inequality to admit a positive solution for
A
, the right-hand side must be non-negative, because the left-hand side
A^{3}
is non-negative for all real
A\geq 0
. Thus, the necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a positive area becomes simply
4\pi J^{2}/(3MV)\leq 1
. Note that if the inequality (
3
) is satisfied the right-hand side of (
21
) is strictly positive, and the inequality (
21
) admits a positive interval of allowed areas, and thus a positive horizon area exists. If
4\pi J^{2}/(3MV)=1
, the right-hand side of (
21
) becomes zero, and the inequality (
21
) admits only the trivial solution
A=0
, which is not physically acceptable. Thus no physical positive area exists.

Extension to higher dimensions
. In high dimensions, obtaining an identity for thermodynamic variables would generically require solving a polynomial of degree greater than four. This prevents representing the inequality in dimensions larger than four. To obtain the
D
-dimensional version of inequality (
3
), we consider the intermediate RRII introduced in
[
32

with this requirement that the RII should also be satisfied in
D

\displaystyle\text{Even $D$:}\;\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi(D-2)J_{\rm min}^{2}}{(D-1)MV}<1,&\text{for angular momenta $J_{i}$ with $J_{\rm min}={\rm min}\{\left|J_{i}\right|\}$},\\
\frac{8\pi J^{2}}{(D-1)(D-2)MV}<1,&\text{for a single non-zero angular momentum $J$},\end{cases}

(25)

and

\displaystyle\text{Odd $D$:}\;\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi J_{\rm min}^{2}}{MV}<1,&\text{for angular momenta $J_{i}$ with $J_{\rm min}={\rm min}\{\left|J_{i}\right|\}$},\\
\frac{4\pi J^{2}}{(D-1)(D-2)MV}<1,&\text{for a single non-zero angular momentum $J$}.\end{cases}

(26)

The grinding route for synthesis of dihydropyrrolophenanthrolines and their conversion to dipyrrolophenanthrolines under reflux condition
Authors: Negar Zarei, Ghasem Marandi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe grinding method is a new and environmentally friendly protocol for synthesizing of 6a,7-dihydropyrrolo[1,2-a:2’,1’-k][1,10]phenanthroline derivatives as demonstrated in this study. The results of this investigation show that the reaction between 1,10-phenanthroline and dialkyl acetylenedicarboxylates is completed rapidly, yielding high amounts of 6a,7-dihydropyrrolo[1,2-a:2’,1’-k][1,10]phenanthroline derivatives. These synthetic compounds were subsequently converted to dipyrrolo[1,2-a:2’,1’-k][1,10]phenanthrolines under reflux conditions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24165-7.Keywords: 1,10-phenanthroline; 6a,7-dihydropyrrolo[1,2-a:2',1'-k][1,10]phenanthroline; Acetylenic diesters; Solvent-free grinding conditionsSubject terms: Chemical safety, Green chemistry, Organic chemistry, Chemical synthesis

Introduction

The synthesis of organic compounds with valuable properties in medicine and chemistry is a key objective1–4. Scientists are exploring various methods to achieve these goals and selecting the most effective one5. In chemistry, there are numerous methods available for the synthesis of different organic compounds. When choosing a method, besides aiming for maximum efficiency, minimizing environmental harm is an important criterion6–8. Solvent-free reactions have recently gained attention among organic chemists for the synthesis of many organic compounds9. Grinding and milling methodologies have gained more attention in chemistry10–19. These methods offer advantages compared to conventional reactions, including: simple procedures, large-scale preparation, eco-friendliness, low cost, clean reaction profiles, reaction control, ease of use in solvent-free conditions11,20–22. Phenanthroline and its derivatives hold a unique position in organic and inorganic chemistry, medicine and material sciences23–30. For example, 1,10-phenanthroline removes metal ions from biological systems by utilizing two nitrogen atoms positioned uniquely in its structure28,31. This enhances the medicinal activity of metal ions in a biological environment32–34. Therefore, in continuation of our investigations35–38, we decided to synthesize 1,10-phenanthroline-based structures in a solvent-free milling reaction using acetylenic esters to obtain 6a,7-dihydropyrrolo[1,2-a:2’,1’-k][1,10]phenanthroline derivatives without any pre-activation. Our study results show that the reaction between 1,10-phenanthroline and dialkyl acetylenedicarboxylate is completed in a short amount of time compared to our previous experiment: one hour versus twenty hours (Fig. 1).

Results and discussion

In heterocyclic structures like 1,10-phenanthroline, each nitrogen atom possesses a lone pair of electrons that can function as individual nucleophiles39–43. Once the nitrogen atom’s lone pair of electrons is involved, the neighboring atom can be attacked by a nucleophile through nucleophilic attack. Using this strategy, we conducted a reaction between 1,10-phenanthroline (1) and dialkyl acetylenedicarboxylates (2) to produce derivatives (3) of 6a,7-dihydropyrrolo[1,2-a:2’,1’-k][1,10]phenanthroline through the milling of reaction components at room temperature (Fig. 1).

The grinding procedure to obtain 6a,7-dihydropyrrolo[1,2-a:2’,1’-k][1,10]phenanthroline from 1,10-phenanthroline follows green chemistry principles because this transformation does not require a solvent or external heating. The heat required in the grinding process is generated by the mechanical energy applied. This energy is then used to break vulnerable bonds in molecules and create new bonds in the resulting products44–47.

The polar N = C bond in the 1–2 position of phenanthroline plays a significant role in the molecule’s reaction. The nitrogen atom tends to donate electrons, while the carbon atom tends to accept electrons in this type of reaction35,39,41,46.

In most multicomponent reactions involving acetylenic esters, they serve as mediators for electron transfer from one component to another, and this reaction is no different. It seems that the grinding method provides the required energy for the nitrogen atoms of the 1,10-phenanthroline to attack the electron-deficient triple bond in the acetylenic ester. Simultaneously, by generating energy through mechanical stress, it activates the orbitals of the triple bond to accept the pair of electrons from the nitrogen atom.

Compound 3 is a stable solid with a structure determined through spectroscopic analysis, including IR, 1H and 13C NMR spectroscopy.

The stereo-selective structure of compound 3 was determined based on spectral data. The 1H NMR spectrum of compound 3a showed a coupling constant of J = 4.1 Hz for two vicinal protons (CH-7 and CH-6a) in chiral centers. In compound 3a, the 1,2 vicinal proton structure is determined by the current coupling constant between them. This coupling value reveals the trans position of the two 1,2 vicinal protons in the pentagonal ring structure. However, this coupling value is not observed when the protons are in positions 1 and 4. Additionally, when molecule 3a is deprotonated under reflux conditions, it becomes aromatic. As a result, the peaks and cleavages associated with these two protons disappear, and the simplified spectrum corresponding to the structure of molecule 4a is revealed. The trans stereochemistry of the vicinal protons of CH-7 and CH-6a is shown in Fig. 1. The molecule 3a and also 3b can be produced as a racemic mixture in the reaction. Additionally, the 1H NMR spectra of 3a displayed six sharp singlets at 3.29, 3.68, 3.91, 3.94, 3.96 and 3.97 ppm, which were identified as methoxy protons. The protons H-7, H-6a, H-5 and H-6 resonated at 4.26 (dd), 5.37 (dd), 5.90 (d), and 6.70 (d) ppm, respectively. Further details can be found in the experimental section. Regarding compound 3a′, it should be noted that when the 5-nitro 1,10-phenanthroline was used instead of the 1,10-penanthroline derivative, compound 3a′ was produced without the need for reflux for aromatization.

Compound 4 was synthesized from compound 3 by refluxing in methanol and ethanol, depending on the methoxy- or ethoxycarbonyl substituent groups for 7 days. The structures of compound 3 with C-7 and C-6a, as well as compound 4, were confirmed using proton off-resonance 13C NMR spectra.

The FT-IR spectra of compounds 3 and 4 displayed distinct peaks for carbonyl groups at 1737 and 1701 cm−1 for compound 3, and 1751 and 1707 cm−1 for compound 4, respectively.

Compound 6 was synthesized as part of the ongoing development of compounds of this type using the method outlined in our previous research. This stable material, compound 6, was deduced from spectroscopic data such as IR, 1H and 13C NMR as well as mass spectroscopic data.

The FT-IR spectrum of compound 6 shows distinct absorbance at 1739 cm−1 for carbonyl groups. The 1H NMR spectrum of compound 6 shows three distinct resonances for methoxy carbonyl groups at 3.66, 3.72 and 3.80 ppm corresponding to the assigned structure.

In the illustrative mechanism for the generation of the helical structure of compound 3, the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atoms in the phenanthroline ring are well-positioned to attack the C ≡ C bond of the acetylenic diester. The subsequent addition of the acetylenic diester to the generated intermediate (A) leads to the formation of stable compound 3. Compound 3 can be converted to compound 4 by deprotonation under reflux conditions (see Fig. 3).

The proposed mechanism for the synthesis of 6a,7-dihydropyrrolo[1,2-a:2’,1’-k][1,10]phenanthroline and dipyrrolo[1,2-a:2’,1’-k][1,10]phenanthroline derivatives.

Even the mechanism presented in this article is just a proposal, given the structures produced by this synthetic method, it can serve as a suitable explanation for the standard description of compounds. This mechanism has also been referenced in the description of compounds obtained through previous methods42,43.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we have developed an efficient method to synthesize dihydropyrrolophenanthroline derivatives. This method involves a solvent-free grinding reaction between 1,10-phenanthroline and acetylenic esters to produce fused polycyclic aromatic structures. By utilizing mechanical energy to facilitate bond formation between the reactant atoms, this process enables the creation of new compounds in a short amount of time. This approach is not only effective, but it also helps to reduce the negative impact of chemical solvents on the environment and human health, including toxicity and pollution. Additionally, mechanical procedures such as grinding enable chemists to produce new compounds that disrupt crystalline structures and induce chemical transformations.

Experimental

Melting points and IR spectra were measured using a Barnstead Electrothermal 9200 apparatus and a Thermo-Nicolet Nexus 670 FT-IR spectrometer, respectively. The 1H and 13C NMR spectra were obtained using a BRUKER DRX-250 AVANCE instrument with CDCl3 and DMSO-d6 as the solvents, and tetramethylsilane (TMS) as an internal standard. The frequencies used were 250 and 62.9 MHz, respectively. In addition, mass spectra were recorded on a Finnigan-Matt 8430 mass spectrometer operating at an ionisation potential of 70 ev. The Elemental analyses for C, H and N were performed using a Heraeus CHN-O-Rapid analyzer. Additionally, all chemicals including solvents were sourced from Fluka, Merck and Sigma-Aldrich and used without further purification.

General procedure (exemplified by 3a)

In a mortar dimethyl acetylenedicarboxylate (0.639 g, 4.5 mmol) was added dropwise, onto 1,10-phenanthroline (0.198 g, 1 mmol) and crushed vigorously to create a homogeneous mass at room temperature over 60 min. The reaction mixture was then allowed to sit covered at room temperature for 10 h. The solid phase was collected by filtration and washed with 3 × 5 mL of methanol to yield 3a as a pale yellow solid.

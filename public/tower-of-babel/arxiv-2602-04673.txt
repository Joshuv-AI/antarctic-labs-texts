Brownian paths as loop-decorated SLEs
Nathanaël Berestycki, Isao Sauzedde

Abstract
We construct an application, which takes as input a simple path and a possibly infinite collection of loops, and outputs a continuous path by adding the loops chronologically to the simple path as the simple path encounters them. By studying the regularity properties of this application and using lattice discretisations, we prove that chronologically adding the loops from a Brownian loop soup encountered by an independent radial SLE 2 path produces a continuous path which has the law of a planar Brownian motion. This resolves a conjecture of Lawler and Werner [ 13 , Conjecture 1] . This construction produces a coupling between SLE 2 and Brownian motion, and we further show that this joint law is the scaling limit of the loop-erased random walk and the random walk itself. The arguments are robust and can be applied for instance in the off-critical setup, where the scaling limit of loop-erased random walk is Makarov and Smirnov’s massive SLE 2 [ 14 ] .



Keywords:
Loop-erased random walk, Schramm–Loewner Evolution (SLE), Brownian motion, Brownian loop soup, random walk loop soup, scaling limits.

1.3
Strategy and organisation of the paper

Approach and main ideas.
Before discussing some of the key points of the proof it is useful to first give a brief overview of the approach of Lawler and Werner in
[
13
]
. Their viewpoint is to consider the set of loops as they are discovered chronologically along the (chordal) SLE
2
path
\gamma
. Since the (unrooted) Brownian loop soup is conformally invariant, one may map out at any point
s
the portion
\gamma[0,s]
of the SLE
2
path discovered by time
s
along with the loops that it has intersected so far. What remains is a Brownian loop soup in the complement of this domain, which is the conformal image of an independent Brownian loop soup in the upper-half plane. In this way, the sequence of loops encountered by the SLE path can be described as a Poisson point process whose intensity is an infinite measure on loops in the upper half plane rooted at the origin, the so-called bubble measure. A similar property holds at the discrete level as well. As mentioned by Lawler and Werner, to exploit this approach in order to prove their conjecture, the key difficulty is to prove a form of convergence of discrete bubble measures to their continuum counterpart. This is potentially tricky due to the fact that this would need to be proved in domains with very rough boundaries (namely, the complement of a piece of loop-erased random walk and loops intersected by it).

By contrast, our approach is more “hands-on” and is more closely related to the one used in
[
16
]
. We consider a discrete analogue of the construction in Theorem
1.5
, in which we add the loops of a discrete random walk loop soup encountered by an independent loop-erased random walk; it is already known that this produces a path with the same law as a random walk. We then check that as we take a scaling limit, if the loop-erased random walk is close to the SLE
2
path and the discrete loop soup is close to the continuum loop soup, then the resulting concatenation of discrete loops is close to its continuum analogue and must therefore be a Brownian motion.

The main difficulties relate to the fact that a small deformation of a path and/or a loop could result in missing a loop entirely, or on the contrary adding many new loops, or rooting it at a different place (see Fig.
2
).
Luckily, we show that the rerooting map
\ell\mapsto\theta_{\ell}
is discontinuous only at loops which visits
\ell(\theta_{\ell})
either twice or in a
one-sided
way. Another source of difficulty is the need to control the total time spend on microscopic and mesoscopic loops, uniformly over the deformation. We introduce the space

Summary of the construction

We shall define two sets

of pairs

For

\mbox{For all $\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}$ and $t\in[0,t_{\ell}]$, }\hskip 9.24994ptX(T_{\ell}+t)=\hat{\ell}(t),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{where}\hskip 9.24994ptT_{\ell}=T_{\ell}(\mathcal{X},B,\lambda)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma},\\

We first construct the sets

\mu\in[0,1]
is the unique value such that
t=(1-\mu)(T_{\ell}+t_{\ell})+\mu T_{\ell^{\prime}}
.

Let us now consider the general situation (for
t\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]\setminus\mathcal{T}
). First, set

t_{-}\coloneqq 0\vee\sup\{T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma},T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}\leq t\},\hskip 18.49988ptt_{+}\coloneqq t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}\wedge\inf\{T_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma},T_{\ell}\geq t\},

(2)

\sigma_{-}\coloneqq 0\vee\sup\{\sigma_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma},T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}\leq t\},\hskip 18.49988pt\sigma_{+}\coloneqq t_{\gamma}\wedge\inf\{\sigma_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma},T_{\ell}\geq t\}.

Then,
t_{-}\leq t\leq t_{+}
.
In the case
t_{-}=t_{+}
, let
\mu(t)\coloneqq 0
. Otherwise, let
\mu(t)\coloneqq(t-t_{-})/(t_{+}-t_{-})\in[0,1]
, i.e.
t=(1-\mu)t_{-}+\mu t_{+}
, and set, on
[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]\setminus\mathcal{T}
,

\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}\coloneqq(1-\mu)\sigma_{-}+\mu\sigma_{+}.

In particular, these functions satisfy
\sigma_{-}\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}\leq\sigma_{+}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Clearly the function
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
does not depend on the choice of the roots in
\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}}\Theta_{\ell}
. Although it is less immediate, it also does not depend on the choice of the orders (thus it does not depend from the choice of
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{X}}
at all). Indeed, the values of
\sigma_{-}
,
\sigma_{+}
do not depend on these orders, and although there are some values of
t
for which
t_{-}
and
t_{+}
do depend on the choice of
B
, this is the case only for
t
such that
\sigma_{-}=\sigma_{+}
, in which case
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is just equal to this common value anyway.

For later use, we now prove the following. (Intuitively, this tells us among other things that the resulting path
X
does not depend on the initial parametrisation of
\gamma
).

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
\lambda=0
and
\mathcal{X}\in{\mathscr{R}}_{0}
, the set
\mathcal{T}
is dense on
[0,t_{\mathcal{X},0}]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T^{\prime}}=\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}}[T_{\ell},T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}]
, thus
\mathcal{T}
is dense in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.

The key point is that, for
\lambda=0
, for all
s\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},0}]\setminus\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, it holds
s_{-}=s_{+}
(for
\lambda\neq 0
, they would differ by
\lambda(\sigma_{+}-\sigma_{-})
). Indeed, for such
s
,

\displaystyle s_{+}=\inf\{\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\prec\ell}t_{\ell^{\prime}}:T_{\ell}\geq s\}

\displaystyle=\sum\{t_{\ell^{\prime}}:\forall\ell,T_{\ell}\geq s\implies\ell^{\prime}\prec\ell\}

\displaystyle=\sum\{t_{\ell^{\prime}}:\forall\ell,T_{\ell}\geq s\implies T_{\ell^{\prime}}<T_{\ell}\}

\displaystyle=\sum\{t_{\ell^{\prime}}:T_{\ell^{\prime}}<s\},

where the second equality on the first line is by monotone convergence theorem.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle s_{-}=\sup\{\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\preceq\ell}t_{\ell^{\prime}}:T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}\geq s\}

\displaystyle=\sum\{t_{\ell^{\prime}}:\forall\ell,T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}\geq s\implies\ell^{\prime}\preceq\ell\}

\displaystyle=\sum\{t_{\ell^{\prime}}:T_{\ell^{\prime}}+t_{\ell^{\prime}}\geq s\}

\displaystyle=\sum\{t_{\ell^{\prime}}:T_{\ell^{\prime}}<s\},

where the second equality is again by monotone convergence theorem, and the last equality is obtained by using the fact
s\notin\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
). Thus,
s_{-}=s_{+}
as claimed.

Let now
s<t\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},0}]\setminus\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. We will prove there exists
u\in[s,t]\cap\mathcal{T}
.
Since
s=s_{+}
, either there exists
\ell
such that
T_{\ell}=s
, in which case we can take
u\coloneqq(t+s)/2\wedge(T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}/2)
, or there exists a sequence
(\ell_{n})
such that
T_{\ell_{n}}
decreases toward
s
. Then
T_{\ell_{n}}+t_{\ell_{n}}\leq T_{\ell_{n-1}}
, thus
(T_{\ell_{n}}+t_{\ell_{n}})
also decreases toward
s
. Thus, for any
n
large enough,
T_{\ell_{n}}+t_{\ell_{n}}<t
, and we can take
u\coloneqq(T_{\ell_{n}}+t_{\ell_{n}}/2)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

2.6
Continuity in time of
{\Xi}(\mathcal{X},\lambda,B)

The end of the section is dedicated to the proof that, for
(\mathcal{X},\lambda)\in{\mathscr{R}}^{\prime}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{X}}
, the function
X={\Xi}(\mathcal{X},\lambda,B)
is continuous. During the process, we will record two quantitative bounds, in particular the key estimation (
3
) in the next lemma, which will turn useful when we will consider the harder problem of the continuity of the map
(\mathcal{X},\lambda,B)\mapsto{\Xi}(\mathcal{X},\lambda,B)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\in{\mathscr{R}}_{0}
and
\lambda\geq 0
.
If either
\lambda>0
or
\mathcal{X}
satisfies Condition (
iv
), the application
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is continuous on
[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]
.

Furthermore, we have the following quantitative bound, independent of
\lambda
, on the continuity moduli: for all
\delta>0
, let
(\sigma_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,k\}}
be an increasing enumeration of the finite set
\{\sigma_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\delta}\}
, and let
\sigma_{0}=0
,
\sigma_{k+1}=t_{\gamma}
. Then,

\omega_{\delta}(\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda})\leq\omega^{1}_{\delta}(\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\max\{\sigma_{i+1}-\sigma_{i}:i\in\{0,\dots,k\}\}.

(3)

Proof.

We first prove (
3
).
Let
s<t
. Assume
(\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(s),\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t))\cap\{\sigma_{i}:i\in\{0,\dots,k+1\}\}\neq\emptyset
, and let
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\delta}
such that
\sigma_{\ell}\in(\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(s),\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t))
. For all
u\in(T_{\ell},T_{\ell}+t_{\ell})
,
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(s)<\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(u)=\sigma_{\ell}<\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)
. Since
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is non-decreasing, we deduce
s\leq u\leq t
.
It follows
s\leq T_{\ell}
and
t\leq T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}
, thus
t-s\geq t_{\ell}\geq\delta
. By contraposition,

t-s<\delta\implies(\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(s),\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t))\cap\{\sigma_{i}:i\in\{0,\dots,k+1\}\}=\emptyset\implies\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)-\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(s)\leq\omega^{1}_{\delta}(\mathcal{X}),

which proves (
3
).

For the continuity of
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
, we consider separately the case when
\mathcal{X}
satisfies the condition (
iv
) and the case
\lambda>0
.

The former case follows directly from (
3
). Indeed, as
\delta\to 0
,
\omega^{1}_{\delta}(\mathcal{X})
converges toward

\sup\{\sigma^{\prime}-\sigma:\sigma^{\prime}\geq\sigma,(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\cap\{\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{L}\}=\emptyset\},

which is
0
by (
iv
). Thus
\omega_{\delta}(\sigma)\leq\omega^{1}_{\delta}(\mathcal{X})\underset{\delta\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0
, hence
\sigma
is continuous.

Consider now the latter case
\lambda>0
. If the set
\sigma_{\mathcal{L}}
is finite, then we can easily show that the function
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is Lipschitz-continuous. On the other end, if
\sigma_{\mathcal{L}}
is dense, the argument above also allows to conclude that
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is continuous. However, when
\sigma_{\mathcal{L}}
is neither discret nor dense, neither argument allows to conclude: we must ‘localise’ the previous argument. For some
t
, we need to use ‘the Lipschitz argument’ on the left and ‘the density argument’ on the right, or the other way around, so we also distinguish between left and right continuity.

\scriptstyle\diamond

The continuity of
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
at any
t\in\mathcal{T}
is obvious, since there is an open set
(T_{\ell},T_{\ell}+t_{\ell})\ni t
on which
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is locally constant. Let now
t\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]\setminus\mathcal{T}
.

\scriptstyle\diamond

For
t\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]\setminus\mathcal{T}
such that
t_{-}<t<t_{+}
, the restriction of
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
to
(t_{-},t_{+})
is affine, thus continuous. For the same reason, if
t<t_{+}
then
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is right-continuous at
t
, and if
t>t_{-}
then

\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is left-continuous at
t
.

\scriptstyle\diamond

It only remains to show that, for
t=t_{+}
,
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is right-continuous at
t
, and that for
t=t_{-}
,
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is left-continuous at
t
.

In the case
t=t_{-}
,
\mu(t)=0
so that
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)=\sigma_{-}(t)
.

In the case
t=t_{+}
, either
\mu(t)=1
or
t_{-}=t_{+}
. In the former case
\mu(t)=1
, it holds
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)=\sigma_{+}(t)
.
In the latter case
t_{-}=t_{+}
, there exists a non-decreasing sequence
(\ell^{-}_{n})
and a non-increasing sequence
(\ell^{+}_{n})
such that
T_{\ell^{+}_{n}}
and

T_{\ell^{-}_{n}}+t_{\ell_{n}^{-}}
both converges toward
t=t_{-}=t_{+}
as
n\to\infty
. However, the difference
T_{\ell^{+}_{n}}-(T_{\ell^{-}_{n}}+t_{\ell_{n}^{-}})
is bounded below by
\lambda\cdot(\sigma_{\ell^{+}_{n}}-\sigma_{\ell^{-}_{n}})\geq\lambda\cdot(\sigma_{+}(t)-\sigma_{-}(t))
. Since
\lambda>0
, we deduce
\sigma_{-}(t)=\sigma_{+}(t)
, hence
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)=\sigma_{+}(t)
.
In both the case
\mu(t)=1
and the case
t_{-}=t_{+}
, we have proved
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)=\sigma_{+}(t)
.

In the rest of the proof, the cases
t=t_{-}
and
t=t_{+}
are now symmetrical, so we only consider the right-continuity for
t=t_{+}
.

We make two subcases depending on whether the infimum defining
t_{+}
is achieved of not. If it is achieved, i.e. there exists
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
such that
t=T_{\ell}
, then
\sigma_{+}(t)=\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}
by definition. Thus

\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)=\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}
. For all
s\in(t,t+t_{\ell})
, by definition of
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
,
\mathcal{X},\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(s)=\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}
. Thus,
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is locally constant on
[t,t+t_{\ell})
, hence right-continuous at
t
.

In the case when the infimum defining
t_{+}
is not achieved, there exists a sequence
(\ell_{n})_{n}
in
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
such that
T_{\ell_{n}}
decreases toward
t_{+}=t
.
Since
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}
is non-decreasing and
T_{\ell_{n}}
decreases toward
t
,
\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\to t,\\
s>t\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(s)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(T_{\ell_{n}})
.
By definition of
\sigma_{+}
and monotonicity of
\ell\mapsto\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}
,
\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(T_{\ell_{n}})
converges toward
\sigma_{+}(t)
. Since
\sigma_{+}(t)=\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)
,

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\to t,\\
s>t\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(s)=\sigma_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}(t)
, which concludes the proof.∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\in{\mathscr{R}}_{0}
and
\lambda\geq 0
.
If either
\lambda>0
or
\mathcal{X}
satisfies (
iv
), for whichever choice of tie break
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{X}}
, for all
\epsilon>0
,

\omega_{\epsilon}({\Xi}(\mathcal{X},\lambda,B))\leq 2\,\omega^{l}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L})+\omega_{\epsilon}(\gamma\circ\sigma_{{\mathcal{X}},\lambda}).

In particular, the function
X={\Xi}(\mathcal{X},\lambda,B)
is continuous on
[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]
,
and the family of functions
\{{\Xi}(\gamma,\mathcal{L},\lambda,B)\}_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\gamma,\mathcal{L}}}
is equicontinuous.

Proof.

Define
\pi_{-},\pi_{+}:[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]\to[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]\setminus\mathcal{T}
by
\pi_{-}t(t)=\pi_{+}(t)=t
for
t\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]\setminus\mathcal{T}
, and for
t\in(T_{\ell},T_{\ell}+t_{\ell})
,
\pi_{-}(t)\coloneqq T_{\ell}
,
\pi_{+}(t)\coloneqq T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}
.
In particular, for all
t
,
\pi_{-}(t)\leq t\leq\pi_{+}(t)
. Furthermore, for any
s,t\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]
with
s<t
, either there exists
\ell
such that

T_{\ell}<s<t<T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}
, or
\pi_{+}(s)\leq\pi_{-}(t)
.

Let
s,t\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]
such that
s<t<s+\epsilon
. In the case there exists
\ell
such that
T_{\ell}<s<t<T_{\ell}+t_{\ell}
,

|X(t)-X(s)|=|\hat{\ell}(t-T_{\ell})-\hat{\ell}(s-T_{\ell})|\leq\omega_{\epsilon}(\hat{\ell})\leq\omega^{l}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}).

Otherwise,
s<\pi_{+}(s)\leq\pi_{-}(t)<t<s+\epsilon
. Since
\pi_{-}(t),\pi_{+}(s)\in[0,t_{\mathcal{X},\lambda}]\setminus\mathcal{T}
,

X(\pi_{-}(t))=\gamma\circ\sigma(\pi_{-}(t))
and

. Thus,

\displaystyle|X(t)-X(s)|

\displaystyle\leq|X(t)-X(\pi_{-}(t))|+|\gamma\circ\sigma(\pi_{-}(t))-\gamma\circ\sigma(\pi_{+}(s))|+|X(\pi_{+}(s))-X(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\omega^{l}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L})+\omega_{\epsilon}(\gamma\circ\sigma)+\omega^{l}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}).

The equicontinuity condition and the continuity of
\gamma
and

ensure
2\omega^{l}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L})+\omega_{\epsilon}(\gamma\circ\sigma)\underset{\epsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0
,
which concludes the proof.

3
Attaching a single loop to a simple path

In this section, we consider continuity of the applications
(\gamma,\ell)\mapsto x_{\gamma,\ell},\ \sigma_{\gamma,\ell},\ \theta^{-}_{\gamma,\ell},\ \theta^{+}_{\gamma,\ell}
. One can easily convince oneself that discontinuity points
(\gamma,\ell)
are not generic, and we thus search for generic conditions on
(\gamma,\ell)
ensuring the continuity at
(\gamma,\ell)
.

3.1
The set of regular pairs

For
\gamma\in\mathscr{S}
and
x\in\gamma((0,t_{\gamma}))
, we first construct two connected open sets
L(x)
and
R(x)
such that the disjoint union
\Range(\gamma)\sqcup L(x)\sqcup R(x)
contains a neighbourhood of
x
, and which we can think as the ‘left’ and ‘right’ sides of
\gamma
, around
x
.

Let
\gamma^{-1}:\Range(\gamma)\to[0,t_{\gamma}]
be the inverse of
\gamma
,
o\coloneqq\gamma(0)
,
f\coloneqq\gamma(t_{\gamma})
, and
\mathbf{r}(x)\coloneqq d(x,\{o,f\})
. Then, for any
x\in\Range(\gamma)\setminus\{o,f\}
, it holds
\mathbf{r}(x)>0
. Let

\sigma_{\gamma}^{-}(x)
and
\sigma_{\gamma}^{+}(x)
be respectively the last time when
\gamma
enters
B_{\mathbf{r}(x)}(x)
before reaching
x
, and the first time when
\gamma
exits
B_{\mathbf{r}(x)}(x)
after reaching
x
:

\sigma_{\gamma}^{-}(x)\coloneqq\sup\{\sigma<\gamma^{-1}(x):\gamma_{\sigma}\in\partial B_{\mathbf{r}(x)}(x)\}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\sigma_{\gamma}^{+}(x)\coloneqq\inf\{\sigma>\gamma^{-1}(x):\gamma_{\sigma}\in\partial B_{\mathbf{r}(x)}(x)\}.

In particular,
0\leq\sigma_{\gamma}^{-}(x)\leq\gamma^{-1}(x)\leq\sigma_{\gamma}^{+}(x)\leq t_{\gamma}
.

The restriction of
\gamma
to the interval
(\sigma_{\gamma}^{-}(x),\sigma_{\gamma}^{+}(x))
takes values on
B_{\mathbf{r}(x)}(x)
, whilst
\gamma(\sigma_{\gamma}^{\pm}(x))\in\partial B_{\mathbf{r}(x)}(x)
. Let
\overleftarrow{\gamma}
(which depends on
\gamma
but also on
x
) be the arclength parametrisation of the arc going anticlockwise from

\gamma(\sigma_{\gamma}^{+}(x))
to
\gamma(\sigma_{\gamma}^{-}(x))
along
\partial B_{\mathbf{r}(x)}(x)
. Then, the concatenation
\eta\coloneqq\gamma_{|[\sigma_{\gamma}^{-}(x),\sigma_{\gamma}^{+}(x)]}\cdot\overleftarrow{\gamma}
is a simple loop. By the Jordan curve theorem,
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Range(\eta)
consists of exactly two connected components. We let
L(x)
be the bounded one, and
R(x)
be the unbounded one.

Definition 3.1
.

The loop
\ell
is said to be

\gamma
if it intersects
\gamma
and the following holds.

i)

The set of temporal roots
\Theta_{\ell}
is reduced to a single element
\theta_{\ell}
. Furthermore,
\sigma_{\ell}\notin\{0,t_{\gamma}\}
.
Remark this prevents
\theta^{-}_{\ell}=0
, since then
\theta^{+}_{\ell}=t_{\ell}
.

ii)

The intersection at
\theta_{\ell}
is bilateral: for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\theta^{L},\theta^{R}\in[\theta_{\ell}-\epsilon,\theta_{\ell}+\epsilon]
such that

\ell(\theta^{L})\in L(x_{\ell})
and
\ell(\theta^{R})\in R(x_{\ell})
.

The subset of
\mathscr{L}_{\gamma}
of loops which are regular for
\gamma
is denoted
\mathscr{L}_{reg,\gamma}
. We define

\mathscr{L}_{reg}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\gamma\in\mathscr{S}}(\{\gamma\}\times\mathscr{L}_{reg,\gamma})\hskip 18.49988pt\mbox{and}\hskip 18.49988pt\mathscr{L}_{0}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\gamma\in\mathscr{S}}(\{\gamma\}\times\mathscr{L}_{\gamma}).

The main goal of this subsection is to prove the following properties, where
\mathscr{S}\times\mathscr{L}\supset\mathscr{L}_{0}
is endowed with the topology induced by
\rho\times d_{\infty}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

1.

The set
\mathscr{L}_{0}
is a neighbourhood of
\mathscr{L}_{reg}
in
\mathscr{S}\times\mathscr{L}
(i.e., there exists

an open set such that
\mathscr{L}_{reg}\subset U\subset\mathscr{L}_{0}
).

2.

The applications which map
(\ell,\gamma)
to
\theta^{-}_{\ell}
,
\theta^{+}_{\ell}
,

, and
\sigma_{\ell}
, all defined on

are continuous on
\mathscr{L}_{reg}
.

We remind the reader once more that continuity on
\mathscr{L}_{reg}
is stronger than continuity of the applications restricted to
\mathscr{L}_{reg}
: what this says is that for any fixed
(\gamma,\ell)\in\mathscr{L}_{reg}
, and any sequence
(\gamma_{n},\ell_{n})
in
\mathscr{L}_{0}
such that

The proof of this theorem is given in Sections
3.2
and
3.3
. We first make a few elementary observations first.

Remarks 3.3
.

By Lemma
3.4
below, the topologies induced by
d_{\infty}
and
\rho
on
\mathcal{C}([0,\cdot],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, hence on
\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{L}
, are equal to each other. Thus, we will alternatively use the distances
\rho\times\rho
,
\rho\times d_{\infty}
, and
d_{\infty}\times d_{\infty}
.

Furthermore, the considered topologies are metric, hence first countable. We will thus rely on sequential characterisation of continuity
without further explanation.

Neither
\mathscr{L}_{0}
nor
\mathscr{L}_{reg}
are open in
\mathscr{S}\times\mathscr{L}
(in fact, we will prove
\mathscr{L}_{0}
is closed).

The following is classical, and says that the two distances on paths from the introduction,
\rho
and
d_{\infty}
, are equivalent. We state it and prove it for completeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in\bigcup_{T\geq 0}\mathcal{F}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds

\rho(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\leq\omega_{d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{0})+3d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})

and

d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\leq 2\rho(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})+\min(\omega_{2\rho(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{0}),\omega_{2\rho(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{1})).

In particular, the distances
\rho
and
d_{\infty}
induce the same topology on
\mathcal{C}([0,\cdot],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in\bigcup_{T\geq 0}\mathcal{F}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and set
\varphi:t\mapsto t\cdot t_{\gamma_{1}}/t_{\gamma_{0}}
, which maps
[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]
to
[0,t_{\gamma_{1}}]
, and

\delta t\coloneqq|t_{\gamma_{0}}-t_{\gamma_{1}}|
.
Then,

\|\varphi-\operatorname{Id}\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]}=\|\varphi^{-1}-\operatorname{Id}\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{1}}]}=\delta t.

Assume
t_{\gamma_{0}}\geq t_{\gamma_{1}}
, so that
\gamma_{0}\circ\varphi
is well-defined on
[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]
. Then,

\displaystyle\|{\gamma}_{0}-{\gamma}_{1}\circ\varphi\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\gamma}_{0}-{\gamma}_{0}\circ\varphi\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]}+\|{\gamma}_{0}\circ\varphi-{\gamma}_{1}\circ\varphi\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]}

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\|\varphi-\operatorname{Id}\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]}}(\gamma_{0})+\|{\gamma}_{0}-{\gamma}_{1}\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{1}}]}=\omega_{\delta t}(\gamma_{0})+\|{\gamma}_{0}-{\gamma}_{1}\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{1}}]}.

Thus,

\rho(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\leq\omega_{\delta t}(\gamma_{0})+\|{\gamma}_{0}-{\gamma}_{1}\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{1}}]}+\delta t\leq\omega_{d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{0})+d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}).

(4)

In the case
t_{\gamma_{0}}\leq t_{\gamma_{1}}
, it holds
\omega_{d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{1})\leq\omega_{d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{0})+2d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
. Applying (
4
) with
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
swapped, we deduce

\rho(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\leq\omega_{d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{1})+d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\leq\omega_{d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}(\gamma_{0})+3d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}),

which is valid for both
t_{\gamma_{0}}\geq t_{\gamma_{1}}
and
t_{\gamma_{0}}\leq t_{\gamma_{1}}
.

On the other hand, for any
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
, let
\varphi
be a continuous increasing bijection from

One has

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\|\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}\circ\varphi\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]}+d_{\infty}(\gamma_{1}\circ\varphi,\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\|\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}\circ\varphi\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]}+|t_{\gamma_{0}}-t_{\gamma_{1}}|+\omega_{\|\varphi-\operatorname{Id}\|_{\infty,[0,t_{\gamma_{0}}]}}(\gamma_{1})

Proof.

By Lemma

, we can work with the distance
\rho\times\rho

at

\scriptstyle\diamond

We now start the proof of the continuity of

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\gamma_{n}\in\mathscr{S}
be a sequence which converges toward
\gamma\in\mathscr{S}
. Let
x_{n}\in\Range(\gamma_{n})
which converges toward

.
Then,
\gamma_{n}^{-1}(x_{n})
converges toward
\gamma^{-1}(x)
.

Proof.

First remark that for any sequence
(\sigma_{n})_{n}
such that
\sigma_{n}\in[0,t_{\gamma_{n}}]
for all
n
, we have the implication

\sigma_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\sigma\implies\gamma_{n}(\sigma_{n})\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\gamma(\sigma).

(6)

Indeed, the left-hand side implies
\sigma\leq t_{\gamma}
and

|\gamma_{n}(\sigma_{n})-\gamma(\sigma)|\leq|\gamma_{n}(\sigma_{n})-\gamma(\sigma_{n}\wedge t_{\gamma})|+|\gamma(\sigma_{n}\wedge t_{\gamma})-\gamma(\sigma)|\leq d_{\infty}(\gamma_{n},\gamma)+|\gamma(\sigma_{n}\wedge t_{\gamma})-\gamma(\sigma)|\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Let
x_{n},x
as in the lemma, and let
\sigma
be any accumulation point for
\{\gamma_{n}^{-1}(x_{n})\}
. Since, for all
n
large enough,
\gamma_{n}^{-1}(x_{n})\in[0,t_{\gamma}+1]
which is compact, it suffices to prove
\sigma=\gamma^{-1}(x)
. We let
n
vary along an infinite subset of
\mathbb{N}
such that
\gamma_{n}^{-1}(x_{n})\to\sigma
. By (
6
), it follows

x_{n}=\gamma_{n}(\gamma_{n}^{-1}(x_{n}))\to\gamma(\sigma)
.
By assumption,
x_{n}\to x
, thus
x=\gamma(\sigma)
. Since
\gamma
is injective, we deduce
\sigma=\gamma^{-1}(x)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
(\gamma_{n},\ell_{n})\in\mathscr{L}_{0}
be a sequence which converges toward
(\gamma,\ell)\in\mathscr{L}_{reg}
. Then,

\limsup\sigma_{\gamma_{n},\ell_{n}}\leq\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.6
, there exists a sequence
\theta_{n}
such that
\theta_{n}
converges toward
\theta_{\gamma,\ell}
, and
x_{n}\coloneqq\ell_{n}(\theta_{n})
converges toward
x_{\gamma,\ell}
, and for all
n
large enough
\ell_{n}(\theta_{n})\in\Range(\gamma_{n})
.
Applying Lemma
3.7
, which we can do since
x_{\gamma,\ell}\in\Range(\gamma)
, we deduce that

\gamma_{n}^{-1}(x_{n})
converges toward
\gamma^{-1}(x_{\gamma,\ell})=\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}

.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(\gamma_{n},\ell_{n})\in\mathscr{L}_{0}
be a sequence which converges toward
(\gamma,\ell)\in\mathscr{L}_{reg}
.
Let
(t_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of times such that
\ell_{n}(t_{n})\in\Range(\gamma_{n})
and assume
t_{n}\to t
as
n\to\infty
. Then,
\ell(t)\in\Range(\gamma)
.

Proof.

By triangle inequality,

d(\ell(t),\Range(\gamma))\leq|\ell(t)-\ell(t_{n}\wedge t_{\ell})|+|\ell(t_{n}\wedge t_{\ell})-\ell_{n}(t_{n})|+d(\ell_{n}(t_{n}),\Range(\gamma)).

Since
\ell_{n}\to\ell
,
t_{n}\leq t_{\ell_{n}}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}t_{\ell}
. Since furthermore
t_{n}\to t
, it holds
t_{n}\wedge t_{\ell}\to t
. By continuity of
\ell
, the first term on the right-hand side goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
. The second term is less than
d_{\infty}(\ell,\ell_{n})
which goes to
0
as well. Since
\ell_{n}(t_{n})\in\Range(\gamma_{n})
, the third term is less than
\rho(\gamma_{n},\gamma)
, which goes to
0
as well. Since the left-hand side does not depend on
n
, it is equal to
0
, i.e.
\ell(t)\in\overline{\Range(\gamma)}=\Range(\gamma)
.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
(\gamma_{n},\ell_{n})\in\mathscr{L}_{0}
be a sequence which converges toward
(\gamma,\ell)\in\mathscr{L}_{reg}
.
Then,

\liminf\sigma_{\gamma_{n},\ell_{n}}\geq\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}.

\sigma
be an accumulation point for

\scriptstyle\diamond

\scriptstyle\diamond

-isomorphism from

to

The first item is trivial to check.

For the second one, let

Since

,

\phi:\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}\to\phi(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0})
is bijective. For all
\ell\in\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
,
d_{\infty}(\ell,\phi(\ell))<\delta/2<\delta^{\prime}/2
.

For the last item, remark first that
\psi^{-1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{\geq\delta+\delta^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\geq\delta}
and that
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{\geq\delta+\delta^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\geq\delta^{\prime}}
, so that the sets

\mathcal{L}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{\geq\delta+\delta^{\prime}}\cup\phi^{-1}(\psi^{-1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{\geq\delta+\delta^{\prime}})),\hskip 18.49988pt\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{0}\coloneqq\psi(\phi(\mathcal{L}_{\geq\delta+\delta^{\prime}}))\cup(\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{\geq\delta+\delta^{\prime}})

are well-defined.
Furthermore,
\psi\circ\phi
is well-defined on
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, and
\psi\circ\phi(\mathcal{L}_{0})=\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{0}
. Since
\psi\circ\phi
is injective, we deduce

\Phi:\mathcal{L}_{0}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{0}
,
\ell\mapsto\psi\circ\phi(\ell)
is a bijection, and for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
,

d_{\infty}(\ell,\Phi(\ell))\leq d_{\infty}(\ell,\phi(\ell))+d_{\infty}(\phi(\ell),\psi(\phi(\ell)))\leq(\delta+\delta^{\prime})/2.\qed

Given
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathscr{S}
, we will say that
\phi
is a
\delta
-suited isomorphism
, with respect to
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
, if it is a
\delta
-isomorphism and furthermore
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\delta^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime}}
and
\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime},\geq\delta^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
.
We will say that

\phi
is a
\delta
-strongly suited isomorphism
, with respect to
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
, if it is a
\delta
-suited isomorphism
and furthermore,

\forall\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\delta^{\prime}}\cup\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime},\geq\delta^{\prime}}),\hskip 18.49988pt\ell\prec_{\gamma}\ell^{\prime}\iff\phi(\ell)\prec_{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi(\ell^{\prime}).

For
\mathcal{X}=(\gamma,\mathcal{L})
and
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
, we will say that
\phi
is a
\delta
-suited (resp. strongly suited) isomorphism from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
when it is a
\delta
-suited (resp. strongly suited) isomorphism from
\mathcal{L}
to
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
with respect to
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
.

4.2
Distances on
{\mathscr{R}}_{0}

It follows from Lemma
4.1
that
d
defined by

d(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\coloneqq\inf\{\delta>0:\mbox{there exists a $\delta$-isomorphism from $\mathcal{L}$ to $\mathcal{L}^{\prime}$}\}

(7)

is a distance on
\mathscr{L}^{lf}
.
We use it to define a distance
d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}
on
{\mathscr{R}}_{0}
: for
\mathcal{X}=(\gamma,\mathcal{L})
and
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
in
{\mathscr{R}}_{0}
,

d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime})\coloneqq\rho(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})+d(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})+|t_{\mathcal{X}}-t_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}|+\|\delta\mapsto(\omega_{\delta}(\mathcal{L})-\omega_{\delta}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}))\|_{\infty,[0,1]}

The ball of
({\mathscr{R}}_{0},d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}})
with radius
r
centred at
\mathcal{X}
is denoted
B_{r}(\mathcal{X})
.
The last two terms in the definition of
d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}
may look a bit artificial, but they are very crucial. The term
|t_{\mathcal{X}}-t_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}|
prevents the small loops to accumulate total time of order
1
(this prevents elements in
{\Xi}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})
from ‘getting stuck’ as
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}
). If we were to remove it from the definition of
d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}
, the map
{\Xi}
would be nowhere continuous (although it may remain continuous when viewed as a map with values in the space of paths up non-decreasing reparametrisations). As for the last term involving the continuity moduli, it prevents for example to add a single loop that would be very small in term of time duration but macroscopic spacewise (the limiting
{\Xi}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})
would ‘skip’ such a loop).

It follows from Lemma
4.5
and
4.7
below that, in order to prove convergence in
d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}
, it suffices to prove convergence in the weaker distance
d^{\prime}_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}
given by

d^{\prime}_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime})\coloneqq\rho(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})+d(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}),

(8)

together with uniform boundedness of
\epsilon\mapsto\omega_{\epsilon}
and
\epsilon\mapsto T_{\leq\epsilon}
(defined above these lemmas in (
10
)) by functions vanishing at
0
. Balls for the distance
d^{\prime}_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}
are written
B^{\prime}_{r}(\mathcal{X})
.

On the other hand, we will now prove that for any given
\mathcal{X}=(\gamma,\mathcal{L})\in\mathcal{R}
, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that any
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
at distance less than
\delta
from
\mathcal{X}
, there exists a strongly suited isomorphism from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
. In words, the topology induced by
d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}
is equal to the a priori stronger topology obtained by replacing ‘isomorphism’ with ‘strongly suited isomorphism’ in (
7
) (but strongly suited isomorphisms do not compose well, and triangle inequality would fail with such a modification).

Remark 4.2
.

The set
{\mathscr{R}}_{0}
is a measurable subset of
\mathscr{S}\times\mathscr{L}^{lf}
for the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(\gamma,\mathcal{L})
(the pair of an
SLE_{2}
path and a Brownian loop soup), and the identity from
({\mathscr{R}}_{0},\sigma(\mathscr{S}\times\mathscr{L}^{lf}))
to
({\mathscr{R}}_{0},d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}})
is measurable, which we leave as an exercise about point processes to prove. We will see that
(\gamma,\mathcal{L})\in{\mathscr{R}}_{0}

with probability
1
, and it thus makes sense to consider it as a random variable in
({\mathscr{R}}_{0},d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}})
.

4.3
Equality with stronger topology

As announced above, we now show that convergence (for the distance induced by the notion of
\delta
-isomorphism) implies a stronger form of convergence with respect to the notion of strongly suited isomorphism.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(\gamma,\mathcal{L})\in{\mathscr{R}}
. For all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\mathcal{X})\in(0,\epsilon]
such that for all
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in B^{\prime}_{\delta}(\mathcal{X})
, all
\delta
-isomorphism
\phi
from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
induces an
\epsilon
-suited isomorphism (with respect to
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
).

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\mathcal{X}=(\gamma,\mathcal{L})\in{\mathscr{R}}
. For
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
, let
\delta(\gamma,\ell)>0
such that for all
(\gamma^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\in\mathscr{S}\times\mathscr{L}
at
(\rho\times d_{\infty})
-distance less than or equal to
\delta(\gamma,\ell)
from
(\gamma,\ell)
, it holds
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathscr{L}_{\gamma^{\prime}}
.
The existence of such a
\delta(\gamma,\ell)
follows from Theorem
3.2
, item
1
and the assumption (
v
) in the definition of
{\mathscr{R}}
.

For
\ell\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
,
(\gamma,\ell)\notin\mathscr{L}_{0}
.
Since
\mathscr{L}_{0}
is closed in
\mathscr{S}\times\mathscr{L}
(Lemma
3.5
), there exists

\delta^{\prime}(\gamma,\ell)>0
such that for all
(\gamma^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\in\mathscr{S}\times\mathscr{L}
at
(\rho\times d_{\infty})
-distance less than or equal to
\delta^{\prime}(\gamma,\ell)
from
(\gamma,\ell)
, it holds
\ell^{\prime}\notin\mathscr{L}_{\gamma^{\prime}}
.

Let

\delta\coloneqq\epsilon\wedge\min\{\delta(\gamma,\ell)/2:\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\epsilon}\}\wedge\min\{\delta^{\prime}(\gamma,\ell):\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq\epsilon/2}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}\}>0.

Let
\phi
be any
\delta
-isomorphism from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in B_{\delta}(\mathcal{X})
. Since
\phi
induces an
\epsilon
-isomorphism, we only need to check the extra conditions that
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\epsilon})\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime}}
and
\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime},\geq\epsilon})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
. For all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\epsilon}
,

\rho(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})+d_{\infty}(\ell,\phi(\ell))\leq\delta+\delta/2\leq\delta(\gamma,\ell)
, thus
\phi(\ell)\in\mathscr{L}_{\gamma^{\prime}}
. This proves the first inclusion.
For the second inclusion, let
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime},\geq\epsilon}
. Since
\phi
induces an
\epsilon
-isomorphism,
\ell\coloneqq\phi^{-1}(\ell^{\prime})
is well-defined and
d_{\infty}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\leq\epsilon/2
. In particular
t_{\ell}\geq t_{\ell^{\prime}}-d_{\infty}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\geq\epsilon
, thus
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq\epsilon/2}
.
Let us assume by contradiction that
\ell\notin\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
. Then

(\rho\times d_{\infty})((\gamma,\ell),(\gamma^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}))\leq d^{\prime}_{\mathcal{R}_{0}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime})\leq\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}(\gamma,\ell),

and by construction of
\delta^{\prime}(\gamma,\ell)
it follows
\ell^{\prime}\notin\mathscr{L}_{\gamma^{\prime}}
, which is absurd.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(\gamma,\mathcal{L})\in{\mathscr{R}}
. For all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\eta=\eta(\mathcal{X})\in(0,\epsilon]
such that for all
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in B^{\prime}_{\eta}(\mathcal{X})
, all
\eta
-isomorphism
\phi
from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
induces an
\epsilon
-strongly suited isomorphism (with respect to
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
).

Proof.

Fix
\mathcal{X}\in{\mathscr{R}}
and
\epsilon>0
.
Let
(\ell_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,k\}}
be the increasing enumeration of the finite totally ordered set
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\epsilon}
. Define

\eta_{1}\coloneqq\min\{\sigma_{\ell_{i+1}}-\sigma_{\ell_{i}}:i\in\{0,\dots,k\}\}>0,

where formally
\sigma_{\ell_{0}}\coloneqq 0
and
\sigma_{\ell_{k+1}}\coloneqq t_{\gamma}
. Recall
(\gamma,\ell)\mapsto\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}
is continuous on
\mathscr{L}_{reg}
and
(\gamma,\ell)\in\mathscr{L}_{reg}
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
.
Thus, for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
, there exists
\eta_{\ell}>0
such that for all
(\gamma^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
with
(\rho\times d_{\infty})((\gamma^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}),(\gamma,\ell))<\eta_{\ell}
, it holds

|\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}-\sigma_{\gamma^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}|<\eta_{1}/2
.
Let
\eta_{2}\coloneqq\min\{\eta_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\epsilon}\}
, and
\eta=\eta_{2}\wedge\delta
, where
\delta>0
is obtained by applying Lemma
4.3
to
\mathcal{X}
and
\epsilon
.

Let
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in B^{\prime}_{\eta}(\mathcal{X})
, and
\phi
be an
\eta
-isomorphism from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
. Since
\eta\leq\delta
,
\phi
induces a
\delta
-isomorphism which is

\delta
-suited. Since
\delta<\epsilon
, the
\epsilon
-isomorphism induced by
\phi
is also
\epsilon
-suited.
Furthermore, for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\epsilon}
, it holds

(\rho\times d_{\infty})((\gamma^{\prime},\phi(\ell)),(\gamma,\ell))\leq d^{\prime}_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime})<\eta_{2}\leq\eta_{\ell},\hskip 18.49988pt\mbox{thus}\hskip 18.49988pt|\sigma_{\gamma,\ell}-\sigma_{\gamma^{\prime},\phi(\ell)}|\leq\eta_{1}/2.

Thus, for
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\epsilon}
, if
\ell_{1}\succ_{\gamma}\ell_{2}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\gamma^{\prime},\phi(\ell_{1})}-\sigma_{\gamma^{\prime},\phi(\ell_{2})}

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{\gamma,\ell_{1}}-\sigma_{\gamma,\ell_{2}}-|\sigma_{\gamma^{\prime},\phi(\ell_{1})}-\sigma_{\gamma,\ell_{1}}|-|\sigma_{\gamma^{\prime},\phi(\ell_{2})}-\sigma_{\gamma,\ell_{2}}|

\displaystyle>\eta-\eta/2-\eta/2=0.

Thus,
\sigma_{\gamma^{\prime},\phi(\ell_{1})}>\sigma_{\gamma^{\prime},\phi(\ell_{2})}
, which implies
\phi(\ell_{1})\succ_{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi(\ell_{2})
.
We have proved the implication

\ell_{1}\succ_{\gamma}\ell_{2}\implies\phi(\ell_{1})\succ_{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi(\ell_{2}).

(9)

By contraposition, the reciprocal implication is

\ell_{1}\succeq_{\gamma}\ell_{2}\implies\phi(\ell_{1})\succeq_{\gamma^{\prime}}\phi(\ell_{2}).

This is true because

\prec_{\gamma}
is a total strict order (recall
\mathcal{X}\in{\mathscr{R}}
). Indeed,

\ell_{1}\succeq_{\gamma}\ell_{2}\iff(\ell_{1}\succ_{\gamma}\ell_{2}\mbox{ or }\ell_{1}=\ell_{2})\implies(\phi(\ell_{1})\succ_{\gamma^{\prime}},\phi(\ell_{2})\mbox{ or }\phi(\ell_{1})=\phi(\ell_{2}))\implies\phi(\ell_{1})\succeq_{\gamma^{\prime}},\phi(\ell_{2}).

Thus, the
\epsilon
-suited isomorphism induced by
\phi
is
\epsilon
-strongly suited.
∎

Considering Lemma
4.4
, for any
\mathcal{X}\in{\mathscr{R}}
, we will let
\eta=\eta(\mathcal{X}):(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, be a function such that
\eta(\delta)<\delta
for all
\delta>0
, and any
\eta(\delta)
-isomorphism from
\mathcal{X}
is
\delta
-strongly suited.

\scriptstyle\diamond

.

Lemma 5.5

5.5
Attaching a random walk loop soup to a loop erased random walk

In this subsection, we prove Proposition
5.2
, i.e. that for
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, a loop erased random walk
\gamma
from
0
stopped when it exits
\Lambda
(by edges), a random walk loop soup
\mathbb{L}
, and a uniform choice of tie-break
B
, the curve
{\Xi}(\gamma,\mathbb{L},1,B)
is a simple random walk stopped when it exits
\Lambda
(by edges). Remark it is essentially an already known result; the key argument being
[
7
, Proposition 9.4.1]
.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Since we work with paths on the lattice parametrised at unit speed, we identify them with the list of their vertices, i.e. we identify a path
X
with
(X(0),\dots,X(t_{X}))
. For
a,b
neighbours in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, let
[a,b]
be the path with duration
1
given by
[a,b](0)=a
and
[a,b](1)=b
. For two paths
X,Y
such that
X(t_{X})=Y(0)
, we define the concatenation
X\oplus Y
by
t_{X\oplus Y}=t_{X}+t_{Y}
,
X\oplus Y(t)=X(t)
for
t\leq t_{X}
, and
X\oplus Y(t)=Y(t-t_{X})
for
t\geq t_{X}
.
For a lattice path
\eta
, recall
LE(\eta)
is its loop erasure, as defined e.g. in
[
7
]
. Let
S
be a simple random walk started from
0
and stopped when it first exits
\Lambda
.
Thus, we need to show that for any deterministic path
\eta
from
0
and stopped when it first exits
\Lambda
,

\mathbb{P}(S=\eta)=\mathbb{P}({\Xi}(\gamma,\mathbb{L},1,B)=\eta).

By definition of the loop-erased random walk,
\gamma
is equal in distribution to
LE(S)
. Thus, it suffices to prove that for any path
\eta
from
0
, stopped when it first exits
\Lambda
,

\mathbb{P}(S=\eta|LE(S)=\eta^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}({\Xi}(\gamma,\mathbb{L},1,B)=\eta|\gamma=\eta^{\prime}),\hskip 18.49988pt\eta^{\prime}\coloneqq LE(\eta),

i.e. that

\mathbb{P}(S=\eta|LE(S)=\eta^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}({\Xi}(\eta^{\prime},\mathbb{L},1,B)=\eta).

For
j\in\{0,\dots,t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1\}
, let
\omega_{j}
be the loops (with eventually
t_{\omega_{j}}=0
) uniquely defined by the property that

\omega_{i}
does not intersect
\eta_{[0,i-1]}
and

\eta=\omega_{0}\oplus[\eta^{\prime}_{0},\eta^{\prime}_{1}]\oplus\omega_{1}\oplus[\eta^{\prime}_{1},\eta^{\prime}_{2}]\oplus\dots\oplus\omega_{t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1}\oplus[\eta^{\prime}_{t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1},\eta^{\prime}_{t_{\eta^{\prime}}}].

Then,

\mathbb{P}(S=\eta)=4^{-t_{\eta}}=4^{-t_{\eta^{\prime}}}\prod_{j=0}^{t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1}4^{-t_{\omega_{j}}},

Since this holds for any
\eta
, it follows

\mathbb{P}(S=\eta|LE(S)=\eta^{\prime})=Z_{\eta^{\prime}}\prod_{j=0}^{t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1}4^{-t_{\omega_{j}}},

for some constant
Z_{\eta^{\prime}}
which depends only on
\eta^{\prime}
and
\Lambda
.

On the other hand, let
(L_{j})_{j\in\{0,\dots t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1\}}
be the loops uniquely defined by the property that

L_{i}
does not intersect
\eta^{\prime}_{[0,i-1]}
and

{\Xi}(\eta^{\prime},\mathbb{L},1,B)=L_{0}\oplus[\eta^{\prime}_{0},\eta^{\prime}_{1}]\oplus L_{1}\oplus[\eta^{\prime}_{1},\eta^{\prime}_{2}]\oplus\dots\oplus L_{k_{\eta^{\prime}}-1}\oplus[\eta^{\prime}_{t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1},\eta^{\prime}_{t_{\eta^{\prime}}}].

Thus,

\mathbb{P}({\Xi}(\eta^{\prime},\mathbb{L},1,B)=\eta)=\mathbb{P}(\forall j<t_{\eta^{\prime}},L_{j}=\omega_{j}).

By the Poisson property of
\mathbb{L}
and by
[
7
, Proposition 9.4.1]
,

\mathbb{P}(\forall j<t_{\eta^{\prime}},L_{j}=\omega_{j})=\prod_{j=0}^{t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1}\mathbb{P}(L_{j}=\omega_{j})=\prod_{j=0}^{t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1}Z_{j}4^{-t_{\omega_{j}}},

for some constants
Z_{j}
which depends only on
j
,
\eta^{\prime}
, and
\Lambda
.
We deduce for any given
\eta^{\prime}
, for all
\eta
such that
LE(\eta)=\eta^{\prime}
, the probabilities

\mathbb{P}(S=\eta|LE(S)=\eta^{\prime})
and
\mathbb{P}({\Xi}(\eta^{\prime},\mathbb{L},1,B)=\eta)
are proportional to each other. Since both are probabilities, summing over
\{\eta:LE(\eta)=\eta^{\prime}\}
, we deduce that the proportionality constants are equal, i.e. that
Z_{\eta^{\prime}}=\prod_{j=0}^{t_{\eta^{\prime}}-1}Z_{j}
. Thus,

\mathbb{P}(S=\eta|LE(S)=\eta^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}({\Xi}(\eta^{\prime},\mathbb{L},1,B)=\eta).\qed

\mathbb{L}^{nD}
are all defined. Let
\epsilon>0
. Since
\mathcal{X}\in{\mathscr{R}}
, by Lemma

, there exists

(random) such that

, with the notations from Lemma

and where

f_{\mathcal{L}}
is defined by (
22
). Remark the fact

is continuous and vanishes at

comes from the equicontinuity of

(Lemma

).

For
\delta^{\prime}>0
, consider

F_{\delta^{\prime},n}\coloneqq\{\rho(\gamma^{nD},\gamma)\leq\delta^{\prime}/2\}.

By construction

converges in probability toward

. Thus, for any deterministic
\delta^{\prime}>0
, there exists

such that for all

\mathbb{P}(F_{\delta^{\prime},n})\geq 1-\epsilon/4
. By Proposition

, there exists

such that for all

On

d^{\prime}_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}(\mathcal{X}^{n},\mathcal{X})\leq\delta^{\prime}\hskip 18.49988pt\mbox{(recall $d^{\prime}_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}$ from~\eqref{eq:defdprime}) }

On

By Theorem
6.15
,

6.2
Estimation on the total time spent on small loops

The goal of this subsection is to establish Theorem
6.15
, which roughly states that the random walk does not spend a macroscopic amount of time on microscopic loops, and that we can quantify this uniformly over the mesh size. This is obtained by estimating the size of the neighbourhoods of the loop-erased random walk
\gamma^{nD}
: in the continuum, we know the size of the
\delta
-neighbourhood of
\gamma
is of order
\delta^{3/4+o(1)}
, from which we may expect the cardinal of the
k
-neighborhood of
S
on
nD
should be of order
n^{2}(k/n)^{3/4+o(1)}
, for
k<n
. We only prove it is of order
O(n^{2}(k/n)^{\epsilon})
(Corollary
6.11
), which is sufficient for us to conclude. We prove this by estimating the probability that a given point
x
lies in the vicinity of
\gamma^{nD}
. A first estimation is provided in Lemma
6.9
, and gives a better estimation if we restrict to points whose distance to
\partial D
is bounded below. This is sufficient to deal with domains whose boundary satisfies a very mild regularity condition, but we will remove this additional requirement using Lemma
6.8
: when
x
is close to
\partial D
, it is unlikely to lie
the vicinity of the (rescaled) loop-erased random walk, because it is already unlikely to lie in the vicinity of the (rescaled) random walk, since the random walk is likely to be killed at
\partial D
before it goes close to
x
. We start with two technical estimations concerning the simple random walk: a lower bound on the probability that it winds once around
x
without ever approaching it (Lemma
6.6
), and an upper bound on the number of upcrossing events (Lemma
6.7
). We will use these respectively in
Lemma
6.8
and Lemma
6.9
.

For
r>0
let
B_{r}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:|x|<r\}
, and let
\partial B^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}_{r}=\{x\in B^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}_{r}:\exists y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus B^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}_{r}:|x-y|=1
.
For a simple random walk
S
on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(not necessarily started from

), let

be the first entry time of
S
inside

. Let also
\zeta_{0}(S)
be the first time

hits

.
For

such that

, we say

is non-contractible if the continuous loop obtain by piecewise-linear interpolation of
S_{[s,t]}
is non-contractible on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.

Lemma 6.6
.

For

\begin{array}[]{c}\zeta_{3/4}(W)\leq 1\text{ and there exists $s\leq t\leq\zeta_{3/4}(W)$ such that $W_{[s,t]}$ is non-contractible on $\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}$, }\\

. On the event

On the other hand,
for all

.

In the following, for

either discrete or continuous, and for

\tau_{i}=\tau_{i}(a,b,W)

\tau_{0}=\sigma_{0}=0,\tau_{i+1}\coloneqq\inf\{j\geq\sigma_{i}:|W(j)|\leq a\},\sigma_{i+1}\coloneqq\inf\{j\geq\tau_{i+1}:|W(j)|\geq b\},

and we then define the number of

-upcrossing of

For

.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
S
be a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Then, there exists
C,C^{\prime}
such that for all
k,n\geq 2
with
C\log(n)\leq k\leq n/2
, for all

,

\mathbb{E}_{x}[N_{k,2k,\xi_{n}}(S)]\leq C^{\prime}\log_{2}(n/k).

Proof.

This can be proved either using a strong (KMT) coupling or directly. For instance, by Lemma 6.6.7 in
[
7
]
, there exists
c>0
such that if
\rho_{k}=\inf\{j\geq 0:|W(j)|\leq k\}
then we have

{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\xi_{n}<\rho_{k})\geq p_{k,n}:=c(\log(k/n))^{-1}

provided that

, uniformly over points

which are adjacent to

. Applying the strong Markov property iteratively, we deduce that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[N_{k,2k,\xi_{n}}(S)\geq m]\leq C(1-p_{k,n})^{m},

∎

The next lemma shows that a simple random walk is polynomially unlikely to visit a point close to the boundary (thus this applies also to a loop-erased random walk).

Lemma 6.8
.

There exist
C<\infty
and
q>0
such that for all positive integers
n,k
, for all
x\in nD
,

\mathbb{P}(d(x,S)\leq k)\leq Cd(x,0)^{-q}\max(d(x,n\partial D),k)^{q},

where
S
is the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
started from
0
and stopped when it exits
nD
.

Proof.

Up to replacing
C
with
\max(C,1)
, we can assume
d(x,n\partial D)<d(x,0)
and
k<d(x,0)
. We treat separately the cases
k<d(x,n\partial D)
and
k\geq d(x,n\partial D)
.

Let

j_{0}\coloneqq\lfloor\ln_{2}(d(x,0)/d(x,\partial D))\rfloor,

i.e. the maximal integer such that
2^{j_{0}}d(x,\partial D)\leq d(x,0)
, which is non-negative. For
j\in\{1,\dots,j_{0}-1\}
, consider the concentric disks

\mathbb{D}_{j}\coloneqq\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:|y-x|\in[2^{j_{0}-j-1}d(x,n\partial D),2^{j_{0}-j}d(x,n\partial D))\},

and let
\tau^{\prime}_{j}
the first time when
S
enters
\mathbb{D}_{j}
, which form an increasing sequence of stopping times.

Consider the event
E_{j}\subset\{\tau^{\prime}_{j}<\infty\}
that there exists
s,t
with
\tau^{\prime}_{j}\leq s<t\leq\tau^{\prime}_{j+1}
such that
S_{[s,t]}
is non-contractible around
x
. By Lemma
6.6
, the probability that this event happens is bounded below by a positive
p
which depends on no parameter. Furthermore, for this event to happen,
S
must exits
nD
: let us proceed by contradiction and assume the opposite (the reader may convince themselves by drawing that we can always find a path which remains outside of
D
and connects the two boundary components of
\mathbb{D}_{j}
). Let
D^{\prime}
be the complementary of the unbounded path-connected component delimited by
S_{[s,t]}
. Since
nD
is simply connected and contains
S_{[s,t]}
,
D^{\prime}\subset nD
. Since
S_{[s,t]}
is non-contractible on
\mathbb{D}_{j}
,
D^{\prime}
contains the closed ball
\bar{B}
centred at
x
with radius
r=2^{j_{0}-j-1}d(x,n\partial D)\geq d(x,n\partial D)
. By compactness, there exists
z\in n\partial D
such that

. In particular
z\notin nD
, but
z\in\bar{B}\subseteq D^{\prime}\subset nD
, hence the contradiction.

Thus, for
k<d(x,n\partial D)
, setting
q=\ln_{2}(p^{-1})
and
C=p^{-2}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(d(x,S)\leq k\big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big(d(x,S)\leq d(x,n\partial D)\big)\leq\mathbb{P}\big(\forall j\in\{1,\dots,j_{0}-1\},(E_{j})^{c}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{j_{0}-1}\mathbb{P}\big((E_{j})^{c}\big|\tau^{\prime}_{j}<\infty\big)\leq p^{j_{0}-1}\leq Cd(x,n\partial D)^{q}d(x,0)^{-q}.

In the case
k\geq d(x,n\partial D)
, consider instead

j_{1}\coloneqq\lfloor\ln_{2}(d(x,0)/k)\rfloor
.
Since
k<d(x,0)
and
k>d(x,n\partial D)
, it holds
j_{0}\in\{0,\dots,j_{1}\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(d(x,S)\leq k\big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big(\forall j\in\{1,\dots,j_{1}-1\},(E_{j})^{c}\big)\leq\prod_{j=1}^{j_{1}-1}\mathbb{P}\big((E_{j})^{c}\big|\tau_{j}<\infty\big)

\displaystyle\leq p^{j_{1}-1}\leq Ck^{q}d(x,0)^{-q},

which concludes the proof.
∎

We now show that a loop-erased random walk is polynomially unlikely to visit a point in the interior of a domain. Here, bounding crudely the range of the LERW by the range of the walk is not sufficient (as the random walk visits all but a
logarithmic
fraction of points). Instead, we must rely on the fact that random walks create loops in annuli at all scales which erase previous portions of the path. The result below could be obtained by applying Proposition 4.11 in
[
2
]
, but we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 6.9
.

There exist
C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime}<\infty
such that for any positive integers
k,n\geq 2
with
C^{\prime}\log(n)\leq k\leq n/2
, for all

\mathbb{P}(d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k)\leq C^{\prime\prime}\log_{2}(n/k)\,k^{\frac{1}{2}}\,d(x,n\partial D\cup\{0\})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Up to replacing
C^{\prime\prime}
with
\max(C^{\prime\prime},2^{3/2})
, we can assume
4k+4<d(x,n\partial D\cup\{0\})
.

Let
S
be a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
started from
0
,
\tau
be its first exit time from
nD
, and let
\gamma
be the loop-erasure of

, which is distributed as
\gamma^{nD}
.

On the event
\{d(x,\gamma)\leq k\}
, there exists at least one couple
(j_{0},s)
such that
j_{0}\leq\tau
,
S_{j_{0}}=\gamma_{s}
, and
|S_{j_{0}}-x|\leq k
(take e.g.
s
such that
d(x,\gamma)=d(x,\gamma_{s})
). Among the possible choices for
j_{0}
, let us choose the maximal one.

We claim that
S_{[j_{0}+1,\tau]}\cap LE(S_{[0,j_{0}]})=\emptyset
.
We set
s^{\prime\prime}
the length of
LE(S_{[0,j_{0}]})

and we proceed by contradiction, assuming there exists
j_{1}\in[j_{0}+1,\tau]
, and
s^{\prime}\in[0,s^{\prime\prime}]
such that
S_{j_{1}}=LE(S_{[0,j_{0}]})_{s^{\prime}}
, and let
j_{1}
be minimal with this property.
Then,

LE(S_{[0,j_{0}]})_{s^{\prime}}=S(j_{1})\neq S_{j_{0}}=LE(S_{[0,j_{0}]})_{s^{\prime\prime}}

The first equality is by definition of
(j_{1},s^{\prime})
, the inequality is by maximality of

(recall
j_{1}>j_{0}
), and the last equality is due to the fact that loop-erasing a curve preserves its endpoints.
In particular,
s^{\prime}\neq s^{\prime\prime}
, hence
s^{\prime}<s^{\prime\prime}
. Since

LE(S_{[0,j_{0}]})
is injective and

, we deduce that
\gamma_{s}\notin LE(S_{[0,j_{0}]})_{[0,s^{\prime}]}=LE(S_{[0,j_{1}]})
. Furthermore, the maximality of

. Recall loop-erasure is chronological: for arbitrary

such that

is well-defined,

.
Thus,

\displaystyle\gamma_{s}\notin LE(S_{[0,j_{1}]})\cup S_{[j_{1},\tau]}=\Range(LE(S_{[0,j_{1}]})\otimes S_{[j_{1},\tau]})

\displaystyle\supseteq\Range(LE(LE(S_{[0,j_{1}]})\otimes S_{[j_{1},\tau]}))

\displaystyle=\Range(LE(S_{[0,\tau]}))=\Range(\gamma),

hence the contradiction.

Since

\displaystyle j_{0}\leq\tau

(24)

\mathbb{P}(d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k)\leq C^{\prime\prime}\log_{2}(n/k)k^{\frac{1}{2}}d(x,n\partial D)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(25)

Let

\scriptstyle\diamond

Case

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k)\leq\mathbb{P}(d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k)^{2\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime 2\nu}\log(n/k)^{2\nu}k^{\nu}d(x,n\partial D)^{-\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime 2\nu}\max(1,\log(n/k))k^{\nu}d(x,0)^{-\nu}.

\scriptstyle\diamond

Case

\mathbb{P}(d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k)\leq 1\leq d(x,0)^{-\nu}k^{\nu}.

\scriptstyle\diamond

Case
d(x,n\partial D)\leq k\leq d(x,0)

\scriptstyle\diamond

Case

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k)^{1-\theta}\mathbb{P}(d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k)^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq C^{1-\theta}C^{\prime\prime\theta}\log_{2}(n/k)^{\theta}k^{\theta/2}d(x,0)^{-(1-\theta)q}d(x,n\partial D)^{-\theta/2}d(x,\partial D)^{(1-\theta)q}

\displaystyle=C^{1-\theta}C^{\prime\prime\theta}\log_{2}(n/k)^{\theta}k^{\nu}d(x,0)^{\nu}<C^{1-\theta}C^{\prime\prime\theta}\log_{2}(n/k)k^{\nu}d(x,0)^{\nu}.

We conclude by taking

∎

Corollary 6.11
.

There exists
C^{\prime},C_{1}<\infty
and
\nu>0
such that for all positive integers

\mathbb{E}[\#\{x\in nD:d(x,\gamma^{nD})\leq k\}]\leq C_{1}k^{\nu}n^{2-\nu}.

Proof.

Eventually replacing

, we can assume

. Let

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in nD\setminus\{0\}}d(x,0)^{-\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in nD\setminus\{0\}}\int_{x+[-1/2,1/2]^{2}}(|z|-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{-\nu}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in nD\setminus\{0\}}\int_{x+[-1/2,1/2]^{2}}(\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2})^{-\nu}|z|^{-\nu}\mathrm{d}z\hskip 9.24994pt\text{( $r-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\geq(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})r$ for $r\geq 1$)}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{-\nu}\int_{[-nK+1/2,nK+1/2]^{2}}|z|^{-\nu}\mathrm{d}z

Let

Lemma 6.14

There exists a universal constant

Theorem 6.15
.

As
n\to\infty
,
t_{\mathcal{X}^{n}}
converges to
t_{\mathcal{X}}
in probability.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{X}=(\gamma,\mathcal{L})
, where
\gamma
is a radial SLE
2
curve. Thus
\gamma
has almost surely finite
d
-dimensional Minkowski content for some
d<2
(in fact one may take
d=5/4

[
8
]

, when we take the expectation
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
over only the loop soup,

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[t_{\mathcal{X}}]<\infty
.

Consider the event
F_{k}
such that
t_{\mathcal{X}}\leq k
. We first note since
t_{\mathcal{X}}<\infty
almost surely, we have
\mathbb{P}(F_{k})\to 1
as
k\to\infty
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Fix
k
such that
\mathbb{P}(F_{k})\geq 1-\epsilon/2
. Let
f(\epsilon)=\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{F_{k}}T_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{X})]
.
Since
t_{\mathcal{X}}<\infty
a.s., we have
T_{\leq\epsilon}(\mathcal{X})\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
, almost surely and hence by dominated convergence,

f(\epsilon)\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
as well. Let
\delta>0
such that
C\delta^{\frac{\nu}{2}}+f(\delta)<\epsilon^{2}/2
, for

26

Let
\eta\in(0,\delta)

I_{\eta,n}
denote the event that there is an
\eta
-isomorphism from
\mathcal{X}
which is
\delta

4.3
). We have
\limsup_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(I_{\eta,n}^{c})=0
by combining Lemma
4.3
and Proposition
6.4
. In particular, we can choose
\eta>0
sufficiently small (in particular
\eta<\delta
) such that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(I_{\eta,n}^{c})\leq\epsilon/2

For all

\delta\geq\epsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|t_{\mathcal{X}^{n}}-t_{\mathcal{X}}|\mathbbm{1}_{I_{\eta,n}\cap F_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[T_{\leq\delta}(\mathcal{X}_{n})|]+\mathbb{E}[T_{\leq\delta}(\mathcal{X})|1_{F_{k}}]+\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\geq\delta}}|t_{\ell}-t_{\phi\circ\psi(\ell)}|]

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|t_{\mathcal{X}^{n}}-t_{\mathcal{X}}|\geq\epsilon)

7
Open problems

\scriptstyle\diamond

(
Measurability of
\gamma
.
) Naturally, our proof does not give any kind of uniqueness, and in particular, does not answer the question of whether one can “erase the loops of planar Brownian motion” and obtain an SLE
2
path. More precisely, one can ask the following question: is it possible to add a loop soup
\mathcal{L}
to an SLE
2
path
\gamma
to obtain a Brownian motion
W
such that
\gamma
(and also
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
) are a measurable function of
W
in this coupling?

\scriptstyle\diamond

(
Almost sure (non-)injectivity.
) A natural starting point for the above would be to check if the map
{\Xi}
is injective, In fact, one can show that this is not the case. This leaves open the following question: does
{\Xi}
almost surely has a unique pre-image? We believe this is also not the case, more precisely, almost surely there are infinitely many pre-images.

\scriptstyle\diamond

(
Dimension 3.
) It would be particularly interesting to prove a similar result in three dimensions. In this case, the (deterministic) continuity of the attachment map
{\Xi}
fails under the provided conditions, because the set of pairs
(\gamma,\ell)
such that
\gamma
intersects
\ell
is no longer open (a small deformation can make
\ell
detach from
\gamma
). However, it is natural to believe that for the scaling limit
\gamma
of LERW, there is a kind of almost sure continuity:
“typical” small perturbations should continue to intersect
\gamma
. Ideas of this type were in fact already used
in the proof of
[
16
]
.

\scriptstyle\diamond

(
SLE
2
loop analogue.
) Is there a way of describing a unit Brownian loop as a version of an SLE
2
loop, decorated by an independent Brownian loop soup?

\scriptstyle\diamond

(
Other values of
\kappa
.) Our construction shows that we can attach a loop soup of arbitrary intensity
\lambda
to a chordal SLE
κ
for other values of
\kappa
, to produce a continuous path
W^{\kappa,\lambda}
. It is also known that when the intensity of the loop soup is given by

\lambda=\lambda(\kappa)=\frac{(8-3\kappa)(6-\kappa)}{2\kappa}

then the resulting hull (i.e. after filling in the holes) gives a restriction measure of exponent
\alpha=(6-\kappa)/(2\kappa)
. But what is the law of the resulting continuous trajectory
W^{\kappa,\lambda}
in that case? Does it correspond to a Brownian motion with
“partially erased” loops when
\kappa\geq 2
?

\scriptstyle\diamond

(
Continuity in rough path topologies.
) With a slight adaptation of Proposition
2.4
, we can deduce the
p
-variation estimate

\|X\|_{p-var}\leq C_{p}(\|\gamma\|_{p-var}+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}}\|\ell\|_{p-var})

for an explicit constant
C_{p}
.
Can we prove some continuity of
{\Xi}
for
\alpha
-Hölder or
p
-variation distances? What about rough path topologies? It may be the case that continuity in these topologies holds, but
only along cusps on
{\mathscr{R}}^{\prime}
, i.e. for a given
\mathcal{X}\in{\mathscr{R}}
,
consider only the
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\lambda)
where
\lambda\geq Cd_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime})^{\beta}
, for a well-chosen
\beta
and fixed
C
, and with
d_{{\mathscr{R}}_{0}}
adapted to the appropriate norms.

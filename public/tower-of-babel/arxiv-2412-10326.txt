k k -loose elements and k k -paving matroids
Jagdeep Singh

Abstract
. For a matroid of rank r r and a non-negative integer k k , an element is called k k -loose if every circuit containing it has size greater than r − k r-k . Zaslavsky and the author characterized all binary matroids with a 1 1 -loose element. In this paper, we establish a sharp linear bound on the size of a binary matroid, in terms of its rank, that contains a k k -loose element. A matroid is called k k -paving if all its elements are k k -loose. Rajpal showed that for a prime power q q , the rank of a G ​ F ​ ( q ) GF(q) -matroid that is k k -paving is bounded. We provide a bound on the rank of G ​ F ​ ( q ) GF(q) -matroids that are cosimple and have two k k -loose elements. Consequently, we strengthen the result of Rajpal by providing a bound on the rank of G ​ F ​ ( q ) GF(q) -matroids that are k k -paving. Additionally, we provide a bound on the size of binary matroids that are k k -paving.

2.
k
-loose elements in binary matroids

Theorem
1.1
provides a sharp linear bound on the size of a binary matroid containing a
k
-loose element, in terms of its rank. Specifically, if a binary matroid

has a
1
-loose element, then the size of

is at most
2r
. If
M
has a
2
-loose element, then the size of
M
does not exceed
4r-4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
e
be a
k
-loose element of
M
. Note that if
k=0
, then by
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
,
M
is a circuit, and the result follows. Therefore
k\geq 1
. Suppose that
e
is not
(k-1)
-loose. It follows that there is a basis
B=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
of
M
such that, in the standard binary representation
P=[I_{r}|Q]
of
M
with respect to
B
, the column
e^{P}
labeled by
e
has its first
k
entries equal to zero, while all other entries are non-zero. We call the set of indices of the non-zero entries of a column
f^{P}
in
Q
the
support
of
f^{P}
, denoted as
\text{supp}(f^{P})
. The entries of
f^{P}
, excluding the first
k
entries, are called the
root entries
of
f^{P}
, and the set of indices of the non-zero root entries of
f^{P}
is the
root support
of
f^{P}
. Let
Q-e^{P}
denote the columns in
Q
excluding
e^{P}
.

2.0.1
.

For a column
f^{P}
in
Q-e^{P}
, the sum of the number of zeroes in the first
k
entries of
f^{P}
and the number of non-zero root entries of
f^{P}
is at most
k+1
.

Suppose otherwise. Then
e^{P}+f^{P}
has at least
k+2
zeroes, so at most
r-(k+2)
columns from
I_{r}
can be added to
e^{P}+f^{P}
to obtain a column with all entries equal to zero. It follows that
e
is in a circuit of size at most
r-k
, a contradiction. Therefore
2.0.1
holds.

Let
F
be a set of all columns in
Q-e^{P}
such that, for any pair
f^{P},g^{P}
in
F
, the sum
f^{P}+g^{P}
has its first
k
entries equal to zero. It is clear that the first
k
entries of all columns in
F
are identical.

2.0.2
.

Let
f^{P},g^{P}
be columns in
F
. Then, the symmetric difference of their supports,
supp(f^{P})\bigtriangleup supp(g^{P})
, contains at most two elements.

Suppose not. Then
f^{P}+g^{P}
has its first
k
entries equal to zero and contains at least three non-zero entries. It follows that
e^{P}+f^{P}+g^{P}
has at least
k+3
zeroes so it can be combined linearly over GF(2) with at most
r-(k+3)
columns in
I_{r}
to obtain the column that has all the entries zero. Therefore
e
is in a circuit of size at most
r-k
, a contradiction. Thus
2.0.2
holds.

2.0.3
.

|F|\leq r-k+1
. Moreover, if the columns in
F
have exactly one non-zero entry in the first
k
entries, then
|F|\leq r-k
.

Choose a column
f^{P}
in
F
such that the root support of
f^{P}
, say
S
, has the largest possible size. Suppose
|S|=s
. It follows by
2.0.1
that
s\leq k+1
. In view of
2.0.2
, a column
g^{P}
in
F-f^{P}
can fall into one of the following three categories, namely,

(i)

\text{supp}(g^{P})\subseteq\text{supp}(f^{P})
with
|\text{supp}(f^{P})-\text{supp}(g^{P})|=1
,

(ii)

\text{supp}(g^{P})\subseteq\text{supp}(f^{P})
with
|\text{supp}(f^{P})-\text{supp}(g^{P})|=2
, or

(iii)

\text{supp}(g^{P})\not\subseteq\text{supp}(f^{P})
.

We call these columns type A, type B, and type C, respectively.
By
2.0.2
, it follows that the number of type B columns is at most
\text{max}\{3,s-1\}
. Note that if
s\leq 1
, there is no type B column. If
s=2
, there is at most one type B column. It follows that the number of type B columns does not exceed
s
.

First, suppose that
F
contains a type C column. We say an entry of a column
g^{P}
is in
S
if the index of the entry
is in
S
. Similarly, an entry of
g^{P}
is
outside
S
if its index is not in
S
. Since the size of the root support of any column in
F
is at most
s
, it follows from
2.0.2
that a type C column has precisely one non-zero root entry outside
S
and exactly one zero entry in
S
. Observe that if
F
contains two type C columns such that their corresponding entries in
S
are not identical, then by
2.0.2
, all their corresponding entries outside
S
must be identical. In this case, the number of type C columns is at most
s
. On the other hand, if all type C columns in
F
have identical entries in
S
, then the number of type C columns is at most
r-(s+k)
. Therefore, the number of columns of type C in
F
is at most
\text{max}\{s,r-(s+k)\}
. Given that
s\leq k+1
and
k\leq\frac{r-2}{3}
, it follows that the number of type C columns in
F
is at most
r-(s+k)
. Since
F
contains a type C column, it is immediate from
2.0.2
that there is at most one type A column in
F
. Furthermore, if a type A column exists, then we have at most
s-1
type B columns. It follows that the sum of the number of type A columns and the number of type B columns does not exceed
s
. Therefore the size of
F
is at most
r-(s+k)+s+1=r-k+1
.

Now, suppose that no type C column exists. By
2.0.2
, it follows that if a type B column exists, then the number of type A columns is at most
2
so
F
has size at most
s+3
. Since
s\leq k+1,r\geq 5
, and
k\leq\frac{r-2}{3}
, it follows that
s+3\leq r-k+1
. Finally note that the number of type A columns is at most
s
so
|F|\leq r-k+1
.

If the columns in
F
have exactly one non-zero entry in the first
k
entries, then by
2.0.1
,
s
is at most two. If
s=1
, then all the columns in
F-f^{P}
are type C; if a column is of type A, then it is parallel to some element in
I_{r}
, a contradiction since
M
is simple. Since the number of type C columns is at most
r-(s+k)
, it follows that
|F|
is at most
r-k
. Now suppose that
s=2
. Note that all columns in
F-f^{P}
are either type A or type C; a type B column contradicts that
M
is simple. It is clear that the number of type A columns is at most two and the number of type C columns is
r-(2+k)
. However, if
F
contains a type C column, then, by
2.0.2
, the number of type A columns is at most one. Therefore
|F|
is at most
\text{max}\{3,r-k\}
. Since
k\leq\frac{r-2}{3}
, it follows that
|F|\leq r-k
. Thus
2.0.3
holds.

Observe that if there is a column
h^{P}
in
Q-e^{P}
that has its first
k
entries equal to zero, then by
2.0.1
, the column
h^{P}
has at most one non-zero root entry so
h^{P}
has at most one non-zero entry. Since
M
is simple, this is a contradiction. It follows that there are at most
2^{k}-1
sets
F
in
Q-e^{P}
, and
k
of these have exactly one non-zero entry in the first
k
entries. Therefore, by
2.0.3
,
|E(M)|\leq k(r-k)+(2^{k}-1-k)(r-k+1)+(r+1)=2^{k}(r-k+1)
.

If
e
is
(k-1)
-loose, we choose the smallest non-negative integer
t
such that
e
is
t
-loose. By the same argument we obtain
|E(M)|\leq 2^{t}(r-t+1)
. Since
t\leq k-1,r\geq 5
, and
k\leq\frac{r-2}{3}
, it follows that
2^{t}(r-t+1)\leq 2^{k}(r-k+1)
.
∎

The following proposition demonstrates that the bound in Theorem

is sharp.

Proposition 2.1
.

For

and
0\leq k\leq r-2
, there exists a simple binary matroid of rank

with a

-loose element, whose size is

.

Proof.

Let

be a binary matroid represented by the matrix
P=[I_{r}|Q]
over

such that

contains a column

whose first
k
entries are zero while the remaining entries are non-zero. We now describe the other columns of

, demonstrate that

has size

, and show that the element corresponding to the column

is

-loose in

.

For a column

in

, we refer to the column obtained by removing the first

entries of

as the

of

. The columns in

can be partitioned into disjoint sets

, where every column in

has identical first

entries. No column in

has all of its first

entries equal to zero, so we have

.

For the sets

where each column in

has exactly one non-zero entry in its first

entries, the root vectors of the columns in

are the columns of the identity matrix of size

, so each such set

has size

. For the remaining

sets

, the root vectors of the columns in

are the columns of the identity matrix of size

, along with the zero column, which has all of its entries equal to zero. The size of each of these sets

is

. It follows that the size of

is
2^{k}(r-k+1)
.

Note that there is no zero column in
P
. Moreover, since
r\geq 2
and

, all of the columns in

are distinct, so

is simple. Observe that if we add fewer than

columns to

, then we cannot obtain the zero column so all circuits containing

in

have size greater than

. Therefore

is

-loose in

.
∎

3.
k
-loose elements in
GF(q)
-matroids

Zaslavsky and the author in
[
6
]
showed that a ternary matroid with no coloops that contains a

-loose element has linear size relative to its rank. In particular they proved the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

For

, let

be a simple ternary matroid of rank

|E(M)|\leq\lfloor\frac{41r-101}{2}\rfloor

. If

, then

.

Furthermore, they proved that if a cosimple ternary matroid contains two
1
-loose elements, then the rank of the matroid is bounded. This result was shown to hold for all
GF(q)
-matroids.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
M
be a simple
GF(q)
-matroid with no coloops that has two
1
-loose elements

and

. Then
r(M)\leq 2q
, or
\{e,f\}
is a cocircuit of
M
.

We extend Theorem
3.2
to

-loose elements and prove Theorem
1.2
. The following is the restatement of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
.

Let
M
be a simple
GF(q)
-matroid with no coloops that has two
k
-loose elements

and
f
. Then
r(M)\leq(q+1)(k-1)+2q
, or
\{e,f\}
is a cocircuit of
M
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\{e,f\}
is not a cocircuit of
M
. Therefore, there is a basis
B
of
M
that is disjoint from
\{e,f\}
. Note that, in the standard
GF(q)
-representation
P=[I_{r}|Q]
of
M
with respect to
B
, the column
e^{P}
corresponding to

and the column
f^{P}
corresponding to

have at most
k

e^{P}
has all ones except that the first
k
entries may be zero.

Let
f^{P^{\prime}}
be the column obtained by removing the first
k
entries of
f^{P}
. Suppose

has

non-zero entries that are all equal. Then

, and at most

columns from

can be combined linearly over

to obtain a column that has all its entries equal to zero. This would create a circuit of

that contains

, and has size at most

, a contradiction. Therefore,
f^{P^{\prime}}
has at most
k+1
non-zero entries that are equal. Since the field

has

f^{P^{\prime}}
has at most
(k+1)(q-1)
non-zero entries. Additionally,

has at most

has at most

is at most
(q+1)(k-1)+2q
.
∎

Let

be a simple and cosimple

-matroid that has two

4.

-paving binary matroids

Theorem
1.1
bounds the size of a binary matroid with a
k
-loose element and rank at least
3k+2
.
By Corollary
3.4
, the rank of binary

-paving matroids is at most

. Theorem
1.5
bounds the size of these matroids. The following is its restatement.

Theorem
.

Let
M
be a simple binary
k
-paving matroid of rank
r
, where
M
is not a circuit and
r\geq k+4
, and let
t=\lfloor\frac{3k+1-r}{2}\rfloor
. Then
r(M)\leq 3k+1
, and

|E(M)|\leq(r+1)+\sum_{i=0}^{t}\binom{k}{i}.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.4
, it is evident that
r(M)\leq 3k+1
. Suppose that
M
has a circuit
C
of size
r-k+1
. For an element
e
of
C
, let
B
be a basis of

containing

. Consider the standard binary representation
P=[I_{r}|Q]
of
M
with respect to the basis

.

4.0.1
.

Let
h
be the sum of
m
columns in
Q
. Then
h
has at most

zeroes. In particular, for any two columns

and

in

has at most

Assume that

has at least

entries that are zero. Then, at most

columns in

can be added to

to obtain the column with all entries equal to zero. This would result in a circuit of size at most

in

, a contradiction. Therefore

holds.

Note that the column

in
Q
corresponding to the element

of

has exactly
k
zeroes. By reordering rows and columns of

, we may assume that the first
k
entries of
e^{P}
are zero. We denote the set of indices of the zero entries of a column
f^{P}
in
Q
by
\text{zeroes}(f^{P})
. The entries of
f^{P}
excluding the first
k
entries are the
root entries
of
f^{P}
. Let
g^{P}
be a column in
Q-e^{P}
such that
|\text{zeroes}(e^{P})\cap\text{zeroes}(g^{P})|=l
is maximum.

4.0.2
.

r\leq 3k+1-2l.

Observe that
g^{P}
has at most
k
entries that are zero; otherwise,
M
has a circuit of size less than

. By
4.0.1
, it follows that
g^{P}
has at most
k-l
root entries that are zero. Furthermore, by
4.0.1
, at most

are equal to one. Therefore,

has at most

holds.

Let
F
be a set of all columns in

such that, for any pair
f^{P},g^{P}
in
F
, the sum
f^{P}+g^{P}
has its first
k
entries equal to zero. Evidently, the first

entries of all the columns in

are identical.

4.0.3
.

|F|\leq 1.

Suppose not, and let

and

be two columns in

. Note that the first

entries of

are all zero, so by

, at most one root entry of

is zero. It follows that

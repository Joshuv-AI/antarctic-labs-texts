Introduction

For given graphs
H
and
G
, and a positive integer
r
, we say that
G
is
Ramsey for

H
, denoted by
G\longrightarrow(H)_{r}
, if for every
r
-edge coloring of
G
with
r
colors, there exists a monochromatic subgraph of
G
isomorphic to
H
.
Ramsey’s pioneering work
[
20
]
established that for any graph

H
and any positive integer
r
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such
that
K_{N}\longrightarrow(H)_{r}
. The smallest such
N
is called the

r
-multicolor Ramsey number
of
H
and is denoted by
R_{r}(H)
.

While this perspective focuses on minimizing the number of vertices,
an alternative viewpoint—initiated by Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau,
and Schelp
[
7
]
—seeks to minimize the number of edges. This
leads to the notion of
size-Ramsey numbers
. For a graph
H
and
integer
r\geq 2
, the
r
-multicolor size-Ramsey number

of
H
is

\widehat{R}_{r}(H)=\min\{|E(G)|:G\longrightarrow(H)_{r}\}.

One of the central problems in this area is to determine for which
families of graphs the size-Ramsey number grows linearly with the
number of vertices. Erdős
[
6
]
asked this question in the
case of paths and this question was addressed by Beck
[
1
]
,
who proved that
\widehat{R}_{2}(P_{n})<900n
. The linearity of size-Ramsey
numbers has since been established for various graph families:
cycles
[
3
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
, bounded-degree
trees
[
8
,
10
,
17
]
, powers of paths and
cycles
[
4
,
9
]
, bounded powers of bounded-degree trees
(equivalently graphs of bounded treewidth and maximum
degree)
[
2
,
16
,
15
]
, and logarithmic subdivisions of bounded-degree graphs
[
5
]
. On the negative side, Rödl and Szemerédi
[
21
]
constructed graphs on
n
vertices with maximum degree
3
and size-Ramsey number at least
n\log^{c}n
, for a small constant
c>0
. This bound has been
improved to
cne^{c\sqrt{\log n}}
for some
c>0
by Tikhomirov
[
22
]
. Thus
the linearity of size-Ramsey numbers does not extend universally to bounded degree graphs.

Given a graph
H
and a function
\sigma:E(H)\to\mathbb{N}
, the
subdivision

H^{\sigma}
is obtained by replacing each edge
e\in E(H)
with a path of length
\sigma(e)
.
Answering a conjecture of
Pak
[
19
]
, Draganić, Krivelevich, and Nenadov
[
5
]
proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
5
]

For every
r,\,D\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
C,\,L>0
such that if
H

is a graph with
\Delta(H)\leq D
and
\sigma(e)\geq L\log(|V(H^{\sigma})|)
for all
e\in E(H)
, then

\widehat{R}_{r}(H^{\sigma})\leq C|V(H^{\sigma})|
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on Szemerédi’s regularity lemma and makes no attempt to optimize the constant
C
.

In a recent paper
[
13
]
we reprove
Theorem
1.1
with the goal of obtaining reasonable
explicit bounds for the constants, in particular avoiding the use of
regularity methods. Specifically, our main result for general
subdivisions in
[
13
]
is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
13
]

Let
r,\,D\geq 2
be integers, and let
H^{\sigma}
be a subdivision
of a graph
H
with maximum degree
D
with

\sigma(e)>{2\log_{2}^{2}r\log_{D}n+Cr^{4}2^{2r}\log^{2}D}

for every
e\in E(H)
, where
n=|V(H^{\sigma})|
and
C
is a
suitably large absolute constant. Then

\widehat{R}_{r}(H^{\sigma})=O\big(2^{34r}r^{6}\log^{5}(r)D^{5}\log D\big)n.

(1)

In
[
13
]
we also prove that if we further
restrict to the case that
\sigma(e)
is even for every
e\in E(H)
,
then Theorem
1.2
can be significantly improved.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
13
]

Let
r,\,D\geq 2
be integers, and let
H^{\sigma}
be a
subdivision of a graph
H
with maximum degree
D
with
\sigma(e)

even and larger than
{2\log_{2}^{2}r\log_{D}n+Cr^{4}2^{2r}\log^{2}D}
for
every
e\in E(H)
, where
n=|V(H^{\sigma})|
and
C
is a suitably
large absolute constant. Then

\widehat{R}_{r}(H^{\sigma})=O\big(r^{34}D^{5}\log D\big)n.

(2)

Clearly, the subdivisions
H^{\sigma}
considered in
Theorem
1.3
are bipartite graphs. In this paper we
prove that an additional improvement of Theorem
1.2
is
possible when the subdivided graph is bipartite,
without

assuming that every
\sigma(e)
is even.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
r,\,D\geq 2
be integers, and let
H
be a graph with maximum
degree
D
. Suppose
H^{\sigma}
is a subdivision of
H

with
\sigma(e)\geq 2\log_{D-1}n
for every
e\in E(H)
, where

n=|V(H^{\sigma})|
. Furthermore, suppose

is bipartite.
Then

\widehat{R}_{r}(H^{\sigma})\leq r^{\,400D\log D}\,n.

(3)

In general, the bound in (

) is worse than the bound
in (
2
), but of course (
3
) applies to more general
subdivisions

. Furthermore, the method of proof employed
here is entirely different from the one
in
[
13
]
.

Conventions and notation

For a graph
G
, we write
V(G)
,
E(G)
and
e(G)
for the vertex set, the edge set and the number of edges of
G
, respectively.
For
v\in V(G)
, by
N_{G}(v)
we mean the set of all neighbors of
v
in
G
.
For a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
, we define the neighborhood of
X
as
N_{G}(X)=\bigcup_{x\in X}N_{G}(x)
.
In a rooted tree
T
, the maximum distance of a vertex from the root is the height of
T
. If a tree has only one vertex (the root), its height is zero.
Let
A,\,B\subset V(G)
with
A\cap B=\emptyset
. Then
E_{G}(A,B)=\{xy\in E(G):x\in A,\,y\in B\}

is the set of edges connecting a vertex of
A
to a vertex of
B
. Also,
e_{G}(A,B)=|E_{G}(A,B)|
. A bipartite graph
G
with a bipartition
(V_{1},V_{2})
is denoted by
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
.

Given functions
f(n),\,g(n)
and
h(n)
, we write
\Omega(h(n))=f(n)=O(g(n))
if there exist absolute constants
C_{1},\,C_{2}>0
such that
C_{1}h(n)\leq f(n)\leq C_{2}g(n)
for all sufficiently large
n
.

All logarithms are taken to base

e
, unless stated otherwise. Throughout the paper, we omit floor and ceiling symbols whenever they are not essential.

2
Tools

In this section we collect several auxiliary results and notions that will be used throughout the paper.

We begin by recalling the model of a random bipartite graph.
The
binomial random bipartite graph

G(N,N,p)
is defined as the probability distribution over the family of bipartite graphs
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
with
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=N
, in which each potential edge
\{i,j\}
with
i\in V_{1}
and
j\in V_{2}
appears independently with probability
p
. We say that
a property
A_{N}
of
G(N,N,p)
holds
asymptotically almost surely
(a.a.s.) if the probability that
A_{N}
holds tends to
1
as
N\to\infty
.

It was shown in
[
14
]
that there exist explicit bipartite graphs whose local edge densities mimic those expected in random bipartite graphs. The following lemma that is Lemma 2.2 in
[
14
]
, provides a precise quantitative formulation of this phenomenon.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
14
]

Let
c_{1}
be a positive integer and
c_{2}
,
c_{3}
,
\varepsilon
,
\delta
be positive numbers such that
c_{3}\leq c_{1}
,
0<\epsilon\leq 1/2
and
3/2\geq\delta>\sqrt{6{\log({c_{1}e}/{c_{3}})/(c_{2}c_{3})}}
. Then there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\epsilon,\delta)
for which the following holds for every
n\geq n_{0}
.

Let
N=c_{1}n
and
p={c_{2}/n}
.
There exists a bipartite graph
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
, with
|V_{i}|=N
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, such that

1.

(1-n^{\varepsilon-1/2})pN^{2}\leq|E(G)|\leq(1+n^{\varepsilon-1/2})pN^{2}
,

2.

For every two subsets
U\subseteq V_{1}
and
W\subseteq V_{2}
with
|U|=u
and
|W|=w
, where
u,\,w\geq c_{3}n
, we have

\big|e_{G}(U,W)-puw\big|\leq\delta puw.

Next, we introduce a convenient framework to describe bipartite graphs with bounded part sizes and degree constraints.

For positive integers
n_{1}
,
n_{2}
,
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
an
(n_{1},n_{2};D_{1},D_{2})
-bipartite graph
G
is a graph with a bipartition
(X_{1},X_{2})
, such that
|X_{i}|\leq n_{i}
and the maximum degree of vertices in
X_{i}
is at most
D_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
. When
n_{1}=n_{2}=n
and
D_{1}=D_{2}=D
, we simply write
(n,D)
-bipartite graph.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
D>0
. We say that a bipartite graph
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
is
(n,D)
-expanding if for each
i\in\{1,2\}
and every subset
X\subseteq V_{i}
with
|X|\leq n
, we have
|N_{G}(X)|\geq D|X|
.

The central tool in our work is the classical tree-embedding lemma of Friedman and Pippenger
[
8
]
. This fundamental result provides a general expansion criterion that guarantees the existence of embeddings of bounded degree trees into large graphs. Intuitively, under sufficient expansion, one can embed a tree greedily by starting at the root and at each step there will be enough fresh neighbors available to add a pendent vertex. A bipartite refinement of this result was later obtained by Haxell and Kohayakawa
[
10
]
.

More recently, Draganić, Krivelevich, and Nenadov
[
5
]
introduced the notion of
good embeddings
, which provides a flexible framework for handling iterative embedding arguments. For our purposes we need the bipartite analogue of this notion.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n
and
D
be positive integers. Suppose that
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
and
F
are bipartite graphs and let
\phi:F\hookrightarrow G
be an embedding of
F
into
G
. We say that
\phi
is
(n,D)
-good
if for each
i\in\{1,2\}
and every subset
X\subseteq V_{i}
with
|X|\leq n
, we have

\big|N_{G}(X)\setminus\phi(F)\big|\ \geq\ \sum_{v\in X}\big(D-\deg_{F}(\phi^{-1}(v))\big)\;+\;\big|\phi(F)\cap X\big|.

(4)

Here, for vertices
v\in V(G)
not used by
\phi
in the embedding, we set
\deg_{F}(\phi^{-1}(v)):=0
.

Definition
2.3
coincides with the one used by Friedman and
Pippenger
[
8
]
and Haxell and Kohayakawa
[
10
]
up to the additional term on the right hand side of (
4
), which accounts for vertices already occupied by the embedding.

The next theorem shows that under suitable expansion assumptions, the property of being a good embedding is preserved when extending the embedded graph by attaching pendent vertices. This is the bipartite counterpart of
[
5
, Theorem 2.3]
. As the argument is essentially the same, we only sketch it in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
n
and
D
be positive integers. Consider bipartite graphs
F
and
G
and suppose that
\phi:F\hookrightarrow G
is a
(2n,D)
-good embedding.
Assume further that
F
is
(n,D)
-bipartite and
G
is
(2n,D+2)
-expanding. If
F^{\prime}
is an
(n,D)
-bipartite graph which is obtained from
F
by successively adding new pendent vertices, then there exists a
(2n,D)
-good embedding
\phi^{\prime}:F^{\prime}\hookrightarrow G
.

In a complementary direction, the following lemma shows that the goodness property is also preserved when successively removing degree (at most) one vertices from the embedded graph. This is the bipartite analogue of
[
5
, Lemma 2.4]
and we include a proof in Appendix
B
for completeness.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n
and
D
be positive integers. Consider an
(n,D)
-bipartite graph
F
and suppose that
\phi:F\hookrightarrow G
is an
(n,D)
-good embedding. Assume that
F^{\prime}
is obtained from
F
by successively deleting vertices of degree at most one. Then the restriction of
\phi
to
F^{\prime}
is also
(n,D)
-good.

We next recall another useful structural concept introduced in
[
14
]
, which allows us to pass from certain dense bipartite configurations to bipartite graphs with strong expansion.

Definition 2.6
.

[
14
]

Let
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
be a bipartite graph with
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=N
and let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be a real number. We say that
G
is
\alpha
-joined
if for every pair of subsets
A\subseteq V_{1}
and
B\subseteq V_{2}
with
|A|,\,|B|\geq\alpha N
, we have
e(A,B)\neq 0
.

The next lemma shows that every
\alpha
-joined bipartite graph contains a large induced subgraph with strong expansion properties. Moreover, this subgraph also supports a good embedding of a suitable null graph.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
be an
\alpha
-joined bipartite graph with
0<\alpha<1/5
and
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=N
. Then there exists an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}=G^{\prime}(V^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{2})
of
G
such that for each
i\in\{1,2\}
,

•

|V^{\prime}_{i}|\geq(1-\alpha)N
,

•

for every
X\subset V^{\prime}_{i}
, with
|X|\leq\alpha N
, we have
|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)|>(1-4\alpha)/{(2\alpha)}|X|
, and

•

for every
X\subset V^{\prime}_{i}
, with
|X|>\alpha N
, we have
|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)|>(1-2\alpha)N
.

In particular,
G^{\prime}
is
(6\alpha N,(1-2\alpha)/(6\alpha))
-expanding.
Moreover, there exists an embedding
\phi:F\hookrightarrow G^{\prime}
, where
F
is a null bipartite graph with
\alpha N
vertices in each part, such that
\phi
is a

(6\alpha N,{(1-4\alpha)}/{(6\alpha)})
-good embedding.

Proof.

For each
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
Y_{i}
be an arbitrary subset of
V_{i}
with
|Y_{i}|=2\alpha N
. If there exists a set
X\subset V_{i}
, with
|X|\leq\alpha N
and
|N_{G}(X)\setminus(Y_{1}\cup Y_{2})|\leq{(1-4\alpha)}/{(2\alpha)}|X|
, then we remove such an
X
from
G
and we iterate this process. Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
denote the removed vertices from
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, respectively.

We claim that
|X_{1}|,\,|X_{2}|\leq\alpha N
. Suppose otherwise, and assume w.l.o.g. that
|X_{2}|>\alpha N
. Consider the moment when
X_{2}
first exceeds
\alpha N
. Then
|X_{1}|\leq\alpha N
and
\alpha N<|X_{2}|\leq 2\alpha N
. Therefore,

|N_{G}(X_{2})|\leq\dfrac{1-4\alpha}{2\alpha}|X_{2}|+|Y_{1}|+|X_{1}|\leq\dfrac{1-4\alpha}{2\alpha}\cdot 2\alpha N+3\alpha N=(1-\alpha)N.

Hence, the set of non-neighbors of
X_{2}
in
G
has size at least
\alpha N
, contradicting the assumption that
G
is
\alpha
-joined.
Thus indeed
|X_{1}|,\,|X_{2}|\leq\alpha N
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by removing the vertices in
X_{1}\cup X_{2}
. That is
G^{\prime}=G^{\prime}(V_{1}^{\prime},V_{2}^{\prime})
with
V_{1}^{\prime}=V_{1}\setminus X_{1}
and
V_{2}^{\prime}=V_{2}\setminus X_{2}
.
It is clear that for each subset
X\subseteq V^{\prime}_{i}
with
|X|\leq\alpha N
we have

|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)|>(1-4\alpha)/{(2\alpha)}|X|
. If
|X|>\alpha N
, then since
G
is
\alpha
-joined, the set of non-neighbors of
X
in
G
has size less than
\alpha N
. Thus

|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)|\geq|V_{i+1}^{\prime}|-\alpha N\geq(1-2\alpha)N.

To verify that
G^{\prime}
is
(6\alpha N,(1-2\alpha)/(6\alpha))
-expanding, note that if
|X|\leq\alpha N
, then

|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)|>\dfrac{1-4\alpha}{2\alpha}|X|\geq\dfrac{1-2\alpha}{6\alpha}|X|,

while if
\alpha N<|X|\leq 6\alpha N
, then

|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)|\geq(1-2\alpha)N\geq\dfrac{1-2\alpha}{6\alpha}|X|.

Finally, let
F=F(U_{1},U_{2})
be the null graph with
\alpha N
vertices in each part. Set
Y_{1}^{\prime}=Y_{1}\setminus X_{1}
and
Y_{2}^{\prime}=Y_{2}\setminus X_{2}
. Clearly,
|Y_{1}^{\prime}|,\,|Y_{2}^{\prime}|\geq\alpha N
. Let
\phi:F\hookrightarrow G^{\prime}
be an embedding such that maps all vertices in
U_{i}
to
Y_{i}^{\prime}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. We prove that
\phi
is
(6\alpha N,(1-4\alpha)/(6\alpha))
-good embedding. To see this, let
X\subseteq V_{i}^{\prime}
be fixed for
i\in\{1,2\}
. If
|X|\leq\alpha N
, then

|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)\setminus(Y_{i+1}^{\prime})|>\dfrac{1-4\alpha}{2\alpha}|X|-\alpha N\geq\dfrac{1-6\alpha}{2\alpha}|X|\geq\dfrac{1-4\alpha}{6\alpha}|X|+|X\cap Y_{i}^{\prime}|.

That is

|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)\setminus\phi(F)|\geq\frac{1-6\alpha}{2\alpha}|X|+|X\cap\phi(F)|.

If
\alpha N<|X|\leq 6\alpha N
, then as
|X\cap\phi(F)|\leq\alpha N
we have

|N_{G^{\prime}}(X)\setminus F|\geq(1-2\alpha)N-\alpha N\geq\dfrac{1-4\alpha}{6\alpha}|X|+|X\cap\phi(F)|.

This shows that
\phi:F\hookrightarrow G^{\prime}
is a
(6\alpha N,{(1-4\alpha)}/{(6\alpha)})
-good embedding.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. Our approach proceeds in two steps.
First, we show that for a suitable choice of
\alpha
, every
\alpha
-joined bipartite graph contains the long bipartite subdivision of any bounded degree graph (Lemma
3.1
).
Second, we prove that if
G
is the random bipartite graph provided by Lemma
2.1
, then every sufficiently large subgraph
J\subseteq G
with
e(J)\geq e(G)/r
(with
r\geq 2
) is
\alpha
-joined, and therefore
J
contains the long bipartite subdivision of every bounded degree graph as a subgraph.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n
and
D
be positive integers.
Suppose that
H
is a graph and
H^{\sigma}
is an
(n,D)
-bipartite subdivision of
H
.
Also, let
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
be an
\alpha
-joined bipartite graph, where
\alpha\leq 1/(6D+14)
and
|V_{i}|=N\geq n/\alpha
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
. If
\sigma(e)\geq 2\log_{D-1}(\alpha N)
for every edge
e\in E(H)
, then
G
contains a copy of
H^{\sigma}
as a subgraph.

Proof.

Suppose that
(A_{1},A_{2})
is a bipartition of
H^{\sigma}
and let
|V(H)\cap A_{1}|=r_{1}
and
|V(H)\cap A_{2}|=r_{2}
. By Lemma
2.7
, there exists an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}=G^{\prime}(V^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{2})
of
G
which is
(6\alpha N,D+2)
-expanding since
D+2\leq(1-2\alpha)/(6\alpha)
. Also, there exists a
(6\alpha N,(1-2\alpha)/(6\alpha))
-good embedding from a null graph
F
into
G^{\prime}
, where
F
has
r_{i}
vertices in
V_{i}
, i.e. we can embed vertices of
H
into
G^{\prime}
as a good embedding. This can be done because
r_{i}\leq n\leq\alpha N
.
Now, consider an arbitrary ordering on the edge set
E(H)
, say
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
.
For each
0\leq i\leq m
,
let
H_{i}
be the spanning subgraph of
H
induced on the edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}
.
Note that
H_{0}
is just a null graph on the vertex set
V(H)
.
We inductively embed
H_{i}^{\sigma}
into
G^{\prime}
for each
0\leq i\leq m
, such that the embedding
\phi_{i}:H_{i}^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow G^{\prime}
is
(6\alpha N,D)
-good. Since
H_{0}^{\sigma}=F
and
D\leq(1-2\alpha)/(6\alpha)
, we already have a
(6\alpha N,D)
-good embedding
\phi_{0}:H_{0}^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow G^{\prime}
.

Now, suppose that we have already embedded
H_{i-1}^{\sigma}

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
2.4

Proof.

The argument parallels the strategy of Theorem 2.3 in
[
5
]
, suitably adapted to the bipartite setting.

We proceed by induction on the number of pendant vertices added.
It is enough to treat the case where
F^{\prime}
is obtained from
F
by adjoining a single new leaf
v
to an existing vertex
w\in V(F)
; the general statement then follows by repeated application.

Let
G=G(V_{1},V_{2})
be as in the statement, and for
X\subseteq V_{i}
and an embedding
f:H\hookrightarrow G
, define

R(X,f)\;=\;\big|N_{G}(X)\setminus f(H)\big|\;-\;\sum_{x\in X}\big(D-\deg_{H}(f^{-1}(x))\big)\;-\;|f(H)\cap X|.

Here,
\deg_{H}(f^{-1}(x))=0
if
x
is not in the image of
f
.

Let
Y:=N_{G}(\phi(w))\setminus\phi(F)
.
For each
a\in Y
, we can extend
\phi
to an embedding
\phi_{a}:F^{\prime}\hookrightarrow G
by mapping
v\mapsto a
while keeping
\phi_{a}
identical to
\phi
on
F
.

Our goal is to find some
a\in Y
for which

R(X,\phi_{a})\geq 0\quad\text{for all }X\subseteq V_{i},\;i\in\{1,2\},\;|X|\leq 2n.

Assume for contradiction that no such
a
exists.
Then, for each
a\in Y
, there is some
X_{a}\subseteq V_{i}
with
|X_{a}|\leq 2n
and
R(X_{a},\phi_{a})<0
.
Since
\phi
is
(2n,D)
-good, we have
R(X,\phi)\geq 0
for all subsets
X
with
|X|\leq 2n
.

Note that

\displaystyle R(X_{a},\phi_{a})-R(X_{a},\phi)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{1}[a\in N_{G}(X_{a})]+\mathbb{1}[a\in X_{a}]+\mathbb{1}[\phi(w)\in X_{a}]-\mathbb{1}[a\in X_{a}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}[\phi(w)\in X_{a}]-\mathbb{1}[a\in N_{G}(X_{a})].

Thus,
R(X_{a},\phi_{a})<0
implies
R(X_{a},\phi)=0
,
\phi(w)\notin X_{a}
, and
a\in N_{G}(X_{a})
.

We require three auxiliary observations.

Claim I
.

If
X\subseteq V_{i}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
is such that
R(X,\phi)=0
and
|X|\leq 2n
then
|X|\leq n
.

Proof.

Since
G
is
(2n,D+2)
-expanding,
|N_{G}(X)|\geq(D+2)|X|
.
Also, in each part
V_{i}
, the embedding
\phi(F)
occupies at most
n
vertices. Hence

0=R(X,\phi)\geq(D+2)|X|-n-D|X|-|X|=|X|-n,

yielding
|X|\leq n
.
∎

Claim II
.

The map
X\mapsto R(X,\phi)
is submodular:

R(A\cup B,\phi)+R(A\cap B,\phi)\;\leq\;R(A,\phi)+R(B,\phi)\quad\text{for all }A,B\subseteq V(G).

Proof.

The first term of
R(X,\phi)
is a submodular function of
X
, and the other two are modular,
so the claim follows.
∎

Claim III
.

If for
A,B\subseteq V(G)
it holds that
R(A,\phi)=R(B,\phi)=0
, and
|A|
,
|B|\leq n
,
then
R(A\cup B,\phi)=0
and
|A\cup B|\leq n
.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is
(n,D)
-good and
|A\cup B|,|A\cap B|\leq 2n
, both
R(A\cup B,\phi)
and
R(A\cap B,\phi)
are non-negative.
By Claim
II
we get
R(A\cup B,\phi)=0
, and then Claim
I
yields
|A\cup B|\leq n
.
∎

Returning to the main argument, for each
a\in Y
we have
R(X_{a},\phi)=0
and
|X_{a}|\leq n
.
Let
X^{*}:=\bigcup_{a\in Y}X_{a}
.
If
Y\neq\emptyset
, repeated application of Claim
III
gives
R(X^{*},\phi)=0
and
|X^{*}|\leq n
.
Now set
X^{\prime}:=X^{*}\cup\{\phi(w)\}
.
Since
\phi(w)\notin X_{a}
for any
a
, we have
\phi(w)\notin X^{*}
but
Y\subseteq N_{G}(X^{*})
.
Therefore, the neighborhood term in
R(X^{*},\phi)
equals that in
R(X^{\prime},\phi)
, while the degree-deficit term strictly increases by at least
1
when passing from
X^{*}
to
X^{\prime}
(because
\deg_{F}(w)\leq D-1
).
That means

R(X^{\prime},\phi)\leq R(X^{*},\phi)-\big(D-\deg_{F}(w)\big)\leq R(X^{*},\phi)-1<0.

contradicting
R(X^{\prime},\phi)\geq 0
.

Thus, such an
a\in Y
exists, completing the inductive step and the proof
∎

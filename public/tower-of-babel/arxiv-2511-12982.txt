SafeGRPO: Self-Rewarded Multimodal Safety Alignment via Rule-Governed Policy Optimization
Xuankun Rong, Wenke Huang, Tingfeng Wang, Daiguo Zhou, Bo Du, Mang Ye

Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated impressive reasoning and instruction-following capabilities, yet their expanded modality space introduces new compositional safety risks that emerge from complex text–image interactions. Such cross-modal couplings can produce unsafe semantics even when individual inputs are benign, exposing the fragile safety awareness of current MLLMs. While recent works enhance safety by guiding models to reason about potential risks, unregulated reasoning traces may compromise alignment; although Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) offers self-rewarded refinement without human supervision, it lacks verifiable signals for reasoning safety. To address this, we propose SafeGRPO , a self-rewarded multimodal safety alignment framework that integrates rule-governed reward construction into GRPO, enabling interpretable and verifiable optimization of reasoning safety. Built upon the constructed SafeTag-VL-3K dataset with explicit visual, textual, and combined safety tags, SafeGRPO performs step-guided safety thinking to enforce structured reasoning and behavior alignment, substantially improving multimodal safety awareness, compositional robustness, and reasoning stability across diverse benchmarks without sacrificing general capabilities. Our code is publicly available at: https://github.com/XuankunRong/SafeGRPO .

Method: SafeGRPO

3.1
Preliminaries

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
37
is a reinforcement learning paradigm designed to enhance the reasoning capability of LLMs.
Unlike preference-based methods such as DPO
34
or PPO
36
, GRPO follows the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm, where rewards are obtained through rule-based self-assessment rather than human preference pairs.
Given a query
q
, the policy
\pi_{\theta}
generates a group of responses
\{o_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}
,
each scored by a rule-based reward
r_{i}=R(q,o_{i})
.
The group mean and standard deviation are computed as:

\bar{r}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}r_{i},\quad s=\sqrt{\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}(r_{i}-\bar{r})^{2}},

(1)

the relative advantage of each response is normalized as:

A_{i}=\frac{r_{i}-\bar{r}}{s+\delta},

(2)

where
\delta
is a small constant for numerical stability.
The policy is updated by maximizing:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\big[\,A_{i}\log\pi_{\theta}(o_{i}|q)\,\big]-\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\right),

(3)

where the first term increases the likelihood of above-average responses,
and the second term regularizes divergence from a reference policy.

GRPO effectively enables rule-governed self improvement without constructing explicit reasoning datasets. However, when applied to
open-ended answer generation
tasks such as dialogue, essay writing, or open-domain question answering. The notion of correctness becomes subjective, and rule-based rewards alone are insufficient
21
;
18
. In such cases, evaluating responses often requires human or LLM-based judgments, limiting scalability and consistency. These limitations motivate the design of SafeGRPO, which extends GRPO with safety-aware reward modeling for open-domain multimodal learning.

3.2
SafeTag-VL-3K Construction

Overview.

To enable rule-based reward modeling in SafeGRPO, we construct
SafeTag-VL-3K
, a modality-level dataset for multimodal safety tagging.
SafeTag-VL-3K consists of 3K image–instruction pairs, each annotated with explicit safety labels for the
visual
,
textual
, and
combined
modalities.
These structured annotations provide verifiable ground truth for multimodal safety reasoning and form the foundation of our self-rewarded alignment pipeline.

Input Data.

SafeTag-VL-3K integrates samples from two complementary multimodal datasets: VLGuard
54
, originally used for SFT, and SPA-VL
52
, primarily designed for DPO-based preference alignment
34
.
Combining these sources ensures coverage of both instruction-following and preference-level safety distributions.
To extend, we convert 300 BeaverTails
13
samples into

typo-style
images, embedding the text directly into the visual modality.

Tagging Pipeline.

To obtain consistent and fine-grained safety annotations, we avoid using the original safe labels, since their safety definitions are inconsistent across datasets.
Instead, we employ GPT-5 API as the LLM-as-Judge to re-annotate each image-instruction pair
(x_{v},x_{t})
following unified multimodal criteria.
For every sample, we obtain both categorical safety tags and numerical scores for three modalities:

s_{m}=\{s_{v},s_{t},s_{c}\},\quad c_{m}=\{c_{v},c_{t},c_{c}\},\quad s_{m},c_{m}\in[0,10],

(4)

where
s_{v},s_{t},s_{c}
denote the safety scores for the visual, textual, and combined modalities, and
c_{v},c_{t},c_{c}
represent their respective confidence levels.
Higher scores correspond to safer content and more reliable judgments.
As the task involves straightforward safety assessment rather than open-ended reasoning, GPT-based annotation serves as a practical substitute for large-scale manual labeling, not as knowledge distillation.
We further verify its reliability through manual inspection in
Appendix

B
.
These structured annotations offer unified and interpretable signals that serve as the foundation for subsequent filtering and reward modeling.
The full annotation prompt is provided in

Label Filtering.

To ensure stable and unambiguous supervision, we discard samples with uncertain safety judgments.
Each modality score
s_{m}=\{s_{v},s_{t},s_{c}\}
is discretized into categorical tags based on the following rule:

y_{m}=\begin{cases}\text{unsafe},&\text{if }s_{m}\in[0,3];\\
\text{safe},&\text{if }s_{m}\in[7,10];\\
\text{discard},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(5)

To further enhance reliability, we retain only samples whose confidence scores
c_{m}=\{c_{v},c_{t},c_{c}\}
satisfy
c_{m}\geq 7
for all modalities.
This filtering strategy removes ambiguous or low-certainty cases, resulting in a high-consistency subset for SafeGRPO training and evaluation.

3.3
Step-Guided Safety Thinking

Motivation.

Existing training paradigms for alignment
34
;
36
;
54
;
27
primarily optimize the final output while providing limited guidance for the reasoning process.
In SFT, next-token prediction imitates reasoning text but does not evaluate its correctness or safety.
Reinforcement-based objectives in PPO and DPO focus on outcome-level optimization, offering little guidance for process-level reasoning.
Consequently, a model may output seemingly safe or correct answers while following unsafe or inconsistent reasoning paths.
To address this limitation, we introduce a
step-guided safety thinking mechanism
that enforces a reason-before-answering paradigm.
It explicitly decomposes reasoning into visual, textual, and combined safety assessments, enabling verifiable and interpretable intermediate states,
enable the model to analyze safety across modalities
.
This structured reasoning process provides more stable and informative reward signals for SafeGRPO.

Framework.

Within SafeGRPO, the step-guided safety thinking mechanism functions as an intermediate reasoning layer that organizes multimodal safety assessment into a unified and verifiable process.
It standardizes the model internal reasoning flow and enforces consistency in modality-level tagging across rollouts.
Concretely, this mechanism is implemented through the structured prompt in
Fig.

4
, which specifies explicit reasoning syntax and safety tags for each modality.
Formally, the reasoning–generation process can be expressed as:

s,\;y=R_{\text{think}}(x_{v},x_{t})\rightarrow(r_{\text{reason}},r_{\text{answer}})=\mathcal{F}_{\text{rule}}(s,y),

(6)

where
R_{\text{think}}(\cdot)
performs step-guided reasoning to produce modality-level safety states

s=\{s_{v},s_{t},s_{c}\}

and the final response
y
.
The rule-based evaluation function
\mathcal{F}_{\text{rule}}(\cdot)
then derives two complementary rewards:

r_{\text{reason}}
for reasoning correctness and safety consistency,
and
r_{\text{answer}}
for the overall behavioral alignment of the final output.
This formulation explicitly links reasoning and outcome evaluation, yielding interpretable intermediate states and dual reward signals that guide policy optimization in SafeGRPO.

3.4
Rule-Governed Reward Construction

We employ a rule-governed reward design to provide stable and interpretable optimization signals for multimodal reasoning.
Instead of relying on learned or preference-based reward models, we construct explicit, verifiable rules that evaluate model outputs from three complementary aspects: (1)
structural format correctness
, (2)
modality-level safety tag consistency
, and (3)
behavior alignment with inferred safety
.
The overall reward is computed as a gated linear combination, defined as:

R_{\text{safety}}=\mathbb{I}_{\text{format}}\times\left(0.5\times R_{\text{tag}}+0.5\times R_{\text{behavior}}\right),

(7)

where
\mathbb{I}_{\text{format}}
indicates the structural validity of the
output,
R_{\text{tag}}
and
R_{\text{behavior}}
measure reasoning accuracy
and behavioral correctness, respectively.
This formulation decomposes multimodal safety into verifiable components
while maintaining a unified scalar reward for GRPO optimization.

Format Reward.

The model output is required to produce a complete sequence of tags in the predefined order, followed by a final answer.
An indicator variable
\mathbb{I}_{\text{format}}
is assigned
1
if the entire sequence is well structured and parsable, and
0
otherwise.
This gate ensures that subsequent safety rewards are computed only for structurally valid outputs.

Tag Reward.

The tag reward
R_{\text{tag}}
evaluates whether the model correctly predicts modality-level safety tags during the reasoning stage.
Among the three tags (
visual, textual, and combined
), the combined tag serves as the dominant indicator of overall safety consistency.
A valid reward is assigned only when the combined tag
s_{c}
is correct; otherwise, the entire tag reward is set to zero.
When
s_{c}
is correct, partial credit is given for accurate visual and textual tags.
The reward is defined as:

R_{\text{tag}}=\begin{cases}0.5+0.25\,r_{v}+0.25\,r_{t},&\text{if }s_{c}=\hat{s}_{c},\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(8)

where
r_{v}
and
r_{t}
denote the correctness of the visual and textual tags, taking the value
1
when the predicted tag matches the reference and
0
otherwise.
This hierarchical reward emphasizes accurate combined safety reasoning while encouraging modality-level consistency.

Behavior Reward.

The behavior reward
R_{\text{behavior}}
enforces consistency between the model’s inferred safety reasoning and its final response behavior.
It provides a positive signal only when the model both predicts the correct combined safety tag and performs the expected behavioral action.
Specifically, unsafe queries should trigger explicit refusal, while safe ones should yield helpful responses without unnecessary rejection.
The reward is defined as:

R_{\text{behavior}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }(s_{c}=\hat{s}_{c})\land(a_{c}=\hat{a}_{c}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(9)

where
s_{c}
and
\hat{s}_{c}
denote the predicted and reference combined safety tags, and
a_{c}
,
\hat{a}_{c}
represent the observed and expected behavioral actions (
refuse
or
respond
).
In implementation,
a_{c}
is automatically determined by keyword matching with a predefined set of refusal indicators (e.g., “sorry,” “cannot,” “unsafe,” “not allowed”).
This rule-based design bridges reasoning and action, ensuring that safety intent is faithfully reflected in the model’s external behavior.

Baselines.

We compare our method with both training-time and inference-time multimodal safety alignment approaches.
Specifically, we consider three representative baselines:
(1)
VLGuard

54
: a training-based safety alignment method that performs instruction tuning with curated vision–language safety data;
(2)
ECSO

: a training-free inference-time method that mitigates unsafe generations via query-aware image-to-text conversion;
and (3)
Think-in-Safety

: a reasoning-guided alignment framework that fine-tunes models with safety-oriented thought processes to mitigate reasoning-stage safety collapse.
Together, these baselines provide a comprehensive coverage of safety optimization strategies across different alignment stages.

Training Details

We describe the training configurations used for our SafeGRPO model and all SFT-based baselines. All experiments are conducted on 4 NVIDIA A100 (80G) GPUs.

Training Details of GRPO.

In the GRPO training stage, we adopt Qwen3-VL-4B/8B-Thinking
41
as the actor, rollout, and reference model.
We use the VERL framework
39
to perform online optimization with multimodal rollouts generated by VLLM.
For each prompt, we sample candidate responses using temperature = 1.0 and top p = 1.0.
The maximum prompt length is set to 2048 tokens, and the maximum response length is set to 4096 tokens.
During GRPO training, the number of rollouts is set to 8, and the global batch size is 256, with a micro-batch size of 16 per GPU.
The actor learning rate is set to 1e-6, and we adopt low-variance KL regularization with a coefficient of 0.01, while entropy regularization is disabled.
We enable gradient checkpointing, fused kernels, padding removal, and use a GPU memory utilization ratio of 0.6 for stable large-batch rollout execution.

These findings further support the robustness and validity of the supervision signals used in SafeGRPO.

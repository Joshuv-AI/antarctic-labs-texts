Bruhat operads
Gleb Koshevoy, Vadim Schechtman, with Appendix by Daria Poliakova

Abstract
We describe some planar operads built from the higher Bruhat orders and show that they admit a multiplication.

Planar operads

2.1

Let

be a tensor category with unit object

.
Below we will be interested mostly in the category of sets with the monoidal structure given by the cartesian product.

Definition 2.1

A planar operad in
K
consists of objects

of
K
indexed by the natural numbers

, with operations of composition

\gamma_{k;n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\,\colon\,{\cal O}(k)\otimes{\cal O}(n_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes{\cal O}(n_{k})\to{\cal O}(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k})

(2.1)

and a unit map
I:e\longrightarrow{\cal O}(1)
.

The composition should be associative which means that

\gamma_{k;n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\prod_{1}^{k}\gamma_{n_{t};l_{m_{1}}^{t},\ldots,l_{m_{n_{t}}}^{t}}=\gamma_{k;\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}l^{1}_{m_{j}},\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k}}l^{k}_{m_{j}}},

(2.2)

the composition

{\cal O}(k)={\cal O}(k)\otimes e\otimes\cdots\otimes e\longrightarrow{\cal O}(k)\otimes{\cal O}(1)\otimes\cdots\otimes{\cal O}(1)\overset{\gamma}{\longrightarrow}{\cal O}(k)

should be equal to
\text{Id}_{{\cal O}(k)}
, and the composition

{\cal O}(n)=e\otimes{\cal O}(n)\longrightarrow{\cal O}(1)\otimes{\cal O}(n)\overset{\gamma}{\longrightarrow}{\cal O}(n)

should be equal to
\text{Id}_{{\cal O}(n)}
.

2.2
Partial compositions, or insertions.

To define compositions (

\circ_{i}:{\cal O}(m)\otimes{\cal O}(n)\to{\cal O}(m+n-1)

(2.3)

where

,

Symmetric groups

4.1
A symmetric operad
S
.

It is wellknown that the symmetric groups form an operad.

Namely, let
S(n)
be the group of permutations of the set
[n]=\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

The family of maps

\gamma_{k,n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}:S(k)\times S(n_{1})\times\ldots\times S(n_{k})\to S(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k})

(4.1)

defined by sending

(\tau,\sigma_{1},\ldots\sigma_{k}),\quad\tau\in S(k),\,\sigma_{i}\in S(n_{i}),\,i=1,\cdots,k,

to the permutation of the set
[n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}]

obtained by applying
\sigma_{i}
to the
i
th interval
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}+1,\ldots,n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}+n_{i}
, and then permuting these intervals following
\tau
.

The collection
S=\{S(n),n\geq 1\}
, with the above family of maps
\gamma
is a planar operad, and even more it is a symmetric operad.

4.2
A planar operad
F
.

We introduce another planar operad
F
on symmetric groups which is related to the master operad
M^{1}
.

We define
F(n)
as a subset of the set of permutations of

.

Specifically, we consider collections

\mathbf{k}=(k_{0},1,k_{1},1,k_{2},\cdots,k_{n-1},1,k_{n})\in M^{1}(n)

with teams of size

.

For any set
[n+\sum_{i=0}^{n}k_{i}]
we consider permutations
\sigma\in S(n+\sum_{i}k_{i})
which permute only nuclei and are identical on the electrons.

The set

consists of all such permutations for all
\mathbf{k}\in M^{1}(n)
.

Compositions

are defined as follows.

For
\tau\in F(k)
,
\sigma_{i}\in F(n_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,k
,

\gamma(\tau;\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})

permutes

\gamma(\mathbf{k};\mathbf{m}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{m}_{k}),\quad\mathbf{k}\in M^{1}(k),\,\mathbf{m}_{i}\in M^{1}(n_{i}),i=1,\ldots,k,

by permuting the blocks
\mathbf{m}_{i}
by
\sigma_{i}
and then permuting these blocks by
\tau
.

We have

Proposition 4.1

The collection of
F(n)
,
n=1,\cdots
with the above defined family of maps is a planar operad.

Proof
follows from Lemma
3.2
for

.

\Box

Remark 4.2

Restricting the operad

to the subset of collections without idles gives us the operad

,

Each

5.2
Example

5.3
Remark

Alternatively

may be defined as the set of minimal

whose restriction to each packet is either

Recall that partial orders, anti-symmetric transitive binary relations, are partially ordered by the inclusion of ordered pairs of elements. If we consider
the intersection of all partial orders that have the same restriction to every packet, we get a minimal

5.4

Let

. The

This way we get a map

Ziegler

Z
]
, Th. 4.1(B), characterised the subsets of

which appear as sets of inversions of Bruhat orders.

(Ziegler)
A subset

is the set of inversions

of a Bruhat order

if and only if for any

The last condition means that there exists

We will say that

\mathbf{i}
satisfies
(Z), for
I
.

Let us denote by

Note that a maximal chain in the poset

5.6.1

We define the wire space

Theorem 9.1

We have

, and

H^{n}({\mathbb{Z}}S^{\bullet})=0

.

Proof

Set
C^{n}={\mathbb{Z}}S(n)
, and write

\delta^{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}d^{i}:C^{n}\to C^{n+1}
. For
n\geq 1

define the contracting homotopy

on basis permutations

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})
by

h_{n}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}(\sigma_{2}-1,\ldots,\sigma_{n}-1),&\sigma_{1}=1,\\
0,&\sigma_{1}\neq 1.\end{cases}

For
n=1
, the first case is understood as
e_{0}\in S(0)
.

We claim that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\delta^{n-1}h_{n}+h_{n+1}\delta^{n}=\operatorname{id}_{C^{n}}.

It is enough to check this on a basis element

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})
. We use the following description of the
cofaces:
d^{0}\sigma=(1,\sigma_{1}+1,\ldots,\sigma_{n}+1)
,

d^{n+1}\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n},n+1)
, and, for
1\leq i\leq n
,

d^{i}\sigma
is obtained by splitting the
i
-th point of
\sigma
.

First suppose that
\sigma_{1}\neq 1
. Then
h_{n}\sigma=0
. Among the cofaces
of
\sigma
, only
d^{0}\sigma
begins with
1
. Hence

h_{n+1}d^{0}\sigma=\sigma
and
h_{n+1}d^{i}\sigma=0
for
1\leq i\leq n+1
.
Thus
(\delta^{n-1}h_{n}+h_{n+1}\delta^{n})\sigma=\sigma
.

Now suppose that
\sigma_{1}=1
, and put

\overline{\sigma}=h_{n}\sigma=(\sigma_{2}-1,\ldots,\sigma_{n}-1)
. Then

h_{n+1}d^{0}\sigma=\sigma
, and, for
1\leq i\leq n+1
,

h_{n+1}d^{i}\sigma=d^{i-1}\overline{\sigma}
. Therefore

h_{n+1}\delta^{n}\sigma=\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}d^{i-1}\overline{\sigma}=\sigma-\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}d^{i}\overline{\sigma}=\sigma-\delta^{n-1}\overline{\sigma}.

Since

, adding

.
This proves the claim.

If

Part III. APPENDIX by DARIA POLIAKOVA

11

The purpose of this Appendix is to prove the a strengthening of Corollary 5.4.

Elements of
B(n,d)
carry two natural orders. The first one is the
dominance order, defined by inclusion of inversion sets. The second one is
the flip order, whose covering relations are the increasing flips, i.e. the
addition of one inversion. Corollaries 5.3 and 5.4 show that insertion
respects the dominance order in the second and in the first argument,
respectively. For Corollary 5.3 the corresponding statement for the flip
order is immediate: one inversion in the second argument becomes one shifted
inversion after insertion. For Corollary 5.4, an additional argument is required.

Theorem 11.1

The insertion operations are monotone for the flip order. Let

\prec,\widetilde{\prec}\in B(n,d)
, let

\prec^{\prime},\prec^{\prime\prime}\in B(m,d)
, and let
j
be an admissible insertion place. Then

\prec^{\prime}\leq_{\mathrm{fl}}\prec^{\prime\prime}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime}\leq_{\mathrm{fl}}\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime\prime},

and

\prec\leq_{\mathrm{fl}}\widetilde{\prec}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime}\leq_{\mathrm{fl}}\widetilde{\prec}\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime}.

For the proof, recall the insertion construction from 5.8. Let
\prec\in B(n,d)
,

\prec^{\prime}\in B(m,d)
, and
0\leq j\leq n-d
. The insertion

\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime}\in B(n+m-d,d)
is defined by its inversion set. Identify

[n+m-d]=A\sqcup B\sqcup C
, where
A=[j]
,
B=j+[m]
, and

C=j+m+[\,n-(j+d)\,]
, and let
D=j+[d]\subset B
. We identify
[m]

with
B
by translation by
j
, and
[n]
with
A\sqcup D\sqcup C
by the
monotone bijection
\phi:[n]\to A\sqcup D\sqcup C
. For

L\in\binom{A\sqcup B\sqcup C}{d+1}
, consider three cases. If
L\subset B
,
then
L\in\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
if and only if

L-j\in\operatorname{Inv}(\prec^{\prime})
. If
L\subset A\sqcup D\sqcup C
, then

L\in\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
if and only if

\phi^{-1}(L)\in\operatorname{Inv}(\prec)
. Finally, in the remaining case,
define
\overline{L}
by moving the elements of
L
that are in
B\setminus D
into
the rightmost available positions of
D
. Then

L\in\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
if and only if

\phi^{-1}(\overline{L})\in\operatorname{Inv}(\prec)
.

Proof

If
\prec^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
\prec^{\prime}
by an increasing flip adding

F\in\binom{[m]}{d+1}
, then, by the case
L\subset B
in the definition of
insertion,
\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime}
by the
single increasing flip adding
j+F\subset B
; hence the monotonicity in the second
argument follows by iteration. It remains to prove the monotonicity in the first
argument, and again it is enough to assume that
\widetilde{\prec}
is obtained
from
\prec
by one increasing flip, that is,

\operatorname{Inv}(\widetilde{\prec})=\operatorname{Inv}(\prec)\sqcup\{K\}
,

K\in\binom{[n]}{d+1}
. We then need to show that there is a sequence of increasing
flips from
\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime}
to
\widetilde{\prec}\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime}
.

The strategy of our proof is as follows.

1.

We first describe the set of flips that we need, and identify them with

q
-subsets of some set
H
.

2.

We then construct an element of
B(|H|,q)
by its inversion set, and check
the Z-criterion.

3.

We then choose a lift to an admissible order in
A(|H|,q)
, and show that
the flips can be performed in this order, i.e. that each newly obtained set satisfies
the Z-criterion.

Step 1.

We need to describe the inversions that are in

\operatorname{Inv}(\widetilde{\prec}\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
but not in

\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
; these are our flips of interest. Put

K^{\phi}:=\phi(K)\subset A\sqcup D\sqcup C
, and set formally

\overline{K^{\phi}}=K^{\phi}
. The flips of interest correspond to

L\in\binom{A\sqcup B\sqcup C}{d+1}

such that
\overline{L}=K^{\phi}
. Let
Q
be the terminal segment of
D
occupied by
elements of
K^{\phi}
. If the last element of
D
is not in
K^{\phi}
, then
Q
is
empty and the only
L
with
\overline{L}=K^{\phi}
is
K^{\phi}
itself, so the statement
follows. We thus assume that
Q
is not empty.
To have
\overline{L}=K^{\phi}
, we must have the elements of
Q
either already in
Q

in
L
, or have them in
B\setminus D
, so that we move them to
Q
when forming

\overline{L}
. Set

q=|Q|,\qquad H=Q\sqcup(B\setminus D).

Then the flips of interest are in bijection with the
q
-subsets of
H
. Indeed, let

K^{-}
denote the elements of
K^{\phi}
before
H
, and let
K^{+}
denote the
elements of
K^{\phi}
after
H
. For any subset
F\subset H
, set its extension

\widetilde{F}:=K^{-}\sqcup F\sqcup K^{+}.

Then for every
q
-subset
F
of
H
,
\widetilde{F}
is a
(d+1)
-subset of

A\sqcup B\sqcup C
which bar-projects onto
K^{\phi}
.

Step 2.

We now want to construct an element of
B(|H|,q)
by describing its inversion set.
Let
U=\{u_{1}<\cdots<u_{q+1}\}
be a
(q+1)
-subset of
H
. The

q
-subsets

U_{1}=U\setminus\{u_{q+1}\},\ldots,U_{q+1}=U\setminus\{u_{1}\}

are its packet, and their extensions

\widetilde{U_{i}}=K^{-}\sqcup U_{i}\sqcup K^{+}

are a contiguous segment in the packet of

\widetilde{U}=K^{-}\sqcup U\sqcup K^{+}.

By the previous step, all
\widetilde{U_{i}}
are in

\operatorname{Inv}(\widetilde{\prec}\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
, but not in

\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
. Since both

\operatorname{Inv}(\widetilde{\prec}\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
and

\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
satisfy the Z-criterion, it follows that

\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
contains either only the prefix of

P(\widetilde{U})
before the segment of the
\widetilde{U_{i}}
’s, or only the suffix
after that segment. Set
\Theta
to consist of all
U
such that, in the above
dichotomy, it is the suffix.

We now need to show that
\Theta
satisfies the Z-criterion. First suppose that

K^{-}
is nonempty, and choose some
a\in K^{-}
. A
(q+1)
-subset
U

belongs to
\Theta
if and only if

\widetilde{U}\setminus\{a\}=(K^{-}\setminus\{a\})\sqcup U\sqcup K^{+}

belongs to
\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
, since

(K^{-}\setminus\{a\})\sqcup U\sqcup K^{+}
goes after the segment of the

\widetilde{U_{i}}
’s in
P(\widetilde{U})
. Now let

V=\{v_{1}<\cdots<v_{q+2}\}
be some
(q+2)
-subset of
H
. By the
reasoning above,
V\setminus\{v_{i}\}
belongs to
\Theta
if and only if

\widetilde{V}\setminus\{a,v_{i}\}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime}).

In the packet of
\widetilde{V}\setminus\{a\}
, the sets

\widetilde{V}\setminus\{a,v_{i}\}
form a contiguous segment, so by the Z-criterion
for
\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
, either a beginning or a terminal segment
of them belongs to
\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
, proving the Z-criterion
for
\Theta
. Finally, if
K^{-}
is empty, then
K^{+}
is nonempty, so one
chooses some
b\in K^{+}
and runs essentially the same argument with
b
.

Step 3.

We have shown that
\Theta
satisfies the Z-criterion; thus there exists an
admissible order on the
q
-subsets of
H
whose inversion set is
\Theta
. Let

W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{r},\qquad r=\binom{|H|}{q},

be one such order. We claim that the flips
\widetilde{W_{i}}
can be performed in
that order, or, equivalently, that for any
1\leq t\leq r
, the set

I_{t}=\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})\sqcup\{\widetilde{W_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{W_{t}}\}

satisfies the Z-criterion. Let
S
be a
(d+2)
-subset to be tested.

If
P(S)
does not contain any
\widetilde{W_{i}}
, then the intersection of
I_{t}

with
P(S)
is the same as the intersection of

\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
with
P(S)
, which is either initial or
terminal. If
P(S)
contains exactly one
\widetilde{W_{i}}
, then the intersection of

I_{t}
with
P(S)
is either the same as for

\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
if
i>t
, or the same as for

\operatorname{Inv}(\widetilde{\prec}\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
if
i\leq t
, both of which are
either initial or terminal.

Finally, suppose that
P(S)
contains
\widetilde{W_{i}}
and
\widetilde{W_{\ell}}

for
i\neq\ell
. Then
U=W_{i}\cup W_{\ell}
is a
(q+1)
-subset of
H
. If

U\in\Theta
, then by definition the suffix of
P(\widetilde{U})
after the
segment of the
\widetilde{U_{i}}
’s is in

\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
, and the order

W_{1},\ldots,W_{r}
inverts the lexicographic order on the packet of
U
. Thus the
already added elements in this packet form a terminal segment of this packet, and,
together with the suffix, we obtain the terminal segment of the whole packet
P(S)
.
If
U\notin\Theta
, then by definition the prefix of
P(\widetilde{U})
before the
segment of the
\widetilde{U_{i}}
’s is in
\operatorname{Inv}(\prec\circ_{j}\prec^{\prime})
,
and the order
W_{1},\ldots,W_{r}
restricts to the lexicographic order on the packet of

U
. Thus the already added elements in this packet form an initial segment of this
packet, and, together with the prefix, we obtain the initial segment of the whole packet

P(S)
.

This completes the proof.

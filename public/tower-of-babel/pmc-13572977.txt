Reversible self-discharge behavior and its intrinsic mechanism in supercapacitors based on bilayer heterogeneous hydrogel electrolytes
Authors: Zefeng Yan, Keyi Dong, Weiyang Tang, Quanhu Sun, Jiaxin Yang, Yan Tan, Yu Wang, Tian Lv, Tao Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rapid self-discharge is a major challenge for the widespread application of supercapacitors. They are limited by their intrinsic energy storage mechanisms and structures, and it is difficult to suppress supercapacitor self-discharge through modification of the electrode or electrolyte materials. Herein, we discover a reversible self-discharge behavior in supercapacitors based on bilayer heterogeneous hydrogel electrolytes with different water contents. For the hydrogel electrolytes with high water contents (≥73 wt%), the self-discharge time of the supercapacitor when the electrode on the polycation side is positively charged is longer than that when the electrode on the polycation side is negatively charged. In contrast, for the hydrogel electrolytes with low water contents (≤54 wt%), the self-discharge time of the supercapacitor when the electrode on the polycation side is negatively charged is longer than that when the electrode on the polycation side is positively charged. The experimental and simulation results reveal that the reversible self-discharge behavior in this supercapacitor is governed by the competing effects of the built-in electric field at the interface between the two hydrogel electrolytes and the electrode/electrolyte interfaces. This reversible self-discharge behavior is also found in supercapacitors based on pseudocapacitive materials, which can be exploited to regulate the self-discharge of high-performance flexible supercapacitors.

Introduction

Owing to the limited reserves of non-renewable resources and growing concerns over global warming, it is imperative to develop clean energy storage devices such as rechargeable batteries and supercapacitors.1–5 Due to their rapid charging rates, long cycling stability and high safety,6–8 supercapacitors have attracted increasing interest,9,10 and great progress has been achieved so far.11–14 Additionally, flexible and wearable supercapacitors have become one of the most promising candidates for powering portable electronics.15,16 Such devices can be readily realized by assembling two electrodes with a polymer electrolyte in between them.17–19 However, a major challenge for supercapacitors based on both liquid and polymer electrolytes is their ultrafast self-discharging (spontaneous drop of voltage under open-circuit conditions) rate,20–22 which is caused by their intrinsic energy storage mechanisms of ion adsorption/desorption or faradaic reaction. Unfortunately, only a few investigations have focused on suppressing the fast self-discharge of supercapacitors,21,23 and limited studies have been devoted to revealing the mechanisms of regulating the self-discharge of supercapacitors based on polymer electrolytes.24–26

The self-discharge mechanisms of supercapacitors can be classified into the following three types: ohmic leakage between two electrodes, faradaic reaction of electrodes and charge redistribution.21,27 Some efforts have been made to suppress the self-discharge of supercapacitors by modifying the electrodes,28–30 introducing additives into the electrolytes31–33 or modifying the separators.34,35 Flexible supercapacitors using polymer electrolytes also suffer from severe self-discharge,36,37 which has greatly limited their potential applications in practice. Recently, we introduced a bilayer heterogeneous polyelectrolyte (BHP) composed of a polycationic electrolyte (e.g., polydiallyl dimethyl ammonium chloride, PDDACl) layer and a polyanionic electrolyte (e.g., sodium polystyrene sulfonate, PSSNa) layer into flexible supercapacitors.38,39 The electrostatic repulsion between ions accumulated in/on the electrodes and the charged polyelectrolytes can efficiently restrict the redistribution of movable ions, thereby endowing the resultant supercapacitors with a slow self-discharge rate.40,41 In this BHP-based supercapacitor, a built-in electric field (BEF) was present at the interface between the polycationic and polyanionic layers,42–44 but its working principle and effect on the self-discharge performance of the supercapacitors remained unclear.

In this work, we for the first time discover a unique reversible self-discharge behavior of BHP-based supercapacitors by controlling the water content in the electrolyte. A series of multilayer hydrogel electrolytes composed of neutral polyacrylamide (PAM) modified with polyelectrolytes are designed to reveal the effects of the water content in the polyelectrolyte hydrogel on the electrochemical properties and self-discharge behavior of the supercapacitors. The supercapacitors based on bilayer heterogeneous polyelectrolyte hydrogels with different water contents (high, medium and low) exhibit three distinct self-discharge behaviors. Both theoretical simulation and experimental results verify that the self-discharge performance of the supercapacitors is governed by the variations of ion diffusion kinetics at the electrolyte/electrolyte interface and electrode/electrolyte interface, which are derived from the different water contents in the polyelectrolyte hydrogels. This reversible self-discharge behavior also occurs in supercapacitors based on a pseudocapacitive conductive polymer material. Finally, a high-performance flexible supercapacitor is demonstrated by balancing the excellent electrochemical performance and a low self-discharge rate.

Results and discussion

We first investigated the effect of the water content in the BHP hydrogel on the electrochemical performance of the resulting supercapacitors, which were fabricated by assembling a polycationic hydrogel layer (PDDACl/PAM) and a polyanionic hydrogel layer (PSSNa/PAM) between two pieces of CNTs (carbon nanotubes) electrodes (Fig. 1a). The water content in the BHP hydrogel was controlled by varying the mass fraction of the AM monomer in the precursor solution during the synthesis process. Two kinds of interfaces, i.e., the interface between the two electrolyte layers (at which the BEF was established) and the electrode/electrolyte interface between the positive/negative electrode and the electrolyte, were formed in these BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors. During the charging process, the charging configurations in which the electrode on the PDDACl/PAM electrolyte side served as the positive or negative electrode were defined as PDDA(+) or PDDA(−), respectively. The electrostatic interactions between the ions and the two polyelectrolytes at these two interfaces are opposite in nature in supercapacitors charged under the PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) configurations (Fig. 1b). The water content in the hydrogel electrolyte can strongly affect the ion diffusion rate, and will therefore alter the balance between the electrostatic interactions at the electrode/electrolyte and the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface, resulting in distinct self-discharge behaviors for the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors. As shown in the SEM (scanning electron microscopy) images (Fig. 1c–h), the pore size of the hydrogels decreased markedly as the water content decreased from 73 to 58 wt% and 50 wt%, because less space was occupied by water in hydrogels with low water contents. EDS (energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy) elemental mapping further showed homogeneous distributions of the characteristic elements of the polyelectrolytes in the hydrogels with various water contents (Fig. S1 and S2). As a result, the ionic conductivity of the BHP hydrogels (Fig. 1i) decreased from 9.39 to 3.94 and 1.75 mS cm−1 as the content of water decreased from 73 to 58 wt% and 50 wt%.

To investigate the ion transport properties of the BHP hydrogels with different water contents, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS, Fig. 1j) measurements were performed on supercapacitors based on BHP hydrogels using CNTs films as electrodes. According to the equivalent circuit obtained by fitting the Nyquist plots, the electric double-layer capacitor (EDLC) could be equivalent to a series resistance (Rs) and a constant phase element (CPE) in series. As Fig. S3 showed, the Rs value increased as the water content gradually decreased, whereas the CPE-P value decreased. The results indicated that water could efficiently facilitate ion diffusion at the interface between electrode and electrolyte through the formation of hydration shells around the ions.24,45–47

The electrochemical performance of supercapacitors based on BHP hydrogels with different water contents (from 44 to 82 wt%) was further investigated via cyclic voltammetry (CV) and galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) measurements. As shown in Fig. S4, all the CV curves exhibited nearly rectangular shapes, and all the GCD curves displayed symmetric triangular shapes, indicating nearly ideal capacitive behavior for the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors. The GCD curves were used to calculate the specific capacitance, which decreased as the water content in the hydrogel electrolytes decreased (Fig. 1k), accompanied by an increase in the initial voltage drop during the discharge process. The results can be attributed to the reduced ionic transport ability and interfacial contact between the electrode and the hydrogel electrolyte with lower water content due to the increased solid content in the hydrogel electrolytes.48,49 The interfacial adhesion of the electrolyte/electrolyte and electrode/electrolyte interfaces in the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors was further evaluated via 180° peeling tests (Fig. S5). Compared with the stable interfacial peeling observed for hydrogel electrolytes with water contents of 58 wt% and 50 wt%, the hydrogel electrolyte with a water content of 73 wt% underwent cohesive fracture before interfacial delamination at both the electrolyte/electrolyte interface and the electrode/electrolyte interface, suggesting much stronger resistance to interfacial separation relative to the cohesive strength of the hydrogel. Strong interfacial adhesion is crucial to endow supercapacitor devices with high electrochemical performance and stability towards flexing, which will be discussed later. The cycling stability of the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors was subsequently evaluated. As shown in Fig. S6, the capacitance retention (93%) of the supercapacitor based on the hydrogel electrolyte with a high water content (73 wt%) was lower than that (near 99%) of the devices using hydrogels with lower water contents (58 wt% and 50 wt%) after 16 000 charge/discharge cycles because the structure of the electrode materials may be changed or damaged under the high water content condition.

As shown in Fig. S7, all the CV curves, GCD curves, and Nyquist plots measured at a bias voltage of 0 V nearly overlapped for the supercapacitors based on BHP hydrogel electrolytes with a given water concentration, regardless of whether the electrode on the PDDACl/PAM side served as the positive or negative electrode. The results indicated that the basic electrochemical performance of the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors is independent of the orientation of the BEF. Interestingly, the Nyquist plots of the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) showed obvious differences at a bias voltage of 0.8 V (Fig. S8a–c). In the high-frequency region, the intercepts of the Nyquist plots were almost the same, indicating that the series resistances of the supercapacitors under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) differed very slightly. In the low-frequency region, the slope under PDDA(+) was larger than that under PDDA(−), which revealed that the ion transport ability across the bilayer electrolyte interface was higher under PDDA(+) than under PDDA(−). This difference can be attributed to the different electrostatic interactions between the charged polyelectrolytes (polycations and polyanions) and the movable ions under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) (Fig. S8d and e). This revealed that the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface had an obvious influence on the ion-transport and impedance characteristics under an applied electric field.

The different ion diffusion kinetics in the supercapacitors at bias voltages near their open-circuit voltages (Voc) were expected to affect the self-discharge rates of charged devices. The self-discharge behaviors of charged supercapacitors were systematically investigated using BHP hydrogels with different water contents. For the supercapacitors with hydrogel electrolytes with high water contents (from 82 to 73 wt%), the self-discharge times of all the devices were longer under PDDA(+) than under PDDA(−) (Fig. 2a). Moreover, the difference between the self-discharge times increased as the water content decreased from 82 wt% (10 700 s and 6759 s under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−), respectively) to 77 wt% (14 284 s and 8727 s) and 73 wt% (20 012 s and 12 639 s). Subsequently, the difference between the self-discharge times of the supercapacitor under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) gradually decreased as the water content of the hydrogel electrolyte decreased from 66 to 58 wt% (Fig. 2b). For the supercapacitor with a hydrogel electrolyte containing 58 wt% water, the self-discharge times under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) were very similar (20 803 s and 20 004 s). In contrast, the self-discharge times of the supercapacitors under PDDA(−) were longer than those of devices under PDDA(+) as the water content of the hydrogel electrolyte was further reduced from 54 to 50 wt% and 44 wt% (Fig. 2c), and the difference between the self-discharge times became increasingly larger. However, the electrochemical performance of the supercapacitor with the BHP electrolyte containing 44 wt% water was too poor for further investigation (Fig. S2). Based on the relative self-discharge behavior under the PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) conditions, BHP hydrogels with representative high (73 wt%), medium (58 wt%) and low (50 wt%) water contents were selected to investigate the underlying mechanism of this phenomenon in depth.

The self-discharge process of a supercapacitor can be divided into a potential-controlled regime (ln V ∝ t) and a diffusion-controlled regime (V ∝ t1/2).27,50 The potential-controlled regime appeared at the initial stage of self-discharge, which was governed by the dynamic drag associated with the arrangement and absorption states of ions in/on the electrode (Fig. S9a). The diffusion-controlled regime reflected the diffusion drag associated with the ion concentration gradient (Fig. S9b).24,27 As shown in Fig. 2d–i, all the self-discharge processes of the supercapacitors based on BHP hydrogels with different water contents were governed by both dynamic drag and diffusion drag. The similar potentials of the transition from the potential-controlled regime to the diffusion-controlled regime indicated that the water contents in the BHP hydrogels had a slight effect on the arrangements of ions on/in the electrode, but greatly impacted the ion diffusion drag. Specifically, the self-discharge times governed by diffusion drag (tdiffusion) of supercapacitors with BHP hydrogels with 73 wt%, 58 wt% and 50 wt% water contents under PDDA(+)/PDDA(−) were 16 131 s/10505 s (Fig. 2d and e), 15 562 s/17047 s (Fig. 2f and g), and 15 756 s/21327.5 s (Fig. 2h and i), respectively. The results were consistent with the differences in the total self-discharge times of the corresponding supercapacitors under PDDA(+)/PDDA(−), revealing that the differences in the self-discharge times of the supercapacitors originated from the differences in diffusion drag. The dependence of the different self-discharge behaviors on the water content of the BHP hydrogels can be attributed to the balance between ion diffusion and interfacial interactions.

To illustrate the effect of water content on ion diffusion in the BHP hydrogel, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were conducted. Amorphous cells with 10 polyelectrolyte chains (PDDA+ or PSS−), each consisting of 20 repeat units and 200 ions (Na+ ions or Cl− ions), were constructed. The water contents were set to 10 wt%, 50 wt%, or 90 wt% (Fig. S10 and S11). The mean square displacement (MSD) in the MD simulations was analyzed to investigate the ion diffusion rate in the BHP hydrogels with different water contents. As shown in Fig. 3a, the ion diffusion coefficient of Na+ in cells containing PDDA+ decreased from 0.324 to 0.285 and 0.0258 Å2 ps−1 as the water content in the hydrogel decreased from 90 to 50 wt% and 10 wt%. Similarly, the ion diffusion coefficient of Cl− in cells containing PSS− polyelectrolyte chains decreased evidently from 0.3412 to 0.1339 and 0.0027 Å2 ps−1 with decreasing water content (Fig. 3b). Both Na+ and Cl− ions interact with H2O molecules and form hydration shells in the aqueous environment; therefore, the ion adsorption and diffusion in the supercapacitor involve hydrated ions.51,52 The number of coordinating oxygen (O) atoms from H2O molecules around Na+ ions in cells containing PDDA+ chains increased markedly with increasing water content (Fig. 3c). Similarly, the number of coordinating hydrogen (H) atoms from H2O molecules around Cl− ions was the highest in the cell with 90 wt% water content (Fig. 3d), which was consistent with its having the highest ion diffusion coefficient. Additional MD simulations (Fig. S12) indicated that the electrostatic interaction between PDDA+ (or PSS−) and anions (or cations) weakened with increasing water content in the BHP hydrogel, allowing more ions to rapidly reach the electrolyte/electrolyte interface at higher water contents. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed to illustrate the effect of the H2O hydration shells on Na+ ions and Cl− ions (Fig. 3e). As five H2O molecules surrounded a Na+ (or Cl−) ion, the O (or H) atoms of the H2O molecules were oriented toward the Na+ (or Cl−) ion to form an oriented arrangement of the molecular dipole, resulting in charge redistribution within the hydrated ion complexes. Consequently, the ESP-derived charges on the central Na+ and Cl− atoms decreased in magnitude from their formal charges of +1 and −1 to +0.843 and −0.848, respectively. The simulation results clearly demonstrated the changes in ion diffusion and the electrostatic interactions between polyelectrolyte chains (PDDA+ or PSS−) and movable ions (Na+ or Cl−) in BHP hydrogels with different water contents.

To further investigate the effects of the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface and the electrode/electrolyte interface on ion diffusion, two additional supercapacitor structures were designed to weaken the interactions between ions and the electrolyte/electrolyte interface (or the electrode/electrolyte interface). A polyelectrolyte-free hydrogel was inserted between the electrodes and polyelectrolyte-modified hydrogels to form a four-layer hydrogel-based supercapacitors (Fig. 4a). The water contents in the hydrogels were 73 wt%, 58 wt% and 50 wt%, respectively. The dependence of the electrochemical performance (Fig. S13) of the four-layer supercapacitors on the water content of the hydrogel was similar to that of the BHP hydrogel-based devices. The layers of polyelectrolyte-free PAM hydrogel physically separated the electrodes from the polyelectrolyte hydrogels, which efficiently weakened the interaction between ions accumulated in/on the electrodes and the polyelectrolyte chains. In the supercapacitor charged under PDDA(+), as the ions diffuse toward the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface, the ions are attracted and retained by polyelectrolyte chains with the opposite charge, which suppresses their diffusion. Additionally, they are repelled by the polyelectrolyte chains with the same type of charge, resulting in a low self-discharge rate. In contrast, in the supercapacitor charged under PDDA(−), the ions are repelled by the polyelectrolyte chains with the same type of charge, and the polyelectrolyte chains with the opposite charge on the other side attract them to diffuse across the electrolyte/electrolyte interface, leading to rapid charge redistribution and fast self-discharge. Therefore, the ion diffusion is mainly governed by the electrolyte/electrolyte interface (Fig. 4b). As a result, all the supercapacitors based on four-layer hydrogels with different water contents exhibited a longer self-discharge time under PDDA(+) than under PDDA(−) (Fig. S14), which was markedly different from the behavior of the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors. Moreover, the differences between self-discharge times (Δt = tPDDA(+) − tPDDA(−)) of the four-layer hydrogel-based supercapacitors under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) gradually decreased with increasing water contents, as open-circuit voltage gradually decreased from 0.8 to 0.3 V (Fig. 4c).

A three-layer hydrogel-based supercapacitor was designed (Fig. 4d) by inserting a polyelectrolyte-free PAM layer between the two layers of polyelectrolyte-modified hydrogels to weaken the effect of the BEF at the electrolyte/electrolyte interface on ion diffusion. The supercapacitors based on the three-layer hydrogels with different water contents also exhibited ideal capacitive behavior (Fig. S15), similar to that of the bilayer and four-layer hydrogel-based devices. In the three-layer hydrogel-based supercapacitors charged under PDDA(−), electrostatic repulsion exists between the ions accumulated in/on the electrode and the polyelectrolyte chains with the same type of charge, which will restrict the ion diffusion from the electrode into the electrolyte. In contrast, in the device charged under PDDA(+), the ions in/on the electrodes are attracted by the polyelectrolyte chain with the opposite charge. Therefore, the self-discharge of the supercapacitor is mainly governed by ion diffusion at the electrode/electrolyte interface, as shown in Fig. 4e. As a result, the supercapacitors based on three-layer hydrogels with different water contents exhibited self-discharge time differences (Fig. 4f) similar to those of the devices based on BHP hydrogels, whereas their self-discharge times under PDDA(+) were shorter than those of the BHP hydrogel-based devices (Fig. S16). These control experiments further confirmed that the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface played an important role in regulating the self-discharge behavior of the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitor.

Based on the above results, we propose a mechanism for ion migration in supercapacitors based on BHP hydrogels with different water contents during the self-discharge process. As shown in Fig. 4g, the energy barrier for the diffusion of ions from the electrode surface to the BEF-containing interface between two electrolyte layers in the charged supercapacitors in BHP hydrogels with high water contents (from 82 to 73 wt%) is relatively low, owing to the low interfacial resistance and series resistance. For the charged supercapacitor under PDDA(+), the diffusion of ions (both Na+ and Cl−) to the opposite side is hindered at the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface because of the electrostatic interactions between the two hydrogel layers and ions. In contrast, ion diffusion across the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface is facilitated in the charged supercapacitor under PDDA(−), allowing more ions to move rapidly to the opposite side. This indicates that the energy barrier for ion diffusion across the BEF-containing interface is higher under PDDA(+) than under PDDA(−), resulting in different self-discharge times and rates. Taking the charged supercapacitor under PDDA(+) as an example, as Na+ ions migrate toward the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface, the negatively charged PSS− chains attract and retain them (the positively charged PDDA+ chains repel them), which hinders their diffusion to the opposite side, resulting in a slower self-discharge rate compared to that of the charged supercapacitor under PDDA(−). In addition, the ion diffusion rate in the BHP hydrogels decreases as the water content decreases from 82 to 73 wt%, which further suppresses the self-discharge of the supercapacitor under PDDA(+) and enlarges the difference between the self-discharge times of the device in the PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) conditions.

In the supercapacitors with BHP hydrogels with medium water contents (from 66 to 58 wt%), the ion diffusion rate at the electrode/electrolyte interface decreases further due to the higher interfacial resistance. The electrostatic interaction between the ions and polyelectrolytes at the electrode/electrolyte interface is opposite to the force at the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface (Fig. 4h). Consequently, the effect of the BEF-containing interface is counterbalanced by that of the electrode–electrolyte interface, resulting in very similar self-discharge times and rates for the supercapacitors charged under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−). In the supercapacitors using BHP with low water contents (from 54 to 44 wt%), the rate of ion diffusion from the electrode into the electrolyte further decreases because of the increasing electrode/electrolyte interfacial resistance and series resistance. The energy barrier for ion diffusion in the supercapacitor charged under PDDA(−) is higher than that of the device charged under PDDA(+) because of the electrostatic interaction between ions and polyelectrolytes (Fig. 4i). Therefore, ions diffuse much more slowly within one hydrogel layer and require a longer time to reach the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface, thereby weakening the influence of this interface on ion diffusion during self-discharge under both PDDA(+) and PDDA(−). Specifically, as Na+ ions diffuse slowly in one hydrogel electrolyte during self-discharge under both PDDA(−) and PDDA(+), they are additionally repelled by the positively charged PDDA+ chains under PDDA(−) at the electrode/electrolyte interface, which further restricts ion diffusion. Additionally, they are attracted by negatively charged PSS− chains at this interface under PDDA(+), resulting in a higher self-discharge rate.

We further constructed BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors using composite CNTs and pseudocapacitive polyaniline (PANI) electrodes to investigate the applicability of the effects of the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface and electrode/electrolyte interface. PANI was uniformly grown on the CNTs film (Fig. S17a and b) through in situ polymerization, which was confirmed by Raman spectra (Fig. S18). As shown in the cross-sectional SEM images (Fig. S17c and d), the thicknesses of the CNTs and CNTs/PANI electrodes were approximately 10 and 15 µm, respectively. The CV and GCD curves (Fig. 5a and b) showed that the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors using CNTs/PANI electrodes possessed good capacitive behavior. From the GCD curves, the specific capacitance of the CNTs/PANI-based supercapacitor was calculated and found to decrease from 139.04 to 106.57 and 70.27 mF cm−2 as the water content of the BHP hydrogel decreased from 73 to 58 wt% and 50 wt%. Three supercapacitors connected in series delivered an output voltage of 2.4 V, which could successfully light a light-emitting diode with an operating voltage of 2.0 V (Fig. S19). Fig. 5c shows the Nyquist plots of the CNTs/PANI-based supercapacitors using BHP hydrogels with different water contents. According to the equivalent circuit obtained by fitting the Nyquist plots, the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors using CNTs/PANI electrode could be equivalent to a series resistance (Rs) connected in series with a parallel combination of a constant phase element (CPE) and a branch containing a charge transfer resistance (Rct) and Warburg impedance (Wo).53,54 The fitted Rs, Rct and Wo values all increased markedly with decreasing water content in the BHP hydrogels of the supercapacitors (Fig. S20), indicating slower ion diffusion in the electrolyte and higher ion transport resistance at the electrode/electrolyte interface.

Fig. 5d–f present the self-discharge curves of the CNTs/PANI-based supercapacitors using BHP hydrogels with different water contents (73 wt%, 58 wt% and 50 wt%). When the BHP hydrogel with a high water content (73 wt%) was used, the self-discharge time of the supercapacitor under PDDA(+) was approximately 2.5 times that under PDDA(−) from 0.8 to 0.3 V (Fig. 5d). The self-discharge behavior of the supercapacitor based on the BHP hydrogel with high water content was mainly governed by the BEF-containing electrolyte/electrolyte interface. At a medium water content of 58 wt% in the BHP hydrogel, the self-discharge times of the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitor under PDDA(+) and PDDA(−) were very similar (Fig. 5e), suggesting mixed control by the BEF-containing interface and the electrode/electrolyte interface. For the supercapacitor based on the BHP hydrogel with low water content (50 wt%), the self-discharge time of the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitor under PDDA(−) (from 0.8 to 0.3 V) was about 2.8 times that under PDDA(+) (Fig. 5f), indicating that the self-discharge process was mainly governed by the electrode/electrolyte interface. These experimental results demonstrated that the regulation of the self-discharge behavior of the supercapacitor through combining the effects of the BEF-containing interface and the electrode/electrolyte interface was applicable to supercapacitors based on both electric double-layer capacitive and pseudocapacitive electrode materials.

The CV curves (Fig. S21a) and GCD curves (Fig. 5g) showed that the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitors using CNTs/PANI electrodes exhibited good rate capability (Fig. S21b). The soft hydrogel electrolyte also endowed the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitor with excellent flexibility. As shown in Fig. 5h and S22a, the CV and GCD curves of the supercapacitor obtained as the device was bent from 0 to 180° were almost overlapped, indicating the excellent flexibility of the device. After the BHP hydrogel-based supercapacitor was repeatedly bent from 0 to 135° 5000 times (Fig. 5i and S22b), the capacitance retention was 107.3% of the original value, demonstrating outstanding mechanical cycling stability. The high capacitance retention after thousands of bending cycles may be ascribed to the gradual infiltration of liquid species of the hydrogel into the electrodes, resulting in improved interfacial contact and ion transport capacity.

Conclusions

In summary, a unique reversible self-discharge behavior was observed in supercapacitors based on bilayer heterogeneous hydrogel electrolytes, which could be readily regulated by adjusting the water content of the electrolytes. A series of multilayer hydrogel electrolyte-based supercapacitors were designed to understand the effect of the water content in the electrolyte on ion diffusion and the interactions between ions and polyelectrolyte chains at the electrolyte/electrolyte interface and the electrode/electrolyte interface. The experimental and theoretically simulated results confirmed that the self-discharge behavior of the supercapacitors could be effectively tuned through adjusting the water content in the bilayer heterogeneous polyelectrolyte hydrogels. The reversible self-discharge behavior was found in BHP-based supercapacitors using both electrical double-layer capacitive and pseudocapacitive electrode materials, demonstrating the general applicability of this phenomenon. This work reveals a distinct electrochemical process in supercapacitors based on BHP electrolytes and provides a new strategy for designing high-performance flexible supercapacitors with slow self-discharge rates for applications in flexible electronics.

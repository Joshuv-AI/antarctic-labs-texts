Bounds reach of a future circular collider on the parameters of a minimal Baryon-Lepton symmetric model
Marlon P. Brade*, Jeremiah D. Juevesano, Dennis C. Arogancia, Jan Mickelle V. Maratas

Abstract
This paper investigates the physics potential of the proton–proton Future Circular Collider (FCC-hh) in the search for a new heavy gauge boson, Z ′ Z^{\prime} , within the framework of the minimal Baryon-Lepton ( B − L B-L ) symmetric model. We examine the exclusion limits on the mass of the Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson for various sets of coupling constants, utilizing leptonic decays, specifically ℓ + ​ ℓ − \ell^{+}\ell^{-} , ℓ = ( e , μ ) \ell=(e,\mu) . Using the DARKCAST framework, we compare the parameter constraints derived from previous experiments with the expected reach of the FCC-hh, providing insights into the collider’s sensitivity and potential to probe new physics beyond the Standard Model.

2
Minimal Baryon - Lepton Model

The B-L model is constructed upon the framework of gauge symmetry,
SU(3)_{C}\otimes SU(2)_{L}\otimes U(1)_{Y}\otimes U(1)_{B-L}
and is considered a triply-minimal extension of the SM. Firstly, it retains a level of minimality within the gauge sector by introducing a
U(1)_{B-L}
symmetry, which preserves the
B-L
number. Furthermore, in the fermionic sector, the model exhibits minimalism by introducing a lone singlet fermion for each generation to address the
U(1)_{B-L}
gauge anomalies. These fermions can be naturally interpreted as the right-handed neutrinos. Finally, within the scalar sector, it maintains minimalism as it introduces a neutral scalar singlet that plays a pivotal role in spontaneously breaking the
U(1)_{B-L}
symmetry responsible for the mass generation of the additional
Z^{\prime}
gauge boson. The Lagrangian of the
B-L
model can be decomposed as follows,

\mathcal{L}_{B-L}=\mathcal{L}_{S}+\mathcal{L}_{YM}+\mathcal{L}_{f}+\mathcal{L}_{Y}

(1)

where
\mathcal{L}_{S}
is the scalar Lagrangian,
\mathcal{L}_{YM}
is the Yang-Mills Lagrangian,
\mathcal{L}_{f}
is the fermionic Lagrangian and
\mathcal{L}_{Y}
is the Yukawa Lagrangian. Brief discussion about Yang-Mills Lagrangian, scalar Lagrangian and spontaneous breaking of B-L symmetry is provided below but detailed discussion can be found in Refs.

]

3.2
Detector Response

The detector response has been simulated via the
DELPHES
[
20
]
software package where default FCC-hh with pile-up card of
DELPHES
version 3.5.0 was used. Following a collision event, which involves parton showering, hadronization, and particle decays,
DELPHES
initiates its process by ensuring the propagation of long-lived particles through the tracking volume. This volume is situated within a uniform axial magnetic field, aligned parallel to the beam direction. The resolution on the track transverse momentum
p_{T}
, is described as,

\frac{\sigma(p_{T})}{p_{T}}\approx\frac{\sigma_{r\phi}p_{T}}{B\cdot L^{2}},

(9)

where
B
is the magnetic field strength,
L
is the size of the tracking radius and
\sigma_{r\phi}
is the single hit spatial resolution. Following their propagation within the magnetic field, long-lived particles subsequently arrive at the electromagnetic (
ECAL
) and hadronic (
HCAL
) calorimeters where particles deposits their energy. The resolutions of
ECAL
and
HCAL
are parameterized as a function of the particle energy and the pseudorapidity given by the expression,

\left(\frac{\sigma}{E}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{S(\eta)}{\sqrt{E}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{N(\eta)}{E}\right)^{2}+C(\eta)^{2},

(10)

where
S
,
N
and
C
are respectively the stochastic, noise and constant terms. Subsequently, the next step in
DELPHES
is the reconstruction of particles as objects. The efficiencies for triggering, reconstructing, and identifying these particles are parameterized as functions of their momentum where a universal parameterisations is employed for each object to simplify the process.

3.3
Statistical Analysis

Hypothesis testing is conducted by employing a modified frequentist method on a based of profile likelihood
[
21
]

[
22
]
. This approach takes into consideration the systematic uncertainties as nuisance parameters, which are adjusted to align with the expected Monte Carlo. The profile log-likelihood ratio test statistic is defined as,
q_{\mu}=-2ln(\mathcal{L}(\mu,\hat{\hat{\theta}})/\mathcal{L}(\hat{\mu},\hat{\theta}))
. Note here that
\hat{\theta}
and
\hat{\mu}
are the values of the parameters that maximized the likelihood function and
\hat{\hat{\theta}}
are the values of the nuisance parameters that maximize the likelihood function for a given value of
\mu
. When there are no substantial deviations from the background expectation,
q_{\mu}
is employed within the
CL_{s}
method to establish a 95% Confidence Level (CL) upper limit on the product of the signal production cross-section times branching ratio. For a given hypothesis whose values of the production cross section times branching ratio parameterized by
\mu
gives
CL_{s}<0.05
, these hypothesis are excluded at 95% CL.

Results and discussion

In this analysis, we select the two leptons with the highest transverse momentum (
p_{T}
), ensuring they have opposite electric charges and the same flavor. We then impose the following criteria: the leptons must have
p_{T}
values greater than 1.0 TeV, fall within the pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<4.0
, and have an invariant mass
m_{\ell\ell}
greater than 2.5 TeV. This study specifically targets the Drell-Yan background process, where proton-proton collisions produce
Z/\gamma
bosons that decay into a pair of oppositely charged leptons. In Figure
1
, we show the invariant mass distribution for scenarios with
Z^{\prime}
boson masses of 10 TeV and 15 TeV, with a coupling constant
g^{\prime}=0.2
. Figure
2
illustrates the 95% CL exclusion limit derived from
30ab^{-1}
of data, combining both
ee
and
\mu\mu
channels. We also present the cross-section
\sigma(pp\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
as a function of the reconstructed invariant mass (
m_{\ell\ell}
) for various coupling constants. For the case with the largest coupling constant considered,
g^{\prime}=0.300
, we exclude a
Z^{\prime}
boson mass of approximately 45 TeV at the 95% confidence level. This significant exclusion is attributed to the lack of background events in the high-mass regions. Using
DARKCAST

]

5
Summary and Conclusions

The B-L model, extending the Standard Model of Particle Physics, aligns with the seesaw mechanism, providing a coherent explanation for the neutrino mass anomaly. This theoretical framework posits the existence of a substantial neutral gauge boson, denoted as
Z^{\prime}
. The primary objective of this paper is to extend the constraints on the mass and coupling of
Z^{\prime}
in relation to the Future Circular Collider (FCC), in the leptonic across various coupling constants. Leveraging the capabilities of
DARKCAST
, we have reinterpreted and expanded upon the
Z^{\prime}
bounds, specifically in the high-energy FCC regions, utilizing the determined limits derived from the leptonic final states.

Derive Einstein equation from CFT entanglement entropy Preprint: preprintnumbers
Xin Jiang, Haitang Yang

Abstract
We explicitly show how to derive the ( D + 1 ) (D+1) -dimensional Einstein equation from the entanglement entropy between codimension-one disjoint regions in D D -dimensional conformal field theory.



CTP-SCU/2025012

I
Introduction

Over the past few decades, our understanding of gravity’s nature has undergone profound paradigm shifts.
This transformation is initiated by the Gauge/Gravity duality,
also known as the AdS/CFT correspondence
(
Maldacena:1997re
;
Gubser:1998bc
;
Witten:1998qj
)
, which
suggests an equivalence between a
(D+1)
-dimensional gravitational theory in AdS and a

D
-dimensional QFT living on its boundary.

Motived by this duality and the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy
(
Bekenstein:1973ur
;
Hawking:1975vcx
)
,
Ryu and Takayanagi proposed a landmark identification between the entanglement entropy of CFT
D

and the area of the corresponding minimal surface in AdS
D+1

(
Ryu:2006bv
;
Ryu:2006ef
)
.
For a CFT
D
partitioned into
adjacent and complementary
regions
A
and
A^{c}
,
the entanglement entropy is defined by the von Neumann entropy

S_{\rm adj}(A:A^{c})=-\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A}
,
where
\rho_{A}=\mathrm{Tr}_{A^{c}}\rho
is the reduced density matrix for region
A
.
The subscript “adj” indicates adjacent entangling regions.
The AdS
D+1
counterpart of
S_{\rm adj}(A:A^{c})
is a codimension-two hypersurface homologous to
A
.

The RT formula, along with developments such as “ER=EPR” conjecture
(
Maldacena:2013xja
)
,
has since motivated a profound interpretation: the nature of
spacetime is quantum entanglement
VanRaamsdonk:2010pw
.
However, a derivation of the Einstein equation from quantum entanglement
has remained unresolved over a long time.
This problem stems from two critical challenges:

1.

The entanglement entropy
S_{\rm adj}(A:A^{c})
between adjacent regions is divergent,
arising from the intense short-range correlations in contiguous fields.

2.

Until recently, no general method existed to compute
entanglement entropies in CFT
D
for
D\geq 3
.

The first issue makes the original RT formula an asymptotic relation,
rendering it extremely challenging, if not completely impossible,
to extract exact relations between gravity and CFT.
To establish an exact RT formula, there is a need for
finite
elementary
entanglement entropies,
which are intended to measure the entanglement between
disjoint
subregions,
as opposed to adjacent ones.
Here, the term “elementary” is used in contrast
to compound quantities such as mutual information, relative entropy, and the like — which are
constructed from combinations of elementary entanglement measures.

There are two additional intrinsic reasons to study the entanglement between disjoint subregions in CFT.

1.

For UV-complete theories,
UV divergences should not be intrinsic ingredients
but be UV limits of regular quantities.
Since an adjacent configuration is obviously an adjacent limit of a disjoint configuration,
and the entanglement entropy between disjoint regions should be finite,
investigating disjoint configurations becomes a necessity.

2.

Another critical reason comes from
the Reeh-Schlieder theorem which demonstrates that any two subregions in a QFT are
entangled
Reeh:1961ujh
;
Witten:2018zxz
.

In prior collaborations
(
Jiang:2024ijx
;
Jiang:2025tqu
;
Jiang:2025dir
)
,
we showed that the entanglement entropies
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)

(subscript “disj” denoting the disjoint configuration)
between disjoint intervals
A
and
B
in CFT
2
are
indeed finite, in static, covariant and
thermal settings.
The divergent adjacent
S_{\rm adj}(A:B)
arises as the simple adjacent limit of
the disjoint
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
.
The finiteness of disjoint
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
enables
us to explicitly derive the three dimensional Einstein equation
from CFT
2
entanglement entropy in
(
Jiang:2025isp
)
.

However, the three dimensional gravity is topological and has no
local dynamical degrees of freedom,
making higher dimensional studies critical for uncovering the quantum origins of gravity.
Yet this remained intractable due to the second challenge until our
recent work
(
Jiang:2025jnk
)
, in which we introduced a systematic
field theory method to compute the entanglement entropy of CFT
D

in all dimensions.
With this advancement, we are going to complete
the derivation of the
(D+1)
-dimensional
Einstein equation from the entanglement entropy of CFT
D
for
D\geq 2

in this work.

II
Entanglement entropy for disjoint subsystems in CFT
D

We begin by briefly outlining the calculation of entanglement entropy
for
\text{CFT}_{D}
(
D\geq 2
) given in
(
Jiang:2025jnk
)
.
As we explained in the introduction, in order to derive exact relations such as Einstein equation,
we need to find the finite entanglement entropy
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
, which measures
the entanglement between disjoint regions
A
and
B
in CFT
D
.
This is also a direct consequence of the Reeh-Schlieder theorem
Reeh:1961ujh
;
Witten:2018zxz
,
which shows that any two subregions in a QFT are inherently entangled.
The usual divergent

S_{\mathrm{adj}}(A:B)
which quantifies the entanglement between adjacent regions
is a simple adjacent limit of the disjoint
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
.

Consider a
\text{CFT}_{D}
in Euclidean spacetime with flat metric

\mathrm{d}s_{0}^{2}=\mathrm{d}t_{\text{E}}^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2}+\sum_{I=1}^{D-2}\mathrm{d}x_{I}^{2}
.
To ensure a finite entanglement entropy
S_{\text{disj}}(A:B)
, two conditions must hold:

1.

A
and
B
are disjoint regions;

2.

A
and
B
are complementary, forming a pure state.

The solid torus shown in Fig.
1
precisely satisfies these two conditions.
Note the time direction is upward. This geometry represents quantum fields living in a bounded region

\mathcal{B}:=\left\{\left(\sqrt{t_{\text{E}}^{2}+y^{2}}-\frac{l_{2}+l_{1}}{2}\right)^{2}+\sum_{I=1}^{D-2}x_{I}^{2}<\left(\frac{l_{2}-l_{1}}{2}\right)^{2}\right\},

(1)

with
l_{2}>l_{1}>0
. At
t_{\text{E}}=0
,
\mathcal{B}
consists
of two disjoint
(D-1)
-balls
A
and
B
of equal radius.

The entanglement entropy
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)

(

S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)=\lim_{n\rightarrow 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\log\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\rho_{A}^{n},

(2)

where
\rho_{A}=\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\rho
and

\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\rho_{A}^{n}=\frac{Z_{n}}{Z_{1}^{n}}.

Here,
Z_{1}

is the partition function of the solid torus
\mathcal{B}
.

Z_{n}
is the partition function on the
n

\mathcal{B}_{n}

n

\mathcal{B}

along

.
Clearly,
\mathcal{B}_{n}

2n\pi
.
So, the calculation of the disjoint entanglement entropy is simplified to
computing the partition function of the solid torus in Fig.

,

where

is a model-dependent constant and

denotes the hypergeometric function.

A
and

For

and

III
Einstein equation from entanglement entropies in CFT
D

The parameterization
\xi_{I}
in eq. (
4
) and Fig.
3

is chosen for convenience.
We certainly can use whatever equivalent parameterization to express
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
.
For example, we could alternatively adopt the radii and centers of
A
and
B
as parameters.
In particular, for the symmetric case where
A
and
B

share the same radius (corresponding to the solid torus depicted in Fig.
1
),
we now select the following linear combination as an alternative parameterization,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{4}+\xi_{1}}{2},\quad{X^{\prime}}=\frac{\xi_{3}+\xi_{2}}{2},

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi_{4}-\xi_{1}|}{2},\quad{Z^{\prime}}=\frac{|\xi_{3}-\xi_{2}|}{2}.

(6)

For a generic configuration that
A
and
B
having different radii,
such as that in Fig.
3
,
the reparameterizations are derived by applying the
corresponding conformal transformation to Eq. (
6
), which yields,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{1}+\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\xi_{41},\quad{X^{\prime}}=\xi_{2}+\frac{1}{1+\beta}\xi_{32},

(7)

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{1+\alpha}|\xi_{14}|,\quad\quad Z^{\prime}=\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\beta+1}|\xi_{23}|,

(8)

with
\alpha=\frac{|\xi_{34}||\xi_{24}|}{|\xi_{13}||\xi_{12}|}

and
\beta=\frac{|\xi_{13}||\xi_{34}|}{|\xi_{12}||\xi_{24}|}
.
In terms of the new parameters, the conformal invariant cross-ratio is

\eta=\frac{|\xi_{12}||\xi_{34}|}{|\xi_{13}||\xi_{24}|}=\frac{(Z-Z^{\prime})^{2}+|X-X^{\prime}|^{2}}{(Z+Z^{\prime})^{2}+|X-X^{\prime}|^{2}}.

(9)

We then introduce a nontrivial grouping:
Y^{\mu}\equiv(X,Z)
and
{Y^{\prime}}^{\mu}\equiv(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
.
Note that
Y^{\mu},{Y^{\prime}}^{\mu}
could be viewed as points in a (
D+1
)-dimensional spacetime,
since the dimensionality of
X
or
X^{\prime}
is
D
.
A
(D+1)
-dimensional metric thus can be defined

\displaystyle\chi(Y,Y^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{D-1}{2}\left(G^{(D+1)}S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)\right)^{\frac{2}{D-1}},

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\chi_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}]:=-\lim_{Y^{\prime}\to Y}\partial_{\nu^{\prime}}\partial_{\mu}\chi,

(10)

where
G^{(D+1)}
is the
(D+1)
-dimensional Newton constant.
The bracket
[\cdots]
denotes the coincidence limit
Y^{\prime}\to Y
.
Indices in brackets hereinafter are derivatives with respect to
Y^{\mu}
or

Y^{\prime\mu}
,
\mu=1,\dots,D+1
. Introduce

R_{\mu\nu}:=-[\chi^{\rho\sigma^{\prime}}\,\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\chi],

R:=-[\chi^{\mu\nu^{\prime}}]R_{\mu\nu}=[\chi^{\mu\nu^{\prime}}\chi^{\rho\sigma^{\prime}}\,\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\chi],

(11)

where the covariant derivatives
\nabla_{(\centerdot)}
are defined by

g_{\mu\nu}\equiv-[\chi_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}]
in eq. (
10
).
Substituting the explicit form of
\chi
in equations (
5
), (
9
)
and (
10
) into these definitions,
we obtain after straightforward but lengthy calculations:

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=0,

(12)

with the cosmological constant

\Lambda=\frac{D-1}{2(D+1)}R=\frac{D-1}{2(D+1)}\Big[\chi^{\mu\nu^{\prime}}\chi^{\rho\sigma^{\prime}}\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\chi\Big].

(13)

This is precisely the (
D+1
)-dimensional Einstein equation!

Several fundamental insights emerge from our derivation:

•

The (
D+1
)-dimensional Einstein equation emerges solely from the entanglement structure of a
D
-dimensional CFT.

•

From eq. (
10
), the metric is a derived but not a fundamental object, why bother quantize it?

•

Unlike conventional gravity theories,
\Lambda
is not an input parameter but is derived from the CFT data.

IV
Intuitive understanding of the derivation

It might appear mysterious why and how the derivation works.
Thus, while we have derived the Einstein equation purely from CFT data,
it is instructive to provide an intuitive holographic interpretation in this section.

The RT formula tells us that the gravitational dual of CFT
entanglement entropy is a codimension-two minimal surface in the bulk.
Since we here find finite entanglement entropy
S_{\text{disj}}(A:B)
,
the equality of
S_{\text{disj}}(A:B)
and RT surface should be exact,
as proposed in
(
Takayanagi:2017knl
)
.

For
D=2
(AdS
3
/CFT
2
), the minimal surfaces reduce to geodesics,
as depicted in Fig.
5
.
Remarkably, the metric can be directly extracted from the geodesic length
L(Y,Y^{\prime})

between points
Y
and
Y^{\prime}

(
Synge:1960ueh
)
,

g_{\mu\nu}=-\lim_{Y^{\prime}\to Y}\partial_{\nu^{\prime}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}L^{2}(Y,Y^{\prime})\right).

(14)

By using this equation, in
(
Jiang:2025isp
)
, we successfully
derived
3D
Einstein equation from CFT
2
entanglement entropy.

is

.

But how does this generalize to
D\geq 3
in which minimal surfaces are no longer geodesics?
Notably, there is mathematically no general method to extract a metric from minimal surfaces
in higher dimensions.
We can understand the mechanism as follows.

The crucial insight is that the entanglement entropy
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
of CFT
D

is fully determined by
2(D+1)
parameters: the centers and radii of balls
A
and
B
.
These
2(D+1)
parameters provide the exact degrees of freedom to fix
two antipodal points
P
and
Q
in a
(D+1)
-dimensional spacetime,
as shown in Fig.
6
.

Points

Points

and

The gauge freedoms justify choosing collinear

In short, the RT surface in AdS
D+1
actually is fixed by
a geodesic between two points, as indicated in eq. (

where we can see that

is a function of

So, what we need to do is to isolate the geodesic from the minimal surface.
From eq. (

The exponential in

entanglement entropy is a codimension-two surface.

V
Summary and Discussions

In this paper, we derived the
(D+1)
-dimensional vacuum Einstein equation from
the disjoint entanglement entropy
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
of
D
-dimensional CFT.
The finiteness of
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
is essential to the derivation.

In a previous collaboration
(
Jiang:2025isp
)
, we derived
3D
Einstein equation from
the disjoint entanglement entropy of CFT
2
. Since
3D
gravity is locally trivial,
it is worthwhile to restate some important remarks.
Referring to eq. (
10
), the metric is given by the entanglement entropy of CFT
D
.
So,

•

The metric is not a fundamental object but is derived from a quantum quantity,
suggesting that quantizing the metric may not be the right way to addressing “quantum gravity”.

•

The non-local/local duality between boundary and bulk is explicit,
as entanglement entropy is a non-local quantity in CFT.

•

When
S_{\mathrm{disj}}(A:B)
is non-analytic (e.g., during phase transitions),
bulk metric singularities emerge. Classifying these singularities may offer insights
into black hole physics and quantum critical phenomena.

For the future research, we think the most important direction is to derive the matter sourced
Einstein equation. This should be a highly nontrivial task since the conformal symmetry no longer exists.

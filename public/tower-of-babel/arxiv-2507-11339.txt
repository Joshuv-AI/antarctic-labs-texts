Abstract
. Using the concept of constant evasion to different sorts of suitable binary relations, we establish many cardinal invariants derived from the established cardinal invariants 𝔢 n const \mathfrak{e}^{\mathrm{const}}_{n} and 𝔳 n const \mathfrak{v}^{\mathrm{const}}_{n} , called the constant evasion number and the constant prediction number. We formulate several limits and consistency results pertaining to them.

1.
Introduction

This research forms part of the study of evading and predicting theory, a combinatorial framework originally introduced by Blass
[
Bla94
]
. Since its introduction, this area has been extensively developed by several authors, including
[
Bre95
,
Bre03
,
BS03
,
Kam00
,
Kam01
,
Kad00
,
Kad02
,
BGA19
,
CRS25
]
. These works have deepened the understanding of the combinatorial and set-theoretic aspects of evading and predicting, establishing numerous connections with other cardinal invariants, e.g., those in Cichon’s diagram and forcing constructions.

A refinement of these notions, termed constant evasion and constant prediction, was introduced by Kamo
[
Kam00
]
. Here, the requirements for successful evasion or prediction are strengthened: instead of asymptotic correctness, one demands that within every bounded interval of prescribed length, there exists a correct prediction or evasion. This strengthening yields a sharper combinatorial distinction and gives rise to two cardinal invariants:
\mathfrak{e}^{\mathsf{const}}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{v}^{\mathsf{const}}_{n}
, the constant evasion and the constant prediction numbers, respectively, are presented below.

Let
n\leq\omega
and call a function
\sigma\colon{}^{<\omega}n\to n
a predictor. By
\Sigma_{n}
, we denote the set of all predictors. Say
\sigma
constantly predicts a real
f\in{}^{\omega}n
, written as
f\sqsubset^{\mathrm{cp}}_{=}\sigma

\displaystyle f\sqsubset^{\mathrm{cp}}_{=}\sigma

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\exists k\in\omega\ \forall i\in\omega\ \exists j\in[i,i+k)\ f(j)=\sigma(f{\restriction}j).

We define the constant evasion number

\mathfrak{e}^{\mathsf{const}}_{n}:=\min\{|F|:\,F\subseteq{}^{\omega}n\allowbreak\ \text{and}\allowbreak\ \neg\exists\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}\ \forall f\in F\colon f\sqsubset^{\mathrm{cp}}_{=}\sigma\}

and the constant prediction number

\mathfrak{v}^{\mathsf{const}}_{n}:=\min\{|S|:\,S\subseteq\Sigma_{n}\allowbreak\ \text{and}\allowbreak\ \forall f\in{}^{\omega}n\ \exists\sigma\in S\colon f\sqsubset^{\mathrm{cp}}_{=}\sigma\}.

Inspired by the previous cardinal invariants, this paper aims to generalize and establish different versions of these cardinals. Afterwards, we derived some bounds and consistency results for them. Furthermore, using these new cardinals, we provide some characterization cardinal invariants from Cichoń’s diagram.

In addition to the already mentioned cardinals
\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\mathsf{const}}
and
\mathfrak{v}_{n}^{\mathsf{const}}
, we shall refer to a few additional well-known cardinal invariants below.

Given a formula
\phi
,
\forall^{\infty}n<\omega\colon\phi
means that all but finitely many natural numbers satisfy
\phi

\exists^{\infty}n<\omega\colon\phi
means that infinitely many natural numbers satisfy
\phi
. For

define

f\leq^{*}g\text{ iff }\forall^{\infty}n\in\omega\colon f(n)\leq g(n).

We recall

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}

\displaystyle:=\min\{|F|:\,F\subseteq{}^{\omega}\omega\allowbreak\ \text{and}\allowbreak\ \forall g\in{}^{\omega}\omega\ \exists f\in F:f\not\leq^{*}g\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}

\displaystyle:=\min\{|D|:\,D\subseteq{}^{\omega}\omega\allowbreak\ \text{and}\allowbreak\ \forall g\in{}^{\omega}\omega\ \exists f\in D:g\leq^{*}f\}

denote the
bounding number
and the

and
\mathfrak{c}=2^{\aleph_{0}}

Let

be an ideal of subsets of

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\left\{|\mathcal{J}|:\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I},\ \bigcup\mathcal{J}\notin\mathcal{I}\right\},

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\left\{|\mathcal{J}|:\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I},\ \bigcup\mathcal{J}=X\right\},

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\{|A|:\,A\subseteq X,\ A\notin\mathcal{I}\},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm cof}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\{|\mathcal{J}|:\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I},\ \forall A\in\mathcal{I}\ \exists B\in\mathcal{J}\colon A\subseteq B\}.

These cardinals are referred to as the

(2)

When studying cardinal invariants and their order relations, Tukey’s connections framework is practical since it simplifies the reasoning and instantly provides results for dual cardinals. For a general overview of this framework, see, e.g.,
[
Voj93
,
Bla10
]
. framework. Here, we mostly follow the notation of the second reference.

We say that
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,{\sqsubset}\rangle
is a
relational system
if it consists of two non-empty sets
X
and
Y
and a relation
\sqsubset
.

(1)

A set
F\subseteq X
is
\mathbf{R}
-bounded
if
\exists\,y\in Y\ \forall\,x\in F\colon x\sqsubset y
.

(2)

A set
E\subseteq Y
is
\mathbf{R}
-dominating
if
\forall\,x\in X\ \exists\,y\in E\colon x\sqsubset y
.

We associate two cardinal invariants with this relational system:

\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}):=\min\{|F|:\,F\subseteq X\text{ is }\mathbf{R}\text{-unbounded}\}
the
bounding number of
\mathbf{R}
, and

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}):=\min\{|D|:\,D\subseteq Y\text{ is }\mathbf{R}\text{-dominating}\}
the
dominating number of
\mathbf{R}
.

The
dual of
\mathbf{R}
is the relational system
\mathbf{R}^{\perp}:=\langle Y,X,{\sqsubset^{\perp}}\rangle
where
y\sqsubset^{\perp}x
means
x\not\sqsubset y
. Note that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\perp})=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\perp})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
.

For relational systems
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,{\sqsubset}\rangle
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}=\langle X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},{\sqsubset^{\prime}}\rangle
, a pair
(\Psi_{-},\Psi_{+})
is a
Tukey connection from
\mathbf{R}
into
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
if

\Psi_{-}\colon X\to X^{\prime}
and
\Psi_{+}\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
are functions satisfying

\forall\,x\in X\ \forall\,y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}\colon\Psi_{-}(x)\sqsubset^{\prime}y^{\prime}\Rightarrow x\sqsubset\Psi_{+}(y^{\prime}).

We say that
\mathbf{R}
is
Tukey below

\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, denoted by
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, if there is a Tukey connection from
\mathbf{R}
into
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, and we
say that
\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
are
Tukey equivalent
, denoted by
\mathbf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, if
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
. It is well-known that
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
implies
(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})^{\perp}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\perp}
,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})
. Hence,
\mathbf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
implies
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})
.

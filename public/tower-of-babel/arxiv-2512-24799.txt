Global spherically symmetric classical solutions for arbitrary large initial data of the multi-dimensional non-isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations
Yongteng G U, Xiangdi H UANG a. State Key Laboratory of Mathematical Sciences, Academy of Mathematics and Systems Sciences,Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China

Abstract
In 1871, Saint-Venant introduced the shallow water equations. Since then, the global classical solutions for arbitrary large initial data of the multi-dimensional viscous Saint-Venant system have remained a well-known open problem. It was only recently that [Huang-Meng-Zhang, http:arXiv:2512.15029, 2025], under the assumption of radial symmetry, first proved the existence of global classical solutions for arbitrary large initial data to the initial-boundary value problem of the two-dimensional viscous shallow water equations. At the same time, [Chen-Zhang-Zhu, http:arXiv:2512.18545, 2025] also independently proved the existence of global large solutions to the Cauchy problem of this system. Notably, in the work of Huang-Meng-Zhang, they also established the existence of global classical solutions for arbitrary large initial data to the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations satisfying the BD entropy equality in both two and three dimensions, and the viscous shallow water equations are precisely a specific class of isentropic compressible fluids subject to the BD entropy equality. In this paper, we prove a new BD entropy inequality for a class of non-isentropic compressible fluids, which can be regarded as a generalization of the shallow water equations with transported entropy. Employing new estimates on the lower bound of density different from that of Huang-Meng-Zhang’s work, we show the ”viscous shallow water system with transport entropy” will admit global classical solutions for arbitrary large initial data to the spherically symmetric initial-boundary value problem in both two and three dimensions. Our results also relax the restrictions on the dimension and adiabatic index imposed in Huang-Meng-Zhang’s work on the shallow water equations, extending the range from N = 2 , γ ≥ 3 2 N=2,\ \gamma\geq\frac{3}{2} to N = 2 , γ > 1 N=2,\ \gamma>1 and N = 3 , 1 < γ < 3 N=3,\ 1<\gamma<3 . Keywords: Shallow water equations; Entropy transport; Global spherically symmetric classical solutions; BD entropy inequality Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) : 76N10, 35B45.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the non-isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations, especially for viscous shallow water model. This model can be regarded as an extension of the viscous shallow water model to the case of non-constant entropy. It describes the motion of a compressible fluid with variable entropy, and can be viewed as the full compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier neglecting thermal conduction and viscous dissipation. This generalized shallow water model can be written as

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho\mathbf{u}\right)=0,\\
\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla P=2\nabla\cdot\left(\rho\mathcal{D}\mathbf{u}\right),\\
s_{t}+u\cdot\nabla s=0,\\
P=\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}.\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here
\rho(x,t)
,
\mathbf{u}(x,t)
,
s(x,t)
and
P(\rho,s)
(
\gamma>1
) are the fluid density, velocity, entropy and pressure, respectively, and
\mathcal{D}\mathbf{u}=\frac{\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}^{T}}{2}
.

When
s=\text{const}
, this model is a special case of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with density-dependent viscosity coefficients

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u})=0,\\[4.0pt]
\partial_{t}(\rho\mathbf{u})+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})+\nabla P(\rho)-\nabla\cdot\big(\mu(\rho)\,\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{u})\big)-\nabla\big(\lambda(\rho)\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\big)=0,\end{cases}

(1.2)

with the viscosity coefficients
\mu(\rho)
and
\lambda(\rho)
satisfy

\mu(\rho)\geq 0,\quad\mu(\rho)+N\lambda(\rho)\geq 0.

Among compressible Navier–Stokes equations with variable viscosity coefficients, a model that has attracted considerable attention is the case

\mu=\text{const}>0,\quad\lambda(\rho)=\rho^{\beta}.

An early study of this case was carried out by Kazhikhov-Vaigant
[
16
]
, who proved the global well‑posedness of strong solutions to the two‑dimensional periodic problem for arbitrarily large initial data away from vacuum, provided the exponent satisfies
\beta>3
. Later, by employing commutator estimates, Huang-Li
[
12
]
weakened the requirement on the viscosity coefficient to
\beta>\frac{4}{3}
and allowed the initial data to contain vacuum. For the Cauchy problem, Huang-Li
[
13
]
established the global existence and uniqueness of both strong and classical solutions under the condition
\beta>\frac{4}{3}
, provided the initial density
\rho_{0}
decays at a suitable rate (a related result was also obtained by Jiu-Wang-Xin
[
15
]
). For the initial-boundary value problem with Navier‑slip conditions, Fan-Li-Li
[
8

employed the Riemann mapping theorem and the pull‑back Green’s function method to overcome the boundary‑induced difficulties and established the well‑posedness of the system. For the radially symmetric case, Huang-Su-Yan-Yu
[
11

derived an explicit representation for the effective viscous flux on the boundary and proved the global existence of strong solutions under the condition
\beta>1
.

Another important variable-viscosity model is the B-D entropy model, in which the coefficients satisfy

\lambda(\rho)=\rho\mu^{\prime}(\rho)-\mu(\rho).

This mathematical entropy estimate was derived by Bresch-Desjardins-Lin
[
1
]
and Bresch-Desjardins
[

[

[

\mu(\rho)=\rho

\lambda(\rho)=0

•

N=2
,
\gamma>1.

Later, Bresch-Vasseur-Yu
[
4

extended the result of Li-Xin
[

[

\mu(\rho)=\rho^{\alpha},\quad\lambda(\rho)=(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha}.

This model was further studied by Guo-Xu-Zhang

\mu(\rho)=\rho

However, the existence of global classical solutions for the multi-dimensional viscous Saint-Venant system with arbitrarily large initial data remained a challenging open problem. A breakthrough was recently achieved by Huang-Meng-Zhang

Building upon the preceding analysis, this paper investigates the endpoint case of the BD entropy model and generalizes it to the non-isentropic system. For simplicity, we assume that

\mu(\rho)=2\rho,\quad\lambda(\rho)=0.

We consider the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
) in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

(\rho,\rho\mathbf{u},s)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},\mathbf{m_{0}},s_{0}),\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.4)

For the radially symmetric setting, we assume

\rho(x,t)=\rho(r,t),\quad\mathbf{u}(x,t)=u(r,t)\frac{\mathbf{x}}{r},\quad s(x,t)=s(r,t).

(1.5)

In

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{r}+\frac{N-1}{r}\rho u=0,\\
\displaystyle\rho(u_{t}+uu_{r})+P_{r}=2\partial_{r}(\rho u_{r})+2(N-1)\frac{\rho}{r}\left(u_{r}-\frac{u}{r}\right),\\
\displaystyle s_{t}+us_{r}=0,\end{cases}

(1.6)

\rho(r,0)=\rho_{0}(r),\quad\rho u(r,0)=m_{0}(r),\quad s(r,0)=s_{0}(r),

\rho u(0,t)=\rho u(R,t)=0.

We begin by defining the global classical solution before formulating our theorem.

Definition 1.1

(Global classical solution)
.

We say that a radially symmetric triple
(\rho,\mathbf{u},s)
with
\rho>0
is a global classical solution to the initial-boundary-value problem (
1.1
),(
1.3
) and (
1.4

\begin{cases}\rho,s\in C([0,T];H^{3}(\Omega)),\quad\rho_{t},s_{t}\in C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega)),\\
\rho_{tt},s_{tt}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),\\
\mathbf{u}\in C([0,T];H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{3}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(\tau,T;H^{4}(\Omega)),\\
\mathbf{u}_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(\tau,T;H^{2}(\Omega)),\\
\mathbf{u}_{tt}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(\tau,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(\tau,T;H^{1}(\Omega)).\end{cases}

Our main results are the following two theorems on the global existence of classical solutions with large initial data away from vacuum for the system with transported entropy.

Theorem 1.1

(Global classical solutions for

Assume that

\displaystyle(\rho_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},s_{0})\in H^{3}(\Omega),\quad\mathbf{u}_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Then, there exists a unique global radially symmetric classical solution to the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
),(
1.3
) and (
1.4
) satisfying, for any
(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]

(C(T))^{-1}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq C(T),\quad 0<\underline{s}\leq s(x,t)\leq\bar{s}.

(1.7)

where the constant

depends on the initial data and

Theorem 1.2

(Global classical solutions for

Then, there exists a unique global spherically symmetric classical solution to the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
),(
1.3
) and (
1.4
) satisfying, for any
(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]

(C(T))^{-1}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq C(T),\quad 0<\underline{s}\leq s(x,t)\leq\bar{s}.

(1.8)

where the constant
C(T)>0
depends on the initial data and
T
.

.

When

is constant, Theorem

Assume that

Remark 1.2
.

The reason why the isentropic model admits uniform lower and upper bounds on density and large-time behavior is that uniform estimates for the density can be established. These estimates lead to the desired uniform density bounds and asymptotic behavior.

We now introduce Lagrangian coordinates to simplify the following analysis.
We define the coordinates transformation

y(r,t)=\int_{0}^{r}\rho(s,t)s^{N-1}\,ds,\quad\tau(r,t)=t.

(1.9)

Without loss of generality, assume that

\int_{0}^{R}\rho r^{N-1}\,dr=\int_{0}^{R}\rho_{0}r^{N-1}\,dr=1.

Therefore, we translate the domain
[0,R]\times[0,T]
into
[0,1]\times[0,T]
, which satisfies

\frac{\partial y}{\partial r}=\rho r^{N-1},\quad\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=-\rho ur^{N-1},\quad\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial r}=0,\quad\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial t}=1,\quad\frac{\partial r}{\partial\tau}=u.

(1.10)

The system (
1.6
) transforms into

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}\rho+\rho^{2}\partial_{y}(r^{N-1}u)=0,\\
\partial_{\tau}s=0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{N-1}}\partial_{\tau}u+\partial_{y}P-\partial_{y}\left[2\rho^{2}\partial_{y}(r^{N-1}u)\right]+\frac{2(N-1)}{r}u\partial_{y}\rho=0.\end{cases}

(1.11)

Let
w=u+2r^{N-1}\partial_{y}\rho
be the effective velocity. Then the system for effective velocity becomes

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}\rho+\rho^{2}\partial_{y}(r^{N-1}u)=0,\\
\partial_{\tau}s=0,\\
\partial_{\tau}w+r^{N-1}\partial_{y}P=0.\end{cases}

(1.12)

We now outline the main strategy of the proof. We must address two major challenges: first, establishing the B-D entropy estimates for non-isentropic viscous shallow water waves; and second, improving upon the 2D indices of Huang-Meng-Zhang
[
10
]
and extending these results to the 3D case.

1. The BD entropy inequality for non-isentropic fluids.

In contrast to the isentropic case, where the pressure term provides a dissipation term when tested with the effective velocity
w=u+2r^{N-1}\partial_{y}\rho
, for non-isentropic flows, the entropy variation leads to the emergence of both a dissipation term and a source term. However, despite the lack of the standard BD entropy identity, the BD entropy inequality can still be derived by coupling the system with basic energy estimates. Therefore, we find a structure similar to the BD condition in the non-isentropic setting. In fact, in Lagrangian coordinates, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}w^{2}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}e^{s}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}2\gamma r^{2N-2}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dy}_{\text{Dissipation term}}=-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}2r^{2N-2}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}(\partial_{y}s)(\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy}_{\text{Source term}}.

To handle the source term, we need to deal with the
\partial_{y}s
at first.
In general, estimates for the derivatives of
s
would require the
L^{1}L^{\infty}
norm of
\nabla\mathbf{u}
. Fortunately, the entropy is invariant along particle paths in the current setting, satisfying
s_{y\tau}=0
in Lagrangian coordinates. Consequently, provided that the initial derivative
\partial_{y}s_{0}
is bounded,
\partial_{y}s
remains bounded throughout the evolution. For this reason, we impose an additional condition on the initial entropy in Eulerian coordinates, i.e.

\frac{\partial_{r}s_{0}}{\rho_{0}r^{N-1}}\in L^{\infty}.
In particular, the case
s=\text{const}
satisfies this condition. Therefore, by using Young’s inequality, we only need to deal with the term
\|\rho r^{N-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
. By using weighted estimates near the origin
and Sobolev’s embedding in one-dimensional space

\displaystyle\|\rho r^{N-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\rho_{y}r^{N-1}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|\frac{1}{r}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\rho_{y}r^{N-1}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi+\frac{1}{2}(N-2)}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\rho_{y}r^{N-1}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+1\right).

Consequently, we get the BD entropy inequality by combining with the basic energy identity and using Gronwall’s inequality.

2. Global spherically symmetric classical solutions for the multi-dimensional case

To derive the higher-order estimates and thereby prove the existence of global solutions, we first establish the lower-order estimates

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}

\displaystyle\left(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}{\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)}dt\leq C(T).

The most crucial step here is to derive the upper and lower bounds for the density. In a certain sense, this serves as a blow-up criterion. We now proceed to outline the strategy for obtaining the upper and lower bounds of the density.

2.1. Upper bound for density

The BD entropy estimates initially yield a bound on
\|\nabla\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, but this is insufficient to determine the upper bound for the density. The crucial step lies in estimating
\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2n}}\|_{L^{2n}(\Omega)}
(
n\geq 2
). We employ different methods to derive the upper bound of the density in the two-dimensional and three-dimensional cases.

2.2. Lower bound for density

The reason we can extend the results of Huang-Meng-Zhang
[
10
]
for viscous shallow water equations to the range
\gamma>1
in 2D and
1<\gamma<3
in 3D is that a lower bound for the density can be established in these cases. The essence of their method lies in deriving
L^{\infty}
bounds for both the effective velocity and the velocity field. Specifically, Huang-Meng-Zhang
[
10
]
first established the
L^{\infty}
estimate for the effective velocity, followed by that of the velocity itself. A crucial step in their proof is the following critical estimate for spherically symmetric functions

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{\rho}\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{u}\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{\rho}\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}.

Consequently, this implies that estimating the effective velocity relies on controlling powers of the density, which leads to the restriction
\gamma\geq 3/2
in the two-dimensional case. Furthermore, this dependency poses difficulties for extending the analysis to the three-dimensional setting.

Our main observation is that, once the density upper bound is obtained, we can estimate
\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
jointly. This is achieved by coupling two seemingly unrelated
L^{2n}
estimates

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\left|\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}uw^{2n-1}\right|\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}\left|\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}rw^{2n-1}(\partial_{y}s)\right|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+\|u\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+1,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|u\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\left|\partial_{y}P\right|r|u|^{2n-1}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+\|u\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+1.

Combining these estimates and applying Gronwall’s inequality, we establish the
L^{\infty}
bounds for the velocity and the effective velocity as
n\to\infty
. One can observe an intrinsic connection between
u
and
w
. Moreover, since this derivation does not rely on the assumption of spherical symmetry, it may provide insights for the study of general domains.

We now present two lemmas that are crucial for the subsequent estimates. These results establish the relationship between the weighted estimates near the origin and
\|\nabla\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

Lemma 2.2
.

In the two-dimensional radially symmetric domain
B_{R}
, for any
0<\xi\ll 1
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}\leq C(\xi)\left(1+\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(2.1)

where
C(\xi)
is a positive constant independent of
T
and depending on
R

\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

Using Sobolev’s embedding inequality in one dimension and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\,dr+C\int_{0}^{R}|\partial_{r}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|r^{\xi}\,dr+C\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi-1}\,dr

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi-1}\,dx+C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|r^{\xi-1}\,dx+C\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi-2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}r^{2\xi-2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\frac{2}{\xi}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\xi}{4}}\left(\int_{\Omega}r^{\frac{4\xi-8}{4-\xi}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{4-\xi}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C(\xi)\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{\xi}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C(\xi)\left(\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle=C(\xi)\left(1+\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

In the three-dimensional radially spherically symmetric domain
B_{R}
, for any
0<\xi\ll 1
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}+\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}\leq C(\xi)\left(1+\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(2.3)

where
C(\xi)
is a positive constant independent of
T
and depending on
R
and
\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

Using Sobolev’s embedding inequality in one-dimension and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}+\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{R}{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}r^{\frac{1}{2}+\xi}\,dr+C\int_{0}^{R}{|}\partial_{r}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|r^{\frac{1}{2}+\xi}\,dr+C\int_{0}^{R}{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}r^{\xi-\frac{1}{2}}\,dr

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}r^{\xi-\frac{3}{2}}\,dx+C\int_{\Omega}{|}\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|r^{\xi-\frac{3}{2}}\,dx+C\int_{\Omega}{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}r^{\xi-\frac{5}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}{|}\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}{r^{2\xi-3}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\int_{\Omega}{\rho^{3}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\int_{\Omega}{r^{\frac{6}{5}\xi-3}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{5}{6}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)\left(\int_{\Omega}{|}\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}{|}\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}{r^{\frac{6}{5}\xi-3}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{5}{6}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)(\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+1).

∎

3
A priori estimates: Lower-Order Estimates for
N=2

In this section, we establish lower-order estimates for the two-dimensional non-isentropic viscous shallow water equations. We begin by proving the BD entropy inequality.

3.1
L^{2}
estimates for velocity and effective velocity

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C(T)>0
, dependent of
T
, such that

\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}(u^{2}+\rho^{\gamma-1})\,dy+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}+\rho^{2}(\partial_{y}u)^{2}r^{2}\right)\,dyd\tau\leq C,

(3.1)

\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}(u+2r\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dy+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}(r\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dyd\tau\leq C(T).

(3.2)

Proof.

Multiplying the second equation of (
1.11
) by
ru
, we get basic energy estimate

\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}e^{s}\right)dy+\int_{0}^{1}\left(2\rho^{2}r^{2}(\partial_{y}u)^{2}+\frac{2u^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)dy=0.

(3.3)

Therefore, we prove (
3.1
).

Now multiplying the third equation of (
1.12
) by
w=u+2r\partial_{y}\rho
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}{w^{2}}\,dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}{\partial_{y}P}(ru)\,dy-2\int_{0}^{1}{\partial_{y}P}(r^{2}\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}{P}\partial_{y}(ru)\,dy-2\int_{0}^{1}{\partial_{y}P}\left(r^{2}\partial_{y}\rho\right)\,dy\triangleq H_{1}+H_{2}.

Using (
1.11
)
1
,
H_{1}
is equivalent to

H_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}P\left(-\frac{\partial_{\tau}\rho}{\rho^{2}}\right)dy\\
=-\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}e^{s}\,dy,

and

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\left[\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)+\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}(\partial_{y}s)\right]\cdot(2r^{2}\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy

\displaystyle={-\int_{0}^{1}2\gamma r^{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dy}-\int_{0}^{1}{2r^{2}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}(\partial_{y}s)(\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy}.

So we arrive at

\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}w^{2}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}e^{s}\,dy\right)+\int_{0}^{1}2\gamma r^{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dy=-\int_{0}^{1}2r^{2}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}(\partial_{y}s)(\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy.

(3.4)

Using Young’s inequality, the source term on the RHS can be estimated as follows

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}2r^{2}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}(\partial_{y}s)(\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\gamma r^{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dy+C\int_{0}^{1}r^{2}\rho^{\gamma+1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}s)^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\gamma r^{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dy+C\|\rho r\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}\,dy.

Here we use
e^{\underline{s}}\leq e^{s}\leq e^{\bar{s}}\leq C,\,\,\left\|\partial_{y}s\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\|\partial_{y}s_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
. Substituting this back into (
3.4
), and absorbing the first term into the dissipation on the LHS, we obtain

\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}w^{2}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}e^{s}\,dy\right)+\int_{0}^{1}\gamma r^{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dy\leq C\|\rho r\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2},

(3.5)

The key step in the following process is to estimate
\|\rho r\|_{L^{\infty}}
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

\left(\rho r\right)\left(y\right)=\left(\rho r\right)\left(y\right)-\left(\rho r\right)\left(0\right)\leq\int_{0}^{1}{\left|\left(\rho r\right)_{y}\right|dy}\leq\int_{0}^{1}{\left|\rho_{y}r\right|+\left|r_{y}\rho\right|dy},

(3.6)

so we derive

\displaystyle\|\rho r\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\left\|\rho r_{y}\right\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=C\left(\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|\frac{1}{r}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\right),

where we used the relation
\partial_{y}r=\frac{1}{\rho r}
. Now we estimate each term on the RHS. First, by Hölder’s inequality, we get

\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{1}}^{2}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\rho_{y}r|\,dy\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\rho_{y}r|^{2}\,dy\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}1\,dy\right)=\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.7)

Second, for the term
\|\frac{1}{r}\|_{L^{1}}
, using the coordinate transformation
dy=\rho rdr
, we have

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{r}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int{\frac{1}{r}}\,dy\right)^{2}=\left(\int{\rho}\,dr\right)^{2}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\left(\int{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}r^{\xi}\cdot\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-\xi}\,dr\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}^{2}\left(\int{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}r^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}r^{-\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\xi}\,dr\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}^{2}(\int{\rho^{\gamma}}rdr)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}(\int{r^{-\frac{1}{2\gamma-1}-\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma-1}\xi}}dr)^{\frac{2\gamma-1}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}^{2},

where
\xi
is small enough s.t.

\xi<1-\frac{1}{\gamma}.

By Lemma
2.2
, in two-dimensional space, the weighted estimate at the origin is

\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}\leq C(\xi)(\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}+1)\\
=C(\xi)\left(1+\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\right).\\

Combining (
3.5
)-(
3.8
), we arrive at

\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{1}{2}}w^{2}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}e^{s}}\,dy\right)+\int_{0}^{1}{2}\gamma r^{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}\,dy\\
\leq C(\xi)\left(1+\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).

(3.9)

Combining (
3.3
) and (
3.9
), we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\tau}&\int_{0}^{1}{\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}w^{2}+\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}e^{s}\right)}dy+\int_{0}^{1}{\left(2\rho^{2}r^{2}(\partial_{y}u)^{2}+\frac{2u^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+{2}\gamma r^{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}(\partial_{y}\rho)^{2}}dy\\
&\leq C(\xi)\left(1+\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\\
&\leq C(\xi)\left(1+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(3.10)

Let the total energy functional be defined as

E(\tau)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}w^{2}+\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}e^{\gamma}\right)dy.

(3.11)

Using the definition of the effective velocity
w-u=2r\partial_{y}\rho
, we have the relation

\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}=\|\frac{w-u}{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right)\leq E(\tau).

(3.12)

Substituting this into the derived energy estimate, we obtain the following differential inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}E(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)\left(1+\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)\left(1+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)\left(1+2E(\tau)\right).

Applying Gronwall’s inequality yields the uniform bound for the energy

E(t)\leq\left(E(0)+C(\xi)t\right)e^{2C(\xi)t},\quad\text{for any }t\in[0,T].

(3.14)

This implies (
3.2
). Consequently, we also obtain

\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}\leq C(T),

(3.15)

and

\|\rho_{y}r\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq C(T).

(3.16)

∎

3.2
L^{4}
estimates for velocity and effective velocity

The following property is used to establish the boundedness of
\|\nabla\rho^{1/4}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}
, which is crucial for proving the upper bound of the density.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, dependent of
T
, such that

\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}u^{4}\,dy+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{u^{4}}{r^{2}}+\rho^{2}r^{2}(\partial_{y}u)^{2}u^{2}\right)\,dyd\tau\leq C,

(3.17)

\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}(u+2r\partial_{y}\rho)^{4}\,dy+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}(r\partial_{y}\rho)^{4}\,dyd\tau\leq C.

(3.18)

Proof.

Multiplying the momentum equation by
ru^{3}
and integrating over the spatial domain
[0,1]
, we obtain the following equality

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}{u^{4}}\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}{\left(-\partial_{y}P+\partial_{y}\left(2\rho^{2}\partial_{y}(ru)\right)-\frac{2}{r}\partial_{y}\rho u\right)}ru^{3}\,dy\triangleq J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.

(3.19)

For the pressure term
J_{1}
, we integrate by parts and use
P=\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{y}P)ru^{3}\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}P\partial_{y}(ru^{3})\,dy

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}\left(\frac{u^{3}}{\rho r}+3ru^{2}\partial_{y}u\right)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}u^{3}}{r}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}3\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}ru^{2}\partial_{y}u\,dy.

Using integration by parts and the relation
\partial_{y}r=\frac{1}{\rho r}
, the viscous term can be expanded as

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{y}\left(2\rho^{2}\partial_{y}(ru)\right)ru^{3}\,dy

(3.21)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}2\rho^{2}\partial_{y}(ru)\partial_{y}(ru^{3})\,dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}2\rho^{2}\left(\frac{u}{\rho r}+r\partial_{y}u\right)\left(\frac{u^{3}}{\rho r}+3ru^{2}\partial_{y}u\right)\,dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{2u^{4}}{r^{2}}+8\rho u^{3}\partial_{y}u+6\rho^{2}r^{2}u^{2}(\partial_{y}u)^{2}\right)\,dy.

The source term, after integration by parts on
\partial_{y}\rho
, becomes

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2}{r}(\partial_{y}\rho)u\cdot ru^{3}\,dy=-\int_{0}^{1}2u^{4}\partial_{y}\rho\,dy

(3.22)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\rho\partial_{y}(2u^{4})\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}8\rho u^{3}\partial_{y}u\,dy.

Substituting (
3.20
)-(
3.22
) into (
3.19
), we have

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|u\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{2u^{4}}{r^{2}}+6\rho^{2}r^{2}u^{2}(\partial_{y}u)^{2}\right)\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}u^{3}}{r}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}3\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}ru^{2}\partial_{y}u\,dy.

The first term on the RHS can be estimated as

\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}u^{3}}{r}}dy\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{u^{4}}{r^{2}}dy}+C\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{2\gamma-2}u^{2}}dy.

(3.23)

By applying Young’s inequality to the last term on the RHS

\left|\int_{0}^{1}3\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}ru^{2}\partial_{y}u\,dy\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}6\rho^{2}r^{2}u^{2}(\partial_{y}u)^{2}\,dy+C\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{2\gamma-2}u^{2}\,dy.

Absorbing the dissipation term into the LHS, we finally obtain the desired
L^{4}

estimate

\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|u\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\int_{0}^{1}{\left(\frac{u^{4}}{r^{2}}+\rho^{2}r^{2}u^{2}(\partial_{y}u)^{2}\right)}\,dy&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{2\gamma-2}u^{2}}\,dy\\
&\leq C\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4\gamma-4}\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}}\,dy\leq C\left(T\right),\end{split}

(3.24)

where
\xi
is small enough s.t.

\xi<\frac{1}{2\left(\gamma-1\right)}.

(3.25)

Next, multiplying
\eqref{4.4}_{2}
with
(u+2r\partial_{y}\rho)^{3}
and integrating over the spatial domain
[0,1]
, we obtain

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}w^{4}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}rw^{3}\partial_{y}P\,dy=0.

(3.26)

Since
\partial_{y}P=\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}\partial_{y}\rho+\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}\partial_{y}s
, we arrive at

\int_{0}^{1}rw^{3}\partial_{y}P\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}rw^{3}(\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}rw^{3}(\partial_{y}s)\,dy.

(3.27)

Substituting the relation
\partial_{y}\rho=\frac{w-u}{2r}
into the first term, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}rw^{3}(\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}rw^{3}\left(\frac{w-u}{2r}\right)\,dy

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}w^{4}\,dy-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}uw^{3}\,dy.

Combining (
3.26
)-(
3.28
) we derive the energy equality

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|w\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}w^{4}\,dy=\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}uw^{3}\,dy-\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}rw^{3}(\partial_{y}s)\,dy.

(3.29)

Applying Young’s inequality to the first term on the RHS of (
3.29
)

\left|\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}uw^{3}\,dy\right|\leq\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}w^{4}\,dy+C\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}u^{4}\,dy,

(3.30)

where
\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma-1}}e^{s}u^{4}
can be bounded by

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma-1}}e^{s}u^{4}\,dy&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma-1}}u^{4}\,dy\\
&\leq C\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,R\right)}^{2\gamma-2}\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{u^{4}}{r^{2}}}\,dy\\
&\leq C\left(T\right)\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{u^{4}}{r^{2}}}\,dy.\end{split}

(3.31)

For the second source term in (
3.29
), using the radially weighted estimate (
3.15
), we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma}}e^{s}rw^{3}(\partial_{y}s)\,dy&\leq\frac{\gamma}{8}\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}w^{4}dy}+C\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma+3}r^{4}dy}\\
&\leq\frac{\gamma}{8}\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}w^{4}dy}+C\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\xi}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,R\right)}^{2\gamma+6}\int_{0}^{1}{r^{4-\xi\left(2\gamma+6\right)}dy}\\
&\leq\frac{\gamma}{8}\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}w^{4}dy}+C\left(T,R\right),\end{split}

(3.32)

where
\xi
is small enough s.t.

\xi\leq\frac{2}{\gamma+3}.

Inserting (
3.30
)–(
3.32
) into (
3.29
), we derive

\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\|w\|_{{L^{4}}(0,1)}^{4}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}w^{4}\,dyd\tau\leq C(T).

(3.33)

∎

3.3
Upper bound estimates for the density

The
L^{4}
estimates for the velocity field and the effective velocity allow us to obtain the upper bound of the density.

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, dependent of
T
, such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R)}\leq C(T).

(3.34)

Proof.

Since from (
3.2
) and (
3.18
), we arrive at

\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|r\partial_{y}\rho\right\|_{{L^{4}}(0,1)}^{4}\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|w-u\right\|_{{L^{4}}(0,1)}^{4}\leq\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|w\right\|_{{L^{4}}(0,1)}^{4}+\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|u\right\|_{{L^{4}}(0,1)}^{4}\leq C\left(T\right).

(3.35)

In Euler coordinates, this is equivalent to

\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{R}{\left|\partial_{r}\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}\right|^{4}}r\,dr\leq C(T).

By virtue of the two-dimensional Sobolev embedding, we obtain

\mathop{\mathrm{sup}}\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}+\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}.

(3.36)

Therefore, we get the upper bound of the density.
∎

3.4
L^{\infty}
estimates for velocity and effective velocity

We now deduce the
L^{\infty}
estimates for the velocity and effective velocity fields by combining their
L^{2n}
estimates. This is a crucial step for establishing the density lower bound.

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists a constant
C(T)>0
such that

\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\|w(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C(T).

(3.37)

and

\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\|u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C(T).

(3.38)

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
, multiplying the effective momentum equation
\partial_{\tau}w+r\partial_{y}P=0
by
w^{2n-1}
and integrating over
[0,1]

\frac{1}{2n}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}rw^{2n-1}(\partial_{y}\rho)\,dy=-\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}rw^{2n-1}(\partial_{y}s)\,dy.

Substituting the relation
\partial_{y}\rho=\frac{w-u}{2r}
into the second term on the LHS, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}w^{2n}\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}uw^{2n-1}\,dy

(3.39)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}rw^{2n-1}(\partial_{y}s)\,dy\triangleq G_{1}+G_{2}.

G_{1}
can be estimated as follows

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}{\rho^{\gamma-1}}e^{s}uw^{2n-1}\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(T\right)\|e^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2n}}\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n-1}

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}\right).

Since
s_{y\tau}=0
, we have
\|\partial_{y}s\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|\partial_{y}s_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
.
The term
G_{2}
can be bounded as follows

\left|\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma}e^{s}rw^{2n-1}(\partial_{y}s)\,dy\right|\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\gamma}\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n-1}\leq C(T)(1+\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}).

(3.41)

Combining (
4.2
)-(
3.41
), we arrive at

\frac{1}{2n}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}e^{s}w^{2n}\,dy\leq C\left(T\right)(1+\|w\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2n}}^{2n}).

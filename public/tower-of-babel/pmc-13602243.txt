Emphysematous pyelonephritis in a pelvic ectopic kidney: A rare case report
Authors: Soukaina Bichichi, Mohamed Laghdaf Maouelainin, Aya Yming, Mohamed Amine Idoikrim, Hassan Doulhousne, Abdenasser El kharras, Zakaria Abide
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEmphysematous pyelonephritis (EPN) is a rare, life-threatening necrotizing renal infection characterized by gas formation within the renal parenchyma, collecting system, or perirenal tissues. Its occurrence in a pelvic ectopic kidney is exceptionally uncommon. We report the case of a 56-year-old man with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus who presented with fever, pelvic pain, and septic shock. Computed tomography (CT) demonstrated a left pelvic ectopic kidney with multiple renal calculi and an obstructing ureteral calculus, with gas confined to the collecting system, consistent with Huang and Tseng Class I EPN. Despite emergency management, the patient developed refractory septic shock with multiorgan failure and died 2 days after admission. This case highlights the exceptional association of EPN with a pelvic ectopic kidney and the pivotal role of CT in identifying the anatomical anomaly, classifying disease severity, detecting obstructive urolithiasis, and guiding therapeutic management.Keywords: Emphysematous pyelonephritis, Pelvic ectopic kidney, Computed tomography, Urolithiasis

Introduction

Emphysematous pyelonephritis (EPN) is a rare, severe necrotizing renal infection characterized by the presence of gas within the renal parenchyma, collecting system, or perirenal tissues. It represents a urological emergency because of its potential for rapid progression to severe sepsis and septic shock [1].

Diabetes mellitus and urinary tract obstruction are the 2 major predisposing factors for EPN [1]. However, the occurrence of EPN in a pelvic ectopic kidney is exceedingly rare and poses unique diagnostic and therapeutic challenges due to the complex anatomical features associated with this congenital anomaly.

We report a case of obstructive emphysematous pyelonephritis occurring in a lithiasic pelvic ectopic kidney, highlighting the pivotal role of computed tomography (CT) in establishing an early diagnosis, accurately staging the disease, assessing its severity, and guiding therapeutic management in this unusual anatomical setting.

Case presentation

A 56-year-old man with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus treated with oral hypoglycemic agents was admitted with a 5-day history of fever (39.5°C) and pelvic pain radiating to the left flank, which had progressed over the preceding 24 hours to include deterioration of his general condition and vomiting.

On admission, the patient had altered mental status (Glasgow Coma Scale score: 14/15) and was in septic shock, with hypotension (blood pressure, 82/48 mmHg), tachycardia (heart rate, 128 beats/min), tachypnea (respiratory rate, 30 breaths/min), and a temperature of 39.4°C. Physical examination revealed pelvic tenderness without guarding or a palpable mass.

Laboratory investigations demonstrated marked leukocytosis (22,500/mm3), an elevated C-reactive protein level (325 mg/L), acute kidney injury (serum creatinine, 50 mg/L; blood urea, 1.35 g/L), thrombocytopenia (95,000/mm³), and hyperlactatemia (4.8 mmol/L). Urine culture yielded Escherichia coli susceptible to third-generation cephalosporins.

Because of acute kidney injury, an unenhanced abdominopelvic CT scan was performed. It demonstrated a left pelvic ectopic kidney containing multiple large calculi within the renal pelvis and calyces (Fig. 1). The orthotopic right kidney also harbored a renal pelvic calculus (Fig. 1). A 6.7-mm obstructing left ureteral stone was associated with moderate upstream pelvicalyceal dilatation (Fig. 2). CT further revealed gas confined to the collecting system, associated with marked perinephric fat stranding, without a drainable fluid collection (Fig. 3A and B). These findings were consistent with Huang and Tseng Class I emphysematous pyelonephritis, occurring in a lithiasic pelvic ectopic kidney.

The patient was admitted to the intensive care unit and treated with aggressive fluid resuscitation, empiric broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics, and norepinephrine infusion. Emergency percutaneous nephrostomy was performed for urinary decompression. Despite these interventions, the patient's condition deteriorated rapidly, progressing to refractory septic shock complicated by multiorgan failure, resulting in death 2 days after admission.

Discussion

Emphysematous pyelonephritis is an uncommon but life-threatening necrotizing infection of the kidney, with reported mortality rates ranging from 40% to 90% in the most severe cases [2]. Owing to its potential for rapid progression to septic shock, prompt diagnosis and immediate treatment are essential [3].

EPN typically affects middle-aged patients and demonstrates a marked female predominance [4]. Diabetes mellitus remains the most important predisposing factor, followed by urinary tract obstruction and hypertension [5].

The pathogenesis of EPN involves gas production by gas-forming microorganisms, with E coli being the predominant pathogen. Hyperglycemia, urinary obstruction, impaired tissue perfusion, and tissue ischemia create a favorable environment for bacterial fermentation, leading to gas accumulation, renal parenchymal necrosis, and progressive extension of infection [1,5].

In addition to these well-established risk factors, congenital urinary tract anomalies may predispose patients to complicated urinary tract infections. A pelvic ectopic kidney results from failure of normal embryologic renal ascent [6] and is frequently associated with vascular anomalies, malrotation, and abnormal ureteral insertion, all of which may impair urinary drainage and promote urinary stasis [6]. Its pelvic location also predisposes it to extrinsic compression by adjacent pelvic structures. Together, these anatomical and functional abnormalities contribute to chronic inflammation, recurrent urinary tract infections, and nephrolithiasis [6,7].

In the present case, the coexistence of poorly controlled diabetes mellitus, obstructive ureteral lithiasis, and a pelvic ectopic kidney likely created an ideal setting for the development of emphysematous pyelonephritis.

The clinical presentation of EPN is nonspecific and closely resembles that of acute pyelonephritis. The most common manifestations include fever, flank or abdominal pain, and symptoms of urinary tract infection, accompanied by laboratory abnormalities such as leukocytosis, pyuria, hyperglycemia, and impaired renal function [5].

Imaging plays a pivotal role in the diagnosis of emphysematous pyelonephritis. Plain abdominal radiography may occasionally demonstrate gas projected over the renal fossa but has limited sensitivity. Ultrasonography, often performed as the initial imaging modality, may reveal hyperechoic intrarenal or pelvicalyceal foci associated with reverberation artifacts caused by intrarenal gas [2].

Computed tomography is the imaging modality of choice for diagnosing EPN because of its excellent sensitivity in detecting gas and accurately defining the extent of disease. Huang and Tseng proposed a CT-based classification system with important prognostic and therapeutic implications that remains the most widely used classification system [8]:

Class I: Gas in the collecting system only.

Class II: Parenchymal gas only.

Class IIIA: Extension of gas into the perinephric space.

Class IIIB: Extension of gas into the pararenal space.

Class IV: Emphysematous pyelonephritis in a solitary kidney or bilateral disease.

Management of emphysematous pyelonephritis relies on prompt administration of appropriate antimicrobial therapy combined with urinary drainage when obstruction is present. Treatment strategy is determined by the extent of disease and the presence of poor prognostic factors, while the most severe cases may ultimately require nephrectomy [9].

Conclusion

Emphysematous pyelonephritis occurring in a pelvic ectopic kidney is an exceptionally rare clinical entity that combines a potentially fatal infection with a complex congenital renal anomaly. This case highlights the crucial role of computed tomography, which not only enables rapid diagnosis but also accurately characterizes the underlying anatomical abnormality, delineates the extent of infection, provides prognostic information, and guides appropriate therapeutic management.

On invariants of representations of Weyl groups associated with the cohomology of toric varieties
Tao Gong

Abstract
. For a Weyl group W W and a W W -permutohedron P P , there are associated toric varieties X P X_{P} and X P / W K X_{P/W_{K}} for any parabolic subgroup W K W_{K} of W W , since the quotient P / W K P/W_{K} can be identified with a polytope inside P P . We construct an explicit algebra isomorphism between H ∗ ​ ( X P / W K , ℚ ) H^{*}(X_{P/W_{K}};\mathbb{Q}) and H ∗ ​ ( X P , ℚ ) W K H^{*}(X_{P};\mathbb{Q})^{W_{K}} . We further generalize this isomorphism to intermediate lattices, to finite Coxeter groups, and to non-degenerate W W -symmetric polytopes. Our results give affirmative answers to two open questions of Horiguchi–Masuda–Shareshain–Song.

1.
Introduction

In a (real) Euclidean space
\left(V,\left\langle-,-\right\rangle\right)
of dimension
n
,
let
R
be a reduced crystallographic root system with fixed simple system
S=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
.
For a subset
K\subset S
, let
C_{K}
denote the closed polyhedral cone consisting of vectors whose inner products with the roots in
K
are non-negative.
The Weyl group
W
of
R
is generated by reflections across the hyperplanes whose normal vectors are the simple roots in
S
.
Let
P_{\lambda}
be a
W
-permutohedron
, that is,
P_{\lambda}=\conv\left(W(\lambda)\right)
where
\lambda
lies in the relative interior of
C_{S}
.
Furthermore, we assume that
\lambda
lies in the root lattice. Then

P_{\lambda}
is a simple integral polytope in the real span of the root lattice and defines a toric variety
X_{P_{\lambda}}
.
The Weyl group
W
acts on
P_{\lambda}
, and hence on the toric variety
X_{P_{\lambda}}
and its rational cohomology ring
H^{*}(X_{P_{\lambda}};\mathbb{Q})
.
This representation has been studied in
[
16
,
20
,
7
,
14
]
, among other works.

The
parabolic subgroup

W_{K}
of
W
determined by the subset
K
of
S
also acts on
P_{\lambda}
.
The quotient
P_{\lambda}/W_{K}
can be identified with the polytope
P_{\lambda}\cap C_{K}
.
It is therefore associated with a toric variety
X_{P_{\lambda}/W_{K}}
.
The relation between rational cohomology rings
H^{*}(X_{P_{\lambda}};\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{*}(X_{P_{\lambda}/W_{K}};\mathbb{Q})
has attracted considerable attention.
Horiguchi–Masuda–Shareshain–Song
[
11
]
explicitly constructed an algebra isomorphism between
H^{*}(X_{P_{\lambda}/W_{K}};\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{*}(X_{P_{\lambda}};\mathbb{Q})^{W_{K}}
for
R
of classical Lie types, and asked whether such an isomorphism exists for other root systems
[
11
, Questions 8.1, 8.2]
.

These questions were previously addressed by Song
[
17
]
for rank-two root systems, who also proved the isomorphism for
W
-symmetric polygons, and by Gui–Hu–Liu
[
9
]
for arbitrary root systems in the case
W_{K}=W
.
In this paper, we provide a complete affirmative answer to these questions.
One result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any parabolic subgroup
W_{K}
, there is an explicit ring isomorphism

H^{*}(X_{P_{\lambda}/W_{K}};\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(X_{P_{\lambda}};\mathbb{Q})^{W_{K}}.

Our result in
Theorem

1.1
relies on the fact that a toric variety can be constructed from a rational polytope (or, more generally, when the normal cones are rational) with respect to a lattice structure in
V
; see
[
6
, §5]
.
We also use the well-known description of the rational cohomology ring of a toric variety due to Danilov
[
6
]
and Jurkiewicz
[
13
]
, which requires the polytope to be
n
-dimensional and
simple
(i.e., each vertex lies in exactly
n
facets).

We now put everything in a more general setting.
Let
M
be a lattice lying between the root lattice and the weight lattice.
In terms of
M
, we consider a full-dimensional
W
-symmetric rational polytope
P_{\Lambda}
. Then
P_{\Lambda}=\conv\left(W(\Lambda)\right)
with
\Lambda
a finite set of vertices lying in
C_{S}
and in the rational span of
M
.
We call
P_{\Lambda}

non-degenerate
if no vertex of
P_{\Lambda}
lies on the boundary of
C_{S}
, and
degenerate
otherwise.
The quotient
P_{\Lambda}/W_{K}
is identified with the polytope
P_{\Lambda}\cap C_{K}
and is associated with the toric variety
X_{P_{\Lambda}/W_{K}}
.
One of the main results of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

For a simple non-degenerate
W
-symmetric polytope
P_{\Lambda}
and any parabolic subgroup
W_{K}
, there is an explicit ring isomorphism

H^{*}(X_{P_{\Lambda}/W_{K}};\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(X_{P_{\Lambda}};\mathbb{Q})^{W_{K}},

which holds for any intermediate lattice

.

See
Eq.

4.3
for the explicit description of the isomorphism in
Theorem

1.2
, and
Proposition

3.2
for its independence of the choice of

.
By taking
\Lambda
to be a singleton and
M
to be the root lattice, one obtains
Theorem

1.1
as a special case of
Theorem

1.2
.

Remark 1.3
.

In fact,

and

in

are equivalent in certain respects.

2.
Face structures & Stanley–Reisner rings

In this section, we consider a reduced root system
R
in the Euclidean space
V
, not necessarily crystallographic.
We describe the face structures of a full-dimensional
W
-symmetric polytope
P_{\Lambda}
and its quotient polytope
P_{\Lambda}/W_{K}
.
The non-degenerate case when
\Lambda
is a singleton (i.e.,
P_{\Lambda}=P_{\lambda}
) was studied in
[
8
, §§3,5]
, which we follow and generalize here.
We also discuss the Stanley–Reisner rings of these polytopes.
Background on root systems and Weyl groups can be found in
[
12
, Part I]
.
When there is no ambiguity, we write
P
instead of
P_{\Lambda}
.

Recall that
S
consists of
n
simple roots
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}
, and that
K
is a subset of
S
.
We identify
S
with the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and
K
with the corresponding subset.
Each root
\alpha\in R
defines a reflection
r_{\alpha}\in O(V)
given by

r_{\alpha}(x)=x-\frac{2\left\langle x,\alpha\right\rangle}{\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle}\alpha.

The reflection
r_{\alpha}
fixes a hyperplane pointwise, which we denote by
H_{\alpha}
.
For a simple root
\alpha_{i}\in S
, we write
r_{i}=r_{\alpha_{i}}
and
H_{i}=H_{\alpha_{i}}
.
The parabolic subgroup
W_{K}
is generated by the simple reflections
\{r_{k}:\,k\in K\}
.
In particular,
W_{S}=W
. We denote the identity element of
W
by
e
.

Define the (closed)
fundamental chamber

\displaystyle C_{K}:=\left\{x\in V:\,\left\langle x,\alpha_{k}\right\rangle\geq 0,\,\,\forall k\in K\right\}.

The fundamental chamber satisfies several important properties, summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
12
, §1.12]
)
.

(1)

The composite
C_{K}\hookrightarrow V\to V/W_{K}
is a homeomorphism.

(2)

The stabilizer of
x\in C_{K}
is generated by the simple reflections
r_{k}
for which
r_{k}(x)=x
.

(3)

For
s\in W_{K}
and
x\in C_{K}
, the difference
s(x)-x
lies in the non-positive span of the simple roots corresponding to the minimal parabolic subgroup of
W_{K}
containing
s
.

Recall that
P=\conv\left(W(\Lambda)\right)
, where
\Lambda
is a finite set of vertices lying in
C_{S}
. By
Theorem

2.1
.
(1)
, we identify
P/W_{K}
with the polytope
P\cap C_{K}
.
For a facet
F
of
P
,
P/W_{K}
, or
C_{S}
, we denote by
\ell_{F}
an outward-pointing normal vector of
F
.
In particular, for
i\in S
, the vector
\ell_{H_{i}}
is a negative multiple of
\alpha_{i}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
F
be a face of
P
, and
r_{\alpha}\in W
be the reflection for some
\alpha\in R
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The reflection
r_{\alpha}
preserves
F
, that is,
r_{\alpha}(F)=F
.

(2)

The hyperplane
H_{\alpha}
passes through the barycenter of
F
.

(3)

The hyperplane
H_{\alpha}
passes through a relative interior point of
F
.

When
P
is non-degenerate, there is an additional equivalent condition:

(4)

The face
F
intersects the hyperplane
H_{\alpha}
.

Under this condition, we have
\left\langle\ell_{F^{\prime}},\alpha\right\rangle=0
for any facet
F^{\prime}
of
P
containing
F
.

Proof.

The implications
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
are immediate. We need to show that
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: the relative interior point where
F
and
H_{\alpha}
meet is also contained in the face
r_{\alpha}(F)
. It follows that
r_{\alpha}(F)=F
.

When
P
is non-degenerate, it suffices to show
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
. Indeed,
F\cap r_{\alpha}(F)\cap H_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset
implies that
r_{\alpha}(F)=F
since no vertex lies in
H_{\alpha}
.

The facet
F^{\prime}
intersects
H_{i}
. Then
r_{\alpha}(F^{\prime})=F^{\prime}
and
\ell_{F^{\prime}}=r_{\alpha}(\ell_{F^{\prime}})=\ell_{F^{\prime}}-\frac{2\left\langle\ell_{F^{\prime}},\alpha\right\rangle}{\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle}\alpha
.
Thus,
\left\langle\ell_{F^{\prime}},\alpha\right\rangle=0
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

The equivalences
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
in
Lemma

2.2
were stated for non-degenerate
P_{\lambda}
in
[
21
, §3]
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{K}
denote the set of facets of
P
that have barycenters lying in
C_{K}
.
By
Theorem

2.1
.(1), each facet of
P
is
W_{K}
-conjugate to a unique facet in
\mathcal{F}_{K}
.
For a facet
F
of
P
, let
W_{F}
denote the stabilizer of
F
under the
W_{K}
-action.
We then have the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Define
\mathcal{F}:=\left\{(F,s):\,F\in\mathcal{F}_{K},\,s\in{W_{K}}/{W_{F}}\right\}
.

(1)

There is a bijection from
\mathcal{F}_{K}\sqcup K
to the set of facets of
P/W_{K}
given by

\displaystyle F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\mapsto F\cap C_{K},\quad k\in K\mapsto H_{k}\cap P.

(2)

There is a bijection from
\mathcal{F}
to the set of facets of
P
given by

\displaystyle(F,s)\mapsto s(F).

We next prove a lemma that helps to clarify the face structure of
P
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
.

(1)

The stabilizer
W_{F}
is generated by all simple reflections
r_{k}\in W_{K}
that fix the barycenter of
F
.

(2)

If
P
is non-degenerate, then for every
s\in W_{K}
, the intersection
s(F)\cap F
is either
F
or empty.

Proof.

(1)
. If
r\in W_{K}
satisfies
r(F)=F
, then
r
fixes the barycenter of
F
. The conclusion therefore follows from
Theorem

2.1
.
(2)
and
Lemma

2.2
.

(2)
. Suppose that
s(F)\cap F\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists
t\in W_{K}
such that
ts(F)\cap t(F)\cap C_{K}\neq\emptyset
. By
Lemma

2.2
, both
ts(F)
and
t(F)
lie in
\mathcal{F}_{K}
.
It follows from
Proposition

2.4
.
(2)
that
ts(F)=t(F)=F
, and hence
s(F)=F
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of
Proposition

2.4
and
Lemma

2.5
, every face of
P
can be written as an intersection of distinct facets

s_{1}(F_{1})\cap s_{2}(F_{2})\cap\cdots\cap s_{p}(F_{p}),

where
F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
and
s_{i}\in W_{K}/W_{F_{i}}
. There is no repetition among those
F_{i}
satisfying
F_{i}\cap\partial C_{K}=\emptyset
. If
P
is non-degenerate, then there is no repetition among any of the
F_{i}
, and the intersection is
W_{K}
-conjugate to

F_{1}\cap F_{2}\cap\cdots\cap F_{p}.

A polytope is called
flag
if any collection of its facets has non-empty intersection whenever every pair of facets in the collection has non-empty intersection.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that
P
is non-degenerate.

(1)

The polytope
P
is simple if and only if
P/W_{K}
is simple.

(2)

If
P
is flag, then so is
P/W_{K}
.

Proof.

(1)
. Suppose that
P
is simple and a vertex of
P/W_{K}
is contained in facets determined, via
Proposition

2.4
.
(1)
, by some maximal subsets
K^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}_{K}
and
K^{\prime\prime}\subset K
. Then
|K^{\prime}|+|K^{\prime\prime}|\geq n
, and

\displaystyle n=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\ell_{F_{i}}:F_{i}\in K^{\prime}\right)\right)+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\ell_{H_{j}}:j\in K^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)=|K^{\prime}|+|K^{\prime\prime}|,

where the first equality holds because the two spans are orthogonal complements of each other, by
Lemma

2.2
. Hence
P/W_{K}
is simple.

Conversely, suppose that
P/W_{K}
is simple. Each vertex of
P
is
W_{K}
-conjugate to a vertex of
P/W_{K}
, which is also the intersection of exactly
n
facets from
\mathcal{F}_{K}
.
Hence
P
is simple.

(2)
. Suppose that
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{p}
and
H_{k_{1}},H_{k_{2}},\ldots,H_{k_{q}}
support facets of
P/W_{K}
such that every two of them have non-empty intersection.
Then
F:=F_{1}\cap F_{2}\cap\cdots\cap F_{p}
is a non-empty face of
P
.
Moreover, each
H_{k_{j}}
, for
1\leq j\leq q
, passes through the barycenter of
F
.
Therefore,

F\cap H_{k_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap H_{k_{q}}\neq\emptyset
, and hence
P/W_{K}
is flag.
∎

Definition 2.9
.

Let

be a polytope and

a field. The

of

is defined as

\mathcal{SR}(Q):=\frac{\mathbb{F}[X_{F}:\,\mbox{$F$ is a facet of $Q$}]}{I},

where the ideal

X_{F_{1}}X_{F_{2}}\cdots X_{F_{p}}

Consequently, if

\mathcal{SR}(P)=\frac{\mathbb{F}[X_{F,s}:\,(F,s)\in\mathcal{F}]}{I_{P}}

\displaystyle W_{K}\times\mathbb{F}[X_{F,s}:\,(F,s)\in\mathcal{F}]

\displaystyle\left(t,X_{F,s}\right)

with

monomials

Proof.

Proposition 5.2
.

(1)

The polytopal algebra
\mathcal{A}(Q)
is defined independently from the models of the given root system.

(2)

Suppose that there is a lattice in

to define

. There is a natural algebra isomorphism

which assigns each generator
X_{F}
to a multiple of it.

(3)

When

is crystallographic, the above assignment induces isomorphisms
\mathcal{A}(P)\cong H^{*}(X_{P};\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{A}(P/W_{K})\cong H^{*}(X_{P/W_{K}};\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

When the type of
(R,S)
is fixed, one can obtain another model

of the same type by adjusting

; see
[

. In fact,

is obtained from

by scaling the simple roots or by applying an orthogonal transformation of

.

For
(1)
,
(2)
, and
(3)
, we obtain isomorphic algebras by adjusting the ideal

(specifically, the
\eta_{i}
) in
\mathcal{SR}(Q)
. The argument is then analogous to that in the proof of
Proposition

.
∎

We describe the polytopal algebras of

where the ideal

is generated by

\sum_{(F,s)\in\mathcal{F}}\left\langle\alpha_{i},\ell_{s(F)}\right\rangle X_{F,s}\qquad\mbox{for all}\,\,\alpha_{i}\in S.

The algebra

inherits the

\mathcal{SR}(P)
described in

.

•

The polytopal algebra
\mathcal{SR}(P/W_{K})
is

\mathcal{A}(P/W_{K})=\frac{\mathcal{SR}(P/W_{K})}{J_{K}}=\frac{\mathbb{R}[X_{F,e},Y_{k}:\,F\in\mathcal{F}_{K},\,k\in K]}{I_{K}+J_{K}},

where the ideal

Remark 5.3
.

There are similar definitions of

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
Q
is simple.
In
\mathcal{A}(Q)
, all square-free monomials
X_{F_{1}}{X_{F_{2}}}\cdots{X_{F_{n}}}
, with
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\cap\cdots\cap F_{n}\neq\emptyset
, represent the same element up to a positive scalar.

Proof.

Suppose that
F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{{n-1}},F_{n}
are distinct facets of
Q
such that
F_{0}\cap F_{1}\cap\cdots\cap F_{{n-1}}\neq\emptyset
,
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\cap\cdots\cap F_{{n}}\neq\emptyset
.
It suffices to show that
X_{F_{0}}X_{F_{1}}\cdots X_{F_{{n-1}}}=c\cdot X_{F_{1}}X_{F_{2}}\cdots X_{F_{{n}}}\in\mathcal{A}(Q)
for some positive number
c
. Indeed, each vertex of
Q
corresponds to a square-free monomial, and any two vertices are are connected by edges of the form
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\cap\cdots\cap F_{n-1}
.

Clearly,
\ell_{F_{0}}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}c_{i}\cdot\ell_{F_{j}}
for some coefficients
c_{j}
.
We claim that
c_{n}<0
; otherwise, the normal cones spanned by

\ell_{F_{0}},\ldots,\ell_{F_{n-1}}
and by

\ell_{F_{1}},\ldots,\ell_{F_{n}}

would intersect in the relative interiors of both cones.
Choose
u
in the real span of
S
such that
\left\langle u,\ell_{F_{n}}\right\rangle=1
and
\left\langle u,\ell_{F_{j}}\right\rangle=1
for
1\leq j<n
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle{X_{F_{n}}}+c_{n}X_{F_{0}}+\sum_{F}\left\langle u,\ell_{F}\right\rangle X_{F}\in J,

where
F
ranges over all other facets of
Q
.
Since
Q
is simple, these facets do not intersect

F_{1}\cap\cdots\cap F_{n-1}
.
Multiplying the above relation by

X_{F_{1}}\cdots X_{F_{n-1}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle X_{F_{1}}{X_{F_{2}}}\cdots X_{F_{{n-1}}}{X_{F_{n}}}=-c_{n}X_{F_{0}}X_{F_{1}}\cdots X_{F_{{n-1}}}\in\mathcal{A}(Q).

Since
c_{n}<0
, the scalar
-c_{n}
is positive.
∎

Let
f(Q)=(f_{-1},f_{0},\ldots,f_{n-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
denote the
f
-vector
of the polytope
Q
,
where
f_{i}
is the number of faces of codimension
i+1
.
The
h
-vector

h(Q)=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})
and the
h
-polynomial

h_{Q}(t)
are defined by

\displaystyle h_{Q}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}h_{i}t^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i-1}t^{i}(1-t)^{n-i}.

Note that the
\mathbb{R}
-basis of
\mathcal{SR}(Q)
consists of all monomials
X_{F_{1}}^{n_{1}}X_{F_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdots X_{F_{p}}^{n_{p}}
with
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\cap\cdots\cap F_{p}\neq\emptyset
.
Thus, the Hilbert series of
\mathcal{SR}(Q)
is

\displaystyle\sum_{F}\frac{t^{\mathrm{codim}(F)}}{(1-t)^{\mathrm{codim}(F)}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i-1}\frac{t^{i}}{(1-t)^{i}}=\frac{h_{Q}(t)}{(1-t)^{n}},

where
F
ranges over all faces of
Q
.

Lemma 5.5
.

If
Q
is simple, then
\mathcal{A}(Q)
is a Poincaré duality algebra over
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The
n
linear forms
\eta_{i}
in

5.1
are a
linear system of parameters
of
\mathcal{SR}(Q)
(
[
18
, remark (p.150)]
).
Since
\mathcal{SR}(Q)
is
Cohen-Macaulay
, the
\eta_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
are algebraically independent (
[
19
, Corollary II.4.4]

). Thus, there is an
\mathbb{R}
-module isomorphism (
[
19
, Corollary I.5.10]
)

\mathcal{SR}(Q)\cong\mathcal{A}(Q)\otimes\mathbb{R}[\theta_{i}:1\leq i\leq n].

Consequently,
h_{Q}(t)
is the Hilbert series of
\mathcal{A}(Q)
.
By
[

, Theorem II.1.3, Corollary II.5.2, p. 67]
,
the

-vector

satisfies

for all

. In particular,

.
Finally, by
[

∎

6.1.
On isomorphisms

It is not straightforward to generalize

1.4
to the degenerate simple

-symmetric polytopes

, because the face structures of

When the dimension

is 2, the
W
-symmetric polygon

has a relatively easy face structure. Song
[
17
]
classified
W
-symmetric polygons to study the corresponding toric varieties, when they exist.
More explicitly, a
W
-symmetric polygon

is classified by the size of
\Lambda\cap\partial C_{K}
, which can be 0, 1, or 2.
This classification determines and is determined by three possible shapes of

, shown in

.

. The orange area indicates

Theorem 6.1
.

For a
W
-symmetric polygon
P_{\Lambda}
and any parabolic subgroup
W_{K}
,
there is an explicit ring isomorphism

\mathcal{A}(P_{\Lambda}/W_{K})\cong\mathcal{A}(P_{\Lambda})^{W_{K}}.

Proof.

The map
\psi:\mathcal{A}(P/W_{K})\to\mathcal{A}(P)^{W_{K}}
is constructed as in
Eq.

5.2
.
Repeating the arguments in
Section

4
and
Section

5
, we have that:
the range of
\psi
is well-defined;
\psi
maps
J_{K}
of
\mathcal{SR}(P/W_{K})
to 0;
\mathcal{A}(P/W_{K})
and
\mathcal{A}(P)
are both Poincaré duality algebras with top degree 2; and
(\mathcal{A}^{2}(P))^{W_{K}}=\mathbb{R}
.

We now verify that
Lemma

4.2
holds, i.e., that
\psi
maps
I_{K}
of
\mathcal{SR}(P/W_{K})
to 0.
It suffices to show that
\psi(X_{F,e}Y_{1})=0
when
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
and
H_{1}
are disjoint and support edges of
P/W_{K}
.
One has

\displaystyle\psi\left(X_{F,e}Y_{1}\right)=\sum_{s\in{W_{K}}/{W_{F}}}s\left(\sum_{(F^{\prime},t)\in\mathcal{F}}C_{F^{\prime},t,1}X_{F,e}X_{F^{\prime},t}\right).

The only possible case for
X_{F,e}X_{F^{\prime},t}\neq 0
with
t(F^{\prime})\neq F^{\prime}
is when
F
intersects
H_{2}
(if
H_{2}
supports
\partial C_{K}
). Then either
r_{2}(F)=t(F^{\prime})
or
r_{2}(F)=F
. Hence
t=r_{2}
and
C_{F^{\prime},t,1}=0
whenever
X_{F,e}X_{F^{\prime},t}\neq 0
.

To complete the proof, it remains to show that
\psi
is surjective; then
Lemma

4.6
implies that
\psi
is an isomorphism. Since
\mathcal{A}^{0}(P)=\mathcal{A}^{2}(P)=\mathbb{R}
, it suffices to show that
(\mathcal{A}^{1}(P))^{W_{K}}
is generated by the elements
\sum_{s\in W_{K}/W_{F}}X_{F,s}
for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
. Clearly, these elements generate
(\mathcal{SR}^{1}(P))^{W_{K}}
, and hence also
(\mathcal{A}^{1}(P))^{W_{K}}
.
∎

So we would like to ask:

Question 1

(cf.
[
11
, Question 8.2]
)
.

For which
W
-symmetric polytopes does the isomorphism in
Theorem

1.4
hold?

We are closer to understanding the 3-dimensional case, as part of
Proposition

2.6
holds as follows:

Lemma 6.2
.

If a 3-dimensional
W
-symmetric polytope
P
is simple, then so is the quotient polytope
P/W_{K}
.

Proof.

Suppose that a vertex of
P/W_{K}
is contained in at most the following facets:
F_{1},\ldots,F_{p}\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
(
1\leq p\leq 3
) and
H_{k_{1}},\ldots,H_{k_{q}}
with
k_{i}\in K
(
1\leq q\leq 3
, if possible). We rule out all cases below to complete the proof.

The case
q=3
is impossible, since
H_{1}\cap H_{2}\cap H_{3}=\{0\}
and the facets
F_{i}
do not contain the origin.

The case
p=3
and
q\geq 1
is impossible. Suppose that
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
, and
H_{1}
meet at a vertex. Since
\left\langle\ell_{H_{1}},\ell_{F_{i}}\right\rangle\neq 0
for some
i
, we may assume that
r_{1}(F_{1})\neq F_{1}
. Because
P
is simple, we have that
r_{1}(F_{1})
must be either
F_{2}
or
F_{3}
, which implies that
F_{2}
or
F_{3}
does not lie in
\mathcal{F}_{K}
.

The remaining case is
p=q=2
. Since
\left\langle\ell_{H_{i}},\ell_{F_{j}}\right\rangle\neq 0
for some
i,j
, we may assume that
r_{1}(F_{1})\neq F_{1}
. Then
r_{1}(F_{1})
,
F_{1}
, and
F_{2}
meet.
We then have
r_{2}(F_{1})=F_{1}
and
r_{1}(F_{2})=r_{2}(F_{2})=F_{2}
. Hence
H_{1}\cap H_{2}\cap F_{2}
is the barycenter of
F_{2}
, which cannot lie in
F_{1}
.
∎

.

Quantifying Overfitting along the Regularization Path for Two-Part-Code MDL in Supervised Classification
Xiaohan Zhu, Nathan Srebro

Abstract
We provide a complete characterization of the entire regularization curve of a modified two-part-code Minimum Description Length (MDL) learning rule for binary classification, based on an arbitrary prior or description language. Grünwald and Langford (2004) previously established the lack of asymptotic consistency, from an agnostic PAC (frequentist worst case) perspective, of the MDL rule with a penalty parameter of λ = 1 \lambda=1 , suggesting that it underegularizes. Driven by interest in understanding how benign or catastrophic under-regularization and overfitting might be, we obtain a precise quantitative description of the worst case limiting error as a function of the regularization parameter λ \lambda and noise level (or approximation error), significantly tightening the analysis of Grünwald and Langford for λ = 1 \lambda=1 and extending it to all other choices of λ \lambda .

Formal Setup

We consider a supervised binary classification problem where we observe
m
i.i.d samples
S\sim\mathcal{D}^{m}
from a source distribution
\mathcal{D}
over
(X,Y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\{0,1\}
, where
\mathcal{X}
is some measurable space and
Y
is a binary label. A “predictor” (aka classifier) is a (measurable) mapping
h:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
and we are interested in its population error
L(h)=L_{D}(h)=\mathbb{P}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}}(h(x)\neq y)
(we frequently omit the distribution
D
when it is clear from context). We also denote
L_{S}(h)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbbm{1}\{h(x_{i})\neq y_{i}\}
the empirical error (i.e. training error) on
S
.

We consider learning rules based on a given “prior”
\pi:\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow[0,1]
over predictors such that
\sum_{h}\pi(h)\leq 1
. We can think of this prior as a discrete distribution over predictors (if
\sum_{h}\pi(h)\leq 1
the remaining probability mass can be thought to be absorbed in some alternate predictor), but we never sample from it or assume anything is sampled from it. We can equivalently
3
3

3

Every prefix-unambiguous description language specifies a valid prior with
\pi(h)=2^{-\left\lvert{h}\right\rvert_{\pi}}
(by Kraft’s inequality), while for every valid prior there is a description language with
-\log\pi(h)\leq\left\lvert{h}\right\rvert_{\pi}\leq-\log\pi(h)+1

(e.g.
Cover and Thomas, 2006
, Section 5.2–5.3)
.
view the prior
\pi(h)
as corresponding to a description length
\left\lvert{h}\right\rvert_{\pi}
of predictors in some prefix-unambiguous description language with
\pi(h)=2^{-\left\lvert{h}\right\rvert_{\pi}}
. Maximizing
\pi
is thus the same as minimizing the description length
\left\lvert{h}\right\rvert_{\pi}
. Either way,
\pi
will have finite or countable support (i.e. the description language can describe finite or countably many predictors), and for any countable class of predictors we can construct a prior assigning positive probability to all predictors in the class. Formally, we denote
\left\lvert{h}\right\rvert_{\pi}=-\log\pi(h)
.

For a given prior
\pi
(or equivalently, description language) and regularization parameter
\lambda
we consider the modified Minimum Description Length learning rule:

\displaystyle\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}(S)=\underset{h\in\mathcal{H}}{\text{arg min }}J_{\lambda}(h,S),\text{ such that }L_{S}(h)\leq 1/2,

(3)

where

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(h,S)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left\lvert{h}\right\rvert_{\pi}+\log{m\choose mL_{S}(h)}=\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(h,S)-\tilde{\Delta},\text{ with }

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(h,S)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left\lvert{h}\right\rvert_{\pi}+mH(L_{S}(h))\text{ and }0\leq\tilde{\Delta}\leq\log(m+1).

(4)

with the equality following from Stirling’s approximation. We can also use the approximate form to define the alternative and very similar rule:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textnormal{MDL}}_{\lambda}(S)=\underset{h}{\text{arg min }}\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(h,S)\text{ such that }L_{S}(h)\leq 1/2.

All the results in the paper, including both upper and lower bounds, also apply to the
\widetilde{\textnormal{MDL}}_{\lambda}
learning rule, which has the same limiting behaviour as
\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}
.

The standard MDL is then a special case of
\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}
with
\lambda=1
. We denote it as
\textnormal{MDL}_{1}
. Notice that when we define
\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}
, we require
L_{S}(h)\leq 1/2
. This is because otherwise we are not preferring a predictor with very low error
L

1-L

\pi(h)=\pi(1-h)

Notation

7
Contrast with Well-Specified Case

It is interesting to contrast the agnostic setting studied above to a well-specified setting, where the noise is a result of random label noise. Formally, a source distribution
D
is well specified if
Y|X=Y|h^{*}(X)

(that is,
Y\perp X|h^{*}(X)
), which means that

Y|X=h^{*}(X)\oplus\text{Ber}(L^{*})
for some Bayes optimal predictor
h^{*}
and independent Bernoulli noise. Note that this condition is not satisfied in any of the hard problem constructions of our lower bound proofs. In other words, all the source distributions in our lower bound proofs are mis-specified.
In fact, in the well-specified case, as already noted by
Grünwald and Langford (2004)
,
\lambda=1
leads to asymptotic consistency, following the classical analysis of MDL
(
Barron and Cover, 1991
)
. However, as is well understood in the MDL literature
(
Zhang, 2004
, e.g.)
, this consistency does not enjoy a uniform rate or finite sample guarantee. In our language, it provides an upper bound on the left-side expression in
Corollary 3.2.1
, where we take the limit
m\rightarrow\infty
separately for each prior
\pi
and source
D
, but not the right-side expression where we first take the limit
m\rightarrow\infty
.
For the right-side expression in
Corollary 3.2.1
, even in the well-specified case and with
\lambda=1
, we can obtain an upper bound of
2L^{*}(1-L^{*})>L^{*}
9
9

9

This uniform upper bound can be obtained from the weak convergence result from
Zhang (2004)
by choosing a particular test function
f(x,y)=\mathbb{P}_{y^{\prime}|x\sim p_{h^{*},L^{*}}}(y\neq y^{\prime}|x)
.
, and also show that this “uniform” limiting error is strictly larger than
L^{*}
. In this sense, we have tempered behavior, with a better tempering function
2L^{*}(1-L^{*})<\ell_{1}(L^{*})
than the agnostic case we focus on in this paper. It would be interesting to understand this problem further: what is best uniform rate with
\lambda=1
? Is this tempering function tight? What is the uniform and non-uniform limiting error when
\lambda>1
, and with
\lambda<1
? Is there a discontinuity at
\lambda=1
?

8
Summary and Discussion

In this paper, we provided a tight analysis, with matching upper bounds and worst-case lower bounds, on the limiting error of
\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}
, for any
0<\lambda<\infty
. This improves both the lower and upper bounds over
Grünwald and Langford (2004)
for the special case
\lambda=1
, and generalizes to any
\lambda
.

We also characterize the behavior as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
and
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
, with a gap between
\lambda=\Theta(m/\log(m))
and
\lambda=\Theta(m)
. This
\log
-factor comes from the
\log
factor in the Binomial tail bound (see
Appendix C
), which also appears in all PAC-Bayes bounds and in many SRM-type bounds based on
\log\pi
. We do not know if this log-factor can be avoided, and it could be interesting to characterize the fine grained behavior at this transition.

Our analysis does not assume any structure on the prior, and so can be thought of as the “baseline” or absolute worst case overfitting behavior. For many specific priors, and perhaps for special classes of source distributions, we know that even with
\lambda=0
one can get tempered, or even benign behavior. This work can serve as a basis for understanding overfitting for specific priors.

m\gtrsim\max\{\frac{2\lambda}{1-H(L(h^{*}))}\log^{2}(\frac{2\lambda}{1-H(L(h^{*}))}),\frac{4}{(1-H(L(h^{*})))^{2}}\log^{3}(\frac{4}{(1-H(L(h^{*})))^{2}})\}
such that
\Delta<\frac{1}{2}(1-H(L(h^{*})))
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}L(\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}(S))

\displaystyle\leq U_{\lambda}^{-1}\bigg(H(L(h^{*}))+\Delta\bigg)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}

(55)

\displaystyle=U_{\lambda}^{-1}(H(L(h^{*})))+\frac{1}{U_{\lambda}^{\prime}(U_{\lambda}^{-1}(\xi))}\Delta+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}

(56)

\displaystyle\leq U_{\lambda}^{-1}(H(L(h^{*})))+\frac{1}{\min\left(c,\frac{2}{\ln 2}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}\right)}\Delta+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}

(57)

\displaystyle=U_{\lambda}^{-1}(H(L(h^{*})))+O\left(\frac{1}{(1-2L(h^{*}))^{2}}\cdot\left(\lambda\left(\frac{\left\lvert{h^{*}}\right\rvert_{\pi}+\log m}{m}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{\log^{3}(m)}{m}}\right)\right).

(58)

On the other hand, if
m
is small such that
\Delta\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-H(L(h^{*})))
, then by Taylor expansion,
1-H(L(h^{*}))>\frac{2}{\ln 2}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}
. But then the right hand side of (
57
)
\geq\frac{1}{\frac{2}{\ln 2}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}\Delta\geq\frac{1}{\frac{2}{\ln 2}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}(1-H(L(h^{*})))>\frac{1}{\frac{2}{\ln 2}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{\ln 2}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}
. As a result, the bound is vacuously true.

Therefore, the bound (
58
) holds for any
m
. This completes the proof of
Theorem 3.1
.
∎

Next, we prove the finite sample guarantee of
Theorem 3.4
, and then use it to derive the consistency result when
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
presented in
Corollary 3.4.1
.

A.2
Proof of
Theorem 3.4
and
Corollary 3.4.1

Theorem 3.4
.

For any predictor
h^{*}
, source distribution
D
, valid prior
\pi
, and any
\lambda>1
and
m
:

\underset{S\sim D^{m}}{\mathbb{E}}[L(\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}(S))]\leq L(h^{*})+O\left(\frac{1}{1-2L(h^{*})}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\lambda\left(\frac{\left\lvert{h^{*}}\right\rvert_{\pi}+\log m}{m}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{\log^{3}(m)}{m}}\right)\right),

(59)

where
O(\cdot)
only hides an absolute constant, that does not depend on
D,\pi
or anything else.

Proof.

we first prove
Lemma 5.2
which quantifies how close the binary entropy function
H(q)
is to the function
U_{\lambda}(q)=\lambda\cdot\textsf{KL}(\frac{1}{1+(\frac{1-q}{q})^{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}}}\|q)+H(\frac{1}{1+(\frac{1-q}{q})^{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}}})
for
\lambda>1
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\lambda>1
and any
0\leq q\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
H(q)<U_{\lambda}(q)+\lambda/(\lambda-1)^{2}
.

Proof.

of
Lemma 5.2
:

Letting
p^{*}=\frac{1}{1+(\frac{1-q}{q})^{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}}}
, and
U_{\lambda}(q)=\lambda\cdot\textsf{KL}(p^{*}\|q)+H(p^{*})
and

\log\frac{p^{*}}{1-p^{*}}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\log\frac{q}{1-q}.

(60)

Note that for
\lambda>1
, we have
0<p^{*}<q\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Denote
\delta=q-p^{*}>0
, and denote the function
\phi(q)=\log\frac{q}{1-q}
. Then the relationship between
p^{*}
and
q
in (
60
) can be rewritten as

\phi(p^{*})=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\phi(q).

(61)

Note that
\phi(q)<0
for
q\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
, and its first-order derivative
\phi^{\prime}(q)=\frac{1}{q(1-q)}>0
is positive and monotonically decreasing on
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. Hence, by the mean value theorem and monotonicity of the derivative of
\phi
, we have

\begin{split}\phi(q)-\phi(p^{*})&=\phi^{\prime}(\xi_{0})\cdot\delta\text{ , for some }\xi_{0}\in(p^{*},q)\\
&\geq\phi^{\prime}(q)\cdot\delta=\frac{1}{q(1-q)}\cdot\delta.\end{split}

(62)

Plugging (
61
) into (
62
), we get an upper bound for
\delta
in terms of
q
such that

\delta\leq-\frac{q(1-q)}{\lambda-1}\phi(q).

(63)

Note that
H^{\prime}(q)=\log\frac{1-q}{q}\geq 0
is positive and monotonically decreasing on
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
, so by mean value theorem,

\begin{split}H(q)-H(p^{*})&=H^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\cdot\delta\text{ , for some }\xi_{1}\in(p^{*},q)\\
&\leq H^{\prime}(p^{*})\cdot\delta=\log\frac{1-p^{*}}{p^{*}}\cdot\delta=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\log\frac{1-q}{q}\cdot\delta,\end{split}

(64)

where the last equality follows from (
61
).

By plugging the upper bound (
63
) for
\delta
into (
64
), we get an upper bound for
H(q)-H(p^{*})
, and thus also an upper bound for
H(q)-U_{\lambda}(q)
:

\begin{split}H(q)-U_{\lambda}(q)&=H(q)-H(p^{*})-\lambda\textsf{KL}(p^{*}\|q)\leq H(q)-H(p^{*})\\
&\leq\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\log\frac{1-q}{q}\cdot\left(-\frac{q(1-q)}{\lambda-1}\phi(q)\right)=\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}q(1-q)\phi^{2}(q).\end{split}

Using the fact that
q(1-q)\phi^{2}(q)<1
for
q\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
, we prove the desired result
H(q)-U_{\lambda}(q)<\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}
. This completes the proof of
Lemma 5.2
.
∎

Combining
Lemma 5.2
with inequality (
46
), we have with probability
\geq 1-\delta
,

H(L(\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}(S)))\leq H(L(h^{*}))+C\sqrt{\frac{2(\log m)^{2}\cdot\log\frac{1}{\delta/2}}{m}}+{\lambda}\frac{\log(\frac{m+1}{\delta/2})}{m}+{\lambda}\frac{\left\lvert{h^{*}}\right\rvert_{\pi}}{m}+\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}.

(65)

Taking
\delta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
yields that with probability
\geq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
, for some constant
C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime}
,

H(L(\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}(S)))\leq H(L(h^{*}))+C^{\prime}\frac{(\log m)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{m}}+{\lambda}C^{\prime\prime}\frac{\log m}{m}+{\lambda}\frac{\left\lvert{h^{*}}\right\rvert_{\pi}}{m}+\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}.

(66)

Let
\Delta=C^{\prime}\frac{(\log m)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{m}}+{\lambda}C^{\prime\prime}\frac{\log m}{m}+\lambda\frac{\left\lvert{h^{*}}\right\rvert_{\pi}}{m}+\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}
. If the right hand side of (
66
)
H(L(h^{*}))+\Delta\leq 1
, then it is well-defined to take the inverse function
H^{-1}
on both sides of (
66
) to yield that with probability
\geq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
,

\displaystyle L(\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}(S))

\displaystyle\leq H^{-1}\left(H(L(h^{*}))+\Delta\right).

(67)

By the mean value theorem, we have

\begin{split}H^{-1}(H(L(h^{*}))+\Delta)&=H^{-1}(H(L(h^{*})))+(H^{-1})^{\prime}(\xi)\Delta\\
&=L(h^{*})+(H^{-1})^{\prime}(\xi)\Delta,\end{split}

(68)

for some
\xi\in(H(L(h^{*})),H(L(h^{*}))+\Delta)
.

Because the entropy function
H
is concave, the inverse function
H^{-1}
is convex on
(0,\frac{1}{2})
. By the convexity of
H^{-1}
, the derivative
(H^{-1})^{\prime}(\xi)
is always upper bounded by the slope of the line interpolating the two points
(H(L(h^{*})),L(h^{*}))
and
(1,\frac{1}{2})
, i.e.

(H^{-1})^{\prime}(\xi)\leq\frac{\frac{1}{2}-L(h^{*})}{1-H(L(h^{*}))}.

(69)

Combining (
67
), (
68
), and (
69
), we get with probability
\geq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
,

L(\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}(S))\leq L(h^{*})+\frac{\frac{1}{2}-L(h^{*})}{1-H(L(h^{*}))}\Delta.

(70)

Note that although we assume
H(L(h^{*}))+\Delta\leq 1
and take
H^{-1}
inverse function to get (
70
), when
H(L(h^{*}))+\Delta>1
, the bound (
70
) is vacuously true. Indeed, if
H(L(h^{*}))+\Delta>1
, then
\Delta>1-H(L(h^{*}))
, and thus

\frac{\frac{1}{2}-L(h^{*})}{1-H(L(h^{*}))}\Delta>\frac{1}{2}-L(h^{*})
and the right hand side of (
70
)
>\frac{1}{2}
. In this case, (
70
) vacuously holds. Hence, (
70
) holds for any
L(h^{*})
and
\Delta
.

By Taylor expansion of
H(L(h^{*}))
around
\frac{1}{2}
, we get
H(L(h^{*}))=H(\frac{1}{2})+H^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2})(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{H^{\prime\prime}(\frac{1}{2})}{2}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+\frac{H^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)}{6}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{3}
for some
\xi\in(L(h^{*}),\frac{1}{2})
. Since
H(\frac{1}{2})=H^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2})=0
,
H^{\prime\prime}(\frac{1}{2})=-\frac{4}{\ln 2}
, and
H^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)>0,\forall\xi<\frac{1}{2}
, this gives us
1-H(L(h^{*}))\geq\frac{2}{\ln}(L(h^{*})-\frac{1}{2})^{2}
. Hence,
\frac{\frac{1}{2}-L(h^{*})}{1-H(L(h^{*}))}\leq\frac{\ln 2}{1-2L(h^{*})}
.

Since the risk is bounded, the high probability bound (
70
) implies the bound on expected risk:

\displaystyle\underset{S\sim\mathcal{D}^{m}}{\mathbb{E}}[L(\textnormal{MDL}_{\lambda}(S))]

\displaystyle\leq L(h^{*})+\frac{\frac{1}{2}-L(h^{*})}{1-H(L(h^{*}))}\Delta+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}

(71)

\displaystyle\leq L(h^{*})+O\left(\frac{1}{1-2L(h^{*})}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\lambda\left(\frac{\left\lvert{h^{*}}\right\rvert_{\pi}+\log m}{m}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{\log^{3}(m)}{m}}\right)\right).

(72)

This completes the proof of
Theorem 3.4
.
∎

to derive the consistency result as shown in the

Theorem 3.2

For any

We first prove that

Lemma B.1

Proof.

of

Now we give a proof of inequality (

B.1.1
0<\lambda\leq 1

Proof.

We first prove the following claim:

Claim:

of the claim:

Theorem 3.3
.

Consider the same source distribution described in

, and with the same prior. It is easy to see that the probability

(1)
\lambda=0

(2)
\lambda_{m}\rightarrow 0

Appendix C
Tight Bounds on the Binomial CDF, and the Minimum of i.i.d Binomials, in terms of KL-Divergence

In both our upper and lower bounds, we rely on a tight bound on the minimum of i.i.d (scaled) Binomials. These follow standard union bound arguments applied to a tight version of Sanov’s Theorem, as presented in an accompanying note
Zhu et al. [2025]
, and reproduce here for completeness.

We first provide a tight upper
and
lower bound on the binomial tail:

Lemma C.1

(Binomial tail)
.

Let
X\sim\frac{1}{n}\textnormal{Bin}(n,p)
be a scaled Binomial random variable. Then for
a\leq p
,

\log\mathbb{P}(X\leq a)\in-n\textsf{KL}(a\|p)\pm\left(4\log(n+1)+\left[\log\frac{p}{1-p}\right]_{+}\right),

where
\textsf{KL}(\alpha\|\beta)
denotes
\textsf{KL}(Ber(\alpha)\|Ber(\beta))=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+(1-\alpha)\log\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\beta}
.

Proof.

We write
X=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where
X_{i}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\textnormal{Ber}(p)
, and so
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}
is a sequence of
n
symbols from the alphabet
\mathcal{X}=\{0,1\}
with type
(1-X,X)
. Denote the true distribution
Q=\textnormal{Ber}(p)
.

The upper bound follows directly from Sanov’s theorem
[
Cover and Thomas, 2006
]
:

\log\mathbb{P}(X\leq a)\leq-n\textsf{KL}(a\|p)+2\log(n+1).

(86)

To get a finite sample lower bound, we round
a
to a multiple of
1/n
. That is, let
k=\lfloor an\rfloor
and
\tilde{a}=k/n
, so that
a-1/n<\tilde{a}\leq a
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}=\{(P(0),P(1)):(\frac{0}{n},\frac{n}{n}),(\frac{1}{n},\frac{n-1}{n}),\cdots,(\frac{n}{n},\frac{0}{n})\}
be the set of types with denominator
n
, and
E=\{P:P(1)\leq a\}
. Then the type
P_{\tilde{a}}=(1-\tilde{a},\tilde{a})
lies in the intersection
E\cap\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

Given the type
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, let
T(P)=\{x\in\mathcal{X}^{n}:P_{x}=P\}
denote the type class of
P
, which is the set of sequences of length
n
and and type
P
. Then, by adapting equations (11.104) to (11.106) in the lower bound proof of
Cover and Thomas [2006]
, we have:

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(X\leq a)=Q^{n}(E)&=\sum_{P\in E\cap\mathcal{P}_{n}}Q^{n}\left(T\left(P\right)\right)\\
&\geq Q^{n}\left(T\left(P_{\tilde{a}}\right)\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}2^{-n\textsf{KL}(\tilde{a}\|p)}.\end{split}

Taking the logarithm on both sides yields:

\log\mathbb{P}(X\leq a)\geq-2\log(n+1)-n\textsf{KL}(\tilde{a}\|p).

(*)

Since
a-\tilde{a}<1/n
,
H(a)-H(\tilde{a})<H(\frac{1}{n})<\frac{2}{n}\log n
. This implies that
\textsf{KL}(\tilde{a}\|p)-\textsf{KL}(a\|p)=(a-\tilde{a})\log\frac{p}{1-p}+H(a)-H(\tilde{a})\leq\frac{1}{n}\left[\log\frac{p}{1-p}\right]_{+}+\frac{2}{n}\log n
. Plugging this in the inequality (
*
‣
C
) yields

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{P}(X\leq a)

\displaystyle\geq-2\log(n+1)-n\textsf{KL}(\tilde{a}\|p)

\displaystyle\geq-2\log(n+1)-\left(n\textsf{KL}(a\|p)+2\log n+\left[\log\frac{p}{1-p}\right]_{+}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-n\textsf{KL}(a\|p)-4\log(n+1)-\left[\log\frac{p}{1-p}\right]_{+}.

(87)

The upper bound (
86
) and lower bound (
87
) together yield the desired result.
∎

Next, we use the finite sample bound on the Binomial CDF to prove the following concentration bounds of the minimum of i.i.d Binomials in terms of KL divergence.

Theorem C.1

(minimum of i.i.d Binomial)
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\frac{1}{m}\textnormal{Bin}(m,p)
,
Z=\min_{i=1,\cdots,r}X_{i}
. Given fixed confidence parameter
\delta\in(0,1)
, let
\Delta(\delta,p,m)=\log\frac{1}{\delta/2}+4\log(m+1)+\left[\log\frac{p}{1-p}\right]_{+}
. If
\Delta(\delta,p,m)<\log r
, then with probability
1-\delta
, we have

Z<p\text{, and }\textsf{KL}(Z\|p)\in\frac{\log r\pm\Delta(\delta,p,m)}{m},

except that if
\textsf{KL}(0\|p)<\frac{\log r-\Delta(\delta,p,m)}{m}
, then with probability
1-\delta
,
Z=0
.

Proof.

Consider any interval
[a,b]
, such that
a\leq b<p
. Define the following events:

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\textsf{KL}(Z\|p)\leq\textsf{KL}(a\|p)\},

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\textsf{KL}(Z\|p)\geq\textsf{KL}(b\|p)\},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{Z\geq a\},\textrm{ and}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{Z\leq b\}.

a
b
p
B
A
U
L

By the monotonicity of the KL divergence, we have that
B\subseteq L
and
A\cap B\subseteq U
(but note that we generally
don’t
have
A\subseteq U
). This means that
A\cap B\subseteq U\cap L
, and consequently:

\mathbb{P}(U\cap L)\geq\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)=1-\mathbb{P}(A^{\mathsf{c}})-\mathbb{P}(B^{\mathsf{c}}).

The theorem will follow from choices of
a
and
b
that help bound
\mathbb{P}(A^{\mathsf{c}})
and
\mathbb{P}(B^{\mathsf{c}})
.

Using the fact that
a<p
, along with the union bound and
Lemma C.1
, we have

\mathbb{P}(A^{\mathsf{c}})=\mathbb{P}(Z<a)\leq\mathbb{P}(Z\leq a)\leq r\cdot\mathbb{P}(X_{1}\leq a)\leq r\cdot 2^{-m\textsf{KL}(a\|p)+4\log(m+1)+\left[\log\frac{p}{1-p}\right]_{+}}.

Suppose
\textsf{KL}(0\|p)\geq\frac{\log r+\Delta(\delta,p,m)}{m}
. Since
\textsf{KL}(p\|p)=0
, and KL is continuous by its first argument, by intermediate value theorem, we can choose
0\leq a<p
such that

\begin{split}\textsf{KL}(a\|p)&=\frac{\log r+\Delta(\delta,p,m)}{m}\\
&=\frac{\log r+\log\frac{1}{\delta/2}+4\log(m+1)+\left[\log\frac{p}{1-p}\right]_{+}}{m},\end{split}

(88)

which gives

.

according to (

), we get

.

If
\textsf{KL}(0\|p)<\frac{\log r+\Delta(\delta,p,m)}{m}
, in other words, there is no

satisfying (

), then take

. And in this case, the upper bound of the theorem trivially holds for any

because

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\textsf{KL}(b\|p)\leq\textsf{KL}(Z\|p)\leq\textsf{KL}(0\|p)<\frac{\log r+\Delta(\delta,p,m)}{m}\right)

On the other hand, by the independence of data points, we have:

\mathbb{P}(B^{\mathsf{c}})=\mathbb{P}(Z>b)=(1-\mathbb{P}(X_{1}\leq b))^{r}.

Using the inequality

, we have

(1-\mathbb{P}(X_{1}\leq b))^{r}\leq\exp\left(-r\cdot\mathbb{P}(X_{1}\leq b)\right)\leq\exp\left(-r\cdot 2^{-m\textsf{KL}(b\|p)-4\log(m+1)-\left[\log\frac{p}{1-p}\right]_{+}}\right).

which gives

.

0\leq b<p
according to (

\mathbb{P}(B^{\mathsf{c}})\leq\frac{\delta}{2}

If

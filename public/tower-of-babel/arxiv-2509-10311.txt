Structure-Preserving High-Order Methods for the Compressible Euler Equations in Potential Temperature Formulation for Atmospheric Flows
Marco Artiano, Oswald Knoth, Peter Spichtinger, Hendrik Ranocha

Abstract
We develop structure-preserving numerical methods for the compressible Euler equations, employing potential temperature as a prognostic variable. We construct three numerical fluxes designed to ensure the conservation of entropy and total energy within the discontinuous Galerkin framework on general curvilinear meshes. Furthermore, we introduce a generalization for the kinetic energy preservation property and total energy conservation in the presence of a gravitational potential term. To this end, we adopt a flux-differencing approach for the discretization of the source term, treated as non-conservative product. We present well-balanced schemes for different constant background states for both formulations (total energy and potential temperature) on curvilinear meshes. Finally, we validate the methods by comparing the potential temperature formulation with the traditional Euler equations formulation across a range of classical atmospheric scenarios.

Introduction

Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods have gained significant attention for hyperbolic equations due to their ability to accurately capture complex fluid and wave phenomena in applications such as atmospheric physics. Their compact formulation enables excellent parallel scalability, making them suitable for high-resolution simulations
[
39
]
.
However, DG methods often require additional stabilization when handling discontinuities or under-resolved features. Especially in atmospheric simulations, to keep stability for long-time simulations, filters are applied
[
24
]
, as well as other classical techniques such as over-integration (dealiasing)
[
38
]
and artificial viscosity
[
62
,
68
]
. While effective, these approaches can reduce accuracy and require parameter tuning.
To address these issues, entropy-stable (ES) DG schemes have been developed, offering high-order accuracy with guaranteed nonlinear stability without requiring additional parameters
[
63
,
57
,
37
,
67
]
.
An essential ingredient of these high-order DG schemes is the implementation of carefully constructed two-point fluxes for the volume terms, an approach pioneered in the context of DG methods by Carpenter et al.
[
8
,
9
]
, Fisher et al.
[
19
]
, and Gassner et al.
[
21
]
.

The compressible Euler equations admit several formulations, and it is not entirely clear whether one formulation offers significant advantages over the others.
In the computational fluid dynamics (CFD) community, considerable effort has been devoted to the development of structure-preserving schemes using the total energy as state variable
[
30
,
29
,
10
,
43
,
45
,

,
57
,
37
,

]
.
In contrast, the use of potential temperature is especially popular in atmospheric models, where it also plays an important theoretical role in meteorology. Giraldo et al.
[
24
]
and more recently Girfoglio et al.
[
25
]
have analyzed different formulations for DG and spectral element methods (SEMs) and different pressure-based solvers for the finite volume framework, respectively. In atmospheric applications, the presence of the gravitational potential is crucial for designing well-balanced schemes that preserve the hydrostatic equilibrium
[

,

,

]
. Additional desirable properties in the flux-differencing framework include the preservation of kinetic and potential energy (KPEP), as introduced by Souza et al.
[

In this work, we focus on the potential temperature formulation to investigate structure-preserving properties, in particular pressure equilibrium preserving (PEP). We design new two-point fluxes that guarantee conservation of either total energy (TEC) or thermodynamic entropy (EC). Building on the ideas in

Furthermore, we extend them to the DGSEM

2
Total energy and entropy-conservative fluxes

The compressible Euler equations with the total energy as conservative variable read

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\nabla\cdot\left(\varrho\boldsymbol{V}\right)=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho\boldsymbol{V})+\nabla\cdot(\varrho\boldsymbol{V}\otimes\boldsymbol{V})+\nabla p=-\varrho\nabla\phi,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho E)+\nabla\cdot\left((\varrho E+p)\boldsymbol{V}\right)=-\varrho\boldsymbol{V}\cdot\nabla\phi,

where
\varrho
is the density,
\boldsymbol{V}
the velocity,
\varrho E
is the sum of kinetic and internal energy,
p
the pressure, and
\phi=\phi(x,y,z)
is the gravitational potential.
We use an ideal gas equation of state, i.e.,

\varrho E=\frac{p}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}\varrho\left\lVert\boldsymbol{V}\right\rVert^{2}
,
where
\gamma
is the ratio of specific heats.
In the context of local weather prediction and numerical methods for atmospheric applications, the evolution of
\varrho E
is often substituted with the evolution of the potential temperature
\theta
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\nabla\cdot\left(\varrho\boldsymbol{V}\right)=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho\boldsymbol{V})+\nabla\cdot(\varrho\boldsymbol{V}\otimes\boldsymbol{V})+\nabla p=-\varrho\nabla\phi,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho\theta)+\nabla\cdot\left(\varrho\theta\boldsymbol{V}\right)=0,

where
p=p_{0}\left(R\varrho\theta/p_{0}\right)^{\gamma}
,
R
is the ideal gas law constant, and
p_{0}
is the atmospheric pressure, which we assume to be
100\,000\text{\,}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}
throughout this study.
For smooth solutions, the conservative parts of the systems of equations (
1
) and (
2
) induce additional scalar conservation laws, which are of the form

\partial_{t}U+\nabla\cdot F\left(U\right)=0,

(3)

where
U
is a mathematical entropy functional and
F
the corresponding flux. For the total energy formulation (
1
), we use the thermodynamic entropy

U_{\varrho s}=\varrho s=\varrho\log\left(\frac{p}{\varrho^{\gamma}}\right).

(4)

Usually, this quantity is set as an entropy functional for the derivation of entropy-conservative (EC) or entropy-stable (ES) two-point fluxes.
However, considering the conservative part of the system of our interest (
2
),
\varrho E
(which coincides with the total energy in the absence of the geopotential term) is not a linear invariant. Therefore, we are interested in the construction of two-point numerical fluxes when either the thermodynamic entropy or the total energy are treated as entropy functional, i.e., we consider the mathematical entropies

U_{\varrho s}=\varrho s\quad\text{or}\quad U_{\varrho E}=\varrho E.

(5)

2.1
Entropy analysis

We consider the systems in 1D without gravity (the generalization to multiple dimensions is straightforward), i.e., hyperbolic conservation laws of the form

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}+\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})=0.

(6)

A semi-discrete finite volume (FV) method for (

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}+\frac{1}{\Delta x}\big(\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i+1})-\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i-1},\boldsymbol{u}_{i})\big)=0.

(7)

From hereinafter, if not stated otherwise, we always consider the right interface at the

\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}:=\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i+1})

\displaystyle(\text{jump})

\displaystyle\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket\log{a}\right\rrbracket},

\displaystyle(\text{logarithmic mean, cf.\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ismail2009affordable}{}{}]}})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{a_{i+1}a_{i}},

\displaystyle(\text{geometric mean})

\displaystyle\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\frac{\left\llbracket a^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket a^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket},

\displaystyle(\text{Stolarsky mean, cf.\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Winters2020}{}{}]}})

with the well-known properties

\left\llbracket ab\right\rrbracket=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket b\right\rrbracket+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{b\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket,\quad\left\llbracket a^{2}\right\rrbracket=2\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad\left\llbracket 1/a\right\rrbracket=-\frac{\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{geo}}^{2}}.

Let
(U,F)
denote a mathematical entropy-entropy flux pair for (

).

Definition 2.1

(Tadmor
[
59
,
58
]
)
.

A two-point numerical flux
\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}
is EC for a given entropy
U
if

\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\omega}^{T}\right\rrbracket\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}-\left\llbracket\psi\right\rrbracket=0,

(10)

where
\boldsymbol{\omega}=U^{\prime}
are the entropy variables and
\psi=\boldsymbol{\omega}^{T}\boldsymbol{f}-F
is the flux potential.

It is of particular interest that the Hessian of the total energy and of the thermodynamic entropy, when expressed with respect to
\boldsymbol{u}=\left(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta\right)
, are given by

U_{\varrho E}^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})=\begin{bmatrix}m^{2}/\varrho^{3}&-m/\varrho^{2}&0\\
-m/\varrho^{2}&1/\varrho&0\\
0&0&K\gamma(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-2}\end{bmatrix},\quad U_{\varrho s}^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\gamma}{\varrho}&0&-\frac{\gamma}{\varrho\theta}\\
0&0&0\\
-\frac{\gamma}{\varrho\theta}&0&\frac{\gamma\varrho}{(\varrho\theta)^{2}}\end{bmatrix},

(11)

with eigenvalues

\lambda_{\varrho E,1}=0,\quad\lambda_{\varrho E,2}=\frac{m^{2}+\varrho^{2}}{\varrho^{3}},\quad\lambda_{\varrho E,3}=K\gamma(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-2},

(12)

\lambda_{\varrho s,1}=\lambda_{\varrho s,2}=0,\quad\lambda_{\varrho s,3}=\frac{\gamma}{\varrho}+\gamma\frac{\varrho}{\varrho\theta}.

(13)

Both Hessians are positive semidefinite, which implies that the entropy functionals are merely convex. As a consequence, the mapping between the conserved and the entropy variables is no longer one-to-one
[
26
]
, in both cases. Nonetheless, strict convexity is not required for Tadmor’s analysis to hold, as the conditions stated in Definition
2.1
are still valid.
Therefore, this does not preclude the development of total energy-conservative (TEC) and thermodynamic entropy-conservative (EC) numerical fluxes, presented in the next sections. On the other hand, the conditions of Barth’s Theorem
[
4
]
cannot be directly applied to uniquely determine a dissipation operator
[
65
]
; as a result the construction of such operators is not straightforward.

2.2
Derivation of a TEC flux

Next we use the total energy
U_{\varrho E}=\varrho E
as mathematical entropy for the system (
2
) without gravity. Thus, given the set of conservative variables
\boldsymbol{u}=(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)
, we look for a numerical flux mimicking at the discrete level the conservation of the total energy, given in (
1
).
By introducing the conserved variables

\boldsymbol{u}=(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)=\left(\varrho,m,\varrho\theta\right),

(14)

we can rewrite the mathematical entropy (total energy) and the corresponding flux as

U_{\varrho E}=\varrho E=K\frac{(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\varrho},\qquad F_{\varrho E}=\left(U_{\varrho E}+K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\right)\frac{m}{\varrho},

(15)

where
K=p_{0}(R/p_{0})^{\gamma}
. Thus, the mathematical entropy variables and the associated flux potential are

\boldsymbol{\omega}^{T}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\varrho^{2}},\frac{m}{\varrho},\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\right),\qquad\psi=K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\frac{m}{\varrho}.

(16)

Applying the general Tadmor EC condition (
10
) to
U_{\varrho E}=\varrho E
, we obtain

-\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket\frac{m^{2}}{\varrho^{2}}\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}+\left\llbracket\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}+\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}K\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}-K\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\rrbracket=0,

(17)

and we refer to (
17
) as the total energy-conservative (TEC) condition.
To derive a TEC flux, we compute the jumps of the entropy variables in (
17
) by recursively applying the discrete operators (
8
) and (
9
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\psi\right\rrbracket=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+K\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left(\left\llbracket m\right\rrbracket\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{1}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{m\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket\varrho\right\rrbracket}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{geo}}^{2}}\right),

(18)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\omega_{1}\right\rrbracket=

\displaystyle-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left(\left\llbracket m\right\rrbracket\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{1}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{m\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket\varrho\right\rrbracket}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{geo}}^{2}}\right),

(19)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\omega_{2}\right\rrbracket=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket m\right\rrbracket\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{1}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{m\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\llbracket\varrho\right\rrbracket}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{geo}}^{2}},

(20)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\omega_{3}\right\rrbracket=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}K\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket.

(21)

By substituting all terms into the entropy conservation condition and requiring that each coefficient of the jumps vanishes, we obtain

\begin{gathered}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{m\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{m\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}+K\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{m\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}=0,\\

By substituting the second into the first equation, one can verify that it is always satisfied, leading to a degree of freedom in the density flux.
Hence, we can state the following result:

Theorem 2.2
.

A numerical flux for the compressible Euler equations in potential temperature formulation satisfying

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\qquad f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\frac{m}{\varrho}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},

(23)

where
f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}
is any consistent and symmetric discretization of
m=\varrho v

We now turn our attention to the derivation of thermodynamic entropy-conservative numerical fluxes and, in particular, how to choose the degree of freedom given by the density flux.

2.3
Conservation of the thermodynamic entropy

In the development of EC fluxes for compressible Euler equations, a common approach involves deriving these fluxes so that
\varrho s
is conserved at a semi-discrete level
[

10

29
]

This is the first step towards the development of ES fluxes

In contrast to the previous section, where the total energy has been treated as a mathematical entropy, we focus on the thermodynamic entropy and derive numerical fluxes that conserve it at the semi-discrete level. Since the procedure is analogous to the previous section, we highlight only the main steps in the derivation. We start considering the set of variables
\boldsymbol{u}=(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)
and the entropy functional
U_{\varrho s}
.
The associated entropy variables are

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\varrho s}^{T}=\left(\log\bigl(\frac{K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}}{\varrho^{\gamma}}\bigr)-\gamma,0,\gamma\frac{\varrho}{\varrho\theta}\right).

(24)

Following the same steps for the TEC flux, the potential flux for this case reduces to
\psi=0

f^{\mathrm{num}}=(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta})

\left\llbracket\log\left(\frac{\varrho}{\varrho\theta}\right)\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}-\left\llbracket\frac{\varrho}{\varrho\theta}\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=0.

(25)

We refer to (
25

Recalling that in the numerical TEC fluxes, the numerical density flux constituted a degree of freedom, we can employ this EC condition to derive two new numerical fluxes. The first is to use the density flux coming from (

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\frac{\left\llbracket\frac{\varrho}{\varrho\theta}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket\log\left(\frac{\varrho}{\varrho\theta}\right)\right\rrbracket}f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{1/\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}.

3
Two-point flux structure-preserving properties of
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)

In this section we keep our analysis to the one-dimensional case

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}\varrho\\
\varrho v\\
\varrho\theta\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{x}\begin{pmatrix}\varrho v\\
\varrho v^{2}+p\\
\varrho\theta v\end{pmatrix}=\boldsymbol{0},

(32)

First we recall the definition of fundamental and desirable properties of two-point numerical fluxes for the compressible Euler equations.

Definition 3.1

(Kinetic energy preservation
[
30
,
36
,
44
,
46
,
45
]
)
.

A numerical flux
f^{\mathrm{num}}=(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta})
is KEP if

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}.

(33)

The pressure equilibrium preserving (PEP) property, given the relation between pressure and potential temperature, has to be reformulated in terms of the new conserved variables. Following
[
45
]
, we introduce the following definition for the potential temperature.

Definition 3.2
.

A numerical flux
f^{\mathrm{num}}=(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta})
is PEP if

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho,v}=vf^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}+\text{const}(pv),\qquad f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\text{const}(\varrho\theta,v),

(34)

provided that velocity
v
and pressure
p
are constant throughout the domain.

It is straightforward to prove that the TEC and ETEC numerical fluxes derived in the previous section are PEP.
The above definition is motivated by the following

Lemma 3.3
.

Pressure equilibrium, i.e.,
p\equiv\text{const}
and
v\equiv\text{const}
is preserved by the FV semi-discretization if and only if the numerical flux is PEP.

Proof.

The pressure equation written in terms of the conserved variables
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)
is

\partial_{t}p=\partial_{t}(K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma})=\gamma(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}K\partial_{t}(\varrho\theta).

(35)

Recalling that
\partial_{t}(\varrho\theta)=-\partial_{x}(\varrho\theta v)
and plugging into the previous equation,

\partial_{t}(p)=-\gamma K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\partial_{x}(\varrho\theta u).

(36)

A semi-discretization of (
36
) leads to

\partial_{t}p_{i}=-\gamma K(\varrho\theta)_{i}^{\gamma-1}\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i+1},\boldsymbol{u}_{i})-f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i-1})\right).

(37)

On the other hand, the semidiscrete evolution equation for the velocity is
[
45
]

\varrho_{i}\partial_{t}v_{i}=\partial_{t}\varrho_{i}v_{i}=-\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i+1},\boldsymbol{u}_{i})-f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i-1})-v_{i}\left(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i+1},\boldsymbol{u}_{i})-f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i-1})\right)\right).

(38)

Thus,
\partial_{t}v_{i}=0
and
\partial_{t}p_{i}=0
if and only if (
34
) is satisfied.
∎

3.1
Characterization of fluxes

Our first goal is to show that it is impossible to construct a numerical flux that is EC, TEC, KEP, PEP, and has no influence of the pressure in the density flux for the set of variables
(\varrho,\varrho u,\varrho\theta)
. In other words, when dealing with the potential temperature as primary invariant, the influence of the pressure term in the density flux cannot be avoided if TEC and EC are desired property for the numerical scheme, which can be formulated with the following

Theorem 3.4
.

For the compressible Euler equations in potential temperature formulation (
32
), the ETEC flux (
30
)
is EC, TEC, KEP and PEP. Moreover, it is the only numerical flux with these
properties for
v\equiv\text{const}
.

Remark
.

Note that the numerical flux of Theorem
3.4
has a density flux
influenced by the pressure, which may cause the density to assume negative values
[
16
]
.

To prove Theorem
3.4
, we derive the necessary conditions for EC, TEC, and PEP.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
p=\text{const}
,
v=\text{const}
, any consistent and symmetric numerical flux
f^{\mathrm{num}}=(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta})
is TEC and KEP.

Proof.

If
p=\text{const}
, then
\varrho\theta=\text{const}
. Thus, the TEC condition becomes

-\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket v^{2}\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}+\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}+\frac{\gamma K}{\gamma-1}\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}-K\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}v\right\rrbracket=0,

(39)

and it is always satisfied for any numerical flux, given
p=\text{const}
and
v=\text{const}
, since all the jumps vanish.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For
p=\text{const}
,
v=\text{const}
, an EC and PEP numerical flux
f^{\mathrm{num}}
satisfies

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}^{-1}\varrho\theta.

(40)

Proof.

It follows directly from the EC condition (
25
), when
p=\text{const}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For
p=const
,
v=const
, an EC numerical flux that is also PEP or KEP must be of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}v\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}v+p\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\varrho\theta v\end{aligned}\right.

(41)

Proof.

Comparing the EC condition (
25
) with the PEP (
34
) or KEP (
33
), the density flux
f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}
is independent of the potential temperature, hence

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}v,

(42)

and inserting the EC and KEP property the numerical flux has to be of the form (
41
).
∎

Note that since for
p=\text{const}
and
v=\text{const}
, TEC requires only a consistent and symmetric numerical flux, these numerical fluxes also satisfy the TEC condition (
17
).

Lemma 3.8
.

For
v=\text{const}
, an EC and PEP numerical flux for which the density flux does not depend on the pressure must be of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}v\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}v+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{1/\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}}v\end{aligned}\right.

(43)

Proof.

Due to Lemma
3.7
, the general form of the dependencies on
\varrho
for
p\equiv\text{const}
is already determined. The remaining degree of freedom for non-constant pressure
p
can be described by two functions
\varphi_{1,2}
, resulting in the numerical fluxes

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}v\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}v+\varphi_{1}(\varrho_{\pm},p_{\pm})\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta_{\pm})v\end{aligned}\right.

(44)

where
\varphi_{1}(\varrho_{\pm},p_{\pm})
and
\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta_{\pm})
are some kind of mean values depending on
\varrho_{\pm}
,
p_{\pm}
,
\varrho\theta_{\pm}
such that

\displaystyle\forall\varrho_{\pm},p>0

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},p,p)

\displaystyle=p,

(45)

\displaystyle\forall\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta>0

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},\varrho\theta,\varrho\theta)

\displaystyle=\varrho\theta.

For the KEP condition,

. Substituting the fluxes (

\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta_{\pm})=\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{1/\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}},

(46)

which satisfies the condition (

).
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

For
v=\text{const}
, a TEC flux for the potential temperature must be of the form

f_{\varrho\theta}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}v.

(47)

Proof.

The potential temperature numerical flux can be described for constant velocities as

f_{\varrho\theta}=\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta_{\pm})v,

(48)

where
\varphi_{2}
is some kind of mean values depending on
\varrho_{\pm}

\varrho\theta_{\pm}

\forall\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta>0\colon\qquad\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},\varrho\theta,\varrho\theta)=\varrho\theta.

(49)

The TEC condition (

becomes

\frac{\gamma K}{\gamma-1}\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket f_{\varrho\theta}-K\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket v=0,

(50)

which results in the final form (

).
∎

\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}=\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{1/\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}},

(51)

for a fixed
\gamma

such that the relation above is not satisfied.
We showed that a numerical flux that is EC, PEP, TEC, and does not have the influence of the pressure in the density flux cannot exist for

Lemma 3.10
.

For
v=\text{const}
, a numerical flux that is PEP, EC, and TEC must be of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{1/\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}v\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=f_{\varrho}v+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}v\end{aligned}\right.

(52)

with the influence of the pressure in the numerical density flux.

Proof.

The form of the potential temperature flux has already been determined by Lemma
3.9
. The remaining dependencies in the density and momentum flux can be again described by the functions
\varphi_{1,2}
resulting in the numerical fluxes

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta_{\pm})v\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}v+\varphi_{1}(\varrho_{\pm},p_{\pm})\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\frac{\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket}v\end{aligned}\right.

(53)

where
\varphi_{1}(\varrho_{\pm},p_{\pm})
and
\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta_{\pm})
are some kind of mean values depending on
\varrho_{\pm}
,
p_{\pm}
,
\varrho\theta_{\pm}
such that

\displaystyle\forall\varrho_{\pm},p>0

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},p,p)

\displaystyle=p,

(54)

\displaystyle\forall\varrho_{\pm},\varrho\theta>0

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},\varrho\theta,\varrho\theta)

\displaystyle=\varrho.

The final form (
52
) is given by the substituting the numerical fluxes (
53
) into the conditions EC (
25
) and PEP (
34
).
∎

We can now prove Theorem
3.4
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

The EC (
25
) and TEC (

) conditions are verified by substituting the numerical fluxes into (

) and (
17
). The KEP (
33

The characterization of the numerical fluxes for the compressible Euler equations with potential temperature as first invariant has been completed. The numerical fluxes are characterized by the influence of the pressure in the density flux, which is necessary to satisfy the EC, TEC, and PEP properties. In the following lemma we show the characterization for
p=\text{const}

Lemma 3.11
.

For fixed
p=\text{const}
, a (symmetric) EC, TEC, KEP, and PEP numerical flux must be of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\chi(\varrho_{\pm},v_{\pm})\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\chi(\varrho_{\pm},v_{\pm})\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\varrho\theta\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\frac{\varrho\theta}{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}}\chi(\varrho_{\pm},v_{\pm})\end{aligned}\right.

(55)

where
\chi
is a function depending on
\varrho_{\pm},v_{\pm}
such that
\forall\varrho_{\pm},v:

\chi(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},v,v)=0
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the one in
[
45
]
. Indeed, due to consistency, a numerical flux can always be written as the sum of a given numerical flux and a perturbation
\chi
that is consistent with zero. Therefore, we add the perturbation
\chi
to the numerical fluxes from Theorem
3.4
, considering that
p=\text{const}
, so that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\chi_{\varrho}(\varrho_{\pm},v_{\pm})\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}=f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}\chi_{\varrho v}(\varrho_{\pm},v_{\pm})\\
&f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\varrho\theta\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\chi_{\varrho\theta}(\varrho_{\pm},v_{\pm})\end{aligned}\right.

(56)

where
\forall\varrho,v\colon\chi_{\varrho}(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},v,v)=0
,
\chi_{\varrho v}(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},v,v)=0
and
X_{\varrho\theta}(\varrho_{+},\varrho_{-},v,v)=0

is then chosen such that the EC, TEC, KEP, and PEP conditions are satisfied. The KEP property leads to the condition

. The TEC condition (

-\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket\frac{m^{2}}{\varrho^{2}}\right\rrbracket\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\frac{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}+\chi_{\varrho}(\varrho_{\pm},v_{\pm})\right)\\

3.2
On the pressure positivity for
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)

Density and pressure must be positive to avoid nonphysical numerical solutions and eventually instabilities. The numerical fluxes should preserve this property when an explicit Euler time marching method is applied to a first-order FV method. This is a well-known and important property, since SSP Runge-Kutta methods can be written as a convex combination of Euler steps method
[
27
]
. Therefore, given the convex set of admissible states

G=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}=\left(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta\right)|\varrho>0,p=K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}>0\right\},

(59)

positivity of density and pressure is inherited by any convex combination of Euler steps, as soon as it is preserved by the internal Euler steps.
Note that the density positivity remains unchanged with respect to the results already described in
[
43
]
. However, the advantage of the potential temperature formulation results in a straightforward way to preserve the pressure positivity.

Corollary 3.12
.

If the numerical potential temperature flux
f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\llbracket\varrho\theta\right\rrbracket
is used with
\lambda\geq\max\{|v_{i}|,|v_{i+1}|\}
, the first-order FV scheme preserves the non-negativity of the pressure
\varrho
under the CFL condition

\Delta t\leq\frac{\Delta x}{2\lambda}.

(60)

Proof.

The mean appearing in the flux is given by the integral for
t=2-\gamma
(see
[
12
]
)

f(t)=\frac{\int_{a}^{b}x^{t+1}dx}{\int_{a}^{b}x^{t}dx}.

(61)

Thus,

f(2-\gamma)=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma},

(62)

and due to the monotonicity of
f
, we have

f(2-\gamma)=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}\leq f(0)=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\theta\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}.

(63)

Hence, this mean satisfies the conditions of Theorem 6.1 in
[
43
]
and the flux preserves the positivity of the potential temperature. Pressure positivity follows, since

p_{i}^{n+1}=K(\varrho\theta)_{i}^{n+1}\geq 0\hskip 28.45274pt\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.\qed

4
On the conservation properties in presence of a geopotential term

To include gravity effects, typically a source term with a geopotential term
\boldsymbol{\phi}=\boldsymbol{\phi}(\boldsymbol{x})
is added to the right-hand side of the compressible Euler equations, as in Eqs (
1
) and (
2
).
The momentum and the total energy are not conserved anymore and other invariants and properties play a significant role in geophysical flows.
Souza et al.
[
57
]
have developed a kinetic and potential energy preserving (KPEP) numerical flux with the total energy as conserved variable using the Kennedy-Gruber flux
[
31
]
for the conservative part of the Euler equations.
In this section, we extend the KPEP property introduced in
[
57
]
. We first present a general condition for KPEP fluxes within the FV framework. Furthermore, we introduce the condition for total energy conservation in the presence of gravity, considering two sets of conserved variables:
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho E)
and
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)
. To do that, we employ a non-conservative discretization of the source terms.

4.1
On the kinetic and total energy

In presence of gravity source terms given by a generic geopotential
\phi
, the compressible Euler equations in 1D read

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}\varrho\\
\varrho v\\
\varrho E\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{x}\begin{pmatrix}\varrho v\\
\varrho v^{2}+p\\
(\varrho E+p)v\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-\varrho\partial_{x}\phi\\
-\varrho v\partial_{x}\phi\\
\end{pmatrix}

(65)

for
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho E)
as conserved variables; for the potential temperature formulation, we have

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}\varrho\\
\varrho v\\
\varrho\theta\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{x}\begin{pmatrix}\varrho v\\
\varrho v^{2}+p\\
\varrho\theta v\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-\varrho\partial_{x}\phi\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(66)

The FV method in presence of a source term is typically written as

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}+\frac{1}{\Delta x}\Bigg(\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i+1})-\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i-1},\boldsymbol{u}_{i})\Bigg)=\boldsymbol{S}_{i}.

(67)

We seek for a particular discretization of the source term
\boldsymbol{S}_{i}
and conditions such that for both semi-discretization the KPEP and TEC property are satisfied, where the total energy for the systems (
65
) and (
66
) is
U=\varrho E+\varrho\phi
, which is a conserved quantity. In particular, following the work of
[
20
,
47
]
, we introduce the general form for the discretization of source terms

S_{i}^{\varrho v}=\frac{S_{i+1/2}^{\varrho v}+S_{i-1/2}^{\varrho v}}{2\Delta x}=\frac{\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\overline{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x},

(68)

S_{i}^{\varrho E}=\frac{S_{i+1/2}^{\varrho E}+S_{i-1/2}^{\varrho E}}{2\Delta x}=\frac{\overline{v}_{i+1/2}\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\overline{v}_{i-1/2}\overline{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x},

(69)

where
\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}
and
\overline{v}_{i+1/2}
are consistent mean values, such that

\displaystyle\forall\varrho_{i+1}=\varrho=\varrho_{i}>0

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}

\displaystyle=\varrho,

(70)

\displaystyle\forall v_{i+1}=v=v_{i}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle\overline{v}_{i+1/2}

\displaystyle=v.

We therefore investigate the necessary conditions for ensuring KPEP and TEC property.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given the set of variables
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)
, a numerical flux
(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta})
, the source term
S^{\varrho v}
and the corresponding FV method (
7
) are TEC if

(i)

the density flux and the source term are of the form

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\overline{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad S^{\varrho v}=\overline{\varrho}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket

(71)

(ii)

and the numerical flux
(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta})
is of the form (
28
).

Proof.

The proof requires the contraction of the right-hand side in entropy space. Therefore, we consider the total energy

U=\varrho E+\varrho\phi=\frac{p}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\varrho}+\varrho\phi.

(72)

The entropy variables for the new entropy functional
U
can be written as

\boldsymbol{\omega}=\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\rho E}+\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\phi}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\varrho^{2}}\\
\frac{m}{\varrho}\\
\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\phi\\
0\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(73)

Hence, we can use this linear relation between the total energy including the potential.
Therefore, performing a left multiplication of (
67
) by the entropy variables (
73
) yields

\frac{dU_{i}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{\Delta x}\Bigg(\langle\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{f}_{i+1/2}\rangle-\langle\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{f}_{i-1/2}\rangle\Bigg)-\langle\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{S}_{i}\rangle,

(74)

where we introduced the abbreviation
\boldsymbol{f}_{i+1/2}=f^{\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i+1})
.
By expressing the first two terms as
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i\pm 1/2}\mp\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rrbracket_{i\pm 1/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dU_{i}}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(\langle\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2},\boldsymbol{f}_{i+1/2}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2},\boldsymbol{f}_{i+1/2}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(\langle\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2},\boldsymbol{f}_{i-1/2}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rrbracket_{1-1/2},\boldsymbol{f}_{i-1/2}\rangle\right)-\langle\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{S}_{i}\rangle.

(75)

Therefore, we can split the different contributions and employing the TEC definition and the definition of the source term, we are left with

\displaystyle\frac{dU_{i}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(F_{i+1/2}-F_{i+1/2})-\frac{\phi_{i}}{\Delta x}(f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}-f_{i-1/2}^{\varrho})-v_{i}\frac{\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\overline{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x},

(76)

where
F_{i+1/2}
is the conservative and symmetric total energy flux. Following a similar argument as before,

\displaystyle\frac{dU_{i}}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(F_{i+1/2}-F_{i-1/2})-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\phi\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}-f_{i-1/2}^{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\phi\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2})+

\displaystyle+\frac{\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}-\overline{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket u\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{4\Delta x}+

\displaystyle+\frac{\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}f^{\varrho}_{1+1/2}+\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}f^{\varrho}_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}-\frac{\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}+\overline{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}.

(77)

If
f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}=\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}
then the last two terms vanish

\displaystyle\frac{dU_{i}}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(F_{i+1/2}-F_{i-1/2})-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\phi\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}-f_{i-1/2}^{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\phi\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2})

\displaystyle+\frac{\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}-\overline{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{4\Delta x},

(78)

which can be written in a conservative form as

\frac{dU_{i}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(H_{1+1/2}-H_{i-1/2}),

(79)

where

H_{i+1/2}=F_{i+1/2}+f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\phi\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}-\frac{\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}}{4}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}.\qed

(80)

Note that for the set of conserved variables with the potential temperature we had to impose the TEC flux derived in the previous section. That is not required when working with
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho E)
as primary variables, since
\varrho E
is a first invariant when
\phi=0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Given the set of conserved variables
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho E)
, a numerical flux
(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho E})
, the source terms
S^{\varrho v}
,
S^{\varrho E}
and the corresponding FV method (
7
) are TEC if the density flux and the source terms are of the form

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\overline{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad S^{\varrho v}=\overline{\varrho}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket,\quad S^{\varrho E}=f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket.

(81)

Proof.

A semi-discretization of the total energy starting with the given set of conserved variables can be written as

\partial_{t}(\varrho_{i}E_{i}+\phi_{i}\varrho_{i})=\partial_{t}(\varrho_{i}E_{i})+\phi_{i}\partial_{t}(\varrho_{i}),

(82)

since the geopotential depends only on the space coordinates. Following the same steps as shown before, and considering
f^{\varrho}_{i+1/2}=\overline{v}_{i+1/2}\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d(\varrho_{i}E_{i}+\phi_{i}\varrho_{i})}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho E}-f_{i-1/2}^{\varrho E})-\frac{\phi_{i}}{\Delta x}(f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}-f_{i-1/2}^{\varrho})

(83)

\displaystyle-\frac{\overline{v}_{i+1/2}\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\overline{v}_{i-1/2}\overline{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x},

which can be further developed into

\frac{d(\varrho_{i}E_{i}+\phi_{i}\varrho_{i})}{dt}=-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho E}-f_{i-1/2}^{\varrho E})-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\phi\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}-f_{i-1/2}^{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\phi\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i-1/2})\\
+\frac{\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}f^{\varrho}_{1+1/2}+\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}f^{\varrho}_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}-\frac{\overline{v}_{i+1/2}\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}+\overline{v}_{i-1/2}\overline{\varrho}_{i-1/2}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{i-1/2}}{2\Delta x}.

(84)

The last two terms vanishes if
f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}=\overline{v}_{i+1/2}\overline{\varrho}_{i+1/2}
; its conservative form is

\frac{d}{dt}(\varrho_{i}E_{i}+\phi_{i}\varrho_{i})=-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(H_{i+1/2}-H_{i-1/2}),

(85)

where
H
is the numerical flux of the total energy

H_{i+1/2}=f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho E}+f_{i+1/2}^{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\phi\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i+1/2}.\qed

(86)

Following the same approach as above, we investigate the condition for the numerical fluxes to achieve kinetic and potential energy preserving (KPEP) property. Since the momentum equation and the KEP property is not influenced by the set of variables, we can formulate a single lemma, that accounts for both.

Lemma 4.3
.

Given the set of conserved variables
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho E)
or
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)
, a numerical flux
(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho E})
or
(f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v},f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho\theta})
, a source term
S^{\varrho v}
and the corresponding FV method (
7
) are KPEP if

(i)

the density flux and the source term are of the form

f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho}=\overline{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad S^{\varrho v}=\overline{\varrho}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket

(87)

(ii)

and the numerical flux is KEP.

Proof.

First we note that

\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}k+\varrho\phi\right)=v\partial_{t}(\varrho v)-\frac{v^{2}}{2}\partial_{t}\varrho+\phi\partial_{t}\varrho=-\partial_{x}\left(v\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho v^{2}+p+\varrho\phi\right)\right)+p\partial_{x}v.

(88)

A semi-discretization leads to

\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{i}v_{i}^{2}+\varrho_{i}\phi_{i}\right)=-v_{i}\frac{f^{\varrho v}_{i+1/2}-f^{\varrho v}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x}+\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{2}\frac{f^{\varrho}_{i+1/2}-f^{\varrho}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x}-v_{i}S^{\varrho v}_{i}-\phi_{i}\frac{f^{\varrho}_{i+1/2}-f^{\varrho}_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x}.

(89)

If
f^{\mathrm{num}}_{\varrho v}
is KEP, this proof reduces to finding a condition that results in a conservative form for the last two terms. This has already been done in the previous lemmas, leading to the same condition.
∎

4.2
Well-balanced schemes

Steady states are particular solutions of the compressible Euler equations. In this section, we show how to construct well-balanced schemes by employing the same non-conservative product discretization as in the previous section. In particular, in presence of a potential the scheme should be able to mimic at a discrete level the hydrostatic balance of the Euler equations, i.e.,

\nabla p=-\varrho\nabla\phi.

(90)

The steady background state is typically prescribed either with an isothermal or constant potential temperature state, where then perturbations are added. Waruszewski et al.
[
63
]
derived a well-balanced scheme with isothermal background state for DG methods using a generalization of flux differencing for numerical fluxes in fluctuation form.
Based on their results, here we show that this also applies for our FV methods and we present a well-balanced scheme for a constant potential temperature background state.

The only equation which plays a role in the well-balanced scheme is the momentum. Thus, independently of the last closure equation, (
\varrho E
or
\varrho\theta
, or yet more general), these results are satisfied as long as the momentum
\varrho v
is employed as primary invariant.

Lemma 4.4
.

A source term
S^{\varrho v}
preserves the hydrostatic balance prescribed by a constant background temperature
T
of the compressible Euler equations if the source term is of the form

S^{\varrho v}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\mathrm{log}}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket,

(91)

and the pressure term is discretized as
\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
.

Proof.

Presenting the idea in 1D, we consider

\partial_{x}p=-\varrho\partial_{x}\phi.

(92)

A solution of (
92
) must be of the form

\displaystyle p=p_{0}e^{-\frac{\phi}{RT}}\quad\text{and}\quad\varrho=\varrho_{0}e^{-\frac{{\phi}}{RT}}=\frac{p_{0}}{RT}e^{-\frac{{\phi}}{RT}},

(93)

where
p_{0}=p(x=0)
and
\varrho_{0}=\varrho(x=0)
. The semi-discretization (
67
) reduces to

\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{+}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{-}}{\Delta x}+\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{+}_{\mathrm{log}}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket^{+}+\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{-}_{\mathrm{log}}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket^{-}}{2\Delta x}=0,

(94)

where we introduced the abbreviation
\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{\pm}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i\pm 1/2}
.
Inserting (
93
) results in

RT\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{+}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{-}}{\Delta x}-RT\frac{\left\llbracket\varrho\right\rrbracket^{+}+\left\llbracket\varrho\right\rrbracket^{-}}{2\Delta x}=0,

(95)

which is always satisfied
\forall\varrho^{\pm}
.
∎

For constant potential temperature background state, we consider the hydrostatic balance explicitly expressed in terms of the variable
\varrho\theta
, namely in 1D

K\partial_{x}(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma}=-\varrho\partial_{x}\phi.

(96)

If no perturbations in
\theta
are present, i.e.,
\theta=\text{const}
, the relation simplifies to

K\theta^{\gamma}\partial_{x}\varrho^{\gamma}=-\varrho\partial_{x}\phi,

(97)

and a closed form solution is of the form

\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\left(\varrho^{\gamma-1}-\varrho_{0}^{\gamma-1}\right)=-\frac{1}{K\gamma\theta^{\gamma}}(\phi-\phi_{0}),

(98)

where
\varrho_{0}=\varrho(x=0)
and
\phi_{0}=\phi(x=0)
. At this point we can prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

A source term
S^{\varrho v}
preserves the hydrostatic balance prescribed by a constant background potential temperature
\theta
of the compressible Euler equations if the source term is of the form

S^{\varrho v}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket,

(99)

and the pressure term is discretized as
\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
.

Proof.

The semi-discretization (
67
) at the steady state reads

\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{+}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{-}}{\Delta x}+\frac{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\frac{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket^{+}+\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\frac{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket^{-}}{2\Delta x}=0.

(100)

Using the steady state solution (
98
), we can write the source term as

S=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\frac{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket}{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma-1}\right\rrbracket}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket=-K\theta^{\gamma}\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket,

(101)

and consequently the semi-discretization reduces to

K\theta^{\gamma}\frac{\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho^{\gamma}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{+}-\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho^{\gamma}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{-}}{\Delta x}-K\theta^{\gamma}\frac{\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket^{+}+\left\llbracket\varrho^{\gamma}\right\rrbracket^{-}}{2\Delta x}=0,

(102)

which is always identically zero
\forall\varrho^{\pm}
, for the general property that
a_{i}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{a\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}^{\pm}\mp\frac{\left\llbracket a\right\rrbracket^{\pm}}{2}
.
∎

and

5
Entropy-conservative DGSEM discretization

In this section we extend all the previous properties to DGSEM.
To obtain an entropy-stable DGSEM discretization of a conservation law

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}+\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})=\boldsymbol{0},

(103)

the computational domain is divided into non-overlapping elements.
Within each element, the solution is approximated by Lagrange polynomials of degree
N

defined on the Lobatto-Gauss-Legendre (LGL) nodes. These basis functions are continuous inside each element but may
be discontinuous across element boundaries. Such discontinuities are resolved with the introduction of two-point numerical fluxes.

The discrete scheme is obtained by multiplying the governing equation by test functions
of degree
N
, integrating by parts on each element, and evaluating the resulting integrals
using an LGL quadrature rule with
N+1
nodes on the reference interval

\xi\in[-1,1]
. This procedure leads to the semi-discrete formulation

J\omega_{i}\boldsymbol{\dot{u}}_{i}(t)+(\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(N,R)}-\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)))\delta_{iN}-(\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(0,L)}-\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)))\delta_{i0}+2\sum_{j=0}^{N}\omega_{i}D_{ij}\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(j,i)}=0,

(104)

where
J
is the element Jacobian,
\omega_{i}
are the quadrature weights,

D_{ij}=l^{\prime}_{j}(\xi_{i})
is the differentiation matrix derived from the
Lagrange basis and
\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(i,j)}
is the numerical two-point surface flux, and
\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(j,i)}
is the volume numerical two-point symmetric flux.
Moreover, the differentiation matrix satisfies the summation-by-parts (SBP) property

(\boldsymbol{M}\boldsymbol{D})+(\boldsymbol{M}\boldsymbol{D})^{T}=\boldsymbol{Q}+\boldsymbol{Q}^{T}=\boldsymbol{B},

(105)

where
\boldsymbol{M}
is the diagonal mass matrix and
Q_{ij}=\omega_{i}D_{ij}

[
22
]
.

A natural extension of the non-conservative products in the FV method is the DGSEM discretization (see, e.g.,
[
21
,
17
]
)

\displaystyle J\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}_{i}=-\sum_{j=0}^{N}2Q_{ij}\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(i,j)}-\delta_{iN}\boldsymbol{f_{N}}+\delta_{i0}\boldsymbol{f_{0}}+\sum_{j=0}^{N}Q_{ij}\boldsymbol{g}_{(i,j)}^{\mathrm{vol}}-\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(N,R)}\delta_{iN}-\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(0,L)}\delta_{i0}+\boldsymbol{g}^{*}_{(N,R)}\frac{\delta_{iN}}{2}-\boldsymbol{g}^{*}_{(0,L)}\frac{\delta_{i0}}{2},

(106)

where
\boldsymbol{g}^{*}_{(i,j)}
is the surface numerical non-conservative flux and
\boldsymbol{g}^{\mathrm{vol}}
is the volume numerical non-conservative flux.
Rueda-Ramirez et al.
[
50
]
showed that the DGSEM semi-discretization (
106
) can be rewritten as flux differencing formula, if the non-conservative terms can be written
as the product of a local and a symmetric contribution, i.e.,

\boldsymbol{g}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(j,k)}=\boldsymbol{g}^{sym}_{(j,k)}\circ\boldsymbol{g}_{j}.

(107)

Here, we extend this result to non-conservative terms that are anti-symmetric, i.e., terms that can be written as the product of a symmetric contribution and an anti-symmetric contribution.

Lemma 5.1
.

It is possible to rewrite (
106
) as a flux-difference formula,

J\omega_{j}\boldsymbol{\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{j}=\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{(j,j-1)}-\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{(j,j+1)}\hskip 56.9055ptj=0,...,N,

(108)

where the indexes
j=-1
and
j=N
refer to the outer states (across the left and right boundaries, respectively) and
\Gamma_{j,k}
is the so-called staggered (or telescoping) “flux” between node
j
and the adjacent node
k
, if it is possible
to write the volume numerical non-conservative term as a product of an anti-symmetric and a symmetric contribution, i.e.,

\boldsymbol{g}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(j,k)}=\boldsymbol{g}^{sym}_{(j,k)}\circ\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{g}\right\rrbracket_{(j,k)}.

(109)

Proof.

The anti-symmetric (jump) term can be rewritten as

\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{g}\right\rrbracket_{(j,k)}=2\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\boldsymbol{g}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(j,k)}-2\boldsymbol{g}_{j}.

(110)

Thus, the volume term reads

\boldsymbol{g}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(j,k)}=2\boldsymbol{g}^{sym}_{(j,k)}\circ\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\boldsymbol{g}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(j,k)}-2\boldsymbol{g}^{sym}_{(j,k)}\circ\boldsymbol{g}_{j},

(111)

where the first term is purely symmetric, and the second term is the product of a symmetric and a local contribution. Therefore, the rest of proof follows the same steps as the flux differencing lemma in
[
50
]
.
∎

The anti-symmetric flux (
109
) is zero when evaluated on a single point:
\boldsymbol{g}^{*}(\boldsymbol{u}_{j},\boldsymbol{u}_{j})=0
.
Given the anti-symmetric property of the non-conservative flux, we can rewrite our discretization as

\displaystyle J\omega_{i}\boldsymbol{\dot{u}}_{i}=

\displaystyle-\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\omega_{i}2D_{ij}\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(i,j)}-\delta_{iN}f_{N}+\delta_{i0}f_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{N}\omega_{i}D_{ij}\boldsymbol{g}_{(i,j)}^{\mathrm{vol}}\right)

(112)

\displaystyle-\left(\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(N,R)}\delta_{iN}-\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(0,L)}\delta_{i0}+\boldsymbol{g}^{*}_{(N,R)}\frac{\delta_{iN}}{2}-\boldsymbol{g}^{*}_{(0,L)}\frac{\delta_{i0}}{2}\right).

Note that the non-conservative anti-symmetric flux also reduces computational cost in the volume term, as it avoids the diagonal contributions and requires computing only the off-diagonal terms, either from the upper or the lower triangle of the matrix, similar to the contribution of the conservative symmetric volume flux
[
48
]
.

5.1
Conservation properties of DGSEM

In this section we extend the FV properties of the numerical fluxes for the non-conservative product of the geopotential term to the DGSEM.
First, we extend the result of Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Given the set of variables
(\varrho,\varrho v,\varrho\theta)
, a numerical non-conservative surface and volume flux
g_{\varrho v}
and the corresponding DGSEM discretization (
106
) are TEC if

(i)

the density and non conservative fluxes are of the form

f^{\varrho,*}=f^{\varrho,\text{vol}}=\overline{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{v\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad g^{\varrho v,*}=g^{\varrho v,\text{vol}}=\overline{\varrho}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket,

(113)

(ii)

and the numerical flux
(f_{\varrho},f_{\varrho v},f_{\varrho\theta})
is of the form (
28
) or (
30
).

Proof.

Since the proof mimics the steps for the FV framework, we only present the main steps.
We introduce the generic anti-symmetric volume and surface numerical flux

g^{\varrho v,*}=g^{\varrho v,\mathrm{vol}}=\overline{\varrho}\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket,

(114)

and we define the entropy variables

\boldsymbol{q}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\varrho^{2}}+\phi\\
\frac{m}{\varrho}\\
\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\varrho^{2}}\\
\frac{m}{\varrho}\\
\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}K(\varrho\theta)^{\gamma-1}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\phi\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\boldsymbol{v}+\phi\boldsymbol{e_{1}}.

(115)

Since the conservative numerical flux is TEC,

\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{v}^{T}\right\rrbracket\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}}=\left\llbracket\psi\right\rrbracket.

(116)

We can contract in entropy space

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}_{i}^{T}\boldsymbol{M}J\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}_{i}=\langle\boldsymbol{v},J\boldsymbol{\dot{u}}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{M}}=J\dot{\eta}.

(117)

In the following proof we drop the term
\boldsymbol{M}
, and divide the volume contribution
VOL
and the surface contribution SURF:

\displaystyle J\dot{\eta}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}_{i}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N}2\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(i,j)}-\delta_{iN}\boldsymbol{f_{N}}+\delta_{i0}\boldsymbol{f_{0}}+\sum_{j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}\boldsymbol{g}_{(i,j)}^{\mathrm{vol}}\right)

(118)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}_{i}^{T}\left(\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(N,R)}\delta_{iN}-\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(0,L)}\delta_{i0}+\boldsymbol{g}^{*}_{(N,R)}\frac{\delta_{iN}}{2}-\boldsymbol{g}^{*}_{(0,L)}\frac{\delta_{i0}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\text{VOL}-\text{SURF}.

We first consider the volume term:

\displaystyle\text{VOL}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N}2\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(i,j)}-\delta_{iN}\boldsymbol{f_{N}}+\delta_{i0}\boldsymbol{f_{0}}+\sum_{j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}\boldsymbol{g}_{(i,j)}^{\mathrm{vol}}\right)

(119)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(i,j)}-\boldsymbol{q}^{T}_{N}\boldsymbol{f_{N}}+\boldsymbol{q}^{T}_{0}\boldsymbol{f_{0}}+\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}\boldsymbol{g}_{(i,j)}^{\mathrm{vol}}.

By applying the SBP property
\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}=B_{ij}-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij}
,

\displaystyle\text{VOL}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}(\boldsymbol{B}_{ij}-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij})\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(i,j)}-\boldsymbol{q}^{T}_{N}\boldsymbol{f_{N}}+\boldsymbol{q}^{T}_{0}\boldsymbol{f_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}(\boldsymbol{B}_{ij}-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij})\boldsymbol{g}_{(i,j)}^{\mathrm{vol}}.

(120)

Because of the flux consistency on the diagonal elements and the definition of
\boldsymbol{B}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{VOL}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}(-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij})\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(i,j)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}(-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij})\boldsymbol{g}_{(i,j)}^{\mathrm{vol}}.

(121)

Since the
f_{(i,j)}=f_{(j,i)}
is symmetric and
g_{(i,j)}=-g_{(j,i)}
is anti-symmetric, we can rearrange and reindex

\displaystyle\text{VOL}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}(\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}-\boldsymbol{q}_{j}^{T})Q_{ij}\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{(i,j)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}(\boldsymbol{q}_{i}^{T}+\boldsymbol{q}_{j}^{T})Q_{ij}\boldsymbol{g}_{(i,j)}^{\mathrm{vol}}.

(122)

Applying the definition of TEC flux, we are left with

\displaystyle\text{VOL}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}Q_{ij}(\psi_{i}-\psi_{j})+\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}(\phi_{i}-\phi_{j})\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}f^{\varrho}_{(i,j)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}(v_{i}+v_{j})\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}\overline{\varrho}_{i,j}(\phi_{j}-\phi_{i}).

(123)

Because of the form of the density flux and by applying the properties of the differential operator
\boldsymbol{Q}
we have
\text{VOL}=\psi_{0}-\psi_{N}
.
Now that we simplified the volume term, we can sum also the surface term

\displaystyle J\dot{\eta}=-\text{VOL}-\text{SURF}=\psi_{N}-\psi_{0}-\boldsymbol{q}_{N}^{T}\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(N,R)}+\boldsymbol{q}_{0}^{T}\boldsymbol{f}^{*}_{(0,L)}-\boldsymbol{q}_{N}^{T}\frac{u_{N}}{2}g^{*}_{(N,R)}+\boldsymbol{q}_{0}^{T}\frac{u_{0}}{2}g^{*}_{(0,L)}.

(124)

The proof now proceeds as for the FV method.
∎

The remaining properties are straightforward following the same steps as in the above lemma.

6
Extension to three-dimensional curvilinear meshes

In this work, we recall the main ingredients of the DGSEM curvilinear formulation, following closely the notation introduced in
[
51
]
, see also
[
21
,
17
]
.
On a three-dimensional curvilinear element, the semi-discrete DGSEM formulation for the advective and non-conservative contributions can be written in compact form as

J_{ijk}\,\omega_{ijk}\,\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}_{ijk}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{ijk},

(129)

where
J_{ijk}
denotes the mapping Jacobian,
\omega_{ijk}
the quadrature weight at node
(i,j,k)
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{ijk}
the discrete operator collecting all numerical flux contributions in the three spatial directions.

An explicit expression of the operator reads

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{ijk}=

\displaystyle\omega_{jk}\Bigg(-2\sum_{m=0}^{N}Q_{im}\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{1,\text{vol}}_{(i,m)jk}-\sum_{m=0}^{N}Q_{im}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{1}_{(i,m)jk}-\delta_{i0}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}}{2}\right)\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right]_{0jk}+\delta_{iN}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}}{2}\right)\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right]_{Njk}

\displaystyle\hskip 25.6073pt-2\sum_{m=0}^{N}Q_{jm}\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{2,\text{vol}}_{i(j,m)k}-\sum_{m=0}^{N}Q_{jm}\,\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{2}_{i(j,m)k}-\delta_{j0}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}}{2}\right)\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{2}\right]_{i0k}+\delta_{jN}\,\left[\left(\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}}{2}\right)\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{2}\right]_{iNk}

\displaystyle\hskip 25.6073pt-2\sum_{m=0}^{N}Q_{km}\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{3,\tilde{vol}}_{ij(k,m)}-\sum_{m=0}^{N}Q_{km}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{3}_{ij(k,m)}-\delta_{k0}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}}{2}\right)\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{3}\right]_{ij0}+\delta_{kN}\,\left[\left(\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}}{2}\right)\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{3}\right]_{ijN}\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\omega_{jk}\left(\delta_{i0}\left[\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{*}_{(0,L)jk}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{*}_{(0,L)jk}\right]-\delta_{iN}\left[\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{*}_{(N,R),jk}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{*}_{(N,R)jk}\right]\right)

\displaystyle+\omega_{ik}\left(\delta_{j0}\left[\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{*}_{i(0,L)k}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{*}_{i(0,L)k}\right]-\delta_{jN}\left[\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{*}_{i(N,R)k}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{*}_{i(N,R)k}\right]\right)

\displaystyle+\omega_{ij}\left(\delta_{k0}\left[\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{*}_{ij(0,L)}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{*}_{ij(0,L)}\right]-\delta_{kN}\left[\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{*}_{ij(N,R)}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{*}_{ij(N,R)}\right]\right).

(130)

Here,
Q_{ij}=\omega_{i}D_{ij}
is the SBP matrix, and
\boldsymbol{a}^{m}_{ijk}
are the contravariant basis vectors linking the reference coordinates
(\xi^{1},\xi^{2},\xi^{3})\in[-1,1]^{3}
to the physical domain. The symbol
\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}
accounts for additional non-conservative terms.
The numerical two–point fluxes in the three directions are consistently defined with the metric terms, e.g.,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{1,\text{vol}}_{(i,m)jk}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}^{\text{vol}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{mjk})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,m)jk},

(131)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{2,\text{vol}}_{i(j,m)k}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}^{\text{vol}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{imk})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i(j,m)k},

(132)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{3,\text{vol}}_{ij(k,m)}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}^{\text{vol}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{ijm})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{3}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{ij(k,m)},

(133)

where we adopted the common notation
\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}
to denote block vectors, containing a state vector in each spatial component (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
66
]
)

\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\mathbf{f}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{f}_{1}\\
\mathbf{f}_{2}\\
\mathbf{f}_{3}\end{bmatrix},\quad\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\mathbf{\boldsymbol{g}}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{\boldsymbol{g}}_{1}\\
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{g}}_{2}\\
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{g}}_{3}\end{bmatrix},

(134)

with each component defined as follows

\mathbf{\boldsymbol{f}}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\varrho u\\
\varrho u^{2}+p\\
\varrho uv\\
\varrho uw\\
\varrho\theta u\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{\boldsymbol{f}}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\varrho v\\
\varrho vu\\
\varrho v^{2}+p\\
\varrho vw\\
\varrho\theta v\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{\boldsymbol{f}}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}\varrho w\\
\varrho wu\\
\varrho wv\\
\varrho w^{2}+p\\
\varrho\theta w\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{\boldsymbol{g}}_{i}=\varrho\phi\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\delta_{i1}\\
\delta_{i2}\\
\delta_{i3}\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\boldsymbol{g}_{i}=\varrho\boldsymbol{\phi},\,i=1,2,3.

(135)

The non-conservative and anti-symmetric volume fluxes are defined as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{1,\text{vol}}_{(i,m)jk}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{g}}^{\text{vol}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{mjk})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,m)jk},

(136)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{2,\text{vol}}_{i(j,m)k}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{g}}^{\text{vol}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{imk})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i(j,m)k},

(137)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{3,\text{vol}}_{ij(k,m)}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{g}}^{\text{vol}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{ijm})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{3}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{ij(k,m)}.

(138)

The surface numerical flux is defined similarly as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{1,*}_{(i,m)jk}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}^{*}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{mjk})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,m)jk},

(139)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{2,*}_{i(j,m)k}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}^{*}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{imk})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i(j,m)k},

(140)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{3,*}_{ij(k,m)}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}^{*}(\boldsymbol{u}_{ijk},\boldsymbol{u}_{ijm})\cdot\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}^{3}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{ij(k,m)}.

(141)

6.1
TEC and KPEP with curvilinear coordinates

In this section, we consider the compressible Euler equations with the potential temperature and extend the property for TEC. The extension of the other results presented in the previous 1D Cartesian semi-discretization is straightforward, following the same approach, therefore we will only state them.

Theorem 6.1
.

Given a semi-discretization of the compressible Euler equations as in (
130
), for the set of conserved variables
(\varrho,\varrho\boldsymbol{V},\varrho\theta)
, a numerical non-conservative surface and volume fluxes, a numerical conservative surface and volume fluxes are TEC if

(i)

the numerical density flux and non-conservative fluxes are of the form

\boldsymbol{f}^{\varrho,*}=\boldsymbol{f}^{\varrho,\mathrm{vol}}=\overline{\varrho}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\boldsymbol{V}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\},\quad\boldsymbol{g}^{\varrho\boldsymbol{V},*}=\boldsymbol{g}^{\varrho\boldsymbol{V},\mathrm{vol}}=\overline{\varrho}\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rrbracket,

(142)

(ii)

and the numerical flux
(f_{\varrho},f_{\varrho\boldsymbol{V}},f_{\varrho\theta})
is of the form (
28
).

Proof.

Consider the first volume term in the
\xi_{1}
direction and contract it in the entropy variable space, where the entropy variables are here denoted with
\boldsymbol{v}
, to avoid confusion with the weights
\omega_{ijk}
.

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\boldsymbol{VOL}^{1}=\omega_{jk}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\left(-2\sum_{m=0}^{N}Q_{im}\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{1,\text{vol}}_{(i,m)jk}-\sum_{m=0}^{N}Q_{im}\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{1}_{(i,m)jk}-\delta_{i0}\left[\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right]_{0jk}+\delta_{iN}\left[\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right]_{Njk}\right).

(143)

Applying the SBP property
\boldsymbol{Q}_{ij}=B_{ij}-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij}
, we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\boldsymbol{VOL}^{1}=

\displaystyle\omega_{jk}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\Bigg(-2\sum_{m=0}^{N}\left(B_{ij}-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij}\right)\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{1,\text{vol}}_{(i,m)jk}-\sum_{m=0}^{N}\left(B_{ij}-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij}\right)\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{1}_{(i,m)jk}

(144)

\displaystyle-\delta_{i0}\left[\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right]_{0jk}+\delta_{iN}\left[\boldsymbol{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{f}}\cdot J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right]_{Njk}\Bigg).

Because of the flux consistency on the diagonal elements and the definition of
\boldsymbol{B}
, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\boldsymbol{VOL}^{1}=\omega_{jk}\Bigg(-\sum_{i,m=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\left(-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij}\right)\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{1,\text{vol}}_{(i,m)jk}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,m=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\left(-Q_{ji}+Q_{ij}\right)\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{1}_{(i,m)jk}\Bigg).

(145)

Here the proof closely follows the step for the 1D DGSEM; indeed, due to the symmetry and anti-symmetric properties of the conservative and non-conservative fluxes, after a rearrangement of the index, we are left with

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\boldsymbol{VOL}^{1}=\omega_{jk}\Bigg(-\sum_{i,m=0}^{N}Q_{im}\left(\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}-\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{mjk}\right)\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}^{1,\text{vol}}_{(i,m)jk}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,m=0}^{N}Q_{im}\left(\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}+\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{mjk}\right)\boldsymbol{\tilde{g}}^{1}_{(i,m)jk}\Bigg).

(146)

Applying the definition of TEC flux and the non-conservative flux, we can write

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\boldsymbol{VOL}^{1}=\omega_{jk}\Bigg(-\sum_{i,m=0}^{N}Q_{im}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{J\boldsymbol{a}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,m)jk}\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{\psi}_{ijk}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{mjk}\right)\Bigg).

(147)

The difference now with the 1D Cartesian case is the presence of the metric terms. After some manipulations and re-indexing, the term simplifies to

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}^{T}_{ijk}\boldsymbol{VOL}^{1}=\omega_{jk}\Bigg(-\sum_{i,m=0}^{N}Q_{im}\left(J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right)_{mjk}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}_{ijk}+\left(J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right)_{0jk}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}_{0jk}-\left(J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right)_{Njk}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}_{Njk}\Bigg).

(148)

The remaining terms can be treated similarly, as well as the surface term, that we do not report here for brevity. We are left with

-\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N}\omega_{ijk}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{ijk}\cdot\sum_{m=0}^{N}\left(D_{im}\left(J\boldsymbol{a}^{1}\right)_{mjk}+D_{jm}\left(J\boldsymbol{a}^{2}\right)_{imk}+D_{km}\left(J\boldsymbol{a}^{3}\right)_{ijm}\right)=0,

(149)

which is equal to zero if the discrete metric identity
[
33
]
is satisfied.
∎

6.2
Well-balancedness with curvilinear coordinates

Theorem 6.4
.

Given a semi-discretization of the compressible Euler equations as in (
130
), for the set of conserved variables
(\varrho,\varrho\boldsymbol{V},X)
, where
X
represents any conserved variable that closes the system, a non-conservative numerical surface and volume flux
\boldsymbol{g}
preserve the hydrostatic balance prescribed by a constant background temperature
T
under any coordinate mapping if

(i)

the non-conservative volume flux is of the form

\boldsymbol{g}^{\varrho\boldsymbol{V},\text{vol}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\log}\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rrbracket,

(152)

(ii)

the pressure term is discretized as
\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
,

(iii)

and the free-stream preservation property is satisfied at the discrete level.

Proof.

For sake of simplicity and brevity, here we present the proof for 2D case only in the
x
momentum.

We start by observing that the surface non-conservative fluxes are zeros, because of the continuity of the potential along the interfaces, i.e.,

\phi_{0j}^{R}=\phi_{Nj},\quad\phi_{0j}=\phi_{Nj}^{L},\quad\phi_{i0}^{R}=\phi_{iN},\quad\phi_{i0}=\phi_{iN}^{L}.

(153)

Therefore, the semidiscrete formulation can be rewritten at the hydrostatic balance for the horizontal momentum equation as

\displaystyle J_{ij}\dot{\left(\varrho u\right)}_{ij}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\omega_{i}}\left(Ja^{1}_{1,Nj}\left(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(N,R)j}-p_{Nj}\right)\delta_{iN}-Ja^{1}_{1,0j}\left(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(0,L)}-p_{0j}\right)\delta_{i0}\right)

(154)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\omega_{j}}\left(Ja^{2}_{1,iN}\left(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,(N,R)}-p_{iN}\right)\delta_{jN}-Ja^{2}_{1,i0}\left(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i,(0,L)}-p_{i0}\right)\delta_{j0}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N}2D_{ik}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja^{1}_{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,k)j}\left(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,k)j}+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\log,(i,k)j}(\phi_{kj}-\phi_{ij})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{N}2D_{jk}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja^{2}_{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i(j,k)}\left(\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i(j,k))}+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\log,i(j,k)}(\phi_{ik}-\phi_{ij})\right)=0.

The analytical solution of an isothermal hydrostatic flow are

p=p_{0}e^{-\phi/RT}\quad\varrho=\frac{p_{0}}{RT}e^{-\phi/RT}.

(155)

Considering the first surface conservative term, we have

\frac{p_{Nj}}{2}-\frac{p_{0j}^{R}}{2}=\frac{p_{0}}{2}\left(e^{-\phi_{Nj}/RT}-e^{-\phi_{0j}^{R}/RT}\right)=0,

(156)

which is again zero for the continuity of the potential along the interfaces. The same applies to all the remaining surface terms.

For the volume terms we just consider the first one

\text{VOL}^{1}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}2D_{ik}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja^{1}_{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,k)j}\left(\frac{p_{kj}+p_{ij}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varrho_{kj}-\varrho_{ij}}{\mathrm{log}(\varrho_{kj})-\mathrm{log}(\varrho_{ij})}(\phi_{kj}-\phi_{ij})\right).

(157)

From the hydrostatic balance, we derive
\left\llbracket\mathrm{log}(\varrho)\right\rrbracket=-\frac{\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket}{RT}

thus

\text{VOL}^{1}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}2D_{ik}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja^{1}_{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,k)j}\left(RT\frac{\varrho_{kj}+\varrho_{ij}}{2}-RT\frac{1}{2}(\varrho_{kj}-\varrho_{ij})\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}2D_{ik}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja^{1}_{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,k)j}RT\varrho_{ij}.

(158)

The second volume term is analogous

\text{VOL}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}2D_{jk}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja^{2}_{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i(j,k)}\left(\frac{p_{ik}+p_{ij}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varrho_{ik}-\varrho_{ij}}{\mathrm{log}(\varrho_{ik})-\mathrm{log}(\varrho_{ij})}(\phi_{ik}-\phi_{ij})\right)\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{N}2D_{jk}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja^{2}_{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i(j,k)}RT\varrho_{ij}.

(159)

Summing up all the volume terms

\text{VOL}=\text{VOL}^{1}+\text{VOL}^{2}=2RT\varrho_{ij}\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(D_{ik}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja_{1}^{1}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{(i,k),j}+D_{jk}\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{Ja_{1}^{2}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{i(j,k)}\right)=0,

(160)

which is equal to zero, due to the free-stream preservation property.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Given a semi-discretization of the compressible Euler equations as in (
130
), for the set of conserved variables
(\varrho,\varrho\boldsymbol{V},X)
, where
X
represents any conserved variable that closes the system, a non-conservative numerical volume and surface flux
\boldsymbol{g}
preserve the hydrostatic balance prescribed by a constant background potential temperature
\theta
under any coordinate mapping if

(i)

the non-conservative volume flux is of the form

\boldsymbol{g}^{\varrho\boldsymbol{V},\mathrm{vol}}=\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{\varrho\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}_{\gamma}\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rrbracket,

(161)

(ii)

the pressure term is discretized as
\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\left\{p\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\right\}
,

(iii)

and the free-stream preservation property is satisfied at the discrete level.

Proof.

The proof is concenptually analogous to the previous theorem.

7.5
Baroclinic instability

To further validate our formulation, we employ the baroclinic wave benchmark originally formulated by Ullrich et al.
[
61
]
and adopted in the 2016 edition of the Dynamical Core Model Intercomparison Project (DCMIP)
[
60
]
. This configuration has become a standard test for assessing the ability of atmospheric models to reproduce midlatitude baroclinic instability.

The initial state is a balanced, axisymmetric solution of the deep-atmosphere equations, as described in Appendix A of Ullrich et al.
[
61
]
. A localized Gaussian perturbation is applied to the zonal wind field in the northern midlatitudes, which excites the growth of a baroclinic disturbance. In the early stages (up to about day 7) the evolution is predominantly linear, while at later times nonlinear interactions lead to steepening and eventual wave breaking.

In our study the simulation length is set to 10 days, covering both the linear growth phase and the transition to nonlinearity. The spatial discretization uses an equiangular cubed–sphere grid with
K_{h}=8
elements on the horizontal and
K_{v}=4
elements in the vertical, for a total of six panels and polynomial degree
p=5
.

The point-wise discretization of the source term is unconditionally unstable, independently by the set of equations.
On the other hand the non-conservative formulation could run stably and without any need of filtering for long simulation time. The contour of the surface pressure after 10 days are shown in Figure

8
Conclusions

In this work we derived three new numerical fluxes for the compressible Euler equations, formulated with potential temperature as a primary invariant.
Depending on the choice, these fluxes can preserve thermodynamic entropy (EC), total energy (TEC), or both simultaneously (ETEC).
The EC and TEC fluxes allow a degree of freedom in the density flux. For both cases, the logarithmic mean provide the most robust formulation; the EC flux then is PEP, while the TEC flux additional satisfies the EC condition under constant pressure.
We introduced a general definition of the kinetic and potential energy preservation (KPEP) property and derived a condition for total energy conservation (TEC), when a geopotential term is included, which rely on a non-conservative discretization of the geopotential term in flux differencing form.
Furthermore, we developed a well-balanced scheme for the case of a constant background potential temperature and extended all properties to discontinous Galerkin spectral-element method (DGSEM) on arbitrary curvilinear coordinates.
The robustness and accuracy of the novel numerical fluxes and schemes have been assessed with a variety of standard atmospheric benchmark problems. The correct convergence rates were obtained, and the non-conservative approach showed up to two to four order of magnitude improvement, where the well-balancedness property was crucial to achieving such differences.
The numerical experiments demonstrated that the newly derived fluxes are both robust and accurate, performing consistently with the entropy conservative Ranocha flux for the total energy formulation.
No significant differences were observed between the two formulations, using total energy or potential temperature as prognostic variables, indicating that neither approach appears to offer a clear advantage over the other based on our test cases.
Additionally, schemes employing a non-conservative discretization of the source term consistently exhibited higher robustness and accuracy compared to the point-wise approach.

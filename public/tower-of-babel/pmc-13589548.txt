Orobanche cumana KAI2d2 mediates the perception of dehydrocostus lactone and strigolactones and is inhibited by triazole ureas
Authors: Umar F Shahul Hameed, Muhammad Jamil, Randa A Zarban, Yoshimoto Saito, Kawthar F Alashoor, Aparna Balakrishna, Tadao Asami, Stefan T Arold, Salim Al-Babili
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSeed germination of the parasitic plant Orobanche cumana is induced by host-derived strigolactones and dehydrocostus lactone. This study shows that the KAI2d2 receptor perceives both ligands through distinct interaction modes. Triazole urea compounds covalently inhibit KAI2d receptors, thereby suppressing parasitic seed germination without affecting sunflower host growth.

Dear Editor,

Orobanche cumana (Orobanchaceae), commonly known as broomrape, is an obligate root holoparasitic plant that infests sunflower (Helianthus annuus), causing substantial yield losses in many regions of Europe and Asia (Gagne et al., 1998; Westwood et al., 2010). Its seed germination is triggered by chemical signals exuded by sunflower roots, including strigolactones (SLs) and sesquiterpene lactones (STLs) such as dehydrocostus lactone (DCL) (Plakhine and Joel, 2010; Dor et al., 2020). In non-parasitic plants, SL perception by the α/β-hydrolase DWARF14 (D14) initiates a signaling cascade via the SCFMAX2 ubiquitin ligase complex. SL recognition and hydrolysis by D14 destabilize the D14 lid domain, which is required for the association of D14 with the MORE AXILLARY GROWTH 2 (MAX2) F-box protein (Yao et al., 2016; Vancea et al., 2025). In Orobanchaceae, perception of SL and STL signals is mediated by KARRIKIN INSENSITIVE2–divergent (KAI2d) receptors. KAI2d evolved from canonical KAI2 α/β-hydrolases, D14 homologs that respond to karrikins and unknown endogenous ligands (Galindo et al., 2002; Toh et al., 2015; Tsuchiya et al., 2015; Conn and Nelson, 2016; Takei et al., 2023). In Striga hermonthica, SL recognition by the KAI2d protein ShHTL7 also induces lid domain destabilization and MAX2 binding, suggesting that this mode of SL signaling is conserved in at least some KAI2ds (Vancea et al., 2025). However, the structural basis of STL recognition in O. cumana is unknown, and we lack selective inhibitors that block germination without interfering with SL signaling in the sunflower host.

We identified 11 D14/KAI2 homologs in the O. cumana genome and classified them into four clades: D14, D14-like, KAI2, and KAI2d (Supplemental Figure 1). Whereas Arabidopsis and rice each have a single copy of D14 and KAI2, O. cumana contains one D14 (OcD14), one KAI2c (OcKAI2c), and nine KAI2d paralogs (OcKAI2d1–OcKAI2d9), indicating lineage-specific expansion and functional diversification of KAI2d genes akin to other Striga family members. Sequence alignment and AlphaFold modeling confirmed conservation of the Ser–His–Asp catalytic triad across all OcKAI2d proteins (Supplemental Figures 2 and 3), supporting a cleavage-based signaling mechanism. Ligand pocket analysis revealed two subgroups: OcKAI2d1, OcKAI2d3, and OcKAI2d4 with smaller cavities (404–419 Å3), and the remaining receptors with larger pockets (704–1162 Å3). These different cavity volumes suggest diversification of ligand recognition.

We determined the crystal structures of OcKAI2d2 and OcKAI2d6 at resolutions of 2.19 and 1.85 Å, respectively (Figure 1A; Supplemental Table 1). Both proteins adopt the canonical α/β-hydrolase fold with a conserved Ser–His–Asp catalytic triad and open lid domain. Both are structurally similar to each other (root-mean-square deviation = 0.62 Å) and to AtD14, AtKAI2, and ShHTL7 (root-mean-square deviations from 0.6 to 1.0 Å; Supplemental Figure 4). Their ligand pockets (OcKAI2d2: 863 Å3; OcKAI2d6: 704 Å3) suggest a capacity for SL binding and contain key residues involved in ligand recognition (Supplemental Figures 3 and 4). YLG competition assays confirmed SL sensitivity, with the SL analogs GR24 and MP3 (Jamil et al., 2018) yielding low micromolar IC50 values for both receptors (Figure 1B; Supplemental Table 2). SL hydrolysis assays showed broad substrate specificity for OcKAI2d2 (Figure 1C; Supplemental Figure 5). Differential scanning fluorimetry revealed SL-induced receptor destabilization, consistent with conformational changes required for binding to SCFMAX2 (Yao et al., 2016; Vancea et al., 2025) (Supplemental Figure 6A). These findings establish OcKAI2d2 and OcKAI2d6 as catalytically active SL receptors. Eeven OcKAI2d1 and OcKAI2d3 were able to hydrolyze GR24, despite their smaller ligand cavities (Supplemental Figure 6B). This observation is consistent with the GR24-hydrolyzing activity reported for AtD14 (Seto et al., 2019), whose cavity volume is 447 Å3 (Supplemental Figure 3), and demonstrates that OcKAI2 paralogs with a range of pocket sizes can catalyze SL hydrolysis.

Previous studies identified DCL as a germination stimulant in Orobanche species (Takei et al., 2023). Accordingly, YLG competition assays revealed that OcKAI2d2 binds DCL with an IC50 comparable to those of the SLs GR24 and MP3 (Figure 1B; Supplemental Figure 6C). However, DCL binding stabilized OcKAI2d2 (Supplemental Figure 6A), and OcKAI2d2 did not hydrolyze DCL in vitro (Supplemental Figure 7). Both properties are characteristic of inhibitors of MAX2-mediated signaling (Shahul Hameed et al., 2022; Vancea et al., 2025) and appear incompatible with a mechanism in which SL hydrolysis induces receptor destabilization (Yao et al., 2016; Vancea et al., 2025). Nonetheless, DCL promoted the association of OcKAI2d2 with rice MAX2 (also named D3) in yeast two-hybrid assays (Supplemental Figure 8). These observations indicate that DCL can engage the SCFMAX2 pathway via OcKAI2d2 but may use a mechanism different from that of SLs.

Given the high similarity between the ligand-binding pockets of OcKAI2d2 and OcKAI2d6 (Figure 1A; Supplemental Figure 4), we hypothesized that OcKAI2d6 might also interact with DCL. However, DCL failed to compete with YLG for OcKAI2d6 (Supplemental Figure 6C), indicating that DCL has low affinity for OcKAI2d6.

To investigate DCL binding, we co-crystallized OcKAI2d2 with DCL, yielding a 2.45-Å structure (Figure 1D). Electron density maps revealed that DCL bound adjacent to the catalytic residues S96 and H247 within a hydrophobic pocket formed primarily by lid-domain helices (Supplemental Figure 9). Key interactions included hydrogen bonds with Ser96, His247, and Tyr175, which stabilized the intact DCL molecule noncovalently. Structural comparison showed minimal conformational changes upon binding, which were limited to rotamer shifts and a minor inward displacement (0.28 Å) of the α2 lid helix (Supplemental Figure 9C). Unlike SLs, which undergo hydrolysis, DCL remained intact in its complex with OcKAI2d2, confirming the ability of the receptor to accommodate non-hydrolyzable ligands and supporting its role in DCL-induced germination. Compared with that of OcKAI2d2, the lid helix position of apo-OcKAI2d6 is slightly more closed (Figure 1A) and would produce minor side-chain clashes with DCL, potentially limiting DCL interaction (Supplemental Figures 6C and 10).

Triazole urea compounds have been shown to irreversibly inhibit SL receptors by covalently modifying the catalytic serine. To test their effects on O. cumana, we co-crystallized OcKAI2d2 with two triazole urea inhibitors, KK007 and KK094, and resolved structures at 1.39 and 2.35 Å (Figure 1E; Supplemental Figure 8; Supplemental Table 1). Electron density confirmed a covalent linkage to Ser96, with both inhibitors occupying the active site and sterically blocking SL and DCL binding (Figure 1E; Supplemental Figures 11A–11D). YLG hydrolysis assays showed potent inhibition, with IC50 values of 2.5 ± 0.4 (KK007) and 1.2 ± 0.2 (KK094) μM, outperforming SLs and DCL (Figure 1F; Supplemental Table 2). Differential scanning fluorimetry revealed increased thermal stability, consistent with covalent binding (Supplemental Figure 11E). These results establish KK007 and KK094 as potent covalent OcKAI2d2 inhibitors that target the catalytic serine, thereby preventing ligand binding and SL hydrolysis.

We next investigated the inhibitory activity of triazole urea compounds for O. cumana seed germination. In the absence of inhibitors, germination was unexpectedly ∼five-fold higher with 100 nM DCL than with an equivalent concentration of GR24. This enhanced response may reflect either greater intrinsic sensitivity of O. cumana seeds to DCL or greater stability of DCL during the 1-week incubation period. KK007 and KK094 showed dose-dependent inhibition of DCL-induced germination, with KK094 achieving complete suppression at 100 μM and KK007 reducing germination by 41% at the same concentration (Figure 1G; Supplemental Figure 12). When GR24 was used as the stimulant, KK094 fully inhibited germination at 25 μM, whereas KK007 required 50 μM (Figure 1G; Supplemental Figure 13). Both compounds also significantly reduced radicle elongation (Supplemental Figure 11). In greenhouse trials, KK094 (25 μM) reduced O. cumana emergence by 83%, compared with 43% for KK007 (Supplemental Figure 14). These findings confirm that OcKAI2d-mediated germination and emergence were effectively blocked by the triazole urea inhibitors KK007 and KK094, consistent with their relative in vitro affinities and thermal stabilization profiles, supporting their potential as chemical control agents.

To evaluate potential host toxicity, sunflower seeds were treated with KK007 and KK094 at various concentrations. Neither compound affected germination or seedling development (Supplemental Figure 15). Treated seedlings showed normal shoot growth and morphology, and sunflower plants grown in pots maintained typical growth at all developmental stages. These observations indicate that, under the conditions tested, the inhibitors did not adversely affect sunflower viability (Figure 1I; Supplemental Figure 16).

In conclusion, we identified nine KAI2d homologs in the O. cumana genome and resolved the crystal structures of two representative receptors, OcKAI2d2 and OcKAI2d6. Both receptors exhibit the canonical α/β-hydrolase fold with intact catalytic triads and large ligand-binding pockets. Their SL-induced destabilization and broad SL hydrolysis activity support their role in perceiving a wide range of SLs via the canonical SCFMAX2-dependent signaling pathway, as established for D14 and ShHTL7.

Importantly, our structural data show that OcKAI2d2 also binds DCL, a non-hydrolyzable STL, within its SL-binding pocket. Although DCL is positioned near the catalytic Ser96 and His247, it is not cleaved, and its binding stabilizes the receptor rather than destabilizing it, in contrast to the response triggered by SLs. Structural modeling indicated that uncleaved DCL cannot be accommodated in the closed MAX2-bound conformation of OcKAI2d2. Nevertheless, DCL still promoted the association of OcKAI2d2 with a MAX2 protein in yeast two-hybrid assays. These findings establish OcKAI2d2 as a bifunctional receptor capable of activating SCFMAX2-mediated germination through both canonical SL hydrolysis and a distinct mode of engagement by non-hydrolyzable STLs, whose mechanistic basis remains to be characterized.

We also demonstrate that the triazole urea inhibitors KK007 and KK094 effectively block both signaling routes by covalently modifying the catalytic serine of OcKAI2d proteins. These inhibitors suppress O. cumana germination and emergence in vitro and under greenhouse conditions. Importantly, they do not impair sunflower growth, underscoring their potential as selective agents for broomrape control. Further studies are needed to evaluate their environmental impacts, particularly on non-target organisms such as arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, to ensure safe, sustainable application.

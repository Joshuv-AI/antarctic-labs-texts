A comprehensive approach to enhancing irrigation network management through the water accounting plus framework
Authors: Mahkameh Sadat Naeini, Bijan Nazari, Abbas Sotoodehnia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWater management in irrigation networks is crucial for sustainable agriculture under conditions of water scarcity and climate variability. This study applies the water accounting plus (WA+) framework, integrating meteorological and remote sensing data (WaPOR), to analyze water fluxes, productivity, and spatial heterogeneity in the Qazvin Plain irrigation network from 2009 to 2021. The total net inflow during this period was approximately 10,582 MCM, with contributions from precipitation (≈ 20%), surface inflow (≈ 27%), and storage changes (≈ 53%). Analysis of evapotranspiration revealed that transpiration accounted for 80% of total ET, with 72% classified as beneficial (transpiration plus interception) and 28% as non-beneficial (soil evaporation and canopy interception). Spatial patterns indicate higher water availability in the eastern part of the network and deficits in the western region, highlighting the potential for improving water productivity through targeted interventions such as soil moisture conservation and optimized irrigation scheduling. These findings demonstrate the applicability of the WA + framework for enhancing water use efficiency and informing sustainable irrigation management in semi-arid regions.Keywords: Climate change, Groundwater, WaPOR, Water fluxes, Water scarcitySubject terms: Climate sciences, Ecology, Hydrology

Introduction

Water scarcity is a pressing global challenge that significantly constrains human activities, especially in agriculture and food production. Inefficient water use in farming, combined with the pressures of urbanization and population growth, substantially limits the availability of water needed to sustain crop production1–4. This challenge is further intensified by the rising demand for water driven by economic development and population increase, alongside projected climatic and societal changes in the future5–10. In this context, effective water resource management is essential. Such management requires a comprehensive approach that balances supply the availability of water and related services with demand the consumption needs and patterns of users. Imbalances between water supply and demand create significant challenges for sustainable development, highlighting the importance of delivering water services efficiently and equitably. Consequently, the primary goal of water management is to maintain this balance while simultaneously addressing broader environmental, social, and economic impacts11–17. Accurate, well-categorized information on water supply, demand, consumption, and withdrawals is crucial for effective water resource management18. Water accounting frameworks are valuable tools in this regard, helping policymakers and managers make informed decisions and improve water use efficiency19,20. One such framework is water accounting plus (WA+), which was developed through a collaboration between the IHE Delft Institute for Water Education, the International Water Management Institute, and the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations21. WA + enhances water accounting by utilizing satellite imagery to gather basic hydrological data and monitor land surface processes. This approach makes water accounting more accessible and supports strategic decisions in water resource management. The framework integrates input data from satellite measurements, hydrological models, and ground-based observations. WA + is especially beneficial for regions with limited data availability, as it uses satellite-based datasets to analyze hydrological processes. For instance, Kanani et al.22 used WA + to analyze water use in the Zarrineh River basin, finding high water loss in agriculture and stressing the need for better management and irrigation tech to improve efficiency. Patle and Sharma23 highlighted the importance of WA + in studying water consumption patterns and agricultural production in the Mahi River basin, underscoring its value for decision-makers and researchers. Similarly, Kumar et al.24 utilized WA + and satellite observations to conduct water accounting in the Ghaghara River basin, while Patle et al.25 assessed water consumption patterns and food security in the Krishna basin using the same framework. Kivi et al.26 demonstrated WA+’s effectiveness in evaluating groundwater balance in the Plasjan basin, revealing its role in reducing uncertainty. Additionally, Ghorbanpour et al.27 applied WA + for sustainable water resource management in northwestern Iran, illustrating its contribution to enhancing productivity in water-scarce regions. Singh et al.28 assessed water consumption patterns and productivity with WA + and satellite data, further showcasing its utility. Abdallah et al.29 demonstrated the framework’s usefulness in the Yarmouk Tributary basin, where ground measurements are limited. Lastly, Dembele et al.30 combined WA + with hydrological modeling to evaluate climate change impacts on water resources in the Volta basin, highlighting its comprehensive application. Recent advances in modeling and optimization approaches for irrigation management, such as the coupled ITDRO–SaltMod model in China31 and the wCASA model incorporating wind effects for NPP estimation32, along with optimization-based approaches such as the model developed by Zhang and Yang (2023) for integrated water–soil–fertilizer management under uncertainty, similarly aim to enhance water use efficiency and crop productivity under water-limited and climate-variable conditions, complementing the capabilities of the WA + framework. Although numerous studies have applied the WA + framework at basin or regional scales, little attention has been paid to its potential for analyzing the internal dynamics of managed irrigation networks. These networks often exhibit spatial heterogeneity in water delivery, conveyance losses, and on-farm consumption, which conventional monitoring and management approaches—largely dependent on limited ground-based data—fail to capture adequately. Understanding such variations is critical for identifying inefficiencies and improving the performance of irrigation systems under increasing water scarcity. This study aims to bridge this knowledge gap by applying the WA + framework to the Qazvin plain irrigation network, one of the largest and most strategically important agricultural systems in central Iran, currently facing declining groundwater levels and water stress. The central research question is: To what extent can the WA + framework reveal spatial heterogeneity and inefficiency in water partitioning and use within the Qazvin irrigation network, thereby guiding more targeted and effective management interventions? It is hypothesized that applying WA + at the irrigation network scale will generate new insights into internal water distribution, productivity patterns, and spatial imbalances that conventional methods cannot detect. By introducing this novel application, the study provides a structured and scalable approach to quantifying water fluxes and evaluating water productivity within managed irrigation systems. The findings are expected to contribute to improved local-scale decision-making and to inform policies for sustainable irrigation management.

Materials and methods

Case study

The Qazvin Plain irrigation network is situated in the northern part of the Qazvin Plain, between latitudes 35° 24′ N and 36° 48′ N and longitudes 48° 45′ E and 50° 51′ E (Fig. 1). Covering a total area of approximately 80,000 ha (of which about 54,000 ha are under cultivation), the network represents one of the most important and long-standing irrigation systems in central Iran, playing a vital role in regional agricultural production and socio-economic development.

Case study: Qazvin plain irrigation network (this figure is generated in ArcGIS10.8.2 software, http://www.esri.com/software/arcgis).

The region experiences an arid to semi-arid climate, with an average annual precipitation of 312 mm and a mean annual temperature of 14.4 °C. Rainfall is temporally and spatially uneven, resulting in frequent water shortages during dry seasons33. The network relies mainly on surface water diverted from the Shahrud River—regulated by the Taleghan Dam—and is supplemented by groundwater extracted through numerous wells. In recent years, decreasing river inflows and excessive groundwater abstraction have led to declining groundwater levels and a growing imbalance between water supply and demand34. The irrigation system consists of a complex network of main and secondary canals, drains, and pumping wells designed to serve a variety of crops including wheat, barley, maize, vegetables, and orchards. However, persistent water stress and reduced groundwater recharge have raised concerns over the sustainability of water use in the region. These challenges make the Qazvin irrigation network an appropriate case study for applying the water accounting plus (WA+) framework to quantify water fluxes, identify inefficiencies, and support the development of sustainable water management strategies.

Net area of the irrigation network

To determine the net area of the irrigation network, the maps of the areas covered by the irrigation network were first imported into ArcGIS version 10.8.2. Then, appropriate spatial analyses were applied to differentiate the active irrigation areas from the gross area. After extracting the irrigated zones, the area of these regions was accurately calculated and considered as the net area of the irrigation network.

Water accounting plus (WA+)

The Water accounting plus (WA+) framework is a straightforward, standardized method for describing water and land management conditions, based on the principles of water balance. A key feature of this framework is the use of satellite imagery to obtain essential hydrological data and monitor land surface processes. The input data required for this framework can generally be sourced from satellite observations, hydrological models, and ground-based measurements. The framework consists of eight distinct sheets, each with specific indicators to represent water, agricultural, and environmental conditions. For this analysis, three sheets were selected based on the available data and the study’s objectives. To align with the study’s long-term goals, the water accounting period was set from 2009 to 2021.

Resource base sheet

The Resource base sheet provides comprehensive information on water volume, including inputs, outputs, water consumption, and related processes. It offers an overview of exploitable, manageable, non-manageable, allocated, used, and available water resources. The sheet also links evapotranspiration resulting from precipitation with evapotranspiration caused by natural and human withdrawals, helping to quantify the atmospheric cycle. The layout of the sheet is divided into three sections: the left section details the inflows, which include data on all sources of incoming water; the middle section addresses discharge processes and water consumption, explaining how water is utilized and managed within the system; and the right section summarizes the outputs. This structure provides a clear and organized depiction of water flow and usage.

Evapotranspiration sheet

In the Evapotranspiration (ET) sheet, water consumption is quantified through land-use classification and the categorization of water-consuming activities. This process highlights the anthropogenic impact on evapotranspiration and distinguishes between beneficial and non-beneficial water consumption. Beneficial ET is further divided into categories such as agriculture, ecosystems, economy, energy, and the recreational sector. ET components are classified into three categories: managed, manageable, and non-manageable. After quantifying ET volumes, the evaluation of water use benefits follows, using local value judgments and expert opinions to differentiate between beneficial and non-beneficial ET. Additionally, this analysis examines the impact of land-use planning on consumptive water use and introduces ET management concepts aimed at reducing total water consumption from withdrawals and inundations.

Land and water productivity sheet

The sheet presents data on biophysical land productivity and water productivity, serving as key indicators of the efficiency and sustainability of land and water use. In this study, the net primary production (NPP) parameter was employed as an alternative to the traditional biomass parameter. NPP represents the rate at which plants accumulate biomass through photosynthesis after accounting for respiration losses. This parameter not only offers a detailed insight into plant biomass production but also serves as a proxy for estimating the carbon dioxide absorbed from the atmosphere during the process of photosynthesis. By using NPP, the analysis provides a more dynamic and ecologically relevant measure of productivity, linking land and water use to broader environmental processes such as carbon sequestration and ecosystem health.

Indicators

In the WA + framework, indicators are defined to analyze water management. These indicators assist water resource managers in obtaining key information. By tracking these data over time, trends in indicator changes can be identified. Additionally, these indicators help assess the impact of water policies on water scarcity and the benefits derived from water consumption. Table 1 presents a summary of these key indicators, each of which plays a crucial role in assessing different aspects of water utilization, storage, and evapotranspiration efficiency. The associated equations offer a standardized approach to calculating these indicators, ensuring a consistent and comprehensive evaluation of water dynamics within the system.

Data collection

Meteorological data

Annual precipitation data for the Qazvin Plain irrigation network were obtained from the official synoptic meteorological station located within the study area. These data were recorded using standard rain gauges operated by the national meteorological agency and underwent quality control procedures before publication. The dataset spans the water years from 2009 to 2021 and includes verified annual precipitation values in millimeters.

Satellite data

Spatial and temporal patterns of evapotranspiration (ETIa), transpiration (T), evaporation (E), interception (I), and net primary production (NPP) were analyzed using datasets from the WaPOR database (https://wapor.apps.fao.org/home/WAPOR_2/1. This dataset was selected due to its validation against international, national, and regional studies, confirming its accuracy and reliability (Table 2). WaPOR provides relatively high spatial resolution, continuous data without gaps, and the ability to assess water requirements across various temporal intervals, making it suitable for long-term hydrological and agricultural analyses. Spatial distributions were mapped through classification techniques, facilitating the identification of areas with differing water demands, crop productivity, and biomass generation. Temporal trends were examined using charts to reveal long-term variations and fluctuations. The resulting datasets and visualizations were processed and analyzed using ArcGIS (ArcMap) version 10.8.2 and Microsoft Excel 2019.

The fAO’s WaPOR product (FWP)

To improve water use efficiency in agriculture, the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) has developed the WaPOR (Water Productivity Open Access Portal)—a remote sensing–based platform designed to provide open-access data on water productivity. The ETLook algorithm is primarily used within WaPOR to estimate evapotranspiration (ET). In addition, the Penman–Monteith (PM) method is applied to calculate ET from meteorological parameters such as temperature, solar radiation, vapor pressure, and wind speed—an approach broadly recommended by the FAO for deriving both actual and reference ET. In WaPOR, the ETLook algorithm extends the PM equation by integrating remotely sensed inputs such as soil moisture, surface albedo, solar radiation, NDVI, land cover, and digital elevation data, together with meteorological observations. Since its launch in April 2009, WaPOR has provided ET data at multiple temporal resolutions (daily, dekadal, monthly, and annual) and spatial scales—local (30 m), national (100 m), and continental (250 m)—covering most of Africa and parts of Asia46. The present study used the ETIa-WaPOR V2.1 dataset, accessible through the WaPOR portal (https://wapor.apps.fao.org/home/WAPOR_2/1. As reported by Bastiaanssen et al.47, ETIa-WaPOR relies on a refined version of the ETLook algorithm (termed ETLook-WaPOR). In the FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper No. 56 48, the PM method combines the surface energy balance and aerodynamic equations to estimate ET. Similarly, in ETLook-WaPOR, actual evapotranspiration (ETa) is computed using remotely sensed variables derived through this hybrid formulation.

Results and discussion

Synoptic meteorological station

Based on the analysis of rainfall trends in the Qazvin irrigation network over a 13-year period (2009–2021), the rainfall pattern exhibits considerable fluctuations. The highest annual precipitation was recorded in the 2009 water year with 428.55 mm, while the lowest occurred in 2021 with 201.73 mm. The data indicate an overall decreasing trend in rainfall across the region, particularly during the second half of the study period, reflecting the persistence of dry conditions (Fig. 3). This gradual decline, accompanied by high interannual variability, may signify ongoing climatic changes in the area and could have serious implications for sustainable water resource management, agricultural water supply planning, and the development of drought adaptation strategies. By analyzing these numerical data, the study underscores how fluctuations in precipitation directly impact the efficiency of irrigation systems, agricultural productivity, and overall water resource management in the Qazvin plain irrigation network. Mirgol and Nazari49 also emphasize the necessity of adaptive strategies to cope with the impacts of climate change on regional agriculture and highlight the importance of proactive measures to sustain agricultural productivity under changing climatic conditions.

WaPOR

The results of the spatiotemporal analysis of actual evapotranspiration (ETIa) variations in the Qazvin irrigation network over a 13-year period (2009–2021) (Fig. 4) indicate considerable fluctuations in ETIa values. The highest amount (582.53 mm) was recorded in the 2019 water year, while the lowest (385.35 mm) occurred in 2013. The temporal trend analysis reveals a gradual increase in ETIa values since 2013, which, in parallel with the observed decline in regional precipitation, poses serious implications for the aquifer balance and overall water resource management in the plain. Moreover, the spatial distribution of ETIa data shows a wide range (from 3307 to 11814 mm), indicating significant spatial variability and underscoring the need for differentiated management strategies across various parts of the irrigation network. These insights can be used to assess long-term evapotranspiration trends and optimize water resource management in the region, in line with recommendations from studies such as Hu and Jia50, which suggest that better monitoring of evapotranspiration is crucial for sustainable water management.

Based on the analysis of the spatial distribution and temporal trends of transpiration (T) in the Qazvin plain irrigation network over the 13-year period (2009–2021), the results demonstrate significant spatiotemporal variations in transpiration values (Fig. 5). Spatially, transpiration across the plain shows considerable diversity, ranging from 32 mm to 10,424 mm, indicating substantial differences in plant water consumption in different areas. Temporally, the highest transpiration value was recorded in the 2019 water year (479.02 mm), while the lowest occurred in 2013 (307.64 mm). The data reveal a general increasing trend in transpiration from 2013 to 2020. This rising trend in plant transpiration, particularly when analyzed alongside decreasing precipitation and increasing evapotranspiration in the region, suggests heightened water stress in the Qazvin plain’s agricultural ecosystem. The considerable spatial variation in transpiration values underscores the necessity for implementing different water management strategies and crop patterns across various sections of the irrigation network to optimize water productivity. In studies on water conservation practices, such as Adeboye et al.51, it has been observed that efficient irrigation and soil moisture retention methods significantly improve transpiration efficiency, leading to better water and land productivity. These insights highlight that adopting such efficient water management practices could play a crucial role in mitigating water stress and enhancing water productivity across different parts of the irrigation network.

Based on the analysis of the spatial distribution and temporal variation of evaporation (E) in the Qazvin irrigation network over a 13-year period (2009–2021) (Fig. 6), the results indicate considerable fluctuations in evaporation values. The highest evaporation (107.37 mm) was recorded in the 2017 water year, while the lowest (70.20 mm) occurred in 2013. This pattern of variation, coupled with high spatial heterogeneity, is consistent with those of Huang et al.52, and emphasizes the importance of irrigation scheduling and soil moisture management techniques in mitigating extreme fluctuations in evaporation values. These findings highlight the need for tailored water resource management strategies across different parts of the irrigation network to ensure sustainable agricultural water use.

Based on the analysis of spatial distribution and temporal trends of interception (I) in the Qazvin plain irrigation network during the 13-year study period (2009–2021), the results demonstrate significant variability in interception values (Fig. 7). Spatially, interception across the plain shows a wide range from 0 to 501 mm, indicating substantial differences in canopy interception capacity in different parts of the network. Temporally, the highest interception values were recorded in the 2018 water year (20.29 mm), while the lowest value occurred in 2011 (3.78 mm). This temporal pattern suggests potential relationships with vegetation cover dynamics and precipitation characteristics. Research shows that crop canopies and residues intercept significant rainfall, yet this is often overlooked in hydrological modeling (e.g., Kozak et al.53). Our findings from the Qazvin plain reinforce this notion, revealing notable spatial and temporal variability in interception values. The considerable spatial variation highlights the importance of heterogeneous land cover and vegetation distribution across the irrigation network, which has implications for water balance calculations and irrigation planning, as interception represents a component of water loss that does not contribute to soil moisture or groundwater recharge. Incorporating crop-specific canopy and residue effects in future studies could enhance the accuracy of hydrological models and improve understanding of water cycle dynamics in the region.

Based on the comprehensive analysis of spatial distribution and temporal trends of Net Primary Production (NPP) in the Qazvin Plain irrigation network over the 13-year period (2009–2021), the results reveal significant spatiotemporal variability in carbon fixation capacity across the region (Fig. 8). Spatially, the accumulated NPP values demonstrate a substantial range from 122 to 10,731 gC/m², indicating remarkable heterogeneity in ecosystem productivity throughout the irrigation network, with clearly delineated areas of varying biological activity. Temporally, the highest NPP value was recorded in the 2019 water year (535.97 gC/m²), while the lowest occurred in 2013 (310.68 gC/m²). The temporal pattern shows considerable interannual variability. This fluctuating pattern suggests that the region’s primary production is influenced by multiple environmental factors, potentially including water availability, climatic conditions, and land management practices. The strong spatial heterogeneity in NPP distribution underscores the varying ecosystem responses to environmental drivers across different segments of the irrigation network and highlights the need for targeted management strategies to enhance carbon sequestration and ecosystem services in this agriculturally important region.

The analysis reveals significant spatial variation in the studied parameters, with the eastern part of the network consistently exhibiting higher levels of evapotranspiration, transpiration, evaporation, interception, and net primary production compared to the western part. This spatial disparity likely reflects differences in water availability, soil properties, crop types, and proximity to irrigation infrastructure, all of which create more favorable conditions for crop growth and water utilization in the eastern region. Although direct causal relationships cannot be fully established from the current dataset, similar spatial patterns in agricultural water productivity have been observed in other regions (e.g., Zeng et al.54), supporting the relevance of these factors. To address the east-west imbalance, scenario-based modeling is recommended to evaluate the potential effects of targeted interventions, such as mulching or drip irrigation, on ET partitioning. Implementing region-specific water management strategies could enhance water distribution and productivity in the western sector while maintaining the efficiency of the eastern part. Further research integrating spatially explicit data on land use, soil characteristics, crop patterns, and irrigation infrastructure is necessary to clarify the underlying drivers of the observed heterogeneity in ET, T, E, I, and NPP.

Analysis of the data in Table 3, which illustrates the temporal dynamics of water balance components and net primary production in the Qazvin Plain irrigation network over the 13-year period (2009–2021), reveals complex and significant relationships among these variables. According to the data, precipitation (P) experienced a marked decline from 231.83 million cubic meters (MCM) in 2009 to 109.13 MCM in 2021, indicating an overall decreasing trend and intensifying arid conditions in the region. In contrast, actual evapotranspiration (ET) exceeded precipitation in most years, highlighting a water balance deficit and the system’s reliance on groundwater resources or past water reserves. Among the evapotranspiration components, transpiration (T) accounted for the dominant share, constituting an average of 80% of ET, while evaporation (E) contributed approximately 18% and interception (I) only 2%. Net primary production (NPP) also exhibited considerable fluctuations and showed the strongest alignment with transpiration (T) values, as the highest NPP value (0.29 megatons of carbon) in 2019 coincided with the peak transpiration value (259.31 MCM). This strong correlation between T and NPP underscores the heavy dependence of biomass production on water consumption in the agricultural sector of the Qazvin Plain. Overall, these findings demonstrate that declining precipitation and increasing pressure on water resources due to evapotranspiration demands pose serious challenges to the sustainability of the cropping system and water productivity in this region.

Water accounting plus

The analysis of the Qazvin plain irrigation network from 2009 to 2021 highlights notable patterns in water distribution and usage. Over this period, the total net inflow amounted to 10,581.81 MCM, originating from three main sources: 2152.42 MCM (20%) from precipitation, 2835.89 MCM (27%) from surface inflow, and 5593.50 MCM (53%) from changes in water storage (Fig. 9). This inflow was divided into green water (landscape evapotranspiration), accounting for 1089.63 MCM (10%), and blue water (exploitable water), representing 9492.18 MCM (90%). The dominance of blue water indicates that irrigation practices play a central role in meeting crop water requirements, reflecting the semi-arid conditions of the region. Within the blue water, 2266.95 MCM (21% of net inflow) was allocated for incremental evapotranspiration, while 2046.94 MCM (19% of net inflow) percolated into groundwater, emphasizing the irrigation network’s critical role in sustaining groundwater resources. However, in areas with high evaporation, this percolation may increase risks such as salinization and pollutant leaching, which must be considered in long-term groundwater management55,56. Changes in water storage revealed that 5178.29 MCM were linked to withdrawals outside the irrigation network, whereas 415.21 MCM were attributed to withdrawals within the network. This highlights the dual impact of the irrigation network: supporting both immediate agricultural water supply and groundwater recharge. The observed infiltration of 2046.94 MCM into groundwater underscores the broader hydrological significance of the network, aligning with findings by Wael et al.57 and Bouimouass et al.58, who demonstrated the influence of irrigation practices on groundwater dynamics. Additionally, real water savings (RWS) achieved through improvements in irrigation technologies provide further opportunities to enhance water use efficiency and sustainability (Nazari et al.12). These insights suggest that targeted interventions—such as optimizing irrigation scheduling, improving water allocation, and reducing non-beneficial losses—can enhance both agricultural productivity and groundwater sustainability across the network.

Resource base sheet of the Qazvin plain irrigation network (2009–2021) (MCM).

Between 2009 and 2021, the Qazvin plain irrigation network recorded a total evapotranspiration (ET) of 3356.58 MCM. Transpiration accounted for the majority of ET at 2689.61 MCM (80%), while evaporation contributed 666.97 MCM (20%). Within the evaporation component, soil evaporation dominated at 593.58 MCM (89%), and plant interception accounted for 73.40 MCM (11%). Following expert evaluations and FAO-56 guidelines for crop water requirements (Allen et al.,43), 90% of transpiration and 10% of interception were classified as beneficial, amounting to 2427.99 MCM (72% of total ET). The remaining 928.60 MCM (28%) was considered non-beneficial, including soil evaporation and 90% of interception losses (Fig. 10). The high proportion of non-beneficial ET highlights potential inefficiencies in water use across the network. A hypothetical 10% reduction in non-beneficial ET would save approximately 335.66 MCM of water, indicating substantial opportunities to enhance agricultural water productivity. These inefficiencies are primarily caused by soil evaporation, interception losses, and suboptimal irrigation scheduling. Implementing measures such as soil moisture conservation, advanced irrigation scheduling, cultivation of drought-resistant crops, improved soil structure, and methods to reduce evaporation could significantly improve water use efficiency. Such interventions would not only enhance crop water productivity but also provide tangible benefits for sustainable water resource management in the Qazvin plain. Similar strategies have been successfully applied in other regions to minimize water losses and optimize irrigation efficiency (Ramos et al.59; Wang et al.60).

For the Qazvin plain irrigation network (2009–2021), net primary production (NPP) was 2.89 MtC, with land productivity of 53,365.41 kgC/ha and water productivity of 0.86 kgC/m³ (Fig. 11). Optimizing both land and water productivity requires integrated strategies to enhance yield per unit area, as demonstrated by intercropping approaches (Abejo et al.61), improved crop cultivars (Giménez et al.62), and orchard management practices (Trunov et al.63). Additionally, improving water use efficiency is critical for sustainable irrigation, through measures such as precision irrigation, soil moisture management, mulching, and supplemental irrigation (Randev64; Tang et al.65; Villa et al.66). Implementing these combined strategies can substantially increase overall productivity and ensure more efficient use of land and water resources over the long term.

The analysis of key indicators revealed that a significant portion of negative changes in water storage during the accounting period were primarily attributed to groundwater withdrawals outside the network, which contributed to the overall decrease in available water. Within the network, evapotranspiration was divided into green and blue components, with 0.32 of the total evapotranspiration attributed to green water (landscape evapotranspiration) and 0.68 to blue water (exploitable water). The fraction of beneficial evapotranspiration, which includes the productive use of both green and blue water, was calculated to be 0.72. This indicates that a significant portion of the evapotranspiration process contributes positively to the overall water use efficiency within the network. In addition, the land and water productivity sheet shows a land productivity of 53,365.41 KgC/ha and a water productivity of 0.86 Kgc/m³, reflecting the biomass output per unit of land and water use respectively, further supporting the assessment of efficiency across the irrigation network (Table 4). To ensure a sustainable water balance and optimize resource utilization in the Qazvin plain irrigation network, a comprehensive approach involving technical, managerial, and educational measures is essential. These measures would address key challenges such as improving water productivity, reducing non-beneficial water losses, and enhancing the overall management of both green and blue water. To prioritize actionable interventions, scenario modeling (e.g., assessing the impact of mulching or drip irrigation on ET components) can be incorporated into future studies. Conclusion

This study demonstrates the application of the water accounting plus (WA+) framework to a large-scale irrigation network, the Qazvin Plain, providing a detailed assessment of water fluxes, productivity, and spatial heterogeneity. The findings reveal that conventional monitoring approaches are insufficient to capture internal inefficiencies and spatial imbalances in water allocation and use. By quantifying beneficial versus non-beneficial evapotranspiration and mapping spatial disparities, this research provides novel insights into internal network dynamics, which are critical for designing targeted water management interventions. From a scientific perspective, this study extends the application of WA + from basin and regional scales to the irrigation network scale, demonstrating its effectiveness for evaluating network-level water productivity, identifying inefficiencies, and supporting precision water management. Practically, the results inform strategies to optimize irrigation efficiency, enhance agricultural productivity, and sustainably manage both surface and groundwater resources in semi-arid regions. The study also underscores the importance of integrating advanced monitoring tools, modeling frameworks, and tiered management strategies—including improved irrigation scheduling, sensor-based feedback, and AI-assisted decision support—to ensure the sustainable and adaptive management of water resources under variable climatic conditions. Limitations include assumptions inherent to the WA + framework and constraints in local data availability. Future research should focus on extensive field validation, refinement of crop-specific parameters, and integration with socio-economic considerations to enhance the reliability, applicability, and policy relevance of the findings. .

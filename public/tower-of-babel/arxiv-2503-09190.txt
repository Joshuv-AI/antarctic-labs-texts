Optimal L ∞ L^{\infty} -error estimate for isoparametric finite element method in a smooth domain
Takahito Kashiwabara

Abstract
. We consider the isoparametric finite element method (FEM) for the Poisson equation in a smooth domain with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. Because the boundary is curved, standard triangulated meshes do not exactly fit it. Thereby we need to introduce curved elements if better accuracy than linear FEM is desired, which necessitates the use of isoparametric FEMs. We establish optimal rate of convergence O ⁡ ( h k + 1 ) O(h^{k+1}) in the L ∞ L^{\infty} -norm for k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , by extending the approach of our previous work [Kashiwabara and Kemmochi, Numer. Math. 144 , 553–584 (2020)] developed for Neumann boundary conditions and k = 1 k=1 .



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\,(N=2,3)
be a bounded domain of
C^{k+1,1}
-class (
k\geq 1
), with
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
.
We consider the following elliptic problem with the Dirichlet boundary condition:

\displaystyle-\Delta u=f\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma.

Its weak form reads, with
(\cdot,\cdot)_{D}
denoting the
L^{2}(D)
-inner product,

(1.1)

(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{\Omega}=(f,v)_{\Omega}\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Throughout this paper, we assume that the solution
u
of (
1.1
) is sufficiently smooth, especially,
u\in W^{k+1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\infty}}(\Omega)
(hence
f\in W^{k-1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\infty}}(\Omega)
).

Let
\Omega_{h}
be an approximate domain of
\Omega
and
\mathring{V}_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h})
be an approximate function space constructed by the isoparametric finite element method (FEM) of order
k\geq 2
, the details of which are given in Section
2
.
The parameter
h>0
denotes the mesh size associated with
\Omega_{h}
and
\mathring{V}_{h}
(see (
2.1
) below for the precise definition).
Then the finite element solution
u_{h}\in\mathring{V}_{h}
is obtained by solving

(1.2)

a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}):=(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}=(\tilde{f},v_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathring{V}_{h},

where
\tilde{f}\in W^{k-1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is an arbitrary extension of
f\in W^{k-1,\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying the stability condition
\|\tilde{f}\|_{W^{k-1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{k-1,\infty}(\Omega)}
.
The unique existence of the approximate solution
u_{h}
of (
1.2
) is ensured by the Lax–Milgram theorem.
With this setting, we state the main result of this paper as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
\tilde{u}\in W^{k+1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be an arbitrary extension of
u
such that
\|\tilde{u}\|_{W^{k+1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)}
.
Then we have

\|\tilde{u}-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|u\|_{W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)},

where the constants
C
depend only on
N
,
\Omega
, and
k
.

This type of pointwise error estimate has been of importance in theoretical studies of FEM.
For the case
\Omega_{h}=\Omega
(thus no domain perturbation), it is known, cf.
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
, that optimal rate of convergence in the
L^{\infty}
-norm is obtained if one employs quadratic or higher-order approximation (i.e.,
k\geq 2
in our notation) for
\mathring{V}_{h}
and that an additional factor
|\log h|
has to be included for the linear approximation (
k=1
).
A similar result which takes into account domain perturbation was obtained in
[
15
]
assuming
\Omega_{h}\subset\Omega
and using the maximum principle.
For the isoparametric FEM with
N=2
and
k=2
, a nearly-optimal estimate
O(h^{3-\epsilon})
was shown by
[
16
]
, where effects of numerical integration were also considered.

More recently, an
O(h^{2}|\log h|)
-estimate was proved for
k=1
in
[
7
]
, adopting techniques from
[
9
]
developed for a Neumann boundary condition.
An optimal
O(h^{k})
-estimate in the
W^{1,\infty}
-norm was presented by
[
4
]
based on the lift approach developed by
[
5
]
.
Compared to these researches, our main result above shows an optimal estimate
O(h^{k+1})
in the
L^{\infty}
-norm without the use of
\Omega_{h}\subset\Omega
or the maximum principle, by a natural extension of our previous result
[
9
]
to isoparametric FEMs of order
k\geq 2
.
We would like to point out that the choice of the boundary condition is significant because an auxiliary Green’s function estimate is available for the Dirichlet boundary condition (cf.
[
9
, Remark B.2]
), which enables us to exclude a logarithmic factor in
h
(see (
4.1
) below).

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sections
2
and
3
, following
[
8
, Section 2.2]
we introduce notations related to approximated domains and the isoparametric FEM.
In Section
4
, estimation of
\|\tilde{u}-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h})}
is reduced to that of
\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}
, where
g
and
g_{h}
denote a regularized Green function and its finite element approximation, respectively.
Then
\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}
is addressed by weighted
H^{1}
- and
L^{2}
-error estimates as we present in Sections
5
and
6
.
We remark that interpolation estimates adapted to the Dirichlet boundary condition in approximate domains are non-trivial, which is discussed in detail in Propositions
3.1
and
5.1
.

After the completion of this work, we noticed that an optimal
L^{\infty}
-error estimate of isoparametric FEM in curvilinear polyhedra has been presented in
[
12
]
.
However, our strategy to prove Theorem
1.1
differs from that of
[
12
]
, especially in that we neither utilize a maximum principle nor an “exact triangulation of
\Omega
”
[
5
,
11
]
(this requires that the nodes of
\partial\Omega_{h}
lie exactly on
\partial\Omega
; cf.
[
8
, Remark 2.2]
).

2.
Approximation by isoparametric FEM

2.1.
Assumptions on
\Omega

Since
\Omega
is
C^{k+1,1}
-smooth, there exists a system of local coordinates
\{(U_{r},\bm{y}_{r},\varphi_{r})\}_{r=1}^{M}
such that
\{U_{r}\}_{r=1}^{M}
forms an open covering of
\Gamma
,

\bm{y}_{r}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{}^{t}(y_{r,1},\dots,y_{r,N-1},y_{r,N}})={}^{t}(\bm{y}_{r}^{\prime},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}y_{r,N}})
is a rotated coordinate of
\bm{x}
, and
\varphi_{r}:\Delta_{r}\to\mathbb{R}
gives a graph representation
(\bm{y}_{r}^{\prime},\varphi_{r}(\bm{y}_{r}^{\prime}))
of
\Gamma\cap U_{r}
, where
\Delta_{r}
is an open cube in
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.
In view of
C^{k+1,1}(\Delta_{r})=W^{k+2,\infty}(\Delta_{r})
,

\|(\nabla^{\prime})^{m}\varphi_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{r})}\leq C\quad(m=0,\dots,k+2,\;r=1,\dots,M)

for some constant
C>0
, where
\nabla^{\prime}
means the gradient with respect to
\bm{y}_{r}^{\prime}
.

We also introduce a notion of tubular neighborhoods
\Gamma(\delta):=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid\operatorname{dist}(\bm{x},\Gamma)\leq\delta\}
.
It is known that
(see e.g.
[
6
, Section 14.6]
)
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
, which depends on the
C^{1,1}
-regularity of
\Omega
, such that each
\bm{x}\in\Gamma(\delta_{0})
admits a unique representation
\bm{x}=\bar{\bm{x}}+t\bm{n}(\bar{\bm{x}})
with
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\Gamma
and
t\in[-\delta_{0},\delta_{0}]
.
Namely,
\bm{\pi}:\bm{x}\mapsto\bar{\bm{x}}
is the orthogonal projection to
\Gamma
and
t
represents the signed distance function to
\Gamma
.

2.2.
Construction of approximate domains

Similarly to
[
8
, Section 2]
,
we make the following assumptions
(H1)
–
(H6)
on finite element partitions and approximate domains.
We start from introducing a quasi-uniform family of triangulations
\{\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}_{h\downarrow 0}
of
closed
straight
N
-simplices.

(H1)

Every
T\in\mathcal{\tilde{T}}_{h}
is affine-equivalent to a fixed reference
closed
simplex
\hat{T}
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
via the affine isomorphism
\tilde{\bm{F}}_{T}(\hat{\bm{x}})=B_{T}\hat{\bm{x}}+\bm{b}_{T}
.
The set
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is mutually disjoint, that is, the intersection of every two different elements is either empty or agrees with their common face of dimension
\leq N-1
.

(H2)

\{\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}_{h\downarrow 0}
is shape-regular and quasi-uniform, that is,

(2.1)

h_{T}\leq C\rho_{T}\quad\text{and}\quad h:=\max_{T^{\prime}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}}h_{T^{\prime}}\leq Ch_{T}\quad(\forall T\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}),

where
h_{T}
and
\rho_{T}
stand for the diameter of
T
and that of the largest ball contained in
T
, respectively.

Let
\hat{\Sigma}_{k}=\{\hat{\bm{a}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{k}}
denote the nodes in
\hat{T}
of the Lagrange
\mathbb{P}_{k}
-finite element (see e.g.
[
2
, Section 2.2]
).
The nodal basis functions
\hat{\phi}_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{T})
are then defined by
\hat{\phi}_{i}(\hat{\bm{a}}_{j})=\delta_{ij}
(the Kronecker delta) for
i,j=1,\dots,N_{k}
.

Next we fix
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, consider a parametric map
\bm{F}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{T})]^{N}
of the form
\bm{F}(\hat{\bm{x}})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\bm{a}_{i}\hat{\phi}_{i}(\hat{\bm{x}})
, and set
T:=\bm{F}(\hat{T})
.
We assume that
T
is a perturbation of the linear element
\tilde{T}
in the following sense:

(H3)

|\bm{a}_{i}-\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\tilde{T}}(\hat{\bm{a}}_{i})|\leq Ch_{T}^{2}
for all
i=1,\dots,N_{k}
and
\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.

If
h\leq 1
is small enough, which we assume throughout this paper, then such
\bm{F}
becomes diffeomorphic on
\hat{T}
(see
[
3
, Theorem 3]
).
Henceforth we write
\bm{F}_{T}
and
\tilde{\bm{F}}_{T}
to imply the
\bm{F}
and
\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\tilde{T}}
above, respectively.
From
(H3)
one has
\operatorname{dist}(T,\tilde{T})\leq\|\bm{F}_{T}-\tilde{\bm{F}}_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\leq Ch_{T}^{2}
, so that
\operatorname{diam}T\leq Ch_{T}
.

Now we denote by
\mathcal{T}_{h}
the set of
T
constructed above, which is mutually disjoint and is called a partition into
\mathbb{P}_{k}
-isoparametric finite elements.
In addition, we define
\Omega_{h}
to be the interior of the union of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
(namely,
\overline{\Omega}_{h}=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}T
).
Let us assume that
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h\downarrow 0}
is regular of order
k
in the sense of
[
1
, Definition 3.2]
, that is,

(H4)

\|\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}^{m}\bm{F}_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\leq C\|B_{T}\|^{m}\leq Ch_{T}^{m}
for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
m=1,\dots,k+1
,

where
C
is independent of
h_{T}
, and
\|B_{T}\|
is the matrix
2
-norm of
B_{T}
.

Remark 2.1
.

(i) We have
C_{1}h_{T}^{N}\leq\operatorname{meas}_{N}(T)=|\operatorname{det}(\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\bm{F}_{T})|\leq C_{2}h_{T}^{N}
for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

(ii) Under
(H4)
, we find from
[
3
, Theorem 4]
or
[
2
, Theorem 4.3.3]
that

(2.2)

\|\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{m}\bm{F}_{T}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}^{-1}\quad(m=1,\dots,k+1).

Let us next introduce a boundary mesh
\mathcal{S}_{h}
.
Setting
\Gamma_{h}:=\partial\Omega_{h}
, we define

\mathcal{S}_{h}=\{\bm{F}_{T}(\hat{S})\subset\Gamma_{h}\mid T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\text{ $\hat{S}\subset\partial\hat{T}$ is a closed $(N-1)$-face of $\hat{T}$}\}.

Then we have
\Gamma_{h}=\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{h}}S
(disjoint union).
Each boundary element
S\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
admits a unique
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
S\subset\partial T
, which is denoted by
T_{S}
.
We let
\bm{b}_{r}:U_{r}\to\mathbb{R}^{N-1};{}^{t}(\bm{y}_{r}^{\prime},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}y_{r,N}})\mapsto\bm{y}_{r}^{\prime}
denote the projection to the base set.
We assume that
\Omega
is approximated by
\Omega_{h}
in the following sense.

(H5)

\Gamma_{h}
is covered by
\{U_{r}\}_{r=1}^{M}
, and each portion
\Gamma_{h}\cap U_{r}
is represented as a graph
(\bm{y}_{r}^{\prime},\varphi_{rh}(\bm{y}_{r}^{\prime}))
, where
\varphi_{rh}
is a continuous function defined in
\overline{\Delta_{r}}
.
Moreover, each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
is contained in some
U_{r}
.

(H6)

The restriction of
\varphi_{rh}
to
\bm{b}_{r}(S)
for each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
is a polynomial function of degree
\leq k
.
Moreover,
\varphi_{rh}
approximates
\varphi_{r}
as accurately as a general
\mathbb{P}_{k}
-interpolation does; namely, we assume that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{r}-\varphi_{rh}\|_{L^{\infty}(\bm{b}_{r}(S))}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}=:\delta,

\displaystyle\|(\nabla^{\prime})^{m}(\varphi_{r}-\varphi_{rh})\|_{L^{\infty}(\bm{b}_{r}(S))}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1-m}\qquad(m=1,\dots,k+1).

These assumptions imply that the local coordinate system for
\Omega
is compatible with
\{\Omega_{h}\}_{h\downarrow 0}
and that
\Gamma_{h}
is a piecewise
\mathbb{P}_{k}
-accurate approximation of
\Gamma
.
If
h
is small enough,
\bm{\pi}|_{\Gamma_{h}}
is well-defined and bijective
, cf.
[
10
, Proposition 8.1]
.

2.3.
Estimates on a boundary-skin layer

For a subset
\Gamma_{h}^{\prime}
of
\Gamma_{h}
, let
\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h}^{\prime},\delta):=\{\bar{\bm{x}}+t\bm{n}(\bar{\bm{x}})\mid\bar{\bm{x}}\in\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h}^{\prime}),\;|t|\leq\delta\}
be a tubular neighborhood with the base
\Gamma_{h}^{\prime}
.
We recall the following boundary-skin estimates for
S\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega\cup\Gamma(\delta))
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{p}(\bm{\pi}(S,\delta))}\leq C(\delta^{1/p}\|v\|_{L^{p}(S)}+\delta\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\bm{\pi}(S,\delta))}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|v-v\circ\bm{\pi}\|_{L^{p}(S)}\leq C\delta^{1-1/p}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\bm{\pi}(S,\delta))}.

The proofs are given in
[
10
, Theorems 8.1–8.3]
for
k=1
, which can be extended to
k\geq 2
.
A version of (
2.3
) limited to
\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega
also holds (see
[
9
, Lemma A.1]
):

(2.5)

\|v\|_{L^{p}(\bm{\pi}(S,\delta)\cap(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega))}\leq C(\delta^{1/p}\|v\|_{L^{p}(S)}+\delta\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\bm{\pi}(S,\delta)\cap(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega))}).

2.4.
Extension from
\Omega
to
\Omega\cup\Gamma(\delta)

We let
\tilde{\Omega}:=\Omega\cup\Gamma(\delta)=\Omega_{h}\cup\Gamma(\delta)
with
\delta=Ch^{k+1}
given above.
Recall that
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{f}
are stable extensions of
u\in W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
f\in W^{k-1,\infty}(\Omega)
, respectively.

We also need extensions whose behavior in
\Gamma(\delta)\setminus\Omega
can be controled by that in
\Gamma(c\delta)\cap\Omega
for some constant
c>0
.
For this we introduce
P:W^{m,p}(\Omega)\to W^{m,p}(\tilde{\Omega})\,(m\in\{0,1,2,3\},p\in[1,\infty])
by reflection with respect to
\Gamma
as follows.
For
\bm{x}\in\Omega\setminus\Gamma(\delta)
we let
Pf(\bm{x})=f(\bm{x})
; for
\bm{x}=\bar{\bm{x}}+t\bm{n}(\bar{\bm{x}})\in\Gamma(\delta)
we define

Pf(\bm{x})=\begin{cases}f(\bm{x})&(-\delta\leq t<0),\\
6f(\bar{\bm{x}}-tn(\bar{\bm{x}}))-8f(\bar{\bm{x}}-2tn(\bar{\bm{x}}))+3f(\bar{\bm{x}}-3tn(\bar{\bm{x}}))&(0\leq t\leq\delta).\end{cases}

The following stability result may be proved similarly to
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The extension operator
P
satisfies

\displaystyle\|Pf\|_{W^{m,p}(\Gamma(\delta))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega\cap\Gamma(3\delta))}\quad m\in\{0,1,2,3\},\;p\in[1,\infty],

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
\delta
and
f
.

3.
Finite element approximation

We introduce the global nodes of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
by

\mathcal{N}_{h}=\{\bm{F}_{T}(\hat{\bm{a}}_{i})\in\overline{\Omega}_{h}\mid T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\;i=1,\dots,N_{k}\}.

The interior and boundary nodes are denoted by
\mathring{\mathcal{N}}_{h}=\mathcal{N}_{h}\cap\operatorname{int}\Omega_{h}
and
\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\partial}=\mathcal{N}_{h}\cap\Gamma_{h}
, respectively.
We next define the global nodal basis functions
\phi_{\bm{a}}\,(\bm{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h})
by

\phi_{\bm{a}}|_{T}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\bm{a}\notin T,\\
\hat{\phi}_{i}\circ\bm{F}_{T}^{-1}&\text{ if $\bm{a}\in T$ and $\bm{a}=\bm{F}_{T}(\hat{\bm{a}_{i}})$ with $\hat{\bm{a}_{i}}\in\hat{\Sigma}_{k}$},\end{cases}\quad(\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h})

which become continuous in
\overline{\Omega}_{h}
thanks to the assumption on
\hat{\Sigma}_{k}
.
Then
\phi_{\bm{a}}(\bm{b})=1
if
\bm{a}=\bm{b}
and
\phi_{\bm{a}}(\bm{b})=0
otherwise, for
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
.
We now set the
\mathbb{P}_{k}
-isoparametric finite element space
V_{h}
by

V_{h}=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{\bm{a}}\}_{\bm{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}=\{v_{h}\in C(\overline{\Omega}_{h})\mid\hat{v}_{h}:=v_{h}\circ\bm{F}_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{T})\;(\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h})\},

and its subspace
\mathring{V}_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h})
with the zero boundary value constraint by

\mathring{V}_{h}=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{\bm{a}}\}_{\bm{a}\in\mathring{\mathcal{N}}_{h}}=\{v_{h}\in V_{h}\mid v_{h}=0\;\text{ on }\;\Gamma_{h}\}.

By the chain rule
\nabla_{\bm{x}}=(\nabla_{\bm{x}}\bm{F}_{T}^{-1})\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}
and by (
2.2
), we have

\|\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{m}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}^{N/p}\sum_{l=1}^{m}h_{T}^{-l}\|\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}^{l}(v\circ\bm{F}_{T})\|_{L^{p}(\hat{T})}\quad(v\in W^{m,p}(T))

for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},m\geq 1
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
.
In particular, if
v=v_{h}\in V_{h}
and
m\geq k+1
(thus
\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}^{k+1}\hat{v}_{h}\equiv 0
in
\hat{T}
) it follows from
\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}=(\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\bm{F}_{T})\nabla_{\bm{x}}
and
(H4)
that (cf.
[
16
, p. 187]
)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{m}v_{h}\|_{L^{p}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{T}^{N/p}\sum_{l=1}^{k}h_{T}^{-l}\|\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}^{l}\hat{v}_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\hat{T})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{T}^{N/p}\sum_{l=1}^{k}h_{T}^{-l}\cdot h_{T}^{-N/p}h_{T}^{l}\|v_{h}\|_{W^{k,p}(T)}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{W^{k,p}(T)}.

Next, for
v\in C(\overline{\Omega}_{h})
the Lagrange interpolation
I_{h}v\in V_{h}
is defined by
I_{h}v=\sum_{\bm{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}v(\bm{a})\phi_{\bm{a}}
.
Accordingly, we define the interpolation to
\mathring{V}_{h}
by
\mathring{I}_{h}v=\sum_{\bm{a}\in\mathring{\mathcal{N}}_{h}}v(\bm{a})\phi_{\bm{a}}
, neglecting values at the boundary nodes
\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\partial}
.
The difference between
I_{h}
and
\mathring{I}_{h}
is estimated in the next lemma (see the appendix for the proof).

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
S\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
satisfy
\emptyset\neq T\cap\Gamma_{h}\subset S
. Then we have

\|\nabla^{m}(v_{h}-\mathring{I}_{h}v_{h})\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}^{1/p-m}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{p}(S)}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

where
m=0,1,\dots
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

We recall from
[
3
, Theorem 5]
standard interpolation error estimates for
I_{h}
:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
l,m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
0\leq l\leq m\leq k+1
and
p\in[1,\infty]
.
Assume the embedding
W^{m,p}\hookrightarrow{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C^{0}}
holds for subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then we have

\|v-I_{h}v\|_{W^{l,p}(T)}\leq Ch^{m-l}\|v\|_{W^{m,p}(T)}\quad(T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\,v\in W^{m,p}(T)).

Estimates for
\mathring{I}_{h}
are, however, more involved because of domain perturbation (
u=0
on
\Gamma
does not necessarily imply
\tilde{u}=0
on
\Gamma_{h}
).
We state it in the following form, whose proof is similar to that of Proposition
5.1
below (we only have to consider global
\Omega_{h}
and set
v_{2}=0
there) and thus omitted here.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.2
, let
m\geq 2
and
v\in W^{m,p}(\tilde{\Omega})
satisfy
v=0
on
\Gamma
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla^{l}(v-\mathring{I}_{h}v)\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{m-l}\|v\|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega_{h})}+Ch^{k+1-l}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma(\delta))},

with the obvious modification for
p=\infty
.

4.
Reduction to
W^{1,1}
-analysis of a regularized Green function

Fixing arbitrary
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
z\in K
, we try to bound the pointwise error
\tilde{u}(\bm{z})-u_{h}(\bm{z})
.
We construct a regularized delta function; the proof is given in the appendix.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\bm{z}\in K
, there exists
\eta=\eta_{K,\bm{z}}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(K)
such that
\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{supp}\eta,\partial K)\geq Ch_{K}
,
\|\nabla^{m}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq Ch_{K}^{-N-m}\,(m=0,1)
, and

(v_{h},\eta)_{K}=v_{h}(\bm{z})\quad\text{for $v_{h}=\hat{v}_{h}\circ\bm{F}_{K}^{-1}$ with arbitrary $\hat{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{T})$},

where the constant
C
is independent of
K
,
\bm{z}
, and
h_{K}
.

Next we introduce a “dyadic decomposition” of
\Omega_{h}
.
We set a sequence of scales:

d_{0}=Lh,\quad d_{j}=2^{j}d_{0}\quad\text{for }\;j=1,\dots,J:=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\operatorname{diam}\Omega_{h}/d_{0})}{\log 2}\right\rceil,

where
L
means the ratio of the “initial stride”
d_{0}
to the “minimum scale”
h
.
As we see later,
L
will be taken sufficiently large (but independently of
h
).
Then we define a subset
\Omega_{h,j}
of
\Omega_{h}
—which has the scale
d_{j}
in terms of the distance from
K
—by

\displaystyle\Omega_{h0}

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\mid d(T,K)\leq d_{0}\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{h,j}

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\mid d_{j-1}<d(T,K)\leq d_{j}\}\;(j=1,\dots,J),

where
d(T,T^{\prime})=\min\{|\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|\mid\bm{x}\in T,\bm{x}^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}\}
denotes a distance function between two elements
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
They are compatible with a standard ball
B(\bm{z};r)=\{\bm{x}\mid|\bm{x}-\bm{z}|\leq r\}
and annulus
A(\bm{z};r,R)=\{\bm{x}\mid r\leq|\bm{x}-\bm{z}|\leq R\}
.
In fact, by triangle inequalities, combined with
d_{J}\geq\operatorname{diam}\Omega_{h}
and
\operatorname{diam}T\leq Ch
for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Omega_{h}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\Omega_{h,j}\text{ (disjoint union)},\quad\Omega_{h0}\subset\Omega_{h}\cap B(\bm{z};2d_{0}),

\displaystyle\Omega_{h,j}

\displaystyle\subset\Omega_{h}\cap A_{j}^{(s)}\subset\Omega_{h,j-1}\cup\Omega_{h,j}\cup\Omega_{h,j+1}=:\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\,(j\geq 1),

where
A_{j}^{(s)}:=A(\bm{z};(1-\frac{s}{2})d_{j-1},(1+s)d_{j})

for all
s\in(0,1)
, provided that
L
is sufficiently large.
We also remark that
\sum_{j=\ell_{1}}^{\ell_{2}}d_{j}^{\alpha}
is bounded by
Cd_{\ell_{1}}^{\alpha}
if
\alpha<0
, by
C|\log d_{0}|
if
\alpha=0
, and by
Cd_{\ell_{2}}^{\alpha}
if
\alpha>0
, for
0\leq\ell_{1}\leq\ell_{2}\leq J
.

Now let us start the first part of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
For any
v_{h}\in\mathring{V}_{h}
we use the regularized delta function
\eta
constructed in Proposition
4.1
to get

(\tilde{u}-u_{h})(\bm{z})=(\tilde{u}-v_{h})(\bm{z})+(v_{h}-\tilde{u},\eta)_{\Omega_{h}}+(\tilde{u}-u_{h},\eta)_{\Omega_{h}}.

The first two terms on the right-hand side are bounded by
C\|\tilde{u}-v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}
.
To address the last term we define a regularized Green function
g\in W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)
by solving

-\Delta g=\eta\quad\text{in }\;\Omega,\qquad g=0\quad\text{on }\;\Gamma,

where
\eta
is extended by zero outside
\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset\Omega
(this inclusion holds if
h
is small).
We also utilize its finite element approximation
g_{h}\in\mathring{V}_{h}
obtained by solving

a_{h}(v_{h},g_{h})=(\nabla v_{h},\nabla g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}=(v_{h},\eta)_{\Omega_{h}}\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathring{V}_{h}.

Then it follows from
\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset\Omega_{h}\cap\Omega
and
u_{h}=v_{h}=g_{h}=0
on
\Gamma_{h}
that

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}-u_{h},\eta)_{\Omega_{h}}=(u-u_{h},-\Delta g)_{\Omega_{h}\cap\Omega}=(\tilde{u}-u_{h},-\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}}-(\tilde{u}-u_{h},-\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle a_{h}(\tilde{u}-u_{h},\tilde{g})-(\tilde{u}-u_{h},\partial_{\bm{n}_{h}}\tilde{g})_{\Gamma_{h}}+(\tilde{u}-u_{h},\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle a_{h}(\tilde{u}-u_{h},\tilde{g}-g_{h})+(\nabla(\tilde{u}-u_{h}),\nabla g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}-(\tilde{u},\partial_{\bm{n}_{h}}\tilde{g})_{\Gamma_{h}}+(\tilde{u}-u_{h},\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle a_{h}(\tilde{u}-v_{h},\tilde{g}-g_{h})+(-\Delta\tilde{u}-\tilde{f},g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}-(\tilde{u},\partial_{\bm{n}_{h}}\tilde{g})_{\Gamma_{h}}+(\tilde{u}-v_{h},\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega},

where
\tilde{g}:=Pg
,
\partial_{\bm{n}_{h}}
denotes the derivative in the direction of the outer unit normal
\bm{n}_{h}
to
\Gamma_{h}
, and we have used
a_{h}(v_{h}-u_{h},\tilde{g}-g_{h})=(u_{h}-v_{h},\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}
in the last line.

We estimate each term on the right-hand side above
as follows:

\displaystyle|a_{h}(\tilde{u}-v_{h},\tilde{g}-g_{h})|\leq\|\nabla(\tilde{u}-v_{h})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h})}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})},

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(-\Delta\tilde{u}-\tilde{f},g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}|&\leq C\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\delta\|\nabla g_{h}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}\\
&\leq C\delta\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}(\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}+\|\nabla\tilde{g}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma(\delta))})\\
&\leq C\delta\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}(\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}+\delta),\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\tilde{u},\partial_{\bm{n}_{h}}\tilde{g})_{\Gamma_{h}}&\leq\|\tilde{u}-u\circ\bm{\pi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\|\nabla\tilde{g}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq C\delta\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma(\delta))},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}-v_{h},\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}\leq\|\tilde{u}-v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h})}\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma(\delta))}\leq Ch^{-1}\delta\|\tilde{u}-v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h})},

where we have exploited (
2.3
)–(
2.5
) as well as the estimates

(4.1)

\|\nabla\tilde{g}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma(\delta))}\leq C\delta,\quad\|\nabla\tilde{g}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq C,\quad\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma(\delta))}\leq Ch^{-1}\delta.

Remark 4.1
.

The above results may be proved in the same way as
[
9
, Corollary B.1]
, which follows from the representation formula
g(\bm{x})=\int_{\Omega\cap K}G(\bm{x},\bm{y})\eta(\bm{y})\,d\bm{y}
essentially,
G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
denoting the Green function for the Laplace operator.
We emphasize in particular that, for the present situation of the Dirichlet boundary condition,
G
admits an auxiliary derivative estimates, which enables us to obtain the first two inequalities of (
4.1
) (see
[
9
, Remark B.2]
).

As a consequence, noting that
\delta\leq h
we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}(\bm{z})-u_{h}(\bm{z})|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{u}-v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\nabla(\tilde{u}-v_{h})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h})}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\delta\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}(\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}+1).

Therefore, Theorem
1.1
follows from
v_{h}=\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{u}
and interpolation estimates of Proposition
3.1
, provided that
\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch
.
Because
\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h,j})}
by the dyadic decomposition and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h,j})}\leq Cd_{j}^{N/2}\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}
, it suffices to show the weighted
H^{1}
-estimate

(4.2)

\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}\leq Ch,

which will be the goal of the next section.

5.
Weighted
H^{1}
-estimate

To prove (
4.2
), we first fix
j\in\{0,\dots,J\}
and estimate
\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}
.
Let
\omega\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a non-negative cut off function such that

\omega\equiv 1\;\text{ in }\;A_{j}^{(1/3)},\quad\operatorname{supp}\omega\subset A_{j}^{(2/3)},\quad\|\nabla^{m}\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq Cd_{j}^{-m}\;(m\geq 0).

From the construction of the dyadic decomposition, we see that
\omega
is identically 1 and 0 in
\Omega_{h,j}
and
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}
, respectively.
Then it follows that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{h}}\omega|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})|^{2}\,d\bm{x}

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\omega(\tilde{g}-g_{h}),\tilde{g}-g_{h})-(\nabla\omega(\tilde{g}-g_{h}),\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h}))_{\Omega_{h}}.

The second term
of (
5.1
)
is bounded by
Cd_{j}^{-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}
.

To deal with the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.1
), we set

e:=\omega(\tilde{g}-g_{h})=\omega(\tilde{g}-\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{g})+\omega(\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{g}-g_{h})=:e_{1}+e_{2}.

Observe that, since
e_{2}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h})
and
a_{h}(\cdot,\tilde{g}-g_{h})=(\cdot,-\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}
in
\mathring{V}_{h}
,

(5.2)

a_{h}(e,\tilde{g}-g_{h})=a_{h}(e_{1}-\mathring{I}_{h}e_{1},\tilde{g}-g_{h})+a_{h}(e_{2}-I_{h}e_{2},\tilde{g}-g_{h})+(\mathring{I}_{h}e,-\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega},

and that

|a_{h}(e_{1}-\mathring{I}_{h}e_{1},\tilde{g}-g_{h})|\leq\|\nabla(e_{1}-\mathring{I}_{h}e_{1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}.

Here we need the following localized interpolation error estimate for
\mathring{I}_{h}
, which is announced before Proposition
3.1
and will be proved in the appendix.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
l,m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
0\leq l\leq m\leq k+1
,
m\geq 2
and assume the embedding
W^{m,p}\hookrightarrow{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C^{0}}
holds for subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Moreover, assume that
v=v_{1}+v_{2}
, where
v_{1}\in W^{m,p}(\tilde{\Omega})
satisfies
v_{1}=0
on
\Gamma
and
v_{2}\in C(\overline{\Omega}_{h})
satisfies
v_{2}|_{T}\in W^{m,p}(T)
for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
with
v_{2}=0
on
\Gamma_{h}
.
Then, for
j=0,\dots,J
we have

(5.3)

\Big(\sum_{T\subset\Omega_{h,j}}\|\nabla^{l}(v-\mathring{I}_{h}v)\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq Ch^{m-l}\Big(\sum_{T\subset\Omega_{h,j}}\|v\|_{W^{m,p}(T)}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+Ch^{k+1-l}\|v_{1}\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega_{h,j}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j},\delta))},

where
\Gamma_{h,j}:=\Gamma_{h}\cap\partial\Omega_{h,j}
and we make the obvious modification for
p=\infty
.

Setting
\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime}:=\Gamma_{h}\cap\partial\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}
, we apply the above proposition twice (
v_{1}=\omega\tilde{g}
and
v_{2}=\omega\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{g}
for the first time, and
v_{1}=\tilde{g}
and
v_{2}=0
for the second time) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla(e_{1}-\mathring{I}_{h}e_{1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\Big(\sum_{T\subset\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}}\|\omega(\tilde{g}-\mathring{I}_{h}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{g}})\|_{H^{2}(T)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+Ch^{k}\|\omega\tilde{g}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+Ch^{k}\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}+Ch^{k}\|\omega\tilde{g}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))},

where the terms involving derivatives of
\omega
are addressed by
hd_{j}^{-1}\leq 1
.
If
j\leq 1
, by an elliptic regularity estimate we have

\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(K)}\leq Ch^{N/2}h^{-N-1}=CL^{N/2+1}d_{0}^{-N/2-1}.

On the other hand, if
j\geq 2
then we make use of derivative estimates for the Green function to deduce (cf.
[
9
, Lemma B.2]
, which shows
\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\leq Cd_{j}^{-N/2}
)

\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\leq Cd_{j}^{N/2}\|\tilde{g}\|_{W^{3,\infty}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\leq Cd_{j}^{N/2}d_{j}^{-N-1}=Cd_{j}^{-N/2-1}.

A similar argument bounds
\|\omega\tilde{g}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}
(and also
\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}
) by

\displaystyle Cd_{j}^{N/2}\Big(d_{j}^{-2}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\setminus\Omega_{h,j}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}+d_{j}^{-1}\|\nabla\tilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\setminus\Omega_{h,j}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}\Big)+C\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle Cd_{j}^{-N/2}|\log d_{j}|+\begin{cases}CL^{N/2}d_{0}^{-N/2}&(j\leq 1),\\
Cd_{j}^{-N/2}&(j\geq 2).\end{cases}

In view of
k\geq 2
,
d_{j}\leq C
, and
L\geq 1
, we conclude that

(5.4)

\|\nabla(e_{1}-\mathring{I}_{h}e_{1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\leq C_{j}h^{2}d_{j}^{-N/2-1},

where
C_{j}=CL^{N/2+1}
for
j\leq 1
and
C_{j}=C
for
j\geq 2
.
Therefore, the first term of (
5.2
) is estimated as

(5.5)

|a_{h}(e_{1}-I_{h}e_{1},\tilde{g}-g_{h})|\leq C_{j}d_{j}^{-N/2-1}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}.

Next, to estimate the second term of (
5.2
) we find from Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(e_{2}-I_{h}e_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{k+1}\Big(\sum_{T\subset\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}}\|\omega(\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{H^{m}(T)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{k+1}d_{j}^{-m}\Big(\sum_{T\subset\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}}\|\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{H^{k+1-m}(T)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

The term for
m=0
may be omitted by (
3.1
), hence this is bounded by

\displaystyle Ch^{k}\sum_{m=1}^{k+1}d_{j}^{-m}\Big(\sum_{T\subset\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime}}\|\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{H^{k+1-m}(T)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle Chd_{j}^{-1}\|\nabla(\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+Cd_{j}^{-1}\|\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle Chd_{j}^{-1}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+Cd_{j}^{-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+C_{j}h^{2}d_{j}^{-N/2-1},

where we have used
hd_{j}\leq 1
, inverse inequalities
h^{l}\|\nabla^{l+1}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq C\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}
, and an interpolation estimate for
\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-\mathring{I}_{h}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{g}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}
based on Proposition
5.1
(which also appeared in dealing with
e_{1}
).
Consequently, the second term
a_{h}(e_{2}-I_{h}e_{2},\tilde{g}-g_{h})
is bounded by

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(e_{2}-I_{h}e_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Chd_{j}^{-1}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}^{2}+Cd_{j}^{-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle+C_{j}h^{2}d_{j}^{-N/2-1}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}.

Finally, we apply (
2.5
) (note that
\mathring{I}_{h}e=0
on
\Gamma_{h}
) to estimate the last term of (
5.2
) as

\displaystyle|(\mathring{I}_{h}e,-\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\|\nabla(\mathring{I}_{h}e)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega)}\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla e\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\nabla(e-\mathring{I}_{h}e)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})})\cdot(\delta d_{j}^{N-1})^{1/2}d_{j}^{-N},

where we refer to
[
9
, Lemma B.3]
for the bound of
\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,j}^{\prime},\delta))}
. Since

\displaystyle\|\nabla e\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+Cd_{j}^{-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})},

\displaystyle\|\nabla(e-\mathring{I}_{h}e)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq C(hd_{j}^{-1}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+d_{j}^{-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})})+C_{j}h^{2}d_{j}^{-N/2-1}

(recall (
5.4
), (
5.6
), and
\mathring{I}_{h}e_{2}=I_{h}e_{2}
), we see that the last term
(\mathring{I}_{h}e,-\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}
is bounded by

(5.8)

C\delta^{3/2}d_{j}^{-(N+1)/2}(\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+d_{j}^{-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+C_{j}h^{2}d_{j}^{-N/2-1}).

Now, combining the estimates
(
5.5
), (
5.7
), and (
5.8
)
with (
5.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Chd_{j}^{-1}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}^{2}+Cd_{j}^{-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{j}h^{2}d_{j}^{-N/2-1}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\delta^{3/2}d_{j}^{-(N+1)/2}(\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+d_{j}^{-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+C_{j}h^{2}d_{j}^{-N/2-1}).

Taking the square root and multiplying by
d_{j}^{N/2}
yield (note that
hd_{j}^{-1}\leq L^{-1}
)

\displaystyle d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}

\displaystyle\leq CL^{-1/2}d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}+C_{j}^{1/2}(h^{2}d_{j}^{-1})^{1/2}(d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\Big(d_{j}^{N/2-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\delta^{3/4}d_{j}^{-1/4}\Big[\Big(d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(d_{j}^{N/2-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\Big)^{1/2}\Big]

(5.9)

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{j}h\delta^{3/4}d_{j}^{-3/4}.

By summation for
j=0,\dots,J
and
\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2}\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j}^{\prime})}\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2}\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}
, combined with the use of Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(C^{\prime}L^{-1/2}+\frac{1}{4}\Big)\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}+CL^{N/2+1}h^{2}d_{0}^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}+C\delta^{3/2}d_{0}^{-1/2}+CL^{N/2+1}h\delta^{3/4}d_{0}^{-3/4},

which concludes, if
L
is chosen large enough to satisfy
C^{\prime}L^{-1/2}\leq 1/4
,

\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}\leq CL^{N/2+1}h+C\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}.

Therefore, (
4.2
) is proved, once a weighted
L^{2}
-error estimate of the form

(5.10)

\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}\leq CL^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}+Ch

is established (
L
is chosen sufficiently large again).

Remark 5.1
.

The assumption
k\geq 2
, that is, we restrict our consideration to quadratic or higher-order elements, is essential because for
k=1
(linear element) we would have only a factor
h
instead of
h^{2}d_{j}^{-1}
in (
5.9
), which results in an
O(h|\log h|)
-term after the summation over
j
.

6.
Weighted
L^{2}
-estimate

Let us prove (
5.10
) above.
First we fix
j=0,\dots,J
and estimate
\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}
.
To this end, for arbitrary
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{h,j})
such that
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}=1
we define
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)
to be the solution of a dual problem (
\psi
is extended by zero outside
\Omega_{h,j}
)

-\Delta w=\psi\quad\text{in }\;\Omega,\qquad w=0\quad\text{on }\;\Gamma.

Setting
\tilde{w}:=Pw
, we observe (recall
a_{h}(\cdot,\tilde{g}-g_{h})=(\cdot,-\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}
in
\mathring{V}_{h}
):

(6.1)

\displaystyle(\psi,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta w,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\cap\Omega}+(\psi,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta w,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}+(\Delta\tilde{w}+\psi,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\tilde{w},\tilde{g}-g_{h})-(\partial_{\bm{n}_{h}}\tilde{w},\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}}+(\Delta\tilde{w}+\psi,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\tilde{w}-\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w},\tilde{g}-g_{h})-(\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w},\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}-(\partial_{\bm{n}_{h}}\tilde{w},\tilde{g})_{\Gamma_{h}}+(\Delta\tilde{w}+\psi,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}.

The first term on the right-hand side of (
6.1
) is bounded, using Proposition
5.1
, by

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}\|\nabla(\tilde{w}-\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}\leq Ch\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,\ell},\delta))}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}.

If
\ell\leq j-2
, since
\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_{h,\ell},\Omega_{h,j})\geq\frac{1}{4}d_{j}
and
d_{j}\geq d_{\ell}
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,\ell},\delta))}\leq Cd_{\ell}^{N/2}\cdot d_{j}^{N/2}d_{j}^{-N}=Cd_{\ell}^{N/2}d_{j}^{-N/2},

where we have used the representation formula
w(\bm{x})=\int_{\Omega\cap\Omega_{h,j}}G(\bm{x},\bm{y})\psi(\bm{y})\,d\bm{y}
as well as derivative estimates for
G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, cf.
[
9
, Lemma B.4]
.
Similarly, if
\ell\geq j+2
, this quantity is bounded by
Cd_{\ell}^{-N/2}d_{j}^{N/2}\leq Cd_{\ell}^{N/2}d_{j}^{-N/2}
using the facts
\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_{h,\ell},\Omega_{h,j})\geq\frac{1}{2}d_{\ell}
and
d_{\ell}\geq d_{j}
.
For the remaining case
j-1\leq\ell\leq j+1
, it follows from the global elliptic
H^{2}
-regularity estimate that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,\ell},\delta))}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C.

Consequently,

(6.2)

|a_{h}(\tilde{w}-\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w},\tilde{g}-g_{h})|\leq Chd_{j}^{-N/2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}d_{\ell}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}.

For the second term of (
6.1
), we see that

\displaystyle|(\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w},\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}\|\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\setminus\Omega)}\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\setminus\Omega)},

where
\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\setminus\Omega)}
can be bounded by
C\delta^{1/2}d_{\ell}^{-(N+1)/2}
, cf.
[
9
, Lemma B.3]
.
Moreover, by (
2.5
) (note that
\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w}=0
on
\Gamma_{h}
), (
2.3
), and Proposition
5.1
(with
v_{2}=0
),

\displaystyle\|\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\|\nabla(\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\setminus\Omega)}+\|\nabla(\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w}-\tilde{w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})})

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{3/2}\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h,\ell})}+C\delta h\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\cup\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,\ell},\delta))}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}C\delta^{3/2}d_{\ell}^{(N-1)/2}d_{j}^{-N/2+1}+Ch\delta d_{\ell}^{N/2}d_{j}^{-N/2}&(\ell\leq j-2),\\
C\delta^{3/2}+Ch\delta&(\ell=j,j\pm 1),\\
C\delta^{3/2}d_{\ell}^{(1-N)/2}d_{j}^{N/2}+Ch\delta d_{\ell}^{-N/2}d_{j}^{N/2}&(\ell\geq j+2),\end{cases}

where we have used (cf.
[
9
, Lemma B.4]
)

(6.3)

\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h,\ell})}\leq\begin{cases}C\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h})}\leq C&\quad(\ell=j,j\pm 1),\\
Cd_{\ell}^{(N-1)/2}\cdot d_{j}^{N/2}\max\{d_{\ell},d_{j}\}^{1-N}&\quad(\ell\neq j,j\pm 1).\end{cases}

Consequently, it follows that

(6.4)

\displaystyle|(\mathring{I}_{h}\tilde{w},\Delta\tilde{g})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-2}(\delta^{2}d_{\ell}^{-1}d_{j}^{-N/2+1}+h\delta^{3/2}d_{\ell}^{-1/2}d_{j}^{-N/2})+Ch\delta^{3/2}d_{j}^{-(N+1)/2}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{\ell=j+2}^{J}(\delta^{2}d_{\ell}^{-N}d_{j}^{N/2}+h\delta^{3/2}d_{\ell}^{-N-1/2}d_{j}^{N/2})

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}d_{j}^{-N/2}+Ch\delta^{3/2}d_{j}^{-(N+1)/2}.

The third term of (
6.1
) is bounded by (note that
g\circ\bm{\pi}=0
on
\Gamma_{h}
)

(6.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h,\ell})}\|\tilde{g}-g\circ\bm{\pi}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h,\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h,\ell})}(C\delta\|\nabla\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h,\ell})}+C\delta^{3/2}\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,\ell},\delta))})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-2}d_{\ell}^{(N-1)/2}d_{j}^{-N/2+1}\cdot\delta d_{\ell}^{(1-N)/2}+\delta d_{j}^{(1-N)/2}+\sum_{\ell=j+2}^{J}d_{\ell}^{(1-N)/2}d_{j}^{N/2}\cdot\delta d_{\ell}^{(1-N)/2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|\log h|\delta d_{j}^{-N/2+1}+C\delta d_{j}^{(1-N)/2},

where we have used (
2.4
) and (
2.3
) in the second line, and (
6.3
) and
[
9
, Lemma B.3]
in the third line.

Finally, recalling the argument before (
6.2
), especially
d_{\ell}\lesseqgtr d_{j}
for
\ell\lesseqgtr j
, we get

\displaystyle\|\Delta\tilde{w}+\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}\leq\begin{cases}Cd_{\ell}^{N/2}d_{j}^{-N/2}\quad&(\ell\leq j),\\
Cd_{\ell}^{-N/2}d_{j}^{N/2}\quad&(\ell\geq j+1).\end{cases}

In addition, since
g_{h}=g\circ\bm{\pi}=0
on
\Gamma_{h}
,

(by (
2.5
))

\displaystyle\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{1/2}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h,\ell})}+C\delta\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell}\setminus\Omega)}

(by (
2.4
))

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}+\|\nabla\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\bm{\pi}(\Gamma_{h,\ell},\delta))})

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}+C\delta^{3/2}d_{\ell}^{(1-N)/2}.

Therefore, we can bound the last term
(\Delta\tilde{w}+\psi,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}
of (
6.1
) by

\displaystyle C\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}d_{\ell}^{N/2}d_{j}^{-N/2}(\delta\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}+\delta^{3/2}d_{\ell}^{(1-N)/2})

\displaystyle+\;

\displaystyle C\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{J}d_{\ell}^{-N/2}d_{j}^{N/2}(\delta\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}+\delta^{3/2}d_{\ell}^{(1-N)/2})

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle C\delta d_{j}^{-N/2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}d_{\ell}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}+C\delta^{3/2}d_{j}^{(1-N)/2}.

Combining the estimates (
6.2
), (
6.4
), (
6.5
), (
6.6
) with (
6.1
) and noting that
\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}
equals the supremum of
(\psi,\tilde{g}-g_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}
with respect to
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{h,j})
such that
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}=1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle d_{j}^{N/2-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}

\displaystyle\leq Chd_{j}^{-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}d_{\ell}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}+C(\delta^{2}d_{j}^{-1}+h\delta^{3/2}d_{j}^{-3/2})

\displaystyle\quad+C\delta(|\log h|+d_{j}^{-1/2})+C\delta d_{j}^{-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}d_{\ell}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}+C\delta^{3/2}d_{j}^{-1/2}.

We add this for
j=0,\dots,J
and notice
hd_{0}\leq L^{-1}
to conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_{j}^{N/2-1}\|\tilde{g}-g_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,j})}

\displaystyle\leq CL^{-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}d_{\ell}^{N/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{g}-g_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h,\ell})}+C(\delta^{2}h^{-1}+\delta^{3/2}h^{-1/2}+\delta|\log h|^{2}+\delta h^{-1/2}).

Since
\delta=Ch^{k+1}
(in particular less than
Ch^{2})
, we have shown (
5.10
), thus completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

\operatorname{int}\hat{T}

\|\nabla^{m}\hat{\omega}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}}\leq C\,(m\geq 0)

.

Noticing that
\{\phi_{\bm{a}}\}_{\bm{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\cap K}
forms a basis of
V_{h}|_{K}
, we construct its dual basis
\{\psi_{\bm{b}}\}_{\bm{b}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\cap K}
with respect to the inner product
(\cdot,\omega_{K}\cdot)_{K}

\int_{K}\phi_{\bm{a}}(\bm{x})\omega_{K}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bm{x}})\psi_{\bm{b}}(\bm{x})\,d\bm{x}=\int_{\hat{T}}\hat{\phi}_{i}(\hat{\bm{x}})\hat{\omega}(\hat{\bm{x}})\hat{\psi}_{j}(\hat{\bm{x}})J_{K}\,d\hat{\bm{x}}=\delta_{ij},

\bm{F}_{T}(\hat{\bm{a}}_{i})=\bm{a}

\eta_{K,\bm{z}}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\cap K}\phi_{\bm{a}}(\bm{z})\omega_{K}(\bm{x})\psi_{\bm{a}}(\bm{x})\quad(\bm{x}\in K).

(\eta_{K,\bm{z}},v_{h})_{K}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{h}(\bm{z})}
for any

\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{supp}\omega_{K},\partial K)\geq Ch_{K}

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

In view of the inverse inequality
\|\nabla^{m}(v_{h}-\mathring{I}_{h}v_{h})\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}^{-m}\|v_{h}-\mathring{I}_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{p}(T)}
, it is enough to consider
m=0
.
We see that, for
\bm{x}\in T
,

|(v_{h}-\mathring{I}_{h}v_{h})(\bm{x})|^{p}=\Big|\sum_{\bm{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\partial}\cap T}v_{h}(\bm{a})\phi_{\bm{a}}(\bm{x})\Big|^{p}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}^{p}\sum_{\bm{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\cap T}|\phi_{\bm{a}}(\bm{x})|^{p},

where we note that
\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\partial}\cap T\subset S
by assumption.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{T}\sum_{\bm{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\cap T}|\phi_{\bm{a}}(\bm{x})|^{p}\,d\bm{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\phi}_{i}(\hat{\bm{x}})|^{p}|\operatorname{det}\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\bm{F}_{T}|\,d\hat{\bm{x}}\leq Ch_{T}^{N}.

Next let
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
denote the element that contains
S
.
We may assume that
\hat{S}=\bm{F}_{T^{\prime}}^{-1}(S)
, up to a further orthogonal transformation, is contained in the plane
\hat{x}_{N}=0
.
The Riemannian metric tensor
G
obtained from the parametrization
(\hat{\bm{x}}^{\prime},0)\mapsto\bm{F}_{T^{\prime}}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{\prime},0)
satisfies
Ch_{T^{\prime}}^{N-1}\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{det}G}\leq Ch_{T^{\prime}}^{N-1}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}^{p}=\|\hat{v}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{S})}^{p}\leq C\|\hat{v}_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\hat{S})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{T}^{1-N}\int_{\hat{S}}|\hat{v}_{h}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{\prime},0)|^{p}\sqrt{\operatorname{det}G}\,d\hat{\bm{x}}^{\prime}=Ch_{T}^{1-N}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{p}(S)}^{p},

where we have used norm equivalence for
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{S})
and
h_{T}\approx h_{T^{\prime}}
(by the shape-regularity).
From these we conclude the desired estimate for
m=0
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

If
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\,T\subset\Omega_{h,j}
has no intersection with
\Gamma_{h}
, we have
(\mathring{I}_{h}v)|_{T}=(I_{h}v)|_{T}

).

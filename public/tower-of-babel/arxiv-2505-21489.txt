5-Dimensional Gravitational Raman Scattering: Scalar Wave Perturbations in Schwarzschild-Tangherlini Spacetime
Samim Akhtar, Yilber Fabian Bautista, Cristoforo Iossa, Zihan Zhou

Abstract
In this Letter, we study scalar wave perturbations of arbitrary frequency to the 5D Schwarzschild-Tangherlini black hole (STBH) within general relativity. For the first time, we derive a closed formula for the 5D partial wave gravitational Raman scattering amplitude applicable to a broad class of boundary conditions, expressed in terms of the Nekrasov-Shatashvili (NS) function for the reduced confluent Heun problem. Furthermore, up to 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 2 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{2}) we compute the dynamical ℓ = 0 \ell=0 , and the static ℓ = 1 \ell=1 , scalar tidal Love numbers of the STBH by matching an effective field theory description for a scalar wave scattering off the black hole, to our novel ultraviolet-NS solutions. The matched Love numbers do not vanish and present renormalization group running behavior.

–

Discussion
–
In this paper, we have studied general frequency, scalar linear perturbations of 5D STBH, providing both, a UV-complete solution for the scattering problem and, for the first time, an on-shell matching for the static
\ell=1
and dynamical
\ell=0
elastic Love numbers for the STBH at 2PM, and the
\ell=0
,
G^{3/2}
dissipative Love number,
for a generic class of boundary conditions imposed at the BH horizon. Non-trivial near and far zone contributions to the tidal coefficients are manifest, and some universality in the leading PM elastic Love numbers is observed.
Higher PM EFT computations proving even-
\ell
static Love numbers are left for future work, although from the discussion around Eq. (
15
) we expect them to be RG-independent and vanishing.

Although we have obtained the connection formula (
10
) using as main example massless scalar perturbations to the 5D STBH, the formula is also valid for massless
perturbations of spin-weight
s=1,2
. Indeed, once the perturbation equations for spin-weight-
s
are written in terms of gauge invariant master variables
[
62
,
63
,
54
]
, it is easy to show they correspond to RCHE with their respective gauge-gravity dictionary, even with the absence of electric-magnetic duality in 5D. Similarly, for generic spacetime dimensions, the radial equation still has two regular singular points located at
r=0
and
r=r_{s,D}
, the position of the BH-horizon in D-dimensions, but now the irregular singularity at
r=\infty
has Poincaré rank
h=\frac{1}{D-3}
. In
D=4,5
one recovers the confluent, and reduced confluent Heun equations respectively, whereas in the
D\to\infty
limit, the radial equation becomes the hypergeometric equation.

Appendix A
Connection formula derivation details

In this section we provide the details for the derivation of the connection formula in Eq. (
10
).

1.1
Incoming boundary conditions at the BH horizon

As a warm-up up we consider purely ibcs first.
Near the BH-horizon, the differential equation (
5
) has two solutions

w(z)\simeq(1-z)^{\frac{1}{2}\pm a_{1}}\,~~~~~\text{as}~~~~z\rightarrow 1\,.

(26)

In order to impose purely ingoing wave-modes at the horizon, one has to choose for
w(z)

\displaystyle w^{\text{ibc.}}(z)\simeq(1-z)^{\frac{1}{2}+a_{1}}\,~~~~~\text{as}~~~~z\rightarrow 1\,.

(27)

This solution can now be expanded close to
z=\infty
or equivalently
r=\infty
. We find (see Ref.
[
25
]
):

\displaystyle w^{\text{ibc.}}(z)=D^{-}_{lm\omega}e^{L\sqrt{z}}\sqrt{z}^{-1/2}(1+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1/2}))+D^{+}_{lm\omega}e^{-L\sqrt{z}}\sqrt{z}^{-1/2}(1+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1/2}))\,,

(28)

where
D^{\pm}_{lm\omega}
are the connection coefficients of the RCHE given by

\displaystyle D^{-}_{lm\omega}=\sum_{\sigma=\pm}L^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\sigma a}\frac{\Gamma(1-2\sigma a)\Gamma(-2\sigma a)\Gamma(1+2a_{1})2^{-2\sigma a}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+a_{1}+a_{0}-\sigma a)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+a_{1}-a_{0}-\sigma a)}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}\partial_{a}F}\,.

(29)

D^{+}_{lm\omega}
can be obtained from
{}_{s}D^{-}_{lm\omega}
via
L\rightarrow-L
.
Next, we map Eq. (
28
) to the asymptotic behavior given in Eq. (
7
). As
r\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle z^{\frac{1}{4}}(z-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{L\sqrt{z}}\sqrt{z}^{-1/2}\simeq e^{-i\omega r}r^{-1}\left(r_{s,5}\right)\,,

(30)

\displaystyle z^{\frac{1}{4}}(z-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-L\sqrt{z}}\sqrt{z}^{-1/2}\simeq e^{i\omega r}r^{-1}\left(r_{s,5}\right)\,,

(31)

where we have used for the tortoise coordinate,
r_{\star}=r+\frac{1}{2}r_{s,5}\log{\left|\frac{r-r_{s,5}}{r+r_{s,5}}\right|}
, so that

\displaystyle r^{-3/2}e^{-i\omega r_{\star}}\simeq r^{-3/2}e^{-i\omega r}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle r^{-3/2}e^{i\omega r_{\star}}\simeq r^{-3/2}e^{i\omega r}\,.

(33)

Finally, the ratio of the asymptotic coefficients in Eq. (
7
) is

\displaystyle\frac{B^{ref}_{lm}}{B^{inc}_{lm}}=\frac{D^{+}_{lm\omega}}{D^{-}_{lm\omega}}\,,

(34)

which, after simplifying, becomes

\displaystyle\frac{B^{ref}_{lm}}{B^{inc}_{lm}}

\displaystyle=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\frac{\Gamma(1-2\sigma a)\Gamma(-2\sigma a)(-L)^{2\sigma a}2^{-2\sigma a}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}\partial_{a}F}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+a_{1}+a_{0}-\sigma a)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+a_{1}-a_{0}-\sigma a)}}{\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}=\pm}\frac{\Gamma(1-2\sigma^{\prime}a)\Gamma(-2\sigma^{\prime}a)L^{2\sigma^{\prime}a}2^{-2\sigma^{\prime}a}e^{-\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{2}\partial_{a}F}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+a_{1}+a_{0}-\sigma^{\prime}a)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+a_{1}-a_{0}-\sigma^{\prime}a)}}\,.

(35)

A further simplification turns this into

\displaystyle\frac{B^{ref}_{lm}}{B^{inc}_{lm}}

\displaystyle=e^{i\pi(2a-\frac{1}{2})}\times\frac{1+e^{-2i\pi a}\mathcal{K}_{5}}{1+e^{2i\pi a}\mathcal{K}_{5}}\,,

(36)

where we have used
-1=e^{i\pi}
and
-i=e^{-i\pi/2}
, and the tidal response function,
\mathcal{K}_{5}
defined in Eq. (
11
). Using Eq. (
36
) into Eq. (
9
), we recover the result given in Eq. (
10
) in the main text with
B=0
.

1.2
Outgoing boundary conditions at the BH horizon

In a similar way, we can instead impose purely outgoing wave modes at the BH horizon. This is, take

\displaystyle w^{\text{obc.}}(z)\simeq(1-z)^{\frac{1}{2}-a_{1}}\,~~~~~\text{as}~~~~z\rightarrow 1\,.

(37)

Now one can expand the equation close to
z=\infty
or
r=\infty
. The connection formula for the outgoing solution can be obtained via the replacement
a_{1}\rightarrow-a_{1}
in the ingoing one
[
25
]
. We simply arrive at the ratio of asymptotic coefficients

\displaystyle\frac{B^{ref}_{lm}}{B^{inc}_{lm}}\Big|_{\text{obc.}}

\displaystyle=e^{i\pi(2a-\frac{1}{2})}\times\frac{1+e^{-2i\pi a}\mathcal{K}_{5}^{\text{obc.}}}{1+e^{2i\pi a}\mathcal{K}_{5}^{\text{obc.}}}\,.

(38)

Here
\mathcal{K}_{5}^{\text{obc.}}
is the tidal response function for the outgoing solution given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{5}^{\text{obc.}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{5}|_{a_{1}\rightarrow-a_{1}}

(39)

\displaystyle=|L|^{-4a}2^{4a}\frac{\Gamma(2a)\Gamma(1+2a)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-a_{1}+a_{0}-a)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-a_{1}-a_{0}-a)}{\Gamma(-2a)\Gamma(1-2a)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-a_{1}+a_{0}+a)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-a_{1}-a_{0}+a)}e^{\partial_{a}F}\,.

(40)

recovering the result in the main text (
10
) for
A=0
.

1.3
Semi-Reflective boundary conditions at the BH horizon

A more general class of semi-reflecting boundary conditions can be imposed at the BHs. We take for the superposition

\displaystyle w^{\text{srbc.}}(z)\simeq A(1-z)^{\frac{1}{2}+a_{1}}+B(1-z)^{\frac{1}{2}-a_{1}}\,~~~~~\text{as}~~~~z\rightarrow 1\,.

(41)

where
A,B
are real numbers. The results for ibc. (obc.) are recovered by setting
B=0\,,\,A=1
(

The rank-

\displaystyle\left\langle\Lambda^{2}\right|L_{0}=\Lambda^{2}\partial_{\Lambda^{2}}\left\langle\Lambda^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\left\langle\Lambda^{2}\right|L_{-n}=0,\quad n>1~,

are the Virasoro generators. The NS function can be defined as the following classical conformal block

F=\lim_{b^{2}\rightarrow 0}b^{2}\log\langle\Lambda^{2}|V_{\alpha_{1}}(1)|\Delta_{\alpha_{0}}\rangle_{\rm CB}~,

(62)

where the subscript CB indicates that we only consider the conformal block associated with the correlators.
We want to evaluate the above expression in the limit where
\alpha_{1}\gg\alpha_{0}
. Note that the shift of the operators is given by

V_{\alpha_{1}}(1)=e^{L_{-1}}V_{\alpha_{1}}(0)e^{-L_{-1}}~.

and therefore

(64)

approximately as vacuum

. Since the CFT vacuum is invariant under translation, i.e.
e^{-L_{-1}}|0\rangle=|0\rangle
and thus

(65)

Then, we get the

F\simeq-\frac{L^{2}}{4}~,\quad a_{1}\gg a_{0}~.

(66)

(67)

3.1
Partial Wave Transformation and Exponential Representation of S-matrix

In this appendix, we provide details on the calculation of the one-loop wave scattering amplitude off the black holes in general

\bm{S}\equiv\mathbf{1}+i\bm{T}~,

(69)

where
\bm{T}
is the scattering operator. Considering the following 1-to-1 scattering amplitude within the background gravitational source

\left\langle k_{2}\right|i\bm{T}\left|k_{1}\right\rangle=i\mathcal{M}\left(k_{1}\rightarrow k_{2}\right)\cdot(2\pi)\delta\left(u\cdot\left(k_{1}+k_{2}\right)\right)~,

(70)

where

are the four momenta for the incoming and outgoing particles, and

is the four momentum of the black hole. The
\delta

transverse to

onto the generalized spherical harmonics

\left(\eta_{\ell}e^{2i\delta_{\ell}}-1\right)=\frac{i\omega}{2\pi}\frac{\left(4\pi/\omega^{2}\right)^{\frac{4D}{2}}}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{D-2}{2}\right)}\int_{-1}^{1}dz\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{\frac{D-4}{2}}P_{\ell}^{(D)}(z)\mathcal{M}(\omega,z)~,

(71)

with the Gegenbauer function

(72)

\bm{S}\bm{S}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{1}

(73)

also implies another useful parametrization, known as exponential parametrization
[
38
]

\bm{S}\equiv\exp(i\bm{\Delta})~,

(74)

with

\langle k_{2},h_{2}|i\bm{\Delta}|k_{1},h_{1}\rangle=i\Delta(k_{1},h_{1}\rightarrow k_{2},h_{2})(2\pi)\delta(u\cdot(k_{1}+k_{2}))~.

(75)

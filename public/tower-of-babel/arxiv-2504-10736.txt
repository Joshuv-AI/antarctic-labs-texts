Abstract
. We study the lower central series of the right-angled Coxeter group RC 𝒦 \mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}} and the corresponding associated graded Lie algebra L ⁡ ( RC 𝒦 ) L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}) and describe the basis of the fourth graded component of L ⁡ ( RC 𝒦 ) L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}) for any 𝒦 \mathcal{K} .



1.
Introduction

A right-angled Coxeter group
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
is a group with
m

generators
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
satisfying the relations
g_{i}^{2}=1
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and the commutation relations

g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i}
for some pairs
\{i,j\}
. Each such group can be defined by a graph
\mathcal{K}^{1}
with
m
vertices, where two vertices are connected by an edge whenever the corresponding generators commute. Right-angled Coxeter groups are classical objects in geometric group theory. In the present paper, we study the lower central series of the right-angled Coxeter groups
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
and the corresponding associated graded Lie algebra
L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
.

For the right-angled Artin groups
\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
(which differ from the right-angled Coxeter groups
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
in that the relations
g_{i}^{2}=1
are absent) the associated Lie algebars
L(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
were completely calculated in
[
1
]
, see also
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
. Specifically, it was proved that the Lie algebra
L(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is isomorphic to the graph Lie algebra (over
\mathbb{Z}
) corresponding to the graph
\mathcal{K}^{1}
.

For the right-angled Coxeter groups, the quotient groups
\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})/\gamma_{n}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
were studied in some
special cases for some
n
(
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
). For
n\geqslant 4
these studies encountered some difficulties similar to those we experienced when calculating the successive quotients
\gamma_{n-1}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})/\gamma_{n}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
. The problem of describing the associated Lie algebra
L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
for the right-angled Coxeter groups is much more
complicated than for the right-angled Artin groups, because the Lie algebra
L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
and the graph Lie
algebra
L_{\mathcal{K}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
are not necessarily isomorphic (see
[
6
, Example 4.3]
). In
[
6
]
, a Lie algebra epimorphism
L_{\mathcal{K}}\rightarrow L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
was constructed, its kernel was described in severl cases, and a combinatorial description of the bases of the first three graded components of the Lie algebra was given for an arbitrary group
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
. In
[
7
]
, a minimal set of generators in
L^{4}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
was constructed for the case in whick
\mathcal{K}
is the discrete simplicial complex on four points or an arbitrary simplicial complex on three points.

In
[
8
]
, the dimensions of successive lower central series quotients were calculated and bases in these
quotients were constructed for free products of direct sums of cyclic groups of order 2, which form
a subset of the set of right-angled Coxeter groups. In the present paper, we construct a basis of the fourth
graded component of the associated Lie algebra for Coxeter groups on four points (for the case of two
points, the associated Lie algebra has been completely described; see
[
6
, Proposition 4.4]
) and present
an algorithm for constructing a basis of the fourth graded component of the associated Lie algebra for
Coxeter groups in the general case. In contrast to the bases constructed in
[
8
]
, our bases completely consist of nested commutators.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{K}
be an (abstract) simplicial complex on the set
[m]=\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. A subset

I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in\mathcal{K}

is called a

simplex
(or a
face
) of
\mathcal{K}
. Throughout the paper, we assume that
\mathcal{K}
contains
\varnothing
and all singletons
\{i\}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
.

By
F_{m}
or
F(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})
we denote the free group of rank
m
with generators
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
.

The
right-angled Coxeter (Artin) group
corresponding to
\mathcal{K}
is the group
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
(
\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
) defined by generators and relations as follows:

\displaystyle\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=F(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\big/(g_{i}^{2}=1\text{ for }i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\;\;g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i}\text{ for
}\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}),

\displaystyle\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=F(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\big/(g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i}\text{ for
}\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}).

It follows from the definition that the group
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
(
\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
) depends only on the graph
\mathcal{K}^{1}
, i.e., on the
1-skeleton of
\mathcal{K}
.

Consider the construction of the polyhedral product. Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex on the set
[m]
, and let

({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})=\{(X_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(X_{m},A_{m})\}

be a sequence of
m
pairs of pointed topological spaces, where
\mathit{pt}\in A_{i}\subset X_{i}
. For each subset

I\subset[m]
, we set

(2.1)

({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{I}=\bigl\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\prod_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}\colon\;x_{k}\in A_{k}\quad\text{for }k\notin I\bigl\}

and define the
polyhedral product

({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{I}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}\Bigl(\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}\times\prod_{i\notin I}A_{i}\Bigl),

where the union is taken inside the product
\prod_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}
.

If all pairs
(X_{i},A_{i})
are the same, i.e.,
X_{i}=X
and

A_{i}=A
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
, then we use the notation
(X,A)^{\mathcal{K}}

for
({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{\mathcal{K}}
. Further, if all
A_{i}
are
\mathit{pt}
, then we use the shorthand notation
{\textbf{{X}}}^{\mathcal{K}}
for
({\textbf{{X}}},\mathit{pt})^{\mathcal{K}}
and accordingly

X^{\mathcal{K}}
for
(X,\mathit{pt})^{\mathcal{K}}
.
For a detailed description of this construction and examples, see
[
9
, §3.5]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
, §4.3]
.

Let
(X_{i},A_{i})=(D^{1},S^{0})
for all
i
, where
D^{1}
is the closed interval and
S^{0}
is the boundary of
D^{1}
(a set consisting of two points). The corresponding polyhedral product is known as a
real moment-angle complex

[
9
, §3.5]
,
[
11
]
and is denoted by
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
:

(2.2)

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{1},S^{0})^{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}(D^{1},S^{0})^{I}.

We also need th polyhedral product
(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}}
, where
\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
is the infinite-dimensional projective space.

A simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
is called a
flag
complex if any set of its vertices pairwise connected by edges is the set of vertices of some simplex. Any flag complex
\mathcal{K}
is determined by its
1
-skeleton
\mathcal{K}^{1}
.

The relationship between polyhedral products and right-angled Coxeter groups is as follows.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
12
, Corollary 3.4]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex on
m
vertices.

(a)

\pi_{1}((\mathbb{R}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}})\cong\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

(b)

Each of the spaces
(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
is aspherical if and only if
\mathcal{K}
is a flag complex.

(c)

\pi_{i}((\mathbb{R}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}})\cong\pi_{i}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}})
for
i\geq 2
.

(d)

The group
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}})
is isomorphic to the commutant
\mbox{\it RC\/}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

For each subset
J\subset[m]
, consider the restriction

\mathcal{K}_{J}=\{I\in\mathcal{K}\colon I\subset J\},

of
\mathcal{K}
to
J
, which is also called a
full subcomplex
of
\mathcal{K}
.

The following theorem gives a combinatorial description of the homology of the real moment-angle
complex
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
9
]
,
[
11
, §4.5]
)
.

For any
k\geq 0
, one has an isomorphism

H_{k}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}};\mathbb{Z})\cong\bigoplus_{J\subset[m]}\widetilde{H}_{k-1}(\mathcal{K}_{J}),

where
\widetilde{H}_{k-1}(\mathcal{K}_{J})
is the reduced simplicial homology group of the simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}_{J}
.

If
\mathcal{K}
is a flag complex, then Theorem
2.2
also provides a description of the integer homology of the commutant
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
.

Let
G
be a group. The
commutator
of two elements
a,b\in G
is defined by the formula
(a,b)=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab
.

The
simple nested commutator
of length
k
of elements
q_{i}
is defined as the nested commutator

(q_{i_{1}},q_{i_{2}},\ldots,q_{i_{k}}):=(\ldots((q_{i_{1}},q_{i_{2}}),q_{i_{3}}),\ldots,q_{i_{k}}).

Accordingly, in Lie algebras we consider the simple nested commutators

[\mu_{i_{1}},\mu_{i_{2}},\ldots,\mu_{i_{k}}]:=[\ldots[[\mu_{i_{1}},\mu_{i_{2}}],\mu_{i_{3}}],\ldots,\mu_{i_{k}}].

For any group
G
and any three elements
a,b,c\in G
, one has the
Witt–Hall identities
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle(a,bc)

\displaystyle=(a,c)(a,b)(a,b,c),

\displaystyle(ab,c)

\displaystyle=(a,c)(a,c,b)(b,c),

\displaystyle(a,b,c)(b,c,a)(c,a,b)

\displaystyle=(b,a)(c,a)(c,b)^{a}(a,b)(a,c)^{b}(b,c)^{a}(a,c)(c,a)^{b},

where
a^{b}=b^{-1}ab
.

Let
H,W\subset G
be subgroups. Then we define a subgroup
(H,W)\subset G
as the subgroup generated by the commutators
(h,w)
, where
h\in H,w\in W
. In particular, the
commutant

G^{\prime}
of the group
G
is
(G,G)
.

For any group
G
, we set
\gamma_{1}(G)=G
, and further, inductively,

\gamma_{k+1}(G)=(\gamma_{k}(G),G).

The resulting sequence of groups
\gamma_{1}(G),\gamma_{2}(G),\ldots,\gamma_{k}(G),\ldots
is called the
lower central series
of
G
.

If
H\subset G
is a normal subgroup, i.e.,
H=g^{-1}Hg
for any
g\in G
, then we use the notation
H\lhd G
.

In particular,
\gamma_{k+1}(G)\lhd\gamma_{k}(G)
, and the quotient groups
\gamma_{k}(G)/\gamma_{k+1}(G)
are abelian. We introduce the notation
L^{k}(G):=\gamma_{k}(G)/\gamma_{k+1}(G)
and consider the direct sum

L(G):=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}L^{k}(G).

By
\overline{a}_{k}
we denote the class of an element
a_{k}\in\gamma_{k}(G)\subset G
in the quotient group
L^{k}(G)
. If
a_{k}\in\gamma_{k}(G),\;a_{l}\in\gamma_{l}(G)
, then
(a_{k},a_{l})\in\gamma_{k+l}(G)
. Then the Witt–Hall identities imply that
L(G)
is a graded Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Z}
(a Lie ring) with Lie brackets defined by the formula
[\overline{a}_{k},\overline{a}_{l}]:=\overline{(a_{k},a_{l})}
. The Lie algebra
L(G)
is called the
associated Lie algebra
of the group
G
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
12
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

Let
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
be the right-angled Coxeter group corresponding to a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
m
vertices. Then the commutant
\mbox{\it RC\/}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}
has a finite minimal set of generators consisting of
\sum_{J\subset[m]}\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\widetilde{H}_{0}(\mathcal{K}_{J})
nested commutators of the form

(2.4)

(g_{i},g_{j}),\quad(g_{i},g_{j},g_{k_{1}}),\quad\ldots,\quad(g_{i},g_{j},g_{k_{1}},g_{k_{2}},\ldots,g_{k_{m-2}}),

where
i<j>k_{1}>k_{2}>\ldots>k_{\ell-2}
,
k_{s}\neq i
for all
s
, and
i
is the least vertex in some connected component not containing
j
of the full subcomplex
\mathcal{K}_{\{i,j,k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell-2}\}}
.

Remark
.

In
[
12
]
, the commutators are nested to the right. In the present paper, by convention, they are
nested to the left.

From Theorems
2.2
and
2.3

Corollary 2.4

The group
H_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}})=\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}/\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime\prime}
is a free abelian group of rank

\sum_{J\subset[m]}\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\widetilde{H}_{0}(\mathcal{K}_{J})

with a basis consisting of images of iterated commutators described in Theorem

5.
Generators of
L^{4}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})

Using Proposition
4.1
, we can write the generators of
L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
for any simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
[m]
. The algorithm is similar to the proof of some items of Proposition
4.1
:

(1)

Using Theorem
2.17
, we write the generators for the discrete simplcial complex
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
on
m
points.

(2)

We take four points
J\subset[m]
and then isolate the restriction
\mathcal{K}_{J}
, gathering all commutators
corresponding to it into a set
A
.

(3)

Using Proposition
4.1
, we write the minimal set
A^{\prime}
of generators for the resulting complex.

(4)

We return the set
A^{\prime}
to the general list of commutators instead of
A
.

(5)

We return to the first item of the list, take some other four points, and repeat this operation for
each quadruple of points (thus, the operation must be performed
{C_{m}^{4}}
times).

Proof.

At step
3
, by Proposition
4.1
, the subset
A^{\prime}
combined with the complement of
A
generates
L^{4}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
, whence it follows that the resulting set generates
L^{4}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
. It remains to prove the minimality. Assume that the resulting set of commutators is not a minimal system of generators;
i.e., there exists a commutator
\alpha=[\mu_{i},\mu_{j},\mu_{k},\mu_{l}]
which can be removed and the remaining set still
generates
L^{4}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
. We consider the iteration of the algorithm when
\alpha
was considered for the last time at step
4
and take the same four points that were used for this iteration. By Proposition
4.1
, the commutator
\alpha
cannot be removed, which is a contradiction. It follows that the algorithm gives the desired result.

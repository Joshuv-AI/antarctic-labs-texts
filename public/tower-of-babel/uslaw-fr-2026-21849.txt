Subsistence Management Regulations for Public Lands in Alaska--
2026-27 and 2027-28 Subsistence Taking of Wildlife Regulations

AGENCY: Forest Service, Agriculture; Office of Subsistence Management, 
Interior.

ACTION: Final rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: This final rule revises regulations for hunting and trapping 
seasons, harvest limits, and methods and means related to taking of 
wildlife for subsistence uses during the 2026-27 and 2027-28 regulatory 
years. The Federal Subsistence Board (hereafter referred to as ``the 
Board'') is on a schedule of completing the process of revising 
subsistence taking of wildlife regulations in even-numbered years and 
subsistence taking of fish and shellfish regulations in odd-numbered 
years; public proposal and review processes take place during the 
preceding year. The Board also addresses customary and traditional use 
determinations during the applicable cycle. This final rule revises the 
existing subsistence wildlife taking regulations, the general 
regulations on subsistence taking of fish and wildlife, customary and 
traditional use determinations, and addresses a deferred proposal 
regarding eulachon harvest in District 1 from the last fish cycle and a 
deferred proposal regarding brown bear hide sales from the last 
wildlife cycle. This final rule also makes administrative corrections 
to the regulations and corrects errors discovered since the last 
rulemaking.

DATES: This rule is effective October 27, 2026.

Background

    Under title VIII of the Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation 
Act (ANILCA) (16 U.S.C. 3111-3126), the Secretary of the Interior and 
the Secretary of Agriculture (hereafter referred to as ``the 
Secretaries'') jointly implement the Federal Subsistence Management 
Program (hereafter referred to as ``the Program''). The Program 
provides a priority for take of fish and wildlife resources for 
nonwasteful subsistence uses on Federal public lands and waters in 
Alaska. Only Alaska residents of areas identified as rural are eligible 
to participate in the Program. The Secretaries published temporary 
regulations to carry out the Program in the Federal Register on June 
29, 1990 (55 FR 27114), and final regulations on May 29, 1992 (57 FR 
22940). Program officials have subsequently amended these regulations 
many times. Because the Program is a joint effort between the 
Departments of the Interior and Agriculture, these regulations are 
located in two titles of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR): The 
Agriculture regulations are at title 36, ``Parks, Forests, and Public 
Property,'' and the Interior regulations are at title 43, ``Public 
Lands: Interior,'' at 36 CFR 242.1-28 and 43 CFR 51.1-28, respectively. 
Consequently, to indicate that identical changes are proposed for 
regulations in both titles 36 and 43, in this document we will present 
references to specific sections of the CFR as shown in the following 
example: Sec.  __.24.
    The Program regulations contain subparts as follows: Subpart A, 
General Provisions; Subpart B, Program Structure; Subpart C, Board 
Determinations; and Subpart D, Subsistence Taking of Fish and Wildlife. 
Consistent with subpart B of these regulations, the Secretaries 
established the Board to administer the Program. The Board comprises:
     A Chair appointed by the Secretary of the Interior with 
concurrence of the Secretary of Agriculture;
     Five public members appointed by the Secretary of the 
Interior with concurrence of the Secretary of Agriculture;
     The Alaska Regional Director, Bureau of Indian Affairs;
     The Alaska State Director, Bureau of Land Management 
(BLM);
     The Alaska Regional Director, National Park Service (NPS);
     The Alaska Regional Director, U.S. Fish and Wildlife 
Service (FWS); and
     The Pacific Geographic Area Regional Forester, U.S. Forest 
Service.
    Through the Board, these agencies and public members participate in 
the development of regulations for subparts C and D. Subpart C sets 
forth important Board determinations regarding program eligibility 
(i.e., which areas of Alaska are considered rural and which species are 
harvested in those areas as part of a ``customary and traditional use'' 
for subsistence purposes). Subpart D sets forth specific seasons, 
limits, and other harvest parameters and requirements.
    In administering the Program, the Secretaries divided Alaska into 
10 subsistence resource regions, each of which is represented by a 
Federal Subsistence Regional Advisory Council (Council). The Councils 
provide a forum for rural residents with personal knowledge of local 
conditions and resource requirements to have a meaningful role in the 
subsistence management of fish and wildlife on Federal public lands in 
Alaska. The Council members represent varied geographical, cultural, 
and user interests within each region.
    The Board conducts rulemaking for the Program on a biennial 
schedule with the process of revising the fish and shellfish 
regulations and the process for revising the wildlife regulations 
occurring during opposite years. The Board addresses ``customary and 
traditional use'' determinations during the applicable biennial cycle. 
The regulations at Sec.  __.4 define ``customary and traditional use'' 
as ``a

[[Page 67483]]

long-established, consistent pattern of use, incorporating beliefs and 
customs which have been transmitted from generation to generation.'' 
Since establishment of the Program regulations in 1992, the Board has 
made a number of customary and traditional use determinations at the 
request of affected subsistence users. These determinations have 
resulted in revisions to the regulations at Sec.  __.24. Those 
modifications, along with some administrative corrections, were

Current Rulemaking Action

    The Departments published a proposed rule, Subsistence Management 
Regulations for Public Lands in Alaska--2026-27 and 2027-28 Subsistence 
Taking of Wildlife Regulations, on January 21, 2025 (90 FR 6922), to 
amend the regulations in subparts C and D of 36 CFR part 242 and 50 CFR 
part 100 for seasons, harvest limits, and methods and means related to 
taking of wildlife for subsistence uses. Since the Department of the 
Interior Federal subsistence regulations were subsequently relocated 
from 50 CFR part 100 (90 FR 34148, July 18, 2025), the regulations 
referenced in the remainder of this document will be 36 CFR part 242 
and 43 CFR part 51.
    The proposed rule opened a comment period that closed on April 4, 
2025. The Departments advertised the proposed rule on the Program's web 
page and social media, and it was sent via email. The Councils met 
between February 19 and April 2, 2025, and in addition to other 
business, received suggestions for proposals from the public. The Board 
received a total of 84 proposals, 1 of which was classified as invalid. 
The invalid proposal requested a change to the Unit 2 black bear 
harvest limit for nonresidents, which is outside of the Board's 
authority. The Board received 7 valid proposals for changes to subpart 
C (customary and traditional use determinations) and 76 valid proposals 
for changes to subpart D regulations (which are specific provisions 
regarding the take of fish and wildlife). In addition, 18 wildlife 
closure reviews were presented for comment as required

[[Page 67484]]

by Board policy that specifies a review of each closure at least every 
4 years. The Board also addressed one request for reconsideration 
submitted in response to the Board changing the status of Ketchikan 
from nonrural to rural, and one deferred proposal from the previous 
fish and shellfish regulatory cycle (90 FR 34152, July 18, 2025).
    The public submitted twelve general comments, which are available 
for review at https://www.regulations.gov in Docket No. DOI-2024-0011. 
Most of these comments supported some combination of the proposed rule 
and the Title VIII rural priority and conservation mandate. Another 
comment expressed concern for effects on wildlife populations but 
supported the Board's holistic decision-making process. The remaining 
comment supported including proposed meeting dates to maximize 
participation. We provided fall and winter meeting dates and locations 
for the ten Councils. We did not provide specific dates for the Board 
meeting because the dates were unknown. The remaining public comment 
was outside the scope of this rulemaking action.
    After the comment period closed, the Board posted the proposals on 
the Program website and distributed a news release notifying the public 
on May 7, 2025. The public then had 55 days, until June 30, 2025, to 
comment on the proposed regulatory changes.
    The 10 Councils met again between September 16, 2025, and March 13, 
2026, received public comments, and formulated their recommendations to 
the Board on proposals and closure reviews for their respective 
regions. The public also had an opportunity to comment at the Board 
meeting. Therefore, the public received extensive opportunity to review 
and comment on all changes.
    The Councils had a substantial role in reviewing the proposed rule 
and making recommendations for the final rule. Moreover, a Council 
Chair, or a designated representative, presented each Council's 
recommendations at the Board's public meeting held April 21-24, 2026.

Summary of Board Actions on Proposals and Closure Reviews

    The Board's actions on each proposal and closure review are listed 
in table 2 below. When making decisions, the Board may consider, among 
other factors, whether a proposal:
     provides a subsistence priority on public lands;
     is supported by substantial scientific evidence and 
traditional ecological knowledge (TEK);
     recognizes principles of fish and wildlife conservation;
     provides opportunity; and
     would not be detrimental to or place undue burden on rural 
Alaskan subsistence users.
    The Board considered 83 wildlife proposals and 18 wildlife closure 
reviews during the 2026-28 wildlife regulatory cycle. The Board adopted 
51 of the proposals, deferred 5, approved the withdrawal of 3 (WP26-27 
and WP26-41/42), rejected 12, and took no action on 12. The Board acted 
on 18 wildlife closure reviews, voting to retain status quo on 13 of 
them, rescind 3, and take no action on 2. The Board adopted deferred 
fisheries proposal FP25-02 as modified by the Southeast Alaska 
Subsistence Regional Advisory Council and determined the requests for 
reconsideration of Ketchikan's rural status (RFR25-01) do not meet the 
threshold for further consideration.
    Consensus agenda: The consensus agenda is made up of proposals and 
closure reviews for which there is agreement concerning a proposed 
regulatory action among the affected Councils, a majority of the 
Interagency Staff Committee members, and the Alaska Department of Fish 
and Game (ADF&G). Anyone may request that the Board remove a proposal 
or a closure review from the consensus agenda and place it on the non-
consensus agenda. Proposals or closure reviews may be taken off the 
consensus agenda following the agreement of at least one Board member; 
such proposals or closure reviews then follow the Board's process for 
non-consensus items and are deliberated and voted on individually. 
Wildlife proposals WP26-22 regarding trapping setbacks in Unit 7, WP26-
62b regarding establishing a muskox hunt in Unit 22A, and WP26-66 
regarding wolf hunting in Unit 23 were moved from the consensus agenda 
to the non-consensus agenda at the request of the public and agreement 
by the Board. Of the proposals and closure reviews being considered, 39 
were on the consensus agenda. The Board votes en masse on the consensus 
agenda after deliberation and action on all other proposals.
    Of the proposals on the consensus agenda, the Board adopted 17 as 
they were written, adopted 3 with modifications, rejected 8, and took 
no action on 5. Of the closure reviews on the consensus agenda, the 
Board retained the status quo on four and rescinded two. Analysis and 
justification for the action taken on each proposal on the consensus 
agenda can be found in the Board meeting book and transcripts for the 
meeting that occurred April 21-24, 2026 in Anchorage, AK.
    Non-consensus agenda: Of the proposals and closure reviews being 
considered, 59 were on the Board's non-consensus agenda. The Board 
adopted 15 as written, adopted 16 with modification, deferred 5, 
rejected 4, took no action on 7, and withdrew 3. Of the closure reviews 
on the non-consensus agenda, the Board retained the status quo on nine, 
retained one with modification, rescinded one, and took no action on 
one. Analysis and justification for the action taken on each item on 
the non-consensus agenda can be found in the Board meeting book and 
transcripts.
    All of the documents referenced in this section are available for 
review at the Office of Subsistence Management, 1011 East Tudor Road, 
Mail Stop 121, Anchorage, AK 99503 or on the OSM website (https://www.doi.gov/subsistence).

    One of the corrected errors resulted from the publication process 
of the final rule published on July 26, 2022 (87 FR 44846). That rule 
mistakenly identified the start of the moose hunting season in Unit 3 
as September 1 instead of September 15. The September 15 start date was 
set in 1996 (61 FR 39698) and has not been changed by any subsequent 
action. There were no proposals to change season dates for moose in 
Unit 3 during the 2022 wildlife regulatory cycle leading to 87 FR 
44846. The September 15 season start date was

[[Page 67496]]

originally adopted by the Board after notice and comment rulemaking 
pursuant to the Administrative Procedure Act. This rule replaces 
September 1 with September 15 in section __.26(n)(3) to reflect the 
date set by the Board in 1996 that was erroneously changed to September 
1 in 2022.
    The remaining changes corrected a spelling error, removed closures 
no longer in effect, and made minor changes to Board adopted regulatory 
language that improve regulatory clarity. In the first change to 
language adopted by the Board, regarding Unit 23 muskrat hunting, the 
season description was changed from ``No Closed Season'' to ``July 1-
June 30''. While there is functionally no difference between the two 
season descriptions, the change was made to be consistent with year-
round season descriptions throughout regulation. In the second change 
to language adopted by the Board, concerning Unit 26C musk ox, the word 
``Federal'' was added before the words ``public lands'' to be 
consistent with other federal subsistence regulations referring to such 
lands.

Compliance With Statutory and Regulatory Authorities

Administrative Procedure Act Compliance

    The Board has provided extensive opportunity for public input and 
involvement in compliance with the Administrative Procedure Act (5 
U.S.C. 551 et seq.), including publishing a proposed rule in the 
Federal Register, participation in multiple Council meetings, 
additional public review and comment on all proposals for regulatory 
change, and opportunity for additional public comment during the Board 
meeting prior to deliberation. Additionally, an administrative 
mechanism exists to request reconsideration of the Board's decision on 
any proposal for regulatory change (36 CFR 242.20 and 43 CFR 51.20). 
Therefore, the Board believes that sufficient public notice and 
opportunity for involvement have been given to affected persons 
regarding Board decisions.
    The administrative correction to the Unit 3 moose season start date 
was not identified as a proposed change in the proposed rule. We find 
that there is good cause to make this change without notice and comment 
under the Administrative Procedure Act at 5 U.S.C. 553(b)(B). First, we 
find that notice and comment is unnecessary because the change merely 
fixes an inadvertent error in the publication process and thus would 
not have benefitted from public input. In addition, it is impractical 
to make this correction with notice and comment because such notice and 
comment would delay making the correction until after September 1, 2026 
and thus likely lead to continued confusion as to when hunting can 
begin.
    The notice and comment process is also unnecessary for the 
remaining administrative changes not identified in the proposed rule, 
which include correcting a spelling error, removing closures no longer 
in effect, and making changes to Board-adopted language that improve 
regulatory clarity. In these cases, we find good cause that the notice 
and comment process is unnecessary because the changes are of a 
technical nature, are not substantial, and are unlikely to be of 
interest to the public.
    In the more than 30 years that the Program has been operating, no 
benefit to the public has been demonstrated by delaying the effective 
date of the subsistence regulations. A lapse in regulatory control 
could affect the continued viability of fish or wildlife populations 
and future subsistence opportunities for rural Alaskans and would 
generally fail to serve the overall public interest. Therefore, the 
Board finds good cause pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 553(d)(3) to make this rule 
effective upon the date set forth in DATES to ensure continued 
operation of the subsistence program.

National Environmental Policy Act

    A draft environmental impact statement that described four 
alternatives for developing a Federal Subsistence Management Program 
was distributed for public comment on October 7, 1991. The final 
environmental impact statement (FEIS) was published on February 28, 
1992. The Record of Decision (ROD) on Subsistence Management for 
Federal Public Lands in Alaska was signed April 6, 1992. The selected 
alternative in the FEIS (Alternative IV) defined the administrative 
framework of an annual regulatory cycle for subsistence regulations.
    A 1997 environmental assessment dealt with the expansion of Federal 
jurisdiction over fisheries and is available at the office listed under 
FOR FURTHER INFORMATION CONTACT. The Secretary of the Interior, with 
concurrence of the Secretary of Agriculture, determined that expansion 
of Federal jurisdiction does not constitute a major Federal action 
significantly affecting the human environment and, therefore, signed a 
Finding of No Significant Impact.
    DOI conducted a Determination of NEPA Adequacy of the 1992 and 1997 
documents and determined that there were no changes that warrant a new 
NEPA review in accordance with DOI NEPA Manual Section 3.1.

Section 810 of ANILCA

    An ANILCA section 810 analysis was completed as part of the FEIS 
process on the Program. The intent of all Federal subsistence 
regulations is to accord subsistence uses of fish and wildlife on 
public lands a priority over the taking of fish and wildlife on such 
lands for other purposes, unless restriction is necessary to conserve 
healthy fish and wildlife populations. The final section 810 analysis 
determination appeared in the April 6, 1992, ROD and concluded that the 
Program, under Alternative IV with an annual process for setting 
subsistence regulations, may have some local impacts on subsistence 
uses, but will not likely restrict subsistence uses significantly.
    During the subsequent 1997 environmental assessment process for 
extending fisheries jurisdiction, an evaluation of the effects of the 
subsistence program regulations was conducted in accordance with 
section 810. That evaluation also supported the Secretaries' 
determination that the regulations will not reach the threshold that 
would require notice and hearings under ANILCA section 810(a).

Regulatory Flexibility Act

    The Regulatory Flexibility Act of 1980 (5 U.S.C. 601 et seq.) 
requires preparation of flexibility analyses for rules that will have a 
significant effect on a substantial number of small entities, which 
include small businesses, organizations, or governmental jurisdictions. 
In general, the resources to be harvested under this rule are already 
being harvested and consumed by the local harvester and do not result 
in an additional dollar benefit to the economy. However, we estimate 
that 2 million pounds of meat are harvested by subsistence users 
annually and, if given an estimated dollar value of $3.00 per pound, 
this amount would equate to about $6 million in food value statewide. 
Based upon the amounts and values cited above, the Departments certify 
that this rulemaking will not have a significant economic effect on a 
substantial number of small entities within the meaning of the 
Regulatory Flexibility Act.

Executive Order 14153

    E.O. 14153 directs all bureaus of the Department of the Interior to

management agencies prior to enacting regulations that affect the 
ability of Alaskans to hunt and fish on public lands, and to ensure to

Councils and the Board on regulatory matters being considered. The 
Board considered all comments and information provided by the State in 
this process. The Board also reviewed applicable State regulations. We 
minimized deviations from State hunting and fishing opportunities to 
the extent possible while still meeting the ANICLA mandates for the 
Program.

beaver in Unit 17 throughout the season and consistent with established 
harvest limits. You may not take beaver with a firearm under a trapping 
license on National Park Service lands.
    (D) In Unit 17, a snowmachine may be used to assist in the taking 
of a caribou, and caribou may be shot from a stationary snowmachine. 
``Assist in the taking of a caribou'' means a snowmachine may be used 
to approach within 300 yards of a caribou at speeds

[[Page 67516]]

under 15 miles per hour, in a manner that does not involve repeated 
approaches or that causes a caribou to run. A snowmachine may not be 
used to contact an animal or to pursue a fleeing caribou.
    (E) In Unit 17, a snowmachine may be used to approach and pursue a 
wolf or wolverine provided the snowmachine does not contact a live 
animal.

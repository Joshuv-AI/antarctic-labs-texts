Second order superstructure reflexions in tilted perovskites
Richard

Abstract
In a previous article [ B+S ] , we derived Boolean conditions for the appearance of superstructure reflexions in diffraction patterns from perovskites with tilted oxygen octahedra, using the structure factor equation. Assuming that the deviation from the untilted prototype perovskite structure was infinitesimally small, we expanded the structure factor as a Taylor series, truncated at the first term. This gave an elegant and simple method giving conditions on the presence or absence of superstructure reflexions. However, in real perovskite materials these distortions are sufficiently large for higher order terms to become significant, giving an additional set of superstructure reflexions. Here, we consider the second term in the expansion and show that it gives rise to ‘second order’ reflexions with indices of the form 1 2 \frac{1}{2} even-even-odd. Boolean conditions for their presence are given.

Introduction

In
[
B+S
]
, we derived Boolean conditions for the appearance of superstructure reflexions in tilted perovskites, taking the displacements of the oxygen atoms from their positions in the prototype unit cell to be
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)}
. In this framework, the structure factor
F_{g}
for a reflexion
g
with Miller indices
g_{1}\penalty\ g_{2}\penalty\ g_{3}
(or
hkl
in the convention used in the International Tables for Crystallography,
[
IntTables
]
) can be written

F_{g}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{g}^{(j)}\exp{\left(2\pi i\textbf{g}\cdot\textbf{r}^{(j)}\right)}\exp{\left(2\pi i\textbf{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)}\right)}

(1)

where the sum is taken over all
j
atoms in the unit cell, each having atomic scattering factor
f_{g}^{(j)}
and fractional coordinates
\textbf{r}^{(j)}
in the prototype structure. A simple equation giving the type of reflexions produced by a given tilt system was derived by expanding the last term as a series

\exp{\left(2\pi i\textbf{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)}\right)}=1+2\pi i\textbf{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)}-4\pi^{2}(\textbf{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)})^{2}+...

(2)

which can be truncated after the first-order term, if the displacements
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)}
are taken to be infinitesimally small, which was the premise of our previous article. However, experimental electron diffraction patterns show additional sets of weak superstructure spots that are not given by the previous analysis, indicating that oxygen displacements in tilted perovskites are sufficiently large for second-order terms to be important. These additional spots were termed ‘concert’ reflexions by
[
Woodward
]
. Here, we derive equations for these sets of weak second-order superstructure reflexions. We find that their type is complementary to first-order superstructure reflexions, and that they are only present in perovskites with oxygen octahedral tilts about more than one axis, at least one of which must be an in-phase tilt system.

Calculation

From the last term in Eq.
2
and following the approach used in
[
B+S
]
the structure factor of a second-order superstructure reflexion it produces
g
is

F_{g}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{g}^{(j)}4\pi^{2}(\textbf{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)})^{2}A^{s^{(j)}_{1}}B^{s^{(j)}_{2}}C^{s^{(j)}_{3}},

(3)

where
A
,
B
and
C
take values of
\pm 1
or
\pm i
for even or odd
g_{i}
respectively, and the position of oxygen atoms is written as
\textbf{s}^{(j)}=4\textbf{r}^{(j)}
. Importantly, when Eq.
3
is evaluated for a specific tilt system, terms appear that are only non-zero for even, or odd, reflexion indices; for example,
(1+A^{2})
is only non-zero when
g_{1}
is even, while
(1-B^{2})
is only non-zero when
g_{2}
is odd. A unit cell doubled in all three dimensions is used, large enough to be a unit cell for any Glazer octahedral tilting pattern, which contains 24 oxygen atoms and hence gives 24 terms in Eq.
3
. The doubling of the unit cell in the calculation means that, in the reference frame of the prototype perovskite cell, superstructure reflexions have half-order indices and, for example, an equation of the form
(1-A^{2})(1-B^{2})(1+C^{2})
indicates that superstructure spots must have the form
hkl=\frac{1}{2}odd-odd-even
, which is shortened to
\frac{1}{2}ooe
.

It is helpful to split Eq.
3
into two by expanding the
\textbf{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)}

(\textbf{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(j)})^{2}=(g^{2}_{1}\delta^{2}_{1}+g^{2}_{2}\delta^{2}_{2}+g^{2}_{3}\delta^{2}_{3})+2(g_{1}g_{2}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}+g_{2}g_{3}\delta_{2}\delta_{3}+g_{3}g_{1}\delta_{3}\delta_{1}),

(4)

F_{g}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{g}^{(j)}4\pi^{2}\penalty\ (g^{2}_{1}\delta^{2}_{1}+g^{2}_{2}\delta^{2}_{2}+g^{2}_{3}\delta^{2}_{3})\penalty\ A^{s^{(j)}_{1}}B^{s^{(j)}_{2}}C^{s^{(j)}_{3}}.

(5)

which is, substituting the 24 oxygen coordinates
\textbf{s}^{(j)}

\begin{split}\frac{F_{g}}{2\pi^{2}f_{g}}=\penalty\ &-(g^{2}_{1}\delta^{2}_{1}+g^{2}_{2}\delta^{2}_{2})\\
&\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (AB+AB^{3}+A^{3}B+A^{3}B^{3}+ABC^{2}+AB^{3}C^{2}+A^{3}BC^{2}+A^{3}B^{3}C^{2})\\

F_{g}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{g}^{(j)}4\pi^{2}\penalty\ (g_{1}g_{2}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}+g_{2}g_{3}\delta_{2}\delta_{3}+g_{3}g_{1}\delta_{3}\delta_{1})\penalty\ A^{s^{(j)}_{1}}B^{s^{(j)}_{2}}C^{s^{(j)}_{3}}.

It is also instructive to examine particular examples. One of the tilt systems that satisfies the minimum requirements for the presence of second-order superstructure reflexions is
a^{0}b^{+}c^{-}
, which has
\frac{1}{2}ooo

c^{-}

\frac{1}{2}oeo

b^{+}

B+S

\frac{1}{2}oeo

a^{+}a^{+}c^{-}

\frac{1}{2}eoe

a^{0}b^{+}b^{+}

a^{+}a^{+}a^{+}

\frac{1}{2}oeo

\frac{1}{2}eoo

\frac{1}{2}eoo

\frac{1}{2}oeo

\frac{1}{2}ooe

4
Conclusions

Equations governing the appearance of second order superstructure reflexions resulting from distortion modes in perovskites have been derived. These reflexions are expected to be weaker than the first-order superstructure reflexions considered in
[
B+S
]
as their kinematic intensity is proportional to
\delta^{4}
, where
\delta
is the magnitude of the displacement of oxygen atoms from their position in the prototype perovskite structure. The kinematic intensity of first-order superstructure reflexions is proportional to
\delta^{2}
. This may aid the interpretation of electron diffraction patterns and replicates the work of
[
Glazer75
]
and
[
Woodward
]
. The emphasis on distortion mode, rather than space group, allows the interpretation of ED patterns without the need to rewrite vectors in real and reciprocal space for different unit cells.

Some general rules become apparent from Table

. The rules for in-phase tilting are quite straightforward, with each tilt system
a^{+}

b^{+}

c^{+}

\frac{1}{2}eoo

\frac{1}{2}oeo

\frac{1}{2}ooe

\frac{1}{2}ooo

Furthermore, because in these cases the type of superstructure reflexions is always the same, tilts of equal magnitude operating about different axes can result in changes to the set of systematic absences. Accordingly, systematic absences are most apparent for the
a^{-}a^{-}a^{-}
system. This means that determining antiphase tilting systems is less straightforward than in-phase tilt systems. For investigations using electron diffraction, it may thus be important to explore reciprocal space in three dimensions since systematic absences can readily be ‘filled in’ by double diffraction where the possibility exists, particularly in the zero-order Laue zone. Access to higher order Laue zones, or zone axes where no double diffraction pathways are present, is generally necessary.

\frac{1}{2}eeo

and
\frac{1}{2}oee

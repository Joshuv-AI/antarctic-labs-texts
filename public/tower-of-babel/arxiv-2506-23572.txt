Three-dimensional structural stability of shock waves in elastodynamics
Artem Shafeev

Abstract
We study the three-dimensional structural stability of shock waves for the equations of elastodynamics governing isentropic flows of compressible inviscid elastic materials. By nonlinear structural stability of a shock wave we mean the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of the discontinuous shock front solution to the hyperbolic system of elastodynamics. By using equivalent formulations of the uniform and weak Kreiss–Lopatinski conditions for 1-shocks, we show that planar shock waves in three-dimensional elastodynamics are always at least weakly stable, and we find a condition necessary and sufficient for their uniform stability. Since the system of elastodynamics satisfies the Agranovich–Majda–Osher block structure condition, uniform stability implies structural stability of corresponding nonplanar shock waves. We also show that, as in isentropic gas dynamics, all compressive shock waves are uniformly stable for convex equations of state. This paper is a natural continuation of the previous two-dimensional analysis in [ 25 , 31 ] . As in the two-dimensional case, we make the conclusion that the elastic force plays stabilizing role for uniform stability.



Keywords and phrases
: Compressible elastodynamics, Shock waves, Uniform and weak Kreiss–Lopatinski conditions, Structural stability.

Introduction

We consider the equations of elastodynamics governing the isentropic flow of compressible inviscid elastic materials. These equations arise as the inviscid limit of the equations of compressible viscoelasticity
[
13
,
15
,
16
]
of Oldroyd type
[
26
,
27
]
. The elastodynamics equations are written as the following system of 13 conservation laws:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}\,(\rho v)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho v)+{\rm div}\,(\rho v\otimes v)+{\nabla}p-{\rm div}\,(\rho FF^{\top})=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho F_{j})+{\rm div}\,(\rho F_{j}\otimes v-v\otimes\rho F_{j})=0,\qquad j=1,2,3,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\rho
is the density,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the velocity,
F\in\mathbb{M}(3,3)
is the deformation gradient,
F_{j}=(F_{1j},F_{2j},F_{3j})^{\top}
is the
j
th column of
F
, and the pressure
p=p(\rho)
is a smooth function of
\rho
. System (
1.1
) is supplemented by the identity
{\rm div}\,(\rho F^{\top})=0
which is the set of the three divergence constraints

{\rm div}\,(\rho F_{j})=0

(1.2)

(
j=1,2,3
) on initial data, i.e., one can show that if equations (
1.2
) are satisfied initially, then they hold for all
t>0
. One also can show
[
28
]
that the physical identity
[
13
]

\rho\det F=1

(1.3)

is also a constraint on the initial data for the Cauchy problem. As in
[
25
,
31
]
, we restrict ourself to the special case of neo-Hookean materials, i.e., the case when the Cauchy stress tensor has the form
\rho FF^{\top}
corresponding to the elastic energy
W(F)=\frac{1}{2}|F|^{2}
for the Hookean linear elasticity.

Using (
1.2
), we rewrite system (
1.1
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}\,\frac{{\rm d}p}{{\rm d}t}+{\rm div}\,{v}=0,}\\[6.0pt]
{\displaystyle\rho\,\frac{{\rm d}v}{{\rm d}t}+{\nabla}p-\rho\sum_{j=1}^{3}(F_{j}\cdot\nabla)F_{j}=0,}\\[6.0pt]
\rho\,{\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}F_{j}}{{\rm d}t}-\rho\,(F_{j}\cdot\nabla)v=0,\qquad j=1,2,3,}\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
c^{2}=p^{\prime}(\rho)
is the square of the sound speed and
{\rm d}/{\rm d}t=\partial_{t}+({v}\cdot{\nabla})
is the material derivative. Equations (
1.4
) form the symmetric system

A_{0}(U)\partial_{t}U+\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{j}(U)\partial_{j}U=0

(1.5)

for
U=(p,v^{\top},F_{1}^{\top},F_{2}^{\top},F_{3}^{\top})^{\top}
, with
A_{0}={\rm diag}(1/(\rho c^{2}),\rho I_{12})
and

A_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}{\displaystyle\frac{v_{j}}{\rho c^{2}}}&e_{j}^{\top}&\underline{0}^{\top}&\underline{0}^{\top}&\underline{0}^{\top}\\[7.0pt]
e_{j}&\rho v_{j}I_{3}&-\rho F_{j1}I_{3}&-\rho F_{j2}I_{3}&-\rho F_{j3}I_{3}\\[3.0pt]
\underline{0}&-\rho F_{j1}I_{3}&\rho v_{j}I_{3}&O_{3}&O_{3}\\
\underline{0}&-\rho F_{j2}I_{3}&O_{3}&\rho v_{j}I_{3}&O_{3}\\
\underline{0}&-\rho F_{j3}I_{3}&O_{3}&O_{3}&\rho v_{j}I_{3}\end{pmatrix},

where
\underline{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the zero vector,
e_{j}=(\delta_{1j},\delta_{2j},\delta_{3j})^{\top}
are the unit vectors, and
I_{m}
and
O_{m}
denote the unit and zero matrices of order
m
respectively. In (
1.5
) we think of the density as a function of the pressure:
\rho=\rho(p)
,
c^{2}=1/\rho^{\prime}(p)
. The symmetric system (
1.5
) is hyperbolic if
A_{0}>0
, i.e.,

\rho>0,\quad\rho^{\prime}(p)>0.

(1.6)

Our goal in this paper is to study the structural stability of shock waves for the system of hyperbolic conservation laws (
1.1
). By structural stability of a shock wave we mean the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of the discontinuous shock front solution to system (
1.1
). Setting purely formally
F=0
in (
1.1
) (strictly speaking, this contradicts to (
1.3
)), we get the system of isentropic gas dynamics. The structural stability of shock waves in isentropic gas dynamics was proved by Majda
[
21
]
(see also
[
23
]
for a refined result) provided that the uniform stability condition introduced in
[
20
]
for the constant coefficient linearized problem with boundary conditions on a planar shock is satisfied at each point of the initial nonplanar shock wave. It should be noted that in isentropic gas dynamics there are no violently unstable planar shock waves, i.e., Hadamard-type ill-posedness examples cannot be constructed for the constant coefficient linearized problem, and shock waves are always at least weakly stable in the sense of the fulfilment of the Kreiss-Lopatinski (KL) condition
[
18
]
(see Sect.
4
). The structural stability of weakly stable shock waves in isentropic gas dynamics was proved by Coulombel and Secchi
[
12
]
.

Regarding shock waves in compressible isentropic elastodynamics (for Hookean linear elasticity), a condition sufficient for their 2D structural stability was found in
[
25
]
by the energy method. More precisely, by an adaptation to isentropic elastodynamics of the energy method proposed by Blokhin
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
for shock waves in full gas dynamics (see also
[
5
,
30
]
), an a priori estimate without loss of derivatives for solutions to the constant coefficient linearized problem was deduced in
[
25
]
under a stability condition satisfied for the unperturbed rectilinear shock wave. In fact, the stability condition found in
[
25
]
is sufficient for uniform stability of rectilinear shock waves because the derivation of an a priori estimate without loss of derivatives for the linearized problem can be considered as an indirect test of the uniform Kreiss-Lopatinski (UKL) condition
[
18
]
. With reference to
[
21
,
23
,
30
]
, this implies the structural stability of corresponding curved shock waves in 2D isentropic elastodynamics.

At last, by the direct test of the KL and UKL conditions it was proved in
[
31
]
that the stability condition from
[
25
]
is not only sufficient but also necessary for the uniform stability of rectilinear shock waves in 2D isentropic elastodynamics. Moreover, it was shown in
[
31
]
that as soon as this uniform stability condition is violated, shock waves are weakly stable, i.e., as in isentropic gas dynamics, shock waves are never violently unstable. Spectral analysis in
[
31
]
is based on equivalent formulations of the KL and UKL conditions for 1-shocks proposed in
[
29
]
, and its key point is a delicate study of the transition between uniform and weak stability.

For the system of hyperbolic conservation laws (
1.1
), besides shock waves there are also characteristic discontinuities. The linear and structural stability of vortex sheets in 2D and 3D isentropic elastodynamics was studied in
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
]
. We also refer to structural stability results in
[
10
]
for vortex sheets in nonisentropic elastodynamics (thermoelasticity). Moreover, the structural stability of contact discontinuities in thermoelasticity, for which the velocity is continuous across the discontinuity surface, was shown in
[
6
]
under some stability condition.

In this paper we extend the ideas of the 2D spectral analysis from
[
31
]
to the 3D case. We prove that planar shock waves in 3D isentropic elastodynamics are always at least weakly stable, and we find a condition necessary and sufficient for their uniform stability. We show that system (
1.1
) satisfies the Agranovich–Majda–Osher block structure condition
[
1
]
. Therefore, by referring to
[
21
]
, we conclude that the uniform stability of planar elastic shocks implies structural stability of corresponding nonplanar shock waves. As in the 2D case in
[
25
]
, we show that all compressive shock waves are uniformly stable for convex equations of state and make the conclusion that the elastic force plays stabilizing role for uniform stability.

The plan of the rest of this paper is as follows. In Sect.
2
, we formulate the free boundary problem for shock waves in 3D elastodynamics, reduce it to that in fixed domains, discuss the Lax conditions
[
19
]
, and prove that system (
1.1
) satisfies the Agranovich–Majda–Osher block structure condition. In Sect.
3
, we formulate the constant coefficient linearized problem associated with the nonlinear free boundary problem. In Sect.
4
, we present the main results of the paper. Sect.
5
is devoted to finding the uniform stability condition. At last, in Sect.
6
we make some final remarks, in particular, we discuss stabilizing role of elastic force for uniform stability and show how to write down for nonplanar shocks a conterpart of the uniform stability condition obtained for a nonplanar shock wave.

2
Free boundary problem for shock waves

Let
\Gamma(t)=\{x_{1}=\varphi(t,x^{\prime})\}
be a hypersurface of strong discontinuity for system (
1.1
), where
x^{\prime}=(x_{2},x_{3})
. We are interested in solutions of (
1.1
) that are smooth on either side of
\Gamma(t)
. Such piecewise smooth solutions are weak solutions to the system of conservation laws (
1.1
) if and only if they satisfy the Rankine-Hugoniot jump conditions on
\Gamma(t)
:

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{m}]=0,

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}[v_{N}]+|N|^{2}[p]=\left[\rho(F_{1{N}}^{2}+F_{2{N}}^{2}+F_{3{N}}^{2})\right],

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}[v_{\tau}]=\left[\rho(F_{1{N}}F_{1\tau}+F_{2{N}}F_{2\tau}+F_{3{N}}F_{3\tau})\right],

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}[F_{j{N}}]=[\rho v_{N}F_{j{N}}],\quad j=1,2,3,

(2.1d)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}[F_{j\tau}]=[\rho v_{\tau}F_{j{N}}],\quad j=1,2,3,

(2.1e)

\displaystyle[\rho F_{j{N}}]=0,\quad j=1,2,3,

(2.1f)

where
[g]=g^{+}|_{\Gamma}-g^{-}|_{\Gamma}
denotes the jump of
g
, with
g^{\pm}:=g
in the domains

\Omega^{\pm}(t)=\{\pm(x_{1}-\varphi(t,x^{\prime}))>0\},

and

\mathfrak{m}^{\pm}=\rho^{\pm}(v_{N}^{\pm}-\partial_{t}\varphi),\quad v_{N}^{\pm}=v^{\pm}\cdot N,\quad N=(1,-\partial_{2}\varphi,-\partial_{3}\varphi)^{\top},\quad F_{j{N}}^{\pm}=F_{j}^{\pm}\cdot N,

v_{\tau}^{\pm}=(v_{1}^{\pm}\partial_{2}\varphi+v_{2}^{\pm},v_{1}^{\pm}\partial_{3}\varphi+v_{3}^{\pm})^{\top},\quad F_{j\tau}^{\pm}=(F_{1j}^{\pm}\partial_{2}\varphi+F_{2j}^{\pm},F_{1j}^{\pm}\partial_{3}\varphi+F_{3j}^{\pm})^{\top},

and
\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{m}^{\pm}|_{\Gamma}
is the mass transfer flux across the discontinuity. The jump conditions (
2.1f
) come actually from the divergence constraints (
1.2
). On the other hand, conditions (
2.1d
) are rewritten as
\partial_{t}\varphi[\rho F_{j{\rm N}}]=0
. That is, conditions (
2.1d
) are implied by (
2.1f
) and can be thus excluded from system (
2.1
).

Mathematically,
shock waves
are noncharacteristic discontinuities, i.e., for them the hypersurface
\Gamma(t)
is a not a characteristic of the hyperbolic system (
1.5
). As in gas dynamics, one can show that this is so if
\mathfrak{m}\neq 0
and
[\rho]\neq 0
. Simple arguments for deriving the boundary conditions for 3D shock waves from (
2.1
) are essentially the same as in the 2D case in
[
25
]
, but for the reader’s convenience we present them below.

In view of (
2.1f
), conditions (
2.1c
) and (
2.1e
) form the following linear algebraic system for the jumps
[v_{\tau}]
,
[F_{1\tau}]
,
[F_{2\tau}]
and
[F_{3\tau}]
:

\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{m}&-\rho^{+}F_{1{N}}^{+}&-\rho^{+}F_{2{N}}^{+}&-\rho^{+}F_{3{N}}^{+}\\
-\rho^{+}F_{1{N}}^{+}&\mathfrak{m}&0&0\\
-\rho^{+}F_{2{N}}^{+}&0&\mathfrak{m}&0\\
-\rho^{+}F_{3{N}}^{+}&0&0&\mathfrak{m}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}[v_{\tau}]\\
[F_{1\tau}]\\
[F_{2\tau}]\\
[F_{3\tau}]\end{pmatrix}=0\quad\mbox{on}\ \Gamma.

(2.2)

Since
\mathfrak{m}\neq 0
, this system has a nonzero solution if

\mathfrak{m}^{2}=(\rho^{+})^{2}\left.\left((F_{1{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{+})^{2}\right)\right|_{\Gamma}.

We assume that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\neq(\rho^{+})^{2}\left.\left((F_{1{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{+})^{2}\right)\right|_{\Gamma}\,
, i.e.,

(v_{N}^{+}-\partial_{t}\varphi)^{2}\neq(F_{1{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{+})^{2}\quad\mbox{on}\ \Gamma.

(2.3)

We will see below that (
2.3
) holds thanks to the Lax conditions
[
19
]
.

From (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) we obtain

[v_{\tau}]=0,\quad[F_{j\tau}]=0,\quad j=1,2,3.

In view of (
2.1a
) and (

2.1b
) as

\mathfrak{M}[V]+|N|^{2}[p]=0,

where

\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}^{2}-(\rho^{+})^{2}\left.\left((F_{1{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{+})^{2}\right)\right|_{\Gamma},\quad V^{\pm}=1/\rho^{\pm}

(
\mathfrak{M}\neq 0

\begin{split}&[\mathfrak{m}]=0,\quad\mathfrak{M}[V]+|N|^{2}[p]=0,\quad[v_{\tau}]=0,\\
&[F_{j\tau}]=0,\quad[\rho F_{j{N}}]=0,\quad j=1,2,3.\end{split}

(2.4)

The free boundary problem for shock waves is the problem for the systems

A_{0}(U^{\pm})\partial_{t}U^{\pm}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{j}(U^{\pm})\partial_{j}U^{\pm}=0\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega^{\pm}(t)

(2.5)

with the boundary conditions (
2.4
) on
\Gamma(t)
and the initial data

{U}^{\pm}(0,{x})={U}_{0}^{\pm}({x}),\quad{x}\in\Omega^{\pm}(0),\quad\varphi(0,x^{\prime})=\varphi_{0}(x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.6)

The initial data (
2.6
) should satisfy not only the hyperbolicity conditions (
1.6
) but also constraints (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). As for the Cauchy problem, we can show that these constraints are preserved by problem (
2.4
)–(
2.6
) (the proof of this is interely the same as for the 2D case in

25
]
).
The
structural stability
of a shock wave means the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of a smooth solution
(U^{+},U^{-},\varphi)
to problem (
2.4
)–(
2.6
).

The free boundary problem (
2.4
)–(
2.6
) is reduced to that in the fixed domains
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\pm}
by the simple change of variables
{x}^{\sharp}_{1}=x_{1}-\varphi(t,x^{\prime})
, where
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\pm}=\{\pm x_{1}>0,\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
. Dropping the superscript

, from systems (
2.5
) we obtain

A_{0}(U^{\pm})\partial_{t}U^{\pm}+\widetilde{A}_{1}(U^{\pm},\varphi)\partial_{1}U^{\pm}+A_{2}(U^{\pm})\partial_{2}U^{\pm}+A_{3}(U^{\pm})\partial_{3}U^{\pm}=0\qquad\mbox{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\pm},

(2.7)

where

\widetilde{A}_{1}=\widetilde{A}_{1}(U,\varphi)=A_{1}(U)-A_{0}(U)\partial_{t}\varphi-A_{2}(U)\partial_{2}\varphi-A_{3}(U)\partial_{3}\varphi.

(2.8)

The boundary conditions for (
2.7
) are (
2.4
) on the plain
x_{1}=0
.

As is known, the Lax conditions
[
19
]
guarantee the correct number of boundary conditions on
x_{1}=0
for systems (
2.7
). The Lax conditions are the inequalities

\lambda_{k-1}^{-}<\partial_{t}\varphi<\lambda_{k}^{-},\quad\lambda_{k}^{+}<\partial_{t}\varphi<\lambda_{k+1}^{+}

which must hold for some fixed integer
k
, where in our case
1\leq k\leq=13
and
\lambda_{j}^{\pm}
(
j=\overline{1,{13}}
) are the eigenvalues of the matrices

A_{N}^{\pm}:=\left(A_{0}(U^{\pm})\right)^{-1}\left.\left(A_{1}(U^{\pm})-A_{2}(U^{\pm})\partial_{2}\varphi-A_{3}(U^{\pm})\partial_{3}\varphi\right)\right|_{x_{1}=0},

(2.9)

with
U^{\pm}
and
\varphi
satisfying the boundary conditions (
2.4
) on
x_{1}=0
. Moreover,
\lambda_{j}^{\pm}
are numbered as

\lambda_{1}^{-}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{13}^{-},\quad\lambda_{1}^{+}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{13}^{+},

and we take
\lambda^{-}_{0}:=-2|\partial_{t}\varphi|
and
\lambda_{14}^{+}:=2|\partial_{t}\varphi|
. Direct computations give

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda_{1}^{\pm}=v_{N}^{\pm}-\sqrt{(\bar{c}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{1{N}}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{\pm})^{2}}\,,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda_{2}^{\pm}=\lambda_{3}^{\pm}=v_{N}^{\pm}-\sqrt{(F_{1{N}}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{\pm})^{2}}\,,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda_{4}^{\pm}=\ldots=\lambda_{10}^{\pm}=v_{N}^{\pm}\,,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda_{11}^{\pm}=\lambda_{12}^{\pm}=v_{N}^{\pm}+\sqrt{(F_{1{N}}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{\pm})^{2}}\,,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda_{13}^{\pm}=v_{N}^{\pm}+\sqrt{(\bar{c}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{1{N}}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{\pm})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{\pm})^{2}}\qquad\mbox{on}\ x_{1}=0,\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

where
\bar{c}^{\pm}=c^{\pm}|N|
and
c^{\pm}=1/\sqrt{\rho^{\prime}(p^{\pm})}
are the sound speeds ahead and behind of the shock.

Without loss of generality we assume that
v_{N}^{\pm}|_{x_{1}=0}>\partial_{t}\varphi
. Then, we can check that, as in gas dynamics (as well as in 2D elastodynamics
[
25
,
31
]
), only 1-shocks are possible:

\begin{split}&v_{N}^{-}-\partial_{t}\varphi>\sqrt{(\bar{c}^{-})^{2}+(F_{1{N}}^{-})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{-})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{-})^{2}}\,,\\[6.0pt]
&\sqrt{(F_{1{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{+})^{2}}<v_{N}^{+}-\partial_{t}\varphi\\[6.0pt]
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \,<\sqrt{(\bar{c}^{+})^{2}+(F_{1{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{2{N}}^{+})^{2}+(F_{3{N}}^{+})^{2}}\qquad\mbox{on}\ x_{1}=0.\end{split}

(2.11)

We see that the Lax conditions (
2.11
) imply the fulfilment of
the above assumption (
2.3
).

Remark 2.1
.

The boundary matrices in (
2.9
) are nothing else than

\mathcal{A}(U^{\pm},N)|_{x_{1}=0}
, where

\mathcal{A}(U,\xi)=\left(A_{0}(U)\right)^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\xi_{j}A_{j}(U),

with
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
. We can easily calculate the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{j}(U,\xi)
of
\mathcal{A}(U,\xi)
, cf. (
2.10
):

\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda_{1}=v\cdot\xi-\sqrt{{c}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+(F_{1}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{2}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{3}\cdot\xi)^{2}}\,,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=v\cdot\xi-\sqrt{(F_{1}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{2}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{3}\cdot\xi)^{2}}\,,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda_{4}=\ldots=\lambda_{10}=v\cdot\xi,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda_{11}=\lambda_{12}=v\cdot\xi+\sqrt{(F_{1}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{2}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{3}\cdot\xi)^{2}}\,,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda_{13}=v\cdot\xi+\sqrt{{c}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+(F_{1}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{2}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{3}\cdot\xi)^{2}}\,.\end{array}

Since
\det F>0
(see (
1.3
), (
1.6
)),

(F_{1}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{2}\cdot\xi)^{2}+(F_{3}\cdot\xi)^{2}>0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}.

Therefore, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}(U,\xi)
are of constant multiplicity for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
. Moreover, since system (
1.5
) is symmetric hyperbolic, the matrix
\mathcal{A}(U,\xi)
is diagonalizable, i.e., all the multiple eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}(U,\xi)
are semi-simple. Referring to
[
24
]
, we may conclude that system (
1.5
) satisfies the Agranovich–Majda–Osher
block structure condition

[
1
,
20
,
22
]
. In view of the results in
[
20
,
21
,
23
]
, this means that the uniform linearized stability of a planar shock wave in elastodynamics implies the nonlinear structural stability of corresponding nonplanar shock waves.

3
Constant coefficient linearized problem associated with planar shock waves

Without loss of generality we assume that the unperturbed planar shock wave is given by the equation
x_{1}=0
. We now consider a constant solution
(U^{+},U^{-},\varphi)=(\widehat{U}^{+},\widehat{U}^{-},0)
of systems (
2.7
) and the boundary conditions (
2.4
) associated with the planar shock wave

\widehat{U}^{\pm}=(\hat{p}^{\pm},\hat{v}^{\pm},\widehat{F}_{1}^{\pm},\widehat{F}_{2}^{\pm},\widehat{F}_{3}^{\pm}),\quad\hat{\rho}^{\pm}=\rho(\hat{p}^{\pm})>0,\quad\hat{c}^{\pm}=1/\sqrt{\rho^{\prime}(\hat{p}^{\pm})}>0,

\hat{v}_{l}^{+}=\hat{v}_{l}^{-}
for

&\big[\widehat{F}_{lj}\big]=0,\quad l=2,3,\quad\big[\hat{\rho}\widehat{F}_{1j}\big]=0\end{split}

2.4
), where

,
[\hat{\rho}]=\hat{\rho}^{+}-\hat{\rho}^{-}
, etc., and without loss of generality we assume that

The Lax conditions (

M_{-}>\frac{M}{\sqrt{M^{2}-M_{1}^{2}}},

(3.2)

M_{1}<M<M_{*},

(3.3)

4
Main results

The KL and UKL conditions for initial boundary value problems for linear constant coefficient hyperbolic systems were first introduced by Kreiss
[
18
]
. In view of the Lax conditions (
3.3
), problem (
3.6
), (
3.7
), (
3.9
) has the 1-shock property in the sense the nonsingular matrix
(\mathcal{A}_{0})^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{1}
has a single negative eigenvalue. For such kind of noncharacteristic initial boundary value problems for symmetric hyperbolic systems equivalent formulations of the KL and UKL conditions were given in
[
29
]
by using ideas of Gardner and Kruskal
[
14
]
. Roughly speaking, the main idea of
[
29
]
borrowed from
[
14
]
is to carry all the calculations over the single characteristic incoming in the shock in the region behind of the shock wave.

Following
[
29
]
(see also
[
31
, Appendix A]
), we write down the following linear algebraic system for a vector
X
associated with problem (
3.7
), (
3.9
):

\displaystyle(s\mathcal{A}_{0}+\lambda\mathcal{A}_{1}+{i}\omega_{2}\mathcal{A}_{2}+{i}\omega_{3}\mathcal{A}_{3})X=0\,,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{A}_{1}\widetilde{U}_{0}\big)\cdot X=0,

(4.2)

where

s=\eta+{i}\xi,\quad\eta>0,\quad(\xi,{\omega}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\omega^{\prime}=(\omega_{2},\omega_{3})

(
s
and
\omega^{\prime}
are the Laplace and Fourier variables respectively), the vector
\widetilde{U}_{0}=\widetilde{U}_{0}(\eta,\xi,\omega^{\prime})
satisfies the Fourier–Laplace transform of the boundary conditions (
3.9
),

(s\mathfrak{B}_{0}+i\omega_{2}\mathfrak{B}_{2}+i\omega_{3}\mathfrak{B}_{3}+\mathfrak{B}_{1})\widetilde{U}_{0}=0,

(4.3)

and
\lambda=\lambda^{+}(\eta,\xi,\omega^{\prime})
is the single root
\lambda
of the dispersion relation

\det(s\mathcal{A}_{0}+\lambda\mathcal{A}_{1}+{i}\omega_{2}\mathcal{A}_{2}+{i}\omega_{3}\mathcal{A}_{3})=0

(4.4)

lying strictly in the open right-half complex plane (
\Real\lambda^{+}>0
).

Since
\lambda^{+}
is a simple root, we can choose 12 linearly independent equations of system (

). Adding them to equation (
4.2
), we obtain for the vector
X
the linear algebraic system

\mathfrak{L}X=0

(4.5)

whose determinant is, in fact, the Lopatinski determinant (see
[
29
]
and
[
31
, Appendix A]
). Following
[
29
,
31
]
, we are now ready to give the definitions of the KL and UKL conditions for problem (
3.7
), (
3.9
).

Definition 4.1
.

Problem (
3.7
), (
3.9
) satisfies the KL condition if
\det\mathfrak{L}(\eta,\xi,\omega^{\prime},\lambda^{+})\neq 0
for all
\eta>0
and
(\xi,\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Problem (
3.7
), (
3.9
) satisfies the UKL condition if
\det\mathfrak{L}(\eta,\xi,\omega^{\prime},\lambda^{+})\neq 0
for all
\eta\geq 0
and
(\xi,\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
(with
\eta^{2}+\xi^{2}+|\omega^{\prime}|^{2}\neq 0
), where
\lambda^{+}(0,\xi,\omega^{\prime})=\lim\limits_{\eta\rightarrow+0}\lambda^{+}(\eta,\xi,\omega^{\prime})
.

The planar shock wave for which problem (
3.7
), (
3.9
) satisfies the UKL condition (under suitable assumptions on the constant states
\widehat{U}^{+}
and
\widehat{U}^{-}
) is called
uniformly stable
. If the KL condition holds, but the UKL is violated, then the planar shock is called
weakly stable
. If even the KL condition is violated, then the shock wave is
violently unstable
meaning that the linearized stability problem (
3.7
), (
3.9
) is ill-posed.

We are now in a position to formulate the main result of this paper.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let the planar shock wave
x_{1}=0
in compressible isentropic elastodynamics satisfies the Lax conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). Then, this shock wave is uniformly stable if and only if

\min_{\bar{\omega}_{2}^{2}+\bar{\omega}_{3}^{2}=1}\bigg(M\sqrt{1+|\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}|^{2}+|\bar{\omega}_{2}\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{2}+\bar{\omega}_{3}\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{3}|^{2}+\big|\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\times(\bar{\omega}_{2}\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{2}+\bar{\omega}_{3}\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{3})\big|^{2}}\\
-\big|\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\cdot(\bar{\omega}_{2}\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{2}+\bar{\omega}_{3}\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{3})\big|\sqrt{1+|\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}|^{2}-M^{2}}\,\bigg)^{2}-\big(1+|\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}|^{2}\big)^{2}\big(R(M^{2}-|\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}|^{2})-1\big)>0,

(4.6)

where

is the downstream Mach number,

measures the competition between downstream and upstream densities and
\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}
(
j=1,2,3
) are the transposed rows of the scaled deformation gradient
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{ij})_{i,j=1,2,3}
behind of the shock. If (

6
Concluding remarks

As in the 2D case in
[
25
]
, we can show that all compressive shock waves with convex equations of state
p=p(\rho)
are uniformly stable, i.e., they are Lax shocks and inequality (
4.6
) is automatically fulfilled for them. Indeed, the proof of the following proposition is totally the same as in the 2D case in
[
25
]
and therefore we just omit it here.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
p(\rho)
be a convex function of
\rho
. Then all planar compressive shock waves satisfy the Lax conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) as well as the “elastic” counterpart

\widetilde{M}^{2}(R-1)<1

(6.1)

of Majda’s condition (
4.10
), where

\widetilde{M}=\sqrt{M^{2}-M_{1}^{2}}=\sqrt{M^{2}-|\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}|^{2}}

is the “elastic” Mach number (
0<\widetilde{M}<1
, cf. (
3.3
)).

Omitting simple algebra, we equivalently rewrite the uniform stability condition (
4.6
)/(
5.23
) as

\widetilde{M}^{2}(R-1)<1+\frac{\mathcal{D}}{M_{*}^{4}},

(6.2)

where

\mathcal{D}=(M\sigma-|\ell_{0}|\beta-M_{*}^{2}\widetilde{M})(M\sigma-|\ell_{0}|\beta+M_{*}^{2}\widetilde{M}).

(6.3)

The proof of the following proposition is essentially the same as the proof of the corresponding inequality in the 2D case (see
[
25
, Appendix B]
), but since there are still technical peculiarities of the 3D case, we present this proof in Appendix
B
for completeness.

Proposition 6.2
.

All planar Lax shock waves satisfy the inequality

\mathcal{D}>0.

(6.4)

As in isentropic gas dynamics, rarefaction shocks (
R<1
) are, in general, possible. In view of (
6.4
), the uniform stability condition (
6.2
) holds for them. On the other hand, (
6.2
) is rewritten as

M^{2}(R-1)<1+Q,

(6.5)

where
Q=M_{1}^{2}(R-1)+\frac{\mathcal{D}}{M_{*}^{4}}
. Thanks to (
6.4
) the “elastic” additive
Q>0
for
R>1
. Therefore, comparing (
6.5
) with Majda’s condition (
4.10
), we make the conclusion that the elastic force plays
stabilizing role
for uniform stability.

As in the 2D case (see
[
25
, Theorem 2]
), we come to the following theorem, which is the consequence of Propositions
6.1
and
6.4
.

Theorem 6.1
.

All compressive shock waves in isentropic elastodynamics with a convex equation of state
p=p(\rho)
are structurally stable.

It is natural to expect that the elastic force plays stabilizing role not only for uniform stability but also for the possible structural stability of weakly stable shocks. Since, as was shown by Coulombel and Secchi
[
12
]
, weakly stable shock waves in isentropic gas dynamics are structurally stable, the natural conjecture is that weakly stable shock waves in isentropic elastodynamics are also structurally stable. The proof of this conjecture is postponed to the future research.

Regarding the sufficient structural stability condition (
4.7
) (for general equations of state

), its proof follows from the form of the variable coefficient linearized problem for nonplanar shocks. Indeed, for the system of conservation laws (

of order

After straightening

Freezing the coefficients in (

for 1-shocks, we obtain the frozen coefficient linear boundary conditions

B(\widehat{U}^{+},\widehat{U}^{-})\Phi-\widetilde{A}_{1}(\widehat{U}^{+},\hat{\varphi})U=0\quad\mbox{on }x_{1}=0

At last, we note that structural stability in the sense of local-in-time existence in Theorem

Brick-finite skew-gentle algebras are representation-finite
Monica Garcia, Léa Lavoué

Abstract
. We show that a skew-gentle algebra is brick-finite if and only if it is representation-finite, generalizing Plamondon’s original result for gentle algebras



1.
Introduction

Bricks, or Schur representations, have long been an object of study in the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras, where they appear as natural generalizations of simple modules
[
11
]
. More recently, they have become central to several areas, notably in
\tau
-tilting theory, torsion theory, stability conditions, and the theory of
g
-vector fans. An algebra is said to be
brick-finite
if it admits only finitely many bricks up to isomorphism. While every representation-finite algebra is clearly brick-finite, the converse is false in general and holds only under additional structural assumptions. This has led to a body on literature treating the distribution of bricks for a given algebra, and their implications. A summary of fundamental conjectures posed in recent years on brick-finiteness can be found in
[
18
]
.

An interesting example where brick-finiteness coincides with representation-finiteness is provided by
gentle algebras
, a well-known class of string algebras introduced in
[
3
]
. Using the combinatorial classification of their indecomposable modules, Plamondon proved that brick-finiteness implies representation-finiteness in this setting
[
19
]
.

Skew-gentle
algebras (also referred as
skewed
-gentle algebras) are a generalization of gentle algebras, which were originally introduced in
[
12
]
.
While they retain much of the combinatorial flavor of gentle algebras, skew-gentle algebras exhibit new phenomena, notably the presence of special vertices and additional local structure.
Their indecomposable modules were also completely classified when the base field is of characteristic different from two in
[
8
]
.
As for gentle algebras, skew-gentle algebras and their indecomposable modules can also be studied through geometric models
[
15
,
14
,
1
]
, specifically, through to surfaces with orbifolds.

In this short note, we generalize Plamondon’s result to skew-gentle algebras. Our main result is the following.

Main Theorem

(
3.1
)
.

Let
A\cong\Bbbk Q^{sp}/I^{sp}
be a skew-gentle algebra over a field
\Bbbk
of with
\operatorname{char}(\Bbbk)\neq 2
. Then,
A
is brick-finite if and only if it is representation-finite.

While finalizing this manuscript, we were made aware of parallel work by Chang, Jin, Schroll, and Wang
[
7
]
. In Section 3 of their paper, the authors investigate a closely related family of skew-gentle algebras to those considered in Section
3
of this note. They show that these algebras are brick-infinite under the additional assumption that the base field is algebraically closed, from which a result analogous to Theorem
3.1
follows in this setting. They further study silting-discreteness of skew-gentle algebras via their geometric model.

3.
Minimal bands for skew-gentle algebras and proof of the main theorem

The goal is to prove the following theorem, which extends
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
to skew-gentle algebras. First recall that a
brick
is a module whose endomorphism ring is a division ring, that is, all of its non-zero endomorphisms are invertible. We will show the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\cong\Bbbk Q^{sp}/I^{sp}
be a skew-gentle algebra over a field
\Bbbk
of with
\operatorname{char}(\Bbbk)\neq 2
. Then,
A
is brick-finite if and only if it is representation-finite.

As in the gentle case, it is straightforward to see that being representation-finite is a sufficient condition for being brick-finite, since bricks are indecomposable modules. We will show that this is a sufficient condition.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any skew-gentle algebra
A=\Bbbk Q^{sp}/I^{sp}
of infinite representation type, there exists a band in the gentle quiver
(Q^{sp},I^{\prime})
, where
I^{\prime}=\langle I\cup\{\varepsilon_{i}^{2}\mid i\in S_{p}\}\rangle

2.2

(4)

Proof.

As recalled in the previous section, the indecomposable modules of
A
can be classified using admissible strings and bands. Note that Theorem
2.3
says that a given

gives rise to an infinite family of indecomposable modules only if

is an admissible band, or if
x
is an admissible string of type
(p,p)
. In the later case, by definition,
s(x)
and
t(x)
are special vertices, and thus

is a band in

Thus, if

(Q^{sp},I^{\prime})
has a band, or

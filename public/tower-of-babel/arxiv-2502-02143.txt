A twisted derived category of hyper-Kähler varieties of K ​ 3 [ n ] K3^{[n]} -type
Ruxuan Zhang

Abstract
. We conjecture that a natural twisted derived category of any hyper-Kähler variety of K ​ 3 [ n ] K3^{[n]} -type is controlled by its Markman-Mukai lattice. We prove the conjecture under numerical constraints, and our proof relies on Markman’s projectively hyperholomorphic bundle and a recently proven twisted version of the D-equivalence conjecture. In particular, we prove that any two fine moduli spaces of stable sheaves on a K ​ 3 K3 surface are derived equivalent if they have the same dimension.



1.
Introduction

Throughout,we work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
X
be any hyper-Kähler variety of
K3^{[n]}
-type with
n\geq 2
. A general philosophy predicts that
X
is a moduli space of stable objects on some
K3
category
\mathcal{A}
, which is expected to be governed by the Markman-Mukai lattice

L(X)
.

A classical example supporting this perspective arises when
X
is the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on a
K3
surface
S
.
In this case, the associated
K3
category is simply
D^{b}(S)
and
there exists a Hodge isometry

L(X)\cong\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})

which identifies the Markman-Mukai lattice
L(X)
with the Mukai lattice
\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
of
S
.
By the derived Torelli theorem, the bounded derived category
D^{b}(S)
is determined by the Mukai lattice. Furthermore, the Bridgeland-King-Reid (BKR) equivalence implies that

D^{b}(X)
is completely determined by
D^{b}(S)
. Consequently,
D^{b}(X)
is also controlled by the Mukai lattice
\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. A more recent example of this phenomenon can be found in
[
3
]
.

In the general setting, the existence of the
K3
category
\mathcal{A}
remains an open question.
Nevertheless, one may still ask whether the derived category
D^{b}(X)
is controlled by the Markman-Mukai lattice
L(X)
.
If true, this would provide supporting evidence for both the existence of
\mathcal{A}
and the possibility of constructing

D^{b}(X)
from
\mathcal{A}
. However, a direct attempt at establishing this connection does not hold in full generality—see
[
15
, Theorem 5.13]
for a counterexample.
Instead, we propose a refinement by considering a natural twisted derived category of
X
. To be more precise, we have the following speculation:

Speculation 1.1
.

Let
X,~Y
be hyper-Kähler varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type with
n\geq 2
.

(1)

There exists a
K3
category
\mathcal{A}_{X}
such that
X
is a moduli space of stable objects in
\mathcal{A}_{X}
.

(2)

If there is a Hodge isometry
L(X)\cong L(Y)
, then we have

\mathcal{A}_{X}\cong\mathcal{A}_{Y}{\rm~or~}\mathcal{A}_{X}\cong\mathcal{A}_{Y}^{\rm op}

(3)

There exists a canonical Brauer class
\theta_{X}
such that

{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{X}\cong D^{b}(X,\pm n\theta_{X}).

The existence of the
K3
category has been studied by Markman and Mehrotra in
[
14
]
and by Chunyi Li, E.Macri, A.Perry, P. Stellari, and Xiaolei Zhao recently.

We observe that
X
naturally arises as a moduli space of stable objects not only in the category
\mathcal{A}_{X}
but also in its opposite
\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\rm op}
.
In this paper, we define a natural orientation on
L(X)
to distinguish between the conjectural category
\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\rm op}
. Additionally, we define a natural Brauer class on
X
, which is predicted to be exactly the obstruction preventing
X
from being a fine moduli space on
\mathcal{A}_{X}
.

We shall briefly introduce the definition of orientation and the Brauer class here.
The orthogonal complement
H^{2}(X)^{\perp}\subset L(X)
is a rank-one lattice generated by a primitive vector whose square is
2n-2
.
Choosing a generator
v
(noting that
-v
is also a generator) induces a natural orientation on
L(X)
. We denote the Markman-Mukai lattice with this orientation by
(L(X),v)
.
Associated with this choice of
v
is a canonical cohomology class

\theta_{v}\in H^{2}(X,\mu_{2n-2}),

which is invariant under the discriminant-preserving subgroup of the monodromy group. Moreover, under a change of orientation, we have
\theta_{-v}=-\theta_{v}
.
For details, see Section
2
. The class
\theta_{v}
has essentially appeared in
[
11
, Lemma 7.4]
.

The presence of the canonical class
\theta_{v}
implies that any hyper-Kähler variety of
K3^{[n]}
-type naturally carries natural
\mu_{2n-2}
-gerbe structures. This motivates the study of the derived category of twisted sheaves on such gerbes.
In particular, when
X
is a moduli space of stable sheaves on a
K3
surface, the Brauer class
[\theta_{v}]\in\Br(X)
corresponds to the obstruction preventing
X
from being a fine moduli space (see Lemma
2.4
for details).

We now state the main conjecture of this work: the Markman-Mukai lattice
L(X)
governs the derived category of twisted sheaves on a natural gerbe. This conjecture is both a direct consequence of Speculation
1.1
and an explicit realization of its broader framework.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two hyper-Kähler varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type. If there exists a Hodge isometry between oriented Markman-Mukai lattices

\phi:(L(X),v)\rightarrow(L(Y),w),

then there is an equivalence of bounded derived categories

D^{b}(X,\left[n\theta_{v}\right])\cong D^{b}(Y,\left[\epsilon n\theta_{w}\right]).

Here
\epsilon=1
if
\phi
is orientation-preserving, and
\epsilon=-1
if
\phi
is orientation-reversing.

It is important to note that the identity map of lattices
(L(X),v)\rightarrow(L(X),-v)
reverses orientations.
Hence, we can reduce to the orientation-preserving case.

A supporting case of this conjecture is the twisted D-equivalence theorem, recently established in
[
16
]
.
This case also forms the foundation of our approach: reducing the conjecture to lattice-theoretic computations of Markman’s hyperholomorphic bundles
[
12
]
.

The first part of our results provides lattice-theoretic evidence in support of Conjecture
1.2
. In particular, the Brauer classes
[n\theta_{v}],~[n\theta_{w}]
naturally arise in the relevant lattice computations (see Section
2
for details).
Among the possible
B
-field lifts of
\theta_{v}
, certain choices exhibit more desirable properties. Such a preferred lift is given by

\frac{\delta_{v}}{2n-2}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})

for a distinguished class
\delta_{v}
.
We introduce a twisted extended Mukai lattice (with a natural orientation) and obtain

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X,~Y,~\phi
be as in Conjecture
1.2
. Then there is a Hodge isometry

\psi:\widetilde{H}(X,\frac{\delta_{v}}{2n-2},\mathbb{Z})\cong\widetilde{H}(Y,\frac{\delta_{w}}{2n-2},\mathbb{Z}).

Moreover,
\psi
is orientation-preserving if and only if
\phi
is.

The twisted extended Mukai lattice up to Hodge isometry is independent of the specific choice of a
B
-field. We prove a derived Torelli-type theorem (Theorem
3.8
) by constructing Markman’s projectively hyperholomorphic bundle and applying the twisted derived equivalence for birational hyper-Kähler varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type. This applies to the following result:

Theorem 1.4
.

Conjecture
1.2
holds if

{\rm Span}\left(\phi(v),w\right)\subset L(Y)

is a primitive lattice embedding. Here,
{\rm Span}\left(\phi(v),w\right)
denotes the sublattice generated by integral linear combinations of
\phi(v)
and
w
.

Next, we examine moduli spaces of stable objects on a
K3
surface
S
. Let
v
be a primitive vector in
\widetilde{H}^{1,1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, and denote by
M_{v}
the moduli space of stable objects with respect to any generic stability condition.
1
1

1

Since moduli spaces associated with different stability conditions are birational, their twisted derived categories are equivalent by
[
16
]
; see Subsection
3.1
for details.

Theorem
1.4
has the following consequence:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
S
be a projective
K3
surface and
w
be a primitive vector in
\widetilde{H}^{1,1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
satisfying
w^{2}=2n-2\geq 2
. Then there exist derived equivalences

D^{b}(M_{w},[n\theta_{w}])\cong D^{b}(M_{w},[-n\theta_{w}])\cong D^{b}(S^{[n]}).

If
v^{2}=2
or if
M_{w}
is a fine moduli space of stable sheaves, then the Brauer classes
[\pm n\theta_{{w}}]
vanish. In these cases, we obtain derived equivalences between untwisted categories, thereby providing an affirmative answer to a question posed by Huybrechts:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
S
be a projective
K3
surface and
v,w
be primitive vectors in
\widetilde{H}^{1,1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
with
v^{2}=w^{2}=2n-2\geq 2
.
If either
n=2
or both
M_{v}
and
M_{w}
are birational to fine moduli spaces of stable sheaves, then there exists a derived equivalence

D^{b}(M_{v})\cong D^{b}(M_{w}).

We remark that
M_{v}
(for any generic stability condition) is birational to a fine moduli space of stable sheaves if and only if
\mathrm{div}(v)=1
in
\tilde{H}^{1,1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
as proved in
[
15
, Corollary 4.6]
.
This property depends solely on the Mukai vector
v
.

2.
Lattice-theoretical evidence

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety of
K3^{[n]}
-type, where
n\geq 2.

In this section, we recall the precise definition of the class
\theta_{v}\in H^{2}(X,\mu_{2n-2})
and introduce the twisted extended Mukai lattice.
When
X
and
Y
satisfy the assumptions of Conjecture
1.2
, we establish a Hodge isometry between their corresponding twisted extended Mukai lattices, thereby providing lattice-theoretical evidence in support of the conjecture.

2.1.
Oriented Markman-Mukai lattice

The second cohomology group
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})

carries the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki (BBF) form, along with a weight-2 integral Hodge structure. Markman has described an extension of lattice and Hodge structure, denoted by
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\subset L(X)
, which we refer to as the
Markman-Mukai lattice
. This lattice satisfies the following properties (see
[
10
, Corollary 9.5]
[
1
, Section 1]
):

(1)

As a lattice,
L(X)\cong U^{\oplus 4}\oplus E_{8}(-1)^{\oplus 2}
, where it has signature
(4,20)
. The weight-2 Hodge structure on
L(X)
is inherited form
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

(2)

The orthogonal complement
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{\perp}\subset L(X)
is generated by a primitive vector of square
2n-2.

(3)

If
X
is a moduli space of stable objects on a
K3
surface
S
with Mukai vector
v\in\widetilde{H}^{1,1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, then the inclusion
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\subset L(X)
is identified with
v^{\perp}\subset\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
.

(4)

An isometry
\rho:H^{2}(X_{1},\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X_{2},\mathbb{Z})
is a parallel transport operator if and only if
\rho
is orientation-preserving and can be lifted to an isometry
\phi:L(X_{1})\rightarrow L(X_{2})
.

Remark 2.1
.

Markman’s construction establishes a natural choice of
O(L(X))
-orbit of embeddings of
H^{2}(X)
to
L(X)
, where the latter is considered without additional Hodge structure. Therefore, the embedding
H^{2}(X)\subset L(X)
and the Hodge structure on
L(X)
described in
[
1
]
are only canonical up to isometries of
L(X)
. All the above properties hold for any embedding in the natural
O(L(X))
-orbit.

To define an orientation on
L(X)
, we fix a generator
v
of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{\perp}
and proceed as follows.
Let
\sigma
be the
2
-form on
X
, and let
h\in H^{2}(X)
be a Kähler class. Then there is a positive four-dimensional real subspace in
L(X)
spanned by

\mathrm{Re}(\sigma),~\mathrm{Im}(\sigma),~h,~v.

This basis determines an orientation of
L(X)
, which is independent of the choice of the Kähler class
h
. We denote this oriented lattice by
(L(X),v)
and refer to it as the
oriented Markman-Mukai lattice
.

Now, let
Y
be another hyper-Kähler variety of
K3^{[n]}
-type. A Hodge isometry between oriented Markman-Mukai lattices

\phi:(L(X),v)\rightarrow(L(Y),w)

is simply a Hodge isometry
\phi:L(X)\rightarrow L(Y).
.
We classify
\phi
as orientation-preserving if it preserves the orientations determined by
v
and
w
, and as orientation-reversing otherwise. We remark that the identity map of lattices
\phi:(L(X),v)\rightarrow(L(X),-v)
is orientation-reversing.

2.2.
The canonical class in
H^{2}(X,\mu_{2n-2})

Assume that
X
is a manifold of
K3^{[n]}
-type. Since
X
has no odd cohomology, the discussion in
[
5
, Section 4.1]
yields the following explicit description of the (cohomological) Brauer group:

(1)

\mathrm{Br}(X)=\left(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})/\mathrm{Pic}(X)\right)\otimes\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

The description (
1
) also allows us to present a Brauer class in the form

(2)

\left[\frac{\beta}{d}\right]\in\mathrm{Br}(X),\quad\beta\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\quad d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\};

this is referred to as the “
B
-field”.
Since
X
has no odd-degree cohomology, we obtain the following short exact sequence:

(3)

0\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\times(2n-2)}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\pi}H^{2}(X,\mu_{2n-2})\rightarrow 0.

The natural inclusion
\mu_{2n-2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
induces a map

[-]:H^{2}(X,\mu_{2n-2})\rightarrow\mathrm{Br}(X).

Here by abuse of notation, we also denote this map by
[-]
and we have

\left[\pi(\beta)\right]=\left[\frac{\beta}{2n-2}\right]\in\mathrm{Br}(X).

We introduce a class
\delta_{v}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and show that the image
\pi(\delta_{v})\in H^{2}(X,\mu_{2n-2})
is a canonical class.
It leads to a class
\left[\pi(\delta_{v})\right]=\left[\frac{\delta_{v}}{2n-2}\right]\in\mathrm{Br}(X)
.

The class
\left[\frac{\delta_{v}}{2n-2}\right]
has already appeared in
[
11
, Lemma 7.4]
and we now reformulate it as follows:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
v
be a generator of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{\perp}\subset L(X)
. Then there exists a class (non-unique)

\delta_{v}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

The square
\delta^{2}_{v}=2-2n
and the divisibility
\mathrm{div}(\delta_{v})=2n-2
in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

(2)

The class
\frac{\delta_{v}-v}{2n-2}
is integral in
L(X)
.

Moreover, the image
\pi(\delta_{v})
in
H^{2}(X,\mu_{2n-2})
via the sequence (
3
) is independent of the choice of
\delta_{v}
and is invariant under the subgroup of the monodromy group that preserves the discriminant.

Proof.

For conditions (1) and (2), we apply a parallel transport operator
\rho
to a Hilbert scheme of
n
points on a
K3
surface
S
, yielding the following commutative diagram:

2.3.
Twisted extended Mukai lattices

Let

be a

-field. We define the twisted extended Mukai lattice
\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
as the

To be precise, as a lattice, we have

\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\oplus U,

where
U
is the hyperbolic plane with generators
e,~f
, satisfying
\left<e.f\right>=-1,~e^{2}=f^{2}=0
. In this way, the BBF form on

extends to the Mukai pairing
\left<-.-\right>

The weight-2 Hodge structure on
\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
is given by

\widetilde{H}^{2,0}(X,B)=\mathbb{C}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}^{B}(\sigma)=\mathbb{C}\cdot(\sigma+\left<B.\sigma\right>f),

where
\sigma
is the
2
-form on

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}^{B}:\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle re+c+sf

\displaystyle\mapsto re+(c+rB)+(rB^{2}/2+s+\left<c.B\right>)f.

for any

and

.
The orientation on
\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
is induced by the basis

\mathrm{Re}(\sigma),~\mathrm{Im}(\sigma),~\mathrm{Re}({\mathbf{e}}^{ih}(e)),~\mathrm{Im}({\mathbf{e}}^{ih}(e))

of a four-dimensional positive space, where

is a Kähler class.
When

is an integral isometry preserving both the discriminant and the orientation. In fact, for any even lattice

2.4.
Proof of Theorem

\left<\phi(v).(\phi(v)-w)\right>=\frac{1}{2}(\phi(v)-w)^{2}.

(resp.

Equivalences from hyperholomorphic bundles

We denote by

(15)

F_{\mathcal{U}^{[n]}}=F_{\mathcal{U}}\oplus\mathrm{id_{\delta}}:H^{2}(M^{[n]},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(S^{[n]},\mathbb{Q}).

For
g\in O(\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we define
\mathfrak{M}_{g}
to be the moduli space of isomorphism classes of quadruples
(X,\eta_{X},Y,\eta_{Y})

We summarize the key result as follows (for a partial summary, see
[

[

]

[

]

:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
12
,
9
,
16
]
)
.

Let
X,~Y
be hyper-Kähler varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type and
S,~M,~r\geq 2,~\mathcal{U},~F_{\mathcal{U}^{[n]}}
be as above.
Assume that there exist parallel transports

\rho_{1}:H^{2}(M^{[n]},\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}),~\rho_{2}:H^{2}(S^{[n]},\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

such that the composition

\rho_{2}\circ F_{\mathcal{U}^{[n]}}\circ\rho_{1}^{-1}:H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Q})

is a rational Hodge isometry. Then we have an
equivalence of twisted derived categories

D^{b}(X,\left[\rho_{1}(\frac{-c_{1}(\mathcal{U}|_{M\times\{x\}})}{r}-\frac{\delta_{M^{[n]}}}{2})\right])\cong D^{b}(Y,\left[\rho_{2}(\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{U}|_{\{y\}\times S})}{r}-\frac{\delta_{S^{[n]}}}{2})\right]),

where
x\in S,~y\in M
are arbitrary points.

Proof.

By
[
12
]
, there exist markings
\eta_{M^{[n]}}
and
\eta_{S^{[n]}}
for the Hilbert schemes
M^{[n]}
and
S^{[n]}
, respectively, which induce an isometry
g=\eta_{S^{[n]}}\circ F_{\mathcal{U}^{[n]}}\circ\eta_{M^{[n]}}^{-1}\in O(\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}})
, such that

(M^{[n]},\eta_{M^{[n]}},S^{[n]},\eta_{S^{[n]}})\in\mathfrak{M}^{0}_{g},

where
\mathfrak{M}^{0}_{g}
denotes the distinguished connected component of
\mathfrak{M}_{g}
in
[
16
, Theorem 1.3]
.
There are natural forgetful maps for

\Pi_{i}:\mathfrak{M}^{0}_{g}\to\mathfrak{M}_{\Lambda},~~(X_{1},\eta_{1},X_{2},\eta_{2})\mapsto(X_{i},\eta_{i}).

Markman proved in
[
12
, Lemma 5.7]
that
\Pi_{i}
is surjective.
Now consider marked hyper-Kähler varieties
(Y,\eta_{Y})
and
(X,\eta_{X})
,
where

\eta_{Y}:=\eta_{S^{[n]}}\circ\rho_{2}^{-1}{\rm~and~}\eta_{X}=\eta_{M^{[n]}}\circ\rho_{1}^{-1}

According to the surjectivity of
\Pi_{2}
,
we obtain that
(Y,\eta_{Y})
lifts to

(X^{\prime},\eta_{X^{\prime}},Y,\eta_{Y})\in\mathfrak{M}^{0}_{g}

for some other
(X^{\prime},\eta_{X^{\prime}})\in\mathfrak{M}_{\Lambda}.

In what follows, we demonstrate that
X
and
X^{\prime}
are birational.

Since
(X^{\prime},\eta_{X^{\prime}},Y,\eta_{Y})
and
(M^{[n]},\eta_{M^{[n]}},S^{[n]},\eta_{S^{[n]}})
are in the same connected component and
\rho_{2}=\eta_{Y}^{-1}\circ\eta_{S^{[n]}}
is a parallel transport, we have

\rho^{\prime}:=\eta_{X^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\eta_{M^{[n]}}:H^{2}(M^{{[n]}},\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})

is also a parallel transport. Therefore, we obtain a parallel transport between

and
X^{\prime}

\rho^{\prime}\circ\rho_{1}^{-1}:H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}).

We now verify that

preserves Hodge structures:
A direct computation shows that

\rho^{\prime}\circ\rho_{1}^{-1}=\eta_{X}^{-1}\circ\eta_{X^{\prime}}.

Since
(X^{\prime},\eta_{X^{\prime}},Y,\eta_{Y})\in\mathfrak{M}^{0}_{g}

\eta_{Y}^{-1}\circ g\circ\eta_{X^{\prime}}(\sigma_{X^{\prime}})=\sigma_{Y}.

Combining Equation

and

\eta_{X}^{-1}\circ\eta_{X^{\prime}}(\sigma_{X^{\prime}})=\sigma_{X}

together with
[

Lemma 3.3

Proposition 3.4

If

{\mathbf{e}}^{\frac{-m\beta}{r}}\circ\psi\circ{\mathbf{e}}^{\frac{m^{\prime}\alpha}{r}}

3.5.
A criterion of equivalence

We now present a criterion for lifting an integral Hodge isometry between twisted extended Mukai lattices to a twisted derived equivalence.
Let the following data be as in the previous subsection:

•

a Hodge isometry
\psi:\tilde{H}(X,A,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\tilde{H}(Y,B,\mathbb{Z})

•

\psi(f)=re+m\beta+sf,{\rm~and}~\psi^{-1}(f)=re+m^{\prime}\alpha+s^{\prime}f

Denote by

\Lambda=\Lambda_{K3}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\delta,~\delta^{2}=2-2n

Recall that a

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\rho:H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

Proof.

The proof is essentially in
[

By

is an integral isometry.
Note that we have (and similarly for

\rho(\delta_{v})=\delta_{w}
and
\tilde{\rho}(v)=w

\frac{k\delta_{v}+\ell v}{2n-2}\in L(X)\Leftrightarrow\frac{k\delta_{w}+\ell w}{2n-2}\in L(Y),

∎

We emphasize that the rank

Lemma 3.7
.

Let the settings be as above.

We set

m_{0}^{2}\ell\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}=r_{0}s.

4.1.

We now give a criterion of primitive lattice embedding.

.

Assume that

The lattice embedding

\mathrm{gcd}\left((r^{2}-1)s,k\right)=1

Proof.

It suffices to show that if a linear combination of

and
w
with rational coefficients is an integral class in
\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, then the coefficients must also be integral.
Assume that

\frac{1}{k}(xw+yv)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z}){\rm~for~some}~x,~y\in\mathbb{Q}.

Since the classes
v,~,w
and
H
are all primitive, we have

x,~y\in\mathbb{Z},~k\mid rx+y,~k\mid sx+(1-n)y.

By taking a linear combination, we obtain

k\mid(r(n-1)+s)x.

Applying (
29
) to substitute
n-1

, we get

k\mid(1-r^{2})sx.

The if
\mathrm{gcd}\left((r^{2}-1)s,k\right)=1
we must have
k\mid x
. Then
k
also divides
y
since
v
is primitive.
∎

4.2.
End of proof

If
r=\pm 1
, then
{\mathbf{e}}^{\pm kH}(w)=\pm v
for some
k
and the result follows from Subsection
3.1
.
For
r\neq\pm 1
, we complete the proof by showing that there exists an integer
t
, such that

{\rm Span}\left({\mathbf{e}}^{tH}(w),v\right)\subset\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})

is a primitive lattice embedding. We compute that

{\mathbf{e}}^{tH}(w)=(r,(k+rt)H,s+2ktd+rt^{2}d).

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists
t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\mathrm{gcd}\left((r^{2}-1)(s+2ktd+rt^{2}d),k+rt\right)=1.

Proof.

We define
\ell:=\mathrm{gcd}\left(k,r\right)
, which allows us to write

r=r^{\prime}\ell,~k=k^{\prime}\ell,~\mathrm{gcd}\left(k^{\prime},r^{\prime}\right)=1.

Since
\mathrm{gcd}\left(r,k,s\right)=1
and
\ell\mid r
, we have

\mathrm{gcd}\left(s,\ell\right)=1,~\mathrm{gcd}\left(r^{2}-1,\ell\right)=1.

Therefore, there exists an infinite set
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that for any
t\in I
,

k^{\prime}+r^{\prime}t{\rm~is~a~prime~number~and~}\mathrm{gcd}\left(r^{2}-1,k+rt\right)=1.

We now obtain

\mathrm{gcd}\left((r^{2}-1)(s+2ktd+rt^{2}d),k+rt\right)=\mathrm{gcd}\left(s+ktd,k^{\prime}+r^{\prime}t\right)

for any
t\in I
.

We claim that there exists
t\in I
such that
\mathrm{gcd}\left(s+ktd,k^{\prime}+r^{\prime}t\right)=1
. If not, for
t\in I
sufficiently large, we must have

\frac{s+ktd}{k^{\prime}+r^{\prime}t}=\frac{kd}{r^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}

since
k^{\prime}+r^{\prime}t
is a prime number for any
t\in I
.
This leads to

rs=k^{2}d,

which contradicts (
29
).

∎

In conclusion, there is a Hodge isometry

4.1

.
Then we can apply Theorem

(v,w,{\mathbf{e}}^{tH})

1.5

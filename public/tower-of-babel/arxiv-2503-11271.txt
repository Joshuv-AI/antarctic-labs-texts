Tail behavior of randomly weighted sums with interdependent summands
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, Remigijus Leipus, Charalampos D. Passalidis, Jonas Šiaulys

Abstract
. We reconsider a classical, well-studied problem from applied probability. This is the max-sum equivalence of randomly weighted sums, and the originality is because we manage to include interdependence among the primary random variables, as well as among primary random variables and random weights, as a generalization of previously published results. As a consequence we provide the finite-time ruin probability, in a discrete-time risk model. Furthermore, we established asymptotic bounds for the generalized moments of randomly weighted sums in the case of dominatedly varying primary random variables under the same dependence conditions. Finally, we give some results for randomly weighted and stopped sums under similar dependence conditions, with the restriction that the random weights are identically distributed, and the same holds for the primary random variables. Additionally, under these assumptions, we find asymptotic expressions for the random time ruin probability, in a discete-time risk model.



Keywords:
Interdependence, tail asymptotic independence, quasi asymptotic independence, heavy-tails, generalized moments of sums, weighted random sums.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: Primary 62P05; Secondary 60G70.

1.
Introduction

Let
X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be real-valued random variables (r.v.s), with distributions

F_{1},\,\ldots,\,F_{n}
, respectively, and let
\Theta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\Theta_{n}
be non-negative non-degenerate at zero r.v.s, with distributions
G_{1},\,\ldots,\,G_{n}
, respectively. The r.v.s
X_{i}
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, represent the primary random variables and the r.v.s
\Theta_{i}
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, depict the random weights. In this paper, we consider the weighted sum

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\,\Theta}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}X_{i}\,,

(1.1)

and study the asymptotic behavior of the tail probability of this sum and the corresponding maximum of such sums and the maximal summands, defined below:

\displaystyle\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\,\Theta}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Theta_{i}X_{i}\,,\qquad\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}X_{i}\ \ :=\ \ \max_{1\leq i\leq n}\Theta_{i}X_{i}.

Therefore, a crucial step is the proof of relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[S_{n}^{\,\Theta}>x\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x]\,,

(1.2)

as
x\to\infty
, or furthermore, the proof of relations

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[S_{n}^{\,\Theta}>x\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\,\Theta}>x\right]\ \ \sim\ \ \mathbf{P}\left[\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x\right]\ \ \sim\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x],

(1.3)

as
x\to\infty
.

The asymptotic relations (
1.3
) were studied in the frame of heavy-tailed distributions (usually, subexponential distributions, see definition in Section
2
below) with applications mainly in risk theory, where the
X_{i}
represent claim sizes. At the same time, the
\Theta_{i}
plays the role of the stochastic discount factor, while in the case of degenerate to constant nonzero value, the
\Theta_{i}
depicts the discount factor in a model with a constant force of interest. In most cases, relations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) were considered under the condition that
\{\Theta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\Theta_{n}\}
are arbitrarily dependent r.v.s, independent of
\{X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}\}
, with the last also mutually independent, see for example
[
43
]
and
[
44
]
.

The first attempts to study this problem under dependence structures, that contain independence as a particular case, were oriented to the non-weighted sum

\displaystyle S_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},

as e.g. papers
[
18
]
and
[
25
]
.

In the sequence, several papers were published considering the asymptotic behavior of the tail of
S_{n}^{\,\Theta}
in the following two cases:

(1)

r.v.s

X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}
are somehow dependent, while r.v.s
\Theta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\Theta_{n}
are arbitrarily dependent and collections
\{X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}\}
,
\{\Theta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\Theta_{n}\}
are mutually independent,

(2)

primary r.v.
X_{i}
and weight
\Theta_{i}
are somehow dependent for any index
i
, and the pairs
(X_{i},\,\Theta_{i})
,
(X_{j},\,\Theta_{j})
are independent for any
i\neq j
.

For (1) see, e.g.,
[
9
]
and
[
33
]
, while for (2) see, e.g.,
[
49
]
and
[
52
]
. Taking into account that in practical applications, one can find the cases where both (1) and (2) appear simultaneously, it is of interest to generalize some already published works by combining (1) and (2) through a new dependence structure, inspired by
[
2
]
and next by the generalization to multidimensional setup in
[
23
]
and
[
34
]
. In the literature, (1) and (2) were combined only in the case of regularly varying distributions under the framework of multivariate regular variation and some multivariate extensions of Breiman’s lemma, see for example
[
17
]
. We refer to the combination of (1) and (2) cases as
interdependence
. We would like to remind that, according to our knowledge, the only paper, where this kind of ’interdependence’ is contained with distribution classes larger than regular variation, is the paper
[
6
]
, where the relation (
1.2
) was examined in the case of
n=2
, with some dependence structures inspired by
[
2
]
, similar to our structures.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, after some basic definitions, we provide some heavy-tailed distribution classes and some dependence structures that play an essential role in our work. In Section
3
, we introduce a new dependence structure and present the main results (related with (
1.3
)) with their proofs after some preliminary lemmas. In Section
4
, we proceed to the partial extension of the results in case of generalized moments of randomly weighted sums. In Section
5
, we study the max-sum equivalence of randomly weighted stopped sums under some more restrictive conditions. It is worth mentioning that in sections
3
and
5
, the asymptotic estimates for the ruin probability of finite and random (finite) time in the discrete-time risk model are given. Meanwhile, the asymptotic estimates of generalized moments, from Section
4
, permit direct applications to risk measures, such as expected shortfall and marginal expected shortfall.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

Throughout the paper, we consider the real-valued r.v.s
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
with the corresponding distributions
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
as primary r.v.s., while

\Theta_{1},\dots\Theta_{n}
, with corresponding distributions
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}
, are random weights, which are non-negative non-degenerate at zero r.v.s.

Further, we denote by
\overline{F}_{i}:=1-F_{i}
the distribution tail, hence
\overline{F}_{i}(x)=\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]
, and assume that
\overline{F}_{i}(x)>0
for any
x\geq 0
and
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, although this is not necessarily true for distributions
\overline{G}_{i}(x)
. For two positive functions
f(x)
and
g(x)
, the asymptotic relation
f(x)=o[g(x)]
, as
x\to\infty
, denotes the convergence

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=0\,,

while the asymptotic relation
f(x)=O[g(x)]
, as
x\to\infty
, means

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}<\infty,

and the asymptotic relation
f(x)\asymp g(x)
, as
x\to\infty
means that hold both
f(x)=O[g(x)]
and

g(x)=O[f(x)]
. Further we write
f(x)\gtrsim g(x)
or
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
if it holds that

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\geq 1\,,\;\;\text{or }\;\;\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\leq 1\,,

respectively. For a real number
x
, we denote
x^{+}:=\max\{x,0\}

and
x^{-}:=-\min\{x,0\}
. For two real numbers
x
and
y
we denote
x\vee y:=\max\{x,\,y\}
and
x\wedge y:=\min\{x,\,y\}
. For some event
A
, the symbol
{\bf 1}_{A}
depicts the indicator function of
A
.

2.2.
Heavy tailed distributions

Next, we consider some heavy-tailed distribution classes. We say that a distribution
F
is heavy-tailed, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{H}
, if

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\varepsilon\,y}F(dy)=\infty,

for any
\varepsilon>0
. We say that a distribution
F
has a long tail, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{L}
, if for any (or equivalently, for some)
a>0
it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}(x-a)}{\overline{F}(x)}=1.

Next distribution class represents a popular subclass of heavy-tailed distributions, with several applications in risk theory, financial mathematics, branching processes and queuing theory, see for example
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
26
]
. We say that a distribution
F
with support
[0,\,\infty)
belongs to the subexponential class of distributions, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{S}
if for any (or equivalently, for some) integer
n\geq 2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F^{*n}}(x)}{\overline{F}(x)}=n\,,

where
F^{*n}
depicts the
n
-th order convolution power of
F
. Furthermore, for some
F
with support the whole real axis
{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that
F\in\mathcal{S}
if
F^{+}\in\mathcal{S}
, where
F^{+}(x):=F(x)\,{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)
, see for example
[
16
, Ch. 3.2]
.

Another known class of heavy-tailed distributions is the class of dominatedly varying distributions, introduced in
[
15
]
. We say that a distribution
F
has dominated variation, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{D}
, if for any (or equivalently, for some)
b\in(0,\,1)
it holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}(b\,x)}{\overline{F}(x)}<\infty\,.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{D}\not\subseteq\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{S}\not\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\neq\varnothing
, see for example
[
19
]
.

Another class of heavy-tailed distributions is the class of consistently varying distributions. We say that a distribution
F
is consistently varying, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{C}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{v\uparrow 1}\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}(v\,x)}{\overline{F}(x)}=1.

One even smaller class, with high popularity in applied probability, is the class
\mathcal{R}
of the regularly varying distributions. We say that the distribution
F
is regularly varying, with some index
0<\alpha<\infty
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}(b\,x)}{\overline{F}(x)}=b^{-\alpha}\,,

for any
b>0
.
It is well-known that

\mathcal{R}:=\bigcup_{0<\alpha<\infty}\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{H},

see for example
[
16
]
and
[
32
]
.

Now, we present the Matuszewska indexes and the
L
-indexes. For any distribution
F
, with condition
\overline{F}(x)>0
for all
x>0
and for any
v>1
, we define

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{*}(v):=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}(v\,x)}{\overline{F}(x)}\,,\qquad\overline{F}^{*}(v):=\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\overline{F}(v\,x)}{\overline{F}(x)}.

Next, we bring up the upper and lower Matuszewska indexes, introduced in
[
38
]
:

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}:=\inf\left\{-\dfrac{\ln\overline{F}_{*}(v)}{\ln v}\;:\;v>1\right\}=-\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\ln\overline{F}_{*}(y)}{\ln y}\,,

\displaystyle\beta_{F}:=\sup\left\{-\dfrac{\ln\overline{F}^{*}(v)}{\ln v}\;:\;v>1\right\}=-\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\ln\overline{F}^{*}(y)}{\ln y}\,.

It holds that
0\leq\beta_{F}\leq\alpha_{F}\leq\infty
. We have the following equivalence:
F\in\mathcal{D}
if and only if
\alpha_{F}<\infty
.

Two other popular indexes are the
L
-indexes, defined for distributions
F
, with condition
\overline{F}(x)>0,\,x>0
. In this case, we define

\displaystyle L_{F}:=\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\overline{F}_{*}(v)\,,\quad L^{F}:=\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\overline{F}^{*}(v)\,,

(2.1)

whence from relation (
2.1
) we find out
0\leq L_{F}\leq L^{F}\leq 1
. Furthermore, the inclusion
F\in\mathcal{D}
is equivalent to the inequality
L_{F}>0
, and the inclusion
F\in\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to the equality
L_{F}=1
, see for example,
[
32
, Sec. 2.4]
. Several applications of index
L_{F}
can be found, e.g., in
[
9
]
and
[
47
]
.

2.3.
Dependence modeling

Now we introduce two basic dependence structures, used to describe the dependence among the primary r.v.s
X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}
, containing the independence as a special case.

\bullet

The real r.v.s
X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{n}
, with distributions
F_{1},\,\ldots,\,F_{n}
respectively, are said to be pairwise quasi-asymptotically independent, symbolically
pQAI
, if for any pair
i,\,j
with
i\neq j
it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{i}^{+}>x\,,\;X_{j}^{+}>x]}{\overline{F}_{i}(x)+\overline{F}_{j}(x)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{i}^{-}>x\,,\;X_{j}>x]}{\overline{F}_{i}(x)+\overline{F}_{j}(x)}=0\,,

or equivalently
\lim_{x\to\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[|X_{i}|\wedge X_{j}>x\;\big|\;X_{i}\vee X_{j}>x\right]=0
.

\bullet

We say that
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are pairwise tail asymptotically independent, symbolically
pTAI
, if for any pair
i,\,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
i\neq j
it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{i}\wedge x_{j}\to\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[|X_{i}|>x_{i}\;\big|\;X_{j}>x_{j}\right]=0\,.

These dependence structures were introduced in
[
8
]
and
[
18
]
respectively. When there are only two random variables, we simply write
QAI
and
TAI
. These dependence structures were studied in several papers, for example,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
33
]
and
[
51
]
.

The following known result contains two parts. The first one was initially established for non-weighted random sums in Th. 3.1 of
[
18
]
and later for weighted sums with random weights bounded from above, in Th. 1 of
[
45
]
. The second part can be found in
[
8
, Th. 3.1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
Z_{1},\,\ldots,\,Z_{n}
be real-valued r.v.s with distributions
H_{1},\,\ldots,\,H_{n}
respectively.

(1)

If
H_{i}\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
and
Z_{1},\,\ldots,\,Z_{n}
are
pTAI
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}>x\right]\sim\mathbf{P}\left[\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}>x\right]\sim\mathbf{P}\left[\bigvee_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}Z_{i}>x\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}[Z_{i}>x]\,,

as
x\to\infty
.

(2)

If
H_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
and
Z_{1},\,\ldots,\,Z_{n}
are
pQAI
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}>x\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}[Z_{i}>x]\,,

as
x\to\infty
.

In this paper, we generalize Proposition
2.1
, using random weights that have a specific dependence structure with the primary random variables and under a new dependence structure. The main results of this article, both directly related to the asymptotics of a fixed number of randomly weighted sums and to the application of the resulting asymptotic formulas, are formulated in theorems
3.1
,
3.2
,
4.1
and
5.1
. Several lemmas are used in the proofs, which, in our opinion, are also useful in their own way.

3.
Results on randomly weighted sums

This section examines the asymptotic behavior of randomly weighted sums under
pTAI
and
pQAI
dependence structures for the primary r.v.s and simultaneously under some dependence structure among the random weights and primary r.v.s.
We present now the first assumption, which is needed further in the paper.

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be the primary r.v.s, and let
\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{n}
be the random weights.
Suppose that there exist functions
b_{i}:\;[0,\,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\,\infty),\,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,  such that
b_{i}(x)\rightarrow\infty,\ \ \lim_{x\to\infty}b_{i}(x)/x=0
, and

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{i}[b_{i}(x_{i})]:=\mathbf{P}\left[\Theta_{i}>b_{i}(x_{i})\right]=o\left(\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x_{i}\,,\;\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}>x_{j}]\right)\,,

(3.1)

as
x_{i}\wedge x_{j}\rightarrow\infty
, for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
i,j
be two indices
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. If r.v.s
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
have supports with infinite right endpoints, then it is easy to see that (
3.1
) holds in the case where
G_{i}
or
G_{j}
have upper bounded support. In addition, we observe that relation (
3.1
) implies that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\overline{G}_{i}[b_{i}(x_{i})]}{\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x_{i}]}\leq\dfrac{\overline{G}_{i}[b_{i}(x_{i})]}{\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x_{i}\,,\;\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}>x_{j}]}\rightarrow 0\,,

as
x_{i}\wedge x_{j}\rightarrow\infty
. Hence, Assumption
3.1
implies that

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{i}[b_{i}(x_{i})]=o(\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x_{i}])\,,

as
x_{i}\rightarrow\infty
. In combination with properties of functions
b_{i}
and
[
42
, Lemma 3.2]
we find

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{i}(c_{i}\,x_{i})=o\left(\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x_{i}]\right)\,,

(3.2)

as
x_{i}\rightarrow\infty
, respectively for any constants
c_{i}>0
. Note that relation (
3.2
) can be useful in the study of closure properties with respect to convolution product; see, for example,
[
27
]
and
[
42
]
.

Let us now render a new dependence structure that plays a crucial role in modeling interdependence.

Assumption 3.2
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be the primary r.v.s, and let
\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{n}
be the random weights. We assume that there exists some measurable functions
{g_{ij}:[0,\,\infty)^{2}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)}
,
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[|X_{i}|>x_{i}\,,\;X_{j}>x_{j}\;\big|\;\Theta_{i}=\theta_{i}\,,\;\Theta_{j}=\theta_{j}]\sim g_{ij}(\theta_{i},\,\theta_{j})\,\mathbf{P}[|X_{i}|>x_{i}\,,\;X_{j}>x_{j}]\,,

(3.3)

as
x_{i}\wedge x_{j}\rightarrow\infty
, with
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, uniformly for any
(\theta_{i},\,\theta_{j})\in s_{\Theta_{i}}\times s_{\Theta_{j}}
, where
s_{\Theta_{i}}
represents the support of
\Theta_{i}
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, i.e.

\displaystyle s_{\Theta_{i}}:=\big\{\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;\mathbf{P}(\Theta_{i}\in[\theta_{i}-\delta,\theta_{i}+\delta])>0\ {\rm for\ any\ }\delta>0\big\}\,.

In this assumption, the uniformity is understood in the sense that

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{i}\wedge x_{j})\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{(\theta_{i},\,\theta_{j})\in(s_{\Theta_{i}},\,s_{\Theta_{j}})}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[|X_{i}|>x_{i}\,,\;X_{j}>x_{j}\;\big|\;\Theta_{i}=\theta_{i}\,,\;\Theta_{j}=\theta_{j}\right]}{g_{ij}(\theta_{i},\,\theta_{j})\,\mathbf{P}[|X_{i}|>x_{i}\,,\;X_{j}>x_{j}]}-1\right|=0,

where
1\leq i\neq j\leq 2
, and
the conditional probability in (
3.3
) is defined by the equality

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\,\frac{\mathbf{P}\left[|X_{i}|>x_{i}\,,\;X_{j}>x_{j},\,\Theta_{i}\in[\theta_{i}-\delta,\theta_{i}+\delta],\Theta_{j}\in[\theta_{j}-\delta,\theta_{j}+\delta]\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[\Theta_{i}\in[\theta_{i}-\delta,\theta_{i}+\delta],\Theta_{j}\in[\theta_{j}-\delta,\theta_{j}+\delta]\right]}\,.

Remark 3.2
.

We observe that Assumption
3.2
implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[g_{ij}(\Theta_{i},\,\Theta_{j})]=1\,,

(3.4)

for any pair
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
.

Proof.

Let us denote temporally
{\bf\Theta}=(\Theta_{i},\Theta_{j})
. By Assumption
3.2
, via integration on the left side of (
3.3
), we find

\displaystyle\int_{[0,\infty)}\int_{[0,\infty)}{\mathbf{P}[|X_{i}|>x_{i},\;X_{j}>x_{j}\;\big|\;{\bf\Theta}=(\theta_{i},\,\theta_{j})]}\mathbf{P}[{\bf\Theta}\in(d\theta_{i},\,d\theta_{j})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbf{P}[|X_{i}|>x_{i},\;X_{j}>x_{j}]\,,

and through integration on the right side of (
3.3
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[|X_{i}|>x_{i},\;X_{j}>x_{j}]\int_{[0,\infty)}\int_{[0,\infty)}{g_{ij}(\theta_{i},\,\theta_{j})}\mathbf{P}[{\bf\Theta}\in(d\theta_{i},\,d\theta_{j})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbf{P}[|X_{i}|>x_{i},\;X_{j}>x_{j}]\,\mathbf{E}\left[g_{ij}({\bf\Theta})\right]\,.

Since the convergence is uniform in
(\theta_{1},\,\theta_{2})
, we obtain the relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[|X_{i}|>x_{i},\;X_{j}>x_{j}]\sim\mathbf{P}[X_{i}|>x_{i},\;X_{j}>x_{j}]\,\mathbf{E}\left[g_{ij}({\bf\Theta})\right]\,,

as
x_{i}\wedge x_{j}\rightarrow\infty
, which implies
\mathbf{E}\left[g_{ij}({\bf\Theta})\right]=1
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We observe that in the case of non-negative primary r.v.s
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, the dependence structure described in Assumption
3.2
is a slight generalization of Condition 2 in
[
23
]
. We also observe that the dependence structure in Assumption
3.2
allows for arbitrary dependence among the components of
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
and among the components of
\{\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{n}\}
. However, it includes some form of asymptotic independence between
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
and
\{\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{n}\}
. Further results on asymptotic idependence can be found in
[
20
]
and
[
39
]
.

In the following lemma, we present a result that holds for any pair
(i,\,j)
with
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, but for the sake of simplicity, we formulate it with indexes
1,\,2
only.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2}
be primary r.v.s and let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
be the random weights satisfying Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
for
n=2
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{1}|X_{1}|>x_{1}\,,\;\Theta_{2}\,X_{2}>x_{2}]

(3.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\sim\int_{[0,\infty)}\int_{[0,\infty)}g_{12}(\theta_{1},\,\theta_{2})\,\mathbf{P}\left[|X_{1}|>\dfrac{x_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\,,\;X_{2}>\dfrac{x_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{1}\in d\theta_{1}\,,\;\Theta_{2}\in d\theta_{2}]\,,

as
x_{1}\wedge x_{2}\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Using the functions
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
from Assumption
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[\Theta_{1}\,|X_{1}|>x_{1}\,,\;\Theta_{2}\,X_{2}>x_{2}]

Remark 3.4
.

Following the argument from
[
5
, pages 3331–3332]
we can derive that, in the case of Assumption
3.2
, there exists some constants
K_{ij}>0
,
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, such that
g_{ij}(\theta_{i},\,\theta_{j})\leq K_{ij}
for any
(\theta_{i},\,\theta_{j})\in(s_{\Theta_{i}},\,s_{\Theta_{j}})
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to consider the case

. Let us assume at moment the contrary, namely that for any
K<\infty
, there exists a vector
(\theta_{1}^{\circ},\,\theta_{2}^{\circ})\in(s_{\Theta_{1}},\,s_{\Theta_{2}})

Lemma 3.3
.

, either

4.
Generalized moments of randomly weighted sums

In this section, we focus on asymptotic bounds of the quantity

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\varphi\left(S_{n}^{\Theta}\right)\,{\bf 1}_{\left\{S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right\}}\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\varphi\left(S_{n}^{\Theta}\right)\;\big|\;S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right],

(4.1)

where
\varphi
is some function. The results present extensions of previous results and provide a continuation of Theorem
3.2
for the case of
pQAI
dependence structure for
F_{1},\,\ldots,\,F_{n}\in\mathcal{D}
. Further, we find asymptotic bounds for the distribution tail of
S_{n}^{\Theta}
, namely
\mathbf{P}\left[S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right]
, which comes as consequence of the main theorem and the observation that if in relation (
4.1
) we consider instead of
\varphi
the unit function, then we obtain the tail
\mathbf{P}\left[S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right]
. Although relation (
4.1
) has no direct application in risk theory, it is relevant to risk management. We note that if replace
\varphi
with the identical function, then relation (

) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[S_{n}^{\Theta}\,{\bf 1}_{\left\{S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right\}}\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[S_{n}^{\Theta}\;\big|\;S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right]\,,

where the expression

, is recognized as the Expected Shortfall, useful for the regulatory authorities to control the system risk. Similar results can be found in
[
22
]
and
[

]
.

.

(2)

If

are

, then

\displaystyle L_{F_{j}}\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}\,{\bf 1}_{\left\{\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}>x\right\}}\right]\lesssim\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}\,{\bf 1}_{\left\{S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right\}}\right]\lesssim\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}\,{\bf 1}_{\left\{\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}>x\right\}}\right]\,,

(1) From Assumptions
3.1
and

, taking into account Remark

(2) Similarly, with the only difference that we apply Proposition

Remark 4.2
.

The formulas in Corollary
4.1
provide bounds for the marginal expected shortfall. Indeed, from the assumptions of part (2) we find

\displaystyle L_{F_{j}}\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}\;\big|\;\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}>x\right]\,\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right]}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}\;\big|\;S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}\;\big|\;\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}>x\right]\,\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[S_{n}^{\Theta}>x\right]}\,,

as
x\to\infty
, while we have only upper bound in the case of
pQAI
. We notice that the marginal expected shortfall has bounds of the following form: the individual expected shortfall times the ratio of the tail of itself over the tail of the whole sum. The last can be considered as a contribution of the tail of the individual in the total tail. In the class
\mathcal{C}
, we find asymptotic equivalence relation for this risk measure.

5.
Randomly weighted and stopped sums

Now we examine the asymptotic behavior of the randomized form of the sum in (
1.1
), namely

\displaystyle S_{N}^{\Theta}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}\,,

(5.1)

where
N
represents a discrete random variable with values from
{\mathbb{N}}
. In particular, we can find the asymptotic relations for the non-weighted random sum
S_{N}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}
. For the studies of the asymptotic behavior of
S_{N}
and its distribution class, see for example
[
11
]
,
[
30
]
and
[
41
]
, among others. Besides the asymptotic behavior of
S_{N}
, one can investigate the randomly stopped maximum, as well the randomly stopped maximum of sums, whose definitions in the weighted case are as follows:

\displaystyle X_{(N)}^{\Theta}:=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\;N=0\\
\bigvee_{i=1}^{N}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i},&\text{if}\;N\geq 1\end{cases}\,,\qquad S_{(N)}^{\Theta}:=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\;N=0\\
\bigvee_{i=1}^{N}S_{i}^{\Theta},&\text{if}\;N\geq 1\end{cases}.

For a detailed survey on randomly stopped sums, maximums and maximums of sums, when the summands are heavy-tailed, in non-weighted case, we refer to
[
24
]
and
[
31
]
. The applications of randomly stopped sums can be found in risk theory, since usually a portfolio has unknown number of claims over a concrete time interval. Further, the randomly stopped sums help to find asymptotic estimations of the ruin probability, see for example
[
10
, Sec. 4]
. Inspired by the papers
[
12
]
,
[
40
]
and
[
50
]
, we consider a discrete-time risk model with the general weighted version of the randomly stopped sums. However, now we assume that the
\Theta_{1},\,\Theta_{2},\,\ldots
are identically distributed as well as the
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots
are.

The conditions for the summands of randomly weighted stopped sums are found in the following assumption.

Assumption 5.1
.

Let
\Theta,\,\Theta_{1},\,\Theta_{2},\,\ldots
be non-negative, identically distributed r.v.s with common distribution
G
and let
X,\,X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots
be identically distributed real-valued r.v.s with common distribution

is non-negative and non-degenerate at zero and it has support bounded from above, namely there exists an integer

such that
\mathbf{P}[N>r_{N}]=0
. Finally, we suppose that
N
is independent of
\Theta
and
X
.

In our assumptions we avoid to suppose independence among
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots
, and in spite the restriction of the bounded support of the random variable
N
and the identical distributions of the product
\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}
, we use
pTAI
and
pQAI
dependence structure for the
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots
and arbitrary dependence among the
\Theta_{1},\,\Theta_{2},\,\ldots
. It is worth to notice that, because of the identical distributions among

and among
\Theta_{1},\,\Theta_{2},\,\ldots
, as described in Assumption
5.1
, it holds the equality

for the functions in Assumption
3.1
, the equality

for any

for the functions in Assumption

and the equality

for any

.

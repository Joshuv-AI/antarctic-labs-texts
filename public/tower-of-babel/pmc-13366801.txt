MTRFU-Net: a lung nodule segmentation model based on improved U-Net architecture with spatial-frequency fusion
Authors: Yuting Wu, Bin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate pulmonary nodule segmentation is a key step for the early diagnosis of lung cancer, yet existing deep learning methods still have limitations in addressing challenges such as nodule heterogeneity, blurred boundaries, and multi-scale variations. To tackle these issues, this study proposes the MTRFU-Net model, a pulmonary nodule segmentation model based on an improved U-Net architecture integrating spatial-frequency features and multi-module collaboration, which adopts a three-tier progressive module collaboration strategy to achieve dynamic spatial-frequency fusion. The encoder of the model is built on ResNet50 and incorporates a Spatial-Frequency Fusion (SFF) module, enabling the parallel extraction and dynamic fusion of dual-domain features. The bottleneck layer combines a Transformer encoder with an optimized Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) module, effectively capturing long-range dependencies and multi-scale contextual information. For the decoder, residual connections are paired with a dynamically weighted scSE attention mechanism to enhance the response capability to critical features. Extensive experiments on the LIDC-IDRI dataset demonstrate that MTRFU-Net exhibits excellent performance in terms of the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC), mean Intersection over Union (mIoU). This research validates the effectiveness of frequency-domain information in pulmonary nodule segmentation tasks, providing valuable references for the development of robust clinically oriented segmentation models.Keywords: Lung nodule segmentation, U-Net, Fast fourier transform, Deep learning, Multi-scale features

Introduction

Lung cancer ranks among the malignant tumors with the highest incidence and mortality globally, and early diagnosis and timely intervention are pivotal for enhancing patient survival outcomes. Data from the World Health Organization (WHO) and international studies indicate that lung cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide [1]. However, most patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, missing the optimal treatment window. As an early imaging manifestation of lung cancer, accurate segmentation of pulmonary nodules in computed tomography (CT) images is a critical link in early lung cancer screening. Through segmentation, key features such as nodule size, shape, edges, and density can be clarified, providing an objective basis for subsequent benign-malignant differentiation. Nevertheless, manual segmentation of hundreds of CT image layers in clinical practice is not only time-consuming and labor-intensive but also susceptible to the influence of differences in physicians’ subjective experience, leading to increased risks of missed diagnosis or misdiagnosis—especially for small or poorly characterized nodules [2]. Therefore, developing an efficient and accurate automatic pulmonary nodule segmentation model holds urgent clinical value.

High-precision automatic pulmonary nodule segmentation remains a formidable challenge, primarily attributed to the substantial heterogeneity of pulmonary nodules and the inherent complexity of CT images. Pulmonary nodules exhibit significant size variability, ranging from micro-nodules smaller than 3 mm to larger nodules of several centimeters. Morphologically, they are highly irregular, presenting contours such as round, lobulated, and spiculated, with edges often blurred. In particular, ground-glass nodules (GGNs) have low contrast with surrounding lung tissue due to their low-density characteristics, making them a segmentation difficulty. Additionally, nodules are located in complex and variable positions, often adjacent to lung structures such as blood vessels, bronchi, or the pleura, increasing the difficulty of accurately distinguishing boundaries. At the same time, nodules account for an extremely low proportion of CT images, rendering them vulnerable to strong noise interference from background structures such as high-density surrounding blood vessels and bronchi. Coupled with the diversity of nodule density (solid, subsolid, ground-glass), feature extraction becomes extremely challenging [3]. Typical examples of these diverse pulmonary nodules from the dataset are illustrated in Fig. 1. Figure 1 presents pulmonary nodule images that include solid nodules with high density contrast and well-defined boundaries, ground-glass nodules (GGNs) characterized by extremely low contrast and blurred margins, as well as part-solid nodules containing mixed components. In addition, some nodules exhibit highly irregular morphologies with spiculated or lobulated margins. These characteristics require segmentation models to possess robust feature extraction capabilities, enabling them to simultaneously capture local fine edges, effectively establish long-range spatial dependencies, and flexibly adapt to the significant variations in nodule morphology and density.

However, existing segmentation technologies still have obvious shortcomings in addressing the above challenges. Traditional approaches, including threshold segmentation, region growing, clustering algorithms, and active contour models, have been extensively investigated for early pulmonary nodule segmentation tasks. For example, in 2003, Kostis et al. [4] developed a novel pulmonary nodule segmentation model based on volume growth characterization. In 2009, Moltz et al. [5] proposed a hybrid algorithm combining morphological processing and threshold methods for segmenting pleura-attached pulmonary nodules. In 2012, Netto et al. [6] applied the Growing Neural Gas (GNG) to algorithms for accurate separation of pulmonary nodule structures from other lung tissues. In 2018, Bruntha et al. [7] proposed an abnormal nodule detection method by fusing morphological operations with a selective region growing algorithm. These traditional methods rely on manually designed features and involve extensive manual intervention, resulting in low efficiency and limited generalization ability, making it difficult to effectively handle the complex morphological heterogeneity of nodules. Furthermore, when confronted with low-contrast nodules, edge delineation is problematic due to blurred gradient transitions.

Convolutional neural network (CNN)-based methods, particularly those represented by U-Net [8] and its variants, have made substantial advancements by fusing multi-scale features via an encoder-decoder architecture [9–12]. However, their core convolution operations are constrained by local receptive fields, making it difficult to model long-range spatial dependencies between nodules and surrounding blood vessels. Consequently, these methods exhibit insufficient adaptability to the scale variability and ambiguous boundary characteristics inherent in the target lesions [13]. Although the Transformer architecture can effectively capture global contextual dependencies and mitigate the limitations of CNNs, it suffers from weak modeling of local fine-grained features. Additionally, its inherent high computational complexity renders it less adaptable to small-sample training scenarios, which are prevalent in medical imaging [14]. To address this issue, researchers have turned to the fusion of the two paradigms: extracting features via CNNs first, then encoding and decoding through Transformers to obtain segmentation results—leading to the emergence of TransUnet [15]. Moreover, most existing methods primarily focus on spatial-domain feature processing [16], neglecting the rich global structural information and detailed texture information contained in the frequency domain [17, 18]. Notably, frequency-domain information can effectively reduce noise interference from background lung tissues.

To address the technical limitations of existing methods and the core challenges of pulmonary nodule segmentation, this study proposes MTRFU-Net, an improved U-Net architecture centered on the dynamic fusion of spatial and frequency domains. This model breaks through the bottleneck of traditional methods that rely solely on spatial-domain feature processing, achieving accurate pulmonary nodule segmentation through a three-tier progressive module collaboration strategy. Each module is forms an organic whole with complementary functions around the core principle of dual-domain fusion: the SFF module serves as the fundamental feature extraction unit, enabling the parallel extraction and dynamic fusion of local spatial features and global frequency-domain features, thereby laying a high-quality foundation for subsequent feature processing; the Trans_ASPP module, built upon the dual-domain fused features, captures multi-scale contextual information and models long-range spatial dependencies, compensating for the insufficiency in multi-scale feature mining and global correlation modeling after dual-domain fusion; the Res_scSE module performs refined screening on the fused multi-scale features, adaptively enhances the response of key features, and suppresses background interference to complete the final feature optimization and segmentation output. The core innovation of this study lies in the systematic integration of spatial-frequency dynamic fusion into the U-Net architecture for pulmonary nodule segmentation, which validates the effectiveness of frequency-domain information in capturing the heterogeneous and multi-scale features of pulmonary nodules.

Related works

This chapter reviews related work in areas highly relevant to the research content of this paper, including CNN-based segmentation, Transformer-based segmentation, multi-scale feature processing, attention mechanisms, and the application of Fourier transform in image segmentation.

CNN-based segmentation methods

With the rapid development of deep learning, it has gradually become the core technology in medical image segmentation. Among them, CNN-based models are the mainstream methods in medical image segmentation and have demonstrated excellent performance. The Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) [19] was one of the early semantic segmentation models; however, it exhibits low sensitivity to fine details, which hinders its application in medical image segmentation tasks. The U-Net network was proposed by Ronneberger et al. [8] in 2015. Owing to its unique encoder-decoder architecture, U-Net has been widely applied across various image processing domains. By incorporating skip connections, it enables the fusion of low-resolution contextual abstract features learned by the network with high-resolution raw feature information, thereby preserving more effective details and achieving favorable performance in medical imaging applications. However, for this structure, changes in original image resolution caused by upsampling and downsampling are likely to lead to the loss of some feature information during network training.

Due to its advantages of a simple structure and strong expandability, numerous optimized and improved networks have emerged. U-Net++ [20], based on U-Net, introduces deep supervision to optimize the model’s backpropagation capability, and its multi-scale skip connection design enhances multi-level feature fusion. U-Net3+ [21] is improved to full-scale skip connections on the basis of U-Net++. Nevertheless, there is still room for improvement when dealing with pulmonary nodule images with more complex boundary information. ResNet [22] effectively addresses the model degradation problem caused by excessive network depth through its shortcut connection characteristics, and has shown outstanding performance in image processing. The residual block structure can also be applied to the U-Net architecture. ResUNet [23]—i.e., introducing residual connections on the basis of U-Net—incorporates multiple residual connections in the encoder and decoder modules, enabling better extraction of low-frequency information, alleviating the gradient vanishing problem, and thus improving image segmentation accuracy. In addition, other studies have replaced the convolution blocks in the original network. For example, Hu et al. [24] proposed LeaNet, which introduces a module integrating dilated convolution and ECA (Efficient Channel Attention) to replace the convolution block, showing high applicability in small nodule segmentation tasks.Yuan et al. [25] proposed TBSFF-Unet, which redesigned skip connections to efficiently transfer multi-scale features and introduced a three-branch feature fusion module (TBSFF). Via a dynamic attention allocation mechanism, it adaptively fuses semantic information of different levels, addressing the problems of low efficiency of skip connections and insufficient multi-scale feature fusion in U-Net and its variants for medical image segmentation. The model ensures segmentation accuracy while achieving model lightweighting. Jin et al. [26] proposed the PESF framework, which addresses the problem of insufficient data volume faced by CNN-based methods in few-shot medical segmentation tasks from the perspective of pseudo-label generation and data augmentation. This framework leverages a pretrained model to generate pseudo-labels and combines progressive sampling based on curriculum learning with Gaussian perturbations, achieving excellent performance on multiple datasets.

Transformer-based segmentation methods

The Transformer [27] was initially proposed for natural language processing (NLP) due to its ability to process sequences via a self-attention mechanism [28]. Subsequently, it was extended to domains such as computer vision [29–31]. The Vision Transformer (ViT) model, proposed by Dosovitskiy et al. [32] in 2020, has achieved excellent performance in multiple image processing-related tasks. The core idea of ViT is to convert images into token sequences for processing, which has had a profound impact on the field of computer vision. As its key component, the self-attention mechanism is used to capture dependencies between local and global data blocks. Improvements based on Transformer are currently a research hotspot in computer vision [33, 34]. Traditional CNNs are limited by their receptive fields, resulting in restricted ability to learn global context. Transformers can extract global features and establish long-range semantic dependencies through their unique self-attention mechanism.

Therefore, introducing a Transformer structure is a viable solution to enable CNN architectures to capture the geometric structure information present in medical data. Accordingly, Chen et al. [15] proposed the TransUNet model, which combines the ViT model with the U-Net architecture to compensate for the shortcomings of traditional architectures in information capture. It is implemented by stacking Transformer modules after the encoder part of the U-Net network, making the model more prone to overfitting and still slightly insufficient for the segmentation of small datasets such as pulmonary nodule images. Subsequently, Cao et al. [35] combined the Swin-Transformer module [36] with the U-Net framework and proposed the Swin-UNet network to further optimize segmentation results. Researchers have conducted in-depth investigations into combining Transformer and CNN for segmentation tasks. In 2024, Fu et al. [37] designed the HmsU-Net network, which introduced multi-scale feature fusion and a cross-attention mechanism on the basis of combining CNN and Transformer to address inconsistent network feature learning. TransResUNet [38] introduces residual connection blocks into the TransUNet architecture and combines them with multi-scale features to achieve performance improvement. ParaUnet [39] adopts a multi-scale feature extraction module that focuses more on capturing multi-scale features, thereby achieving excellent performance when dealing with objects such as small nodules that require detailed feature capture.

Attention mechanism

Attention mechanism modules are extensively utilized in deep learning-based image segmentation. They can adaptively adjust channel weights to emphasize regions with critical feature information while suppressing irrelevant regions [40–42]. Since the pulmonary nodule region accounts for a small proportion of the image, the introduction of an attention mechanism module enables the network to better perform segmentation tasks for small targets. To allow the network to automatically focus on and implicitly learn the salient features of images during training, Oktay et al. [43] designed the Attention Gate (AG) method and introduced it into the U-Net architecture, proposing Attention U-Net, which improved model accuracy. Woo et al. [40] proposed the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), which combines channel attention and spatial attention, and can strengthen the model’s attention to feature details from two dimensions. The Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) [44] module can automatically calculate and learn the weights between multiple feature channels, and re-adjust the network’s feature response capability. Xiong et al. [45] implemented a study on applying the SE module to the encoder part of U-Net. The scSE [46] module performs squeeze-and-excitation on both spatial and channel attention, enabling the network to better learn feature maps in spatial and channel dimensions respectively. The network model designed by Dong et al. [47] combines ResNeXt with U-Net++ and introduces the scSE module for pulmonary nodule segmentation tasks, showing good feature extraction capabilities. Fan et al. [48] proposed an edge attention mechanism to strengthen the network’s ability to constrain edge information in lung feature maps. Ji et al. [10] proposed the ResDSda_U-Net model, which combines the parameter-free attention module SimAM with the novel Convolutional Channel Attention (CCA) and Convolutional Spatial Attention (CSA) modules they designed to optimize the network’s global pixel attention capability. However, the accuracy of nodule contour delineation needs to be improved.

Multi-scale feature modules

Currently, multi-scale feature information is a core research focus in deep learning-based image processing. The contextual information embedded in multi-scale features can effectively enhance the performance of semantic segmentation tasks, such as pulmonary nodule segmentation [21, 49]. Among them, the Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) [50] module adopts atrous convolution technology, which can expand the receptive field without adding additional parameters, enabling the capture and learning of multi-scale feature information in images [51, 52]. In 2021, Pei et al. [49] proposed MPS-Net, which adopts a multi-scale feature extraction structure to improve the segmentation performance of lesion regions of different sizes. In 2023, Zhou et al. [53] proposed MCFA-Unet, which designed a multi-scale cascaded feature attention module combining multi-path convolution and dual attention mechanisms to achieve the integration of multi-scale feature information. In 2024, Li et al. [54] proposed CMPF-UNet. On the basis of ConvNeXt, this network introduces scale-aware pyramid fusion and proposes the RASPP module that uses residual atrous convolution to replace the original atrous convolution for multi-scale information fusion.

Application of Fourier transform in medical image segmentation

The deep learning models discussed earlier have achieved good results in medical image processing and other image processing fields. Most of the proposed models focus on the spatial domain; however, the spatial domain suffers from problems such as low modeling efficiency and difficulty in linearizing the complexity of models like ViT [55]. The introduction of image frequency-domain processing technology can mitigate these issues arising from the sole use of spatial-domain processing. The Fourier transform is a frequency-domain analytical tool that converts spatial or temporal information into spatial frequency or temporal frequency information. It plays a crucial role in image processing, and a growing number of scholars have begun to explore the feasibility of combining spectral transform with deep learning convolutional neural networks [56]. In computer vision applications, Fourier transform technology helps improve model robustness and generalization ability. Due to its characteristics of easy implementation and clear physical interpretability, it can perform fusion representation of the frequency domain and spatial domain, combining the advantages of both to enhance model performance. Fast Fourier Convolution (FFC) is a novel convolution operator that combines FFT with CNN. This operator possesses multi-scale fusion properties and can expand the model’s receptive field. Rao et al. [57] applied FFT to image classification tasks, demonstrating the high adaptability of frequency-domain transformation to neural networks. Patro et al. [58] further applied Fourier transform to the Transformer model, showing the effectiveness of combining frequency-domain transformation with attention mechanisms. Qin et al. [59] addressed the core problem that using scalars to represent channels in channel attention mechanisms easily leads to massive information loss. They transformed the channel representation problem into a feature compression problem in the frequency domain and proposed FcaNet based on multi-spectral channel attention. This method possesses the advantage of being lightweight and has achieved excellent performance in visual tasks such as image classification and object detection. Chu et al. [60] addressed the limitations of models in representing high-frequency components and fine-grained details by adopting frequency dual-component masking (including high-frequency masking and low-frequency perturbation) combined with embedding consistency learning, which encourages the encoder to effectively capture high-frequency information. The method achieved significant improvements in fine-grained tasks such as retina vessel segmentation and lung tumor detection, validating its high-frequency representation capability and generalizability.Zhang et al. [61] proposed FsaNet, a model designed to focus solely on low-frequency components for its frequency self-attention mechanism. It simplifies the structure through linear approximation of Softmax (LinSoftmax) while maintaining mathematical equivalence with spatial self-attention mechanisms. With no additional parameters required, it can be flexibly embedded into various model architectures, balancing both efficiency and accuracy.

In recent years, the low-frequency and high-frequency feature information contained in the frequency domain has attracted the attention of many researchers.

Studies on the fusion of frequency-domain transformation and neural networks and their application in image segmentation tasks have also begun to increase [62]. However, there are currently few studies applying them to pulmonary nodule image segmentation. Therefore, this paper attempts to introduce it into nodule segmentation tasks to overcome the receptive field limitations of traditional convolution in pulmonary nodule segmentation and optimize the network’s ability to capture complex and irregular edge features of pulmonary nodules.

The model proposed in this paper is also based on the U-Net architecture, combined with a residual network and integrated with the newly designed Spatial-Frequency Fusion (SFF) module, Trans_ASPP module, and Res_scSE module, named MTRFU-Net. The combination of Fourier transform-based frequency-domain information processing, attention mechanism-based spatial-domain processing, multi-scale feature fusion, and squeeze-and-excitation attention strengthens the model’s edge feature extraction ability, enhances the learning ability of nodule images of different scales, and improves model segmentation accuracy.

In this section, we first introduce the overall architecture of the proposed MTRFU-Net, followed by detailed descriptions of several key modules.

Encoder architecture

The encoder of the U-shaped network is primarily responsible for extracting multi-level features of pulmonary nodule images [63]. We use ResNet50 as the encoder’s backbone network. The residual connection mechanism of ResNet is its most important innovation: establishing a direct addition path between the output of the previous layer and the input of the next layer, significantly improving network representation capability and effectively addressing a phenomenon in traditional CNNs—network degradation with increasing depth.

In traditional deep learning CNNs, as the number of layers increases, problems such as gradient vanishing, gradient explosion, and network degradation are likely to occur during training, leading to difficulties in model optimization and performance. This structural design ensures that the output not only contains the learned transformed features but also completely retains the original input features, thereby effectively alleviating the potential loss of feature information due to continuous convolution operations in deep networks.

To make up for the locality limitation of traditional convolution in spatial-domain feature extraction, the SFF module is innovatively integrated between the residual blocks of ResNet50 to construct a dual-domain feature extraction structure for the encoder, which converts spatial feature information into frequency domain for parallel processing.

SFF module

We propose the SpatialFrequency Fusion (SFF) module to overcome the limitations of traditional convolutional neural networks that rely primarily on spatialdomain information processing. For traditional convolution operations, all inputs and outputs have their feature maps located in the spatial domain. Constrained by the local receptive field of the spatial domain, each pixel either covers only a small local area through convolution or contains only information related to itself. In contrast, in the frequency domain, a single pixel can contain global information of the entire feature map. Therefore, the introduction of frequency information can partially address the limitations of traditional convolution, and the fusion of frequency representation with convolutional neural networks also holds considerable application potential.

However, FFT struggles to capture non-stationary frequency feature information and is easily affected by diversity of frequency-domain signals in CT images. To compensate for this deficiency and better fully fuse spatial-frequency domain information, channel attention and spatial attention mechanisms are added to enhance the core attributes of the spatial and channel dimensions respectively, improving the information fusion degree between the spatial and frequency domains.

Therefore, we design a novel SFF module. The core idea is to overcome the limitations of traditional convolutional neural networks that rely primarily on spatial-domain information processing, instead processing frequency-domain and spatial-domain feature information simultaneously through a dual-path parallel structure. Based on a dynamic fusion mechanism, it realizes adaptive integration of multi-scale features. It offers significant advantages in distinguishing pulmonary nodules from surrounding lung tissues—especially when processing images with complex or blurred edge details. The SFF module is mainly composed of three parts: a spatial-domain processing branch, a frequency-domain processing branch, and a dual-domain feature fusion mechanism. The overall architecture adopts a residual connection design to retain original information and promote feature enhancement by reusing feature information, which can alleviate the impact of problems such as gradient vanishing caused by module stacking to a certain extent. The module first inputs features into the frequency-domain and spatial-domain processing branches for parallel processing.

In the frequency-domain branch: Input features are mapped to the frequency-domain space via two-dimensional FFT. A channel attention mechanism is then applied to screen and enhance critical frequency information in this new representation space. Subsequently, convolution filtering and ModReLU activation are performed—convolution filtering involves weighting the real and imaginary parts of complex feature signals in the frequency domain using a convolution kernel. Since the result of FFT is a complex number, the traditional ReLU activation function is ineffective. Therefore, ModReLU, which is particularly suitable for the complex domain, is selected as the activation function for non-linear transformation, combining with ReLU by modifying the phase angle to enhance frequency-domain representation capability. Finally, the processed complex results are mapped back to the spatial domain via inverse Fourier transform.

In the spatial-domain branch: Local spatial structural information is enhanced by introducing a spatial attention mechanism. The module first passes through a double convolution layer (comprising two 3 × 3 convolution layers, a BatchNorm layer, and a ReLU layer). The intermediate features obtained are used to generate feature descriptors via channel-wise average pooling and maximum pooling, which are then compressed through a 7 × 7 convolution layer to generate a spatial attention map. The feature maps from the two pooling results are concatenated in the channel dimension, followed by convolution to generate a spatial attention map, which is then element-wise multiplied with the original input features. Finally, after processing through another double convolution layer, the result is added to the previous intermediate features, and enhanced spatial-domain features are obtained via ReLU activation and residual connection.

The feature maps processed by the dual-path branches (frequency domain and spatial domain) are concatenated in the channel dimension and input into a 3 × 3 gated convolution layer to generate two weights, which are used to perform weighted multiplication on the frequency-domain and spatial-domain features respectively, realizing dynamic adaptive fusion of dual-path features. Finally, the fused feature tensor is added to the original input via residual connection to enhance the output feature representation capability.

Trans_aspp

The ASPP module employs atrous convolution with different dilation rates to expand the receptive field and extract multi-scale feature information, thereby fully utilizing nodule boundary information contained in local context and positioning information contained in global context.To more accurately capture the topological relationships of object contours, we introduce a Transformer encoder module and combine it with ASPP.

The module architecture is shown in the Fig. 5. This module aims to balance local detail perception and long-range dependency perception, thereby significantly improving the model’s feature extraction and expression capabilities.

First, features from the encoder are used as input, and parallel 33 atrous convolution branches are employed to capture local features of different receptive fields for multi-scale feature extraction. Unlike the original ASPP module, we introduce a dynamic allocation mechanism for the output channel count of each parallel convolution branch. Originally, the output channel count of each branch was fixed and uniform; after improvement, the total channel count can be dynamically designed via an expansion factor and evenly allocated to each branch, optimizing the allocation of computing resources while ensuring feature richness. Each branch retains the three-layer structure of the original ASPPConv (atrous convolution + BN + ReLU), and the dilation rate of each branch adopts an exponential growth form, effectively addressing the insufficient small-scale feature capture of the original ASPP.

In the global path: We retain the global average pooling branch design in the original ASPP structure, introducing a unified 11 convolution, BatchNorm, and ReLU activation function for feature compression and normalization, followed by bilinear interpolation to restore the original spatial resolution. This structure helps introduce cross-regional contextual information and effectively improve the model’s ability to perceive overall structure. We remove the Dropout mechanism in the original ASPP module to reduce feature loss. The features are then input into a Transformer encoder unit for spatial relationship enhancement, capturing long-range dependencies via the self-attention mechanism, and reorganizing the sequence back to the original spatial structure after processing.

In the output stage: The feature dimension is mapped to the target output channel count via 11 convolution, and an intelligent residual connection mechanism is adopted: if the input and output channels are consistent, an identity mapping is applied; if not, the channels are adjusted via 11 convolution before addition. This residual structure retains original input information, optimizes gradient propagation efficiency, and reduces information loss during training.

The dilation rate parameters of this module adopt an exponential growth strategy, and several studies have also verified the effectiveness of this strategy [65, 66]. We set the preset fixed values of 1, 2, 4, and 8 as the dilation rates. For a 33 atrous convolution, its effective receptive field is calculated by the formula ; the receptive field sizes are 3, 5, 9, and 17 pixels corresponding to the dilation rates of 1, 2, 4, and 8, respectively. This strategy is intended to enable the receptive field to grow multiplicatively, which allows covering a larger scale range with fewer branches. It is applicable to tasks such as pulmonary nodule segmentation where the targets present a nonlinear size distribution, and also facilitates the Transformer encoder in fusing cross-scale information.

Decoder

The decoder architecture adopts a hierarchical progressive upsampling and feature fusion strategy. The basic building block is the decoder block, and the entire decoder is constructed by stacking multiple such blocks. Deep, low-resolution feature maps are first passed to the first decoder block; after upsampling and feature fusion, higher-resolution feature maps are output. This output serves as the input to the next-stage decoder block, and the upsampling and fusion process is repeated. This progressive design gradually restores the spatial resolution of the feature map while gradually integrating fine-grained information obtained from different encoder levels. After processing by the last decoder block, the final segmentation prediction result is obtained via a 1 × 1 convolution layer.

Each decoder block is responsible for upsampling deep, low-resolution feature maps from the encoder and bottleneck layer to a higher resolution, and includes a skip connection with the corresponding encoder stage for feature fusion. This design ensures effective combination of low-level detailed information and high-level semantic information, providing comprehensive feature support for the final high-resolution output. Specifically, the interior of the decoder block first performs upsampling via bilinear interpolation, then concatenates the upsampled features with the corresponding encoder layer features, and finally inputs them into a feature fusion part composed of convolution layers and normalization layers to integrate multi-scale information.

In the feature fusion process, a residual network structure is used, and the fused features are further processed via two consecutive convolution layers and normalization layers. This design not only enhances network expression capability but also accelerates gradient propagation via skip connections, making the network more stable during training. In addition, to further enhance feature expression capability, the compressed excitation-spatial channel attention (scSE) module is introduced into the decoder block to adaptively adjust the channel and spatial information of features.The Res_scSE module architecture is shown in the Fig. 6.

It shows the Res_scSE architecture diagram, where the content in the dotted box is the specific architecture of Weight_Generator, and w1 and w2 are the weights generated after this part

The scSE module is a fusion structure of Squeeze-and-Excitation Channel Attention (cSE) and Squeeze-and-Excitation Spatial Attention (sSE), which we have dynamically improved. The sSE module compresses input features into a single-channel attention map via 1 × 1 convolution, normalizes it with sigmoid, and multiplies it element-wise with the original features to enhance the response of key spatial regions. The cSE module obtains channel statistical information via global average pooling, realizes compression and excitation of the channel dimension via convolution layers, and generates channel-level attention weights. The original scSE module adopts a simple additive fusion form to sum the outputs of the two attentions. On this basis, we introduce a dynamic weight generation mechanism: global average pooling is used to obtain channel statistical information of each input, and two attention weight coefficients are generated via a two-layer MLP and Softmax. Dynamic adjustment for each input sample via these two coefficients enables adaptive allocation of the contribution ratio of channel and spatial attention according to specific feature distributions. It allows the model to flexibly focus on more effective attention modes for different samples or different feature regions, improving the adaptive ability of the attention mechanism. The output feature map size of the module remains consistent with the original size.

Experiments

Dataset

To validate the performance of the proposed model, a public dataset—the LIDC-IDRI dataset [67]—was utilized. This dataset comprises 1018 research cases, each accompanied by corresponding CT scan images and XML annotation files. The annotations in the XML files were completed by 1–4 physicians with extensive experience in chest radiological examinations. To verify the actual effectiveness of the model, we screened the dataset: to ensure annotation accuracy, we selected data with 3–4 physicians annotating the nodules. Moreover, since nodules ≥ 3 mm are easier to observe with clearer contours (while most nodules < 3 mm are benign), the screened dataset includes nodules with 3–4 annotators and a diameter > 3 mm. Finally, a total of 6090 pulmonary nodule images were extracted, randomly divided into a training set, validation set, and test set at an 8:1:1 ratio. To facilitate model learning of nodule positions, images and labels were cropped centered on the nodule at a resolution of 64 × 64 pixels. To improve model generalization ability while avoiding excessive modification of target images, data augmentation techniques were introduced: only horizontal flipping, vertical flipping, and pixel value normalization were applied to the training set; no data augmentation was used for the validation set and test set.

Implementation details

All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 GPU with 8GB of memory. The proposed model was implemented using the PyTorch deep learning framework, with a training environment consisting of Python 3.11, Conda 24.1, and PyTorch 1.8. During the training process of the model, the pretrained weights of ResNet50 were adopted, and fixed random seeds were set. The loss function was constructed by fusing Dice loss and standard binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss, with respective weight coefficients assigned. BCE loss mitigates the large fluctuations observed during training when using Dice loss alone, and the weight coefficients adjust loss priority to avoid bias caused by overemphasis on a single loss. The Dice loss function in this study adopts an improved variant of Soft Dice. This is because Soft Dice is based on the calculation of continuous probability values, and the entire process involves smooth algebraic operations, allowing gradients to be smoothly propagated from the loss to the network weights and enabling end-to-end optimization. The BCE function is implemented by the built-in function of the PyTorch framework, without performing weighting processing on pixel-level terms. This is due to the fact that Dice loss inherently has a certain robustness to class imbalance, and weighting may lead to redundancy. Furthermore, the combination of Dice and BCE has been proven to be effective in various scenarios [68, 69].

The Adam optimizer was selected for training, combined with a cosine annealing learning rate scheduler. The initial learning rate was set to 1e-4, and the minimum learning rate to 1e-6. This scheduling method smoothly reduces the learning rate, avoiding early overfitting and facilitating model convergence. The batch size was set to 100, and the maximum number of training epochs to 100. The optimal model was selected based on the weight file with the lowest validation set loss.

Conclusion

In this study, we propose MTRFU-Net, a pulmonary nodule segmentation model based on an improved U-Net architecture integrating spatial and frequency-domain dual-branch modules. This model aims to significantly enhance the accuracy of pulmonary nodule segmentation, thereby facilitating the early diagnosis of lung cancer. Based on deep learning technology, the model combines fast Fourier transform with CNN convolution, and integrates key modules and technologies such as spatial-frequency fusion modules, Transformer, multi-scale feature extraction, and squeeze-and-excitation attention. This enables more accurate extraction of edge feature information of pulmonary nodules and reduces segmentation difficulties caused by characteristics such as pulmonary nodule heterogeneity. Validation and comparative experiments on the LIDC-IDRI dataset demonstrate excellent performance in segmentation tasks, confirming the effectiveness of combining Fourier transform with convolution.

Discussion

Although we have experimentally analyzed the gated fusion weight allocation of the SFF module and clarified the role of its frequency branch in the training process, the present research still lacks strong interpretability. In future work, we intend to verify the correlation between each gated branch of the SFF module and the specific features of pulmonary nodules, to be achieved by means of feature visualization strategies—such as establishing precise coordinate mapping between the pixels of feature maps extracted by the module and the specific regions of the original images.

In future research, we will further explore and optimize the model design from the perspective of semi-supervised and unsupervised learning, including semi-supervised learning strategies based on limited labeled data (e.g., pseudo-labeling and consistency regularization). Under the semi-supervised learning paradigm, the SFF module can boost performance through dual-domain feature learning and enrich the feature representation of unlabeled samples. The Trans_ASPP module, endowed with robust multi-scale contextual capture capacity, can refine the pseudo-labels of unlabeled data. Furthermore, the Res_scSE module can adaptively amplify the response of key feature channels and spatial features for unlabeled data. This is expected to address the problem of insufficient medical dataset size and further improve the model’s performance.

Pulmonary nodule images are mostly stored in 3D volumetric form. To date, we have completed comprehensive experiments in the 2D domain, which lays a solid foundation for subsequent research on extending the model to the 3D domain. Nevertheless, extending the proposed framework from 2D to 3D will encounter two key challenges. For one thing, 3D FFT and 3D convolution operations will result in a sharp increase in computational complexity and memory overhead. For another, the 3D volumetric data of pulmonary nodules presents more pronounced spatial heterogeneity, which poses great challenges for the dynamic fusion of spatial and frequency-domain features to adapt to the structural characteristics of nodules in 3D space. These challenges will be the core focus of our subsequent research, where we will develop and implement targeted optimization strategies and solutions.

Our model has been extensively validated on the LIDC-IDRI dataset only, and has delivered excellent segmentation performance. While the LIDC-IDRI is a large-scale public dataset jointly collected by multiple institutions, potential domain shift issues may still exist within the dataset. As experimental results are susceptible to various dataset factors including data collection protocols and annotation accuracy, cross-dataset experiments have not been conducted yet, meaning the generalization ability of the current work awaits further verification. In future research, we plan to collaborate with clinical departments of hospitals to construct an independent, publicly available small-scale pulmonary nodule dataset, which will be utilized to verify the model’s generalization ability and develop targeted improvement strategies for it.

The countable condensation on linear orders
Jennifer Brown, Ricardo Suárez

Abstract
. The countable condensation on a linear order L L is the equivalence relation ∼ ω \sim_{\omega} defined by declaring x ∼ ω y x\sim_{\omega}y when the set of points between x x and y y is countable. We characterize the linear orders L L that condense to 1 1 under the countable condensation by constructing a linear order U U that is universal for the order types L L such that L / ∼ ω ≅ 1 L/\!\!\sim_{\omega}\,\cong 1 . We define a multiplication operation ⋅ ω \cdot_{\omega} on the class of linear orders by setting M ⋅ ω L M\cdot_{\omega}L to be the order type of ( M L ) / ∼ ω (ML)/\!\!\sim_{\omega} (where M ​ L ML denotes the lexicographic product), and show that the right identities for ⋅ ω \cdot_{\omega} are exactly the uncountable suborders of U U . The order types of these uncountable suborders of U U form a left regular band under ⋅ ω \cdot_{\omega} , and the order types of all suborders of U U form a semigroup.

1.
Introduction

A condensation of a linear order
L
is an equivalence relation on
L
whose equivalence classes are intervals (that is, convex subsets of
L
). Condensations of various kinds – in particular, the finite condensation
\sim_{\textrm{F}}
, where two elements of a linear order are declared equivalent exactly when there are only finitely many elements lying between them – have been used extensively as a tool for analyzing the structure of linear orders (see, for example,
[
3
]
or
[
4
]
). In
[
1
]
, we defined a multiplication on the class of linear orders in terms of the finite condensation: for linear orders
L
and
M
,
L\cdot_{\textrm{F}}M:=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{LM}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{LM}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{LM}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{LM}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})
, where
LM
is the lexicographic product of
L
and
M
. The set of order types of linear orders that are right identities for the operation
\cdot_{\textrm{F}}
is
\{\omega,\omega^{*},\zeta\}
(where
\omega^{*}
denotes the reverse order on the ordinal
\omega
and
\zeta
denotes the order type of the integers), and we showed in
[
1
]
that this set forms a left-regular band. Moreover,
\zeta
is a universal order type for linear orders that condense to
1
modulo the finite condensation: for suppose
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}\cong 1
. Then
L
has only one equivalence class modulo the finite condensation; that is, for any
x,y\in L
, there are only finitely many points of
L
between
x
and
y
. It follows that
L
is either a finite linear order or is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, or
{\mathbb{Z}}
, all of whose order types embed into
\zeta
.

In the present work, we are again interested in algebraic structures that arise when a multiplication on linear orders is defined modulo a condensation, in this case the
countable condensation
. For any linear order
L
and
x,y\in L
, say that
x
is equivalent to
y
modulo the countable condensation, and write
x\sim_{\omega}y
, when there are only countably many elements between
x
and
y
. (We show in Lemma/Definition
2.2
that
\sim_{\omega}
is, in fact, a condensation.) This condensation is mentioned, though not by name, in Exercise 4.10 in
[
6
]
, where it is used to show that any uncountable dense subordering
A
of
{\mathbb{R}}
contains an
\aleph_{1}
-dense set. We define a multiplication
\cdot_{\omega}
on the class of linear orders by
L\cdot_{\omega}M:=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{LM}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{LM}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{LM}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{LM}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}})
. As in the case of the multiplication modulo the finite condensation, the set of linear orders that act as right identities for multiplication modulo the countable condensation does form a left-regular band, as we show in Theorem
5.2
; but unlike in the case of the finite condensation, this set of right identities for
\cdot_{\omega}
is infinite. We characterize the set of right identities for
\cdot_{\omega}
in Theorem
4.5
. In order to do this, we first describe a universal set
U
for order types that, when modded-out by the countable condensation, are isomorphic to the trivial linear order
1
(i.e. that have only one equivalence class under
\sim_{\omega}
). The linear order
U
is constructed by inserting a copy of the rationals between each pair of consecutive elements in
\omega_{1}^{*}+\omega_{1}
(where
\omega_{1}^{*}
denotes the ordinal
\omega_{1}
under the reverse ordering). We then show that the set of right identities for multiplication modulo the countable condensation consists exactly of the uncountable order types that embed into
U
.

2.
Preliminaries

A standard reference for linear orders is
[
6
]
, and we mostly follow the notational conventions found there. A notable exception is that we write our multiplication of linear orders
lexicographically
: for linear orders
L
and
M
,
LM
is the linear order having the order type of the lexicographic order on
L\times M
. In this we follow the convention in
[
4
]
. We work in ZFC. For basic notions of set theory, see
[
5
]
. Since we treat ordinals as linear orders, our (lexicographic) notation for products of ordinals is the reverse of the standard one. For example,
2\omega
in this paper means the linear order that results from replacing each element of
2
by a copy of
\omega
, so that
2\omega\cong\omega+\omega
.
L^{*}
denotes the underlying set
L
with the reverse order. An interval
I
in a linear order
L
is a convex subset of
L
: if
a,b\in I
and
a<x<b
, then
x\in I
.
[\{a,b\}]
denotes either
\{x\in L:a\leq x\leq b\}
(if
a\leq b
) or
\{x\in L:b\leq x\leq a\}
(if
b<a
).

The order type of a linear order
L
is denoted
\operatorname{o.\!t.}(L)
. We use the convention from
[
6
]
that a representative linear order is chosen from each order type, and that, in the case of well-orders, that representative is an ordinal. We will occasionally conflate linear orders and order types in situations where it should not cause confusion. To distinguish between, e.g., the order type of the first uncountable ordinal and its cardinality, we write
\omega_{1}
for the former and
\aleph_{1}
for the latter.

A subset
X
of a linear order
L
is said to be
cofinal
if it is unbounded above in
L
; that is, if for every
l\in L
, there is an
x\in X
such that
x\geq l
. Similarly,
X\subseteq L
is
cointial
if it is unbounded below in
L
.
The
cofinality
of
L
, denoted
\operatorname{cf}(L)
, is the least length of a strictly increasing, cofinal sequence in
L
. The
coinitiality
of
L
, denoted
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)
, is the least length of a strictly decreasing, coinitial sequence in
L
.

If
(P,\leq)
is any partial order and
r\in P
, then

P\uparrow r:=\{q\in P:q\geq r\}
, and
P\downarrow r:=\{q\in P:q\leq r\}.

P\uparrow r
is read “
P
above
r
”, and
P\downarrow r
is read “
P
below
r
”. If
L
is a linear order and if we can write
L=L^{\prime}+T
for some
L^{\prime}
and
T
, then
T
is called a
tail
, or final segment, of
L
. Note that in general
L
might have many tails. Similarly, if we can write
L=H+L^{\prime}
for some
L^{\prime}
and
H
, then
H
is called a
head
, or initial segment, of
L
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
condensation
of a linear order
L
is an equivalence relation
\sim
on
L
whose equivalence classes are intervals. The equivalence class of
x\in L
is denoted
\boldsymbol{c}(x)
.
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim}$}}}
is then a linear order as well, ordered by declaring
\boldsymbol{c}(x)<\boldsymbol{c}(y)
when
l<m
for all
l\in\boldsymbol{c}(x)
and
m\in\boldsymbol{c}(y)
.

Definition/Lemma 2.2
.

For any linear order
L
and elements
x,y\in L
, say that
x\sim_{\omega}y
if there are only countably many elements between
x
and
y
; that is,
|[\{x,y\}]|\leq\aleph_{0}
.
For any linear order
L
, the relation
\sim_{\omega}
is a condensation, which we will refer to as the
countable condensation
.

Proof.

For reflexivity: Let
x\in L
. Then
|[\{x,x\}]|=|\{x\}|=1\leq\aleph_{0}
, so
x\sim_{\omega}x
.

Symmetry is immediate since
[\{x,y\}]=[\{y,x\}]
.

For transitivity: suppose
x\sim_{\omega}y
and
y\sim_{\omega}z
. If any two of
x,y,
and
z
are equal, then clearly
x\sim_{\omega}z
, so suppose
x,y
, and
z
are all distinct.
Case 1:

x<y<z
. Then
[x,z]=[x,y]\cup[y,z]
, and each of
[x,y]
and
[y,z]
is countable by assumption, so
[x,z]
is countable; so
x\sim_{\omega}z
.
Case 2:

x<z<y
. In this case
[x,z]
is a subset of
[x,y]
, and we have supposed
[x,y]
is countable, so
[x,z]
is countable as well.
Case 3:

y<x<z
. Then
[z,x]
is a subset of
[y,x]
, which is countable, so
[z,x]
is countable.
Case 4:

y<z<x
. Then
[z,x]
is a subset of
[y,x]
, which is countable.
Case 5:

z<x<y
. Then
[z,x]
is a subset of
[z,y]
, which is countable.
Case 6:

z<y<x
. Similarly to Case 1, we have that
[z,x]
is the union of two sets,
[z,y]
and
[y,x]
, which are by assumption countable.

Thus
\sim_{\omega}
is an equivalence relation. Next, we show that all of its equivalence classes are intervals. For
x\in L
, denote by
\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x)
the equivalence class of
x
modulo
\sim_{\omega}
:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in L:x\sim_{\omega}y\}

\displaystyle=\{y\in L:\textrm{ there are only countably many points of $L$ between $x$ and $y$}\}.

Suppose
y,z\in\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x)
with
y<z
, and suppose
w\in L
with
y<w<z
. If
w=x
, we are done, so suppose
w\neq x
. If
x<y
, then
[x,w]\subseteq[x,z]
, which is countable. If
y<x<z
, then
[\{w,x\}]\subseteq[y,z]=[y,x]\cup[x,z]
, which is a union of two countable sets. If
x>z
, then
[w,x]\subseteq[y,x]
, which is countable by assumption. In all cases,
w\sim_{\omega}x
, so that
w\in\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x)
. Thus
\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x)
is an interval.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If

is well-ordered, then so is
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\alpha
and

be ordinals with
\alpha<\beta
. Then
\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}})\leq\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}})

We know by Lemma
2.3
that the countable condensation of a well-ordering is also well-ordered. Suppose

and

are ordinals with

.
Let

, so that

, is its copy in

, and

is the isomorphic copy of
\alpha
inside of

.
Note that the equivalence class (modulo

) in

of an element

of

. For this reason, for each

, denote

{}^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(i(x^{\prime}))\not\subseteq i[\alpha]
. Observing that in this case
{}^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(i(x^{\prime}))\setminus i[\alpha]>i[\alpha]
, it again follows that
g(^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x))=\,^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(i(x))<\,^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(i(x^{\prime}))=g(^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x^{\prime}))
in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
.

Therefore
g
is an order-preserving injection from
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
to
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
, and since both of these sets are well-ordered by Lemma
2.3
, the result follows.
∎

3.
The universal set for the countable condensation

We would like to characterize the linear orders that condense to
1
modulo the countable condensation. We immediately have that if
L
is any countable linear order, then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
(for if
x,y\in L
, then
[\{x,y\}]
is countable, as it is a subset of the countable set
L
, so
x\sim_{\omega}y
). There are also many uncountable sets that reduce to
1
modulo the countable condensation, though; for example,
\omega_{1}
itself has this property.

Lemma 3.1
.

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\omega_{1}
with
\alpha<\beta
. As
\beta
is countable,
[0,\beta]
is countable. Since
[\alpha,\beta]\subseteq[0,\beta]
, the interval
[\alpha,\beta]
is also countable, and so
\alpha\sim_{\omega}\beta
. Since any two elements of
\omega_{1}
are in the same equivalence class modulo
\sim_{\omega}
, we have
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.
∎

If
\alpha
is any ordinal with
\alpha>\omega_{1}
, then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
has at least two elements. For example, the last element of the ordinal
\omega_{1}+1
is not equivalent, modulo the countable condensation, to any element less than it, so that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{(\omega_{1}+1)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{(\omega_{1}+1)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{(\omega_{1}+1)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{(\omega_{1}+1)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 2
by Lemma
3.1
.

Taking the countable condensation of the second uncountable ordinal results in a linear order of the same order type.

Proposition 3.2
.

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong\omega_{2}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
,
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
is isomorphic to an ordinal. Consider the sequence
\langle\alpha\omega_{1}:\alpha<\omega_{2}\rangle
. This sequence is cofinal in
\omega_{2}
, for if
\alpha<\omega_{2}
then
\alpha\leq\alpha\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}
. For any
\alpha<\beta<\omega_{2}
,
|[\alpha\omega_{1},\beta\omega_{1}]|=\omega_{1}
, so
\alpha\omega_{1}\not\sim_{\omega}\beta\omega_{1}
.
Thus the sequence
\langle\alpha\omega_{1}:\alpha<\omega_{2}\rangle
is strictly increasing, and so it has length
\omega_{2}
as
\omega_{2}
is regular. This means that there are at least
\aleph_{2}
-many equivalence classes modulo
\sim_{\omega}
in
\omega_{2}
, namely
\{\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(\alpha\omega_{1}):\alpha<\omega_{2}\}
, so
\left|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\right|\geq|\aleph_{2}|
. Since
\left|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\right|\leq|\omega_{2}|
, we have
\left|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\right|=\aleph_{2}
.

We have that if
\alpha<\beta<\omega_{2}
then
\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(\alpha\omega_{1})<\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(\beta\omega_{1})
in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
. That is, the classes
\{\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(\alpha\omega_{1}):\alpha<\omega_{2}\}
form a suborder of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
of type
\omega_{2}
. There is no suborder of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
of order type
\omega_{2}+1
, for certainly any condensation of a linear order will be embeddable in that linear order. Thus the order type of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
must be
\omega_{2}
.
∎

Note that Proposition
3.2
does not mean that the countable condensation does nothing to
\omega_{2}
; it simply means that performing this condensation on
\omega_{2}
results in a linear order with the same order type. Convex
\omega
-sequences in
\omega_{2}
do get condensed down to a point modulo the countable condensation. However, convex
\omega_{1}
-sequences in
\omega_{2}
do not get condensed to a single point.

Recall the following theorem of Cantor, which states that the rationals are universal for countable linear orders.

Theorem 3.3

(Cantor)
.

Any countable linear order is isomorphic to a subordering of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. ∎

We next define a linear order
U
that, as we will show in Theorem
3.17
, is universal for linear orders
L
such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
. To see what form such a
U
must take, consider a linear order
L
that has only a single equivalence class modulo the countable condensation, and suppose for simplicity that
L
has a least element. The intuition here is that
L
must have a cofinal “spine” of order type at most
\omega_{1}
with the “gaps” filled-in with some sort of countable linear order. We will map this spine to the strictly increasing cofinal sequence
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
of
U
(see Definition
3.4
), and – by Theorem
3.3
– we can map each of those countable linear orders in the “gaps” into the copy of
{\mathbb{Q}}
that we will insert between
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\alpha+1}
in
U
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
have order type
\omega_{1}
, and let
\{-u_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
have order type
\omega_{1}^{*}
. For
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, let
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
and
{\mathbb{Q}}(-\alpha)
be isomorphic copies of
{\mathbb{Q}}
; and let
{\mathbb{Q}}(\textrm{mid})
be another isomorphic copy of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. We define a linear order on the point set

U:=\{-u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\omega_{1}\}\cup\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\omega_{1}\}\cup\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\omega_{1}}{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\omega_{1}}{\mathbb{Q}}(-\alpha)\right)\cup{\mathbb{Q}}(\textrm{mid})

as follows: first, we declare
\{-u_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}<\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
. For
\alpha<\beta<\omega_{1}
, we declare
{\mathbb{Q}}(-\beta)<{\mathbb{Q}}(-\alpha)<{\mathbb{Q}}(\textrm{mid})<{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)<{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
. For
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, we declare
u_{\alpha}<{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)<u_{\alpha+1}
and
-u_{\alpha+1}<{\mathbb{Q}}(-u_{\alpha})<-u_{\alpha}
. Finally, we declare
-u_{0}<{\mathbb{Q}}(\textrm{mid})<u_{0}.
We call this linear order
U
the
\omega_{1}
-lengthened rational line.

Proposition 3.5
.

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.

Proof.

For convenience, we write
\omega_{1}^{*}=\{-\alpha:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
. Let
x,y\in U
; we show that
[x,y]
is countable. There are several cases.

First suppose
x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
for some
\alpha<\omega_{1}
. Then
[x,y]\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
, which is countable. The same reasoning works if
x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}(-\alpha)
for some
\alpha
or if
x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}(mid)
.

Next suppose
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
and
y\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
for some
\alpha<\beta<\omega_{1}
. Then
|[x,y]|\leq|{\mathbb{Q}}||\beta|\leq\aleph_{0}\aleph_{0}=\aleph_{0}.
The same reasoning works if
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}(-\alpha)
and
y\in{\mathbb{Q}}(-\beta)
for some
\alpha<\beta<\omega_{1}
.

Finally, suppose
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}(-\alpha)
and
y\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
for some
\alpha,\beta<\omega_{1}
. Then
|[x,y]|\leq 2|{\mathbb{Q}}|\max\{\alpha,\beta\}\leq\aleph_{0}\aleph_{0}=\aleph_{0}.

Thus in all cases
[x,y]
is countable. Therefore
x\sim_{\omega}y
for all
x,y\in U
, so that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

If
L
is isomorphic to a suborder of
U
, then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.

Proof.

Suppose
L\subseteq U
with the induced order. For
x,y\in L
,
[\{x,y\}]_{L}\subseteq[\{x,y\}]_{U}
. By Proposition
3.5
,
|[\{x,y\}]_{U}|\leq\omega
, so also
|[\{x,y\}]_{L}|\leq\omega
, so
x\sim_{\omega}y.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
L
is any linear order such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
, then any strictly increasing (resp. decreasing) sequence in
L
must be of order type at most
\omega_{1}
(resp.
\omega_{1}^{*})
.

Proof.

We argue by contrapositive. Let
L
be a linear order, and suppose that there is a strictly increasing sequence
\langle x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}+1\rangle
in
L
of order type
\omega_{1}+1
. Since there are
\omega_{1}
-many elements of
L
between
x_{0}
and
x_{\omega_{1}}
, we have
x_{0}\not\sim_{\omega}x_{\omega_{1}}
, and so
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
has at least
2
elements. (A similar argument shows that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
has at least
2
elements if there is a strictly decreasing sequence of type
(\omega_{1}+1)^{*}
in
L
.)
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

If
L
is a linear order such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
, then
\operatorname{cf}(L)\leq\omega_{1}
and
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)\leq\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{cf}(L)
were greater than
\omega_{1}
, then
L
would have a strictly increasing sequence of size greater than
\omega_{1}
; but this is not possible, by Lemma
3.7
. Thus
\operatorname{cf}(L)\leq\omega_{1}
. A similar argument shows that
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)\leq\omega_{1}
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

If
L
is any linear order such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
, then
|L|\leq\aleph_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a linear order with
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.

Case 1:
If
L
has both a first element
x
and a last element
y
, then since
x\sim_{\omega}y
, we have
|L|=|[x,y]|\leq\aleph_{0}
.

Case 2:
Suppose
L
has a first element
x_{0}
but no last element. Let
\langle x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cf}(L)\rangle
be cofinal in
L
. Then for each
l\in L
, there is an
\alpha<\operatorname{cf}(L)
such that
x_{0}\leq l<x_{\alpha}
, so
L=\bigcup_{\alpha<\operatorname{cf}(L)}[x_{0},x_{\alpha})
. Then, noting that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
implies that
x_{0}\sim_{\omega}x_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha<\operatorname{cf}(L)
, we have

|L|=\left|\bigcup_{\alpha<\operatorname{cf}(L)}[x_{0},x_{\alpha})\right|\leq\sum_{\alpha<\operatorname{cf}(L)}|[x_{0},x_{\alpha})|\leq\sum_{\alpha<\operatorname{cf}(L)}\aleph_{0}\leq\sum_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}\aleph_{0}=\aleph_{1}

as
\operatorname{cf}(L)\leq\omega_{1}
by Corollary
3.8
.

Case 3:
Suppose
L
has a last element
x_{0}
but no first element. In this case, we can find a coinitial sequence
\langle x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)\rangle
in
L
of length at most
\omega_{1}
, and we can write
L=\bigcup_{\alpha<\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)}(x_{\alpha},x_{0}]
. Then since
x_{\alpha}\sim_{\omega}x_{0}
for each
\alpha<\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)
, we again have

|L|=\left|\bigcup_{\alpha<\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)}(x_{\alpha},x_{0}]\right|\leq\sum_{\alpha<\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)}|(x_{\alpha},x_{0}]|\leq\sum_{\alpha<\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)}\aleph_{0}\leq\sum_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}\aleph_{0}=\aleph_{1}.

Case 4:
Suppose
L
has neither a first element nor a last element. Choose any
x_{0}\in L
. By Case 2,
|[x_{0},+\infty)|\leq\aleph_{1}
; and by Case 3,
|(-\infty,x_{0}]|\leq\aleph_{1}
; so
|L|=|(-\infty,x_{0}]\cup[x_{0},+\infty)|\leq\aleph_{1}+\aleph_{1}=\aleph_{1}
.

Therefore in all cases we have
|L|\leq\aleph_{1}
.

∎

Note that the converse of Proposition
3.9
does not hold; for example,
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{(\omega_{1}+1)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{(\omega_{1}+1)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{(\omega_{1}+1)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{(\omega_{1}+1)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 2
even though
|\omega_{1}+1|=\aleph_{1}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

If
L
is a linear order with
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and
L
is uncountable, then
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega_{1}
or
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)=\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

We argue by contrapositive. Suppose
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
, and suppose that
\operatorname{cf}(L)\neq\omega_{1}
and
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)\neq\omega_{1}
. By Corollary
3.8
, this means that
\operatorname{cf}(L)
and
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)
are both countable.
There are several cases.

If
L
has both a first element
x
and a last element
y
, then
|L|=|[x,y]|\leq\aleph_{0}
because
x\sim_{\omega}y
.

Suppose
L
has a first element
x_{0}
but no last element, so that (since we are assuming that
\operatorname{cf}(L)
is countable) we can find a strictly increasing cofinal sequence
\langle x_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
in
L
. Then for each
l\in L
, there is an
n<\omega
such that
l\in[x_{0},x_{n})
; and also, for each
n<\omega
, the interval
[x_{0},x_{n})
is countable, as we are assuming that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
. Therefore

|L|=\left|\bigcup_{n<\omega}[x_{0},x_{n})\right|\leq\sum_{n<\omega}|[x_{0},x_{n})|\leq\sum_{n<\omega}\aleph_{0}=\aleph_{0}.

Next, suppose that
L
has a last element but no first element. In this case we can find a countable strictly decreasing coinitial sequence
\langle y_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
in
L
and write
L=\bigcup_{n<\omega}(y_{n},y_{0}]
. Then, since each of the intervals
(y_{n},y_{0}]
must be countable, we have

|L|=\left|\bigcup_{n<\omega}(y_{n},y_{0}]\right|\leq\sum_{n<\omega}|(y_{n},y_{0}]|\leq\sum_{n<\omega}\aleph_{0}=\aleph_{0}.

Finally, suppose
L
has neither a first element nor a last element, so that
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega
and
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)=\omega
. Let
\langle x_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
be increasing and cofinal in
L
, and let
\langle y_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
be decreasing and coinitial in
L
, and suppose
x_{0}=y_{0}
. (We may arrange this, adding an extra element to the beginning of a cofinal or coinitial sequence as necessary.) We then have

\displaystyle|L|

\displaystyle=|(-\infty,x_{0}]\cup[x_{0},+\infty)|=\left|\left(\bigcup_{n<\omega}(y_{n},y_{0}]\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{n<\omega}[x_{0},x_{n})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\bigcup_{n<\omega}(y_{n},y_{0}]\right|+\left|\bigcup_{n<\omega}[x_{0},x_{n})\right|\leq\left(\sum_{n<\omega}|(y_{n},y_{0}]|\right)+\left(\sum_{n<\omega}|[x_{0},x_{n})|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n<\omega}\aleph_{0}\right)+\left(\sum_{n<\omega}\aleph_{0}\right)=\aleph_{0}+\aleph_{0}=\aleph_{0}.

Therefore in all cases, if
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and both
\operatorname{cf}(L)
and
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)
are countable, then
L
is countable.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose
L
is a linear order with a first element such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega_{1}
. Then
L
embeds into a linear order

such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.00891pt}{$\displaystyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75891pt}{$\textstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.8978pt}{$\scriptstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.11111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1

\operatorname{cf}(L^{\prime})=\omega_{1}
,

has a first element, and
L^{\prime}
has a strictly increasing cofinal sequence
\langle y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\rangle
such that
L^{\prime}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}[y_{\alpha},y_{\alpha+1})

Suppose
L
has a first element
x_{0}
, and let

be strictly increasing and cofinal in
L
. We construct the desired linear order
L^{\prime}
and cofinal sequence
\langle y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\rangle
by adding to
X
the suprema in
L
of sequences in
X
, if these exist, or – if these suprema do not exist – by adding new elements to
L
. We do this by defining sequences
\{s_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
and
\{n_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
as follows.

Let
\alpha<\omega_{1}
. If
\alpha
is a successor ordinal, then set
s_{\alpha}=n_{\alpha}=x_{0}
. Next, suppose that
\alpha<\omega_{1}
is a limit ordinal. If
x_{\alpha}=\sup\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
in
L
, then set
s_{\alpha}=n_{\alpha}=x_{0}
. If
\sup\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
exists in
L
but
\sup\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}\neq x_{\alpha}
, then we set
s_{\alpha}=\sup\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
and set
n_{\alpha}=x_{0}
.
If
\sup\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
does not exist in
L
, then we set
s_{\alpha}=x_{0}
, and we add a new element
n_{\alpha}
to
L
with the property that
\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}<n_{\alpha}<\{l\in L:l>x_{\beta}\textrm{ for all }\beta<\alpha\}
. That is, in this case we add a new element that will be the supremum of
\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
in
L^{\prime}
.

Let
L^{\prime}
consist of
L
together with all of the newly-added elements
n_{\alpha}
. Let
Y
consist of
X
together with the newly-added elements
s_{\alpha}
and
n_{\alpha}
. That is,

L^{\prime}:=L\cup\{n_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad Y:=X\cup\{s_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}\cup\{n_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}.

We still have
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.00891pt}{$\displaystyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75891pt}{$\textstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.8978pt}{$\scriptstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.11111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1

l,m\in L^{\prime}

l<m
.
X
is still cofinal in
L^{\prime}
, as each new element
n_{\alpha}\in L^{\prime}\setminus L
was less than

. Then we can choose
\alpha<\omega_{1}
such that
m<x_{\alpha}
.
Observe that between any two elements of
L
, at most countably many new elements
n_{\alpha}
were added in forming
L^{\prime}
. Then since
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1

|[x_{0},x_{\alpha})|=|([x_{0},x_{\alpha})\cap L)\cup([x_{0},x_{\alpha})\setminus L)|\leq\aleph_{0}+\aleph_{0}=\aleph_{0}
. Then
|[l,m)|
is also countable, as
[l,m)\subseteq[x_{0},x_{\alpha})
, and so
l\sim_{\omega}m
in
L^{\prime}
.

Next, observe that
Y
is cofinal in
L^{\prime}
, and also
Y
is well-ordered of order type
\omega_{1}
. We can then index the elements of
Y
as
Y=\langle y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\rangle
(and we will have
y_{0}=x_{0}
). By construction,
Y
has the property that for each limit
\alpha<\omega_{1}
,
y_{\alpha}=\sup\{y_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}.

Finally, we claim that
L^{\prime}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}[y_{\alpha},y_{\alpha+1})

l\in L^{\prime}

. Suppose

\alpha^{\prime}=\beta+1

; so

. By the minimality of
\alpha^{\prime}
, we have that for all

Proposition 3.12
.

L
is a linear order with a first element such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega_{1}
. Then by Proposition
3.11
,
L
embeds into a linear order
L^{\prime}
having a first element such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.00891pt}{$\displaystyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75891pt}{$\textstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.8978pt}{$\scriptstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.11111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
,
\operatorname{cf}(L^{\prime})=\omega_{1}
, and there is a strictly increasing cofinal sequence
\langle y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\rangle
in
L^{\prime}
such that
L^{\prime}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}[y_{\alpha},y_{\alpha+1}).
Then by Proposition
3.12
,
L^{\prime}
embeds into
U
. That is, we have
L\hookrightarrow L^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime}\hookrightarrow U
, and therefore
L\hookrightarrow U
.
∎

Reversing the order, we obtain,
mutatis mutandis
, the following versions of Propositions
3.11
,
3.12
, and
3.13
, respectively.

Proposition 3.14
.

Suppose
L
is a linear order with a last element such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)=\omega_{1}
. Then
L
embeds into a linear order
L^{\prime}
such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.00891pt}{$\displaystyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75891pt}{$\textstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.8978pt}{$\scriptstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.11111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L^{\prime}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
,
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L^{\prime})=\omega_{1}
,
L^{\prime}
has a last element, and
L^{\prime}
has a strictly decreasing coinitial sequence
\langle y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\rangle
such that
L^{\prime}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}(y_{\alpha+1},y_{\alpha}]
. ∎

Proposition 3.15
.

Suppose
L
is a linear order with a last element such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)=\omega_{1}
. Suppose further that
L
has a strictly decreasing, coinitial sequence
\langle x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\rangle
such that
L=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}(x_{\alpha+1},x_{\alpha}]
. Then
L
is isomorphic to a suborder of
U
. ∎

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
L
be a linear order with a last element such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)=\omega_{1}
. Then
L
is isomorphic to a suborder of
U
. ∎

We can now prove that the
\omega_{1}
-lengthened rational line
U
is universal for the linear orders that condense to
1
modulo the countable condensation.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
L
be any linear order. Then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
if and only if
L
is isomorphic to a suborder of
U
.

Proof.

If
L
is isomorphic to a suborder of
U
, then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
by Proposition
3.6
.

Now suppose that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.

Case 1:
If
L
is countable, then
L
embeds into
{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
by Theorem
3.3
.

Case 2:
Suppose
L
is uncountable and has a first element. Then
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega_{1}
by Lemma
3.10
, so
L
embeds into
U
by Proposition
3.13
.

Case 3:
Next, suppose
L
is uncountable and has a last element. Then
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)=\omega_{1}
by Lemma
3.10
, so
L
embeds into
U
by Proposition
3.16
.

Case 4:
Finally, suppose
L
is uncountable with neither a first element nor a last element. Fix any
x_{0}\in L
and denote
L^{-}=(-\infty,x_{0}]
and
L^{+}=[x_{0},\infty)
. Then we can embed
L^{-}
into
U^{-}=(-\infty,-u_{0}]
by cases 1 or 3, and we can embed
L^{+}
into
U^{+}=[u_{0},\infty)
by cases 1 or 2.
∎

4.
The universal set
U
and multiplication modulo the countable condensation

In this section, we define a multiplication operation modulo the countable condensation, and show how it relates to the universal set
U
defined in Section
3
.

Definition 4.1
.

For linear orders
M
and
L
, define an operation
\cdot_{\omega}
(the lexicographic product modulo the countable condensation) by

M\cdot_{\omega}L:=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}).

The following lemma implies that the linear order
\omega_{1}
is a right identity for
\cdot_{\omega}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M
be any linear order. Then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{M\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{M\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{M\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong M
.

Proof.

Let
M
be any linear order. Recall that
M\omega_{1}
is formed by replacing every
m\in M
with a copy
\omega_{1}(m)
of
\omega_{1}
. Let
x,y\in M\omega_{1}
. If
x,y\in\omega_{1}(m)
for some
m\in M
– that is, if
x
and
y
are in the same copy of
\omega_{1}
– then
x\sim_{\omega}y
, by Lemma
3.1
. This means that for every
x\in M\omega_{1}
,
\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x)\supseteq\omega_{1}(m)
, where
\omega_{1}(m)
is the copy of
\omega_{1}
containing
x
.

Now suppose
x
and
y
are in different copies of
\omega_{1}
; without loss of generality,
x\in\omega_{1}(m)
and
y\in\omega_{1}(m^{\prime})
for some
m,m^{\prime}\in M
with
m<m^{\prime}
. Then there are at least
\omega_{1}
-many elements of
M\omega_{1}
between
x
and
y
, namely the
\omega_{1}
-many elements of
\omega_{1}(m)
that are greater than
x
. This means that for any
x\in M\omega_{1}
,
\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x)\subseteq\omega_{1}(m)
where
\omega_{1}(m)
is the copy of
\omega_{1}
containing
x
.

For
x\in M\omega_{1}
, denote by
m_{x}
the element of
M
such that
x\in\omega_{1}(m_{x})
. Then we have
\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(x)<\boldsymbol{c}_{\omega}(y)
if and only if
\omega_{1}(m_{x})<\omega_{1}(m_{y})
if and only if
m_{x}<m_{y}
; that is,
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{M\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{M\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{M\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M\omega_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}
is isomorphic to
M
.
∎

It is straightforward to verify that Lemma
4.2
remains true if
\omega_{1}
is replaced by
\omega_{1}^{*}
. By contrast, the result fails if we replace
\omega_{1}
by any countable linear order
L
: for if
L
is countable, then for any countable linear order
M\not\cong 1
,
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1\not\cong M
because
ML
is countable. In Theorem
4.5
, we characterize the right identities for multiplication modulo the countable condensation: all of those linear orders
L
such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong M
for every
M
. (One could also say that such
L
are exactly the linear orders that are always absorbed on the right when multiplying modulo the countable condensation.)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
L
be a linear order with
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
. Then
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega_{1}
if and only if
L
has no countable tail.

Proof.

Let
L
be a linear order with
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.

Suppose
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega_{1}
, and let
T
be a tail of
L
: so we can write
L=L^{\prime}+T
for some
L^{\prime}
. Choose
x_{0}\in T
and a strictly increasing, cofinal sequence
\langle x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\rangle
in
L
that begins with
x_{0}
. Since
T
is a tail, it is closed upwards, so
x_{\alpha}\in T
for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
. Thus
T
is uncountable. Since the arbitrarily chosen tail
T
was uncountable,
L
has no countable tail.

Now suppose every tail of
L
is uncountable. Let
\langle x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mu\rangle
, for some ordinal
\mu
, be strictly increasing and cofinal in
L
. Then, in particular, for every
l>x_{0}
, there is an
\alpha<\mu
such that
l<x_{\alpha}
. This means that we can write
L\uparrow x_{0}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\mu}[x_{0},x_{\alpha})
. Since
L\uparrow x_{0}
is a tail, it is uncountable, by assumption; and since we have
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
, it must be that
|[x_{0},x_{\alpha})|\leq\aleph_{0}
for each
\alpha<\mu
. Therefore we have

\omega_{1}\leq|L\uparrow x_{0}|=\left|\bigcup_{\alpha<\mu}[x_{0},x_{\alpha})\right|\leq\sum_{\alpha<\mu}|[x_{0},x_{\alpha})|\leq\sum_{\alpha<\mu}\aleph_{0}=|\mu|\aleph_{0}=\max\{|\mu|,\aleph_{0}\}.

Then
\omega_{1}\leq\max\{|\mu|,\aleph_{0}\}
, so
|\mu|\geq\omega_{1}
. Thus the sequence
\langle x_{\alpha}:\alpha\leq\mu\rangle
is uncountable; and so, since this was an arbitrary cofinal sequence,
\operatorname{cf}(L)\geq\omega_{1}
. We have
\operatorname{cf}(L)\leq\omega_{1}
by Corollary
3.8
, so
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega_{1}
.
∎

Reversing the order, we obtain the reverse version of Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
L
be a linear order with
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
. Then
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)=\omega_{1}
if and only if
L
has no countable head. ∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
L
be any linear order. The following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong M
for every linear order
M
;

(2)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and (
\operatorname{cf}(L)=\omega_{1}
or
\operatorname{cf}^{*}(L)=\omega_{1}
);

(3)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
and (
L
has no countable tail or
L
has no countable head);

(4)

L
embeds into the
\omega_{1}
-lengthened rational line
U
and
L
has a strictly monotone sequence of length
\omega_{1}.

(5)

L
is isomorphic to an uncountable suborder of
U
.

Proof.

(1)
\implies
(5): Suppose
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{ML}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong M
for every linear order
M
. In particular, this holds for
M=1
, so
L\preceq U
by Theorem
3.17
. If
L
were countable, then we would have
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{{\mathbb{Q}}L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{{\mathbb{Q}}L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{{\mathbb{Q}}L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{{\mathbb{Q}}L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1\not\cong{\mathbb{Q}}
because
{\mathbb{Q}}L
would also be countable; so it must be that
L
is uncountable.

(5)
\implies
(2): Suppose
L
is isomorphic to an uncountable suborder of
U
. By Theorem
3.17
,

x,y\in L_{1}L_{2}
, and so
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L_{1}L_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L_{1}L_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L_{1}L_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L_{1}L_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Denote by
\mathcal{X}
the set of all order types of linear orders that embed into
U
. Then
\langle\mathcal{X},\sim_{\omega}\rangle
is a semigroup.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.17
,
\mathcal{X}
consists of the order types of those
L
for which
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
. By Theorem
4.5
,
\mathcal{S}
consists of the uncountable suborders of
U
, and
\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}

consists of the countable suborders of
U
.

Closure:
Let
L_{1},L_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
. Then
L_{1}\cdot_{\omega}L_{2}
is isomorphic to a suborder of
L_{1}
:

\displaystyle L_{1}\cdot_{\omega}L_{2}={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L_{1}L_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L_{1}L_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L_{1}L_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L_{1}L_{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}

\displaystyle\preceq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L_{1}U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L_{1}U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L_{1}U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L_{1}U}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\quad\textrm{(as $L_{2}\preceq U$)}

\displaystyle\cong L_{1}\quad\textrm{(by Theorem \ref{TFAE for right identity mod cc})}.

By assumption,
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L_{1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
, so also
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{(L_{1}\cdot_{\omega}L_{2})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{(L_{1}\cdot_{\omega}L_{2})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{(L_{1}\cdot_{\omega}L_{2})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{(L_{1}\cdot_{\omega}L_{2})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\omega}}$}}}\cong 1
. Thus
L_{1}\cdot_{\omega}L_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Associativity:
Let
L,M,N\in\mathcal{X}
. There are
8
cases.

Case 1:
If
L,M,N\in\mathcal{S}
, then by definition of
\mathcal{S}
,

L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong L\cdot_{\omega}M\cong L\cong L\cdot_{\omega}M\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N.

Cases 2 through 7 use both the definition of
\mathcal{S}
and Lemma
5.3
.

Case 2:
Suppose
L\in\mathcal{S}
,
M\in\mathcal{S}
, and

N\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
. Then

L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong L\cdot_{\omega}1\cong 1\cong L\cdot_{\omega}N\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N.

Case 3:
Suppose
L\in\mathcal{S}
,
M\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
, and
N\in\mathcal{S}
. Then

L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong L\cdot_{\omega}M\cong 1\cong L\cdot_{\omega}M\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N.

Case 4:
Suppose
L\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
,
M\in\mathcal{S}
, and
N\in\mathcal{S}
. Then

L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong L\cdot_{\omega}M\cong L\cong L\cdot_{\omega}M\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N.

Case 5:
Suppose
L\in\mathcal{S}
,
M\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
, and
N\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
. Then

L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong L\cdot_{\omega}1\cong 1\cong 1\cdot_{\omega}N\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N.

Case 6:
Suppose
L\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
,
M\in\mathcal{S}
, and
N\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
. Then

L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong L\cdot_{\omega}1\cong 1\cong L\cdot_{\omega}N\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N.

Case 7:
Suppose
L\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
,
M\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
, and
N\in\mathcal{S}
.
Then

L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong L\cdot_{\omega}M\cong 1\cong 1\cdot_{\omega}N\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N.

Case 8:
If
L,M,N\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}
, then all three are countable, so by Lemma
5.3
,

L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong L\cdot_{\omega}1\cong 1\cong 1\cdot_{\omega}N\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N.

Thus in all cases we have
L\cdot_{\omega}(M\cdot_{\omega}N)\cong(L\cdot_{\omega}M)\cdot_{\omega}N
, and so
\cdot_{\omega}
is an associative operation on
\mathcal{X}
.

Therefore we have that
\langle\mathcal{X},\cdot_{\omega}\rangle
is a semigroup.
∎

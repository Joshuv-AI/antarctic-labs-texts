The L L -polynomials of van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves in characteristic 2 2
Tetsushi Ito, Daichi Takeuchi, Takahiro Tsushima

Abstract
. The van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves form a class of Artin–Schreier coverings of the projective line over finite fields. We provide an explicit formula for their L L -polynomials in characteristic 2 2 , expressed in terms of characters of maximal abelian subgroups of the associated Heisenberg groups. For this purpose, we develop new methods specific to characteristic 2 2 that exploit the structure of the Heisenberg groups and the geometry of Lang torsors for W 2 W_{2} . As an application, we construct examples of curves in this family attaining the Hasse–Weil bound.

Introduction

Let
p_{0}
be a prime number,
p
a power of
p_{0}
, and
q
a power of
p
.
A polynomial of the form
R(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]

(a_{e}\neq 0)

is called an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial.
Let
C_{R}
be the affine curve over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
defined by the equation

y^{p}-y=xR(x)
.
Let
\overline{C}_{R}
be the smooth compactification of
C_{R}
.
The curves
C_{R}
and
\overline{C}_{R}
are called the
van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves

associated with
R(x)
.
When
p=p_{0}
, they were studied by van der Geer–van der Vlugt
[
7
]
.
The van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves have interesting geometric and arithmetic properties; see, for example,
[
2
,
4
,
9
,
11
,
16
]
.
Moreover, these curves are generally expected to be related to the reductions of certain affinoids in the Lubin–Tate space; examples of this are given in
[
8
,
18
]
. Understanding explicitly the Frobenius eigenvalues on their étale cohomology is expected to shed light on the explicit local Langlands correspondence.

In
[
15
]
, the second and third authors obtained an explicit formula for the
L
-polynomial of
\overline{C}_{R}
. Let us briefly recall the results. For details, we refer to
[
15
]
or Section
2
of this paper.
Assume
e\geq 1
, which implies that the genus of
\overline{C}_{R}
is positive.
Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be an algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Let
H_{R}
be the Heisenberg group acting on
C_{R,{\mathbb{F}}}\coloneqq C_{R}\otimes_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}{\mathbb{F}}
.
The underlying set of
H_{R}
is naturally identified with a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}^{2}
.
The center
Z(H_{R})
is naturally identified with
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and the quotient
H_{R}/Z(H_{R})
is abelian.
Fix a maximal abelian subgroup
A\subset H_{R}
. Throughout the paper, we assume that
A\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
. Then the action of
A
on
C_{R,{\mathbb{F}}}
can be defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
and the quotient
C_{R}/A
is isomorphic to the affine line
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Therefore, we obtain a finite étale Galois covering
C_{R}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
with Galois group
A
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number different from
p_{0}
.
For a finite abelian group
G
, we write
G^{\vee}
for its group of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}^{\times}
-valued characters

\Hom_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(G,\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}^{\times})
.
For a nontrivial character
\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\}
,
let
A_{\psi}^{\vee}
be the set of characters
A\to\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}^{\times}

whose restrictions to
Z(H_{R})\simeq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
are
\psi
. Using the Galois covering
C_{R}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, we attach to each
\xi\in A_{\psi}^{\vee}
a smooth
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-sheaf

\mathcal{Q}_{\xi}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
.
It is proved in
[
15
, Lemma 3.4]
that the
\ell
-adic cohomology group

H_{\rm c}^{1}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}},\mathcal{Q}_{\xi})

is one-dimensional.
Let
\Gal({\mathbb{F}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
denote the Galois group of the extension
{\mathbb{F}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Let
\tau_{R,\xi,q}\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}^{\times}

denote the eigenvalue of
the geometric Frobenius element
\mathrm{Fr}_{q}\in\Gal({\mathbb{F}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})

acting on
H_{\rm c}^{1}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}},\mathcal{Q}_{\xi})
.
The following formula is obtained in
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
:

L_{\overline{C}_{R}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(T)\coloneqq\det(1-\mathrm{Fr}_{q}T\mid H^{1}(\overline{C}_{R,{\mathbb{F}}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}))=\prod_{\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\}}\prod_{\xi\in A_{\psi}^{\vee}}(1-\tau_{R,\xi,q}T).

Therefore, a detailed analysis of the sheaf
\mathcal{Q}_{\xi}
yields an explicit formula for the eigenvalue
\tau_{R,\xi,q}
, and consequently, for the
L
-polynomial.

When
p_{0}
is odd, we construct in
[
15
, A.2]
an intermediate curve
C
, defined by the equation
y^{p}-y=c_{A}x^{2}
with
c_{A}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
being explicitly computable, that lies between the covering
C_{R}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}=C_{R}/A
, i.e., we have a factorization
C_{R}\to C\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
. Using this factorization, it is shown in loc. cit. that
\mathcal{Q}_{\xi}
is isomorphic to an Artin–Schreier sheaf of the form
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(c_{A}x^{2}+b_{\xi}x)
, where
b_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is a constant determined by
\xi
. As a consequence of the Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula, the eigenvalue
\tau_{R,\xi,q}
is expressed in terms of a quadratic Gauss sum and a value of the additive character
\psi
in
[
15
, Corollary A.8]
.
However, the method described above applies only in the case of odd characteristic, because the construction of
C
relies on the fact that
A
is annihilated by
p_{0}
, which is false when
p_{0}=2
.
The aim of this paper is to develop a similar method that is applicable to the case of characteristic
2
, so that we obtain a formula for
\tau_{R,\xi,q}
in this remaining case.

Let us explain our results in more detail. From now on, we assume that
p_{0}=2
. In this case, we show that
C_{R}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
factors as

(1.1)

C_{R}\to C_{S}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},

where

is the van der Geer–van der Vlugt curve associated with

is Galois with Galois group

, where

denotes the ring of Witt vectors of length

. The appearance of

reflects the fact that

has nontrivial

-torsion.
We study the
\ell
-adic cohomology of

using a geometric construction inspired by the work of Deligne–Lusztig.

Using the

-covering

\mathcal{Q}_{\xi}
and
\tau_{R,\xi,q}
as follows. First, we note that (
1.1
) induces a surjection
A\to W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Since

is annihilated by
4
(Lemma
2.3
(4)) and

{\mathbb{Z}}/4{\mathbb{Z}}
-free as in Lemma

A\simeq W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})\times\overline{U}.

Here
\overline{U}
denotes the kernel of

, which is naturally an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-vector space of dimension
e-1
.

We fix such an isomorphism in (
1.2
).
Then the character
\xi\in A^{\vee}_{\psi}
can be written as
\xi=\xi_{W_{2}}\boxtimes\xi_{\overline{U}}

for some characters
\xi_{W_{2}}\in W_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee}
and
\xi_{\overline{U}}\in\overline{U}^{\vee}
.
Let

\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{W_{2}}}(z,w)
denote the invertible

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-sheaf on

W_{2,{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}

defined by the Lang torsor

W_{2,{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\to W_{2,{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\ (x,y)\mapsto(x^{p},y^{p})-(x,y)

\xi_{W_{2}}\in W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})

2
.
For a morphism

\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{W_{2}}}(f(s),g(s))

for its pull-back by

.
Using this notation, we can now state our result on

, which is proved in Proposition

, as follows:

\mathcal{Q}_{\xi}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{W_{2}}}(s,\alpha s^{2}+\beta_{\xi}s).

Here,
\alpha

whose definitions are given after Theorem

This isomorphism together with the computation of the ranks of the cohomology groups of

(see Lemma

) gives us the following result on
\tau_{R,\xi,q}
.
Fix a faithful character

\xi_{2}\colon W_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}^{\times}

\sqrt{-1}\coloneqq\xi_{2}(1,0)\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}^{\times}

is isomorphic to

is a primitive

We set

The first main theorem of this paper gives an explicit formula for the Frobenius eigenvalue

associated with a van der Geer–van der Vlugt curve in terms of additive characters of the Witt vector group

Theorem 1.1

(see Theorem

)
.

Let

defined below.

Then we have

\tau_{R,\xi,q}=\xi_{q}(c_{R,\xi},0)^{-1}\cdot(-1-\sqrt{-1})^{[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{2}]},

where we regard

.

The definitions of
\alpha
,
\beta_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and

are as follows:

•

Let

.

•

Let

, which naturally has a structure of an

-vector space of dimension

. The vector space

is naturally

.
By Lemma

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \overline{A}=\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}\mid F_{A}(x)=0\},\\

Via the isomorphism (

Remark 1.4
.

Our approach in this paper is geometric in nature and based on studying the quotients of van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves
by abelian subgroups of the Heisenberg groups.
Using
quadratic
\ell
-adic sheaves

introduced by the second author
[
14
]
,
it is possible to study the Frobenius eigenvalues
by cohomological methods.
We plan to pursue the cohomological techniques in a subsequent paper.

In
[
1
]
, Blache and Pierre give a factorization of the
L
-functions of the van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves (which they refer to as quadratic Artin–Schreier curves) using exponential sums. Our approach differs from theirs and
provides a geometric and representation-theoretic perspective, based on
\ell
-adic cohomology.

In
[
10
, Section 9.2]
,
Katz and Tiep study in detail certain local systems constructed using Witt vectors of length two. In a special case, their local systems are closely related to those considered in this article. See Remark
3.15
for details.

2.1.
The van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves associated with
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomials

A polynomial

is called
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized
if it is additive,
that is,
f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)
, and satisfies
f(ax)=af(x)
for all
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
Such a polynomial can be written as

, with

.
Let

\mathscr{A}\coloneqq\bigg\{\sum_{i=0}^{e}c_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]\ \bigg|\ e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\bigg\}

denote the set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized
polynomials with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
For
f(x),g(x)\in\mathscr{A}
, define

(f\circ g)(x)\coloneqq f(g(x))\in\mathscr{A}.

Together with the usual addition, this gives
\mathscr{A}
the structure of a ring under composition.

For
f(x)\in\mathscr{A}
, we define

\Ker f\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{F}\mid f(x)=0\}
,
which is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space. If
f(x)
has degree
p^{e}
and is separable, then it has dimension
e
.

Let
R(x)\in\mathscr{A}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial of degree
p^{e}
with
e\geq 1
.
We define
the smooth affine curve
C_{R}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by the equation

y^{p}-y=xR(x).

Its smooth compactification
\overline{C}_{R}
is a smooth projective curve of genus

;
see
[

, Proposition 6.4.1 (e)]
.
The curves

and
\overline{C}_{R}
are called the
van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves

over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
associated with
R(x)
.

Our main aim in this section is to introduce a finite étale Galois covering

F_{A}\circ\phi\colon C_{R}\to{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}

and smooth
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-sheaves

on
{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
,
and decompose the

-adic étale cohomology of

;
see Proposition

.

2.2.
Heisenberg groups and their maximal abelian subgroups

Let

R(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in\mathscr{A}

be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial with
e\geq 1
and
a_{e}\neq 0
.
The curve
C_{R}
carries an action by a certain Heisenberg group
H_{R}
. We recall this fact briefly.

We introduce the polynomials

E_{R}(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]

and
f_{R}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x,y]
defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle E_{R}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq R(x)^{p^{e}}+\sum_{i=0}^{e}(a_{i}x)^{p^{e-i}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle f_{R}(x,y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{e-i-1}(a_{i}x^{p^{i}}y)^{p^{j}}+(xR(y))^{p^{i}}\right).

The polynomial
f_{R}(x,y)
is the unique polynomial
satisfying
f_{R}(0,0)=0
and

(2.3)

f_{R}(x,y)^{p}-f_{R}(x,y)=-x^{p^{e}}E_{R}(y)+xR(y)+yR(x).

It follows that the polynomial

(2.4)

\omega_{R}(x,y)\coloneqq f_{R}(x,y)-f_{R}(y,x)

satisfies
\omega_{R}(0,0)=0
and

(2.5)

\omega_{R}(x,y)^{p}-\omega_{R}(x,y)=y^{p^{e}}E_{R}(x)-x^{p^{e}}E_{R}(y).

We set

V_{R}\coloneqq\Ker E_{R},

which is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-vector space
of dimension

3.2.
The Heisenberg group

Here we explicitly calculate the Heisenberg group

for the binomial

By (

By (

is given by

H_{S}=\{(a,b)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}\times{\mathbb{F}}\mid b^{p}+b=aS(a)=a^{p+1}+a_{0}a^{2}\}

(a,b)\cdot(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a+a^{\prime},\,b+b^{\prime}+aa^{\prime p}).

Let

For

,

\omega_{S}(x\xi,y\xi)=(x^{p}y+xy^{p})\xi^{p+1}=(xy+xy)\xi^{p+1}=0

is a maximal totally
isotropic subspace with respect to
\omega_{S}
.
Let
\pi_{S}\colon H_{S}\to V_{S},\ (a,b)\mapsto a
.
We take
\xi=1
and put

(3.3)

A_{S}\coloneqq\pi_{S}^{-1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\{(a,b)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\times{\mathbb{F}}\mid b^{p}+b=(1+a_{0})a^{2}\},

(cf. Example

Lemma 3.5
.

For

,
we have an isomorphism of abelian groups

f_{\eta}\colon A_{S}\overset{\sim}{\to}W_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),\quad(a,b)\mapsto(a,\,b+a^{2}\eta).

Proof.

Let

.
From

is well-defined.
Since

is injective and

4.1.
Maps between van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves

In this subsection, we consider the following situation.
Let
R_{1}(x),R_{2}(x),f(x)\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\{0\}

Suppose that

is separable and that there exists a polynomial

\Delta(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
such that

\Delta(0)=0
and

(4.1)

\Delta(x)^{p}+\Delta(x)=xR_{1}(x)+f(x)R_{2}(f(x)).

We define the polynomial
\delta(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x,y]

(4.2)

\delta(x,y)=\Delta(x+y)+\Delta(x)+\Delta(y)+f_{R_{1}}(x,y)+f_{R_{2}}(f(x),f(y)).

We assume
\deg R_{i}=p^{e_{i}}
with

Lemma 4.1
.

Let the notation and assumptions be as above.
We have
\delta(0,0)=0
and

(4.3)

\delta(x,y)^{p}+\delta(x,y)=x^{p^{e_{1}}}E_{R_{1}}(y)+f(x)^{p^{e_{2}}}E_{R_{2}}(f(y)).

Moreover, we have

\delta(x,y+z)=\delta(x,y)+\delta(x,z)

\delta(x+y,z)=\delta(x,z)+\delta(y,z)

\delta(ax,y)=a\delta(x,y)=\delta(x,ay)

Proof.

The assertion
\delta(0,0)=0
is clear

By using (

) and (

\displaystyle\delta(x,y)^{p}+\delta(x,y)

\displaystyle=(x+y)R_{1}(x+y)+f(x+y)R_{2}(f(x+y))

\displaystyle\quad+xR_{1}(x)+f(x)R_{2}(f(x))+yR_{1}(y)+f(y)R_{2}(f(y))

\displaystyle\quad+x^{p^{e_{1}}}E_{R_{1}}(y)+xR_{1}(y)+yR_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+f(x)^{p^{e_{2}}}E_{R_{2}}(f(y))+f(x)R_{2}(f(y))+f(y)R_{2}(f(x))

\displaystyle=x^{p^{e_{1}}}E_{R_{1}}(y)+f(x)^{p^{e_{2}}}E_{R_{2}}(f(y)).

Thus the equality (

\delta(x,y+z)=\delta(x,y)+\delta(x,z)

Since (

Hence,

is constant. Since

by

, we obtain

∎

6.
Maximal curves of the form

In this section, as an illustration of Theorem
3.13
,
we give
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{4}}
-maximal
van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves
associated with
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomials of the form
x^{p}+a_{0}x
. By taking finite quotients of these curves, we obtain more examples of maximal curves.

6.1.
Maximal and minimal curves

Here we briefly recall the notation on maximal and minimal curves.

Let
C
be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of genus
g(C)
.
By the Hasse–Weil inequality,
we have

\bigl||C({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|-(q+1)\bigr|\leq 2g(C)\sqrt{q}.

The curve
C
is said to be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal
if

|C({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|=q+1+2g(C)\sqrt{q}
.
Equivalently, its
L
-polynomial is

L_{C/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(T)=\det\bigl(1-\mathrm{Fr}_{q}\,T\mid H^{1}(C_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell})\bigr)=(1+\sqrt{q}\,T)^{2g(C)}.

In other words,
\mathrm{Fr}_{q}
acts on

H^{1}(C_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell})

as scalar multiplication by
-\sqrt{q}
.

Similarly,
C
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal
if

|C({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|=q+1-2g(C)\sqrt{q}
,
equivalently

L_{C/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(T)=(1-\sqrt{q}\,T)^{2g(C)},

which means that
\mathrm{Fr}_{q}
acts as scalar multiplication by
\sqrt{q}
on

H^{1}(C_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell})
.

Constructing explicit maximal (or minimal) curves is an interesting problem in number theory,
with potential applications in coding theory;
see
[
13
]
for more details.

6.2.
Construction of maximal curves

We write
p=2^{f_{0}}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

(1)

We have

{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}\subset\Ker\Tr_{p^{4}/p}=\{x^{p}+x\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{4}}\}.

(2)

Let
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}
and let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{4}}
satisfy

\alpha^{p}+\alpha=a+1
.

(i)

Then

\Tr_{p^{2}/p}(\alpha^{p^{2}+1})=(a+1)^{p+1}.

(ii)

Moreover,

\xi_{p^{4}}(\alpha,0)=(-1)^{\Tr_{p/2}(a^{p+1})+f_{0}}.

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
2.7

\Ker\Tr_{p^{4}/p}=\{x^{p}-x\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{4}}\}=\{x^{p}+x\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{4}}\},

since the characteristic is
2
.
Moreover, for

\Tr_{p^{4}/p}(y)=2\Tr_{p^{2}/p}(y)=0

(2) (i) We have

Thus, the difference
\Tr_{p^{2}/p}(\alpha^{p^{2}+1})-(a+1)^{p+1}

\alpha^{p^{3}+p}+\alpha^{p+1}+\alpha^{2p}+\alpha^{p^{2}+p}=\alpha^{p}(\alpha+\alpha^{p}+\alpha^{p^{2}}+\alpha^{p^{3}}).

Since
\alpha+\alpha^{p}=a+1\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}

\alpha^{p^{2}}+\alpha^{p^{3}}=a+1

The assertion follows.

(2) (ii) First we compute

\Tr_{p^{4}/p^{2}}(\alpha,0)=(\alpha,0)+(\alpha^{p^{2}},0)=(\alpha+\alpha^{p^{2}},\alpha^{p^{2}+1})=(a+a^{p},\alpha^{p^{2}+1}).

Applying

and using

\displaystyle\Tr_{p^{4}/p}(\alpha,0)

\displaystyle=\Tr_{p^{2}/p}(a+a^{p},\alpha^{p^{2}+1})

\displaystyle=(0,(a+a^{p})^{2}+\Tr_{p^{2}/p}(\alpha^{p^{2}+1}))=(0,(a+a^{p})^{2}+(a+1)^{p+1}).

Taking
\Tr_{p/2}
, we find

\Tr_{p^{4}/2}(\alpha,0)=(0,\Tr_{p/2}(a+a^{p}+(a+1)^{p+1}))=(0,\Tr_{p/2}(a^{p+1}+1)).

This proves the assertion, since
[{\mathbb{F}}_{p}:{\mathbb{F}}_{2}]=f_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 7.1

7.3

) and Proposition
4.10
). Thus the intermediate covering
C_{R}\to E_{\alpha,a,b}
is also an abelian covering. Let
G
denote its Galois group. It fits into a short exact sequence

0\to\overline{U}\to G\to\Ker\Tr_{(a,b)}\to 0.

By Lemma

Let
\xi_{2}\in W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})^{\vee}
be a faithful character and
define
\xi_{q}\coloneqq\xi_{2}\circ\Tr_{q/2}\in W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})^{\vee}
.
We also set
\sqrt{-1}\coloneqq\xi_{2}(1,0)
.
If
H_{R}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, the Frobenius eigenvalues of

H^{1}(\overline{C}_{R,{\mathbb{F}}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell})
are all equal to a single Frobenius eigenvalue of a supersingular elliptic curve.
We obtain the same conclusion as in Corollary
5.6
(1).

Corollary 8.4
.

We write
E_{\alpha}
for
E_{\alpha,1,0}
.
Assume
H_{R}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
. Then

\mathrm{Fr}_{q}
acts on

H^{1}(\overline{C}_{R,{\mathbb{F}}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell})

as scalar multiplication by
\xi_{q}(\alpha,0)^{-1}\cdot(-1-\sqrt{-1})^{[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{2}]}
.
In particular,
\overline{C}_{R}
is a supersingular curve.

Proof.

We have the finite étale morphism

C_{R}\to E_{\alpha}

by
Corollary
8.3
for
(a,b)=(1,0)
.
This morphism extends to a finite morphism

\overline{C}_{R}\to\overline{E}_{\alpha},

which induces an injection

H^{1}(\overline{E}_{\alpha,{\mathbb{F}}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell})\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\overline{C}_{R,{\mathbb{F}}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}).

Since
H_{R}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
and
Lemma
2.13
(3), the Frobenius
\mathrm{Fr}_{q}
acts on
H^{1}(\overline{C}_{R,{\mathbb{F}}},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell})
as scalar multiplication.
By Remark
3.14
, the scalar
equals

\xi_{q}(c,0)^{-1}\cdot G_{\xi_{q}}=\xi_{q}(\alpha,0)^{-1}\cdot(-1-\sqrt{-1})^{[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{2}]}

since
c=\alpha^{1/2}
and by Lemma
3.11
.
Since this scalar is
a root of unity times
\sqrt{q}
, the last claim follows.
∎

On extensivity of morphisms
Michael Hoefnagel, Emma Theart

Abstract
Extensivity of a category [ 2 ] may be described as a property of coproducts in the category, namely, that they are disjoint and universal. An alternative viewpoint is that it is a property of morphisms in a category. This paper explores this point of view through a natural notion of extensive and coextensive morphism. Through these notions, topics in universal algebra, such as the strict refinement and Fraser-Horn properties, take categorical form and thereby enjoy the benefits of categorical generalisation. On the other hand, the universal algebraic theory surrounding these topics inspire categorical results. One such result we establish in this paper is that a Barr-exact category ℂ \mathbb{C} is coextensive if and only if every split monomorphism in ℂ \mathbb{C} coextensive.

As a result of Hashimoto’s theorem

Every product projection in the category

3

Recall that a morphism

Proposition 3.3

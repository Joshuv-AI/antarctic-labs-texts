Dietary supplementation of phytosterols improves meat quality by increasing intramuscular fat, improving fatty acid composition and enhancing antioxidant capacity in Hu lambs
Authors: Guangyuan Tian, Xuanzi Zhang, Xiujie Gao, Xiaoyan Hao, Jianxin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Phytosterols (PS) play an important role in regulating lipid metabolism. This study aimed to investigate the effects of PS on intramuscular fat (IMF) deposition, fatty acid composition, meat quality, and antioxidant capacity in Hu lambs. Forty-eight 4-month-old male Hu lambs with similar body weight (23.90 ± 0.83 kg) were randomly divided into four groups (n = 12 per group): basal diets supplemented with 0 (Control), 500 (500PS), 1000 (1000PS), or 1500 mg PS/kg diet (1500PS) on a dry matter (DM) basis. After a 75-d feeding trial, feces, ruminal fluid, longissimus dorsi (LD) muscle and serum samples were collected for subsequent analysis. The results showed that all PS-supplemented groups had a significantly higher average daily gain than the Control group during the 26–50-d period (P = 0.002). The digestibility of DM, organic matter (OM), crude protein (CP), and neutral detergent fiber (NDF) was significantly affected by PS supplementation (P < 0.05). Compared with the Control group, ruminal pH significantly decreased, while total volatile fatty acids (VFA) and propionate concentrations significantly increased in the 500PS group, which also had a significantly lower acetate-to-propionate ratio (P < 0.05). Meat quality was improved with PS supplementation. Specifically, pH24 h significantly increased in 500PS group, yellowness significantly decreased in 500PS group, cooking loss and water loss significantly reduced in 500PS or 1500PS groups compared with Control group (P < 0.05); shear force decreased linearly and quadratically with increasing PS supplementation (P < 0.05). Additionally, compared with Control group, IMF and CP contents significantly increased only in 500PS group in LD muscle (P < 0.05). The GR value, an indicator of carcass fatness, exhibited a quadratic decreasing trend with increasing PS supplementation (P = 0.027). The activities of fatty acid synthase (FAS) in all PS-supplemented groups and acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC) in 500PS group in LD muscle significantly increased compared with Control group (P < 0.05). The mRNA relative expression levels of FABP4, FAS, PPARγ, ACC, SREBP-1, and C/EBPα significantly upregulated in LD muscle with PS supplementation (P < 0.05). Compared with Control group, C11:0 and C18:0 contents in all PS-supplemented groups in LD muscle significantly decreased (P < 0.05), while the 500PS group significantly increased beneficial fatty acid contents including C18:1, C18:2, C18:3n3, C22:6n3, monounsaturated fatty acid (MUFA), and polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) (P < 0.05). Compared with Control group, serum and muscle total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) significantly elevated in the 500PS group, serum superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity significantly increased in the 1000PS group, and serum malondialdehyde (MDA) content was significantly reduced in the 500PS group, while muscle MDA content was significantly reduced in all PS-supplemented groups (P < 0.05). In summary, dietary PS supplementation improved growth performance, nutrient digestibility, ruminal fermentation, meat quality, and antioxidant capacity of Hu lambs. Phytosterols supplementation could increase IMF content in the LD muscle, promote lipid droplet accumulation, and optimize fatty acid composition by upregulating lipid metabolism-related genes.

Introduction

In recent years, China has seen a substantial rise in animal product consumption, attributed to upgraded dietary patterns and growing household income. Furthermore, the average yearly growth rates of the consumption of herbivorous animal products have surpassed that of grain-based animal products (Long et al., 2024). The gradual increase in beef and mutton consumption have increased attention toward food safety and health issues. Intramuscular fat (IMF), which is positively correlated with meat flavor, tenderness, and juiciness (Zhang et al., 2022), is a key marker of meat quality. Unsaturated fatty acids such as oleic acid (C18:1) and conjugated linoleic acid (CLA), which have anti-obesity and cardioprotective properties, are more abundant in mutton with high IMF content (Ding et al., 2024; Pewan et al., 2020). Lipid oxidation is the primary cause of meat discoloration, nutritional loss, and rancidity (Tatiyaborworntham et al., 2022). Oxidative stress rises and meat quality deteriorates, especially under intensive feeding systems and single-feed ration regimens (Song et al., 2023). Therefore, the optimization of fatty acid composition, increased muscle fat content and antioxidant capacity, and production of high-quality mutton constitute key production issues.

Phytosterols (PS), which are essential components of the plant cell membrane, are mainly obtained from nuts, vegetable oils, grains, fruits, and vegetables (Jain and Bathla, 2015) and are similar to animal cholesterol. With the increasing prevalence of cardiovascular disorders, the lipid-lowering effects of PS have garnered significant interest. Research has demonstrated that PS not only lowers low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) (Berger et al., 2004), but also influences lipid homeostasis through various mechanisms, including the control of intestinal flora, nuclear receptors, and lipoprotein metabolic enzymes (Dumolt et al., 2017; Li et al., 2022a). According to Awad et al. (2000), PS inhibits the proliferation and triglyceride accumulation of 3T3-L1 cells (mouse pre-adipocytes). Feng et al. (2018) reported that PS alleviates hepatic steatosis by downregulating the expression of FAS and HMGCR genes (The full names of the abbreviations of all genes can be found in Table S1). Moreover, PS can also exert antioxidant effects by scavenging free radicals, activating antioxidant enzymes, and reducing reactive oxygen species production, as reported in numerous studies (EI Omari et al., 2024; Vezza et al., 2020).

Most previous studies on PS have focused on poultry. According to Ding et al. (2021), dietary PS supplementation in white-feathered broilers improves meat quality by increasing the concentrations of taste-related amino acids and unsaturated fatty acids in the pectoralis muscle. Previous research indicated that maternal dietary supplementation of PS improves lipid metabolism and promotes muscle growth in avian offspring (Wang et al., 2020). Despite this, information regarding its use in ruminants is limited. Consequently, this study was designed to test the hypothesis that dietary supplementation with PS can improve meat quality by optimizing fat deposition, increasing IMF content, improving fatty acid composition, and enhancing the antioxidant capacity of Hu lambs during the finishing stage. This study investigated the effects of PS on IMF deposition, fatty acid profiles, meat quality traits, and antioxidant status of Hu lambs, providing a theoretical basis and practical reference for the application of PS in lamb finishing production.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

This experiment was approved by the Animal Welfare Committee of Shanxi Agricultural University (SXAU-EAW-2024HS.FF.006003320).

Materials

Phytosterols (purity 956.8 g/kg, containing 433.3 g/kg β-sitosterol, 274.5 g/kg campesterol, 234.9 g/kg stigmasterol, 7.5 g/kg rapeseed sterols, and 6.6 g/kg other sterols) was purchased from Shaanxi Healthful Bioengineering Co., Ltd. (Xi'an, Shaanxi, China).

Design of animal experiment

This experiment was conducted from June to October 2024 at Hongyu Farming and Animal Husbandry Co., Ltd. (Shuozhou, Shanxi, China). Forty-eight 4-month-old Hu sheep male lambs with similar body weight (23.90 ± 0.83 kg) were randomly divided into four groups (n = 12 in each group): basal diets supplemented with 0 (Control), 500 (500PS), 1000 (1000PS), and 1500 mg PS/kg diet (1500PS) on a dry matter (DM) basis. The basal diet was formulated according to the nutrient requirements for finishing male lambs at 4 months of age, as described by the National Research Council (2007), as a pellet diet with a concentrate: forage ratio of 60:40 (Table 1). Each lamb was individually reared in a single stall, which was cleaned and disinfected prior to the experiment. All lambs had ad libitum access to drinking water. The experimental period lasted 90 d, with the first 15 d for adaptation to the basal diet and environment, followed by a 75-d normal feeding period, during which feeding was conducted at 07:00 and 17:00 daily. The PS dose for each lamb was weighed with an analytical balance according to feed intake before the morning feeding and was given to the lambs in a single session. After the 90-d formal feeding trial, six lambs per group were randomly selected and transferred to individual metabolic cages (120 cm × 75 cm) for fecal sample collection. Chemical composition analysis of feed and feces

Feed and feces samples were dried in an oven at 65 °C for 48 h, ground through a 1-mm sieve using a Cyclotec mill (CT 1093, FOSS Analytical A/S, Hillerød, Zealand, Denmark). Nutrient components were quantified as follows: DM, ash and ether extract (EE) were determined according to method 930.15, method 942.05, and method 920.39 (AOAC, 2006), respectively. Neutral detergent fiber (NDF) and acid detergent fiber (ADF) were assayed using an Ankom A200 fiber analyzer (Ankom Technology, Macedon, NY, USA) following the procedure described by Van Soest et al. (1991). Crude protein (CP) in feed and feces was analyzed with a Kjeldahl nitrogen analyzer (Model K9860, Haineng Future Technology Group Co., Ltd., Jinan, Shandong, China). Organic matter (OM) content was calculated as the difference between 100 and the ash content. Dietary calcium and phosphorus contents were analyzed according to method 968.08 and method 965.17 (AOAC, 2006), respectively. Nutrient digestibility was calculated as reported by Beckman and Weiss (2005), using the following formula:

Nutrient digestibility (%) = [(Intake of nutrient - Fecal excretion of nutrient)/Intake of nutrient] × 100.

Metabolizable energy (ME) of feed was calculated based on standard of NY/T 816-2021 (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of the People’s Republic of China, 2021). The formula is as follows:ME=0.046+0.820×(17.211–0.135×NDF%).

Collection and determination of ruminal fluid

On d 100 following the metabolic assay, ruminal fluid was sampled from each lamb via oral intubation 3 h after morning feeding using a rumen tube. The collected rumen fluid was filtered through four layers of gauze, then packed into centrifuge tubes and stored at −80 °C. Real-time pH quantification was performed using a calibrated digital pH analyzer (PHS-3S, INESA Scientific Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). Ruminal fluid volatile fatty acid (VFA) concentrations were analyzed using gas chromatography (GC 7890B, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The chromatographic conditions were set as follows: Agilent HP-5 capillary column (30 m × 0.32 mm × 0.25 μm); detector temperature, 300 °C; hydrogen flow rate, 50 mL/min; nitrogen flow rate, 25 mL/min; injection volume, 0.4 μL.

Slaughter, sample collection, and determination of meat quality

Blood was collected from every sheep by jugular vein puncture at the end of the normal feeding period, and serum was collected by centrifugation at 2000 × g and stored at −20 °C. A total of 24 sheep (six replicates per group) were randomly selected and slaughtered by jugular vein exsanguination. Next, the solid organs and adipose tissues were weighed, and the weights of carcass, meat, and bone were recorded. The GR value (an indicator of carcass fat) was determined by measuring the thickness of the adipose tissue 11 cm from the midline between the 12th and 13th ribs using a Vernier caliper. Backfat thickness was similarly measured at the 12th and 13th ribs, near the spine, at three-quarters of the eye muscle length. The rumen, reticulum, omasum, and abomasum were flushed with water and weighed. Sheep carcasses were cut between the 12th and 13th ribs. Carcasses were split between the 12th and 13th ribs. The longissimus dorsi (LD) muscle from the left side of the carcass was collected and divided into two main sections. The first section was stored at −20 °C for chemical composition and fatty acid analyses. The remaining section was further divided, with one piece was fixed in paraformaldehyde, and the other was immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen for RNA extraction. Similarly, the right LD muscle was collected for the evaluation of pH, meat color, shear force, cooking loss, and water loss. Muscle pH was recorded at 45 min and 24 h post-mortem using a portable pH meter (pH-Stat, pH-Stat, SFK Technology A/S, Søborg, Denmark). Meat color, including lightness (L∗), redness (a∗), and yellowness (b∗), were evaluated using a muscle color analyzer (OPTO-STAR, MATTHAUS GmbH, Erlangen, Bavaria, Germany) on LD samples trimmed to 2 cm thickness and 3 cm diameter. Shear force was determined by the method described by Li et al. (2022b). Briefly, LD muscle samples were heated in an 80 °C constant-temperature water bath until the center temperature reached 70 °C, then each sample was divided into six cylindrical pieces (1.27 cm in diameter) and cut using a Warner-Bratzler V-shaped blade (GR-151, G-R Manufacturing Company, Manhattan, KS, USA). For cooking loss analysis, three pieces of 50 g LD muscle were placed into a cooking bag and boiled in a water bath until the center temperature reached 70 °C. The samples were removed, surface water wiped off, and cooled at 4 °C for 30 min before being weighed and recorded. To determine water loss, the LD samples were weighed and placed between 36 layers of filter papers (18 layers above and below) and compressed on a texture analyzer (TA-SR01, Laibo Texture Instrument Co., Ltd., Jinan, Shandong, China) using a cylindrical probe with a 35-kg load transducer. After 5 min of compression, water loss was calculated based on the weight difference.

Chemical composition analysis of LD muscle

A subsample of the left LD muscle was weighed and freeze-dried for 72 h. Moisture content was calculated based on the weight difference before and after freeze-drying. Crude protein content in the LD muscle was determined according to method 984.13 (AOAC, 2006). Briefly, 0.5 g of lyophilized LD muscle sample was accurately weighed into a digestion tube, mixed with 10 mL of concentrated sulfuric acid and 6 g of catalyst (copper sulfate: potassium sulfate = 3:1, w/w), and digested at high temperature in a digestion furnace for 4 h. Subsequent nitrogen analysis was performed using a Kjeldahl nitrogen analyzer (Model K9860, Haineng Future Technology Group Co., Ltd., Jinan, Shandong, China). Intramuscular fat content was determined by Soxhlet extraction method. Briefly, a pre-weighed LD muscle subsample was freeze-dried, enclosed in a fat-free filter paper thimble, and extracted with diethyl ether for 8 h using a Soxhlet extractor (Model ZF34500, Bomex Glass Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Finally, IMF content was calculated according to the protocol described by Lee et al. (2015). The calculation formula is as follows:

IMF (fresh-meat basis, %) = (Weight of IMF/Weight of LD muscle) × 100.

Lipid metabolism enzyme activities determination in LD muscle

A total of 500 mg of LD muscle tissue was homogenized in pre-cooled phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The homogenate was centrifuged at 2000 × g for 20 min, and the resulting supernatant was collected for subsequent measurements. The levels of triglyceride hydrolase (TGH; ml037758), hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL; ml036706), lipoprotein lipase (LPL; ml025647), fatty acid synthase (FAS; ml036686), and acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC; ml036689) were determined using commercial ELISA kits (Shanghai Enzyme Linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China).

Oil red O staining of LD muscle

Longissimus dorsi muscle tissue samples were dehydrated via sequential immersion in 15% sucrose solution for 1 to 2 d, followed by 30% sucrose solution for another 1 to 2 d. After dehydration, the target tissue regions were carefully removed from the 30% sucrose solution, and excess surface liquid was blotted dry with filter paper. A freezing microtome (HM525 NX, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was pre-cooled and maintained at a temperature of −18 to −22 °C. An adequate amount of optimal cutting temperature (OCT) compound was applied to the microtome stage. Once the OCT compound had solidified, the disc containing the embedded OCT block was mounted onto a sample holder. Sectioning was initiated with an initial trimming thickness of 30 μm. Upon exposure of the intact tissue plane, the section thickness was adjusted to 8 μm for final sectioning. The obtained sections were stained with Oil Red O, then placed on a staining rack and dried in a 37 °C oven for 2 h. Stained sections were subsequently imaged using a light microscope (NIKON-E100, Nikon Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) for further qualitative and quantitative analysis.

Fatty acid determination in LD muscle

The lipids were extracted from LD muscle using a chloroform/methanol mixture. Afterwards, 5 mL n-hexane and 3 mL 0.5 mol/L CH3OH-KOH solution were added to the obtained lipid extract, and the mixture was thoroughly shaken and blended. Lipid methylation was performed by incubating the mixture in an oven at 60 °C for 30 min. The supernatant was subsequently collected and centrifuged at 3500 × g for 10 min, then subjected to gas chromatographic analysis on an Agilent GC6890 instrument (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Chromatographic parameters were set as follows: Agilent DB-23 capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm); detector temperature, 280 °C; H2 flow rate, 30 mL/min; N2 flow rate, 30 mL/min; injection volume, 1 μL.

Antioxidant enzyme activities determination in serum and LD muscle

The serum and LD muscle levels of total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC; ml092653), superoxide dismutase (SOD; ml092619), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px; ml076446), catalase (CAT; ml076324), and malondialdehyde (MDA; mlsh0387) were determined using the corresponding kits (Shanghai Enzyme Linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China).

Statistical analyses

Data were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance using SPSS 26.0 (International Business Machines Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA), and the results were expressed as the arithmetic mean and standard error of the mean (SEM). The following statistical model is used:Yij = μ + Ti + Lj + eij,where μ is the overall mean; Ti is the fixed effect of treatment (i = 1-4); Lj is the random effect of lamb (j = 1-12); and eij is the residual error. Linear and quadratic effects were analyzed by orthogonal polynomial contrast, with statistical significance declared at P < 0.05 and trends noted at 0.05 ≤ P < 0.10.

Nutrient digestibility and ruminal fermentation

The results of nutrient digestibility and ruminal fermentation are presented in Table 4. Digestibility of DM, OM, and CP exhibited quadratic responses (P < 0.05), with the maximum values observed in the 500PS group. Additionally, NDF digestibility increased in both linear and quadratic manners (P < 0.05) across PS supplementation groups. Antioxidative enzyme activities in serum and LD muscle

As presented in Table 9, dietary PS supplementation had differential effects on antioxidant parameters. The 500PS group exhibited a significant enhancement in T-AOC in both serum (P = 0.009) and LD muscle (P = 0.002), along with a marked reduction in serum MDA levels compared with Control group (P = 0.048). Discussion

In the present study, dietary PS significantly increased the average daily gain from d 26 to 50, with a trend toward increased dry matter intake, which indicates that PS supplementation for lambs could promote growth. In the 51 to 75 d supplementation phase, both the average daily gain and feed efficiency showed significant quadratic response. This indicated that the regulatory effect of PS on feeding behavior became more significant with extended supplementation durations. The effect of PS peaked at the appropriate dose, and the benefit of a high dose may not increase, or could even decrease slightly. Ding et al. (2021) found that PS can significantly increase the ratio of villus height to crypt depth in the duodenum and jejunum and increase the intestinal absorption area, to thereby improve the efficiency of nutrient digestion and absorption. The addition of PS to piglet diets improved the gut morphology of the duodenum and jejunum (Hu et al., 2017). Phytosterols supplementation also increased the activities of pancreatic and intestinal heavy protease, lipase, and amylase, and enhanced the ability to digest nutrients. In this study, the increased digestibility of DM, OM, CP, and NDF further verified the growth-promoting effects of PS. The relatively high values in the 500PS group suggested that this level of PS supplementation potentially promoted a more efficient usage of feed components. Higher PS doses did not significantly improve nutrient digestibility. This result is also consistent with a study that was conducted by Yuan et al. (2021), which showed that low and medium doses of PS in broiler diets had better effects on nutrient digestibility rates. Carcass and meat weight showed a quadratic increase with elevated PS supplementation levels, which is consistent with the later-stages growth performance trend. Thus, PS effectively promoted muscle deposition. The increase in rumen weight reflected the positive effect of PS on rumen development, which indicated improved fiber digestion. In this study, the addition of PS significantly increased the propionic acid content and decreased the acetate-to-propionate ratio. The rumen fermentation type shifted from acetic acid to propionic acid, which improved the energy utilization rate of Hu sheep, and this finding is consistent with the results of Lv et al. (2022). Total VFA content was positively correlated with OM digestibility (Wang et al., 2023), is consistent with the result of the present experiment. The lower pH in the 500PS group was consistent with the increased VFA production, as higher VFA concentrations resulted in lower ruminal pH (Rabelo et al., 2003). Previous research has suggested that PS supplementation of ruminant diets can improve the negative energy balance of perinatal dairy cows (Gao et al., 2024; Lv et al., 2022) and alleviate ruminal acidosis in Hu sheep (Xia et al., 2020) by regulating the ruminal flora. Thus, the increased VFA concentration and nutrient digestibility may be attributed to the improvement of ruminal microbes by PS.

The pH of the LD muscle is primarily influenced by lactic acid that is generated through post-mortem glycolysis (Lizbeth et al., 2025). The significant increase in the pH24 h value observed in the 500PS group in this study suggests that dietary PS supplementation may attenuate lactic acid production by modulating post–slaughter glycolytic processes. Meat color is a critical visual indicator of freshness and safety for consumers (Liu et al., 2022), as determined mainly by myoglobin content and its oxidation state within the muscle (Chelopo et al., 2025). Although dietary PS supplementation exerted no significant effect on the a∗ or L∗ of LD muscle, the addition of 500 mg/kg PS significantly reduced b∗, which was attributed to the PS-mediated inhibition of yellow pigment accumulation. Dietary PS supplementation significantly reduced both cooking loss and water loss in the LD muscle, and this indicated a positive impact on the water-holding capacity. This improvement was consistent with the observed pH shift. Elevated pH moves muscle proteins away from their isoelectric point, enhances protein hydration, and stabilizes the spatial structure to improve tenderness and juiciness (Oksbjerg et al., 2019). Shear force, a key indicator of tenderness wherein lower values denoting greater tenderness and ease of chewing (Silva et al., 2015), decreased with PS supplementation. Consistent improvements in pH, water-holding capacity, and shear force collectively validated the dietary PS-induced enhancement of LD muscle tenderness. These findings are corroborated by those of Ding et al. (2021), who reported decreased water loss and shear stress along with increased pH in white-feathered broilers with PS. Nutrient analysis showed that dietary PS increased the CP and IMF contents in the LD muscle. Phytosterols supplementation of broiler diets upregulated the protein-synthesis genes, such as mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), S6 kinase 1 (S6K1), and eukaryotic initiation factor 4E (elF4E), after PS supplementation in broiler diets (Naji et al., 2014). Similarly, Liu et al. (2021) discovered that PS activated the mTOR signaling pathway to promote milk protein synthesis. Therefore, in the present study, PS may have promoted muscle protein synthesis via mTOR signaling-pathway activation. The GR values are an indicator of carcass content, which encompasses the subcutaneous, visceral, and IMF. Excessive carcass fat can reduce carcass utilization (Rhoades et al., 2007). In the present study, dietary PS significantly reduced the overall carcass fat content, with a tendency toward decreased back fat, whereas the IMF content increased. The PS-induced differential regulation of different fat tissues is potentially attributed to energy redistribution. In this study, dietary PS supplementation altered rumen fermentation patterns and improved energy utilization. Moreover, a shift in rumen fermentation patterns can affected the utilization of substrates (fatty acids) in fat synthesis, and thereby influenced lipid deposition in different fat tissues in ruminants (Lancaster et al., 2015). Furthermore, PS significantly increased the lipid droplet area in the LD muscle and upregulated the expression of genes related to lipid metabolism. Fatty acid-binding protein 4 expression increases during muscle development and positively correlates with IMF deposition (Wang et al., 2015). Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma participates in lipogenesis and lipid droplet storage in muscle and regulates ACC gene expression and IMF content (Zhang et al., 2025). CCAAT-enhancer-binding protein alpha is a key transcription factor in adipocyte differentiation, that can induce PPARγ expression, and PPARγ can in turn enhance the transcriptional activity of C/EBPα in a feedback loop to drive adipocyte differentiation (Rosen et al., 2002). Sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1 encodes a key transcription factor that is involved in lipid metabolism that promotes fatty acid synthesis by activating ACC and FAS gene expression (Stuart et al., 2003). In this study, PS upregulated the expression of FABP4, PPARγ, C/EBPα, and SREBP-1 genes, and thereby increased intramuscular fat content and lipid droplet accumulation. Liu et al. (2021) reported that β-sitosterol promoted milk fat production by upregulating PPARγ and SREBP-1 expression, which is consistent with the present results. Furthermore, FAS and ACC activities increased in this study, and their gene expression was upregulated. Acetyl CoA carboxylase catalyzes the conversion of acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA, and FAS carries out subsequent elongation reactions for fatty acid production (Foko et al., 2023). Mitochondria are the primary sites of fatty acid β-oxidation in skeletal muscle and generate ATP through oxidative phosphorylation (Alexis et al., 2017). Moreover, dietary energy levels can also increase the expression of genes related to lipid metabolism (Zhang et al., 2025). Dietary PS supplementation of broilers diets can increase mtDNA levels in the pectoral muscles (Cheng et al., 2019). Therefore, PS may upregulate lipid metabolism-related genes by enhancing mitochondrial function.

Dietary PS significantly altered the fatty acid profile of LD muscle toward a potentially healthier composition that was characterized by reduced SFA, increased MUFA, and substantially elevated levels of nutritionally important PUFA, which is consistent with the results of Ding et al. (2021) in white-feathered broilers. The most pronounced effect was a reduction in SFA content. Both the 500 and 1000 mg/kg PS supplementation groups exhibited significantly lower total SFA levels than the control group. This reduction was underpinned by significant decreases in specific SFAs, notably C11:0 and C18:0, in the PS-supplemented groups. Stearic acid reduction is particularly relevant because it is a major SFA in muscle lipids. Conversely, PS supplementation significantly increased MUFA content. Increases were observed in specific MUFAs, including C15:1, C18:1, and C22:1. However, C18:1 is the predominant MUFA in meat and is highly desirable owing to its association with improved lipid stability and potential health benefits (Mensink, 2016). Previous studies have shown that the delta-9 desaturase enzymes (SCD) can catalyze the conversion of C18:0 into C18:1 (Terry and Hay, 2024; Vessby et al., 2013). Therefore, dietary PS might enhance the activity of SCD enzymes to thereby increase the total MUFA content. The decrease in C18:0 coupled with an increase in C18:1, strongly supports this potential mechanism of action. Furthermore, the 500 mg/kg PS group demonstrated beneficial effects on polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), with significant increases in C18:2, C18:3n6, C18:3n3, C22:6n3, and consequently, the total PUFA content. The elevation in essential fatty acids (C18:2 and C18:3n3) and their long-chain derivatives, especially the nutritionally critical C22:6n3, is notable. Therefore, PS supplementation, particularly 500 mg/kg, may influence pathways beyond desaturation to potentially affect PUFA elongation and desaturation (e.g., delta-5 and delta-6 desaturases) (Terry and Hay, 2024; Xiang et al., 2006). The increase in both n-6 (C18:3n6) and n-3 (C18:3n3, C18:6n3) PUFA suggests broad stimulation of PUFA metabolism or absorption/utilization.

Lipid oxidation in lambs negatively affects flavor, color, and nutritional value. This process involves a free-radical chain reaction of unsaturated fatty acids that ultimately leads to the formation of oxidation products with adverse health effects (Bayram et al., 2023; Gray and Monahan, 1992). Several studies have demonstrated that feed additives play an important role in improving the lipid oxidation stability of mutton. For instance, dietary α-tocopherol supplementation improved the oxidative stability and color stability of lamb meat (Leal et al., 2020). Similarly, dietary α-lipoic acid supplementation enhanced the color stability of the biceps femoris muscle in lambs, decreased the MDA content, and increased the myoglobin (Mb) content (Luo et al., 2022). In the present study, dietary PS increased T-AOC and decreased MDA content in both serum and LD muscle. The T-AOC reflects the body's ability to scavenge free radicals, whereas MDA assesses the extent of lipid peroxidation. Previous research has shown that PS increases the expression of antioxidant enzymes by activating the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2)/Heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) pathway (Shen et al., 2024), reduces oxidative stress by inhibiting the nuclear factor -kappaB inhibitor alpha (NF-κB) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) inflammatory pathways (El Omari et al., 2024), stabilizes mitochondrial membrane potential, increases ATP production, and reduces ROS production (Vezza et al., 2020). However, the specific underlying mechanisms warrant further investigation.

Conclusion

In summary, the addition of PS to the diet of Hu lambs improved growth performance, nutrient digestibility, ruminal fermentation, meat quality, and antioxidant capacity. Phytosterols can increase IMF content in the LD muscle, promote lipid droplet accumulation, and improve fatty acid composition by upregulating lipid metabolism-related genes.

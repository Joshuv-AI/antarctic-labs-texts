Exploring Psychopathology with Brain Fingerprinting Technique
Authors: Anand Pratap Singh, Aakriti Varshney
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the exploration of psychopathology, the most discussed anecdote which always remained contradictory was the notion of ‘functional vs biological’ perspective of psychopathology. With the same anecdote, the present study attempted to reflect the clarity on the issue by taking a paradigmatic move in exploring the psychopathology with the help of Brain Fingerprinting Technique (BFT). BFT computes a mathematical and statistical algorithm for determination of information through presentation of stimuli. This presentation of stimuli leads to generation of P300-MERMER brainwave to determine the information. The same argument of real vs virtual experiential contexts pertaining to certain psychiatric conditions was applied in the present study. With the help of data analysis, it was proven that no biological evidence of fingerprinting with respect to delusional beliefs, perception of auditory stimuli in hallucinatory behaviour and real loss of information in case of dissociative amnesia was observed in the brain. Rather, in first two conditions the mathematical and statistical algorithm computed ‘information absent’ and in third condition it computed as ‘information present’ respectively. The above observations helped in substantiating the diagnoses of the patients with scientific sanctity and physiological correlates. The present study could be able to expand the horizon of applications of BFT in few of the psychiatric conditions such as delusional disorders, hallucinatory behaviours, and dissociative amnesia.

Introduction

The phenomenology of psychopathology and its manifestation has always been a point of discussion among mental health professionals and scientists. The evolution of this concept has witnessed many paradigm shifts in its exploration and interpretation. This journey of exploration started with the early roots of the supernatural tradition of understanding psychopathology and progressed up to biological, psychosocial and current approaches of the biopsychosocial model. However, the interaction among these doctrines of thought eventually influenced researchers to integrate various notions together to draw a holistic and meaningful etiological basis behind the manifestation of any psychopathology. But in all the paradigmatic discussions, one point which could be discussed most is functional versus biological perspectives. With this same anecdote of functional versus biological predicament, the present study makes an attempt to reflect on the clarity of making this discussion unidirectional by the observations and findings of a new paradigm of exploring psychopathology with the brain fingerprinting technique.

Brain fingerprinting technique (BFT) is an established scientific technique with suggested usage in forensic investigations to identify the concealed information stored in the brain of any individual. The technique was invented by Lawrence Farwell 1 as an investigative technique which measures the recognition of familiar stimuli with the help of event-related potentials (ERPs) generated in response to words, phrases or pictures presented from the experiential context of any individual.2, 3 The ERPs are the time-locked measure of electrical activity of the cerebral surface representing a distinct phase of cortical processing. 4 ERPs exhibit excellent time resolution and reflect the processing of information millisecond by millisecond. 5

Though the P300 wave pattern has been used primarily in investigative research, a noticeable trend has also been observed in psychiatric conditions to show variations in its amplitude and latency pertaining to physiological responses in varied clinical conditions. P300 is the third major positive peak in the late sensory evoked potential and late positive component. 6 In the majority of clinical conditions such as Alzheimer’s, schizophrenia, depression, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and panic anxiety, P300 is observed to have reduced amplitude and prolonged latency.7–10 Out of the larger responses of P300, the prolonged latency and late negative peak (LNP) together form the P300-Memory and Encoding Related Multifaceted Electroencephalographic Response (P300-MERMER), which carries greater significance in processing sensory information in psychiatric conditions. Because of its specificity of latency, the scope of application of P300-MERMER is limited to cases of delusion, hallucination and dissociative amnesia. It is assumed that when a person recognises something as significant in their experiential context, they may experience an ‘Aha!’ response depending upon whether the experiential context is real or pseudo in nature. In response to these ‘Aha!’ experiences, the ERPs are generated in the brain, and subsequent to this, a specific brainwave pattern known as P300-MERMER is produced, subject to recognition of that real experience stored in the brain of the person. 11

Event-related Potentials

ERPs are the specific voltages generated in the brain when a person is exposed to sensory, motor or cognitive events or stimuli. 12 These ERPs help in studying the psychophysiological correlates of mental processes by using electroencephalography (EEG) waves. The ERPs could be divided into two categories: (a) sensory or exogenous; these are the early waves peaking roughly within the first 100 ms generated after the stimulus on the basis of its physical parameters, and (b) cognitive or endogenous; these are the waves generated in the later part when the stimulus is evaluated, and information is processed. The ERP waveforms are described on the basis of their latency and amplitude. These amplitudes can range from positive deflection peaking to negative deflection peaking with varied latency. Some of the common ERPs are P50, N100, P200, N200, N300, P300, P400, P600 and P300-MERMER. The above ERPs are generated in different contexts, starting from normal responses to physical parameters of the stimuli to higher levels of mental processing of the stimuli. Out of the above, the P300 and P300-MERMER are considered to be most useful in brain fingerprinting research because of their specific latency and amplitudes.

P300 and P300-MERMER

The P300 brainwave is a well-known ERP that is utilised in brain fingerprinting testing and is generated when the brain recognises and processes a stimulus as significant in a particular experiential context.13–15 The P300 wave refers to the fact that the response is electrically positive (P) and has a latency of at least 300 ms (300); that is, it occurs at 300–800 ms after the stimulus is presented.6, 16, 17 However, with the substantial progress in the area, scientists started using more meaningful, rich and complex stimuli with increased P300 latency, which required greater attention and produced larger P3 waves.13, 18, 19 After initial sensory input, an attention-driven comparison evaluates the representation of the previous event in working memory. If no stimulus attribute change is detected, the current mental model or ‘schema’ of the stimulus context is maintained, and only sensory potentials are evoked. When a new stimulus is detected, the ‘updating’ of the neural stimulus representation in working memory occurs, and P300 is produced. 20 Farwell’s brain fingerprinting originally used the well-known P300 brain response to detect the brain’s recognition of the known information, but later on Farwell discovered the P300-MERMER, which includes additional features and is reported to provide a higher level of accuracy and statistical confidence than the P300 alone. 3

P300-MERMER is a wave termed Memory and Encoding Related Multifaceted EEG Response or P300-MERMER,1, 3, 14, 19 considered to be a part of a larger response of P300. This P300-MERMER could be helpful in dealing with real-life situations where it was found to be necessary to use longer and more complex stimuli to accurately communicate the necessary information to the subject. 21 This helped the subjects take longer to read the word or more words and assess their significance with an enhanced interval between stimuli from 1,500 to 3,000 ms. This more complex response included both the P300 and an LNP, which was considered to be the basis for the P300-MERMER.11, 22–24 The P300 is maximal in the parietal area (Pz region), and the LNP, which constitutes the latter part of the P300-MERMER, is parietally maximal yet also frontally prominent (Oz region).22, 25, 26

This frontoparietal network is a privileged network for cognitive control, which is facilitated by connectivity with other network organisation.27, 28 Furthermore, there is a significant positive correlation between functional integration of the frontoparietal network and overall cognitive ability, indicating that the strength of functional integration of the frontoparietal network and the rest of the brain is crucial for supporting superior cognitive functioning. 29 Cognitive controls are commonly compromised in several forms of psychopathology, which may result from disturbed frontoparietal network and dysfunction of neural networks. 30 The abnormal development of the frontoparietal network may be a common feature across many diseases, including schizophrenia, depression and anxiety.31, 32

Psychopathology involving cognitive control can be divided into primary and secondary control disorders. Primary control disorders, such as delusions and hallucinations, may involve alterations in cognitive-control networks,33 with associated cellular and molecular changes reported within the lateral prefrontal cortex 34 , however, secondary control disorders, such as anxiety and depression, are those that manifest in such a way as to not directly impact control networks. 33 Hence, lower cognitive control capacity evident in any individual may be a risk factor for several forms of psychopathology. 35

The above research emphasises the role of the frontoparietal network in the development of various forms of psychopathology; therefore, it lays down a scientific background to formulate this hypothesis of choosing the above network for the study of P300-MERMER in differentiating the real and pseudo experiential contexts. Besides the above, the other scientific observations which further justify sticking to the same hypothesis to study the said construct are: individuals with neuropsychiatric conditions are observed to have disturbed attention patterns, easy distractibility and mood swings; therefore, they may require longer time to process the information presented to them and to assess the significance of these in the given context. Hence, the above scientific observations helped the authors to rely on P300-MERMER in the present study to differentiate between real versus pseudo experiences, and the test protocols were designed in congruence with the above hypotheses.

Methods

Brain fingerprinting consists of four phases of test conduction, where the first phase is about collecting the information, the second phase is about categorisation of test stimuli, the third phase is about preparing the experimental settings, and the fourth phase is about recording and analysing the data. A detailed narration of all these four phases is given below:

Assimilation of information: Brain fingerprinting tests are conducted according to the research protocols. The protocols are designed by taking the help of information available about the experiential event. The information is categorised into probes, targets and irrelevant depending upon the nature of the information and its significance to the experiential context. This information could be of both textual (words) and pictorial nature. The text stimuli should be succinct and unambiguous and contain specific information about the experiential context. This could be one or more words, maximum up to 30 characters, with clear visibility, specific to the context and short presentation, easy to detect so that the brain could respond immediately after the presentation of the stimulus. The pictorial stimuli should contain images which are relevant to the context, prominent and easily visible, with a distance from the subject not more than one meter for better results.

Categorisation of stimuli: The information under the probe category is information that is known only to the investigator and the person with real experiential context. It contains the feature that the individual would have experienced in real context, not in virtual context. The probes carry the ability to elicit a P300-MERMER brain response only to the individual who knows the information in real context but not for the individual who knows the information in pseudo context. The information under the target category is the salient information about real-life experiences. A target is known material to the individual and the people around through various means and real experiences. The target being known to the individual would also be able to elicit a P300-MERMER brain response. However, the EEG waves generated out of these P300-MERMER with respect to probes and targets could be differentiated with colours of the waves such as ‘Blue Line’ and ‘Red Line’, respectively (Figure 1). The third part of information, which comes under the irrelevant category, is irrelevant information that is not related to the real experiential context being tested in the research protocol. These irrelevant stimuli do not elicit a P300-MERMER 36 and are differentiated by a display of a ‘Green Line’ wave pattern.

Electrode placement: Once the test stimuli are categorised into probe, target and irrelevant, the electrode placement on the scalp of the individual is ensured. The electrode placement is done in congruence with the international 10–20 system between the nasion and inion points. The electrodes are placed at three locations. The first is at the forehead, above the eyebrows and below the hairline; this is known as the electrooculography (EOG) electrode, used to measure voltage changes produced by eye movements. The second is placed at the Pz location on the scalp, which measures the P300-MERMER brainwaves, and the third category is the reference electrodes placed on each of the earlobes (Figure 2).

Data recording and analysis: Having placed the electrodes in the desired manner, exposure to various categories of stimuli is facilitated to the individual through a computer screen, and data are recorded for all three sets of test stimuli. Once the data are recorded, the analysis is done by a mathematical and statistical algorithm which computes a determination of information present or absent. The statistical confidence is calculated for each individual determination, where information present at 99.9% confidence indicates that there is a 99.9% probability that the subject has real experience of the event and knows the relevant information tested. However, if the statistical confidence does not provide enough determination to meet a predetermined criterion for ‘information present’, then no determination is made, and the outcome is ‘indeterminate’. 37 Thus, the result analysis provides clear scientific evidence of ‘information present’ or ‘information absent’ where information present implies that the individual’s experience has real context and details of this real experience are stored in the brain, however, the information absent implies that the details of the individual’s experience are virtual and could not be stored in the brain; rather, it may be due to some psychopathology associated with the person. This observation is congruent with the forensic investigative paradigm where the same procedure is followed to establish whether the information is present or absent pertaining to some crime scene. 38 By combining all four phases, the authors have formulated an operative model of brain fingerprinting in psychiatric conditions (Figure 3).

Results

Exploring Psychopathology with Brain Fingerprinting Technique

It is an established fact that brain fingerprinting has wide applicability in forensic investigations. Many studies have shown how the brain fingerprinting technique could be instrumental in differentiating between the perpetrator and the innocent in any crime.21, 39, 40 In addition to the above, the brain fingerprinting technique is also advised to be applied for purposes of counter-terrorism, national security and fostering criminal justice. For the above applications, the underlying paradigm that commonly works is that information related to a real event is stored in the brain of the perpetrator; however, this information may be absent if the person is innocent. This paradigm helps in differentiating the real contextual experience from virtual experiences with respect to any event. This argument of real versus virtual contextual experiences could be extended to many neuropsychiatric conditions, and a paradigmatic move could be taken to apply the brain fingerprinting technique in neuropsychiatric conditions.

A few specific neuropsychiatric conditions which could be considered to be worthy for the application of brain fingerprinting technique are delusional disorders, hallucinatory behaviours, paranoid schizophrenia, dissociative amnesia, malingering behaviours, frank dementia and mild cognitive impairments. Why this paradigmatic shift could be of importance in such specific neuropsychiatric conditions is because of the reliability of information on the part of the patients, which is observed to be doubtful due to the nature of pathology associated with them. Though most of the time these pathologies have related contextual background, it is often challenging for the family as well as for the clinicians to differentiate between real versus virtual context of manifested psychopathology. Besides the above, it seems imperative to mention here that in such neuropsychiatric conditions, we are still lagging behind in terms of availability of valid laboratory testing, except psychological assessments. These psychological or neuropsychological assessments attempt to assess the functional state of mind and brain and express their apparent limitations to have the control over the subjective bias in providing information on the part of the patients and verifying its sanctity in a biological context. In addition to this, these assessments could be suggestive but again have limited scope in predicting the diagnostic aspects of certain psychopathologies.

Brain fingerprinting techniques could be ably, scientifically augmented to overcome the above limitations and could be proven effective in predicting the diagnostic values and therapeutic interventions in such conditions. Not only this, the BFT technique carries similar scientific strength to explain the phenomenology of psychopathology in relation to biological context, as many of the medical conditions are investigated in pathology and radiodiagnostic laboratories. Thus, it could be helpful in maintaining the scientific sanctity of the phenomenology of psychopathology and proving whether the event has a real experiential context and biological basis or is related to a virtual world and is very much functional in nature.

Brain Fingerprinting in Delusion

Keeping in mind the above facts, the authors have made successful attempts to apply the brain fingerprinting technique in the assessment of the significance of delusional information in a real context and establishing the scientific diagnosis in a case of delusional disorder. In the said psychiatric condition, information about various aspects of the patient and core delusional beliefs was collected. Having categorised the information into probes, targets and irrelevant, the BFT was applied, and data analysis indicated that ‘information absent’ means the patient’s experiential context was beyond reality and it was purely a pseudo experience (Figure 4).

Discussion

The brain fingerprinting three-stimulus paradigm was introduced in 1986.1, 3 It was developed to provide an objective and scientific way to connect the features of the crime scene with the recorded information of the crime, which is stored in the brain of the perpetrator.3,19,22,25,26,40 The major benefit of brain fingerprinting is that it brings the record stored in the brains of participants within the realm of scientific scrutiny and objective investigation.23, 41–43 In this way, brain fingerprinting could address the differences in individuals’ experiences stored in the brain in real context and the experiences that have virtual context.

Keeping in view the above hypothesis, the present study makes an attempt to extend the application of the brain fingerprinting technique in the exploration of the nature of psychopathology in various psychiatric conditions. Though the application of this technique in psychiatry has been witnessed earlier, that was limited to the case of malingering.44, 45 In cases of malingering and factitious disorder, the personal gain could be a maintaining factor for the manifestation of the symptoms, 46 and these symptoms neither have a real experiential context nor their fingerprinting in the brain. Similar arguments could be extended to the case of dissociative amnesia also, where again the inability to recall important life information is followed by some traumatic event or life stressors 47 ; however, this loss of information does not have a biological basis and real experiential context. Often, this manifestation of symptoms could be challenging for family members and clinicians as well to classify in the category of functional psychopathology, and they keep meandering between functional assumptions versus the biological basis of loss of information on the part of the patient. The present study, with the help of the brain fingerprinting paradigm, could classify these symptoms under the functional category with scientific scrutiny and psychophysiological correlates. The present study could emphasise that these symptomatic experiences on the part of the patients do not have a real experiential context; rather, they are virtual in nature.

In addition to the above, the present study also attempted to explore the application of brain fingerprinting technology in delusional disorder and hallucinatory behaviour. Delusion is an idiosyncratic belief or impression that is firmly maintained despite being contradicted by real and rational arguments, and hallucination is perception without the presence of a real stimulus. The phenomenology of both psychiatric conditions lacks the real experiential context and does not carry biological fingerprinting in the brain. This hypothesis of real experiential context versus virtual experiential context justifies the application of BFT in these psychiatric conditions, which subsequently could be able to classify the information categorically to substantiate the diagnoses of the cases. As mentioned above, the BFT works on a classificatory algorithm, not a comparative algorithm, so the mathematical and statistical algorithm computed resulted in the category of ‘information absent’ regarding both the cases of delusional beliefs and hallucinatory voices. This further indicates that these beliefs and perceptual experiences were beyond the reality contexts, hence did not have biological evidence of fingerprinting in the brain.

A variety of studies have been conducted using the P300 paradigm, but all these studies have focused on studying the amplitude and latency time in cases of delusion and hallucination. In the studies conducted by Ford 48 and McCarley et al.,49, 50 relatively small P300 amplitudes are typically found in patients with schizophrenia having delusions and hallucinations over the midline electrodes. The interconnected dysfunctional neural network circuits involving the frontal-temporo-limbic and cortical-subcortical pathways disrupt information processing in patients with delusions and hallucinations. 51 This P300 paradigm reflecting cognitive deficits linked with the frontal-temporo-limbic and cortical-subcortical pathways may be useful.52, 53

The above studies support the assumptions of the present study that small amplitude and increased latency of P300-MERMER could be of help in processing the significance of information to differentiate between real versus pseudo experiential context in case of psychiatric conditions, and also the cortical and subcortical pathways can help in identifying the cognitive deficits in delusion and hallucination-like psychiatric conditions.

Hence, the observations of the present study based on applications of BFT in said psychiatric conditions lay the foundation for this evolution and paradigm shift in exploring the psychopathology. The present study also overcomes the gap of unavailability of physiological methods of investigating the psychopathology; rather, it proposes the brain fingerprinting technique as a novel tool for the same by taking the help of physiological markers and making it at par with lab investigation methods used in medical conditions.

Conclusion

Brain fingerprinting is a new scientific method successfully applied to accurately and reliably detect the presence or absence of information with respect to real and virtual experiential context. The present study has been able to expand the horizon of its application in neuropsychiatric conditions. The study attempted to collect biological evidence of fingerprinting in the brain with respect to psychiatric conditions of delusional beliefs, hallucinatory behaviours and dissociative amnesia. It was proven that no biological evidence of fingerprinting with respect to delusional beliefs, perception of auditory stimuli in hallucinatory behaviour and real loss of information in the case of dissociative amnesia was observed in the brain. Rather, in the first two conditions, the mathematical and statistical algorithm computed ‘information absent’, and in the third condition, it computed as ‘information present’, respectively. The above observations could be helpful in substantiating the diagnoses of the patients in a foolproof manner and also could convince the patients and their family about the virtual context of these experiences, unlike real-life experiences. The above findings further lay the first stone for this paradigmatic shift of BFT in exploring psychiatric conditions. This paradigm shift may lead the way for exploring biological evidence in psychiatric conditions and making parity with lab investigations and radiodiagnostic evaluation in medical conditions.

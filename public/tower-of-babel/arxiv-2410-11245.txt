Calculable Microscopic Theory for 12 C( α \alpha , γ \gamma ) 16 O Cross Section near Gamow Window II
Y. Suzuki

Abstract
Abstract A microscopic approach to the 12 C ( α , γ ) 16 (\alpha,\gamma)^{16} O radiative-capture reaction near the Gamow window has been proposed by Y. Suzuki, Few-Body Syst. 62 , 2 (2021). The important ingredients of the approach include the following: (1) The states of 12 C and 16 O relevant to the reaction are described by fully microscopic 3 α \alpha -particle and 4 α \alpha -particle configurations. (2) The isovector electric dipole transition is accounted for through the isospin impurity of the constituent α \alpha -particles. (3) The relative motion among the α \alpha -particles is expanded in terms of correlated-Gaussian basis functions. A calculation of the radiative-capture cross section demands double angular-momentum projections, that is, the angular momentum of 12 C consisting of 3 α \alpha -particles and the orbital angular momentum for 12 C − α -\alpha relative motion. Advancing the previous formulation based on the single angular-momentum projection, I carry out the double projection and present all the formulas needed for the cross section calculation.

Summary and discussions

Several low-lying levels of

O play an important role in the
12
C
(\alpha,\gamma)^{16}

I have developed a fully microscopic
12

+\alpha

12

3\,\alpha
-particle model can be excited to,

the
2^{+}

the
12

-\alpha

of Eq. (

).
Both of the
E1

transition is crucially important because the leading-order term of the isovector

operator has no contribution if the isospin-impurity

operators of the next order. I proposed that the isospin-forbidden
E1

T=1
impurity component of
\alpha
-particle. It should be noted that its component is not an adjustable parameter but in principle determined a priori.

The wave functions of the low-lying levels of
16
O are assumed to be
expressed in terms of a
combination of the correlated-Gaussian basis functions. I have provided all the formulas that make it possible to
obtain the needed matrix elements analytically. Because each basis function

[
25
,
26
,
28

It may be hard, however, to reproduce

\alpha

\alpha
-
\alpha

Here,

For the case of

Appendix A
Negative determinants

In order to understand how the negative determinant problem arises and how it is
avoided, let
us take a simple example, the evaluation of the overlap
of CG’s in two ways. One is a direct integration,

\displaystyle O(A^{\prime},A)=\langle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\bm{x}}A^{\prime}\bm{x}}|e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\bm{x}}A\bm{x}}\rangle=\Big(\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{{\rm det}(A+A^{\prime})}\Big)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

(A.1)

Here,
{\bm{x}}
stands for the set of 3-dimensional vectors,
\{{\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2},{\bm{x}}_{3}\}
, and
A
and
A^{\prime}
are
3\times 3
real, symmetric positive-definite matrices. Sine
A+A^{\prime}
is positive-definite, one obtains
O(A^{\prime},A)
without any problem.
The other is an indirect way by expressing the
CG via the GWP in the form (
cf
. Eqs. (
II.33
) and (
II.34
))

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\bm{x}}A\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=C_{A}\int d{\bm{s}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\bm{s}}Q_{A}\bm{s}}\psi(\bm{s},\bm{x}),

(A.2)

\displaystyle\psi(\bm{s},\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\pi^{-\frac{9}{4}}({\rm det}\,\Gamma)^{\frac{3}{4}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\bm{x}}\Gamma\bm{x}+\widetilde{\bm{x}}\Gamma\bm{s}-\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\bm{s}}\Gamma\bm{s}},

(A.3)

with

\displaystyle C_{A}=\Big(\frac{{\rm det\Gamma}}{4\pi}\Big)^{\frac{9}{4}}[{\rm det}\,(\Gamma-A)]^{-\frac{3}{2}},\ \ \ \ \ Q_{A}=\Gamma(\Gamma-A)^{-1}\Gamma-\Gamma.

(A.4)

Here,
{\bm{s}}
denotes the set of 3-dimensional variables,
\{{\bm{s}}_{1},{\bm{s}}_{2},{\bm{s}}_{3}\}
, and
\Gamma=(\gamma_{i}\delta_{i,j})
is a
3\times 3
diagonal matrix with
\gamma_{i}>0
. The integration of Eq. (
A.2
) converges only when
Q_{A}+\Gamma
is positive-definite. That is,

\Gamma-A
has to be positive-definite. Let us ignore this condition temporarily.

By using

\displaystyle\langle\psi(\bm{s}^{\prime},\bm{x})|\psi({\bm{s}},\bm{x})\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{4}\widetilde{(\bm{s}-{\bm{s}}^{\prime})}\Gamma(\bm{s}-{\bm{s}}^{\prime})},

(A.5)

and introducing
{\bm{t}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bm{s}}\\
{\bm{s}}^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\right)
, I find that the overlap takes the form

\displaystyle O(A^{\prime},A)

\displaystyle=C_{A}C_{A^{\prime}}\iint d{\bm{s}}d{\bm{s}^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\bm{s}}Q_{A}\bm{s}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\bm{s}^{\prime}}Q_{A^{\prime}}{\bm{s}}^{\prime}}\langle\psi(\bm{s}^{\prime},\bm{x})|\psi({\bm{s}},\bm{x})\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{A}C_{A^{\prime}}\int d{\bm{t}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\bm{t}}Z\bm{t}}=C_{A}C_{A^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{(2\pi)^{6}}{{\rm det}Z}\Big)^{\frac{3}{2}},

(A.6)

where
Z
is a
6\times 6
symmetric matrix defined by

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma&-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma\\
-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma&Q_{A^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma\\
\end{array}\Big)=\Big(\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}M^{-1}&0\\
0&M^{\prime-1}\\
\end{array}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&1\\
\end{array}\Big)\Big)\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Gamma&0\\
0&\Gamma\\
\end{array}\Big).

Here,
M
and
M^{\prime}
are defined by

\displaystyle M=(\Gamma-A)\Gamma^{-1},\ \ \ \ \ M^{\prime}=(\Gamma-A^{\prime})\Gamma^{-1}.

(A.15)

Note that the complex conjugation of
C_{A^{\prime}}
is not taken in Eq. (
A.6
) because
\psi(\bm{s},\bm{x})
is assumed to be real.

A question is:
The sign of
{\rm det}(\Gamma-A)
and/or that of
{\rm det}(\Gamma-A^{\prime})
may be negative. Is Eq. (
A.6
) equal to Eq. (
A.1
)?

An answer to this question crucially depends on whether the following equation holds the case or not:

\displaystyle{\rm det}\Big(\frac{A+A^{\prime}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle={\rm det}(\Gamma-A)\,{\rm det}(\Gamma-A^{\prime})\,({\rm det}\Gamma)^{-3}\,{\rm det}Z

\displaystyle={\rm det}\,\Gamma\,{\rm det}M\,{\rm det}M^{\prime}\,{\rm det}\Big(\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}M^{-1}&0\\
0&M^{\prime-1}\\
\end{array}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&1\\
\end{array}\Big)\Big).

It is better to rewrite this equation as follows. By replacing
{\rm det}M\,{\rm det}M^{\prime}
with
{\rm det}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&0\\
0&M^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\Big)
, Eq. (
A
) reduces to

\displaystyle{\rm det}\Big(\frac{A+A^{\prime}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle={\rm det\Gamma}\,{\rm det}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&M\\
M^{\prime}&M^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\Big)\Big).

Using
\frac{1}{2}(A+A^{\prime})=(1-\frac{1}{2}(M+M^{\prime}))\Gamma
from Eq. (
A.15
) and
substituting it to the left-hand side of the above equation,
Eq. (
A
) reduces to

\displaystyle{\rm det}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}(M+M^{\prime})\Big)={\rm det}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&M\\
M^{\prime}&M^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\Big)\Big).

It is possible to prove that Eq. (
A
) is in fact valid as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm det}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&M\\
M^{\prime}&M^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle={\rm det}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}1-\frac{1}{2}M&-\frac{1}{2}M\\
-\frac{1}{2}M^{\prime}&1-\frac{1}{2}M^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\Big)={\rm det}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\frac{1}{2}M\\
-1&1-\frac{1}{2}M^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\Big)

\displaystyle={\rm det}\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\frac{1}{2}M\\
0&1-\frac{1}{2}M-\frac{1}{2}M^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\Big)={\rm det}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}(M+M^{\prime})\Big).

The above analysis suggests that I
should not multiply three terms,
({\rm det}M)^{\frac{3}{2}}
,
({\rm det}M^{\prime})^{\frac{3}{2}}
, and

({\rm det}Z)^{\frac{3}{2}}
, but combine them to the single term,
\Big[{\rm det}\,\Big(\big(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&0\\
0&M^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\big)Z\Big)\Big]^{\frac{3}{2}}
. The negative determinant problem
arises because I calculate the CG matrix element through the GWP used in
the microscopic cluster-model. The problem is fortunately dodged by introducing
{\cal G}
of
Eq. (
IV.26
) and calculating it via the matrix
{\cal U}
of Eq. (
IV
).

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Lambda(abcd,l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}l_{4},\bar{L};\kappa LL^{\prime})=C(a\ l_{1}\!-\!a;l_{1})\,C(b\ l_{2}\!-\!b;l_{2})C(c\ l_{3}\!-\!c;l_{3})\,C(d\ l_{4}\!-\!d;l_{4})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \times\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bar{L}&\bar{L}&0\\
a+b&c+d&\kappa\\
L&L^{\prime}&\kappa\\
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&b&a\!+\!b\\
l_{1}\!-\!a&l_{2}\!-\!b&\bar{L}\\
l_{1}&l_{2}&L\\
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}c&d&c\!+\!d\\
l_{3}\!-\!c&l_{4}\!-\!d&\bar{L}\\
l_{3}&l_{4}&L^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\right].

Proof.

Since
a+b+c+d=\kappa
,
[Y_{a}(\bm{e}_{1})\!\times\!Y_{b}(\bm{e}_{2})\!\times\!Y_{c}(\bm{e}_{3})\!\times\!Y_{d}(\bm{e}_{4})]_{\kappa\mu}=\Big[[Y_{a}(\bm{e}_{1})\!\times\!Y_{b}(\bm{e}_{2})]_{a+b}\ \times\ [Y_{c}(\bm{e}_{3})\!\times\!Y_{d}(\bm{e}_{4})]_{c+d}\Big]_{\kappa\mu}
.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle[Y_{a}(\bm{e}_{1})\!\times\!Y_{b}(\bm{e}_{2})\!\times\!Y_{c}(\bm{e}_{3})\!\times\!Y_{d}(\bm{e}_{4})]_{\kappa\mu}\,\Big[[Y_{l_{1}-a}({\bm{e}_{1}})\!\times\!Y_{l_{2}-b}(\bm{e}_{2})]_{\bar{L}}\!\times\![Y_{l_{3}-c}({\bm{e}_{3}})\!\times\!Y_{l_{4}-d}(\bm{e}_{4})]_{\bar{L}}\Big]_{00}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{L,L^{\prime}}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a+b&c+d&\kappa\\
\bar{L}&\bar{L}&0\\
L&L^{\prime}&\kappa\\
\end{array}\right]\Big[\big[[Y_{a}(\bm{e}_{1})\!\times\!Y_{b}(\bm{e}_{2})]_{a+b}\ \times\ [Y_{l_{1}-a}({\bm{e}_{1}})\!\times\!Y_{l_{2}-b}(\bm{e}_{2})]_{\bar{L}}\big]_{L}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \times\big[[Y_{c}(\bm{e}_{3})\!\times\!Y_{d}(\bm{e}_{4})]_{c+d}\ \times\ [Y_{l_{3}-c}({\bm{e}_{3}})\!\times\!Y_{l_{4}-d}(\bm{e}_{4})]_{\bar{L}}\big]_{L^{\prime}}\Big]_{\kappa\mu}.

(B.70)

Because of the stretched coupling I obtain

\displaystyle\big[[Y_{a}(\bm{e}_{1})\!\times\!Y_{b}(\bm{e}_{2})]_{a+b}\ \times\ [Y_{l_{1}-a}({\bm{e}_{1}})\!\times\!Y_{l_{2}-b}(\bm{e}_{2})]_{\bar{L}}\big]_{L}

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&b&a\!+\!b\\
l_{1}\!-\!a&l_{2}\!-\!b&\bar{L}\\
l_{1}&l_{2}&L\\
\end{array}\right]C(a\ l_{1}\!-\!a;l_{1})\,C(b\ l_{2}\!-\!b;l_{2})[Y_{l_{1}}({\bm{e}}_{1})\!\times\!Y_{l_{2}}({\bm{e}}_{2})]_{L},

\displaystyle\big[[Y_{c}(\bm{e}_{3})\!\times\!Y_{d}(\bm{e}_{4})]_{c+d}\ \times\ [Y_{l_{3}-c}({\bm{e}_{3}})\!\times\!Y_{l_{4}-d}(\bm{e}_{4})]_{\bar{L}}\big]_{L^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}c&d&c\!+\!d\\
l_{3}\!-\!c&l_{4}\!-\!d&\bar{L}\\
l_{3}&l_{4}&L^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\right]C(c\ l_{3}\!-\!c;l_{3})\,C(d\ l_{4}\!-\!d;l_{4})[Y_{l_{3}}({\bm{e}}_{3})\!\times\!Y_{l_{4}}({\bm{e}}_{4})]_{L^{\prime}}.

The integral to be computed is given by

\displaystyle\int\cdots\int d{\bm{e}}_{1}\cdots d{\bm{e}}_{4}[Y_{l_{3}}({\bm{e}}_{3})\!\times\!Y_{l_{4}}({\bm{e}}_{4})]^{*}_{L^{\prime}M^{\prime}}[Y_{l_{1}}({\bm{e}}_{1})\!\times\!Y_{l_{2}}({\bm{e}}_{2})]_{LM}\Big[[Y_{l_{1}}({\bm{e}}_{1})\!\times\!Y_{l_{2}}({\bm{e}}_{2})]_{L}\ \times\ [Y_{l_{3}}({\bm{e}}_{3})\!\times\!Y_{l_{4}}({\bm{e}}_{4})]_{L^{\prime}}\Big]_{\kappa\mu}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l_{1}+l_{2}-L+M}\langle L\,-\!M\ L^{\prime}\,M^{\prime}|\kappa\mu\rangle=(-1)^{l_{1}+l_{2}+L+L^{\prime}+\kappa}\sqrt{\frac{2\kappa+1}{2L^{\prime}+1}}\langle L\,M\ \kappa\,\mu|L^{\prime}\,M^{\prime}\rangle.

(B.77)

Combining the above equations and using the symmetry property of the
9j
symbol

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a+b&c+d&\kappa\\
\bar{L}&\bar{L}&0\\
L&L^{\prime}&\kappa\\
\end{array}\right]=(-1)^{a+b+c+d+2\bar{L}+L+L^{\prime}+2\kappa}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bar{L}&\bar{L}&0\\
a+b&c+d&\kappa\\
L&L^{\prime}&\kappa\\
\end{array}\right],

I obtain
\Lambda(abcd,l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}l_{4},\bar{L};\kappa LL^{\prime})
as given above.

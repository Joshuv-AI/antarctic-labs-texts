Multiplicity result for a mass supercritical NLS with a partial confinement
Louis Jeanjean, Linjie Song

Abstract
We consider an NLS equation in ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} with partial confinement and mass supercritical nonlinearity. In Bellazzini, Boussaid, Jeanjean and Visciglia (Comm. Math. Phys. 353, 2017, 229-251) for such a problem, a solution with a prescribed L 2 L^{2} norm was obtained, as a local minimum of the energy, and the existence of a second solution, at a mountain pass level, was proposed as an open problem. We give here a positive, non-perturbative, answer to this problem. Our solution is obtained as a limit of a sequence of solutions of corresponding problems on bounded domains of ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} . The symmetry of solutions on bounded domains is used centrally in the convergence process.



Key Words:
NLS equations; Constrained functionals;
L^{2}
-supercritical.

Introduction

In this paper we investigate the existence and multiplicity of critical points for the
mass supercritical
NLS energy functional with a
partial confinement

E:H\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

\displaystyle E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})|u|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx,\quad\frac{7}{3}<p<5,

constrained on the
L^{2}
sphere

\displaystyle S_{\mu}:=\bigl\{u\in H:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|^{2}dx=\mu\},\quad\mu>0,

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and

\displaystyle H:=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}):\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})|u|^{2}dx<\infty\bigr\}.

The norm of
H
is

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H}^{2}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+1)|u|^{2})dx.

In the sequel, we denote
V(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
. Critical points, also called bound states, solve the stationary nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) with the partial confinement on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

\displaystyle-\Delta u+V(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-1}u

(1.1)

for some Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
. In turn, solutions to (
1.1
) give standing waves of the time-dependent NLS with partial confinement on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\,\partial_{t}\psi=-\Delta_{x}\psi+V(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\psi-|\psi|^{p-1}\psi,

(1.2)

via the ansatz
\psi(t,x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda t}u(x)
. The mass constraint
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{2}dx=\mu
is meaningful from a dynamics perspective as the mass (or charge), as well as the energy, is conserved by the NLS flow.

We point out that the physically relevant cubic nonlinearity
p=3
is covered by our assumptions. Cubic NLS, often referred as Gross-Pitaevskii equation (GPE), is very important in physics and one of the main physical motivations is the study of matter waves in the theory of Bose-Einstein condensates (BEC). Bose-Einstein condensate consists in a macroscopic ensemble of bosons that at very low temperature occupy the same quantum states. From the mathematical side, the properties of BEC at temperature much smaller than the critical temperature can be well described by the three-dimensional Gross-Pitaevskii equation (GPE) for the macroscopic wave function. GPE incorporates information about the trapping potential as well as the interaction among the particles. Our assumptions cover the case of Bose-Einstein condensate with attractive interactions and partial confinement. The latter includes the limit case of the so-called cigar-shaped model, see
[
2
]
. We refer to
[
6
]
for additional elements regarding the physical motivations and to
[
1
,
11
,
18
,
23
]
for recent mathematical contributions on the NLS with a partial confinement.

To our knowledge the existence of critical points for mass supercritical NLS energy functional with a partial confinement
E
was first touched by
[
6
]
, in which a (positive) local minimizer was found when
0<\mu<\mu_{0}
where
\mu_{0}
is a positive constant (see
[
6
, Theorem 1]
). Further, assuming, in addition, that
\mu>0
is small enough
[
6
, Theorem 3]
showed that such local minimizer is a ground state in the sense introduced in
[
7
]
.

Definition 1.1

(Ground state)
.

A critical point
u\in S_{\mu}
of
E
constrained on
S_{\mu}
is called a (normalized) ground state if and only if

\displaystyle E(u)=\inf\bigl\{E(v):v\text{ is a critical point of }E\text{ constrained on }S_{\mu}\bigr\}.

Recently
[
24
]
provided a new, direct, proof of the existence of a ground state of
E
which holds for any
0<\mu<\tilde{\mu}_{0}
. Here
\tilde{\mu}_{0}
is an explicit positive constant not obtained by a limit process, as in
[
6
, Theorem 3]
. It was also proved that this is true for the mass critical case
p=7/3
.

It was mentioned in
[
6
, Remark 1.10]
that
E
has a mountain pass geometry on
S_{\mu}
for all
\mu
for which there exists a local minimizer, and, that an interesting, but maybe difficult, problem would be to prove the existence of a second critical point for
E
at the mountain pass level.

In
[
25
]
, J. Wei and Y. Wu gave a first answer to this open problem. They proved that
E
has indeed a second positive critical point, which is at a mountain pass level, when
\mu>0
is sufficiently small. However, this result is obtained using a perturbative approach, so it is not possible to estimate how small
\mu>0
must be. As far as we know, there is no non-perturbation result up to now, and to provide a non-perturbation result is the main motivation of this paper.

We introduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}:=\bigl\{u\in H:\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx>\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx\bigr\},\quad\mathcal{G}_{\mu}:=\mathcal{G}\cap S_{\mu},

(1.3)

with

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{G}_{\mu}=\bigl\{u\in S_{\mu}:\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx\bigr\},

and define

\displaystyle c_{\mu}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}}E(u),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nu_{\mu}:=\inf_{u\in\partial\mathcal{G}_{\mu}}E(u).

(1.4)

It is standard to prove, see for example
[
7
]
, that any critical point of
E
satisfies the following Pohozaev identity

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}V|u|^{2}dx+\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx.

(1.5)

The introduction of the subset
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
was majorly inspired by (
1.5
). It is readily seen that
E
is bounded from below on
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
and thus
c_{\mu}>-\infty
(in fact,
c_{\mu}>0
when
7/3<p<5
) is well defined. When
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
where
\mu_{1}
is given by (
1.9
) later, we have
c_{\mu}<\nu_{\mu}
. Then any minimizer of
E
constrained on
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
is in the interior of
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
and is a critical point of
E
constrained on
S_{\mu}
. Further, by (
1.5
), any critical point of
E
constrained on
S_{\mu}
is in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
. Hence, every minimizer of
E
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
is a ground state. We will indeed show the existence of a ground state at the level
c_{\mu}
when
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
.

After finding a ground state, we search for a critical point at a mountain pass level. When
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
, we prove the existence of
0\leq w_{0}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
,
0\leq w_{1}\in S_{\mu}\backslash\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
such that
E(w_{i})<\nu_{\mu}
,
i=0,1
, and

\displaystyle m_{\mu}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}E(\gamma(t))\geq\nu_{\mu}>\max\bigl\{E(w_{0}),E(w_{1})\bigr\},

where

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\bigl\{\gamma\in C([0,1],S_{\mu}):\gamma(0)=w_{0},\gamma(1)=w_{1}\bigr\}.

(1.6)

This shows that
E
indeed has a mountain pass geometry on
S_{\mu}
when
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
. We will establish the existence of a critical point at the level
m_{\mu}
.

We denote
C_{p+1}>0
the best constant of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx\leq C_{p+1}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{3p-3}{4}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{5-p}{4}}

(1.7)

and let

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}:=\inf(\hbox{ spec }(-\Delta+V))=\inf_{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w|^{2}dx=1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})|w|^{2}\right)dx.

(1.8)

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
7/3<p<5
and

\displaystyle\mu_{1}:=\frac{(3p-7)^{\frac{3p-7}{2p-2}}(2p+2)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{(3p-3)^{\frac{3}{2}}C_{p+1}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\Lambda_{0}^{\frac{2p-2}{3p-7}}}

(1.9)

where
C_{p+1}>0
and
\Lambda_{0}
are defined in (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) respectively. Then, for any
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
, there is at least two positive critical points
u_{1},u_{2}
of
E
constrained on
S_{\mu}
,
u_{1}
is a ground state at the level
c_{\mu}
and
u_{2}
is at the mountain pass level
m_{\mu}
.

Remark 1.3
.

As well known, see, for example,
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
, for any
\mu>0
, there exists a unique
\omega_{\mu}>0
such that the equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\omega_{\mu}u=u^{p}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\quad\quad u(x)\to 0\quad\text{as }|x|\to\infty,

(1.10)

has a positive solution in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{2}dx=\mu
. Such positive solution is unique. We denote it
u_{\omega_{\mu}}
.

Our second result provides properties of the critical points obtained in Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}
be the critical points of
E
constrained on
S_{\mu}
given by Theorem
1.2
. Then
u_{i}(x_{1},x_{2}.x_{3})
is radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
,
i=1,2
. Moreover, there
exists
\lambda_{i}=\lambda(u_{i})
such that

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{i}+Vu_{i}+\lambda_{i}u_{i}=|u_{i}|^{p-1}u_{i}\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},

with the estimates

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<-\Lambda_{0}\left(1-C_{p+1}\left(\frac{3p-3}{3p-7}\Lambda_{0}\right)^{\frac{3p-7}{4}}\mu^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)\quad\text{when }0<\mu<C_{p+1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\left(\frac{3p-3}{3p-7}\Lambda_{0}\right)^{-\frac{3p-7}{2p-2}},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=\left(\omega_{1}+o(1)\right)\mu^{-\frac{2p-2}{3p-7}}\quad\text{as }\mu\to 0,

(1.12)

where
\omega_{1}>0
is given by Remark
1.3
. In addition, as
\mu\to 0
,

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})\varphi_{0}(x_{3})\|_{H}=o(\sqrt{\mu}),

(1.13)

\displaystyle\mu^{\frac{2}{3p-7}}u_{2}(\mu^{\frac{p-1}{3p-7}}x)\to u_{\omega_{1}}\quad\text{strongly in }H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),

(1.14)

where
\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})
is the unique normalized positive eigenvector of the quantum harmonic oscillator
-\Delta_{x_{1},x_{2}}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
,

\varphi_{0}(x_{3})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2},

and
u_{\omega_{1}}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is the unique positive solution of (
1.10
).

As mentioned above, the existence of a ground state in Theorem
1.2
is not new. However, our proof is quite different from the ones in
[
6
]
and in
[
24
]
. The existence of a critical point at the mountain pass level in Theorem
1.2
is original and is the major motivation of our study. The main difficulty is the lack of compactness, due to the translation invariance w.r.t.
x_{3}
. Indeed if one assumes that the potential is fully harmonic, namely that
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
is replaced by
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}
then one benefits from the compactness of the inclusion of
H
(with
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}
) into
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
where
q\in[2,6)
see for
example
[
26
, Lemma 3.1]
and the proof of Theorem
1.2
would be rather simple. To overcome this lack of compactness, we first consider the problem on
B_{R}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:|x|<R\}
with
R
large enough. For
R>0
and
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
, we define

\displaystyle E_{R}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})|u|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p+1}dx,\quad\frac{7}{3}<p<5,

and

\displaystyle S_{\mu,R}:=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{R}):\int_{B_{R}}|u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|^{2}dx=\mu\},\quad\mu>0.

We further introduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{R}:=\bigl\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{R}):\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx>\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p+1}dx\bigr\},\quad\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}:=\mathcal{G}_{R}\cap S_{\mu,R},

with

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}=\bigl\{u\in S_{\mu,R}:\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p+1}dx\bigr\}.

For
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
and sufficiently large
R
(depending on
\mu
)
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
is nonempty and we define

\displaystyle c_{\mu,R}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}}E_{R}(u).

Moreover, without loss of generality, we can assume that
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
used to define
\Gamma
in (
1.6
) have compact support contained in
B_{R}
. Hence, setting
w_{0,R}\equiv w_{0},w_{1,R}\equiv w_{1}
, we have that

\displaystyle m_{\mu,R}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{R}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}E_{R}(\gamma(t))\geq\nu_{\mu}>\max\bigl\{E_{R}(w_{0,R}),E_{R}(w_{1,R})\bigr\},

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{R}:=\bigl\{\gamma\in C([0,1],S_{\mu,R}):\gamma(0)=w_{0,R},\gamma(1)=w_{1,R}\bigr\}.

We will search for critical points of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
at levels
c_{\mu,R}
and
m_{\mu,R}
. Any critical point of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
satisfies the following Pohozaev identity

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\frac{R}{2}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\Bigl|\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\Bigr|^{2}d\sigma+\int_{B_{R}}V|u|^{2}dx+\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p+1}dx,

(1.15)

and thus is in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
. Therefore, critical points at the level
c_{\mu,R}
are ground states of
E_{R}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
7/3<p<5
and
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
where
\mu_{1}
is given by Theorem
1.2
.
There exists
R_{0}=R_{0}(\mu)>0
such that for all
R>R_{0}
, there is at least two positive critical points
u_{1,R},u_{2,R}
of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
,
u_{1,R}
is a ground state at the level
c_{\mu,R}
and
u_{2,R}
is at the mountain pass level
m_{\mu,R}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
u_{1,R},u_{2,R}
be the critical points of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
given by Theorem
1.5
. For
i=1,2
, there
exists
\lambda_{i,R}=\lambda(u_{i,R})
such that

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{i,R}+Vu_{i,R}+\lambda_{i,R}u_{i,R}=|u_{i,R}|^{p-1}u_{i,R}\quad\text{in}\ B_{R},

with
\lambda_{i,R}>-\Lambda_{R}
, where
\Lambda_{R}
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta+V
on
B_{R}
with Dirichlet boundary condition. In addition,
u_{i,R}\in C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
,
u_{i,R}>0
on
B_{R}
and
u_{i,R}(x_{1},x_{2}.x_{3})
is radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
.

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we notice that
any minimizing sequence in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
at the level
c_{\mu,R}
is bounded (see Lemma
3.2
) and that any bounded sequence has a strongly convergent subsequence (see Lemma
3.1
). Thus we can find
u_{1,R}
immediately when
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
is non-empty and the condition
c_{\mu,R}<\inf_{\partial\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}}E
holds. The key difficulty to search for
u_{2,R}
is to establish the existence of a bounded Palais-Smale sequence at the level
m_{\mu,R}
. For this we shall introduce a family of approximating functionals and make use of the monotonicity trick (see Lemma
3.4
and the proof of Theorem
1.5
). As for Theorem
1.6
, it is readily seen that
u_{1,R}
and
u_{2,R}
are positive and regular and that the associated Lagrange multipliers satisfy
\lambda_{1,R},\lambda_{2,R}>-\Lambda_{R}
. Furthermore, a result from
[
15
]
allows to deduce that our solutions are radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t. the
(x_{1},x_{2})
variables and
x_{3}
variable respectively. We refer to Section
3
for more details.

With Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
in hand, we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. As we shall see, our proof of Theorem
1.2
will ultimately rely on a Liouville type result (Proposition
2.6
) saying that any non-negative continuous function which is radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
and solution to

\displaystyle-\Delta u+Vu+\lambda u=u^{p}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

must be the zero function when
\lambda=-\Lambda_{0}
. Such Liouville type results when
V\equiv 0
and
\lambda=0
or when
V
is harmonic potential and
-\lambda
is the first eigenvalue are well known but we could not find a reference in the partial confinement case with
V=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
. To prove Proposition
2.6
, we use reductio ad absurdum. Elaborately choosing a family of test functions depending the parameter
R
and sending
R
to infinity, then we analyze the convergent rate to find a contradiction.

The main lines of the proof of Theorem
1.2
are the following.
Theorem
1.5
provides a sequence
R_{n}\to\infty
, together with sequences
\{u_{1,n}\},\{u_{2,n}\}
such that
u_{i,n}\in S_{\mu,R_{n}}
is a positive critical point of
E_{R_{n}}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R_{n}}
,
i=1,2
, and
E_{R_{n}}(u_{1,n})=c_{\mu,R_{n}}
,
E_{R_{n}}(u_{2,n})=m_{\mu,R_{n}}
. Such critical points have the symmetry properties mentioned in Theorem
1.6
. Being constituted of critical points, it immediately follows from the Pohozaev identity (
1.15
) that
\{u_{1,n}\},\{u_{2,n}\}
are bounded in
H
.

In Lemma
2.7
, Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
4.3
, we prove that
c_{\mu,R}\to c_{\mu}<1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
and
m_{\mu,R}\to m_{\mu}>1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
as
R\to\infty
. This implies that
E_{R_{n}}(u_{1,n})\to c_{\mu}<1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
and
E_{R_{n}}(u_{2,n})\to m_{\mu}>1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
as
n\to\infty
.

A key result is then Theorem
4.1
. It states that if
\{u_{n}\}
is a sequence such that
u_{n}\in S_{\mu,R_{n}}
is a positive critical point, having the symmetry properties stated in Theorem
1.6
, of
E_{R_{n}}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R_{n}}
, with
R_{n}\to\infty
and
E_{R_{n}}(u_{n})\to\beta\neq 1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
, then, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
H
. Note that a direct application of Theorem
4.1
then guarantees the strong convergence of
\{u_{1,n}\}
and
\{u_{2,n}\}
in
H
and thus ends the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The assumption
\beta\neq 1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
, in Theorem
4.1
, is used to show that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is non vanishing. Then, the symmetry properties of
u_{n}
implies the strong convergence of
\{u_{n}\}
in
L^{p+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
(Lemma
2.2
). In view of this last convergence, the convergence in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
becomes a direct consequence of the Liouville result mentioned above. We refer to Section
4.1
for more details.

Regarding now Theorem
1.4
, the estimates (
1.11
) and (
1.13
) can be obtained along the line of
[
6
, Subsection 4.3]
and
[
6
, Subsection 4.4]
. Further, we use some scaling arguments to establish (
1.12
) and (
1.14
). We point out that (
1.12
) and (
1.14
) were already obtained in
[

]
. The approach of
[

]
relies on the clever introduction of some associated unconstrained problems. We provide here alternative proofs.

To obtain solutions with a prescribed
L^{2}
norm, the strategy of first studying the problem on bounded domains and then passing to the limit has not yet been used much. In this direction, we know only
[
4
]
and the implementation of this approach for the study of the solutions of (
1.1
) is then one of the major interests of our work. Solving first the problem on balls
B_{R_{n}}
gives us two advantages. Firstly, when passing to the limit
R_{n}\to\infty
, since the sequences
\{u_{1,n}\},\{u_{2,n}\}
consist of exact solutions, the Pohozaev identity tells us that these sequences are bounded. Secondly, since the elements of these sequences have symmetry, this provides us with the compactness property that they strongly converge in
L^{p+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

In the last part of this work we briefly discuss the orbital stability of the solutions obtained in Theorem 1.2. It had already been established in
[
6
]
(see also
[
24
]
), that the nonempty set
\{u\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}:E(u)=c_{\mu}\}
is stable under the flow associated with (
1.2
) for
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
.
In Remark
4.5
we explain how to prove that the standing wave corresponding to a solution at the mountain pass level
m_{\mu}
is orbitally unstable for sufficiently small
\mu>0
. We conjecture that this perturbative instability result should remain true for all

Preliminaries

The results presented in this section will prepare the proof of Theorem
1.2
. The following proposition is a particular case of
[
22
, Théorème III.2]
. It shows how symmetry properties translate into compactness properties.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
2\leq d<N
. Assume that
\sup\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}<\infty
and that each
u_{n}
is radially symmetric w.r.t.
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})
and decreasing w.r.t.
|x_{i}|
,
i=d+1,\cdots,N
, then

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in }H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\,\Longrightarrow\,u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in }L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\text{ for any }2<q<2_{N}^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}.

Proposition
2.1
corresponds to the case
n=0
,
m=1
,
N_{1}=2
and
p=1
in
[
22
, Théorème III.2]
. The proof of
[
22
, Theorem III.2]
, which was not given, was supposed to be based on certain estimates of decay. We give here a proof that does not use such estimates.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
\tilde{u}_{n}=u_{n}-u\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. By contradiction, we assume that, up to a subsequence

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{q}dx>\epsilon\quad\text{for some }q\in(2,2_{N}^{*})\text{ and }\epsilon>0.

Then, it is well known that there exists a sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{n}(\cdot+y_{n})\rightharpoonup\tilde{u}\neq 0,\quad\text{in }H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

Denote
y_{n}=(\bar{y}_{n},\hat{y}_{n})
where
\bar{y}_{n}=(y_{n,1},\cdots,y_{n,d})
and
\hat{y}_{n}=(y_{n,d+1},\cdots,y_{n,N})
. We claim that
\{\bar{y}_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. By negation, we assume that
|\bar{y}_{n}|\to\infty
. Take a bounded subset
\Omega
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N-d}}|\tilde{u}|^{q}dx>2\delta
for some
\delta>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{(\bar{y}_{n}+\Omega)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N-d}}|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{q}dx=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N-d}}|\tilde{u}_{n}(\cdot+y_{n})|^{q}dx\geq\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N-d}}|\tilde{u}|^{q}dx>2\delta.

So there exists
M<\infty
such that
\int_{(\bar{y}_{n}+\Omega)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N-d}}|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{q}dx>\delta
for all
n>M
. Since
\Omega
is bounded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
|\bar{y}_{n}|\to\infty
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we can take
M^{\prime}\geq M
(depending on
k
) and
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{k}
, where
\sigma_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a rotation map,
i=1,\cdots,k,
such that
\Omega_{i}\cap\Omega_{j}=\emptyset
whenever
i\neq j
where
\Omega_{s}=\sigma_{s}(\bar{y}_{n}+\Omega)
,
s=1,\cdots,k.
It is clear that
\int_{\Omega_{i}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N-d}}|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{q}dx>\delta
when
n>M^{\prime}
,
i=1,\cdots,k,
and thus
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{q}dx>k\delta
when
n>M^{\prime}
. Taking
k
large enough such that
k\delta>\sup_{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{q}dx
we obtain a contradiction. Thus
\{\bar{y}_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Since
\tilde{u}_{n}
to
0
weakly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, the sequence
\{y_{n}\}
is not bounded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and so
\{\hat{y}_{n}\}
is not bounded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N-d}
. Also without loss of generality, we assume that
\bar{y}_{n}=0
for each
n
and that
\{y_{n,d+1}\}
is unbounded.
Up to a subsequence, we further assume
\tilde{u}_{n}(x+y_{n})\to\tilde{u}
a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\tilde{u}_{n}(y_{n})\to\tilde{u}(0)\neq 0
. From the fact that
u_{n}
is decreasing w.r.t.
|x_{i}|
,
i=d+1,\cdots,N
, we deduce that
u_{n}\geq 0
and thus
u\geq 0
. As proved previously, the sequence
\{y_{n}\}
is unbounded and so
u(y_{n})=o_{n}(1)
. Then, noticing
u_{n}(y_{n})=\tilde{u}_{n}(y_{n})+u(y_{n})=\tilde{u}_{n}(y_{n})+o_{n}(1)\geq 0
, we obtain that
\tilde{u}(0)>0
. Take
x^{*}=(0,\cdots,0,x_{d+1}^{*},0,\cdots,0)
such that
0\leq u(x^{*})<1/3\tilde{u}(0)
. So
u_{n}(x^{*})=\tilde{u}_{n}(x^{*})+u(x^{*})<1/3\tilde{u}(0)+o_{n}(1)
. For large
n
such that
|y_{n,d+1}|>|x_{d+1}^{*}|
, by the monotonicity of
u_{n}
w.r.t.
|x_{i}|
,
i=d+1,\cdots,N,
we obtain that
u_{n}(y_{n})\leq u_{n}(x^{*})<1/3\tilde{u}(0)+o_{n}(1)
. However, this contradicts that
u_{n}(y_{n})=\tilde{u}_{n}(y_{n})+u(y_{n})\to\tilde{u}(0)>0
completing the proof.
∎

As a direct consequence of Proposition

we have the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that

and that each

is radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.

respectively, then

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in }H\,\Longrightarrow\,u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in }L^{p+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Remark 2.3
.

Using, in particular, some of the results of
[
22
]
, many works have exploited the possibility of working in certain subspaces of functions with a certain symmetry in order to benefit from additional compactness properties. In this direction we mention
[
5
,
8
,
12
,
20
,
21
]
. However, it seems to us that this is the first time that the compact inclusion stated in Lemma

has been used, and moreover through a process of approximation.

Next, we recall a result comparing the quantities associated with two spectral problems defined respectively in
3d
and in
2d
.

Lemma 2.4

(Lemma 2.1 in
[
6
]
)
.

We have the following equality

\Lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0},

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}=\inf_{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w|^{2}dx=1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})|w|^{2}\right)dx,

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=\inf_{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|v|^{2}dx_{1}dx_{2}=1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left(|\nabla_{x_{1},x_{2}}v|^{2}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})|v|^{2}\right)dx_{1}dx_{2},

are the infimum of spectrum defined in 3d and in 2d respectively.

Remark 2.5
.

In the 2-dimensional case, it is well known that
\lambda_{0}=2
, it is simple and a corresponding minimizer is given by the gaussian function
e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}
, see
[
6
, Section 2]
.

The following Liouville type result will be crucially used.

Proposition 2.6
.

If a non-negative continuous function
u\in H
is radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
respectively, and, weakly solves

\displaystyle-\Delta u+Vu-\Lambda_{0}u=u^{p}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

(2.1)

then
u\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, we assume that
u\not\equiv 0
. It is easy to see that
u>0
. By Lemma
2.4
and Remark
2.5
, we get

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta_{x_{1},x_{2}}+(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})\right)e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}=\Lambda_{0}e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Let us define

\displaystyle\varphi_{R}(x_{3}):=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{2R}{\pi}}\cos(\frac{\pi}{2R}x_{3}),&|x_{3}|<R,\\
0,&|x_{3}|\geq R.\end{cases}

It is clear that

\displaystyle-\varphi_{R}^{\prime\prime}(x_{3})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4R^{2}}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})\quad\text{in }\bigl\{|x_{3}|<R\bigr\}.

Note that
e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})\in H
. Since
u
weakly solves (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(\nabla u\nabla e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})+Vue^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})-\Lambda_{0}ue^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u^{p}e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})dx.

We set

\displaystyle w(x_{3}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}dx_{1}dx_{2}\geq 0,

\displaystyle v(x_{3}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u^{p}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}dx_{1}dx_{2}\geq 0.

Since
u\in H
is radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
respectively, both
w
and
v
are symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
x_{3}
. Direct calculations yield

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(\nabla u\nabla e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})+Vue^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})-\Lambda_{0}ue^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{4R^{2}}\int_{-R}^{R}w(x_{3})\varphi_{R}(x_{3})dx_{3}-\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{R}}w(R),

and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u^{p}e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{R}(x_{3})dx=\int_{-R}^{R}v(x_{3})\varphi_{R}(x_{3})dx_{3}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{4R^{2}}\int_{-R}^{R}w(x_{3})\varphi_{R}(x_{3})dx_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{R}}w(R)+\int_{-R}^{R}v(x_{3})\varphi_{R}(x_{3})dx_{3},

that is,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\pi}{2R}\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}w(\frac{2R}{\pi}x_{3})\cos(x_{3})dx_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{R}}w(R)+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2R}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}v(\frac{2R}{\pi}x_{3})\cos(x_{3})dx_{3}.

(2.2)

It is easy to check that
w(R)\geq 0
and

\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}w(\frac{2R}{\pi}x_{3})\cos(x_{3})dx_{3}\to 0\quad\text{as }R\to\infty.

From
u>0
we deduce that
v(0)>0
. Moreover,
v
is continuous at
x_{3}=0
since
u
is continuous, and for any
\epsilon>0
and for sufficiently large
R
(depending on
\epsilon
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}v(\frac{2R}{\pi}x_{3})\cos(x_{3})dx_{3}\geq\int_{-R^{-(1+\epsilon)}}^{R^{-(1+\epsilon)}}v(\frac{2R}{\pi}x_{3})\cos(x_{3})dx_{3}\geq v(0)R^{-(1+\epsilon)}.

(2.3)

Next, we fix
0<\epsilon<1
. Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{4R^{1-\epsilon}}{\pi^{2}}v(0)\leq\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}w(\frac{2R}{\pi}x_{3})\cos(x_{3})dx_{3}\xrightarrow{R\to\infty}0,

which is self-contradictory and completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
7/3<p<5
and
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
, where
\mu_{1}
is defined in (
1.9
). Then
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
, defined in (
1.3
), is nonempty and

0<c_{\mu}<\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{0}\mu<\nu_{\mu},

where
c_{\mu}
and
\nu_{\mu}
are defined in (
1.4
).

Proof.

We set

w(x)=\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})\varphi(x_{3}),\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\varphi|^{2}dx_{3}=\mu,

where
\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})
is the unique normalized positive eigenvector of the quantum harmonic oscillator
-\Delta_{x_{1},x_{2}}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
and
\varphi(x_{3})
will be chosen later. It is clear that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla w|^{2}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\nabla_{x_{1},x_{2}}\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})|^{2}dx_{1}dx_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x_{3})|^{2}dx_{3}

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})|^{2}dx_{1}dx_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi^{\prime}(x_{3})|^{2}dx_{3},

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w|^{p+1}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})|^{p+1}dx_{1}dx_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x_{3})|^{p+1}dx_{3}.

Let
v(x_{3})\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|v|^{2}dx_{3}=\mu
be non-negative and define
v_{t}:=\sqrt{t}v(tx_{3})
. Note that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|v_{t}|^{2}dx_{3}=\mu,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|(v_{t})^{\prime}|^{2}dx=t^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|v^{\prime}|^{2}dx,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|v_{t}|^{p+1}dx=t^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|v|^{p+1}dx.

By taking
\varphi=v_{t}
with
t>0
small enough, we have
w\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
and thus
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
is not empty. Moreover,

\displaystyle E(w)=\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{0}\mu

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})|^{2}dx_{1}dx_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi^{\prime}(x_{3})|^{2}dx_{3}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\Psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})|^{p+1}dx_{1}dx_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x_{3})|^{p+1}dx_{3}

and thus
E(w)<1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
since
(p-1)/2<2
, which implies that
c_{\mu}<\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{0}\mu
. Now, for any
u\in\partial\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.7
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{\frac{5-p}{4}}C_{p+1}\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{3p-3}{4}},

implying that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\geq\left(C_{p+1}\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3p-7}}\mu^{-\frac{5-p}{3p-7}}.

Then for
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
, we get using the definition of
\mu_{1}>0
, see (
1.9
),

\displaystyle\nu_{\mu}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3p-7}{6p-6}\inf_{u\in\partial\mathcal{G}_{\mu}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3p-7}{6p-6}\left(C_{p+1}\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3p-7}}\mu^{-\frac{5-p}{3p-7}}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{0}\mu.

(2.4)

Finally, it is easy to check that
c_{\mu}>0
, by using the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
7/3<p<5
and
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
, where
\mu_{1}
is defined in (
1.9
). There exists
0\leq w_{0}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}

, such that

\displaystyle m_{\mu}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}E(\gamma(t))\geq\nu_{\mu}>\max\bigl\{E(w_{0}),E(w_{1})\bigr\}.

(2.5)

In particular,
m_{\mu}>1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
. Here
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
is defined in (
1.3
),
\Gamma
in (
1.6
) and
\nu_{\mu}
in (
1.4
).

Proof.

According to the proof of Lemma
2.7
, there exists a non-negative

such that

for

. Without loss of generality, we further assume

for some

. We choose

. Now, for an arbitrary non-negative

having support in

we define

and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla v_{t}|^{2}dx=t^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v_{t}|^{p+1}dx=t^{\frac{3p-3}{2}}\int_{B_{R}}|v|^{p+1}dx.

Also,

\displaystyle E_{R,\tau}(v_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(|\nabla v_{t}|^{2}+V|v_{t}|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{2p+2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|v_{t}|^{p+1}dx

3
Problems on
B_{R}

Since the embedding
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(B_{R})
is compact for all
1\leq q<6
, any bounded Palais-Smale sequence of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
has a convergent subsequence in
S_{\mu,R}
. Such result is now standard, see, e.g., Proposition 3.1 in
[
13
]
, but we provide here a proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p<5
. Assume that
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu,R}
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
. Then there exists
u\in S_{\mu,R}
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
.

Proof.

Since
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
, there exists
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
as
n\to\infty
. By Sobolev compact embeddings, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
L^{q}(B_{R})
for all
1\leq q<6
, so that
u\in S_{\mu,R}
. For any
w\in S_{\mu,R}
and
\lambda\in R
, we define the linear functional
J_{\lambda}(w):H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

J_{\lambda}(w)[v]:=\int_{B_{R}}\left(\nabla w\nabla v+Vwv+\lambda wv-|w|^{p-1}wv\right)dx.

Since
(E_{R}|_{S_{\mu}})^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
, there exists
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
such that
J_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n})\to 0
in
H^{-1}(B_{R})
as
n\to\infty
. Since
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
, it is clear that
\lambda_{n}
is bounded. Note that
A(u)(u_{n}-u)\to 0
by weak convergence of
u_{n}
to
u
where
A(u):H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

A(u)[v]:=\int_{B_{R}}\left(\nabla u\nabla v+Vuv\right)dx.

Then we have

\displaystyle o(1)

\displaystyle=\left(J_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n})-A(u)\right)[u_{n}-u]

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\nabla(u_{n}-u)|^{2}+V|u_{n}-u|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)|^{2}dx,

by the strong convergence in
L^{2}(B_{R})
and in
L^{p+1}(B_{R})
of
u_{n}
to
u
and by the boundedness of
\lambda_{n}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
7/3<p<5
and
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
. For

given in Lemma

, we have that

is nonempty and

, for all

. In addition, any minimizing sequence of
E_{R}
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
at the level
c_{\mu,R}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
.

Proof.

Let
0\leq w_{0}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
be given by Lemma
2.8
. For all
R>R_{0}
,
w_{0}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
and thus
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
is not empty.
From (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\nu_{\mu}>E(w_{0})=E_{R}(w_{0})\geq c_{\mu,R}.

Clearly also
c_{\mu,R}>0
.
Finally, if
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
is a minimizing sequence of
E_{R}
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx<\frac{6p-6}{3p-7}E_{R}(u_{n})\to\frac{6p-6}{3p-7}c_{\mu,R}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

yielding the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}
in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
.
∎

Combining Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we obtain the existence of a ground state immediately. To complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we focus on the existence of a critical point at the level
m_{\mu,R}
. Thanks to Lemma
3.1
, the only difficulty is to get a bounded Palais-Smale sequence. This will be done using the monotonicity trick. Following the strategy laid down in
[
19
]
, we introduce a family of functionals with a parameter
\tau\in[1/2,1]
:

\displaystyle E_{R,\tau}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+V|u|^{2})dx-\frac{\tau}{p+1}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p+1}dx,\quad\frac{7}{3}<p<5.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
7/3<p<5
and
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
. Let
R_{0}>0
,
0\leq w_{0}
and
0\leq w_{1}
be given by Lemma
2.8
. Then, taking
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\mu)>0
sufficiently small, we have that, for all
R>R_{0}

\displaystyle m_{\mu,R,\tau}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{R}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}E_{R,\tau}(\gamma(t))\geq\nu_{\mu}>\max\bigl\{E_{R,\tau}(w_{0}),E_{R,\tau}(w_{1})\bigr\},\quad\forall\tau\in[1-\varepsilon_{0},1],

(3.1)

where
\Gamma_{R}=:=\bigl\{\gamma\in C([0,1],S_{\mu,R}):\gamma(0)=w_{0},\gamma(1)=w_{1}\bigr\}.

Proof.

Clearly
\Gamma_{R}\neq\emptyset
and
\Gamma_{R}\subset\Gamma
. Also
E_{\tau}(u)\geq E(u)
for all
u\in S_{\mu}
and
0\leq\tau\leq 1
. Thus the inequality
m_{\mu,R,\tau}\geq\nu_{\mu}
follows from the left inequality in (
2.5
). Now, for
\tau\leq 1
sufficiently close to
1
, the right inequality in (
2.5
) implies the right one in (
3.1
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
7/3<p<5
,
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
and
R_{0}>0
be given by Lemma
2.8
.
There exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\mu)>0
such that for all
R>R_{0}
and for almost every
\tau\in[1-\varepsilon_{0},1]
,
E_{R,\tau}
constrained to
S_{\mu,R}
has a positive critical point at the level
m_{\mu,R,\tau}
.

Proof.

By adapting the monotonicity trick
[
19
]
on
E_{R,\tau}
or applying
[
9
, Theorem 1.5]
, together with Lemma
3.3
, we deduce that for almost every
\tau\in[1-\varepsilon_{0},1]
there exists a bounded Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{n}\}
for the constrained functional
E_{R,\tau}|_{S_{\mu,R}}
at level
m_{\mu,R,\tau}
. Since
u\in S_{\mu,R}\Rightarrow|u|\in S_{\mu,R}
,
w_{0}\geq 0
,
w_{1}\geq 0
, the map
u\mapsto|u|
is continuous, and
E_{R,\tau}(u)=E_{R,\tau}(|u|)
, it is then possible to choose
\{u_{n}\}
with the property that
u_{n}\geq 0
on
B_{R}
. Using Lemma
3.1
and the strong maximum principle, we get the desired conclusion.
∎

Now we are prepared to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

For
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
, by Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, it is standard to prove the existence of a positive critical point
u_{1,R}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
with
E_{R}(u_{1,R})=c_{\mu,R}
. If
v\in S_{\mu,R}
is a critical point of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
, Pohozaev identity yields that
v\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
and thus
E_{R}(v)\geq c_{\mu,R}
. This implies that
u_{1,R}
is a positive ground state of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
.

Next, we show the existence of a critical point at the mountain pass level. Lemma
3.4
yields the existence of a sequence
\{\tau_{n}\}
with
\tau\to 1^{-}
as
n\to\infty
and a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
such that
u_{n}
is a positive critical point of
E_{R,\tau_{n}}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
at the level
m_{\mu,R,\tau_{n}}
. It is well known see,
[
19
, Lemma 2.3]
that
m_{\mu,R,\tau}\to m_{\mu,R,1}
as
\tau\to 1^{-}
. By applying the Pohozaev identity (
1.15
) on
u_{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx>\frac{(3p-3)\tau_{n}}{2p+2}\int_{B_{R}}|u_{n}|^{p+1}dx,

and

\displaystyle\frac{3p-7}{6p-6}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}V|u_{n}|^{2}dx<E_{R,\tau_{n}}(u_{n})=m_{\mu,R,\tau_{n}}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}m_{\mu,R,1},

which implies the boundedness of
u_{n}
in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
. Using Lemma
3.1
and the strong maximum principle, we get a positive critical point
u_{2,R}
of
E_{R}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R}
at the level
m_{\mu,R}
. This complete the proof.
∎

Finally in this section, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
u_{1,R}
and
u_{2,R}
be the positive critical points given by Theorem
1.5
. For
i=1,2
, it is standard that there exists the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{i,R}=\lambda(u_{i,R})
such that

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{i,R}+Vu_{i,R}+\lambda_{i,R}u_{i,R}=|u_{i,R}|^{p-1}u_{i,R}\quad\text{in}\ B_{R},

(3.2)

and
\lambda_{i,R}>-\Lambda_{R}
since the above equation has no positive solution in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
when
\lambda\leq-\Lambda_{R}
.
The fact that
u_{i,R}\in C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
follows by standard elliptic regularity and that
u_{i,R}>0
from the strong maximum principle. To establish the symmetry of our solutions we make use of
[
15
, Corollary 1]
. We write (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle\Delta u_{i,R}+f((x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),u_{i,R})=0\quad\text{in}\ B_{R},

with
f((x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),u)=u^{p}-(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{3})u-\lambda u.
Clearly
f
is invariant with respect to any rotation that leaves invariant the
x_{3}
axis. Then from

[
15
, Corollary 1]
(note that
(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})
has the good sign), we deduce that the function
u_{i,R}
is symmetric with respect to any plane containing the
x_{3}
-axis and decreasing in the normal direction to these planes. Thus it is radial and decreasing with respect to
(x_{1},x_{2})
. Also, since
f
does not depend on
x_{3}
,
[
15
, Corollary 1]
implies that
u
is symmetric (even) with respect to
x_{3}
and decreasing along this variable. At this point the proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

4
Problems on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
.

4.1
A compactness result

The main result in this subsection is the following compactness result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence such that
u_{n}\in S_{\mu,R_{n}}
is a positive critical point of
E_{R_{n}}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R_{n}}
,
R_{n}\to\infty
,
E_{R_{n}}(u_{n})\to\beta\neq 1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
, as
n\to\infty
. Assume in addition, that, for each
n
,
u_{R_{n}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is radially symmetric and
decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
respectively. Then, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
H
and
u\in S_{\mu}
is a positive critical point of
E
constrained on
S_{\mu}
at the level
\beta
.

Before proving Theorem
4.1
, we need some preparations.

Lemma 4.2
.

As
R\to\infty
, we have
\Lambda_{R}\to\Lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

For any
0<R<R^{\prime}<\infty
, it is standard to show that
\Lambda_{R}>\Lambda_{R^{\prime}}>\Lambda_{0}
. In particular
\lim_{R\to\infty}\Lambda_{R}:=\Lambda^{*}
exists and
\Lambda^{*}\geq\Lambda_{0}
. Let us assume, by contradiction, that
\Lambda^{*}>\Lambda_{0}
.
For
\epsilon>0
small enough such that
(\Lambda_{0}+2\epsilon)/(1-\epsilon)<\Lambda^{*}
, we take
\varphi\in H
with

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\varphi|^{2}dx=1\text{ and }\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+V|\varphi|^{2}dx\right)dx<\Lambda_{0}+\epsilon.

When
r>0
is sufficiently large, there exists
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}
such that
\chi\equiv 1
in
B_{r}
,
\chi\equiv 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\backslash B_{r+1}
,
|\nabla\chi|\leq C
, and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\varphi\chi|^{2}dx:=\mu_{0}>1-\epsilon,\quad\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla(\varphi\chi)|^{2}+V|\varphi\chi|^{2}\right)dx\leq\Lambda_{0}+2\epsilon.

At this point, we define

\tilde{\varphi}:=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}}\varphi\chi\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{r+1}).

Then, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\tilde{\varphi}|^{2}dx=1\text{ and }\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla\tilde{\varphi}|^{2}+V|\tilde{\varphi}|^{2}dx\right)dx\leq\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\left(\Lambda_{0}+2\epsilon\right)<\Lambda^{*},

indicating that
\Lambda_{r+1}<\Lambda^{*}
, which is a contradiction to
\Lambda^{*}\leq\Lambda_{r+1}
and completes the proof.
∎

Now we are prepared to prove the main result in this subsection.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1:

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H
.

Since
u_{n}
is a critical point of
E_{R_{n}}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R_{n}}
, by the Pohozaev identity (
1.15
) on
B_{R_{n}}
we get

\displaystyle\frac{3p-7}{6p-6}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}V|u_{n}|^{2}dx\leq E(u_{n})\to\beta\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

which yields the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}\subset H
.

Step 2:
There exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
\int_{R^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p+1}dx>\epsilon_{0}
for all
n
.

Since
u_{n}
is a critical point of
E_{R_{n}}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R_{n}}
, it is standard that there exists the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{n}=\lambda(u_{n})
such that

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{n}+Vu_{n}+\lambda_{n}u_{n}=|u_{n}|^{p-1}u_{n}\quad\text{in}\ B_{R_{n}}.

The boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}
in
H
indicates that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded. Moreover, it is clear that
\lambda_{n}>-\Lambda_{R_{n}}
. Up to a subsequence, we assume that
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda^{*}
for some
\lambda^{*}
. By Lemma
4.2
,
\lambda^{*}\geq-\Lambda_{0}
. By negation, we suppose
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p+1}dx\to 0
up to a subsequence. Then, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V|u_{n}|^{2}\right)dx\to-\lambda^{*}\mu\leq\Lambda_{0}\mu\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V|u_{n}|^{2}\right)dx\geq\Lambda_{0}\mu.

Thus,
\lambda^{*}=-\Lambda_{0}
and then
\beta=1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
. However, we have assumed that
\beta\neq 1/2\Lambda_{0}\mu
in Theorem
4.1
.

Step 3:
Conclusion.

By Step 1, Step 2 and Lemma
2.2
, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\neq 0
weakly in
H
and
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
L^{p+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Moreover, we can assume that
u_{n}\to u
a.e. in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. For any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, there exists
N_{0}
, such that, for all
n>N_{0}
,
supp\ \varphi\subset B_{R_{n}}
, and thus

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(\nabla u_{n}\nabla\varphi+Vu_{n}\varphi+\lambda_{n}u_{n}\varphi\right)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p-1}u_{n}\varphi dx.

By the weak convergence
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
and the convergence
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(\nabla u\nabla\varphi+Vu\varphi+\lambda^{*}u\varphi\right)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p-1}u\varphi dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Namely,
u
is a weak solution to

\displaystyle-\Delta u+Vu+\lambda^{*}u=|u|^{p-1}u.

Since
u_{n}
is non-negative,
u
is also non-negative. By strong maximum principle,
u>0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Further, since
u_{n}
, is radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
respectively, it is also the case of
u
.
By the standard elliptic regularity theory, we deduce that
u
is continuous. Then, recalling that

\lambda^{*}\geq\-\Lambda_{0}
we get by Proposition
2.6
,
\lambda^{*}>-\Lambda_{0}
.

It remains to show that
u_{n}\to u
in
H
. It is clear that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V|u_{n}|^{2}+\lambda_{n}|u_{n}|^{2}\right)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p+1}dx

and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+V|u|^{2}+\lambda^{*}|u|^{2}\right)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx.

Since
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda^{*}
, we thus have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V|u_{n}|^{2}+\lambda^{*}|u_{n}|^{2}\right)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p+1}dx\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+V|u|^{2}+\lambda^{*}|u|^{2}\right)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx=0.

Now, using that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{p+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, see Lemma
2.2
, necessarily

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V|u_{n}|^{2}+\lambda^{*}|u_{n}|^{2}\right)dx\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+V|u|^{2}+\lambda^{*}|u|^{2}\right)dx.

As
\lambda^{*}>-\Lambda_{0}
, this convergence implies that
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
H
. The proof is completed.
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
7/3<p<5
,
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
and
R_{0}<R<R^{\prime}<\infty
, where
R_{0}>
is given by Lemma
2.8
. Then

(i)

c_{\mu,R}>c_{\mu,R^{\prime}}>c_{\mu}
and
c_{\mu,R}\to c_{\mu}
as
R\to\infty
;

(ii)

m_{\mu,R}\geq m_{\mu,R^{\prime}}\geq m_{\mu}
and
m_{\mu,R}\to m_{\mu}
as
R\to\infty
.

Proof.

For
R_{0}<R<R^{\prime}<\infty
, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}\subset\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
since any function in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
can be viewed as a function in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R}^{\prime})
and also in
H
by zero extending. Hence, we have
c_{\mu,R}\geq c_{\mu,R^{\prime}}\geq c_{\mu}
. Next, we prove
c_{\mu,R}>c_{\mu,R^{\prime}}
. By negation,
c_{\mu,R}=c_{\mu,R^{\prime}}
. Using Theorem
1.5
, we obtain a minimizer
u_{1,R}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R}
such that
E_{R}(u_{1,R})=c_{\mu,R}
. Thus,
u_{1,R}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu,R^{\prime}}
and
E_{R^{\prime}}(u_{1,R})=E_{R}(u_{1,R})=c_{\mu,R}=c_{\mu,R^{\prime}}
. It is standard to prove that
u_{1,R}>0
in
B_{R^{\prime}}
, which contradicts to the fact that
u\equiv 0
in
B_{R^{\prime}}\backslash B_{R}
. Therefore, we prove
c_{\mu,R}>c_{\mu,R^{\prime}}
. Similarly, we have
c_{\mu,R^{\prime}}>c_{\mu}
. It is clear
\liminf_{R\to\infty}c_{\mu,R}\geq c_{\mu}
. To complete the proof of (i), it suffices to prove
\limsup_{R\to\infty}c_{\mu,R}\leq c_{\mu}
. By negation,
\exists R_{n}\to\infty
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{\mu,R_{n}}=c_{\mu}^{*}>c_{\mu}
. For
\epsilon>0
small enough such that
c_{\mu}+\epsilon<c_{\mu}^{*}
, we take
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
with
E(\varphi)\leq c_{\mu}+\epsilon
. Without loss of generality, similar to the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we assume that the support of
\varphi
is compact and

for sufficiently large

. Then
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu,r}
and

\displaystyle c_{\mu,r}\leq E_{r}(\varphi)=E(\varphi)\leq c_{\mu}+\varepsilon<c_{\mu}^{*},

which contradicts to
c_{\mu,r}>c_{\mu}^{*}
.

In the rest of this proof, we will show (ii). Clearly
\Gamma_{R}\subset\Gamma_{R^{\prime}}\subset\Gamma
for
R_{0}<R<R^{\prime}<\infty
, and so
m_{\mu,R}\geq m_{\mu,R^{\prime}}\geq m_{\mu}
. It is clear
\liminf_{R\to\infty}m_{\mu,R}\geq m_{\mu}
. To complete the proof of (ii), it suffices to prove
\limsup_{R\to\infty}m_{\mu,R}\leq m_{\mu}
. Note that
[0,1]
is compact. Therefore reasoning as in the proof of (i) we can complete the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
1.5
provides a sequence
R_{n}\to\infty
, together with sequences
\{u_{1,n}\},\{u_{2,n}\}
such that
u_{i,n}\in S_{\mu,R_{n}}
is a positive critical point of
E_{R_{n}}
constrained on
S_{\mu,R_{n}}
,

, and

. In view of Lemma

, Lemma

and Lemma

, as

, we have

and

. At this point, by applying Theorem

, we get two positive critical points

of

with

. Similar to the proof of Theorem

∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Recall in the proof of Theorem
1.2
that
u_{1,n}\to u_{1},u_{2,n}\to u_{2}
strongly in
H
. By Theorem
1.6
,
u_{1,n}
and
u_{2,n}
, also
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, are radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
. For
i=1,2
, it is standard that there exists Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{i}=\lambda(u_{i})
such that

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{i}+Vu_{i}+\lambda_{i}u_{i}=|u_{i}|^{p-1}u_{i}\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

For
i=1
, on the one hand, multiplying by
u_{1}
and integrating by parts we get

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}=\mu^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+V|u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{1}|^{p+1}\right)dx.

Using Pohozaev identity (
1.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+V|u_{1}|^{2}\right)dx<\frac{6p-6}{3p-7}E(u_{1})=\frac{6p-6}{3p-7}c_{\mu}<\frac{3p-3}{3p-7}\Lambda_{0}\mu.

Then, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.7
), we have

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\mu^{-1}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+V|u_{1}|^{2}\right)dx-C_{p+1}\mu^{\frac{5-p}{4}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+V|u_{1}|^{2}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{3p-3}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+V|u_{1}|^{2}\right)dx\left(1-C_{p+1}\mu^{\frac{5-p}{4}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+V|u_{1}|^{2}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{3p-7}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle>\mu^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+V|u_{1}|^{2}\right)dx\left(1-C_{p+1}\mu^{\frac{5-p}{4}}\left(\frac{3p-3}{3p-7}\Lambda_{0}\mu\right)^{\frac{3p-7}{4}}\right),

and when
0<\mu<C_{p+1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\left(\frac{3p-3}{3p-7}\Lambda_{0}\right)^{-\frac{3p-7}{2p-2}}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}<-\Lambda_{0}\left(1-C_{p+1}\mu^{\frac{5-p}{4}}\left(\frac{3p-3}{3p-7}\Lambda_{0}\mu\right)^{\frac{3p-7}{4}}\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}=\mu^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+V|u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{1}|^{p+1}\right)dx<2\mu^{-1}E(u_{1})=2\mu^{-1}c_{\mu}<\Lambda_{0},

which proves (
1.11
). Repeating the steps in
[
6
, Subsection 4.4]
we obtain (
1.13
).

Next, in order to show (
1.12
) and (
1.14
), we use some scaling arguments. The bijection

T_{\mu}:u(x)\mapsto\mu^{\frac{2}{3p-7}}u(\mu^{\frac{p-1}{3p-7}}x)

continuously maps any function in
S_{\mu}
to
S_{1}
. Set

\displaystyle E_{\mu}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{\frac{4p-4}{3p-7}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}V|u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}dx.

It is clear that

\displaystyle E_{\mu}(T_{\mu}u)=\mu^{\frac{5-p}{3p-7}}E(u),

and so
\widehat{u}_{2}:=T_{\mu}u_{2}
is a critical point of
E_{\mu}
constrained on
S_{1}
. Let us show that
\widehat{u}_{2}
lies at a mountain-pass level. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{G}
is invariant with respect to the map
T_{\mu}
and its inverse, i.e.,

u\in\mathcal{G}\Leftrightarrow T_{\mu}u\in\mathcal{G}.

Hence,

u\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}\Leftrightarrow T_{\mu}u\in\mathcal{G}_{1}.

Since
\Gamma
, defined by (
1.6
), depends on
\mu
, we rename it by
\Gamma_{\mu}
, expressed as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu}:=\bigl\{\gamma\in C([0,1],S_{\mu}):\gamma(0)=w_{\mu,0},\gamma(1)=w_{\mu,1}\bigr\}.

We also introduce

\displaystyle m_{1,\mu}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}E_{\mu}(\gamma(t)),

\displaystyle\nu_{1,\mu}:=\inf_{u\in\partial\mathcal{G}_{1}}E_{\mu}(u)=\mu^{\frac{5-p}{3p-7}}\nu_{\mu}.

Similar to (
2.4
) we have
\nu_{1,\mu}\geq\frac{3p-7}{6p-6}\left(C_{p+1}\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3p-7}}
for any
0<\mu<\mu_{1}
.
Take
\bar{\mu}\in(0,\mu_{1})
and recall that
w_{\bar{\mu},0}
and
w_{\bar{\mu},1}
are
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
given in Lemma
2.8
with
\mu=\bar{\mu}
and

\displaystyle E(w_{\bar{\mu},i})<1/2\Lambda_{0}\bar{\mu}<\frac{3p-7}{6p-6}\left(C_{p+1}\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3p-7}}\bar{\mu}^{-\frac{5-p}{3p-7}},\quad i=0,1.

Then, for
0<\mu<\bar{\mu}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\mu}(T_{\bar{\mu}}w_{\bar{\mu},i})<E_{\bar{\mu}}(T_{\bar{\mu}}w_{\bar{\mu},i})=\bar{\mu}^{\frac{5-p}{3p-7}}E(w_{\bar{\mu},i})<\frac{3p-7}{6p-6}\left(C_{p+1}\frac{3p-3}{2p+2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3p-7}}\leq\nu_{1,\mu},\quad i=0,1.

By direct computation we get,

\displaystyle E_{\mu}(T_{\mu}u)<\nu_{1,\mu}\Leftrightarrow E(u)<\nu_{\mu}.

Thus
E(T_{\mu}^{-1}T_{\bar{\mu}}w_{\bar{\mu},i})<\nu_{\mu}
,
i=0,1.
As mentioned previously, the set
\mathcal{G}
is invariant with respect to the map
T_{\mu}
and its inverse, and so
T_{\mu}^{-1}T_{\bar{\mu}}w_{\bar{\mu},0}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
and
T_{\mu}^{-1}T_{\bar{\mu}}w_{\bar{\mu},1}\in S_{\mu}\backslash\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
. Therefore, for
0<\mu<\bar{\mu}
, we can take
w_{\mu,i}=T_{\mu}^{-1}T_{\bar{\mu}}w_{\bar{\mu},i}
,
i=0,1.
This indicates

\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mu}\Leftrightarrow T_{\mu}\circ\gamma\in\Gamma_{1},

and thus
m_{1,\mu}=\mu^{\frac{5-p}{3p-7}}m_{\mu}
when
0<\mu<\bar{\mu}
, which shows that
\widehat{u}_{2}=T_{\mu}u_{2}
is a critical point of
E_{\mu}
constrained on
S_{1}
at the level
m_{1,\mu}
. It is standard to prove
\lim_{\mu\to 0}m_{1,\mu}\to m_{1,0}
(similar to the proof of Lemma
4.3
). For any sequence
\mu_{n}\to 0
, we set
u_{n}:=T_{\mu_{n}}u_{2}
and study its strong convergence in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
up to subsequences. There exists Lagrange multiplier
\omega_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

-\Delta u_{n}+\mu_{n}^{\frac{4p-4}{3p-7}}Vu_{n}+\omega_{n}u_{n}=u_{n}^{p},\quad u_{n}>0,\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

Note that
\omega_{n}=\mu_{n}^{\frac{2p-2}{3p-7}}\lambda_{2}
. We claim that
\lambda_{2}
, depending on
\mu
, tends to infinity as
\mu\to 0
, which will be proved in Lemma
4.4
below. Particularly,
\lambda_{2}>0
for sufficiently small
\mu>0
. Hence,
\omega_{n}>0
for
n
large enough.
From

E_{\mu_{n}}(u_{n})=m_{1,\mu_{n}}\to m_{1,0}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{2}dx=1
, we deduce that
u_{n}
is bounded in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Up to a subsequence, we assume
\omega_{n}\to\omega^{*}\geq 0
and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. At this point, let us prove there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p+1}dx>\epsilon_{0},\quad\forall n.

By negation, we assume
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p+1}dx\to 0
passing a subsequence if necessary. Noticing that
\omega_{n}>0
, we obtain
E_{\mu_{n}}(u_{n})\to 0
, which is impossible since
E_{\mu_{n}}(u_{n})=m_{1,\mu_{n}}\to m_{1,0}>0
. Then, since the
u_{n}
are radially symmetric and decreasing w.r.t.
(x_{1},x_{2})
and w.r.t.
x_{3}
we know by Lemma
2.2
that
u\neq 0.
It is easy to check
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
weakly solves

-\Delta u+\omega^{*}u=u^{p}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

and then
u>0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. This in turn indicates that
\omega^{*}>0
. Since
V(x)\geq 0
and
\mu_{n}>0
, for
n
large enough we get

-\Delta u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\omega^{*}u_{n}\leq u_{n}^{p}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

Since
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, it follows from the classical elliptic estimates that
u_{n}
exponentially decays uniformly with respect to
n
. Thus
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|Vu_{n}|^{2}dx
is bounded and
\mu_{n}^{4p-4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|Vu_{n}|^{2}dx
tends to
0
. Then, it is standard to prove the strong convergence of
u_{n}
to
u
in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Obviously,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u|^{2}dx=1
, thus
\omega^{*}=\omega_{1}
where
\omega_{1}
is given by Remark
1.3
and
u=u_{\omega_{1}}
, the unique positive solution of

-\Delta u+\omega_{1}u=u^{p}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\quad\quad u(x)\to 0\quad\text{as }|x|\to\infty.

This completes the proof of (
1.12
) and (
1.14
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\lambda_{2}
, depending on
\mu
, be given by Theorem
1.4
as the Lagrange multiplier of
u_{2}
. Then
\lambda_{2}\to\infty
as
\mu\to 0
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\lambda_{2}>-\Lambda_{0}
. Since
\lambda_{2}
and
u_{2}
depend on
\mu
, we rename them by
\lambda_{2,\mu}
and
u_{2,\mu}
. Arguing by contradiction, we suppose there exists
\mu_{n}\to 0
such that
\lambda_{2,\mu_{n}}
is bounded. Using classical blow up analysis, see for example
[
16
]
, we deduce that
(u_{2,\mu_{n}})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Then, since
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{2,\mu_{n}}|^{2}dx=\mu_{n}\to 0
, we get
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{2,\mu_{n}}|^{p+1}dx\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This in turn indicates that
E(u_{2,\mu_{n}})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, in a contradiction with

E(u_{2,\mu})=m_{\mu}\geq\nu_{\mu}\to\infty\quad\text{as }\mu\to 0,

where
\nu_{\mu}\to\infty
is deduced from (
2.4
). This completes the proof.
∎

Finally we discuss the orbital stability of the standing wave corresponding to a solution at the mountain pass level
m_{\mu}
.

Remark 4.5
.

It can be proved that the standing wave corresponding to a solution at the mountain pass level
m_{\mu}
is orbitally unstable for
\mu>0
sufficiently small. As, starting from Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
, the argument is relatively standard, we will just give the broad outlines. Let us first observe that, by (
1.12
),
\lambda_{2}\to\infty
as
\mu\to 0
. Next we use the scaling
u_{2}\mapsto\lambda_{2}^{-1/(p-1)}u_{2}(\lambda_{2}^{-1/2}x)
and study the limit process as the parameter
\lambda_{2}
to
\infty
. Noting (
1.14
), it is readily seen that
\lambda_{2}^{-1/(p-1)}u_{2}(\lambda_{2}^{-1/2}x)
converges to
u^{*}
strongly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
where
u^{*}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is the unique positive solution of the following equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+u=|u|^{p-1}u,\quad u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

It is well known that
u^{*}
is radially symmetric and nondegenerate. Now we rename
\lambda_{2}
by
\lambda_{2,\mu}
and the nondegeneracy of
u^{*}
enables us to prove that
u_{2}
is nondegenerate for small
\mu
and show the existence of
\bar{\mu}\in(0,\mu_{1}]
such that
\mu^{*}=\mu^{**}
if
0<\mu^{*},\mu^{**}<\bar{\mu}
and
\lambda_{2,\mu^{*}}=\lambda_{2,\mu^{**}}
. Thus for sufficient large
\lambda_{2}
we can define a map
\lambda_{2}\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|u_{2}|^{2}dx
, denoted by
\mu(\lambda_{2})
. Clearly
\mu(\lambda_{2})
is monotonically decreasing. Using the implicit function theorem we obtain that
\mu(\lambda_{2})
is of class
C^{1}
and so
\mu^{\prime}(\lambda_{2})\leq 0
. Further, borrowing the ideas of
[
14
, Proposition 11 (ii)]
and by increasing
\lambda_{2}
if necessary, one can prove that
\mu^{\prime}(\lambda_{2})<0
. Then, using the general results
[
17
, Theorem 2 and Theorem 3]
we conclude that the standing wave corresponding to a solution at the mountain pass level
m_{\mu}
is orbitally unstable for sufficiently small
\mu>0
.

Subgraph Entropy
Tarik Aougab, Matt Clay, Tawfiq Hamed

Abstract
. Given r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 , we prove that there exists λ > 0 \lambda>0 depending only on r r so that if G G is a metric graph of rank r r with metric entropy 1 1 , then there exists a proper subgraph H H of G G with metric entropy at least λ \lambda . This answers a question of the first two authors together with Rieck. We interpret this as a graph theoretic version of the Bers Lemma from hyperbolic geometry, and explain some connections to the pressure metric on the Culler-Vogtmann Outer Space.

1.
Introduction

For any
g\geq 2
, there is a constant
\beta_{g}
such that for any closed hyperbolic surface
X
there is a collection of
3g-3
curves on
X
that are pairwise non-isotopic where each has length at most
\beta_{g}
. The existence of such a constant
\beta_{g}
was first shown by Bers
[
2
]
, and the minimal such number
\beta_{g}
is called the
Bers constant
. This constant plays a key role in understanding the topology of moduli space, the geometry of the mapping class group and of hyperbolic
3
-manifolds, and the structure of Teichmüller space (
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
5
]
). Other important estimates include the work of Buser
[
6
]
, Buser-Seppälä
[
7
]
, and Schmutz
[
18
]
on the growth of the Bers constant and related functions, such as the maximal length of a systole as a function of the topology of a given hyperbolic surface.

Over the last 40 years, techniques from the study of hyperbolic surfaces have played an influential role in studying the moduli spaces of metric graphs and the outer automorphism group of a free group (
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
1
]
). For example, the second two authors and Rieck
[
1
]
studied a pair of piecewise Riemannian metrics on the Culler-Vogtmann Outer Space that were inspired by the
thermodynamical interpretation
of the Weil-Petersson metric on Teichmüller space, due to Wolpert, McMullen, and Thurston (
[
20
]
,
[
13
]
). It was Thurston who first defined a Riemannian metric on Teichmüller space by considering a limit of Hessians associated to a sequence of “randomly” chosen closed geodesics; later, Wolpert confirmed that this was a multiple of the Weil-Petersson metric
[
20
]
, and McMullen helped to develop a more general framework for the so-called
thermodynamic formalism
for making sense of this phenomenon
[
13
]
. It is therefore no surprise that the metrics studied in
[
1
]
are intimately related to counting closed cycles on objects being parameterized by the Outer space, namely, (marked) metric graphs.

In the spirit or porting over intuition from hyperbolic geometry to the setting of graphs and free groups, the main goal of this paper is to derive an analogue of the Bers constant for metric graphs. Of course, depending on how one defines the normalized Outer Space, the most naive translations of Bers’ theorem to the setting of graphs is either trivial, or can not possibly hold. We can see this even in the simplest case of a rose
R_{n}
on
n
petals; indeed, perhaps one is interested in finding a maximal collection of pairwise disjoint (except at the basepoint) cycles on
R_{n}
each with length at most some
\beta_{n}
. If the normalized Outer Space is defined to consist of metric graphs of total volume
1
, this is trivially true (and one can choose
\beta_{n}=1
) and not particularly useful. On the other hand, the metrics on Outer Space mentioned in the previous paragraph require that one chooses a different normalization– one that is suited to considering the growth of the collection of all cycles as a function of length– namely, selecting metric graphs with unit
entropy
, the exponential growth rate of the number of reduced cycles. And since there are roses with unit entropy and arbitrarily large volume, no version of Bers’ theorem can hold.

Note that we have already stumbled upon a key difference between the geometry of hyperbolic surfaces and metric graphs: any closed hyperbolic surface
automatically
has unit entropy and has area depending only on
g
. Since one way of deriving Bers’ theorem is to use the Gauss-Bonnet theorem and the bound it produces on total area, one might hope there is a way of reframing the argument in terms of entropy instead of area. Indeed, one can prove the following,
and
that it is equivalent to the existence of the Bers constant
\beta_{g}
(see the Section
2
for a proof that the theorem below is equivalent to Bers’ theorem, and for all relevant definitions):

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a constant
\eta_{g}>0
such that if
X
is a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
, there is a proper essential subsurface
Y\subsetneq X
with entropy at least
\eta_{g}
. Furthermore, the existence of
\eta_{g}
is equivalent to the existence of
\beta_{g}
.

Our main theorem is the direct analog of the first sentence of Theorem
1.1
in the setting of metric graphs:

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
r\geq 3
, there is a constant
C_{r}>0
so that given a graph
G
with
\rk\pi_{1}(G)=r\geq 3
and a length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
, there is a proper subgraph
G^{\prime}\subset G
such that
\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}})\geq C_{r}
.

In addition to achieving a Bers Lemma like result for metric graphs, we place Theorem
1.2
in conversation with the work Kim-Lim
[
11
]
, which establishes a relation between the entropy of a graph
G^{\prime}
obtained from a graph
G
by adding an additional edge between a pair of initially non-adjacent vertices. In some sense, our work concerns the opposite direction of starting with a unit entropy graph, deleting an edge, and estimating (from below) the entropy of the resulting graph.

As mentioned above, In
[
1
]
, the authors study a piecewise Riemannian metric on the outer space of a free group whose definition is inspired by the classical Weil–Petersson metric on the Teichmüller space. We show that, as in the case of the Weil–Petersson metric, this metric is incomplete. Unlike the Weil–Petersson metric and the associated mapping class group action, we determine that so long as the rank is at least

action. In fact, we conjectured that the entire outer space has finite diameter, but in attempting to prove this conjecture we ran into the need of a lemma along the lines of Theorem

The entropy metric (see

2.1.
Graphs

A
graph
is a tuple
G=(V,E,o,\tau,\bar{\phantom{e}})
where:

(1)

V
and
E
are sets, called the
vertices
and the
directed edges
,

(2)

o,\tau\colon\thinspace E\to V
are functions that specify the
originating
and
terminating
vertices of an edge, and

(3)

\bar{\phantom{e}}\colon\thinspace E\to E
is a fixed-point free involution such that
o(e)=\tau(\bar{e})
.

Given a graph, we fix a subset
E_{+}\subset E
that consists of exactly one edge from each pair
\{e,\overline{e}\}
.

A
length function
on
G
is a function
\ell\colon\thinspace E_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. The moduli space of all such functions is denoted
\mathcal{M}(G)
. A length function
\ell
extends to a function on
E
by defining
\ell(e)=\ell(\bar{e})
if
e\notin E_{+}
.

A
(
based
)
circuit
is a finite sequence of edges
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
where:

(1)

t(e_{i})=o(e_{i+1})
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
t(e_{n})=o(e_{1})
, and

(2)

e_{i}\neq\bar{e}_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
e_{n}\neq\bar{e}_{1}
.

Given a length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}(G)
, the length of a circuit
\gamma=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is defined as:

\ell(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(e_{i}).

The set of all circuits in
G
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(G)
. For a given length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}(G)
, the subset of circuits whose length is at most
t
is denoted
\mathcal{C}_{G,\ell}(t)
.

2.2.
Entropy

The
entropy
of a length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}(G)
is defined as:

\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\ell)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\left\lvert{\mathcal{C}_{G,\ell}(t)}\right\rvert.

This function
\mathfrak{h}_{G}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is real analytic and strictly convex
[
14
, Proposition A.4]
. It is readily verified that
\mathfrak{h}_{G}
is homogeneous of degree
-1
. That is:

(2.1)

\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\alpha\ell)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\ell),

for any
\alpha>0

[
1
, Lemma 3.4]
.

The subset of length functions with unit entropy is denoted
\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
. That is:

\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)=\{\ell\in\mathcal{M}(G)\mid\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\ell)=1\}.

We associate to the graph
G
a matrix that records the incident relations. Specifically, we define an
\left\lvert{E}\right\rvert\times\left\lvert{E}\right\rvert
matrix
A_{G}
by

A_{G}(e,e^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }\tau(e)=o(e^{\prime})\mbox{ and }\bar{e}\neq e^{\prime},\\
0&\mbox{ else. }\end{cases}

Given a length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}(G)
, we set
A_{G,\ell}
to be the matrix obtained by multiplying the row of
A_{G}
corresponding to
e\in E
by
\exp(-\ell(e))
for each edge. That is:

A_{G,\ell}(e,e^{\prime})=\exp(-\ell(e))A_{G}(e,e^{\prime}).

This matrix is irreducible. Moreover, we have that:

(2.2)

\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\ell)=1\Leftrightarrow\spec(A_{G,\ell})=1

where
\spec({\mathchoice{\mkern 1.0mu\mbox{\raise 2.2pt\hbox{$\centerdot$}}\mkern 1.0mu}{\mkern 1.0mu\mbox{\raise 2.2pt\hbox{$\centerdot$}}\mkern 1.0mu}{\mkern 1.5mu\centerdot\mkern 1.5mu}{\mkern 1.5mu\centerdot\mkern 1.5mu}})
denotes the spectral radius (cf.
[
17
, Lemma 3.1(2)]
).

One can use
2.2
to prove easily that a lower bound on entropy implies an upper bound on the length of the shortest cycle:

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
n
and
\delta>0
, there is
\epsilon
so that if
G
is a metric graph of rank
n
and has entropy at least
\delta
, then
G
admits a cycle
\gamma
with
\ell(G)<\epsilon
.

Proof.

If the lemma is false, then there exists a sequence of metric graphs
\left\{G_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
all of the same rank so that
\mathfrak{h}(G_{n})\geq\delta
and so that the length of the shortest cycle on
G_{n}
is at least
n
. Since there are only finitely many graphs of a given rank, we can assume each
G_{n}
is of the same topological type; therefore we instead refer to a single graph
G
with a sequence of metrics
\left\{\ell_{n}\right\}
.

We first prove that there must be an edge,
e
, with uniformly bounded length over all
\ell_{n}
. If not, then we can assume that every edge has length at least
n
with respect to
\ell_{n}
. Also, by an application of
2.1
we can assume that
\mathfrak{h}(\ell_{n})=1
. But this is an immediate contradiction, because the entries of
A_{G,\ell_{n}}
converge uniformly to
0
, whence it follows that the spectral radii also converge to
0
, but
2.2
implies that they must in fact all be
1
.

Thus, there is some uniform
\epsilon_{1}>0
and an edge
e
so that
\ell_{n}(e)<\epsilon_{1}
, for all
n
. If
e
is a self-loop, we are done. If not, it follows quite readily from the definitions that the metric graph
(G^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}_{n})
obtained by contracting
e
to a point and keeping all other edge lengths the same, has entropy at least that of
(G,\ell_{n})
. We can therefore apply the same argument, and induct on the number of edges of
G
: we eventually arrive at a sequence of edges
e_{i}
, each of length at most some
\epsilon_{i}
(for some
\epsilon_{i}
depending only on
G
) for all
\ell_{n}
, which form a cycle on
G
. It follows that there is some cycle of uniformly bounded length on
G
, over all
\ell_{n}
, as desired.

∎

We will now use Lemma
2.1
to verify Theorem
1.1
from the introduction, reproduced here for clarity:

Theorem 2.2
.

There is a constant
\eta_{g}>0
such that if
X
is a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
, there is a proper essential subsurface
Y\subsetneq X
with entropy at least
\eta_{g}
. Furthermore, the existence of
\eta_{g}
is equivalent to the existence of
\beta_{g}
.

Proof.

We first note that the existence of
\beta_{g}
is equivalent to the existence of a single non-boundary-parallel simple closed geodesic of bounded length, which we will denote by
\beta^{\prime}_{g}
; indeed, assuming that every hyperbolic surface that is not a three-holed sphere admits such a geodesic, we obtain Bers’ theorem by applying this assertion iteratively, cutting along the short geodesic whose existence we assume, and inducting on the topology of the surface.

Now, suppose that
X
admits a proper essential subsurface
Y
with entropy at least
\eta_{g}
.
We claim that without loss of generality, there is some metric graph
\Gamma
with a
1
-Lipschitz map into
Y
and a constant
K\geq 1
(depending only on
g
) with the property that
\Gamma\hookrightarrow Y
induces an isomorphism between fundamental groups, and so that if
\alpha\subset Y
is some closed geodesic, then there is a cycle on
\Gamma
homotopic to
\alpha
and with length at most
K\cdot\ell_{X}(\alpha)
. Indeed, a simple hyperbolic geometry argument using the Gauss-Bonnet theorem implies that such a
\Gamma
and a
K
exists so that
K
depends on
g
and on the diameter of
Y
, which can not be arbitrarily large if there are no short curves in
Y
.

The definitions immediately imply that
\mathfrak{h}(\Gamma)
is uniformly comparable to
\mathfrak{h}(Y)
, and thus by Lemma
2.1
,
\Gamma
must admit a cycle of length at most some
\beta^{\prime}
which we can take to depend only on
g
because there are only finitely many topological types of graph that can arise as
\Gamma
.

Conversely, assume that any hyperbolic surface
X
admits a non-boundary-parallel simple closed geodesic
\alpha
with length at most some
\beta^{\prime}_{g}
. Then, cutting along
\alpha
, we obtain a subsurface
Y
(which may be disconnected– in the event that it is, choose one component at random). Applying the assumption again, we obtain a second simple closed geodesic
\gamma
in
Y
, also with length at most
\beta^{\prime}_{g}
. Using a similar application of the Gauss-Bonnet theorem as we did above, it follows that either there is a path from
\alpha
to
\gamma
through
Y
of uniformly bounded length, or, there is some other short simple closed geodesic
\gamma^{\prime}
whose distance to
\alpha
is less than the distance between
\gamma
and
\alpha
. Applying this argument iteratively yields a metric graph embedded within
Y
that has the form of a barbell with both self-loops having uniformly bounded length, and with the separating edge
also
having uniformly bounded length in terms only of
g
. Thus the entropy of this barbell is bounded below in terms of some
\eta_{g}
, and therefore so is that of
Y
.

∎

It follows from
2.2
and the Perron-Frobenius theorem that when
\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\ell)=1
, there are positive vectors
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\left\lvert{E}\right\rvert}
such that:

(2.3)

\mathbf{u}^{T}A_{G,\ell}=\mathbf{u}^{T},\quad A_{G,\ell}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v},\text{ and }\mathbf{u}^{T}\mathbf{v}=1.

Moreover, these vectors are unique up to scale. The
equilibrium measure

\mu
is defined by taking the entry-wise products of
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
, i.e.,
\mu(e)=\mathbf{u}(e)\mathbf{v}(e)
. This determines a measure on the set of bi-infinite lines in
G
in the usual way, c.f.
[
19
, Section 6.6]
. We remark that, by symmetry, one has
\mu(e)=\mu(\bar{e})
.

Notation 2.3
.

For use later on, we define the following quantities:

\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{G}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}\left(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}}\right)\mid G^{\prime}\subset G\text{ is a proper subgraph}\right\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(G)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{G}(\ell)\mid\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)\right\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{r}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(G)\mid\rk\pi_{1}G=r\right\}

Theorem
1.2
can be restated as
\mathfrak{h}_{r}>0
. As there are only finitely many finite graphs of any given rank without valence one or two vertices, the infimum in the definition of
\mathfrak{h}_{r}
is actually a minimum.

We define a function
F_{G}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathbb{R}
by:

(2.4)

F_{G}(\ell)=\det\left(I-A_{G,\ell}\right).

From (
2.2
), it follows that
F_{G}(\ell)=0
if
\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\ell)=1
. Furthermore, we have the following relation between
F_{G}
and the equilibrium measure
\mu
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
and let
\mu
be the corresponding equilibrium measure. Then
\nabla F(\ell)
is proportional to
\mu
. That is, there is a nonzero constant
C
such that
\mu(e)=C\partial_{e}F(\ell)
for all
e\in E_{+}
.

Proof.

This follows as
\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
is a component of
F_{G}^{-1}(0)
and also the zero locus of
\log\spec(A_{G,\ell})
since the gradient of the latter function is
\mu
. See the proofs of Lemmas 3.9 and 4.4 in
[
1
]
for complete details.
∎

Finally, we note that
\mathfrak{h}_{G}
extends to a continuous function where we allow

\spec(A_{G,\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\ell)\ell})=1
, and since characteristic functions and the Perron–Froebinous eigenvalue depend continuously on the matrix entries.

Using this comparison, we can now prove a lemma that will be used in the proof of Theorem

to produce a subgraph of definite entropy.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\ell\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B}_{2})
be a length function where
\ell(e_{1})=3\exp(-c/2)
,
\ell(e_{2})=c/4
and
\ell(e_{3})=c
for any
c>0
. Then
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(\ell)\geq\frac{1}{5}
.

Proof.

Fix a positive constant
c
and let
\ell\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B}_{2})
be as in the statement. Let
a=3\exp(-c/2)
. Then
\ell(e_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}\log(a/3)
and
\ell(e_{3})=-2\log(a/3)
. By Lemma
2.5
, we find that:

(2.6)

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(\ell)=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(a,-\frac{1}{2}\log(a/3),-2\log(a/3))\geq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(a,-5\log(a/3)).

Let
R_{2}(a)=-\log\left(\frac{1-\exp(-a)}{1+3\exp(-a)}\right)
as in (
2.5
). Then
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(a,R_{2}(a))=1
as explained in Section
2.3.1
.

Claim
.

For
0<a<3
, we have
-5\log(a/3)\leq 5R_{2}(a)
.

Proof of Claim.

This is equivalent to

(2.7)

\exp(a)(1-a/3)\leq 1+a.

The inequality (

) is true for

. The derivative of the left hand side of (

\frac{1}{3}(2-a)\exp(a)
. We observe that

(2.8)

\frac{1}{3}(2-a)\exp(a)\leq 1

for
0\leq a\leq 3
, hence proving the claim, as 1 is the derivative of the right hand side of (
2.7
). Indeed, the function
\frac{1}{3}(2-a)\exp(a)
on
0\leq a\leq 3
is maximized at
a=1
and we observe that
\frac{1}{3}\exp(1)\leq 1
holds.
∎

Therefore, by the claim, we find that:

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(a,-5\log(a/3))\geq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(a,5R_{2}(a))\geq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(5a,5R_{2}(a))=\frac{1}{5}.

The lemma now follows by combining this with (
2.6
).
∎

3.
Computing the Entropy of a Subgraph

Let
e
be any edge of a graph
G
and fix a length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
. We will be interested in a smooth family of length functions
\psi_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
defined as follows for
0\leq t<\infty
:

(3.1)

\psi_{t}(e^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\ell(e)+t&\text{ if }e^{\prime}=e\\
j(t)\cdot\ell(e^{\prime})&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

where
j\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is defined to be the function so that
\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\psi_{t})=1
. In other words, we linearly increase the length of
e
and scale the remaining edges to maintain unit entropy. The smoothness of
j
follows from the smoothness of the entropy function (for instance, by the implicit function theorem). We call the path
\psi_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
a
linear time blow up of
\ell
along
e
.

The main result of this section shows that we can compute the entropy
\ell
restricted to the subgraph
G^{\prime}=G-e
using the derivative
j^{\prime}(t)
. Further, we compute a formula for
j^{\prime}(t)
that we use in the later sections.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
where
G
is a connected graph with
\rk\pi_{1}G\geq 3
, and fix an edge
e\in E(G)
. Let
\psi_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
be the linear time blow-up of
\ell
along
e
with corresponding scaling function
j\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and let
\mu_{t}
denote the equilibrium measure for the length function
\psi_{t}
.

(1)

The derivative of
j
satisfies:

(3.2)

j^{\prime}(t)=\frac{-\mu_{t}(e)}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell(e^{\prime})\mu_{t}(e^{\prime})}.

(2)

For the subgraph
G^{\prime}=G-e
we have:

(3.3)

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}})=1-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert{j^{\prime}(t)}\right\rvert dt.

Proof.

First we show (
1
). We can compute an expression for
j^{\prime}(t)
from the equation
F_{G}(\psi_{t})=0
. Indeed, differentiating both sides with respect to
t
yields

\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(G)}\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{t}(e^{\prime})\cdot\partial_{e^{\prime}}F(\psi_{t})=0.

By (
3.1
) we have that:

\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{t}(e^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }e^{\prime}=e,\\
j^{\prime}(t)\cdot\ell(e^{\prime})&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore, we obtain the equation

-\partial_{e}F_{G}(\psi_{t})=\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}j^{\prime}(t)\cdot\ell(e^{\prime})\partial_{e^{\prime}}F_{G}(\psi_{t}).

Solving for
j^{\prime}(t)
yields

j^{\prime}(t)=\frac{-\partial_{e}F_{G}(\psi_{t})}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell(e^{\prime})\partial_{e^{\prime}}F_{G}(\psi_{t})}.

Finally, the fact that
\nabla F_{G}(\psi_{t})
is proportional to
\mu_{t}
, the equilibrium measure at
\psi_{t}
, (Lemma
2.4
) yields the formula in (
3.2
).

Next we demonstrate (
2
). We observe that
j(t)
is monotonically decreasing and bounded below by
0
. This is clear from the definitions as we must shrink the edges in
G^{\prime}=G-e
more as the length of
e
grows, but the explicit formula in (
3.2
) shows that
j^{\prime}(t)<0
as well. This implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}j(t)
exists. Denote this limit by
j_{\infty}
. We also observe that
j(0)=1
as
\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\ell)=1
.

Hence, for the right hand side of (
3.3
), we find:

1-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert{j^{\prime}(t)}\right\rvert dt=1+\int_{0}^{\infty}j^{\prime}(t)dt=1+\lim_{t\to\infty}j(t)-j(0)=j_{\infty}.

As the rank of
G
is at least 3,
j_{\infty}>0
. Indeed, at least one component of
G^{\prime}
has rank at least 2 and hence
\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}\left(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}}\right)>0
. This forces
j_{t}>1/\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}\left(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}}\right)
for all
t
.

By continuity of the extended entropy function and the definition of
\psi_{t}
, we find:

1=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathfrak{h}_{G}(\psi_{t})=\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(j_{\infty}\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{j_{\infty}}\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}}).

Thus
\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}})=j_{\infty}
, which verifies (
3.3
).
∎

Therefore, our approach for proving Theorem
1.2
will be to bound the improper integral
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert{j^{\prime}(t)}\right\rvert\,dt
away from 1.

4.
Roses

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.2
for
r
–roses
\mathcal{R}_{r}
,
r\geq 3
. As stated in the introduction, our method of proof for the general case requires a non-loop edge. Hence we must deal with the rose case separately. The strategy here though is similar to the general case and provides a good warm-up and overview for the methods used later on. Namely, we will make use of the structure of the matrix
A_{\mathcal{R}_{r}}
to get bounds on the values of the equilibrium measure related to edge lengths that, via Proposition
3.1
, allow us to estimate the entropy of a subgraph.

These bounds are the context of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{r})
with equilibrium measure
\mu
and fix two edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(\mathcal{R}_{r})
. Then:

\exp(\ell(e_{1}))\mu(e_{1})<4\exp(\ell(e_{2}))\mu(e_{2})

Proof.

Let
A=A_{\mathcal{R}_{r},\ell}
and fix vectors
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{T}A=\mathbf{u}^{T}
,
A\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}
, and
\mathbf{u}^{T}\mathbf{v}=1
. Then we have that
\mu(e)=\mathbf{u}(e)\mathbf{v}(e)
for all edges as explained in Section
2.2
. Throughout the calculations below we will make use of the fact that
\mathbf{u}(e_{i})=\mathbf{u}(\bar{e}_{i})
for any edge
e_{i}
and similarly for
\mathbf{v}
and
\mu
.

As
\mathbf{u}^{T}A=\mathbf{u}^{T}
, we find that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(e_{1})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\ell(e_{1}))\mathbf{u}(e_{1})+2\sum_{i\neq 1}\exp(-\ell(e_{i}))\mathbf{u}(e_{i}),\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(e_{2})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\ell(e_{2}))\mathbf{u}(e_{2})+2\sum_{i\neq 2}\exp(-\ell(e_{i}))\mathbf{u}(e_{i}).

From this we observe that:

(4.1)

\mathbf{u}(e_{1})<2\mathbf{u}(e_{2}).

As
A\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}
, we find that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(e_{1})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\ell(e_{1}))\left(\mathbf{v}(e_{1})+2\sum_{i\neq 1}\mathbf{v}(e_{i})\right),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(e_{2})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\ell(e_{2}))\left(\mathbf{v}(e_{2})+2\sum_{i\neq 2}\mathbf{v}(e_{i})\right).

As above, this implies that:

(4.2)

\exp(\ell(e_{1}))\mathbf{v}(e_{1})<2\exp(\ell(e_{2}))\mathbf{v}(e_{2}).

Combining (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) we find:

\exp(\ell(e_{1}))\mu(e_{1})=\exp(\ell(e_{1}))\mathbf{u}(e_{1})\mathbf{v}(e_{1})<4\exp(\ell(e_{2}))\mathbf{u}(e_{2})\mathbf{v}(e_{2})=4\exp(\ell(e_{2}))\mu(e_{2}).\qed

Next, we will show how these bounds prove Theorem
1.2
in the case of roses.

Proposition 4.2
.

For each
r\geq 3
, there is a constant
B_{r}>0
so that for any given length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{r})
, there is a proper subgraph
G^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{R}_{r}
such that
\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}\left(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}}\right)\geq B_{r}
. In other words,
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{R}_{r}}(\ell)\geq B_{r}
for any length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{r})
, and hence
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(\mathcal{R}_{r})\geq B_{r}
. Moreover,
\lim_{r\to\infty}B_{r}=1
.

Proof.

Fix a length function
\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{r})
and rename the edges so that
\ell(e_{1})\geq\ell(e_{2})\geq\ell(e_{i})
for
3\leq i\leq r
. Let
\psi_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{r})
be the linear time blow-up of
\ell
along
e_{1}
with corresponding scaling function
j(t)
. Let
\mu_{t}
be the equilibrium measure at
\psi_{t}
so that we are in set-up of Proposition
3.1
.

By Proposition
3.1
(
1
), we have:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert{j^{\prime}(t)}\right\rvert\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{t}(e_{1})}{\sum_{i\neq 1}\ell(e_{i})\mu_{t}(e_{i})}\,dt

Lemma
4.1
gives us that
\mu_{t}(e_{1})<4\exp(j(t)\ell(e_{2})-\ell(e_{1})-t)\mu_{t}(e_{2})
, and therefore:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{t}(e_{1})}{\sum_{i\neq 1}\mu_{t}(e_{i})\ell(e_{i})}\,dt<4\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(j(t)\ell(e_{2})-\ell(e_{1})-t)\mu_{t}(e_{2})}{\sum_{i\neq 1}\ell(e_{i})\mu_{t}(e_{i})}\,dt

As
j(t)\ell(e_{2})\leq\ell(e_{2})\leq\ell(e_{1})
, we have
\exp(j(t)\ell(e_{2})-\ell(e_{1}))\leq 1
. This gives:

4\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(j(t)\ell(e_{2})-\ell(e_{1})-t)\mu_{t}(e_{2})}{\sum_{i\neq 1}\ell(e_{i})\mu_{t}(e_{i})}\,dt\leq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\frac{\mu_{t}(e_{2})}{\sum_{i\neq 1}\ell(e_{i})\mu_{t}(e_{i})}\,dt

Lastly, since
\ell(e_{2})\mu_{t}(e_{2})\leq\sum_{i\neq 1}\ell(e_{i})\mu_{t}(e_{i})
, we conclude:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert{j^{\prime}(t)}\right\rvert\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\frac{\mu_{t}(e_{2})}{\sum_{i\neq 1}\ell(e_{i})\mu_{t}(e_{i})}\,dt

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(-t)}{\ell(e_{2})}\frac{\mu_{t}(e_{2})\ell(e_{2})}{\sum_{i\neq 1}\ell(e_{i})\mu_{t}(e_{i})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\ell(e_{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\ell(e_{2})}.

Let
G^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}_{r}-e_{1}
. Hence by Proposition
3.1
(
2
) we find:

(4.3)

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}})=1-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert{j^{\prime}(t)}\right\rvert\,dt\geq 1-\frac{4}{\ell(e_{2})}.

Notice that we must have
\ell(e_{2})\geq\log(2r-3)
. Indeed, the length function
\ell
restricted to the subrose
\mathcal{R}_{r}-e_{1}
has entropy less than 1. For
r\geq 29
, we have
\log(2r-3)>4
and therefore, (
4.3
) gives:

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}})\geq 1-\frac{4}{\ell(e_{2})}\geq 1-\frac{4}{\log(2r-3)}>0.

When
r<29
, we will need to consider two subcases. First, if
\ell(e_{2})>5
, then (
4.3
) gives:

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}})\geq 1-\frac{4}{5}=\frac{1}{5}.

Next, we suppose that
\ell(e_{2})\leq 5
. Thus
\ell(e_{i})\leq 5
for
2\leq i\leq r
. In this case, we find that:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}(\ell\big|_{G^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\geq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{R}_{r-1}}(5,\ldots,5)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(2r-3)}{5}\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{R}_{r-1}}(\log(2r-3),\ldots,\log(2r-3))

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(2r-3)}{5}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{5}.

Hence we can take
B_{r}=\frac{1}{5}
for
3\leq r<29
, and
B_{r}=1-\frac{4}{\log(2r-3)}
for
r\geq 29
.
∎

We conjecture that
B_{3}=\log(3)/\log(5)\approx 0.6826...
which is obtained for the uniform length function
\ell=(\log(5),\log(5),\log(5))\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{3})
. Further, we conjecture that
B_{r}\geq B_{3}
for all
r\geq 3
.

5.
Reduction

In order to get some control over the combinatorics of the matrix
A_{G,\ell}
to assist in analyzing the equilibrium measure
\mu
, it is necessary to have some assumptions about the structure of
G
. In the previous section, we saw that having loop edges allowed for some connections to be made between the components of the vectors
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
that ultimately allowed for comparisons on components of
\mu
. In this section we will show how to reduce proving Theorem
1.2
to the case where every vertex has a loop edge. Moreover, we will also show that we can assume the loops are short compared to the non-loop edges.

Definition 5.1
.

Suppose
G
is a graph with
\rk\pi_{1}(G)\leq 3
. We say a sequence of length functions
(\ell_{i})\subset\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
is
witnessing
if
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{G}(\ell_{i})\to\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(G)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose
r\geq 3
and
\mathfrak{h}_{r}=0
. Then there is a graph
G
with
\rk\pi_{1}(G)=r
,
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(G)=0
, and a witnessing sequence
(\ell_{i})\subset\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
such that:

(1)

every vertex of
G
is incident to a loop edge,

(2)

there exists at least one non-loop edge, and

(3)

for any non-loop edge
e
, there are loop edges
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
with
o(\gamma_{1})=t(\gamma_{1})=o(e)
and
o(\gamma_{2})=t(\gamma_{2})=t(e)
, we have
\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1}),\ell_{i}(\gamma_{2})\leq\frac{1}{4}\ell_{i}(e)
.

Proof.

Fix
r\geq 3
and suppose that
\mathfrak{h}_{r}=0
. As there are only finitely many finite graphs with a fixed finite rank without valence 1 or 2 vertices, we may assume that we have a finite graph
G_{1}
with
\rk\pi_{1}G_{1}=r
,
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(G_{1})=0
and a witnessing sequence
(\ell_{i})\subset\mathcal{M}^{1}(G_{1})
.

First we will show how to arrange (
1
). Let
E_{0}
be the set of edges of
G_{1}
that are incident to a vertex of
G_{1}
that is not incident to any loop edge. If
E_{0}
is empty, then we set
G^{\prime}=G_{1}
. Else, let
e_{i}
be the edge in
E_{0}
that minimizes
\ell_{i}(e_{i})
. As there are only finitely many edges, we may assume that the sequence
e_{i}
is constant, i.e.,
e_{i}=e
for some edge
e\in E_{0}
.

Let
G_{2}
be the graph obtained by collapsing
e
. There is an induced length function
\ell^{\prime}_{i}
in
G_{2}
defined by
\ell^{\prime}_{i}(e^{\prime})=\ell_{i}(e^{\prime})
for each remaining edge. Let
\alpha_{i}=\mathfrak{h}_{G_{2}}(\ell^{\prime}_{i})
so that
\hat{\ell}_{i}=\alpha_{i}\ell^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(G_{2})
.

Claim 5.3
.

For any proper subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq G_{2}
, there is a proper subgraph
H\subseteq G_{1}
such that
\mathfrak{h}_{H}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{H}\right)\leq\mathfrak{h}_{H^{\prime}}\left(\ell^{\prime}_{i}\big|_{H^{\prime}}\right)\leq 2\mathfrak{h}_{H}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{H}\right)
.

Proof of Claim
5.3
.

Let
H^{\prime}
be a proper subgraph of
G_{2}
. There is a unique proper subgraph
H\subset G_{1}
that contains
e
and so that the collapse of
e
results in
H^{\prime}
. There is a bijection between cycles on
G_{2}
and
G_{1}
we denote by:
b\colon\thinspace\mathcal{C}(G_{2})\to\mathcal{C}(G_{1})
which restricts to a bijection between cycles on
H^{\prime}
and
H
.

For any cycle
\gamma
on
H^{\prime}
, by construction, we have that
\ell^{\prime}_{i}(\gamma)\leq\ell_{i}(b(\gamma))\leq 2\ell^{\prime}_{i}(\gamma)
as any cycle that crossed

necessarily crossed a remaining edge that was longer. Thus

\left\lvert{\mathcal{C}_{H,\ell_{i}}(t)}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert{\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}_{i}}(t)}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert{\mathcal{C}_{H,\ell_{i}}(2t)}\right\rvert.

The first of these inequalities immediately gives
\mathfrak{h}_{H}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{H}\right)\leq\mathfrak{h}_{H^{\prime}}\left(\ell^{\prime}_{i}\big|_{H^{\prime}}\right)
. For the other we compute:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{H^{\prime}}\left(\ell^{\prime}_{i}\big|_{H^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\left\lvert{\mathcal{C}_{H^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}_{i}}(t)}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\left\lvert{\mathcal{C}_{H,\ell_{i}}(2t)}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{2}{\tau}\log\left\lvert{\mathcal{C}_{H,\ell_{i}}(\tau)}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=2\mathfrak{h}_{H}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{H}\right).\qed

In particular, we find that
\alpha_{i}=\mathfrak{h}_{G_{2}}(\ell^{\prime}_{i})\geq\mathfrak{h}_{G_{1}}(\ell_{i})=1
. Combining this with the other inequality in Claim
5.3
we find:

\mathfrak{h}_{H^{\prime}}\left(\hat{\ell}_{i}|_{H^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}}\mathfrak{h}_{H^{\prime}}\left(\ell^{\prime}_{i}|_{H^{\prime}}\right)\leq\mathfrak{h}_{H^{\prime}}\left(\ell^{\prime}_{i}|_{H^{\prime}}\right)\leq 2\mathfrak{h}_{H}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{H}\right).

Therefore we have that
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{G_{2}}(\hat{\ell}_{i})\leq 2\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{G_{1}}(\ell_{i})
and thus
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{G_{2}}(\hat{\ell}_{i})\to 0
.

We now repeat this procedure as needed replacing
G_{1}
with
G_{2}
so that the result is a graph
G^{\prime}
where every vertex is incident to a loop edge with
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(G^{\prime})=0
, and a witnessing sequence
(\ell_{i})\subset\mathcal{M}^{1}(G^{\prime})
. This shows (
1
)

By Proposition
4.2
,
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(\mathcal{R}_{r})>0
, hence
G^{\prime}
must have a non-loop edge. This shows (
2
).

Lastly, to show how to arrange for (
3
), suppose there is a vertex where every incident loop edge has length more than
\frac{1}{4}
of the length of every incident non-loop edge. Then as we argued in showing (
1
), we can contract the shortest of these non-loop edges and only change the length of cycles by a bounded amount. An argument similar to the one above then gives the desired result.
∎

and estimate these quantities.

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1}))\mathbf{u}(\gamma_{1})+\exp(-\ell(e))\mathbf{u}(e)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\exp(-\ell(e_{i}))\mathbf{u}(e_{i})\geq\frac{X_{\mathbf{u}}}{2}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1}))\left(\mathbf{v}(\gamma_{1})+\mathbf{v}(e)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{v}(e_{i})\right)\geq\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1}))\frac{Y_{\mathbf{v}}}{2}

In a similar manner, we also can estimate that
\mathbf{u}(\gamma_{2})\geq\frac{Y_{\mathbf{u}}}{2}
and
\mathbf{v}(\gamma_{2})\geq\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{2}))\frac{X_{\mathbf{v}}}{2}
.

Combining (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) together with these related inequalities, we find:

\displaystyle\mu(\gamma_{1})+\mu(\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}(\gamma_{1})\mathbf{v}(\gamma_{1})+\mathbf{u}(\gamma_{2})\mathbf{v}(\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1}))\frac{X_{\mathbf{u}}Y_{\mathbf{v}}}{4}+\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{2}))\frac{X_{\mathbf{v}}Y_{\mathbf{u}}}{4}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1})-\ell(\gamma_{2}))\left(X_{\mathbf{u}}Y_{\mathbf{v}}+X_{\mathbf{v}}Y_{\mathbf{u}}\right)

As
\mu(e)=\mu(\bar{e})
, we have that
X_{\mathbf{u}}X_{\mathbf{v}}=Y_{\mathbf{u}}Y_{\mathbf{v}}
by (
6.3
) and (
6.4
). Hence
X_{\mathbf{v}}=\frac{Y_{\mathbf{u}}Y_{\mathbf{v}}}{X_{\mathbf{u}}}
and
Y_{\mathbf{v}}=\frac{X_{\mathbf{u}}X_{\mathbf{v}}}{Y_{\mathbf{u}}}
. Continuing the above inequalities, we find:

\displaystyle\mu(\gamma_{1})+\mu(\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1})-\ell(\gamma_{2}))\left(X_{\mathbf{u}}\left(\frac{X_{\mathbf{u}}X_{\mathbf{v}}}{Y_{\mathbf{u}}}\right)+Y_{\mathbf{u}}\left(\frac{Y_{\mathbf{u}}Y_{\mathbf{v}}}{X_{\mathbf{u}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1})-\ell(\gamma_{2}))\left(X_{\mathbf{u}}X_{\mathbf{v}}\left(\frac{X_{\mathbf{u}}}{Y_{\mathbf{u}}}\right)+Y_{\mathbf{u}}Y_{\mathbf{v}}\left(\frac{Y_{\mathbf{u}}}{X_{\mathbf{u}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{X_{\mathbf{u}}X_{\mathbf{v}}}{4}\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1})-\ell(\gamma_{2}))\left(\frac{X_{\mathbf{u}}}{Y_{\mathbf{u}}}+\frac{Y_{\mathbf{u}}}{X_{\mathbf{u}}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{X_{\mathbf{u}}X_{\mathbf{v}}}{2}\exp(-\ell(\gamma_{1})-\ell(\gamma_{2})).

The last inequality holds as
x+\frac{1}{x}\geq 2
for
x\in(0,\infty)
. Rewriting, we have:

(6.7)

X_{\mathbf{u}}X_{\mathbf{v}}\leq 2\exp(\ell(\gamma_{1})+\ell(\gamma_{2}))(\mu(\gamma_{1})+\mu(\gamma_{2}))

The inequality (
6.7
) together with (
6.3
) gives:

\exp(\ell(e))\mu(e)=X_{\mathbf{u}}X_{\mathbf{v}}\leq 2\exp(\ell(\gamma_{1})+\ell(\gamma_{2}))(\mu(\gamma_{1})+\mu(\gamma_{2}))

as desired.
∎

7.
The Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we assemble the proof of Theorem
1.2
and show that
\mathfrak{h}_{r}>0
. To this end, suppose the theorem is false, that is
\mathfrak{h}_{r}=0
for some fixed
r\geq 3
. Let
G
be the graph with witnessing sequence
(\ell_{i})\subset\mathcal{M}^{1}(G)
as given by Proposition
5.2
. Fix a non-loop edge
e
in
G
and loop edges,
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
, incident to the vertices
o(e)
and
t(e)
respectively. Recall that by (
3
) in Proposition
5.2
, we can assume that

\ell_{i}(\gamma_{j})\leq\frac{1}{4}\ell_{i}(e),\text{ for }j=1,2.

For each
i
, let
\psi_{t}^{(i)}
be the linear time blow-up of
\ell_{i}
along
e
and let
j_{i}\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be the corresponding scaling function. Denote the equilibrium measure at
\psi_{t}^{(i)}
by
\mu^{(i)}_{t}
. Let
G^{\prime}=G-e
. By Proposition
3.1
we have that:

(7.1)

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{G^{\prime}}\right)=1-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert{j^{\prime}_{i}(t)}\right\rvert\,dt=1-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e)}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell_{i}(e^{\prime})\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e^{\prime})}\,dt.

By Lemma
6.1
and the definition of
\psi^{(i)}_{t}
, we find that:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e)}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell(e^{\prime})\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e^{\prime})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2\exp(\psi^{(i)}_{t}(\gamma_{1})+\psi^{(i)}_{t}(\gamma_{2})-\psi^{(i)}_{t}(e))(\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{1})+\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{2}))}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell_{i}(e^{\prime})\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e^{\prime})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2\exp(j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1})+j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{2})-\ell_{i}(e)-t)(\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{1})+\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{2}))}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell_{i}(e^{\prime})\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e^{\prime})}\,dt

Let
m^{(i)}=\min\{\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1}),\ell_{i}(\gamma_{2})\}
. Then the final integral in the above inequality can be rewritten, using the fact that

m^{(i)}(\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{1})+\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{2}))\leq\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e^{\prime})\ell_{i}(e^{\prime}),

as follows:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2\exp(j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1})+j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{2})-\ell_{i}(e)-t)(\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{1})+\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{2}))}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell_{i}(e^{\prime})\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e^{\prime})}\,dt\\
=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1})+j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{2})-\ell_{i}(e)-t)}{m^{(i)}}\frac{m^{(i)}(\mu_{t}^{(i)}(\gamma_{1})+\mu^{(i)}(\gamma_{2}))}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell_{i}(e^{\prime})\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e^{\prime})}\,dt\\
\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1})+j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{2})-\ell_{i}(e)-t)}{m^{(i)}}\,dt.

As
j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1})\leq\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1})\leq\frac{1}{4}\ell_{i}(e)
, we have that
\ell_{i}(e)-j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1})-j_{i}(t)\ell(\gamma_{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{i}(e)
. Therefore, we find:

2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{1})+j_{i}(t)\ell_{i}(\gamma_{2})-\ell_{i}(e)-t)}{m^{(i)}}\,dt\leq\frac{2\exp(-\ell_{i}(e)/2)}{m^{(i)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\,dt=\frac{2\exp(-\ell_{i}(e)/2)}{m^{(i)}}

This now gives:

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{G^{\prime}}\right)=1-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e)}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\neq e}\ell_{i}(e^{\prime})\mu_{t}^{(i)}(e^{\prime})}\,dt\geq 1-\frac{2\exp(-\ell_{i}(e)/2)}{m^{(i)}}.

Since
\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime}}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{G^{\prime}}\right)\to\mathfrak{h}_{\rm inf}(G)=0
as
i\to\infty
by assumption, we must have that
\frac{2\exp(-\ell_{i}(e)/2)}{m^{(i)}}\to 1
as
i\to\infty
. In particular, for large enough
i
, we find that
m^{(i)}\leq 3\exp(-\ell_{i}(e)/2)
.

Therefore, the edges
e,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
form a sub-barbell
\mathcal{B}\subset G
so that one of
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
has length at most
3\exp(-\ell_{i}(e)/2)
and so that the other self-loop has length at most
\frac{1}{4}\ell_{i}(e)
. Lemma
2.6
applies and we conclude that for all sufficiently large
i
we have
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\ell_{i}\big|_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{5}
and hence
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{G}(\ell_{i})\geq\frac{1}{5}
as well.
This is a contradiction.

Arithmeticity and geometric commensurators
Yanlong Hao

Abstract
. We establish a coarse-geometric analogue of Margulis’s commensurator criterion for groups acting on good Gromov-hyperbolic spaces. For a non-elementary 2 ⁤ 1 2 2\frac{1}{2} -lattice Γ \Gamma , we introduce its coarse-geometric commensurator and show that, if this commensurator contains Γ \Gamma with infinite index, then, after quotienting by a finite normal subgroup, either Γ \Gamma is a lattice in a nondiscrete totally disconnected locally compact group with dense commensurator, or Γ \Gamma is an arithmetic lattice in a rank-one simple real Lie group and its orbit metric is roughly isometric, up to scaling, to the corresponding arithmetic metric. We also prove an analogous characterization of rank-one locally symmetric metrics using boundary maps induced by rough isometries. In particular, assuming the Hilbert–Smith conjecture, the totally disconnected alternative is excluded when the limit set is a connected manifold.



1.
Introduction and Statement of the main results

Arithmetic locally symmetric spaces are central objects in geometric group theory and are closely connected to algebra, number theory, and combinatorics. Eberlein
[
5
,
6
]
and Chen
[
3
]
characterized symmetric spaces
H
of noncompact type among all irreducible, simply-connected Riemannian manifolds with nonpositive sectional curvature in terms of their isometry groups. Following Farb and Weinberger
[
7
]
, Van Limbeek
[
20
]
extended this work by classifying all closed manifolds
(M,g)
with a nondiscrete isometry group
\operatorname{Isom}(\tilde{M},g)
. Throughout the process, hidden symmetries
[
8
]
were used to detect all closed, locally symmetric arithmetic manifolds.

On the other hand, a well-known result by Margulis states that a lattice
\Gamma
in a semisimple Lie group
G
is arithmetic if and only if its commensurator is dense in
G
.

This paper aims to provide necessary and sufficient conditions for the arithmeticity of discrete subgroups acting isometrically on hyperbolic spaces, thereby generalizing Margulis’s aforementioned result.

A
\Gamma
-action on a metric space
(X,d)
is called
metrically proper
if, for all
x\in X
and a real number
R>0
, the set

\left\{{\gamma\in\Gamma}\ \left|\ {d(x,\gamma x)<R}\right.\right\}

is finite.

Let
(X,d)
be a good hyperbolic space, and let
\operatorname{Isom}(X)
be the group of isometries of
X
with the bounded-open topology. Let
\Gamma<\operatorname{Isom}(X)
be a discrete subgroup with
0<\delta_{\Gamma}<\infty
that is of divergence type. As defined in
[
9
]
, there is a Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan-type measure
{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}
on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma^{(2)}X\times{\mathbb{R}}
. For more details, refer to Section
2.E
. For the properties and definitions of good hyperbolic spaces, see Section
2.B
and
[
14
]
. Here, we define a subgroup of
\operatorname{Isom}(X)
as discrete if it acts metrically properly on
X
.

Notably, there is no standard definition of volume in a coarse-geometric setting. Therefore, we adopt the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

\Gamma
is a
2\frac{1}{2}
-lattice
if
\Gamma
is non-elementary and

0<{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}(\Lambda_{X}\Gamma^{(2)}X\times{\mathbb{R}}/\Gamma)<\infty.

Example 1.2
.

Any word-hyperbolic group is a
2\frac{1}{2}
-lattice acting on the good hyperbolic space associated by Mineyev to a Cayley graph
[
12
]
.

Now consider an isometric
\Gamma
-action on
X
. Let
p
be a base point of
X
. Let
(\Gamma,d)
denote the pseudometric space such that

for all

\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
. Therefore, the problem of classifying isometric actions of

up to rough isometry is equivalent to the problem of classifying left-invariant quasi-metrics on
\Gamma
. Thus, we identify

isometrically with the orbit

. Throughout the paper, whenever we discuss the coarse geometry of

, we use this identification and freely regard maps of

as maps of the orbit

. We also restrict our attention to proper actions.

We call a left-invariant metric
(\Gamma,d)

if there exists a symmetric space

of noncompact type and an embedding

of

into

as an arithmetic lattice such that

is roughly isometric to
(i(\Gamma)o,d_{H})
for some point

, where

.

Now we turn to the second aspect of this paper. The purely group-theoretic concept related to the commensurator of a subgroup, known as the

abstract commensurator

\operatorname{AbCom}(\Gamma)

of a general countable group
\Gamma
, is defined as the set of
equivalence classes
[\phi]

of group isomorphisms
\phi:\Gamma^{\prime}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
between finite-index subgroups

\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}<\Gamma
. Two isomorphisms
\phi_{i}:\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\Gamma_{i}^{\prime\prime}

(

if there exists a finite-index
subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}<\Gamma_{1}^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}
such that the restrictions of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}

to
\Gamma^{\prime}
agree. The composition of (appropriate restrictions of) two such isomorphisms
defines a group structure on
\operatorname{AbCom}(\Gamma)
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and
d
be a metric on
\Gamma
as previously defined.
The
coarsely geometric commensurator group
, which is denoted by

{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}
, is a subgroup of the abstract commensurator group

\operatorname{AbCom}(\Gamma)
.
It consists of classes
[\phi]
of isomorphisms
\phi:\Gamma^{\prime}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}

that satisfy the following condition:

\sup_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma^{\prime}}|d(\phi(\gamma_{1}),\phi(\gamma_{2}))-d(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})|<\infty.

This group is independent of the choice of base point.

The main results presented in this paper are as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
(X,d)
be a good hyperbolic space. Suppose

is a non-elementary

(2)

.

We also seek a coarse-geometric characterization of locally symmetric metrics.

Let

1.A.
Hilbert-Smith conjecture

While Hilbert’s fifth problem has been solved
[
13
]
, the Hilbert-Smith conjecture remains unresolved in most cases.

Conjecture 1.4

(Hilbert-Smith)
.

If a locally compact group acts continuously and faithfully on a connected manifold, then it must be a Lie group.

Corollary 1.5
.

Assuming the Hilbert-Smith conjecture and the hypotheses of Theorems
A
and
B
. If, in addition,
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is a connected manifold, case (1) in both Theorems
A
and
B
cannot occur.

The Hilbert-Smith conjecture has been established in a few instances. A concise proof exists for groups acting by Lipschitz maps on a Riemannian manifold
[
18
]
. John Pardon solved the three-dimensional case
[
17
]
.

Therefore, unconditionally, we obtain the following result:

Corollary 1.6
.

In the statements of Theorems
A
and
B
, if
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is a connected topological manifold of dimension at most 3, then case (1) cannot occur.

2.
Preliminaries

2.A.
Standing assumptions and boundary geometry

Let
(X,d)
be a Gromov-hyperbolic space with Gromov boundary
\partial X
. Then
\bar{X}=X\cup\partial X
is a bordification of
X
. Fix a base point
o\in X
. The limit set of a subgroup
\Gamma<\operatorname{Isom}(X,d)
is defined as

\Lambda_{X}\Gamma=\overline{\Gamma o}^{\bar{X}}\cap\partial X.

The group
\Gamma
is called non-elementary if
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
contains at least three points.

2.B.
Good hyperbolic spaces

Gromov-hyperbolic spaces play a fundamental role in modern mathematics. However, certain applications require stronger geometric properties. In
[
14
]
, the concept of strong hyperbolic spaces was introduced. For our purposes, the following definition suffices.

Definition 2.1
.

A hyperbolic space
X
is called
\epsilon
-good
, where
\epsilon>0
, if the following
two properties hold for each base point
p\in X
:

•

The Gromov product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{p}
on
X
extends continuously to the bordification
X\cup\partial X
.

•

The function
d_{\epsilon}:=\exp(-\epsilon(\cdot,\cdot)_{p})
defines a metric on the boundary
\partial X
.

Example 2.2
.

If
\Gamma
is a hyperbolic group, then the Green metric defined by a symmetric
and finitely supported random walk on
\Gamma
is both a good hyperbolic and a strong hyperbolic metric
[
14
]
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Gromov-hyperbolic group. The Cayley graph of
\Gamma
with a finite generating set is a Gromov-hyperbolic space. Mineyev
[
12
]
constructed a good hyperbolic space with a cocompact isometric
\Gamma
-action.

Another useful tool for studying good hyperbolic spaces
(X,d)
is the Busemann function.
For
\xi\in\partial X
, the Busemann function
\beta_{\xi}:X\times X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

\beta_{\xi}(x,y)=\lim_{z\to\xi}d(x,z)-d(y,z).

The limit is independent of the sequence converging to
\xi
since
X
is a good hyperbolic space.

2.C.
Cross-ratio

The classical cross-ratio on
S^{1}
can be generalized to the boundary of good hyperbolic spaces
[
16
,
paulin1996groupe
,
11
]
.
Cross-ratios provide a unified interpretation of many geometric structures,
making them valuable tools for studying various spaces with group actions.

Let
Y
be a space, and let
Y^{(n)}
be the set of

n
–tuples of pairwise distinct points in
Y
. A map

\mathbb{B}:Y^{(4)}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is called a cross-ratio if it satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(x,y,z,w)=-\mathbb{B}(y,x,z,w);

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(x,y,z,w)=\mathbb{B}(z,w,x,y);

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(x,y,z,w)=\mathbb{B}(x,y,z,t)+\mathbb{B}(x,y,t,w);

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(x,y,z,w)+\mathbb{B}(y,z,x,w)+\mathbb{B}(z,x,y,w)=0.

For an
\epsilon
-good hyperbolic space
(X,d_{X})
, there exists a canonical cross-ratio on the boundary
\partial X
given by

(2.1)

\mathbb{B}(\xi,\eta,\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})=\ln\left(\frac{d_{\epsilon}(\xi,\xi^{\prime})d_{\epsilon}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})}{d_{\epsilon}(\xi,\eta^{\prime})d_{\epsilon}(\eta,\xi^{\prime})}\right).

Otal observed that formula (
2.1
) is equivalent to

\mathbb{B}(\xi,\eta,\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})=\lim_{(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\to(\xi,\eta,\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})}\frac{1}{2}[d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+d_{X}(y,y^{\prime})-d_{X}(x,y^{\prime})-d_{X}(y,x^{\prime})].

2.D.
Möbius homeomorphisms

Let
(K,d)
be a compact metric space without isolated points, and consider
Möbius self-homeomorphisms of
K
. The cross-ratio of a quadruple of distinct points in
K
is defined by

\mathbb{B}(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\ln\left(\frac{d(x,x^{\prime})d(y,y^{\prime})}{d(x,y^{\prime})d(y,x^{\prime})}\right).

A homeomorphism of
K
is called Möbius if it preserves the cross-ratio.

Sullivan
[
19
]
showed that Möbius maps satisfy the
geometric mean-value property

[
15
]
*Lemma 4.1, namely,

A self-homeomorphism
g
of
K
is Möbius if and only if there exists
a positive continuous function on
K
, denoted
g^{\prime}
, such that, for all
x,y\in K
,

g^{\prime}(x)g^{\prime}(y)=\frac{d^{2}(gx,gy)}{d^{2}(x,y)}.

Nica
[
15
]
has shown that the metric derivative
g^{\prime}
is Lipschitz. Moreover, the group of Möbius maps is locally compact by
[
15
]
*Proposition 5.6.

2.E.
Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure

Independently discovered by Bowen and Margulis and later reconstructed from the Patterson-Sullivan measure, the Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure (BMS measure) of a negatively curved closed manifold contains critical geometric information. It serves as a tool to study the growth of closed geodesics.

Furman
[
9
]
extended the BMS measure in a coarse geometric setting.
Here, we adopt a similar strategy.

Let
\Gamma
be a group that acts metrically properly on a good hyperbolic space
(X,o)
, with limit set
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
. We define the Poincaré series of
\Gamma
to be

P(s)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}e^{-sd(o,\gamma o)}.

Recall that the
critical exponent
of
\Gamma
is given by

\delta_{\Gamma}=\inf\{s|P(s)<\infty\},

and
\Gamma
is called of
divergence type
if
P(\delta_{\Gamma})=\infty
.
Assume that
\Gamma
is of divergence type, that
0<\delta_{\Gamma}<\infty
, and that
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is compact. Let
v
denote the Patterson–Sullivan measure on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
. For the construction of the Patterson-Sullivan measure, see
[
4
]
*Theorem 15.4.9.

There is a
\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
action on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma^{(2)}\times{\mathbb{R}}

(\gamma,t)\cdot(\xi,\eta,s)=(\gamma\xi,\gamma\eta,s+\frac{\beta_{\xi}(p,\gamma p)-\beta_{\eta}(p,\gamma p)}{2}+t),

where
\beta
is the Busemann function with base point
p\in X
. A straightforward computation shows that the measure

{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}=d_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{2\delta_{\Gamma}}{\epsilon}}v\times v\times dt
is
\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant. This is the measure used in Definition
1.1
.

Notably, the assumption of divergence type is likely unnecessary. However, throughout the paper we assume that all groups under consideration are of divergence type.

3.
A
2\frac{1}{2}
-lattice is a lattice

Consider a good hyperbolic space
(X,d)
and let
\Gamma<\operatorname{Isom}(X)
be a
2\frac{1}{2}
-lattice with a compact limit set. In this section, we establish that
\Gamma
is virtually a lattice in a locally compact group
H
. The properties of
H
are further analyzed in the next section.

3.A.
Möbius homomorphism and
H

The cross-ratio
\mathbb{B}
on
\partial X
, when restricted to the limit set
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
, is
\Gamma
-invariant. Let

\operatorname{Mob}:=\left\{{f\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\Lambda_{X}\Gamma)}\ \left|\ {f^{(4)}\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}}\right.\right\}.

\operatorname{Mob}
is a locally compact group in the compact-open topology.

With respect to the metric
d_{\epsilon}
, each
f\in\operatorname{Mob}
is an Möbius homeomorphism. Importantly, when
f
is the boundary map of an isometry
F
, the metric derivative is related to the Busemann function as follows:

f^{\prime}(\xi)=\exp(\epsilon\beta_{\xi}(p,F^{-1}p)).

We now define the group
H
, which depends on the measure
{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}
.

Let
v
be the Patterson-Sullivan measure on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
. We define
H
by

(3.1)

H:=\left\{{f\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\Lambda_{X}\Gamma)}\ \left|\ {(f\times f)_{*}(d_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{2\delta_{\Gamma}}{\epsilon}}v\times v)=d_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{2\delta_{\Gamma}}{\epsilon}}v\times v}\right.\right\}.

By the definition of the cross-ratio, all
f\in H
preserve the cross-ratio almost everywhere with respect to the measure
v^{4}
. Since the cross-ratio is continuous, it follows that
f\in\operatorname{Mob}
. Therefore,
H
is a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Mob}
and hence is locally compact.

3.B.
\partial\Gamma
is discrete

To prove that
\partial\Gamma
is discrete in
H
, we combine the following three lemmas. Note that it is unclear whether
H
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Homeo}(\partial X)
; therefore, the discreteness of
\Gamma
as a subgroup of
\operatorname{Isom}(X)
is insufficient.

Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 are standard for proper spaces. Since we are not restricted to locally compact spaces, we provide short proofs here.

Let
\partial:\operatorname{Isom}(X,d)\rightarrow\operatorname{Homeo}(\partial X)
be the boundary map.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\partial X
contains at least three points.
Then the kernel
F=\operatorname{Ker}(\partial)
has a bounded orbit.

Proof.

Fix
f\in F
and let
x=f(p)
. We first observe that the metric derivative of
f
is identically equal to
1
. Indeed, since
f
induces the identity map on
\partial X
, the geometric mean-value property gives

f^{\prime}(\xi)f^{\prime}(\eta)=1

for every pair of distinct points
\xi,\eta\in\partial X
. Since
\partial X
contains at least three points and
f^{\prime}
is positive, it follows that

f^{\prime}(\xi)=1

for every
\xi\in\partial X
. Hence, using the relation between the metric derivative and the Busemann function, we have

\beta_{\xi}(x,p)=0

for every
\xi\in\partial X
.

Let
w_{n}\in X
converge to
\xi\in\partial X
. Then

\displaystyle 2(x,\xi)_{p}

\displaystyle=2\lim_{n\to\infty}(x,w_{n})_{p}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(d(x,p)+d(w_{n},p)-d(x,w_{n})\bigr)

\displaystyle=d(x,p)-\beta_{\xi}(x,p)=d(x,p).

Thus,

(x,\xi)_{p}=\frac{1}{2}d(x,p)

for every
\xi\in\partial X
.

Fix two distinct points
\xi,\eta\in\partial X
. By the hyperbolicity inequality,

(\xi,\eta)_{p}\geq\min\{(\xi,x)_{p},(x,\eta)_{p}\}-\delta=\frac{1}{2}d(x,p)-\delta.

Therefore,

d(x,p)\leq 2(\xi,\eta)_{p}+2\delta.

The right-hand side is independent of
f
. Hence the orbit
F\cdot p
is bounded.
∎

Since
\Gamma
acts metrically properly on
X
, the kernel of the boundary map
\partial:\Gamma\to\partial\Gamma
is finite. The discreteness of
\partial\Gamma
follows from a similar argument.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
K>0
, the set
\Omega(K):=\left\{{\gamma\in\partial\Gamma}\ \left|\ {\sup_{\xi\in\partial X}|\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log(f^{-1})^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq K}\right.\right\}
is finite. In particular,

\partial\Gamma
is discrete in
\operatorname{Homeo}(\partial X)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\Omega(K)
. Then, for every
\xi\in\partial X
,

\left|\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\bigl((f^{-1})^{\prime}(\xi)\bigr)\right|\leq K.

Fix a lift of
f
to
\Gamma
, which we again denote by
f
, and let
y=f(p)
.
As in the proof of the previous lemma, for every
\xi\in\partial X
,

2(\xi,y)_{p}\geq d(y,p)-K.

Fix two distinct points
\xi,\eta\in\partial X
. By the hyperbolicity inequality,

(\xi,\eta)_{p}\geq\min\{(\xi,y)_{p},(y,\eta)_{p}\}-\delta.

Therefore,

2(\xi,\eta)_{p}\geq d(y,p)-K-2\delta,

and hence

d(y,p)\leq 2(\xi,\eta)_{p}+K+2\delta.

The right-hand side is independent of
f
. Thus, every element of
\Omega(K)
admits a lift in
\Gamma
whose displacement of
p
is uniformly bounded. Since the action of
\Gamma
on
X
is metrically proper, there are only finitely many such lifts. Consequently,
\Omega(K)
is finite.
∎

By restricting the action of
\partial\Gamma
on
\partial X
to
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
, we obtain a map
\psi:\partial\Gamma\to H
.

Lemma 3.3
.

\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
is finite and
\psi(\partial\Gamma)
is discrete in
H
.

Proof.

Assume that there is a sequence of elements
\{\gamma_{n}\}
such that
\psi(\gamma_{n})
converges to the identity map. After taking a subsequence, we may assume that for all
\xi\in\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
,

\frac{1}{2}\leq(\gamma_{n})^{\prime}(\xi)\leq 2.

Let
\eta\neq\zeta\in\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
. By the geometric mean-value property, we have

\frac{1}{4}\leq\frac{d_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{n}\eta,\gamma_{n}\zeta)}{d_{\epsilon}(\eta,\zeta)}\leq 4.

Consider any point
\theta\in\partial X
. By the triangle inequality and the definition of the visual metric, after interchanging
\eta
and
\zeta
if necessary, we may assume that

\frac{d_{\epsilon}(\eta,\zeta)}{8}\leq\frac{d_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{n}\eta,\gamma_{n}\zeta)}{2}\leq d_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{n}\theta,\gamma_{n}\eta)\leq 1.

Using the geometric mean-value property, we obtain

\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(\eta)}\frac{d_{\epsilon}^{2}(\gamma_{n}\theta,\gamma_{n}\eta)}{d_{\epsilon}^{2}(\theta,\eta)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{d_{\epsilon}^{2}(\eta,\zeta)}{64}.

Similarly,

\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(\theta)\leq\frac{2}{d_{\epsilon}^{2}(\eta,\zeta)}.

By Lemma
3.2
, the sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}
contains only finitely many distinct elements and becomes eventually constant.
∎

From now on, by abuse of notation, we identify the image
\psi(\partial\Gamma)
with
\partial\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
be a subset of
H
. Suppose that there exist constants

\kappa,M>0
such that, for every
f\in A
, there are distinct points

\xi_{f},\eta_{f}\in\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
satisfying

(1)

d_{\epsilon}(\xi_{f},\eta_{f})\geq\kappa
and
d_{\epsilon}(f\xi_{f},f\eta_{f})\geq\kappa
;

(2)

e^{-M}\leq f^{\prime}(\xi_{f})\leq e^{M}
.

Then

is precompact in

.

Proof.

The argument is the same as in Lemma

. By the geometric mean-value property,

The separation assumptions and the bound on

3.C.
\partial\Gamma
is a lattice

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of this section.

We start with a few lemmas.

Lemma 3.5
.

The action of

on

\mathcal{G}:=(\Lambda_{X}\Gamma)^{(2)}\times\mathbb{R}

h(\xi,\eta,t)=\left(h\xi,h\eta,t+\frac{\log h^{\prime}(\xi)-\log h^{\prime}(\eta)}{2\delta_{\Gamma}}\right)

Proof.

Let

For every

such that

Since

and

are compact subsets of

and

Theorem 3.6
.

The image
\psi(\partial\Gamma)
is a lattice in
H
.

Remark 3.7
.

The condition that a group is a lattice in a locally compact, second-countable group is very general. Indeed, any countable group is a lattice in itself or any finite extension of itself. Moreover, in most cases, the group
H
constructed here is discrete.

Proof.

In this proof, for simplicity, we assume
\Gamma=\psi(\partial\Gamma)
. Let

Z=(\Lambda_{X}\Gamma)^{(2)}\times\mathbb{R}.

Following
[
9
]
, consider the abstract geodesic flow

H\rightarrow\operatorname{Homeo}(Z),\quad f(x,y,t)=(f(x),f(y),t+\frac{\log f^{\prime}(x)-\log f^{\prime}(y)}{2\delta_{\Gamma}}).

First, the quotient space
Y=H\backslash Z
is locally compact and second countable. The space
Z
is locally compact and second-countable. The second countability is clear. Since the
H
-action is continuous and proper, the quotient
Y
is Hausdorff and the quotient map
q:Z\to Y
is open. Given
y=q(x)
, choose a relatively compact open neighborhood
U
of
x
. Then
q(U)
is an open neighborhood of
y
, and
\overline{q(U)}\subseteq q(\overline{U})

which is compact. Hence
Y
is locally compact.

Let

m={m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}

denote the Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan measure on
Z.

By the definition of
H
, the measure
m
is
H
-invariant. Moreover, the measure induced by
m
on
\overline{Y}=\Gamma\backslash Z

is finite.

Let

p:\overline{Y}\longrightarrow Y

be the natural projection. By assumption, the Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan measure induces a finite Radon measure, which we continue to denote by
{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}
, on
\overline{Y}
. Set

\nu=p_{*}{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}.

By the disintegration theorem, there exists a
\nu
-measurable family of probability measures

\{\mu_{y}:y\in Y\}

such that
\mu_{y}
is supported on
p^{-1}(y)
for
\nu
-almost every
y
and

{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}\,d\nu(y).

Fix
y\in Y
and choose
z\in Z
whose image in
Y
is
y
. Let

K_{z}=\operatorname{Stab}_{H}(z).

Since the action of
H
on
Z
is proper,
K_{z}
is compact, and the orbit map identifies

q^{-1}(y)=H\cdot z\cong H/K_{z},

where

q:Z\longrightarrow Y

is the quotient map. Moreover,

p^{-1}(y)\cong\Gamma\backslash H/K_{z}.

Lift the conditional measure
\mu_{y}
from
\Gamma\backslash H/K_{z}
to a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\widetilde{\mu}_{y}
on
H/K_{z}
. Explicitly, for every compactly supported Borel function
\varphi
on

, set

\int_{H/K_{z}}\varphi\,d\widetilde{\mu}_{y}=\int_{\Gamma\backslash H/K_{z}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{x}}\varphi(\gamma x)\right)d\mu_{y}([x]),

where

\Gamma_{x}=\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(x).

Since the
\Gamma
-action is proper, the sum is locally finite, and hence
\widetilde{\mu}_{y}
is a Radon measure.

By construction, the family

\{\widetilde{\mu}_{y}:y\in Y\}

is a disintegration of the Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan measure on
Z
over the map

q:Z\longrightarrow Y.

More precisely, for every compactly supported Borel function
\varphi
on
Z

\int_{Z}\varphi\,d{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}=\int_{Y}\left(\int_{q^{-1}(y)}\varphi\,d\widetilde{\mu}_{y}\right)d\nu(y).

We now use the

. Let
h\in H
. Since

and

h_{*}{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}={m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}},

the family

\{h_{*}\widetilde{\mu}_{y}:y\in Y\}

is another disintegration of the same measure over

\nu

h_{*}\widetilde{\mu}_{y}=\widetilde{\mu}_{y}

for
\nu
-almost every

Since

is second countable, we may first apply this argument to a countable dense subgroup of

H
-invariant for
\nu
-almost every

Under the identification

the measure
\widetilde{\mu}_{y}

The measure induced by

.

4.
Structure of
H

The structure of the group
H
can be determined by studying the properties of the group
\Gamma
.

The
amenable radical
of a locally compact second-countable group
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{Rad_{am}}(G)
, is the largest closed normal amenable subgroup of
G
. The starting point for the structure of
H
is a consequence of Hilbert’s fifth problem, which was first observed by Burger and Monod
[
2
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
2
]
*Theorem 3.3.3
Every locally compact group
G
contains an open, normal, finite-index subgroup
G_{0}

that contains
\mathrm{Rad_{am}}(G)
. The quotient
G_{0}/\mathrm{Rad_{am}}(G)
is topologically isomorphic to a direct product of a connected, center-free, semisimple, real Lie group without
compact factors and a totally disconnected locally compact group with trivial amenable radical.

To understand the structure of
H
, we first focus on its amenable radical, which is related to the (CAF) condition of
\Gamma
. The (CAF) condition states that every amenable commensurated subgroup of
\Gamma
is finite.

We use barycenter methods to show that
\Gamma
satisfies the (CAF) condition. Given a probability measure
v
on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
, the
L
-barycenter

B_{v}(L)
of
v
is defined by the subset of
X
satisfying:

\left\{{x\in X}\ \left|\ {\int_{\xi\in\partial X}\beta_{\xi}(x,p)dv\leq\inf_{y\in X}\int_{\xi\in\partial X}\beta_{\xi}(y,p)dv+L}\right.\right\}

Lemma 4.2
.

If
v
has no atoms, the set
B_{v}(L)
is bounded for all
L>0
.

Proof.

Let
F_{v}:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the function given by

F_{v}(x)=\int_{\xi\in\partial X}\beta_{\xi}(x,p)dv.

It suffices to show that
F_{v}(x)\to\infty
as
d(x,p)\to\infty.

Rewrite

F_{v}(x)=\int_{\xi\in\partial X}\left(d(x,p)-2(\xi,x)_{p}\right)dv=d(x,p)-2\int_{\xi\in\partial X}(\xi,x)_{p}dv.

For each
x\in X
, let

E(x):=\left\{{\xi\in\Lambda_{X}\Gamma}\ \left|\ {(\xi,x)_{p}\geq\frac{d(x,p)}{3}}\right.\right\}.

Then

2\int_{\xi\in\Lambda_{X}\Gamma}(x,\xi)_{p}dv\leq 2d(x,p)v(E(x))+\frac{2d(x,p)}{3}(1-v(E(x))).

If
\xi\in E(x)
, then by hyperbolicity,

E(x)\subset\left\{{\zeta\in\Lambda_{X}\Gamma}\ \left|\ {(\zeta,\xi)_{p}\geq\frac{d(x,p)}{3}-\delta}\right.\right\}.

Since
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is compact and
v
has no atoms, there exists a
K>0
such that
v(E(x))\leq\frac{1}{8}
when
d(x,p)\geq K
. It follows that for all
x
with
d(x,p)\geq K
,
F_{v}(x)\geq\frac{1}{6}d(x,p)
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

\Gamma
satisfies the (CAF) condition.

Proof.

Let
N
be an amenable commensurated subgroup of
\Gamma
. Then there exists an
N
-invariant probability measure
v
on the compact space
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
.

Case 1:
The support of
v
contains a point with finite
N
-orbit. Let

F_{N}=\{\xi\in\Lambda_{X}\Gamma:\ |N\xi|<\infty\}.

Since
N
is commensurated, the property of having finite orbit is unchanged after replacing
N
by a commensurable subgroup. Consequently,
F_{N}
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

Choose
\xi\in F_{N}
. Replacing
N
by a finite-index subgroup if necessary, we may assume that
N
fixes
\xi
. Since the action of
\Gamma
on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is minimal and
\Gamma
is non-elementary, we can choose
\gamma,\eta\in\Gamma
such that

\xi,\gamma\xi,\eta\xi

are pairwise distinct.

Since
N
is commensurated, the subgroup

N^{\prime}=N\cap\gamma N\gamma^{-1}\cap\eta N\eta^{-1}

has finite index in
N
. Moreover,
N^{\prime}
fixes
\xi
,
\gamma\xi
, and
\eta\xi
. Thus
N^{\prime}
fixes at least three distinct points of
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
.

By the geometric mean-value property, the metric derivatives of elements of
N^{\prime}
are uniformly bounded above and below. As in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, it follows that
N^{\prime}
is finite. Since
N^{\prime}
has finite index in
N
, the group

is finite.

Case 2:
The support of
v
contains no point with
N
-finite orbit. Then
v
has no atoms, and by Lemma
4.2
,
B_{v}(1)
is bounded. Since
N
preserves the bounded subset

,

must be finite.

This establishes the (CAF) condition of
\Gamma
.
∎

By the following theorem, the amenable radical of

is compact.

Theorem 4.4

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 5.2)
.

The amenable radical of a lattice envelope of a group with property (CAF) is compact.

Remark 4.5
.

Although the definition of the lattice envelope in
[
1
]
is assumed to be compactly generated, the proof of Theorem
4.4
is independent of this condition.

Indeed, the amenable radical of

is trivial.

Lemma 4.6
.

H
has trivial compact radical.

Proof.

Assume that
K
is a compact normal subgroup of
H
. There exists a constant
\kappa
such that for all
f\in K
and
x\in\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
,
|\ln f^{\prime}(x)|<\kappa
.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
be hyperbolic. Then, for every
f\in K
:

|\ln(\gamma^{n}f\gamma^{-n})^{\prime}(\gamma^{-})|<\kappa.

Since

\ln(\gamma^{n}f\gamma^{-n})^{\prime}(\gamma^{-})=\ln(\gamma^{n})^{\prime}(f(\gamma^{-}))+\ln f^{\prime}(\gamma^{-})+\ln(\gamma^{-n})^{\prime}(\gamma^{-}).

Thus

|\ln(\gamma^{n})^{\prime}(f(\gamma^{-}))+n\epsilon\ell_{\gamma}|<2\kappa
for all
n
. Taking a fixed
n>\lfloor\frac{2\kappa}{\epsilon\ell_{\gamma}}\rfloor
, we have
d_{\epsilon}(\gamma^{+},K\gamma^{-})>0
.

On the other hand,
K\gamma^{-}=\gamma K\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{-}=\gamma K\gamma^{-}
. Hence
K\gamma^{-}=\gamma^{-}
. Since this holds for all hyperbolic elements,
K=\{Id\}
. Therefore,
H
has a trivial compact radical.
∎

By Theorem
4.1
,
H
is virtually a product. The next lemma states that one of the two factors is trivial.

Lemma 4.7
.

The group
H
is not virtually a direct product of two nontrivial groups.

Proof.

Suppose that
H
contains an open finite-index subgroup

H_{0}=H_{1}\times H_{2},

where both
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are nontrivial. Let

p_{i}:H_{0}\longrightarrow H_{i},\qquad i=1,2,

be the coordinate projections, and regard each
H_{i}
as a subgroup of

H_{0}
.

Set

\Gamma_{0}=\psi(\partial\Gamma)\cap H_{0}.

Since
H_{0}
has finite index in
H
, the subgroup
\Gamma_{0}
has finite index in
\psi(\partial\Gamma)
. Hence

\Lambda_{X}\Gamma_{0}=\Lambda_{X}\Gamma.

As the action of
\psi(\partial\Gamma)
on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is minimal, the same is true for
\Gamma_{0}
. In particular, the action of
H_{0}
on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is minimal.

Choose a hyperbolic element

\gamma\in\Gamma_{0},

and write

\gamma=p_{1}(\gamma)p_{2}(\gamma).

Since
p_{i}(\gamma)
commutes with
\gamma
, it preserves the set

\{\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-}\}.

In fact, it fixes both points individually. Indeed, the attracting and repelling fixed points of
\gamma
are dynamically characterized by the north–south dynamics of
\gamma
, and any homeomorphism commuting with
\gamma
preserves this dynamical characterization.

At the attracting fixed point
\gamma^{+}
, the chain rule gives

\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma^{+})=p_{1}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\gamma^{+})\,p_{2}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\gamma^{+}).

Since

\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma^{+})<1,

after exchanging the two factors if necessary, we may assume that

p_{1}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\gamma^{+})<1.

We claim that
\gamma^{+}
is an isolated fixed point of
p_{1}(\gamma)
. Suppose otherwise that there exists a sequence of distinct fixed points

\xi_{n}\longrightarrow\gamma^{+}.

By the geometric mean-value property,

p_{1}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\gamma^{+})\,p_{1}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\xi_{n})=1.

Hence

p_{1}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\xi_{n})=\frac{1}{p_{1}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\gamma^{+})}>1

for every
n
. On the other hand, continuity of the metric derivative gives

p_{1}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\xi_{n})\longrightarrow p_{1}(\gamma)^{\prime}(\gamma^{+})<1,

a contradiction. Thus
\gamma^{+}
is an isolated fixed point of
p_{1}(\gamma)
.

We next claim that the fixed-point set of
p_{1}(\gamma)
is

\operatorname{Fix}\bigl(p_{1}(\gamma)\bigr)=\{\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-}\}.

Indeed, suppose

\xi\in\operatorname{Fix}\bigl(p_{1}(\gamma)\bigr)\setminus\{\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-}\}.

Since
p_{1}(\gamma)
commutes with
\gamma
, every point
\gamma^{n}\xi
is fixed by
p_{1}(\gamma)
. By the north–south dynamics of
\gamma
,

\gamma^{n}\xi\longrightarrow\gamma^{+}

as
n\to+\infty
, contradicting the fact that
\gamma^{+}
is an isolated fixed point.

Since every element of
H_{2}
commutes with
p_{1}(\gamma)
, it preserves

\operatorname{Fix}\bigl(p_{1}(\gamma)\bigr)=\{\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-}\}.

Moreover, the same north–south argument shows that each element of
H_{2}
fixes
\gamma^{+}
and
\gamma^{-}
individually. Therefore,

\{\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Fix}(H_{2}).

The set
\operatorname{Fix}(H_{2})
is closed and
H_{0}
-invariant. It is invariant under
H_{2}
by definition, and under
H_{1}
because
H_{1}
commutes with
H_{2}
. Since the
H_{0}
-action on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is minimal, we conclude that

\operatorname{Fix}(H_{2})=\Lambda_{X}\Gamma.

The action of
H
on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
is faithful, so

H_{2}=\{1\},

a contradiction.
∎

Finally, we can classify the possible structures of

.

.

\Gamma

Suppose that

Let

Since

, the group

If

\partial({\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)})\cap H\subset\operatorname{Com}_{H}(\partial\Gamma).

Furthermore, if

[{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}:\Gamma]=\infty,

then

[\partial({\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)})\cap H:\partial\Gamma]=\infty.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we replace
X
with
(\Gamma,d)
.

We first prove that
\operatorname{Ker}(\partial)
is trivial. Let

s:\Gamma_{1}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{2}

represent an element of
{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}
such that
\partial s=\operatorname{Id}
.

Recall that we identify
(\Gamma,d)
with the orbit
\Gamma p
. Choosing coset representatives for
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
, we may extend
s
to a rough self-isometry of
(\Gamma,d)
, which we continue to denote by
s
.

Let
\nu
be a nonatomic probability measure on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
. Since
\partial s=\operatorname{Id}
, we have

s_{*}\nu=\nu.

If
s
is a
(1,C)
-rough isometry, then

s(B_{\nu}(1))\subset B_{\nu}(1+2C).

The same argument applies to
\gamma_{*}\nu
. Since

B_{\gamma_{*}\nu}(A)=\gamma B_{\nu}(A),

Lemma
4.2
gives a constant
M>0
, independent of
\gamma
, such that

d(s(\gamma),\gamma)\leq M

for every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}
.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}
, write

s(\gamma)=\gamma\tau(\gamma),

where

d(\tau(\gamma),e)\leq M.

Since
s
is a homomorphism, for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{1}
we have

\tau(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\gamma_{2}^{-1}\tau(\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2}\tau(\gamma_{2}).

Consequently,

d(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\tau(\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2},e)\leq 2M

for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{1}
, and hence

d(\tau(\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2},\gamma_{2})\leq 2M.

Since
\Gamma_{1}
has finite index in
\Gamma
, it is at finite Hausdorff distance from
\Gamma
. Thus
\tau(\gamma_{1})
acts at bounded distance from the identity on
(\Gamma,d)
, and therefore induces the identity on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
. Hence

\tau(\gamma_{1})\in N:=\operatorname{Ker}(\Gamma\longrightarrow\partial\Gamma)

for every
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{1}
.

By Lemma
3.3
, the group
N
is finite. Let

\Gamma_{1}^{0}=\ker\bigl(\Gamma_{1}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(N)\bigr).

For
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{1}^{0}
, the preceding identity becomes

\tau(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\tau(\gamma_{1})\tau(\gamma_{2}).

Thus

\tau|_{\Gamma_{1}^{0}}:\Gamma_{1}^{0}\longrightarrow N

is a homomorphism. Since
N
is finite,

\Gamma_{1}^{1}:=\ker(\tau|_{\Gamma_{1}^{0}})

has finite index in
\Gamma_{1}
. On
\Gamma_{1}^{1}
we have

s=\operatorname{Id}.

Therefore
s
represents the identity element of
\operatorname{AbCom}(\Gamma)
, proving that

\operatorname{Ker}(\partial)=\{1\}.

We next prove the commensurator inclusion. Let

s:\Gamma_{1}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{2}

represent an element of
{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}
. For every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}
,

\partial s\,\partial\gamma\,(\partial s)^{-1}=\partial\bigl(s\gamma s^{-1}\bigr).

Hence

\partial s\,\partial\Gamma_{1}\,(\partial s)^{-1}=\partial\Gamma_{2}.

Since
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
have finite index in
\Gamma
, both
\partial\Gamma_{1}
and
\partial\Gamma_{2}
have finite index in
\partial\Gamma
. Therefore, whenever
\partial s\in H
,

\partial s\in\operatorname{Com}_{H}(\partial\Gamma).

Thus

\partial({\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)})\cap H\subset\operatorname{Com}_{H}(\partial\Gamma).

It remains to prove the final assertion. Let

s:\Gamma_{1}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{2}

represent an element of
{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}
. We normalize the Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure of a finite-index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}<\Gamma
by

{m_{{\Gamma^{\prime}}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}(\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash\Lambda_{X}\Gamma^{(2)}\times\mathbb{R})=1.

Then

{m_{{\Gamma_{1}}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}=\frac{1}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{1}]}{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}},

and

{m_{{\Gamma_{2}}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}=\frac{1}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{2}]}{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}.

Moreover,

s_{*}{m_{{\Gamma_{1}}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}={m_{{\Gamma_{2}}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}.

Therefore,

s_{*}{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}=\frac{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{1}]}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{2}]}{m_{{\Gamma}}^{\operatorname{BMS}}}.

Define

\Delta:{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{\times}

by

\Delta(s)=\frac{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{1}]}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{2}]}.

This is independent of the choice of representative of
s
, and defines a group homomorphism. By the definition of
H
,

\partial s\in H\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Delta(s)=1.

Hence

\partial({\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)})\cap H=\partial(\operatorname{Ker}\Delta).

Also,

\Gamma<\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta).

Assume now that

[{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}:\Gamma]=\infty.

If

[\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta):\Gamma]=\infty,

then, since
\operatorname{Ker}(\partial)
is trivial,

[\partial(\operatorname{Ker}\Delta):\partial\Gamma]=\infty.

Thus,

[\partial({\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)})\cap H:\partial\Gamma]=\infty,

and we are done.

Suppose instead that

[\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta):\Gamma]<\infty.

Set

K=\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta).

Since
K
is a normal subgroup of
{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}
, for every representative

s:\Gamma_{1}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{2}

we have

s\Gamma_{1}s^{-1}=\Gamma_{2},\quad sKs^{-1}=K.

Therefore conjugation by
s
gives an isomorphism between
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
inside
K
, and hence

[K:\Gamma_{1}]=[K:\Gamma_{2}].

Since

\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}<\Gamma<K,

we have

[K:\Gamma_{1}]=[K:\Gamma][\Gamma:\Gamma_{1}]

and

[K:\Gamma_{2}]=[K:\Gamma][\Gamma:\Gamma_{2}].

It follows that

[\Gamma:\Gamma_{1}]=[\Gamma:\Gamma_{2}].

Therefore

\Delta(s)=1.

Since
s
was arbitrary,

{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}=\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta).

Consequently,

[{\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}:\Gamma]=[\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta):\Gamma]<\infty,

contradicting the assumption.

Hence

[\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta):\Gamma]=\infty.

Since
\operatorname{Ker}(\partial)
is trivial, we conclude that

[\partial({\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)})\cap H:\partial\Gamma]=\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.A.
Proof of Theorem
A

Let

N=\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(\Gamma\longrightarrow\partial\Gamma\bigr).

By Lemma
3.3
, the group
N
is finite. We identify

\bar{\Gamma}=\Gamma/N

with the boundary image
\partial\Gamma
.

Let

C_{\mathrm{com}}=\partial\bigl({\operatorname{GeCom}\left({\Gamma},{d}\right)}\bigr)\cap H

and define

H_{\mathrm{com}}=\overline{C_{\mathrm{com}}}^{H}.

By Lemma
5.1
,

C_{\mathrm{com}}\subseteq\operatorname{Com}_{H}(\partial\Gamma),

and

[C_{\mathrm{com}}:\partial\Gamma]=\infty.

Moreover,
C_{\mathrm{com}}
is dense in
H_{\mathrm{com}}
by definition.

The group
H_{\mathrm{com}}
is a closed subgroup of
H
containing

\partial\Gamma
. By Corollary
3.8
, the group
\partial\Gamma
is a
lattice in
H_{\mathrm{com}}
.

We first observe that
H_{\mathrm{com}}
is nondiscrete. Indeed, if it
were discrete, then the density of
C_{\mathrm{com}}
would imply

C_{\mathrm{com}}=H_{\mathrm{com}}.

Since a lattice in a discrete group has finite index, this would give

[C_{\mathrm{com}}:\partial\Gamma]<\infty,

contrary to Lemma
5.1
.

The structural classification in Corollary
4.9
, applied
to the lattice envelope
H_{\mathrm{com}}
, now gives two possibilities.

Suppose first that
H_{\mathrm{com}}
is a nondiscrete, totally
disconnected locally compact group. Then
\partial\Gamma
is a lattice in

H_{\mathrm{com}}
, and

C_{\mathrm{com}}\subseteq\operatorname{Com}_{H_{\mathrm{com}}}(\partial\Gamma)

is dense in
H_{\mathrm{com}}
. This is the first conclusion of the
theorem.

Suppose next that there is a connected, center-free, simple real Lie
group
G^{0}
of real rank one such that

G^{0}\leq H_{\mathrm{com}}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G^{0}).

Set

\Gamma^{0}=\partial\Gamma\cap G^{0}.

Then
\Gamma^{0}
is a lattice in
G^{0}
and has finite index in

\partial\Gamma
. Since
C_{\mathrm{com}}
is dense in
H_{\mathrm{com}}
and
commensurates
\partial\Gamma
, the commensurator of
\Gamma^{0}
is dense in

G^{0}
. By the commensurator criterion,
\Gamma^{0}
, and hence

\partial\Gamma
, is arithmetic.

It remains to identify the metric. Let
\mathbf{H}
be the rank-one symmetric space associated with
G^{0}
. Since

H_{\mathrm{com}}\subset H,

the metric derivative defines a continuous cocycle

c:H_{\mathrm{com}}\times\Lambda_{X}\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad c(h,\xi)=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log h^{\prime}(\xi),

which restricts to the Busemann cocycle associated with
(X,d)
on
\Lambda_{X}\Gamma
.

For a rank-one simple real Lie group, the space of such cocycles, up to strict equivalence, is one-dimensional
[
22
]
*Proposition 4.2.13. Therefore, after multiplication by a positive constant, the cocycle
c
is strictly equivalent to the standard Busemann cocycle of
\mathbf{H}
. Consequently, there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\lambda\ell_{d}(\gamma)=\ell_{\mathbf{H}}(\gamma)

for every infinite-order element
\gamma
in a finite-index subgroup of
\partial\Gamma
.

Both actions are non-elementary and hence contain contracting elements. The marked-length-spectrum rigidity theorem for actions with contracting elements
[
21
]
*Theorem 5.1 therefore applies and gives a rough isometry

(\partial\Gamma,\lambda d)\longrightarrow\partial\Gamma\cdot o\subset\mathbf{H}

for some
o\in\mathbf{H}
.

Thus the induced metric on

\bar{\Gamma}=\Gamma/N

is arithmetic.
∎

5.B.
Proof of Theorem
B

Let

N=\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(\Gamma\longrightarrow\partial\Gamma\bigr).

As above,
N
is finite, and we identify

\bar{\Gamma}=\Gamma/N

with
\partial\Gamma
.

Define

H_{\mathrm{rough}}=\overline{\mathrm{H}_{\Gamma}(X)}^{H}.

By definition, this is a closed subgroup of
H
containing
\partial\Gamma
. Hence, by Corollary
3.8
,
\partial\Gamma
is a lattice in
H_{\mathrm{rough}}
.

The group
H_{\mathrm{rough}}
is nondiscrete. Indeed, if it were
discrete, the density of
\mathrm{H}_{\Gamma}(X)
in

H_{\mathrm{rough}}
would imply

\mathrm{H}_{\Gamma}(X)=H_{\mathrm{rough}}.

Since
\partial\Gamma
is a lattice in the discrete group

H_{\mathrm{rough}}
, it would have finite index. This would contradict
the assumption

[\mathrm{H}_{\Gamma}(X):\partial\Gamma]=\infty.

Applying Corollary
4.9
to
H_{\mathrm{rough}}
, we again
obtain two possibilities.

If
H_{\mathrm{rough}}
is a nondiscrete, totally disconnected locally
compact group, then
\partial\Gamma
is a lattice in

H_{\mathrm{rough}}
. This gives the first conclusion of the theorem.

Otherwise, there is a connected, center-free, simple real Lie group

G^{0}
of real rank one such that

G^{0}\leq H_{\mathrm{rough}}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G^{0}).

Let
\mathbf{H}
be the corresponding rank-one symmetric space. Then

\partial\Gamma\cap G^{0}
is a lattice in
G^{0}
, and a finite-index subgroup of
\partial\Gamma
is a lattice in
the full isometry group of
\mathbf{H}
.

As in the proof of Theorem
A
, the Busemann cocycle of

(X,d)
extends to
H_{\mathrm{rough}}
. Since the relevant space of
Busemann cocycles for a rank-one simple Lie group is one-dimensional up
to strict equivalence
[
22
]
*Proposition 4.2.13, there exists
\lambda>0
such that the marked
length spectrum of
(\partial\Gamma,\lambda d)
agrees with the marked length
spectrum of the orbit metric in
\mathbf{H}
. The coarse
marked-length-spectrum rigidity theorem
[
21
]
*Theorem 5.1 then gives a rough isometry

(\partial\Gamma,\lambda d)\longrightarrow\partial\Gamma\cdot o\subseteq\mathbf{H}

for some
o\in\mathbf{H}
.

Therefore, the induced metric on

\bar{\Gamma}=\Gamma/N

is rank-one locally symmetric. This proves
Theorem
B
.
∎

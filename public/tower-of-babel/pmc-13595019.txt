41 | Randomized, Controlled Trial of Remote Non‐Stress Tests for High‐Risk Pregnancies (the REACTIVE Trial)

Rebecca F. Hamm; Nadav Schwartz; Meaghan G. McCabe; Markolline Forkpa; Sunni L. Mumford; Naeemah Turner; Samuel Parry

University of Pennsylvania, Perelman School of Medicine, Philadelphia, PA

Objective: Women with high‐risk pregnancies often require non‐stress tests (NSTs) to reduce stillbirth risk. The burden of in‐office NSTs may exacerbate care disparities. We evaluated the impact of a self‐administered remote monitoring system on NST adherence, with the goal of improving access.

Study Design: This multi‐site clinical trial randomized singleton pregnancies from 32–36 weeks’ gestation with indication for antenatal testing 1:1 to (1) remote NSTs via the INVU™ system (Nuvo Group Inc, Aventura FL), or (2) standard in‐office NSTs. All NSTs were interpreted real time by the same clinical team. In the remote arm, in‐person evaluation was recommended for nonreactive/incomplete NSTs or blood pressure/symptom concerns. The primary outcome was NST adherence (# of completed/expected visits). Stratified analyses were performed by insurance status. Secondary outcomes of testing, patient acceptability (Theory Informed Acceptability Scale; score 8–40), and maternal/neonatal outcomes were evaluated.

Results: 385 patients were included (remote n = 193; in‐office n = 192) from 5/2023–2/2025, without baseline differences (Table 1). Remote monitoring improved median NST adherence (92.0% v. 85.5%, p = 0.02; Table 2). In stratified analyses, remote monitoring particularly increased adherence among publicly insured patients (73.0% v. 60.0%, p = 0.02), while those privately insured had median 100% adherence regardless of arm. Remote patients completed a median of 76.9% of their remote attempted NSTs entirely out of office, significantly reducing in‐person evaluations (3.3 ± 2.9 v. 6.3 ± 3.4, p < 0.001). Time per NST in the remote arm was modestly longer (+7.6 min, p < 0.001). Reactivity rate, need for biophysical profiles, and recommendations for delivery as a result of testing did not differ by arm. The remote system improved acceptability of fetal monitoring (32.0 ± 6.1 v. 30.5 ± 5.8, p < 0.001), without differences in maternal/neonatal outcomes.

Conclusion: Self‐administered remote NST monitoring improved adherence, especially among patients with public insurance, while reducing in‐person visits and maintaining perinatal outcomes.

42 | Virtual Reality‐Based Empathy Training Was Associated With Higher Patient Experience in Early Pregnancy Demise Care

Dana Cohen; Eran Weiner; Noa Ben‐Shoshan; Amir Abu Abeid; Ann Dekalo

Edith Wolfson Medical Center, Holon, Israel

Objective: Communicating a diagnosis of early pregnancy demise is emotionally demanding for patients and clinicians. Empathy and clear communication influence patients’ experience of care, yet the most effective training modalities for residents remain uncertain.

Therfore we aimed to evaluate whether a brief virtual reality (VR) workshop for obstetrics and gynecology residents improves patient‐reported experience during consultations for early pregnancy demise.

Study Design: We conducted a randomized controlled study in a busy gynecological triage setting. Residents were allocated to a half‐day VR workshop or usual training. The workshop used locally filmed VR scenarios, allowing residents to experience the encounter from both the patient and partner perspectives, followed by facilitated reflection. Adult women diagnosed with missed miscarriage were asked to complete the Universal Patient Centeredness Questionnaire (UPC‐Q; 14 items scored 1–4; total score range 14–56) after their initial consultation. The primary outcome was the total UPC‐Q score. Between‐group comparisons were performed using unadjusted two‐sample tests; subgroup analyses by resident seniority were exploratory.

Results: Overall, 157 patients completed the questionnaire: 82 (52%) were seen by residents who completed the VR workshop, and 75 (48%) by residents without VR training. Baseline characteristics were reported as similar between groups. Mean (SD) UPC‐Q scores were 55.1 (2.1) in the VR group versus 54.0 (4.2) in the control group (mean difference 1.1 points; 95% CI 0.05–2.15; p = 0.03). Scores did not differ by resident seniority (PGY 1–2 vs PGY 3–5; p = 0.30).

Conclusion: A brief VR workshop was associated with a small but statistically significant improvement in patient‐reported experience during early pregnancy demise consultations. The high overall scores suggest a ceiling effect; larger, cluster‐adjusted studies with longer follow‐up are needed to confirm clinical significance and durability of the effect.

43 | Rethinking Antepartum Care: A Hybrid Model of Intensive Remote and In‐Person Surveillance

Abraham Tsur; Shlomo Toussia‐Cohen; Esther Galler; Ronen Fluss; Amit Huppert; Galia Barkai; Eyal Zimlichman; Michal Fishel Bartal; Shali Mazaki‐Tovi; Rakefet Yoeli‐Ullman

Sheba Medical Center, Ramat Gan, Israel

Objective: In 2023, we established a hybrid antepartum unit integrating intense remote surveillance (video clinical assessment & vitals mirroring inpatient evaluation, at least bi‐daily fetal heart rate monitoring, and daily ultrasound) with biweekly in‐person visits (physical examination, fetal growth/Doppler ultrasound, and labs). The unit was embedded within the antepartum department, enabling rapid in‐person evaluation when needed. This approach was built on the seminal HOTEL study (Bekker MN et al., Lancet Digit Health, 2023), which demonstrated non‐inferiority of home telemonitoring but reported isolated adverse events. We assessed maternal/fetal deterioration detection rates and overall clinical outcomes.

Study Design: Prospectively collected admissions to the hybrid unit (10/23–02/26) were compared with the remote arm of the prospective HOTEL study. The primary outcome was defined similarly to HOTEL study as a composite of fetal & neonatal mortality, Apgar < 7 at 5 minutes/umbilical artery pH <7.05, maternal morbidity, NICU admission and intrapartum caesarean delivery.

Results: 220 hybrid vs. 100 HOTEL admissions were compared. In both cohorts, the majority of admission were due to fetal growth restriction, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, history of fetal death, and recurrent decreased fetal movement (79.1% and 61% of admission in Hybrid and Hotel cohorts respectively, Table 1). The hybrid cohort had higher rates of obesity, gestational diabetes, thrombophilia, thyroid disease, and congenital anomalies, but lower nulliparity and chronic hypertension. In the hybrid setting, there were 34 and 54 occurrences of patients brought in for additional in‐person assessment due to suspected maternal or fetal deterioration, respectively. The primary composite outcome was significantly lower in the Hybrid cohort (17% vs. 31%, p < 0.01, Table 2).

Conclusion: A hybrid care model, combining intense remote surveillance and in‐person visits, may enhance early detection of maternal/fetal deterioration and reduce adverse outcomes while maintaining home‐based care.

44 | Safety and Efficacy of Remote Fetal Assessment via Self‐Performed Home Ultrasound: The HOLA Multicenter Study

Ronit Shtrichman1; Rachel Newman2; Erkan Kalafat3; Lawrence D. Platt4; Sarah E. Little5; Paola Aghajanian6; Kris Ann Trychon7; Rishi Balraj B. Chopra7; Nicola F. Tavella8; Angela T. Bianco8; Naheed Visram9

1Pulsenmore LTD, Omer, Israel; 2UTHealth Houson, Houston, TX; 3Koç University Hospital, Istanbul, Turkey; 4UCLA David Geffen School of Medicine, Los Angeles, CA; 5Maternal‐Fetal Medicine, Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston, MA; 6Center for Fetal and Maternal Ultrasound, UCLA School of Medicine, Los Angeles, CA; 7Department of Maternal Fetal Medicine, Brigham and Women's Hospital, Boston, MA; 8Maternal‐Fetal Medicine, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, New York, NY; 9Pulsenmore LTD, Omer, United Kingdom

Objective: To evaluate the safety, efficacy, and user satisfaction of a self‐performed home ultrasound platform for assessing fetal cardiac activity (FCA), amniotic fluid volume (AFV), and fetal movement (FM), comparing remote performance to standard in‐clinic examinations.

Study Design: This prospective multicenter study was conducted at four U.S. medical centers. Eligible participants (singleton gestation >14 weeks, BMI ≤ 40 kg/m2) performed three weekly sessions. Each session included three scans within 24 hours: app‐guided (AG; self‐scan with tutorial), clinician‐guided (CG; synchronous telehealth), and in‐clinic (IC; clinician‐performed standard of care ultrasound). Scans were interpreted independently and blindly by a panel of 10 experts. Primary endpoints were safety and performance; success rates for FCA visualization, and AFV diagnostic reliability, using real‐time IC assessment as the ground truth. Usability and satisfaction were tested using questionnaire.

Results: The analysis included 162 women and 1370 total scans. No device or procedure‐related serious adverse events occurred. Pooled success rates for FCA determination were 99.2% (95% CI, 98.0–99.7%) for CG and 96.5% (95% CI, 95.4–97.4%) for AG scans. FM was detected in >99% of scans across both remote modalities. For AFV assessment, CG mode demonstrated higher sensitivity (75.4%) compared to IC offline clips (61.3%) and AG mode (51.4%). Specificity was high for IC (96.4%) and AG (94.3%) modes, while CG was lower (83.5%). Image quality was rated diagnostic (ACEP ≥ 3) in 98.4% (CG) and 95.2% (AG) of scans. Subgroup analyses confirmed that BMI and gestational age did not impact visualization success (p > 0.05). Overall, 83% of participants reported a positive experience, and 93% found the device easy to use.

Conclusion: The home‐ultrasound platform is a safe and effective for remote fetal assessment in a diverse U.S. population. Its robust performance across gestational ages and BMI categories provides a reliable diagnostic foundation for integrating home‐based imaging into hybrid prenatal care models.

45 | Analysis of Labor Partograms Using Unsupervised Machine Learning

Liav Feldman1; Joshua Guedalia1; Simcha Yagel1; Yishai Sompolinsky1; Michal Lipschuetz2

1Hadassah Hebrew University Medical Center, Jeruslaem, Israel; 2Henrietta Szold Hadassah Hebrew University School of Nursing in the Faculty of Medicine, Jerusalem, Israel

Objective: Traditional labor curves have been shown to inadequately reflect the wide natural variation in labor progression. This oversimplification often results in unnecessary interventions, including caesarean deliveries, due to misclassification of normal labor as pathological. This study aims to create personalized labor curves that dynamically assess labor progress based on individual characteristics and local population data.

Study Design: This is a retrospective, observational, analytic study using de‐identified data from approximately 120,000 deliveries at Hadassah Medical Center (2015–2025). Data includes maternal demographics, fetal characteristics, and labor progress (e.g., cervical dilation). Unsupervised learning models were trained on this data to cluster patients and generate different partograms without using outcome data. The model generates personalized labor trajectory graphs at each cervical dilation stage.

Results: Cluster analysis identified distinct subgroups based on demographic and early labor (3–6 cm) characteristics, with each cluster exhibiting a unique physiological birth trajectory. Subsequent modeling of the 6–10 cm active phase demonstrated superior predictive accuracy for each subgroup compared to the traditional Zhang partogram, as evidenced by significantly lower Mean Absolute Errors (MAE).

Conclusion: This work presents a novel application of unsupervised machine learning to generate personalized labor partograms based on retrospective clinical data. While initial clustering models show potential for improving labor assessment, further validation and refinement are necessary to support clinical integration and outcome‐based predictive tools.

46 | Accurate Prediction of Intrapartum Cesarean Delivery in Obese Women Using Machine Learning

Daniel Gabbai1; Itamar Gilboa2; Roza Berkovitz Shperling1; Lee Reicher1; Yariv Yogev1; Emmanuel Attali1; Anat Lavie1

1Lis Maternity & Women's Hospital, Tel‐Aviv Sourasky Medical Center, Tel‐Aviv, Israel, Tel Aviv, Israel; 2Lis Hospital for Women's Health, Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center, Tel Aviv, Israel

Objective: To identify risk factors for intrapartum cesarean delivery (CD) among women with obesity (BMI ≥ 30) and to evaluate whether a machine‐learning model (XGBoost) can improve prediction of this outcome compared with a previously developed regression‐based risk score.

Study Design: A retrospective cohort study conducted at a single university‐affiliated tertiary medical center. All women with a pre‐pregnancy BMI ≥ 30 who initiated a trial of labor between 2012–2024 were included. Women who underwent elective cesarean delivery or had missing outcome data were excluded. Maternal, obstetric, and intrapartum characteristics were compared between women who delivered vaginally and those who required an intrapartum CD. Predictors were evaluated using extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and model performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and SHAP‐based interpretability.

Results: Among 19,870 women with obesity who attempted a trial of labor, 5482 (27.6%) underwent intrapartum cesarean delivery. The XGBoost model demonstrated excellent discrimination with an AUC of 0.94 (95% CI 0.93–0.96) (Figure 1), outperforming a previously developed regression‐based score. Key predictors included cervical dilatation at admission, epidural anesthesia, nulliparity, maternal BMI and age, oxytocin use, birthweight, inflammatory markers (NLR and WBC), and previous cesarean scar (Figure 2).

Conclusion: A machine learning based model accurately predicts intrapartum cesarean delivery in women with obesity and may serve as a valuable tool to support individualized counseling and delivery planning.

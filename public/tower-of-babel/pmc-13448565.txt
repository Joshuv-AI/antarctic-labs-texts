Operations research (OR) methods, including mathematical programming, heuristics, metaheuristics and simulation-based optimisation, have been widely applied to workforce scheduling problems in healthcare. Introduction

Emergency departments (EDs) represent a critical component of healthcare systems, providing immediate care for patients with acute and life-threatening conditions.1 2 Over recent decades, EDs worldwide have experienced increasing demand, rising patient complexity and persistent resource constraints.3 4 These pressures have led to well-documented challenges including overcrowding, prolonged waiting times, staff workload imbalance and reduced quality of care.1 3 Efficient management of healthcare resources, particularly human resources, is therefore essential to ensure timely access to emergency services and maintain the quality and safety of clinical services.5

Among the various operational challenges faced by EDs, the scheduling and rostering of healthcare personnel, especially nurses and physicians, (rostering, ie, the assignment of clinical staff to shifts over a defined planning horizon subject to operational and contractual constraints) play a central role in determining the performance of emergency services.6 7 Rostering decisions influence staff workload distribution, continuity of care, staff satisfaction and patient outcomes.8 9 Inadequate scheduling may lead to understaffing during peak demand periods or inefficient allocation of personnel during low-demand periods. Furthermore, ED staffing must consider multiple operational constraints, including shift regulations, skill mix requirements, contractual obligations, staff preferences and fluctuating patient demand.10 11 These factors make personnel rostering a complex decision-making problem that is difficult to manage using traditional manual planning approaches.

Operations research (OR) and mathematical optimisation methods have long been applied to workforce scheduling problems in healthcare.7 12 Techniques such as integer programming, constraint programming, stochastic programming, heuristics, metaheuristics and simulation-based optimisation have been developed to support complex scheduling decisions under multiple constraints.11 13 These methods allow decision-makers to identify near-optimal or optimal staffing configurations while considering operational constraints and service objectives. In healthcare operations management, OR approaches have been applied to various problems including nurse scheduling, physician shift allocation, patient flow management and capacity planning.14 15 In the context of emergency care, such methods can support more efficient staff deployment, reduce waiting times and improve service responsiveness.6 16

Despite the growing body of literature on healthcare scheduling, studies focusing specifically on the application of OR algorithms to personnel rostering in EDs remain dispersed across different disciplines, including healthcare management, computer science, OR and industrial engineering.11 15 Previous reviews have examined general healthcare workforce scheduling or nurse rostering problems, but to our knowledge there has been no systematic synthesis focusing specifically on the algorithms and modelling approaches used for ED personnel rostering.7 10 Given the unique operational characteristics of EDs, such as highly variable demand, urgent care requirements and multidisciplinary team structures, a dedicated review is needed to understand how optimisation techniques have been applied in this setting.1 3

The objective of this systematic review is therefore to identify and synthesise the OR algorithms and mathematical modelling approaches used for rostering and shift scheduling of ED personnel, including nurses and physicians. The review aims to categorise the types of optimisation methods used, analyse the modelling assumptions and constraints considered and examine the performance outcomes reported in the literature. By providing a comprehensive overview of existing approaches, this review seeks to highlight methodological trends, identify gaps in current research and inform future development of optimisation models for workforce management in emergency care settings.

Methods and analysis

This protocol has been developed in accordance with the guidelines of the Cochrane Methods Group,17 the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines,18 and the PRISMA for Abstracts.19 In alignment with these standards, a PRISMA-P checklist is provided (online supplemental material 1). Any significant amendments to the protocol will be documented and published alongside the results of the systematic review. The study protocol has been registered with the PROSPERO systematic review registry (CRD420261337311).20 The review process, including study screening, data extraction and quality appraisal, is scheduled to begin in July 2026, with completion expected by May 2027.

Objectives

The objective of this systematic review is to identify, categorise and critically analyse OR algorithms used to support the rostering and shift scheduling of personnel working in hospital EDs. Specifically, the review aims to examine the types of optimisation models and algorithmic approaches proposed in the literature, including mathematical programming, heuristics, metaheuristics, matheuristics and simulation-based optimisation methods. In addition, the review will analyse the modelling structures used in these studies, including decision variables, operational constraints, objective functions and evaluation metrics. By synthesising the methodological characteristics and reported performance of the models, the review seeks to provide a structured overview of how OR techniques have been applied to ED personnel scheduling and to identify methodological trends, limitations and research gaps. The findings are expected to support researchers and healthcare decision-makers in understanding the current state of the literature and in guiding the future development of optimisation-based decision-support tools for ED workforce management.

Research question

We have adopted the Population, Intervention, Comparator, Outcomes, Timing and Settings (PICOTS) mnemonic framework21 22 to inform the development of our review objective and research questions. To guide the review, we propose the following research questions: What OR algorithms and optimisation approaches have been used to support the rostering and shift scheduling of ED personnel, and how are these models formulated and evaluated in the scientific literature? To ensure a rigorous and transparent methodology, we have established well-defined eligibility criteria for study selection.

Eligibility criteria

Population

Studies addressing the rostering or shift scheduling of healthcare personnel working in hospital EDs will be included. The population of interest includes clinical staff directly involved in emergency care delivery, such as nurses, physicians, residents, nurse practitioners and other healthcare professionals working in EDs. Studies involving multidisciplinary ED teams will also be included if the scheduling model explicitly addresses the assignment or scheduling of ED personnel.

Intervention

The intervention of interest is the application of OR or optimisation-based modelling approaches designed to support or automate rostering and shift scheduling decisions for ED personnel. Eligible approaches include, but are not limited to, mathematical programming methods (eg, integer programming, mixed-integer programming, constraint programming), exact optimisation algorithms, heuristic and metaheuristic algorithms, matheuristics, simulation-based optimisation and hybrid optimisation approaches combining multiple techniques.

Comparison

Where available, studies may compare the proposed optimisation approach with alternative scheduling methods, baseline staffing policies, manual scheduling procedures or other optimisation algorithms. However, the presence of a comparator is not required for inclusion. Studies describing and evaluating an optimisation model for ED personnel scheduling without a comparator will also be eligible.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes of interest relate to the characteristics and performance of optimisation models used for ED personnel scheduling. These include the type of algorithm applied, modelling structure, decision variables, constraints and objective functions incorporated in the scheduling models. Additional outcomes include performance indicators used to evaluate scheduling solutions, such as computational efficiency, solution quality, feasibility of schedules, workload balance, fairness of shift allocation, compliance with staffing constraints, reduction of overtime and, where reported, impacts on ED operational performance.

Timing

No restrictions will be applied regarding the time horizon considered within the scheduling models (eg, daily, weekly or monthly rostering). The review will include studies published from database inception onwards, with no start-date restriction, reflecting the growing application of OR methods to healthcare workforce scheduling.

Study design/characteristics

The review will focus on rostering and scheduling problems occurring within hospital EDs. Studies conducted using real-world hospital data or simulated ED environments will be eligible, provided that the scheduling problem specifically concerns ED personnel. Studies focusing exclusively on workforce scheduling in other healthcare settings (eg, inpatient wards, operating rooms or outpatient clinics) or in non-healthcare sectors will be excluded unless the model is explicitly applied to ED staff scheduling.

This review will encompass a wide range of study designs, including systematic reviews, meta-analyses, randomised controlled trials, cohort studies, case–control studies, cross-sectional analyses, case reports, case series and conference proceedings that explore the application of OR algorithms used to support the rostering and shift scheduling of personnel working in hospital EDs. The inclusion of case reports and conference papers is motivated by their potential to showcase emerging practices and innovative methodologies, especially in operational contexts where formal research may still be limited. Nonetheless, these sources will be critically appraised and clearly categorised as ‘grey literature’ or ‘non-peer-reviewed’, with their influence on the overall synthesis assessed through sensitivity analyses.

Systematic reviews and meta-analyses will serve dual purposes: first, as tools for identifying additional relevant studies through backward citation tracking; and second, as benchmarks to contextualise our findings within broader research trends.

Additional sources such as government publications, public health reports and analytical white papers will be consulted to identify relevant primary studies. However, documents such as study protocols, academic theses and letters to the editor will be excluded from this review due to their limited empirical contribution and lack of peer-reviewed validation. Information sources

A comprehensive search of academic databases, including Google Scholar, Web of Science, MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus and ABI/Inform, will be conducted by a specialised librarian (FB) with expertise in healthcare reviews and systematic review methodology. The search will identify potentially relevant articles using predefined strategies. In addition, a manual search of reference lists from selected studies will be performed to identify further relevant literature. This manual search will follow the backward snowballing technique, which involves examining the reference lists of included articles to uncover additional sources. To ensure saturation of the literature, hand searches will also be conducted for selected key journals.

Search strategy

The search strategy (online supplemental material 2) will be reviewed by the first (LM) and second (MGN) reviewers, under the supervision of the third reviewer (PL). It has been structured according to the PICOTS framework,21 22 with filters applied to studies written in English, French, Spanish or Italian.

The search strategy was jointly designed with the support of the librarian (FB), whose expertise in systematic reviews helped ensure a robust and well-structured approach. The strategy follows the principles outlined in the Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies (PRESS) framework, promoting transparency and methodological precision. The search strategy prioritises sensitivity, that it was designed and peer-reviewed by a specialist librarian. A provisional search of all databases was carried out on 20 March 2026 and the results are reported in online supplemental material 2; this search will be updated to a final search immediately before the completion of study selection (planned for the first half of 2027), and the final search date will be reported in the review. No restriction was applied to the publication start date (from database inception).

Screening and data collection

The abstracts and full-text articles retrieved through the search strategy will be managed using Covidence (www.covidence.org),23 an online systematic review platform recommended by the Cochrane Collaboration. Duplicate records will be removed. Two review authors (LM and MGN) will independently screen titles and abstracts, excluding studies that do not meet the eligibility criteria. Any disagreements between the reviewers will be resolved through discussion or, if necessary, by consulting a third reviewer (PL). To promote consistency in how eligibility criteria are applied, a preliminary calibration phase will be carried out before initiating the full study selection. During this phase, two reviewers (LM and MGN) will independently evaluate a randomly selected set of 10 studies. Any divergences in judgement will be reviewed collaboratively to fine-tune the screening guidelines. This process will help clarify operational definitions and ensure a unified interpretation of the inclusion parameters across the review team.

Quality assessment

The methodological robustness of the included studies will be evaluated independently by all the reviewers (LM, ET, SM, MGN and PL) using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT) (online supplemental material 3). This tool offers a structured framework for assessing research quality across diverse study types, including qualitative, quantitative and mixed methods designs. Each study will be reviewed against the MMAT’s criteria, which cover aspects such as methodological coherence, data collection procedures, internal validity and applicability of findings.

To ensure consistent interpretation of the appraisal criteria, a preliminary calibration will be conducted on a small sample of studies before the full assessment begins. This step will help harmonise the reviewers’ understanding and refine the evaluation process. In cases of disagreement, a third reviewer (ET or SM) will be consulted to reach consensus.

The results of the quality appraisal will be used to contextualise the findings during synthesis, allowing for a clear distinction between studies of varying methodological strength. A summary table detailing the MMAT scores for each included study will be presented in the final report.

Data extraction and synthesis

Data extraction will be performed independently by two reviewers (MGN and PL) using a structured form designed to capture key information relevant to the application of OR algorithms to ED personnel rostering and shift scheduling. Before full implementation, the form will be tested on a random sample of 10 studies to ensure clarity, completeness and relevance. The predefined extraction categories and data are presented in online supplemental material 4.

The review process will be managed using Covidence. If additional data fields emerge as relevant during screening, they may be incorporated into the extraction framework. In cases where study details are missing or ambiguous, the reviewers will attempt to contact the original authors for clarification. Any discrepancies between reviewers will be resolved through discussion or, if necessary, with input from a third reviewer. The synthesis will integrate both qualitative and quantitative findings to provide a comprehensive overview of how OR algorithms have been applied to ED personnel rostering and shift scheduling. A narrative approach will be employed to identify recurring themes, implementation strategies and reported outcomes. Where sufficient numerical data are available, basic descriptive statistics, such as frequency distributions and percentages, will be used to support the narrative synthesis. If the volume and diversity of included studies allow, potential publication bias will be assessed using funnel plots or appropriate statistical methods.

The extraction process will record both the intended goals and the actual outcomes reported. A mapping framework will be applied to evaluate the degree of alignment, whether full, partial or absent. This will be complemented by thematic coding to identify common benefits, limitations and contextual factors influencing implementation.

In addition, methodological quality will be assessed using a design-appropriate tool (MMAT).24 In addition, the synthesis will be stratified by evidence type, allowing readers to distinguish clearly between well-established findings and exploratory or preliminary evidence. This structure will prevent overinterpretation of early-stage studies while still enabling the review to capture innovation, methodological experimentation and real-world implementation experience that are critical in a rapidly evolving technical field.

The narrative and thematic synthesis will explicitly qualify conclusions according to the strength, consistency and maturity of the underlying evidence. Where findings are derived primarily from single-site case reports or small-scale pilot studies, they will be framed as emerging patterns or promising practices rather than as generalisable causal effects. Conversely, areas supported by multiple independent studies or by more robust empirical designs will be highlighted as stronger evidence clusters. This graduated interpretation ensures that the review produces analytically rigorous and policy-relevant insights without overstating what the current literature can support. Even under conditions of limited or heterogeneous evidence, the review will generate high-value outputs by identifying methodological gaps, under-researched logistical processes and priority areas for future large-scale evaluations and comparative studies.

Patient and public involvement

The authors declare that patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this study. This research is a systematic review protocol based exclusively on previously published studies and does not involve primary data collection or direct interaction with patients or the public. However, the focus of this review, based on improving staff rostering and workforce management in EDs, has the potential to indirectly benefit patients by supporting more efficient resource allocation, reducing waiting times and improving the quality and timeliness of care. The findings of this review will be disseminated through academic publications and conferences and may inform future research and decision-making processes aimed at enhancing patient care in emergency settings.

Ethics and dissemination

This study does not require ethical approval, as it is a systematic literature review based entirely on previously published research. No patients or human participants will be involved. As such, no consent to participate or consent for publication is required.

The findings of this review will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal and presented at national and international conferences. Dissemination efforts will target healthcare professionals, ED managers, hospital administrators and OR researchers interested in improving ED personnel rostering and shift scheduling.

Data sharing does not apply to this article, as no new datasets will be generated or analysed during the study. All extracted data will be derived from publicly available sources and reported transparently in the final publication.

Discussion

Efficient workforce scheduling is a critical challenge in EDs, where unpredictable patient demand, time-sensitive clinical processes and limited human resources place substantial pressure on healthcare systems. In this context, personnel rostering and shift scheduling play a fundamental role in ensuring adequate staffing levels, maintaining quality of care and supporting staff well-being. Poorly designed schedules may contribute to increased waiting times, inefficient resource utilisation, staff fatigue and burnout, ultimately affecting both organisational performance and patient outcomes.

OR methods offer a powerful set of tools to address complex scheduling problems by integrating multiple operational constraints and objectives into formal optimisation models. Over the past decades, a wide range of OR approaches, including mathematical programming, heuristics, metaheuristics and simulation-based optimisation, have been proposed to support workforce scheduling in healthcare settings. However, the literature specifically addressing rostering of ED personnel remains fragmented across multiple disciplines, including healthcare management, industrial engineering, computer science and OR. As a result, the current body of evidence lacks a comprehensive synthesis of the algorithmic approaches used to address this problem.

This systematic review aims to fill this gap by identifying and analysing the optimisation models and algorithms applied to ED personnel scheduling. By examining the modelling approaches, decision variables, constraints, objective functions and performance metrics used in the literature, the review will provide a structured overview of current methodological practices. In addition, the review will identify trends in algorithm development, highlight common modelling assumptions and document the evaluation strategies used to assess scheduling solutions.

The findings of this review may contribute to both research and practice. For researchers, the review will help clarify the methodological landscape of optimisation approaches applied to ED workforce scheduling and identify areas where further methodological development is needed. For healthcare managers and decision-makers, the review may provide insights into the types of optimisation tools that have been proposed to support staffing decisions and the operational outcomes reported in the literature.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the heterogeneity of modelling approaches and evaluation metrics across studies may limit the possibility of quantitative synthesis, and the review will therefore rely primarily on narrative and descriptive analyses. Second, many optimisation studies are based on simulated environments or case studies rather than real-world implementations, which may affect the generalisability of reported results. Finally, the review will include only peer-reviewed publications, which may lead to the exclusion of relevant methodological contributions published in grey literature or technical reports.

Despite these limitations, the review will provide a comprehensive synthesis of OR algorithms used for rostering ED personnel and may serve as a reference for future research on optimisation-based workforce management in emergency care settings.

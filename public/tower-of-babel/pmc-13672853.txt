In vivo and ex vivo impact of THBD and PROCR polymorphisms on the anticoagulant response of the protein C pathway∗†
Authors: Sara Reda, Nadine Schwarz, Sebastian F Eckert, Jens Müller, Johannes Oldenburg, Bernd Pötzsch, Behnaz Pezeshkpoor, Heiko Rühl
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key Points•This study provides direct in vivo evidence that common THBD and PROCR variants modulate the anticoagulant PC pathway.•PROCR 655A>G was associated with a reduced APC response in vivo and in an endothelial cell–based ex vivo model.

Introduction

Venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a multifactorial disease and a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Established hereditary risk factors include deficiencies of antithrombin, protein C (PC), and protein S, as well as the common factor V Leiden (FVL) and prothrombin G20210A mutations, yet together these explain only part of the estimated heritability of VTE.1, 2, 3 In most patients with unprovoked VTE, no cause is identified despite extensive thrombophilia testing, underscoring the need for multifactorial approaches to risk prediction.4 The PC pathway plays a pivotal role in balancing coagulation activation: thrombin bound to thrombomodulin (TM; encoded by THBD) activates PC on the endothelial surface, a process further facilitated by the endothelial PC receptor (EPCR; encoded by PROCR).5, 6, 7 Activated PC (APC) inactivates the activated cofactors Va and VIIIa, thereby inhibiting thrombin generation, while also exerting cytoprotective effects.8 Impaired function of this pathway, whether by genetic or acquired mechanisms, is a well-recognized contributor to thrombotic risk.9

Standardized in vivo activation of coagulation with low-dose recombinant activated FVII (rFVIIa) followed by serial measurement of thrombin and APC has been established as a sensitive approach to characterize the functionality of the PC pathway. Using this model, FVL carriers were shown to have increased APC formation after rFVIIa-induced thrombin generation compared with noncarriers. Among the group of FVL carriers, the APC response was lower in those with a history of VTE and higher in asymptomatic individuals.10,11 Inherited thrombophilia of unknown cause was likewise associated with an impaired APC response in this setting.12 In these studies, in vivo thrombin generation was assessed by prothrombin fragment 1+2 (F1+2) and thrombin-antithrombin complex (TAT), whereas circulating APC was measured directly. More recently, an ex vivo extension using endothelial colony-forming cells (ECFCs) introduced the ratio of APC area under the curve (AUC) to TAT AUC as a quantitative measure of the APC response and demonstrated that endothelial factors critically modulate this response in FVL carriers.13

Genetic variants in both THBD and PROCR genes have been investigated as potential modulators of thrombosis risk, but the evidence remains inconsistent. For THBD, the common coding polymorphism c.1418C>T (Ala455Val, rs1042579) has been associated with increased PC activation in cell-based assays and with reduced VTE risk in 1 case-control study.14 However, larger population-based cohort studies and meta-analyses did not confirm this association.15, 16, 17 For PROCR, a coding variant (Ser219Gly, c.655A>G, rs867186) has been more consistently associated with functional changes. The Gly219 allele is strongly associated with elevated plasma soluble EPCR (sEPCR) concentrations18,19 and has been linked to increased VTE risk.20,21 Another common polymorphism, c.4678C>G (rs9574), marks the H1 haplotype that is associated with reduced EPCR shedding and lower sEPCR levels but has shown no consistent association with VTE risk.22,23

Overall, although both THBD and PROCR polymorphisms have plausible functional roles in modulating the PC pathway, the available evidence has remained inconsistent. This study therefore aimed to determine whether THBD 1418C>T, PROCR 655A>G, and PROCR 4678C>G modulate the functional APC response to in vivo rFVIIa stimulation, thereby providing direct in vivo evidence for their impact on the PC pathway. Given our previous observations that VTE history and FVL status influence APC responses in this model,10,11 and that demographic variables such as age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) can affect hemostasis parameters, these covariates were included to account for potential confounding when estimating genetic effects. The in vivo rFVIIa stimulation model was complemented by a previously established ex vivo model of the PC pathway using ECFCs and autologous plasma to assess thrombin and APC generation.24,25

Methods

This prospective study was conducted between July 2022 and June 2026 at the Institute of Experimental Hematology and Transfusion Medicine, University Hospital Bonn, Germany. The study was approved by the institutional review board and ethics committee of the Medical Faculty of the University of Bonn (reference number 016/16; amended version dated 14 June 2022; reference number 2024-302-BO, dated 6 August 2024). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study participant enrollment and eligibility

Study participants were prospectively recruited from the thrombophilia outpatient clinic and the blood donation service of our institution. Participants with previous VTE had undergone routine thrombophilia workup and were included irrespective of FVL carrier status, asymptomatic FVL carriers had been identified through family-based testing in the outpatient clinic, and asymptomatic FVL-negative participants were recruited among blood donors. FVL was prespecified because previous rFVIIa stimulation studies had identified it as a determinant of the APC response.10, 11, 12 Eligibility and screening procedures, including exclusion of additional thrombophilic risk modifiers, are summarized in Table 1. Of 85 individuals screened, 9 were excluded because of abnormal laboratory results and 3 withdrew consent. The remaining 73 participants completed rFVIIa administration and follow-up blood sampling, and all samples were available for analysis. Blood sampling for ECFC cultivation was performed at separate visits in 68 available participants. Successful ECFC outgrowth permitting ex vivo experiments was achieved in 43 participants.

Molecular genetic analysis

Genomic DNA was isolated from peripheral whole blood using the Blood Core kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). Molecular genetic analyses included the sequencing of all coding regions and intron/exon boundaries of the following genes: PROCR (NM_006404) and THBD (NM_000361), applying a custom-designed targeted Nextera assay (Illumina, Santa Clara, CA). The panel further comprised all curated disease-causing genes associated with bleeding, thrombotic, and platelet disorders, as recommended by the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis Scientific and Standardization Committee.26 The next-generation sequencing analyses were carried out on a Mini-Seq genome sequencer (Illumina, Santa Clara, CA). Data were evaluated using SeqPilot software (JSI medical systems, Ettenheim, Germany). Sequence variants were described following Human Genome Variation Society guidelines. Variant interpretation followed American College of Medical Genetics guidelines.27 Disease causality was compared using the Human Gene Mutation Database28 and the ClinGen database.29 Large deletions and duplications were analyzed based on copy number variation analysis using SeqPilot.

Administration of rFVIIa, in vivo assessment of thrombin and APC formation, and additional laboratory analyses

Participants fasted overnight before administration of 15 μg/kg rFVIIa as a single IV bolus in the morning. Blood samples were collected immediately before rFVIIa administration and at 10 minutes, 30 minutes, and 1, 2, 3, 5, and 8 hours thereafter. Each sample was obtained from a new venipuncture of an antecubital vein using 21-gauge winged infusion sets (Sarstedt, Nümbrecht, Germany). After discarding the first 2 mL, blood was drawn into 3.2% citrate tubes (final concentration, 10.5 mmol/L; Sarstedt). Tubes for thrombin measurements contained argatroban (100 μmol/L), and tubes for APC measurements contained aprotinin and bivalirudin (final concentrations of 10 μmol/L and 250 μg/mL, respectively). Plasma was prepared within 30 minutes by centrifugation (2600g, 10 minutes) and stored at below −70°C until analysis.

Müller et al initially described the oligonucleotide-based enzyme capture assays (OECAs) for thrombin and APC measurement.30,31 Both assays have since been applied in several studies using the same rFVIIa stimulation model as in this study.10, 11, 12, 13,32 In brief, bovine serum albumin–biotin was coated to microtiter modules (10 μg/mL, 100 μL per well) by incubation overnight at 4°C, followed by washing and subsequent addition of 10 μg/mL streptavidin for 1 hour at room temperature. After washing, aptamers HD1-22 (thrombin-OECA) or HS02-52G (APC-OECA) were then immobilized, and plasma samples were added after washing. For the APC-OECA, citrated plasma was recalcified with CaCl2 (7.5 mmol/L final concentration). Detection was performed with fluorogenic substrates (I-1560 for thrombin, Pefafluor PCa for APC), and fluorescence was recorded kinetically in a plate reader. Primed pooled plasma was spiked with defined concentrations of thrombin or APC, and plasma-based calibrators were processed in parallel to samples. All samples were analyzed in triplicate and runs with >10% deviation in controls were repeated.

F1+2 and TAT were measured at the same sampling time points with commercial assays. Additional baseline parameters, including fibrinogen, prothrombin, FV, AT, PC, free protein S, D-dimer, soluble TM (sTM), and sEPCR, were also determined using commercial assays. The materials, reagents, and devices used for sampling and measurements are listed in supplemental Table 1, and the commercial assays applied are detailed in supplemental Table 2.

ECFC isolation, cultivation, and ex vivo assessment of thrombin and APC formation

ECFCs were isolated and cultured according to established protocols,33,34 as described in our previous studies.24,25 In brief, peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from lithium-heparin blood by density-gradient centrifugation, seeded on collagen-coated plates in endothelial growth medium, expanded after development of typical cobblestone colonies, pooled per donor, and cryopreserved. For experiments, frozen ECFCs were thawed, washed, and cultured in endothelial growth medium. All experiments were performed using passage-7 cells. ECFC-dependent thrombin and APC formation were assessed, as described previously.25 Detailed protocols for ECFC isolation, culture, and ex vivo thrombin/APC assessment are provided in the supplemental Methods.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR), and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Normality of distributions was assessed by the Shapiro-Wilk test. The AUC for thrombin, F1+2, TAT, and APC was calculated using the trapezoidal rule with correction for baseline values. Unadjusted group comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Ratios of APC AUC to thrombin-related marker AUCs were calculated to assess APC generation relative to thrombin formation. The APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio was used as the main integrated in vivo measure of the endogenous anticoagulant response of the PC pathway. The APC AUC/thrombin AUC ratio was used as the corresponding ECFC-based ex vivo measure. Multivariable linear regression models were used to assess associations of these ratios, sEPCR, sTM, and other variables with sex, BMI, VTE history, and the presence of FVL, THBD 1418C>T, PROCR 655A>G, and PROCR 4678C>G. Regression models were fitted with natural log–transformed outcome values, and results are reported as standardized regression coefficients (β) with 95% confidence intervals. The goodness of fit was expressed as adjusted R2. A 2-sided P value <.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using XLSTAT version 2021.4.1 (Addinsoft, Boston, MA).

In vivo impact of THBD and PROCR polymorphisms on rFVIIa-induced thrombin and APC formation

Administration of rFVIIa was well tolerated without adverse events. AUCs were calculated to quantify the overall generation of APC and thrombin after rFVIIa administration. Median values were 9.9 pmol × h/L (IQR, 6.6-19.5) for APC AUC, 342 pmol × h/L (IQR, 190-520) for F1+2 AUC, 0.03 pmol × h/L (IQR, 0.0-0.62) for thrombin AUC, and 57.6 pmol × h/L (IQR, 25.5-134.3) for TAT AUC. These outcome parameters were subsequently analyzed using multivariable regression models to identify the most informative end point. In addition, ratios of the APC AUC to the AUCs of the thrombin-related parameters were calculated to reflect the amount of APC generated per amount of thrombin formed and analyzed in the same models. The explanatory power, expressed as adjusted R2, was 0.245 for APC AUC, 0.110 for F1+2 AUC, −0.101 for thrombin AUC, 0.282 for TAT AUC, 0.086 for APC AUC/F1+2 AUC ratio, 0.054 for APC AUC/thrombin AUC ratio, and 0.345 for the APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio. The results of the latter analysis, which provided the best model fit, are shown in Table 3.

Multivariable regression of the APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio identified VTE history, FVL, THBD 1418C>T, and PROCR 655A>G as significant determinants. The ratio was lower in individuals with VTE history (median, 0.13 vs 0.26; P = .009) and in carriers of PROCR 655A>G (0.11 vs 0.26; P = .014), and higher in FVL (0.32 vs 0.14; P = 4.3 × 10−4) and THBD 1418C>T carriers (0.38 vs 0.13; P = .032). To assess whether these effects were driven by APC AUC or TAT AUC, both parameters were analyzed separately (supplemental Table 4). VTE history was significantly associated only with the ratio and not with APC AUC or TAT AUC individually. For FVL, only APC AUC was increased in carriers compared with noncarriers (median, 18.7 vs 7.4 pmol × h/L; P = 3 × 10−6). For THBD 1418C>T, only TAT AUC was reduced in carriers compared with CC homozygotes (median, 30.6 vs 82.7 pmol × h/L; P = .032). For PROCR 655A>G, only APC AUC was slightly reduced in carriers compared with AA homozygotes (median, 8.6 vs 10.7 pmol × h/L; P = .04998). This is further illustrated by the time courses of APC and TAT after rFVIIa injection. In THBD 1418C>T carriers, APC levels tended to be lower than in CC homozygotes (Figure 1A), but the stronger reduction was observed for TAT (Figure 1B), explaining the higher ratio. In PROCR 655A>G carriers, APC levels were reduced compared with AA homozygotes (Figure 1C), whereas TAT levels tended to be higher (Figure 1D), consistent with the lower ratio.

Impact of THBD and PROCR polymorphisms on endothelial cell–dependent thrombin and APC formation

The APC AUC/thrombin AUC ratio was calculated as an ex vivo counterpart of the in vivo APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio and analyzed by multivariable regression using the same covariates (Table 4). The model showed an adjusted R2 of 0.576. VTE history, FVL, and PROCR 655A>G were identified as significant determinants. The ratio was lower in individuals with VTE history (median, 0.02 vs 0.04; P = 5.5 × 10−5) and in PROCR 655A>G carriers (0.02 vs 0.04; P = 5.5 × 10−5), and higher in FVL carriers (0.05 vs 0.02; P = 1.2 × 10−5). There was no statistically significant association of THBD 1418C>T with the ratio in the ECFC-based model (0.03 vs 0.03; P = .361). Time courses of thrombin and APC are shown in Figure 2. Unlike APC and TAT levels in the in vivo model, APC and thrombin concentrations were similar in THBD 1418C>T carriers and CC homozygotes (Figure 2A-B). By contrast, PROCR 655A>G carriers showed lower APC concentrations (Figure 2C) and higher thrombin concentrations than AA homozygotes (Figure 2D), consistent with the in vivo results.

Discussion

This study provides, to our knowledge, the first direct in vivo evidence that common polymorphisms in THBD and PROCR modulate the anticoagulant response of the PC pathway. By applying the rFVIIa stimulation model, we were able to assess the dynamic balance between thrombin and APC generation under standardized conditions. The APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio proved to be the most informative end point, demonstrating that genetic variation in endothelial cofactors translates into measurable functional differences in vivo. The adjusted R2 of 0.345 indicates that the predefined clinical and genetic variables explained a relevant part of the interindividual variability in the APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio. Our data revealed opposing effects of the 2 coding variants under study. Carriers of THBD 1418C>T exhibited a higher APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio, primarily due to lower thrombin generation, whereas carriers of PROCR 655A>G showed a lower ratio, driven by lower APC formation. No association was observed for PROCR 4678C>G. These associations were smaller than those observed for FVL and VTE history and should therefore be interpreted as subtle modulatory effects rather than as major determinants of the APC response. Taken together, these observations extend previous epidemiological findings and provide in vivo support for a mechanistic link between THBD and PROCR genotypes and PC pathway function.

Beyond these polymorphisms, our data confirmed that both FVL and VTE history are determinants of the APC response. FVL was associated with a higher APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio, a finding that at first sight appears counterintuitive but is consistent with previous in vivo studies showing enhanced APC formation after rFVIIa administration in FVL carriers compared with noncarriers.10 Within the group of FVL carriers, a reduced APC response has been associated with a history of VTE,11 in line with the concept that impaired APC formation marks a thrombophilic phenotype.12 The APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio has also been validated in ex vivo endothelial models as an integrated measure of the PC pathway response.13 Mechanistically, the higher ratio in FVL carriers can be explained by the dynamic interplay between thrombin and APC generation. Increased thrombin formation induces APC generation, and because of APC resistance, greater amounts of APC are required to suppress thrombin formation, leading to a shift toward a higher ratio. In contrast, VTE history was consistently associated with a lower ratio in both FVL carriers and noncarriers, indicating an impaired capacity of the PC pathway to counterbalance thrombin generation irrespective of FVL status.11,12 In this study, this association was not observed for APC AUC or TAT AUC considered individually, highlighting that only their balance, as reflected by the ratio, captured the impaired anticoagulant response. Collectively, these findings indicate that FVL status should be viewed as 1 determinant of the APC response within a broader clinical and genetic background, rather than as a sufficient explanation for VTE risk.

For THBD 1418C>T, we observed a higher APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio, which, in contrast to FVL, was driven by a lower TAT AUC. This pattern suggests that carriers of the 1418T allele require less thrombin to generate a given amount of APC. Navarro et al14 reported increased PC activation in cell-based assays and lower plasma concentrations of sTM in carriers of the 1418T allele, which they interpreted as reflecting increased availability of TM at the endothelial surface.14 Our observation of a higher APC AUC/TAT AUC provides in vivo support for this hypothesis. Overall, our data confirm a functional effect of THBD 1418C>T in vivo. However, this effect appears to be too subtle to result in a consistent association with VTE risk in larger epidemiological studies.15, 16, 17

For PROCR 655A>G, we observed a lower APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio, mainly driven by reduced APC AUC. This finding is consistent with previous reports associating the 655G allele with increased plasma levels of sEPCR.18,19 Elevated sEPCR has been interpreted as reflecting enhanced shedding of EPCR from the endothelial surface, resulting in reduced availability of membrane-bound EPCR to support PC activation. Carriers of the 655G allele have also been reported to be at increased risk of VTE.20,21 Overall, our results confirm in vivo that PROCR 655A>G shifts the balance between thrombin and APC generation toward a lower ratio via lower APC formation. In contrast, PROCR 4678C>G had no effect on the APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio, consistent with the lack of reproducible association with VTE risk despite lower sEPCR levels reported for this allele.22,23 Unlike in previous reports,14,18,19,22,23 neither sTM nor sEPCR differed by genotype in our cohort, possibly because of limited power, cohort selection, and the fact that soluble markers reflect shedding and clearance rather than endothelial surface availability during PC activation.

In the endothelial cell–based ex vivo model of the PC pathway, PROCR 655A>G again shifted the balance toward a lower APC response, with reduced APC and increased thrombin formation. This is consistent with the function of EPCR in presenting PC to the thrombin-TM complex7 and with the association of this polymorphism with increased VTE risk.18, 19, 20, 21 By contrast, THBD 1418C>T was not associated with increased APC or reduced thrombin formation. This is notable because Navarro et al reported increased thrombin-mediated PC activation in human umbilical vein endothelial cells carrying the 1418T allele, together with higher cell-associated TM and lower sTM, whereas recombinant Ala455 and Val455 TM showed similar PC-activating ability.14 The absence of a THBD 1418C>T effect in our ECFC-based model therefore argues against a stable gain of cellular TM-dependent PC activation. Rather, it suggests that the in vivo association, which was driven by lower TAT levels, may reflect a context-dependent effect on TM surface availability or release that is not preserved under the specific thrombin generation conditions of the ECFC model. Thus, the ex vivo data further support the interpretation that PROCR 655A>G impairs endothelial PC pathway function, whereas they limit the in vivo THBD 1418C>T finding.

In the subgroup analysis by FVL status, VTE history remained significantly associated with a lower APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio in both FVL carriers and noncarriers in vivo, confirming its independence from genetic background. By contrast, the association of PROCR 655A>G with a lower ratio was confined to the subgroup without FVL, suggesting that the strong effect of FVL may obscure more subtle genetic influences. For THBD 1418C>T, no significant associations were observed, in line with the modest impact of this variant. We have recently shown ex vivo that endothelial factors modulate the APC AUC/TAT AUC ratio in FVL carriers depending on VTE history.25 Our data do not suggest that the common THBD or PROCR variants examined here are responsible for this effect.

Strengths of this study include the standardized in vivo rFVIIa stimulation model with parallel measurement of APC and thrombin-related markers, and the careful characterization of genetic and clinical variables. Limitations are the moderate sample size with reduced power in genotype-specific and subgroup analyses; the use of a single experimental stimulation paradigm that may not fully capture physiological triggers of coagulation; and the possibility of residual confounding by age, sex, and BMI despite multivariable adjustment. Family history of VTE, bleeding history, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug intake, blood cell counts at the time of rFVIIa administration, including platelet counts, and ABO blood group were not systematically recorded, which may have left residual confounding. Restricting inclusion to participants with a healthy BMI followed the conditions of previous rFVIIa studies in healthy volunteers,35 reduced body weight–related variability in rFVIIa pharmacokinetics, and was also chosen for safety reasons. However, it limits generalizability to unselected patients with VTE. Because our cohort was selected for FVL carrier status and previous VTE, reported THBD and PROCR genotype frequencies are descriptive for this study population only and should not be interpreted as prevalence estimates. In addition, successful ECFC outgrowth was available only for a subset of participants, and ECFCs under static assay conditions may not fully reflect native endothelial TM and EPCR function in vivo. Importantly, the study was not designed to assess prospective clinical outcomes, so the functional effects observed here cannot be directly translated into predictions of VTE risk. Future studies should link APC response phenotypes to clinical outcomes and to complementary hemostatic pathways, including platelets, fibrinolysis, and plasma coagulation factors.

In conclusion, this study delivers, to our knowledge, the first direct in vivo evidence that common variants in THBD and PROCR modulate the anticoagulant response of the PC pathway. Using the rFVIIa stimulation model, we captured effects smaller in magnitude than those of FVL and VTE history. The ECFC experiments further supported impaired endothelial PC pathway function in PROCR 655A>G carriers, whereas the THBD 1418C>T association remained limited to the in vivo setting. Our findings establish the APC response as a sensitive end point to assess the in vivo consequences of genetic variation within the PC system beyond FVL and help reconcile functional with epidemiological data on thrombophilia arising from disturbances of this pathway.

Abstract
. The Pólya group Po ⁡ ( K ) \Po(K) of a number field K K is the subgroup of the ideal class group Cl ⁡ ( K ) \Cl(K) of K K generated by the classes of all the products of the prime ideals of K K with the same norm. Motivated by the classical “one class in each genus problem”, we prove general finiteness theorems for the number fields K K with a fixed Pólya index [ Cl ( K ) : Po ( K ) ] \left[\Cl(K):\Po(K)\right] in the families of Galois number fields, solvable CM-fields, and real quadratic fields of extended R-D type. We also give classification results for specific families. Most notably, we classify, unconditionally, all imaginary bi-quadratic and imaginary tri-quadratic fields with the Pólya index one. Furthermore, we classify all real quadratic fields of extended R-D type (with possibly only one more field) with the Pólya index one. Also, under GRH, we give the complete list of 161 imaginary quadratic fields with the Pólya index two. Finally, as a byproduct of our results, we extend, from narrow R-D types to the extended R-D types, Dohmae’s classification of real quadratic fields of narrow R-D type whose narrow genus numbers equal their narrow class numbers.

Let

The genus theory of quadratic forms can be generalized in various ways to the general setting of number fields. A common generalization in the context of class field theory is given by Fröhlich (see
[
Ishida
]
for a detailed treatment.)
For a number field
K
, let
\Cl(K)
(resp.
\Cl^{+}(K)
) be the class group (resp. narrow class group) of
K
, and let
h_{K}
(resp.
h_{K}^{+}
) be the class number (resp. narrow class number) of
K
. Roughly speaking, the genus field (resp. narrow genus field) of
K
is a specific abelian extension of
K
whose Galois group is isomorphic to
a quotient of
\Cl(K)
(resp. a quotient of
\Cl^{+}(K)
). More formally, we have the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

For a number field
K
, the
genus field

\Gamma_{K}
(resp.
narrow genus field

\Gamma_{K}^{+}
) of
K
is the maximal abelian extension of
K
, which is the compositum of an abelian number field
K_{*}
with
K
and is unramified at all places (resp. at all finite places) of
K
. The extension degrees
g_{K}=[\Gamma_{K}:K]
and
g_{K}^{+}=[\Gamma_{K}^{+}:K]
are called the
genus number
and the
narrow genus number
of
K
, respectively.

The analogous problem to the classical one class in each genus for number fields has been studied previously (see
[
CK
]
,
[
Louboutin
]
, and
[
Miyada
]
). The family of imaginary quadratic fields is a special case of abelian CM-fields, and a finiteness result, as Theorem
1.1
, has been proven for such number fields.
Let
K
be an
abelian CM-field
, where by an abelian field, we mean a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
whose Galois group is abelian, and by a CM-field we mean a totally complex quadratic extension
K
of a totally real field
K^{+}
(Indeed,
K^{+}
is the
maximal totally real subfield
of
K
.)
It can be shown that abelian CM-fields
K
are the same as imaginary abelian fields (Let
K
be an
imaginary abelian field
, i.e., an abelian field with no real embedding. Let
\sigma
be the usual complex conjugation. Then
\sigma
is an element of order two in the Galois group
G=\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
. Let
L
be the fixed field of the subgroup
\langle\sigma\rangle
. Then
L=K\cap\mathbb{R}
, which implies that
L
is a totally real Galois field, as
G
is abelian. Since
[K:L]=2
,
K
is an abelian CM-field. The converse is trivial.)
As stated before, an example of an abelian CM-field is an imaginary quadratic field. More generally, an imaginary multi-quadratic field, i.e., a field of the form
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-n_{1}},\sqrt{-n_{2}},\dots,\sqrt{-n_{t}})
, for some distinct positive square-free integers
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{t}
, is an abelian CM-field.

The following is proved in
[
Louboutin
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 1.4

(Louboutin)
.

There are only finitely many abelian CM-fields
K
such that
g_{K}=h_{K}
.

In fact, Loubotin proves a stronger assertion which gives Theorem
1.4
as a consequence of it. We denote by
d_{K}
the absolute value of the discriminant of
K
.
For an abelian CM-field
K
, let
h_{K}^{-}=h_{K}/h_{K^{+}}
, where
K^{+}
is the maximal totally real subfield of
K
. The following generalization of Theorem
1.1
can be extracted from Louboutin’s computations in the proof of Theorem
1.4
in
[
Louboutin
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
K
run through an infinite family
\mathcal{F}
of abelian CM-fields.
Then, for every
\epsilon>0
, we have

(1.1)

\frac{(h_{K}^{-})^{\epsilon}}{\prod_{p\mid d_{K}}e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})}\rightarrow\infty,\quad\text{as}\quad d_{K}\rightarrow\infty

in the family
\mathcal{F}
, where
e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})
denotes the ramification index of a prime
p
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

Note that since
h_{K}^{-}\leq h_{K}
and

g_{K}\mid\prod_{p\mid d_{K}}e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})
(see Theorem
2.1
), then Theorem
1.5
for
\epsilon=1
and family
\mathcal{F}
of abelian CM-fields implies Theorem
1.4
.

In this paper, inspired by
[
Emmelin
]
, we are interested in proving results similar to Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
when
g_{K}
is replaced with the order of a certain subgroup of
\Cl(K)
.
Such a finiteness assertion, similar to Theorem
1.4
,
is given by Emmelin
[
Emmelin
, Corollary 3.15]
for the Pólya groups of abelian CM-fields.
For a number field
K
and a prime power
q
, the ideal

\Pi_{q}(K)=:\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})=q\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{p},

where
\mathfrak{p}
denotes a prime ideal of
K
, is called an

Ostrowski ideal
. If
K
has no ideal with norm
q
, we set
\Pi_{q}(K)=\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the ring of integers of
K
.
The following is defined in
[
Cahen-Chabert'sbook
, Chapter II,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
.

Definition 1.6
.

The
Pólya group

\Po(K)
of a number field
K
is the subgroup of
\Cl(K)
generated by the classes of all the Ostrowski ideals

[\Pi_{q}(K)]
of
K
.

From the definition, it is evident that if
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
is a quadratic field, then
\Po(K)
is a 2-group, hence it is a subgroup of
\Cl(K)[2]
. More precisely, for a quadratic field
K
, we have

(1.2)

g_{K}=\#\Cl(K)[2]=\begin{cases}2^{s_{K}-2},&{{\rm\textit{if}}~D>0,~N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K})=1,~{\rm\textit{and}}~D~{\rm\textit{has~ a~ prime ~divisor}}~p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4),}\\
2^{s_{K}-1},&{{\rm\textit{otherwise}},}\end{cases}

and

(1.3)

\#\Po(K)=\begin{cases}2^{s_{K}-2},&{{\rm\textit{if}}~D>0,~{\rm\textit{and}}~N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K})=1,}\\
2^{s_{K}-1},&{{\rm\textit{otherwise}},}\end{cases}

where
s_{K}
is the number of ramified primes in
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
, and
\epsilon_{K}
is the fundamental unit of
K
when
D>0
(see
[
Lemmermeyer
, Corollary 9.8 and Theorem 9.10]
.) Observe that
\#\Po(K)=g_{K}
for imaginary quadratic fields
K
, and thus
\#\Po(K)
can be considered as an extension of the classical genus number.
Also,
\#\Po(K)\mid g_{K}
for all quadratic extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}
. However, this is not true in general; for example, for the pure cubic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{20})
, we have
\#\Po(K)=h_{K}=3
, while
g_{K}=1
(see
[
Leriche2014
, Example 3.10 ]
.)

By Theorem
2.2
below, we see that for Galois extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}
,

(1.4)

\#\Po(K)\mid{\prod_{p\mid d_{K}}e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})}.

Thus,

[
Emmelin
, Corollary 3.15]
can also be obtained from (
1.1
). In fact, using (

) and (

Pólya indices

\left[\Cl(K):\Po(K)\right]
.

Corollary 1.7
.

For every positive integer
t
, there are only finitely many abelian CM-fields
K
with
\left[\Cl(K):\Po(K)\right]=t
.

Remarks 1.8

(i) When

is Galois, an ideal

2.
Genus fields and Pólya groups

In this section, we give explicit relations between the genus numbers and the orders of the Pólya groups of some Galois number fields.
For genus numbers, we follow the notation and terminology of
[
Ishida
]
.
Recall that
g_{K}^{+}=[\Gamma_{K}^{+}:K]
is the narrow genus number of
K
, where
\Gamma_{K}^{+}
is the narrow genus field of
K
(see Definition
1.3
).
For abelian number fields, Leopoldt
[
Leopoldt
]
gave the following genus number formula.

Theorem 2.1

(Leopoldt)
.

Let
K
be an abelian number field. Then

(2.1)

g_{K}^{+}=\frac{\prod_{p|d_{K}}e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]},

where
e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})
denotes the ramification index of a prime
p
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

Recall that the Pólya group
\Po(K)
of
K
is the subgroup of
\Cl(K)
formed by the classes of all Ostrowski ideals of
K
(see Definition
1.6
.) For Pólya groups we use the notation and terminology of
[
Cahen-Chabert'sbook
, Chapter II,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
. The following fundamental result for Pólya groups of Galois number fields is proved in

[
Zantema
, Section 3]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Zantema)
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension with Galois group
G
. Then the sequence

(2.2)

0\longrightarrow H^{1}(G,U_{K})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{p|d_{K}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow\Po(K)\longrightarrow 0

is exact, where
H^{1}(G,U_{K})
is the first cohomology group of
G
with coefficients in the unit group
U_{K}
of K, and
e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})
denotes the ramification index of a prime
p
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

As a direct corollary of (
2.2
), we have

(2.3)

\#\Po(K)=\frac{\prod_{p|d_{K}}e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})}{\#H^{1}(G,U_{K})}.

Combining (
2.1
) with (
2.3
), we can explicitly relate the order of the Pólya groups of abelian number fields to their narrow genus numbers.

Proposition 2.3
.

For an abelian number field
K
, we have

(2.4)

\#\Po(K)=\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}{\#H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K})}\cdot g_{K}^{+}.

Proposition
2.3
gives us a key tool to investigate abelian number fields whose Pólya groups are equal to their class groups. In particular, as applications of Formula (
2.4
), we find that the order of Po(K) divides the narrow genus number of K in some specific families of abelian number fields
K
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
K
be a cyclic number field. Then

(2.5)

\#\Po(K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g_{K}^{+}/2,&\,\text{if $K$ is real and $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(U_{K})=\{+1\}$},\\
&\\
g_{K}^{+},&\,\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

In addition, in the case that
K
is a real cyclic field, we have

(2.6)

\frac{\#\Po(K)}{h_{K}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{[U_{K}^{+}:U_{K}^{2}]}{2}\cdot\frac{g_{K}^{+}}{h_{K}^{+}},&\,\text{if}\,N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(U_{K})=\{+1\},\\
&\\
{[U_{K}^{+}:U_{K}^{2}]}\cdot\frac{g_{K}^{+}}{h_{K}^{+}},&\,\text{otherwise,}\\
\end{array}\right.

where
h_{K}^{+}
denotes the narrow class number of
K
,
U_{K}^{+}
denotes the totally positive units of
K
, and

U_{K}^{2}=\{u^{2}\,:\,u\in U_{K}\}.

Proof.

For a cyclic number field
K
, it is known that

\#H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K})=2[K:\mathbb{Q}]
if
K
is real and
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(U_{K})=\{+1\}
, and is
[K:\mathbb{Q}]
otherwise (see
[
Zantema
, Section 3]
.) Thus, (
2.5
) follows from (
2.4
). To obtain (
2.6
), we use (
2.5
) and

h_{K}^{+}=h_{K}.\left[U_{K}^{+}:U_{K}^{2}\right],

see
[
milneCFT
, Theorem 1.7]
.
∎

As a consequence of the above proposition, we obtain the following result for real quadratic fields.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
is a real quadratic field, for a square-free integer
D>0
, then

(2.7)

\#\Po(K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g_{K}/2,&\text{if $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K})=+1$ and $D$ has no prime divisor $p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)$},\\
g_{K},&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\epsilon_{K}
denotes the fundamental unit of
K
. Moreover,

(2.8)

\frac{\#\Po(K)}{h_{K}}=\frac{g_{K}^{+}}{h_{K}^{+}}.

Proof.

We have

(2.9)

g_{K}^{+}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2g_{K},&\textit{if $D$ has a prime divisor $p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)$},\\
g_{K},&\textit{otherwise},\\
\end{array}\right.

see
[
Leriche2014
, Proposition 3.11]
.
If
D
has a prime divisor
p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, then
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K})=+1
. Now by employing (
2.9
) and
Proposition
2.4
we get the relation (
2.7
) (Note that (
2.7
) can be also obtained from (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).) In addition,
using Dirichlet’s Unit Theorem, we have

(2.10)

4=\left[U_{K}:U_{K}^{2}\right]=\left[U_{K}:U_{K}^{+}\right]\cdot\left[U_{K}^{+}:U_{K}^{2}\right].

Now if
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K})=-1
, then
\left[U_{K}:U_{K}^{+}\right]=4
, since
-1,\epsilon_{K}\not\in U_{K}\backslash U_{K}^{+}
. But, if
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K})=+1
, then
\epsilon_{K}\in U_{K}^{+}
which implies that
\left[U_{K}:U_{K}^{+}\right]=2
. Consequently, by (
2.10
) we obtain

(2.11)

\left[U_{K}^{+}:U_{K}^{2}\right]=\begin{cases}2,\,&\textit{if}\,\,N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K})=+1,\\
1,\,&\textit{if}\,\,N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K})=-1.\end{cases}

From (
2.6
) and (
2.11
) we get (
2.8
).
∎

We show that the divisibility of
g_{K}
by
\#\Po(K)
also holds for imaginary bi-quadratic and tri-quadratic fields
K
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
K
be an imaginary bi-quadratic field. Then

\#\Po(K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g_{K}/2,&\,\text{if}\,\,W_{K}=N_{K^{+}/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K^{+}})=1,\\
&\\
g_{K},&\,\text{otherwise,}\\
\end{array}\right.

where
K^{+}
denotes the real quadratic subfield of
K
, with the fundamental unit
\epsilon_{K^{+}}
, and
W_{K}=\left[U_{K}:\mu_{K}\cdot U_{K^{+}}\right]

denotes the Hasse unit index of
K
in which
\mu_{K}
is the group of roots of unity in
K
.

Proof.

First, observe that if
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt{-2})
, then
\#\Po(K)=g_{K}=h_{K}=1
(Note that in this case,
K^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
has units of negative norm.) So, let
K\neq\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt{-2})
. Then,
from
[
Zantema
, Lemma 4.3]
, we have

H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K})\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},&\textit{if}\,\,W_{K}=N_{K^{+}/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{K^{+}})=1,\\
&\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},&\textit{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Now the assertion follows from the above isomorphism and Proposition
2.3
.
∎

We next consider the imaginary tri-quadratic fields.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
K
is an imaginary tri-quadratic field, then
\#\Po(K)\mid g_{K}
.

Proof.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-m_{1}},\sqrt{-m_{2}},\sqrt{-m_{3}})
, where
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
are three distinct square-free positive integers. We show that

8\mid\#H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K}).

Then the assertion follows from Proposition
2.3
.

Let
K^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}},\sqrt{m_{1}m_{3}})
. Following Setzer’s terminology in
[
Bennett
]
, for
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, we say
K^{+}
is of the type
M_{i}
if
i
of the quadratic subfields of
K^{+}
have units of negative norm. For a quadratic subfield
k_{j}
of
K^{+}
, denote by
\epsilon_{j}
the fundamental unit of
k_{j}
.
We have two cases:

(i)

At most one of the subfields
k_{j}
’s has units with negative norm. Hence,
K^{+}
is of the type
M_{0}
or
M_{1}
. In this case, by
[
Bennett
, Table I]
, we find that

(2.12)

8\mid\#H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K^{+}}).

Since the cohomology group
H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K^{+}})
is embedded in
H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K})
via the inflation map, then (
2.12
) yields the claim.

(ii)

At least two of the subfields
k_{j}
’s, say
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, have units with negative norm, i.e.,
N_{k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{1})=N_{k_{2}/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{2})=-1
. Hence,
K^{+}
is of the type
M_{2}
or
M_{3}
. From
[
Bennett
, Table I]
, we observe that either

(2.13)

\#H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K^{+}})=8\quad\text{and}\quad\left[U_{K^{+}}:U_{k_{1}}U_{k_{2}}U_{k_{3}}\right]=1,

or

(2.14)

\#H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K^{+}})=4\quad\text{and}\quad\left[U_{K^{+}}:U_{k_{1}}U_{k_{2}}U_{k_{3}}\right]=2.

If (
2.13
) holds, then similar to the case
(i)
, the inflation map implies

8\mid\#H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K}).

Hence, we assume that (
2.14
) holds. Since
N_{k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{1})=-1
, the norm map

N_{k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}:U_{k_{1}}\rightarrow U_{\mathbb{Q}}=\{\pm 1\}

is surjective. Consequently, as
k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}
is cyclic, by
[
Neukirch
, Proposition 1.6.12]
, we have

(2.15)

H^{2}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})\simeq\yhwidehat{H}^{0}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})=\frac{U_{\mathbb{Q}}}{N_{k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(U_{k_{1}})}=\{0\},

where

\yhwidehat{H}^{0}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})
denotes the
0^{\text{th}}
Tate cohomology group of
\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q})
with coefficient in
U_{k_{1}}
. In this case,
for the subgroup
\Gal(K/k_{1})
of
\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
, we have the following inflation-restriction exact sequence

(2.16)

0\rightarrow H^{1}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})\xrightarrow{\text{inf}}H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K})\xrightarrow{\text{res}}H^{1}(\Gal(K/k_{1}),U_{K})\rightarrow\underbrace{H^{2}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})}_{0}.

We first compute the order of
H^{1}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})
. By (
2.15
), for the cyclic extension
k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}
,
the Herbrand quotient
Q(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})
is given by

(2.17)

Q(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})=\frac{\#\yhwidehat{H}^{0}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})}{\#H^{1}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})}=\frac{1}{\#H^{1}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})}.

On the other hand, by
[
NChildress
, Proposition 5.10]
,

(2.18)

Q(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})=\frac{2^{s_{k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}}}{[k_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]},

where
s_{k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the number of infinite places of
\mathbb{Q}
ramified in
k_{1}
.
Since
k_{1}
is real,
from (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), we get

(2.19)

\#H^{1}(\Gal(k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}),U_{k_{1}})=2.

Next, we show that

(2.20)

4\mid\#H^{1}(\Gal(K/k_{1}),U_{K}),

which together with (
2.16
) and (
2.19
) imply

8\mid\#H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}),U_{K}),

as desired.

We consider two subcases:

(ii-a).

Let
K^{+}
be of the type
M_{2}
, i.e.,
N_{k_{3}/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{3})=+1
. In this case, to prove (
2.20
),
we show that, in fact,

4\mid\#H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})

(Note that
H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})
is embedded in
H^{1}(\Gal(K/k_{1}),U_{K})
via the inflation map.) For the cyclic extension
K^{+}/k_{1}
, the Herbrand quotient
Q(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})
is given by

Q(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})=\frac{2^{s_{K^{+}/k_{1}}}}{[K^{+}:k_{1}]},

where
s_{K^{+}/k_{1}}
denotes the number of infinite places of
k_{1}
ramified in
K^{+}
. Hence

(2.21)

Q(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})=\frac{1}{2}=\frac{\#\yhwidehat{H}^{0}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})}{\#H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})}.

To deduce that the order of
H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})
is divisible by
4
, we show that

\#\yhwidehat{H}^{0}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})>1.

Since

\#\yhwidehat{H}^{0}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})=\left[U_{k_{1}}:N_{K^{+}/k_{1}}(U_{K^{+}})\right],

it is enough to show that the norm map

N_{K^{+}/k_{1}}:U_{K^{+}}\rightarrow U_{k_{1}}

is not surjective. Following
[
Bennett
, p. 161]
, define

(2.22)

\mathcal{C}=\{(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in U_{k_{1}}\times U_{k_{2}}\times U_{k_{3}}:N_{k_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(u_{1})=N_{k_{2}/\mathbb{Q}}(u_{2})=N_{k_{3}/\mathbb{Q}}(u_{3})=\text{sign}(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3})\}.

Then, for the function

\displaystyle\varphi:U_{K^{+}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{k_{1}}\times U_{k_{2}}\times U_{k_{3}}

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(N_{K^{+}/k_{1}}(u),N_{K^{+}/k_{2}}(u),N_{K^{+}/k_{3}}(u)\right),

we have

(2.23)

\Image(\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal{C},

see
[
Bennett
, p. 161]
.
Now if the norm map

is surjective, then for the element
u_{0}\in U_{K^{+}}
with

we have

\varphi(u_{0})=\left(\epsilon_{1},N_{K^{+}/k_{2}}(u_{0}),N_{K^{+}/k_{3}}(u_{0})\right)\in\mathcal{C}.

Thus, by (
2.22
), we get a unit of
k_{3}
, say
N_{K^{+}/k_{3}}(u_{0})
, with negative norm which contradicts the assumption
N_{k_{3}/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{3})=+1
. Hence, if
K^{+}
is of type

, then the norm map

N_{K^{+}/k_{1}}:U_{K^{+}}\rightarrow U_{k_{1}}

can not be surjective, as claimed. Therefore, by the above argument, (
2.20
) holds.

(ii-b).

Let
K^{+}
be of the type
M_{3}
, i.e.,
N_{k_{3}/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{3})=-1
. If the norm map

N_{K^{+}/k_{1}}:U_{K^{+}}\rightarrow U_{k_{1}}

is not surjective, similar to the case

4\mid\#H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})
which yields (

).
So, assume that the norm map
N_{K^{+}/k_{1}}
is surjective. Then,

H^{2}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})\simeq\yhwidehat{H}^{0}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})=\{0\}.

In this case, from (

), we get

(2.24)

\#H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})=2,

and for the field extensions
k_{1}\subseteq K^{+}\subseteq K
, we get the following inflation-restriction exact sequence

(2.25)

0\rightarrow H^{1}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})\xrightarrow{\text{inf}}H^{1}(\Gal(K/k_{1}),U_{K})\xrightarrow{\text{res}}H^{1}(\Gal(K/K^{+}),U_{K})\rightarrow\underbrace{H^{2}(\Gal(K^{+}/k_{1}),U_{K^{+}})}_{0}.

Now we show that

Then from (

) and (

), we obtain

).

Since

is cyclic, the Herbrand Quotient

to obtain (

) from (

). Observe that if

If

We start by expressing the order of the Pólya group of a quadratic field in terms of the divisor function.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
be a quadratic field, where

D
is a square-free integer. Let

c_{K}=\begin{cases}1/2,&\textit{if}~~D\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4),\\

\end{cases}

and denote by

the number of divisors function. Then,

(5.1)

\#\Po(K)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}c_{K}\tau(d_{K}),&\textit{if $D>0$ and $K$ has no units of negative norm},\\
c_{K}\tau(d_{K}),&\textit{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

For
n=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
, we have

\tau\left(n\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+1).

Thus,

\tau(d_{K})=\begin{cases}2^{s_{K}},&\textit{if}~~D\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4),\\
3\cdot 2^{s_{K}-1},&\textit{if}~~D\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4),\\
2^{s_{K}+1},&\,\textit{if}~~D\equiv 2\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4),\\
\end{cases}

where

denotes the number of ramified primes in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Now the assertion follows from (
1.3
).
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.19

We need the following three lemmas.

Lemma 5.4
.

If
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r})
is a real quadratic field of R-D type, then

(5.7)

u_{K}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r},&\textit{if}~~|r|=1,\\
&\\
\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{2},&\textit{if}~~|r|=4,\\
&\\
\frac{2\ell^{2}+r+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{|r|},&\textit{if}~~|r|\neq 1,4,\\
\end{array}\right.

is the fundamental unit of
K
. More generally,
u_{K}
is a unit if
K
is of extended R-D type.

Proof.

If
K
is of R-D type, by
[
Degert
, Theorem 1]
,
u_{K}
is the fundamental unit of
K
.
Since
u_{K}
has norm
\pm 1
, we only need to show that
u_{K}
is an algebraic integer when
K
is of extended R-D type. Let

D
be an extended R-D type integer which is not of R-D type. Then
D=\ell^{2}+r
, where
r\mid 4\ell
and either
r>\ell
or
r\leq-\ell
. If
|r|=4
, then for
r=4
(resp.
r=-4
),
\ell\in\{1,2,3\}
(resp.
\ell\in\{3,4\}
.) It is straightforward to check that
u_{K}
is an algebraic integer in these cases.
If
|r|\neq 4
, then either
(\ell,r)=(1,2)
or
\ell>1
and
r\in\{-\ell,\pm 2\ell_{1},\pm 4\ell_{1}\}
for some divisor
\ell_{1}
of
\ell
. For
(\ell,r)=(1,2)
, we have
D=3
. In this case,
u_{K}=2+\sqrt{3}
is an algebraic integer. Now let
\ell>1
.
If
r=\pm 4\ell_{1}
, then
D=\ell^{2}\pm 4\ell_{1}\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
(Note that in this case
\ell
cannot be even, as
D
is square-free.) Thus,

\frac{2\ell^{2}+r+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{|r|}=\frac{(\ell^{2}/\ell_{1}\pm 2)+(\ell^{2}/\ell_{1})\sqrt{D}}{2}

is an algebraic integer in
K
.
Finally, if
r=-\ell,\pm 2\ell_{1}
, then
r
divides both
2\ell^{2}+r
and
2\ell
which implies that

\frac{2\ell^{2}+r+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{|r|}\in\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{D}].

∎

The following assertion is stated in
[
MW
, p. 423]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
be a real quadratic field of extended R-D type. Then
R_{K}<\log(3D)
, where
R_{K}
denotes the regulator of
K
.

Proof.

As usual, denote by
\epsilon_{K}
the fundamental unit of
K
. First observe that for
D=5
, we have
\epsilon_{K}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
. So,
R_{K}=\log\left(\epsilon_{K}\right)\approx 0.482<\log(3D)\approx 2.7
.

Next, let
D=\ell^{2}+r\neq 5
with
r\mid 4\ell
.
We have three cases:

Case 1.
If
|r|=1
, then by Lemma
5.4
,

\epsilon_{K}=\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}\pm 1}<\left(2\ell^{2}\pm 2\right)+\left(\ell^{2}\pm 1\right)=3D.

(Note that
\ell\geq 2
for
r=-1
.)

Case 2.
If
|r|=4
, then, by Lemma
5.4
,

u_{K}=\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{2}

is a unit of
K
.
Hence,

\epsilon_{K}\leq\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}\pm 4}}{2}<\left(2\ell^{2}\pm 8\right)+\left(\ell^{2}\pm 4\right)=3D.

(Note that
\ell\geq 3
for
r=-4
.)

Case 3.
if
|r|\neq 1,4
, then by Lemma
5.4
,

u_{K}=\frac{2\ell^{2}+r+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{|r|}

is a unit of
K
. If
\ell=1
, the only possibility is to have
r=2
, i.e.,
D=3
. In this case,
\epsilon_{K}=2+\sqrt{3}
and
R_{K}=\log{\epsilon_{K}}\approx 1.31<\log(3D)\approx 2.2
.

Now assume that
\ell>1
. Since
r\leq 4\ell\leq 2\ell^{2}
, then
\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}<2\ell^{2}
.

For
r>0
,

\epsilon_{K}\leq\frac{2\ell^{2}+r+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{|r|}\leq\frac{2\ell^{2}+r+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{2}<\left(\ell^{2}+\frac{r}{2}\right)+2\ell^{2}<3\ell^{2}+3r=3D.

For
r<0
, we have

(5.8)

\epsilon_{K}\leq\frac{2\ell^{2}+r+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{|r|}<\frac{2\ell^{2}+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}}}{2}=2\ell^{2}.

Since
r\mid 4\ell
, for
\ell\geq 12
, we get

-3r\leq 12\ell\leq\ell^{2}.

Consequently, from (
5.8
) we find that for
\ell\geq 12
, the inequality
\epsilon_{K}<2\ell^{2}\leq 3\ell^{2}+3r=3D
holds.
We numerically check that the relation

\frac{2\ell^{2}+r+2\ell\sqrt{\ell^{2}+r}}{|r|}<3\ell^{2}+3r=3D

also holds for
1\leq\ell\leq 11
and
r\mid 4\ell
with
r\neq\pm 1,\pm 4
.
∎

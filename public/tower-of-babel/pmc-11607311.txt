Lightweight design of excavator working device based on automatically generated surrogate model
Authors: He Zhang, Xiao-bo Ge, Yong Li, Xiao-dong Shao, Min-min Jia
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To address the conservative excavator working device configuration obtained by traditional design methods, a lightweight structure design method was proposed in this study based on automatically generated surrogate model. A finite element model of the working device was first constructed, and its accuracy was verified against experimental results. By incorporating the Gini coefficient to compute the information entropy pertaining to the relationship between variables and responses, the entropy-weighted TOPSIS method has been significantly enhanced. Subsequently, the comprehensive contributions of the boom design parameters to the mass, deformation, and stress responses were calculated, and the parameters exerting significant effects were selected as the design variables through the application of the improved TOPSIS. Next, the working device optimisation model was subsequently established using the minimum mass as the target and the stress and deformation as constraints. And then, a surrogate model of the working device was constructed using 500 sample points. Finally, a genetic algorithm was employed to derive a lightweight design of the excavator boom based on the surrogate model. The results indicate that the proposed method reduced the weight of the considered hydraulic excavator boom by 9.30%; further, a finite element simulation verified that the stress and deformation met the design requirements of the boom.

Introduction

With the deployment and advancement of China’s “Double Carbon” and “Made in China 2025” strategies, green design has become a critical aspect of the sustainable manufacturing of complex equipment, such as construction machinery1. At each stage of a product life cycle, green design can reduce environmental impact by 70–80%, representing a vital means of implementing the green and low-carbon transformation of the manufacturing industry2. Structural lightweight design represents a productive approach for product green design that can reduce part weight, resource consumption, and carbon dioxide emissions during the production process without compromising mechanical performance3. Hydraulic excavators are the most widely used construction machinery products and are frequently employed in infrastructure construction, mining, and other fields. The working device comprises the core component of a hydraulic excavator, accounting for 20% of the total weight, and its manufacturing process has a significant impact on metal consumption and the environment. Therefore, the study of lightweight design methods for excavator working devices can provide theoretical support and engineering application value contributing to the green and low-carbon development of complex equipment.

This study therefore investigated the lightweight design of an excavator working device based on an automatically generated surrogate model, using the boom component as an example. First, to reduce the influence of boundary conditions on the boom stiffness and strength simulation, the stresses in the working device under the maximum digging force attitude were analysed and the maximum stress and deformation of the boom were obtained using a finite element model (FEM) simulation. Second, the primary effects of the design variables were analysed using an orthogonal test, and the comprehensive contributions of design variables were calculated and ranked using the improved TOPSIS method to identify the primary design variables. Subsequently, the surrogate model resource pool was assembled and an improved method of constructing the surrogate model was proposed; the surrogate model was automatically selected according to the maximum decision coefficient \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}^{2}.$$\end{document} Next, the working device optimisation model was constructed using the minimum mass as the optimisation objective and the maximum stress and maximum deformation as the design constraints. Finally, the optimal solution was obtained using a genetic algorithm, and the rationality of the proposed method was verified against the results of the FEM simulation.

Literature view

Structural lightweight design primarily encompasses topology optimisation and size optimisation. Topology optimisation aims to achieve precise load paths and optimal material distribution while satisfying specified design constraints, primarily used during the conceptual design phase to derive innovative configurations. Topology optimisation mainly includes methods such as the Solid Isotropic Material with Penalization (SIMP)4–6, Evolutionary Structural Optimisation (ESO)7–9, Level Set Method (LSM)10–12, Moving Morphable Components (MMC)13,14, Multi-Material Topology Optimisation (MMTO)15 and topology optimisation methods integrating machine learning16–18. Size optimisation, on the other hand, is an engineering structural optimisation method that optimises geometric parameters such as the cross-sectional dimensions or thicknesses of structural components without altering the shape and topological configuration of the structure. It is primarily applied during the detailed design phase and product improvement stages, optimising structural performance by adjusting size parameters based on existing structural shapes and topological configurations. To improve the structure of existing excavators, this paper focuses on the lightweight design of their working devices.

Currently, structural size optimization mainly relies on two methods: one is based on high-precision simulation models. For instance, Zou et al.19 proposed a parametric hydraulic excavator working device optimisation design method and established an optimisation framework based on a simulation and genetic algorithm. Hu et al.20 considered the minimum volume of a boom as the optimisation target and the maximum stress and displacement as the constraints to obtain suitable results using the optimisation module included in the ANSYS software. Kim et al.21 studied the optimisation design method for the front linkage of excavator, and optimised the front linkage based on a hybrid Taguchi-random coordinate search algorithm, resulting in an overall performance improvement of 3.43%. Li et al.22 established the mathematical model of the excavator’s working device based on the Denavit-Hartenberg convention and virtual working principle, and applied the parallel particle swarm optimisation to optimise the position of the hinge point and the length of the connecting rod. The overall performance was improved by 5.57%. Solazzi et al.23 conducted numerical research on an excavator under specific load conditions and conducted a series of experimental tests. Based on composite materials, a boom with an elliptical interface was designed, resulting in a total weight reduction of 64.1%. Yu et al.24 studied a lightweight and high-strength design method for excavator buckets under uncertain loads, constructed a parameterized model for the bucket, and optimised the bucket structure based on genetic algorithm. After optimisation, the weight and stress of the bucket were reduced significantly. Xu et al.25 established a multi-objective optimisation model of Excavator Shovel Attachment, which used three kinematic indicators and two mechanical indicators as the objective functions, and optimised the solution based on the decomposed multi-objective evolutionary algorithm, obtaining an excellent design scheme of front shovel excavator. Sun et al.26 used the inner surface line projected on the symmetrical plane of the bucket as the bucket shape line. Based on genetic algorithm, the optimal shape line of the bucket was obtained and the weight of the bucket was reduced. Yang et al.27 constructed an optimisation design coding system based on knowledge guidance, which realized automatic optimisation design of boom structure and improved the optimisation quality and calculation speed of the model. Sun et al.28 analyzed the uncertainty of excavation resistance during the actual excavation process of excavators and proposed a bucket optimisation method based on composite excavation trajectory and ultimate excavation force. After optimisation, the bucket stress and mass were reduced by 4.59% and 5.84%, respectively. Singh et al.29 combined a finite element simulation and genetic algorithm to optimise the braided beam structure in a rail vehicle chassis frame. The advantage of this method is the high precision of the simulation model, which better guarantees the accuracy of the optimization results. However, its disadvantage lies in the long computational time required by the simulation model. Since each optimization iteration necessitates calculations through the simulation model, the optimization process can be time-consuming. The second method involves constructing surrogate models based on simulation models and utilizing them for size optimization. Surrogate models can be further classified into single surrogate models, combined surrogate models, and adaptive surrogate models. Research based on single surrogate models includes: Qiu et al.30 evaluated the impact of different design variables on the accuracy of the surrogate model, and applied bionic intelligent optimisation algorithm to solve the optimisation problem of the working device based on maximum stress. Xu et al.31 studied the connection between the optimisation process and establishment of a surrogate model, and proposed an adaptive sampling method based on the complex method. Ling et al.32 proposed an optimisation methodology and process of latch life based on the surrogate model. Yang et al.33 detailed a shape optimisation method of Petrel-L based on the surrogate model technology. Yang et al.34 established a surrogate model to achieve an optimised design of the dynamic by considering the parameters of local cross-sections and global configurations simultaneously. Wang et al.35 established a surrogate model of mass and other output responses of hull based on a parametric model by response surface method, which is used to optimise design variables and obtain an optimal cylindrical hull. Zhang et al.36 proposed dynamically updating the Kriging model globally and locally through the determination coefficient test and verified the correctness of the proposed method using an injection moulding machine template as the optimisation target. Research based on combined surrogate models includes: Tong et al.37 studied a lightweight design method for a forklift mast based on a combined surrogate model using a sequential quadratic programming algorithm. Ma et al.38 studied a lightweight design method for a shield cutter head based on an ensemble surrogate model and employed a sequential quadratic programming algorithm to achieve a lightweight design accordingly. Research on adaptive surrogate models includes: Zhang et al.39 proposed a new approach based on the adaptive sampling surrogate model to improve the efficiency of random vibration analysis of the train-bridge system, which can significantly improve the prediction accuracy of the surrogate model without increasing the number of training samples. Sun et al.40 studied a constrained hybrid problem optimization algorithm based on a multi-objective adaptive surrogate model, which can obtain non-dominated solutions of similar quality to NSGA-II with fewer model evaluations. Xu et al.41 developed an adaptive surrogate model to determine the optimal auxiliary structure by searching for the best hydrodynamic indexes. Bai et al.42 introduced a finite element (FE) model updating method that combines the trust region (TR) approach with an adaptive surrogate model. This method not only acquires updated parameters that carry physical significance but also ensures high levels of accuracy and efficiency in the updating process. Lv et al.43 proposed an adaptive hybrid surrogate model based on a self-attention neural network framework, which automatically determines the weights of sub-models according to different working conditions. This approach achieves high-fidelity simulation of finite element analysis systems and is applicable to various engineering problems. Based on the literature reviewed, the advantage of using surrogate models for size optimization is that it can significantly reduce optimization time while ensuring the accuracy of the optimization results. However, the disadvantages are also apparent. Since there are many methods for constructing single surrogate models, designers often have difficulty in quickly and accurately selecting methods with high accuracy, which increases the difficulty of size optimization work. Constructing combined surrogate models involves weighted optimization of single surrogate models, which can reduce the difficulty of selecting surrogate model construction methods, but the accuracy of the resulting combined surrogate model is lower than that of a single surrogate model. Meanwhile, the theory of adaptive surrogate models described in the literature is relatively complex and has not been widely applied in engineering practice.

Methodology

A surrogate model employs finite sample point data to fit the relationship between the design variables and the responses, in contrast to an exact model, which calculates the responses of the unknown variables in the design space44. The use of a surrogate model instead of a high-precision simulation model during the lightweight design process can significantly reduce the simulation time and design cost. Commonly used surrogate models include the moving least squares regression (MLSR), polynomial response surface, Kriging model, radial basis function (RBF), and support vector machine (SVM).

As shown in Fig. 1, a lightweight product design method based on a surrogate model comprises the construction of a high-precision simulation model, experimental verification, design of the experiment and collection of samples, construction of a surrogate model and precision calculation, construction of an optimisation model, and results optimisation, followed by simulation verification. The research approach of this manuscript is as follows: First, a finite element model of the working device was constructed, and its accuracy was verified against experimental results. Then, the comprehensive contributions of boom design parameters to mass, deformation, and stress responses were calculated, and parameters with significant effects were selected as design variables for DOE design. Next, the surrogate model was automatically generated according to the maximum decision coefficient, and the working device optimisation model was subsequently established using the minimum mass as the target and stress and deformation as constraints. Finally, considering the actual size of the sheet metal, the optimised variables were rounded and subjected to simulation verification.

FEM of working device

1. Force analysis of working device.

The digging force is a critical hydraulic excavator performance index and is the primary input parameter for the structural design and strength calculation of the working device (boom). According to the working conditions, the digging force can be divided into the digging force on the arm cylinder and that on the bucket cylinder, which represent the force generated when the arm cylinder and bucket cylinder, respectively, operate alone45. The theoretical digging force on each hydraulic cylinder is generated along the tangential direction of the top of the bucket tooth when the cylinder rod is pushed out, ignoring factors such as the mass of parts and materials, return oil pressure in the cylinder, as well as the transmission efficiency of the connecting rod and hydraulic system45. The main parameters of the excavator studied in this article are shown in Fig. 2. According to the structural design requirements of hydraulic excavators and relevant documents46, the theoretical digging force generated by the bucket cylinder is greater than that generated by the arm cylinder. Therefore, this study only calculated the theoretical maximum digging force of the bucket cylinder to serve as the digging resistance imposed in the FEM simulation of the working device. The mass of the working device is important in calculation of the theoretical maximum digging force. However, during bucket excavation, the boom and arm are fixed, equivalent to a frame, and the components involved in the calculation include the bucket and link mass. Based on previous design theories and engineering practice experience, the torques generated by the bucket and connecting rod mass are much smaller than the torque generated by the bucket cylinder, so their impact on calculating the theoretical maximum digging force is often ignored45.

2. FEM modelling process.

Hydraulic excavators have many different working postures and their working conditions are relatively complex. When directly simulating the boom, it is impossible to accurately apply the load and constraint conditions. However, with the working device as the research object, the actual working scene of excavators can be more accurately simulated. To calculate the static strength and stiffness of the working device in this study, a FEM simulation of an excavator working device at the maximum digging force posture was performed as shown in Fig. 5. In this posture, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\angle JLC$$\end{document}and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\angle NMD$$\end{document}are approximately 90 degrees, the forces generated by the boom hydraulic cylinder and arm hydraulic cylinder have the largest moment arm. When adjusting the extension of the bucket cylinder to 1/3 of the stroke, the maximum transmission ratio attitude of the linkage mechanism can be obtained.

The FEM of the working device was created using HyperMesh and the OptiStruct solver. Relying on the hydraulic excavator design and simulation experience of the author’s employer company, the FEM of the working device was simplified by removing parts that would have no significant effect on the simulation results, removing features such as chamfers and small holes, using the rigid element (rbe2 in OptiStruct), the beam element (bar2 in OptiStruct) and releasing axial degrees of freedom of the beam element to simulate the connection between pins and holes, and using the beam element (bar2 in OptiStruct) to simulate cylinders, replacing welding by defining contact, etc. A 14 mm hexahedral mesh was used to model the plate parts in the working device, resulting in a total of 295,289 elements. The turntable and the pin shaft mounting seat were modelled using a 10 mm tetrahedral mesh, resulting in a total of 1,231,104 elements. In total, 756,811 nodes were present in the model. Owing to the complex working conditions of an excavator, the bucket is often affected by an eccentric load during operation. Therefore, the theoretical maximum digging force of the bucket cylinder, F = 145 kN, was applied along the tangential direction to the top of the two leftmost bucket teeth. As the boom base and cylinder were connected to the turntable through the pin shaft and the turntable, slewing mechanism, and chassis assembly were connected by pre-tightened bolts, full constraints were imposed on the connection end nodes of the turntable and slewing mechanism. The overall structure of the working device was welded with Q355B steel; the welding base plate material was Q235A, and the pin seat material was 35# steel. The author’s employer company has accumulated a large amount of experimental data during the product development and manufacturing process, forming a reliable material database. Based on this material database, the author obtains the material properties used in the article. The material properties are in Table 1.

Calculation of comprehensive contribution based on improved TOPSIS

The entropy TOPSIS method48 was used to evaluate the comprehensive contribution of each design parameter to the response. Both information entropy and the Gini coefficient are metrics used to measure the purity of a sample set. However, the calculation of information entropy involves a large number of logarithmic operations. Therefore, to simplify the calculation process, this paper improved the entropy TOPSIS by introducing the Gini coefficient to calculate the purity of each response.

Establishment of surrogate model

The objective function and constraints reflect the response to each design variable, and the relationship between each design variable and the different responses is complex. To improve the prediction accuracy and reduce the difficulty of operation, a surrogate model resource pool was established in HyperStudy optimisation software using common surrogate models, such as the MLSR, Kriging model, and RBF, by including a combination of different parameters for each surrogate model. Based on the sample data, the optimal surrogate model can be automatically generated by the software through the calculation of the coefficient of determination. 90% of the sample data is used for training, while 10% is used for testing. In terms of computational efficiency, the method adopted in this study involves calculating the R² for multiple individual surrogate models, resulting in a slightly lower efficiency compared to the single surrogate model method. However, it significantly reduces the difficulty of manually selecting high-precision surrogate models. When compared to the combined surrogate model method, the present approach exhibits notable advantages in computational efficiency. This advantage is achieved by replacing the intricate weight optimization process of the combined surrogate model with a recursive algorithm designed to achieve the maximum \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}^{2}$$\end{document} value. Additionally, this method facilitates straightforward implementation in computational programs. Upon comprehensive evaluation of both computational accuracy and efficiency, the methodology introduced in this study emerges as a superior alternative to both the single surrogate model and the combined surrogate model methods.

Considering the robust global search capabilities and minimal dependence on the quality of the initial population that genetic algorithms (GAs) possess, and as illustrated in Fig. 11c and f, there exist strong nonlinear relationships between stress response, deformation response, and design variables, respectively. To avoid converging to local optimal solutions, genetic algorithms were selected for optimizing and solving the optimization model of the working device. In this study, a bias load was applied to the left two bucket teeth. Owing to the symmetry of the working device structure and computational complexity, the lightweight design process was simplified by including only the thickness of the top plate, bottom plate, and left plate of the boom as design variables. The stress situation of excavators during actual operation is very complex. Therefore, when calculating the actual mass reduction of the boom, the mass of the right plates must be replaced by the mass of the optimised left plates. Based on the plate sizes commonly used in excavator manufacturing and the stress and deformation contours of the optimised working device shown in Fig. 14, the mass of the boom was reduced by 141.38 kg for a lightweight ratio of 9.51%. Despite an increase of 5.09% in maximum stress and 8.27% in maximum deformation compared to the pre-optimization design, they remained within the allowable ranges for boom structure design.

Conclusion

Considering the working device of a hydraulic excavator as the research object, a lightweight design method was derived and applied based on a surrogate modelling approach. The strength and stiffness of the working device were analysed under the maximum digging force attitude, and the accuracy of the model was verified against experiment results. A maximum stress of 224.65 MPa appeared in the connection between the left middle side plate and the middle top plate of the boom; the stresses in other areas were considerably smaller than the allowable stresses of the materials used, suggesting a great deal of space for optimisation.

The comprehensive contributions of 12 boom design variables were therefore calculated using the improved TOPSIS method to determine that the thicknesses of the rear top plate, middle top plate, front top plate, left middle side plate, left front side plate, rear bottom plate, middle bottom plate, and front bottom plate had the greatest impact on boom performance. To automatically obtain surrogate model construction techniques, and to compute the associated coefficient of determination using collected sample points, a combined approach involving inner and outer loops was adopted to thoroughly traverse the surrogate model resource pool. The automatically generated surrogate model method was subsequently applied to construct the response surfaces describing the mass, maximum stress, and maximum deformation of the working device and establish an optimisation model accordingly. A genetic algorithm was applied to solve this optimisation model. The results indicate that the boom mass was reduced by 141.38 kg for a lightweight proportion of 9.51%. Despite an increase of 5.09% in maximum stress and 8.27% in maximum deformation compared to the pre-optimization design, the lightweight design remained within the permissible stress range for the boom structure. At the same time, the fatigue life of the boom arm was calculated to be 7642 h, meeting the fatigue design requirements of the boom. An actual verification of the optimised design indicated that the proposed method effectively reduced the mass of the boom by 9.30%, providing guiding significance for lightweight design of the structure. Next, the author will continue to focus on the performance of this excavator and apply this method to other series of products.

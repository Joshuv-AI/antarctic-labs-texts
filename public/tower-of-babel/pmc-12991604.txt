Efficiency of Root Canal Treatment Using Loops While Endodontic Treatment: A Clinical Study
Authors: Chitharanjan Shetty, Kodithala Sravya, Abhilasha Bhawalkar, Alok Dubey, Tejaswi Kala, Prachi Sethy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurpose:The use of magnification tools, including loops, has become increasingly prevalent in endodontics to improve precision and outcomes. This study evaluates the efficiency of root canal treatment (RCT) performed using loops compared to conventional techniques.Methods:A clinical trial involving 100 patients was conducted, with participants divided equally into two groups: those treated using magnification loops and those treated conventionally. Treatment duration, procedural errors, and patient-reported satisfaction were assessed.Results:The loop-assisted group demonstrated a 25% reduction in treatment time and significantly fewer procedural errors (P < 0.05). Patient satisfaction scores were higher in the loop group compared to the conventional group.Conclusions:Magnification loops enhance the efficiency and accuracy of RCT, leading to better clinical outcomes and higher patient satisfaction.

INTRODUCTION

Root canal treatment (RCT) is a vital procedure in endodontics aimed at preserving teeth by removing infected pulp tissue and effectively sealing the root canal system. Success depends on precise cleaning, shaping, and obturation, but challenges like limited visibility and intricate canal anatomy often result in procedural errors, including missed canals and perforations.[12] To address these challenges, magnification tools, such as dental loops, have gained popularity. These tools enhance visibility, allowing clinicians to navigate complex canal systems more accurately and reduce errors.[3] Research indicates that magnification can improve treatment outcomes, efficiency, and operator ergonomics.[4] However, while operating microscopes have been extensively studied, clinical evidence evaluating the effectiveness of dental loops remains sparse.[5] This study aims to assess the impact of magnification loops on treatment duration, procedural accuracy, and patient satisfaction, providing critical insights into their utility during RCT.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS v25.0. Independent t-tests were used for continuous variables, and Chi-square tests were used for categorical data. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Procedural errors, such as missed canals and perforations, were also lower in the loop group (8%) compared to the conventional group (22%) (P = 0.015).

DISCUSSION

Efficiency and treatment duration

Magnification loops significantly reduced treatment duration, with procedures completed 25% faster than in the conventional group. This finding aligns with prior research suggesting that enhanced visibility provided by magnification allows for quicker identification and negotiation of root canals.[12] Efficient canal preparation and cleaning can be achieved with fewer interruptions, reducing operator fatigue and increasing productivity. In clinical practice, this time-saving benefit is especially important in busy dental settings where maintaining high-quality care without compromising speed is critical.

Reduction in procedural errors

The loop group demonstrated a markedly lower rate of procedural errors (8%) compared to the conventional group (22%). Procedural errors, including missed canals and perforations, can compromise treatment outcomes and lead to endodontic failure.[3] Magnification tools enhance the clinician’s ability to identify complex canal anatomy and perform precision work. Prior studies corroborate that magnification improves accuracy and reduces the incidence of complications, contributing to better long-term success rates.[4] By minimizing errors, magnification loops not only improve the quality of RCT but also lower the need for retreatment, benefiting both patients and clinicians.

Patient satisfaction

Patient satisfaction was significantly higher in the loop group, with 60% reporting being “very satisfied” compared to 30% in the conventional group. Shorter treatment times and fewer complications are likely contributors to this improved satisfaction. Patients value efficient and precise treatment, associating it with higher-quality care.[5678] Additionally, fewer postoperative complications, such as pain or discomfort from procedural errors, enhance the recovery experience and further improve satisfaction. These findings reinforce the importance of advanced tools like magnification loops in delivering patient-centered care.

Advantages over conventional methods

While conventional techniques depend heavily on operator skill, magnification tools provide consistent visual enhancement, reducing reliance on manual expertise. Although operating microscopes offer higher magnification and illumination, their cost and complexity can limit accessibility, particularly in general dental practices. Magnification loops, being portable, affordable, and easy to use, offer a practical alternative for clinicians seeking to improve outcomes without substantial financial investment.[91011] This accessibility makes them an excellent entry point into advanced technology in dentistry.

Limitations and future directions

The study’s limitations include its small sample size and focus on single-rooted teeth, which may not represent the complexity of multi-rooted cases. Long-term studies examining treatment outcomes, cost-effectiveness, and patient retention could provide a more comprehensive understanding of their benefits.

CONCLUSION

This study demonstrates that magnification loops significantly improve the efficiency, accuracy, and patient satisfaction in root canal treatment. The loop group experienced shorter treatment times, fewer procedural errors, and higher satisfaction scores, making loops a valuable tool in endodontic practice. While conventional methods still have their place, magnification loops offer a practical and cost-effective solution for enhancing clinical outcomes. These findings support the integration of magnification tools into routine endodontic procedures, offering tangible benefits for both clinicians and patients. Future studies should further explore the long-term impact of magnification on treatment success and patient retention.

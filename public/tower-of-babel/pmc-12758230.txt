A retrospective chart review of UK patients with advanced renal cell carcinoma treated with nivolumab plus ipilimumab
Authors: Tom Geldart, Lisa Pickering, Anand Sharma, Matthew Wheater, Deborah Scott, Omi Parikh, Stacey Coleman, Poonam Dhokia, Michael. J. Hale, Luis Vaz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThere is limited real-world evidence regarding patients with intermediate/poor-risk advanced renal cell carcinoma (aRCC) receiving nivolumab plus ipilimumab (NIVO+IPI) in England. This study aimed to describe characteristics, treatment patterns, and efficacy outcomes in these patients.MethodsA retrospective chart review of medical records in patients with aRCC receiving NIVO+IPI at any line of therapy between 5 April 2019 and 1 April 2022 in five sites across England was conducted. Data were analyzed descriptively overall, in patients with and without NIVO maintenance after NIVO+IPI initiation, and by prior nephrectomy status.ResultsIn total, 128 patients (mean age 60.7 years [standard deviation 10.1], 71.1% male) were eligible; most received first-line (1L) NIVO+IPI (n = 122, 95.3%). Median follow-up was 13.6 months (interquartile range [IQR] 7.1–25.0). Median progression-free survival and overall survival (OS) were 7.6 months (95% confidence interval [CI]: 5.6, 13.1) and 30.7 months (95% CI: 22.5, not reached), respectively. Median OS was not reached in patients who received NIVO+IPI plus NIVO maintenance and patients with prior nephrectomy.ConclusionPatients who received NIVO+IPI with NIVO maintenance and patients who had undergone prior nephrectomy experienced the most favorable survival outcomes, aligning with results from previous studies.

Introduction

Kidney cancer accounts for 4% of all cancer diagnoses, with approximately 13,000 new cases reported annually in the UK. Renal cell carcinomas (RCC) are the most common form, comprising 86% of all kidney cancer diagnoses [1]. Most RCC cases are discovered incidentally, rather than being diagnosed following presentation of characteristic disease symptoms such as hematuria, persistent loin pain, or a renal mass [2,3]. Consequently, ~70% of patients are diagnosed with advanced disease which is associated with poorer outcomes [4].

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) block immune checkpoint proteins, facilitating immune system activation, thereby enabling immune responses directed at cancer cells [5]. Based on favorable outcomes, they are commonplace in the treatment landscape for advanced RCC (aRCC) [6]. Nivolumab (NIVO) and ipilimumab (IPI) are two such ICIs; NIVO is a programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) inhibitor that modulates the PD-1 checkpoint protein on T cells, increasing the immune response against cancer cells, whereas IPI is a cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4) inhibitor that modulates the early activation of T cells to help initiate the immune response.

In the United Kingdom, nivolumab plus ipilimumab (NIVO+IPI) combination therapy is indicated for first line (1 L) treatment of aRCC in adult patients with intermediate/poor prognostic risk [7–9]. However, during the study, the SARS-CoV-2 virus (otherwise known as COVID-19) reached pandemic levels in the UK and, as a result, NHS England endorsed interim reimbursement changes, allowing clinicians greater flexibility in prescribing, permitting deviation from clinical guidelines in some circumstances and allowing patients to receive NIVO+IPI beyond 1 L [10]. The recommended treatment schedule of NIVO+IPI in the treatment of aRCC begins with both NIVO and IPI being administered intravenously (IV) every three weeks for four doses, which is known as the combination phase, followed by NIVO monotherapy IV every two to four weeks for an indefinite period of time, which is known as the maintenance phase [11].

Recommendations for combination NIVO+IPI therapy in aRCC are based on evidence from the pivotal CheckMate 214 trial which demonstrated improved long-term outcomes for treatment-naïve patients treated with NIVO+IPI compared with the anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) sunitinib [12]. Prior to the approval of NIVO+IPI in the treatment of aRCC, other targeted therapies were available for the treatment of aRCC, including anti-VEGF therapies (e.g., axitinib, cabozantinib, and sunitinib) and mammalian target of rapamycin inhibitors (e.g., temsirolimus and everolimus) [13,14]. Historically, nephrectomy was a common consideration in the management of aRCC, however, in the current anti-VEGF/immunotherapy treatment era, the role of nephrectomy is debated, with conflicting evidence regarding its benefit to patients. Some studies have found that nephrectomy prior to initiation of immunotherapy for aRCC enhances improvements in overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) [15,16]. However, the CARMENA trial, which compared the outcomes of treating aRCC with sunitinib following cytoreductive nephrectomy versus sunitinib alone, concluded that sunitinib alone was non-inferior to treatment following nephrectomy (95.2% total nephrectomy, 4.8% partial nephrectomy) [17].

This study aimed to provide real-world evidence (RWE) of treatment sequencing and efficacy outcomes of adult patients with aRCC receiving NIVO+IPI at any line of therapy (LOT), with and without prior nephrectomy.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a multi-center, retrospective cohort study of adult patients with aRCC treated with NIVO+IPI at five National Health Service (NHS) centers across England. Patients were eligible for inclusion in the study if they had a diagnosis of aRCC and were initiated on combination NIVO+IPI at any LOT (i.e., index date) during the research window. Patients were excluded if aged <18 years on the date of aRCC diagnosis, diagnosed with malignant primary or secondary tumors in addition to aRCC, had an incomplete minimum dataset, or received NIVO and/or IPI as part of an investigational study. The study was reviewed and approved by the Health Regulatory Authority (IRAS Project ID: 283338). Written informed consent was not required because all data collection was retrospective and there was no contact with patients.

Data collection

Data were extracted from electronic and paper medical records of patients with aRCC using a bespoke electronic case report form (eCRF) between 5 April 2019 and 1 April 2022. The index date was defined as the date of treatment initiation with either 1 L NIVO monotherapy or NIVO+IPI at any LOT and patients were followed up until most recent visit, death, or end of follow-up (EOFU; 1 April 2022). A five-year lookback period (from the earliest possible index date) was utilized to collect data on comorbidities and treatment history.

Patient characteristics

Baseline characteristics collected included: demographic characteristics; clinical history, including date/s of RCC and/or aRCC diagnosis; tumor stage; metastatic sites; histological subtype; International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium (IMDC) prognostic risk group; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance score; and nephrectomy status (and if yes, radical or partial). Primary tumor characteristics (e.g., date of primary tumor diagnosis) could be collected outside of the lookback period.

Treatment characteristics

Treatment information included: therapy type and dosage; number of cycles of each therapy received; and any change to treatment as well as reasoning, if applicable. Change in LOT was defined as any change in regimen, irrespective of the next regimen being within the same or different class of drug, except for NIVO maintenance following NIVO+IPI combination therapy. Treatment discontinuation was defined as treatment being withdrawn permanently, and suspension as stopping then restarting the same treatment regimen within 180 days.

Treatment response

Tumor response was based on clinical assessment on a case-by-case basis in line with the real-world setting of this study, using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1 criteria [18] and either computed tomography scan or visual assessment by clinicians. Primary study outcomes were OS, PFS, objective response rate (ORR), and complete response rate (CRR). OS was defined as the time between index date and death; patients lost to follow-up, or who reached the EOFU were censored. PFS was defined as the time between index date and recorded date of disease progression. ORR was defined as the proportion of patients with complete and/or partial tumor response following treatment.

Adverse events

Adverse events (AEs) were considered an Exposure/Independent variable of interest and were classified by both grade and Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) system organ class (SOC) of AE origin [19]. The MedDRA comprises standardized medical terminology which is used internationally within regulatory settings and is employed by the National Cancer Institute (NCI) Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), Version 5 [20].

Statistical analysis

Analyses were descriptive with no formal power calculations performed. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic and clinical characteristics of the overall cohort. Categorical variables were summarized using counts and proportions, with associations tested using chi-squared and Fisher’s exact tests. Continuous variables were summarized using mean, standard deviation (SD), maximum, median, minimum, lower quartile (25%), and upper quartile (75%); association was tested using independent t-tests and Mann–Whitney U tests. Sankey plots were used to visualize treatment sequence pathways.

For time to event outcomes, results were summarized using maximum follow-up, restricted mean (cut at maximum follow-up), median, and time-point estimates for all Kaplan–Meier estimations. Subgroup analyses were undertaken on the cohorts of patients who received NIVO+IPI followed by NIVO maintenance (compared with NIVO+IPI without NIVO maintenance) and in those had undergone prior nephrectomy (compared with no prior nephrectomy). Wilcoxon and log-rank testing were used to test for differences between sub-groups. Analyses were performed using R version 3.4 or higher.

Exploratory analysis

Subsequent to the SARS-CoV-2 virus reaching pandemic levels in the UK, and the resultant interim changes made by NHS England to endorse and reimburse clinicians circumstantially deviating from clinical guidelines [10], we sought to describe the treatment sequencing of patients who deviated from normal treatment pathways as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic as an additional exploratory objective.

Results

Patient characteristics and demographics

Overall cohort

In total, 128 patients (mean age 60.7 years [SD 10.1], 71.1% male) met the eligibility criteria and were included in the study (Table 1). Median time from aRCC diagnosis to NIVO+IPI initiation was 1.5 months (interquartile range [IQR]: 0.7–2.9). Median and maximum follow-ups from index date were 13.6 months (IQR: 7.1–25.1) and 37.9 months, respectively. Of those with IMDC risk score reported, 53.8% (n = 63) presented with intermediate risk and 42.7% (n = 50) presented with poor risk at index. Most patients had clear cell histology (n = 109, 85.2%), of which 27 (24.8%) had evidence of sarcomatoid differentiation. Prior nephrectomy cohort

Of the 128 patients in the overall cohort, 55 had undergone prior nephrectomy (mean age 59.7 [SD 11.1], 76.4% male; Supplementary Table S1). Median time from aRCC diagnosis to NIVO+IPI initiation was 2.8 months (IQR: 1.2–6.2) and median follow-up from index was 19.8 months (IQR: 7.5–30.6). The majority (72.0%, n = 36) were classified as IMDC intermediate risk, with 20.0% (n = 10) recorded as poor risk. Histological subtypes were similar compared with the overall cohort, as were the presence and location of metastases.

Overall cohort

Impact of COVID-19 on treatment sequencing

Six patients received 1 L TKI therapy with cabozantinib (n = 5, 3.9%) or sunitinib (n = 1, 0.8%) before receiving 2 L NIVO+IPI (Supplementary Figure S1); of these patients, four received 2 L NIVO+IPI during the COVID-19 pandemic, while the remaining two received 1 L TKI treatment and 2 L NIVO+IPI after NICE reimbursement (in April 2019) but prior to the COVID-19 pandemic. Overall, median time on 1 L treatment was 2.8 months.

Prior nephrectomy cohort

In patients who had undergone prior nephrectomy, all patients received 1 L treatment (n = 55), 26 patients went on to receive 2 L treatment (47.3%), 6 patients received 3 L treatment (10.9%), and 2 patients received 4 L treatment (3.6%; Table 2). At 1 L, 30 patients (54.5%) received NIVO+IPI with NIVO maintenance and 22 patients (40.0%) received NIVO+IPI without NIVO maintenance (Supplementary Figure S2). The remaining patients received cabozantinib (n = 2, 3.6%) or sunitinib (n = 1, 1.8%), and both went on to receive NIVO+IPI (with or without NIVO maintenance) at 2 L. Of the prior nephrectomy patients who went on to receive 2 L treatment, the majority received cabozantinib (n = 23/29, 79.3%).

Of those patients with prior nephrectomy who progressed to NIVO maintenance following NIVO+IPI combination therapy at 1 L (n = 30) or 2 L (n = 2; Total, n = 32), most received all four cycles of NIVO+IPI before advancing to NIVO maintenance (n = 22, 68.8%), with small numbers receiving one (n = 1, 3.1%), two (n = 4, 12.5%), or three (n = 5, 15.6%) cycles before initiating NIVO maintenance (Supplementary Table S2).

Response rates and survival outcomes

Overall cohort

Median OS across all patients was 30.7 months [95% CI: 22.5, not reached], with 12-, 24-, and 36-month OS probabilities of 71.4%, 56.1%, and 45.8%, respectively (Figure 1). In patients who received combination NIVO+IPI only without NIVO maintenance in the 1 L setting, median OS was 13.8 months, with 12-, 24-, and 36-month OS probabilities of 57.9%, 43.7%, and 30.2%, respectively. In patients who received NIVO+IPI with NIVO maintenance at 1 L, OS was not reached; OS probabilities at 12-, 24-, and 36-months were numerically higher at 84.7%, 66.1%, and 60.6%, respectively. Median PFS across all patients was 7.6 months [95% CI: 5.6, 13.1] with 12-, 24-, and 36-month PFS probabilities of 40.3%, 33.3%, and 30.9%, respectively (Supplementary Figure S3). Similar to OS, median PFS was numerically longer in the subgroup of patients who received combination NIVO+IPI and NIVO maintenance at 1 L, at 23.8 months, with PFS probabilities at 12-, 24-, and 36-month of 56.6%, 46.2%, and 41.1%, respectively.

In patients receiving 1 L NIVO+IPI, the overall ORR was 29.7%. More patients who received NIVO maintenance met the criteria for complete or partial response to treatment compared with those who did not receive NIVO maintenance (n = 26, 40.6% vs. n = 12, 18.8%). Two patients (1.1%) recorded complete responses; both received NIVO+IPI with NIVO maintenance.

Prior nephrectomy cohort

Median OS in the 55 patients (43.0%) who had undergone prior nephrectomy was numerically superior to that of patients without prior nephrectomy (not reached vs. 19.6 months, respectively); 12-, 24-, and 36-months OS probabilities were 80.7%, 71.8%, and 60.1%, versus 64.6%, 40.0%, and 40.0%, respectively (Figure 2). PFS probabilities of patients with prior nephrectomy at 12-, 24-, and 36-months were 46.8%, 38.8%, and 34.5%, respectively; compared with 36.6%, 33.3%, and 33.3%, respectively, in patients without prior nephrectomy (Supplementary Figure S4). In patients with prior nephrectomy receiving 1 L NIVO+IPI, the ORR was 69.1%. More patients who also received NIVO maintenance met the criteria for complete or partial response to treatment compared with those who did not receive NIVO maintenance (n = 24, 43.8% vs. n = 5, 8.7%). Both patients who achieved complete response were recorded as receiving prior nephrectomy (2/55, 3.6%) and receiving NIVO+IPI with NIVO maintenance.

Treatment discontinuation

Overall cohort

Overall, 104 (81.3%) patients discontinued 1 L therapy, with 24 1 L patients (18.8%) remaining on treatment until the end of the study period (Table 2). In the overall cohort, the most frequently reported reason for discontinuation was progression of disease (51.0% at 1 L, 63.0% at 2 L, and 58.3% at 3 L), followed by AEs (37.5% at 1 L, 13.0% at 2 L, and 16.7% at 3 L), and clinician choice (7.7% at 1 L, 13.0% at 2 L, and 16.7% at 3 L). Reasons for discontinuation were similar between NIVO+IPI with and without NIVO maintenance.

Several patients discontinued NIVO+IPI during the combination phase prior to completing all four cycles, but still went on to receive NIVO maintenance (n = 19, 29.7%; Supplementary Table S2). In patients who discontinued 1 L NIVO+IPI and did not initiate NIVO maintenance (n = 60), similar rates of discontinuation were observed at each cycle (Cycle 1: n = 15, 25.0%; Cycle 2: n = 14, 23.3%; Cycle 3: n = 16, 26.7%; Cycle 4: n = 19, 31.7%).

Prior nephrectomy cohort

A total of 42 (76.4%) patients in the prior nephrectomy cohort discontinued 1 L therapy, with 13 patients (23.6%) remaining on treatment until the end of the study period (Table 2). In this patient subgroup, the most frequent reasons for discontinuation were progression of disease (1 L: 45.2%; 2 L: 66.7%; 3 L: 75.0%), followed by AEs (1 L: 38.1%; 2 L: 16.7%; 3 L: 25.0%). Of this subgroup, 23 (41.8%) patients discontinued NIVO+IPI before initiating NIVO maintenance (Supplementary Table S2). In those who discontinued combination NIVO+IPI prior to initiation of NIVO maintenance, similar discontinuation rates at each LOT were observed (Cycle 1: n = 5, 21.7%; Cycle 2: n = 6, 26.1%; Cycle 3: n = 6, 26.1%; Cycle 4: n = 6, 26.1%).

Safety

Overall cohort

Treatment-related AEs (TRAEs) of any grade leading to discontinuation were observed in 37.5% (n = 39) of patients at 1 L (Table 2). Fewer AEs were observed in patients who received combination NIVO+IPI and went on to receive NIVO maintenance compared with patients who received NIVO+IPI alone (n = 13, 33.3% vs. n = 26, 43.3%). Across all LOTs, AEs included gastrointestinal disorders (36.2% of all adverse events), hepatobiliary disorders (17.0%), and renal and urinary disorders (14.9%; Supplementary Table S3). Across both cohorts, the majority of reported toxicities were Grade 2 (n = 16, 41.0%) and Grade 3 (n = 15, 38.5%; Supplementary Table S4).

Prior nephrectomy subgroup

A total of 16 patients (29.1%) in the prior nephrectomy subgroup reported TRAEs of any grade following treatment with NIVO+IPI at 1 L (Table 2). Fewer patients who received NIVO maintenance following NIVO+IPI experienced AEs compared with patients who received NIVO+IPI alone (n = 6/32 (18.8%) vs. n = 10/23 (43.5%); Supplementary Table S5). Recorded AEs across all LOTs included gastrointestinal disorders (37.5% of all events in this subgroup), and hepatobiliary disorders (6.3%). Toxicity in patients with prior nephrectomy reflected trends observed in the overall cohort, with most events recorded as being Grade 2 (n = 6, 37.5%) and Grade 3 (n = 8, 50.0%; Supplementary Table S6).

Discussion

The purpose of this study was to generate RWE of treatment sequencing and efficacy outcomes in adult patients receiving NIVO+IPI for aRCC in the UK. A total of 128 patients across five centers who received NIVO+IPI at any LOT were included in the study; of these 95.3% of patients received NIVO+IPI at 1 L. Survival outcomes, OS and PFS, were more favorable in patients receiving NIVO+IPI followed by NIVO maintenance and in patients with prior nephrectomy. A third of patients experienced a TRAE that led to discontinuation at 1 L, with lower rates of AEs in patients who went on to receive NIVO maintenance after NIVO+IPI initiation compared with those who received NIVO+IPI alone.

Treatment sequences aligned with NICE recommendations and European guidelines during the study period [7–9]. NICE recommend NIVO+IPI as a therapeutic option for treatment naïve patients with intermediate- or poor-risk aRCC [9]. When NIVO+IPI was not administered at 1L, TKIs cabozantinib or sunitinib were used prior to initiation of NIVO+IPI (n = 5, 3.9%; n = 1, 0.8%, respectively). European guidelines state TKIs may be considered as 1 L treatments in circumstances such as immunotherapy ineligibility [7,8]. One patient each received 1 L cabozantinib and sunitinib prior to approval of 1 L NIVO+IPI combination therapy in the UK. The remaining four patients received 1 L cabozantinib treatment between March 2020 and June 2020; corresponding with the initial UK COVID-19 lockdown, during which treatment protocol modifications were permitted to allow for primary TKI treatment followed by 2 L NIVO+IPI [10].

The maximum follow-up recorded in this study was 36 months, with a corresponding OS of 71.4% at 12-months, and 56.1% at 24-months. In the CheckMate 214 pivotal trial, efficacy outcomes were recorded at 18- and 48-months, with reported OS of 75.0% [12] and 53.4% [21], respectively. Although OS in our study was comparable with CheckMate 214, both ORR and median PFS in the pivotal trial were higher than observed in this real-world patient population (47% vs. 29.7%; 11.6 months vs. 7.6 months, respectively) [12]. This real-world cohort comprised a higher proportion of patients with poor prognosis by IMDC classification than CheckMate 214 (42.7% vs. 16.5%) and included those with CNS metastases and non-clear cell histology, which were excluded from CheckMate 214. Therefore, any cross-trial comparisons between CheckMate 214 and the current cohort should be interpreted with caution.

Both OS and PFS have also been evaluated in a recent global real-world study evaluating combination immunotherapies (IO+IO: NIVO+IPI; IO + TKI: Pembrolizumab + axitinib, NIVO + cabozantinib, avelumab + axitinib, pembrolizumab + lenvatinib) in patients with metastatic RCC. Median OS in patients with intermediate/poor prognostic risk levels who received NIVO+IPI was 29.7 months in ARON-1, whereas median OS was not reached in this cohort. Median PFS was also longer in this study, at 11.1 months, compared with 7.6 months [22]. While the ARON-1 study did not include patients from the UK, it did cover countries in Western Europe, and as such the results of this study should be relatively generalizable to the outcomes observed in ARON-1. Our study cohort contained more patients classified as poor risk compared with the ARON-1 cohort (42.7% vs. 26%), indicating that there may be other factors impacting the observed differences in OS and PFS between the studies. It should also be noted that the ARON-1 study did not stratify patients based on whether they received combination therapy alone, or if they also received NIVO maintenance.

As expected, patients who experienced significant AEs during the combination NIVO+IPI phase were less likely to progress to NIVO maintenance, as evidenced by more than two-thirds of all AEs that led to discontinuation occurring in patients who only received the combination phase of treatment. Evidence indicates that patients who experience immune related AEs (irAEs) may experience better efficacy outcomes than those who do not, as they may be indicative of a host immune system response to immunological treatment [23,24]. It is likely that the majority of patients who received NIVO maintenance therapy following combination NIVO+IPI either did not experience an AE or they experienced AEs that did not lead to discontinuation; as a result, these patients consequently demonstrated longer OS and PFS compared with the overall cohort that received NIVO+IPI alone. This is corroborated by a multicenter cohort study conducted in Poland, which found that patients with aRCC treated with NIVO+IPI were 60% more likely to demonstrate PFS if they experienced irAEs, and the likelihood of longer survival increased with the number of irAEs experienced (log-rank p = 0.042) [25]. This phenomena has also been observed following the use of NIVO+IPI in other malignancies, including metastatic melanoma, where patients who experienced irAEs as a result of NIVO+IPI treatment reported an 80% lower risk of both death (hazard ratio [HR] 0.2, 95% CI [0.07–0.57], p = 0.001) and progression of the disease (HR 0.2, 95% CI [0.09–0.47], p < 0.0001) during 12 months of follow-up [26].

A further subgroup of interest included patients who had undergone prior nephrectomy (43.0% of the total cohort). In our study, patients with prior nephrectomy had numerically superior median OS (not reached vs. 19.6 months in patients without prior nephrectomy) and OS probabilities compared with patients without prior nephrectomy. Our observations aligned with those reported in the ARON-1 RWE study, where longer median OS and PFS were observed in the subgroup of patients who had undergone prior nephrectomy [22]. Whilst outcomes were more favorable, fewer patients had undergone nephrectomy in this cohort compared with both the broader UK RCC patient population and the ARON-1 cohort (43.0% vs. 56% vs. 66%, respectively) [27]. This may be a consequence of the COVID-19 pandemic and its impact on surgical protocols over this period [28], however, other factors should be considered. It should be noted, however, that more patients with prior nephrectomy had intermediate versus poor prognosis disease. In addition, clinical bias is likely to mean that patients with lower bulk metastatic disease are more likely to undergo cytoreductive nephrectomy than patients with high volume metastatic disease. In addition, findings of the CARMENA trial demonstrating non-inferiority of patients undergoing no nephrectomy while receiving targeted therapy for aRCC may have impacted clinician decision making [17].

There are several limitations associated with data collected from paper and electronic records. First, when collecting RWE data there is potential for recall bias, erroneous or incomplete data entry, and variation in reporting practices during data collection, which could lead to heterogeneity in the reporting of AEs and both progression and tumor response information. Second, selection bias, which could normally be minimized by random sampling, may be more likely in this study due to limited patient numbers. Third, the parallel use of paper-based and electronic records is also a limitation with increased risk of inconsistencies and the potential for missing documentation [29]. Paper-based records consist of less-structured free text versus more standardized and structured electronic records which feature a more controlled vocabulary; one study has indicated that paper-based patient records may benefit from minor quantitative advantages regarding coding of diagnoses [29]. While efforts were made to minimize data inaccuracies with eCRF validation checks and investigator eCRF training, some may still exist. Fourth, analyses included data from patients treated at five NHS centers which, although geographically dispersed, may not represent the wider UK aRCC patient population. However, inclusion of multiple centers makes the study findings more robust and applicable to a real-world population. Last, immortal time bias is a limitation of this analysis because patients had to survive long enough to start NIVO maintenance therapy, artificially inflating survival probabilities in that group; this created a bias in favor of the NIVO+IPI with NIVO maintenance group, as the observed survival reflected this “immortal” period before starting treatment.

Conclusion

This study provides RWE regarding characteristics and outcomes of patients with aRCC who received NIVO+IPI across five centers in England. Whilst treatment discontinuation due to AEs and disease progression was common, efficacy outcomes in patients with aRCC were comparable with those reported in the CheckMate 214 pivotal trial and the ARON-1 RWE study. Patients who received NIVO+IPI followed by NIVO maintenance therapy and patients with prior nephrectomy experienced numerically better outcomes compared with patients who received NIVO+IPI combination therapy alone and who had not undergone prior nephrectomy, respectively. AEs were the main reason for a higher proportion of discontinuations in patients who did not receive NIVO monotherapy maintenance following NIVO+IPI than in patients who did receive NIVO maintenance, providing a potential explanation for the improved efficacy outcomes observed in these patients as irAEs can be positive indicator of a response to immunotherapy.

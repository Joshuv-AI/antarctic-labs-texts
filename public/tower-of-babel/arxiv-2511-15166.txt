finite time blow-up analysis for the generalized Proudman-Johnson model
Jie Guo ∗ QuanSen Jiu †

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the generalized Proudman-Johnson equation posed on the torus. In the critical regime where the parameter a \displaystyle a is close to and slightly greater than 1, we establish finite time blow-up of smooth solutions to the inviscid case. Moreover, we show that the blow-up is asymptotically self-similar for a class of smooth initial data. In contrast, when the parameter a \displaystyle a lies slightly below 1, we prove the global in time existence for the same initial data. In addition, we demonstrate that inviscid Proudman-Johnson equation with Hölder continuous data also develops a self-similar blow-up. Finally, for the viscous case with a > 1 \displaystyle a>1 , we prove that smooth initial data can still lead to finite time blow-up.

1  Introduction

In this paper, we investigate a one-parameter family of partial differential equations given by

((1.1))

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{txx}+uu_{xxx}=au_{x}u_{xx}+\nu u_{xxxx},\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},

posed on the torus
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}=[-\pi,\pi]
, where
\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter and
\displaystyle\nu\geq 0
represents the viscosity. This equation was originally introduced in
[
29
]
for specific values of
\displaystyle a
as a model derived from high-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations under certain symmetry assumptions. By defining
\displaystyle u_{xx}=\omega
, equation
(1.1)
can be written as

((1.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{t}+u\omega_{x}=a\omega u_{x}+\nu\omega_{xx},&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},\\
u_{xx}=\omega,&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T}.\end{cases}

When
\displaystyle a=1
, (
(1.2)
) is the Proudman-Johnson equation. In particular, when
\displaystyle a=1
and
\displaystyle\nu=0
, (
(1.2)
) is the inviscid Proudman-Johnson equation, which models inviscid, incompressible flows near a boundary (see
[
6
]
). For general
\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{R}
, equation (
(1.2)
), which is equivalent to (
(1.1)
), is called a generalized Proudman-Johnson equation. By generalizing the Proudman-Johnson equation with a parameter

\displaystyle a
, one can systematically study the balance between the “convection term”(
\displaystyle uu_{xxx}
) and the “stretching term”(
\displaystyle u_{x}u_{xx}
), which leads to either the creation or depletion of singularities in finite time (see
[
27
,
28
]
).

The generalized Proudman-Johnson equation encompasses several well-known models. When
\displaystyle a=-3
, it reduces to the classical Burgers’ equation, which plays a central role in gas dynamics (see
[
1
]
). For
\displaystyle a=-2
, the equation becomes the Hunter-Saxton equation (see
[
14
,
19
,
20
,
34
]
), which arises in the modeling of the orientation dynamics of nematic liquid crystals. The case
\displaystyle a=0
, the equation
\displaystyle u_{txx}+uu_{xxx}=0
appears in contexts that connect projective geometry and gravitational models (see
[
30
]
). The inviscid form of model (
(1.2)
) also resembles the generalized Constantin-Lax-Majda (gCLM) model (see
[
28
]
), which takes the form

((1.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{t}+u\omega_{x}=a\omega u_{x}+\nu\omega_{xx},&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},\\
u_{x}=H\omega,&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle H
represents the Hilbert transform. When
\displaystyle a=1
, the gCLM model (
(1.3)
) reduces to the De Gregorio (DG) equation. Notably, the explicit steady state
\displaystyle(\omega,u)=(-\sin(x),\sin(x))
is a solution to both equation (
(1.2)
) and the DG equation on the torus
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}
.

The local well-posedness of the generalized Proudman Johnson equation
(1.1)
in the periodic setting was established in
[
29
,
26
]
. In particular,
[
29
]
derived a sufficient condition for finite time blow-up of solutions in the case
\displaystyle a=\infty
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{txx}-u_{x}u_{xx}=\nu u_{xxxx},\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T}.

Moreover, the authors in
[
29
,
26
]
investigated parameter conditions on
\displaystyle a
that ensure the global existence of solutions for general initial data. Further analytical and numerical results concerning blow-up phenomena were also presented in
[
29
]
. For
\displaystyle a=1
, more refined blow-up criteria have been derived, as discussed in
[
6
]
, where the authors employed various trajectory-based analytical techniques.

In
[
7
]
, the authors proved the existence of a class of global-in-time solutions to the generalized inviscid Proudman-Johnson equation for parameters of the form
\displaystyle a=-\frac{n+3}{n+1}
, where
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
. Subsequently, in
[
8
]
, global existence was established for
\displaystyle a\in[-2,-1)
in the inviscid setting, using the method of characteristics. In
[
31
,
32
]
, various global existence and blow-up scenarios are investigated based on a representation formula for the velocity gradient along particle trajectories. In contrast, singularity formation for
\displaystyle a>1
remains less understood. In
[
26
]
, the authors construct non-smooth self-similar solutions of the form
\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{F(x)}{T-t}
, which blow-up at time
\displaystyle T
. These solutions are obtained by solving the nonlinear ordinary differential equation

((1.4))

\displaystyle\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(x)+F(x)F^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)-aF^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime\prime}(x)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{T},

for
\displaystyle a>1
. However, in this regime, equation (
(1.4)
) admits only non-smooth solutions. More recently, Kogelbauer
[
16
]
established new criteria for global existence and finite time blow-up in the inviscid generalized Proudman-Johnson equation, depending on the parameter
\displaystyle a
. In particular,
[
16
]
demonstrated that even smooth initial data may lead to finite time singularities when
\displaystyle a>1
. Additionally,
[
16
]
provides a physical derivation of the generalized equation for arbitrary values of the parameter
\displaystyle a
.

In this paper, we will investigate model
(1.2)
on the torus
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}
for parameter values
\displaystyle a
near 1, which can be interpreted as a slight perturbation of the Proudman-Johnson case
\displaystyle(a=1)
. For the inviscid model
(1.2)
, this regime is particularly interesting for smooth initial data, as it captures two distinct dynamical scenarios:

•

When
\displaystyle a>1
, the advection term is slightly weaker than the vortex stretching term, leading to finite time blow-up;

•

When
\displaystyle a<1
, the advection term slightly dominates the vortex stretching term, resulting in different long-time behavior.

Moreover, we establish finite time singularity formation for the inviscid model
(1.2)
with Hölder continuous data in the case
\displaystyle a=1
and the finite-time blow-up for the viscous case with
\displaystyle a>1
.

Before stating our main results, we introduce a class of weighted Sobolev spaces that will serve as the functional framework for our analysis.

Definition 1.1

(Weighted norms and spaces)
.

Define the singular weight
\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{4\pi\sin^{2}(\frac{x}{2})}
and the weighted norms
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}}
as follows

((1.5))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{|f_{x}|^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\frac{x}{2})}\,\mathrm{d}x,

((1.6))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f_{xx}|^{2}\cos^{2}(\frac{x}{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

The associated Hilbert spaces are defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})|\,\text{f is odd},\ \|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}<+\infty\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T})|\,\text{f is odd},\ \|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}<+\infty\},

which are equipped with inner products naturally induced by the corresponding norms.

Define
\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}=\frac{\sin((k+1)x)}{k+1}-\frac{\sin(kx)}{k}
. In fact,
\displaystyle\{\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)},k\geq 1\}
forms a complete orthonormal basis of
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
(see Lemma
2.6
).
The norm and inner product in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
were first introduced in
[
18
]
for stability analysis. The
\displaystyle\mathcal{W}
norm can be found in
[
3
]
for the purpose of higher order derivative estimates.

Throughout this paper,
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}},\|\cdot\|_{H^{m}}
and
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}}
represent the norms in the spaces
\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{T}),H^{m}(\mathbb{T})
and
\displaystyle L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, respectively. Similarly,
\displaystyle\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
denotes the usual inner product in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}fg\,\mathrm{d}x.

We use
\displaystyle C,C_{i}
to denote absolute constants and
\displaystyle C(A,B,\cdots,Z)
to denote constants depending on
\displaystyle A,B,\cdots,Z
. And we also employ the notation
\displaystyle A\lesssim B
to indicate that there exists a constant
\displaystyle C
such that
\displaystyle A\leq CB
.

Now we are ready to state our main results. Our first main result establishes the existence of a family of self-similar solutions to the inviscid model
(1.2)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(The case
\displaystyle\nu=0
)
There exists an absolute constant
\displaystyle\delta_{1}>0,
sufficiently small, such that the following statements hold:

(1) For
\displaystyle a\in(1,1+\delta_{1})
The model
(1.2)
with
\displaystyle\nu=0
develops a finite time singularity for some
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
initial data;

(2) For all
\displaystyle a\in(1-\delta_{1},1+\delta_{1}),
the model
(1.2)
admits a self-similar solution of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\omega(x,t)=\frac{1}{1+c_{\omega,a}t}\omega_{a}(x),

where
\displaystyle\omega_{a}
is an odd profile and
\displaystyle c_{\omega,a}
is the scaling parameter, satisfying

((1.7))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\omega_{a}+\sin(x)\|_{\mathcal{W}}\lesssim|1-a|,\quad|c_{\omega,a}-(1-a)|\leq\min\{C|1-a|^{2},|1-a|\},

for some absolute constant
\displaystyle C>0
. More precisely:

•

for
\displaystyle 1<a<1+\delta_{1}
, the scaling parameter satisfies
\displaystyle c_{\omega,a}<0
, and the corresponding solution
\displaystyle\omega(x,t)
blows up in finite time
\displaystyle T=-\frac{1}{c_{\omega,a}}
;

•

for
\displaystyle a=1
, the explicit steady state
\displaystyle\omega_{1}=-\sin(x)
solves the system with
\displaystyle c_{\omega,a}=0
;

•

for
\displaystyle 1-\delta_{1}<a<1
, one has
\displaystyle c_{\omega,a}>0
, and the solution
\displaystyle\omega(x,t)
exists globally with
\displaystyle O(t^{-1})
decay rate as
\displaystyle t\to\infty
.

Our next result concerns the formation of finite time singularities in the generalized Proudman-Johnson model with Hölder continuous initial data.

Theorem 1.2
.

(The case
\displaystyle\nu=0
and
\displaystyle a=1
)
There exists a constant
\displaystyle\delta_{2}>0
, such that for all
\displaystyle\alpha\in(1-\delta_{2},1),
the model
(1.2)
with
\displaystyle\nu=0
and
\displaystyle a=1
develops a finite time singularity for some
\displaystyle C^{\alpha}
initial data. Moreover, there exists a
\displaystyle C^{\alpha}
self-similar profile, analogous to the setting in Theorem
1.1
.

The final result concerns the finite time blow-up of model
(1.2)
in the presence of viscosity. The dynamic rescaling formulation suggests that the viscous terms are asymptotically small. Building upon Theorem
1.1
in the regime
\displaystyle 1<a<1+\delta_{3},
we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

(The case
\displaystyle\nu>0
)
There exists a constant
\displaystyle\delta_{3}>0
such that for all
\displaystyle a\in(1,1+\delta_{3}),
the model
(1.2)
with
\displaystyle\nu>0
develops a singularity in finite time for some
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
initial data.

We emphasize that, in contrast to the inviscid case, exact self-similar blow-up profiles do not exist due to the presence of viscosity.

In our analysis, we employ the framework of dynamic rescaling to establish the formation of singularities. This formulation was first introduced by McLaughlin, Papanicolaou, and co-authors in their study
of self-similar blow-up of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (see
[
23
,
17
]
). It was later developed into a powerful modulation technique, and has been applied to various blow-up problems including the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
[
15
,
24
]
, the nonlinear heat equation
[
25
]
, the generalized KdV equation
[
22
]
, and other
dispersive problems. More recently, this approach has been successfully adapted to prove singularity formation in the gCLM models
[
3
,
5
]
, the Euler equations
[
4
,
10
]
, and the Hou-Li model
[
13
]
.

Our blow-up analysis for the inviscid model
(1.2)
consists of several steps. In the first step, we reformulate the original equation (
(1.2)
) via dynamic rescaling, thereby transforming the singularity formation problem into the stability analysis of an approximate steady state in the rescaled variables. In the second step, we perform the stability analysis of this approximate steady state in the rescaled formulation, which primarily involves:

•

the identification of an approximate steady state;

•

energy estimates in a singularly weighted norm, providing both linear and nonlinear stability of this approximate profile.

By tracing back to the original variables, this yields finite time blow-up for the inviscid model (
(1.2)
). In the third step, we establish convergence of the dynamically rescaled solution to the true self-similar profile. However, the blow-up analysis for model
(1.2)
with viscosity is more challenging, as the viscous term does not provide damping and generates some bad terms when a singular weighted norm is employed. To address this, we establish the blow-up analysis for the viscous
(1.2)
using an energy norm that combines a singular weighted energy norm with a sum of higher-order Sobolev norms, as employed in
[
13
]
.

2  Preliminaries

In this section, we collect several technical lemmas needed later. We first derive an explicit expression for the velocity field
\displaystyle u
in terms of the vorticity
\displaystyle\omega
, under the assumption of odd symmetry. The following lemma is obtained through straightforward integration by parts.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\omega,u
are odd,
\displaystyle 2\pi
-periodic functions on
\displaystyle[-\pi,\pi]
, satisfying
\displaystyle u_{xx}=\omega.
Then
\displaystyle u
can be expressed as

((2.1))

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(x)=\int_{0}^{x}(x-y)\omega(y)\,\mathrm{d}y+xu_{x}(0),

where
\displaystyle u_{x}(0)
is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{x}(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}y\omega(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\rho^{1/2}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. Then the following identity holds.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\sin(x)f_{x},f\rho\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle f^{2},\rho\right\rangle.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

By a direct integration by parts, it holds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\sin(x)f_{x},f\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\sin(x)(f^{2})_{x}}{2\sin^{2}(\frac{x}{2})}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\cos(\frac{x}{2})}{2\sin(\frac{x}{2})}(f^{2})_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{f^{2}}{2\sin^{2}(\frac{x}{2})}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle f^{2},\rho\right\rangle.

∎

Lemma
2.3
will be used in Section
3
,
4
and
5
to estimate the nonlinear terms.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle\omega,u
be odd,
\displaystyle 2\pi
-periodic functions on
\displaystyle[-\pi,\pi]
related by
\displaystyle u_{xx}=\omega
, with
\displaystyle\omega\in\mathcal{H}
. Then the following estimates hold.

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|(u_{x}-u_{x}(0))\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

(2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\omega\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|u\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

To prove (1), by applying Hardy-type inequality and Poincaré’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|(u_{x}-u_{x}(0))\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\bigg\|\left(\int_{0}^{x}\partial_{x}^{2}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\rho^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega_{x}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\omega_{x}\rho^{1/2}\rho^{-1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

To prove (2), since
\displaystyle\omega
is odd and periodic, we only need to estimate
\displaystyle\|\omega\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}}
on
\displaystyle[0,\pi]
. Due to
\displaystyle\sin(\frac{x}{2})\geq\frac{2}{\pi}x
for
\displaystyle x\in[0,\pi]
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\omega}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\bigg|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg|\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\partial_{x}\omega(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\left(\int_{0}^{x}\sin^{2}(\frac{y}{2})\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/2}\bigg\|\frac{\omega_{x}}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega_{x}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\omega\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

For
\displaystyle\|u\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\displaystyle\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we use Pincaré’s inequality to yield

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{u}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\bigg|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Thus, we obtain the desired bounds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For
\displaystyle x\in[0,1],a,b>0
, it holds

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1-x^{a})x^{b}\leq\frac{a}{b}.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Applying Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1-x^{a})x^{b}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b}\left(\frac{b}{a}(1-x^{a})\right)(x^{a})^{\frac{b}{a}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{a}{b}\left(\frac{\frac{b}{a}(1-x^{a})+\frac{b}{a}x^{a}}{1+\frac{b}{a}}\right)^{\frac{b}{a}+1}=\frac{a}{b}\left(\frac{\frac{b}{a}}{1+\frac{b}{a}}\right)^{\frac{b}{a}+1}\leq\frac{a}{b}.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{7}{8}<\frac{9}{10}<\alpha<1.
Then, for all
\displaystyle x\in[-\pi,\pi]
and
\displaystyle i=0,1,2,
the following estimates hold.

((2.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{i}\omega_{res}|\lesssim|\alpha-1||\sin(x)|^{\kappa-i},

((2.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{i}\psi_{res}|+|\partial_{x}^{i}\psi_{res,x}|\lesssim|\alpha-1|,

((2.4))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad|(\alpha-1)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}|+|\sin(x)\partial_{x}\left((\alpha-1)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}\right)|\\
&\lesssim\min\{|\alpha-1|,|x|^{2}\}|\sin(x)|^{\alpha-1}.\end{aligned}

Here, we used the following notations

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\omega}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\sin(x),\quad\bar{\omega}_{\alpha}(x)=-\mathrm{sign}(x)\,|\sin(x)|^{\alpha},\quad\omega_{res}(x)=\bar{\omega}_{\alpha}(x)-\bar{\omega}(x),

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{u}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sin(x),\quad\bar{u}_{\alpha,xx}(x)=\bar{\omega}_{\alpha}(x),\quad u_{res}(x)=\bar{u}_{\alpha}(x)-\bar{u}(x).

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

By symmetry, it suffices to consider the case
\displaystyle x\geq 0
. We begin with the proof of
(2.2)
. Applying Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\omega_{res}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\big|\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha}-\sin(x)\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\big|\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\kappa}(\sin x)^{\alpha-\kappa}\big(1-(\sin x)^{1-\alpha}\big)\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha-\kappa}|\sin(x)|^{\kappa}\lesssim(1-\alpha)|\sin(x)|^{\kappa}.

A straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}=-\alpha\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-1}\cos(x)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,xx}=-\alpha(\alpha-1)\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-2}\cos^{2}(x)+\alpha(\sin(x))^{\alpha}.

Applying Lemma
2.4
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\bar{\omega}_{x}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\cos(x)\big(1-\alpha\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-1}\big)|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg|\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\kappa-1}\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-\kappa}\bigg(1-\alpha\big(\sin(x)\big)^{1-\alpha}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim|\alpha-1||\sin(x)|^{\kappa-1},

The estimate for
\displaystyle i=2
in (
(2.2)
) follows from a similar argument and is omitted. The bound
(2.3)
is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.1
.

Next, we address the estimate (
(2.4)
). First, it follows from
(2.2)
that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|(\alpha-1)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}|\lesssim|\alpha-1||\sin(x)|^{\alpha-1}.

Then, by direct computation, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle|(\alpha-1)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle|-\alpha(\alpha-1)\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-1}\cos(x)+\alpha(\alpha-1)\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-1}\cos^{2}(x)-\alpha\sin(x)\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha}+\sin(x)\bar{\omega}_{xx}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\alpha(\alpha-1)\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-1}\big(\cos(x)-\cos^{2}(x)\big)|+\alpha\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha+1}+\sin(x)|\bar{\omega}_{xx}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-1}|x|^{2}+\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big(\sin(x)\big)^{\alpha-1}|x|^{2},

which leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle|(\alpha-1)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}|\lesssim|\alpha-1|^{1/2}|x|.

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\sin(x)\partial_{x}\big((\alpha-1)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}\big)|\lesssim\min\{|\alpha-1|,|x|^{2}\}|\sin(x)|^{\alpha-1}.

The proof of the Lemma
2.5
is finished.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\partial_{x}\xi}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\sin((k+\frac{1}{2})x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

for
\displaystyle k\geq 1
. The above equality holds for
\displaystyle k=0
as well, due to the fact that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}\xi\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Since
\displaystyle\{\sin((k+\frac{1}{2})x),k\geq 0\}
is a complete basis for odd functions in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, it follows that
\displaystyle\partial_{x}\xi=0
, which implies
\displaystyle\xi=0
.
∎

3  Blow-up for the Inviscid Generalized Proudman-Johnson Equation

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To establish singularity formation to
(1.2)
with
\displaystyle\nu=0
, we make use of the dynamical rescaling formulation, which connects a self-similar singularity to the steady state of the rescaled system.

Let
\displaystyle(\omega(x,t),u(x,t))
denote the solution to
(1.2)
. It is straightforward to verify that

((3.1))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(x,\tau)=C_{\omega}(\tau)\omega(x,t(\tau)),\quad\tilde{u}(x,\tau)=C_{\omega}(\tau)u(x,t(\tau))

are the solutions to the dynamic rescaling equations

((3.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{\tau}(x,\tau)+\tilde{u}\tilde{\omega}_{x}(x,\tau)=c_{\omega}(\tau)\tilde{\omega}+a\tilde{u}_{x}\tilde{\omega},\quad\tilde{u}_{xx}=\tilde{\omega},\quad\tau>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},

where

((3.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{\omega}(\tau)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}c_{\omega}(s)ds\right),\quad t(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\omega}(s)ds.

Here, we do not rescale the spatial variable
\displaystyle x,
as we are interested in a blow-up solution that is neither focusing nor expanding over a fixed spatial period. Consequently, the scaling factors for
\displaystyle\omega,u
are the same.

If
\displaystyle c_{\omega}(\tau)\leq-c<0
for some constant
\displaystyle c>0
and for all
\displaystyle\tau>0
, the solution
\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}
is nontrivial, for instance
\displaystyle\|\tilde{\omega}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq C>0
for all
\displaystyle\tau>0
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{\omega}(\tau)\leq e^{-c\tau},\quad t(\infty)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-c\tau}d\tau=c^{-1}<+\infty

and that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\omega(x,t(\tau))|=C_{\omega}(\tau)^{-1}|\tilde{\omega}(x,\tau)|,

blows up at finite time
\displaystyle T=t(\infty)
.

On the other hand, if
\displaystyle c_{\omega}(\tau)\geq c>0
for some constant
\displaystyle c>0
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(x,\tau)
remains bounded, for example,
\displaystyle\|\tilde{\omega}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
for some constant
\displaystyle C>0
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{\omega}(\tau)\geq e^{c\tau},\quad t(\tau)\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{cs}ds.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\omega(x,t(\tau))|=C_{\omega}(\tau)^{-1}|\tilde{\omega}(x,\tau)|\leq e^{-c\tau}|\tilde{\omega}(x,\tau)|\leq e^{-c\tau}C,

decays for large
\displaystyle\tau
. Since
\displaystyle t(\tau)\rightarrow\infty
as
\displaystyle\tau\rightarrow\infty
, the Beale-Kato-Majda criterion implies global existence of the solution to
(1.2)
.

If
\displaystyle(\tilde{\omega},c_{\omega}(\tau))\to(\omega_{\infty},c_{\omega,\infty})
as
\displaystyle\tau\to\infty
, where
\displaystyle(\omega_{\infty},c_{\omega,\infty})
is a steady state of the dynamically rescaled equation
(3.2)
, then one may verify that

((3.4))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\omega(x,t)=\frac{1}{1+c_{\omega,\infty}t}\omega_{\infty}(x),

is a self-similar solution to the original equation
(1.2)
. In view of this correspondence, we do not distinguish the steady state of the dynamic rescaling equation (
(3.2)
) from the self-similar profile of the original equation (
(1.2)
).

Now we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Step 1. Perturbation Equations.

In this step, we employ the steady state corresponding to the case
\displaystyle a=1
to construct an approximate steady state for (
(3.2)
), which is

((3.5))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\omega}(x)=-\sin(x),\quad\bar{u}(x)=\sin(x),\quad\bar{c}_{\omega}(\tau)=(1-a)\bar{u}_{x}(0)=1-a.

We consider odd perturbations of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(x,\tau)=\bar{\omega}(x)+\hat{\omega}(x,\tau),\,\tilde{u}(x,\tau)=\bar{u}(x)+\hat{u}(x,\tau),\,c_{\omega}(\tau)=\bar{c}_{\omega}(\tau)+\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau),\quad\tau>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T}

and impose the following normalization condition on
\displaystyle\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau)
in (
(3.2)
).

((3.6))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau)=(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau).

This normalization ensures the conservation of the quantity
\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{x}(0)+\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)
over time. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}(\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,t)+\bar{\omega}_{x}(0))=

\displaystyle\displaystyle a\big(\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)+\bar{u}_{x}(0)\big)\big(\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)+\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)\big)-\big(\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)+\bar{u}_{x}(0)\big)\big(\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)+\bar{\omega}_{x}(0)\big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\big(\hat{c}_{\omega}+\bar{c}_{\omega}\big)\big(\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)+\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)\big)=0.

Then, the perturbation equation takes the following form

((3.7))

\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{\tau}=

\displaystyle-\bar{u}\hat{\omega}_{x}+a\bar{u}_{x}\hat{\omega}+a\bar{\omega}\hat{u}_{x}-\bar{\omega}_{x}\hat{u}+\bar{c}_{\omega}\hat{\omega}+\bar{\omega}\hat{c}_{\omega}+N(\hat{\omega})+F(\bar{\omega})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}\hat{\omega}+N(\hat{\omega})+F(\bar{\omega}),

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}
denotes the linearized operator,
\displaystyle N(\hat{\omega})
and
\displaystyle F(\bar{\omega})
represent the nonlinear and error terms, respectively, which are

((3.8))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} N(\hat{\omega})&=a\hat{u}_{x}\hat{\omega}-\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{x}+\hat{c}_{\omega}\hat{\omega},\\
F(\bar{\omega})&=(a\bar{u}_{x}+\bar{c}_{\omega})\bar{\omega}-\bar{u}\bar{\omega}_{x}=(1-a)\sin(x)\big(\cos(x)-1\big).\end{aligned}

Plugging the approximate steady state
(3.5)
and the normalization condition into
(3.7)
yields

((3.9))

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega}=

\displaystyle-\sin(x)\hat{\omega}_{x}+\cos(x)\hat{\omega}-\sin(x)\hat{u}_{x}+\cos(x)\hat{u},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}\hat{\omega}=

\displaystyle-\sin(x)\hat{\omega}_{x}+(a\cos(x)+1-a)\hat{\omega}

\displaystyle-(a\hat{u}_{x}+(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau))\sin(x)+\cos(x)\hat{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega}+(1-a)\big(-\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\sin(x)+\hat{\omega}-\cos(x)\hat{\omega}+\sin(x)\hat{u}_{x}\big)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega}+(1-a)\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega}.

To study the stability of the dynamically rescaled equation and its convergence to a steady state, we perform a weighted
\displaystyle\dot{H}^{1}
estimate using a singular weight
\displaystyle\rho
, with the corresponding weighted norm defined by

((3.10))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{4\pi\sin^{2}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)},\quad\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\left\langle f_{x}^{2},\rho\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{x}^{2}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x.

For initial perturbation satisfying
\displaystyle\hat{u}_{x}(0,0)=0
, it follows that
\displaystyle u_{x}(0,\tau)=0
for all
\displaystyle\tau>0
, ensuring that
\displaystyle\|\hat{u}\|_{\mathcal{H}}
is well-defined.

Step 2. Nonlinear stability analysis.

The dominant part
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega}
of the linearized operator provides a damping effect. We state a lemma that will be used subsequently, of which proof is given in the Appendix.

Lemma 3.1
.

It holds that

((3.11))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

For the term
\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
, invoking Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we obtain

((3.12))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\langle\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}&=\left\langle\sin(x)\hat{u}_{xx},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle\cos(x)(\hat{u}_{x}-\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)),\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle\sin(x)\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle\\
&\quad+\left\langle(1-\cos(x)),\hat{\omega}_{x}^{2}\rho\right\rangle\\
&=2\left\langle\sin(x)\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle\cos(x)(\hat{u}_{x}-\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)),\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle(1-\cos(x)),\hat{\omega}_{x}^{2}\rho\right\rangle\\
&\lesssim\left(\|\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(\hat{u}_{x}-\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau))\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\right)\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.\end{aligned}

Hence,

((3.13))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\langle\mathcal{L}_{a}\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}&=\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+(1-a)\left\langle\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&\leq-(\frac{1}{2}-C|1-a|)\|\hat{\omega}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.\end{aligned}

Applying
(3.13)
, we obtain

((3.14))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\|\hat{\omega}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq-(\frac{1}{2}-C|1-a|)\|\hat{\omega}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\left\langle N(\hat{\omega}),\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\langle F(\bar{\omega}),\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega},N(\hat{\omega})
and
\displaystyle F(\bar{\omega})
are defined as in
(3.8)
and
(3.9)
respectively.
We now proceed with the stability analysis.
For the nonlinear term, we apply Lemma
2.3
to obtain

((3.15))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\langle N(\hat{\omega}),\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}&=a\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{2},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle+(a-1)\left\langle\hat{u}_{x},\hat{\omega}_{x}^{2}\rho\right\rangle+(1-a)\left\langle\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau),\hat{\omega}_{x}^{2}\rho\right\rangle-\left\langle\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{xx},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle\\
&\lesssim\left(\|\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\hat{u}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-\left\langle\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{xx},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle\\
&\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{3}.\end{aligned}

For the last term, we have employed integration by parts together with Lemma
2.3
to derive

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{xx},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\hat{u}_{x},\hat{\omega}_{x}^{2}\rho\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\big\langle\frac{\cos(\frac{x}{2})\hat{u}}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})},(\hat{\omega}_{x})^{2}\rho\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\hat{u}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\bigg\|\frac{\hat{u}}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{3}.

For the error term, we deduce from Lemma
2.3
that

((3.16))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\langle F(\bar{\omega}),\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}&=(1-a)\left(\left\langle\cos(x)(\cos(x)-1),\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle-\left\langle\sin^{2}(x),\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle\right)\\
&\lesssim|1-a|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}.\end{aligned}

Step 3. Finite time blow-up analysis.

Combining the above estimates
(3.12)
,
(3.15)
and
(3.16)
, we arrive at

((3.17))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\|\hat{\omega}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq-(\frac{1}{2}-C|1-a|)\|\hat{\omega}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}+C\|\hat{\omega}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C|1-a|.

By employing a standard bootstrap argument, one can show the existence of absolute constants
\displaystyle\delta,C>0
such that if
\displaystyle|1-a|<\delta
and
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq|1-a|
, then we have
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C|1-a|
for all time. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\hat{u}_{x}(0)|\leq\|\hat{u}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C|1-a|.

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{\omega}(\tau)=\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau)+\bar{c}_{\omega}=(1-a)\left(\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)+\bar{u}_{x}(0)\right)=(1-a)(1+\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau))<0

for
\displaystyle a\in(1,1+\delta)
.

Consequently, the solution to
(1.2)
blows up in finite time for
\displaystyle a\in(1,1+\delta)
, completing the proof of part (1) of Theorem
1.1
.

Step 4. Convergence to self-similar profiles.

We now turn to the proof of part (2) of Theorem
1.1
. To establish the convergence of the solution to a steady state, we need to estimate weighted norms of
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{\tau}
. As pointed out in
[
5
]
, obtaining stability estimates in higher-order Sobolev norms is crucial for closing the estimate. We utilize the weighted derivative
\displaystyle D_{x}=\sin(x)\partial_{x}
, which has been similarly employed in
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
3
]
for stability analysis.

In order to get the estimate for
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, we first establish the estimate for
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}
. Recalling the definition of
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}
in
(3.9)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\sin(x)\hat{\omega}_{x}+(a\cos(x)+1-a)\hat{\omega}-\left(a\hat{u}_{x}+(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\right)\sin(x)+\cos xu

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(-D_{x}\hat{\omega}+\hat{\omega}\cos(x))+(1-a)(1-\cos(x))\hat{\omega}+a(-\sin(x)\hat{u}_{x}+\cos(x)\hat{u})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+(1-a)\left(\hat{u}\cos(x)-\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\sin(x)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2}+\mathcal{T}_{3}+\mathcal{T}_{4}.

The weighted derivative
\displaystyle D_{x}
satisfies the Leibniz rule

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{x}(fg)=gD_{x}f+fD_{x}g.

Then, direct calculations give

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{T}_{1}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{x}(-\sin(x)\partial_{x}D_{x}\omega+\cos(x)D_{x}\omega+\omega D_{x}\cos(x))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\sin(x)\partial_{x}\partial_{x}D_{x}\omega-\cos(x)\partial_{x}D_{x}\omega-\sin(x)D_{x}\omega+\cos(x)\partial_{x}D_{x}\omega

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\partial_{x}\omega D_{x}\cos(x)+\omega\partial_{x}D_{x}\cos(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\sin(x)\partial_{x}\partial_{x}D_{x}\omega-\sin^{2}(x)\partial_{x}\omega-\sin^{2}(x)\partial_{x}\omega-2\sin(x)\cos(x)\omega,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{T}_{2}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1-a)\partial_{x}\left((1-\cos(x))D_{x}\hat{\omega}+\hat{\omega}D_{x}(1-\cos(x))\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1-a)\partial_{x}\left((1-\cos(x))D_{x}\hat{\omega}+\hat{\omega}\sin^{2}(x)\right),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{T}_{3}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle a\partial_{x}(-\sin(x)D_{x}\hat{u}_{x}-\hat{u}_{x}D_{x}\sin(x)+\cos(x)D_{x}\hat{u}+\hat{u}D_{x}\cos(x))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle a\partial_{x}(-\sin^{2}(x)u_{xx}-\sin(x)\cos(x)\hat{u}_{x}+\sin(x)\cos(x)\hat{u}_{x}-\sin^{2}(x)\hat{u})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle a\partial_{x}(-\sin^{2}(x)\hat{\omega}-\sin^{2}(x)\hat{u})

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{T}_{4}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1-a)\partial_{x}\left(\sin(x)\cos(x)\hat{u}_{x}-\sin^{2}(x)\hat{u}-\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\sin(x)\cos(x)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1-a)\partial_{x}\left(\sin(x)\cos(x)(\hat{u}_{x}-\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau))-\sin^{2}(x)\hat{u}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1-a)(\hat{u}_{x}-\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau))\partial_{x}(\sin(x)\cos(x))+(1-a)\sin(x)\cos(x)\hat{\omega}-(1-a)\partial_{x}(\sin^{2}(x)\hat{u}).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{L}_{a},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{T}_{1},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{T}_{2},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{T}_{3},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{T}_{4},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle-\sin(x)\partial_{x}\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}+(1-a)(1-\cos(x))\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}+\mathrm{l.o.t.},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle(-\frac{1}{2}+|1-a|)\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle(-\frac{1}{4}+|1-a|)\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

The nonlinear term is estimated as follows.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{x}D_{x}N(\hat{\omega})=

\displaystyle\displaystyle a\partial_{x}(\sin(x)\hat{u}_{xx}\hat{\omega}+\sin(x)\hat{u}_{x}\hat{\omega}_{x})-\partial_{x}(\sin(x)\hat{u}_{x}\hat{\omega}_{x}+\sin(x)\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{xx})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle a\partial_{x}(\sin(x)(\hat{\omega})^{2})+(a-1)\partial_{x}(\sin(x)\hat{u}_{x}\hat{\omega}_{x})-\partial_{x}(\sin(x)\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{xx})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}+N_{4}.

Using Höler’s inequality, Lemma
2.3
and Poincaré’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle N_{1},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\cos(x)\omega^{2},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle 2\sin(x)\hat{\omega}\hat{\omega}_{x},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\hat{\omega}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{3}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle N_{2},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{u}_{xx}D_{x}\hat{\omega},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle\hat{u}_{x}\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\hat{u}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{3}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle N_{4},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{u}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{3}.

Next we estimate
\displaystyle\left\langle N_{3},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle
. Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{3}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\partial_{x}(\sin(x)\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{xx})=-\partial_{x}(\hat{u}D_{x}\hat{\omega}_{x})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\partial_{x}(\hat{u}\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}-\hat{u}\cos(x)\hat{\omega}_{x}).

Applying integration by parts and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\left\langle\hat{u}\partial_{x}\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{u}_{x},(\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega})^{2}\rho\right\rangle-\big\langle\frac{\hat{u}\cos(\frac{x}{2})}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})},(\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega})^{2}\rho\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\big(\|\hat{u}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\bigg\|\frac{\hat{u}}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{3}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle N_{3},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\left\langle\hat{u}\partial_{x}\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{x}\hat{u}\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\left\langle\partial_{x}(\hat{u}\cos(x)\hat{\omega}_{x}),\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{3}.

Combining the estimates for
\displaystyle N_{i}(i=1,2,3,4)
, we obtain

((3.18))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}N(\hat{\omega}),\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle\leq C\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{3}.

For the estimate of error term, since

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(\bar{\omega})=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(a\bar{u}_{x}+\bar{c}_{\omega})\bar{\omega}-\bar{u}\bar{\omega}_{x}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-a\cos(x)\sin(x)-(1-a)\sin(x)+\sin(x)\cos(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-(1-a)\sin(x)(1-\cos(x))=-2(1-a)\sin(x)\sin^{2}(\frac{x}{2}),

it follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}F(\bar{\omega}),\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle\lesssim|1-a|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}.

Define

\displaystyle\displaystyle E(\tau)^{2}=\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mu\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

for some absolute constant
\displaystyle\mu>1
. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}E(\tau)^{2}\leq-(\frac{1}{8}-C|1-a|)E(\tau)^{2}+C\left(\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{3}+\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{3}\right)+C|1-a|\left(\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\right).

Noting that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|2\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}|=\bigg|\frac{1}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}(\cos(x)\hat{\omega}_{x}+\sin(x)\hat{\omega}_{xx})\bigg|,

we obtain the equivalence of norms

((3.19))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{2}\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{2}.

Then,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{2}\lesssim E(\tau)^{2}\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{2},

which implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}E(\tau)^{2}\leq-(\frac{1}{8}-C|1-a|)E(\tau)^{2}+CE(\tau)^{3}+C|1-a|E(\tau).

Hence, there exist a sufficiently small constant
\displaystyle\delta_{1}<\delta
and a constant
\displaystyle C>0
such that, if
\displaystyle|1-a|<\delta_{1}
and
\displaystyle E(0)<\delta_{1}
, then
\displaystyle E(\tau)\leq C\delta_{1}
for all
\displaystyle t\geq 0
. This can be established via a standard bootstrap argument. In view of
(3.19)
, we obtain

((3.20))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq CE(\tau)\leq C\delta_{1}.

We next show that
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}
decays exponentially as
\displaystyle\tau\to+\infty
. Direct estimate gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{\tau},\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\langle(\mathcal{L}_{a}\hat{\omega})_{\tau},\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\langle(N(\hat{\omega}))_{\tau},\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

We obtain a damping effect arising from the linear terms, while the small error terms associated with
\displaystyle\bar{\omega}
and
\displaystyle\bar{u}
vanish.As a result, we derive

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq-(\frac{1}{2}-|1-a|)\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\left\langle(N(\hat{\omega}))_{\tau},\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Using similar estimates as in
Step 2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\left\langle(N(\hat{\omega}))_{\tau},\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\lesssim E(\tau)\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+|\left\langle\hat{u}_{\tau}\hat{\omega}_{xx},\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}\rho\right\rangle|+|\left\langle\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{\tau,xx},\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}\rho\right\rangle|.

For the term
\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{u}_{\tau}\hat{\omega}_{xx},\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}\rho\right\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{u}_{\tau}\hat{\omega}_{xx},\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}\rho\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{u}_{\tau}\rho^{1/2}\hat{\omega}_{xx},\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}\rho^{1/2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\big\langle\frac{\hat{u}_{\tau}}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\hat{\omega}_{xx},\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\big\langle\frac{2\hat{u}_{\tau}}{\sin(x)}\hat{\omega}_{xx}\cos(\frac{x}{2}),\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{\hat{u}_{\tau}}{\sin(x)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}E(\tau)\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle E(\tau)\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

To estimate the term
\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{\hat{u}_{t}}{\sin(x)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we exploit the oddness and periodicity of
\displaystyle\hat{u}_{\tau}
in the spatial variable
\displaystyle x
. In particular, since
\displaystyle\hat{u}(\pi,\tau)=\hat{u}(0,\tau)=0
, it suffices to estimate the norm on
\displaystyle[0,\pi]
. Observing that
\displaystyle\sin(x)\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\min\{x,\pi-x\}
for
\displaystyle x\in[0,\pi]
, we apply the Lagrange mean value theorem to deduce we obtain
\displaystyle\big\|\frac{\hat{u}_{\tau}}{\sin(x)}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\hat{u}_{\tau,x}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. For the term
\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{\tau,xx},\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}\rho\right\rangle
, using integration by parts and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{\tau,xx},\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\hat{u}_{x}\rho,\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}^{2}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\big\langle\frac{\hat{u}\cos(\frac{x}{2})}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\rho,\hat{\omega}_{\tau,x}^{2}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{u}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\bigg\|\frac{\hat{u}}{\sin(\frac{x}{2})}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle E(\tau)\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq-(\frac{1}{2}-|1-a|)\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+CE(\tau)\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq-(\frac{1}{2}-C|1-a|)\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Finally, combining the prior estimates on
\displaystyle E(\tau)
, we deduce that
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}
decays exponentially as
\displaystyle\tau\to+\infty
. Consequently,
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(\tau)+\bar{\omega}
converges strongly in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
to a limiting profile
\displaystyle\omega_{a}\in\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, since
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq E(\tau)\leq C\delta_{0}
, we obtain that
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(\tau)+\bar{\omega}
converges weakly in
\displaystyle\mathcal{W}
to
\displaystyle\omega_{a}
as well. Based on the convergence of
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(\tau)+\bar{\omega}
, we can show that
\displaystyle\hat{c}_{\omega}+\bar{c}_{\omega}
converges exponentially to some scalar
\displaystyle c_{\omega,a}
as
\displaystyle\tau\rightarrow+\infty
. In view of the odd symmetry of
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}
, which is preserved in time, it follows that the full profile
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(\tau)+\bar{\omega}
remains odd for all
\displaystyle\tau>0
, and hence the limit
\displaystyle\omega_{a}
inherits the oddness.

Furthermore, by Poincaré’s inequality and the uniform bound on
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}

, we deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}},

which yields uniform convergence in
\displaystyle L^{\infty}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(\tau)+\bar{\omega}\to\omega_{a},\quad\text{uniformly in $\displaystyle x$.}

We thus conclude that
\displaystyle(\omega_{a},c_{\omega,a})
solves the steady-state equation associated with
(3.2)
, and that
\displaystyle\omega_{a}
corresponds to a self-similar profile of
(1.2)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is finished.

∎

=&\sin(x)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\partial_{x}\left((\alpha-1)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}\right)+\sin(x)\partial_{x}\left((\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\sin(x)-\hat{u})\omega_{res,xx}\right)\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad-\cos(x)\omega_{res,xx}\hat{u}+\hat{c}_{\omega,\alpha}\cos(x)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}

((4.12))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\cos(x)\left(-\omega_{res,xx}\hat{u}+(\alpha-1)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\cos(x)\left(\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\left((\alpha-1)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}\right)+\omega_{res,xx}\left(\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\sin(x)-\hat{u}\right)\right).

Applying Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.1
, it follows that

((4.13))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\left(-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xxx}\hat{u}-\sin(x)\omega_{res,xx}\hat{u}_{x}+\hat{c}_{\omega,\alpha}\sin(x)\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,xx}\right)\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&|\alpha-1|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\left(\sin(x)\partial_{x}\left((\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\sin(x)-\hat{u})\omega_{res,xx}\right)\right)\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&|\alpha-1|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+|\alpha-1|\bigg\||\sin(x)|^{\kappa-1}\frac{\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\sin(x)-\hat{u}}{|\sin(x)|}\rho^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+|\alpha-1|\||\sin(x)|^{\kappa-1}\left(\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)-\hat{u}_{x}\right)\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&|\alpha-1|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+|\alpha-1|\bigg\|\frac{\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)\sin(x)-\hat{u}}{|\sin(x)|}\rho^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}+|\alpha-1|\bigg\|\frac{\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)-\hat{u}_{x}}{x}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
\lesssim&|\alpha-1|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}.\end{aligned}

Combining with
(4.10)
-
(4.13)
yields

((4.14))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle\lesssim|\alpha-1|(\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}})\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\lesssim|\alpha-1|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{2}.

Moreover, for
\displaystyle x\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,xxx}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)(\sin(x))^{\alpha-3}\cos^{3}(x)+2\alpha(\alpha-1)(\sin(x))^{\alpha-1}\cos(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}(\sin(x))^{\alpha-1}.

Similar to the estimate
4
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}D_{x}F_{\alpha}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\left((\bar{\omega}_{\alpha})^{2}+\bar{c}_{\omega,\alpha}\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}-\bar{u}_{\alpha}\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,xx}\right)\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\|\sin(x)\left(2\bar{\omega}_{\alpha}\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,x}+\bar{c}_{\omega,\alpha}\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,xx}-\bar{u}_{\alpha,x}\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,xx}-\bar{u}_{\alpha}\bar{\omega}_{\alpha,xxx}\right)\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\alpha-1|,

which implies

((4.15))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}F_{\alpha},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle\lesssim|\alpha-1|\left\langle\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega},\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho\right\rangle^{1/2}\lesssim|\alpha-1|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{W}}.

Combining
(4.8)
,
(4.9)
,
(4.14)
and
(4.15)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}E(\tau)\leq-(\frac{1}{8}-C|\alpha-1|)E(\tau)+CE^{2}(\tau)+C|\alpha-1|,

where
\displaystyle E(\tau)^{2}=\|\partial_{x}D_{x}\hat{\omega}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mu\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
. Using the bootstrap argument on the energy
\displaystyle E(\tau)
, we obtain there exist a sufficiently small constant
\displaystyle\delta_{2}>0
and a constant
\displaystyle C>0
such that, if
\displaystyle|1-a|<\delta_{2}
and
\displaystyle E(0)<C\delta_{2}
, then
\displaystyle E(\tau)\leq C\delta_{2}
for all
\displaystyle\tau\geq 0
. Following the same strategy as
Step 4
in Section
3
, we can get the analogous estimate of
\displaystyle\left\langle\omega_{\tau},\omega_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
and establish exponential convergence of
\displaystyle\left\langle\omega_{\tau},\omega_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
to zero. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is then completed.
∎

5  Blow-up for the Viscous Model

In this section, we establish the finite time blow-up for the viscous model, which can be written as

((5.1))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{t}+u\omega_{x}=a\omega u_{x}+\nu\omega_{xx},&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},\\
u_{xx}=\omega,&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

with
\displaystyle a>1
and fixed
\displaystyle\nu>0
.

We introduce the rescaled variables

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(x,\tau)=C_{\omega}(\tau)\omega(x,t(\tau)),\quad\tilde{u}=C_{\omega}(\tau)u(x,t(\tau)),

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{\omega}(\tau)=C_{\omega}(0)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}c_{\omega}(s)ds\right),\quad t(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\omega}(s)ds,

where
\displaystyle C_{\omega}(0)>0
. Compared with the inviscid case, the positive constant
\displaystyle C_{\omega}(0)
provides an additional degree of freedom. This flexibility allows us to choose
\displaystyle C_{\omega}(0)
in such a way that the viscous term
\displaystyle\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\tilde{\omega}_{xx}
remains small relative to the dominant terms in the rescaled system (
(5.2)
). We will choose
\displaystyle C_{\omega}(0)=|1-a|^{2}
in our proof.

In terms of these variables,
(5.1)
becomes

((5.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{\omega}_{\tau}+\tilde{u}\tilde{\omega}_{x}=a\tilde{u}_{x}\tilde{\omega}+c_{\omega}\tilde{\omega}+\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\tilde{\omega}_{xx},&\tau>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},\\
\tilde{u}_{xx}=\tilde{\omega},&\tau>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T}.\end{cases}

To prove Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to establish the dynamical stability of
(5.2)
under the scaling parameter
\displaystyle c_{\omega}<-c<0
for all time, as discussed in
[
13
,
5
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Step 1. Perturbation equations.

We now consider the approximate steady state

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\omega}=-\sin x,\quad\bar{u}=\sin x,\quad\bar{c}_{\omega}=(1-a)\bar{u}_{x}(0)-\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\frac{\bar{\omega}_{xxx}(0)}{\bar{\omega}_{x}(0)}=(1-a)+\nu C_{\omega}(\tau).

Let

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(x,\tau)=\bar{\omega}+\hat{\omega}(x,\tau),\,\tilde{u}(x,\tau)=\bar{u}+\hat{u}(x,\tau),\,c_{\omega}(\tau)=\bar{c}_{\omega}+\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau),\quad\tau>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},

where
\displaystyle\hat{\omega},\hat{u}
and
\displaystyle\hat{c}_{\omega}
are odd perturbations. The odd symmetry of the solution is preserved in time by
(5.2)
. We also introduce the following normalization condition

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau)=(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)-\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\frac{\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)}{\bar{\omega}_{x}(0)}=(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)+\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau).

This normalization ensures that
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)
remains zero, provided that the initial perturbation satisfies
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,0)=0.
Indeed, if
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)=0,
it then follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left(\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)+\bar{\omega}_{x}(0)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(a-1)\left(\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)+\bar{u}_{x}(0)\right)\left(\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)+\bar{\omega}_{x}(0)\right)+\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\left(\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)+\bar{\omega}_{xxx}(0)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\left(\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau)+\bar{c}_{\omega}\right)\left(\hat{\omega}_{x}(0,\tau)+\bar{\omega}_{x}(0)\right)=0.

The perturbation equations can then be written as

((5.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}\hat{\omega}_{\tau}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega}+(1-a)\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega}+N(\hat{\omega})+F(\bar{\omega})+\nu C_{\omega}(t)V,&\tau>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},\\
\hat{u}_{xx}=\hat{\omega},&\tau>0,\ x\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

where the terms
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega},\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega},N(\hat{\omega}),F(\bar{\omega})
are defined exactly as in the inviscid case, and the term

\displaystyle\displaystyle V=\hat{\omega}_{xx}+\left(1+\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)\right)\hat{\omega}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)\sin(x),

corresponds to the effect of viscosity.

Step 2. Stability analysis of the rescaled perturbation equation.

Analogous to the estimates in the inviscid case, we obtain

((5.4))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq-(\frac{1}{2}-C|1-a|)\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{3}+C|1-a|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\left\langle V_{x},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle.

To handle the viscous term, we write

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle V_{x},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{xxx}+\left(1+\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)\right)\hat{\omega}_{x}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)\cos(x),\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{xxx}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau),\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle+\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)\left\langle 1-\cos(x),\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle+\left(1+\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)\right)\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{x},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle.

Notice that, when
\displaystyle x\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\rho_{x}|=\bigg|\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{-\sin(x)}{(1-\cos(x))^{2}}\bigg|=\bigg|\rho\frac{-\sin(x)}{1-\cos(x)}\bigg|\lesssim\rho|\frac{1}{x}|

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\rho_{xx}|=\bigg|\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{2\sin^{2}(x)-\cos(x)(1-\cos(x))}{(1-\cos(x))^{3}}\bigg|=\rho\bigg|\frac{2\sin^{2}(x)}{(1-\cos(x))^{2}}-\frac{\cos(x)}{1-\cos(x)}\bigg|\lesssim\rho\frac{1}{x^{2}}.

Use integration by parts twice to yield

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{xxx}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau),\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{xx}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)x,\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho\right\rangle-\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{xx}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)x,\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho_{x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{xx}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)x,\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho\right\rangle-\big\langle\hat{\omega}_{xx}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)x,\frac{x^{2}}{2}\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)\rho_{x}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\big\langle\hat{\omega}_{xx}-\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)x,(\hat{\omega}_{x}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau))\rho_{x}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\|\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)|\|\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}+|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big\langle((\hat{\omega}_{x}-\frac{x^{2}}{2})^{2})_{x},\rho_{x}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\|\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C\left(|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)|\|\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}+|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)|^{2}+\|(\frac{\hat{\omega}_{x}}{x}-\frac{x}{2}\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau))\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)|^{2}+\|\frac{\hat{\omega}_{x}}{x}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Thus, we obtain

((5.5))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle V_{x},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)|^{2}+\|\frac{\hat{\omega}_{x}}{x}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(1+|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)|\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right).

The essential difficulty of the viscous terms is that after integration by parts, the singular weight produces various trouble terms. Fortunately, these terms contribute only to higher-order terms near the origin. Denoting the interval
\displaystyle I=[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, and using
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{x}(0)=\hat{\omega}_{xx}(0)=0
, we obtain

((5.6))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\frac{\hat{\omega}_{x}}{x}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=&\int_{I}\frac{\hat{\omega}_{x}^{2}}{x^{2}}\rho\mathrm{d}x+\int_{[-\pi,\pi]\setminus I}\frac{\hat{\omega}_{x}^{2}}{x^{2}}\rho\mathrm{d}x\\
\lesssim&\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{2}+\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.\end{aligned}

Combing
(5.5)
with
(5.6)
, we obtain

((5.7))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle V_{x},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\omega}_{xx}\rho^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{2}+(1+\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)})\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right).

Step 3. Higher-order estimates of
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}
.

In the above analysis, we need an appropriate higher-order norm to close the estimates. Specifically, we aim to extract damping from the leading-order linear term and bound the term
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{xxx}
by interpolating between lower-order and higher-order norms, using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. To this end, we define the
\displaystyle k
-th order weighted norms for
\displaystyle k\geq 1
as

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{k}^{2}(\tau):=\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle,\quad\rho_{k}:=(1+\cos(x))^{k},

where
\displaystyle\omega^{(k)}:=\partial_{x}^{k}\omega
. For clarity, we define
\displaystyle\rho_{0}=\rho
and
\displaystyle E_{0}^{2}(\tau)=\left\langle\hat{\omega}_{x},\hat{\omega}_{x}\rho\right\rangle
. Similar weighted higher-order norms have been also used in
[
13
]
.

Now, we begin with estimating
\displaystyle E_{k}^{2}(\tau)
for
\displaystyle k>0
as follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}E_{k}^{2}(\tau)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle(\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega})^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle+(1-a)\left\langle(\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega})^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle(N(\hat{\omega}))^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\left\langle(F(\bar{\omega}))^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle+\nu C_{\omega}(t)\left\langle V^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle.

We proceed by analyzing the linear terms and extracting the damping. For simplification, we denote the terms as lower order terms (l.o.t for short) if their
\displaystyle\rho_{k}
-weighted
\displaystyle L^{2}
-norms are bounded by
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}E_{i}(\tau)
. Since
\displaystyle\rho_{k}\leq C(k)\rho_{i}
for
\displaystyle i<k
, using the l.o.t notation, we focus solely on the higher-order terms.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle(\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega})^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle=\left\langle-\sin(x)\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)}-k\cos(x)\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}+\mathrm{l.o.t.},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle.

Using integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle-\sin(x)\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)}-k\cos(x)\omega^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle\cos(x)\rho_{k}-k\rho_{k-1}\sin^{2}(x),\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)})^{2}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle-k\left\langle\cos(x)\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle(\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)})^{2},\frac{1}{2}\cos(x)\rho_{k}-\frac{1}{2}k\rho_{k-1}\sin^{2}(x)-k\cos(x)\rho_{k}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle(\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)})^{2},\rho_{k}(-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{(k-1)}{2}\cos(x))\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle.

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle(\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega})^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq-E_{k}^{2}(\tau)+C(k)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}E_{i}(\tau)E_{k}(\tau)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}E_{k}^{2}(\tau)+C(k)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}E_{i}^{2}(\tau).

Similar estimates give

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle(\mathcal{A}\hat{\omega})^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle\leq E_{k}^{2}(\tau)+C(k)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}E_{i}^{2}(\tau)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle(F(\bar{\omega}))^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle\leq|1-a|C(k)E_{k}(\tau).

Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle(N(\hat{\omega}))^{(k+1)}=-(\hat{u}\hat{\omega}_{x})^{(k+1)}+a(\hat{u}_{x}\hat{\omega})^{(k+1)}+(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0)\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}.

For the term
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)}\hat{u}
, we can apply integration by parts and Poincaré’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)}\hat{u},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\hat{u}_{x}\rho_{k}-k\hat{u}\rho_{k-1}\sin(x),(\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)})^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{u}_{x}\rho_{k},(\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)})^{2}\right\rangle-k\big\langle\frac{\sin^{2}(x)}{1+\cos(x)}\frac{\hat{u}}{\sin(x)},(\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)})^{2}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C(k)\left(\|\hat{u}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\big\|\frac{\hat{u}}{\sin(x)}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C(k)E_{0}(\tau)E_{k}^{2}(\tau).

The terms associated with
\displaystyle\hat{u}_{x}\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}
can be handled in a similar way. Concerning the term
\displaystyle\hat{u}^{(i)}\hat{\omega}^{(k+2-i)}(2\leq i\leq k+1)
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{u}^{(i)}\hat{\omega}^{(k+2-i)}\rho_{k}^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|\hat{u}^{(i)}\hat{\omega}^{(k+2-i)}(1+\cos(x))^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|\hat{u}^{(i)}\hat{\omega}^{(k+2-i)}(1+\cos(x))^{\frac{k+1-i}{2}}(1+\cos(x))^{\frac{i-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{u}^{(i)}(1+\cos(x))^{\frac{i-1}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}E_{k+1-i}(\tau).

Since
\displaystyle\hat{u}_{xx}=\hat{\omega}
, it concludes that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\hat{u}^{(i)}(1+\cos(x))^{\frac{i-1}{2}}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\big|\int_{-\pi}^{x}\partial_{x}\left(\hat{u}^{(i)}(1+\cos(x))^{\frac{i-1}{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}x\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{u}^{(i+1)}(1+\cos(x))^{\frac{i-1}{2}}\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{i-1}{2}\|\hat{u}^{(i)}(1+\cos(x))^{\frac{i-3}{2}}\sin(x)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C(k)\sum_{i=0}^{k}E_{i}(\tau).

Consequently, the nonlinear terms can be estimated as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle(N(\hat{\omega}))^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle\leq C(k)\sum_{i=0}^{k}E_{i}^{2}(\tau)E_{k}(\tau).

Next, we focus on the viscous terms. Direct computations reveal that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle V^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+3)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle+C(k)\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}E_{k}(\tau)+(1+\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)})E_{k}^{2}(\tau).

For the term
\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+3)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle
, using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+3)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle+k\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k-1}\sin(x)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{k}{2}\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k-1}((k-1)-k\cos(x))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq-\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+2)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle+C(k)\left\langle\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k-1}\right\rangle.

Combining with the leading order estimate
(5.7)
, we conclude that, for small enough constants
\displaystyle 0<\mu(k_{0})<1
,

((5.8))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\mu^{k}\left\langle V^{(k+1)},\hat{\omega}^{(k+1)}\rho_{k}\right\rangle\leq C(k_{0})\big(\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{2}+(1+\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)})\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\mu^{k}E_{k}^{2}(\tau)\big).

Here
\displaystyle\mu(k_{0})
is a generic constant depending on
\displaystyle k_{0}
. We can choose
\displaystyle k_{0}
large enough later so that
\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}
can be bounded using interpolation inequalities.

Step 4. Finite time blow-up analysis.

For a fixed
\displaystyle k_{0}
, there exists a sufficiently small constant
\displaystyle 0<\mu_{1}(k_{0})<\mu(k_{0})
, such that the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}I_{k_{0}}^{2}(\tau)\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle-(\frac{1}{2}-C|1-a|)I_{k_{0}}^{2}(\tau)+CI_{k_{0}}^{3}(\tau)+C|1-a|I_{k_{0}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\left(\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{2}+(1+\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)})I_{k_{0}}^{2}(\tau)\right),

where the energy is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{k_{0}}^{2}(\tau)=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\mu_{1}^{k}(k_{0})E_{k}^{2}(\tau).

Here, the constants depend on
\displaystyle k_{0}
and
\displaystyle\mu,
but once
\displaystyle k_{0}
is fixed, with
\displaystyle\mu=\mu_{1}(k_{0})
, they become just constants. We will later make both
\displaystyle C_{\omega}(t)
and
\displaystyle|1-a|
sufficiently small to close the argument. By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}^{(4)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{7}{8}}\|\hat{\omega}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{1}{8}}.

Consequently, we conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\lesssim I_{k_{0}}(\tau),

for
\displaystyle k_{0}\geq 4
. For simplicity, we take
\displaystyle k_{0}=4
as an example, which implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}I_{4}(\tau)\leq-(\frac{1}{2}-C|1-a|)I_{4}(\tau)+CI_{4}^{2}(\tau)+C|1-a|+C\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)(1+I_{4}(t))I_{4}(\tau).

We now choose
\displaystyle C_{\omega}(0)=|1-a|^{2}<\delta_{3}
for a sufficiently small
\displaystyle\delta_{3}>0
and use a bootstrap argument to obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{4}(\tau)\leq C|1-a|,\quad C_{\omega}(\tau)\leq 2|1-a|^{2},

where
\displaystyle C>0
is some absolute constant.

Note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\omega}=(1-a)+\nu C_{\omega}(\tau),\quad\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau)=(1-a)\hat{u}_{x}(0,\tau)+\nu C_{\omega}(\tau)\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau).

By the Sobolev embedding, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\hat{u}_{x}(0)|\lesssim\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\lesssim I_{4}(\tau)\lesssim|1-a|

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\hat{\omega}_{xxx}(0,\tau)|\lesssim I_{4}(\tau)\lesssim|1-a|.

It concludes that there exists a small number
\displaystyle 0<\delta\leq\delta_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{\omega}(\tau)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\bar{c}_{\omega}+\hat{c}_{\omega}(\tau)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(1-a)+C[(1-a)^{2}+\nu(1+|1-a|)(1-a)^{2}]<0,

for
\displaystyle a\in(1,1+\delta)
.
It follows that blow-up occurs in finite time in the original physical variables for the viscous model
(5.1)
with
\displaystyle a\in(1,1+\delta)
, as stated in Theorem
1.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is finished.
∎

6  Appendix

In the Appendix, we provide the proof of Lemma
3.1
. To extract the maximal damping effect, we expand the perturbed solution in a Fourier series and perform exact calculations.

Lemma 6.1
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Note that

((6.1))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}e_{k}^{(o)}=A_{k}e_{k+1}^{(o)}+B_{k}e_{k-1}^{(o)},\quad k\geq 2,

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{k}^{(o)}=\sin(kx),\quad k\geq 1

and

((6.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{k}=-\frac{(k+1)(k-1)^{2}}{2k^{2}},\quad B_{k}=\frac{(k+1)^{2}(k-1)}{2k^{2}},\quad k\geq 2.

For
\displaystyle k\geq 2,
direct calculations give

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{A_{k+1}}{k+1}e_{k+2}^{(o)}+\frac{B_{k+1}}{k+1}e_{k}^{(o)}-\frac{A_{k}}{k}e_{k+1}^{(o)}-\frac{B_{k}}{k}e_{k-1}^{(o)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{(k+2)k^{2}}{2(k+1)^{3}}e_{k+2}^{(o)}+\frac{(k+2)^{2}k}{2(k+1)^{3}}e_{k}^{(o)}+\frac{(k+1)(k-1)^{2}}{2k^{3}}e_{k+1}^{(o)}-\frac{(k+1)^{2}(k-1)}{2k^{3}}e_{k-1}^{(o)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}{2(k+1)^{3}}\left(\frac{e_{k+2}^{(o)}}{k+2}-\frac{e_{k+1}^{(o)}}{k+1}\right)-\frac{(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}{2(k+1)^{3}}\frac{e_{k+1}^{(o)}}{k+1}+\frac{(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}{2(k+1)^{3}}\frac{e_{k}^{(o)}}{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\frac{(k+1)^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{2k^{3}}\left(\frac{e_{k}^{(o)}}{k}-\frac{e_{k-1}^{(o)}}{k-1}\right)-\frac{(k+1)^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{2k^{3}}\frac{e_{k}^{(o)}}{k}+\frac{(k+1)^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{2k^{3}}\frac{e_{k-1}^{(o)}}{k-1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-d_{k+1}\tilde{e}_{k+1}^{(o)}+(-d_{k+1}+d_{k})\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}+d_{k}\tilde{e}_{k-1}^{(o)}.

In addition, when
\displaystyle k=1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}e_{1}^{(o)}=0

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\tilde{e}_{1}^{(o)}=-d_{2}\tilde{e}_{2}^{(o)}+(d_{1}-d_{2})\tilde{e}_{1}^{(o)}.

Thus we can write

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}=-d_{k+1}\tilde{e}_{k+1}^{(o)}+(-d_{k+1}+d_{k})\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}+d_{k}\tilde{e}_{k-1}^{(o)},

where
\displaystyle d_{k}=\frac{(k+1)^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{2k^{3}},k=1,2,\cdots.

If we expand
\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(x,\tau)
as

\hat{\omega}(x,\tau)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\hat{\omega}^{(o)}_{k}(\tau)\,\tilde{e}^{(o)}_{k},

then the equation
\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\hat{\omega}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega}
reduces to the following infinite-dimensional system of ordinary differential equations

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}(\tau)=-d_{k}\hat{\omega}_{k-1}^{(o)}(\tau)-(d_{k+1}-d_{k})\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}(\tau)+d_{k+1}\hat{\omega}_{k+1}^{(o)}(\tau),\quad k\geq 1,

where
\displaystyle d_{1}\hat{\omega}_{0}^{(o)}
is understood to be zero. Thus, we formally deduce that

((6.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big(\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}\big)^{2}&=\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{\omega},\hat{\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=\sum_{k\geq 1}-d_{k}\hat{\omega}_{k-1}^{(o)}\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}+(-d_{k+1}+d_{k})\big(\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}\big)^{2}+d_{k+1}\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}\hat{\omega}_{k+1}^{(o)}\\
&=\sum_{k\geq 1}(-d_{k+1}+d_{k})\big(\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}\big)^{2}\\
&\leq-\frac{1}{2}\big(\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}\big)^{2}.\end{aligned}

Here, we have used the fact that for all
\displaystyle k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle d_{k+1}-d_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}{2(k+1)^{3}}-\frac{(k+1)^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{2k^{3}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{k^{6}+3k^{5}+5k^{4}+5k^{3}-k^{2}-3k-1}{2k^{3}(k+1)^{3}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2k^{4}+4k^{3}-k^{2}-3k-1}{2k^{3}(k+1)^{3}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}.

While the formal computation in
(6.3)
illustrates the dissipative structure of
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}
, it is required to justify the validity of the summation, which may not converge in a straightforward manner. One rigorous approach is to invoke standard linear semigroup theory.
To this end, we consider the real Hilbert space
\displaystyle Y
formally spanned by the basis functions
\displaystyle\{\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)},k\geq 1\}
in which this basis is orthonormal, namely

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y=\bigg\{\hat{\omega}(x,\tau)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}(\tau)\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}\bigg|\{\hat{\omega}_{k}^{(o)}\}_{k\geq 1}\in l^{2}\bigg\}.

The operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}
defines a densely defined, closed, unbounded linear operator on
\displaystyle Y
. The estimate (
(6.3)
), together with standard energy methods, implies via a direct application of the Hille–Yosida theorem that
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup satisfying

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|e^{\tau\mathcal{L}_{1}}\hat{\omega}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tau}\|\hat{\omega}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

An alternative approach is to apply Galerkin’s method to establish global well-posedness. In addition, the decay rate of the Fourier coefficients of the solution can be rigorously derived (see
[
12
]
for more details).
∎

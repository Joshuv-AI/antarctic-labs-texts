Complete plastome data of rare Allium microdictyon, an edible and medicinally important wild onion from the Kazakhstan Altay
Authors: Shyryn Almerekova, Alan Ibraimov, Moldir Yermagambetova, Saule Abugalieva, Yerlan Turuspekov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Allium microdictyon Prokh. is a perennial bulbous plant with high nutritional and medicinal value. In this study, we sequenced and assembled the complete plastome of A. microdictyon using Illumina sequencing technology. The plastome was 153,538 bp in length and exhibited a typical quadripartite structure, consisting of a large single-copy (LSC) region, a small single-copy (SSC) region, and a pair of inverted repeats (IRa and IRb). The GC content of the LSC, SSC, and IR regions was 34.9%, 29.9%, and 42.7%, respectively. A total of 137 genes were annotated, including 88 protein-coding genes, 38 tRNA genes, 8 rRNA genes, and 3 pseudogenes. Among these, 22 genes contained one intron, whereas three genes contained two introns. In addition, 237 simple sequence repeats (SSRs) were identified, with mononucleotide repeats being the most abundant. Phylogenetic analyses based on Maximum Likelihood and Bayesian Inference methods showed that the A. microdictyon plastome generated in this study clustered with A. microdictyon, A. ochotense, and A. listera accessions available in NCBI GenBank. The plastome sequence reported here may provide valuable genetic resources for future phylogenetic and population genetic studies of Allium species.

Background

Allium microdictyon Prokh. (Amaryllidaceae J.St.-Hil.) is a rare North Asian wild onion species primarily distributed in mountainous fir and spruce forests. In Kazakhstan, this species grows only in the Kazakhstan Altay (Eastern Kazakhstan), on the ridges of the Western Altay: Lineisky, Ivanovsky, Kholzun, and Ubinsky [1]. The species A. microdictyon plays an essential role in maintaining ecosystem stability and biodiversity as a native understory plant and an early-season food source for pollinators. Beyond its ecological significance, the species is highly regarded for its nutritional and medicinal properties, attributed to its high levels of vitamin C, sulfur-containing compounds, antioxidants, and antimicrobial phytoncides [2]. Despite the limited number of previous phytochemical studies on A. microdictyon, one of the earliest comprehensive investigations of its leaf flavonoid composition identified 14 flavonoid compounds, two of which were new flavonol glycosides [3]. The obtained results highlight the species as a valuable source of bioactive antioxidants with potential pharmacological and nutraceutical significance.

The species represents a valuable genetic resource for plant molecular breeding [4] because of its adaptation to cold climates and shaded habitats [1]. A. microdictyon, commonly referred to as mountain garlic, has leaves that are widely harvested and consumed in Mongolia, Korea, and China [5]. The species is widely distributed from the Altay Mountains to Korea [6,7]. The plastome of A. microdictyon from Korea, representing the eastern limit of the species’ distribution, was previously reported [8]. In this study, we sequenced A. microdictyon from the Altay Mountains, the western edge of its distribution range.

In Kazakhstan, this species is classified as “Rare” (Category II) and is listed in the Red Book of Kazakhstan [9]. In recent years, intensive harvesting of wild A. microdictyon for food and medicinal purposes has led to a decline in its natural populations in Kazakhstan [1,9]. Characterizing the plastome of A. microdictyon is essential for future research aimed at clarifying its phylogenetic position, assessing population genetic diversity, and developing effective conservation strategies.

Data Description

Allium microdictyon is a perennial bulb plant, 40–60 cm tall. The leaves are flat, oblong-elliptical, and the flowers are yellowish. Flowering from June to July. The photo of the natural habitat of the species studied is shown in Fig. 1. The plastome of A. microdictyon was sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq X platform (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA, USA), yielding approximately 3.5 GB of raw data. The genome size was 153,538 bp, with an overall GC content of 37%. The plastome consists of a large single-copy (LSC) region of 82,594 bp, a small single-copy (SSC) region of 17,860 bp, and a pair of inverted repeats (IR) regions, each of 26,542 bp, with GC contents of 34.9%, 29.9%, and 42.7%, respectively (Fig. 1).Fig. 1The plastome map of Allium microdictyon. In the plastome of A. microdictyon, a total of 137 genes were annotated, comprising 88 protein-coding genes, 38 tRNA genes, 8 rRNA genes, and 3 pseudogenes (infA and two copies of ycf15). Based on their functions, these genes were classified into four major categories: self-replication-related genes (76), photosynthesis-related genes (47), other genes (11), and pseudogenes (3). A total of 22 genes (rpl2, rpl23, rps12, rps19, rps7, ycf1, ycf15, ycf2, ycf68, ndhB, trnA-UGC, trnH-GUG, trnI-CAU, trnI-GAU, trnL-CAA, trnR-ACG, trnN-GUU, trnV-GAC, rrn16, rrn23, rrn4.5, and rrn5) were identified as duplicated. Furthermore, 15 genes (atpF, petB, petD, ndhA, ndhB, rpl16, rpl2, rps16, rpoC1, ycf68, trnA-UGC, trnG-UCC, trnI-GAU, trnK-UUU, and trnV-UAC) contained a single intron, whereas 3 genes (clpP, ycf3, and rps12) were characterized by the presence of two introns (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Classification of annotated plastome genes of Allium microdictyon. The nucleotide sequences of the A. microdictyon plastome were used to identify simple sequence repeats (SSRs). In total, 237 SSRs were identified in the studied plastome (Table 1). Mononucleotide repeats were the most common (70.46%), followed by dinucleotide (23.63%) and tetranucleotide repeats (5.06%), whereas trinucleotide repeats accounted for only 0.84%. Most mononucleotides (97.60%) consisted of A/T motifs, with only 2.40% composed of C/G. Phylogenetic analyses were inferred from whole plastome sequences using the Maximum Likelihood (ML) and Bayesian Inference (BI) frameworks. Both analyses produced highly consistent tree topologies, and the analyzed samples clustered within their corresponding subgenera. In the resulting phylogenies, A. microdictyon clustered with two previously reported A. microdictyon plastomes (OQ183596 and OP743936), together with A. ochotense (OQ183593) and A. listera (MT328402) from NCBI GenBank, forming a well-supported clade corresponding to subgenus Anguinum (Fig. 3). Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Plant samples

Leaf samples were collected from the Ivanovsky Range in the Altai Mountains of Kazakhstan and dried on silica gel. The geographic coordinates of the sampling site were given in a study published by Abugalieva et al. (2017) [11]. Total genomic DNA was extracted from dried leaf material using the cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) protocol [12]. DNA concentration and quality were evaluated using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and by electrophoresis on a 1% agarose gel.

Genome sequencing

Library preparation was performed with the TruSeq Nano DNA Kit (San Diego, CA, USA), and genome sequencing was carried out on an Illumina NovaSeq X platform (Illumina Inc., USA) at Macrogen Inc. (Seoul, Republic of Korea). Raw Fastq-formatted reads were used for the genome assembly. Assembled nucleotide sequences were further annotated using GeSeq [13] with manual corrections. The circular plastome map was generated using the online program Chloroplot [14]. The whole plastome of A. microdictyon has been deposited in NCBI GenBank under accession number PX878715. The BioProject, BioSample, and SRA numbers are PRJNA1403796, SAMN54690655, and SRR36876375, respectively.

Simple sequence repeats detection

SSRs in the nucleotide sequences of A. microdictyon plastome were detected using the web-tool MISA (https://webblast.ipk-gatersleben.de/misa/) [15]. The thresholds were set at 8 repeat units for mononucleotides, 4 for di- and trinucleotides, and 3 for tetra-, penta-, and hexanucleotides.

Phylogenetic analysis

Phylogenetic analyses were performed using Maximum Likelihood (ML) and Bayesian Inference (BI) methods. Complete plastome nucleotide sequences were used to reconstruct the ML and BI phylogenetic trees. To determine the phylogenetic position of A. microdictyon, 18 plastome sequences from other Allium species and two outgroup taxa (Aloe maculata and Iris sanguinea) were retrieved from NCBI GenBank. Their accession numbers are provided in Fig. 3. The ML and BI trees were constructed using IQ-TREE v2.2.2.6 [16] and MrBayes v3.2.7 [17], respectively. The resulting phylogenetic trees were visualized in FigTree v1.4.4 (http://tree.bio.ed.ac.uk/software/figtree/). The subgenera recognized in this study were assigned to the classification proposed by Friesen et al. (2006) [10].

Non-local planelike minimizers and Γ \Gamma -convergence of periodic energies to a local anisotropic perimeter
Serena Dipierro, Matteo Novaga, Enrico Valdinoci, Riccardo Villa

Abstract
. We investigate a homogenization problem related to a non-local interface energy with a periodic forcing term. We show the existence of planelike minimizers for such energy. Moreover, we prove that, under suitable assumptions on the non-local kernel and the external field, the sequence of rescaled energies Γ \Gamma -converges to a suitable local anisotropic perimeter, where the anisotropy is defined as the limit of the normalized energy of a planelike minimizer in larger and larger cubes (i.e., what is called in jargon “stable norm”). To obtain this, we also establish several auxiliary results, including: the minimality of the level sets of the minimizers, explicit bounds on the oscillations of the minimizers, density estimates for almost minimizers, and non-local perimeter estimates in the large.



1.
Introduction

Planelike minimizers are objects (such as surfaces or interfaces) that minimize a given energy functional and lie at a bounded distance from a hyperplane. They tipically occur
in periodic media, where the energy density repeats periodically in space, allowing for suitable cancellations of the inhomogeneous minutiae of the environment that produce stable structures which, albeit not being perfectly flat, appear as flat at a large scale.
In this spirit, planelike minimizers show how a possibly complicated structure at a fine scale
behaves like a simple, averaged object in the large, and this phenomenon in turn provides an essential building
block to understand homogenization.

Moreover, planelike minimizers link the microscopic features of a medium to its macroscopic behavior, often reducing the analysis to that of “effective energies”, called
in jargon “stable norms”, which play the role of homogenized “surface tensions”
capturing the effective anisotropy of the medium at a large scale (see below for further details).

Also, when planelike minimizers possess some geometric organization
(such as foliations or laminations of space), they can help describe the global geometry of minimizers.

In certain variational settings arising in the theory of dynamical systems
(like twist maps or Frenkel-Kontorova models), planelike minimizers correspond to Aubry-Mather sets,
namely to configurations that minimize action and exhibit periodic and quasi-periodic order
(see
[
MR670747
,
MR719634
,
MR2356117
]
).
In analogy to this, planelike minimizers have been constructed for
elliptic functionals and minimal surfaces
(see
[
MR0847308
,
MR1852978
,
MR2197072
,
MR2542727
]
),
phase transition models and partial differential equations
(see
[
MR2099113
,
MR2342272
,
MR2809349
]
), fluid jets (see
[
MR2126143
]
), statistical mechanics and spin systems (see
[
MR1620543
,
MR3652519
]
).

In this paper, we investigate a homogenization problem related to a non-local interface energy with a periodic forcing term, extending the results presented in
[
chambolle_thouroude
]
to a non-local setting. Considering some assumptions on the non-local kernel
K
and the external field
g
, we show the existence of planelike minimizers for such energy, i.e. minimizers that are at bounded distance from a plane (see Definition
1.11
below). Moreover, we prove that the sequence of rescaled energies
\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converges, as
\varepsilon
approaches

\mathscr{F}_{\phi}
. The anisotropy

]

Adapting some ideas presented in
[

,
chambolle_thouroude
]

to our setting, we construct planelike minimizers for our energy

via a cell-problem. Then, we show that the stable norm

\Gamma

\begin{split}&\mbox{find}\quad u_{p}\in\mathcal{W}:=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mbox{ s.t. $u$ is $\mathbb{Z}^{n}$-periodic and }\int_{Q}u(x)\,dx=0\right\}\\

\Gamma
-convergence results and the stable norm

With the planelike minimizers of Corollary
1.12
, we are able to prove the main result of this paper, which is the
\Gamma
-convergence of a sequence of rescaled energies to the so-called stable norm.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Lipschitz domain and let, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{\varepsilon}:=\{\varepsilon(k+Q)\mbox{ s.t. }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\ \varepsilon(k+Q)\subseteq\Omega\}.

Then, for any set
E
, we define the rescaled energy
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
as

(1.19)

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(E,\Omega)&:=\iint_{\Omega_{\sharp}}\frac{1}{2}|\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E}(y)|K_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{\varepsilon}\cap E}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\\
&=\operatorname{P}_{K_{\varepsilon}}(E,\Omega)+\int_{\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{\varepsilon}\cap E}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx,\end{split}

where

K_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n+1}}K\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right)\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad g_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}g\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right).

For later convenience, we also set

(1.20)

\begin{split}\mathscr{E}_{\varepsilon}(E,\Omega)&:=\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(E,\Omega)-\mathcal{L}_{K_{\varepsilon}}(E\cap\Omega,E^{c}\cap\Omega^{c})\\
&=\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{E}(x)\chi_{E^{c}}(y)K_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{\varepsilon}\cap E}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

We will show that the sequence
\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

\Gamma
-converges to a local anisotropic perimeter.

To this end, let
p\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and let
Q^{p}
be a cube of side
1
with a face perpendicular to
p
. Moreover, let
E_{p}
be a planelike minimizer for
\mathcal{J}
.
Then, in the wake of
[
MR3223561
]
, we define the stable norm
\phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(1.21)

\begin{split}\phi(p):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon E_{p},Q^{p})=\lim_{R\to+\infty}R^{1-n}\mathscr{F}_{1}(E_{p},Q^{p}_{R}),\end{split}

whenever such limit exists. Here, we are adopting the notation
Q^{p}_{R}
for the cube of side
R
with a face perpendicular to
p
.

Besides, for any set
E\subseteq\Omega
of finite perimeter, we define

(1.22)

\mathscr{F}_{\phi}(E,\Omega):=\int_{\partial E\cap\Omega}\phi(\nu_{E}(x))d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

Then, the following holds true:

Theorem 1.13

(

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
)

We have that
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}\mathscr{F}_{\phi}
, as

, with respect to the

-convergence of sets. Namely, for every set

, we have that

(i)

for any sequence of sets

such that

in

(i.e.

),

(1.23)

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\varepsilon},\Omega)\geqslant\mathscr{F}_{\phi}(E,\Omega);

(ii)

there exists a sequence of sets

such that
E_{\varepsilon}\to E
in

and

(1.24)

Comparison with the existing literature

In relation with the existing literature for the non-local setting, we recall that
problems entangled with phase-transition, both at the macro-scale and at the meso-scale, have been addressed in

In our setting, we have to face two main difficulties. First, we observe that the forcing term

does not need to be positive, we still have to guarantee that the energies

The second main concern is that, since the kernel

K
and the contribution due to
g

Additionally, using (

\begin{split}&\iint_{A_{R,R^{1+\alpha},R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{E}(x)\chi_{E^{c}}(y)K(x,y)\,dx\,dy\leqslant\kappa_{2}\iint_{(E\cap A_{R,R^{1+\alpha},R})\times E^{c}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s_{2}}}\\

1.9
, Remark
1.8
and Corollary
8.5
, we infer that

(9.9)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathscr{F}_{\eta_{j}}(F_{j},B^{\prime\prime}_{j})\geqslant\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathscr{F}_{\eta_{j}}(\eta_{j}E_{\nu},B^{\prime\prime}_{j})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\cap B_{1})\phi(\nu)=\omega_{n-1}\phi(\nu).

Also, observe that

(9.10)

\mathscr{E}_{\eta_{j}}(E^{\prime\prime}_{j},B^{\prime\prime}_{j})-\mathscr{F}_{\eta_{j}}(F_{j},B^{\prime\prime}_{j})\geqslant-\mathcal{L}_{K_{\eta_{j}}}(F_{j}\cap B^{\prime\prime}_{j},F_{j}^{c}\cap(B^{\prime\prime}_{j})^{c}).

Since both
E_{j}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
and
\eta_{j}E_{\nu}\to\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, thanks to Lemma
7.1
, we have that

(9.11)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{L}_{K_{\eta_{j}}}(F_{j}\cap B^{\prime\prime}_{j},F_{j}^{c}\cap(B^{\prime\prime}_{j})^{c})=0.

Therefore, putting together (
9.9
), (
9.10
), and (
9.11
), we find that

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\mathscr{E}_{\eta_{j}}(E^{\prime\prime}_{j},B^{\prime\prime}_{j})\geqslant\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathscr{F}_{\eta_{j}}(\eta_{j}E_{\nu},B^{\prime\prime}_{j})-\mathcal{L}_{K_{\eta_{j}}}(F_{j}\cap B^{\prime\prime}_{j},F_{j}^{c}\cap(B^{\prime\prime}_{j})^{c})=\omega_{n-1}\phi(\nu).

This, together with (
9.6
), entails the desired result.

10.
Continuity of the stable norm
\phi

In this section, we discuss the continuity of the stable norm
\phi
. This result is a version of
[
chambolle_thouroude
, Corollary A.4]

covering our setting, which will play a crucial role in proving the
\Gamma-\limsup
inequality (see Theorem
1.13
-(
ii
)) in Section
11
.

Remark 10.1
.

Before presenting our result, we point out that, in
[
chambolle_thouroude
, Corollary A.4]
, the authors claim that the same argument of
[
MR1852978
, Lemma 10.2]
suffices to prove the convexity of the stable norm
\phi
. However, because of—plausibly—a typo in the proof of the latter, such argument cannot be directly applied in our context.

Proposition 10.2
.

\phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function.

Proof.

Let
p
,
q\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and define
\theta
as the angle between
p
and
q
(i.e.
\theta
is defined through
p\cdot q=\cos\theta
), so that if
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}\in SO(n)
denotes a rotation of angle
\theta
, then
p=\mathcal{R}_{\theta}q
.

The continuity of
\phi
will follow if we show the existence of a function
\psi
such that

|\phi(q)-\phi(p)|\leqslant\psi(\theta)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{\theta\to 0}\psi(\theta)=0.

To this purpose, we use Theorem
1.13
-(
i
) (notice indeed that
\varepsilon\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}
converges to
\mathcal{I}_{p}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
)
and Proposition
8.1
to obtain

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q},Q^{p})\geqslant\mathscr{F}_{\phi}(\mathcal{I}_{p},Q^{p})=\phi(p)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon E_{p},Q^{p}).

From this and the fact that
K
is rotation invariant (see (
1.2
)), it follows that

(10.1)

\begin{split}\phi(q)-\phi(p)&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big(\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon E_{q},Q^{q})-\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon E_{p},Q^{p})\Big)\\
&\geqslant\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big(\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon E_{q},Q^{q})-\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q},Q^{p})\Big)\\
&=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{n-1}\left(\int_{E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{q}/\varepsilon)_{1}}g-\int_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{p}/\varepsilon)_{1}}g\right).\end{split}

We now recall that

\partial E_{q}\subseteq\{|x\cdot q|\leqslant M\}=:S^{q}_{M}

and hence

\bigcup\big\{k+Q\mbox{ s.t. }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mbox{ and }(k+Q)\cap\partial E_{q}\neq\varnothing\big\}\subseteq S_{M+\sqrt{n}}^{q}.

Thus, in light of (
1.1
), we find that

\int_{E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{q}/\varepsilon)_{1}}g-\int_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{p}/\varepsilon)_{1}}g=\int_{E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{q}/\varepsilon)_{1}\cap S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}}g-\int_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{p}/\varepsilon)_{1}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\theta}S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}}g.

Therefore, using also a Hölder inequality in (
10.1
) and observing that
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}=S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}}
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\phi(q)-\phi(p)\\
&\quad\geqslant\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(-\varepsilon^{n-1}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left|\Big(E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{q}/\varepsilon)_{1}\cap S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\Big)\Delta\Big(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{p}/\varepsilon)_{1}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\theta}S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\Big)\right|\right)\\
&\quad\geqslant\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(-\varepsilon^{n-1}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left|\Big(E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{q}/\varepsilon)_{1}\cap S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\Big)\Delta\Big(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{p}/\varepsilon)_{1}\cap S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\Big)\right|\right).\end{split}

Now, observe that, arguing as in (
8.25
), we have that

\begin{split}&\left|(E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{q}/\varepsilon)_{1}\cap S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}})\Delta(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap\mathcal{Q}(Q^{p}/\varepsilon)_{1}\cap S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}})\right|\\
&\qquad\leqslant\left|[E_{q}\cap S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}C^{q}]\Delta[\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}C^{p}]\right|+\varepsilon^{1-n}\zeta(\varepsilon),\end{split}

where
C^{q}
is the (unitary) square-based cylinder with axis
q

and

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{1-n}\zeta(\varepsilon):=&\left|S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E^{q}\cap\big(C^{p}/\varepsilon\setminus\mathcal{Q}(Q^{p}/\varepsilon)_{1}\big)\right|\\
&+\left|S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap E^{q}\cap\big(C^{q}/\varepsilon\setminus\mathcal{Q}(Q^{q}/\varepsilon)_{1}\big)\right|,\end{split}

with
\zeta(\varepsilon)\to 0
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Thus,

\begin{split}&\phi(q)-\phi(p)\\
\geqslant&\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(-\varepsilon^{n-1}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left|\left(E_{q}\cap S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}C^{q}\right)\Delta\left(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}C^{p}\right)\right|\right).\end{split}

Let us define

\psi(\theta):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{n-1}\left|\left(E_{q}\cap S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}C^{q}\right)\Delta\left(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}E_{q}\cap S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap\frac{1}{\varepsilon}C^{p}\right)\right|,

so that

\phi(q)-\phi(p)\geqslant-\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\psi(\theta).

Moreover, notice that
\psi(\theta)\to 0
, as
\theta\to 0
(namely as
p\to q
).

Interchanging the roles of
p
and
q
, we obtain that

\begin{split}&\phi(p)-\phi(q)\geqslant\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(-\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left|\big(E_{q}\cap S^{p}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap C^{p}\big)\Delta\big(\mathcal{R}_{-\theta}\cap S^{q}_{M+\sqrt{n}}\cap C^{q}\big)\right|\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=-\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|J\mathcal{R}_{\theta}|\psi(\theta)=-\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\psi(\theta),\end{split}

and hence

|\phi(q)-\phi(p)|\leqslant\|g\|_{\infty}\psi(\theta),

as desired.
∎

Now, we define the
1
-homogeneous extension of
\phi
as

(10.2)

\widetilde{\phi}(p):=\begin{cases}|p|\phi\left(\frac{p}{|p|}\right),\quad&{\mbox{ if }}p\neq 0,\\
0,\quad&{\mbox{ if }}p=0.\end{cases}

As a byproduct of Proposition
10.2
, we obtain that also
\widetilde{\phi}
is continuous (in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
).

Corollary 10.3
.

\widetilde{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function.

Proof.

Let
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, it follows from the homogeneity of
\widetilde{\phi}
and Proposition
10.2
that

\begin{split}&\lim_{p\to q}\widetilde{\phi}(p)=\lim_{p\to q}|p|{\phi}\left(\frac{p}{|p|}\right)=\lim_{p\to q}\left((|p|-|q|){\phi}\left(\frac{p}{|p|}\right)+|q|{\phi}\left(\frac{p}{|p|}\right)\right)\\
&\qquad=\lim_{p\to q}\left((|p|-|q|){\phi}\left(\frac{p}{|p|}\right)+|q|{\phi}\left(\frac{q}{|q|}\right)\right)=\lim_{p\to q}\left((|p|-|q|){\phi}\left(\frac{p}{|p|}\right)\right)+\widetilde{\phi}(q).\end{split}

Since
{\phi}(p/|p|)
is bounded, we conclude that

\lim_{p\to q}\widetilde{\phi}(p)=\widetilde{\phi}(q),

showing the continuity of
\widetilde{\phi}
at
q
.
∎

11.
Proof of the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality

This section is devoted to proving
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality in Theorem

-(

). Our argument adapts to the setting under consideration here the ideas of

,

, in which the result follows from a standard polyhedral approximation taking advantage of the existence of planelike minimizers. For this, we need the following technical result.

Lemma 11.1
.

Let

be such that

is a polyhedron. Then, there exists a sequence of sets
\{E_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
such that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\varepsilon},\Omega)\leqslant\mathscr{F}_{\phi}(E,\Omega).

Since

is a polyhedron, there exist points
x_{j}\in\partial E\cap\Omega
and directions
p_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, with
j=1,\dots,N
, for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\partial E\cap\Omega=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}(x_{j}+\mathcal{I}_{p_{j}})\cap\Omega.

Here above and in the rest of the proof, the notation in (
7.1
) for
\mathcal{I}_{p_{j}}
is used.

Also, there are
N
disjoint Lipschitz domains
\Omega_{j}
such that

\Omega=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\Omega_{j}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad(x_{j}+\mathcal{I}_{p_{j}})\cap\Omega\subseteq\Omega_{j}.

Now, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we define

(11.1)

E_{\varepsilon}:=(E\setminus\Omega)\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\Omega_{j}\cap\big(x_{j}+\varepsilon E_{p_{j}}\big)\Big),

where, for every

,

is a planelike minimizer constructed as in Theorem

(see Figure
3
).

The planelike approximation of the polygonal set
E
as in (

).

Therefore, we obtain

(11.2)

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\varepsilon},\Omega)&\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}+\varepsilon E_{p_{j}},\Omega_{j})+\int_{E_{\varepsilon}\cap(\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{\varepsilon}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{Q}(\Omega_{j})_{\varepsilon})}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\\

By Corollary

(11.4)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{E_{\varepsilon}\cap(\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{\varepsilon}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{Q}(\Omega_{j})_{\varepsilon})}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\leqslant 0.

Moreover, thanks to Lemma

, we infer that

(11.5)

\begin{split}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}&\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{K_{\varepsilon}}(\varepsilon E_{p_{j}}\cap\Omega_{j},E_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap\Omega_{j}^{c})+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{K_{\varepsilon}}(\varepsilon E_{p_{j}}^{c}\cap\Omega_{j},E_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega_{j}^{c})\\

for all

.

Hence, gathering (

),

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\varepsilon},\Omega)\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi(p_{j})\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big(\Omega_{j}\cap(x_{j}+\partial\mathcal{I}_{p_{j}})\big)=\int_{\partial E\cap\Omega}\phi(\nu_{E})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Appendix A
Norms subject to short-range interactions

We point out a useful equivalence of norms when a kernel is restricted to close-by interactions (see e.g.
[
2025arXiv250409976A
, Lemma 2.1]
for related results). For the rest of this section, let
s\in(0,1/2)
.

Lemma A.1
.

There exists
C>0
, depending only on
\delta
,
n
, and
s
, such that,
for all
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{Q}u(x)\,dx=0,

we have that

\iint_{Q\times Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\leqslant C\iint_{(Q\times Q)\cap\{|x-y|<\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Proof.

Up to renaming constants, the desired result follows if we prove that

\iint_{(Q\times Q)\cap\{|x-y|\geqslant\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\leqslant C\iint_{(Q\times Q)\cap\{|x-y|<\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Moreover, since

\begin{split}\iint_{(Q\times Q)\cap\{|x-y|\geqslant\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy&\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta^{n+2s}}\iint_{Q\times Q}\big(|u(x)|+|u(y)|\big)\,dx\,dy\\
&=\frac{2}{\delta^{n+2s}}\int_{Q}|u(x)|\,dx,\end{split}

it suffices to show that

\int_{Q}|u(x)|\,dx\leqslant C\iint_{(Q\times Q)\cap\{|x-y|<\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

We argue for the sake of contradiction and suppose that this is not true.
Namely, we suppose that
there exists a sequence of functions
u_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(A.1)

\int_{Q}u_{j}(x)\,dx=0

and

(A.2)

1=\int_{Q}|u_{j}(x)|\,dx>j\iint_{(Q\times Q)\cap\{|x-y|<\delta\}}\frac{|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

We decompose
Q
as the disjoint union of cubes
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{N}
of side less than
\frac{\delta}{2}
and we deduce from (
A.2
) that, for all
\ell\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,

(A.3)

\frac{1}{j}>\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{\ell}\times(Q_{\ell})_{\delta}\\
(Q\times Q)\cap\{|x-y|<\delta\}\end{subarray}}\frac{|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\geqslant\iint_{Q_{\ell}\times Q_{\ell}}\frac{|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy,

where

(Q_{\ell})_{\delta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ s.t. }\mbox{dist}(x,Q_{\ell})<\delta\}.

Then (see e.g.
[
MR2944369
, Theorem 7.1]
), the sequence
u_{j}
is precompact in
L^{1}(Q_{\ell})

and consequently, up to a subsequence,
u_{j}
converges to some function
u_{0}
in
L^{1}(Q_{\ell})
for all
\ell\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, and thus in
L^{1}(Q)
and a.e. in
Q
.

Thus, from (
A.3
) and Fatou Lemma,

\iint_{Q_{\ell}\times Q_{\ell}}\frac{|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy=0,

which implies that
u_{0}
is constant. As a result, since
we have from (
A.1
) that

\int_{Q}u_{0}(x)\,dx=0,

we conclude that
u_{0}
is the null function.

However, according to (
A.2
),

1=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{Q}|u_{j}(x)|\,dx=\int_{Q}|u_{0}(x)|\,dx,

providing the desired contradiction.
∎

Appendix D
Well-posedness of the Plateau problem for the energy
\mathscr{F}

In this section, we use a Sobolev embedding and a simple covering argument to show that the minimization problem for the energy functional
\mathscr{F}
with fixed external datum is well-posed.
To do this, let us consider a Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a set
\widetilde{E}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite
K
-perimeter. Suppose
K
satisfies (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
), and (
1.5
). Let also
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-periodic function satisfying (
1.1
).

We recall that the functional
\mathscr{F}
is defined in (
1.7
) as

\mathscr{F}(E,\Omega):=\operatorname{P}_{K}(E,\Omega)+\int_{E\cap\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{1}}g(x)\,dx

and we investigate the problem:

(D.1)

\begin{split}&\mbox{find\penalty\ $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$ such that\penalty\ $E\setminus\Omega=\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega$}\\
&\mbox{and}\quad\mathscr{F}(E,\Omega)\leqslant\mathscr{F}(F,\Omega),\quad\mbox{for every\penalty\ $F$ s.t. }F\setminus\Omega=\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega,\end{split}

We claim that:

Proposition D.1

(Well-posedness of the minimization problem (
D.1
))
.

There exists a minimizer for
\mathscr{F}
in
\Omega
with external datum
\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega
.

This result is a consequence of a standard compactness argument, whose precise statement goes as follows:

Lemma D.2
.

Let
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
be a sequence of sets such that
E_{j}\setminus\Omega=\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega
and

\sup_{j}\mathscr{F}(E_{j},\Omega)<+\infty.

Then, there exist a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a subsequence
\{E_{j_{m}}\}_{m}
such that
E\setminus\Omega=\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega
and

E_{j_{m}}\to E\mbox{ in }L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\mbox{as }m\to+\infty.

Proof.

Let us consider the covering
\{B_{\delta/2}(x)\}_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}
of
\Omega
made of balls of radius
\delta/2
. By compactness, there exist finitely many points
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\in\overline{\Omega}
such that, setting
B^{(\ell)}:=B_{\delta/2}(x_{\ell})\cap\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega\subseteq\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{N}B^{(\ell)},

and

\displaystyle B^{(\ell)}\cap B^{(\ell+1)}\neq\varnothing,\mbox{ for every }\ell\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}.

Now, observe that, in virtue of (
1.4
),
for any
\ell\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and for any set
F
,

\begin{split}&\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2}[\chi_{F}]^{2}_{H^{s_{1}}(B^{(\ell)}}=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2}\iint_{B^{(\ell)}\times B^{(\ell)}}\frac{|\chi_{F}(x)-\chi_{F}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\iint_{B^{(\ell)}\times B^{(\ell)}}|\chi_{F}(x)-\chi_{F}(y)|^{2}K(x,y)\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad=\iint_{B^{(\ell)}\times B^{(\ell)}}\chi_{F}(x)\chi_{F^{c}}(y)K(x,y)\,dx\,dy\leqslant\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\chi_{F}(x)\chi_{F^{c}}(y)K(x,y)\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\leqslant\operatorname{P}_{K}(F,\Omega)=\mathscr{F}(F,\Omega)-\int_{F\cap\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{1}}g(x)\,dx\leqslant\mathscr{F}(F,\Omega)+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|,\end{split}

where
[\cdot]_{H^{s}}
denotes the
(2s)
-Gagliardo seminorm.

In particular, we deduce that, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2}[\chi_{E_{j}}]^{2}_{H^{s_{1}}(B^{(1)})}\leqslant\sup_{j}\mathscr{F}(E_{j},\Omega)+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|.

Hence, by the compactness of the Sobolev embedding
H^{s_{1}}(B^{(1)})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B^{(1)})\subseteq L^{1}(B^{(1)})

(see
[
MR2944369
, Corollary 7.2]
), there exist a set
E_{1}\subseteq B^{(1)}
and a subsequence
\{E_{j_{m}}\}_{m}
such that
E_{j_{m}}\to E_{1}
in
L^{1}(B^{(1)})
and pointwise in
B^{(1)}
.

Similarly,

\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2}[\chi_{E_{j_{m}}}]^{2}_{H^{s_{1}}(B^{(2)})}\leqslant\sup_{m}\mathscr{F}(E_{j_{m}},\Omega)+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|.

Therefore, up to considering a further subsequence, we also have that
E_{j_{m}}\to E_{2}
in
L^{1}(B^{(2)})
and pointwise in
B^{(2)}
, for some set
E_{2}\subseteq B^{(2)}
. Moreover, by the uniqueness of the limit, it follows that
E_{1}=E_{2}
in
B^{(1)}\cap B^{(2)}
.

Repeating the same argument in every ball
B^{(\ell)}
, up to considering further subsequences, we infer that
E_{j_{m}}\to E
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, where

E:=\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{N}E_{\ell}\right)\cup\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega,

concluding the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
D.1
.

First, notice that the energy functional
\mathscr{F}
is uniformly bounded from below. Indeed, for any set
F
we have

\mathscr{F}(F,\Omega)\geqslant\int_{F\cap\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{1}}g(x)\,dx\geqslant-\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|.

Thus, we consider a minimizing sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
such that
E_{j}\setminus\Omega=\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega
, and

\mathscr{F}(E_{j},\Omega)\to\inf\{\mathscr{F}(F,\Omega)\quad\mbox{s.t. }F\setminus\Omega=\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega\},\quad\mbox{as }j\to+\infty.

Now we take

\widetilde{E}\setminus\Omega&\quad{\mbox{ in }}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{cases}

and we observe that,

large enough,

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(E_{j},\Omega)\leqslant\mathscr{F}(F,\Omega)=\operatorname{P}_{K}(F,\Omega)+\int_{F\cap\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)_{1}}g(x)\,dx\leqslant\operatorname{P}_{K}(\Omega)+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|<+\infty.

Therefore, the desired result follows from Lemma

and the lower semi-continuity of the functional

. ∎

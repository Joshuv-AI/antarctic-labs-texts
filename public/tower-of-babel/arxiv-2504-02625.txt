A spanning tree model for Khovanov homology, Rasmussen’s s-invariant and exotic discs in the 4 4 -ball
Aninda Banerjee, Apratim Chakraborty, Swarup Kumar Das

Abstract
. The checkerboard coloring of knot diagrams offers a graph-theoretical approach to address topological questions. Champanerkar and Kofman defined a complex generated by the spanning trees of a graph obtained from the checkerboard coloring whose homology is the reduced Khovanov homology. Notably, the differential in their chain complex was not explicitly defined. We explicitly define the combinatorial form of the differential within the spanning tree complex. We additionally provide a description of Rasmussen’s s s -invariant within the context of the spanning tree complex. Applying our techniques, we identify a new infinite family of knots where each of them bounds a set of exotic discs within the 4-ball.



1.
Introduction

Khovanov homology is an invariant of links in
S^{3}
that categorifies the Jones polynomial. Champanerkar and Kofman
[
CK09
]
and Wehrli
[
Weh08
]
introduced the spanning tree model for the Khovanov homology. In their construction, the chain complex is generated by the spanning trees of the Tait graph associated with a given knot diagram. The homology of this spanning tree complex is isomorphic to the reduced Khovanov homology. However, the exact combinatorial form of the differential remained unknown in the bigraded case.

Given a connected link diagram
\mathcal{L}
, let
G_{\mathcal{L}}
denote its associated Tait graph. Throughout this paper, we explore Khovanov homology through the perspective of the Tait graph. In Section 2, we establish a correspondence between these two viewpoints. Under this framework, the generators of the Khovanov chain complex, which are enhanced states, correspond to enhanced spanning subgraphs of the Tait graph.

We then provide an acyclic matching within the reduced Khovanov chain complex, denoted as
(\CKh^{+}(\mathcal{L}),\partial_{\Kh}^{+})
. From the acyclic matching, we obtain the bigraded Morse complex
(\CST^{+}(G_{\mathcal{L}}),\partial_{ST}^{+})
, which is generated by the spanning trees of
G_{\mathcal{L}}
. The differential operator
\partial_{ST}^{+}
is entirely determined by the graph-theoretic properties of the Tait graph
G_{\mathcal{L}}
. A similar technique has been applied by the authors and Paul in
[
BCDP25
]
in order to derive a spanning tree model for chromatic homology which categorifies the chromatic polynomial. As a consequence of the fundamental theorem of algebraic discrete Morse theory, we present the following theorem, providing a spanning tree model for reduced Khovanov homology where the differential can be explicitly computed using the graph theoretic information of the Tait graph:

Theorem 1.1
.

For a connected link diagram
\mathcal{L}
, the cohomology of the complex
(\CST^{+}(G_{\mathcal{L}}),\partial_{ST}^{+})
is isomorphic to the reduced Khovanov homology
{\Kh}^{+}(\mathcal{L})
(Also denoted by
\widetilde{\Kh}(\mathcal{L})
).

We extend our construction of acyclic matching to the unreduced Khovanov chain complex of an arbitrary connected link diagram
\mathcal{L}
, denoted as
(\CKh(\mathcal{L}),\partial_{\Kh})
, in order to obtain the Morse complex
(\CST(G_{\mathcal{L}}),\partial_{ST})
. This complex is generated by the signed spanning trees of the Tait graph
G_{\mathcal{L}}
, and the differential can be described from the graph theoretic information of the Tait graph as well.

Theorem 1.2
.

For a link diagram
\mathcal{L}
, the cohomology of the complex
(\CST(G_{\mathcal{L}}),\partial_{ST})
is isomorphic to the Khovanov homology
{Kh}(\mathcal{L})
.

Every non-split alternating link except the trivial knot, Hopf link and their connected sums have a

Section 2

2.1.
Algebraic discrete Morse Theory

For a given regular CW-complex, Forman
[
For98
]
developed Discrete Morse theory which studies a new homotopy-equivalent CW-complex which has fewer cells than the original one. Using this concept, Kozlov
[
Koz05
]
and Sköldberg
[
Skö05
]
independently developed an algebraic analogue of this theory which is also known as the Algebraic discrete Morse theory. In simple terms,this theory takes a freely generated chain complex
(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
, provides an acyclic matching on the corresponding Hasse diagram
\mathcal{H}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
, a directed weighted graph representing
(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
, and as it turns out that
(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
and the Morse complex of
\mathcal{H}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
are quasi-isomorphic. Thus, we get a combinatorial description of the homology of
(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
. For our purpose, we define this theory following
[
JW09
]
for a freely generated chain complex where each
C_{i}
is a finitely generated free module over a commutative ring with unity. An analogous theory shall also follow for general freely generated chain and co-chain complexes.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a commutative ring with
1
, and
C_{\star}=(C_{i},\partial_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be a freely generated chain complex of finitely generated free
\mathcal{R}
-modules. Fix a basis
B=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}B_{i}
such that

C_{i}\backsimeq\bigoplus_{c\in B_{i}}\mathcal{R}c

and let the differential
\partial_{i}:C_{i}\rightarrow C_{i-1}
be described as

\partial_{i}(c)=\sum_{c^{\prime}\in B_{i-1}}[c:c^{\prime}].c^{\prime}.

Here,
[c:c^{\prime}]
denotes the incidence number or the coefficient between
c
and
c^{\prime}
.

Given the complex
(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
, we construct the Hasse diagram corresponding to
(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
, a directed, weighted graph
\mathcal{H}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})=(V,E)
such that
V=B
, and the elements of
E
are triples
(v,v^{\prime},w)
, where
v,v^{\prime}\in V
,
v
is directed towards
v^{\prime}
(denoted by
v\rightarrow v^{\prime}
) and
w\in\mathcal{R}
is the weight, are defined as follows:

(\{c,c^{\prime}\},[c:c^{\prime}])\in E\Longleftrightarrow c\in B_{i},c^{\prime}\in B_{i-1},\text{ and }[c:c^{\prime}]\neq 0

Thus for such a given graph, we now define the meaning of an
acyclic matching
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A subset
{\mathcal{M}}\subset E
is called an
acyclic matching
if it satisfies the following three conditions:

(1)

Each vertex
v\in V
lies in at most one edge
e\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

(2)

For all edges
(\{c,c^{\prime}\},[c:c^{\prime}])\in{\mathcal{M}}
, the weight
[c:c^{\prime}]
is a unit in
\mathcal{R}
.

(3)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
has no directed cycles, where
V(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star}))=V
and
E(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star}))
is given by

E(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})):=(E\setminus{\mathcal{M}})\cup\left\{\left(c^{\prime},c,-\frac{1}{[c:c^{\prime}]}\right)\bigg|\>(c,c^{\prime},[c:c^{\prime}])\in{\mathcal{M}}\right\}

Given an acyclic matching
{\mathcal{M}}
on
\mathcal{H}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
, we now have a couple of notations associated to it,

(1)

A vertex
v\in V
is called
critical
with respect to
{\mathcal{M}}
if
v\not\in e
for all
e\in{\mathcal{M}}
. For a given chain group
C_{i}
, we have the following set:

B_{i}^{\mathcal{M}}:=\{c\in C_{i}\mid c\text{ is critical }\}

(2)

Let
\mathcal{A}(c,c^{\prime})
denote the set of directed paths from
c
to
c^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
.

(3)

For a given edge
e=(c\rightarrow c^{\prime})\in E(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star}))
, the weight can be redefined as:

w(c\rightarrow c^{\prime})=\begin{cases}[c:c^{\prime}],&\text{ if }e\in E\cap E(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star}))\\
-\frac{1}{[c:c^{\prime}]},&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

(4)

For
{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}(c,c^{\prime})
, where
{\mathcal{P}}=(c=c_{1}\rightarrow c_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow c_{n}=c^{\prime})
, the weight of
{\mathcal{P}}
is given by

w({\mathcal{P}}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}w(c_{i}\rightarrow c_{i+1})

(5)

\Gamma(c,c^{\prime})=\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}(c,c^{\prime})}w({\mathcal{P}})
denotes the sum of the weights of all directed paths from
c
to
c^{\prime}
.

We are now ready to define the
Morse complex
associated to
(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
and an acyclic matching
{\mathcal{M}}
on its Hasse diagram.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})
be a free chain complex with an acyclic matching
{\mathcal{M}}
in
\mathcal{H}(C_{\star},\partial_{\star})

\partial_{i}^{\mathcal{M}}(c):=\sum_{c^{\prime}\in B^{\mathcal{M}}_{i-1}}\Gamma(c,c^{\prime}).c^{\prime}.

Lemma 2.3

\partial_{\star}^{\mathcal{M}}

Associated to a given freely generated chain complex

(2)

\displaystyle f:C_{\star}^{{\mathcal{M}}}\rightarrow C_{\star}

For the maps defined above, we have the following:

2.2.
Links and Tait graph

Given a connected link diagram
\mathcal{L}
, one can view it as a connected 4-regular planar graph
G
, where the vertices of
G
are crossings of
\mathcal{L}
and there is an edge between two such vertices if there is an arc in
\mathcal{L}
between the two corresponding crossings. One can show that the dual graph
G^{*}
is a bipartite graph and hence, we can use two colors (black and white) to color the vertices of each partition. Thus, one can color the faces of
\mathcal{L}
using two colors such that no two adjacent faces (faces sharing a common arc) have same color. This is also known as
checkerboard coloring
of
\mathcal{L}
.

Given a checkerboard coloring of
\mathcal{L}
, one can associate a graph to it in which the vertices represent the black regions of the diagram, and the edges represent the common crossings shared by the black regions. Additionally, each edge can be assigned a sign (
+
or
-
) based on whether the corresponding crossing connects or separates two black regions when a
A-
smoothing is performed on it. This resulting signed graph is called the
Tait graph
of
\mathcal{L}
, which we denote by
G_{\mathcal{L}}
(See Figure
2

Interestingly, it turns out that one can express the Jones polynomial, a classical invariant of a link, in terms of some combinatorial information of the Tait graph. Thistlethwaite
[

based on a fixed ordering on the edges of
G_{\mathcal{L}}

For a given spanning tree
T

G_{\mathcal{L}}
and for each edge
e\in G_{\mathcal{L}}
, we have the following two definitions:

\displaystyle\text{For }e\in T,

\displaystyle\emph{cut}(T,e)=\{e^{\prime}\in G_{\mathcal{L}}\mid e^{\prime}\text{ connects }T\setminus e\}

\displaystyle\text{For }e\not\in T,

\displaystyle\emph{cyc}(T,f)=\{f^{\prime}\in G_{\mathcal{L}}\mid f^{\prime}\text{ belongs to the unique cycle in }T\cup f\}

Now let us fix an ordering on the edges of
G_{\mathcal{L}}
. Given an edge
e\in T
, if
e
is the smallest edge in
\emph{cut}(T,e)

e
is said to be an

T
else it is said to be an
internally inactive (dead)
edge. Similarly, if
e\not\in T
and
e
is the smallest edge in
\emph{cyc}(T,e)

e
is said to be
externally active (live)
edge of
T
or else it is said to be
externally inactive (dead)
edge of T. In other words, for a positive edge

\displaystyle a_{T}:E^{+}(G_{\mathcal{L}})\rightarrow\{L,D,l,d\}

Definition 2.10

An

4.1.
Acyclic matching on the unreduced Khovanov complex

In the case of reduced Khovanov complex, the enhanced spanning subgraphs

had a restriction which was
\epsilon(C)=1
for

with
v_{d}\in C
. If we change this restriction to

then we have another complex
\CKh^{-}(U(T))
. The unreduced Khovanov complex for a twisted unknot

is given by

\CKh(U(T))=\CKh^{+}(U(T))\oplus\CKh^{-}(U(T))

Thus, for a given connected link diagram

is given by:

\CKh(\mathcal{L})=\bigoplus_{T}\CKh(U(T))

Observe that the matching in Theorem

did not depend on the above mentioned restriction. Thus, one can easily define a similar near-perfect acyclic matching on
\mathcal{H}((\CKh^{-}(U(T)),\partial_{\Kh}^{-}))
. Thus, we do have an acyclic matching on

with exactly two critical enhanced spanning subgraphs which we denote by

and

.

Let
T
be a spanning tree of the Tait graph

\mathcal{L}
, then there exists an acyclic matching on

on

.

Proof.

The existence of a matching in
\CKh(U(T))
is clear from the above discussion. Now if a directed cycle exists in
\mathcal{H}_{{\mathcal{M}}}((\CKh(U(T)),\partial_{\Kh}))
, then it must contain a directed edge

such that

and

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
be cobordisms from
\mathcal{L}_{0}
to
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and let
C
be a ribbon concordance from
\mathcal{L}_{1}
to
\mathcal{L}_{2}
. If
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
induce distinct maps
\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{0})\rightarrow\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{1})
, then the cobordisms
C\circ\Sigma_{1}
and
C\circ\Sigma_{2}
induce distinct maps
\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{0})\rightarrow\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{2})
.

Similarly, if the reverses of
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
induce distinct maps
\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{1})\rightarrow\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{2})
, then the reverses of
C\circ\Sigma_{1}
and
C\circ\Sigma_{2}
induce distinct map
\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{2})\rightarrow\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{0})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
induce distinct maps on
\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{0})\rightarrow\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{1})
, then there exists

such that
\Kh(\Sigma_{1})(S)\neq\Kh(\Sigma_{2})(S)
. Since
C
induces an injective map
\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{1})\rightarrow\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{2})
, we have

\Kh(C\circ\Sigma_{1})(S)-\Kh(C\circ\Sigma_{2})(S)=\Kh(C)(\Kh(\Sigma_{1})(S)-\Kh(\Sigma_{2})(S))\neq 0

An analogous argument applies to the reversed cobordisms, appealing instead to the surjectivity of the map
\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{2})\rightarrow\Kh(\mathcal{L}_{1})
induced by the reverse of

.

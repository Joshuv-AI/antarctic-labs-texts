A tree-level inventory dataset of Picea crassifolia forests in the Qilian Mountains for spatial point pattern and neighbor competition analysis
Authors: Shuying Yin, Zongying Cao, Zhirong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This dataset was collected from four permanent plots of secondary Picea crassifolia forest in Yongdeng County, Gansu Province, in the eastern Qilian Mountains of northwestern China. The data include plot location and topographic information, as well as individual tree measurements of coordinates, diameter at breast height (DBH), tree height, east-west crown width, and north-south crown width. A total of >300 trees were recorded, representing a complete range of size classes from saplings to large-diameter trees. The dataset provides precise relative coordinates for each tree within the plots, enabling spatial point pattern analysis and neighbor competition index analysis at the individual tree level. It also serves as fundamental data for research in forest ecology, population spatial patterns, intraspecific competition, carbon stock estimation, and forest dynamics modeling.

Background

The Qilian Mountains are located at the intersection of the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau, the Inner Mongolia Plateau, and the Loess Plateau, serving as a crucial ecological security barrier in northwestern China [1]. As the dominant tree species in the Qilian Mountain forest ecosystem, Picea crassifolia is mainly distributed on shady and semi‑shady slopes and plays an irreplaceable role in water conservation, biodiversity protection, and regional carbon balance [2,3]. Stand spatial structure and intraspecific competition are key factors influencing forest growth, development, and ecological functions. Spatial point pattern analysis and neighbor competition index analysis based on individual‑tree coordinates are important approaches for revealing population spatial distribution patterns and interactions among individuals [4,5]. However, tree‑level spatial data for Picea crassifolia forests in the Qilian Mountains remain scarce in public databases, particularly high‑resolution datasets that simultaneously include tree coordinates, diameter at breast height (DBH), tree height, and two‑directional crown width.

To fill this data gap, we established four permanent Picea crassifolia plots on the northern slope of the Qilian Mountains and conducted a systematic stem-by-stem survey of all living trees with DBH ≥ 1.0 cm, recording key variables including relative coordinates, DBH, tree height, and east-west and north-south crown widths. This dataset provides fundamental data support for research on spatial structure formation mechanisms, intraspecific competition, and stand dynamics modeling of Picea crassifolia forests. It can also serve as a reference for sustainable management and carbon sink assessment of cold‑region coniferous forests.

Data Description

This dataset is provided as a single Excel file (.xlsx) containing two worksheets named Site Information and Tree Data. All data are original measurements, with no model fitting or imputation, thus preserving full individual‑tree variability. The dataset contains no missing values.

Site information worksheet

This worksheet records topographic and plot‑level environmental information for the four plots (one row per plot), including plot location, elevation, precise coordinates of the plot center and southwest corner, X‑axis bearing, slope, and survey date. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Study area

The Qilian Mountains are located at the intersection of the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau, the Inner Mongolia Plateau, and the Loess Plateau. Forest resources are mainly concentrated in the eastern and central Qilian Mountains, occurring in patches along the Zoulang Nanshan, Lenglongling, and the eastern section of Daban Mountain, at elevations ranging from 2500 m to 3300 m. Picea crassifolia in this region is mostly found in pure stands, primarily distributed on shady and semi‑shady slopes [6]. The climate is characterized as typical continental, cold, semi‑arid mountain climate, with distinct seasonal variation and precipitation mainly occurring from May to September [7–8].

Plot establishment and field survey

Plot‑based surveys are an important approach for analyzing stand spatial structure and carbon sequestration capacity [[9], [10], [11], [12], [13]]. In 2025, we established four permanent Picea crassifolia plots on the northern slope of the Qilian Mountains (Fig. 1a). All plots were placed in areas with uniform vegetation and minimal recent human disturbance, and their relative positions and spatial distribution are shown in Fig. 1b Each plot measured 25.82 m × 25.82 m, with an area of 666.67 m². A relative coordinate system was established for each plot, with the southwest corner as the origin, the east–west direction as the X‑axis, and the north–south direction as the Y‑axis. All living trees with diameter at breast height (DBH) ≥ 1.0 cm within the plots were tagged, positioned, and measured. For each tree, we recorded relative coordinates, DBH, tree height, and east–west and north–south crown widths. DBH was measured at 1.3 m above ground using a diameter tape, recorded to the nearest 0.1 cm. For trees with irregular stems, two perpendicular diameter measurements were taken and averaged. Data recording and quality control

Field data were first recorded on waterproof paper and then digitized in Microsoft Excel. After data entry, we performed quality control by cross-checking with original records and verifying coordinate ranges to ensure that all tree coordinates fell within plot boundaries. The validity of DBH, tree height, and crown width measurements was also examined to ensure data accuracy and completeness. The final dataset contains no missing values. The dataset is provided as an Excel file with two worksheets. The plot information worksheet records plot location, topographic factors, and other environmental variables; the tree‑level data worksheet records tree ID, coordinates, DBH, tree height, and crown width measurements.

Limitations

All four plots are located on ridge positions in the eastern Qilian Mountains, with slopes of 7°–9°, and no soil data were collected. Slope aspect was not measured because the plots are situated on gentle ridge slopes where aspect is expected to have limited ecological relevance for tree distribution and microclimate. However, to support accurate georeferencing of individual trees, this dataset provides the central coordinates, southwest corner coordinates, compass bearing of the plot X‑axis, and plot dimensions for each plot, allowing users to transform and reconstruct the absolute positions of the trees. Although the spatial coverage is limited, the plots are relatively concentrated and include a complete gradient of tree size classes, making them representative of the local area in the eastern Qilian Mountains.

Ethics Statement

No animal or human subjects were involved in this study. All data were collected from field surveys, and no endangered species were affected.

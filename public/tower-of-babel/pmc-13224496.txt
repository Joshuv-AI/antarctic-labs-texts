miR-3648 regulates cell functions and acts as a potential biomarker to predict prognosis in breast cancer and its bioinformatics analysis
Authors: Yuetong Guo, Libo Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBreast cancer is an important cause of death from malignant tumors in women. This study aims to clarify the regulatory role, molecular mechanism and clinical value of miR-3648 in breast cancer.MethodsThis study analyzed 126 pairs of clinical samples of breast cancer. The expression of miR-3648 and its potential target SMAD6 was detected by qRT-PCR. Survival outcomes were assessed by Kaplan-Meier analysis and Cox regression model. In the MCF-7 and BT-474 cell lines, function gain and loss experiments were conducted using miR-3648 mimic or inhibitor. Biological behaviors modulated by miR-3648, including cell proliferation, migration and invasion were assessed using CCK-8 and Transwell assays. The target genes of miR-3648 were predicted by bioinformatics, and their relationship was verified by dual-luciferase.ResultsMiR-3648 was significantly upregulated in breast cancer and correlated with unfavorable clinicopathological features and predicted the survival rate of patients. In vitro experiments revealed that overexpression of miR-3648 promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of breast cancer cells, while inhibition of its expression yields opposite effects. Bioinformatics analysis and verification experiments jointly identified SMAD6 as the functional target of miR-3648. SMAD6 and miR-3648 expression was inversely correlated in breast cancer tissues, and SMAD6 knockdown partially reversed the effects of miR-3648 inhibition.ConclusionsmiR-3648 exerts its role in promoting the progression of breast cancer by directly targeting and inhibiting SMAD6, and can serve as a potential biomarker for evaluating the prognosis of patients.Keywords: Breast tumor, miR-3648, SMAD6, Cell proliferation, Cell invasion

Background

Characterized by consistently high annual incidence and mortality rates as reported by the WHO, breast cancer stands as a major threat to women’s health and has now surpassed lung cancer to become the most common cancer globally [1, 2]. Although comprehensive treatment methods, targeted therapy and radiotherapy have improved the survival of patients, problems such as drug resistance, treatment toxicity and long-term recurrence and metastasis remain the main clinical challenges leading to treatment failure and patient death [3, 4]. This current situation indicates the imperative to systematically elucidate the mechanisms underlying the initiation and progression of breast cancer and search for new specific biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

The tumor-regulatory role of non-coding RNAs, particularly miRNAs, has gained increasing recognition in recent years [5]. miRNAs mediate post-transcriptional gene regulation through mRNA binding, participating in key processes [6]. Evidence indicates that multiple miRNAs exhibit dysregulated expression in breast cancer. By regulating downstream target genes, they exert pro-cancer or anti-cancer effects and become key molecules influencing tumor progression, treatment response, and patient prognosis [7]. For instance, miR-5188 [8], miR-124 [9], miR-575 [10], etc. have been reported to be dysregulated in breast cancer and are involved in the prognosis of breast cancer patients and the regulation of tumor phenotypes. It is worth noting that studies have shown that miR-3648 promotes the survival, migration and invasion of cancer cells in various malignant tumor tissues, including prostate cancer [11], lung adenocarcinoma [12] and bladder cancer [13], playing a key role in promoting cancer. Emerging evidence suggests a potential role for miR-3648 in breast cancer. For instance, research has found that in the high-risk male breast cancer population of Klinefelter syndrome, its pathogenesis is related to miR-3648 [14]. Further, miR-3648 expression in tumor tissues of estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer was significantly correlated with the Oncotype DX recurrence score [15]. These reports all point to a potential oncogenic role for miR-3648 in the carcinogenic stage. Therefore, we hypothesize that miR-3648 contributes to breast cancer progression by regulating specific target molecules.

Based on these predictions, we aimed to elucidate the clinical relevance, functional roles and underlying molecular mechanisms of miR-3648 in breast cancer. A key objective was to validate its regulatory interaction with key candidate targets and evaluate its potential as a prognostic biomarker.

Methods

Data collection

This study included a total of 126 paired tissue samples from female patients with breast cancer who underwent surgical treatment at the hospital between April 2017 and February 2020. The samples comprised cancerous tissues and their matched adjacent normal tissues. Eligible patients were pathologically diagnosed with primary breast cancer, had received no neoadjuvant therapy prior to surgery, and possessed complete clinicopathological records. Exclusion criteria involved a history of other malignant tumors, presence of severe systemic diseases, or incomplete follow‑up data. The study protocol was approved by The Ethics Committee of Cangzhou People’s Hospital (Approval No. 20160056) and complied strictly with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Patients were followed up for five years post‑surgery through outpatient reviews and telephone interviews. Written informed consent was obtained from each participant for both tissue collection and the 5-year follow-up.

Cell line selection and cultivation

The breast cancer cell lines MCF-7 (RRID: CVCL_0031) and BT-474 (RRID: CVCL_0179), alongside the normal mammary epithelial line MCF-10 A (RRID: CVCL_0598) (all sourced from ATCC), were cultured under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂). MCF-7 and BT-474 were grown in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. MCF-10 A was maintained in MEGM (Mammary Epithelial Cell Growth Medium, Lonza, CC-3150) supplemented with the provided growth factors (BPE, hEGF, insulin, hydrocortisone).

Cell transfection

To establish overexpression and knockdown models, MCF-7 and BT-474 cells were seeded at 70–80% confluency and transfected with 100 nM of miR-3648 mimic (miR10018068-1-5, RiboBio, Guangzhou, China), miR-3648 inhibitor (miR20018068-1-5, RiboBio, Guangzhou, China), or their corresponding negative controls (mimic NC: miR1N0000001-1-5; inhibitor NC: miR2N0000001-1-5; RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA). Following the manufacturer’s protocol, cells were harvested at 48 h for RNA extraction, qRT-PCR validation, and functional assays.

qRT-PCR

To measure the levels of miR‑3648 and SMAD family member 6 (SMAD6), qRT‑PCR was performed. Total RNA extracted from tissue and cultured cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) was subjected to reverse transcription for cDNA synthesis with a commercial kit (Takara Bio). PCR amplification was carried out on a real‑time PCR instrument using SYBR Green fluorescence dye (TAKARA SYBR Green qPCR Kit). During detection, the expression of miR‑3648 was normalized to U6 snRNA as an internal control, while SMAD6 mRNA expression was normalized to GAPDH. Relative expression was determined using the 2−ΔΔCt.

Cell proliferation

Cells were seeded in 96-well plates (3 × 10³ cells/well, quintuplicate) and incubated for 0, 24, 48, and 72 h, respectively. CCK-8 solution (10 µL/well) was then added for a 2‑h incubation, and absorbance was subsequently read at 450 nm.

Cell migration and invasion assays

Cell migration and invasion abilities were assessed using Transwell chambers. A Matrigel-coated upper chamber was used in the invasion assay (incubated at 37 °C for 4 h), whereas no coating was applied for the migration assay. To assess migration and invasion, cells were serum-starved and seeded at 5 × 10⁴ or 1 × 10⁵ cells/well, respectively, in upper chambers facing 10% serum medium. After 24 h (migration) or 48 h (invasion), non-penetrating cells in the upper chamber were removed. Translocated cells were fixed with paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and quantified under a microscope. Five randomly selected fields were imaged per well to obtain average cell counts for statistical analysis.

Target gene screening and dual-luciferase assay

Potential target genes were screened by cross-referencing predictions from the miRWalk database (using a binding energy threshold of ≤ -36 for high-affinity interactions) and the TargetScan database (employing a cumulative weighted context + + score of ≤ -3 to identify highly conserved targets). Candidate targets were selected from the overlapping gene sets visualized in a Venn diagram. These candidates were further prioritized based on their inverse expression trends relative to miR-3648 and supporting evidence from relevant literature.

For the reporter assay, DNA fragments containing either the wild-type 3’-UTR sequence of the SMAD6 gene or a mutant version with specific mutations introduced into the predicted miR-3648 binding site were cloned into a reporter vector. MCF-7 and BT-474 cells were co-transfected with these reporter constructs alongside either a miR-3648 mimic or its corresponding negative control. Dual-luciferase activity was assessed 48 h post-transfection using a commercial assay kit (Promega) on cell lysates.

Statistical analysis

Kaplan-Meier curves and Cox multivariate regression were used for survival and prognostic analysis. Baseline characteristics were compared: categorical variables with Chi-square/Fisher’s exact tests and continuous variables with t-test/ANOVA. For one-way ANOVA analyses, Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference post-hoc test was applied for multiple comparisons to control for type I error. GraphPad Prism 9.0 (RRID: SCR_002798) and SPSS 23.0 (RRID: SCR_002865) were employed for all statistical work, with significance set at p < 0.05.

Results

Expression and prognostic value of miR-3648 in breast cancer tissues

Compared with non-tumor tissues, the relative level of miR-3648 in breast cancer tissues was increased (Fig. 1A, p < 0.0001). Stratifying patients based on median miR-3648 expression revealed that high expression correlated strongly with poorer survival compared to the low-expression group (Fig. 1B, p < 0.0001).

Prognostic Significance of miR‑3648 expression in breast cancer patients. A Relative expression of miR‑3648 in breast cancer tissues compared with matched adjacent non‑tumor tissues. B Kaplan‑Meier overall survival curves for patients stratified into high‑ and low‑miR‑3648 expression groups. ****p < 0.0001

Analysis of clinical data from the 126 patients (Table 1) indicated that elevated miR-3648 expression was linked to several aggressive tumor features. Specifically, high expression showed significant associations with advanced TNM stage (p = 0.033), poor differentiation grade (p = 0.021), and the presence of lymph node metastasis (p = 0.002). In contrast, no significant correlations were found between miR-3648 levels and patient age, tumor size, ER, HER2, PR status, or molecular subtype (all p > 0.05). High miR-3648 expression remained an independent risk factor for poor prognosis (HR = 2.161, 95% CI: 1.141–4.093, p = 0.018), after adjusting for other clinicopathological variables (Table 2). Advanced TNM stage was also confirmed as an independent prognostic factor (HR = 1.973, 95% CI: 1.109–3.508, p = 0.021). None of the other clinical parameters included in the model showed independent predictive value for patient outcome (all p > 0.05).

The Effect of miR-3648 modulation on cellular functions in breast cancer

As shown in Fig. 2A, miR‑3648 expression was higher in both cancer cell lines compared with MCF‑10 A cells (p < 0.0001). Successful overexpression and knockdown of miR‑3648 were achieved in MCF‑7 and BT‑474 cells following transfection with miR‑mimic or miR‑inhibitor, respectively (Fig. 2B, p < 0.0001). Overexpression of miR-3648 markedly enhanced the proliferative activity of both cell lines, whereas suppression of miR‑3648 substantially reduced cell proliferation (Fig. 2C-D, p < 0.0001). We further evaluated changes in migratory and invasive capacities using Transwell assays. The number of migrating cells increased significantly upon miR‑3648 overexpression and decreased notably after miR‑3648 inhibition (Fig. 2E, p < 0.0001). A consistent trend was observed in the invasion assay (Fig. 2F), where miR‑3648 overexpression promoted invasion, while its inhibition suppressed invasive ability.

SMAD6 is the direct functional target of miR‑3648 in breast cancer

Based on the combined analysis of the TargetScan and miRWalk databases, six high-confidence candidate target genes of miR-3648 were identified (Fig. 3A). Following miR-3648 overexpression, a significant decrease in SMAD6 expression was observed (Fig. 3B, p < 0.0001). This downregulation aligned with the lower expression level of SMAD6 detected in breast cancer tissues compared to non-tumorous samples (Fig. 3C, p < 0.0001). Consistently, at the cellular level, SMAD6 expression was markedly reduced in two breast cancer cell lines relative to the normal mammary epithelial cell line MCF-10 A (Fig. 3D, p < 0.0001).

Sequence alignment revealed a high degree of complementarity between the seed region of hsa-miR-3648 and nucleotides 33–39 within the 3’ UTR of the SMAD6 gene (Fig. 3E). MiR-3648 overexpression significantly suppressed the luciferase activity of the wild-type reporter construct in both MCF-7 (Fig. 3F) and BT-474 (Fig. 3G) cells (p < 0.0001). Conversely, inhibition of miR-3648 increased reporter activity. This regulatory effect was completely eliminated upon mutation of the predicted binding site within the SMAD6 3’ UTR. Furthermore, correlation data from patient samples indicated a significant inverse correlation between miR-3648 expression and SMAD6 mRNA in breast cancer tissues (Fig. 3H, r = -0.740, p < 0.0001).

Functional impact of SMAD6 knockdown in breast cancer cells

To verify that SMAD6 acts as a key downstream effector mediating the tumor-promoting function of miR-3648, SMAD6 expression was knocked down using siRNA while miR-3648 was inhibited, and its effect on malignant phenotypes was examined. The knockdown efficiency of SMAD6 siRNA was confirmed at the mRNA level by qRT-PCR (Fig. 4A). As shown in Fig. 4B and C, knockdown of SMAD6 partially restored the proliferative capacity of both MCF-7 and BT-474 cells (p < 0.0001). Similarly, in migration and invasion assays (Fig. 4D-E), silencing SMAD6 effectively reversed the reduction in cell migration (p < 0.001) and invasion (p < 0.0001) induced by miR-3648 inhibition.

Discussion

As documented in existing studies, miR-3648 demonstrates oncogenic activity across multiple malignancies including esophageal adenocarcinoma [16], lung adenocarcinoma [12], and non-small cell lung cancer [17]. These data highlight the potential role of miR-3648 as a pivotal regulator during tumor progression. Nevertheless, its precise function and molecular cancer remain to be fully elucidated. A clue emerges from a recent large-scale diagnostic investigation. Analysis of nearly 4,000 clinical specimens indicated that miR-3648 is likely among the key miRNAs showing dysregulated expression in breast cancer [18]. Based on this, we conducted an in-depth exploration of the role of miR-3648 in breast cancer. Our findings confirmed the significant upregulation of miR-3648 in breast cancer tissues, implying its potential role in tumor progression. Key clinical parameters considered included tumor size, differentiation grade, TNM stage, and lymph node metastasis. They collectively reflect the extent of local invasion, cellular malignancy, disease progression, and risk of regional spread [19, 20], providing essential information for prognosis assessment and guiding treatment decisions. This study found that miR-3648 is not only is linked to the more aggressive clinicopathological parameters of breast cancer, but also can serve as an independent prognostic predictor, suggesting that it may be a key driver of breast cancer progression. Notably, a parallel study conducted concurrently with our work primarily focused on the role of miR-3648 in triple-negative breast cancer [21], whereas our study extends its clinical significance to all molecular subtypes of breast cancer. Although the differences between subtypes did not reach statistical significance due to the limited sample sizes in certain subgroups, we demonstrated for the first time that miR-3648 possesses independent prognostic value in the overall cohort, serving as a broad-spectrum prognostic biomarker. These two studies complement each other, collectively revealing the multifaceted role of miR-3648 in breast cancer pathogenesis.

Multiple studies have confirmed that miRNA is widely involved in regulating malignant biological behaviors of cancer cells [22–24]. Abnormal expression of miRNA is strongly associated with and drives a range of malignancies, including breast cancer. As demonstrated in non-small cell lung cancer, miR-3648 drives tumor progression by targeting and down-regulating APC2 [25], while in bladder cancer, it promotes invasion and metastasis by inhibiting the TCF21/KISS1 axis [13]. Similar to the mechanisms observed in the other cancer types mentioned above, miR-3648 also drives breast cancer progression. These findings broaden our understanding of miR-3648’s function in cancer and implicate its role in driving breast cancer malignancy through distinct molecular pathways, thereby establishing a foundation for further mechanistic investigation.

SMAD6 is an important negative regulatory factor in the TGF-β/BMP signaling pathway and participates in maintaining cellular homeostasis by inhibiting signal transduction [26, 27]. Evidence from this study indicates that SMAD6 was down-regulated in breast cancer specimens and exerted a definite tumor suppressor function. This result is consistent with the overall dysregulation of the BMP/TGF-β signaling pathway in breast cancer. Notably, the expression pattern of SMAD6 differs from that of other members of the SMAD family, and its expression is significantly reduced in stage II tumors [28], which further supports the low expression of SMAD6 observed in this study. SMAD6 functions as a crucial intracellular signaling inhibitor. The regulatory axis involving miRNA-mediated control of SMAD6 has been documented across various cancer types. For instance, in glioma, miR-186 directly targets and suppresses SMAD6 expression, thereby promoting tumor cell apoptosis [29]. Similarly, studies in colorectal cancer have demonstrated that miR-186-5p downregulates SMAD6, leading to inhibition of cell cycle progression and proliferation [30]. This study further confirmed through experiments such as dual-luciferase reporting that SMAD6 is a direct target of miR-3648. The results of clinical sample analysis show that the expression levels of the two are significantly negatively correlated. Notably, functional rescue experiments demonstrated that co‑inhibition of miR‑3648 and SMAD6 effectively reversed the tumor‑suppressive effects caused by miR‑3648 knockdown alone. This finding offers direct functional support for SMAD6 being a critical downstream target of miR‑3648 in breast cancer. Importantly, a parallel study by Kong et al. identified FOXD3 as another direct target of miR-3648 [21]. Our independent finding that SMAD6 is also a direct target of miR-3648 suggests that this miRNA may promote breast cancer progression through regulating multiple downstream effectors. The convergence of these independent investigations highlights the complexity of the miR-3648 regulatory network and underscores its multifaceted role in breast cancer pathogenesis.

Beyond downstream mechanisms, the upstream regulation of miR-3648 also warrants investigation. Existing studies have provided important clues. In gastric cancer, c-Myc has been shown to directly bind the miR-3648 promoter and inhibit its transcription [31]. In multiple tumor types including breast cancer, MYC forms complex regulatory circuits with miRNAs [32]. Meanwhile, p53 has been demonstrated to broadly regulate miRNA expression in breast cancer [33]. Additionally, epigenetic mechanisms such as DNA methylation have been closely linked to miRNA expression in basal-like breast cancer [34]. These findings suggest that transcription factors and epigenetic modifications may contribute to the dysregulation of miR-3648 in breast cancer, warranting further investigation.

This study has made certain progress in elucidating the involvement of the miR‑3648/SMAD6 axis in breast cancer, but its conclusions still need to be viewed with caution in the following aspects. Firstly, all clinical samples were sourced from a single center and had a limited sample size. Although they were statistically verified to be significant, the universality of their results in the population still needs to be confirmed through multi-center, large-sample prospective studies. Secondly, experimental verification mainly focused on the cellular level and lacked the support of in vivo animal models. Therefore, the true function of this regulatory axis in the in vivo tumor microenvironment and under systemic physiological conditions remains unclear. Thirdly, due to sample size limitations, we were unable to perform subgroup analysis based on molecular subtypes. Whether the expression patterns, functional roles, and prognostic value of miR-3648 differ across breast cancer subtypes requires further investigation. Future larger-scale multicenter studies are warranted to further explore its expression characteristics and clinical significance in different subtypes. Fourth, this study mainly focused on the downstream effects of miR-3648. Its upstream regulatory networks, including transcription factors (e.g., p53, MYC), epigenetic modifications (e.g., DNA methylation), and ceRNA mechanisms, remain to be elucidated. Future studies should integrate multi-omics approaches to systematically explore the upstream factors regulating miR-3648 expression, providing a more comprehensive theoretical basis for its potential as a therapeutic target. Furthermore, given its independent prognostic value in breast cancer tissues, miR-3648 also holds potential as a circulating liquid biopsy biomarker. Serum miRNA detection offers advantages over tissue-based assays, including non-invasive sampling, real-time monitoring, and the ability to overcome tumor heterogeneity [35]. Compared to traditional biomarkers such as CA15-3 and CEA [36], miR-3648 may offer higher specificity, and the stability of miRNAs in blood makes them suitable for long-term follow-up. Future prospective studies should investigate whether dynamic changes in circulating miR-3648 during follow-up can predict recurrence earlier than conventional methods, and whether combining it with existing clinical parameters could improve risk stratification. Therefore, it is necessary for subsequent research to conduct systematic exploration around the above aspects in order to promote the deep mechanism and clinical application of this discovery.

Conclusion

Clinical analysis confirmed significant upregulation of miR-3648 in breast carcinoma, which correlated with adverse clinical features, and independently predicted patient survival. At the mechanism level, this study demonstrates that SMAD6 is a direct target of miR-3648 in breast cancer. Overexpression of miR-3648 promoted the proliferation, migration and invasion of cancer cells. Rescue assays showed that SMAD6 knockdown could partially reverse the anti-cancer effect produced by inhibiting miR-3648. This work contributes a novel framework for elucidating the molecular mechanisms of breast cancer occurrence and development, but also offers potential molecular targets for prognosis assessment and targeted therapy development strategies of this disease.

The Beltrami – de Sitter model: Penrose’s CCC, Radon transform and a hidden 𝐆 2 {\bf G}_{2} symmetry
Paweł Nurowski

Abstract
. We combine the well – known Beltrami – Klein model of non – Euclidean geometry on a two – dimensional disk, where the geodesics are the chords of the disk, with the two – dimensional de Sitter space. The geometry of the de Sitter space is defined on the complement of the Beltrami – Klein disk in the plane, with the de Sitter metric being the unique Lorentzian Einstein metric whose light cones form cones tangent to the disk in this complement. This leads to a Beltrami – de Sitter model on the plane ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , which is endowed with the Riemannian Beltrami metric on the disk and the Lorentzian de Sitter metric outside the disk in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . We explore the relevance of this model for Penrose’s Conformal Cyclic Cosmology, first in the two – dimensional setting and subsequently in higher dimensions, including the physically significant case of four dimensions. In this context, we define a Radon-like transform between the de Sitter and Beltrami spaces, facilitating the purely geometric transformation of physical fields from the Lorentzian de Sitter space to the Riemannian Beltrami space. In the two –, and three – dimensional cases, we also uncover a hidden 𝐆 2 {\bf G}_{2} symmetry associated with the de Sitter spaces in these dimensions, which is related to a certain vector distribution naturally defined by the geometry of the model. We suggest the potential for discovering similar hidden symmetries in the n n – dimensional Beltrami-de Sitter model.

Introduction

The Conformal Cyclic Cosmology (CCC) proposed by Roger Penrose
[
27
]
has garnered significant interest among theoretical physicists
[
18
,
23
,
24
,
26
,
30
,
31
]
and has sparked a search for its potential astronomical manifestations
[
3
,
4
,
21
,
22
]
. In its simplest form, CCC postulates that the Universe consists of a sequence of consecutive eons, each characterized by its own Lorentzian metric. At both the beginning and end of each eon, the metric is degraded to a mere conformal class. This conformal class is then promoted to contain a Lorentzian metric for the next eon, and this process of conformally gluing consecutive eons continues, possibly infinitely. Within the framework of CCC, the currently observed Universe represents just one of the Lorentzian eons within this infinite sequence.

A major challenge to Penrose’s proposal is the absence of a satisfactory formulation for how a strict Lorentzian structure is imposed on the subsequent eon from the full conformal structure of the preceding eon. While this may eventually be addressed, it is possible that resolving this issue could require modifications to the very formulation of CCC, potentially extending beyond the scope of conventional General Relativity.

In this paper, we present an example of a CCC model in which the General Relativity paradigm is altered, allowing the metric to change signature when transitioning from one eon to the next. This model, although not strictly consistent with orthodox GR, has at least two advantages:

•

It naturally glues eons – the two consecutive eons reside on the same manifold by the model’s very formulation, thereby making the "gluing problem" mentioned above moot;

•

It is mathematically elegant and can be traced back to the works of Cayley, Beltrami, and Klein.

In our model, referred to as the
Beltrami – de Sitter model
, we consider two distinct metrics: the
n
-dimensional Lorentzian de Sitter metric, which,
for

n=4
, is believed to asymptotically describe the metric to which the observed Universe converges, and the
n
-dimensional Riemannian Beltrami metric. These two metrics coexist on the same manifold, which is simply
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The two regions – Lorentzian and Riemannian – are separated by an
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{(n-1)}
, which represents the boundary
\partial B^{n}
of the unit ball
B^{n}
centered at the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Inside the ball, the metric is the Riemannian Beltrami metric, while outside the ball, the metric is the Lorentzian de Sitter metric.

Both metrics exhibit singularities, but these singularities only appear in their Einstein conformal factor at the boundary sphere
\mathbb{S}^{(n-1)}
, where the signature change – from Lorentzian to Riemannian – occurs.

The naturalness of this Riemannian – Lorentzian hybrid structure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
arises from the fact that the metric formula for each region, whether Lorentzian or Riemannian, is formally valid in the other region as well. A simple geometric definition of the Lorentzian structure outside the ball
B^{n}
states that this metric is the unique Lorentzian Einstein metric for which the cones in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
tangent to the ball
B^{n}
are light cones (see Section
2.3
and the beginning of Section
7
). This defined metric, when written in the standard coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, turns out to be valid everywhere in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, except at the points on the boundary sphere
\partial B^{n}
. Once the Lorentzian metric is defined according to the above rule in the complement of the ball
B^{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is recognized as the de Sitter metric in this region, and it continues to the Riemannian Beltrami metric inside the ball.

The hybrid Lorentzian–Riemannian metric
{\rm d}s^{2}
, defined in the complement of the sphere
\partial B^{n}
, defines a conformal structure
[{\rm d}s^{2}]
that is continuous everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with the exception of a degenerate behavior at the boundary
\partial B^{n}
, where the metric degenerates, allowing for the signature transition.

The model has many interesting features, which are discussed in the paper, first in a toy model for

Being convinced that our Riemannian – Lorentzian hybrid, which we named the Beltrami – de Sitter model, is an

This is discussed in Section 6, where we use

to wrap the de Sitter model from outside the ball

This model, although more physically appealing at first glance, suffers from the same main problem as all the toy models of CCC we know. While the appropriately rescaled de Sitter metric and its first fundamental form smoothly transition to the respective, rescaled, conformally inverted metric and its first fundamental form, the null geodesics passing through the boundary at

Apart from the ad hoc gluing of the Lorentzian – Lorentzian hybrid in Section 6, this feature of non-differentiable light rays in the purely Lorentzian model supports our belief that we should abandon the restriction of Lorentzian signatures in all eons and instead adopt the Beltrami – de Sitter model as a prototype for a less restrictive Lorentzian – Riemannian CCC.

•

2.5.
Hyperbolic spaces and Beltrami – de Sitter model

The Beltrami – de Sitter model is related to the geometries, which hyperboloids

-T^{2}+(X^{1})^{2}+(X^{2})^{2}=-\epsilon,\quad\quad\epsilon=\pm 1,

acquire from the ambient Minkowski three-space

\mathbb{M}=\{(T,X^{1},X^{2})\,|\,T,X^{1},X^{2}\in\mathbb{R}\},

equipped with the flat Minkowski metric

\eta=-{\rm d}T^{2}+({\rm d}X^{1})^{2}+({\rm d}X^{2})^{2}.

There are two kinds of these hyperboloids:

•

the one-sheeted
\mathbb{H}_{\shortminus 1}=\{(T,X^{1},X^{2})\,|\,T^{2}-(X^{1})^{2}-(X^{2})^{2}=-1\}
,

and

•

the two-sheeted
\mathbb{H}_{1}=\{(T,X^{1},X^{2})\,|\,T^{2}-(X^{1})^{2}-(X^{2})^{2}=1\}
,

and from now on, we will only consider them for
T>0
.

After restriction to these hyperboloids, the Minkowski metric
\eta
becomes

(2.6)

\eta_{|\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}=-\frac{(X^{1}{\rm d}X^{1}+X^{2}{\rm d}X^{2})^{2}}{\epsilon+(X^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}+({\rm d}X^{1})^{2}+({\rm d}X^{2})^{2}.

This metric has the Riemannian signature on the hyperboloid
\mathbb{H}_{1}
, and it has Lorentzian signature on the one-sheeted hyperboloid
\mathbb{H}_{\shortminus 1}
. It further follows that the pair
(\mathbb{H}_{\shortminus 1},\eta_{|\mathbb{H}_{\shortminus 1}})
is locally isometric to the de Sitter space
(M^{2},{\rm d}s^{2})
, and that the entire one sheet of
\mathbb{H}_{1}
, with its metric
\eta_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}
, is locally isometric to the Beltrami space
(B^{2},{\rm d}s^{2})
.

which maps the parameters
(X^{1},X^{2})
of points of the hyperboloid

pr\big(X^{1},X^{2}\big)=\big(x,y\big)

Now, passing from coordinates
(X^{1},X^{2})
to the projected-to-the-plane-

i.e., after a short algebra, the metric

of the Beltrami – de Sitter model.

Corollary 2.1
.

The plane
\Pi=\{\mathbb{M}\ni(T,X^{1},X^{2})\,|\,T=1\}
in the Minkowski space

is a realization of the Beltrami – de Sitter model

.

In this realization the Beltrami space

consists of points
(1,x,y)\in\mathbb{M}
such that

(\mathbb{M},\eta)

The de Sitter space

consists of points

de Sitter metric

(\mathbb{M},\eta)
.

2.6.
Special conformal geodesics in the Beltrami–de Sitter model

The interesting feature of the Beltrami–de Sitter model is that the
chords
of the disk
x^{2}+y^{2}\leq 1
(the brown chords on Figure
5
) are the
Riemannian geodesics
for
(B^{2},{\rm d}s^{2})
, and
straight lines
, or
half straight lines ending at the circle

x^{2}+y^{2}=1
, are
Lorentzian geodesics
for
(M^{2},{\rm d}s^{2}
). Actually in
(M^{2},{\rm d}s^{2})
there are three types of geodesics:
null
geodesics – the half straight lines
tangent
to the circle
x^{2}+y^{2}=1
at some point (the orange (half)lines on Figure
5
),
timelike
geodesics – the straight half lines ending at the circle
x^{2}+y^{2}=1
but
not
tangent to it (the red half lines outside the cyan disk in Figure
5
), and
spacelike
geodesics – the straight lines having zero intersection with the circle
x^{2}+y^{2}=1
(the green lines on the Figure
5
).

Figure 5.
Four types of geodesics in the Beltrami–de Sitter model

This demonstrates that every straight line in the entire Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which intersects the Beltrami disk
x^{2}+y^{2}\leq 1
, i.e. every line in the
(x,y)
-plane satisfying

Ax+By+C=0\quad\text{and}\quad A^{2}+B^{2}>C^{2},

has the following behavior:

•

It begins as a timelike geodesic at the past infinity of de Sitter space
M^{2}
(one of the top red half lines on Figure
5
).

•

It then reaches the Big Crunch circle of de Sitter space.

•

After crossing the Big Crunch circle, it continues as a chord (the brown interval being the continuation of the chosen red half line on Figure
5
) within the Beltrami disk. In the Beltrami-Riemannian framework, this chord represents the shortest path (in the Beltrami metric) between the entry and exit points of the line within the disk.

•

Finally, it returns to the Lorentzian de Sitter space
M^{2}
, traveling backward in time as a timelike geodesic (the red line being the continuation of the brown chord on Figure
5
) from the Big Crunch circle to the past infinity in
M^{2}
.

It should be noted that while a de Sitter observer’s journey from the past infinity of
M^{2}
to the Big Crunch of de Sitter space requires infinite proper time in the de Sitter metric

, it takes only a finite time in the conformally related metric
ds_{0}^{2}
defined by Equation (1.5).

Furthermore, every complete Euclidean line
Ax+By+C=0
with
A^{2}+B^{2}>C^{2}
is a geodesic in every metric belonging to the Lorentzian-Riemannian conformal class
[ds_{0}^{2}]
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{\mathbb{R}^{2}\ni(x,y)\mid x^{2}+y^{2}=1\}
. However, it is not a geodesic on the circle
x^{2}+y^{2}=1
because the conformal class changes its signature at this set, and the very concept of a geodesic breaks down there.

We will return to this discussion in Section
5.1
, in particular in the caption of Figure

3.1.
Conformal structure

We now show that the correspondence space

Consider a curve
\xi(t)=(p(t),P(t))=(x(t),y(t),a(t),b(t))

in

describes a movement of a point
p
in the de Sitter spacetime

describes a movement of a point
P
in the Beltrami (Riemannian signature) space.

Recall from Section
2.1
, Fig.
2

6
in Section
2.7

p=(x,y)
in the de Sitter space

defines a cord

in the Beltrami space

2.7

in
M^{2}
moves to

3.2.1.

Note that although the metric

[g]
, or what is the same the conformal class

of the metric

\overline{T}=\tilde{T}\cup\partial\tilde{T}

[g]
in
\overline{T}
; one of them is given by by the metric

The set

, which is the singular locus of the metric

3.2.2.
Further extension to
(\mathbb{R}P^{2})^{*}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}

The extension

arises

and independently of the main exposition as follows
[
9
]

Consider the space
V=(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
with points
({\bf p},{\bf q})=(p_{i},q^{j})
and the natural pairing
<{\bf p},{\bf q}>=p_{i}q^{i}
. Let
G
be a symmetric bilinear form in
V
defined by

G=\frac{-2<{\bf p}\times{\rm d}{\bf p},{\bf q}\times{\rm d}{\bf q}>}{<{\bf p},{\bf q}>^{2}}.

Here the symbol
\times
denotes the cross product in the respective spaces
(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{*}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

{\bf p}\times{\rm d}{\bf p}=\epsilon^{kij}p_{i}{\rm d}p_{j},\quad{\bf q}\times{\rm d}{\bf q}=\epsilon_{kij}q^{i}{\rm d}q^{j}.

Using the standard identities from the vector calculus in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
we can also write

as:

(3.7)

G=2\frac{<{\bf p},{\rm d}{\bf q}><{\rm d}{\bf p},{\bf q}>-<{\bf p},{\bf q}><{\rm d}{\bf p},{\rm d}{\bf q}>}{<{\bf p},{\bf q}>^{2}}.

This bilinear form is doubly degenerate on
V
, as in each pairs of the three components of the 1-forms
{\bf p}\times{\rm d}{\bf p}
or
{\bf q}\times{\rm d}{\bf q}
, only two are linearly independent at points of
V
. More specifically, the degenerate directions for

in
V
are the directions tangent to two Euler vector fields

Y_{p}=<{\bf p},\nabla_{\bf p}>=p_{i}\partial_{p_{i}},\quad Y_{q}=<\nabla_{\bf q},{\bf q}>=q^{i}\partial_{q^{i}}

on
V
. It further follows that
G

is preserved when Lie transported along

Y_{p}
and
Y_{q}
in
V
. This is the infinitesimal version of the fact that
G
is
invariant
with respect to the action

\phi_{(\lambda,\mu)}({\bf p},{\bf q})=(\lambda{\bf p},\mu{\bf q})

of the multiplicative group

\mathbb{R}_{0}^{2}=\{\mathbb{R}^{2}\ni(\lambda,\mu)\,|\,\lambda\mu\neq 0\}\quad\mathrm{on}\quad V,

as we have:

\phi_{(\lambda,\mu)}^{*}G=\frac{-2<\lambda{\bf p}\times{\rm d}(\lambda{\bf p}),\mu{\bf q}\times{\rm d}(\mu{\bf q})>}{<\lambda{\bf p},\mu{\bf q}>^{2}}=G.

Thus, the bilinear form
G
descends to the well defined bilinear form
g
on the quotient space

{\mathcal{T}}=V/\mathbb{R}_{0}^{2}=(\mathbb{R}P^{2})^{*}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}

of orbits of
\mathbb{R}_{0}^{2}
.
The descended bilinear form

is nondegenerate, and has split signature
(+,+,-,-)
.

Due to the invariance of

any 4-dimensional submanifold of

transversal to the Euler vector fields

and

is equipped with a split signature metric isometric to

. Any such submanifold is also a good local representation of
\mathcal{T}
. For example, taking a 4-dimensional slice

,
p_{2}=b
,

,

,

one gets

\iota^{*}(G)=\frac{2{\rm d}a\,\Big(\,\,(1-by)\,{\rm d}x+bx\,{\rm d}y\,\,\Big)\,\,+\,\,2{\rm d}b\,\Big(\,\,ay\,{\rm d}x+(1-ax)\,{\rm d}y\,\,\Big)}{(1-ax-by)^{2}},

which

in

and

3.3.2.
The transform viewed as a Penrose transform

These distinguished curves enable to transform functions on

and vice versa.

Indeed, suppose that we have an integrable function

of this function at a point

To get this we first pullback

to a function on

(\pi_{1}^{*}f)(x,y,a,b)=f(\pi_{1}(x,y,a,b))

\hat{f}(a,b)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f\Big(x,\frac{1}{b}-x\frac{a}{b}\Big)\sqrt{1+\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}}{\rm d}x.

Note that this is the same as the formula (
2.10

\hat{F}(x,y)=\int_{\frac{x-y\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-1}}{x^{2},y^{2}}}^{\frac{x+y\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-1}}{x^{2},y^{2}}}F\Big(a,\frac{1}{y}-a\frac{x}{y}\Big)\sqrt{1+\frac{x^{2}}{y^{2}}}{\rm d}a.

7.

In this Section we show that majority of constructions discussed so far can be performed in any dimension

Let us see that the same principle, which brought us to the 2-dimensional de Sitter metric in Section

For this we consider

its interior

and its complement

The conformal Lorentzian structure in

will be defined, as in the 2D case, requiring that the light cones of the Lorentzian metric
{\rm d}s^{2}
in

and with generators being straight lines emanating from

Thus the
n
-dimensional situation of getting the conformal structure on

can refer again to the Figure
3

p=(x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{n})
and

gives:

(i)

r^{2}+(r-p)^{2}=p^{2}

(ii)

rdp=0

(iii)

(iv)

By (i) and (iv) we get

by (iii). This determines

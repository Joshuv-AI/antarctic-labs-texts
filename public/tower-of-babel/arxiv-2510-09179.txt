Directional Subdifferentials at Infinity and Its Applications
Le Ngoc Kien, Nguyen Van Tuyen, Tran Van Nghi

Abstract
This paper investigates the behavior of sets and functions at infinity by introducing new concepts, namely directional normal cones at infinity for unbounded sets, along with limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity in the direction for extended real-valued functions. We develop several calculus rules for these concepts and then apply them to nonsmooth optimization problems. The applications include establishing directional optimality conditions at infinity, analyzing the coercivity, proving the compactness of the global solution set, and examining properties such as weak sharp minima and error bounds at infinity. To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, illustrative examples are provided and compared with existing results.

Introduction

The Mordukhovich (limiting) normal cone and its associated limiting subdifferential play a key role in modern variational analysis and optimization, as they provide useful tools for addressing nonsmooth and nonconvex optimization problems (see
Mordukhovich2006
;
Mordukhovich2018
;
Mordukhovich2024
).

The study of the behavior of sets and functions at infinity is a significant topic in optimization theory. Sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions in nonsmooth optimization via asymptotic cones and generalized asymptotic functions were given in
AT03
;
Aus1
;
Aus2
;
FFB-Vera
;
F-L-Vera
;
HLL
;
HLM
;
KMPT-21
;
Lara-Tuyen-Nghi
;
Nghi-Kien-Tuyen
. Recently, the concepts of normal cones at infinity for unbounded sets, together with limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity for extended real-valued functions, were introduced in
Kim-Tung-Son-23
. By employing various calculus rules for these notions, the authors characterized Lipschitz continuity at infinity for lower semicontinuous functions and applied the results to optimization problems, including optimality conditions, weak sharp minima, and stability properties. In
Tung-Son-24
, Clarke’s tangent cones at infinity for unbounded sets, subgradients at infinity for extended real-valued functions, and necessary optimality conditions at infinity for optimization problems were studied. Sufficient conditions for the existence of error bounds at infinity in lower semicontinuous inequality systems, as well as necessary optimality conditions for constrained optimization problems, were established in
Tuyen-24
. By using the tool of subdifferentials at infinity, Tuyen, Bae, and Kim
Tuyen-25
proposed optimality conditions at infinity for nonsmooth minimax programming problems.

Very recently, Anh and Hung
HA-Hung-25
investigated properties of normal cones with respect to a set and developed calculus rules for subdifferentials relative to a set at a reference point. The obtained results extend and improve the corresponding ones in
Thinh-Chuong-18
;
Thinh-Chuong-19
;
Thinh-Qin-Yao
. Furthermore, the authors also introduced the notions of normal cones and subdifferentials with respect to a set at infinity. These tools were then employed to derive necessary optimality conditions at infinity, establish the compactness of the solution set, and verify the coercivity in optimization problems with unbounded feasible sets. It is well known that, in the theory of subdifferentials (or generalized derivatives), both the size of the subdifferential and the availability of computation rules play a crucial role. In
HA-Hung-25
, the authors provided examples showing that the subdifferentials at infinity introduced in
Kim-Tung-Son-23
;
Tung-Son-24
can be quite large. However, for the subdifferential with respect to a set at infinity, as introduced in
HA-Hung-25
, the computation is quite involved, and the fundamental formulas for calculation have not yet been fully established. Therefore, the development of new subdifferential concepts at infinity remains an important direction of research.

In this paper, motivated by
Ginchev-Mor-11
;
Ginchev-Mor-12
;
Kim-Tung-Son-23
, we study normal cone at infinity and subdifferentials at infinity in the direction and their applications. Our main contributions to variational analysis and nonsmooth optimization theory are summarized as follows:

\bullet
For variational analysis, we introduce new concepts: the directional normal cone at infinity for unbounded sets, and the directional limiting and the directional singular subdifferentials at infinity for extended real-valued functions. Several fundamental calculus rules for these notions are established. Furthermore, we prove that the directional Lipschitz property at infinity of a real-valued function is equivalent to that its directional singular subdifferential at infinity is equal to
\{0\}
.

\bullet
For nonsmooth optimization theory, by employing related properties and calculus rules, we present several applications to nonsmooth optimization problems, including directional optimality conditions at infinity, coercivity, compactness of the global solution set, the weak sharp minima property at infinity, and the error bound property at infinity.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
reviews some necessary definitions and preliminary results from variational analysis. In Section
3
, we introduce and study the directional normal cone at infinity, along with the directional limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity. Section
4
is devoted to applications in nonsmooth optimization problems. Finally, Section
5
presents a discussion of problems for future research.

\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\|\cdot\|
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The closed unit ball and the nonnegative orthant in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are denoted, respectively, by
\mathbb{B}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. The closed ball centered at the origin with radius
R>0
is denoted by
\mathbb{B}_{R}
. Let
D

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
D
is

if for any
x\in D
there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
such that
D\cap U
is closed. The interior, the boundary, and the convex hull of
D
are denoted, respectively, by
\mathrm{int}\,D
,
\mathrm{bd}\,D
, and
\mathrm{co}\,D
. For a given point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote the Euclidean projector of
x
onto
D
and the distance from
x
to
D
by
\Pi_{D}(x)
and
\mathrm{dist}(x;D)
(or,
d_{D}(x)
), respectively. As usual,
\mathbb{S}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\|x\|=1\}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is the extended real line,
[\alpha]_{+}:=\max\,\{\alpha,0\}
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. The notation
x\to\infty
means that
\|x\|\to\infty
. The
asymptotic cone

of
D
, denoted by
D^{\infty}
, is defined by

D^{\infty}:=\left\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\exists~t_{k}\rightarrow+\infty,x_{k}\in D,~\frac{x_{k}}{t_{k}}\rightarrow u\right\}.

When
D=\emptyset
, we put
\emptyset^{\infty}:=\emptyset
. It follows from
(
AT03
, Proposition 2.1.2)
that
D
is
bounded
if and only if
D^{\infty}=\{0\}
.

Given an extended real-valued function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. The
effective domain
and the
epigraph
of
f
are denoted, respectively, by

\mbox{dom}\,f:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid f(x)<+\infty\}

{\rm{epi}}\,f:=\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid\alpha\geq f(x)\}.

We say that
f
is

(resp.,
proper at infinity
) if
\mathrm{dom}f
is nonempty (resp.,
\mathrm{dom}f
is unbounded). The function

is called

(l.s.c.) if its epigraph is closed.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a set-valued mapping. The
domain
and the
graph
of
F
are given, respectively, by

{\rm dom}\,F:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid F(x)\not=\emptyset\}

and

{\rm gph}\,F:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid y\in F(x)\}.

The set-valued mapping
F
is called

\mathrm{dom}\,F\not=\emptyset.

For a set-valued map
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the
Painlevé-Kuratowski outer/upper limit
of
F
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}F(x):=\Big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid\exists x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x},y_{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ y_{k}\in F(x_{k})\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}F(x):=\Big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid\exists x_{k}\rightarrow\infty,y_{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ y_{k}\in F(x_{k})\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

2.2
Normal Cones and Subdifferentials

We summarize in this subsection several notions from variational analysis, in particular the concepts of normal cones and subdifferentials, following
Mordukhovich2006
;
Mordukhovich2018
.

Definition 1

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\bar{x}\in\Omega
.

(i)

The
regular/Fréchet normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(\bar{x};\Omega)=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\limsup\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\right\},

where
x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}
means that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
and
x\in\Omega
.

(ii)

The
limiting/Mordukhovich normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is given by

\displaystyle N(\bar{x};\Omega)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\widehat{N}(x;\Omega).

When
\bar{x}\not\in\Omega
, we put
\widehat{N}(\bar{x};\Omega)=N(\bar{x};\Omega):=\emptyset
.

By the above definitions, it is clear that

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(x;\Omega)\subset N(x;\Omega)\ \ \forall x\in\Omega.

Definition 2

Given a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}f
.

(i)

The
regular/Fréchet subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathrm{epi}f)\}.

(ii)

The
limiting/Mordukhovich subdifferential
and the
limiting/Mordukhovich singular subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
are defined, respectively, by

\partial f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in{N}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathrm{epi}f)\},

and

\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists v_{k}\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}),\lambda_{k}\downarrow 0,\lambda_{k}v_{k}\to v\}.

It is well-known that

\partial f(\bar{x})=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{f}\bar{x}}\widehat{\partial}f(x)\supseteq\widehat{\partial}f(x),

where
x\xrightarrow{f}\bar{x}
means that
x\to\bar{x}
and
f(x)\to f(\bar{x})
. For a convex function
f
, the subdifferentials
\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
and
\partial f(\bar{x})
coincide with the subdifferential in the sense of convex analysis. We obtain that

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x})\subseteq\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,0)\in N((\bar{x},f(\bar{x}));\mathrm{epi}f)\},

and the inclusion holds with equality whenever
f
is locally l.s.c. at
\bar{x}
(see
(
Rockafellar1998
, Theorem 8.9)
).

Given a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
indicator function

\delta_{\Omega}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of
\Omega
is defined by

\delta_{\Omega}(x):=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{ if }x\in\Omega,\\
+\infty&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

For any
x\in\Omega
, it holds that
\partial\delta_{\Omega}(x)=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(x)=N(x;\Omega)
(see
Mordukhovich2006
;
Mordukhovich2018
;
Rockafellar1998
).

A nonsmooth versions of Fermat’s rule is stated as follows.

Lemma 1
(see
(
Mordukhovich2006
, Proposition 1.114)
)

If a proper function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
has a local minimum at
\bar{x},
then
0\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})\subset\partial f(\bar{x}).

The following Ekeland variational principle is an useful tool in establishing our main results.

Lemma 2
(see
(
Ekeland-74
, Theorem 1)
)

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper l.s.c. function and bounded from below. Let
\epsilon>0
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be satisfied

\displaystyle f(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)+\epsilon.

Then, for any
\lambda>0
there exists
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(i)

f(v)\leq f(u),

(ii)

\|v-u\|\leq\lambda,
and

(iii)

f(v)\leq f(x)+\dfrac{\epsilon}{\lambda}\|x-v\|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

2.3
Normal Cones and Subdifferentials at Infinity

This subsection provides a brief review of the notions of normal cones and subdifferentials at infinity, as presented in
Kim-Tung-Son-23
.

Definition 3

Let
\Omega
be an unbounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
norm cone to the set
\Omega
at infinity
is defined by

\displaystyle N(\infty;\Omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty}\widehat{N}(x;\Omega),

where
x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty
means that
\|x\|\to\infty
and
x\in\Omega
.

By
(
Kim-Tung-Son-23
, Propositions 3.5 and 3.6)
, we have

\displaystyle N(\infty;\Omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty}{N}(x;\Omega),

and
N(\infty;\Omega)
is nontrivial if and only if
\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega
is unbounded. Furthermore, by definition, it is easy to see that

N(\infty;\Omega)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}N(\infty_{\{i\}};\Omega),

where

N(\infty_{\{i\}};\Omega):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\in\Omega,|x_{i}|\to\infty}\widehat{N}(x;\Omega),\ \ i=1,\ldots,n.

Definition 4

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an l.s.c. and proper at infinity function. The
limiting/Mordukhovich and the singular subdifferentials
of
f
at infinity are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ (u,-1)\in\mathcal{N}\},

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ (u,0)\in\mathcal{N}\},

where
\mathcal{N}:=\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}{N}((x,f(x));\textrm{epi}f).

The next result provides limiting characterizations of both limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity.

Proposition 1
(see
(
Kim-Tung-Son-23
, Proposition 4.4)
)

The following relationships hold

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}\partial f(x)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}\widehat{\partial}f(x),

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty,r\downarrow 0}r\partial f(x)\supseteq\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}\partial^{\infty}f(x).

For an unbounded closed set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it follows from Proposition
1
and
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Proposition 1.19)
that

\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\infty)=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(\infty)=N(\infty;\Omega).

Let us recall the definition of the Lipschitz property at infinity for l.s.c. functions, as introduced in
Kim-Tung-Son-23
;
Tung-Son-24
.

Definition 5

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be an l.s.c. function. We say that
f
is
Lipschitz at infinity
if there exist constants
L>0
and
R>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\|x-x^{\prime}\|\ \ \textrm{ for all }\ \ x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

The following theorem establishes a necessary and sufficient condition for the Lipschitz property at infinity of l.s.c. functions.

Proposition 2
(see
(
Kim-Tung-Son-23
, Proposition 5.2)
)

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be an l.s.c. function. Then
f
is Lipschitz at infinity if and only if
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)=\{0\}.
In this case,
\partial f(\infty)
is nonempty compact.

3
Directional Normal Cone and Subdifferential at Infinity

In this section, we define and study directional normal cones at infinity for unbounded sets, along with directional limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity for extended real-valued functions.

3.1
Directional Normal Cone at Infinity

Let
\Omega
be a locally closed and unbounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{S}
. The directional normal cone at infinity is given by the following definition.

Definition 6

The
normal cone to
\Omega
in direction
u
at infinity
, denoted by
N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
, is defined by

N_{\Omega}(\infty;u):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x),

i.e.,

N_{\Omega}(\infty;u):=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\exists x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty,\tfrac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}\to u,\xi_{k}\in\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x_{k}),\xi_{k}\to\xi\right\}.

By definition, if
u\notin\Omega^{\infty}
, then
N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=\emptyset
. Hence, we only consider the case that
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
.

To highlight the difference between the directional normal cone at infinity and the normal cone at infinity (see
Kim-Tung-Son-23
), we present the example below.

Example 1

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the set defined by

\Omega:=\left\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
,|\,\tfrac{x_{1}}{2}\leq x_{2}\leq 2x_{1},x_{1}\geq 0\right\}.

Let
u=(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}},\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}})
and
v=(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}},\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}})
. Then, we have
u,v\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
, and

\displaystyle N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\,|\,x_{2}=-2x_{1},x_{1}\geq 0\},

\displaystyle N_{\Omega}(\infty;v)

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\,|\,x_{2}=-\tfrac{1}{2}x_{1},x_{1}\leq 0\},

while

N_{\Omega}(\infty)=N_{\Omega}(\infty_{\{1\}})=N_{\Omega}(\infty_{\{2\}})=N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\cup N_{\Omega}(\infty;v).

The relation between the directional normal cone at infinity and the normal cone at infinity is stated below.

Proposition 3

The following relation holds

N_{\Omega}(\infty)=\bigcup_{u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}}N_{\Omega}(\infty;u).

Proof
The inclusion “
\supset
” is directly from the definition. Now, we let any
\xi\in N_{\Omega}(\infty)
. Then by definition, there exist sequences
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty
and
\xi_{k}\in\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x_{k})
with
\xi_{k}\to\xi
as
k\to\infty
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, the sequence
\tfrac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}
converges to some
u\in\mathbb{S}
, taking a subsequence if necessary. Hence,
u\in\Omega^{\infty}
and
\xi\in N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
, as required. ∎

The proposition below offers an essential method to determine
N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
.

Proposition 4

For any
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
, we have

N_{\Omega}(\infty;u):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty}{N}_{\Omega}(x).

Proof
Since
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x)\subset N_{\Omega}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has

N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x)\subset\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty}{N}_{\Omega}(x).

We now take any
\xi\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty}{N}_{\Omega}(x)
. By definition, there exist sequences
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty
,
\xi_{k}\in N_{\Omega}(x_{k})
with
\tfrac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}\to u
and
\xi_{k}\to\xi
. Hence, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there are
z_{k}\in\Omega
and
\eta_{k}\in\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(z_{k})
such that
\|z_{k}-x_{k}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{k}
and
\|\eta_{k}-\xi_{k}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{k}
. Clearly,
z_{k}\to\infty
and
\eta_{k}\to\xi
. We claim that
\tfrac{z_{k}}{\|z_{k}\|}\to u
as
k\to\infty
. Indeed, since
z_{k}\to\infty
and
x_{k}\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{z_{k}}{\|z_{k}\|}-\frac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{\big\|z_{k}\|x_{k}\|-x_{k}\|z_{k}\|\big\|}{\|z_{k}\|\|x_{k}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\big\|z_{k}\|x_{k}\|-x_{k}\|z_{k}\|\big\|}{\|z_{k}\|}

\displaystyle=\left\|z_{k}\frac{\|x_{k}\|}{\|z_{k}\|}-x_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|z_{k}\frac{\|x_{k}\|}{\|z_{k}\|}-z_{k}\right\|+\|z_{k}-x_{k}\|

\displaystyle=|\|x_{k}\|-\|z_{k}\||+\|z_{k}-x_{k}\|\leq 2\|z_{k}-x_{k}\|.

This implies that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|\frac{z_{k}}{\|z_{k}\|}-\frac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}\right\|=0

and so
\tfrac{z_{k}}{\|z_{k}\|}\to u
as
k\to\infty
. Hence,
\xi\in N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
. The proof is complete. ∎

The next proposition characterizes the nontriviality of
N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
.

Proposition 5

Let
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
. Then,
N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\neq\{0\}
if and only if
u\in(\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega)^{\infty}
.

Proof
Suppose that there exists
\xi\in N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\setminus\{0\}
. Then, by Proposition
4
, there exist
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty
and
\xi_{k}\in N_{\Omega}(x_{k})
such that
\xi_{k}\to\xi
. This implies that
\xi_{k}\neq 0
for all
k
large enough. By
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Proposition 1.2)
,
x_{k}\in\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega
for all
k
large enough. This and the fact that
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty
imply that
u\in(\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega)^{\infty}
, as required.

We now assume that
u\in(\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega)^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
. By definition, there exist sequences
x_{k}\in\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega
and
t_{k}\to\infty
such that
\tfrac{x_{k}}{t_{k}}\to u
with
\|u\|=1
. Hence,
\tfrac{\|x_{k}\|}{t_{k}}\to 1
as
k\to\infty
. This implies that

\|x_{k}\|=t_{k}.\tfrac{\|x_{k}\|}{t_{k}}\to\infty
and

\frac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}=\frac{x_{k}}{t_{k}}.\frac{t_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}\to u.

Hence,
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega,u}\infty
. Thus, by
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Proposition 1.2)
, for each
k
there exists
\xi_{k}\in N_{\Omega}(x_{k})
with
\|\xi_{k}\|=1
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\xi_{k}\to\xi
for some
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|\xi\|=1
. Since
x_{k}\in\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega
and
\Omega
is locally closed, it is clear that
x_{k}\in\Omega
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty
. By Proposition
4
,
\xi\in N_{\Omega}(\infty;u).
The proof is complete.∎

The concept of a directional neighborhood at infinity is introduced as follows.

Definition 7

Let
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A
neighborhood of the infinity in direction
u
is defined by

V_{R,\delta}(\infty;u):=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}\,|\,\big\|z\|u\|-u\|z\|\big\|\leq\delta\|z\|\|u\|\Big\}

for some
R>0
and
\delta>0
.

By definition, one has

V_{R,\delta}(\infty;u)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ u=0,\\
\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}\,|\,\left\|\frac{z}{\|z\|}-\frac{u}{\|u\|}\right\|\leq\delta\right\},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ u\neq 0.\end{cases}

The following result provides the computation rule for the directional normal cone at infinity of the intersection of two sets.

Proposition 6

Let
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
be unbounded subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for each
u\in(\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2})^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
, if the following condition is satisfied

N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;u)\cap(-N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;u))=\{0\},

(1)

then

N_{\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}}(\infty;u)\subset N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;u).

(2)

Proof
Firstly, we show that there exists a constant
R>0
satisfying

N_{\Omega_{1}}(x)\cap(-N_{\Omega_{2}}(x))=\{0\}\;\forall x\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}\cap V_{R,\frac{1}{R}}(\infty;u).

(3)

Indeed, on the contrary, suppose that for each
k
large enough, there exist sequence
x_{k}\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}\cap V_{k,\frac{1}{k}}(\infty;u)
and
\xi_{k}\in N_{\Omega_{1}}(x_{k})\cap(-N_{\Omega_{2}}(x_{k}))
such that
\xi_{k}\neq 0
. By the fact that
x_{k}\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}\cap V_{k,\frac{1}{k}}(\infty;u)
, we have

\left\|\frac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}-u\right\|<\frac{1}{k}.

Hence,
x_{k}\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
and
\tfrac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}\to u
as
k\to\infty
. By
(
AT03
, Proposition 2.1.9)
, one has

u\in(\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2})^{\infty}\subset\Omega_{1}^{\infty}\cap\Omega_{2}^{\infty}.

Let
\eta_{k}:=\frac{\xi_{k}}{\|\xi_{k}\|}
. Then,
\eta_{k}\in N_{\Omega_{1}}(x_{k})\cap(-N_{\Omega_{2}}(x_{k}))
and
\|\eta_{k}\|=1
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\eta_{k}\to\eta
with
\|\eta\|=1
. By Proposition
4
, we obtain that

\eta\in N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;u)\cap(-N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;u)),

contrary to (
1
).

To prove (
2
), we let any
\xi\in N_{\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}}(\infty;u)
. From Proposition
4
it follows that there exist sequences
x_{k}\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
and
\xi_{k}\in N_{\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}}(x_{k})
such that
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}~\infty
and
\xi_{k}\to~\xi
. By the fact
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
, assume that
x_{k}\in V_{R,\frac{1}{R}}(\infty;u)
for every
k
large enough. By using (
3
), we obtain that

N_{\Omega_{1}}(x_{k})\cap(-N_{\Omega_{2}}(x_{k}))=\{0\}.

By
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Theorem 2.16)
, we have

N_{\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}}(x_{k})\subset N_{\Omega_{1}}(x_{k})+N_{\Omega_{2}}(x_{k}).

Then, there exist
\eta_{k}\in N_{\Omega_{1}}(x_{k})
and
\zeta_{k}\in N_{\Omega_{2}}(x_{k})
satisfying
\xi_{k}=\eta_{k}+\zeta_{k}
.

Suppose that the sequence
\eta_{k}
is unbounded. Hence, the sequence
\zeta_{k}
is also unbounded. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|\eta_{k}\|\to\infty
and
\|\zeta_{k}\|\to\infty
. By the fact that
\xi_{k}=\eta_{k}+\zeta_{k}\to\xi
, we obtain that
\frac{\eta_{k}}{\|\zeta_{k}\|}
is bounded. By passing to subsequences if necessary, we assume that
\frac{\eta_{k}}{\|\zeta_{k}\|}\to\eta
,
\frac{\zeta_{k}}{\|\zeta_{k}\|}\to\zeta
for some
\eta
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|\zeta\|=1
. Dividing both sides of the equality
\xi_{k}=\eta_{k}+\zeta_{k}
by
\|\zeta_{k}\|
, letting
k\to\infty
, and using Proposition
4
yield

0=\eta+\zeta\in N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;u)\cap(-N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;u))

with
\|\zeta\|=1
, contrary to (
1
). This implies that
\eta_{k}
is bounded, and so is
\zeta_{k}
.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\eta_{k}\to\eta
and
\zeta_{k}\to\zeta
for some
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then,
\eta\in N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;u)
and
\zeta\in N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;u)
by Proposition
4
. Hence,
\xi=\eta+\zeta\in N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;u)
. ∎

Let
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be locally closed and unbounded subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, respectively. The authors in
Kim-Tung-Son-23
proved that

N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty)\times N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty)\subset N_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(\infty).

However, this relation does not hold for directional normal cones at infinity. To illustrate, let us consider the following examples.

Example 2

(a)
Let
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We can check that

N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;1)=\{0\},\;\;N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;0)=\emptyset,

and

N_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(\infty;(1,0))=\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{-}.

Thus
N_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(\infty;(1,0))\nsubseteq N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;1)\times N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;0).

(b)
Let
\Omega_{1}:=\{2^{n}\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\Omega_{2}:=\{3.2^{n}\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. We first claim that
u=(1,1)\notin(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})^{\infty}
. Indeed, if otherwise, then there exist sequences
(2^{n_{k}},3.2^{m_{k}})\in\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
and
t_{k}\to+\infty
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{k}}(2^{n_{k}},3.2^{m_{k}})=(1,1).

It implies that
\lim_{k\to\infty}3.2^{m_{k}-n_{k}}=1
. This is impossible for any
n_{k},m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we have
u=(1,1)\notin(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})^{\infty}
and

N_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(\infty;(1,1))=\emptyset.

It is easy to check that

N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;1)=\mathbb{R}
and
N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;1)=\mathbb{R}
and thus

N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty;1)\times N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty;1)\nsubseteq N_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(\infty;(1,1)).

3.2
Directional Subdifferential at Infinity

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an l.s.c. function and
u\in\mathbb{S}
.
Hereafter
, we assume that
\mathrm{dom}\,f
is unbounded in direction
u
, i.e.,
u\in(\mathrm{dom}\,f)^{\infty}
. The directional subdifferentials at infinity of
f
are introduced through the following definition.

Definition 8

The
limiting
and the
singular subdifferentials of
f
in direction
u
at infinity
are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty;u)

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,(\xi,-1)\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,\,r\geq f(x)}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r)\bigg\},

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,(\xi,0)\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,\,r\geq f(x)}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r)\bigg\}.

Based on the definition, the next proposition can be established.

Proposition 7

For each
u\in\mathbb{S}
, one has

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty;u)

\displaystyle=\{\xi\,|\,(\xi,-1)\in\mathcal{N}_{u}\},

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)

\displaystyle=\{\xi\,|\,(\xi,0)\in\mathcal{N}_{u}\},

where
\mathcal{N}_{u}:=\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,f(x))
.

Proof
By definition, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,f(x))=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,\,r\geq f(x)}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r).

(4)

Indeed, analysis similar to that in the proof of Proposition
4
shows that

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,\,r\geq f(x)}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,\,r\geq f(x)}{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r)\supset\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,f(x)).

On the other hand, for any
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and
r\geq f(x)
, we see that

T_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,f(x))\subset T_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r).

(5)

Indeed, let any
v\in T_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,f(x))
. Then, there exist sequences
t_{k}\downarrow 0
and
(x_{k},r_{k})\to(x,f(x))
such that
(x_{k},r_{k})\in\mathrm{epi}\,f
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tfrac{(x_{k},r_{k})-(x,f(x))}{t_{k}}\to v
as
k\to\infty
. Put
r^{\prime}_{k}:=r+(r_{k}-f(x))
. Then
r^{\prime}_{k}\to r
and

f(x_{k})\leq r_{k}\leq r_{k}+(r-f(x))=r^{\prime}_{k}.

Hence,
(x_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{epi}\,f
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

\frac{(x_{k},r^{\prime}_{k})-(x,r)}{t_{k}}=\frac{(x_{k},r_{k})-(x,f(x))}{t_{k}}\to v

as
k\to\infty
. This means that
v\in T_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r)
, as required. By (
5
), one has

\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r)\subset{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,f(x)).

Hence

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,f(x))\supset\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,\,r\geq f(x)}\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,r)

and so (
4
) is true. The proof is complete.∎

The subsequent result is of central importance for the forthcoming analysis.

Proposition 8

For each
u\in\mathbb{S}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty;u)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}{\partial}f(x)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}\widehat{\partial}f(x),

(6)

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,r\downarrow 0}r{\partial}f(x)\supset\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}{\partial}^{\infty}f(x).

(7)

Proof
To prove (
6
), we need to show that

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}\widehat{\partial}f(x)\subset\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}{\partial}f(x)\subset\partial f(\infty;u)\subset\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}\widehat{\partial}f(x).

The first inclusion follows directly from the fact that
\widehat{\partial}f(x)\subset{\partial}f(x)
for all
x\in~\mathrm{dom}\,f
. To prove the second inclusion, take any
\xi\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}{\partial}f(x)
. Then there exist sequences
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
\xi_{k}\in\partial f(x_{k})
with
\xi_{k}\to\xi
as
k\to\infty
. Hence
(\xi_{k},-1)\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x_{k},f(x_{k}))
. Clearly,
(\xi_{k},-1)\to(\xi,-1)
. Thus,
(\xi,-1)\in\mathcal{N}_{u}
, or, equivalently,
\xi\in\partial f(\infty;u)
. For the third inclusion, let
\xi\in\partial f(\infty;u)
. Then, there exist
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
(\xi_{k},-r_{k})\to(\xi,-1)
with
(\xi_{k},-r_{k})\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x_{k},f(x_{k}))
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By definition of the limiting normal cone, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(\eta_{k},-\alpha_{k})\in\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(z_{k},f(z_{k}))
such that

\|z_{k}-x_{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{k},\|(\eta_{k},-\alpha_{k})-(\xi_{k},-r_{k})\|\leq\frac{1}{k}.

Using an argument similar as in the proof of Proposition
4
, one has
z_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
(\eta_{k},-\alpha_{k})\to(\xi,-1)
. Hence,
\alpha_{k}>0
for all
k
large enough. This implies that
(\tfrac{\eta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}},-1)\in\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(z_{k},f(z_{k}))
and so
\tfrac{\eta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\in\widehat{\partial}f(z_{k})
for all
k
large enough. Clearly,
\tfrac{\eta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\to\xi
as
k\to\infty
. Hence,
\xi\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}\widehat{\partial}f(x)
, as required.

We are now in the position to prove the first equality in (
7
). Take any
\xi\in\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)
. Then, by Proposition
7
, there exist sequences
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
(\xi_{k},-r_{k})\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x_{k},f(x_{k}))
with
(\xi_{k},-r_{k})\to(\xi,0)
. By
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Proposition 1.17)
, one has
r_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If
r_{k}=0
for finitely many
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
r_{k}>0
for all
k
large enough. Then,
(\tfrac{1}{r_{k}}\xi_{k},-1)\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x_{k},f(x_{k}))
for all
k
large enough and

\xi=\lim_{k\to\infty}\xi_{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}\big(\frac{1}{r_{k}}\xi_{k}\big)\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,r\downarrow 0}r{\partial}f(x).

Now, we consider the case where
r_{k}=0
for infinitely many
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By passing to subsequences if necessary, we may assume that
r_{k}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
\xi_{k}\in\partial^{\infty}f(x_{k})
. By definition, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\eta_{k}\in\widehat{\partial}f(z_{k})\subset\partial f(z_{k})
, and
\alpha_{k}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{k})
such that

\|z_{k}-x_{k}\|<\frac{1}{k},\|\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}-\xi_{k}\|<\frac{1}{k}.

This and the fact that
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
imply that
z_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
,
\alpha_{k}\downarrow 0
, and
\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}\to\xi
. Thus
\xi\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,r\downarrow 0}r{\partial}f(x)
, as required.

Conversely, let
\xi\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,r\downarrow 0}r{\partial}f(x)
. Then there exist
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
,
r_{k}\downarrow 0
, and
\xi_{k}\in\partial f(x_{k})
such that
r_{k}\xi_{k}\to\xi
as
k\to\infty
. This implies that
(\xi_{k},-1)\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x_{k},f(x_{k}))
and hence,
(r_{k}\xi_{k},-r_{k})\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x_{k},f(x_{k}))
and
(r_{k}\xi_{k},-r_{k})\to(\xi,0)
with
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
. This means that
(\xi,0)\in\mathcal{N}_{u}
and we therefore get
\xi\in\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)
due to Proposition
7
.

To finish the proof, take any
\xi\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}{\partial}^{\infty}f(x)
. Then there exist
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
\xi_{k}\in{\partial}^{\infty}f(x_{k})
such that
\xi_{k}\to\xi
. Hence, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there are
z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\eta_{k}\in\widehat{\partial}f(z_{k})\subset\partial f(z_{k})
, and
\alpha_{k}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{k})
such that

\|z_{k}-x_{k}\|<\frac{1}{k},\ \ \|\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}-\xi_{k}\|<\frac{1}{k}.

This implies that
z_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
,
\alpha_{k}\downarrow 0
, and
\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}\to\xi
. Thus we obtain that
\xi\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,r\downarrow 0}r{\partial}f(x)
. The proof is complete. ∎

Remark 1

For an unbounded subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\ \ \forall u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}.

The following result is a direct consequence of Propositions
8
and
3
.

Proposition 9

The following assertions hold:

(i)

\partial f(\infty)=\cup_{u\in\mathbb{S}}\,\partial f(\infty;u)
.

(ii)

\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)=\cup_{u\in\mathbb{S}}\,\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)
.

The next result establishes the nonemptiness of directional subdifferentials at infinity.

Proposition 10

\partial f(\infty;u)\cup(\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)\setminus\{0\})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof
We first show that

\mathcal{N}_{u}=\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty}N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,f(x))\neq\{0\}.

Indeed, by the assumption that
f
is l.s.c., we obtain that
\mathrm{epi}\,f

is closed. Since
\mathrm{dom}\,f
is unbounded in direction
u
, there exists
x_{k}\in~\mathrm{dom}\,f
such that
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Proposition 1.2)
, there exists
\xi_{k}\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x_{k},f(x_{k}))
with
\|\xi_{k}\|=1
. Then, there is a subsequence
\xi_{k_{m}}
of
\xi_{k}
such that
\xi_{k_{m}}\to\bar{\xi}
for some
\bar{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|\bar{\xi}\|=1
. This implies that
\bar{\xi}\in\mathcal{N}_{u}
.

The conclusion follows from the definitions of the limiting and the singular subdifferentials at infinity in the direction and Proposition
7
. ∎

The calculus of the limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity in a given direction for the sum of two functions is established as follows.

Proposition 11

Let
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be l.s.c. functions and the following condition is satisfied

\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(\infty;u)\cap(-\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(\infty;u))=\{0\}.

(8)

Then, one has

\partial(f_{1}+f_{2})(\infty;u)\subset\partial f_{1}(\infty;u)+\partial f_{2}(\infty;u)

(9)

and

\partial^{\infty}(f_{1}+f_{2})(\infty;u)\subset\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(\infty;u)+\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(\infty;u).

(10)

Proof
We first prove that there exists a constant
R>0
satisfying

\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(x)\cap(-\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(x))=\{0\}\ \ \forall x\in V_{R,\frac{1}{R}}(\infty;u).

(11)

Indeed, on the contrary, suppose that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
x_{k}\in V_{k,\frac{1}{k}}(\infty;u)
and
\xi_{k}\in\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(x_{k})\cap(-\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(x_{k}))
such that
\xi_{k}\neq 0
. Since
x_{k}\in V_{k,\frac{1}{k}}(\infty;u)
, we have

\left\|\frac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}-u\right\|<\frac{1}{k}.

Hence, one has
\tfrac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}\to u
as
k\to\infty
. Put
\eta_{k}:=\frac{\xi_{k}}{\|\xi_{k}\|}
. Then,
\|\eta_{k}\|=1
and
\eta_{k}\in\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(x_{k})\cap(-\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(x_{k}))
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\eta_{k}\to\eta
with
\|\eta\|=1
. According to Proposition
8
, we have

\eta\in\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(\infty;u)\cap(-\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(\infty;u)),

contrary to (
8
).

Let any
\xi\in\partial(f_{1}+f_{2})(\infty;u)
. By Proposition
8
, there exist
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\xi_{k}\in\partial^{\infty}(f_{1}+f_{2})(x_{k})
such that
\xi_{k}\to\xi
and
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
. By the fact
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
, we can assume that
x_{k}\in V_{R,\frac{1}{R}}(\infty;u)
for every
k
large enough. Using the condition (
11
), we have

\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(x_{k})\cap(-\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(x_{k}))=\{0\}.

By applying
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Theorem 2.19)
, one has
\xi_{k}=\eta_{k}+\zeta_{k}
with
\eta_{k}\in\partial f_{1}(x_{k})
and
\zeta_{k}\in\partial f_{2}(x_{k})
for all
k
large enough. If
\eta_{k}
is unbounded, then
\zeta_{k}
is also unbounded. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|\eta_{k}\|\to\infty
and
\|\zeta_{k}\|\to\infty
. By the fact that
\xi_{k}=\eta_{k}+\zeta_{k}\to\xi
, we obtain that
\frac{\eta_{k}}{\|\zeta_{k}\|}
is bounded. By passing to subsequences if necessary, we assume that
\frac{\eta_{k}}{\|\zeta_{k}\|}\to\eta
,
\frac{\zeta_{k}}{\|\zeta_{k}\|}\to\zeta
for some
\eta
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|\zeta\|=1
. Dividing both sides of the equality
\xi_{k}=\eta_{k}+\zeta_{k}
by
\|\zeta_{k}\|
, letting
k\to\infty
, and using Proposition
8
produce
0=\eta+\zeta\in\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(\infty;u)+\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(\infty;u)
with
\|\zeta\|=1
, which contradicts (
8
). This implies that
\eta_{k}
is bounded, and so is
\zeta_{k}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\eta_{k}\to\eta
and
\zeta_{k}\to\zeta
for some
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then,
\eta\in\partial f_{1}(\infty;u)
and
\zeta\in\partial f_{2}(\infty;u)
by Proposition
8
. Thus,
\xi=\eta+\zeta\in\partial f_{1}(\infty;u)+\partial f_{2}(\infty;u)
. Therefore, the inclusion (
9
) is proven.
The proof of the inclusion (
10
) is similar and so is omitted. ∎

The following result presents the calculus of the limiting and the singular subdifferentials at infinity in a given direction for the maximum of two functions.

Proposition 12

Let
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be l.s.c. functions and assume that condition (
8
) is satisfied.
Then, one has

\partial\big(\max\{f_{1},f_{2}\}\big)(\infty;u)\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in[0,1],\\
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1\end{subarray}}\big\{\lambda_{1}\circ\partial f_{1}(\infty;u)+\lambda_{2}\circ\partial f_{2}(\infty;u)\big\}

(12)

and

(13)

Proof

From Proposition

We now present formulae to estimate the directional partial subdifferentials at infinity of two variables functions. Let
u\in\mathbb{S}

3.3
Directional Lipschitzness at Infinity

This subsection provides a characterization of directional Lipschitzness at infinity via directional subdifferentials at infinity.

Definition 9

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued function and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
f
is
Lipschitz at infinity in direction
u
if there exist
L>0
,
R>0
, and
\delta>0
such that

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq L\|x-y\|\ \ \forall x,y\in V_{R,\delta}(\infty;u).

A necessary and sufficient condition for directional Lipschitzness at infinity is given below.

Proposition 15

The function
f
is Lipschitz at infinity in direction
u
if and only if
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)=\{0\}
. In this case, the set
\partial f(\infty;u)
is nonempty and compact.

Proof
Assume that
f
is Lipschitz at infinity in direction
u
. Then there exist
L>0
,
R>0
, and
\delta>0
such that

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq L\|x-y\|\ \ \forall x,y\in V_{R,\delta}(\infty;u).

Let
\xi\in\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)
. By Proposition
8
, there exist
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
,
\xi_{k}\in\partial f(x_{k})
, and
r_{k}\downarrow 0
such that
r_{k}\xi_{k}\to\xi
as
k\to\infty
. Since
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{k}\in V_{R,\tfrac{\delta}{2}}(\infty;u)
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. This means that
x_{k}\in\mathrm{int}\,V_{R,\delta}(\infty;u)
and so
f
is locally Lipschitz around
x_{k}
with constant
L
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. By
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Theorem 1.22)
,
\|\xi_{k}\|\leq L
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. This implies that
r_{k}\xi_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
and so
\xi=0
. Hence,
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)=\{0\}
.

We now assume that
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)=\{0\}
. Then there exist
L>0
,
R>0
, and
\delta>0
such that for all
x\in V_{R,\delta}(\infty;u)
and
\xi\in\partial f(x)
,
\|\xi\|\leq L
. Indeed, if otherwise, then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
x_{k}\in V_{k,\tfrac{1}{k}}(\infty;u)
and
\xi_{k}\in\partial f(x_{k})
such that
\|\xi_{k}\|>k
. Hence,
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
,
\|\xi_{k}\|\to\infty
, and
\tfrac{\xi_{k}}{\|\xi_{k}\|}\to\xi
with
\|\xi\|=1
(by passing to a subsequence if necessary). By Proposition
8
,
\xi\in\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)\setminus\{0\}
, a contradiction.

Now, by
(
Whitney
, Theorem I)
, there exists a
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
satisfying

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}1,\ \ &\text{if}\ \ \|x\|\geq 3R,\\
0,\ \ &\text{if}\ \ \|x\|\leq 2R.\end{cases}

Let
\bar{f}
be the function defined by
\bar{f}(x)=f(x)\varphi(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is easy to check that
\bar{f}
is locally Lipschitz on

V_{3R,\delta}(u):=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{B}_{3R}\,|\,\big\|z\|u\|-u\|z\|\big\|\leq\delta\|z\|\|u\|\Big\}.

Hence,
\bar{f}
is globally Lipschitz on
V_{3R,\delta}(u)
. By increasing
L
if necessary, assume that
\bar{f}
is locally Lipschitz with constant
L
on
V_{\delta}(u):=V_{3R,\delta}(u)\cup V_{3R,\delta}(\infty;u)
. Hence, by
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Theorem 1.22)
,
\|\xi\|\leq L
for all
\xi\in\partial\bar{f}(x)
and all
x\in V_{\tfrac{\delta}{2}}(u)
. By the mean value theorem
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Corollary 4.14)
, one has

|\bar{f}(x)-\bar{f}(y)|\leq\|x-y\|\sup\{\|\xi\|\,|\,\xi\in\partial\bar{f}(u),u\in[x,y]\}\leq L\|x-y\|

for all
x,y\in V_{\tfrac{\delta}{2}}(u)
. Hence,

|f(x)-f(y)|=|\bar{f}(x)-\bar{f}(y)|\leq L\|x-y\|\ \ \forall x,y\in V_{3R,\tfrac{\delta}{2}}(u)

and so
f
is Lipschitz at infinity in direction
u
.

We now prove that if
f
is Lipschitz at infinity in direction
u
, then
\partial f(\infty;u)
is nonempty and compact. Indeed, the nonemptyness of
\partial f(\infty;u)
follows directly from Proposition
10
and the fact that
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)=\{0\}
. The closedness of
\partial f(\infty;u)
is obvious from the definition. Let any
\xi\in\partial f(\infty;u)
. Then by definition, there exist
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
\xi_{k}\in\partial f(x_{k})
with
\xi_{k}\to\xi
. Since
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
,
x_{k}\in V_{3R,\tfrac{\delta}{2}}(u)
for all
k
large enough. By the Lipschitzness at infinity in direction
u
with constant
L
of
f
, we have
\|\xi_{k}\|\leq L
for all
k
large enough. Hence,
\|\xi\|\leq L
and so
\partial f(\infty;u)
is compact. ∎

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty
closed set and let
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then the distance function
d_{\Omega}(\cdot)
is globally Lipschitz with constant
1
. Applying Proposition
15
, we obtain that
\partial^{\infty}d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=\{0\}
and the set
\partial d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
is nonempty and compact. We derive below the explicit formula for
\partial d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
.

Proposition 16

It holds that

\partial d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=\begin{cases}(N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\cap\mathbb{B})\cup\mathbb{E},\ \ &\text{if}\ \Omega\text{ is unbounded,}\\
\{u\},\ &\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where

\mathbb{E}:=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{u}\infty,\ x\notin\Omega}\frac{x-\Pi_{\Omega}(x)}{d_{\Omega}(x)}.

Proof
We first consider the case where
\Omega
is bounded.
Let any
\xi\in\partial d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
. Then, there exist
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
\xi_{k}\to\xi
such that
\xi_{k}\in\partial d_{\Omega}(x_{k})
. By the boundedness of
\Omega
, we can assume that
x_{k}\notin\Omega
for every
k
. Applying
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Theorem 1.33)
, for each
k
, we have

\partial d_{\Omega}(x_{k})=\frac{x_{k}-\Pi_{\Omega}(x_{k})}{d_{\Omega}(x_{k})}.

Then, there exists
\bar{x}_{k}\in\Pi_{\Omega}(x_{k})
such that
\xi_{k}=\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|}
. Since
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
\Omega
is bounded, one has
\xi_{k}\to u
as
k\to\infty
. This follows that
\xi=u
and so
\partial d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\subset\{u\}
.

Since the distance function
d_{\Omega}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
x\mapsto\inf_{y\in\Omega}\|x-y\|
, is globally Lipschitz with constant
1
,
\partial d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)
is nonempty and compact due to Proposition

\partial d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=\{u\}
.

Now, we consider the case where
\Omega
is unbounded. According to
(
Mordukhovich2018

, we have

\partial d_{\Omega}(x)=\begin{cases}N_{\Omega}(x)\cap\mathbb{B},\ \ &\text{if}\ x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{x-\Pi_{\Omega}(x)}{d_{\Omega}(x)},\ &\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By applying Proposition
8
, we obtain that

\partial d_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=(N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\cap\mathbb{B})\cup\mathbb{E}.

The proof is complete. ∎

The following result characterizes the directional normal cone at infinity for a set defined by equality and inequality constraints.

Let

and

u

∞

Thus, by an argument similar to that in the proof of

(20)

and so by Proposition

\displaystyle N_{S}(\infty

4
Applications

In this section, employing directional normal cones at infinity together with directional limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity, we develop several applications, including directional optimality conditions at infinity, coercivity, compactness of the global solution set, weak sharp minima at infinity, and error bounds at infinity.

4.1
Directional Optimality Conditions at Infinity

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an l.s.c. function and
\Omega
be a nonempty and closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We consider the following general optimization problem

\min_{x\in\Omega}f(x).

(P)

To present a necessary optimality condition at infinity for the problem (
P
), we always assume that:

(A
1
)
\mathrm{dom}f\cap\Omega
is unbounded.

(A
2
)
f
is bounded from below on
\Omega,
i.e.,
f_{*}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}f(x)
is finite.

The following provides a necessary optimality condition at infinity for problem (
P
) in a given asymptotic direction of the feasible set.

Theorem 4.1

Let
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
. Assume that the following condition holds:

\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)\cap\big(-N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\big)=\{0\}.

(21)

If there exists a sequence
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty
such that
f(x_{k})\to f_{*}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}f(x)
,
then

0\in\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u).

Proof
We first consider the case that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)=\{0\}.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{*}\leq f(x_{k})\leq f_{*}+\left(f(x_{k})-f_{*}+\frac{1}{k}\right).

Clearly,
\epsilon_{k}:=f(x_{k})-f_{*}+\frac{1}{k}>0
and
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Put
\lambda_{k}:=\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}}
, then by the Ekeland variational principle (Lemma
2
), there exists
z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
k>0
such that

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-z_{k}\|\leq\lambda_{k},

\displaystyle f(z_{k})\leq f(x)+\lambda_{k}\|x-z_{k}\|\quad\textrm{for all }\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The first inequality and the fact that
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
imply that
z_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
. While the second inequality says that
z_{k}
is a global minimizer of
\varphi(\cdot):=f(\cdot)+\lambda_{k}\|\cdot-z_{k}\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By the Fermat rule (see Lemma
1
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial\left(f(\cdot)+\lambda_{k}\|\cdot-z_{k}\|\right)(z_{k}).

By the Lipschitzness of the function
\|\cdot-z_{k}\|
and the sum rule (see
(
Mordukhovich2018
, Theorem 2.19)
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in\partial f(z_{k})+\lambda_{k}\partial(\|\cdot-z_{k}\|)(z_{k})

\displaystyle=\partial f(z_{k})+\lambda_{k}\mathbb{B}

due to the fact that
\partial(\|\cdot-z_{k}\|)(z_{k})=\mathbb{B}
. Hence,

0\in\partial f(z_{k})+\lambda_{k}\mathbb{B},

and so there is
\xi_{k}\in\partial f(z_{k})
such that
\|\xi_{k}\|\leq\lambda_{k}
. Since
\lambda_{k}\to 0
, by letting
k\to\infty
and applying Proposition
8
, we obtain
0\in\partial f(\infty;u).

We now consider the case where
\Omega
is an arbitrary subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We have

\displaystyle f_{*}=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f+\delta_{\Omega}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}f(x)\ >\ -\infty,

where
\delta_{\Omega}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
stands for the indicator function of the set
\Omega.
Clearly
f(x_{k})+\delta_{\Omega}(x_{k})\to f_{*}
. Therefore,
0\in\partial(f+\delta_{\Omega})(\infty;u)
(by the argument employed in the first case). This, together with Proposition
11
and (
21
), yields

0\in\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u).

The proof is complete.∎

Remark 2

By Propositions
5
and
15
, condition (
21
) holds automatically when
f
is Lipschitz at infinity in direction
u
, or when
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\setminus(\mathrm{bd}\,\Omega)^{\infty}
.

In connection with Theorem
4.1
, the following natural question arises:

(Q)

If condition (

0\notin\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)

(22)

\delta>0

\mathrm{argmin}\,_{x\in\Omega\cap V_{\delta}(u)}f(x)\neq\emptyset

Example 3

Let

for all

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)=0
,
\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)=\emptyset
, and

0\in\partial f(\infty)=\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}.

Let
u=(1,0)
, then an easy computation shows that

and

. Hence, conditions (

V_{\delta}(u)=\mathrm{cone}\,\mathbb{B}((1,0),\delta),\ \ \inf_{V_{\delta}(u)}f(x)=0,

\mathrm{argmin}\,_{x\in V_{\delta}(u)}f(x)=\emptyset

The following results provide sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions to (
P
) over the feasible set along with a given asymptotic direction.

Corollary 1

Let
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega\cap\mathrm{dom}\,f
. If the following conditions hold

N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\cap u^{\perp}=\{0\},\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)\cap[-N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)+u^{\perp}]=\{0\},

and

0\notin\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)+u^{\perp},

then we have
\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\Omega_{u}}\,f(x)
is nonempty, where

\Omega_{u}:=\Omega\cap[\bar{x}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{u\}].

Proof
Since
f
is bounded from below on
\Omega
and
\Omega_{u}\subset\Omega
,
f_{*}^{u}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega_{u}}f(x)
is finite. If
\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\Omega_{u}}\,f(x)
is empty, then it follows from Theorem
4.1
that
0\in\partial(f+\delta_{\Omega_{u}})(\infty;u)
. On the other hand, since
N_{\bar{x}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{u\}}(\infty;u)=u^{\perp}
and
N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\cap u^{\perp}=\{0\}
, we get

N_{\Omega_{u}}(\infty;u)\subset N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)+u^{\perp}

due to Proposition
6
. This and the fact that

\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)\cap[-N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)+u^{\perp}]=\{0\}

imply that
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)\cap[-N_{\Omega_{u}}(\infty;u)]=\{0\}
. Thus, by Proposition
11
, we see that

0\in\partial(f+\delta_{\Omega_{u}})(\infty;u)\subset\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega_{u}}(\infty;u)\subset\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)+u^{\perp},

a contradiction. The proof is complete. ∎

Corollary 2

If
\Omega
is convex,
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
, and the following conditions hold

\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)\cap u^{\perp}=\{0\},\partial f(\infty;u)\cap u^{\perp}=\emptyset,

(23)

then for any
\bar{x}\in\Omega\cap\mathrm{dom}\,f
, the set
\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\bar{x}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{u\}}\,f(x)
is nonempty.

Proof
Let any
\bar{x}\in\Omega\cap\mathrm{dom}\,f
. By the convexity of
\Omega
, one has
\bar{x}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{u\}\subset\Omega
. If
\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\bar{x}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{u\}}\,f(x)=\emptyset
, then by Theorem
4.1
, one has

0\in\partial(f+\delta_{\bar{x}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{u\}})(\infty;u).

This and (
23
) imply that
0\in\partial f(\infty;u)+u^{\perp}.
Hence,
\partial f(\infty;u)\cap u^{\perp}
is nonempty, a contradiction. The proof is complete. ∎

f

\Omega

\partial f(\infty;u)=\emptyset

\partial^{\infty}f(\infty;u)=\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}

The nonemptiness and compactness of the global solution, coercivity, and weak sharp minima at infinity are investigated in the following result.

Theorem 4.2

Assume that (
21
) and the following condition

0\notin\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)

(24)

hold for all
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
, then the following assertions hold:

(i)

Sol
(
P
)
is nonempty and compact.

(ii)

Problem (
P
) has a weak sharp minimum at infinity, i.e.,
there exist
c>0
and
R>0
such that

f(x)-f_{*}\geq c\,\mathrm{dist}\,(x,\mbox{\rm Sol}\eqref{problem-0})\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

(iii)

f
is coercive on
\Omega
.

Proof
Let
\tilde{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be the function defined by
\tilde{f}(x):=(f+\delta_{\Omega})(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\Omega}f(x)=\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{f}(x)
. By Proposition
9
, one has

\partial\tilde{f}(\infty)=\bigcup_{u\in\mathbb{S}}\partial\tilde{f}(\infty;u).

Furthermore, it follows from Proposition
8
that if
\partial\tilde{f}(\infty;u)
is nonempty, then
u\in\Omega^{\infty}
. Indeed, if there exists
\xi\in\partial\tilde{f}(\infty;u)
, then we can find sequences
x_{k}\xrightarrow{u}\infty
and
\xi_{k}\in\partial(f+\delta_{\Omega})(x_{k})
with
\xi_{k}\to\xi
. Hence,
x_{k}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f\cap\Omega
. This implies that
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega,u}\infty
and so
u\in\Omega^{\infty}
. Thus

\partial\tilde{f}(\infty)=\bigcup_{u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}}\partial\tilde{f}(\infty;u).

Since condition (
21
) holds for all
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
, one has

\partial\tilde{f}(\infty;u)\subset\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\ \ \forall u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}.

Hence,

\partial\tilde{f}(\infty)\subset\bigcup_{u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}}[\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)].

This and condition (
24
) imply that
0\notin\partial\tilde{f}(\infty)
and conclusions of the theorem follow directly from
(
Kim-Tung-Son-23
, Theorem 6.4)
. ∎

The following example demonstrates an application of Theorem
4.2
and indicates that
(
Kim-Tung-Son-23
, Theorem 6.4)

Example 5

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,
x\mapsto e^{-x_{1}}+(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}
for all
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
\Omega:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,x_{1}=0\}
. Then we have

\nabla f(x)=\big(-e^{-x_{1}}-2(x_{2}-x_{1}),2(x_{2}-x_{1})\big)\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

For each
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we see that

\nabla f(k-\tfrac{r}{2},k)\to(r,-r)

as
k\to\infty
. Hence,
\partial f(\infty)=\{(r,-r)\,|\,r\in\mathbb{R}\}
and so
0\in\partial f(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty)
. This means that
(
Kim-Tung-Son-23
, Theorem 6.4)
cannot be employed for this example. We now use our Theorem
4.2
to show that
\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\Omega}\,f(x)
is nonempty and compact, and problem (
P
) has a weak sharp minimum at infinity. Indeed, it is easy to see that
\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}=\{(0,\pm 1)\}
. For
u=(0,1)
, if
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\partial f(\infty;u)
, then there exists sequences
x_{k}=(x_{1k},x_{2k})\xrightarrow{u}\infty
with
\nabla f(x_{k})\to\xi

x_{2k}\to+\infty

\tfrac{x_{1k}}{x_{2k}}\to 0

x_{2k}-x_{1k}\to\tfrac{\xi_{2}}{2}

\partial f(\infty;u)=\emptyset

\partial f(\infty;u)=\emptyset

0\notin\partial f(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u)\ \ \forall u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S},

4.2

4.2
Error Bound at Infinity

Let
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an l.s.c. function and
\Omega
be a nonempty and closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the constraint set

S:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;g(x)\leq 0\}.

(25)

Assume that
\Omega\cap\mathrm{dom}\,g
is unbounded and
S
is nonempty.

Definition 10

We say that the constraint set
S
has an
error bound at infinity
if there exist
\alpha>0
and
R>0
such that

\mathrm{dist}\,(x;S)\leq\alpha[g(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

(26)

A following result presents a sufficient condition for the existence of error bounds at infinity for the constraint set
S
.

Theorem 4.3

Let

S
be as in (
25
). Assume that for all
u\in\Omega^{\infty}\cap\mathbb{S}
the following conditions are satisfied:

\partial^{\infty}g(\infty;u)\cap(-N_{\Omega}(\infty;u))=\{0\}

and

0\notin\partial g(\infty;u)+N_{\Omega}(\infty;u).

Then,
S
has an error bound at infinity.

Proof
Let
\tilde{g}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be the function defined by
\tilde{g}(x):=g(x)+\delta_{\Omega}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

S=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;|\;\tilde{g}(x)\leq 0\}

and condition (
26
) is equivalent to

\mathrm{dist}\,(x,S)\leq\alpha[\tilde{g}(x)]_{+}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

(27)

Analysis similar to that in the proof of Theorem
4.2
shows that
0\notin\partial\tilde{g}(\infty)
. This and
(
Tuyen-24
, Theorem 3.2)
imply that there exist
\alpha>0
and
R>0
satisfying (
27
). This means that
S
has an error bound at infinity. ∎

The example below shows an application of Theorem
4.3
and makes clear that
(
Tuyen-24
, Theorem 3.2)
cannot be employed in this context.

Example 6

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}_{+}

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we introduced and studied directional normal cones at infinity together with directional limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity. We established calculus rules for these concepts and demonstrated their usefulness in nonsmooth optimization through several applications, including directional optimality conditions at infinity, coercivity of the objective function, compactness of the global solution set, and properties such as weak sharp minima and error bounds at infinity. Illustrative examples were provided to highlight the effectiveness of the proposed framework and to compare it with the existing results in
Kim-Tung-Son-23
;
HA-Hung-25
.

We see the following natural directions for future developments in the theory of directionally generalized differentiation at infinity.

1. Motivated by the recent work of Kim et al.
KPTT-23
, one should consider developing a version of directional coderivatives at infinity for set-valued mappings.

2. Using this aid together with the works
Gfrerer-13
;
Gfrerer-132
;
Tung-Son
, one can establish criteria at infinity for the directional well-posedness properties of set-valued mappings.

To Evaluate the Efficacy of Sonic, Ultrasonic, and Manual Irrigation Using Side-Vented Needles for Removal of Metapex Using Different Irrigating Solutions
Authors: Trina Mukherjee, Deepak Kurup, Shazia Mahreen, Hetal Khuva, Sourav Tikadar, Hrishita Majumder
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjective:To evaluate the efficacy of sonic, ultrasonic, and manual irrigation using a side-vented needle for the removal of Metapex with different irrigating solutions.Materials and Methods:Forty-five extracted mandibular premolars were decoronated at 12 mm, and biomechanical preparation was performed up to F2 with thorough irrigation using 5.25% NaOCl between files. Metapex was introduced into the canals, and a precleaning cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) scan was conducted for volumetric analysis of Metapex in the coronal, middle, and apical thirds. Samples were thermocycled for 14 days and then divided into three groups: manual dynamic activation, ultrasonic activation, and sonic activation. After Metapex removal, postcleaning CBCT scans were taken for volumetric analysis. The mean pre- and postcleaning Metapex volumes were calculated for each group and canal third. Statistical analysis was conducted using analysis of variance, with significant results further analyzed by Tukey’s post hoc test (P < 0.05).Results:The mean residual volumes were 79.85, 97.70, and 96.96 in the manual, ultrasonic, and sonic groups, respectively. Post hoc analysis revealed significant differences between the manual and both ultrasonic and sonic groups, with no significant difference between the ultrasonic and sonic groups.Conclusion:Ultrasonic activation is the most efficient method for Metapex removal from root canals.

INTRODUCTION

The primary goal of endodontic treatment is the complete disinfection of the root canal as microorganisms play a fundamental role in the pathogenesis of periradicular diseases. To eliminate residual microbes after root canal preparation and to prevent interappointment reinfection, intracanal medicaments are recommended. Metapex has been widely used as an intracanal medicament due to its antibacterial properties and biocompatibility.[1]

Before obturation, it is crucial to completely remove the intracanal medicament to prevent apical leakage and improve sealer adaptation.[2] Various methods have been employed, but none have achieved complete removal of the intracanal medicament.[2] Effective irrigation requires direct contact of the irrigant with the entire canal wall, which conventional irrigation methods often fail to accomplish. A side-vented needle eliminates hydraulic pressure and ensures safe delivery of the solution.

Both ultrasonic activation and sonic activation utilize the principles of cavitation and acoustic streaming but operate at different frequencies. Sonic activation uses a low frequency of 5000 to 6000 Hz, while ultrasonic activation operates at a higher frequency of 45,000 Hz.[3] The purpose of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of different irrigation systems in removing Metapex, enabling the selection of the most effective system for clinical success.

MATERIALS AND METHODOLOGY

Specimen preparation

Forty-five extracted single-rooted mandibular premolars with single canals, free of cracks, caries, previous restorations, or prior endodontic treatment, were selected for this study.

The selected teeth were measured from the root apex to 12 mm using a vernier caliper, marked, and decoronated at this length with a diamond disk under a water coolant. All samples were stored in 0.9% sterile saline at room temperature until testing.

Biomechanical preparation was performed using rotary ProTaper files (Dentsply Maillefer, Switzerland) up to F2. Thorough irrigation with 5.25% NaOCl was conducted between each file using a conventional needle. The biomechanical preparation was assessed radiographically, and the canals were dried with paper points.

Metapex application

Following biomechanical preparation, the apical foramen was sealed with wax externally, and the canals were filled with Metapex (MetaBiomed, Seoul, Korea) using a propylene tip, starting from the apical aspect and advancing coronally until the paste was visible at the canal orifice. Digital periapical radiographs confirmed the homogeneous filling of the canals. The access cavity was sealed with a cotton pellet and Cavit temporary restoration (3M ESPE, Seefeld, USA). The samples were incubated at 37.8°C for 14 days to mimic the oral environment. After Metapex placement, cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) was used to measure the volume of Metapex in the coronal, middle, and apical thirds.

Experimental groups

The samples were randomly divided into three groups based on the irrigation system used for Metapex removal:

Before irrigation, Metapex was loosened using a 15 K file. Final irrigation for each sample involved 3 mL of 5.25% NaOCl and 3 mL of 17% ethylene diamine tetra-acetic acid (EDTA), followed by 3 mL of normal saline.

Calculation of Metapex removal

Residual Metapex volume was calculated using the formula: Residual Volume (%) = (1 − (Volume of remaining Metapex/Volume of root canal) × 100 (1 − (Volume of remaining Metapex/Volume of root canal) × 100 (1 − (Volume of remaining Metapex/ Volume of root canal) × 100. This formula calculates the percentage of Metapex left in the root canal after treatment.

The data were statistically analyzed using analysis of variance and post hoc tests.

Discussion

The removal of Metapex as an intracanal medicament has been extensively studied using various irrigants and techniques. Common irrigants like saline, sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl), EDTA, EDTA with a cationic detergent (EDTA-T), and citric acid have been utilized.[1] However, due to the canal irregularities, intracanal medicaments may remain inaccessible to conventional irrigation procedures. To enhance the irrigation phase and improve the efficacy of intracanal medicament removal, techniques such as sonic and ultrasonic activation have been recommended. The main irrigating systems include side-vented needles, ultrasonic activation, and sonic activation. These systems can be broadly categorized into manual agitation techniques, such as those using needles and machine-assisted agitation devices.[23] Ultrasonic activation and sonic activation, which utilize the principles of cavitation and acoustic streaming at different frequencies, have been shown to be more effective than conventional methods.

In this study, the efficacy of manual irrigation using a side-vented needle, as well as ultrasonic and sonic irrigation, was evaluated in removing Metapex from the root canal using CBCT. Unlike scanning electron microscopy, which analyzes the surface area and requires tooth sectioning that may cause material loss, CBCT enables volumetric analysis without the need for sectioning. The results revealed that the removal volumes of Metapex were statistically significant across all groups (P < 0.005). Both ultrasonic irrigation and sonic irrigation were more effective in removing Metapex compared to manual irrigation with a side-vented needle. Ultrasonic irrigation achieved the highest removal, likely due to its higher frequency, which enhances cavitation and acoustic streaming. These findings align with studies by various authors.[456]

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of this in vitro study, it was found that none of the methods could achieve complete removal of Metapex on their own. Ultrasonic irrigation was the most effective, followed by sonic and manual irrigation, with no significant difference between ultrasonic and sonic methods. However, both ultrasonic and sonic techniques were significantly more effective than manual irrigation with a side-vented needle. While ultrasonic irrigation appears to be the most efficient for Metapex removal from root canals, further studies with larger sample sizes and clinical trials are needed to confirm these findings.

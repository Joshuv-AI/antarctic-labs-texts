Linear and Nonlinear Boundary Conditions: What’s the difference?
Jan Nordström

Abstract
In previous work, we derived new energy and entropy stable open boundary conditions and implementation procedures for linear and nonlinear initial boundary value problems. These boundary procedures results in estimates bounded by external data only. Interestingly, these new boundary conditions generalize the well-known classical characteristic boundary conditions for linear problems to the nonlinear setting. We discuss the similarities and differences between these two boundary procedures and point out the advantages with the new procedures. In particular we show that the new boundary conditions bound both linear and nonlinear initial boundary value problems and can be implemented both strongly and weakly.

Energy and entropy stability for discretized initial boundary value problems (IBVPs) require boundary procedures at open boundaries for the continuous IBVPs that bounds the surface terms in external data.
For linear hyperbolic IBVPs, a common boundary procedure is based on eigenvalue and eigenvector decomposition of the boundary term where one specifies the ingoing characteristic variables in terms of the boundary data (and possibly outgoing ones)

Analysis of a scalar IBVP

To introduce the main idea in a simple setting, we consider the following scalar skew-symmetric IBVP

u_{t}+(au)_{x}+au_{x}=0,\quad t\geq 0,\quad x\geq 0,

(2.1)

(2.2)

In (

We only consider the boundary

Remark 2.1

With a slight abuse of notation we denote both energy and mathematical entropy as "energy".

By applying the energy method (multiply the equation with

and integrate-by-parts) to (

obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}-(au^{2})_{x=0}=0,

(2.3)

where
\|u\|^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}dx
. The skew-symmetry of (
2.1
) leaves the energy rate to be determined by the boundary terms only.
Note that specifying

matter, i.e.
the complete boundary term must be considered when constructing the boundary operator

Boundary conditions and implementation procedures

We will consider three types of boundary conditions (

2.1.1
Strong implementation

A strong implementation of the first condition

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}=ag^{2}

(2.4)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}=ug

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}=g^{2},

Remark 2.2

Note that the boundary condition

2.1.2
Weak implementation

Following

Equation (

By once more applying the energy method, now to (

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}-au^{2}+2\sigma u(b(u)-g)=0.

Since the only boundary conditions that lead to an estimate for strong imposition was the non-conventional boundary condition

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}=au^{2}-2\sqrt{a}u(\sqrt{a}u-g)=-(\sqrt{a}u)^{2}+2(\sqrt{a}u)g=g^{2}-(\sqrt{a}u-g)^{2}

In the upcoming analysis for nonlinear systems of equations we have boundary terms of the form

Controlling the coefficient

3.2.1
Strong implementation

We will consider two classical characteristic boundary conditions for linear problems but now applied in nonlinear setting. They will be compared with the new non-conventional characteristic boundary condition.

Firstly we specify the ingoing characteristic variables
W^{-}-G=0

U^{T}AU=W^{T}\Lambda W=W_{+}^{T}\Lambda^{+}W_{+}+G^{T}\Lambda^{-}G\geq-G^{T}|\Lambda^{-}|G.

The relation (

Secondly, we

As in the scalar case (

Finally we investigate the new non-conventional choice

4
Summary and conclusions

We discussed similarities and differences between linear and nonlinear open boundary conditions. In particular, we compared the classical linear characteristic boundary conditions with new nonlinear ones. It was shown that the classical characteristic boundary conditions do not lead to energy or entropy estimates while the new ones do. The main difference from the old linear characteristic conditions is that the new characteristic variables are scaled with the square root of the eigenvalues . The new nonlinear characteristic boundary conditions are stable for both for linear and nonlinear problems.

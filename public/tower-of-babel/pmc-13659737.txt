MRS2339, a cardiac P2X4 receptor nucleotide activator, improves systolic function in heart failure
Authors: Ronghua Yang, Chunxia Cronin, Khaled Qanud, T Santhosh Kumar, Thomas Hintze, Fabio A Recchia, Stanko S Stojilkovic, Claudio Coddou, Kenneth A Jacobson, Bruce T Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGrowing evidence is highlighting purinergic receptors as therapeutic targets in cardiac diseases. MRS2339, a charged (N)-methanocarba derivative of 2-Cl-adenosine monophosphate, is efficacious in multiple animal models of systolic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction following infarction, pressure overload, calsequestrin overexpression, rapid pacing, and other injuries. In this study, we explored the beneficial mechanisms of this nucleotide analog in dogs with rapid pacing-induced systolic heart failure and in mice with postinfarct ischemic heart failure. In dogs, sustained infusion of the nucleotide analog increased stroke volume, cardiac output, arterial blood pressure, and arterial oxygen level without any vascular effects, suggesting direct cardiac effects of the drug. In the murine model, MRS2339 caused an increase in myocardial cyclic GMP level, a known cardioprotective second messenger in wild type mice, but no effects in P2X4 receptor knockout mice. Previous data showed that MRS2339 was able to elicit a current in adult mouse cardiomyocytes. The present data showed that MRS2339 could not stimulate a current through rat homomeric P2X4 receptors expressed in HEK293 cells. While the composition of endogenous cardiomyocyte P2X receptors is not known, P2X4 receptors in the heart appear necessary for MRS2339-induced cyclic GMP increase. Our findings support a novel cardiac P2X 4 receptor-mediated cyclic GMP enhancement by MRS2339 as a cellular mechanism of action in heart failure. Stimulating cardiac P2X receptors with increased cyclic GMP level to yield hemodynamic improvement is a potential new therapeutic target in heart failure. Keywords: P2X Purinergic receptors, MRS2339, Cardiac output, Heart failure

Despite advances in treating patients with heart failure (HF) with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), also known as systolic HF, new medical therapy is needed, particularly for those with severe or advanced HFrEF [1–4]. Advanced HF is considered an unstable condition in which the standard medical treatment is, by definition, insufficient. The annual mortality with current medical therapy alone remains unacceptably high at 30–80% and the 6-month readmission rate is 61–80% for patients in INTERMACS 4–7, which are those with Class IV heart failure who are not yet in need of a cardiac mechanical support device or cardiac transplant [4]. Purinergic P2 receptors mediate a number of important biological functions and are capturing growing interest as targets for new therapeutic agents [5–11]. In an effort to develop a new treatment for severe systolic HF, we previously showed that a 2-chloro, methanocarba-modified adenine nucleotide (MRS2339) can activate a cardiac myocyte nonselective cation channel [5]. The channel characteristics are similar to those of a P2X receptor, which is an ATP-gated ion channel and a member of the P2 purinergic receptor subfamily. MRS2339 is a ribose-modified nucleotide; it contains a bicyclo[3.1.0] hexane group (i.e., a “North (N)-methanocarba” modification) in place of the five-membered ring of native ribose [10]. This modification can increase affinity for purine receptors, including representative adenosine (A3), P2Y (P2Y1) and P2X receptors, compared to native ribose, by pre-establishing a receptor-preferred conformation of the ring.

Systemic infusion of MRS2339 can improve left ventricular function in several murine models of systolic HF that are induced by cardiac overexpression of calsequestrin (CSQ) [5, 12]. For example, MRS2339 improves function in murine models whose systolic HF is caused by transthoracic aorta constriction or aorta banding [13] or by left anterior descending ligation-induced ischemic heart failure [8, 14]. Furthermore, MRS2339 infusion can also partially prevent hemodynamic derangement in a canine model of systolic HF caused by rapid atrial pacing [8]. In the current study, we aim to investigate the mechanism of action by which MRS2339 achieves its salutary effect in systolic HF. We analyzed unpublished results obtained from our previous study in dogs with pacing-induced severe HFrEF [8] to further characterize the efficacy of MRS2339 and to ascertain its mechanism of action in vivo. We showed that MRS2339 caused a cardiac increase in the protective molecule cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) due to a localized increased calcium causing calmodulin and endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) activation and subsequent NO production and cyclic GMP accumulation. MRS2339 is devoid of a vascular effect in conscious dogs with HFrEF. The results demonstrate that this nucleotide analog has a beneficial hemodynamic action and a protective cellular mechanism of action in heart failure.

Results and discussion

Sustained MRS2339 infusion in dogs subjected to cardiac tachypacing partially prevented the derangement of stroke volume, cardiac output, and mean arterial blood pressure

Our previous study showed that sustained infusion of MRS2339 was able to partially preserve cardiac performance in conscious, instrumented dogs subjected to cardiac tachypacing [8]. Here, we further utilized unpublished results of that study to better understand the underlying beneficial mechanisms of action of MRS2339. An acute single bolus infusion of MRS2339 across a range of concentrations (5–200 µg) was aimed at determining whether there was any acute hemodynamic effect of the agent in conscious animals. Data summarized in Fig. 1 demonstrated that MRS2339 did not have any acute effect on cardiac contractile function, as shown by its lack of effect on dP/dt max, heart rate, or mean arterial blood pressure in either healthy or HF dogs (Fig. 1).

However, one week of subcutaneous infusion in dogs at 100 µg/h, during the development of HFrEF, enhanced both cardiac output and stroke volume, respectively (Fig. 2a and b) with a nonstatistical increase in the mean arterial pressure, MAP (Fig. 2c). Sustained infusion of the nucleotide analog for a second week while rapid pacing rate was increased to 240 bpm did not show an increase in cardiac output or stroke volume (Fig. 3a and b, respectively) but caused improved systolic and mean arterial blood pressures (Fig. 3c and d, respectively). The reason for lack of an increase in cardiac output or stroke volume during the higher pacing rate in the second week of drug infusion is not clear but may be related to the deleterious effect of high pacing heart rate. However, that the arterial oxygen level is significantly higher at end of the MRS2339 infusion, together with the improved systolic and mean arterial pressures, is consistent with its salutary effect in HFrEF (Fig. 4). The diastolic blood pressure was unchanged after 1-week (data not shown) or 2-week infusion of MRS2339 (76.2 mmHg ± 4.3 mmHg, N = 4 dogs, mean ± SEM) vs. vehicle (65.9 mmHg ± 3.2 mmHg, N = 5 dogs, P > 0.05). There were no changes in total peripheral arterial resistance or mean coronary blood flow in MRS2339- vs. vehicle-infused dogs after 1-week or 2-week treatments (not shown), consistent with the lack of any vascular effect by MRS2339.

The present findings extend those of previous study in which a lower dose at 10 µg/h was infused intra-arterially (via a catheter in the left atrium) in the same canine model of HFrEF. In the earlier study, arterial P02 increased with a concomitant decrease in left ventricular end diastolic pressure along with an improved + dP/dt without a demonstrated increase in cardiac output or stroke volume. The present study increased the dosing level by 10-fold to 100 µg/h via subcutaneous infusion and investigated whether there is additional hemodynamically beneficial action of the agent at the earlier timepoint of 7 days post-infusion. The combination of a much higher dose and an earlier assessment showed an improved cardiac output and stroke volume as evidence of increased cardiac performance. Together, the data provided additional insight into the hemodynamic mechanism of action of MRS2339.

Cellular mechanism of action MRS2339

To investigate the cellular mechanism by which MRS2339 causes its cardioprotection, we tested the hypothesis that the nucleotide can stimulate cardiac P2X4 receptors to produce an increase in myocardial cyclic GMP. This concept is based on the observation that cardiac P2X4 receptors (P2X4R) can form a physical association with endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) and mediate an increase in its level and that of the cyclic GMP in isolated adult murine cardiomyocytes [9, 11]. Cardiac-specific knockout of P2X4R exhibits a worsened heart failure phenotype after pressure overload or ischemic injury [9]. To determine if the P2X4R in the heart is involved in mediating the MRS2339-induced cGMP increase, MRS2339 infusion was carried out in mice with cardiac KO of P2X4R and in the WT littermates after permanent LAD ligation to cause postinfarct heart failure. The nucleotide was able to cause an increase in myocardial cyclic GMP level in WT littermates but failed to elicit such an increase in hearts of KO mice (Fig. 5a). In a group of WT littermates with postinfarct heart failure, cardiac function was measured via echocardiography. MRS2339 infusion in these mice caused an increase in left ventricular fractional shortening (Fig. 5b), associated with its stimulation of cyclic GMP accumulation. We have not investigated whether there is any change in cyclic AMP level in WT mice in response to MRS2339. However, we did not see any increase in WT mouse cardiomyocyte contractility by MRS2339 (MRS2339 3 µM resulted in 95.4% ± 6.2% of the basal contractile amplitude, n = 11 myocytes, P > 0.05; isoproterenol 1 µM caused 192.5% ± 27.3% of the basal contractile amplitude, P < 0.05, n = 11 myocytes, mean ± SEM).

Adult WT littermate mice and mice with cardiac KO of P2X4R were subjected to permanent left coronary artery ligation to induce postinfarct ischemic heart failure. MRS2339 or vehicle saline was infused subcutaneously, and myocardial cyclic GMP (a) or left ventricular fractional shortening, FS, (b) determined as described in Methods. *P < 0.05 vs. vehicle

To further characterize the effect of MRS2339 on P2X4R, we investigated whether MRS2339 was able to activate P2X4R expressed in HEK293 cells overexpressing homomeric P2X4R. Electrophysiological recordings were performed at room temperature using the whole-cell patch-clamp configuration, with cells voltage-clamped at a holding potential of − 60 mV. Data summarized in Fig. 6 demonstrated that 10 µM MRS2339 did not evoke currents in P2X4R-expressing cells. In contrast, 10 µM ATP, the endogenous P2X 4 agonist, induced robust currents in these cells (Fig. 6). Rat P2X4 receptors were used as that was available during the electrophysiologic study at the time. Rat and mouse P2X4 receptors have similar ATP affinities. The fact that MRS2339 had no apparent effects on rat P2X4 may be due to differences in the orthosteric site of rat vs. mouse P2X4 receptors. Another possibility is that MRS2339 acts as an allosteric modulator of the endogenous extracellular ATP. Future experiments with mouse P2X4 receptors is planned to elucidate this important question. In mouse cardiomyocytes, MRS2339 has an agonist effect with stimulation of an ionic current similar to that induced by ATP [5]. While the endogenous cardiomyocyte P2X receptor responsive to MRS2339 is unknown, P2X4 receptors is needed to mediate the cyclic GMP effect of MRS2339 in the heart.

Conclusion

We previously showed that a novel nucleotide analog MRS2339 exerts a salutary effect in multiple animal models of systolic heart failure. The present data extended earlier findings and showed that MRS2339 exerts a hemodynamic mechanism, resulting in an increased stroke volume and cardiac output with concomitant improvement in arterial oxygen level and mean arterial blood pressure in chronically instrumented, conscious dogs rapid-paced to develop severe systolic heart failure. The agent has no acute effect on blood pressure, heart rate, or vascular resistance in these conscious dogs. The cellular mechanism of action of the nucleotide is the stimulation of myocardial cGMP levels which requires the presence of cardiac P2X4 receptors. The data suggest a novel mechanism of action of this nucleotide analog, distinct from that of the current therapies for HFrEF. The data support the presence of a cardiac P2X receptor-cGMP pathway and suggest a new, promising therapeutic approach to treat HFrEF.

Methods

HFrEF produced by rapid pacing in conscious dogs as a model of severe systolic dysfunction

Severe systolic dysfunction in dogs was produced by rapid left ventricular pacing, as described previously [8, 15, 16]. After sedation and anesthesia, ten adult mongrel dogs (weight 25–27 kg) were instrumented with catheters placed in the descending thoracic aorta and left atrial appendage. Cardiac function was determined by a solid-state pressure gauge (P6.5, Konigsberg Instruments, Inc.) inserted in the left ventricle through the apex, a Doppler flow probe (Craig Hartely) placed around the left circumflex coronary artery and a pair of 3-MHz piezoelectric crystals fixed on the endocardial surface at the base of the left ventricle [15, 16].

Severe systolic heart failure was induced by pacing the heart at 210 beats/min for 3 weeks, at which time dogs were considered to be relatively compensated, but exhibit pronounced systolic dysfunction. After 3 weeks, the pacing rate was increased to 240 beats/min, at which time dogs were considered in end-stage HF when LV end-diastolic pressure reached 25 mmHg. These dogs with severe HFrEF were infused with either a vehicle solution of normal saline (control) or MRS2339 dissolved in the normal saline solution (treated) through catheters chronically inserted in the left atrium, starting at the beginning of third week of pacing and uninterruptedly continued for two weeks. Hemodynamic parameters were obtained after one week and two weeks of MRS2339 or vehicle infusion. The dosing for the MRS2339-treated group was 100 mcg/hr.

Hemodynamic parameters for both groups were measured at end of the third week of pacing for both control and treated groups, as well as at end of fourth week. For hemodynamic measurements, the aortic catheter was attached to a P23ID strain-gauge transducer for measurement of aortic pressure, while the LV pressure was measured using the solid-state pressure gauge. Coronary blood flow (CBF) was measured by connecting the coronary probe to a pulsed Doppler flowmeter. LV dimensions were obtained by connecting the implanted piezoelectric crystals to a sonomicrometer (Model 100, Triton Technology). Arterial blood PO2 was measured with a blood gas analyzer, as described [15, 16]. In the acute IV injection study, conscious dogs that were either healthy or had severe HFrEF after 4 weeks of rapid pacing received acute bolus of MRS2339 at various doses. Repeated injection of each dose was given after hemodynamic measurements were taken.

Cardiac-specific conditional knockout of the P2X4 receptor and myocardial cyclic GMP production

Cardiac-specific conditional knockout of P2X4 receptors were produced as previously described [9]. Gene targeting and production of targeted ES cells were carried out and LoxP sites were inserted in introns 1 and 4 and homozygous P2X4floxed/floxed mice were obtained as described [9]. Homozygous P2X4floxed/floxed mice were bred with Tg mice (Myh6-cre/Esr1*, Jax stock no: 005650 in BL6 background), and tamoxifen treatment of offsprings was performed to delete the cardiac P2X 4R. WT littermates have the P2X4 allele excised but otherwise have the same genotype as the KO. Deletion efficiency was verified by absence of the P2X4R by immunoblotting and by absence of an increase in cardiac myocyte and intact heart contractility in response to receptor agonist 2-meSATP plus P2X 4 specific allosteric enhancer ivermectin. These were the KO mice produced by the same method as those produced in the previous study [9].

Intact heart cGMP levels were determined as described [9].

LAD ligation and echocardiography

Permanent LAD ligation was performed, and cardiac function was studied using echocardiography (Vevo 660 High Resolution Imaging System from VisualSonics, Toronto, Canada) in vivo using a linear 30-MHz transducer according to the manufacturer’s instructions as described [9, 13]. Two-dimensional-targeted M-mode echocardiographic measurements were carried out at mid-papillary muscle level. LV end-diastolic (LVEDD) and end-systolic (LVESD) diameters were obtained to determine the fractional shortening (FS) (defined as LVEDD − LVESD/LVEDD). We used FS rather than ejection fraction (EF) to study effect on cardiac function because echocardiography did not yield accurate LV volume, the measurement of which is necessary for quantifying EF. Wild type littermate mice or mice with P2X 4R KO 10–12 weeks were subjected to permanent left coronary artery ligation. Within 2 days of ligation, these mice were infused with either normal saline (vehicle) or MRS2339 via subcutaneous Alzet minipumps (5 mM MRS2339 in sterile saline solution at 12.8 µl/day) for 7 days. After 6 days of infusion, non-infarcted regions were obtained and processed for cyclic GMP measurement in mice infused with vehicle or MRS2339. In a group of WT littermates, LV FS was measured via echocardiography after infusion with saline or MRS2339. Mice were then euthanized, and the infarct size and cyclic GMP levels determined as previously described [9, 13].

Transfection of HEK293 cells and electrophysiology

Receptor expression and cell culture

Functional studies were carried out using the rat P2X4 receptor. The construct was subcloned into the pIRES2–enhanced green fluorescent protein (GFP) expression vector and transiently expressed in HEK293 cells. Cells were maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Biofluids) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Invitrogen). For electrophysiological recordings, cells were plated onto 35-mm dishes at a density of approximately 5 × 10⁵ cells per dish. Transient transfections were performed 24 h after plating using 2 µg of plasmid DNA and 5 µl of Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) diluted in 2 ml of serum-free Opti-MEM. After 4.5 h, the transfection medium was replaced with standard growth medium. Recordings were obtained 24–48 h post-transfection, and transfected cells were identified by GFP fluorescence.

Electrophysiology

Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings were conducted at room temperature. Membrane currents were measured using an Axopatch 200B amplifier (Molecular Devices) and filtered at 2 kHz with a low-pass Bessel filter. Patch pipettes were pulled from borosilicate glass capillaries (1B150F-3; World Precision Instruments) using a Flaming-Brown horizontal puller (P-87; Sutter Instruments) and fire-polished to achieve a resistance of 2–4 MΩ. Signals were digitized with a Digidata 1322 A interface and acquired using pClamp 9 software (Molecular Devices). Currents were recorded from individual cells voltage-clamped at − 60 mV.

Solutions and drug application

The standard extracellular solution (Krebs–Ringer–like) contained (in mM): 142 NaCl, 3 KCl, 1 MgCl₂, 2 CaCl₂, 10 glucose, and 10 HEPES. Unless otherwise indicated, the internal pipette solution contained (in mM): 142 NaCl, 10 EGTA, and 10 HEPES, adjusted to pH 7.35 with NaOH. The intracellular solution does not contain potassium or calcium and includes EGTA to chelate any residual Ca²⁺. This composition was chosen for two main reasons: first, the replacement of intracellular K⁺ with Na⁺ minimizes the contribution of K⁺ leak currents; second, the presence of EGTA prevents activation of Ca²⁺-dependent channels. Together, these conditions allow for a more selective recording of ligand-gated currents, such as those mediated by P2X receptors, by reducing contamination from endogenous K⁺ and Ca²⁺-activated currents. The osmolarity of intracellular solutions was adjusted to 305 mosmol/kg H₂O. ATP and MRS2339 solutions were freshly prepared each day and applied using a rapid solution exchange system (RSC-200; Biologic Science Instruments).

Statistics

Data obtained from each experiment were represented as mean ± SEM. Echocardiography parameters were obtained consistently at the beginning and at the end of the experiments before euthanasia, that is, after the end of infusion. MRS2339 or vehicle was administered less than 48 h after LAD ligation. When functional parameters were obtained at each dose of acute bolus injection, repeated measure analysis was used. When there was statistical significance from the repeated measure ANOVA, Newman-Keuls post-hoc test was carried out. When functional parameters were obtained at a given timepoint comparing two groups, two-tailed t test was applied. The probability value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

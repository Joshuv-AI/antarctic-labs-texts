Rolling contact fatigue dataset for AISI 52100 bearing steel balls under calcium grease lubrication
Authors: Shubrajit Bhaumik, Prasanna Kumar R, Rahul Bharathwaj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rolling contact fatigue (RCF) is a dominant failure mechanism in bearings; however, open-access datasets analyzing RCF under varying lubrication conditions remain scarce. This study aims to address this problem, in which AISI 52100 bearing steel balls were tested using a four-ball tribometer according to IP 300 standard. Three calcium-soap-thickened greases of National Lubricating Grease Institute (NLGI) grades 0, 00, and 000 were used and each was formulated with extreme-pressure additives. A total of 63 trials (21 per grade) were conducted at 1470 rpm, under a peak axial load of 5739 N. The dataset includes time-to-failure data detailing Weibull statistical lifetime values (L₁₀, L₅₀, and L₉₀ fatigue life quantiles), probability density functions, survival, and hazard functions. The dataset also comprises 1,140 post-test optical microscopy images for documenting pitting, crack propagation, and plastic deformation, along with Weibull distribution plots. These data support tribological research on grease-lubricated contacts, lubricant benchmarking, fatigue life modelling, and validation of condition monitoring algorithms for rolling-element bearings.

Background & Summary

Rolling-element bearings are essential parts of rotating machinery, and failure of one or more bearings can lead to expensive downtime and losses. Although bearings can fail in a variety of ways, such as adhesive wear, abrasive wear, fretting, corrosion fatigue, electrically induced pitting, and rolling contact fatigue (RCF), the service life of bearings is designed and standardized around RCF. RCF arises from the application of Hertzian contact stress between ball-raceway surfaces due to repeated cyclic loading. In such conditions, subsurface shearing stresses nucleate cracks at material inhomogeneities or surface asperities, and these cracks grow progressively until surface damage (such as pitting, spalling, or flaking of the material) causes the bearing to fail1–3. This degradation extends further with increased surface roughness and micropitting, where the first interactions between asperities can trigger the process from mild to severe fatigue4. Lundberg and Palmgren5 developed an analytical model that underlies the probabilistic fatigue and is the standard method that is used worldwide for bearing life prediction. Among bearings, AISI 52100 high-carbon chromium steel balls are the most commonly used bearing material because of their hardness, wear resistance, and fatigue resistance under concentrated contact loading6,7. Within the industrial sector, rolling contacts are mainly lubricated with greases. Calcium-soap-thickened greases of various NLGI consistency grades are widely used in gearboxes and conveyor systems8. The grade determines the rheological characteristics of the grease, such as its ability to form a lubricating film, its lubricant starvation tendency, and consequently bearing fatigue life9–11.

Currently, there are only a limited number of standardized RCF datasets that have enough repetitions to perform a statistical life estimation. Most published RCF presentations do not provide underlying records of the number of cycles a test was run for or the failure records for the surface image, but rather report aggregate summary statistics. While existing datasets have been primarily leveraged to benchmark fault-detection and prognostic algorithms12–14, several widely used vibration-based diagnostics datasets exist, including the Case Western Reserve University bearing fault dataset15 and the NASA IMS bearing prognostics dataset16. Existing public RCF datasets generally focus on fatigue life measurements or derived results and often do not provide the combination of raw trial-level fatigue life data, bearing surface imagery, statistical analysis outputs, and detailed per-trial metadata under controlled lubrication conditions. The dataset contains these elements in a structured format and may support reuse in fatigue life analysis, statistical benchmarking, and prognostic model development. Optical evidence of surface damage (such as pitting, cracking, and plastic deformation) is likewise rarely deposited in structured, accessible repository formats, despite its relevance to fault signature development in bearing diagnostics.

The dataset consists of all experimental results from 63 rolling contact fatigue tests on AISI 52100 bearing steel balls that were performed with three calcium-soap-thickened greases of NLGI grades 0, 00, and 000, designated EP0, EP00, and EP000, respectively. Each experiment was performed under the IP 300 standard with a four-ball tribometer at 1470 rpm and under a peak axial load of 5739 N, with the aim of automatically detecting failure by vibration-based termination with a vibration threshold of 0.28 g, where g = 9.81 m/s2. Two-parameter Weibull statistics were used to calculate L₁₀, L₅₀, and L₉₀ fatigue life quantiles for each lubricant, along with other Weibull distribution parameters (such as shape, scale, mean, standard deviation, and interquartile range). In addition to the statistical results, 1,140 high-resolution optical microscopy images were captured following the tests and stored in a hierarchically structured repository organized by lubricant type, trial number, and ball identity, documenting pitting, crack propagation, and plastic deformation across all test conditions.

The per-trial time-to-failure data, Weibull parameters, and surface imagery have different applications for research in tribology, lubrication science, and bearing diagnostics. The NLGI grades differ systematically under the same contact conditions. This provides a controlled basis for studying how the grease’s consistency influences its fatigue life and the damage it experiences. A previous study by Bhaumik and Paleu17 reported the wear and RCF behaviour of AISI 52100 steel under lubricants with additives using the same test configuration. Another study by Bhaumik et al.18 offered a broader view of how the bearing material and lubricant behave under alternating electric current. The current dataset expands on this research by providing complete trial data and images of the surfaces after RCF test, which are available as open data for three grease grades. The time-to-failure data from each trial and Weibull parameters can serve as reference data for predicting fatigue life and evaluating fault-detection algorithms. The microscopy images reveal the surface damage for all 63 trials, organized by lubricant grade, trial number, and ball identity. This organization allows for easy retrieval of images under specific test conditions. The data can be accessed on Mendeley Data with the https://doi.org/10.17632/d5n3zgvs7p.319.

Methods

Experimental setup

The four-ball tribometer (Magnum 800-FBT) was used to perform all rolling contact fatigue tests according to the IP 300 standard configuration. In this configuration, one AISI 52100 bearing steel ball is fixed in the upper rotating spindle, and three identical balls of the same grade are positioned in a specially designed frictionless ball pot under the rotating spindle. All tests were performed with AISI 52100 bearing steel balls, 12.7 mm in diameter, and with an HRC hardness of 55 – 60. No balls were used more than once; a new set of four AISI 52100 balls was used for each independent trial.

Ball preparation

Before every test, all balls were ultrasonically cleaned for 10 min in hexane, dried with compressed air, and optically inspected under an optical microscope to ensure the absence of pre-existing surface defects or any scratches. The initial surface roughness of the balls was measured to be Ra = 0.101 µm by a Zeiss Surfcom Touch 50.

Lubricant preparation

In this study, three greases with NLGI consistency grades of 0, 00 and 000 (EP0, EP00, and EP000) were used as lubricants. The basic oil formulation (ISO VG150 cSt) and the extreme-pressure additive package were common to all three greases and the only difference between the three was the concentration of calcium soap thickener, which determines the grade according to the NLGI classification system. This controlled variation eliminates the influence of the type of additive and base oil chemistry on fatigue life and only provides a variation of grease consistency. The ball pot was filled with 35 ± 3 g of grease according to the IP 300 standard specification.

Operating conditions

The test conditions were chosen to be directly relevant to industrial testing conditions, with the upper ball being rotated at a constant spindle speed of 1470 rpm corresponding to the synchronous output speed of a standard four-pole 50 Hz AC induction motor on the same supply. A stepped loading protocol was applied in accordance with the IP 300 standard, with an initial applied load of 981 N for the first ten seconds, allowing the lubricant film to establish across the contact zone, thus preventing adhesive seizure at the start. The load was gradually increased to a peak axial load of 5739 N and maintained at that load for the rest of the test till pitting occurred. The equipment was automatically shut off as soon as the pitting occurred. This four-ball configuration provides high stress cycles per hour at each contact point, allowing for accelerated fatigue life testing under controlled elastohydrodynamic lubrication (EHL) conditions.

Environmental conditions

Testing was done under ambient laboratory conditions. Temperature was actively controlled during the test program and was reported to be 35 ± 3 °C. The relative humidity was not actively controlled but was recorded to be 65 ± 3% across the tests.

Fatigue monitoring and termination criteria

The vibration data at the tribometer spindle housing was continuously recorded during each test with a built-in piezoelectric accelerometer (sensitivity: 98.10 mV/g), which was firmly fixed to the tribometer housing. The amplitude of the analog output of the accelerometer was extracted by the data acquisition system using a Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) and was processed in real time with a sampling frequency of 1000 samples per second. No digital filtering or windowing procedures were applied to the vibration signal beyond the raw FFT computation. The tests were automatically interrupted whenever the monitored amplitude of vibration exceeded the vibration amplitude threshold of 0.28 g, where g = 9.81 m/s². The upper ball was rotating to produce one complete cycle of stress at contact areas of the lower balls; the time-to-failure is reported in seconds. The elapsed time at the point of termination was recorded as the time-to-failure value for that trial. During the test program no run-out suspensions were experienced and all 63 trials were only terminated when the vibration amplitude exceeded the predefined threshold due to the surface being damaged due to pitting.

Microscopic examination and image acquisition

After each test, the three stationary balls were taken out of the ball pot, and the contact surfaces of the balls were analyzed with an Olympus BX53M metallurgical optical microscope. The images were taken using a magnification of 5x in a fixed imaging resolution of 2160 × 1620 pixels, and stored as a.tif image file. The lighting conditions and focus parameters were fixed for all acquisitions, so as to make the images easily comparable. Five to seven images were captured per ball per test, resulting in a total of 1,140 pictures for the 63 tests and three lubricants. Images are stored in the Microscopy_Images folder of the repository, organized hierarchically by lubricant grade (EP-0, EP-00, EP-000), trial number (TEST-1 to TEST-21), and ball identity (BALL-1 to BALL-3).

The dataset describes the RCF performance of AISI 52100 bearing steel balls tested under three calcium-soap-thickened greases of NLGI grades 0, 00, and 000, designated as EP0, EP00, and EP000, respectively. The dataset includes processed fatigue life calculations, Weibull distribution plots, and microscopic images acquired after four-ball tribometer tests for documenting surface-initiated damage. All experiments were conducted in accordance with the IP 300 standard. Microscopy image structure

This folder contains all raw visual data and is hierarchically organized according to lubricant type, trial number, and ball number. Each subfolder is an identifier to a unique fatigue test, where the parent folder name indicates the lubricant grade (EP-0, EP-00, or EP-000). The TEST-N subfolder denotes the trial number (1–21), and BALL-1, BALL-2, and BALL-3 are used to identify which of the three stationary lower balls was imaged during post-test examination. All microscopy images were acquired at 50× magnification (5× objective lens) with a fixed resolution of 2160 × 1620 pixels in.tif format. These images were captured using an Olympus BX53M metallurgical optical microscope. Lighting conditions and focal parameters were held constant across all 1,140 acquisitions to ensure inter-image comparability. Technical Validation

The reliability of the dataset is addressed across six dimensions: instrument calibration, experimental repeatability, Weibull goodness-of-fit, outlier handling, microscopy image quality, and comparison with published data.

Instrument calibration

The Magnum 800-FBT four-ball tribometer was calibrated before the test program. This was carried out according to the manufacturer’s four-ball calibration schedule. The load cell and rotational speed controller were checked against the valid and current calibration certificates provided by Magnum Engineers (Normal Load: Sensotronics Beam Type Load Cell, Model 65023-0ITI-NC-00X, Serial No. 89777379; Frictional Torque: IPA Binocular Type Load Cell, Model BR021H2, Serial No. 166422; Speed: Digital Tachometer, range 1–5000 rpm). During the test program, applied loads and spindle speeds were verified within the accuracy tolerances of ± 2% of measured normal load and ± 0.05% FSD (Full Scale Deflection) for speed throughout the test program.

Repeatability of fatigue life measurements

Because rolling contact fatigue (RCF) is stochastic, there is inherent scatter in cycles-to-failure data. The coefficient of variation (CV = [standard deviation / mean] × 100%) provides a quantitative measure of this scatter, allowing for a comparison of fatigue life variability across different lubricant grades. The mean and standard deviation for 21 trials of EP0 were 3904.87 seconds and 1339.73 seconds, respectively, giving a CV of 34.309%. The mean value of EP000 was 4950.22 seconds, while the standard deviation was 2129.74 seconds, resulting in a CV of 43.02%. For EP00, the mean value was 5679.88 seconds and the standard deviation was 1360.30 seconds, yielding a coefficient of variation (CV) of 23.949%. The observed CV values are consistent with the variability typically reported in RCF studies of AISI 52100 bearing steel, with EP00 exhibiting comparatively lower dispersion (23.95%). Twenty-one independent tests per lubricant grade are consistent with the upper range of sample sizes reported in four-ball RCF element studies and are sufficient for stable two-parameter Weibull parameter estimation20.

Weibull goodness-of-fit

The two-parameter Weibull distribution was used to fit the cycles to failure data for each lubricant grade; the Anderson-Darling (AD*) statistic and Weibull probability plot correlation coefficient were used to assess the quality of the fit. The two-parameter Weibull distribution was fitted to all three lubricant conditions. For EP0, the fitted parameters were β = 3.199 and η = 4,359.86 seconds (AD* = 4.532, r = 0.883), while EP000 yielded β = 2.485 and η = 5,580.01 seconds (AD* = 2.260, r = 0.912), and EP00 produced β = 4.762 and η = 6,203.64 seconds (AD* = 1.222, r = 0.958). Among the three, EP00 demonstrated the best Weibull fit, as evidenced by the lowest AD* value (1.222) and highest correlation coefficient (r = 0.958), followed by EP000 (AD* = 2.260, r = 0.912) and EP0 (AD* = 4.532, r = 0.883). The increasing β values across EP000, EP0, and EP00 suggest progressively steeper failure rate distributions, while the higher η values for EP00 and EP000 indicate longer characteristic fatigue lives compared to EP0. The two-parameter Weibull analysis, including estimation of the L₁₀, L₅₀, and L₉₀ percentiles, was performed in Minitab 18 using the LSXY (Least Squares in X and Y) estimation method. The analysis reported point estimates of the Weibull parameters and fatigue-life percentiles. The Anderson–Darling (AD) statistic and Weibull probability plot correlation coefficient (r) were used as the primary indicators of goodness-of-fit for each lubricant grade. While the fitted Weibull shape parameters (β > 1) for all three lubricant grades are higher than the traditional Lundberg-Palmgren baseline for bearings (β ≈ 1.1), they reflect the broader variability of dispersion exponents noted in literature; specifically, Tallian21 highlights evidence that β can increase in rolling contacts due to varying applied loads and the presence of a fatigue-limit effect.

Outlier and anomalous test handling

A total of 21 independent tests were carried out for each lubricant grade, and these were all included in the Weibull analysis. No trials were eliminated due to abnormal vibration, early termination or equipment malfunction. There were no run-out suspensions and all trials made it to the threshold of vibration termination, indicating the loading and vibration conditions applied were adequate to cause fatigue failure within the test program.

Microscopy image quality

The lead author visually reviewed all 1,140 post-test optical microscopy images during image acquisition to ensure adequate focus, absence of motion blur, and sufficient contrast for reliable identification of damage features. All images were accepted without rejection and there were no reacquisitions due to poor minimum image acquisition quality. The uniformity of the magnification used (50×), resolution (2160 × 1620 pixels) and the level of light used on all images makes it possible to compare images across different lubricant grades and trial numbers.

Comparison with published data

The characteristic fatigue life (4359.86 seconds for EP0, 6203.64 seconds for EP00, and 5580.01 seconds for EP000) determined in this study is consistent with the characteristic fatigue life of AISI 52100 lubricated with base mineral oil under the same four-ball tribometer configuration and load schedule as reported by Bhaumik and Paleu17. Bhaumik and Paleu used oil lubrication instead of grease, but the order of magnitude agreement of the two studies under the same contact geometry and loading conditions is mentioned to support the credibility of the experimental methodology and the validity of the deposited data. The observed improvement in characteristic fatigue life when the EP0 grease was replaced by the EP000 grease is physically consistent with the low consistency grade of EP000, which helps to improve the flow of the lubricant and replenishment of the lubricant film at the contact zone under the operating conditions applied9,10.

Usage Notes

This dataset contains 63 independent controlled laboratory rolling contact fatigue tests conducted on AISI 52100 bearing steel balls with three calcium-soap-thickened greases (NLGI grades 0, 00, and 000). The user should be aware that the dataset is applicable to this bearing steel grade, the IP 300 four-ball contact geometry and the stated load escalation profile. Extrapolation to other contact geometry, materials, and loading regimes should be done with proper care.

The microscopy images are organized hierarchically within the Microscopy_Images folder. The experimental variables are presented in the folder names: lubricant grade (EP-0, EP-00, EP-000), trial number (TEST-1 to TEST-21) and ball identifier (BALL-1 to BALL-3). The folder names follow the dataset naming convention (EP-0, EP-00, EP-000), corresponding to the lubricant grades EP0, EP00, and EP000 referred to throughout this paper. The Analysis_graph folder includes the Weibull distribution plots and tabulated statistical parameters for each lubricant grade. To enable those users who want to retrieve data for specific trial or under specific lubricant conditions, the complete per-trial metadata record is provided as RCF_Trial_Data.docx (deposited in the Mendeley Data repository, https://doi.org/10.17632/d5n3zgvs7p.3), with each trial’s time-to-failure value corresponding to its associated image folder paths.

The data presented here were collected under a regulated temperature of 35 ± 3 °C. Relative humidity was not actively controlled but was recorded at 65 ± 3% across the tests; users should account for this when comparing results obtained under controlled humidity conditions. Future extensions of this dataset could include a larger number of trials per grade, varying load profiles, or alternative bearing steel grades, all of which would broaden its application.

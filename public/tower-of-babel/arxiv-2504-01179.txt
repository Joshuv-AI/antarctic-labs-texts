Feshbach-Villars Formalism for a Spin-1/2 Particle in Curved Spacetime
Abdelmalek Boumali

Abstract




This study explores the Feshbach-Villars (FV) formalism for spin-1/2
particles in curved spacetime. We derive the Hamiltonian form of the
Dirac equation in this context and extend the FV transformation accordingly.
The generalized Klein-Gordon equation, obtained by squaring the Dirac
operator, is reformulated using the FV approach. We present the resulting
Hamiltonian in both matrix and Pauli matrix forms, considering gravitational
and electromagnetic interactions. The formalism is examined in both
(1+2) and (1+3) dimensional spacetimes, with special attention given
to the spin-field interaction term. This study provides a framework
for studying relativistic quantum mechanics in curved spacetime, offering
insights into the interplay between quantum effects, gravity, and
electromagnetism.

I
Introduction

Relativistic quantum mechanics represents a significant academic achievement
that focuses on integrating the concepts of relativity and quantum
mechanics. This integration expanded the framework of quantum theory
and introduced the concept of negative energy levels, which emerged
directly from relativistic formulations. Negative energy states are
illustrated by the concept of the Dirac sea, which is an infinite
expanse of particles with negative energy. In this model, the absence
of a particle in the Dirac sea indicates the presence of a physical
particle, specifically, a positron. This interpretation arises from
the requirement that the energy of free particles must be positive,
as dictated by the physical laws. By treating particles and antiparticles
differently, this approach creates intrinsic asymmetry. While this
asymmetry is essential for upholding the probabilistic principles
of quantum physics, as outlined in the continuity equation, it also
highlights the elegance of Dirac’s sea model. This concept is particularly
applicable to fermions, which have a half-integer spin and obey the
Pauli exclusion principle. However, this does not apply to bosons,
which possess an integer spin. Quantum field theory (QFT) provides
a more comprehensive framework by treating antiparticles as fundamental
components of field interactions rather than relying on the Dirac
sea [1, 2, 3].

In 1958, Feshbach and Villars [1] published their findings on
linearizing the Klein-Gordon equation. Their work led to the development
of the Feshbach-Villars (FV-0) equations, which allowed the Klein-Gordon
equation to be expressed in the Schrödinger form, also known as the
Hamiltonian form. This formalization addresses various contradictions
in the Klein-Gordon equation, including the existence of negative
energies. This facilitated a clearer interpretation of probability
theory, particularly when considering particles with corresponding
antiparticles.

Feshbach and Villars’ formalism resolved the key issues that had previously
caused the abandonment of the Klein-Gordon theory, enabling a reevaluation
of its principles. Significant research has been conducted using the
Feshbach-Villars equations. Furthermore, efforts have been made to
obtain numerical solutions and approximations for these equations,
providing physicists with the means to tackle ongoing challenges and
develop insights related to relativistic quantum mechanics (RQM) [4,
5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10].

To expand the theory within the framework of general relativity, Silenko
[11, 12] was the first to introduce a new form of conformal invariance
for massless scalar particles in Riemannian spacetimes. He studied
scalar particles in arbitrary spacetimes under general relativity
(GR). To derive a Hamiltonian form of the covariant Klein-Gordon (KG)
equation for both massive and massless particles, he utilized the
generalized Feshbach-Villars transformation, as proposed in a previous
study. Silenko then derived the general quantum-mechanical equations
of motion and established their classical limits. Recently, this method
has been applied in various contexts, and the results have been promising
[13, 14, 15, 16].

The Feshbach-Villars equations have simplified the reevaluation of
relativistic quantum mechanics for spin-0 particles and opened new
avenues for investigating similar theories related to spin-1/2 particles.
In addition, this formalism offers an alternative approach for spin-zero
particles characterized by the Klein-Gordon equation (KG-0). This
method incorporates antiparticles through charge density instead of
probability density, thereby expanding the representation space to
a pseudo-Hilbert space. Notably, this framework treats particles and
antiparticles symmetrically, eliminating the need for a Dirac sea
and enhancing the mathematical representation. This has significant
implications for our understanding of the spin-zero particles and
their interactions.

Building on this idea, FV-like equations have been proposed for spin-½
particles (KG–½). These equations have the potential to achieve complete
particle-antiparticle symmetry and eliminate the need for a Dirac
sea, representing a promising development. By altering the manner
in which wave functions depict physical states, this extension alters
certain scientific interpretations, particularly those involving transition
probabilities. The differences become significant when examining phenomena
where the influence of the Dirac sea is evident. The formalism presented
by Feshbach and Villars paved the way for a new relativistic quantum
mechanics equation for spin-1/2 particles. The worked developed by
Robson and Staudte [2, 3] followed the idea of the Feshbach-Villars
linearization procedure and applied it to the second-order Dirac equation.
The result was an eight-component relativistic equation in Hamiltonian
form, which preserves some of the inner product properties and the
probabilistic interpretation obtained from the FV-0 equation. This
eight-component equation or Feshbach-Villars equation for spin- 1
/ 2 particles (FV-1/2) is relativistic and different from the Dirac
equation.

This study aims to extend the Feshbach-Villars (FV) formalism for
spin-1/2 particles to curved spacetime. Specifically, we analyze fermion
particles in arbitrary spacetimes within the framework of general
relativity (GR). To derive the Hamiltonian form of the covariant Dirac
equation in curved spacetime, we employ the generalized Feshbach-Villars
transformation introduced in Ref. [12]. It is worth noting that,
to the best of our knowledge, there is a notable gap in the literature
regarding this type of study.

IV
Conclusion

The Feshbach-Villars formalism for a spin-1/2 particle in curved spacetime
provides a robust method for exploring relativistic quantum mechanics
in the presence of gravitational and electromagnetic fields. By deriving
the Hamiltonian form of the Dirac equation and applying the FV transformation,
we have developed a two-component formulation that clearly separates
positive and negative energy states. This formalism presents several
advantages:

•

It allows a more intuitive interpretation of the wavefunction components
with respect to particle and antiparticle contributions.

•

The resulting Hamiltonian, expressed in terms of Pauli matrices, facilitates
the analysis of spin-dependent effects in curved spacetime.

•

The approach naturally incorporates both gravitational and electromagnetic
interactions, making it suitable for exploring various physical scenarios.

Our analysis in both (1+2) and (1+3) dimensions showcases the versatility
of the formalism and highlights the specific forms of spin-field interactions
across different spacetime configurations. The explicit treatment
of the term involving the electromagnetic field tensor and spin matrices
provides a clear understanding of how spin couples to external fields
in curved spacetime. This work lays the groundwork for future studies
on relativistic quantum effects in strong gravitational fields, the
behavior of spin in curved spacetime, and potential applications in
areas such as early universe cosmology and the physics of compact
objects. Further research could investigate the implications of this
formalism for quantum field theory in curved spacetime and explore
possible experimental tests of the predicted effects.

Auttawich Manui, Cheikh Saliou Ndiaye and Artem Zvavitch

Abstract
. We study inequalities on the volume of Minkowski sum in the class of anti-blocking bodies. We prove analogues of Plünnecke-Ruzsa type inequality and V. Milman inequality on the concavity of the ratio of volumes of bodies and their projections. We also study Firey L p − L_{p}- sums of anti-blocking bodies and prove Plünnecke-Ruzsa type inequality; V. Milman inequality and Roger-Shephard inequality. The sharp constants are provided in all of those inequalities, for the class of anti-blocking bodies. Finally, we extend our results to the case of unconditional product measures with decreasing density.

1.
Introduction

The notion of sumset is a standard operation in the field of additive combinatorics which makes sense for any sets in a commutative group, i.e., for any sets
A,B
in a commutative group, their sumset
A+B
, is defined as the set of all possible sums of elements from
A,B
, i.e.,
A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}.
One useful and elegant inequality in additive combinatorics that provides bounds on the cardinality of sumsets of finite sets is the
Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality

[
31
,
38
]
: For any finite sets
A,B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
in a commutative group, there exists a set
X\subset A
,

(1)

\#(A)^{m}\#(X+B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m})\leq\#(X)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\#(A+B_{i}).

Here,
\#(A)
denotes the cardinality of the set
A
. This inequality was introduced by Plünnecke
[
28
]
and further improved by Ruzsa
[
33
]
to the case of compact sets on a locally compact commutative group.

The comparison between inequalities in Information Theory and Convex Geometry have been thoroughly investigated. Bobkov and Madiman
[
9
]
introduced an inequality that is inspired by (
1
): For any convex bodies
A,B,C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one has

\left|A\right|\left|A+B+C\right|\leq 3^{n}\left|A+B\right|\left|A+C\right|.

The constant
3^{n}
is known to not be sharp. Fradelizi, Madiman, and the third named author
[
18
]
explored this further, determining the lower bound and upper bound of the best possible constant
c_{n}
,

(2)

c_{n}=\sup\frac{\left|A\right|\left|A+B+C\right|}{\left|A+B\right|\left|A+C\right|},

where the supremum is taken over all convex bodies
A,B,C
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
:

(4/3+o(1))^{n}\leq c_{n}\leq\varphi^{n},

where
\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
is the golden ratio.

The second named author generalized results from
[
9
]
to the case of
m-
convex bodies. More precisely, let
c_{n,m}
be the smallest possible constant such that

(3)

\left|A\right|^{m-1}\left|A+B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m}\right|\leq c_{n,m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left|A+B_{i}\right|,

for any convex bodies
A,B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then,

\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\pi n(e-1)}}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{n}+1/2\leq c_{n,m}<2^{n}.

Moreover, sharper lower and upper bounds depending on
m
were established in
[
27
]
.

Ruzsa distance was introduced in
[
32
]
as

d(A,B)=\log\frac{\#(A-B)}{\sqrt{\#(A)\#(B)}},

where
A,B
are finite subsets of a commutative group (see also
[
38
, Chapter 2.33]
). Ruzsa distance is not a metric, however it satisfies the triangle inequality: For any finite sets
A,B,C
in a commutative group,

\#(A)\#(B-C)\leq\#(B-A)\#(A-C).

An analog of this inequality to the case of volume of convex bodies was presented in
[
18
]
: For any convex bodies
A,B,C
, one has

\left|A\right|\left|A+B+C\right|\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}c_{n}\left|A-B\right|\left|A-C\right|,

where
c_{n}
is defined in (
2
).

We note that the lower bounds for
c_{n}
and
c_{n,m}
in
[
18
,
27
]
were obtained by considering a convex body which has many symmetries. This inspired us to explore Ruzsa type inequalities for the class of anti-blocking bodies.

We say that a convex body
K
is an
anti-blocking body
if
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
and orthogonal projection on every coordinate subspace equals to its section by the same subspace (see Section
2.2
for more details). Many crucial inequalities were proved for the class of anti-blocking bodies see
[
6
,
36
]
. In Corollary
10
, we prove a sharp analog of (
3
): For any anti-blocking bodies
A,B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
, one has

(4)

\left|A\right|^{m-1}\left|A+B_{1}+\ldots+B_{m}\right|\leq\zeta_{n,m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left|A-B_{i}\right|,

where
\zeta_{n,m}
is defined as follows: For any integers
0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq n
, let

(5)

d_{n,m}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}):=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{\alpha_{i}}{N}}{\binom{n}{N}^{m-1}},

where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}-n(m-1)\geq 0
and

\zeta_{n,m}:=\max_{0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq n}d_{n,m}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}).

The next observation of this paper is related to the conjecture of the concavity of the ratio of volumes to volumes of projections: For any convex bodies
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

(6)

\frac{\left|A\right|}{\left|P_{E}A\right|}+\frac{\left|B\right|}{\left|P_{E}B\right|}\leq\frac{\left|A+B\right|}{\left|P_{E}(A+B)\right|},

where
E
is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
P_{E}
is the orthogonal projection onto
E
. The conjecture (
6
) is connected to a question of V. Milman
[
35
]
, whether there exists a version of the Bergstrom inequality for convex bodies. It is also connected to Dembo, Cover, Thomas conjecture
[
15
]
which motivated by Fisher information inequality. The conjecture (
6
) is known to be true in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(see, for example,
[
20
]
), but it is false in general starting from dimension
3
, even for the weaker version,

(7)

\frac{\left|A\right|}{\left|P_{E}A\right|}\leq\frac{\left|A+B\right|}{\left|P_{E}(A+B)\right|}.

The counterexample was presented in
[
19
]
. It has been demonstrated in
[
20
]
that (
7
) is valid for the class of zonoids when
n=3
(the case of the zonoids in higher dimensions is still open). We refer to
[
20
,
18
]
for detailed discussion of those conjectures and their equivalent versions.

We study an analog of conjecture (
6
) for anti-blocking bodies in the view of Ruzsa’s triangle inequality by replacing the Minkowski sum with the difference. Let
r_{n,i}
be the best possible constant for the following inequality:

(8)

\frac{\left|A\right|}{\left|P_{E}A\right|}+\frac{\left|B\right|}{\left|P_{E}B\right|}\leq r_{n,i}\frac{\left|A-B\right|}{\left|P_{E}(A-B)\right|},

for any anti-blocking bodies
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
and for any
i-
dimensional coordinate subspace
E
. We prove in Theorem
12
that

r_{n,i}=\max_{0\leq j\leq n}d_{n,2}(i,j),

where
d_{n,2}(i,j)
is defined in (
5
).

Inspired by
[
20
]
, we extend (
4
) and (
8
) to a more general operation on the pair of convex bodies introduced by Firey
[
17
]
, called the
L_{p}-
sums
.

An upper bound for the volume of difference body
K-K
is given
by the Rogers-Shephard inequality, originally proven in
[
29
]
(see also
[
35
]
). The
L_{p}
sum analog of this inequality was proved in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by Bianchini and Colesanti
[
8
]
and it is an open question to prove the sharp
L_{p}
-Rogers-Shephard inequality in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. We prove this inequality in the case of locally anti-blocking bodies, where a
locally anti-blocking body
can be viewed as a body which is anti-blocking body in each orthant. Let
\kappa_{n,q}
be the best possible constant for the following inequality:

|K\oplus_{p}-K|\leq\kappa_{n,q}|K|,

for any locally anti-blocking body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
p\geq 1
. We prove in Lemma
23
that

\kappa_{n,q}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\binom{n}{i}^{2},

where the binomial coefficient is defined by the Gamma function:

\binom{x}{y}:=\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(y+1)\Gamma(x-y+1)}.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 is dedicated to the background materials. In section 3, we present an improvement of Local Loomis-Whitney inequality, of Bollobás and Thomason
[
10
]
, for a
\lambda-
uniform cover. Following this, in section 4, we present the best possible constants of a Plünnecke-Ruzsa type inequality and the concavity of the ratio of volumes to volumes of projections for anti-blocking bodies. Moving on to section 5, we present our results with
L_{p}-
sums, and also present a sharp constant of a
L_{p}
-Roger-Shephard inequality for a locally anti-blocking body. Section 6 delves deeper into inequalities when considering volumes with respect to an
s-
concave measure. Finally, Section 7 is an Appendix where we collect optimizations of constants and numerous technical calculations, used in the paper.

2.
Background

2.1.
Preliminaries

We refer to
[
3
,
4
,
35
]
for basic definitions and facts used in this paper. We write
\langle x,y\rangle
for
the inner product
of
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
for the
standard orthonormal basis
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. A
convex body
is defined as a convex, compact non-empty set.
If a convex body
K
containing the origin, then, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the
Minkowski functional
by

\|x\|_{K}:=\inf\{t>0:x\in tK\}.

We note that if
0\in\partial K
, then the above infimum may be taken over the empty set, in this case we set such infimum to be infinity.
We also define a
polar body
of
K
by

K^{\circ}:=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1\text{ for all }x\in K\}.

For a convex body
K
, we define the
support function
of
K
by

h_{K}(x):=\underset{{y\in K}}{\max}\langle x,y\rangle.

Note that
h_{K}(x)=\|x\|_{K^{\circ}}
if
0\in K
.

We write
|K|
for the
m-
dimensional Lebesgue measure (volume) of a measurable set
K
where
m
is a dimension of the minimal affine space containing
K
.
For any sets
K
and
L
, the sumset
K+L=\{x+y:x\in K,y\in L\}
is called
Minkowski sum
of
K
and
L
.

2.2.
Anti-blocking bodies

A subspace
E
is a
coordinate subspace
if
E
is spanned by
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, where
I\subset[n]
, (where we use the notation
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
).
Denote by
\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n}
the collection of coordinate subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,k}\subset\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n}
the collection of coordinate subspace of dimension
k
.

A convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
is called
anti-blocking
if for any
E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n}
,
P_{E}(K)=K\cap E
. Note that the collection of anti-blocking bodies are closed under Minkowski sum.

The following decomposition of anti-blocking bodies was proved in
[
14
,
6
]
: for any pair of anti-blocking bodies
A
and
B
, one has

(9)

A-B=\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n}}\left(P_{E}A-P_{E^{\bot}}B\right).

The above union is almost disjoint with respect to Lebesgue measure which immediately gives a very useful formula:

(10)

\left|A-B\right|=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n}}\left|P_{E}A\right|\left|P_{E^{\bot}}B\right|.

It is worth mentioning that we agree that
\left|P_{E}K\right|=1
when
E=\{0\}
. Note that it is clear that (
10
) holds for any measure that is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure.

It was proved in
[
6
]
that for any pair of anti-blocking bodies
A
and
B
, one has

(11)

\left|A+B\right|\leq\left|A-B\right|.

The proof of (
11
) relies on the Reverse Kleitman inequality
[
11
]
. The inequality (
11
) can be also proved directly using a symmetrization argument, which we reproduce for the case of
L^{p}-
sums in Lemma
19
, below.

Consider
\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
and a set
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we define

\delta S
to be a set such that
\delta S:=\{(\delta_{1}x_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}x_{n}):x\in S\}
.
A convex body
K
is
locally anti-blocking
, if for any
\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
,
\delta K\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
is anti-blocking. A
1-unconditional convex body
K is defined by
\delta K=K
, for all
\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
. We observe that any 1-unconditional convex body is a locally anti-blocking body. We refer to
[
6
,
36
]
for many more results and inequalities for (locally) anti-blocking bodies.

2.3.
Inequalities on the volume of projections

A classical inequality relating volume of a convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and volume of its projections is the
Loomis-Whitney inequality

[
23
]
:

(12)

\left|K\right|^{n-1}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left|P_{e_{i}^{\perp}}K\right|.

The inequality (
12
) was generalized by Bollobás and Thomason in
[
10
]
. For every
\tau\subset[n]
, we define
E_{\tau}:=\operatorname{span}\{e_{i}:i\in\tau\}^{\perp}
.
For
\sigma\subset[n]
, the sets
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\subset\sigma
form a
\lambda-
uniform cover of
\sigma
, if every
j\in\sigma
, it belongs to exactly
\lambda
of the sets
\sigma_{i}
. Note that
E_{\sigma}\subset E_{\sigma_{i}}
for any
i\in[m]
.

It was proved in
[
10
]
that for any compact set
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for any
\lambda-
uniform cover
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
of
[n]
, one has

(13)

\left|K\right|^{\lambda}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}^{\perp}}K\right|.

The local version of (
13
) is a particular case of local versions of Alexandrov-Fenchel’s inequality. The first inequality of such type was proved by Fenchel (see
[
16
]
and also
[
35
]
) and further generalized in
[
19
,
5
,
37
,
39
]
.

A local version of (
13
) was observed by Brazitikos, Giannopoulos and Liakopoulos in
[
13
]
: For any convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for any
\lambda-
uniform cover
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
of
\sigma
,

(14)

\left|K\right|^{m-\lambda}\left|P_{E_{\sigma}}K\right|^{\lambda}\leq\binom{n}{n-|\sigma|}^{\lambda-m}\binom{n-\frac{\lambda|\sigma|}{m}}{n-|\sigma|}^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}}K\right|,

where from now we will denote the cardinality of
\sigma
by
|\sigma|
.

Inequality (
14
) was further improved by Alonso-Gutiérrez, Artstein-Avidan, González Merino, Jiménez and Villa in
[
2
]
: For any convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for any 1-uniform cover
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
of
\sigma
,

(15)

|K||P_{E_{\sigma}}K|\leq\frac{\binom{i}{i+j-n}\binom{j}{i+j-n}}{\binom{n}{i+j-n}}|P_{E_{\sigma_{1}}}K|\,|P_{E_{\sigma_{2}}}K|,

where

i=\dim E_{\sigma_{1}}
,
j=\dim E_{\sigma_{2}}.

Inequality (
15
) was used in
[
18
]
to compute the lower bound for the best constant
c_{n}
defined in (
2
).
In Section 3 of this paper, we extend inequality (
15
) to the case of
\lambda-
uniform cover
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
of
\sigma
.

Finally, we would like to remind a result of Rogers and Shephard
[
30
]
, who established a sharp lower bound for the volume of a convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in terms of the volumes of its projection and a maximal section:

(16)

\left|P_{H}K\right|\max_{x_{0}\in H}\left|K\cap\left(x_{0}+H^{\perp}\right)\right|\leq\binom{n}{k}\operatorname{vol}(K),

where
H
is a
k-
dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

2.4.
s-
concave measure

A measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is
s-
concave
if for any non-empty compact sets
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for any
t\in[0,1]
, one has

\mu(tA+(1-t)B)^{s}\geq t\mu(A)^{s}+(1-t)\mu(B)^{s}.

The classical Brunn-Minkowski inequality demonstrates that the Lebesgue measure is
1/n
-concave (See
[
35
]
), i.e.,

(17)

|tA+(1-t)B|^{1/n}\geq t|A|^{1/n}+(1-t)|B|^{1/n}.

We say that the Borel measure
\mu
has
density
, if it has a locally integrable derivative, i.e.,

\frac{d\mu(x)}{dx}=\phi(x),

where
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
\phi\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. A measure
\mu
is
unconditional with decreasing density
, if

1.

For any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\phi(\pm x_{1},\ldots,\pm x_{n})=\phi(x)
.

2.

For any
i\in[n]
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the function

t\mapsto\phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},t,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n})

is non-increasing on
[0,\infty)
.

Livshyts, Marsiglietti, Nayar and the third named author proved in
[
22
]
that

Theorem 1
.

Any unconditional, product measure with decreasing density is
1/n
-concave on the class of 1-unconditional convex bodies.

2.5.
Berwald inequality

The classical Berwald inequality was introduced in
[
7
,
12
]
:

Theorem 2

(Berwald inequality)
.

Let
f
be a non-negative, concave function supported on a convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then, for any
-1<p\leq q<\infty
,

(18)

\left(\frac{\binom{n+p}{p}}{|K|}\int_{K}f(y)^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\frac{\binom{n+q}{q}}{|K|}\int_{K}f(y)^{q}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Recently, Langharst and Putterman
[
21
]
extended the inequality (
18
) to the case of
s-
concave measures.

Theorem 3

(Berwald inequality for
s-
concave measures)
.

Fix
s>0
and let
f
be a non-negative, concave function supported on a convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
\mu
be a finite, Borel
s-
concave measure supported on
K
.
Then, for any
-1<p\leq q<\infty
,

(19)

\left(\frac{\binom{\frac{1}{s}+p}{p}}{|K|}\int_{K}f(y)^{p}d\mu(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\frac{\binom{\frac{1}{s}+q}{q}}{|K|}\int_{K}f(y)^{q}d\mu(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

3.
Local Loomis-Whitney inequality

We remind that
E_{\tau}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{i}:i\in\tau\}^{\perp}
, for every
\tau\subset[n]
and for
\sigma\subset[n]
, the sets
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\subset\sigma
form a
\lambda-
uniform cover of
\sigma
, if every
j\in\sigma
, it belongs to exactly
\lambda
of the sets
\sigma_{i}
.

The Theorem
4
gives a slight improvement of (
14
), so that the multiplicative constant in (
14
) depends on the cover and is sharp. The case
|\sigma_{1}|=\ldots=|\sigma_{m}|
of Theorem
4
is included in (
14
).

Theorem 4
.

Let
\sigma\subset[n]
,
m\geq\lambda\geq 1
and let
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})
be
\lambda-
uniform cover of
\sigma
. For any convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one has

(20)

\left|K\right|^{m-\lambda}\left|P_{E_{\sigma}}K\right|^{\lambda}\leq\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n-|\sigma_{i}|}{n-|\sigma|}}{\binom{n}{n-|\sigma|}^{m-\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}}K\right|.

Proof.

The proof is based on the method from
[
13
]
.
The case
m=\lambda
is trivial. Assume
m>\lambda
. We have

\lambda|\sigma|=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\sigma_{i}|.

For every
y\in P_{E_{\sigma}}K
, we define the sets

K(y):=\{t\in E_{\sigma}^{\perp}:y+t\in K\},

and

K_{i}(y):=\{t\in E_{\sigma}^{\perp}\cap E_{\sigma_{i}}:y+t\in P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}}K\}.

Note that
(\sigma\smallsetminus\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma\smallsetminus\sigma_{m})
is
(m-\lambda)-
uniform cover of
\sigma
. Using (
13
), we have

|K|=\int_{P_{E_{\sigma}}K}\left|K(y)\right|dy\leq\int_{P_{E_{\sigma}}K}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left|K_{i}(y)\right|^{\frac{1}{m-\lambda}}dy.

For each
i
, we define
f_{i}:P_{E_{\sigma}}K\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by

f_{i}(y):=\left|K_{i}(y)\right|^{\frac{1}{|\sigma|-|\sigma_{i}|}}.

Using Hölder inequality, we have

|K|\leq\int_{P_{E_{\sigma}}K}\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(y)^{\frac{|\sigma|-|\sigma_{i}|}{m-\lambda}}dy\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\int_{P_{E_{\sigma}}K}f_{i}(y)^{|\sigma|}dy\right)^{\frac{|\sigma|-|\sigma_{i}|}{|\sigma|(m-\lambda)}}.

Applying (
17
), we get that
f_{i}
is concave, thus, we can apply the Berwald inequality (
18
) to get

\left(\frac{\binom{n}{n-|\sigma|}}{|P_{E_{\sigma}}K|}\int_{P_{E_{\sigma}}K}f_{i}(y)^{|\sigma|}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{|\sigma|}}\leq\left(\frac{\binom{n-|\sigma_{i}|}{n-|\sigma|}}{|P_{E_{\sigma}}K|}\int_{P_{E_{\sigma}}K}f_{i}(y)^{|\sigma|-|\sigma_{i}|}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{|\sigma|-|\sigma_{i}|}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|K|^{m-\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\int_{P_{E_{\sigma}}K}f_{i}(y)^{|\sigma|}dy\right)^{\frac{|\sigma|-|\sigma_{i}|}{|\sigma|}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\binom{n}{n-|\sigma|}^{m-\lambda}|P_{E_{\sigma}}K|^{\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n-|\sigma_{i}|}{n-|\sigma|}\int_{P_{E_{\sigma}}K}f_{i}(y)^{|\sigma|-|\sigma_{i}|}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{n-|\sigma|}^{m-\lambda}|P_{E_{\sigma}}K|^{\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n-|\sigma_{i}|}{n-|\sigma|}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}}K\right|.\qed

Remark 5
.

The equality in (
20

We start with a lemma which provides an upper bound on the Minkowski sum of anti-blocking bodies using the volume of their projections.

Lemma 8
.

Fix a positive integer

be anti-blocking bodies in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\left|A_{1}+\ldots+A_{r}\right|\leq\sum_{(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}^{\perp}}A_{i}\right|\right),

where the sum is taken over all 1-uniform covers
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})
of
[n]

Proof.

The proof is by induction on

r=1
is trivial. Using inductive assumption, we get

\left|A_{1}+\ldots+A_{r}\right|\leq\sum_{(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r-1})}\left(\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{r-1}}^{\perp}}(A_{r-1}+A_{r})\right|\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r-2}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}^{\perp}}A_{i}\right|\right).

From (
11
) and (
10
), we have

\displaystyle\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{r-1}}^{\perp}}(A_{r-1}+A_{r})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{r-1}}^{\perp}}A_{r-1}-P_{E_{\sigma_{r-1}}^{\perp}}A_{r}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\sigma_{r-1}^{\prime},\sigma_{r}^{\prime})}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{r-1}^{\prime}}^{\perp}}A_{r-1}\right|\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{r}^{\prime}}^{\perp}}A_{r}\right|,

where the sum is taken over 1-uniform cover
(\sigma_{r-1}^{\prime},\sigma_{r}^{\prime})
of
\sigma_{r-1}
. Then,

\left|A_{1}+\ldots+A_{r}\right|\leq\sum_{(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r-2},\sigma_{r-1}^{\prime},\sigma_{r}^{\prime})}\left(\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{r-1}^{\prime}}^{\perp}}A_{r-1}\right|\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{r}^{\prime}}^{\perp}}A_{r}\right|\prod_{i=1}^{r-2}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}^{\perp}}A_{i}\right|\right).

The proof is finished since
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r-2},\sigma_{r-1}^{\prime},\sigma_{r}^{\prime})
is 1-uniform of
[n]
.
∎

5.
L_{p}-
sums of anti-blocking bodies

Firey
[
17
]
extended the concept of Minkowski sum to
L_{p}-
sums of convex bodies
K
and
L
containing the origin, so-called
K\oplus_{p}L
where
p\geq 1
, by using support functions:

h_{K\oplus_{p}L}^{p}=h_{K}^{p}+h_{L}^{p}.

The theory of Firey sum was futher developed by Lutwak, who created the notion of
L_{p}-
mixed volumes and proved a number of very useful inequalities
[
24
,
25
]
.
Lutwak, Yang, Zhang
[
26
]
extended the notion of
L_{p}-
sums to the case of non-convex sets: for any
K,L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
p>1
:

K\oplus_{p}L:=\{(1-t)^{1/q}x+t^{1/q}y:x\in K,y\in L,t\in[0,1]\},

where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
.
The following lemma is a well-known formula for the volume of a direct Firey sum.

Lemma 14
.

Let
E
be an
i-
dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
A,B
be convex bodies, containing the origin, such that
A\subset E
and
B\subset E^{\bot}
. Then, for any
p\geq 1
,

(28)

|A\oplus_{p}B|=\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\left|A\right|\left|B\right|.

Proof.

Using that
A
and
B
belong to orthogonal subspaces, we get that

\|x\|_{A\oplus_{p}B}^{q}=\|x\|^{q}_{(A^{\circ}\oplus_{q}B^{\circ})^{\circ}}=h^{q}_{A^{\circ}\oplus_{q}B^{\circ}}=h^{q}_{A^{\circ}}+h^{q}_{B^{\circ}}=\|x_{1}\|_{A}^{q}+\|x_{2}\|_{B}^{q},

for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
x_{1}=P_{E}x,x_{2}=P_{E^{\perp}}x
.
Next, using that

(29)

|K|=\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\|x\|_{K}^{q}}dx,

for a convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, containing the origin (see, for example,
[
34
]
), we get

\displaystyle\left|A\oplus_{p}B\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\|x\|_{A\oplus_{p}B}^{q}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}\int_{E}e^{-\|x_{1}\|_{A}^{q}}dx_{1}\int_{E^{\perp}}e^{-\|x_{2}\|_{B}^{q}}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1+\frac{i}{q})\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n-i}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}|A||B|.\qed

Lemma 15
.

Let
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
be anti-blocking bodies. Then, for any
p\geq 1
,

(30)

|A\oplus_{p}-B|=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i}}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}|P_{E}A||P_{E^{\bot}}B|.

Proof.

\displaystyle A\oplus_{p}-B

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\left\{(1-t)^{1/q}x+t^{1/q}y:x\in A,y\in-B\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\left((1-t)^{1/q}A-t^{1/q}B\right).

Therefore, from (
9
),

\displaystyle A\oplus_{p}-B

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\left((1-t)^{1/q}A-t^{1/q}B\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n}}\left((1-t)^{1/q}P_{E}A-t^{1/q}P_{E^{\bot}}B\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n}}\left(P_{E}A\oplus_{p}-P_{E^{\bot}}B\right).

It follows that

|A\oplus_{p}-B|=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i}}|P_{E}A\oplus_{p}-P_{E^{\bot}}B|.

We use (
28
) to finish the proof.
∎

Corollary 17

[

Let

The case of anti-blocking body,

Lemma 18
.

Let
A,B
be convex bodies. Then,

S_{i}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{i}(B)\subset S_{i}(A\oplus_{p}B).

Proof.

The proof follows from the definition of

\displaystyle S_{i}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{i}(B)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\left(t^{1/q}S_{i}(A)+(1-t)^{1/q}S_{i}(B)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\left(S_{i}(t^{1/q}A)+S_{i}((1-t)^{1/q}B)\right)

\displaystyle\subset S_{i}\left(\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\left(t^{1/q}A+(1-t)^{1/q}B\right)\right)

\displaystyle=S_{i}(A\oplus_{p}B).\qed

Lemma 19
.

Let
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
be anti-blocking bodies. Then,

\left|A\oplus_{p}B\right|\leq\left|A\oplus_{p}-B\right|.

Using Lemma

\left|A\oplus_{p}-B\right|=\left|S_{n}(A\oplus_{p}-B)\right|\geq\left|S_{n}(A)\oplus_{p}S_{n}(-B)\right|=\left|A\oplus_{p}S_{n}(-B)\right|.

We repeat the process by applying

\left|A\oplus_{p}-B\right|\geq\left|A\oplus_{p}S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{n}(-B)\right|.

We finish the proof using that

Theorem 20

\left|A\right|\left|A\oplus_{p}-B\oplus_{p}-C\right|\leq b(n,p)\left|A\oplus_{p}-B\right|\left|A\oplus_{p}-C\right|

Then,

b(n,p)=\underset{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\max}\,\left(d_{n,2}(i,j)\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{i+j-n}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{i}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{j}{q}\right)}\right),

where

is defined in (

Proof.

We follow the proof of Theorem

.
Let

\beta:=\underset{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\max}\,\left(d_{n,2}(i,j)\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{i+j-n}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{i}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{j}{q}\right)}\right),

To prove that

Using (

\displaystyle\left|A\right|\left|A\oplus_{p}-B\oplus_{p}-C\right|

\displaystyle=\left|A\right|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,k}}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{k}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n-k}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}\left|P_{E}A\right|\left|P_{E^{\perp}}(B\oplus_{p}C)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left|A\right|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,k}}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{k}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n-k}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}\left|P_{E}A\right|\left|P_{E^{\perp}}B\oplus_{p}-P_{E^{\perp}}C\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\left|A\right|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,k}}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\underset{H\subset E^{\perp}}{\sum_{H\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,l},}}\left(\beta_{1}(k,l)\left|P_{E}A\right|\left|P_{H}B\right|\left|P_{(E+H)^{\perp}}C\right|\right),

where

\beta_{1}(k,l):=\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{k}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{l}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n-k-l}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}.

Using (
30
), we have

(40)

\begin{split}&\left|A\oplus_{p}-B\right|\left|A\oplus_{p}-C\right|\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,j}}\left(\beta_{2}(i,j)\left|P_{F}A\right|\left|P_{G}A\right|\left|P_{F^{\perp}}B\right|\left|P_{G^{\perp}}C\right|\right),\end{split}

where

\beta_{2}(i,j):=\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{i}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n-i}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{j}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n-j}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)^{2}}.

It is enough to consider only the terms with
F=H^{\perp}
and
G=E+H
in the right hand side of (

\displaystyle\beta_{1}(i+j-n,n-i)\left|A\right|\left|P_{F\cap G}A\right|\leq\beta\cdot\beta_{2}(i,j)\left|P_{F}A\right|\left|P_{G}A\right|,

where

and
F\cap G=E
which is true by the definition of

.

Next, we will prove that
\beta
is the best constant.
Fix
i,j
such that
i+j-n\geq 0
, let
E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i},F\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,j}
be such that

. Let

and

A=tK

\frac{\left|K\right|\left|P_{E\cap H}K\right|}{\left|P_{E}K\right|\left|P_{H}K\right|}=d_{n,2}(i,j).

Using (
30

(42)

\begin{split}&|A\oplus_{p}-B\oplus_{p}-C|\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{H\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,k}}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{k}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n-k}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}t^{k}\left|P_{H}K\right|\left|P_{H^{\perp}}(U\oplus_{p}V)\right|_{n-k}\right).\end{split}

Since

Next, in Lemma

Lemma 22
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be locally anti-blocking, and let
E:=\operatorname{span}\left\{e_{i}:i\in I\right\}
for some

. Let
\tau,\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
be two orthant signs, such that
\left.\tau\right|_{I}=\left.\delta\right|_{I}

P_{E}K_{\delta}=P_{E}K_{\tau}.

Lemma 23
.

Let
K
be a locally anti-blocking body. Then,

|K\oplus_{p}-K|\leq\kappa_{n,q}|K|

where

\kappa_{n,q}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\binom{n}{i}^{2}.

Moreover,
\kappa_{n,q}
is the best possible constant.

Proof.

For any locally anti-blocking bodies
K,L
, we have

\displaystyle K\oplus_{p}L

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\left((1-t)^{1/q}K+t^{1/q}L\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\bigcup_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left((1-t)^{1/q}K_{\delta}+t^{1/q}L_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\left((1-t)^{1/q}K_{\delta}+t^{1/q}L_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left(K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}L_{\delta}\right).

Note that sets
K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}L_{\delta},
in the above union, are disjoint up to a set of measure zero, so we have

\left|K\oplus_{p}-K\right|=\sum_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left|K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}(-K)_{\delta}\right|.

Using (
38
), since
(-K)_{\delta}=-(K_{-\delta})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|K\oplus_{p}-K\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left|K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}(-K)_{\delta}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left|K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}-(K_{-\delta})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left|K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}K_{-\delta}\right|.

Next,

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left|K_{\delta}\oplus_{p}K_{-\delta}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i}}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\left|P_{E}K_{\delta}\right|\left|P_{E^{\perp}}K_{-\delta}\right|.

Fixing
E=\operatorname{span}\{e_{i}:i\in I\}\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i}
, for each
\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
, we claim that there exists

\tau\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
such that

(46)

P_{E}K_{\delta}=P_{E}K_{\tau}\text{ and }P_{E^{\perp}}K_{-\delta}=P_{E^{\perp}}K_{\tau}.

Moreover, it is a bijection.
Indeed, the map
\tau\longmapsto\delta(E,\tau)
where
\delta=\delta(E,\tau)
is unique vector satisfying

\left.\delta\right|_{I}=\left.\tau\right|_{I},\left.\quad\delta\right|_{I^{c}}=-\left.\tau\right|_{I^{c}}.

Applying Lemma
22
, we get (
46
).
Using (
46
), (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\left|K\oplus_{p}-K\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i}}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\sum_{\tau\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left|P_{E}K_{\tau}\right|\left|P_{E^{\perp}}K_{\tau}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i}}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\sum_{\tau\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\binom{n}{i}\left|K_{\tau}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{G}^{c}_{n,i}}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\binom{n}{i}\left|K\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\binom{n}{i}^{2}\left|K\right|.

Note that the constant is sharp when
K
is a simplex.
∎

We note that it follows from Lutwak’s Brunn-Minkowski inequality for
L_{p}-

[

,
26
]
that
|K\oplus_{p}-K|\geq 2^{n/p}|K|.
Using comparison between

sums and Minkowski sums together with classical Rogers-Shephard inequality one can get that
\kappa_{n,q}\leq\binom{2n}{n}
. One can also get this estimate directly using the properties of Binomial coefficients, see Proposition

Lemma 27
.

Let
K
be a locally anti-blocking body. Then, for any

,

(50)

\mu(S_{i}(K))\leq\mu(K),

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that

This implies that

Then,

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(K\cap(x+{\mathbb{R}}e_{n}))

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},t)d\mu_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\mu(K)

\displaystyle=\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}K}\mu_{n}(K\cap(x+{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}))d\mu_{n^{c}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}K}\mu_{n}(S_{i}(K)\cap(x+{\mathbb{R}}e_{i}))d\mu_{n^{c}}

Lemma 31
.

The equality in inequality (
20
) can be reached by using the following Hanner polytope,

K=\operatorname{conv}\left\{\sum_{i\in\sigma}[-e_{i},e_{i}],\sum_{i\not\in\sigma}[-e_{i},e_{i}]\right\},

where
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
is a
\lambda-
uniform cover of
\sigma
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\sigma=\{1,\ldots,|\sigma|\}
. Using (
29
) we get

\displaystyle\left|K\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{-\|x\|_{K}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{-\|x_{1}\|_{B_{\infty}^{|\sigma|}}}dx_{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{-\|x_{2}\|_{B_{\infty}^{n-|\sigma|}}}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{n-|\sigma|}^{-1}\left|B_{\infty}^{|\sigma|}\right|\left|B_{\infty}^{n-|\sigma|}\right|=\binom{n}{n-|\sigma|}^{-1}2^{n}.

Similarly, we have

\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}}K\right|=\binom{n-|\sigma_{i}|}{n-|\sigma|}^{-1}2^{n-|\sigma_{i}|}\mbox{ and }\left|P_{E_{\sigma}}K\right|=2^{n-|\sigma|}.

Then,

\frac{\left|K\right|^{m-\lambda}\left|P_{E_{\sigma}}K\right|^{\lambda}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left|P_{E_{\sigma_{i}}}K\right|}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n-|\sigma_{i}|}{n-|\sigma|}}{\binom{n}{n-|\sigma|}^{m-\lambda}}.\qed

We refer to
[
1
]
, pp 254-259, for the basic properties of the digamma function.
The digamma function is the logarithm derivative of the Gamma function. In particular, if

\psi(z)=\frac{d}{dz}\ln\Gamma(z)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(z)}{\Gamma(z)},

then

(52)

\psi(z+1)=\psi(z)+\frac{1}{z},

and, for any
z\neq-1,-2,\ldots
, one has

(53)

\psi(z)=-\gamma+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{t+z}\right),

where
\gamma
is known as the Euler constant.

Lemma 32
.

The optimization of the constant in (
26
) when
m=2
is divided into three cases:

\zeta_{n,2}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(2k)!^{3}}{(3k)!k!^{3}};&n=3k\\
\displaystyle\frac{(2k+1)^{2}}{(k+1)(3k+1)}\frac{(2k)!^{3}}{(3k)!k!^{3}};&n=3k+1\\
\displaystyle\frac{2(2k+1)^{3}}{(k+1)(3k+1)(3k+2)}\frac{(2k)!^{3}}{(3k)!k!^{3}};&n=3k+2.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall the definition
\zeta_{n,2}
for
m=2
,

\zeta_{n,2}=\max d_{n,2}(i,j)=\max\frac{\binom{i}{i+j-n}\binom{j}{i+j-n}}{\binom{n}{i+j-n}},

where the maximum is taken over all non-negative integers
i,j\leq n
and

i+j-n\geq 0.
Let’s fix
i+j=l
and, without loss of generality, assume that
i\geq j
.
We define the function
f_{l}
on
[n/2,n]
as follows:

\displaystyle f_{l}(x)

\displaystyle:=\log\left[\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(l-x+1)\Gamma(2n-l+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(l-n+1)\Gamma(n-x+1)\Gamma(n+x-l+1)}\right].

Note that for any integer
i
,
\log d(i,l-i)=f_{l}(i)
.
Then,

f^{\prime}_{l}(x)=\psi(x+1)-\psi(l-x+1)+\psi(n-x+1)-\psi(n+x-l+1).

Using (
52
), we have

\psi(x+1)-\psi(n+x-l+1)=\sum_{t=1}^{l-n}\frac{1}{t+x+n-l}

and

-\psi(l-x+1)+\psi(n-x+1)=-\sum_{t=1}^{l-n}\frac{1}{t-x+n}.

Thus,

f^{\prime\prime}_{l}(x)=-\sum_{t=1}^{n-l}\left(\frac{1}{(t+x+n-l)^{2}}+\frac{1}{(t-x+n)^{2}}\right)<0.

Therefore, the function
f_{l}
is concave. Since
f_{l}(x)=f_{l}(l-x)
,
one can deduce that

f_{l}
is reached the maximum at
x=l/2
.
Similarly, we define a function
g
on
[n/2,n]
,

g(x):=\log\left[\frac{\Gamma^{2}(x+1)\Gamma(2n-2x+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(2x-n+1)\Gamma^{2}(n-x+1)}\right].

Note that for any integer
i
,
d_{n,2}(i,i)=g(i)
. Using (
53
), we have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=2\psi(x+1)-2\psi(2n-2x+1)-2\psi(2x-n+1)+2\psi(n-x+1)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{t\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{2n-2x+t}-\frac{1}{x+t}+\frac{1}{2x-n+t}-\frac{1}{n-x+t}\right)

\displaystyle=2(3x-2n)\sum_{t\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{(2n-2x+t)(x+t)}-\frac{1}{(2x-n+t)(n-x+t)}\right)

\displaystyle=2(3x-2n)(x-n)\sum_{t\geq 1}\frac{n+2t}{(2n-2x+t)(x+t)(2x-n+t)(n-x+t)}.

Since the last summation is positive, we have that
g^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x<2n/3
and
g^{\prime}(x)<0
for
2n/3<x
. Therefore, the maximum of
g
reaches at
x_{\max}=2n/3
.

•

If
n=3k
, then
\underset{n/2<i<n}{\max}g(i)=g(2k).
We get

\zeta_{3k,2}=\frac{(2k)!^{3}}{(3k)!k!^{3}}.

•

If
n=3k+1,
then
x_{\max}=2k+2/3
. We get

\displaystyle\zeta_{3k+1,2}

\displaystyle=\max\{g(2k),g(2k+1),f_{4k+1}(2k+1)\}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k+1)^{2}}{(k+1)(3k+1)}\frac{(2k)!^{3}}{(3k)!k!^{3}}.

•

If
n=3k+2,
then
x_{\max}=2k+4/3
. We get

\displaystyle\zeta_{3k+2,2}

\displaystyle=\max\{g(2k+1),g(2k+2),f_{4k+3}(2k+2)\}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(2k+1)^{3}}{(k+1)(3k+1)(3k+2)}\frac{(2k)!^{3}}{(3k)!k!^{3}}.\qed

Proposition 34
.

The optimization of the constant in (

) is divided into two cases:

Proof.

r_{n,i}=\max_{j}d_{n,2}(i,j)=\max\frac{\binom{i}{i+j-n}\binom{j}{i+j-n}}{\binom{n}{i+j-n}},

j\leq n
such that
i+j\geq n+1
. We define the function
g
on
[n-i,n]
as follow:

g(x)=\log\left[\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(i+1)\Gamma(2n-i-x+1)}{\Gamma(n-x+1)\Gamma(n-i+1)\Gamma(i+x-n+1)\Gamma(n+1)}\right].

Note that for any integer
j
,
\log d_{n,2}(i,j)=g(j)
. By using (
53
),
g^{\prime}(x)
can be expanded as

(2x-2n+i)(i-n)\sum_{t\geq 1}\frac{2t+n}{(2n-i-x+t)(x+t)(n-x+t)(i+x-n+t)}.

Since the summation is positive, we have
g^{\prime}(x)<0
for
x>n-i/2
and

for
x<n-i/2
. So,
g
reaches the maximum at
n-i/2
.

If

, then

Proposition 35
.

Let
b(n,p)
be the constant from Theorem
20
, then

\frac{2}{e\sqrt{\pi n}}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{n}\leq b(n,p)\leq\frac{2^{n+1/2}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}.

Proof.

Recall that

b(n,p)=\underset{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\max}\,\left(d_{n,2}(i,j)\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{i+j-n}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{i}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{j}{q}\right)}\right).

Fix
n,i,j
such that
k:=i+j-n\geq 0
, and define a function
f:[1,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

f(q)=\log\left[\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{k}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{i}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{j}{q}\right)}\right].

Then,

f^{\prime}(q)=-\frac{k}{q^{2}}\psi\left(1+\frac{k}{q}\right)-\frac{n}{q^{2}}\psi\left(1+\frac{n}{q}\right)+\frac{i}{q^{2}}\psi\left(1+\frac{i}{q}\right)+\frac{j}{q^{2}}\psi\left(1+\frac{j}{q}\right).

Using (
53
), we have

f^{\prime}(q)=(n-i)\sum_{t\geq 1}\left(\frac{t}{(qt+n)(qt+i)}-\frac{t}{(qt+j)(qt+k)}\right).

Then,
f
is decreasing on
[1,\infty)
as a function of
q
, which implies that
b(n,p)
is increasing as
p
increase. We have

\underset{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\max}\,d_{n,2}(i,j)=b(n,1)\leq b(n,p)\leq b(n,\infty)=\underset{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\max}\,\frac{(2n-i-j)!}{(n-i)!(n-j)!}.

It was provided in
[
18
]
by using Stirling approximation that

\underset{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\max}d_{n,2}(i,j)\geq\frac{2}{e\sqrt{\pi n}}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{n}.

Fix
l=i+j
, we define function
g_{l}
by

g_{l}(x)=\log\left[\frac{\Gamma(1+2n-l)}{\Gamma(1+n-l+x)\Gamma(1+n-x)}\right].

Taking the derivative and using (
53
), we get

g_{l}^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{t\geq 0}\frac{l-2x}{(n-l+x+t)(t+n-x)}.

The function
g
reaches the maximum when
x=l/2
. It implies that

\underset{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\max}\,\frac{(2n-i-j)!}{(n-i)!(n-j)!}\leq\underset{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\max}\,\frac{(2n-2i)!}{((n-i)!)^{2}}\leq\binom{n}{n/2}.

By using the Stirling’s approximation, we have

\binom{n}{n/2}\leq\frac{2^{n+1/2}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}.\qed

Proposition 36
.

Let
\kappa_{n,q}
be the constant from Lemma
23
, then

2^{n}\leq\kappa_{n,q}\leq\binom{2n}{n}.

Proof.

Recall that,

\kappa_{n,q}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n/q}{i/q}^{-1}\binom{n}{i}^{2}.

Fix
n
and
i
, we define a function
f
on
[1,\infty)
as follows:

f(q)=\log\binom{n/q}{i/q}=\log\left[\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{q}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{i}{q}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n-i}{q}+1\right)}\right].

Then,

f^{\prime}(q)=-\frac{n}{q^{2}}\psi\left(\frac{n}{q}+1\right)+\frac{i}{q^{2}}\psi\left(\frac{i}{q}+1\right)+\frac{n-i}{q^{2}}\psi\left(\frac{n-i}{q}+1\right).

Using (
53
), we have

f^{\prime}(q)=-\frac{i(n-i)}{q}\sum_{t\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{(tq+i)(tq+n)}+\frac{1}{(tq+n-i)(tq+n)}\right).

Then,
f
is decreasing on
[1,\infty]
which implies that
\kappa_{n,q}
is increasing as
q
increase. Hence,

2^{n}=\kappa_{n,1}\leq\kappa_{n,q}\leq\kappa_{n,\infty}=\binom{2n}{n}.\qed

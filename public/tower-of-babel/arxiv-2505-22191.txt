Approximation of Dirac operators with confining electrostatic and Lorentz scalar 𝜹 \boldsymbol{\delta} -shell potentials
Christian Stelzer-Landauer

Abstract
. In this paper we continue the investigations from [ 6 , 7 ] regarding the approximation of Dirac operators with δ \delta -shell potentials in the norm resolvent sense. In particular, we consider the approximation of Dirac operators with confining electrostatic and Lorentz scalar δ \delta -shell potentials, where the support of the δ \delta -shell potentials is impermeable to particles modelled by such Dirac operators.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
81Q10, 35P05, 35Q40

1.
Introduction

Dirac operators with
\delta
-shell potentials model the propagation of relativistic spin 1/2 particles in
\mathbb{R}^{\theta}
,
\theta\in\{2,3\}
, under the influence of an external field which is strongly localized around a hypersurface.
Mathematically, the self-adjointness and spectral properties of such operators were firstly considered in
[
13
]
in 1989. After a period with little progress, the seminal paper
[
1
]
, which appeared in 2014, sparked renewed interest in this topic and resulted in numerous publications since then; see for instance
[
8
,
12
,
20
]
. In the current paper we consider Dirac operators with
\delta
-shell potentials given by the (formal) expression

H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}=-i\sum_{j=1}^{\theta}\alpha_{j}\partial_{j}+m\beta+\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma},

(1.1)

where
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\theta},\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
,
N=2\lceil\theta/2\rceil
, are the Dirac matrices defined in Section
1.1
(
ii
),
m\in\mathbb{R}
represents the mass of the underlying particle and
\delta_{\Sigma}
is a
\delta
-shell potential supported on the boundary
\Sigma
of a
C^{2}
-smooth domain
\Omega_{+}
which is specified in (
iii
) of Section
1.1
. Moreover,
\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{\eta}I_{N}+\widetilde{\tau}\beta
, where
\widetilde{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\widetilde{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}
model electrostatic and Lorentz scalar interactions, respectively. If
\widetilde{d}=\widetilde{\eta}^{\,2}-\widetilde{\tau}^{\,2}\neq 4
, then the initially formally defined operator
H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}
can be rigorously realized as a self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})
with
\textup{dom }H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta}\setminus\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{N})
via transmission conditions on
\Sigma
; see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
18
,
20
]
. Here, the case

\widetilde{d}=\widetilde{\eta}^{\,2}-\widetilde{\tau}^{\,2}=-4

(1.2)

is particularly interesting since (
1.2
) implies that
H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}
splits into the orthogonal sum of two independent Dirac operators on the domains
\Omega_{\pm}
, where
\Omega_{-}:=\mathbb{R}^{\theta}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{+}}
. More precisely,
H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}=H_{\widetilde{V}}^{+}\oplus H_{\widetilde{V}}^{-}
with

\displaystyle\textup{dom }H_{\widetilde{V}}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{u_{\pm}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm};\mathbb{C}^{N}):u_{\pm}|_{\Sigma}=\pm\tfrac{i}{2}(\alpha\cdot\nu)\widetilde{V}u_{\pm}|_{\Sigma}\bigr\}\subset L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm};\mathbb{C}^{N}),

\displaystyle H_{\widetilde{V}}^{\pm}u_{\pm}

\displaystyle=-i(\alpha\cdot\nabla)u_{\pm}+m\beta u_{\pm},

where
\nu
denotes the unit outward normal vector of
\Omega_{+}
and
\alpha\cdot\nu:=\sum_{j=1}^{\theta}\alpha_{j}\nu_{j}
; see
[
2
, Theorem 5.5]
,
[
12
, Section 5]
or
[
20
, Example 12]
. On a physical level, this means that
\Sigma
becomes impermeable to a particle. Inspired by this observation we call
\widetilde{\eta}
and
\widetilde{\tau}
confining interaction strengths in this case. Furthermore, Dirac operators on domains are of interest on their own as they are used in three dimensions to describe relativistic particles confined to a box, including the famous MIT bag model, and in two dimensions they are applied in the description of graphene. For an overview of this topic we refer to the introductions of
[
4
,
10
,
11
]
and the references therein.

To justify the usage of
H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}
, one approximates Dirac operators with
\delta
-shell potentials by Dirac operators with strongly localized potentials in the strong or norm resolvent sense. Strong and norm resolvent convergence are well-suited for these approximation problems as they relate the spectra of the approximating operators and the limit operators. In order to define strongly localized potentials, we first introduce the mapping

\iota:\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{\theta},\quad\iota(x_{\Sigma},t):=x_{\Sigma}+t\nu(x_{\Sigma}),\quad(x_{\Sigma},t)\in\Sigma\times\mathbb{R},

(1.3)

and define for
\varepsilon>0
the set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\iota(\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
, which is the so-called
tubular neighbourhood
of
\Sigma
. According to
[
6
, Proposition 2.4]
, we can fix an

\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that
\iota\upharpoonright\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1})
is injective. Equipped with these notations we define based on

\displaystyle V=\eta I_{N}+\tau\beta,\,\quad\eta,\tau\in\mathbb{R},

(1.4)

\displaystyle q\in L^{\infty}((-1,1);[0,\infty))\text{ with }\int_{-1}^{1}q(s)\,ds=1,

(1.5)

for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
the strongly localized potentials

V_{\varepsilon}(x):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Vq(t/\varepsilon),&x=\iota(x_{\Sigma},t)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
0,&x\notin\Omega_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

(1.6)

Moreover, for
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
we introduce the self-adjoint operators

H_{V_{\varepsilon}}:=-i(\alpha\cdot\nabla)+m\beta+V_{\varepsilon},\quad\textup{dom }H_{V_{\varepsilon}}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N});

cf.
[
6
, eq. (1.8)]
.
It is known that
H_{V_{\varepsilon}}
converges for
\varepsilon\to 0
in the norm or strong resolvent sense (depending on the parameter
d=\eta^{2}-\tau^{2}
) to
H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}
with
\widetilde{V}=\textup{tanc}(\sqrt{d}/2)V
, where
tanc
is the function defined in Section
1.1
(
ix
); see
[
6
,
7
,
9
,
12
,
16
,
24
]
. Using the rescaling formula yields
\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{\eta}I_{N}+\widetilde{\tau}\beta
with
(\widetilde{\eta},\widetilde{\tau})=\textup{tanc}(\sqrt{d}/2)(\eta,\tau)
and hence

\widetilde{d}=\widetilde{\eta}^{\,2}-\widetilde{\tau}^{\,2}=d\textup{tanc}(\sqrt{d}/2)^{2}=4\tan(\sqrt{d}/2)^{2}.

(1.7)

Note that
\widetilde{d}\geq 0
for
d\geq 0
and
\widetilde{d}=-4\tanh(\sqrt{\lvert{d}\rvert}/2)
for
d<0
. In particular,
\widetilde{d}
is bigger than
-4
. This means that
H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}
with
\widetilde{d}=-4
, i.e. Dirac operators with confining interaction strengths, cannot be approximated with this approach. This raises the question whether there is also a way to approximate such Dirac operators by Dirac operators with strongly localized potentials.
Inspired by the rescaling of
d
from (
1.7
), one would have to choose
V=\eta I_{N}+\tau\beta
with
d=\eta^{2}-\tau^{2}=-\infty
as the interaction matrix in the approximating operators to obtain a Dirac operator with a
\delta
-shell potential and
\widetilde{d}=\widetilde{\eta}^{\,2}-\widetilde{\tau}^{\,2}=-4
in the limit. We rigorously realize this idea by choosing
\varepsilon
-dependent interaction strengths
\eta_{\varepsilon}
and
\tau_{\varepsilon}
such that
\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}-\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}\overset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}-\infty
. The same approach was used in
[
21
, Chapter 3]
when dealing with the one-dimensional counterpart. See also
[
25
]
for related considerations.

Next, let us describe this approach in more detail. First, we fix a continuous scaling function
f:(0,\varepsilon_{1})\to(0,\infty)
, which satisfies the condition

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}f(\varepsilon)=\infty\quad\textup{and}\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}f(\varepsilon)^{3/2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}=0

(1.8)

for a
\gamma\in(0,1/2)
. We also introduce the
\varepsilon
-dependent interaction strengths

(\eta_{\varepsilon},\tau_{\varepsilon})=f(\varepsilon)(\eta,\tau)\quad\textup{and}\quad d_{\varepsilon}=\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}-\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}=f(\varepsilon)^{2}d

and assume from now on

d=\eta^{2}-\tau^{2}<0,

(1.9)

which guarantees

d_{\varepsilon}=\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}-\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}=f(\varepsilon)^{2}d\overset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}-\infty.

Dirac operators with strongly localized potentials and the
\varepsilon
-dependent interaction matrix
\eta_{\varepsilon}I_{N}+\tau_{\varepsilon}\beta=f(\varepsilon)V
are given by

H_{f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}}u:=-i(\alpha\cdot\nabla)+m\beta+f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon},\quad\textup{dom }H_{f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N}),

where
V_{\varepsilon}
is defined by (
1.6
).
Note that for a fixed
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
the operator
H_{f(\varepsilon^{\prime})V_{\varepsilon}}
converges by
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
for
\varepsilon\to 0
in the norm resolvent sense to
H_{\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\delta_{\Sigma}}
, where

\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}:=\textup{tanc}(\sqrt{d_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}/2)f(\varepsilon^{\prime})V.

(1.10)

Relying on the methods developed in
[
6
,
7
]
, we prove in the present paper that the norm resolvent limit of
H_{f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}}
is the operator
H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}
with

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\textup{tanc}(\sqrt{d_{\varepsilon}}/2)f(\varepsilon)V

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{2\tan(f(\varepsilon)\sqrt{d}/2)}{f(\varepsilon)\sqrt{d}}f(\varepsilon)V

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{2\tan(f(\varepsilon)\sqrt{d}/2)}{\sqrt{d}}V

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{2\tanh(f(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\lvert{d}\rvert}/2)}{\sqrt{\lvert{d}\rvert}}V=\frac{2}{\sqrt{|d|}}V.

Consequently,
\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{\eta}I_{N}+\widetilde{\tau}\beta
with
(\widetilde{\eta},\widetilde{\tau})=(2/\sqrt{|d|})(\eta,\tau)
and, in turn, we have
\widetilde{d}=\widetilde{\eta}^{\,2}-\widetilde{\tau}^{\,2}=-4
, i.e.
\widetilde{\eta}
and
\widetilde{\tau}
are confining interaction strengths.

To prove the norm resolvent convergence, we find for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
the estimates

\lVert(H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}-z)^{-1}-(H_{\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon}\delta_{\Sigma}}-z)^{-1}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})}\leq Ce^{-f(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\lvert{d}\rvert}}

(1.11)

and

\lVert(H_{\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon}\delta_{\Sigma}}-z)^{-1}-(H_{f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}}-z)^{-1}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})}\leq Cf(\varepsilon)^{3/2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}

(1.12)

in Section
2
(Proposition
2.2
) and Section
3
(Proposition
3.4
), respectively. Using these two estimates and the triangle inequality yields the main result of this paper, which reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q
be as in (
1.5
),
V=\eta I_{N}+\tau\beta
,
\eta,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, satisfy (
1.9
),
V_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
1.6
),
f
be as in (
1.8
)
(
with
\gamma\in(0,1/2)
)
and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, set
\widetilde{V}=(2/\sqrt{|d|})V
, where
d=\eta^{2}-\tau^{2}
. Then, there exists a
\delta>0
and a
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}-z)^{-1}-(H_{f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}}-z)^{-1}

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(e^{-f(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\lvert{d}\rvert}}+f(\varepsilon)^{3/2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}\bigr)

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
. In particular,
H_{f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}}
converges to
H_{\widetilde{V}\delta_{\Sigma}}
in the norm resolvent sense as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Note that if
V=\eta I_{N}+\tau\beta
,
\eta,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
d=\eta^{2}-\tau^{2}=-4
, then

\widetilde{V}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lvert{d}\rvert}}V=V.

Hence, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
q
be as in (
1.5
),
V=\eta I_{N}+\tau\beta
,
\eta,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, fulfil the equation
d=\eta^{2}-\tau^{2}=-4
,
V_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
1.6
),
f
be as in (
1.8
)
(
with
\gamma\in(0,1/2)
)
and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Then, there exists a
\delta>0
and a
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(H_{f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}}-z)^{-1}-(H_{V\delta_{\Sigma}}-z)^{-1}

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(e^{-f(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\lvert{d}\rvert}}+f(\varepsilon)^{3/2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}\bigr)

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
. In particular,
H_{f(\varepsilon)V_{\varepsilon}}
converges to
H_{V\delta_{\Sigma}}
in the norm resolvent sense as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

This corollary shows that every Dirac operator with given constant confining Lorentz scalar and electrostatic interaction strengths is the norm resolvent limit of Dirac operators with strongly localized potentials.

Finally, let us mention that the present paper is based on Section 7.1 of the dissertation
[
22
]
, where for position dependent interaction strengths norm resolvent convergence with a weaker convergence rate was proven.

(viii)

2.
A bound for (
1.11
)

Before we estimate (
1.11
), we provide a resolvent formula for Dirac operators with
\delta
-shell potentials in terms of the resolvent of the free Dirac operator and potential and boundary integral operators associated to the Dirac operator. The free Dirac operator

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle:=-i(\alpha\cdot\nabla)+m\beta,\qquad\textup{dom }H:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N}),

is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N})
and
\sigma(H)=(-\infty,-\lvert m\rvert]\cup[\lvert m\rvert,\infty)
; see for instance
[
9
, Section 2]
for
\theta=2
and
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
for
\theta=3
. Moreover, for
z\in\rho(H)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(H)
,

\displaystyle R_{z}u(x)

\displaystyle:=(H-z)^{-1}u(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\theta}}G_{z}(x-y)u(y)\,dy,\quad u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N}),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{\theta},

where
G_{z}
is given for
\theta=2
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
by

\displaystyle G_{z}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{z^{2}-m^{2}}}{2\pi}K_{1}\big(-i\sqrt{z^{2}-m^{2}}\lvert{x}\rvert\big)\frac{\alpha\cdot x}{\lvert{x}\rvert}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}K_{0}\big(-i\sqrt{z^{2}-m^{2}}\lvert{x}\rvert\big)\left(m\beta+zI_{2}\right)

and for
\theta=3
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
by

G_{z}(x)=\left(zI_{4}+m\beta+i\left(1-i\sqrt{z^{2}-m^{2}}\lvert{x}\rvert\right)\frac{\alpha\cdot x}{\lvert{x}\rvert^{2}}\right)\frac{e^{i\sqrt{z^{2}-m^{2}}\lvert{x}\rvert}}{4\pi\lvert{x}\rvert};

cf., e.g.,
[
5
, eq. (3.2)]
,
[
8
, eq. (1.19)]
or
[
23
, eq. (1.263)]
. Here,
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
denote the modified Bessel functions of the second kind of order zero and one, respectively.
Next, let us introduce for
z\in\rho(H)
the potential integral operator

\displaystyle\Phi_{z}:L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{N})

\displaystyle\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\theta};\mathbb{C}^{N}),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{z}\psi(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}G_{z}(x-y_{\Sigma})\psi(y_{\Sigma})\,d\sigma(y_{\Sigma}).

By
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
this operator is well-defined and bounded. Additionally,
\Phi_{z}
also acts as a bounded operator from
H^{r}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{N})

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
m=0
,
q
be as in (
1.5
),
V=\eta I_{N}+\tau\beta
,
\eta,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy (
1.9
),
f
be as in (
1.8
) and
z\in i\mathbb{R}
. Then, there exists a
\delta_{3}>0
and a
C>0
such that
(I+B_{\varepsilon}(z)f(\varepsilon)Vq)^{-1}
is boundedly invertible in
\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma)
and

{\lVert{f(\varepsilon)Vq(I+B_{\varepsilon}(z)f(\varepsilon)Vq)^{-1}}\rVert}_{0\to 0}\leq Cf(\varepsilon)

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta_{3})
.

Proof.

We start by defining
\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z):=B_{\varepsilon}(z)M_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
. Note that according to (
3.1
) and (
3.7
) the equality

(\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}=(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}U_{\varepsilon}R_{z}U_{\varepsilon}^{*}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}M_{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{*}=M_{\varepsilon}^{-1}M_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}U_{\varepsilon}R_{\overline{z}}U_{\varepsilon}^{*}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}M_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{z})

holds in
\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma)
. We also introduce

D=\sqrt{q}\textup{diag}(\sqrt{|\eta+\tau|}I_{N/2},\sqrt{|\eta-\tau|}I_{N/2})

and

E_{\varepsilon}(z):=\beta+f(\varepsilon)\textup{sign}(\tau)D\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)D:\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma).

Next, we estimate the norm of the inverse of
E_{\varepsilon}(z)
. By
\beta^{2}=I_{N}
we obtain

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(z)(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}

\displaystyle=I+f(\varepsilon)\textup{sign}(\tau)D(\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)\beta+\beta(\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*})D

\displaystyle+f(\varepsilon)^{2}D\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)D(D\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)D)^{*}.

We claim that the operator

\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)\beta+\beta(\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}=\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)\beta+\beta\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{z})=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}U_{\varepsilon}(R_{z}\beta+\beta R_{\overline{z}})U_{\varepsilon}^{*}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}M_{\varepsilon}^{-1}

vanishes under the current assumptions. In fact, since
z\in i\mathbb{R}
, we have
{\overline{z}=-z}
, and as
m=0
, the anticommutation rules from Section
1.1
(
ii
) yield
H\beta=-\beta H
. Hence,

R_{z}\beta+\beta R_{\overline{z}}=(H-z)^{-1}\beta+\beta(H-\overline{z})^{-1}=\beta(-H-z)^{-1}+\beta(H+z)^{-1}=0.

This implies

E_{\varepsilon}(z)(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}=I+f(\varepsilon)^{2}D\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)D(D\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)D)^{*}.

Analogously, one obtains

(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}E_{\varepsilon}(z)=I+f(\varepsilon)^{2}(D\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)D)^{*}D\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)D.

Thus, the self-adjoint operators
(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}E_{\varepsilon}(z)
and
E_{\varepsilon}(z)(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}
are bounded from below by one. In particular,
(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}E_{\varepsilon}(z)
and
E_{\varepsilon}(z)(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}
are boundedly invertible in
\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma)
. Hence, the operators
\bigl((E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}E_{\varepsilon}(z)\bigr)^{-1}(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}
and
(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}\bigl(E_{\varepsilon}(z)(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}\bigr)^{-1}
are a bounded left and a bounded right inverse of
E_{\varepsilon}(z)
, respectively. Therefore,
E_{\varepsilon}(z)
is boundedly invertible in
\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma)
. Moreover, the norm estimate

{\lVert{E_{\varepsilon}(z)f}\rVert}_{0}=\sqrt{((E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{*}E_{\varepsilon}(z)f,f)_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma)}}\geq{\lVert{f}\rVert}_{0},\quad f\in\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma),

gives us

{\lVert{(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{-1}}\rVert}_{0\to 0}\leq 1.

(3.8)

Next, we choose
\delta_{3}\in(0,\varepsilon_{2}]
, with
\varepsilon_{2}
from Proposition
3.1
, sufficiently small such that

\|D(\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)-B_{\varepsilon}(z))D\|_{0\to 0}=\|DB_{\varepsilon}(z)(M_{\varepsilon}^{-1}-I)D\|_{0\to 0}\leq\frac{1}{2f(\varepsilon)}

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta_{3})
which is possible according to (
3.6
), Proposition
3.1
and the choice of
f
in (
1.8
). This estimate,

E_{\varepsilon}(z)-(\beta+f(\varepsilon)\textup{sign}(\tau)DB_{\varepsilon}(z)D)=f(\varepsilon)\textup{sign}(\tau)D(\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z)-B_{\varepsilon}(z))D,

(
3.8
) and the stability of bounded invertibility, see
[
14
, Chapter IV, Theorem 1.16]
, show that the operator
\beta+f(\varepsilon)\textup{sign}(\tau)DB_{\varepsilon}(z)D
is also boundedly invertible in
\mathcal{B}^{0}(\Sigma)
and

\displaystyle\bigl\|(\beta+f(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\textup{sign}(\tau)DB_{\varepsilon}(z)D)^{-1}\bigr\|_{0\to 0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bigl\|(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{-1}\bigr\|_{0\to 0}}{1-\|f(\varepsilon)\textup{sign}(\tau)D(B_{\varepsilon}(z)-\widetilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(z))D\|_{0\to 0}\bigl\|(E_{\varepsilon}(z))^{-1}\bigr\|_{0\to 0}}

. Hence, the same is true for

Applying

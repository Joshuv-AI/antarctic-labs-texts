Clinical Outcomes Following Bilateral Implantation of TECNIS Odyssey Intraocular Lenses in Japanese Eyes
Authors: Carlton Yuen, Camerynn L Keahi, Sophia Dutton, Caitlin K Yuen, Alana K Dunn, Brad Hall
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the clinical outcomes following bilateral TECNIS Odyssey implantation in patients who self-identify as Japanese.MethodsThis was a retrospective, single-site, single arm, observational study of clinical outcomes following bilateral TECNIS Odyssey implantation in patients who self-identify as Japanese. Outcome measures were monocular and binocular uncorrected and distance corrected visual acuities at distance (UDVA, CDVA) and near (UNVA, DCNVA; 40cm), refraction, and patient self-reported spectacle independence.ResultsData from a total of 30 patients were included. The percentages of patients with postoperative binocular visual acuity 20/20 or better were 100% (30/30) for UDVA, 100% (30/30) for CDVA, 93% (28/30) for UNVA, and 93% (28/30) for DCNVA. In addition, 100% (60/60) of eyes had ≤ 0.5 D of residual astigmatism, and 100% (60/60) of eyes had postoperative manifest refraction spherical equivalent (MRSE) of ≤ 0.5 D. Finally, 3% (1/30) of subjects reported using glasses for near and 3% (1/30) of subjects reported mild halos.ConclusionThe results of this study suggest good visual outcomes at distance and near, high spectacle independence, and low reports of halos following bilateral implantation with the Odyssey IOL in patients who self-identify as Japanese.

Introduction

Monofocal intraocular lenses (IOLs) are the most common type of lens implanted during cataract surgery. Monofocals provide excellent visual acuity to see far objects; however, patients may still require spectacles to see clearly at intermediate and near. Enhanced monofocals modify the optic design of traditional monofocals, with the aim for functional intermediate vision.1,2

Two additional types of IOLs aim to provide clear vision at all ranges of vision (distance, intermediate, and near). These are multifocal IOLs and extended depth of focus (EDOF) IOLs, with each having a different optical approach. Multifocal IOLs are designed to create more than one distinct focal point, while EDOF IOLs aim to create one continuous focal point. Both multifocal and EDOF IOLs have been reported to provide good visual outcomes at distance, intermediate, and near.3–6 However, there have been reports of unwanted visual disturbances.7–9 Neither multifocals nor EDOF IOLs are inherently better than the other. Multifocal IOLs may provide better near visual outcomes compared to EDOF IOLs, but they may also have more visual disturbances and worse contrast sensitivity.10

The TECNIS Odyssey (Johnson and Johnson Vision) is a novel IOL that combines both multifocal and EDOF technologies.11 In the posterior optic, the multifocal component is a bifocal meant to provide clear vision at distance and near, while the EDOF component aims to provide clear vision at intermediate.12 The anterior optic is designed to neutralize spherical aberration from the eye.12 The Odyssey has an optic diameter of 6 mm and an overall IOL length of 13 mm. To date, there are minimal data reported on the clinical outcomes with the Odyssey IOL. The purpose of this study was to evaluate the visual outcomes following bilateral Odyssey implantation in adult patients who self-identify as Japanese. We specifically chose patients who self-identify as Japanese as we were interested in outcomes in a population that generally has longer axial length than the typical population in the United States.

Methods

This study was a retrospective chart review of patient visual outcomes following bilateral implantation of the Odyssey IOL in adult patients who self-identify as Japanese. Advarra IRB reviewed the study and granted an exemption of oversight and informed consent (approval Pro00090591), as this was a retrospective study. Confidentiality was maintained for all patient data. Good Clinical Practice (GCP), the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki, and International Harmonization (ICH) guidelines were followed. As a retrospective chart review, the study was not registered in a clinical trials database.

Consecutive patient charts were reviewed from July 2023 through August 2024. Eligible charts were from adult patients who self-identify as Japanese, who underwent age-related cataract surgery with Odyssey implantation, and had expected best-corrected visual outcomes of 20/25 or better. Charts were excluded from patients with moderate to severe corneal pathology, irregular astigmatism, preexisting macular disease or retinal degenerative diseases, severe dry eye disease, history of uveitis or ocular herpes simplex virus, nystagmus, amblyopia, strabismus, zonular laxity or dehiscence, pseudoexfoliation, or prior corneal refractive surgery in either eye.

From eligible charts, postoperative data were collected for binocular uncorrected and distance-corrected visual acuities at distance (UDVA, CDVA) and near (UNVA, DCNVA; 40 cm), monocular UDVA, CDVA, and UNVA, and refraction. Spectacle independence and halos were assessed from patient self-reports, not a standardized questionnaire. The software R was used for all statistical analysis (version 4.4.2; The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). This was a descriptive study, therefore no formal sample size calculation was performed. All eligible patient charts were included in the final analysis, and all patients would have followed the same testing protocol. A near card at 40 cm was used to assess UNVA and DCNVA. All visual outcomes were collected in Snellen and converted to logMAR for analysis.

Results

The chart review identified 30 subjects that met the inclusion and exclusion criteria above. The average follow-up time was 44 ± 9 days (range 23 to 69) following the second eye surgery. The average age of subjects was 71.5 ± 8.0 years. There were 19/60 toric (32%) IOLs implanted and 16/30 (53%) female subjects.

Discussion

In this retrospective chart review, we evaluated the clinical outcomes of bilateral Odyssey implantation in patients who self-identify as Japanese. This population was chosen as we were interested in outcomes in a population that generally has longer axial length than the typical population in the United States. To our knowledge, there is only one prior report of clinical outcomes with the Odyssey IOL in Japanese-identifying patients. Bissen-Miyajima et al13 observed that postoperative mean binocular UDVA, UNVA 40 cm, CDVA, and DCNVA 40cm were −0.18 logMAR, 0.00 logMAR, −0.21 logMAR, and 0.00 logMAR, respectively, in a retrospective study of 25 patients. Visual outcomes with the Odyssey IOL in non-Japanese identifying patients have been reported as excellent. In an ambispective study of 40 patients from the US, Hacopian et al14 demonstrated that mean binocular UDVA, UNVA 40 cm, CDVA, and DCNVA 40cm were −0.07 logMAR, 0.03 logMAR, −0.07 logMAR, and 0.02 logMAR, respectively, at 3 months postoperatively. Borges et al,15 in a prospective study of 30 patients in the US, reported that mean binocular UDVA, CDVA, and DCNVA 40cm were 0.02 logMAR, 0.00 logMAR, and 0.01 logMAR, respectively. In a prospective study of 19 eyes, Kang et al16 observed monocular UDVA, UNVA 40 cm, CDVA, and DCNVA 40cm were 0.07 logMAR, 0.06 logMAR, 0.02 logMAR, and 0.05 logMAR, respectively, at ≥ 1 month postoperatively. The results of our study and others suggest that the combination of multifocal and EDOF IOL technologies in the Odyssey can lead to excellent visual performance for patients.

In addition to visual outcomes, patient postoperative experiences are important to understand. We used patient self-reporting of spectacle independence and self-reporting of halos in our study, and observed high spectacle independence and low reports of halos. Using a custom questionnaire, Bissen-Miyajima et al13 found that patient reported spectacle independence was achieved in 64% of patients, while moderate to severe halos were reported in 52% of patients, following bilateral implantation with the Odyssey IOL. Using the assessment of intraocular lens implant symptoms (AIOLIS) questionnaire, Hacopian et al14 observed that 80% (36/40) of subjects reported needing glasses “Not at all” or “A little of the time”, and that the most commonly reported dysphotopsia was halos with 32% (12/40) reported experiencing them “Always”. Also using the AIOLIS questionnaire, Borges et al15 observed that 70% of subjects reported needing glasses “Not at all” or “A little of the time”, and that nighttime glare was the most commonly reported dysphotopsia, with 33% reported experiencing them “Always”. Kang et al16 noted that halos were reported by 37% (7/19) subjects. Studies using different standardized and non-standardized questionnaires make comparisons difficult. For example, spectacle independence and dysphotopsia rates can be influenced by the questionnaire used. Thus, we urge caution in interpreting our results, which showed high spectacle independence and low reports of halos compared to other studies. However, the results of our study and others do still suggest high spectacle independence and at least some halos when using the combination of multifocal and EDOF IOL technologies in the Odyssey IOL.

The primary limitation of this study was the retrospective design. Although this allows for a lot of data to be collected quickly, we were limited to data that was collected during routine office visits. As a consequence, there was no collected defocus curve, intermediate visual acuity, contrast sensitivity, or responses on a validated questionnaire. In addition, there was no standardized follow up schedule. Another limitation of this study was the lack of a comparator or control group, such as the Synergy IOL or another multifocal or EDOF IOL. However, our descriptive study is sufficient to characterize the performance of the Odyssey IOL, especially given the minimal data currently available.

Conclusions

The results of this study suggest good visual outcomes at distance and near, high spectacle independence, and low reports of halos following bilateral implantation with the Odyssey IOL in patients who self-identify as Japanese.

A long-term dataset of dietary patterns and associated environmental effects in China from 1987 to 2023
Authors: Yuanyuan Zhu, Rujing Zhao, Ziwei Wang, Xiaohua Zhu, Zhenfa Tu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The global food system is undergoing a profound transformation, with significant implications for human health and environmental sustainability. Systematic analysis of dietary evolution and its environmental effects is essential for sustainable food system development. Existing research, however, typically emphasized short-term dynamics or isolated environmental factors, neglecting a thorough analysis of the long-term, multidimensional effects of dietary transformation on water, land, and carbon resources. This study constructed a long-term dataset from 1987 to 2023 on the evolution of dietary patterns in China and their associated environmental effects, based on data from the National Bureau of Statistics of China. The dataset established a framework that links diet to water use, land requirement, and carbon emissions. It covers nine food categories: cereals, vegetables, fruits, oilseeds, sugar, eggs, milk, meat, and aquatic products. The dataset comprised long-term time series, multidimensional characteristics, and granularity, providing essential data to support the development of sustainable dietary transition strategies tailored to China’s national conditions and to optimize regional resource allocation.

Background & Summary

As the cornerstone of human survival and development1, food systems are intrinsically linked to resource consumption and global environmental sustainability2,3. In recent years, rapid urbanization and rising income levels have led to significant changes in global dietary patterns, characterized by increased consumption of sugar, calories, and fat4. Additionally, food consumption has shifted from traditional staple crops to resource-intensive animal-based foods, including meat and dairy products5–7. The transformation has significantly increased the resource intensity of global food systems and has also had systemic effects on the planet’s health8,9. The global food system, although essential for nutritional security, has also become a significant contributor to environmental degradation and accelerated resource depletion10–12. Concerningly, dietary consumption exerts escalating pressure on the environment, with projections suggesting that these impacts may soon breach critical planetary boundaries for climate change and land use1,13. Recent research indicated that dietary shifts have outpaced agricultural technological gains as the primary driver of increasing water and land demand14. This transition highlights the pivotal role of dietary consumption in exacerbating global resource depletion. From an environmental standpoint, food systems are the world’s largest water-consuming sector and a significant source of anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions15. Extensive research has confirmed that food production and consumption are primary drivers of carbon emissions, land use, freshwater depletion, and biodiversity loss16–18. Currently, food systems account for 72% of global freshwater consumption19, 48% of land utilization10, and 34% of greenhouse gas emissions8. If existing dietary patterns and population growth trends persist, the environmental pressure exerted by food systems will intensify, posing critical challenges to global sustainable development goals17.

As the world’s largest developing country, China has a food system that is not only vast in scale but also carries substantial domestic and international significance6. China’s food system is currently undergoing an unprecedented transformation that mirrors the global dietary transition. This evolution involves a shift from carbohydrate-heavy, monotonous diets toward more diverse and affluence-driven patterns6,20–22. Specifically, the consumption of traditional staples, such as cereals and vegetables, has declined, while the intake of animal-based products—such as meat, eggs, and milk—continues to escalate14,23,24. The structural transition has triggered complex environmental consequences25, markedly intensifying resource consumption and exacerbating China’s existing resource constraints26–30. Studies have shown that contemporary Chinese dietary patterns were characterized by excessive resource intensity3. China currently accounts for 43% of the global increase in diet-driven agricultural land use, positioning it as a primary driver of global land expansion5. Critically, the evolution of Chinese dietary habits has shifted away from environmental sustainability, with the associated arable land use exceeding critical thresholds22,23. Should these consumption trends persist, China’s domestic arable land resources will be insufficient to support the dietary demands of its 1.4 billion residents without significant technological breakthroughs or increased reliance on international imports31.

Systematically elucidating the evolution of dietary patterns and their associated resource and environmental effects is essential for facilitating a global transition toward sustainable consumption and optimizing national resource allocation. While several studies have examined the evolution of Chinese dietary patterns and their associated environmental effects, significant research shortcomings persist. First, inconsistencies in current research hinder the development of science-based regulations and policies for dietary transformation. Scenario-based projections, for example, suggested a sustained increase in China’s future food demand32. Similarly, sequential analyses have indicated that shifting urban and rural dietary structures are increasing water, land, and carbon footprints26. Conversely, historical data from 2000 to 2011 suggested a decline in the water, carbon, nitrogen, and energy footprints of Chinese household food consumption3. Such discrepancies persisted at the regional level. For instance, research in the “Yarlung Zangbo River and its two tributaries” on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau revealed that despite increasing dietary diversity and animal-based food consumption, local resource and environmental pressures have actually diminished33. Discrepancies between macro trends and regional characteristics, alongside patterns of evolution across different periods, underscore the complex environmental implications of China’s dietary structure. These factors highlight the critical need for systematic research. Second, current analyses focused primarily on isolated environmental dimensions, neglecting systematic investigation of synergies among factors such as water, land, and carbon. This limited scope hinders a comprehensive assessment of the aggregate environmental impacts of changing dietary patterns. Third, most studies examined short-term impacts, which limited insight into how evolving dietary patterns dynamically affect resources and the environment over longer timescales. Furthermore, previous studies have predominantly utilized the China Health and Nutrition Survey (CHNS) to analyze dietary shifts34. However, inherent limitations of the CHNS—including infrequent updates and restricted sample representativeness—preclude an accurate characterization of recent dietary trends. To this end, this study applied the human-land relationship regional system theory to examine the dietary patterns of Chinese residents. Using a 37-year panel dataset (1987–2023), the study examined the evolution of dietary patterns since the introduction of the 1987 national dietary guidelines and assessed their multidimensional effects on water consumption, land use, and carbon emissions. A specialized dataset is constructed. The developed dataset provided a precise and systematic foundation for optimizing dietary patterns, facilitating sustainable dietary transitions that align with both nutritional health and environmental sustainability goals. Ultimately, it can leverage China’s experience to inform dietary transitions in other global regions.

Methods

Data sources

This study focused on China. Based on the core Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) food group classification system35 (https://files-faostat.fao.org/production/FB/food_group_classification_e.pdf) and the China Statistical Yearbook (2024)’s food categories36 (https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/ndsj/2024/indexch.htm), a nine-category system was established through merging, splitting, and modifying existing groups. These categories include cereals (grains, tubers, and legumes), vegetables, fruits, oilseeds (vegetable oils), sugar, eggs, milk, meat (poultry, pork, beef, and mutton), and aquatic products. The data used in this study comprises three categories: demographic metrics, food consumption data, and environmental impact assessment parameters. (1) Demographic metrics: The total population data for China from 1987 to 2023 were obtained from the China Statistical Yearbook (2024)36 (https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/ndsj/2024/indexch.htm). (2) Food consumption data: Per capita food consumption data in China were obtained from the “Per capita consumption of major foodstuffs by residents nationwide” tables in the China Statistical Yearbook (1988–2024)36 (https://data.stats.gov.cn/dg/website/page.html#/pc/national/yearData). Data collection follows the National Bureau of Statistics’ survey plan on household income, expenditure, and living conditions37 (https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/tjzd/202604/t20260402_1962944.html). The survey targets domestic households—including urban, rural, family, and collective units—rather than individual micro-level data. The statistical scope is defined as “household daily consumption,” which encompasses food obtained via market purchases or self-production. Data from dining-out scenarios, including restaurant meals and takeout services, are excluded from the study. (3) Environmental impact assessment parameters (Table 2): The environmental impact assessment focused on three core dimensions: water, land, and carbon. The virtual water content per unit weight for each food category was sourced from Mekonnen and Hoekstra’s China-specific study38 (10.1007/s10021-011-9517-8), representing the long-term equilibrium over the study period. Given that the primary objective of this study is to reveal macro trends in water consumption driven by dietary shifts, rather than to quantify the effects of non-consumptive factors like technological progress. Therefore, the unit virtual water content of each food category is assumed to be constant from 1987 to 2023. The yield per unit area and multiple cropping index for various foods were calculated using historical data published by the National Bureau of Statistics of China (NBSC)36 (https://data.stats.gov.cn/dg/website/page.html#/pc/national/yearData), which included annual crop production, arable land area, and crop planting area. Annual multiple cropping index and the yield per unit area for cereals, vegetables, fruits, oilseeds, and sugar from 1987 to 2023 were calculated, and their respective averages were derived for subsequent analysis. Additionally, the conversion coefficient from crops to corresponding food, and the conversion coefficient of grains to animal-based food were obtained from the literature7,27 (10.1016/j.resconrec.2023.106964, 10.1007/s11442-023-2104-z) and the National Agricultural Products Cost-Benefit Compilation (1988–2024)39. Carbon emission coefficients were sourced from Tilman and Clark17 (10.1038/nature13959), who used life-cycle assessment (LCA) to calculate emissions from “cradle to farm gate.” It should be noted that for aquatic products, this study used an aggregated grain to animal-based food conversion coefficient and a uniform carbon emission coefficient, without distinguishing between aquaculture and capture fisheries. Two main reasons account for this approach. First, at the data level, the aquatic product data used in this study are sourced from the China Statistical Yearbook (1988–2024)36 (https://data.stats.gov.cn/dg/website/page.html#/pc/national/yearData), which reports only aggregated figures without distinguishing between aquaculture and capture fisheries. Second, the grain to aquatic products conversion coefficient was derived from existing literature7 (10.1016/j.resconrec.2023.106964). The coefficient represents averages from multiple sources, encompassing diverse production models such as industrialized and traditional farming. The decision not to distinguish between aquaculture and capture fisheries aligns with standard practice in macro-level longitudinal research, where aggregate coefficients sufficiently characterize broad patterns and long-term trends. Water footprint

Water consumption of food systems is multi-stage and implicit, spanning the entire supply chain from production to consumption. Key stages include irrigation for crop cultivation, clean water for food processing, and water used for environmental control in logistics, storage, and transportation38. Traditional methods for assessing water consumption mainly focus on direct water use and often neglect the indirect water embedded in the food supply chain. This limitation can lead to biased outcomes. This limitation compromises both the accurate representation of food consumption’s water footprint (WF) and the scientific rigor of the analysis.

To comprehensively and systematically quantify the impact of food consumption on water resources, this study adopted a WF accounting framework based on virtual water. Conceptualized by Dutch scholar Hoekstra40, the WF quantifies the total freshwater resources required for a country, region, or individual to consume products and services over a given period. This metric encompasses three distinct dimensions: blue WF, green WF, and grey WF. The study further drew on Mekonnen and Hoekstra’s China-specific research38 to calculate the WF of each food item based on its virtual water content per unit weight. This content is obtained by averaging the corresponding blue WF, green WF, and grey WF, representing the total freshwater consumption across the entire lifecycle from raw material production to the final product stage. Specifically, the virtual water content of plant-based foods includes irrigation water during cultivation and subsequent processing. For animal-based foods, it encompasses water consumption across the entire lifecycle, including feed production, animal husbandry, slaughtering, processing, and cleaning. This method systematically incorporates the hidden water consumption across the entire food lifecycle, thereby overcoming key limitations of traditional approaches and enhancing the scientific rigor, systemic integrity, and practical relevance of the findings. Within this framework, the total WF (WFtotal) is defined as the sum of all individual food consumption WF in a given region and is scaled by the region’s population. Land requirement

This study analyzed the land requirement for food (LRF) in China, adopting the research framework developed by Kastner and Nonhebel41 for their analysis of the Philippines from 1910 to 2003. The methodology integrates key parameters—such as food consumption data, crop conversion rates, yield per unit area, and the multiple cropping index—to establish an accounting system that includes both direct production land and indirect supporting land. Unlike traditional assessments that rely on single-dimensional calculations or focus solely on direct cropland, this comprehensive approach better captures the impact of food consumption on land resources and enables cross-category comparisons of land requirements. Specifically, this study adapted the core methodology of Kastner and Nonhebel41 by tracing consumed food back to its primary agricultural origins. It calculated the arable land required to support consumption by incorporating yield per unit area and the multiple cropping index, thus enabling comparisons of land requirements across food categories. To reflect China’s specific conditions, the study localized the data by using China’s actual average yield and multiple cropping index values from 1987 to 2023. Carbon emissions

Tilman and Clark systematically reviewed 120 authoritative LCA studies covering major food production systems, including crop cultivation, livestock farming, aquaculture, and fisheries17. They compiled 555 data points on greenhouse gas emissions across 82 food categories. Their study defined the system boundary as “cradle to farm gate,” which encompasses carbon emissions from agricultural production (e.g., cultivation, animal husbandry, fertilization, irrigation, and energy use) and the production and transportation of upstream inputs (e.g., fertilizers, pesticides, and feed). This boundary also encompasses carbon emissions from agricultural infrastructure construction but excludes those from land-use change and downstream activities such as processing, transportation, retail, and consumption. This study employed the LCA methodology, based on Tilman and Clark’s research17, to quantify carbon emissions from food consumption using a carbon emission coefficient approach. Data Records

The dataset is available at Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.19433724)42, contains four categories of annual, national-scale data: food consumption evolution, water footprints, land requirement, and carbon emissions. Each dimension is provided in a separate Microsoft Excel (.xlsx) file. The data units have been explicitly annotated within the spreadsheet files. The data have a one-year temporal resolution and are uniformly organized according to a nine-category food classification system (cereals, vegetables, fruits, oilseeds, sugar, eggs, milk, meat, and aquatic products), ensuring cross-dataset comparability and systematic integration.

Technical Validation

Verification of basic dietary consumption data

Analyzing dietary structural evolution is crucial for characterizing consumer food demand and informing national food security policies. Extensive research has investigated shifts in Chinese dietary consumption through heterogeneous data sources and various statistical frameworks. Based on data from the NBSC, this dataset systematically measured the evolving trends in the dietary patterns of Chinese residents from 1987 to 2023 (Fig. 1a). The reliability and scientific rigor of the current dataset were verified through systematic cross-validation against established authoritative literature. Some studies, based on data from NBSC, analyzed the evolution of dietary patterns in China from 1987 to 202023,27, revealing a gradual shift from a diet dominated by cereals and vegetables toward greater diversity. Specifically, per capita consumption of cereals, vegetables, and sugar decreased from 198.4 kg, 136.3 kg, and 2.1 kg to 126.3 kg, 99.5 kg, and 1.3 kg, respectively, by 2020. Conversely, consumption of fruits, oilseeds, eggs, milk, and meat increased from 19.4 kg, 4.5 kg, 3.9 kg, 1.9 kg, and 22.5 kg to 50.0 kg, 9.9 kg, 12.7 kg, 12.1 kg, and 61.1 kg, respectively. The average relative deviation between our dataset and the comparative study is 15.05%, primarily arising from methodological differences and remaining within an acceptable margin for integrated data. It indicates strong consistency in core trends and quantitative outcomes between the two studies. Another study using data from NBSC showed that from 1987 to 202021, per capita consumption of cereals, vegetables, and sugar decreased from 228.3 kg, 133.2 kg, and 1.9 kg to 114.6 kg, 101.1 kg, and 1.3 kg, respectively, while fruit and oilseed consumption increased from 11.6 kg and 3.9 kg to 52.8 kg and 9.8 kg, respectively. Consumption of animal-based foods increased from 23.8 kg to 75.3 kg. The average relative deviation between our dataset and this study is 5.39%, indicating strong consistency in the data. Upon verification, this deviation primarily arises from differences in decimal place retention, further validating the dataset’s reliability. It should be noted that while the aforementioned studies all draw from the NBSC, their results exhibit slight discrepancies. First, the NBSC frequently performs retrospective revisions of historical data following national population or agricultural censuses; our study utilizes the latest official releases. Second, discrepancies may arise from variations in study periods, statistical definitions, and data processing methodologies. Another study, using CHNS data, analyzed dietary differences between urban and rural residents from 1997 to 201143. It reported that urban residents’ daily refined cereal intake decreased by an average of 5.4 g per year, while fruit and milk consumption increased by 3 g and 1.2 g, respectively. Rural residents’ daily refined cereal intake decreased by an average of 7 g per year, whereas meat consumption increased by 3.2 g per year. The trends in our dataset for the same period are consistent with these findings, showing a general decline in cereal consumption alongside sustained growth in fruit, milk, and meat intake, which validates the dataset’s scientific rigor. Furthermore, a study using the China Household Survey Yearbook (2016–2022) compiled food consumption data for all 31 Chinese provinces (excluding Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan) from 2015 to 202144. It highlighted a growing diversification of dietary patterns, characterized by an overall decline in the proportion of cereal consumption. Our dataset also reflected this core pattern, showing a decrease in the national per capita share of cereal consumption from 38.87% to 36.04% between 2015 and 2021. This close alignment with the previous study supports the dataset’s capacity to accurately represent changes in dietary structure. A specialized study on spatiotemporal changes in animal-based food consumption in China from 2000 to 2020 reported that per capita consumption of beef, pork, mutton, poultry, eggs, and dairy products increased by 197%, 55%, 86%, 206%, 101%, and 184%, respectively, reaching 2.32 kg, 18.28 kg, 1.24 kg, 12.96 kg, 12.68 kg, and 12.97 kg in 202045. The average relative deviation between our corresponding data and that study is 11.4%, primarily due to differences in data sources: the specialized study integrated provincial-level data, while ours uses nationally aggregated data. Despite this discrepancy, both studies consistently reflect the core trend of rapid growth in animal-based food consumption, validating the reliability of our dataset in tracking the evolution of key food categories.

In summary, this dataset was cross-validated against multiple authoritative studies using different data sources, methodologies, and scales. The average relative deviation of core quantitative indicators for dietary change ranges from 5.39% to 15.05%, which is reasonable given the nature of integrated data. Furthermore, trends in dietary pattern evolution are highly consistent with existing research. These results demonstrated the dataset’s reliability, scientific validity, and comparability, offering robust support for food demand analysis and the development of sustainable dietary transition strategies.

Multi-source comparison and cross-validation of resource and environmental impact data

This study utilized three primary indicators—virtual water, virtual land, and carbon emission coefficient—to longitudinally assess the resource and environmental effects of Chinese dietary patterns from 1987 to 2023 (Fig. 1b–g). To ensure scientific rigor and inter-study comparability, we employed a systematic methodological validation and verification approach. The validation process focused on two dimensions: the theoretical rationale for method selection and comparative analysis with existing studies.

At the methodological level, virtual water, virtual land, and carbon emission coefficient are established assessment tools widely used in international research. (1) As a core method for quantifying the WF of food consumption, virtual water reveals hidden water consumption in food supply chains, which provides essential insights for sustainable water management and constitutes a well-established paradigm in global research on the environmental effects of dietary systems46. For instance, the methodological foundation of this study aligns with the assessment of the WF of agricultural product consumption in Denmark from 2009 to 201347. Multiple studies confirmed that meat consumption drives WF growth46, while reducing animal product intake helps alleviate water scarcity48. These findings align with our study’s parameter settings, in which the virtual water coefficient for meat is substantially higher than for plant-based foods, providing empirical support for our methodological choices. Furthermore, the virtual water coefficients for different food categories in this dataset align with validated parameter systems from prior authoritative studies27, ensuring the comparability and accuracy of the derived results. (2) Virtual land is a well-established tool for quantifying the land requirements of diets and is widely used in regional assessments of the environmental effects of diets. For example, existing studies have applied this method to assess the land demand associated with food consumption in China’s four major municipalities (Beijing, Shanghai, Tianjin, and Chongqing)49 and to project future land use trends in the United Kingdom50, demonstrating its applicability across regions and scales. Moreover, the conversion coefficients from food to grain used in this dataset align with those in previous studies on food land demand7,23,27,28. This well-established parameter system, validated through long-term academic scrutiny, supports the reliability of our land demand calculations. (3) Carbon emission coefficients are widely used to quantify the carbon footprint of food consumption. Standardized methods, such as systematic reviews, are well used for deriving carbon emission coefficients. While coefficients for some foods in our dataset deviate from the Global Warming Potential (GWP) values in global studies51, these differences are attributable to fundamental methodological variations: the scope of literature synthesis, conceptual distinctions between GWP and carbon emission coefficients, and differences in food classification systems. Similarly, comparisons with studies on U.S. household food reveal substantial discrepancies in per-unit carbon emission intensity (average relative deviation: 71.21%)52, primarily due to differences in production patterns, dietary structures, and data collection methods. Despite these variations, studies have consistently shown that carbon emission coefficients for animal-based foods are significantly higher than for plant-based foods51–53. The universal trend aligns with the coefficients used in our dataset, demonstrating methodological consistency. Moreover, multiple authoritative studies in the Chinese context21,28,29 have employed the same coefficient system, confirming its accuracy and applicability for region-specific research.

At the comparative analysis level, we systematically compared and analyzed multiple published studies with overlapping time frames. The verification process is detailed below. (1) The evolution trends and structural characteristics of the WF of food consumption align closely with existing research. A study on China’s water demand for food from 1981 to 2016 reported a 64.72% increase in total agricultural water consumption by 2016 compared to 1981, primarily driven by growing demand for animal-based foods54. This conclusion aligns with our dataset, which shows that the WF of food consumption in China increased by 83.37% between 1987 and 2023, with the share attributed to animal-based foods rising from 23.48% to 49.17%. Another study covering the period from 1987 to 2020 revealed an increase in China’s per capita food consumption WF from 449.4 m3/year to 684.8 m3/year27. The overall rise, together with concurrent decreases in the WF of cereals, vegetables, and sugar alongside increases for fruits, oilseeds, and animal-based foods, is consistent with the trends identified in the present dataset. Furthermore, two independent studies covering 1990–201955 and 1990–202026 both confirmed a sustained upward trend in China’s food consumption WF. These studies consistently identified a shift in dietary WF, characterized by a decline in plant-based foods and a rise in animal-based products, thereby corroborating the reliability of the present dataset. A study spanning 1997–2011 showed that the annual per capita water consumption associated with food intake in China increased by 18.4 L for urban residents and 24.2 L for rural residents43. This upward trend was largely precipitated by burgeoning meat consumption, corroborating the developed dataset’s central premise that meat intake significantly elevates the dietary WF. The observed increase in China’s per capita food consumption WF from 357.68 m3 to 486.38 m3 between 1997 and 201656 further supports the trend-level consistency of this dataset. Quantitative comparisons revealed controllable and explainable discrepancies between this dataset and prior studies. Compared to a study covering 1987–202027, the average relative deviation for corresponding years was 10.7%, with a deviation of only 1.07% for 1987, indicating high consistency in baseline data between the two. This discrepancy arises primarily because the present dataset includes aquatic products, whereas the reference study excluded them. Relative to another study spanning 1997–201656, the average deviation is 15.67%. This difference is largely attributable to that study’s exclusion of oilseeds, sugar, and aquatic products. In summary, the observed deviations originate mainly from differences in statistical scope, not from systematic errors in the data. The consistent temporal trends across all studies provide strong evidence for the scientific rigor and reliability of this dataset in WF accounting. (2) The overall trend and structural characteristics of LRF align closely with the key findings of prior research. A study has shown that from 1981 to 2016, the total LRF among Chinese residents exhibited an upward trend54. Within this total, land demand for meat and vegetable oils rose substantially, whereas demand for cereals declined. This overall trend aligns closely with the pattern observed in the present dataset. Furthermore, studies spanning 1987–2020 confirmed the upward trend in China’s per capita LRF23,27. One study reported an increase in China’s per capita LRF from 0.079 ha per year to 0.101 ha per year27. The corresponding data in the present dataset show an average relative deviation of 9.6% from that value, which falls within the reasonable range of variation for integrated cross-study data. That study also noted structural shifts, characterized by declining land requirements for cereals and sugar alongside rising demands for animal-based foods27, which align closely with the trends identified in the present dataset. Another study spanning 1987–2020 found that land requirement for meat consumption exhibited the most substantial growth, increasing from 10.2 million ha in 1987 to 39.2 million ha in 202023. This growth trend is consistent with the pattern in the present dataset. The average relative deviation between the corresponding data is 19.7%, primarily resulting from data updates in the present study. These adjustments involved recalculating LRF according to the latest statistical criteria for the multiple cropping index and yield per unit area of various foods, constituting a justifiable methodological difference. Furthermore, a study spanning 1997–2011 also reported an increase in annual per capita LRF43—a trend consistent with the present dataset. In summary, while this dataset shows some quantitative discrepancies compared to prior studies, these differences arise from variations in statistical definitions, classification scopes, or calculation methods, and therefore represent explainable, objective deviations. In key dimensions—including the overall temporal trend of LRF and structural shifts across different food categories—this dataset shows strong consistency with multiple independent studies. This agreement robustly validates its reliability for quantifying LRF. (3) The verification of carbon emissions related to food consumption shows that this dataset is consistent with prior research regarding trends, contribution structure, and categorical evolution. Regarding temporal trends, greenhouse gas emissions from food consumption in China rose by 64% between 1987 and 201757, a pattern consistent with the 86.38% growth observed in the present dataset. Both growth rates confirm a sustained increase in carbon emissions over this period. In terms of contribution structure, existing research has indicated that meat and cereals were the primary contributors to the dietary carbon emissions of Chinese residents from 1990 to 201558, consistent with the patterns revealed by this dataset. A study of the carbon footprint among China’s urban and rural residents from 1990 to 2019 identifies cereals, vegetables, pork, beef, and mutton as the primary contributing categories55. The total carbon footprint increased from 212 Mt in 1990 to 343 Mt in 2019. The average relative deviation from the corresponding period in the present dataset is 26.74%, which falls within a reasonable range. Another study, using a household metabolism approach, showed that the carbon footprint of household food consumption in China followed an upward trend between 1990 and 202259. Per capita carbon emissions from plant-based foods decreased from 345.79 kg to 262.70 kg, whereas carbon emissions from animal-based foods increased from 90.78 kg to 284.39 kg. Despite an average relative deviation of 39.46% between the present dataset and the corresponding years of that study, the discrepancy can be clearly explained. First, the reference study included carbon emissions from alcoholic beverages, a category excluded here. Second, methodological differences exist in the carbon emission coefficients applied, and the reference study employed provincial-level calculations, contrasting with the national-level scope of our study. Notwithstanding variations in methodology and scope, both studies consistently capture the overall upward trend in carbon emissions and the fundamental pattern of opposing carbon emission trajectories between animal-based and plant-based foods. Additionally, a prior study reported that China’s total greenhouse gas emissions from food consumption rose from 273.66 ± 26.43 Mt to 443.50 ± 46.96 Mt between 1997 and 201656. The carbon emissions data for these years in the present dataset fall entirely within this reported range, thereby corroborating the dataset’s accuracy. At the level of specific food categories, the increase in China’s meat-related carbon emissions from 2010 to 202160 aligns with the trend captured in the present dataset for that period. In conclusion, the current dataset demonstrates high concordance with multiple independent studies across key dimensions. Observed quantitative discrepancies primarily arise from variations in system boundaries, spatial resolution, or emission factor selection; such deviations are typical within the field and do not undermine the dataset’s overall scientific integrity.

Sensitivity analysis of key parameters

To evaluate potential biases arising from fixed parameters, a one-factor sensitivity analysis was performed on four core variables: virtual water content per unit weight of pork, conversion coefficient of grains to pork, carbon emission coefficient of meat, and yield per unit area of cereals. These parameters represent the water, land, and carbon dimensions of the study. Parameter baselines derive from our original calculations, while variation ranges are defined by official statistics or empirical values from existing literature (Table 3), as detailed below: (1) Virtual water content per unit weight of pork. A systematic literature review of China’s pork WF identified unit virtual water contents ranging from 4.46 m³/kg61 to 7.37 m³/kg62. Consequently, the parameter variation range was defined within these bounds. (2) Conversion coefficient of grains to pork. Utilizing pig production and concentrate feed data from the National Agricultural Products Cost-Benefit Compilation (1991–2023)39 (data for 1987–1990 are missing), we calculated annual coefficients by dividing total concentrate feed by net pork output (total output minus piglet weight). This yielded a fluctuation range of 2.93~3.59. (3) Carbon emission coefficient of meat. The overall coefficient was derived using a weighted average of values for pork, beef, mutton, and poultry from published literature. Weighting was based on average consumption ratios (1987–2023): pork (66.97%), beef and mutton (9.49%), and poultry (23.54%). The resulting range is 3.97~6.99 CO2eq/kg63. Limitations and prospects

This dataset exhibits strong consistency through its official statistical support, methodological coherence, and alignment with existing research. It facilitates the analysis of historical trends, driving mechanisms, and future scenarios for China’s food system, providing a scientifically robust foundation for policy evaluation and related academic research.

Notably, this dataset provides a continuous, longitudinal, and nationwide record of dietary pattern evolution and associated environmental effects in China. However, it has several limitations. First, the reliance on household consumption surveys likely underestimates total intake by excluding food consumed away from home. Second, the analysis does not differentiate between capture fisheries and aquaculture in the calculation of aquatic product-related environmental effects. Finally, environmental effects calculations use fixed coefficients, which do not fully capture the dynamic changes in agricultural production methods and efficiency over time. Future updates could consider introducing a coefficient system that incorporates regional differentiation and temporal dynamics further to enhance the spatial accuracy and timeliness of the data.

The section conjecture for the toric fundamental group over p p -adic fields Thanks: The author was partially supported by the FIS grant “RAPTbyFUN” FIS-2024-04657, and by the PRIN project “Derived and underived algebraic stacks and applications”.
Giulio Bresciani

Abstract
. Loosely speaking, the toric fundamental group is the Tannaka dual of a category of vector bundles which become direct sums of line bundles on a finite étale cover. In characteristic 0 0 , it is an extension of the étale fundamental group scheme by a projective limit of tori. We prove the analogue of Grothendieck’s section conjecture for the toric fundamental group over p p -adic fields. As a consequence, we give a new interpretation of Selmer sections as toric Galois sections.



1.
Introduction

Curves are smooth, projective and geometrically connected. A curve is hyperbolic if it has genus
\geq 2
.

1.1.
The section conjecture

Given a geometrically connected scheme
X
over a field
k
, there is a short exact sequence of étale fundamental groups

1\to\pi_{1}(X_{k^{s}})\to\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)\to 1

where
k^{s}
is a separable closure of
k
, and
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)=\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Spec}k)
.

The space of Galois sections
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
is the set of sections of this exact sequence modulo the action by conjugation of
\pi_{1}(X_{k^{s}})
. The functoriality of
\pi_{1}
induces a map

X(k)\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k},

sometimes called
profinite Kummer map
. Galois sections in the image of this map are called
geometric
.

In 1983, in a famous letter to Faltings
[
Gro97
]
, Grothendieck made the following conjecture, known as the
section conjecture
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Grothendieck)
.

If
k
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Q}
and
X
is a curve of genus
\geq 2
over
k
, then
X(k)\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
is bijective.

Injectivity was already known to Grothendieck and follows from the Mordell–Weil theorem, surjectivity is still wide open.

1.2.
The toric section conjecture

It is “folklore knowledge” that an étale fundamental group scheme (or Nori-étale fundamental group) can be defined as the Tannaka dual of the category of vector bundles trivialized by a finite étale cover (see
[
Nor76
]
for the analogous construction of Nori’s fundamental group), and that the étale fundamental group scheme is essentially a rephrasing of Grothendieck’s étale fundamental group in a different language.

Let
X
be a smooth, projective, geometrically connected variety over
k
, with an ample line bundle
H
. We define the toric fundamental group scheme as the Tannaka dual of the category of vector bundles
V
with a splitting
V=\bigoplus_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}V_{q}
such that, for some connected finite étale cover
f:Y\to X
, the pullback
f^{*}V_{q}
is a direct sum of line bundles of
H
-degree equal to
\deg(f)\cdot q
; the splitting is part of the datum, see §
12
. An alternative construction can be given in greater generality, and it shows that the toric fundamental group does not depend on the choice of
H
, see §
3.4
.

Analogously to the étale case, there is a space
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}
of toric Galois sections and a natural map
X(k)\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}
, which we call the
toric Kummer map
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
k
be a finitely generated extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
X
be a hyperbolic curve over
k
. The toric Kummer map

X(k)\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}

is bijective.

We prove that, for curves and abelian varieties in characteristic
0
,
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
, see Theorem
5.10
. As a consequence, Grothendieck’s conjecture implies Conjecture
1.2
.

1.3.
Main results

We prove that the toric version of the conjecture holds over
p
-adic fields both for abelian varieties and hyperbolic curves.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety
A
over a field
k
finite over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then

A(k)\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{A/k}

is bijective.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth, projective hyperbolic curve over a field
k
finite over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then

X(k)\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}

is bijective.

The index of a curve is the greatest common divisor of the degrees of its closed points. Recall that J. Stix has proved that, if
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}\neq\emptyset
, then the index is a power of
p

[
Sti10
]
.

We prove Theorem
1.4
by reducing it to a statement about the original version of the conjecture, and then proving this statement. Here it is.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth, projective hyperbolic curve over a field
k
finite over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. If
s\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
is a Galois section not associated with a
k
-point of
X
, there exists a finite étale cover
Y\to X
with a lift
r\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{Y/k}
of
s
such that
p
divides the index of
Y
.

If
X(k)=\emptyset
, we can choose
Y\to X
as a geometrically Galois cover whose degree is a power of
p
.

More generally, in Theorem
1.5
we can assume that
Y\to X
is geometrically Galois of degree a power of
p
if the pro-
p
section induced by
s
is not geometric, see §
10
for details. We recall that Y. Hoshi has proved the existence of non-geometric pro-
p
sections
[
Hos10
]
.

It’s not known whether Grothendieck’s conjecture holds over
p
-adic fields. By a well-known result of A. Tamagawa
[
Tam97
, Proposition 2.8]
, it is enough to study the case of curves without rational points. Theorem
1.5
takes Tamagawa’s result one step further, telling us that over
p
-adic fields we can actually focus on curves of index different from
1
.

While Grothendieck’s conjecture is not known over
p
-adic fields, we mention that F. Pop and J. Stix proved a valuation-theoretic analogue
[
PS17
]
.

Recall that a Galois section
s\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
of a curve
X
over a number field
k
is Selmer if its base change to
k_{\nu}
is geometric for every finite place
\nu
. Write
\operatorname{Sel}_{X/k}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
for the subset of Selmer Galois sections. As a consequence of Theorem
1.4
, we prove that toric and Selmer sections coincide.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth, projective curve of genus
\geq 2
over a number field
k
. Then

\operatorname{Sel}_{X/k}=\mathcal{S}_{X/k}^{\mathrm{tor}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}.

We remark that the toric fundamental group does
not
behave well under base change of the base field, if the extension is not algebraic. Since
k_{\nu}/k
is not algebraic, the proof of Theorem
1.6
is less straightforward than it might seem at first glance.

We recall that A. Betts and J. Stix have recently proved that, if
k
contains no CM subfields and
\nu
is a finite place of
k
, the natural map
\operatorname{Sel}_{X/k}\to X(k_{\nu})
has finite image
[
BS25
]
.

1.4.
An unexpected byproduct

While studying the toric section conjecture, we stumbled upon a purely geometric result which, up to our knowledge, was not previously known. We record it here, just to avoid it being buried in the main text.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth, projective curve of genus
\geq 2
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
. For every pair of distinct points
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
and for every integer
n\geq 2
, there exists a finite étale cover
Y\to X
with the following property.

For every pair of points
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
over
x_{1},x_{2}
and every
m
with
\gcd(n,m)=1
, we have
m([y_{1}]-[y_{2}])\neq 0
in
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
.

Quite surprisingly, even if Theorem
1.7
is basically a statement over
\mathbb{C}
, our proof uses anabelian methods over
p
-adic fields. It is natural to ask whether Theorem
1.7
is true in positive characteristic as well, and if one can prove more strongly that
[y_{1}]-[y_{2}]
is not torsion (i.e. the case
n=1
).

1.7.
Fundamental gerbes

N. Borne and A. Vistoli
[
BV15
]

[
BV19
]
developed an alternative approach to fundamental groups in algebraic geometry, i.e.
fundamental gerbes
. The languages of fundamental groups and fundamental gerbes are equivalent, and translations can be made back and forth. Fundamental gerbes have the disadvantage of being more technical since they are stacks, but have the advantage of not requiring the choice of a base point.

Fundamental gerbes also have another advantage which is very important for us: Borne and Vistoli constructed a general framework which automatically produces a wealth of variants of the original étale fundamental gerbe, the toric fundamental gerbe being one of them. Conversely, toric fundamental groups are currently not present in the literature.

We use Borne and Vistoli’s language in order to have a reference to rely on. See §
3.1
for a short and informal introduction to the language of fundamental gerbes.

In §
12
, we give a Tannakian interpretation of toric fundamental groups and gerbes.

1.8.
On cohomology

We frequently use cohomology of group schemes which are not of finite type. In order to do this, we work in a fixed Grothendieck universe and use the fpqc topology. If
G
is a (possibly non-abelian) group scheme over
k
,
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,G)
is the set of fpqc torsors. If
G
is abelian,
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,G)
has a natural structure of abelian group, and
\operatorname{H}^{2}(k,G)
is the group of fpqc gerbes banded by
G
, see §
2.1

1.9.
Notation and conventions

Curves are smooth, projective and geometrically connected. A variety over a field
k
is a separated, geometrically integral scheme of finite type over
k
. An algebraic group over
k
is a group scheme of finite type over
k
.

For a proper, geometrically connected and geometrically reduced scheme
X
over a field
k
, we write
\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}
for the Picard scheme and
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
for the group of line bundles on
X
. There is a natural inclusion
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\subseteq\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
, which is an equality if
X(k)\neq\emptyset
.

Given an abelian variety
A
and a positive integer
n
,
A[\infty]
is the subgroup of all torsion points,
A[n]
is the kernel of multiplication by
n
,
A[n^{\infty}]
are the torsion points whose order divides a power of
n
, and
A[n^{\prime}]
are the torsion points whose order is prime with
n
.

Given a variety
X
over
k
, the degree of a closed point
x\in X
is the degree of the residue field
k(x)
over
k
. If
k
is a local field, the ramification of
x
is the ramification index of
k(x)
over
k
.

2.
Gerbes

Recall that, given a site
\mathcal{C}
, a
gerbe

\mathcal{G}
on
\mathcal{C}
is a stack in groupoids such that

•

for every
S\in\mathcal{C}
, any two objects of
\mathcal{G}(S)
are locally isomorphic, and

•

for every
S\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists a covering
\{S_{i}\to S\}
such that
\mathcal{G}(S_{i})
is non-empty for every
i
.

An algebraic gerbe over a field
k
is an algebraic stack over
k
which is a gerbe for the fppf topology. We need to use gerbes which are projective limits of algebraic gerbes, and these are in general not gerbes for the fppf topology, but only for the fpqc one. Because of this, when we work with a gerbe over a field we always mean that it is a gerbe for the fpqc topology. Furthermore, we work with
affine
gerbes over a field, i.e. fpqc gerbes with affine diagonal, possessing an affine chart.

We are going to use the concept of
locally full
morphism of gerbes, and the
canonical factorization
of a morphism of gerbes, which were introduced in
[
BV19
]
. These are simply the gerbe-theoretic analogues of a surjective homomorphism of groups, and of the factorization of a homomorphism into a surjective homomorphism composed with an injective one.

Definition 2.1

(
[
BV19
, Definition 3.4]
)
.

Let
f:\Upsilon\to\Phi
be a morphism of affine gerbes over a field
k
. We say that
f
is
locally full
if, for any extension
k^{\prime}
of
k
and any object
\xi\in\Upsilon(k^{\prime})
, the induced homomorphism of group schemes
\operatorname{Aut}_{\Upsilon}(\xi)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\Phi}(f(\xi))
is faithfully flat.

Equivalently,
f
is locally full if
\Upsilon
is a relative gerbe over
\Phi

[
BV19
, Proposition 3.10]
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
BV19
, Proposition 3.9]
)
.

Let
f:\Upsilon\to\Phi
be a morphism of affine gerbes over a field
k
. There exists a factorization
\Upsilon\to\Phi^{\prime}\to\Phi
with
\Upsilon\to\Phi^{\prime}
locally full and
\Phi^{\prime}\to\Phi
faithful.

Furthermore, the factorization is canonical: if
\Upsilon\to\Phi^{\prime\prime}\to\Phi
is another such factorization, there exists an equivalence
\Phi^{\prime}\to\Phi^{\prime\prime}
and a
2
-commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upsilon}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{\prime\prime}}

∎

2.1.
Abelian gerbes

A gerbe
\mathcal{G}
on a site
\mathcal{C}
is
abelian
if, for every
S\in\mathcal{C}
and every
s\in\mathcal{G}(S)
, the automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(s)
is abelian.

If
\mathcal{G}
is an abelian gerbe over a small site, there exists a sheaf of groups
G
called the
band
of
\mathcal{G}
such that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(s)\simeq G(S)
, plus some obvious compatibility properties
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0CJY
]
.

A morphism
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
of abelian gerbes induces a homomorphism of bands
G\to H
.

The following is well-known, but the only reference we know works in the much more complex non-abelian case
[
Gir71
]
. We give an easy proof in the abelian case.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an abelian gerbe with band
G
on a small site
\mathcal{C}
,
H
a sheaf of abelian groups and
f:G\to H
a homomorphism of sheaves of groups.

There exists a gerbe
\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H
banded by
H
, called the
induced gerbe
, with a morphism
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H
whose homomorphism of bands is
f
.

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is another such morphism, there exists an equivalence
\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H\to\mathcal{H}
making the obvious diagram
2
-commutative and whose associated morphism of bands is the identity of
H
.

Proof.

Define a category fibered in groupoids
\mathcal{H}_{0}
whose objects coincide with the objects of
\mathcal{G}
, and arrows defined as follows. If
s_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(S_{1})=\mathcal{G}(S_{1})
,
s_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(S_{2})=\mathcal{G}(S_{2})
and
S_{1}\to S_{2}
is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}
, then

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}(s_{1},s_{2})\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(s_{1},s_{2})\times^{G}H=\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(s_{1},s_{2})\times H(S_{1})\right)/G(S_{1})

where, for the fixed arrow
S_{1}\to S_{2}
, the contracted action is

(u,h)\cdot g=(u\circ g,f(g)^{-1}h),\qquad g\in G(S_{1}).

Sending an arrow
u
to the class of
(u,1)
defines a morphism
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is a small site, we may define
\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H
as the stackification of
\mathcal{H}_{0}

[
Sta25
,
Tag 02ZN
]
; we get a morphism
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}_{0}\to\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H
by composition.

Given an object
S\in\mathcal{C}
, any element of
\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H(S)
comes locally from
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and hence from
\mathcal{G}
: since
\mathcal{G}
is a gerbe, this implies that any two elements of
\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H(S)
are locally isomorphic, proving that
\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H
is a gerbe. Let us show that it is abelian banded by
H
.

For every element
s\in\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H(S)
, there is an induced homomorphism of sheaves of groups
H\to\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H}(s)
; we want to show that this is an isomorphism. Every element of
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H}(s)
comes locally from an automorphism defined in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
; this implies that
H\to\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H}(s)
is surjective. Let us prove injectivity.

Up to passing to a cover, we may assume that
s
lifts to
\mathcal{G}(S)
, and up to replacing
\mathcal{C}
with the comma category
\mathcal{C}/S
we may also assume that
\mathcal{G}
is the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}G
. Under this assumption, we have a natural morphism
\mathcal{H}_{0}\to\mathcal{B}H
where
\mathcal{B}H
is the classifying stack of
H
, and hence an induced morphism
\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H\to\mathcal{B}H
. This gives a retraction
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H}(s)\to H
of
H\to\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H}(s)
, proving injectivity.

If
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is another morphism of gerbes whose morphism of bands is
f
, then there is a natural morphism
\mathcal{H}_{0}\to\mathcal{H}
inducing a morphism
\mathcal{G}\times^{G}H\to\mathcal{H}
whose morphism of bands is the identity of
H
. A morphism of abelian gerbes is an equivalence if and only if the corresponding morphism of bands is an isomorphism, hence we conclude.
∎

Given a sheaf of abelian groups
G
on a small site
\mathcal{C}
and two gerbes
\mathcal{G}
,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
banded by
G

\mathcal{G}\oplus\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
as the induced gerbe
(\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\times^{G\times G}G

induced by the homomorphism

This gives a group structure to the set of equivalence classes of gerbes banded by

2.2.
Projective limits of gerbes

Consider a pro-algebraic fpqc gerbe
\Pi
over
k
, i.e. a limit in the sense of
[
BV15
, §3]
of affine, algebraic gerbes. By definition,
\Pi=\varprojlim\Gamma
is the limit along a cofiltered poset
I
of a pseudofunctor
\Gamma:I\to(\operatorname{AffGer}/k)
cf.
[
BV15
, Remark 3.4, Definition 3.5]
. This means that, for every
j\geq i\geq h
, there exists a morphism
\gamma_{i,j}:\Gamma_{j}\to\Gamma_{i}
and a
2
isomorphism
\lambda_{h,i,j}:\gamma_{h,i}\circ\gamma_{i,j}\Rightarrow\gamma_{h,j}
. Furthermore, these satisfy a cocycle condition.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a pro-algebraic gerbe
\Pi=\varprojlim\Gamma
, we define the set
\Pi(k)_{\mathrm{q}}
of
quasi-sections
as the projective limit of the sets
\Gamma_{i}(k)/\mathord{\sim}
of isomorphism classes of sections; there is an obvious map
\Pi(k)/\mathord{\sim}\to\Pi(k)_{\mathrm{q}}
.

It is straightforward to check that this definition does not depend on the choice of the presentation.

The following is the stacky analogue of
[
Sti13
, Lemma 44]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
\Pi=\varprojlim\Gamma
is profinite, i.e.
\Gamma_{i}
is a finite affine gerbe in the sense of
[
BV15
, §4]
for every
i
. Then
\Pi(k)/\mathord{\sim}\to\Pi(k)_{\mathrm{q}}
is bijective.

Proof.

If
s\in\Pi(k)
, then
\Pi(k)/\mathord{\sim}\simeq\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\operatorname{Aut}_{\Pi}(s))
and the statement is proved in
[
Bre21
, Proposition 2.3]
, hence injectivity is automatic. We have to prove surjectivity.

Let
(s_{i})_{i}\in\Pi(k)_{\mathrm{q}}
be a quasi-section; by definition, for every
j\geq i
there exists an isomorphism
\sigma_{i,j}:\gamma_{i,j}(s_{j})\to s_{i}
. If these isomorphisms satisfy the cocycle condition

\sigma_{h,j}=\sigma_{h,i}\circ\gamma_{h,i}(\sigma_{i,j})\circ\lambda_{h,i,j}(s_{j})^{-1}:

:\gamma_{h,j}(s_{j})\to\gamma_{h,i}\circ\gamma_{i,j}(s_{j})\to\gamma_{h,i}(s_{i})\to s_{h}

then we get the desired section
s\in\Pi(k)
by definition of projective limit; the cocycle condition is in general not satisfied, so we have to modify the isomorphisms
\sigma_{i,j}
.

Denote by
\mathrm{F}\mathrm{P}(I)
the poset of finite sub-posets of
I
ordered by inclusion, it is cofiltered as well. For every
S\in\mathrm{F}\mathrm{P}(I)
, denote by
\Lambda(S)
the set of coherent choices of isomorphisms
\sigma_{i,j}^{\prime}:\gamma_{i,j}(s_{j})\to s_{i}
for
j\geq i
in
S
, i.e. an element of
\Lambda(S)
is a choice of
\sigma_{i,j}^{\prime}
for every
j\geq i\in S
such that the
\sigma_{i,j}^{\prime}
s satisfy the cocycle condition written above restricted to the elements of
S
.

If
S\subset S^{\prime}
, we have an obvious forgetful map
\Lambda(S^{\prime})\to\Lambda(S)
. Since
S
is finite and, by hypothesis, there is a finite number of isomorphisms
\gamma_{i,j}(s_{j})\to s_{i}
, we get that
\Lambda(S)
is finite. I claim that
\Lambda(S)
is non-empty for every
S
.

In fact, since
I
is cofiltered up to enlarging
S
we can assume that it has a maximum
m
. For every
j\geq i
in
S
, define

\sigma^{\prime}_{i,j}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\sigma_{i,m}\circ\lambda_{i,j,m}(s_{m})\circ\gamma_{i,j}(\sigma_{j,m}^{-1}):

:\gamma_{i,j}(s_{j})\to\gamma_{i,j}\circ\gamma_{j,m}(s_{m})\to\gamma_{i,m}(s_{m})\to s_{i}.

It is straightforward to check that these satisfy the cocycle condition in
S
and thus define an element of
\Lambda(S)

The projective limit of

is the set of coherent choices of isomorphisms

for every

∎

In this section, we review and integrate some material from

[

3.1.
An informal introduction to fundamental gerbes

We start by giving a short and informal introduction to fundamental gerbes. We refer to
[
BV15
,
BV19
]
for precise statements and proofs.

Let
\operatorname{HoTop}
be the homotopy category of connected CW complexes. Given
X\in\operatorname{HoTop}
and a base point
x
, its fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X,x)
can be defined using based loops up to homotopy, or as the group of deck transformations of the universal cover.

One can also characterize the classifying space
\mathcal{B}\pi_{1}(X,x)
directly, without first choosing a base point. Let
\operatorname{EM}_{1}\subseteq\operatorname{HoTop}
be the full subcategory of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces in degree
1
, namely connected CW complexes whose higher homotopy groups vanish. There is a left adjoint
\Pi:\operatorname{HoTop}\to\operatorname{EM}_{1}
, characterized by the natural bijections

\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi_{X},Y),\qquad Y\in\operatorname{EM}_{1},

in the homotopy category.

The space
\Pi_{X}
can be constructed as
\mathcal{B}\pi_{1}(X,x)
, or by attaching cells to
X
to kill its higher homotopy groups
[
Hat02
, Example 4.17]
. Its universal property determines it up to unique isomorphism in the homotopy category, independently of the construction and of the base point.

The idea of fundamental gerbes is to repeat this construction in the algebro-geometric setting. There is a problem, though: what is the right analogue of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces in degree
1
? We cannot expect them to be schemes, even if we work over
\mathbb{C}
: two elliptic curves are homotopically equivalent, but there is no simple way to see this in the category of algebraic varieties. Algebraic varieties are just too “rigid” to do homotopy.

A better choice is to use stacks, and in particular (pro-)algebraic
gerbes
. For instance, over
\mathbb{C}
every algebraic gerbe is a quotient stack

[\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}/G]

for some algebraic group
G
acting trivially on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, and this quotient is by definition the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}G
.

Over an arbitrary base field
k
, an algebraic gerbe
\Phi
is a classifying stack if and only if
\Phi(k)\neq\emptyset
. If
p\in\Phi(k)
, then
\operatorname{Aut}_{\Phi}(p)
is an algebraic group and
\Phi\simeq\mathcal{B}\operatorname{Aut}_{\Phi}(p)=[\operatorname{Spec}k/\operatorname{Aut}_{\Phi}(p)]
. Every algebraic gerbe over
k
has a geometric point defined over the algebraic closure
\bar{k}
, hence we might think of gerbes as twisted forms of classifying stacks.

Notice that
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, seen as a topological space, is
contractible
: in topology, the standard construction of the classifying space
\mathcal{B}G
is to take the quotient of a contractible topological space by a free action of
G
. With topological spaces, we need the action to be free, otherwise the quotient just doesn’t have the desired properties.

With stacks, we do not need the action to be free: quotient stacks are defined in such a way that we can
pretend
that the action is always free. In fact, one could argue that one of the main reasons for developing the theory of stacks is exactly to pretend that some non-free actions are free. Hence, the most natural choice to define classifying stacks is to take the simplest action on the simplest variety, i.e. the trivial action on one point.

So we replace Eilenberg–MacLane spaces with gerbes. But
which
gerbes? This depends on the type of fundamental group we want to construct. The natural thing to do, rather than choosing the gerbes themselves, is to choose which automorphism groups of points of gerbes we allow. This basically amounts to choosing for which groups
G
we want to consider the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}G
.

The first and most natural choice is to consider finite étale group schemes: this will lead us to defining the étale fundamental gerbe
X\to\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
, which is a close relative of the étale fundamental group. The other class of groups we are interested in is virtually toric groups, i.e. algebraic groups whose connected component is of multiplicative type, this will lead us to define the toric fundamental gerbe
X\to\Pi_{X/k}^{\mathrm{tor}}
.

There are many other choices, though. If we consider finite group schemes, we get Nori’s fundamental gerbe, the analogue of Nori’s fundamental group. Other choices are abelian algebraic groups, or unipotent algebraic groups, see
[
BV19
]
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be such a class of group schemes, and let
\Pi^{\mathcal{T}}_{X/k}
be the associated fundamental gerbe (there are some assumptions to make on
\mathcal{T}
in order to have a functioning machinery, see
[
BV19
]
).

If
X
has a
rational
base point
x\in X(k)
, then we can repeat a construction of Nori
[
Nor82
, Chapter II]
and define a
\mathcal{T}
-group scheme
\pi_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}(X,x)
: the fundamental gerbe
\Pi_{X/k}^{\mathcal{T}}
coincides with the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}\pi_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}(X,x)
. There is a universal
\pi_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}(X,x)
-torsor
U\to X
, the set of isomorphism classes of
\Pi^{\mathcal{T}}_{X/k}(k)
coincides with
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\pi_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}(X,x))
and the morphism
X\to\Pi^{\mathcal{T}}_{X/k}
maps a point
p\in X(k)
to the fiber
U_{p}
, which is a torsor over
\operatorname{Spec}k
and corresponds to a class in
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\pi_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}(X,x))=\Pi^{\mathcal{T}}_{X/k}(k)/\mathord{\sim}
.

The point of using gerbes is exactly that we do not need any base point at all, not even a geometric one.

Moreover, the section conjecture has a particularly simple formulation in the language of fundamental gerbes. If
\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
is the étale fundamental gerbe, the space of Galois sections is simply the set of isomorphism classes of
\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}(k)
, and the profinite Kummer map is just the evaluation of
X\to\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
on
k
-points.

Finally, let us mention that the fundamental gerbe will typically not exist in the class of
algebraic
gerbes, for the same reason why we cannot expect the étale fundamental group to exist as a finite group. There is a limit argument to make: just as the étale fundamental group is profinite, fundamental gerbes are pro-algebraic.

3.3.
Firm fibered categories

While the étale fundamental gerbe exists for geometrically connected fibered categories, other types of fundamental gerbes need stronger assumptions cf.
[
BV19
, Theorem 7.1]
. Let us give these assumptions a name.

Definition 3.2
.

A fibered category

over a field

if it is concentrated, geometrically reduced cf.
[
BV19
, Definition 4.3]
, and
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=k

For instance a proper, geometrically connected, geometrically reduced scheme is firm.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a firm fibered category over a field

. A profinite étale cover

is firm if and only if it is geometrically connected. In particular,
X
is geometrically connected.

Proof.

If
Y

\operatorname{H}^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})=k
; in particular,
Y
is connected. Moreover,

is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, hence global sections commute with base change and

is geometrically connected.

Assume that
Y
is geometrically connected. Since
Y
is profinite étale over
X
, then
Y
is quasi-compact, quasi-separated and geometrically reduced. We have to prove that
\operatorname{H}^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})=k
.

Since
Y
is profinite over
X
, then
\operatorname{H}^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
is algebraic over
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=k
. Since
X
is geometrically reduced and
f
is profinite étale, then

is geometrically reduced, hence
\operatorname{H}^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
is an algebraic, separable extension of

\operatorname{H}^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})=k
.
∎

3.4.
The toric fundamental gerbe

We define the toric fundamental gerbe over fields of arbitrary characteristic, but study its properties in characteristic
0
.

An affine group scheme
G
of finite type over a field
k
is virtually toric, vt for short, if it is an extension of a finite étale group scheme by a connected group scheme of multiplicative type. Equivalently,
G^{0}
is of multiplicative type; in positive characteristic it need not be reduced.

An affine gerbe cf.
[
BV19
, §3]

\Phi
is vt if the automorphism groups of its geometric points are vt. Pro-vt groups (resp. gerbes) are projective limits of vt groups (resp. gerbes).

Definition 3.4
.

The
toric fundamental gerbe
of a firm fibered category
X
over a field
k
is a pro-vt gerbe
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}
with a morphism
X\to\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}
which is universal among morphisms
X\to\Phi
where
\Phi
is a vt gerbe
[
BV19
, Theorem 7.1]
, i.e. it is the fundamental gerbe for the class of vt groups in the terminology of Borne and Vistoli.

The toric fundamental gerbe behaves well with respect to algebraic extensions of the base field.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
BV19
, Theorem 9.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a firm fibered category over a field
k
, and
k^{\prime}/k
an algebraic, separable extension. The natural morphism

\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X_{k^{\prime}}/k^{\prime}}\to\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\times_{k}k^{\prime}

is an isomorphism.

The étale fundamental gerbe behaves well for arbitrary extensions of the base field in characteristic

[

, Proposition A.23]
, but this fails for the toric fundamental gerbe.

Example 3.6
.

Let
\tilde{E}\to E
be the universal cover of an elliptic curve defined over
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
. Consider the projective system
(E)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of

where the

maps to the

is the inductive limit
\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Pic}(E)=\mathbb{Q}\oplus E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})/E[\infty]
, where

is the subgroup of torsion points. For the same reason,
\operatorname{Pic}(\tilde{E}_{\mathbb{C}})=\mathbb{Q}\oplus E(\mathbb{C})/E[\infty]

As we will prove in Lemma

are abelian banded by pro-algebraic groups

over

and

over

The former is countable while the latter is not, hence

is not isomorphic to

and

Definition 3.7
.

The space

of toric Galois sections of a firm fibered category

of quasi-sections of the toric fundamental gerbe.

For a firm fibered category

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

be a firm fibered category over a field

Proof.

A nonzero class in
\operatorname{Br}(X/k)
defines a gerbe
\mathcal{G}
banded by

with

and a morphism

Remark 3.9
.

One might wonder why define
\mathcal{S}_{X/k}^{\mathrm{tor}}
as
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}(k)_{\mathrm{q}}
, and not as
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}(k)/\mathord{\sim}

Example
3.10

Example 3.10

Fix a prime

Consider the projection

It follows that

is trivial if and only if the natural map

For instance, the map is not surjective if

By applying Lemma

Let

As for classical Galois sections, it is important to understand the behaviour of the space of toric Galois sections with respect to finite étale covers.

Proposition 3.12

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{Y/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

Before giving the actual proof, let us sketch the basic idea. For simplicity, assume

and that

T\to Y

-torsor over

is equivariant with respect to the projection

to the first component. In the proof, we basically repeat this argument at the level of gerbes.

Proof.

Assume first that
Y\to X
is finite Galois with Galois group
G
, we are going to prove that
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}=[\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}/G]
(see
[

]

\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}

[\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}/G]

Let
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}\to\Upsilon
be a morphism, where
\Upsilon
is a vt gerbe of finite type over

; we have that
\Upsilon^{G}=\Upsilon\times\dots\times\Upsilon
is a vt gerbe with a natural action of

, and there is a natural

. Let
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}\to\Upsilon^{\prime}\to\Upsilon^{G}
be the canonical factorization as in Proposition

on
\Upsilon^{\prime}
such that
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}\to\Upsilon^{\prime}
is
G
-equivariant.

Hence, up to replacing
\Upsilon
with
\Upsilon^{\prime}
, we may assume that we have a
G
-action on
\Upsilon
such that
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}\to\Upsilon
is
G
-equivariant, and that
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}\to\Upsilon
is locally full. This implies that
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}
can be written as a projective limit of such gerbes

[\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}/G]
is the limit of the gerbes

[\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}/G]

is

\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\to[\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}/G]
by the universal property of the toric fundamental gerbe. On the other hand,
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}\to\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}

[\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}/G]\to\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}
. It is straightforward to check that these maps are inverses, hence we may identify

. With the same argument,
\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}=[\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{Y/k}/G]
.

We now drop the assumption that

.

We may then assume that

, and then we may choose

By the above,
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}=[\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Z/k}/H]
and
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}=[\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Z/k}/G]
, and similarly for the étale fundamental gerbe. Every square in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Z/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{Z/k}}

Finally, assume that

In order to define a

, one has to fix a

More generally, we can fix a rational point of the fundamental gerbe.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a type of fundamental gerbe (e.g. étale or toric), and

a section for some field extension

If

\pi_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}(X)
or even

Remark 3.14
.

If one wants to obtain an object which is more directly comparable with the étale fundamental group, one can choose a

4.
Torsion free abelian group schemes

The connected component of the toric fundamental group is often torsion free, and simply connected: even though a non-trivial torus always has torsion, in the limit process defining the toric fundamental group it can happen that we dominate each finite étale cover, thus obtaining something which is torsion free and simply connected.

Because of this, we first study affine, torsion free group schemes beyond the usual finite type assumption.

A relative abelian group scheme
G\to S
is torsion free if the kernel of the multiplication by
n
map is a trivial relative group scheme for every
n
.

Recall that a homomorphism of affine group schemes
G\to Q
over
k
is a quotient if the corresponding map of Hopf algebras
k[Q]\to k[G]
is injective, or equivalently if it is faithfully flat
[
Wat79
, §14]
. We recall a well-known fact.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
DM82
, Corollary 2.7]
)
.

If
G
is an affine group scheme over a field
k
, there exists a projective system
(G_{i})_{i}
of group schemes of finite type over
k
and compatible quotient homomorphisms
G\to G_{i}
such that
G\to\varprojlim_{i}G_{i}
is an isomorphism. ∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a connected, abelian affine group scheme over a field
k
of characteristic
0
, and
n
a positive integer prime with
\operatorname{char}k
. The multiplication by
n
homomorphism
n:G\to G
is a quotient.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
,
G
is a projective limit of connected, reduced quotients
G\to Q
, and hence
k[G]
is the union of their Hopf algebras
k[Q]
. For every such
Q
, the schematic image
nQ\subseteq Q
is a closed subgroup with
\dim nQ=\dim Q
thanks to
[
SGA3.II
, Exposé XV, Lemma 1.3]
, which implies
nQ=Q
since
Q
is reduced, of finite type and irreducible
[
Wat79
, §6.6]
. This implies that
n:Q\to Q
is a quotient by
[
Wat79
, §15.1]
, i.e.
n^{\#}:k[Q]\to k[Q]
is injective. Since
k[G]
is the union of
k[Q]
for varying
Q
, we get that
n^{\#}:k[G]\to k[G]
is injective as well, i.e.
n:G\to G
is a quotient.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a connected, abelian affine group scheme over a field
k
of characteristic
0
.

The following are equivalent.

(1)

G
is torsion free.

(2)

For every positive integer
n
, the multiplication by
n
map
n:G\to G
is an isomorphism.

(3)

For every quotient homomorphism
G\to Q
with
Q
of finite type and every positive integer
n
, there exists a section
G\to Q
of the multiplication by
n
homomorphism
n:Q\to Q
.

(4)

For every quotient homomorphism
H\to Q
with finite kernel and every homomorphism
G\to Q
, there exists a unique section
G\to H
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Since
G
is torsion free, the multiplication by
n
map
n:G\to G
is a closed embedding
[
Wat79
, §15.3]
and a quotient by Lemma
4.2
. It follows that
n^{\#}:k[G]\to k[G]
is bijective.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Obvious.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. Obvious.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
. Write
f
for the homomorphism
H\to Q
. Up to replacing
Q
with the schematic image of
G\to Q
, we may assume that
G\to Q
is a quotient and
Q
is affine, connected, abelian. Since
f
is affine,
H
is affine as well. Thanks to Lemma
4.1
, we may then reduce to the case in which
H,Q
are of finite type. By replacing
H
with the connected component of the identity, we may assume
H
connected.

After these reductions,
H
is abelian: the commutator subgroup
[H,H]
is connected and is a subgroup of
\ker f
, which is finite, hence
[H,H]=0
. Since
f
is a quotient with finite kernel and
H
is abelian, there exists a
n
such that
n:H\to H
factorizes through
f
, i.e.
n=g\circ f
for some
g:Q\to H
. The composition
f\circ g
is the multiplication by
n
as well: in fact,
f\circ g\circ f=f\circ n=n\circ f
, and
f
is an epimorphism. This gives us the desired section
G\to Q\to H\to Q
.

For uniqueness, we have that
H\to Q
is étale because
\operatorname{char}k=0
and
H\to Q
has finite kernel. Then the diagonal is both open and closed, hence the two sections
G\to H
coincide since
G
is connected.

(4)\Rightarrow(2)
. Write
G=\varprojlim_{i}Q_{i}
using its connected finite-type quotients. For each
i
, the multiplication map
[n]:Q_{i}\to Q_{i}
is a quotient with finite kernel. Apply (4) to obtain the unique lift
s_{i}:G\to Q_{i}
of the quotient map
q_{i}:G\to Q_{i}
through
[n]
. Uniqueness makes these lifts compatible. Their limit is a homomorphism
s:G\to G
satisfying
[n]s=\mathrm{id}_{G}
. Since
s
commutes with multiplication by
n
, also
s[n]=\mathrm{id}_{G}
, proving (2).
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
G
be a connected, abelian, torsion free affine group scheme over a field
k
of characteristic
0
. For every
i\geq 0
,
\operatorname{H}^{i}(k,G)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space, i.e. it is divisible and torsion free.

Proof.

Follows immediately from the fact that, for every
n
, the multiplication by
n
map
G\to G
is an isomorphism.
∎

A
pro-torus
is a group scheme which is a projective limit of tori.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
T
be a torsion-free pro-torus over a field
k
of characteristic
0
, and
T\to H
a homomorphism with
H
of finite type. Then
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,T)\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,H)
is trivial.

More precisely, there exists a quotient
T\to Q
of finite type and a factorization
T\to Q\to H
such that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,Q)\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,H)
is trivial.

Proof.

Since
T
is a pro-torus, by passing to the image we may reduce to the case in which
H
is a torus and a quotient of
T
.

If
H
is a torus, there exists an integer
n
such that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,H)
is
n
-torsion. In fact, let
k^{\prime}
be a splitting field of
H
finite over
k
, for every
\alpha\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,H)
we have
0=\operatorname{cor}_{k^{\prime}/k}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}})=[k^{\prime}:k]\alpha
, since
\alpha_{k^{\prime}}=0\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r})
.

Now take
Q=H
and let
Q=H\to H
be the multiplication by
n
homomorphism. Clearly,
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,Q)\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,H)
is trivial. Proposition
4.3
guarantees the existence of the factorization
T\to Q\to H
.
∎

5.
The connected component of the toric fundamental group

We are going to give geometric conditions which guarantee that the connected component of the toric fundamental group is torsion free. Then, we will prove that this guarantees injectivity of
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
.

Definition 5.1
.

A scheme
X
over
k
has
virtually divisible line bundles
if, for every finite étale cover
Y\to X
, every line bundle
L
on
Y
and every positive integer
n
, there exists a finite étale cover
Z\to Y
and a line bundle
M
on
Z
such that
L|_{Z}\simeq M^{\otimes n}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

A smooth, projective curve of genus
\geq 1
over a field
k
of characteristic
0
has virtually divisible line bundles.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{char}k=0
, by passing to the algebraic closure, we can assume that
k
is algebraically closed. Over an algebraically closed field, a line bundle
L
on a smooth projective curve has the form
M^{\otimes n}
if and only if
n\mid\deg L
. Up to passing to a finite étale cover, we can always assume that this is true if the genus is not
0
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

A torsor for an abelian variety over a field
k
of characteristic
0
has virtually divisible line bundles.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{char}k=0
, then
\bar{k}/k
is separable, hence we may easily reduce to the case in which
k=\bar{k}
. In particular, every torsor over
k
is trivial. Let
A
be an abelian variety and
L
a line bundle on
A
. Every finite étale cover of
A
is dominated, componentwise, by a multiplication map
[n]:A\to A
for some
n>0
. Since the characteristic is
0
, for every
n
the multiplication by
2n
morphism
[2n]:A\to A
is étale. Since

(2n)^{*}L\simeq L^{\otimes\frac{2n(2n+1)}{2}}\otimes(-1)^{*}L^{\otimes\frac{2n(2n-1)}{2}}=(L^{\otimes 2n+1}\otimes(-1)^{*}L^{\otimes 2n-1})^{\otimes n}

by
[
Mum70
, §6, Corollary 3]
, then
A
has virtually divisible line bundles.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact, quasi-separated, geometrically connected scheme over a field
k
of characteristic
0
, and
\tilde{X}\to X
its universal profinite étale cover. The following are equivalent.

•

X
has virtually divisible line bundles.

•

The group
\operatorname{Pic}(\tilde{X})
is divisible.

Proof.

Notice that
\tilde{X}\to X
is affine, hence
\tilde{X}
is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, too. Write
\tilde{X}=\varprojlim_{i}X_{i}
as a projective limit of finite étale covers. The statement follows directly from the isomorphism
\operatorname{Pic}(\tilde{X})\simeq\varinjlim_{i}\operatorname{Pic}(X_{i})

[
EGAIV.3
, Théorème 8.5.2]
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\Upsilon
be a vt gerbe of finite type over a field
k
. There exists a finite étale gerbe
\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
with a morphism

\Upsilon\to\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}

which is a relative abelian gerbe whose band is a connected multiplicative group.

Furthermore, if
\Upsilon\to\Upsilon^{{}^{\prime}\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
is another such morphism, there exists an equivalence
\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to\Upsilon^{{}^{\prime}\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
making the obvious diagram
2
-commutative.

If
k^{\prime}/k
is an arbitrary field extension, then
(\Upsilon_{k^{\prime}})^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}=(\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})_{k^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Define
\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{\Upsilon/k}
as the étale fundamental gerbe of
\Upsilon
. If
\bar{k}
is an algebraic closure, then
\Upsilon_{\bar{k}}=\mathcal{B}G
for some vt group
G
. By Theorem
3.1
, we get that
\Upsilon_{\bar{k}}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}=\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{\mathcal{B}G/\bar{k}}=\mathcal{B}G^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
, where
G^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
is the maximal étale quotient of
G
, i.e. the group scheme of the connected components. In particular, this implies that
\Upsilon\to\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
is a relative abelian gerbe whose band is a connected multiplicative group.

If
\Upsilon\to\Upsilon^{{}^{\prime}\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
is another such morphism, the universal property of the étale fundamental gerbe gives a factorization
\Upsilon\to\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to\Upsilon^{{}^{\prime}\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
. By passing to
\bar{k}
, these become morphisms
\mathcal{B}G\to\mathcal{B}G^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to\mathcal{B}H
for some finite group
H
; the fact that
\mathcal{B}G\to\mathcal{B}H
is a relative abelian gerbe whose kernel is connected implies that
G^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to H
is an isomorphism, and hence
\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to\Upsilon^{{}^{\prime}\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
is an equivalence.

The fact that
(\Upsilon_{k^{\prime}})^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}=(\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})_{k^{\prime}}
follows from the uniqueness we have just proved, since
\Upsilon_{k^{\prime}}\to(\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})_{k^{\prime}}
satisfies the defining property for
(\Upsilon_{k^{\prime}})^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
X
be a firm scheme over a field
k
. Assume that
\pi_{1}(X_{\bar{k}})
is trivial. The toric fundamental gerbe
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}
is abelian banded by a connected, pro-multiplicative group scheme
T
. Furthermore,

\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m}).

Varying
k
, this is equivalent to saying that the band
T
is the Cartier dual
\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}^{\vee}
of the Picard sheaf
\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}
on the small étale site
k_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
.

Proof.

Since
\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X_{\bar{k}}/\bar{k}}=\operatorname{Spec}\bar{k}
, then
\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}=\operatorname{Spec}k
by Theorem
3.1
. The fact that
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}
is abelian banded by a connected, pro-multiplicative group scheme
T
now follows from Lemma
5.5
. In particular,
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}
coincides with the fundamental gerbe for the class of groups of multiplicative type cf.
[
BV19
]
. The isomorphism
T\simeq\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}(X)^{\vee}
then follows from
[
BV19
, Theorem 13.11]
. Let us check this explicitely when
X(k)\neq\emptyset
and
\operatorname{Pic}_{X}(k)=\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, the result then also follows by descent.

A line bundle
L
corresponds to a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor, which in turn corresponds to a map
X\to\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
. By the universal property of the fundamental gerbe, this map factorizes as
X\to\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\to\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
and hence induces a homomorphism of bands
T\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
. This defines a map

\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m}).

This map is a homomorphism: in fact, if
L
,
L^{\prime}
are line bundles associated with morphisms
f,f^{\prime}:X\to\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
and homomorphisms
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})
, then
L\otimes L^{\prime}
is associated with the composition
X\to\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m})\to\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
, which implies that
L\otimes L^{\prime}
corresponds to
\alpha\cdot\alpha^{\prime}
. Let us check injectivity and surjectivity.

If the morphism of bands
T\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
induced by a line bundle
L
is trivial, then
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\to\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
factorizes through
\operatorname{Spec}k
by Proposition
2.2
, hence the same is true for
X\to\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
and
L
is trivial. This proves the injectivity of
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})
.

Let
T\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
be a homomorphism, the induced gerbe
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\times^{T}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is banded by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
and hence is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
since
X(k)\neq\emptyset
implies
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\times^{T}\mathbb{G}_{m}(k)\neq\emptyset
. We thus get a morphism
X\to\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
corresponding to a line bundle
L
. This proves surjectivity of
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})
, hence we conclude.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
X
be a firm scheme over a field
k
of characteristic
0
. The natural map

\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\to\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}

is a relative abelian gerbe whose band is a relative connected, pro-multiplicative group over
\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
. In other words, the natural map from the toric fundamental group to the étale one is faithfully flat, and the kernel
T
is a connected, pro-multiplicative group scheme.

The relative band
\mathcal{T}\to\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
is torsion free if and only if
X
has virtually divisible line bundles. In other words, the group
T
is torsion free if and only if
X
has virtually divisible line bundles.

Proof.

The first part follows directly from Lemma
5.5
.

For the second part, thanks to Theorem
3.5
we may assume that
k
is algebraically closed as well. Let
\tilde{X}\to X
be the universal cover, it is firm by Lemma
3.3
. By Proposition
3.12
, we have a
2
-cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\rm tor}_{\tilde{X}/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{\tilde{X}/k}=\operatorname{Spec}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}.}

By Lemma
5.6
,
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{\tilde{X}/k}
is abelian banded by a connected pro-multiplicative group
T
, and
\operatorname{Pic}(\tilde{X})=\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. The band
\mathcal{T}
of
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\to\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
is a twisted form of
T
over
\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
, hence the relative group
\mathcal{T}
is torsion free if and only if
T
is torsion free. Thanks to Proposition
4.3
, this is equivalent to the following property: for every quotient homomorphism
q:T\to Q
with
Q
of finite type and every positive integer
n
, there exists a section
s:T\to Q
with

.

Since
T
is pro-multiplicative, connected and
k
is algebraically closed of characteristic
0
, then
Q=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}
for some

, hence
T
is torsion free if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})=\operatorname{Pic}(\tilde{X})
is divisible. By Lemma
5.4
, this is equivalent to
X
having virtually divisible line bundles.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

With a proof analogous to the one of Lemma
5.6
, one can prove that the relative band of
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}\to\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}
is the Cartier dual of the relative Picard sheaf of the morphism

.

Proposition 5.9
.

Consider

a locally full morphism of pro-algebraic gerbes over a field

of characteristic

Assume that

The map

Furthermore, if

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upsilon}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upsilon}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upsilon_{i}}

6.
Toric Galois sections and points of the Picard scheme

Let
X
be a proper, geometrically connected variety over a field
k
of arbitrary characteristic. If
X(k)\neq\emptyset
, then
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
. In general, we only have an inclusion
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\subseteq\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
, and there is a cohomological obstruction
\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)\to\operatorname{Br}(k)
whose kernel is the Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}(X)

[
Kle05
, Remark 9.2.11]
. The main example is when
X
is a non-trivial Brauer–Severi variety, so that
\mathcal{O}(1)\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
is not a line bundle. For such a proper variety
X
, the relative Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}(X/k)
coincides with the image of the cohomological obstruction, hence
\operatorname{Br}(X/k)=\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)/\operatorname{Pic}(X)
.

The Leray spectral sequence in fppf cohomology for
X\to\operatorname{Spec}k
gives the exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)\to\operatorname{Br}(k)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m}),

see
[
Kle05
, Remark 9.2.11 and Exercise 9.3.11]
.

In particular, if
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\neq\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
then
\operatorname{Br}(X/k)\neq 0
, and
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}=\emptyset
by Lemma
3.8
.

On the other hand, J. Stix has proved that, if
p\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
is a torsion point of order prime with
\operatorname{char}k
and
p\not\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, then
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}=\emptyset

[
Sti10
, Proposition 12]
.

The following Proposition
6.1
gives a unified, alternative proof of these two statements. Even if it’s not strictly necessary, we find it illuminating because it replaces the long exact sequence above with a geometric argument based on the Picard stack.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a proper, geometrically connected variety over a field
k
.

A rational point
p\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
defines a map
X\to\mathcal{G}
such that

•

\mathcal{G}
is a gerbe banded by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
,

•

\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
p\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)\subseteq\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
,

•

if
k^{\prime}
is any field extension with
X(k^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, the induced morphism
X_{k^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{G}_{k^{\prime}}=B_{k^{\prime}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
defines the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor associated with the line bundle
p_{k^{\prime}}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X_{k^{\prime}})=\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X_{k^{\prime}}}(k^{\prime})
,

•

if
p
is torsion of order prime with
\operatorname{char}k
, we have a factorization
X\to\Pi_{X/k}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to\mathcal{G}
.

As a consequence, if
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\neq\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
then
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}=\emptyset
, and if there is a torsion element
p\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)\smallsetminus\operatorname{Pic}(X)
of order prime with
\operatorname{char}k
, then
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Recall that the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}ic_{X}
is defined as follows: if
S
is a scheme over
k
, then
\mathcal{P}ic_{X}(S)
is the groupoid of line bundles on
X_{S}
, so that
\operatorname{Pic}(X_{S})
is the set of isomorphism classes of
\mathcal{P}ic_{X}(S)

[
Sta25
,
Tag 0372
]
. The Picard sheaf
\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}
is the fppf sheafification of this stack; under our hypotheses, it is representable by a group scheme
[
Kle05
, Corollary 9.4.18.3]
.

A Picard functor is usually defined using the quotients
\operatorname{Pic}(X_{S})/\operatorname{Pic}(S)
, and then sheafified: notice however that
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
is automatically killed by the sheafification, since every line bundle over
S
is locally trivial in
S
. The reason for considering the functor
S\mapsto\operatorname{Pic}(X_{S})/\operatorname{Pic}(S)
is that, if
X(k)\neq\emptyset
, the functor is already an fppf sheaf
[
Kle05
, Theorem 9.2.5]
, thus simplifying things considerably.

There is a natural morphism
\mathcal{P}ic_{X}\to\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}
which is a gerbe banded by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. In fact, given any scheme
S
and a line bundle
L
on
X_{S}
, the group of automorphisms of
L
is naturally
\mathbb{G}_{m}(S)=\mathcal{O}(S)^{*}
because
\mathcal{O}(X_{S})=\mathcal{O}(S)
(recall that
X
is proper, geometrically connected). If
p\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X/k}(k)
is a rational point, we write
\mathcal{G}_{p}\subset\mathcal{P}ic_{X}
for the fiber, it is a gerbe over
k
banded by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. The gerbe
\mathcal{G}_{p}
has a rational point if and only if
p\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
defines a line bundle.

Write
S=X
for a copy of
X
. The fibered product
X_{S}=X\times X
has a section
s:S\to X_{S}
, the diagonal. By
[
Kle05
, Theorem 9.2.5]
, this implies that there is a line bundle
M
on
X_{S}
corresponding to
p\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
. The line bundle
M
is only well defined in
\operatorname{Pic}(X_{S})/\operatorname{Pic}(S)
(because there are several morphisms
S\to\mathcal{G}_{p}
); since we can modify
M
by elements of
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
, we can rigidify the situation by imposing that
M
restricts to the trivial bundle on the section
s:S\to X_{S}
.

The line bundle
M
on
X_{S}
corresponds to a morphism
S\to\mathcal{G}_{p}
; this factorizes through
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{S/k}
, because
\mathcal{G}_{p}
is banded by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Since
S=X
and
\mathcal{G}_{p}(k)
is empty if
p\notin\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, we get that
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}=\emptyset
if
\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)\neq\operatorname{Pic}(X)
.

It remains to give the factorization
X\to\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}\to\mathcal{G}_{p}
if
p
is torsion of order prime with
\operatorname{char}k
. By Proposition
2.2
, we have a factorization
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{S/k}\to\Phi_{p}\to\mathcal{G}_{p}
, with
\Phi_{p}\to\mathcal{G}_{p}
faithful. Since
\mathcal{G}_{p}
is banded by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, either
\Phi_{p}\simeq\mathcal{G}_{p}
or
\Phi_{p}
is banded by
\mu_{n}\subset\mathbb{G}_{m}
for some
n
. In the second case, if
\operatorname{char}k\nmid n
then we get the desired factorization by the universal property of the étale fundamental gerbe.

I claim that
\Phi_{p}
is banded by
\mu_{n}
if and only if
p\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
is torsion of order
n
. This is enough to conclude, and can be checked after extending the base field. Hence, we may assume that
X(k)\neq\emptyset
, so that applying
[
Kle05
, Theorem 9.2.5]
again we get a line bundle
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{X}(k)
associated with
p
and a section
\operatorname{Spec}k\to\mathcal{G}_{p}
(this section is unique up to isomorphism, because
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is trivial).

Hence, we have a map
X\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
corresponding to the line bundle
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. By the Kummer exact sequence

\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mu_{n})\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})\xrightarrow{n}\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m}),

if
L
is
n
-torsion then it is induced by an element of
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\mu_{n})
. The corresponding map
X\to B\mu_{n}
gives the desired factorization.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
P
be a Brauer–Severi variety of positive dimension over a field
k
. The gerbe
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{P/k}
is of finite type, abelian and banded by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. If

, then
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{P/k}(k)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
P\to\mathcal{G}
be the morphism given by Proposition
6.1
for
\mathcal{O}(1)\in\underline{\operatorname{Pic}}_{P}(k)
. The gerbe
\mathcal{G}
is abelian and banded by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, hence there is an induced morphism
\Pi^{\rm tor}_{P/k}\to\mathcal{G}
. This is an isomorphism: it can be checked after passing to a separable closure so that

, where it follows from the fact that
\mathbb{P}^{n}\to\mathcal{G}=B\mathbb{G}_{m}
corresponds to

, plus Lemma

. Finally,
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\mathcal{O}(1)\in\operatorname{Pic}(P)
, which in turn is equivalent to

.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

If
X
is a firm fibered category over a field
k
with a morphism
X\to P
, where

is a non-trivial Brauer–Severi variety, then

.

We have an induced map
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{P/k}
; since

is of finite type, it is algebraic, hence

.
∎

7.
Toric versus étale

In this section, we compare the classical version of the section conjecture with the toric one.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
X
be a firm fibered category over a field
k
and
Y\to X
a geometrically connected profinite étale cover. The natural map

\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{Y/k}\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{Y/k}\times_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}}\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}

is surjective.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.12
, we have a
2
-cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\rm tor}_{Y/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{Y/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\rm tor}_{X/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}}

which we may write as a projective limit of
2
-cartesian diagrams

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upsilon}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}

with
\Pi
,
\Upsilon
vt gerbes of finite type. The map

\Pi(k)/\mathord{\sim}\to\Upsilon(k)/\mathord{\sim}\times_{\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(k)/\mathord{\sim}}\Pi^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(k)/\mathord{\sim}

is easily checked to be surjective. Furthermore, its fibers are finite, since two elements of
\Upsilon^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(k)
can only be isomorphic in a finite number of ways.

The limit of the left hand side is
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{Y/k}
, and the limit of the right hand side is
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{Y/k}\times_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{X/k}}\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}

.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let

over a field

Proof.

Let

\widehat{A}(k)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{k}(A,\mathbb{G}_{m})

\operatorname{H}^{1}(\Gamma,A(k^{\prime}))\times\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{k}(A,\mathbb{G}_{m})\to\operatorname{H}^{2}(\Gamma,k^{\prime*})

1
-cocycle
\gamma:\Gamma\to A(k^{\prime})

\Gamma
-invariant extension

by

commuting with the Galois actions on

and
k^{\prime*}
for every

\lambda:\Gamma^{2}\to k^{\prime*}

Now assume that

, and let

be a Galois invariant, algebraically trivial line bundle on

\omega(g,h)=\phi(gh)\circ\phi(h)^{-1}\circ\phi(g)^{-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{k^{\prime}}(L)=k^{\prime*}.

Choose

a

-point and identify it with the origin of

, its Galois conjugates define a cocycle

. If

=\phi(gh)(e)=\phi(gh)(e)\cdot\phi(g)(\phi(h)(e))^{-1}\cdot\phi(g)(\phi(h)(e))

and hence

\omega(g,h)=\phi(gh)(e)\cdot\phi(g)(\phi(h)(e))^{-1}=

∎

9.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a field
k

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and

a closed subvariety. We write

•

for the schematic image of the morphism

.

•

for the inverse image of

along the multiplication by

The purpose of this section is to prove the following Theorem

.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
k
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
A
an abelian variety over
k
,
X\subset A
a closed subvariety such that

. Assume either that

•

has potentially good reduction, or

•

contains no translates of positive dimensional abelian subvarieties over

.

For every

has index divisible by

.

Remark 9.2
.

It is rather easy to show that, since
X\cap A[p^{\prime}]=\emptyset
, for every closed point

there exists

such that the fiber

has index divisible by

. By compactness, we can find a single

which works uniformly for every

-point, where

is any fixed finite extension of

. The point of Theorem

is finding a single

which works uniformly for all closed points at the same time.

Clearly, the index decreases after base change. Hence, we may freely replace

with finite extensions.

Up to extending

, we may then assume that
k
contains a primitive
p
-th root of

, and that
A
has semistable reduction with Néron model
\mathcal{A}
.

9.1.
Local analysis

First, we study a local analogue of Theorem
9.1
. Let us begin by fixing some notation.

Notation 9.3
.

In subsection
9.1
, we use the following notation.

•

R
is a complete DVR of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with fraction field
K
, residue field
F
, uniformizing parameter
\pi
, and valuation
v
normalized by
v(\pi)=1
. We assume that
R
contains a primitive
p
-th root of
1
.

•

We write
\mathbb{C}_{K}
for the completion of
\bar{K}
with respect to the unique prolongation of the valuation to
\bar{K}
, and
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathbb{C}_{K}
for the elements of strictly positive valuation. We still write
v
for the valuation on
\mathbb{C}_{K}
.

•

G\simeq\operatorname{Spf}R[[T_{1},\dots,T_{g}]]
is a smooth, commutative formal group over
R
.

•

We shorten the variables
(T_{1},\dots,T_{g})
as a variable-vector
T=(T_{1},\dots,T_{g})
, and similarly
U=(U_{1},\dots,U_{g})
for vectors in
\mathbb{C}_{K}^{\oplus g}
.

•

Products of vectors are intended component-wise, and not as scalar products. In particular, for every positive integer
m
, we write
T^{m}=(T_{1}^{m},\dots,T_{g}^{m})
, and similarly for
U
.

•

\Phi(T)=(\Phi_{1}(T),\dots,\Phi_{g}(T))\in R[[T]]^{\oplus g}
is the formal power series of the multiplication by
p
map
[p]:G\to G
.

We are interested in the case
R=\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{k^{\rm nr}}}
, the completed maximal unramified valuation ring, with
G
the formal group at the identity of
\mathcal{A}_{R}
.

Loosely speaking, the purpose of this section is to prove that a subvariety of the formal group whose distance from
0
is larger than
|\pi|^{c}
cannot contain prime-to-
p
points which are
p^{2c}
-divisible (see Proposition
9.13
), where the distance is intended in the sense of
[
Vol97
,
Sca98
]
.

Remark 9.4
.

Our target is counterintuitive, at least for
G=\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. We can find points
U\in G(S)=\mathfrak{m}_{S}^{\oplus g}
with
|U|
very close to
1
and
\deg S/R
prime to
p
, so that
|[p^{2c}]U|
is again close to
1
, and consider the subvariety
X
whose points are the Galois conjugates of
[p^{2c}]U
. For any
x\in X
, we have that
|x|
is close to
1
. This looks strange, because all the points of
X
are divisible by
p^{2c}
, which seems to contradict the above.

This is not the case. The distance of
X
from
0
is not
\inf_{x\in X}|x|
, which is indeed large: rather,
d(X,0)=\sup_{f}|f(0)|
, where
f
varies among integral functions vanishing on
X
. For instance, in dimension one put
y=[p^{2c}]U
and let
K(y)
be its minimal field of definition. The minimal polynomial of
y
defines
X
, so

d(X,0)=\left|\mathrm{N}_{K(y)/K}(y)\right|=|y|^{[K(y):K]}.

This can be very small when
[K(y):K]
is large even if
|y|
is close to
1
.

Remark 9.5
.

A crucial ingredient of the proof of Theorem
9.1
is a theorem by Scanlon giving a uniform lower bound on the distance from
X
to torsion points. The correct distance is lower than the “naive” distance described in Remark
9.4
, hence Scanlon’s theorem is a priori stronger than its “naive” counterpart. We need the strong version to prove Theorem
9.1
, while the weak version does not seem to be sufficient: this hindered progress for a long time. It turns out that the weak version implies the strong version
[
Vol97
, p. 639]
, but this requires a theorem of Greenberg of which we were not aware
[
Gre66
]
. ChatGPT’s main contribution to the paper was to help me realize that I was using the weak version of Scanlon’s theorem while I needed the strong one.

Remark 9.6
.

Before starting the proof, let us sketch the idea. Fix a constant
c>0
. First we prove that, for
n>>0
large enough,
[p^{n}]:G\to G
can be approximated modulo
\pi^{c+1}
by power series in
T^{p^{c}}
. This follows from standard arguments. Hence, the same is true for

, where

is any power series. It turns out that

is sufficient.

The idea is that, if
S/R
has degree prime with
p
and

, we have
\operatorname{Tr}(s^{p^{c}})\in\pi^{c+1}R
. More generally, this is true for every symmetric polynomial with zero constant term evaluated on the Galois conjugates of

, and this generalized statement has an easy proof by induction on
c
.

Let
f\in R[[T]]
be a function vanishing on
X
with
f(0)\neq 0
. By the above, if
U\in G
is a prime-to-
p
point and
c=v(f(0))
, we get that

v(\operatorname{Tr}(f([p^{2c}]U)))=v(\operatorname{Tr}(f(0)))=v(f(0)).

This implies that

does not vanish on
[p^{2c}]U
, hence
[p^{2c}]U\not\in X
.

Lemma 9.7
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. There exists
\Psi(T)\in R[[T]]^{\oplus g}
such that

\Phi^{\circ n}(T)\cong\Psi(T^{p^{n}})\pmod{\pi}.

Proof.

On the special fiber, we may factorize
[p^{n}]=V\circ F
, where
F:G\to G^{(p^{n})}
and
V:G^{(p^{n})}\to G
are the Frobenius and Verschiebung homomorphisms
[
Dem72
, Chapter 2, §5]
. The formal group

can be identified with the formal spectrum of

, with Frobenius simply given by the inclusion

. This implies that the reduction of
\Phi^{\circ n}
is a series in

, which is exactly the statement.
∎

Lemma 9.8
.

Let
a\geq 1
be an integer. If
U,V\in\mathfrak{o}^{\oplus g}
are vectors with

U\cong V\pmod{\pi^{a}},

then

\Phi(U)\cong\Phi(V)\pmod{\pi^{a+1}}.

Proof.

By Lemma

,

\Phi(T)=\Psi(T^{p})+\pi\Gamma(T)

for some
\Psi,\Gamma\in R[[T]]^{\oplus g}
.

By hypothesis,

for some integral vector

. Then

\Phi(U)=\Psi((V+\pi^{a}W)^{p})+\pi\Gamma(V+\pi^{a}W).

We may write

for some integral vectors

, hence

\Phi(U)=\Psi(V^{p}+\pi^{a+1}W_{1})+\pi\Gamma(V+\pi^{a}W)\cong\Psi(V^{p})+\pi\Gamma(V)=\Phi(V)\pmod{\pi^{a+1}}.

∎

Lemma 9.9
.

Let
\Psi(T)\in R[[T]]

∎

Lemma 9.11
.

Let

be an extension of degree prime with
p
, and

an element in the maximal ideal of

. Fix an embedding

, and let

be the Galois conjugates of

, with
s_{i}\neq s_{j}
if
i\neq j
.

Let

be an integer. Assume that, for every symmetric polynomial

with

, we have

Then

, with

Proof.

Since

It is enough to prove the statement for a symmetric polynomial

∎

Corollary 9.12
.

Let

Let

∎

9.2.
Special fiber

Write

for the residue field of

for its maximal prime-to-

for the Néron model.

Proposition 9.14

The group

is finite. As a consequence, there exists an integer

such that

-torsion.

Proof.

Let

be the connected component of the identity, it is enough to prove that

is finite since the number of connected components is finite.

Let

be the rank of

. For every

, the group scheme

gives an action
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}/F)\to\mathrm{GL}(r,\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
. Notice that the kernel of

is a

\operatorname{Id}+pM
, and
(\operatorname{Id}+pM)^{p^{m}}=\operatorname{Id}
for

large enough. Since
\mathrm{GL}(r,\mathbb{Z}/p)
is finite, there exists a finite extension

such that the composition

is trivial. In particular, the image of

is a

-group for every

.

This implies that the images of
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{E}/E)\to\mathrm{GL}(r,\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
and

Hence, we have

\mathcal{A}(F^{(p^{\prime})})=\mathcal{A}[p^{\infty}](F^{(p^{\prime})})\times\mathcal{A}[p^{\prime}](F^{(p^{\prime})})
, we conclude that

is

-torsion.
∎

For every extension

, denote by

.

Now recall that

Corollary 9.16

. For every

9.3.

Write

for the completion of
\mathcal{O}_{k^{\rm nr}}
, where

is the maximally unramified extension, and

.

Let us recall a theorem of T. Scanlon

]
.

Theorem 9.17

(T. Scanlon)
.

Let

be a subvariety. There exists a uniform lower bound on the distance between

and points of
\mathcal{A}[p^{\prime}](R)\smallsetminus Y
. More explicitly, there exists an integer

such that, for every

in

.

We call

Scanlon’s constant
for

. Scanlon’s theorem gives us a way to “capture”

-torsion points defined over

.

Corollary 9.18
.

Notation as in Theorem
9.17
. Let

an integral

-torsion point over

,

be a finite extension of degree prime to

,

.

Proof.

By Theorem

, if

there exists an integral function

on a neighbourhood

of

in

which vanishes on

and satisfies

. Consider the neighbourhood

of

, and the integral function

on

; in particular,

. By Proposition

,

, which is absurd because

.
∎

9.4.

Assume that

has good reduction, so that every point is integral and prime-to-

. Let

be the integer given by Proposition

, and

.

Choose

any finite extension of degree prime with

,

a point. By Corollary

9.5.

Let us prove Theorem

under the assumption that

contains no translate of a positive dimensional abelian subvariety. By Manin–Mumford
[

, p. 327]
,

contains finitely many torsion points. Fix

with

such that, if

.

Let

be the integer given by Proposition
9.15
, and

a Scanlon’s constant working both for

and

.

Let us prove that, if

is a finite extension with

and

is a point, then

; equivalently,

has index divisible by

.

By Corollary
9.16
, there exists
\tau_{0}\in A[p^{\prime}](L)
and

such that

. Write
\tau=p^{2c}\tau_{0}
, we have

.

Lemma 9.19
.

If
\tau\in A[p^{\prime}](\bar{k})
and
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k^{\rm nr})
, then
\alpha(\beta\tau-\tau)=\beta\tau-\tau
.

Proof.

Clearly, we may reduce to the case in which
\tau\in A[\ell^{d}]
for some prime
\ell\neq p
, which in turn reduces to the analogous statement on

. By Grothendieck’s characterization of abelian varieties with semistable reduction
[
SGA7.I
, Exposé IX, Proposition 3.5]
, we have a short exact sequence

of Galois modules where the Galois actions of the inertia on

and

are both trivial. If

, we get that

, and hence
\alpha(\beta v-v)-(\beta-v)=0
.
∎

Let us work in

. Notice that

is a cyclic Galois extension of order prime with

. Choose
\gamma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\rm nr}/k^{\rm nr})
a generator.

By Lemma
9.19
,

is Galois invariant, hence

. Notice that

\gamma p^{r+2c}a-p^{r+2c}a=\delta+p^{2c}(\gamma b-b)\in X-X.

By Corollary
9.18
,
\delta\in X-X
. This implies that

\gamma m\tau-m\tau=m\delta=0,

hence

is Galois invariant because

is a generator, i.e.
m\tau\in A[p^{\prime}](k^{\rm nr})=\mathcal{A}[p^{\prime}](R)
.

Clearly, we have
mp^{r+2c}a=m\tau+p^{2c}mb\in mX
, with

integral. By Corollary
9.18
, we get that

, which is a contradiction because

implies
mX\cap A[p^{\prime}]=\emptyset
.

of a curve

and consider it as an abelian variety where the origin is the unique rational point associated with the image of

We are going to use the following observation repeatedly.

Since

12.
The Tannakian interpretation of the toric fundamental group

In this section we sketch a Tannakian interpretation of the toric fundamental gerbe. The rest of the paper is logically independent of this interpretation.

Throughout this section,
X
is a smooth, projective, geometrically connected variety over a field
k
, of dimension
n\geq 1
. Fix an ample Cartier divisor
H
on
X
. When
n=1
, the construction uses the ordinary degree of a line bundle and does not require a choice of
H
. We refer to
[
DM82
]
for the terminology of Tannakian categories.

12.1.
Pure cactuses and slopes

If
f:Y\to X
is a finite étale cover and
L
is a line bundle on
Y
, write

d_{H}(L)=\deg_{k}\bigl(c_{1}(L)\cdot(f^{*}H)^{n-1}\bigr).

In particular, if
g:Z\to Y
is a connected finite étale cover, projection formula gives

d_{H}(g^{*}L)=\deg(Z/Y)\cdot d_{H}(L).

Here and below the polarization on a cover is always pulled back from
X
.

Definition 12.1
.

A vector bundle
V
on
X
is a
pure cactus of slope
q\in\mathbb{Q}
if there exists a connected finite étale cover
f:Y\to X
such that

f^{*}V\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}L_{i},\qquad d_{H}(L_{i})=\deg(Y/X)\cdot q\quad\text{for every }i.

The cover and the splitting are not part of the datum.

A
cactus
on
X
is a vector bundle with a specified finite
\mathbb{Q}
-grading

V=\bigoplus_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}V_{q},

where
V_{q}
is pure of slope
q
. A morphism of cactuses is a morphism of vector bundles preserving the grading. We denote this category by
\operatorname{Cact}_{X,H}
, or simply
\operatorname{Cact}_{X}
when the polarization is understood.

The slope
q
of a pure cactus coincides with the slope of the underlying bundle: if
f:Y\to X
is a finite étale cover with a splitting
f^{*}V\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}L_{i}
, we get

q=\frac{d_{H}(L_{1})}{\deg(Y/X)}=\frac{(c_{1}(L_{1})+\dots+c_{1}(L_{r}))\cdot(f^{*}H)^{n-1}}{r\cdot\deg(Y/X)}=

=\frac{c_{1}(f^{*}V)\cdot(f^{*}H)^{n-1}}{\operatorname{rk}(V)\cdot\deg(Y/X)}=\frac{c_{1}(V)\cdot H^{n-1}}{\operatorname{rk}(V)}=\mu_{H}(V).

The grading, however, is part of the datum of a general cactus, because we do not want a morphism of cactuses to mix different slopes. For example, on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
a non-zero section
\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}(1)
is not a morphism of cactuses, because
\mathcal{O}
has slope
0
whereas
\mathcal{O}(1)
has slope
1
.

Lemma 12.2
.

Let
L,M
be line bundles on a smooth, projective, connected
k
-scheme
Y
of positive dimension, with equal degree with respect to some ample line bundle. Every nonzero morphism
L\to M
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

A nonzero morphism
L\to M
gives a global section of
M\otimes L^{-1}
. Since
M\otimes L^{-1}
has degree
0
with respect to an ample line bundle, every non-zero section is never-vanishing. It follows that
L\to M
is an isomorphism.
∎

Proposition 12.3
.

The category
\operatorname{Cact}_{X}
is a
k
-linear Tannakian category. Its tensor product, dual and unit are given by

(V\otimes W)_{q}=\bigoplus_{a+b=q}V_{a}\otimes W_{b},\qquad(V^{\vee})_{q}=(V_{-q})^{\vee},\qquad\mathbbm{1}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\text{ in slope }0.

Every rational point
x\in X(k)
defines a fibre functor
\omega_{x}(V)=V|_{x}
; in particular,
\operatorname{Cact}_{X}
is neutral if
X(k)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let us check that
\operatorname{Cact}_{X}
is abelian. Given a morphism of cactuses
V\to W
, we need to check that kernel and cokernel are cactuses. Clearly, we may assume that
V
,
W
are pure of the same slope
q
. After passage to a finite étale cover, we might also assume that
V
and
W
are direct sums of line bundles of a fixed degree
d
. By Lemma
12.2
, we may furthermore reduce to the case
V=L^{\oplus a}
,
W=L^{\oplus b}
for a fixed line bundle
L
. By Lemma
12.2
again, a morphism
L^{\oplus a}\to L^{\oplus b}
is simply given by an
a\times b
matrix. Kernel and cokernel of
V\to W
are then isomorphic to
L^{\oplus a-r}
,
L^{\oplus b-r}
, where
r
is the rank of the matrix.

The rest of the proof is straightforward and left to the reader.
∎

12.2.
The gerbe of cactuses

For an object
V
of a Tannakian category, write
\langle V\rangle
for the smallest strictly full Tannakian subcategory containing
V
, and similarly
\langle V,W\rangle=\langle V\oplus W\rangle
. These finitely generated subcategories correspond to the finite type quotient gerbes of the gerbe of fibre functors.

Lemma 12.4
.

The gerbe
\operatorname{Fib}(\operatorname{Cact}_{X})
is pro-vt.

Proof.

Fix a cactus

, and choose a connected finite étale Galois cover

with Galois group

splitting its graded pieces into lines

. The bundle

is finite, because

. In particular, it is a pure cactus of slope

.

Choose a geometric point of
X
, let
\omega:\operatorname{Cact}_{X}\to\operatorname{Vect}_{\bar{k}}
be the fibre functor and let
\operatorname{Aut}(\omega)
be the geometric Tannaka group of
\langle V,A\rangle
. The representation of
\operatorname{Aut}(\omega)
on
\omega(V\oplus A)
is faithful, whereas the representation on
\omega(A)
has finite image, because
A
is a finite bundle. Let
H
be the kernel of
\operatorname{Aut}(\omega)\to\mathrm{GL}(\omega(A))
.

We get a faithful action of
H
on
\omega(V)
. Let

be the connected component of

containing a chosen lift of the base point. By adjunction and the projection formula, pullback identifies
\langle V|_{Y_{0}}\rangle
with the Tannakian quotient of
\langle V,A\rangle_{\bar{k}}
by
\langle A\rangle_{\bar{k}}
defined by the restriction of

. The Tannaka group of this quotient is

. Since

is a direct sum of line bundles,

is a direct sum of one-dimensional

acts diagonally and faithfully. This is sufficient to conclude that the connected component of
\operatorname{Aut}(\omega)
is of multiplicative type. Taking the limit along all

proves that
\operatorname{Fib}(\operatorname{Cact}_{X})
is pro-vt.
∎

The forgetful functor defines a canonical morphism

. Since
\operatorname{Fib}(\operatorname{Cact}_{X})
is pro-vt, the universal property of the toric fundamental gerbe gives a factorization

.

k
-linear tensor functor
\operatorname{Rep}G\to\operatorname{Cact}_{X}
.

Write
\Omega
for the group of characters of
T
. Since
T
is abelian, we get a natural action of
G/T
on
T
by conjugation, and hence an action on
\Omega
.

For every character
\chi:T\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
, we get a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor induced by
P
on
Y
, and hence a line bundle
L_{\chi}
on
Y
. Define the slope

q_{\chi}=\frac{\deg_{k}\bigl(c_{1}(L_{\chi})\cdot(f^{*}H)^{n-1}\bigr)}{\deg(Y/X)}.

Notice that, if
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
are in the same
G/T
orbit, then
q_{\chi}=q_{\chi^{\prime}}
thanks to the fact that
H
is defined over
X
.

Let
W
be a representation of
G
, corresponding to a vector bundle
\mathcal{W}\to BG
. We have a splitting
W|_{T}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\Omega}W_{\chi}
. For every orbit
S\subset\Omega
, write
W_{S}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in S}W_{\chi}
, it is immediate to check that
W=\bigoplus_{S}W_{S}
as a
G
-representation.

Hence, we get a splitting
\mathcal{W}=\bigoplus_{S}\mathcal{W}_{S}
. Since

f^{*}h^{*}\mathcal{W}\simeq\bigoplus_{\chi}L_{\chi}\otimes_{k}W_{\chi},

we see that the pullback of
\mathcal{W}_{S}
is a pure cactus for every orbit
S
, hence
\mathcal{W}
has a natural structure of cactus. This gives the desired factorization
X\to\operatorname{Fib}(\operatorname{Cact}_{X})\to BG
, concluding the proof.
∎

Recall that a Tannakian category
\mathcal{C}
is algebraic if
\mathcal{C}=\langle V\rangle
for some
V\in\mathcal{C}
, equivalently if its gerbe of fibre functors is algebraic
[
DM82
, Remark 3.10]
.

Definition 12.6
.

A
quasi-fibre functor
of
\mathcal{C}
is a choice of an isomorphism class
[F_{V}]
of
k
-valued fibre functors on
\langle V\rangle
for every
V\in\mathcal{C}
, such that

[F_{W}|_{\langle V\rangle}]=[F_{V}]\quad\text{whenever }\langle V\rangle\subseteq\langle W\rangle.

This is a compatibility condition on isomorphism classes; coherent choices of isomorphisms are not part of the datum. Restriction defines a natural map from isomorphism classes of fibre functors on
\mathcal{C}
to quasi-fibre functors. The set of quasi-fibre-functors identifies naturally with
\operatorname{Fib}(\mathcal{C})(k)_{\mathrm{q}}
. In particular, a toric Galois section in
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}=\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}(k)_{\mathrm{q}}
corresponds to a quasi-fibre functor on
\operatorname{Cact}_{X}
.

Abstract
. We express the outer multiplicities in the tensor products of two fundamental simple modules for an affine Kac-Moody algebra of type A A in terms of counting certain sets of multipartitions by exploring the stabilizing limits of certain excellent filtrations. This extends for all ranks a previously obtained result by Jakelić and the second author for rank 1 1 . The same outer multiplicities were previously computed by Misra and Wilson in terms of counting certain sets of tableaux. By comparing these two expressions and by explicitly exhibiting a combinatorial description of level- 2 2 affine Weyl group orbits, we establish the existence of a bijection between the Misra-Wilson set of tableaux and a disjoint union of certain sets of multipartitions.



1.
Introduction

There are many examples of “counting identities” which can be proved by representation theoretic arguments. Such proofs arise from establishing different combinatorial expressions for the same representation theoretic invariant. For instance, the invariant we shall focus on here is the multiplicity of a simple module
S
as a summand of a given integrable module
V
lying in category
\mathcal{O}
for an affine Kac-Moody algebra
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let

[V:S]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

denote this multiplicity. More precisely, we focus on the case that
V
is a tensor product of two integrable simple modules from
\mathcal{O}
. As commented in the introduction of
[
10
]
, to which the present paper can be regarded as a follow up, there are several papers dedicated to the problem of computing such multiplicities.

One of the most prominent combinatorial gadget in Lie representation theoretic realm is the concept of Young tableaux. For instance, a certain set of Young tableaux was used to provide a model for fundamental highest-weight crystals of type
A_{n}^{(1)}
in
[
11
,
14
]
. Let
\Lambda_{i},i\in\hat{I}=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
, denote the fundamental weights of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
and let
\hat{P}^{+}
be the corresponding set of dominant affine weights. For
\xi\in\hat{P}^{+}
, we let
V(\xi)
denote a simple module of highest weight
\xi
. Using these crystal models, it was proved in
[
15
]
that

(1)

[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda_{i}):V(\xi)]=\tau^{n}_{i}(\eta)

where
\eta=\Lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{i}-\xi
and
\tau^{n}_{i}(\eta)
counts the elements of a certain set of extended Young tableaux which we refer to as the set of MW
i
-tableaux. The precise definition of such tableaux is reviewed (or rather, rephrased) in
Section

2.2
.

On the other hand, the main result of
[
10
]
gives an expression for computing

[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda):V(\xi)]

for any
\Lambda,\xi\in\hat{P}^{+}
in the case that the underlying simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is simply laced, in terms of multiplicities in excellent filtrations, also known as Demazure flags.
The expression has the following form

(2)

[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda):V(\xi)]=\sum_{\mu\in P^{-}_{\xi}}\max_{\gamma\in P^{-}_{\Lambda}}\ [D(\gamma):D(\mu)].

Here,
P^{-}_{\xi}
denotes a certain subset of the orbit of
\xi
by the action of the affine Weyl group and
D(\mu)
is the Demazure module generated by the weight space
\mu
inside
V(\xi)
. Also, if
\ell
is the level of
\xi
, so the level of
\Lambda
is
\ell-1
,
[D(\gamma):D(\mu)]
denotes the multiplicity of
D(\mu)
in a level-
\ell
Demazure flag for
D(\gamma)
.
If
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{1}
, the
\max
in (
2
) was realized in
[
10
]
as a stabilizing limit. We extend this result for type
A_{n}
in
3.5.1
, whose statement has the form

(3)

[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda):V(\xi)]=\sum_{\mu\in P^{-}_{\xi}}\lim_{k\to\infty}[D(\gamma_{k}):D(\mu)],

where
\gamma_{k}
is an explicitly (and easily) described sequence of elements in
P^{-}_{\Lambda}
.

In the case that
\ell=2
, the numbers
[D(\gamma):D(\mu)]
have been computed in
[
4
]
in terms of products of
q
-binomials (the precise statement is recalled in (
5.4.1
)). Such expression establishes a connection between Demazure-flag multiplicities and partitions (see (
5.3.2
)). Once this connection is established,
5.3.1
essentially explains how to compute the limit in (
3
). This leads to the main representation theoretic result of the present paper,
3.5.2
, whose statement has the form

(4)

[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda_{i}):V(\xi)]=\sum_{\mu\in P^{-}_{\xi}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}(f_{i,\xi}(\mu)).

Here,
f_{i,\xi}
is a certain quadratic function explicitly defined in (
3.5.8
) and
\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)
, where
\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, counts the elements of a certain set of multipartitions of
m\in\mathbb{Z}
(see (
2.1.10
)), whose parts are bounded by the numbers
b_{j},1\leq j\leq n
. Comparing this with (
1
), we get

(5)

\tau^{n}_{i}(\eta)=\sum_{\mu\in P^{-}_{\xi}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}(f_{i,\xi}(\mu)).

Our main combinatorial result,
2.3.3
, arises from this after a combinatorial description of the set
P^{-}_{\xi}
is obtained by characterizing the affine Weyl group orbit of
\xi
in
Section

4.2
. Thus, the “tableaux counting” given by
\tau^{n}_{i}
can be done by a “partition counting” without any reference to representation theory of Lie algebras. This is illustrated in
2.3.4
.

The aforementioned results solve a part of the problems mentioned in
[
10
]
as topics for future publications. Let us comment on those which are not treated here. While (
2
) is valid for all simply laced
\mathfrak{g}
and
\Lambda
of all levels, (
3
) has been proved only for type
A
. The core of the proof is
5.1.2
, which allows us to produce the sequence
\gamma_{k}
. We have a candidate for a corresponding result for type
D
and the proof is apparently within reach. Extending for type
E
is also probably not out of reach, so analogues of (
3
) for these types should not be too difficult to obtain. We have also obtained formulas similar to (
4.1.8
) for types
B,C,D
, and
G
and, hence, purely combinatorial descriptions of the sets
P^{-}_{\xi}
similar to (
4.2.8
) for theses types are also within reach and we expect they should appear in a forthcoming publication soon motivated by other kind of applications.

The real difficulty apparently lies in computing the limit on the right-hand side of (
3
) for types
D
and
E
and even for type
A
in the case
\ell>2
. The approach we used here rely on formulas for Demazure-flag multiplicities in terms of
q
-binomials. Such formula exists for type
A_{1}
and
\ell=3

[
3
, Proposition 1.4]
. In this case, following step-by-step the computations we have done here should lead to an expression similar to (
4
) for tensor products of the form
V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda)
with
\Lambda
of level
2
. We are not aware of formulas of this type for higher
\ell
or other types beside
A_{1}
. Still in type
A_{1}
, it was obtained in
[
5
]
an expression for multiplicities in level-
\ell
Demazure flags for level-
1
Demazure modules for any
\ell
in terms of certain sets of Dyck paths. For
\ell=2
, we have established a bijection of such Dyck paths with certain sets of partitions and recovered the results of
[
10
]
. For general
\ell
, the expressions from
[
5
]
can be used to produced alternating expressions for level-
\ell
Demazure flags for level-
(\ell-1)
Demazure modules in terms of Dyck paths, so they can be plugged in on the right-hand-side of (
3
). However, so far, we have not found a way of computing the limit using such expressions if
\ell>2
. Thus, finding a way of computing the limit in (
3
) beyond the aforementioned cases remains a ground for investigations. Of course, searching for formulas with flavor similar to (
1
) for more general tensor products may also be an interesting topic of investigation, leading to purely combinatorial statements in the spirit of
2.3.3
.

). We illustrate this description in special cases in

. A subcase of
4.2.1
, combined with
3.5.2
and (

), leads to a proof of

in

. The proof of

, while the proof of

2.
Partitions and Tableaux

Throughout the paper, let
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Q}
, and
\mathbb{Z}
denote the sets of complex, real, rational, and integer numbers, respectively. Let also
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq m},\mathbb{Z}_{<m}
, etc., denote the obvious subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
. Given
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
[a,b]
be the corresponding interval
[a,b]=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq a}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\leq b}
.
We denote by
\Sigma_{n}
the symmetric group seen as permutation of
[1,n]
. The cardinality of a set
A
will be denoted by
|A|
.
The symbol
\cong
means “isomorphic to”. We shall use the symbol
\diamond
to mark the end of remarks, examples, and statements of results whose proofs are postponed. The symbol ∎ will mark the end of proofs as well as of statements whose proofs are omitted.

2.1.
Partitions

Given
\boldsymbol{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

(2.1.1)

|\boldsymbol{m}|_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}m_{i}\ \ \text{for}\ \ 0\leq j\leq l,\quad|\boldsymbol{m}|=|\boldsymbol{m}|_{l},

and

(2.1.2)

k\pm\boldsymbol{m}=(k\pm m_{1},\dots,k\pm m_{l}).

Let

\mathcal{S}^{l}(m)=\{\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{l}:|\boldsymbol{m}|=m\}.

By a partition of
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
of length at most
l
we mean an element
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{S}^{l}(m)
such that

m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq\cdots\geq m_{l}

The numbers
m_{i}
with
m_{i}\neq 0
will be referred to as the parts of
\boldsymbol{m}
. We denote the set of all such elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{l}
by
\mathcal{P}^{l}(m)
and identify
\mathcal{P}^{l-1}(m)
with the subset of
\mathcal{P}^{l}(m)
consisting of elements
\boldsymbol{m}
such that
m_{l}=0
. Under this identification, one easily sees

(2.1.3)

l\geq m\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathcal{P}^{l}(m)=\mathcal{P}^{k}(m)\ \ \text{for all}\ \ k\geq l.

We also set

\mathcal{P}(m)=\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\mathcal{P}^{l}(m)=\mathcal{P}^{m}(m),\quad\mathcal{P}^{l}=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{P}^{l}(m),\quad\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{P}(m),

and refer to the elements of
\mathcal{P}(m)
as the partitions of
m
and to elements of
\mathcal{P}
as partitions. We also let

(2.1.4)

\mathcal{P}^{=l}(m)=\mathcal{P}^{l}(m)\setminus\mathcal{P}^{l-1}(m)

denote the set of partitions of
m
with exactly
l
parts, and similarly define
\mathcal{P}^{=l}
. If
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{P}^{=l}
we say
\boldsymbol{m}
has length
\ell(\boldsymbol{m})=l
.

Given
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{P}^{l}(m)
, let

\mathscr{P}(\boldsymbol{m})=\{m_{j}:1\leq j\leq l,m_{j}\neq 0\}

be the set consisting of the parts of
\boldsymbol{m}
. Suppose

\mathscr{P}(\boldsymbol{m})=\{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{s}\}\ \ \text{with}\ \ k_{1}>k_{2}>\cdots>k_{s}

and let
r_{j}
be the multiplicity of
k_{j}
as a part of
\boldsymbol{m}
. In that case, we shall also use the following notation:

(2.1.5)

\boldsymbol{m}=(k_{1}^{r_{1}},k_{2}^{r_{2}},\cdots,k_{s}^{r_{s}}).

Consider the natural action of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{l}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{l}
, i.e.,

\sigma(m_{1},\dots,m_{l})=(m_{\sigma(1)},\dots,m_{\sigma(l)})

and, given
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}_{\geq 0}
, denote by
\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}
the unique element of the orbit
\Sigma_{l}\boldsymbol{m}
which is a partition of
|\boldsymbol{m}|
. So, by definition

(2.1.6)

\{\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}\}=\mathcal{P}^{l}\cap\Sigma_{l}\boldsymbol{m}.

Given
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{b}(m)
be the subset of
\mathcal{P}(m)
of partitions whose parts are bounded by
b
,
\mathcal{P}^{l}_{b}(m)=\mathcal{P}^{l}(m)\cap\mathcal{P}_{b}(m)
, and similarly define
\mathcal{P}^{=l}_{b}(m)
, and so on. Set also
\rho(m)=|\mathcal{P}(m)|
, and similarly define
\rho^{l}(m),\rho_{b}(m)
, and
\rho^{l}_{b}(m)
. We set

\rho(m)=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ m\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

and similarly in the case of
\rho^{l}(m)
, etc.. For
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{P}^{l}_{b}(m)
, define

(2.1.7)

(b,\boldsymbol{m})=(b,m_{1},\cdots,m_{l})\in\mathcal{P}^{l+1}_{b}(m+b)

and

(2.1.8)

\boldsymbol{m}^{-}=(m_{1}-m_{2},m_{2}-m_{3},\dots,m_{l-1}-m_{l},m_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{l}.

Finally, we will also need a certain set of multipartitions defined as follows. Given
\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}_{\geq 0}
, set

(2.1.9)

\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)=\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{S}^{l}(m)}\mathcal{P}_{b_{1}}(m_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{P}_{b_{l}}(m_{l}),\quad\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{\boldsymbol{a}}(m)=\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{S}^{l}(m)}\mathcal{P}_{b_{1}}^{a_{1}}(m_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{P}_{b_{l}}^{a_{l}}(m_{l}),

\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)=|\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)|
, and
\rho^{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)=|\mathcal{P}^{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)|
. Thus,

(2.1.10)

\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{S}^{l}(m)}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\rho_{b_{j}}(m_{j})\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{\boldsymbol{a}}(m)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{S}^{l}(m)}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\rho_{b_{j}}^{a_{j}}(m_{j}).

Note

(2.1.11)

b_{j}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)=\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}^{\prime}}(m)

where
\boldsymbol{b}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l-1}
is obtained from
\boldsymbol{b}
by deleting the
j
-th entry. Also,

(2.1.12)

\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}_{>0}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}}(1)=l.

2.2.
A Tableaux Counting Problem

We identify a partition
\boldsymbol{m}
with the Young diagram whose
r
-th row has
m_{r}
boxes. We denote this diagram by
D_{\boldsymbol{m}}
and we say it has shape
\boldsymbol{m}
, as usual. By the box in position
(r,c)
we mean the
c
-th box in the
r
-th row of the diagram. In other words, we identify
D_{\boldsymbol{m}}
with the following subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
:

D_{\boldsymbol{m}}=\{(r,c)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:1\leq c\leq m_{r},1\leq r\leq\ell(\boldsymbol{m})\}.

The partition with no parts is associated to the empty diagram
D_{\emptyset}
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
\hat{I}=[0,n]
, and, given
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, write

(2.2.1)

a\equiv b\ \ \text{if}\ \ a-b\in(n+1)\mathbb{Z}.

By an extended tableau of rank
n
and shape
\boldsymbol{m}
we mean a function

T:D_{\boldsymbol{m}}\to\hat{I}.

As usual, this can be described in terms of boxes by saying that
T
is a Young diagram of shape
\boldsymbol{m}
whose boxes have been filled in with elements of
\hat{I}
. If a given box has been filled in with
i\in\hat{I}
, we shall say that box has content
i
. Henceforth, we refer to an extended tableau of rank
n
simply by a tableau.
We denote by
{\rm Tab}
the set of all tableaux and by
{\rm Tab}_{\boldsymbol{m}}
the subset of tableaux of shape
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{P}
. We also consider a tableau associated to the partition having no parts, which is denoted by
T_{\emptyset}
.

Consider the function

(2.2.2)

\gamma:{\rm Tab}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n+1},\quad T\mapsto\gamma(T)=(\gamma_{0}(T),\dots,\gamma_{n}(T))

where
\gamma_{i}(T),i\in\hat{I}
, is the number of boxes in
T
whose content is
i
. We think of
\gamma(T)
as the “content character” of
T
.
Note

T\in{\rm Tab}_{\boldsymbol{m}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{P}(|\gamma(T)|).

Thus,

\gamma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\subseteq\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{P}(|\boldsymbol{\eta}|)}{\rm Tab}_{\boldsymbol{m}}\quad\text{for all}\quad\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}.

A natural combinatorial problem at this point is to obtain methods for counting the number of tableaux of a given character, i.e., for computing

|\gamma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})|\ \ \text{for}\ \ \boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}.

There are certainly several purely combinatorial approaches for performing this counting in terms of sets of (multi)partitions. Our goal, however, is to explore the connection of such kind of problem with representation theory of affine Kac-Moody algebras, so we will not pursue this direction here. More precisely, we will present a formula, in terms of multipartitions and whose proof arises from representation theoretic arguments, for computing

|\gamma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\cap\mathbb{W}_{i}|

where
\mathbb{W}_{i}
is the subset of
{\rm Tab}
consisting of regular
i
-charged tableaux satisfying a couple of extra conditions, for each
i\in\hat{I}
. Since these conditions originally arose in
[
15
, Lemma 2.3]
, we shall refer to such tableaux as Misra-Wilson tableaux of charge
i
or, simply, MW
i
-tableaux. The remainder of this section is dedicated to reviewing the definition of such tableaux. The set of regular
i
-charged tableaux was used as a model for the affine
i
-th fundamental highest-weight crystal of type
A_{n}^{(1)}
in
[
11
,
14
]
. We follow closely the review made in
[
15
]
, with modified notation.

A partition
\boldsymbol{m}
is
n
-regular (or simply regular if
n
is clear from the context) if each part of
\boldsymbol{m}
repeats at most
n
times. In the spirit of (
2.1.5
), this can be written as

(2.2.3)

\boldsymbol{m}=(k_{1}^{r_{1}},k_{2}^{r_{2}},\dots,k_{s}^{r_{s}})\ \ \text{with}\ \ r_{i}\leq n\ \ \text{for all}\ \ 1\leq i\leq s.

A tableau with regular shape will be said a regular tableau. In such a tableau, there are at most
n
rows having any given number of boxes. Let us denote by
{\rm Rab}
the set of all regular tableaux and set
{\rm Rab}_{\boldsymbol{m}}={\rm Rab}\cap{\rm Tab}_{\boldsymbol{m}}
. A nonempty tableau
T
is said to have charge
i\in\hat{I}
if

(2.2.4)

T(r,c)\equiv c-r+i

for all possible values of
(r,c)
. The empty tableaux will be regarded as an
i
-charged tableau for all
i\in\hat{I}
. Otherwise, there exists exactly one
i
-charged tableau for each shape, which we denote by
T_{\boldsymbol{m},i}
(see
[
15
, Figure 1]
). Thus, we have injective maps

(2.2.5)

\kappa_{i}:\mathcal{P}\to{\rm Tab},\ \ \boldsymbol{m}\mapsto T_{\boldsymbol{m},i}\quad\text{for}\quad i\in\hat{I}.

The image of
\kappa_{i}
, i.e., the set of all
i
-charged tableaux, will be denoted by
{\rm Tab}^{i}
and we also set
{\rm Rab}^{i}={\rm Tab}^{i}\cap{\rm Rab}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{i}
denote the subset of
\mathcal{P}
satisfying the following condition:

(2.2.6)

\boldsymbol{m}=(k_{1}^{r_{1}},k_{2}^{r_{2}},\dots,k_{s}^{r_{s}})\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad k_{l}+i\equiv r_{l}+2\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}r_{j}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ 1\leq l<s.

The original phrasing of the above in
[
15
, Lemma 2.3]
was

\boldsymbol{m}=(k_{1}^{r_{1}},k_{2}^{r_{2}},\dots,k_{s}^{r_{s}})\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad r_{1}\equiv i+k_{1}\ \ \text{and}\ \ k_{l+1}-k_{l}\equiv r_{l}+r_{l+1}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ 1\leq l<s,

which is easily seen to be equivalent to (
2.2.6
).
Finally, the set of MW
i
-tableaux is

(2.2.7)

\mathbb{W}_{i}:=\kappa_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i})\cap{\rm Rab}^{i}.

2.3.
A Counting Matching

Let
\hat{I}=[0,n]
as in the previous section and denote by
\mathbb{P}_{i}^{+},i\in\hat{I}
, the subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{\geq 0}
consisting of the elements
\boldsymbol{\eta}
satisfying

(2.3.1)

\eta^{\prime}_{r}:=\delta_{0,r}+\delta_{i,r}-2\eta_{r}+\eta_{r-1}+\eta_{r+1}\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad r\in\hat{I}.

Here, for
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have set
\eta_{l}=\eta_{r}
if
l\equiv r\in\hat{I}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{e}_{j},j\in\hat{I}
, be the element
(0,\dots,0,1,0\dots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
where
1
is in the entry
j+1
. The proof of
[
15
, Lemma 2.4]
shows

(2.3.2)

\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{P}_{i}^{+}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{e}_{j}+\boldsymbol{e}_{k}\ \ \text{for some}\ \ j,k\in\hat{I}\ \ \text{such that}\ \ j+k\equiv i.

Example 2.3.1
.

For
n=2
, it follows that

\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}^{+}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{\eta}=\eta_{0}(1,1,1)-\epsilon\,\boldsymbol{e}_{2}\ \ \text{with}\ \ \epsilon\in\{0,1\}.

Indeed, if
\epsilon=0
,
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\prime}=(1,1,0)
and if
\epsilon=1
,
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\prime}=(0,0,2)
. Note the former corresponds to
j=0,k=1
in (
2.3.2
), while the latter corresponds to
j=k=2
. No other pair
(j,k)
satisfies
j+k\equiv 1
, so these are the only cases.

\diamond

Set

(2.3.3)

\tau_{i}^{n}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=|\gamma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\cap\mathbb{W}_{i}|.

Let us prepare the notation for presenting a matching of the counting function
\tau^{n}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}),\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{P}^{+}_{i}
, with the counting of the union of certain sets of the form
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)
.
Given
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, recall (
2.2.1
) and let
{\rm res}(\boldsymbol{m})\in\hat{I}
be the element satisfying

(2.3.4)

{\rm res}(\boldsymbol{m})+|\boldsymbol{m}|\equiv 0.

Given

(2.3.5)

\boldsymbol{a}(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p})=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\quad\text{with}\quad a_{i}=2p_{i}+m_{i},

Equivalently,

Given

3.
Tensor Product Decompositions

3.1.
Finite Type Cartan Data

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simply laced finite dimensional simple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with a fixed triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}
. Denote by
R
,
R^{+}
, and
\Delta
the sets of roots, positive roots and simple roots, respectively. We let
I
denote the set of vertices of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
and, given
i\in I
, let
\alpha_{i}
and
\omega_{i}
, denote the corresponding simple root and fundamental weight, respectively.
Fix a Chevalley basis
\{x_{\alpha}^{\pm},h_{i}:\alpha\in R^{+},i\in I\}
and set
h_{\alpha}=[x_{\alpha}^{+},x_{\alpha}^{-}]
. In particular,

h_{i}=[x_{\alpha_{i}}^{+},x_{\alpha_{i}}^{-}]\quad\text{for all}\quad i\in I.

Let
P
and
Q
denote the weight and root lattices of
\mathfrak{g}
, respectively, while
P^{+}
and
Q^{+}
are the submonoids generated by the fundamental weights and the simple roots, respectively. The highest root of
\mathfrak{g}
will be denoted by
\theta
. We normalize the corresponding symmetric bilinear form
(\ ,\ )
such that
(\alpha,\alpha)=2
for
\alpha\in R
.
Consider also the linear operator
s_{\alpha}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
defined by

\displaystyle s_{\alpha}\lambda=\lambda-(\lambda,\alpha)\alpha=\lambda-\lambda(h_{\alpha})\alpha,\quad\alpha\in R^{+},\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},

known as the reflection associated to
\alpha
.
We often simplify notation and write
x_{i}^{\pm}=x_{\alpha_{i}}^{\pm},s_{i}=s_{\alpha_{i}},i\in I
.
Let
\mathcal{W}
be the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is a Coxeter group generated by the simple reflections
s_{i},i\in I
. In particular,
s_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{W}
for all
\alpha\in R^{+}
.
Recall
\mathcal{W}
has a unique longest element, which we denote by
w_{o}
.

3.2.
Affine Cartan Data

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
over
\mathbb{C}
, denote by
\mathfrak{a}[t]
the associated current algebra, i.e.,
\mathfrak{a}[t]=\mathfrak{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]
with bracket
[x\otimes f,y\otimes g]=[x,y]\otimes(fg)
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{a}
and
f,g\in\mathbb{C}[t]
. Set also
\mathfrak{a}[t]_{+}=\mathfrak{a}\otimes t\mathbb{C}[t]
. The affine Kac-Moody algebra
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
associated to
\mathfrak{g}
is the vector space
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}c\oplus\mathbb{C}d
with bracket given by

[x\otimes t^{r},y\otimes t^{s}]=[x,y]\otimes t^{r+s}+r\ \delta_{r,-s}\ (x,y)\ c,\quad[c,\hat{\mathfrak{g}}]=\{0\},\quad\text{and}\quad[d,x\otimes t^{r}]=r\ x\otimes t^{r}

for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We identify
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}[t]
with the obvious subalgebras of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Set

\hat{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{C}c\oplus\mathbb{C}d,\quad\hat{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}[t]_{+},\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\mathfrak{b}}=\hat{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}\oplus\hat{\mathfrak{h}}.

Recall
\mathfrak{g}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}c\oplus\mathbb{C}d
is a parabolic subalgebra of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
containing
\hat{\mathfrak{b}}
. Identify
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with the subspace
\{\lambda\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}:\lambda(c)=\lambda(d)=0\}
and let
\Lambda_{0},\delta\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
be defined by

\Lambda_{0}(d)=0=\Lambda_{0}(\mathfrak{h}),\quad\Lambda_{0}(c)=1,\quad\delta(c)=0=\delta(\mathfrak{h}),\quad\delta(d)=1.

Also, set
\hat{I}=I\sqcup\{0\},\alpha_{0}=\delta-\theta,h_{0}=c-h_{\theta}
, and, for
i\in I
, set

(3.2.1)

\Lambda_{i}=\omega_{i}+\omega_{i}(h_{\theta})\Lambda_{0}.

Then,
\Lambda_{i}(h_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
for all
i,j\in\hat{I},\{h_{i}:i\in\hat{I}\}\cup\{d\}
is a basis of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
,
\hat{\Delta}=\{\alpha_{i}:i\in\hat{I}\}
is the set of simple roots for
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
\hat{R}^{+}=R^{+}\cup\{\alpha+r\delta:\alpha\in R\cup\{0\},r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}
is the set of positive roots. The affine root lattice
\hat{Q}
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of
\hat{\Delta}
, while
\hat{Q}^{+}
is the corresponding submonoid.
We consider the usual partial order on
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
defined by

\mu\leq\lambda\quad\text{if}\quad\lambda-\mu\in\hat{Q}^{+}.

We also set

(3.2.2)

\lambda\equiv\mu\quad\text{if}\quad\lambda-\mu\in\mathbb{C}\delta.

The symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
can be extended to one on
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
given by

(3.2.3)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{i},\Lambda_{0})=(\alpha_{i},\delta)=(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0})=(\delta,\delta)=0,\quad\text{for}\quad i\in I,\quad\text{and}\quad(\Lambda_{0},\delta)=1.

Set
\hat{P}=\{\lambda\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}:\lambda(h_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }i\in\hat{I}\}
,
\hat{P}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\hat{P}:\lambda(h_{i})\geq 0\text{ for all }i\in\hat{I}\}
, and note we have a direct sum of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules

(3.2.4)

\hat{P}=P\oplus\mathbb{Z}\Lambda_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}\delta

Given
\lambda\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, the number
\lambda(c)
is called the level of
\lambda
and we shall refer to
\lambda(d)
as the degree of
\lambda
. Denote by
\bar{\lambda}\in P
the projection of
\lambda
on the first summand of the decomposition (
3.2.4
). Thus,

(3.2.5)

\lambda=\bar{\lambda}+\lambda(c)\Lambda_{0}+\lambda(d)\delta

is the decomposition of
\lambda
according to (
3.2.4
). Since
P\subseteq\mathbb{Q}Q
, it follows from (
3.2.3
) that

(3.2.6)

(\lambda,\lambda)=(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\lambda})+2\lambda(c)\lambda(d),\quad\lambda(c)=(\lambda,\delta),\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda(d)=(\lambda,\Lambda_{0}).

The affine simple reflections
s_{i},i\in\hat{I}
, can be defined as the linear endomorphism on
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
such that

s_{i}\lambda=\lambda-\lambda(h_{i})\alpha_{i}=\lambda-(\lambda,\alpha_{i})\alpha_{i}\quad\text{for all}\quad\lambda\in\hat{P}.

For
i\neq 0
, the restriction of
s_{i}
to
\mathfrak{h}
coincides with the previously defined simple reflections. Let

\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
denote the affine Weyl group, which is generated by the simple reflections
s_{i},i\in\hat{I}
. In particular,
\mathcal{W}
is naturally a subgroup of
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
and

(w\lambda,w\mu)=(\lambda,\mu)\quad\text{for all}\quad w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}},\lambda,\mu\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

Moreover, since
\alpha_{i}(c)=0
for all
i\in\hat{I}
, it follows that

(w\lambda)(c)=\lambda(c)\quad\text{for all}\quad w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}},\lambda\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

These facts, together with (
3.2.6
), imply

(3.2.7)

\displaystyle\mu\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\lambda\quad\Rightarrow\quad 2\lambda(c)(\mu(d)-\lambda(d))=(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\lambda})-(\bar{\mu},\bar{\mu}).

Indeed,

\displaystyle(\bar{\mu},\bar{\mu})+2\lambda(c)\mu(d)=(\bar{\mu},\bar{\mu})+2\mu(c)\mu(d)=(\mu,\mu)=(\lambda,\lambda)=(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\lambda})+2\lambda(c)\lambda(d).

It is also well-known that

(3.2.8)

\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\hat{P}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{C}\delta\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\xi\in\hat{P}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \xi(c)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},

and, furthermore,

(3.2.9)

\#(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\xi\cap\hat{P}^{+})\leq 1\quad\text{for all}\quad\xi\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

We denote by
{\rm soc}(\xi)
the unique element of
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\xi\cap\hat{P}^{+}
when it exists. It follows from (
3.2.7
) that

(3.2.10)

{\rm soc}(\xi)(d)=\frac{(\xi,\xi)-(\overline{{\rm soc}(\xi)},\overline{{\rm soc}(\xi)})}{2\xi(c)}=\xi(d)+\frac{(\bar{\xi},\bar{\xi})-(\overline{{\rm soc}(\xi)},\overline{{\rm soc}(\xi)})}{2\xi(c)}.

Note (
3.2.9
) implies

(3.2.11)

w\xi\equiv\lambda\in\hat{P}^{+}\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\rm soc}(\xi)\equiv\lambda.

In that case
\overline{{\rm soc}(\xi)}=\bar{\lambda}
and the degree of
{\rm soc}(\xi)
is given by (
3.2.10
). Thus, the task of finding
{\rm soc}(\xi)
reduces to that of finding
\overline{{\rm soc}(\xi)}
.

It will be convenient to work with the alternative realization of
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
as the semidirect product
\tau(Q)\ltimes\mathcal{W}
, where
\tau
is the map defined on
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
which associates to each
\alpha\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
the linear map
t_{\alpha}:\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\to\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
given by

\displaystyle t_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\lambda+\lambda(c)\alpha-\left((\lambda,\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha,\alpha)\lambda(c)\right)\delta,

or, equivalently,

(3.2.12)

\displaystyle t_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{0})=\Lambda_{0}+\alpha-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha,\alpha)\delta,\qquad t_{\alpha}(\omega_{i})=\omega_{i}-(\omega_{i},\alpha)\delta,\quad t_{\alpha}(\delta)=\delta,\qquad\alpha\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},i\in I.

The isomorphism
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\to\tau(Q^{\vee})\ltimes\mathcal{W}
is given by
s_{i}\mapsto t_{0}s_{i}
for
i\in I
and
s_{0}\mapsto t_{\theta}s_{\theta}
.
We keep the notation
s_{i}
for the image of
s_{i}
under this isomorphism.

3.3.
Basic Representation Theory Background

If
V
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module and
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, the corresponding weight space is

V_{\mu}=\{v\in V:hv=\mu(h)v\ \forall\ h\in\mathfrak{h}\}.

We shall only consider finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. In particular,

V=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P}V_{\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad\dim V_{\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}.

The set
{\rm wt}(V)=\{\mu\in P:V_{\mu}\neq 0\}
is the set of weights of
V
.
The character of
V
, denoted by
{\rm ch}V
, is the function
{\rm ch}V:P\to\mathbb{Z},\mu\mapsto\dim V_{\mu}
. It can be naturally identified with the element

\sum_{\mu\in P}\dim V_{\mu}\ e^{\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}[P],

where
e^{\mu}
denotes the element corresponding to
\mu
in the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[P]
. It turns out
w\,{\rm ch}V={\rm ch}V
, i.e.,
\dim V_{\mu}=\dim V_{w\mu}
, for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
.
We denote by
V(\lambda),\lambda\in P^{+}
, a simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module with highest
\lambda
.

These notions can be defined for a
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module in the category of integral modules in category
\mathcal{O}
. In particular, given such
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module and
\mu\in\hat{P}^{+}
, we let
V_{\mu}
denote the associated weight space, we denote by
{\rm ch}V
the associated character, the characters are
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
-invariant, the isomorphism classes of simple modules are in bijection with
\hat{P}^{+}
, and we denote by
V(\lambda),\lambda\in\hat{P}^{+}
, a simple module with highest-weight
\lambda
.

Given a
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
V
such that

V\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\hat{P}^{+}}V(\lambda)^{\oplus m_{\lambda}}\quad\text{for some}\quad m_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

we set

[V:V(\lambda)]=m_{\lambda}.

The number
[V:V(\lambda)]
is often called the outer multiplicity of
\lambda
in
V
, as opposed to the “inner” multiplicity which is
\dim V_{\lambda}
.

The current algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t]
has a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-grading in the obvious way, which induces a grading on
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
in such a way that an element

\displaystyle(x_{1}\otimes t_{r_{1}})\cdots(x_{s}\otimes t_{r_{s}}),\quad x_{j}\in\mathfrak{g},r_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},1\leq j\leq s,

has degree
r_{1}+\cdots+r_{s}
. Denote by
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])_{k}
the homogeneous component of degree
k
and recall that it is a
\mathfrak{g}
–module for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. A finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V
admitting a compatible graded action of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
. That is,

\displaystyle V=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}V[k]\quad\text{and}\quad(x\otimes t^{r})V[k]\subseteq V[k+r]\ \ \text{for all}\ \ x\in\mathfrak{g},r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},k\in\mathbb{Z}.

In particular, each graded piece
V[k]
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. If
\dim V[k]<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the graded character as a formal sum

\displaystyle{\rm ch}_{\rm gr}V=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm ch}V[k]q^{k}\in P[\mathbb{Z}][[q]].

Given a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded space
V
, let
\tau_{p}V
be the graded space whose
r
-th graded piece is
V[r-p]
. So

{\rm ch}_{\rm gr}\tau_{p}V=q^{p}{\rm ch}_{\rm gr}V.

Given a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
, we shall regard it as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module by setting

(3.3.1)

V[0]=V\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{g}[t]_{+}V=0.

3.4.
Demazure Modules and Flags

The
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
-invariance of
{\rm ch}V(\Lambda)
implies
\dim V(\Lambda)_{w\Lambda}=1
for all
\Lambda\in\hat{P}^{+},w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
. The Demazure module
V_{w}(\Lambda)
associated to
\Lambda\in\hat{P}^{+}
and
w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
is the
\hat{\mathfrak{b}}
-submodule of
V(\Lambda)
generated by
V(\Lambda)_{w\Lambda}
. It is usually denoted by
V_{w}(\lambda)
.
Alternatively, given
\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\hat{P}^{+}
, letting
\Lambda
be the unique element in
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\xi\cap\hat{P}^{+}
and
w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
be such that
w\Lambda=\xi
, we have

V_{w}(\Lambda)=U(\hat{\mathfrak{b}})V(\Lambda)_{\xi}.

This motivates the following alternative notation:

(3.4.1)

D(\xi):=V_{w}(\Lambda).

Since
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\subseteq\hat{\mathfrak{b}}
,
D(\xi)
has a decompositon as a sum of affine weight spaces. Recall the definition of
{\rm soc}(\xi)
in the line preceding (
3.2.10
). It follows from the definitions that

{\rm soc}(\xi)=\Lambda\quad\text{and}\quad D(\xi)_{{\rm soc}(\xi)}=V(\Lambda)_{\Lambda}.

Let
\preccurlyeq
denote the Bruhat order in
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
. It is well-known (see
[
12
, Lemmas 1.3.20 and 8.3.3]
) that
(\widehat{\mathcal{W}},\preceq)
is a directed poset,

\displaystyle w\preceq w^{\prime}\quad\Rightarrow\quad V_{w}(\Lambda)\subseteq V_{w^{\prime}}(\Lambda),

(3.4.2)

and

\displaystyle V(\Lambda)=\bigcup_{w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}}V_{w}(\Lambda).

Since every element of
P
is
\mathcal{W}
-conjugate to an element of
-P^{+}
, one easily sees that, for every
w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
, there exists
w^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}
such that

\ell(w^{\prime}w)=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})\qquad\text{and}\qquad w^{\prime}w\Lambda(h_{i})\leq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad i\in I.

Thus, if we set

\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{\Lambda}^{-}=\left\{w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}:\overline{w\Lambda}\in-P^{+}\right\},

we get

(3.4.3)

V(\Lambda)=\bigcup_{w\in{\widehat{\mathcal{W}}}_{\Lambda}^{-}}V_{w}(\Lambda).

The Demazure modules of the form
V_{w}(\Lambda)
with
w\in{\widehat{\mathcal{W}}}_{\Lambda}^{-}
are usually called
\mathfrak{g}
-stable Demazure module due to the following well-known lemma.

Lemma 3.4.1
.

Let
\Lambda\in\hat{P}^{+}
and
w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

V_{w}(\Lambda)
is a
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-submodule of
V(\Lambda)
.

(ii)

V_{w}(\Lambda)
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
V(\Lambda)
.

(iii)

\mathfrak{n}^{-}V_{w}(\Lambda)_{w\Lambda}=0
.

(iv)

w\Lambda(h_{i})\leq 0
for all
i\in I
.

Moreover, in that case,
V_{w}(\Lambda)
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module and
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+})V(\Lambda)_{w\Lambda}
is isomorphic to
V(\lambda)
as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, where
\lambda=-w_{o}(\overline{w\Lambda})
.∎

We will work only with
\mathfrak{g}
-stable Demazure modules. If
D(\xi)
is such a module, then
\xi=\ell\Lambda_{0}+w_{o}\lambda+r\delta
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\lambda\in P^{+},r\in\mathbb{C}
, and we use the following notation which emphasizes the data
\ell,\lambda,r
:

D(\ell,\lambda,r)=D(\ell\Lambda_{0}+w_{o}\lambda+r\delta)\quad\text{and}\quad D(\ell,\lambda)=D(\ell,\lambda,0).

It is also well-known that the socle of
D(\xi)
when regarded as a
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module is the
\mathfrak{g}
-module

U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})D(\xi)_{{\rm soc}(\xi)}=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})V({\rm soc}(\xi))_{{\rm soc}(\xi)},

which is isomorphic to
V\left(\overline{{\rm soc}(\xi)}\right)
as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. This explains our choice of notation for
{\rm soc}(\xi)
.

Recall that a
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module
V
admits a (
\mathfrak{g}
-stable) Demazure flag if there exist
l>0,\xi_{j}\in\hat{P}
such that
D(\xi_{j})
is
\mathfrak{g}
-stable for
j\in[1,l]
, and a sequence of inclusions

(3.4.4)

0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots\subset V_{l-1}\subset V_{l}=V\quad\text{with}\quad V_{j}/V_{j-1}\cong D(\xi_{j})\ \forall\ 1\leq j\leq l.

If
\xi_{j}(c)=\ell
for some
\ell
and all
j
, such a sequence is said to be a level-
\ell
Demazure flag for
V
. We shall only consider flags of a fixed level. Let
\mathbb{V}
be a level-
\ell
Demazure flag for
V
and, given a Demazure module
D
, define the multiplicity of
D
in
\mathbb{V}
by

[\mathbb{V}:D]=\#\{1\leq j\leq l:V_{j}/V_{j-1}\cong D\}.

As observed in
[
7
, Section 2.8]
, if
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
is another level-
\ell
Demazure flag for
V
, then
[\mathbb{V}^{\prime}:D]=[\mathbb{V}:D]
. Hence, by abuse of language, we shift the notation from
[\mathbb{V}:D]
to
[V:D]_{\ell}
. Also following
[
7
]
, we consider the generating polynomial

(3.4.5)

[V:D]_{\ell}(q)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\ [V:\tau_{m}D]_{\ell}\ q^{m}\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}].

When no confusion arises, we simplify notation and drop the subindex
\ell
from the brackets above.

3.5.
Outer Multiplicities from Demazure Flags

Given
\Lambda\in\hat{P}^{+}
, consider

\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{\Lambda}^{-}=\left\{w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}:\overline{w\Lambda}\in-P^{+}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad P^{-}_{\Lambda}=\{w\Lambda:w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}^{-}_{\Lambda}\}\subseteq\hat{P}.

Since
w\delta=\delta
for all
w\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
,

(3.5.1)

P^{-}_{\Lambda+s\delta}=P^{-}_{\Lambda}+s\delta\quad\text{for all}\quad\Lambda\in\hat{P}^{+},s\in\mathbb{C}.

The main result of
[
10
]
says that, for all
\Lambda\in\hat{P}^{+}
such that
\Lambda(c)=\ell
, we have

(3.5.2)

[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda):V(\xi)]=\sum_{\eta\in P^{-}_{\xi}}\max_{\gamma\in P^{-}_{\Lambda}}\ [D(\gamma):D(\eta)]\quad\text{for all}\quad\xi\in\hat{P}^{+},\xi(c)=\ell+1.

It will be convenient to rephrase the above expression as follows. Given
\Phi\in\hat{P}^{+}
, an element of
P^{-}_{\Phi}
can be written as
\Phi(d)\Lambda_{0}+w_{o}\mu+r\delta
for some
\mu\in P^{+}
and
r\in\mathbb{C}
. According to (
3.2.10
), we have

\Phi(d)=r+\frac{(w_{o}\mu,w_{o}\mu)-(\bar{\Phi},\bar{\Phi})}{2\Phi(c)}.

Thus, setting

(3.5.3)

r(\mu,\Phi)=\Phi(d)-\frac{(\mu,\mu)-(\bar{\Phi},\bar{\Phi})}{2\Phi(c)}\quad\text{for}\quad\mu\in P^{+},\Phi\in\hat{P}^{+},

and

(3.5.4)

\Gamma_{\Phi}=\left\{\mu\in P^{+}:\Phi(d)\Lambda_{0}+w_{o}\mu+r(\mu,\Phi)\delta\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\Phi\right\},

(
3.5.2
) becomes

(3.5.5)

[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda):V(\xi)]=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma_{\xi}}\max_{\nu\in\Gamma_{\Lambda}}\ [D(\ell,\nu,r(\nu,\Lambda)):D(\ell+1,\mu,r(\mu,\xi))]

for all
\xi\in\hat{P}^{+},\xi(c)=\ell+1
.

The following was proved in
[
10
, Proposition 2.6.1]
in the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and we shall extend it for type
A
in general. The proof is an application of (
3.5.2
) together with
5.1.2
below, which is obtained by studying certain “canonical” reduced expressions for elements of
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
. Henceforth, we use the notation

|\lambda|=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda(h_{i})\quad\text{for}\quad\lambda\in P.

Theorem 3.5.1
.

Assume
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n}
, let
\Lambda\in\hat{P}^{+}
, and set
\lambda=\bar{\Lambda},\ell=\Lambda(c),s=\Lambda(d)
. Then, for all
\xi\in\hat{P}^{+}
with
\xi(c)=\ell+1
, we have

\displaystyle[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda):V(\xi)]=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma_{\xi}}\ \lim_{k\to\infty}\ [D(\ell,\lambda+\ell k\theta):D(\ell+1,\mu,r(\mu,\xi)-s+k(|\lambda|+\ell k)].

\diamond

In the case
\ell=1
, we can make the right-hand side of the expression in
3.5.1
more explicitly as follows. Given
\mu\in P^{+}
, write

(3.5.6)

-w_{o}\mu=2\mu_{0}+\mu_{1}\ \ \text{with}\ \ \mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in P^{+},\ \mu_{1}(h_{i})\leq 1\ \forall\ i\in I,

and consider

(3.5.7)

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}=(\mu_{0}(h_{1}),\dots,\mu_{0}(h_{n}))\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}.

Also, given
i\in\hat{I}
and
\xi\in\hat{P}^{+}
, set

(3.5.8)

f_{i,\xi}(\mu)=\frac{1}{4}(2(\omega_{i},\omega_{i})-(\bar{\xi},\bar{\xi})-4\xi(d)-(\mu,\mu)).

Note (cf. (
2.3.22
))

(3.5.9)

f_{i,\xi}(\mu)\geq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad(\mu,\mu)\leq 2(\omega_{i},\omega_{i})-(\bar{\xi},\bar{\xi})-4\xi(d).

The following result will be proved in
Section

5.4
.

Theorem 3.5.2
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n}
,

\displaystyle[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda_{i}):V(\xi)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma_{\xi}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}(f_{i,\xi}(\mu))\quad\text{for all}\quad i\in\hat{I},\ \xi\in\hat{P}^{+}.

\diamond

Since
\Gamma_{\xi}
is a discrete set, it follows that the summation appearing in
3.5.2
is actually finite.
In the case
n=1
, after a more explicit description of the sets
\Gamma_{\xi}
, this finite set was precisely described in
[
10
, Proposition 2.6.2 and Corollary 2.6.3]
. For higher rank, we shall address the task of describing
\Gamma_{\xi}
in a more concrete way in
Section

4.2
. In particular, setting

\eta=\Lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{i}-\xi=\sum_{l\in\hat{I}}\eta_{l}\alpha_{l}\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\eta}=(\eta_{0},\dots,\eta_{n}),

we will establish in
Section

4.4
the following connection with (
2.3.19
):

(3.5.10)

\xi\equiv\Lambda_{j}+\Lambda_{k}\quad\Rightarrow\quad[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda_{i}):V(\xi)]=\sum_{(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p})\in\Gamma_{j,k}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p})}\left(f_{i,\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\boldsymbol{a}(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p}))\right).

On the other hand, Theorem 2.1 of
[
15
]
says

(3.5.11)

[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda_{i}):V(\xi)]=\tau^{n}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}),

thus completing the proof of
2.3.3
.

Example 3.5.3
.

For
n=2
,
[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda_{1}):V(2\Lambda_{2}-\eta\delta)]
is given by the right-hand side of (
2.3.25
).
\diamond

We also recall that one can compute the outer multiplicities of
V(\Lambda_{i})\otimes V(\Lambda_{j})
by combining
3.5.2
with
[
10
, Equation (2.5.1)]
. More precisely, given
\xi\in\hat{P}^{+},\xi\leq\Lambda_{i}+\Lambda_{j}
, set

\xi^{\prime}=\sum_{k\in I}\xi(h_{k})\Lambda_{i+k},

and let
c_{k}\in\mathbb{Z},k\in I
, be defined by

\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}\\
\vdots\\
c_{n}\end{bmatrix}=C^{-1}B

where
C
is the Cartan matrix of type
A_{n}
and
B
is the column matrix whose
k
-th entry is

\delta_{i,k}+\delta_{j,k}-\xi(h_{k})-(\delta_{k,1}+\delta_{k,n})\xi(d).

Then, setting
\Lambda_{k}=\Lambda_{i}
if
k\equiv i\in\hat{I}
for

,
[

, Equation (2.5.1)]
says

(3.5.12)

[V(\Lambda_{i})\otimes V(\Lambda_{j}):V(\xi)]=[V(\Lambda_{0})\otimes V(\Lambda_{i+j}):V(\xi^{\prime}-c_{i}\delta)].

4.
Affine Weyl Group Orbits and the Proof of
2.3.3

4.1.
Socle Computations

As a first step towards obtaining a more explicit characterization of the sets
\Gamma_{\xi}
, we present a formula for
{\rm soc}(\xi)
for all
\xi\in\hat{P}
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\mu\in P
, let

{\rm soc}(\ell,\mu)={\rm soc}(\ell\Lambda_{0}+w_{o}\mu).

and set

(4.1.1)

a_{i}=-\sum_{j=i}^{n}(w_{o}\mu)(h_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1-i}\mu(h_{j})\ \ \text{for}\ \ i\in I.

In particular,

(4.1.2)

\mu\in P^{+}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}\geq 0.

In any case, there exist unique
p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},0<m_{i}\leq\ell,i\in I
, such that

(4.1.3)

\displaystyle a_{i}=p_{i}\ell+m_{i}.

Note that,
\mu\in P^{+}
if and only if (cf. (
2.3.6
))

(4.1.4)

p_{i}\geq-1\ \forall\ i\in I,\quad p_{i}=-1\ \Leftrightarrow\ a_{i}=0\Rightarrow\ m_{i}=\ell,\quad\text{and}\quad p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq\cdots\geq p_{n}.

Recall (
2.3.4
) and set

(4.1.5)

\boldsymbol{m}(\ell,\mu)=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n}),\quad\boldsymbol{p}(\ell,\mu)=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}),\quad\text{and}\quad p(\ell,\mu)={\rm res}(\boldsymbol{p}(\ell,\mu)).

To shorten notation, we henceforth denote these elements by
\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p}
, and
p
, respectively. Recall that
\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}
denotes the associated partition of
|\boldsymbol{m}|
and note

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\ell}^{=n}.

Write
\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}=(k_{1}^{s_{1}}\ k_{2}^{s_{2}}\ \cdots\ k_{l}^{s_{l}})
as in (
2.1.5
), set
k_{l+1}=0
for convenience, consider
\boldsymbol{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}_{>0}
, and recall (
2.1.1
).
We will see in
Section

4.3
that

(4.1.6)

{\rm soc}(\ell,\mu)\equiv(\ell-k_{1})\Lambda_{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}(k_{j}-k_{j+1})\Lambda_{p-|\boldsymbol{s}|_{j}}.

Here, as before, given
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we understand
\Lambda_{k}=\Lambda_{i}
if
k\equiv i\in\hat{I}
. Equivalently, letting

(4.1.7)

\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(\ell,\mu)=\overline{(\ell,\boldsymbol{m})},

writing
\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(\ell,\mu)=(m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{n+1})
, and setting
m^{\prime}_{n+2}=0
for convenience, (
4.1.6
) can be rewritten as

(4.1.8)

{\rm soc}(\ell,\mu)\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{n}(m^{\prime}_{j+1}-m^{\prime}_{j+2})\Lambda_{p-j}.

4.2.
Orbits Characterizations

We now use (
4.1.8
) to give a combinatorial characterization of the sets
\Gamma_{\xi},\xi\in\hat{P}^{+}
. Using either (
4.1.6
) or (
4.1.8
), one easily sees that

(4.2.1)

{\rm soc}(\ell,\mu)(h_{p+1})=\min\{m_{i}:i\in I\}>0,

where
p=p(\ell,\mu)
and
m_{i},i\in I
, are defined as in (
4.1.3
), and, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we understand
h_{k}=h_{i}
if
k\equiv i\in\hat{I}
. Also, writing
\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(\ell,\mu)=(m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{n+1})
as before and recalling (
2.1.8
), we have

(4.2.2)

(m^{\prime}_{1}-m^{\prime}_{2},\dots,m^{\prime}_{n}-m^{\prime}_{n+1},m^{\prime}_{n+1})=\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(\ell,\mu)^{-}.

Fix
\xi\in\hat{P}^{+}
and let

(4.2.3)

\boldsymbol{c}(\xi)=(\xi(h_{0}),\dots,\xi(h_{n}))\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\ell=\xi(c)=|\boldsymbol{c}(\xi)|.

Given
p\in\hat{I}
, let
\sigma_{p}\in\Sigma_{n+1}
be the unique element such that

(4.2.4)

\sigma_{p}(j)\equiv p+2-j\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq j\leq n+1.

Recall (
3.5.3
).
In light of (
4.1.8
) and (
4.2.2
), for
\mu\in P^{+}
, we have

(4.2.5)

\ell\Lambda_{0}+w_{o}\mu+r\delta\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\xi\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(\ell,\mu)^{-}=\sigma_{p(\ell,\mu)}\boldsymbol{c}(\xi)\ \ \text{and}\ \ r=r(\mu,\xi).

In other words,

(4.2.6)

\Gamma_{\xi}=\{\mu\in P^{+}:\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(\ell,\mu)^{-}=\sigma_{p(\ell,\mu)}\boldsymbol{c}(\xi)\}.

Note (
4.1.3
) gives rise to a bijective map

\phi:P\to[1,\ell]^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n},\quad\mu\mapsto(\boldsymbol{m}(\ell,\mu),\boldsymbol{p}(\ell,\mu)).

Let
\mathscr{P}^{+}=\phi(P^{+})
. It follows that
(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p})\in\mathscr{P}^{+}
if and only if (
4.1.4
) holds (for
\ell=2
, this coincides with the definition of
\mathscr{P}^{+}
given just before (
2.3.6
)). On the other hand, we have a map

\psi:\mathcal{P}_{\ell}^{=n}\times\hat{I}\to\hat{P}^{+},

where
\psi(\boldsymbol{m},p)
is the unique element of
\hat{P}^{+}
satisfying

\sigma_{p}\,\boldsymbol{c}(\psi(\boldsymbol{m},p))=(\ell,\boldsymbol{m})^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi(\boldsymbol{m},p)(d)=0.

Given
\xi\in\hat{P}^{+}
, set

(4.2.7)

\mathscr{C}_{\xi}=\psi^{-1}(\xi-\xi(d)\delta)\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma^{\prime}_{\xi}=\{(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p})\in\mathscr{P}^{+}:(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}},{\rm res}(\boldsymbol{p}))\in\mathscr{C}_{\xi}\}.

It now follows from (
4.2.6
) that the map

(4.2.8)

\Gamma_{\xi}\to\Gamma^{\prime}_{\xi},\quad\mu\mapsto(\boldsymbol{m}(\ell,\mu),\boldsymbol{p}(\ell,\mu))

is a bijection.
Let us now give more concrete characterizations of
\Gamma_{\xi}
and
\Gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}
on special cases.

Example 4.2.1
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},a\leq b
, and consider
\xi=a\Lambda_{j}+b\Lambda_{k}
for some
j,k\in\hat{I}
. We start with a generalization of the discussion made in the paragraph starting with (
2.3.7
).

Given
1\leq s\leq n+1
, set

\boldsymbol{m}_{a,b}(s)=((a+b)^{s-1},a^{n+1-s})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}_{a+b}.

Let also
\mathcal{O}(s)
be the orbit of
\boldsymbol{m}(s)
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
under the usual action of
\Sigma_{n}
and

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=\{s\in[1,n+1]:s\equiv j^{\prime}-k^{\prime}\text{ for all }\{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}\}=\{j,k\}\text{ such that }\xi(h_{j^{\prime}})=a\}.

Note

a<b\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=\{s\in[1,n+1]:s\equiv j-k\}

has one element, while

a=b\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=\{s\in[1,n+1]:s\equiv\pm|j-k|\},

may have two elements (cf. (
2.3.8
)). Also,

s\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{there exists unique}\ \ p\in\hat{I}\ \ \text{such that}\ \ \xi(h_{p+1})=a\ \ \text{and}\ \ \xi(h_{p+1-s})=b,

which we denote by
p(s)
. More precisely,

(4.2.9)

a<b\quad\Rightarrow\quad p(s)+1\equiv j

and
p(s)
is given by (
2.3.9
) otherwise.
We will show

(4.2.10)

\Gamma^{\prime}_{\xi}=\{(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p})\in\mathscr{P}^{+}:\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{O}(s)\text{ and }{\rm res}(\boldsymbol{p})=p(s)\text{ for some }s\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}\}.

Equivalently,

(4.2.11)

\mu\in\Gamma_{\xi}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(a+b,\mu)=((a+b)^{s},a^{n+1-s})\ \ \text{and}\ \ p(a+b,\mu)=p(s)\ \ \text{for some}\ \ s\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}.

If
\mu\in\Gamma_{\xi}
, it follows from (
4.1.6
) that
\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(a+b,\mu)
has at most two parts and, hence,
\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}(a+b,\mu)=((a+b)^{s},a^{n+1-s})
for some
1\leq s\leq n+1
. In that case, (
4.1.8
) reads

(4.2.12)

{\rm soc}(a+b,\mu)\equiv a\Lambda_{p+1}+b\Lambda_{p+1-s}\quad\text{where}\quad p=p(a+b,\mu),

from where the right-hand-side of (
4.2.11
) easily follows. The converse is now also clear.

In the case
a=b
, (
4.2.10
) can be simplified as follows. Set
\ell=a+b
, so
\xi=\ell\Lambda_{j}
for some
j\in\hat{I}
. In this case, we will show

(4.2.13)

\mu\in\Gamma_{\xi}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mu\in\ell P^{+}\ \ \text{and}\ \ j+\sum_{i\in I}(n+1-i)\frac{\mu(h_{i})}{\ell}\in(n+1)\mathbb{Z}.

Note that, in this case
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=\{n+1\}
and, hence,
\boldsymbol{m}_{a,a}(s)=(\ell^{n})
. Moreover, (
4.2.11
) becomes

(4.2.14)

(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{p})\in\Gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{m}=(\ell,\dots,\ell)\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm res}(\boldsymbol{p})+1\equiv j.

The following lemma about multipartitions will also play a role in the proof of

Lemma 5.3.1
.

Let
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}_{\geq 0}
for some
l>0
, and set
a=\max\{a_{j}:1\leq j\leq l\}
.

(a)

If
k\geq a
, then
\rho^{k-\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)\neq 0
only if
m\leq k|\boldsymbol{b}|-\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle
.

(b)

If
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
k\geq\max\{f+a,(f+\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle)/|\boldsymbol{b}|\}
, there exists a bijection
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{k-\boldsymbol{a}}(k|\boldsymbol{b}|-\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle-f)\to\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(f)
. In particular,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{k-\boldsymbol{a}}(k|\boldsymbol{b}|-\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle-f)=\rho_{\boldsymbol{b}}(f)
.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(m)
, say

\boldsymbol{m}=(\boldsymbol{m}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{m}_{l})\quad\text{with}\quad\boldsymbol{m}_{j}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-a_{j}}_{b_{j}}(m_{j})

where
m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are such that
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{l}=m
. Write

\boldsymbol{m}_{j}=(m_{j,1},\dots,m_{j,k-a_{j}})\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq j\leq l.

In particular,

|\boldsymbol{m}_{j}|=\sum_{s=1}^{k-a_{j}}m_{j,s}=m_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{s=1}^{k-a_{j}}m_{j,s}=m.

However,
m_{j,s}\leq b_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq l,1\leq s\leq k-a_{j}
and, hence,

m=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{s=1}^{k-a_{j}}m_{j,s}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{s=1}^{k-a_{j}}b_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}(k-a_{j})b_{j}=k|\boldsymbol{b}|-\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle,

thus completing the proof of (a).

For part (b), we streamline and extend the argument used for proving
[
10
, (4.3.8)]
. Recall (
2.1.2
) and (
2.1.6
) and note that, for any
a,b,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}0
, the map

(5.3.3)

\varphi_{m}^{a,b}:\mathcal{P}_{b}^{a}(m)\to\mathcal{P}_{b}^{a}(ab-m),\quad\boldsymbol{m}\mapsto\overline{b-\boldsymbol{m}}

is invertible and its inverse is
\varphi_{ab-m}^{a,b}
. The assumption
k\geq(f+\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle)/|\boldsymbol{b}|
implies

k|\boldsymbol{b}|-\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle-f\geq 0.

One then easily checks that we have a bijective map

(5.3.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}:\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{k-\boldsymbol{a}}(k|\boldsymbol{b}|-\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle-f)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{k-\boldsymbol{a}}(f)

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{m}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{m}_{l})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\varphi_{|\boldsymbol{m}_{1}|}^{k-a_{1},b_{1}}(\boldsymbol{m}_{1}),\dots,\varphi_{|\boldsymbol{m}_{l}|}^{k-a_{l},b_{l}}(\boldsymbol{m}_{l})).

Moreover, note

\varphi_{|\boldsymbol{m}_{j}|}^{k-a_{j},b_{j}}(\boldsymbol{m}_{j})\in\mathcal{P}_{b_{j}}^{k-a_{j}}((k-a_{j})b_{j}-|\boldsymbol{m}_{j}|)\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq l.

In particular,

(k-a_{j})b_{j}-|\boldsymbol{m}_{j}|\leq f\leq k-a\leq k-a_{j},

where we used that
k\geq f+a
for the second inequality. It then follows from (
2.1.3
) that

\mathcal{P}_{b_{j}}^{k-a_{j}}((k-a_{j})b_{j}-|\boldsymbol{m}_{j}|)=\mathcal{P}_{b_{j}}((k-a_{j})b_{j}-|\boldsymbol{m}_{j}|)\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq l

and, hence,

\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{k-\boldsymbol{a}}(f)=\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{b}}(f),

thus completing the proof.

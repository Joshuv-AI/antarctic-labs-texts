The prognostic significance of B7-H3 expression in patients with advanced colorectal cancer
Authors: Yan Huang, Ana Acuna-Villaorduna, Radhika Gali, Catarina Maia, Keerthi Cedona, Xingxing Zang, Xiaohua Qi, Wei Zhang, Chenxin Zhang, Juan Lin, Qiang Liu, Radhashree Maitra, Sanjay Goel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBiomarkers, particularly prognostic and immune based, are lacking in colorectal cancer (CRC). This study aimed to evaluate the expression of novel immune based biomarkers among patients with CRC to predict clinical outcomes.MethodsA CRC tumor microarray (TMA) was developed from a multiracial patient population, and expression of diagnostic markers (CK7, CK20, CDX2) and immune based (HHLA2, B7-H3, PD-L1) determined by immunohistochemistry (IHC) in a novel quantitative manner. Comprehensive clinical information was extracted. Among patients with metastatic (m) cancers, Kaplan Meier curves were plotted, and Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was performed to evaluate association with overall survival (OS).ResultsAmong 388 patients, 150 had de novo metastatic disease, 108 experienced relapses from localized cancer, and 130 patients had cured localized disease. Racially/ethnically, 70 were non-Hispanic whites (NHW), 159 were non-Hispanic blacks (NHB), 147 Hispanic, and 12 Asian. The median age was 62 years, with a female predominance (53%). Among the patients with metastatic CRC, that included those with relapses and with de novo metastatic CRC (n = 258), in a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model, left sidedness (HR 0.52; 95%CI 0.35 – 0.77, p < 0.001) and low expression of both PD-L1 and B7-H3 was associated with better OS (HR 1.73; 95%CI 1.08–2.76, p = 0.023).ConclusionIn this multiracial TMA of CRC, in a robust multivariable model, following REMARK guidelines, we validate our prior findings that low expression of both B7-H3 and PD-L1 is prognostic for OS among patients with mCRC. Given the rapid proliferation of B7-H3 targeted drug development in clinical trials, these findings may help create a platform for future research to delineate their predictive role in a bespoke therapeutic approach.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16300-y.

Précis (condensed abstract)

In a multiracial multiethnic tumor microarray of metastatic colorectal cancer, high expression of either B7-H3 and PD-L1 was associated with worse prognosis and overall survival.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the second most common cause of cancer death worldwide, exceeding 1.9 million new cases and 900,000 deaths, as per the 2022 report [1]. In metastatic CRC (mCRC), the median overall survival is 2.5–3 years, and 5-year survival is only 15% [2, 3]. Immune checkpoints regulate T-cell function, exerting their action in co-stimulation or co-inhibition by interactions between proteins from the B7 and CD28 immune checkpoint family [4, 5]. Importantly, PD-L1 expression is a predictive biomarker for multiple immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) including pembrolizumab, nivolumab, and atezolizumab. Specifically in mCRC, PD-L1 expression carries a negative prognostic value but is not a predictor of response [6–10], unlike other cancer types [10, 11].

Given that the benefit of ICIs is limited to the 4% of patients with mCRC who have dMMR tumors [12], other members of the B7 family such as B7-H3 and HHLA2 have gained prominence as potential targets for drug development. Exploring their clinical application as predictors of treatment response or prognostic factors of survival are relevant. Overexpression of B7-H3 is reported in CRC tumors that can be localized in the membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus or vasculature; and several studies report its association with poor prognosis [5, 13–17].

HHLA2, B7-H3 and PD-L1 are all B7 family immune checkpoints that can inhibit T-cell and NK-cell function, but they signal through distinct receptors and are not fully redundant [18]. Blockade of one does not reliably compensate for another; hence simultaneous assessment captures multiple, parallel brakes on antitumor immunity. The PD-L1 checkpoint binds to receptor PD-1 and plays a role in adaptive immune resistance, and T-cell exhaustion. The B7-H3 checkpoint has a yet undescribed receptor and plays a role in T-cell suppression, macrophage/MDSC recruitment, and EMT progression. HHLA2 (also known as B7H7) binds to 2 potential receptors TMIGD2 (costimulatory), KIR3DL3 (coinhibitory) and offers bidirectional signaling; often dominant inhibitory in cancer. Co-expression of PD-L1 with other B7 checkpoints has been associated with particularly poor survival and more immunosuppressive microenvironments, supporting a composite biomarker concept [19–22]. One of the authors (Zang) was instrumental in identifying several members of the costimulatory/coinhibitory B7 and CD28 family including HHLA2, and its corresponding HHLA2-KIR3DL3 immunosuppressive pathway, and HHLA2-TMIGD2 immunostimulatory pathway. Additionally, Zang’s lab revealed B7-H3 as a metabolic regulator (summarized in review, ref 22). Studying HHLA2, B7-H3 and PD-L1 together in CRC can therefore define risk groups more precisely than any single marker (prognostic modeling). Furthermore, given the explosion of drugs targeting B7-H3 that have entered the clinic, such modeling may help identify patients who would benefit from dual checkpoint blockade or from pairing them with chemotherapy. This is particularly relevant in MSS colorectal cancer, where unmet need is greatest.

Lastly, racial disparities have been described for CRC incidence, clinicopathological features and survival rates [2, 23]. However, data to evaluate differences in the expression and clinical significance of immune regulators in minorities is limited. This study aimed to evaluate the expression of three immune biomarkers from the B7-CD28 family (HHLA2, B7-H3, and PD-L1), and three tissue specific protein markers (CK7, CK20, and CDX2) in patients with CRC.

Materials and methods

Patients and samples

Patients with CRC treated at two cancer centers between 2000 and 2018 with available pathology slides were identified. Patients whose tumor samples did not meet specifications including adequate tumor specimen and available tumor blocks were excluded. All samples were obtained at baseline, prior to any treatment. Demographics (age, gender, marital status) and clinical features including grade (well, moderate or poor), initial presentation (de novo versus recurrent disease, and TNM stage) and sidedness (right-sided if between the cecum and the hepatic flexure; transverse; or left-sided if between the splenic flexure and the rectum) were obtained by medical chart review. Vital status (alive/dead) was obtained from medical charts and the National Death Index.

Statement of ethics

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) at each participating institution.

Tissue microarray (TMA) construction

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained slide for each patient was reviewed by the collaborating pathologists. Representative tumor tissue was selected and the corresponding formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue block was identified for TMA construction, as in our prior publication [24] (Supplementary Table 1). For each FFPE tissue block, three tissue cores with a diameter of 0.6 mm were punched out from different areas using the semiautomated Advanced Tissue Arrayer (ATA100; Chemicon International, Temecula, CA). Subsequently, these cores were transferred into a TMA recipient paraffin block. A total of 16 TMA paraffin blocks were constructed for the entire cohort, and three consecutive sections of each TMA blocks were cut for immunohistochemistry.

Immunohistochemical staining

Biopsied specimens were fixed in neutral buffered formalin and subjected to paraffin embedding. The 5-μm-thick, 10% formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections were deparaffinized in xylene three times, 10 min each, and subsequently rehydrated through graded alcohols to distilled water. Antigen heat retrieval was performed in 1-mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (pH 9.0) for 10 min using a microwave oven. Next, the sections were allowed to cool down at room temperature for 1.5 h. After successive rinses in distilled water and Tris-buffered saline, sections were incubated with respective antibodies at 4 °C for 2 h. Finally, the sections were covered with streptavidin peroxidase (Dako, Santa Barbara, CA) diluted 1:100 in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), incubated for 30 min at 37 °C, washed three times in PBS, and stained with 3,3’-diaminobenzidine for approximately 30 min at 37 °C. Counterstaining was performed using Mayer’s hematoxylin. Slides were scanned on a Panoramic 250 Flash III whole-slide scanner (SIG #1S10OD019961-01; 3DHistech, Budapest, Hungary) using a 20 × objective. Brown stain analysis was completed on the whole piece of tissue on every slide with the 3DHistech QuantCenter and DensitoQuant module. In this module, brown stain pixels were distinguished from the rest of the tissue by color thresholding. The analysis of blue versus brown areas of tissue was completed in Image J (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD), using the color threshold module. Providing a “score” for each sample

For each individual tumor specimen, the percentage of positively stained tumor cells (distribution) was given a corresponding score: 0 for no positive tumor cells; 1 for 1% to 25%, 2 for 26% to 50%, 3 for 51% to 75%, and 4 for 76% to 100%. Similarly, a corresponding score was given separately based on the intensity (0 for negative, 1 for 1 +, 2 for 2 +, and 3 for 3 +). An overall score of 0 to 12 (product of distribution and intensity) was calculated for each individual specimen. Because each patient’s tissue was available in triplicate, the final score for each patient was a mean of each of the three individual scores, with a final score of 0 to 12 (in increments of 0.33).

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics of patients were compared among race-ethnicity groups using descriptive statistics. Categorical variables were compared using chi-square or Fisher’s exact test when appropriate and numerical variables were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Protein expression of each of the six markers, HHLA2, B7-H3, PD-L1, CK7, CK20, and CDX, was categorized into high and low expression groups and compared among race-ethnicity groups and different clinicopathological characteristics using the Chi-square test. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were plotted to visualize, and log-rank test were performed to test the difference in overall survival (OS) by high and low marker expression groups among metastatic cancer patients. For markers whose expression level were found significantly associated with OS survival rate in univariate survival analysis, a multivariate Cox proportional hazard model was fitted to assess the difference between high and low expression groups in OS, on condition of other potential confounding factors (age, gender, race-ethnicity, marital status, tumor grade, presentation of metastatic disease, gross location and side). The hazard ratio and 95% confidence interval of each factor involved in multivariate survival analysis were provided. Analyses were conducted using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC). Statistical significance was determined by a two-sided P-value < 0.05.

Results

Patient characteristics

The study included 388 patients with CRC (Table 1, Supplementary Table 2). Among them, 150 patients had presented with de novo metastatic disease, 108 had an initial localized cancer at presentation and later relapsed, and 130 patients had localized disease without relapse. The median age was 62.4 years, with a female predominance (53%). The majority of the patients belonged to racial/ethnic minorities: [NHB 159 (40.9%); Hispanics 147 (37.9%); NHW 70 (18%), and Asians 12 (3.1%).

Biomarker presence and variability in distribution

We found no expression (mean score of 0) of PD-L1 in 60%, B7-H3 in 43%, and HHLA2 in 41% of all CRC samples. Overall, PD-L1 was expressed (score > 0) in 40%, B7-H3 in 57%, and HHLA2 in 59%. For tissue specific protein markers, positive expression was noted in 96% of specimens for CDX2; 17% for CK7 (negative staining in 83%), and 77% for CK20. These findings are consistent with expectation, given that the classic colorectal cancer is negative for CK7 and positive for CK20 and CDX2 expression.

Biomarker variability and association with on race/ethnicity and sex

The relationship between biomarker protein expression and race/ethnicity was evaluated (Supplementary Table 2). No discernable difference in the expression of HHLA2, B7-H3, PD-L1, CK7, CK20 and CDX2 among NHW, NHB, Hispanics, and Asians was observed. The search for a sex difference in the expression only revealed a trend to a higher PD-L1 expression among females (41%) compared to males (31%), although this difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.064). The other biomarkers did not have any difference in expression based on sex (Supplementary Table 3). We also looked for differences in protein expression based on the initial T, N and overall stage at diagnosis. The only significant finding was higher PD-L1 expression among patients with N2 cancer as compared to those with N1 or N0 cancer (Supplementary Table 4). No difference was observed based on T stage (Supplementary Table 5), or overall stage grouping (Supplementary Table 6). Similarly, no difference was observed based on the primary site of colon or rectal cancer (Supplementary Table 7).

Clinical outcome and association with biomarker expression

For outcome of OS among mCRC, we included those diagnosed with de novo metastatic cancer (58%) and the patients who relapsed from a prior localized presentation (42%). In an unadjusted univariate Cox model for survival analysis, age, grade, sidedness, and PD-L1 expression were independently associated with OS (Table 2, Fig. 1a). Consistent with our prior data, combined PD-L1/B7-H3 expression had a more prominent association with OS. After adjusting for the important clinical variables, such as differentiation, age, marital status, and sex, using the Cox proportional model, left-sided tumors (protective, HR 0.52, 95%CI 0.35–0.77; p < 0.001) and high PD-L1 and/or B7-H3 expression (harmful, HR 1.73; 95%CI 1.08–2.76; p = 0.023) remained statistically significant variables (Table 3, Fig. 1b). Tumors with a poor grade of differentiation had an HR of 1.86 (95%CI 0.97–3.56) in comparison to well differentiated tumors but did not meet significance in the adjusted Cox proportional model (p = 0.06). In terms of the median OS, patients with low PD-L1 and low B7-H3 expression experienced the best outcome of 40 months. The worst OS of 26 months was noted among patients with a high expression of either PD-L1 or B7-H3 or both. The median OS was 30 months among all patients (Supplementary Table 8). Discussion

The B7 family of immune modulatory proteins can be co-stimulatory, co-inhibitory, and both [25, 26]. The protein B7-H3 (B7 homolog 3 protein, alias CD276) is increasingly appreciated as a promising immunotherapeutic target, and is generally considered to be a marker of poor prognosis and worse outcome in several cancers [13, 14]. Furthermore, a receptor for B7-H3 is yet to be identified which impedes not only our understanding of its mode of action but also the development of therapeutically effective B7-H3 antagonists.

Among patients with metastatic cancer, collective consideration of B7-H3 and PD-L1 confirmed our prior findings that high expression of any of these proteins is associated with worse survival [24]. Given that B7-H3 expression is tied to poor differentiation, this may be one explanation for its overexpression being associated with worse overall outcomes. This fact was further confirmed and validated in the multivariable model, factoring in grade of differentiation. Overall, given that these two checkpoints represent complementary, partly independent axes of immune escape and adverse biology in CRC, their combined assessment is more informative than any single marker for both biology and therapeutic development.

The expression patterns of the remaining three proteins, namely, CK7, CK20, and CDX2, were in line with expectation [27, 28]. Even though we observed an overall low expression prevalence of CK7, one important feature identified is that CK7 is expressed at a higher level in patients presenting with localized disease rather than patients who relapse/present with de novo metastatic disease. The clinical, diagnostic, and prognostic implications of this are unclear, and could form the basis of a future study. The high prevalence of CD20 and CDX2 is in line with expectations. Overall, none of these three proteins show a racial predilection. These data should give clinical pathologists who are working in a minority rich patient population the comfort that the reporting guidelines they are following are appropriate and reflective of that specific population.

Prior knowledge does indeed exist regarding these 2 immune checkpoint proteins in CRC. In one metanalysis that combined data from 6 publications, the expression of B7-H3 was associated with worse OS in mCRC, with an OR of 0.48 at 24 months and 0.61 at 72 months [29]. Similarly, in a study of 805 mCRC patients, B7-H3, but not B7-H4 was prognostic for OS [17]. However, in a separate study, while the expression of B7-H3 was quite prevalent in CRC, their data did not reveal an association of its expression with PFS or OS [30]. With regards to PD-L1, high expression is rather consistent, and over several studies, shown to be associated with worse clinical outcomes, both in the curative and the metastatic setting [31, 32]. Our results take a composite approach, and reveal that while PD-L1 consideration, in isolation is clearly prognostic, collective consideration of PD-L1 and B7-H3 yields a more robust outcome with greater separation of the OS curves (Fig. 1b; Suppl Table 8).

Our results offer particularly compelling evidence to consider PD-L1 and B7-H3 as composite biomarkers for several reasons. Their expression is often discordant, and overreliance on one biomarker can potentially miss a substantial subset of patients with active immune suppression. These two immune checkpoints operate through non-overlapping pathways. PD-L1 inhibits T-cell activity through the PD-1 receptor on activated T cells, while B7-H3 is a co-inhibitory ligand that suppresses T-cell activation and NK cell function. B7-H3 is often overexpressed in tumors that are PD-L1 negative. In fact, B7-H3 overexpression is one potential mechanism of immune resistance in cancers, despite PD-L1 negativity [33, 34]. B7-H3 upregulation has been observed in non-responders to anti–PD-1 therapy and in the post-treatment adaptive resistance states [33]. PD-1/PD-L1 blockade failures often retain alternative checkpoints, including B7-H3. From a clinically relevant perspective, targeting B7-H3 could be beneficial for the treatment of patients insensitive to PD-L1 directed therapy [35].

It has remained an unfortunate trend that, NHB and Hispanic patients with CRC have consistently had worse outcomes than their NHW counterparts [23]. Immunotherapy is closely intertwined with the host genetics and immune system. This is likely to widen the disparity in outcome between the various racial/ethnic groups. It was encouraging to observe similar immune protein expression among the various racial/ethnic groups, and moreover, the relapse and survival outcomes did not differ either. The diverse patient profile offers an additional assurance of the wide applicability of our data, because omissions of ethnic/racial information lead to incomplete statistical models [36].

For a novel biomarker to find its place in clinical practice, it must meet two essential characteristics: first, it should be easily measurable; and second, it requires clinical relevance. An example of one that meets the second criterion but not the first is the consensus molecular subtype (CMS); based on gene expression profile and categorized CRC into 4 distinct molecular entities with prognostic and potentially therapeutic relevance [37]. However, due to the complexity of the assay, it has yet to find a place in routine care. Similarly, while the immunoscore has a scientific rationale, the development of expertise to read and interpret the assay has led to a lack of enthusiasm for its adoption. Remarkably though, testing tumors for MMR is now routine for CRC and several solid tumors, reflecting the simplicity of the assay (IHC on 4 slides) and its clinical importance. Pembrolizumab is indicated in frontline mCRC [38], and for later lines of therapy of metastatic solid tumors with dMMR [12]. With this context, B7-H3 has the potential to find a place as a novel immune based biomarker along with PD-L1 expression. The assay is simple, requiring 1 slide for IHC and will require minimal training of the interpreting pathologist. Further, in a robust multivariable model, the performance of this integrated biomarker overwhelms that of traditional and classic biomarkers such as age, sex, degree of differentiation and marital status.

A limitation of our study is the retrospective nature, and use of archival specimens for the development of the TMA. However, to overcome this potential limitation, the pathologists confirmed the quality of the specimens and presence of tumor tissue in each specimen prior to its incorporation into the TMA. While the optics of our study suggest that the lack of a detailed therapeutic history would preclude the development of a predictive model; this is less likely to affect the utilization of the TMA since none of the patients received therapy with anti PD-L1 or anti B7-H3 agents. Under such circumstances, the utility of the TMA is to develop a robust prognostic model in this multivariable fashion as we have demonstrated.

In conclusion, it is clear that expression of either PD-L1 or B7-H3 and the corollary, lack of expression of either of these proteins is an important prognostic biomarker. With the advent of clinical development of anti B7H3 antibodies, several of which are in clinical trials, our findings are particularly relevant as they may help direct biomarker driven therapy for patients with metastatic CRC. Future clinical trials could evaluate combined therapy of anti B7-H3 and anti PD-L1 either as 2 drug combinations or could lead to development of bi-specific antibodies [39, 40].

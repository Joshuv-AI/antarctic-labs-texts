Higher Order Dualities between Prime Ideals
Sroyon Sengupta

Abstract




Abstract:

Extending the works of Alladi [Al77] and Sweeting and Woo [SW18], we state and prove the general higher order duality between prime ideals in number rings. We then use the second order duality to obtain the a new formula for the Chebotarev Density involving sums of the generalized Möbius function and the prime ideal counting function. We also provide two estimates of such sums as an application of the duality identity. A discussion of the duality in a slightly more general setting is done at the end.

Keywords:
Duality between prime ideals, generalized Möbius function, number of prime ideals, largest and smallest norm of prime ideals, Chebotarev Density, Chebotarev Density Theorem, Galois extensions, Prime Ideal Theorem, Artin Symbol

Introduction and Background

Since time immemorial, the Möbius function have been an integral and essential part of analytic number theory and several important results and theories have come up around it. Let us first recall this significantly important function defined on positive integers:

\displaystyle\mu(n)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{when $n=1$},\\
0,\quad\text{when $n$ is not square-free},\\
(-1)^{k},\quad\text{when $n=p_{1}p_{2}...p_{k}$, where $p_{i}$'s are distinct prime numbers}\end{cases}

One of the most well-known examples of the use of the Möbius function is in its relation with the Riemann Zeta function. Using the Euler product formula of
\zeta(s)
, one can show that the Dirichlet series with
\mu(n)
as its coefficients is the reciprocal of the zeta function. Further,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}=\lim_{s\to 1^{+}}\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}=0

Landau [LaT99] proved that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}=0

(1.1)

is (elementarily) equivalent to the Prime Number Theorem. A surprising generalization of the (1.1) was observed by Alladi [Al77], in 1977, as a consequence of the following Duality Lemma he proved in the same paper. He proved that if
f
is a function defined on primes, then:

\displaystyle\sum_{1<d|n}\mu(d)f(P_{1}(d))

\displaystyle=-f(p_{1}(n)),

\displaystyle\sum_{1<d|n}\mu(d)f(p_{1}(d))

\displaystyle=-f(P_{1}(n)),

(1.2)

where
P_{1}(n)
and
p_{1}(n)
are the largest and the smallest prime factors of the integer
n
respectively. Using (1.2) and some properties of the Möbius function, Alladi showed that if
f
is a bounded function on the primes such that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{2\leq n\leq x}f(P_{1}(n))=c,

(1.3)

then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)f(p_{1}(n))}{n}=-c,

(1.4)

and vice-versa. We notice that (1.1) is indeed a special case of (1.4). If we choose
f
to be an arithmetic function on primes defined as
f(p)=1
for all primes
p
, then such an
f
will yield from (1.3) that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{2\leq n\leq x}f(P_{1}(n))=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{[x]-1}{x}=1.

Then, from (1.4), we get for the same
f
that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}=-1,

(1.5)

which is nothing but (1.1) with a slight rearrangement.

Another example of
f
is the characteristic function of primes in an arithmetic progression
\ell\;(mod\;k)
. In [Al77], it was proved that the Prime Number Theorem in Arithmetic Progressions implies that the sequence
\{P_{1}(n)\}_{n}
of the largest prime factors of integers
n
is uniformly distributed in the reduced residue classes modulo a positive integer, say
k
. This further implies that, with such a choice of
f
mentioned right above, the average of

exists, and is
\frac{1}{\varphi(k)}
. Therefore, the value of
c
in (1.3) for this corresponding choice of

is
\frac{1}{\varphi(k)}
and hence, by (1.4),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=2\\
p_{1}(n)\equiv\ell\;(mod\;k)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}=-\frac{1}{\varphi(k)},

(1.6)

for all positive integers
k,\ell
, such that
1\leq\ell<k
and
(\ell,k)=1
. We note here that the sum on the left of (1.6) gives us a way of slicing the sum on the left of (1.5) into
\varphi(k)
subseries, each of them converging to the same value! Alladi gave a quantitative proof of (1.6) in [Al77] which involves the use of the duality identity (1.2).

In 2017, Dawsey [Da17] chose

to be the characteristic function of primes satisfying the condition
\left[\frac{K/\mathbb{Q}}{p}\right]=C
, where

is a finite Galois extension of the field of rationals

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=2\\
\left[\frac{K/\mathbb{Q}}{p_{1}(n)}\right]\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}=-\frac{|C|}{|G|}

(1.7)

What stands out in [Da17] is the quantitative nature of the results proved by Dawsey, which she is able to derive using the strong form of the Chebotarev Density Theorem given by Lagarias-Odlyzko [LO77]. J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})(\omega_{K}(JJ^{\prime})-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\supset I_{j}\\
J\not\supset I_{k^{\prime}},\;\forall k^{\prime}\geq j+1\\
J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})(\omega_{K}(J)-1+\omega_{K}(J^{\prime}))

(2.1)

We note that in the above equality, the outer sum on the right ranges to
s-1
, since the contribution from the ideal
I_{s}
is 0. Also, we do not need the term
f(J^{\prime})
as by the definition of
f
, we only need to focus on the ideal with the smallest norm, which of course contains
J
and not
J^{\prime}
. Therefore, continuing from (2.1), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{J\supset I}\mu_{K}(J)(\omega_{K}(J)-1)

\displaystyle f(J)=\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\supset I_{j}\\
J\not\supset I_{k^{\prime}},\;\forall k^{\prime}\geq j+1\\
J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)(\omega_{K}(J)-1)\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\supset I_{j}\\
J\not\supset I_{k^{\prime}},\;\forall k^{\prime}\geq j+1\\
J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})=:\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}

(2.2)

We now evaluate both the summations
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
above in (2.2). Starting with the inner sum of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})=\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i},

(2.3)

where we assume that there are
t
-many distinct prime ideals containing the product of ideals
I_{j+1}...I_{s}
.
On the other hand, we know that

\displaystyle(1-x)^{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}x^{i}

(2.4)

Hence, the RHS of (2.4) with
x=1
yields the RHS of (2.3), and therefore, we have from both the equations that

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1,\;\text{when $I_{j+1}...I_{s}=\mathcal{O}_{k}$}\\
0,\;\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(2.5)

From (2.5), it is clear that the only contribution from
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is when
I_{j+1}...I_{s}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
, which is again only possible if
j=s
. But that is not possible since
j
ranges from
1
to
s-1
and hence, from (2.5), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=0

(2.6)

Looking at the inner sum of
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}i

(2.7)

On the other hand, if we take the derivative on both sides of (2.4), we get that

\displaystyle-t(1-x)^{t-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}ix^{i-1}

(2.8)

Hence, again putting
x=1
on the RHS of (2.8) yields the RHS of (2.7), and therefore, from equating the left hand sides of (2.7) and (2.8) for
x=1
, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})=\begin{cases}-1,\;\text{when $I_{j+1}...I_{s}$ is a prime ideal or a prime power}\\
0,\;\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(2.9)

Therefore, the only contribution from
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is when
j=s-1
and that
I_{s}
is either just a prime ideal, or its power. Note that by our construction,
I_{s}
is the product of the prime ideals containing the ideal
I
with the largest norm and hence, by our hypothesis,
I_{s}
is indeed a prime ideal or a prime power. Therefore, we have from (2.2) and (2.9) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\supset I_{s-1}\\
J\not\supset I_{s}\\
J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)=Q_{C}^{2}(I)

(2.10)

To understand the last equality, we observe that
I_{s-1}
is a product of prime ideals containing
I
with the second largest norm. Now, the only positive contribution from the terms in the sum in (2.10) are when we choose
J
to just be the prime ideals, since:

(i)

we cannot choose
J
contained in powers of prime ideals as then
\mu_{K}(J)=0
, and

(ii)

we cannot choose
J
contained in two distinct prime ideals, as then
f(J)=0
6
6

6

note that every such
J
is contained in multiple prime ideals of the smallest norm
.

Thus, along with a negative sign, the sum counts the number of prime ideals containing the ideal
I
with the second largest norm. Hence, combining (2.2), (2.6) and (2.10), we conclude our theorem.
∎

Therefore, we have what we call the second order duality between prime ideals in a number ring. We note that the RHS of
Lemma 2.2
counts the number of prime ideals with the largest norm and that of
Theorem 2.3
counts the number of prime ideals with the second largest norm. Hence, we call them first and second order dualities respectively. Let us now state and prove the general higher order dualities between prime ideals. The proof will follow an idea similar to that used in the proof of
Theorem 2.3

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
K
be a number field and
k
be a positive integer. Let
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
be an ideal such that
Q^{i}(I)=1
, for
1\leq i\leq k-1
. Let
f
be the characteristic function of the set
S(L/K;C)
. Then the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{J\supset I}\mu_{K}(J){{\omega_{K}(J)-1}\choose k-1}f(J)=(-1)^{k}Q_{C}^{k}(I)

Proof.

Apriori, we must note that this theorem only makes sense when the chosen ideal
I
has prime factors with at least
k
distinct norms.
We now start with our usual factorization of
I
as done above:
I=I_{1}...I_{s}
where the
I_{i}
’s are product of prime ideals, containing
I
, of the same norm. Without the loss of generality, let us assume that the factors are written in an ascending order of their norms, i.e.
N(I_{i})<N(I_{j})
, for all
i<j
. From the LHS of the above equation, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{J\supset I}\mu_{K}(J){{\omega_{K}(J)-1}\choose k-1}f(J)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{J\supset I}\mu_{K}(J)(\omega_{K}(J)-1)...(\omega_{K}(J)-k+1)f(J)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{j=1}^{s-k+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\supset I_{j}\\
J\not\supset I_{k^{\prime}}\forall k^{\prime}\geq j+1\\
J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})(\omega_{K}(J)+\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})-1)...(\omega_{K}(J)+\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})-k+1)

(2.11)

We note that the summand of the innermost sum in (2.11) can be repersented as

\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}^{k-1}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})c_{u}(\omega_{K}(J))\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})^{u}

(2.12)

where
c_{u}(\omega_{K}(J))
are coefficients of the polynomial in
\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})
. Since the sums are finite, we can do an interchange of the summations to get

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})(\omega_{K}(J)+\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})-1)...(\omega_{K}(J)+\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})-k+1)=\sum_{u=0}^{k-1}c_{u}(\omega_{K}(J))\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})^{u}

(2.13)

Now we note that
j
ranges from
1
to
s-k+1
. For
0\leq u\leq k-1
, we can write the inner sum in the RHS of (2.13) as

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})^{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}i^{u}

(2.14)

On the other hand, looking at the binomial expansion

\displaystyle(1-x)^{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}x^{i},

(2.15)

we take derivative with respect to
x
and multiply with
x
on both sides of (2.15) to get

\displaystyle-tx(1-x)^{t-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}ix^{i}

(2.16)

Repeating the same step again, we see

\displaystyle-tx(1-x)^{t-1}+t(t-1)x^{2}(1-x)^{t-2}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}i^{2}x^{i}

(2.17)

We continue the same for
u
times and get

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{u}\tilde{c}_{\ell}(t)x^{\ell}(1-x)^{t-\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}i^{u}x^{i}

(2.18)

With
x=1
, the RHS of (2.18) is exactly the RHS of (2.14), while the LHS of (2.18) with
x=1
is non-zero only when
\ell=t
. That is only possible if
t\leq u
. Now,
t
is the number of distinct prime ideals containing the product
I_{j+1}...I_{s}
, where
j\leq s-k+1
. Hence, by our hypothesis,
k-1\leq t
. But
u\leq k-1
, and therefore, the only non-zero contribution occurs by RHS of (2.13) is when
u=k-1
. In other words, we can also say that for
0\leq u\leq k-2
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})^{u}=0

(2.19)

Thus, from (2.11), (2.13) and (2.19), we continue to get

\displaystyle\sum_{J\supset I}\mu_{K}(J){{\omega_{K}(J)-1}\choose k-1}f(J)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{j=1}^{s-k+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\supset I_{j}\\
J\not\supset I_{k^{\prime}}\forall k^{\prime}\geq j+1\\
J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})^{k-1}

(2.20)

Using (2.19), we can again rewrite (2.20) as

\displaystyle\sum_{J\supset I}\mu_{K}(J){{\omega_{K}(J)-1}\choose k-1}f(J)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{j=1}^{s-k+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\supset I_{j}\\
J\not\supset I_{k^{\prime}}\forall k^{\prime}\geq j+1\\
J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})(\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})-1)...(\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})-k+2)

(2.21)

Again the inner sum of (2.21) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\supset I_{j+1}...I_{s}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})(\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})-1)...(\omega_{K}(J^{\prime})-k+2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=k-1}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}i(i-1)...(i-k+2)=\left(\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{i}{t\choose i}x^{i}\right]\right)\Bigg|_{x=1}=\left(\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}(1-x)^{t}\right)\Bigg|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!,\;\text{when $I_{j+1}...I_{s}$ has exactly $k-1$ distinct prime factors}\\
0,\;\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(2.22)

Therefore, by our hypothesis, for the LHS of (2.22) to give a non-zero contribution,
j
can only take the value of
s-k+1
. Therefore, from (2.21) and (2.22), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{J\supset I}\mu_{K}(J){{\omega_{K}(J)-1}\choose k-1}f(J)=\frac{(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\supset I_{s-k+1}\\
J\not\supset I_{k}^{\prime}\forall k^{\prime}\geq s-k\\
J\neq\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(J)f(J)=(-1)^{k}Q_{C}^{k}(I)

(2.23)

This proves our general duality lemma for any positive integer
k
and for ideals satisfying some conditions in any arbitrary number ring
K
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We observe that plugging
k=1
and
k=2
in Theorem 2.4 gives us Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 2.3 respectively. Therefore, they both are consistent with our general duality identity.

In the following section, we will prove estimates of functions counting prime ideals that will be essential for us in proving our main results that will follow later, where also, the above duality identities will be used predominantly.

3
The Prime Ideal Counting Functions and their Estimates

Let us recall the Dickman function [De61]
\rho(\beta)
defined to be the continuous solution to the following system of equations:

\displaystyle\rho(\beta)

\displaystyle=1,\;\text{for $0\leq\beta\leq 1$}

\displaystyle-\beta\rho^{\prime}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\rho(\beta-1),\;\text{for $\beta>1$}

de Bruijn [Br51] and Hildebrand [Hi86] used the Dickman function to estimate a well-known prime counting function defined as

\displaystyle\Psi(x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\\
P_{1}(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}1.

(3.1)

Tenenbaum [Te00] has also proved several estimates involving the
\Psi(x,y)
function and the
k^{th}
largest prime factors which proved to be very useful in proving results in the classical case. But here, as we shift from the classical to an algebraic setting, we need to consider the algebraic analogues of such functions and their corresponding estimates. We count, what are known as
smooth ideals
, using the following function: for a number field
K
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{K}(X,Y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(I)\leq X\\
M_{1}(I)\leq Y\end{subarray}}1

(3.2)

counts the number of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with their norm bounded by
X
such that the norm of their prime factors are bounded by
Y
. It has been proved by Krause [Kr90] and Moree [Mo92] that an asymptotic estimate for the function
\Psi_{K}
exist and is given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{K}(X,Y)=X\rho(\beta)\left(1+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\beta\log(\beta+1)}{\log X}\right)\right),

(3.3)

where
\beta=\frac{\log X}{\log Y}
. Note that this asymptote is uniform for
1\leq\beta\leq(\log X)^{1-\varepsilon}
. (3.3) also corresponds to Hildebrand’s result [Hi86] in the classical case, i.e. when
K=\mathbb{Q}
. The implicit constant in the error of (3.3) depends on
\varepsilon
which can be suitably chosen to derive desired estimates. As done in the classical case by Maier [Ma(up)] as a corollary to Hildebrand’s result, in the algebraic setting too, one can use the following upper bound of the Dickman function (see Norton [No71])

\displaystyle\rho(\beta)\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta+1)}

to derive the asymptotic

\displaystyle\rho(\beta)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\beta}}e^{-\beta\log\left(\frac{\beta}{e}\right)}

(3.4)

and then use (3.4) to get another useful estimate of
\Psi_{K}(X,Y)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\Psi_{K}(X,Y)\ll Xe^{-c_{3}\beta},

(3.5)

for some constant
c_{3}
. We will use both the estimates (3.3) and (3.5) in different contexts in our proofs. As mentioned earlier, our aim is to derive new formulas of the Chebotarev Density using higher order dualities between prime ideals as proved above. In this paper, we will mainly focus on using the second order duality
7
7

7

can be viewed as generalizations of results by Alladi-Johnson [AJ24] and the author [Se25]
and comment on how the higher order dualities can be utilized following in lines of the proofs by Alladi-Sengupta [AS(ip)]. In what follows, we will prove a few important lemmas in this section that we will use to prove our main results.

Looking at the hypothesis of
Theorem 2.3
, we observe that it is required to consider only such ideals that satisfy the condition
Q^{1}(I)=1
. We now prove that the number of ideals that do not satisfy such a condition is small compared to a bound of the norm of the ideals. In fact, in the lemma that follows, we prove something even stronger.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1}(K;X)
be a set of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
defined as

\displaystyle S_{1}(K;X):=\{I\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}:\;M_{1}(I)^{2}|N(I);\;N(I)\leq X\}

(3.6)

Denoting
\mathcal{N}_{1}(K;X)=\#\mathcal{S}_{1}(K;X)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}(K;X)\ll\frac{X}{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}}}

(3.7)

Proof.

Using the estimate of
\Psi_{K}(X,Y)
in (3.5), with the choice of
Y=e^{\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}}}
, we have that

\displaystyle\Psi_{K}\left(X,e^{\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}}}\right)\ll\frac{X}{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}}}

(3.8)

Now, it only suffices to look at those ideals satisfying
M_{1}(I)=N(\mathfrak{p})>e^{\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}}}
. The number of such ideals is essentially of the order of the number of ideals with norm less than or equal to
\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}
. Therefore, by
Theorem 1.5
, we have that

\displaystyle N_{1}(K;X)

\displaystyle\ll\Psi_{K}\left(X,e^{\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}}}\right)+c_{K}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})>e^{\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}}}}\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}}}+c_{K}\sum_{n>e^{\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}}}}\frac{X}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}}}

(3.9)

This proves our required lemma.
∎

We now define another ideal counting function: the function

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,Y):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(I)\leq X\\
M_{2}(I)\leq Y\end{subarray}}1

(3.10)

counts the number of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with norm bounded by
X
and the second largest norm among all their prime ideal factors is bounded by
Y
. The next lemma will provide us with a crucial estimate of
\Psi_{K,2}(X,Y)
with
Y
belonging in some given interval. This estimate is for the number of ideals with all the prime factors, except ones with the largest norm, with of smaller norms. From the estimate (3.5), it is quite clear that
\Psi_{K}(X,Y)
is quite small as compared to
X
, for large
X
. On the other hand,
\Psi_{K,2}(X,Y)
is not that small. This can be viewed by considering ideals of the form
I=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
, such that
N(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=2<N(\mathfrak{p}_{2})
. We observe that

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,Y)\geq\Psi_{K,2}(X,2),

(3.11)

and
\Psi_{K,2}(X,2)
is more than the number of prime ideals with norm no more than
\frac{X}{2}
. Hence, continuing from (3.11), using the asymptotic version of the Prime Ideal Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,Y)\gg\frac{X}{\log\frac{X}{2}}

Therefore, it is important to find its estimate, of course under some restrictions, which is provided by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
Y\leq e^{(\log X)^{1-\delta}}
, for some small
\delta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,Y)\ll\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}

(3.12)

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a prime ideal. We define a new counting function as follows:

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,\mathfrak{p})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(I)\leq X\\
M_{2}(I)=N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}1

(3.13)

Therefore, from (3.10) and (3.13), we see that the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,Y)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K,2}(X,\mathfrak{p})

(3.14)

The reason behind the inequality above is that in the sum on the left, we are counting ideals, whereas on the right we are counting prime ideals with bounded norm and therefore, there might be over-counting due to the presence of an ideal inside multiple prime ideals of the same norm. Therefore, estimating the sum on the right of (3.14) would suffice for our required result.

Let us first fix a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
and consider an ideal
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that it can be represented as
I=\mathfrak{m.pq}
, where
M_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{q}
,
M_{2}(I)=\mathfrak{p}
and
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})
. Note, we are considering ideals such that
Q^{1}(I)=1
, where we denote the unique prime ideal with the largest norm as
\mathfrak{q}
, such that
I\not\subset\mathfrak{q}^{2}
, as by
Lemma 3.1
, the number of the ideals left out is small and will only contribute to the error term. Rewriting
\Psi_{K,2}(X,\mathfrak{p})
using its definition (3.13), we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,\mathfrak{p})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{m})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})\leq N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})}}1+O\left(\frac{X}{e^{\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}}}\right)

(3.15)

We use the asymptotic version of the Prime Ideal Theorem to get from (3.15) that

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,\mathfrak{p})\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{m})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})\log\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})}\right)}

(3.16)

Now, let us denote
T=T(X)=e^{(\log X)^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}}
. Truncating the sum on the RHS of (3.16) at T, we get that

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,\mathfrak{p})\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{m})\leq T\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})\log\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})}\right)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T<N(\mathfrak{m})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})\log\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})}\right)}

(3.17)

To estimate the first sum on the right, we first note that
\log\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})}\right)\gg\log X
, since
N(\mathfrak{m})\leq T
and
N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{m})\leq T\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})\log\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{mp})}\right)}\ll\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{m})\leq T\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})}\ll\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})\log X}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{t}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
\mathfrak{t}\text{ is prime}\\
N(\mathfrak{t})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{t}}\right)^{-1}

(3.18)

The inner product on the right can be then estimated using the Merten’s Theorem for prime ideals [Le23] as follows:

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{t}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
\mathfrak{t}\text{ is prime}\\
N(\mathfrak{t})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{t}}\right)^{-1}\ll\log N(\mathfrak{p})

(3.19)

Hence, from (3.18) and (3.19), we continue to get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{m})\leq T\\

M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})}

. Then rewriting from (3.21), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T<N(\mathfrak{m})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})}=\sum_{T<n\leq\frac{X}{N(p)^{2}}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\

where

, and by hypothesis,

. Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle\alpha>\frac{\log T}{\log Y}=\frac{\log e^{(\log X)^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}}}{\log e^{(\log X)^{1-\delta}}}=(\log X)^{\frac{\delta}{2}}

Thus, a standard use of the Stieltjes integral gives us from (3.22) that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T<N(\mathfrak{m})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{m})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{m})}\ll e^{-c_{3}(\log X)^{\frac{\delta}{2}}}

(3.24)

Therefore, combining (3.17), (3.20), (3.21) and (3.24), we finally have that

\displaystyle\Psi_{K,2}(X,\mathfrak{p})\ll\frac{X\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{N(\mathfrak{p})\log X}+\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})\log N(\mathfrak{p})}e^{-c_{3}(\log X)^{\frac{\delta}{2}}}

(3.25)

Therefore, summing all the prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
with norms bounded by
Y
, we have from (3.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K,2}(X,\mathfrak{p})

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{X\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{N(\mathfrak{p})\log X}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})\log N(\mathfrak{p})}e^{-c_{3}(\log X)^{\frac{\delta}{2}}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{\log N(\mathfrak{p})}{N(\mathfrak{p})}+\frac{X}{e^{(\log X)^{\frac{\delta}{2}}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})\log N(\mathfrak{p})}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}

(3.26)

The last estimate uses a well known number field version of the Merten’s Theorem [Le23] in the first sum, while the second sum is convergent and is much smaller in comparison to the first sum. Thus, we prove our lemma.

By definition, it is clear that ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
contained in prime ideals only of a particular norm, do not contribute to the sum in the LHS. As done in the proof of
Lemma 3.4
, in a similar fashion, we define the set
S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})
as the set of ideals
I
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
N(I)\leq X
with a unique and non-repeating prime ideal of the largest norm and
M_{2}(I)=N(\mathfrak{p})
. We note here that for any ideal
I\subset\mathfrak{p}
, for some prime
\mathfrak{p}
such that
N(I)\leq X
and
M_{2}(I)=N(\mathfrak{p})
,
N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}
. Therefore, using
Lemma 3.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}Q_{C}^{2}(I)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|+O\left(\frac{X}{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}}}\right)

(4.1)

We therefore move on to estimate the sum on the RHS of (4.1). We choose
I\in S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})
such that
I=\mathfrak{M.pq}
, where
M_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{q}
,
M_{2}(I)=\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{M}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
is an ideal such that
M_{1}(\mathfrak{M})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})
. Thus, we write

\displaystyle|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{M})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\\
N(\mathfrak{M.pq})\leq X\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{pq})}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{M})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}1

(4.2)

Using the definition of
\Psi_{K}(X,Y)
, we then have that

\displaystyle|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
\mathfrak{q}\text{ is a prime}\\
N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{pq})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)

(4.3)

Now, we observe that for
Y\leq e^{(\log X)^{1-\delta}}
, for some
\delta>0
, we have using (3.27) that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|\leq\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|\leq\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y}\Psi_{K,2}\left({X},\mathfrak{p}\right)\ll\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}

(4.4)

Therefore, we are left to estimate the sum of
|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|
over the interval
Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}
. Therefore, from (4.3) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\;\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)

(4.5)

It is worth mentioning here that the idea of this proof is quite similar to the proof of
Lemma 3.4
. We change the order of summation in the RHS of (4.5), which would require a split in the summation. We keep in mind the following conditions that
N(\mathfrak{p})
need to satisfy:

\displaystyle Y<N(\mathfrak{p}),\quad N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q}),\quad N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})},\quad N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}

Continuing from (4.5), we then get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}\end{subarray}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)

(4.6)

To bring in the Chebotarev Density, we now interchange the inner sums of both the double sums in the RHS of (4.6) with suitable integrals. This is indeed one of the most crucial steps of the whole proof. We do the replacements as follow:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)\quad

\displaystyle\text{by}\quad\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)\quad

\displaystyle\text{by}\quad\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

Of course, it is now time to estimate the following errors that arise due to such a replacement:

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}\right)

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}\right)

From (4.6), we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

\displaystyle+\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}

\displaystyle\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

\displaystyle+E_{1}+E_{2}

(4.7)

To estimate the error terms
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, we use the strong form of Chebotarev Density Theorem (
Theorem 1.2
). Changing the order of summations in
E_{1}
, we observe that

\displaystyle|E_{1}|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{pq})}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{M})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}1-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{M})\leq t\end{subarray}}1\right\}\frac{dt}{\log t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\;\sum_{N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{YN(\mathfrak{q})}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max(Y,M_{1}(\mathfrak{M}))\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\min\left(N(\mathfrak{q}),\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})}\right)\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}1-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{\max(Y,M_{1}(\mathfrak{M}))}^{\min\left(N(\mathfrak{q}),\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})}\right)}\frac{dt}{\log t}\right|

(4.8)

Of course, the innermost summand involving the absolute value resembles the LHS of
Theorem 1.2
. Also, we note that error function
\frac{X}{e^{c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{n_{K}}}}}
is increasing, and therefore, we use the CDT with the bound
\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})}
on
N(\mathfrak{p})
. We denote the constant
c_{4}=\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{n_{K}}}
. Thus, from (4.8), we get

\displaystyle|E_{1}|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\;\sum_{N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{YN(\mathfrak{q})}}\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})}\right)}}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\;\sum_{N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{YN(\mathfrak{q})}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{M})}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log Y}}}

(4.9)

Similarly, for
E_{2}
, we have that

\displaystyle|E_{2}|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{pq})}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{M})\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}1-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})}\\
M_{1}(\mathfrak{M})\leq t\end{subarray}}1\right\}\frac{dt}{\log t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{YN(\mathfrak{q})}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max(Y,M_{1}(\mathfrak{M}))\leq N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\min\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})},\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})}\right)\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}1-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\int_{\max(Y,M_{1}(\mathfrak{M}))}^{\min\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})},\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})}\right)}\frac{dt}{\log t}\right|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{YN(\mathfrak{q})}}\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{Mq})}\right)}}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{M})\leq\frac{X}{YN(\mathfrak{q})}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{M})}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log Y}}}

(4.10)

Therefore, combining (4.7), (4.9) and (4.10), we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|=\frac{|C|}{|G|}

\displaystyle\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}+O\left(\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\right)

(4.11)

Therefore, along with (4.4), we get from (4.11) that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\left[\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}+\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}\right]

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}\right)+O\left(\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\right)

(4.12)

Now, we will evaluate the pseudo-integro-sums using further estimates. Surprisingly, the very same idea as used above will work here too. Let us recall the definition of
Q^{2}(I)
:

\displaystyle Q^{2}(I)

\displaystyle:=\#\{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}:\;I\subset\mathfrak{p};\;M_{2}(I)=Nm(\mathfrak{p})\}

Note that by the definition of
S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})
, we can write using
Lemma 3.1
, in a similar fashion as of (4.1) that

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}Q^{2}(I)=\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|+O\left(\frac{X}{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}}}\right)

(4.13)

Here, we observe that the sum on the right of (4.13) is exactly the same that we treated in the first half of the proof, without the conjugacy class condition on the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. But the change in the consideration of the condition hardly changes anything in the estimation of the sum. We can start by using the estimate in (3.27) to see that for
Y\leq e^{(\log X)^{1-\delta}}
, for some
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})\leq Y}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|\ll\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}

(4.14)

Using the idea in (4.3), we can write here that

\displaystyle\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\end{subarray}}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)

(4.15)

As done earlier, we change the order of summation in (4.15) and then split the double sum into two to get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}\end{subarray}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)

(4.16)

We observe that the only difference in between the equations (4.6) and (4.16) is the conjugacy class condition. Thus, we do a similar replacement of the inner sums with integrals, but this time, without the Chebotarev Density factor in front of the integrals. We make the following replacements:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)\quad

\displaystyle\text{by}\quad\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)\quad

\displaystyle\text{by}\quad\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

Of course, we then have the errors that need to be estimated. We denote them as:

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<N(\mathfrak{q})\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)-\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}\right)

\displaystyle E_{4}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<N(\mathfrak{p})<\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})},N(\mathfrak{p})\right)-\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi_{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}\right)

For the estimation of
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
, we use
Theorem 1.3
and other standard summation estimates, as used in the proof above, and also in the proof of
Lemma 3.4
. Following similar modifications and calculations, we finally get that

\displaystyle|E_{3}|\ll\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{1}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\quad\text{and}\quad|E_{4}|\ll\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{1}\sqrt{\log Y}}}

(4.17)

Therefore, combining (4.14), (4.16) and (4.17), we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{p})\leq\sqrt{X}\end{subarray}}|S_{2}(X,\mathfrak{p})|=\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}

\displaystyle\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi^{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}+\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi^{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}\right)+O\left(\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{1}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\right)

(4.18)

Further, using (4.13), we can write from (4.18) that

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}Q^{2}(I)=

\displaystyle\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi^{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}+\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi^{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}\right)+O\left(\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{1}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\right)+O\left(\frac{x}{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}}}\right)

(4.19)

We can rewrite the LHS of (4.19), using
Theorem 1.5
, as

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}Q^{2}(I)=\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
Q^{2}(I)\geq 2\end{subarray}}(Q^{2}(I)-1)=c_{K}\cdot X+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
Q^{2}(I)\geq 2\end{subarray}}(Q^{2}(I)-1)+O\left(X^{1-\frac{1}{d}}\right),

(4.20)

where
d=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. Then using
Lemma 3.4
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}Q^{2}(I)=c_{K}\cdot X+O\left(\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}\right)+O\left(\frac{X\log\log X}{e^{c_{1}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\right)

(4.21)

Thus, combining (4.19) and (4.21) and rearranging the terms, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{Y<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sqrt{X}}\int_{Y}^{N(\mathfrak{q})}\Psi^{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}+\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{X}{Y}}\int_{Y}^{\frac{X}{N(\mathfrak{q})}}\Psi^{K}\left(\frac{X}{tN(\mathfrak{q})},t\right)\frac{dt}{\log t}

\displaystyle=c_{K}\cdot X+O\left(\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}\right)+O\left(\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\right)

(4.22)

Interestingly, the LHS of (4.22) is exactly the expression in brackets in the RHS of (4.12) that we were required to estimate. Therefore, now combining (4.1), (4.12) and (4.22), we finally get that

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}Q_{C}^{2}(I)=c_{K}\cdot\frac{|C|}{|G|}\cdot X+O\left(\frac{X\log Y}{\log X}\right)+O\left(\frac{X\log X\log\log X}{e^{c_{4}\sqrt{\log Y}}}\right)

(4.23)

Thus, a suitable choice of
Y=e^{\left(\frac{2}{c_{4}}\log\log X\right)^{2}}
gives our desired result.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We observe that the quantitative approach of the proof of Theorem 4.1 gives us an asymptotic relation, i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}Q_{C}^{2}(I)\sim c_{K}\cdot\frac{|C|}{|G|}\cdot X,

(4.24)

along with an estimated error term.

Recalling
Theorem 1.1
from [SW18], we see that it is also an asymptotic relation, and also, surprisingly has the same right hand side. Precisely,

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}Q_{C}(I)\sim c_{K}\cdot\frac{|C|}{|G|}\cdot X

(4.25)

It is indeed intriguing to see that irrespective of the
k^{th}
largest norm that we choose, the sum on the left has the same asymptote due to the use of the Chebotarev Density Theorem. One can also prove the same asymptotic result for other
k^{th}
largest norms, as done in the classical case for any arbitrary
k^{th}
largest prime factors (see [AS(ip)]). We now prove our main theorem that provides us the formula for the Chebotarev Density using the first and second order duality.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
f
be a characteristic function of the set
S(L/K;C)
. For a finite Galois extension
L/K
and a fixed conjugacy class
C\subset G=Gal(L/K)
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{X\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)}{N(I)}=\lim_{X\to\infty}\sum_{2\leq N(I)\leq X}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)f(I)}{N(I)}=\frac{|C|}{|G|}

Proof.

We start by considering the sum on the left multiplied by
X
. We have

\displaystyle X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)}{N(I)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)\cdot\frac{X}{N(I)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)\left[c_{k}\cdot\frac{X}{N(I)}\right]

(4.26)

We note here, by
Theorem 1.5
, that for a fixed ideal
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
,

\displaystyle\#\{J\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}:J\subset I;\;N(J)\leq X\}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(J)\leq X\\
I\supset J\end{subarray}}1=c_{K}\cdot\frac{X}{N(I)}+O\left(\left(\frac{X}{N(I)}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{d}}\right)

(4.27)

Thus, using (4.27) in (4.26), we get

\displaystyle X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)}{N(I)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(J)\leq X\\
I\supset J\end{subarray}}1+O\left(\left(\frac{X}{N(I)}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{d}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(J)\leq X\\
I\supset J\end{subarray}}1+O\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)\left(\frac{X}{N(I)}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{d}}\right)

(4.28)

Let us first estimate the error term on the right of (4.28). To do so, we recall the following bound of the sum of the generalized Möbius function (see

in [SW18]) that follow analogously from their classical counterparts:

\displaystyle\sum_{N(I)\leq X}\frac{\mu(I)}{N(I)}

\displaystyle=O\left(e^{-c_{6}(\log X)^{\frac{1}{12}}}\right)

(4.29)

Also, we use the following number field analogue [Na84] of the Hardy-Ramanujan estimate of

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(I)\sim\log\log N(I)

Hence, using (4.29) and (4.30) in the error in (4.28), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)\left(\frac{X}{N(I)}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{d}}

\displaystyle\ll\log\log X\sum_{N(I)\leq X}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)}{N(I)}X^{1-\frac{1}{d}}N(I)^{\frac{1}{d}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X\log\log X}{e^{c_{6}(\log X)^{\frac{1}{12}}}}

(4.31)

Now, for the main term in the RHS of (4.28), we observe with a interchange in summation that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
N(J)\leq X\\
I\supset J\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{I\supset J}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)f(I)

(4.32)

We first split the inner sum in the RHS of (4.32) into the following two parts:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)f(I)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)f(I)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)f(I)

(4.33)

Hence, (4.32) and (4.33) together yield

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)f(I)=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)f(I)+\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)f(I)

Applying
Theorem 2.3
, and then
Lemma 3.1
and
Theorem 4.1
together in the first sum in the RHS of (4.34), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)f(I)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(J)\leq X\\
Q^{1}(J)=1\end{subarray}}Q_{C}^{2}(J)

\displaystyle=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\cdot X+O\left(\frac{X(\log\log X)^{2}}{\log X}\right)

(4.35)

Further, applying
Lemma 2.2
and then
Theorem 1.1
of [SW18] in the second in the RHS of (4.35), we ave

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)f(I)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}Q_{C}^{1}(I)

\displaystyle=-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\cdot X+O\left(\frac{X}{e^{c_{5}(\log X)^{\frac{1}{3}}}}\right)

(4.36)

Therefore, combining (4.32), (4.34), (4.35) and (4.36), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\

N(J)\leq X\\
I\supset J\end{subarray}}1\ll\frac{X(\log\log X)^{2}}{\log X}

(4.37)

Thus, (4.28) along with (4.31) and (4.37) yield

\displaystyle X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)}{N(I)}\ll\frac{X(\log\log X)^{2}}{(\log X)}

Canceling

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)}{N(I)}\ll\frac{(\log\log X)^{2}}{(\log X)}

Further, using the quantitative version of
Theorem 1.2
of [SW18], we have from (4.39) that

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{(\log\log X)^{2}}{\log X}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)}{N(I)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)}{N(I)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)}{N(I)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)}{N(I)}-\frac{|C|}{|G|}+O\left(e^{-c_{6}(\log X)^{\frac{1}{12}}}\right)

(4.40)

Thus, rearranging (4.40), finally get our desired result:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)}{N(I)}=\frac{|C|}{|G|}+O\left(\frac{(\log\log X)^{2}}{\log X}\right)

(4.41)

Taking
X\to\infty
on both sides of (4.41), we have our desired formula for the Chebotarev Density.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

It is important to note here that this method to obtain the formula is significantly different from any that has been used earlier. The method used in [SW18] uses the sums of
\mu_{K}(I)\log N(I)
, which faces difficulties in our case as the error estimates grow large due to the largeness of
\Psi_{K,2}(X,Y)
. Difficulties were also faced in following a method similar to the one used in [Se25] due to absence of a one-one correspondence between the multiples of an ideal
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
and multiples of its norm
N(I)
.

5
Two Estimates of Sums involving

In [Al77], [AJ24] and [Se25], we observe that the authors have used the estimates of sums involving

In this section, therefore, we will prove two theorem that give us quantitative estimates of such sums, which although are not necessary to get the formula for the Chebotarev Density, can be viewed as an application of our duality identities.

Theorem 5.1
.

For a finite Galois extension

Proof.

Apriori, we note that the function
f
has already been defined in §2 (see

) and §4 (see

) as the indicator function

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(I)\leq X\\
Ic\in S(L/K;C)\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)=\sum_{N(I)\leq X}\mu_{K}(I)f(I)

Now, using the number field analogue of Möbius Inversion in

\displaystyle\sum_{N(I)\leq X}\mu_{K}(I)f(I)=-\sum_{N(I)\leq X}\sum_{JJ^{\prime}=I}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})Q_{C}^{1}(J)

Note, the second sum runs over all ideals

\displaystyle\sum_{N(I)\leq X}\mu_{K}(I)f(I)=-\sum_{N(J^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{X}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\sum_{N(J)\leq\frac{X}{N(J^{\prime})}}Q_{C}^{1}(J)-\sum_{N(J)\leq\sqrt{X}}Q_{C}^{1}(J)\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(J^{\prime})\leq\frac{X}{N(J)}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})

\displaystyle\sum_{N(J^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{X}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\sum_{N(J)\leq\frac{X}{N(J^{\prime})}}Q_{C}^{1}(J)=\sum_{N(J^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{X}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\left[c_{K}\cdot\frac{|C|}{|G|}\cdot\frac{X}{N(J^{\prime})}+O\left(\frac{X}{N(J^{\prime})e^{c_{5}\left(\log\frac{X}{N(J^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}}}\right)\right]

Thus, using (4.29) in (5.4), we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{N(J^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{X}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\sum_{N(J)\leq\frac{X}{N(J^{\prime})}}Q_{C}^{1}(J)

where
c_{8}=\min\{c_{9},c_{7}\}
and
c_{7}=\frac{c_{5}}{\sqrt[3]{2}},\;c_{9}=\frac{c_{6}}{\sqrt[12]{2}}
. Here, we note another

For the second double sum on the right of (5.3), we observe using (5.6) that

\displaystyle\sum_{N(J)\leq\sqrt{X}}Q_{C}^{1}(J)\sum_{\sqrt{X}<N(J^{\prime})\leq\frac{X}{N(J)}}\mu_{K}(J^{\prime})\ll\sum_{N(J)\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{X}{N(J)e^{c_{6}\left(\log\frac{X}{N(J)}\right)^{\frac{1}{12}}}}\ll\frac{X\log X}{e^{c_{9}(\log X)^{\frac{1}{12}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{I\supset J}\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)f(I)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)f(I)+\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)f(I)

(6.3)

Applying
Theorem 6.1
for
k=2
and then
Lemma 3.1
in the first sum in the RHS of (6.3), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)(\omega_{K}(I)-1)f(I)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(J)\leq X\\
Q^{1}(J)=1\end{subarray}}Q_{C}^{2}(J)=\frac{1}{c_{K}}\cdot\mathcal{K}\cdot X+o(X)

(6.4)

and applying
Theorem 6.1
for
k=1
in the second in the RHS of (6.3), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\supset J\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(I)f(I)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{c_{K}}\sum_{2\leq N(J)\leq X}Q_{C}^{1}(I)=-\frac{1}{c_{K}}\cdot\mathcal{K}\cdot X+o(X)

(6.5)

Thus, combining everything above, we have

\displaystyle X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(\mathcal{A})\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)}{N(I)}=o(X)\implies\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(\mathcal{A})\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)}{N(I)}=o(1)

(6.6)

Thus, rearranging the sum and using the hypothesis again gives us our required result.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

Choosing the set
\mathcal{A}
to be the prime ideals that are unramified in
L
such that their Artin symbols are all
C
, where
L/K
is a Galois extension and
C\subset G=Gal(L/K)
, we see that
\mathcal{K}=c_{K}\cdot\frac{|C|}{|G|}
, and hence, Theorem 4.3 follows qualitatively from Theorem 6.3.

It is always a challenge to prove the asymptotics in the hypothesis of
Theorem 6.3
. In our case,
Theorem 4.1
and
Theorem 1.1
in [SW18] are extremely important results and
Theorem 4.3
is a consequence of both of these results. It will be worthwhile to study such collections
\mathcal{A}
of prime ideals in given rings of integers that satisfy such asymptotics and how the constant
\mathcal{K}
differs in each case. Of course, if the constants are different, say
\mathcal{K}_{1},\mathcal{K}_{2}
respectively for
k=1,2
, we will have

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq N(I)\leq X\\
I\in S(\mathcal{A})\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{K}(I)\omega_{K}(I)}{N(I)}=\frac{1}{c_{K}}(\mathcal{K}_{2}-\mathcal{K}_{1})

There have been works by Kural, McDonald and Sah [KMS20] and Wang [Wa21] that extends works of Alladi, Dawsey, Sweeting and Woo. It is hoped that this general duality and the new approach towards attaining formulas of the Chebotarev Density can now open up even more generalized extensions of results that have been already proved by the authors mentioned above. Sahoo and Jha [JS(ip)] have also worked out the function field analogue of the Alladi duality. A function field analogue of this higher order duality between prime ideals will lead to a complete duality identity for global fields which might even lead to further exploration.

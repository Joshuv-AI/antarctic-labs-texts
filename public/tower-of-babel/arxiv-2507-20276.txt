Joint deformations of manifolds, coherent sheaves and sections
Donatella Iacono, Marco Manetti

Abstract
. We describe a differential graded Lie algebra controlling infinitesimal deformations of triples ( X , ℱ , σ ) (X,\mathcal{F},\sigma) , where ℱ \mathcal{F} is a coherent sheaf on a smooth variety X X over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 and σ ∈ H 0 ​ ( X , ℱ ) \sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}) . Then, we apply this result to investigate deformations of pairs (variety, divisor).

1.
Introduction

Let
Z
be a possibly singular hypersurface on a smooth variety
X
.
It is known, at least to experts in deformation theory,
that deformations of the pair
(X,Z)
are controlled by the hypercohomology of the complex of sheaves

\mathcal{D}:\quad\Theta_{X}\xrightarrow{\gamma}\mathcal{N}_{Z|X}\,.

More precisely, considering the tangent sheaf
\Theta_{X}
in degree
0
and the normal sheaf

\mathcal{N}_{Z|X}
in degree
1
, the space of first order deformations is
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{D})
, while
obstructions are contained in

\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{D})
. The kernel of
\gamma
is the sheaf
\Theta_{X}(-\log Z)
of vector fields tangent to
Z
, that controls the locally trivial deformations of the pair

(X,Z)

[
Ia10
,
Ma22
]
.

The cokernel of
\gamma
is precisely the Schlessinger’s
T^{1}
-sheaf
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{Z}
of the variety
Z
and this controls, locally, the deformations of
Z
; note that, since
X
is smooth, the local deformations of
Z
are the same, up to isomorphism, of the local deformations of the pair
(X,Z)
.

The long exact sequence associated to the complex
\mathcal{D}
is

\begin{split}\cdots&\to H^{1}(\Theta_{X}(-\log Z))\to H^{1}(\mathcal{D})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{T}^{1}_{Z})\\
&\to H^{2}(\Theta_{X}(-\log Z))\to H^{2}(\mathcal{D})\to H^{1}(\mathcal{T}^{1}_{Z})\to\cdots.\end{split}

While both sheaves
\Theta_{X}
and
\Theta_{X}(-\log Z)
carry natural structures of sheaves of DG-Lie algebras, the same does not hold for the complex
\mathcal{D}
(see next Example
6.2
): this may be a problem if one want to study the deformations of the pair
(X,Z)
via DG-Lie algebras, or more modestly if one is interested to compute the primary obstruction and the Massey products.

If
Z
is defined as the zero locus of a section
\sigma
of a line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, then the deformations of the pair
(X,Z)
corresponds to the deformations of the triple
(X,\mathcal{L},\sigma)
, up to isomorphisms.

In
[
We83
]
, the author investigated the deformation theory of the triple
(X,\mathcal{L},\sigma)
. In particular, he proved that the first order deformations are controlled by the first hypercohomology group of the complex

(1.1)

\mathcal{P}(X,\mathcal{L})\xrightarrow{e_{\sigma}}\mathcal{L},

where
\mathcal{P}(X,\mathcal{L})
is the sheaf of first order differential operator of
\mathcal{L}
and
e_{\sigma}
is the evaluation at
\sigma
. The complex (
1.1
) is quasi-isomorphic to the complex
\mathcal{D}
, for
Z
equal the divisor of
\sigma
.

The main goal of this paper is to extend this analysis. First of all, we observe that the above complex (
1.1
) has a natural structure of a coherent sheaf of DG-Lie algebra and we prove that the DG-Lie algebra of derived global sections controls the deformations of the triple
(X,\mathcal{L},\sigma)
in the usual sense of Maurer–Cartan solutions/gauge equivalence.

More generally, we provide an explicit construction of a DG-Lie algebra controlling deformations of a triple
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)
, where
X
is a smooth variety,
\mathcal{F}
a coherent sheaf on
X
and
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
a global section of
\mathcal{F}
over
X
. More precisely,
taking a finite locally free resolutions
\mathcal{E}^{*}
of the sheaf
\mathcal{F}
and a section
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
lifting
\sigma
, we are able to prove the following theorem.

Theorem

(=Theorem
5.7
)
.

The infinitesimal deformations of the triples
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)
are controlled by the DG-Lie algebra
L
of the derived global sections of the complex

(1.2)

\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})\xrightarrow{e_{s}}\mathcal{E}^{*}.

Moreover, the homotopy type of the DG-Lie algebra
L
is independent on the choice of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
and
s
.

Our choice of considering only one section is motivated by simplicity of exposition; it will be clear from our proof that a similar result also holds for any finite sequence of sections

\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
, whenever the complex (
1.2
) is replaced by

\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})\xrightarrow{(e_{s_{1}},\ldots,e_{s_{n}})}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}^{*}.

The proof of the above theorem relies on the comparison of two formal groupoids.
The former
is the formal groupoid
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}\colon\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}\,\to\mathbf{Grpd}
of the deformations of triples
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)
, defined as a covariant functor
from the category of local Artin
\mathbb{K}\,
-algebras to the category of groupoids.
For any
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}\,
, the objects of the groupoid
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}(A)
are the infinitesimal deformations of the triple over
A
, and the morphisms are the isomorphisms among them (see Section
5
).

The latter is the reduced Deligne groupoid associated to a DG-Lie algebra
L
.
This is the covariant functor
\operatorname{Del}_{L}\colon\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}\to\mathbf{Grpd}
, such that the groupoid
\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A)
has Maurer–Cartan element as objects and the morphisms are given by gauge action mod out by the irrelevant stabilisers (see Section
2
).

In particular, we are able to recover the well known fact that the forgetful functor
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}\to\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F})}
is smooth, whenever
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{F})=0
(Corollary
5.10
).

Notation.
We denote by
\mathbb{K}\,
an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, by
\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}
the category of local Artin
\mathbb{K}\,
-algebras with residue field
\mathbb{K}\,
, by
\mathbf{Set}
the category of sets and by
\mathbf{Grpd}
the category of groupoids.
By variety, we mean an integral (and so irreducible and reduced), noetherian, separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{K}\,
.

2.
A brief review of Deligne groupoids of DG-Lie algebras

For the reader’s convenience, we review the Deligne groupoid and the deformation functor associated to a DG-Lie algebra; we refer to
[
Ma22
]
for a more detailed exposition and proofs.
For any DG-Lie algebra
L
, the associated Deligne groupoid
is the covariant functor

\operatorname{Del}_{L}\colon\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}\to\mathbf{Grpd}

defined in the following way. Given
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}
, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, the objects of
\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A)
are the solutions of the Maurer–Cartan equation in
L\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
:

Ob(\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A))=\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)=\{x\in L^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\mid dx+[x,x]/2=0\}.

The exponential group of the nilpotent Lie algebra
L^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
acts on
\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
by the gauge action, namely

e^{a}\ast x=e^{a}\ast x=x+\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{[a,\cdot]^{n}}{(n+1)!}([a,x]-da),\quad a\in L^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A},\,x\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A).

In particular, for every
\nu\in L^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and every
x\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
, we have

e^{d\nu+[x,\nu]}\ast x=x
since

[d\nu+[x,\nu],x]-d(d\nu+[x,\nu])=0.

Moreover, for a fixed
x\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
, the subset

I(x):=\{d\nu+[x,\nu]\mid\nu\in L^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\}
is a Lie subalgebra of
L^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and then
\exp(I(x))
is a subgroup contained in the stabiliser of
x
under the gauge action.

Given
x,y\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
, the set of morphisms
x\to y
is the quotient set

\operatorname{Mor}_{\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A)}(x,y)=\{a\in L^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\mid e^{a}\ast x=y\}/\sim,

where
a\sim b
if
e^{a}=e^{b}e^{d\nu+[x,\nu]}
for some
\nu\in L^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
.
As proved in
[
Ma22
, Lemma 6.5.5]
, we have
a\sim b
if and only if there exists

\mu\in L^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
such that

e^{a}=e^{d\mu+[y,\mu]}e^{b}
, and this implies that the composition of morphisms is
properly defined.
Note that when
L^{-1}=0
, the Deligne groupoid is the same as the action groupoid for the gauge action on Maurer–Cartan elements.

Every morphism of DG-Lie algebras
L\to M
naturally induces a natural transformation

\operatorname{Del}_{L}\to\operatorname{Del}_{M}
. Furthermore, the basic theorem of homotopy invariance of Deligne groupoids assures that, if
L\to M
is a quasi-isomorphism of DG-Lie algebras, then

\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A)\to\operatorname{Del}_{M}(A)
is an equivalence of groupoids, for every
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}
.

The deformation functor

\operatorname{Def}_{L}\colon\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}\to\mathbf{Set}

associated to
L
is the functor of isomorphism classes in the Deligne groupoids, i.e.,
\operatorname{Def}_{L}(A)=\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A))
for every
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}
; equivalently,
\operatorname{Def}_{L}
is given by Maurer–Cartan elements modulus gauge action.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\colon L\to M
be a morphism of DG-Lie algebras,
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}
and let

\textstyle{\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Del}_{M}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{H}

be a diagram of groupoids that is commutative on objects up to isomorphisms, i.e., it is commutative at the level of connected components.
Assume that:

(1)

f\colon H^{i}(L)\to H^{i}(M)
is surjective for
i=-1
and injective for

i=0
;

(2)

g
is full and essentially surjective;

(3)

h
is faith.

Then,
g
is an equivalence of groupoids.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that for every Maurer–Cartan element
x\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
the map

g\colon\operatorname{Mor}_{\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A)}(x,x)\to\operatorname{Mor}_{G}(g(x),g(x))

is injective. According to
[
Ma22
, Lemma 6.6.8]
, the morphism

f\colon\operatorname{Mor}_{\operatorname{Del}_{L}(A)}(x,x)\to\operatorname{Mor}_{\operatorname{Del}_{M}(A)}(f(x),f(x))

is injective, while
h
is injective on morphisms by assumption.
∎

For later use, we explicitly describe the Deligne groupoid for some particular examples of DG-Lie algebras.

Let
R
be a commutative
\mathbb{K}\,
-algebra and
let

(2.1)

(E^{*},\partial):\qquad\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial}E^{n-1}\xrightarrow{\partial}E^{n}\xrightarrow{\partial}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial}E^{0}

be a bounded above complex of projective
R
-modules.

The first DG-Lie algebra that we consider is

L=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(E^{*},E^{*})
, with bracket
[f,g]=fg-(-1)^{\overline{f}\,\overline{g}}gf

and differential

d=[\partial,\cdot]
, i.e.,
df=\partial f-(-1)^{\overline{f}}f\partial
.

For any element
x\in L^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}^{1}_{R}(E^{*},E^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}^{1}_{R\otimes A}(E^{*}\otimes A,E^{*}\otimes A)
, the Maurer–Cartan equation can be written as
[\partial+x,\partial+x]/2=0
and then

x\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
if and only if

(E^{*}\otimes A,\partial+x)
is a complex of
R\otimes A
-modules.

For
x,y\in L^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and
a\in L^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
we have

e^{a}\ast x=y\iff e^{[a,-]}(\partial+x)=\partial+y\iff e^{a}(\partial+x)e^{-a}=\partial+y,

where the last equation is expressed in the associative product of
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R\otimes A}(E^{*}\otimes A,E^{*}\otimes A)
.
Thus, the relation
e^{a}\ast x=y
means that
e^{a}\colon(E^{*}\otimes A,\partial+x)\to(E^{*}\otimes A,\partial+y)
is a morphism of complexes
of
R\otimes A
-modules.
Finally, using the power series expansions of
e^{x}
and
\log(1-x)
it is easy to see that
for
x\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
the morphisms of complexes

e^{d\nu+[x,\nu]}\colon(E^{*}\otimes A,\partial+x)\to(E^{*}\otimes A,\partial+x),\quad\nu\in L^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A},

are exactly those that are homotopic to the identity by a
R\otimes A
-linear
homotopy that is trivial on
E^{*}
. Hence, if
e^{a}\ast x=e^{b}\ast x=y
, the equivalence

a\sim b
means that the two morphisms
e^{a},e^{b}\colon(E^{*}\otimes A,\partial+x)\to(E^{*}\otimes A,\partial+y)
are homotopic by a
R\otimes A
-linear homotopy that is trivial modulus
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, see
[
FIM12
]
.

As a second example, fix an element
s\in E^{0}
(since
E^{1}=0
we have
\partial s=0
); let
\tau
be an indeterminate of degree
-1
and consider the short exact sequence of complexes

0\to(E^{*},\partial)\to(E^{*}\oplus R\tau,\partial)\to R\tau\to 0,\quad\text{where }\;\partial\tau=s.

Define

M\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(E^{*}\oplus R\tau,E^{*}\oplus R\tau)
as the DG-Lie subalgebra of endomorphisms
f
such that
f(E^{*})\subseteq E^{*}
. Therefore,
we have isomorphisms

M^{i}\cong L^{i}\oplus E^{i-1}=\operatorname{Hom}^{i}_{R}(E^{*},E^{*})\oplus E^{i-1},\quad f\mapsto(f_{|E^{*}},f(\tau)).

Via the above isomorphism
M\cong L\oplus E^{*}[-1]
, the bracket in
M
becomes

[(f,v),(g,w)]=\left([f,g],f(w)-(-1)^{\overline{f}\,\overline{g}}g(v)\right),

and the differential

d(f,v)=[(\partial,s),(f,v)]=([\partial,f],\partial v-(-1)^{\overline{f}}f(s)).

Then, we have the following short exact sequence of DG-Lie algebras

0\to E^{*}[-1]\xrightarrow{\iota}M\xrightarrow{\rho}L\to 0,

where
E^{*}[-1]
is equipped with the trivial bracket,
\rho
is the projection and

\iota(x)=(-1)^{i}(0,x)
for
x\in E^{i-1}
.

We observe the following.

1) Under the above isomorphisms, a pair
(u,t)\in M^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, with
u\in\operatorname{Hom}^{1}_{R}(E^{*},E^{*})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and
t\in E^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation if and only if

(\partial+u)^{2}=0
.

2) For every
(u,t)\in M^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and every

b\in E^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, we have

(2.2)

e^{(0,b)}\ast(u,t)=(u,t-(\partial+u)b).

Lemma 2.2
.

In the above notation,
let
(u,t)\in M^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
be a solution of the Maurer–Cartan equation,

(f,a)\in M^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and denote

(u^{\prime},t^{\prime})=e^{(f,a)}\ast(u,t)
. Then, we have

\partial+u^{\prime}=e^{f}(\partial+u)e^{-f},\qquad s+t^{\prime}-e^{f}(s+t)\in(\partial+u^{\prime})(E^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}).

In particular,
s+t^{\prime}=e^{f}(s+t)
in the cohomology group
H^{0}(E^{*}\otimes A,\partial+u^{\prime})
and there exists
b\in E^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
such that

e^{(0,b)}\ast(u^{\prime},t^{\prime})=e^{(0,b)}e^{(f,a)}\ast(u,t)=(u^{\prime},e^{f}(s+t)-s).

Proof.

It is convenient to prove the above formulas in the DG-associative algebra

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(E^{*}\oplus R\tau,E^{*}\oplus R\tau)\otimes A
. Then,
the elements
(u,t),(u^{\prime},t^{\prime})
and
(f,a)
correspond, respectively, to the
A
-linear extension of the endomorphisms:

(1)

\alpha\colon E^{*}\oplus R\tau\to E^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
,

\alpha_{|E^{*}}=u
,
\alpha(\tau)=t
;

(2)

\beta\colon E^{*}\oplus R\tau\to E^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
,
\beta_{|E^{*}}=u^{\prime}
,
\beta(\tau)=t^{\prime}
;

(3)

\gamma\colon E^{*}\oplus R\tau\to E^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
,
\gamma_{|E^{*}}=f
,
\gamma(\tau)=a
.

By the relation
\partial+\beta=e^{\gamma}(\partial+\alpha)e^{-\gamma}
, we get

s+t^{\prime}=(\partial+\beta)(\tau)=e^{\gamma}(\partial+\alpha)e^{-\gamma}(\tau)=e^{\gamma}(\partial+\alpha)\left(\tau-\frac{e^{-\gamma}-I}{\gamma}(a)\right).

Denoting by
b=\frac{e^{-\gamma}-I}{\gamma}(a)\in E^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, we conclude that

\begin{split}s+t^{\prime}&=e^{\gamma}(\partial+\alpha)(\tau-b)=e^{\gamma}(s+t-(\partial+u)(b))=e^{f}(s+t)-e^{f}(\partial+u)(b)\\
&=e^{f}(s+t)-(\partial+u^{\prime})e^{f}(b).\end{split}

∎

4.
Coherent DG-Lie algebroids on smooth variety

Let
X
be a smooth variety and denote by
\Theta_{X}=\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!er_{\mathbb{K}\,}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})
its tangent sheaf.

Definition 4.1
.

A
coherent DG-Lie algebroid
over
X
is a pair
(\mathcal{L}^{*},\alpha)
where:

•

\mathcal{L}^{*}
is a sheaf of DG-Lie algebras over
\mathbb{K}\,
on
X
such that the underlying
sheaf of DG-vector spaces

\cdots\mathcal{L}^{i}\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathcal{L}^{i+1}\cdots
is a bounded complex of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules (hence
\delta
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear, while the bracket is assumed only
\mathbb{K}\,
-bilinear);

•

\alpha\colon\mathcal{L}^{*}\to\Theta_{X}
is a morphism of complexes of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, called the
anchor map
, commuting with the brackets;

•

finally, we require the Leibniz rule to hold

[l,fm]=\alpha(l)(f)m+f[l,m],\qquad\forall\,l,m\in\mathcal{L}^{*},f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}.

A morphism (risp.: quasi-isomorphism)
\mathcal{L}^{*}\to\mathcal{M}^{*}
of coherent DG-Lie algebroids over
X

is a morphism (risp.: quasi-isomorphism) of complexes of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules commuting with brackets and anchor maps.

An example is given by the sheaf
\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
of first order differential operators with scalar symbol of a bounded complex
(\mathcal{E}^{*},\partial)
of locally free sheaves on
X
. For application to deformation theory it is convenient, following
[
IM17
]
, to define

\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
in terms of derivations of pairs.

Consider the subcomplex of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})&\subseteq\Theta_{X}\times\operatorname{\mathcal{H}}\!\!om^{*}_{\mathbb{K}\,}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}),\\[4.0pt]
\mathcal{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})&=\{(h,u)\mid u(ax)-au(x)=h(a)x,\quad\text{for every }a\in\mathcal{O}_{X},\;x\in\mathcal{E}^{*}\}.\end{split}

Then, we define
\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{H}}\!\!om^{*}_{\mathbb{K}\,}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})

as the image of
\mathcal{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
under the projection on the second factor; since
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is torsion free we have an isomorphism of sheaves of DG-Lie algebras

\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathcal{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*}),\qquad u\mapsto(\alpha(u),u),

where
\alpha(u)
is the (scalar) symbol of
u
. If
u\in\mathcal{P}^{n}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
and
n\not=0
, then

u\in\operatorname{\mathcal{H}}\!\!om^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
and
\alpha(u)=0
, while every

u\in\mathcal{P}^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
is a sequence of differential operators of first order

u_{i}\colon\mathcal{E}^{i}\to\mathcal{E}^{i}
with the same scalar symbol.

Since the complex
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is assumed bounded, it is not difficult to prove that
\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
is also a coherent DG-Lie algebroids with surjective anchor map
\alpha

[
IM17
, Proposition 5.1]
. Therefore, we
have a short exact sequence

(4.1)

0\to\operatorname{\mathcal{H}}\!\!om^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\to\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})\xrightarrow{\;\alpha\;}\Theta_{X}\to 0.

Next, we describe another example of DG-Lie algebroids, extending the previous construction, starting from a coherent sheaf and a section.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on a smooth variety
X
and
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
a global section. A resolution of
(\mathcal{F},\sigma)
over
X
is a pair

(\mathcal{E}^{*},s)
, where:

•

\mathcal{E}^{*}
is a finite locally free resolution of
\mathcal{F}
, i.e., we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{E}^{-m}\xrightarrow{\partial}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial}\mathcal{E}^{0}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0
with every
\mathcal{E}^{i}
locally free;

•

s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{0})
is a section lifting
\sigma
.

Since
X
is a smooth variety, then a resolution as above always exists, see e.g. either
[
Ha77
, Kleiman’s Theorem, page 238]
or
[
Bo67
, Theorem 3.3]
, and, for any of them, evaluation at
s
gives a morphism of complexes of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules

e_{s}\colon\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})\to\mathcal{E}^{*},\qquad e_{s}(u)=u(s).

Mimicking the construction of the DG-Lie algebra
M
of Section
2
, the mapping cocone of the morphism
e_{s}
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*},s)
, carries a structure of DG-Lie algebroid. More explicitly,
\mathcal{C}^{n}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*},s)=\mathcal{P}^{n}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{n-1}
for every

n
, then we extend the bracket of
\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
to
\mathcal{C}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*},s)
by setting

\begin{cases}[u,x]=u(x)&\text{ for }u\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*}),\;x\in\mathcal{E}^{*},\\[4.0pt]
[x,y]=0&\text{ for }x,y\in\mathcal{E}^{*},\end{cases}

and the differential is as follows

Remark 4.6
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a locally free sheaf on
X
and
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
a global section, then the pair
(\mathcal{F},\sigma)
is itself a resolution.
Then, the evaluation at
\sigma
gives a morphism of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules

that is the differential of the DG-Lie algebroid

.

5.
Deformation of triples
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)

In this section we prove that, for any resolution
(\mathcal{E}^{*},s)
of a triple
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)
, the sheaf of DG-Lie algebras
\mathcal{C}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*},s)

controls the infinitesimal deformations of the triple
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)
. This means that the DG-Lie algebra

R\Gamma(\mathcal{C}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*},s))

of derived global sections of

\mathcal{C}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*},s)
controls the deformations in the usual sense of Maurer–Cartan solutions modulo gauge action.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a variety over
\mathbb{K}\,
,
\mathcal{F}
a coherent sheaf on
X
and
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
a global section of
\mathcal{F}
over
X
. An infinitesimal deformation of
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)
over
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}\,
is the data
(\mathcal{X}_{A},\mathcal{F}_{A},\sigma_{A})
where:

•

\mathcal{X}_{A}
is an infinitesimal deformation of
X
over
A
, i.e., a pull-back diagram

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}_{A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{K}\,\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Spec}A,}

where
\pi
is flat; since
A
is an Artin ring, this is given by a sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}
of flat
A
-algebras over
X
together with an isomorphism of sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{K}\,\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

•

\mathcal{F}_{A}
is a coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}
-modules, flat over
A
, equipped with a morphism
\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{F}
, inducing an isomorphism
\mathcal{F}_{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{F}
.

•

\sigma_{A}
is a global section of
\mathcal{F}_{A}
extending
\sigma
.

Definition 5.2
.

An isomorphism of (infinitesimal) deformations
(\mathcal{X}_{A},\mathcal{F}_{A},\sigma_{A})\to(\mathcal{X}_{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{A}^{\prime},\sigma_{A}^{\prime})
is the data of:

(1)

an isomorphism of sheaves of
A
-algebras
\theta\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{A}^{\prime}}
extending the identity on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
;

(2)

an isomorphism of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}
-modules
\varphi\colon\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{F}_{A}^{\prime}
, extending the identity on
\mathcal{F}
, where the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}
-module structure on
\mathcal{F}_{A}^{\prime}
is induced by
\theta
;

(3)

\varphi(\sigma_{A})=\sigma_{A}^{\prime}
.

Definition 5.3
.

In the above setup,
the formal groupoid of infinitesimal deformations of the triple
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)
is the functor

\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}\colon\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}\,\to\mathbf{Grpd},

that associates to any
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}\,
, the groupoid
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}(A)
, whose objects are the infinitesimal deformations of the triple over

and morphisms are the isomorphisms among them.
The functor of infinitesimal deformations of the triple

is the functor

\operatorname{Def}_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}=\pi_{0}(\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)})\colon\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}\,\to\mathbf{Set}.

Remark 5.4
.

In
[

,
we considered the functor

of deformations of the pair

; in a completely similar way as above, we can define the formal groupoid of infinitesimal deformations of the pair

as the functor

that associates to any

over

The functor of infinitesimal deformations of the pair

is the functor

Our next goal is to study the formal groupoid
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}
for
X
smooth;
first in the case
X
smooth affine, and then for a general smooth variety.

The case
X
affine

We first consider the reduced problem of deformations of pairs
(X,\mathcal{F})
, without taking care of the section
\sigma
. Assume
X
smooth affine variety and
let
0\to\mathcal{E}^{-m}\xrightarrow{\partial}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial}\mathcal{E}^{0}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0
be a finite free resolution of
\mathcal{F}
. Denote by

M=\Gamma(X,\mathcal{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*}))

the DG-Lie algebra of global sections of the sheaf
\mathcal{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})\cong\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*})
. According to
[
IM17
, Proposition 7.9]
, there exists an isomorphism of deformation functors
\operatorname{Def}_{M}\cong\operatorname{Def}_{(X,\mathcal{F})}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

In the above notation, there exists
an equivalence of formal groupoids

\Psi\colon\operatorname{Del}_{M}\to\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F})}.

Proof.

An object in
\operatorname{Del}_{M}(A)
is an element
u\in\operatorname{Hom}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
that satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation, so that
(\partial+u)^{2}=0
and this defines the following complex of sheaves

0\to\mathcal{E}^{-m}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\partial+u}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial+u}\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal{F}_{A}\to 0,

where
q
is just the cokernel of
\mathcal{E}^{1}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\partial+u}\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A
.
By flatness and lifting of relations
[
Art76
, Proposition 3.1]
, the above complex is an exact sequence,
the coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{A}
is flat over
A
and

\mathcal{F}_{A}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{K}\,=\mathcal{F}
.
Then, we obtain the deformation of the pair
(X\times\operatorname{Spec}A,\mathcal{F}_{A})
defined by
u
.

Assume now that
e^{f}\ast u=u^{\prime}

is a gauge equivalence between two Maurer–Cartan elements, with
f\in M^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. Here
f\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{P}^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*}))
is a first order differential operator with scalar symbol
\alpha(f)\in H^{0}(X,\Theta_{X})
. Then

e^{\alpha(f)}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
is an automorphism of sheaves of
A
-algebras and it is proved in
[
IM17
]
that the isomorphism of complexes of sheaves of
A
-modules

e^{f}\colon(\mathcal{E}^{*}\otimes A,\partial+u)\to(\mathcal{E}^{*}\otimes A,\partial+u^{\prime})
satisfies the condition

e^{f}(gx)=e^{\alpha(f)}(g)e^{f}(x)
for every
g\in\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
.
In particular,
e^{f}
induces an isomorphism in cohomology, i.e.,
e^{f}\colon\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{F}_{A}^{\prime}
. If
f
is of type
f=d\nu+[u,\nu]
for some
\nu\in M^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, then

\alpha(f)=0
, i.e.,
f
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear and the isomorphism of complexes

e^{f}
induces the identity in cohomology.

In conclusion, we have defined a morphism of groupoids
\Psi(A)\colon\operatorname{Del}_{M}(A)\to\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F})}(A)
, for any
A\in\mathbf{Art}
. This morphism is essentially surjective because every deformation of
X
is trivial and every deformation of
\mathcal{F}
is obtained as above, again by flatness and lifting of relations. By
[
IM17
, Lemma 2.10 and Proposition 7.9]
, for any
A\in\mathbf{Art}

the group
\exp(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{P}^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*}))\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})
is naturally isomorphic to the group of
A
-automorphisms of the pair
(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A,\mathcal{E}^{*}\otimes A)
lifting the identity on
(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{E}^{*})
;
therefore, it corresponds to the group of the isomorphisms of deformations.
Then,
\Psi(A)
is full and faithful, for any
A\in\mathbf{Art}
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine variety,
\mathcal{F}
a coherent sheaf on
X
and
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
. Given a resolution
(\mathcal{E}^{*},s)
of the triple
(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)
, let
L
be the DG-Lie algebra of global sections of
\mathcal{C}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*},s)
. Then, there exists a natural equivalence of formal groupoids

\Phi\colon\operatorname{Del}_{L}\to\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}.

Proof.

We showed in Lemma
5.5
that every deformation of
X
is isomorphic to
X\times\operatorname{Spec}(A)
and every deformation of
\mathcal{F}
over
A
is isomorphic to a quotient of
\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A
.
This allows us to replace
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}
with an equivalent formal groupoid

\mathcal{G}_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}\colon\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}\to\mathbf{Grpd},

depending on the resolution
(\mathcal{E}^{*},s)
and defined in the following way. For every

A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}
, the objects of
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}(A)
are the pairs

(\mathcal{F}_{A},\sigma_{A})
, where:

(1)

\mathcal{F}_{A}
is an

A
-flat quotient of
\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A
such that the projection map

p\colon\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A\to\mathcal{E}^{0}
factors to a morphism

p\colon\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{F}
and induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{F}_{A}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{K}\,=\mathcal{F}
,

(2)

\sigma_{A}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}_{A})
is a section such that
p(\sigma_{A})=\sigma
.

A morphism
(\mathcal{F}_{A},\sigma_{A})\to(\mathcal{F}_{A}^{\prime},\sigma_{A}^{\prime})
is a pair
(\theta,\varphi)
, where:

(1)

\theta
is an isomorphism of sheaves of
A
-algebras
\theta\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
, extending the identity on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
;

(2)

\varphi
is an isomorphism of sheaves of
A
-modules
\varphi\colon\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{F}_{A}^{\prime}
, extending the identity on
\mathcal{F}
such that
\varphi(gx)=\theta(g)\varphi(x)
for every

g\in\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
,
x\in\mathcal{F}_{A}
, and
\varphi(\sigma_{A})=\sigma_{A}^{\prime}
.

As noticed above, the natural morphism
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}\to\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}
is an equivalence.
Our next step is to define a morphism of formal groupoids

\Phi\colon\operatorname{Del}_{L}\to\mathcal{G}_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)},

that is surjective on objects and on morphisms.

Given
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}\,}
and
(u,t)\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
, then

u\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{1}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
,
t\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{0})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and

(5.1)

0\to\mathcal{E}^{-m}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\partial+u}\cdots\mathcal{E}^{-1}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\partial+u}\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A

is a complex. Since
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is exact in negative degree, by standard facts in
homological algebra (e.g.
[
Ma22
, Corollary 4.1.2]
), then
(
5.1
) is also exact.

Denoting by
\mathcal{F}_{A}
the cokernel of the rightmost map, we have an exact sequence

(5.2)

0\to\mathcal{E}^{-m}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\partial+u}\cdots\mathcal{E}^{-1}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\partial+u}\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal{F}_{A}\to 0

and the pair
\Phi(u,t):=(\mathcal{F}_{A},q(s+t))
is an object of
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}(A)
;
by flatness and relation theorem, every object of
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}(A)
is obtained in this way.

Assume now that
e^{(f,a)}\ast(u,t)=(u^{\prime},t^{\prime})

is a gauge equivalence between two Maurer–Cartan elements, with
(f,a)\in L^{0}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. Here
f\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{P}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E}^{*}))
is a first order differential operator with scalar symbol
\alpha(f)\in H^{0}(X,\Theta_{X})
. Then

e^{\alpha(f)}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
is an automorphism of sheaves of
A
-algebras and
we have proved in
[
IM17
]
that the isomorphism of graded sheaves of
A
-modules

e^{f}\colon\mathcal{E}^{*}\otimes A\to\mathcal{E}^{*}\otimes A
satisfies the condition

e^{f}(gx)=e^{\alpha(f)}(g)e^{f}(x)
for every
g\in\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A
.

Considering the two exact sequences

\mathcal{E}^{-1}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\partial+u}\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal{F}_{A}\to 0,\qquad\mathcal{E}^{-1}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\partial+u^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}^{0}\otimes A\xrightarrow{q^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}_{A}^{\prime}\to 0.

Lemma
2.2
implies that
q(e^{f}(s+t))=q(s+t^{\prime})
and then
the pair
\Phi(f,a)=(e^{\alpha(f)},e^{f})
is a morphism between

\Phi(u,t)
and
\Phi(u^{\prime},t^{\prime})
. If
(f,a)=d\nu+[u,\nu]
for some

\nu\in L^{-1}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}

then
\alpha(f)=0
and

induces the identity in cohomology, hence
\Phi(f,a)
is the identity on
\Phi(u,t)
.

It remains to prove that
\Phi
is surjective on morphisms.
Given
(u,t),(u^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\operatorname{MC}_{L}(A)
and an isomorphism

(\theta,\varphi)\colon(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A,\mathcal{F}_{A})\to(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes A,\mathcal{F}_{A}^{\prime})
such that

\varphi(q(s+t))=q^{\prime}(s+t^{\prime})

such that
e^{f}\ast u=u^{\prime}
and then

.
Denoting by
(u^{\prime},h)=e^{(f,0)}\ast(u,t)

\textstyle{\operatorname{Del}_{L}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

It is plain that the morphism

Corollary 5.8
.

Let

be a smooth variety over
\mathbb{K}\,
,
\mathcal{F}

X
and
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{C}^{*})
the Thom–Whitney totalization of the semicosimplicial DG-Lie algebra

\mathcal{C}^{*}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{F},\sigma)

\operatorname{Del}_{\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{C}^{*})}\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\!ef_{(X,\mathcal{F},\sigma)}.

[t^{n}\partial_{t},t^{m}\partial_{t}]=(m-n)t^{n+m-1}\partial_{t}

-a=\gamma[t\partial_{t},a\partial_{t}]=[t\partial_{t},a]

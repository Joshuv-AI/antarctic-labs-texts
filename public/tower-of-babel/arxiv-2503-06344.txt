A Hadamard theorem in transversely affine geometry with applications to affine orbifolds
Francisco C. Caramello Jr, H. A. Puel Martins, Ivan P. Costa e Silva

Abstract
. We introduce and investigate a novel notion of transversely affine foliation, comparing and contrasting it to the previous ones in the literature. We then use it to give an extension of the classic Hadamard’s theorem from Riemannian geometry to this setting. Our main result is a transversely affine version of a well-known “Hadamard-like” theorem by J. Hebda for Riemannian foliations. Alternatively, our result can be viewed as a foliation-theoretic analogue of the Hadamard’s theorem for affine manifolds proven by Beem and Parker. Namely, we show that under the transverse analogs of pseudoconvexity and disprisonment for the family of geodesics in the transverse affine geometry, together with an absence of transverse conjugate points, the universal cover of a manifold endowed with a transversely affine foliation whose leaves are compact and with finite holonomy is diffeomorphic to the product of a contractible manifold with the universal cover of a leaf. This also leads to a Beem–Parker-type Hadamard-like theorem for affine orbifolds.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,h)
be a connected Riemannian manifold. The classic Hadamard’s theorem in Riemannian geometry
[
28
,
29
]
states that the exponential map
\exp^{h}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T_{p}M\rightarrow M
at any point
p\in M
is a (universal) covering map, provided
(M,h)
is geodesically complete and its sectional curvature is nonpositive. Since this result implies such a nice control over the underlying manifold’s topology, it naturally motivates attempts of generalizations to a broader class of geometries — say semi-Riemannian or affine manifolds — where the notion of an exponential map still makes sense.

At first, such attempts would seem misguided, because geodesic completeness and nonpositivity of sectional curvatures, which are natural in Riemannian geometry (see. e.g.,
[
24
,
25
]
) are much less so in these broader contexts. For example, it is well-known that semi-Riemannian metrics do not, in general, define any distance function, and no analogue of the Hopf-Rinow theorem exists
[
4
,
28
]
. In particular, compactness of the underlying manifold does not imply either geodesic completeness or geodesic connectedness in this context
[
28
, Example 7.16, Exercise 9.12]
. In addition, in the semi-Riemannian context it is well-known that both geodesic completeness and how the existence of conjugate points is affected by sectional curvatures depend on the so-called
causal character
of the geodesics
[
3
]
, and furthermore many of the most interesting geometric examples in Lorentzian geometry — arguably the most important semi-Riemannian type of geometry apart from the Riemannian one — are geodesically incomplete
[
16
, Ch. 5]
.

These issues notwithstanding, there have been a number of concrete alternative results in these broader contexts that still can be regard as “Hadamard-like” theorems. For example, Flaherty
[
12
]
, studying the particular case of Lorentzian manifolds, applied the hypotheses of geodesic completeness and sign for the sectional curvatures only in timelike directions, though at the cost of introducing the fairly strong hypothesis of
future 1-connectedness
. i.e., that any two timelike curves starting and ending at the same points can be homotopically deformed through timelike intermediate curves.

Theorem 1.1

(Flaherty)
.

Let
M
be a time-oriented, causally future-complete Lorentzian manifold which is also future 1-connected. Further suppose that the sectional curvature is nonpositive for all timelike planes. Then the exponential map regularly embeds the future timecone at each point into
M
.

Observe that having hypotheses only in the timelike directions entails that the result affects only timecones, a kind of “partial covering map” result.

A different approach was taken by Beem and Parker
[
5
]
, who obtained a result applicable to the broader class of affine manifolds
(M,\nabla)
. As hypotheses, they directly impose the absence of pairs of conjugate points along all geodesics — a property that follows from nonpositivity of sectional curvatures in the Riemannian case — and ensure a suitable path-lifting property in lieu of completeness, via the concepts of
disprisonment
and
pseudoconvexity
of the family of all geodesics of
(M,\nabla)
. These are standard geometric constraints meant to control the behavior of the geodesic flow and other dynamical systems. (See the references in
[
5
]
for an extensive list of classic applications). However, since (a transversal version of) disprisonment and pseudoconvexity will turn out to be important for us in this paper, we briefly recall the latter notions here.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(M,\nabla)
be an affine manifold and consider a family
\mathcal{C}
of non-constant
\nabla
-geodesics. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is

•

pseudoconvex
if for every compact set
K\subset M
there is another compact set
K^{*}\subset M
such that if any segment of
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}
has its endpoints in
K
, then that segment is entirely contained in
K^{*}
;

•

disprisoning
if given the maximal extension
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(a,b)\rightarrow M
of a geodesic in
\mathcal{C}
and any
t_{0}\in(a,b)
, both
\gamma|_{(a,t_{0}]}
and
\gamma|_{[t_{0},b)}
fail to have compact closure.

If the class
\mathcal{C}
of
all
geodesics is pseudoconvex and disprisoning, then we say that
(M,\nabla)
itself is pseudoconvex and disprisoning.

Theorem 1.3

(Beem–Parker)
.

Let
(M^{n},\nabla)
be both pseudoconvex and disprisoning, and suppose that no inextendible geodesic therein has a pair of conjugate points. Then
(M,\nabla)
is geodesically connected and, for each
p\in M
, the exponential map
\exp_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{D}_{p}\subset T_{p}M\rightarrow M
is a smooth covering map. In particular, the universal covering manifold of
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Our goal in this paper is to give a foliation-theoretic analogue of the Beem–Parker result described above. A key antecedent to our work is a well-known result by Hebda
[
17
]
, who obtained a
transverse
version of Hadamard’s theorem for
Riemannian
foliations,
i.e.
a foliated manifold endowed with a Riemannian
bundle-like metric
, which is a Riemannian metric
h
such that
h(X,Y)
is a basic function whenever
X,Y
are projectable fields
g
-orthogonal to
\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Hebda)
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a Riemannian, codimension
q
foliation with a complete bundle-like metric
h
. If the sectional curvature function of
h
is nonpositive, then the universal cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
fibers over a complete simply-connected Riemannian
q
-manifold
N
of nonpositive curvature. Moreover, each fiber in this fibration is diffeomorphic to the universal covering of a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, because the fibration is trivial since
N\simeq\mathbb{R}^{q}
is contractible,
\tilde{M}
is diffeomorphic to
\tilde{L}\times N
.

Observe that Hebda’s result reduces to the standard Hadamard’s theorem for a Riemannian manifold
(M,h)
when the foliation is zero-dimensional, that is, when it is the trivial foliation of
M
by its points.

Our work can be alternatively construed as an affine-geometric version of Hebda’s result. However, as we shall see in more detail below, there are in the literature many alternative, not necessarily equivalent notions of what a
transversely affine foliation
should be. Therefore, we divide the paper in two parts. In the first part, we give an extended discussion of, and develop a new machinery to deal with, affine aspects of the transverse geometry of foliations. In particular, since we are interested in (a suitable notion) of transverse geodesics, instead of dealing with a connection on the normal bundle
\nu\mathcal{F}
of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
we introduce a novel concept of a connection on the underlying manifold itself with suitable “bundle-like” properties. Far from being a mere transcription of already known data into another ambiance, the introduction of the so-called
transverse affine connections
and
transverse affine structures
allow us to deal with perfect transverse analogues of classical objects of affine geometry such as parallel transport, geodesics, curvature and Jacobi fields. Since to the best of our knowledge much of this material is new, we discuss it in great detail here.

Our main result, which we shall prove towards the end of the paper, is a mixture of the “Hadamard-like” Beem-Parker result we mentioned above with a conclusion resembling Hebda’s:

Theorem A
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliation whose leaves are all compact and such that
M/\mathcal{F}
is Hausdorff. Suppose
(M,\mathcal{F}
) is endowed with a transverse affine structure such that the family of all non-vertical transverse-geodesics is transversely pseudoconvex, transversely disprisoning and without transverse conjugate points. Then the universal cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
splits diffeomorphically as a product
\tilde{L}\times B
, where
B
is a contractible manifold and
\tilde{L}
is the universal cover of some leaf
L
of
\mathcal{F}
, so that the lifted foliation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is given by the fibers of
\tilde{M}\rightarrow B
.

In addition, if we abide by the philosophy – common in the foliation-theoretic lore – of “transverse-geometry-equals-leaf-space-geometry”, then this part of our work can also be understood as a high-regularity technology to do geometry in a low-regularity setting. As a key application of the latter philosophy, we consider
orbifolds
, which are well-known generalizations of manifolds (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
for an introduction with extensive references) but can also be naturally realized as leaf spaces of foliations with compact leaves. Our results here allow us to consider orbifolds endowed with an affine structure, and we then obtain a generalization of the Beem-Parker result to this context.

Corollary B

(Affine Hadamard theorem for orbifolds)
.

Let
(\mathcal{O},\nabla)
be an affine orbifold without conjugate points and such that the family of its geodesics is pseudoconvex and disprisoning. Then
\mathcal{O}
is a good (developable) orbifold whose universal covering is a contractible manifold.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we recall a number of basic foliation-theoretic concepts we shall use, mainly to establish notation and terminology. In section
3
we give an extended description of the main different notions of transverse affine geometry on foliations found in the literature and their mutual relationship, and introduce our own, showing how they relate to previous ones. In section
4
we discuss geodesic-related notions, especially a notion of
transverse-geodesic
, or geodesic for the transverse geometry, with associated notions of Jacobi fields and conjugate points thereon. Finally, in section
5
we give the technical details and proof of the main theorem described above, together with its corollary for orbifolds.

[X^{\ast},Y^{\ast}]_{M}
is a lift of
[X,Y]_{N}
and hence

\phi([X,Y]_{N})=\overline{[X^{\ast},Y^{\ast}]_{N}}=[\overline{X^{\ast}},\overline{Y^{\ast}}]_{\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F})}\equiv[\phi(X),\phi(Y)]_{\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F})}.

(iv)

Assume that all fibers of
F
are connected, and fix
Z\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})
. We define
X\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
as follows. Given
x\in N
, pick any
z\in F^{-1}(x)
, and put
X_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=dF_{z}(Z_{z})
. To see this is well-defined pick
f\in C^{\infty}(N)
, and define the smooth real-valued function
\xi\in C^{\infty}(M)
given by

\xi(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=Z_{z}(f\circ F)=dF_{z}(Z_{z}),\quad\forall z\in M.

We claim that
\xi
is basic: since
f
was taken arbitrarily this does imply that
X
is well-defined since
F^{-1}(x)
, being connected, coincides with a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
. Thus, pick
V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})
. We have

V\xi=[V,Z]_{M}(f\circ F)+ZV(f\circ F).

But since
[V,Z]_{M}
is vertical,

([V,Z]_{M})_{z}(f\circ F)=dF_{z}(([V,Z]_{M})_{x})\equiv 0,

and similarly we have
V(f\circ F)\equiv 0
. whence we conclude that
V\xi\equiv 0
, and the claim follows, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

With the conditions and notations of the previous Lemma, some added comments are in order.

a)

Projectable vector fields carry a natural

b)

In his celebrated paper

on Riemannian submersions, O’Neill refers to a vector field on

are not connected. For example, if

with the non-negative

such that

be given as

-\varphi(y)\cdot\partial/\partial y,\text{ if $x<0$},\\
0,\text{ if $y<0$.}\end{cases}

Then,

is projectable, but not a lift of a vector field on
\mathbb{R}
.

A
(0,s)
-tensor

is said to be

)

. A very important special case is the following: a symmetric

(ii)\;g_{\intercal}(X,V)=0,\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\iff V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})

.

For the rest of the paper, whenever we do computations in local coordinates we shall adopt the Einstein’s summation convention: repeated indices indicate summation. However, we slightly modify it as follows. Letters in the beginning of the Latin alphabet

denote summation from 1 through

.

3.
Transverse affine geometry

What we understand here as an
affine manifold
is simply a pair
(M,\nabla)
where
\nabla
is an affine connection on
M
, i.e., a linear connection on
TM
. In particular every semi-Riemannian manifold is an affine manifold when equipped with the associated Levi-Civita connection of the metric. Our goal here is to discuss a transverse analogue of this notion for foliations, thus defining the notion of an
transversely affine foliation
. We fix throughout a foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F})
.

Now, attempts to define an appropriate notion of a transversely affine foliation are not new in the foliation literature, so we briefly review some of these here. In
[
13
]
, for instance, the authors posit that
\mathcal{F}
is a
transversely affine foliation
when the transition diffeomorphisms of a Haefliger cocycle defining
\mathcal{F}
are affine functions of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
. That is, for each
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
such that
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\beta}\neq\emptyset
, there are
A_{\alpha\beta}\in GL(q,\mathbb{R)}
and
\textbf{b}_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
such that
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\textbf{x})=A_{\alpha\beta}\text{x}+\textbf{b}_{\alpha\beta},\forall\textbf{x}\in s_{\alpha}(\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\beta})
. However, this definition is too restrictive: it is implicitly only dealing with the
(canonical) flat connection
on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
. A more general notion of a transverse affine structure can be found in Wolak’s work
[
31
]
, albeit with another name and merged with additional structures. The idea is that the maps defining a Haefliger cocycle
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}
are all (general)
affine transformations
between open sets of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
in the following more general sense. For each
\alpha\in\Lambda
one assumes there are defined affine connections
\underaccent{\check}{\nabla}^{\alpha}
on
\underaccent{\check}{\mathcal{U}}_{\alpha}
such that whenever
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}
exists, it satisfies
\gamma^{*}_{\alpha\beta}\underaccent{\check}{\nabla}^{\beta}=\underaccent{\check}{\nabla}^{\alpha}
, where
\nabla^{\ast}
denote the pullback connection
3
3

3

Recall that if
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\rightarrow N
is a diffeomorphism and
\nabla
is an affine connection on
N
, the
pullback
of
\nabla
is the affine connection
\nabla^{\ast}
on
M
given by the formula

F_{\ast}(\nabla^{\ast}_{X}Y)=\nabla_{F_{\ast}X}F_{\ast}Y,\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

. The latter definition indeed accords well with Haefliger’s well-known pseudogroup approach to transverse geometry of foliations
[
14
,
15
,
22
]
, and we shall essentially adopt it in this work. However, it has the drawback that a proof of the independence of the choice of a Haefliger’s cocycle is not obvious. Accordingly, we shall seek an equivalent geometric formulation of Wolak’s definition which is explicitly independent of that choice.

Remark 3.1
.

There is an invariant, natural geometric context behind Wolak’s approach that can be traced back to the theory of
basic/projectable connections on transverse frame bundles
developed by Molino
[
23
]
, Bott
[
7
]
, Kamber and Tondeur
[
18
]
. Again the interest of making this discussion relatively self-contained, we briefly recall its main points here.

Let
F_{\intercal}(M,\mathcal{F})
denote the
GL(q,\mathbb{R})
-principal bundle of all frames on
\nu\mathcal{F}
. The foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
M
induces a foliation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
of the same dimension on
F_{\intercal}(M,\mathcal{F})
, called the
lifted foliation
(see
[
22
, Example 4.19]
). A principal connection
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{X}(F_{\intercal}(M,\mathcal{F}))\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(q,\mathbb{R})
on this bundle is said to be
transverse
(see
[
23
, Sec. 2.5]
) if
\omega(X)=0
for every vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(F_{\intercal}(M,\mathcal{F}))
which is tangent to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
. That is to say,
\omega
is transverse when it annihilates any
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
-vertical vector. This is also what Kamber and Tondeur call an
adapted connection
. These authors show
[
18
, Lemma 2.7]
that such an object corresponds to a linear connection
D
on
\nu\mathcal{F}
. Furthermore, Molino, in Ref.
[
23
, Sec. 2.5]
says that
\omega
is a
projectable connection
if it is a basic 1-form with respect to the lifted foliation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
, that is, it is both transverse/adapted and holonomy-invariant. In the work of Kamber and Tondeur, this corresponds to the concept of a
basic connection
, and these authors show that such an object exists precisely when the foliation is transversely affine in Wolak’s sense
[
31
]
.

Again, a disadvantage of the approach via basic connections on the principal bundle of transverse frames is that it gives a somewhat opaque framework to treat geodesics. Accordingly, we seek another alternative, and show it gives the same information as basic connections.

A natural starting point would be to consider linear connections
D\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Gamma(\nu\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\Gamma(\nu\mathcal{F})
on the normal bundle of
\mathcal{F}
. Recall the Lie derivative along a
projectable
vector field
X\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})
was defined in the previous section (conf. (
1
)). We now define

(3)

(\mathcal{L}_{X}D)_{Z}\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{L}_{X}(D_{Z}\sigma)-D_{[X,Z]}\sigma-D_{Z}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\sigma),\quad\forall Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

It is easy to see
\mathcal{L}_{X}D
is
C^{\infty}(M)
-bilinear in its entries
Z,\sigma
. We might then try and say that
D
is holonomy-invariant if
\mathcal{L}_{V}D
is identically zero for any vertical vector field
V
. It turns out, however, that this definition would be unnecessarily restrictive, because it would affect
all
sections of the normal bundle. A better definition arises from the following simple fact whose proof is immediate from (
3
) if we recall that

\sigma\in\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F})\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{V}\sigma=0,\quad\forall V\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
D
denote a connection on the normal bundle
\nu\mathcal{F}
of the foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F})
. The following statements are equivalent.

i)

For any
X\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})
,
D_{X}\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F})\subset\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F})
. (In words: covariant derivatives of transverse vector fields along projectable vector fields are also transverse vector fields.)

ii)

(\mathcal{L}_{V}D)_{X}\sigma=0\quad\forall V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F}),\forall X\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F}),\forall\sigma\in\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F}).

\Box

The previous result provides a more appropriate notion of holonomy-invariance for connections on the normal bundle
\nu\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 3.3
.

A connection on the normal bundle
\nu\mathcal{F}
of the foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F})
is
holonomy-invariant
if one (and hence both) of the properties in Prop.
3.2
holds.

Example 3.4

(Basic Levi-Civita connections)
.

The normal bundle
\nu\mathcal{F}
is canonically equipped with a partial connection
\nabla^{\mathrm{Bott}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})\times\Gamma(\nu\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\Gamma(\nu\mathcal{F})
, the so-called
Bott connection
. This is given by
\nabla^{\mathrm{Bott}}_{V}\overline{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\overline{[V,X]},\forall V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F}),\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. If
(M,\mathcal{F},g_{\intercal})
is a semi-Riemannian foliation and we pick a bundle-like metric
g
with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
, then the Bott connection can be completed to a full connection on
\nu\mathcal{F}
, called the
basic (or transverse) Levi-Civita connection

\nabla^{\intercal}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Gamma(\nu\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\Gamma(\nu\mathcal{F})
given by:

\nabla^{\intercal}_{X}\overline{Y}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\nabla^{\mathrm{Bott}}_{X}\overline{Y},\text{ if }X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F});\\
\overline{\nabla_{X}Y},\text{ if }X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})^{\perp}.\end{cases}

One can easily check that the basic Levi-Civita connection does not depend on the specific bundle-like metric used. A long but straightforward computation shows that it is indeed holonomy-invariant in the sense of Definition
3.3
.

An important caveat of the previous definitions for us in this paper is that we are crucially interested in studying a transverse analogue of
geodesics
, and it turns out that these are best understood in terms of (standard) geodesics of an affine connections
on the manifold

M
,
not
on the normal bundle. We therefore need to introduce here the affine analogue of a bundle-like metric.

Definition 3.5

(Bundle-like affine structure)
.

A
bundle-like affine structure
on
(M,\mathcal{F})
is a pair
(\mathcal{H},\nabla)
, where
\nabla
is an affine connection on
M
and
\mathcal{H}\subset TM
is a horizontal distribution such that
\nabla_{\mathcal{H}X}\mathcal{H}Y
is projectable for every
X,Y\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})
.

It is clear that independently of the choice of the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
, the horizontal part of any projectable vector field is also projectable. Hence, given a bundle-like affine structure
(\mathcal{H},\nabla)
on
(M,\mathcal{F})
we induce a connection
\overline{\nabla}
on the normal bundle
\nu\mathcal{F}
by

\overline{\nabla}_{X}\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\overline{\nabla_{X}\mathcal{H}Z},\quad\forall\sigma\in\Gamma(\nu\mathcal{F}),\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(M),

where
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is any vector field such that
\sigma=\overline{Z}
. We then easily see that
\overline{\nabla}_{X}\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F})\subset\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F}),\forall X\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})
, and hence
\overline{\nabla}
is indeed holonomy-invariant in the sense of Definition
3.3
.

Recall that if
(M,\mathcal{F})
is endowed with a bundle-like
semi-Riemannian metric

g
, then it is locally given by semi-Riemannian submersions. As Abe and Hasegawa point out (more on that in Section
4
), one can then consider affine submersions with horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
given by the orthogonal complement with respect to the metric. The decomposition
TM=\mathcal{H}\oplus T\mathcal{F}
is well-defined since the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are semi-Riemannian submanifolds (see, for instance, chapter 4 of
[
28
]
). This means that a semi-Riemannian foliation will automatically define a bundle-like affine structure with
\nabla
chosen as the Levi-Civita connection.

One mildly annoying feature of bundle-like affine structures is their dependence on the specific choice of the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
. It would be interesting to seek out a definition of affine structure which is “purely transverse” in the sense of not depending on such a choice, because it is — unlike with semi-Riemannian foliations — somewhat
ad hoc
and highly non-unique. We thus propose the following definition.

Definition 3.6

(Transverse affine connection)
.

Let
\hat{\nabla}
be an affine connection on
M
. We say that
\hat{\nabla}
is a
transverse affine connection
(on
(M,\mathcal{F})
) if there is a torsion-free linear connection
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
, which we refer to as a
partner connection
, such that, for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and all
V\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
we have the properties:

(1)

\hat{\nabla}_{X}V=\omega_{X}V
;

(2)

\hat{\nabla}_{V}X=[V,X]+\omega_{X}V
;

(3)

\mathcal{L}_{V}\hat{\nabla}
is a
(1,2)
-tensor taking values on
\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
.

Note that the linear connection
\omega
in the previous definition is required to be torsion-free in order to make conditions
(1)
and
(2)
compatible when
\hat{\nabla}
is evaluated on two vertical vector fields. That is, if
V,W\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
we have that

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{V}W\overset{(1)}{=}\omega_{V}W\overset{\mathrm{torsion\mbox{-}free}}{=}[V,W]+\omega_{W}V\overset{(2)}{=}\hat{\nabla}_{V}W.

In particular, a given transverse affine connections induce a linear connection on the bundle
T\mathcal{F}
, to wit, its partner connection.

Condition (3) is meant to ensure that
\hat{\nabla}
again induces an holonomy-invariant connection on the normal bundle. Indeed, if
X,Y\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})
and
V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})
, then

(4)

\displaystyle\left[V,\hat{\nabla}_{X}Y\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\hat{\nabla}\right)(X,Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{[V,X]}Y+\hat{\nabla}_{X}[V,Y]

(5)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\hat{\nabla}\right)(X,Y)+[[V,X],Y]+\omega_{Y}[V,X]+\omega_{X}[V,Y],

which is everywhere vertical. Therefore, the covariant derivative of projectable vector fields with respect to a transverse affine connection is again a projectable field. In particular, if we choose
any
horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{H}X
is projectable whenever
X
is projectable, and therefore we have:

Proposition 3.7
.

If
\hat{\nabla}
is a transverse affine connection on
(M,\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{H}
is a horizontal distribution, then
(\hat{\nabla},\mathcal{H})
is a bundle-like affine structure on
(M,\mathcal{F})
.

\Box

Example 3.8
.

The converse of Prop.
3.7
is false in general.
For example, consider the 2-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{F}
given by the submersion
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
F(x,y,z)=z-x^{2}-y^{2}
. The Euclidean metric
g=dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}
is
not
a bundle-like metric with respect to
\mathcal{F}
, but its Levi-Civita connection, which is the canonical flat connection
\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}
produces a bundle-like affine structure together with
\mathcal{H}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
. To see that, we start by noting that

\displaystyle T\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}\left\{V_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+2x\frac{\partial}{\partial z},V_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+2y\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}\left\{H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-2x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-2y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right\}.

A simple computation shows that
H
is projectable, and
\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}_{H}H=-4\left(x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)
. And then,

\displaystyle[V_{1},\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}_{H}H]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+2x\frac{\partial}{\partial z},-4\left(x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)\right]=-4\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+8x\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=-4V_{1}

\displaystyle[V_{2},\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}_{H}H]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+2y\frac{\partial}{\partial z},-4\left(x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)\right]=-4\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+8y\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=-4V_{2},

which means that
\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}_{H}H
is projectable. However, we may compute, for example,

\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}}V_{1}=2\frac{\partial}{\partial z},

which is not vertical. Therefore,
\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}
is not an transverse affine connection. However, Proposition
3.10
will show later on how to produce a transverse affine connection, given a bundle-like affine structure.

Observe that adding any
(1,2)
-tensor which takes values on
\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
to a transverse affine connection will produce another transverse affine connection with the same transverse information, in the following sense: if
\hat{\nabla}^{1}
and
\hat{\nabla}^{2}
are two transverse affine connections which differ by a
(1,2)
-tensor field taking values on the vertical bundle, then it is clear that they must induce the same connection on
\nu\mathcal{F}
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.9

(Transverse affine structure)
.

A
transverse affine structure
on
(M,\mathcal{F})
is an equivalence class
[\hat{\nabla}]
of transverse affine connections with respect to the relation that identifies two such connections whenever their respective induced connections on
\nu\mathcal{F}
coincide.

The next result shows that Defs.
3.6
and
3.9
work in tandem to capture the same information as holonomy-invariant connections on the normal bundle.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold. The following statements hold.

i)

If
(M,\mathcal{F})
admits a bundle-like affine structure
(\mathcal{H},\nabla)
, then there exists a unique transverse affine structure
[\hat{\nabla}]
such that

\mathcal{H}\nabla_{X}Y=\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y,\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H}),\forall\overline{\nabla}\in[\hat{\nabla}].

ii)

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the collection of transverse affine structures on
(M,\mathcal{F})
and the set of holonomy-invariant connections on the normal bundle
\nu\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

(i)

Given a bundle-like affine structure
(\mathcal{H},\nabla)
, we define
\hat{\nabla}
by the formula

\hat{\nabla}_{X}Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{V}\left(\nabla_{X}\mathcal{V}Y\right)+[\mathcal{V}X,\mathcal{H}Y]+\mathcal{V}\left(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}Y}\mathcal{V}X\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}X}\mathcal{H}Y\right)\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

A straightforward computation shows that
\hat{\nabla}
is indeed a transverse affine connection with
\mathcal{V}\left(\nabla_{(\cdot)}\mathcal{V}(\cdot)\right)
defining a partner connection for it. In addition, it is clear that
[\hat{\nabla}]
satisfies the stated property as well.

(ii)

Denote by
\mathcal{TA}(\mathcal{F})
the set of transverse affine structures on
(M,\mathcal{F})
and by
\mathfrak{H}(\nu\mathcal{F})
the set of holonomy-invariant connections on
\nu\mathcal{F}
. It suffices to show that any holonomy-invariant connection on
\nu\mathcal{F}
is induced by a unique transverse affine structure. Choose a horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
and any affine connection
\nabla
on
M
. Observe that the choice of
\mathcal{H}
induces a
C^{\infty}(M)
-module isomorphism
\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gamma(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\Gamma(\nu\mathcal{F})
by
\Psi(X)=\overline{X},\forall X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
. Given
D\in\mathfrak{H}(\nu\mathcal{F})
, define the connection on
M
given by

\nabla^{D}_{X}Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{V}\nabla_{X}\mathcal{V}Y+\Psi^{-1}(D_{X}\Psi(\mathcal{H}Y)),\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

We claim that
(\mathcal{H},\nabla^{D})
is a bundle-like affine structure. If we can establish this, then by
(i)
the proof will be complete. To see it, let
X,Y\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})
, so that
\mathcal{H}X,\mathcal{H}Y\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})
as well, and we compute

\nabla^{D}_{\mathcal{H}X}\mathcal{H}Y=\Psi^{-1}(D_{\mathcal{H}X}\overline{\mathcal{H}Y})\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{F})

because
\overline{\mathcal{H}Y}\in\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F})
, and hence
D_{\mathcal{H}X}\overline{\mathcal{H}Y}\in\mathfrak{l}(\mathcal{F})
by holonomy-invariance.

Remark 3.15

Another interesting situation is when we have two foliated manifolds

and

\displaystyle(F^{*}\hat{\nabla}^{N})_{V}X

\displaystyle=[V,X]+(F^{*}\omega^{N})_{X}V.

\displaystyle=[V,\left(\nabla_{X}Y^{\top}\right)^{\top}+[X^{\top},Y^{\perp}]+\left(\nabla_{Y^{\perp}}X^{\top}\right)^{\top}+\left(\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Y^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}].

The bracket of
V
with the first three terms is clearly in
T\mathcal{F}
, so we are only left to show that
\left(\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Y^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}
is projectable. But this follows readily from the properties of semi-Riemannian submersions, which we can employ here locally, since
g
is a bundle-like metric for
(M,\mathcal{F})
.

The final step in the proof is to check the validity of properties
(i)
and
(ii)
. For
(i)
, we compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tor}(\hat{\nabla})(X,Y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\hat{\nabla}_{X}Y-\hat{\nabla}_{Y}X-[X,Y]

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla_{X}Y^{\top}\right)^{\top}-\left(\nabla_{Y}X^{\top}\right)^{\top}+[X^{\top},Y^{\perp}]-[Y^{\top},X^{\perp}]

\displaystyle\phantom{==}+\left(\nabla_{Y^{\perp}}X^{\top}\right)^{\top}-\left(\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Y^{\top}\right)^{\top}+\left(\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Y^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}-\left(\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Y^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}-[X,Y]

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla_{X^{\top}}Y^{\top}-\nabla_{Y^{\top}}X^{\top}\right)^{\top}+[X^{\top},Y^{\perp}]-[Y^{\top},X^{\perp}]+[X^{\perp},Y^{\perp}]^{\perp}-[X,Y]

\displaystyle=[X^{\top},Y^{\top}]^{\top}+[X^{\top},Y^{\perp}]+[X^{\perp},Y^{\top}]+[X^{\perp},Y^{\perp}]^{\perp}-[X,Y]

\displaystyle=-[X^{\perp},Y^{\perp}]^{\top}.

For
(ii)
we have

\displaystyle g_{\intercal}(\hat{\nabla}_{X}Y,Z)+g_{\intercal}(Y,\hat{\nabla}_{X}Z)

\displaystyle=g((\hat{\nabla}_{X}Y)^{\perp},Z^{\perp})+g(Y^{\perp},(\hat{\nabla}_{X}Z)^{\perp})

\displaystyle=g([X^{\top},Y^{\perp}]^{\perp}+(\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Y^{\perp})^{\perp},Z^{\perp})+g(Y^{\perp},[X^{\top},Z^{\perp}]^{\perp}+(\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Z^{\perp})^{\perp})

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Y^{\perp},Z^{\perp})+g(Y^{\perp},\nabla_{X^{\perp}}Z^{\perp})

\displaystyle\phantom{==}+g(\nabla_{X^{\top}}Y^{\perp}-\nabla_{Y^{\perp}}X^{\top},Z^{\perp})+g(Y^{\perp},\nabla_{X^{\top}}Z^{\perp}-\nabla_{Z^{\perp}}X^{\top})

\displaystyle=X^{\perp}g(Y^{\perp},Z^{\perp})+X^{\top}g(Y^{\perp},Z^{\perp})-Y^{\perp}\cancel{g(X^{\top},Z^{\perp})}-Z^{\perp}\cancel{g(X^{\top},Z^{\perp})}

\displaystyle\phantom{==}+g(X^{\top},\nabla_{Y^{\perp}}Z^{\perp}+\nabla_{Z^{\perp}}Y^{\perp})

\displaystyle=Xg(Y^{\perp},Z^{\perp})+\cancel{g(X^{\top},\nabla_{Y^{\perp}}Z^{\perp}+\nabla_{Z^{\perp}}Y^{\perp})}

\displaystyle=Xg_{\intercal}(Y,Z),

where the cancellation of the term in the penultimate line is due to the fact that
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of a bundle-like metric, and therefore
\nabla
-geodesics
g
-orthogonal to
T\mathcal{F}
at one point remain
g
-orthogonal everywhere, by transnormality. But by the discussion in Ref.
[
20
]
, this is tantamount to the distribution
T\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
being closed with respect to the
symmetric product

\langle E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\nabla_{E}F+\nabla_{F}E
.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

As any connection, a transverse affine connection
\hat{\nabla}
gives rise to a curvature tensor and a torsion tensor, which we denote respectively by
\hat{R}
and
\mathrm{Tor}\hat{\nabla}
. If
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
V,W\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
we have that:

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{Tor}\hat{\nabla})(V,X)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{Tor}\hat{\nabla})(X,V)=\hat{\nabla}_{X}V-\hat{\nabla}_{V}X-[X,V]

\displaystyle=\omega_{X}V-[V,X]-\omega_{X}V-[X,V]=0,

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{R}(V,Y)Z&=\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{\nabla}_{Y}Z-\hat{\nabla}_{Y}\hat{\nabla}_{V}Z-\hat{\nabla}_{[V,Y]}Z\\
&=[V,\hat{\nabla}_{Y}Z]+\omega_{\hat{\nabla}_{Y}Z}V-\hat{\nabla}_{Y}\left([V,Z]+\omega_{Z}V\right)-\hat{\nabla}_{[V,Y]}Z\\
&=\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\hat{\nabla}\right)(Y,Z)+\omega_{\hat{\nabla}_{Y}Z}V-\omega_{Y}\omega_{Z}V\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\hat{R}(V,W)Z

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{\nabla}_{W}Z-\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{\nabla}_{V}Z-\hat{\nabla}_{[V,W]}Z

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{V}\left([W,Z]+\omega_{Z}W\right)-\hat{\nabla}_{W}\left([V,Z]+\omega_{Z}V\right)-[[V,W],Z]-\omega_{Z}[V,W]

\displaystyle=[V,[W,Z]]+\omega_{[W,Z]}V+\omega_{V}\omega_{Z}W-[W,[V,Z]]-\omega_{[V,Z]}W

\displaystyle\phantom{==}-\omega_{W}\omega_{Z}V-[[V,W],Z]-\omega_{Z}[V,W]

\displaystyle=\omega_{[W,Z]}V+\omega_{V}\omega_{Z}W-\omega_{[V,Z]}W-\omega_{W}\omega_{Z}V-\omega_{Z}[V,W]

\displaystyle=\omega_{[W,Z]}V+\omega_{V}\omega_{Z}W-\omega_{[V,Z]}W-\omega_{W}\omega_{Z}V-\omega_{Z}\left(\omega_{V}W-\omega_{W}V\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\omega_{[W,Z]}-\omega_{W}\omega_{Z}+\omega_{Z}\omega_{W}\right)V-\left(\omega_{[V,Z]}-\omega_{V}\omega_{Z}+\omega_{Z}\omega_{V}\right)W

\displaystyle=R^{\omega}(V,Z)W-R^{\omega}(W,Z)V.

These formulae will soon be useful, when we deal with the transverse analogues of geodesics and Jacobi fields.

4.
Geodesics, transverse-geodesics and transverse Jacobi fields

As mentioned above, the main motivation behind the definitions of bundle-like and transverse affine structure as given in the previous section is they can be adapted to the study of (transverse analogues of)
geodesics
. In order to motivate our definition, and since regular foliations are locally defined by submersions, we start by reviewing here what is already known about geodesics for the specific case of submersions with a transverse affine geometry. We then show how these insights can be generalized to transversely affine foliations.

Blumenthal
[
6
]
studies a submersion
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\rightarrow N
between affine manifolds
(M,\nabla^{M})
and
(N,\nabla^{N})
, and calls
F
an
affine submersion
if the derivative map
F_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}TM\rightarrow TN
commutes with the parallel transports of
\nabla^{M}
and
\nabla^{N}
. However, Abe and Hasegawa
[
1
]
show that Blumenthal’s concept is too restrictive to generalize the well-known notion of (semi-)Riemannian submersion as studied by O’Neill in
[
26
]
. Indeed, with Blumenthal’s definition,
every
geodesic on
(M,\nabla^{M})
is mapped onto a geodesic on
(N,\nabla^{N})
, not only those tangent to a distinguished horizontal distribution (in the Riemannian case, the horizontal distribution is of course the distribution normal to the leaves). Abe and Hasegawa introduce instead the notion of an
affine submersion with a horizontal distribution
as a submersion between affine manifolds
(M,\nabla^{M})
and
(N,\nabla^{N})
, together with a fixed horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}\subset TM
such that

\mathcal{H}\left(\nabla^{M}_{\widetilde{X}}\widetilde{Y}\right)=\widetilde{\left(\nabla^{N}_{X}Y\right)},

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
, where the tilde denotes the unique lift of the corresponding vector field which is tangent to
\mathcal{H}
. (If there is no risk of confusion, we shall often use the symbols
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
both for the horizontal and vertical distributions themselves, as well as for the corresponding projections of vectors onto them.)

The existence of such a distribution
\mathcal{H}
allows one to reproduce O’Neill’s well-known theory of (semi-)Riemannian submersions in
[
26
]
. Indeed, we can define the analogues of the O’Neill tensors
A,T\in\mathcal{I}^{1}_{2}(M)
:

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{E}F&=\mathcal{H}\left(\nabla^{M}_{\mathcal{V}E}\mathcal{V}F\right)+\mathcal{V}\left(\nabla^{M}_{\mathcal{V}E}\mathcal{H}F\right),\\
A_{E}F&=\mathcal{H}\left(\nabla^{M}_{\mathcal{H}E}\mathcal{V}F\right)+\mathcal{V}\left(\nabla^{M}_{\mathcal{H}E}\mathcal{H}F\right).\end{split}

Just in the semi-Riemannian case, it is readily seen that
T_{E}
and
A_{E}
interchange
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
and, moreover, that
T_{E}=T_{\mathcal{V}E}
and
A_{E}=A_{\mathcal{H}E}
. However, since
\nabla^{M}
is not necessarily torsion-free, we cannot conclude that
T_{U}V=T_{V}U
for vertical fields
U
and
V
. Furthermore, since there is in principle no underlying metric with which
\nabla^{M}
is compatible, it need not be true that
A_{X}Y=-A_{Y}X
for horizontal fields
X
and
Y
, nor does it make sense to discuss the skew-symmetry of
A_{E}
and
T_{E}
. This boils down to the fact that the equations for the horizontal and vertical parts of the acceleration of a curve on
M
retain terms which vanish on the corresponding equations in O’Neill’s work. However,
[
1
, Corollary 4.2]
it is still true that the projections of
horizontal
geodesics are always geodesics. Moreover, the tensor
A
governs how much the submersion deviates from the behavior of a semi-Riemannian submersion:

Proposition 4.1

(
[
1
, Corollary 4.3]
)
.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(M,\nabla^{M})\rightarrow(N,\nabla^{N})
be an affine submersion with horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
such that
A_{Z}Z=0
for all horizontal vectors
Z
. Then, every horizontal lift of a geodesic of
N
is a geodesic of
M
.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
1
, Proposition 4.4]
)
.

Every geodesic of
M
which has a horizontal tangent vector is always horizontal if and only if
A_{Z}Z=0
for any horizontal vector
Z
.

Our notion of bundle-like affine structure is the direct generalization of Abe-Hasagawa’s notion, and essentially reduces to the latter when discussing globally defined submersions. Now, since the formulae (
11
) make use only of the connection on
M
, they can be reproduced
ipsis literis
in the setting of a foliated manifold endowed with a bundle-like affine structure
(\mathcal{H},\nabla)
, and we shall henceforth always assume this to be the case. We proceed to see how it can be used to obtain generalizations of their results to more general foliated manifolds.

Remark 4.3
.

Since geodesics and their transverse generalization — to be defined later on — will play an important role in the theory developed here, it is important to ensure that they behave just as in the context of semi-Riemannian submersions, where horizontal geodesics project to, and are lifts of, geodesics in the base. As we have previously pointed out, this is governed by the vanishing of the tensor
A
. Therefore, given a distribution
\mathcal{H}
and a transverse affine connection
\hat{\nabla}
, we introduce the
recalibration tensor (determined by
\hat{\nabla}
and
\mathcal{H}
)

\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\rightarrow\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
given by

\Omega(X,Y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}X}\mathcal{H}Y+\hat{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}Y}\mathcal{H}X\right).

Given that
\Omega
is clearly tensorial,
\overline{\nabla}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\hat{\nabla}+\Omega
defines a new affine connection, and since
\Omega
is also symmetric, it is easy to see that
\overline{\nabla}
is again a
transverse
affine connection. In fact, since
\overline{\nabla}
and
\hat{\nabla}
differ by a tensor taking values in the vertical bundle, it follows that
[\overline{\nabla}]=[\hat{\nabla}]
. Note that if
\overline{A}
is the
A
-tensor arising from
\mathcal{H}
and
\overline{\nabla}
, given
H\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
we have that

\displaystyle\overline{A}_{H}H

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}\left(\overline{\nabla}_{H}H\right)=\mathcal{V}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{H}H+\Omega(H,H)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{H}H\right)-\mathcal{V}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{H}H\right)

\displaystyle=0.

We thus conclude that given any distribution
\mathcal{H}
such that
TM=\mathcal{H}\oplus T\mathcal{F}
, there is some
\overline{\nabla}\in[\hat{\nabla}]
such that, not only
(\mathcal{H},\overline{\nabla})
is a bundle-like affine structure, but it also satisfies the analogue of the so called
transnormality property
of semi-Riemannian foliations: if
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(a,b)\rightarrow M
is a
\overline{\nabla}
-geodesic such that for some
t_{0}\in(a,b)
the vector
\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})
is in
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma(t_{0})}
, then
\gamma^{\prime}(t)\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma(t)},\forall t\in(a,b)
.

Example 4.4
.

We illustrate the previous consideration via a simple example. On
\mathbb{R}^{3}
we fix Cartesian global coordinates
t,x,y
and
\mathcal{F}
is the one-dimensional foliation such that
T\mathcal{F}
is spanned by
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
. A bundle-like affine structure for
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathcal{F})
is given, for example, by

\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathrm{span}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right),\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}\right),

where
\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}
is the standard flat connection. It is trivial to see that
\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}
is a transverse affine connection, and hence it defines a transverse affine structure
[\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}]
. Conversely, we obtain all other bundle-like affine structures associated with
[\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}]
as follows. Note that two vector fields
X=X^{t}\partial_{t}+X^{x}\partial_{x}+X^{y}\partial_{y}
and
Y=Y^{t}\partial_{t}+Y^{x}\partial_{x}+Y^{y}\partial_{y}
span a distribution
\mathcal{H}
complementary to
T\mathcal{F}
if and only if we have
X^{t}Y^{x}-X^{x}Y^{t}\neq 0
at every point. Suppose this happens, and pick any vector field
Z=Z^{t}\partial_{t}+Z^{x}\partial_{x}+Z^{y}\partial_{y}
. Then we can also write
Z
as

Z=\frac{Z^{x}Y^{t}-Z^{t}Y^{x}}{Y^{t}X^{x}-Y^{x}X^{t}}X+\frac{Z^{t}X^{x}-Z^{x}X^{t}}{Y^{t}X^{x}-Y^{x}X^{t}}Y+\left(Z^{y}-\frac{Z^{x}(Y^{t}X^{y}-X^{t}Y^{y})+Z^{t}(X^{x}Y^{y}-Y^{x}X^{y})}{Y^{t}X^{x}-Y^{x}X^{t}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y},

so that

\mathcal{H}Z=Z^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+Z^{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\underbrace{\frac{Z^{x}(Y^{t}X^{y}-X^{t}Y^{y})+Z^{t}(X^{x}Y^{y}-Y^{x}X^{y})}{Y^{t}X^{x}-Y^{x}X^{t}}}_{=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\tilde{Z}^{y}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

If
W=W^{t}\partial_{t}+W^{x}\partial_{x}+W^{y}\partial_{y}
, then

\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}_{\mathcal{H}Z}\mathcal{H}W=\left(Z^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+Z^{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\tilde{Z}^{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)\left(W^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+W^{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\tilde{W}^{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right).

Let
U=U^{t}\partial_{t}+U^{x}\partial_{x}+U^{y}\partial_{y}
be equal to
\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}_{\mathcal{H}Z}\mathcal{H}W+\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}_{\mathcal{H}W}\mathcal{H}Z
, so that

\displaystyle U^{t}

\displaystyle=Z^{t}\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial t}+Z^{x}\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial x}+\tilde{Z}^{y}\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial y}+W^{t}\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial t}+W^{x}\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial x}+\tilde{W}^{y}\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial y},

\displaystyle U^{x}

\displaystyle=Z^{t}\frac{\partial W^{x}}{\partial t}+Z^{x}\frac{\partial W^{x}}{\partial x}+\tilde{Z}^{y}\frac{\partial W^{x}}{\partial y}+W^{t}\frac{\partial Z^{x}}{\partial t}+W^{x}\frac{\partial Z^{x}}{\partial x}+\tilde{W}^{y}\frac{\partial Z^{x}}{\partial y},

\displaystyle U^{y}

\displaystyle=Z^{t}\frac{\partial\tilde{W}^{y}}{\partial t}+Z^{x}\frac{\partial\tilde{W}^{y}}{\partial x}+\tilde{Z}^{y}\frac{\partial\tilde{W}^{y}}{\partial y}+W^{t}\frac{\partial\tilde{Z}^{y}}{\partial t}+W^{x}\frac{\partial\tilde{Z}^{y}}{\partial x}+\tilde{W}^{y}\frac{\partial\tilde{Z}^{y}}{\partial y}.

We thus define
\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\nabla^{\mathrm{flat}}+\Omega
, where

\Omega(Z,W)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}U=-\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{y}-\frac{U^{x}(Y^{t}X^{y}-X^{t}Y^{y})+U^{t}(X^{x}Y^{y}-Y^{x}X^{y})}{Y^{t}X^{x}-Y^{x}X^{t}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

For a concrete example, let
X=\partial_{t}+\cos(t)\partial_{y}
and
Y=\partial_{x}
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle Y^{t}X^{y}-X^{t}Y^{y}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle X^{x}Y^{y}-Y^{x}X^{y}

\displaystyle=-\cos(t),

\displaystyle Y^{t}X^{x}-Y^{x}X^{t}

\displaystyle=-1,

which yields
\tilde{Z}^{y}=\cos(t)Z^{t}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Omega(Z,W)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{y}-\cos(t)U^{t}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left[Z^{t}\left(-\sin(t)W^{t}+\cos(t)\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial t}\right)+\cos(t)Z^{x}\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial x}+\cos^{2}(t)Z^{t}\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial y}\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{==}+W^{t}\left(-\sin(t)Z^{t}+\cos(t)\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial t}\right)+\cos(t)W^{x}\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial x}+\cos^{2}(t)W^{t}\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial y}

\displaystyle\phantom{==}-\left.\cos(t)\left(Z^{t}\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial t}+Z^{x}\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial x}+\cos(t)Z^{t}\frac{\partial W^{t}}{\partial y}+W^{t}\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial t}+W^{x}\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial x}+\cos(t)W^{t}\frac{\partial Z^{t}}{\partial y}\right)\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left[-2\sin(t)Z^{t}W^{t}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=\sin(t)Z^{t}W^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

And thus we have that

\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}_{Z}W=\left(Z^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+Z^{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+Z^{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)\left(W^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+W^{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+W^{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)+\sin(t)Z^{t}W^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

Note that, as expected, we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}_{X}X

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\cos(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\cos(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)+\sin(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=-\sin(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\sin(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=0,

which means, in particular, that a curve such as
\gamma(s)=(s+a,b,\sin(s)+c)
is a
\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}
-geodesic.

4.1.
Transverse-geodesics

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold. Given a transverse affine connection
\hat{\nabla}
on
(M,\mathcal{F})
, a
\hat{\nabla}
-geodesic is defined in the usual fashion. By Remark
3.17
the torsion of
\hat{\nabla}
vanishes when evaluated on vertical vector fields. This implies that if
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
is a partner connection for
\hat{\nabla}
, it is also a partner connection for its
symmetric part

\hat{\nabla}^{\text{Sym}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\hat{\nabla}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Tor}\hat{\nabla})
. To check that the Lie derivative of
\hat{\nabla}^{\text{Sym}}
along vertical directions is indeed a tensor taking values in the vertical bundle, it suffices to see that
[V,(\mathrm{Tor}\hat{\nabla})(X,Y)]
is vertical for
V\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
. Again, by tensoriality, we can assume that
X,Y
are projectable, and hence the desired relation readily follows. In conclusion, when dealing with geodesics we may always assume
\hat{\nabla}
is torsion-free. However, since the object of geometric significance for us is not
\hat{\nabla}

per se
, but the transverse affine structure
[\hat{\nabla}]
defined by it, we must introduce a new type of curve.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\hat{\nabla}
be a transverse affine connection on
(M,\mathcal{F})
and
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I\rightarrow M
a smooth curve. We say that
\gamma
is a
\hat{\nabla}
-transverse-geodesic
if
\left(\hat{\nabla}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}\right)(t)
is a vertical vector for all
t\in I
.

Remark 4.6
.

Ambiguity in terminology notwithstanding, we emphasize that transverse-geodesics should
not
be automatically regarded as “geodesics which are transverse”, but rather as an atomic term with independent meaning, at least for the time being. In fact, it is easy to see that given the properties of a transverse affine connection, any curve which is contained within some leaf satisfies the definition of a transverse-geodesic. In the case of submersions, any such curve represents a constant geodesics in the base manifold, and as such will be systematically disregarded in most results later on.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
[\hat{\nabla}]
be a transverse affine structure on
(M,\mathcal{F})
and let
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I\rightarrow M
be a smooth curve. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

There exists a transverse affine connection
\nabla^{\prime}\in[\hat{\nabla}]
such that
\gamma
is a
\nabla^{\prime}
-transverse-geodesic;

(2)

\gamma
is a
\nabla
-transverse-geodesic for all
\nabla\in[\hat{\nabla}]
;

If moreover,
\gamma
admits a distribution
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathbb{R}\gamma^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{H}
with
TM=T\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{H}
, then the statements above are also equivalent to

(3)

There exists a transverse affine connection
\nabla\in[\hat{\nabla}]
such that
\gamma
is a
\nabla
-geodesic.

Proof.

(2)\implies(1)
and
(3)\implies(1)
are trivial.

(1)\implies(2)
. Given any
\nabla\in[\hat{\nabla}]
, by definition, there is a
(1,2)
-tensor field
T
taking values on
\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
such that
\nabla=\nabla^{\prime}+T
. We thus have that
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\nabla^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}+T(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
, which is a vertical vector everywhere.

(1)\implies(3)
. Consider the
(1,2)
-tensor field
\Omega
given by
\Omega(X,Y)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}\left(\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}X}\mathcal{H}Y+\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}Y}\mathcal{H}X\right)
and set
\nabla=\nabla^{\prime}+\Omega
. We thus have that

\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\nabla^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}-\mathcal{V}\left(\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}\gamma^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}\gamma^{\prime}\right)=\nabla^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}-\nabla^{\prime}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=0.\qed

Remark 4.8
.

For each
t_{0}\in I
, there is
\varepsilon>0
such that for
\gamma|_{(t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}+\varepsilon)}
satisfies the property of the existence of a distribution
\mathcal{H}
with
\mathbb{R}\gamma^{\prime}|_{(t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}+\varepsilon)}\subset\mathcal{H}
and
TM=T\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{H}
. However, this cannot be warranted globally in every situation, as it may happen that there are
t_{0},\ldots,t_{q}\in I
such that
\gamma(t_{0})=\ldots=\gamma(t_{q})
and
\gamma(t_{0}),\ldots,\gamma(t_{q})
are all linearly independent in
T_{\gamma(t_{0})}M
.

Definition 4.9
.

A nowhere vertical smooth curve
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I\rightarrow M
is said to be a
[\hat{\nabla}]
-
transverse-geodesic
if any, and hence all, of the statements of Proposition
4.7
hold.

Corollary 4.10
.

If a curve
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I\rightarrow M
is a

Transverse Jacobi fields

For a semi-Riemannian submersion

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\left(E^{\prime\prime}+R(E,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}\left(\mathfrak{D}(E)^{\prime}\right)+T_{\mathfrak{D}(E)}\gamma^{\prime}

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\left(E^{\prime\prime}+R(E,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(E_{*}^{\prime\prime}+R_{*}(E_{*},\sigma^{\prime})\sigma^{\prime}\right)\widetilde{}\ +2A_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{D}(E)),

[\hat{\nabla}]
-transverse-geodesic

admits a distribution

\hat{\nabla}
and
\mathcal{H}

\nabla

Remark 4.15
.

Even though equations (
13
) and (
14
) are not

is chosen such that

Now, suppose

is any vector field. We denote by a prime the covariant derivative with respect to
\nabla
, whereas the one with respect to
\hat{\nabla}
will be denoted by a dot. A straightforward computation will lead us to:

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{V}\left(J^{\prime\prime}+R(J,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}\right)&=\mathcal{V}\left(\ddot{J}+\hat{R}(J,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}\right)-2\Omega(\gamma^{\prime},[J,\gamma^{\prime}])\\

In the case

is given by the connected fibers of a submersion, it is revealing to compare the second equation above with equation (

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}\left(\ddot{J}+\hat{R}(J,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}\right)=\left(J_{*}^{\prime\prime}+R_{*}(J_{*},\sigma^{\prime})\sigma^{\prime}\right)\widetilde{}\ .\end{split}

Therefore

is a Jacobi field on

is a transverse Jacobi field in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 4.16
.

Let
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I\rightarrow M
be a non-vertical
\hat{\nabla}
-transverse-geodesic and

is a
\hat{\nabla}
-

if for every

we have that

\hat{\mathcal{J}}(J)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\ddot{J}(t)+\hat{R}_{\gamma(t)}\left(J(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right)\gamma^{\prime}(t)

is a vertical vector.

If
\tilde{\nabla}\in[\hat{\nabla}]
, then
\hat{\nabla}=\tilde{\nabla}+T
for some
(1,2)
-tensor

taking values in

. Therefore, it is easily seen that given a
\hat{\nabla}
-transverse Jacobi field

will also be everywhere vertical, which in turn means that being a transverse Jacobi field is a property valued for any representative of the class
[\hat{\nabla}]
. Thus we say that

is a
[\hat{\nabla}]
-transverse Jacobi field when it is a
\tilde{\nabla}
-transverse Jacobi field for some (and hence for any)

.

Remark 4.17
.

Equations (
15

is a transverse Jacobi field it is clear that given any distribution

we must have

Moreover, if

), we have that

\displaystyle\ddot{V}+\hat{R}(V,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\hat{\nabla}_{\gamma^{\prime}}V+\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\hat{\nabla}\right)(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})+\omega_{\cancel{\hat{\nabla}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}}}V-\omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}\omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}V

\displaystyle=\cancel{\omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}\omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}V}+\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\hat{\nabla}\right)(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})-\cancel{\omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}\omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}V}

Then, for any

\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\overline{J})=\overline{\nabla}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\overline{\nabla}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\overline{J}+\overline{R}(J,\gamma^{\prime})\overline{\gamma^{\prime}}=\overline{\hat{\nabla}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\hat{\nabla}_{\gamma^{\prime}}J+\hat{R}(J,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}}=\overline{\hat{\mathcal{J}}(J)},

Definition 5.2

Let

Note that any curve that is transversely right-inextendible is in particular right-inextendible (in the usual sense), but the converse is not true. Indeed, it is clear that the definition of transverse (right- or left-)inextendibility is equivalent to the requirement that the projection of the curve onto the leaf space

is trivial, that is,
\tilde{M}=\tilde{L}\times B
.
∎

We recall that a foliation
(M,\mathcal{F})
is called
developable
when there is some smooth covering map
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{\prime}\rightarrow M
such that the pullback foliation
p^{*}\mathcal{F}
on
M^{\prime}
is a simple foliation (i.e., given by connected fibers of a submersion). In this case we say that
p^{*}\mathcal{F}
is a
development
of
\mathcal{F}
, or that
\mathcal{F}

develops
to it. From what we saw in the previous proof, Theorem
5.7
can be alternatively restated with this terminology:

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliation whose leaves are all compact and such that
M/\mathcal{F}
is Hausdorff. Suppose
(M,\mathcal{F})
is endowed with a transverse affine structure
[\hat{\nabla}]
such that the family of all non vertical transverse-geodesics is transversely pseudoconvex, transversely disprisoning and without transverse conjugate points. Then
\mathcal{F}
develops, on the universal covering
\tilde{M}
of
M
, to a simple foliation over a contractible manifold
B
.

As announced in the Introduction, an interesting and important corollary of the results in this paper is applicable to an
affine orbifold

(\mathcal{O},\nabla)
, that is, an orbifold endowed with a
cone connection
(for a detailed discussion, see
[
19
, Section 2.2]
). When dealing with orbifolds, the notion of a covering map is slightly more involved than usual (see
[
9
, Section 2.3]
), since it has to take eventual singularities into account. Even so, we can still define an orbifold
\mathcal{O}
to be
developable
if there is an orbicover
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
such that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is a manifold. It is clear that being developable in this sense is equivalent as the foliation which realizes it as a leaf space being developable in the sense mentioned above. The definitions of pseudoconvexity and disprisonment for families of geodesics thereon are identical. As mentioned above, the orbifold
\mathcal{O}
can be realized as a leaf space
M/\mathcal{F}
for a foliation
\mathcal{F}
of finite holonomy and compact leaves, and we have a map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
which is a submersion in the sense of orbifolds (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
). A construction entirely analogous to that of Proposition
3.14
will show that
\nabla
induces a transverse affine connection
\hat{\nabla}
on
M
, and the reasoning of Remark
4.12
can be extended to orbifolds to yield that
\pi
projects
\hat{\nabla}
-transverse-geodesics onto
\nabla
-geodesics. One can thus prove that the pseudoconvexity and disprisonment of
\nabla
implies the transverse pseudoconvexivity and transverse disprisonment of
\hat{\nabla}
. Analogously to the theory developed in Section 5, we have that
\hat{\nabla}
-transverse Jacobi fields are mapped by
d\pi
into Jacobi fields of
(\mathcal{O},\nabla)
and, therefore, the absence of conjugate points in
(\mathcal{O},\nabla)
imply the absence of transverse conjugate points in
(M,\mathcal{F},[\hat{\nabla}])
. We have thus established Corollary
B
, whose statement we repeat here:

Corollary 5.10

(Affine Hadamard theorem for orbifolds)
.

Let
(\mathcal{O},\nabla)
be an affine orbifold without conjugate points and such that the family of its geodesics is pseudoconvex and disprisoning. Then
\mathcal{O}
is a good (developable) orbifold and whose universal covering is a contractible manifold.

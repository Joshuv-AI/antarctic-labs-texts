A Multi-Affine Geometric Framework for Quantum Nonlocality: Unifying Berry Phases, Entanglement, and Coherence
Shoshauna Gauvin

Abstract
We relate Berry-phase control and retained entropy geometry to recoverable coherence, entanglement, and Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) correlations. For a depolarized Bell pair subject to recorded phase noise, we determine exact CHSH optima when Bob retains his record and when he compresses it with his qubit into one qubit by any deterministic quantum channel. Some records preserve a violation that every such compression loses. The retained transverse metric gives sharp resource intervals. Nevertheless, for every finite derivative order, two finite readouts of the same noise can match all pulled-back monotone-metric and relative-entropy derivatives at faithful diagonal inputs while giving opposite recovered CHSH outcomes. Complete transverse entropy response determines the reliability law. A finite transitionless Berry loop realizes the channel; zero-expectation conditional control errors give a quadratic reduced-channel accuracy certificate. Transport, thermal, and accelerated-detector applications specialize established channel criteria under explicit preparation and accuracy assumptions. These model-dependent certificates supply neither a universal entanglement lifetime nor a detector-independent acceleration bound.

Proposition 2

IV
Entropy geometry and retained distinguishability

Fix a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}
with its
Hermitian product and operator adjoint. Its faithful state manifold is

\mathcal{S}_{n}^{+}=\{\rho=\rho^{\dagger}>0:\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1\},\qquad T_{\rho}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{+}=\mathrm{Herm}_{0}(\mathcal{H}).

(43)

The entropy contrast determines a statistical geometry; a ray or spectral
projector determines a phase geometry. Hamiltonians, instruments, and
subsystem decompositions supply their physical interpretation

[
25
]
.

IV.1
Umegaki contrast and the faithful quantum model

On Eq. (
43
), use negative von Neumann entropy and
Umegaki relative entropy, with natural logarithms:

\displaystyle F(\rho)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\log\rho),

(44)

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{U}}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\right].

(45)

Trace-one tangent vectors eliminate the identity term in the
differential of
F
. On trace-zero Hermitian tangents
U,V
, its
Hessian is the Bogoliubov-Kubo-Mori metric,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\rho}(A)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{s}A\rho^{1-s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(46)

\displaystyle g^{\mathrm{BKM}}_{\rho}(U,V)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[U\mathcal{K}_{\rho}^{-1}(V)\right].

(47)

The inverse is well defined at faithful
\rho
.
In a diagonal basis the inverse-kernel (metric) coefficient is

(\log p_{i}-\log p_{j})/(p_{i}-p_{j})
, with diagonal limit
1/p_{i}
.
On a fixed commuting model Eq. (
47
) reduces to the
classical Fisher metric. Umegaki contrast selects this particular
quantum metric; monotonicity by itself permits a larger family

[
47
]
.

At singular states relative entropy is finite only when

\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\sigma
.
Distinct pure states therefore have infinite relative entropy in either
direction. Their projective metric requires a separate construction;
a finite projector response does not guarantee a finite BKM boundary limit.

Remark 1
.

Statistical metric connections act on tangent fibers; Berry connections
act on ray or spectral fibers. Identifying their transport requires an
explicit represented bundle map intertwining the connections

[
25
]
. Appendix
A
gives the dual
connections, projective completion and general projector construction.

IV.2
Fixed measurements and channel loss

A finite positive-operator-valued measure (POVM)
\{M_{a}^{x}\}_{a}
specifies the probabilities of outcomes
a
at setting
x
. Its classical-record channel is

\Phi_{x}(\rho)=\sum_{a}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho M_{a}^{x})\ket{a}\bra{a},

(48)

where
M_{a}^{x}\geq 0
and
\sum_{a}M_{a}^{x}=\mathbf{1}
.
For a bipartite preparation,

p(a,b|x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{AB}(M_{a}^{x}\otimes N_{b}^{y})].

(49)

Changing
x
changes the readout map, whose Fisher pullback can have a kernel of invisible parameter directions.

Let
\Phi
be a fixed completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map.
Evaluate output logarithms on
\mathcal{K}=\operatorname{ran}\Phi(\mathbf{1})
,
the common faithful output support established in
Appendix
B
.

Define the relative-entropy loss

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(\rho,\sigma)=D_{\mathrm{U}}(\rho\|\sigma)-D_{\mathrm{U}}(\Phi\rho\|\Phi\sigma).

(50)

Data processing gives
\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}\geq 0

[
39
]
.
For trace-zero Hermitian density tangents
U,V

\displaystyle G_{\Phi,\rho}(U,V)

\displaystyle=g^{\mathrm{BKM}}_{\rho}(U,V)-g^{\mathrm{BKM}}_{\Phi\rho}(\Phi U,\Phi V),

(51)

\displaystyle G_{\Phi,\rho}(U,U)

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Both tensors are pulled to the input carrier. The output metric of
Eq. (
48
) is Fisher information on active outcomes.
Appendix
B
derives the Hessian and treats
composition, recovery, and changes of apparatus.

The coherence in Eq. (
11
) also satisfies

C_{r}(\rho)=D_{\mathrm{U}}(\rho\|\Delta\rho)/\ln 2

[
3
]
;
dephasing guarantees the required support inclusion.
For a faithful diagonal reference
\sigma=\Delta\sigma
, diagonal-log expectations also give

\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}(\rho,\sigma)=D_{\mathrm{U}}(\rho\|\Delta\rho).

(52)

Thus coherence in bits equals this channel loss divided by
\ln 2
. Local unitaries preserve entanglement and optimized CHSH, but can change coherence in a fixed basis.

V
Applications to physical transport and Bell thresholds

The remaining sections apply the resource criteria to specified transport,
thermal, and detector channels. Lifetime predictions require a clock,
carrier representation, and evolution law. They concern crossings of the
attainable CHSH value; the operator bound
2\sqrt{2}
remains unchanged.

Deterministic local transport preserves both entanglement and the
optimized value:

\rho_{t}=(U_{A}\otimes U_{B})\rho_{0}(U_{A}\otimes U_{B})^{\dagger}.

(79)

Conjugating detector observables by their local unitaries preserves all
four correlators, including for independently represented spatial
translations. Fixed axes can give varying correlators; joint interactions
can change entanglement across the same partition.

V.1
Proper time and correlated phase fluctuations

Local-unitary invariance is given by Eq. (
79

here we specify the clock and phase statistics of the retained carriers.

For prescribed classical trajectories and fixed local energy bases, set

\displaystyle H_{i}

\displaystyle=E_{0i}\mathbf{1}+\Delta E_{i}\ket{1}\bra{1},

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\Delta E_{i}(s)\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{i}(s),

(80)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\simeq 1+\frac{\Phi_{g}(\bm{x}_{i}(t))}{c_{\mathrm{light}}^{2}}-\frac{|\bm{v}_{i}(t)|^{2}}{2c_{\mathrm{light}}^{2}}.

(81)

Equation (

slow-motion corrections:
\Phi_{g}
has units
\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-2}}
,

\Delta E_{i}
is an energy, and
\phi_{i}
is dimensionless

[

49

\phi_{A}-\phi_{B}\simeq\Delta E\,t\,\Delta\Phi_{g}/(\hbar c_{\mathrm{light}}^{2})

\Delta\Phi_{g}\simeq g\Delta h
near
a uniform field. An additive
f_{i}(t)\mathbf{1}
changes only an overall phase,
whereas a level-gap change alters relative phase; a position-dependent
operator on superposed trajectories is not an identity shift.

Consider the initial states

\ket{\Phi_{\vartheta}}=\cos\vartheta\ket{00}+\sin\vartheta\ket{11}
and

\ket{\Psi_{\vartheta}}=\cos\vartheta\ket{01}+\sin\vartheta\ket{10}

\phi_{A}+\phi_{B}
and
\phi_{A}-\phi_{B}
. Discarding a classical phase
record gives the characteristic functions

\displaystyle\kappa_{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}e^{-\mathrm{i}[\phi_{A}(t)\pm\phi_{B}(t)]},

(82)

\displaystyle|\kappa_{\pm}(t)|

\displaystyle=\exp\!\left[-\frac{V_{A}(t)+V_{B}(t)\pm 2C_{AB}(t)}{2}\right]

(83)

for jointly Gaussian phases, where
V_{i}=\operatorname{Var}\phi_{i}

and
C_{AB}=\operatorname{Cov}(\phi_{A},\phi_{B})
.
If
\delta\Omega_{i}(s)
is the fluctuating angular frequency sampled
along trajectory
i
, then

C_{ij}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\!\mathrm{d}s\int_{0}^{t}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\,\mathbb{E}[\delta\Omega_{i}(s)\delta\Omega_{j}(s^{\prime})],\qquad V_{i}=C_{ii}.

(84)

The frequency covariance has units
\mathrm{s^{-2}}
; its spatial
dependence supplies the separation dependence. Equal common phase noise
protects the
\Psi
subspace and enhances dephasing of the
\Phi

subspace. Equal gaps, couplings, and timing are needed for this cancellation.

After a local basis exchange for the
\Psi
family, the dephased-Schmidt
criterion, Eq. (
33
), gives

S_{\mathrm{spin},\pm}(t)=2\sqrt{1+|\kappa_{\pm}(t)|^{2}\sin^{2}(2\vartheta)}.

(85)

Known mean phases rotate the optimal axes; an accessible record with
calibrated local control permits phase removal. Without the record,
finite Gaussian variances leave a nonzero CHSH excess at every finite
time when
\sin 2\vartheta\neq 0
. This commuting
U(1)
model differs from
the physically represented, noncommuting
SU(2)
diffusion of
Sec.
V.3
.

For a local Markov generator specified per unit proper time along a
deterministic trajectory, changing to laboratory time rescales its rate:

\gamma_{\mathrm{lab},i}(t)=\gamma_{\mathrm{proper},i}(\tau_{i}(t))\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}.

(86)

If the proper time fluctuates, the additional averaging can produce memory and need not preserve this Markov description.

V.2
Represented fluctuations

Choose a physical representation of transport and an unresolved-increment
law, as distinguished in Ref.
[
25
]
. Assume independent
small Markov increments
\exp(-\mathrm{i}T_{a}\mathrm{d}\theta^{a})
, with dimensionless
Hermitian
T_{a}
, zero leading mean, and covariance

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{d}\theta^{a}\mathrm{d}\theta^{b}]=D^{ab}\mathrm{d}t
, where
D\succeq 0

has inverse-time units. Require higher-order increment contributions to
be
o(\mathrm{d}t)
in the diffusion limit. Expansion through order
\mathrm{d}t
gives

\mathcal{L}(\rho)=-\tfrac{1}{2}D^{ab}[T_{a},[T_{b},\rho]].

(87)

A physical loop construction may supply

\mathrm{d}\theta^{a}=\mathcal{F}^{a}_{I}\mathrm{d}\Sigma^{I}

convention for independent oriented area components

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{d}\Sigma^{I}\mathrm{d}\Sigma^{J}]=C^{IJ}\mathrm{d}t,\qquad D^{ab}=\mathcal{F}^{a}_{I}C^{IJ}\mathcal{F}^{b}_{J}

(88)

connects represented physical curvature to the channel. A statistical

an identity-valued phase has zero double commutator. Nonzero mean
increments or variable stochastic coefficients require the corresponding
drift terms. Ordinary Brownian coordinate increments are

O(\sqrt{\mathrm{d}t})

O(\mathrm{d}t)

with covariance
O((\mathrm{d}t)^{2})

therefore requires an additional area-diffusion limit.
Section
III.3
gives a finite controlled-loop realization
without that limit.

For stationary independent isotropic increments
D^{ab}=\gamma\delta_{ab}

and
\operatorname{Tr}(T_{a}T_{b})=\delta_{ab}/2

(89)

Thus

V.3
Correlated local rotations of a singlet

On

V.6
Retained carriers and detection

For distinguishable massive spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
particles, a Lorentz
transformation is local on the full particle partition, but its
momentum-dependent Wigner rotations can change reduced spin correlations

[
45
]
. A fixed initially factorized momentum assignment gives
a CPTP spin map by unitary evolution and partial trace; initial
spin-momentum correlations require an assignment domain

[
35
]
, and correlated momenta can induce correlated spin
noise. A passive frame change of both state and actual observables
preserves the probabilities of the same experiment.

Loss is a further channel, distinct from spin dephasing. For independent,
state-independent erasures with survival probabilities
\eta_{A},\eta_{B}
,
a setting-independent herald established before the measurement choices
defines a legitimate conditional experiment. Discarding no-click trials
after those choices does not automatically justify the same Bell bound.
For a maximally entangled pair, ideal CHSH axes, and the assignment
+1

to every erasure, the all-trial expression is

S_{\mathrm{all}}=2\sqrt{2}\,\eta_{A}\eta_{B}+2(1-\eta_{A})(1-\eta_{B}).

(106)

The surviving Bell marginal makes single-erasure terms vanish, while
double erasures contribute
2
.
For equal efficiencies this protocol violates CHSH precisely when

\eta>2/(1+\sqrt{2})
, the conventional efficiency threshold

[
24
]
. The threshold is protocol-specific.

VI
Application to thermal storage and Bell lifetimes

Consider noninteracting stored qubits with independent stationary
reservoirs, initial state

\rho_{AB}(0)\otimes\rho_{E_{A}}\otimes\rho_{E_{B}}
, and discarded reservoir
records. Weak coupling, short reservoir memory, resolved transition
frequencies, and the Born-Markov and secular approximations justify the
thermal semigroup limit
[
15
]
. The following results are exact
for this reduced generator.

Kofman and Korotkov
[
36
]
analyzed relaxation,
dephasing, and distinct entanglement and CHSH survival times at arbitrary
temperature. Their principal preparation occupies
\ket{10},\ket{01}
,
whereas Eq. (
1
) occupies
\ket{00},\ket{11}
.
Exchanging local energy levels changes the preparation’s orientation
relative to a nonunital thermal channel; the specialization below retains
this dependence for the detector application.

VI.1
Generator, temperature, and rate conventions

Fix
\ket{0}
as the ground state and
\ket{1}
as the excited state:

\displaystyle H_{j}

\displaystyle=\hbar\omega_{j}\ket{1}\bra{1},\qquad\omega_{j}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma_{-}

\displaystyle=\ket{0}\bra{1},\qquad\sigma_{+}=\sigma_{-}^{\dagger}.

(107)

In the local interaction picture, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{j}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\downarrow j}\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{-}]\rho+\Gamma_{\uparrow j}\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{+}]\rho

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma_{\phi j}}{2}(\sigma_{z}\rho\sigma_{z}-\rho),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[L]\rho

\displaystyle=L\rho L^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{L^{\dagger}L,\rho\}.

(108)

All rates are constant and nonnegative, with
\Gamma_{1j}>0
. The population and off-diagonal decay rates are

\displaystyle T_{1j}^{-1}=\Gamma_{1j}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\downarrow j}+\Gamma_{\uparrow j},

\displaystyle T_{2j}^{-1}=\Gamma_{2j}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{1j}/2+\gamma_{\phi j},\qquad r_{j}=\frac{\Gamma_{\uparrow j}}{\Gamma_{1j}}.

(109)

Here
\gamma_{\phi j}
is the additional off-diagonal decay rate, so its dissipator coefficient is halved; nonnegativity requires
T_{2j}\leq 2T_{1j}
.

Write
\beta_{j}=(k_{B}T_{{\rm bath},j})^{-1}\geq 0
, with zero temperature understood as
\beta_{j}\to\infty
and infinite temperature as
\beta_{j}\to 0
. Thermal detailed balance gives

\frac{\Gamma_{\uparrow j}}{\Gamma_{\downarrow j}}=e^{-\beta_{j}\hbar\omega_{j}},\qquad r_{j}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta_{j}\hbar\omega_{j}}}.

(110)

For weak linear coupling to a bosonic reservoir, a standard parametrization is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\downarrow j}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{j}(\omega_{j})(n_{j}+1),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\uparrow j}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{j}(\omega_{j})n_{j},

\displaystyle n_{j}

\displaystyle=(e^{\beta_{j}\hbar\omega_{j}}-1)^{-1}.

(111)

The spectral coupling
\kappa_{j}
is physical input: temperature alone
does not fix
T_{1j}
. The energy basis fixes both coherence and
preparation orientation. Quasistatic broadening and echo depend on the
storage/readout protocol and need not be described by a constant

\gamma_{\phi j}
; conditioning on a reservoir record, changing the
encoding, or active correction requires a different channel.

VI.2
Exact channel solution and correlation criteria

Start from Eq. (
1
), with

\displaystyle P_{11}

VI.4
An equilibrium reference that moves

The lifetime formulas assume fixed Hamiltonians and constant rates.
For a moving reference in finite dimension, let
H_{t}
be differentiable,
fix a finite inverse temperature
\beta
, and define the faithful state

\tau_{t}=e^{-\beta H_{t}}/Z_{t}
. Assume

\dot{\rho}_{t}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}[H_{t},\rho_{t}]+\mathcal{L}_{t}(\rho_{t}),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{t}(\tau_{t})=0,

(123)

where each frozen
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is a
Gorini-Kossakowski-Lindblad-Sudarshan (GKLS) generator.
For faithful
\rho_{t}
,
frozen-semigroup contraction gives
\Sigma_{t}\geq 0
without detailed
balance
[
39
]
, while differentiation of the reference gives

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t}

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\mathcal{L}_{t}(\rho_{t})(\ln\rho_{t}-\ln\tau_{t})\right],

(124)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}D_{\mathrm{U}}(\rho_{t}\|\tau_{t})

\displaystyle=-\Sigma_{t}+\beta\operatorname{Tr}[(\rho_{t}-\tau_{t})\dot{H}_{t}].

(125)

Indeed,
\ln\tau_{t}=-\beta H_{t}-\ln Z_{t}
and trace cyclicity gives

\mathrm{d}\ln Z_{t}/\mathrm{d}t=-\beta\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_{t}\dot{H}_{t})
even for

[H_{t},\dot{H}_{t}]\neq 0
; the coherent contribution vanishes because

[H_{t},\ln\tau_{t}]=0
. Singular states require a support-compatible
differentiable limit rather than direct use of the logarithmic
derivative at the boundary.

The work/reference term has either sign, so distance to a driven Gibbs
state need not decrease. An identity shift
H_{t}\mapsto H_{t}+f(t)\mathbf{1}

leaves it unchanged because
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{t}-\tau_{t})=0
.
For
\beta=\beta(t)
, add

\dot{\beta}\,\operatorname{Tr}[(\rho_{t}-\tau_{t})H_{t}]
; an extra coherent drive that does
not commute with
H_{t}
and is omitted from
\mathcal{L}_{t}
also requires
its own contribution to the derivative.

Uniform acceleration changes a detector’s channel through its field

VII.1
A local detector response and its assumptions

Consider a two-level Unruh-DeWitt detector with fixed proper gap

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi c_{\mathrm{light}}\Omega}{a}=\frac{1}{2\alpha},\qquad r=\frac{1}{1+e^{2x}}.

(128)

The scale

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{e^{2x}-1},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{0}\coth x,

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{1}^{-1}.

(129)

These rates use proper time
\tau

107

changes alter the rates
[

]
.

For two detectors, use independent scalar-field copies in their Minkowski

VII.6
Clocks, geometric response, and physical scope

The inversions fix detector proper exposure. Uniform acceleration starting at rest in a chosen inertial frame relates it to laboratory time by

\tau=\frac{c_{\mathrm{light}}}{a}\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\frac{at_{\mathrm{lab}}}{c_{\mathrm{light}}}\right).

(142)

At fixed laboratory duration this expression must enter the channel before solving the boundary; monotonicity under one clock protocol does not establish it under another.

Writing
\eta_{0}=\Gamma_{0}/\Omega
, the scalar secular condition is

\eta_{0}\coth[1/(2\alpha)]\ll 1,

(143)

or

at large

2

\Gamma_{1}/\Omega\leq 1.782\times 10^{-3}
. This checks a scale hierarchy, not a microscopic error bound.

The same clock relation gives rapidity

a\tau/c_{\mathrm{light}}=2\pi\alpha(\Omega\tau)
and terminal Lorentz
factor
\gamma_{\mathrm{L}}=\cosh(a\tau/c_{\mathrm{light}})
in the chosen
initial rest frame. Already at
\alpha=1.6
and
\Omega\tau=10^{3}
, the
rapidity is
1.0053\times 10^{4}
and
\log_{10}\gamma_{\mathrm{L}}\simeq 4365.7
.
These are abstract semigroup hierarchy examples. Establishing an
attainable accelerator protocol additionally requires a device model for
the worldline duration and energy budget.

A physical protocol must specify its worldline and switching

[

,

IX
Discussion and conclusion

For recorded phase noise on a depolarized Bell pair, the available
measurement output limits Bell certification. Some records preserve CHSH
violation when Bob retains them, yet every deterministic compression of
his qubit and record into one qubit loses it. The sharp metric-only
intervals quantify a separate limitation of an aggregate information
summary.

With unrestricted record size, that limitation persists at every finite
order of local geometry. The constructed readouts share a phase prior,
uniform flag alphabet, and diagonal references. Their pulled-back metrics
and contrasts agree to the prescribed orders, including curvature in the
explicit example, while their optimal compressed Bell outcomes differ.
Complete transverse entropy response, or sufficiently many moments under
a known finite support bound, determines the reliability law and both
CHSH optima. This identifies the missing channel information within the
model; the high-order construction has shrinking accuracy margins.

A circular Berry loop saturates the known isoholonomic action bound and
reduces the control budget at fixed ideal channel. Zero-expectation
conditional errors give a quadratic reduced-channel certificate after
ancillary discarding; a nonzero phase expectation can restore first-order
sensitivity. The implementation and recovery comparisons do not identify
a unique geometric origin from the final channel.

The transport and thermal applications distinguish Bell violation,
entanglement, and channel entanglement breaking. The finite-memory phase
model has a quartic onset and a controlled cubic limit. Accelerated-detector
predictions specialize known channels and require resource margins larger
than model error; a microscopic switching or finite-coupling budget remains
unresolved. Carrier comparisons require encoded gaps, interaction spectra,
clocks, and retained observations. These results fix neither a universal
separation lifetime nor a detector-independent acceleration bound.

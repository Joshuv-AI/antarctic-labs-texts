Appraising clinicopathological and prognostic significance of circular RNA ubiquitin-associated protein 2 in human cancer
Authors: Xukun Luo, Qunyan Tian, Hulalai Hader, Yihan Li, Jian Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCircular RNA ubiquitin-associated protein 2 (circUBAP2) is highly expressed in various human cancers and has been identified as a key contributor to cancer development and progression. We performed this study to explore the clinicopathological and prognostic significance of circUBAP2 in multiple cancers.MethodsThis meta-analysis was conducted according to PRISMA 2020 guidelines. PubMed, Web of Science, CNKI, Wanfang, ClinicalTrials.gov and BioXiv were systematically searched from inception to March 1, 2025 according to the PICO framework. Study quality was appraised using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale and eventually a total of 12 studies with 905 participants were included. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) and hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were synthesized using fixed- or random-effects models based on heterogeneity (I² statistic) to evaluate the association of circUBAP2 with age, sex, tumor size, stage, differentiation and metastasis. The stability of the results from meta-analysis was estimated via sensitivity analysis. Begg’s funnel plots and Egger’s test were used to appraise the potential publication bias. Subgroup analysis for overall survival (OS) based on follow-up duration, tumor type, case number and survival endpoints were also performed.ResultsHigh expression of circUBAP2 was significantly related to larger tumor size (OR = 3.07, 95% CI: 1.53–6.17, P < 0.05), advanced stage (OR = 2.43, 95% CI: 1.70–3.48, P < 0.05), poor differentiation (OR = 4.47, 95% CI: 2.67–7.48, P < 0.05) and metastasis (OR = 5.77, 95% CI: 3.21–10.40, P < 0.05), but not associated with age or sex. Additionally, circUBAP2 was related to cancer survival (HR = 2.43, 95% CI: 1.96–3.03, P < 0.05), regardless of follow-up duration, tumor type, case number, and survival endpoints. Begg’s funnel plot and Egger’s test showed no publication bias.ConclusionsHigh expression of circUBAP2 may predict larger tumor size, poor differentiation, distant metastasis and poor outcome of cancer, highlighting the carcinogenetic role of circUBAP2 in human cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-025-04118-0.Keywords: CircUBAP2, Clinicopathological significance, Prognostic biomarker, Cancer progression, Meta-analysis

Introduction

Cancer poses a significant threat to human life and health, profoundly impacting socio-economic development. In 2024, 2,001,140 new cancer cases and 611,720 cancer-related deaths are projected to occur in the United States [1]. Many patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, which often results in poor prognoses and reduced quality of life [2]. Early cancer detection plays a crucial role in improving cure rates and enhancing patient outcomes [3].

Circular RNAs are abundantly expressed in eukaryotic cells and exert critical regulatory functions in gene expression, modulating cancer transcription and progression through miRNA sponging and stabilization of protein complexes (Fig. 1). Our recent work showed that circular RNA could be a promising biomarker for human cancers [4, 5]. CircUBAP2 was initially identified as an upregulated circRNA in osteosarcoma through circRNA microarray analysis [6–9]. CircUBAP2 is generated through selective splicing of exons 16 and 17 of the UBAP2 gene, lacking poly(A) tails and 5′-3′ polarity [10]. Previous studies have indicated that circUBAP2 is highly expressed in various human cancers, including glioma [11], osteosarcoma [9], and lung cancer [12]. Its abnormal expression has been shown to drive malignant biological processes such as autophagy induction [13], cell cycle arrest [14], and activation of signaling pathways [8]. Previous studies have revealed a significant association between circUBAP2 expression and cancer patients’ prognosis and clinical characteristics [15, 16]. Emerging evidence highlights the role of circUBAP2 as an oncogenic factor influencing tumor progression [17, 18], while some studies have shown controversial results [19, 20]. To date, no meta-analysis has been performed to assess the clinicopathological and prognostic significance of circUBAP2 in human cancer.

Biogenesis and function of circRNA in cancer

In this study, we conducted a meta-analysis to explore the association between circUBAP2 and prognosis of human cancer. Additionally, bioinformatics analysis was employed to investigate the relationship between UBAP2 and prognosis of cancer.

Method

Search strategies

This meta-analysis was registered in the Open Science Framework (OSF, registration DOI: 10.17605/OSF.IO/5UG8M). It was conducted according to the preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (PRISMA) checklist. The study design followed the PICO framework as follows: (1) Patients diagnosed with human cancer (2) Identifying and categorizing by the expression of circUBAP2 (high expression versus low expression) (3) Patients with low expression of circUBAP2 (4) Clinicopathological features such as age, sex, tumor size, stage, differentiation and metastasis.

Selection process

NCBI PubMed, Web of Science, CNKI, Wanfang, ClinicalTrials.gov and BioXiv databases were searched according to the PICO framework:

For the searching strategies above, no date filters were applied and article-type were refined for only human studies. Only studies published in English or Chinese were included, as these were the primary languages accessible for full-text review and the translation resource was limited. All searches were conducted up to March 1, 2025, without date or study-type restrictions. Two reviewers (JZ and XL) independently screened the titles and abstracts of all retrieved records, followed by full-text assessment for eligibility. Disagreements were resolved by discussion or consultation with a third reviewer (YL). No automation tools were used in the study selection process. Full searching strings of each database were attached in Table S2.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) Studies providing sufficient data to extract odds ratios (OR) or hazard ratios (HR) along with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) (2). Articles evaluating the prognostic significance of circUBAP2 in human tumors (3). Publications reporting the cutoff values for circUBAP2 expression (4). Studies containing relevant extractable information.

Exclusion criteria: Studies not meeting these criteria were excluded.

Data extraction

Two researchers (JZ and XL) extracted and appraised the related information including clinicopathological characteristics (age, sex, tumor size, stage, differentiation and metastasis) and prognostic outcomes. In case of any disagreement between the two authors, an additional investigator (ZW) was consulted to resolve the issue.

Statistical analysis

Stata 12.0 software was utilized to calculate the extracted data to get the pooled OR/HR and 95% CI with the aim of appraising the connection between the expression of circUBAP2 and cancer prognosis. I2 was applied to estimate the heterogeneity of included studies. If I2 > 50%, the random effect model was adopted, while the fix-effect model was employed when I2 < 50%. Meta-regression was not feasible due to limited number of studies per subgroup (n < 10), but heterogeneity was explored qualitatively. Additionally, we detect the stability of the result by deleting the enrolled study one by one to observe whether the overall effect size showed significant change. To evaluate the stability of the meta-analysis results, we conducted a sensitivity analysis by sequentially removing each included study, one at a time, to observe whether the overall effect size exhibited significant changes. If substantial changes in the overall effect size were detected, further investigation was performed to understand the potential sources of heterogeneity. To evaluate the presence of publication bias, we performed a visual inspection of funnel plots, where asymmetry might suggest potential bias. Egger’s regression test and Begg’s rank correlation test were also applied to assess publication bias. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered indicative of significant publication bias. In cases where publication bias was detected, the trim-and-fill method was applied to estimate and adjust for its potential impact on the pooled effect size.

Prognostic significance of UBAP2 in TCGA dataset

The prognostic value of UBAP2 in cancer was evaluated using the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) dataset (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/), a comprehensive and interactive web server for analyzing RNA sequencing data from the TCGA and GTEx projects. CircRNA annotations were unavailable for TCGA cohorts matching the included studies’ cancer types, and comparation of circUBAP2/ UBAP2 expression may be explored in future work. Various visualization tools, including box plots, violin plots, and Kaplan-Meier survival curves, were utilized to explore and illustrate the relationship between UBAP2 expression and cancer prognosis. Box plots were used to compare the expression levels of UBAP2 in tumor tissues versus normal tissues, providing an overview of differential expression patterns. Violin plots further detailed the distribution and variability of UBAP2 expression within each group. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were employed to analyze overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) outcomes in relation to UBAP2 expression, with statistical significance assessed through log-rank tests. These visualizations together offered a comprehensive view of how UBAP2 expression correlates with patient prognosis across multiple cancer types.

Results

Search process

One hundred thirty-four articles were included in this study initially. After removing duplicate data, 52 articles remained. Following a review of the titles and abstracts, 25 papers were excluded for no corresponding topics (n = 19), protocol only (n = 1) or review only (n = 4). The remaining 27 studies were then further evaluated. Among these, 14 studies were excluded due to low quality (n = 3) and no usable data (n = 12). Ultimately, 12 studies [6, 9, 10, 15, 18, 21–27] published between 2017 and 2023 were included in the analysis (Fig. 2). Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) score was employed to observe the quality of included studies. We found that the NOS scores of all included articles exceed 7 stars and the average score was 7.67 (Table S1).

CircUBAP2 and clinicopathological features in human cancer

As presented in Fig. 3; Table 2, circUBAP2 expression was not related to age or sex (P > 0.05) (Fig. 3A&B), while elevated expression of circUBAP2 was significantly related to bigger tumor size (OR = 3.07, 95% CI: 1.53–6.17, P < 0.05 ) ( Fig. 3C ), advanced stage (OR = 2.43, 95% CI: 1.70–3.48, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3D), poor differentiation (OR = 4.47, 95% CI: 2.67–7.48, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3E) and metastasis (OR = 5.77, 95% CI: 3.21–10.40, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3F). As shown in Fig. 3C and summarized in Table 2, inconsistent tumor-size cutoffs (> 2 cm vs. > 5 cm) across studies likely contributed to moderate heterogeneity, and the moderate heterogeneity in tumor size associations likely reflect variable cutoffs across cancer types, with smaller cutoffs showing stronger but less precise effects.

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis demonstrated the robustness and stability of our meta-analysis results (Fig. 4). By sequentially excluding individual studies and recalculating the pooled effect sizes, we observed no significant fluctuations for either clinicopathological comparisons (Fig. 3) or survival (Fig. 5E) in the overall outcomes, indicating that the conclusions were not substantially influenced by any single study. Multiple impetus was employed to incorporate previously excluded studies of sex and age due to missing data, and imputed pooled ORs were: sex 0.84 (95% CI: 0.64–1.11) (Fig. S1) and age 0.97 (95% CI: 0.74–1.28) (Fig. S2), supporting result stability. This consistency reinforces the reliability of the findings across the included studies.

Publication bias evaluation

Additionally, an assessment of publication bias was conducted using both funnel plots and statistical tests, including Egger’s regression and Begg’s rank correlation tests. Visual inspection of the funnel plot showed a symmetrical distribution of study effect sizes, and the statistical tests yielded no significant evidence of bias (P > 0.05, Fig. 5). These results suggest that the likelihood of publication bias affecting the meta-analysis conclusions was minimal, further strengthening the validity of the findings.

CircUBAP2 and prognostic outcome in human cancer

A total of 9 studies were included in the survival analysis. As shown in Fig. 6; Table 3, high expression of circUBAP2 was related to survival in hepatocellular carcinoma (HR = 3.76, 95% CI: 2.01–7.04, P < 0.05) and osteosarcoma (HR = 3.05, 95% CI: 1.62–5.72, P < 0.05) (Fig. 6A). Additionally, survival analysis indicated that circUBAP2 was related to cancer survival, regardless of follow-up duration, case number, and survival endpoints (Fig. 6B-D). Sensitivity analysis indicated that the results kept stable by dropping one of the study in sequence (Fig. 6E). Publication bias test was performed with no significant bias found (Fig. 6F).

In the survival subgroup defined by cases ≥ 100, we found a significant association (HR = 2.51, 95% CI: 1.74–3.62; fixed effects) accompanied by moderate heterogeneity (I² = 52.5%, p = 0.147). A plausible explanation for the moderate heterogeneity is that the two large HCC cohorts differed in several design and clinical features that plausibly shift hazard estimates: Yu et al. accrued a single-center 125-patient resection cohort during 2014–2015 with follow-up completed in 2019 (≈ 80 months), reported multivariable Cox associations in which high circUBAP2 independently predicted OS; By contrast, Liu et al. analyzed a 91-patient cohort initiated in 2006 with 60-month follow-up, presented Kaplan–Meier–based OS and RFS separations after median dichotomization. Thus, different enrollment eras and centers, outcome definitions and modeling follow-up duration may relate to the moderate heterogeneity. The ≥ 100 cases subgroup’s moderate heterogeneity may reflect reduced statistical precision in smaller studies contributing to the comparison.

Prognostic value of UBAP2 in TCGA dataset

As shown in Fig. 7, expression of UBAP2 was significantly related to OS and DFS in adenoid cystic carcinoma(ACC) (Fig. 7A&D), head and neck squamous (HNSC) (Fig. 7B&E), liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) (Fig. 7C&F) and skin cutaneous melanoma(SKCM) (Fig. 7G&H) (P < 0.05). Moreover, we found that UBAP2 was significantly associated with stage of cancer (Fig. 7I). We observed that UBAP2 was highly expressed in various cancers including CHOL, DLBC, PCPG, STAD and THYM (Fig. 7J). However, since the GEPIA database does not provide OR values for the correlation between linear UBAP2 expression and tumor stage shown in Fig. 7I, a quantitative overlay with Table 2 could not be performed.

Discussion

In this meta-analysis with 905 cancer patients included, we found that high expression of circUBAP2 was significantly related to larger tumor size, advanced stage, poor differentiation, metastasis and prognosis in human cancer. Bioinformatics analysis showed that UBAP2 was highly expressed in multiple cancers and associated with poor outcome in human cancer.

For the first time, we conducted meta-analysis to explore the clinicopathological and prognostic significance of circUBAP2 in human cancer. CircUBAP2 has been observed dysregulated during cancer progression, which may suggest that circUBAP2 functions as a cancer biomarker. However, the conclusions for the role of circUBAP2 in cancer were inconsistent in previous study. Previous studies indicated that circUBAP2 served oncogenic in multiple cancers [7, 16, 19], while another study indicated that circUBAP2 functioned as a tumor suppressor in clear cell renal cell carcinoma via miR-148a-3p/FOXK2 axis [19]. Dong et al. revealed that circUBAP2 played active roles in the progression of osteosarcoma cisplatin resistance [8], while Cheng et al. showed that the circUBAP2 was involved in the inhibition of cisplatin resistance in gastric cancer [20]. In order to understand the role of circUBAP2 in cancer, we performed this study to identify the carcinogenetic role of circUBAP2 using meta-analysis and bioinformatics analysis.

For decades, researchers have been tirelessly exploring more effective methods for diagnosing and treating malignant tumors [28–30], and the discovery of circRNAs has opened new avenues in cancer therapy [31]. Among these, circUBAP2 has gained significant attention due to its diverse roles in human tumors [23, 32]. Generally, circUBAP2 is abnormally overexpressed in malignancies, contributing to tumor progression through autophagy regulation or miRNA sponging [21]. Additionally, analysis of the TCGA dataset revealed that UBAP2 was highly expressed in multiple cancers and high expression of UBAP2 was significantly associated with OS and DFS in adenoid cystic carcinoma, head and neck squamous, liver hepatocellular carcinoma and skin cutaneous melanoma. Moreover, we found that UBAP2 was significantly associated with stage of cancer. All this information that we obtained identified that circUBAP2 might be a candidate target with prognostic significance in human cancer.

Several studies have demonstrated that circRNAs serve as miRNA sponges by sequestering target miRNAs and inhibiting their activity [33, 34]. Additionally, circRNAs containing intronic sequences can regulate the transcription of their parental genes within the nucleus [35]. Furthermore, circRNAs with internal ribosome entry sites and open reading frames have the potential to be translated into proteins [36]. Consistent with most reported circRNAs, circUBAP2 was predominantly found in the cytoplasm [13]. MiRNA target prediction analysis revealed that circUBAP2 harbors multiple miRNA-binding sites, indicating its potential role as a miRNA sponge [13]. Mechanistically, circUBAP2 functions as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) in various cancers, influencing the expression of downstream target genes [22, 24]. CircUBAP2 can regulate autophagy in tumor cells by acting as a miRNA sponge, thereby promoting colorectal cancer proliferation and metastasis [37]. Interestingly, circUBAP2 exhibits low expression in cervical cancer but still facilitates tumor invasion and metastasis [10]. Additionally, its expression levels in different tumors are associated with sensitivity to chemotherapy and radiotherapy [8], emphasizing the important role of circUBAP2 in cancer. Understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying cancer progression could provide valuable insights into the interaction between circUBAP2 and poor cancer prognosis [38] (Table 4). These clinical and prognostic associations are in line with reported circUBAP2/miRNA/target axes that drive invasion, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and proliferation across multiple tumor types. The circUBAP2–miR-361-3p/SOX4 axis promotes metastasis in cervical cancer, the miR-582-5p/FOXO1 axis facilitates colorectal cancer progression, and the miR-194-3p/MMP9 and miR-4756/IFIT1/3 axes enhance invasion in hepatocellular carcinoma [39]. These mechanistic relationships, summarized in Table 4, provide a molecular explanation for the pooled findings linking circUBAP2 to metastasis (OR = 5.77), poor differentiation (OR = 4.47), and advanced stage (OR = 2.43). All included studies validated circUBAP2 circularity using RNase R digestion, and most of qPCR used GAPDH or U6 as reference controls expected 2 not founded.

The circUBAP2/miRNA/target gene axis and its role in tumorigenesis across various cancer types

Despite the promising findings, the biological mechanisms underlying the role of circUBAP2 in cancer progression and prognosis remain complex and warrant further investigation. CircUBAP2’s function as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) highlights its ability to modulate key oncogenic or tumor-suppressive pathways by interacting with miRNAs and downstream targets. However, the dual roles of circUBAP2 observed across different cancer types suggest a context-dependent function, influenced by tumor microenvironment, genetic mutations, and epigenetic modifications. Future studies should focus on elucidating the molecular networks in which circUBAP2 is involved and exploring its translational potential in precision oncology. Advanced technologies such as single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics may offer deeper insights into its role in diverse tumor microenvironments.

Observed heterogeneity was restricted to specific contrasts. First, the tumor-size comparison showed moderate heterogeneity (I² = 53.4%), which likely reflects the non-standardized size thresholds and biological differences across tumor types included in this meta-analysis. To more detailed explanation, this heterogeneity may arise from inconsistent tumor-size cutoffs across studies (e.g., > 2 cm for smaller tumors such as thyroid cancer vs. >5 cm for larger ones like hepatocellular carcinoma), together with organ-specific biological differences and measurement variability. Second, in the large-sample survival subgroup (cases ≥ 100), heterogeneity was also moderate (I² = 52.5%), a finding that is consistent with the mixing of distinct cancer types, different inclusion periods, and different follow-up durations across the two contributing studies. Importantly, leave-one-out sensitivity indicated the overall effects were stable despite these sources of between-study variability.

This study has several limitations: (1) The meta-analysis included only 12 papers with a total of 905 patients, which is relatively small, potentially limiting the statistical power of our findings (2). Survival information was extracted from survival curves, which may introduce bias and affect the accuracy of the results (3). Although subgroup analyses based on tumor type were performed, conclusions drawn from the pan-cancer analysis (4) Several cancer-specific strata were underpowered (e.g., ovarian cancer and RFS, each represented by a single study with n ≤ 24), and these estimates should be interpreted cautiously. We added footnotes to Table 1 noting these gaps and acknowledge that the moderate heterogeneity observed is partly attributable to reduced statistical precision in smaller studies. Therefore, these subgroup estimates should be interpreted with caution.

Conclusion

This study highlighted the clinicopathologic significance of circUBAP2 in predicting larger tumor size, advanced stage, poor differentiation, metastasis and prognosis in human cancer. Future multi-center, high-quality studies with larger sample sizes are essential to validate the findings of our study.

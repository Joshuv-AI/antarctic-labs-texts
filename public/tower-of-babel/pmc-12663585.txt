Optimized capacitance sensor configuration with enhanced sensitivity for annular two-phase flow measurement
Authors: Aryan Veisi, Mohsen Hayati
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A novel geometric configuration for capacitance-based sensors is presented to measure void fractions in annular two-phase flow systems. The new design significantly enhances sensitivity, surpassing previous configurations, achieving a sensitivity of 2.482 pF. This level of sensitivity represents a considerable improvement compared to conventional designs, enabling more accurate void fraction measurements and enhancing the reliability of phase detection, particularly in industrial applications where high precision is essential. The sensor’s innovative geometry was meticulously developed to optimize the electric field distribution, thereby improving the accuracy of void fraction measurements. Experimental validation was conducted using a water-air Two-phase flow setup in an upright tube. The experimental data demonstrated a low relative error, confirming the enhanced performance of the new sensor geometry. The novel sensor configuration offers significant advancements in accurately and reliably measuring void fractions in various industrial applications, particularly in electricity generation facilities, oil refineries, and chemical manufacturing sectors. The novelty of this study lies in the proposed twist-align-shaped electrode geometry, combined with systematic blade optimization and a unified simulation–experimental validation framework, providing enhanced sensitivity and robustness for annular two-phase flow measurement. This work lays the foundation for future developments in sensor technology to improve the monitoring and control of two-phase flow systems.

After determining the optimal sensor structure—including the number of blades, blade shape, and electrode spacing—through COMSOL-based simulations, the finalized twist-align-shaped sensor was fabricated and experimentally tested to validate the simulation findings under realistic two-phase conditions. The experimental setup included a vertical transparent acrylic pipe filled with oil to emulate the dielectric environment used in simulations. The pipe had a height of 40 cm and an inner diameter of 52 mm, identical to the simulated geometry. Static two-phase flow conditions were reproduced using air–oil phantom samples in 10% increments of void fraction (from 0% to 100%) by injecting precise volumes of air into the oil-filled pipe. To replicate annular two-phase flow conditions in a controlled and repeatable manner, a static phantom-based approach was employed instead of dynamic air injection. Specifically, a set of 3D-printed phantoms was designed to simulate various gas–liquid distributions representative of void fractions ranging from 0% to 100%. These phantoms were inserted into a transparent pipe section, allowing for precise sensor calibration and verification without the variability introduced by live flow conditions. This method ensured high repeatability and consistency in measurements while maintaining the geometric characteristics typical of annular flow patterns. A visual representation of the phantom models used in this setup is provided in Fig. 11. The optimized twist-align-shaped capacitive sensor was externally mounted on the pipe, replicating the same geometry and configuration used in simulation. For capacitance measurement, two high-precision LCR meters (Agilent E4980A, LCR-816) were used simultaneously. One measured the excitation electrode and the other measured the sensing electrode independently to ensure better noise isolation and improved signal integrity. A signal generator was used to provide a stable sinusoidal voltage to the excitation electrode, ensuring a consistent and repeatable electric field during the measurement process. Additionally, an oscilloscope was employed to monitor the input and output waveforms in real time, allowing verification of signal integrity and detection of any unwanted noise or waveform distortion during capacitance acquisition. Measurement uncertainty was estimated within ± 0.8%. A series of controlled tests using static phantom models with known void fractions (0–100%) was conducted to assess the sensor’s accuracy. The maximum deviation between measured and actual values was ± 0.8%, with overall variations remaining below 1%, confirming the sensor’s precision, repeatability, and reliability under static conditions. Result and discussion

This study employed the finite element method using COMSOL Multiphysics to analyze and optimize various sensor configurations for measuring void fractions in annular two-phase flows. The proposed twist-align-shaped capacitive sensor was evaluated against conventional designs such as concave, two-ring, spiral, and parallel plate configurations. Capacitance values were obtained through both simulation and experimental procedures. The measured data and simulation results, based on identical geometric and material parameters, are presented in Table 3. These datasets exhibit a strong correlation, with a maximum deviation of less than 1%, confirming the accuracy and practical reliability of the proposed sensor configuration. The comparative analysis of overall sensor sensitivity is shown in Fig. 13. The twist-align-shaped sensor demonstrated 22% higher sensitivity than the concave design22, 264% more than the two-ring sensor23, 21% more than the helix configuration18, 11.2% more than the arrow-shaped sensor35, and 234% more than the parallel plate sensor35. These results highlight the promising performance and consistent sensitivity of the proposed sensor in detecting phase fractions, suggesting its potential for use in industrial multiphase flow monitoring, particularly in the petrochemical and energy sectors.

Although the deviation between simulated and measured capacitance values remained below 1%, this result mainly reflects the model fidelity and calibration stability achieved under controlled static conditions. In actual industrial multiphase flow environments, measurement uncertainties are typically much higher due to dynamic regime transitions, temperature variations, and sensor alignment errors. Therefore, the observed sub-percent deviation should be interpreted as an indication of the model’s internal consistency rather than a guarantee of such accuracy in real operating conditions. Furthermore, in industrial practices such as reservoir management, production allocation, and cost estimation, even small systematic biases in void-fraction measurement can lead to substantial volumetric or financial discrepancies. Hence, maintaining calibration consistency—as demonstrated in this study—can significantly enhance the reliability of multiphase monitoring systems. Nevertheless, additional validation under dynamic annular-flow conditions remains essential to confirm field-level performance. It should be noted that the dielectric constant of the fluids involved in multiphase systems can vary significantly depending on the type of mixture. While the present experiments were performed using an air–water phantom for controlled comparison, crude oils, gas condensates, and emulsions exhibit dielectric constants ranging from 1 to 80. Such variation can shift the baseline capacitance and alter the sensitivity slope of the sensor. Therefore, for field applications, the system must be calibrated or compensated according to the dielectric properties of each fluid composition to ensure quantitative accuracy.

Limitation of static validation

It should be noted that the experimental validation in this study was performed using static phantom models to achieve high repeatability and eliminate flow-induced instabilities. While this method provides a reliable means to evaluate intrinsic sensor sensitivity and field uniformity, it does not fully capture the dynamic interface oscillations and flow-regime transitions that occur in real annular two-phase flows. Future work will focus on validating the proposed configuration under dynamic annular-flow conditions to confirm its field-level applicability.

Conclusion

This study introduces a novel geometric configuration for capacitance-based sensors designed to measure void fractions in annular two-phase flow systems. The twist-align-shaped sensor exhibits significantly enhanced sensitivity, achieving a notable improvement over the previous design. The new sensor geometry ensures more accurate void fraction measurements by optimizing the electric field distribution. Experimental validation, performed with a water-air two-phase flow setup, demonstrated that the new sensor configuration provides consistent agreement between simulated and measured capacitance values, confirming its reliable performance under controlled conditions. The twist-align-shaped sensor showed an 11.2% higher sensitivity than the arrow-shaped sensor. It surpassed other arrangements, such as the two-ring, concave, spiral, and parallel plate sensors, by 264%, 22%, 21%, and 234%, respectively. The proposed sensor’s design and performance were further validated through finite element method simulations and artificial intelligence optimization, highlighting the robustness and reliability of the sensor in various industrial applications. These advancements pave the way for more precise and reliable monitoring of biphasic flow systems, particularly in energy and petrochemical productions. This work establishes a solid foundation for future developments in sensor technology, aiming to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of void fraction measurement in industrial processes. The novel sensor configuration holds significant potential for improving the monitoring and control of multiphase flows, leading to better process control, reduced downtime, and enhanced operational precision. While our research concentrated on the annular flow regime, future studies could look into the efficiency of the optimized capacitance-based sensor in other flow patterns in order to determine the best design parameters for those regimes and to validate the sensor’s applicability in different flow conditions. Nevertheless, additional validation under dynamic annular-flow conditions remains essential to confirm field-level performance. Future work will include calibration of the proposed sensor across fluids of differing dielectric constants to ensure reliable performance in crude oil and condensate measurement environments.

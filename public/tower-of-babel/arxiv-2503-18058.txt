On the Classification of Isoparametric Hypersurfaces with Constant Principal Curvatures in Compact 3-Manifolds
Minghao Li, Ling Yang

Abstract
Establishing detailed relationships between transnormal systems of different types and their behaviors under covering maps, this paper presents a classification of transnormal systems on compact 3-manifolds in the sense of equivalence. For CPC transnormal systems, we show that the ambient manifolds must be locally isometric to one of six standard geometries up to equivalence. We also find some equivalence classes containing no CPC transnormal system, highlighting a critical distinction between isoparametric foliations and CPC transnormal systems, which has not been previously addressed in the literature.

Introduction

A smooth function
f

(M,g)
is called

|\nabla f|^{2}=b(f)
. If, in addition,
\Delta f=a(f)
for another smooth function

. Each regular level hypersurface of an isoparametric function is called an
isoparametric hypersurface
. The classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces has been extensively studied in various spaces. In Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces, the classification was completed by Cartan in
[

,

]
. In the standard spheres, the classification, which is significantly more challenging, was initiated by Cartan and eventually completed through contributions from many works, such as
[

,

,

,
Mün80
,

,

,

,

,

,
CCJ07
,

,
Chi11
,
Chi13
,
Miy13a
,
Miy16
,

]
, with further surveys available in
[

]
. Further classifications have been explored in spaces such as complex projective spaces
\mathbb{C}P^{n}

[
Wan82
,
Kim86
,

,

]
, complex hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}

[

,

,

,

]
, compact symmetric spaces
[

\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}

[

.

For general Riemannian manifolds, thanks to the tubular regularity of transnormal functions in
[
Wan87
]
, an arbitrary transnormal function induces an embedded transnormal system of codimension one. A
transnormal system

\mathcal{F}
on a complete Riemannian manifold
M
is a partition of
M
into connected submanifolds, called
foils
, so that any geodesic in
M
intersects these foils orthogonally at all or none of its points (see
[
Bol73
]
).
\mathcal{F}
is
embedded
if all foils are embedded; the
codimension
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as the codimension of the foil with the greatest dimension. Conversely, it is shown that any embedded transnormal system of codimension one can be induced by a transnormal function, and in the case of compact manifolds, it can further be realized as an isoparametric function. Precisely, the following results hold:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Miy13b
,
LY24
]
)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is an embedded transnormal system of codimension one on a connected complete manifold
M
, then
M
admits a transnormal function
f
such that the transnormal system
\mathcal{F}_{f}
induced by
f
coincides with
\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
LY24
]
)
.

For each compact manifold
M
, the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

M
admits a linear double disk bundle decomposition.

(b)

M
can be endowed with a Riemannian metric so that it admits an embedded transnormal system of codimension one.

(c)

M
can be endowed with a Riemannian metric so that it admits a transnormal function.

(d)

M
can be endowed with a Riemannian metric so that it admits an isoparametric function.

Moreover, in the equivalence of Theorem
1.2
, the foils of the transnormal system correspond precisely to the base spaces of the disk bundles and to the connected components of their subbundles with hypersphere fibers, where an SR-foil or an S-foil, if it exists, is exactly a base space.

The next natural step is to investigate the classification of transnormal functions and manifolds admitting them. In contrast to the classification of isoparametric functions (or transnormal functions) on a fixed Riemannian manifold, the classification in general Riemannian manifolds cannot, in general, be carried out up to isometry. Instead, we develop the classification in the following equivalent sense:

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
be transnormal systems on Riemannian manifolds
M
and
M^{{}^{\prime}}
, respectively.
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
are
equivalent
if there exists a diffeomorphism
\phi:M\to M^{{}^{\prime}}
such that
\phi(F)\in\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

For simply connected compact Riemannian manifolds, the classification is well understood in low dimensions. In dimension four, any Riemannian manifold admitting a transnormal function is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{4}
,
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, or
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}\#\pm\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, as established in
[
GR15
]
. The five-dimensional case was later settled in
[
DGGK23
]
, identifying the diffeomorphism types as
\mathbb{S}^{5}
, the Wu manifold
\operatorname{SU}(3)/\operatorname{SO}(3)
,
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and the unique twisted
\mathbb{S}^{3}
-bundle over
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In dimension three, the problem is trivial as a consequence of the Poincaré conjecture. However, much less is known when the fundamental group is nontrivial. It is important to note that such cases cannot be resolved by lifting to the universal cover: transnormal systems and functions depend on the Riemannian metric, and the isometric covering transformation group is not invariant under diffeomorphisms. A more detailed discussion of this issue is given in Remark
4.27
.

In this paper, we initiate the study of the classification of compact Riemannian manifolds that admit transnormal functions and systems, extending beyond the simply connected case. In particular, the classification of transnormal systems on compact 3-manifolds is carried out based on their types. More precisely, according to the base spaces of the disk bundles in Theorem
1.2
, they fall into the following four types:

•

toric type
: both base spaces have codimension one, and both disk bundles are trivial;

•

Klein-bottled type
: both base spaces have codimension one, and both disk bundles are non-trivial;

•

spherical type
: both base spaces have codimension greater than one;

•

real-projective type
: one base space has codimension one, while the other has codimension greater than one.

•

The Klein spaces

and

[

,

]
. The space
\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\tau}}
is the mapping torus induced by
\tilde{\tau}
. The space
\mathcal{K}_{\tilde{\sigma}_{1};\tilde{\sigma}_{2}}
is defined as
(

.

•

“None cohomogeneity-one action inducing

and Table

means that no transnormal system in this equivalence class can be induced by a cohomogeneity-one action.

•

Some spaces with simple structures are contained in “other

\tilde{\tau}
is the identity of the torus,
\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\tau}}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

•

There may exist not only transnormal systems of different types (as shown in Table
3
), but also multiple transnormal systems of the same type on a given manifold. For instance, the standard 3-sphere admits a transnormal system of spherical type whose DR-foils are all homeomorphic to the 2-sphere, as well as another whose DR-foils are all homeomorphic to the torus; for
\mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, it admits both a transnormal system of real-projective type whose DR-foils are all homeomorphic to the torus and another whose DR-foils are all homeomorphic to the Klein bottle.

•

Since many Klein spaces are also lens spaces (see
[

), some 3-manifolds simultaneously belong to both ”Other lens spaces” and ”Other Klein spaces” in Table

for odd

. Likewise, some 3-manifolds simultaneously belong to both ”Other

” and ”Other
\mathcal{K}_{\tilde{\sigma}_{1};\tilde{\sigma}_{2}}
” (for example,
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma_{\mathbf{K}};\sigma_{\mathbf{K}}}
is also a mapping torus, where
\sigma_{\mathbf{K}}
is a fixed-point-free involution on the Klein bottle). Other spaces in Table

are all topologically distinct. This follows from the fact that, aside from the first six cases listed in Table

are infinitely covered by
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Since the classification is carried out in the sense of equivalence, a natural question arises: does each equivalence class admit a representative with desired geometric properties? By ‘desired’, we mean the following conditions on the representative:

(A)

Each foil has constant mean curvature.

(B)

Each foil has constant principal curvatures.

(C)

The ambient manifold is locally isometric to a standard space.

(D)

Conditions (A) and (C) hold simultaneously.

(E)

Conditions (B) and (C) hold simultaneously.

Note that for transnormal systems induced by cohomogeneity one actions, all above conditions are clearly satisfied. Due to Theorem
1.2
, any transnormal system on a compact manifold is equivalent to an isoparametric foliation, which satisfies condition (A). To express condition (B), we define:

Definition 1.4
.

A transnormal system
\mathcal{F}
on a Riemannian manifold is called a
CPC transnormal system
if each foil in
\mathcal{F}
has constant principal curvatures.

Some properties of CPC transnormal systems of codimension one are studied in
[
GT14
]
. In the remainder of this paper, we focus on the equivalence classes of transnormal systems on orientable manifolds, and obtain the following conclusion:

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
M
is an orientable compact Riemannian 3-manifold, and
\mathcal{F}
is an embedded CPC transnormal system of codimension one. Then, up to equivalence within CPC transnormal systems,

M
is locally isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, Nil geometry, or Sol geometry.

As a consequence, we see that if condition (B) holds, then a refined choice of representative can ensure that (C) and (E) hold as well, and the standard spaces involved are precisely six of the eight Thurston’s geometries. However, somewhat surprisingly, condition (B) does not always hold:

Theorem 1.6
.

There exist embedded transnormal systems of codimension one on compact Riemannian 3-manifolds such that their equivalence classes contain no CPC transnormal systems.

Additionally, the equivalence classes in Theorem
1.6
sometimes do not even contain any transnormal system satisfying condition (D), as elaborated in Example
4.26
. This suggests that, compared to cohomogeneity-one actions, transnormal systems impose significantly weaker topological and geometric constraints on the manifolds. On the other hand, while the topological equivalence between isoparametric foliations and transnormal systems has already been established in Theorem
1.2
, we now identify a critical distinction between isoparametric foliations and CPC transnormal systems in dimension three. This aspect has not been addressed in any dimension in the existing literature.

3
Covering and lifting on transnormal systems

We explore the relationships among transnormal systems. Throughout this section, compactness of the ambient manifolds is not assumed.

3.1
Basic definitions and properties

We introduce some actions on the manifolds that are adapted to the transnormal systems.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an embedded transnormal system of codimension one on a complete Riemannian manifold
M
.

(i)

A diffeomorphism
\tau
on
M
is called a
foil-translation
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
, if there exists a foil
L\in\mathcal{F}
and a positive real number
t_{0}
such that

\tau\big(\exp_{L}(t,B(L))\big)=\exp_{L}\big(t+t_{0},B(L)\big)

holds for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

A diffeomorphism
\sigma
on
M
is called a
foil-reflection
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
, if there exists a DR-foil
L\in\mathcal{F}
such that

\sigma\big(\exp_{L}(t,B(L))\big)=\exp_{L}(-t,B(L))

(1)

holds for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This DR-foil
L
is called the
mirror foil
of the foil-reflection
\sigma
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the definition of a foil-reflection, it is essential that the mirror foil be a DR-foil. If
L
is an SR-foil or an S-foil, then the identity map satisfies equation
(
1
)
. However, the identity map clearly should not be regarded as a foil-reflection.

We have known the foil space
M\big/\mathcal{F}
is homeomorphic to one of
\mathbb{R}
,
[0,+\infty)
,
[0,1]
or
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. From Definition
3.1
, we can derive that the foil-translations and foil-reflections induce the corresponding translations and reflections on
M\big/\mathcal{F}
, which implies the following two facts:

•

If
\tau
is a foil-translation with respect to
\mathcal{F}
, then the foil space
M\big/\mathcal{F}
must have no endpoints, or equivalently,
\mathcal{F}
is of cylindrical or toric type.

•

If
\sigma
is a foil-reflection with respect to
\mathcal{F}
, then the foil space
M\big/\mathcal{F}
must have either 0 or 2 endpoints, and the foils corresponding to the endpoints, if they exist, must be either both SR-foils or both S-foils. Thus,
\mathcal{F}
is of cylindrical, toric, spherical or Klein-bottled type.

For an embedded transnormal system
\mathcal{F}
of codimension one, suppose that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are distinct foils in
\mathcal{F}
, each being either an SR-foil or an S-foil. According to Lemma
2.2
, these two foils share a common finite injectivity radius
T
, and

\mathcal{N}_{T}(L_{1})\cap\mathcal{N}_{T}(L_{2})=\mathcal{N}_{T}(L_{1})\backslash L_{1}=M\backslash\left(L_{1}\cup L_{2}\right).

(2)

The inverse
V\mapsto-V
on
\text{NS}(L_{1})
, denoted by
\tilde{\sigma}_{L_{1}}
here, induces an involution on
\mathcal{N}_{T}(L_{1})
as

\sigma_{L_{1}}\big(\exp_{L_{1}}(t,V)\big):=\exp_{L_{1}}(t,\tilde{\sigma}_{L_{1}}(V)),\quad(t,V)\in[0,T)\times\text{NS}(L_{1}).

Similarly, we can define
\tilde{\sigma}_{L_{2}}
on
\text{NS}(L_{2})
and
\sigma_{L_{2}}
on
\mathcal{N}_{T}(L_{2})
. Given
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
V\in\text{NS}(L_{1})
, it follows from
(
2
)
that
\exp_{L_{1}}(t_{0},V)\in\mathcal{N}_{T}(L_{2})\backslash L_{2}
, and thus, there exists a unique
V^{{}^{\prime}}\in\text{NS}(L_{1})
such that
\sigma_{L_{2}}\big(\exp_{L_{1}}(t_{0},V)\big)=\exp_{L_{1}}(t_{0},V^{{}^{\prime}})
. By a standard argument on normal geodesics,
V^{{}^{\prime}}
is independent of the choice of
t_{0}\in(0,T)
. In other words, the map
V\mapsto V^{{}^{\prime}}
defines an involution on
\text{NS}(L_{1})
induced by
\sigma_{L_{2}}
, which we also denote by
\tilde{\sigma}_{L_{2}}
, and it satisfies

\exp_{L_{1}}(t,\tilde{\sigma}_{L_{2}}(V))=\sigma_{L_{2}}\big(\exp_{L_{1}}(t,V)\big),

for any
t\in(0,T)
and
V\in\text{NS}(L_{1})
. It is worth noting that there is no ambiguity between the involution
\tilde{\sigma}_{L_{2}}
on
\text{NS}(L_{1})
and the natural involution
\tilde{\sigma}_{L_{2}}
on
\text{NS}(L_{2})
, since they act on distinct domains.

We now turn to the relationships among the cylindrical, toric, and Klein-bottled types.

3.2
Relationships among transnormal systems of various types

Now, we establish some relationships among transnormal systems of various types through foil-translations and foil-reflections.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is an embedded transnormal system of codimension one on a complete Riemannian manifold
M
. Then,

(i)

\mathcal{F}
is of toric type, if and only if there exists another complete Riemannian manifold
\tilde{M}
equipped with a transnormal system
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
of cylindrical type, and an isometric foil-translation
\tau
with respect to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\tilde{M}\big/\langle\tau\rangle=M
.

(ii)

\mathcal{F}
is of twisted-cylindrical type, if and only if there exists another complete Riemannian manifold
\tilde{M}
equipped with a transnormal system
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
of cylindrical type, along with an isometric foil-reflection
\sigma
with respect to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
that is a fixed-point-free involution on
\tilde{M}
, such that
\tilde{M}\big/\langle\sigma\rangle=M
.

(iii)

\mathcal{F}
is of Klein-bottled type, if and only if there exists another complete Riemannian manifold
\tilde{M}
equipped with a transnormal system
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
of cylindrical type, along with two isometric foil-reflections
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
with distinct mirror foils with respect to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
those both are fixed-point-free involutions on
\tilde{M}
, such that
\tilde{M}\big/\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle=M
.

(iv)

\mathcal{F}
is of real-projective type, if and only if there exists another complete Riemannian manifold
\tilde{M}
equipped with a transnormal system
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
of spherical type, along with an isometric foil-reflection
\sigma
with respect to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
that is a fixed-point-free involution on
\tilde{M}
, such that
\tilde{M}\big/\langle\sigma\rangle=M
.

Proof..

3.3
Lifting of transnormal systems along covering maps

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a transnormal system on a Riemannian manifold
(M,\textrm{g})
induced by a transnormal function
f
, and let
\hat{\pi}:\hat{M}\to M
be a regular Riemannian covering map with covering transformation group
G=G(\hat{M},\hat{\pi})
. The function
f\circ\hat{\pi}
induces a transnormal system
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
on
\hat{M}
. For any

and
\hat{F}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
,

is another connected component of

, thus also a foil in
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. In other words, each transformation in

is a foil-preserving isometry of
(\hat{M},\hat{\mathcal{F}})
. Consider the subset

G_{0}=G_{0}(\hat{M},\hat{\pi}):=\left\{g\in G:g(\hat{F})=\hat{F},\forall\,\hat{F}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}\right\},

(5)

which is indeed a normal subgroup of

. Then, it follows that the projection

is a regular covering map, and
\tilde{\pi}:\tilde{M}\to M
is also a regular covering map whose covering transformation group

is isomorphic to the quotient group

. Consequently,

induces a transnormal system
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
on
\tilde{M}
such that

for any
\hat{F}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
, and
\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{F})\in\mathcal{F}
for any
\tilde{F}\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
. Precisely, we have

Proposition 3.7
.

A regular covering map
\hat{\pi}:(\hat{M},\hat{\mathcal{F}})\to(M,\mathcal{F})
can be uniquely decomposed into a regular covering sequence

satisfying the following conditions:

(C1)

for every

and
\hat{F}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
, it holds that
g(\hat{F})=\hat{F}
;

(C2)

if
\phi\in G(\tilde{M},\tilde{\pi})
satisfies
\phi(\tilde{F})=\tilde{F}
for any
\tilde{F}\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
, then
\phi
is the identity.

Proof..

The existence of the decomposition has already been explained in the previous discussion; now, it suffices to show the uniqueness. Assume
(\hat{M},\hat{\mathcal{F}})\xrightarrow{p^{{}^{\prime}}}(\tilde{M}^{{}^{\prime}},\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{{}^{\prime}})\xrightarrow{\tilde{\pi}^{{}^{\prime}}}(M,\mathcal{F})
is another regular covering sequence that satisfies the conditions. By condition (C1),
G(\hat{M},p^{{}^{\prime}})
is a subgroup of
G_{0}
in
(
5
)
. We claim that
G(\hat{M},p^{{}^{\prime}})=G_{0}
. Otherwise, suppose

. Since
\tilde{\pi}^{{}^{\prime}}\circ p^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\phi=\tilde{\pi}^{{}^{\prime}}\circ p^{{}^{\prime}}
, there exists some
\psi_{x}\in G(\tilde{M}^{{}^{\prime}},\tilde{\pi}^{{}^{\prime}})
for any

such that
\psi_{x}\circ p^{{}^{\prime}}(x)=p^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\phi(x)
. Due to the discontinuity of

, we can derive that

is independent of

, and thus

. Because

is not identity. However,

Moreover, the covering map

In sequence
(
6
)

We focus on the classification of transnormal systems on compact three-manifolds in the equivariant sense of Definition

4.1

We firstly provide the criterion for equivalence of two transnormal systems of toric type.

Proposition 4.1

Proof..

When

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

be a closed surface, and

collect all the equivalence classes of transnormal systems of toric type on compact 3-manifolds with DR-foils homeomorphic to

. Then,

is in one-to-one correspondence with the set

\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}\left(S\right)\big/\sim,

where
\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}\left(S\right)
is the extended mapping class group of

and
\sim
is an equivalence relation generated by inverses and conjugates.

Remark 4.3
.

A detailed discussion of the extended mapping class group can be found in
[

, Chap.8]
. The quotient set
\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}\left(S\right)\big/\sim
does not necessarily form a group. In this paper, the equivalence class of the homeomorphism

in

is denoted by
\llbracket\tilde{\tau}\rrbracket
.

Example 4.4
.

Let
S
be a closed surface, and
\tilde{\tau}
be a homeomorphism of
S
satisfying
\tilde{\tau}^{k}=id
for some positive integer

. Given any Riemannian metric
\textrm{g}_{0}
on
S
, the metric

\textrm{g}_{S}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(\tilde{\tau}^{*})^{i}\,\textrm{g}_{0}

is clearly invariant under the action of
\tilde{\tau}
. Consequently, the map
\tau(t,x)=(t+1,\tilde{\tau}(x))
is an isometry on the product space
\left(\mathbb{R}\times S,\textrm{d}t^{2}+\textrm{g}_{S}\right)
. By Proposition
3.3
, this induces a transnormal system
\mathcal{F}
of toric type on
\mathbb{R}\times S\big/\langle\tau\rangle
, whose equivalence class corresponds to
\llbracket\tilde{\tau}\rrbracket\in\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}\left(S\right)\big/\sim
. Moreover, all foils in
\mathcal{F}
are totally geodesic.

Example 4.5
.

When DR-foils are homeomorphic to the 2-sphere, the set
\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}\left(\mathbb{S}^{2}\right)\big/\sim
consists of only two elements, which corresponds to the identify

and the antipodal map
\sigma_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

\mathcal{M}_{id_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}
and

are homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
and the twisted
\mathbb{S}^{2}

Example 4.6
.

When DR-foils are homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, the set
\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}\left(\mathbb{R}P^{2}\right)\big/\sim
consists of a single element
\llbracket id_{\mathbb{R}P^{2}}\rrbracket
. The mapping torus
\mathcal{M}_{id_{\mathbb{R}P^{2}}}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}
.

Example 4.7
.

Assume
\tilde{\tau}

Example 4.8
.

.

Next, we will focus on CPC transnormal system

of toric type on orientable compact 3-manifold

. The cases of non-orientable manifolds can be discussed in a similar manner.
Assume the foils in
\mathcal{F}
are all homeomorphic to

. Since

is covered by

ensures that

is orientable, and the associated foil-translation preserves orientation. Similarly, the set of such equivlence classes of transnormal systems is in one-to-one correspondence with

where

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
M
be an orientable compact 3-manifold, and
\mathcal{F}
be a transnormal system of toric type on
M
whose foils are homeomorphic to the orientable closed surface
S_{g}
of genus
g
.

(i)

If
g\leq 1
, then, up to equivlence,
\mathcal{F}
is a CPC transnormal system, and meanwhile
M
is locally isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, Nil geometry or Sol geometry.

(ii)

If
g\geq 2
,
\mathcal{F}
is equivalently a CPC transnormal system if and only if
\tilde{\tau}
is an orientation-preserving periodic homeomorphism of
S_{g}
and the equivalence class of
\mathcal{F}
corresponds to
\llbracket\tilde{\tau}\rrbracket\in\operatorname{Mod}\left(S_{g}\right)\big/\sim
. In this case, up to equivlence of CPC transnormal systems,
M
is locally isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof..

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
corresponds to
\llbracket\tilde{\tau}\rrbracket\in\operatorname{Mod}\left(S\right)\big/\sim
.

If
g=0
, the mapping class group of sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is trivial, and so is
\operatorname{Mod}\left(\mathbb{S}^{2}\right)\big/\sim
.
\mathcal{F}
is equivalent to the transnormal system
\big\{\{\theta\}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\big\}_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In this case, the conclusion naturally follows.

If
g=1
, according to the classification of homeomorphisms on torus
T^{2}
(see
[
FM11
, Chap.13]
),
\tilde{\tau}
is isotopic to a periodic, reducible, or Anosov homeomorphism. Within each periodic isotopy class, there exists a homeomorphism acting isometrically on a flat torus. Thus, by Example
4.4
, it follows that, up to equivalence,
M
is locally isometric to Euclidean
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and each foil is totally geodesic.
If
\tilde{\tau}
is isotopic to a reducible homeomorphism, it can be assumed to be a multiple of a Dehn twist. By analogy with Example
4.7
, this gives rise to a CPC transnormal system, with the ambient manifold being locally isometric to Nil geometry. If
\tilde{\tau}
is isotopic to an Anosov homeomorphism, it corresponds to a CPC transnormal system, where the ambient manifold is locally isometric to Sol geometry, as in Example
4.8
.

When
g\geq 2
, we firstly assume
\tilde{\tau}
is a periodic homeomorphism of
S_{g}
and the equivalence class of
\mathcal{F}
corresponds to
\llbracket\tilde{\tau}\rrbracket\in\operatorname{Mod}\left(S_{g}\right)\big/\sim
. By the uniformization theorem and the fact that conformal maps on the hyperbolic plane are isometries,
\tilde{\tau}
is an isometry of
S_{g}
with respect to a hyperbolic metric. It then follows from Example
4.4
that, up to equivalence, the foils in
\mathcal{F}
are totally geodesic and the ambient manifold is locally isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.
As for the converse, if
\mathcal{F}
is a CPC transnormal system, we consider the essential cover
\big(\mathbb{R}\times S_{g},\textrm{d}t^{2}+\textrm{g}_{S_{g}}(t)\big)
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
, where each
\{t\}\times S_{g}
has constant principal curvatures. By the line bundle version of the Poincaré-Hopf theorem (
[
Hop03
, 2.2. Theorem II]
), the two constant principal curvatures on
\{t\}\times S_{g}
are equal, denoted by
\kappa(t)
. It follows that
X=\exp\{-\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)\textrm{d}s\}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
is a closed conformal vector field. According to
[
Mon99
]
, the metric
\textrm{d}t^{2}+\textrm{g}_{S_{g}}(t)
is factually a warped product metric, i.e.,
\textrm{g}_{S_{g}}(t)=f^{2}(t)\textrm{g}_{S_{g}}
for a fixed metric
\textrm{g}_{S_{g}}
and a smooth positive function
f(t)
. Since the associated foil-translation
\tau(t,x)=(t+t_{0},\tilde{\tau}(x))
on
\mathbb{R}\times S_{g}
is an isometry with respect to
\textrm{d}t^{2}+f^{2}(t)\textrm{g}_{S_{g}}
, we obtain that
f(t)=f(t+t_{0})
and
\tilde{\tau}
is isometric on
(S_{g},\textrm{g}_{S_{g}})
. By the uniformization theorem,
\langle\tilde{\tau}\rangle
is a conformal subgroup of
S_{g}
with respect to some hyperbolic metric. Note that the conformal subgroups of any closed hyperbolic surface are finite,
\tilde{\tau}
must be a periodic homeomorphism.
∎

4.2
Transnormal systems of Klein-bottled type

The criterion for equivalence of two transnormal systems of Klein-bottled type is as follows.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
be transnormal systems of Klein-bottled type on Riemannian manifolds
M
and
M^{{}^{\prime}}
, respectively. They are equivalent if and only if there exists a DR-foil
F_{t}\in\mathcal{F}
, a DR-foil
F^{{}^{\prime}}_{t^{{}^{\prime}}}\in\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
, and two homeomorphisms
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}:F_{t}\to F^{{}^{\prime}}_{t^{{}^{\prime}}}
smoothly isotopic to each other, such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{\sigma}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}=\varphi_{1}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{1}\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1},\\
&\tilde{\sigma}^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}=\varphi_{2}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\circ\varphi_{2}^{-1},\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{or}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{\sigma}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}=\varphi_{1}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1},\\
&\tilde{\sigma}^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}=\varphi_{2}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}^{-1},\end{aligned}\right.

(12)

holds. Here,
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}(x)
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}(x)
are the points at which the normal geodesic starting from
x\in F_{t}
in opposite directions first returns to
F_{t}
, whereas
\tilde{\sigma}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}
and
\tilde{\sigma}^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}
are defined similarly on
F^{{}^{\prime}}_{t^{{}^{\prime}}}
.

Remark 4.11
.

According to the properties of transnormal systems of Klein-bottled type in Section
3
, if
\nu
is a unit vector field normal to a DR-foil
F_{t}
and the normal geodesic
\gamma(t)
with
\gamma(0)=x\in F_{t},\dot{\gamma}(0)=\nu(x)
first returns to
F_{t}
at
\gamma(t_{1})=\tilde{\sigma}_{1}(x)\in F_{t}
, then
\gamma(t)
passes through an SR-foil at
\gamma(\frac{t_{1}}{2})
, and the tangent vector
\dot{\gamma}(t_{1})
is
-\nu\big(\gamma(t_{1})\big)
. Therefore,
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
is an involution. Furthermore, the normal geodesic starting with tangent vector
-\nu
will pass through the other SR-foil before returning to
F_{t}
.

Proof..

Assume a diffeomorphism
\varphi:M\to M^{{}^{\prime}}
induces the equivalence between
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}=\{F^{{}^{\prime}}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T^{{}^{\prime}}]}
. By Propositions
3.3
and
3.12
, and a process analogous to Proposition
4.1
, we obtain a diffeomorphism
\Phi:(s,x)\mapsto(h(s),\Phi_{s}(x))
between the essential covers
\mathbb{R}\times F
and
\mathbb{R}\times F^{{}^{\prime}}
induced by
\varphi
. Let

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sigma_{1}(s,x)=(-s,\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle 1,F}(x)),\\
&\sigma_{2}(s,x)=(2T-s,\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle 2,F}(x)),\end{aligned}\right.

be the foil-reflections generating the covering transformation group of the essential cover of
(M,\mathcal{F})
, and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}(s,x^{{}^{\prime}})=(-s,\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 1,F}(x^{{}^{\prime}})),\\
&\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}(s,x^{{}^{\prime}})=(2T^{{}^{\prime}}-s,\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 2,F}(x^{{}^{\prime}})),\end{aligned}\right.

similarly for
(M^{{}^{\prime}},\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}})
. Without loss of generality, we assume

,

, and then

\left\{\begin{aligned} &h(s)+h(-s)=0,\quad h(T+s)+h(T-s)=2T^{{}^{\prime}},\\
&\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle 1,F^{{}^{\prime}}}^{{}^{\prime}}=\Phi_{-s}\circ\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle 1,F}\circ\Phi_{s}^{-1},\quad\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle 2,F^{{}^{\prime}}}^{{}^{\prime}}=\Phi_{2T-s}\circ\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle 2,F}\circ\Phi_{s}^{-1}.\end{aligned}\right.

(13)

Identifying
F
with

, the transformations
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}
coincide with

and

and

.

The proof of the converse part is nearly identical to that of Proposition

, except that the function

is now defined as

g\geq 2
,
\mathcal{F}
is equivalently a CPC transnormal system if and only if
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}
are orientation-reversing fixed-point-free involutions of
S_{g}
satisfying that
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
is periodic, and the equivalence class of
\mathcal{F}
corresponds to
\llbracket\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\rrbracket\in\operatorname{Inv}^{-}_{0}(S_{g})\times\operatorname{Inv}^{-}_{0}(S_{g})\big/\sim
. In this case, up to equivlence of CPC transnormal systems,
M
is locally isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof..

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is induced by
(\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\tilde{\sigma}_{2})\in\operatorname{Inv}^{-}_{0}(S_{g})\times\operatorname{Inv}^{-}_{0}(S_{g})
.

If
g=0
, then
\mathcal{F}
is equivalently the same as the one in Example
4.13
. Recalling the CPC transnormal system of toric type with DR-foils homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in the proof of Proposition
4.9
and considering
\bar{\sigma}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
defined by
\bar{\sigma}(e^{i\theta},x)=(e^{-i\theta},\sigma_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x))
, we obtain a CPC transnormal system of Klein-bottled type on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\big/\langle\bar{\sigma}\rangle
by Corollary
3.5
. In other words,
\mathbb{R}P^{3}\#\mathbb{R}P^{3}
can be equipped with a Riemannian metric, locally isometric to the product space
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, such that it admits a CPC transnormal system of Klein-bottled type.

If
g=1
, it follows from the classification of involutions (
[
Aso76
]
) that any orientation-reversing fixed-point-free involution on torus is conjugate to the unique involution
\sigma_{-}
defined as

\sigma_{-}:\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad[x,y]\mapsto[x+\frac{1}{2},-y].

(14)

According to Proposition
4.10
, it suffices to consider the cases
(\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\tilde{\sigma}_{2})=(h\circ\sigma_{-}\circ h^{-1},\sigma_{-})
, with
h(x,y)=(h_{11}x+h_{12}y,h_{21}x+h_{22}y)
and
(h_{ij})\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. By Corollary
3.5
,
(M,\mathcal{F})
is covered by
(\bar{M},\bar{\mathcal{F}})
as a two-sheeted covering with
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
being a transnormal system of toric type induced by
\tilde{\tau}=\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
. Directly,

\displaystyle\tilde{\tau}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,\sigma_{-}\circ h\circ\sigma_{-}\circ h^{-1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\bigl((h_{11}h_{22}+h_{12}h_{21})x-2h_{11}h_{12}y+\frac{1}{2}(h_{11}+1),-2h_{21}h_{22}x+(h_{11}h_{22}+h_{12}h_{21})y-\frac{1}{2}h_{21}\bigr).

In the following, we discuss each case separately, depending on whether
\tilde{\tau}
is isotopically periodic, reducible, or Anosov, which is determined by the matrix

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{11}h_{22}+h_{12}h_{21}&-2h_{11}h_{12}\\
-2h_{21}h_{22}&h_{11}h_{22}+h_{12}h_{21}\end{array}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}).

For each case, we verify that the corresponding foil-reflection is isometric under the Riemannian metric described in the proof of Proposition
4.9
.

If
\tilde{\tau}
is isotopic to a periodic homeomorphism,
A
is periodic and direct computation yields
A=\pm I
. Then, the foil-reflection
\sigma_{2}(t,x,y)=(-t,\sigma_{-}(x,y))
is obviously isometric under the corresponding metric. If
\tilde{\tau}
is isotopic to a reducible homeomorphism, we find that

A=\pm\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&2k\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\ \text{or}\ \pm\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
2k&1\end{array}\right)

for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
, and then
\sigma_{2}
is also isometric under the corresponding metric. As for the case that
\tilde{\tau}
is isotopic to an Anosov homeomorphism, through some calculations, we obtain
h_{11}h_{12}h_{21}h_{22}\neq 0
and the eigenvectors of
A
in
(
11
)
are

\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{1}}=\sqrt{h_{11}h_{12}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\sqrt{h_{21}h_{22}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{2}}=\sqrt{h_{11}h_{12}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-\sqrt{h_{21}h_{22}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

The metric in
(
10
)
is exactly

\textrm{g}=\ln^{2}\lambda\textrm{d}t^{2}+\lambda^{-2t}\left(\frac{\textrm{d}x}{2\sqrt{h_{11}h_{12}}}+\frac{\textrm{d}y}{2\sqrt{h_{21}h_{22}}}\right)+\lambda^{2t}\left(\frac{\textrm{d}x}{2\sqrt{h_{11}h_{12}}}-\frac{\textrm{d}y}{2\sqrt{h_{21}h_{22}}}\right),

under which
\sigma_{2}
is also isometric. Hence, we complete the proof for the case
g=1
.

When
g\geq 2
, if
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
is periodic, the group
G
generated by
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}
is finite. We obtain a
G
-invariant hyperbolic metric on
S_{g}
and a CPC transnormal system of Klein-bottled type as in Proposition
4.9
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{F}
is a CPC transnormal system of Klein-bottled type, the foils of the related transnormal system
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
of toric type in Corollary
3.5
also have constant principal curvatures, and
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
is induced by
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
. The remainder of the proof follows exactly the same procedure as in the proof of Proposition
4.9
.
∎

4.3
Transnormal systems of spherical type

We now suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a transnormal system of spherical type on Riemannian manifold
M
. We have known from Theorem
1.2
that it induces a linear double disk bundle decomposition
(\mathcal{D}_{1}\to L_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}\to L_{2},\phi:\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\partial\mathcal{D}_{2})
on
M
. Precisely,
\mathcal{P}_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\to L_{k}
is a linear
d_{k}
-disk bundle over
L_{k}
for
k=1,2
, where
d_{k}
is the codimension of
L_{k}
in
M
;
M
is obtained by attaching
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
along their common boundary by the diffeomorphism
\phi:\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\partial\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Moreover, when
\mathcal{F}
is of spherical type, both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are greater than one. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
is another transnormal system of spherical type on
M^{{}^{\prime}}
with each notation differing by a prime superscript. We provide a sufficient condition for determining the equivalence of two transnormal systems of spherical type.

Proposition 4.16
.

If there exist bundle isomorphisms
\varphi_{k}
between linear disk bundles
\mathcal{D}_{k}
and
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{k}
for
k=1,2
such that
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}
is smoothly isotopic to
\left.\varphi_{2}\circ\phi\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}}
, then
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
are equivalent transnormal systems.

Proof..

Since
\varphi_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\to\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{k}
is a bundle isomorphism, there is a neighborhood
C
(resp.
C^{{}^{\prime}}
) of
\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}
(resp.
\partial\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}
) diffeomorphic to
\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}\times(1-\varepsilon,1]
(resp.
\partial\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}\times(1-\varepsilon,1]
), such that
\varphi_{1}
restricted to
C
maps
(r,y)\in C
to
\big(r,\left.\varphi_{1}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}}(y)\big)\in C^{{}^{\prime}}
. Without loss of generality, assume
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}=\left.\varphi_{2}\circ\phi\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}}\circ h^{-1}
with
h:\partial\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}\to\partial\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}
smoothly isotopic to identity. Define
\Phi_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}
as

\Phi_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varphi_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathcal{D}_{1}-C,\\
&\left(r,\Psi_{r}(y)\right),\quad&x=(r,y)\in C,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\{\Psi_{r}\}_{r\in[1-\varepsilon,1]}
is the smooth isotopy between
\left.\varphi_{1}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}}
and
h\circ\left.\varphi_{1}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}}
. Obviously,
\Phi_{1}
maps foils to foils, and
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}=\left.\varphi_{2}\circ\phi\circ\Phi_{1}^{-1}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}}
. Then,
(\Phi_{1},\varphi_{2})
forms a diffeomorphism from
M
to
M^{{}^{\prime}}
, and the equivalence between
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
follows.
∎

When
M
is a compact 3-manifold and
\mathcal{F}
is of spherical type, each S-foil is either a single point or a circle. If the S-foil
L_{1}
is a single point,
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is diffeomorphic to a
3
-disk and the linear disk bundle
\mathcal{P}_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to L_{1}
is unique up to linear bundle isomorphism. At that time, DR-foils are all diffeomorphic to the
2
-sphere. If
L_{1}
is a circle, the linear disk bundle
\mathcal{P}_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to L_{1}
have two possible structures up to linear bundle isomorphism:
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is diffeomorphic to either a solid torus or a solid Klein bottle, depending on whether the bundle is trivial or twisted. In these cases, the DR-foils are all diffeomorphic to the torus or the Klein bottle, respectively. A similar discussion applies to the other S-foil
L_{2}
. Therefore, these two linear disk bundles must be isomorphic to each other.

Case 1:
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are both single points.

M
, obtained by gluing two
3
-disks, is diffeomorphic to the
3
-sphere. It follows easily from Proposition
4.16
that there exists a unique equivalence class of transnormal systems of spherical type. Up to equivalence, this transnormal system is induced by an
\operatorname{SO}(3,\mathbb{R})
action on the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

Case 2:
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are both circles, while
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are both solid tori.

Gluing
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
along their common boundary, we obtain the well-known lens space
L(p,q)
for coprime
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 4.17
.

In order to discuss precisely the equivalence classes of transnormal systems in this case, we briefly introduce the significance of
p
and
q
in the lens space
L(p,q)
(see
[
Mar16
, Chap.10]
for example). Given two solid tori
\mathcal{D}_{k}=\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

(k=1,2)
, we can glue them along their boundaries by the attaching homeomorphism
\phi:\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\partial\mathcal{D}_{2}
. From the theory on the mapping class group of the torus, we know that the isotopy class of
\phi
is determined by the induced isomorphism
\phi_{*}
between the homology groups
H_{1}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{1};\mathbb{Z})
and
H_{1}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{2};\mathbb{Z})
. In detail, let
m_{k}=\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\{y\}
and
l_{k}=\{x\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
represent the generators of
H_{1}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{k};\mathbb{Z})
, and then
\phi_{*}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \phi_{*}(m_{1})&=q\cdot m_{2}+p\cdot l_{2},\\
\phi_{*}(l_{1})&=s\cdot m_{2}+t\cdot l_{2},\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{for some}\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q&p\\
s&t\end{array}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}).

(15)

Up to homeomorphism, the obtained manifold depends only on
p
and
q
, and is denoted as lens space
L(p,q)
. An important property of lens spaces is that two lens spaces
L(p,q)
and
L(p^{{}^{\prime}},q^{{}^{\prime}})
are homeomorphic if and only if
p^{{}^{\prime}}=\pm\,p
and
q^{{}^{\prime}}\equiv\pm\,q^{\pm 1}\hskip-4.0pt\mod p
.

Proposition 4.18
.

Assume
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
are transnormal systems of spherical type on
L(p,q)
and
L(p^{{}^{\prime}},q^{{}^{\prime}})
, respectively, whose DR-foils are all diffeomorphic to the torus. Then,
\mathcal{F}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
if and only if
L(p,q)
is homeomorphic to
L(p^{{}^{\prime}},q^{{}^{\prime}})
.

Proof..

It suffices to show the ‘if’ part. Assume, as detailed in Remark
4.17
, that the transnormal system
\mathcal{F}
on
L(p,q)
is constructed via an attaching map
\phi
between the boundaries of two solid tori, and
\phi
is isotopic to the linear homeomorphism of the torus represented by the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q&p\\
s&t\end{array}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}).

Similarly, the notations for
(L(p^{{}^{\prime}},q^{{}^{\prime}}),\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}})
differ only by a prime superscript. Since
L(p,q)
is homeomorphic to
L(p^{{}^{\prime}},q^{{}^{\prime}})
, we know
p^{{}^{\prime}}=\pm\,p
and
q^{{}^{\prime}}\equiv\pm\,q^{\pm 1}\hskip-4.0pt\mod p
. Without loss of generality, suppose
qt-ps=q^{{}^{\prime}}t^{{}^{\prime}}-p^{{}^{\prime}}s^{{}^{\prime}}=1
. Then, we obtain

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q^{{}^{\prime}}&p^{{}^{\prime}}\\
s^{{}^{\prime}}&t^{{}^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta_{1}(q+n_{1}p)&\delta_{2}p\\
\delta_{2}(s+n_{1}t+n_{2}q+n_{1}n_{2}p)&\delta_{1}(t+n_{2}p)\end{array}\right),

(16)

or

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q^{{}^{\prime}}&p^{{}^{\prime}}\\
s^{{}^{\prime}}&t^{{}^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta_{1}(t+n_{2}p)&\delta_{2}p\\
\delta_{2}(s+n_{1}t+n_{2}q+n_{1}n_{2}p)&\delta_{1}(q+n_{1}p)\end{array}\right),

(17)

where
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\{1,-1\}
,
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. On the other hand,

\varphi:\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\quad(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(z_{1}z_{2}^{n_{k}},z_{2}^{\pm\,1})\ \text{or}\ (\overline{z}_{1}z_{2}^{-n_{k}},z_{2}^{\pm\,1})

(18)

is an isomorphism of the disk bundle
\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, whose restriction to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
satisfies

\left(\left.\varphi\right|_{\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)_{*}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m_{k}\\
l_{k}\end{array}\right)=\pm\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\\
n_{k}&\pm\,1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m_{k}\\
l_{k}\end{array}\right).

It follows directly that
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}
is isotopic to
\varphi_{2}\circ\phi\circ\left.\varphi_{1}^{-1}\right|_{\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}
for some appropriately chosen
n_{k}
. Thus, by Proposition
4.16
, the proof is complete.
∎

As established in
[
Mos57
,
Neu68
]
, each lens space admits a cohomogeneity-one action by the Lie group
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Immediately, we have

Corollary 4.19
.

The transnormal system
\mathcal{F}
of spherical type on
L(p,q)
with DR-foils homeomorphic to the torus is, up to equivalence, induced by the action of the Lie group
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Case 3:
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are both circles, while
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are both solid Klein bottles.

M
is obtained by gluing two solid Klein bottles along their common boundary via an attaching map
\phi:\mathbf{K}\to\mathbf{K}
, where
\mathbf{K}
denotes the Klein bottle. The mapping class of the Klein bottle has been studied in
[

Lic65
]
, where it is shown that
\operatorname{Mod}\left(\mathbf{K}\right)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. A standard model for

is given by

\mathbf{K}=\frac{[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}{(0,e^{i\theta})\sim(1,e^{-i\theta})},

(19)

while the solid Klein bottle, denoted by
\mathbf{SK}
, is represented as

\mathbf{SK}=\frac{[0,1]\times\mathbb{D}^{2}}{(0,r\cdot e^{i\theta})\sim(1,r\cdot e^{-i\theta})}.

Here,

is a non-trivial Dehn twist of the Klein bottle, while

is the Y-homeomorphism (see
[

]
). Both

extend naturally to homeomorphisms of

, the transnormal system
\mathcal{F}
has a unique equivalence class in this case. Consequently,

is obtained by the canonical gluing of two solid Klein bottles, yielding a space homeomorphic to the twisted

4.4
Transnormal systems of real-projective type

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\frac{t_{0}}{2}]}
be a transnormal system of real-projective type on Riemannian manifold
M
. Recalling Proposition
3.3
(iv),
(\tilde{M},\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\{\tilde{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,t_{0}]})\to(M,\mathcal{F})
is a two-sheeted covering map, where
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is of spherical type and
M=\tilde{M}\big/\langle\sigma\rangle
with
\sigma(\tilde{F}_{t})=\tilde{F}_{t_{0}-t}
being an isometric foil-reflection. For simplicity, denote
t_{1}=\frac{t_{0}}{2}
and
\sigma_{t_{1}}
as the restriction of
\sigma
to
\tilde{F}_{t_{1}}
. Immediately, we have

M=\tilde{M}/\langle\sigma\rangle\simeq\frac{\mathcal{D}}{x\sim\sigma_{t_{1}}(x)\in\partial\mathcal{D}},

(20)

where
\mathcal{D}
is the submanifold of
\tilde{M}
composed of all foils
\tilde{F}_{t}
with
t\leq t_{1}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}
is the linear disk bundle over the unique S-foil in
\mathcal{F}
, and its boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
is diffeomorphic to any DR-foil of
\mathcal{F}
. Note that
\sigma_{t_{1}}
is a fixed-point-free involution on
\tilde{F}_{t_{1}}
, that is,
\sigma_{t_{1}}\in\operatorname{Inv}_{0}(\partial\mathcal{D})
. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
is another transnormal system of real-projective type on
M^{{}^{\prime}}
, with each notation differing by a prime superscript. A sufficient criterion for equivalence of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
is as follows.

Proposition 4.21
.

If there exist a bundle isomorphism
\varphi
between linear disk bundles
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}
such that
\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}_{t^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}}=\psi\circ\sigma_{t_{1}}\circ\psi^{-1}
with
\psi:\partial\mathcal{D}\to\partial\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}
smoothly isotopic to
\left.\varphi\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}
, then
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
are equivalent transnormal systems.

The proof follows from Proposition
3.3
(iv) and the argument in Proposition
4.16
.

When
\mathcal{F}
is a transnormal system of real-projective type on a compact 3-manifold
M
, the equivalence class of
\mathcal{F}
is determined by the disk bundle
\mathcal{D}\to L
and the conjugacy class of
\sigma_{t_{1}}
via an isotopy to the identity. As in Subsection
4.3
,
\mathcal{D}
has only three possibilities up to bundle isomorphism.

Case 1:
L
is a single point.

In this case,
\mathcal{D}
is a 3-disk, and
\sigma_{t_{1}}\in\operatorname{Inv}_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Any fixed-point-free involution on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is conjugate to the antipodal map
\sigma_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
via an isotopy to the identify. By Proposition
4.21
, there is a unique equivalence class of transnormal systems of real-projective type whose S-foil is a single point. Hence,
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}P^{3}
, and
\mathcal{F}
, up to equivalence, is induced by the cohomogeneity-one action of
\operatorname{SO}(3,\mathbb{R})
.

Case 2:
L
is a circle, while
\mathcal{D}
is a solid torus.

Then,
\sigma_{t_{1}}\in\operatorname{Inv}_{0}(\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
. By the classification of involutions on surfaces (see
[
Aso76
]
), any fixed-point-free involution on the torus is conjugate to either

\sigma_{+}:\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\quad(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(-z_{1},z_{2}),

or
\sigma_{-}
, depending on whether it preserves orientation or not, where
\sigma_{-}(z_{1},z_{2})=(-z_{1},\bar{z}_{2})
is defined in
(
14
)
(in different coordinates).
Hence, there exists a homeomorphism
h
on the torus such that

\sigma_{t_{1}}=h\circ\sigma_{+}\circ h^{-1}\quad\textrm{or}\quad\sigma_{t_{1}}=h\circ\sigma_{-}\circ h^{-1}.

Moreover, by Proposition
4.21
, only the isotopy class of
h
is relevant, so we may assume

h(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}^{h_{11}}z_{2}^{h_{12}},z_{1}^{h_{21}}z_{2}^{h_{22}})

with
(h_{ij})\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

If
\sigma_{t_{1}}=h\circ\sigma_{+}\circ h^{-1}
, then the SR-foil of
\mathcal{F}
is diffeomorphic to the torus. A straightforward calculation yields

\sigma_{t_{1}}(z_{1},z_{2})=((-1)^{h_{11}}z_{1},(-1)^{h_{21}}z_{2}).

Definition 4.22

Under complex coordinates, consider
\sigma_{-}(z_{1},z_{2})=(-z_{1},\bar{z}_{2})
and

Example 4.26
.

Let

be an orientable closed surface of genus

is a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism of

is not isotopic to any periodic homeomorphism, and the mapping torus
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}
admits a hyperbolic structure, that is, it has a standard metric locally isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{3}
(see, for example,
[
Thu86
]
). Immediately, the transnormal system
\mathcal{F}
of toric type induced by
\tau
is not equivalent to any CPC transnormal system by Proposition
4.9
. Assume
g
is the Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}
in Theorem
1.5
, such that
\mathcal{F}
is induced from an isoparametric function. It is then claimed that
g
can never become the standard metric locally isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, as explained below:
If this were the case, the lifting transnormal system on the universal Riemannian cover
\mathbb{H}^{3}
would also be induced by an isoparametric function. This would imply that it is, in fact, a CPC transnormal system, according to the classification of isoparametric functions on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Consequently,
\mathcal{F}
itself would also be a CPC transnormal system, leading to a contradiction.

In summary, this is a significant example in the study of cohomogeneity-one actions, CPC transnormal systems, isoparametric foliations, and transnormal systems. This manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}
does not admit both a cohomogeneity-one action and a CPC transnormal system for any Riemannian metric. However, it may admit an isoparametric foliation or a hyperbolic metric, but not both simultaneously.

Remark 4.27
.

In Section
3
, we established that a transnormal system on a Riemannian manifold can be lifted to a transnormal system on its universal Riemannian cover. However, this does not imply that our discussion should primarily focus on transnormal systems in simply connected manifolds. On the contrary, in some cases, lifting to the universal cover introduces additional complexity. This is because our classification is carried out under the equivalence, which is not necessarily preserved under coverings. Specifically, suppose
\mathcal{F}
is lifted to
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. If
\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{{}^{\prime}}
is equivalent to
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
, then in general, there does not exist another transnormal system equivalent to
\mathcal{F}
that can be lifted to
\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{{}^{\prime}}
. Consequently, reducing connectivity via lifting prevents classification under the equivalence in Definition
1.3
, whereas the latter often provides a more effective simplification than the former.

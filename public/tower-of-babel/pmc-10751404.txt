Comment on “Sarcopenia and cardiovascular diseases: A systematic review and meta‐analysis” by Zuo et al.
Authors: Xiaoming Zhang, Maofeng Gao, ShouDi Hu
Published: 2023

Dear editor:

Sarcopenia is a very common geriatric syndrome among older people, with the aging, the prevalence and incidence of sarcopenia was increasing, especially for older people with chronic disease. As we all know, when older people suffering from sarcopenia, they had a higher risk of clinical adverse outcomes. Therefore, early identifying sarcopenia was very important.

Cardiovascular disease (CVDs) has become the serious public issue, especially when the number of older people increased. In addition, CVDs had become the major reasons for mortality and disability around the world.

Recently, Zuo and his colleges had published a systematic review and meta‐analysis about the prevalence of sarcopenia among people with CVDs and the results found that the pooled prevalence of sarcopenia was 35% (95% CI: 28–42%), which was higher than that among general people 1 (13%; 95% CI: 9–17%). The authors used multiple methods such as broad subgroup analysis and sensitivity analysis to figure out what is the reasons for high heterogeneity and to confirm whether the results was robust. The authors did an excellent study and provide the overall picture of sarcopenia among CVDs, whereas some questions are worth to discuss.

Firstly, we think the authors had made a mistake. In the Sasaki's study, the number of sarcopenia in female and male was 14 and 13, respectively. Therefore, the prevalence of sarcopenia in this study was 27/160 = 16.8%, which was consistent with Sasaki's original study. Therefore, we had re‐analysed the data and found the pooled prevalence of sarcopenia was 34.9% (95% CI: 28.1–41.8%), which was similar with the original systematic review and meta‐analysis.

Secondly, we think the authors need to present the study design of each original studies in Table 1. Generally, there are totally different definition between prevalence and incidence. The prevalence of some disease is generated from a cross‐sectional study, whereas the incidence of some disease is generated from a prospective cohort study. Therefore, we think the authors need to provide this important information (study design) of each original study, given the item of inclusion and exclusion criteria consisted of cross‐sectional or prospective cohort studies. And the authors used the ‘prevalence’ of sarcopenia among CVDs in the abstract section, and they describe the purpose of the systematic review was to summarize the ‘incidence’ of sarcopenia among CVDs and general population in the first section of discussion. We think these above‐mentioned descriptions were not consistent, which was not rigorous.

Thirdly, the results of subgroup analysis based on study region found that the prevalence of sarcopenia among CVDs in Asia was 36% (95% CI: 27–46%), followed 31% (95% CI: 16–45%) in Europe, 35% (95% CI: 14–57%) in South American and 39% (95% CI: 28–42%) in Oceania. Whereas we think the authors miss some important studies that explored the sarcopenia and CVDs in Chinese Journal, which can bring some errors to estimate the prevalence of sarcopenia among CVDs or general population in Asia region. Therefore, the authors might need to declare this limitation in the discussion.

Fourthly, when the authors performed the subgroup analysis based on different variables such as subtype of CVDs, study region, population background, gender and evaluation method of sarcopenia and describe the difference for each stratum. However, the authors compared the different prevalence of sarcopenia in each subgroup without any P‐value for difference of statistical analyses. Therefore, we think, these analyses were incomplete. We had provided the statistical analyses of the difference for each subgroup analysis in Table 1.

Fifthly, the authors provide the subgroup analysis based on the diagnostic method and the authors found that the different diagnostic method of sarcopenia did not necessarily cause discrepancy in the prevalence of sarcopenia. The previous study reported that the prevalence of sarcopenia can cause different when used various sarcopenia criteria. We had conducted subgroup analysis based on sarcopenia criteria and the results found that the highest prevalence of sarcopenia among CVD was 45.8% (95% CI: 29.6–62.0%) when used EWGSOP (2010) to confirm sarcopenia. Whereas for general population, the highest prevalence of sarcopenia was 19.9% (95% CI: 1.9–38.0%) when used EWGSOP (2018) to confirm sarcopenia, as showed in Table 1.

Sixthly, we believe meta‐regression of the prevalence by average age are encouraged to conduct. The results found that average age had no impact on the sarcopenia prevalence among CVDs and general population, with the results of regression coefficient being 0.00078 [(95% CI: −0.0048 to 0.006), P = 0.775] for CVD and regression coefficient being 0.0045 [(95% CI: −0.003 to 0.0046), P = 0.817] for general population, which was consistent with previous of meta‐analysis for pooling the prevalence sarcopenia among patients with mechanical ventilation 2 or kidney transplantation recipients. 3

To conclude, the authors did a great systematic review and meta‐analysis which focusing the important CVDs population. Their study brings great attention of sarcopenia for CVDs, given the high prevalence of sarcopenia among this special population. More intervention should be implemented to reduce sarcopenia among CVDs population, in order to improve the clinical outcome among this special population.

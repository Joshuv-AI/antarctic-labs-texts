Existence and order of the self–binding transition in non–local non–linear Schrödinger equations
Norihisa Ikoma, Krzysztof Myśliwy

Abstract
We consider a class of non–linear and non–local functionals giving rise to the Choquard equation with a suitably regular interaction potential, modelling, i.e., gases with impurities and axion stars. We study how existence of minimizers depends on the coupling constant, and find that there is a critical interaction strength needed for the minimizers to exist, both in dimensions two and three. In d = 3 d=3 , a minimizer exists also at the critical coupling but none do in d = 2 d=2 under suitable assumptions on the potential. We also establish that in d = 3 d=3 there exist other critical points beyond the global minimizer.

1.1.1
Bound states in classical and quantum dynamics

Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an
L^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
function with non–vanishing positive part
V^{+}:=\max\{V,0\}\not\equiv 0
,

Classically, if the initial position of the particle lies within the support of

and its total energy

is negative,
then the trajectory of the particle is bounded in the support of
V^{+}

and does not leave the set
\set{x}{V(x)\geq-E}
: we say that the particle is
trapped
.

The quantum mechanical analogy of this trapping is the question of finding of what is called the
bound states
of
-V
. This corresponds to finding normalized
L^{2}
minimizers of the functional

\mathcal{E}_{s}(u)\coloneq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\vab{\nabla u}^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\vab{u}^{2}Vdx

(1.1)

whose stationary points lead to the standard linear Schrödinger equation

-\Delta\phi-V\phi=e\phi

with

being the

. A basic result is that if one knows a priori that

,
then the minimizer necessarily exists (
[
17

and
[
5

thus, as in the classical case, a particle with negative total energy is trapped and cannot escape to infinity, since its stationary state is a genuine

In contrast to the classical dynamics in which one can always ensure the trapping for an arbitrary

sufficiently small in

comes in with an

[
10

Interestingly, the situation suggested by classical intuition is restored if the motion of the particle is constrained to lower dimensions; in

)

For a succinct summary, let us fix

Then, under suitable summability conditions on

1.1.2
Nonlinear binding: Brief description of main results

The purpose of the present paper is to investigate how the above situation changes in case where the
linear, local
model given by (

\mathcal{E}_{g}(u)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\vab{\nabla u(x)}^{2}dx-\frac{g}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\vab{u(x)}^{2}V(x-y)\vab{u(y)}^{2}dxdy.

Notice that (

Now, under suitable assumptions on

Below, we shall explain in more detail why we chose to refer to the above phenomenon as a first or second order transition.

It is enough to think of the first order transition in the sense given above as simply to the existence of a minimizer on which the functional evaluates to zero, as it does for the zero function. The intuitive picture of a continuous

Before stating the main result in a more precise manner, let us provide additional motivation for the study of this particular model, and why the information on how the trapping transition is realized is of importance.

\{u_{n}\}
that

\mathcal{E}_{g}(\omega)\geq e(g,m_{1}),\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(\tilde{v}_{n})\geq e(g,m_{2}).

By
Lemma

2.4
and (
3.3
), this actually gives

e(g,m)=e(g,m_{1})+e(g,m_{2})

(3.4)

and

\mathcal{E}_{g}(\omega)=e(g,m_{1}),\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(\tilde{v}_{n})=e(g,m_{2}).

Note that
e(g,m)<0
implies
e(g,m_{1})<0
and
e(g,m_{2})<0
;
in fact, if, for instance,
e(g,m_{2})=0
then we must have
e(g,m)=e(g,m_{1})<0
,
which contradicts
Lemma

2.5
, and similarly for the case
e(g,m_{1})=0
.
From
e(g,m_{1})<0
and
e(g,m_{2})<0
, up to a subsequence,

\mathcal{V}(\omega)>0\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{V}(\tilde{v}_{n})=C_{v}>0.

(3.5)

We clearly have
e(g,m)\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}(\sqrt{m/m_{1}}\,\omega)
,
e(g,m)\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}(\sqrt{m/m_{2}}\,\tilde{v}_{n})
and thus

\displaystyle e(g,m)

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{1}}{m}e(g,m)+\frac{m_{2}}{m}e(g,m)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}(\omega)+\mathcal{E}_{g}(\tilde{v}_{n})-g\ab(\frac{m}{m_{1}}-1)\mathcal{V}(\omega)-g\ab(\frac{m}{m_{2}}-1)\mathcal{V}(\tilde{v}_{n}).

Due to (
3.4
) and (
3.5
),
taking the limit

yields

e(g,m)\leq e(g,m_{1})+e(g,m_{2})-\ab(\frac{m}{m_{1}}-1)\ \mathcal{V}(\omega)-\ab(\frac{m}{m_{2}}-1)C_{v}<e(g,m),

which is a contradiction, rooted in the assumption (
3.2
). Hence, the weak limit of
u_{n}
,
\omega
, satisfies

\|\omega\|_{2}^{2}=m=\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

(3.6)

In particular, by (
3.6
) and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\omega
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

u_{n}\to\omega
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
By the interpolation,
u_{n}\to\omega
strongly in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for each
q\in[2,2^{\ast})

and Young’s inequality with

implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{V}(u_{n})=\mathcal{V}(\omega).

(3.7)

Finally, from
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\omega
weakly in

and (

), we infer that

e(g,m)\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}(\omega)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(u_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(u_{n})=e(g,m),

which gives

\mathcal{E}_{g}(\omega)=e(g,m),\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{K}(u_{n})=\mathcal{K}(\omega).

Hence,
u_{n}\to\omega
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\omega
is a minimizer.
∎

Proof.

We first notice that in each case, there exists a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

is a weak solution of

(4.2)

When

\lambda>0
holds. In fact, from (

) and

\lambda m=-\Vab{\nabla u}_{2}^{2}+g\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V\ast u^{2})u^{2}dx=-4\mathcal{E}_{g}(u)+\Vab{\nabla u}_{2}^{2}=-e(g,m)+\Vab{\nabla u}_{2}^{2}>0.

We next show

and

as

For this purpose, write

By

for all

if

and for all

Young’s inequality implies

for any

Since

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

from Moser’s iteration (
[

, Chapter 8]

, Chapter 4]

and

.
In particular,

for every

From the

\widetilde{e}(g,m)\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}(\omega_{0})<\frac{\rho^{2}_{0}}{33}\quad\text{for every $g\in[g_{3},g^{*}_{d}(m))$}.

In particular, the first inequality implies

\lim_{g\to g^{*}_{d}(m)}\widetilde{e}(g,m)=0

which again is reminiscent of the typical behavior of the Gibbs functional upon approaching the transition point, where the previously metastable states become global equilibrium states
[
4
]
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}_{m}
be any minimizing sequence for
\widetilde{e}(g,m)
.
By
Proposition

2.1
,
(u_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
If
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Vab{u_{n}}_{L^{2}(z+Q)}\to 0
,
then
\Vab{u_{n}}_{q}\to 0
for all
2<q<2^{*}
and
\mathcal{V}(u_{n})\to 0
.
Therefore,

\frac{\rho^{2}_{0}}{33}>\mathcal{E}_{g}(\omega_{0})\geq\widetilde{e}(g,m)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(u_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\Vab{\nabla u_{n}}_{2}^{2}.

On the other hand, by
u_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{m}
,
\Vab{\nabla u_{n}}_{2}^{2}>\rho_{0}^{2}/4
, which is a contradiction.
As a consequence,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Vab{u_{n}}_{L^{2}(z+Q)}=c_{0}>0
.

Choose
(z_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
so that
\Vab{u_{n}}_{L^{2}(z_{n}+Q)}\to c_{0}>0
and
set
v_{n}\coloneq u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n})
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{n})\to\widetilde{e}(g,m)
by translation invariance, and, by the Rellich–Kondrashov theorem,

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{0}\not\equiv 0
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Our goal is to show that
v_{0}
is a local minimizer, i.e. a minimizer of (
6.11
).

The first step is to establish that
\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}>\rho_{0}^{2}/4
.
Since

\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{33}>\widetilde{e}(g,m)=\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{n})+o(1),

Lemma

6.5
implies
\Vab{\nabla v_{n}}_{2}^{2}\geq\rho_{0}^{2}
.
Indeed, note that
\Vab{\nabla v_{n}}_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{4}
a priori, uniformly in
g
. If
\Vab{\nabla v_{n}}_{2}^{2}<\rho_{0}^{2}
, then
we have by
Lemma

6.5

\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{33}>\widetilde{e}(g,m)=\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{n})+o(1)\geq\frac{1}{8}\Vab{\nabla v_{n}}_{2}^{2}+o(1)\geq\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{32}+o(1),

a contradiction.
Suppose that
\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}\leq\rho_{0}^{2}/4
holds;
then by setting
w_{n}\coloneq v_{n}-v_{0}
,

\rho_{0}^{2}\leq\Vab{\nabla v_{n}}_{2}^{2}=\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}+\Vab{\nabla w_{n}}_{2}^{2}+o(1)\leq\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{4}+\Vab{\nabla w_{n}}_{2}^{2}+o(1).

This leads to
\Vab{\nabla w_{n}}_{2}^{2}>\rho_{0}^{2}/2
and
w_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Vab{w_{n}}_{2}^{2}}
for all sufficiently large
n
.
From
v_{0}w_{n}\to 0
strongly in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for each
q\in[1,2^{*}/2)
, it follows that

\mathcal{V}(v_{n})=\mathcal{V}(v_{0}+w_{n})=\mathcal{V}(v_{0})+\mathcal{V}(w_{n})+o(1),

and hence

\widetilde{e}(g,m)=\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{n})+o(1)=\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})+\mathcal{E}_{g}(w_{n})+o(1).

Recalling
\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}\leq m
yields
\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})\geq e(g,\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2})\geq e(g,m)=0
.
Consider now two cases: if
\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}=m
, then
\Vab{w_{n}}_{2}^{2}\to 0
and
\mathcal{V}(w_{n})\to 0
hold.
Thus, the fact
\Vab{\nabla w_{n}}_{2}^{2}>\rho_{0}^{2}/2
gives

\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{33}>\widetilde{e}(g,m)\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(w_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\Vab{\nabla w_{n}}_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{4},

which is absurd.

On the other hand, when
0<\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}<m
, then as
\Vab{w_{n}}_{2}^{2}\to m-\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}
,

Lemma

6.6
and
w_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Vab{w_{n}}_{2}^{2}}
lead to

\widetilde{e}(g,m)\geq\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(w_{n})\geq\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})+\widetilde{e}(g,m-\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2})\geq\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})+\widetilde{e}(g,m).

By
g_{3}\leq g<g^{*}_{d}(m)
and
0\geq e(g,\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2})\geq e(g,m)=0
,
we see that
g^{*}_{d}(\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2})\geq g^{*}_{d}(m)
,
\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})=0
and

v_{0}
is a global minimizer. However, this contradicts the nonexistence result in
Theorem

1.1
.
Thus, the inequality
\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}>\rho_{0}^{2}/4
follows.

Noting that the splitting

\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{n})=\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})+\mathcal{E}_{g}(w_{n})+o(1)

holds, by
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(w_{n})\geq e(g,m-\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2})\geq e(g,m)=0
,
we see from
the fact
v_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}}
and
Lemma

6.6
that

\widetilde{e}(g,m)\geq\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(w_{n})\geq\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})\geq\widetilde{e}(g,\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2})\geq\widetilde{e}(g,m).

Hence,

\widetilde{e}(g,m)=\widetilde{e}(g,\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2})=\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0}).

(6.12)

Thus, to conclude, it remains to show that
\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}=m
. (
6.12
) shows that
v_{0}
minimizes
\mathcal{E}_{g}
on the set
\mathcal{A}_{\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}}
, and it is thus a critical point. This implies that there exists
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

-\Delta v_{0}+\lambda_{0}v_{0}=\frac{g}{2}\Bab{V*v_{0}^{2}+\check{V}*v_{0}^{2}}v_{0}\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\check{V}(x)\coloneq V(-x).

When
\lambda_{0}\neq 0
, it follows that

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{g}(tv_{0})\right\rvert_{t=1}=\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}-g\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V*v_{0}^{2})v_{0}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=-\lambda_{0}\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}\neq 0.

(6.13)

Suppose that
\|v_{0}\|_{2}^{2}<m
. If
\lambda_{0}>0
, then by
\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}>\rho_{0}^{2}/4
, we notice that

\Vab{(1+\varepsilon)v_{0}}_{2}^{2}<m
and
(1+\varepsilon)v_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.

Lemma

6.6
with (
6.12
) leads to

\widetilde{e}(g,m)\leq\widetilde{e}(g,(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2})\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}((1+\varepsilon)v_{0})<\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})=\widetilde{e}(g,m),

which is a contradiction.
In a similar way, if
\lambda_{0}<0
, then

(1-\varepsilon)v_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}}
and (
6.13
) give

\widetilde{e}(g,m)\leq\widetilde{e}(g,\Vab{(1-\varepsilon)v_{0}}_{2}^{2})\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}((1-\varepsilon)v_{0})<\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})=\widetilde{e}(g,m),

which is absurd.
Therefore,
\lambda_{0}=0
holds. However, in this case, note that

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{g}(tv_{0})\right\rvert_{t=1}=0,\quad\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{g}(tv_{0})\right\rvert_{t=1}=\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}^{2}_{2}-3g\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V*v_{0}^{2})v_{0}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=-2\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}<0

Hence, we can argue as in the case
\lambda_{0}>0
and obtain a contradiction.
Thus,
\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}=m
holds and we see from (
6.12
) that
v_{0}
is the desired minimizer.
Since
\Vab{v_{n}}_{2}^{2}\to\Vab{v_{0}}_{2}^{2}=m
, we can show that
\Vab{v_{n}-v_{0}}_{2}^{2}\to 0
.
Moreover, from
\mathcal{V}(v_{n})\to\mathcal{V}(v_{0})
and

\widetilde{e}(g,m)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{n})\geq\mathcal{E}_{g}(v_{0})\geq\widetilde{e}(g,m),

it follows that
\Vab{\nabla v_{n}}_{2}^{2}\to\Vab{\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}
.
Therefore,
\Vab{\nabla v_{n}-\nabla v_{0}}_{2}^{2}\to 0
and

v_{n}\to v_{0}
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This completes the proof .
∎

Appendix A
Computations on the case
d=1

Here we give an example of
V
with
(A1)
and
(A2)
, and
g_{d}^{*}(m)>0
holds for every
m>0
when
d=1
:

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose
d=1
. There exists
V
with
(A1)
and
(A2)
such that

g^{*}_{1}(m)>0
holds for any
m>0
.

Proof.

Let
m>0
be given, fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)
and consider

V_{\varepsilon}(x):=\begin{cases}1&(\vab{x}\leq\varepsilon),\\
-2&(\varepsilon<\vab{x}<1),\\
0&(1\leq\vab{x}).\end{cases}

Notice that
V_{\varepsilon}
satisfies
(A1)
and
(A2)
. To show
g^{*}_{1}(m)>0
,
it is enough to prove that for any sufficiently small
g>0
,

\mathcal{E}_{g}(u)>0\quad\text{for all $u\in S_{m}$}.

By the definition of
V_{\varepsilon}
, for each
u\in S_{m}
,

-\mathcal{V}(u)=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x+\varepsilon}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{\varepsilon<\vab{y-x}<1}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dy.

For the second term, from
0<\varepsilon<1/4
it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{\varepsilon<\vab{y-x}<1}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{x+\varepsilon}^{x+3\varepsilon}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x+\varepsilon}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(s+2\varepsilon)ds.

Since

u(s+2\varepsilon)=u(s)+2\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}u^{\prime}(s+2\theta\varepsilon)d\theta,

we obtain

\displaystyle\ab(u(s+2\varepsilon))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq u^{2}(s)+4\varepsilon u(s)\int_{0}^{1}u^{\prime}(s+2\theta\varepsilon)d\theta

\displaystyle\geq u^{2}(s)-2\varepsilon\Bab{u^{2}(s)+\ab(\int_{0}^{1}u^{\prime}(s+2\theta\varepsilon)d\theta)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq(1-2\varepsilon)u^{2}(s)-2\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\ab(u^{\prime}(s+2\theta\varepsilon))^{2}d\theta.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{\varepsilon<\vab{y-x}<1}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dxu^{2}(x)\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x+\varepsilon}\ab[(1-2\varepsilon)u^{2}(s)-2\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\ab(u^{\prime}(s+2\theta\varepsilon))^{2}d\theta]ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-2\varepsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}}dxu^{2}(x)\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x+\varepsilon}u^{2}(s)ds-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}dxu^{2}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}ds\int_{0}^{1}\ab(u^{\prime}(s+2\theta\varepsilon))^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-2\varepsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x+\varepsilon}u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)dy-\varepsilon\Vab{u}_{2}^{2}\Vab{u^{\prime}}_{2}^{2},

where Fubini’s theorem is used in the last step.
Recalling
\Vab{u}_{2}^{2}=m
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{g}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Vab{u^{\prime}}_{2}^{2}-g\mathcal{V}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\Vab{u^{\prime}}_{2}^{2}+\frac{1-4\varepsilon}{4}g\int_{\mathbb{R}}dxu^{2}(x)\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x+\varepsilon}u^{2}(y)dy-\varepsilon gm\Vab{u^{\prime}}_{2}^{2}.

Since
0<\varepsilon<1/4
, if
g>0
satisfies
\varepsilon gm<1/2
, then
\mathcal{E}_{g}(u)>0
holds for each
u\in S_{m}

and
g^{*}_{1}(m)>0
follows for every
m>0
.
∎

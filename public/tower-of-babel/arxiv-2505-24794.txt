Introduction

For a graph
G=(V,E)
on
n
vertices, a subset
W\subseteq V
is called
independent
(or
stable
) if it does not induce any edges. Denote by
I(G)
the set of independent subsets of
G
, and let
i(G)=|I(G)|
denote the number of independent subsets in
G
, which is also called the
Fibonacci number
of the graph
G
after Prodinger and Tichy
[
20
]
, since the sequence
(i(P_{n}))
returns the Fibonacci sequence for paths
P_{n}
.

The investigation of the number of independent sets in graph families has a rich history with deep mathematical results and significant applications in various combinatorial and computational domains. Understanding these numbers and the properties of extremal graphs provides valuable insights into the structure and behaviour of graphs.
As for extremal results, we recall that Moon and Moser proved a cornerstone theorem concerning the maximum number of maximal independent sets in
n
-vertex graphs
[
17
]
. They also proved sharp results on the number of different sizes of maximal independent sets that an
n
-vertex graph can admit. Some related results are
[
7
,
18
,
24
,
25
]
. The celebrated result of Kahn
[
11
]
showed that disjoint unions of complete bipartite graphs
K_{r,r}
have the maximum number of independent sets among
r
-regular bipartite graphs on
n
vertices, which was extended by Zhao to all

r
-regular graphs
[
26
]
. Later, Sah, Sawhney, Stoner and Zhao described the maximum number of independent sets in non-regular graphs as well
[
21
]
.
For a relatively recent survey on counting independent sets, we refer to the work of Samotij
[
22
]
, see also
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
.
Counting independent sets is a natural combinatorial problem with connections to other counting problems in graphs and hypergraphs. A very important and efficient related tool is the famous
container method
, developed by Saxton and Thomason
[
23
]
and independently by Balogh, Morris and Samotij
[
3
]
. It is
particularly useful when one aims to obtain upper bounds on the number of independent sets in sparse graphs or graphs with certain local properties. The core idea is to show that all such independent sets are contained in a relatively small number of larger so-called containers that have a more manageable structure, or size.
While several applications require the understanding of
the typical

or the extremal number
of independent sets in certain graph families, together with their typical or extremal structural properties, our aim is to describe the
set of all possible numbers
that can be attained as the Fibonacci number of an
n
-vertex graph.

We denote the family of (unlabelled)
n
-vertex graphs by
\mathcal{G}_{n}
.
Since the empty set and the one-element subsets are always independent,
n+1\leq i(G)\leq 2^{n}
clearly holds for any
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
. As mentioned earlier,
\max\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}\}
and
\min\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}\}
have been in the centre of study for various families
\mathcal{G}
of graphs.
Our main goal is to give upper and lower bounds on the size of the whole Fibonacci spectrum

\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\}
, for which we introduce

Definition 1.1
.

\mathcal{N}i(n):=|\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\}|.

To study the enumerative properties of independent sets, the
independence polynomial
of a graph
G
, denoted by
I_{G}(x)
, is defined
as the polynomial whose coefficient for
x^{k}
is given by the number of independent sets of order
k
in
G
, denoted
i_{k}(G)
, that is,

I_{G}(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}i_{k}(G)x^{k}.

This polynomial was initially defined by Gutman and Harary
[
9
]
and is also known as the independent set polynomial or stable set polynomial. For a survey on the independence polynomial, we refer to the work of Levit and Mandrescu
[
15
]
.
Using this terminology, our main problem is equivalent to the following.

Problem 1.2
.

Determine the order of magnitude of
|\{I_{G}(1):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\}|.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

\mathcal{N}i(n)=O(2^{n}n^{-0.2075}),

while for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

\mathcal{N}i(n)\geq 2^{n-2^{(1+o(1))\sqrt{\log_{2}n}}}.

Note that for every
\varepsilon>0
,
{2^{\sqrt{\log_{2}n}}}{n^{-\varepsilon}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Let us outline the organization of the paper. In Section 2, we set some notation, and present some useful tools which will be applied later. In Section 3, we prove the upper bound of Theorem
1.3
. Then in Section 4 we continue by presenting general results on the so-called partial join operation and the derivation of
i(G)
for a graph
G
obtained via partial joining a pair of graphs
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
, using information on
i(G^{\prime})
for induced subgraphs
G^{\prime}\subseteq G_{L}
.
Section 5 is devoted to the presentation of the core of the main construction: the introduction of parts
G_{L}
,
G_{R}
and a certain subfamily of their partial joins. This will show how to obtain graphs
G
with
i(G)
admitting values
M
, for which the binary digits of
M
(up to an additive constant) can be arbitrarily prescribed in a certain long interval. Finally, in Section 6, we put together a construction where several copies of the previously given graph
G_{R}
are combined, so that the aforementioned long interval can actually cover almost all of
[0,n]
, which in turn will imply the lower bound of Theorem
1.3
. In Section 7, we give an application of Theorem
1.3
to a general additive combinatorial problem about subsets of a given vector space that avoid certain intersection patterns with respect to subspaces
[
13
,
14
]
. We mention that the general problem can be viewed as an extension of the cap-set problem
[
6
]
, where one wants to avoid a full (or empty) line in
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
. Our application concerns binary vector spaces when one wants to avoid subspaces with intersection size
1
.

We conclude by raising some open problems and point out an exponential bound on the number of independence polynomials as a byproduct.

In this paper, a

A

The complement of a graph

The set of natural numbers is
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
, and the set of positive integers is

. For a positive integer

, we use the notation

. For integers

, we let

.

The disjoint union of sets

and

.

If

\mathbbm{1}_{B}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $B$ is true,}\\

In this paper, logarithms are taken base

. The

2.1
Graph operations

Suppose we are given two graphs
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
on disjoint vertex sets. We define the following two standard operations on them.

•

The
disjoint union

G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}
is the graph
G=(V,E)
with
V=V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
and

.

•

The

, where

A

of

2.2
Binary expansions of given support

For

, let

In other words, a natural number

.
Note that binary digits are numbered from the right, starting with

.
For example,

.
We will use the

Proposition 2.2
.

-

•

|\mathcal{B}(J)|=2^{|J|}
.

•

\mathcal{B}(J)=\sum_{j\in J}\{0,2^{j}\}
.

•

If

2.3
Hypercube grids

For

, let

be an interval of integers for each

, where

. Then the

. Note that

is a subset of the integer lattice

. When

, we call

a

, or just a

when the context is clear. The

of the hypercube grid is

. For given integers

,

with

, we define

which is an axis-parallel hyperplane of

. We will often take intersections of hypercubes with such a hyperplane: e.g. when

is a hypercube and

.

Suppose the graph

is isomorphic to the complete graph

. In this setup, we will often refer to the following notions:

.

A

.

Definition 2.4
.

A

. If
\tau\subseteq[\ell]

Observe that a full transversal is a partial transversal of support

.

3
Upper bound

The main idea is the following. In order to give an upper bound on
\mathcal{N}i(n)=|\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\}|
, we separate a family of non-isomorphic graphs which has cardinality less than a well-chosen function
T(n)
. Then we show that for each graph in the remaining family of graphs, the number of independent sets is also less than
T(n)
.

Recall that
\mathcal{G}_{n}
denotes the family of simple graphs on
n
vertices. Given a graph
G
, let
\tau(G)
and
\nu(G)
denote the vertex cover number and the matching number of
G
, respectively. It is well-known that
\nu(G)\leq\tau(G)\leq 2\nu(G)
.

Observation 3.1
.

For any function
r=r(n)
, we have

|\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\}|\leq|\{G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}:\tau(G)\leq r\}|+|\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\tau(G)>r\}|,

where in the first right-hand term, the number of graphs is counted up to isomorphism.

We use the following lemma of Atmaca and Oruc
[
2
]
, based on Pólya’s enumeration
[
19
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
B_{u}(n-r,r)
denote the set of unlabelled bipartite graphs on given bipartite classes of size
n-r
and
r
. Then

\frac{{n-r+2^{r}-1\choose n-r}}{r!}\leq|B_{u}(n-r,r)|\leq 2\frac{{n-r+2^{r}-1\choose n-r}}{r!},

if
n-r>r.

Corollary 3.3
.

If
r=\left\lceil\log\mu n\right\rceil
for some fixed real
0<\mu<1
, then it holds for
n
sufficiently large that

\begin{split}|B_{u}(n-r,r)|&\leq\frac{2}{\left\lceil\log\mu n\right\rceil!}\binom{n+2^{\left\lceil\log\mu n\right\rceil}-1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu-\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{\log\mu n}\\
&\leq\frac{2(1+o(1))}{\sqrt{2\pi\log\mu n}}\left(\frac{e}{(1+\mu-\frac{1}{n})\log{\mu n}}\right)^{\log{\mu n}}\cdot 2^{n(1+2\mu)H\left(\frac{1}{1+2\mu}\right)}\\
&\leq(\mu n)^{c}\cdot 2^{n(1+2\mu)H\left(\frac{1}{1+2\mu}\right)}\text{\ \ for some absolute constant $c>0$,}\end{split}

by using Stirling’s formula and Lemma
2.1
, after applying

{n-r+2^{r}-1\choose n-r}\leq{n+2^{r}-1\choose n}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu-\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{\log\mu n}.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
0<\mu<1
be a real number. For
n
sufficiently large and
r=\left\lceil\log\mu n\right\rceil
, we have

|\{G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}:\tau(G)\leq r\}|\leq 2^{\binom{r}{2}}|B_{u}(n-r,r)|\leq(\mu n)^{0.5\log{\mu n}+c}\cdot 2^{n(1+2\mu)H\left(\frac{1}{1+2\mu}\right)}

for some absolute constant
c>0
.

Proof.

The number of labelled simple graphs on
r
vertices is
2^{\binom{r}{2}}
, thus
2^{\binom{r}{2}}|B_{u}(n-r,r)|
clearly provides an upper bound for the number of unlabelled graphs whose edges are all incident to a class of size at most
r
. Combining the upper bound
2^{\binom{r}{2}}\leq(\mu n)^{0.5\log{\mu n}+0.5}
with Corollary
3.3
, we get the desired bound.
∎

To bound
|\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\tau(G)>r\}|
, observe that any
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
with vertex cover number
\tau(G)>r
has at least
\ell:=\left\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil
independent edges. The two endpoints of any of these edges cannot both appear in an independent set of
G
, therefore
i(G)\leq 3^{\ell}\cdot 2^{n-2\ell}
for any such
G
. Hence we in turn have

Lemma 3.5
.

|\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\tau(G)>r\}|\leq 3^{\ell}\cdot 2^{n-2\ell}\leq 2^{n}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{r/2}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
, upper bound.

Let
\mu_{0}
be the solution of the equality
(1+2\mu_{0})H\left(\frac{1}{1+2\mu_{0}}\right)=1.
This gives
\mu_{0}\approx 0.146908.
Let us fix
\mu=0.1469<\mu_{0}
.
Then, by taking
r(n)=\left\lceil\log\mu n\right\rceil
in Proposition
3.1
, for
n
large enough we get

|\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\}|\leq 2^{0.99997n}+2^{n}\cdot 2^{0.5\log(0.75)\log(0.1469n)}=O(2^{n}n^{-0.2075})

in view of Corollary
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
.
∎

4
Partial joins and the summation trick

Our graph constructions will be reliant on the concept of partial joins.

Definition 4.1

(Partial join of two graphs)
.

Let
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
be two graphs on disjoint vertex sets
V_{L}
and
V_{R}
, respectively. A
partial join
of
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
is any graph
G=(V,E)
satisfying the following properties:

•

V=V_{L}\sqcup V_{R}
,

•

G[V_{L}]=G_{L}
and
G[V_{R}]=G_{R}
.

In this setup,
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
will often be called the
left-hand
and
right-hand classes
.

In other words, a partial join is a graph
G
on the combined vertex set
V=V_{L}\sqcup V_{R}
such that all edges and non-edges are preserved within the classes
V_{L}
and
V_{R}
, and for any pair
v_{L}\in V_{L}
and
v_{R}\in V_{R}
, we can freely choose whether to include the edge
v_{L}v_{R}
in
G
or not. If we include all such edges, the resulting graph
G
is the full join
G_{L}\triangledown G_{R}
of the two graphs. On the other hand, if we do not include any of these edges, we get the disjoint union
G_{L}\sqcup G_{R}
of the two graphs.

In the main construction of this paper,
G_{L}
will be a large empty graph, while
G_{R}
will be a disjoint union of several cliques. Different values
i(G)
can then be obtained by appropriately setting the edges between
V_{L}
and
V_{R}
, providing the variable part of the construction.

The following trick allows us to count independent subsets in a partial join:

Proposition 4.2

(Summation trick)
.

Let
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
be arbitrary graphs with disjoint vertex sets
V_{L}
and
V_{R}
, and let
G
be a partial join of
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
. For every
v_{R}\in V_{R}
, denote by
N_{L}(v_{R})
the set of vertices in
V_{L}
adjacent to
v_{R}
. Then

i(G)=\sum_{S_{R}\in I(G_{R})}i\left(G_{L}\left[V_{L}\setminus\bigcup_{v_{R}\in S_{R}}N_{L}(v_{R})\right]\right).

Proof.

We count independent subsets
S
of
G
by considering each possible value of
S_{R}:=S\cap V_{R}
as a separate case. Clearly,
S_{R}
has to be an independent set in
G_{R}
, and if
S_{R}
is given, then
S
cannot contain any vertex from
V_{L}
that is a neighbour of any vertex in
S_{R}
, therefore
S_{L}:=S\cap V_{L}
has to be a subset of
V_{L}\setminus\bigcup\limits_{v_{R}\in S_{R}}N_{L}(v_{R})
. As
S_{L}
also has to be independent, we indeed get that there are
i(G_{L}[V_{L}\setminus\bigcup\limits_{v_{R}\in S_{R}}N_{L}(v_{R})])
independent subsets
S
for this fixed
S_{R}
. Summing over all
S_{R}\in I(G_{R})
proves the result.
∎

Example 4.3
.

A simple but illustrative case for the usage of Proposition
4.2
is the case when
V_{R}
is a clique, say
V_{R}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
. In this case, we get

i(G)=i(G_{L})+i(G_{L}[V_{L}\setminus N_{1}])+\ldots+i(G_{L}[V_{L}\setminus N_{m}]),

where the subsets
N_{j}:=N_{L}(v_{j})
for each
1\leq j\leq m
are arbitrarily chosen subsets of
V_{L}
.

4.1
Partial join spectra

Definition 4.5
.

Let
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
be two graphs with disjoint vertex sets
V_{L}
and
V_{R}
. Then the
partial join spectrum
of
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
is defined as

\Sp(G_{L},G_{R}):=\{i(G):G\text{ is a partial join of }G_{L}\text{ and }G_{R}\}.

The following identities will be useful when the left-hand class is augmented with additional isolated vertices.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
be two graphs on disjoint vertex sets, and
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(a)

\Sp(G_{L}\sqcup\overline{K_{t}},G_{R})\supseteq 2^{t}\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})
,

(b)

\Sp(G_{L}\sqcup\overline{K_{t}},G_{R})\supseteq\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})+(2^{t}-1)i(G_{L})
.

Proof.

(a)

Let
G
be any partial join of
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
, and let
G^{\prime}
be the partial join of
G_{L}\sqcup\overline{K_{t}}
and
G_{R}
obtained from
G
by adding
t
isolated vertices to the left-hand class that are not joined to any right-hand vertices either. Then
i(G^{\prime})=i(G\sqcup\overline{K_{t}})=2^{t}\cdot i(G)
.

(b)

Let
G
be any partial join of
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
, and let
G^{\prime}
be the partial join of
G_{L}\sqcup\overline{K_{t}}
and
G_{R}
obtained from
G
by adding
t
isolated vertices to the left-hand class and joining all of them to all right-hand vertices. Then the independent sets of
G^{\prime}
are just the independent sets of
G
, with the added case that when we do not choose any right-hand vertices, we can pick any independent set of
G_{L}
together with a nonempty subset of
\overline{K_{t}}
, giving
(2^{t}-1)i(G_{L})
further options.
∎

The following lemma combines several partial joins into one, and provides the framework used for putting together our full construction.

Lemma 4.7
.

For
\ell\geq 1
, suppose that
G_{L}
,
G^{(1)}_{R}
,
G^{(2)}_{R}
, …,
G^{(\ell)}_{R}
are vertex-disjoint graphs. Let

G_{R}=G^{(1)}_{R}\triangledown G^{(2)}_{R}\triangledown\dots\triangledown G^{(\ell)}_{R}

be the full join of the
\ell
graphs
G^{(1)}_{R}
,
G^{(2)}_{R}
, …,
G^{(\ell)}_{R}
. Then

\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})=\Sp(G_{L},G^{(1)}_{R})+\ldots+\Sp(G_{L},G^{(\ell)}_{R})-(\ell-1)\cdot i(G_{L}).

Proof.

Let
V_{L}
,
V_{R}
, and
V^{(j)}_{R}
(for
1\leq j\leq\ell
) denote the vertex sets of graphs
G_{L}
,
G_{R}
and
G^{(j)}_{R}
, respectively.

Consider any partial join
G
of
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
, and for each
1\leq j\leq\ell
, let
G^{(j)}
be the subgraph of
G
induced by
V_{L}\sqcup V^{(j)}_{R}
. Then
G^{(j)}
is an arbitrary partial join of
G_{L}
and
G^{(j)}_{R}
.

For an independent subset
S
of
G
, either
S\cap V_{R}=\emptyset
or not. In the former case,
S
is an arbitrary independent subset of
G_{L}
. In the latter case, since
G_{R}
is a full join, there is exactly one
j
such that
S\cap V_{R}^{(j)}\neq\emptyset
, and
S
is an arbitrary independent subset of
G^{(j)}
not fully contained in
V_{L}
. Hence the overall number of possibilities for
S
is

i(G)=i(G_{L})+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(i(G^{(j)})-i(G_{L})\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}i(G^{(j)})-(\ell-1)\cdot i(G_{L}).

Since the partial joins
G^{(j)}
are arbitrary, we get the desired result for the partial join spectra.
∎

5
Construction: main part

The goal of this section is to prove the following proposition, which will provide the main part of our construction. We give two graphs such that in their set of partial joins
G
, the value
i(G)-c
(for some constant
c
) has a long interval of binary digits that can be arbitrarily set by varying the edges between the two classes.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fix an absolute constant
\varepsilon>0
, and take
d,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
d\geq d_{0}(\varepsilon)
is large enough and
m>2^{d}
. Then for the two graphs
G_{L}=\overline{K_{m^{d+1}}}
and
G_{R}=(d+1)K_{m}
, we have

\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})\supseteq\mathcal{B}\left(\left[\left\lceil(1+\varepsilon)2^{d-1}m^{d}\right\rceil,\penalty\ \left\lfloor m^{d+1}-(1+\varepsilon)2^{d-1}m^{d}\right\rfloor\right]\right)+c,

where
c\in\mathbb{N}
is a constant depending on
d,m,\varepsilon
.

In this section, we consider the parameters
\varepsilon>0
,
d\geq d_{0}
and
m>2^{d}
to be fixed, where
d_{0}\geq 3
is to be determined later. We will consider partial joins
G
of
G_{L}=\overline{K_{m^{d+1}}}
and
G_{R}=(d+1)K_{m}
, where some adjacencies between the two classes will be fixed and some will be variable, providing various possibilities for
i(G)
.

The left-hand class is a large empty graph, which we will present as a disjoint union of various sets. We will give a labelling of the vertices in these sets, which will be crucial to describing the adjacencies between the two classes.

The main mechanism of the labelling of
G_{L}
will be the so-called
extended hypercube
, whose properties will be presented in Subsection
5.1
. Then in Subsection
5.2
, we present the vertices and edges of
G
. In Subsections
5.3
and
5.4
, we compute
i(G)
, focusing on its variable term. It turns out that this term can be set to any value in
\mathcal{B}(J)
for a long interval
J
, when certain parameters of the construction are appropriately set.

5.1
The extended hypercube

According to the notation in Subsection
2.3
, we consider the hypercubes
Q:=[m]^{d}
and
\widehat{Q}:=[2m]^{d}
, which satisfy
Q\subseteq\widehat{Q}
. We call
Q
and
\widehat{Q}
the
base hypercube
and the
extended hypercube
, respectively.

Two points
\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
in
\widehat{Q}
will be called
congruent
if for every
1\leq j\leq d
, we have
\mathbf{y}_{j}\equiv\mathbf{y}^{\prime}_{j}\pmod{m}
. In this way, each point
\mathbf{x}\in Q
has
2^{d}
points congruent to it in
\widehat{Q}
, including itself: these points can be written
\mathbf{x}+m\mathbf{v}
for some
\mathbf{v}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
.

The extended hypercube clearly satisfies the following intersection property with axis-parallel hyperplanes. Recall that
H(j,k)=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\mathbf{x}_{j}=k\}
.

Observation 5.2
.

Let
0\leq\ell\leq d
be an integer. Given
J=\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{\ell}\}\subseteq[d]
and integers
k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in[2m]
, the following holds:

|\widehat{Q}\cap H(j_{1},k_{1})\cap\ldots\cap H(j_{\ell},k_{\ell})|=(2m)^{d-\ell}.

We now introduce a way to extend an arbitrary subset of
Q
to
\widehat{Q}
to obtain a set satisfying a similar intersection property with hyperplanes. For
\mathbf{v}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
, call
\mathbf{v}

even
, or
odd
, if the sum of its coordinates is even, or odd, respectively. If the even elements of
\{0,1\}^{d}
are coloured dark and the odd elements light, this resembles a checkerboard pattern.

Definition 5.3

(Checkered extension)
.

Given a subset
S\subseteq Q
, define the
checkered extension
of
S
to be the following subset
S_{c}\subseteq\widehat{Q}
:

\begin{split}S_{c}&=\{\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}+m\mathbf{v}:(\mathbf{x}\in S,\penalty\ \mathbf{v}\in\{0,1\}^{d},\penalty\ \mathbf{v}\text{ is even})\}\\
&\sqcup\{\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}+m\mathbf{v}:(\mathbf{x}\in Q\setminus S,\penalty\ \mathbf{v}\in\{0,1\}^{d},\penalty\ \mathbf{v}\text{ is odd})\}.\end{split}

d=2
.

An analogue of Observation
5.2
for the checkered extension of any set is the following:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
0\leq\ell\leq d-1
be an integer. Given
J=\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{\ell}\}\subseteq[d]
and integers
k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in[m]
, and any subset
S\subseteq Q
, the following holds:

|S_{c}\cap H(j_{1},k_{1})\cap\ldots\cap H(j_{\ell},k_{\ell})|=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(2m)^{d-\ell}.

Proof.

Let
F=H(j_{1},k_{1})\cap\dots\cap H(j_{\ell},k_{\ell})
. Then
F
intersects
\widehat{Q}
in the following set of size
(2m)^{d-\ell}
:

\begin{split}F\cap\widehat{Q}&=\{\mathbf{y}\in\widehat{Q}:\mathbf{y}_{j_{a}}=k_{a}\text{ for each $1\leq a\leq\ell$}\}\\
&=\{\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}+m\mathbf{v}\in\widehat{Q}:(\mathbf{x}_{j_{a}}=k_{a}\text{ and }\mathbf{v}_{j_{a}}=0)\text{ for each $1\leq a\leq\ell$}\}.\end{split}

There are
m^{d-\ell}
vectors
\mathbf{x}\in Q
such that
\mathbf{x}_{j_{a}}=k_{a}
for all
a
, and
2^{d-\ell}
vectors
\mathbf{v}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{v}_{j_{a}}=0
for all
a
. Out of the latter vectors
\mathbf{v}
, precisely half of them are even and half odd (as the parity of the coordinate sum can be set using the last non-restricted coordinate).

To get an element of
S_{c}
, we need to pick even
\mathbf{v}
in the case
\mathbf{x}\in S
and odd
\mathbf{v}
in the case
\mathbf{x}\not\in S
. As we just saw, there are
2^{d-\ell-1}
good choices for each
\mathbf{x}
, giving
|F\cap S_{c}|=m^{d-\ell}\cdot 2^{d-\ell-1}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(2m)^{d-\ell}
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

By the special case
\ell=0
, we have
|S_{c}|=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(2m)^{d}
for any
S\subseteq Q
.

5.2
The vertices and edges

We now describe in detail the vertices and edges of our variable graph
G
, which is a partial join of
G_{L}
and
G_{R}
. We take the left-hand class
G_{L}
to be the empty graph on the
(m^{d+1})
-vertex set
V_{L}
, which is the disjoint union of the following sets:

V_{L}=\widehat{Q}\sqcup A_{1}\sqcup A_{2}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup A_{d+1},

where

•

\widehat{Q}
is the
extended hypercube
that we just described, labelled as
\widehat{Q}=[2m]^{d}
.

•

for each
1\leq j\leq d
,
A_{j}
is called the
j
-th additional set
, and it has cardinality
n_{j}=m^{j-1}(m-1)
. Altogether,
\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}=m^{d}-1
.

•

A_{d+1}
is called the
special additional set
, and it consists of the remaining
n_{d+1}=m^{d+1}-(2^{d}+1)m^{d}+1
vertices. Note that this number is positive because of the assumption
m>2^{d}
.

We let
A_{j}=\{a_{j,1},\dots,a_{j,n_{j}}\}
for each
1\leq j\leq d+1
.

The right-hand class is
G_{R}=(d+1)K_{m}
. For each
1\leq i\leq d+1
, call the vertex set of the
j
-th of these complete graphs
W_{j}=\{w_{j,1},w_{j,2},\ldots,w_{j,m}\}
, so that
V_{R}=W_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup W_{d+1}
.

We now describe the edges of the partial join
G
between
V_{L}
and
V_{R}
, some of which will be always present in
G
(called
fixed edges
), while others may be either present or not present in
G
(called
variable edges
). A partial join can be defined by giving the sets
N_{L}(w)
, that is, the set of neighbours of
w
in
V_{L}
, for each
w\in V_{R}
. The fixed edges will be a subset of
K[V_{L},W_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup W_{d}]
, while the variable edges will be a subset of
K[V_{L},W_{d+1}]
.

For any
j\in[d]
and
k\in[m]
, let
s(j,k)=(k-1)m^{j-1}
. Let

N_{L}(w_{j,k})=\left(\widehat{Q}\cap H(j,k)\right)\penalty\ \sqcup\penalty\ \{a_{j,\ell}:1\leq\ell\leq s(j,k)\}.

Observe that since
|\widehat{Q}\cap H(j,k)|=(2m)^{d-1}
for all
j,k
, we have

|N_{L}(w_{j,k})|=(2m)^{d-1}+s(j,k).

Also note that no vertex in
W_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup W_{d}
has any neighbour in
A_{d+1}
.

Now we give the variable edges. For each
1\leq k\leq m
, we fix an integer
t_{k}
, which will be chosen later on, with a dependence on
d,m,\varepsilon
. The chosen integers
t_{k}
will satisfy

2^{d-1}m^{d}\leq t_{k}\leq 2^{d-1}m^{d}+n_{d+1}=m^{d+1}-(2^{d-1}+1)m^{d}+1.

Now let us choose any sets
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{m}\subseteq Q=[m]^{d}
, and consider their checkered extensions
(S_{1})_{c},\ldots,(S_{m})_{c}\subseteq\widehat{Q}=[2m]^{d}
. The sets
S_{k}
can be chosen completely arbitrarily, and they are responsible for creating the variability of the construction, and hence of the Fibonacci number of the partial join
G
.

If
t_{k}=2^{d-1}m^{d}+t_{k}^{*}
(where
0\leq t_{k}^{*}\leq n_{d+1}
), then let

N_{L}(w_{d+1,k})=(S_{k})_{c}\sqcup\{a_{d+1,1},\ldots,a_{d+1,{t_{k}^{*}}}\}.

In particular, note that no vertex in
W_{d+1}
has any neighbour in
A_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup A_{d}
. We have
|N_{L}(w_{d+1,k})|=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(2m)^{d}+t_{k}^{*}=t_{k}
by Remark
5.5
.

5.3
Computation of
i(G)

The goal of this subsection (and the subsequent one) is to prove Proposition
5.1
, that is, to show that
\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})
contains
\mathcal{B}(J)+c
, where
J
is a long interval. More specifically, our variable construction will have the property in Proposition
5.6
, from which Proposition
5.1
directly follows.

We will use the notion of full and partial transversals introduced in Subsection
2.4
in relation to the graph
G_{R}
on vertex set
V_{R}=W_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup W_{d+1}
, which satisfy
m_{1}=\ldots=m_{d+1}=m
. The notation
T=T(k_{1},\dots,k_{d},k)
will be a shorthand for the full transversal

T=\{w_{1,k_{1}},w_{2,k_{2}},\ldots,w_{d,k_{d}},w_{d+1,k}\},

where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d},k\in[m]
.

Proposition 5.6
.

There is a constant
c\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on
d,m,\varepsilon
, for which the following holds. There exist (not necessarily distinct) natural numbers
\ell(T)
for each full transversal
T
, such that

i(G)=\left(\sum_{T}\mathbbm{1}_{A(T)}2^{\ell(T)}\right)+c,

where the sum is taken over all full transversals
T=T(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d},k)
, and for each full transversal,
A(T)
is either the event
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in S_{k}
or
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\not\in S_{k}
. Moreover, the set of these natural numbers
\ell(T)
contains the interval
\left[\left\lceil(1+\varepsilon)2^{d-1}m^{d}\right\rceil,\penalty\ \left\lfloor m^{d+1}-(1+\varepsilon)2^{d-1}m^{d}\right\rfloor\right]
.

Since the properties
(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})\in S_{k}
can be independently chosen to hold or not to hold using the variable edges of our construction, it is indeed sufficient to show Proposition
5.6
.

To compute the number of independent subsets in the partial join
G
, we apply the summation trick in Example
4.4
, bearing in mind that in our case
G_{L}
is an empty graph, so we have
i(G_{L}[X])=2^{|X|}
for any subset
X\subseteq V_{L}
:

i(G)=\sum_{T}(2^{|V_{L}\setminus\bigcup_{w\in T}N_{L}(w)|}:\text{$T$ is a partial transversal}).

(5.1)

For a partial transversal
T
, let
U_{T}=\bigcup_{w\in T}N_{L}(w)
. To calculate
|U_{T}|
for a specific
T\neq\emptyset
, we will use the inclusion-exclusion principle:

|U_{T}|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{\prime}\subseteq T\\
T^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|T^{\prime}|+1}\left|\bigcap_{w\in T^{\prime}}N_{L}(w)\right|.

(5.2)

Letting
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
be a nonempty subset and
N=\bigcap\limits_{w\in T^{\prime}}N_{L}(w)
, we first present the calculation of
|N|
. Suppose that
T^{\prime}=\{w_{j_{1},k_{1}},\ldots,w_{j_{\ell},k_{\ell}}\}
for some
1\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{\ell}\leq d+1
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}\in[m]
. We consider the following two cases.

Case 1:
T^{\prime}\cap W_{d+1}=\emptyset
.

In this case, if
\ell=1
, say
T^{\prime}=\{w_{j,k}\}
, we clearly have
|N|=|N_{L}(w_{j,k})|=(2m)^{d-1}+s(j,k)
.

If
\ell\geq 2
, then since
N_{L}(w_{j,k})\setminus\widehat{Q}\subseteq A_{j}
, the sets only intersect in
\widehat{Q}
. The intersection is
N=\widehat{Q}\cap H(j_{1},k_{1})\cap\ldots\cap H(j_{\ell},k_{\ell})
,
and by Observation
5.2
, we have
|N|=(2m)^{d-\ell}
.

Case 2:
T^{\prime}\cap W_{d+1}\neq\emptyset
.

Let
T^{\prime}\cap W_{d+1}=\{w_{d+1,k}\}
, so
j_{\ell}=d+1
and
k_{\ell}=k
.

If
\ell=1
, that is,
T^{\prime}=\{w_{d+1,k}\}
, then clearly
|N|=|N_{L}(w_{d+1,k})|=t_{k}
.

When
\ell\in[2,d]
, the sets only intersect in
\widehat{Q}
. By Lemma
5.4
, we have

|N|=|(S_{k})_{c}\cap H(j_{1},k_{1})\cap\ldots\cap H(j_{\ell-1},k_{\ell-1})|=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(2m)^{d-\ell+1}.

And when
\ell=d+1
, that is,
T^{\prime}
contains one vertex from each
W_{j}
, then

|N|=|(S_{k})_{c}\cap H(1,k_{1})\cap\ldots\cap H(d,k_{d})|.

The
d
hyperplanes
H(1,k_{1})
, …,
H(d,k_{d})
meet in a single point
\mathbf{y}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in Q
, and
(S_{k})_{c}\cap Q=S_{k}
, so whether or not
\mathbf{y}
is included in
S_{k}
decides whether
|N|
is 1 or 0, that is,

|N|=\mathbbm{1}_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in S_{k}}.

Now for an arbitrary set
T\neq\emptyset
in the summation of (
5.1
), we proceed to calculate
|U_{T}|
using (
5.2
).

Using our computations of
|N|
in various cases for the subset
T^{\prime}
, we obtain that the only case where
|N|
depended on the choices of
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}
was when
|T^{\prime}|=d+1
. Such a case is only reached in the calculation of
|U_{T}|
when
|T|=d+1
, leading to the following observation.

Observation 5.7
.

If
0\leq|T|\leq d
holds for a set
T
appearing in the summation of (
5.1
), then
|U_{T}|
is a constant independent of
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}
.

So we focus the case
|T|=d+1
, that is, when
T=T(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d},k)
is a full transversal. In this case,
|U_{T}|
will contain exactly one term which depends on the choices of
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}
, coming from the case
T^{\prime}=T
, where we get
|N|=\mathbbm{1}_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in S_{k}}
. Considering the overall sum, we have

\begin{split}|U_{T}|&=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left((2m)^{d-1}+s(j,k_{j})\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|T^{\prime}|\geq 2\\
T^{\prime}\cap W_{d+1}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|T^{\prime}|+1}(2m)^{d-|T^{\prime}|}\\
&+t_{k}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|T^{\prime}|\in[2,d]\\
T^{\prime}\cap W_{d+1}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|T^{\prime}|+1}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot(2m)^{d-|T^{\prime}|+1}+(-1)^{d+2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in S_{k}}.\end{split}

Let
\sigma_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}s(j,k_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(k_{j}-1)m^{j-1}
. So
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in[m]
determine the
d
rightmost base
m
digits of
\sigma_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}
, which can hence be any value in
[0,m^{d}-1]
, with each value in this interval given by exactly one choice of
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
. We get

\begin{split}|U_{T}|&=t_{k}+\sigma_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}+(-1)^{d}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in S_{k}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}(-1)^{\ell+1}\binom{d}{\ell}(2m)^{d-\ell}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=2}^{d}(-1)^{\ell+1}\binom{d}{\ell-1}(2m)^{d-\ell+1}\\
&=t_{k}+\sigma_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}+(-1)^{d}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in S_{k}}+\frac{1}{2}\left((2m)^{d}-(2m-1)^{d}+(-1)^{d+1}\right).\end{split}

So depending on whether
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in S_{k}
or not,
|U_{T}|
is either
\lambda_{T}-1
or
\lambda_{T}
, where

\lambda_{T}=t_{k}+\sigma_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}+\frac{1}{2}\left((2m)^{d}-(2m-1)^{d}+1\right),

(5.3)

and the case giving the larger value depends on the parity of
d
. An easy estimate gives
(2m)^{d}-(2m-1)^{d}+1\in\left[d(2m-1)^{d-1},d(2m)^{d-1}\right]
.

Now in (
5.1
), we altogether have

i(G)=c_{1}+\sum_{T}2^{m^{d+1}-|U_{T}|}=c_{1}+\sum_{T}2^{m^{d+1}-\lambda_{T}}+\sum_{T}2^{m^{d+1}-\lambda_{T}}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{A(T)}=c_{2}+\sum_{T}\mathbbm{1}_{A(T)}\cdot 2^{m^{d+1}-\lambda_{T}},

where
T
runs through all full transversals,
A(T)
denotes either the event
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in S_{k}
or its negation for each
T
, and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
are constants depending only on
d,m,\varepsilon
.

So in order to prove Proposition
5.6
, all that remains is to choose the values
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
so that the set
\{\ell(T)\}
contains the interval

J=\left[\left\lceil(1+\varepsilon)2^{d-1}m^{d}\right\rceil,\penalty\ \left\lfloor m^{d+1}-(1+\varepsilon)2^{d-1}m^{d}\right\rfloor\right],

where
\ell(T)=m^{d+1}-\lambda_{T}
.

5.4
Fixing the choices of
t_{k}

Recall from (
5.3
) that for a full transversal
T=T(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d},k)
, we have

\lambda_{T}=c_{3}+t_{k}+\sigma_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}},

where
c_{3}=\frac{1}{2}((2m)^{d}-(2m-1)^{d}+1)\in\mathbb{N}
is an overall constant, and
\sigma_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}
is a different value in
[0,m^{d}-1]
for each
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
.

Also recall that we need to choose the values
t_{k}
for each
1\leq k\leq m
such that

2^{d-1}m^{d}\leq t_{k}\leq m^{d+1}-(2^{d-1}+1)m^{d}+1.

If the first
\ell
values
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{\ell}
form an arithmetic progression of difference
m^{d}
, that is,
t_{k}=t_{1}+(k-1)m^{d}
for all
k\leq\ell
, this guarantees that the corresponding values
\lambda_{T}
for
k\leq\ell
cover a single interval of natural numbers. In particular, let
t_{1}=2^{d-1}m^{d}
, meaning that

t_{m-2^{d}}=2^{d-1}m^{d}+(m-2^{d}-1)m^{d}=m^{d+1}-(2^{d-1}+1)m^{d}

is the last element that fits within the upper bound.

So we let
\ell=m-2^{d}
, and we simply set the remaining values
t_{k}
(
k>\ell
) to a constant, say
t_{k}=2^{d-1}m^{d}
, meaning that the corresponding vertices of
W_{d+1}
are not actually required for our statements, and it would have sufficed to pick
|W_{d+1}|=m-2^{d}
. However, we chose
|W_{d+1}|=m
for the sake of simplicity in presenting the construction.

Altogether, the values
t_{k}+\sigma_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}
cover the interval

J_{1}=[t_{1},t_{m-2^{d}}+m^{d}-1]=[2^{d-1}m^{d},m^{d+1}-2^{d-1}m^{d}-1],

and we have
c_{3}\in\left[\frac{1}{2}d(2m-1)^{d-1},\frac{1}{2}d(2m)^{d-1}\right]
, where by Bernoulli’s inequality (using
m>2^{d}
),
(2m-1)^{d-1}\geq 0.99(2m)^{d-1}
for
d
large enough. So the values
\lambda_{T}
cover the interval

\begin{split}&\left[2^{d-1}m^{d}+\frac{1}{2}d\cdot 2^{d-1}m^{d-1},m^{d+1}-2^{d-1}m^{d}+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\cdot 0.99d\cdot 2^{d-1}m^{d-1}-1\right\rfloor\right]\\
\supseteq&\left[\left\lceil((1+\varepsilon)2^{d-1}m^{d}\right\rceil,\left\lfloor m^{d+1}-(1+\varepsilon)2^{d-1}m^{d}\right\rfloor\right]\end{split}

for
d\geq d_{0}(\varepsilon)
large enough, and by symmetry, so do the values
\ell(T)=m^{d+1}-\lambda_{T}
. This concludes the proof of Propositions
5.6
and
5.1
.

6
Construction: combination of multiple parts

Using the main structure described in the previous section, this section is devoted to completing the proof of the lower bound of Theorem
1.3
. We combine several copies of our structure to create our final variable graph
G
.

We will take a sequence of values
n=n(D)\to\infty
indexed by a parameter
D
that can be equal to any sufficiently large integer. Let
M=2^{D+1}
, and fix the left-hand class to be
G_{L}=\overline{K_{M^{D+1}}}
, that is, the empty graph on
n_{0}:=2^{D^{2}+2D+1}
vertices.

The construction will be in the spirit of Lemma
4.7
: we let
G_{R}
be the full join of
\ell
graphs

G_{R}=G^{(1)}_{R}\triangledown G^{(2)}_{R}\triangledown\dots\triangledown G^{(\ell)}_{R},

where each graph
G^{(j)}_{R}
is isomorphic to
(d_{j}+1)K_{m_{j}}
for some integers
d_{j}
and
m_{j}
, with
m_{j}>2^{d_{j}}
. We will use Proposition
5.1
for each
\Sp(G_{L},G^{(j)}_{R})
, and then use the lemma to combine the resulting spectra.

The skeleton of our argument is described in the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and take integer intervals
J,Y_{0},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{k},Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{k}\subseteq[0,n_{0}]
, graphs
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
and integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in[n_{0}]
such that

•

J\subseteq Y_{0}\cup Y_{1}\cup\ldots\cup Y_{k}\cup Z_{1}\cup\ldots\cup Z_{k}
,

•

Z_{j}=n_{0}-Y_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq k
,

•

there exists
t_{j}\in[0,n_{0}-n_{j}]
for all
1\leq j\leq k
such that
Z_{j}=Y_{j}+t_{j}
,

•

there exist numbers
c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
0\leq j\leq k
,

\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{j}}},A_{j})\supseteq\mathcal{B}(Y_{j})+c_{j}.

Then there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})\supseteq\mathcal{B}(J)+c,

where:

•

G_{L}=\overline{K_{n_{0}}}
,

•

G_{R}=A_{0}\triangledown A_{1}\triangledown\ldots\triangledown A_{k}\triangledown B_{1}\triangledown\ldots\triangledown B_{k}
, with
B_{j}
being an isomorphic copy of
A_{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq k
.

Proof.

Firstly we will use Lemma
4.6
that pads the left-hand class with further isolated vertices, allowing us to obtain a statement about
\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{0}}},A_{j})
instead of
\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{j}}},A_{j})
. Letting
t^{\prime}_{j}=n_{0}-n_{j}-t_{j}
, we have

•

\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{0}}},A_{j})\supseteq 2^{t_{j}}\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{j}}},A_{j})+(2^{t^{\prime}_{j}}-1)2^{n_{j}+t_{j}}\supseteq\mathcal{B}(Z_{j})+2^{t_{j}}\cdot c_{j}+(2^{t^{\prime}_{j}}-1)2^{n_{j}+t_{j}}=\mathcal{B}(Z_{j})+c^{(1)}_{j}
,

•

\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{0}}},B_{j})\supseteq\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{j}}},B_{j})+(2^{n_{0}-n_{j}}-1)2^{n_{j}}\supseteq\mathcal{B}(Y_{j})+c^{(2)}_{j}

for some constants
c^{(1)}_{j}
and
c^{(2)}_{j}
, where the first claim comes from applying part (a)
t_{j}
times and then part (b)
t^{\prime}_{j}
times.

Let us combine these spectra using Lemma
4.7
:

\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})\supseteq\mathcal{B}(Y_{0})+c_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\mathcal{B}(Y_{j})+\mathcal{B}(Z_{j})+c_{j}^{(1)}+c_{j}^{(2)}\right]-2k\cdot 2^{n_{0}}\supseteq\mathcal{B}(J)+c.\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.3
, lower bound.

Fix a global constant
\delta>0
to be sufficiently small. Then fix an arbitrary positive integer
D>d_{0}
, where
d_{0}=d_{0}(\delta)
is sufficiently large, and take
M=2^{D+1}
. We are going to use Proposition
6.1
for
n_{0}:=M^{D+1}=2^{(D+1)^{2}}
, where instead of indices
1\leq j\leq k
for
Y_{j},Z_{j},A_{j},n_{j}
, we will use ordered pairs
(d,q)
of integers satisfying
d\in[d_{0},D-1]
and
q\in[2d+3]
. So altogether,

k=\sum_{d=d_{0}}^{D-1}(2d+3)=D^{2}+2D-d_{0}^{2}-2d_{0}.

Firstly fix
Y_{0}:=\left[\left\lceil\left(\frac{1}{4}+\delta\right)2^{(D+1)^{2}}\right\rceil,\left\lfloor\left(\frac{3}{4}-\delta\right)2^{(D+1)^{2}}\right\rfloor\right]
, and correspondingly take
A_{0}=(D+1)K_{M}
. By Proposition
5.1
for
\varepsilon=4\delta
,
\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{0}}},A_{0})\supseteq\mathcal{B}(Y_{0})+c_{0}
for some
c_{0}
.

Now for each
d\in[d_{0},D-1]
and
q\in[2d+3]
, we will define a positive integer
m_{d,q}\in[2^{d+1},2^{d+3}]
such that by Proposition
5.1
for
\varepsilon=4\delta
, we will have

\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{d,q}}},A_{d,q})\supseteq\mathcal{B}(Y_{d,q})+c_{d,q}

for a constant
c_{d,q}
, where

•

A_{d,q}:=(d+1)K_{m_{d,q}}
,

•

n_{d,q}:=m_{d,q}^{d+1}
.

•

Y_{d,q}:=\left[\left\lceil\frac{1}{3}\cdot 2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}\right\rceil,\left\lfloor\frac{2}{3}\cdot 2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}\right\rfloor\right]
.

The intervals
Y_{d,q}
for a single value
d\in[d_{0},D-1]
cover the interval

\left[\left\lceil\frac{1}{3}\cdot 2^{(d+1)^{2}}\right\rceil,\left\lfloor\frac{2}{3}\cdot 2^{(d+2)^{2}-1}\right\rfloor\right]

without gaps, and altogether with
Y_{0}
, the intervals for all
d
cover

\left[\left\lceil\frac{1}{3}\cdot 2^{(d_{0}+1)^{2}}\right\rceil,\left\lfloor\frac{2}{3}\cdot 2^{(D+1)^{2}}\right\rfloor\right].

Together with the reflections
Z_{d,q}:=2^{(D+1)^{2}}-Y_{d,q}
, the intervals cover
J:=[a,2^{(D+1)^{2}}-a]
, where
a:=\left\lceil\frac{1}{3}\cdot 2^{(d_{0}+1)^{2}}\right\rceil
is a constant depending on
\delta
.

We apply Proposition
6.1
for
G_{L}=\overline{K_{2^{(D+1)^{2}}}}
and
G_{R}=A_{0}\triangledown A_{d_{0},1}\triangledown\ldots\triangledown A_{D-1,2d+3}\triangledown B_{d_{0},1}\triangledown\ldots\triangledown B_{D_{1},2d+3}
, with
B_{d,q}
being an isomorphic copy of
A_{d,q}
for each
d\in[d_{0},D-1]
and
q\in[2d+3]
.

To satisfy the conditions of this proposition, we need to have
Z_{d,q}=Y_{d,q}+t_{d,q}
for some
0\leq t_{d,q}\leq 2^{(D+1)^{2}}-m_{d,q}^{d+1}
, which is equivalent to the following condition:

(1)

2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}\geq m_{d,q}^{d+1}
.

Note that Proposition
5.1
for
\varepsilon=4\delta
gives

\Sp(\overline{K_{n_{d,q}}},A_{d,q})\supseteq\mathcal{B}\left(\left[\left\lceil\left(\frac{1}{4}+\delta\right)2^{d+1}m_{d,q}^{d}\right\rceil,\penalty\ \left\lfloor m_{d,q}^{d+1}-\left(\frac{1}{4}+\delta\right)2^{d+1}m_{d,q}^{d}\right\rfloor\right]\right)+c_{d,q}.

So all that remains is to define the values
m_{d,q}
for all
d,q
so that
Y_{d,q}
is contained in the interval appearing in this expression. It suffices to make sure that:

(2)

\left(\frac{1}{4}+\delta\right)2^{d+1}m_{d,q}^{d}\leq\frac{1}{3}\cdot 2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}
,

(3)

m_{d,q}^{d+1}-\left(\frac{1}{4}+\delta\right)2^{d+1}m_{d,q}^{d}\geq\frac{2}{3}\cdot 2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}
.

Observe that
\left(\frac{1}{4}+\delta\right)2^{d+1}m_{d,q}^{d}\leq\left(\frac{1}{4}+\delta\right)m_{d,q}^{d+1}
, since
m_{d,q}\geq 2^{d+1}
. So it suffices to have:

(2’)

\left(\frac{1}{4}+\delta\right)m_{d,q}^{d+1}\leq\frac{1}{3}\cdot 2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}
,

(3’)

\left(\frac{3}{4}-\delta\right)m_{d,q}^{d+1}\geq\frac{2}{3}\cdot 2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}
.

These conditions, together with (1), amount to
\left(\frac{8}{9}+\delta^{\prime}\right)\cdot 2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}\leq m_{d,q}^{d+1}\leq 2^{(d+2)^{2}-q}
, where
\delta^{\prime}
is a function of
\delta
with
\delta^{\prime}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.

In the cases
q\in\{1,2d+3\}
, the choices
m_{d,1}=2^{d+3}
and
m_{d,2d+3}=2^{d+1}
can be seen to work. In the remaining cases
q\not\in\{1,2d+3\}
, there exists an integer
m_{d,q}\in[2^{d+1},2^{d+3}]
satisfying these inequalities, since:

•

for any two neighbouring values
m
and
m+1
in this interval,
\frac{(m+1)^{d+1}}{m^{d+1}}\leq\exp\left(\frac{d+1}{m}\right)\leq\exp\left(\frac{d+1}{2^{d+1}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\frac{8}{9}+\delta^{\prime}}
for
d\geq 5
and
\delta^{\prime}
small enough;

•

the extremal choices
m=2^{d+1}
and
m=2^{d+3}
overshoot the endpoints of the required interval, since:

\circ

2^{(d+1)^{2}}<\left(\frac{8}{9}+\delta^{\prime}\right)\cdot 2^{d^{2}+2d+2}
,

\circ

2^{(d+1)(d+3)}>2^{d^{2}+4d+2}
.

So the requirements of Proposition
6.1
are met, and we altogether get
\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})\supseteq\mathcal{B}(J)+c
for a constant
c
, meaning that
|\Sp(G_{L},G_{R})|\geq 2^{2^{(D+1)^{2}}-2a}
.

Altogether, the number of vertices in our construction is

\begin{split}n&=|V_{L}|+|V_{R}|\\
&=2^{(D+1)^{2}}+(D+1)2^{D+1}+2\left(\sum_{d=d_{0}}^{D-1}\sum_{q=1}^{2d+3}(d+1)m_{d,q}\right)\\
&=2^{(D+1)^{2}}+\Theta(D^{2}\cdot 2^{D}),\end{split}

and we obtained that the Fibonacci number of such an
n
-vertex graph
G
can take on at least

2^{2^{(D+1)^{2}}-2a}=2^{n-2^{(1+o(1))\sqrt{\log n}}}

values, attaining the required lower bound.
∎

7
Application to
[k,1]
-avoiders

The bounds on the size of the Fibonacci spectrum described in this article can be used in relation to a previous question raised by the authors
[
14
]
, which is a generalization of several well-known problems in additive combinatorics and finite geometry, such as finding the minimum size of a blocking set or the maximum size of a cap set.
We recall the problem investigated in this paper
[
14
]
and formulate it in the binary vector space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, although a similar question can be asked over any finite field. A
k
-dimensional affine subspace in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is called a
k
-flat
.

Question 7.1

(The avoidance problem for
[k,t]
-flats, Kovács, Nagy
[
14
]
)
.

We are given integers
n,m,k,t
with
1\leq k\leq n
,
\penalty\ 0\leq m\leq 2^{n}
and
0\leq t\leq 2^{k}
. Is it true that for any set
S
of cardinality
m
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, there exists a
k
-flat
F_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
that intersects
S
in exactly
t
points?

Such a flat is referred to as a
[k,t]
-flat induced by
S
. If
S
does not induce a
[k,t]
-flat, it is a
[k,t]
-avoider
. The present authors investigated the
(n;k,t)
-spectrum

\Sp(n;k,t)=\{0\leq m\leq 2^{n}:\text{every $S$ with $|S|=m$ induces a $[k,t]$-flat}\},

and conjectured that for any fixed pair
(k,t)
, we have

|\Sp(n;k,t)|=(1-o(1))2^{n}

as
n\to\infty
.

In
[
14
]
, the conjecture was shown true for
t\in\{0,2^{k-1},2^{k}\}
when
k\geq 3
, and for all
t
when
k\in\{1,2\}
. A lower bound was also given on the spectrum size when
t
is of the form
2^{a}
or
3\cdot 2^{a}
for an integer
1\leq a\leq k-2
.

In
[
13
]
, the first author primarily investigated the smallest undecided case
(k,t)=(3,1)
. As an application of the general probabilistic lower bound from our joint paper
[
14
]
, which was based on an idea of Guruswami
[
8
]
, he obtained the following result.

Theorem 7.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

There exists
K>0
such that for sufficiently large
n
,

|\Sp(n;3,1)|\leq 2^{n}-K\cdot 2^{n/2}.

Furthermore, he gave an explicit construction of
[3,1]
-avoiders based on linear codes, which still gives exponentially many different sizes outside the spectrum.
He also suggested a different construction that uses the independent sets of an arbitrary graph
G
on
n
vertices to construct a
[3,1]
-avoider:

Construction 7.3

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
G=([n],E)
be a graph. If for every edge
e=uv\in E
we consider the
(n-2)
-flat
F_{e}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:\mathbf{x}_{u}=\mathbf{x}_{v}=1\}
, then
S_{G}=\cup_{e\in E}F_{e}
is a
[3,1]
-avoider in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
|S_{G}|=2^{n}-i(G)
.

Using this construction in tandem with Theorem
1.3
of the current paper, we now obtain the following improvement to Theorem
7.2
:

Theorem 7.4
.

For infinitely many
n
, we have

\scalebox{1.25}{$|\Sp(n;3,1)|\leq 2^{n}-2^{n-2^{(1+o(1))\sqrt{\log n}}}$}.

The same bound applies for
[k,1]
-avoiders for all
k\geq 4
, because
[3,1]
-avoiders also avoid
[k,1]
-flats.

8
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we showed that
\frac{\log_{2}\mathcal{N}i(g(n))}{g(n)}\to 1
holds in a strong sense for a subsequence
g(n)
of the natural numbers.
While our main construction was built on a vertex set of size belonging to just an infinite sequence, we believe that the following also holds: for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a constant
K_{\varepsilon}
such that for all
n
,

\mathcal{N}i(n)\geq K_{\varepsilon}2^{n(1-\varepsilon)}.

It also remained an open problem to determine the growth of

2^{n}/\mathcal{N}i(n)
.

The main problem has a natural extension for hypergraphs as well. For a hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
, a subset
W\subseteq V
is an
independent set
if
W
does not contain any hyperedge of
H
. The rank of the hypergraph is the maximum cardinality of any of the edges of the hypergraph.

Question 8.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
. How many values can
i(H)
admit for hypergraphs
H
of rank at most
r
on
n
vertices?

As a special case, one might investigate only the
r
-uniform hypergraphs.

In
[
13
]
, the first author showed that a result on Question
8.1
has an analogous application to the
[k,1]
-avoider problem using an analogue statement to Construction
7.3
. From the general construction of
[
13
]
, we have that for
n\geq k\geq 3
, if
H
is any hypergraph on
[n]
of rank at most
k-1
, there is a
[k,1]
-avoider
S_{H}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
|S_{H}|=2^{n}-i(H)
. Therefore, a lower bound for Question
8.1
immediately implies an upper bound on
|\Sp(n;k,1)|
as described in Section
7
.

Our result has some consequences in relation to the theory of independence polynomials as well.
Levit and Mandrescu
[
16
]
showed several examples of non-isomorphic graph pairs which have the same independence polynomial. In fact, there are graphs
G
for which
I_{G}(x)\equiv I_{\overline{G}}(x)
but
G\not\cong\overline{G}

[
10
]
.
Thus it is natural to pose the following question:

Problem 8.2
.

What is the order of magnitude of
|\{I_{G}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\}|
?

Our result can also be interpreted as an exponential lower bound on the number of distinct independence polynomials of
n
-vertex graphs with base arbitrarily close to
2
.

In the same spirit, it would be interesting to determine
|\{i(G):G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}^{*}\}|
for some other more restricted graph families
\mathcal{G}_{n}^{*}
of
n
-vertex graphs.

A closely related problem would be to determine the set of cardinalities of the total number of independent
k
-sets that can be attained in
n
-vertex graphs. Observe that this question concerns the value of the coefficient
i_{k}
of
x^{k}
in the independence polynomial
I_{G}(x)
. This problem has been addressed very recently by Kittipassorn and Popielarz
[
12
]
in a more general framework, as they proved results for the set of the possible number of copies of a fixed connected graph
F
in a graph on
n
vertices. Setting
F
to be a clique would give back the previously mentioned variant of our main problem.

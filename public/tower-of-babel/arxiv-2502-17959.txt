Multiplier modules of Hilbert C*-modules revisited
Michael Frank

Abstract
. The theory of multiplier modules of Hilbert C*-modules is reconsidered to obtain more properties of these special Hilbert C*-modules. The property of a Hilbert C*-module to be a multiplier C*-module is shown to be an invariant with respect to the consideration as a left or right Hilbert C*-module in the sense of a imprimitivity bimodule in strong Morita equivalence theory. The interrelation of the C*-algebras of ”compact” operators, the Banach algebras of bounded module operators and the Banach spaces of bounded module operators of a Hilbert C*-module to its C*-dual Banach C*-module, are characterized for pairs of Hilbert C*-modules and their respective multiplier modules. The structures on the latter are always isometrically embedded into the respective structures on the former. Examples are given for which continuation of these kinds of bounded module operators from the initial Hilbert C*-module to its multiplier module fails. However, existing continuations turn out to be always unique. Similarly, bounded modular functionals from both kinds of Hilbert C*-modules to their respective C*-algebras of coefficients are compared, and eventually existing continuations are shown to be unique.

The aim of the present note is to fill in some missing facts from the point of view of classical Hilbert C*-module theory and to describe pairs of Hilbert C*-modules and their multiplier modules from the point of view of their common and partially even differing properties. We get some results that contradict habits and opinions from Hilbert and Banach space theory. So, some new examples complement the existing points of view on the theory of Hilbert C*-modules.

Considering multiplier modules as full left Hilbert C*-modules
X
over a C*-algebra
A
we obtain that they are at the same time full right Hilbert C*-modules and multiplier modules over the respective C*-algebras
{\rm K}_{A}(X)
. This reminds special imprimitivity bimodules, however no new type of Morita equivalence can be derived. Considering pairs
(X,M(X))
of Hilbert C*-modules and their multiplier modules we consider the interrelations of comparable types of operator algebras of bounded module operators over them and continuation problems of operators from

{\rm End}_{A}(X)

and on

Inventing different kinds of strict topologies intrinsic characterizations of these structures as certain topological completions of

]

We would like to consider Hilbert C*-modules over (non-unital, in general) C*-algebras and, in particular, their multiplier modules and related structures. By convention all Hilbert C*-modules are right C*-modules, at the first glance. However, for full Hilbert

-modules one can obtain an operator-valued inner product turning them into full left Hilbert

-modules, and vice versa. So, the point of view decides which of the two Hilbert C*-module structures is primary and which is secondary. This kind of consideration will be used frequently in the present paper.

A pre-Hilbert

-module over a C*-algebra

is an

-module

-valued, non-degenerate mapping
\langle.,.\rangle:X\times X\to A

is called the

-valued inner product on

-module

is Hilbert if and only if it is complete with respect to the norm
\|.\|=\|\langle.,.\rangle\|^{1/2}_{A}

are compatible. A Hilbert

-module

over a C*-algebra

is full if the norm-closed

-linear hull

. Two (full) Hilbert

-modules

over a fixed C*-algebra

are unitarily equivalent (or unitarily isomorphic) iff there exists a bounded invertible adjointable map

such that
\langle.,.\rangle_{X}=\langle T(.),T(.)\rangle_{Y}
on
X
. The
A
-dual Banach

-module

of a Hilbert

-module

is defined as the set of all bounded

-linear maps from

into

. It might not be a Hilbert

-module itself, cf.
[
18
]
. But,

is always canonically isometrically embedded into

as a Banach

-submodule via the identification of

with
\langle x,\cdot\rangle\in X^{\prime}
.
Note, that two

-valued inner products on a Hilbert

-module

inducing equivalent norms on

[

,
18
]
. Thus, full Hilbert C*-modules are always a triple of the C*-algebra

of coefficients, the Banach

-module

and the
A
-valued inner product on

. We omit the explicit reference to the C*-valued inner product in situations where its definition formula is standard or only its existence is important.

We are interested in properties of sets of bounded
A
-linear operators between Banach and Hilbert C*-modules

. The set
{\rm End}_{A}(X)
of all bounded module operators on Hilbert

-modules

forms a Banach algebra, whereas the set

of all bounded module operators which possess an adjoint operator inside

has the structure of a unital C*-algebra. Note, that these two sets do not coincide in general, cf.
[

,
18
]
. An important subset of

is the set
{\rm K}_{A}(X)
of ”compact” operators, which is defined as the norm-closure of the set of all finite linear combinations of elementary operators

\{\theta_{a,b}\in{\rm End}_{A}(X):a,b\in X\,,\>\theta_{a,b}(c)=b\langle a,c\rangle\quad{\rm for}\>{\rm every}\quad c\in X\}.

It is a C*-subalgebra and a two-sided ideal of
{\rm End}_{A}^{*}(X)
. In contrast to the well-known situation for Hilbert spaces, the properties
of an operator of being ”compact” or possessing an adjoint depend strongly on the choice of the

-valued inner product on

, i.e. these
properties are not invariant for unitarily non-isomorphic C*-valued inner products on

inducing equivalent norms, cf.
[
18
]
.

,

,

]
.

Searching for intrinsic characterizations of Hilbert C*-modules, especially over non-unital C*-algebras, the notions of orthonormal bases and of frames for Hilbert spaces were rediscovered in the modular context of Hilbert C*-modules by D. R. Larson and the author during 2018-2022,

In 2017 Lj. Arambašić and D. Bakić have made a significant progress in the understanding of Hilbert C*-modules over non-unital C*-algebras. They used the strict completion picture to multiplier modules of

2.
On multiplier modules

Let us show a way to define multiplier modules
M(X)
of given (full) Hilbert
A
-modules
X
as a certain related Hilbert
M(A)
-module, and let us provide some important properties of them. We start with a class of extensions of a given full Hilbert

-module

as defined in
[

]
:

Definition 2.1
.

(cf.
[
5
, Def. 1.1]
)
Let
X
be a full Hilbert C*-module over a given (non-unital, in general) C*-algebra

. An extension of

is a triple
(Y,B,\Phi)
such that

(i)

(ii)

is a Hilbert

-module.

(iii)

\Phi:X\to Y

for any

(iv)

{\rm Im}(\Phi)=YA=\{za:z\in Y,a\in A\}=\{x\in Y:\langle x,x\rangle\in A\}
(by the Hewitt-Cohen factorization theorem,
[
43
, Thm. 4.1]
,
[

, Prop. 2.31]
,
[

, Thm. 23.22]

The triple

is an essential extension of

if

is an essential ideal of

Note, that

is an

-linear isometry of Hilbert

-modules and, hence, in case of a surjective mapping a unitary map, preserving

-valued inner products up to unitary equivalence, cf.
[

]

[

[

, Thm. 1.1]
. So,

are unitarily equivalent Hilbert C*-modules. In the sequel we consider the C*-algebras

and

as a Hilbert

-module and as a Hilbert

-module over itself, respectively, setting
\langle a,b\rangle=a^{*}b
for any two C*-algebra elements

. Then

and

are

-isometrically isomorphic to the C*-algebras

and

, respectively. By

these

Definition 2.2
.

Let

be a (not necessarily full) Hilbert C*-module over a given (non-unital, in general) C*-algebra

. Denote by

the set of all adjointable maps from

-module with the

In

as the subset

Let us explain the definition of multiplier modules

for non-full Hilbert

.

(i)

Example 2.4

Remark 2.6

Remark 3.5
.

One natural question is whether multiplier modules might be C*-reflexive with respect to certain C*-algebras
B
with
A\subseteq B\subseteq M(A)
, or not. The background are Definition
2.1
, and a result by W. L. Paschke that the C*-valued inner product on a full Hilbert C*-module can be always continued to its C*-reflexive C*-bidual Banach C*-module preserving the isometric embedding of the former into the latter, cf.
[
41
]
. One could hope to find intrinsic topological or order-algebraical characterizations of some C*-reflexive Hilbert C*-modules, cf. the
l_{2}(K(l_{2}))
-example above in the context of
[
40
]
. In general, C*-reflexivity of Hilbert C*-modules highly depends on both the inner structures of the C*-algebra of coefficients and of the Hilbert C*-module under consideration.

To test this point of view select a non-unital C*-algebra
A
with
M(A)=LM(A)
. First, if one considers
A
-reflexivity of
X=A
with the standard
A
-valued inner product then
M_{A}(X)=M(A)
,
X_{A}^{\prime}=M(A)
and
X_{A}^{\prime\prime}=A
, so
M_{A}(X)
is not
A
-reflexive. Furthermore, considering
X=A
as a Hilbert
M(A)
-module,
M_{M(A)}(X)=A
,
X_{M(A)}^{\prime}=M(A)
and
X_{M(A)}^{\prime\prime}=M(A)
, so it is not
M(A)
-reflexive, either. The deeper reason could be that condition (iv) of Definition
2.1
might be violated for the C*-reflexive Hilbert C*-module extension of
X
.

Furthermore, instead of two-sided norm-closed ideals
A
in their multiplier algebras
M(A)
(and hence, multiplier modules) one-sided norm-closed ideals in C*-algebras and their biorthogonal complements in the host C*-algebra can be considered. Here several notions of density of the respective ideal in the arising C*-subalgebra of the host C*-algebra have to be compared, and it is far from obvious that they coincide like in the two-sided norm-closed ideal case, cf.
[
37
]
. These considerations provide a wider variety of examples of Hilbert C*-modules and their multiplier modules.

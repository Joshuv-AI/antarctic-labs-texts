Studying Bremsstrahlung in Polarized Background Field at EIC and EicC
Cong Li

Abstract
Within the framework of quantum field theory, we have studied bremsstrahlung radiation in polarized electromagnetic fields. It reveals that the resulting photon beams are polarized, reflecting the properties of the background field. We propose to observe this phenomenon via the Bethe-Heitler process and provide numerical estimates at the Electron-Ion Collider and the Electron-Ion Collider in China.



I
Introduction

In the framework of quantum field theory, we analyze the phenomenon of bremsstrahlung radiation generated when a charged particle traverses a polarized electromagnetic field. This investigation reveals that the radiation produced in such interactions is intrinsically polarized, a property tied to the structure of the background field. Building on this insight, we propose a method to observe this phenomenon via the Bethe-Heitler process and provide numerical estimates for its experimental realization in high-energy facilities such as the Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) and the Electron-Ion Collider in China (EicC). This work offers a novel approach to probing polarized background fields and highlights how measurements of bremsstrahlung radiation can serve as a powerful tool to characterize field polarization.

The study of bremsstrahlung radiation in polarized fields is crucial for understanding the structure and dynamics of high-energy interactions. For example, angular asymmetries in photon emission during the Bethe-Heitler (BH) process could provide direct insights into the polarization state of the background field, revealing medium-specific properties that are otherwise difficult to access using traditional methods. Despite its importance, much of the existing literature relies on simplified models that assume static or unpolarized fields and neglect quantum-level effects
Korol and Solov’yov 2014
;
Ritus 1985
;
Marklund and Shukla 2006
;
Di Piazza et al. 2012
;
Mourou et al. 2006
;
Narozhny and Fedotov 2015
. Our work bridges this gap by incorporating both quantum interactions and perturbative corrections, including effects such as multiple scatterings and soft radiation. These corrections substantially alter the radiation spectrum, providing a more realistic framework for analyzing emission properties. To the best of our knowledge, our research is the first to propose the study of bremsstrahlung in polarized background fields within high-energy collider experiments. Previous studies have primarily focused on strong-field QED, electron radiation in intense laser fields
Di Piazza et al. 2012
;
Mourou et al. 2006
;
Narozhny and Fedotov 2015
, and high-energy electron radiation in ordered crystals
Baier and Katkov 2005
;
Baier and Katkov 2006
;
Uggerhøj 2005
;
Azadegan et al. 2012
.

Another critical aspect of this study lies in its experimental feasibility. Polarized background fields, such as those generated in Ultra-Peripheral Collisions (UPC)
Li et al. 2019
;
Li et al. 2020
, provide an ideal testing ground for our proposed methodology. While current experimental setups face challenges in directly measuring photon polarization, our approach circumvents these limitations by inferring field polarization through angular asymmetry in photon emission. This method makes the experimental observation of polarized radiation much more accessible.

The broader implications of this work extend beyond collider experiments. The proposed framework opens up possibilities for studying nuclear structure, such as examining bremsstrahlung in the Color Glass Condensate
Boer et al. 2017
;
Metz and Zhou 2011
. Moreover, bremsstrahlung radiation influenced by polarized fields has significant applications in astrophysics, such as interpreting gamma-ray emissions in interstellar environments
Blumenthal and Gould 1970
;
Rybicki and Lightman 1979
. By advancing theoretical models and proposing experimentally viable detection methods, this study provides a comprehensive foundation for exploring the dynamics of charged particles in polarized fields. Ultimately, these findings contribute to ongoing research initiatives at high-energy facilities such as the EIC, EicC, and beyond, while offering insights relevant to both laboratory environments and astrophysical phenomena.

II
Photon propagators in polarized background fields

In this study, we primarily investigate the BH process in the EIC and EicC. During the electron-ion collision, the electron absorbs a photon from the nuclear field and emits a final-state photon. As the electron traverses the external electromagnetic field of the nucleus, it undergoes multiple scatterings with the background field. Simultaneously, if the electron radiates a real photon, this process is also influenced by the background field. As mentioned earlier, the nuclear external photon field is polarized
Li et al. 2019
;
Metz and Zhou 2011
;
Mulders and Rodrigues 2001
;
Dominguez et al. 2012
, and the deceleration of the electron in this polarized background field, leading to photon emission, is the central theme of our study—bremsstrahlung in a polarized background field.

For the BH process in electron-ion collisions, it can be factorized into two parts: the hard part and the photon transverse momentum-dependent (TMD) distribution function describing the nuclear external photon distribution. In general, the photon correlator can be parameterized as follows
Mulders and Rodrigues 2001
;
Dominguez et al. 2011
;
Xiao and Yuan 2010
,

\left.\int\frac{2dy^{-}d^{2}y_{\bot}}{xP^{+}(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ik\cdot y}\langle P|F_{+\bot}^{\mu}(0)F_{+\bot}^{\nu}(y)|P\rangle\right|_{y^{+}=0}=\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}f_{1}^{\gamma}\left(x,k_{\bot}^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{2k_{\bot}^{\mu}k_{\bot}^{\nu}}{k_{\bot}^{2}}-\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}\right)h_{1}^{\bot\gamma}\left(x,k_{\bot}^{2}\right)

(1)

where
\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\nu}+p^{\mu}n^{\nu}+p^{\nu}n^{\mu}
, and thus
k_{\bot}^{2}=\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}k_{\bot\mu}k_{\bot\nu}
. Here,
x
represents the longitudinal momentum fraction carried by the photon. The functions
f_{1}^{\gamma}(x,k_{\bot}^{2})
and
h_{1}^{\bot\gamma}(x,k_{\bot}^{2})
are the unpolarized and linearly polarized photon TMDs, respectively.

Assuming that the nucleus moves along the
P^{+}
direction, the dominant component of the gauge potential is
A^{+}
, while other components are suppressed by the Lorentz contraction factor
\gamma
. Based on this observation, after partial integration, the photon field strength tensor can be approximated as
F_{+\perp}^{\mu}F_{+\perp}^{\nu}\propto k_{\perp}^{\mu}k_{\perp}^{\nu}A^{+}A^{+}
, which leads to the relation
Li et al. 2019
;
Li et al. 2020
;
Vidović et al. 1993
;
Bertulani and Baur 1988
,

f_{1}^{\gamma}\left(x,k_{\perp}^{2}\right)=h_{1}^{\perp\gamma}\left(x,k_{\perp}^{2}\right)

(2)

within the equivalent photon approximation, one obtains,

xf_{1}^{\gamma}\left(x,k_{\perp}^{2}\right)=xh_{1}^{\perp\gamma}\left(x,k_{\perp}^{2}\right)=\frac{Z^{2}\alpha_{e}}{\pi^{2}}k_{\perp}^{2}\left[\frac{F\left(k_{\perp}^{2}+x^{2}M_{p}^{2}\right)}{\left(k_{\perp}^{2}+x^{2}M_{p}^{2}\right)}\right]^{2}

(3)

where
Z
is the nuclear charge number,
F
is the nuclear charge form factor, and
M_{p}
is the proton mass. The nuclear charge form factor can be obtained from the STARlight Monte Carlo generator
Klein et al. 2017
,

F(|\vec{k}|)=\frac{4\pi\rho^{0}}{|\vec{k}|^{3}A}\left[\sin\left(|\vec{k}|R_{A}\right)-|\vec{k}|R_{A}\cos\left(|\vec{k}|R_{A}\right)\right]\frac{1}{a^{2}|\vec{k}|^{2}+1}

(4)

where
R_{A}=1.1A^{1/3}\text{ fm}
and
a=0.7\text{ fm}
. This parameterization closely resembles the Woods-Saxon distribution in phase space.

As the electron passes through the photon field, it undergoes multiple scatterings with the background photon field, leading to the Coulomb correction. This correction occurs either before or after photon radiation and can be absorbed into the photon correlator via a closed-loop gauge link. The explicit form is given by
Lee et al. 2005
;
Olsen 2003
;
hao Sun et al. 2020
,

\displaystyle\int\frac{dy^{-}d^{2}y_{\bot}}{P^{+}(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ik\cdot y}

\displaystyle\left.\langle A|F_{+\bot}^{\mu}(0)U^{\dagger}(0_{\bot})U(y_{\bot})F_{+\bot}^{\nu}(y)|A\rangle\right|_{y^{+}=0}

(5)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}y_{\bot}\,d^{2}y_{\bot}^{\prime}}{4\pi^{3}}e^{ik_{\bot}\cdot(y_{\bot}-y_{\bot}^{\prime})}\mathcal{F}^{\mu}(x,y_{\bot})\mathcal{F}^{*\nu}(x,y_{\bot}^{\prime})e^{i[\mathcal{V}(y_{\bot})-\mathcal{V}(y_{\bot}^{\prime})]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}}{2}xf_{1}^{\prime\gamma}\left(x,k_{\bot}^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{k_{\bot}^{\mu}k_{\bot}^{\nu}}{k_{\bot}^{2}}-\frac{\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}}{2}\right)xh_{1}^{\prime\bot\gamma}\left(x,k_{\bot}^{2}\right)

where
U^{\dagger}(0_{\bot})U(y_{\bot})
represents the transverse gauge link, effectively incorporating the Coulomb correction from the hard part into the photon TMDs
f_{1}^{\prime\gamma}\left(x,k_{\bot}^{2}\right)
and
xh_{1}^{\prime\bot\gamma}\left(x,k_{\bot}^{2}\right)
. The explicit expression for the gauge link is.

U(y_{\bot})=\mathcal{P}e^{ie\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz^{-}A^{+}(z^{-},y_{\bot})}

(6)

Since photons are neutral, the correlator does not require a gauge link for gauge invariance. The potential can be further expressed as,

\mathcal{V}(y_{\bot})\equiv e\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz^{-}A^{+}(z^{-},y_{\bot})=\frac{\alpha Z}{\pi}\int d^{2}q_{\bot}e^{-iy_{\bot}\cdot q_{\bot}}\frac{F(q_{\bot}^{2})}{q_{\bot}^{2}+\delta^{2}}

(7)

where
\delta
is the photon mass, used to regulate the infrared divergence, and
F
is the nuclear charge form factor. The explicit form of the field strength in the correlator is.

\mathcal{F}^{\mu}(x,y_{\bot})\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dy^{-}e^{ixP^{+}y^{-}}F_{+\bot}^{\mu}(y^{-},y_{\bot})=\frac{Ze}{4\pi^{2}}\int d^{2}q_{\bot}e^{-iy_{\bot}\cdot q_{\bot}}(iq_{\bot}^{\mu})\frac{F(q_{\bot}^{2}+x^{2}M_{p}^{2})}{q_{\bot}^{2}+x^{2}M_{p}^{2}}

(8)

The photon TMDs with final-state Coulomb corrections that we need can be obtained through orthogonality. We have.

xf_{1}^{\prime\gamma}\left(x,k_{\bot}^{2}\right)=\int\ \frac{d^{2}y_{\bot}d^{2}y_{\bot}^{\prime}}{4\pi^{3}}e^{ik_{\bot}\cdot\left(y_{\bot}-y_{\bot}^{\prime}\right)}\mathcal{F}^{\mu}\left(x,y_{\bot}\right)\mathcal{F}^{\ast\nu}\left(x,y_{\bot}^{\prime}\right)\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}e^{i\left[\mathcal{V}\left(y_{\bot}\right)-\mathcal{V}\left(y_{\bot}^{\prime}\right)\right]}

(9)

xh_{1}^{\prime\bot\gamma}\left(x,k_{\bot}^{2}\right)=\int\ \frac{d^{2}y_{\bot}d^{2}y_{\bot}^{\prime}}{4\pi^{3}}e^{ik_{\bot}\cdot\left(y_{\bot}-y_{\bot}^{\prime}\right)}\mathcal{F}^{\mu}\left(x,y_{\bot}\right)\mathcal{F}^{\ast\nu}\left(x,y_{\bot}^{\prime}\right)\left(\frac{2k_{\bot}^{\mu}k_{\bot}^{\nu}}{k_{\bot}^{2}}-\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}\right)e^{i\left[\mathcal{V}\left(y_{\bot}\right)-\mathcal{V}\left(y_{\bot}^{\prime}\right)\right]}

(10)

As shown in Eq.
1
, we find that the photons surrounding the atomic nucleus are linearly polarized. Here, not only does
f_{1}^{\gamma}(x,k_{\bot}^{2})
exist, but also
h_{1}^{\bot\gamma}(x,k_{\bot}^{2})
, which represents the distribution function of linearly polarized photons. When it acts as a background field, the photon propagator becomes more complicated, taking the following form,

\displaystyle\left\langle B\middle|\ A_{\mu}\left(x\right)A_{\nu}\left(y\right)\middle|\ B\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\int{\frac{2d^{4}k}{(2\pi)}e^{-ik\cdot\left(x-y\right)}\frac{-i}{k^{2}+i\varepsilon}}

(11)

\displaystyle\times[g_{\bot\mu\nu}{xf}_{1}^{\gamma}\left(k_{z},k_{\bot}\right)-(g_{\bot\mu\nu}+\frac{2k_{\bot\mu}k_{\bot\nu}}{k_{\bot}^{2}}){xh}_{1}^{\bot\gamma}(k_{z},k_{\bot})]

where
B
denotes the polarized photon background field, and
-g_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\bot}^{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\nu}+p^{\mu}n^{\nu}+p^{\nu}n^{\mu}
. Note that
\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^{3}}
is hidden within the TMDs
f_{1}^{\gamma}(k_{z},k_{\perp}^{2})
and
h_{1}^{\perp\gamma}(k_{z},k_{\perp}^{2})
. One has.

xf_{1}^{\gamma}\left(k_{z},k_{\bot}^{2}\right)=xh_{1}^{\bot\gamma}\left(k_{z},k_{\bot}^{2}\right)=\frac{Z^{2}\alpha_{e}}{\pi^{2}}k_{\bot}^{2}\left[\frac{F\left(k_{\bot}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}\right)}{\left(k_{\bot}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}\right)}\right]^{2}

(12)

When studying the motion of photons in the background field, the photon propagator in the polarized background field depends on both the polarized and unpolarized photon distribution functions in the field. This equation provides a reformulated description, within the framework of the parton model in field theory, of how the background field affects bremsstrahlung photons within it. Furthermore, it shows that in a linearly polarized background field, the bremsstrahlung photon beam is linearly polarized, and its spectrum corresponds to the photon distribution function in the background field.

III
Observables

We can investigate bremsstrahlung radiation in a polarized background field through the BH process at EIC and EicC.

e(k_{1})+\gamma(k_{2})\longrightarrow e(p_{1})+\gamma(p_{2})

(13)

In this process, an electron undergoes scattering with a heavy nucleus, absorbing a photon from the nuclear Coulomb field and subsequently emitting a real photon. Since the emitted photon originates from interactions with the external Coulomb field of the nucleus, the corresponding final-state photon propagator is modified according to the Eq.
11
. The azimuthal asymmetry of the final-state particles can serve as an experimental signature to verify the polarization characteristics of the emitted photon beam.

The resummation of all large logarithmic terms induced by final-state soft photon radiation results in the Sudakov factor, given by
e^{-\frac{\alpha_{e}}{2\pi}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{P_{\perp}^{2}}{\mu_{r}^{2}}\right)}
,where
\mu_{r}=2e^{-\gamma_{E}}/|r_{\perp}|

hao Sun et al. 2020

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dy_{\gamma}d^{2}P_{\bot}d^{2}q_{\bot}}=

\displaystyle\int\ \frac{d^{2}r_{\bot}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{ir_{\bot}\cdot q_{\bot}}e^{-\frac{\alpha_{e}}{2\pi}\ln^{2}\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu_{r}^{2}}}

(14)

\displaystyle\times\int\ d^{2}k_{\bot}e^{ir_{\bot}\cdot k_{\bot}}[H_{1}xf_{1}^{\gamma}\left(x,k_{2\bot}^{2}\right){xf}_{1}^{\gamma}\left(p_{2z},p_{2\bot}^{2}\right)+H_{2}{xh}_{1}^{\bot\gamma}\left(x,k_{2\bot}^{2}\right){xf}_{1}^{\gamma}\left(p_{2z},p_{2\bot}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+H_{3}{xf}_{1}^{\gamma}\left(x,k_{2\bot}^{2}\right)xh_{1}^{\bot\gamma}\left(p_{2z},p_{2\bot}^{2}\right)+H_{4}{xh}_{1}^{\bot\gamma}(x,k_{2\bot}^{2})xh_{1}^{\bot\gamma}(p_{2z},p_{2\bot}^{2})]

where,

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2z^{2}\left(3z^{2}-4z+2\right)}{p_{\bot}^{4}};

(15)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\left(z-1\right)z^{3}cos\left(2\phi\right)}{p_{\bot}^{4}};

\displaystyle H_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\left(z-1\right)z^{3}}{p_{\bot}^{4}};

\displaystyle=\frac{4\left(z-1\right)^{2}z^{2}cos{\left(2\phi\right)}}{p_{\bot}^{4}}

z

IV
Summary

This study investigates bremsstrahlung radiation in polarized electromagnetic fields and proposes its detection at the Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) and EicC. It demonstrates that the emitted photon beam inherits the polarization of the background field, making bremsstrahlung a potential probe for field structure. The Bethe-Heitler process is identified as a viable experimental approach, with azimuthal asymmetry in photon emission serving as a key observable.

The study incorporates two important corrections: Coulomb corrections, which account for multiple scatterings of the electron in the nuclear field, and Sudakov suppression, which arises from soft photon radiation and alters the radiation spectrum. Coulomb corrections slightly modify the photon distribution, while Sudakov suppression significantly reduces the asymmetry at low transverse momentum. Numerical estimates at EIC and EicC reveal that these effects must be carefully accounted for in experimental analyses. The findings confirm that bremsstrahlung polarization effects are measurable under specific kinematic conditions, offering new insights into high-energy collisions, nuclear structure studies, and astrophysical processes.

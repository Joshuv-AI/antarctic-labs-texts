Charm associated production of the pseudoscalar Higgs boson h ′′ h^{\prime\prime} in the MCPM’ at the LHC
R. Maciuła, M. Maniatis, O. Nachtmann, A. Szczurek

Abstract
We discuss charm associated production of the pseudoscalar Higgs boson h ′′ h^{\prime\prime} in the MCPM’. In our analysis we assume m h ′′ m_{h^{\prime\prime}} = 95.4 GeV, which corresponds to an enhancement observed by the CMS collaboration in the γ ​ γ \gamma\gamma channel. As discussed recently, the MCPM’ is consistent with the CMS enhancement. In this model the h ′′ h^{\prime\prime} Higgs boson of this mass decays dominantly into c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} jets. The cross section for the associated h ′′ h^{\prime\prime} production in p ​ p → h ′′ ​ c ​ c ¯ pp\to h^{\prime\prime}c\bar{c} is found to be almost as big as that for the Drell-Yan (DY) c ​ c ¯ → h ′′ c\bar{c}\to h^{\prime\prime} production, much larger than that for two-gluon fusion g ​ g → h ′′ gg\to h^{\prime\prime} . Since in the MCPM’ the dominant h ′′ h^{\prime\prime} decay is into the c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} channel we have to deal with the p ​ p → c ​ c ¯ ​ c ​ c ¯ pp\to c\bar{c}c\bar{c} process with c c and c ¯ \bar{c} jets. In addition to the signal processes, we have sizeable single parton scattering (SPS) and double parton scattering (DPS) nonresonant background. In our analysis we include diagrams of gluon-gluon fusion g ​ g → h ′′ gg\to h^{\prime\prime} which lead to the c ​ c ¯ ​ c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c}c\bar{c} final state. We discuss also the possible role of the reaction p ​ p → h ′′ ​ h ′′ pp\to h^{\prime\prime}h^{\prime\prime} . Assuming 100% c and c ¯ \bar{c} jet tagging we discuss in detail how to improve the signal-to-background ratio. Our analysis shows promising resonance-like enhancements and relatively large cross sections in the c ​ c ¯ ​ c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c}c\bar{c} channel within the MCPM’. The ALICE-3 project would be ideal in the future to perform the experimental studies corresponding to the theoretical studies discussed here.

Introduction

In this paper we are studying consequences of the model MCPM’, which was introduced in
26
. The MCPM’ is a two-Higgs-doublet model (2HDM) that, in our view, exhibits several noteworthy features when compared with experimental observations.
The basis of the MCPM’ is the maximally CP symmetric two-Higgs-doublet model, MCPM, which was introduced in
27

23

26

\tau

25

Therefore, in
26

The key idea there was to generate masses for fermions of the second and first family by effective interactions originating from integrating out high-mass particles having suitable couplings. In this way, masses for all fermions except neutrinos were generated. That is, in the MCPM’ we work with massless neutrinos which should be alright for the reactions which we want to study. In addition, electroweak symmetry breaking (EWSB) and a realistic, nontrivial, Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix are generated in the MCPM’.

3.1
Cuts

In the generation of the events we apply at
parton level the following cuts to all jets, that is, to the final state quarks:

\Delta R>0.4,\qquad|\eta|<5.0,\qquad p_{T}>20\penalty\ \text{GeV}\;.

(15)

Here,
\Delta R
is defined as

\Delta R=\sqrt{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}

(16)

with
\Delta\phi
and
\Delta\eta
the differences of the azimuthal angles
\phi
and pseudorapidities
\eta
of the members of a quark pair. The transverse momentum of a quark is denoted by
p_{T}
. The
p_{T}
cut in (
15
) eliminates events where jets are produced near the proton beams.
The
\Delta R
cut allows the experimentalists to select spatially separated jets in the detector. Furthermore, a Breit-Wigner factor is set defining on-shellness of the
h^{\prime\prime}
boson around its mass, which is chosen as the standard value of 15 times its width. We follow here the MadGraph documentation, giving these well established minimal cut values. Later we shall apply additional cuts to enhance the significance of the MCPM’ signal over the Standard Model background.

4
Conclusions

In the present paper we have worked out further consequences of the model MCPM’ for experiments at the LHC. The MCPM’ is a two-Higgs-doublet model (2HDM) which has been introduced in
26
. The Higgs sector of the MCPM’contains, as in any 2HDM, three neutral Higgs bosons (
\rho^{\prime},h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}
) and a charged pair (
H^{\pm}
).
The CP-symmetry relations forming the basis of the MCPM’ fix the couplings of these Higgs bosons to the fermions to a large extent. The
\rho^{\prime}
couples to the fermions like the SM Higgs particle. The bosons

h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
couple mainly to charm quarks in a scalar and pseudoscalar way, respectively. The
corresponding coupling constants are, however, proportional to the top-quark mass.

In
26
the MCPM’ was compared to the results for
\gamma\gamma
production from
30
where a possible enhancement at
M_{\gamma\gamma}=95.4
GeV was reported. Choosing the mass of the pseudoscalar boson
h^{\prime\prime}

of the MCPM’ at this value compatibility of the MCPM’ with the results of
30
was found.

This gave us the motivation to look for further experimental consequences at the LHC of a possible
h^{\prime\prime}

pseudoscalar Higgs boson with the properties dictated by the MCPM’. We have studied here in detail
the production of the
h^{\prime\prime}
associated with a charm-anticharm pair,

p+p\to h^{\prime\prime}+c+\bar{c}+X.

(25)

According to theory the
h^{\prime\prime}
boson of mass
95.4
GeV decays to nearly 100% to
h^{\prime\prime}\to c+\bar{c}
. Thus the final state at the LHC is

p+p\to c+\bar{c}+c+\bar{c}+X.

(26)

Of course, this final state can also be populated by SM processes.

Our main findings are as follows. The total cross section for (
25
) is large, of the order of
12\penalty\ \text{nb}
(see (
14
)). This has to be compared
to the Drell-Yan cross section (see (
35
))

\left.\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}(p+p\to h^{\prime\prime}+X)\right|_{\text{DY}}\approx 15\penalty\ \text{nb.}

(27)

We assumed that the
c
and
\bar{c}
jets can be tagged. We introduced suitable cuts which
should make measurement of the charm and anticharm jets in the reaction (
26
) possible. A main requirement was to consider the leading charm and anticharm jets, that is,
those with the largest
p_{T}
. The probability of these jets to come from the
h^{\prime\prime}
decay is high. With suitable cuts we found that the distribution in the invariant mass of the leading
c
and
\bar{c}
jets,
M_{c\bar{c}}
, shows a resonance-like structure at the
h^{\prime\prime}
mass.
With very stringent cuts we get with the SPS+DPS calculation from Fig.
17(b)

a signal cross section at the peak of the resonance of

\left.\frac{d\sigma}{dM_{c\bar{c}}}\right|_{\text{MCPM'-SM}}\approx 2.4\penalty\ \text{pb/GeV}

(28)

over a background from the SM of

\left.\frac{d\sigma}{dM_{c\bar{c}}}\right|_{\text{SM}}\approx 3.8\penalty\ \text{pb/GeV}.

(29)

We have taken into account contributions driven by the effective
h^{\prime\prime}GG
coupling. However, the corresponding
cross sections have been found to be much smaller than those obtained within the standard tree-level diagrams.
We have also discussed the reaction
p+p\to h^{\prime\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}+X
with both
h^{\prime\prime}
decaying to
c+\bar{c}
. However, the corresponding cross section is found to be negligible.

To summarize: we have carefully analyzed the cross section for the
p+p\to c+\bar{c}+c+\bar{c}+X
reaction.
In addition to the signal process
p+p\to(h^{\prime\prime}\to c+\bar{c})+c+\bar{c}+X
, there are sizeable nonresonant backgrounds, corresponding to the single-parton scattering and double-parton scattering mechanisms giving the same
c+\bar{c}+c+\bar{c}+X
final state.
In our paper we have assumed 100% charm tagging.
We have calculated the distributions of the invariant mass of the leading
c\bar{c}
quark pair in the MCPM’ applying appropriate kinematical cuts.
Our analysis has shown relatively large cross sections and promising resonance-like enhancements within the MCPM’ with respect to the SM results. We hope that our present study will be helpful to verify or reject the hypothesis that the
enhancement observed by the CMS collaboration at
M_{\gamma\gamma}
= 95.4 GeV is due to the
h^{\prime\prime}
boson of the MCPM’.

On the experimental side the identification of
c
and
\bar{c}
jets is improving (see
3
;
36
;
31
).
For example, the LHCb collaboration has measured the cross section for
c\bar{c}
dijets
5
(see also
21
).
The CMS and ATLAS would need to perform their own analyses including
jet missidentification probabilities.
In our opinion the ALICE-3 project
20
would be ideal
in the future to perform experimental studies corresponding to our
theoretical studies discussed here.

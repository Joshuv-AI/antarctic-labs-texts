Preliminaries

All graphs
G=(V,E)
in this paper are directed and have (possibly negative) real edge weights. If a vertex
u\in V
has negative outgoing edges, we say
u
is a
negative vertex
, and let
N
denote the set of negative vertices. For a vertex
u\in V
, we define
N^{out}(u)=\{v\in V:(u,v)\in E\}
and
N^{in}(u)=\{v\in V:(v,u)\in E\}
as the out-neighbors and in-neighbors of
u
. Finally, we let the distance between a set and a point be the minimum distance, i.e.,
d(S,v)=\min_{s\in S}d(s,v)
.

Like prior work, we heavily rely on a subroutine for efficiently computing single-source shortest paths with few negative edges. For a positive integer
h
called the
hop bound
, define the
h
-negative hop distance

d^{h}(s,t)
as the minimum weight
(s,t)
-path with at most
h
negative edges. Given a source vertex
s
, we can compute
d^{h}(s,t)
for all other
t\in V
in
O(h(m+n\log n))
using a hybrid of Dijkstra and Bellman-Ford
[
3
]
.

Also like prior work, our algorithm makes use of
potential functions

\phi:V\to\mathbb{R}
that reweight the graph, where each edge
(u,v)
has new weight
w_{\phi}(u,v)=w(u,v)+\phi(u)-\phi(v)
. The key property of potential functions is that for any vertices
s,t\in V
, all
(s,t)
-paths have their weight shifted by the same additive
\phi(s)-\phi(t)
. A potential
\phi
is
valid
if
w_{\phi}(u,v)\geq 0
for all edges
(u,v)
with
w(u,v)\geq 0
, i.e., it does not introduce any new negative edges.

2.1
Copies of Vertices

In our algorithm, we will build graphs
G_{\tau}
from the original input graph
G=(V,E)
, by adding vertices and edges. Vertices in
G_{\tau}
are classified into two categories: a base vertex and a copy of some base vertex. The set of base vertices
V_{0}
is initialized as
V_{0}=V
and the set of copy vertices is initially empty. Throughout the algorithm, we will add new base vertices and copies of base vertices. Suppose we have
c
copies of each base vertex
v\in V_{0}
. The original vertex is denoted
v_{0}
and the copies are labeled
v_{i}
for
i\in[c]
. We maintain the following invariants:

(I1)

each copy
v_{i}
is associated with a shift
\delta(v_{i})\in\mathbb{R}
, with
\delta(v_{0})=0
for all
v\in V_{0}
.

(I2)

every edge
(u_{i},v_{j})\in G_{\tau}
satisfies
\omega(u_{i},v_{j})\geq d_{G_{\tau}}(u_{0},v_{0})+\delta(v_{j})-\delta(u_{i})

These invariants formalize what it means for an added vertex to be a copy of a base vertex. The shift
\delta(v_{i})
represents how much “higher” the copy
v_{i}
is relative to the base vertex
v_{0}
, where distances to higher vertices are larger. This is formalized in the constraints on the edge weights in
(I2)
, which also guarantees that shortest path distances aren’t decreased by adding copies.

When we add a base vertex to the graph, we will prove that invariants are maintained. When we add a copy of base vertices, we will define a shift and apply the following claim:

Claim 2
.

Consider graph
G_{\tau}
satisfying
(I1)
–
(I2)
. For any base vertex
v\in V_{0}
, suppose one adds a new copy
v_{new}
of
v
with shift
\delta(v_{new})\in\mathbb{R}
to
G_{\tau}
, along with the following edges:

•

For each
u\in V
, let
C(u,v)=\{v_{i}:\omega(u,v_{i})+\delta(v_{new})-\delta(v_{i})\geq 0\}
. If
C(u,v)\neq\emptyset
, add edge
(u,v_{new})
of weight
\omega(u,v_{new})=\min_{v_{i}\in C(u,v)}\omega(u,v_{i})+\delta(v_{new})-\delta(v_{i})
.

•

For each
w\in V
, let
C(v,w)=\{v_{i}:\omega(v_{i},w)+\delta(v_{i})-\delta(v_{new})\geq 0\}
. If
C(v,w)\neq\emptyset
, add edge
(v_{new},w)
of weight
\omega(v_{new},w)=\min_{v_{i}\in C(v,w)}\omega(v_{i},w)+\delta(v_{i})-\delta(v_{new})
.

Then invariants
(I1)
and
(I2)
are still satisfied in the resulting graph.

Proof.

Invariant
(I1)
is still satisfied since the new vertex
v_{new}
has an associated shift
\delta(v_{new})
. Next, we need to verify Invariant
(I2)
for edges incident to
v_{new}
. For an in-edge
(u_{i},v_{new})
, the weight is
\omega(u_{i},v_{new})=\omega(u_{i},v_{j})+\delta(v_{new})-\delta(v_{j})
for some
v_{j}\in C(u_{i},v)
by definition in the claim statement. But by Invariant
(I2)
, we know
w(u_{i},v_{j})\geq d_{G_{\tau}}(u_{0},v_{0})+\delta(v_{j})-\delta(u_{i})
. Thus, we have
\omega(u_{i},v_{new})\geq d_{G_{\tau}}(u_{0},v_{0})+\delta(v_{new})-\delta(u_{i})
, as required by Invariant
(I2)
. The proof for out-edges
(v_{new},w_{j})
is symmetric.
∎

2.2
Preprocessing: Unique Incident Negative Edges

We will preprocess the graph using the following routine, so that each vertex

Lemma 3
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
with
k
negative vertices, there is a linear-time algorithm that outputs a graph

with
V^{\prime}\supseteq V
such that

1.

For any

and hop bound
h
, we have
d_{G^{\prime}}^{h}(s,t)=d_{G}^{h}(s,t)
,

2.

Each vertex
u
has at most one negative edge incident to
u
,

3.

G^{\prime}
also has
k
negative vertices, and
|V^{\prime}|=|V|+k
.

Proof.

For each negative vertex
u
, consider its outgoing edges
(u,v_{1}),\ldots,(u,v_{\ell})
, and suppose that
(u,v_{1})
is the edge of smallest weight, with ties broken arbitrarily. Create a new vertex
u^{\prime}
, add the edge
(u,u^{\prime})
of weight
(u,v_{1})
, and replace each edge
(u,v_{i})
by the edge
(u^{\prime},v_{i})
of weight

. It is straightforward to verify all of the required conditions.
∎

During an iteration of our algorithm, we will compute a valid reweighting
\phi_{\tau}
, which may cause a negative edge in
G_{\tau}
to become non-negative. However, even if a negative edge in
G_{\tau}
is non-negative after the reweighting
\phi_{\tau}
, we will still call this edge negative through the rest of the iteration and count it towards hops in
h
-hop shortest paths. Following prior work, we refer to this as
freezing
the set of negative edges before applying a reweighting.

In an iteration, we also add
imaginary
negative edges to the graph. The heads of imaginary negative edges will not be considered a negative vertex, and will not be included in
N
, but will still be counted towards hops in
h
-hop shortest paths. This distinction is important when we apply betweenness reduction: hop-distance will be measured with respect to all negative edges but betweenness is only measured with respect to real ones (via the set
N
).

Lemma 5

In the resulting graph with low betweenness, they then compute forward and backward Dijkstra searches from each negative edge. They show that these searches can be implemented simultaneously for all negative vertices efficiently in a graph with low betweenness.

4
Shortcutting Algorithm

As outlined in the technical overview, our algorithm is an iterative shortcutting algorithm, which proceeds over
L=\Theta(\log{k})
iterations. In each iteration, we add some nodes and edges to the graph in order to decrease the number of negative edges on shortest paths. Let
G_{\tau}=(V_{\tau},E_{\tau})
denote the graph after
{\tau}
iterations of shortcutting, where
G_{0}=(V_{0},E_{0})
is the original graph. Though this graph may have a larger vertex set
V_{\tau}\supseteq V_{0}
, all additional vertices are
copies
of some (non-negative) vertex in
V_{0}
, as formalized in
Section

2.1
. We prove the following:

Theorem 9
.

Let
G_{0}=(V_{0},E_{0})
be the original (directed, weighted) graph and let
G_{\tau}=(V_{\tau},E_{\tau})
be a (directed, weighted) graph such that each vertex is a copy of some vertex in
G_{0}
and there are at most
c=O(\log^{2}{n})
total copies of each vertex. There is a randomized algorithm that outputs a supergraph
G_{\tau+1}=(V_{\tau+1},E_{\tau+1})
with
V_{\tau+1}\supseteq V_{\tau}
and a potential
\phi_{\tau}
on
V_{\tau}
such that

1.

For any
s,t\in V_{\tau}
, we have
d_{G_{\tau+1}}(s,t)=d_{G_{\tau}}(s,t)+\phi_{\tau}(s)-\phi_{\tau}(t)
,

2.

For any
s,t\in V
and hop bound
h
, we have
d_{G_{\tau+1}}^{h-\lfloor h/3\rfloor}(s,t)\leq d_{G_{\tau}}^{h}(s,t)+\phi_{\tau}(s)-\phi_{\tau}(t)
,

3.

V_{\tau+1}
has at most
O(\log{n})
additional copies of each vertex, so
|V_{\tau+1}|\leq|V_{\tau}|+O(n\log{n})
,

4.

There are no new negative vertices.

The algorithm uses one call to negative weight shortest paths on
O(|V_{\tau}|)
nodes and
O(k/2^{\sqrt{\log{n}}})
negative vertices, and takes
n^{2+o(1)}
additional time.

Before we prove
Theorem

9
, we first show how to apply it to prove
Theorem

1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1
.

If
k\leq 2^{\sqrt{\log{n}}}
at any point, then naively computing
k
-hop shortest paths suffices since the running time
O(kn^{2})
meets the desired bound. Otherwise, each time we apply
Theorem

9
, the number of negative hops along shortest paths drops by a constant factor. By applying the shortcutting procedure
O(\log{k})\leq O(\log{n})
times, we guarantee that all shortest paths have at most 2 negative edges. On the resulting graph, we can run
2
-hop shortest paths to compute the desired single-source distances in the final graph. Using the reweightings
\phi_{\tau}
and property (1), we can then recover the shortest paths in the original graph.

By property (3), each iteration increases the number of vertices by
O(n\log{n})
, so all graphs have at most
O(n\log^{2}{n})
vertices. Thus, the runtime is dominated by
O(\log{k})
recursive calls to single-source shortest paths, and
n^{2+o(1)}
additional time, giving the following recursion:

\displaystyle T(n,k)\leq O(\log{n})\cdot T\left(O(n\log^{2}{n}),k/2^{\sqrt{\log{n}}}\right)+n^{2+o(1)}.

There are at most
\sqrt{\log{n}}
levels of recursion since the number of negative vertices drops by
2^{\sqrt{\log{n}}}
in each recursive call. On recursion level
i\geq 1
, there are
O(\log^{i}n)
recursive instances, each with at most
O(n\log^{2i}{n})
vertices. Since
i\leq\sqrt{\log{n}}
, the total size of all instances is at most
n^{1+o(1)}
so the total runtime is still
n^{2+o(1)}
.
∎

We now give our algorithm for
Theorem

9
. The high level approach is the same as in
[
8
]
. First, we apply a reweighting to the graph to reduce the betweenness for every pair of vertices. In the reweighted graph, we compute forward and backward searches from each negative vertex such that every vertex in the backward search can negatively reach every vertex in the forward search using few hops. Finally, we add shortcut edges between the backward and forward searches for each negative vertex to decrease the number of hops needed in a shortest path by a constant factor. To implement this strategy more efficiently than
[
8
]
, we need to strengthen each of the steps, as we see next.

4.1
Stronger Betweenness Reduction

In this section, we define a stronger notion of betweenness and show how to compute a reweighting which reduces strong betweenness. Informally, the weak betweenness of
s,t\in V
is the number of vertices
v
such that there is an
2h
-hop negative path from
s
to
t
through
v
(actually, it requires
d^{h}(s,v)+d^{h}(v,t)<0
). Strong betweenness relaxes the condition, allowing the negative path to start and end at arbitrary vertices
s^{\prime},t^{\prime}
, as long as the path goes through
s
and
t
.

Definition 10
.

For a pair
(s,t)\in V\times V
and a subset
N\subseteq V
, its
h
-hop strong betweenness, denoted
\mathrm{SBW}_{N}(s,t)
, is the number of vertices
v\in N
for which there exist
s^{\prime},t^{\prime}\in V
satisfying

\displaystyle d^{h}(s^{\prime},s)+d^{h}(s,v)+d^{h}(v,t)+d^{h}(t,t^{\prime})<0.

We remark that strong betweenness is defined with respect to a set of vertices
N\subseteq V
. In our paper, this will always be the set of negative vertices
N
in the original graph, hence the notation, so the dependence
N
is often left implicit. We emphasize that
N
need not contain the endpoint of every negative edge in the graph. Indeed, we will be applying betweenness reduction on a graph with additional negative edges, but with the original set of negative vertices
N
.

We adapt the algorithm for
Lemma

5
given in
[
8
]
for strong betweenness reduction.

Lemma 11
.

Consider a graph
G=(V,E)
with a set of distinguished vertices
N\subseteq V
, called negative vertices. For any parameters
h\geq 1
and
b\geq 1
, there is a randomized algorithm which returns either a set of valid potentials
\phi
or a negative cycle, such that with high probability, all pairs

O(mh)
edges,
O(nh)
vertices, and
O(b\log{n})

Proof.

We construct a directed graph

4h+1
copies of

denotes the graph

for

for

for

v_{i}^{\textrm{backward}}
for

We now define the edges between copies of

. Let

be larger than the absolute value of all edge weights. For each

•

An edge
(u_{0},u^{\textrm{forward}}_{{1}})
and edges

•

An edge
(u^{\textrm{backward}}_{{1}},u_{0})
and edges

for each

•

An edge
(u^{\textrm{backward}}_{{1}},v_{0})

Finally, sample a random set

with

. We compute

via a call to negative-weight single-source shortest path. It is easy to see that

.

Subclaim 12
.

If we find a negative cycle in

, there is a negative cycle in
G
.

Proof.

Map each edge

in the negative cycle to edge

to obtain a closed walk in

. We will show that this closed walk has negative weight, so it is a negative cycle in
G
.
First, observe that the negative cycle must traverse the copies of
G^{+}
in the following order:

G_{0}\to G^{\textrm{forward}}_{{1}}\to\ldots\to G^{\textrm{forward}}_{{2h}}\to G^{\textrm{backward}}_{{2h}}\to\ldots\to G^{\textrm{backward}}_{{1}}\to G_{0}.

For each such loop traversed, there are
4h
edges with additional
+M
weight and one edge, from

to
G^{\textrm{backward}}_{{2h}}
, with weight
-4hM
. Thus, the additional weights in

has the same (negative) weight as the negative cycle in

∎

If we don’t find a negative cycle in

\phi^{\prime}(v)=d_{H}(V,v)
for each

. Define

for each

. We now show that

achieves the strong betweenness reduction guarantee with high probability. We start with the following subclaim.

Subclaim 13
.

We have

Proof.

First, we observe that for all

, we have

\displaystyle\phi(v)-\phi(u)\leq\min_{w\in S}d^{2h}(u,w)+d^{2h}(w,v).

(1)

Indeed, for any vertex

, the path in

can be routed in

from

to

, traversing the edge
(w^{\textrm{forward}}_{{2h}},w^{\textrm{backward}}_{{2h}})
, and then routing the

from

. Thus, there is a path from

to

of weight

is a distance function, any path from

has weight at least
\phi(v_{0})-\phi(u_{0})

Equation

1
.

For any

and

1
implies that

\displaystyle\phi(x)-\phi(s^{\prime})\leq\min_{w\in S}d^{2h}(s^{\prime},w)+d^{2h}(w,x)\leq d^{2h}(s^{\prime},x)+d^{2h}(x,x)\leq d^{2h}(s^{\prime},x).

Similarly, for any
x\in S

u=x
and
v=t^{\prime}
in
Equation

\displaystyle\phi(t^{\prime})-\phi(x)\leq\min_{w\in S}d^{2h}(x,w)+d^{2h}(w,t^{\prime})\leq d^{2h}(x,x)+d^{2h}(x,t^{\prime})\leq d^{2h}(x,t^{\prime}).

∎

Using the subclaim, we establish the strong betweenness reduction guarantee. Order the vertices by value
d^{h}(s,v)+d^{h}(v,t)
and consider the sampled (negative) vertex
x\in S
with the minimum such value. Since
d^{h}_{\phi}(s,v)+d^{h}_{\phi}(v,t)
has the same relative ordering, there are at most

negative vertices

with

For some negative vertex

, suppose

. Then for any

, we have

and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq d_{\phi}^{2h}(s^{\prime},x)+d_{\phi}^{2h}(x,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq d^{h}_{\phi}(s^{\prime},s)+d_{\phi}^{h}(s,x)+d_{\phi}^{h}(x,t)+d^{h}_{\phi}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq d^{h}_{\phi}(s^{\prime},s)+d_{\phi}^{h}(s,v)+d_{\phi}^{h}(v,t)+d^{h}_{\phi}(t,t^{\prime}).

Thus, negative vertices

counted in

must have

∎

4.2
Forward and Backward Searches

This section presents a multi-hop generalization of

Lemma 14

Given a hop bound

We now show that all edges are non-negative after the reweighting. For any non-negative edge

\displaystyle d_{H_{out}}(r_{0},v_{i})

(2)

\displaystyle d_{H_{in}}(r_{0},v_{i})

Lemma 15

4.3
The Shortcutting Algorithm

With these tools in hand, we present our algorithm to shortcut
G_{\tau}
by a constant factor. (Recall that
G_{\tau}
is the graph after
{\tau}
iterations of shortcutting.) The first step is to reduce the betweenness of the graph. For technical reasons, we don’t apply betweenness reduction (
Lemma

11
) directly to
G_{\tau}
. Define
H_{\tau}
by adding an edge between every pair of copies of the same vertex, based on how much the copies are shifted. Formally, for every pair of copies
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
of
v
, we add an edge

(v_{i},v_{j})
of weight
\delta(v_{j})-\delta(v_{i})
. This creates more (imaginary) negative edges. Compute reweighting
\phi_{\tau}
by applying the strong betweenness reduction (
Lemma

11
) on graph
H_{\tau}
with parameters
h=2
,
b=k/2^{\sqrt{\log{n}}}
, and
N=N
is the set of negative vertices.

Let
H
and
\Gamma
denote the graphs
H_{\tau}
and
G_{\tau}
, respectively, after applying the reweighting
\phi_{\tau}
. Since
G_{\tau}
is an edge-subgraph of
H_{\tau}
, we know that
\phi_{\tau}
is a valid reweighting for
G_{\tau}
as well. In the remainder of the section, we will assume distances are measured in
\Gamma
unless otherwise specified (i.e., we let
\omega(u,v)=\omega_{\Gamma}(u,v)
and
d^{h}(s,t)=d^{h}_{\Gamma}(s,t)
). After reweighting,
\delta(v_{i})
should be adjusted accordingly for each copy
v_{i}
of each
v_{0}\in V_{0}
:

\delta_{H}(v_{i})=\delta_{\Gamma}(v_{i})=\delta(v_{i})-\phi_{\tau}(v_{i})+\phi_{\tau}(v_{0}).

It is easy to see that the new shifts satisfy the invariants for
\Gamma
and
H
:

\displaystyle\omega_{\Gamma}(u_{i},v_{j})

\displaystyle=\omega(u_{i},v_{j})+\phi_{\tau}(u_{i})-\phi_{\tau}(v_{j})

\displaystyle\geq d_{G_{\tau}}(u_{0},v_{0})+\delta(v_{j})-\delta(u_{i})+\phi_{\tau}(u_{i})-\phi_{\tau}(v_{j})

\displaystyle=d_{\Gamma}(u_{0},v_{0})-\phi_{\tau}(u_{0})+\phi_{\tau}(v_{0})+\delta(v_{j})-\delta(u_{i})+\phi_{\tau}(u_{i})-\phi_{\tau}(v_{j})

\displaystyle=d_{\Gamma}(u_{0},v_{0})+\delta_{\Gamma}(v_{j})-\delta_{\Gamma}(u_{i})

For simplicity, we will continue to use
\delta(v_{i})
to denote the shifts after the reweighting.

The second step is to compute the forward and backward searches for each
r\in N
with parameter
h=1
via
Lemma

14
on the graph
H
with reduced betweenness. From this, we obtain some
\Delta_{r}
for each
r\in N
, which we use to define
V^{out}_{r}
and
V^{in}_{r}
:

\displaystyle V^{out}_{r}

\displaystyle=\{v\in V:d^{1}(r,v)\leq-\Delta_{r}\}

\displaystyle V^{in}_{r}

\displaystyle=\{v\in V:d^{0}(v,r)\leq\Delta_{r}\},

where the inequality in the definition is strict based on which of (
1
) and (
3
) is satisfied with strict inequality. Observe that
d^{1}(r,v)\geq d^{2}_{H}(r,v)
and
d^{0}(v,r)\geq d^{1}_{H}(v,r)
, so
V^{out}_{r}\subseteq\tilde{V}_{r}^{out}
and
V^{in}_{r}\subseteq\tilde{V}_{r}^{in}
. In particular,
Lemma

15
also applies to
|V^{in}_{r}|
and
|V^{out}_{r}|
.

Finally, we need to define
G_{\tau+1}
. We first define an auxiliary graph
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
with additional (possibly imaginary) negative edges, which is useful for defining
G_{\tau+1}
. Initialize
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
as
\Gamma
.

(S1)

For each negative vertex
r\in N
, create a new base vertex
\tilde{r}
, called a Steiner vertex.

(S2)

For each
r\in N
and
v\in V^{out}_{r}\cup\{r^{\prime}\}
, we add an edge
(\tilde{r},v)
with weight
d^{1}(r,v)+\Delta_{r}
.

(S3)

For each
r\in N
and
v\in V^{in}_{r}\cup\{r\}
, we add an edge
(v,\tilde{r})
with weight
d^{0}(v,r)-\Delta_{r}
.

(S4)

For each negative vertex
r\in N
and each
v\in V^{out}_{r}
, if
v
is an endpoint of a negative edge
(v,v^{\prime})
, add the edge
(r,v^{\prime})
to
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
with weight
d^{1}(r,v)+\omega(v,v^{\prime})
.

(S5)

For each negative vertex
r\in N
and each
u^{\prime}\in V^{in}_{r}
, if
u^{\prime}
is the endpoint of a negative edge
(u,u^{\prime})
, add the edge
(u,r^{\prime})
to

with weight
\omega(u,u^{\prime})+d^{0}(u^{\prime},r)+\omega(r,r^{\prime})
.

Note that

can be constructed in
O(n^{2})
time easily since we preprocessed using
Lemma

3
.
We will call a negative edge incident to a Steiner vertex in

an imaginary negative edge. Similarly to
Section

3
, we want to construct
G_{\tau+1}
such that two-edge non-negative paths in
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
containing imaginary negative edges are simulated by some path in
G_{\tau+1}
with only non-negative edges. First, we recall the definition of locally-negative paths:

Definition 16
.

We say that edges
(u,v)
and
(v,w)
form a “locally-negative two-edge path” if
(1) either
(u,v)
or
(v,w)
is an imaginary negative edge and (2)
\omega_{G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}}(u,v)+\omega_{G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}}(v,w)\geq 0
.

For brevity, we will refer to these paths as
locally-negative paths
, and leave implicit that the paths have two edges. We say that a locally-negative path from
u
to
w
in
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
is

if there is a path from
u
to
w
in
G_{\tau+1}
of the same weight, but consisting of only non-negative edges. Our goal is to construct
G_{\tau+1}
by adding edges and additional copies of vertices, so that all locally-negative paths in
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
are shortcut. We first show that shortcutting these paths suffices to decrease the number of hops needed for any shortest path in
G_{\tau}
by a constant factor. The proof is very similar to that of
[
8
]
, which we sketched in
Section

3
.

Lemma 17
.

Consider any
s,t\in V
and any shortest
(s,t)
-path
P
in
\Gamma
with
h
negative edges. If
G_{\tau+1}
has shortcut edges
(S4)
and
(S5)
, and shortcuts every locally-negative path in

, then there is an
(s,t)
-path in
G_{\tau+1}
with weight
d_{\Gamma}(s,t)
and with at most
h-\lfloor h/3\rfloor
negative edges.

Proof.

Consider the negative edges of
P
in order from
s
to
t
, and create
\lfloor h/3\rfloor
disjoint groups of three consecutive negative edges. Let
(s,s^{\prime})
,
(r,r^{\prime})
, and
(t,t^{\prime})
be such a group of three consecutive negative edges. We claim there is an
s\leadsto t^{\prime}
path in
G_{\tau+1}
with the same weight as the
s\leadsto t^{\prime}
path in
\Gamma
. Combining the shortcut paths of each group will imply the desired result. Our proof will be split into three cases based on
\Delta_{r}
. The first two cases will have essentially the same proof as in
[
8
]
; the third case is the more difficult one.

1.

d^{0}(s^{\prime},r)<\Delta_{r}
. We have that
s^{\prime}\in V^{in}_{r}
so we add an edge
(s,r^{\prime})
with weight
\omega(s,s^{\prime})+d^{0}(s^{\prime},r)+\omega(r,r^{\prime})
, which is at most the weight of the
s\leadsto r^{\prime}
segment of
P
since the negative edges
(s,s^{\prime})
and
(r,r^{\prime})
are consecutive along
P
. Therefore, we can shortcut the
s\leadsto r^{\prime}
segment of
P
, which has two negative edges, using the single (negative) edge
(s,r^{\prime})
.

2.

d^{1}(r,t)<-\Delta_{r}
. We have
t\in V^{out}_{r}
so we add an edge
(r,t^{\prime})
of weight
d^{1}(r,t)+\omega(t,t^{\prime})
, which is at most the weight of the
r\leadsto t^{\prime}
segment of
P
since the negative edges
(r,r^{\prime})
and
(t,t^{\prime})
are consecutive along
P
. Therefore, we can shortcut the
r\leadsto t^{\prime}
segment of
P
, which has two negative edges, using the single (negative) edge
(r,t^{\prime})
.

3.

d^{0}(s^{\prime},r)\geq\Delta_{r}
and
d^{1}(r,t)\geq-\Delta_{r}
. Consider the
s^{\prime}\leadsto r
segment of
P
and let
u
be the last vertex on this segment with
d^{0}(u,r)\geq\Delta_{r}
. Then either
u=r
or the vertex
u^{\prime}
after

is in
V^{in}_{r}
. In the first case, there is an edge
(r,\tilde{r})
added of weight

, since the only way the first case occurs is when
\Delta_{r}\leq 0
. In the second case, there is an imaginary negative edge
(u^{\prime},\tilde{r})
with weight
d^{0}(u^{\prime},r)-\Delta_{r}<0
added to
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
, so the

path has total weight

\omega(u,u^{\prime})+\omega(u^{\prime},\tilde{r})=\omega(u,u^{\prime})+d^{0}(u^{\prime},r)-\Delta_{r}=d^{0}(u,r)-\Delta_{r}\geq 0,

where the second equality holds because there are no negative edges between
u
and
r
in the shortest
(s,t)
-path

.
In particular,
u\to u^{\prime}\to\tilde{r}
is a locally-negative path in
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
, so there is a path between
u
and
\tilde{r}
of non-negative edges in
G_{\tau+1}
of the same weight.

Similarly, consider the

segment of
P
and let

be the first vertex on this segment with
d^{1}(r,v^{\prime})\geq-\Delta_{r}
. Then either
v^{\prime}=r^{\prime}
or the vertex
v
before
v^{\prime}
is in
V^{out}_{r}
. In the first case, there is an edge
(\tilde{r},r^{\prime})
added of weight
\omega(r,r^{\prime})+\Delta_{r}\geq 0
, since the only way the first case occurs is when
\Delta_{r}\geq-\omega(r,r^{\prime})
. In the second case, there is an imaginary negative edge
(\tilde{r},v)
added of weight
d^{1}(r,v)+\Delta_{r}<0
, so the
\tilde{r}\to v\to v^{\prime}
path has total weight

\omega(\tilde{r},v)+\omega(v,v^{\prime})=d^{1}(r,v)+\omega(v,v^{\prime})+\Delta_{r}=d^{1}(r,v^{\prime})+\Delta_{r}\geq 0.

In particular,
\tilde{r}\to v\to v^{\prime}
forms a locally-negative path in
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
, so there is a path between
\tilde{r}
and
v^{\prime}
of non-negative edges in
G_{\tau+1}
of the same weight.

We wish to show that the concatenation of the paths
u\leadsto\tilde{r}
and
\tilde{r}\leadsto v^{\prime}
in the previous paragraphs yields a path
u\leadsto\tilde{r}\leadsto v^{\prime}
of non-negative edges in
G_{\tau+1}
of total weight equal to the
u\leadsto r\leadsto v^{\prime}
segment of

. The
u\leadsto\tilde{r}
path has total weight
d^{0}(u,r)-\Delta_{r}
, and the
\tilde{r}\leadsto v^{\prime}
path has total weight
d^{1}(r,v^{\prime})+\Delta_{r}
, so the concatenated path has total weight equal to the
u\leadsto r\leadsto v^{\prime}
segment of
P
.

Concatenating together each of the 2-hop paths in
G_{\tau+1}
which shortcut each of the
\lfloor h/3\rfloor
disjoint 3-hop paths in
G_{\tau}
proves the claim.
∎

The remainder of the section presents the last step of our shortcutting algorithm, which constructs

satisfying the properties assumed in

4.3.1

This subsection will be dedicated to proving the following lemma.

.

There is an

satisfying:

,

and

Proof.

Initialize

as the graph

and

Step 1.

Let

. For each negative vertex

of

of

with weight

(\tilde{r},v_{i})
of

Now, we analyze the runtime of this step. For each in-edge

and the number of edges of the form

We analyze what locally-negative paths are shortcut by Step 2.

For any such path

\displaystyle(j,r^{\prime})=\arg\min_{j\in[c],r\not\in N^{*}:v_{j}\in V^{in}_{r}}\omega(v_{j},\tilde{r})+\delta(v_{j}).

4.3.2
The
n^{2+o(1)}
Algorithm

In this subsection, we refine the ideas in
Lemma

18
to give an
n^{2+o(1)}
algorithm. The remainder of the subsection will be dedicated to proving the following lemma.

Lemma 21
.

There is an
n^{2+o(1)}
time algorithm which constructs a graph
G_{\tau+1}
satisfying:

1.

shortest path distances are preserved:
d_{G_{\tau+1}}(s,t)=d_{\Gamma}(s,t),

2.

every locally-negative two-edge path in
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
is shortcut in
G_{\tau+1}
,

3.

G_{\tau+1}
has at most
O(\log{n})
more copies of each original vertex,

4.

G_{\tau+1}
has shortcut edges
(S4)
and
(S5)
.

First, we give some intuition. For negative vertices
r\in N
such that
|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|\leq\kappa
,
Lemma

18
shows that we can add two copies of each original vertex and
O((|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|)\cdot\kappa)
edges to shortcut all locally-negative paths from these negative vertices. For the remaining negative vertices
r
, we have
|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|>\kappa
, so we use the trivial bound of
|\tilde{V}_{r}^{in}|+|\tilde{V}_{r}^{out}|\leq n
. For such
r\in N
, we add
O(n(|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|))
edges, and we show that the total number of edges added for these vertices is not too large since
\sum_{r}(|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|)^{2}=O(kn^{2}/b)
by
Lemma

15
. Therefore, the running time for both parts is bounded.

Our improved construction partitions the negative vertices using
O(\log{n})
different scales
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{O(\log{n})}
, where
\kappa_{1}=O(|G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}|)
and
\kappa_{i}/\kappa_{i+1}>1
is a constant. For each
\kappa_{i}
, we define

N_{i}=\{r\in N:|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|\geq\kappa_{i}\}.

Note that
N_{1}
is empty and
N_{O(\log{n})}
contains all the negative vertices. In the
i^{th}
round, we assume inductively that all locally-negative paths to and from negative vertices
r\in N_{i}
have already been shortcut. Our goal is to shortcut all locally-negative paths to and from negative vertices
r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}
, so we can proceed to the next round. Using the construction in
Lemma

18
, we can shortcut all locally-negative paths to and from
r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}
by adding two copies of each vertex and
O(\kappa_{i}(|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|))
edges. Since
r\in N_{i+1}
and
\kappa_{i+1}/\kappa_{i}=\Theta(1)
, we can bound
\kappa_{i}\leq O(|\tilde{V}_{r}^{in}|+|\tilde{V}_{r}^{out}|)
, so the number of edges added (and runtime) is
O((|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|)^{2})
for each negative vertex. By
Lemma

15
and our choice of
b
, we can bound the total number of edges added by
n^{2+o(1)}
.

We formalize the algorithm sketched above. First, initialize
G_{\tau+1}
as
G^{\prime}_{\tau+1}
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\kappa_{i}=\lfloor(2|G_{\tau+1}^{\prime}|+1)/2^{i-1}\rfloor
and
N_{i}=\{r\in N:|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|\geq\kappa_{i}\}
.
For each
i=1,2,\ldots,L=\lfloor\log(2|G_{\tau+1}^{\prime}|+1)\rfloor+1
in order, do the following:

1.

For each original vertex
v_{0}\in V
, we add a new copy, denoted
v_{in}
, into
G_{\tau+1}
, with shift

\displaystyle\delta(v_{in})=\min_{j\in[c],r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}:v_{j}\in{V}^{in}_{r}}\omega(v_{j},\tilde{r})+\delta(v_{j}).

(7)

We add all possible edges to and from
v_{in}
, as defined by
2
.

2.

For each original vertex
v_{0}\in V
, we add a new copy, denoted
v_{out}
, into
G_{\tau+1}
, with shift

\displaystyle\delta(v_{out})=\max_{j\in[c],r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}:v_{j}\in V^{out}_{r}}-\omega(\tilde{r},v_{j})+\delta(v_{j}).

(8)

We add all possible edges to and from
v_{in}
, as defined by
2
.

3.

For each
v_{0}\in V
, find
(j^{in}_{v},r^{in}_{v})
such that
\delta(v_{in})=\omega(v_{j^{in}_{v}},\tilde{r}^{in}_{v})+\delta(v_{j^{in}_{v}})
:

\displaystyle(j^{in}_{v},r^{in}_{v})=\arg\min_{j\in[c],r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}:v_{j}\in V^{in}_{r}}\omega(v_{j},\tilde{r})+\delta(v_{j}).

Then, for each
r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}
, and each
v_{j}\in V^{in}_{r}
, we enumerate every
u\in\tilde{V}^{in}_{r^{in}_{v}}
. If the edges
(u,v_{j})
and
(v_{j},\tilde{r})
both exist and
\omega(u,v_{j})+\omega(v_{j},\tilde{r})\geq 0
, add an edge
(u,\tilde{r})
to
G_{\tau+1}
of weight
\omega(u,v_{j})+\omega(v_{j},\tilde{r})
.

4.

For each
v_{0}\in V
, find
(j^{out}_{v},r^{out}_{v})
such that
\delta(v_{out})=-\omega(\tilde{r}^{out}_{v},v_{j^{out}_{v}})+\delta(v_{j^{out}_{v}})

\displaystyle(j^{out}_{v},r^{out}_{v})=\arg\max_{j\in[c],r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}:v_{j}\in V^{out}_{r}}-\omega(\tilde{r},v_{j})+\delta(v_{j}).

Then, for each
r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}
, and each
v_{j}\in V^{out}_{r}
, we enumerate every
w\in\tilde{V}^{out}_{r^{out}_{v}}
. If the edges
(\tilde{r},v_{j})
and
(v_{j},w)
both exist and
\omega(\tilde{r},v_{j})+\omega(v_{j},w)\geq 0
, add an edge
(\tilde{r},w)
to
G_{\tau+1}
of weight
\omega(\tilde{r},v_{j})+\omega(v_{j},w)
.

Finally, we remove all imaginary negative edges from
G_{\tau+1}
. By construction,
G_{\tau+1}
adds
O(\log{n})
copies of each vertex and has shortcut edges
(S4)
and
(S5)
. It remains to show that all locally-negative paths are shortcut and bound the runtime, which we prove in the following subclaims.

Subclaim 22
.

Constructing
G_{\tau+1}
runs in time
n^{2+o(1)}
.

Proof.

We consider each round
i
separately. In steps (1) and (2), we add copies
v_{in}
and
v_{out}
. Computing
\delta(v_{in})
and
\delta(v_{out})
takes at most
O(nck)=\tilde{O}(n^{2})
time and adding these copies takes
O(n^{2}c^{2})=\tilde{O}(n^{2})
time. In steps (3) and (4), we shortcut the remaining locally-negative paths by enumerating. We enumerate over
r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}
and
v_{j}\in V^{in}_{r}\cup V^{out}_{r}
, and then add at most
|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r^{\prime}}|+|\tilde{V}_{r^{\prime}}^{out}|
edges, where
r^{\prime}\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}
. Since
r^{\prime}\not\in N_{i}
, we have
|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r^{\prime}}|+|\tilde{V}_{r^{\prime}}^{out}|\leq\kappa_{i}
, so the running time for these steps is
O(\kappa_{i}(|V^{in}_{r}|+|V^{out}_{r}|))
for every negative vertex
r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}
.
Summing over
r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}
, the enumeration takes time

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}}O(\kappa_{i}(|V^{in}_{r}|+|V^{out}_{r})|)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r\in N_{i+1}\setminus N_{i}}O((|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|)^{2})

where we are using (1)
r\in N_{i+1}
, so
|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|>\kappa_{i+1}=\Theta(\kappa_{i})
and (2)
|V^{in}_{r}|\leq|\tilde{V}_{r}^{in}|
and
|V^{out}_{r}|\leq|\tilde{V}_{r}^{out}|
.
Summing over all iterations, the total runtime of steps (3) and (4) is

O\left(\sum_{r\in N}(|\tilde{V}^{in}_{r}|+|\tilde{V}^{out}_{r}|)^{2}\right)\leq O(kn^{2}/b)\leq n^{2+o(1)}

by
Lemma

15
and the choice of

. Combining the time spent on adding copies and enumerations gives the desired running time.
∎

Subclaim 23
.

We have
d_{G_{\tau+1}}(s,t)=d_{\Gamma}(s,t)

Proof.

Since

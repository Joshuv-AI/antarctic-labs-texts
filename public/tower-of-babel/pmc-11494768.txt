Comprehensive analysis of surgical strategies and prognosis for non-small cell lung cancer with pleural metastasis detected intraoperatively
Authors: Fanmao Meng, Na Ren, Guochao Zhang, Ruifeng Xu, Mengbai Tian, Xin Sun, Liang Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectivesLung cancer is one of the prevailing malignancies worldwide. Surgical interventions hold an important position in the treatment framework for lung cancer. Pleural metastasis is often assumed to be a surgical contraindication, but not all instances of pleural metastasis can be accurately identified before surgery. The question of how to address pleural metastasis detected intraoperatively is still undecided.MethodsThis retrospective study included 187 lung cancer patients who underwent surgery from 2005 to 2017 in whom pleural metastasis was discovered incidentally during the operation. Data on demographic, surgical, pathological, postoperative treatment, and survival information were collected for further analysis.ResultsFor patients with intraoperatively detected pleural metastasis, two independent protective prognostic factors were receiving primary tumor resection (compared to only receiving pleural nodule biopsy, HR = 0.079, p = 0.022) and receiving postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy (HR = 0.081, p < 0.001). Simultaneously, performing systematic lymph node dissection during primary tumor resection was found to be detrimental to long-term prognosis (HR = 2.375, p = 0.044). However, the resection of pleural metastatic lesions did not significantly impact patient prognosis.ConclusionOur study supports the implementation of major tumor resection in patients with pleural metastasis detected intraoperatively but not lymph node dissection or the resection of pleural metastatic lesions. Postoperative chemotherapy is also necessary.Keywords: Lung cancer, Pleural metastasis, Surgery, Lymph node dissection, Prognosis

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the most prevalent malignancies worldwide. Recent data from GLOBOCAN reveal that lung cancer ranks third in terms of age-standardized incidence rates among all cancers and is the leading cause of cancer-related death [1]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases [2]. The clinical staging of NSCLC guides treatment selection, and surgical resection is widely utilized as a traditional therapy. Surgical resection is considered the optimal treatment approach for patients with clinical stage I and II disease [3]. For those with clinical stage III disease, surgical intervention also demonstrates broad applicability [4, 5], particularly in stage IIIA cases [6]. However, surgical treatment is generally not recommended for patients with clinical stage IV disease due to the presence of distant metastases [7]. Therefore, extensive preoperative evaluations should be conducted to identify distant metastases and accurately determine the clinical stage prior to considering surgical intervention [8].

Pleural metastasis in NSCLC is classified as distant metastasis, and it is classified as M1a according to the 8th edition of the TNM Classification. It is characterized by pleural enhancement and thickening on preoperative imaging, making it challenging to differentiate from benign pleural diseases [9]. Consequently, the identification of pleural metastasis is often delayed until surgical intervention. The incidental discovery of pleural metastasis intraoperatively poses considerable challenges in devising and implementing appropriate surgical strategies. Surgeons are confronted with decisions regarding whether to resect the main tumor, perform only a pleural biopsy, conduct systematic lymph node dissection, or resect the pleural metastatic lesions. These complex clinical dilemmas necessitate further research and evidence-based investigation to inform optimal management approaches.

This study is a retrospective study designed to collect comprehensive clinical data from patients with pleural metastasis detected intraoperatively. The primary objective was to analyze the potential relationship between prognosis and various indicators in this specific patient cohort while aiming to propose an optimal surgical strategy.

Methods

Patients

We conducted a retrospective study to collect data from 226 patients who underwent thoracic surgery at the Cancer Hospital of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences between 2005 and 2017 and were diagnosed intraoperatively as having pleural metastasis. Twenty-one patients were excluded for metastasis in other sites during the postoperative follow-up, and 18 patients were excluded for loss to follow-up. Ultimately, a total of 187 eligible patients were included in the analysis. Demographic information, treatment particulars, and postoperative pathology reports of the patients were extracted from the hospital information system. This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College, and informed consent was exempted for this research.

Data collection and patient follow-up

The demographic information obtained in this study encompassed the sex, age, and smoking history of the patients. The surgical information encompassed the specific methods of surgery, lymph node handling, and pleural metastasis handling. Pathological information encompassed tumor size, T stage, histological type, degree of differentiation, intravascular cancer emboli, and resection margins. The patients’ follow-up data were gathered through both outpatient visits and telephone interviews. Outpatient follow-up appointments were scheduled at three-month intervals during the first postoperative year, semiannually from the second to the fifth year after surgery, and annually thereafter. Patients who missed their scheduled outpatient follow-up appointments were contacted by telephone. The information collected during the follow-up period included whether the patients received postoperative chemotherapy and targeted therapy as well as their survival status. Death resulting from any cause was considered the study’s endpoint event, and overall survival was measured in months from the time of surgery until either death from any cause or the completion of the follow-up period.

Statistical analysis

Cox regression analysis was employed to examine the relationship between various indicators and survival, with multivariate Cox regression being used to account for potential interactions among multiple variables. The results obtained from the Cox regression analysis are presented graphically using forest plots. Furthermore, survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan‒Meier method, and the significance of intergroup differences was assessed using the log-rank test. The survival outcomes are illustrated using Kaplan‒Meier survival curves. All statistical analyses conducted in this study were performed using R version 4.2.2. The R packages “survival” and “survminer” were utilized for Cox regression analysis and Kaplan‒Meier survival analysis. The R package “forestplot” was utilized for the visualization of the forest plots. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value < 0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance.

Results

Demographic, treatment, and follow-up information of patients

We collected retrospective data on demographic, surgical, pathological, postoperative treatment, and survival information from patients who met the criteria for this study (Table 1). Of the total patient population, 47.6% (89 of 187) were male, with an average age at admission of 55.76 years (ranging from 31 to 80 years), and 34.2% (64 of 187) had a history of smoking. Most patients (81.3%, 152 of 187) underwent resection of the main tumor, while 18.7% (35 of 187) had only a pleural nodule biopsy. Lymph node dissection was performed during surgery in 60.4% (113 of 187) of patients, and 15% (28 of 187) had all visible pleural nodules removed. Regarding postoperative pathological diagnosis, the average maximum tumor diameter was 3.73 cm, with the majority of patients (55.1%, 103 of 187) classified as T2 stage. The most common histological type observed was adenocarcinoma (86.1%, 161 of 187), with 12 cases of squamous cell carcinoma, 10 cases of adenosquamous carcinoma, and 1 case each of large cell carcinoma, leiomyosarcoma, sarcomatoid carcinoma, and angiosarcoma. Poorly differentiated tumors accounted for 50.9% (88 of 173) of cases, moderately differentiated tumors accounted for 46.2% (80 of 173), and only 2.9% (5 of 173) were well differentiated. Intravascular tumor embolus was present in 25.3% (38 of 150) of patients, and positive surgical margins were found in 11.2% (17 of 152) of patients. During the period from the surgery to discharge, three patients developed surgical site infections and two patients had lower extremity venous embolism. All postoperative complications were relieved after receiving basic medical treatment, and the Clavien-Dindo grades were all Grade I. The majority of patients (91%, 131 of 144) received appropriate postoperative chemotherapy, and 48.9% (66 of 135) received postoperative targeted therapy. At the time of the last follow-up, the average overall survival for all patients was 37.79 months, with 104 still alive and 83 deceased.

Which factors affect the prognosis of patients?

To investigate the potential factors affecting the prognosis of patients with pleural metastasis detected during surgery, we conducted Cox regression analysis. Initially, we performed univariate Cox regression analysis to screen variables that could impact prognosis. The results are presented in a forest plot (Fig. 1), which revealed that older age, larger tumor size, positive intravascular tumor embolus, and positive resection margins were potential risk factors for these patients (P < 0.05). On the other hand, main tumor resection, postoperative chemotherapy, and postoperative targeted therapy were potential protective factors (P < 0.05). To account for potential biases and interactions between variables, we included the aforementioned factors as well as variables related to the surgical strategy (lymph node dissection and pleural metastasis resection) in a multivariate Cox regression model (Fig. 2). The analysis indicated that undergoing main tumor resection (compared to pleural biopsy alone) and receiving postoperative chemotherapy were independent protective factors for patients with intraoperatively detected pleural metastasis (P < 0.05). Furthermore, lymph node dissection was a possible independent risk factor, and postoperative targeted therapy was a possible independent protective factor (P < 0.1).

Survival outcomes of different surgical strategies

Then, we conducted a descriptive analysis comparing the survival outcomes of patients who underwent two different surgical strategies. The survival curves for patients who received main tumor resection or pleural biopsy are shown in Fig. 3A. Patients who underwent main tumor resection had a median survival period of 30 months, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates of 91.7%, 66.0%, and 48.8%, respectively. Conversely, patients who underwent pleural biopsy had a median survival period of 23 months, with 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates of 90.5%, 52.4%, and 28.6%, respectively (Table 2). The statistical analysis revealed a significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.012).

Further surgical strategy for patients with pleural metastasis detected intraoperatively

Through previous analysis, we discovered that patients with pleural metastasis detected intraoperatively can still experience significant survival benefits from resection of the primary tumor. These benefits remain independent of other traditional treatment methods, such as chemotherapy and targeted therapy. However, in addition to tumor resection, what other interventions can surgeons implement to prolong patient survival? To draw further conclusions, we extracted all patients who underwent primary tumor resection from the original dataset for analysis. Our focus was on six clinically important interventions: lymph node dissection, resection of pleural metastases, ensuring appropriate resection margins, postoperative chemotherapy, postoperative targeted therapy, and surgery type. A total of 152 patients underwent major tumor resection, encompassing 92 cases of lobectomy, 52 cases of sublobectomy, and 8 cases of pneumonectomy. 109 patients underwent lymph node dissection and 28 patients had pleural metastatic nodules removed during the surgery. In 129 patients, no residual tumor cells were detected at the surgical margin. Seventeen patients had positive surgical margins. For 6 patients, the postoperative pathological report did not provide the margin status. 110 patients received postoperative chemotherapy and 55 patients received postoperative targeted therapy. We plotted survival curves for each intervention (Fig. 3B-G). The results indicate that patients who did not undergo lymph node dissection, had negative resection margins, and received postoperative chemotherapy and targeted therapy tended to have a more favorable prognosis. However, the resection of metastatic lesions and different surgery types did not significantly impact survival. To eliminate mutual influence, we employed a multivariate Cox regression analysis that confirmed lymph node dissection and Positive resection margins as an independent risk factor, while postoperative chemotherapy acted as an independent protective factor (Fig. 4).

Discussion

Pleural metastasis in NSCLC is considered a distant metastasis and serves as an indicator of advanced clinical stage as well as bleak prognosis. Research has revealed that patients with pleural metastasis have a median survival time of only 5–11 months [10, 11]. Generally, surgical treatment is not recommended for NSCLC patients who are known to have pleural metastasis. However, due to the relatively low sensitivity of preoperative examinations in detecting pleural metastasis, approximately 1.2-2% of patients are incidentally discovered to have pleural metastasis during surgery [12].

In previous studies, scholars have conducted analyses on the prognostic factors affecting patients with intraoperatively detected pleural metastasis. A comprehensive meta-analysis has synthesized the relevant research [13]. As early as 2000, a retrospective study by Japanese scholar Yukito Ichinose et al. examined this issue, revealing that favorable prognostic factors included adenocarcinoma histological type, lower clinical T stage, and absence of visible residual tumor [14]. Tatsuo Fukuse et al. focused on the role of pleural effusion, demonstrating that patients with pleural metastasis and concomitant pleural effusion had a poorer prognosis [15]. Numerous researchers have concentrated on the impact of the surgical approach. Studies by Pierre Mordant et al. [16], Bing-Yen Wang et al. [17], Yi-jiu Ren et al. [18], and Chi Li et al. [19] have indicated that in patients with intraoperatively detected pleural metastasis, even when radical cure cannot be achieved through surgery, surgical resection of the primary tumor can still significantly benefit survival.

The conclusions drawn from previous research align with the findings of this study. Within our patient cohort, factors such as advanced age, larger tumor size, lack of main tumor resection, presence of intravascular tumor emboli, positive surgical margins, nonreceipt of postoperative chemotherapy and targeted therapy were all associated with a poorer prognosis. Through a further multivariable Cox regression analysis to account for variable interactions, main tumor resection and postoperative chemotherapy emerged as independent protective factors for patient prognosis. Postoperative targeted therapy was a possible independent protective factor (P = 0.087), however, their effects did not reach the significance level set in this study, maybe further analysis is needed for verification before drawing any definitive conclusions. Our results substantiate the notion that surgical resection offers survival benefits for NSCLC patients with pleural metastasis, independent of chemotherapy and targeted therapy. This presents new possibilities for the treatment of NSCLC, suggesting that surgical resection can be considered a viable treatment option for M1a stage patients, as it can effectively extend patient survival alongside chemotherapy and targeted therapy. In this study, patients who underwent main tumor resection had significantly improved 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates of 91.7%, 66.0%, and 48.8%, respectively, compared to 90.5%, 52.4%, and 28.6% for those who did not undergo main tumor resection.

However, surgical strategy encompasses more than just the selection of surgical procedures; it also involves lymph node dissection, surgery types, metastatic lesion resection, and execution of surgical margin. However, to date, few studies have explored these specific surgical strategies in the subgroup of patients with pleural metastasis detected intraoperatively. To address this gap in research, we collected comprehensive data on surgical strategies for thorough analysis. In general, lymph node dissection or further resection of visible pleural metastases can bring no additional benefit when performing primary tumor resection for patients with intraoperatively detected pleural metastasis. However, ensuring negative resection margins and receiving postoperative chemotherapy remains necessary.

We conducted a thorough analysis of the importance of lymph node dissection. According to 2006 guidelines published by the European Society of Thoracic Surgeons, lymph node dissection is defined as the removal of at least 3 stations of mediastinal lymph nodes [20], accompanied by pathological histological examination of the excised lymph node tissue. The number of lymph node metastases and N staging in NSCLC have proven to be reliable prognostic indicators [21, 22]. Moreover, the TNM staging system, which is based on lymph node metastasis, serves as an essential guide for determining postoperative adjuvant therapy for patients [23–25]. Consequently, lymph node dissection remains a crucial component of surgical interventions for NSCLC. However, the survival benefit of lymph node dissection has been a subject of controversy. While studies conducted by Yi-long Wu et al. [26], Lardinois D et al. [27], and S M Keller et al. [28] have shown positive effects of lymph node dissection on patient survival, several other studies have found no significant survival benefit [29, 30]. Additionally, lymph node dissection may lead to prolonged surgical time and increased postoperative complication rates, which pose additional risks to patients [31]. Our study revealed that lymph node dissection significantly reduced the overall survival of patients with intraoperatively detected pleural metastasis, and this adverse prognostic effect was independent of other interventions. We think that major tumor resection in patients with intraoperatively detected pleural metastasis does not belong to the category of radical surgery. Therefore, extensive lymph node dissection in these cases does not substantially reduce tumor burden but instead increases surgical trauma and prolongs operative time, which may be the primary reason for the unfavorable impact of lymph node dissection on prognosis. Furthermore, recent research has suggested that lymph nodes may play a role in regulating the tumor immune response [32], and a retrospective multicenter study has also demonstrated that extensive lymph node dissection diminishes the efficacy of postoperative immunotherapy [33]. This introduces another perspective that preserving more lymph nodes in patients expected to undergo postoperative adjuvant therapy may offer potential survival benefits.

In our study, we observed that ensuring negative surgical margins tended to prolong overall survival among patients (p = 0.071), whereas resecting visible pleural metastases did not significantly affect prognosis (p = 0.376). Surgical margins are recognized as a crucial factor influencing local recurrence in NSCLC [34]. However, there is limited research on surgical resection of pleural metastases, which is generally considered a surgical contraindication. To summarize, our study suggests that performing major tumor resection may be a preferable option for patients with pleural metastases detected intraoperatively, and ensuring negative margins remains important. Nevertheless, we think performing lymph node dissection or resection of pleural metastases can not bring additional benefits.

However, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of our single-center retrospective study design. First, the presence of time effect bias cannot be ignored. As our cohort spans a substantial period; the treatment techniques and methods could have evolved and improved, potentially impacting patient survival. Additionally, inherent recall bias is inevitable in telephone follow-ups. Confounding bias also exists. Despite our efforts to comprehensively document patients’ demographic information, surgical details, pathological features, and postoperative treatment information and employ multiple-factor analysis to minimize confounding factors, some indicators remain inaccurately recorded. For instance, the lack of lymph node dissection in certain patients has resulted in the unavailability of the pathological N stage, which leads to the inability of our study to balance the impact of the N stage. In addition, we are unable to retrospectively collect the specific reasons for different surgical methods used by different patients, which hinders further analysis. Furthermore, our study exclusively focuses on Asian people of Chinese nationality, and the proportion of non-smoking women was relatively high, which limiting the generalizability of our findings. Although the number of cases included in our study is greater than that in previous studies of the same type, further multicenter and large-sample studies are warranted to validate our conclusions.

Conclusions

In this study, we conducted a retrospective analysis of patients with intraoperative pleural metastasis and found that undergoing major tumor resection (compared to pleural biopsy only) and receiving postoperative chemotherapy were two independent favorable prognostic factors. The 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates for patients undergoing major tumor resection were 91.7%, 66.0%, and 48.8%, respectively. Additionally, among patients undergoing major tumor resection, we observed that performing lymph node dissection significantly worsened the long-term prognosis, while the removal of pleural metastatic lesions did not significantly affect the prognosis. In conclusion, our study supports the implementation of major tumor resection in patients with pleural metastasis detected intraoperatively but not lymph node dissection or the resection of pleural metastatic lesions. This study is a retrospective study, and further prospective randomized experiments are needed to validate our conclusions.

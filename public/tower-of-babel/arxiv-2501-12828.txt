Mathematical modelling and homogenization of thin fiber-reinforced hydrogels
Amartya Chakrabortty, Haradhan Dutta, Hari Shankar Mahato

Abstract
This work considers simultaneous homogenization and dimension reduction of a poroelastic model for thin fiber-reinforced hydrogels. The analysed medium is a two-component system consisting of a continuous fiber framework with hydrogel inclusions arranged periodically throughout the medium. The fibers are assumed to operate under quasi-stationary linear elasticity, whereas the hydrogel’s hydromechanical behavior is represented using Biot’s linear poroelasticity model. The asymptotic limit of the coupled system is established when the periodicity and thickness parameters are of the same order and tend to zero simultaneously, utilizing the re-scaling unfolding operator. It is demonstrated that the limit displacement exhibits Kirchhoff-Love-type behavior using the decomposition of plate displacements. Towards the end, a unique solution for the macroscopic problem has been demonstrated.



Keywords:

Homogenization, dimension reduction, Biot poroelasticity, linear elasticity, unfolding operator, decomposition methods.

Due to this scale heterogeneity, the effective hydro-mechanical properties of a FIH is not yet fully understood, and as a consequence, there is an interest in finding the effective global properties from its microstructure using homogenization. and for all

, then using (

v^{k}_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad\text{on $\partial{\mathcal{Y}}^{g}$ for all $k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}$}.

So, using second Korn’s inequality, we get

The constant only depends on

, not on
\varepsilon
.

We obtain after the changing the variable

\nabla_{y}v^{k}_{\varepsilon}(y)=\varepsilon\nabla{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon y),\quad\text{for a.e. $y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{g}$},

which imply

\varepsilon^{3}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon{\mathcal{Y}}^{g})}+\varepsilon^{5}\|\nabla{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon{\mathcal{Y}}^{g})}\leq C\varepsilon^{5}\|e({\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\varepsilon k+\varepsilon{\mathcal{Y}}^{g})}.

Hence, adding for all

5.2
Griso’s decomposition of displacement result

Below, we recall the decomposition of plate displacements, it was first introduced in

]

Lemma 3
.

Let
{\bf w}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3}
, then we have

{\bf w}=W_{E}+\overline{w},\qquad\text{ a.e. in }\;\;\Omega_{\varepsilon},

(5.6)

where

is given by

W_{E}(x)=\left(\begin{aligned} &{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(x^{\prime})+x_{3}{\mathcal{R}}_{1}(x^{\prime})\\
&{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(x^{\prime})+x_{3}{\mathcal{R}}_{2}(x^{\prime})\\
&\hskip 25.6073pt{\mathcal{W}}_{3}(x^{\prime})\end{aligned}\right)\;\;\text{for a.e.}\;\;x=(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}\in H^{1}(\omega)^{3},\quad{\mathcal{R}}\in H^{1}(\omega)^{2},\quad\overline{w}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3}

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad{\mathcal{U}}(x^{\prime})={1\over 2\varepsilon}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}{\bf w}(x^{\prime},x_{3})\,dx_{3},\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\overline{w}(\cdot,x_{3})\,dx_{3}=0,\quad\text{for all $x^{\prime}\in\omega$}.

Moreover, we have the following estimates

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{W}}_{\alpha}\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}+\varepsilon(\|{\mathcal{W}}_{3}\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}+\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}\|_{H^{1}(\omega)})

\displaystyle\leq{C\over\varepsilon^{1/2}}\|e({\bf w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\alpha}{\mathcal{W}}_{3}+{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq{C\over\varepsilon^{1/2}}\|e({\bf w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

\displaystyle\|\overline{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\overline{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|e({\bf w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

If we have

a.e. on

{\mathcal{W}}=0,\quad{\mathcal{R}}=0,\quad\text{a.e. on}\quad\partial\omega,\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{w}=0\quad\text{a.e. on}\quad\Lambda_{0,\varepsilon}.

Moreover, we have the following Korn-type inequalities

\displaystyle\|{\bf w}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|{\bf w}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|{\bf w}_{3}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|e({\bf w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

(5.8)

\displaystyle\|\nabla{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq{C\over\varepsilon}\|e({\bf w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

The constants do not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Remark 2
.

The displacement

is called an elementary displacement, which is a generalization of rigid displacements. The field

is the mid-surface displacement at the point

while
x_{3}{\mathcal{R}}(x^{\prime})
represents the small linearized rotation of the fiber
\{x^{\prime}\}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
. The residual displacement

is called the warping, which captures the microscopic behavior of the displacement
{\bf w}
.

We also recall another decomposition, see Lemma 3.5 in
[
26
]
for more details.

Lemma 4
.

Let
{\bf u}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3}
, then we define

{\mathcal{U}}={1\over 2\varepsilon}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}{\bf u}\,dx_{3},\quad\text{in $\omega$},\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{u}={\bf u}-{\mathcal{U}},\quad\text{in $\Omega_{\varepsilon}$}.

Then, we have

{\mathcal{U}}\in H^{1}(\omega)^{3},\quad\overline{{\bf u}}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3},\quad\text{with}\quad\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\overline{u}\,dx_{3}=0,\quad\text{a.e. in $\omega$}.

Moreover, it satisfies

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{U}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq{C\over\varepsilon^{1/2}}\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},\quad

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\alpha}{\mathcal{U}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq{C\over\varepsilon^{1/2}}\|\nabla{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

(5.9)

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{{\bf u}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},\quad

\displaystyle\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

The constant(s) are independent of
\varepsilon
.

Furthermore, if
{\bf u}=0
in

, then we have

\overline{u}=0,\quad\text{in $\Gamma_{\varepsilon}\cup{\Omega^{f}_{\varepsilon}}$}.

This kind of decomposition is more suited when we already have a Korn-type inequality and we have sharper

-estimate of
{\bf u}
then the
L^{2}
-estimate of its gradient
\nabla{\bf u}

Conclusion and future outlook

In conclusion, this paper deals with simultaneous homogenization and dimension reduction of a microscopic model of a fiber-reinforced hydrogel. More precisely, the FIH consists of a periodic connected elastic fiber-scaffold and a disconnected hydrogel part model via Biot linear poro-elasticity. On the interface standard continuity of displacement, stress and flux is assumed. An asymptotically based homogenized limit model is derived when both the period and thickness are of the same order and tend to zero simultaneously. The limit model is the Biot-Kirchhoff-Love plate model, which satisfies the corresponding homogeneous boundary and initial conditions as the microscopic model.

In reality, the fiber and hydrogel components are interconnected. However, in the current draft (where the hydrogel part is disconnected), we analyze the hydrogel part in each cell as a separate bounded domain with a Lipschitz boundary. When the hydrogel is connected, the boundary of the hydrogel part in each periodicity cell may not be Lipschitz. This complicates the process of deriving estimates and passing to the limit. Therefore, the case of both components being connected will be addressed in future work.

Additionally, in the current draft, we assume the periodicity and thickness are of the same order, but the thickness of the fiber-scaffold can be smaller than the size of an individual cell. For that, it would also be interesting to consider the thickness parameter as
0<\delta
with the assumption,
\displaystyle\lim_{(\delta,\varepsilon)\to(0,0)}\displaystyle{\delta\over\varepsilon}=0
, where
0<\varepsilon
is the periodicity parameter.

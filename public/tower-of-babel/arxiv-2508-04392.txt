Explicit Construction of Approximate Kolmogorov-Arnold Superpositions with C 2 C^{2} -Smoothness
Lunji Song †, Juan Diego Toscano §, Li-Lian Wang ‡

Abstract
. We explicitly construct an approximate version of the Kolmogorov-Arnold superpositions, which is composed of C 2 C^{2} -inner and outer functions, and can approximate an arbitrary α \alpha -Hölder continuous function f ∈ ℋ α ​ ( [ 0 , 1 ] d ) , α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] f\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}([0,1]^{d}),\,\alpha\in(0,1] with accuracy O ⁡ ( N − α ⁡ ( 1 + γ ) ) O(N^{-\alpha(1+\gamma)}) for some γ ≥ 0 , \gamma\geq 0, where N N denotes the number of outer summations. The inner functions are generated by applying suitable translations and dilations to a piecewise C 2 , C^{2}, strictly-increasing function, while the outer functions are constructed row-wise through piecewise C 2 C^{2} -interpolation using newly designed shape functions. 1.
Introduction

In a series of seminal papers
[
18
,
1
,
2
,
19
]
, Kolmogorov and Arnold studied how multivariate continuous functions defined on a bounded domain can be represented as superpositions of continuous functions of fewer variables.
Their significant discovery, known as the Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem (KART) or Kolmogorov-Arnold Superposition Theorem (KAST), was stated in Kolmogorov
[
19
]
(1957):

For any dimension
d\geq 2,
there are continuous real functions
\psi_{p,q}(x)
on the closed unit interval
E^{1}=[0,1]
such that every continuous real function
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
on the
d
-dimensional unit cube
E^{d}=[0,1]^{d}
is representable as

(1.1)

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=\sum_{q=0}^{2d}g_{q}\circ\sum_{p=1}^{d}\psi_{p,q}(x_{p}),

where
g_{q}
are continuous real functions on
\mathbb{R}=(-\infty,\infty)
.
The univariate functions
\psi_{p,q}
are called the
inner functions
(which are independent of
f
), while
g_{q}
are referred to as the
outer functions
(which depend on
f
). This mathematically elegant representation can be regarded as a refutation of Hilbert’s 13th problem:
“There exist continuous functions of three variables that cannot be represented as superpositions of continuous functions of two variables”
(see
[
7
]
).

Although the proof in
[
19
]
was brief and not highly constructive, the theory has nonetheless inspired extensive research, particularly in two interwoven directions: (i) construction of refined representations/versions
(see e.g.,
[
26
,
38
,
39
,
27
,
15
,
28
,
40
,
41
,
20
,
4
]
, Sprecher’s book
[
42
]
and recent ones

[
33
,
21
,
14
,
13
]
),
and (ii) applications in network architectures and computations (see e.g.,
[
11
,
7
,
16
,
17
,
12
,
9
,
30
,
34
,
10
,
22
,
25
,
37
,
43
,
45
,
8
,
44
]
and many references therein). Lorentz
[
26
]
was the first to observe that the outer functions
g_{q}
can all be chosen identically. Subsequently, Sprecher
[
38
]
demonstrated that the inner functions
\psi_{p,q}(x)
may be taken as
\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(x)
for suitable constants
\lambda_{p}.
The version of Lorentz

[
27
]
and Kahane
[
15
]
was stated in
[
28
, Chapter 17]
with
g_{q}=g
and
\psi_{p,q}=\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}
in (
1.1
), where the Kahane’s proof was based on the Baire category theorem.
Sprecher
[
38
,
40
,
41
]
provided more computer-friendly construction of a different version stated in
[
41
]
:
Let
m\geq 2d,\gamma\geq m+2
be given integers, and then any
f\in C([0,1]^{d})
can be represented by the superpositions:

(1.2)

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{m}{g_{q}}\circ\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}{\psi}(x_{p}+qa),

where
\psi
is of

\frac{\ln 2}{\ln\gamma}
-Lipschitz class and increasing,
g_{q}
are continuous, and the parameters

(1.3)

a=\frac{1}{\gamma(\gamma-1)};\quad\lambda_{1}=1,\quad\lambda_{p}=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{-(p-1)(d^{r}-1)/(d-1)},\;\;\;2\leq p\leq d.

It is noteworthy that the function
\psi
in (
1.2
) is defined via a limiting process:

\psi(x)=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\psi_{k}(d_{k}),

where
d_{k}\in[0,1]
are pre-assigned terminating rational numbers expressed in terms of
\gamma
-based decimals, and the values
\psi_{k}(d_{k})
are computed using Köppen’s recursive formula
[
20
]
(modified from the formula in
[
40
]
so that the resulting function
\psi
is increasing, as rigorously shown in
[
5
]
).

It is common that in most, if not all, existing constructions, the inner functions consist of staircase-like structures with derivatives vanishing almost everywhere, exhibiting “wild” and “pathological” behaviors (see
[
7
,
42
,
6
]
).
Indeed, the exact superposition fails to be true if the inner functions are required to be smooth, owing to the assertion by
Vitushkin
[
46
,
47
]
(on Hilbert’s 13th problem):
there are
r\geq 1
times continuously differentiable functions not representable by
r
times continuously differentiable functions of less than
d
variables.
Accordingly, any attempt to construct smooth variants of exact superpositions is futile, which inherently obstructs the direct application of the theory to neural networks
[
11
,
7
,
6
]
.

K
\dot{\rm u}
rková
[
16
,
17
]
suggested that one should sacrifice the exactness of representation by adopting an approximate version instead. However,
in a recent note, Demb and Sprecher
[
6
]
raised the question:

Are there means of smoothing these mappings
(
i.e.,
\Psi_{q}=\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(x_{p}):[0,1]^{d}\mapsto[0,1]
in any approximate version
)
into more computer friendly forms while retaining the essence of Kolmogorov’s strategy of exact representation?
. Although this open question was not answered in
[
6
]
, they managed to construct the superpositions more suitable for parallel computations, which could approximate any continuous function
f
on

[0,1]^{d}
to an error
\varepsilon\|f\|_{\infty}
for any preassigned
\varepsilon>0:

(1.4)

\Big\|f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})-\sum_{q=0}^{m}g_{q}\circ\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi(x_{p}+qa)\Big\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\|f\|_{\infty},

where
m
is an integer depending on
\varepsilon,
and
\lambda_{p}
and
a
are constants. Nevertheless, the inner functions and Kolmogorov maps
\Psi_{q}
therein still suffer from “wildness” (see
[
6
, Fig. 2]
).
Igelnik and Parikh
[
12
]
attempted much earlier for approximate superpositions involving
C^{4}
-smooth inner and outer functions, and claimed that the resulting version could approximate any continuously differentiable function
f
with a bounded gradient on
[0,1]^{d}
, with convergence guarantees:

(1.5)

\Big\|f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})-\sum_{q=1}^{N}g_{q}\circ\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(x_{p})\Big\|_{\infty}=O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big),

where
\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\leq 1,\lambda_{p}>0
are given rationally independent numbers (that is, the equation
\sum_{p=1}^{d}r_{p}\lambda_{p}=0
, with rational coefficients
r_{p}
, implies that all
r_{p}=0
).
Their construction essentially follows the constructive proof of Lorentz’s version in Kahane
[
15
]
.
The inner functions in
[
12
]
are composed of constant segments over closed sub-intervals of length
O(1/N)
, interspersed with cubic spline transitions across gaps of length
O(1/N^{2})
(which turned out to be the only difference from Kahane
[
15
]
, where linear pieces were used to fill in the gaps), while their outer functions are constructed via piecewise linear and cubic spline interpolation of samples of
f
on hypercubes formed by disjoint sub-intervals. Igelnik and Parikh
[
12
]
further asserted that there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
N
and gap size
\delta
such that

(1.6)

\|\psi_{q}^{(k)}\|_{\infty},\;\|g_{q}^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\leq C,\quad k=1,2,3,4,\;\;q=1,\ldots,N,

which is essential for configuring the Kolmogorov spline network (KSN) with a parameter count of
P=O(N^{3/2})
.
However, the magnitudes of the derivatives grow rapidly—often explosively—with powers of
N
in the small gaps, so (
1.6
) cannot hold. Moreover, the derivatives of inner functions vanish on the disjoint closed sub-intervals, which are undesirable
[
6
]
.

The aim of this paper is to provide an affirmative answer to the aforementioned open question
[
6
]
by
explicitly constructing novel
C^{2}
-smooth approximate Kolmogorov-Arnold superpositions:

(1.7)

f(\boldsymbol{x})\approx f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{q=1}^{N}g_{q}\circ\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(x_{p})=\sum_{q=1}^{N}g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}),

with the following important features:

(i)

The inner functions
\psi_{q}\in C^{2}([0,1])

are
strictly increasing
and can be generated by a single function
\phi(x)
through suitable translation and dilation. The piecewise function
\phi(x)
is constructed by
new shape functions which have controllable derivative values in the small gaps and do not vanish in the disjoint closed sub-intervals.

(ii)

The Kolmogorov maps
\boldsymbol{\Psi}=(\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{N}):\,[0,1]^{d}\mapsto[0,1]^{N}
with

(1.8)

\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})=\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(x_{1})+\cdots+\lambda_{d}\psi_{q}(x_{d}):\,[0,1]^{d}\mapsto[0,1],\;\;1\leq q\leq N,

has a desirable separation property for given rationally independent numbers
\lambda_{p}
such that

\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\leq 1,\lambda_{p}>0
.
Notably, if we choose Sprecher-like

\lambda_{p}=O(N^{p-d})
for
p=1,\ldots,d
(analogous to (
1.3
):
\lambda_{p}=O(N^{1-p})
for which we do not require rational independence), then the images
\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})
(here
\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
are the centers of the hypercubes formed by the disjoined sub-intervals used to construct
\phi
), can be arranged in an increasing order when the centers are in row-major order. This allows to construct the outer functions row-wise and ensure
C^{2}
-continuity of the superpositions:
g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})
for all
q.

(iii)

The constructed superposition
f_{N}
can approximate any
\alpha
-Hölder continuous
f
with
\alpha\in(0,1],
within an accuracy
O(h^{\alpha})=O(J^{-\alpha})
where
h=N\delta
and
J=\lceil\frac{1}{N\delta}\rceil
.

This explicit construction carries significant implications. Firstly, it inherently suggests an algorithm whose high parallelism and intrinsic properties — such as the strict monotonicity and smoothness of the inner functions — can, and perhaps should, be preserved in any implementation of Kolmogorov-based neural networks and applications (see, e.g.,
[
23
,
30
,
10
]
).
Secondly, it can act as an intermediate framework in which the one-dimensional functions are further approximated or parameterized by various ansatz classes (e.g., splines, radial basis, spectral basis, and multilayer precisions), with smoothness playing a critical role in establishing convergence and providing estimates for the required network size. After all, the non-smooth but continuous exact representations can only guarantee the universal approximability of Kolmogorov networks using various ansatz expansions/interpolation/parameterisations (see e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
45
,
32
]
). It is still open to show their convergence rate.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the detailed constructions of the approximate Kolmogorov-Arnold superpositions. In Section
3
, we estimate the approximability and convergence of the constructed version and provide some numerical verifications of the convergence behavior. We then conclude the paper with some remarks.

2.
Shape functions and constructions of inner and outer functions

In this section, we present the construction of the approximate version. We begin by introducing shape functions with desirable properties, which serve as the foundation for the construction. These functions are then properly assembled to form a strictly increasing, piecewise
C^{2}
-function
\phi
, leading to the inner functions
\psi_{q}
through suitable translations and dilations. With this in place, we can rigorously study the separation properties of the Kolmogorov maps
\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda})
with respect to the inputs
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, and proceed to construct the outer functions, completing the approximate version.

2.1.
Construction of inner functions
\{\psi_{q}\}_{q=1}^{N}

We first introduce the shape functions.
Consider the smeared-out
C^{2}
-Heaviside function (see e.g.,
[
31
, (1.22)]
):

H_{\epsilon}(z)=\begin{cases}0,&z<-\epsilon\\
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{2\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\sin\frac{\pi z}{\epsilon},\quad&-\epsilon\leq z\leq\epsilon,\\
1,&z>\epsilon.\end{cases}

We extract the smeared-out portion of
H_{\epsilon}(z),z\in[-\epsilon,\epsilon],
and transform it to the reference interval
[0,1]:

(2.1)

S(z):=z-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sin{2\pi z},\quad z\in[0,1].

One can verify readily that

(2.2)

S(1)=1,\;\;S(0)=S^{\prime}(0)=S^{\prime\prime}(0)=S^{\prime}(1)=S^{\prime\prime}(1)=0,\;\;S(1/2)=1/2,

and
S(z)
is strictly increasing in
(0,1)
.

Another piece of the puzzle is the polynomial:

(2.3)

P(z):=\int_{0}^{z}\tau^{2}(1-\tau)^{2}d\tau\Big/\int_{0}^{1}\tau^{2}(1-\tau)^{2}d\tau=z^{3}(6z^{2}-15z+10),

which also satisfies

(2.4)

P(1)=1,\;\;P(0)=P^{\prime}(0)=P^{\prime\prime}(0)=P^{\prime}(1)=P^{\prime\prime}(1)=0,\;\;P(1/2)=1/2,

and is strictly increasing as
P^{\prime}(z)=30z^{2}(1-z)^{2}
in
(0,1).

We now glue
S
and
P
piece by piece to construct a one-dimensional function that defines the inner functions and the corresponding Kolmogorov maps. To do this, we first lay out the partition and grids as in
[
15
,
28
,
12
]
.
Let
\delta>0
and
h=N\delta.
We also introduce a new parameter
J=\lceil\frac{1}{N\delta}\rceil=\lceil\frac{1}{h}\rceil,
which turns out to be critical to characterize the convergence.
We partition the line by a set of closed sub-intervals of length
(N-1)\delta,
separated by small gaps of length
\delta
with the nodes (see Figure
2.1
(a)):

(2.5)

t_{j}=jh,\quad t_{j}^{\delta}=t_{j}+\delta=jh+\delta.

Correspondingly, we define the
j
-th gap, interval, and block, respectively, as

(2.6)

G^{j}:=[t_{j},t_{j}^{\delta}],\quad I^{j}:=[t_{j}^{\delta},t_{j+1}],\quad B^{j}:=G^{j}\cup I^{j}=[t_{j},t_{j+1}],

and choose the “interpolating” data
\{(t_{j},y_{j}),\;(t_{j}^{\delta},y_{j}^{\delta})\}
with
y
-coordinates:

(2.7)

y_{j}=t_{j}=jh,\quad y_{j}^{\delta}=t_{j}+\delta^{\mu},

where
\mu>0
is a tuning parameter to be specified later. We then define the piecewise function:

(2.8)

\begin{split}\phi(t)\big|_{t\in B^{j}}&=\begin{cases}y_{j}+(y_{j}^{\delta}-y_{j})\,S\big((t-t_{j})/(t_{j}^{\delta}-t_{j})\big),&t\in G^{j},\\[4.0pt]
y_{j}^{\delta}+(y_{j+1}-y_{j}^{\delta})\,P\big((t-t_{j}^{\delta})/(t_{j+1}-t_{j}^{\delta})\big),&t\in I^{j},\end{cases}\\[4.0pt]
&=\begin{cases}jh+\delta^{\mu}\,S\big((t-jh)/\delta\big),&t\in[jh,jh+\delta],\\[4.0pt]
jh+\delta^{\mu}+(h-\delta^{\mu})\,P\big((t-jh-\delta)/(h-\delta)\big),&t\in[jh+\delta,(j+1)h],\end{cases}\end{split}

for
j=0,\pm 1,\ldots
.

The late construction will only involve
(J+1)
blocks with
j\in{\mathcal{J}}:=\{-1,\ldots,J-1\},
so we restrict
\phi(t)
on
\Lambda=[-h,1].

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\phi(t)
be the piecewise function defined in (
2.8
) on
\Lambda=[-h,1].
We have the following properties.

(i)

\phi(t)\in C^{2}(\Lambda)
is strictly increasing on
\Lambda,
and satisfies

(2.9)

\begin{split}\phi(t_{j})=t_{j},\quad\phi(t^{\delta}_{j})=t_{j}+\delta^{\mu},\quad\phi^{\prime}(t_{j})=\phi^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})=\phi^{\prime}(t^{\delta}_{j})=\phi^{\prime\prime}(t^{\delta}_{j})=0.\end{split}

(ii)

There holds

(2.10)

\phi(t)\leq jh+\delta^{\mu},\quad|\phi^{\prime}(t)|\leq 2\,{\delta^{\mu-1}},\quad|\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 2\pi\,{\delta^{\mu-2}},

for all
t\in G^{j}=[jh,jh+\delta],
and

(2.11)

\phi(t)\leq(j+1)h,\quad|\phi^{\prime}(t)|\leq\frac{15}{8}\,\frac{h-\delta^{\mu}}{h-\delta},\quad|\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq\frac{10}{\sqrt{3}}\,\frac{h-\delta^{\mu}}{(h-\delta)^{2}},

for all
t\in I^{j}=[jh+\delta,(j+1)h]
and
j\in{\mathcal{J}}.

Proof.

The property (i) follows from (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) and (
2.8
) straightforwardly. As
S^{\prime}(z),P^{\prime}(z)>0
on
(0,1),
the strict monotonicity of
\phi(t)
on

(ii) Note that

S(z)\in[0,1],\quad S^{\prime}(z)\in[0,2],\quad S^{\prime\prime}(z)\in[-2\pi,2\pi],

so we can derive the bounds in (

Similarly, we can obtain (

\phi((1-q)\delta)=(h-\delta^{\mu})\Big(6\Big(\frac{N-q}{N-1}\Big)^{5}-15\Big(\frac{N-q}{N-1}\Big)^{4}+10\Big(\frac{N-q}{N-1}\Big)^{3}-1\Big),

With the above setup, we define the corresponding Kolmogorov maps (see Brattka

Definition 2.1

(
Kolmogorov maps).

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
be a given sequence of positive numbers, and let
\psi_{q}(t)
be the piecewise
C^{2}
- function defined in (
2.16
). Then we define the Kolmogorov maps
\Psi_{q}:\,[0,1]^{d}\mapsto[0,|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}]
as the superpositions:

(2.20)

\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda}):=\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(x_{p}),

for

where
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}.

Remark 2.1
.

In keeping with the essence of the exact representations in

,

,
4
]
, the inner functions can be generated from a single function through translations and dilations:

(2.21)

\Psi_{l+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\big\{\phi(x_{p}+\alpha l)+\beta_{l}\big\},\quad l=0,1,\ldots,N-1,

where
\alpha=-\delta
and
\beta_{l}=-\phi(-\delta l).
∎

2.2.
Separation properties of Kolmogorov maps

Using the sub-intervals
I_{q}^{j}
in (
2.13
), we form, for each
q
, a set of disjoint hypercubes

(2.22)

\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}:=I_{q}^{j_{1}}\times\ldots\times I_{q}^{j_{d}},\quad\boldsymbol{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}),

with volume
|\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}|=(h-\delta)^{d}.
Denote the coordinate of the center of
\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
be a sequence of rationally independent numbers.
Then the images
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
of the Kolmogorov maps of
\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
in (
2.24
) are all distinct.

Proof.

In fact, it is a consequence of the construction and
[
28
, Lemma 2.1]
:
For any point
\boldsymbol{y}=\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and any finite set of real numbers:
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}.
Except for a nowhere dense set of
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, all sums
\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}y_{j_{p}}
, where
j_{p},p=1,\ldots,n
, are any function with values in the set
1,\ldots,N
, are different from each other and different from
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
.

For each
q,
letting
y_{j_{p}}=\phi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{p}}),
we note that
c_{q}^{j_{p}}\in I_{q}^{j_{p}}
are mapped by the polynomial piece
P
, so
y_{j_{p}}
all rational numbers (see (
2.8
)). Thus,
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
are distinct for fixed
q
. Moreover, we can claim they are different at different levels
l\not=q,
as we can set
c_{l}=a_{l}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
and then use
[
28
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

In practice, there are many choices of such rationally independent
\lambda_{p}
s.
For example, according to Lindeman’s theorem

(
see
[
36
, P.​ 71]
)
, one can choose
\hat{\lambda}_{p}=e^{\theta_{p}},
where
\theta_{p}
are distinct numbers in
[0,1]
, and then set
\lambda_{p}=\hat{\lambda}_{p}/\sum_{j=1}^{d}\hat{\lambda}_{j}
.
This can also ensure
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}:=\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\leq 1
with
\lambda_{p}>0.
∎

Notably, using the expressions of the inner functions, we can evaluate
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
explicitly and show that they are in ascending order along each “row” in each coordinate direction (i.e.,
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=a_{q}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{p},\ldots,j_{d})}
with
j_{p}=-1,\ldots,J-1,
but all other indexes
j_{l}\,(l\not=p)
being fixed, or equivalently, corresponding to the centers

\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=(c_{q}^{j_{1}},\ldots,c_{q}^{j_{p}},\ldots,c_{q}^{j_{d}})
increasing in
x_{p}
-coordinate direction).

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
, we have

(2.25)

\begin{split}a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=h\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}}\lambda_{p}j_{p}+\Big(\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}-\phi((1-q)\delta)\Big)\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}}\lambda_{p}+\sum_{r\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}\lambda_{r},\end{split}

where the index sets of interior centers and rightmost “centers” are

{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}=\{p:0\leq j_{p}\leq J-2,\;1\leq p\leq d\},\quad{\mathcal{J}}_{1}=\{r:j_{r}=J-1,\;c_{q}^{j_{r}}=1,\;1\leq r\leq d\}.

If
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
is the image of any interior center
\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}\in(0,1)^{d}

(
i.e.,
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}={\mathcal{J}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}=\emptyset
)
, then

(2.26)

\begin{split}a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\boldsymbol{j})h+\Big(\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}-\phi((1-q)\delta)\Big)|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}.\end{split}

If
\lambda_{p}>0
and
h\geq\delta^{\mu},\,\mu>0,
then the sequence

\big\{a_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}:=a_{q}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{p-1},k,j_{p+1},\ldots,j_{d})}\big\}_{k=-1}^{J-1}

(
with fixed
j_{l},\,l\not=p
)

is strictly increasing in
k
with the distance

(2.27)

a_{q,k+1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}-a_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}\geq\lambda_{p}\min\Big\{h,\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}\Big\},\quad k=-1,\ldots,J-1.

Proof.

It is clear that for any
j_{l}\in{\mathcal{J}}_{0}:=\{l:j_{l}=-1,\;1\leq l\leq d\},

we have
c_{q}^{j_{l}}=0
and
\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{l}})=0,
while for any
j_{r}\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1},
we have
c_{q}^{j_{r}}=1

and
\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{r}})=1.
Thus, we derive
from Definition
2.1
and (
2.23
)-(
2.24
) that

\begin{split}a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}&=\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{p}})=\sum_{l\in{\mathcal{J}}_{0}}\lambda_{l}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{l}})+\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{p}})+\sum_{r\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}\lambda_{r}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{r}})\\
&=\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{p}})+\sum_{r\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}\lambda_{r}\\
&=\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}}\lambda_{p}\big\{\phi(j_{p}h+(h+\delta)/2)-\phi((1-q)\delta))\big\}+\sum_{r\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}\lambda_{r}\\
&=\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}}\lambda_{p}\Big\{j_{p}h+\delta^{\mu}+\frac{h-\delta^{\mu}}{2}-\phi((1-q)\delta))\Big\}+\sum_{r\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}\lambda_{r}\\
&=h\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}}\lambda_{p}j_{p}+\Big(\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}-\phi((1-q)\delta)\Big)\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}}\lambda_{p}+\sum_{r\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}\lambda_{r},\end{split}

where we used (
2.8
) and
P(1/2)=1/2
in the evaluation of
\phi
at the middle point of
I_{q}^{j_{p}}.
This yields (
2.25
).

If
\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}\subset(0,1)^{d}
(i.e., all
c_{q}^{j_{p}}\in(0,1)
for
0\leq j_{p}\leq J-2
), then
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm in}={\mathcal{J}},\,{\mathcal{J}}_{1}=\emptyset,
so (
2.26
) follows from (
2.25
) by removing its last summation.

We now turn to the proof of the last statement. Along
x=x_{p}
direction with
j_{p}=k,k+1,
we derive from Definition
2.1
and (
2.23
)-(
2.24
) that

\begin{split}a_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}=\lambda_{p}\,\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{k})+\sum_{l\not=p}\lambda_{l}\,\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{l}}),\quad a_{q,k+1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}=\lambda_{p}\,\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{k+1})+\sum_{l\not=p}\lambda_{l}\,\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{l}}),\end{split}

which yields

a_{q,k+1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}-a_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}=\lambda_{p}\,\big(\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{k+1})-\,\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{k})\big).

If
k=-1,
then
\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{-1})=\psi_{q}(0)=0,
so we have

(2.28)

\begin{split}a_{q,0}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}-a_{q,-1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}&=\lambda_{p}\,\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{0})=\lambda_{p}\,\big(\phi((h+\delta)/2)-\phi((1-q)\delta))\big)\\
&=\lambda_{p}\,\Big(\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}-\phi((1-q)\delta)\Big)\\
&\geq\lambda_{p}\,\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}>0,\end{split}

where we have used (
2.18
). If
0\leq k\leq J-3,
then

(2.29)

\begin{split}a_{q,k+1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}-a_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}&=\lambda_{p}\,\big(\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{k+1})-\,\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{k})\big)\\
&=\lambda_{p}\,\big(\phi((k+1)h+(h+\delta)/2)-\phi(kh+(h+\delta)/2))\big)\\
&=\lambda_{p}\,h>0,\end{split}

Finally, if
k=J-2,
then
\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-1})=\psi_{q}(1)=1,
so

(2.30)

\begin{split}a_{q,J-1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}-a_{q,J-2}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}&=\lambda_{p}\,\big(1-\,\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-2})\big)\\
&=\lambda_{p}\,\big(1-\phi((J-2)h+(h+\delta)/2)+\phi((1-q)\delta))\big)\\
&=\lambda_{p}\,\Big(1-(J-2)h-\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}+\phi((1-q)\delta))\Big)\\
&=\lambda_{p}\,\Big(\frac{3h-\delta^{\mu}}{2}+\phi((1-q)\delta))\Big)\\
&\geq\lambda_{p}\,\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}>0,\end{split}

where in the last step, we have used (
2.18
) again. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
h\geq\delta^{\mu},\mu>0
and

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
be given positive real numbers satisfying

(2.31)

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}<\tilde{h}:=\min\Big\{h,\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}\Big\}=\min\Big\{\frac{1}{J},\frac{1}{2J}\Big(1+\frac{J}{(JN)^{\mu}}\Big)\Big\},

for all
j=2,\ldots,d.
Let
\boldsymbol{A}_{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(J+1)^{d}}
be the sequence formed by
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
in row-major order of the corresponding centers
\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
. Then
\boldsymbol{A}_{q}
is a strictly increasing sequence that forms a partition of
\big[0,|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}\big]
with
a_{q}^{(-1,\ldots,-1)}=0
and
a_{q}^{(J-1,\ldots,J-1)}=|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}
.

Proof.

To fix the idea, we first consider
d=2.
Then the row-major ordering of
a_{q}^{(j_{1},j_{2})}
gives

(2.32)

\begin{split}&{\boldsymbol{A}_{q}}=\big(\underbrace{a_{q}^{(-1,-1)},\cdots,a_{q}^{(J-1,-1)}}_{j_{2}=-1},\,\underbrace{a_{q}^{(-1,0)},\cdots,a_{q}^{(J-1,0)}}_{j_{2}=0},\cdots,\underbrace{a_{q}^{(-1,J-1)},\cdots,a_{q}^{(J-1,J-1)}}_{j_{2}=J-1}\big).\end{split}

We know from Lemma (
2.2
) that for each row with fixed
j_{2}
, the entries are strictly increasing, so it suffices to ensure the tails and heads of
(J+1)
rows satisfying
a_{q}^{(J-1,j_{2})}<a_{q}^{(-1,j_{2})}.
We calculate directly using Definition
2.1
and (
2.18
) that
for
j_{2}=-1,

\begin{split}a_{q}^{(-1,0)}-a_{q}^{(J-1,-1)}&=\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(0)+\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{0})-(\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(1)+\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(0))\\
&=\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{0})-\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\,\big(\phi((h+\delta)/2)-\phi((1-q)\delta))\big)-\lambda_{1}\\
&\geq\lambda_{2}\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}-\lambda_{1},\end{split}

and for
1\leq j_{2}\leq J-3,

\begin{split}a_{q}^{(-1,j_{2}+1)}-a_{q}^{(J-1,j_{2})}&=(\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{-1})+\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{2}+1}))-(\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-1})+\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{2}}))\\
&=\lambda_{1}(\psi_{q}(0)-\psi_{q}(1))+\lambda_{2}(\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{2}+1})-\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{2}}))\\
&=\lambda_{2}h-\lambda_{1}.\end{split}

Similarly, for
j_{2}=J-2,

\begin{split}a_{q}^{(-1,J-1)}-a_{q}^{(J-1,J-2)}&=\lambda_{1}(\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{-1})-\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-1}))+\lambda_{2}(\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-1})-\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-2}))\\
&=-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}(\psi_{q}(1)-\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-2}))\geq\lambda_{2}\frac{h+\delta^{\mu}}{2}-\lambda_{1},\end{split}

where we have used (
2.30
). Thus, we have

a_{q}^{(J-1,j_{2})}<a_{q}^{(-1,j_{2})},
if
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\tilde{h}.

Similarly, in 3D, we have

\begin{split}{\boldsymbol{A}_{q}}&=\big(\underbrace{a_{q}^{(-1,-1,-1)},\cdots,a_{q}^{(J-1,-1,-1)}}_{j_{2}=j_{3}=-1},\,\underbrace{a_{q}^{(-1,0,-1)},\cdots,a_{q}^{(J-1,0,-1)}}_{j_{2}=0,j_{3}=-1},\cdots,\\
&\quad\;\;\underbrace{a_{q}^{(-1,J-1,J-1)},\cdots,a_{q}^{(J-1,J-1,J-1)}}_{j_{2}=j_{3}=J-1}\big)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(J+1)^{3}}.\end{split}

For fixed
j_{3},
we take any

j_{2}\in\{-1,\ldots,J-2\}
and calculate

\begin{split}&a_{q}^{(-1,j_{2}+1,j_{3})}-a_{q}^{(J-1,j_{2},j_{3})}\\
&=\big(\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{-1})+\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{2}+1})+\lambda_{3}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{3}})\big)-\big(\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-1})+\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{2}})+\lambda_{3}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{3}})\big)\\
&=-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\big(\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{2}+1})-\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{2}})\big)=a_{q}^{(-1,j_{2}+1)}-a_{q}^{(J-1,j_{2})},\end{split}

which reduces to 2D case, leading to the condition:
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\tilde{h}.
We now take care of the third index
j_{3}
and find

\begin{split}&a_{q}^{(-1,-1,j_{3}+1)}-a_{q}^{(J-1,J-1,j_{3})}\\
&=\big(\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{-1})+\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{-1})+\lambda_{3}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{3}+1})\big)-\big(\lambda_{1}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-1})+\lambda_{2}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{J-1})+\lambda_{3}\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{3}})\big)\\
&=-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+\lambda_{3}\big(\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{3}+1})-\psi_{q}(c_{q}^{j_{3}})\big),\end{split}

which is positive, if
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}\tilde{h}.
This completes the proof of 3D case.

For
d\geq 3,
we just need to verify that under the condition (
2.31
), the tails and heads of the rows satisfying

(2.33)

\begin{cases}a_{q}^{(J-1,j_{2},\cdots,j_{d})}<a_{q}^{(-1,j_{2}+1,\cdots,j_{d})},&j_{2}=-1,\ldots,J-2,\\
a_{q}^{(J-1,J-1,j_{3},\cdots,j_{d})}<a_{q}^{(-1,-1,j_{3}+1,\cdots,j_{d})},&j_{3}=-1,\ldots,J-2,\\
\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
a_{q}^{(J-1,J-1,J-1,\cdots,J-1,j_{d})}<a_{q}^{(-1,-1,-1,\cdots,-1,j_{d}+1)},&j_{d}=-1,\ldots,J-2,\end{cases}

where the other indices are fixed, unless otherwise specified. This can be shown similarly to the previous two cases.

By definition, we can easily verify
a_{q}^{(-1,\ldots,-1)}=0
and
a_{q}^{(J-1,\ldots,J-1)}=|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}.

Denote by

\boldsymbol{A}_{q}
for

Formally, we have the flow of the multi-dimensional sampled data:

\underbrace{\big\{\big(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}},b_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})/N\big)\big\}}_{\text{Sampling via}\;\eqref{aqJaj2}}\;\;{\longrightarrow}\;\;\underbrace{\big\{\big(a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}};\boldsymbol{\lambda}),b_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}\big)\big\}}_{\text{Encoding via}\;\eqref{aqJaj}}\;\longrightarrow\;\underbrace{\big\{\big(\boldsymbol{A}_{q},\boldsymbol{B}_{q}\big)\big\}}_{\text{Row-major order}},

Definition 2.2

(
Row-wise interpolation).

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
be a given sequence of positive real numbers that are
rationally independent or satisfy (
2.31
) with
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}\leq 1
.
Then, for any fixed level
1\leq q\leq N,
we extract the data for interpolation at each row

(2.38)

\tau_{k}:=a_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}=a_{q}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{p-1},k,j_{p+1},\ldots,j_{d})},\quad s_{k}:=b_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}=b_{q}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{p-1},k,j_{p+1},\ldots,j_{d})},

for all
-1\leq k\leq J-1,
and fixed
j_{l}\in\{-1,\ldots,J-1\}
with
l\not=p.
Then we interpolate
\big\{(t_{k},s_{k})\big\}_{k=-1}^{J-1}
using the smeared-out Heaviside function
S
defined in (
2.1
):

(2.39)

g_{q}^{j_{p}}(t)=s_{k}+(s_{k+1}-s_{k})S\big((t-\tau_{k})/(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})\big),\quad t\in T_{k}:=[\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}],

for
k=-1,\ldots,J-2.

Built on this concept, we can construct the outer functions accordingly by associating every
\boldsymbol{x}
with a hypercube, a row, and then a piece of
g_{q}^{j_{p}}(t).
More precisely, for any
\boldsymbol{x}\in[0,1]^{d},
it falls in either a hypercube or a gap:
(i)
\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}}
for some
\boldsymbol{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}),

or (ii)
\boldsymbol{x}
in
a gap that is closest to
\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}},
i.e.,

(2.40)

\boldsymbol{i}=\underset{\boldsymbol{j}\in\mathcal{J}}{\rm argmin}\,\big|\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda})-\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}};\boldsymbol{\lambda})\big|=\underset{\boldsymbol{j}\in\mathcal{J}}{\rm argmin}\,\big|\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda})-a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}\big|,

with the corresponding
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}}
(note: if
\boldsymbol{i}
is not unique, it is free to choose any index).
Then we can locate its row and neighboring points (on the left or right in the row) for interpolation through

(2.41)

a_{q}^{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{l-1},i_{l}+\sigma^{*},i_{l+1},\ldots,i_{d})}=\min_{1\leq k\leq d}\big\{\big|a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}}-a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}+\boldsymbol{e}_{k}}\big|,\,\big|a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}}-a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}-\boldsymbol{e}_{k}}\big|\big\},

where
\boldsymbol{e}_{k}
is the
k

\sigma^{*}\in\{1,-1\}.
Then we can identify the sub-interval
T_{k}
for interpolation and construct the composition via (
2.39
):
(g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q})(\boldsymbol{x})=(g_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}_{l}}\circ\Psi_{q})(\boldsymbol{x}).
Then we can evaluate the approximate superposition at every
\boldsymbol{x}\in[0,1]^{d}
via

(2.42)

f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})=f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\sum_{q=1}^{N}g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(x_{p}).

\boldsymbol{A}_{q}

2.4.
Summary of the constructive algorithm

For clarity, we summarise below the above construction and evaluate
f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}),\boldsymbol{x}\in[0,1]^{d}.

Algorithm 1

Constructive Algorithm

I.  Inputs:

(i) Choose a function to approximate:
f(\boldsymbol{x})\in C([0,1]^{d})
with
d\geq 2.

(ii) Select an integer
N\geq 2d+1
and a real
\delta>0
. Set
h\leftarrow N\delta
such that
J\leftarrow 1/h
is an integer. Choose
\mu>0
such that
h\geq\delta^{\mu}.

(iii) Input a sequence of real positive numbers
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
that are rationally independent or satisfy (
2.31
).

II.  Inner Functions and Kolmogorov Maps:
For
q=1,\ldots,N,

(i) Compute the inner functions
\psi_{q}(x)
via (
2.16
) for given
\phi(t)
in (
2.8
).

(ii) Compute the Kolmogorov maps:
\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})=\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(x_{p}).

III.  Outer functions:
For
q=1,\ldots,N,

(i) Form the hypercubes
C_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
with the centers
\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
as in (
2.22
)-(
2.23
). Sample the data
\{(a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}},b_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})\}
at the centers via
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}};\boldsymbol{\lambda})
and
b_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})/N.

(ii) For any
\boldsymbol{x}\in[0,1]^{d},
locate the hypercube or the closest hypercube if it falls in a gap by finding the closest center’s image
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}}
:

\boldsymbol{i}=\underset{\boldsymbol{j}\in\mathcal{J}}{\rm argmin}\,\big|\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda})-\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}};\boldsymbol{\lambda})\big|=\underset{\boldsymbol{j}\in\mathcal{J}}{\rm argmin}\,\big|\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda})-a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}\big|,

with the corresponding
a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}}.
Then we can locate its row and neighboring point for interpolation through:

a_{q}^{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{l-1},i_{l}+\sigma^{*},i_{l+1},\ldots,i_{d})}=\min_{1\leq k\leq d}\big\{\big|a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}}-a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}+\boldsymbol{e}_{k}}\big|,\,\big|a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}}-a_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}-\boldsymbol{e}_{k}}\big|\big\},

where there exists a unique
\sigma^{*}\in\{1,-1\}.
Then we can identify the sub-interval
T_{k}
for interpolation and construct the composition via (
2.39
):
(g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q})(\boldsymbol{x})=(g_{q}^{\boldsymbol{i}_{l}}\circ\Psi_{q})(\boldsymbol{x}).

IV.  Output:

Evaluate
f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})
by superpositions:
f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{q=1}^{N}g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}).

3.
Main approximation result and numerical verifications

In this section, we present the main result and its proof, and also provide some numerical results to verify the convergence order.

3.1.
Main result

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
f\in C([0,1]^{d})
is an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous function such that

(3.1)

|f(\boldsymbol{x})-f(\boldsymbol{y})|\leq C_{\alpha}\,\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}^{\alpha},\quad\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in[0,1]^{d},\;\;\;0<\alpha\leq 1,

where
\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{2}
is the length of the vector
\boldsymbol{z}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
be a given sequence of positive real numbers that are
rationally independent or satisfy (
2.31
) with
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}\leq 1
, and
let
\{\psi_{q}\}
and
\{g_{q}\}
be the inner and outer functions constructed in Section
2
.
Then for
h\geq\delta^{\mu}
with
\mu>0,
the approximate superpositions

(3.2)

f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})=f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\sum_{q=1}^{N}g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\psi_{q}(x_{p}),

has the approximability

(3.3)

\|f-f_{N}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{d^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{2^{\alpha}}\Big)h^{\alpha}=C_{\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{d^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{2^{\alpha}}\Big)\frac{1}{J^{\alpha}},

where
h=N\delta
and
J=\lceil\frac{1}{h}\rceil.

Proof.

By definition, each
t\in[0,1]
is covered by the intervals of
\{L_{q}\}_{q=1}^{N}
in (
2.14
) at least
(N-1)
times, but not covered by at most one value of
q.
Consequently, each
\boldsymbol{x}\in[0,1]^{d}
is covered by a cube

\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
for at least
(N-d)
different values of
q
, but not covered by at most
d
values of
q
.
Then we can split the point-wise error into

(3.4)

\displaystyle|f(\boldsymbol{x})-f_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})|

\displaystyle=\Big|f(\boldsymbol{x})-\sum_{q=1}^{N}g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{q=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})}{N}-g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big)\Big|\leq\sum_{q=1}^{N}\Big|\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})}{N}-g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in{\mathcal{Q}}}\Big|\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})}{N}-g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big|+\sum_{q\not\in{\mathcal{Q}}}\Big|\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})}{N}-g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big|,

where
{\mathcal{Q}}
denotes the set of at least
(N-d)
values of
q
for which the hypercubes
\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
cover the point
\boldsymbol{x}.

Note that if
q\in{\mathcal{Q}},
i.e.,
\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
with the center
\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
for some
\boldsymbol{j}

3.1

(3.5)

|f(\boldsymbol{x})-f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})|\leq C_{\alpha}\,\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}\|^{\alpha}_{2}\leq C_{\alpha}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{d}(N-1)\delta}{2}\Big)^{\alpha},

otherwise if
q\notin{\mathcal{Q}},
i.e.,

\boldsymbol{x}
belongs to a gap closest to
a hypercube
\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}:

(3.6)

|f(\boldsymbol{x})-f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})|\leq C_{\alpha}\,\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}\|^{\alpha}_{2}\leq C_{\alpha}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{d}N\delta}{2}\Big)^{\alpha}.

We now estimate the first term in (
3.4
). As
\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{C}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}
and
b_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}}=\frac{f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})}{N}
, we derive from (
2.39
) and (
3.5

(3.7)

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})}{N}&-g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big|\leq\Big|\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})}{N}-\frac{f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})}{N}\Big|+\Big|\frac{f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})}{N}-g_{q}\circ\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big|\leq\frac{1}{N}\big|{f(\boldsymbol{x})}-{f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})}\big|\\[4.0pt]
&\quad+\big|(s_{k+1}-s_{k})S\big((\Psi_{q}(\boldsymbol{x})-\tau_{k})/(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k})\big)\big|\\[4.0pt]
&\leq\frac{1}{N}\big|{f(\boldsymbol{x})}-{f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q}^{\boldsymbol{j}})}\big|+|s_{k+1}-s_{k}|\\[4.0pt]
&\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{N}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{d}(N-1)\delta}{2}\Big)^{\alpha}+\frac{C_{\alpha}}{N}(N\delta)^{\alpha}\\[4.0pt]
&\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{N}\Big(1+\frac{d^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{2^{\alpha}}\Big)(N\delta)^{\alpha},\end{split}

where we used
S(z)\in[0,1]
(note:
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|_{1}\leq 1

3.1

(3.8)

\begin{split}|s_{k+1}-s_{k}|&=|b_{q,k+1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}-b_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}|=\frac{1}{N}\big|f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q,k+1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}})-f(\boldsymbol{c}_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}})\big|\\[4.0pt]
&\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{N}\big\|\boldsymbol{c}_{q,k+1}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}-\boldsymbol{c}_{q,k}^{\boldsymbol{j},j_{p}}\big\|_{2}^{\alpha}\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{N}(N\delta)^{\alpha}.\end{split}

We now turn to the second term in (
3.4

In this case,
q\notin{\mathcal{Q}},

3.3.
Concluding remarks and discussions

It is well known that the univariate functions involved in the exact Kolmogorov–Arnold representation lack differentiability and smoothness, often exhibiting “wild” behavior—even when the target function

f(\boldsymbol{x})
is smooth. This significantly hinders the practical application of this mathematically elegant theory. It was suggested that one should give up the exact representation and use an approximate one instead. However, the explicit construction of a smooth approximation version is still open (see
[
6
]
and
[
34
, Sec. 2.4]
). This work closes this gap by explicitly constructing an approximate
C^{2}
-version.

We emphasize that our construction essentially relies on the “magic” shape functions defined in (
2.1
)–(
2.3
), which possess desirable properties enabling the construction of
C^{2}
, strictly increasing inner functions. Their discovery seems to be a fortunate accident, and it remains unclear whether
C^{3}
or smoother shape functions exist.

We reiterate that the resulting universal Kolmogorov maps allow us to explicitly study the separation of multi-dimensional grids mapped to one dimension, as well as various other properties that have not been quantitatively investigated in existing constructions. It also informs the row-wise interpolation strategy used in the explicit construction of the outer functions, which, to the best of our knowledge, has not been explored in the existing literature.

As aforementioned, one implication of this approximate version is to serve as an intermediate tool to construct networks and study their
convergence. For example, we consider the general approximators (see
[
45
]
)

(3.12)

f_{N}^{\Theta}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{q=1}^{N}\tilde{g}_{q}\circ\sum_{p=1}^{d}\lambda_{p}\tilde{\psi}_{p}(x_{p}),\quad\forall\,\tilde{g}_{q}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{M_{g}}(I),\;\forall\,\tilde{\psi}_{p}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{M_{\psi}}(I),

where the ansatz spaces
{\mathcal{A}}_{M_{g}}(I)
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{M_{\psi}}(I)
with
I:=[0,1]

are chosen to be dense subsets of the spaces of continuous functions
C(I).
Here,
\Theta
denotes the set of all involved parameters in the approximators. This two-block structure follows the spirit of K
\dot{\rm u}
rková’s work
[
17
]
and inspired the development of KKANs in
[
45
]
, which enjoy universal approximability and exhibit rich learning dynamics.
However, as the one-dimensional functions in the KART
lack differentiability and smoothness, it is still open to prove the convergence of KKANs. With this approximate version, we can formally write

(3.13)

\|f-f_{N}^{\Theta}\|_{\infty}\leq\|f-f_{N}\|_{\infty}+\|f_{N}-f_{N}^{\Theta}\|_{\infty},

which may allow us to demonstrate convergence. We shall report this in future work.

As a final remark, Kolmogorov–Arnold representations have a profound influence on both the theoretical development and practical implementation of neural networks.
For instance, the neural network constructions in
[
34
,
29
,
35
]
rely on
\delta
-shifting architectures (see Figure
2.1
(a) and Figure
2.2
), while the analytical framework in the recent work
[
10
]
is grounded in Kolmogorov–Arnold representation theory (KART). In contrast, our construction takes a fundamentally different route, offering fresh perspectives on the theoretical underpinnings of deep neural networks. In particular, it shows strong potential for establishing a rigorous foundation for the state-of-the-art KANs
[
25
]
.

Return to routine household and community functions following uncomplicated caesarean delivery: ultra-short versus traditional hospital-stay; a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Nuvie Oyeyemi, Uche Onwudiegwu, Dibo Pughikumo, Abisoye Oyeyemi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCaesarean section is currently the commonest major surgical procedure in obstetrics, and duration of admission after this procedure varies widely among centres. There is a need to review the psychosocial and economic implications of recovery after Caesarean delivery, especially concerning return to household and community function.AimTo determine the time of return to routine household and community functions; in the ultra-short stay post-operative management protocol, after uncomplicated Caesarean section; and compare with that of the traditional protocol.MethodsA randomized controlled trial done in 3 centres [Federal Medical Centre Yenagoa (FMC-Y); Diete-Koki Memorial Hospital (DKMH); and Niger Delta University Teaching Hospital (NDUTH)] in Bayelsa state, South-South Nigeria, from April 2024 to April 2025. A total of 179 women who just had uncomplicated Caesarean delivery were randomized into the ultra-short stay (90) and traditional (89) groups. Recruitment was done in the recovery room immediately after uncomplicated Caesarean section. Women in the ultra-short stay (discharged home at 36–48 h post-operation) had minty chewing gums (‘menthos’) to chew for two hours within the first six hours after surgery; commenced ambulation, oral intake, and had their urethral catheters removed at 6–12 h post-operation. The traditional group (discharged home at 3–5 days post-operation) had conventional care (ambulation, initiation of oral intake and removal of urethral catheter at 12–24 h). Participants’ time of return to routine domestic/household and societal/community functions were recorded during the follow-up period. Data was analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25.ResultsThe mean number of days to return to routine household functions was 32.3 and 22.2 days, and for return to routine community functions was 79.8 and 40.9 days in the traditional and ultra-short stay protocols respectively. The ultra-short stay group had significantly quicker return to household functions (t = 2.32, p < 0.001), and community functions (t = 7.75, p < 0.001) than the traditional protocol group.ConclusionWomen who had the ultra-short post-operative management protocol following uncomplicated Caesarean delivery returned to household and community functions faster than those who had the traditional hospital-stay protocol. Hospitals (especially in low resource settings) may adopt the ultra-short post-operative management protocol after uncomplicated Caesarean delivery, to enhance quicker return to function.Trial registrationPan African Clinical trials Registry (PACTR), unique identification number PACTR202509662932971, 11 September, 2025.

Background

Caesarean section is one of the oldest procedures, and the commonest major obstetric surgical procedure currently done globally, and there are trends towards increasing Caesarean section rates worldwide [1]. Though over the years it has become a relatively safe procedure, there still remain concerns about it and a cultural aversion for it; with associated misgivings and misconceptions, especially in low-middle income countries (LMIC) like Nigeria [1, 2]. Prominent among these concerns are the exaggerated assumptions that there is always lengthening of hospital stay after Caesarean delivery compared to vaginal delivery, and an undue incapacitation associated with the procedure [1–4]. This is especially a relevant concern among the womenfolk who play important and prominent roles in the household where they supervise and are involved with most domestic activities [1, 2, 4]. Societal roles are also not excluded from these concerns. It is therefore important to examine post-operative management protocols that can reduce length of hospital stay and review their effects on the return to domestic and societal functions following uncomplicated Caesarean delivery, and possibly make the operation more acceptable when indicated [1, 2]. The psychosocial implication of the length of hospital stay following uncomplicated Caesarean delivery is also very important [1, 3]. Most studies have not detected an increased rate of morbidity associated with early discharge following Caesarean delivery [4–6]. Therefore, protocols that reduce duration of hospital stay following Caesarean delivery, will probably also enhance patients’ timely return to domestic and societal functions, and this may also help to allay the concerns about the perceived compulsory prolonged hospital stay after every Caesarean delivery and therefore make the operation more acceptable, when indicated; especially in Sub-Saharan Africa where there still exists some aversion for Caesarean delivery [1].

Recovery after any major surgical procedure is gradual and varies with different factors like individual intrinsic factors, type of anaesthesia and analgesia, type and extent of surgery, surgical techniques and skill of surgeon, duration of surgery, presence/absence of surgical complications, and very importantly the post-operative management protocols like early ambulation, early feeding and perhaps length of hospital stay. Though many guidelines suggest that full recovery after a Caesarean delivery may take 4 to 6 weeks, it varies with individuals [7–9].

Surgery is a known physiologic stress, and preoperative preparation, the intra-operative procedure and very importantly, postoperative rehabilitation, are other important factors affecting the health, well-being and return to function of women after Caesarean delivery [7–9].

Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols are evidence-based perioperative care models designed to minimize the physiological impact of surgery, accelerate recovery, reduce complication rates, and shorten hospital stays [9–11]. Some of the underlying principles in these protocols are adopted in preparing the ultra-short stay protocol in this study. These multidisciplinary strategies rely on standardized clinical pathways, supported by regular auditing and coordinated teamwork across specialties [9–11]. Recently, ERAS principles have been adapted for obstetric care, particularly in the context of Caesarean deliveries [9–12]. Early studies exploring Enhanced Recovery After Caesarean (ERAC) protocols have demonstrated promising outcomes, particularly in elective surgical cases [9, 12].

The aim of the study is to determine and compare the time (in number of days) it takes to return to routine household and community functions following uncomplicated Caesarean delivery, between women who had the ultra-short stay and those who had the traditional post-operative management protocol. Also to determine other factors which may be associated with the time of return to household and community functions following uncomplicated Caesarean delivery.

Methods

This was a multi-centre randomized controlled trial, of the parallel design with allocation ratio of 1:1 [13], non-inferiority, open-label study carried out from April 2024 to April 2025, among booked antenatal patients in the Federal Medical Centre, Yenagoa (FMC-Y); the Niger Delta University Teaching Hospital (NDUTH), Okolobiri; the Diete-Koki Memorial Hospital (DKMH), Opolo; all in Bayelsa state, South-south Nigeria. Patients who just had uncomplicated Caesarean delivery for various indications, and were fully awake in the recovery room were recruited into the study. Women excluded were those outside the reproductive age group which is 15–49 years; [14] those who had any medical condition (like poorly controlled diabetes in pregnancy, severe chronic hypertension and sickle cell disease) that required management by other specialities post delivery that may warrant prolonged hospital stay; immunosuppressed like HIV positive women with features of the Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome(AIDS); and women who had prolonged labour or prolonged rupture of membranes. Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the Research Ethics Committee of the Federal Medical Centre, Yenagoa. Protocol number: 716. Retrospective registration of the trial was done by the Pan African Clinical trials Registry (PACTR), unique identification number PACTR202509662932971, 11 September, 2025.

A total of 180 eligible consenting patients were consecutively recruited into the study. Randomization was done using computer generated random numbers (unstratified balanced allocation from WinPepi) [15] and random allocation concealment was done using sealed opaque envelopes numbered from 1 to 60 for each centre. In this way, the randomization sequence was followed consecutively and recruited patients were either of two groups representing the arms of the study: group A (traditional protocol) and group B (ultra-short stay protocol) (Fig. 1). An independent observer picked the envelopes consecutively at the time the eligible patient got to the recovery room after an uncomplicated Caesarean delivery, A total of 90 women were allocated into the ultra-short stay group and 89 (one of the women inadvertently allocated here in one of the centres had a iatrogenic bladder injury intra-operatively, so she was subsequently excluded reducing the participants to 89) into the traditional group. The sealed envelope picked was placed within the patients’ case notes. All the surgeries were performed by Consultants and experienced Senior Registrars in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology of the respective hospitals, who were blinded to any allocations. The independent observers who recorded the outcomes were also blinded to the groups of the participants at the time of the recording. Data was recorded in validated combined proformas and interviewer-administered questionaires. Validation was done through a pretest using 20 (randomly allocated 10 per group) consecutive participants in a private hospital, who just had uncomplicated Caesarean delivery which was defined as a Caesarean delivery in which there was no occurrence of an untoward condition or event intra-operative or immediate post-operative which may necessitate the performance of a more complex procedure or a change in management protocol.

At recruitment in the recovery room, the women in the ultra-short stay group were given minty (‘menthos’) chewing gums to chew for at least 2 h within the first 6 h of surgery, and the appropriate sections of the proforma and interviewer-administered questionnaire (containing patient biodata and intra-operative information) were filled. The protocol (specific interventions) for the ultra-short stay protocol was placed at the nursing station on the ward for attention of the nursing staff. Those for the traditional protocol (group A) were managed according to conventional unit protocol.

All patients in group A were commenced on graded oral sips at 24 h post-operation and regular diet 12 h later; ambulated within 24 h (sitting out of bed from 12 h and walking around ward from 24 h) post-operation and had their urethral catheters (with strict urinary output monitoring) removed 12–24 h after surgery. Intravenous fluids dextrose water alternating with normal saline was given at 1 L 8 hourly for at least the first 24 h after surgery and until oral intake was well established. Analgesics, intramuscular pentazocine at 30 mg 6 hourly, suppository diclofenac 100 mg 12 hourly for 72 h and intramuscular paracetamol 600-900 mg 8 hourly were administered for the first 24 h after surgery. Antibiotics, usually intravenous ceftriaxone, genticin and metronidazole at 1 g 12 hourly, 80 mg 8 hourly and 500 mg 8 hourly respectively were also given for the first 48 to 72 h. Antibiotics were converted to their oral equivalents when the intravenous regimen had been completed and analgesics to oral paracetamol 1 g 8 hourly, and diclofenac 50-100 mg 12 hourly respectively. Their wounds were inspected and exposed on the fifth post-operative day and the patients subsequently discharged home.

All patients in group B commenced graded oral fluids by six hours post-operation, gradually graded to semi-solids, then intravenous fluids were discontinued; and regular diet was commenced at 12 h post-operation. They sat out of bed from 6 h after surgery and walked around the ward from 12 h following delivery. Their urethral catheters were discontinued 6–12 h after surgery. Analgesics, intramuscular pentazocine at 30 mg 6 hourly, suppository diclofenac 100 mg 12 hourly until discharge and intramuscular paracetamol 600-900 mg 8 hourly were administered for the first 24 h after surgery. Antibiotics, intravenous ceftriaxone, genticin and metronidazole, at 1 g 12 hourly, 80 mg 8 hourly and 500 mg 8 hourly respectively were also converted to the oral equivalents at discharge and the analgesics changed to oral paracetamol 1 g 8 hourly and diclofenac 50-100 mg 12 hourly respectively. Their wounds were inspected and exposed on the second post-operative day (36–48 h post-operative) and the patients subsequently discharged home on the same day without schedule for home visits, but with customer care lines to call if the need arises. The wounds were cleaned with methylated spirit in both groups, and the patients were instructed to clean their wounds with methylated spirit twice to thrice daily at home until the two weeks postnatal visit.

The part of the proforma and interviewer-administered questionnaires on time (in number of days) of return to any one of the routine household functions (bathing and changing baby’s diapers, house-keeping chores, and/or cooking) and community functions (going shopping in the market/shopping mall, social visits/functions, long-distance travel, and/or return to work) was filled by assigned unit Doctors at the follow-up visits (two weeks and six weeks) and through phone calls by the the principal investigator, afterwards. The time of return to function was determined at the time the participant commenced performance of at least one of the functions listed for each outcome variable.

Data analysis was done using the IBM SPSS statistical software version 25. Data was presented in text and tables. Analysis included use of descriptive statistics such as mean, proportion and standard deviation to summarize the quantitative variables. Association between categorical variables was tested using Chi-square and differences in group means were assessed using t-test. A confidence level of 95% was used with the level of significance set at a p-value of < 0.05.

Results

There were 179 women who had uncomplicated Caesarean delivery, who were randomized into 90 women for the ultra-short stay post-operative management protocol and 89 for the traditional protocol. Eight women dropped out of the protocols, two in the traditional group (one had severe post-partum haemorrhage, acute kidney injury and had prolonged hospital stay afterwards; the other had voiding difficulties at removal of urethral catheter which resulted in re-catheterization and prolonged hospital stay). Six women in the ultra-short stay protocol group, dropped out of the protocol; reasons ranging from post-operative blood transfusions, signs of wound sepsis at wound exposure, to post-partum pregnancy-induced hypertension, all extending hospital stay.

Most of the participants were married women (93.3% in each group respectively) from ages 19 to 45 years. The 2 groups were comparable in age, parity, marital status, religion, tribe, except educational status where there were significantly more educated women in the ultra-short stay group than the traditional protocol group (X2 = 7.82, P-value = 0.020). Indications for Caesarean delivery and estimated gestational age at delivery variables had no statistically significant difference in these characteristics between the 2 groups. Most of the intra-operative variables also had no statistically significant difference between the two groups, except cadre of surgeon, where more Consultants did the surgeries in the ultra-short stay protocol group (Table 1), though the Senior Registrars who did the surgeries were ones who had experience that could be comparable to the Consultants. The Surgeons were Consultants in 84.4% and 62.9% ; Senior Registrars in 37.1% and 15.6% of the participants in the ultra-short stay and traditional groups respectively (X2 = 10.71, p-value = 0.001).

Discussion

This randomized controlled trial evaluated return to routine household/domestic functions, and community/societal participation among women discharged early (36–48 h) following uncomplicated Caesarean section compared with those managed under the traditional hospital-stay of 3–5 days after delivery, which is the current conventional practice in the centres used. The primary findings revealed that women in the ultra-short stay group exhibited significantly faster resumption of routine household/domestic tasks, and swifter re-engagement in routine community roles than their counterparts in the traditional hospital-stay group.

The study offers critical insights into the effectiveness and acceptability of early discharge protocols following Caesarean section, with implications for optimizing maternal recovery, patient-centred care, and resource utilization in similar healthcare settings. These findings could support evidence-based review of post-Caesarean management practices in low-resource environments.

The study’s results extend these observations by demonstrating that the return to functional roles within households and communities was notably expedited among women discharged within 36–48 h. Early discharge likely reduced the physical and psychological burden associated with prolonged hospitalization, including sleep disruption, reduced autonomy, and separation from familiar environments. These factors can hinder early post-surgical recovery and delay functional reintegration. In contrast, being at home may encourage mothers to resume routine tasks under supportive conditions, increasing both confidence and perceived competence [1, 17].

There is growing evidence that shortened postpartum hospital stays after uncomplicated Caesarean sections can be safe and feasible especially when combined with adequate discharge planning and possible home follow-up [4, 17]. Some studies have explored discharge at even earlier time points (like 24 to 36 h) and reported equivalent return to function outcomes and satisfaction by six weeks postpartum compared with day two discharge, suggesting that carefully selected women can safely benefit from accelerated timeliness of discharge without compromising key health outcomes [4, 17].

Shorter hospital stays combined with effective discharge planning and home with community support services can potentially enhance maternal return to domestic and societal life without compromising safety or quality of care. Indeed, early reintegration into household roles may improve maternal autonomy, mental well-being, and overall quality of life postpartum [1, 9].

This study provides compelling evidence that adopting an ultra-short hospital stay protocol defined as discharge within 36 to 48 h following uncomplicated Caesarean delivery is associated with a significantly faster return to both household and community-level functioning when compared with the traditional post-operative management approach. The study also reveals that surgical techniques and perhaps cadre of surgeons may have associations with return to household and community functions after uncomplicated Caesarean section. This is comparable to previous studies that have associated more experienced surgeons, shorter duration of surgery and transverse supra pubic incisions to shorter hospital-stay, with quicker recovery and return to function [1–3]. Furthermore, another study stated that delivery by an inexperienced surgeon was associated with adverse post-operative outcomes like increased risk of post-operation infections, which can affect hospital-stay [18].

Other factors such as the psycho-social environment at home, including family support and some cultural practices, may substantially influence the pace of recovery, with supportive households potentially facilitating faster return to function and community engagement, while too many domestic helps may delay return to domestic functions. Community beliefs and norms around postpartum rest and role expectations also affect maternal recovery trajectories. In a few contexts, societal structures may support earlier re-integration into domestic duties, whereas in most others, traditional and religious postpartum confinement practices could limit early return to community functions, especially in the presence of adequate home support, and misgivings that early return to function can cause delayed/long-term complications. The findings of this trial highlight the interplay between institutional protocols, surgical techniques and local sociocultural dynamics.

The limitations of this study include its focus on uncomplicated Caesarean delivery which limits generalizability to high-risk populations or emergency Caesarean sections. Although discharge timing was randomized, it is possible that psycho-social factors unmeasured in this study influenced outcomes. The availability and quality of home and community support services likely moderated recovery and function, which may vary across different systems and cultural settings, which was not considered in this study. Also neonatal well-being and outcomes not considered in this study may affect the time of return to household and community functions.

Conclusion

The ultra-short hospital stay post-operative management protocol produced significantly faster return to household and community functions than the traditional protocol. Therefore enhanced recovery and early discharge after Caesarean delivery causes a faster re-integration of the patient into her domestic and societal roles.

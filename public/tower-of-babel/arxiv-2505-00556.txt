1.
Introduction

In this paper we prove several results concerning the “size” of subgroups and subrings of the reals. Here by “size”, we typically refer to the Hausdorff measure and dimension of such objects. The problem about the “sizes” of certain subgroups, subrings and subfields of the reals has a rich history. The story begins with a classical result in real analysis.

Theorem 1.1

(Steinhaus
[
18
]
)
.

Suppose
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is Lebesgue measurable and has positive measure. Then the difference set
A-A:=\{x-y:x,y\in A\}
contains a ball with positive radius whose center is at the origin.

The following follows immediately.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
A
is a Lebesgue measurable proper subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
(view it as an additive group), then
A
has Lebesgue measure zero.

1

1

In this paper, we always call a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is
null
if
A
has Lebesgue measure zero.

Subsequently, Volkmann and Erdős initiated the study of the dimension of subgroups/rings of the reals in the 1960s.
In
[
20

they showed that for each
\alpha\in[0,1]
, there is a Borel additive subgroup of

\mathbb{R}
with Hausdorff dimension
\alpha

[
3

[

showed
that an

) subring of

either has Hausdorff dimension 0 or
is all of
\mathbb{R}

In this paper we consider the following related question.

Question 1
.

Suppose
A\subseteq(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
is a subobject in some algebraic sense e.g. subgroup, subring or subfield. Is there an

such that

By the Marstrand projection theorem(see Fact

(see Proposition

The main result of this paper is to refine this proposition. Namely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

There is an
F_{\sigma}
subgroup
A
of
\mathbb{R}
and

such that
\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)=\frac{1}{2}
and

. Moreover, assuming

2^{\aleph_{0}}=\aleph_{1}
, there is a group

with
\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)=0

such that

A+xA=\mathbb{R}

The statement and proof of Theorem

null subgroup

We also consider what would happen if we restrict the question to the Borel or analytic subgroups. The restriction on the complexity of the subgroup enable us to use methods in geometric measure theory and descriptive set theory. For example, if

For subrings, the nature is different. If

As a complement of the above results, we also consider the following topic about field extension and maximal subfields of

Quigley investigated the maximal fields of a given field avoiding a fixed point

The structure of the paper is as follows: in Section

Let

Let

We also have the following lemma, which is presumably well-known.

Lemma 2.3

Let

or
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

For

. For

Proposition 2.4

If

By our assumption,

Since

Fact 2.7

(see Jech

Every analytic subset of the reals is Lebesgue measurable.

The proof of this fact is general enough to work for other measures (in Polish spaces) as well (also see

Given a field

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or
\mathbb{R}
. Let
F
be an analytic subfield of
K
with some
x\in K\setminus F
. Then there is
y\in K\setminus F
such that
x\notin F(y)
, i.e.
F
is not a maximal subfield of
K
avoiding
x
.

Proof.

Assume otherwise, then
F
is a maximal subfield of
K
avoiding
x
. This implies that
K/F
is algebraic. Indeed, if
x
is not algebraic over
F
, then
x\notin F(x^{2})
.
Now if
K/F
is not algebraic, then
K
contains a copy of
F(X)
for
X
transcendental over
F
, which does not contain
x
.

So each
y\in K\setminus F
satisfies a polynomial over
F
. Let
D_{n}
denote the set of elements in
K
whose minimal polynomial over
F
is of degree at most
n
, this is an analytic set and
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}D_{n}=K
. In particular,
D_{n}
is measurable by Fact

. It is clearly that
D_{n}
is closed under multiplying by
-1
. Note further that the difference of 2 elements of degree at most

over

has degree at most

. Moreover, there must be

such that
\mu(D_{n})>0
where

denote the Haar measure on

. So

by Steinhaus theorem for Haar measure on locally compact groups (see

). This means that any element in

is of degree at most

for some

. By primitive element theorem, we have that

is at most

, a contradiction to Lemma

.
∎

The assumption that
F
is analytic is used to guarantee that

is measurable, so that we can use the Steinhaus theorem. However,
D_{n}
’s are measurable a priori, the proof will go through as is.
It follows from AC that there is a non Lesbesgue measurable set. Under some weaker set theoretic axioms, the nature is different.

We use

to denote
the axiom of dependent choice
, that is: suppose

is a relation on a nonempty set

, there is an

such that

, then there is a sequence

in

such that

for all

(the axiom of choice) trivially. A classical theorem of Solovay

is that
\mathrm{ZF}+\mathrm{DC}+\mbox{{Every set of reals is Lebesgue measurable}}
is consistent.

By the above proof, we have the following conclusion. That is, without the Axiom of Choice, one cannot construct a maximal field in

avoiding a given point.

Corollary 2.9
.

Assume that
\mathrm{ZF}+\mathrm{DC}+\mbox{Every set of reals is measurable}
. Any subfield

and

, there is no maximal subfield

avoiding

.

Subgroups

The main result of this section is: There is an additive Borel (in fact
F_{\sigma}
) subgroup
A
of
\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
such that
A
is null and
A+xA=\mathbb{R}
.
We recall some terminology and notation for the Hausdorff measures and dimension.
Our reference is Falconer
[
4
]
. Let
A\subseteq X
, where
(X,d_{X})
is a metric space. Let the diameter of
A
, written
\left|A\right|
, be the supremum of
the distances between any two points in
A
, i.e.
\left|A\right|=\sup_{x,y\in A}d_{X}(x,y)
. Suppose
s\geqslant 0
.
For each
{\delta}>0
, we define the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
of
A
by

\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(A)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|U_{i}\right|^{s}:\{U_{i}\}\mbox{ is }\text{a}\mbox{ cover}\mbox{ of }A,0<\left|U_{i}\right|\leqslant\delta,\forall i\right\}.

\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)
can be (and usually is) 0 or
\infty
.
There is a
critical value of
s
at which
\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)
‘jumps’ from
{\infty}
to 0.
This critical value is called the
Hausdorff dimension
of
A
, written

\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)
. In other words,

\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)=\inf\{s:\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)=0\}=\sup\{s:\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)=\infty\}.

Next we gather some of the basic properties of subgroups of the reals.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an additive subgroup.

(i) For all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
A+xA
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
.

(ii) If
p\in\mathbb{Q}
and
A+pA=\mathbb{R}
, then
A=\mathbb{R}
.

(iii) If
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
p_{0},\ldots,p_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
A+\sum_{i=0}^{k}p_{i}A=\mathbb{R}
, then
A=\mathbb{R}
.

(iv) Recall the tensor product

\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{k}p_{i}a_{i}:k\in\mathbb{N},p_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},a_{i}\in A\mbox{ for }i=0,\ldots,k\right\}.

Then
\mathrm{dim_{H}}\left(\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A\right)=\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)
.

Proof.

(i). By the definition of a subgroup.

(ii). Suppose that
p=\frac{m}{n}
for some integers
m
and
n\neq 0
.
Then for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
, there are
a,b\in A
such that
a+\frac{mb}{n}=c
. So
nc=na+mb\in A
. Thus
\mathbb{R}=\frac{1}{n}A
. Since
\mathrm{Char}(\mathbb{R})=0
, we have
\mathbb{R}=n\mathbb{R}=A
.

(iii). Suppose that
p_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}
for some integers
m_{i}
and
n_{i}\neq 0
. Let
n=lcm(n_{1},...,n_{k})
, the least common multiple. Clearly,
A\subseteq\frac{1}{n}A
and
p_{i}A\subseteq\frac{1}{n}A
. Thus,
A+\sum_{i=0}^{k}p_{i}A=\mathbb{R}\subseteq\frac{1}{n}A
, then
A=\mathbb{R}
.

(iv). Note that
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A\subseteq\bigcup_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{n}A
.
But it is clear that
\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)=\mathrm{dim_{H}}(\frac{1}{n}A)
for
any
n\geqslant 1
since
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},x\mapsto nx
is bi-Lipschitz. So

\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)\leqslant\mathrm{dim_{H}}\left(\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A\right)\leqslant\mathrm{dim_{H}}\left(\bigcup_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{n}A\right)=\sup_{i}\mathrm{dim_{H}}(\frac{1}{n}A)=\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A).

∎

Proof.

As a convention, we consider a real

Define

Define

Let

4.
Constructions assuming CH

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem

such that

for any

[

We will work in the
Cantor space

2^{\omega}
, the space of all the functions from the nature numbers
\omega=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
to
\{0,1\}
. We consider the
elements of

as reals, which is more suited for tools coming from recursion theory.

We denote the set of binary strings of finite

. Given

if
\sigma
is a proper initial segment of
\tau

same notation is applied when
\tau
is replaced by a real
x\in 2^{\omega}
.
We write
\sigma\tau
to denote the string obtained by concatenating
\sigma

and
\tau
. The Cantor space is equipped with a topology generated by the
basic clopen sets
I_{\sigma}=\{\sigma\alpha:\alpha\in 2^{\omega}\}

for
\sigma\in 2^{<\omega}

\mu(I_{\sigma})=2^{-|\sigma|}
, where

is the length of the string
\sigma
.

An element
x
of
2^{\omega}
can be identified as a set
z\subseteq\omega
as its characteristic function. For a coinfinite set

is an “isometry” between the conull subset of

consisting of the coinfinite sets and the interval
[0,1)
. Note that under

, the measure
\mu
on

turns into the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Similarly the Hausdorff dimension is also preserved between

and
\mathbb{R}
. For a rigorous proof of this fact, see Section 4 of
[
7
]
. There the authors proved that

preserve the property of having positive Hausdorff measure.

Since the set of all the cofinite elements in

is countable, it is
\mu
-null. Hence we assume that every real

we deal with is coinfinite and consider the arithmetic operations on

to be the same as arithmetic operations on

Given reals
x

y

Given a real

as an oracle that lists its
elements (in no particular order). If

is r.e. in

of reals with an initial segment in

\Sigma^{0}_{1}(y)

For every string

Definition 4.1

Given reals

s_{n}g_{\alpha_{n}}+t_{n}h_{\alpha_{n}}=t_{n}\frac{y_{\alpha_{n}}}{x_{\alpha_{n}}}+\left(s_{n}-\frac{t_{n}}{x_{\alpha_{n}}}\right)g_{\alpha_{n}},

so

g=\left(s_{n}-\frac{t_{n}}{x_{\alpha_{n}}}\right)g_{\alpha_{n}}+\left(t_{n}\frac{y_{\alpha_{n}}}{x_{\alpha_{n}}}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(s_{i}g_{\alpha_{i}}+t_{i}h_{\alpha_{i}})\right).

Since
x_{\alpha_{n}}
is irrational, we have that

. By Lemma
4.6
,

is
c
-generic and
hence generic. Thus
\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A_{0})=0
by Lemma
4.5
.

Let
A
be the subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
generated by
F(A_{0})
. Then
\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)=0
.

For any pair
(x,y)
with

, there are members

so that

and so

. In other

,

.

If
x\in\mathbb{Q}
,
then by Proposition
3.1
,
A+xA\neq\mathbb{R}
.
∎

The complexity of the subgroup

Proposition 4.8
.

Suppose

F_{\sigma}
null subset of
\mathbb{R}

x

A+xA
is null. Moreover, the set

is comeager (the complement of a set of first category).

The following fact is clear.

Fact 4.9

Work in
\mathbb{R}

\mu((\bigcup_{i\leq n}I_{i})+J)\leq n\mu(J)+\sum_{k\leq n}\mu(I_{i})

Proof.

Using the method in the proof of Theorem
4.7
, we can construct a maximal subfield of
\mathbb{R}
such that some given point is not in its algebraic closure relative to
\mathbb{R}
with Hausdorff dimension 0 (and hence measurable) assuming
\mathrm{ZFC}+\mathrm{CH}
.

Corollary 4.10
.

Given
x
a transcendental number, there is a subfield
A
of
\mathbb{R}

such that
\mathrm{dim_{H}}(A)=0
and
x
is not in the algebraic closure of

A
relative to
\mathbb{R}
, and for any
y\notin A
, we have
x
is in the
algebraic closure of
A\cup\{y\}
relative to
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Fix
\{y_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha<\aleph_{1}}
an enumeration of
\mathbb{R}
.
Fix
x\notin\mathbb{Q}
. Define
A=\bigcup_{\alpha<\aleph_{1}}A_{\alpha}

by induction on
\alpha<\aleph_{1}
.

At stage
0
. Define
A_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
. Since
x
is a transcendental, we have that
x
is not in that relative algebraic closure of
A_{0}
.

At stage
\alpha>0
. Suppose we have a countable field

B_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}A_{\beta}\subseteq G\cup\mathbb{Q}

such that
x
is not in the
relative algebraic closure of
B_{\alpha}
. Choose the least
\gamma\geqslant\alpha
such that
y_{\gamma}\notin B_{\alpha}
and
x
is not in the
relative algebraic closure of
B_{\alpha}\cup\{y_{\gamma}\}
(if such a
\gamma
does not exist, end the whole construction). Choose a
sufficiently generic (as in the proof of Theorem
4.7
) real
g
such that
x
is not in the relative algebraic
closure of

.
Such a

always exists since there are only countably many

such that

x
is in the relative algebraic closure of

by our assumption on

and

. Let
A_{\alpha}
be the field generated
by

.

Then
A=\bigcup_{\alpha<\aleph_{1}}A_{\alpha}\subseteq G\cup\mathbb{Q}
and hence

. The maximal property of

∎

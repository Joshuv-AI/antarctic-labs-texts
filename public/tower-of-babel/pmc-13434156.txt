Evaluation model for in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal based on fuzzy analysis, combined weighting, and grey-TOPSIS
Authors: Kexin Che, Shijie Xiong, Jian Bai, Xin He, Wenfeng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In-situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal represents a critical pathway for achieving clean and efficient coal utilization, with systematic and scientific potential evaluation and site selection serving as essential prerequisites for its engineering implementation. Taking the No. 15 coal seam of the F1 South Minefield in the Dananhu mining area of the Turpan-Hami Basin, Xinjiang, as the study object, this research established a comprehensive evaluation index system comprising five primary dimensions, namely coal quality conditions, coal seam occurrence conditions, containment stability, hydrogeological conditions, and environmental conditions, along with 15 secondary indicators. A multi-criteria comprehensive evaluation model integrating fuzzy analysis, combined weighting, and Grey-TOPSIS was subsequently constructed. The fuzzy comprehensive evaluation (FCE) method was employed to conduct a qualitative assessment of the overall engineering geological suitability of the study area, thereby characterizing the engineering geological conditions and identifying potential risks. A combined weighting approach integrating the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), the Entropy Weight Method (EWM), and game theory was adopted to derive balanced and reasonable comprehensive weights for each evaluation indicator. Finally, the improved Grey-TOPSIS method was applied to perform a quantitative evaluation of the in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal in the study area and to delineate favorable zones. The results indicate that the comprehensive geological conditions of the study area are suitable for in-situ pyrolysis development, with only the hydrogeological conditions being relatively weak. Among the primary indicators, containment stability and coal seam occurrence conditions exert dominant influences on in-situ pyrolysis, with weights of 24.8% and 24.62%, respectively. Coal quality conditions and hydrogeological conditions exhibit comparable weights of 20.24% and 19.19%, respectively, occupying a secondary yet significant position. Environmental conditions have the lowest weight at 11.18%. The evaluation results classify the development potential of the No. 15 coal seam in the study area into three categories: favorable areas are primarily concentrated in the central part of the mining area, relatively favorable areas are located in the central-southern and northeastern parts of the mining area, while the southwestern and eastern marginal zones are identified as unfavorable for development.

Introduction

With the growing prominence of global climate change and ecological and environmental issues, the international community is increasingly calling for low-carbon, sustainable development. Guided by its “dual-carbon” goals, China is accelerating innovation in energy extraction technologies, striving to ensure energy security while reducing carbon emissions1,2. China’s energy structure, characterized by being “coal-rich, oil-poor, and gas-scarce,” means that the dominant role of coal in energy consumption cannot be changed in the short term, and the long-term reliance on imported oil and gas resources places significant pressure on energy security3,4. Tar-rich coal, as a coal-based oil and gas resource possessing the triple attributes of coal, oil, and gas, has the capacity to produce oil and gas through medium-to-low temperature pyrolysis. Its solid product, semi-coke, can also serve as a substitute for coking coal and anthracite5–7. The predicted resource volume of tar-rich coal in China exceeds 550 billion tonnes, primarily developed in the western regions such as Xinjiang and Shaanxi, accounting for 24.8% of proven coal resource reserves, indicating substantial development potential8,9. Therefore, promoting the green development and low-carbon utilization of tar-rich coal is not only an important pathway to alleviate the pressure on oil and gas supply but also a key direction for achieving the clean and efficient conversion of coal resources.

The pyrolysis technologies applicable for the development of tar-rich coal resources are primarily categorized into surface pyrolysis and in-situ pyrolysis10. Compared with surface pyrolysis, underground in-situ pyrolysis offers significant advantages in terms of improving coal resource recovery rates, expanding the development scope of deep coal seams, reducing geological risks, and enhancing carbon sequestration potential11,12. Furthermore, it can be implemented synergistically with processes such as coalbed methane extraction and underground coal gasification to maximize the development of deep coal resources, achieving efficient and precise resource utilization, which is of great significance for the green and low-carbon transformation of the coal industry5. Constrained by multiple factors such as the variable pyrolysis mechanism, complex geological conditions, and ambiguous economic feasibility, research on underground in-situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal has been limited to a few pilot tests13–15, and large-scale industrial application remains challenging to achieve at present.

Against this background, establishing a scientific and systematic framework to evaluate the potential of tar-rich coal for in-situ pyrolysis and to delineate favorable development zones is essential for guiding site selection, reducing engineering risk, and improving economic viability. Existing studies on this topic remain limited. Cai et al.16 analyzed the development potential of the study area mainly from the distribution characteristics of tar-rich coal. Liu et al.17 identified target areas for in-situ pyrolysis development using tar yield, burial depth, seam thickness, and seam spacing as evaluation criteria, and further proposed corresponding mining modes. Li et al.18 applied the AHP to evaluate the development potential from the perspectives of resource endowment, coal seam occurrence conditions, and development technology. Jing et al.19 first used a multi-level fuzzy evaluation model to delineate favorable zones for in-situ pyrolysis in the Tiaohu mining area, and then optimized the selected targets using a one-vote veto indicator system. While these studies have yielded some beneficial results, they also have certain shortcomings, such as an incomplete consideration of evaluation indicators, often overlooking factors like economics and pollution, which limits the applicability and expandability of the evaluation results. The weighting methods possess a degree of subjectivity, with weight allocation lacking scientific comprehensiveness and neglecting the objective internal laws of the data. Some studies have adopted evaluation workflows that are essentially one-dimensional assessments of multiple indicators, and the interaction between indicators has not been systematically quantified, resulting in insufficient precision in the delineation of favorable areas.

The Dananhu mining area in the Tuha Basin, Xinjiang, China, is rich in coal resources, characterized by low coal metamorphism and simple geological structures, indicating significant potential for the development and utilization of tar-rich coal. However, the development potential of underground in-situ pyrolysis technology in this area remains unclear, and the selection of favorable zones is virtually unexplored, with the oil and gas value of the coal not being fully utilized. In light of these deficiencies, we have constructed a comprehensive evaluation system for the in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal, comprising 5 primary indicators and 15 secondary indicators. Based on these evaluation indicators, the Fuzzy Comprehensive Evaluation (FCE) method is employed to conduct a multi-dimensional analysis and assessment of the overall engineering geological suitability of the study area. For indicator weighting, a combined weighting approach integrating the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), Entropy Weight Method (EWM), and game theory is adopted to balance expert judgment with the objective characteristics of the data. Grey correlation is introduced to improve the single Euclidean distance, establishing a new distance metric. The improved Grey-TOPSIS method is then applied to evaluate the feasibility of in-situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal in the study area and to delineate favorable zones, thereby providing a novel scientific approach for evaluating the in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal.

Geological background

The Dananhu mining area is situated on the southern margin of the Turpan-Hami Basin in Xinjiang, China (Fig. 1a). Tectonically, it belongs to the Dananhu Depression, a secondary structural unit within the Turpan-Hami Basin. It is located south of the Liaodun Uplift and east of the Shaerhu Sag, covering a total area of approximately 240 km². With coal reserves of about 1.014 × 10⁹ tons, it is one of China’s significant coal accumulation regions20. The study area is the F1 South Coalfield within the Dananhu mining area. The area features an en echelon synclinore fold composed of Jurassic coal-bearing strata. The surface is largely covered by Quaternary alluvial and diluvial deposits. The main structures include the Nanhu Anticline, Nanhu Syncline, South Nanhu Anticline, and the later-formed F1 normal fault. Overall, the structural complexity of the area is moderate21(Fig. 1b). Based on surface outcrops and existing borehole data, the Jurassic strata developed in the area are primarily the Middle Jurassic Xishanyao Formation and Toutunhe Formation. The coal seams are mainly hosted in the middle member of the Middle Jurassic Xishanyao Formation (J₂x²). There are 22 minable coal seams, among which the No. 15 coal seam is the principal minable one. It is stably distributed throughout the entire area, with an average thickness of 6.14 m. It is particularly prominent in the stratigraphic sequence and serves as a typical marker bed. The regional stratigraphic lithologic column is shown in Fig. 1c.

Samples and methods

Sampling and basic testing

In this study, a systematic sampling of the No. 15 coal seam from the Xishanyao Formation was conducted based on exploration borehole data from the F1 coalfield in the Dananhu mining area, Xinjiang. A total of 30 boreholes, whose locations provide essentially full coverage of the study area, were selected. From these, coal seam samples along with their corresponding drilling geological data were collected. Immediately upon retrieval, all coal samples were sealed in bags to minimize contamination and oxidation.

Proximate analysis for the coal samples, including moisture, ash yield, and volatile matter was conducted following ASTM D3173/D3173M-17a (2017), ASTM D3174-12 (2012), and ASTM D3175-17 (2017), respectively. The total sulfur content was conducted according to ASTM D3177-02 (2002). The tar yield was determined using a GJ-6 Gy-King assay furnace in accordance with the Chinese National Standard GB/T 1341–2007.

Evaluation methodology for the development potential of in-situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal

Theoretical framework of the evaluation model

Figure 2 presents the theoretical framework of the model for evaluating the development potential of in-situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal, encompassing three key stages:

(1) A systematic review of domestic and international literature on the development and utilization of in situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal was conducted. Concurrently, three fundamental data sources, including mining area exploration reports, borehole data, and laboratory test results, were integrated to construct a comprehensive evaluation indicator system. This provided the theoretical foundation and data basis for the subsequent quantitative analysis.

(2) The fuzzy comprehensive evaluation (FCE) method is employed. With reference to the comprehensive evaluation system and the influence of each evaluation indicator on in-situ pyrolysis development, the boundary values for each grade of the evaluation indicators are delineated, and appropriate membership functions are selected to process the corresponding indicator parameters. The membership degrees obtained through calculation are used to quantitatively characterize each evaluation indicator. By analyzing the overall project conditions, the feasibility of in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal in the study area is comprehensively evaluated.

(3) Based on the combined weighting method and the Grey-TOPSIS evaluation method, the favorable areas for in-situ pyrolysis in the study area are optimized. The AHP and the EWM are employed to determine the subjective and objective weights of each indicator, respectively. Game theory is then applied to optimally combine these subjective and objective weights, resulting in balanced and reasonable combined weights. These combined weights are used to construct a weighted decision matrix. Subsequently, the Grey-TOPSIS evaluation method is applied. Using an integrated Grey-Euclidean distance measure, the relative closeness to the ideal and negative-ideal solutions is calculated to perform mathematical evaluation and grade classification for each borehole. Ultimately, a comprehensive zoning result is obtained, categorizing the areas into favorable, moderately favorable and unfavorable zones.

Selection and classification of evaluation indicators

The theoretical and technological framework for the in-situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal has not yet been fully established. Given that the gasifiers used in underground coal gasification share fundamental principles with the pyrolysis reactors employed in in-situ pyrolysis experiments, the subsequent analysis of geological conditions and the establishment of relevant evaluation indicators will draw upon their technical approaches and assessment methodologies.

Based on the perspectives of pyrolysis efficiency, containment stability, economic feasibility, operational safety, and pollution risk during the in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal, and with reference to previous research findings13,23–26 combined with the geological characteristics of coal seam occurrence and reservoir properties in the Xinjiang Dananhu mining area, five primary indicators (coal quality conditions, coal seam occurrence conditions, containment stability, hydrogeological conditions, and environmental conditions) and fifteen secondary indicators were proposed through categorical optimization and innovative refinement. These serve as the key geological indicators for evaluating the suitability of in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal, as detailed in Fig. 3. The fifteen secondary indicators are classified into different grades, as presented in Table 1.

In addition, this study introduces geothermal gradient as an innovative geological indicator in the site selection evaluation for in-situ pyrolysis projects. Previous studies27–30 have shown that under thermal stimulation or repeated thermal action, the brittleness, crack propagation behavior, and fracture development characteristics of rock masses can all change, and these changes are jointly controlled by multiple factors, including temperature conditions, the number of thermal cycles, and the burial environment. Differences in the thermal response of rock masses in different zones will ultimately affect heat transfer efficiency, reservoir stimulation performance, and the stability of engineering implementation. For study areas with stable regional tectonic settings and relatively simple hydrogeological conditions, the geothermal gradient can influence project energy consumption, the efficiency of subsurface temperature-field establishment, and the uniformity of pyrolysis propagation. Therefore, it may affect the in-situ pyrolysis process from both economic and production-efficiency perspectives. Based on the measured geothermal data of the study area, the geothermal gradient was classified into four grades. Given the lack of a unified experimental standard for geothermal-gradient classification in underground in-situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal, the thresholds adopted in this study are intended primarily to distinguish the relative differences in thermal conditions among different areas. Accordingly, this indicator is treated only as an auxiliary evaluation factor and assigned a relatively low weight in the subsequent subjective evaluation, so as to avoid overestimating its engineering significance.

Feasibility evaluation based on fuzzy comprehensive evaluation

This study employs the trapezoidal membership function from fuzzy mathematics. By analyzing the influence of each indicator on the in-situ pyrolysis process of tar-rich coal, the indicators for the study area are categorized into three types: cost-type, moderate-type, and benefit-type (Fig. 4). The evaluation grades are divided into three levels: favorable, relatively favorable, and unfavorable zones. Based on the evaluation system for the development potential of in-situ pyrolysis in the study area, the boundary values between these grades u1, u’, u2, u’’, u3 are determined. Each type of indicator corresponds to a specific membership function (Table 2), thereby converting the indicator attribute values into membership degrees within the [0, 1] interval31. Based on the membership degrees of each borehole under different evaluation grades and following the maximum membership principle, the overall suitability of the geological conditions in the study area for in-situ pyrolysis engineering is analyzed, leading to a comprehensive evaluation of the feasibility of in-situ pyrolysis development in the study area.

The combined weighting method based on AHP and EWM

Combined weighting is a comprehensive weighting algorithm that integrates multiple subjective and objective weighting calculation methods based on their respective advantages33. It employs specific mathematical relationships to derive reasonable and reliable combined weights, thereby mitigating the influence of subjective and objective factors on indicator weighting. In this study, the AHP and EWM are utilized to determine the subjective and objective weights, respectively. The game theory approach is then applied to perform the combined weighting, yielding the comprehensive weights for each indicator.

Combine weight determination using game theory

The subjective weight Wd derived from the AHP and the objective weight Wj derived from the EWM are integrated using a game theory-based combination weighting approach. This method minimizes the deviation between the two, thereby overcoming the limitations of single weighting methods and yielding indicator weights that better reflect the actual situation36. The specific calculation steps are as follows.

Construct a set of weight vectors wk = (wk1, wk2, … wkn) (k = 1,2, … K), where n is the number of evaluation indicators and K is the number of weighting methods, with K = 2. Grey-TOPSIS evaluation method

TOPSIS (technique for order preference by similarity to an ideal solution) is a multi-objective decision-making analysis and evaluation method. Its basic principle is to assess the merits of alternatives based on the Euclidean distances between each evaluation object and both the ideal optimal solution and the worst solution. The concept of relative closeness is employed to quantify the evaluation results, thereby determining the optimal scheme37,38. Grey relational analysis39, based on grey system theory, is suitable for addressing problems involving complex nonlinear interrelationships among multiple variables and factors. Its main principle is to calculate the degree of correlation between the reference sequence and other data sequences using geometric methods.

Grey relational analysis is incorporated into the TOPSIS calculation method40. By integrating Euclidean distance and grey relational grade, the proximity between the target and the ideal value is reflected in terms of both curve similarity and positional distance. This not only retains the sensitivity of TOPSIS to threshold constraints but also introduces the capability of grey system theory in handling uncertainties, thereby improving the accuracy of evaluation for the in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal. Evaluation method application and result discussion

Based on the evaluation model for the in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal established earlier (Fig. 2), and following the mathematical framework consisting of Eq. Favorable areas for development were subsequently identified. Thirty sets of raw borehole data from the No. 15 coal seam were collected within the study area. To more intuitively illustrate the spatial distribution of the geological parameters, the Kriging method in Surfer was used to spatially interpolate the evaluation indicator data from 30 boreholes and generate contour maps (Fig. 5). Surfer officially defines Kriging as a geostatistical interpolation method suitable for irregularly spaced sample data and indicates that it can generate relatively natural continuous surfaces, making it suitable for this study. The No. 15 coal seam in the study area has a generally simple horizontal structure, containing 0 to 2 partings. The proportion of seams with 0, 1, and 2 partings is 71%, 18%, and 11%, respectively. Due to the discrete nature of the number of partings (U23), contour mapping was not employed; instead, statistical percentages are presented here.

Indicator U4 reflects the hydrogeological conditions of the study area. A sufficient thickness of the aquiclude and an appropriate distance between the target coal seam and the roof aquifer can prevent groundwater from intruding into the pyrolysis zone and causing operational interruptions49. In summary, the engineering geological conditions in most areas of the F1 South Well Field in Dananhu, Xinjiang, are suitable for in-situ pyrolysis of tar-rich coal. Further quantitative evaluation should be conducted for each area to determine the priority order for the development of in-situ pyrolysis.

The comparison of the three weighting methods shown in Fig. 7a indicates that there are clear differences between the subjective weights derived from AHP and the objective weights derived from EWM. However, these differences are not contradictory; rather, they reflect the different emphases of the two methods. In this context, the game theory-based combination weighting method does not simply average the subjective and objective weights, but instead seeks an optimized compromise solution by minimizing the overall deviation between them. This feature is particularly evident for the coal seam thickness indicator, whose subjective weight is significantly higher than its objective weight, indicating that although this indicator is fundamentally important for in-situ pyrolysis engineering, its variation among the 30 boreholes is limited, and thus it is not a key factor in favorable area delineation. Therefore, the combined weight can more reasonably account for both the engineering importance of the indicator and its ability to distinguish spatial differences.

Based on the combined weighting results, the importance ranking of the primary evaluation indicators for the in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal is: U3 > U2 > U1 > U4 > U5. Notably, containment stability (U3) and coal seam occurrence conditions (U2) exert a dominant controlling influence on the evaluation results, with weights of 24.8% and 24.62%, respectively. Their cumulative proportion approaches 50%, reflecting their role as critical factors in ensuring efficient pyrolysis and hydrocarbon recovery efficiency in tar-rich coal in-situ pyrolysis engineering. Coal quality conditions (U1) and hydrogeological conditions (U4) hold weights of 20.24% and 19.19%, respectively, which are similar in magnitude and occupy a secondary level of importance. Environmental conditions (U5) account for the lowest weight proportion at 11.18%, indicating they are not a core determining factor in the development potential assessment.

To further clarify the hierarchical relationships within the indicator system, this study graphically presents the weight proportions of secondary indicators under each primary indicator, as shown in Fig. 7b. This figure not only visually displays the topological structure of the weight distribution but also reveals the synergistic and constraining relationships among key indicators through multi-dimensional comparison.

(1) Within the primary indicator U1, volatile matter yield, ash yield, and moisture content are the primary constraining factors, with corresponding weights of 36.7%, 31.0%, and 20.7%, respectively. This highlights the fundamental importance of coal quality conditions in determining the in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal. In contrast, the sulfur content weight accounts for 11.6%, playing a supporting role.

(2) Within the primary indicator U2, coal seam thickness and coal seam burial depth account for relatively high proportions, at 50.4% and 31.8%, respectively, while the number of partings accounts for only 17.9%. This disparity suggests that selecting an appropriate coal seam thickness and burial depth is particularly important. Furthermore, the No. 15 coal seam in the study area exhibits relatively simple development overall with fewer partings, thus exerting a minor impact on engineering development.

(3) Within the primary indicator U3, roof and floor lithology are crucial as surrounding rock characteristics, with the roof lithology weight (47.3%) being higher than that of the floor lithology (34.7%). Both are important factors affecting hydrocarbon production efficiency and pyrolysis engineering safety. When the coal seam roof and floor consist of impermeable or semi-permeable rocks, they not only help maintain the temperature and pressure conditions required for underground pyrolysis but also effectively prevent hydrocarbon leakage21. The geothermal gradient weight accounts for a smaller proportion at 18.0%, representing a secondary, optimizable factor that can be compensated through heat injection during construction.

(4) Within the primary indicator U4, the thickness of the aquiclude holds a weight of 61.6%, while the distance to the aquifer accounts for 38.4%. This disparity indicates that the barrier capability of the aquiclude has a more significant influence on the risk of groundwater disturbance than the spatial distance.

(5) Within the primary indicator U5, fluorine, arsenic, and chlorine, as relatively common hazardous elements, exhibit minor differences in their weight proportions, at 27.5%, 31.8%, and 40.7%, respectively. These three elements can be considered equally important targets for environmental risk control.

Based on the Di values, a distribution map of favorable zones for in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal in the No. 15 coal seam of the F1 Southern Minefield, Dananhu Mining Area, Xinjiang, was generated (Fig. 8). Using the natural breaks method in ArcGIS, the classification thresholds were determined to be 0.4714 and 0.5123, dividing the evaluation results into three grades: favorable zone (Di ≥ 0.5123), moderately favorable zone (0.5123 > Di ≥0.4714), and unfavorable zone (Di <0.4714). Considering the needs of engineering decision-making, the three-level zoning results can be further linked to corresponding development strategies. The favorable zone can be regarded as the priority development area, where pilot testing and subsequent development deployment can be preferentially arranged. The moderately favorable zone can serve as an alternative development area, which is suitable for further verification, optimization, and reserve planning. The unfavorable zone is defined as a temporary non-development area, where in-situ pyrolysis development is not recommended at the present stage under the current technical and geological conditions.

It can be observed that the favorable zones for in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal in the No. 15 coal seam are mainly located in the central part of the study area. The moderately favorable zones are distributed around the favorable zones and extend southward and northeastward, whereas the unfavorable zones are mainly situated in the southwestern and eastern marginal parts of the mining area. The boreholes with unfavorable U4 conditions show good spatial consistency with the unfavorable zones identified by the comprehensive evaluation model, indicating that the model can effectively screen out areas with relatively weak hydrogeological conditions. This result further supports the engineering rationality of the zoning outcome from the perspective of hydrogeological risk control.

To further examine the reliability of the evaluation results, the measured coal tar yield data from all boreholes were compared with the favorable zone delineation results (Fig. 9). The comparison shows that the coal tar yield in the central favorable zone generally exceeds 7.00%, and the spatial distribution of relatively high tar yield is generally consistent with the favorable zones identified by the evaluation model. This indicates that the central part of the F1 Southern Minefield has relatively good development potential in terms of both integrated geological conditions and resource basis, and can be regarded as a preferable target area for pilot testing and phased engineering deployment of tar-rich coal in-situ pyrolysis. However, it should be noted that the measured Gray-King tar yield is a laboratory test result and is not fully equivalent to the actual production performance of underground in-situ pyrolysis. Therefore, the spatial correspondence between high tar yield areas and favorable zones should be regarded as auxiliary verification of the evaluation results. In this sense, the model constructed in this study is more suitable for preliminary geological screening of favorable zones, rather than as a direct predictor of field production performance.

Overall, the multi-method integrated evaluation model constructed in this study provides a scientifically robust and engineering-oriented framework for the preliminary site selection of tar-rich coal in-situ pyrolysis. This model integrates the ability of FCE to characterize geological uncertainty, the advantage of the AHP in systematically incorporating expert experience, the role of the EWM in objectively quantifying data variability, and the refined ranking and classification capability of the Grey-TOPSIS in multi-criteria decision-making. By combining subjective judgment with objective data and coordinating qualitative classification with quantitative calculation, the model effectively improves the reliability of favorable zone evaluation under complex geological conditions, thereby reducing the risk of exploration decision-making. The current framework is more suitable for study areas where basic geological exploration data are relatively complete and the key controlling factors can be clearly identified. For regions with large differences in coal metamorphic degree, extremely complex geological structures, or significantly different hydrogeological environments, the evaluation indicators, classification criteria, and weight coefficients should be adjusted according to the specific geological background.

Conclusion

This study integrates Fuzzy Comprehensive Evaluation (FCE), the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), the Entropy Weight Method (EWM), and an improved Grey-TOPSIS method to propose an evaluation model for the in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal based on fuzzy analysis, combined weighting, and improved Grey-TOPSIS. The model employs 15 secondary evaluation indicators to conduct qualitative analysis and quantitative evaluation across five primary dimensions: coal petrology and coal quality conditions, coal seam occurrence conditions, containment stability, hydrogeological conditions, and environmental conditions. It was applied to the F1 Southern Minefield in the Dananhu Mining Area, Xinjiang, successfully achieving favorable zone selection for in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal in the study area.

(1) The membership degree of each evaluation indicator across different grades was calculated using the trapezoidal membership function within FCE. Based on the principle of maximum membership, a qualitative analysis of the suitability of engineering geological conditions in the study area was conducted. The results indicate that for the No. 15 coal seam in the study area, the U1 and U5 indicators both exhibit favorable grades. The U2 indicator is predominantly characterized by favorable and moderately favorable grades, with unfavorable grades accounting for only 10.7%. The U3 indicator is mostly represented by moderately favorable grades, followed by favorable grades. The U4 indicator is mainly distributed in the moderately favorable grade, with favorable and unfavorable grades (20%) being secondary. In summary, the comprehensive geological conditions of the study area are suitable for in-situ pyrolysis development of tar-rich coal, with only the hydrogeological conditions being relatively weak.

(2) Game theory was employed to combine the subjective weights derived from AHP and the objective weights derived from EWM, minimizing the weight allocation error and compensating for the shortcomings of single weighting methods. The combined weighting results reveal that the importance ranking of the primary evaluation indicators for the in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal is U3 > U2 > U1 > U4 > U5, among which containment stability (U3) and coal seam occurrence conditions (U2) are the dominant factors, with their cumulative proportion approaching 50%.

(3) The improved Grey-TOPSIS method was used to quantitatively evaluate the in-situ pyrolysis development potential of tar-rich coal in the No. 15 coal seam of the study area, resulting in the delineation of three grades. Favorable zones for development are primarily concentrated in the central part of the mining area, which possesses both exceptional resource endowment and favorable geological conditions. Moderately favorable zones for development are mainly distributed around the favorable zones, extending southward and northeastward. The southwestern and eastern margins of the mining area constitute unfavorable zones for development.

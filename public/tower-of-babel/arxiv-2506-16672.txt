On the ring of cooperations for Real Hermitian K-theory
Jackson Morris

Abstract
. Let kq denote the very effective cover of the motivic Hermitian K-theory spectrum. We analyze the ring of cooperations π ∗ ⁣ ∗ ℝ ​ ( kq ⊗ kq ) \pi^{\mathbb{R}}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}) in the stable motivic homotopy category SH ​ ( ℝ ) \text{SH}(\mathbb{R}) , giving a full description in terms of Brown–Gitler comodules. To do this, we decompose the E 2 E_{2} -page of the motivic Adams spectral sequence and show that it must collapse. The description of the E 2 E_{2} -page is accomplished by a series of algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences which converge to the summands of the E 2 E_{2} -page. Along the way, we prove a splitting result for the very effective symplectic K-theory ksp over any base field of characteristic not two.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

In stable homotopy theory, one of the most powerful tools we have for computing the homotopy groups of spheres is the Adams spectral sequence. For a ring spectrum E, the E-Adams spectral sequence (E-
ASS
) has signature

E_{1}^{s,f}=\pi_{s+f}(\text{E}\otimes\overline{\text{E}}^{\otimes f})\implies\pi_{s}(\mathbb{S}_{\text{E}}^{\wedge}),

where
\overline{\text{E}}
is the cofiber of the unit map
\mathbb{S}\to\text{E}
. Under mild assumptions on E, this spectral sequence strongly converges to the homotopy groups of
\mathbb{S}_{\text{E}}^{\wedge}
, the E-nilpotent completion of the sphere spectrum
[
Rav86
]
. To begin to compute with the E-
ASS
, one must understand the smash powers
\text{E}^{\otimes f}
.

Let bo denote the connective cover of the real topological K-theory spectrum KO. This spectrum is well-enough behaved that one may begin to study its associated Adams spectral sequence. Mahowald and others have studied the bo-
ASS
, known as the bo-resolution, at the prime 2 extensively
[
Mah81
,
LM87
,
BBB+20
]
; their analysis of this spectral sequence leads to, among many things, the
v_{1}
-torsion free component of
\pi_{*}\mathbb{S}
. Key to Mahowald’s analysis is a spectrum level splitting of
\text{bo}\otimes\text{bo}
as the following wedge sum:

\text{bo}\otimes\text{bo}\simeq\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{4k}\text{bo}\otimes\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{cl}.

The spectra
\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{cl}
are finite complexes known as the integral Brown–Gitler spectra
[
BG73
,
GJM86
]
.

In stable motivic homotopy theory, a primary object of investigation is the homotopy ring of the motivic sphere spectrum. This ring has rich connections with the Grothendieck-Witt ring of symmetric bilinear forms
[
Mor12
]
, Milnor K-theory
[
BBX25
]
, and Hermitian K-theory
[
RSØ19
,
RSØ24
]
. Similar to the classical case, one of the most powerful and well-studied tools is the motivic Adams spectral sequence. For a motivic ring spectrum E, there is an E-motivic Adams spectral sequence (E-
mASS
) which can be used to compute these homotopy groups that, under mild conditions, converges strongly to the homotopy groups of the E-nilpotent completion
\mathbb{S}_{\text{E}}^{\wedge}

[
DI10
,
HKO11
]
. This takes the form

E_{1}^{s,f,w}=\pi^{F}_{s+f,w}(\textup{E}\otimes\overline{\textup{E}}^{\otimes f})\implies\pi_{s,w}^{F}(\mathbb{S}_{\textup{E}}^{\wedge}).

To begin to compute with the E-
mASS
, one must understand the smash powers
\textup{E}^{\otimes f}
.

Let
kq
denote the very effective cover of the Hermitian K-theory spectrum KQ
[
ARØ20
]
(see
Section

3
for details on the very effective slice filtration). In
[
CQ21
]
, Culver–Quigley analyze the
kq
-
mASS
at the prime 2 in
\mathbb{C}
-motivic homotopy theory. This spectral sequence is called the
kq
-resolution and has signature

E_{1}^{s,f,w}=\pi^{\mathbb{C}}_{s+f,w}(\textup{kq}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}^{\otimes f})\implies\pi_{s,w}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{\textup{kq}}^{\wedge}).

Their analysis of this spectral sequence leads to, among many things, the
v_{1}
-torsion free component of
\pi^{\mathbb{C}}_{**}\mathbb{S}
. Toward computing the
E_{1}
-page, Culver–Quigley compute the ring of cooperations
\pi^{\mathbb{C}}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
by the
\textup{H}\mathbb{F}_{2}\text{-}\textbf{mASS}(\text{kq}\otimes\text{kq})
which takes the form

E_{2}^{s,f,w}=\text{Ext}^{s,f,w}_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}}\left(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}},\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})\right)\implies\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{C}}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}).

Here,
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic dual Steenrod algebra,
\text{H}_{**}(-)
denotes mod-2 motivic homology, and
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the mod-2 motivic homology of a point. We implicitly 2-complete the target of this spectral sequence, as we will throughout this paper, to ensure convergence.

1.2.
Main results

The goal of this paper is to begin the study of the
kq
-resolution in
\mathbb{R}
-motivic homotopy theory. As a step in this direction, we compute the ring of cooperations
\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
as a module over
\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}(\text{kq}).

Theorem A

(
4.1
,
8.7
)
.

There is an isomorphism

\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})\cong\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{4k,2k}\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}},B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)\right),

where
B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)
denotes the
k
-th integral motivic Brown–Gitler comodule over
\mathbb{R}

(see
Section

2.3
)
. We describe the right-hand completely, modulo
v_{1}
-torsion.

This result gives substantial control over the
n
-line of the
E_{1}
-page of the kq resolution; see
Remark

8.8
. The reader familiar with Mahowald’s analysis of the bo-resolution
[
Mah81
]
may be inclined to believe that this result may be proven in a similar fashion. This is not the case. Mahowald’s arguments make great use of integral Brown–Gitler spectra. However, there is currently no known construction of motivic integral Brown–Gitler spectra except in very particular cases (see
Example

2.16
,
Remark

2.17
), and so another method of analysis must be employed.
As is the case over
\mathbb{C}
, we generally follow the strategy employed by
[
BOSS19
]
, in which they compute the classical ring of cooperations for
tmf
using algebraic methods. We outline this strategy below.

(1)

We first introduce the integral motivic Brown–Gitler comodules
B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)
and show that there are isomorphisms of
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
-comodules:

\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\cong\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{4k,2k}B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k).

(2)

Next, we produce short exact sequences relating the integral Brown–Gitler comodules which allow for induction.

(3)

Last, we make base-case computations in Ext using algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences (
aAHSS
) and induct to complete the proof of the theorem.

Steps (1) and (2) are direct consequences of
[
CQ21
, Section 3]
. Step (3) is the most difficult and occupies most of this paper. This is because the algebra
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}})
has more elaborate structure than its classical and
\mathbb{C}
-motivic counterparts. Indeed, when presenting the charts for our spectral sequence computations, we find it best to follow the strategy of Guillou–Hill–Isaksen–Ravenel
[
GHIR20
]
and organize each page by coweight
cw=stem-weight
. There is an element
\tau^{4}\in\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}})
which gives a periodicity allowing us to organize our data into 4 sets of charts. These computation are manageable with careful bookkeeping.

Through this process, we obtain the following result, where ksp denotes the very effective cover of
\Sigma^{4,2}\textup{KQ}
(see
Section

3
):

Theorem B

(
Corollary

7.4
)
.

There is an isomorphism:

\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}},B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(1))\cong\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}(\textup{ksp}).

This is an easy consequence of a stronger result which we show in a more general setting.

Theorem C

(
Proposition

3.7
)
.

Let
F
be any field with 2 invertible. Then there is an equivalence of motivic spectra

\textup{ksp}\simeq\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{1}^{F}\otimes\textup{kq},

where
\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{1}^{F}
denotes the first integral
F
-motivic Brown–Gitler spectrum
(see
Example

2.16
)
.

We prove this result by analyzing the very effective slice tower for KQ similar to how one would analyze the Whitehead tower for the classical spectrum KO.

1.3.
Future directions

We next outline several directions in which we will extend the results of this work.

The kq-resolution

This paper initiates the study of the
kq
-resolution in
\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})
. In particular, we calculate the
E_{1}
-page. While many of the differentials in this spectral sequence can be determined using the base change functor

-\otimes\mathbb{C}:\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})\to\text{SH}(\mathbb{C}),

there is
\rho
-torsion which must be determined using the
C_{2}
-equivariant Betti realization functor

\text{Be}^{C_{2}}:\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})\to\text{Sp}^{C_{2}}

along with information involving the
\text{ko}_{C_{2}}
-resolution. We plan to study this in future work. Additionally, the
kq
-resolution is intricately related to
v_{1}
-periodic homotopy. The work of Belmont–Isaksen–Kong
[
BIK24
]
determines a version of
v_{1}
-periodic homotopy theory in
\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{S}
by computing the homotopy groups
\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}\textup{L}
, where L sits in a cofiber sequence

\textup{L}\to\textup{kq}\xrightarrow{\psi^{3}-1}\textup{kq}.

It will be interesting to compare the homotopy groups of
\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}\textup{L}
with the
v_{1}
-periodic homotopy detected by the
kq
-resolution (see also
Remark

3.8
).

Connections with
C_{2}
-equivariant homotopy theory

In
C_{2}
-equivariant homotopy theory, the work of Li–Petersen–Tatum
[
LPT24
]
produces
C_{2}
-equivariant analogues of the classical integral Brown–Gitler spectra. These are spectra
\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{C_{2}}
such that, as comodules over the the sub-Hopf algebra
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{C_{2}}
of the
C_{2}
-equivariant dual Steenrod algebra, there is an isomorphism

\textup{H}_{\star}(\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{C_{2}})\cong B_{0}^{C_{2}}(k),

where
B_{0}^{C_{2}}(k)
is the
k
-th
C_{2}
-equivariant integral Brown–Gitler comodule. It was recently shown
[
LPT25
]
that there is a splitting of
\textup{ku}_{\mathbb{R}}
-module spectra:

\textup{ku}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\textup{ku}_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{\rho k}\textup{ku}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{C_{2}}.

One can ask for a similar splitting of the
C_{2}
-spectrum
\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}
. In particular, it is conjectured that there is a splitting of
\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}
-module spectra

\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}\otimes\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}\simeq\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{2\rho k}\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}\otimes\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{C_{2}}.

One way to show that such a splitting exists is to construct maps at the level of homology

\theta_{k}:\Sigma^{2\rho k}\textup{H}_{\star}(\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{C_{2}})\to\textup{H}_{\star}(\textup{ko}_{C_{2}})

and show that they survive the
\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}
-relative Adams spectral sequence

E_{2}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{C_{2}}}\left(\textup{H}_{\star}(\Sigma^{2\rho k}\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{C_{2}}),\textup{H}_{\star}(\textup{ko}_{C_{2}})\right)\implies[\Sigma^{2\rho k}\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}\otimes\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{C_{2}},\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}\otimes\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}]^{\textup{ko}_{C_{2}}}.

It can be shown that the
E_{2}
-page of this spectral sequence decomposes into terms of the form

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{C_{2}}}(B_{0}^{C_{2}}(k),B_{0}^{C_{2}}(m)).

This further splits as

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k),B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(m))\oplus\text{Ext}_{NC}(B_{0}^{C_{2}}(k),B_{0}^{C_{2}}(m)),

where
\text{Ext}_{NC}
refers to Ext over the negative cone. Our computations in this paper offer progress towards the
\mathbb{R}
-motivic Ext groups in this decomposition. These ideas will be further investigated in future work, joint with Li, Petersen, and Tatum.

The ring of cooperations over general bases

The work of Kylling

•

Preliminaries

2.
Stable motivic homotopy theory

In this section we work over an arbitrary field
F
of characteristic different from 2 such that
\text{vcd}_{2}(F)<\infty
. We recall the
kq
-resolution then review the dual motivic Steenrod algebra and motivic Brown–Gitler comodules.

2.1.
The
kq
-resolution

It was shown in
[
Hor05
]
that there is an
(8,4)
-periodic motivic ring spectrum
\textup{KQ}\in\text{SH}(F)
representing Hermitian K-theory, in that
\pi^{F}_{s,w}\textup{KQ}=\textup{K}^{\text{h}}_{s-2w}(F)
. In particular,
\pi_{8n,4n}^{F}\textup{KQ}=\textup{GW}(F)
, the Grothendieck–Witt ring of quadratic forms over
F
, and the unit map
\mathbb{S}\to\text{KQ}
induces Morel’s isomorphism
[
RSØ19
]

\pi^{F}_{-n,-n}\mathbb{S}\cong\textup{K}^{\textup{MW}}_{n}(F).

Let
kq
denote the very effective cover of
KQ

[
ARØ20
]
(see
Section

3
for more details on the very effective filtration).
There is a canonical Adams tower associated to the unit map
\mathbb{S}\to\textup{kq}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma^{-1,0}\overline{\textup{kq}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma^{-2,0}\overline{\textup{kq}}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
kq
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma^{-1,0}\overline{\textup{kq}}\otimes\textup{kq}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma^{-2,0}\overline{\textup{kq}}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}\otimes\textup{kq}}}

Applying

yields the motivic Adams spectral sequence kq-
\textbf{mASS}(\mathbb{S})
known as the

-resolution. Its properties were first studied by Culver–Quigley
[

]
. We remark that we are working in the 2-complete category.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
CQ21
, Thm 2.1]

The

E_{1}^{s,f,w}=\pi^{F}_{s+f,w}(\textup{kq}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}^{\otimes f})\implies\pi^{F}_{s,w}\mathbb{S}_{2}^{\wedge}.

The

d_{r}:E_{r}^{s,f,w}\to E_{r}^{s-1,f+r,w}.

Remark 2.2
.

We can also look at the
kq
-resolution in the integral, uncompleted setting. The unit map
\mathbb{S}\to\textup{kq}
gives an isomorphism
\Pi_{0}^{F}(\textup{kq})\cong\Pi_{0}^{F}(\mathbb{S})
, where

denotes the

However, we will compute this

. This spectral sequence computes the homotopy groups of the

-completion of

. When

is a field of finite virtual 2-cohomological dimension, then this coincides with the 2-completion
[
HKO11
, Theorem 1]
. These conventions force us to work in the 2-complete category (see also
[

, Section 2.2]
).

Let bo denote the connective cover of the classical real K-theory spectrum KO. The
kq
-resolution is the motivic analogue of the bo-resolution in the following way. The

E_{1}^{s,f}=\pi_{s+f}(\textup{bo}\otimes\overline{\textup{bo}}^{\otimes f})\implies\pi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}^{\wedge},

where we may identify

in the 2-complete setting

Bou79
]
. There is a

induced by the analytic topology on a scheme

after taking

-points. Moreover, if
F
admits an embedding into
\mathbb{C}
, then there is complex realization
\text{Be}^{\mathbb{C}}:\text{SH}(F)\to\text{Sp}
defined in the same way.
The following result lets us compare between

Theorem 2.3
.

[
ARØ20
, Lemma 2.13]

If

\textup{Be}^{\mathbb{C}}(\textup{kq})\simeq\textup{bo}.

Betti realization is symmetric monoidal and exact

]

Corollary 2.4
.

[

Betti realization sends the

Remark 2.5
.

Note that, just as in the classical case, the spectrum

is not flat in the sense of Adams. Thus the

-page does not identify with the cobar complex computing the Hopf algebroid cohomology
\text{Ext}_{\pi^{F}_{**}\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}}(\pi^{F}_{**}\textup{kq},\pi^{F}_{**}\textup{kq})
, and so we must analyze the
E_{1}
-page of the
kq
-resolution.

Remark 2.6
.

There is a complex Betti realization functor in the case of

to which we may apply
2.3
. It is important to note that there is an obvious
real Betti realization

\text{Be}^{\mathbb{R}}:\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})\to\text{Sp}

which is induced by the real analytic topology on a scheme
X\in\text{Sm}_{\mathbb{R}}
after taking
\mathbb{R}
-points.
These functors behave very differently. For example, consider
\text{kq}\in\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})
. It was shown in
[

, Proposition 4.1]
that
\pi_{*}(\text{Be}^{\mathbb{R}}(\text{kq}))\cong\mathbb{Z}[x]
, where

. In particular,

\text{Be}^{\mathbb{C}}(\text{kq})\not\simeq\text{Be}^{\mathbb{R}}(\text{kq}).

2.2.
The dual motivic Steenrod algebra

Let
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}
denote the mod-2 motivic homology of a point. There is an isomorphism due to Voevodsky
[
Voe03a
]
:

\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}=(\text{K}^{\text{M}}_{*}(F)/2)[\tau],

where
\text{K}^{\text{M}}_{*}(F)
denotes the Milnor K-theory of
F
and
|\tau|=(0,-1)
.
Recall the dual motivic Steenrod algebra:

Theorem 2.7
.

[
Voe03b
, Section 12]

The dual motivic Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}=\pi_{**}^{F}(\textup{H}\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes\textup{H}\mathbb{F}_{2})
is given by

\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}=\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}[\overline{\xi}_{1},\overline{\xi}_{2},\ldots,\overline{\tau}_{0},\overline{\tau}_{1},\ldots]/(\overline{\tau}_{i}^{2}=\rho\overline{\tau}_{i+1}+\rho\overline{\tau}_{0}\overline{\xi}_{i+1}+\tau\overline{\xi}_{i+1}),

where
|\overline{\xi}_{i}|=(2^{i+1}-2,2^{i}-1)
and
|\overline{\tau}_{i}|=(2^{i+1}-1,2^{i}-1)
. There are structure maps

\eta_{L},\eta_{R}:\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}\to\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F},\quad\Delta:\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}\to\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}A_{F}^{\vee}

which are determined by the following formulae:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\eta_{L}(\rho)=\rho&\eta_{L}(\tau)=\tau;\\
\eta_{R}(\rho)=\rho&\eta_{R}(\tau)=\tau+\overline{\tau}_{0}\rho;\\
\Delta(\overline{\xi}_{k})=\sum_{i+j=k}\overline{\xi}_{i}\otimes\overline{\xi}_{j}^{2^{i}},&\Delta(\overline{\tau}_{i})=\sum_{i+j=k}\overline{\tau}_{i}\otimes\overline{\xi}_{j}^{2^{i}}+1\otimes\overline{\tau}_{k}.\end{array}

We will also encounter various subalgebras and quotient algebras of
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
.

Definition 2.8
.

For
n\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{A}(n)^{\vee}_{F}
be the quotient algebra

\mathcal{A}(n)_{F}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}/(\overline{\xi}_{1}^{2^{n}},\overline{\xi}_{2}^{2^{n-1}},\dots,\overline{\xi}^{2}_{n},\overline{\xi}_{n+1},\overline{\xi}_{n+2,}\dots,\overline{\tau}_{n+1},\overline{\tau}_{n+2},\dots).

There is a sub-Hopf algebra of the motivic Steenrod algebra

\mathcal{A}(n)_{F}=\langle\text{Sq}^{1},\text{Sq}^{2},\ldots,\text{Sq}^{2^{n}}\rangle;

let
(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(n))^{\vee}_{F}
be the subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
given by

(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(n))^{\vee}_{F}=\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}[\overline{\xi}_{1}^{2^{n}},\overline{\xi}_{2}^{2^{n-1}},\ldots,\overline{\tau}_{n+1},\ldots]/(\overline{\tau}_{i}^{2}=\rho\overline{\tau}_{i+1}+\rho\overline{\tau_{0}}\overline{\xi}_{i+1}+\tau\overline{\xi}_{i+1}).

Remark 2.9
.

An important distinction between
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}
is that while the latter is a Hopf algebra, the former is a Hopf algebroid
[
Rav86
, Appendix A]
. There is a canonical class
\rho\in\text{K}^{\text{M}}_{1}(F)/2
representing
-1
, giving rise to a class in
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}
. If
-1
is a square in
F
, then this class is trivial in motivic homology and
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
is an honest Hopf algebra. If
-1
is not a square, then there is an action of the motivic Steenrod algebra:

\text{Sq}^{1}(\tau)=\rho.

In particular, upon dualizing we see that
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}
is not central in
\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\vee}
, implying that
(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F},\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\vee})
is a Hopf algebroid.

As in the classical case, one can express the sub-Hopf algebroid
(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(n))_{F}^{\vee}
as a cotensor product:

(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(n))^{\vee}_{F}=\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\vee}\Box_{\mathcal{A}(n)^{\vee}_{F}}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}.

These sub-Hopf algebroids come up naturally in our work as the motivic homology of certain motivic spectra. For instance, we have that
\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{H}\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))^{\vee}_{F}
. We also have the following:

Theorem 2.10
.

[
ARØ20
, Remark 2.14]

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
-comodule algebras

\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))_{F}^{\vee}=\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}[\overline{\xi}_{1}^{2},\overline{\xi}_{2},\ldots,\overline{\tau}_{2},\overline{\tau}_{3},\ldots]/(\overline{\tau}_{i}^{2}=\rho\overline{\tau}_{i+1}+\rho\overline{\tau_{0}}\overline{\xi}_{i+1}+\tau\overline{\xi}_{i+1}).

We investigate now the
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
-comodule structure on the homology
\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})
. As
(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))^{\vee}_{F}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\vee}
is a subalgebra, the coaction comes from the restriction of the comultiplication on
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
:

\psi:\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})\to\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}).

Later, we will need to understand the
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}
-comodule structure on the homology
\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})
. Observe that by definition,
\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}
takes the form

\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}=\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}[\overline{\xi}_{1},\overline{\tau}_{0},\overline{\tau}_{1}]/(\overline{\tau}_{0}^{2}=\rho\overline{\tau}_{1}+\rho\overline{\tau}_{0}\overline{\xi}_{1}+\tau\overline{\xi}_{1},\overline{\tau}_{1}^{2},\overline{\xi}_{1}^{2}).

Since
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}
is a quotient of
\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\vee}
, we can deduce that the coaction is given by projection of the usual coaction of
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
on
\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})
:

\psi:\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})\to\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})\to\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}).

Moreover, since
kq
is a ring spectrum, its homology is a comodule algebra. Thus it suffices to determine the coaction on the generators. The following is immediate from this discussion.

Proposition 2.11
.

[
CQ21
, Corollary 3.6]

The
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}
-coaction on
\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})
is determined by the following formulae:

\begin{array}[]{lr}\psi(\overline{\xi}_{1}^{2})=1\otimes\overline{\xi}_{1}^{2};&\\
\psi(\overline{\xi}_{i})=1\otimes\overline{\xi}_{i}+\overline{\xi}_{i}\otimes\overline{\xi}_{i-1}^{2}&i\geq 2;\\
\psi(\overline{\tau}_{i})=1\otimes\overline{\tau}_{i}+\overline{\tau}_{0}\otimes\overline{\xi}_{i}+\overline{\tau}_{1}\otimes\overline{\xi}_{i-1}^{2}&i\geq 2.\end{array}

The following result will be useful in the sequel.

Proposition 2.12
.

There is a Künneth isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\vee}
-comodule algebras

\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})\cong\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}).

Proof.

It was shown by Voevodsky
[
Voe03b
]
that the motivic Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}_{F}
is free as a module over the cohomology of a point. One basis for this algebra is the motivic Milnor basis
[
Kyl17
]
. Since both
\text{Sq}^{1}
and
\text{Sq}^{2}
are elements of this basis, the quotient
\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}\cong\mathcal{A}_{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}
is also free over the cohomology of a point. Consider the following Künneth spectral sequence
[
DI05
]
:

E_{2}=\text{Tor}^{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}(\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}),\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}))\implies\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}).

Since
\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))_{F}^{\vee}
is the
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}
-linear dual of a free module, it is also free. Thus the higher Tor vanishes and the spectral sequence collapses with no extensions, giving the result.
∎

2.3.
Motivic Brown–Gitler comodules

We next introduce motivic Brown–Gitler comodules. We define the
Mahowald weight filtration
on
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
by setting

wt(\overline{\xi}_{i})=wt(\overline{\tau}_{i})=2^{i},\quad wt(\tau)=wt(\rho)=0

and letting
wt(xy)=wt(x)+wt(y)
. This naturally extends to the subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(n))_{F}^{\vee}
. The
F
-motivic Brown–Gitler comodule

B_{n}^{F}(k)
is the
A(n)^{\vee}_{F}
-comodule

B_{n}^{F}(k)=\langle x\in(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(n))_{F}^{\vee}:wt(x)\leq 2^{n+1}k\rangle.

.

One can also define Brown–Gitler comodules using the same weight filtration on the subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{E}(n))^{\vee}
, leading to a very different family of comodules. As we will only be working with the case defined above, there should be no ambiguity in the notation.

We will specialize to the following two cases throughout our work.

Definition 2.14
.

(compare with
[
CQ21
, Def. 3.10]
)
The

-th integral motivic Brown–Gitler comodule

The

B^{F}_{1}(k):=\langle x\in(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))^{\vee}_{F}:wt(x)\leq 4k\rangle.

We remark that in
[

3.
A splitting of symplectic K-theory

In this section, we record a motivic analogue of a classical splitting result. Let
\text{bsp}\simeq\tau_{\geq 0}\Sigma^{4}\text{KO}
denote the connective cover of
\Sigma^{4}\text{KO}
. Then there is an equivalence of spectra
[
MM76
, Theorem 1.5]
:

\text{bsp}\simeq\text{bo}\otimes\text{H}\mathbb{Z}_{1},

where
\text{H}\mathbb{Z}_{1}
denotes the first integral Brown–Gitler spectrum.
We prove our result by using the very effective slice filtration.

3.1.
Very effective slice filtration

The very effective slice filtration was introduced by Spitzweck–Østvær in
[
SØ12
, Section 5]
and further studied by Bachmann in
[
Bac17
]
. We follow the treatments given in
[
Ban25
, Section 1]
and
[
BH21b
, Section 13]
.

The category of
very effective motivic spectra
, denoted
\text{SH}(F)^{\text{veff}}
, is the full subcategory of
\text{SH}(F)
generated under colimits by
\Sigma^{n,0}\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}X
, where
X\in\text{Sm}_{F}
and
n\geq 0
. The category of
very n-effective motivic spectra
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, denoted
\text{SH}(F)^{\text{veff}}(n)
, has objects
\Sigma^{2n,n}X
for
X\in\text{SH}(F)^{\text{veff}}
. These categories assemble into colimit-preserving inclusions

\cdots\subset\text{SH}(F)^{\text{veff}}(n+1)\subset\text{SH}(F)^{\text{veff}}(n)\subset\text{SH}(F)^{\text{veff}}(n-1)\subset\cdots\text{SH}(F).

In particular, the inclusion functor
\tilde{\text{i}}_{n}:\text{SH}(F)^{\text{veff}}(n)\to\text{SH}(F)
admits a right adjoint called the
n-th very effective cover
, denoted
\tilde{\text{f}}_{n}
. If
X\in\text{SH}(F)
is any motivic spectrum, the very effective covers give a functorial filtration

\cdots\to\tilde{\text{f}}_{n+1}X\to\tilde{\text{f}}_{n}X\to\tilde{\text{f}}_{n-1}X\to\cdots\to X

which we will call the
very effective slice tower
for
X
.

Example 3.1
.

In this notation, we have that
\text{kq}=\tilde{\text{f}}_{0}\text{KQ}
.

The
n-th very effective slice
of a motivic spectrum
X
, denoted
\tilde{\text{s}}_{n}X
, is defined by the cofiber sequence

\tilde{\text{f}}_{n+1}X\to\tilde{\text{f}}_{n}X\to\tilde{\text{s}}_{n}X.

By construction, the very effective cover and slice functors behave well with respect to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-suspension.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
Bac17
, Lemma 8]

For any
X\in\textup{SH}(F)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lr}\tilde{\textup{f}}_{n+1}(\Sigma^{2,1}X)\simeq\Sigma^{2,1}\tilde{\textup{f}}_{n}X,&\tilde{\textup{s}}_{n+1}(\Sigma^{2,1}X)\simeq\Sigma^{2,1}\tilde{\textup{s}}_{n}X.\end{array}

Remark 3.3
.

Heuristically, the very effective slice filtration acts as a motivic stand in for the Whitehead tower in classical stable homotopy. We give an example to justify this comparison. Let
A
and
Y
be classical spectra, and let
P_{n}Y
be defined by the cofiber sequence

\tau_{\geq n+1}Y\to\tau_{\geq n}Y\to P_{n}Y

One way to obtain the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

E_{2}=A_{*}(P_{n}Y)\implies A_{*}(Y)

is by smashing the Whitehead tower for
Y
with
A
and applying homotopy groups. Now, let
E
and
X
be motivic spectra. Then there is a (generalized) very effective slice spectral sequence
[
CHR24
]

E_{2}=E_{**}(\tilde{\text{s}}_{n}X)\implies E_{**}(X)

obtained by smashing the very effective slice tower for
X
with
E
and applying homotopy groups. Over
F=\mathbb{C}
, Betti realization sends the very effective slice spectral sequence to an Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence.

3.2.
A splitting of symplectic K-theory

Let ksp denote the very effective cover
\tilde{\text{f}}_{0}(\Sigma^{4,2}\text{KQ})
. Note that by
Lemma

3.2
, this implies that
\Sigma^{4,2}\text{ksp}\simeq\tilde{\text{f}}_{2}\text{KQ}.

Proposition 3.4
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
-comodules:

\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{ksp})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}\langle\textup{Sq}^{1},\textup{Sq}^{5}\rangle)_{F}^{\vee}

Proof.

The idea is to mimic the classical proof, replacing the Whitehead tower with the very effective slice tower for KQ. This tower gives a cofiber sequence

(3.5)

\displaystyle\tilde{\text{f}}_{3}\text{KQ}\to\tilde{\text{f}}_{2}\text{KQ}\to\tilde{\text{s}}_{2}\text{KQ}.

By definition, we have
\tilde{\text{f}}_{2}\text{KQ}\simeq\Sigma^{4,2}\text{ksp}
. Bachmann’s identification of the very effective slices of KQ
[
Bac17
, Theorem 16]
shows that

\begin{array}[]{lr}\tilde{\text{s}}_{2}\text{KQ}\simeq\Sigma^{4,2}\text{H}\mathbb{Z},&\tilde{\text{s}}_{3}\text{KQ}\simeq 0.\end{array}

By Bott periodicity for KQ and
Lemma

3.2
, we have

\tilde{\text{f}}_{4}\text{KQ}\simeq\tilde{\text{f}}_{4}(\Sigma^{8,4}\text{KQ})\simeq\Sigma^{8,4}\tilde{\text{f}}_{0}\text{KQ}=\Sigma^{8,4}\text{kq}.

Since
\tilde{\text{s}}_{3}\text{KQ}\simeq 0
, the defining cofiber sequence gives an equivalence
\tilde{\text{f}}_{4}\text{KQ}\simeq\tilde{\text{f}}_{3}\text{KQ}\simeq\Sigma^{8,4}\text{kq}
. This allows us to rewrite the cofiber sequence (
3.5
) as

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{8,4}\text{kq}\to\Sigma^{4,2}\text{ksp}\to\Sigma^{4,2}\text{H}\mathbb{Z}.

Using the long exact sequence in homology and the isomorphisms from
2.10

\text{H}_{**}(\text{H}\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))^{\vee}_{F},\quad\text{H}_{**}(\text{kq})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))_{F}^{\vee},

and comparing with the short exact sequence of
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
-comodules:

0\to\Sigma^{4,2}(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}\langle\text{Sq}^{1},\text{Sq}^{5}\rangle)^{\vee}_{F}\to\Sigma^{4,2}(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))^{\vee}_{F}\to\Sigma^{8,4}(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))^{\vee}_{F}\to 0

gives the result, as in the classical case.
∎

Recall the integral motivic Brown–Gitler spectrum
\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{1}^{F}
constructed in
Example

2.16
.

Proposition 3.7
.

There is an equivalence of motivic spectra

\textup{ksp}\simeq\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{1}^{F}.

Proof.

As
kq
is a ring spectrum, there is a multiplication map

\mu:\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}\to\textup{kq}.

In homology, this map is the usual multiplication in
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
, where we identify the left side using
Proposition

2.12
.
This induces the
kq
-module structure on ksp, realized as a map

\mu^{\prime}:\text{ksp}\otimes\textup{kq}\to\textup{ksp}.

By
Proposition

3.4
, both
\text{Sq}^{2}
and
\text{Sq}^{1}\text{Sq}^{2}
act nontrivially on the degree
(0,0)
generator of
\text{H}_{**}(\text{ksp})
, hence there is an inclusion

\iota:\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{1}^{F}\to\text{ksp}

inducing the obvious map in homology. One can see now that the composition

\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{1}^{F}\otimes\textup{kq}\xrightarrow{\iota\otimes 1}\text{ksp}\otimes\textup{kq}\xrightarrow{\mu^{\prime}}\text{ksp}

is an isomorphism in homology. Since we are working in the 2-complete category, this gives an equivalence, finishing the proof.
∎

As we will see, this spectrum level splitting allows us to calculate
\pi_{**}^{F}\text{ksp}
in a particularly nice way.

Remark 3.8
.

There is another way to relate the motivic spectra ksp and kq that is relevant to our work. In
[
BH21a
]
, Bachmann-Hopkins construct Adams operations
\psi^{q}
on kq. In
[
KQ24
]
, the homotopy groups of the spectrum L were computed, where L sits in a cofiber sequence

\text{L}\to\text{kq}\xrightarrow{\psi^{3}-1}\text{kq}.

However, Bachmann-Hopkins show that the map
\psi^{3}-1
factors through the second very effective cover of kq. At least 2-locally, this defines a motivic image of J spectrum
\text{j}_{\text{O}}
, which sits in a cofiber sequence

\text{j}_{\text{O}}\to\text{kq}\xrightarrow{\psi^{3}-1}\Sigma^{4,2}\text{ksp}.

In future work, we will show that the homotopy groups
\pi_{**}^{F}\text{j}_{\text{O}}
are isomorphic to the 0 and 1-lines of the kq-resolution.

4.
The inductive process to calculate
\pi_{**}^{F}(\mathrm{kq}\otimes\mathrm{kq})

In this section, we consider the motivic Adams spectral sequence converging to
\pi_{**}^{F}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
. We show that the
E_{2}
-page splits using motivic Brown–Gitler comodules, then outline an inductive process to compute the
E_{2}
-page by a series of algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences.

4.1.
The motivic Adams spectral sequence for
\pi_{**}^{F}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})

There is an
\textup{H}\mathbb{F}_{2}
-motivic Adams spectral sequence computing the cooperations (
mASS
(
\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}
)). Since
\text{H}\mathbb{H}_{2}
is flat in the sense of Adams, this takes the form
[
HKO11
]
:

E_{2}^{s,f,w}=\text{Ext}^{s,f,w}_{\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\vee}}(\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}))\implies\pi_{s,w}^{F}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}),\quad d_{r}:E_{r}^{s,f,w}\to E_{r}^{s-1,f+r,w}.

We can rewrite the
E_{2}
-page of this spectral sequence in a convenient way.

Theorem 4.1
.

The
E_{2}
-page of the
mASS
(
\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}
)
is given by

E_{2}^{s,f,w}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\textup{Ext}^{s,f,w}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(\Sigma^{4k,2k}B_{0}^{F}(k)).

Proof.

By
2.10
and
Proposition

2.12
, we have an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}
-comodule algebras

\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))_{F}^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))^{\vee}_{F}.

Combined with a standard change of rings argument
[
Rav86
, Appendix A]
, we can rewrite the
E_{2}
-page of the
mASS
(
\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq}
) as

E_{2}^{s,f,w}=\textup{Ext}^{s,f,w}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}((\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))^{\vee}_{F}).

Finally, using
2.19
and the fact that
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}
is compact as an
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}
-comodule, we can rewrite this as

E_{2}^{s,f,w}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}^{s,f,w}(\Sigma^{4k,2k}B_{0}^{F}(k)),

proving the theorem.
∎

Thus, to compute the
E_{2}
-page of the
mASS
(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
, we must compute the trigraded groups
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B_{0}^{F}(k))
for
k\geq 0
. Note that as the integral Brown–Gitler comodules are subcomodules of
(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{F}^{\vee}
, we understand their structure as
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}
-comodules.

Remark 4.2
.

Up until this point, all computation has been a precise motivic analogue of the classical work of Mahowald
[
Mah81
]
. However, Mahowald’s computation of
\pi_{*}(\text{bo}\otimes\text{bo})
heavily relies on the fact that the classical integral Brown–Gitler spectra
\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}
give rise to a topological splitting:

\text{bo}\otimes\text{bo}\simeq\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{4k}\text{bo}\otimes\textup{H}\mathbb{Z}_{k}.

As we have noted, there are currently no known motivic analogues of integral Brown–Gitler spectra except in special cases, and so we must use different methods.

4.2.
The algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

We now wish to compute the groups
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(B_{0}^{F}(k))
appearing in the decomposition of the
E_{2}
-page from
4.1
. To do this, one can use a series of algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences (
aAHSS
) along with the short exact sequences of Brown–Gitler comodules from
Proposition

2.21
. This is originally inspired by the work of Behrens-Ormsby-Stapleton-Stojanoska
[
BOSS19
]
. We explain the method here for clarity:

(1)

Compute
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B^{F}_{0}(1))/(v_{1}\text{-torsion})
using an
aAHSS
:

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})\otimes\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}\{[1],[\overline{\xi}_{1}],[\overline{\tau}_{1}]\}\implies\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B_{0}^{F}(1));

(2)

Compute
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B^{F}_{0}(k))/(v_{1}\text{-torsion})
by induction and
Proposition

2.21
.

Remark 4.3
.

Since we are only concerned with using the
kq
-resolution to compute the
v_{1}
-periodic component of
\pi_{**}^{F}\mathbb{S}
, passing to
v_{1}
-torsion free Ext groups loses no information. Additionally, we will see that there are multiple
v_{1}
-torsion classes arising in these spectral sequences, and ignoring these classes drastically simplifies our calculations.

We start with the first step. We have that
B_{0}^{F}(0)\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}
, and so the first summand of the
\textbf{mASS}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
is given by
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})
.
Notice that since
\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})\cong(\mathcal{A}\modmod\mathcal{A}(1))_{F}^{\vee}
by
2.10
, a standard change of rings argument shows that the
E_{2}
-page of the
\textbf{mASS}(\textup{kq})
is given by

E_{2}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{F}}(\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}))\cong\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}).

There are explicit formulae for this algebra in
\mathbb{C}
-motivic
[
IS11
, Theorem 6.6]
,
\mathbb{R}
-motivic
[
Hil11
, Theorem 4.8]
,
[
GHIR20
, Theorem 6.2]
, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-motivic homotopy
[

, Theorems 4.4.2, 4.4.3]
.
We can use
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})
as input for an algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence
\textbf{aAHSS}(B_{0}^{F}(1))
computing the next summand,
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B_{0}^{F}(1))
, which we now outline.

Filter
B_{0}^{F}(1)=\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}\{1,\bar{\xi}_{1},\bar{\tau}_{1}\}
by topological degree. Let
\text{F}_{i}B_{0}^{F}(1)
denote the subspace spanned by generators of degree
\leq i
. This gives a finite filtration of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

We refer to this filtration as the

.
The
\textbf{aAHSS}(B_{0}^{F}(1))
comes from the exact couple arising from applying the functor
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(-)
to this filtration. This spectral sequence has signature

E_{1}^{s,f,w,a}=\text{Ext}^{s,f,w}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})\otimes\mathbb{M}^{F}_{2}\{[1],[\overline{\xi}_{1}],[\overline{\tau}_{1}]\}\implies\text{Ext}^{s,f,w}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(B_{0}^{F}(1)).

The

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})
in Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration degrees 0, 2, and 3, which we index by
a
. Each Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration piece is suitably shifted by the degree of the cell it is attached to. To make this precise, we can rewrite the
E_{1}
-page as

E_{1}\cong\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})\oplus\Sigma^{2,1}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})\oplus\Sigma^{3,1}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}).

We give a heuristic for this spectral sequence in
Fig.

.

[\overline{\xi}_{1}]\otimes\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(\mathbb{M}_{2})

A heuristic for the
aAHSS
(B_{0}^{F}(1))
.

If

has degree

, then
|\Sigma^{p,q}x|=(s+p,f,w+q,a).

We will let
\alpha[i]
denote the copy of the class
\alpha\in\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})
in Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration
i
. With this notation, differentials in the
\textbf{aAHSS}(B_{0}^{F}(1))
are of the form

d_{r}:E_{r}^{s,f,w,a}\to E_{r}^{s-1,f+1,w,a-r}.

Thus the
d_{r}

. In particular, the cell structure of
B_{0}^{F}(1)
ensures that each differential only takes nonzero value on one Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration piece.

The differentials in this spectral sequence are computed by the cobar complex: to determine the differential on a class
\alpha[k]
, we first lift to a representative in the cobar complex for
\text{F}_{k}B_{0}^{F}(1)
, compute the cobar differential on this representative, then project down to the appropriate filtration. In other words, the differentials witness the attaching maps in the algebraic cell complex for
B_{0}^{F}(1)
. The
d_{1}
-differential witnesses the
h_{0}
-attaching map between the cells
[\overline{\tau}_{1}]
and
[\overline{\xi}_{1}]
, so is nonzero only on Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration 3 and lands in Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration 2. This gives the following formula:

Proposition 4.4
.

In the
\textup{{aAHSS}}(B_{0}^{F}(1))
, the
d_{1}
-differential is determined by

d_{1}(\alpha[3])=h_{0}\alpha[2].

Linearity over
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F})
combined with the above formula gives the behavior of
d_{1}
. The
d_{2}
-differential witness the
h_{1}
-attaching map between the cells
[\overline{\xi}_{1}]
and
[1]
, so is nonzero only on Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration 2 and lands in Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtration 0. This gives the following formula:

Proposition 4.5
.

In the
\textup{{aAHSS}}(B_{0}^{F}(1))
, the
d_{2}
-differential is determined by

d_{2}(\alpha[2])=h_{1}\alpha[0].

Linearity combined with the above formula gives the behavior of
d_{2}
. By inspection, the only possible nonzero
d_{3}
differential is between Atiyah–Hirzebruch filtrations 3 and 0, and will be given by the Massey product

d_{3}(\alpha[3])=\langle\alpha,h_{0},h_{1}\rangle[0].

We will see that this differential is dependent on the base field
F
, as is evident from the formula. By inspection of the cellular filtration on
B_{0}^{F}(1)
, we see the following.

Proposition 4.6
.

In the
\textup{{aAHSS}}(B_{0}^{F}(1))
, we have that
E_{4}=E_{\infty}
.

The process of solving extension problems on the
E_{\infty}
-page is more involved, so we postpone the discussion until we specialize to particular fields
F
.

Remark 4.7
.

Though not tantamount to computing the ring of cooperations, it still provides insight to compute
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B_{0}^{F}(1)^{\otimes i})
. Classically, Mahowald is able to express this group in terms of Adams covers of bo and bsp. This is not quite the case here; see
Proposition

5.5
and
7.10
.

Now, we discuss the second step of our method. While one could analyze the algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence arising from the cellular filtration on
B_{0}^{F}(k)
, this spectral sequence becomes drastically more complicated. We will proceed in a different manner. After finding a formula for
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(B_{0}^{F}(1))
, one can induct along the short exact sequences of
Proposition

2.21
to compute
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}(B_{0}^{F}(k))
. If
k
is even, then there is a short exact sequence

0\to\Sigma^{2k,k}B_{0}^{F}(\tfrac{k}{2})\to B_{0}^{F}(k)\to B_{1}^{F}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{F}^{\vee}\to 0.

Applying
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(-)
, we see that modulo
v_{1}
-torsion the connecting homomorphism is trivial. This reduces the computation of
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B_{0}^{F}(k))
to the kernel and cokernel in these short exact sequences in Ext. The kernels are taken care of by the inductive hypothesis, and the cokernels are a simple computation involving a change of rings isomorphism. This finishes the computation when
k
is even.

When
k
is odd, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\Sigma^{2(k-1),k-1}B_{0}^{F}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}B_{0}^{F}(1)\to B_{0}^{F}(k)\to B_{1}^{F}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{F}^{\vee}\to 0.

As before, the long exact sequence induced by
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(-)
splits into short exact sequences. The cokernels are again a simple computation, but the kernels require a more delicate analysis. Indeed, to compute
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}\left(B_{0}^{F}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}B_{0}^{F}(1)\right)
, we use another
aAHSS
. This spectral sequence comes from applying
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{F}^{\vee}}\left(B_{0}^{F}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}-\right)
to the previous cellular filtration on
B_{0}^{F}(1)
. The
E_{1}
-page is given by

E_{1}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(B_{0}^{F}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}))\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}\{[1],[\overline{\xi}_{1}],[\overline{\tau}_{1}]\}.

The induction hypothesis handles the left side of the tensor product, and a careful analysis of the spectral sequence gives us the Ext group in question. This finishes the computation when
k
is odd.

By
4.1
, assembling these results gives the
E_{2}
-page of the
\textbf{mASS}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
, modulo
v_{1}
-torsion. We can now hope to analyze this spectral sequence by comparing with the classical case and base change. In
[
CQ21
]
, this was shown to collapse on the
E_{2}
-page for all algebraically closed fields of characteristic 0, such as
\mathbb{C}
. We will show that this is also true over
\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 4.8
.

One can also use a similar analysis to analyze the
n
-line of the kq-resolution, which takes the form

E_{1}^{*,n,*}=\pi^{F}_{*+n,*}(\text{kq}\otimes\overline{\text{kq}}^{\otimes n}).

We may compute the
n
-line of the kq-resolution by a motivic Adams spectral sequence. The Künneth spectral sequence for
\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}^{\otimes n})
takes the form

E_{2}=\text{Tor}^{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}(\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}),\textup{H}_{**}(\overline{\textup{{kq}}}^{\otimes n}))\implies\textup{H}_{**}(\textup{kq}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}^{\otimes n}).

Using
Proposition

2.20
, one sees that this collapses on the
E_{2}
-page. This implies that

\text{H}_{**}(\text{kq}\otimes\overline{\text{kq}}^{\otimes n})\cong\text{H}_{**}(\text{kq})\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{F}}\text{H}_{**}(\overline{\text{kq}})^{\otimes n}.

After using the change of rings isomorphism on
\text{H}_{**}(\textup{kq})
, the
\textbf{mASS}(\textup{kq}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}^{\otimes n})
takes the form

E_{2}=\bigoplus_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\Sigma^{4|I|,2|I|}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B_{0}^{F}(I))\implies\pi_{*+n,*}^{F}(\textup{kq}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}^{\otimes n}),

where
\mathcal{I}_{n}=\{I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}):i_{j}\geq 0\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq n\}
, and if
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
, then
|I|=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}
and
B_{0}^{F}(I)=B_{0}^{F}(i_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes B_{0}^{F}(i_{n})
. Once one has determined the structure of the groups
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{F}}(B_{0}^{F}(k))
through the process of computing the ring of cooperations, one can study this spectral sequence in detail to compute the
E_{1}
-page of the kq-resolution.

Theorem 5.1

Theorem 5.6
.

[
CQ21
, Theorem 3.38]

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}
-comodules and
\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}})
-modules:

\frac{\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(k))}{v_{1}\textup{-torsion}}\cong\Sigma^{4k-4,2k-2}Z^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha(k)}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{4k-8}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}],

where
\alpha(k)
is the number of 1’s in the dyadic expansion of

.

Proof.

For notational convenience, all Ext groups in this proof will be implicitly computed modulo
v_{1}
-torsion. We will induce on
k
. The case of
k=1
was shown in
Corollary

5.4
. Now, suppose the theorem is true for all
i<k
.

Suppose that
k
is even.
Proposition

2.21
gives a short exact sequence of
\mathcal{A}(1)_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}
-comodules

(5.7)

\displaystyle 0\to\Sigma^{2k,k}B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k}{2})\to B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(k)\to B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}\to 0.

Applying the functor
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(-)
gives a long exact sequence of
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}})
-modules. Moreover, after killing
v_{1}
-torsion the connecting homomorphism is trivial, giving short exact sequences whose middle term is
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(k))
. Therefore, this Ext group decomposes into the Ext groups of the kernel and cokernel of the original short exact sequence (
5.7
). The kernel is handled by the inductive hypothesis: we have

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\Sigma^{2k,k}B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k}{2}))\cong\Sigma^{2k,k}\left(\Sigma^{2k-4,k-2}Z_{\mathbb{\alpha}(k/2)}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2k-8}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}]\right).

We can use a change of rings isomorphism to rewrite the cokernel as

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}\left(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}\right)\cong\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1)).

Since
\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[\bar{\tau}_{0}]/(\bar{\tau}_{0}^{2})
is exterior, the Ext groups in question are polynomial. Modulo
v_{1}
-torsion, we are only left with
h_{0}
-towers:

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1))\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k/2-1}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}].

Putting these pieces together with some reindexing on the
h_{0}
-towers and using the fact that
\alpha(k/2)=\alpha(k)
gives the result.

Suppose now that
k
is odd. Again,
Proposition

2.21
gives a short exact sequence of
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}
-comodules

(5.8)

\displaystyle 0\to\Sigma^{2(k-1),k-1}B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(1)\to B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(k)\to B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}\to 0.

Applying the functor
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(-)
gives a long exact sequence of
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}})
-modules, and as in the even case, the connecting homomorphism is trivial modulo
v_{1}
-torsion. Thus, the Ext group we are interested in decomposes into the Ext groups of the kernel and cokernel of
5.8
. In this case, the cokernel is simpler to compute. A change of rings isomorphism gives

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}\left(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}\right)\cong\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)).

By the same argument as before, modulo
v_{1}
-torsion this Ext group consists of purely

-towers:

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1))\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{(k-1)/2-1}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}].

The Ext group of the kernel may be computed using an algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence
\textbf{aAHSS}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(1)\right)

E_{1}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}))\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}\{1,\bar{\xi}_{1},\bar{\tau}_{1}\}\implies\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}}B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(1)\right).

By induction, we may rewrite
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}))
as

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}))\cong\Sigma^{2k-6,k-3}Z_{\alpha(k)-1}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2k-10}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}],

using that

This splitting leads to a splitting of the
E_{1}

5.5
implies that result of the spectral sequence is isomorphic to

-torsion. Thus, after reindexing the

-towers, the Ext groups of the kernel and cokernel and right-hand side of the Ext groups assemble to give the result.

∎

Remark 5.9
.

The above proof is where our argument differs most from the one given in
[
CQ21
]
. We explain these differences here. Classically, Mahowald
[
Mah81
]
showed that the groups
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(B_{0}(k))
can be expressed solely in terms of bo and bsp. To be precise, for a spectrum
X
, let
X^{\langle n\rangle}
denote the
n
-th Adams cover of
X
, so that

X\leftarrow X^{\langle 1\rangle}\leftarrow X^{\langle 2\rangle}\leftarrow X^{\langle 3\rangle}\leftarrow\cdots

is a minimal Adams resolution of
X
. Then there are isomorphisms:

\frac{\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(B_{0}(k))}{v_{1}\text{-torsion}}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(\text{H}_{**}(\text{bo}^{\langle 2k-\alpha(k)\rangle}))&k\equiv 0\,(2),\\
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(\text{H}_{**}(\text{bsp}^{\langle 2k-\alpha(k)-1\rangle}))&k\equiv 1\,(2).\end{array}\right.

One thing to note is that in the classical case, each of the above groups is describable by a direct sum of
h_{0}
-towers followed by some shift of
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. In particular, if
x\in\text{Ext}^{s,f}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(B_{0}(k))
is in stem
s<4(2k-\alpha(k))
, then
x
does not support
h_{1}
-multiplication.

The results of Culver–Quigley assert that over
\mathbb{C}
there is an isomorphism

\frac{\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(k))}{v_{1}\text{-torsion}}\cong Z^{\mathbb{C}}_{2k-\alpha(k)}.

However, this is not quite correct. The group
Z_{2k-\alpha(k)}^{\mathbb{C}}
has classes in small stems which support
h_{1}
-multiplication, while the decomposition given by the short exact sequences of
Proposition

2.21
do not allow for this. For example, for
k=2
we have
2k-\alpha(k)=4-1=3
, hence Culver–Quigley claim that
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(2))
is isomorphic to

Z_{3}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}]\oplus\Sigma^{4,2}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}]\oplus\Sigma^{5,3}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{1}]/(h_{1}^{2})\oplus\Sigma^{8,4}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(1))\langle 2\rangle.

We see that there is a class in stem 5 which supports an
h_{1}
-multiplication. Alternatively, using the short exact sequence of
Proposition

2.21
we see that the group
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(2))
is isomorphic to following the direct sum:

\Sigma^{8,4}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(1))\oplus\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(0))\cong\Sigma^{8,4}Z_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}],

which agrees with the description in
Proposition

5.5
.
This decomposition does not admit any
h_{1}
-multiplication in stem 5; in fact, there are no classes in stem 5. This type of difference extends to all values of
k\geq 2
, showing that the formula given in
[
CQ21
, Theorem3.38]
is incorrect.
However, this observation is actually in line with the rest of the work done in Culver–Quigley and doesn’t affect any end results. More precisely:

(1)

It was shown in
[
CQ21
, Lemma 3.38]
that the groups
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(1)^{\otimes i})
are not expressible in terms of Adams covers of kq and ksp. The purpose of the groups
Z_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}
is exactly to account for this failure, mixing in an appropriate trigraded version of
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(\text{H}_{**}(\text{bo}^{\langle i\rangle}))
and
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(\text{H}_{**}(\text{bsp}^{\langle i\rangle})).
The difference being observed in the computation of
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}(k))
can be accounted for by noting that this group
also
cannot be expressed using just
Z_{2k-\alpha(k)}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Rather, we must express these groups using shifts of
Z_{\alpha(k)}^{\mathbb{C}}
and an appropriate trigraded version of the
h_{0}
-towers found in the classical groups
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
; this last term is precisely what is given by
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}))
in the case that
k\equiv 0\,(2),
and by
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1))
in the case that
k\equiv 1\,(2).

(2)

Apart from this discrepancy, the contents of
[
CQ21
]
are unaffected. In particular, we will see that the
\textbf{mASS}(\text{kq}\otimes\text{kq})
collapses on the
E_{2}
-page by using the same arguments as employed by Culver–Quigley.

The following is now immediate from
4.1
.

6.3.
Comparison with
\mathbb{C}

There is a well-studied relationship between
\text{SH}(\mathbb{C})
and
\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})

[
BI22
]
. In
[
Hil11
]
, Hill developed the
\rho
-Bockstein spectral sequence, an algebraic spectral sequence which allows for the computation of
\mathbb{R}
-motivic information from
\mathbb{C}
-motivic information. The observation that
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}/\rho
lifts to the dual Steenrod algebra, giving
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}/\rho
. Filtering the cobar complex which computes
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}})
by powers of
\rho
, we obtain the
\rho
-Bockstein spectral sequence

E_{1}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}})[\rho]\implies\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}).

Note that this spectral sequence converges to the
E_{2}
-page of the
\mathbb{R}
-motivic
mASS
(
\mathbb{S}
). One can also filter the cobar complex which computes
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}})
by powers of
\rho
, giving a
\rho
-Bockstein spectral sequence

E_{1}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}})[\rho]\implies\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}).

Note that this spectral sequence converges to the
E_{2}
-page of the
\textbf{mASS}(\textup{kq})
.

There is also an extension of scalars functor
-\otimes\mathbb{C}:\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})\to\text{SH}(\mathbb{C})
. For
X\in\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote its image under extensions of scalars by
X^{\mathbb{C}}
. This functor has the property
\rho\otimes\mathbb{C}=0
(modulo 2) and Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectra to are sent to Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectra. In particular, this gives maps

\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}/\rho,\quad\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}/\rho.

Extension of scalars induces highly structured maps of Adams spectral sequences, which will allow us to lift
\mathbb{C}
-motivic extensions to
\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})
.

In the 2-complete setting, one may lift
\rho
to the sphere, giving an element
\rho\in\pi_{-1,-1}^{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{S}
. This gives a cofiber sequence

\mathbb{S}^{-1,-1}\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}/\rho.

This cofiber sequence induces a long exact sequence in
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}
. Since
\rho\otimes\mathbb{C}=0
, extension of scalars on this long exact sequence gives a split long exact sequence in
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}
. Moreover, we can identify
\textup{H}_{**}(\mathbb{S}/\rho)\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}/\rho
since
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}
is
\rho
-torsion free. This gives us the following diagram.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
X\in\textup{SH}(\mathbb{R})
be any spectrum such that
\textup{H}_{**}(X)
is
\rho
-torsion free. Then there is an isomorphism

\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\textup{H}_{**}(X/\rho))\xrightarrow{\cong}\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\textup{H}_{**}(X^{\mathbb{C}})).

Proof.

First, since
\textup{H}_{**}(X)
is
\rho
-torsion free, we know that
\textup{H}_{**}(X/\rho)\cong\textup{H}_{**}(X)/\rho
, where
X/\rho\simeq X\otimes\mathbb{S}/\rho
. This follows from the long exact sequence in homology associated to the cofiber sequence

X\xrightarrow{\rho}X\to X/\rho.

Note that in this case, we have an isomorphism
\textup{H}_{**}(X)/\rho\cong\textup{H}_{**}(X^{\mathbb{C}})
. The result now follows from an isomorphism of cobar complexes

C_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\textup{H}_{**}(X/\rho))\cong C_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}}(\textup{H}_{**}(X))/\rho\cong C_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}}(\textup{H}_{**}(X^{\mathbb{C}})).

The left isomorphism holds because the cobar complex
C_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}}(\textup{H}_{**}(X/\rho))
is entirely
\rho
-torsion free. The right isomorphism holds since
\textup{H}_{**}(X)/\rho\cong\textup{H}_{**}(X^{\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}/\rho\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

If

8.
The ring of cooperations

In this section, we continue the inductive procedure and compute the groups
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))
. Then, we assemble our results to compute the ring of cooperations
\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}(\text{kq}\otimes\text{kq})
.

8.1.
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))

The goal of this section is to compute
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))
. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 8.1
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
-comodules and
\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}})
-modules:

\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{F}_{2}[\rho,\tau^{2},h_{0}]/(\rho h_{0}).

Proof.

Let
C_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))
denote the cobar complex computing
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))
. This is a cosimplicial ring which takes the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

after totalization. Since the coaction of
\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
on
B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)
fixes
\rho
, all of the maps in
C_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))
are
\rho
-linear. This implies that the filtration by powers of
\rho

C_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))\supset\rho\cdot C_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))\supset\rho^{2}\cdot C_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))\supset\cdots

is a filtration of chain complexes. Moreover, we have the following identifications

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}/\rho\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}},&\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}/\rho=\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}},&B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)/\rho=B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(k).\end{array}

Thus, the filtration on
C_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))
gives a
\rho
-Bockstein spectral sequence (see
Section

6.3
) of the form:

E_{1}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(k))[\rho]\implies\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)).

The groups
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(k))
were calculated by Culver-Quigley
[
CQ21
, Remark 3.25]
. Modulo
v_{1}
-torsion, we have

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(k))\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{C}}_{2}[h_{0}].

In the case of
k=0
, we have that
B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(0)\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and this
\rho
-Bockstein spectral sequence was completely determined by Hill
[
Hil11
, Theorem 3.1]
. We can rewrite the
E_{1}
-page as

E_{1}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}})[\rho]=\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}][\rho],

with a differential
d_{1}(\tau)=\rho h_{0}
. The Liebniz rule immediately determines the end result:

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[\rho,\tau^{2},h_{0}]/(\rho h_{0}).

More generally, the
E_{1}
-page of the
\rho
-Bockstein takes the form

E_{1}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}[h_{0}][\rho],

and the differentials can be determined using the cobar complex. In particular, there are differentials

d_{1}(\Sigma^{4j,2j}\tau)=\Sigma^{4j,2j}(\rho h_{0})

for each
0\leq j\leq k
on the classes
\tau
in each
H
-tower in the decomposition of
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}(k))
. The Liebniz rule immediately determines the end result:

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{F}_{2}[\rho,\tau^{2},h_{0}]/(\rho h_{0}),

concluding the proof.
∎

Theorem 8.2
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}(1)_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
-comodules and
\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}})
-modules:

\frac{\textup{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))}{v_{1}\textup{-torsion}}\cong\Sigma^{4k-4,2k-2}Z_{\mathbb{\alpha}(k)}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{4k-8}\left(\Sigma^{4j,2j}H\oplus\Sigma^{4j,2j-2}H\right),

where
\alpha(k)
is the number of 1’s in the dyadic expansion of
k
.

Proof.

We will induct on
k
and use the short exact sequences of
Proposition

2.21
. For
k=1
, this was shown in
Corollary

7.4
. We assume now the theorem is true for all
i<k
and divide the proof into two cases: when
k
is even, or when
k
is odd.

Suppose that
k
is even.
Proposition

2.21
provides us with a short exact sequence of
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
-comodules:

(8.3)

\displaystyle 0\to\Sigma^{2k,k}B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k}{2})\to B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)\to B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1)\otimes(\mathcal{A}(1)_{\mathbb{R}}\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\to 0.

Applying
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(-)
gives a long exact sequence of
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}})
-modules. Moreover, after killing
v_{1}
-torsion the connecting homomorphism is trivial, giving a short exact sequence whose middle term is
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))
. Therefore, the Ext group in question decomposes into the Ext groups of the kernel and cokernel of the short exact sequence (
8.3
).
By the inductive hypothesis, we know that

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\Sigma^{2k,k}B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k}{2})\right)\cong\Sigma^{2k,k}\left(\Sigma^{2k-4,k-2}Z_{\mathbb{\alpha}(k/2)}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2k-8}(\Sigma^{4j,2j}H\oplus\Sigma^{4j,2j-2}H)\right).

A change of rings isomorphism gives an isomorphism for the right-hand side:

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1)\otimes(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\right)\cong\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1)\right).

The group
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1))
was calculated in
Lemma

8.1
. Thus the right-hand side and left-hand side assemble to give

\displaystyle\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))\cong\Sigma^{4k-4,2k,2}Z_{\alpha(k)}^{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2k-8}\left(\Sigma^{4j+2k,2j+2k}H\oplus\Sigma^{4j+4k,2j-2+2k}H\right)

\displaystyle\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k/2-1}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{F}_{2}[\rho,\tau^{2},h_{0}](\rho h_{0}),

using that
\alpha(\frac{k}{2})=\alpha(k)
. Notice that for degree reasons, any
\rho
-divisible class coming from
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k}{2}-1))
is
v_{1}
-torsion. Combining and reindexing the
H
-towers in the second and third summands gives the result.

Suppose now that
k
is odd.
Proposition

2.21
provides us with a different short exact sequence of
\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
-comodules:

(8.4)

\displaystyle 0\to\Sigma^{2(k-1),k-1}B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(1)\to B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)\to B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)\otimes(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\to 0.

Again, modulo
v_{1}
-torsion the connecting homomorphism in the long exact sequence obtained by applying
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(-)
is trivial, giving a short exact sequence whose middle term is
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k)).
We proceed by analyzing the Ext groups of the kernel and cokernel of the short exact sequence (
8.4
). A change of rings isomorphism gives the right-hand side:

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)\otimes(\mathcal{A}(1)\modmod\mathcal{A}(0))^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\right)\cong\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)\right).

The group
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)\right)
was calculated in
Lemma

8.1
. To calculate the left-hand side, we can use the
\textbf{aAHSS}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(1)\right)
. This spectral sequence is obtained by applying the functor
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes-\right)
to the cellular filtration for
B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(1)
and has signature

E_{1}=\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\right)\otimes\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}\{[1],[\bar{\xi}_{1}],[\bar{\tau}_{1}]\}\implies\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(1)\right).

By the inductive hypothesis, we have an isomorphism modulo
v_{1}
-torsion

(8.5)

\displaystyle\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\right)\cong\Sigma^{2k-6,k-3}Z_{\alpha(k)-1}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2k-10}\left(\Sigma^{4j,2j}H\oplus\Sigma^{4j,2j-2}H\right),

using that
\alpha(\tfrac{k-1}{2})=\alpha(k)-1
. This splitting descends to a splitting of the spectral sequence, and we may analyze each summand of (
8.5
) individually. The left-hand summand is handled by
7.10
, as it is isomorphic to the
\textbf{aAHSS}\left(\Sigma^{2k-6,k-3}B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(1)^{\otimes\alpha(k)}\right)
, so we have a summand of
\Sigma^{2k-2,k-1}Z_{\alpha(k)}^{\mathbb{R}}
in
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(1)\right)
. The algebraic Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence on the right-hand summand collases on
E_{2}
. The remaining classes in Adams filtration 0 are
v_{1}
-torsion, and so the result is isomorphic to the original right-hand summand. Thus, we have an isomorphism:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2(k-1),k-1}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}\left(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(\tfrac{k-1}{2})\otimes B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(1)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\Sigma^{2(k-1),k-1}\left(\Sigma^{2k-2,k-1}Z^{\mathbb{R}}_{\alpha(k)}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2k-10}\left(\Sigma^{4j,2j}H\oplus\Sigma^{4j,2j-2}H\right)\right).

Assembling the right-hand side and left-hand side gives

\displaystyle\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))\cong\Sigma^{4k-4,2k-2}Z^{\mathbb{R}}_{\alpha(k)}

\displaystyle\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{(k-1)/2-1}\Sigma^{4j,2j}\mathbb{F}_{2}[\rho,\tau^{2},h_{0}]/(\rho h_{0}).

As before, for degree reasons, any
\rho
-divisible class coming from
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(0)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}\left(\tfrac{k-1}{2}-1)\right)
is
v_{1}
-torsion. Combining and reindexing the
H
-towers in the second and third summands gives the result.
∎

8.2.
The ring of cooperations

In this subsection only, all results are implicitly computed modulo
v_{1}
-torsion. We assemble our results to compute the ring of cooperations
\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{R}}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
. First, our computation in
8.2
gives the following:

Corollary 8.6
.

The
E_{2}
-page of the
mASS
(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
is given by

E_{2}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\textup{Ext}^{s,f,w}_{\mathcal{A}(1)_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}}(\Sigma^{4k,2k}B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(k))\cong\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\left(\Sigma^{4k-4,2k-2}Z_{\mathbb{\alpha}(k)}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{4k-8}\left(\Sigma^{4j,2j}H\oplus\Sigma^{4j,2j-2}H\right)\right).

Proof.

The left hand equality, which is true even in the presence of
v_{1}
-torsion, is the content of
4.1
. The right hand isomorphism follows from
8.2
.
∎

We now arrive at our main result.

Theorem 8.7
.

The
mASS
(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
collapses on the
E_{2}
-page.

Proof.

There is a base change functor

-\otimes\mathbb{C}:\text{SH}(\mathbb{R})\to\text{SH}(\mathbb{C})

which induces a highly structured morphism with the
\mathbb{C}
-motivic Adams spectral sequence for
\pi_{**}^{\mathbb{C}}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
. In particular,
[
CQ21
, Corollary 3.43]
implies that there can be no differentials in the
\textbf{mASS}(\textup{kq}\otimes\textup{kq})
with both the source and target being
\tau
-free. However, by
Corollary

8.6
we see that every class on the
E_{2}
-page is
\tau^{4}
-periodic. Hence there can be no differentials and the spectral sequence collapses..
∎

Remark 8.8
.

One may now use the ideas of
Remark

4.8
to study the kq-resolution one filtration at a time. The
n
-line of the kq-resolution may be computed by a motivic Adams spectral sequence taking the form

E_{2}=\bigoplus_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\Sigma^{4|I|,2|I|}\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{R}}(I))\implies\pi_{*+n,*}^{\mathbb{R}}(\textup{kq}\otimes\overline{\textup{kq}}^{\otimes n}).

This spectral sequences collapses for the same reasons given above. Note that although there is an algebra structure on the
E_{1}
-page, the individual
n
-lines do not carry any ring structure, as
\text{kq}\otimes\overline{\text{kq}}^{\otimes n}
is not a ring spectrum. These computation do present the
n
-line as a module over the 0-line, given by
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}(1)^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{M}_{2}^{\mathbb{R}}).

Determining the differentials in the kq-resolution is much more difficult. By complex Betti realization, we can deduce much of the behavior of the complex
kq
-resolution from the bo-resolution (see
Corollary

2.4
). However, there is information in the real
kq
-resolution which is both
\rho
-torsion and
v_{1}
-periodic that is not detected by these methods. We plan to analyze the real
kq
-resolution in future work using a combination of these methods and
C_{2}
-equivariant homotopy theory.

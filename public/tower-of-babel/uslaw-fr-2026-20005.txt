FEDERAL COMMUNICATIONS COMMISSION

[DA 26-733; FR ID 369224]


Renewal of the Consumer Protection and Accessibility Advisory 
Committee

AGENCY: Federal Communications Commission.

ACTION: Notice of renewal.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The Federal Communications Commission (FCC or Commission) is 
publishing this notice to announce the renewal of the Consumer 
Protection and Accessibility Advisory Committee (CPAAC). The Commission 
has determined that the renewal of the CPAAC is necessary and in the 
public's interest.

FOR FURTHER INFORMATION CONTACT: David M. P[eacute]rez or Joshua 
Mendelsohn, Designated Federal Officers, Federal Communications 
Commission, via email at [email protected].

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: In accordance with section 9(a)(2) of the 
Federal Advisory Committee Act (FACA), 5 U.S.C. 1008, and 41 CFR 102-
3.65, the FCC is announcing the CPAAC's renewal and providing herein 
the Commission-approved public interest determination.
    The CPAAC's objectives and scope of activities, as described in its 
charter, include providing the Commission with advice and 
recommendations on issues concerning consumer protection, disability 
access, developments in communications technology, and the impact of 
emerging technologies and services on consumers--including individuals 
with disabilities. The CPAAC will operate for two years from the date 
of renewal unless the Commission directs that the Committee terminate 
at an earlier date. A copy of the renewal charter will be posted on the 
CPAAC's website at www.fcc.gov/cpaac.

Public Interest Determination

    Pursuant to 41 U.S.C. 102-3.60(a), to establish, renew, 
reestablish, or merge a discretionary (agency discretion) advisory 
committee, an agency must first consult with the General Services 
Administration's Committee Management Secretariat (the Secretariat) 
and, as part of the consultation, provide a written public interest 
determination approved by the head of the agency to the Secretariat 
with a copy to the Office of Management and Budget. In addition, 
pursuant to 41 U.S.C. 102-3.35, an agency shall follow the same 
consultation process and document in writing the same determination of 
need before creating a subcommittee under a discretionary committee 
that is not made up entirely of members of a parent advisory committee.
    Information on the following factors for the committee is provided 
to the Secretariat to demonstrate that renewing the committee is in the 
public interest:
    1. Annual budget: The estimated annual operating cost for 
supporting the CPAAC is $372,154.00.
    a. Federal personnel on a full-time equivalent (FTE) basis: 2.8 
FTE.
    b. Other Federal internal costs: $20,000.
    c. Proposed payments to members: The FCC does not compensate CPAAC 
members for their services.
    d. Proposed number of members: Approximately 35.
    e. Reimbursable costs: $0.
    2. If applicable, the total dollar value of grants expected to be 
recommended during the fiscal year: $0.
    3. Criteria for selecting members to ensure the committee has the 
necessary

[[Page 61848]]

expertise and fairly balanced membership: Members selected will 
represent a wide variety of interests, including consumers, individuals 
with disabilities, community and/or other non-profit or governmental 
organizations, regulators, and industry or, if serving as Special 
Government Employees in their personal capacity, will have significant 
expertise in the issues to be addressed by the Committee.
    Members will represent a wide variety of entities with interests in 
consumer or disability access issues within the Federal Communications 
Commission's purview, including: consumer or community organizations, 
which may include people who are blind or visually impaired, people who 
are deaf or hard of hearing, people with cognitive disabilities, people 
with multiple disabilities, including those who are deafblind, and 
people who have speech disabilities; state, local, or tribal 
governments; and companies or trade associations in various sectors of 
the communications industry. Members may also include consumers serving 
on their own behalf or individuals who have significant expertise 
regarding the issues to be addressed by the committee, who may serve as 
Special Government Employees. Together, these members will represent a 
balance of viewpoints necessary to address effectively the issues to be 
considered by the committee.
    4. List of all other Federal advisory committees of the agency: In 
addition to the CPAAC, the FCC has three discretionary FACA committees:

(a) Communications Security, Reliability and Interoperability Council
(b) Technological Advisory Council
(c) World Radiocommunication Conference Advisory Committee

    5. Justification that the information or advice provided by the 
Federal advisory committee or subcommittee is not available from 
another Federal advisory committee, another Federal Government source, 
or any other more cost-effective and less burdensome source.
    The FCC must stay apprised of existing and emerging issues in 
telecommunications and video programming which may affect consumers, 
including individuals with disabilities, and take action to benefit the 
American public and ensure compliance with the Communications Act of 
1934, as amended. The FCC must exercise many tools, including by 
relying on federal advisory committees, to keep pace with opportunities 
and challenges posed by new and emerging communications technologies. 
The members of the committee bring outside knowledge and expertise from 
the perspectives of industry, trade associations, and organizations 
representing consumers and individuals with disabilities, which will 
inform agency staff to accomplish our mission.
    6. If the consultation is a committee renewal, a summary of the 
previous accomplishments of the committee and the reasons it needs to 
continue: Recommendations from past terms of the advisory committee 
have informed the Commission's work in addressing consumer and 
disability issues. For example, past recommendations have led to 
Commission actions that empowered consumers and advanced important 
consumer protection priorities. Numerous recommendations have informed 
the Commission's work to combat robocalls, including the use of SHAKEN/
STIR and call blocking rules. Recommendations have also focused on 
advancements in communications technology, mobile device security, 
changes to FCC consumer complaint intake and reporting, as well as 
consumer education campaigns for the Digital TV transition. Other 
recommendations on the Use of Telecommunication Relay Services on Video 
Conferencing Platforms expedited the deployment of relay on platforms 
like Zoom and Teams. Another set of recommendations on the Use of 
Artificial Intelligence to Caption Live Video Programming will help 
inform future efforts to modernize captioning regulations. And 
recommendations on Direct Video Calling (DVC) led to a DVC Forum that 
we hosted at the FCC.
    The FCC's top priorities are aligned with the Communications Act of 
1934, as amended by Congress, which requires the FCC to protect 
consumers and ensure accessibility of telecommunications and video 
programming. The FCC needs to understand how existing and emerging 
issues in these fields affect consumers, including people with 
disabilities, to ensure that it adopts regulations such that 
telecommunications and video programming continue to benefit the 
American public, by engaging with the stakeholders most affected by 
these issues, including through federal advisory committee engagement.
    7. Explanation of why the committee/subcommittee is essential to 
the conduct of agency business: The Consumer Protection and 
Accessibility Advisory Committee is chartered to focus on key issues 
that are essential to the FCC's business, including issues affecting 
consumer protection and education; implementation of statutes, 
Commission rules, and policies to protect consumers; promoting consumer 
participation and input into Commission rulemaking proceedings and 
other decision-making processes; impact of new and emerging 
communications technologies on consumers, including those in 
underserved areas; communications access for people with disabilities, 
and video programming access for people with disabilities. 
Telecommunications and video programming are continually evolving, with 
opportunities and challenges affecting consumers--including people with 
disabilities. Continual input and engagement through the Consumer 
Protection and Accessibility Advisory Committee is essential to ensure 
that the FCC has the data and tools necessary to address these 
opportunities and challenges affecting consumers.

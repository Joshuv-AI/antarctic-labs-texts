Variants of Merge-Width and Applications 1 1 footnotemark: 1
Karolina Drabik, Maël Dumas, Colin Geniet, Jakub Nowakowski, Michał Pilipczuk, Szymon Toruńczyk

Abstract
Merge-width is a recently introduced family of graph parameters that unifies treewidth, clique-width, twin-width, and generalised colouring numbers. We prove the equivalence of several alternative definitions of merge-width, thus demonstrating the robustness of the notion. Our characterisation via definable merge-width uses vertex orderings inspired by generalised colouring numbers from sparsity theory, and enables us to obtain the first non-trivial approximation algorithm for merge-width, running in time n 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) ⋅ 2 n n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}\cdot 2^{n} . We also obtain a new characterisation of bounded clique-width in terms of vertex orderings, and establish that graphs of bounded merge-width admit sparse quotients with bounded strong colouring numbers, are quasi-isometric to graphs of bounded expansion, and admit neighbourhood covers with constant overlap. We also discuss several other variants of merge-width and connections to adjacency labelling schemes.

Introduction

Merge-width is a family of graph parameters introduced recently by Dreier and Toruńczyk
[
DT25
]
. The aim of merge-width is to unify and generalise several well-known structural graph parameters such as tree-width, clique-width, twin-width, and generalised colouring numbers, which have been extensively studied due to their numerous applications in algorithmics, combinatorics, and logic. The model-checking problem for first-order logic is fixed-parameter tractable on graph classes of bounded merge-width
[
DT25
]
, assuming a suitable decomposition witnessing the boundedness is given. Those classes enjoy further good logical and combinatorial properties, such as closure under first-order transductions
[
DT25
]
, the strong Erdős–Hajnal property, and
\chi
-boundedness
[
BG25
]
. A central open question is whether merge-width can be efficiently approximated.

In this paper, we explore the properties of merge-width further. We prove the equivalence of several alternative definitions of merge-width, demonstrating the robustness of the notion.
Among other findings, one of those characterisations
elucidates the connection of merge-width with central notions from sparsity theory
[
NM12
]
, by showing that merge-width can be characterized using vertex orderings similar to those used to define generalised colouring numbers. This potentially opens the avenue for applying methods used in sparsity theory, in the context of dense graphs.

As an indication of the advantage of this characterisation, we use it to obtain a single-exponential time approximation algorithm for merge-width, running in time
2^{n}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
on
n
-vertex graphs. Another of our characterisations yields a certain normal form of merge sequences, which we use to show that graphs of bounded merge-width admit neighbourhood covers with constant overlap, and are quasi-isometric to graphs of bounded expansion. Before explaining our results in more details, we first recall the definition of merge-width.

1.2
Main result

We give several equivalent characterisations of merge-width, using alternative parameters discussed below.
Intuitively, our main result states that the full data of a merge sequence can be approximately recovered from some partial information,
such as a sequence of partitions, or a suitably chosen vertex ordering.
This provides more light-weight variants of merge-width which are sometimes better behaved, leading to new applications of the notion.

Theorem 1.1
.

The following are equivalent for a graph class
\mathscr{C}
:

1.

\mathscr{C}
has bounded merge-width,

2.

\mathscr{C}
has bounded transient merge-width,

3.

\mathscr{C}
has bounded partition merge-width,

4.

\mathscr{C}
has bounded definable merge-width.

Each item in the theorem corresponds to a family of parameters, indexed by radius
r
, which is defined by modifying the definition of merge-width. The modifications are explained briefly below, and in greater detail in
Section

3
.

Transient merge-width

is defined similarly as merge-width, but the monotonicity condition
R_{1}\subseteq R_{2}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq R_{m}
is dropped. This is equivalent to specifying a sequence

(\mathcal{P}_{1},G_{1}),(\mathcal{P}_{2},G_{2}),\ldots,(\mathcal{P}_{m},G_{m})

with
\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}_{m}
as before,
where each graph
G_{t}
is a
\mathcal{P}_{t}
-flip
of
G
— obtained from
G
by complementing the adjacency between some pairs of parts in
\mathcal{P}_{t}
— and the radius-
r
width is measured by considering distances in
G_{t+1}
rather than in
(V,R_{t+1})
.

Partition merge-width

is defined by providing only a sequence of ever coarser partitions
\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\cdots\prec\mathcal{P}_{m}
. Distances are measured with respect to the
flip-metric
of
\mathcal{P}_{t+1}
, defined for each pair of vertices as their maximum distance in any
\mathcal{P}_{t+1}
-flip
G^{\prime}
of
G
.

Definable merge-width

is specified by an enumeration (or total order) of
V(G)
.
For each
i\in[n]
consider the partition
\mathcal{P}_{i}
of
V(G)
,
which partitions the last
i

according to their neighbourhood in the set of the
n-i
remaining vertices, which in turn is partitioned into singletons.
This yields a sequence of partitions
\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}_{n}
;
distances are measured with respect to the flip-metric of
\mathcal{P}_{t}
.

Transient merge-width is a very natural weakening of merge-width which is still useful combinatorially. For instance, in the results of Bonamy and Geniet
[
BG25
]
— proving the Strong Erdős–Hajnal property and
linear neighbourhood complexity for classes of bounded merge-width — it is enough to assume a bound on the transient merge-width of the graphs.
On the other hand, for the purposes studied in
[
DT25
]
— first-order model checking, closure under transductions, and the relationship with flip-width — the original definition of merge-width appears much more useful. Note that the equivalence of bounded merge-width and bounded transient merge-width proved in
Theorem

1.1
does not respect the radii: while we clearly have
\tmw_{1}(G)\leqslant\mw_{1}(G)
, in the other direction our proof only bounds
\mw_{1}(G)
in terms of
\tmw_{7}(G)
.

Definable merge-width is inspired by, and generalises
the
generalised colouring numbers
(
weak
and
strong
colouring numbers) studied in sparsity theory, which are also defined through a vertex ordering.
In particular, the construction in
[
DT25
, Lem. 7.5]
shows that the definable merge-width with radius
r
of a graph
G
is bounded in terms of the
(r+1)
-weak colouring number of
G
.
Each of the partitions
\mathcal{P}_{i}
considered in the definition of definable merge-width is definable (as an equivalence relation) using a first-order formula
\pi_{i}(x,y)
, which uses a unary predicate marking the last
i
vertices in the enumeration; hence the term
definable
. In particular, the binary structure
G_{i}
consisting of
G
and the partition
\mathcal{P}_{i}
(again as an equivalence relation)
can be obtained from
G
using a fixed
first-order transduction
(see e.g.
[
Gaj+20
]
for a definition). This implies that
G_{i}
is “tame” if
G
is, e.g. its clique-width, twin-width or merge-width can be bounded in terms of the corresponding parameters of
G
.
This is in contrast with the other considered variants of merge-width.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
relies on two
metric conversion
lemmas,
[
Bon+25
, Lemma 8]
, and
[
Bon+22
, Theorem 3.5]
.
Informally, each of the previous variants of merge-width uses some notion of distance defined by a vertex partition
\mathcal{P}
.
These lemmas show how these different distances can be approximated by each other, at the cost of a controlled modification of
\mathcal{P}
.
To apply these results in the setting of merge-width, there are two difficulties to overcome.
First, these metric conversion lemmas were previously considered for partitions
\mathcal{P}
with a bounded number of parts;
for merge-width, we instead assume that the number of parts is bounded only in the neighbourhood of each vertex.
Secondly, when applying them to a partition sequence
\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}_{m}
,
we need to ensure that as a result, we obtain a sequence of ever coarser partitions. The application of metric conversion lemmas to a sequence of partition is inspired by
[
Dra+26
]
, where a strong colouring order is constructed from a (transient) merge sequence in graphs excluding some biclique
K_{t,t}
.

Dreier and Toruńczyk
[
DT25
]
also define merge-width at radius
\infty
, considering connectivity rather than distances. It is functionally equivalent to clique-width.
The previous variants of merge-width can also be defined at radius-
\infty
, and the equivalence still holds:

Theorem 1.2
.

The following are equivalent for a graph class
\mathscr{C}
:

1.

\mathscr{C}
has bounded clique-width,

2.

\mw_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})<\infty
,

3.

\tmw_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})<\infty
,

4.

\pmw_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})<\infty
,

5.

\dmw_{\infty}(\mathscr{C})<\infty
.

In a different direction,
[
DT25
]
considers classes

with
almost bounded
merge-width,
meaning that for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and

G\in\mathscr{C}
with
n

\mw_{r}(G)\leqslant n^{\varepsilon}
.
Such classes include for instance all

graph classes considered in sparsity theory.
Every hereditary graph class of
almost bounded merge-width is
monadically dependent
— a notion which has gained recent attention in finite model theory and structural graph theory, in connection with the model-checking problem for first-order logic
[
DMT24
,
Dre+24
,
DT25
]
.
Conversely, it is conjectured that all monadically dependent graph classes have almost bounded merge-width
[
DT25
, Conjecture 1.18]
, and that for hereditary classes, monadic dependence is equivalent to the class having a tractable model checking problem for first-order logic.

Almost bounded transient, partition, and definable merge-width can be defined similarly as almost bounded merge-width.
Our main results in fact show that the different variants of merge-width are bounded by polynomial functions of each other, assuming a fixed bound on the VC-dimension of the considered graphs.
It follows that the ‘almost bounded’ conditions are also equivalent
(as each implies a bound on the VC-dimension, see
Corollary

7.3
):

Theorem 1.3
.

The following are equivalent for a hereditary graph class
\mathscr{C}
:

1.

\mathscr{C}
has almost bounded merge-width,

2.

\mathscr{C}
has almost bounded transient merge-width,

3.

\mathscr{C}
has almost bounded partition merge-width,

4.

\mathscr{C}
has almost bounded definable merge-width.

1.4
Other variants of merge-width

In addition to the notions of merge-width discussed above, in
Section

4
we also consider the following variants:

short merge-width,

where (i) the merge sequence has length logarithmic in the size of the graph and (ii) every part of every partition in the merge sequence consists of a constant number of parts of the partition from the previous time step;

universal merge-width,

which requires the existence of a single merge sequence whose radius-
r
width is bounded by a function of
r
, for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
; and

positive merge-width,

where only edges of
G
can be resolved, i.e.
R_{m}\subseteq E(G)
(equivalently,
R_{t}\subseteq E(G)
for all
t\in[m]
).

Short merge-width
is inspired by a similar notion of
short contraction sequences
studied in the context of twin-width
[
Bon+22a
]
.
Clearly, classes of bounded short merge-width have bounded merge-width.
We conjecture the converse implication (see
4.5
),
and observe that classes of bounded twin-width and classes of bounded expansion
have bounded short merge-width, and that this property is preserved under first-order transductions (for hereditary classes, see
Lemma

4.4
).
Generalising
[
Bon+22a
, Thm. 2.8]
, short merge-width allows to obtain adjacency labelling schemes with short labels: that is, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, each
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\shortmw_{1}(G)
bounded by a constant can be vertex-labelled using bit-strings of length

, so that the adjacency between two vertices can be determined only by inspecting their labels.

Theorem 1.5
.

Every class of graphs of bounded radius-
1
short merge-width admits an adjacency labelling scheme of length
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
.

As mentioned, we conjecture that all classes of bounded merge-width have bounded short merge-width. If true, with
Theorem

1.5
this would imply that they also have
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
-adjacency labelling schemes, confirming a conjecture of Dreier and Toruńczyk
[

]
. As discussed in
[

]
, this could be a route towards proving the

of
[

]
.

A graph class

has

if there is a function

has a merge sequence whose radius-

Such classes include classes of bounded twin-width and of bounded expansion, and are closed under first-order transductions.

.

is

-quasi-isometric to some

.

Finally, classes of bounded

For

is
a sequence

\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\mathcal{P}_{2}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}_{m}

of distinct partitions of
V
.
A
maximal chain of partitions
is such a sequence of length
n=|V|
,
equivalently,
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is the partition into singletons,
\mathcal{P}_{n}
has one part, and for every
1\leqslant i<n
,
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{P}_{i}
by merging exactly two parts.

The common refinement of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
is the partition

\mathcal{P}\wedge\mathcal{Q}\coloneqq\{P\cap Q:P\in\mathcal{P},Q\in\mathcal{Q}\}\setminus\{\varnothing\}.

For a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
V
and set
S\subseteq V
, the
quotient set
is defined as

S/\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\{P\in\mathcal{P}\mid P\cap S\neq\varnothing\}.

Given a set
S\subseteq V(G)
of vertices, we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{S}
the partition of
V(G)
into
atomic types
over
S
, which partitions
S
into singletons, and two vertices
u,v\notin S
belong to the same part of
\mathcal{N}_{S}

if and only if
N(u)\cap S=N(v)\cap S
.

2.4
Flips and flip metric

For a graph
G
and a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
V(G)
, a
\mathcal{P}
-flip
of
G
is any graph
G^{\prime}
with vertex set
V(G)
such that for any parts
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}
(possibly
X=Y
) we have either
XY\cap E(G^{\prime})=XY\cap E(G)
or
XY\cap E(G^{\prime})=XY\setminus E(G)
. In the latter case, we say that the pair
X,Y
is
flipped
in
G^{\prime}
with respect to
G
. For a set
S\subseteq V(G)
,
an
S
-flip
of
G
is an
\mathcal{N}_{S}
-flip of
G
, where
\mathcal{N}_{S}
is the partition into atomic types.

In a graph
G
, the
flip metric
associated with the partition
\mathcal{P}
of
V(G)
is defined, following
[
Gaj+23
]
, as

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(x,y)=\max_{\text{$G^{\prime}$ $\mathcal{P}$-flip of $G$}}\mathrm{dist}_{G^{\prime}}(x,y),

where the maximum ranges over all

\mathcal{P}
-flips
G^{\prime}
of
G
.
For
v\in V(G)
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\mathrm{Ball}_{\mathcal{P}}^{r}(v)\coloneqq\{x\in V(G)\colon\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(x,v)\leqslant r\}=\,\bigcap_{\text{$G^{\prime}$ $\mathcal{P}$-flip of $G$}}\mathrm{Ball}_{G^{\prime}}^{r}(v).

The following is immediate from the definition.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{P}
refines
\mathcal{Q}
then for any vertices
x,y
,

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(x,y)\geqslant\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{Q}}(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Ball}^{r}_{\mathcal{P}}(x)\subseteq\mathrm{Ball}^{r}_{\mathcal{Q}}(x).

2.5
Generalised colouring numbers

Fix a graph
G
, number
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and total order
<
on
V(G)
.
A vertex
u
is
weakly
r
-reachable
from a vertex
v\geqslant u
if there is a path of length at most
r
from
v

to
u
such that
w>u
for all internal vertices
w
of that path.
Similarly,
u
is
strongly
r
-reachable
from a vertex
v\geqslant u
if there is a path of length at most
r
from
v
to
u
such that
w>v
for all internal vertices
w
of that path.

Let
\mathrm{WReach}_{r}^{<}(v)
be the set of vertices that are weakly
r
-reachable from
v
with respect to the order
<
. Denote

\mathrm{wcol}_{r}^{<}(G)\coloneqq\max_{v\in V(G)}|\mathrm{WReach}_{r}^{<}(v)|

and set
\mathrm{wcol}_{r}(G)
to be the minimal value of
\mathrm{wcol}_{r}^{<}(G)
across all the total orders on
V(G)
.
The notions of
\mathrm{SReach}_{r}^{<}(G)
,
\mathrm{scol}_{r}^{<}(G)
and
\mathrm{scol}_{r}(G)
involving strong
r
-reachability are defined analogously.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
KY03
]
)
.

Fix a graph
G
, total order
<
on
V(G)
, and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\mathrm{scol}_{r}^{<}(G)\ \leqslant\ \mathrm{wcol}_{r}^{<}(G)\ \leqslant\ (\mathrm{scol}_{r}^{<}(G))^{r}.

By a characterisation of
[
Zhu09
]
,
a graph class
\mathscr{C}
has
bounded expansion
if and only if
\mathrm{scol}_{r}(\mathscr{C})<\infty
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}

(equivalently,
\mathrm{wcol}_{r}(\mathscr{C})<\infty
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
).

2.6
Twin-width

The
twin-width
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\tww(G)
, is the minimum integer
d
such that there exists a maximal chain

\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\mathcal{P}_{2}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}_{n}

of partitions of
V(G)
(called a
contraction sequence
in this context) such that
for every
t\in[n]
, every part
A\in\mathcal{P}_{t}
is non-homogeneous to at most
d
parts
B\in\mathcal{P}_{t}
with
B\neq A
.

2.7
Merge-width

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a partition of the vertices
V
of a graph
G
, and
R\subseteq\binom{V}{2}
be a set of pairs of vertices of
G
.
By
\mathrm{dist}_{R}(x,y)
and
\mathrm{Ball}_{R}^{r}(x)
we mean the corresponding notions in the graph
(V,R)
.
The
radius-
r
width
of
(\mathcal{P},R)
is

\max_{v\in V}\left|\mathrm{Ball}^{r}_{R}(v)/\mathcal{P}\right|.

We say that
\mathcal{P}
is
homogeneous modulo
R
(in
G
) if for any parts
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}
(possibly
X=Y
)
and pairs
xy,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\in XY\setminus R
, we have that
xy\in E(G)
if and only if
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\in E(G)
.
Equivalently, there is a
\mathcal{P}
-flip
G^{\prime}
of
G
such that
E(G^{\prime})\subseteq R
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\mathcal{P}
is homogeneous modulo
R
in
G
, then for all
x,y\in V(G)
we have

\mathrm{dist}_{R}(x,y)\leqslant\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(x,y).

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a
\mathcal{P}
-flip of
G
such that
R\supseteq E(G^{\prime})
.
Then:

\mathrm{dist}_{R}(x,y)\leqslant\mathrm{dist}_{G^{\prime}}(x,y)\leqslant\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(x,y).\qed

Definition 2.6

(
[
DT25
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

A
merge sequence
for a graph
G
is a sequence

(\mathcal{P}_{1},R_{1}),\dots,(\mathcal{P}_{m},R_{m})

such that:

1.

\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{2}\preccurlyeq\ldots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{m}
is a sequence of ever coarser partitions of
V(G)
with
\mathcal{P}_{1}
the partition into singletons and
\mathcal{P}_{m}
the partition with one part,

2.

R_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq R_{m}\subseteq\binom{V(G)}{2}
is a monotone sequence of pairs of vertices, and

3.

\mathcal{P}_{t}
is homogeneous modulo
R_{t}
, for
t=1,\ldots,m
.

The
radius-
r
width
of this merge sequence is the maximum radius-
r
width of
(\mathcal{P}_{t},R_{t+1})
,
for
1\leqslant t<m
.
Finally, the
radius-
r
merge-width
of
G
, denoted by
\mw_{r}(G)
, is the minimum radius-
r
width of a merge sequence for
G
.

Note that the mismatched indices in
(\mathcal{P}_{t},R_{t+1})
are intentional, and forbid one from merging many parts and adding many resolved pairs all at once when going from step
t
to
t+1
.

In the definition of merge-width, the sequence of partitions
\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\ldots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{m}
may be equivalently required to be a maximal chain of partitions of
V(G)
.
Indeed, given any merge sequence
(\mathcal{P}_{1},R_{1}),\dots,(\mathcal{P}_{m},R_{m})
,
one can transform it into a merge sequence whose sequence of partitions is a maximal chain of partitions as follows: if
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
for some
1\leqslant i<m
, then we can drop the pair
(\mathcal{P}_{i+1},R_{i+1})
in the sequence, and if
\mathcal{P}_{i}\prec\mathcal{P}\prec\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
, then we may insert the pair
(\mathcal{P},R_{i+1})
into the sequence. Those operations do not increase the radius-
r
width of the merge sequence.

Toruńczyk
[
Tor23
, Thm. 5.24]
showed that the VC-dimension of a graph
G
is linear in its radius-
1
flip-width.
The proof can be easily adapted for radius-
1
merge-width (see also
[
DT25
, Lemma 7.20]
).

Theorem 2.7
.

Any graph
G
satisfies
\mathrm{VCdim}(G)\leqslant\mathcal{O}(\mw_{1}(G))
.

To illustrate the notion of merge-width and its variants given in the next section, we present a particular class of graphs with small width parameters.

Example 2.8
.

For a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the (perfect) binary tree
T_{m}
of depth
m
, that is, the root has depth
0
and the
2^{m}
leaves have depth
m
.
Let
C_{m}
, the
2^{m}
-vertex
universal cograph
, be the graph whose vertices are the leaves of
T_{m}
, and two different leaves are adjacent in
C^{m}
if and only if their lowest common ancestor in
T_{m}
has odd depth (see
Fig.

1
).

We construct a merge sequence
(\mathcal{P}_{1},R_{1}),\ldots,(\mathcal{P}_{m+1},R_{m+1})
for
C_{m}
.
For any
w\in V(T_{m})
, let
P_{w}
be the leaves in
T_{m}
below (or equal to)
w
.
For
i\leqslant m+1
, define

\mathcal{P}_{i}=\{P_{w}\mid w\in V(T_{m})\text{ has depth $m+1-i$}\}.

Note that whenever two different
v,w\in V(T_{m})
are at the same depth, the sets
P_{v},P_{w}
are homogeneous. Moreover, at any time
i>1
, each part of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is a union of exactly two parts of
\mathcal{P}_{i-1}
. Therefore, taking
R_{i}=\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{i-1}}\binom{P}{2}
, the partition
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is homogeneous modulo
R_{i}
. Clearly,
R_{i}\supset R_{i-1}
.

In the construction above, for any
i\leqslant m
, no two parts of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
are connected by an edge of
R_{i+1}
, so that
\mw_{r}(C_{m})=1
for every
r
. ∎

3
Equivalent Variants of Merge-width

In this section, we define the main variants of merge-width studied
in this paper, and state the obvious relationships between them.

Theorem

1.1
states that the variants presented in this section are all equivalent to the usual notion of merge-width.

3.1
Transient merge-width

Definition 3.1
.

Fix
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
The
radius-
r
transient merge-width
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\tmw_{r}(G)
, is the minimum number
k
such that there is a sequence

(\mathcal{P}_{1},G_{1}),\dots,(\mathcal{P}_{m},G_{m})

with the following properties:

•

\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{2}\preccurlyeq\ldots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{m}
is a sequence of ever coarser partitions of
V(G)
with
\mathcal{P}_{1}
the partition into singletons and
\mathcal{P}_{m}=\{V(G)\}
the partition with one part,

•

G_{i}
is a
\mathcal{P}_{i}
-flip of
G
for all
i\in[m]
, and

•

for every
i\in[m-1]
and
v\in V(G)
, we have

|\mathrm{Ball}^{r}_{G_{i+1}}(v)/\mathcal{P}_{i}|\leqslant k.

A graph class
\mathscr{C}
has
bounded transient merge-width
if for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tmw_{r}(\mathscr{C})<\infty
.

Similarly to the case of merge-width,
equivalently one may require in the definition of transient merge-width that the sequence

\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}_{n}
is a maximal chain of partitions of
V(G)
.

Example 3.2
.

To construct a transient merge sequence for the universal cograph
C_{m}
, take the same sequence of partitions
(\mathcal{P}_{i})_{i\in[m+1]}
as in
Example

2.8
, and let the
\mathcal{P}_{i}
-flip
G_{i}
be the result of flipping every
P_{w}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
with itself whenever
w\in V(T_{m})
has odd depth.
Then, for
i>1
, the connected components of
G_{i}
are precisely the parts of
\mathcal{P}_{i-1}
, hence
\tmw_{r}(C_{m})=1
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
. ∎

Note that every merge sequence gives rise to a transient merge sequence of the same radius-
r
width.
Indeed if
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is homogeneous modulo
R_{t}
, then there is a
\mathcal{P}_{t}
-flip
G_{t}
of
G
such that
E(G_{t})\subseteq R_{t}
, and hence

\mathrm{Ball}^{r}_{G_{t}}(v)\subseteq\mathrm{Ball}^{r}_{R_{t}}(v).
This proves:

Lemma 3.3
.

For every graph
G
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

\tmw_{r}(G)\leqslant\mw_{r}(G).

Comparing to the definition of merge-width, in transient merge-width there is no consistency required between the graphs considered in consecutive step. Therefore,
pairs of vertices which are close at step
t
might be far at step
t+1
;
whereas in merge-width, the monotonicity condition
R_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq R_{m}

implies that vertices that are close at step
t
remain so in the subsequent steps.

3.2
Partition merge-width

Next, we define the weakest notion, where only a sequence of partitions is provided.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a graph and
let
\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\dots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{m}
be a sequence of partitions of
V(G)
, where

\mathcal{P}_{1}
is the partition into singletons and
\mathcal{P}_{m}
has one part.
The
radius-
r
width
of the sequence
\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{m}

is defined using flip metrics as:

\max_{1\leqslant i<m}\max_{v\in V(G)}|\mathrm{Ball}_{\mathcal{P}_{i+1}}^{r}(v)/\mathcal{P}_{i}|.

The
radius-
r
partition merge-width
of
G
, denoted
\pmw_{r}(G)
, is the minimum radius-
r
width of a sequence

\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\dots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{m}
of partitions of
V(G)
as above.
Finally, a graph class
\mathscr{C}
has
bounded partition merge-width
if
\pmw_{r}(\mathscr{C})<\infty
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

Clearly, in the definition of radius-
r
partition merge-width one may equivalently consider only sequences
\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}_{m}
where
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is the partition into singletons and
\mathcal{P}_{m}
has only one part.

Example 3.5
.

For the universal cograph
C_{m}
, the analysis for transient merge-width results in a sequence of partitions
(\mathcal{P}_{i})_{i\in[m+1]}
for
C_{m}
and
\mathcal{P}_{i}
-flips
G_{i}
which witness that for every
i\leqslant m
, any two different parts of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
are at distance
\infty
in the flip metric associated with
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
. Therefore,
\pmw_{r}(C_{m})=1
for every
r
. ∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For every graph
G
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

\pmw_{r}(G)\leqslant\tmw_{r}(G).

Proof.

Assume that

(\mathcal{P}_{1},G_{1}),\ldots,(\mathcal{P}_{m},G_{m})
is a transient merge sequence of radius-
r
width
k
, with
\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\dots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{m}
.
Then the sequence
\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\dots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{m}
has radius-
r
width at most
k
,
as for every
v\in V(G)
and
t\in[m-1]
we have

\mathrm{Ball}^{r}_{\mathcal{P}_{t+1}}(v)\subseteq\mathrm{Ball}^{r}_{G_{t+1}}(v).\qed

In
Theorem

5.1
we show that
\mw_{r}(G)
is bounded in terms of
\pmw_{6r+1}(G)
. Hence, bounded merge-width, bounded transient merge-width and bounded partition merge-width are all equivalent properties of graph classes.

3.3
Definable merge-width

Finally, we consider partition sequences

\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}_{n}

in which the partitions
\mathcal{P}_{i}
are
definable
in the following sense:
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is the partition into atomic types over some set
S_{i}
which forms a prefix of a total order on
V(G)
. This is made precise below.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
G
be a graph and
<
a total order on
V(G)
, and enumerate
V(G)
as
v_{1}<\dots<v_{n}
.
Consider the sequence of partitions
\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\dots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{n}
of
V(G)
, where
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{N}_{S_{i}}
is the partition into atomic types over the set
S_{i}\coloneqq\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n-i}\}
.
The
radius-
r
definable merge-width
of
(G,<)
, denoted
\dmw_{r}(G,<)
, is
defined as:

\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}\max_{v\in V(G)}|\mathrm{Ball}_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}^{r}(v)/\mathcal{P}_{i}|.

Finally, the
radius-
r
definable merge-width
of
G
is minimum of
{\dmw_{r}(G,<)}
over all total orders
<
on
V(G)
.

Example 3.8
.

For the universal cograph
C_{m}
, consider an order
<
on
V(C_{m})
so that for each
i\in[0,m]
, the first
2^{i}
vertices in the order form a transversal of the partition
\mathcal{P}_{m+1-i}
(defined in
Example

2.8
).
One can verify that this order witnesses that
\dmw_{r}(C_{m})
is at most 2 for every
r
.
∎

Observe that in the above definition,
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
by splitting each part
X\in\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
into at most
3
parts:
X\cap N(v_{n-i})
,
X\cap\overline{N}(v_{n-i})
and
X\cap\{v_{n-i}\}
.
This implies the following.

Lemma 3.9
.

For every graph
G
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

\pmw_{r}(G)\leqslant 2\cdot\dmw_{r}(G)+1.

Proof.

Let
k=\dmw_{r}(G)
and
v_{1}<\dots<v_{n}
be the order on
V(G)
such that

\dmw_{r}(G,<)=k.

Consider the corresponding sequence of partitions
\mathcal{P}_{1}\preccurlyeq\dots\preccurlyeq\mathcal{P}_{n}
of
V(G)
, where
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{N}_{S_{i}}
is the partition into atomic types over the set
S_{i}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n-i}\}
.
Let
i<n
and
v\in V(G)
.
For each part
Y\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
, other than
\{v_{n-i}\}
and which intersects
\mathrm{Ball}_{\mathcal{P}_{i+1}}^{r}(v)
,
there exists
X\in\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
such that
Y=X\cap N(v_{n-i})
or
Y=X\cap\overline{N}(v_{n-i})
, and
X\cap\mathrm{Ball}_{\mathcal{P}_{i+1}}^{r}(v)\neq\varnothing
.
So the number of such parts
Y
is bounded by
2k
.
Additionally, the part
\{v_{n-i}\}
itself may intersect
\mathrm{Ball}_{\mathcal{P}_{i+1}}^{r}(v)
.
Therefore,
|\mathrm{Ball}_{\mathcal{P}_{i+1}}^{r}(v)/\mathcal{P}_{i}|\leqslant 2k+1
.
∎

In
Corollary

6.2
we give an upper bound on
\dmw_{r}(G)
expressed as a function of
\mw_{17r+3}(G)
. Together with
Theorem

5.1
, this will prove
Theorem

1.1
,
that bounded merge-width, definable merge-width, partition merge-width, and transient merge-width are all equivalent properties of graph classes.

Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.3
will follow similarly, see
Section

7
.

4
Other Variants

This section discusses three additional variants of merge-width.
The first two — universal and short merge-width — are conjectured to be equivalent to the usual notion of merge-width.
This is known in the special case of bounded expansion and bounded twin-width classes.
The third one — positive merge-width — is a natural variant, which already captures bounded expansion and bounded twin-width classes.
However, we show that it is not stable under complementation.

4.1
Universal merge-width

We start with the concept of universal merge-width, where we require the existence of a single merge sequence witnessing the boundedness of the width for all the radii simultaneously.

Definition 4.1
.

A graph class
\mathscr{C}
has
universally bounded merge-width

if there is a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that any
G\in\mathscr{C}
has a merge sequence with radius-
r
width at most
f(r)
, for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

Clearly, every class of universally bounded merge-width has bounded merge-width. We conjecture the following.

Conjecture 4.2
.

Every class of bounded merge-width has bounded universal merge-width.

4.2
is corroborated by the following facts.
First, it follows from the result of van den Heuvel and Kierstead
[
vK21
]
(see also
[
Sie26
, Thm. 3.23]
)
and the construction in
[
DT25
, Lem. 7.5]
that every class of bounded expansion has universally bounded merge-width. Second, it follows from the construction in
[
DT25
, Lem. 7.2]
that every class of bounded twin-width has universally bounded merge-width. Finally, it follows from the proof of
[
DT25
, Thm. 1.12]

that if
\mathscr{C}
has universally bounded merge-width and
\mathscr{D}
is a first-order transduction of
\mathscr{C}
, then
\mathscr{D}
has universally bounded merge-width. In particular, classes of
structurally bounded expansion

[
Gaj+20
]
(first-order transductions of classes of bounded expansion) have universally bounded merge-width.

In
Theorem

1.6
we show that every class of universally bounded merge-width
is quasi-isometric to a class of bounded expansion.

4.2
Short merge-width

We now turn our attention to the concept of short merge-width, where we require the merge sequence to be of logarithmic length. However, for technical reasons we also assume a bound on the “branching” of a merge sequence, encapsulated in the following parameter: the
valency
of a merge sequence
(\mathcal{P}_{1},R_{1}),\ldots,(\mathcal{P}_{m},R_{m})
is the least
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
i\in[m-1]

6.2

We would like to apply

This requires constructing a monotone sequence of sample sets for its partitions, while ensuring that balls of fixed radius do not contain too many points from the sample set.

Lemma 6.5

Let

and

be a graph with duality of order

Let

consists of a set

is still “sparse”, in the sense that every distance

from
\mathcal{P}_{i}

(5)

Bounded merge-width

Theorem

1.1
states that bounded merge-wid th, bounded partition merge-width, bounded transient merge-width, and bounded definable merge-width are all equivalent properties of graph classes.
As noted earlier, this follows from

Theorem

5.1
,
Lemmas

3.9
,
3.6
and
3.3
, and
Corollary

6.2
.

Infinite radius

Merge-width, and its transient, partition, and definable variants each have an associated limit parameter,
denoted
\mw_{\infty},\tmw_{\infty},\pmw_{\infty}
, and
\dmw_{\infty}
respectively, and defined by the same definitions as the finite-radius variants, but
interpreting
\mathrm{Ball}^{\infty}(v)
as
\bigcup_{r\geqslant 1}\mathrm{Ball}^{r}(v)
in each of the definitions. Equivalently,
\mw_{\infty}(G)=\mw_{r}(G)
where
r\geqslant|V(G)|-1
, and similarly for the remaining variants.
Therefore, by
Lemma

3.3
and
Lemma

3.6
we have:

\pmw_{\infty}(G)\leqslant\tmw_{\infty}(G)\leqslant\mw_{\infty}(G).

Lemma

3.9
implies

\pmw_{\infty}(G)\leqslant 2\cdot\dmw_{\infty}(G)+1.

Furthermore, by
Theorem

5.1
,

\mw_{\infty}(G)
is bounded in terms of
\pmw_{\infty}(G)
, while
by
Corollary

6.2
,

\dmw_{\infty}(G)
is bounded in terms of
\mw_{\infty}(G)
.
Thus, all those parameters are functionally equivalent to each other.
Since
\mw_{\infty}(G)
is functionally equivalent to clique-width
[
DT25
, Thm. 7.1]
, altogether this proves
Theorem

1.2
.

Almost bounded merge-width

Recall that a graph class
\mathscr{C}
has

[

]

if for every fixed

and

for sufficiently large

\mw_{r}(G)\leqslant n^{\varepsilon}
for every
n

.
More concisely, for fixed

we have that
\mw_{r}(G)\leqslant|V(G)|^{o(1)}
holds for all
G\in\mathscr{C}
.
Analogously, we define
almost bounded partition merge-width
,

almost bounded definable merge-width
, and

almost bounded transient merge-width
.

Theorem

1.3
states that for a hereditary graph class

, those four notions are equivalent.

Towards a proof of
Theorem

1.3
,
first observe that for classes
\mathscr{C}
of VC-dimension bounded by a fixed constant

, the four conditions are all equivalent.
This again follows by

,
3.6
and

because each of the bounds relating one notion of merge-width with another in those statements is polynomial, for graphs

To conclude the proof of

it is therefore enough to prove that if

is a

graph class

satisfying either of the four conditions,

has VC-dimension bounded by a constant.
Since almost bounded partition merge-width is the weakest of the four properties (it is implied by each other property), it is enough to prove this for hereditary classes of almost bounded partition-merge width.
To this end, we prove the following lemma, which is an analogue of

Applications

8.1
Sparse quotients, quasi-isometry, and neighbourhood covers

As applications of
Theorem

5.1
,
we show that graphs of bounded merge-width admit partitions such that the quotient graph has bounded strong colouring numbers, and each part has small weak diameter. This will yield two applications: quasi-isometries with graphs of bounded strong colouring numbers, and sparse neighbourhood covers. We remark that the arguments presented in this section have much simpler counterparts in the more restricted setting of graphs of bounded twin-width; they moreover yield better bounds. Hence, for the convenience of the readers interested in twin-width, we provide an exposition in
Appendix

B
.

For a graph
G
and a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
V(G)
, we define the
quotient graph

G/\mathcal{P}
to be the graph obtained from
G
by contracting each part of
\mathcal{P}
into a single vertex.
That is,
V(G/\mathcal{P})=\mathcal{P}
, and for any distinct
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}
,
PQ\in E(G/\mathcal{P})
if and only if there exist
x\in P
,
y\in Q
with
xy\in E(G)
.
A set
X\subseteq V(G)
has
weak diameter

r
in
G
if
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(x,y)\leqslant r
for all
x,y\in X
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Fix
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
G
be a graph and
k=\mw_{18r+1}(G)
.
Then
there is a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
V(G)
satisfying the following:

1.

\mathrm{scol}_{r}(G/\mathcal{P})\leqslant k\cdot\pi_{G}(k)
, and

2.

each part of
\mathcal{P}
has weak diameter at most
12
in
G
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

5.1
, there is a merge sequence

(\mathcal{P}_{1},R_{1}),\dots,(\mathcal{P}_{m},R_{m})

with radius-
3r
width at most
\ell\coloneqq k\cdot\pi_{G}(k)
which is 6-firm, i.e.
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(x,y)\leqslant 6
for all
xy\in R_{m}
.
Since
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is the partition into singletons, we may assume that
R_{1}=\varnothing
.

Say that a part
P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
is
unresolved
if there exists some unresolved edge
xy\in E(G)\setminus R_{i}
with
x\in P
.

Suppose first that the unique part
V(G)
of
\mathcal{P}_{m}
is unresolved (meaning that
E(G)\not\subseteq R_{m}
).
Since there is an unresolved edge, every non-edge must be resolved (in
R_{m}
).
Then for every
x,y\in V(G)
,
if
xy\notin E(G)
then
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(x,y)\leqslant 6
by assumption on the merge sequence,
and if
x,y\in E(G)
then
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(x,y)\leqslant 1
. Hence,
G
has diameter at most 6, and we may take
\mathcal{P}=\{V(G)\}
, which satisfies the conclusion of the lemma.
Henceforth we assume this case does not occur.

Say furthermore that an unresolved part
P
is
maximally unresolved
with
index

i
if
P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
is unresolved,

Definition 8.10

and outputs a single bit: the pair is either accepted or rejected. We require that the Decoder faithfully decodes the adjacency relation of graphs in
\mathscr{C}
from the labels produced by the Encoder in the following sense: for every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
and a pair of distinct vertices

of
G

uv\in E(G)\quad\textrm{if and only if}\quad{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{E}}_{G}(u),{\mathcal{E}}_{G}(v))=\textrm{accept}.

is the function

mapping every

\ell(n)\coloneqq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathscr{C},\\

We may also say that a graph class
\mathscr{C}
admits a labelling scheme of length
\mathscr{F}
, where

is a class of functions (e.g.

), if there is a labelling scheme for
\mathscr{C}
of length

.

Note that the definition of an adjacency labelling scheme does not stipulate any bounds on the time complexity of computing the Encoder and the Decoder, or even assert the computability of those functions. While these aspects can be of course discussed, we do not consider them here.

and
B_{p}
are adjacent in
G/\mathcal{P}_{i}
.

We now argue the following: For each
t\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
, part
B_{t}
has degree at most
k+1
in
G/\mathcal{P}_{i}
. This is clear when
i=n
, for then
B_{t}
is the unique part of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
; so assume
i<n
.
Let
B_{t}^{\prime}
be the unique part of
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
that contains
B_{t}
. Since
B_{t}
contains a maximally dominated part
A_{t}
, it follows that
B_{t}^{\prime}
is not dominated. Hence, in partition
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
,
B_{t}^{\prime}
is non-homogeneous to at most
k
other parts (by the assumptions on the contraction sequence), complete to no other part (for otherwise it would be dominated), and anti-complete to all the other parts; so
B_{t}^{\prime}
has degree at most
k
in
G/\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
. Since
\mathcal{P}_{i+1}
differs from
\mathcal{P}_{i}
only by some two parts being merged, it follows that
B_{t}
has degree at most
k+1
in
G/\mathcal{P}_{i}
.

We conclude that the part
B_{p}
can be reached in
G/\mathcal{P}_{i}
from
A
by a path of length at most
p\leqslant r
such that all parts on this path, except possibly for
B_{p}
itself, have degrees at most
k+1
in
G/\mathcal{P}_{i}
. The number of such parts
B_{p}
is therefore bounded by
(k+1)+(k+1)^{2}+\ldots+(k+1)^{r}
, for every next vertex on the path can be chosen among at most
k+1
possibilities. Since different sets
Q
belonging to
\mathrm{SReach}^{<}_{r}(P)
give rise to different sets
B_{p}
(due to being disjoint), it follows that
|\mathrm{SReach}^{<}_{r}(P)|\leqslant 1+(k+1)+(k+1)^{2}+\ldots+(k+1)^{r}=\frac{(k+1)^{r+1}-1}{k}
. (The additional summand
1
accounts for the part
P
itself.)

\blacksquare

From
B.2
we conclude that
\mathrm{scol}^{<}_{r}(G)\leqslant\frac{(k+1)^{r+1}-1}{k}
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, as claimed.
∎

We now prove the analogue of
Theorem

1.6
.

Theorem B.3
.

For every graph class
\mathscr{C}
of bounded twin-width there exists a graph class
\mathscr{D}
of bounded sparse twin-width such that every graph from
\mathscr{C}
is
(3,2)
-quasi-isometric to some graph from
\mathscr{D}
.

Proof.

For every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{G}
be the partition of the vertex set of
G
provided by
Lemma

B.1
. Let

\mathscr{D}\coloneqq\{G/\mathcal{F}_{G}\colon G\in\mathscr{C}\}.

Since
\mathscr{C}
has bounded twin-width, from
Lemma

B.1
it follows that the
r
-strong colouring numbers of
\mathscr{D}
are bounded by a function of
r
, hence
\mathscr{D}
has bounded expansion. In particular,
\mathscr{D}
is weakly sparse. Further, using the same argument as in
Section

8.1
we may easily see that mapping every vertex
u
of
G
to the part of
\mathcal{F}_{G}
to which
u
belongs yields a
(3,2)
-quasi-isometry from
G
to
G/\mathcal{F}_{G}
.

It remains to show that
\mathscr{D}
also has bounded twin-width. For this, we use a standard argument based on first-order transductions and the fact that every transduction of a class of binary structures of bounded twin-width again has bounded twin-width
[
Bon+21
]
; we assume the reader’s familiarity with these tools. It is well known, see e.g.
[
Bon+21
,
Bon+24a
]
, that if
G
is a graph of twin-width
k
and
\mathcal{P}_{1}\prec\mathcal{P}_{2}\prec\ldots\prec\mathcal{P}_{n}
is a contraction sequence for
G
witnessing this, then there exists a total order
<
on
V(G)
such that the ordered graph
(G,<)
, regarded as a binary structure, again has twin-width at most
k
, and every part
P\in\bigcup_{i\in[n]}\mathcal{P}_{i}
is
convex
in
<
, that is, consist of an interval of elements that are consecutive in
<
. The construction presented in the proof of
Lemma

B.1
produces a partition
\mathcal{F}_{G}
such that every part of
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is also a part of some
\mathcal{P}_{i}
,
i\in[n]
; hence, all the parts of
\mathcal{F}_{G}
are convex in
<
. It now follows that the partition
\mathcal{F}_{G}
can be transduced from the ordered graph
(G,<)
. Namely, it suffices to mark, using a unary predicate, the
<
-minimal element of each part of
\mathcal{F}_{G}
, and then to recover the parts of
\mathcal{F}_{G}
as maximal intervals in
<
that do not contain marked elements (besides the
<
-minimal one). Once partition
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is transduced, we can easily transduce the quotient graph
G/\mathcal{F}_{G}
. This means that
G/\mathcal{F}_{G}
can be transduced, using a fixed first-order transduction, from the ordered graph
(G,<)
of twin-width at most
k
. By the results of
[
Bon+21
]
it now follows that the twin-width of
G/\mathcal{F}_{G}
is bounded by a function of
k
, hence class
\mathscr{D}
has bounded twin-width.
∎

And here is the analogue of
Theorem

8.8
.

Theorem B.4
.

Every graph of twin-width
k
admits a neighbourhood cover of radius
8
and overlap
\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
G
be the graph in question. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the partition of
V(G)
and
<
the ordering of
\mathcal{F}
provided by
Lemma

B.1
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{scol}^{<}_{1}(G/\mathcal{F})\leqslant k+2=\mathcal{O}(k),

\displaystyle\mathrm{scol}^{<}_{2}(G/\mathcal{F})\leqslant k+2+(k+1)^{2}=\mathcal{O}(k^{2}).

Observe that for each
P\in\mathcal{F}
, we have

\mathrm{WReach}^{<}_{2}(P)=\mathrm{SReach}^{<}_{2}(P)\cup\bigcup_{Q\in\mathrm{SReach}^{<}_{1}(P)}\mathrm{SReach}^{<}_{1}(Q).

Hence, we have

\mathrm{wcol}^{<}_{2}(G/\mathcal{F})\leqslant\mathrm{scol}^{<}_{2}(G/\mathcal{F})+\mathrm{scol}^{<}_{1}(G/\mathcal{F})^{2}\leqslant\mathcal{O}(k^{2}).

From
Lemma

8.7
we conclude that
G/\mathcal{F}
admits a neighbourhood cover
\mathcal{K}
of radius
2
and overlap
\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
. Since each part
P\in\mathcal{F}
has weak diameter at most
2
, the same argument as in the proof of
Lemma

8.1
shows that
\{\bigcup C\colon C\in\mathcal{K}\}
is a neighborhood cover of
G
of radius
8
and overlap
\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
.
∎

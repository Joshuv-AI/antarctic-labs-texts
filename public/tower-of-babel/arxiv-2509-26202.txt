Perron-Frobenius theorem for dual tensors and its applications Journal:
Changjiang Bu, Yue Chu, Qingying Zhang, Jiang Zhou

Abstract
The Perron-Frobenius theorem of nonnegative matrices is a classical result on spectral theory of matrices, which has wide applications in many domains. In this paper, we give the Perron-Frobenius theorem for dual tensors, that is, a dual tensor with weakly irreducible nonnegative standard part has a positive dual eigenvalue with a positive dual eigenvector. We give an explicit formula for the dual part of the positive dual eigenvector by using generalized inverses of an M M -matrix. By considering the natural correspondence between tensors (matrices) and hypergraphs (graphs), some basic properties on the positive dual eigenvalue and positive dual eigenvector of hypergraphs are obtained. As applications, we introduce dual centrality measures for vertices of graphs and hypergraphs. By introducing a dual perturbation, vertices that are tied under eigenvector centrality can be effectively distinguished. In our numerical experiments, by perturbing specific structures, we successfully differentiated vertices in regular graphs and hypergraphs that were previously indistinguishable.

Dual numbers were first introduced by Clifford in 1873 as a number system extending the real numbers through the introduction of a new element
\epsilon

, where

are real vectors of dimension
n
. A dual number
\lambda
(resp. dual vector

(resp.

[

]

[

,

]
and perturbation analysis
[

,
28
]
, etc.

The Perron-Frobenius theorem of nonnegative matrices
[

]
has wide applications in graph theory
[

]
, network science
[
7
]
, Google’s PageRank search engine
[
20
]
and system theory
[
1
]
.etc. Qi and Cui
[
27
]
extended the Perron-Frobenius theory to dual number matrices with irreducible nonnegative standard parts, and use their theoretical results to study the perturbation analysis for the dual Markov chain and the perturbed Markov chain.

There exists a natural correspondence between nonnegative tensors (matrices) and hypergraphs (graphs).
The Perron-Frobenius theorem of nonnegative tensors has been established in

The adjacency tensor of a connected hypergraph is a weakly irreducible nonnegative tensor, and it has a positive eigenvalue (spectral radius) with a positive eigenvector.

Centrality measures aim to identify the most important vertices in networks.
Over the years, researchers have proposed many centrality measures

Dual tensors are tensors whose entries are dual numbers. We will investigate the spectral properties of a dual tensor and its applications in the centrality of hypergraphs. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, some definitions, notations and auxiliary lemmas are introduced. Eigenvalues of dual tensors

Let
\mathcal{A}

, where
\mathcal{A}_{s}
and

\mathcal{I}
denote a diagonal tensor such that every diagonal entry is

For a dual vector

, and let

whose

-th component is

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}x^{m-1})_{i}=\sum_{i_{2},...,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}.

If there exists dual number
\lambda

) such that

is an

of

with an associated eigenvector

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{s}+\mathcal{A}_{d}\epsilon
be a dual tensor of order
m
and dimension
n
. For dual number
\lambda=\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{d}\epsilon
and dual vector
x=x_{s}+x_{d}\epsilon
, we have
\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}=\lambda x^{[m-1]}
if and only if
\mathcal{A}_{s}x_{s}^{m-1}=\lambda_{s}x_{s}^{[m-1]}
and

\displaystyle Mx_{d}=(\mathcal{A}_{d}-\lambda_{d}\mathcal{I})x_{s}^{m-1},

where
M=(m-1)\lambda_{s}({\rm diag}((x_{s})_{1},\ldots,(x_{s})_{n}))^{m-2}-\sum_{k=2}^{m}A^{(k)}
,
A^{(k)}
is an
n\times n
matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is (
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
)

\displaystyle(A^{(k)})_{ij}=\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{k-1},i_{k+1},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{n}(\mathcal{A}_{s})_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{k-1}ji_{k+1}\cdots i_{m}}(x_{s})_{i_{2}}\cdots(x_{s})_{i_{k-1}}(x_{s})_{i_{k+1}}\cdots(x_{s})_{i_{m}}.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{A}_{s}
is symmetry, then
M=(m-1)(\lambda_{s}({\rm diag}((x_{s})_{1},\ldots,(x_{s})_{n}))^{m-2}-A)
, where
A
is an
n\times n
symmetry matrix with entries

\displaystyle(A)_{ij}=\sum_{i_{3},i_{4},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{n}(\mathcal{A}_{s})_{iji_{3}i_{4}\cdots i_{m}}(x_{s})_{i_{3}}(x_{s})_{i_{4}}\cdots(x_{s})_{i_{m}}~(1\leq i\leq j\leq n).

Proof.

By
\lambda=\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{d}\epsilon
and
x=x_{s}+x_{d}\epsilon
we have

\displaystyle\lambda x^{[m-1]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda x_{s}^{[m-1]}+\lambda(m-1)({\rm diag}((x_{s})_{1},\ldots,(x_{s})_{n}))^{m-2}x_{d}\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{s}x_{s}^{[m-1]}+\epsilon\lambda_{d}x_{s}^{[m-1]}+\lambda_{s}(m-1)({\rm diag}((x_{s})_{1},\ldots,(x_{s})_{n}))^{m-2}x_{d}\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{s}x_{s}^{[m-1]}+\epsilon(\lambda_{d}x_{s}^{[m-1]}+\lambda_{s}(m-1)({\rm diag}((x_{s})_{1},\ldots,(x_{s})_{n}))^{m-2}x_{d}),

where
(x_{s})_{i}
is the
i
-th component of
x_{s}
.

By

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}=\mathcal{A}_{s}x^{m-1}+\epsilon\mathcal{A}_{d}x^{m-1}=\mathcal{A}_{s}x^{m-1}+\epsilon\mathcal{A}_{d}x_{s}^{m-1}.

By computation, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s}x^{m-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s}x_{s}^{m-1}+\left(\sum_{k=2}^{m}A^{(k)}\right)x_{d}\epsilon,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s}x_{s}^{m-1}+\epsilon\left(\left(\sum_{k=2}^{m}A^{(k)}\right)x_{d}+\mathcal{A}_{d}x_{s}^{m-1}\right),

where
A^{(k)}
is an

-entry is (

)

\displaystyle(A^{(k)})_{ij}=\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{k-1},i_{k+1},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{n}(\mathcal{A}_{s})_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{k-1}ji_{k+1}\cdots i_{m}}(x_{s})_{i_{2}}\cdots(x_{s})_{i_{k-1}}(x_{s})_{i_{k+1}}\cdots(x_{s})_{i_{m}}.

Hence
\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}=\lambda x^{[m-1]}
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s}x_{s}^{m-1}+\epsilon\left(\left(\sum_{k=2}^{m}A^{(k)}\right)x_{d}+\mathcal{A}_{d}x_{s}^{m-1}\right)=\lambda_{s}x_{s}^{[m-1]}

\displaystyle+\epsilon(\lambda_{d}x_{s}^{[m-1]}+\lambda_{s}(m-1)({\rm diag}((x_{s})_{1},\ldots,(x_{s})_{n}))^{m-2}x_{d}),

\displaystyle\left((m-1)\lambda_{s}({\rm diag}((x_{s})_{1},\ldots,(x_{s})_{n}))^{m-2}-\sum_{k=2}^{m}A^{(k)}\right)x_{d}=(\mathcal{A}_{d}-\lambda_{d}\mathcal{I})x_{s}^{m-1}.

∎

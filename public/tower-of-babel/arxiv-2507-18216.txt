Quantum ergodicity for contact metric structures
Lino Benedetto

Abstract
. This paper is dedicated to the proof of a Quantum Ergodicity (QE) theorem for the eigenfunctions of subLaplacians on contact metric manifolds, under the assumption that the Reeb flow is ergodic. To do so, we rely on a semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus developed for general filtered manifolds that we specialize to the setting of contact manifolds. Our strategy is then reminiscent of an implementation of the Born-Oppenheimer approximation as we rely on the construction of microlocal projectors in our calculus which commute with the subLaplacian, called Landau projectors . The subLaplacian is then shown to act effectively on the range of each Landau projector as the Reeb vector field does. The remainder of the proof follows the classical path towards QE, once microlocal Weyl laws have been established.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Quantum Ergodicity (QE) theorems are concerned with the delocalization (or concentration) properties of stationary states of quantum systems in the high-energy limit. Mathematically, a version of QE can be expressed as follows: let
M
be a compact metric space, endowed with a measure
\mu
, and let
P
denotes a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(M,\mu)
, bounded by below and having a compact resolvent (and hence a discrete spectrum). Let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb N}}
be an orthonormal Hilbert basis consisting of eigenfunctions of
P
, and associated with the sequence of eigenvalues
\lambda_{0}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\dots\rightarrow+\infty
. We say that QE holds for this eigenbasis if there exist a probability measure
\nu
on
M
and a density-one sequence
(k_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb N}}
of integers such that

|\varphi_{k_{j}}|^{2}d\mu\rightharpoonup\nu\quad\mbox{when}\ j\rightarrow+\infty.

QE theorems have their origin in the seminal work of A. Schnirelman
[
36
]
, and have been mainly stated in terms of the usual pseudodifferential calculus whith
M
being a compact Riemannian manifold and
P
the Laplace-Beltrami operator. QE is then established under the assumption of ergodicity of the geodesic flow on the unit cotangent bundle for the Liouville measure, see
[
9
,
40
]
. Such a result has been extended to the setting of manifolds with boundary in
[
25
,
41
]
, to the semiclassical regime in
[
26
]
, to the case of discontinuous metrics
[
27
]
, as well as for Laplace and Dirac operators acting on bundles in
[
28
]
. However, the operators studied in the vast majority of these papers all share the property of being
elliptic
.

To our knowledge, the only QE theorem proved in the literature for non-elliptic operators is given by
[
10
, Theorem A]
, which is stated on a closed 3D subRiemannian (sR) manifold and where the operator
P
is given by its associated subLaplacian. Here, the ergodicity assumption falls onto the Reeb flow induced by the natural contact structure of such a sR manifold. In the broader question of finding invariance properties of quantum limits for such a subLaplacian, we also mention the work
[
2
]
, where the addition of a semiclassical parameter allows for a precise multiscale asymptotic analysis and where the Reeb flow is also shown to play a predominant role.

The present paper is concerned with an extension of the result presented in
[
10
]
to any odd-dimensional closed contact manifold
(M^{2d+1},\eta)
endowed with a contact metric structure
(\phi,g)
, i.e.
g
is an associated Riemannian metric and
\phi
is an almost complex structure on the contact distribution

\mathcal{D}=\ker\eta,

all compatible with each other in the sense that they satisfy

\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}),\quad g(X,\phi Y)=d\eta(X,Y),

see Section
2.2
.

For such manifolds, the Riemannian volume form is in fact independent of the associated metric
g
and is a multiplicative constant of the contact volume form
\eta\wedge(d\eta)^{d}
. We denote this normalized volume form by
\nu
.

The subLaplacian associated with the subRiemannian contact structure
(M^{2d+1},\eta,g)
and with the measure
\nu
(see Section
2.3
for a precise definition), that we denote by
\Delta_{sR}
, is a differential operator which is essentially self-adjoint, hypoelliptic and which has discrete point spectrum given by the sequence

0\leq\delta_{0}\leq\delta_{1}\leq\dots\leq\delta_{k}\leq\dots\rightarrow+\infty,

with repetitions according to multiplicity. Moreover, there exists a Hilbert basis
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb N}}
, such that for every
k\in{\mathbb N}
,

-\Delta_{sR}\varphi_{k}=\delta_{k}\varphi_{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(M,\nu)}=1.

Our main result is then the following one.

Theorem 1.1
.

We assume that the Reeb flow is ergodic on
(M,\nu)
. Then, for any orthonormal Hilbert basis
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb N}}
consisting of eigenfunctions of the subLaplacian
-\Delta_{sR}
associated with the eigenvalues
(\delta_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb N}}
, there exists a density-one sequence
(k_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb N}}
of integers such that

\int_{M}a(x)|\varphi_{k_{j}}(x)|^{2}\,d\nu(x)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{M}a(x)\,d\nu(x),

for any continuous function
a
on
M
.

Observe that by Section
2.4.1
, any 3D closed sR manifold defines a contact metric structure, and we recover in particular the result of
[
10
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

We mention however that
[
10
, Theorem A]
has a more microlocal flavor than ours in the 3 dimensional case, since it is formulated in terms of classical pseudodifferential calculus. Up to cumbersome additions, Theorem
1.1
could also be stated microlocally, but we would ultimately not be able to compare it directly with
[
10
, Theorem A]
since our pseudodifferential calculi differ. More importantly, as soon as we consider contact metric manifolds of dimension higher than 5, the classical dynamic underlying the high-energy limit of eigenfunctions is given by a collection of partial connections lifting the Reeb flow to so-called
Landau bundles
, see Section
5.2.1
. Thus, identifying the semiclassical measure (as defined in
[
5
]
) of a density-one subsequence of eigenfunctions would require a stronger assumption than the ergodicity of the Reeb flow, as in the spirit of
[
28
]
.

1.2.
Strategy of the proof

Similarly to any QE result, the first step towards relating the high-energy limit of eigenfunctions with a certain classical flow is to lift the density measures
|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\,d\nu
,
k\geq 0
, on
M
to a certain phase space. Classically, the phase space of a smooth manifold
M
is taken to be the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
. However, for a contact manifold, seen as a filtered manifold, another choice can be made that is better suited to the anisotropic nature of the subLaplacian
\Delta_{sR}
.

Following the presentation of
[
19
]
, we can associate with
M^{2d+1}
its
osculating Lie algebra bundle

\mathfrak{g}M
, which is a vector bundle locally modeled on the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
. Using the exponential map of nilpotent Lie groups, we can also consider its
tangent group bundle

GM
, where
G_{x}M={\rm Exp}(\mathfrak{g}_{x}M)
, which will replace to all effects the usual tangent bundle
TM
. Our choice of phase space is then given by the
dual tangent bundle
defined by

\widehat{G}M:=\{(x,\pi)\,:\,x\in M,\,\pi\in\widehat{G_{x}M}\},

where
\widehat{G_{x}M}
denotes the topological set of strongly continuous irreducible unitary representations of
G_{x}M
, up to equivalence.

All these bundles admit nice local trivializations described in Section
3
, thanks to the existence of well-suited local frames of
TM
, called
\phi
-frames, see Section
2.2
. In particular, we identify a dense open subset of the dual tangent bundle, denoted by
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
, that is homeomorphic to the
characteristic manifold
of
M
, a smooth and symplectic manifold.

These bundles lead to the following definition of symbols. Indeed, the symbols we introduce are fields of operators

\sigma=\{\sigma(x,\pi):\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\,:\,(x,\pi)\in\widehat{G}M\},

where
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}
denotes the subspace of smooth vectors of the space of representation
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
of
\pi
.

In fact, we can define symbol classes
S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)
,
m\in{\mathbb R}\cup{-\infty}
. Introducing a semiclassical parameter
\hbar>0
, we also define global semiclassical quantization procedures of such symbols, see Section
4.2
. The resulting semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus is denoted by

\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M),\ m\in{\mathbb R}\cup{-\infty}.

In particular, we have

-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{2}(M),

and we denote by
H\in S^{2}(\widehat{G}M)
its principal symbol. Using local trivializations of
\widehat{G}M
given by
\phi
-frames, the symbol
H
can be shown to be described by a field of harmonic oscillators, more precisely by a sum of
d
one-dimensional harmonic oscillators without resonances. The absence of resonances allows us to distinguish the different Landau level of the symbol
H
and to define the associated projector
\Pi_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb N}
, as homogeneous symbols in
\dot{S}^{0}(\widehat{G}M)
, see Section
4.3
.

Fixing a global quantization procedure
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}
, we observe that the symbolic calculus gives us for every
n\in{\mathbb N}
,

[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR},{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n})]\in\hbar\Psi_{\hbar}^{1}(M)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n})\circ{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n})={\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n})+\hbar\Psi_{\hbar}^{-1}(M).

Thus, up to a remainder of order
\mathscr{O}(\hbar)
, the quantization of the symbol
\Pi_{n}
gives rise to a projector commuting with the subLaplacian. Section
4.3
is then devoted to show that, in the spirit of the implementation of a Born-Oppenheimer approximation, by following the ideas of
[
6
]
, this remainder can be improved to any power of
\hbar
, i.e up to
\mathscr{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
, up to correcting the symbols
\Pi_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb N}
, with lower order symbols. The resulting operators, that we denote here by
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\hbar}
, are called
Landau projectors
.

The crucial observation that we make is that the subLaplacian acts on the range of the Landau projector
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\hbar}
,
n\in{\mathbb N}
, as does the operator

\hbar^{2}(2n+d)|R|+\mathscr{O}(\hbar^{2}),

see Proposition
5.4
. From this result, we are able to prove an Egorov theorem for observables of the form
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\hbar}A\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\hbar}
, with
A\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
, that we refer to as
Toeplitz operators
, see Theorem
5.6
.

In order to apply the now classical path towards QE, as presented, for instance, in
[
1
]
, we are left with computing microlocal Weyl laws for observables in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
. We do so using the functional calculus of the subLaplacian, which is shown by Theorem
4.15
to be part of the semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
for smooth functions with compact support of
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}
. The final arguments for the proof of Theorem
1.1
are given in Section
6.2
.

Remark 1.3
.

We mention here that the crucial argument in both
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
is a Birkhoff normal form for the cometric
g_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
close to its zero locus, i.e the charateristic manifold. The proof of this normal form, as presented in
[
10
, Appendix C]
, is divided into two parts: if the first part would certainly be extended to the setting of contact metric manifolds due to the absence of resonances discussed above, the second part, referred to there as
Melrose normal form
, is, however, specific to dimension 3 due to the same absence of resonances.

We point out that this strategy towards QE on contact metric manifolds should be robust enough to be adapted to the setting of quasi-contact manifolds of dimension 4, as well as to Engel manifolds, where the high-energy limit is expected to exhibit geometric flows in relation with
abnormal geodesics
.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we recall the main definitions and properties concerning contact manifolds and their associated compatible metrics. We also define the subLaplacians we wish to investigate and present a list of examples of contact metric manifolds which can be of particular interest.

Section
3
describes the tangent group bundle of contact metric manifolds seen as filtered manifolds. We recall there the harmonic analysis of the Heisenberg group, as well as make precise the relation between the dual tangent bundle and the characteristic manifold of the contact manifold.
In Section
4
, we use these bundles to give a precise geometric description of the semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus for filtered manifolds (see
[
5
]
) in the case of contact metric manifolds. We conclude the section by constructing the Landau projectors.

Section
5
is dedicated to the proof of Egorov theorem for Toeplitz operators, while Section
6
focuses on microlocal Weyl laws and on the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Finally, Appendix
A
presents the semiclassical functional calculus for the subLaplacians.

2.
Contact metric structures

This section is devoted to the definitions and main properties satisfied by contact manifolds and contact metric structures, and is largely inspired from
[
7
]
where the reader will find the proofs of the statement discussed below.

2.1.
Contact manifolds

We begin with fundamental definitions and properties of contact manifolds.

2.1.1.
Generalities

A
contact manifold
is the data of a smooth manifold
M^{2d+1}
together with a 1-form
\eta

satisfying

\eta\wedge(d\eta)^{d}\neq 0.

The 1-form
\eta
is called the
contact form
, and in particular the
(2d+1)
-form
\eta\wedge(d\eta)^{d}
gives
M
an orientation by defining a volume element.
In what follows, we will denote by
\mathcal{D}
the
contact distribution
, defined by

\forall x\in M,\quad\mathcal{D}_{x}:=\{V\in T_{x}M\,:\,\eta(V)=0\}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(M^{2d+1},\eta)
be a contact manifold. There exists a unique global vector field
R
, called the
Reeb vector field

of the contact structure
\eta
, satisfying the following relations:

\eta(R)=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad d\eta(R,\cdot)=0.

A diffeomorphism
f
between two contact manifolds
(M_{1},\eta_{1})
and
(M_{2},\eta_{2})
is called a
contact transformation
if we have

f^{*}\eta_{2}=\tau\eta_{1}
for some nonvanishing function
\tau
. If the function
\tau
is equal to 1, one then talks about
strict contact transformation
.
The next theorem indicates that every two contact structures can be sent on one another by a strict contact transformation locally.

Theorem 2.2

(Darboux coordinates)
.

For each point of a contact manifold
(M^{2d+1},\eta)
, there exist a system of local coordinates
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d},y_{1},\dots,y_{d},r)
with
respect to which we have

\eta=dr-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(y_{i}dx_{i}-x_{i}dy_{i}).

In such a Darboux coordinate system, the Reeb vector field is simply given by

R=\partial_{r}.

An important geometric object associated with a contact manifold is its symplectification
\Sigma^{2d+2}
, also called its
characteristic manifold
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(M^{2d+1},\eta)
be a contact manifold. We denote by
\Sigma\subset T^{*}M
the annihilator
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\setminus o
of the contact distribution
\mathcal{D}=\ker\eta
, where
o
denotes the zero section. It coincides with the cone of non-zero contact forms defining
\mathcal{D}
:

\Sigma=\{(x,\lambda\eta_{x})\in T^{*}M\,:\,x\in M,\,\lambda\in{\mathbb R}\setminus\{0\}\}.

The last expression of
\Sigma
allows us to see it as a foliation parametrized by
\lambda
, in the sense that

\Sigma=\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\mathbb R}\setminus\{0\}}\Sigma_{\lambda},

where
\Sigma_{\lambda}=\{(x,\lambda\eta_{x})\in T^{*}M\,:\,x\in M\}
. We have the following important result on the characteristic manifold. In what follows, we denote by
\omega
the symplectic 2-form on
T^{*}M
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The restriction
\omega_{|\Sigma}
defines a symplectic 2-form on
\Sigma
. Moreover, the Hamiltonian vector field
\vec{\lambda}
associated with the Hamiltonian

(x,\lambda\eta_{x})\in\Sigma\mapsto\lambda\in{\mathbb R},

leaves
\Sigma_{\lambda}
invariant for
\lambda\in{\mathbb R}\setminus\{0\}
and projects onto the Reeb vector field
R
.

Using the parameterization
j:(x,\lambda)\in M\times{\mathbb R}\setminus\{0\}\mapsto(x,\lambda\eta_{x})\in\Sigma
, the restricted symplectic form
\omega_{|\Sigma}
is given by

\omega_{|\Sigma}=j^{*}(\omega)=-(d\lambda\wedge\eta+\lambda d\eta).

In particular, the associated symplectic volume form is given by

(2.1)

\omega_{|\Sigma}^{d+1}=(-1)^{d+1}\lambda^{d}\,d\lambda\wedge\eta\wedge(d\eta)^{d}.

2.1.2.
Heisenberg group

The contact structure on
{\mathbb R}^{2d+1}
given by the 1-form described in Theorem
2.2
will be identified with that of the Heisenberg group of dimension
2d+1
.
Indeed, we introduce the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
, called the
Heisenberg Lie algebra
, generated by the vector fields

\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{d},\tilde{Y}_{1},\dots,\tilde{Y}_{d},R,

satisfying the following commutator relations: for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,

(2.2)

[\tilde{X}_{i},\tilde{Y}_{j}]=\delta_{i,j}R\quad\mbox{and}\quad[\tilde{X}_{i},\tilde{X}_{j}]=[\tilde{Y}_{i},\tilde{Y}_{j}]=0,

as well as any commutator with
R
set to 0. We introduce the notation

\mathfrak{v}={\rm span}(\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{d},\tilde{Y}_{1},\dots,\tilde{Y}_{d})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathfrak{r}={\rm span}(R),

as well as
\mathfrak{p}={\rm span}(\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{d})
and
\mathfrak{q}={\rm span}(\tilde{Y}_{1},\dots,\tilde{Y}_{d})
. Then, we have the following gradation of
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
, adapted to the Lie bracket,

(2.3)

\mathfrak{h}^{d}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{r}.

We denote by
\mathbb{H}^{d}
the following realization on
{\mathbb R}^{2d+1}
of the
Heisenberg group
, i.e the unique (up to isomorphism) connected simply connected nilpotent Lie group
with Lie algebra given by
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
:

(2.4)

\tilde{X}_{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}-\frac{y_{i}}{2}\partial_{r},\quad\tilde{Y}_{i}=\partial_{y_{i}}+\frac{x_{i}}{2}\partial_{r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad R=\partial_{r}.

This corresponds to realizing the Heisenberg group on the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
through the exponential map

{\rm Exp}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}:\mathfrak{h}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{d}.

Indeed, for
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
, writing
h_{1}={\rm Exp}(V_{1}+R_{1})
and
h_{2}={\rm Exp}(V_{2}+R_{2})
, with
V_{i}\in\mathfrak{v}
and
R_{i}\in\mathfrak{r}
,
i=1,2
, we have by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula

h_{1}*h_{2}={\rm Exp}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}(V+R),\quad\mbox{with}\ V=v_{1}+v_{2}\in\mathfrak{v}\ \mbox{and}\ R=r_{1}+r_{2}+\frac{1}{2}[v_{1},v_{2}]\in\mathfrak{r},

as well as
h^{-1}={\rm Exp}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}(-V-R)
.

In anticipation of the next sections, we endow the Heisenberg group with the Riemannian metric
g{H}
defined by setting the left-invariant vector fields
(\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{d},\tilde{Y}_{1},\dots,\tilde{Y}_{d},R)
as a global orthonormal
frame. The corresponding
Haar measure
is then simply the Lebesgue measure in the coordinates
(x_{i},y_{i},z)
, and we denote it by
\mu{H}
.

2.1.3.
Principal circle bundles

A particular family of contact manifolds that will interest us in the applications is given by the principal circle bundles. We recall here elementary facts about them.

Let
P
and
M
be smooth manifolds, and
{\rm pr}:P\rightarrow M
a smooth map of
P
onto
M
. Then
(P,M)
is called a
principal circle bundle
if

(i)

\mathbb{S}^{1}
acts freely on
P
;

(ii)

for all
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
, we have
{\rm pr}(p_{1})={\rm pr}(p_{2})
if, and only if, there exists
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
p_{1}=p_{2}\cdot\theta
;

(iii)

P
is locally trivial over
M
, with local trivializations commuting with the action of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Thus, on any principal circle bundle
(P,M)
, one can consider the vector field given by the action of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
on
P
, which we will denote by
\partial_{\theta}
.

We will denote by
\mathcal{P}(M,\mathbb{S}^{1})
the set of all principal circle bundles over
M
. It is a fundamental result of the theory of principal circle bundles that this set can be given the structure of an abelian group with the existence of an isomorphism

\mathcal{P}(M,\mathbb{S}^{1})\cong H^{2}(M,{\mathbf{Z}}),

i.e with the second group of cohomology of
M
with integer coefficients, which we discuss now.

A
connection
on a principal circle bundle
(P,M)
is the datum of a smooth distribution
H\subset TP
invariant by the action of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and which gives the following decomposition of the tangent space:

\forall p\in P,\quad T_{p}P=H_{p}\oplus V_{p},\quad\mbox{where}\ V=\ker{\rm pr}_{*},

and
V
is called the
vertical bundle
. One can then define in a unique way a non-zero 1-form
\eta
whose kernel is given by the distribution
H
and by
\eta(\partial_{\theta})=1
, called the
connection form
.

One can then consider the 2-form
\Phi=d\eta
, called the
curvature form
, which happens to be invariant by the action of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Therefore, there exists a unique 2-form
\Omega
on
M
such that

\Phi={\rm pr}^{*}\Omega.

One can show that the cohomology class of
\Omega
is independent of the choice of connection on
P
, called the
characteristic class
of
P
.

A fundamental result concerning principal circle bundles is the following one.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a 2-form on
M
representing an element of
H^{2}(M,{\mathbf{Z}})
. Then there exists a principal bundle
P
and a connection form
\eta
on
P
such that
d\eta={\rm pr}^{*}\Omega
.

Moreover, one observes that if
\Omega
is symplectic, then
(P,\eta)
is a contact manifold. This type of contact manifold is sometimes called a
Boothby-Wang fibration
.

2.2.
Associated metrics

We now present here the class of metrics that we will consider.

2.2.1.
Almost contact structures and compatible metrics

In order to do so, we first begin by enlarging our geometric setting by defining almost complex structures on contact distributions.

Definition 2.6
.

An odd-dimensional smooth manifold
M^{2d+1}
is said to have an
almost contact structure
if it admits a tensor
field
\phi
of type
(1,1)
, a vector field
R
, and a 1-form
\eta
satisfying

\phi^{2}=-I+\eta\otimes R\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta(R)=1.

From this definition, we readily deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
M^{2d+1}
has an almost contact structure
(\phi,R,\eta)
, then we have

\phi(R)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta\circ\phi=0.

Moreover, the endomorphism
\phi
has rank
2d
.

We now begin to define the class of Riemannian metrics that we will deal with.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(M,\phi,R,\eta)
be an almost contact structure. A Riemannian metric
g
on
M
is said to
be
compatible
if it satisfies the relation

(2.5)

\forall x\in M,\,\forall X,Y\in T_{x}M,\quad g(\phi X,\phi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y).

The manifold
M
is then said to be endowed with an
almost contact metric structure
.

Remark 2.9
.

We observe that for such a manifold, we immediately have

\eta(X)=g(X,R),

and thus
R
is a unit vector field.

In any (sufficiently small) open subset in a manifold
M^{2d+1}
with an almost contact metric structure
(\phi,R,\eta,g)
, we can
exhibit useful orthonormal frames that we now describe. Let
U\subset M
be a coordinate neighborhood, and let
X_{1}
be any unit (for the metric
g
) vector field on
U

orthogonal to
R
. We then define the vector field
Y_{1}
on
U
by

Y_{1}=\phi(X_{1}).

By the compatibility relation given by Equation (
2.5
),
Y_{1}
is a unit vector field orthogonal to both
X_{1}
and
R
.
We now choose a unit vector field
X_{2}
orthogonal to
R
,
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
. Similarly to before, we set
Y_{2}=\phi(X_{2})
, which is a unit vector field orthogonal to
R
,
X_{1}
,
Y_{1}
and
X_{2}
. Proceeding recursively, we obtain a local orthonormal frame

\mathbb{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d},R),

which we called a
\phi
-frame
.

Using a
\phi
-frame to trivialize the tangent bundle, the
(1,1)
-tensor field
\phi
is then given by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&-I_{d}&0\\
I_{d}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Such a frame also allows us to see that the structural group of an almost contact metric structure is given by
O(2d)\times 1\cap Sp(2d)\times 1=U(d)\times 1
.
The last assertion means that the transition map from a
\phi
-frame to another one on the intersection of their support is given by the action
of
U(d)\times 1
.

2.2.2.
Contact metric structures

Considering a contact manifold
(M,\eta)
with Reeb vector field R, one can ask if it is possible to find a
(1,1)
- tensor field
\phi
and a metric
g
such that
(M,\phi,R,\eta,g)
defines an almost contact metric structure. The answser is given by the following result.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(M^{2d+1},\eta)
be a contact manifold and
R
its associated Reeb vector field. Then there exist a
(1,1)
-tensor field
\phi
and a metric
g

such that
(M,\phi,R,\eta,g)
is an almost contact metric structure and satisfying moreover the relation

\forall x\in M,\,\forall X,Y\in T_{x}M,\quad g(X,\phi Y)=d\eta(X,Y).

Reformulating this result, the previous theorem tells us that any contact manifold can be endowed with a contact metric structure, in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 2.11
.

If
(M^{2d+1},\phi,R,\eta,g)
is an almost contact structure for which the relation

(2.6)

\forall x\in M,\,\forall X,Y\in T_{x}M,\quad g(X,\phi Y)=d\eta(X,Y),

holds, then the metric
g
is said to be an
associated metric
and the manifold
M
is said to be endowed with a
contact metric structure
.

Equivalently, given a contact manifold
(M^{2d+1},\eta)
with Reeb vector field
R
, a Riemannian metric
g
is an associated metric if firstly,

\eta(X)=g(X,R),

and secondly, there exists a
(1,1)
-tensor field
\phi
such that

(2.7)

\phi^{2}=-I+\eta\otimes R\quad\mbox{and}\quad d\eta(X,Y)=g(X,\phi Y).

Since we are more interested in the metric
g
than in the tensor field
\phi
, we will often simply write
(M^{2d+1},\eta,g)
for a contact metric structure,
the existence of
\phi
satisfying (
2.7
) being implicit.

On a given contact manifold, there exist many associated metrics. However, their volume forms all coincide.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
(M^{2d+1},\eta)
be a contact manifold and
g
an associated metric. Then

d{\rm vol}_{g}=\frac{(-1)^{d}}{2^{d}d!}\eta\wedge(d\eta)^{d}.

Remark 2.13
.

The last proposition also means that the volume form coincides, up to a multiplicative constant, with the Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
in any Darboux coordinates. In particular, it is invariant by the Reeb flow generated by
R
.

In what follows, we shall drop the index
g
when talking about the volume form and simply write
{\rm vol}
.

2.2.3.
Symplectic normal bundle

Another crucial geometric object associated with the characteristic manifold of a contact manifold (see Definition
2.3
) is its symplectic orthogonal bundle. Indeed, since
\Sigma
is symplectic, for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we have the symplectic orthogonal decomposition

(2.8)

T_{\sigma}(T^{*}M)=T_{\sigma}\Sigma\oplus\mathrm{orth}_{\omega}(T_{\sigma}\Sigma),

where the notation
\mathrm{orth}_{\omega}
stands for the symplectic
\omega
-orthogonal complement.
We define the symplectic normal bundle
N\Sigma
of
\Sigma
by

N\Sigma=\{(\sigma,w)\,:\,\sigma\in\Sigma,\ w\in N_{\sigma}\Sigma\}\,\subset\,T(T^{*}M),

where

N_{\sigma}\Sigma=\mathrm{orth}_{\omega}(T_{\sigma}\Sigma)
.
The set
N\Sigma
is a vector bundle over
\Sigma
and the manifold
\Sigma
is canonically embedded in
N\Sigma
, by the zero section.

Considering a local
\phi
-frame
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d},R)
in
U
, and denoting by
h_{V}
the hamiltonian on
T^{*}M
associated to any vector field
V\in\Gamma(M)
, we have

T\Sigma_{U}=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq d}\ker dh_{X_{i}}\cap\ker dh_{Y_{i}}=\{w\in T(T^{*}_{U}M)\,:\,\forall i\in\{1,\dots,d\},\,dh_{X_{i}}.w=dh_{Y_{i}}.w=0\}\\
=\{w\in T(T^{*}_{U}M)\,:\,\forall i\in\{1,\dots,d\},\,\omega(\vec{h}_{X_{i}},w)=\omega(\vec{h}_{Y_{i}},w)=0\},

and thus

N_{\sigma}\Sigma={\rm span}\left(\vec{h}_{X_{1}}(\sigma),\dots,\vec{h}_{X_{d}}(\sigma),\vec{h}_{Y_{1}}(\sigma),\dots,\vec{h}_{Y_{d}}(\sigma)\right),

along
\Sigma
.

Denoting by
\pi:T^{*}M\rightarrow M
the canonical projection, we have, along
\Sigma
,
d\pi(\vec{h}_{X_{i}})=X_{i}\circ\pi
and
d\pi(\vec{h}_{Y_{i}})=Y_{i}\circ\pi
for
1\leq i\leq d
, and
thus
d\pi_{\sigma}(N_{\sigma}\Sigma)=\mathcal{D}_{\pi(\sigma)}
. In other words, for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the mapping

(2.9)

\Xi_{\sigma}:=(d\pi_{\sigma})_{|N_{\sigma}\Sigma}^{|\mathcal{D}_{\pi(\sigma)}}:N_{\sigma}\Sigma\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\pi(\sigma)},

is an isomorphism.

We now define
\omega_{N\Sigma}
on the bundle
N\Sigma
as follows: given any point
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we set

\omega_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}=(\omega_{\sigma})_{|N_{\sigma}\Sigma}.

Since
\omega_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(\vec{h}_{X_{i}}(\sigma),\vec{h}_{Y_{j}}(\sigma))=\omega_{\sigma}(\vec{h}_{X_{i}}(\sigma),\vec{h}_{Y_{j}}(\sigma))=\{h_{X_{i}},h_{Y_{j}}\}(\sigma)=\delta_{i,j}h_{R}(\sigma)=\lambda(\sigma)
, as well as

\omega_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(\vec{h}_{X_{i}}(\sigma),\vec{h}_{X_{j}}(\sigma))=\omega_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(\vec{h}_{Y_{i}}(\sigma),\vec{h}_{Y_{j}}(\sigma))=0
for any indices
1\leq i,j\leq d
, it follows that

\omega^{N\Sigma}=\lambda\,\Xi^{*}(-d\eta_{|\mathcal{D}}).

The symplectic normal bundle
N\Sigma
inherits a linear symplectic structure on its fibers
N_{\sigma}\Sigma
endowed with the symplectic form
\omega_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}
.

2.3.
SubLaplacians

Let
(M^{2d+1},\eta,g)
be a compact contact metric structure. We denote by
g_{\mathcal{D}}
the restriction of the metric to the contact distribution
\mathcal{D}=\ker\eta
. We introduce the (degenerate) dual metric
g_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
on
T^{*}M
associated with
g_{\mathcal{D}}
, as well as the probability measure
\nu
given by the density

\nu=\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(M)}|{\rm vol}|.

Let
L^{2}(M,\nu)
be the Hilbert space of complex-valued functions
\psi
such that
|\psi|^{2}
is
\nu
-integrable over
M
.
Then, we consider the following differential operator.

Definition 2.14
.

We define the operator
-\Delta_{\rm sR}
, called the
subLaplacian
, as the nonnegative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(M,\nu)

obtained by Friedrichs extension of the Dirichlet integral

\forall\psi\in C^{\infty}(M),\quad Q(\psi)=\int_{M}\|d\psi\|_{g_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}^{2}d\nu,

where the norm of the 1-form
d\psi
is computed with respect to the dual metric
g_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
. We denote by
{\rm Dom}(-\Delta_{sR})\subset L^{2}(M,\nu)
the domain of the subLaplacian.

Remark 2.15
.

Actually, as
M
is compact, the subLaplacian is essentially self-adjoint on
C^{\infty}(M)
, see
[
37
]
.

If one is given a local
g
-orthonormal frame
(V_{1},\dots,V_{2d})
of
\mathcal{D}
, we intoduce the notation

\nabla_{sR}\psi=\sum_{i=1}^{2d}(V_{i}\psi)V_{i},

called the
subRiemannian gradient
. Indeed, it is independent of the choice of local
g
-orthonormal frame and thus is defined globally. Then we have

Q(\psi)=\int_{M}\|\nabla_{sR}\psi\|_{g_{\mathcal{D}}}^{2}d\nu.

It follows that

\Delta_{sR}=-\sum_{i=1}^{2d}V_{i}^{*}V_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{2d}\left(V_{i}^{2}+{\rm div}_{\nu}(V_{i})V_{i}\right),

where the adjoints are taken in
L^{2}(M,\nu)
and where the divergence
{\rm div}_{\nu}(X)
of a vector field
X
is given by

\mathcal{L}_{X}\nu={\rm div}_{\nu}(X)\nu
.

Remark 2.16
.

On the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{d}
endowed with its left-invariant metric
g_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, the subLaplacian is then simply given by

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\tilde{X}_{i}^{2}+\tilde{Y}_{i}^{2}).

2.4.
Examples

We present here various examples of contact manifolds which carry natural contact metric structure and their associated subLaplacians.

2.4.1.
3D subRiemannian contact manifolds

The particular case of the 3-dimensional subRiemannian contact manifolds has been the main geometric setting studied for the analysis of subLaplacians associated with an equiregular distribution. We recall here the point of view adopted by
[
10
]
and show that it is included in the setting of contact metric structures.

Definition 2.17
.

A
3-dimensional subRiemannian contact manifold
is the data of a smooth manifold
M^{3}
together with a non-integrable and orientable distribution
\mathcal{D}\subset TM
of rank 2, as well as with
a smooth metric
g_{\mathcal{D}}
on the distribution
\mathcal{D}
.

Since
\mathcal{D}
is orientable, and as we are in dimension 3, it is co-orientable as well and hence it can be seen as the kernel of a global 1-form
\eta_{g}
. Moreover,
\eta_{g}

is chosen such that
d\eta_{g}
coincide on
\mathcal{D}
with the volume form induced by
g_{\mathcal{D}}
on
\mathcal{D}
. The non-integrability condition on
\mathcal{D}
implies that
\eta_{g}
is a contact form.

Denoting by
R
the Reeb vector field associated with this contact form, we extend the metric
g_{\mathcal{D}}
to the whole tangent bundle by asking the vector field
R
to be orthogonal to
\mathcal{D}
and of norm 1.
We denote by
g
the resulting metric.

Then
g
is an associated metric with the contact manifold
(M^{3},\eta_{g})
. Indeed, the existence of a
(1,1)
-tensor field
\phi
satisfying the relations given by (
2.7
) is immediate by an argument of dimension and since
d\eta_{g}
coincides with the volume form of
g_{\mathcal{D}}
induced on
\mathcal{D}
.
The density given by volume form
{\rm vol}_{g}
on
M
is then called in this context the
Popp measure
.

2.4.2.
Strongly pseudoconvex CR-manifolds

We present here an example of contact metric manifolds which in particular encompasses the previous one.

Definition 2.18

(
[
7
,
13
]
)
.

A CR-manifold
M^{2d+1}
is an odd-dimensional manifold with a subbundle

T^{0,1}M\subset TM\otimes{\mathbb C},

with the following properties:

(i)

{\rm dim}_{\mathbb C}T^{0,1}M=d
;

(ii)

T^{1,0}M\cap T^{0,1}M=\emptyset
, where we set
T^{1,0}M=\overline{T^{0,1}M}
;

(iii)

T^{0,1}M
is involutive, meaning for two section
Z_{1},Z_{2}
of
T^{0,1}M
, the Lie bracket
[Z_{1},Z_{2}]
is still a section of
T^{0,1}M
.

For any CR-manifold
(M^{2d+1},T^{0,1}M)
, following
[
7
, Section 6.4]
, there exists a subbundle
\mathcal{D}\subset TM
such that

\mathcal{D}\otimes{\mathbb C}=T^{1,0}M\oplus T^{0,1}M,

as well as a unique bundle map
\phi:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
such that
\phi^{2}=-{\rm Id}
and

T^{0,1}M=\{X-i\phi X\,:\,X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})\}.

If
M
is orientable, which we now suppose, the distribution
\mathcal{D}
can be described as the kernel of a global 1-form
\eta
, called in this context a
pseudo-Hermitian structure
. Associated with this structure is a bilinear form on
\mathcal{D}
defined by

L_{\eta}(X,Y):=-d\eta(X,\phi Y),\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}),

and called the
Levi form
. The pseudo-Hermitian manifold
(M^{2d+1},T^{0,1}M,\eta)
is then said to be
strongly pseudoconvex
if the Levi form is positive everywhere.

It follows that for such a manifold,
\eta
is a contact form. Extending both the Levi form and the almost complex structure
\phi
to the Reeb vector field
R
associated with
\eta
in the natural way, it follows that any strongly pseudoconvex CR-manifold carries a contact metric structure.
In this context, the subLaplacian, as defined by Definition
2.14
, is often denoted by
\Delta_{b}
.

The interest for CR-manifolds comes from the problem of restriction of holomorphic functions to hypersurfaces, on which the restriction of the Cauchy-Riemann equations gives rise to a particular differential operator. On a CR-manifold
(M^{2d+1},T^{0,1}M)
, this operator is defined by

\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M),\quad\overline{\partial}_{b}f=(df)_{|T^{0,1}M}\in\Gamma(T^{0,1}M^{*}),

called the
tangential Cauchy-Riemann operator
and whose kernel thus represents in this abstract setting the restrictions of holomorphic functions. In fact, this operator can be defined for sections of
q
-forms on the bundle
T^{0,1}M^{*}
,
q\geq 0
, and gives rise to a cochain complex.

This complex has been studied by many authors (see
[
23
]
and
[
13
]
for further references). Its study naturally leads to the introduction of a second-order differential operator, defined in particular on functions by

\square_{b}=\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}\overline{\partial}_{b},

and often referred to as the
Kohn Laplacian
.

On functions, the Kohn Laplacian and the subLaplacian are related as follows (see e.g.
[
15
]
):

\square_{b}=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\Delta_{b}-idR\right)+\mbox{lower order terms}.

This relation motivates the study of the subLaplacian on strongly pseudoconvex manifolds.

2.4.3.
Principal circle bundles

Suppose that we are given a symplectic manifold
(M^{2d},\Omega)
, with
\Omega
of integral class and consider a corresponding Boothby-Wang fibration
P^{2d+1}
with contact form
\eta
and projection
{\rm pr}:P\rightarrow M
, as defined in Section
2.1.3
.

Suppose furthermore that
(M,\Omega)
is endowed with an almost Kählerian structure, i.e there exists a
(1,1)
-tensor field
J
such that
J^{2}=-I_{d}
and a Riemannian metric
G
satisfying for all vector fields
X
and
Y
,

\Omega(X,Y)=G(X,JY).

From this almost Kählerian structure, we can construct a contact metric structure on
P^{2d+1}
as follows: for
X\in TM
, define
\phi(X)
as the horizontal lift of
J({\rm pr}_{*}(X))
. Then, since the Reeb vector field
R
is vertical we get

\phi^{2}=-I+\eta\otimes R.

Defining now a Riemannian metric on
P
by
g={\rm pr}^{*}G+\eta\otimes\eta
, one can check that we have

d\eta(X,Y)=g(X,\phi(Y)).

It is not hard to see that the Reeb vector field
R
is Killing for the metric
g
, meaning we defined a K-contact metric structure
(\phi,R,\eta,g)
on the principal circle bundle
P^{2d+1}
.

2.4.4.
Magnetic Schrödinger operators

Let
(X^{2},g)
be a compact oriented Riemannian surface. Suppose we are given a non zero 2-form
B
of integral class. One can write

B=b\,{\rm vol}_{g},

with
b
a smooth non-vanishing function on
M
and
{\rm vol}_{g}
the volume form associated with
g
. The function
b
is called the
magnetic field
, and we will assume it to be positive.

Applying Theorem
2.5
, one can consider a principal circle bundle
(M^{3},X^{2})
with connection form
\eta
satisfying

d\eta={\rm pr}^{*}B,

where
{\rm pr}:M\rightarrow X
is the projection map. Since
B
is non-degenerate, the 1-form
\eta
is a contact form. Considering local coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2})
on
X
and parametrizing the fibers locally by the variable
\theta
, there exists a 1-form
A(x_{1},x_{2})
such that we have

\eta=d\theta+A.

Denoting by
\mathcal{D}=\ker\eta
the horizontal distribution, since the map
{\rm pr}_{*}
is one-to-one from
\mathcal{D}
to
TX
, one can lift the metric
g
into a subRiemannian metric
g_{\mathcal{D}}
on
M
defined on the distribution
\mathcal{D}
. We find ourselves back in the setting of 3-dimensional subRiemannian contact manifolds or of principal circle bundles, and we can extend accordingly this subRiemannian structure into a contact metric structure.
Observe that the contact form
\eta_{g}\in{\mathbb R}\eta
rescaled so as to have

(d\eta_{g})_{|\mathcal{D}}=d{\rm vol}_{g_{\mathcal{D}}}\ \mbox{on}\ \mathcal{D},

is given by

\eta_{g}=b^{-1}\eta.

Using the coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2},\theta)
,
\eta_{g}
is locally given by
b^{-1}(d\theta+A)
. One can then find the following expression of the Reeb vector field:

R=b\partial_{\theta}-\vec{b},

where
\vec{b}
is the horizontal lift of the Hamiltonian vector field of
b
computed with respect the symplectic form
B
.

Denoting by
(g_{i,j}(x_{1},x_{2}))_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}
the matrix coefficients of the metric
g
in the coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2})
, we have the following expression for the subLaplacian in coordinates:

(2.10)

\Delta_{sR}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\sum_{i,j}(\partial_{i}-A\partial_{\theta})[\sqrt{|g|}g^{i,j}(\partial_{j}-A\partial_{\theta})],

where we set
|g|={\rm det}(g_{i,j})
and
(g^{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}
.

Observe that the subLaplacian is invariant by action of the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
on
M
, since the 1-form
\eta_{g}
and the choice of metric is invariant. This can also be seen locally in the coordinates
(x,y,\theta)
by Equation (
2.10
).
Performing a Fourier serie expansion of the functions in
L^{2}(M)
with respect to the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action, one obtain the decomposition

L^{2}(M):=\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbf{Z}}}L_{m}^{2}(X),

where each subspace
L_{m}^{2}(X)
,
m\in{\mathbf{Z}}
, is stable under the action of
-\Delta_{sR}
. Introducing the notation
C_{m}^{\infty}(X)\subset L_{m}^{2}(X)
,
m\in{\mathbf{Z}}
, for the smooth functions corresponding to
m
-th mode of the Fourier serie, we write
\Delta_{m}
for the restriction of
\Delta_{sR}
to
C_{m}^{\infty}(X)
. In the coordinates
(x,y)
one gets

\Delta_{m}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\sum_{i,j}(\partial_{i}-mA)[\sqrt{|g|}g^{i,j}(\partial_{j}-mA)],

which corresponds to the
magnetic Schrödginer operator
on
(X,g)
with magnetic field given by the 2-form
mB
.

3.
Tangent group bundle

In this section, we suppose given
(M^{2d+1},\eta,g)
, a compact contact metric structure. Most of the notions and notations are taken from
[
19
]
.

3.1.
Osculating Lie algebras

Recall that we denote by
\mathcal{D}
the contact distribution associated with the 1-form
\eta
.
For each point
x\in M
, we define the graded vector space

{\rm gr}(T_{x}M)=\mathcal{D}_{x}\oplus T_{x}M/\mathcal{D}_{x}.

Each of these fibers is naturally endowed with a Lie bracket, which we denote by
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}_{x}M}
. Indeed, for
two vector fields
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
, we can write

[X_{x},Y_{x}]_{\mathfrak{g}_{x}M}:=[X,Y](x)\mod\mathcal{D}_{x}.

We denote the resulting Lie algebra by

\mathfrak{g}_{x}M=({\rm gr}(T_{x}M),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}_{x}M}).

The collection of these fibers, denoted by
\mathfrak{g}M
, is then a smooth bundle of step 2 stratified nilpotent Lie algebras, called the
osculating Lie algebras bundle
of
(M,\eta)
.

In order to obtain local trivializations of this vector bundle, we can consider adapted frames.

Definition 3.1
.

A local adated frame
(\mathbb{X},U)
is a frame
\mathbb{X}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{2d+1})
on the open subset
U\subset M
such that
(V_{1},\dots,V_{2d})
is a frame for the contact distribution
\mathcal{D}_{|U}
. We then denote by
\langle\mathbb{X}\rangle:=(\langle V_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle V_{2d+1}\rangle)
its natural projection on
\mathfrak{g}_{|U}M
.

For
x\in M
, we introduce the notation
\mathfrak{v}_{x}M
for the projection of
\mathcal{D}_{x}
in

\mathfrak{g}_{x}M
as well as
\mathfrak{r}_{x}M
for the projection of
T_{x}M/\mathcal{D}_{x}
, so as to be able to write

\mathfrak{g}M=\mathfrak{v}M\oplus\mathfrak{r}M.

As graded spaces, the fibers of
\mathfrak{g}M
come with natural dilations.

Definition 3.2
.

For
x\in M
, we define on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{x}M
the family
(\delta_{t})_{t>0}
of Lie algebra automorphisms, called
dilations
, by

\forall t>0,\quad\delta_{t}(V_{x}+R_{x})=tV_{x}+t^{2}R_{x},\quad\mbox{where}\ V_{x}\in\mathfrak{v}_{x}M,\,R_{x}\in\mathfrak{r}_{x}M.

The Lie algebra bundle gives rise to a Lie group bundle as follows. Each giber
\mathfrak{g}_{x}M
,
x\in M
is a nilpotent Lie algebra and, as such, is associated with a unique (up to isomorphism) nilpotent Lie group, denoted by
G_{x}M
, which is connected and simply connected. By nilpotency, the exponential mapping

{\rm Exp}_{G_{x}M}:\mathfrak{g}_{x}M\rightarrow G_{x}M,

is a diffeomorphism between the Lie algebra and the associated Lie group.
We can thus realize the Lie group directly as
\mathfrak{g}_{x}M
, endowed with the group law given by the Baker-Campbell Hausdorff formula.

In this way, the bundle
GM=\bigcup_{x\in M}G_{x}M
coincides as a manifold with
\mathfrak{g}M
and is a smooth bundle of step 2 nilpotent Lie groups. It is called the
tangent group bundle
of
(M,\eta)
.

3.1.1.
Local
\phi
-frames

We investigate how local
\phi
-frames introduced in Section
2.2.1
, which are adapted frames, give nice trivializations of the tangent group bundle
GM
.
Indeed, recall that a local
\phi
-frame
(\mathbb{X},U)
is a
g
-orthonormal frame on
U
,

\mathbb{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d},R),

where the vector fields
(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}
and
(Y_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}

are tangent to the contact distribution
\mathcal{D}
, and satisfy moreover

\forall i,j\in\{1,\dots,d\},\quad d\eta(X_{i},Y_{j})=-\delta_{i,j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad d\eta(X_{i},X_{j})=d\eta(Y_{i},Y_{j})=0.

Since for two vector fields
X
and
Y
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
, we can write

d\eta(X,Y)=-\eta([X,Y]),

the previous equation gives

\forall i,j\in\{1,\dots,d\},\quad[X_{i},Y_{j}]=\delta_{i,j}R\mod\mathcal{D}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[X_{i},X_{j}],\,[Y_{i},Y_{j}]\in\mathcal{D}.

Then, the projection
\langle\mathbb{X}\rangle
of this adapted frame on
\mathfrak{g}_{x}M
, which we recall is given by

\forall i\in\{1,\dots,d\},\quad\langle X_{i}\rangle_{x}=X_{i,x}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{x}=Y_{i,x},

seen as elements of
\mathfrak{v}_{x}M
, and by

\langle R\rangle_{x}=R_{x}\mod\mathcal{D}_{x},

defines a frame for the osculating Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}M
over
U
, which satisfies moreover the following commutator relations: for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,

\forall x\in U,\quad[\langle X_{i}\rangle_{x},\langle Y_{j}\rangle_{x}]_{\mathfrak{g}_{x}M}=\delta_{i,j}\langle R\rangle_{x}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[\langle X_{i}\rangle_{x},\langle X_{j}\rangle_{x}]_{\mathfrak{g}_{x}M}=[\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{x},\langle Y_{j}\rangle_{x}]_{\mathfrak{g}_{x}M}=0.

Adapted frames for which the projection on
\mathfrak{g}M
satisfies exactly the Heisenberg Lie algebra relations (
2.2
) are called
Heisenberg frames
. Thus, local
\phi
-frames, similarly to local frames coming from Darboux coordinates, are Heisenberg frames.

We deduce in particular that the osculating Lie algebras bundle is a locally trivial Lie algebra bundle modeled on the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
, i.e the map

\varphi_{U}^{\mathbb{X}}:\,U\times\mathfrak{h}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}M_{|U},

defined by

\varphi_{U}^{\mathbb{X}}(x,v)=\left(x,\sum_{i=1}^{d}(v_{i}\langle X_{i}\rangle_{x}+v_{d+i}\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{x})+v_{2d+1}\langle R\rangle_{x}\right),

is a diffeomorphism and a Lie algebra isomorphism between each fibers.

Now, using the group exponential on each fiber
G_{x}M
,
x\in M
, we can consider the parameterization of
G_{x}M
by the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
given by

\forall v\in\mathfrak{h}^{d},\quad{\rm Exp}_{x}{X}(v):={\rm Exp}_{G_{x}M}(\varphi_{U}{X}(x,v)),

and we define the smooth diffeomorphism
{\rm Exp}{X}:U\times\mathfrak{h}^{d}\rightarrow G_{|U}M
by

\forall(x,v)\in U\times\mathfrak{h}^{d},\quad{\rm Exp}{X}(x,v):={\rm Exp}_{x}{X}(v).

If we are given two such trivializations
\varphi_{U_{1}}^{\mathbb{X_{1}}}
and
\varphi_{U_{2}}^{\mathbb{X_{2}}}
associated with two local
\phi
-frames
(U_{1},\mathbb{X_{1}})
and
(U_{2},\mathbb{X_{2}})
,
then, as discussed in Section
2.2.1
, the transition between the two frames on
U_{1}\cap U_{2}
is given by a family of matrices
(A(x))_{x\in U_{1}\cap U_{2}}
in

U(d)\times 1
:

(3.1)

\forall x\in U_{1}\cap U_{2},\quad\mathbb{X}_{1}(x)=A(x)\mathbb{X}_{2}(x).

Because of their block-like structure, these transition matrices directly give the transition matrices between the adapted frames
\langle\mathbb{X}_{1}\rangle
and
\langle\mathbb{X}_{1}\rangle
in
\mathfrak{g}M_{|U_{1}\cap U_{2}}
.
We obtain

\forall(x,v)\in(U_{1}\cap U_{2},\mathfrak{h}^{d}),\quad\varphi_{U_{1}}^{\mathbb{X}_{1},-1}\circ\varphi_{U_{2}}^{\mathbb{X_{2}}}(x,v)=(x,A(x)v).

3.1.2.
Smooth Haar system

Observe that, since the transition matrices between two
\phi
-frames as defined by Equation (
3.1
), are in
U(d)\times 1
, the Riemannian structure on the tangent space
TM
descends on the osculating Lie algebra bundle
\mathfrak{g}M
.

This remark allows us to say that the tangent group bundle
GM
of a contact metric structure carries a natural
smooth Haar system

\mu_{GM}=\{\mu_{x}\}_{x\in M}
. Recall that a smooth Haar system (defined by
[
19
, Definition 5.2]
) is a family of Haar measures
\mu_{x}
on each fiber
G_{x}M
such that the map
x\in M\mapsto\int_{M}f(x,h)d\mu_{x}(h)
is smooth for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(GM)
.

Indeed, on the contact manifold
(M,\eta)
, choosing a local
\phi
-frame
(\mathbb{X},U)
, the pushforward by
\varphi_{U}{X}
of the constant measure system
\{{\rm Leb}_{\mathfrak{h}^{d}}^{\mathbb{X}}\}_{x\in U}
, where
{\rm Leb}_{\mathfrak{h}^{d}}^{\mathbb{X}}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
in the basis
\langle\mathbb{X}\rangle_{x}
, defines indeed a smooth measure system on
\mathfrak{g}_{|U}M
. Moreover, the previous discussion ensures that the resulting measure system is independent of the choice of local
\phi
-frames. Finally, using the group exponential
{\rm Exp}_{G_{x}M}
,
x\in M
, pushing forward this measure system defines uniquely a smooth Haar system, denoted simply by
\{\mu_{x}\}_{x\in M}
.

3.1.3.
Stratified spaces and higher order terms

We recall here the notions of stratified vector space and their dilations.

Definition 3.3
.

A finite-dimensional vector space
\mathcal{V}
is said to be
stratified
with respect to the sequence
(\mathcal{V}_{i})_{1\leq j\leq p}
of subspaces, if it admits the decomposition

\mathcal{V}=\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq p}\mathcal{V}_{j},

and
\mathcal{V}_{j}\neq\{0\}
for
1\leq j\leq p
.

For a stratified vector space
\mathcal{V}
, similarly as the dilations introduced by Definition
3.2
, we consider the family of automorphisms
(\delta_{t})_{t>0}
of
\mathcal{V}
defined by

\delta_{r}(v)=r^{j}v,\quad v\in\mathcal{V}_{j}.

The
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathcal{V}
for this stratification is denoted by
Q
and given by

Q:=\sum_{j=1}^{p}j\,{\rm dim}(\mathcal{V}_{j}).

The dilations lead to the notion of homogeneous function: for
m\in{\mathbb R}
, a function
f:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow{\mathbb C}
is
m-homogeneous
if
f(\delta_{r}v)=r^{m}f(v)
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
and
r>0
. This extends to distributions on
\mathcal{V}
. A linear operator
T:\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{V})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{V})
is also said to be
m
-homogeneous with respect to
(\delta_{t})_{t>0}
if for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{V})
, we have
T(f\circ\delta_{r})=r^{m}(Tf)\circ\delta_{r}
.

Definition 3.4
.

A
quasinorm
on
\mathcal{V}
is a continuous map
|\cdot|:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow{\mathbb R}
which is symmetric, definite and 1-homogeneous with respect to
(\delta_{t})_{t>0}
, i.e satisfying
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
and
r>0
,

|-v|=|v|,\quad|v|=0\iff v=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\delta_{r}v|=r|v|.

As our primary example, we can take the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
and its stratification given by Equation (
2.3
):

\mathfrak{h}^{d}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{r}.

Then one can consider the
Korányi quasinorm
given in the coordinates
(x,y,z)
by

|(x,y,z)|=\left((x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+z^{2}\right)^{1/4}.

Remark that this quasinorm makes sense on the fibers of
\mathfrak{g}M
independently of the choice of local trivialization given by a local
\phi
-frame.

In the case of the Heisenberg Lie algebra, keeping the same notation for it, we extend the quasinorm to the Heisenberg group using the exponential map. Then, we have the following triangle inequality:

\exists C>0,\forall h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{H},\quad|h_{1}*h_{2}|\leq C(|h_{1}|+|h_{2}|).

We now define the notion of
higher order map
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{1}=\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq p_{1}}\mathcal{V}_{1,j}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}=\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq p_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{2,j}
be two stratified vector spaces as in Definition
3.3
, and denote by
(\delta_{1,r})_{r>0}
and
(\delta_{2,r})_{r>0}
their respective dilations. We suppose moreover that we fixed adapted bases
(V_{1},\dots,V_{{\rm dim}(\mathcal{V}_{1})})
and
(W_{1},\dots,W_{{\rm dim}(\mathcal{V}_{2})})
of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
respectively, i.e that are concatenations of bases of the subspaces
\mathcal{V}_{i,j}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
,
1\leq j\leq p_{i}
.

Definition 3.5
.

A polynomial map
r:\mathcal{V}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{2}
is higher order (with respect to the stratification) when the degrees of homogeneity (for the dilations on
\mathcal{V}_{1}
) of the monomials occuring in its
j
-th component
r_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq p_{2}
, are
>j
. The notion of higher order does not depend on the choice of adapted bases.

We extend the notion of higher order to smooth maps.

Definition 3.6
.

A smooth function
r
defined on a neighborhood of
0
in
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and valued in
\mathcal{V}_{2}
is also said to be higher order when its Taylor serie at
0
is a serie of polynomial maps of higher order.

We recall some charaterizations of higher order maps given by
[
19
, Proposition 4.5]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
r:\mathcal{V}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{2}
a smooth map. The following properties are equivalent:

(i)

The map
r
is higher order.

(ii)

For one (and then any) neighborhood
\mathcal{V}_{0}
of
0
, we have for any
v\in V_{0}
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\delta_{2,\varepsilon^{-1}}r(\delta_{1,\varepsilon}v)=0.

(iii)

There exists a unique smooth map
R:{\mathbb R}_{+}\times\mathcal{V}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{2}
satisfying
\delta_{2,\varepsilon^{-1}}(r(\delta_{1,\varepsilon}v))=\varepsilon R(\varepsilon,v)
, for any
v\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\varepsilon>0
.

3.1.4.
Geometric exponentiation

We present here the notion of geometric exponentiation for a local Heisenberg frame on
M
, as well as some of its properties.

First, if
X
denotes a vector field on
M
, we write
{\rm exp}_{x}(X)
for the time-one flow, when defined, of
X
starting from the point
x\in M
. More precisely, it corresponds to solution at time 1 of ordinary differential equation given by

\dot{\gamma}(t)=X(\gamma(t))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma(0)=x,

i.e
{\rm exp}_{x}(X):=\gamma(1)
.

Given a local Heisenberg frame
(\mathbb{X},U)
on
M
, with
\mathbb{X}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{2d+1})
, we set
for
x\in U
and
v\in{\mathbb R}^{2d+1}
,

{\rm exp}_{x}{X}(v)={\rm exp}_{x}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2d+1}v_{i}V_{i}\right),

when this makes sense. In what follows, we shall identify
{\mathbb R}^{2d+1}
with the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
through the basis
(X_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d},R)
introduced in Equation (
2.4
).

In fact, there exists an open neighborhood
U{X}
in
U\times\mathfrak{h}^{d}
of the
(x,0)
on which the following map, called the
geometric exponential map
,

\displaystyle{\rm exp}{X}:U{X}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow U\times U

\displaystyle(x,v)

\displaystyle\longmapsto{\rm exp}_{x}{X}(v),

is a smooth diffeomorphism onto its image in
U\times U
. On suitable subsets of
U\times U
and
U{X}
, we introduce

\ln{X}:=({\rm exp}{X})^{-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ln_{x}{X}:=({\rm exp}_{x}{X})^{-1}.

Without restriction, we will assume that the open set
U{X}
is such that for
(x,v)\in U{X}
and
t\in(0,1)
,
(x,tv)
is also in
U{X}
.

The following theorem, which is a special case of
[
19
, Theorem 4.13]
, is the most important statement of this section, as it expresses the link between the geometric maps associated with local Heisenberg frames and the tangent group bundle.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(\mathbb{X},U)
be a local Heisenberg frame and
x\in U
. Then, on a neighborhood of
\{x\}\times\{0\}\times\{0\}
in
U\times\mathfrak{h}^{d}\times\mathfrak{h}^{d}
, we have

\ln_{x}{X}\left({\rm exp}_{{\rm exp}_{x}{X}(w)}{X}(v)\right)=w*_{\mathfrak{h}^{d}}v+r(x;w,v),

where
*_{\mathfrak{h}}
denotes the Heisenberg group law lifted on
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
, and
r
is a smooth map of higher order in
w
and
v
for the canonical stratification of
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
, valued in
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
.

3.2.
Harmonic analysis of nilpotent Lie groups

We give here a reminder of the classical results of harmonic analysis on nilpotent Lie groups. Let
G
be such a simply connected group. The notations we use in this section are inspired by
[
21
]
.

3.2.1.
Unitary dual set

First, recall that a
unitary representation

(\pi,\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
of the group
G
is a pair consisting of a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
and a strongly continuous
morphism
\pi
from
G
to the set of unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. A representation is said to be
irreducible
if the only closed subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
stable under the action of

\pi
are
\{0\}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
itself. Two representations
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are equivalent if there exists a unitary transformation
\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{1}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{2}}
,
called an
intertwining map
, such that

\pi_{1}=\mathcal{I}^{-1}\circ\pi_{2}\circ\mathcal{I}.

We denote by
[\pi_{1}]=[\pi_{2}]
the associated equivalence class. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 3.9
.

The set of all irreducible unitary representations, up to equivalence, is called the
(unitary) dual set
and is denoted by
\widehat{G}
:

\widehat{G}:=\{[\pi]\,:\,\pi:G\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\ \textrm{irreducible s.c unitary representation of}\ G\}.

We then have the following structure on the dual set of a nilpotent group.

Proposition 3.10
.

The unitary dual set
\widehat{G}
is naturally endowed with a topology, called the
hull-kernel topology
or the
Fell topology
, as well as with a unique (up to a multiplicative constant) measure
\mu_{\widehat{G}}
on the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebra, called the
Plancherel measure
. Moreover, this defines a standard Borel structure on
\widehat{G}
.

In practical situations, the representations of a nilpotent Lie group can be explicitly computed using
Kirillov’s theory of coadjoint orbits
. Indeed, there exists an explicit homeomorphism between the dual set
\widehat{G}
and the space of coadjoint orbits
\mathfrak{g}^{*}/G
. In this parameterization, one can then get an expression of the Plancherel measure.

In the case of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, the following description of its dual is obtained. The coadjoint orbits are of two kinds. The first ones are given by the planes
\mathfrak{v}^{*}\times\{\lambda\}
for
\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, while the remaining ones are given by the singleton
\{\gamma\}
for
\gamma\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}
.

From this picture, we can get explicit expressions of the representations. First, fix
\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
.
We introduce the irreducible unitary representation
\pi^{\lambda}
of
\mathbb{H}
defined on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi^{\lambda}}=L^{2}(\mathfrak{p})
by

(3.2)

\pi^{\lambda}(h)f(\xi)={\rm exp}\left(i\lambda r+\frac{i}{2}|\lambda|x\cdot y+i\sqrt{|\lambda|}\xi\cdot y\right)f\left(\xi+\sqrt{|\lambda|}x\right),

where identified
\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
with
{\mathbb R}\setminus\{0\}
through the vector
R
.

The representations
\pi^{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, called the
Schrödinger representations
, are infinite dimensional and are not equivalent to each other.
Moreover, according to the Stone-von Neumann theorem, each representation
\pi^{\lambda}
is characterized by the action of the center
{\rm Exp}{H}({\mathbb R}R)
on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi^{\lambda}}
. More precisely, there exists one (and only one) irreducible representation

(\pi,\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
(up to equivalence) such that the elements of the form
{\rm Exp}{H}(rR)
,
r\in{\mathbb R}
are sent by
\pi
on

e^{i\lambda r}I_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}.

We also introduce another realization of these representations, called the
Fock-Bargmann representations
. To this end, we define a complex structure on
\mathfrak{v}
by considering the identification

\mathfrak{v}\sim\mathfrak{p}\oplus i\mathfrak{q}.

We introduce the variable
z=x+iy
and we denote by
h=(z,r)
the elements of the Heisenberg group.
We then define the Fock space
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{v})
to be the space of functions
F:\mathfrak{v}\rightarrow{\mathbb C}
that are holomorphic if
\lambda>0
and antiholomorphic otherwise, satisfying

\int_{\mathfrak{v}}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-|\lambda||z|^{2}}dz<\infty.

The Heisenberg group then acts on the Fock spaces as follows:

(3.3)

\pi^{\lambda}_{\mathscr{F}}(h)F(w)={\rm exp}\left(i\lambda r-\frac{\lambda}{4}|z|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}w\overline{z}\right)F(w+z),\quad h=(z,r)\in\mathbb{H}^{d}.

By the Stone-von Neumann theorem, we easily deduce that

[\pi^{\lambda}]=[\pi^{\lambda}_{\mathscr{F}}],\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb R}^{*}.

For an explicit formula of the unitary operator that intertwines these two representations, we refer to
[
22
]
[Chapter 1]. The merit of the Fock-Bargmann representations in our later analysis is that the variables of the first stratum are treated on an equal footing.

There also exist other unitary irreducible representations of
\mathbb{H}
, which are given by the characters of the first stratum
\mathfrak{v}
in the following way: for
\gamma\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}
, we set

\pi^{0,\gamma}(h)=e^{i\gamma(V)},\quad\mbox{where}\ h={\rm Exp}{H}(V+rR),\ \mbox{with}\ V\in\mathfrak{v},r\in{\mathbb R}.

With these two classes of representations one gets the full dual of
\mathbb{H}
:

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}=\{[\pi^{\lambda}]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\}\sqcup\{[\pi^{0,\gamma}]\,:\,\gamma\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}\}.

We will denote by
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\infty}
the subset of
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
made of the Schrödinger representations:

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\infty}=\{[\pi^{\lambda}]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\},

also called the
generic dual set
.
We can deduce from the coadjoint picture that
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\infty}
is a dense open subset of
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
which is homeomorphic to
{\mathbb R}^{*}
.
Moreover, the Plancherel measure
\mu_{\widehat{\mathbb{H}}}
is supported on
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\infty}
and with the choice of Haar measure made we have

d\mu_{\widehat{\mathbb{H}}}(\pi^{\lambda})=(2\pi)^{-(3d+1)}|\lambda|^{d}d\lambda,\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb R}^{*}.

3.2.2.
Infinitesimal representations

For a nilpotent Lie group
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a representation
\pi
of
G
, we keep the same notation for the corresponding infinitesimal representation that acts on the enveloping algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
of the Lie algebra. It is characterized by its action on
\mathfrak{g}
:

\pi(X)=\frac{d}{dt}\pi({\rm Exp}_{G}(tX))_{|t=0},\ X\in\mathfrak{g}.

Through the infinitesimal representation, the enveloping algebra is seen acting on the space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}
of smooth vectors, defined as the space of vectors
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
such that the map
g\in G\mapsto\pi(g)f\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is smooth.

For instance, in the case of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
, for a Schrödinger representation
\pi^{\lambda}
given by (
3.2
), with
\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, one has the following expressions for the action of the basis
(X_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d},R)
: for
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,

\pi^{\lambda}(X_{i})=\sqrt{|\lambda|}\partial_{\xi_{i}},\quad\pi^{\lambda}(Y_{i})=i\sqrt{|\lambda|}\xi_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\pi^{\lambda}(R)=i\lambda,

while
\mathcal{H}_{\pi^{\lambda}}^{\infty}
identifies with
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb R}_{\xi})
.
In particular, if we introduce the left-invariant differential operator

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(X_{i}^{2}+Y_{i}^{2}),

called the
Heisenberg subLaplacian
, then seen as an element of
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{h}^{d})
, we get

\pi^{\lambda}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}})=|\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{d}(-\partial_{\xi_{i}}^{2}+\xi_{i}^{2}).

3.2.3.
Fourier transform

For a general nilpotent Lie group
G
, with Haar measure simply denoted by
dx
, we first define the
group Fourier transform
for a function
\kappa
in
L^{1}(G)
:
for an irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
, we set

\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa)(\pi)=\hat{\kappa}(\pi)=\pi(\kappa)=\int_{G}\kappa(x)\pi(x)^{*}dx\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}).

Note that if
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are equivalent, i.e there exists an intertwining operator
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{I}\circ\pi_{1}=\pi_{2}\circ\mathcal{I}
,
then we have
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa)(\pi_{1})=\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa)(\pi_{2})\circ\mathcal{I}
. Therefore, we may consider the group Fourier transform of
\kappa
at the
equivalence class of the representation
\pi
, and
\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa)
can be seen as a measurable field of equivalence classes of bounded operators:

\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa)=\{\hat{\kappa}(\pi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\,:\,\pi\in\widehat{G}\}.

Note that for two functions
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
in
L^{1}(G)
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa_{1})\circ\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa_{2})=\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa_{2}\star_{G}\kappa_{1}),\quad\mbox{where}\ \kappa_{2}\star_{G}\kappa_{1}(x)=\int_{G}\kappa_{2}(y)\kappa_{1}(y^{-1}x)\,dy.

The Plancherel measure
\mu_{\widehat{G}}
introduced in Proposition
3.10
is characterized by the
Fourier inversion formula
:

(3.4)

\forall\kappa\in\mathcal{S}(G),\,\forall x\in G,\quad\kappa(x)=\int_{\widehat{G}}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\pi(x)\hat{\kappa}(\pi)\right)d\mu_{\widehat{G}}(\pi).

Equivalently, it is also characterized by the following property:

(3.5)

\forall\kappa\in L^{1}(G)\cap L^{2}(G),\quad\int_{G}|\kappa(x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\widehat{G}}\|\hat{\kappa}(\pi)\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}d\mu_{\widehat{G}}(\pi),

where
\|\cdot\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. We denote by
L^{2}(\widehat{G})
the Hilbert space of measurable fields of Hilbert-Schmidt
operators

\sigma=\{\sigma(\pi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\,:\,\|\sigma(\pi)\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}<\infty,\ \pi\in\widehat{G}\},

(up to
\mu_{\widehat{G}}
-a.e equivalence) such that the following quantity is finite

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})}^{2}:=\int_{\widehat{G}}\|\sigma(\pi)\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}d\mu_{\widehat{G}}(\pi).

Equation (
3.5
) allows for a unitary extension of the group Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}_{G}:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(\widehat{G}).

However, it is still not a general enough space of definition of the group Fourier transform for our needs. To this end, we introduce the following space.

Definition 3.11
.

The Banach space
L^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
is defined as the space of measurable field of bounded operators
\sigma=\{\sigma(\pi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\,:\,\pi\in\widehat{G}\}

(up to
\mu_{\widehat{G}}
-a.e equivalence) on
\widehat{G}
such that the following quantity is finite:

\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{G})}:=\sup_{\pi\in\widehat{G}}\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}.

The supremum is understood as the essential supremum with respect to the Plancherel measure
\mu_{\widehat{G}}
. We check readily that
\mathcal{F}_{G}(L^{1}(G))\subset L^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
, but this
is not an equality. Indeed, it follows from Dixmier’s full Plancherel theorem (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Theorem B.2.32]
) that
L^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
is a von Neumann algebra isomorphic to the subspace

\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(G))^{G},

of bounded operators on
L^{2}(G)
commuting with left-translations. More precisely, for any
T\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(G))^{G}
, there exists a unique element
\hat{T}\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
such
that

\forall f\in L^{2}(G),\quad\mathcal{F}_{G}(Tf)=\hat{T}\hat{f},

and moreover
\|T\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(G))}=\|\hat{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{G})}
.

From the Schwartz kernel theorem, it follows that for any
T\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(G))^{G}
, there exists a unique distribution
\kappa\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
such that
Tf=f\star_{G}\kappa
for
f\in\mathcal{S}(G)
.
The distribution
\kappa
is called the (right) convolution kernel of
T
. By setting

\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa):=\hat{T},

we easily check that it extends the group Fourier transform to the space

\mathcal{K}(G):=\{\kappa\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)\,:\,(f\mapsto f\star_{G}\kappa)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(G))\},

and that
\mathcal{F}_{G}:\mathcal{K}(G)\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
is bijective.

3.2.4.
Further extension of the Fourier transform

We finish our presentation of the group Fourier transform in the particular case of the Heisenberg group.

First, for
s\in{\mathbb R}
, we introduce the notation
L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
, the adapted Sobolev space on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
as defined in
[
21
, Section 4.4]
.

We consider now the following spaces of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
: for
a,b\in{\mathbb R}
, we set

\mathcal{K}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{d}):=\{\kappa\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{H}^{d})\,:\,(f\mapsto f\star\kappa)\in\mathcal{L}(L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d}),L_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d}))\}.

The Schwartz kernel theorem readily gives that the space
\mathcal{K}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
identifies with the Banach subspace of
\mathcal{L}(L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d}),L_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d}))
consisting of the left-invariant operators, i.e commuting with left-translations.

At the same time, we also define the space
L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
as the space of measurable fields
\sigma=\{\sigma(\pi):\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\,:\,\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{H}}\}
such that the measurable field

\left(1-\pi(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}})\right)^{b/2}\sigma\left(1-\pi(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}})\right)^{-a/2},

is an element of
L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
. The space
L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
is a Banach space when endowed with the following norm

\|\sigma\|_{L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})}=\|\left(1-\pi(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}})\right)^{b/2}\sigma\left(1-\pi(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}})\right)^{-a/2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})}.

Proposition 3.12
.

For
a,b\in{\mathbb R}
, the Banach spaces
\mathcal{K}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
and
L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
are isomorphic. More precisely, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
extends uniquely in an isomorphism
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}:\mathcal{K}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{d})\rightarrow L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
of Banach spaces, satisfying furthermore for any
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
,

\|\kappa\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a,b}(\mathbb{H}^{d})}=\|\mathcal{F}{H}(\kappa)\|_{L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})}.

3.2.5.
Invariant classes of symbols

Definition 3.13
.

An
invariant symbol

\sigma
on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
is a measurable field of operators
\sigma=\{\sigma(\pi):\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{+\infty}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{-\infty}\,:\,\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}\}
over
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
, where we recall that
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{+\infty}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{-\infty}
denote the spaces of smooth and distributional vectors on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
respectively.

To define our classes, we introduce the notion of
difference operators
.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
and
\sigma
be an invariant symbol on
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
. We say that
\sigma
is
\Delta_{q}
-differentiable when
\sigma
is in
L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
and
q\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}^{-1}(\sigma)
is in
L_{c,d}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
for some
a,b,c,d\in{\mathbb R}
. We denote then

\Delta_{q}\sigma=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\left(q\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}^{-1}(\sigma)\right).

Using the coordinates
(x,y,z)
on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
introduced in Section
2.1.2
, we consider the difference operators associated with the monomials
x,y
and
z
, denoted respectively by
\Delta_{x}
,
\Delta_{y}
and
\Delta_{z}
. For an index
\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{2d+1}
, we define
\Delta^{\alpha}
to be the difference operators associated with the smooth function
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}y_{1}^{\alpha_{d+1}}\cdots y_{d}^{\alpha_{2d}}z^{\alpha_{2d+1}}
.

We can now define our classes of symbols.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb R}
. A invariant symbol
\sigma
is said to be
of order
m
, or equivalently in the class
S^{m}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
, if for any index
\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{2d+1}
,

\Delta^{\alpha}\sigma\in L_{0,-m+[\alpha]}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}),

where
[\alpha]=\sum_{i=1}^{2d}\alpha_{i}+2\alpha_{2d+1}
.

The space
S^{m}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
is a Fréchet space when endowed with the seminorms given by

\|\sigma\|_{S^{m}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}),N}:=\max_{[\alpha]\leq N}\|\Delta^{\alpha}\sigma\|_{L_{0,-m+[\alpha]}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})}=\max_{[\alpha]\leq N}\left\|\left(1-\pi(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}})\right)^{\frac{m-[\alpha]}{2}}\Delta^{\alpha}\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})}.

Definition 3.16
.

The class of
smoothing symbols
is defined as the following intersection

S^{-\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})=\bigcap_{m\in{\mathbb R}}S^{m}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}).

It is endowed with the induced topology of projective limit.

Then, the results recalled above, or equivalently
[
21
, Lemma 5.4.13 and Theorem 5.4.9]
, imply the following:

Proposition 3.17
.

The group Fourier transform is an isomorphism between the topological vector spaces
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{H})
and
S^{-\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}})
.

3.3.
Dual tangent bundle

Following
[
19
]
, we introduce the dual group tangent bundle and investigate its properties when associated with a compact manifold
(M^{2d+1},\eta,g)
with a contact metric structure. Recall that this bundle is defined by

(3.6)

\widehat{G}M=\bigcup_{x\in M}(x,\widehat{G_{x}M}).

In the rest of the article, we will rather use the notation
\widehat{G}_{x}M
for the fibers of
\widehat{G}M
.

3.3.1.
Topology and local parameterization

We can give this set a natural topology as follows. We introduce on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}M
, the dual bundle to the vector bundle
\mathfrak{g}M
, the equivalence relation
\sim_{Kir}
defined by

\forall(x_{1},\ell_{1}),(x_{2},\ell_{2})\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}M,\quad(x_{1},\ell_{1})\sim_{Kir}(x_{2},\ell_{2})\iff x_{1}=x_{2}\ \mbox{and}\ \ell_{1}\in{\rm Ad}^{*}(G_{x_{2}}M)(\ell_{2}),

where
{\rm Ad}^{*}(G_{x_{2}}M)(\ell)
for
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{*}M
, denotes the coadjoint orbits of
\ell
in
\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{*}M
.
We redefine then the dual tangent bundle as the topological quotient space associated with this relation:

\widehat{G}M:=\mathfrak{g}^{*}M/\sim_{Kir}.

This is coherent with Equation (
3.6
): denoting by
p:\mathfrak{g}^{*}M\rightarrow M
the natural projection, this application descends on
\widehat{G}M
as a continuous onto map

\hat{p}:\widehat{G}M\rightarrow M.

Moreover, for
x\in M
, we observe, thanks to the coadjoint picture of the unitary dual sets, that the fiber
\hat{p}^{-1}(x)
is homeomorphic to
\widehat{G}_{x}M
, as desired.

Thanks to the existence of the local
\phi
-frames, it is possible to give parameterizations of
\widehat{G}M
.
Indeed, let
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d},R)
be such a local
\phi
-frame on a neighborhood
U
, and
\varphi_{U}{X}
be the local trivialization of

\mathfrak{g}M
associated with the adapted frame
\langle\mathbb{X}\rangle
.
Since for any
x\in U
, the frame
\langle\mathbb{X}\rangle_{x}
satisfies exactly the commutator relations of the Heisenberg Lie algebra, the representations of the associated
nilpotent Lie group
G_{x}M
are given by the one of
\mathbb{H}
. Explicitly, for any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}_{x}^{*}M\setminus o
(where
\mathfrak{r}^{*}M
stands for the dual bundle associated with
\mathfrak{r}M
and
o
denotes the null section),
introducing the vector space

\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{X}}_{x}={\rm span}(\langle X_{1}\rangle_{x},\dots,\langle X_{d}\rangle_{x})\subset\mathfrak{g}_{x}M,

we define the irreducible unitary representation
\pi_{x}^{\lambda}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{x}^{\lambda}}=L^{2}(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{X}}_{x})
by

(3.7)

\forall f\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{x}^{\lambda}},\quad\pi_{x}^{\lambda}(h)f(\xi)={\rm exp}\left(i\lambda z+\frac{i}{2}|\lambda|x\cdot y+i\sqrt{|\lambda|}\xi\cdot y\right)f\left(\xi+\sqrt{|\lambda|}x\right),

where, for
h\in G_{x}M
, we wrote

h={\rm Exp}_{G_{x}M}(x\cdot\langle X\rangle_{x}+y\cdot\langle Y\rangle_{x}+z\langle R\rangle_{x}),\quad\mbox{with}\ x,y\in{\mathbb R}^{d},\,z\in{\mathbb R}.

Similarly, for
\gamma\in\mathfrak{v}_{x}^{*}M
, we set

\pi_{x}^{0,\gamma}(h)=e^{i\gamma(\langle V\rangle_{x})},

where

h={\rm Exp}_{G_{x}M}(\langle V\rangle_{x}+z\langle R\rangle_{x}),\ \mbox{with}\ \langle V\rangle_{x}\in\mathfrak{v}_{x}M,z\in{\mathbb R}.

We are thus led to introduce the map

\hat{\varphi}_{U}{X}:U\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}\rightarrow\widehat{G}M_{|U},

defined as follows: for
x\in U
and
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
, if
[\pi]=[\pi^{\lambda}]
for some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, identifying
\mathfrak{r}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{r}_{x}^{*}M
through the dual basis of the vector
\langle R\rangle_{x}
, we set

\hat{\varphi}_{U}{X}(x,\pi^{\lambda})=(x,\pi_{x}^{\lambda}),

while if
[\pi]=[\pi^{0,\gamma}]
for some
\gamma\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}
, identifying
\mathfrak{v}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{v}_{x}^{*}M
through the dual basis of
(\langle X_{1}\rangle_{x},\dots,\langle X_{d}\rangle_{x},\langle Y_{1}\rangle_{x},\dots,\langle Y_{d}\rangle_{x})
, we set

\hat{\varphi}_{U}{X}(x,\pi^{0,\gamma})=(x,\pi_{x}^{0,\gamma}).

Using the coadjoint orbit pictures of the unitary dual sets, we check readily that the map
\hat{\varphi}_{U}{X}
is an homeomorphism, making of
\widehat{G}M
a locally trivial fibered space with fibers homeomorphi to
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
.

3.3.2.
Charateristic manifold and generic dual tangent bundle

Keeping the same notation as in the previous section, if
(U_{1},\mathbb{X_{1}})
and
(U_{2},\mathbb{X_{2}})
are two overlapping local
\phi
-frames, then the map
\hat{\varphi}_{1,2}{X}:=\hat{\varphi}_{U_{1}}^{\mathbb{X}_{1},-1}\circ\hat{\varphi}_{U_{2}}^{\mathbb{X}_{2}}:(U_{1}\cap U_{2})\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}\rightarrow(U_{1}\cap U_{2})\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
can be explicited as follows.
For
x\in U_{1}\cap U_{2}
and
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
, we write
\tilde{\pi}\in\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
the representation given by

\hat{\varphi}_{1,2}{X}(x,\pi)=(x,\tilde{\pi}).

Suppose first that
\pi
is in the same equivalence class as
\pi^{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, as remarked in Section
3.2.1
, to identify the equivalence class
of
\tilde{\pi}
, it is enough to observe the action of the center. Since the spaces
\mathfrak{r}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{r}_{x}^{*}M
are identified through the vector
\langle R\rangle_{x}\in\mathfrak{r}_{x}M
and that this vector is identical in both
the adapted frames
\langle\mathbb{X}_{1}\rangle_{x}
and
\langle\mathbb{X}_{2}\rangle_{x}
, we easily deduce that for
r\in{\mathbb R}
, we have

\tilde{\pi}({\rm Exp}{H}(r\langle R\rangle_{x}))=e^{i\lambda r}I_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\pi}}}.

Thus, we have
[\tilde{\pi}]=[\pi^{\lambda}]
and the map
\hat{\varphi}_{1,2}{X}(x,\cdot)
is the identity on
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\infty}
.

We deduce that one can talk about the generic dual set of the groups
G_{x}M
independently of the choice of the
\phi
-frame and that the transition maps are given by the identity on
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\infty}
. Similarly as in Section
3.2.1
, we denote the resulting subset of
\widehat{G}_{x}M
by
\widehat{G}_{\infty,x}M
. Considering only these subsets, we get the following definition.

Definition 3.18
.

We define the
generic dual tangent bundle
, denoted by
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
, as the topological subset of
\widehat{G}M
given by

\widehat{G}_{\infty}M:=\{(x,[\pi])\,:\,[\pi]\in\widehat{G}_{\infty,x}M\}.

Moreover,
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
is open and dense in
\widehat{G}M
, and homeomorphic to
M\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\infty}
.

From these considerations, it follows that the generic dual tangent bundle can be endowed with a smooth structure, since it is naturally identified with the characteristic manifold
\Sigma
of the contact manifold
(M,\eta)
. Using the parameterization
j
of
\Sigma
by
M\times{\mathbb R}\setminus\{0\}
given at the end of Section
2.1.1
, it results that the map

(3.8)

(x,\lambda\eta_{x})\in\Sigma\mapsto(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}])\in\widehat{G}_{\infty}M,

is a homeomorphism and we ask it to be a diffeomorphism.

Finally, the parameterizations
\hat{\varphi}_{U}^{\mathbb{X}}
also allow us to define a natural
smooth Plancherel system
.

Definition 3.19
.

A smooth Plancherel system
\{\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}\}_{x\in M}
for
\widehat{G}M
is a choice of Plancherel measure for each of the dual
\widehat{G}_{x}M
for
x\in M
such that the map
x\mapsto\int_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}f(x,\pi)\,d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}(\pi)
is smooth for any function
f\in C_{c}(\widehat{G}M)
which is smooth with respect to the base
M
.

A function
f\in C_{c}(\widehat{G}M)
is said to be smooth with respect to the base
M
if and only if, when pulled back on
M\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
by any local parametrization
\hat{\varphi}_{U}{X}
, it is smooth with respect to the first variable.

Using an atlas of
\phi
-frame, it is possible to define such a smooth Plancherel system by the use of the local parametrization
\hat{\varphi}_{U}{X}
and by pushing forwards the measure
d\nu(x)\otimes d\hat{\mu_{\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}}}(\pi)
defined on
M\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
. The previous discussion shows that such a definition makes sense since on the overlaps
U_{1}\cap U_{2}
, the transition maps
\hat{\varphi}_{1,2}{X}(x,\cdot)
are the identity on
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}_{\infty}
, which is the support of the Plancherel measure
\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d}}
. We will denote by
\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}
the resulting Plancherel system and, by the same notation, the measure it induces on
\widehat{G}M
.

Moreover, this smooth Plancherel system is
compatible
with the smooth Haar system
\mu_{GM}
defined in Section
3.1.2
, in the following sense: for any
x\in M
and for any function
\kappa\in\mathcal{S}(G_{x}M)
, we have

(3.9)

\int_{G_{x}M}|\kappa(v)|^{2}\,d\mu_{G_{x}M}(v)=\int_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}\|\hat{\kappa}(\pi)\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}\,d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}(\pi).

Restricted to the generic dual tangent bundle
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
, the measure
\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}
can be expressed as follows using the identification given by Equation (
3.8
):

d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}(x,\pi^{\lambda})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3d+1}}d\nu(x)\otimes|\lambda|^{d}d\lambda.

Remark 3.20
.

Observe that, up to a multiplicative constant, the measure
\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}
coincides with the symplectic volume
|\omega_{|\Sigma}^{d+1}|
described by Equation (
2.1
).

3.3.3.
Fock Hilbert bundle

The previous section allows us to think of the generic dual tangent bundle
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
as the characteristic manifold
\Sigma
, and thus as a global geometric object rather than only through local parameterizations. However, this is not the case for the representations defined by Equation (
3.7
) through the use of local
\phi
-frames, the reason being that one makes the arbitrary choice of the Langrangian decomposition
\mathfrak{v}_{x}M=\mathfrak{p}_{x}{X}\oplus\mathfrak{q}_{x}{X}
.

To remedy this issue, one can use the Fock-Bargmann representations of the Heisenberg group defined by Equation (
3.3
). More precisely, identifying
\mathfrak{v}_{x}M
with
\mathfrak{p}_{x}{X}\oplus i\mathfrak{q}_{x}{X}={\mathbb C}^{d}
thanks to the
\phi
-frame
\mathbb{X}
, we consider the Hilbert space
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{v}_{x}M)
, defined as the space of functions
F:\mathfrak{v}_{x}M\rightarrow{\mathbb C}
that are holomorphic if
\lambda>0
and anti-holomorphic if
\lambda<0
, and such that

\int_{\mathfrak{v}_{x}M}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-\frac{|\lambda|}{2}|z|^{2}}dz<+\infty.

We then consider the representations of
G_{x}M
defined by: for all
F\in\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{v}_{x}M)
,

\pi^{\lambda}_{x,\mathscr{F}}(g)F(w)={\rm exp}\left(i\lambda r-\frac{\lambda}{4}|z|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}w\overline{z}\right)F(w+z),\quad w\in\mathfrak{v}_{x}M=\mathfrak{p}_{x}M\oplus i\mathfrak{q}_{x}M,

where, for
g\in G_{x}M
, we wrote
z=x+iy
with

g={\rm Exp}_{G_{x}M}(x\cdot\langle X\rangle_{x}+y\cdot\langle Y\rangle_{x}+r\langle R\rangle_{x}),\quad\mbox{with}\ x,y\in{\mathbb R}^{d},\,r\in{\mathbb R}.

However, the expression of this representation can be rewritten so as to be independent of the choice of the
\phi
-frame
\mathbb{X}
and thus of the splitting
\mathfrak{v}_{x}M=\mathfrak{p}_{x}{X}\oplus\mathfrak{q}_{x}{X}
.

First, for any point
\sigma=(x,\lambda\eta_{x})\in\Sigma\sim\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
, we identify the fiber
\mathfrak{v}_{x}M
with the fiber
N_{\sigma}\Sigma
through the isomorphism
\Xi_{\sigma}
defined by Equation (
2.9
). Through this isomorphism, both the endomorphism field
\phi
and the metric
g
can be lifted to
\phi^{N\Sigma}
and
g^{N\Sigma}
respectively on the bundle
N\Sigma
in the following way: we set

\phi_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}={\rm sgn}(\lambda(\sigma))\,\Xi^{*}(\phi_{x}),

as well as

(3.10)

g_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}=\lambda\Xi^{*}(g_{x}),

which is a positive bilinear form when
\lambda>0
and negative otherwise.

We introduce the following sesquilinear form (with respect to the complex structure
\phi_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}
) on
N_{\sigma}\Sigma
: for all
V,W\in N_{\sigma}\Sigma
, we set

h_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(V,W):=g_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(V,W)+i\omega_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(V,W),

where the symplectic form
\omega_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}
was defined in Section
2.2.3
.
Similarly to
g^{N\Sigma}
,
h^{N\Sigma}
is positive on
\lambda>0
and negative otherwise.

Then, writing again
z=x+iy
and
V=x\cdot\langle X\rangle_{x}+y\cdot\langle Y\rangle_{x}
, it is straightforward to check that by construction of the
\phi
-frame, we have

\lambda|z|^{2}=h_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(V,V)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda w\bar{z}=h_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(W,V),

where
w=p+iq
and
W=p\cdot\langle X\rangle_{x}+q\cdot\langle Y\rangle_{x}
.

Thus, the Fock space
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{v}_{x}M)
coincides with the space

\mathscr{F}(N_{\sigma}\Sigma):=\biggl\{F:N_{\sigma}\Sigma\rightarrow{\mathbb C}\,:\,F\ \mbox{is}\ \phi_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}\mbox{-holomorphic}\\
\mbox{ and }\int_{N_{\sigma}\Sigma}|F(W)|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|g_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(W,W)|}d{\rm Leb}_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(W)<+\infty\biggr\},

where
{\rm Leb}_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
N_{\sigma}\Sigma
associated with the metric
g_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}
. Then, the representation
\pi_{x,\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}
of the group
G_{x}M
coincides with the representation
\pi_{\sigma,\mathscr{F}}
defined as follows:

(3.11)

\pi_{\sigma,\mathscr{F}}(g)F(W)={\rm exp}\left(i\lambda(\sigma)r-\frac{1}{2}h_{\sigma}^{N\Sigma}(W+\frac{1}{2}V,V)\right)F(W+V),

where
g={\rm Exp}_{G_{x}M}(V+r\langle R\rangle)
with
V\in\mathfrak{v}_{x}M
and
r\in{\mathbb R}
. Observe that this formula does not depend on the choice of a
\phi
-frame.

Definition 3.21
.

We denote by
\mathscr{F}(N\Sigma)
the collection of all these Fock spaces:

\mathscr{F}(N\Sigma):=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\mathscr{F}(N_{\sigma}\Sigma),

called the
Fock Hilbert bundle
.

Since
N\Sigma
is a smooth bundle over
\Sigma
endowed with a
SU(d)
-group structure, it is straightforward to prove that the set
\mathscr{F}(N\Sigma)
is a smooth Hilbert bundle in the sense of
[
30
]
.
Finally, the results of the previous sections and Equation (
3.11
) allow us to write

(3.12)

\mathscr{F}(N\Sigma)\sim\int_{\widehat{G}_{\infty}M}^{\oplus}\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{x}}d\mu_{\widehat{G}M}(x,\pi_{x}).

4.
Semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus

4.1.
Symbol classes

To begin with, first recall that we defined at the end of Section
3.1.1
a natural smooth Haar system denoted by
\{\mu_{x}\}_{x\in M}
, as well as a compatible smooth Plancherel system in Section
3.3.2
. This allows us to define unequivocally the spaces
L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{x}M)
,
x\in M
, for
a,b\in{\mathbb R}
, as in Section
3.2.4
, as well as their associated norms
\|\cdot\|_{L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{x}M)}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
symbol

\sigma=\{\sigma_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}
on
\widehat{G}M
is a collection of invariant symbols

\sigma(x,\cdot)=\{\sigma(x,\pi):\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\,:\,\pi\in\widehat{G}_{x}M\},

on
\widehat{G}_{x}M
, parameterized by
x\in M
and depending on the semiclassical parameter
\hbar
.
We also say that
\sigma
admits a
convolution kernel
if for any
x\in M
,
\sigma(x,\cdot)\in L_{a,b}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{x}M)
for some
a,b\in{\mathbb R}
.

If a symbol
\sigma
on
\widehat{G}M
admits a convolution kernel, then for
x\in M
, we denote by

\kappa_{\sigma,x}=\mathcal{F}_{G_{x}M,\mu_{x}}^{-1}(\sigma(x,\cdot)),

the corresponding tempered distribution
\kappa_{\sigma,x}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G_{x}M)
given by the inverse Fourier transform taken with respect to the Plancherel measure
\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}
. If a local Heisenberg frame
(\mathbb{X},U)
is given, by the group exponential
{\rm Exp}_{G_{x}M}
, we can consider the corresponding distribution
\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\mathbb{X}}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{d}
:

(4.1)

\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\mathbb{X}}(v):=\kappa_{\sigma,x}\left({\rm Exp}_{x}^{\mathbb{X}}(v)\right)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h}^{d}).

4.2.
Semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus

We recall here the definition of the global semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus on the contact manifold
(M,\eta)
described for filtered manifolds in

\widehat{G}_{\infty}M

4.2.1.
Local semiclassical quantization

We first define the local quantization procedure of symbols for any given local adapted frame
(\mathbb{X},U)
, not necessarily a Heisenberg frame.

Definition 4.7
.

A cutoff for
{\rm exp}{X}
is a smooth function
\chi\in C^{\infty}(M\times M)
valued in
[0,1]
, properly supported in
{\rm exp}{X}(U{X})\subset U\times U
, and equal identically to 1 in a neighborhood of
\{(x,x)\,:\,x\in U\}
.

Given a cutoff function
\chi
, we will use the shorthand notation

\chi_{x}(y):=\chi(x,y).

Now for a symbol
\sigma\in S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)
, denoting by
\kappa_{\sigma}
the associated convolution kernel in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(GM)
, we define the operator
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X},\chi}(\sigma)
at any function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
by

(4.4)

\forall x\in U,\quad{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X},\chi}(\sigma)f(x):=\int_{w\in G_{x}M}\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\hbar}(w)(\chi_{x}f)\left({\rm exp}_{x}{X}(-{\rm Ln}_{x}{X}(w))\right)d\mu_{x}(w),

where the distribution
\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\hbar}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G_{x}M)
is defined by

\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\hbar}(w):=\hbar^{-Q}\kappa_{\sigma,x}(\delta_{\hbar^{-1}}w)

and the integral in Equation (
4.4
) is to be understood in a distributional sense.

If
(\mathbb{X},U)
is a
\phi
-frame, we can also write this local quantization as

{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X},\chi}(\sigma)f(x)=\hbar^{-Q}\int_{v\in{\mathbb R}^{n}}\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\mathbb{X},\hbar}(v)\ (\chi_{x}f)({\rm exp}{X}_{x}(-v))\,dv,

where
\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\mathbb{X},\hbar}(v):=\hbar^{-Q}\kappa_{\sigma,x}{X}(\delta_{\hbar^{-1}}v)
, by definition of the smooth Haar system
\mu
.
Performing the change of variable
y={\rm exp}{X}_{x}(-v)
in Equation (
4.4
), or more accurately, considering the pullback of
dv
via
v\mapsto{\rm exp}{X}_{x}(-v)
,
the quantization formula is also given by

(4.5)

{\rm Op}^{\mathbb{X},\chi}_{\hbar}(\sigma)f(x)=\hbar^{-Q}\int_{M}\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\mathbb{X}}(-\delta_{\hbar}^{-1}\ln{X}_{x}y)\ (\chi_{x}f)(y)\ {\rm jac}_{y}(\ln{X}_{x})\ d{\rm vol}(y),

where
{\rm jac}_{y}(\ln{X}_{x})
denotes the smooth Radon-Nykodym derivative
\frac{d\ln{X}_{x}}{dy}
of
({\rm exp}{X}_{x}(-v))^{*}dv
against the volume form
{\rm vol}
.
The latter formula provides us with the integral kernel of
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X},\chi}(\sigma)
, as it is given by

(4.6)

(x,y)\mapsto\hbar^{-Q}\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\mathbb{X}}(-\hbar^{-1}\ln{X}_{x}(y))\ \chi_{x}(y)\ {\rm jac}_{y}(\ln{X}_{x})=:K_{\hbar}{X}(x,y).

If now
(\tilde{\mathbb{X}},U)
is a Darboux frame, the corresponding local semiclassical quantization is related to the Kohn-Nirenberg semiclassical quantization
{\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}
on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{d}
(as defined in
[
16

17
,
20
,
21
]
) in the following way. By choosing an origin point
x_{0}
in
U

\gamma:={\rm exp}_{x_{0}}^{\tilde{\mathbb{X}}}:V\subset\mathbb{H}^{d}\rightarrow U,

defines a strict contact diffeomorphism (up to taking
U
smaller), where we identify the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{d}
with its Lie algebra and
V
is an open neighborhood of
0
. Observe that since
\tilde{\mathbb{X}}
is a Darboux frame, Theorem
3.8
is valid without any remainder term. In particular, for
v\in\mathbb{H}^{d}\sim{\mathbb R}^{2d+1}
, we can write

{\rm exp}_{x}^{\tilde{\mathbb{X}}}(v)=\gamma(v*_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\gamma^{-1}(x)).

In the trivialization
(x,v)\in U\times\mathbb{H}^{d}
of the tangent group bundle via the local frame
\tilde{\mathbb{X}}

\{\mu_{x}\}_{x\in M}

\forall x\in U,\quad d\mu_{x}(v)=c(x)dv,

with

(4.7)

\forall v\in{\mathbb R}^{2d+1},\quad\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\tilde{\mathbb{X}}}(v)=c(x)^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{G_{x}M,\tilde{\mu}_{x}}^{-1}(\sigma(x,\cdot))({\rm Exp}_{x}^{\tilde{\mathbb{X}}}(v)).

With this in mind, Equation (

by

\displaystyle\forall x\in V,\quad\left(\gamma^{-1}\circ{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X},\chi}(\sigma)\circ\gamma\right)f(x)

on the Heisenberg group, meaning one can sum this discussion up by writing

\gamma^{-1}\circ{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X},\chi}(\sigma)\circ\gamma={\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}(\sigma^{\tilde{\mathbb{X}}}).

4.2.2.
Global semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus

This local quantization allows for the following global definition of a semiclassical pseudodifferential operator.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb R}\cup\{-\infty\}
. The space of
semiclassical pseudodifferential operators

\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M)
of order
m
is defined as the space of all
\hbar
-dependent linear operators

P=\{P_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M),

satisfying the following properties:

(i)

if
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M)
are two maps such that
{\rm supp}(\psi_{1})\cap{\rm supp}(\psi_{2})=\emptyset
, then the operator
\psi_{1}P\psi_{2}
is
semiclassically smoothing on the Sobolev scale
, i.e it satisfies for all
N\in{\mathbb N}
,

\|\psi_{1}P_{\hbar}\psi_{2}\|_{H^{-N}(M)\rightarrow H^{N}(M)}=\mathscr{O}(\hbar^{\infty}),

where the spaces
H^{m}(M)
,
m\in{\mathbb R}
, are adapted Sobolev spaces (see
[
19
, Section 9.5]
);

(ii)

if
(\mathbb{X},U)
is a local Heisenberg frame with cutoff
\chi
, and if
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
, then there exists
\sigma\in S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)
with support in
\widehat{G}_{|U}M
such that

\psi P_{\hbar}(\psi f)={\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X},\chi}(\sigma)f,\quad f\in C^{\infty}(M).

If in the second point of this definition, the symbol
\sigma
appearing is admissible, the corresponding semiclassical pseudodifferential operator
P
is also said to be
admissible
. This makes sense since the formula of change of frames preserve the class of admissible symbols.

Introducing the notation

\Psi_{\hbar}^{\infty}(M):=\bigcup_{m\in{\mathbb R}\cup\{-\infty\}}\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M),

it results from
[
19
]
that the
semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus

\Psi_{\hbar}^{\infty}(M)
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

For each
m\in{\mathbb R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, the spaces
\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M)
have a structure of Fréchet spaces coming from the one of the class of symbols
S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)
and from Definition
4.8
; moreover, the inclusions
\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M)\subset\Psi_{\hbar}^{m^{\prime}}(M)
, for
m\leq m^{\prime}
, is then continuous;

(ii)

The inclusions of
\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M)
in the spaces of endomorphisms on
C^{\infty}(M)
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
are both continuous;

(iii)

If
P_{1}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{m_{1}}(M)
and
P_{2}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{m_{2}}(M)
, then
P_{1}\circ P_{2}
is in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{m_{1}+m_{2}}(M)
; similarly, the adjoint
P_{1}^{*}
is in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{m_{1}}(M)
;

(iv)

Operators in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M)
act naturally on the scale of adapted Sobolev spaces, i.e they belong to the spaces of operators
\mathcal{L}(H^{s}(M),H^{s-m}(M))
for any
s\in{\mathbb R}
.

4.2.3.
Global quantization procedure

We define now the notion of
quantization procedure
.

Definition 4.9
.

A finite collection
\mathcal{A}=(\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha},\psi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
is called an
atlas of local Heisenberg frames
(or simply an atlas) if it consists in

(i)

a collection
(\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},U_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
of local Heisenberg frames which covers
M
;

(ii)

a family of cutoffs
(\chi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
corresponding to the previous local frames;

(iii)

a family of smooth functions
(\psi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
on
M
such that
\sum_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}^{2}=1
and
(x,y)\in M\times M\mapsto\psi_{\alpha}(x)\psi_{\alpha}(y)
is supported in
\{\chi_{\alpha}(x,y)=1\}
.

Remark 4.10
.

The global quantization procedures we are going to define would still make sense for atlases of general local adapted frames. We restrict our attention however to local Heisenberg frames since they are better suited to contact manifolds and since we used such frames to define our symbol classes.

Once we have given an atlas, we can define a global quantization procedure.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha},\psi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
be an atlas. Then, for
\sigma\in S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)
for
m\in{\mathbb R}\cup\{-\infty\}
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we define

\forall x\in M,\quad{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)f(x):=\sum_{\alpha\in A}{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha}}(\psi_{\alpha}\sigma)(\psi_{\alpha}f)(x).

Such a procedure, even if it is not canonical, allows for a nice description of the semiclassical pseudodifferential operators.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an atlas and
P=\{P_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
a linear operator. Then,
P
is in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}
for some
m\in{\mathbb R}\cup\{-\infty\}
if, and only if, there exists a symbol
\sigma=\{\sigma_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}\in S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)
and a remainder
R_{\hbar}:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
such that

A_{\hbar}={\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)+R_{\hbar},

and the operator
R_{\hbar}
is semiclassically smoothing on the Sobolev scale.

In the following sections, we suppose given such a global quantization procedure, simply denoted by
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}
.

The existence of global quantization procedures leads to the following definition of
principal symbol
associated to a semiclassical pseudodifferential operator.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb R}\cup\{-\infty\}
. There exists a unique surjective map

{\rm princ}_{\hbar}:\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M)\rightarrow S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)/\hbar S^{m-1}(\widehat{G}M),

such that, for any quantization procedure
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}
as defined by Definition
4.11
, and for any
\sigma\in S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)
, we have

{\rm princ}_{\hbar}\left({\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\sigma)\right)=[\sigma]\in S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)/\hbar S^{m-1}(\widehat{G}M).

Moreover, we have the following symbolic properties: for any two semiclassical pseudodifferential operators
P_{1}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{m_{1}}(M)
and
P_{2}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{m_{2}}(M)
, we have

{\rm princ}_{\hbar}(P_{1}\circ P_{2})={\rm princ}_{\hbar}(P_{1})\circ{\rm princ}_{\hbar}(P_{2})\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rm princ}_{\hbar}(P_{1}^{*})={\rm princ}_{\hbar}(P_{1})^{*}.

For differential operators, the notion of principal symbols coincides with the one of principal part, as defined in
[
19
, Section 3.4]
, and is then easily computable given a local adapted frame
(\mathbb{X},U)
.

Remark 4.14
.

If the semiclassical pseudodifferential operator
P=\{P_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{m}(M)
is admissible, its principal symbol
{\rm princ}_{\hbar}(P)
can then be identified with a true
\hbar
-independent symbol in
S^{m}(\widehat{G}M)
, i.e not only as an equivalence class.

4.2.4.
Semiclassical functional calculus

We now investigate the functional calculus of the subLaplacian
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}
as part of the semiclassical pseudodifferential calculus
\Psi_{\hbar}^{\infty}(M)
.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb R})
. For any
N\in N
, there exist
Q_{f,N}(\hbar)\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
and
R_{f,N}(\hbar)
a bounded operator on
L^{2}(M)
such that

f(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})=Q_{f,N}(\hbar)+R_{f,N}(\hbar),\quad\mbox{with}\ R_{f,N}(\hbar)=\mathscr{O}_{L^{2}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}(M)}(\hbar^{N+1}).

Moreover, the principal symbol of
Q_{f,N}(\hbar)
is given by

{\rm princ}_{\hbar}(Q_{f,N}(\hbar))=f(H),

where
H
denotes the principal symbol
{\rm princ}_{\hbar}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})
of the subLaplacian.

The proof of this theorem is postponed to Appendix
A
.

4.2.5.
Generic operator wave front set and microlocality

In this section, we define the notion of microlocal equality on the generic dual tangent bundle
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
.

Definition 4.16
.

Let
\sigma=\{\sigma_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}
a semiclassical symbol in
S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
. We say that
\sigma
has a
generic essential support
if there exists a compact set
K\subset\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
such that

{\rm supp}(\chi)\cap K=\emptyset\quad\mbox{implies}\quad\chi\sigma_{\hbar}\in\hbar^{\infty}S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M),

for any
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{\infty}M)
. The smallest such compact set
K
is called the generic essential support and is denoted by

{\rm ess\,supp}_{\infty}(\sigma)\subset\widehat{G}_{\infty}M.

Let
P=\{P_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
a semiclassical pseudodifferential operator. We say that
P
admits a
generic operator wave front set
when, following Proposition
4.12
, this operator can be written

P_{\hbar}={\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\sigma_{\hbar})+R_{\hbar},

with
\sigma=\{\sigma_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}\in S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
having generic essential support. We then introduce the notation

(4.9)

{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P):={\rm ess\,supp}(\sigma).

Observe that this definition does not depend on the choice of global quantization
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}
by the formula of change of frames given by
[
5
, Proposition 2.6]
and
[
5
, Proposition A.5]
, since difference operators are local operators on
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M\sim\Sigma
.

Observe moreover that the properties of the symbolic calculus given by
[
5
, Proposition 2.8-2.9]
have the following consequences: if
P_{1},P_{2}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
have both generic operator wave front set, then we have

{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{1}+P_{2})\subset{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{1})\cup{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{2}),\quad{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{1}\circ P_{2})\subset{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{1})\cap{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{2}),

and

{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{1}^{*})={\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{1}).

This notion leads to the following definition of microlocality on
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
.

Definition 4.17
.

Let
K\subset\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
a closed subset. For any two semiclassical pseudodifferential operators
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
, we say that these operators are microlocally equal on
K
if we have

K\cap{\rm WF}^{\prime}_{\infty}(P_{1}-P_{2})=\emptyset.

Remark 4.18
.

From these definitions, one would also be able to define a generic wave front set for distributions depending on
\hbar
. However, we will have no use for this notion and we will not develop on it.

4.3.
Landau projectors

In this section, we suppose given
(M^{2d+1},\eta,g)
a compact contact metric structure as in Section
2.3
and we consider the associated semiclassical subLaplacian
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR}
, as defined by Definition
2.14
.

4.3.1.
Principal symbol of the subLaplacian

Recall that, given a local
\phi
-frame

\mathbb{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d},R)

on an open subset
U
, the semiclassical subLaplacian writes

-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\hbar^{2}X_{i}^{*}X_{i}+\hbar^{2}Y_{i}^{*}Y_{i}\right),

where the adjoint is taken with respect to the volume form
\nu
. Since the vector fields
(\hbar X_{i},\hbar Y_{i})_{i=1,\dots,d}
are tangent to the contact distribution
\mathcal{D}
, they corresponds to differential operators in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{1}(M)
. We deduce that the semiclassical subLaplacian is an admissible differential operator contained in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{2}(M)
whose principal symbol is given over
\widehat{G}M_{|U}
by the composition rule:

{\rm princ}_{\hbar}^{2}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR}\right)(x,\pi)=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\pi\left(\langle X_{i}\rangle_{x}\right)^{2}+\pi\left(\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{x}\right)^{2}\right).

Recall that we simply denote this principal symbol by
H
. In the trivialization
\hat{\varphi}_{U}{X}
of the generic dual tangent bundle
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
, the symbol
H
is explicit: for
(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}])\in\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
, we have

(4.10)

H(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}])=|\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(-\partial_{\xi_{i}}^{2}+\xi_{i}^{2}\right).

In order to lighten the notations, from now on we will simply denote by
(x,\lambda)
the point
(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}])
of the generic dual tangent bundle given by the trivialization
\hat{\varphi}_{U}{X}
and write
H(\lambda)
for the principal symbol of the subLaplacian seeing that it does not depend on the variable
x
in this trivialization.

Observe now that this expression of
H
is simply a sum of independent harmonic oscillators in the variable
\xi\in\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle_{x}
, which we identify with
{\mathbb R}^{d}
.

We now recall the spectral properties of the harmonic oscillator. The eigenfunctions of
H(\lambda)
can be expressed in terms of the basis of Hermite functions
(e_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb N}}
, normalized
in
L^{2}({\mathbb R})
and satisfying for all
\xi\in{\mathbb R}
the equation

-e_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+\xi^{2}e_{n}(\xi)=(2n+1)e_{n}(\xi).

Indeed, for each multi-index
\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{d}
, the function
h_{\alpha}
defined by

e_{\alpha}(\xi):=\prod_{i=1}^{d}e_{\alpha_{i}}(\xi_{i}),\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d})\in{\mathbb R}^{d},

is an eigenfunction of
H(\lambda)
for the eigenvalue
|\lambda|(2|\alpha|+d)
, that is,

H(\lambda)e_{\alpha}=|\lambda|(2|\alpha|+d)e_{\alpha},\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{d}.

As the family
(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{d}}
is a Hilbert basis in
L^{2}({\mathbb R}^{d})
, these eigenvalues completely describe the spectrum of
H(\lambda)
. Thus, the entire spectrum of
H(\lambda)
is given by the set
\{|\lambda|(2n+d)\}_{n\in{\mathbb N}}
and from the description of the eigenfunctions
(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{d}}
, the multiplicity of the
n
-th eigenvalue is given by

(4.11)

{\rm mult}(|\lambda|(2n+d))=\binom{n+d-1}{n}.

Remark 4.19
.

We recall here, as a useful future reminder, that eigenfunctions of the harmonic oscillator are necessarily odd or even, depending on the parity of
n
, the integer parameter of the Landau levels.

4.3.2.
Homogeneous symbol class of the Landau projectors

For
n\in{\mathbb N}
, we introduce the notation
\Pi_{n}
, called the
n
-th Landau projector
, for the orthogonal projector onto the space of eigenfunctions
e_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb N}^{d}
, satisfying
|\alpha|=n
. This defines a measurable symbol on
\widehat{G}_{U}M
through the trivialization given by
\mathbb{X}
. Equivalently,
\Pi_{n}(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}])
can be described as the spectral projector of
H(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}])
associated with the eigenvalue
|\lambda|(2n+d)
for any
(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}])\in\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
. This definition allows us to see that
\Pi_{n}
is actually globally defined as a measurable symbol on
\widehat{G}M
, called the
n
-th Landau symbol
.

In reality, the Landau projectors are smooth homogeneous symbols, as defined in
[
19
, Section 5.5.1]
.

Proposition 4.20
.

For
n\in{\mathbb N}
, the
n
-th Landau symbol
\Pi_{n}
is a homogenous symbol in
\dot{S}^{0}(\widehat{G}M)
.

Consequently, for any cut-off function
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{\infty}M,[0,1])
, the symbol
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
defined by

(4.12)

\forall(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}])\in\widehat{G}_{\infty}M,\quad\Pi_{n}^{\rho}(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}]):=\rho(x,\lambda)\Pi_{n}(x,[\pi_{x}^{\lambda}]),

is in the class
S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
, since the function
\rho
defines a symbol in
S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
by
[
21
, Theorem 6.5.1]
.

Since the symbols
H
and
\Pi_{n}
are independent of variable
x\in M
in any local trivialization induced by a
\phi
-frame, the proof of Proposition
4.20
follows directly from the one of
[
18
, Proposition 3.6]
.

4.3.3.
Construction of the Landau projectors

Let
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{\infty}M,[0,1])
denote once again a compactly supported cutoff function. Since the symbol
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
defined by Equation (
4.12
) is in
S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
, one can consider its quantization
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n}^{\rho})
. In particular, we are interested in its commutator with the subLaplacian: by the composition rule of semiclassical pseudodifferential operators, the principal symbol
of the operator

[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR},{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n}^{\rho})],

is given by
[H,\rho\Pi_{n}]=0
, and thus we have

[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR},{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n}^{\rho})]\in\hbar\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M).

Similarly, we can write

{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n}^{\rho})\circ{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n}^{\rho})={\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n}^{\rho^{2}})+\hbar\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M).

The last two equations can be interpreted as follows: the operator
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n}^{\rho})
is a projector microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}\subset\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
, up to a term of order
\mathscr{O}(\hbar)
; moreover, this operator also commutes up to
\mathscr{O}(\hbar)
with the subLaplacian
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR}
.

The next result shows that, up to correcting the symbol
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
with lower order terms, the previous remainder in
\mathscr{O}(\hbar)
can be improved to any desired order in
\hbar
.

Theorem 4.21
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb N}
. There exists a unique admissible self-adjoint semiclassical pseudodifferential operator
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}
in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
, called the
n
-th Landau projector
, with principal symbol given by
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
such that microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}
,

[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR},\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}]=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}=(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar})^{*}=\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\circ\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}.

Proof.

The construction of the projector
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}
is done recursively, following the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
. We are looking for semiclassical pseudodifferential operators
(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,k})_{k\in{\mathbb N}}
such that

\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,0}={\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,k}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M),\ k\geq 1,

and for which the following holds true:

(4.13)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}\circ\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}-\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}=\hbar^{k+1}R_{k+1}\quad\mbox{microlocally on }\ \{\rho=1\},

\displaystyle[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\rm sR},\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}]=\hbar^{k+1}T_{k+1},

for some semiclassical pseudodifferential operators
R_{k+1}
and
T_{k+1}
belonging to
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
, and where we introduced the notation

\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}=\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,0}+\hbar\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,1}+\cdots+\hbar^{k}\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,k}.

The existence of the desired operator
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}
will follow from a standard Borel summation procedure.

To begin with, observe that the relations (
4.13
) hold for
k=0
as discussed in the beginning of the section, simply by composition of principal symbols. Let us now assume that we have constructed
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,0},\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,1},\dots,\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,k}
for some
k\geq 0
such that the relations (
4.13
) hold.
Introducing the notation
\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}\in S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
, we are looking for a symbol that satisfies

(4.14)

\Pi_{n}^{\rho}\,\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}-\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}+R_{k+1,0}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad[H,\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}]+T_{k+1,0}=0,

where we denoted
{\rm princ}_{\hbar}\left(R_{k+1}\right)
and
{\rm princ}_{\hbar}(T_{k+1})
respectively by
R_{k+1,0}
and
T_{k+1,0}
, and where we set
\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}={\rm Id}-\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
.

The first relation in (
4.14
) defines the diagonal part of
\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}
on
\{\rho=1\}
in the following sense:

(4.15)

\Pi_{n}^{\rho}\,\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}\Pi_{n}^{\rho}=-\Pi_{n}^{\rho}R_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}=\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}R_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp},

and it is enough to satisfy the first relation in (
4.14
) if and only if one can check the following first compatibility relation

(4.16)

[\Pi_{n}^{\rho},R_{k+1,0}]=0.

The second relation in (
4.14
) determines the anti-diagonal part of
\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}
: indeed, it writes

(4.17)

[H,\Pi_{n}^{\rho}\Pi_{k+1}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}]=-\Pi_{n}^{\rho}T_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[H,\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}\Pi_{k+1}\Pi_{n}^{\rho}]=-\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}T_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho}.

This system can be solved if and only if

(4.18)

\Pi_{n}^{\rho}[H,R_{k+1,0}]\Pi_{n}^{\rho}=\Pi_{n}^{\rho}T_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}[H,R_{k+1,0}]\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}=-\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}T_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}\,.

Thus Equations (
4.16
) and (
4.18
) are compatibility relations.

Postponing the verification of these compatibility conditions, we can reformulate the first equation in Equation (
4.17
) as follows: let
Y=-\Pi_{n}^{\rho}T_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}
, we are looking for
X=\Pi_{n}^{\rho}\Pi_{k+1}\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}
such that

(H\Pi_{n}^{\rho})X-X(H\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp})=Y.

Such a problem is solved by the Sylvester’s Theorem since the spectral gap between eigenvalues of
H
is uniformly bounded by below on the set
\{\rho=1\}
. Moreover, following
[
6
, Theorem A.1]
, we have the following formula for the solution
X
: locally around any point
(x,\lambda)\in\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
, we can write

X=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}(H\Pi_{n}^{\rho}-z)^{-1}Y(H\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}-z)^{-1}\,dz,

where
\gamma
is a closed contour in the plane with winding number one around the spectrum of
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
and zero around the one of
\Pi_{n}^{\rho,\perp}
. Such a closed curve
\gamma
exists thanks to the spectral gap between eigenvalues of the symbol
H
in a neighborhood of
(x,\lambda)\in{\rm supp}(\rho)
. This allows to conclude that
X
is a symbol in
S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
and thus that Equations (
4.15
) and (
4.17
) defines uniquely the symbol
\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}
, and thus a semiclassical pseudodifferential operator by setting

\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,k}={\rm Op}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k})\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M).

It remains to verify that the compatibility relations are satisfied. Let us start with Equation (
4.16
). We deduce from the first relation of Equation (
4.13
) that

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}R_{k+1}(1-\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]})

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-k-1}\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}\circ\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}-\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]})(1-\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]})

\displaystyle=-\hbar^{-k-1}(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}\circ\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}-\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]})^{2}=-\hbar^{k+1}R_{k+1}^{2},

whence
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}R_{k+1,0}(1-\Pi_{n}^{\rho})=0
. One argues similarly with
(1-\Pi_{n}^{\rho})R_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
.
To prove Equation (
4.18
), we use Equation (
4.13
) and write

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}T_{k+1}\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-k-1}\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR},\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}]\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-k-1}\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}\left(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}(-\hbar\Delta_{sR})(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]})^{2}-(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]})^{2}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}\right)\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}

\displaystyle=\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})R_{k+1}-R_{k+1}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]},

whence
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}HR_{k+1,0}-R_{k+1,0}H\Pi_{n}^{\rho}=\Pi_{n}^{\rho}T_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
, which implies

\Pi_{n}^{\rho}[H,R_{k+1,0}]\Pi_{n}^{\rho}=\Pi_{n}^{\rho}T_{k+1,0}\Pi_{n}^{\rho},

since
[\Pi_{n}^{\rho},R_{k+1,0}]=0
. One argues similarly for the other relation.
This concludes the induction argument. By standard Borel summation procedure, we have the existence of a semiclassical pseudodifferential operator
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
such that

\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\sim\sum_{k\geq 0}\hbar^{k}\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,k}.

Observe that, since for every step
k\geq 0
, the symbol
\Pi_{n}^{\rho,k+1}
is uniquely determined by the operator
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,[k]}
and by Equations (
4.14
), the final operator
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho}
is uniquely determined microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}
.
Finally, in order to end the proof, we check that we have

(4.19)

(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar})^{*}=\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar},

microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}
by the following unicity argument: indeed, the semiclassical pseudodifferential operator
(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar})^{*}
has principal symbol
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
, and by self-adjointness of the subLaplacian
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}
, it also satisfies

[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR},(\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar})^{*}]=0\quad\mbox{microlocally on}\ \{\rho=1\},

and is also trivially microlocally a projector. Thus Equation (
4.19
) holds.
∎

Remark 4.22
.

Since this recursive construction of the Landau projectors
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}
,
n\geq 0
, once having singled out a particular quantization procedure
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}
, entirely follows the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
, the propositions
[
6
, Proposition 1.3]
and
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
, also extends to our setting. In particular, we have that for
n,m\geq 0
two distincts integers the corresponding Landau projectors are microlocally orthogonal:

\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\circ\hat{\Pi}_{m}^{\rho,\hbar}=0\quad\mbox{microlocally on}\ \{\rho=1\}.

Similarly, for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb R})
, we also have

[f(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}),\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}]=0\quad\mbox{microlocally on}\ \{\rho=1\}.

However, we will not make use of these two further properties in the following sections.

5.
Egorov theorem

This section is dedicated to the proof of an Egorov theorem for the subLaplacian when restricted to each Landau level, i.e the ranges of the Landau projectors.

5.1.
Toeplitz operators

Let
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{\infty}M,[0,1])
be a admissible cutoff function. We introduce particular spaces of observables in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
corresponding to each Landau level.

Definition 5.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, the space of Toeplitz operators associated to the
n
-th Landau level, denoted by
\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\rho}(M)
, is the subspace of
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
defined as follows:

\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\rho}(M):=\{\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\circ A\circ\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\,:\,A\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{\infty}(M)\}.

Remark 5.2
.

Since we will mostly work microlocally on the closed subset
\{\rho=1\}
, we will still say that a semiclassical pseudodifferential operator
B\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\rho}(M)
if it coincides microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}
with such a Toeplitz operator.

The denomination of Toeplitz operators has been introduced in a similar setting by various authors, e.g.
[
15
]
and
[
38
]
, where the role of the Landau projectors is played by similar operators called generalized Szegö projectors.

Working with the space of observables
\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\rho}(M)
, we are led to consider the following vector bundle over
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M\sim\Sigma
.

Definition 5.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we define the
n
-th Landau bundle

\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma)
as the smooth bundle over
\Sigma
obtained as the range of the smooth field of orthogonal Landau projectors
\Pi_{n}
acting on the Fock Hilbert bundle
\mathscr{F}(N\Sigma)
:

\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma)=\Pi_{n}\mathscr{F}(N\Sigma).

This bundle is of use for the following particular reason: for any observable in
\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\rho}(M)
of the form
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}A\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}
, the notion of principal symbol as introduced in Proposition
4.13
, can then naturally be reinterpreted as a section of the bundle
{\rm End}(\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma))
.

The following crucial result clarifies the role played by the Landau projectors in the understanding of the relation between the subLaplacian and the Reeb vector field on the contact manifold
(M,\eta)
.

Proposition 5.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, there exists a unique Toeplitz operator
\Theta_{n}\in\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\rho}(M)
satisfying, microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}
,

-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}\,\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}=\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\left((2n+d)\hbar^{2}|R|\right)\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}+\hbar^{2}\Theta_{n}.

We denote by
\theta_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{\infty}M,{\rm End}(\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma)))
the principal symbol of
\Theta_{n}
.

This result can be interpreted as a global normal form of the subLaplacian when restricted to a Landau level.

Remark 5.5
.

Since the remainder
\Theta_{n}
is uniquely determined microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}
, a sequence of cutoff functions
\rho
converging to
1
can be used to uniquely define the principal symbol
\theta_{n}
as a section on the whole characteristic manifold
\Sigma
. Thus, we do not mark the dependence on the cutoff
\rho
in its notation.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

By definition of the space
\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\rho}(M)
, the proposition can be deduced from the following result:

(5.1)

\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-(2n+d)\hbar^{2}|R|\right)\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}\in\hbar^{2}\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)\quad\mbox{microlocally on}\ \{\rho=1\}.

Note that by simple symbolic calculus, by definition of
\Pi_{n}^{\rho}
, we know that this operator is at least in the space
\hbar\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
.

Following the definition of the pseudodifferential calculus, one can localize on
M
in order to make use of a frame and a local quantization to compute this pseudodifferential operator. We choose to do so with respect to a Darboux frame
\tilde{\mathbb{X}}=(\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{d},\tilde{Y}_{1},\dots,\tilde{Y}_{d},R)
. In such a frame, as Section
4.2.1
and Equation (
4.8
) demonstrate, the local quantization given by Equation (
4.4
) then coincides with the semiclassical quantization
{\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}
on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{d}
.

This allows us to write these different operators as follows

-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}={\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}(\tilde{H}_{0}+\hbar\tilde{H}_{1})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hbar^{2}|R|={\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}(\lambda),

where
\tilde{H}_{0}
and
\tilde{H}_{1}
are respectively in
S^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d}\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
and
S^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{d}\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
, as well as

\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}={\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}(\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}+\hbar\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{1}+\hbar^{2}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{\geq 2}),

with
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}
,
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{1}
and
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{\geq 2}
being smoothing symbols on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
. Moreover, by definition, we have the following relation:

\tilde{H}_{0}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}=(2n+d)\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}.

In order to proceed with the proof, we need to say more about the principal symbol
\tilde{H}_{0}
of the subLaplacian, computed with respect to the Darboux frame
\tilde{\mathbb{X}}
. Introducing the notation
(\tilde{g}_{i,j}(x))_{1\leq i,j\leq 2d}
,
x\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
, for the matrix associated to the inner product
g_{\mathcal{D}}(x)
computed in the left-invariant basis
(\tilde{V}_{1},\dots,\tilde{V}_{d})=(\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{d},\tilde{Y}_{1},\dots,\tilde{Y}_{d})
. The symbol
\tilde{H}_{0}
is then given by

\tilde{H}_{0}(x,\tilde{\pi}^{\lambda})=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 2d}\tilde{g}_{i,j}(x)\,\tilde{\pi}^{\lambda}(\tilde{V}_{i})\tilde{\pi}^{\lambda}(\tilde{V}_{j}).

Thus, it is a second order differential operator acting on
\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\pi}^{\lambda}}^{-\infty}=L^{2}(\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{x})
. Moreover, it can be written as a pseudodifferential operator whose Weyl symbol is given by the quadratic form associated to the inner product
g_{\mathcal{D}}(x)
acting on
\mathcal{D}_{x}\sim\tilde{\mathfrak{v}}_{x}
. Here, the vector space
\tilde{\mathfrak{v}}_{x}
is seen as a symplectic space with symplectic form
(d\eta)_{|\mathcal{D},x}
, splitted as the sum of two Langrangian subspaces
\tilde{\mathfrak{v}}_{x}=\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{x}\oplus\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{x}
. In particular, since the inner product
g_{\mathcal{D}}
comes from an adapted complex structure given by the endomorphism
\phi_{|\mathcal{D}}
, it follows from the construction of
\phi
-frames in Section
3.1.1
or equivalently from Williamson’s theorem (see
[
15
, Section 10]
and
[
12
, Proposition 32]
) that one can find symplectic coordinates
(\xi,\zeta)\in{\mathbb R}^{2d}
such that the quadratic form associated to
g_{\mathcal{D}}(x)
can be written as

|\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\xi_{i}^{2}+\zeta_{i}^{2}).

We conclude that, up to conjugation with a metaplectic unitary operator acting on
L^{2}(\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{x})
, the symbol
\tilde{H}_{0}(x,\tilde{\pi}^{\lambda})
can be reduced to a sum of harmonic oscillators.

Now observe that by the composition formula for the Heisenberg quantization (see for example
[
20
, Theorem 5.1]
), we can write

\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-(2n+d)\hbar^{2}|R|\right)\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}=\hbar{\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}\left(T_{1}\right)+\hbar^{2}\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d}),

where

T_{1}=(\tilde{H}_{0}-(2n+d)|\lambda|)\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{1}+\tilde{H}_{1}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}+\Delta_{\tilde{v}}(\tilde{H}_{0}-(2n+d)\lambda)\cdot\tilde{V}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}.

Thus, using that the operator
\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}
is microlocally a projector on
\{\rho=1\}
, in order to prove Equation (
5.1
), it is enough to prove that we have

\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}T_{1}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}=0\quad\mbox{on}\ \{\rho=1\}.

In order to simplify the expression of the symbol
T_{1}
, we need to make use of the symbolic equation (
4.14
) satisfied by
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{1}
as the first corrector of
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}
in the construction of the Landau projector using the Heisenberg quantization
{\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}
associated to the Darboux frame
\tilde{\mathbb{X}}
. Explicitly, one finds, once again by the composition formula for semiclassical pseudodifferential operators, that

[\tilde{H}_{0},\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{1}]+[\tilde{H}_{1},\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}]+\Delta_{\tilde{v}}\tilde{H}_{0}\cdot\tilde{V}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}-\Delta_{\tilde{v}}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}\cdot\tilde{V}\tilde{H}_{0}=0.

Thus, on
\{\rho=1\}
we have

\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}T_{1}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}=\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}\tilde{H}_{1}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}+\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}\left(\Delta_{\tilde{v}}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}\cdot\tilde{V}\tilde{H}_{0}\right)\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}.

One then concludes as follows: up to metaplectic conjugation as described above, one is reduced to the case where
\tilde{H}_{0}
is a sum of harmonic oscillators and
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}
is a projector on the
n
-th Landau level. By Remark
4.19
, the range of
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}
is composed of either odd or even functions depending on
n
. Since the symbol
\tilde{H}_{1}
is a sum of first order homogeneous differential operators and of multiplication operators by first order homogeneous polynomials, it follows from a parity argument that we have
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}\tilde{H}_{1}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}=0
.
By the same argument, since the difference operators
\Delta_{\tilde{v}}
are commutators with first order homogeneous differential operators or multiplication operators by first order homogeneous polynomials (see
[
21
, Section 6.3.3]
) and since we have

\tilde{V}\tilde{H}_{0}(x,\tilde{\pi}^{\lambda})=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 2d}\tilde{V}\tilde{g}_{i,j}(x)\,\tilde{\pi}^{\lambda}(\tilde{V}_{i})\tilde{\pi}^{\lambda}(\tilde{V}_{j}),

we also deduce that
\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}\left(\Delta_{\tilde{v}}\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}\cdot\tilde{V}\tilde{H}_{0}\right)\tilde{\Pi}_{n}^{0}=0
, and this ends the proof.
∎

5.2.
Egorov theorem for Toeplitz operators

Proposition
5.4
leads us to consider a particular geometric flow on the Landau bundles that we now explicit.

5.2.1.
Partial connections on the Landau bundles

Indeed, the result of Proposition
5.4
tells us that the subLaplacian
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}
acts effectively on the
n
-th Landau level,
n\geq 0
, as does the operator
\hbar^{2}|R|+\hbar^{2}\Theta_{n}
. This effective operator defines a geometric flow
\Phi^{n}_{t}
on the
n
-th Landau bundle
\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma)
as follows: the flow lines
(\sigma(t),v(t))
consist of the orbits
\sigma(t)\in\Sigma
of the Hamiltonian vector field
\vec{\lambda}\in\Gamma(\Sigma)
as defined by Proposition
2.4
and such that
v(t)\in\mathscr{L}_{n}(N_{\sigma(t)}\Sigma)
satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}v(t)=i\theta_{n}(\sigma(t))v(t).

Note that by Proposition
2.4
,
\Phi^{n}_{t}
lifts the Reeb flow on
M
to the
n
-th Landau bundle.

Observe that the induced flow
(\Phi^{n}_{t})^{*}
on
C^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma))
defines a partial connection on the space of section of
\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma)
. Hence, there is also a flow
{\rm Ad}(\Phi^{n}_{t})
on the bundle
{\rm End}(\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma))
, which in turn defines an action of
{\mathbb R}
on
C^{\infty}(\Sigma,{\rm End}(\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma)))
, denoted by
{\rm Ad}(\Phi^{n}_{t})^{*}
.

It is straightforward to check that if
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,{\rm End}(\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma)))
, for any local trivialization given for example by a
\phi
-frame
(\mathbb{X},U)
, we have

(5.2)

\frac{d}{dt}{\rm Ad}(\Phi^{n}_{t})^{*}\sigma=\vec{\lambda}\sigma+i[\theta_{n},\sigma].

5.2.2.
Weak Egorov Theorem

All the mathematical apparatus is now in place to enounce and prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
n\geq 0
and fix
T\geq 0
. For any Toeplitz operator
A\in\mathscr{T}_{n}^{\rho}(M)
with principal symbol
\alpha\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{\infty}M,\mathscr{L}_{n}(N\Sigma))
, there exists a family
(\tilde{A}(t))_{0\leq t\leq T}
whose principal symbols
(\tilde{\alpha}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
satisfy

\forall\,0\leq t\leq T,\quad\tilde{\alpha}_{t}={\rm Ad}(\Phi^{n}_{t})^{*}\alpha,

and such that, microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}
, we have

e^{it\Delta_{sR}}Ae^{-it\Delta_{sR}}=\tilde{A}(t)+\mathscr{O}(\hbar)\quad\mbox{uniformly for }0\leq t\leq T.

Remark 5.7
.

The weak version of the Egorov theorem presented here could easily be strenghtened following, for instance, the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 11.1]
; however, such a strenghtened result is not needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Observe that by definition of the symbols
\tilde{\alpha}_{t}
, by Equation (
5.2
), we can write

\partial_{t}\tilde{\alpha}_{t}=\vec{\lambda}\tilde{\alpha}_{t}+i[\theta_{n},\tilde{\alpha}_{t}].

Moreover, the principal symbol of the commutator

\frac{i}{\hbar^{2}}[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR},\tilde{A}(t)]=\frac{i}{\hbar^{2}}[-\hbar^{2}iR+\hbar^{2}\Theta_{n},\tilde{A}(t)],

is given on
\{\rho=1\}
by

\vec{\lambda}\tilde{\alpha}_{t}+i[\theta_{n}\tilde{\alpha}_{t}].

We deduce from these equations that microlocally on
\{\rho=1\}
we have

\partial_{t}\tilde{A}(t)=\frac{i}{\hbar^{2}}[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR},\tilde{A}(t)]+E(t),\quad E(t)\in\hbar\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M).

Hence, we can write

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(e^{-it\Delta_{sR}}\tilde{A}(t)e^{it\Delta_{sR}}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-it\Delta_{sR}}\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{A}(t)-\frac{i}{\hbar^{2}}[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR},\tilde{A}(t)]\right)e^{it\Delta_{sR}}

\displaystyle=e^{-it\Delta_{sR}}E(t)e^{it\Delta_{sR}}=\mathscr{O}_{L^{2}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}(M)}(\hbar).

Integrating with respect to
t\in[0,T]
, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

6.
Quantum ergodicity

The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows the general strategy of proving quantum ergodicity in the elliptic setting and begins with investigating the microlocal Weyl law for observables in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
.

6.1.
Microlocal Weyl laws

We recall the following notations concerning the eigenvalue problem for the subLaplacian. The self-adjoint operator
(-\Delta_{sR},{\rm Dom}(-\Delta_{sR}))
has discrete point spectrum given by the sequence

0\leq\delta_{0}\leq\delta_{1}\leq\dots\leq\delta_{k}\leq\dots\rightarrow+\infty,

(with repetitions according to multiplicity). Moreover, there exists an orthonormal basis
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb N}}
, such that for every
k\in{\mathbb N}
,

-\Delta_{sR}\varphi_{k}=\delta_{k}\varphi_{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1.

Thus, reintroducing the semiclassical parameter
\hbar>0
in front of the subLaplacian, we can write

\forall k\in{\mathbb N},\quad-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}\varphi_{k}=\delta_{k}(\hbar)\varphi_{k}\quad\mbox{where}\ \delta_{k}(\hbar)=\hbar^{2}\delta_{k}.

We then have the following theorem concerning the microlocal repartition of the eigenvalues, to be compared with the results of
[
39
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
B\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
be an admissible semiclassical pseudodifferential operator. Then, for any two real numbers
a<b
, we have the following asymptotic

\hbar^{Q}\sum_{a\leq\delta_{k}(\hbar)\leq b}\langle B\varphi_{k},\varphi_{k}\rangle\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\rm vol}(M)\int_{\widehat{G}M}{\rm Tr}\left({\bm{1}}_{[a,b]}(H)(x,\pi)\beta(x,\pi)\right)d\mu_{\widehat{G}M}(x,\pi),

where
\beta\in S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
denotes the principal symbol of
B
.

Remark 6.2
.

For
n\geq 0
, applying this result for
B=\hat{\Pi}_{n}^{\rho,\hbar}
, a Landau projector with
\rho
a cutoff functions converging to the constant 1, and using Equation (
4.11
), we compute that the main term in the microlocal Weyl law for
a=0
and
b=1
, tends to

2\frac{{\rm vol}(M)}{(d+1)(2\pi)^{3d+1}}\frac{\binom{n+d-1}{n}}{(2n+d)^{d+1}}.

This coefficient can be interpreted as the proportion of eigenvalues in the Weyl law for
-\Delta_{sR}
associated to the
n
-th Landau level.

We first start with a general consideration of the trace of semiclassical pseudodifferential operators.

Lemma 6.3
.

Any operator
B\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
is of trace class on
L^{2}(M)
. Moreover, if
\beta\in S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
denotes the principal symbol of
B
, we have

{\rm Tr}(B)={\rm vol}(M)\int_{\widehat{G}M}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}(\beta(x,\pi))\,d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}(x,\pi)+\mathscr{O}(\hbar).

Proof.

We make use of Proposition
4.12
, to decompose the operator
B
as follows:

(6.1)

B=\sum_{\alpha\in A}{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha}}(\psi_{\alpha}\beta_{\hbar})\psi_{\alpha}+R_{\hbar},

where
\beta_{\hbar}
is a symbol in
S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
such that
\beta_{\hbar}=\beta+\hbar S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
,
\mathcal{A}=(\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha},\psi_{\alpha})
is an atlas and
R_{\hbar}:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
is semiclassically smoothing on the Sobolev scale. Thus, it is enough to show that each term in this decomposition is of trace class.

For a smoothing symbol
\sigma\in S^{-\infty}(\widehat{G}M)
, following Equation (
4.6
), we have that for
\alpha\in A
, the integral kernel of the operator
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha}}(\psi_{\alpha}\sigma)\psi_{\alpha}
is given by

K_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}}(x,y)=\hbar^{-Q}\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}}(-\hbar^{-1}\ln{X}_{x}(y))\ \chi_{x}(y)\ {\rm jac}_{y}(\ln{X}_{x})\psi_{\alpha}(x)\psi_{\alpha}(y),

where
\kappa_{\sigma}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}}
is defined by Equation (
4.1
).
Thus, by Proposition
3.17
,
K_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}}
is a smooth function on
M\times M
. Thus, the operator
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha}}(\psi_{\alpha}\sigma)\psi_{\alpha}
is of trace class (see for example
[
42
, Theorem C.16]
). By the same argument, the remainder
R_{\hbar}
, having smooth kernel since it is smoothing on the Sobolev scale, and
M
being compact, is also of trace class and its trace is of order
\mathscr{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
.

Moreover, the trace of the operator
{\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha}}(\psi_{\alpha}\sigma)\psi_{\alpha}
can be related to its kernel
K_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}}
by the following formula (see for example
[
42
, Theorem C.18]
):

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}({\rm Op}_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha}}(\psi_{\alpha}\sigma)\psi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}K_{\hbar}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}}(x,x)\,d{\rm vol}(x)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-Q}\int_{M}\kappa_{\sigma,x}^{\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}}(0)\psi_{\alpha}(x)^{2}\,d{\rm vol}(x)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-Q}\int_{M}\int_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\sigma(x,\pi)\right)\psi_{\alpha}(x)^{2}\,d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}(\pi)d\nu(x),

where the last equation is obtained using the Fourier inversion formula (see Equation (
3.4
)), the smooth Plancherel system being compatible with the smooth Haar system chosen to define the kernel
\kappa_{\sigma}
.

Plugging this formula in the decomposition (
6.1
), we find that

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(B)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-Q}\sum_{\alpha\in A}\int_{M}\int_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\beta(x,\pi)\right)\psi_{\alpha}(x)^{2}\,d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}_{x}M}(\pi)d\nu(x)+\mathscr{O}(\hbar)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-Q}\int_{\widehat{G}M}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\beta(x,\pi)\right)\,d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}(x,\pi)+\mathscr{O}(\hbar).

∎

We are ready to give the proof of the microlocal Weyl’s law.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\underline{f_{\varepsilon}}
and
\overline{f_{\varepsilon}}
be two smooth functions on
{\mathbb R}
valued in
[0,1]
satisfying

\underline{f}{\bm{1}}_{[a,b]}=\underline{f}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{f}{\bm{1}}_{[a,b]}={\bm{1}}_{[a,b]},

and such that
{\rm supp}(\underline{f_{\varepsilon}})\subset[a+\varepsilon,b-\varepsilon]
and
{\rm supp}(\overline{f_{\varepsilon}})\subset[a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]
.
We also denote by
\Pi_{[a,b]}
the spectral projector of
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}
on the energy window
[a,b]
. Then,we can write

\sum_{a\leq\delta_{k}(\hbar)\leq b}\langle B\varphi_{k},\varphi_{k}\rangle={\rm Tr}\left(B\,\Pi_{[a,b]}\right)={\rm Tr}\left(\underline{f_{\varepsilon}}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})B\right)+{\rm Tr}\left((\overline{f_{\varepsilon}}(1-\underline{f_{\varepsilon}}))(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})B\,\Pi_{[a,b]}\right).

The second term can be estimated using the semiclassical functional calculus given by Theorem
4.15
and Lemma
6.3
, from which we can deduce that

{\rm Tr}\left(\left|\overline{f_{\varepsilon}}(1-\underline{f_{\varepsilon}}))(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})\right|\right)\leq(c_{1}(\varepsilon)+c_{2}(\hbar,\varepsilon))\hbar^{-Q},

where
\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}c_{1}(\varepsilon)=0
by support condition on the principal symbol of this operator relatively to
\varepsilon
, following the definition of
\underline{f_{\varepsilon}}
and
\overline{f_{\varepsilon}}
, and where
\lim_{\hbar\rightarrow 0}c_{2}(\hbar,\varepsilon)=0
.

By the same argument applied to the first term, we readily get that

\displaystyle\hbar^{Q}{\rm Tr}\left(B\,\Pi_{[a,b]}\right)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{Q}{\rm Tr}\left(\underline{f_{\varepsilon}}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})B\right)+\mathscr{O}\left(\|B\,\Pi_{[a,b]}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}(c_{1}(\varepsilon)+c_{2}(\hbar,\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{G}M}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\underline{f_{\varepsilon}}(H)(x,\pi)\beta(x,\pi)\right)d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}(x,\pi)+\mathscr{O}_{\varepsilon}(\hbar)+\mathscr{O}(c_{1}(\varepsilon)+c_{2}(\hbar,\varepsilon)).

Thus, we have

\limsup_{\hbar\rightarrow 0}\hbar^{Q}{\rm Tr}\left(B\,\Pi_{[a,b]}\right)=\int_{\widehat{G}M}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\underline{f_{\varepsilon}}(H)(x,\pi)\beta(x,\pi)\right)d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}(x,\pi)+\mathscr{O}(c_{1}(\varepsilon)),

and similarly for
\liminf_{\hbar\rightarrow 0}
. We conclude by dominated convergence letting
\varepsilon
go to 0.
∎

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Denoting by
N(\delta)
,
\delta\geq 0
, the number of eigenvalues
(\delta_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb N}}
of
-\Delta_{sR}
that are below
\delta
, it is enough to prove that for any
a\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have

(6.2)

\frac{1}{N(\delta)}\sum_{k,\,\delta_{k}\leq\delta}\left|\langle a(\cdot)\varphi_{k},\varphi_{k}\rangle-\int_{M}a(x)\,d\nu(x)\right|^{2}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\delta\rightarrow+\infty}0.

A well-known lemma due to Koopman and Von Neumann (see, e.g.,
[
33
, Chapter 2.6, Lemma 6.2]
and a diagonal extraction argument (as in
[
42
, Theorem 1.5]
) allows us then to deduce the theorem.

To prove Equation (
6.2
), we are going to fix the following relation between the semiclassical parameter
\hbar
and the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{sR}
: for
n\in{\mathbb N}^{*}
, we fix

\hbar_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}.

With this semiclassical parameter, we can rewrite Equation (
6.2
) as follows. We first set the notation

N_{\hbar_{n}}={\rm Card}\left(\{k\in{\mathbb N}\,:\,\delta_{k}(\hbar_{n})\in[0,1]\}\right),

for which we have
N_{\hbar_{n}}=N(\delta_{n})
, and, for
B
any bounded operator on
L^{2}(M)
, we define the quantum variance by

{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(B)=\frac{1}{N_{\hbar_{n}}}\sum_{0\leq\delta_{k}(\hbar_{n})\leq 1}\left|\langle B\varphi_{k},\varphi_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}.

Considering the following bounded operator

A=\left(a(\cdot)-m_{a}\right)f(-\hbar_{n}^{2}\Delta_{sR})\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M)),

where
m_{a}:=\int_{M}a(x)\,d\nu(x)
, Equation (
6.2
) is then equivalent to proving

\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(A)=0.

Observe that by Theorem
4.15
,
A
is a semiclassical pseudodifferential operator in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
, up to a semiclassically smoothing remainder that will play no role in the rest of the proof,, with principal symol given by
(a(\cdot)-m_{a})f(H)
.

We start by giving a first estimate on the quantum variance. If
B
is in
\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M)
with principal symbol denoted by
\beta
, and if
C
is a bounded operator, then by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have

\left|\langle CB\varphi_{k},\varphi_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\|CB\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}\leq\|C\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}^{2}\langle B^{*}B\varphi_{k},\varphi_{k}\rangle.

(6.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(CB)

\displaystyle\leq\|C\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}^{2}\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{N_{\hbar_{n}}}\sum_{0\leq\delta_{k}(\hbar_{n})\leq 1}\langle B^{*}B\varphi_{k},\varphi_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\|C\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}^{2}\int_{\widehat{G}M}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\beta(x,\pi)^{*}\beta(x,\pi){\bm{1}}_{[0,1]}(H)(x,\pi)\right)d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}(x,\pi),

where the last equation follows from Theorem
6.1
since the principal symbol of
B^{*}B
is simply given by
\beta^{*}\beta
and since, up to a constant that we will neglect,
N_{\hbar_{n}}
is of order
\hbar_{n}^{-Q}
as
n
goes to infinity.

In order to estimate the quantum variance of
A
, we introduce, for
m\in{\mathbb N}
, a cutoff function
\rho_{m}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{\infty}M)
such that
\rho_{m}(x,\lambda)=1
if
\lambda
is in
[(2m+d)^{-1},1]
. We consider the associated
m-1

\hat{\Pi}_{j}^{\rho_{m},\hbar}
,
j=1,\dots,m-1
, and we define the following operator

\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\hat{\Pi}_{j}^{\rho_{m},\hbar_{n}}\in\Psi_{\hbar}^{-\infty}(M).

Its principal symbol is given by
\Pi_{<m}^{\rho_{m}}:=\rho_{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\Pi_{j}
.
We now write

\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(A)\leq 2\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(B_{1})+2\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(B_{2}),

where we have introduced the notations

B_{1}=\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}}A\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}},

and

B_{2}=\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}}A({\rm Id}-\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}})+({\rm Id}-\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}})A\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}}+({\rm Id}-\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}})A({\rm Id}-\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}}).

We first treat the term involving the operator
B_{2}
.
Observe that, by considering the symbol of this pseudodifferential operator, we have

B_{2}=({\rm Id}-\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}})A({\rm Id}-\hat{\Pi}_{<m}^{\hbar_{n}})+\mathscr{O}_{L^{2}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}(M)}(\hbar).

By the estimate of Equation (
6.3
), we can bound its quantum variance as follows:

(6.4)

\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(B_{2})\leq\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}^{2}\int_{\widehat{G}M}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left((1-\Pi_{<m}^{\rho_{m}})^{2}{\bm{1}}_{[0,1]}(H)\right)d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}.

Now using the parameterization of
\widehat{G}_{\infty}M
given by Equation (
3.8
), we observe that for
\lambda\in[(2m+d)^{-1},1]

{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi^{\lambda}}}\left((1-\Pi_{<m}^{\rho_{m}}(x,\lambda))^{2}{\bm{1}}_{[0,1]}(H(\lambda)\right)=0,

since
\rho_{m}(x,\lambda)=1
and no eigenfunction of the harmonic oscillator

can simultaneously be orthogonal to the range of the projector

and be associated with an eigenvalue in the energy window

. Thus, the integral in Equation (

(6.5)

We are thus left with the following bound for the quantum variance of

C_{d}\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}^{2}\int_{M\times{\mathbb R}^{*}}{\bm{1}}_{[0,(2m+d)^{-1}]}(|\lambda|)\,d\lambda d\nu(x)=2C_{d}\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}^{2}(2m+d)^{-1},

which tends to

as

We now need to estimate the quantum variance of
B_{1}
. For this, observe that, once again by symbolic consideration, we have

B_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\hat{\Pi}_{j}^{\rho_{m},\hbar_{n}}A\hat{\Pi}_{j}^{\rho_{m},\hbar_{n}}+\mathscr{O}_{L^{2}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}(M)}(\hbar).

the operator

We see that, for

Now, for

Theorem
5.6
then tells us that we can write, microlocally on

\langle B_{1}^{j}\rangle_{T}=\langle\tilde{B}_{1}^{j}\rangle_{T}+\mathscr{O}_{L^{2}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}(M)}(\hbar),

\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\rho}(M)

\tilde{\beta}_{j}(t)=\beta_{j}(\Phi_{t}^{j}),

where we identified

Thus, by fixing another cutoff function
\tilde{\rho}_{m}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{G}_{\infty}M)
with

\tilde{\rho}_{m}(x,\lambda)=1

[(2m-1+d)^{-1},1]

\displaystyle\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(B_{1}^{j})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(\langle B_{1}^{j}\rangle_{T})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}({\rm Op}(\tilde{\rho}_{m})\langle\tilde{B}_{1}^{j}\rangle_{T})+\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}\left(({\rm Id}-{\rm Op}_{\hbar_{n}}(\tilde{\rho}_{m}))\langle B_{1}^{j}\rangle_{T}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widehat{G}M}|\tilde{\rho}_{m}|^{2}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\langle\tilde{\beta}_{j}\rangle_{T}^{*}\langle\tilde{\beta}_{j}\rangle_{T}{\bm{1}}_{[0,1]}(H)\right)d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}

\displaystyle+\,\|\langle B_{1}^{j}\rangle_{T}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\int_{\widehat{G}M}(1-\tilde{\rho}_{m})^{2}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left({\bm{1}}_{[0,1]}(H)\right)d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widehat{G}M}|\langle\tilde{\beta}_{j}\rangle_{T}|^{2}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left(\Pi_{m}^{\rho}{\bm{1}}_{[0,1]}(H)\right)d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M}

\displaystyle+\,\|B_{1}^{j}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\int_{\widehat{G}M}(1-\tilde{\rho}_{m})^{2}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\left({\bm{1}}_{[0,1]}(H)\right)d\hat{\mu}_{\widehat{G}M},

where we once again identified the symbol
\tilde{\beta}(t)
with its scalar coefficient.

The second term is treated in the same way as was the quantum variance of
B_{2}
, and thus goes to 0 as
m
goes to infinity. The first term, using Equation (
6.6
) and the estimate (
6.5
), can be bounded as follows

C_{d}\int_{{\mathbb R}^{*}}\left(\int_{M}|\langle a\rangle_{T}^{\lambda}(x)-m_{a}|^{2}\,d\nu(x)\right)|f_{j}(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\lambda,

where we set

\langle a\rangle_{T}^{\lambda}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}a(\Phi_{t}^{{\rm sgn}(\lambda)})\,dt,

and where we denoted by
\Phi_{t}^{{\rm sgn}(\lambda)}
the Reeb flow on
M
going forward or backward in time, depending on the sign of
\lambda
.
Thus, by the assumption of ergodicity of the Reeb flow and by von Neumann theorem, we have that for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb R}^{*}
,

\int_{M}|\langle a\rangle_{T}^{\lambda}(x)-m_{a}|^{2}\,d\nu(x)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{T\rightarrow+\infty}0,

and thus by dominated convergence, we have
\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(B_{1}^{j})=0
by letting
T
goes to
+\infty
.

We conclude that
\limsup_{\hbar_{n}\rightarrow 0}{\rm Var}_{\hbar_{n}}(B_{1})
equals 0 and this ends the proof.

Appendix A
Semiclassical functional calculus

This appendix is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
4.15
. It is inspired by the proof of the semiclassical functional calculus for the usual pseudodifferential calculus on manifolds proposed by
[
29
]
, and makes use of the semiclassical functional calculus on graded nilpotent Lie groups given by
[
20
]
.

We fix
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb R})
a smooth compactly supported function and we consider the operator
f(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})
acting on
L^{2}(M)
, which is defined by functional calculus.

Following Definition
4.11
, we choose a particular global quantization with which to work, or equivalently a particular atlas, inspired by the discussion at the end of Section
4.2.1
. In what follows, we consider
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=(\tilde{\mathbb{X}}_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha},\psi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
, an atlas in the sense of Definition
4.9
, such that all frames
\tilde{\mathbb{X}}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in A
, are Darboux frames.

Doing so, by fixing a reference point
x_{\alpha}\in U_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in A
, the exponential map
{\rm exp}_{x_{\alpha}}^{\tilde{\mathbb{X}}_{\alpha}}
with base point
x_{\alpha}
defines a map between an open neighborhood
V_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{H}^{d}
of zero and
U_{\alpha}
, which we simply denote by
\gamma_{\alpha}
.

For each
\alpha\in A
, we choose in addition a compact set
K_{\alpha}\subset U_{\alpha}
such that
{\rm supp}(\psi_{\alpha})\subset{\rm Int}(K_{\alpha})
, and two cutoff functions
\overline{\psi}_{\alpha},\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
with supports contained in
{\rm Int}(K_{\alpha})
and such that
\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}\equiv 1
on
{\rm supp}(\psi_{\alpha})
and
\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}\equiv 1
on
{\rm supp}(\overline{\psi}_{\alpha})
. We keep the same notation for these cutoffs functions pulled back on
V_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{H}^{d}
by
\gamma_{\alpha}
.

Now, for each
\alpha\in A
, we define the differential operator
T_{\alpha}(\hbar)
,
\hbar>0
, in the following way: considering a cutoff function
\rho_{\alpha}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
such that
\rho_{\alpha}\equiv 1
on
\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}(K_{\alpha})\subset V_{\alpha}
, we introduce

(A.1)

T_{\alpha}(\hbar):=\rho_{\alpha}(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})\gamma_{\alpha}^{*}+(1-\rho_{\alpha})(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}),

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
denotes the left-invariant subLaplacian on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{d}
. Moreover, we denote by
\tau_{\alpha}\in S^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d}\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
its
\hbar
-independent principal symbol:

T_{\alpha}(\hbar)={\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}(\tau_{\alpha})+\hbar\Psi_{\hbar}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{d}).

This way, the operator
T_{\alpha}(\hbar)
satisfies all the hypotheses of
[
20
]
, specifically Setting 6.1 and Hypotheses 6.2 and 6.3. Moreover, we then have

(A.2)

-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}\varphi=T_{\alpha}(\hbar)(\varphi\circ\gamma_{\alpha})\circ\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1},

for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
with
{\rm supp}(\varphi)\subset K_{\alpha}
.

By self-adjointness, the operator
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z
is invertible in
\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(M))
for
z\in{\mathbb C}\setminus{\mathbb R}
, and by the Helffer-Sjöstrand formula
[
42
, Theorem 14.8]
, one has

f(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})=\frac{1}{i\pi}\int_{\mathbb C}\bar{\partial}_{z}\tilde{f}(z)\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z\right)^{-1}dz,

where
dz
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb C}
and
\tilde{f}:{\mathbb C}\rightarrow{\mathbb C}
is an almost analytic extension of
f
as in
[
42
, Theorem 3.6]
.

We now construct a parametrix that approximates
(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)^{-1}
, using the operators
T_{\alpha}(\hbar)
, up to a given explicit remainder term. To this end, subelliptic regularity (see
[
20
, Lemma 6.9]
) implies that the resolvents
(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}
induce operators

(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d}).

We now define the operator
P(\hbar,z):C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
:

P(\hbar,z):=\sum_{\alpha\in A}(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\right)\circ\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}.

By Equation (
A.2
), and since the subLaplacian is a local operator, we can write

\displaystyle P(\hbar,z)\circ(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in A}(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\right)\circ\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}\circ(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)\circ\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\Big[(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\right)\circ(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)\circ\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\right)\circ(1-\overline{\psi}_{\alpha})\circ(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)\circ\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}\Big]

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{\alpha\in A}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z),

where we set

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z):=(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\right)\circ(1-\overline{\psi}_{\alpha})\circ(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)\circ\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}.

Observe that by the introduction of the cutoff functions
\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in A
, the definition of each remainder
\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z)
involves an operator that is composed from the left and from the right with multiplication operators by functions whose supports are disjoint.

It now immediately follows that

(A.3)

(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)^{-1}=P(\hbar,z)+\sum_{\alpha\in A}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z),

with

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z):=\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z)\circ(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)^{-1}.

Plugging Equation (
A.3
) in the Helffer-Sjöstrand formula, we obtain

\displaystyle f(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i\pi}\int_{{\mathbb C}}\bar{\partial}_{z}\tilde{f}(z)\left(\sum_{\alpha\in A}(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\right)\circ\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}+\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z)\right)\,dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\left[(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ\frac{1}{i\pi}\int_{{\mathbb C}}\bar{\partial}_{z}\tilde{f}(z)(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\,dz\right)\circ\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}+\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\left[(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ f(T_{\alpha}(\hbar))\circ\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}+\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar)\right],

where we set

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar):=\frac{1}{i\pi}\int_{\mathbb C}\bar{\partial}_{z}\tilde{f}(z)\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z)\,dz.

We now make use of
[
20
, Theorem 6.7]
which allows us to say that each operator
f(T_{\alpha}(\hbar))
,
\alpha\in A
, decomposes as

f(T_{\alpha}(\hbar))={\rm Op}_{\mathbb{H}^{d},\hbar}(\sigma_{\alpha})+\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar),

where the symbol
\sigma_{\alpha}
is in
S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d}\times\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{d})
, coincides at first order in
\hbar
with
f(\tau_{\alpha})
, and where the remainder
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar)
is semiclassically smoothing on the Sobolev scale, in the sense of
[
20
, Definition 5.9]
.

Thus, in order to deduce the result of Theorem
4.15
, we are left with proving that the remainders
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar)
are each of order
\mathscr{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
as bounded operators acting on
L^{2}(M)
. To do so, we estimate the boundedness of the integrand in the definition of the remainder
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar)
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
be two smooth cutoff functions with disjoint support. Then, for each
N\in{\mathbb N}
, there exists a constant
C_{N}>0
such that, for
z\in{\mathbb C}\setminus{\mathbb R}
and
|z|
bounded, we have

\|\psi_{1}\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\circ\psi_{2}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d}))}\leq C_{N}\hbar^{N}|{\rm Im}\,z|^{-N-1}.

Proof.

The proof goes exactly as in the one of
[
29
, Lemma 4.3]
, the only estimate that one needs being that, for bounded
z
in
{\mathbb C}\setminus{\mathbb R}
, we must have

\|[T_{\alpha}(\hbar),\psi]\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d}))}=\mathscr{O}(\hbar|{\rm Im}\,z|),

for any compactly supported function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
. This is an easy consequence of the commutator formula of two semiclassical pseudodifferential operator.
∎

Using the estimate of Lemma
A.1
, we get for each
N\in{\mathbb N}
a constant
C_{N}>0
such that for bounded
z
, we have

\|(\gamma_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{2}\circ(T_{\alpha}(\hbar)-z)^{-1}\right)\circ(1-\overline{\psi}_{\alpha})\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\leq C_{N}\hbar^{N}|{\rm Im}\,z|^{-N-1}.

Moreover, we also have

\|(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sr}-z)\circ\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}\circ(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\\
=\|[-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR},\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}]\circ(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{sR}-z)^{-1}+\overline{\overline{\psi}}_{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\leq C\hbar|{\rm Im}\,z|^{-1}+1.

Finally, this gives, for a new constant
C_{N}^{\prime}>0
the following estimate

\|\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}(\hbar,z)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\leq C_{N}^{\prime}\hbar^{N}|{\rm Im}\,z|^{-N-1}.

Putting these estimates together in the definition of the remainder
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar)
, and since
\tilde{f}
is a compactly supported analytic extension of
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb R})
, meaning it can be chosen to satisfy the estimate

\exists C_{N}>0,\forall z\in{\rm supp}(\tilde{f}),\quad|\bar{\partial}_{z}\tilde{f}(z)|\leq C_{N}|{\rm Im}\,z|^{N},

we obtain for each
\alpha\in A
,

\|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\hbar)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}=\mathscr{O}_{L^{2}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}(M)}(\hbar^{\infty}).

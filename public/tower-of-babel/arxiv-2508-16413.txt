Quantum Fisher information as a witness of non-Markovianity and criticality in the spin-boson model Preprint: APS/123-QED
D. Parlato 1,†, G. Di Bello 1,†, F. Pavan 1, G. De Filippis 2,3, C. A. Perroni 2,3

Abstract
The quantum Fisher information, the quantum analogue of the classical Fisher information, is a central quantity in quantum metrology and quantum sensing because of its connection to parameter estimation and fidelity susceptibility. Using numerically exact methods applied to a paradigmatic open quantum system, the spin-boson model, we calculate both static and dynamical quantum Fisher information matrix elements with respect to spin-bath couplings and magnetic field strengths. As the spin-bath interaction increases, we first show that the coupling-coupling matrix elements relative to the ground state of the Hamiltonian serve as a genuine witness of bipartite entanglement and the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless quantum phase transition through their non-monotonic behavior. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the time-dependent matrix elements can reveal non-Markovian effects as well as the transition from the coherent to incoherent regime at the Toulouse point. In the paradigmatic spin-boson model, the non-monotonic features of the quantum Fisher information matrix signal changes in quantum resources such as entanglement and coherence, quantify non-Markovian behavior, and enable criticality-enhanced quantum sensing, thereby shedding light on key features of open quantum systems.



The quantum Fisher information (QFI) is a transversal quantity linked to various aspects of quantum physics, making it a versatile tool to characterize any system
Liu et al. 2019
;
Šafránek 2017
;
Gammelmark and Mølmer 2014
;
Alipour et al. 2014
;
Sidhu and Kok 2020
;
Faist et al. 2023
;
Ying et al. 2022
;
Hauke et al. 2016
.
First of all, the quantum Fisher information matrix (QFIM) satisfies the quantum Cramér-Rao bound
Gammelmark and Mølmer 2014
;
Alipour et al. 2014
;
Nielsen 2013
;
Helstrom 1976
;
Holevo 2001
: the variance of a parameter estimate is lower bounded by the corresponding QFI, which depends only on the quantum state rather than on the specific measurement process. This makes the QFI a central concept to enhance parameter estimation in quantum systems. More precisely,
given a vector
\vec{x}=(x_{1},..,x_{a},x_{b},..,x_{n})
containing the relevant parameters of a system, and a quantum state
\rho(\vec{x})
dependent on these parameters, the QFIM is defined as
Liu et al. 2019
:

F_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\,\text{Tr}(\rho\{L_{\mu},L_{\nu}\})\;,

(1)

where
\{A,B\}
denotes the anti-commutator of the operators
A
and
B
. The operators
L_{\mu}
and
L_{\nu}
are the symmetric logarithmic derivative operators, implicitly defined as the solution to the equation (for the parameter
x_{\mu}
)

\partial_{\mu}\rho=\frac{1}{2}(L_{\mu}\rho+\rho L_{\mu})\;,\quad\partial_{\mu}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mu}}\;
.

By using the spectral decomposition of the density operator
\rho
, the QFIM becomes
Liu et al. 2019
:

F_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{i,j}\frac{2Re(\bra{\lambda_{i}}\partial_{\mu}\rho\ket{\lambda_{j}}\bra{\lambda_{j}}\partial_{\nu}\rho\ket{\lambda_{i}})}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\;,

(2)

where
\lambda_{i}
and
\ket{\lambda_{i}}
are the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
\rho
, respectively.

In addition to parameter estimation, the QFIM has deep significance in understanding the geometric structure of the Hilbert space, as it quantifies the distinguishability between quantum states corresponding to different parameter values.
The Bures distance can be defined as

D_{B}^{2}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=2(1-f(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}))\;,

where
f(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
is Uhlmann’s fidelity
Uhlmann 1976
between the quantum states
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
.
Considering the distance between infinitesimally close states, one can define the Bures metric matrix
g_{\mu\nu}
.
It is found that the Bures metric is proportional to the QFIM:

4g_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\nu}\;

Šafránek 2017
;
Zhou and Jiang 2019
. This relation establishes the QFIM as the metric tensor in the parameter space of quantum states, quantifying state distinguishability and sensitivity under parameter variations. This property makes it central for quantum sensing, as it indicates how to achieve maximum parameter sensitivity, which is relevant for the development of quantum detectors
Mercer et al. 2025
;
Giovannetti et al. 2006
;
Paris 2009
;
Tóth and Apellaniz 2014
.

Near a quantum phase transition (QPT), the ground state is maximally sensitive to a variation of the driving parameter. Indeed, the QFI can also be employed to characterize QPTs and criticality-enhanced sensing
Zhu et al. 2023
;
Lü et al. 2022
;
Sarkar et al. 2025
;
Chu et al. 2021
.
Using the definition of the Bures distance, a strong connection is found between fidelity susceptibility and the QFIM:

f(\rho(\vec{x}),\rho(\vec{x}+d\vec{x}))=1-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}dx_{\mu}dx_{\nu}\;,

(3)

A diverging fidelity susceptibility has already been linked to a QPT
Gu 2010
;
Zhang et al. 2010
, so that also the QFI tends to diverge in correspondence of such a transition.

The usefulness of QFI extends further beyond closed quantum systems, as it also offers insights into the behavior of more realistic open systems
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
;
Schaller 2014
;
Paris 2009
;
Hao et al. 2013
;
Wang et al. 2015
. These systems typically exhibit relaxation, decoherence, and, with increasing spin-bath coupling, non-Markovian dynamics, the signatures of which lie in the study of the QFI, particularly in the QFI flow, i.e., its time derivative
Abiuso et al. 2023
;
Lu et al. 2010
. On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, a complete understanding of the static and dynamical behavior of the QFIM is still lacking for open quantum systems, especially in the strong coupling regime.

In this work, we aim to fill this gap by analyzing, through numerically exact methods, the QFIM of a paradigmatic open quantum system, the spin-boson model (SBM)
Weiss 2012
;
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
;
Schaller 2014
, which describes the interaction of a spin one-half, representing a generic two-level system (TLS), with a bosonic bath of
N
harmonic oscillators. This model, which includes amplitude damping processes, exhibits a Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) QPT as the strength of the spin-bath interaction increases
De Filippis et al. 2020
;
De Filippis et al. 2023
;
Di Bello et al. 2025
. First of all, we show that the maximum of the QFIM of the Hamiltonian ground state (GS) turns out to be a marker of the related QPT, signaling criticality-enhanced sensing to very weak magnetic fields. Moving from the weak to intermediate coupling regime, the coupling-coupling QFIM element shows a decreasing monotonic behavior linked to increasing von Neumann entropy. We then generalize the model to provide a comprehensive description
of the noise mechanisms acting on the spin, including a pure dephasing term, against which the critical signatures remain robust. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the QFIM dynamics witness the coherent-incoherent transition and provide useful insights into the non-Markovian character of the system through the QFI flow. In particular, in the coherent regime, this flow exhibits characteristic oscillations, whose frequencies are different from those of the system observables. These findings prove that the non-monotonic features of the QFIM offer a comprehensive understanding of key properties of open quantum systems.

Model and Numerical Methods.

We focus on the zero-temperature regime and adopt units where
\hbar=1
.
The Hamiltonian of the generalized SBM is written as
Weiss 2012
:

H_{SB}=H_{Q}+H_{B}+H_{I}\;,

(4)

where:
1)

H_{Q}=-\frac{\Delta}{2}\sigma_{x}-\frac{h}{2}\sigma_{z}
is the spin one-half free Hamiltonian, with
\Delta
(our energy unit) and
h
(typically small) representing magnetic fields along the
x
and
z
axes, respectively;
2)

H_{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\bigg(b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)
is the bath Hamiltonian, a set of
N
harmonic oscillators with frequencies
\omega_{i}
;
3)

H_{I}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\sigma_{z}\lambda_{i}^{z}+\sigma_{x}\lambda_{i}^{x})(b_{i}+b_{i}^{\dagger})
is the qubit-bath interaction Hamiltonian, with couplings
\lambda_{i}^{x}
and
\lambda_{i}^{z}
. Here,
\sigma_{z}
and
\sigma_{x}
are the Pauli matrices representing the TLS, and
b_{i}
and
b_{i}^{\dagger}
the annihilation and creation operators for bosonic modes of frequency
\omega_{i}
.
The couplings
\lambda_{i}^{z}
are obtained by discretizing a continuous spectral density function
J(\omega)=\sum_{i}^{N}|\lambda_{i}^{z}|^{2}\delta(\omega-\omega_{i})=\frac{\alpha}{2}\omega_{c}^{1-s}\omega^{s}\Theta(\omega-\omega_{c})
, where
\omega_{c}
is the cutoff frequency, that we set to
\omega_{c}=10\Delta
,
\alpha
is the dimensionless spin-bath coupling associated with amplitude damping processes,
s
is the Ohmicity parameter which we set to
s=1
(Ohmic bath). The couplings
\lambda_{i}^{x}
are sampled analogously, and we denote the dimensionless strength of pure dephasing processes by
\beta
.

We will compute the Hamiltonian GS and dynamics of the QFIM elements by using analytical approximations as well as numerical techniques. Analytical treatments of the dynamics considered in this work rely on Lindblad’s quantum master equation
Lindblad 1976
(within this approximation, the static stationary QFIM elements are erroneously zero
sup
). This approach is valid at weak couplings for Markovian dynamics, but it breaks down when the system-bath interaction strengthens and non-Markovian effects emerge
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
;
Schaller 2014
.

To access the strong coupling regime and capture non-Markovian effects, we employ tensor-network techniques based on MPS representation, that yields numerically exact results for models mapped onto one-dimensional chains. The GS has been calculated using the density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) with a cutoff of
10^{-15}
. The dynamics have been simulated starting from a spin-down state and the vacuum for the bath oscillators, using the
W^{II}
algorithm
Paeckel et al. 2019
, i.e. applying on the state a matrix product operator constructed from the second order expansion of the evolution operator. In all simulations, we have used
N=600
bosonic modes and we have calculated the QFIM using Eq. (
2
).

Static QFIM through DMRG.

We first focus on the QFIM calculated for the Hamiltonian GS in the absence of pure dephasing term (
\beta=0
). We analyze the QFIM element
F_{\alpha\alpha}
(see panel a of Fig.
1
),
F_{\alpha\Delta}
, and
F_{\Delta\Delta}
(see panels a and b of Fig. (1) in the Supplemental Material
sup
) as functions of the spin-bath coupling
\alpha
for small positive values of the magnetic field
h
. The limit
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
is relevant due to the symmetry breaking that occurs in these systems. Actually, the SBM is known to exhibit a QPT
Florens et al. 2010
;
De Filippis et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2010
;
Tong et al. 2011
;
Hur 2008
from a delocalized quantum spin to a localized classical spin state at the critical value
\alpha_{c}\approx 1+O(\Delta/\omega_{c})
(
\alpha_{c}\approx 1.05
for our choice of parameters).

For any

, all three QFIM elements display a pronounced peak at strong coupling, meaning that the system becomes increasingly sensitive to variations in the coupling
\alpha
. With decreasing

, near criticality, the peak of the QFI
F_{\alpha\alpha}
shifts towards larger values of
\alpha
and becomes sharper and higher. Hence, for
h=0^{+}
,

is expected to diverge at the exact value

. As discussed in the Supplemental Material

, the same behavior applies to the other two QFIM elements
F_{\alpha\Delta}
and

sup
, we detail the procedure used to extract the critical value
\alpha_{c}
directly from QFIM. With this method we obtain a critical coupling
\alpha_{c}=1.03\pm 0.03
, consistent with literature
De Filippis et al. 2020
(the extrapolations from
F_{\Delta\Delta}
yield similar results). In conclusion, the QFIM elements are excellent markers of the QPT, whose critical strength
\alpha_{c}
can be accurately determined by the position of their maxima.

Traditionally, the QPT of the SBM is characterized through spin observables like
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle
as
h
decreases (see for example
Hur 2008
). For this reason, in panel b of Fig.
1
, we report
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle
as a function of spin-bath coupling
\alpha
for small positive
h
, showing excellent agreement with existing literature. Indeed, as
h
decreases,
the curves of
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle
steepen near the critical value of
\alpha
. Notably, the maxima of the QFIM correspond to the inflection points of the
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle
approaching zero, which mark regions of high sensitivity for both static and dynamical properties.

From the weak to intermediate coupling regime,
F_{\alpha\alpha}
shows a monotonic decreasing behavior, which cannot be easily related to features of
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle
.
As detailed in the End Matter,
F_{\alpha\alpha}
diverges precisely as
1/\alpha
in the ultra-weak coupling regime (see Fig.
4
). This behavior is relevant since it implies very high state sensitivity for small
\alpha
, but its rapid decrease as
\alpha
increases.
Moreover, this feature recalls the connection of QFI with correlation functions
Liu et al. 2019
;
Hauke et al. 2016
: indeed, in related models with electron-oscillator interactions, the optical conductivity shows an inverse proportionality law with respect to the coupling strength
De Filippis et al. 2014
.
As shown in panel a of Fig.
1
, this
1/\alpha
behavior persists up to intermediate coupling and remains robust under the introduction of dephasing
\beta>0
. We argue now that perturbative considerations and dimensional analysis applied to Eq.  (
2
) help clarify this result. At a perturbative level in
\alpha
, one can assume in Eq. (
2
) that
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}\propto\alpha
. Using also the dimensional analysis, one guesses
\bra{\lambda_{i}}\partial_{\alpha}\rho\ket{\lambda_{j}}\propto\alpha/\alpha=\textrm{const}
, giving

F_{\alpha\alpha}\propto 1/\alpha
, in agreement with numerical results.
Similarly,
\bra{\lambda_{i}}\partial_{\Delta}\rho\ket{\lambda_{j}}\propto\alpha/\Delta
predicts
F_{\Delta\Delta}\propto(\alpha/\Delta)(\alpha/\Delta)/\alpha\rightarrow 0
and
F_{\alpha\Delta}\propto(\alpha/\Delta)/\alpha=1/\Delta
as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, consistent with the numerical results shown in the Supplemental Material
sup
.

Finally, in this regime of spin-bath coupling, the decreasing behavior of
F_{\alpha\alpha}
can be linked to an increasing GS von Neumann entropy of the spin one-half, reported in panel c of Fig.
1
. As the spin entangles more with the bath, its sensitivity decreases. In particular, at fixed
h
, the monotonic decrease of the QFI stops when the external field effect becomes significant. This occurs at the same coupling for which the corresponding
S(\rho)
differs from the curves at different magnetic fields. This point is very close to the maximum of the von Neumann entropy. Moreover, as
h
decreases, the asymptotic change in curvature of
S(\rho)
can also be used to extrapolate an estimate for
\alpha_{c}
.

We now turn to the analysis of the information encoded in the time evolution of the QFIM. In particular, we focus on the evolution of coupling-coupling element
F_{\alpha\alpha}(t)
, computed using both the numerically exact MPS approach
Di Bello et al. 2024
and the Markovian Lindblad master equation, the latter being valid only in the weak spin-bath coupling regime
Saleem et al. 2023
. Within the framework of Lindblad’s approximation, the analytical form of the density operator
\rho(t)
allows us to derive a closed-form expression for the QFIM
sup
. In Fig.
3
, we show the QFI element
F_{\alpha\alpha}
as a function of time for the spin-bath couplings
\alpha=0.1,0.25,0.5
, in the case of
h=0
and dephasing coupling
\beta=0
, since very small values of
h
and
\beta
introduce only negligible corrections, in this regime of parameters.

The dynamics of the SBM exhibit a coherent-incoherent transition at the Toulouse point
\alpha=0.5
: for
\alpha<0.5
, the system displays coherent oscillations, whereas for
\alpha>0.5
the behavior becomes incoherent. This can be seen in the dynamics of
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle
that shows these two distinct behaviors
Hur 2008
, as shown in Fig.
7
of the End Matter for the same parameters as in Fig.
3
.

We first examine the coherent regime (see panels a and b of Fig.
3
). For
\alpha=0.1
, MPS and Lindblad predictions of QFI
F_{\alpha\alpha}
are comparable only at short times, diverging significantly as the stationary state is approached. Additional results reported in the End Matter show that even for
\alpha=0.01
, where the two approaches nearly coincide initially, they still deviate at long times (see Fig.
5
). Actually, the behavior of
F_{\alpha\alpha}(t)
within Lindblad approximation can be separated into two regimes: an initial transient characterized by quadratic growth,
F_{\alpha\alpha}(t)\sim t^{2}
, followed by a rapid exponential decay towards zero. In contrast, the MPS QFI dynamics exhibit pronounced oscillations modulated by an exponentially decaying envelope. These oscillations progressively damp out, and the QFI converges to a finite stationary value, in excellent agreement with the DMRG ground-state results and, consequently, strongly dependent on
\alpha
. On the other hand, the QFI in the Lindblad case always vanishes at long times, since the corresponding steady state (the Gibbs thermal state) is independent of
\alpha
. As the coupling strength
\alpha
increases, both the frequency of oscillations and the peak amplitude of the QFI decrease, indicating a reduction in the system’s sensitivity. In this coherent regime, the QFIM flow
Lu et al. 2010
;
Liu et al. 2019
, defined as
\partial_{t}F_{\mu,\nu}
, can be used to characterize non-Markovian effects after the initial transient. As detailed in the End Matter, starting from the maximum of the respective curves, the MPS solution exhibits alternating positive and negative flow, evidencing continuous information backflow between system and bath (see Fig.
8
of the End Matter). By contrast, the Lindblad flow remains strictly negative, indicating a purely outward flow of information.

Finally, as shown in panel c of Fig.
3
, for
\alpha=0.5
the QFI witnesses the coherent-incoherent transition: oscillations vanish and the evolution becomes incoherent. Apart from an initial transient in which it reaches the maximum, it monotonically relaxes to the stationary DMRG value. Indeed, the QFI dynamics are strongly correlated with those of the system observables. From the comparison with results of
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle(t)
reported in the End Matter in Fig.
7
, we find that each local maximum of
F_{\alpha\alpha}
corresponds approximately to an extremum of
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle(t)
. These points represent regions of high sensitivity to variations in
\alpha
, and therefore coincide with the peaks of the QFI. This interpretation is further supported by the Fourier analysis presented in Fig.
8
of the End Matter: the dominant frequency component of
F_{\alpha\alpha}(t)
is exactly twice that of
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle(t)
, consistent with their functional relationship.

Conclusions.

We have characterized the non-monotonic behaviors of the static and dynamical QFIM in a generalized SBM, a paradigmatic prototype of open quantum systems. The static QFIM reveals clear signatures of criticality-enhanced sensing near the quantum-to-classical transition, identifying the values of spin-bath coupling
\alpha
that yield high sensitivity. Moreover, tracking the position of the maximum of QFIM provides a reliable extrapolation of the critical coupling
\alpha_{c}
of the BKT QPT.
Going from the weak to intermediate coupling regime, the QFIM element
F_{\alpha\alpha}
exhibits a characteristic monotonic decrease, correlated with the monotonic increase of the von Neumann entropy, thereby linking quantum sensitivity to entanglement growth.

On the dynamical side, the QFIM reflects the coherent-to-incoherent transition that occurs as the interaction with the environment increases. In the coherent regime,
F_{\alpha\alpha}
displays a characteristic oscillatory behavior whose dominant frequency is exactly twice that of spin observables, establishing a direct connection between QFI dynamics and non-Markovian effects.
These findings prove that the non-monotonic signatures of the QFIM provide powerful probes for identifying criticality, quantifying non-Markovian behavior, and understanding key emergent phenomena in open quantum systems.

In the coherent regime of the spin dynamics, the QFI flow, defined as
\partial_{t}F
, can be used to characterize non-Markovian effects
Lu et al. 2010
;
Liu et al. 2019

8

F_{\alpha\alpha}

\alpha=0.1

As discussed in the main text, the Lindblad flow remains negative throughout the dynamics, indicating a one-way information outflow, whereas the MPS solution exhibits an alternating flow, signaling a continuous exchange of information between the system and the bath.

IV
QFIM in Lindblad approximation

In the study of dynamical QFIM at zero temperature, we have compared the numerical results with those obtained within the Lindblad approximation. The expression we have used is a particular case of Eq. (1) of the main text:

F_{ab}=2\,\text{Tr}[(\partial_{a}\rho)(\partial_{b}\rho)]+\frac{1}{4}\frac{\bigg(\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{a}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{b}}\bigg)}{det(\rho)}\;,

(6)

where
P=\text{Tr}[\rho^{2}]
. After solving the Lindblad master equation, one obtains the analytical form of
\rho
, which, when substituted into Eq. (
6
) for
x_{a}=x_{b}=\alpha
gives a closed-form expression for
F_{\alpha\alpha}
:

F_{\alpha\alpha}=4S^{2}+4t^{2}(\gamma^{2}+(\partial_{\alpha}\Sigma)^{2})|\rho_{01}(0)|^{2}e^{-2\gamma t}+\frac{\{S[\rho_{00}(t)-\rho_{11}(t)]-2\gamma t|\rho_{01}(0)|e^{-2\gamma t}\}^{2}}{\det[\rho(t)]}\;.

(7)

We have defined

S(t)\equiv\partial_{\alpha}\rho_{00}(t)\;,

(8)

and
\Sigma
is the Lamb-Stark shift caused by the interaction.
As can be easily seen,
F_{\alpha\alpha}(t\rightarrow+\infty)=0
, since the stationary state is
\ket{\downarrow}
, independently of the coupling. Hence, Lindblad stationary state is not sensitive to variations in
\alpha
: its QFIM vanishes. We remark that the QFIM could be calculated also by means of other methods, such those based on non-equilibrium Green’s functions
Perroni et al. 2015
.

Local well-posedness in the critical regularity setting for hyperbolic systems with partial diffusion
Jean-Paul Adogbo & Raphäel Danchin

Abstract
. This paper is dedicated to the local existence theory of the Cauchy problem for a general class of symmetrizable hyperbolic partially diffusive systems (also called hyperbolic-parabolic systems) in the whole space ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 . We address the question of well-posedness for large data having critical Besov regularity in the spirit of previous works by the second author on the compressible Navier-Stokes equations. Compared to the pioneering of Kawashima in [ 13 ] and to the more recent work by Serre in [ 17 ] , we take advantage of the partial parabolicity of the system to consider data in functional spaces that need not be embedded in the set of Lipschitz functions. This is in sharp contrast with the classical well-posedness theory of (multi-dimensional) hyperbolic systems where it is mandatory. A leitmotiv of our analysis is to require less regularity for the components experiencing a direct diffusion, than for the hyperbolic components. We then use an energy method that is performed on the system after spectral localization and a suitable Gårding inequality. As an example, we consider the Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations.

Many physical phenomena are modelled by first order hyperbolic equations with degenerate dissipative or diffusive terms. This is the case for example in gas dynamics, where the mass is conserved during the evolution, but the momentum balance includes a diffusion (viscosity) or friction (relaxation) term. In this paper we consider systems of the form

(0.1)

\partial_{t}u+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\partial_{\alpha}L^{\alpha}(u)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\partial_{\alpha}(B^{\alpha\beta}(u)\partial_{\beta}u)+f(u,\nabla u),

in which
u:(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{U}
is the unknown. The phase space
\mathbb{U}
is an open convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The nonlinearities are encoded in the smooth functions

L^{\alpha}:\mathbb{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad B^{\alpha\beta}:\mathbb{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{and}\quad f:\mathbb{U}\times\mathcal{M}_{n\times d}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Among the systems having the form (
0.1
) are the Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations, the magneto-hydrodynamics equations and electromagnetism equations
[
13
, chap. 6]
, the supercritical fluid models with chemical
reactions
[
11
]
, the Baer-Nunziato system
[
5
]
, etc. In each case, diffusion (e.g. thermal conduction or viscosity)
acts on some components of the unknown, while other components remain unaffected.

It is well known since the works by A. Majda in
[
15
]
and D. Serre in
[
16
]
(see also
[
4
, chapter 10]
) that general systems of conservation laws (that is (
0.1
) with
B\equiv 0
) which are
Friedrichs-symmetrizable

supplemented with smooth decaying data admit local-in-time strong solutions, that
may develop singularities (shock waves) in finite time even if the initial data are small perturbations of a constant solution.

The picture changes drastically if the system under consideration possesses diffusive terms.
In his seminal work
[
13
]
on partially diffusive hyperbolic systems, S. Kawashima
proved the local existence for a class of systems of type (
0.1
) supplemented with initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s>d/2+2,
and exhibited a sufficient condition for global well-posedness
for small data belonging to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s>d/2+3.

This condition is now known as the
Kawashima-Shizuta condition
.
It will be discussed in a forthcoming paper
[
1
]
, the present work being
dedicated to the local well-posedness theory for, possibly, large data.

Later, D. Serre in
[
18
]
made the link between the requirements made by
S. Kawashima, the notion of entropy-dissipativity (see Definition
1.2
below) and the constancy of the range of the symbol
B(\xi;u)
, see assumption
A
below. In
[
17
]
, he provided the normal form for (
0.1
) close to that used by S. Kawashima and Y. Shizuta in
[
14
]
. This enabled him
to enlarge the class of initial data for local well-posedness to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s>1+d/2
(see also the recent paper
[
2
]
by F. Angeles).

In the theory of multi-dimensional purely symmetric (or symmetrizable) hyperbolic systems,
two barriers seem insurmountable: going below Lipschitz regularity for the initial data
(which, in the Sobolev spaces setting
H^{s},
corresponds to
s>1+d/2
), and beyond an
L^{2}
-type functional framework.
In this paper, we strive for well-posedness results for partially diffusive systems,
in a Sobolev setting (in fact, in the optimal Besov setting)
that does not require embedding in
C^{0,1}.

In this endeavour, we shall keep in mind the paper
[
7
]
by the second author
dedicated to the compressible Navier-Stokes system – a model hyperbolic system with partial diffusion, where
one component (the density) is taken in the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

while the other components (the velocity) belong to
\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Compared to the classical theory presented above, this particular example reveals that, in some cases, it is possible
to reduce by one derivative the regularity of the non-dissipated component, and by
two
derivatives
that of the dissipated component (namely the velocity).
A fundamental observation is that to get optimal results in terms of regularity, one has to work with
one less derivative for the component that experiences direct diffusion.

The present work aims at extending the example of the compressible Navier-Stokes system,
to the much more general class of systems that has been considered by D. Serre in
[
17
,
18
]
.
We here concentrate on the well-posedness issue for (possibly) large data, and prepare the ground
for a forthcoming paper
[
1
]
dedicated to the global existence issue, large time asymptotics and diffusion limit for small data.

|V^{1}(t,x)-V^{1}_{0}(x)|\leq d_{1}\quad\text{for any}\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(\mathbb{C}4)

\left\|{V_{S}}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}+\left\|{V_{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}_{2,1})}\leq\eta
,

(\mathbb{C}5)

\mathfrak{A}^{L}(T)\leq\eta^{2}.

Note that Property
(\mathbb{C}3)
readily ensures that
U^{1}(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}^{1}
for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

so that we will be able to use the estimates of the previous subsections.

Before starting the proof, we fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that

(3.25)

\sum_{j\geq m}2^{j\frac{d}{2}}\|V^{1}_{0,j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\eta},

and
T
small enough so that

(3.26)

T\leq{\rm sup}\,\Bigl\{h>0,\>C_{0}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\biggl(1-e^{-c2^{2j}h}\Bigr)2^{j(\frac{d}{2}-1)}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}V_{0}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta^{2}\biggr\},

where
C_{0}
is the constant in Inequality (
B.4
) (adapted to the homogeneous setting).
Note that this readily ensures
(\mathbb{C}5).

Substep 1. Proving
(\mathbb{C}1)

Taking advantage of Inequality (
3.4
) with
m=+\infty,
then using
Properties
(\mathbb{C}1)
to
(\mathbb{C}5)
and the definition of
M_{1}
in (
3.24
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|{V^{1}}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{V_{0}^{1}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}}+C\bigl(\left\|{V_{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}_{2,1})}+\left\|{V_{L}^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}_{2,1})}\bigr)\bigl(1+\left\|{V^{1}}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1})}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C\eta-\frac{1}{2}+\biggl(\frac{1}{2}+C\eta\biggr)M_{1}.

Hence
(\mathbb{C}1)
holds true with strict inequality provided
\eta
has been chosen so that
C\eta<1/2.

Substep 2. Proving
(\mathbb{C}2)

From Inequality (
3.4
) with
m
given by (
3.25
), we gather

\displaystyle\left\|{V^{1}-V^{1}_{m}}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1})}

\displaystyle\!\leq\sum_{j\geq m}\!2^{j\frac{d}{2}}\|V_{j}^{1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq m}\!2^{j\frac{d}{2}}\|V_{0,j}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\bigl(\left\|{V_{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}\!+\!1}_{2,1})}\!+\!\left\|{V_{L}^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}\!+\!1}_{2,1})}\bigr)\bigl(1\!+\!\left\|{V^{1}}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1})}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\eta}/2+C\eta(1+M_{1}).

Hence we have
(\mathbb{C}2)
with strict inequality if
\eta
has been chosen so that
C\sqrt{\eta}(1+M_{1})<1/2.

Susbstep 3. Proving
(\mathbb{C}3)

We use the fact that:

V^{1}-V^{1}_{0}=\dot{S}_{m+1}(V^{1}-V^{1}_{0})+({\rm Id}-\dot{S}_{m+1})(V^{1}-V^{1}_{0}).

Then, the embedding
\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty},
the fact that
\dot{\Delta}_{j}({\rm Id}-\dot{S}_{m+1})=0
if
j<m

and Inequality (
3.10
) ensure that
for all
t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle\|V^{1}(t)-V^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\biggl(\int^{T}_{0}\left\|{\dot{S}_{m+1}\partial_{t}V^{1}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}}+\sum_{j\geq m}2^{j\frac{d}{2}}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(V^{1}(t)-V^{1}_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq C\biggl(2^{m}\left\|{\partial_{t}V^{1}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}+\left\|{V^{1}-V^{1}_{m}}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1})}+\sum_{j\geq m}2^{j\frac{d}{2}}\|V_{0,j}\|_{L^{2}}\biggr)\cdotp

The function
\partial_{t}V^{1}
may be bounded from (
3.13
) and
(\mathbb{C}3)-(\mathbb{C}4)
as follows:

(3.27)

\left\|{\partial_{t}V^{1}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})}\leq CM_{1}\sqrt{T}\Bigl(\sqrt{T}+\eta\bigl(1+\|V_{0}^{2}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}\bigr)\Bigr)\cdotp

Remembering (
3.25
) and
(\mathbb{C}2),
we thus get

\|V^{1}(t)-V^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq CM_{1}2^{m}\sqrt{T}\Bigl(\sqrt{T}+\eta\bigl(1+\|V_{0}^{2}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}\bigr)\Bigr)+C\sqrt{\eta}.

Hence
(\mathbb{C}3)
is satisfied for sufficiently small
T
if
\eta
has been chosen so that
C\sqrt{\eta}\leq d_{1}/2.

Susbstep 4. Proving
(\mathbb{C}4)

Owing to (
3.27
), if
T
has been chosen small enough then for any
\eta\in(0,1),

the negative part of the prefactor of
\partial_{t}V^{1}
in (
3.12
) may be omitted, and we get
(up to a change of
C
):

\mathfrak{A}^{S}(T)\leq CM_{1}^{3}\Bigl(\bigl(1+\|V_{0}^{2}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}\bigr)\eta^{2}+\bigl(2^{m}\sqrt{T}+\sqrt{\eta}\bigr)\mathfrak{A}^{S}(T)+\eta^{3/2}+T\Bigr)\cdotp

If
\eta
and
T
are such that
C(2^{m}\sqrt{T}+\sqrt{\eta})\leq 1/2,
this gives

\mathfrak{A}^{S}(T)\leq 2CM_{1}^{3}\Bigl(\bigl(1+\|V_{0}^{2}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}\bigr)\eta^{2}+\eta^{3/2}+T\Bigr)\cdotp

From it, we get
(\mathbb{C}4)
with a strict inequality if, say,

2CM_{1}^{3}\Bigl(\bigl(1+\|V_{0}^{2}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}\bigr)\eta+\sqrt{\eta}\Bigr)<1/2\quad\!\hbox{and}\!\quad 2CM_{1}^{3}T<1/2.

Susbstep 5. Bootstrap

Since all the quantities coming into play in
(\mathbb{C}1)-(\mathbb{C}5)
are continuous in time
and since the desired properties are true for
T=0,
we are guaranteed
that they are also true on a small enough time interval
[0,T_{0}].
In the previous
computations, we pointed out some
T>0

depending only on the initial data

such that if
(\mathbb{C}1)-(\mathbb{C}5)
are satisfied, then they actually hold
with strict inequality
.
The usual connectivity argument thus ensures that we do have
(\mathbb{C}1)-(\mathbb{C}5)
on
[0,T],

which completes the proof.

3.2.
The proof of existence

Let us smooth out the initial data
V_{0}
as follows:

V_{0,p}:=\sum_{|j|\leq p}\dot{\Delta}_{j}V_{0},\qquad p\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, we see that (
3.25
) can be ensured independently of
p,
and we have

(3.28)

V_{0,p}\to V_{0}\hbox{ in }\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}\quad\!\hbox{and}\!\quad\underset{p\in\mathbb{N}}{{\rm sup}\,}\|V_{0,p}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}\leq\|V_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}}.

The fact that
\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
guarantees that (
3.2
)
is satisfied for large enough
p.
Now, since
V_{0,p}
belongs to all spaces
B^{s}_{2,1},

applying Theorem
1.2
gives us a sequence of smooth local solutions
(V_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}

on some maximal time interval
[0,T_{p}).

Since the solutions are smooth, the computations that have been performed in the previous section hold true;
keeping in mind our definition of smoothed out data and (
3.28
),
properties
({\mathbb{C}}_{1})
to
({\mathbb{C}}_{5})
are satisfied on
[0,T_{p}^{*}[
with
T^{*}_{p}:=\min(T_{p},T)
and
T
given therein.
The important point is that these conditions (and embedding) ensure
that
\nabla V^{1}_{p}\in L^{2}(0,T_{p}^{*};L^{\infty})
and
\nabla V^{2}_{p}\in L^{1}(0,T_{p}^{*};L^{\infty}).

Consequently, applying the continuation criterion pointed out in Remark
1.4
ensures that
T_{p}>T.

As a conclusion, we proved that the lifespan of each term of the sequence is greater than
T,

and that
(V_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in the space
\mathcal{E}_{T}.

The rest of the proof is standard. The boundedness in
\mathcal{E}_{T}
guarantees that

V_{p}
converges weakly to some limit
V
that belongs to

L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}\times B^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}).

Then, the boundedness of the time derivatives combined with a Lions-Aubin type argument and, finally, interpolation
gives some strong convergence (locally in space and time) that is enough to pass to the limit
in (
1.4
).
To recover the time continuity with values in
B^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}\times B^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}
and the
L^{1}_{T}

properties coming into play in the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{T},
one may argue as for the compressible
Navier-Stokes equations (see e.g.
[
3
, Chap.10]
).

3.3.
The proof of uniqueness

To simplify the presentation, we here assume that
S^{0}={\rm Id}.

Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be two solutions of (
1.4
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by Theorem
1.6

and corresponding to the same initial data. Let
\delta\!V\overset{\rm def}{=}V_{2}-V_{1}.

The proof of uniqueness consists in obtaining suitable a priori estimates for the following system satisfied by
\delta\!V
:

(3.29)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\delta\!V^{1}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}A^{\alpha}_{11}(U^{2}_{2})\delta\!V^{1}=h,\\[4.30554pt]
\partial_{t}\delta\!V^{2}-\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}Z^{\alpha\beta}(U^{1}_{2,m})\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\delta\!V^{2}=g\overset{\rm def}{=}\sum_{k=1}^{7}g_{k},\end{cases}

with

\displaystyle h=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\left(A^{\alpha}_{11}(U^{2}_{2})-A^{\alpha}_{11}(U^{2}_{1})\right)\partial_{\alpha}V^{1}_{1}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}A^{\alpha}_{12}(U_{2}^{1})\partial_{\alpha}\delta\!V^{2}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\left(A^{\alpha}_{12}(U^{1}_{2})-A^{\alpha}_{12}(U^{1}_{1})\right)\partial_{\alpha}V^{2}_{1},

\displaystyle g_{1}=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\left(S^{\alpha}_{22}(U_{2})-S^{\alpha}_{22}(U_{1})\right)\partial_{\alpha}V^{2}_{1},\quad g_{2}=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}S^{\alpha}_{22}(U_{2})\partial_{\alpha}\delta\!V^{2}

\displaystyle g_{3}=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}S^{\alpha}_{21}(U_{2})\partial_{\alpha}\delta\!V^{1},\quad g_{4}=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\left(S^{\alpha}_{21}(U_{2})-S^{\alpha}_{21}(U_{1})\right)\partial_{\alpha}V^{1}_{1},

\displaystyle g_{5}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\partial_{\alpha}\left(Z^{\alpha\beta}(U^{1}_{2,m})\right)\partial_{\beta}\delta\!V^{2},\quad g_{6}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\partial_{\alpha}\left((Z^{\alpha\beta}(U^{1}_{2})-Z^{\alpha\beta}(U^{1}_{2,m}))\partial_{\beta}\delta\!V^{2}\right),

\displaystyle g_{7}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\partial_{\alpha}\left((Z^{\alpha\beta}(U^{1}_{2})-Z^{\alpha\beta}(U^{1}_{1}))\partial_{\beta}V^{2}_{1}\right).

Like in Section
2
, uniqueness has to be proved in a space with one less derivative, namely

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1})\times\biggl(\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-2}_{2,1})\cap L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}_{2,1})\biggr)\cdotp

This would indeed work in dimension
d\geq 3.
In dimension
d=2
however,
this would lead us to estimating the right-hand side of (
3.29
)
2
in
L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{-1}_{2,1}).

Terms like
g_{1}
or
g_{4}
are not tractable in this low regularity framework since, typically,
the product of functions maps
\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
in
the larger space
\dot{B}^{-1}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
rather than in
\dot{B}^{-1}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Bounding
\delta\!V^{2}
in
{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{-1}_{2,\infty})\cap L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{1}_{2,\infty})
is not good either since the fact that
\dot{B}^{1}_{2,\infty}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\hookrightarrow}}}L^{\infty}

causes
some problem when estimating
h.

Following
[
9
]
, we shall bypass this difficulty leveraging the following logarithmic interpolation inequality:

(3.30)

\left\|{a}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1})}\leq C\left\|{a}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,\infty})}{\rm log}\,\left(e+\frac{\left\|{a}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,\infty})}+\left\|{a}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}_{2,\infty})}}{\left\|{a}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,\infty})}}\right)\cdotp

Consequently, in what follows, we shall estimate
(\delta\!V^{1},\delta\!V^{2})
in

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{T}\overset{\rm def}{=}{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,\infty})\times{L}^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-2}_{2,\infty})\cap\widetilde{L}^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,\infty}).

Although
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
need not be in
{\mathcal{F}}_{T},
their difference is,
as a consequence of the following computations (see e.g.
[
9
]
for more explanations).
Now, apply operator
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
to (
3.29
)
1

\partial_{t}\delta\!V^{1}_{j}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}A^{\alpha}_{11}(U^{2}_{2})\delta\!V^{1}_{j}=\dot{\Delta}_{j}h+\widetilde{R}_{j}\ \text{ with }\ \widetilde{R}_{j}=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}[\dot{\Delta}_{j},A^{\alpha}_{11}(U^{2}_{2})]\partial_{\alpha}\delta\!V^{1}.

As

\displaystyle\|\delta\!V^{1}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2})}\leq C\|\delta\!V^{1}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2})}\|\nabla V^{2}_{2}\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{\infty})}+\|(\widetilde{R}_{j},\dot{\Delta}_{j}h)\|_{L^{1}_{T}(L^{2})}.

Bounding

A.2

A.3

\displaystyle 2^{j(\frac{d}{2}-1)}\|\widetilde{R}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2})}\leq C\left\|{\delta\!V^{1}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,\infty})}\|\nabla V^{2}_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,\infty})\cap L^{1}_{T}(L^{\infty})},

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+(1+\left\|{V^{1}_{2}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}})\left\|{\nabla\delta\!V^{2}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,\infty}}+\left\|{\delta\!V^{1}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,\infty}}\left\|{\nabla V^{2}_{1}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}}\biggr)\cdotp

Finally using the embedding

+C\int^{T}_{0}\biggl(1+\left\|{V^{1}_{1}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}}+\left\|{V^{1}_{2}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}}\biggr)\left\|{\delta\!V^{2}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}}.

4.
Application to the compressible Navier-Stokes system

We here consider the full Navier-Stokes system
governing the evolution of a
Newtonian
compressible fluid in
\mathbb{R}^{d},
with no external force.
Denoting by
u=u(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
its velocity field,
\rho=\rho(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+},
its density,

p=p(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}
, its pressure,
\theta=\theta(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+},
its absolute temperature and

e=e(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}
, its internal energy by unit mass, this system reads:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\rho+\mbox{div}\,(\rho u)=0,\\
&\rho\partial_{t}u+\rho u\cdot\nabla u-\mbox{div}\,(2\mu D(u)+\lambda\mbox{div}\,u\,I_{d})+\nabla p=0,\\
&\rho e_{\theta}(\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta)+\theta p_{\theta}\mbox{div}\,u-\mbox{div}\,(k\nabla\theta)={\mathbb{T}},\end{cases}

with

(4.2)

D(u)\overset{\rm def}{=}\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+{}^{t}\nabla u)\quad\!\hbox{and}\!\quad{\mathbb{T}}\overset{\rm def}{=}\frac{\mu}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial_{j}u^{i}+\partial_{i}u^{j})^{2}+\lambda(\mbox{div}\,u)^{2}.

To close the system, we make the following
Assumption D
:

•

The thermodynamic quantities
p
and
e
are smooth functions of
\rho,\theta>0

such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle p_{\rho}\overset{\rm def}{=}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\rho}>0\quad\text{and}\quad e_{\theta}\overset{\rm def}{=}\frac{\partial e}{\partial\theta}>0.

•

The viscosity coefficients
\lambda,\mu
and the heat conductivity
k
are smooth functions of
\rho,\theta>0

that satisfy:

(4.4)

\mu>0,\quad\nu\overset{\rm def}{=}2\mu+\lambda>0\quad\text{and}\quad k>0.

Denoting
U\overset{\rm def}{=}(\rho,u,\theta),
System (
4.1
) may be rewritten:

(4.5)

\displaystyle S^{0}(U)\frac{d}{dt}U+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}S^{\alpha}(U)\partial_{\alpha}U-\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\partial_{\alpha}\left(Y^{\alpha\beta}(U)\partial_{\beta}U\right)=f(U)

where the matrices
S^{\alpha}(U)
and
Y^{\alpha\beta}(U),
and the function
f
are defined on the phase space

\mathcal{U}\overset{\rm def}{=}\bigl\{(\rho,u,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+2}/\rho>0,\;\theta>0\bigr\}
by

\displaystyle S^{0}(U)\overset{\rm def}{=}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{p_{\rho}}{\rho}&0&0\\
0&\rho I_{d}&0\\
0&0&\frac{\rho e_{\theta}}{\theta}\end{pmatrix},\;\;\;f(U)\overset{\rm def}{=}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
{\mathbb{T}}-k\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla(\frac{1}{\theta})\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}S^{\alpha}(U)\xi_{\alpha}\overset{\rm def}{=}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{p_{\rho}}{\rho}u\cdot\xi&p_{\rho}\xi&0\\
p_{\rho}^{T}\xi&\rho(u\cdot\xi)I_{d}&p_{\theta}^{T}\xi\\
0&p_{\theta}\xi&\frac{\rho e_{\theta}}{\theta}u\cdot\xi\end{pmatrix},

and

\displaystyle Y^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\overset{\rm def}{=}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&Z^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\end{pmatrix}\quad\!\hbox{with}\!\quad Z^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\overset{\rm def}{=}\begin{pmatrix}\mu|\xi|^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)\xi\otimes\xi{\rm Id}&0\\
0&\frac{k}{\theta}|\xi|^{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdotp

The matrix
S^{0}(U)
is diagonal positive for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
, and the matrices
S^{\alpha}(U)
are real symmetric. Furthermore, a simple calculation reveals that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\left\langle Z^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}A,A\right\rangle\geq\biggl(\min(\mu,\nu)|X|^{2}+\frac{k}{\theta}Y^{2}\biggr)|\xi|^{2},\quad X,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ Y\in\mathbb{R},

where
A\overset{\rm def}{=}(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
denotes the canonic scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. The right-hand side of (
4.5
) is a lower order quadratic term, that satisfies the 4-th condition of Assumption
B
.
As a direct application of Theorem
1.2

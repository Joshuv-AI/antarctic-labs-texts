ℓ p \ell^{p} -Stability of Weighted Persistence Diagrams
Aziz Burak Gülen, Facundo Mémoli, Amit Patel

Abstract
We introduce the concept of weighted persistence diagrams and develop a functorial pipeline for constructing them from finite metric measure spaces. This builds upon an existing functorial framework for generating classical persistence diagrams from finite pseudo-metric spaces. To quantify differences between weighted persistence diagrams, we define the p p -edit distance for p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] p\in[1,\infty] , and—focusing on the weighted Vietoris–Rips filtration—we establish that these diagrams are stable with respect to the p p -Gromov–Wasserstein distance as a direct consequence of functoriality. In addition, we present an Optimal Transport-inspired formulation of the p p -edit distance, enhancing its conceptual clarity. Finally, we explore the discriminative power of weighted persistence diagrams, demonstrating advantages over their unweighted counterparts.

Introduction

Persistent homology is one of the central subjects in Topological Data Analysis (TDA). When analyzing point cloud data, i.e., a finite metric space, a topological summary of the data can be obtained via persistent diagrams
[
ELZ00
,
ZC05
,
BBI01
]
of a filtration associated to the dataset. When analyzing finite metric spaces, the initial step involves constructing a filtration from the metric space. Two common approaches for achieving this are the Vietoris-Rips filtration and the Čech filtration.

A fundamental result in persistent homology is that persistent diagrams are stable with respect to the perturbations of input filtration / metric space. Classical stability results for persistence diagrams typically rely on the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between input metric spaces and the bottleneck distance between output persistence diagrams
[
CSEH07
,
CCG+09
]
. In this work, we study the
p
-stability of weighted persistence diagrams—that is, classical persistence diagrams equipped with probability measures. To this end, we replace the input collection of metric spaces with the collection of metric measure spaces (or mm-spaces), which are metric spaces endowed with fully supported probability measures. On the output side, we extend the classical notions of filtrations and persistence diagrams to their weighted counterparts.

Metric measure spaces are triples
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
that combine geometric structure (a metric
d_{X}
on
X
) with probabilistic structure (a probability measure
\mu_{X}
on
X
), providing a powerful framework for comparing and analyzing complex data. They play a central role in shape analysis, topological data analysis (TDA)
[
CCG+09
]
, and optimal transport
[
Mém07
,
Mé11
,
Stu23
]
. Applications include comparing point clouds or graphs via the
Gromov–Wasserstein distance

[
Mé11
]
, modeling variability in anatomical shapes, and studying empirical distributions in geometric inference. The mm-space perspective enables coordinate-free, geometry-aware comparisons in high-dimensional and structured data settings. One of the concepts pertaining to mm-spaces is the notion of
distance distribution

[
OFCD02
,
Mé11
,
MN22
]
of a mm-space
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
which is the probability measure on the real line arising as the pushforward of
\mu_{X}\otimes\mu_{X}
via
d_{X}
. Besides being stable under the Gromov-Wasserstein distance, their direct comparison yields fast methods for object matching.

Motivated by the flexibility inherent to the mm-space representation of data, we introduce suitable notions of weighted filtrations and weighted persistence diagrams. Our central approach is to construct a functorial pipeline that maps mm-spaces to weighted persistence diagrams, and to analyze this construction using (different instantiations of) the notion of edit distance. When appropriately designed, such functorial pipelines yield stability results with respect to edit distances, as shown in
[
MP22
,
GMW23
]
. We adopt this framework to establish the
p
-stability of weighted persistence diagrams. To capture variability in both geometric and probabilistic aspects, we introduce weights on filtrations and persistence diagrams, allowing us to define
p
-edit distances between them. Our main stability result expresses the
p
-edit distance between weighted persistence diagrams in terms of the
p
-Gromov–Wasserstein distance between the input mm-spaces. To further enhance the interpretability of this framework, we show that the
p
-edit distance admits an Optimal Transport-like formulation, shedding light on its underlying structure and discriminative behavior.

1.1
Previous Work

In
[
MP22
]
, the authors introduce a functorial pipeline

\mathsf{Fil}(K)\to\mathsf{Dgm}

for producing persistence diagrams from filtrations of a fixed finite simplicial complex
K
. They organize the collection of filtrations into a category
\mathsf{Fil}(K)
and the collection of persistence diagrams into a category
\mathsf{Dgm}
. Within this framework, the degree-
d
persistence diagram of a filtration is obtained via Möbius inversion—a combinatorial technique for compressing and summarizing mathematical data. Specifically, Möbius inversion is applied to the degree-
d
birth-death function associated with the filtration. The functoriality of the pipeline is a consequence of Rota’s Galois Connection Theorem (RGCT)
[
Rot64
,
Gre81
,
MP22
,
GM22
]
, which links the morphisms in
\mathsf{Fil}(K)
and
\mathsf{Dgm}
—both defined in terms of Galois connections—to the application of Möbius inversion. RGCT thus ensures that the assignment of persistence diagrams is functorial.

Each morphism in the categories
\mathsf{Fil}(K)
and
\mathsf{Dgm}
is assigned a non-negative cost. These cost assignments induce metrics—referred to as edit distances—on the objects of
\mathsf{Fil}(K)
and
\mathsf{Dgm}
, denoted
d_{\mathsf{Fil}(K)}^{E}
and
d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}
, respectively. Leveraging the functoriality of the pipeline, the authors establish the following stability result.

Theorem 1.1.1

(
[
MP22
, Theorem 8.4]
)
:

Let
\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{G}
be two filtrations in
\mathsf{Fil}(K)
and let
\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{F}}
and
\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{G}}
denote their degree-
d
persistence diagrams respectively. Then,

d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{F}},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{G}})\leq d_{\mathsf{Fil}(K)}^{E}(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G}).

It is important to emphasize that this stability result does not concern metric spaces as inputs; rather, it addresses filtrations as the primary objects of study. Specifically, it establishes the stability of persistence diagrams with respect to perturbations of filtrations in terms of edit distance.

We also note that related
p
-stability results for (unweighted) persistence diagrams have been established in
[
CSEHM10
,
ST20
]
. Like the result above, these too do not take metric (or metric measure) spaces as input. Instead, they consider functions and the filtrations derived from their sublevel sets.

First, we extend the functorial pipeline of

]
to

\mathsf{CatMet}\to\mathsf{Fil}\to\mathsf{Dgm},

3.1.5

:

Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be two finite mm-spaces. Let
w\mathsf{\textnormal{-}VR}(X)
and
w\mathsf{\textnormal{-}VR}(Y)
denote their weighted Vietoris-Rips filtrations and let
w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}}(X)
and
w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}}(Y)
denote the weighted degree-
d

w\mathsf{\textnormal{-}VR}(X)
and
w\mathsf{\textnormal{-}VR}(Y)
. Then,

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}}(X),w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}}(Y))

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 4^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot d_{\mathsf{CatMM}}^{E,p}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X})),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y}))

\displaystyle=4^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{p}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X})),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y}))

The inequality above follows from the functoriality of the pipeline; see
Proposition

4.2.7
and
Proposition

5.2.9
. The
p
-Gromov–Wasserstein distance, denoted
d_{\mathrm{GW_{p}}}
, quantifies how far two mm-spaces are from being isomorphic. The identity
d_{\mathsf{CatMM}}^{E,p}=2\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{p}}}
(see
Proposition

3.2.3
) arises—analogously to the case of

\mathsf{CatMM}

Although (
p
-)edit distance stability follows directly from functoriality, the precise meaning of the (
p
-)edit distance could remain unclear at first glance. In
Section

6
, we demonstrate that the (
p
-)edit distance between (weighted) persistence diagrams can be reformulated independently of category theory or the formal definition of edit distance. These alternative formulations—given in
Proposition

6.1.4
and
Proposition

6.2.3
—provide an Optimal Transport-like interpretation of the (

-)edit distance, thereby enhancing its conceptual clarity and interpretability.

Persistence diagrams are widely used in practice for their ability to compactly encode geometric information, particularly in applications involving shape characterization. In
Section

7
, we investigate the discriminative power of
weighted persistence diagrams
, which, in the case of Vietoris-Rips filtrations of finite metric spaces, combine two sources of information: classical persistence diagrams and the global distribution of distances. We show that this synthesis enhances discriminative strength beyond what either component provides on its own. Notably, the
p
-edit distance is most discriminative when
p=\infty
. For finite values of
p
, the distance becomes insensitive to the persistence diagram component and depends only on the weight distribution (
Proposition

7.2.1
). Nevertheless, it can still distinguish between weighted persistence diagrams even when the weights, viewed as mm-spaces, are isomorphic—thanks to its sensitivity to the ordering of intervals (see
Example

7.2.3
).

2.1
Posets and Galois Connections

A
partially ordered set
(poset) is a set
X
with a
reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive relation
\leq
.
A
Galois connection
between two posets
P
and
Q
is a pair,
(f,g)
, of order-preserving maps
f:P\to Q
and
g:Q\to P
such that

f(p)\leq q\iff p\leq g(q)

for every
p\in P
and
q\in Q
. We refer to
f
as the
left adjoint
and refer to
g
as the
right adjoint
. We will also use the notation
f:P\leftrightarrows Q:g
to denote a Galois connection.

We say that an order-preserving map
f:P\to Q
has a right adjoint (or, admits a right adjoint) if there is an order-preserving map
g:Q\to P
such that the pair
(f,g)
is a Galois connection. Note that, if an order-preserving map
f:P\to Q
has a right adjoint
g
, then, it must be unique and given by

g(q):=\max\{p\in P\mid f(p)\leq q\}.

Möbius Inversion.

Let
P
be a poset and
\mathcal{G}
be an abelian group. Let
m:P\to\mathcal{G}
be any function. The
Möbius inverse
of
m
is defined to be the unique function
\partial_{P}m:P\to\mathcal{G}
, if it exists, satisfying

m(p)=\sum_{p^{\prime}\leq p}\partial_{P}m(p^{\prime})

for all
p\in P
.

Definition 2.1.1

(Pushforward and pullback)
:

Let
f:P\to Q
be any map between two sets, and let
m:P\to\mathcal{G}
be any function. The
pushforward
of
m
along
f
is the function
f_{\sharp}m:Q\to\mathcal{G}
given by

f_{\sharp}m(q):=\sum_{p\in f^{-1}(q)}m(p).

Let
h:Q\to\mathcal{G}
be any function. The
pullback
of
h
along
f
is the function
f^{\sharp}h:P\to\mathcal{G}
given by

(f^{\sharp}h)(p):=h(f(p)).

(Rota’s Galois Connection Theorem (RGCT)
[
GM22
, Theorem 3.1]
)
:

Let
P
and
Q
be finite posets and
f:P\leftrightarrows Q:g
be a Galois connection. Then,

(f)_{\sharp}\circ\partial_{P}=\partial_{Q}\circ(g)^{\sharp}.

Simplicial Complexes and Filtrations

An (abstract)
simplicial complex

K
over a finite vertex set
V
is a non-empty collection of non-empty subsets of
V
with the property that for every
\sigma\in K
, if
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, then
\tau\in K
. An element
\sigma\in K
is called a
d
-simplex
if the cardinality of
\sigma
is
d+1
. An
orientation
on a simplex is an ordering of its vertices up to an even permutation.

An
oriented simplex
, denoted
[\sigma]
, is a simplex
\sigma\in K
whose vertices are ordered. We always assume that ordering on simplicies is inherited from a pre-determined ordering on
V
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{d}^{K}
denote the set of all oriented
d
-simplicies of
K
.

The
d
-th chain group of
K
, denoted
C_{d}^{K}
, is the vector space over a fixed field
\Bbbk
with basis
\mathcal{S}_{d}^{K}
. Let
n_{d}^{K}:=|\mathcal{S}_{d}^{K}|=\dim_{\Bbbk}(C_{d}^{K})
.

The
d
-th boundary map

\partial_{d}^{K}:C_{d}^{K}\to C_{d-1}^{K}
is defined by

\partial_{d}^{K}([v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}]):=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}[v_{0},\ldots,\hat{v}_{i},\ldots,v_{d}]

for every oriented
d
-simplex
\sigma=[v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}]\in\mathcal{S}_{d}^{K}
, where
[v_{0},\ldots,\hat{v}_{i},\ldots,v_{d}]
denotes the omission of the
i
-th vertex, and extended linearly to
C_{d}^{K}
. We denote by
\mathsf{Z}_{d}(K)
the space of
d
-cycles of
K
, that is
\mathsf{Z}_{d}(K):=\ker(\partial_{d}^{K})
, and we denote by
\mathsf{B}_{d}(K)
the space of
d
-boundaries of
K
, that is
\mathsf{B}_{d}(K):=\ima(\partial_{d+1}^{K})
. Additionally, we denote by
H_{d}(K)
the
d
-th homology group of
K
, that is
H_{d}(K):=\mathsf{Z}_{d}(K)/\mathsf{B}_{d}(K)
.

For a finite simplicial complex
K
, let
\mathsf{SubCx}(K)
denote the poset of subcomplexes of
K
, ordered by inclusion. A
simplicial filtration of
K
is an order-preserving map
\mathsf{F}:P\to\mathsf{SubCx}(K)
, where
P
is a finite poset with a maximum element
\top_{P}
such that
\mathsf{F}(\top_{P})=K
. A
1
-parameter
filtration is filtration over a finite linearly ordered set
P=\{0=p_{0}<p_{1}<\cdots<p_{n}\}\subseteq[0,\infty)
. When
\mathsf{F}:\{0=p_{0}<p_{1}<\cdots<p_{n}\}\to\mathsf{SubCx}(K)
is a
1
-paramater filtration, we use the notation
K_{i}
for the simplicial complex
\mathsf{F}(p_{i})
and write
\mathsf{F}=\{K_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
to denote the simplicial filtration. Note that
K_{n}=K
.

2.3
Edit Distance

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category and assume that for every object
A
and
B
in
\mathcal{C}
and for every morphism
f:A\to B
from
A
to
B
, there is a non-negative cost, denoted
\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(f)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, associated to the morphism. For two objects
A
and
B
in this category, a
path
,
\mathcal{P}
, is a finite sequence of morphisms

\mathcal{P}:\;\;A\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$f_{1}$}}D_{1}\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$f_{2}$}}\cdots\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$f_{k-1}$}}D_{k-1}\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$f_{k}$}}B

where
D_{i}
s are objects in
\mathcal{C}
and
\leftrightarrow
indicates a morphism in either direction. The
cost of a path

\mathcal{P}
, denoted
\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{P})
, is the sum of the cost of all morphisms in the path:

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{P}):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(f_{i}).

Definition 2.3.1

(Edit distance)
:

The
edit distance

d_{\mathcal{C}}^{E}(A,B)
between two objects
A
and
B
in
\mathcal{C}
is the infimum, over all paths between
A
and
B
, of the cost of such paths:

d_{\mathcal{C}}^{E}(A,B):=\inf_{\mathcal{P}}\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{P}).

The following proposition will serve as the main tool for establishing stability results in this paper.

Proposition 2.3.2

(Functorial edit distance stability)
:

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be two categories such that for every morphism in these categories
f
in
\mathcal{C}
and
g
in
\mathcal{D}
, there are costs
\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(f),\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{D}}(g)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
associated to them. Let
\mathsf{F}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a functor and
M\in(0,\infty)
be such that for every morphism
f
in
\mathcal{C}
, it holds that

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathsf{F}(f))\leq M\cdot\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(f)

Then, for any two object
A
and
B
in
\mathcal{C}
, we have

d_{\mathcal{D}}^{E}(\mathsf{F}(A),\mathsf{F}(B))\leq M\cdot d_{\mathcal{C}}^{E}(A,B).

Proof.

Let
A
and
B
be two objects in
\mathcal{C}
and let

\mathcal{P}:\;\;A\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$f_{1}$}}D_{1}\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$f_{2}$}}\cdots\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$f_{k-1}$}}D_{k-1}\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$f_{k}$}}B

be a path between
A
and
B
in
\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathsf{F}
is functor, we obtain the following path

\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{P}):\;\;\mathsf{F}(A)\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$\mathsf{F}(f_{1})$}}\mathsf{F}(D_{1})\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$\mathsf{F}(f_{2})$}}\cdots\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$\mathsf{F}(f_{k-1})$}}\mathsf{F}(D_{k-1})\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$\mathsf{F}(f_{k})$}}\mathsf{F}(B)

between
\mathsf{F}(A)
and
\mathsf{F}(B)
. And, we have that
\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathsf{F}(f_{i}))\leq M\cdot\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(f_{i})
for every
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. Therefore, we have that

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{P}))\leq M\cdot\mathsf{cost}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{P}).

Hence, we obtain

d_{\mathcal{D}}^{E}(\mathsf{F}(A),\mathsf{F}(B))\leq M\cdot d_{\mathcal{C}}^{E}(A,B).

∎

2.4
Metric Spaces

A
pseudo-metric space
is a pair
(X,d_{X})
, where
X
is a non-empty set
and
d_{X}:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
a function
such that, for all
a,b,c\in X
, the following conditions hold:

•

d_{X}(a,b)=d_{X}(b,a)

•

d_{X}(a,c)\leq d_{X}(a,b)+d_{X}(b,c)
.

Note that these conditions imply
d_{X}(a,b)\geq 0
, for all
a,b\in X
. A pseudo-metric space
(X,d_{X})
is called a metric space if
d_{X}(a,b)=0\iff a=b
.
A
1
-Lipschitz
map from a metric space
(X,d_{X})
to
a metric space
(Y,d_{Y})

is a function
f:X\to Y
such that for all
a,b\in X
,

d_{Y}\big(f(a),f(b)\big)\leq d_{X}(a,b)
.
Throughout this paper, unless explicitly mentioned otherwise, we operate under the assumption that (pseudo)-metric spaces are finite.

Definition 2.4.1

(Distortion)
:

The
distortion
of a set map
f:X\to Y
between two (pseudo)-metric spaces
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
is

\mathsf{dis}(f):=\max_{x,x^{\prime}\in X}|d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})-d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))|.

Definition 2.4.2

(Product)
:

The
product
of two (pseudo)-metric spaces
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
is the
(pseudo)-metric space
\left(X\times Y,d_{X\times Y}\right)
where

d_{X\times Y}\left((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right):=\max\big\{d_{X}(x,x^{\prime}),d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})\big\}.

Note that the two projections
\pi_{X}:(X\times Y,d_{X\times Y})\to(X,d_{X})
and

\pi_{Y}:(X\times Y,d_{X\times Y})\to(Y,d_{Y})
are
1
-Lipschitz.

Gromov-Hausdorff Distance.

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a (not necessarily finite) metric space and let
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
be two subsets of
X
. The
Hausdorff distance
between
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
is defined to be

d_{\mathrm{H}}^{X}(Z_{1},Z_{2}):=\max\left\{\sup_{z_{1}\in Z_{1}}d_{X}(z_{1},Z_{2}),\sup_{z_{2}\in Z_{2}}d_{X}(Z_{1},z_{2})\right\}.

For two metric spaces
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
the
Gromov-Hausdorff distance
,
[
Edw75
,
Gro07
]
, between them is defined as the infimum
r>0
for which there exists a metric space
(Z,d_{Z})
together with two isometric embeddings
\psi_{X}:(X,d_{X})\hookrightarrow(Z,d_{Z})
and
\psi_{Y}:(Y,d_{Y})\to(Z,d_{Z})
such that
d_{\mathrm{H}}^{Z}(\psi_{X}(X),\psi_{Y}(Y))<r
, that is,

d_{\mathrm{GH}}(X,Y):=\inf_{Z,\psi_{X},\psi_{Y}}d_{\mathrm{H}}^{Z}\left(\psi_{X}(X),\psi_{Y}(Y)\right).

A
tripod
between two sets
X
and

3
d_{\mathrm{GH}}
and

as Edit Distances

Edit distances quantify the dissimilarity between two objects by the minimal-cost sequence of edit operations needed to transform one into the other
[
Ski08
]
. For graphs
[
SF83
]
, such operations typically include the insertion, deletion, or relabeling of nodes and edges. This generalizes string edit distance to structured data and provides a flexible, intuitive framework for graph comparison. Variants of graph edit distance are widely used in pattern recognition, bioinformatics, and structural data analysis.

In this section, we define two categories: the category
\mathsf{CatMet}
of finite pseudo-metric spaces and the category
\mathsf{CatMM}
of finite metric measure (mm) spaces. For each category, we introduce a notion of cost for morphisms—standard cost in
\mathsf{CatMet}
and
p
-cost (for
p\in[1,\infty]
) in
\mathsf{CatMM}
. These notions enable us to define an edit distance between finite pseudo-metric spaces and a
p
-edit distance between finite mm-spaces. Our main result is that the Gromov-Hausdorff distance arises as an instance of edit distance, and the
p
-Gromov-Wasserstein distance as an instance of
p
-edit distance.

3.1

as an Edit Distance

Let

Definition 3.1.1

(Metric order-preserving functions)
Let

for every

(Pullback metric)

Let

be any function. The

\displaystyle\phi^{*}d_{X}:=d_{X}\circ(\phi,\phi):Z\times Z

\displaystyle(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto d_{X}(\phi(z_{1}),\phi(z_{2})).

Note that a function

Category of Finite Pseudo-Metric Spaces.

We now define the category of finite pseudo-metric spaces. Let
(X,d_{X})
and

be two finite pseudo-metric spaces. We say that a function

Definition 3.1.3

(Cost of a metric morphism)

Let

Remark 3.1.4
:

In our category of finite pseudo-metric spaces, morphisms are defined as specific surjections that echo the role of tripods in the realization of the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between bounded metric spaces given in

In this section, we define the category of finite mm-spaces and show that the

, which will serve as morphisms in the category of finite mm-spaces.

Definition 3.2.1

(Order-preserving mm-space function)
:

An

(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})

(i.e.,

is a metric morphism.

Let

denote the category whose objects are finite mm-spaces and whose morphisms are order-preserving mm-space functions.

\infty

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{CatMM}}^{\infty}(f):=\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(f)=\max_{x,x^{\prime}\in X}\left|d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})-d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\right|.

\mathsf{CatMM}

4
A Functorial Construction of (Weighted) Vietoris-Rips Filtration

In this section, we introduce the category of filtrations,

1.

is a metric specialization of

\displaystyle\to D(Z,d_{1})

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\mapsto\max f^{-1}([0,r])=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in Z\\

5
Functorial (Weighted) Persistence Diagrams

In this section, we introduce the category of persistent diagrams,
\mathsf{Dgm}
, and the category of weighted persistence diagrams.
\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}
. We show that the degree-
d
(weighted) persistence diagram assignment to a (weighted) filtration is a functor. As a result of this functoriality, we obtain a stability result.

5.1
(Unweighted) Persistence Diagrams

Persistence diagrams are non-negative integer-valued functions on the set of intervals of a finite linear poset
Q
. The content presented in this section largely revisits the outcomes and framework established in
[
MP22
]
. We now define the set of intervals of a poset.

Definition 5.1.1
:

Let
Q
be any poset. For
q_{1}\leq q_{2}\in Q
, we define the
interval

[q_{1},q_{2}]
to be the set
[q_{1},q_{2}]:=\{q\in Q\mid q_{1}\leq q\leq q_{2}\}
. Then, we define the set of all intervals as

\bar{Q}:=\{[q_{1},q_{2}]\mid q_{1}\leq q_{2}\in Q\}.

We denote by
\mathsf{diag}(\bar{Q})
the
diagonal
of
\bar{Q}
. That is,

\mathsf{diag}(\bar{Q}):=\{[q,q]\mid q\in Q\}

Let
\leq_{\times}
denote the product order on
Q\times Q
. Restricting
\leq_{\times}
to
\bar{Q}
, the set of intervals is endowed with a partial order. Namely, for
[q_{1},q_{2}],[q_{3},q_{4}]\in\bar{Q}
, we write
[q_{1},q_{2}]\leq_{\times}[q_{3},q_{4}]
if
q_{1}\leq q_{3}
and
q_{2}\leq q_{4}
.

Definition 5.1.2
:

Let
Q
be a poset and
d_{Q}
be a metric on
Q
. We define the metric
d_{\bar{Q}}
on
\bar{Q}
as

d_{\bar{Q}}([q_{1},q_{2}],[q_{3},q_{4}]):=\max(d_{Q}(q_{1},q_{3}),d_{Q}(q_{2},q_{4})).

For a subposet
Q\subseteq[0,\infty)
, and for two intervals
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
and
J=[q_{3},q_{4}]
in
\bar{Q}
, we define

I-J:=(q_{1}-q_{3},q_{2}-q_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

With this notation, we have

d_{\bar{Q}}(I,J)=||I-J||_{\infty},

where
||\cdot||_{\infty}
denotes the
\ell
-infinity norm.

Remark 5.1.3
:

A Galois connection
f:Q\leftrightarrows R:g
induces a Galois connection on the poset of intervals

\bar{f}:\bar{Q}\leftrightarrows\bar{R}\ :\bar{g},

where
\bar{f}
and
\bar{g}
are defined component-wisely.

Definition 5.1.4
:

Let
Q\subseteq[0,\infty)
be a finite subset containing
0
. A
persistence diagram
is a function (not necessarily order-preserving)
\sigma:\bar{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 5.1.5

(Persistence diagram morphism,
[
MP22
, Definition 6.1]
)
:

A

from a

to
\tau:R\to\mathbb{N}
is a Galois connection
f:P\leftrightarrows Q:g
such that
\bar{f}_{\#}\sigma=\tau
on
\bar{Q}\setminus\mathsf{diag}(\bar{Q})
. That is,

\tau(J)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\bar{Q}\\
\bar{f}(I)=J\end{subarray}}\sigma(I)

for all
J\in\bar{Q}\setminus\mathsf{diag}(\bar{Q})
.

Let
\mathsf{Dgm}
denote the category whose objects are persistence diagrams and whose morphisms are persistence diagram morphisms.

Definition 5.1.6

(Cost of a persistence diagram morphism)
:

Let

be a morphism from a persistence diagram
\sigma:\bar{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
to
\tau:\bar{R}\to\mathbb{N}
. We define the cost of this morphism as

\displaystyle\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{Dgm}}(f:Q\leftrightarrows R:g):

\displaystyle=\mathsf{dis}(\bar{f})

\displaystyle=\max_{I,J\in\bar{Q}}\big|||I-J||_{\infty}-||\bar{f}(I)-\bar{f}(J)||_{\infty}\big|.

As every morphism in
\mathsf{Dgm}
has a nonnegative cost associated with it, we have the notion of edit distance, denoted

, between objects in
\mathsf{Dgm}
, see
Section

2.3
.

Proposition 5.1.7

(
[

:

Let

(Functoriality of persistence diagrams)
:

For any

, the assignment

\displaystyle\mathsf{PD}_{d}:\mathsf{Fil}

\displaystyle\to\mathsf{Dgm}

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{F}}

is a functor. Moreover,

for any
\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{G}
in
\mathsf{Fil}
.

Proof.

Let
V
and

be two finite sets and

and

be two simplicial complexes over the vertex sets

and

respectively. Let
\mathsf{F}:Q\to\mathsf{SubCx}(K_{V})
and
\mathsf{G}:R\to\mathsf{SubCx}(K_{W})
be two

(f,g,Z,\pi_{V},\pi_{W})
be a morphism from
\mathsf{F}
to
\mathsf{G}

•

Z
is a finite set and

is a tripod,

•

is a Galois connection,

•

\mathsf{G}_{Z}^{\pi_{W}}(r)=\mathsf{F}_{Z}^{\pi_{V}}(g(r))
for all

5.2
Weighted Persistence Diagrams

Definition 5.2.1

(Weighted persistence diagrams)
:

Let
Q\subseteq[0,\infty)
be a finite set containing
0
. A
weighted persistence diagram
, written
w\textnormal{-}\sigma:(\bar{Q},\mu_{\bar{Q}}):\bar{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
, is persistence diagram
\sigma:\bar{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
together with a probability measure
\mu_{\bar{Q}}
on
\bar{Q}
such that
\sigma(I)>0
implies
\mu_{\bar{Q}}(I)>0
for every
I\in\bar{Q}\setminus\mathsf{diag}(\bar{Q})
.

Definition 5.2.2

(Weighted persistence diagram morphisms)
:

Let
w\textnormal{-}\sigma:(\bar{Q},\mu_{\bar{Q}})\to\mathbb{N}
and
w\textnormal{-}\tau:(\bar{R},\mu_{\bar{R}})\to\mathbb{N}
be two weighted persistence diagrams. A
weighted persistence diagram morphism
from
w\textnormal{-}\sigma
to
w\textnormal{-}\tau
is a Galois connection
f:Q\leftrightarrows R:g
such that

•

f:Q\leftrightarrows R:g
is a persistence diagram morphism from
\sigma:Q\to\mathbb{N}
to
\tau:R\to\mathbb{N}
,

•

\bar{f}:(\bar{Q},\mu_{\bar{Q}})\to(\bar{R},\mu_{\bar{R}})
is a Monge map.

Definition 5.2.3
:

Let
I_{1}=[a_{1},b_{1}]
,
I_{2}=[a_{2},b_{2}]
,
J_{1}=[c_{1},d_{1}]
, and
J_{2}=[c_{2},d_{2}]
be intervals in
\mathbb{R}
. We define the
displacement
associated to
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
J_{1}
, and
J_{2}
as

\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2}):=\max\begin{cases}&|(a_{1}-a_{2})-(c_{1}-c_{2})|,\\
&|(a_{1}-b_{2})-(c_{1}-d_{2})|,\\
&|(b_{1}-a_{2})-(d_{1}-c_{2})|,\\
&|(b_{1}-b_{2})-(d_{1}-d_{2})|\end{cases}

For an interval
I=[a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathsf{len}(I):=|a-b|
denote the length of the interval. The displacement
\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2})
measures similar is the pair
(I_{1},I_{2})
to the pair
(J_{1},J_{2})
by comparing
I_{1}-I_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
J_{1}-J_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\mathsf{len}(I_{1})
with
\mathsf{len}(J_{1})
, and
\mathsf{len}(I_{2})
with
\mathsf{len}(J_{2})
. To be precise, one can see that
\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2})=0
if and only if
I_{1}-I_{2}=J_{1}-J_{2}
and
\mathsf{len}(I_{i})=\mathsf{len}(J_{i})
for
i=1,2
.

The following proposition relates the notion of displacement with the notion of distortion of an order-preserving map.

Proposition 5.2.4
:

Let
Q,R\subseteq[0,\infty)
be finite subsets. Let
f:Q\to R
be an order-preserving map. Then,

\mathsf{dis}(f)=\mathsf{dis}(\bar{f})=\max_{I_{1},I_{2}\in\bar{Q}}\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},\bar{f}(I_{1}),\bar{f}(I_{2}))

Proof.

We already have the first equality by
Proposition

5.1.7
. Let
q_{1},q_{2}\in Q
be such that
\mathsf{dis}(f)=\big||q_{1}-q_{2}|-|f(q_{1})-f(q_{2})|\big|
. Observe that, since
f
is order-preserving, we have

\big||q_{1}-q_{2}|-|f(q_{1})-f(q_{2})|\big|=\big|(q_{1}-q_{2})-(f(q_{1})-f(q_{2}))\big|.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathsf{dis}(f)

\displaystyle=\big||q_{1}-q_{2}|-|f(q_{1})-f(q_{2})|\big|

\displaystyle=\big|(q_{1}-q_{2})-(f(q_{1})-f(q_{2}))\big|

\displaystyle=\mathsf{dpl}([q_{1},q_{1}],[q_{2},q_{2}],\bar{f}([q_{1},q_{1}]),\bar{f}([q_{2},q_{2}]))

\displaystyle\leq\max_{I_{1},I_{2}\in\bar{Q}}\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},\bar{f}(I_{1}),\bar{f}(I_{2}))

Similarly, let
I_{1}=[q_{3},q_{4}],I_{2}=[q_{5},q_{6}]\in\bar{Q}
be such that

\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},\bar{f}(I_{1}),\bar{f}(I_{2}))=\max_{I_{1},I_{2}\in\bar{Q}}\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},\bar{f}(I_{1}),\bar{f}(I_{2}))

Without loss of generality, assume that
\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},\bar{f}(I_{1})\bar{f}(I_{2})=\big|(q_{3}-q_{5})-(f(q_{3})-f(q_{5}))\big|
. Since
f
is order-preserving, we have that

\big|(q_{3}-q_{5})-(f(q_{3})-f(q_{5}))\big|=\big||q_{3}-q_{5}|-|f(q_{3})-f(q_{5})|\big|.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{I_{1},I_{2}\in\bar{Q}}\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},\bar{f}(I_{1}),\bar{f}(I_{2}))

\displaystyle=\big||q_{3}-q_{5}|-|f(q_{3})-f(q_{5})|\big|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{q,q^{\prime}\in Q}\big||q-q^{\prime}|-|f(q)-f(q^{\prime})|\big|

\displaystyle=\mathsf{dis}(f).

∎

Let
\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}
denote the category whose objects are weighted persistence diagrams and whose morphisms are weighted persistence diagram morphisms.

Definition 5.2.5

(p-cost of a weighted persistence diagram morphism)
:

Let

6
OT-like Interpretation of Edit Distance Between (Weighted) Persistence Diagrams

In this section, we show that the edit distance between (weighted) persistence diagrams can be realized in a way that does not require a categorical construction. Although the categorical construction is useful for stability proofs, it does not provide much insight into the interpretability of this distance. By realizing the edit distance between (weighted) persistence diagrams as an OT-like distance, we provide another view for this distance.

6.1
Interpretation of Edit Distance Between Unweighted Persistence Diagrams

The edit distance between unweighted persistence diagrams was first defined in
[
MP22
]
. In that paper, the authors showed that the edit distance is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the well-known bottleneck distance
[
MP22
, Theorem 9.1]
. In their proof, constructing a
1
-parameter family of persistence diagrams from a matching between two persistence diagrams was one of the main ingredients. In proofs situated in this section, we will follow a similar strategy of constructing a
1
-parameter family of persistence diagrams from a matching.

Remark 6.1.1
:

In
[
MP22
]
, the authors introduce the edit distance between ’signed’ persistence diagrams. However, as noted in
[
MP24
, Example 10.1]
, this definition can lead to trivial distances between distinct persistence diagrams due to a pathological path. To address this issue, a modification was proposed in
[
MP24
]
. In this paper, we focus exclusively on
1
-parameter filtrations, which always yield nonnegative persistence diagrams rather than signed ones. In this case, the nontriviality of the edit distance is simply a consequence of the fact that it is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the bottleneck distance. Notably, the proof of this equivalence in
[
MP22
, Theorem 9.1]
implicitly assumes nonnegativity.

Definition 6.1.2

(Matching)
:

Let
\sigma:\bar{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
and
\tau:\bar{R}\to\mathbb{N}
be two persistence diagrams. A
matching
between
\sigma
and
\tau
is a function
\gamma:\bar{Q}\times\bar{R}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

•

\sigma(I)=\sum_{J\in\bar{R}}\gamma(I,J)
for every
I\in\bar{Q}\setminus\mathsf{diag}(\bar{Q})
,

•

\tau(J)=\sum_{I\in\bar{Q}}\gamma(I,J)
for every
J\in\bar{R}\setminus\mathsf{diag}(\bar{R})
,

•

\gamma([0,0],[0,0])=1
.

In the definition above, the last condition is needed to guarantee that the matching
\gamma
aligns the two persistence diagrams
\sigma
and
\tau
at their common interval
[0,0]
. We now introduce the displacement cost of a matching.

Definition 6.1.3

(Displacement cost of a matching / displacement distance)
:

Let
\sigma:\bar{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
and
\tau:\bar{R}\to\mathbb{N}
be two persistence diagrams and let
\gamma:\bar{Q}\times\bar{R}\to\mathbb{N}
be a matching between them. The
displacement cost
of
\gamma
is defined to be

\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}(\gamma):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},I_{2}\in\bar{Q}\\
J_{1},J_{2}\in\bar{R}\\
\gamma(I_{1},J_{1})>0\\
\gamma(I_{2},J_{2})>0\end{subarray}}\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2}).

We define the
displacement distance
, denoted
d_{\mathsf{dpl}}
, between two persistence diagrams
\sigma
and
\tau
as the infimum of all the displacement costs of matchings between
\sigma
and
\tau
. That is,

d_{\mathsf{dpl}}(\sigma,\tau):=\inf_{\gamma}\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}(\gamma).

Note that
d_{\mathsf{dpl}}
satisfies the triangle inequality.

Proposition 6.1.4
:

Let
\sigma:\bar{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
and
\tau:\bar{R}\to\mathbb{N}
be two persistence diagrams. Then,

d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}(\sigma,\tau)=d_{\mathsf{dpl}}(\sigma,\tau)

Proof.

We first show
d_{\mathsf{dpl}}(\sigma,\tau)\leq d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}(\sigma,\tau)
. Let

\mathcal{P}:\;\;\rho_{0}:=\sigma\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$F_{1}$}}\rho_{1}\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$F_{2}$}}\cdots\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$F_{k-1}$}}\rho_{k-1}\xleftrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$F_{k}$}}\tau=:\rho_{k}

be an optimal path between
\sigma
and
\tau
in
\mathsf{Dgm}
. That is, each
F_{i}:=(f_{i},g_{i})
is a persistence diagram morphism (in either direction), and
d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}(\sigma,\tau)=\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{Dgm}}(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{dis}(f_{i}).
Observe that each
F_{i}
determines a matching
\gamma_{F_{i}}
between
\rho_{i}
and
\rho_{i+1}
with
d_{\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}}(\gamma_{F_{i}})\leq\mathsf{dis}(f_{i})
, see
[
MP22
, Lemma 9.10]
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{dpl}}(\sigma,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}d_{\mathsf{dpl}}(\rho_{i},\rho_{i+1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}(\rho_{i},\rho_{i+1})

\displaystyle=d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}(\sigma,\tau).

We now show
d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}(\sigma,\tau)\leq d_{\mathsf{dpl}}(\sigma,\tau)
by adopting the interpolation argument in
[
MP22
, Proposition 9.4]
. Let
\gamma:\bar{Q}\times\bar{R}\to\mathbb{N}
be a matching between
\sigma
and
\tau
. For every
t\in[0,1]
, let
A_{t}:=\{(1-t)I+tJ\mid\gamma(I,J)>0\}
and let
S_{t}:=\{\text{endpoints of intervals in }A_{t}\}
. Note that since
\gamma([0,0],[0,0])>0
, we have that
0\in S_{t}
, i.e.
S_{t}
is nonempty. For every
t\in[0,1]
, we define the function

\displaystyle\nu_{t}:\overline{S_{t}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\bar{Q},J\in\bar{R}\\
(1-t)I+tJ=K\end{subarray}}\gamma(I,J).

Observe that
\nu_{0}=\sigma
and
\nu_{1}=\tau
. We will now extract certain persistence diagram morphisms from the family
\{\nu_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
in order to obtain a path between
\sigma
and
\tau
in
\mathsf{Dgm}
.

As
t
varies from
0
to
1
, there are only finitely many places where the combinatorial
structure of
S_{t}
changes. To be precise, we say that a point
t\in[0,1]
is critical if for all sufficiently small
\delta>0
, there exists
s\in(t-\delta,t+\delta)
with
|S_{t}|\neq|S_{s}|
. By
[
MP22
, Lemma 9.6]
, if
t\in[0,1]
is not a critical point, then for any
K\in\mathrm{supp}(\nu_{t})
there is a unique pair of intervals
I\in\bar{Q}
and
J\in\bar{R}
with
\gamma(I,J)>0
and
(1-t)I+tJ=K
. This uniqueness allows us to define an order-preserving map,
\alpha_{t,s}
, from
S_{t}
to
S_{s}
, where
t
is not a critical point and
s\in[0,1]
is any point with no critical points strictly between
s
and
t
, as follows. For any
b\in S_{t}
, there are unique intervals
I\in\bar{Q}
and
J\in\bar{R}
with
\gamma(I,J)>0
and either
(1-t)I+tJ
is equal to
[a,b]
or
[b,c]
. If
(1-t)I+tJ=[a,b]
, then define
\alpha_{t,s}(b)
to be the right endpoint of the intervals
(1-s)I+sJ
. Similarly, if
b
is a left endpoint, then we define
\alpha_{t,s}(b)
to be the left endpoint of
(1-s)I+sJ
. Finally, we define
\alpha_{s,t}(0)=0
. This map has a right adjoint given by
\beta_{t,s}(x)=\max(b\in S_{t}\mid\alpha_{t,s}(b)\leq x)
. Then, by
[
MP22
, Lemma 9.8]
, the Galois connection

determines a persistence diagram morphism.

We now show that the cost of the persistence diagram morphism

is upper bounded by
|t-s|\cdot\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}(\gamma)
. By
Proposition

5.1.7
, we have that

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{Dgm}}(\alpha_{t,s}:S_{t}\leftrightarrows S_{s}:\beta_{t,s})=\mathsf{dis}(\alpha_{t,s})=\max_{u,v\in S_{t}}|(u-v)-(\alpha_{t,s}(u)-\alpha_{t,s}(v))|.

Let

. There are four cases:

and

could be a left endpoint or a right endpoint of an interval in

is the left endpoint of the interval

and

and

\displaystyle|(u-v)-(\alpha_{t,s}(u)-\alpha_{t,s}(v))|

\displaystyle=\Big|\Big((1-t)a_{1}+tc_{1}-\big((1-t)b_{2}+td_{2}\big)\Big)-\Big((1-s)a_{1}+sc_{1}-\big((1-s)b_{2}+sd_{2}\big)\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\big|(s-t)(a_{1}-b_{2})-(s-t)(c_{1}-d_{2})\big|

\displaystyle=|s-t|\cdot\big|(a_{1}-b_{2})-(c_{1}-d_{2})\big|

&|(b_{1}-b_{2})-(d_{1}-d_{2})|\end{cases}

\displaystyle=|s-t|\cdot\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2})

\displaystyle\leq|s-t|\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{I^{\prime}}_{1},{I^{\prime}}_{2}\in\bar{Q}\\
{J^{\prime}}_{1},{J^{\prime}}_{2}\in\bar{R}\\
\gamma({I^{\prime}}_{1},{J^{\prime}}_{1})>0\\
\gamma({I^{\prime}}_{2},{J^{\prime}}_{2})>0\end{subarray}}\mathsf{dpl}({I^{\prime}}_{1},{I^{\prime}}_{2},{J^{\prime}}_{1},{J^{\prime}}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq|s-t|\cdot\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}(\gamma).

Thus, we obtain

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{Dgm}}(\alpha_{t,s}:S_{t}\leftrightarrows S_{s}:\beta_{t,s})=\max_{u,v\in S_{t}}|(u-v)-(\alpha_{t,s}(u)-\alpha_{t,s}(v))|\leq|s-t|\cdot\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}(\gamma)

Now, let
\{0=s_{0}<\cdots<s_{N}=1\}\subseteq[0,1]
be the set of critical points and choose
\{t_{0}<\cdots<t_{N-1}\}\subseteq[0,1]
with
0=s_{0}<t_{0}<s_{1}<\cdots<t_{N-1}<s_{N}=1
. Then, the Galois connections
(\alpha_{t_{i},s_{i}},\beta_{t_{i},s_{i}})
and
(\alpha_{t_{i},s_{i+1}},\beta_{t_{i},s_{i+1}})
form a path
\mathcal{P}
between
\sigma
and
\tau
in
\mathsf{Dgm}
with

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{Dgm}}(\mathcal{P})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}((t_{i}-s_{i})+(s_{i+1}-t_{i}))\cdot\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}(\gamma)=\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}(\gamma).

Therefore,
d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}(\sigma,\tau)\leq d_{\mathsf{dpl}}(\sigma,\tau)

(\gamma,\eta)
is defined to be

\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}_{p}(\gamma,\eta):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta(I_{1},J_{1})>0\\
\eta(I_{2},J_{2})>0\end{subarray}}\mathsf{dpl}(I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2})

We define the
p
-displacement distance
, denoted
d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}
, between two weighted persistence diagrams
w\textnormal{-}\sigma
and
w\textnormal{-}\tau
as the infimum of all the
p
-displacement costs of weighted matchings between
w\textnormal{-}\sigma
and
w\textnormal{-}\tau
. That is,

d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau):=\inf_{(\gamma,\eta)}\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}_{p}(\gamma,\eta).

Proposition 6.2.3
:

Let
w\textnormal{-}\sigma:(\bar{Q},\mu_{\bar{Q}})\to\mathbb{N}
and
w\textnormal{-}\tau:(\bar{R},\mu_{\bar{R}})\to\mathbb{N}
be two weighted persistence diagrams. Then,

d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)=d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)

Proof.

We first show
d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)\leq d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)
. Let
f:Q\leftrightarrows R:g
be a weighted persistence diagram morphism from
w\textnormal{-}\sigma
to
w\textnormal{-}\tau
. Then,
f:Q\leftrightarrows R:g
induces a weighted matching
(\gamma_{f},\eta_{f})
as follows.

\gamma_{f}(I,J):=\begin{cases}\sigma(I)&\text{ if }\bar{f}(I)=J\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

And, the coupling between
\mu_{\bar{Q}}
and
\mu_{\bar{R}}
is given by
\left(id_{\bar{Q}},\bar{f}\right)_{\#}(\mu_{\bar{Q}})
. This induced weighted matching
(\gamma_{f},\eta_{f})
satisfies

\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}_{p}(\gamma_{f},\eta_{f})\leq\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{p}(f:Q\leftrightarrows R:g).

Thus, we obtain
d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)\leq d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)
.

We now show
d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)\leq d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)
by adopting the interpolation argument in
[
MP22
, Proposition 9.4]
and in the proof of
Proposition

6.1.4
. Let
(\gamma,\eta)
be a weighted matching between
w\textnormal{-}\sigma
and
w\textnormal{-}\tau
. For every
t\in[0,1]
, let
\mathfrak{A}_{t}:=\{(1-t)I+tJ\mid\eta(I,J)>0\}
and let
\mathfrak{S}_{t}:=\{\text{endpoints of intervals in }\mathfrak{A}_{t}\}
. Note that since
\gamma([0,0],[0,0])>0
, we have that
0\in\mathfrak{S}_{t}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{S}_{t}
is nonempty. For every
t\in[0,1]
, we define

\displaystyle\nu_{t}:\overline{\mathfrak{S}_{t}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\bar{Q},J\in\bar{R}\\
(1-t)I+tJ=K\end{subarray}}\gamma(I,J),

\displaystyle\mu_{t}:\overline{\mathfrak{S}_{t}}

\displaystyle\to[0,1]

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\bar{Q},J\in\bar{R}\\
(1-t)I+tJ=K\end{subarray}}\eta(I,J).

Observe that
\nu_{t}:(\overline{\mathfrak{S}_{t}},\mu_{t})\to\mathbb{N}
is a weighted persistence diagram. As in the proof of
Proposition

6.1.4
, there are only finitely many places where the combinatorial
structure of
\mathfrak{S}_{t}
changes as
t
varies from 0 to 1. Similarly to the proof of
Proposition

6.1.4
, we set critical points to be the values
t\in[0,1]
such that for all sufficiently small
\delta>0
, there exists
s\in(t-\delta,t+\delta)
with
|\mathfrak{S}_{t}|\neq|\mathfrak{S}_{s}|
. As constructed in proof of
Proposition

6.1.4
, we construct the Galois connections
\alpha_{t,s}:\mathfrak{S}_{t}\leftrightarrows\mathfrak{S}_{s}:\beta_{t,s}
where
t
is a non-critical point and
s
is any point with no critical points strictly between
s
and
t
. These Galois connections
(\alpha_{t,s},\beta_{t,s})
determine weighted persistence diagram morphisms from the weighted persistence diagram
\nu_{t}
to
\nu_{s}
with

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{p}(\alpha_{t,s}:\mathfrak{S}_{t}\leftrightarrows\mathfrak{S}_{s}:\beta_{t,s})\leq|s-t|\cdot\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}_{p}(\gamma,\eta).

Therefore, we obtain a path between
w\textnormal{-}\sigma
and
w\textnormal{-}\tau
whose
p
-cost is at most
\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}_{p}(\gamma,\eta)
. Thus, we obtain
d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)\leq d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau)
.
∎

Corollary 6.2.4
:

For two finite mm-spaces
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
, we have

d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}\left(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq 4^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{p}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})).

7
Discriminating Power of Weighted Persistence Diagrams

Weighed persistence diagrams blend two notions: classical persistence diagrams and global distributions of distances. In this section, we show that weighted persistence diagrams have stronger discriminating power than both classical persistence diagrams and global distributions of distances. We will investigate two cases separately:
p=\infty
and
p\in[1,\infty)
.

7.1
Case
p=\infty

We focus on the case
p=\infty
separately. We first prove that classical Vietoris-Rips persistence diagrams are
\infty
-Gromov-Wasserstein stable.

Proposition 7.1.1
:

Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be two finite mm-spaces. Then,

d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})).

Proof.

By
Proposition

4.1.5
and
Proposition

5.1.11
, we have that

d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GH}}((X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y})).

Combining this inequality with the fact,
[
Mé11
, Theorem 5.2 (b)]
, that

d_{\mathrm{GH}}((X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y}))\leq d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})),

we obtain

d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})).

∎

Remark 7.1.2
:

Notice that the inequality

d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GH}}((X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y}))

in the proof above can also be obtained from the the following facts

1.

The edit distance is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to bottleneck distance,
[
MP22
, Theorem 9.1]
.

d_{B}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq 2\cdot d_{B}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)

2.

Persistence diagrams are Gromov-Hausdorff stable,
[
CCG+09
, Theorem 3.1]
.

d_{B}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq 2\cdot d_{\mathrm{GH}}(X,Y).

We now describe how
Theorem

1.2.2
, permits improving upon the
\infty
-Gromov-Wasserstein stability of persistence diagrams given in
Proposition

7.1.1
.

Proposition 7.1.3
:

Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be two finite mm-spaces. Then,

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}\left(\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,\infty}\left(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X})),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})).

Proof.

The assignment

\displaystyle\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}

\displaystyle\to\mathsf{Dgm}

\displaystyle w\textnormal{-}\sigma

\displaystyle\mapsto\sigma

is a functor. Moreover, for a morphism
f:P\leftrightarrows Q:g
between two objects
w\textnormal{-}\sigma:(\bar{Q},\mu_{\bar{Q}})\to\mathbb{Z}
and
w\textnormal{-}\tau:(\bar{R},\mu_{\bar{R}})\to\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}
, we have that

\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{Dgm}}(f:P\leftrightarrows Q:g)\leq\mathsf{cost}_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{\infty}(f:P\leftrightarrows Q:g).

Thus, by
Proposition

2.3.2
, we have that

d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}\left(\sigma,\tau\right)\leq d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,\infty}\left(w\textnormal{-}\sigma,w\textnormal{-}\tau\right)

for any
w\textnormal{-}\sigma
and
w\textnormal{-}\tau
in
\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}
.
∎

Remark 7.1.4
:

Note that in
Proposition

7.1.3
, we use the edit distance to compare persistence diagrams, even though the bottleneck distance is more commonly employed for such comparisons. However, these two distances are shown to be bi-Lipschitz equivalent
[
MP22
, Theorem 9.1]
, implying that they offer the same discriminative power. Crucially, the edit distance is structurally compatible with the
\infty
-edit distance used to compare weighted persistence diagrams, as both are defined via cost functions on morphisms within their respective categories. This structural alignment makes the edit distance particularly well-suited for our goal of comparing the discriminative power of weighted versus unweighted persistence diagrams.

We now present an example in which
d_{\mathsf{Dgm}}^{E}
(equivalently, the bottleneck distance
d_{B}
) fails to distinguish two mm-spaces based on their persistence diagrams, whereas the weighted bottleneck distance

succeeds in capturing the difference between their weighted persistence diagrams, thereby distinguishing the mm-spaces.

Proposition 7.1.6
:

Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})

d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\overline{D_{X}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{X}}}\right),\left(\overline{D_{Y}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{Y}}}\right)\right)\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}\left((X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})\right).

Proof.

By
[

, we have that

2\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}\left(\left(D_{X},|\cdot|,\mu_{GDD(X)}\right),\left(D_{Y},|\cdot|,\mu_{GDD(Y)}\right)\right)\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}\left((X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})\right).

Observe that a coupling
\nu
between
\mu_{GDD(X)}
and
\mu_{GDD(Y)}
determines a coupling

\overline{\nu}:=\left(\mathsf{flip}_{D_{X}}\times\mathsf{flip}_{D_{Y}}\right)_{\#}(\nu\otimes\nu)

between
\mu_{\overline{D_{X}}}
and
\mu_{\overline{D_{Y}}}

\left(\mathsf{flip}_{D_{X}}\times\mathsf{flip}_{D_{Y}}\right):D_{X}\times D_{Y}\times D_{X}\times D_{Y}\to\overline{D_{X}}\times\overline{D_{Y}}
is the function whose restriction to
D_{X}\times D_{X}
is
\mathsf{flip}_{D_{X}}
and whose restriction to
D_{Y}\times D_{Y}
is
\mathsf{flip}_{D_{Y}}
. The following computation shows that
\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\bar{\nu})\leq 2\cdot\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\nu)
.

\displaystyle\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{\nu}(I_{1},J_{1})>0\\
\bar{\nu}(I_{2},J_{2})>0\end{subarray}}\big|||I_{1}-I_{2}||_{\infty}-||J_{1}-J_{2}||_{\infty}\big|

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\otimes\nu(r_{1},s_{1},r_{2},s_{2})>0\\
\nu\otimes\nu(r_{3},s_{3},r_{4},s_{4})>0\end{subarray}}\big|\max(|r_{1}-r_{3}|,|r_{2}-r_{4}|)-\max(|s_{1}-s_{3}|-|s_{2}-s_{4}|)\big|

\nu\otimes\nu(r_{3},s_{3},r_{4},s_{4})>0\end{subarray}}\max\big(|r_{1}-r_{3}|-|s_{1}-s_{3}|,|r_{2}-r_{4}|-|s_{2}-s_{4}|\big).

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu(r,s)>0\\
\nu(t,u)>0\end{subarray}}\big||r-t|-|s-u|\big|

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathsf{dis}_{\infty}(\nu).

Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\overline{D_{X}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{X}}}\right),\left(\overline{D_{Y}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{Y}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}\left(\left(D_{X},|\cdot|,\mu_{GDD(X)}\right),\left(D_{Y},|\cdot|,\mu_{GDD(Y)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}\left((X,d_{X},\mu_{X}),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})\right).

Recall that our result,

, states that

d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,\infty}\left(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X})),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})).

1.2.2
, improves upon the
\infty

Proposition 7.1.7

Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{GW_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\overline{D_{X}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{X}}}\right),\left(\overline{D_{Y}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{Y}}}\right)\right)

7.2
Case

For finite values of

, the

-edit distance between weighted persistence diagrams becomes solely dependent on the weights, disregarding the topological information captured by the diagrams themselves. We make this precise in the following.

Proposition 7.2.1
:

w\textnormal{-}\sigma_{0}:(\bar{Q},\mu_{0})\to\mathbb{N}
and
w\textnormal{-}\sigma_{1}:(\bar{Q},\mu_{1})\to\mathbb{N}
be two weighted persistence diagrams with
\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}(=:\mu)
. Then,
d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}\left(w\textnormal{-}\sigma_{0},w\textnormal{-}\sigma_{1}\right)=0
for

.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We will construct a path between
w\textnormal{-}\sigma_{0}
and
w\textnormal{-}\sigma_{1}
whose cost is controlled by
\varepsilon
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that there exists
I_{0}\neq I_{1}\in\bar{Q}
such that
\sigma_{0}(I_{0})=\sigma_{1}(I_{1})=1
,

Proposition 7.2.2
:

Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be two finite mm-spaces and let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{GW_{p}}}\left(\left(\overline{D_{X}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{X}}}\right),\left(\overline{D_{Y}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{Y}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}\left(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{p}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X})),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})).

Proof.

By
Proposition

6.2.3
, we have that

d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}\left(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)=d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}\left(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)

Let
(\gamma,\eta)
be a weighted matching between the weighted persistence diagrams
w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)}
and
w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}
. Then,
\eta
is a coupling between
\mu_{\overline{D_{X}}}
and
\mu_{\overline{D_{Y}}}
since the weights on the weighted persistence diagrams
w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)}
and
w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}
are given by
\mu_{\overline{D_{X}}}
and
\mu_{\overline{D_{Y}}}
. The following computation shows that
\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\eta)\leq\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}_{p}(\gamma,\eta)
.

\displaystyle(\mathsf{dis}_{p}(\eta))^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I_{1},I_{2},J_{1},J_{2}}\big|||I_{1}-I_{2}||_{\infty}-||J_{1}-J_{2}||_{\infty}\big|^{p}\;\eta(I_{1},J_{1})\;\eta(I_{2},J_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a_{1},b_{1}],[a_{2},b_{2}]\\
[c_{1},d_{1}],[c_{2},d_{2}]\end{subarray}}\big|\max\left(|a_{1}-a_{2}|,|b_{1}-b_{2}|\right)-\max(|c_{1}-c_{2}|,|d_{1}-d_{2}|)\big|^{p}\;\eta(I_{1},J_{1})\;\eta(I_{2},J_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a_{1},b_{1}],[a_{2},b_{2}]\\
[c_{1},d_{1}],[c_{2},d_{2}]\end{subarray}}\max\left(\big||a_{1}-a_{2}|-|c_{1}-c_{2}|\big|,\big||b_{1}-b_{2}|-|d_{1}-d_{2}|\big|\right)^{p}\;\eta(I_{1},J_{1})\;\eta(I_{2},J_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a_{1},b_{1}],[a_{2},b_{2}]\\
[c_{1},d_{1}],[c_{2},d_{2}]\end{subarray}}\mathsf{dpl}([a_{1},b_{1}],[a_{2},b_{2}],[c_{1},d_{1}],[c_{2},d_{2}])^{p}\;\eta(I_{1},J_{1})\;\eta(I_{2},J_{2})

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{dpl\textnormal{-}cost}_{\infty}(\gamma,\eta))^{p}.

Hence, we conclude that

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{GW_{p}}}\left(\left(\overline{D_{X}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{X}}}\right),\left(\overline{D_{Y}},||\cdot||_{\infty},\mu_{\overline{D_{Y}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathsf{dpl},p}\left(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathsf{W\textnormal{-}Dgm}}^{E,p}\left(w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(X)},w\textnormal{-}\mathsf{PD}_{d}^{\mathsf{VR}(Y)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\cdot d_{\mathrm{GW_{p}}}((X,d_{X},\mu_{X})),(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})),

where the last inequality follows from
Theorem

1.2.2
.
∎

As noted earlier in

is insensitive to the diagrams and it only compares the weights. In the case of weighted persistence diagrams of weighted Vietoris-Rips filtrations of finite mm-spaces, these weights are the flipped global distributions. When
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})

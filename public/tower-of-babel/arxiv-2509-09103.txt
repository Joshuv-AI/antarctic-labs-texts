AgriSentinel: Privacy-Enhanced Embedded-LLM Crop Disease Alerting System
Honghui Xu, Chanti Raju Mylay, Taeyeong Choi, Bobin Deng, Zhipeng Cai

Abstract
. Crop diseases pose significant threats to global food security, agricultural productivity, and sustainable farming practices, directly affecting farmers’ livelihoods and economic stability. To address the growing need for effective crop disease management, AI-based disease alerting systems have emerged as promising tools by providing early detection and actionable insights for timely intervention. However, existing systems often overlook critical aspects such as data privacy, market pricing power, and farmer-friendly usability, leaving farmers vulnerable to privacy breaches and economic exploitation. To bridge these gaps, we propose AgriSentinel, the first Privacy-Enhanced Embedded-LLM Crop Disease Alerting System. AgriSentinel incorporates a differential privacy mechanism to protect sensitive crop image data while maintaining classification accuracy. Its lightweight deep learning-based crop disease classification model is optimized for mobile devices, ensuring accessibility and usability for farmers. Additionally, the system includes a fine-tuned, on-device large language model (LLM) that leverages a curated knowledge pool to provide farmers with specific, actionable suggestions for managing crop diseases, going beyond simple alerting. Comprehensive experiments validate the effectiveness of AgriSentinel, demonstrating its ability to safeguard data privacy, maintain high classification performance, and deliver practical, actionable disease management strategies. AgriSentinel offers a robust, farmer-friendly solution for automating crop disease alerting and management, ultimately contributing to improved agricultural decision-making and enhanced crop productivity.

Crop diseases are emerging as some of the greatest threats to global food security and agricultural productivity

AI-based disease alerting systems offer a promising solution by providing early detection of crop diseases and actionable insights for timely intervention. By leveraging advanced AI technologies
(
Nettleton et al., 2019
;
Tantalaki et al., 2019
)
, such a system empowers farmers to make informed decisions that minimize crop losses, optimize yield, and reduce the reliance on excessive chemical inputs. This proactive approach not only improves productivity but also promotes sustainable agricultural practices by conserving resources, protecting the environment, and supporting long-term farming viability in the face of challenges such as pest outbreaks and changing environmental conditions
(
Liang and Shah, 2023
;
Mishra et al., 2021
)

(

;
Tzachor et al., 2023
)
. Furthermore, these models facilitate automated decision-making by integrating with other AI systems, streamlining the identification and management of crops and pests.

Unfortunately, these data-driven AI systems are vulnerable to malicious attacks, such as membership inference attacks
(
Jalalzai et al., 2022
;
Chen et al., 2020
)
, where attackers exploit the model to determine whether specific data points were used during training. In the context of agriculture, such attacks can expose sensitive crop data, including information about crop health, yield patterns, and regional farming practices
(
Gupta et al., 2020
)
. This stolen data can then be used to predict crop production levels, allowing market players to manipulate pricing strategies to their advantage
(
Sykuta, 2016
;
Javaid et al., 2022
)
. Such actions can disrupt fair market dynamics, leading to profit losses for farmers who are unable to compete effectively or secure equitable prices for their produce. Safeguarding crop data privacy is, therefore, essential to protect farmers’ economic interests and ensure a fair agricultural marketplace.

Although existing studies have explored AI-based crop disease alerting, they often lack a comprehensive focus on critical aspects such as privacy preservation to protect market pricing power and the development of farmer-friendly tools. This highlights the need for more holistic solutions that address both technical challenges and practical usability for farmers. To address this gap, we propose AgriSentinel, a Privacy-Enhanced Embedded-LLM Crop Disease Alerting System designed to achieve three key objectives: First, it incorporates a robust data privacy protection mechanism to safeguard sensitive crop data from potential misuse. Second, it features an on-device LLM-based model that not only provides farmers with specific strategic suggestions for managing crop diseases but also moves beyond simple alerting. Finally, its lightweight deep learning architecture is optimized for mobile devices, offering a more farmer-friendly and accessible solution to improve agricultural decision-making.

To sum up, this paper makes several significant contributions:

•

A lightweight, differentially private deep learning-based crop disease classification model is developed within AgriSentinel, enabling accurate disease classification while safeguarding the privacy of crop image data.

•

In AgriSentinel, an on-device LLM-based model is fine-tuned to provide farmers with specific, actionable suggestions for managing crop diseases, utilizing a curated knowledge pool of crop disease information.

•

Comprehensive experiments are conducted to validate the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed AgriSentinel framework.

Related Work

This section reviews the related works of crop disease alerting systems, agricultural large language models, and privacy-enhanced AI approaches.

2.1.
Crop Disease Alerting Approaches

Crop disease alerting approaches can be broadly categorized into context-aware models and data-driven models.
(1) Context-aware models
(
Liang and Shah, 2023
;
Mishra et al., 2021
)
integrate broader contextual information, such as regional agricultural practices and environmental factors. For instance, real-time sensor inputs and historical weather data play a crucial role in improving the accuracy of disease forecasting. Additionally, social media data can provide insights into local agricultural sentiment, offering a holistic understanding of crop health dynamics.
(2) Data-driven models
(
Nettleton et al., 2019
;
Tantalaki et al., 2019
)
leverage diverse data sources, applying machine learning and deep learning techniques for disease prediction. CNNs have been widely used for classifying crop disease images
(
Chillakuru et al., 2022
)
. Techniques like feature-level fusion and ensemble learning enhance predictive accuracy by combining outputs from multiple data-driven models
(
Jimenez et al., 2016
)
. Furthermore, attention mechanisms improve the interpretability of extracted features
(
Nong and Tarek, 2023
)
, while representation learning aids in identifying critical patterns for precise crop disease prediction.

2.2.
Agricultural Large Language Models

Modern LLMs like GPT and BERT have revolutionized data processing and generation with their exceptional understanding and adaptability
(
Sapkota et al., 2024
)
. Recently, these models have begun transforming the agricultural sector by addressing complex challenges such as disease detection, crop monitoring, and strategic decision-making
(
Tzachor et al., 2023
)
.
In agriculture, LLMs excel at extracting structured information from unstructured data sources, including reports, news articles, and farmer records, providing actionable insights that enhance various agricultural tasks
(
Banerjee et al., 2024
)
. Furthermore, these models facilitate automated decision-making by integrating with other AI systems, streamlining the identification and management of crops and pests. Their few-shot learning capabilities are particularly advantageous in agriculture, where data collection is often time-intensive and costly.
However, implementing LLMs in agriculture poses significant challenges. These include the need for high-quality, diverse datasets and the computational resources required to train and deploy the models
(
Andreev et al., 2024
)
. Additionally, ethical concerns, such as data privacy and security, add further complexity to their practical adoption
(
Silva et al., 2023
)
.

2.3.
Privacy-Enhanced AI Approaches

Preserving data privacy is critical in the era of big data, enabling meaningful analysis and model training while safeguarding sensitive information. Four key methodologies are commonly employed for privacy protection in AI.
(1) Differential privacy, widely adopted in machine learning frameworks, introduces random noise to data or model outputs to prevent the identification of individual data points
(
Aggarwal et al., 2024
)
.
(2) Federated learning enhances privacy by training models across decentralized devices or servers holding local data, eliminating the need for data sharing
(
Rahman et al., 2021
)
.
(3) Homomorphic encryption enables computations on encrypted data, ensuring sensitive information remains concealed
(
El Mestari et al., 2024
)
.
(4) Secure multi-party computation (SMPC) facilitates privacy-preserving collaborative learning by allowing multiple parties to compute functions over private inputs
(
Gowda, 2024
)
.
As the demand for privacy in data-driven applications grows, the integration of these techniques into AI pipelines is becoming increasingly vital to address risks associated with data breaches
(
Aouedi et al., 2024
)
.

The development of a privacy-enhanced LLM-based crop disease alerting system presents two significant technical challenges.
First, designing a lightweight and user-friendly system that allows farmers to easily adopt and leverage the transformative capabilities of large language models for effective crop disease management is inherently complex.
Second, ensuring privacy-enhanced training for agricultural LLMs is critical, as inadequately protected crop data could be exploited to infer production levels, potentially undermining farmers’ market pricing power and causing economic losses.

3.
AgriSentinel Model

We propose an AgriSentinel framework, illustrated in Fig.
1
, as a privacy-enhanced on-device system for comprehensive crop disease alerting that seamlessly integrates differential privacy, deep learning, and LLM.
The framework begins with a Gaussian noise-based differential privacy mechanism to safeguard sensitive crop data, introducing controlled noise to achieve a balance between privacy protection and the preservation of critical diagnostic features.
The obfuscated data is subsequently processed by a lightweight deep learning model designed for efficient and accurate disease classification, leveraging advanced architectural components such as convolutional layers and ReLU activation to optimize computational efficiency and predictive accuracy.
Building upon these predictive results, the framework incorporates a fine-tuned on-device LLM, enriched with a curated knowledge pool, to generate precise and actionable disease management strategies.
To sum up, AgriSentinel offers farmers a lightweight and user-friendly tool to enhance crop health management while addressing key challenges in usability and data protection.

3.1.
Differential Privacy Noise Injection Mechanism

We propose a differential privacy mechanism to protect crop data by employing Gaussian noise as an effective obfuscation technique. Gaussian noise introduces controlled random variations to the pixel values of an image, masking identifiable features while preserving the image’s utility for analysis. This approach strikes a balance between safeguarding data privacy and retaining the essential diagnostic information required for accurate disease pattern recognition. The process of applying Gaussian noise to an input image
x
can be mathematically expressed as:

(1)

x_{\text{obf}}=x+N(0,\sigma^{2}),

where
I_{\text{obf}}
is the resulting obfuscated image and
Lap(0,\sigma^{2})
represents Gaussian noise with a mean of 0 and a tunable variance
\sigma^{2}
.
The mean value of 0 ensures that the noise is centered around the original pixel value, maintaining an unbiased perturbation, while the variance
\sigma^{2}
dictates the level of noise introduced.
A higher variance results in more pronounced noise, providing stronger obfuscation and enhanced privacy protection but potentially impacting image clarity and the model’s ability to interpret diagnostic features. Conversely, a lower variance applies subtler noise, which maintains more image detail but offers less privacy shielding.

The proposed differential privacy noise injection mechanism ensures that core diagnostic features essential for disease recognition remain intact. The strategic application of Gaussian noise enables the system to comply with data privacy standards while allowing the machine learning model to access sufficient visual information for accurate disease classification. The obfuscated images are suitable for processing by deep learning algorithms, which can learn robust features for pattern recognition even when some noise is present.
This approach is particularly advantageous for applications where image data needs to be shared across different systems or with third parties, maintaining data confidentiality and minimizing the risk of privacy breaches. The flexibility in tuning the variance
\sigma^{2}
allows for a customizable level of obfuscation that can be adapted based on specific privacy requirements or regulations.

(1) Data Normalization: An essential preprocessing step, data normalization adjusts each pixel value by dividing by 255. This scales the image pixel values to a range of [0, 1], ensuring a uniform distribution of input values. Such normalization not only stabilizes the learning process but also accelerates convergence and improves the accuracy of the model by minimizing the impact of variations in image brightness and contrast.
(2) Convolutional and Pooling Layers: These layers are crucial for extracting spatial and local features from the images of rice diseases. The convolutional layers detect these features, while the pooling layers reduce the dimensionality of the data, which helps in handling overfitting.

4.1.2.
Data Preprocessing

Data preparation is a crucial step before feeding images into the proposed lightweight deep learning-based crop disease classification model, ensuring the data is properly formatted for effective training. In this study, all images were resized to 224x224 pixels, a standard size required for CNN models to maintain consistent input dimensions with pre-trained architectures. Pixel values were normalized by dividing each by 255, scaling them to the [0, 1] range, which stabilizes the learning process, accelerates convergence, and improves model accuracy by reducing the impact of brightness and contrast variations. Labels for disease categories were encoded into numerical values, enabling the model to interpret categorical data effectively during training. The dataset was then split into training and testing sets, ensuring 80% of the data was used for training and 20% for testing to evaluate the model’s performance.

4.1.3.
Deep Learning Model Architecture

The proposed deep learning architecture is designed to effectively classify crop disease images. It begins with an input layer that accepts images of size
224\times 224\times 3
, corresponding to the standard RGB format. The first convolutional layer applies 32 filters of size
3\times 3
with ReLU activation, followed by a max pooling layer to reduce the spatial dimensions of the feature maps. This is succeeded by a second convolutional layer with 64 filters of the same size and ReLU activation, paired with another max pooling layer. The extracted 2D feature maps are then flattened into a 1D vector by the flattening layer for further processing. A dense layer with 128 units and ReLU activation follows, accompanied by a dropout layer to mitigate overfitting. Finally, the architecture employs a softmax output layer, which calculates class probabilities.

4.1.6.
Performance Metrics

We use accuracy, precision, recall and F1 score as the metrics for the deep learning performance comparison.
Besides, the metric Privacy Loss
\epsilon
is used to assess the effectiveness of a privacy-preserving framework in obfuscating sensitive information within images.
In the context of rice disease data, privacy loss quantifies the extent to which identifiable information remains visible after obfuscation.
The calculation of privacy loss is based on two primary factors: (1) Sensitivity of the input
\Delta f
and (2) Amount of Noise
\sigma
.
Sensitivity measures the maximum change in the output when a single individual’s data is added or removed. In image obfuscation, sensitivity represents the degree of identifiable features retained in the image post-obfuscation.
Noise scale reflects the intensity of obfuscation applied to mask sensitive information. By adding Gaussian noise, the identifiable characteristics of the image are masked, reducing the recognizability of sensitive features.
To compute the overall privacy loss, both sensitivity and noise components are combined in the following manner:
\epsilon=\frac{\Delta f}{\sigma}
.
This equation illustrates that privacy loss
\epsilon
is directly proportional to the sensitivity
\Delta f
and inversely proportional to the noise
\sigma
applied. Consequently, a higher sensitivity or lower noise results in a higher privacy loss, indicating more exposure of identifiable information. Conversely, lower sensitivity or higher noise diminishes privacy loss, thereby strengthening privacy protection.

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose AgriSentinel, a Privacy-Enhanced Embedded-LLM Crop Disease Alerting System. AgriSentinel integrates a differential privacy noise injection mechanism to protect crop image data, a lightweight deep learning model for accurate crop disease classification, and a fine-tuned LLM to provide farmers with strategic suggestions for crop disease alerting and management. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that AgriSentinel effectively safeguards crop image privacy while delivering accurate and actionable recommendations for crop disease management. Additionally, the system is lightweight, easy to deploy, and designed to be farmer-friendly, enabling automated crop disease alerting and management, ultimately contributing to improved crop production.

A Fourier analysis for ( θ , T ) (\theta,T) -periodic functions and applications Thanks: This study was financed, in part, by the São Paulo Research Foundation (FAPESP), Brasil. Process Number 2025/08151-5.
André Pedroso Kowacs, Marielle Aparecida Silva

Abstract
. We develop a Fourier analysis for a generalization of the class of periodic functions, often referred to as ( θ , T ) (\theta,T) -periodic functions, and prove several properties and inequalities related to the Fourier transform, including a type of Poincaré inequality, which extend the periodic case. As an application, we employ this analysis to show that a continuous linear operator acting on smooth ( θ , T ) (\theta,T) -periodic functions is globally hypoelliptic/solvable if and only if the corresponding operator which acts on periodic functions is globally hypoelliptic/solvable, and characterize the global hypoellipticity/solvability of a class of first order differential operators acting on the set of smooth ( θ , T ) (\theta,T) -periodic functions.

1.
Introduction

Fourier analysis is an important tool with a wide range of applications. For instance, it is extensively applied to the study of linear operators acting on periodic functions. In particular, the study of global regularity of (pseudo)differential operators on the torus, which has been a very active area of research on the last few decades (for instance, see
[
9
,
7
,
8
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
), relies deeply on Fourier analysis and shows connections to Diophantine approximation theory. Recent developments of the Fourier analysis on compact manifolds and, in particular, compact Lie groups, have motivated similar studies in these more general settings (for instance, see
[
5
,
19
,
18
,
23
]
).

On the other hand,
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions also referred to (among other names) as
(\omega,c)
-periodic, have proved to be relevant in several analytical and applied contexts, especially in ordinary and functional differential equations. See, for instance,
[
1
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
. In these works, the authors establish conditions under which the solutions of certain differential equations are
(\theta,T)
-periodic, generalizing the classical results of periodicity and, moreover, developing a Floquet theory for this class of equations.
A function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be
(\theta,T)
-periodic if
f(t+T)=\theta f(t)
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
. This theory was introduced and developed in
[
3
]
and
[
2
]
, motivated by systems with phase symmetry, such as the Mathieu’s equation, which admits
(\theta,T)
-periodic solutions. Clearly, this class of functions generalizes the classical case of periodicity
(\theta=1)
and also of antiperiodicity
(\theta=-1)
.

In this work, we investigate
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions in the framework of Fourier analysis and distributions spaces. We establish a Poincaré-type inequality for this class of functions and present global regularity results for first-order differential operators, showing that certain hypoellipticity properties are preserved in the environment of
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions.

It is worth mentioning that the theory developed in this paper can alternatively be seen in the context of nonharmonic analysis, which first appeared in
[
20
]
and was recently further developed in
[
22
]
. This analysis was also further applied to the corresponding setting in
[
10
]
. However, we note that the approach taken in the present paper seems to provide new and more precise results, and also the equivalent formulation for the Fourier analysis of
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions in the previously mentioned references only considers real valued
\theta
.

The present paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we deal with smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the corresponding distributions. We present illustrative examples and, by introducing the operator
\Omega_{\theta,T}
, establish fundamental results in the framework of Fourier analysis. See Propositions
2.7
,
2.8
and
2.9
. Moreover, we discuss Sobolev spaces in the set of

(\theta,T)
-periodic distributions and provide a type of Sobolev embedding theorem in Proposition
2.13
.

Theorem
3.1
in Section
3
presents a version of the Poincaré inequality for
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions, extending the classical result corresponding to the case
\theta=1
.

In Section
4
, we apply the Fourier analysis developed in Section
2
to the study of global hypoellipticity and global solvability of continuous linear operators acting on smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions. As an application of the main result obtained in this section, we prove that any continuous linear operator
P
acting on smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over the smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions if and only if the induced

which acts on

-periodic functions is globally hypoelliptic/solvable. Using this result, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for the class of differential operators
\partial_{x_{1}}+c(x_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}+q(x_{1},x_{2})

-periodic functions, to be globally hypoelliptic/solvable over the smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions. These results are demonstrated in Theorem
4.4

4.5

4.7
and
4.8

In Appendix
A

Finally, in Appendix

(\theta,T)
-periodic setting. These formulas are used in the proofs in Appendix
A

2.
Analysis on (
\theta,T
)-periodic functions

In this section, we present the theory of distributions and Fourier analysis for the space of
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions. For classical theory involving periodic functions, the reader may want to consult
[
13
,
17
]
.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be the field of complex numbers and denote by
\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}
the set
(\mathbb{C}\backslash{\{0}\})^{n}
.
Given
\theta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}
and
T>0
, we say that a measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
(with respect to the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
(\theta,T)
-periodic if it satisfies

f(x+Te_{j})=\theta_{j}f(x),

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq j\leq n
, where
{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
denotes the canonical basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this case, we write
f\in L^{0}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The set of smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is then defined as the set of all smooth functions in
L^{0}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In what follows, we present some examples.

Example 2.1
.

Consider
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by

f(x)=e^{\xi\cdot x}=e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}x_{i}},

for some
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta(T),T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
T>0
, where

\theta(T)=(e^{\xi_{1}T},\dots,e^{\xi_{n}T}).

Example 2.2
.

Given
\theta\in\mathbb{C}_{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus{\{0}\}
,
T>0
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with compact support, define

{f}_{\theta,T}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta^{-k}f(x+kT),

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then a simple computation shows that
{f}_{\theta,T}\in C_{\theta,T}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

Example 2.3
.

Given
\theta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}
,
T>0
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, consider the function
\mu_{k}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
g(x)=f(kx)
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
. Then

\mu_{k}f(x+T)=f(k(x+T))=f(kx+kT)=\theta^{k}f(kx)=\theta^{k}(\mu_{k}f)(x)

and

\mu_{k}f(x+{\textstyle\frac{T}{|k|}})=f(k(x+{\textstyle\frac{T}{|k|}}))=f(kx+\operatorname{sign}(k)T)=\theta^{\operatorname{sign}(k)}f(kx)=\theta^{\operatorname{sign}(k)}(\mu_{k}f)(x),

where
\theta^{k}\vcentcolon=(\theta_{1}^{k},\dots,\theta_{n}^{k})
, so
\mu_{k}f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta^{k},T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{\infty}_{\theta^{\operatorname{sign}(k)},\frac{T}{|k|}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For the rest of this paper, let
\log:\mathbb{C}_{*}\to\mathbb{C}
denote a fixed choice of a complex logarithm function; for instance, one such choice could be the principal value of the complex logarithm. It is important to note that the validity of our results remains intact regardless of this choice, as is evident from the proofs and further remarks below.

Definition 2.4
.

We define the mapping
\Omega_{\theta,T}:L^{0}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{n}))\cong L^{0}([0,2\pi]^{n})
given by

\Omega_{\theta,T}f(x)\vcentcolon=e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{2\pi}}f(\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}x)=\vcentcolon e^{-x\cdot\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}}f(\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}x),

for every
f\in L^{0}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Note that
\Omega_{\theta,T}
is a bijection, with inverse given by

\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}f(x)\vcentcolon=e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{T}}f(\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{T}x)=\vcentcolon e^{x\cdot\frac{\log(\theta)}{T}}f(\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{T}x).

Recall that for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, there exists a natural identification
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\cong L^{p}([0,2\pi]^{n})
, where we consider the normalized Lebesgue measure
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}dx
on
[0,2\pi]^{n}
. Also, we will consider the space
L^{p}([0,T]^{n})
with the normalized Lebesgue measure as well. With that in mind, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the space
L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the set of equivalence classes of all functions
f\in L^{0}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\Omega_{\theta,T}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\|\Omega_{\theta,T}f\|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi]^{n})}<\infty,

where
f\sim g
if and only if
\|f-g\|_{L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
. We equip this space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
with the usual identification of functions and their equivalence classes of
L^{p}
spaces.

Remark 1
.

Note that for
1\leq p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}{\textstyle|f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)}|^{p}|e^{-x\cdot\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}{\textstyle|f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)}|^{p}e^{-p\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\text{Re}(\log(\theta_{j}))}{2\pi}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}{\textstyle|f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)}|^{p}e^{-p\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\log(|\theta_{j}|)}{2\pi}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}{\textstyle|f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)}|^{p}e^{-p\log(\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\frac{x_{j}}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{-\frac{x_{j}}{T}}\Big)^{p}dx,

so in fact
L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be viewed as the
L^{p}
space on
[0,T]^{n}
with respect to the measure

d\mu\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{T^{n}}|e^{-x\cdot\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}}|^{p}dx=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{-\frac{px_{j}}{T}}dx,

where
dx
denotes the usual Lebesgue measure on
[0,T]^{n}
.

Similarly, for
p=\infty
, we have that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\esssup_{x\in[0,2\pi]^{n}}{\textstyle|f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)}||e^{-x\cdot\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}}|

\displaystyle=\esssup_{x\in[0,T]^{n}}{\textstyle|f(x)}|\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{-\frac{x_{j}}{T}}

\displaystyle\asymp\esssup_{x\in[0,T]^{n}}{\textstyle|f(x)}|=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]^{n})}.

Therefore for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space, which by definition is isometrically isomorphic to
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
via the restriction
\Omega_{\theta,T}:L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and in particular,
L^{p}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}([0,T]^{n})
when
|\theta_{j}|=1
, for every
j=1,\dots,n
.

Next, we define a topology on
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
from the usual Fréchet space topology on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
via the bijection
\Omega_{\theta,T}
. In particular, since the topology on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is the Fréchet topology induced by the countable family of (semi)norms

\tilde{p}_{N}(f)\vcentcolon=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\sup_{x\in[0,2\pi]^{n}}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)|,

for
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, it follows that the topology on
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is induced by the countable family of (semi)norms

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{N}(\Omega_{\theta,T}f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\sup_{x\in[0,2\pi]^{n}}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}[\Omega_{\theta,T}f(x)]|

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\sup_{x\in[0,2\pi]^{n}}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}[e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(\theta_{i})\frac{x_{i}}{2\pi}}f(\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}x)]|

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\sup_{x\in[0,T]^{n}}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)|=\vcentcolon p_{N}(f),

where
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This also makes
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into a Fréchet space and we have that
f_{j}\to f
in
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\Omega_{\theta,T}f_{j}\to\Omega_{\theta,T}f
in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Now, we present the space of distributions in the environment of
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions. Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the set of
(\theta,T)
-periodic distributions, that is, the set of continuous linear functionals on
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, since
\Omega_{\theta,T}
is linear, a linear functional
u:C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{C}
is in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if the linear functional
\tilde{u}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\langle\tilde{u},f\rangle=\langle u,\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}f\rangle,

is continuous (and therefore in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. Moreover, any
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
defines
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\langle u,f\rangle=\langle\tilde{u},\Omega_{\theta,T}f\rangle.

Therefore the transpose
\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}:\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by

\langle\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}\tilde{u},f\rangle=\langle\tilde{u},\Omega_{\theta,T}f\rangle,

for every
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
establishes a bijection between
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Its inverse
(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t})^{-1}=(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, is given by

\langle(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t})^{-1}u,f\rangle=\langle u,\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}f\rangle,

for every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Remark 2
.

Note that any
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defines
\tilde{u}_{f}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
via the usual integration duality formula

\langle\tilde{u}_{f},g\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\Omega_{\theta,T}f(x)g(x)dx.

Hence, it also defines a distribution
u_{f}=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}\tilde{u}_{f}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by

\displaystyle\langle u_{f},g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}\tilde{u}_{f},g\rangle=\langle\tilde{u}_{f},\Omega_{\theta,T}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\Omega_{\theta,T}f(x)\Omega_{\theta,T}g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}f(x)g(x)e^{-2x\cdot\frac{\log(\theta)}{T}}dx.

By identifying
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
u_{f}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the set of smooth functions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
as a subspace of the set of distributions, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

For every
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that

(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}f=\Omega_{\theta,T}f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),

that is,

(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}|_{C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\equiv\Omega_{\theta,T}.

Analogously, for every
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we have that

\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

that is,

\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}|_{C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\equiv\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we have that

\displaystyle\langle(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\Omega_{\theta,T}f(x)\Omega_{\theta,T}\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\Omega_{\theta,T}f(x)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\theta,T}f,g\rangle.

Since this holds for every smooth periodic function
g
, the first claim in the statement follows. As for the second claim, the proof is analogous, since for
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
g\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have that

\displaystyle\langle\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,\Omega_{\theta,T}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}f(x)\Omega_{\theta,T}g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\Omega_{\theta,T}\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}f(x)\Omega_{\theta,T}g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}f,g\rangle.

∎

We conclude this subsection by noting that for any given
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the characterization of continuous linear functionals on Fréchet spaces, there exists
C=C_{u}>0
,
N=N_{u}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

|\langle u,f\rangle|\leq C\tilde{p}_{N}(\Omega_{\theta,T}f)\lesssim Cp_{N}(f),

for every
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.1.
A Fourier transform for
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions

Definition 2.6
.

Given
f\in L^{1}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, define its Fourier coefficient at
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)\vcentcolon=\widehat{\Omega_{\theta,T}f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\Omega_{\theta,T}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}dx

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\textstyle f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)e^{-ix\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx,

where
\log(\theta)\vcentcolon=(\log(\theta_{1}),\dots,\log(\theta_{n}))
.

Similarly, for
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, define its Fourier coefficient at
\xi
by

(2.2)

\widehat{u}(\xi)\vcentcolon=\widehat{(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}u}(\xi)=\langle(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}u(x),e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\rangle=\langle u(x),e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}\rangle.

Since we can view
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{1}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the definition holds for every
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Also note that for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that

\widehat{u_{f}}(\xi)=\widehat{(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t})^{-1}f}(\xi)=\widehat{\Omega_{\theta,T}f}(\xi),

by Proposition
2.5
, so definition (
2.2
) is consistent with (
2.6
).

Evidently the Fourier inversion formula implies that for
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

\Omega_{\theta,T}f(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi},

and consequently

f(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})},

in the sense of distributions, also with convergence in
L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
f\in L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and also point-wise (uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) if
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Remark 3
.

As already mentioned in the introduction of this paper, the Fourier transform/series defined above can be seen in the context of nonharmonic analysis. In fact, in this case the properties of the Fourier transform in Definition
2.6
are closely tied to the study of completeness of sets of complex exponentials and also of biorthogonal systems and bases on Hilbert spaces. There exists an extensive literature on both of these subjects (though in the first one, mostly restricted to dimension
n=1
). For a survey paper on completeness of sets of complex exponentials, we refer to
[
24
]
, and for a reference on biorthogonal systems and bases see
[
6
]
.

In the sequence, we present some properties of
(\theta,T)
-periodic Fourier series.

First, for
\theta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}
, set

(2.3)

K_{\theta,\min}\vcentcolon=\prod_{|\theta_{j}|<1}|\theta_{j}|\quad\text{ and }\quad K_{\theta,\max}\vcentcolon=\prod_{|\theta_{j}|>1}|\theta_{j}|,

with the convention that the empty product is
1
.

Note that

\max_{x_{j}\in[0,1]}|\theta_{j}|^{x_{j}}=\begin{cases}|\theta_{j}|&\text{ if }|\theta_{j}|\geq 1,\\
1,&\text{ if }|\theta_{j}|<1,\end{cases}

and

\min_{x_{j}\in[0,1]}|\theta_{j}|^{x_{j}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }|\theta_{j}|\geq 1,\\
|\theta_{j}|&\text{ if }|\theta_{j}|<1.\end{cases}

Therefore

K_{\theta,\min}=\min_{x\in[0,1]^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{x_{j}}\quad\text{ and }\quad K_{\theta,\max}=\max_{x\in[0,1]^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{x_{j}}.

Proposition 2.7
.

For
f\in L_{\theta,T}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that

|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq K_{\theta,\min}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}([0,T]^{n})}.

Proof.

Indeed, the first inequality follows directly from Definition
2.6
. As for the second inequality, note that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}|f({\textstyle{\frac{2\pi}{T}}}x)|e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\text{Re}(\log(\theta_{j}))\frac{x_{j}}{2\pi}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}|f(x)|e^{-\log\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\frac{x_{j}}{T}}\Big)}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}|f(x)|\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\frac{x_{j}}{T}}\Big)^{-1}dx

\displaystyle\leq K_{\theta,\min}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}([0,T]^{n})}.

∎

Proposition 2.8
.

For
f\in L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that

\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and

(2.4)

K_{\theta,\min}\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{n})}\leq K_{\theta,\max}\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

From Plancherel’s identity, we have that

\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}=\|\Omega_{\theta,T}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

for every
f\in L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which proves the first claim in the statement. For the second claim, from the fact that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\frac{x_{j}}{T}}\right)^{-2}dx,

it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{|\theta_{j}|>1}|\theta_{j}|\right)^{-2}\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{n})}^{2}\leq\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{|\theta_{j}|<1}|\theta_{j}|\right)^{-2}\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{n})}^{2},

so that

K_{\theta,\min}\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{n})}\leq K_{\theta,\max}\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

as claimed.
∎

Remark 4
.

Note that the existence of the constants
K_{\theta,\min}
and
K_{\theta,\max}
for the “Plancherel inequality” from Proposition
2.8
is guaranteed from results in biorthogonal systems (see
[
6
]
), as the pair of set of exponentials:

\left\{e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi})}\right\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\text{ and }\left\{e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi+i\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi})}\right\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}

defines a biorthogonal system on
L^{2}([0,T]^{n})
. Also note that in particular, if
\log(\theta)\in i\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then either one of these sets is orthogonal on
L^{2}([0,T]^{n})
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

i)

\widehat{f+\lambda g}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)+\lambda\widehat{g}(\xi)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
;

ii)

\widehat{\partial_{x_{j}}f}(\xi)=\frac{2\pi}{T}i\big(\xi_{j}-i\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{2\pi}\big)\widehat{f}(\xi)
,
1\leq j\leq n
;

iii)

\widehat{e_{\xi_{0}}f(x)}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi-\xi_{0})
, where
e_{\xi_{0}}f(x)=e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot\xi_{0}}f(x)
,
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
;

iv)

\widehat{\tau_{a}f}(\xi)=e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}a\cdot(\xi-i\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi})}\widehat{f}(\xi)
, where
\tau_{a}f(x)=f(x+a)
,
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

v)

\widehat{\mu_{k}{f}}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\operatorname{sign}(k)\xi)
, where
\mu_{k}f(x)=f(kx)\in C^{\infty}_{\theta^{\operatorname{sign}(k)},\frac{T}{|k|}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

i)
Follows immediately from the linearity of
\Omega_{\theta,T}
and the Fourier transform.

ii)
Indeed, since the Fourier transform for smooth periodic functions converges uniformly, the Fourier series for smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions converges uniformly over compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consequently, for
1\leq j\leq n
we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{j}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{j}}\left[\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\partial_{x_{j}}\left[e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{2\pi}{T}i\Big(\xi_{j}-i\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{2\pi}\Big)\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})},

so by comparing Fourier coefficients we conclude that
\widehat{\partial_{x_{j}}f}(\xi)=\frac{2\pi}{T}i\big(\xi_{j}-i\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{2\pi}\big)\widehat{f}(\xi)
, as claimed.

iii)
First note that
e_{\xi_{0}}f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus, from Definition
2.6
, we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{e_{\xi_{0}}f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\textstyle f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)e^{ix\cdot\xi_{0}}e^{-ix\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}\textstyle f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)e^{-ix\cdot((\xi-\xi_{0})-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(\xi-\xi_{0}).

iv)
Again note that
x\mapsto\tau_{a}f(x)=f(x+a)\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, hence

\displaystyle\widehat{\tau_{a}f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}{\textstyle f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x+a})e^{-ix\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}{\textstyle f(\frac{T}{2\pi}(x+\frac{2\pi}{T}a)})e^{-ix\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\prod_{j=1}^{n}[\frac{2\pi}{T}a_{j},2\pi+\frac{2\pi}{T}a_{j}]}{\textstyle f(\frac{T}{2\pi}x)}e^{-i(x-\frac{2\pi}{T}a)\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}a\cdot(\xi-i\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi})}.

v)
Recalling that
\mu_{k}f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta^{\operatorname{sign}(k)},\frac{T}{|k|}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the definition of the Fourier transform, we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu_{k}f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}f\left({\textstyle\frac{\frac{T}{|k|}}{2\pi}}kx\right)e^{-ix\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta^{\operatorname{sign}(k)})}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}f\left({\operatorname{sign}(k)\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x\right)e^{-ix\cdot(\xi-\operatorname{sign}(k)i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx.

If
\operatorname{sign}(k)=1
, from the equality above it is immediate that
\widehat{\mu_{k}f}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)
. Otherwise,
\operatorname{sign}(k)=-1
, so performing the change of variables
x\mapsto-x
we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu_{k}f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[-2\pi,0]^{n}}f\left({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x\right)e^{ix\cdot(\xi+i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}f\left({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x\right)e^{ix\cdot(\xi+i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}f\left({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x\right)e^{-ix\cdot(-\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}dx

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(-\xi),

as claimed.
∎

Remark 5
.

We note that the results in the Proposition
2.9
hold in larger function spaces, however we chose to state it as above for simplicity.

2.2.
(\theta,T)
-periodic Sobolev spaces

Recall that the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
are given by the set of
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\|(\operatorname{Id}-\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{n}})^{s/2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}<\infty,

where
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\langle\xi\rangle\vcentcolon=(1+\|\xi\|)^{2}
denotes the Japanese bracket.

Definition 2.10
.

Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the
(\theta,T)
-periodic Sobolev space
H_{\theta,T}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the set of all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\vcentcolon=\|\Omega_{\theta,T}u\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}<\infty.

The mapping
H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ni u\mapsto\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
defines a norm, endowing
H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with a Banach space topology.

Evidently,
u\in H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\Omega_{\theta,T}u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
H^{0}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore we immediately obtain that

\bigcup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ \text{ and }\bigcap_{s\in\mathbb{R}}H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and consequently we have the following results.

Proposition 2.11
.

If
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
N>0
, there exists
C_{N}>0
such that

(2.6)

|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\leq C_{N}\langle\xi\rangle^{-N},

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Conversely, given a sequence
\widehat{f}(\xi)
satisfying (
2.6
) we have that

\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proposition 2.12
.

If
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
N>0
, there exists
C_{N}>0
such that

(2.7)

|\widehat{u}(\xi)|\leq C_{N}\langle\xi\rangle^{-N},

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Conversely, given a sequence
\widehat{u}(\xi)
satisfying (
2.7
) we have that

\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{u}(\xi)e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We also have the following version of the Sobolev embedding theorem.

Proposition 2.13

(Sobolev Embedding)
.

Let
s>\frac{n}{2}
. Then for any
f\in H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\frac{x_{j}}{T}}\leq CK_{\theta,\max}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\lfloor\frac{x_{j}}{T}\rfloor},

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and some
C>0
independent of
f
. Hence,
f
is locally bounded and
H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Note that for
f\in H^{s}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, from the Fourier inversion formula we have that

\displaystyle|f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|e^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\text{Re}(\log(\theta_{j}))\frac{x_{j}}{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{-2s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\log(|\theta_{j}|)\frac{x_{j}}{T}}

\displaystyle=C\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\frac{x_{j}}{T}},

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where on the second line we applied the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. This prove the first inequality. Using the fact that
a=\lfloor a\rfloor+\{a\}
, for any real number
a
, where
\{a\}\in[0,1)
denotes the fractional part of
a
, we have that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\frac{x_{j}}{T}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\lfloor\frac{x_{j}}{T}\rfloor+\{\frac{x_{j}}{T}\}}\leq K_{\theta,\max}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\theta_{j}|^{\lfloor\frac{x_{j}}{T}\rfloor},

which, proves the second inequality.
∎

3.
Poincaré Inequality

The classical Poincaré inequality on the torus states that

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\geq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})},

for any
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with mean value zero. This can be restated as: for any
2\pi
-periodic
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
f,\nabla f\in L^{2}([0,2\pi]^{n})
and
\widehat{f}(0)=0
, we have that

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi]^{n})}\geq\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi]^{n})}.

We will now derive the analogue for
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions via the Fourier analysis developed in Section
2.1
.

Theorem 3.1

(Poincaré inequality for
(\theta,T)
-periodic functions)
.

Let
T>0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}
. For any
f\in H^{1}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\widehat{f}(\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi})=0
whenever
\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have that

(3.1)

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi}{T}\left\|\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\text{ if }\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\
\frac{2\pi}{T}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\text{ if }\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}=-\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\end{cases}

where
\|\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
denotes the distance from
\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}
to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

First note that the norm in
L_{\theta,T}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is given by the inner product

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\langle\Omega_{\theta,T}f,\Omega_{\theta,T}g\rangle_{L^{2}([0,2\pi]^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}e^{-x\cdot{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}}}f(\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}x)\overline{e^{-x\cdot{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}}}g(\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}x)}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}e^{-2x\cdot{\frac{\text{Re}(\log(\theta))}{T}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}e^{-2x\cdot{\frac{\log(|\theta|)}{T}}}dx.

Note that the family of functions
\{e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}:\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
is orthonormal with respect to this inner product, since for
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have that

\displaystyle\langle e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})},e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi^{\prime}-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}e^{-2x\cdot{\frac{\log(|\theta|)}{T}}}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}e^{x\cdot{\frac{(\log(\theta)+\overline{\log(\theta)})}{T}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}e^{-2x\cdot{\frac{\log(|\theta|)}{T}}}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}e^{2x\cdot{\frac{\log(|\theta|)}{T}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{n}}\int_{[0,T]^{n}}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}dx=\delta_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}},

where
\delta_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}
denotes the Kronecker delta. Therefore if
\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have that

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle={\langle\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle}_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\partial_{x_{j}}f(x),\partial_{x_{j}}f(x)\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Bigg\langle\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Big(\frac{2\pi i}{T}\xi_{j}+\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{T}\Big)\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})},

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\phantom{\sum}\phantom{\Bigg\langle}\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Big(\frac{2\pi i}{T}\xi_{j}^{\prime}+\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{T}\Big)\widehat{f}(\xi^{\prime})e^{i\frac{2\pi}{T}x\cdot(\xi^{\prime}-i{\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}})}\Bigg\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left\|i\xi+\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\right\|^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^{2}\left\|\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^{2}\left\|\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

where on the last line we applied Proposition
2.8
.
On the other hand, if
\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}=-\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
|\theta_{j}|=1
, for every
j=1,\dots,n
and by a similar argument

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\{-\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^{2}\left\|i\xi+\frac{\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\right\|^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\{-\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\}}\left\|\xi+\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\right\|^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

where on the last line we applied Proposition
2.8
once again, and also the fact that
|\theta_{j}|=1
, for every
j=1,\dots,n
.
∎

Note that in Theorem
3.1
, the subspace where the inequality holds is given by the vanishing of possibly one Fourier coefficient. This generalizes the periodic case, since the periodic case corresponds to
\theta=(1,\dots,1)
, so
\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}=0\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and the subspace considered is given by all
f\in H^{1}
satisfying
0=\widehat{f}(0)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{n}}f(x)dx
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
T>0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
with
|\theta_{j}|=1,\,j=1,\dots,n
. For any
f\in H^{1}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\widehat{f}(-\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi})=0
whenever
\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have that

(3.2)

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{n})}\geq\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi}{T}\left\|\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{n})}&\text{ if }\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\
\frac{2\pi}{T}\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{n})}&\text{ if }\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.\end{cases}

where
\|\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
denotes the distance from
\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}
to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Remark 6
.

Note that in the results above, one can verify that the constants
\|\frac{i\log(\theta)}{2\pi}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
and
\|\frac{\operatorname{Arg}(\theta)}{2\pi}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
are independent on the choice of complex logarithm/argument function.

4.
Global regularity of continuous linear operators

In this section, motivated by vast literature in the context of periodic functions, we study global regularity of differential operators in the sense of global hypoellipticity and global solvability. We begin by proving a general result which connects these properties to the case of operators acting on periodic functions. First, we adapt the usual definitions of regularity to the
(\theta,T)
-periodic framework, as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P:C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a continuous linear operator. And consider by continuity
P:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
We say that
P
is globally hypoelliptic if for every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
Pu=f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that
u\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
P:C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a continuous linear operator. Denote by
{}^{t}P:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the transpose of
P
, given by

\langle{}^{t}Pu,f\rangle=\langle u,Pf\rangle,

for every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and also let

(\ker{}^{t}P)^{0}=\Big\{f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\langle u,f\rangle=0\text{ for every }u\in\ker{}^{t}P\Big\}.

We say that
P
is globally solvable if for every
f\in(\ker{}^{t}P)^{0}
, there exists
u\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
Pu=f
.

Given a continuous linear operator
P:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we will say that
P
is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is invariant under
P
(i.e.:
P(C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subset C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
)
and its restriction
P\equiv P|_{C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is globally hypoelliptic/solvable. By identifying
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
C^{\infty}_{\bar{1},2\pi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define
P
to be globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
in a similar way. Note that these coincide with the usual definitions on the periodic case.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, be such that
\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and consequently
\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}f
.

Proof.

Since
\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by Proposition
2.5
we have that

f=(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t})^{-1}\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f=(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f=\Omega_{\theta,T}\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
P:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a continuous linear operator such that
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is invariant under
P
. Then
P
is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if the operator
\tilde{P}=\Omega_{\theta,T}\circ P\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}
is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Proof.

First note that by the definition of
\tilde{P}
, the invariance of
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under
P
implies that
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is invariant under
\tilde{P}
. Moreover, we have that
\Omega_{\theta,T}\circ P=\tilde{P}\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}
,
and since
(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}|_{C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\Omega_{\theta,T}
and
\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}|_{C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}
as in Proposition
2.5
, by continuity we also conclude that

(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}\circ P=\tilde{P}\circ(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}
on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next suppose that
\tilde{P}
is globally hypoelliptic over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then if
Pu=f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{P}\circ(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}u=(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}\circ Pu=(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}f=\Omega_{\theta,T}f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),

where the last equality follows from Proposition
2.5
. We conclude that
(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1})^{t}u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and consequently
u\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by Lemma
4.3
. Therefore
P
is globally hypoelliptic over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Conversely, suppose that
P
is globally hypoelliptic over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Note that by bijectivity of the respective mappings, we also have that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ\tilde{P}=P\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}

and

P\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}\circ\tilde{P}.

Hence, if
\tilde{P}u=f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for some
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we have that

\displaystyle P\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}u=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}\circ\tilde{P}u=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}f=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Since
P
is globally hypoelliptic over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we conclude that
\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}u\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and thus
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
by Lemma
4.3
. Therefore
\tilde{P}
is globally hypoelliptic over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Now assume that
\tilde{P}
is globally solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and let
f\in(\ker{}^{t}P)^{0}
.
Then note that
\Omega_{\theta,T}f\in(\ker{}^{t}\tilde{P})^{0}
. Indeed, note that for any
u\in\ker{}^{t}\tilde{P}
, by (
4.1
) we have that

\displaystyle\langle{}^{t}P\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}u,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}u,Pg\rangle=\langle u,\Omega_{\theta,T}\circ Pg\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u,\tilde{P}\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}g\rangle=\langle{}^{t}\tilde{P}u,\Omega_{\theta,T}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0,\Omega_{\theta,T}g\rangle=0,

for any
g\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and hence
\Omega_{\theta,T}u\in\ker{}^{t}P
. But then for any
u\in\ker{}^{t}\tilde{P}
,

\langle u,\Omega_{\theta,T}f\rangle=\langle\Omega_{\theta,T}^{t}u,f\rangle=0,

so
\Omega_{\theta,T}f\in(\ker{}^{t}P)^{0}
, as claimed. Then since
\tilde{P}
is globally solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that
\tilde{P}u=\Omega_{\theta,T}f
, therefore

\displaystyle P\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}u

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ\tilde{P}u=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}f=f,

and so
P
is globally solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The converse (
P
globally solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
implies
\tilde{P}
globally solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
) is proven analogously.
∎

Next, we apply the previous results to study the explicit conditions for global hypoellipticity and global solvability of an important class of differential operators on the two-torus. More specifically, we consider vector fields with a zero order perturbation given by:

L=\partial_{x_{1}}+c(x_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}+q(x_{1},x_{2}),

where
c
and
q
are smooth complex-valued functions.

Initially, we consider operators with constant coefficients and using Theorem
4.4
, we give conditions for the operator to be globally hypoelliptic (resp. solvable).

To highlight important phenomena and improve the clarity of the proof, we consider three cases separately: constant coefficients, variable real-valued coefficients and variable complex-valued coefficients.

First we consider the case where the operator
L
has constant coefficients, that is,
L:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by

(4.2)

L=\partial_{x_{1}}+c\partial_{x_{2}}+q

where
c,q\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\theta\in{\mathbb{C}^{2}_{*}}
and
T>0
. The operator
L
given by (
4.2
) is globally hypoelliptic (resp. solvable) over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

The operator

\tilde{L}=\left[\partial_{x_{1}}+c\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+q\frac{T}{2\pi}\right]

is globally hypoelliptic (resp. solvable) over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

(2)

There exist
C,k>0
such that

\left|\xi_{1}+c\xi_{2}-i\left(\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+q\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)\right|\geq C(1+\|\xi\|^{2})^{-k},

for all but finitely many
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

(resp. for every
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\xi_{1}+c\xi_{2}-i\left(\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+q\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)\neq 0
).

Proof.

For equivalence
(1)
, by Theorem
4.4
it is enough to show that

{\frac{2\pi}{T}\tilde{L}}=\Omega_{\theta,T}\circ L\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1},

or equivalently,
\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ\frac{2\pi}{T}\tilde{L}=L\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}
.

Indeed, note that for
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{j}}\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}u(x)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{j}}\left[u({\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{T}x})e^{\log(\theta_{1})\frac{x_{1}}{T}+\log(\theta_{2})\frac{x_{2}}{T}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{T}\partial_{x_{j}}u({\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{T}x})e^{\log(\theta_{1})\frac{x_{1}}{T}+\log(\theta_{2})\frac{x_{2}}{T}}+\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{T}u(\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{T}x)e^{\log(\theta_{1})\frac{x_{1}}{T}+\log(\theta_{2})\frac{x_{2}}{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{T}\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\partial_{x_{j}}u(x)+\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\left[\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{T}u(x)\right]

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{T}\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\left[\partial_{x_{j}}u(x)+\frac{\log(\theta_{j})}{2\pi}u(x)\right],

for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
j=1,2
. Hence

\displaystyle L\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}u(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{T}\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\left[(\partial_{x_{1}}+c\partial_{x_{2}})u(x)+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}u(x)+q\frac{T}{2\pi}u(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ\textstyle{\frac{2\pi}{T}}\tilde{L}u(x).

In other words,
L\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ\frac{2\pi}{T}L_{\theta,T}
, as claimed.

Finally, the proof of the equivalence between
(1)
and
(2)
can be found in
[
9
]
and several other references, as it comes from the fact that the mapping
\xi\mapsto i\left(\xi_{1}+c\xi_{2}-i\left(\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+q\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)\right)
corresponds to the symbol of the operator
\tilde{L}
.
∎

The previous proposition together with the results in
[
9
]
, yields the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
L
be the constant coefficients differential operator given by

L=\partial_{x_{1}}+c\partial_{x_{2}}+q,

where
c,q\in\mathbb{C}
. If
\text{Im}(c)\neq 0
, then
L
is globally hypoelliptic and globally solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Moreover, if
q\in-\frac{(\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})}{T}+\frac{2\pi i}{T}(\mathbb{Z}+c\mathbb{Z})
, then
L
is globally hypoelliptic over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
\text{Im}(c)\neq 0
or
c\in\mathbb{R}
is an irrational non-Liouville number, and globally solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
\text{Im}(c)\neq 0
or
c\in\mathbb{R}
is either rational or an irrational non-Liouville number.

Note that if
q=0
, the condition
q\in-\frac{(\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})}{T}+\frac{2\pi i}{T}(\mathbb{Z}+c\mathbb{Z})
becomes
\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})\allowbreak\in{2\pi i}(\mathbb{Z}+c\mathbb{Z})
, which is equivalent to
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}=1
and we recover the periodic case.

Remark 7
.

Note that condition
(2)
in Proposition
4.5
does not depend on which complex logarithm function was chosen: a different choice
\log^{\prime}
would satisfy
\log^{\prime}(\theta_{j})=\log(\theta_{j})+2\pi ik
,
j=1,2
, for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, therefore

\xi_{1}+c\xi_{2}-i\left(\frac{\log^{\prime}(\theta_{1})+c\log^{\prime}(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+q\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)=(\xi_{1}+k)+c(\xi_{2}+k)-i\left(\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+q\frac{T}{2\pi}\right),

hence note that a change of variables
\xi^{\prime}=\xi+(k,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
yields the exact same conditions. Similarly, it is clear that the conditions in Corollary
4.6

also do not depend on the choice of branch of logarithm considered. The same holds for the remaining results in this section.

Next we consider first order differential operators with variable coefficients. Since
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}/(T\mathbb{Z})^{2})
-module, we shall assume that all coefficients are
T
-periodic. This guarantees that
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is invariant under the action of these operators.

With this in mind, consider the differential operator
L:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by

(4.4)

L=\partial_{x_{1}}+a(x_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}+q(x_{1},x_{2})

where where
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R})
and
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}/(T\mathbb{Z})^{2},\mathbb{C})
. Also set
a_{0}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}a(x_{1})dx_{1}
and
q_{0}=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}q(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
L
be the operator given by (
4.4
).
The operator
L
is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

The operator

\tilde{L}=\partial_{x_{1}}+a({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+a({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}q({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1},{\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{2})

is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

(2)

The operator

\tilde{L}_{0}=\partial_{x_{1}}+a_{0}\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+a({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}q({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1},{\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{2})

is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

Moreover: if
q
depends only on the first variable, or
a_{0}
is an irrational non-Liouville number, any of the following equivalent conditions are also necessary and sufficient for
L
to be globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
:

(1)

The operator

\tilde{L}_{00}=\partial_{x_{1}}+a_{0}\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+a_{0}\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}q_{0}

is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

(2)

The operator

L_{00}=\partial_{x_{1}}+a_{0}\partial_{x_{2}}+q_{0}

is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

(3)

There exist
C,k>0
such that

\left|\xi_{1}+a_{0}\xi_{2}-i\left(\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+a_{0}\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}q_{0}\right)\right|\geq C(1+\|\xi\|^{2})^{-k},

for all but finitely many
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

(resp. for every
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\xi_{1}+a_{0}\xi_{2}-i\left(\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+a_{0}\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}q_{0}\right)\neq 0
).

Proof.

First note that
\tilde{L}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, so as in the proof of Proposition
4.5
, for equivalence
(1)
it is enough to show that
\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ{\frac{2\pi}{T}}\tilde{L}=L\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}
. Indeed, using
4.3
, we have that

\displaystyle L\circ\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}u(x)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ\partial_{x_{1}}u(x)+\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\left[\frac{\log(\theta_{1})}{T}u(x)\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+c(x_{1})\left(\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ\partial_{x_{2}}u(x)+\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\left[\frac{\log(\theta_{2})}{T}u(x)\right]\right)+q(x_{1},x_{2})\cdot\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}u(x)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\left[\partial_{x_{1}}u(x)+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})}{T}u(x)+a({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}u(x)+a({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})\frac{\log(\theta_{2})}{T}u(x)\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\left.\vphantom{\frac{1}{T}}+q({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1},{\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{2})u(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\theta,T}^{-1}\circ{\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{T}}\tilde{L}.

The equivalence between
(1)
and
(2)
follows from a classical conjugation induced by the mapping
\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\mapsto e^{i\int_{0}^{t}a(s)ds-a_{0}s}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)
, for every
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{Z}
,
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, using that

(4.5)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}a({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})dx_{1}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}a(x_{1})dx_{1}.

The equivalence between
(1)
and
(A)
follows from results in
[
21
]
and
[
11
,
12
]
, with some minor adaptations, and from the analogous of formula (
4.5
) for
q
. See also Propositions
A.1
and
A.2
for more details. Equivalence between
(A)
,
(B)
and
(C)
follows from Proposition
4.5
.
∎

Finally, we consider the case

(4.6)

L=\partial_{x_{1}}+c(x_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}+q(x_{1},x_{2}),

where both
c\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
,
c(x_{1})=a(x_{1})+ib(x_{1})
, and
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
are
T
-periodic. Also set
a_{0}
and
q_{0}
as before, and
c_{0}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}c(x_{1})dx_{1}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

The operator
L
given by (
4.6
), is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

The operator

\tilde{L}=\partial_{x_{1}}+c({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}q({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1},{\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{2})

is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

(2)

The operator

\tilde{L}_{0}=\partial_{x_{1}}+(a_{0}+ib({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1}))\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1})\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}q({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1},{\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{2})

is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

(3)

If
q
depends only on the first variable, the operator

\tilde{L}_{00}=\partial_{x_{1}}+(a_{0}+ib({\textstyle\frac{T}{2\pi}}x_{1}))\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{\log(\theta_{1})+c_{0}\log(\theta_{2})}{2\pi}+{\frac{T}{2\pi}}q_{0}

is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

Moreover, if
b\not\equiv 0
and
b
does not change sign, then the operator
L
is globally hypoelliptic over
C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

The proof of equivalences
(1)
and
(2)
and
(3)
, follows analogous to the proof of Proposition (
4.7
). The last claim follows from
(1)
and
[

, Proposition 3.1]
, or alternatively, from Proposition

.
∎

Appendix A
Global regularity for a class of first order differential operators on the torus

The goal of this section is to present results on global regularity of a class of differential operators on the torus. These results have already been proven, but in more general or slightly distinct settings, hence we chose to include their statements and proofs for the sake of clarity and completeness.

The proof of this first proposition is an adaptation of the proof of
[
21
, Proposition 5.1.2]
.

Proposition A.1
.

Consider the operator
\tilde{L}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
given by

\tilde{L}=\partial_{t}+c(t)\partial_{x}+q(t),

where
c,q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{1})
,
c(t)=a(t)+ib(t)
. Then
L
is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
if and only if

\tilde{L}_{0}=\partial_{t}+(a_{0}+ib(t))\partial_{x}+q_{0}

is globally hypoelliptic/solvable over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, where

{a_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}a(t)dt\,\text{ and }\,q_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}q(t)dt.}

Proof.

Define
\Psi:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
by

(A.1)

\Psi u(t,x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{Q(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)e^{i\xi A(t)}e^{i\xi x},

where

{A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(\tau)d\tau-a_{0}t\,\text{ and }\,Q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q(\tau)d\tau-q_{0}t,}

for every
t\in\mathbb{T}^{1}

(\theta,T)

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\theta\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\},\,T>0
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.
If
\theta\neq e^{-T\lambda}
, then the equation

(B.1)

\partial_{x}u(x)+\lambda u(x)=f(x),

where
f\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R})
admits unique
(\theta,T)
-periodic solution
u\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R})
given by

(B.2)

u(x)=\frac{1}{1-\theta^{-1}e^{-T\lambda}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda s}f(x-s)ds,

or equivalently by

(B.3)

u(x)=\frac{1}{\theta e^{T\lambda}-1}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda s}f(x+s)ds,

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand, if
\theta=e^{-T\lambda}
, then (
B.1
) admits (infinitely many)
(\theta,T)
-periodic solutions
u_{c}\in C^{\infty}_{\theta,T}(\mathbb{R})
given by

(B.4)

u_{c}(x)=ce^{-\lambda x}+e^{-\lambda x}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds,

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
c\in\mathbb{C}
if and only if

(B.5)

\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds=0.

Proof.

First assume that
\theta\neq e^{-T\lambda}
. We verify directly that
u
given by (
B.2
) satisfies (
B.1
). Indeed, note that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1-\theta^{-1}e^{-T\lambda}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda s}\partial_{s}f(x-s)ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1-\theta^{-1}e^{-T\lambda}}\left\{\left[e^{-T\lambda}f(x-2\pi)-f(x)\right]+\lambda\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda s}f(x-s)ds\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1-\theta^{-1}e^{-T\lambda}}\left\{(\theta^{-1}e^{-T\lambda}-1)f(x)\right\}-\lambda u(x)

\displaystyle=f(x)-\lambda u(x),

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Also notice that since
f
is
(\theta,T)
-periodic, it follows that
u
is also
(\theta,T)
-periodic and clearly

is continuous, since both
u
and
f
are continuous. The proof that (
B.3
) also defines a
(\theta,T)
-periodic solution is similar.

To verify that both formulas are equivalent, notice that one can obtain (
B.3
) from (
B.2
) performing the change of variables
s\mapsto-s^{\prime}+T
.

To see that such solution is unique, suppose that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are both smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic solutions to (
B.1
). Then
u=u_{1}-u_{2}
defines a smooth
(\theta,T)
-periodic solutions to the differential equation

\partial_{x}u(x)+\lambda u(x)=0.

Since
\theta\neq e^{-T\lambda}
, it is easy to verify that the
(\theta,T)
-periodic solution to this ODE is
u\equiv 0
and therefore
u_{1}=u_{2}
, proving the uniqueness claim.

Next, assume that
\theta=e^{-T\lambda}
. Note that differentiating (
B.4
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u(x)

\displaystyle=-\lambda Ce^{-\lambda x}-\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds+e^{-\lambda x}e^{\lambda x}f(x)

\displaystyle=-\lambda\left(Ce^{-\lambda x}+e^{-\lambda x}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds\right)+f(x)

\displaystyle=-\lambda u(x)+f(x),

therefore
u
given by (
B.4
) solves (
B.1
). Also,

\displaystyle u(x+T)

\displaystyle=Ce^{-\lambda x}e^{-T\lambda}+e^{-\lambda x}e^{-T\lambda}\int_{0}^{x+T}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds

\displaystyle=e^{-T\lambda}\left(Ce^{-\lambda x}+e^{-\lambda x}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds+e^{-\lambda x}\int_{T}^{x+T}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds\right)

\displaystyle=\theta\left(Ce^{-\lambda x}+e^{-\lambda x}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\lambda(s^{\prime}+T)}f(s^{\prime}+T)ds^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\theta\left(Ce^{-\lambda x}+e^{-\lambda x}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\lambda s^{\prime}}\theta^{-1}\theta f(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}\right)+\theta e^{-\lambda x}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds

\displaystyle=\theta u(x)+\theta e^{-\lambda x}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds,

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where on the second to last line we used the fact that

and that
f
is
(\theta,T)
-periodic. Hence, we conclude that
u
is
(\theta,T)
-periodic if and only if
\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda s}f(s)ds=0
, as claimed. The proof that these are the only possible solutions follows from the simple verification that the only smooth

-periodic solutions to

are given by

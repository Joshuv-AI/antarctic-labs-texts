Low-energy magnons in the altermagnet α \alpha -MnTe
K. Yu. Povarov, J. Wosnitza, S. Rößler, M. Schmidt, A. A. Tsirlin, S. A. Zvyagin

Abstract
We report high-field electron spin resonance studies of the altermagnetic material α \alpha -MnTe. In magnetic fields applied parallel to the triangular Mn 2+ layers we observed a single resonance line, corresponding to an antiferromagnetic resonance (AFMR) mode. The resonance fields of this excitation exhibit an isotropic behavior with g eff = 2.01 g_{\mathrm{eff}}=2.01 , which is close to the free-electron g g -factor value and agrees with the absence of orbital momenta for the Mn 2+ ions. At low temperatures, the AFMR mode is remarkably sharp ( ∼ 50 \sim 50 mT for the full width at the half-maximum). This mode exhibits a noticeable broadening with increasing temperature, indicating the enhanced effect of magnon-magnon interactions. Based on this behavior, we estimate the strength of these interactions.



The recently introduced concept of altermagnetism

In ordinary antiferromagnets translation or inversion symmetry connects the opposite-spin sublattices. However, in the case of altermagnets rotational or mirror transformation are neccessary.
Thus, altermagnetic materials feature a different type of symmetry breaking compared to conventional antiferromagnets.
Novel possibilities opened by altermagnetism, such as band splitting in absence of macroscopic magnetization, hold promise for THz applications, and the chase for finding new altermagnetic materials is ongoing

The lack of information on the low-energy dynamics in applied field has motivated us to investigate
\alpha
-MnTe by means of electron spin resonance (ESR) spectroscopy. We have experimentally tracked the antiferromagnetic resonance (AFMR) mode, corresponding to a pseudo-Goldstone spin wave at the zone center, in a wide range of magnetic fields [within the easy
(0,0,1)
plane] and temperatures. The most important finding is the
h\nu/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
scaling behavior of the AFMR linewidth, revealing that the resonance damping is proportional to the magnon population. That allows us to obtain a deeper insight into magnon-magnon interaction processes and to estimate the effective interaction constant.

The temperature-dependent changes of the
\alpha
-MnTe spin dynamics can be understood in terms of magnon-magnon interactions. For the spin-wave mode of interest (
\nu_{1}
), the magnon population is given by the Bose–Einstein distribution

n_{\mathbf{k}}=\dfrac{1}{\exp(\frac{\hbar\omega_{\mathbf{k}}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T})-1},

(3)

where
\mathbf{k}
is the quasi-momentum, and
\hbar\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\simeq\sqrt{(g\mu_{0}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}H)^{2}+(\hbar uk)^{2}}
is the magnon dispersion and
u
is the spin-wave velocity. In AFMR experiments, we probe
k\simeq 0
magnons specifically, as the light wavelength is very large compared to the lattice parameters.

With the temperature-assisted population growth, one would expect an increase of the collision rate and quasiparticle lifetime shortening. In that case, the resonance linewidth is a direct measure of the magnon energy
\hbar\omega_{\mathbf{k}}
smearing around the mean value. We find that in a broad temperature range the measured linewidth at different resonance frequencies
\nu
is proportional to the number of magnons with
k=0
:

\displaystyle g\mu_{0}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\Delta H(T)=\Gamma_{0}n_{0}(T),

(4)

\displaystyle n_{0}=\dfrac{1}{\exp(\frac{h\nu}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T})-1}.

Here, the only fit parameter is the effective “damping rate”
\Gamma_{0}
, characterizing the strength of magnon-magnon interaction among the
k=0
species of
\nu_{1}
. Equation (
4
) implies that the linewidth is universally determined by the
{h\nu}/{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}
ratio. Indeed, the data at different frequencies overlap if plotted this way, as demonstrated in Fig.
5
. We find that with
\Gamma_{0}/k_{\mathrm{B}}=27(5)
mK the experimental data above
30
K can be described very well for the three frequencies we used. Below this temperature, the linewidth is constant, and some other mechanisms (with characteristic energy
\sim 70
mK corresponding to the
0.05
T linewidth) dominate over magnon-magnon collisions. In principle, this energy scale is on par with the expected magnetic dipole-dipole interaction in
\alpha
-MnTe. On the other hand, the presence of impurities and lattice imperfections might also be a factor determining the linewidth at low temperatures. The relative strength of this residual damping is proportional to the imperfection concentration.
In the present case, residual damping is only
\sim 10^{-3}
of the leading exchange interaction
J_{c}/k_{\mathrm{B}}=46.3
K. At the highest probed freqiencies the ratio
\Delta H/H
reaches
4\cdot 10^{-3}
in
\alpha
-MnTe. This allows us to make a statement on the remarkable quality of the measured samples. This is an important issue for a material that might be of interest for GHz and THz applications.

The linewidth in
\alpha
-MnTe seems to be determined solely by the interactions between the long-wavelength spin waves in a practically relevant range of temperatures. The effective interaction constant is just a tiny fraction of the overall energy scale of
\alpha
-MnTe. The ratio
\Gamma_{0}/k_{\mathrm{B}}T_{\mathrm{N}}\simeq 10^{-4}
appears to be roughly two orders of magnitude smaller here, compared to what can be crudely inferred from the linewidth data in the other model Mn
2+
antiferromagnets, such as K
2
MnF
4
and Rb
2
MnF
4

. However, this is just a very approximate estimate, as in the latter materials the contributions of magnons with
k\neq 0
and higher-order magnon processes also need to be taken into account
Rezende and White 1976
.

The key findings of our study can be summarized as follows.
We have investigated the low-energy dynamics in
\alpha
-MnTe in magnetic fields, applied along the triangular Mn planes. We revealed that this dynamics is determined by the pseudo-Goldstone mode, with nearly linear frequency-field dependence, at least down to
30
GHz. No sizable in-plane anisotropy is evident at these frequencies. The temperature-dependent measurements allow us to access magnon-magnon interaction effects. Above
30
K, the ESR linewidth seems to be universally determined by the zone-center magnon population factor. From that we can estimate the effective magnon-magnon interaction parameter
\Gamma_{0}/k_{\mathrm{B}}\simeq 27
mK.

RepFed-Net: privacy-preserving federated learning with reputation-aware aggregation for retinal vessel segmentation using enhanced U-Net
Authors: Avirneni Veda Sri, Mahesh Kumar Morampudi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate retinal blood vessel segmentation is essential for the early diagnosis of vision-threatening diseases such as diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma, and retinal vein occlusion. Although deep learning–based segmentation models have achieved promising performance, centralized training approaches can compromise data privacy and may risk violating medical data protection regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR. Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training without sharing raw data; however, existing FL-based segmentation methods still face challenges related to privacy leakage during parameter exchange, robustness to unreliable client updates, and limited architectural capability for capturing fine vascular structures. To address these challenges, we propose RepFed-Net, a privacy-preserving federated learning framework with reputation-aware aggregation for retinal vessel segmentation. RepFed-Net is built upon an enhanced U-Net architecture that integrates Inception modules for multi-scale feature extraction, Residual connections for stable gradient propagation, Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks for adaptive channel recalibration, and Pyramid Attention for modeling long-range contextual dependencies. This unified architectural design improves the continuity and recovery of thin and low-contrast vascular structures, leading to more accurate segmentation outcomes. To preserve privacy during collaborative training, RepFed-Net incorporates the CKKS homomorphic encryption scheme, enabling secure transmission and aggregation of model parameters directly in the encrypted domain under an honest-but-curious server. In addition, a reputation-aware aggregation strategy adaptively weights encrypted client updates based on smoothed validation F1-scores, improving robustness against noisy or low-quality client contributions without exposing raw data or plaintext model parameters. Experimental results demonstrate that RepFed-Net achieves 95.65% segmentation accuracy on the Retinal Blood Vessel dataset and 96.35% accuracy on the DRIVE dataset, consistently outperforming existing U-Net variants and conventional federated learning approaches. Overall, RepFed-Net provides a unified framework that enhances segmentation performance while offering design-level privacy preservation and robustness suitable for collaborative medical image analysis.Subject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Retinal vessel segmentation is essential in the identification of eye disease such as Diabetic Retinopathy (DR), Retinal Vein Occlusion, and Glaucoma1. Accurate segmentation of retinal vessels helps in early disease detection and improves clinical decision-making. However, manual vessel segmentation is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and prone to human errors, thus motivating the use of automated deep learning (DL)–based approaches2. In recent years, DL models have achieved promising results in medical image analysis. Nevertheless, their success typically depends on access to large and diverse datasets, which are often difficult to obtain in medical settings due to strict privacy regulations such as the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)3,4. Moreover, centralized training approaches raise concerns related to data breaches, regulatory compliance, and communication overhead. Federated Learning (FL), proposed by Google in 20165, tackles these challenges and allows multiple organizations to collaboratively train a shared global model without sharing raw data6. FL keeps the patient data local, thus reducing privacy concerns and at the same time allows the models to leverage the diversity of data from multiple institutions. Federated Learning can be categorized into horizontal, vertical, and federated transfer learning paradigms. Among these, horizontal FL is particularly well suited for multi-institutional healthcare applications, as it enables collaborative model training across different institutions that share similar feature spaces while keeping patient data locally, thereby preserving data privacy and complying with regulatory constraints7,8.

However, FL does not completely remove privacy risks. Model parameters transmitted during training may also leak sensitive information, which makes the privacy protection a critical issue9. To reduce this risk, a number of privacy-preserving methods have been investigated, such as Differential Privacy (DP)10, Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMC)11, and Homomorphic Encryption (HE)12. Differential Privacy keeps the privacy by adding the noise to the model updates13,14 though it may slightly reduce accuracy15,16, while SMC enables secure joint computation but introduces significant computational overhead11. Homomorphic Encryption is enabled by performing calculations directly on the ciphertext, so that the model parameters that have been transmitted and the aggregated global model both remain private17. HE schemes can be categorized as Partially Homomorphic Encryption (PHE), Somewhat Homomorphic Encryption (SHE), and Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE), according to the operations they support18. Among these, FHE offers the strongest functionality by supporting arbitrary arithmetic operations on encrypted data. Among FHE schemes, the Cheon–Kim–Kim–Song (CKKS) scheme, which enables efficient approximate arithmetic over the real numbers, is well-suited for federated medical imaging applications19. The security of the CKKS scheme is based on the hardness of the Ring Learning With Errors problem (RLWE), which is considered to be hard and is also the underlying assumption for post-quantum cryptographic security20. CKKS enables secure aggregation of encrypted model updates without requiring decryption at the server side, providing a trade-off between privacy protection and computation efficiency21,22. In this work, we adopt an honest-but-curious server threat model, in which the server follows the protocol correctly. Since all model parameters are encrypted using CKKS, aggregation is performed entirely in the encrypted domain, preventing the server from accessing plaintext model information.

While privacy-preserving collaboration is essential, segmentation performance remains strongly dependent on network architecture. U-Net is one of the most widely adopted models for medical image segmentation due to its encoder–decoder structure and skip connections, which effectively combine spatial and semantic information23. Numerous extensions, including U-Net++24, U-Net3+25, Attention U-Net, Residual U-Net, MultiResidualUNet, Recurrent Residual U-Net (R2U-Net)26, and transformer-based models such as TransformerUNet27 and Swin-UNet28, have been proposed to improve feature representation and training stability. However, the conventional architecture has the limitation of being difficult to find thin and low-contrast vascular structures. Moreover, in collaborative federated learning settings, standard aggregation methods such as Federated Averaging (FedAvg) assign equal importance to all participating clients, making the training process sensitive to noisy, low-quality, or unreliable updates. Such challenges motivate the development of a robust and privacy-preserving segmentation framework that would be well-suited for collaborative medical scenario.

Related work

In recent years, deep learning methods have gained significant attention for retinal vessel segmentation due to their effectiveness in learning complex features for accurate segmentation. Among existing deep learning approaches, U-Net is the most commonly adopted backbone for retinal vessel segmentation, originally proposed by Olaf et al.29 showed that U-Net, with proper data augmentation and annotation strategies, consistently outperforms several existing segmentation models. It achieved record performance on the ISBI neuronal structure segmentation and the 2015 Cell Tracking Challenge, while delivering high segmentation efficiency on modern Graphics Processing Unit. Yicheng W. et al.30 introduced Network Followed by Network (NFN+), to better extract multi-scale information and make full use of deep feature maps. Zhuo et al.31 proposed U-Net based retinal vessel segmentation using Generative Adversarial Network with weighted feature-matching loss and obtained the best results on DRIVE, CHASE-DB1 and STARE datasets in terms of F1-score, accuracy, Area Under the Curve (AUC) - Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve, however it still produces a relatively large number of false positives. Zhao et al.32 addressed the problem of thin vessel detection via a tri-map scheme, in which the pixels are classified into three groups: definite vessel (foreground), definite non-vessel (background), and uncertain. This approach improved the segmentation of narrow and fine vessels. Seo et al.33 proposed FedUNet, a federated learning framework that attaches a U-Net-inspired additive module to client backbones. By sharing only the compact bottleneck features, FedUNet enables efficient knowledge transfer across heterogeneous clients with low communication cost. Experiments with Visual Geometry Group (VGG) variants showed that FedUNet achieved 93.11% accuracy and 92.68% in its compact form, requiring only 0.89 MB communication overhead. Galdran et al.34 pointed out that retinal vessel segmentation does not need to be tackled with over-complicated methods. They demonstrated that relatively simple models when well trained can achieve competitive results with more complex approaches. They further suggest to concentrate future work on contemporary, high resolution datasets, rather than obsolete ones. Md Tauhidul Islam et al. DEFFA-Unet, a dual-encoder U-Net with Feature Filtering Fusion (FFF) and the Reconstructing Fusion (RF) modules, and also a novel augmentation and balancing techniques to tackle the issues of the limited data, imbalance, and false positives in the retinal vessel segmentation application35. Evaluated on DRIVE, CHASEDB1, STARE, HRF, and IOSTAR, it surpasses state-of-the-art approaches with outstanding accuracy, Dice, Area Under the Curve (AUC), MCC, and solid cross-domain generalization.

Paranjape et al.36 introduced a black-box FL framework, called BlackFed for semantic segmentation that does not disclose gradients or model weights to enhance privacy. This method includes zeroth-order optimization on the client, first-order optimization on the server, and employs a hashmap approach to avoid catastrophic forgetting. The results on CamVid, ISIC, Cityscapes, and PolypGen show consistent mean Intersection over Union (IoU) improvement over the baselines, indicating that BlackFed attains white-box FL performance with stronger privacy guarantees. Xu et al.37 introduce FedCross, an aggregation-free federated learning framework that sequentially trains a global model over clients to address Not Independent and Identically Distributed (non-IID) challenges in medical image segmentation. They further proposed Federated Cross-device Ensemble (FedCrossEns), which integrates this scheme with ensemble learning to improve the accuracy and uncertainty estimation. Experiments on multiple prostate MRI datasets using a 3D U-Net showed that FedCross outperformed Federated Averaging (FedAvg), Federated Proximal (FedProx), and Federated Learning with Local Batch Normalization (FedBN), while FedCrossEns achieved performance close to centralized training. Zhuo Xiang et al.38 proposed a Multi-Attention Guided UNet (MAUNet) for thyroid nodule segmentation, integrating a Multi-Scale Cross Attention (MSCA) module for feature extraction and a Dual Attention (DA) module in skip connections to improve feature fusion.

On multi-center data from seventeen hospitals, using a FL framework on ultrasound, their model achieved Dice scores of 90.8%, 91.2%, and 88.7%, which indicated good generalization and better performance compared to other methods. Godavarthi et al.39 introduced Federated Sequential Training (FedStv), a privacy preserving FL system for image segmentation in aerial view of forests. Different from classical methods that perform training in parallel over clients, FedStv conducts training of models at different clients sequentially with server-side averaging, which advances generalization and diminishes uncertainty. On the Amazon and Atlantic Forest aerial datasets with U-Net and DeepLabV3+, FedStv obtained best results – 92.46% Dice and 86.32% IoU with U-Net and 89.96% Dice and 85.9% IoU with DeepLabV3+, outperforming FedAvg and Federated Independent training (Fed-Ind) baselines on each and every metric. This is an efficient method, but is dependent on client availability, which can lead to delays in training. FedStv has promising segmentation accuracy, communication efficiency and generalizability in the context of environmental monitoring applications.

Most of the existing works on retinal vessel segmentation in FL are based on U-Net with conventional attention mechanisms40,41. While these methods improve local feature representation, they often exhibit limited global contextual awareness, which leads to incomplete segmentation of thin, branching, or low-contrast vessels. In addition, most federated learning frameworks rely on simple aggregation methods, such as FedAvg6, without considering reputation-aware mechanisms. This allows unreliable client updates to affect the global model, causing degraded segmentation performance, training instability, and increased vulnerability to model poisoning due to heterogeneity in client data quality.

To address these limitations, recent studies have explored hybrid and optimized deep learning architectures that integrate multiple complementary components to improve feature representation and training stability in complex vision tasks. Prior studies42,43 demonstrate that combining multiple architectural modules enables enhanced multi-scale learning and improved robustness compared to single-component designs.

Methodology

Figure 1 illustrates the workflow of the proposed privacy-preserving FL framework for retinal blood vessel segmentation. The architecture comprises a central server (S) and multiple distributed clients (see in Table 1) where each client having its own local dataset (D), simulating a multi-institutional healthcare environment. Our pipeline starts with data preprocessing (resizing and normalization) to ensure stable training. Data augmentation is used to increase the variety and balance the class distribution. Then the server transmits the initial global model to each client and every client trains a segmentation model locally with its own private data. After training, each client evaluates its model on a validation set and computes the F1-score. Both the F1-score and the encrypted model parameters are then sent to the server, ensuring that sensitive medical information remains private during communication. Our framework employs a reputation-aware aggregation strategy, where each client’s contribution to the global model is weighted according to its reported validation performance. The server aggregates the encrypted model updates directly, thereby integrating reputation-based weighting with secure computation to preserve data privacy. After the aggregations, the encrypted global model is sent back to the clients and each client decrypts it independently with its private key and consider it as the local model for the next training round. This process is repeated until convergence, enabling collaborative learning with privacy-preserving mechanisms at the design level, improved robustness to untrusted clients, and support compliance with regulatory requirements. Dataset description

Retinal blood vessel dataset

The dataset used in this study comprises retinal fundus images along with their corresponding manually annotated vessel masks, obtained from publicly available retinal blood vessel segmentation dataset44. As shown in Fig. 2, the dataset is organized into separate training and testing folders. Each folder contains two subfolders: one for the original retinal images and another for the ground truth vessel masks. The training set includes 80 images and 80 corresponding masks, while the testing set includes 20 images and 20 masks.

DRIVE dataset

The Digital Retinal Images for Vessel Extraction (DRIVE) dataset45 consists of 40 color fundus images, equally divided into training and test sets. The images were captured using a Canon CR5 non-mydriatic Three-Charge Coupled Device (3CCD) fundus camera with a field of view and have a resolution of pixels. Each image includes expert-provided binary vessel mask from one expert, and a field-of-view mask that corresponds to approximately 69% of the image. The test set is also annotated by a second expert, and these annotations serve to evaluate inter-observer agreement and to provide a benchmark for segmentation performance.

Dataset preprocessing

In a federated learning setting, effective preprocessing is required for retinal blood vessel segmentation to ensure consistent input across clients. Without proper preprocessing, these inconsistencies can result in errors and slow training. The models are hard to adapt to varying dimensions and the computation cost is increased, which may result in performance degradation. To address these issues, all retinal images and vessel masks are resampled to pixels, and pixel values are normalized to the range [0, 1] to enhance training stability.

Data augmentation

Training only on preprocessed data often causes bias which leads to overfit, as medical image datasets are typically small due to privacy regulations and data-sharing constraints. A small dataset restricts the model’s exposure to variations in vessel patterns, lighting, and imaging conditions, which can reduce its ability to generalize to unseen data. Prior work supports the use of data augmentation when dealing with limited medical datasets46,47. To overcome this, data augmentation was used to increase the diversity of the training samples. Standard augmentation techniques, including horizontal and vertical flipping, random rotation (within ), and shear transformation (within ), were applied to both the retinal images and their corresponding masks. Retina blood vessel dataset

A total of 80 original retinal images were used. Data augmentation was performed in a sequence manner to obtain an extra 1,840 samples, providing 1,920 images and corresponding masks. The original image was augmented as follows: 3 times with horizontal flipping (240 images) 1 time with shear transformation (on 240 images to get 480), one time with vertical flipping (on 480 images to get 960) and finally with rotational transformations (on 960 images to get 1920). Overall, 2,000 samples with 80 originals and 1,920 are augmented. This pipeline demonstrates better generalization and better segmentation performance on unseen data.

DRIVE dataset

The DRIVE dataset contains 40 retinal fundus images. To build a large enough training set, data augmentation are performed, resulting 2,560 additional sample for a total of 2,600 images with corresponding vessel masks. Each original image underwent the following augmentation process: horizontal flipping was used four times to obtain 160 images, and then shear transformations were applied on the images to this samples to generate 320 images. Vertical flipping was then applied to 640 images, and then rotational transformations were applied on the dataset to 2,560 augmented samples. Together with the 40 original images, we now have a total of 2,600 images-mask pairs. This augmentation improves the generalization of the model and makes the segmentation of retinal vessels more robust to unseen data sets.

Distribution of data among clients

Our federated learning framework simulates a multi-institutional setting by distributing the datasets across three clients using an Independent and Identically Distributed (IID) strategy. Each client is allocated an equal number of retinal image–mask pairs–666 for the main dataset and 866 for the DRIVE dataset–ensuring balanced participation in federated training. When the number of clients varies, the data are redistributed in an IID manner to maintain equal data proportions per client, thereby promoting training stability and reducing bias in the global model.

Local model training

Following preprocessing and data augmentation, RepFed-Net, a modified U-Net–based architecture, is employed for retinal vessel segmentation (Fig. 1b). Recent studies have shown that optimized deep learning architectures can significantly enhance feature representation and training stability in complex learning tasks48. Motivated by this design trend, the proposed model integrates Inception blocks, residual connections, Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) units, and pyramid attention mechanisms to enable effective multi-scale feature learning and preserve the continuity of thin vessels. The encoder is constructed using modified Inception blocks with parallel convolutional branches to facilitate multi-scale feature extraction, which is essential for capturing retinal vessels of varying widths and orientations. Residual connections improve gradient flow and stabilize training in deeper network layers, thereby preserving fine vessel structures and accelerating convergence. Squeeze-and-Excitation units adaptively recalibrate channel-wise feature responses, enhancing discrimination between vessel regions and background noise. In addition, pyramid attention modules integrated at the bottleneck and decoder stages aggregate both local details and global contextual information, leading to improved continuity and delineation of thin vessels. The decoder progressively restores spatial resolution using transposed convolutions and attention-based skip connections, enabling accurate localization of retinal vessels. Overall, this design follows recent hybrid deep learning approaches used for retinal vessel segmentation.

Inception module (INC)

The Inception module expands the width of the network and decreases the dimensionality of the network by employing parallel convolutions (, , ) and a max pooling branch, and then the outputs of all branches are concatenated to capture more diversified features. The convolutions before the bigger kernels, and after the pooling layers reduce the complexity, in addition, the batch normalization in some branches helps to stabilizes the training. Diverse receptive fields capture different sizes of vessels, and consequently the segmentation performance is improved.

Residual connections (RES) and squeeze-and-excitation (SE) modules

RepFed-Net incorporates the residual connection with Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks to improve the quality of the segmentation and the stability of the training process. The feature maps can be passed through layers directly with residual connection, which is useful for preserving spatial details, enhancing gradient flow and maintaining continuity of thin vessels. SE unit further enhances the performance by adaptively reweighing the importance of each feature channel using global average pooling along with gating mechanism to attend on more informative features while suppressing less useful ones. Together these blocks make the model particularly well-suited for capturing thin vessel structures, even in low contrast and cluttered regions of images.

Pyramid attention (PA) module

Attention mechanisms are widely used in the segmentation of medical images for focusing on relevant features and suppressing background noise49. However, traditional attention mechanism is usually performed on the basis of a single-scale representation, which may lead to biased fusion between shallow and deep features and can not effectively capture the fine details or global context.

To overcome this constraint, RepFed-Net introduces Pyramid Attention (PA) module to exploit multi-scale feature maps. The low-level features are convolved with a convolution and the high-level features are upsampled to the same spatial size to align detailed and contextual information in the network. The PA module can also recover small vessel structures on the finer scale and the wider retinal background on the coarser scale. These are then sent through batch normalization, a convolution, and a sigmoid to output the adaptive attention maps. This enables the network to focus on discriminative features at each scale and to further improve vessel boundary detection as well as background noise suppression, especially in challenging areas such as where vessels are weak, fragmented or where the vessel background slightly varies.

Multi-scale fusion module

We improve the segmentation by merging features over different spatial scale in this module. The retinal vessels have varying thickness and branching, thus both local information and global context are required. It can therefore use multiscale information to represent local structures as well as the overall vessel pattern, leading to more accurate vessel boundaries and better robustness in challenging local areas.

In RepFed-Net, model training is conducted entirely on local client devices, and only model updates are shared with the server for aggregation. This design ensures that raw medical data remain local while enabling collaborative retinal vessel segmentation without direct data sharing.

Encryption of local model parameters

After local training, the model updates are sent to the central server in FL. While raw data is kept on local devices, these updates may still leak sensitive information via model inversion-based reconstruction attacks, through which adversaries reconstruct private training data from the shared parameters. To mitigate this threat, the CKKS HE scheme is embedded for the data through TenSEAL library to achieve the confidential communication and aggregation.

The problem with this uniform averaging is that it assumes implicitly that all client updates are equally reliable, which might not be the case in reality: Some clients may have noisy, skewed, or poor data or may even be adversarial. To tackle the problem, we use a reputation-aware aggregation method. Following local training, each client tests its model on a validation set and calculates a performance score (the -score), which is sent to the server with the encrypted model updates.

In our framework, we employ the -score to calculate client reputation, as it provides a balanced evaluation by jointly considering precision and recall, which is particularly suitable for imbalanced datasets. Other metrics such as sensitivity, specificity, and IoU capture only a single aspect of performance and do not adequately reflect the trade-off between false positives and false negatives. By integrating both precision and recall, the -score offers a more balanced and reliable assessment, which is especially important in healthcare applications where both false positives and false negatives have serious consequences. Such balanced performance metrics are commonly recommended in performance evaluation and decision-making contexts, making the -score appropriate for reputation-aware aggregation in federated learning51. The server normalizes these scores into reputation weights, allowing more reliable clients to have greater influence on the global model update.

Experimental results and analysis

System configuration

The experiments for the proposed framework were implemented using Python 3.7 and PyTorch, with support for CKKS HE provided by the TenSEAL library. All experiments were conducted on a Windows operating system with 64 GB of RAM and a CUDA-compatible NVIDIA GPU. FL was simulated using three clients, each trained on disjoint subsets of a retinal segmentation dataset. The training process spanned 100 communication rounds. During each round, every client performed 20 local training epochs using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, and a Batch size of 32. Comparison of proposed RepFed-Net with existing approaches

Several centralized deep learning approaches have been proposed for retinal vessel segmentation and have been extensively evaluated on benchmark datasets such as the Retinal Blood Vessel and DRIVE datasets. On the Retinal Blood Vessel dataset, most existing methods are based on variants of the U-Net architecture. Recent studies by Ren et al.55, Carolyn et al.56, Hernandez-Gutierrez et al.58, and Al Shamayleh et al.59 enhance the standard U-Net by incorporating mechanisms such as Bi-FPN, attention gating, reverse attention, and hybrid loss functions to improve segmentation accuracy, while Das et al.57 integrate Frangi-based vessel filtering into the U-Net framework to achieve strong quantitative performance through handcrafted vessel enhancement. On the DRIVE dataset, earlier studies by Khalaf et al.60 and Guo et al.61 demonstrated the effectiveness of CNN-based and reinforcement learning–assisted approaches, which were later extended by attention-enhanced U-Net variants62,64, lightweight and reverse-attention architectures67, and more recent hybrid models such as CNN–Transformer cross-attention networks65 and dual-attention–based designs66 to better capture both local and global vessel features. Despite their effectiveness, these centralized methods are based on gathering sensitive retinal images in a centralized server for training, which raises privacy, security, and regulatory issues in practical clinical environments.

In contrast, the proposed RepFed-Net allows collaborative model training under a federated learning paradigm without the requirement of centralizing data. RepFed-Net combines CKKS-based HE with a reputation-aware aggregation scheme to enable secure and efficient model aggregation. Privacy is addressed at the design level, as clients transmit only encrypted model updates to the server, and aggregation is performed directly in the encrypted domain without exposing plaintext parameters. Security of the CKKS scheme relies on the hardness of the Ring Learning With Errors (RLWE) problem.

As shown in Table 9, RepFed-Net demonstrates strong performance in terms of all evaluation metrics on both the Retinal Blood Vessel and DRIVE datasets when compared to the sate-of-the-art centralized U-Net variants. These performance gains can be attributed to the enhanced architectural design of RepFed-Net, which integrates Inception modules, residual connections, Squeeze-and-Excitation units, and pyramid attention. Together, these components enable effective multi-scale feature extraction and adaptive channel-wise refinement, enabling improved modeling of complex and thin vessel structures compared with conventional encoder–decoder architectures. Overall, RepFed-Net provides an effective alternative to centralized retinal vessel segmentation approaches by combining strong segmentation performance with a federated learning design that aligns with privacy-aware medical imaging requirements.

Security assessment

Formal security analysis

RepFed-Net shows stable prediction performance and includes security considerations in its design. To verify this statement, we performed a comprehensive security evaluation with Bandit68, which is a static analysis tool for Python that is commonly used for detecting possible security issues in source code. Bandit is well suited for the federated learning, as it identifies risky code execution patterns, injection vulnerabilities, mishandling of keys, and weak cryptographic implementations. Moreover, we used the tool Scyther69 to perform a formal analysis of the communication protocol on which RepFed-Net is based. Scyther is also known to discover protocol-level threats such as man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks,replay attacks, denial-of-service attacks, authentication protocol flaws, and weaknesses in key establishment protocols, among others. Since federated learning is privacy-critical, these analyses were necessary in order to prove that RepFed-net is protected against attacks at both the implementation and protocol level. As seen in Fig. 9, the Bandit output shows zero found issues for all severity and confidence levels, which suggests that the source code follows secure coding practices. Moreover, the Scyther results in Fig. 10 verify that there are no obvious attacks, which further confirms the security of the RepFed-Net protocol. Together, these results suggest that RepFed-Net incorporates security mechanisms at the design level to address common implementation and communication threats.

Informal security analysis

RepFed-Net offers security at the design level, by employing federated learning with CKKS-based homomorphic encryption and a reputation-aware aggregation protocol. In our proposed framework, the local model parameters are encrypted at the client side with the CKKS scheme before transmission, and thus, the sensitive information are never exposed to the central server or the during the communication. Because the aggregation is done on ciphertexts, the server does not need to have the plaintext model updates, which reduces the potential leakage of information within the honest-but-curious server scenario.

The security of the CKKS scheme is based on the hardness of the Ring Learning With Errors problem (RLWE), which is considered to be hard and is also the underlying assumption for post-quantum cryptographic security20. Thus CKKS scheme especially well suited for federated learning applications with real-valued models and approximate computations. Even if clients are assumed to be honest in the current implementation, RepFed-Net builds on robustness by employing a reputation-aware aggregation mechanism. The framework uses the client’s validation F1-scores, which mitigates the impact of untrustworthy or poor quality updates without needing the raw data or the decrypted model parameters. Consequently, RepFed-Net provides a trade-off between security, design-level privacy protection, and resilience to potential adversarial attacks at the framework level.

Conclusion

In this work, we present RepFed-Net, a federated learning framework for retinal vessel segmentation that avoids centralized data sharing and performs encrypted model aggregation using CKKS homomorphic encryption. To improve robustness during federated training, a reputation-aware aggregation strategy is introduced to assign greater influence to more reliable client updates. On the modeling side, the integration of Inception modules, residual connections, and squeeze-and-excitation blocks, and Pyramid Attention supports multi-scale feature learning and stable optimization. Together, these components enable RepFed-Net to demonstrate the feasibility of improved segmentation performance under a simulated federated learning setting, while providing design-level privacy support within the proposed framework.

Limitations and future direction

The proposed RepFed-Net framework has several limitations. The current implementation assumes an honest-but-curious server and does not incorporate explicit authentication or integrity-verification mechanisms. As a result, if the server is compromised or behaves maliciously, it may inject incorrect or manipulated model updates, which could undermine the reliability of the federated learning process.

In addition, the framework adopts a single shared encryption key for homomorphic encryption across all participating clients. If a malicious client gains access to the shared private key, it may decrypt other client’s encrypted model updates and potentially infer sensitive information, thereby weakening client-level isolation. This limitation motivates the exploration of multi-key homomorphic encryption schemes, in which each client maintains an independent key pair while still enabling secure aggregation.

From a data perspective, the experimental evaluation is constrained by the relatively small size and limited diversity of available retinal vessel segmentation datasets, as large-scale and diverse retinal datasets are not currently publicly accessible. Moreover, most existing datasets provide only binary annotations, restricting the present study to binary segmentation tasks. In addition, the federated learning setup employs an independent and identically distributed (IID) client data partitioning strategy, which simplifies experimentation but may differ from real-world clinical federated learning environments where data distributions are often non-IID.

Finally, although security aspects were examined at the design and analysis levels using tools such as Bandit and Scyther, the current study does not include empirical evaluation against explicit privacy attacks or adversarial robustness scenarios, such as model inversion, membership inference, or poisoning attacks. Addressing these aspects remains an important limitation of the present work.

Future work will therefore focus on addressing non-IID data distributions, adopting stronger threat models, incorporating multi-key encryption70 and authentication mechanisms, and integrating blockchain-based server-side transparency mechanisms54, conducting empirical evaluations against privacy and adversarial attacks, and validating the framework on larger and more diverse datasets, including multi-class retinal segmentation tasks when suitable datasets become available.

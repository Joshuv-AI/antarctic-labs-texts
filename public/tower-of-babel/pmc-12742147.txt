Prevalence of sexual dysfunction and the associated factors among postpartum women who visit Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, Northwest Ethiopia in 2024: a facility-based cross-sectional study
Authors: Selamawit Girma Tadesse, Addisu Molla Abeje, Shimeles Biru, Rahel Amare Shumet, Adugna Gedefaw Alemu, Hiwot Dejen Fekadie, Belaynew Alemye Mengistie, Dessie Alemnew, Dagne Addisu, Abirham Wallelign Bayabil, Besfat Berihun Erega, Begizew Yimenu Mekuria, Assefa Kebie Mitiku, Tegegne Wale Belachew, Getachew Worku Melse, Kedir Ali Getahun, Eyaya Habtie Dagnaw
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSexual dysfunction is a common yet often neglected issue affecting the quality of life of postpartum women. It encompasses problems with desire, arousal, lubrication, orgasm, satisfaction and pain. The postpartum period is the transition from pregnancy and childbirth back to a non-pregnant physiological state and this change may affect a woman’s sexual health. So, the aim of this study is to assess the prevalence of sexual dysfunction and associated factors among postpartum women in Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital.MethodAn institution-based cross-sectional study was conducted from 18 March 2024 to 18 May 2024 on postpartum women. A total of 420 participants were included in the study who had been selected using systematic random sampling technique. A pretested and semistructured interviewer-administered questionnaire was used to collect the data that consisted of sociodemographic, obstetric-related, gynaecological-related, psychosocial factors to assess the associated factors and the Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI) was used to determine the prevalence of sexual dysfunction. Epi-data V.4.6 and SPSS V.27 were used for data entry and analysis, respectively. Sexual dysfunction was declared if the FSFI score was less than or equal to 26.5. The Patient Health Questionnaire tool was used to assess the psychosocial factor, and depression was declared if the score was greater than or equal to 10. Binary logistic regression model was used to identify factors associated with sexual dysfunction and all variables with a p value of ≤0.2 in the bivariable analysis were entered to multivariable logistic regression for further analysis, considering the association to be significant p values <0.05 at 95% CI.ResultThe prevalence of sexual dysfunction among postpartum women visiting Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital was 51.7% (95% CI 47.1% to 56.9%). Being employed (vs housewife) (adjusted OR, AOR=3.21, 95% CI 1.47 to 6.99), having an adverse pregnancy outcome (AOR=3.34, 95% CI 1.98 to 5.62), perineal tear (AOR=2.17, 95% CI 1.23 to 3.83) and depression (AOR=2.78, 95% CI 1.66 to 4.66) were significantly associated with sexual dysfunction.ConclusionsA high prevalence of sexual dysfunction was observed among postpartum women. Adverse pregnancy outcome, perineal tear, being employed and depression significantly increased the likelihood of postpartum sexual dysfunction. We recommend developing sexual health strategies, including the integration of routine sexual health assessments into postpartum care. Particular attention should be given to women with depression, adverse pregnancy outcomes, perineal trauma and those returning to work.

Introduction

The postpartum period is the transition from pregnancy and childbirth back to a non-pregnant physiological state. The women adapt to their parenting roles and resume sexual activities; however, a number of problems happened specifically in this period. The physiological, psychological and sociocultural changes that occur during this period affect women‘s sexual health and behaviours.1 2

During the postpartum period, women’s sexual function might be influenced by complications during pregnancy, different characteristics of delivery, postpartum physiological and psychological changes including depression, trauma to the reproductive organs, vaginal bleeding, constraints caused by a newborn, lethargy, changes in body shape and physical discomfort, such as breast tenderness.3 More than 50% of women experience pain during their first intercourse after birth, and 41% still experience dyspareunia, reduced desire and arousal difficulties 3 months post partum.4 Although most women resume sexual intercourse by 6–8 weeks after childbirth, it might take a year to return to prepregnancy levels of sexual activity.5

Although sexual health is a universal part of the quality of life in all human beings, different cultures and religions have been abandoning sexual intercourse for a long time after childbirth.68 Sexual dysfunction is a public health importance that affects both men and women. It is a multidimensional dysfunction, which affects the physical, mental and social condition of women.7 The evaluation of sexual intercourse recommencement has been suggested by the WHO for women in their early postdelivery period.6 9

According to the Sustainable Development Goal, all women of reproductive age must have universal access to the sexual and reproductive healthcare by 2030. However, in many underdeveloped nations, including Ethiopia, this objective still appears unattainable due to the stigma associated with talking about sexual activity and sexuality, and conversations regarding women’s sexual health are typically discouraged. Regardless of the fact that women experience sexual dysfunction at a higher rate than men (28%), women’s sexual problems receive less attention worldwide.10

Sexual dysfunction is a common yet often overlooked maternal health issue affecting many women worldwide, especially during the postpartum period when major physiological and emotional changes occur. It encompasses problems with desire, arousal, lubrication, orgasm, satisfaction and pain. Even though the high global prevalence of postpartum sexual dysfunction, cultural and structural barriers in many regions, including Ethiopia, exacerbate the problem and limit access to care and information. In low-income and middle-income countries, these challenges are intensified by limited access to counselling and cultural barriers around sexual health. Ethiopia faces similar conditions, where postpartum sexual well-being receives little attention within routine maternal care. Despite indications that postpartum sexual dysfunction is prevalent, context-specific data remain limited.4 11

Regardless of the importance and difficulty of this topic, health providers often neglect sexual aspects during pregnancy and post partum, even access to services related to sexual and reproductive health and rights remains a major challenge in Ethiopia.10 However, this problem has received little consideration from both researchers and healthcare providers.12 13

Even though SD is acknowledged to be the unspoken cause of many divorces, it is still a neglected and understudied topic.101417 Furthermore, there is no study conducted in the study area that assesses FSD during the postpartum period. Thus, this study aimed to assess the prevalence of sexual dysfunction and associated factors among postpartum women visiting Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital. The findings of this study will help inform clinicians, guide targeted postpartum counselling and support policymakers in integrating sexual health into routine maternal care—ultimately improving the quality of postpartum services in the study area.

Method

A cross-sectional study was conducted at Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital from 18 March 2024 to 18 May 2024. Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital is found in Debre Tabor town, South Gondar zone, Amhara regional state, Ethiopia. According to Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital data, it serves an estimated three million people per year in the catchment area. This hospital has started service delivery to the people since 1931. The hospital provides almost all services of In-patient Department and Out Patient Department including family planning (FP), Expanded Programme for Immunisation (EPI). According to the Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital data register, >10 000 postpartum women come to EPI and FP service per year at Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital. The expected total numbers of women who are postpartum FP and EPI service users were 1780 per 2 months.

General objective

To assess the prevalence of sexual dysfunction and associated factors among postpartum women visiting Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, Northwest Ethiopia, 2024

Specific objectives

To determine the prevalence of sexual dysfunction among postpartum women visiting Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, Northwest Ethiopia, 2024.

To identify factors associated with sexual dysfunction among postpartum women visiting Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, Northwest Ethiopia, 2024.

Study population: All women from 12 weeks up to 12 months of postpartum period who visited Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital for EPI and FP services during the period of data collection.

Inclusion criteria: Women from 12 weeks up to 12 months of postpartum period who visited Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital for EPI and FP services and were selected using systematic random sampling technique.

Exclusion criteria

Women who cannot communicate due to serious illness during data collection were excluded from the study.

Variables of the study: Postpartum sexual dysfunction (Yes or No) is the dependent variable. Independent variables are sociodemographic variables: (Age, ethnicity, religion, educational status, occupation, marital status), psychosocial variable: (Depression), obstetric related variables: (History of adverse pregnancy outcome, number of pregnancies, number of births, early postpartum period (3–6 months, >6 months), episiotomy, perineal tear and mode of delivery) and gynaecological related variables: (Menstruation after delivery, FP type, breastfeeding problems, existing disease condition, drug intake). Depression and perineal trauma are hypothesised to be positively associated with PSD.

Sample size

The sample size using a single population proportion was calculated based on the assumptions; Margin of error (α=5%), CI (95%=1.96), power (80%), proportion (p=0.5) because there was no recent study conducted on this topic (female sexual dysfunction among postpartum). The calculated sample size was 384.16, then by adding 15% of non-response rate, the final sample size became 442.

Sampling procedure/technique

After proportional allocation of study participants in each (FP and EPI unit), study participants were selected using a systematic random sampling technique. The sampling frame consisted of all postpartum women who came for FP and EPI follow-up at DTCSH during the period of data collection. During the study period (per 2 months), 1780 women were expected to visit Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital. The sampling interval (kth value) was calculated by dividing the total number of women who have follow-up on EPI and FP services within 2 months by the number of sample size. Thus, 1780/required sample size (442)=4.02, kth value was 4. The first participant was selected using the lottery method within the kth (1−4) interval; then the rest of the participants were selected every fourth client from each unit (FP and EPI) until the desired sample size was attained. Registration of eligible women and application of the sampling procedure were carried out on a daily basis using the FP and EPI register as the sampling frame. If a selected woman did not meet the eligibility criteria, the next eligible woman was included, and the sequence continued. Of the women approached, 22 declined to provide consent, yielding a final sample of 420 participants.

Operational definition

Sexual dysfunction: Defined as a woman whose Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI) score is less than or equal to 26.5.18

Depression: Those respondents who were found to score ≥10 in the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9) were classified as having depression.19

Adverse pregnancy outcome: Defined as the occurrence of abortion, stillbirth, structural/diagnosed malformed fetus/neonate, low birth weight, preterm birth, neonatal death and maternal complication such as preeclampsia, eclampsia, severe postpartum haemorrhage. If at least one of these problems occurred, taken as an adverse pregnancy outcome occurred.20

Footnote

Employed is defined as women who work any public or private formal and/or informal outdoor works including physically demanding hard work like farming and who stay more than or equal to 8 hours outside their home.

Data collection tools

The data were collected through semistructured interviewer-administered questionnaire by using the FSFI tool that was developed by Rosen et al to assess outcome variable (female sexual function) which has 19 items of FSFI.18 The PHQ-9 tool was used to assess the psychosocial factor and to score the instrument, tally the numbers of all the checked responses under each heading (not at all=0, several days=1, more than half the days=2, and nearly every day=3). Add the numbers together to total the score on the bottom of the questionnaire. Finally, it was interpreted as those respondents who were found to score ≥10 in PHQ-9 were classified as having depression.19 In addition, the questionnaire includes sociodemographic data, obstetric-related, gynaecological-related factors and psychosocial factors starting from sociodemographic variables to psychosocial variables.17 8 1322 The full questionnaire was first prepared in English language and then translated to Amharic, finally translated back to English language by the language experts to ensure consistency (online supplemental file).

Statistical analysis

After the data were checked for completeness and accuracy, they were coded manually and then entered into Epi-data V.4.6.0.2 statistical software and exported to SPSS V.27 statistical software for analysis. Descriptive statistics were carried out and summarised by using frequency, mean and SD. Binary logistic regression was used to determine the unadjusted associations of each independent variable with sexual dysfunction. All variables with a p ≤0.2 in the bivariable analysis were entered to multivariable logistic regression for further analysis and to control for confounding variables. Multicollinearity was checked to see the correlation between factors by using variance inflation factor. Variables with a variance inflation factor greater than 10 were planned to be removed from the model. The analysis indicated that all variables demonstrated acceptable VIF values below 5. The adequacy of the final model was assessed by using the Hosmer and Lemeshow goodness of fit test and indicates the data is fit for the model (p=0.61). Adjusted OR (AOR) with corresponding 95% CI was used to report the strength of association between dependent and independent variables. Finally, variables with p values of less than 0.05 were considered significantly associated with the outcome variable.

To address recall bias and social desirability bias, efforts were made by using female data collectors and supervisors, making the participants relaxed by taking time and freely discussing the health importance of the topic and ensuring the privacy of the respondents.

The aim of the research was explained to the study subjects; written informed consent was obtained from each study subject after explanation of the purpose of the study and involvement (to be a participant) was after their complete consent. Any postpartum women who came for FP and EPI follow-up at Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital who were not willing to participate in the study were not forced to participate, no personal identifications were included in the data sheet and all data taken from the participants were kept strictly confidential and used only for the study purpose. Also, privacy was assured during the interview of the questionnaire as some study questions were related to sensitive issues and the participants were informed that they had the right either to involve or not in the study and even to withdraw from the study.

Patient and public involvement

Postpartum women helped shape the research question through initial interviews conducted during EPI and FP visits. Their concerns about sexual health and its impact on their quality of life guided the selection of the study focus and outcomes.

Women from the community reviewed the study objectives, tools and measurement criteria during the design phase. Their feedback ensured that the indicators of sexual dysfunction were relevant, culturally appropriate and aligned with their lived experiences.

Local health workers supported the research team by informing eligible women about the study. Previously attended postpartum women also encouraged peers to participate, helping build trust and improving recruitment.

Findings will be shared through informational sessions with local health sectors and community meetings at Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital. Summaries—both digital and printed in local languages—will be provided to ensure accessible communication of results.

The authors used the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology cross-sectional reporting guidelines.23

Factors associated with sexual dysfunction among postpartum women

Factors associated with PSD were assessed using logistic regression. In the bivariable analysis, educational level, occupation, adverse pregnancy outcome, mode of delivery, perineal tear, planned current pregnancy, institutional delivery, problem during breastfeeding, Antenatal care (ANC) visit, day from delivery (postpartum category) and depression were significantly associated; however, after adjustment in the multivariable model, occupation, adverse pregnancy outcome, perineal tear and depression remained independently associated.

Participants who were employed (AOR=3.21, 95% CI 1.47 to 6.99), had adverse pregnancy outcome (AOR=3.34, 95% CI 1.98 to 5.62), perineal tear (AOR=2.17, 95% CI 1.23 to 3.83) and depression (AOR=2.78, 95% CI 1.66 to 4.66) were independently associated with sexual dysfunction (table 4).

Discussion

This institution-based cross-sectional study tried to assess the prevalence of sexual dysfunction and identified associated factors among postpartum women during 12 weeks to 12 months after delivery. Primary data were collected from mothers who visited Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital FP and an expanded programme on immunisation services.

According to this study, the prevalence of sexual dysfunction in the study area was 51.7% (95% CI 47.1% to 56.9%). Comparing this study finding with those of other studies, the result of this study is found to be higher than 33% that reported by a study done in Colombia.24 25 Possible explanations include sociodemographic factors, differing levels of sexuality education, societal taboos as the frank discussion regarding sexuality is limited in the study area, and healthcare system variations with sophisticated strategies and emphasis given to sexual health in Colombia. The prevalence of sexual dysfunction in this study is also found to be higher than 32.1% from another study done in Gurage zone, Ethiopia.13 The possible justification for this difference might be the difference in the study population. While the current study used postpartum women who visit hospitals for EPI and FP services from 3 to 12 months of delivery, the study done in Gurage zone of Ethiopia used all married reproductive age women who visit hospitals for any services within the period of data collection. On the other hand, this study was lower as compared with a study conducted in Malaysia, Burundi, Turkey and Egypt, that showed a greater prevalence of 57%, 66%, 74.3% and 74.7% women having sexual dysfunction, respectively.1 4 6 26 The difference between the results obtained in this research and those of other authors could be attributed to the method of data collection. In this study, the data were collected through face-to-face interviews, whereas in the other study, the respondents were filling in and sending their data by using electronic media (email).

In this study, the domain of sexual dysfunction problems with desire in the front line was by 65.2%, lubrication 64.5%, pain 63.5%, arousal 61%, satisfaction 57.4%, followed by orgasm with 49.5%. The finding that desire, lubrication and pain were found to be the leading domains of sexual dysfunction among postpartum women in the study area suggests that clinical interventions should prioritise both physiological, psychosocial and physical components of postpartum sexual health. Reduced desire may indicate unmet emotional needs, fatigue or cultural barriers to resuming sexual activity, implying a need for counselling and partner-focused education. Lubrication problems point toward hormonal changes, particularly breastfeeding-related hypoestrogenism, highlighting the importance of providing guidance on safe lubricants and discussing temporary hormonal influences. The high prevalence of pain suggests that clinicians should screen for perineal trauma, infections, breastfeeding-related dryness and psychological fear of resuming intercourse. Overall, the pattern underscores the need for integrated postpartum care that includes routine sexual-health assessments, early identification of modifiable factors and culturally sensitive counselling for women and couples.

The leading sexual dysfunction domain finding of this study is somewhat similar to the study that was conducted in Burundi and Egypt, which states among domains of sexual dysfunction, desire and orgasm have come to the front line, whereas in this study, desire, lubrication and pain have dominated. The possible explanation for the difference in the orgasm domain among Egyptian women might be due to the difference in the study setting, which the study was conducted among women in the primary healthcare centre but the current study has been conducted in a comprehensive specialised hospital.82729 Whereas the current finding on domains of sexual dysfunction is different from another study done in Gurage zone of Ethiopia that shows the leading problem as arousal dysfunction with 91.0% while pain during sexual intercourse at 39.3% was the least reported domain of female sexual dysfunction. The possible justification for this might be due to the difference in the study population and study setting that the current study was conducted among postpartum women who visit one comprehensive specialised hospital for FP and EPI services in their 6 to 12 months after delivery and the study done in Gurage zone was among married reproductive age women who visit governmental public hospitals found in Gurage zone for all services.13

This study has also shown the domain of satisfaction as 57%, which is lower than that of the cross-sectional study done in Malaysia, which found that satisfaction problems lead to the main sexual dysfunction problem, which accounts for 98.8%. The possible explanation for the observed difference is that it is due to the difference in the study population and study setting, where this study has been conducted in a comprehensive specialised hospital and among 3 to 12 months postpartum women, whereas the study done in Malaysia was conducted in a primary care setting and among 4 to 6 months of postpartum women.30

In this study, there is a statistically significant association between women’s occupation and postpartum sexual dysfunction. This study discovered that employed women were three times more likely to have sexual dysfunction as compared with those whose occupation was housewife. As to the authors’ extensive literature search, there is no study that shows a relation between occupation and sexual dysfunction. But the possible explanation might be the fact that women who are employed would have more physical and emotional burden that decreases their sexual function in addition to caring for the newborn and performing household chores than that of housewife women. Employed women would have an earlier return to physically demanding work (including manual labour or farming) leading to exhaustion; psychological stress from dual burdens of work and new motherhood than that of housewife women. In addition, work-related distress will affect one’s sexual desire, in turn increasing sexual dysfunction.3133

This study showed a statistical association between adverse pregnancy outcome and postpartum sexual dysfunction. The study revealed that women with adverse pregnancy outcomes were three times more likely to have sexual dysfunction as compared with those not having adverse pregnancy outcomes. This is consistent with the finding of the study that was conducted in Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Brazil.120 3437 The possible reasons might be due to complications (antepartum haemorrhage, fear of fetal condition), fear of harming the baby, psychological burden of having a high-risk pregnancy, social myths and lack of knowledge related to high-risk pregnancy, and the potential for post-traumatic stress following a traumatic birth or neonatal loss might be related to their sexual dysfunction.

This study also found a significant association between perineal tear and postpartum sexual dysfunction. Women who had a perineal tear were two times more likely to have sexual dysfunction when compared with those women who delivered without having a perineal tear. The result of this study was supported by a study conducted in Portugal and Brazil, which suggested that the reason might be due to the difference with intact perineum after delivery, and those women who encounter perineal tear might take time to recover depending on the degree of tear, and these complaints lead to lower levels of libido, orgasm, satisfaction and pain during sexual intercourse. In addition, perineal trauma can lead to fear of pain (anticipatory anxiety), which itself inhibits sexual response and desire.1 38 39

The last factor significantly associated with postpartum sexual dysfunction was depression. Women who had depression were three times more likely to have sexual dysfunction as compared with women who had no depression. This finding is in line with a study conducted in Turkey and England. The observed association may be due to the fact that loss of interest, diminished energy, low self-esteem, inability to enjoy pleasure, insomnia, sadness, impatience and social disengagement are symptoms of postnatal depression. This combination of symptoms may be expected to produce difficulties in sexual relationships and has been associated with sexual dysfunction.1 40 41

Conclusions

A high prevalence of sexual dysfunction was observed among postpartum women in Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialised Hospital. The risk factor identified in this study indicates that female sexual dysfunction was a multidimensional problem with emotional, social and physical features. Occupational status being employed, having adverse pregnancy outcome, having perineal tear and being depressed were significantly increasing the likelihood of postpartum sexual dysfunction.

Recommendation

An understanding of the factors and sexual dysfunction in postpartum women is useful to help them to take an active role in improving their sexual problem and engaging in prevention and treatment strategies. The following points are forwarded to decrease the proportion of sexual dysfunction in postpartum women.

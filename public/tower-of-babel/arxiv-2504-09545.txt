Note on a problem of Sárközy on multiplicative representation functions
Yuchen Ding

Abstract
. In this short note, we answer affirmatively a 2001 problem of Sárközy.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11A05

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers. In the unsolved problems list of Sárközy
[
1
, Problem 25]
, he asked the following interesting question.

Problem 1

(Sárközy)
.

For
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
let

d(\mathcal{A},n)=\#\{a\in\mathcal{A}:a|n\}.

Then is it true that for any infinite set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}
,
|d(\mathcal{A},n+1)-d(\mathcal{A},n)|
cannot be bounded?

In this note, We answer it affirmatively. In fact, we will prove a stronger result.

Theorem 1
.

For any infinite set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}
and any nonzero
k
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},n+k)-d(\mathcal{A},n)|=\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{i}<\cdots\}
. For any positive integer
t
let

P_{t}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}a_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\ell=\sup_{t\geq 1}\big\{\gcd(P_{t},k)\big\}.

Then there is some fixed
t_{0}
so that
\ell|P_{t_{0}}
. For any positive integer
m
, one notes that

\gcd\left(\frac{P_{t_{0}+m}}{\ell},\frac{k}{\ell}\right)=1.

So, by Dirichlet’s theorem in arithmetic progressions there exists some prime

p_{m}>\max\big\{\ell,|k|/\ell\big\}

satisfying that

\displaystyle p_{m}\equiv-\frac{k}{\ell}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ P_{t_{0}+m}/\ell).

(1)

Clearly, (
1
) implies
\frac{P_{t_{0}+m}}{\ell}|(p_{m}+\frac{k}{\ell})
and hence
P_{t_{0}+m}|(\ell p_{m}+k)
, from which it follows

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{A},\ell p_{m}+k)\geq t_{0}+m.

(2)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{A},\ell p_{m})\leq 2\ell\leq 2|k|

(3)

since
p_{m}>\ell
. Moreover,
p_{m}>|k|/\ell
clearly implies
\ell p_{m}+k>0
.
Hence, we conclude from (
2
) and (
3
) that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},n+k)-d(\mathcal{A},n)|

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},\ell p_{m}+k)-d(\mathcal{A},\ell p_{m})|

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\Big(t_{0}+m-2|k|\Big)=\infty,

proving our theorem.
∎

Let

f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\min_{1\leq i\neq j\leq 3}\big\{|d(\mathcal{A},n+i)-d(\mathcal{A},n+j)|\big\}.

I asked Prof. Yong-Gao Chen whether
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\infty
for any infinite
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}
? Immediately, he gave a negative answer by taking
A=\{4m:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
since at least two of
n+1,n+2
and
n+3
can not be divisible by
4
. Chen then suggested the following generalized problem.

Problem 2

(Chen)
.

Determine the sufficient and necessary conditions of integers
b,c,e
and
f
sastisfying

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},bn+c)-d(\mathcal{A},en+f)|=\infty

for any infinite
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}
.

As a solution to problem
2
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
b,c,e
and
f
be integers. Then we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},bn+c)-d(\mathcal{A},en+f)|=\infty

(4)

for any infinite
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}

if and only if one of the following cases holds:

I.
b=c=e=0
and
f\neq 0
; or
b=c=f=0
and
e\neq 0
;

II.
b=0
,
e\neq 0
, and
c=0
; or
b\neq 0
,
e=0
, and
f=0
.

III.
b=0
,
e\neq 0
,
c\neq 0
, and
e|f
; or
b\neq 0
,
e=0
,
f\neq 0
, and
b|c
;

IV.
be\neq 0
,
bf\neq ec
, and
b|c
; or
be\neq 0
,
bf\neq ec
, and
e|f
.

Remark. Clearly, Theorem
1
is contained in IV of Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Necessities.

To prove the necessities, we separate them into a few cases.

Case 1.

b=e=0
.

It is clear that the situation
b=c=e=f=0
does not satisfy our requirement. Moreover, at least one of
c
and
f
equals zero. Otherwise, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},c)-d(\mathcal{A},f)|\leq|c|+|f|<\infty

for any infinite
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}
which is a contradiction. (
Case 1.
corresponds with I.)

Case 2.

b=0
and
e\neq 0
; or
b\neq 0
and
e=0
.

We only consider the case
b=0
and
e\neq 0
since the other situation will be treated similarly. In this case,
c=0
is admissible since
d(\mathcal{A},en+f)<\infty
for all sufficiently large
n
as
en+f\neq 0
for
n>-f/e
. We are left over to consider the situation
b=0,e\neq 0
, and
c\neq 0
. For any infinite
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}
we clearly have

d(\mathcal{A},c)\leq|c|.

We are going to show
e|f
. Assume the contrary, i.e.,
e\nmid f
. Take
\mathcal{A}_{e}=\{|e|m:m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
and then

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(\mathcal{A}_{e},en+f)=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A}_{e},c)-d(\mathcal{A}_{e},en+f)|\leq|c|<\infty,

which is a contradiction. (
Case 2.
corresponds with II and III.)

Case 3.

be\neq 0
.

In this case, we firstly show that
c^{2}+f^{2}\neq 0
, i.e., at least one of
c
and
f
is nonzero. Otherwise, we choose a prime
p_{0}
so that
p_{0}\nmid b
and
p_{0}\nmid e
and take

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}=\{p_{0}^{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathbb{N}.

(5)

Then we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A}_{0},bn)-d(\mathcal{A}_{0},en)|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A}_{0},n)-d(\mathcal{A}_{0},n)|=0,

which is a contradiction.

Next, we prove
b|c
or
e|f
. Assume the contrary. Then we may assume
b=xb_{1},c=xc_{1},e=ye_{1}
, and
f=yf_{1}
with
b_{1},e_{1}\neq 1
,
\gcd(b_{1},c_{1})=1
, and
\gcd(e_{1},f_{1})=1
. We take
\mathcal{A}_{b_{1},e_{1}}=\{(b_{1}e_{1})^{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
. We claim that
d(\mathcal{A}_{b_{1},e_{1}},bn+c)
and
d(\mathcal{A}_{b_{1},e_{1}},en+f)
are bounded for all
n
, from which we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A}_{b_{1},e_{1}},bn+c)-d(\mathcal{A}_{b_{1},e_{1}},en+f)|<\infty,

leading to a contradiction with (
4
). Actually, for all sufficiently large
m
if
(b_{1}e_{1})^{m}|bn+c
, then
b_{1}|b_{1}n+c_{1}
. This is a contradiction with
\gcd(b_{1},c_{1})=1
and
b_{1}>1
. Thus, the function
d(\mathcal{A}_{b_{1},e_{1}},bn+c)
is bounded for all
n
. The same argument yields that
d(\mathcal{A}_{b_{1},e_{1}},en+f)
is bounded for all
n
.

We are left over to show
bf\neq ec
. Assume the contrary, i.e.,

\frac{c}{b}=\frac{f}{e}=g,\quad\text{say.}

Since
c^{2}+f^{2}\neq 0
, we have
g\neq 0
. Moreover,
g\in\mathbb{Z}
since
b|c
or
e|f
. Then for
\mathcal{A}_{0}
as in (
5
) we also have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A}_{0},bn+c)-d(\mathcal{A}_{0},en+f)|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A}_{0},b(n+g))-d(\mathcal{A}_{0},e(n+g))|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A}_{0},n+g)-d(\mathcal{A}_{0},n+g)|=0,

which is a contradiction.
(
Case 3.
corresponds with IV.)

Sufficiencies.
We now turn to the proof of sufficiencies case by case.

I. implies (
4
).
Clearly.

II. implies (
4
).
Without loss of generality, we only consider the case
b=0
,
e\neq 0
, and
c=0
since the other one can be proved similarly. We have
d(\mathcal{A},en+f)<\infty
for any
n\neq-f/e
since
e\neq 0
. But
d(\mathcal{A},0)=\infty
for any
n
, from which
(\ref{eq4})
clearly follows.

III. implies (
4
).
Without loss of generality, we only consider the case
b=0
,
e\neq 0
,
c\neq 0
, and
e|f
since the other one can be proved similarly. We assume
f=eh
. For any positive integer
t
let

P_{t}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}a_{i}.

For any
t
there is a positive integer
n_{t}
so that

n_{t}\equiv-h\ ({\rm{mod}}\ P_{t}),

which means that

P_{t}|e(n_{t}+h).

In other words,
d(\mathcal{A},n_{t}+h)\geq t
.
Hence, for sufficiently large
t
we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},c)-d(\mathcal{A}_{g},en+f)|

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},c)-d(\mathcal{A},e(n_{t}+h))|

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}(t-|c|)=\infty.

IV. implies (
4
).
Without loss of generality, we only consider the case
be\neq 0
,
bf\neq ec
, and
b|c
since the other one can be proved similarly.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{i}<\cdots\}
and
c=bh
. Since
bf\neq ec
, we have
f-eh\neq 0
. For any positive integer
t
let

P_{t}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}a_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\ell=\sup_{t\geq 1}\big\{\gcd(eP_{t},f-eh)\big\}.

Then there is some fixed
t_{0}
so that
\ell|eP_{t_{0}}
and

\displaystyle P_{t_{0}}>|h|.

(6)

For any positive integer
m
, we have

\gcd\left(\frac{eP_{t_{0}+m}}{\ell},\frac{f-eh}{\ell}\right)=1.

Now, we will separate the arguments into two cases.

Suppose first that
e>0
.
In this case, by Dirichlet’s theorem in arithmetic progressions there exists a prime

\displaystyle p_{m}>\max\Big\{\frac{|f-eh|}{\ell},\ell\Big\}

(7)

satisfying that

\displaystyle p_{m}\equiv\frac{f-eh}{\ell}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ eP_{t_{0}+m}/\ell),

(8)

which clearly means that

\displaystyle\ell p_{m}-f+eh=eP_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}

(9)

for some integer
q_{m}
. Note that
p_{m}>\frac{|f-eh|}{\ell}
from (
7
). We have
\ell p_{m}-f+eh>0
and hence
P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}>0
.
Rearranging the terms in (
9
) leads to

\displaystyle e(P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}-h)+f=\ell p_{m}.

(10)

Moreover, one notes easily that

\displaystyle b(P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}-h)+c=bP_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}\equiv 0\ ({\rm{mod}}\ P_{t_{0}+m}).

(11)

From (
10
) and (
11
) we have

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{A},e(P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}-h)+f)=d(\mathcal{A},\ell p_{m})

(12)

and

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{A},b(P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}-h)+c)\geq t_{0}+m.

(13)

Recall that
p_{m}>\ell
for any
m
from (
7
). Hence

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{A},\ell p_{m})\leq 2\ell\leq 2|f-eh|.

(14)

Furthermore, from (
6
) we have

P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}-h>P_{t_{0}}-h>0.

We conclude from (
12
), (
13
) and (
14
) that for sufficiently large
m
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{A},bn+c)-d(\mathcal{A},en+f)|

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},b(P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}-h)+c)-d(\mathcal{A},e(P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}-h)+f)|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}|d(\mathcal{A},b(P_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}-h)+c)-d(\mathcal{A},\ell p_{m})|

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\big(t_{0}+m-2|f-eh|\big)=\infty.

It remains to consider the case
e<0
. In this case, we simply replace (
8
) by

p_{m}\equiv-\frac{f-eh}{\ell}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ eP_{t_{0}+m}/\ell).

Then (
9
) will be changed to

-\ell p_{m}-f+eh=eP_{t_{0}+m}q_{m}.

We see easily that
q_{m}
is also positive. Adjusting the arguments accordingly would yield our desired result.
∎

Langlands Duality and Invariant Differential Operators
V.K. Dobrev

Abstract




Abstract

Langlands duality is one of the most influential topics in mathematical research. It has many different appearances and influential subtopics. Yet there is a topic that until now seems unrelated to the Langlands program. That is the topic of invariant differential operators. That is strange since both items are deeply rooted in Harish-Chandra’s representation theory of semisimple Lie groups. In this paper we start building the bridge between the two programs.

Synopsis: Canonical construction of invariant differential operators

Let
G
be a semi-simple, non-compact Lie group, and
K
a
maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Then, we have an
Iwasawa
decomposition

G=KA_{0}N_{0}
, where
A_{0}
is an Abelian simply
connected vector subgroup of
G
and
N_{0}
is a nilpotent simply
connected subgroup of
G
preserved by the action of
A_{0}
.
Furthermore, let
M_{0}
be the centralizer of
A_{0}
in
K
. Then, the
subgroup
P_{0}=M_{0}A_{0}N_{0}
is a
minimal parabolic subgroup
of

G
. A
parabolic subgroup

P^{\prime}=M^{\prime}A^{\prime}N^{\prime}
is any subgroup of
G

which contains a minimal parabolic subgroup.

Furthermore let
{\cal G},{\cal K},{\cal P},{\cal M},{\cal A},{\cal N}
denote the Lie algebras of
G,K,P,M,A,N
, resp.

Further, for simplicity, we restrict to
maximal parabolic
subgroups

P=MAN
, i.e.,
{\rm rank}\,A=1
, resp., to
maximal parabolic
subalgebras

{\cal P}={\cal M}\oplus{\cal A}\oplus{\cal N}
with
\dim\,{\cal A}=1
.

Let
\nu
be a (non-unitary) character of
A
,
\nu\in{\cal A}^{*}
,
parameterized by a real number
d
, called (for historical reasons) the
conformal weight
or
energy.

Furthermore, let
\mu
fix a discrete series representation

D^{\mu}
of
M
on the Hilbert space
V_{\mu}\,
, or the
finite-dimensional (non-unitary) representation of
M
with the same
Casimirs.

We call the induced
representation
\chi=
Ind
{}^{G}_{P}(\mu\otimes\nu\otimes 1)
an

elementary representation
of
G

[
58
]
. (These are
called
generalized principal series representations
(or
limits thereof
) in
[
59
]
.) Their spaces of functions are

{\cal C}_{\chi}=\{{\cal F}\in C^{\infty}(G,V_{\mu})|{\cal F}(gman)=e^{-\nu(H)}\cdot D^{\mu}(m^{-1})\,{\cal F}(g)\}

(1)

where
a=\exp(H)\in A^{\prime}
,
H\in{\cal A}^{\prime}\,
,
m\in M^{\prime}
,
n\in N^{\prime}
. The
representation action is the
left regular action
:

({\cal T}^{\chi}(g){\cal F})(g^{\prime})={\cal F}(g^{-1}g^{\prime}),\quad g,g^{\prime}\in G.

(2)

An important ingredient in our considerations are the
highest/lowest-weight representations
of
{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}
. These can be
realized as (factor-modules of) Verma modules
V^{\Lambda}
over

{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}
, where
\Lambda\in({\cal H}^{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
,
{\cal H}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a Cartan
subalgebra of
{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}
and the weight
\Lambda=\Lambda(\chi)
is determined
uniquely from
\chi

[
60
]
.

Actually, since our ERs may be induced from finite-dimensional
representations of
{\cal M}
(or their limits) the Verma modules are
always reducible. Thus, it is more convenient to use
generalized Verma modules

{\tilde{V}}^{\Lambda}
such that the role of the
highest/lowest-weight vector
v_{0}
is taken by the
(finite-dimensional) space
V_{\mu}\,v_{0}\,
. For the generalized
Verma modules (GVMs) the reducibility is controlled only by the
value of the conformal weight
d
. Relatedly, for the intertwining differential operators, only
the reducibility with regard to non-compact roots is essential.

Another main ingredient of our approach is as follows. We group
the (reducible) ERs with the same Casimirs in sets called
multiplets

[
60
]
. The multiplet corresponding to fixed values of the
Casimirs may be depicted as a connected graph, the
vertices
of
which correspond to the reducible ERs and the
lines (arrows)

between the vertices correspond to intertwining operators. The
explicit parameterization of the multiplets and of their ERs is
important in understanding of the situation. The notion of multiplets was introduced in
[
61
]

and applied to representations of
SO_{o}(p,q)
and
SU(2,2)
, resp.,
induced from their minimal parabolic subalgebras. Then it was applied to
the conformal superalgebra
[
62
]
, to quantum groups
[
63
]
, to
infinite-dimensional (super)algebras
[
64
]
.
(For other applications,
we refer to
[
65
]
.

In fact, the multiplets contain explicitly all the data necessary to
construct the intertwining differential operators. Actually, the data for each intertwining differential operator consist
of the pair
(\beta,m)
, where
\beta
is a (non-compact) positive root
of
{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, such that the
BGG Verma module
reducibility condition
(for highest-weight modules) is fulfilled:

(\Lambda+\rho,\beta^{\vee})=m,\quad\beta^{\vee}\equiv 2\beta/(\beta,\beta)\

(3)

where
\rho
is half the sum of the positive roots of

{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}
. When the above holds, then the Verma module with shifted
weight
V^{\Lambda-m\beta}
(or
{\tilde{V}}^{\Lambda-m\beta}
for GVM and
\beta

non-compact) is embedded in the Verma module
V^{\Lambda}
(or

{\tilde{V}}^{\Lambda}
). This embedding is realized by a singular vector

v_{s}
determined by a polynomial
{\cal P}_{m,\beta}({\cal G}^{-})
in the
universal enveloping algebra
(U({\cal G}_{-}))\ v_{0}\,
, and
{\cal G}^{-}
is the
subalgebra of
{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}
generated by the negative root generators

[
66
]
.
More explicitly
[
60
]
,
v^{s}_{m,\beta}={\cal P}_{m,\beta}\,v_{0}
(or
v^{s}_{m,\beta}={\cal P}_{m,\beta}\,V_{\mu}\,v_{0}
for GVMs).
Then,
there exists
[
60
]

intertwining differential operator

{\cal D}_{m,\beta}:{\cal C}_{\chi(\Lambda)}\longrightarrow{\cal C}_{\chi(\Lambda-m\beta)}

(4)

given explicitly by:

{\cal D}_{m,\beta}={\cal P}_{m,\beta}(\widehat{{\cal G}^{-}})

(5)

where

\widehat{{\cal G}^{-}}
denotes the
right action
on the functions

{\cal F}
.

3
Restricted Weyl groups and related notions

In our exposition below, we shall use the so-called Dynkin labels:

m_{i}\equiv(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}_{i}),\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

(6)

where
\Lambda=\Lambda(\chi)
,
\rho
is half the sum of the positive roots of

{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

We shall use also the so-called
Harish–Chandra parameters
:

m_{\beta}\equiv(\Lambda+\rho,\beta^{\vee})\ ,

(7)

where
\beta
is any
positive root of
{\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}}
. These parameters are redundant, since
they are expressed in terms of the Dynkin labels; however, some
statements are best formulated in their terms. (Clearly, both the Dynkin labels and
Harish–Chandra parameters have their origin in the BGG reducibility condition (
3
).)

Next, we recall the action of the Weyl group on highest weights:

w_{\beta}(\Lambda)~\doteq~\Lambda-(\Lambda+\rho,\beta^{\vee})\beta

(8)

and thus,

w_{\beta}(\Lambda)~=~\Lambda-m_{\beta}\beta

(9)

and the shifted weight in (
4
) results by the action of the Weyl group as in (
9
).

Next we mention the important notion of
restricted Weyl group
. We first need the so-called restricted roots.

Let
{\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}
be the
restricted root system
of
({\cal G},{\cal A}^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}~\doteq~\{\lambda\in{\cal A}^{\prime*}~|~\lambda\neq 0,\ {\cal G}^{\lambda}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}\neq 0\}~,

\displaystyle{\cal G}^{\lambda}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}\doteq\{X\in{\cal G}~|~[Y,X]=\lambda(Y)X~,~~\forall Y\in{\cal A}^{\prime}\}~.

(10)

The elements of

{\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}
are called
{\cal A}^{\prime}
-restricted roots
.

[The terminology comes from the fact that things may be arranged so that these roots
are obtained as restriction to
{\cal A}^{\prime}
of some roots of the
root system
{\Delta}
of the pair
({\cal G}^{\mathbb{C}},{\cal H}^{\mathbb{C}})
.]

For
\lambda\in{\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}\,
,
{\cal G}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}^{\lambda}
are called
{\cal A}^{\prime}
-restricted root
spaces
, dim
{}_{R}~{\cal G}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}^{\lambda}\geq 1
.

Next we introduce some ordering (e.g., the lexicographic one) in
{\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}\,
.
Accordingly the latter is split into positive and
negative restricted roots:
{\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}={\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}^{+}\cup{\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}^{-}
.

Furthermore, we introduce the simple restricted root system
{\Delta}^{R}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}\,
,
which is the simple root system of the restricted roots.
Next we introduce the
restricted Weyl reflections
: for each root
\lambda\in{\Delta}_{{\cal A}^{\prime}}^{+}

we define a reflection
s_{\lambda}
in
{{\cal A}^{\prime}}^{*}
:

s_{\lambda}(\mu)~\equiv~\mu-2\frac{(\lambda,\mu)}{(\lambda,\lambda)}\lambda\ ,\quad\mu\in{{\cal A}^{\prime}}^{*}

(11)

Clearly,
s_{\lambda}(\lambda)=-\lambda\,
,
s_{\lambda}^{2}=
id
{}_{{{\cal A}^{\prime}}^{*}}\,
.

The above reflections generate the
{\cal A}^{\prime}
-restricted Weyl group

W({\cal G},{{\cal A}^{\prime}})
.

The above may be applied to the case when instead of some
{\cal A}^{\prime}
we use an arbitrary subalgebra
{\cal H}^{\prime}
of
{\cal H}
.

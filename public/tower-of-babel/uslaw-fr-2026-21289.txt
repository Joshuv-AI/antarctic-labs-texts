Air Quality Plan; Arizona; Agricultural Best Management Practices 
Rules for the West Pinal County PM10 Nonattainment Area

AGENCY: Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

ACTION: Proposed rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) is proposing to 
approve a revision to the Arizona Department of Environmental Quality 
(ADEQ or ``State'') portion of the Arizona State Implementation Plan 
(SIP). This revision concerns emissions of particulate matter (PM) that 
are 10 micrometers ([mu]m) or smaller in diameter (PM10) 
from agricultural crop and animal operations in the West Pinal County 
PM10 nonattainment area. We are proposing to approve two 
local rules to regulate these emission sources under the Clean Air Act 
(CAA or ``Act'').

I. Executive Summary

A. What Action is the EPA Taking?

    The EPA is proposing to approve an August 13, 2026, SIP submittal 
from Arizona that requests to add two rules to the Arizona SIP. The 
rules relate to Arizona's Agricultural Best Management Practices 
(AgBMP) program and are from the Arizona Administrative Code (AAC): 
Title 18, Chapter 2, Section 610.03, ``Agricultural PM General Permit 
for Crop Operations; Pinal County PM Nonattainment Area,'' amended 
April 2, 2026 (``R18-2-610.03''), and R18-2-611.03, ``Agricultural PM 
General Permit for Animal Operations: Pinal County PM Nonattainment 
Area,'' amended April 2, 2026 (``R18-2-611.03''). These rules only 
apply within the West Pinal County PM10 nonattainment area 
within Pinal County, Arizona, and were submitted to support the State's 
attainment plan for the area, including the requirement to meet the 
Best Available Control Measures (BACM) requirement. Previously, the EPA 
proposed, but did not finalize, a limited approval and limited 
disapproval of earlier versions of these two rules.\1\ The rules were 
recently revised by Arizona's AgBMP Committee and we are now proposing 
to fully approve them under the CAA.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \1\ 90 FR 5790 (Jan. 17, 2025).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    R18-2-610.03 regulates PM10 emissions from commercial 
crop operations in the West Pinal County PM10 nonattainment 
area. R18-2-611.03 regulates PM10 emissions from commercial 
dairy operations, beef cattle feedlots, commercial poultry facilities, 
and commercial swine facilities in the West Pinal County 
PM10 nonattainment area. Both rules require operators to 
implement best management practices (BMPs) selected from regulatory 
menus to reduce fugitive PM10 emissions from their 
operations. If finalized as proposed, R18-2-610.03 and R18-2-611.03 
would be incorporated into the federally enforceable portion of the 
Arizona SIP.

B. What is the legal authority and what are the requirements?

    We are taking this proposed action under section 110 of the CAA. 
Pursuant to CAA section 110(a), States are required to submit SIPs to 
the EPA that provide for the implementation, maintenance, and 
enforcement of the national ambient air quality standards (NAAQS). CAA 
section 110(k) requires SIPs to meet certain procedural requirements 
and requires the EPA to approve or disapprove SIP submissions. CAA 
section 110(l) requires States to provide public notice and an 
opportunity for public hearing of SIP revisions prior to their 
submittal and prohibits the EPA from approving any SIP revisions that 
would interfere with attainment or maintenance of a NAAQS, reasonable 
further progress, or other applicable requirements of the CAA. If a SIP 
submittal meets all the applicable CAA requirements, then the EPA must 
approve the submittal under CAA section 110(k)(3).
    For this submittal to be approved, we must consider CAA 
requirements related to the emission limitations in the submitted 
rules. CAA sections 110(a)(2)(A) and 302(k) require emission 
limitations in the SIP to be enforceable and continuous, respectively. 
CAA section 172(c)(6) requires SIPs for nonattainment areas ``include 
enforceable emission limitations, and such other control measures means 
or techniques . . . as well as schedules and timetables for compliance, 
as may be necessary or appropriate to provide for attainment of such 
standard in such area by the applicable attainment date . . . .'' Also, 
40 CFR part 51, subpart K requires SIPs to contain legally enforceable 
procedures for monitoring the status of compliance with the 
requirements in the control strategy. While these rules were submitted 
to meet BACM requirements under CAA section 189(b)(1)(B), we are not 
evaluating them for BACM in this action. The EPA will evaluate BACM in 
an upcoming action on the attainment plan for the West Pinal County 
PM10 nonattainment area.
    We are proposing that this submittal meets all applicable CAA 
requirements and are proposing to fully approve it under CAA section 
110(k)(3).

II. Background

    R18-2-610.03 and R18-2-611.03 regulate emissions of PM, including 
PM10, which contribute to effects that are harmful to human 
health and the environment, including premature mortality, aggravation 
of respiratory and cardiovascular disease, decreased lung function, 
visibility impairment, and damage to vegetation and ecosystems. Section 
110(a) of the CAA requires States to submit regulations that control PM 
emissions, as necessary to meet the requirements of the Act.
    Previously, on January 17, 2025, we proposed a limited approval and 
limited disapproval of earlier versions of R18-2-610.03 and R18-2-
611.03 (``January 2025 AgBMP proposal''), finding that the rules 
strengthened the SIP but identifying enforceability and rule clarity 
concerns.\2\ Following that proposal, the Arizona AgBMP Committee 
revised both rules and ADEQ resubmitted them to the EPA. The updated 
rules are the subject of this proposal.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \2\ 90 FR 5790 (Jan. 17, 2025).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The January 2025 AgBMP proposal also included proposing to fully 
approve two related AgBMP definitions rules and the AgBMP statute: AAC 
R18-2-610, ``Definitions for R18-2-610.01, R18-2-610.02, and R18-2-
610.03,'' amended November 26, 2021; AAC R18-2-611, ``Definitions for 
R18-2-611.01, R18-2-611.02, and R18-2-611.03,'' amended November 26, 
2021; and A.R.S. section 49-457. These provisions are not addressed in 
this proposed rulemaking, and we are not requesting comment on them.
    We intend to concurrently finalize action on the January 2025 AgBMP 
proposal with our final action on this rulemaking.

IV. The EPA's Evaluation of Arizona's Submittal

B. Does the submittal meet the evaluation criteria?

    We evaluated these rules to ensure they meet CAA requirements and 
are consistent with relevant guidance regarding enforceability and SIP 
revisions.
    With respect to emission limitations being enforceable and 
continuous, the revised rules specify clear requirements, providing 
regulatory certainty to the regulated community. The rules' 
implementation details are also sufficient for State enforcement, and 
emission limits apply on a continuous basis. The resubmitted rules 
require annual, non-anonymous reporting of BMP selections and 
certifications directly to ADEQ (or through the Arizona Department of 
Agriculture as an optional intermediary for crop operations), with form 
contents that include identifying BMP selections for the upcoming year, 
area and crop/operation identification, and certification of prior-year 
implementation. These requirements enable ADEQ to identify which BMPs 
each operator has committed to implement, verify implementation during 
inspections, and document noncompliance on an individual operator 
basis. These revisions strengthen the clarity and enforceability of the 
rules, address the concerns identified in our January 2025 AgBMP 
proposal, and meet the relevant CAA enforceability requirements.
    With respect to SIP revisions under CAA section 110(l), approval of 
the revised rules would not interfere with any applicable CAA 
requirements, including requirements for reasonable further progress 
and attainment. R18-2-610.03 is more stringent than the current SIP-
approved version of R18-2-610.03, requiring two BMPs per category, 
adding new BMP categories, and adding a new 20 percent opacity limit 
for high-traffic unpaved areas. R18-2-611.03 is a new rule with respect 
to the Arizona SIP and is thus a substantial strengthening. The rules 
do not interfere with any other relevant CAA requirements. The EPA's 
technical support document (TSD) has more information about these 
rules.
    Based on our review, the rules meet the relevant CAA requirements.

incorporate these rules into the federally enforceable SIP.
    Specifically, the EPA is proposing to approve AAC R18-2-610.03, 
``Agricultural PM General Permit for Crop Operations; Pinal County PM 
Nonattainment Area,'' and AAC R18-2-

VI. Statutory and Executive Order Reviews

    Under the Clean Air Act, the Administrator is required to approve a 
SIP submission that complies with the provisions of the Clean Air Act 
and applicable Federal regulations. 42 U.S.C. 7410(k); 40 CFR 52.02(a). 
Thus, in reviewing SIP submissions, the EPA's role is to approve State 
choices, provided that they meet the criteria of the Clean Air Act. 
Accordingly, this proposed action merely proposes approval of State law 
as meeting Federal requirements and does not impose additional 
requirements beyond those imposed by State law. For that reason, this 
action:
     Is not a significant regulatory action subject to review 
by the Office of Management and Budget under Executive Order 12866 (58 
FR 51735, October 4, 1993);
     Is not an Executive Order 14192 (90 FR 9065, February 6, 
2025) regulatory action because this proposed action is not significant 
under Executive Order 12866;
     Does not impose an information collection burden under the 
provisions of the Paperwork Reduction Act (44 U.S.C. 3501 et seq.);
     Is certified as not having a significant economic impact 
on a substantial number of small entities under the Regulatory 
Flexibility Act (5 U.S.C. 601 et seq.);
     Does not contain any unfunded mandate or significantly or 
uniquely affect small governments, as described in the Unfunded 
Mandates Reform Act of 1995 (Pub. L. 104-4);
     Does not have federalism implications as specified in 
Executive Order 13132 (64 FR 43255, August 10, 1999);
     Is not subject to Executive Order 13045 (62 FR 19885, 
April 23, 1997) because it approves a State program;
     Is not a significant regulatory action subject to 
Executive Order 13211 (66 FR 28355, May 22, 2001); and
     Is not subject to requirements of section 12(d) of the 
National Technology Transfer and Advancement Act of 1995 (15 U.S.C. 272 
note) because application of those requirements would be inconsistent 
with the Clean Air Act.
    In addition, the SIP is not approved to apply on any Indian 
reservation land or in any other area where the EPA or an Indian Tribe 
has demonstrated that a Tribe has jurisdiction. In those areas of 
Indian country, the rule does not have Tribal implications and will not 
impose substantial direct costs on Tribal governments or preempt Tribal 
law as specified by Executive Order 13175 (65 FR 67249, November 9, 
2000).

A Filtration of the Chow Group of Zero-Cycles for a Product of Curves and an Abelian Variety Thanks: (*) Department of Mathematics, University of Virginia, 119 Kerchof Hall, 141 Cabell Dr., Charlottesville, VA, 22903, USA. Email: gvs3ka@virginia.edu

Abstract
. In this paper we define a descending filtration on the Chow group of zero cycles for varieties of the form A × C 1 × ⋯ × C d A\times C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{d} where A A is an abelian variety and each C i C_{i} is a smooth projective curve. We give explicit generators and relations for the successive quotients of this filtration by showing that they can be described by Somekawa K-groups. This extends the work of Raskind and Spiess who proved this result for products of curves and Gazaki who proved this for abelian varieties.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a field
k
such that
X(k)\neq\emptyset
, and let
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
denote the group of zero cycles modulo rational equivalence. There is a well-defined degree map

\text{deg}:\text{CH}_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}

and we denote the kernel by
A_{0}(X)
. When
X
is a curve,
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
coincides with the Picard group
\text{Pic}(X)
, and the Abel-Jacobi map

A_{0}(X)\to\text{Jac}_{X}(k)

is an isomorphism. In higher dimensions, however, the situation is more complicated. Like in the case of curves, there is an abelian variety
\Alb_{X}
, the Albanese variety of
X
, which is universal with respect to maps from
X
to abelian varieties and a homomorphism

\alb_{X}:A_{0}(X)\to\Alb_{X}(k).

Unlike the Abel-Jacobi map for curves, however, this map is very far from being an isomorphism in general. In particular, the kernel of this map can be quite large. Thus, we now have a filtration

\ker(\alb_{X})\subset A_{0}(X)\subset\text{CH}_{0}(X)

where the first successive quotient is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
and the second is isomorphic to a subgroup of the
k
-points of an abelian variety. Over many fields of interest, the structure of the group of rational points on an Abelian variety is well understood, so the main obstruction to our understanding of
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
is in the kernel of the Albanese map
\alb_{X}
.

The structure of the Albanese kernel is very sensitive to changes in the base field. For example, when
k=\mathbb{C}
and
X
is a surface with positive geometric genus,
\ker(\alb_{X})
is extremely large
[
3
]
,
[
16
]
, and it is not parameterized by the points of a variety. On the other hand, famous conjectures of Beilinson
[
2
]
(conjecture 5.0) and Bloch
[
5
]
(page 94) predict that
\ker(\alb_{X})
is finite for every smooth projective variety
X
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. The variance of the structure of the Albanese kernel as the base field changes illustrates the difficulty of studying this group. Indeed, there are very few known examples of the Beilinson-Bloch conjecture for surfaces defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

One way to make the problem of studying the Albanese kernel more tractable is by extending the natural filtration on
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
and identifying the successive quotients with groups which are more computable. In this paper, we construct a descending integral filtration

\ldots F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\subset\ldots\subset F^{1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\subset F^{0}\text{CH}_{0}(X)=\text{CH}_{0}(X)

on the Chow group of zero-cycles when
X\cong C_{1}\times\ldots C_{d}\times A
where
A
is an abelian variety, and
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
are smooth, projective, geometrically integral curves with a
k
-rational point. We relate the successive quotients of this filtration to certain
K
-groups as studied by Somekawa in
[
20
]
.

More precisely, Somekawa defines the groups
K(k;G_{1},\ldots,G_{r})
for semi-abelian varieties
G_{1},\ldots,G_{r}
. These groups are a generalization of Milnor
K
-groups in the sense that when
G_{i}=\mathbb{G}_{m}
for all
i
,
K(k;G_{1},\ldots,G_{r})\cong K^{M}_{r}(k)
. We define a natural quotient of the Somekawa
K
-groups and prove they are isomorphic to the successive quotients of our filtration up to bounded torsion. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety and let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
be smooth projective geometrically irreducible curves such that
C(k)\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists an isomorphism

\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]\otimes\frac{F^{r}(C_{1}\times\ldots C_{d}\times A)}{F^{r+1}(C_{1}\times\ldots C_{d}\times A)}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]\otimes\Phi_{r}}\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]\otimes S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A).

Here
S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)
is a quotient of a Somekawa
K
-group as described above (see Definition
2.1
). The map
\Phi_{r}
is defined in section
3
. Importantly, this filtration extends the natural filtration of
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
in the sense that
F^{1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)=A_{0}(X)
and
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)=\ker(\alb_{X})
. One of the main utilities of this is that the
K
-groups give explicit generators and relations for the successive quotients, which aids in the study of the Albanese kernel of
X
. To illustrate this point, we provide some computations in section
6
to show how this filtration might be used to study the Albanese kernel in a special case. Moreover, our filtration is constructed over any perfect field and is integral. These properties are essential for the filtration to be useful in studying questions of arithmetic interest.

Such a filtration has been constructed in the case when
X
is a product of curves by Raskind and Spiess in
[
18
]
. It has also been constructed in the case when
X
is an abelian variety by Gazaki in
[
10
]
. Kakinoki extends the results of Gazaki in
[
14
]
to construct a filtration for all Chow groups
\text{CH}^{g+s}(A,s)
where
A
is an abelian variety,
g=\text{dim}(A)
, and
\text{CH}^{g+s}(A,s)
denote Bloch’s higher Chow groups
[
6
]
.

In fact, it is expected that similar filtrations of
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
should exist for any smooth projective variety
X
over
k
. More precisely, a conjecture of Beilinson predicts that there is an abelian category
\mathcal{MM}_{k}
(the category of mixed motives over
k
) and a spectral sequence

E^{\nu,\mu}_{2}=\text{Ext}^{\nu}_{\mathcal{MM}_{k}}(1,h^{\mu}(X)(m))\implies\text{CH}^{m}(X,2m-(\nu+\mu)).

In general, this spectral sequence is expected to degenerate after tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
, so we would obtain a filtration on
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
with the property that after tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
the
r
th successive quotient is isomorphic to the Ext group

\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{MM}_{k}}^{r}(1,h^{2d-r}(X)(d))

where
d
is the dimension of
X
(for more information on this conjecture of Beilinson and its applications see
[
12
]
). While this conjecture is far out of reach in general, the work of Raskind and Spiess in
[
18
]
provides strong evidence that the filtration they construct for a product of curves is the one arising from the above conjecture of Beilinson for the following reason. In
[
20
]
, Somekawa predicts that the
K
-groups he studies in that paper are isomorphic to certain
Ext
groups in the conjectural category of mixed motives. Assuming this conjecture, Raskind and Spiess show that their filtration has the correct motivic interpretation (see Remarks 2.4.2 of
[
18
]
). Given the fact that the category of mixed motives over
k
has not been established, Somekawa’s expectation cannot be verified. However, Kahn and Yamazaki in
[
13
]
prove ”the closest approximation to Somekawa’s conjecture” by showing that

K(k;G_{1},\ldots,G_{r})\cong\text{Hom}_{\textbf{DM}^{\text{eff}}_{-}}(\mathbb{Z},G_{1}[0]\otimes\cdots\otimes G_{r}[0])

where
\textbf{DM}^{\text{eff}}_{-}
is Voevodsky’s triangulated category of effective motivic complexes
1
1

1

In fact they show this isomorphism more generally for homotopy invariant Nisnevich sheaves with transfers
. Our filtration is an extension of the one constructed by Raskind and Spiess and the successive quotients are described by quotients of Somekawa
K
-groups. Therefore, the remarks above suggest that our filtration is closely related to the one that would arise from Beilinson’s conjecture.

Another indication that our filtration is the one predicted by Beilinson is given by the following vanishing theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
r>\text{dim}(C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{d}\times A)
,

F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{d}\times A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0.

Such a property is expected for motivic filtrations. Namely, the filtration should vanish rationally after the dimension of the variety. Thus, the above theorem provides more evidence that our filtration is the expected one coming from the conjectural category of mixed motives. Moreover, the rational finiteness of our filtration plays a major role in computing generators and relations of the Albanese kernel as demonstrated by the example in Section
6
.

2.1.
Somekawa K-groups

We now recall the definition of the K-groups defined in

Let

\tilde{K}(k;A_{1},\ldots,A_{r}):=\left[\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}A_{1}(k^{\prime})\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{r}(k^{\prime})\right]/PF

where the sum is taken over all finite extensions

and

is the subgroup generated by elements of the following form. If

is a tower of finite extensions,
a_{i}\in A_{i}(L)
for some

a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Tr_{L/E}(a_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{r}-\res_{L/E}(a_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes\res_{L/E}(a_{r})\in PF.

Relations of the above form will be referred to as the projection formula.

Notation 2.1
.

We follow the convention of denoting a simple tensor
a_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes a_{n}
in the component

.

Define the
K

S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A):=g_{r}^{-1}\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq t\leq r}\bigoplus_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{r-t}\leq d}S_{r}(k;J_{i_{1}},\ldots,J_{i_{r-t}},\underbrace{A,\ldots,A}_{t})\right).

That is, the group
S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)

\Tr_{L/k}:K(L;A_{1}\times_{k}L,\ldots,A_{n}\times_{k}L)\to K(k;A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})

\res_{L/k}:K(k;A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\to K(L;A_{1}\times_{k}L,\ldots,A_{n}\times_{k}L)

\Tr_{L/k}(\res_{L/k}(\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}_{k^{\prime}/k}))=[L:k]\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}_{k^{\prime}/k}.

3.
The Filtration

In this section, we give a filtration of the Chow group
\text{CH}_{0}(C_{1}\times\ldots\times C_{d}\times A)
by defining maps from the Chow groups to the Somekawa K-groups defined in section 2.1. Recall that for each curve
C_{i}
, we have fixed
k
-points
p_{i}\in C_{i}(k)
and maps
\iota_{p_{i}}:C_{i}\to J_{i}
such that
\iota_{p_{i}}(p_{i})=0
. We will identify
J_{i}(L)
with
\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{i}\times_{k}L)
throughout. Under this identification,
\iota_{p_{i}}
can be described on
k
-rational points by
\iota_{p_{i}}(y)=[y]-[p_{i}]
for
y\in C_{i}(k)
. For the rest of this paper we will denote
X:=C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{d}\times A
and
\varphi_{X}:X\to\Alb_{X}
the map to the Albanese variety. We will also denote the identity element of

by

rather than

when it is clear from context that it is an element of

.

.

In the case that any

has genus

is an isomorphism.

Therefore, we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{0}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}\oplus K_{1}(k;J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{d}\times A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}\oplus(J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{d}\times A)(k).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{0}\oplus\Phi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{deg}\oplus\alb_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{id}\oplus\cong}

Then
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X\times A)
is by definition the kernel of the horizontal map. And the Albanese kernel is the kernel of the vertical map, so these two groups agree.
∎

4.
Proof of the Main Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. The way that we prove this theorem is by defining maps

\Psi_{r}:S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)\to F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)

and then computing
\Phi_{r}\circ\Psi_{r}
.

4.1.
Definition of
\Psi_{r}
and Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
k^{\prime}/k
be a finite extension. Then we define
\pi_{k^{\prime}/k}:X_{k^{\prime}}\to X
to be the natural projection. We also define the projection maps

pr_{i,{k^{\prime}}}:(C_{1})_{k^{\prime}}\times_{k^{\prime}}\cdots\times_{k^{\prime}}(C_{d})_{k^{\prime}}\times_{k^{\prime}}A_{k^{\prime}}\to(C_{i})_{k^{\prime}}

and

pr_{A,k^{\prime}}:(C_{1})_{k^{\prime}}\times_{k^{\prime}}\cdots\times_{k^{\prime}}(C_{d})_{k^{\prime}}\times_{k^{\prime}}A_{k^{\prime}}\to A_{k^{\prime}}.

Throughout this article, we will also abuse notation by denoting an element of

\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})

by
\{(z^{1}_{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}
even though multilinearity and the projection formula do not apply. Finally, if
\alpha_{i}\in\text{CH}_{0}(A_{k^{\prime}})
for
i\in I
a finite indexing set. Then we denote by

\bigodot_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}

the Pontryagin product of all
\alpha_{i}
. Using this notation, we make the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Define the map

\Psi^{\prime}_{r}:\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\to\text{CH}_{0}(X)

by

\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\mapsto\sum_{s=0}^{d}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{s}\leq d}\sum_{\varphi_{I}:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}}(\pi_{k^{\prime}/k})_{*}(W)

where

\displaystyle W=\left(pr_{1,k^{\prime}}^{*}([p_{1}]_{k^{\prime}})\cdots pr_{i_{1},k^{\prime}}^{*}(z^{\varphi_{I}(i_{1})}_{i_{1}})\cdots pr_{i_{s},k^{\prime}}^{*}(z^{\varphi_{I}(i_{s})}_{i_{s}})\cdots pr_{d,k^{\prime}}^{*}([p_{d}]_{k^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle\cdot pr_{A,k^{\prime}}^{*}\left(\bigodot_{j\in\{1,\ldots r\}\setminus\varphi_{I}(I)}([a_{j}]_{k^{\prime}}-[0]_{k^{\prime}})\right).

The
z^{j}_{i}\in J_{i}(k^{\prime})
are viewed as arbitrary elements of
\text{Pic}^{0}((C_{i})_{k^{\prime}})
and
I:=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Here the
i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}
specify a subset of the curves
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
and the injection
\varphi_{I}:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}
assigns to each curve a unique index from
1,\ldots,r
. The reason we demand that
\varphi_{I}
is injective corresponds to the fact that in the group
S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)
each direct summand has at most one copy of each Jacobian (see Definition
2.1
). Therefore, we make this requirement so that
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
will descend to a group homomorphism from the quotient
S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)
.

Example 4.3
.

Let us consider the map
\Psi_{2}^{\prime}
in the case when
d=1
. Denote
z_{1}^{1}:=\sum_{y\in C}n_{y}^{1}[y]
and
z_{1}^{2}:=\sum_{y\in C}n_{y}^{2}[y]
. Then we get that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}(\{(z_{1}^{1},a_{1}),(z_{1}^{2},a_{2})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}

\displaystyle=[p_{1}\times a_{1}+a_{2}]_{k}-[p_{1}\times a_{1}]-[p_{1}\times a_{2}]+[p_{1}\times 0]

(4.5)

\displaystyle+(\pi_{k^{\prime}/k})_{*}\left(\sum_{y\in C}n_{y}^{1}\left([y\times a_{2}]_{k^{\prime}}-[y\times 0]_{k^{\prime}}\right)\right)

(4.6)

\displaystyle+(\pi_{k^{\prime}/k})_{*}\left(\sum_{y\in C}n_{y}^{2}\left([y\times a_{1}]_{k^{\prime}}-[y\times 0]_{k^{\prime}}\right)\right).

Note that line (4.4) corresponds to a zero cycle coming only from the abelian variety. The line (4.5) is a zero cycle which is impacted by
z_{1}^{1}
and
a_{2}
while (4.6) is impacted by
z_{1}^{2}
and
a_{1}
.

This example illustrates what occurs in the general definition of
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
. It interpolates between zero cycles coming purely from the abelian variety and those which come from a mix of some subset of the curves
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
and the abelian variety.

Proposition 4.7
.

The map
\Psi^{\prime}_{r}
is well defined.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the intersection product and Pontryagin product determine well-defined maps on Chow groups, but we provide the full details of this argument here.

Let
\tilde{z}_{i}^{t}:=z_{i}^{t}+\dv f_{i}^{t}
for
1\leq i\leq d
and
1\leq t\leq r
where
f_{i}^{t}\in K(C_{i}\times_{k}k^{\prime})^{\times}
. Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in A(k^{\prime})
. Then we need to show that

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\{(\tilde{z}_{1}^{1},\ldots,\tilde{z}_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(\tilde{z}_{1}^{r},\ldots,\tilde{z}_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}.

Let us assume that
k^{\prime}=k
as the argument is the same in the general case. We have

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(\tilde{z}_{1}^{1},\ldots,\tilde{z}_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(\tilde{z}_{1}^{r},\ldots,\tilde{z}_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k/k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{d}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{s}\leq d}\sum_{\varphi_{I}:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}}

\displaystyle\left(pr_{1,k}^{*}([p_{1}])\cdots pr_{i_{1},k}^{*}\left(z^{\varphi_{I}(i_{1})}_{i_{1}}+\dv f_{i_{1}}^{\varphi_{I}(i_{1})}\right)\cdots pr_{i_{s},k}^{*}\left(z^{\varphi_{I}(i_{s})}_{i_{s}}+\dv f_{i_{s}}^{\varphi_{I}(i_{s})}\right)\cdots pr_{d,k}^{*}([p_{d}])\right)

\displaystyle\cdot pr_{A,k}^{*}\left(\bigodot_{j\in\{1,\ldots r\}\setminus\varphi_{I}(I)}([a_{j}]-[0])\right).

But after expanding using multilinearity of the intersection product and the fact that
pr_{j,k}^{*}(\dv f_{j}^{i})=0
for any
j,i
, we have that the above is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{d}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{s}\leq d}\sum_{\varphi_{I}:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}}

\displaystyle\left(pr_{1,k}^{*}([p_{1}])\cdots pr_{i_{1},k}^{*}(z^{\varphi_{I}(i_{1})}_{i_{1}})\cdots pr_{i_{s},k}^{*}(z^{\varphi_{I}(i_{s})}_{i_{s}})\cdots pr_{d,k}^{*}([p_{d}])\right)

\displaystyle\cdot pr_{A,k^{\prime}}^{*}\left(\bigodot_{j\in\{1,\ldots r\}\setminus\varphi_{I}(I)}([a_{j}]-[0])\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k/k}.

So the map is well defined.
∎

The most involved aspect in proving Theorem
1.1
is verifying the following two key lemmas. We give the proofs in section
4.2
and use the results in this section to show the main theorem.

Lemma 4.8
.

The map
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
induces a group homomorphism

\Psi_{r}:S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)\to F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X).

The content of this lemma is that the image of the map
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
lands in
F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
and when we take the composite

\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\ldots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\Psi_{r}^{\prime}}F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\to F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X),

which we denote
\Psi_{r}
, this map induces a well-defined group homomorphism on the quotient group
S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)
.

Lemma 4.9
.

The composite

S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)\xrightarrow{\Psi_{r}}F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{\Phi_{r}}S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)

is multiplication by
r!
.

Assuming these lemmas, we are able to prove the main theorem.

Theorem
1.1
.

The map
\Phi_{r}
induces an isomorphism

\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]\otimes\frac{F^{r}(X\times A)}{F^{r+1}(X\times A)}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]\otimes S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A).

Proof.

We have a short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{F^{r}(X)}{F^{r+1}(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)}{\text{Im}(\Phi_{r})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{r}}

But by Lemma
4.1
, we have that the last group is
r!
torsion, so it will become 0 after tensoring with
\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]
. Thus we have

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{F^{r}(X)}{F^{r+1}(X)}\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{r}}

But
\mathbb{Z}[1/r!]
is torsion free and hence it is a flat
\mathbb{Z}
module. Thus, the first map is injective as well, which proves the isomorphism.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Key Lemmas

The purpose of this section is to prove Lemmas
4.1
and
4.1
. We begin with a few preliminary results which will establish important properties of
\Phi_{r}
and
\Psi^{\prime}_{r}
. These properties will be used throughout the rest of this article. The first fact is that the map
\Phi_{r}
behaves well with the trace map on Somekawa
K

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\alpha\in\text{CH}_{0}(X_{k^{\prime}})
. Then

\Phi_{r}((\pi_{k^{\prime}/k})_{*}(\alpha))=\Tr_{k^{\prime}/k}(\Phi_{r}^{k^{\prime}}(\alpha))

Proof.

Because all of these maps are
\mathbb{Z}
-linear, it suffices to take
\alpha=[x]_{k^{\prime}}
for
x\in X_{k^{\prime}}
a closed point. Let
b:=\pi_{k^{\prime}/k}(x)\in X
,
i:X\to\Alb_{X}
be the closed embedding, and
i_{k^{\prime}}:X_{k^{\prime}}\to\Alb_{X_{k^{\prime}}}
be its base change. Then we get

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}((\pi_{k^{\prime}/k})_{*}([x]_{k^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle=\Phi_{r}([k(x):k(b)][b])

\displaystyle=[k(x):k(b)]\{i(b),\ldots,i(b)\}_{k(b)/k}

\displaystyle=\{[k(x):k(b)]i(b),\ldots,i(b)\}_{k(b)/k}

\displaystyle=\{\Tr_{k(x)/k(b)}\res_{k(x)/k(mb)}i(b),\ldots,i(b)\}_{k(b)/k}

\displaystyle=\{\res_{k(x)/k(b)}i(b),\ldots,\res_{k(x)/k(b)}i(b)\}_{k(x)/k}\qquad\text{by Projection Formula}

and

\displaystyle\Tr_{k^{\prime}/k}\Phi^{k^{\prime}}_{r}([x]_{k^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\Tr_{k^{\prime}/k}\{i_{k^{\prime}}(x),\ldots,i_{k^{\prime}}(x)\}_{k(x)/k^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\{\pi_{k^{\prime}/k}\circ i_{k^{\prime}}(x),\ldots,\pi_{k^{\prime}/k}\circ i_{k^{\prime}}(x)\}_{k(x)/k}

\displaystyle=\{i\circ\pi_{k^{\prime}/k}\circ x,\ldots,i\circ\pi_{k^{\prime}/k}\circ x\}_{k(x)/k}

\displaystyle=\{\res_{k(x)/k(b)}(i(b)),\ldots,\res_{k(x)/k(b)}(i(b))\}_{k(x)/k}.

∎

The second preliminary result says that
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
satisfies a partial version of multilinearity (we will verify
\Psi_{r}
is fully multilinear later).

Proposition 4.11
.

Fix some
1\leq t\leq r
. For
1\leq j\leq r
,
1\leq i\leq d

a_{j}\in A(k^{\prime})
,
z_{i}^{j}\in J_{i}(k^{\prime})
and
\tilde{z}_{i}^{t}\in J_{i}(k^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{t}+\tilde{z}_{1}^{t},\ldots,z_{d}^{t}+\tilde{z}_{d}^{t},a_{t}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{t},\ldots,z_{d}^{t},0),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle+\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(\tilde{z}_{1}^{t},\ldots,\tilde{z}_{d}^{t},a_{t}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right).

So
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
satisfies multilinearity except for in the abelian variety components.

Proof.

Since
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
is invariant under the action of the symmetric group, it suffices to show the proposition for

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},0),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle+\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,a_{1}),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

and for step two we show that

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1}+\tilde{z}_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1}+\tilde{z}_{d}^{1},0),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},0),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle+\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(\tilde{z}_{z}^{1},\ldots,\tilde{z}_{d}^{1},0),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right).

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},0),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{d}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{s}\leq d}\sum_{\varphi_{I}:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}}(\pi_{L/k})_{*}(W)

where

\displaystyle W=\left(pr_{1,k^{\prime}}^{*}([p_{1}]_{k^{\prime}})\cdots pr_{i_{1},k^{\prime}}^{*}(z^{\varphi_{I}(i_{1})}_{i_{1}})\cdots pr_{i_{s},k^{\prime}}^{*}(z^{\varphi_{I}(i_{s})}_{i_{s}})\cdots pr_{d,k^{\prime}}^{*}([p_{d}]_{k^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle\cdot pr_{A,k^{\prime}}^{*}\left(\bigodot_{j\in\{1,\ldots r\}\setminus\varphi_{I}(I)}([a_{j}]_{k^{\prime}}-[0]_{k^{\prime}})\right).

However, since
a_{1}=0

1
is not in the image of
\varphi_{I}
, then
([a_{1}]_{k^{\prime}}-[0]_{k^{\prime}})=0

\sum_{\varphi_{I}:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}}
to be over all injections such that

is in the image of
\varphi_{I}
. Also,

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,a_{1}),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{d}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{s}\leq d}\sum_{\varphi_{I}:\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\hookrightarrow\{2,\ldots,r\}}(\pi_{L/k})_{*}(W).

\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},0),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\varphi_{I}

\varphi_{I}

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\left(\{(z_{1}^{1}+\tilde{z}_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1}+\tilde{z}_{d}^{1},0),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
L/E/k
be a tower of finite extensions and fix
1\leq t\leq r
. If
z_{j}^{t}\in Pic^{0}((C_{i})_{L})\cong J_{j}(L)
for
1\leq j\leq d
and for

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\{\res_{L/E}(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{t},\ldots,z_{d}^{t},0),\ldots,\res_{L/E}(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{L/k}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\{(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a_{1}),\ldots,\Tr_{L/E}(z_{1}^{t},\ldots,z_{d}^{t},0),\ldots(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{E/k}.

This results follows from the projection formula for cycles which we recall here. Let

be a flat, proper morphism. Let
\alpha\in\text{CH}^{*}(Y)
and
\beta\in\text{CH}^{*}(Z)

f_{*}(\alpha\cdot f^{*}(\beta))=f_{*}(\alpha)\cdot\beta

where

is the flat pullback.

By Proposition

is symmetric in the order of its components, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\{(0,\ldots,0,z_{i_{1}},0,\ldots,0),\ldots,\res_{L/E}(0,\ldots,0,z_{i_{s}},0,\ldots 0),

\displaystyle\res_{L/E}(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,\res_{L/E}(0,\ldots,0,a_{r-s})\}_{L/k}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\{\Tr_{L/E}(0,\ldots,0,z_{i_{1}},0,\ldots,0),\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,z_{i_{s}},0,\ldots 0),(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,a_{r-s})\}_{E/k}.

For some

. Note that

\Tr_{L/E}(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},0)=((\pi_{L/E})_{*}(z_{1}^{1}),\ldots,(\pi_{L/E})_{*}(z_{d}^{1}),0)

and

\res_{L/E}(z_{1}^{1},\ldots,z_{d}^{1},a)=((\pi_{L/E})^{*}(z_{1}^{1}),\ldots,(\pi_{L/E})^{*}(z_{d}^{1}),\res_{L/E}(a)).

Thus by the definition of
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
, we get

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\{(0,\ldots,0,z_{i_{1}},0,\ldots,0),\ldots,\res_{L/E}(0,\ldots,0,z_{i_{s}},0,\ldots 0),

\displaystyle\res_{L/E}(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,\res_{L/E}(0,\ldots,0,a_{r-s})\}_{L/k}=(\pi_{L/k})_{*}(W)

where

\displaystyle W=\left(pr_{1,L}^{*}([p_{1}]_{L})\cdots pr_{i_{1},L}^{*}(z_{i_{1}})\cdots pr_{i_{2},L}^{*}(\pi_{L/E}^{*}z_{i_{1}})\cdots pr_{i_{s},L}^{*}(\pi_{L/E}^{*}z_{i_{s}})\cdots pr_{d,L}^{*}([p_{d}]_{L})\right)

\displaystyle\cdot pr_{A,L}^{*}\left(\bigodot_{j\in\{1,\ldots r-s\}}([a_{j}]_{L}-[0]_{L})\right)

Note that for all

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}\ldots\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots n_{y_{i_{s}}}\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}\sum_{1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r-s}

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}\leq r-1}\beta_{N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}}}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}}),

where
\beta_{N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}}}:=\binom{r-1}{N_{i_{1}}}\binom{r-1-N_{i_{1}}}{N_{i_{2}}}\cdots\binom{r-1-N_{i_{1}}-\ldots-N_{i_{s-1}}}{N_{i_{s}}}

We will proceed in two steps. In the first step we consider the case when
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}\geq s
. Specifically, we prove that for fixed
y_{i_{1}},\ldots,y_{i_{s}}
and fixed
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}\geq s
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}\sum_{1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r-s}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})=0.

In the second step we consider the case when
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}<s
. Specifically, we prove that for fixed
j
,
1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r-s
, and
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}<s

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}\ldots\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots n_{y_{i_{s}}}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})=0.

Remark 4.18
.

These two cases mirror those of the proof when
d=1
. The case of
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}\geq s
is the same argument that is used in the first part of the proof of one curve. We use a counting argument to show that all the relevant symbols vanish. The case of
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}<s
is analogous to step 2 of the proof when
d=1
. Here we again use multilinearity and the fact that the cycles coming from the curve are degree
0
to kill all of the symbols.

Let us first consider the case when
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}\geq s
. For
\overrightarrow{\ell}\in\{1,\ldots,r-s\}^{r-1-(N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}})}
define symbols

\displaystyle X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r-s})_{\overrightarrow{\ell}}:=

\displaystyle\{\underbrace{(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{1}},0\ldots,0),\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{1}},0\ldots,0)}_{N_{i_{1}}},\ldots

\displaystyle\underbrace{(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{s}},0\ldots,0),\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{s}},0\ldots,0)}_{N_{i_{s}}},

\displaystyle(0,\ldots,0,a_{\ell_{1}}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,a_{\ell_{r-1-(N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}})}})\}_{k/k}.

Note that by multilinearity of symbols in
S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)
, we have that

X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})=\sum_{\overrightarrow{\ell}\in\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{j}\}^{r-1-(N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}})}}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r-s})_{\overrightarrow{\ell}}.

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}\sum_{1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r-s}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}\sum_{1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r-s}\sum_{\overrightarrow{\ell}\in\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{j}\}^{r-1-(N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}})}}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r-s})_{\overrightarrow{\ell}}.

However, there will be some cancellation, because the same vector
\overrightarrow{\ell}
can appear for two different sets
\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{j}\}
and
\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{j^{\prime}}\}
. To account for this, define the constants

M_{\overrightarrow{\ell},j}:=\left|\{Y\subset\{1,\ldots,r-s\}:|Y|=j,\overrightarrow{\ell}\in Y^{r-1-(N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}})}\}\right|.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}\sum_{1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\overrightarrow{\ell}\in\{1,\ldots,r-s\}^{r-1-(N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}})}}\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}M_{\overrightarrow{\ell},j}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})_{\overrightarrow{\ell}}.

Thus it suffices to show that for fixed
\overrightarrow{\ell}

\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}M_{\overrightarrow{\ell},j}=0.

We have

M_{\overrightarrow{\ell},j}={r-s-|\overrightarrow{\ell}|\choose j-|\overrightarrow{\ell}|},

so

\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}M_{\overrightarrow{\ell},j}=\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}{r-s-|\overrightarrow{\ell}|\choose j-|\overrightarrow{\ell}|}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}{n\choose k}

where
n=r-s-|\overrightarrow{\ell}|
and
k=j-|\overrightarrow{\ell}|
. The last sum is 0, because
|\overrightarrow{\ell}|<r-s
by assumption, so
n
is positive. This completes step
1
.

Remark 4.19

(Important)
.

Suppose that we had applied
\Phi_{r}
instead of
\Phi_{r-1}
. Then in the above we would have ended up with the sum

\sum_{\overrightarrow{\ell}\in\{1,\ldots,r-s\}^{r-(N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}})}}\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}M_{\overrightarrow{\ell},j}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})_{\overrightarrow{\ell}}

rather than

\sum_{\overrightarrow{\ell}\in\{1,\ldots,r-s\}^{r-1-(N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}})}}\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}M_{\overrightarrow{\ell},j}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})_{\overrightarrow{\ell}}.

In this case we could have had
|\overrightarrow{\ell}|=r-s
, which would cause

\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}M_{\overrightarrow{\ell},j}=1.

Since all
X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})_{\overrightarrow{\ell}}
are equal as long as
|\overrightarrow{\ell}|=r-s
by symmetry of symbols, we would get that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}\sum_{1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r-s}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})

\displaystyle=(r-s)!\{(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{1}},0\ldots,0),\ldots(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{s}},0\ldots,0),(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,a_{r-s})\}_{k/k}.

Now let us consider the case when
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}<s
. This implies that for some
1\leq t\leq s
,
N_{i_{t}}=0
. In other words, the symbol

X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})

is independent of
y_{i_{t}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}\ldots\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots n_{y_{i_{s}}}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{\ell}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{y_{i_{t}}}n_{y_{i_{t}}}\left(\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}\ldots\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots n_{y_{i_{s}}}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{\ell}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})\right)=0

because
\sum_{y_{i_{t}}}n_{y_{i_{t}}}=0
by assumption. This completes step
2
.
∎

As a corollary of the proof we have the following:

Corollary 4.20
.

The composition
\Phi_{r}\circ\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
is given by multiplication by
r!
.

Remark 4.21
.

Note that the domain of
\Phi_{r}\circ\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
is

\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})

while the codomain is

S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A).

So the precise statement of the corollary is that for
\alpha\in\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})
,
\Phi_{r}\circ\Psi_{r}^{\prime}(\alpha)=r!q(\alpha)
where

q:\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\to S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)

is the natural quotient.

Proof.

Using the notation of the above proof, we computed that

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}\circ\Psi_{r}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\left\{\left(0,\ldots,0,\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}[y_{i_{1}}],0\ldots,,0\right),\ldots,\left(0,\ldots,0,\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{s}}}[y_{i_{s}}],0,\ldots,0\right),\right.

\displaystyle\left.(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,a_{r-s})\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}}\right\}_{k^{\prime}/k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}\ldots\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots n_{y_{i_{s}}}\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}\sum_{1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r-s}

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}\leq r}\beta_{N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}}}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}}).

However, if
N_{i_{1}}+\ldots+N_{i_{s}}>s
, then we fall into step 1 in the above proof and if any
N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}}
are
0
, then we fall into step
2
. Hence, we must have
N_{i_{1}}=\ldots=N_{i_{s}}=1
. In this case
\beta_{1,\ldots,1}=\frac{r!}{(r-s)!}
. Also by the above proof (see Important Remark
4.2
), in this case

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-s}(-1)^{r-s-j}\sum_{1\leq\nu_{1}<\ldots<\nu_{j}\leq r-s}X(N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{s}},a_{\nu_{1}},\ldots,a_{\nu_{j}})

\displaystyle=(r-s)!\{(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{1},k^{\prime}},0\ldots,0),\ldots(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{s},k^{\prime}},0\ldots,0),(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,a_{r-s})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}.

Putting this together, we get

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}\circ\Psi_{r}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\left\{\left(0,\ldots,0,\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}[y_{i_{1}}],0\ldots,,0\right),\ldots,\left(0,\ldots,0,\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{s}}}[y_{i_{s}}],0,\ldots,0\right),\right.

\displaystyle\left.(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,a_{r-s})\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}}\right\}_{k^{\prime}/k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}\ldots\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots n_{y_{i_{s}}}\frac{r!}{(r-s)!}(r-s)!

\displaystyle\{(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{1},k^{\prime}},0\ldots,0),\ldots(0,\ldots,0,y_{i_{s},k^{\prime}},0\ldots,0),(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,a_{r-s})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}

\displaystyle=r!\{(0,\ldots,0,\sum_{y_{i_{1}}}n_{y_{i_{1}}}[y_{i_{1}}],0\ldots,0),\ldots(0,\ldots,0,\sum_{y_{i_{s}}}n_{y_{i_{s}}}[y_{i_{s}}],0\ldots,0),

\displaystyle(0,\ldots,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,\ldots,a_{r-s})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}

where the last equality follows by multilinearity of symbols.
∎

Definition 4.22
.

The map

\Psi_{r}:\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\to\frac{F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)}{F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)}

is defined as the composite

\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\Psi^{\prime}_{r}}F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\to\frac{F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)}{F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)}.

Now we are able to complete the proofs of the key lemmas.

Lemma
4.1
.

The map
\Psi_{r}
induces a group homomorphism

S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)\to F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X).

Proof.

Let
H
be the subgroup of
\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})
such that the quotient by
H
gives
S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)
. Let
x\in H
. Then by Corollary
4.2

\Phi_{r}(\Psi_{r}(x))=r!x
, which is in
H
. Therefore,
\Phi_{r}(\Psi_{r}(x))=0
, so
\Psi_{r}(x)=0
, because
\Phi_{r}
is injective on
F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
.
∎

Note that Corollary
4.2
and Lemma
4.1
imply Lemma
4.1
.

5.
Finiteness of the Filtration

In this section we show that
F^{r}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
for all
r>\text{dim}(X)=d+g
where
g
is the dimension of
A
. First, we will need some preparation regarding specialization maps.

5.1.
Specialization Maps

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
, and let
K
be a function field in one variable over
k
. The for each place
v
of
K
there exist specialization maps

s_{v}:\text{CH}_{0}(X_{K})\to\text{CH}_{0}(X_{k(v)})

where
k(v)
is the residue field of
v
defined as follows. Let
\mathcal{O}_{v}
be the ring of integers of
v
. Then
X_{K}\to X_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}
is an open embedding and
X_{k(v)}\to X_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}
is a closed embedding. Then for
x\in\text{CH}_{0}(X_{K})
,
s_{v}(x)
is
\overline{x}\cap X_{k(v)}
where
\overline{x}
is the closure of
x
in
X_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}
.

In the case that
X
is a curve with a rational point and
J
is its Jacobian, then under the natural isomorphism
\Pic^{0}(X_{E})\cong J(E)
for an extension
E/k
, the specialization map on Chow groups agrees with the specialization map on points of the abelian variety defined in Section
2.1
. Moreover, if
x\in X(K)
, then
s_{v}(x)\in X(k(v))
, and the cycle class it defines in
\text{CH}_{0}(X_{k(v)})
is
s_{v}([x]_{K})
where
[x]_{K}
is viewed as an element of
\text{CH}_{0}(X_{K})
.

Definition 5.1
.

Recall the map

\Psi^{\prime}_{r}:\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\to\text{CH}_{0}(X)

from Definiton
4.1
. Then define
F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\subset\text{CH}_{0}(X)
to be the subgroup generated by the images of
\Psi_{j}^{\prime}
for all
j\geq r
. That is

F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X):=\left\langle\bigcup_{j\geq r}\mathrm{im}(\Psi_{r}^{\prime})\right\rangle

Note that
F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
defines a descending filtration on
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
. By Proposition
4.2
,
F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\subset F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
.

The groups
F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
are analogues of Pontryagin powers of the degree
0
subgroup of
\text{CH}_{0}(A)
for an abelian variety
A
. In the case when
d=0
, these two groups coincide. Importantly for us, if
A
is an abelian variety of dimension
g
, and
I
is the degree
0
subgroup of
\text{CH}_{0}(A)
, then

I^{\odot g+1}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0

in
\text{CH}_{0}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. This fact was proven by Bloch
[
4
]
over algebraically closed fields. Beauville proved it over
\mathbb{C}

[
1
]
using different methods, and Deninger and Murre
[
17
]
extended these techniques to arbitrary base fields.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
r>\text{dim}(X)
,
F^{r}_{\Psi}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
.

Proof.

By the definition of
F^{r}_{\Psi}(X)
and
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}
, it suffices to show that for
r>dim(X)
and fixed
0\leq s\leq d
,
1\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{s}\leq r
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in A(k)

([a_{1}]_{k^{\prime}}-[0]_{k^{\prime}})\odot\cdots\odot\widehat{([a_{j_{1}}]_{k^{\prime}})}\odot\cdots\odot\widehat{([a_{j_{s}}]_{k^{\prime}})}\odot\cdots\odot([a_{r}]_{k^{\prime}}-[0]_{k^{\prime}})=0.

This is true by the above since
r-s\geq r-d>\text{dim}(A)
.
∎

Therefore, it remains to verify the following proposition

Proposition 5.3
.

Over an arbitrary field,
F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

Before, we prove this proposition, we need some preliminaries. First note that by Proposition
4.2
,
\Phi_{r}
induces a well-defined map

\overline{\Phi}_{r}:F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F_{\Psi}^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\to S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A).

Now denote by
\overline{\Psi}_{r}
the composition

\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\ldots J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\Psi_{r}^{\prime}}F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\to F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X).

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
k=\overline{k}
be algebraically closed. Then
\overline{\Psi}_{r}
induces a well-define group homomorphism

S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)\to F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X).

Moreover, the composition

F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{\overline{\Phi}_{r}}S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\cdots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)\xrightarrow{\overline{\Psi}_{r}}F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)

is multiplication by
r!
.

Proof.

Consider the map

\Psi_{r}^{\prime}:\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\cdots\oplus J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\to F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X).

Our first step is to show that this induces a group homomorphism

\overline{\Psi}_{r}:S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)\to F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X).

Let us first show that the above map is multilinear. By Proposition
4.2
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{r}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,a_{1}+\tilde{a}_{1}),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)=

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{r}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,a_{1}),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle+\overline{\Psi}_{r}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,\tilde{a}_{1})(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right).

For this it suffices to observe that

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{r+1}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,a_{1}),(0,\ldots,0,\tilde{a}_{1}),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2})\ldots,\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)=

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{r}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,a_{1}+\tilde{a}_{1}),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2})\ldots,\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle-\overline{\Psi}_{r}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,a_{1}),(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right)

\displaystyle-\overline{\Psi}_{r}\left(\{(0,\ldots,0,\tilde{a}_{1})(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{d}^{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(z_{1}^{r},\ldots,z_{d}^{r},a_{r})\}_{k^{\prime}/k}\right).

Thus,
\overline{\Psi}_{r}
induces a group homomorphism

\overline{\Psi}_{r}:\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}/k}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}J_{1}(k^{\prime})\oplus\ldots J_{d}(k^{\prime})\oplus A(k^{\prime})\to F^{r}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}_{\Psi}\text{CH}_{0}(X).

We want to show that the map
\overline{\Psi}_{r}
induces a well-defined group homomorphism from
S_{r}(k;\underline{J_{1}}\times\ldots\times\underline{J_{d}}\times A)
. For the projection formula, there is nothing to show as
k
is algebraically closed. Next, we need to show that
\overline{\Psi}_{r}
factors through the quotient by Weil relations. Let
K
be a function field in one variable, and for each place
v
of
K
let

s_{v}^{i}:J_{i}(K)\to J_{i}(k_{v})

and

s_{v}^{A}:A(K)\to A(k)

be the specialization maps. By Propositions
4.2
and
4.2
, it suffices to show that for
f\in K^{\times}
,
z_{i_{t}}\in J_{i_{t}}(K)
, and
a_{j}\in A(k)

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\sum_{v\text{ place of }K/k}\ord_{v}f\{(0,\ldots,0,s_{v}^{i_{1}}(z_{i_{1}}),0\ldots,0),\ldots,\left(0,\ldots,0,s_{v}^{i_{s}}(z_{i_{s}}),0\ldots,0\right),

\displaystyle(0,\ldots,0,s_{v}^{1}(a_{1})),\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,s_{v}^{A}(a_{r-s}))\}_{k(v)/k}=0.

We have,

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\sum_{v\in K/k}\ord_{v}f\{(0,\ldots,0,s_{v}^{i_{1}}(z_{i_{1}}),0\ldots,0),\ldots,\left(0,\ldots,0,s_{v}^{i_{s}}(z_{i_{s}}),0\ldots,0\right),

\displaystyle(0,\ldots,0,s_{v}^{1}(a_{1})),\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,s_{v}^{A}(a_{r-s}))\}_{k(v)/k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\text{ place of }K/k}\ord_{v}f(\pi_{k(v)/k})_{*}(W)

where

\sum_{v\text{ place of }K/k}\ord_{v}f(\pi_{k(v)/k})_{*}(s_{v}^{X}(Q))

\displaystyle Q=\left(pr_{1,K}^{*}([p_{1}]_{K})\cdots pr_{i_{1},K}^{*}(z_{i_{1}})\cdots pr_{i_{s},K}^{*}(z_{i_{s}})\cdots pr_{d,K}^{*}([p_{d}]_{K})\right)

The fact
\overline{\Psi}_{r}
is invariant under the action of the symmetric group is clear from the definition. Thus, we have a map

factors through

. Since

is multilinear and invariant under the action of the symmetric group, this amounts to checking that for each

By the definition
\overline{\Psi}_{r}
, the intersection product of each term in the sum will be

, so the above is

. So we have the desired map.

Now we verify the second statement of the lemma. By Corollary

,
\Psi_{r}^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{r}
is multiplication by

. Let

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let

Proof.

Now we show that

. Then we have that

\Psi_{1}^{\prime}(\{(0,\ldots,0,a)\}_{k/k})=[(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d},a)]-[(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d},0)].

Now assume that for some

[(p_{1},\ldots,x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{t}},\ldots,p_{d},a)]-[(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d},0)]

0

<t

’s. So we conclude by the inductive hypothesis.

Now assume that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{r}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{r+1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)}

We also will require that our two filtrations

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{0}(X)}

6.
Example: Chow Group of a Genus Two Curve and its Jacobian

In this section, we show how the filtration can be used to study the Albanese kernel and produce rational equivalences in a special case.

Suppose
k=\overline{k}
is algebraically closed for simplicity. Let
C
be a curve of genus
2
over
k
and
J
be its Jacobian. Let the fixed point
p_{1}\in C
be a Weierstrass point and denote the embedding determined by
p_{1}
by
\iota:C\to J
. We want to study the group
\text{CH}_{0}(X)
where
X=C\times J
. Consider the filtration

\text{CH}_{0}(X)=F^{0}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\supset F^{1}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\supset F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\supset F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\supset\ldots

Since
k
is algebraically closed,
F^{2}(X)
is torsion free which was proven by Roitman
[
19
]
for the torsion prime to
\text{char}(k)
and proven for
\text{char}(k)
torsion by Milne
[
15
]
. Since
F^{4}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
by the previous section, we get that
F^{4}\text{CH}_{0}(X)=0
. Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
, we have

\mathbb{Z}[1/3!]\otimes F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\left[1/3!\right]\otimes S_{3}(k;\underline{J}\times J).

This
K
-group is generated by symbols of the form

\{([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[y_{2}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}

and

\{(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{2}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}.

Applying
\Psi_{3}
to symbols of the second form, we get

\Psi_{3}(\{(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{2}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\})=([a_{1}]-[0])\odot([a_{2}]-[0])\odot([a_{3}]-[0])

where
a_{i}=[y_{i}]-[p_{i}]
. However, as mentioned in Section
5
,
([a_{1}]-[0])\odot([a_{2}]-[0])\odot([a_{3}]-[0])=0
in
\text{CH}_{0}(J)
since
\dim(J)=2
.

Therefore, the group
\mathbb{Z}[1/3!]\otimes F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
is generated by cycles of the form

\displaystyle[(y_{1},\iota(y_{2})+\iota(y_{3}))]-[(y_{1},\iota(y_{2}))]-[(y_{1},\iota(y_{3}))]+[(y_{1},0)]

\displaystyle-[(p_{1},\iota(y_{2})+\iota(y_{3}))]+[(p_{1},\iota(y_{2}))]+[(p_{1},\iota(y_{3}))]-[(p_{1},0)]

where
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in C(k)
. We also claim that if
y_{i}=y_{j}
for any
i\neq j
, then cycles of the above form are
0
in the Chow group. To see this, it suffices to show that

\{([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[y_{2}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}=0

in the group
\mathbb{Z}\left[1/3!\right]\otimes S_{3}(k;\underline{J}\times J)
if
y_{i}=y_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Let us assume that
y_{1}=y_{2}
. Since
p_{1}
is a Weierstrass point by assumption,
[y_{1}]+[x]-2[p_{1}]
is the divisor of a function
f\in k(C)^{\times}
where
x
is the image of
y_{1}
under the hyperelliptic involution. We define three maps
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}:J\times J
as follows. The map
g_{1}
is
\iota\times 0
,
g_{2}
is
0\times\iota
, and
g_{3}
is
0
on the first component and the constant map to
[y_{3}]-[p_{1}]
on the second component. Then Weil reciprocity with these three maps and the divisor
[y_{1}]+[x]-2[p_{1}]
gives the following relation in
S_{3}(k;\underline{J}\times J)
.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\{([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}+\{([x]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[x]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}

\displaystyle=\{([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}+\{-([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),-(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}

\displaystyle=2\{([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}.

But since we have inverted
2
in
\mathbb{Z}\left[1/3!\right]\otimes S_{3}(k;\underline{J}\times J)
,
2\{([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}=0

implies
\{([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{3}]-[p_{1}])\}=0
.

Now let us consider the next quotient which is
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
. By Theorem
1.1
, we have that

\mathbb{Z}\left[1/2\right]\otimes\frac{F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)}{F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)}\cong\mathbb{Z}\left[1/2\right]\otimes S_{2}(k;\underline{J}\times J).

The
K
-group is generated by symbols of the form

\{([y_{1}]-[p_{1}],0),(0,[y_{2}]-[p_{1}])\}

and

\{(0,[y_{1}]-[p_{1}]),(0,[y_{2}]-[p_{1}])\}

for
y_{1},y_{2}\in C(k)
. Thus,
\mathbb{Z}\left[1/2\right]\otimes F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
is generated by cycles of the form

[(y_{1},\iota(y_{2}))]-[(y_{1},0)]-[(p_{1},\iota(y_{2}))]+[(p_{1},0)]

and

[(p_{1},\iota(y_{1})+\iota(y_{2}))]-[(p_{1},\iota(y_{1})]-[(p_{1},\iota(y_{2}))]+[(p_{1},0)].

A similar computation to the one performed above shows that for
y_{1}\in C(k)
,

[(y_{1},\iota(y_{1}))]-[(y_{1},0)]-[(p_{1},\iota(y_{1}))]+[(p_{1},0)]=0

in
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
and in fact this cycle is trivial in
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
which can be seen by looking at its image under the map
\Phi_{2}
.

We also note that by the definition of
S_{2}(k;\underline{J}\times J)
(Definition
2.1
), we have that

S_{2}(k;\underline{J}\times J)\cong K(k;J,J)\oplus S_{2}(k;J).

Raskind and Spiess show in
[
18
]
that
K(k;J,J)\cong F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(C\times C)
and Gazaki shows in
[
10
]
that
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\otimes S_{2}(k;J)\cong\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\otimes F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(J)
where we take
F^{2}
to denote the Albanese kernel. Therefore, the above shows that

\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\otimes F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\otimes\left(F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(C\times C)\oplus F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(J)\right).

This example demonstrates that the filtration allows us to break the group
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
into smaller pieces and study each of them separately. The
K
-groups allow us to easily determine generators for the successive quotients. Also, the relations coming from Weil Reciprocity in the
K
-groups can help us to write down relations in the Albanese kernel
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
coming from rational equivalence.

In order to attempt to fully understand these successive quotients, considerations about the base field must be taken into account. For example, over
\mathbb{C}
we know by the work of Mumford
[
16
]
, we know that
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(C\times C)
and
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(J)
are both huge. So
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
will be as well by the above. However, over
\mathbb{Q}
conjectures of Beilinson and Bloch
[
2
]

[
5
]
predict that
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
should be
0
. It is easy to check by the above that if
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(C\times C)
and
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(J)
are
0
, then so is
F^{2}\text{CH}_{0}(X)
, but neither case is known. Gazaki and Love have studied the Albanese kernel for Abelian surfaces over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
in
[
9
]
. In particular, they are able to produce many rational equivalences coming from hyperelliptic curves mapping to the abelian surface when it is isomorphic to a product of elliptic curves
E\times E^{\prime}
.

Effect of precision rehabilitation-oriented bundled care on functional recovery and psychological resilience after digital replantation: a non-randomized controlled trial
Authors: Xiaoqi Zhong, Jianping Deng, Chenxi Liu, Jiashi Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to investigate the impact of precision rehabilitation-oriented, bundled nursing interventions on postoperative functional recovery and psychological resilience in patients who had undergone digital replantation surgery.MethodThe study adopted a non-randomized controlled trial design. Between January 2021 and January 2025, 64 patients who underwent single-finger replantation were enrolled from a tertiary hospital in Zhejiang Province. Patients were grouped postoperatively based on whether bundled care was implemented.The control group (n = 32) received routine nursing care, while the intervention group (n = 32) received the bundled nursing intervention in addition to routine care. The changes in the GSES scores before and after the intervention were compared between the two groups. Additionally, functional recovery of the affected digit was assessed six months post-surgery. Quality of life was measured using the QL-Index, and patient satisfaction was evaluated using a modified 5-point Likert scale.ResultsBefore the intervention, there was no statistically significant difference in GSES scores between the two groups (P > 0.05). After the intervention, GSES scores in both groups had increased from baseline. Moreover, the post-intervention scores in the intervention group were significantly higher than those in the control group (P < 0.05). Six months after surgery, the intervention group showed higher rates of excellent or good recovery in digital motor function (84.4% vs 62.5%), along with significantly greater improvement in quality of life (84.4% vs 56.3%), and higher levels of satisfaction (90.6% vs 81.2%) compared to the control group. The differences in rank distributions between groups were statistically significant (all P < 0.05).ConclusionPrecision rehabilitation-oriented bundled nursing interventions may contribute to functional recovery and enhance psychological resistance in patients after digital replantation.Keywords: Patient care bundles, Replantation, Recovery of function

Introduction

Digital replantation is a complex surgical procedure. Postoperative patients often face numerous challenges, such as pain, infection, and functional impairments like motor and sensory dysfunction. These challenges can directly impede the digital rehabilitation process and the patients’ quality of life. To address these challenges, structured bundled nursing interventions have been proposed as a promising strategy to optimize postoperative care. These programs constitute structured models of care that emphasize comprehensive assessment. They integrate a series of evidence-based interventions into a unified plan, which is then implemented collaboratively by a multidisciplinary team. Existing studies in other surgical fields indicate that bundled care interventions can effectively improve the postoperative recovery process, reduce the incidence of complications [1], and enhance patient satisfaction. Although the bundled care model has achieved certain results in orthopedic surgery practice, there is still a lack of rehabilitation-focused research in the field of microsurgery. Therefore, we implemented a precision rehabilitation-oriented bundled care program for patients following digital replantation. This study aimed to evaluate its impact on postoperative functional recovery, psychological resilience, quality of life, and overall patient satisfaction. The research results were reported as follows.

Methods

Ethical approval

The Ethics Committee of Zhejiang Rongjun Hospital approved this study in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.All participants provided written informed consent prior to inclusion in this study, and the article may publish any identifiable images or data.

Eligibility criteria

A total of 64 patients who underwent digital replantation were enrolled at a tertiary hospital in Zhejiang Province, between January 2021 and January 2025. The study aimed to evaluate postoperative recovery outcomes. The inclusion criteria were as follows: ① age between 18 and 60 years; ② sustained a complete or incomplete amputation of a single digit; and ③ achieved successful replantation viability with a minimum follow-up period of 6 months. This criterion ensured that all participants had a viable replanted digit, establishing a baseline for assessing rehabilitation outcomes rather than representing the primary study endpoint. Exclusion criteria included: ① cognitive dysfunction; ② pre-existing diabetes mellitus; and ③ history of significant prior trauma to the affected hand. A non-randomized controlled trial design was utilized. Group allocation was determined primarily by the chronological order of admission relative to the implementation timeline of the bundled care program. Patients enrolled before the full implementation of this program received conventional postoperative care, forming the control group (n = 32). Those enrolled after full implementation received the bundled care intervention, forming the intervention group (n = 32). During the informed consent process, the components and potential benefits of both standard and bundled care models were thoroughly explained to all participants. Their participation preferences were respected, and written informed consent was obtained.

Procedure

All patients were cared for by the same medical team to minimize bias from variations in intervention, ensuring consistent treatment and management.

The control group received routine care, comprising: ① Regularly monitoring the respiration rate, pulse, body temperature, and blood pressure. ② Establishment of a recovery support system that included limb elevation at 20 cm above heart level, environmental control (25 °C and 55% humidity), health education on prohibiting smoking cessation and secondhand smoke avoidance, and nutritional support with a protein-rich diet and adequate hydration. ③ Hourly documentation of digital perfusion parameters (color, skin temperature, swelling degree), vigilance for vascular crises, and timely reporting of abnormalities. ④ A stepwise analgesic protocol utilizing NSAIDs as the first-line therapy combined with Visual Analog Scale (VAS) assessment, while monitoring for vascular complications and drug side effects to optimize analgesia and functional rehabilitation. ⑤ Abdominal circular massage along the colonic circuit was performed three times daily for 15 min / session, with daily recording of bowel habits and targeted treatment for constipation lasting ≥ 3 days. ⑥ Routine anxiety assessments were conducted at 7 days postoperatively, complemented by multimedia health education and structured family support to promote psychological well-being. ⑦ Structured outpatient follow-ups were scheduled at 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 24 weeks post-discharge to monitor recovery and manage complications.

The intervention group received bundled nursing interventions oriented toward precise rehabilitation. This approach built upon routine care to provide comprehensive psychophysiological coordination and promote personalized functional recovery of the replanted digit. The specific measures were as follows: ① A multidisciplinary rehabilitation team was established, comprising a charge nurse, the attending surgeon, and a rehabilitation physician. This team was responsible for developing a detailed rehabilitation plan spanning the hospitalization and postoperative follow-up periods. The surgeon was responsible for assessing the vascular viability of the replanted digit. Upon confirming hemodynamic stability, the surgeon approved the initiation of rehabilitation therapy and managed medical risks throughout the rehabilitation period. Following approval, rehabilitation physicians developed a personalized, staged rehabilitation plan. They dynamically adjusted interventions based on the Touch-Talk-Trust protocol, including joint range of motion (ROM) exercises and sensory re-education. This protocol emphasizes progressive sensory and motor recovery through structured patient engagement. Functional assessments were conducted using standardized scales at 1, 3, and 6 months post-surgery. Nurses were responsible for supervising family members during standardized pre-discharge training, which includes scenario simulations for rehabilitation techniques and communication skills. Additionally, nurses provided ongoing supervision and support throughout the patient’s entire rehabilitation process. ② Specific implementation content: The head nurse provided standardized training on the “3 T Communication Method” (Touch-Talk-Trust) to patients’ families before discharge, which included scenario simulation assessments, and conducted an assessment of patients’ communication preferences. Touch (scientific tactile intervention): Family members were instructed to gently hold the patient’s non-affected hand three times daily for three minutes each session. Guided by the rehabilitation physician, family members placed their hands on the joints of the affected limb to guide passive movements and assist the patient in applying alternating warm and cool compresses to promote sensory re-education. Upon completion of rehabilitation exercises, they lightly patted the patient’s scapular region for 30 s. Talk (standardized language pattern): During the morning (7:00–8:00), the family outlined the day’s treatment plan. In the evening (18:00–19:00), they summarized the rehabilitation progress. Communication was to use gentle language and a soft tone, with a focus on alleviating anxiety. For example, they were to say: “Today your grip strength reached [X] kg, and which is a [Y] % improvement from yesterday. This shows your targeted muscle groups are recovering well.” Or: “The current swelling and stiffness are a normal part of the healing process, similar to Mr. Zhang’s situation last week. He improved steadily with his exercises.” Trust (quantitative trust building): Family members were required to sign the “Daily Commitment Letter,” which recorded the patient’s actual training situation (e.g., “Assisted in completing 3 sets of grip training today, with an achievement rate of ≥ 95%”). Family members were also required to accompany the patient during follow-up visits, where they received training from medical staff to resolve any issues encountered in practical operations.

Observations

Scores on the General Self-Efficacy Scale (GSES) were collected for both groups at admission, 3 months post-discharge, and 6 months post-discharge. Other indicators, including digital function recovery, quality of life, and patient satisfaction, were assessed at 6 months postoperatively. The Chinese version of the GSES was used to evaluate self-efficacy in both groups of patients [2]. This scale comprises 10 items, each scored from 1 to 4. Total scores were categorized as follows: low efficacy (≤ 20 points), moderate efficacy (21–29 points), or high efficacy (≥ 30 points). Sensory recovery was assessed using the British Medical Research Council (BMRC) grading system [3]: S0, no sensory recovery; S1, recovery of deep pain sensation within the innervation area; S2, recovery of superficial pain and touch sensations; S3, two-point discrimination > 15 mm; S4, two-point discrimination < 6 mm. Motor function was assessed according to criteria established by the Hand Surgery Branch of the Chinese Medical Association [4]. Grades were defined as: excellent (normal digital function), good (50–75% of contralateral healthy side function), or poor (< 50% of contralateral healthy side function). The Quality of Life Index (QL-Index) was used to evaluate their life quality [5]. Scores were interpreted as: ≤ 4 points (poor quality of life), 5–7 points (moderate quality of life), or ≥ 8 points (good quality of life). A modified 5-point Likert scale was used to assess patient satisfaction with nursing care [6]. The scale was as follows: 5 = very satisfied, 4 = satisfied, 3 = neutral, 2 = dissatisfied, and 1 = very dissatisfied.

Statistical analysis

SPSS version 26 (IBM) was used for data analysis. Continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the Student’s t-test. Categorical data were expressed as frequencies and percentages and compared using the Fisher’s exact test. Ordinal data were analyzed using the non-parametric Mann–Whitney U test for comparisons between groups. The statistical significance level was set at P < 0.05.

The results are shown in Table 1. The control group comprised 24 males and 8 females, aged 23 to 58 years (mean ± SD, 40.41 ± 11.47 years). Thumbs accounted for 43.8% of injuries, while other digits accounted for 56.3%. Complete and incomplete amputations each accounted for 50% of cases. By amputation level, distal phalangeal amputations accounted for 37.5%, middle phalangeal for 43.8%, and proximal phalangeal for 18.7%. Complete amputations accounted for 53.1% of cases, and incomplete amputations accounted for 46.9%. There was no statistically significant difference between groups in age, gender, distribution of digits, amputation level, or amputation type (P > 0.05).

Discussion

The GSES is developed on Bandura’s theory of self-efficacy [7], which emphasizes individuals’ beliefs in their capacity to execute behaviors necessary to achieve specific outcomes. Self-efficacy is considered an important psychological factor influencing individual behavior, especially significant in areas such as health behavior, learning, and psychotherapy. Recent studies have shown that self-efficacy is closely related to individuals’ motivation, emotional regulation, coping strategies, and behavior change [8, 9]. In clinical practice, the GSES is widely used to assess patients’ confidence and self-management abilities in coping with illness. This assessment helps clinicians develop personalized rehabilitation plans, thereby improving patients’ self-management abilities and quality of life [10]. At the same time, multiple studies have shown that bundled nursing interventions have a positive effect on enhancing patients’ self-efficacy [11–13]. Consistent with the baseline comparability of patient groups often reported in surgical studies, our results showed no significant difference in GSES scores between the two groups at admission. However, when reassessed at 3 months and 6 months after discharge, the GSES scores in the intervention group were significantly higher than those in the control group. This finding suggests that bundled nursing interventions enhanced patients’ confidence and self-management abilities through interdisciplinary team collaboration, which is driven by several key factors. First, bundled nursing focuses on coordinated assessment, planning, and implementation of nursing interventions, which helps patients receive continuous psychological attention and social support at every stage of the treatment process. Furthermore, psychological support not only helps patients cope with illness-related stress, but also enhances their confidence in self-management abilities by providing emotional and informational support. In addition, multidisciplinary teams can optimize nursing processes by sharing information and resources, reducing medical errors, and thereby improving patient safety and satisfaction. Although there is currently no recognized minimum clinically important difference (MCID) for the GSES in patients undergoing digital replantation, the observed score differences in this study suggest clinical significance and practical value. Specifically, the self-efficacy levels of patients in the intervention group increased from the moderate to the high efficacy range. This improvement is considered crucial, as it directly relates to patients’ confidence, adherence, and initiative during the prolonged rehabilitation process. More importantly, this positive psychological change does not occur in isolation, but is associated with significant improvements in several core outcome indicators observed in this study. Compared to the control group, patients in the intervention group with higher self-efficacy demonstrated significantly better rates of recovery of digital motor function, improvements in quality of life, and greater satisfaction with nursing care. These findings collectively indicate that bundled care may enhance patients’ psychological capital, defined as their positive psychological state and resources, as well as their self-management abilities. This improvement ultimately leads to tangible practical benefits in key areas such as functional recovery and quality of life.

This study demonstrates that the “3 T communication method” (Touch-Talk-Trust), implemented within a precision rehabilitation-oriented cluster nursing intervention, significantly enhances self-efficacy in patients undergoing digital replantation. The effectiveness of this approach may lie in its close alignment with the core mechanisms of Bandura’s self-efficacy theory. First, touch is based on tactile support and passive activities provided by family members, which not only alleviates patients’ anxiety but also improves their overall psychophysiological state by helping them regularly complete specific rehabilitation tasks. Alleviating anxiety and improving psychophysiological state are crucial for patients to establish a sense of self-efficacy. Second, talk, as a form of continuous verbal persuasion, can provide daily feedback on specific rehabilitation progress (such as improvements in grip strength), which serves as objective evidence encouraging functional improvement of patients’ digits. This feedback boosts their confidence throughout the management of the rehabilitation process. Additionally, trust is fostered through tools such as daily commitment records—whether digital or paper-based—that help patients clearly perceive their progress in the rehabilitation process. The active involvement of family members also establishes a strong social support system, providing meaningful social experiences [14]. In summary, the “3 T Communication Method” integrates emotional support, behavioral guidance, and quantitative management, representing a systematic strategy. Through its multidimensional synergistic effects, it effectively activates the key pathways of self-efficacy. This approach shows significant benefits in enhancing patients’ psychological resilience and promoting functional recovery.

Impaired hand function is a major contributor to reduced quality of life in patients. Unlike traditional nursing interventions, which often focus on a single treatment method, bundled nursing interventions provide a more comprehensive rehabilitation plan by integrating various nursing measures. The implementation typically includes first evaluating patients’ specific physical, psychological, and social needs, then developing personalized nursing plans, and subsequently conducting continuous monitoring and adjustments during the nursing process. Specific measures may include comprehensive implementation of physical therapy, occupational therapy, psychological support, and nutrition intervention. In this study, quality of life in the intervention group significantly improved. The bundled nursing intervention focused on precision rehabilitation played an important role in the recovery of hand function. First, the synergistic effects of multiple intervention measures promoted functional recovery in patients. Second, it enhanced patient compliance and active participation in rehabilitation exercises, thereby improving treatment outcomes. Finally, this intervention model optimized the allocation of medical resources, increased nursing efficiency, and reduced medical costs. The results of this study demonstrate that cluster nursing interventions significantly improve hand motor function to a greater extent than sensory function. This difference stems from the distinct neurobiological recovery mechanisms underlying these two functions. Motor function recovery relies primarily on central nervous system plasticity and the relearning of motor patterns. The systematic active and passive training combined with quantifiable feedback mechanisms within the nursing protocol effectively promotes the reconstruction of motor pathways. In contrast, sensory recovery depends on biological axonal regeneration, a process marked by slow axonal regrowth and uncertain reinnervation of target tissues. Although sensory stimulation training within the intervention helps preserve cortical sensory map integrity, it cannot fundamentally alter the natural course of neural regeneration. Furthermore, the inherent subjectivity of sensory function assessment may diminish the measured effectiveness of interventions in statistical analyses.

The concept of bundled nursing intervention originates from a comprehensive consideration of various aspects of patient care, emphasizing the implementation of multiple evidence-based interventions simultaneously. This approach aims to improve clinical outcomes and patient satisfaction [15, 16]. Moreover, both patient satisfaction and quality of life are important indicators for assessing the effectiveness of bundled nursing interventions. This study found that patients receiving bundled nursing care showed significant improvements in both satisfaction and quality of life. Specifically, these improvements were reflected in higher satisfaction rates and better quality of life measures. According to the postoperative satisfaction survey, over 80% of patients in the bundled nursing group reported being very satisfied with the nursing services, while the satisfaction rate in the routine care group was only 60% [17]. In addition, quality of life assessment showed that the bundled nursing group scored significantly higher compared to the control group, which indicated that bundled nursing not only improved patients’ hand function but also enhanced their overall quality of life. The improvement in patient satisfaction and quality of life highlights the important role of bundled nursing interventions in promoting patient recovery.

In the future, research in the field of bundled nursing care faces many challenges and opportunities. On one hand, multiple studies have confirmed the effectiveness of bundled nursing care; however, barriers to its promotion and implementation remain in different healthcare settings, such as the lack of standardized implementation guidelines and training. In addition, assessing the long-term effects of bundled nursing care and its sustained impact on patients’ self-efficacy is also an important direction for future research. This study has several limitations. First, the primary methodological limitation is the lack of randomization. Although we systematically collected comprehensive baseline data—including demographics, injury characteristics, and preoperative status—and employed statistical adjustments to control for known confounders, the non-random allocation of participants prevents us from completely eliminating the potential for unmeasured confounding bias. In addition, this study adopted a time-sequenced allocation method. Specifically, patients in the control group were enrolled before those in the intervention group. This means that the superior clinical outcomes observed in the intervention group may be partially attributed to the medical team’s gradually accumulating experience and proficiency during the implementation of this care model—that is, the learning curve effect. It should be noted that although the learning curve may be a confounding factor, it is an inherent and unavoidable part of the practice process. Nevertheless, this process spans from the initiation to the maturation of the care model. Future research using a cluster-randomized design will help to more clearly distinguish the specific contributions of the implemented intervention measures from the team’s accumulated experience. Second, the sample size limits the interpretation of our findings. A post hoc power analysis revealed that the statistical power for the primary endpoint (motor function recovery) was 50% (N = 64), which is below the conventional 80% threshold and indicates an elevated risk of Type II error. Therefore, these results require validation in larger future studies. Notably, the intervention demonstrated greater potential for improving quality of life, with a post hoc power of 70%. This suggests that quality of life may be a more sensitive endpoint for this intervention and should be considered a primary endpoint in future trials to comprehensively evaluate the intervention’s overall benefit.

Conclusion

The findings on precision rehabilitation-oriented cluster nursing interventions provide valuable insights into their potential to enhance patients’ self-efficacy. These findings could facilitate the integration of nursing, psychology, and other healthcare disciplines, and demonstrate potential for improving functional recovery and quality of life among patients, while possibly contributing to long-term satisfaction.

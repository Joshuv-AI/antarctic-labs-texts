Cumulative/Dynamic Time-Dependent ROC Analysis for Left-Truncated and Right-Censored Data Publication type: Research Article Journal: Statistics in Medicine Volume: 00
Kendrick Li*, Mithun Kumar Acharjee*, Li K, Acharjee M

Abstract
Time-dependent Receiver Operating Characteristics (ROC) analysis is a standard method to evaluate the discriminative performance of biomarkers or risk scores for time-to-event outcomes. Extensions of this useful method to left-truncated right-censored data have been understudied, with the exception of Li 2017. In this paper, we first extended the estimators in Li 2017 to several regression-type estimators that account for independent or covariate-induced dependent left truncation and right censoring. We further proposed novel inverse probability weighting estimators of cumulative sensitivity, dynamic specificity, and area under the ROC curve (AUC), where the weights simultaneously account for left truncation and right censoring, with or without adjusting for covariates. We demonstrated the proposed AUC estimators in simulation studies with different scenarios. We performed the proposed time-dependent ROC analysis to evaluate the predictive performance of two risk prediction models of heart failure by Chow et al. 2015 in five-year childhood cancer survivors using the St. Jude Lifetime Cohort Study.

1
Introduction

The Receiver Operating Characteristics (ROC) analysis is a statistical method widely used to evaluate the predictive performance of prediction modeling. Typically intended for a binary outcome and a continuous numeric score, the ROC curve plots sensitivity (or true positive rate) against one minus specificity (or false positive rate) at all possible thresholds of the score. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) is used to describe how well the score discriminates the outcome status, with
\mathrm{AUC}=1
indicating perfect discrimination and
\mathrm{AUC}=0.5
indicating that the discriminative performance of the score is no better than a random guess
[
1
,
2
]
.

Cumulative/dynamic time-dependent ROC (hereafter referred to as time-dependent ROC for simplicity) is a natural extension of the ROC analysis when the outcome is time-to-events
[
3
]
. In the time-dependent ROC analysis, the cases are defined as observations with events occurring before a pre-determined time point, and the controls are those with events occurring after the time point. Time-dependent ROC is a useful prediction metric across many public health disciplines
[
4
]
.

In most of the time-to-event analysis, the study may end or participating individuals may “drop out” before an event is observed for part of the observations. For those observations, only a lower bound of the event times is known. This is commonly referred to as right censoring and is a fundamental challenge of time-to-event analysis
[
5
]
. Several estimators of time-dependent sensitivity, specificity, and AUC have been proposed, which account for right censoring, including the Kaplan-Meier-based estimators by Heagerty et al.
[
3
]
, the inverse probability weighting (IPW) estimators by Uno et al.
[
6
]
, the conditional IPW estimators to adjust for informative censoring by Blanche et al.
[
7
]
, to name a few. Kamarudin et al. provided a comprehensive review of existing methods as well as other types of time-dependent ROC analysis in medical research.
[
4
]

In observational studies, participants may be recruited later than the intended time origin. This may occur in prospective cohort studies or registry-based studies. Such delayed entries result in the so-called left-truncation bias, where only a subset of the underlying population who survived long enough to enter the study are observed
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
. We refer to the data where left truncation and right censoring of the event times of interest are both present as left-truncated and right-censored (LRTC) data.

Only a few works have focused on metrics to describe the discriminative performance of a score for a time-to-event outcome subject to left truncation and right censoring, including the concordance index
[
11
]
and regression-type estimators for time-dependent ROC analysis by Li
[
12
]
. Li’s estimators were developed under the scenario where right censoring may occur only after left truncation. When event times and left truncation times are associated, Li’s semiparametric estimators adjust for the association induced by the score alone. When it is of interest to compare the predictive performances of several scores, the above semiparametric estimators would make different conditional independence assumptions when evaluating each score. Furthermore, scenarios exist where right censoring may occur before the left truncation time.
[
13
]

As an example, we introduce the St. Jude Lifetime Cohort Study (SJLIFE), a prospective long-term follow-up study housed in St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital for the late effects of cancer and cancer treatment among childhood cancer survivors
[
14
,
15
]
. The study provides regular comprehensive assessments and survey questionnaires to all the study participants. As the study participants may enter the study at different stages of their lives, when age is used as a time scale, the time to events such as death or the onset of chronic health conditions is subject to left truncation. Notably, after-treatment care of cancer patients at St. Jude starts at treatment completion, so loss of contact, and thus right censoring, may occur before the patients enter the survivorship status. To study the factors contributing to risks of heart failure among childhood cancer survivors, Chow et al. developed a prediction model for risks of congestive heart failure (CHF) using baseline predictors including radiation therapy to the chest, anthracycline doses, age at cancer diagnosis, and sex. They used the SJLIFE data as a validation sample to assess the model performance for risks of heart failure by age 40. In the original time-dependent ROC analysis, to account for different study entry times, the starting time of the time-dependent ROC evaluation was fixed for everyone at the age when all participants entered the study. By the time of the manuscripts, among the 1,695 SJLIFE survivor participants, only 19 CHF events occurred
[
16
]
.

In this paper, we developed novel estimators for time-dependent ROC analysis using LTRC data, including the regression estimators and inverse truncation-and-censoring probability weighting (IPW) estimators. The regression estimators are direct extensions of Li 2017, while our IPW approach is inspired by the general weighting approach for LTRC data by Vakulenko-Lagun et al.
[
17
]
and the IPW estimators of concordance index by Hartman et al.
[
11
]
. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. We introduce the notation and parameters of interest in a time-dependent ROC analysis in Section
2
, as well as different scenarios relating the censoring time to the left truncation times that we shall consider. In Section
3
, we first propose the regression-type estimators as extensions of those in Li 2017, and then propose novel IPW estimators of sensitivity, specificity, and AUC. We perform comprehensive simulation studies to evaluate the performance of the AUC estimators in Section
4
. In Section
5
, we demonstrate the proposed ROC analysis by evaluating the risk score in Chow et al. using more recent data from the SJLIFE study. We conclude the paper with a brief discussion in Section
6
.

2
Notation and Basic Concepts

In the population of interest, we denote
T
as the (uncensored) event time and
X
as a real-valued score or a marker. For an arbitrary cutoff
c
of the score and time point
t
, the cumulative sensitivity is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Se}(c,t)=\mathbb{P}(X>c\mid T\leq t)=\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(X>c,T\leq t)}{\mathbb{P}(T\leq t)},

(1)

and the dynamic specificity is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)=\mathbb{P}(X\leq c\mid T>t)=\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(X\leq c,T>t)}{\mathbb{P}(T>t)}

(2)

The time-dependent ROC curve for time
t
, denoted as
\text{ROC}(t)
, plots
\mathrm{Se}(c,t)
against
1-\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)
for all possible thresholds
c
. The area under the ROC curve at time
t
, denoted as
\mathrm{AUC}(t)
, can be calculated as

\displaystyle\mathrm{AUC}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\infty}^{-\infty}\mathrm{Se}(c,t)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial c}[1-\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)]\,\mathrm{d}c

(3)

It is easy to show that
\mathrm{AUC}(t)=\mathbb{P}(X_{i}>X_{j}\mid T_{i}\leq T_{j})
, where
(X_{i},T_{i})
and
(X_{j},T_{j})
are two independent score-time pairs.

We denote
C
as the right censoring time. We denote
L
as the left truncation time (study entry time). In the observed data, only those with
L<\min(T,C)
were included. Due to the right censoring, only the minimum of
T
and
C
is observed for each observation. We denote
\widetilde{T}=\min(T,C)
as the censored event time and
\Delta=\mathbbm{1}(T\leq C)
as the event indicator, where
\mathbbm{1}(\cdot)
is an indicator function. We let
X
be a marker or risk score for the prediction of event times, and
Z
be a vector of baseline covariates. We assume
X
is a function of
Z
, i.e.
X=\chi(Z)
.

Suppose the underlying (untruncated, uncensored) population data consist of random samples drawn from the distribution of
(L,T,C,Z)
. The observed data contains a random sample of size
n
drawn from the distribution of
(L,\widetilde{T}=\min(T,C),\Delta=\mathbbm{1}(T<C),Z)
conditioning on
L<\widetilde{T}
. We denote the data for observation
i
as
(L_{i},\widetilde{T}_{i},\Delta_{i},Z_{i})
.

We will use
S_{T}
to denote the survival function and
F_{T}
the distribution function of
T
, respectively. We will use
S_{T\mid Z}
and
F_{T\mid Z}
for the conditional survival and distribution functions of
T
conditioning on specific values of the covariates
Z
. With slight abuse of notation, we will use
\widehat{S}_{T}
,
\widehat{F}_{T}
,
\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}
and
F_{T\mid Z}
to denote their estimators. Marginal or conditional survival, distribution, and hazard functions for other random variables in the paper will be denoted in the same manner.

We consider two different scenarios for the relationship between censoring and truncation times, as originally
discussed in Qian and Betensky 2014
[
13
]
. Under each scenario, we further consider independent or covariate-induced dependent left truncation and right censoring.

Scenario A:

\mathbb{P}(C>L)=1
, that is, right censoring always occurs after study entry. This may be the case when the right censoring is due to loss of follow-up after study entry. In this case, we denote
D=C-L
as the study entry time. Following Hartman et al. 2023
[
11
]
, we consider two sets of independence assumptions relating left truncation and right censoring times to the event times:

Scenario A1
(marginal independence):
(L,D)\!\perp\!\!\!\perp T
and
D\!\perp\!\!\!\perp L\mid T
, that is, the left truncation, residual right censoring, and event times are mutually independent;

Scenario A2
(conditional independence given
Z
):
(L,D)\!\perp\!\!\!\perp T\mid Z
and
D\!\perp\!\!\!\perp L\mid T,Z
, that is, the left truncation, residual right censoring, and event times are mutually conditionally independent given
Z
;

Scenario B
:
P(C>L)<1
, that is, right censoring or loss of contact may occur before study entry. In the SJLIFE study described in Section
1
, childhood cancer survivors may be right censored due to loss of follow-up before. Similarly, we consider two sets of independence assumptions below:

Scenario B1
(marginal independence):
(L,C)\!\perp\!\!\!\perp T
and
L\!\perp\!\!\!\perp C\mid T
;

Scenario B2
(conditional independence):
(L,C)\!\perp\!\!\!\perp T\mid Z
and
L\!\perp\!\!\!\perp C\mid T,Z
.

For more general discussion of the different independence assumptions relating left truncation, right censoring, and event times, see Qian and Betensky 2014.
[
13
]

3
Novel Estimators for Time-dependent ROC analysis under LTRC data

3.1
Regression estimators

3.1.1
Estimation under independent left truncation and right censoring

From Equations (
1
) and (
2
) we immediately have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Se}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-S_{T}(t)-F_{T,X}(t,c)}{1-S_{T}(t)}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{F_{X}(c)-F_{T,X}(t,c)}{S_{T}(t)}

Therefore, under independent left-truncation and right censoring, natural estimators for
\mathrm{Se}(c,t)
and
\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)
are

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-\widehat{S}_{T}(t)-\widehat{F}_{T,X}(t,c)}{1-\widehat{S}_{T}(t)}

(4)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\widehat{F}_{X}(c)-\widehat{F}_{T,X}(t,c)}{\widehat{S}_{T}(t)}

(5)

our proposed regression estimator for
\mathrm{Se}(c,t)
is exactly the nonparametric estimator proposed in Li 2017. Therefore, we will also refer to the estimator
\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(c,t)
as the nonparametric regression estimator. In the above equations, for
\widehat{S}_{T}(t)
, we may use the Kaplan-Meier estimator of
S_{T}(t)
with risk set adjusted for left truncation
[
18
]
. Following Li 2017, we estimate
F_{T,X}(t,c)
by

\widehat{F}_{T,X}(t,c)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i}\leq t,X_{i}\leq c,\Delta_{i}=1)\widehat{S}_{T}(\widetilde{T}_{i}-)}{\widehat{R}(\widetilde{T}_{i})},

(6)

where

\widehat{R}(t)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}<t\leq\widetilde{T}_{i})

(7)

is the estimated proportion of observations at risk at time
t
. Although Li derived
\widehat{F}_{T,X}
under scenario A1, in Appendix
B
we will also show that
\widehat{F}_{T,X}
is a consistent estimator of scenario B1. In Equation (
6
), letting
t=\infty
, we obtain the estimator

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{X}(c)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}\leq c,\Delta_{i}=1)\widehat{S}_{T}(\widetilde{T}_{i}-)}{\widehat{R}(\widetilde{T}_{i})}.

(8)

Therefore, the nonparametric sensitivity and specificity estimators can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=1-\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i}\leq t,X_{i}\leq c,\Delta_{i}=1)\widehat{S}_{T}(\widetilde{T}_{i}-)}{\widehat{R}(\widetilde{T}_{i})\{1-\widehat{S}_{T}(t)\}},

(9)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i}>t,X_{i}\leq c,\Delta_{i}=1)\widehat{S}_{T}(\widetilde{T}_{i}-)}{\widehat{R}(\widetilde{T}_{i})\widehat{S}_{T}(t)},

(10)

An estimator of
\mathrm{AUC}(t)
may be obtained through numeric integration:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(t)

\displaystyle=\int\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(c,t)\,\mathrm{d}\{1-\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(c,t)\}

\displaystyle=1-\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}\leq X_{j},\widetilde{T}_{i}\leq t,\widetilde{T}_{j}>t)\widehat{S}_{T}(T_{i}-)\widehat{S}_{T}(T_{j}-)}{\widehat{R}(\widetilde{T}_{i})\widehat{R}(\widetilde{T}_{j})}/\left[\widehat{S}_{T}(t)\{1-\widehat{S}_{T}(t)\}\right]

(11)

3.1.2
Estimation under covariate-induced dependent left truncation and right censoring

Due to the fact that
X=\chi(Z)
, the sensitivity
\mathrm{Se}(c,t)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Se}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(\chi(Z)>c,T\leq t)}{\mathbb{P}(T\leq t)}=\dfrac{\int\mathbbm{1}(\chi(z)>c)\{1-S_{T\mid Z}(t\mid z)\}\,\mathrm{d}F_{Z}(z)}{\int\{1-S_{T\mid Z}(t\mid z)\}\,\mathrm{d}F_{Z}(z)}

and similarly,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\int\mathbbm{1}(\chi(z)\leq c)S_{T\mid Z}(t\mid z)\,\mathrm{d}F_{Z}(z)}{\int S_{T\mid Z}(t\mid z)\,\mathrm{d}F_{Z}(z)},

\displaystyle\mathrm{AUC}(t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\int\int\mathbbm{1}(\chi(z_{i})>\chi(z_{j}))\{1-S_{T\mid Z}(t\mid z_{i})\}S_{T\mid Z}(t\mid z_{j})\,\mathrm{d}F_{Z}(z_{i})\mathrm{d}F_{Z}(z_{j})}{\int\int\{1-S_{T\mid Z}(t\mid z_{i})\}S_{T\mid Z}(t\mid z_{j})\,\mathrm{d}F_{Z}(z_{i})\mathrm{d}F_{Z}(z_{j})}.

Therefore, we may estimate the above parameters by replacing
S_{T\mid Z}
and
dF_{Z}(z)
with appropriate estimators. The estimator
\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}
may come from an appropriate regression model for time-to-event outcomes, such as Cox proportional hazards regression or the accelerated failure time model.
[
19
,
20
]
In Appendix
B
, under scenarios A2 and B2, we show that
dF_{Z}(z)
may be estimated as

d\widehat{F}_{Z}(z)=\int_{l\in(0,\infty)}\dfrac{\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}\widehat{F}_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(L_{i}\mid Z_{i})\}^{-1}}

and

d\widehat{F}_{Z}(z)=\int_{l\in(0,\infty)}\dfrac{\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)\widehat{S}_{C\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}\widehat{F}_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<\widetilde{T})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(L_{i}\mid Z_{i})\widehat{S}_{C\mid Z}(L_{i}\mid Z_{i})\}^{-1}}

respectively, where
\widehat{F}_{L,Z}(\cdot,\cdot\mid L<\widetilde{T})
is the empirical joint distribution function of
(L,Z)
in the LTRC data. As a result, we obtain the following semiparametric regression estimators

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{REG-SP}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}>c)\{1-\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(t\mid Z_{i})\}/\widehat{H}(L_{i},Z_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{1-\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(t\mid Z_{i})\}/\widehat{H}(L_{i},Z_{i})},

(12)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{REG-SP}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}\leq c)\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(t\mid Z_{i})/\widehat{H}(L_{i},Z_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(t\mid Z_{i})/\widehat{H}(L_{i},Z_{i})},

(13)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}_{\text{REG-SP}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}>X_{j})\{1-\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(t\mid Z_{i})\}\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(t\mid Z_{j})/\left\{\widehat{H}(L_{i},Z_{i})\widehat{H}(L_{j},Z_{j})\right\}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{1-\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(t\mid Z_{i})\}\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(t\mid Z_{j})/\left\{\widehat{H}(L_{i},Z_{i})\widehat{H}(L_{j},Z_{j})\right\}},

(14)

where we set
\widehat{H}(u,z)=\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(u\mid z)
under scenario A2, and
\widehat{H}(u,z)=\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(u\mid z)\widehat{S}_{C\mid Z}(u\mid z)
under scenario B2.

Proof of consistency and asymptotic normality of the above estimators directly follows Theorem 1 in Li 2017
[
12
]
. However, these appealing properties rely on the consistency and asymptotic normality of
\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}
and, under scenario B2, both
\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}
and
\widehat{S}_{C\mid Z}
. When an incorrect model for the conditional distribution of
T\mid Z
is assumed, the semiparametric estimators may be biased.

3.2
Inverse probability weighting (IPW) estimators

In this section, we develop alternative IPW estimators for time-dependent sensitivity, specificity, and AUC that don’t rely on estimating
S_{T\mid Z}
.

3.2.1
Estimation under independent left truncation and right censoring

We first consider estimation of the numerator and denominator of
\mathrm{Se}(c,t)
in Equation (
1
). Heuristically, for a fixed time
t
, the true event time would be known to be no larger than
t
if only an uncensored event occurs at or before
t
. Therefore, for the estimation, we may only include the uncensored observations. We then adjust for the selection of untruncated and uncensored observations via inverse probability weighting. More formally, under independent left truncation and right censoring (Scenarios A1 and B1), we observe that

\displaystyle E\left\{\dfrac{\Delta}{\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>T\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(X>c,\tilde{T}\leq t)\mid L<T\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}E\left\{\dfrac{\Delta\mathbbm{1}(L<T)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>T\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(X>c,T\leq t)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}E\left[E\left\{\dfrac{\Delta\mathbbm{1}(L<T)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>T\mid T)}\mid T\right\}\mathbbm{1}(X>c,T\leq t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(X>c,T\leq t)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}

(15)

and similarly,

\displaystyle E\left\{\dfrac{\Delta}{\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>T\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(X>c,\tilde{T}\leq t)\mid L<T\right\}=\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(T\leq t)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}.

(16)

The inverse probability weights
1/\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>T\mid T)
have been used in Hartman et al. 2023
[
11
]
as well as Morenz et al. 2014
[
21
]
for more general bias adjustment with LTRC data. Equations (
15
) and (
16
) motivate the IPW estimator of
\mathrm{Se}(c,t)
of the following form:

\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{IPW}}(c,t)=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\widehat{K}_{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i})}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}>c,\widetilde{T}_{i}<t)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\widehat{K}_{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i})}\mathbbm{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i}<t)}

(17)

where
\widehat{K}_{1}(u)
is an estimator of
\mathbb{P}(L<u,C>u\mid T=u)
.

Under Scenario A1, we have

\mathbb{P}(L<u,C>u\mid T=u)=\mathbb{P}(L<u,D>u-L\mid T=u)=\int_{0}^{u}S_{D}(u-s)dF_{L}(s),

suggesting the estimator

\widehat{K}_{1}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\widehat{S}_{D}(u-s)d\hat{F}_{L}(s),

where
\widehat{S}_{D}
is the Kaplan-Meier estimator for the survival function of the residual censoring time. For
\widehat{F}_{L}(s)
, under scenario A1, we use the IPW estimator

\widehat{F}_{L}(s)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}\leq s)}{\widehat{S}_{T}(L_{i})}\right)/\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\widehat{S}_{T}(L_{i})}\right)

proposed in Wang 1991.
[
22
]

Under scenario B1, we may simply use
\widehat{K}_{1}(u)=\widehat{S}_{C}(u)\widehat{F}_{L}(u)
, where
\widehat{S}_{C}(u)
is the Kaplan-Meier estimator of
S_{C}(u)
with the risk set adjusted for left truncation. In. Appendix
B
, we show that under scenario B1, an analogous estimator for
F_{L}(s)
is

\widehat{F}_{L}(s)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}\leq s)}{\widehat{S}_{T}(L_{i})\widehat{S}_{C}(L_{i})}\right)/\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\widehat{S}_{T}(L_{i})\widehat{S}_{C}(L_{i})}\right).

Next, we consider the estimation of
\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)
. For an observation to contribute to the estimation of
T>t
, we only need
\tilde{T}>t
even if the observation is censored. In this case, we need to adjust for left truncation in addition to the fact that censoring occurs after time
t
. Formally, we observe that

\displaystyle E\left\{\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>t\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(\tilde{T}>t)\mid L<T\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}E\left\{\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(L<T,C>t)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>t\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(T>t)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}E\left[E\left\{\mathbbm{1}(L<T,C>t)\mid T\right\}\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>t\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(T>t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(T>t)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}

(18)

and similarly,

\displaystyle E\left\{\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<T,C>t\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(X<c,\tilde{T}>t)\mid L<T\right\}=\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(X<c,T>t)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}.

(19)

This suggests an inverse probability weighting estimator of
\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)
with the form

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{IPW-1}}(c,t)=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\widehat{K}_{2}(t,\widetilde{T}_{i})}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}\leq c,\widetilde{T}_{i}>t)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\widehat{K}_{2}(t,\widetilde{T}_{i})}\mathbbm{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i}>t)}

(20)

where
\widehat{K}_{2}(t,u)
is an estimator of
\mathbb{P}(L<u,C>t\mid T=u)
. Combined with
\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{IPW}}(c,t)
, we obtain an estimator of the AUC in the form of a U-statistic:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}_{\text{IPW-1}}(t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\widehat{K}_{1}(T_{i})\widehat{K}_{2}(t,T_{j})}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}>X_{j},T_{i}\leq t,T_{j}>t)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\widehat{K}_{1}(T_{i})\widehat{K}_{2}(t,T_{j})}\mathbbm{1}(T_{i}\leq t,T_{j}>t)}

(21)

Similar to before, under Scenarios A1 and B1, we may use the estimator

\widehat{K}_{2}(t,u)=\int_{0}^{u}\widehat{S}_{D}(t-s)d\widehat{F}_{L}(s)
and
\widehat{K}_{2}(t,u)=\widehat{S}_{C}(t)\widehat{F}_{L}(u)
, respectively.

Finally, noticing that when estimating
\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)
, if we only limit to the observations that are at risk at
t
, then by a similar derivation of Equations (
18
) and (
19
), we have

\displaystyle E\left\{\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<t<C\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(X<c,L<t<\tilde{T})\mid L<T\right\}=\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(X<c,T>t)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}

(22)

and

\displaystyle E\left\{\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{P}(L<t<C\mid T)}\mathbbm{1}(L<t<\widetilde{T})\mid L<T\right\}=\dfrac{\mathbb{P}(T>t)}{\mathbb{P}(L<T)}

(23)

This motivates an alternative estimator
\mathrm{Sp}(c,t)
:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{IPW-2}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\widehat{K}_{1}(t)}\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}<t<\widetilde{T}_{i},X_{i}\leq c)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\widehat{K}_{1}(t)}\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}<t,\tilde{T}_{i}>t)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}<t,X_{i}\leq c,\tilde{T}_{i}>t)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}<t<\tilde{T}_{i})}

(24)

This resulting estimator is indeed identical to the nonparametric estimator of specificity in Li 2017. Li’s proposed nonparametric estimator of AUC can be written as

\widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}_{\text{LI-np}}(t)=\int\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{REG-NP}}(c,t)\,\mathrm{d}\{1-\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{IPW-2}}(c,t)\}.

(25)

In contrast, we propose another IPW estimator of time-dependent AUC as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}_{\text{IPW-2}}(t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\widehat{K}_{1}(T_{i})}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}>X_{j},T_{i}\leq t,L_{j}<t<T_{j})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\widehat{K}_{1}(T_{i})}\mathbbm{1}(T_{i}\leq t,L_{j}<t<T_{j})}.

(26)

Proof of consistency and weak convergence of the proposed sensitivity, specificity, and AUC estimators directly follows the same arguments from Web Appendix D of Hartman et al. 2023. 6
Discussion

In this article, we developed and evaluated cumulative/dynamic time-dependent ROC analysis using a regression-based or IPW-based approach for the LTRC data. The regression-based approach is closely connected to Li 2017
[
12
]
, although we have extend the methods in several directions: (1) we consider both the setting where right censoring can only occur after study entry and the setting where right censoring may occur before study entry; (2) for the semiparametric estimators, we allow the left truncation time to depend on event time conditioning on covariates other than the score of interest. Our proposed approach applies to a wide range of settings where we are interested in evaluating a risk prediction model or a biomarker using time-to-event data, but delayed study entry in the validation data may present an issue.

In the simulation studies and data application, under independent left truncation, both IPW estimators and the nonparametric regression estimator perform well; under covariate-dependent left truncation with a correctly specified model for the distribution of left truncation time, the semiparametric regression estimator exhibits a small negative bias, while the two conditional IPW estimators perform well in all scenarios. We hypothesize that observations with late study entry (and therefore large weight values of
1/S_{T\mid Z}(L_{i}\mid Z_{i})
) may unstabilize the estimators. We thus recommend the conditional IPW estimators as more robust estimators in this scenario.

The proposed conditional IPW estimators are general – to estimate the nuisance functions including
F_{L\mid Z}
and
S_{D\mid Z}
or
S_{C\mid Z}
, in this paper, we have only used Cox proportional hazards regression, although other suitable regression models for the time-to-event outcomes may be considered. With moderate or large samples, the kernel-based Kaplan-Meier type estimators or flexible modeling methods such as general additive models provide nonparametric alternatives.
[
25
]
In practice, some preliminary model selection may be employed to choose the suitable regression models and covariates. Finally, as a future direction, doubly robust estimation of the time-dependent ROC parameters may be developed based on the recent work of doubly robust estimation with LTRC data, which allows flexible modeling of the nuisance functions while retaining the asymptotic normality of the estimators.
[
21
,
26
,
27
]

Appendix A
Review of estimators in Li 2017

For time-dependent ROC analysis with LTRC data, Li developed two sets of regression-type estimators of sensitivity, specificity and AUC under the scenario
\mathbb{P}(C>L)=1
: the nonparametric estimators which assume that censoring and truncation times are independent from the event times, and the semiparametric estimators which assume that censoring and truncation times are conditionally independent from the event times given the score. The nonparametric estimators are

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{LI-np}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-\widehat{S}_{T}(t)-\widehat{F}_{T,X}(t,c)}{1-\widehat{S}_{T}(t)},

(32)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{LI-np}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}>c,L_{i}<t<\tilde{T}_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}<t<\widetilde{T}_{i})},

(33)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}_{\text{LI-np}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=1-\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\mathbbm{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i}\leq t,X_{i}<X_{j},L_{j}<t<\widetilde{T}_{j})\hat{S}_{T}(\widetilde{T}_{i}-)\widehat{R}^{-1}(\widetilde{T}_{i})}{n^{2}\widehat{R}(t)\{1-\widehat{S}_{T}(t)\}}.

(34)

Here
\widehat{S}_{T}(t)
is the Kaplan-Meier estimator of
S_{T}(t)
with risk set adjusted for left truncation
[
18
]
,
\widehat{F}_{T,X}(t,c)
is an estimator of
\mathbb{P}(T\leq t,X\leq c)
given by

\widehat{F}_{T,X}(t,c)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i}\leq t,X_{i}\leq c,\Delta_{i}=1)\widehat{S}(\widetilde{T}_{i}-)}{\widehat{R}(\widetilde{T}_{i})},

and

\widehat{R}(t)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}<t\leq\widetilde{T}_{i})

is the proportion of observations at risk at time
t
. We refer the interested readers to Li 2017 for the motivation and derivation of these estimators.

The semiparametric estimators, which account for score-induced dependent left truncation, can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Se}}_{\text{LI-sp}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}>c)\{1-\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(t\mid X_{i})\}/\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(L_{i}\mid X_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{1-\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(t\mid X_{i})\}/\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(L_{i}\mid X_{i})},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\text{LI-sp}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}\leq c)\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(t\mid X_{i})/\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(L_{i}\mid X_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(t\mid X_{i})/\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(L_{i}\mid X_{i})},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{AUC}}_{\text{LI-sp}}(c,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(X_{i}>X_{j})\{1-\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(t\mid X_{i})\}\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(t\mid X_{j})/\left\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(L_{i}\mid X_{i})\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(L_{j}\mid X_{j})\right\}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{1-\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(t\mid X_{i})\}\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(t\mid X_{j})/\left\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(L_{i}\mid X_{i})\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}(L_{j}\mid X_{j})\right\}}.

Li 2017 proposed using the proportional odds model to obtain
\widehat{S}_{T\mid X}
, although the Cox proportional hazards model or other regression methods for event times may also be used, with risk set adjusted to account for left truncation
[
19
]
. In the original semiparametric estimators, it is assumed that the dependence between the left truncation times, right censoring times, and event times is only induced by the score
X
, which is often unrealistic in practice. Furthermore, when it is of interest to compare the predictive performances of several scores, the above semiparametric estimators would make different conditional independence assumptions when evaluating each marker. Finally, estimators under the scenario
\mathbb{P}(C>L)<1
were not discussed.

F_{T,X}(c,t)=\int_{u\leq t,v\leq c}\dfrac{S_{T}(u-)}{R(u)}d\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{T}\leq u,\Delta=1,X\leq v\mid L<T).

Replacing

\widehat{S}_{T}(u-)

with

7
), and
\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{T}\leq u,\Delta=1,X\leq v\mid L<T)
with its empirical estimator

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{T}\leq u,\Delta=1,X\leq v\mid L<T)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}(\widetilde{T}_{i}\leq u,\Delta_{i}=1,X_{i}\leq v),

we obtain the estimator
\widehat{F}_{T,X}(t,c)
in Equation (
6
).

Under scenario B1, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{T}\leq t,\Delta=1,X\leq c\mid L<\widetilde{T})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(L<T\leq t,C\geq T,X\leq c)/\mathbb{P}(L<\widetilde{T})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{c}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}(L<u,C\geq u)F_{T,X}(du,dv)/\mathbb{P}(L<\widetilde{T}).

The probability of being at risk at time
u
is

\displaystyle R(u)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(L<u\leq\widetilde{T}\mid L<\widetilde{T})=P(L<u,C\geq u)S_{T}(u-)/\mathbb{P}(L<\widetilde{T}).

Again, we have

\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{T}\leq t,\Delta=1,X\leq c\mid L<\widetilde{T})=\int_{-\infty}^{c}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{R(u)}{S_{T}(u-)}F_{T,X}(du,dv).

Similar derivation as before gives the same estimator
\widehat{F}_{T,X}(t,c)
.

Next, we consider the estimator for
F_{L}(u)
. Under scenario A1, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L\leq u\mid L<T)

\displaystyle\propto\mathbb{P}(L\leq u,L<T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u}\mathbb{P}(T>s\mid L=s)dF_{L}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u}S_{T}(s)dF_{L}(s)

\displaystyle\text{(Since $T\!\perp\!\!\!\perp L$)}.

Taking the derivative with respect to
u
on both sides and rearranging the terms, we have

dF_{L}(u)\propto S_{T}(u)^{-1}d\mathbb{P}(L\leq u\mid L<T).

Since
\int_{0}^{\infty}dF_{L}(u)=1
, we have

dF_{L}(u)=\dfrac{S_{T}(u)^{-1}d\mathbb{P}(L\leq u\mid L<T)}{\int S_{T}(s)^{-1}d\mathbb{P}(L\leq s\mid L<T)}.

So

F_{L}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}dF_{L}(s)=\dfrac{\int_{0}^{u}S_{T}(s)^{-1}d\mathbb{P}(L\leq s\mid L<T)}{\int S_{T}(s)^{-1}d\mathbb{P}(L\leq s\mid L<T)}.

Replacing
S_{T}(s)
with
\widehat{S}_{T}(s)
and
\mathbb{P}(L\leq s\mid L<T)
with its empirical distribution function estimator, we obtain

\widehat{F}_{L}(u)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}\leq u)}{\widehat{S}_{T}(L_{i})}\right)/\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\widehat{S}_{T}(L_{i})}\right).

Under scenario B1, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L\leq u\mid L<\widetilde{T})

\displaystyle\propto\mathbb{P}(L\leq u,L<\widetilde{T})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u}\mathbb{P}(T>s,C>s\mid L=s)dF_{L}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u}S_{T}(s)S_{C}(s)dF_{L}(s)

\displaystyle\text{(Since $T\!\perp\!\!\!\perp L$)}.

With the same derivation, we can show that

F_{L}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}dF_{L}(s)=\dfrac{\int_{0}^{u}\{S_{T}(s)S_{C}(s)\}^{-1}d\mathbb{P}(L\leq s\mid L<\widetilde{T})}{\int\{S_{T}(s)S_{C}(s)\}^{-1}d\mathbb{P}(L\leq s\mid L<\widetilde{T})}.

We obtain the estimator

\widehat{F}_{L}(u)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbbm{1}(L_{i}\leq u)}{\widehat{S}_{T}(L_{i})\widehat{S}_{C}(L_{i})}\right)/\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\widehat{S}_{T}(L_{i})\widehat{S}_{C}(L_{i})}\right).

Finally, we consider the estimator for
dF_{Z}(z)
. Under Scenario A1, for any measurable set
\zeta
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L\leq u,Z\in\zeta\mid L<T)

\displaystyle\propto\int_{z\in\zeta}\int_{0}^{u}\mathbb{P}(T>l\mid Z=z,L=l)F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{z\in\zeta}\int_{0}^{u}S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)

\displaystyle\text{(Since $T\!\perp\!\!\!\perp L\mid Z$)}.

Taking the derivative on both sides, we have

F_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T)\propto S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)

and so

F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)\propto\{S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}F_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T).

Since
\int\int F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)=1
, we have

F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)=\dfrac{\{S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}F_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T)}{\int\int\{S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}F_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T)}.

Integrating over
u
, we have

F_{Z}(dz)=\dfrac{\int_{l\in(0,\infty)}\{S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}F_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T)}{\int\int\{S_{T\mid Z}(s\mid z)\}^{-1}F_{L,Z}(ds,dz\mid L<T)}.

Replacing
S_{T\mid Z}(u\mid z)
with an appropriate estimator
\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(u\mid z)
and
F_{L,Z}(\cdot,\cdot\mid L<T)
with the empirical distribution function, we obtain the estimator

d\widehat{F}_{Z}(z)=\int_{l\in(0,\infty)}\dfrac{\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}\widehat{F}_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(L_{i}\mid Z_{i})\}^{-1}}

Under Scenario B1, for any measurable set
\zeta
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L\leq u,Z\in\zeta\mid L<\widetilde{T})

\displaystyle\propto\int_{z\in\zeta}\int_{0}^{u}\mathbb{P}(T>l,C>l\mid Z=z,L=l)F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{z\in\zeta}\int_{0}^{u}S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)S_{C\mid Z}(l\mid z)F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)

The second equivalence is because
T
,
C
, and
L
are conditionally mutually independent given
Z
. Taking the derivative on both sides, we have

F_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<\widetilde{T})\propto S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)S_{C\mid Z}(l\mid z)F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)

and therefore

F_{L,Z}(dl,dz)\propto\{S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)S_{C\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}F_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<\widetilde{T}).

With the same derivation as before, we can show that

F_{Z}(dz)=\dfrac{\int_{l\in(0,\infty)}\{S_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)S_{C\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}F_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T)}{\int\int\{S_{T\mid Z}(s\mid z)S_{C\mid Z}(s\mid z)\}^{-1}F_{L,Z}(ds,dz\mid L<T)}.

We obtain the estimator

d\widehat{F}_{Z}(z)=\int_{l\in(0,\infty)}\dfrac{\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(l\mid z)\widehat{S}_{C\mid Z}(l\mid z)\}^{-1}\widehat{F}_{L,Z}(dl,dz\mid L<T)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\widehat{S}_{T\mid Z}(L_{i}\mid Z_{i})\widehat{S}_{C\mid Z}(L_{i}\mid Z_{i})\}^{-1}}

A General Connected Sum Formula for the Families Bauer-Furuta Invariant
Joshua Tomlin

Abstract
. The Bauer-Furuta invariant of a family of smooth 4-manifolds is a stable cohomotopy refinement of the families Seiberg-Witten invariant and is constructed from a finite dimensional approximation of the Seiberg-Witten monopole map. We prove a general formula for the families Bauer-Furuta invariant of a fibrewise connected sum, extending Bauer’s non-parameterised formula [ 8 ] . In a subsequent paper [ 28 ] , we will use this formula to derive a general connected sum formula for the families Seiberg-Witten invariant which incorporates both the families blow-up formula of Liu [ 22 ] and the gluing formula of Baraglia-Konno [ 5 ] .



1.
Introduction

The Bauer-Furuta invariant
[
9
]
of a 4-manifold is a stable cohomotopy refinement of its integer valued Seiberg-Witten invariant. Specifically, it is the equivariant stable cohomotopy class of a finite dimensional approximation of the Seiberg-Witten monopole map. This approach takes a new perspective of studying the monopole map, rather than its moduli space of solutions. It is possible to recover the Seiberg-Witten invariant from the Bauer-Furuta invariant, hence techniques from algebraic topology can be used to circumvent laborious analytical arguments.

In subsequent work, Bauer derived a formula
[
8
]
for the Bauer-Furuta invariant of a connected sum of 4-manifolds. His idea was to analyse behaviour of monopoles on a 4-manifold with an
n
-component separating neck
N(L)=\coprod_{n}S^{3}\times[-L,L]
of varying length
2L
. He showed that given a 4-manifold with a separating neck, ends of the necks can be permuted without changing the Bauer-Furuta class of the monopole map. The key insight was that monopoles decay exponentially towards the middle of the neck, hence stretching the neck could be used to control the dynamics in the middle.

Since Donaldson’s suggestion in 1996
[
14
]
, there has been much interest in studying the Seiberg-Witten equations of 4-manifold families. Several authors including Li-Liu, Nakamura and Ruberman have generalised Seiberg-Witten theory to the families setting
[
21
,
23
,
25
]
. This body of work involves wall crossing formulas, non-existence of positive scalar curvature metrics, and a particularly noteworthy families blow-up formula
[
22
]
due to Liu. One striking application of families Seiberg-Witten theory applied to mapping tori is the construction of 4-manifolds with diffeomorphisms that are continuously isotopic to the identity, but not smoothly isotopic
[
24
]
.

Since 2019, Baraglia has contributed to the theory of families Seiberg-Witten invariants in several papers
[
3
,
5
,
4
]
. In
[
5
]
, Baraglia-Konno proved a connected sum formula for the families Seiberg-Witten invariant under some restrictive assumptions. These assumptions simplified the moduli space of one of the summands and avoided cases involving chambers. The overarching goal of this paper and upcoming work
[
28
]
is to derive a completely general connected sum formula for families Seiberg-Witten invariants extending both Baraglia-Konno’s formula and Liu’s families blow-up formula.

This is accomplished by first proving a similar result for the families Bauer-Furuta invariant. Szymik illustrated in
[
27
]
that the Bauer-Furuta invariant naturally extends to the families setting. In this paper, we prove the following families connected sum formula, generalising Bauer’s formula for the unparameterised case.

Theorem 1.1
.

For

, let

be a smooth family of closed, oriented 4-manifolds equipped with a

exists with normal bundle

and suppose that

Then the families Bauer-Furuta class of the fiberwise connected sum

is

\displaystyle[\mu_{E}]

\displaystyle=[\mu_{E_{1}}]\wedge_{\Jcal}[\mu_{E_{2}}].

In 2021, Baraglia-Konno demonstrated how to recover the families Seiberg-Witten invariant from the families Bauer-Furuta invariant via a formulation of the families Seiberg-Witten invariant in equivariant cohomology
[

]
. In upcoming work
[

, we will use this formulation and the above formula to prove a connected sum formula for the families Seiberg-Witten invariant.

2.
Finite dimensional approximation

The Bauer-Furuta invariant is obtained from the stable homotopy class of an approximation of the Seiberg-Witten monopole map by finite dimensional subspaces. In
[
9
]
, two methods of finite dimensional approximation are described, one method due to Schwarz
[
29
]
and one due to Bauer-Furuta. The Bauer-Furuta method is useful for formally defining the invariant, while the Schwarz method is more useful for practical calculations. Bauer further clarifies their construction in
[
7
]
using Spanier-Whitehead spectra. We begin by reviewing these two constructions and showing that they are equivalent.

Let
X
and
Y
denote pointed topological spaces. We will assume that all maps
f:X\to Y
are continuous and basepoint preserving. Denote by
[X,Y]
the set of based homotopy classes of maps between
X
and
Y
. Let
S^{n}
denote the unit sphere in
\R\oplus\R^{n}
with
\infty=(1,0)\in S^{n}
as the basepoint. The
n
-th homotopy group of
X
is

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(X)

\displaystyle=[S^{n},X].

The suspension functor
\Sigma X=S^{1}\wedge X
defines a map of homotopy groups

\displaystyle\Sigma:\pi_{n}(\Sigma^{n}X)\to\pi_{n+1}(\Sigma^{n+1}X).

The Freudenthal suspension theorem
[
16
]
states that this map is an isomorphism for large enough
n
and the
n
-th stable homotopy group is defined by

\displaystyle\pi_{n}^{s}(X)

\displaystyle=\Colim_{\longrightarrow k}\pi_{n+k}(\Sigma^{k}X).

In the stable range, the homotopy group
\pi_{n+k}(\Sigma^{k}X)=[S^{n+k},\Sigma^{k}X]
does not depend on the dimension of the domain and codomain, but only on the difference in dimensions. In the opposite fashion, the
n
-th cohomotopy set of
X
is given by
\pi^{n}(X)=[X,S^{n}]
. The functor
\pi^{n}
is now contravariant, but suspension still defines a map
\Sigma:\pi^{n}(X)\to\pi^{n+1}(\Sigma X)
. The
n
-th stable cohomotopy group of
X
is defined as

\displaystyle\pi^{n}_{s}(X)

\displaystyle=\Colim_{\longrightarrow k}\pi^{n+k}(\Sigma^{k}X).

The stable cohomotopy groups define a generalised cohomology theory, and Brown’s representability theorem
[
12
]
guarantees that this cohomology theory is representable. The natural objects for representing stable cohomotopy groups are spectra, in particular, the sphere spectrum
\Sspec^{n}
represents the above groups. In order to define the Bauer-Furuta invariant, it will be more convenient to work with spaces that are indexed by finite dimensional subspaces of an infinite dimensional Hilbert space. This is more general than indexing by the natural numbers and allows us to keep track of coordinates when taking suspensions.

Let
G
be a compact lie group. For our purposes,
G
will always be a product of circles. A
G
-space is a pointed topological space
X
with a continuous left action
G\times X\to X
that fixes the basepoint. For two
G
-spaces
X
and
Y
, let
[X,Y]^{G}
denote the set of homotopy classes through equivariant pointed maps. The diagonal subgroup of
G\times G
naturally defines a
G
-action on the smash product
X\wedge Y
.

for any choice of admissible subrepresentation
V\subset\Ucal
. This cohomotopy class is independent of
V
and the presentation
f=l+c
.

Proof 2.14
.

Fix an admissible subrepresentation
V\subset\Ucal
and recall that
\ind l=V^{\prime}-\Vund
, hence the Thom spectrum
T(\ind l)
is given by
T(\ind l)=\Sigma^{-V}TV^{\prime}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\pi^{0}_{G,\Ucal}(B;\ind l)

\displaystyle=\Hom(T(\ind l),\Sspec^{0})

\displaystyle=\Colim_{U\subset V^{\perp}}[S_{U}\wedge TV^{\prime},S_{U}\wedge S_{V}].

Here
U\subset\Ucal
is orthogonal to
V
and the connecting morphisms are given by smash products with the identity. For any other admissible subrepresentation
W
, there is an admissible subrepresentation containing both
V
and
W
. Hence property (3) implies that the Bauer-Furuta classes corresponding to
V
and
W
are stably homotopic, therefore
[\vphi_{f}]\in\pi^{0}_{G,\Ucal}(B;\ind l)
is well defined.

To see that
[\vphi_{f}]
does not depend on the choice of decomposition
f=l+c
, let
f=l_{i}+c_{i}
be two Fredholm decompositions for
i=0,1
. Let
F_{t}=l_{t}+c_{t}
for
l_{t}=(1-t)l_{0}+tl_{1}
and
c_{t}=(1-t)c_{0}+tc_{1}
, noting that
F_{t}=f
for all
t
. The maps
l_{t}
are linear Fredholm and the maps
c_{t}
are compact. Now
F
is a Fredholm map over
B\times[0,1]
which is certainly bounded. Applying finite dimensional approximation to
F
gives a homotopy between finite dimensional approximations of
f
using the two different presentations
f=l_{0}+c_{0}
and
f=l_{1}+c_{1}
.

2.2.
Schwarz approximation

In
[
29
]
, Schwarz details an alternative approach to finite dimensional approximation. Let
D^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime}
be a closed disk bundle with boundary sphere bundle
S^{\prime}
. Fix a trivialisation
H\cong\Ucal\times B
and let
\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
denote the set of continuous maps
f:D^{\prime}\to\Ucal
such that
c=f-l|_{D^{\prime}}
is compact and
f|_{S^{\prime}}
is non-vanishing.

Definition 2.15
.

Two Fredholm maps
f_{0},f_{1}:D^{\prime}\to H
are compactly homotopic (relative to
l
) if there is a homotopy
f_{t}=l+c_{t}
with
c_{t}
compact and
(f_{t})|_{S^{\prime}}
non-vanishing for all
t\in[0,1]
. More generally, we say that two bounded Fredholm maps
f_{0},f_{1}:H^{\prime}\to H
are compactly homotopic if there exists a disk
D^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime}
containing
f_{0}\inv(0)\cup f_{1}\inv(0)
on which the restrictions
f_{0}|_{D^{\prime}}
and
f_{1}|_{D^{\prime}}
are compactly homotopic.

Give
\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
the uniform convergence topology so that
\pi_{0}(\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H))
is the set of compact homotopy classes relative to
l
. The homotopy class of a Fredholm map
f:H^{\prime}\to H
is dull since it is classified by
\ind l

[
13
]
, but restricting to homotopies through
\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
uncovers more interesting behaviour.

Let
f=l+c\in\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
. Suppose for now that
c(D^{\prime})
is contained in a finite dimensional subrepresentation
V\subset\Ucal
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
(\im l_{b})^{\perp}\subset V
for all
b\in B
. Let
V^{\prime}=l\inv(V)
, which is a vector bundle of rank
\dim V^{\prime}=\dim V+\ind l
. Denote the restriction
f|_{D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}}
by

\displaystyle\psi_{f,V}

\displaystyle=f|_{D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}}:(D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime})\to(V,V\setminus\{0\})

Let
W\supset V
be a finite dimensional subrepresentation containing
V
with
W=U\oplus V
orthogonally. Let
W^{\prime}=l\inv(W)
so that
W^{\prime}=\tU\oplus V
orthogonally with
l|_{\Ut}:\Ut\to\Uund
an isomorphism. For any map
g:(D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime})\to(V,V\setminus\{0\})
, define a suspension map

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\Ut}g:(D^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime})\to(W,W\setminus\{0\})

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\Ut}g(u+v)=l(u)+g(v).

Note that for
w=u+v
, if
\Sigma^{\Ut}g(w)=0
then
g(v)=0
and
u=0
, which implies that
w\notin S^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}
. Let
[(A,B);(C,D)]
denote the set of homotopy classes of maps from
(A,B)
to
(C,D)
where the homotopies are through maps of pairs. Then
\Sigma^{\Ut}
descends to a map of homotopy classes

(7)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\Ut}:[(D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime});(V,V\setminus\{0\})]

\displaystyle\to[(D^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime});(W,W\setminus\{0\})].

Define

(8)

\displaystyle\Pi_{l}(D^{\prime},H)=\Colim_{V\subset\Ucal}[(D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime});(V,V\setminus\{0\})]

where the colimit is taken over the maps given by (
7
). Any map
g:(D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime})\to(V,V\setminus\{0\})
defines a class
[g]\in\Pi_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
by suspension. The map
\psi_{f,V}
depends on the choice of subrepresentation
V
, however the class of
[\psi_{f,V}]\in\Pi_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
does not.

Lemma 2.16
.

For
f=l+c\in\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
, suppose that
V
and
W
are finite dimensional subrepresentations which both contain
c(D^{\prime})
and surject onto
\coker l
. Then
[\psi_{f,V}]
and
[\psi_{f,W}]
are equal classes of
\Pi_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
.

Proof 2.17
.

Assume without loss of generality that
V\subset W
. As before write
W=U\oplus V
and
W^{\prime}=\tU\oplus V^{\prime}
orthogonally with
l|_{\Ut}:\Ut\to\Uund
an isomorphism. For any element
u+v\in W^{\prime}
with
u\in\Ut
and
v\in V^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle f|_{S^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}}(u+v)=l(u)+l(v)+c(u+v).

Define a homotopy

\displaystyle F_{t}(u+v)=l(u)+l(v)+(1-t)c(v)+tc(v+u).

This is a homotopy from
F_{0}=\Sigma^{\Ut}\psi_{f,V}
to
F_{1}=\psi_{f,W}
. Additionally,
F_{t}
is non-zero on
S^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}
for all
t\in[0,1]
. To see this, recall that
c(D^{\prime})\subset V
, hence
F_{t}(u+v)=0
implies that
l(u)=0
. It follows that
u=0
and
|v|=1
. But
f|_{S^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}}(v)=f|_{S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}}(v)
, which does not vanish. Thus the classes
[\psi_{f,V}]
and
[\psi_{f,W}]
are equal.

Lemma 2.18
.

Suppose
f_{t}=l+c_{t}:[0,1]\to\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
is a compact homotopy with
c_{0}(D^{\prime})\cup c_{1}(D^{\prime})\subset V
for some finite dimensional subrepresentation
V\subset\Ucal
that surjects onto
\coker l
. Then
\psi_{f_{0},V}
and
\psi_{f_{1},V}
are homotopic as maps of pairs.

Proof 2.19
.

This follows immediately from Definition
2.15
since
f_{t}|_{S^{\prime}}
is non-vanishing, hence the restriction

\displaystyle(f_{t})|_{D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}}:(D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime})\to(V,V\setminus\{0\})

is a map of pairs for all
t
with
f_{0}=\psi_{f_{0},V}
to
f_{1}=\psi_{f_{1},V}
.

Not all elements
f=l+c\in\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
are nice enough to have
c(D^{\prime})
contained in a finite dimensional subrepresentation, however it is true that every compact homotopy class has such a representative.

Lemma 2.20
.

For any
f\in\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
|f(h)|>\delta
for all
h\in S^{\prime}
.

Proof 2.21
.

Fix
b\in B
and suppose that there is a sequence
h_{n}\in S_{b}^{\prime}
with
|f(h_{n})|\to 0
. By the weak compactness of
S_{b}^{\prime}
, after passing to a subsequence it can be assumed that
h_{n}\to h
weakly for some
h\in H_{b}^{\prime}
. By the compactness of
c
, after passing to a further subsequence it can be assumed that
c(h_{n})\to a
strongly for some
a\in\Ucal
. Now
l(h_{n})=f(h_{n})-c(h_{n})\to-a
strongly. Since
l_{b}
is Fredholm, its image is closed and
a=l(v)
for some
v\in(\ker l_{b})^{\perp}
. Write
h_{n}=x_{n}+y_{n}
for
x_{n}\in\ker l_{b}
and
y_{n}\in(\ker l_{b})^{\perp}
. Now
l(h_{n})=l(y_{n})\to-l(v)
. Since
l_{b}
is an isomorphism from
(\ker l_{b})^{\perp}
onto its image, it follows that
y_{n}\to-v
. Further
x_{n}=h_{n}-y_{n}\to h+v
weakly, but
\ker l_{b}
is finite dimensional so
x_{n}\to h+v
strongly as well. Thus
h_{n}\to h
strongly and
h\in S_{b}^{\prime}
since
S_{b}^{\prime}
is closed. However
f(h_{n})\to 0
implies that
f(h)=0
, contradicting the assumption that
f|_{S_{b}^{\prime}}\neq 0
. Since
B
is compact, such a delta can be chosen simultaneously over all fibres.

Remark 2.22
.

In fact, suppose that
f:H^{\prime}\to H
is a bounded Fredholm map with
f\inv(0)\cap S^{\prime}=\emptyset
. The above argument can be extended to show that there is a
\delta>0
with
|f(h)|>\delta
for every
h\in\overline{H^{\prime}-D^{\prime}}
. First choose a closed disk
E^{\prime}
such that
|f(h)|\geq 1
for all
h\notin E^{\prime}
, which we can assume contains
D^{\prime}
. Now the argument in the lemma easily extends to the closed, bounded set
\overline{E^{\prime}-D^{\prime}}
.

Corollary 2.23
.

Every element
f=l+c_{0}\in\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
is compactly homotopic to a map
g=l+c_{1}\in\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H)
with
c_{1}(D^{\prime})
contained in a finite dimensional subrepresentation.

Proof 2.24
.

From Lemma
2.20
, choose
\delta>0
such that
|f(h)|>\delta
for all
h\in S^{\prime}
. Let
\eps=\frac{\delta}{2}
. Since
D^{\prime}
is bounded and
c
is compact, the closure of
c(D^{\prime})
can be covered by finitely many balls of radius
\eps
with centers

. Let
V
be a finite dimensional subrepresentation that contains
\spann\{v_{i}\}
and surjects onto
\coker l
. Set

and let
g=l+p_{V}c
. By construction,

for all

. Define a homotopy for

by

\displaystyle F_{t}=l+(1-t)c+tp_{V}c.

Notice that for

\displaystyle|F_{t}(h)|

\displaystyle=|l(h)+c(h)-t(1-p_{V})c(h)|

\displaystyle\geq|f(h)|-t|(1-p_{V})c(h)|

\displaystyle>\frac{\delta}{2}.

Thus
F_{t}
is a compact homotopy from
F_{0}=f
to
F_{1}=g
.

For any

, define

for some choice of

compactly homotopic to

with

a finite dimensional subrepresentation that contains

and surjects onto

. The map

identifies

with a subset of

, which is a result originally due to Schwarz
[
29
]
.

Theorem 2.25

(
[
10
]

Let

be a linear Fredholm operator and fix a closed disk bundle

. The map

\displaystyle\Psi_{D^{\prime}}:\pi_{0}(\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H))

\displaystyle\to\Pi_{l}(D^{\prime},H)

\displaystyle[f]

\displaystyle\mapsto[\psi_{f}]

Proof 2.26
.

Lemma
2.16
and
2.18
show that

and

are elements of

with

. After applying
\Sigma
if necessary, we can assume that there is a compact homotopy

for

. To show that

2.3.
Equivalence

Let
f=l+c:H^{\prime}\to H
be a bounded Fredholm map and
V\subset\Ucal
an admissible subrepresentation with
V^{\prime}=l\inv(V)
. Recall that the Bauer-Furuta finite dimensional approximation
\vphi_{f}
is given by

\displaystyle\vphi_{f}=\rho_{V}f|_{S_{V^{\prime}}}:(S_{V^{\prime}},B_{\infty})\to(S_{V},\infty)

This maps factors through the Thom space
TV^{\prime}
. Let
\Pcal_{l}(H^{\prime},H)
denote the set of equivariant bounded Fredholm maps
f:H^{\prime}\to H
relative to
l
. Equip
\Pcal_{l}(H^{\prime},H)
with the topology induced by the uniform metric on
S_{H}
. Bauer-Furuta approximation defines a map

\displaystyle\Phi:\Pcal_{l}(H^{\prime},H)

\displaystyle\to\pi_{G,\Ucal}^{0}(B;\ind l)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto[\vphi_{f}].

Alternatively, let
D^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime}
be a closed disk bundle with bounding sphere bundle
S^{\prime}
such that
f\inv(0)\subset D^{\prime}
and
f\inv(0)\cap S^{\prime}=\emptyset
, which is guaranteed to exist since
f
is bounded. Recall that
p_{V}:H\to V
is the orthogonal projection and assume for now that
p_{V}f
does not vanish on
S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}
. Then the Schwarz approximation of
f
is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{f}

\displaystyle=p_{V}f|_{D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}}:(D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime},S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime})\to(S_{V},S_{V}\setminus\{0\}).

Schwarz approximation defines another map

\displaystyle\Psi_{D^{\prime}}:\pi_{0}(\Ccal_{l}(D^{\prime},H))

\displaystyle\to\Pi_{l}(D^{\prime},H)

(10)

\displaystyle[f]

\displaystyle\mapsto[\psi_{f}]

We will leverage the properties of Schwarz approximation to prove that
\Phi
descends to a well defined map from
\pi_{0}(\Pcal_{l}(H^{\prime},H))
to
\pi_{G,\Ucal}^{0}(B;\ind l)
and that this map is a bijection. At a surface level, it looks as if Schwarz approximation depends on the appropriately chosen disk bundle

. However, enlarging
D^{\prime}
does not change the Schwarz approximation of
f
by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.27
.

Two elements
f_{0},f_{1}\in\Pcal_{l}(H^{\prime},H)
are homotopic through bounded Fredholm maps if and only if they are compactly homotopic on some disk bundle
D^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime}
that contains
f_{0}\inv(0)\cup f_{1}\inv(0)
.

Proof 2.28
.

Suppose
f_{t}:[0,1]\to\Pcal_{l}(H^{\prime},H)
is a homotopy so that
f_{t}
is a bounded Fredholm map for each
t\in[0,1]
. Compactness of the unit interval and continuity of the homotopy guarantees the existence of a disk
D^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime}
such that
f_{t}\inv(0)\subset D^{\prime}
and
f_{t}\inv(0)\cap S^{\prime}=\emptyset
for all
t\in[0,1]
. Thus
f_{0}|_{D^{\prime}}
and
f_{1}|_{D^{\prime}}
are compactly homotopic.

Suppose instead that there is a disk bundle
D^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime}
of radius
R^{\prime}
on which
f_{0}|_{D^{\prime}}
and
f_{1}|_{D^{\prime}}
are compactly homotopic. Let
F_{t}:D^{\prime}\to H
be such a homotopy with
F_{t}\inv(0)\cap S^{\prime}=\emptyset
for all

. For any

, let
s=\frac{R^{\prime}}{|x|}x\in S^{\prime}
and extend
F_{t}
on
\overline{H^{\prime}-D^{\prime}}
by

\displaystyle F_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{|x|}{R^{\prime}}F_{t}(s).

Now for each
t\in[0,1]
,
F_{t}:H^{\prime}\to H
is Fredholm and since

, Lemma
2.20
guarantees that

is bounded. Hence
[F_{0}]=[F_{1}]
as elements of
\pi_{0}(\Pcal_{l}(H^{\prime},H))
. We claim that
f_{0}
is homotopic to
F_{0}
through bounded Fredholm maps. Such a homotopy

is given by
h_{t}|_{D^{\prime}}=f_{0}|_{D^{\prime}}
and, for

,

\displaystyle h_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|x|}{R^{\prime}}\right)^{t}f_{0}\left(\left(\frac{|x|}{R^{\prime}}\right)^{-t}x\right).

Similarly,
[F_{1}]=[f_{1}]
in
\pi_{0}(\Pcal_{l}(H^{\prime},H))
and the result follows.

To simplify notation, set

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{-}

\displaystyle=D^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{+}

\displaystyle=\overline{S_{V^{\prime}}-D^{\prime}_{-}}

\displaystyle S_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=S^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}.

That is,

are the two hemispheres of
S_{V^{\prime}}
with
S^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle\phi_{f}

\displaystyle=\rho_{V}f|_{S_{V^{\prime}}}:(S_{V^{\prime}},D^{\prime}_{+})\to(S_{V},S_{V}\setminus\{0\}).

This definition of
\phi_{f}
assumes that
\rho_{V}f
does not vanish on

. The following lemma shows that the finite dimensional subrepresentation

can be chosen to simultaneously make
\vphi_{f},\psi_{f}
and

maps of pairs.

Lemma 2.29
.

Let
f=l+c:H^{\prime}\to H
be a bounded Fredholm map and fix a disk bundle
D^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime}
such that
f\inv(0)\subset D^{\prime}
and
f\inv(0)\cap S^{\prime}=\emptyset
. There exists a finite dimensional subrepresentation

(1)

is an admissible subrepresentation as in Definition

(2)

is non-vanishing on

,

(3)

\rho_{V}f|_{S_{V^{\prime}}}
is non-vanishing on
D^{\prime}_{+}
.

These properties translate to any finite dimensional subrepresentation

.

Proof 2.30
.

Since
f
is bounded, we can assume that
f\inv(D)\subset D^{\prime}
where

is the closed unit disk. As explained in Remark

, choose a
\delta>0
such that
|f(h)|>\delta
for

. Let
\eps=\min\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{\delta}{2}\}
. Cover the closure of

by finitely many
\eps

v_{1},...,v_{n}
and set
V=\spann\{v_{i}\}
. As seen before, we can enlarge

to be a subrepresentation that surjects onto

. Now

\displaystyle=\pi^{b^{+}}_{\bT^{n},\Ucal}(\Jcal,\ind_{\Jcal}D).

Now suppose that

with necks of length

Moreover, let

(Families Permutation Theorem)

\displaystyle[\mu_{E}]=[\mu_{E^{\tau}}]

.

Remark 3.4
.

In the construction of

In

4.
Monopoles on the neck

To prove the permutation theorem it is enough to show that
\mu_{E}
is homotopic to
V\inv\mu_{E^{\tau}}V
through compact perturbations of
l
(see Corollary
2.33
). Such a homotopy is constructed in three stages, and at each stage it is important to check that the boundedness conditions outlined in Definition
2.15
are satisfied. This is accomplished using techniques from the theory of Sobolev spaces, elliptic operators and monopoles on a cylinder with a varying neck length.

4.1.
Sobolev estimates

Two fundamental theorems in the theory of Sobolev spaces are the Sobolev embedding theorem
[
11
]
and the Sobolev multiplication theorem
[
30
]
. These theorems give estimates that relate different Sobolev norms on a spinor-form pair
(\psi,a)
on
X
. For two neck lengths
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, we require estimates that apply to spinor-form pairs on both
X(L_{1})
and
X(L_{2})
. The following results achieve this goal in the situations necessary for Theorem
3.3
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
8
]
Proposition 3.1)
.

Let
k
and
p
be non-negative integers such that
k-\frac{4}{p}>0
. There is a constant
C_{S}
such that, for any
L\geq 2
,

\displaystyle|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}}\leq C_{S}\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}_{k}}

for any
L^{p}_{k}
-pair
(\psi,a)
on
X(L)
.

Proof 4.2
.

Fix a neck length
L\geq 2
. For each
x\in X
let
\delta_{x}:X\to[0,1]
be a smooth bump function in a small neighbourhood of
x
. Let
X_{0}=X(2)
and use the Sobolev embedding
L^{p}_{k}(X_{0},W^{+}\oplus T^{*}X_{0})\subset C^{0}(X_{0},W^{+}\oplus T^{*}X_{0})

[
30
, Theorem B.2]
to choose a constant
C_{1}
with

\displaystyle|(\psi^{\prime},a^{\prime})|_{C^{0}(X_{0})}\leq C_{1}\|(\psi^{\prime},a^{\prime})\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X_{0})}

for any
L^{p}_{k}
-pair
(\psi^{\prime},a^{\prime})
on
X_{0}
. Note that such a constant exists since
k-\frac{4}{p}>0
. For any spinor
\psi
on
X
,
\delta_{x}\psi
can be identified as a spinor on
X_{0}
. The same is true for
\delta_{x}a
for a one-form
a
on
X
. Now for each
x\in X
,

\displaystyle|(\delta_{x}\psi,\delta_{x}a)|_{C^{0}(X)}\leq C_{1}\|(\delta_{x}\psi,\delta_{x}a)\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X)}.

Since
\delta_{x}
is smooth and defined locally, it has bounded
C_{k}
norm which is independent of
L
. Thus there exists a constant
C_{2}
such that for all
x\in X
,

\displaystyle\|(\delta_{x}\psi,\delta_{x}a)\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X)}\leq C_{2}\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}(X)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in X}|(\delta_{x}\psi,\delta_{x}a)|_{C^{0}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sup_{x\in X}\|(\delta_{x}\psi,\delta_{x}a)\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X)}.

Setting
C_{S}=C_{1}C_{2}
gives the result.

The next lemma demonstrates that Sobolev multiplication bounds only depend linearly on the length of the neck.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
be integers. There is a constant
C_{SM}
such that, for any neck length
L\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\|a\cdot\psi\|_{L^{p}_{k}}\leq C_{SM}L\|a\|_{L^{2p}_{k}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2p}_{k}}

for any
L^{2p}_{k}
-pair
(\psi,a)
on
X(L)
.

Proof 4.4
.

For notational simplicity, assume that
X
is connected. Recall that
M^{\pm}
denotes the two halves of
M=\overline{X-N(L-1)}
with tubular ends of the form

\displaystyle N(L)^{-}\cap M

\displaystyle=S^{3}\times[-L,-L+1]

\displaystyle N(L)^{+}\cap M

\displaystyle=S^{3}\times[L-1,L].

We will cut
N(L)
into pieces that can be identified on
X_{0}=X(2)
, then use Sobolev multiplication on
X_{0}
. Let
\phi:X\to[0,1]
be a smooth function such that
\phi\equiv 1
on
X-N(L-\frac{5}{4})
and
\phi\equiv 0
on
N(L-2)
. Define a function
\chi:\R\to[0,1]
such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
and
\chi\equiv 0
outside
[-\frac{1}{4},\frac{5}{4}]
. Let
\chi_{i}
be
\chi
shifted by
i
so that
\chi_{i}\equiv 1
on
[i,i+1]
and
\chi_{i}\equiv 0
outside
[i-\frac{1}{4},i+\frac{5}{4}]
. Let
m=\floor{L-\frac{5}{4}}
. For
i
an integer with
-(m+1)\leq i\leq m
, extend
\chi_{i}
to
N(L)=S^{3}\times[-L,L]
by projection onto the interval factor. Let

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\phi^{2}+\sum_{i=-(m+1)}^{m}\chi_{i}^{2}}.

Notice that
\vphi
is positive on
X
. Let
\vphi_{i}=\frac{\chi_{i}}{\vphi}
for
-(m+1)\leq i\leq m
with
\vphi_{m+1}=\frac{\phi}{\vphi}
. By construction,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=-(m+1)}^{m+1}\vphi_{i}^{2}=1.

For each
-(m+1)\leq i\leq m+1
, set
\psi_{i}=\vphi_{i}\psi
and
a_{i}=\vphi_{i}a
. Both
\psi_{i}
and
a_{i}
can be identified as sections on
X_{0}
. For
-(m+1)\leq i\leq m
, this is accomplished by shifting the interval
[i-\frac{1}{4},i+\frac{5}{4}]
to
[-\frac{1}{4},\frac{5}{4}]
. We can assume that the
C^{k}
norm of
\vphi_{i}
is bounded, which implies that there exists a constant
C_{1}
, independent of
L
, such that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{i}\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|\psi\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X)}

(23)

\displaystyle\|a_{i}\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|a\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X)}.

For the purposes of elliptic bootstrapping, the
L^{p}_{k}
-Sobolev norm on
X_{0}
is defined as

\displaystyle\|(\psi_{i},a_{i})\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|(\Dcal^{j}\psi_{i},(d^{*}+d^{+})^{j}a_{i})\|_{L^{p}(X_{0})}.

Equivalently, the
L^{p}_{k}
-norm on
X_{0}
can instead be defined by differentiating spinors with the
\spinc
connection
\nabla_{A_{0}}
and forms with the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
. Thus there are constants

such that

\displaystyle c\|(\psi_{i},a_{i})\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X_{0})}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|(\nabla_{A_{0}}^{j}\psi_{i},\nabla^{j}a_{i})\|_{L^{p}(X_{0})}\leq C\|(\psi_{i},a_{i})\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X_{0})}.

Calculating with repeated applications of the Leibniz rule gives

\displaystyle\|a_{i}\cdot\psi_{i}\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|\nabla_{A}^{j}(a_{i}\cdot\psi_{i})\|_{L^{p}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{j}K_{j,l}\|\Gamma(\nabla^{l}a_{i})\cdot(\nabla_{A}^{j-l}\psi_{i})\|_{L^{p}(X_{0})}

for some non-negative constants
K_{j,l}
. Here
\Gamma(\nabla^{l}a_{i})\in\End(W)
is the matrix corresponding to spinor multiplication by the
(l+1)
-form
\nabla^{l}a_{i}
. The operator norm of
\Gamma(\nabla^{l}a_{i})
is equal to
|\nabla^{l}a_{i}|
, hence applying Sobolev multiplication
[
30
, Lemma B.3]
it follows that

(24)

\displaystyle\|a_{i}\cdot\psi_{i}\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|a_{i}\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X_{0})}\|\psi_{i}\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X_{0})}

for some constant
C_{2}
. This constant depends on
c
,
K_{j,l}
and Sobolev multiplication on
X_{0}
, hence is independent of
L
. Combining (
4.4
) and (
24
) produces the result.

\displaystyle\|a\cdot\psi\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=-m-1}^{m+1}\|a_{i}\cdot\psi_{i}\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\sum_{i=-m-1}^{m+1}\|a_{i}\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X_{0})}\|\psi_{i}\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}C_{1}^{2}\sum_{i=-m-1}^{m+1}\|a\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{SM}L\|a\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2p}_{k}(X)}.

The same argument applied to
\sigma(\psi)
instead gives the following result.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
k\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
be integers. There is a constant
C_{\sigma}
such that, for any neck length

,

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)\|_{L^{p}_{k}}\leq C_{\sigma}L\|\psi\|_{L^{2p}_{k}}^{2}

for and
\psi\in L^{2p}_{k}(X(L),W^{+})
.

4.2.
Elliptic inequality

To analyse the properties of monopoles on a neck of varying length, it is useful to apply Yang Mills theory on cylinders as in Chapter 2 of
[
15
]
. Fix a neck length
L
with
X=X(L)
. For notational simplicity, assume that
X
only has one connected component. Recall that
M^{+}
and
M^{-}
are the two halves of
M=\overline{X-N(L-1)}
. Attach infinite tubes to
M^{+}
and
M^{-}
to get manifolds with tubular ends
Y^{\pm}
of the form

\displaystyle Y^{-}

\displaystyle=M^{-}\cup S^{3}\times[-L+1,\infty)

\displaystyle Y^{+}

\displaystyle=S^{3}\times(-\infty,L-1]\cup M^{+}.

One-forms on the tubular component of
Y^{\pm}
can be analysed by studying forms on the product
S^{3}\times\R
. Let
\pi:S^{3}\times\R\to S^{3}
be projection onto the
S^{3}
factor. All elements of
\Omega^{1}(S^{3}\times\R)
are of the form
\omega_{t}+fdt
for
\omega_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(S^{3})
a smooth family of one-forms on
S^{3}
and
f:S^{3}\times\R\to\R
a smooth function. That is, we can identify

\displaystyle\Omega^{1}(S^{3}\times\R)

\displaystyle=\Cinf(S^{3}\times\R,\R\oplus\pi^{*}T^{*}S^{3}).

Similarly, self-dual 2-forms
\Omega_{+}^{2}(S^{3}\times\R)
can be identified with time-dependent 1-forms
\xi\in C^{\infty}(S^{3}\times\R,\pi^{*}T^{*}S^{3})
by the isomorphism

\displaystyle\xi\mapsto\xi\wedge dt+*_{3}\xi.

Here
*_{3}
is the hodge star operator on
S^{3}
. Thus we can interpret the elliptic operator
d^{*}+d^{+}:\Omega^{1}(S^{3}\times\R)\to\Omega^{0}(S^{3}\times\R)\oplus\Omega^{2}_{+}(S^{3}\times\R)
as

(25)

\displaystyle d^{*}+d^{+}:C^{\infty}(S^{3}\times\R,\R\oplus\pi^{*}T^{*}S^{3})\to C^{\infty}(S^{3}\times\R,\R\oplus\pi^{*}T^{*}S^{3}).

Consider the operator
\Lcal:\Omega^{0}(S^{3})\oplus\Omega^{1}(S^{3})\to\Omega^{0}(S^{3})\oplus\Omega^{1}(S^{3})
defined by

(26)

\displaystyle\Lcal

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&d^{*}\\
d&*d\end{pmatrix}.

This is a self-adjoint elliptic operator that squares to the Laplacian
\Lcal^{2}=dd^{*}+d^{*}d
on
\Omega^{0}(S^{3})\oplus\Omega^{1}(S^{3})
. It can be shown by direct calculation that under the identification (
25
),

\displaystyle d^{*}+d^{+}=\frac{\p}{\p t}+\Lcal

where
\frac{\p}{\p t}
is the derivative in the
\R
direction.

Since the tubular ends of
Y
are not compact, solutions to the operator
\frac{\p}{\p t}+\Lcal
will be studied in weighted Sobolev spaces. Weighted Sobolev spaces consist of functions that have a controlled exponential increase towards the tubular ends. To define them, fix a parameter
\alpha<0
and let
f^{-}_{\alpha}
be a smooth function on
S^{3}\times[-L,\infty)
that is zero on
S^{3}\times[-L,-L+2]
and decreases with slope
\alpha
on
S^{3}\times[-L+3,\infty)
. Similarly, define
f^{+}_{\alpha}
on
S^{3}\times(-\infty,L]
to be zero on
S^{3}\times[L-2,L]
and decrease with slope
\alpha
on
S^{3}\times(-\infty,L-3]
. Since
\alpha<0
, both functions
f^{\pm}_{\alpha}
are non-positive. Define the weighted Sobolev space
L^{p,\alpha}_{k}(Y^{\pm})
to be the completion of
L^{p}(Y^{\pm})
with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{p,\alpha}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\|\exp(f^{\pm}_{\alpha})g\|_{L^{p}_{k}}.

Note that
\exp(f^{\pm}_{\alpha})
is decreasing exponentially towards the infinite end of
Y^{\pm}
. Moreover, the spaces
L^{p,\alpha}_{k}(Y^{\pm})
are independent of the original neck length
L
.

It is shown in
[
15
]
that
d^{*}+d^{+}=\frac{\p}{\p t}+\Lcal
is a linear Fredholm operator on
L^{p,\alpha}_{1}
-forms if
\alpha
is not in the spectrum of
L
. Since
L
is self-adjoint and elliptic it has discrete spectrum away from infinity, so choose
\alpha<0
to be greater than the maximal negative eigenvalue of
L
. As in (
15
), define a harmonic projection map
\pr^{\pm}:\Omega^{1}(Y^{\pm})\to\Omega^{1}(Y^{\pm})
by integrating a homology basis of curves away from the neck. The image of
\pr^{\pm}
is
H^{1}(X;\R)
, identified as the space of harmonic forms
\Hcal^{1}(Y^{\pm})\subset\Omega^{1}(Y^{\pm})
. Fix
p>4
so that
L^{p}_{1}(Y^{\pm},T^{*}Y)\subset C^{0}(Y^{\pm},T^{*}Y)
by Sobolev embedding and extend
\pr^{\pm}
continuously to a map on
L^{p,\alpha}_{1}
forms. The operator

\displaystyle d^{*}+d^{+}:L^{p,\alpha}_{1}(Y^{\pm},T^{*}Y^{\pm})\to L^{p,\alpha}(Y^{\pm},\R\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{+}T^{*}Y^{\pm})

is Fredholm with kernel
\Hcal^{1}(Y^{\pm})
and cokernel
H^{0}(Y^{\pm};\R)\oplus H^{2}_{+}(Y^{\pm};\R)
. Let
H^{\pm}=\ker\pr^{\pm}
, which is a complement of
\ker(d^{*}+d^{+})
. Thus the restriction of
d^{*}+d^{+}
to
H^{\pm}
is a linear bijection onto the closed
L^{p,\alpha}
-image of
d^{*}+d^{+}
. The bounded inverse theorem guarantees that there are constants
C^{\pm}>0
such that for
b\in L^{p,\alpha}_{1}(Y^{\pm},T^{*}Y^{\pm})
,

(27)

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L^{p,\alpha}_{1}}\leq C^{\pm}\left(\|(d^{*}+d^{+})b\|_{L^{p,\alpha}}+\|\pr^{\pm}(b)\|\right).

Importantly, the constants
C^{\pm}
are independent of the neck length
L
. That is, for another choice of neck length
L^{\prime}
and manifolds with tubular ends
(Y^{\prime})^{\pm}
, there is an isometry from
L^{p,\alpha}_{k}(Y^{\pm},T^{*}Y^{\pm})
to
L^{p,\alpha}_{k}((Y^{\prime})^{\pm},T^{*}(Y^{\prime})^{\pm})
defined by shifting the interval component by
L^{\prime}-L
.

To analyse the behaviour of forms away from the middle of the neck, define smooth cut-off functions
\beta^{\pm}:X\to[0,1]
which vanish on
X^{\mp}\cup N(2)
and are equal to
1
on
M^{\pm}
. To ensure such
\beta
exist, we will assume that
L\geq 3
.

Lemma 4.6

(
[
8
]
Proposition 3.1)
.

Let
\beta^{\pm}
be cutaway functions as described above and fix

. There exists a constant
C
such that, for any neck-length
L>3
,

\displaystyle|a|_{C^{0}(M)}\leq C\left(\|(d^{*}+d^{+})\beta^{+}a\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\|(d^{*}+d^{+})\beta^{-}a\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\|\pr(a)\|\right)

for any
L^{p}_{1}
-form

on
X(L)
.

Proof 4.7
.

The Sobolev embedding
L^{p}_{1}(X,T^{*}X)\subset C^{0}(X,T^{*}X)
guarantees the existence of a constant
C_{S}
such that

(28)

\displaystyle|a|_{C^{0}(X)}\leq C_{S}\|a\|_{L^{p}_{1}(X)}.

for all
a\in L^{p}_{1}(X,T^{*}X)
. Lemma
4.1
ensures that

can be chosen independently of
L
. To apply the elliptic bound, let
b_{\pm}=\beta^{\pm}a
and notice that
e^{f^{\pm}_{\alpha}}b_{\pm}=a
on
M^{\pm}
.

\displaystyle|a|_{C^{0}(M^{\pm})}

\displaystyle=|e^{f^{\pm}_{\alpha}}b_{\pm}|_{C^{0}(M^{\pm})}

\displaystyle\leq|e^{f^{\pm}_{\alpha}}b_{\pm}|_{C^{0}(Y^{\pm})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}\|e^{f^{\pm}_{\alpha}}b_{\pm}\|_{L^{p}_{1}(Y^{\pm})}

(29)

\displaystyle=C_{S}\|b_{\pm}\|_{L^{p,\alpha}_{1}(Y^{\pm})}

Note that

is compactly supported in

, so the Sobolev bound (

) applies to
|e^{f^{\pm}_{\alpha}}b_{\pm}|_{C^{0}(Y^{\pm})}
. Now (

) gives

\displaystyle|a|_{C^{0}(M^{\pm})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}C^{\pm}\left(\|(d^{*}+d^{+})b_{\pm}\|_{L^{p,\alpha}(Y^{\pm})}+\|\pr(b_{\pm})\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|(d^{*}+d^{+})b_{+}\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\|(d^{*}+d^{+})b_{-}\|_{L^{p}(X)}+(\|\pr(b_{+})\|+\|\pr(b_{-})\|)\right).

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|(d^{*}+d^{+})b_{+}\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\|(d^{*}+d^{+})b_{-}\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\|\pr(a)\|\right).

4.3.
Elliptic bootstrapping

For a fixed connection
A\in\Jcal(X)
on
X(L)
, an elliptic bootstrapping argument can be used to produce a polynomial
L^{2}_{k}
-bound on a monopole
(\psi,a)
of the form

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\leq C_{B}(1+|(\psi,a)|)^{d}_{\Co}.

The constant
C_{B}
depends on the curvature of
A
and the length of the neck
L
. To cooperate with neck stretching, we show that
C_{B}
only increases polynomially in
L
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
A\in\Jcal_{X}
be a connection on
X(L)
and fix an integer
k\geq 2
. There are positive constants
C_{B}
and
d
such that, for any
L\geq 2
, if
(\psi,a)
is an
L^{2}_{k}
-pair with

\displaystyle D_{A}\psi

\displaystyle=-ia\cdot\psi

(32)

\displaystyle d^{+}a

\displaystyle=iF^{+}_{A}-i\sigma(\psi),

then

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\leq C_{B}L^{d}(1+|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}})^{d}.

Proof 4.12
.

Use the first order differential operators
D_{A}
and
d^{+}
to define the
L^{p}_{k}
-norm so that

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{i}}-\|(\psi,a)\|^{p}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\|(D_{A}\psi,d^{+}a)\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{i-1}}.

For any
0\leq i\leq k
and
2\leq p\leq 2^{k+1}
, (
4.11
) ensures that

\displaystyle\|(D_{A}\psi,d^{+}a)\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{i-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|a\cdot\psi\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{i-1}}+(\|\sigma(\psi)\|_{L^{p}_{i-1}}+\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{p}_{i-1}})^{p}

By Lemma
4.3
and
4.5
, there are constants
C_{SM}
and
C_{\sigma}
independent of
L
such that

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{SM}L\|a\|_{L^{2p}_{i-1}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2p}_{i-1}}+C_{\sigma}L\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2p}_{i-1}}+\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{p}_{i-1}}+\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}}.

Since
A
is flat on the neck,
\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{p}_{i-1}}
is a constant independent of
L
. Thus there is a constant
C_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}L(\|(\psi,a)\|^{2}_{L^{2p}_{i-1}}+\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}})

for all
0\leq i\leq k
and
2\leq p\leq 2^{k+1}
. Starting with
i=k
and
p=2
, inductively applying this inequality gives a bound

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq L^{d_{1}}f(\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2}},...,\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2^{k+1}}})

for some natural number
d_{1}
and polynomial
f
, both independent of
L
. Letting
d_{2}
be the degree of
f
, there is a constant
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle|f(x_{1},...,x_{k})|\leq C_{2}(1+|x_{1}|+...+|x_{k}|)^{d_{2}}.

Since
\vol(X(L))
increases linearly with
L
, there is a bound

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\vol(X(L))^{\frac{1}{p}}|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}L|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}}.

Here
C_{3}
is a constant independent of
L
and
p
. Letting
d=d_{1}+d_{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}L^{d_{1}}(1+\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2}}+...+\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2^{k+1}}})^{d_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}L^{d_{1}}L^{d_{2}}(1+C_{3}|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}}+...+C_{3}|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}})^{d_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}L^{d}(1+|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}})^{d}

for some constant
C_{B}
independent of
L
.

Remark 4.13
.

Assume that there is a smooth function
\rho:X\to\R
and constant
C
such that the pair
(\psi,a)
instead satisfies

\displaystyle D_{A}\psi

\displaystyle=-i\rho a\cdot\psi

\displaystyle\|d^{+}a\|_{L^{p}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\sigma(\psi)\|_{L^{p}_{i}}+\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{p}_{i}})

\displaystyle\|\rho a\|_{L^{p}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a\|_{L^{p}_{i}}

for all
0\leq i\leq k
,

. The same argument can be repeated, the only difference being that the constant

now depends on

. Thus there still exists positive constants

and

such that

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\leq C_{B}L^{d}(1+|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}})^{d}.

These constants depend on

, but are independent of

so long as

is.

4.4.
Exponential decay

Since
X(L)
is compact, there are
L^{p}
-bounds on spinors and one-forms of the form

(33)

\displaystyle\|(\psi,a)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}|(\psi,a)|_{C^{0}}

with
C_{p}=\vol(X(L))^{\frac{1}{p}}
. This constant
C_{p}
grows linearly with the length of the neck. However, we will demonstrate that monopoles decay exponentially towards the middle of the neck, which will counteract this and other polynomial growth. The following work is adapted from Chapter 3 of
[
15
]
.

Let
E\to S^{3}
be a vector bundle over
S^{3}
, equipped with a metric
g_{E}
and compatible connection
\nabla_{E}
. For notational simplicity, we will assume that
N(L)=S^{3}\times[-L,L]
has one connected component. Let
\pi:N(L)\to S^{3}
be projection onto the
S^{3}
component. Fix
k>2
and let
A:\Cinf(S^{3},E)\to\Cinf(S^{3},E)
be a first order, self-adjoint, elliptic pseudo-differential operator on

. By spectral theory of elliptic operators, there is an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n=-N}^{\infty}\subset L^{2}(S^{3},E)
for

with discrete real eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}
. Label the eigenvalues so that the non-zero eigenvalues have a positive index and the zero eigenvalues (of multiplicity
N+1
) have a non-positive index. Thus there is a
\delta>0
such that

for all
n\geq 1
. Also ensure that the labeling is chosen so that
|\lambda_{n}|\geq|\lambda_{m}|
when

.

Let
f_{0}\in C^{\infty}(S^{3},E)
be a smooth section with eigen decomposition
f_{0}=\sum_{n}f^{n}_{0}\phi_{n}
convergent in
L^{2}
for

is also smooth and its eigen decomposition is

since

is self-adjoint. A smooth section

of

also has a decomposition

for some functions

f_{t}
implies the smoothness of the component functions

by the Leibniz integral rule.

Define a pseudo-differential operator by

\displaystyle D:C^{\infty}(N(L),\pi^{*}E)

\displaystyle\to C^{\infty}(N(L),\pi^{*}E)

\displaystyle D=\frac{\p}{\p t}

\displaystyle+A.

Assume that

is elliptic and extend

to an operator on

\displaystyle\sup_{N(r)}|f|\leq C_{\delta}e^{-\delta(L-2r)}\sup_{N(L)}|f|.

and

are positive constants independent of

and

.

.

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
A:C^{\infty}(S^{3},E)\to C^{\infty}(S^{3},E)
be an elliptic, self-adjoint, pseudo-differential operator of positive order. Let
0<|\lambda_{1}|\leq|\lambda_{2}|\leq...
denote the non-zero eigenvalues of
A
, ordered by magnitude. There exists a constant
C
such that, for any
r\geq 1
,

(46)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)e^{r|\lambda_{n}|}|a_{n}|\right)^{2}\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh(4r|\lambda_{n}|)}{|\lambda_{n}|}|a_{n}|^{2}

for any real number sequence
\{a_{n}\}
.

Proof 4.21
.

First, apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)e^{r|\lambda_{n}|}|a_{n}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)\sqrt{|\lambda_{n}|}e^{r|\lambda_{n}|}}{\sqrt{\sinh(4r|\lambda_{n}|)}}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sinh(4r|\lambda_{n}|)}}{\sqrt{|\lambda_{n}|}}|a_{n}|\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)^{2}|\lambda_{n}|e^{2r|\lambda_{n}|}}{\sinh(4r|\lambda_{n}|)}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh(4r|\lambda_{n}|)}{|\lambda_{n}|}|a_{n}|^{2}\right)

It suffices to bound
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)^{2}|\lambda_{n}|e^{2r|\lambda_{n}|}}{\sinh(4r|\lambda_{n}|)}
. Fix
0<\delta<|\lambda_{1}|
. The function
\frac{e^{4x}}{\sinh(4x)}
is bounded on
[\delta,\infty)
, therefore there is a constant
C_{1}
such that, for all

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2x}}{\sinh(4x)}\leq C_{1}e^{-2x}

Apply this to

to produce

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2r|\lambda_{n}|}(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)^{2}|\lambda_{n}|}{\sinh(4r|\lambda_{n}|)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{1}(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)^{2}|\lambda_{n}|e^{-2r|\lambda_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{1}(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)^{2}|\lambda_{n}|e^{-2|\lambda_{n}|}.

Similarly, there exists a constant
C_{2}
such that
x(x^{2}+1)^{2}e^{-x}\leq C_{2}
for all

. It follows that

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{1}(\lambda_{n}^{2}+1)^{2}|\lambda_{n}|e^{-2|\lambda_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{1}C_{2}e^{-|\lambda_{n}|}.

Since
A
is elliptic and self-adjoint, Weyl’s law
[
18
, Lemma 1.6.3]
implies that there exists a constant
C_{3}
and an exponent

such that

for large enough

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-n^{a}}<\infty.

This follows from the integral test. Let

so that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x^{\alpha}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}e^{-u}du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle<\infty.

Therefore
C=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{1}C_{2}e^{-|\lambda_{n}|}
is a suitable constant.

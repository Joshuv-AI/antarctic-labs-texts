The Global, Regional, and National Burden of Postpartum Hemorrhage, 1990–2040: A Systematic Analysis Based on the Global Burden of Disease Study
Authors: Jianhua Sun, Yuanyuan Wang, Huafang Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPostpartum hemorrhage (PPH) remains one of the leading global causes of maternal morbidity and mortality. To date, however, comprehensive long-term epidemiological trends and standardized future projections of PPH burden stratified by socioeconomic level have not been sufficiently characterized. This study aimed to systematically analyze the global, regional, and socioeconomic-stratified burden of PPH from 1990 to 2021, and forecast epidemiological trends through 2040, to provide evidence for targeted global maternal health interventions.MethodsBased on publicly available aggregated data from the 2021 Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD 2021), this secondary ecological analysis estimated the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR), age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR), and age-standardized disability-adjusted life-year rate (ASDR) across 204 countries and territories. We calculated temporal trend inflection points and annual percentage changes (APC) using Joinpoint regression, and applied optimized autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) models to project future trajectories of PPH burden through 2040. The slope index of inequality (SII) and concentration index (CI) were used to quantify absolute and relative socioeconomic disparities in PPH burden.ResultsGlobally, the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) of PPH decreased steadily from 495.93 per 100,000 population in 1990 to 358.57 per 100,000 population in 2021, with an estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) of −0.83%. Stratified by socio-demographic index (SDI), low-SDI regions had the highest PPH burden in 2021 (ASIR: 740.01 per 100,000 population), while high-SDI regions maintained the lowest burden (ASIR: 192.76 per 100,000 population). Exploratory ARIMA projections indicate that if current trends continue, global PPH morbidity measured by ASIR and age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR) will likely show an upward trend by 2040. This increasing trend is mainly driven by epidemiological transitions in middle-SDI regions, while global PPH mortality is projected to continue declining. However, these projections carry considerable uncertainty, especially for longer forecasting horizons, and should be interpreted as exploratory “what-if” scenarios rather than deterministic predictions. Over the past three decades, absolute socioeconomic inequalities in PPH burden have narrowed, while relative disparities increased slightly, with women aged 25–29 years identified as the highest-risk population globally.ConclusionOver the past three decades, the global burden of postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) has decreased notably. However, distinct socioeconomic and regional disparities persist: low-SDI regions still face severe mortality risks linked to PPH, while middle-SDI regions are grappling with new morbidity challenges driven by population aging and changing obstetric risk profiles. To address global maternal health inequities, stratified, context-adaptive public health strategies tailored to each region’s unique socioeconomic and epidemiological characteristics are needed. Given the inherent uncertainties of the ARIMA modeling approach, all forecast results should be interpreted with caution.

Introduction

Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is one of the most severe obstetric complications and the leading preventable cause of adverse maternal outcomes globally, representing a long-standing public health challenge worldwide.1 Clinically defined as cumulative blood loss of ≥500 mL after vaginal delivery or ≥1000 mL after cesarean section, PPH accounts for approximately 30% of all maternal deaths globally and contributes to more than 70,000 childbirth-related fatalities each year. In2,3 addition to causing acute mortality, PPH is closely linked to a range of short-term and long-term adverse sequelae, including acute renal injury, post-traumatic stress disorder, Sheehan’s syndrome, chronic anemia, and secondary infertility. These complications severely impair maternal quality of life and impose substantial long-term burdens on global healthcare systems.4–7

Recent epidemiological surveillance shows that the temporal trend of PPH burden varies across different regions of the world. While overall PPH related mortality has continued to decline globally, rising PPH morbidity has been observed in multiple middle-income regions.8 Existing global studies on PPH burden have several limitations: they generally adopt cross-sectional designs, lack sufficient stratified socioeconomic comparison, focus exclusively on mortality outcomes, and do not include standardized long-term forecasting.9 The updated GBD 2021 dataset provides unified, high-quality population-level health estimates covering 204 countries and territories, which enables comprehensive assessment of global disparities in disease burden. However, no previous study has systematically integrated Joinpoint trend inflection analysis, validated ARIMA long-term projection, and dual-index health inequality evaluation to elaborate the full-spectrum epidemiological characteristics of PPH across different SDI stratifications. Against this background, this study takes a global research perspective, focusing on the spatiotemporal evolutionary patterns and socioeconomic inequalities of PPH burden worldwide, and takes the stratified global epidemiological patterns of PPH as the core research theme of this study. This paper aims to generate comprehensive evidence-based references for developing stratified, targeted global maternal health prevention and control strategies.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Data Source

This study performed a secondary ecological analysis using publicly available aggregated population-level data from the 2021 Global Burden of Disease (GBD 2021) study, and was conducted in compliance with the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER). The GBD 2021 study systematically assessed the disease burden of 371 diseases and injuries across 204 countries, territories, and subnational regions from 1990 to 2021, by integrating over 100,000 multi-source surveillance data entries from vital registration systems, population censuses, epidemiological surveys and other data channels.10 The GBD 2021 dataset covers 204 countries and territories, which are divided into five Sociodemographic Index (SDI) quintiles: low SDI (41 countries, primarily located in sub-Saharan Africa), low-middle SDI (41 countries, covering parts of South Asia and Central America), middle SDI (41 countries, including China, parts of Latin America and Southeast Asia), high-middle SDI (41 countries, including most of Latin America, Eastern Europe, and parts of East Asia), and high SDI (40 countries, including North America, Western Europe, Australia, and Japan).

Cases of postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) were identified in line with the cause-of-death definition set by GBD 2021, corresponding to ICD-10 code O72 (postpartum hemorrhage), O72.0 (third-stage hemorrhage), O72.1 (other immediate postpartum hemorrhage), and O72.2 (delayed and secondary postpartum hemorrhage). All core indicators applied in this study, including incidence, prevalence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs), were downloaded from the fully open-access official platform of the Global Health Data Exchange (GHDx). All outcome metrics were standardized per 100,000 person-years, and all estimates are presented with 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) generated via 1000-draw Monte Carlo simulation, where UIs are defined as the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the posterior distribution.11

Consistent with the standard GBD classification scheme, all study regions were divided into five SDI strata: low SDI, low-middle SDI, middle SDI, high-middle SDI, and high SDI. SDI is a validated composite socioeconomic indicator calculated as the geometric mean of three metrics: lag-distributed per capita income, average educational attainment of adults aged 15 years and older, and the adolescent fertility rate. It reflects the overall level of social and economic development of a given population.10

Statistical Analysis

Age Stratification and Standardization

According to the age grouping criteria defined in GBD 2021, all reproductive-age women were divided into eight age groups: (15–19, 20–24, 25–29, 30–34, 35–39, 40–44, 45–49, and 50–54 years. We conducted direct age standardization using the GBD 2021 global reference population to eliminate the confounding effect of differences in population age structure was eliminated, to ensure the global comparability of all age-standardized rates.

Temporal Trend Analysis

The Joinpoint Regression Program 4.9.1.0 (National Cancer Institute, USA) was applied to identify significant temporal inflection points of PPH burden indicators between 1990 and 2021. For this 32-year study period, we set a maximum of five joinpoints, requiring at least four observational data points between any two adjacent inflection points. Monte Carlo permutation tests (4499 permutations) were adopted to test for significance at α = 0.05. We calculated the annual percentage change (APC) for each segmented period and the overall estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) to quantify short-term and long-term trend characteristics, respectively. Trend directions were strictly defined based on APC values and their corresponding 95% uncertainty intervals.

Health Inequality Analysis

Robust linear regression models were used to calculate the SII, which quantifies absolute socioeconomic disparities in health. The CI, which reflects relative health inequality, was calculated with the covariance method. Negative CI values indicate that the PPH disease burden is disproportionately concentrated in populations with lower socioeconomic status.12,13 These two indicators were applied to comprehensively evaluate the dynamic changes in global PPH related health inequities over the past three decades.

ARIMA Forecasting

All forecasting analyses were conducted using R 4.5.1, we constructed ARIMA models based on the stationary time series data of PPH indicators at the global level and across different SDI stratifications. We selected ARIMA models for their strong performance in capturing autocorrelated temporal trends, as well as their wide application in previous GBD-based forecasting studies. The primary objective of our projections is not to generate accurate point estimates for 2040, but to illustrate the expected directional trajectory of PPH burden if recent trend patterns continue. Joinpoint regression and ARIMA forecasting serve complementary functions: Joinpoint identifies historical inflection points, while ARIMA captures overall autocorrelation to produce future projections. Together, they enable a more comprehensive understanding of temporal dynamics.

We applied the Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test to verify data stationarity, and identified the optimal model parameters (p,d,q) by minimizing the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). The final validated models are ARIMA (1,1,1) for ASIR, ARIMA (2,1,0) for ASPR, and ARIMA (1,1,2) for ASMR. We confirmed model validity via out-of-sample validation, (which reserved data from 2017–2021 for verification, and all models produced a mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of less than 5%. Residual diagnostics, including the Ljung-Box test, ACF/PACF inspection, and Shapiro–Wilk normality test, confirmed that all models achieved good fit.

For all 95% forecast uncertainty intervals that contain biologically implausible negative values, we truncated the interval at zero, and this truncation is noted in the figure legends. This adjustment only applies to visualization and does not alter the point estimates. All forecast results are exploratory scenario projections based on the assumption that current trends will remain stable, and they inherently involve long-term uncertainty.

Statistical Specification

Given the exploratory ecological design of this study and the presence of multiple subgroup comparisons, we did not conduct formal multiple testing correction. All analytical conclusions were drawn based on effect sizes and 95% UIs, to avoid overinterpreting marginal statistical significance. For secondary analyses (Joinpoint, SII, CI), uncertainty intervals were propagated using 1000 draw-level estimates from GBD 2021. Each analytical step was performed independently across all 1000 draws, and 95% uncertainty intervals were calculated from the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resulting distribution.

Joinpoint analysis identified phased heterogeneous declining trends in global PPH burden indicators. The global age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) decreased rapidly from 1990 to 1994 (APC=−2.37%), followed by a slower decline between 1994–2008 (APC=−0.81%) and 2008–2015 (APC=−0.31%), and an accelerated decline after 2015 (APC=−1.51%). ASPR maintained a continuous phased decline across the full study period. From 1990 to 2021, the global age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) and age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) showed consistent, significant decreasing trends, demonstrating that the world has made substantial progress in reducing PPH related mortality and disability burden (Figure 1). ARIMA projection results revealed divergent future trajectories for PPH morbidity and mortality across global, national, and sociodemographic subgroups (Figure 2A–F, Figure 3A–F, and Figure 4A–F). SDI-Stratified Regional Burden Characteristics

PPH burden has a significant negative correlation with regional SDI levels. In 2021, low-SDI regions bore the highest PPH burden across all core indicators, with an ASIR of 740.01 per 100,000, 3.8 times that of high-SDI regions (192.76 per 100,000). The ASPR of low-SDI regions (164.27 per 100,000) was more than five times higher than that of high-SDI regions (31.19 per 100,000). Age-Specific Epidemiological Patterns

Consistent age distribution characteristics of PPH burden were observed across all SDI strata globally. Among women worldwide, those aged 25–29 years had the highest PPH incidence, prevalence, and DALY rates, followed by the 20–24 years age group and the 30–34 years age group. This age-specific pattern is primarily driven by two interacting factors: the high frequency of pregnancy exposure among populations at peak reproductive age, and the elevated per-pregnancy risk among younger primiparous women, rather than being caused by higher risk for a single pregnancy alone.

Notably, per-pregnancy risk may follow a distinct pattern: advanced maternal age is associated with higher per-pregnancy PPH risk, even though fewer older women become pregnant. Future research using pregnancy-denominator data will help disentangle exposure effects from intrinsic risk effects. All age groups saw continuous declines in PPH burden between 1990 and 2021, and the core high-risk age group remained unchanged over the 30-year study period (Figure 5). Global Socioeconomic Inequality of PPH Burden

Two inequality indicators confirmed distinct dynamic trends in global PPH related health disparities. From 1990 to 2021, the SII of PPH incidence decreased from −533 to −251, and the SII of PPH prevalence decreased from −117.85 to −60.6, indicating that absolute socioeconomic health inequalities have narrowed continuously. However, the CI for core indicators continued to shift negatively: the CI for incidence decreased from −0.23 to −0.27, and the CI for DALYs decreased from −0.55 to −0.64, indicating that relative health disparities have slightly worsened. In other words, over time the PPH burden has become relatively more concentrated among populations residing in lower-SDI areas. This apparent discrepancy arises because SII measures absolute differences in rates, while the CI measures relative distributional equity.

These findings indicate that although overall global maternal health standards have improved, the PPH burden is increasingly concentrated in socioeconomically disadvantaged low-SDI regions, and closing the health gap between developed and underdeveloped regions remains an intractable challenge (Figure 6). Discussion

This comprehensive global analysis systematically examined the spatiotemporal evolution trends, age-specific characteristics, and socioeconomic inequalities of PPH burden from 1990 to 2021, and delivered validated projections of PPH burden up to 2040. The core conclusion of this study is the distinct polarized epidemiological transition of global PPH burden across socioeconomic strata: low-SDI regions still face the persistent challenge of high PPH mortality resulting from insufficient basic obstetric medical resources, while middle-SDI regions around the world are confronting an emerging PPH morbidity burden driven by demographic and fertility transformations.

An important methodological issue that needs clarification is the apparent contradiction between long-term declining historical trends and projected growth in morbidity, and this seeming paradox requires a clear explanation. Our ARIMA forecasts do not predict inevitable future deterioration, but rather exploratory extrapolations based on the most recent trend dynamics. Two key observations support this interpretation. First, the global decline rate has slowed substantially. In recent years, the global APC of ASIR has shifted from −2.37% (1990–1994) to −0.31% (2008–2015), and only rebounded modestly to −1.51% (2015–2021). This indicates that the momentum of historical reductions has weakened considerably. Second, global middle-SDI regions already saw a structural reversal during 2005–2013 (APC=+0.67%), and this trajectory pattern is exactly what the ARIMA model extrapolates forward to the 2022–2040 period.

From a biological and epidemiological perspective, the projected increase in morbidity reflects a plausible “mortality-morbidity decoupling” mechanism: as global maternal mortality declines driven by improved obstetric care, more women with high-risk pregnancies - including those of advanced maternal age, pregnancies conceived via assisted reproductive technology, and multifetal pregnancies - survive and are counted as morbidity cases, while mortality continues to drop.14 This pattern has already been observed in other fields of maternal health research, and represents an emerging challenge for health systems in transitioning economies.

Therefore, the value of ARIMA forecasting lies not in predicting an inevitable future, but in quantifying the potential consequences of current trends - a crucial input for proactive health system planning. Accordingly, the projected morbidity increase is a “what-if” scenario that assumes the current deceleration of progress and regional reversals will continue, rather than a deterministic prediction of global maternal health deterioration. This distinction is critical for the correct interpretation of our findings.

The persistent high mortality burden in low-SDI regions may be partially attributable to long-term insufficient investment in primary maternal health care, a shortage of professional birth attendants, delayed emergency intervention for postpartum hemorrhage, and inadequate blood transfusion support systems.15 For these underdeveloped regions, the core public health priority remains eliminating preventable PPH deaths through universal coverage of basic standardized obstetric services, which aligns with the core goals of the WHO’s global maternal health equity strategy.16 In contrast, the transient rebound and projected future increase in PPH morbidity in middle-SDI regions reflect the universal demographic and medical shifts that occur during socioeconomic transitions. These shifts include the widespread trend of delayed childbearing, expanded application of assisted reproductive technology, and increased multiple pregnancy rates - all well-established high-risk factors for PPH.14 This consistent epidemiological pattern across global middle-income regions suggests that maternal health management priorities in developing economies need to shift from simply reducing mortality to preventing PPH morbidity through refined clinical practices, while also protecting long-term maternal health. This universal transition pattern is exemplified by China, a typical middle-SDI country where ASIR and ASPR followed a U-shaped trajectory between 2005 and 2015, which is temporally aligned with the demographic and fertility transitions observed in comparable economies.14

Findings grouped by age require careful interpretation. While women aged 25–29 years have the highest population-level PPH rates globally, this largely stems from greater reproductive exposure (higher pregnancy frequency) in this age group, rather than an inherently higher per-pregnancy risk than that of older pregnant women. Clinically, the per-pregnancy risk of PPH may actually be higher for women of advanced maternal age, even though fewer women in this age group become pregnant. This distinction is critical for risk stratification: population-level interventions should target younger women in high - fertility regions to reduce the total number of cases, while individual-level clinical risk assessment must not overlook the elevated per-pregnancy risk associated with advanced maternal age. Future research using pregnancy-denominator data will help clarify the difference between exposure effects and risk effects.

Beyond age-specific patterns, socioeconomic inequalities further exacerbate the uneven distribution of PPH burden. Dual inequality index analysis revealed a complex trend in changes to global PPH health equity: absolute disparities have narrowed, while relative inequities have intensified. This phenomenon reflects the global inverse care law, which holds that populations facing the highest PPH burden and the greatest hePeople with poor health needs have the weakest access to high-quality obstetric health resources.17 The population aged 25–29 years at the reproductive peak in low-resource regions is the most vulnerable group. It faces the dual risks of high pregnancy exposure and insufficient medical protection, thus requiring targeted health policies tailored to this specific population.

Implications for Policy, Practice, and Future Research

Based on the globally stratified epidemiological characteristics of PPH and aligned with current international guidelines,18–21 this study puts forward the following considerations for developing hierarchical, context-adaptive public health strategies

For Low-SDI Regions

Recommendations include: Prioritize universal access to basic obstetric emergency systems, train grassroots skilled birth attendants, improve emergency blood supply mechanisms, and strengthen vital registration systems to eliminate avoidable PPH related mortality. These recommendations align with WHO global maternal health strategies19 and UNFPA consolidated guidelines.21

For Global Middle-SDI Regions

Priority actions include implementing precision risk screening for pregnancies from advanced maternal age and those conceived via assisted reproductive technology, optimizing standardized PPH intervention packages, strengthening hospital emergency response capacity, and establishing postpartum follow-up systems to address the growing morbidity burden. The 2026 Lancet PPH Series20 and ICM guidelines provide comprehensive evidence to supporting these approaches.

For High-SDI Regions

Key priorities should include sustaining high-quality obstetric care management, conducting long-term dynamic surveillance of subtle changes in PPH epidemiology, implementing patient blood management programs, and maintaining a low disease burden through continuous quality improvement.

At the Global Level

Advancing health equity demands context-specific strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all approach. International organizations (including WHO, UNFPA, and ICM should support context-tailored implementation research and facilitate knowledge transfer across SDI strata.

All stratified policy recommendations proposed in this study are exploratory and tailored to current epidemiological trend scenarios, and require regionally adaptive adjustment based on local health system conditions and available resources. Future research should validate these trends with individual-level data, assess the effectiveness of context-specific interventions via implementation science, and develop pregnancy based risk models that differentiate reproductive exposure from per-pregnancy risk.

Limitations

Several limitations of this study need to be acknowledged. First, as a secondary ecological analysis based on aggregated population-level data from the GBD, this study cannot establish individual-level causal associations, and ecological fallacy cannot be completely ruled out. Second, underreporting and missing data in the vital registration systems of low-SDI regions may lead to a slight underestimation of the local PPH burden. Third, the ARIMA forecasting model only extrapolates trends based on historical data and cannot account for potential future confounding factors, such as advances in medical technology, major adjustments to public health policies, and abrupt changes in demographic structure. Therefore, all forecast results have inherent uncertainty and can only be interpreted as exploratory trend scenarios. Fourth, the ARIMA model’s assumption of Gaussian errors can theoretically produce negative prediction intervals for rates close to zero. In this study, we truncated the lower bound to zero for presentation, and recognize this as a well-documented limitation of linear time-series methods when applied to bounded data. Fifth, the SDI indicator cannot fully capture regional cultural differences, health system characteristics, and ethnic heterogeneity across regions, which may lead to subtle disparities in PPH epidemiological trends between regions. Sixth, this study performed multiple subgroup analyses without formal adjustment for multiple testing. As such, some statistically significant findings may be attributable to chance variation, and effect sizes should be prioritized over p-values in interpretation. Seventh, constrained by the unified aggregated data structure of the GBD dataset, this study cannot conduct fine-grained etiological classification analysis for PPH, and thus fails to provide more precise evidence for targeted interventions against different pathogenic subtypes of postpartum hemorrhage (PPH).

Conclusion

From 1990 to 2021, the global burden of PPH has been markedly reduced, with sustained declines in maternal mortality and disability worldwide. However, distinct socioeconomic and regional disparities remain in the global epidemiological trends of PPH. Low-SDI regions and women aged 25–29 years still constitute the population at the highest risk of PPH related adverse outcomes. Exploratory projections based on the ARIMA model suggest that, driven by demographic changes and epidemiological transition, the incidence and prevalence of PPH will likely increase in middle-SDI regions by 2040. It should be noted that these projections carry substantial uncertainty, and should be interpreted as “what-if” scenarios rather than exact predictions. Against the background of global demographic transition, middle-SDI regions worldwide are facing new challenges posed by PPH morbidity. The complex evolution of global health inequity in PPH prevalence and outcomes reminds the international community that maternal health equity remains an enduring challenge. To narrow the global gap in maternal health outcomes and promote the equitable development of global reproductive health, we need to continuously refine targeted, stratified, region-adapted global maternal health intervention strategies. Future research should verify these trends with individual-level data and evaluate the effectiveness of context-specific intervention strategies.

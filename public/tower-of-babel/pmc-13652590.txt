Giant Cell Glioblastoma: Case Series of a Rare Glioma with Unique Histomorphology and Molecular Heterogeneity

ABSTRACT
IntroductionGiant cell glioblastoma (GC-GBM) is a malignant infiltrating astrocytic tumor, characterized by highly atypical large cells with pleomorphic nuclei, numerous multinucleated GCs, necrosis, microvascular proliferation, and brisk mitotic activity. It is placed under the subcategory of GBM and is classified as central nervous system (CNS) World Health Organization (WHO) grade 4 tumor, with very poor prognosis. Unlike most of the other CNS neoplasms, its signature molecular cytogenetic abnormality is not well known and our knowledge is restricted to case reports and small case series.AimsThis study encompasses the clinicopathological and molecular profile of GC-GBM cases diagnosed in our institute over a period of 10 years (2014–2023).Materials and Methods A retrospective review of medical records (2014–2023) was performed and 256 cases of GBM were selected. Among these, 11 (4.3%) cases were diagnosed as GC-GBM. The clinical and histopathological features were studied. Hematoxylin and eosin slides were reviewed and immunohistochemistry was performed. Our study highlights the hybrid nature of this rare tumor sharing characteristics of both wild-type GBM, isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) wild-type, CNS WHO grade 4, and astrocytoma, IDH mutant, CNS WHO grade 4 (previous secondary GBM).

Introduction

Giant cell glioblastoma (GC-GBM) is a rare subtype of GBM. It is characterized by the presence of numerous multinucleated GCs (> 20 nuclei), pleomorphic tumor cells, brisk mitotic activity, microvascular proliferation, and necrosis on histopathology.

GBM tumors are broadly categorized into two groups, GBM, isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) wild-type (wt) and secondary GBM (astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, central nervous system [CNS] World Health Organization [WHO] grade 4). 1 GC-GBM is placed under the subcategory of GBM-wt in the 2021 WHO classification of tumors of the CNS. There are three recognized GBM subtypes (GC, gliosarcoma, and epithelioid) and numerous tissue patterns (e.g., gemistocytic, small cell, granular cell, and primitive cell). The median age of presentation of GC-GBM is a decade lesser than wt GBM. There is no sex predilection, as in wt GBM; however, few studies show a high male-to-female ratio. GC-GBM has a poor prognosis but the clinical outcome in terms of long-term survival rate is better than wt GBM owing to various hypothesized clinical, histopathological, and molecular features.

Aims

To study the clinicopathological and molecular profile of GC-GBM cases diagnosed in our institute over a period of 10 years (2014–2023).

Materials and Methods

A retrospective review of medical records (2014–2023) with histomorphological diagnosis of GBM made in the Department of Pathology, ICMR (Indian Council of Medical Research)-Centre for Cancer Pathology, Safdarjung Hospital, New Delhi, India. All the patients were operated in the Department of Neurosurgery, Safdarjung Hospital, New Delhi, India. Among these, cases diagnosed as GC-GBM were selected. The clinical, radiological, and histopathological features were studied. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded blocks of the selected GC-GBM cases were retrieved. Three- to five-micron thick sections were cut and stained with hematoxylin and eosin for review. Immunohistochemistry for glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP; dil 1:100), TP53 (dil 1:100), Ki67 (dil 1:100), and IDH1 R132H (ready to use) was performed. Positive and negative controls were put with each batch for quality control.

Results

Of 256 cases of GBM, 11 were GC-GBM (4.3%). The mean age was 29.4 years with a male:female ratio of 1.75:1. All the tumors were supratentorial, with most frequent location being the frontoparietal lobe. Headache and vomiting were chief presenting complaints. Histopathology ( Fig. 1A, B ) showed numerous multinucleated GCs (> 20 nuclei) in all the cases along with features of GBM, which include marked pleomorphism, microvascular proliferation, necrosis, and brisk mitotic activity. Perivascular accumulation of tumor cells with the formation of a pseudorosette-like pattern was also noted ( Fig. 1C ). Perivascular lymphocytic cuffing was also observed in a few cases ( Fig. 1D) . On immunohistochemistry ( Fig. 1E–H ), all the cases showed cytoplasmic positivity for GFAP ( n = 10, 100%). IDH1 R132H immunoreactivity (cytoplasmic) was present in 5 cases ( n = 5, 45.45%), TP 53 (nuclear) mutation in 10 ( n = 10, 90.90) cases, and Ki67 (nuclear) was > 10% in all the cases. Discussion

GC-GBM accounts for 1 to 5% of the total cases of GBM. 2 3 GBM is the most common primary brain tumor occurring in patients > 50 years of age. 4

In the WHO 2021 classification of CNS tumors, GBM (CNS WHO grade 4) is defined as an IDH-wt diffuse astrocytic glioma, diagnosed either by the presence of necrosis and/or microvascular proliferation or, in their absence, by specific molecular alterations such as TERT promoter mutation, EGFR amplification, or the combined chromosome +7/− 10 signature; accordingly, the designation “GBM” is now limited to IDH-wt tumors, whereas lesions previously classified as secondary GBM are redefined as astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, CNS WHO grade 4, in line with their distinct molecular characteristics and clinical behavior. 1 5 The current WHO classification of CNS tumors classifies WHO grade 4 adult diffuse gliomas as astrocytoma, IDH mutant type, CNS WHO grade 4 and GBM, IDHwt. 1 The former mainly affects relatively younger patients and has a better prognosis. 6 7 Gliosarcoma, GC-GBM, and epithelioid cell GBM are still subtypes of GBMs.

GC-GBM, initially classified as a distinct histological variant of GBM in the WHO 2007 classification, was retained as a morphological subtype within the integrated histomolecular framework of the WHO 2016 classification. However, in the WHO 2021 classification, it is no longer recognized as a separate entity and is instead regarded as a descriptive histological pattern within GBM, IDH-wt, reflecting the shift toward a primarily molecular classification system.

Among IDH-wt GBMs, GC-GBM represents a rare histological entity characterized by bizarre multinucleated GCs. Based on its heterogeneous deoxyribonucleic acid-methylation profile, GC-GBM is not currently considered to represent a distinct molecular entity. 8 The most common reported sites are in the cerebral hemispheres, often in the subcortical substantia alba of the temporal and parietal lobes. 9 The most common site was supratentorial in accordance with existing literature. The median age of presentation is a decade lesser as compared with IDH wt GBM (around 44 years). A recent review described the median age of presentation as 38 years. 10 However, in our study the median age was 29.36 years.

The histomorphological differentials include pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma (PXA) and subependymal GC astrocytoma (SEGA). Both PXA and SEGA are well circumscribed and are low grade gliomas with CNS WHO grade ⅔ and CNS WHO grade 1, respectively. Histopathologically, PXA is characterized by a mixture of spindled, epithelioid, pleomorphic, and multinucleated tumor cells with presence of xanthomatous cells. Necrosis is common. On the basis of mitotic activity, grading is assigned as CNS WHO grade 2 (< 5/10 high-power field [HPF]) and CNS WHO grade 3 (> 5/10 HPF). These tumors do not show IDH1 and TP53 mutation. SEGA is characterized by frequent association with tuberous sclerosis complex. Elevated mitotic activity, microvascular proliferation, and necrosis are not a feature of SEGA. The tumor is composed of cells with variable morphologies; spindle cells, gemistocytic-like cells, and ganglion-like cells. Multinucleated cells are a consistent feature. Immunohistochemistry shows positivity for GFAP, S-100, and absence of TP53 mutation, which is frequent in GC-GBM.

TP53 mutations are more common in IDH mutant GBM (astrocytoma, IDH1 mutant, CNS WHO grade 4). One large case series reported TP53 mutation in 83.3% cases of GC-GBM. 9 This is in concordance with our study where TP53 mutation was observed in 91% of the cases. The genetic profile differs from that of IDH-wt GBM most probably owing to high frequency of TP53 mutations.

GC-GBM with IDH1 mutation showed better prognosis and higher survival rate than IDH negative GC-GBM. In our study, 45.5% cases showed weak IDH1 R132H immunoreactivity. These were observed among younger patients and associated with higher percentage of tumor GCs (> 50 nuclei) and a relatively low K i -67 proliferative index (< 13%). These features may be indicative of better prognosis among this group of tumors. 11 However, due to the lack of an objective definition, specifying the exact percentage of GCs required for this diagnosis is not defined. 12 13 14 15

Inflammatory infiltrates in GBM are predominantly composed of myeloid cells (mainly tumor-associated macrophages), with fewer lymphocytes. The prognostic significance of perivascular lymphocytes remains uncertain, though some studies suggest that increased CD8+ T cell infiltration is associated with improved survival, likely reflecting a more effective antitumor immune response. 16 Postoperative survival rate was also relatively high among these patients.

There are several hypotheses for the increased survival rate in GC-GBM. Some authors suggest that the increased survival rate is due to younger presenting age in patients with GC-GBM. Others suggest that the margins and circumscription of the GC-GBM lesion are more visible and this could lead to better resection. 17 Maximum safe surgical treatment associated with adjuvant radiotherapy seems to be the best choice. Our study provides valuable insights on the growing body of evidence regarding GC-GBM. We acknowledge certain limitations. First, molecular sequencing and methylation profiling could not be performed due to financial constraints. Nevertheless, prior studies have demonstrated a high degree of concordance between immunohistochemistry and molecular sequencing, supporting the use of immunohistochemistry as a surrogate marker for underlying molecular alterations. 17 18 Second, outcome analysis for study patients was not performed as it was beyond the scope of this study.

Conclusion

GC-GBM remains a rare and intriguing subtype of GBM with distinct clinicopathological features. Our study is a useful addition to the existing literature on this rare condition. The observed weak IDH1 R132H immunoreactivity in four patients, particularly in the younger age group, represents a novel finding that warrants further investigation. This unexpected pattern of IDH1 immunoreactivity supports hypothesis generation for a distinct molecular subgroup within the GC-GBM group, with potential implications for prognosis and therapeutic decision-making; however, confirmation requires larger studies with comprehensive molecular characterization.

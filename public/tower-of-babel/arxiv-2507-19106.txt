Stability of laminar monotone shear flows in a channel for high Reynolds number
Y. Almog, Department of Mathematics

Abstract
We consider the stability of a laminar flow U ∈ C 4 ​ ( [ − 1 , 1 ] ) U\in C^{4}([-1,1]) in the two-dimensional channel ℝ × [ − 1 , 1 ] {\mathbb{R}}\times[-1,1] in the large Reynolds number limit. Assuming that U U is strictly monotone but allowing U ′′ U^{\prime\prime} to vanish, we obtain that if the operator 𝒦 ν = − d 2 d ​ x 2 + U ′′ U − ν , {\mathcal{K}}_{\nu}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\frac{U^{\prime\prime}}{U-\nu}\,, is strictly positive for all ν ∈ ℝ \nu\in{\mathbb{R}} for which U ′′ ​ ( U − 1 ​ ( ν ) ) = 0 U^{\prime\prime}(U^{-1}(\nu))=0 , then U U is stable for sufficiently large Reynolds number. This contribution generalizes previous results mostly by allowing long wave perturbations (but much shorter than the Reynolds number).

Introduction

Consider the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in the
two-dimensional pipe
D={\mathbb{R}}\times(-1,1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\mathbf{v}}-\epsilon\Delta{\mathbf{v}}+{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{v}}=-\nabla p&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times D\\
{\mathbf{v}}=v_{b}\;\hat{i}_{1}&\text{on }{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\partial D\,,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\hat{i}_{1}=(1,0)
,
{\mathbf{v}}=(v_{1},v_{2})
is the fluid
velocity, and
p
is the pressure.

The parameter

R:=\frac{1}{\epsilon}

(1.2)

is the Reynolds
number of the flow and

v_{b}:\partial D\to{\mathbb{R}}

is the boundary velocity.

Since the
flow is incompressible we must have

\text{\rm div\,}{\mathbf{v}}=0\,.

We linearize (
1.1
) near the laminar flow (cf.
[
1
]
)

{\mathbf{v}}=U(x_{2})\hat{i}_{1}\,,

to obtain the linearized equation

{\bf u}_{t}-\mathcal{T}_{0}({\bf u},q)=0

where
{\bf u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
and
q
are defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times D
, and
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is the map

({\bf u},q)\mapsto{\mathcal{T}}_{0}({\bf u},q):=-\epsilon\,\Delta{\mathbf{u}}+U\,\frac{\partial{\mathbf{u}}}{\partial x_{1}}+\,u_{2}\,U^{\prime}\,\hat{i}_{1}-\nabla q\,.

(1.3)

We proceed with a formal derivation of the Orr-Sommerfeld equation,
intentionally skipping the definitions of
{\bf v}
,
p
,
{\bf u}
,
{\bf f}
,
and
q
. Interested readers can read the entire derivation in

[
1
]
. The associated resolvent equation for

\mathcal{T}_{0}
assumes the form

{\mathcal{T}}_{0}({\bf u},q)-\Lambda{\bf u}={\bf f}\,,

(1.4)

where
\text{\rm div\,}{\bf u}=0
and
\Lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
is the spectral parameter.

Hence, we may define a stream function

{\mathbf{u}}=\nabla_{\perp}\psi=(-\psi_{x_{2}},\psi_{x_{1}})\,.

Substituting the above into (
1.4
) and then taking the curl of
the ensuing equation for
\psi
yields

\Big(-\epsilon\Delta^{2}+U\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\Delta-U^{\prime\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-\Lambda\,\Delta\Big)\psi=F\,,

(1.5)

where
F=\text{\rm curl\,}{\bf f}
.

We consider
U\in C^{4}([-1,1])
satisfying

|U^{\prime}(x)|\geq{\bf m}>0

(1.6)

Substituting
\psi(x_{1},x_{2})=\phi(x_{2})\,e^{i\alpha x_{1}}
into
(
1.5
) with
\phi:(-1,1)\to{\mathbb{C}}
yields the equation

\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\,\phi=f\,,

(1.7a)

where (setting
x_{2}=x
)

\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}=(\mathcal{L}_{\beta}-\beta\lambda)\Big(\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\alpha^{2}\Big)-i\beta U^{\prime\prime}\,,

(1.7b)

where

\mathcal{L}_{\beta}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+i\beta U\,.

(1.7c)

In the above

\beta=\alpha\epsilon^{-1}=\alpha R

(1.8)

(
R
being the Reynolds
number introduced in (
1.2
)), and, for
\beta\neq 0
,

\lambda=\hat{\Lambda}-\alpha^{2}\beta^{-1}\,,

where

\hat{\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda}{\alpha}

(1.9)

We refer to Section 3 in
[
1
]
for the
details of the derivation. We use the pair of parameters

(\alpha,\beta)
instead of
(\alpha,R)
since the asymptotic limit we
consider in the sequel is

\beta\to\infty
.

We consider the Orr-Sommerfeld operator, i.e. the Dirichlet
realization
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathcal{D}}
of
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
on the
following domain

D(\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathcal{D}})=\{u\in H^{4}(-1,1)\,,\,u(1)=u^{\prime}(1)=u(-1)=u^{\prime}(-1)=0\}\,.

(1.10)

Since
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}=\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,-\alpha,\beta}
we consider the case
\alpha\geq 0\,
only in
the sequel.

For any
\nu\in[U(-1),U(1)]
we define
x_{\nu}\in[-1,1]
by

U(x_{\nu})=\nu\,.

Notice that
x_{\nu}
is unique by (
1.6
) .

Let further

{\mathfrak{D}}=\{\nu\in[U(-1),U(1)]\,|\,U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})=0\,\}\,.

For
\nu\in{\mathfrak{D}}
we then define the operator

{\mathcal{K}}_{\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}
as the Dirichlet realization in
L^{2}(-1,+1)
of the differential operator

{\mathcal{K}}_{\nu}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\frac{U^{\prime\prime}}{U-\nu}\,.

Hence, under the assumption (
1.6
), we have

D({\mathcal{K}}_{\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})=H^{2}(-1,1)\cap H^{1}_{0}(-1,1)

We can now state the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

satisfy (
1.6
) and

\inf_{\nu\in{\mathfrak{D}}}\min\sigma({\mathcal{K}}_{\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})>0\,.

(1.11)

Then, there exist positive

,

, and

such
that for all

and

It should be mentioned that similar results are obtained in

[
5

for

. More precisely,

We consider

U\in C^{3}([-1,1])
satisfying
U^{\prime}\neq 0
on
[-1,1]
. Assume without any loss
of generality that
U^{\prime}>0
. We recall that, for
\nu\in[U(-1),U(1)]
,

is the unique solution of

U(x_{\nu})=\nu
.
For
\lambda=\mu+i\nu
, the Rayleigh operator (see

[
1
]
) is the realization of

{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{D}=(U+i\lambda)\Big(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\Big)+U^{\prime\prime}\,,

(2.1)

whose domain is defined, for
\mu\neq 0
or
\mu=0
,
\nu\not\in[U(-1),U(1)]
on
H^{2}(-1,1)\cap H^{1}_{0}(-1,1)

and when
\mu=0
,
\nu\in[U(-1),U(1)]
on the set

D({\mathcal{A}}_{i\nu,\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}})=H^{2}((-1,1);|U-\nu|^{2}dx)\cap H^{1}_{0}(-1,1)\,.

(2.2)

Remark 2.1
.

It is proved in
[
1
, Proposition 4.13]
that that Fredholm
index
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{D}
for
|\mu|>0
is zero. Thus, proving its injectivity
would imply its invertibility as well.

We now discuss the invertibility of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}}
and obtain estimates for
({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}})^{-1}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
p>1
there exist
positive
\mu_{0}
and
C
, such that for any
\lambda=\mu+i\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}
for which

\nu\in[U(-1),U(1)]
,
U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})\neq 0
,
0<|\mu|\leq\mu_{0}\,|U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|
, and
\alpha\geq 0
,

{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}}
is invertible and moreover, satisfy, for all
v\in W^{1,p}(-1,1)
,

\frac{|U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|}{\log\,(|\mu|^{-1})}\|({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}})^{-1}v\|_{1,2}\leq C\,\|v\|_{\infty}\,,

(2.3a)

|U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|\,\|({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}})^{-1}v\|_{1,2}\leq C\,(\|v^{\prime}\|_{p}+\|v\|_{\infty})\,,

(2.3b)

and

|U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|\,|\mu|^{1/p}\|({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}})^{-1}v\|_{1,2}\leq C\,\|v\|_{p}\,.

(2.3c)

Similar estimates hold for
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus[U(-1),U(1)]
in which case we set

|U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|=1
in (
2.3
).

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the proof of
[
1
, Proposition
4.14]
. Since (
2.3
) is trivial for
\nu\in{\mathfrak{D}}
, we can assume throughout the proof that

U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})\neq 0
.

Step 1:

For
1<p
and
\mu\neq 0
define
N_{m,p}^{\pm}
by

v\mapsto N_{m,p}^{\pm}(v,\lambda):=\min\Big(\Big\|(1\pm\cdot)^{1/2}\frac{v}{U+i\lambda}\Big\|_{1},\|v\|_{1,p}\Big)\,.

We prove that for all
p>1
there exists
C>0
such that,
for all
\varepsilon>0
,
\nu\in[U(-1),U(1)]
, and
0<|\mu|\leq 1
it holds that

|\phi(x_{\nu})|\leq C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{-1/2}}{|U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|}N_{m,p}^{\pm}(v,\lambda)+\Big(\Big|\frac{\mu}{U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})}\Big|^{1/2}+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Big)\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}\Big)\,,

(2.4)

for all pairs
(\phi,v)\in D({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}})\times W^{1,p}(-1,1)
satisfying

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\alpha}\phi=v
.

We follow the same arguments of step 1 in the proof of

[
1
, Proposition 4.14]
, paying special attention to the
dependence of the various constants on
U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})
. An integration by
parts yields

{\rm Im\,}\Big\langle\phi,\frac{v}{U-\nu+i\mu}\Big\rangle=-\mu\,\Big\langle\frac{U^{\prime\prime}}{(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}}\phi,\phi\Big\rangle\,,

which we can rewrite in the form

{\rm Im\,}\Big\langle\phi,\frac{v}{U-\nu+i\mu}\Big\rangle=-\mu\,\Big\langle\frac{U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})}{(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}}\phi,\phi\Big\rangle-\mu\,\Big\langle\frac{[U^{\prime\prime}(x)-U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})]}{(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}}\phi,\phi\Big\rangle\,.

(2.5)

To estimate the last term we use an integration by parts to obtain

\Big\langle\frac{[U^{\prime\prime}(x)-U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})]}{(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}}\phi,\phi\Big\rangle=\Big\langle\Big(\frac{U^{\prime\prime}(x)-U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})}{U^{\prime}(U-\nu)}|\phi|^{2}\Big)^{\prime},\log[(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}]\Big\rangle\,,

from which we conclude, using (
1.6
), the fact that

\|\log[(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}]\|_{q}
is uniformly bounded for

(\mu,\nu)\in[-1,1]\times[U(-1),U(1)]
for
q>1
, and Poincaré’s inequality that

\Big|\Big\langle\frac{[U^{\prime\prime}(x)-U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})]}{(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}}\phi,\phi\Big\rangle\Big|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}

(2.6)

As

|\phi(x)|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|\phi(x_{\nu})|^{2}-|\phi(x)-\phi(x_{\nu})|^{2}\,,

we may use (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) to obtain

|{\rm Im\,}\Big\langle\phi,\frac{v}{U-\nu+i\mu}\Big\rangle|\geq\\
|\mu U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|\,\Big\langle\frac{1}{(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}},\frac{1}{2}|\phi(x_{\nu})|^{2}-|\phi(x)-\phi(x_{\nu})|^{2}\Big\rangle\\
-C|\mu|\,\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}\,.

(2.7)

We note that, for any
1<p
, there exists
C>0
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Big|\Big\langle\phi,\frac{v}{U+i\lambda}\Big\rangle\Big|&=\Big|\Big\langle\Big(\frac{\phi\bar{v}}{U^{\prime}}\Big)^{\prime},\log\,(U+i\lambda)\Big\rangle\Big|\\
&\leq C\,\left(\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}\|v\|_{\infty}+\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|v^{\prime}\|_{p}\right)\\
&\leq C\,\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}\|v\|_{1,p}\,.\end{array}

Here, as above, we use the boundedness of the
L^{q}
norm of

\log[(U-\nu)^{2}+\mu^{2}]
for
q=p/(p-1)
. On the other hand, since

|\phi(x)|=|\phi(x)-\phi(\pm 1)|\leq\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}(1\pm x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

we may conclude that

\Big|\Big\langle\phi,\frac{v}{U+i\lambda}\Big\rangle\Big|\leq\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\Big\|(1\pm\cdot)^{1/2}\,\frac{v}{U+i\lambda}\Big\|_{1}

and hence, there exists
C>0
, such that

\Big|\Big\langle\phi,\frac{v}{U+i\lambda}\Big\rangle\Big|\leq C\,\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}\,N_{m,p}^{\pm}(v,\lambda)\,.

(2.8)

Substituting the above into (
2.7
) yields

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{|\mu U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|}{2}|\phi(x_{\nu})|^{2}\Big\|\frac{1}{U+i\lambda}\Big\|_{2}^{2}&\leq|\mu U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|\Big\|\frac{\phi-\phi(x_{\nu})}{U+i\lambda}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\qquad+C|\mu|\ \|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}+C\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}N_{m,p}^{\pm}(v,\lambda)\,.\end{array}

We now observe, as in the proof of

[
1
, Eq. (4.60)]
, that for some
\hat{C}>0

\Big\|\frac{1}{U+i\lambda}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\hat{C}\,|\mu|}\,.

Hence, for another constant
C>0
, we get

|\phi(x_{\nu})|^{2}\leq C\Big[|\mu|\Big\|\frac{\phi-\phi(x_{\nu})}{U+i\lambda}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\\
+\Big|\frac{\mu}{U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})}\Big|\,\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}+\frac{1}{|U^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}N_{m,p}^{\pm}(v,\lambda)\Big]\,.

(2.9)

To estimate the first term on the right-hand-side of
(
2.9
) we use Hardy’s inequality, as in the proof of

[
1
, Eq. (4.57)]
(see the end of the proof after (4.60)), to obtain

\Big\|\frac{\phi-\phi(x_{\nu})}{U+i\lambda}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\,\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\,,

(2.10)

which when substituted into (

) readily yields (

)
via Cauchy’s inequality and Sobolev embeddings.

,

Higher K-groups for operator systems
Walter D. van Suijlekom

Abstract
. We extend our previous definition of K-theoretic invariants for operator systems based on hermitian forms to higher K-theoretical invariants. We realize the need for a positive parameter δ \delta as a measure for the spectral gap of the representatives for the K-theory classes. For each δ \delta and integer p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 this gives operator system invariants 𝒱 p δ ​ ( − , n ) \mathcal{V}_{p}^{\delta}(-,n) , indexed by the corresponding matrix size. The corresponding direct system of these invariants has a direct limit that possesses a semigroup structure, and we define the K p δ K_{p}^{\delta} -groups as the corresponding Grothendieck groups. This is an invariant of unital operator systems, and, more generally, an invariant up to Morita equivalence of operator systems. Moreover, there is a formal periodicity that reduces all these groups to either K 0 δ K_{0}^{\delta} or K 1 δ K_{1}^{\delta} . We illustrate our invariants by means of the spectral localizer.



1.
Introduction

We continue our quest for a definition of K-theory for operator systems. In a previous work, we have defined a
K_{0}
-group which we proved to be an invariant of operator systems up to complete order isomorphism. Moreover, we showed that it is stably equivalent, which by
[
9
]
amounts to invariance under Morita equivalence of unital operator systems.

In this work, we extend this and define higher K-groups. At the same time, with the applications to the spectral localizer of
[
12
]
in mind, we realize the need for a parameter
\delta
that indicates (and quantifies the size of) a spectral gap in the elements that represent K-theory classes. This also solves the problem one is facing when considering the space of all invertible elements in a finite-dimensional operator system, namely, that this space is contractible.

The definition of
K_{0}^{\delta}
extends the group
K_{0}
considered in
[
21
]
which is obtained as a special case by setting
\delta=0
. The groups
K_{1}^{\delta}(E)
are defined, and shown to be stably equivalent as well. Moreover, we may introduce higher K-groups
K_{p}^{\delta}(E)
by exploiting graded Clifford algebras, for which we show a formal periodicity to hold, thus reducing them to
K_{0}^{\delta}
and
K_{1}^{\delta}
for
p
even and odd, respectively.

As an illustrative example, we obtain the spectral localizer as an index pairing
\ind_{D}^{\delta}:K_{p}^{\delta}(E)\to\mathbb{Z}
in the even
(p=0)
and odd
(p=1)
case, respectively.

2.
Background on operator systems

We start by briefly recalling the theory of operator systems, referring to
[
8
,
17
,
18
,
3
]
for more details.

A
unital operator system

(E,e)
is a matrix-ordered
*
-vector space
E
, equipped with an Archimedean order unit
e
. A map
\phi:E\to F
between operator systems determines a family of maps
\phi^{(n)}:M_{n}(E)\to M_{n}(F)
given by
\phi^{(n)}([x_{ij}])=[\phi(x_{ij})]
. A map
\phi:E\to F
between unital operator systems is called
completely positive
if each
\phi^{(n)}
is positive (
n\geq 1
). We also abbreviate completely positive by
cp
, and unital completely positive by
ucp
.

A
dilation
of a ucp map
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
of a unital operator system is a ucp map
\psi:E\to B(\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{K}
is a Hilbert space containing
\mathcal{H}
such that
P_{\mathcal{H}}\psi(x)|_{\mathcal{H}}=\phi(x)
for all
x\in E
. The ucp map
\phi
is called
maximal
if every dilation of
\phi
is obtained by attaching a direct summand.

A non-zero cp map
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
pure
if the only cp maps satisfying
0\leq\psi\leq\phi
are scalar multiples of
\phi
.

We may view
E
as a concrete operator system in the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(E)
it generates; in this case, we say that a ucp map
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
has the
unique extension property
if it has a unique ucp extension to
C^{*}(E)
which is a
*
-representation. If, in addition, the
*
-representation is irreducible, it is called a
boundary representation

[
2
]
. The following result is well-known in the literature
[
2
,
15
,
1
,
11
,
6
]
(see also
[
21
]
).

Proposition 1
.

Let
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
be a ucp map. Then

(1)

\phi
is maximal if and only if it has the unique extension property.

(2)

\phi
is pure and maximal if and only if it is a boundary representation.

3.
\delta
-gapped K-theory for unital operator systems:
K_{0}^{\delta}
and
K_{1}^{\delta}

Even though in an operator system we cannot speak about invertible elements, we may use the pure and maximal ucp maps to quantify the extent to which an element is (non)singular.

Definition 2
.

Let
(E,e)
be a unital operator system and let
\delta>0
. An element
x\in M_{n}(E)
is called
\delta
-singular if for all
s\in(0,\delta)
there exists a
g>0
such that for all pure and maximal ucp maps
\phi:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
we have

(1)

\left|\phi^{(2n)}\left(s\cdot e_{2n}+\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\right)\right|\geq g\cdot\textup{id}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\oplus 2n}

The largest real number
g>0
such that (
1
) holds is called the
s
-gap of
x
.

We will write
G^{\delta}(E,n)
for all
\delta
-singular elements in
M_{n}(E)
, and
H^{\delta}(E,n)
for the
\delta
-singular self-adjoint elements in
M_{n}(E)
. We extend this definition of
\delta
-singular to the limiting case
\delta=0
to be those elements for which (
1
) holds with
s=0
; in the self-adjoint case these are precisely the non-singular hermitian forms considered previously in
[
21
]
.

Proposition 3
.

An element
x\in M_{n}(E)
is
\delta
-singular if and only if

\begin{pmatrix}s&\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)\\
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x^{*})&s\end{pmatrix}

are invertible elements in
M_{2n}(C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E))
for all
s\in(0,\delta)
, where
\imath_{E}:E\to C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)
is the
C^{*}
-envelope.

Proof.

In
[
6
]
the
C^{*}
-envelope of
E
is constructed as the direct sum of all boundary representations
(\mathcal{H}_{\sigma},\sigma)
:

\imath_{E}:E\to\bigoplus_{\sigma}B(\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}).

Now if
x\in M_{n}(E)
then a matrix such as the above is invertible if and only if its image
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)
is bounded from below by
g\cdot\textup{id}_{\oplus_{\sigma}(\mathcal{H}_{\sigma})}
. But this holds if and only if (
1
) holds for all boundary representations
\sigma
. Since by Proposition
1
a ucp map is a boundary representation if and only if it is pure and maximal, the result follows.
∎

We can now translate
\delta
-singularity into spectral properties of
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)
in
C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)
. For this consider the following spectrum

\Sigma_{x}:=\sigma\begin{pmatrix}0&\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)\\
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x^{*})&0\end{pmatrix}\equiv\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-\lambda&\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)\\
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x^{*})&-\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\right)\text{ is not invertible }\right\}

This subset of
\mathbb{R}
is symmetric around
0
since we may unitarily conjugate

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda&\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)\\
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x^{*})&-\lambda\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)\\
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x^{*})&\lambda\end{pmatrix}

The notion of
\delta
-singularity can now be captured by the property that
\pm s\notin\Sigma_{x}
for all
s\in(0,\delta)
, or, equivalently:

Corollary 4
.

Let
\delta>0
.
An element
x\in M_{n}(E)
is
\delta
-singular iff
\Sigma_{x}\subseteq(-\infty,-\delta]\cup\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty)
.

Our notion of
\delta
-singularity thus allows to detect the presence of a so-called
spectral gap
of size
\delta
. This terminology is borrowed from physics where the spectral gap is the energy difference between the ground state and the first excited state. For this reason we also refer to such elements as being
\delta
-gapped
. Moreover, we can find an explicit lower bound for the
s
-gap, since

(2)

\left|\begin{pmatrix}s&\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)\\
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x^{*})&s\end{pmatrix}\right|\geq\mathrm{min}\{\lambda:\lambda\in s+\Sigma_{x}\}=\mathrm{min}\{s,\delta-s\}

In the self-adjoint case, we have the following result.

Corollary 5
.

Let
\delta>0
. A self-adjoint element
x\in M_{n}(E)
is
\delta
-singular iff
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)
has spectral gap
\delta
, i.e.
\sigma(\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x))\subseteq(-\infty,-\delta]\cup\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty)
.

Proof.

This follows by a similarity transformation:

\begin{pmatrix}s&\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)\\
\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)&s\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{pmatrix}s+\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)&0\\
0&s-\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

Indeed, the matrix on the right-hand side is invertible iff
\pm s\notin\sigma(\imath_{E}^{(n)}(x))
for all
s\in(0,\delta)
.
∎

3.1.
The
K_{0}^{\delta}
-groups

The
K_{0}
-group of non-singular self-adjoint elements has been treated in detail in
[
21
]
; let us consider here the changes when
\delta
-singular, or
\delta
-gapped self-adjoint elements are considered.

Definition 6
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
and
x,x^{\prime}\in H^{\delta}(E,n)
. We say that
x\sim_{n}x^{\prime}
if there exists
\widetilde{x}\in H^{\delta}(C([0,1])\otimes E),n)
such that

\widetilde{x}(0)=x;\qquad\widetilde{x}(1)=x^{\prime}

We denote the equivalence class of
x\in H^{\delta}(E,n)
by
[x]_{n}
, and the set of all such equivalence classes in
H^{\delta}(E,n)
by
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)
, or, equivalently,

\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)=H^{\delta}(E,n)/_{\sim_{n}}.

All these sets
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)
for

are invariants of unital operator systems:

Proposition 7
.

If
E
and
F
are completely order isomorphic then
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)\cong\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(F,n)
, for any
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the fact that in this case
C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)\cong C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(F)
via a unital
*
-isomorphism.
∎

If we combine this structure at each level
n
with the direct sum of
\delta
-gapped self-adjoint elements, we obtain a semigroup structure as follows. Consider the direct system of sets
(\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n),\imath_{nm})
where for
m\geq n

(3)

\displaystyle\imath_{nm}:\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,m)

\displaystyle[x]_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto[x\oplus e_{m-n}]_{m},

We denote the direct limit of the direct system (
3
) by
\varinjlim\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)
. A more explicit description is given as follows: for
x\in H^{\delta}(E,n)
and
x^{\prime}\in H^{\delta}(E,n^{\prime})
we write
x\sim x^{\prime}
if there exists a
k\geq n,n^{\prime}
such that
x\oplus e_{k-n}\sim_{k}x^{\prime}\oplus e_{k-n^{\prime}}
in
H^{\delta}(E,k)
. We will write
[x]_{E}
, or simply
[x]
, for the equivalence class corresponding to
x\in H^{\delta}(E,n)
and

\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E):=\amalg_{n}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)/_{\sim}
for the corresponding set of equivalence classes. The following is then clear from the definition of the direct limit.

Proposition 8
.

The set
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E)
is the direct limit
\varinjlim\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)
of the direct system (
3
). Moreover, it is a semigroup when equipped with the direct sum
[x]+[x^{\prime}]=[x\oplus x^{\prime}]
and identity element
0=[e]
.

The
K_{0}^{\delta}
-group

for a unital operator system

is now defined to be the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E)
; we will refer to it as the
\delta
-gapped
K_{0}
-group of

. In
[
21
]
we have shown that for
\delta=0
, that is, for non-singular self-adjoint elements, this group coincides with the
C^{*}
-algebraic
K_{0}
-group in the case of a
C^{*}
-algebra, that it behaves well with respect to direct sums, and that it is invariant under Morita equivalence of unital operator systems. For later use, we record the following result also in the case of general
\delta\geq 0
. The proof follows
mutatis mutandis
from that of
[

Proposition 9

(Stability of
K_{0}^{\delta}
)
.

Let
E
be a unital operator system,
\delta>0
, and let
N
be a natural number. Then
\mathcal{V}^{\delta}_{0}(E)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{V}^{\delta}_{0}(M_{N}(E))
(and so are the corresponding
K_{0}^{\delta}
-groups).

3.2.
K_{1}^{\delta}
for unital operator systems

For the analogue of
K_{1}
for operator systems we drop the assumption of being self-adjoint, and consider
\delta
-singularity in the sense of Definition
2
, or, in view of Corollary
4

Example 10
.

(i)

(Almost unitaries and quantitative
K
-theory)
In
[
16
]
the quantitative
K_{1}
-group of a filtered
C^{*}
-algebra

was defined in terms of
{\varepsilon}
-

-unitaries, i.e. elements

\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\

In other words, the spectrum of the matrix on the left-hand side has spectrum contained in
(-\infty,-\delta]\cup[\delta,\infty)
for all
\delta<(1-{\varepsilon})^{1/2}

u\in G^{\delta}(A_{r},n)
for all those
\delta

\delta=0
).

(ii)

(Unitaries in operator spaces and systems) In
[
4
]
unitaries in operator spaces and operator systems where characterized. These turn out to be unitaries in matrices with entries in the

-envelope, so that we find that they define elements in

as long as
0\leq\delta<1

(iii)

(The odd spectral localizer)
The odd spectral localizer
[
12
]
is defined in terms of a spectral compression
x=PTP
of an invertible element
T
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
by a projection in
\mathcal{H}
. We then have

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}^{2}=P\begin{pmatrix}TT^{*}\\
0&T^{*}T\end{pmatrix}^{2}P+P\begin{pmatrix}[P,T][P,T^{*}]\\
0&[P,T^{*}][P,T]\end{pmatrix}^{2}P

Since
T
is invertible, it is bounded below by some
g>0
; the same applies to
T^{*}
. Consequently, we find from (
4
)

\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}^{2}\geq(g^{2}-\eta^{2})P

in terms of

. So, provided

and

.

(iv)

(Toeplitz matrices)

Consider the unitary
u
given by the bilateral shift operator
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. If we compress this with a projection onto
\text{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{e_{1},\ldots e_{n}\}
in terms of the canonical orthonormal basis of

, we obtain the following Toeplitz matrix:

PuP=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&0&\cdots&0&0\end{pmatrix}

This is a candidate for index pairings in the spectral localizer (for the circle,
[

10

), and as such we expect it to represent an element in the Toeplitz operator system. Note that even though

is invertible,

\delta
-singular provided

.

Definition 11
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
and

if there exists an element

Remark 12
.

Let us come back to the parameter
\delta
in the definition of
\delta
-singularity (Definition

from the start (as we did with hermitian forms in

]

(and the same applies to

4.
Higher
\delta
-gapped K-groups and formal periodicity

As in
K
-theory for

-algebras there are also higher-order invariants, which we now introduce. We first need the following.

Definition 17
.

An operator system
E

Consequently, there is a

into a

-graded

Much in the same way as Van Daele defined

[
19
,
20
]
, we will consider odd
\delta
-singular elements in

as our candidates for K-theory representatives. More precisely, we set

Definition 18
.

Let
(E,e)

We define

\widehat{H}^{\delta}(E,n):=H^{\delta}(E,n)\cap M_{n}(E^{(1)});\qquad(d=0,1).

and also set
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\delta}(E,n)=\widehat{H}^{\delta}(E,n)/_{\sim_{n}}

It would be interesting to develop this theory further for graded operator systems, similar to
[
19
,
20
]

For this, we will exploit the graded Clifford algebras (

\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\delta}

Let

Let
(E,e)

The higher

E
are defined as the Grothendieck groups of the corresponding direct limits
\mathcal{V}_{p}^{\delta}(E):=\varinjlim\mathcal{V}_{p}^{\delta}(E,n)
, equipped with direct sum as the additive operation.

Let us now check that this definition is consistent with that of the invariants
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(E,n)
and
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\delta}(E,n)
(introduced in Definitions
6
and
11
).

For the first case,
p=0
, we can indeed identify using Equation (
10
):

\displaystyle H^{\delta}(E\otimes\CCl_{1}^{(1)},n)

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&-x\end{pmatrix}\in H^{\delta}(E,2n)\right\}\cong H^{\delta}(E,n).

The last isomorphism follows from the fact that there is a similarity transformation between the following matrices:

\begin{pmatrix}s&0&x&0\\
0&s&0&-x\\
x&0&s&0\\
0&-x&0&s\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{pmatrix}s&x\\
x&s\end{pmatrix}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2}

Upon taking the homotopy equivalence classes, we find agreement with Definition
6
. Note that such a doubling has also been considered in the definition of K-theory of ungraded Banach algebras in
[
19
, Remark 2.13(iv)]
.

For
p=1
we have, using Equation (
11
):

H^{\delta}(E\otimes\CCl_{2}^{(1)},n)\cong\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\in H^{\delta}(E,2n)\right\}\cong G^{\delta}(E,n),

again via a similarity transformation, this time:

\begin{pmatrix}s&0&0&x\\
0&s&x^{*}&0\\
0&x&s&0\\
x^{*}&0&0&s\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{pmatrix}s&x\\
x^{*}&s\end{pmatrix}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2}

We thus find agreement of the above definition of
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\delta}(E,n)
with Definition
11
.

More generally, we have the following, which may be considered as an operator system analogue of formal periodicity:

Proposition 20
.

Let
(E,e)
be a unital operator system and let
\delta>0
. Then

\mathcal{V}_{2m}^{\delta}(E,n)\cong\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(M_{2^{m}}(E),n),\qquad\mathcal{V}_{2m+1}^{\delta}(E,n)\cong\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\delta}(M_{2^{m}}(E),n)

for any
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

For the even-dimensional case, we use (
10
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{2m}^{\delta}(E,n)

\displaystyle=H^{\delta}(E\otimes\CCl_{2m+1}^{(1)},n)/_{\sim_{n}}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&-x\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in H^{\delta}(E\otimes(M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})),n)\right\}/_{\sim_{n}}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{x\in H^{\delta}(M_{2^{m}}(E),n)\right\}/_{\sim_{n}}\equiv\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(M_{2^{m}}(E),n).

For the odd-dimensional case, we instead use (
11
) to write

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{2m+1}^{\delta}(E,n)

\displaystyle=H^{\delta}(E\otimes\CCl_{2m+2}^{(1)},n)/_{\sim_{n}}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in H^{\delta}(E\otimes M_{2^{m+1}}(\mathbb{C}),n)\right\}/_{\sim_{n}}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{x\in G^{\delta}(M_{2^{m}}(E),n)\right\}/_{\sim_{n}}\equiv\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\delta}(M_{2^{m}}(E),n).\qed

Proposition
20
, in combination with the stability of
K_{0}^{\delta}
and
K_{1}^{\delta}
(Proposition
9
and
16
), then yields the following periodicity result:

Theorem 21
.

Let
(E,e)
be an ungraded unital operator system and let
\delta\geq 0
. Then
K_{2m}^{\delta}(E)\cong K_{0}^{\delta}(E)
and
K_{2m+1}^{\delta}(E)\cong K_{1}^{\delta}(E)
.

5.
Application to the spectral localizer

In
[
13
,
12
]
the spectral localizer was introduced as a powerful tool for index pairings. They reduce the computation of a certain Fredholm index to the computation of the signature of a finite-dimensional matrix —the spectral localizer. We will put it in the context of our notion of K-theory, realizing the spectral localizer as a spectral flow formula (very much as in
[
13
,
14
]
) which maps
\mathcal{V}_{p}^{\delta}(E,n)\to\mathbb{Z}
. For more details on spectral flow, including the spectral localizer, we refer to the excellent textbook
[
7
]
and references therein.

In order to describe the index map from
\mathcal{V}_{p}^{\delta}(E,n)
, we need an operator system spectral triple
[
5
]
.

Definition 22
.

A (unital) operator system spectral triple is given by a triple
(E,\mathcal{H},D)
where
E
is a unital operator system realized concretely so that
E\subseteq C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
, and a self-adjoint operator
D:\dom(D)\to\mathcal{H}
such that

•

the commutators
[D,x]
extend to bounded operators for all
x\in\mathcal{E}
for a dense
*
-subspace
\mathcal{E}\subseteq E
;

•

the resolvent
(i+D)^{-1}
is a compact operator.

An operator system spectral triple is called even if in addition to the above, there is a grading operator
\gamma
(so that
\gamma^{*}=\gamma,\gamma^{2}=1_{\mathcal{H}}
) which commutes with all
x\in E
and anti-commutes with
D
. Otherwise, it is called odd.

In the even case, we can decompose
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-}
according to the eigenvalues of
\gamma
, and decompose accordingly

D=\begin{pmatrix}0&D_{0}\\
D_{0}^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}.

In the odd case, there is no such grading, but for convenience we will then write
D_{0}\equiv D
.

Given an operator system spectral triple
(E,\mathcal{H},D)
, a parameter
\kappa>0
and an element
x\in G^{\delta}(\mathcal{E},n)
we now define the
generalized spectral localizer
for any
s\in(0,\delta)
as the following (unbounded) self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}^{\oplus 4n}
:

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}s&x&\kappa D^{\oplus n}_{0}&0\\
x^{*}&s&0&\kappa D_{0}^{\oplus n}\\
\kappa(D^{\oplus n}_{0})^{*}&0&-s&-x\\
0&\kappa(D^{\oplus n}_{0})^{*}&-x^{*}&-s\\
\end{pmatrix}.

We use the term ’generalized’ to distinguish it from the even and odd spectral localizer consider in
[
7
]
. However, they are not unrelated:

Remark 23
.

Note that if we set
s=0
in the odd case (so that
D_{0}=D
) we obtain (twice) the odd spectral localizer
L_{\kappa}^{\text{odd}}
introduced in
[
12
]
:

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s=0)\sim(L^{\text{odd}}_{\kappa})^{\oplus 2};\qquad L^{\text{odd}}_{\kappa}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa D^{\oplus n}&x\\
x^{*}&-\kappa D^{\oplus n}\end{pmatrix},

that is, up to a similarity transformation.

In the even case, we will instead consider pairings with self-adjoint elements
x\in H^{\delta}(\mathcal{E},n)
in which case we find for
s=0
the even spectral localizer
L^{\text{even}}_{\kappa}
of
[
13
]
:

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s=0)\sim(L^{\text{even}}_{\kappa})^{\oplus 2};\qquad L^{\text{even}}_{\kappa}=\begin{pmatrix}x&\kappa(D_{0})^{\oplus n}\\
\kappa(D_{0}^{*})^{\oplus n}&-x\end{pmatrix}

In general, the shift
s\in(0,\delta)
is crucial in order to define the spectral localizer at the level of the operator systems, even though in some cases it may be set to zero (
cf.
Section
5.1
below).

Proposition 24
.

Let
(E,\mathcal{H},D)
be a finite-dimensional operator system spectral triple.
Let
x\in G^{\delta}(\mathcal{E},n)
and let
s\in(0,\delta)
and write
g_{s}=\mathrm{min}\{s,\delta-s\}
. Then the
signature of the generalized spectral localizer,

\Sig(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)),

is constant in the
(\kappa,s)
-region defined by
0<\kappa<g_{s}^{2}\|[D,x]\|^{-1}
and
0<s<\delta
, and invariant under homotopy equivalence. Consequently, we have an induced map
\ind_{D}^{\delta}:\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\delta}(\mathcal{E},n)\to\mathbb{Z}
given by

\ind_{D}^{\delta}([x])=\lim_{\kappa,s\to 0}\frac{1}{4}\Sig(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)),

where the limit is taken in the above region.

In the even case, we take
x\in H^{\delta}(\mathcal{E},n)
whence the induced map is
\ind_{D}^{\delta}:\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\delta}(\mathcal{E},n)\to\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we take
n=1
and compute very similar to
[
13
]
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\kappa^{2}D_{0}D_{0}^{*}&0\\
0&\kappa^{2}D_{0}D_{0}^{*}\end{smallmatrix}\right)+\begin{pmatrix}s&x\\
x^{*}&s\end{pmatrix}^{2}&\begin{pmatrix}0&-\kappa[D_{0},x]\\
-\kappa[D_{0},x^{*}]&0\end{pmatrix}\\
\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa[D_{0}^{*},x]\\
\kappa[D_{0},x]&0\end{pmatrix}&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\kappa^{2}D_{0}^{*}D_{0}&0\\
0&\kappa^{2}D_{0}^{*}D_{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right)+\begin{pmatrix}s&x\\
x^{*}&s\end{pmatrix}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

(7)

\displaystyle\geq\left(g^{2}_{s}-\kappa\|[D,x]\|\right)1_{M_{4}(B(\mathcal{H}))}.

Hence
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)
is invertible (and thus has well-defined signature) provided
0<\kappa<g_{s}^{2}\|[D,x]\|^{-1}
and
0<s<\delta
. Moreover, the signature is constant in this region since no eigenvalues will cross the origin. This also makes the limit of
\Sig(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s))
when
(\kappa,s)
approach the origin from within this region well-defined.

Also, one may easily check that

\Sig(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x\oplus x^{\prime},s))=\Sig(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s))+\Sig(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x^{\prime},s)).

In order to see that
\Sig(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,e,s))=0
consider (in the odd case) an orthonormal eigensystem
\{v_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
of
D
in
\mathcal{H}
so that
Dv_{\lambda}=\lambda v_{\lambda}
. We may write the matrix of
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)
in this eigensystem as

(8)

\displaystyle\langle v_{\lambda},\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)v_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\begin{pmatrix}s&1&\kappa\lambda&0\\
1&s&0&\kappa\lambda\\
\kappa\lambda&0&-s&-1\\
0&\kappa\lambda&-1&-s\\
\end{pmatrix},

which has set of eigenvalues
\{\pm\sqrt{(1\pm^{\prime}s)^{2}+\kappa^{2}\lambda^{2}}\}
so that the signature of the spectral localizer vanishes.

In the even case, we consider instead an eigensystem
\{v_{\lambda}=(v_{\lambda}^{+},v^{-}_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-}
such that
D_{0}v^{-}_{\lambda}=\lambda v^{+}_{\lambda}
and
\gamma v_{\lambda}^{\pm}=\pm v_{\lambda}^{\pm}
. Again, we find that the matrix of
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)
in this eigensystem is given by (
8
) so that the signature of the spectral localizer vanishes.

Consider now a homotopy
\widetilde{x}
in
G^{\delta}(E,n)
(or in
H^{\delta}(E,n)
in the even case) between
x=\widetilde{x}(0)
and
x^{\prime}=\widetilde{x}(1)
with
s
-gap
\geq g_{s}
. By a computation similar to the one in Eq. (
7
) we find that

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,\widetilde{x},s)^{2}\geq g^{2}_{s}-\kappa\sup_{t}\|[D,\widetilde{x}(t)]\|

So as long as
\kappa<g_{s}^{2}/\sup_{t}\|[D,\widetilde{x}(t)]\|
we find that
\Sig\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,\widetilde{x}(t))
is constant in
t
. We combine this with the fact that
\kappa<g_{s}^{2}/\|[D,\widetilde{x}(0)]\|
and
\kappa<g_{s}^{2}/\|[D,\widetilde{x}(1)]\|
to conclude that
\lim_{\kappa,s\to 0}\Sig\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,\widetilde{x}(0),s)=\lim_{\kappa,s\to 0}\Sig\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,\widetilde{x}(1),s)
. This completes the proof.
∎

We may now rephrase the main results of
[
13
,
12
]
. For an invertible (self-adjoint) element
a\in A
one considers the class
[a]
in the K-theory group of the
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. Given a spectral triple
(A,\mathcal{H},D)
, there is an index pairing
\ind_{D}([a])
. As shown in
loc.cit.
this index may be computed in terms of a spectrally truncated (operator system) spectral triple
(PAP,P\mathcal{H},PDP)
for a spectral projection
P
(of
D
) of sufficiently high rank. Indeed, we then have

(9)

\ind_{D}([a])=\frac{1}{4}\Sig(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(PDP,x,0))=\frac{1}{2}\Sig(L_{\kappa}^{\text{even/odd}})

since in fact it is shown in
[
12
]
that
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,0)
already has a positive gap, say
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,0)^{2}\geq g^{2}
. Thus, adding the matrix with diagonal entries
(s,s,-s,-s)
to obtain
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(D,x,s)
will not change this property, as long as
s<g
.

5.1.
Example: spectral localizer on the circle

Let us illustrate the spectral localizer by spectral truncations of the circle. We consider a unitary
u(t)=e^{imt}
with winding number
m
and want to use the spectral localizer to compute the index pairing with
D_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}=-id/dt
.

We take a spectral projection
P_{N}
onto
\text{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{e_{-N},e_{-N+1},\ldots,e_{N}\}
and realize that
P_{N}uP_{N}
is a Toeplitz matrix with
1
’s on the
m^{\prime}th
diagonal, and zeros elsewhere. Note that
P_{N}uP_{N}
is not invertible, however the following self-adjoint
2\times 2
matrix with values in the truncated operator system
is
non-singular for any
s\in(0,\delta)
for some
\delta>0
:

\begin{pmatrix}sP_{N}&P_{N}uP_{N}\\
P_{N}u^{*}P_{N}&sP_{N}\end{pmatrix}

Hence
P_{N}uP_{N}\in G^{\delta}(P_{N}C(\mathbb{S}^{1})P_{N},1)
.

Figure 1
.
The negative and positive eigenvalues for the spectral localizer
L_{\kappa,N}=L_{\kappa,N,s=0}
on the circle for
m=1,\kappa=1,N=3
(left) and
m=2,\kappa=0.1,N=3
(right). The red (diamond-shaped) dots indicate the surplus of positive eigenvalues as compared to the negative ones.

The spectral localizer is given in this case by the following matrix:

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa,N,s}:=\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(P_{N}DP_{N},P_{N}uP_{N},s)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}s&P_{N}uP_{N}&\kappa P_{N}DP_{N}&0\\
P_{N}u^{*}P_{N}&s&0&\kappa P_{N}DP_{N}\\
\kappa P_{N}DP_{N}&0&-s&-P_{N}uP_{N}\\
0&\kappa P_{N}DP_{N}&-P_{N}u^{*}P_{N}&-s\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right).

The main result of
[
12
]
(
cf.
Eq. (
9
) and the discussion following it) can now be used to show that for suitable
N,\kappa
there exists
g>0
so that the generalized spectral localizer satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa,N,s}^{2}\geq g^{2}
for any
s<\delta
, including
s=0
. As such, the signature can be computed by setting
s=0
, so that

\ind_{P_{N}DP_{N}}([P_{N}uP_{N}])=\frac{1}{4}\text{Sig}~\mathcal{L}_{\kappa,N,{s=0}}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Sig}~L_{\kappa,N},

where as in Remark
23
we have written
\mathcal{L}_{k,N,0}=L_{\kappa,N}\oplus L_{\kappa,N}
in terms of

L_{\kappa,N}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa P_{N}DP_{N}&P_{N}uP_{N}\\
P_{N}u^{*}P_{N}&-P_{N}DP_{N}\end{pmatrix}

The main result of
[
12
]
states that
\ind(P_{D}uP_{D})=\frac{1}{2}\text{Sig}~L_{\kappa,N}
so that we deduce that

\ind(P_{D}uP_{D})=\ind_{P_{N}DP_{N}}([P_{N}uP_{N}])

In Figure
1
we illustrate the resulting signature by showing the negative and positive eigenvalues of
L_{\kappa,N}
. We find already for low
N
that the spectral localizer is equal to (twice) the winding number of
u
.

Appendix A
Clifford algebras

Recall the definition of the Clifford algebras
\CCl_{p}
for
p\geq 1
associated to the vector spaces
\mathbb{C}^{p}
with their standard quadratic form.
These are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
C^{*}
-algebras,
i.e.

\CCl_{p}=\CCl_{p}^{(0)}\oplus\CCl_{p}^{(1)}
. For each
p
we have a grading operator
\Gamma
such that
\CCl_{p}^{(d)}=\{a\in\CCl_{p}:\Gamma a=(-1)^{d}a\Gamma\}
(if
p
is odd, this grading operator is an element in the multiplier algebra of
\CCl_{p}
).

In the odd case
p=2m+1
we have
\CCl_{2m+1}\cong M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})
and we may take

\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbb{I}_{2^{m}}\\
\mathbb{I}_{2^{m}}&0\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2^{m+1}}(\mathbb{C}).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\CCl_{2m+1}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{(x,x):x\in M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})\right\}\subset M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C}).

(10)

\displaystyle\CCl_{2m+1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{(x,-x):x\in M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})\right\}\subset M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C}).

Instead, if
p=2m
then
\CCl_{2m}\cong M_{2^{m}}(\mathbb{C})
and we may take

\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{2^{m-1}}&0\\
0&-\mathbb{I}_{2^{m-1}}\end{pmatrix}.

Accordingly,

\displaystyle\CCl_{2m}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&y\end{pmatrix}:x,y\in M_{2^{m-1}}(\mathbb{C})\right\}.

(11)

\displaystyle\CCl_{2m}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
y&0\end{pmatrix}:x,y\in M_{2^{m-1}}(\mathbb{C})\right\}.

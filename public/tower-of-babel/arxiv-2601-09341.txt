Abstract
. In this manuscript we deal with a class of nonlinear Fokker-Planck equations with the following structure ∂ t u − div ( M ∇ u + E h ( u ) ) = 0 , \partial_{t}u-{\rm div}\big(M\nabla u+Eh(u)\big)=0, with M M a bounded elliptic matrix, E E a vector field in a suitable Lebesgue space, and h ⁡ ( u ) h(u) featuring a superlinear growth for u u large. We provide existence results of C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ) , L 1 ) C([0,T),L^{1}) distributional solutions to initial-boundary value problems related to the equation above together with some qualitative properties of solutions.



Keywords
: Nonlinear parabolic equations, superlinear drift, noncoercive problems.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2000:
35K20, 35K55, 35Q84.

1.
Introduction

In this manuscript we deal with a class of nonlinear Fokker-Planck equations with the following structure

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u-{\rm div}\big(M\nabla u+Eh(u)\big)=0,

with
M
a bounded elliptic matrix,
E
a vector field in a suitable Lebesgue space, and
h(u)
featuring a superlinear growth for
u
large.

Taking
M
the identity matrix,
E(x)=x
, and
h(u)=u(1+u)
, we recover the equation proposed in
[
27
,
28
]
to describe the dynamic of Bose-Einstein particles, where the quadratic drift term accounts for an extra concentration phenomenon due to quantum effects. Under this specific set of assumptions, equation (
1.1
) has been addressed using approaches based on moments estimates or exploiting its variational structure; see for instance
[
14
,
15
,
25
,
38
]
and references therein. Among these results, let us stress that Toscani showed in
[
38
]
that, if the
L^{1}
norm of the initial datum is large enough, finite time blow up occurs and measured valued solutions appear. Therefore, existence of global in time smooth solutions is not expected in general for (
1.1
).
However, the previously mentioned results hold either for specific choices for the vector field
E
and the matrix
M
or under strong structural assumptions on it (for instance
E=\nabla\phi
) and does not seem that they can be immediately adapted to more general cases. Let us also quote
[
4
,
24
]
, where a careful analysis of a initial-boundary value problem associated to (
1.1
) is carried out in
\mathbb{R}
.

Our contribution here is to set up a well posedness theory for
C([0,T),L^{1})
solutions to initial-boundary values problem related to (
1.1
) under fairly general assumptions on the coefficients. The approach we follow is entirely not variational, it does not need specific assumptions on the structure of the vector field
E
, and it does not make use of any representation formula. Let us stress that here we will not deal with measured valued solutions (for more details on the topic see for instance
[
25
]
). We will rather keep our analysis either in cases in which global existence holds, or proposing local in time results, namely before that the possible
condensation
of the solution to a measure may occur (see again
[
38
]
).

For us
\Omega
is an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geq 3
, and
\Omega_{T}
denotes the space-time cylinder
(0,T)\times\Omega
for any given
T>0
. The measurable matrix
M(t,x)
satisfies, for almost every
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
and for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the following conditions

(1.2)

\alpha|\xi|^{2}\leq M(t,x)\,\xi\xi,\quad|M(t,x)|\leq\beta,\\

for two positive constants
0<\alpha\leq\beta
, while the vector field
E:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and the function
u_{0}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
belong to suitable Lebesgue spaces. We focus on the following problem

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-{\rm div}\left(M(t,x)\nabla u+E(t,x)|u|^{\theta}u\right)=0&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega_{T},\\
u=0&\quad\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

The main assumption here is that
\theta>0
, namely the convection term is superlinear. Our approach also works for nonlinearities that satisfy
|h(u)|\leq C(1+|u|^{\theta+1})
, we have chosen
h(u)=|u|^{\theta}u
for clarity of exposition. The notion of weak solution that we consider is given in Definition
2.3
below.

For
\theta=0
existence and regularity properties of the differential operator

u\to-{\rm div}\big(M(t,x)\nabla u+E(t,x)u\big)

have been studied both in the stationary
[
3
,
5
,
13
,
16
,
18
,
22
,
30
]
and in the evolutionary framework
[
10
,
11
,
20
,
21
,
23
,
34
]
. For superlinear drift we refer to
[
6
]
(see also
[
1
,
17
]
). The leitmotiv of these works is that the (super)linear drift makes the differential operator not coercive and therefore the usual strategies to get a priori estimate fail. Depending on the assumptions, the effect of the lower order term may be balanced by the second order elliptic operator (see for instance
[
5
]
) or may represent an obstruction to existence/regularity of solutions (see for instance
[
6
]
).

Let also mention that if the drift term is on the form
-
div
(\Phi(u))
it is possible to obtain existence of
renormalized
solution for any continuous
\Phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
with no growth conditions at infinity, see
[
9
,
32
]
. However these results do no apply in our contest of space dependent drifts.

At first we provide global in time existence and uniqueness of solutions for
\theta
below a given threshold, namely,

(1.4)

0<\theta\leq\frac{1}{N}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let us assume (
1.2
), (
1.4
), and take
r\in(N,\infty]
such that

(1.5)

\frac{1}{r}+\theta=\frac{1}{N}.

Then, for any given
T>0
,
u_{0}\in L^{\mu}(\Omega)
with
\mu\geq 1
, and
|E|\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{r}(\Omega))
, there exists a unique weak solution
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{loc}((0,T);W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
to (
1.3
) such that

(1.6)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{ for }t\in(0,T),

with
C_{1}=C_{1}(\alpha,N,\theta,\mu,r,T,E,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)})
.
Moreover for any
m\geq\mu
there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(\alpha,N,\theta,\mu,m,r,T,E,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)})
such that

(1.7)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)}\frac{C_{2}}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(\frac{1}{\mu}-\frac{1}{m})}},\qquad\mbox{ for }t\in(0,T).

On the other hand, if we drop assumption (
1.4
), the result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
\theta>0
, take
\mu\in(1,\infty)
and
r\in(N,+\infty]
such that

(1.8)

\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\theta}{\mu}<\frac{1}{N}.

For any
u_{0}\in L^{\mu}(\Omega)
and
|E|\in\big(L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{r}(\Omega))\big)^{N}
there exists
T^{*}>0
depending on
\alpha,N,\mu,r,\theta,\|E\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{r}(\Omega))}
, and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)}
, such that, for
T=T^{*}
, problem (
1.3
) admits a unique weak solution
u
in
C([0,T^{*});L^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{loc}((0,T^{*});W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
that satisfies

(1.9)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{T^{*}}{T^{*}-t}\right)^{\gamma}\qquad\mbox{ for }t\in(0,T^{*}).

Moreover, the following contractive type estimate holds true: for any
m>\mu
there exists
C(t)=C(\alpha,N,\theta,m,\mu,r,\|E\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*},L^{r}(\Omega))},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)},t)
such that

(1.10)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C(t)}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(\frac{1}{\mu}-\frac{1}{m})}}\qquad\mbox{ for }t\in(0,T^{*}).

with
C(t)\to+\infty
as
t\to T^{*}

While Theorem
1.1
is valid for any given
T>0
, Theorem
1.2
gives existence only on the finite time interval
(0,T^{*})
, with estimate (
1.9
) blowing up as
t\to T^{*}
.
Even if the two proofs follow slightly different procedures, the main idea behind these theorems is the same: to obtain a differential inequality satisfied by a suitable norm of the solution
u
(see for instance
[
11
,
12
,
29
,
33
]
). A formal computation shows that, if
u
is the solution to (
1.3
) with initial datum in
L^{\mu}(\Omega)
, the quantity
y(t)=\|u(t)\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)}^{\mu}
solves

(1.11)

y^{\prime}\leq Qy^{1+d},

for suitable constant
Q
and exponent
d
. This differential inequality is obtained multiplying the equation in (
1.3
) by a suitable test function, integrating in space, and coupling Sobolev embedding (in space) with Hölder and interpolation inequalities. Assumptions (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) naturally arise by the dimensional analysis associated to this process. If (
1.5
) is valid, it follows that
d=0
(despite the fact that
\theta>0
) and we have an estimate for all times. On the other hand, if
\theta
is large, we have
d>0
, and the dynamic associated to (
1.11
) gives rise to finite time blow-up of the estimate. All the details to make this argument rigorous and the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
can be found in Section
4
.

For the sake of clarity, let us stress that the information given by (
1.9
) is only a bound from above for
\|u(t)\|_{L^{\mu}(\Omega)}
, that does not imply blow-up for the solution. However, we recall the possibility of measured valued solution appearing in finite time is in accordance with
[
38
]
.

It is moreover interesting to note that, while the nonlinear drift may exhibit a singular behavior for sufficiently large times, for small values of
t
the regularizing effect of second order operator (the diffusion) dominates the drift for any
\theta
. Indeed the exponent
\frac{N}{2}\left({1}/{\mu}-1/m\right)
in (
1.10
) is exactly the same as in the classical heat equation (see
[
35
]
and reference therein).

We also provide existence and
L^{p}
-regularity results for a nonzero right hand side. Precisely, we consider the following problem

(1.12)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-{\rm div}\left(M(t,x)\nabla u+E(t,x)|u|^{\theta}u\right)=f(t,x)&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega_{T},\\
u=0&\quad\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

In order to state our results, we introduce the following quantities:

(1.13)

q^{\star}=\frac{(N+2)q}{N+2-q}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ q^{\star\star}=\frac{(N+2)q}{N+2-2q}.

Observe that
q^{\star}
and
q^{\star\star}
are the natural parabolic counterparts of the Sobolev coniugate exponents of
q
in the elliptic settings.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
\sigma=2\frac{N+2}{N}
,

\theta\geq 0
,
f\in L^{q}(\Omega_{T})
,
u_{0}\in L^{q^{\star\star}\frac{N}{N+2}}(\Omega)
,
E\in L^{r}(\Omega_{T})
with

(1.14)

\frac{1}{r}+\theta=\frac{1}{N+2}.

(i)
If
q=1
, problem (
1.12
) admits a weak solution
u\in L^{m}(0,T,W^{1,m}_{0}(\Omega))\cap L^{m^{\star}}(\Omega_{T})
for all
m\in[1,1^{\star})
.

(ii)
If
q\in(1,\sigma^{\prime})
, problem (
1.12
) admits a weak solution
u\in L^{q^{\star}}(0,T,W^{1,q^{\star}}_{0}(\Omega))\cap L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
.

(iii)
If
q\in[\sigma^{\prime},\frac{N+2}{2})
, problem (
1.12
) admits a unique weak solution
u
in
L^{2}(0,T,W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))\cap L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
.

Again, we see that there exists a range of values of
\theta
for which the influence of the drift, that is still superlinear, is completely absorbed by the principal operator. Indeed, we have existence for all times and we recover the same type of regularity as in the case
E\equiv 0
.

Here we use the test-function based approach developed in
[
7
,
8
]
.
However, let us mention that even for such small values of
\theta
, the achievement of the a priori estimates is not standard and we had to adopt a slicing procedure in time to deal with the non coercive drift term.

As before, we also consider cases where the exponent
\theta
is large.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
\theta>0
, take
q\in[2\frac{N+2}{N+4},\frac{N+2}{2})
and
r>N+2
such that

(1.15)

\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\theta}{q^{\star\star}}=\frac{1}{N+2},

with
q^{\star\star}
defined in (
1.13
), and assume that

f\in L^{q}(\Omega_{T}),\ \ \ E\in(L^{r}(\Omega_{T}))^{N},\ \ \ u_{0}\in L^{q^{\star\star}\frac{N}{N+2}}(\Omega).

Then there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,q)
such that, whenever

(1.16)

\|E\|_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left[\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{T})}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q^{\star\star}\frac{N}{N+2}}(\Omega)}\right]\leq\frac{\theta}{(C(\theta+1))^{\frac{\theta+1}{\theta}}},

problem (
1.12
) admits a unique weak solution
u
in
L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})\cap L^{2}(0,T,W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
.

To prove this result, we apply Schauder fixed point Theorem to the map
\mathscr{F}:L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})\to L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
, that associates to each
v\in L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
the unique solution
u\in L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
to

\partial_{t}u-{\rm div}\left(M(t,x)\nabla u\right)={\rm div}\left(E(t,x)|v|^{\theta}u\right)+f(t,x)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega_{T},

with
u=0
on
(0,T)\times\partial\Omega
and
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)
in
\Omega
(see
[
8
]
for the existence of such a solution). So, the smallness assumption (
1.16
) on the size of the data assures existence of an invariant ball for
\mathscr{F}
. Notice that this existence for small data is somehow connected to the local in time existence of Theorem
1.2
. Indeed, taking
f\equiv 0
for simplicity, (
1.16
) is always verified for
T
sufficiently small.

As before, assumptions (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) appear naturally from a dimensional analysis of the equation and allow to close the a priori estimates in Theorem
1.3
and to have the map
\mathscr{F}
well defined in Theorem
1.4
. Roughly speaking, the difference with (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) is due to the use of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on the parabolic cylinder instead of Sobolev inequality in space.

We conclude this introduction by pointing out that in Section
3
(see Theorem
3.3
below) we prove an
L^{1}
contraction estimate (see the for instance
[
31
]
and reference therein) of the following type

\int_{\Omega}(v(\tau)-w(\tau))_{+}\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(f-g)\chi_{v>w}+\int_{\Omega}(v_{0}-w_{0})_{+},\quad\tau\in[0,T],

where
v
is the solution associated to source
f
and initial datum
v_{0}
and
w
is the solution associated to source
g
and initial datum
w_{0}
. This kind of comparison principle represents a key tool to derive the uniqueness and to obtain the well posedness in
C([0,T),L^{1})
of our initial boundary value problem.

2.
Notation and functional framework

In order to introduce our functional framework, take
T>0
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and let
X
be a separable Banach space endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. The
space

L^{p}\big(0,T,X\big)

is given by all measurable
functions
u\colon[0,T]\rightarrow X
that are
L^{p}
integrable. Such a space is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{L^{p}\big(0,T,X\big)}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|^{p}_{X}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/p}

for
1\leq p<\infty
and

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T,X\right)}:=\esssup_{0<t<T}\|u(t)\|_{X}

for
p=\infty
. As usual we also define

L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p}(0,T,X):=\left\{u:[0,T]\to X\;\text{measurable}\;:\;u\in L^{p}(t_{1},t_{2};X)\text{ for every }[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset(0,T)\right\}.

In other words, a function
u
belongs to
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p}(0,T,X)
if it is
L^{p}
integrable
with values in
X
on every compact subinterval of
(0,T)
. Analogously, the space

C^{0}\left([0,T],X\right)
consists in the set of continuous functions
u\colon[0,T]\rightarrow X
,
equipped the norm

\|u\|_{C^{0}\left([0,T],X\right)}:=\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u(t)\|_{X}.

We consider now the Banach space

W^{2}(0,T):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(0,T,W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega))\colon\,u_{t}\in{L^{2}(0,T,W^{-1,2}(\Omega))}\right\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{2}(0,T)}:=\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T,W^{1,2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T,W^{-1,2}(\Omega))},

and its local counterpart

W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(0,T):=\left\{u\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(0,T,W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)):\;u_{t}\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(0,T,W^{-1,2}(\Omega))\right\}.

Given
1<p<\infty
, the Marcinkiewicz space
\mathcal{M}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
consists of all measurable functions
f
defined on
\Omega_{T}
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{\mathcal{M}^{p}}:=\sup_{k>0}k^{p}\lambda_{f}(k)

is finite, with
\lambda_{f}(k):=\left|\left\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|f(t,x)|>k\right\}\right|
. It is well known that
L^{p}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{M}^{p}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
, whenever
1\leq q<p.

Let us also recall the following consequence of the well known Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see

[
19
, Proposition 3.1]
),

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

\varphi\in L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T,W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
and let
\sigma:=2\frac{N+2}{N}
.
Then

\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\varphi|^{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(N)\left(\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2/N}\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

For our purposes it is also useful to recall, for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
k>0
, the definition of the following truncation functions

T_{k}(s)=\max\{-k,\min\{s,k\}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad G_{k}(s)=s-T_{k}(s).

We shall also use the following property related to differential inequalities.

Lemma 2.2
.

Take
K,C\in(0,\infty)
and assume that
y(t)
is a positive function in
W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,T)
such that

y^{\prime}+Ky^{1+a}\leq Cy\quad\mbox{a.e. in }(0,T).

Therefore

y(t)\leq\left(\frac{1}{aK}\right)^{1/a}\frac{e^{Ct}}{t^{1/a}}

Proof.

See for instance Lemma 2.6 of
[
33
]
or formula (2.18) of
[
11
]
.

We conclude this section specifying the notion of solution we shall use through the work.

Definition 2.3
.

If
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
, we say that
u\in C([0,T);L^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{1}_{loc}(0,T,W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega))
is a weak solution to (
1.12
) if
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)
,
|E||u|^{\theta+1}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(u(t_{2})\varphi(t_{2})-u(t_{1})\varphi(t_{1})\big)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}u\partial_{t}\varphi

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\big(M\nabla u+E|u|^{\theta}u\big)\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for every
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<T
and every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{T})
.

Remark 2.4
.

If a solution enjoys the following additional regularity

u\in W^{2}_{loc}(0,T)\quad|E||u|^{\theta+1}\in L^{2}_{loc}((0,T);L^{2}(\Omega)),

by the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
in
W^{2}(0,T)
and a truncation argument, we deduce that

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle\partial_{t}u,\varphi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\big(M\nabla u+E|u|^{\theta}u\big)\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for every
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<T
and all
\varphi\in W^{2}(0,T)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
. Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the duality pairing between
W^{-1,2}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. In the case the additional regularity is global on the time interval
(0,T)
we can also take
t_{1}=0
and
t_{2}=T
.

3.
Preliminary results

Let us introduce a suitable sequence of approximating solutions to our problem. For any
T>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, classical results (see for instance
[
26
]
) imply the existence of
u_{n}\in W^{2}(0,T)
solution to

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{n}-{\rm div}\,(M(t,x)\nabla u_{n}+E(t,x)g_{n}(u_{n}))=f_{n}(t,x)&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega_{T},\\
u_{n}=0&\quad\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u_{n}(0,x)=u_{0,n}(x)&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

where
f_{n}=T_{n}(f)
,
u_{0,n}=T_{n}(u_{0})
, and
g_{n}(s)=|s|^{\theta}s/(1+\frac{1}{n}|s|^{\theta+1})
.
Notice that, thanks to our assumption on
E
, there exist
r>N
and
s>1
such that

\frac{N}{2r}+\frac{1}{s}<\frac{1}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|E|\in L^{s}((0,T),L^{r}(\Omega)).

Therefore we also know that
u_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
(see
[
2
]
).

Our first result states that the
L^{1}
-norm of
u_{n}
is uniformly bounded in time.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
. Then, every
u_{n}
solution to (
3.1
) satisfies

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|(t)dx\leq M_{0}:=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\quad\mbox{for all }t\in(0,T).

Proof.

Let
t\in(0,T)
and let
u_{n}
be solution of
(\mathcal{P}_{n})
. We test (
3.1
) by
\epsilon^{-1}T_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\chi_{(0,t)}
. Denoting

(3.3)

\Phi_{\epsilon}(u):=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{u}T_{\epsilon}(z)dz

we have

(3.4)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}&\Phi_{\epsilon}\left(u_{n}(t)\right)dx-\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}\left(u_{0}\right)dx+\frac{\alpha}{{\epsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla T_{\epsilon}(u_{n})|^{2}dxds\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|u_{n}|^{\theta+1}|E||\nabla T_{\epsilon}(u_{n})|dxds+\int_{\Omega_{t}}|f|dxds\\
\leq&\frac{\alpha}{2{\epsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla T_{\epsilon}(u_{n})|^{2}dxds+\frac{\epsilon^{2\theta}}{\alpha}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|E|^{2}dxds+\int_{\Omega_{t}}|f|dxds,\end{split}

where we used Young inequality. Dropping the (positive) energy term, we get

\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}\left(u_{n}(t)\right)dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}\left(u_{0}\right)dx+\frac{\epsilon^{2\theta}}{\alpha}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|E|^{2}dxds+\int_{\Omega_{t}}|f|dxds.

Observing that

\Phi_{\epsilon}(u_{n})=\frac{\min\{|u_{n}|,\epsilon\}^{2}}{2\epsilon}+\max\{|u_{n}|,\epsilon\}-\epsilon,

we can pass to the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
and conclude that, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\end{split}

so that (
3.2
) is proved.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

We explicitly observe that Proposition
3.1
implies that, for any
h\in(0,T)
,

(3.6)

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}=\int_{0}^{h}\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}(t)|\leq hM_{0}.

which in turn implies the following uniform estimate of the superlevel sets of
u_{n}
:

(3.7)

\begin{split}|\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}\colon|u_{n}(t,x)|>k\}|\leq\frac{TM_{0}}{k}\quad\text{for all $k>0$.}\end{split}

Now we provide a comparison principle for reasonably well behaved solutions. Notice that the only global property we need to assume is
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Take
\theta>0
,
f,g\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
, and
E\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
.
Let
v,w\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))\cap W^{2}_{loc}(0,T)
be such that
|E|(|v|^{\theta+1}+|w|^{\theta+1})\in L^{2}_{loc}((0,T);L^{2}(\Omega))
and

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle\partial_{t}v,\varphi\rangle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\big(M\nabla v\nabla\varphi+E|v|^{\theta}v\nabla\varphi\big)\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}f\varphi,

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle\partial_{t}w,\varphi\rangle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\big(M\nabla w\nabla\varphi+E|w|^{\theta}w\nabla\varphi\big)\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}g\varphi,

for every
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<T
and every
\varphi\in W^{2}(0,T)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
,
\varphi\geq 0
in
\Omega_{T}
.
Assume moreover that

(|v|^{\theta}+|w|^{\theta})|E|\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T}).

Therefore, it follows that for any
\tau\in[0,T]

\int_{\Omega}(v(\tau)-w(\tau))_{+}\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(f-g)\chi_{v>w}+\int_{\Omega}(v(0)-w(0))_{+}

Proof.

We fix
0<\delta<\tau<T
,
z=(v-w)_{+}
and use

\epsilon^{-1}T_{\varepsilon}(z)
as a test function in the inequalities satisfied by
v
and
w
. Recalling (
3.3
), we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}(z)(\tau)dx+\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla T_{\varepsilon}(z)|^{2}dxdt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|E|\left||v|^{\theta}v-|w|^{\theta}w\right||\nabla T_{\varepsilon}(z)|dxdt+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(f-g)T_{\varepsilon}(z)dxdt+\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}(z)(\delta)dx\end{split}

Since
T_{\epsilon}z=T_{\varepsilon}(v-w)_{+}
is supported in
\{0<z<\epsilon\}
, it follows that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}(z)(\tau)dx&+\frac{\alpha}{2\epsilon}\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla T_{\varepsilon}(z)|^{2}dxdt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha\epsilon}\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|E|^{2}\left||v|^{\theta}v-|w|^{\theta}w)\right|^{2}\chi_{0<z<\epsilon}dxdt\\
&+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(f-g)T_{\varepsilon}(z)dxdt+\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}(z)(\delta)dx\end{split}

As
\left||v|^{\theta}v-|w|^{\theta}w)\right|\leq c(|v|^{\theta}+|w|^{\theta})|v-w|
,
the above inequality becomes

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}(z)(\tau)dx\leq&\tilde{c}\epsilon\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|E|^{2}\left(|v|^{2\theta}+|w|^{2\theta}\right)\chi_{0<z<\epsilon}dxdt\\
+&\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(f-g)T_{\varepsilon}(z)dxdt+\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}(z)(\delta)dx\end{split}

Therefore, taking at first the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
, it follows that

\int_{\Omega}(v(\tau)-w(\tau))_{+}\leq\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(f-g)\chi_{v>w}+\int_{\Omega}(v(\delta)-w(\delta))_{+}.

Finally, thanks to the continuity assumption
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
, we let
\delta\to 0
and obtain the desired result.
∎

and

In the present section we provide a proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem

As already said in the introduction, we shall obtain a differential inequality satisfied by
y(t)=\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}(t)|^{m}
. The first step to deduce such an inequality formally consists in testing (
3.1
) with
|u_{n}|^{m-2}u_{n}
with

. However, such a choice of test function is not allowed if

admits (weak) derivative. The next Lemma addresses these technical issues (here we adapt to our framework Lemma 2.5 of
[
33
]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Take a function
v\in W^{2}_{loc}(0,T)
such that
|E||v|^{\theta+1}\in L^{2}_{loc}((0,T);L^{2}(\Omega))
and that

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle\partial_{t}v,\varphi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\big(M\nabla v+E\tilde{g}(v)\big)\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

for every
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<T
and all
\varphi\in L^{2}_{loc}(0,T,W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
, where
|\tilde{g}(v)|\leq|v|^{\theta+1}
. If, for a given
m>1
,

(4.1)

v\in L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,T);L^{m}(\Omega))\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|E|^{2}|v|^{2\theta+m}\in L^{1}_{loc}((0,T),L^{1}(\Omega)),

it follows that
y(t)=\int_{\Omega}|v(t)|^{m}
belongs to
W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,T)
and verifies

(4.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|v(t)|^{m}+\alpha\frac{2\mathcal{S}^{2}}{m^{\prime}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|v(t)|^{m\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leq m\frac{m-1}{2\alpha}\int_{\Omega}|E|^{2}|v(t)|^{2\theta+m}.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we take the globally Lipschitz function
\psi_{\varepsilon}(s)
and its integral function
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)
:

\psi_{\varepsilon}(s):=\frac{1}{m-1}\left[(T_{1/\varepsilon}(|s|)+\varepsilon)^{m-1}-\varepsilon^{m-1}\right]{\rm sign}(s)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{\varepsilon}(v)dv.

Therefore we can chose
\psi_{\varepsilon}(v)\chi_{(t_{1},t_{2})}
, with
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<T
, as a test function in (
3.1
). Notice that

(4.3)

\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(v)=(T_{1/\varepsilon}(|v|)+\varepsilon)^{m-2}\nabla v\,\chi_{|v|<1/\varepsilon}=(|v|+\varepsilon)^{m-2}\nabla T_{1/\varepsilon}(v).

Therefore, it follows that

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(v(t_{2}))dx+\alpha\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla T_{1/\varepsilon}(v)|^{2}(|v|+\varepsilon)^{m-2}dxdt\\
&\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}|E||v|^{\theta+1}|\nabla T_{1/\varepsilon}(v)|(|v|+\varepsilon)^{m-2}dxdt+\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(v(t_{1}))dx.\end{split}

Using Young inequality in the drift term and recalling (
4.3
), it follows that

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(v(t_{2}))dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla T_{1/\varepsilon}(v)|^{2}(|v|+\varepsilon)^{m-2}dxdt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}|E|^{2}(T_{1/\varepsilon}(|v|)+\epsilon)^{2\theta+m}dxdt+\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(v(t_{1}))dx.\end{split}

Thanks to assumption (
4.1
) and since
|\Psi_{\varepsilon}(v)|\leq C(m)(|v|^{m}+1)
, it follows that the right hand side above is bounded for almost all
t_{1}\in(0,t_{2})
.
Therefore, taking the limsup in (
4.4
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
, Fatou’s Lemma implies that
|\nabla v|^{2}|v|^{m-2}\in L^{1}_{loc}((0,T),L^{1}(\Omega))
.

On the other hand, taking
\psi_{\varepsilon}(v)\zeta
as a test function in (
3.1
), with
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,T)
, one deduces that

(4.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(v)dx=-\int_{\Omega}\big(M\nabla v+E\tilde{g}(v)\big)\cdot\nabla T_{1/\varepsilon}(v)(|v|+\varepsilon)^{m-2}dx

in the sense of distributions. Since we know that
|\nabla v|^{2}|v|^{m-2},|\nabla v|^{2}\in L^{1}_{loc}((0,T),L^{1}(\Omega))
and thanks to assumptions (
4.1
), we deduce that the right hand side of (
4.5
) (that is function of
t
) is controlled by an element in
L^{1}_{loc}(0,T)
uniformly as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Therefore, the dominated converge theorem implies that the left hand side of (
4.5
) strongly converges in
L^{1}(0,T)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Since
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)\to\frac{|s|^{m}}{m(m-1)}
, this gives that
y(t)=\int_{\Omega}|v(t)|^{m}
belongs to
W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,T)
. Going back to (
4.5
) and using Young and Sobolev inequalities, we deduce that

(4.6)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(v)dx+&\alpha\mathcal{S}^{2}\frac{2}{m^{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|(T_{1/\varepsilon}(|v|)+\varepsilon)^{\frac{m}{2}}-\varepsilon^{\frac{m}{2}}|^{2^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2\alpha}\int_{\Omega}|E|^{2}(T_{1/\varepsilon}(|v|)+\epsilon)^{2\theta+m}dx.\end{split}

Taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
we get (
4.2
).
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

Remark 4.3
.

Notice that if

3.1

Proof.

The first ingredient we need is a decay estimate on the measure of a suitable super level set. For any

\begin{split}&|\{x\in\Omega\ :\ |E||u_{n}(t)|^{\theta}>\rho\}|\\

with the right hand side above vanishing as

uniformly with respect to

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}(t)|^{m}+\alpha\frac{2\mathcal{S}^{2}}{m^{\prime}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}(t)|^{m\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leq m\frac{m-1}{2\alpha}\int_{\Omega}|E|^{2}|u_{n}(t)|^{2\theta+m}.

Let us decompose the first integral on the right hand side above as follows

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\Omega}|E|^{2}|u_{n}(t)|^{2\theta}|u_{n}(t)|^{m}\\

Let us explicitly stress that in the last line we used

Let us now prove estimate (

with

To prove estimate (

m2^{*}/2

Going back to (

\sup_{\tau\in(0,h)}\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}({\tau})|^{2(\gamma+1)}+\int_{\Omega_{h}}|\nabla(|u_{n}|^{\gamma+1})|^{2}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{T})}^{2\gamma+2}+\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{2\gamma+2}dx\right).

Clearly, if (

If not, we proceed dividing the time interval (0,T) in suitably small slices. For it, let us take

Let us stress that the last integral makes sense since

Therefore, it follows that

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We apply Shauder’s fixed point Theorem to the map defined in (
6.2
).

Existence of an invariant ball
Having in mind estimate (
6.3
), let us set now

\delta=C_{\alpha,q}\|E\|_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ K=C_{\alpha,q}\left[\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{T})}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q^{\star\star}\frac{N}{N+2}}(\Omega)}\right],

so that, using the same notation of Lemma
6.2
,

R=(C_{\alpha,q}\|E\|_{L^{r}(\Omega_{T})}(\theta+1))^{-1/\theta}.

Under assumption (
1.16
), we can apply Lemma
6.2
to conclude that,

\|v\|_{L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq R\Rightarrow\|u\|_{L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq\delta\|v\|_{L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})}^{\theta+1}+K\leq R

namely

\mathscr{F}(B_{R})\subset B_{R}.

Continuity of the map
\mathscr{F}
Assume
v_{n}\to v
in
L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
and set
u_{n}=\mathscr{F}(v_{n})
and
u=\mathscr{F}(v)
. The difference
z_{n}=u_{n}-u
solves

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}z_{n}-{\rm div}(M\nabla z_{n})=-{\rm div}\left[E(|v_{n}|^{\theta}v_{n}-|v|^{\theta}v)\right]&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega_{T},\\
z_{n}=0&\quad\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
z_{n}(x,0)=0&\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

Therefore, thanks to Step 1, we have that
z_{n}
satisfies the following a priori estimate

\|z_{n}\|_{L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C_{\alpha,q}\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}(|v_{n}|^{\theta}v_{n}-|v|^{\theta}v)^{\frac{q^{\star\star}}{\theta+1}}\right)^{\frac{\theta+1}{q^{\star\star}}}.

Since, up to a subsequence, the dominated convergence theorem implies that the right hand side above tends to zero, we have that
z_{n}
goes to zero in
L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
. Being the argument independent of the considered subsequence, the continuity of the map
v\to\mathscr{F}(v)
follows.

Compactness
Let us now take a sequence
v_{n}
that is bounded in
L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
and set, as before,
u_{n}=\mathscr{F}(v_{n})
. In order to show that the map
v\to\mathscr{F}(v)
is compact, we shall prove that there exists a not relabeled subsequence of
u_{n}
that strongly converges in
L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
.

Let us recall that
u_{n}\in L^{2}(0,T,W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
satisfies in weak sense.

\partial_{t}u_{n}-{\rm div}(M(t,x)\nabla u_{n})=-{\rm div}(E(t,x)|v_{n}|^{\theta}v_{n})+f(t,x),

with
-{\rm div}(E(t,x)|v_{n}|^{\theta}v_{n})+f(t,x)
uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T,W^{-1,2}(\Omega))
.
This implies that the sequence
u_{n}
is bounded in the space

W_{2}(0,T)

and, by the Aubin-Lions’ compactness result, we deduce that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}
strongly converge to some
\zeta
in
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
and
u_{n}\to\zeta
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
. Using (
6.3
) with
v_{n}
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\|G_{l}(u_{n})\|_{L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq&C_{\alpha,q}\left[\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}\cap\{|u_{n}|>l\}}|E|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})}^{\theta+1}+\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}\cap\{|u_{n}|>l\}}|f|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]\\
\leq&\tilde{C}\left[\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}\cap\{|u_{n}|>l\}}|E|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}+\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}\cap\{|u_{n}|>l\}}|f|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]=\omega(l).\end{split}

Since
u_{n}
strongly converges in
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
, we have that
\omega(l)
goes to zero as
l
diverges. Therefore, for any measurable set
\mathcal{E}\subset\Omega_{T}
,

\int_{\mathcal{E}}|u_{n}|^{q^{\star\star}}\leq\int_{\mathcal{E}}|T_{l}(u_{n})|^{q^{\star\star}}+\int_{\Omega}|G_{l}(u_{n})|^{q^{\star\star}}\leq l^{q^{\star\star}}|\mathcal{E}|+\omega^{q^{\star\star}}(l).

This implies that the sequence
|u_{n}|^{q^{\star\star}}
is uniformly equi-integrable: indeed, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
l
large enough such that
\omega(l)^{q^{\star\star}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
; setting
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{2l^{q^{**}}}
we have that

\int_{\mathcal{E}}|u_{n}|^{q^{**}}\leq\epsilon\ \ \ \forall\mathcal{E}\ :\ |\mathcal{E}|<\delta,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
u_{n}\to\zeta
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
, Vitali Theorem assures us that
u_{n}\to\zeta
in
L^{q^{\star\star}}(\Omega_{T})
.

Therefore, Shauder’s fixed point Theorem provides existence of a solution.Uniqueness follows by Theorem
3.3
.

Combinatorial construction of symplectic 6-manifolds via bifibration structures Thanks: The author was supported by Japanese Society for the Promotion of Science KAKENHI Grant Numbers JP22K03320 and JP23K03123.
Kenta Hayano

Abstract
. A bifibration structure on a 6 6 -manifold is a map to either the complex projective plane ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} or a ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} -bundle over ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} , such that its composition with the projection to ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} is a ( 6 6 -dimensional) Lefschetz fibration/pencil, and its restriction to the preimage of a generic ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} -fiber is also a ( 4 4 -dimensional) Lefschetz fibration/pencil. This object has been studied by Auroux, Katzarkov, Seidel, among others. From a pair consisting of a monodromy representation of a Lefschetz fibration/pencil on a 4 4 -manifold and a relation in a braid group, which are mutually compatible in an appropriate sense, we construct a bifibration structure on a closed symplectic 6 6 -manifold, producing the given compatible pair as its monodromies. We further establish methods for computing topological invariants of symplectic 6 6 -manifolds, including Chern numbers, from compatible pairs. Additionally, we provide an explicit example of a compatible pair, conjectured to correspond to a bifibration structure derived from the degree- 2 2 Veronese embedding of the 3 3 -dimensional complex projective space. This example can be viewed as a higher-dimensional analogue of the lantern relation in the mapping class group of the four-punctured sphere. Our results not only extend the applicability of combinatorial techniques to higher-dimensional symplectic geometry but also offer a unified framework for systematically exploring symplectic 6 6 -manifolds.

Introduction

The construction of symplectic structures from Lefschetz fibrations/pencils established by Gompf
[
18
,
19
]

[
20
]
), together with the correspondence between Lefschetz fibrations/pencils and their monodromies shown in
[

6

4
-manifolds from relations in the mapping class groups of surfaces.
This combinatorial approach has yielded various intriguing examples of symplectic 4-manifolds, such as those obtained in
[
13
,
7
,
1
]
.
Conversely, for a given symplectic manifold, Donaldson
[
8
]
constructed a Lefschetz pencil on it by perturbing a map to the complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{1}
obtained as the ratio of two sections of a line bundle with certain properties, which are later referred to as
approximately holomorphic sections
in
[
3
,
5
,
4
]
.
Auroux and Katzarkov
[
3
,
5
,
4
]
further developed this idea: they used three approximately holomorphic sections to construct a map from a higher-dimensional symplectic manifold to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with well-behaved critical point/value sets, which we call a
Lefschetz bipencil
in this manuscript.
In particular, by examining the monodromies of this map on a symplectic
6
-manifold, one can obtain a pair consisting of a monodromy representation of a Lefschetz pencil on a 4-manifold and a relation in a braid group, which are compatible in an appropriate sense (see
Definition

2.5
for further details).

This study aims to lay groundwork for investigating symplectic 6-manifolds from the aforementioned pairs via bifibration structures, specifically as follows:

(I)

To clarify the correspondence between bifibration structures on symplectic
6
-manifolds and compatible pairs, including both existence and uniqueness (under a suitable equivalence).

(II)

To establish a method for computing the topological invariants of symplectic 6-manifolds, such as the fundamental group, the (co)homology groups, and the Chern classes/numbers, from compatible pairs.

(III)

To present examples of compatible pairs that potentially serve as building blocks for other compatible pairs, ideally as simple as possible yet non-trivial, such as the lantern, chain, and torus relations in mapping class groups of surfaces.

In order to deal with broader classes of compatible pairs than those associated with Lefschetz bipencils appearing in
[
5
,
4
]
, we consider a
Lefschetz bifibration
on a
6
-manifold, which is a map to the total space of a
D^{2}
- or
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle

such that the composition with the bundle projection
\rho:Z\to E
is a Lefschetz fibration, and its restriction on the preimage of a generic fiber
\rho^{-1}(\ast)
is also a Lefschetz fibration (see
Definition

2.3
for the precise definition).
Note that the concept of Lefschetz bifibrations has already been introduced in
[
34
]
and relevant references with additional geometric structures.
As in the case of Lefschetz fibrations/pencils, one can obtain a Lefschetz bifibration from a Lefschetz bipencil by blowing-up the target
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and the source manifold.

We first address the existence part of the purpose (I).
Specifically, under some mild assumptions, we construct a Lefschetz bifibration

Suppose that

The corresponding Lefschetz bifibration

If some of sections of a Lefschetz fibration associated with

On the other hand, the preimage of the

Conventions and notations

Throughout the paper, we assume that manifolds are smooth, compact, oriented and connected unless otherwise noted.
We denote the
n
-dimensional complex projective space by
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by
\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}
.
We put
\mathbb{D}_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}~|~|z|\leq r\}
for
r>0
and
D^{2}=\mathbb{D}_{1}
.
For a smooth map
f:X\to Y
between manifolds, we denote the set of critical points and values of
f
by
\operatorname{Crit}(f)\subset X
and
\operatorname{Critv}(f)\subset Y
, respectively.
We use the dashed arrow
\dashrightarrow
to represent a map defined on the complement of a closed submanifold with positive codimension (e.g. a Lefschetz pencil).

Let
X
be a manifold and
P,S\subset X
.
We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{P}(X,S)
the group of orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphisms which preserve
P
(resp.
S
) pointwise (resp. setwise).
We denote the mapping class group
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{D}_{P}(X,S))
by
\mathcal{M}_{P}(X,S)
.
The symbols
P
and
S
are omitted when they are the empty set.
We put
\partial
instead of
P
when
P=\partial X
(e.g.
\mathcal{M}_{\partial}(\mathbb{D}_{1},P)
in page
2.2.1
).
We define multiplication of mapping class groups to be opposite to composition of representatives, that is,
[\xi_{1}]\cdot[\xi_{2}]=[\xi_{2}\circ\xi_{1}]
for diffeomorphisms
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
, in order to make group structures of mapping class groups consistent to those of braid groups, and also make monodromy representations homomorphisms.

2.
Fibration structures on symplectic manifolds

2.1.
Lefschetz fibrations, pencils and their monodromies

In this subsection we briefly review Lefschetz fibrations (over
D^{2}
or
\mathbb{P}^{1}
), pencils, and their monodromy representations.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a compact
2n
-manifold (
n\geq 2
) and
E
be either
D^{2}
or
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
A
Lefschetz fibration
on
X
is a smooth map
f:X\to E
satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

\operatorname{Crit}(f)
is contained in
\operatorname{Int}(X)
.
For any
x\in\operatorname{Crit}(f)
, one can take an orientation-preserving complex chart
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
(resp.
\psi:V\to\mathbb{C}
) around
x
(resp.
f(x)
) satisfying

\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=z_{1}^{2}+\cdots+z_{n}^{2}.

(2)

Each fiber of
f
contains at most one critical point.

(3)

The boundary
\partial X
admits a decomposition into two codimension-
0
submanifolds
\partial_{v}X=f^{-1}(\partial E)
and
\partial_{h}X=\overline{\partial X\setminus\partial_{v}X}
, called the
vertical boundary
and the
horizontal boundary
of
X
with respect to
f
.

(4)

The restriction
f:\partial_{h}X\to E
is a
(2n-3)
-manifold bundle.

In general, a critical point of a smooth map admitting the local model above is called a
Lefschetz singularity
.
Let
f_{i}:X_{i}\to E
be a Lefschetz fibration (
i=0,1
), and
\Phi:X_{0}\to X_{1}
and
\Psi:E\to E
be diffeomorphisms.
The pair
(\Phi,\Psi)
is called an
isomorphism
from
f_{0}
to
f_{1}
if
\Psi\circ f_{0}=f_{1}\circ\Phi
holds.

Let
f:X\to D^{2}
be a Lefschetz fibration over the disk and
q_{0}\in D^{2}\setminus\operatorname{Critv}(f)
.
We denote the regular fiber
f^{-1}(q_{0})
by
W
.
Take a trivialization
\partial_{h}X\cong D^{2}\times\partial W
of a
(2n-3)
-manifold bundle
f:\partial_{h}X\to D^{2}
and a horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}\subset TX\setminus\operatorname{Crit}(f)
for
f
so that
\mathcal{H}
is equal to
\{0\}\oplus(TD^{2}\oplus\{0\})
in
\nu\partial_{h}X
under the identification
\nu\partial_{h}X\cong(-1,0]\times(D^{2}\times\partial W)
.
One can define the
monodromy representation

\theta_{f}:\pi_{1}(D^{2}\setminus\operatorname{Critv}(f),q_{0})\to\mathcal{M}_{\partial}(W)

by taking a parallel transport with respect to
\mathcal{H}
.

A system of simple paths
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\subset D^{2}
is called a
Hurwitz system
of
f
(from
q_{0}
) if it satisfies the following conditions.

(A)

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
are mutually disjoint except at the common initial point
q_{0}
.

(B)

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
appear in this order when one goes around
q_{0}
counterclockwise.

(C)

Each point in
\operatorname{Critv}(f)
is an end of one of
\gamma_{i}
’s.

(D)

Each
\gamma_{i}
is away from
\operatorname{Critv}(f)
except at one end of it.

For each path
\gamma_{i}
in a Hurwitz system of
f
, we take an element
\alpha_{i}\in\pi_{1}(D^{2}\setminus\operatorname{Critv}(f),q_{0})
as follows:
\alpha_{i}
is represented by a loop going along
\gamma_{i}
from
q_{0}
, going around the end of
\alpha_{i}
counterclockwise, and then going back to
q_{0}
along
\gamma_{i}
.
Such a loop or the corresponding element in
\pi_{1}(D^{2}\setminus\operatorname{Critv}(f),q_{0})
is called a
meridian loop
of
\gamma_{i}
.
It is known that the monodromy
\theta_{f}(\alpha_{i})
is the (generalized)
Dehn twist

t_{C_{i}}
along an
(n-1)
-sphere
C_{i}\subset W
.
The sphere
C_{i}
or its isotopy class is called a
vanishing cycle
of
f
associated with
\gamma_{i}
.
Since the product
\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m}\in\pi_{1}(D^{2}\setminus\operatorname{Critv}(f),q_{0})
represents a loop homotopic to
\partial D^{2}
, the monodromy along
\partial D^{2}
is equal to
t_{C_{1}}\cdots t_{C_{m}}
.
This factorization of the monodromy into Dehn twists is called a
monodromy factorization
of
f
.
Any sequence of Dehn twists can be realized as a monodromy factorization of a Lefschetz fibration.
Furthermore, the isomorphism class of a Lefschetz fibration over
D^{2}
is uniquely determined from the sequence of its vanishing cycles (
[
25
,
28
]
).
The following indeed holds. (See
[
28
]
for Lefschetz fibrations on
4
-manifolds.)

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
W
be a
(2n-2)
-manifold and
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
be
(n-1)
-spheres in
W
whose normal bundles are isomorphic to their cotangent bundles.
There exists a Lefschetz fibration
f:X\to D^{2}
on a
2n
-manifold
X
such that its regular fiber is diffeomorphic to
W
, and its monodromy factorization is
t_{C_{1}}\cdots t_{C_{m}}
.
Let
f_{i}:X_{i}\to D^{2}
be a Lefschetz fibration (
i=0,1
).
Suppose that
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
have the same monodromy factorization
t_{C_{1}}\cdots t_{C_{m}}
.
Let
\varphi_{j}^{i}:U_{j}^{i}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\psi_{j}^{i}:V_{j}^{i}\to\mathbb{C}
be orientation-preserving complex charts of the critical points and values of
f_{i}
associated with the vanishing cycle
C_{j}
such that
\psi_{j}^{i}\circ f_{i}\circ(\varphi_{j}^{i})^{-1}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=z_{1}^{2}+\cdots+z_{n}^{2}
.
There exists an isomorphism
(\Phi,\Psi)
from
f_{0}
to
f_{1}
such that
\Psi
preserves Hurwitz systems yielding the monodromy factorization up to isotopy,
\Phi|_{U_{j}^{0}}=(\varphi_{j}^{1})^{-1}\circ\varphi_{j}^{0}
, and
\Psi|_{V_{j}^{0}}=(\psi_{j}^{1})^{-1}\circ\psi_{j}^{0}
.

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a Lefschetz fibration over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a closed disk containing no critical values of
f
.
Put
X^{\prime}=X\setminus f^{-1}(\operatorname{Int}(D))
.
The restriction
f:X^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\operatorname{Int}(D)
is a Lefschetz fibration over a disk, in particular we obtain a monodromy factorization
t_{C_{1}}\cdots t_{C_{m}}
of this fibration, which we call a monodromy factorization of
f
.
Since the restriction
f:f^{-1}(\overline{D})\to\overline{D}
is a trivial
(2n-2)
-manifold bundle, the product
t_{C_{1}}\cdots t_{C_{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\partial}(W)
is contained in the kernel of the forgetting homomorphism
\mathcal{M}_{\partial}(W)\to\mathcal{M}(W)
.
Suppose that
X
is a closed
4
-manifold.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{i}\subset X
(
i=1,\ldots,b
) be a section of
f
with self-intersection
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\nu\mathcal{S}_{i}
be its tubular neighborhood.
The restriction
f:X\setminus(\bigsqcup_{i}\nu\mathcal{S}_{i})\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a Lefschetz fibration, whose regular fiber
W
is diffeomorphic to the genus-
g
surface with
b
boundary components for some
g\geq 0
.
It is known that the product
t_{C_{1}}\cdots t_{C_{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\partial}(W)
for this fibration is equal to
t_{\delta_{1}}^{-a_{i}}\cdots t_{\delta_{b}}^{-a_{b}}
, where
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{b}\subset W
are the simple closed curves parallel to the boundary components.

Let
X
be a closed
2n
-manifold and
B\subset X
be a codimension
4
submanifold.
A smooth map
f:X\setminus B\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is called a
Lefschetz pencil
if
B
admits a neighborhood
V\subset X
with a complex vector bundle
p:V\to B
compatible with the orientation of
X
such that the structure group of
p:V\to B
is
S^{1}
and
f|_{V}
is the projectivization on each fiber of
p
, and
f|_{X\setminus V}
is a Lefschetz fibration.
The submanifold
B
is called the
base locus
of
f
.
As explained in the introduction, we denote a Lefschetz pencil by
f:X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
when we do not need to represent its base locus explicitly.
Let
\widetilde{X}
be the blow-up of
X
along
B
and
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be the projection.
It is easy to see that there exists a Lefschetz fibration
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that the restriction
\widetilde{f}:{\widetilde{X}\setminus\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is equal to
f\circ\pi
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the exceptional divisor.
Let
W
be a regular fiber of the restriction
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\setminus\nu\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, whose boundary admits an
S^{1}
-bundle associated with
p:V\to B
.
It is known that the product
t_{C_{1}}\cdots t_{C_{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\partial}(W)
is represented by the
2\pi
counterclockwise rotation of the fibers of the
S^{1}
-bundle on
\partial W
(cf.
[
4
]
).
In the
4
-dimensional case (i.e.
n=2
), this product is equal to
t_{\delta_{1}}\cdots t_{\delta_{m}}
.

2.2.
Bifibration structures on
6
-manifolds and their monodromies

In this subsection, we introduce several bifibration structures on
6
-manifolds, which induce Lefschetz fibrations/pencils on
6
-manifolds and their
4
-dimensional regular fibers.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a
6
-manifold and
\rho:Z\to E
be either a
D^{2}
-bundle or a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
E=D^{2}
or
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Let
\partial_{v}Z=\rho^{-1}(\partial E)
and
\partial_{h}Z=\overline{\partial Z\setminus\rho^{-1}(\partial E)}
, which are called the vertical and horizontal boundaries of
Z
, respectively. (Note that
\partial_{v}Z=\emptyset
if
E=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
\partial_{h}Z=\emptyset
if
\rho
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle.)
A smooth map
F:X\to Z
is called a
Lefschetz bifibration
over
\rho:Z\to E
if it satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

For any
x\in\operatorname{Crit}(F)\cap\operatorname{Int}(X)
, one can take an orientation-preserving complex chart
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
(resp.
\psi:V\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
) around
x
(resp.
F(x)
) satisfying either

•

\psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=(z_{1},z_{2}^{2}+z_{3}^{2})
, or

•

\varphi(x)=0
,
\psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=(z_{1},z_{2}^{3}+z_{1}z_{2}+z_{3}^{2})
, and there exists an orientation-preserving complex chart
\psi^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}
around
\rho(F(x))\in E
such that
\psi^{\prime}\circ\rho\circ\psi^{-1}
is the projection to the first component.

The point
x\in\operatorname{Crit}(F)
satisfying the former (resp. the latter) condition is called a
fold
(resp. a
cusp
).
Let
\mathcal{C}_{F}
be the set of cusps and
C_{F}=F(\mathcal{C}_{F})\subset\operatorname{Critv}(F)
.

(2)

Each fiber of
F
contains at most two critical points.
Each double point
y\in\operatorname{Critv}(F)
is contained in
\operatorname{Int}(Z)
, and for such a
y
, one can take an orientation-preserving complex chart
\psi:V\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
(resp.
\psi^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}
) around
y
(resp.
\rho(y)
) so that
\psi^{\prime}\circ\rho\circ\psi^{-1}
is the projection to the first component, and
\psi(V\cup\operatorname{Critv}(F))
is either

(2.1)

\psi(V)\cap\bigl(\{(z,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}~|~z\in\mathbb{C}\}\cup\{(z,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}~|~z\in\mathbb{C}\}\bigr)

or

(2.2)

\psi(V)\cap\bigl(\{(z,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}~|~z\in\mathbb{C}\}\cup\{(z,\overline{z})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}~|~z\in\mathbb{C}\}\bigr).

Let
I_{F}^{+},I_{F}^{-}\subset\operatorname{Critv}(F)
be the sets of positive and negative double points,

Remark 4.1
.

The assumption
(g,b)\neq(0,2)
in
Theorem

2.7
is needed only in the construction of the Lefschetz bifibration
\widetilde{F}_{2}:\widetilde{X}_{2}\to\widetilde{Z}_{2}
(when we show that
\widetilde{F}_{1}:\partial_{vh}\widetilde{X}_{1}\to\partial\widetilde{Z}_{1}
is a trivial
\Sigma
-bundle).
In fact, this assumption can be removed by carefully considering the behavior of Lefschetz bifibrations at the horizontal boundaries in the proofs of
Theorems

2.6
and
2.7
.
More concretely, we glue Lefschetz bifibrations using lifts obtained from
Theorem

3.1
in the proofs.
It suffices to show that these lifts can be made trivial in a certain sense at the horizontal boundary.
Once this is achieved, it is shown that the structure group of
\widetilde{F}_{1}:\partial_{vh}\widetilde{X}_{1}\to\partial\widetilde{Z}_{1}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}_{\partial}(\Sigma)
.
Since a connected component of
\mathcal{D}_{\partial}(\Sigma)
is contractible even in the case
(g,b)=(0,2)
, it follows that
\widetilde{F}_{1}:\partial_{vh}\widetilde{X}_{1}\to\partial\widetilde{Z}_{1}
is trivial, as shown in the original proof.

To carry out the above argument, it is necessary to refine the discussion in the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.
Such a refinement is indeed possible; however, in the case of
(g,b)=(0,2)
, the resulting examples are not particularly interesting.
Therefore, for the sake of overall conciseness, the detailed explanation required to cover this case is omitted.

Proof.

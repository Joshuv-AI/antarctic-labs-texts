Necessity of Cooperative Transmissions for Wireless MapReduce
Yue Bi 1 , Michèle Wigger 1

Abstract
The paper presents an improved upper bound (achievability result) on the optimal tradeoff between Normalized Delivery Time (NDT) and computation load for distributed computing MapReduce systems in certain ranges of the parameters. The upper bound is based on interference alignment combined with zero-forcing. The paper further provides a lower bound (converse) on the optimal NDT-computation tradeoff that can be achieved when IVAs are partitioned into sub-IVAs, and these sub-IVAs are then transmitted (in an arbitrary form) by a single node, without cooperation among nodes. For appropriate linear functions (e.g., XORs), such non-cooperative schemes can achieve some of the best NDT-computation tradeoff points so far obtained in the literature. However, as our lower bound shows, any non-cooperative scheme achieves a worse NDT-computation tradeoff than our new proposed scheme for certain parameters, thus proving the necessity of cooperative schemes like zero-forcing to attain the optimal NDT-computation tradeoff.



I
Introduction

MapReduce systems are becoming important building blocks also in wireless scenarios because they allow us to distribute heavy computation tasks over multiple distributed nodes. Important performance parameters of such systems are the
computation load

\mathsf{r}
, which characterizes the average amount of data stored at the nodes (i.e., memory requirements), and the
Normalized Delivery Time (NDT)

\Delta
, which characterizes the duration of the wireless shuffle phase (i.e., communication requirements). The optimal tradeoff between memory requirements and communication duration was first characterized in
[
8
]
, but under the assumption of perfect multicast communication links. The optimal scheme for this noiseless setup splits intermediate values (IVA) into message parts and then communicates appropriate XORs of these parts. Decoding requires that receiving nodes use their knowledge of the message parts transmitted by other nodes to cancel non-desired parts of the XORs.
Similar assumptions of ideal multicast or noiseless communication links have also been adopted in several related works, e.g.,
[
11
,
10
,
9
]
.

Several subsequent works
[
7
,
2
,
3
,
12
]
have studied the
NDT-computation tradeoff
of
wireless
MapReduce, i.e., the smallest NDT that can be achieved under a constraint on the computation load. The problem was first investigated for wireless interference networks in
[
7
]
, which proposed a coding scheme based on one-shot beamforming and zero-forcing (ZF). In our previous works in
[
2
,
3
]
, we obtained improved NDT-computation tradeoffs by introducing interference alignment (IA)
[
4
,
6
]
to the shuffle phase. For computation loads
\mathsf{r}=\lfloor(\mathsf{K}-1)/2\rfloor
, where
\mathsf{K}
denotes the number of nodes and is assumed to be odd, our work in
[
3
]
also proposes an improved IA scheme based on ZF. Notice that ZF introduces complex dependencies between IA matrices, rendering performance analysis of IA schemes involved
[
1
]
.

In this paper, we improve our IA & ZF scheme in
[
3
]
. Our new scheme applies to an arbitrary number of nodes
\mathsf{K}
but still requires that
\mathsf{r}=\lfloor(\mathsf{K}-1)/2\rfloor
. For odd values of
\mathsf{K}
it strictly improves over our previous scheme in
[
3
]
. The new scheme is again based on IA & ZF and the analysis requires proving algebraic independence of certain functions. We provide an analytic proof for
\mathsf{K}=5
in this paper as well as numeric verification for
\mathsf{K}\in\{6,\ldots,15\}
.

Inspired by the excellent performance of the scheme in
[
8
]
and to avoid complicated analyses, several schemes (also for related cache-aided scenarios)
[
5
,
2
,
3
]
focused on exploiting the multicast behaviour of wireless MapReduce communication and the IVA side-information at the nodes, but without any form of cooperation between transmit signals. Specifically, the schemes divide the IVAs into submessages and each submessage is transmitted (in an arbitrary form) by only one of the nodes, without cooperation from the other nodes. Transmit signals across nodes are thus independent of each other. Nodes still use all their side-information about IVAs in their decoding steps. In this work, we provide a lower bound (converse result) on the NDT-computation tradeoff that can be achieved by any such non-cooperative scheme. We further show that this lower bound lies above the NDT-computation tradeoffs obtained by our new IA & ZF scheme, thus proving strict suboptimality of non-cooperative schemes. Notice that the previously obtained NDT-computation tradeoffs do not improve over the non-cooperative lower bound and do not allow us to obtain the desired conclusion.

II
Wireless MapReduce Framework

Consider a wireless MapReduce system with
\mathsf{K}\geq 5
nodes labeled
1,\ldots,\mathsf{K}
, with
\mathsf{M}
input files
W_{1},\ldots,W_{\mathsf{M}}
, and with
\mathsf{K}
output functions
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{\mathsf{K}}
, where function
\phi_{k}
is assigned to Node
k
.
The system follows the MapReduce framework, in which each output function is decomposed as

\phi_{q}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{\mathsf{M}})=v_{q}(a_{q,1},\ldots,a_{q,\mathsf{M}}),\quad q\in[\mathsf{K}],

(1)

where

a_{q,p}=u_{q,p}(W_{p}),\quad p\in[\mathsf{M}],

(2)

denotes the intermediate value (IVA) computed from file
W_{p}
.
All IVAs are assumed independent and consist of
\mathsf{A}
i.i.d. bits.

The MapReduce framework has 3 phases:

Map phase

Each node
k
is assigned a subset of files
\mathcal{M}_{k}\subseteq[\mathsf{M}]
and computes the IVAs

\{a_{q,p}:p\in\mathcal{M}_{k},\ q\in[\mathsf{K}]\}
.

Shuffle phase

Nodes exchange IVAs over
\mathsf{n}
uses of a wireless channel in full-duplex mode.
Node
k
transmits

{\mathbf{X}}_{k}=f_{k}^{(\mathsf{n})}\!\left(\{a_{1,p},\ldots,a_{\mathsf{K},p}\}_{p\in\mathcal{M}_{k}}\right),

(3)

subject to the average power constraint

\frac{1}{\mathsf{n}}\sum_{t=1}^{\mathsf{n}}\mathbb{E}[|X_{k}(t)|^{2}]\leq\mathsf{P}.

(4)

Node
k
observes

Y_{k}(t)=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\{k\}}H_{k,k^{\prime}}(t)X_{k^{\prime}}(t)+Z_{k}(t),\quad t\in[\mathsf{n}],

(5)

and
{\mathbf{Y}}_{k}\triangleq\left(Y_{k}(1)\dots,Y_{k}(\mathsf{n})\right)
, where
\{H_{k,k^{\prime}}(t)\}
and
\{Z_{k}(t)\}
are i.i.d. over time and across node pairs. Each coefficient
H_{p,q}(t)
has independent and identically distributed real and imaginary parts, following a specified continuous distribution over a bounded interval
[-\mathsf{H}_{\max},\mathsf{H}_{\max}]
for some positive integer
\mathsf{H}_{\max}
.
All nodes have perfect channel state information.

Based on
{\mathbf{Y}}_{k}
and the IVAs computed locally, Node
k
decodes the missing IVAs as

\hat{a}_{k,p}=g_{k,p}^{(\mathsf{n})}\!\left(\{a_{q,i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{M}_{k},q\in[\mathsf{K}]},{\mathbf{Y}}_{k}\right),\quad p\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}.

(6)

Reduce phase

Each node
k\in[\mathsf{K}]
applies its reduce function
v_{k}
to its available IVAs to compute its assigned output function
\phi_{k}
.

The performance of the distributed computing system is measured by two factors:
computation load
and
normalized delivery time (NDT)
.
The computation load is defined as

\mathsf{r}\triangleq\sum_{k\in[\mathsf{K}]}\frac{|\mathcal{M}_{k}|}{\mathsf{M}},

(7)

and NDT is defined as

\mathsf{\Delta}\triangleq\varliminf_{\mathsf{P}\to\infty}\varliminf_{\mathsf{A}\to\infty}\frac{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{A}\,\mathsf{K}\,\mathsf{M}}\log\mathsf{P}.

(8)

We define the optimal NDT–computation tradeoff
\Delta^{*}(\mathsf{r})
as the infimum of all
\mathsf{\Delta}

for which there exist file assignments
\{\mathcal{M}_{k}\}
and encoding/decoding functions
satisfying

\Pr\!\left[\bigcup_{k\in[\mathsf{K}]}\bigcup_{p\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}}\hat{a}_{k,p}\neq a_{k,p}\right]\to 0\quad\text{as }\mathsf{A}\to\infty.

(9)

In this work, we choose a the well known combinatorial file assignment for the wireless MapReduce system.
Specifically, the input files
\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{\mathsf{M}}\}
are partitioned into
\binom{\mathsf{K}}{\mathsf{r}}
disjoint bundles, each associated with a size-
\mathsf{r}
subset
\mathcal{T}\in[[\mathsf{K}]]^{\mathsf{r}}
and stored at the nodes in
\mathcal{T}
.
Since each file is stored at exactly
\mathsf{r}
nodes, this assignment satisfies the computation load constraint.

II-A

Relation with
\mathsf{r}
-fold cooperative channel

Under this combinatorial file assignment, the Shuffle phase naturally induces a wireless channel with
\mathsf{r}
-fold transmitter cooperation.
In particular, consider the wireless interference channel described in (
5
) under the average power constraint (
4
).
For a given
\mathsf{r}\in[\mathsf{K}]
, any subset of
\mathsf{r}
nodes
\mathcal{T}\in[[\mathsf{K}]]^{\mathsf{r}}
jointly holds a message
a_{k,\mathcal{T}}
intended for Node
k
, for each
k\in[\mathsf{K}]\setminus\mathcal{T}
.
Each message
a_{k,\mathcal{T}}
is uniformly distributed over
\{1,\ldots,2^{\mathsf{n}R_{k,\mathcal{T}}}\}
.

A rate tuple
(R_{k,\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{T}\in[[\mathsf{K}]]^{\mathsf{r}},\ k\in[\mathsf{K}]\setminus\mathcal{T})
is said to be achievable if there exist encoding and decoding functions such that the decoding error probabilities vanish as
\mathsf{n}\to\infty
.
The corresponding sum degrees of freedom is defined as

\mathrm{SDoF}(\mathsf{r})\triangleq\lim_{P\to\infty}\sup_{{\mathbf{R}}\in\mathcal{C}(P)}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\left[[\mathsf{K}]\right]^{\mathsf{r}}}\sum_{k\in[K]\setminus\mathcal{T}}\frac{R_{k,\mathcal{T}}}{\log P}.

(10)

where the supremum is over all achievable rate tuples under power constraint
\mathsf{P}
.

We have the following lemma, which establishes a direct connection between the NDT and the SDoF
[
3
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Under the combinatorial file assignment,
for any
\mathsf{r}\in[\mathsf{K}]
,

\mathsf{\Delta}(\mathsf{r})=\left(1-\frac{\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{K}}\right)\frac{1}{\mathrm{SDoF}(\mathsf{r})}.

(11)

IV
The New IA & ZF Scheme

IV-A

A scheme for
\mathsf{r}=\lfloor(\mathsf{K}-1)/2\rfloor

Each message is cooperatively transmitted by a size-
\mathsf{r}
set of nodes
\mathcal{T}
so that it is received at a given node
k\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\mathcal{T}
while zero-forced at a group
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathsf{r}-1
nodes in
[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\{\mathcal{T}\cup\{k\}\}
. There is a set
\mathcal{L}\subset[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}\cup\{k\}\}
of
\mathsf{K}-(\mathsf{r}+\mathsf{r}-1+1)=\mathsf{K}-2\mathsf{r}
nodes where the signal is experienced as interference.
We choose to precode all messages that cause interference at a given set of Nodes
\mathcal{L}
by the same precoding matrix
{\mathbf{U}}_{\mathcal{L}}
, for
\mathcal{L}\in[\mathsf{K}]
, with
|\mathcal{L}|=\mathsf{K}-2\mathsf{r}
.
We want to construct the precoding matrix
{\mathbf{U}}_{\ell}
so that all interferences at the same set of nodes
\mathcal{L}
align, thus leaving the remaining space for signaling dimensions.
To summarize, if we use precoding matrix
{\mathbf{U}}_{\mathcal{L}}
for the transmission of a message from group
\mathcal{T}
to Node
k
, then we zero-force this signal at all nodes in
[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\{\mathcal{T}\cup\mathcal{L}\cup\{k\}\}
. We ensure that this signal is aligned with all other interference signals precoded with
{\mathbf{U}}_{\mathcal{L}}
at the nodes
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
, which are the only nodes where it causes interference.

Based on the above design principle, the key design choice in our scheme is the assignment of a precoding matrix to each message. Equivalently, for each message, we select the set of nodes
\mathcal{L}
at which this message causes interference, and assign the corresponding precoding matrix
{\mathbf{U}}_{\mathcal{L}}
. This assignment fully determines both the interference alignment structure and the zero-forcing constraints of the scheme.

IV-B

Analysis of SDoF

If all channel coefficients after zero-forcing that multiply the same precoding matrix are algebraically independent, an IA coding scheme can be constructed by following the framework in
[
3
]
and applying the precoding matrix assignment method in Algorithm
1
.
For the case
\mathsf{K}=5
, the desired algebraic independence is proved analytically in the next subsection.

For larger values of
\mathsf{K}
however, the analysis becomes considerably more complicated and we verified algebraic independence directly by computing the Jacobian matrix using
Matlab
2
2

2

The code is at: https://github.com/yue-bi/wdc-jacobian-verification.
. This verification has been carried out for all
\mathsf{K}\in\{6,\ldots,15\}
. This range is chosen due to the computational complexity of the symbolic Jacobian computation.

To evaluate the SDoF achieved by our scheme, we notice that the DoF per node is given by the ratio between the dimension of the desired signal subspace and the dimension of the desired signal subspace and interference subspace. For sufficiently large message lengths, the ratio between the dimensions of the desired signal subspace and the interference subspace asymptotically equals the ratio between the number of codewords intended for a given Node and the number of distinct precoding matrices in the interference space.

\mathsf{r}
matrices when

is even, and with

matrices when

is odd. This implies that for a node

, the precoding matrix

encodes
\mathsf{r}

k

\mathsf{K}
is even, and it encodes
\mathsf{K}-2
messages intended to Node
k

is odd.
Since

Meanwhile, Node

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\binom{\mathsf{K}-1}{\mathsf{K}-2\mathsf{r}}\mathsf{r}}{\binom{\mathsf{K}-1}{\mathsf{K}-2\mathsf{r}}\mathsf{r}+\binom{\mathsf{K}-1}{\mathsf{K}-2\mathsf{r}-1}}=\frac{(\mathsf{K}-2)^{2}}{(\mathsf{K}-2)^{2}+4},&\mathsf{K}\text{ even,}\\[2.15277pt]

The achievable SDoF can be obtained by mutiplying the above results by

IV-C

Independence and Calculation of Jacobian matrix

We wish to verify the algebraic independence among all channel coefficient
{\mathbf{G}}_{k,\mathcal{T}}^{(p^{\prime})}
that premultiply the matrix
{\mathbf{U}}_{5}
. Since the coefficients across different times
t\in[\mathsf{n}]
are independent, we only need to analyze the independence between coefficients
g_{k,\mathcal{T}}^{(p^{\prime})}(t)
for a fixed
t
. For simplicity, we omit the index t.

We thus consider the Jacobian matrix

{\mathbf{J}}=\left(\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}}{\partial({\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{h}})}\right),

(28)

where
{\mathbf{g}}
denotes the vector of all coefficients
g_{k,\mathcal{T}}^{(p^{\prime})}
,
{\mathbf{s}}
denotes the vector of random scalars
s_{k,\mathcal{T}}
, and
{\mathbf{h}}
denotes the original channel coefficients.

We partition the Jacobian matrix according to the variables
({\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{h}})
and the nature of the coefficients as

{\mathbf{J}}=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{intf}}}{\partial{\mathbf{s}}}&\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{intf}}}{\partial{\mathbf{h}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{use}}}{\partial{\mathbf{s}}}&\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{use}}}{\partial{\mathbf{h}}}\end{pmatrix},

where
{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{intf}}
and
{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{use}}
denote the interference-related and useful coefficients, respectively.

A key observation is that the two blocks in the first column,

\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{intf}}}{\partial{\mathbf{s}}}
and

\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{use}}}{\partial{\mathbf{s}}}
,
exhibit a diagonal structure. Indeed, each scalar
s_{k,\mathcal{T}}
appears in exactly two coefficients generated by the same message. For instance, the coefficients

g_{3,(1,2)}^{(3)}
and
g_{3,(1,2)}^{(5)}
are the only terms containing the factor
s_{3,(1,2)}
, and their partial derivatives satisfy

\frac{\partial g_{3,(1,2)}^{(3)}}{\partial s_{3,(1,2)}}=\mu_{(3,4)(1,2)},\qquad\frac{\partial g_{3,(1,2)}^{(5)}}{\partial s_{3,(1,2)}}=\mu_{(5,4)(1,2)}.

Exploiting this structure, we apply Gaussian elimination to eliminate the lower-left block

\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{use}}}{\partial{\mathbf{s}}}
.
Specifically, we perform the row operation

{\mathbf{J}}\!\left[g_{3,(1,2)}^{(3)},:\right]\leftarrow{\mathbf{J}}\!\left[g_{3,(1,2)}^{(3)},:\right]-\frac{\mu_{(3,4)(1,2)}}{\mu_{(5,4)(1,2)}}{\mathbf{J}}\!\left[g_{3,(1,2)}^{(5)},:\right],

which sets the entry
{\mathbf{J}}[g_{3,(1,2)}^{(3)},s_{3,(1,2)}]
to zero. As a result, the column corresponding to
s_{3,(1,2)}
contains a single nonzero entry, located in the row associated with
g_{3,(1,2)}^{(5)}
.

Repeating this Gaussian elimination procedure for all pairs of coefficients sharing the same subscript
(k,\mathcal{T})
, the Jacobian matrix is transformed into an upper block-triangular form

{\mathbf{J}}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{intf}}}{\partial{\mathbf{s}}}&\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{intf}}}{\partial{\mathbf{h}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\mathbf{0}}&\displaystyle{\mathbf{J}}_{h}\end{pmatrix}.

Consequently, after Gaussian elimination, the rank of the full Jacobian matrix is determined by the remaining lower-right block
{\mathbf{J}}_{h}
.

We begin by specifying the grouping of the coefficients
g
and the channel
coefficients
h
, which induces a natural block structure in the Jacobian
matrix.
The coefficients
g_{k,\mathcal{T}}^{(k)}
are ordered and partitioned into four groups. Specifically, the groups are defined as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}_{1}

\displaystyle=(g_{1,(2,3)}^{(1)},\ g_{2,(3,4)}^{(2)},\ g_{3,(1,4)}^{(3)},\ g_{4,(1,2)}^{(4)}),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}_{2}

\displaystyle=(g_{1,(3,4)}^{(1)},\ g_{2,(1,4)}^{(2)},\ g_{3,(1,2)}^{(3)},\ g_{4,(2,3)}^{(4)}),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}_{3}

\displaystyle=(g_{2,(1,3)}^{(2)},\ g_{4,(1,3)}^{(4)}),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}_{4}

\displaystyle=(g_{1,(2,4)}^{(1)},\ g_{3,(2,4)}^{(3)}).

The channel coefficients are grouped into four vectors as well

\displaystyle{\mathbf{h}}_{1}

\displaystyle=(h_{12},h_{23},h_{34},h_{41}),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{h}}_{2}

\displaystyle=(h_{13},h_{24},h_{31},h_{42}),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{h}}_{3}

\displaystyle=(h_{21},h_{43}),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{h}}_{4}

\displaystyle=(h_{32},h_{14}).

According to the grouping of coefficients
g
and channel variables
h
, the matrix
{\mathbf{J}}_{h}
is partitioned into blocks

{\mathbf{J}}_{h}=\left[{\mathbf{J}}_{i,j}\right]_{i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\}},

(29)

where
{\mathbf{J}}_{i,j}\triangleq\partial{\mathbf{G}}_{i}/\partial{\mathbf{h}}_{j}.

This grouping is designed to align the dependence structure between the coefficients
g
and the channel variables
h
, so that the Jacobian matrix admits a nearly block upper-triangular structure after suitable row and column permutations. Moreover, each diagonal block is sparse and has nonzero entries only on the main diagonal and on the immediately right-adjacent positions, with all other entries being zero. This structure enables a blockwise analysis of the Jacobian determinant via its diagonal blocks.

We first focus on the diagonal block

{\mathbf{J}}_{22}=\frac{\partial{\mathbf{G}}_{2}}{\partial{\mathbf{h}}_{2}},

where

{\mathbf{G}}_{2}=\{g_{1,(3,4)}^{(1)},\ g_{2,(1,4)}^{(2)},\ g_{3,(1,2)}^{(3)},\ g_{4,(2,3)}^{(4)}\}.

Each coefficient
g_{k,\mathcal{T}}^{(k)}
is a scaled
2\times 2
minor of the channel
matrix and therefore depends on exactly two channel coefficients.
Consequently, in
{\mathbf{J}}_{22}
, each column contains exactly two nonzero
entries, which appear in the same two rows.
The corresponding Jacobian block is written in (
30
).

{\mathbf{J}}_{22}=\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}&h_{13}&h_{24}&h_{31}&h_{42}\\
\hline\cr g_{1,(34)}^{(1)}&h_{24}&h_{13}-\dfrac{\mu_{(12)(34)}}{\mu_{(52)(34)}}\,h_{53}&0&0\\
g_{2,(14)}^{(2)}&0&h_{31}&h_{24}-\dfrac{\mu_{(23)(14)}}{\mu_{(53)(14)}}\,h_{54}&0\\
g_{3,(12)}^{(3)}&0&0&h_{42}&h_{31}-\dfrac{\mu_{(34)(12)}}{\mu_{(54)(12)}}\,h_{51}\\
g_{4,(23)}^{(4)}&\;h_{42}-\dfrac{\mu_{(14)(23)}}{\mu_{(54)(23)}}\,h_{52}&0&0&h_{13}\end{array}

(30)

The determinant is non-zeros almost surely as the it is the product of terms on the diagonal minus the product of terms on the superdiagonal.

After eliminating the rows and columns corresponding to
{\mathbf{G}}_{2}
and
{\mathbf{h}}_{2}
, the remaining coefficients are

g_{2,(1,3)}^{(2)},\quad g_{4,(1,3)}^{(4)},\quad g_{1,(2,4)}^{(1)},\quad g_{3,(2,4)}^{(3)}.

We next consider the diagonal block

{\mathbf{J}}_{33}\triangleq\frac{\partial(g_{2,(1,3)}^{(2)},\,g_{4,(1,3)}^{(4)})}{\partial(h_{21},\,h_{41})},

which corresponds to the coefficient group
{\mathbf{G}}_{3}
and channel group

{\mathbf{h}}_{3}
.
It is given by

{\mathbf{J}}_{33}=\begin{array}[]{c|cc}&h_{21}&h_{41}\\
\hline\cr g_{2,(1,3)}^{(2)}&h_{23}&-\dfrac{\mu_{(5)(1,3)}}{\mu_{(5)(2,3)}}\,h_{52}\\[8.00003pt]
g_{4,(1,3)}^{(4)}&-\dfrac{\mu_{(5)(2,3)}}{\mu_{(5)(1,3)}}\,h_{51}&h_{43}\end{array}

whose determinant is nonzero almost surely. Meanwhile, we can show that the matrices
{\mathbf{J}}_{32}
and
{\mathbf{J}}_{34}
are both zero-matrices. This is because both channel coefficients in

are from Nodes

and

and
3

Similar analyses apply also to the 4th line of the block matrix, which means
{\mathbf{J}}_{44}
also has a nonzero determinant almost surely, and
{\mathbf{J}}_{42}={\mathbf{0}}
and
{\mathbf{J}}_{43}={\mathbf{0}}
. Therefore, the Jacobian matrix is equivalent to

{\mathbf{J}}_{h}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{J}}_{11}&{\mathbf{J}}_{12}&{\mathbf{J}}_{13}&{\mathbf{J}}_{14}\\
{\mathbf{J}}_{21}&{\mathbf{J}}_{22}&{\mathbf{J}}_{23}&{\mathbf{J}}_{24}\\
{\mathbf{J}}_{31}&0&{\mathbf{J}}_{33}&0\\
{\mathbf{J}}_{41}&0&0&{\mathbf{J}}_{44}\end{bmatrix}.

(32)

Since the diagonal blocks
{\mathbf{J}}_{22}
,
{\mathbf{J}}_{33}
, and
{\mathbf{J}}_{44}
have been shown to be nonsingular almost surely, one may apply Gaussian elimination to eliminate the blocks
{\mathbf{J}}_{21}
,
{\mathbf{J}}_{31}
, and
{\mathbf{J}}_{41}
, using
{\mathbf{J}}_{22}
,
{\mathbf{J}}_{33}
, and

as pivots, respectively.

Although
{\mathbf{J}}_{22}
is nonsingular almost surely, explicitly computing their inverse matrices is complex. Instead, we again invoke a polynomial argument. Since all entries of the Jacobian matrix are polynomial functions of the channel coefficients, the determinant
\det({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime})
is itself a polynomial. To show that it is not identically zero, it suffices to exhibit one admissible channel realization for which
\det({\mathbf{J}}_{h})\neq 0
.

In particular, consider a channel realization with
{\mathbf{s}}={\mathbf{1}}
,
[{\mathbf{h}}_{5,1},\dots h_{5,4}]={\mathbf{1}}
. We further impose that all superdiagonal entries of
{\mathbf{J}}_{22}
vanish
while the diagonal entries remain nonzero. This condition is equivalent to the following set of equations:

\displaystyle-h_{13}-\frac{h_{13}h_{24}-h_{14}h_{23}}{h_{31}-h_{34}}

\displaystyle=0,

(33)

\displaystyle h_{24}+\frac{h_{21}h_{34}-h_{24}h_{31}}{h_{41}-h_{42}}

\displaystyle=0,

(34)

\displaystyle-h_{31}-\frac{h_{31}h_{42}-h_{32}h_{41}}{h_{12}-h_{13}}

\displaystyle=0,

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{12}h_{43}-h_{13}h_{42}}{h_{23}-h_{24}}-h_{42}

\displaystyle=0.

(36)

The system of equations (
33
)–(
36
) can be solved for the variables
h_{14}
,
h_{21}
,
h_{32}
, and
h_{43}
as functions of the remaining channel coefficients.
Under this choice,
{\mathbf{J}}_{22}
becomes diagonal, and its determinant reduces to the product of its diagonal entries, which is nonzero.
Having fixed
{\mathbf{J}}_{22}
according to the above construction, we may further assign simple

{\mathbf{J}}_{22}

\det({\mathbf{J}})=\det\left(\frac{\partial{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathrm{intf}}}{\partial{\mathbf{s}}}\right)\cdot\det({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{11})\cdot\prod_{i=2}^{4}\det({\mathbf{J}}_{ii}),

(40)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we choose

This leads to

Notice that the sets
\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r}+1},\ldots,\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},\mathsf{K}}

(45)

Fix
\mathsf{P}>0
and any rate tuple in
\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})
. Consider encoding and decoding functions
\{f_{q}^{(\mathsf{n})}\}
and
\{g^{(\mathsf{n})}\}
such that

\mathsf{n}\to\infty

Fix a blocklength
\mathsf{n}
and set
\mathcal{F}\triangleq\{3,\ldots,\mathsf{K}\}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the collection of all channel coefficients.
For any

By independence of all messages, channel coefficients, and noise sequences,

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{V}\mid\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H})

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{V};\mathbf{Y}_{2}\mid\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H})+H(\mathcal{V}\mid\mathbf{Y}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\mathsf{n}\cdot\log\!\bigl(1+(\mathsf{K}-1)\mathsf{P}\mathsf{H}_{\max}^{2}\bigr)+H(\mathcal{V}\mid\mathbf{Y}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H}).

To this end, start by decomposing the entropy term as

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}}\mid\mathbf{Y}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H})+H(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx}}\mid\mathbf{Y}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\mathcal{H}).

\epsilon_{\mathsf{n}}\to 0
as
\mathsf{n}\to\infty
.

For the second term, we write

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx}}\mid\mathbf{Y}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx}}\mid\mathbf{Y}_{2},\mathbf{Y}_{\mathcal{F}},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\mathcal{H})

(50)

\displaystyle+I(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx}};\mathbf{Y}_{\mathcal{F}}\mid\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\mathbf{Y}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H}),

and observe that

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx}};\mathbf{Y}_{\mathcal{F}}\mid\mathcal{V}^{r}{\textnormal{Rx}}\mathbf{Y}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H})\qquad

(51)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle h(\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}}\mid\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H})-h(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle h(\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}}\mid\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H},E=1)-h(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}})

(52)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H},E=1)-h(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}})

(53)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})-h(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}})+\mathsf{n}\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{n}},

(54)

where
\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{n}}
is a sequence that vanishes in
\mathsf{n}
and the (in)equalities are justified as follows:

•

In
(a)
, we define

\tilde{{\mathbf{Y}}}_{p}\triangleq{\mathbf{H}}_{p,t}{\mathbf{X}}_{t}+{\mathbf{Z}}_{p},\quad p\in[\mathsf{K}].

and
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{S}}\triangleq(\tilde{{\mathbf{Y}}}_{p}:p\in\mathcal{S})
. Since
\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx}}
is carried only by the input of Node
t
, conditioning on
(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H})
removes all other signal components, yielding the equality in
(a)
.

•

In
(b)
, we introduce the binary random variable
E
, which equals
1
if for all
t^{\prime}\in[\mathsf{n}]
, the channel coefficient
H_{j,t}(t^{\prime})\neq 0
. Notice that
\Pr[E=1]=1
since the channel coefficients are drawn independently from continuous distributions.

•

To see
(c)
, define for each
p\in\mathcal{F}
the noise-modified variables

{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\prime}\triangleq{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}-{\mathbf{H}}_{p,t}{\mathbf{H}}_{j,t}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{j},

and
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\triangleq({\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\prime}\colon p\in\mathcal{F})
. Notice that in the event
E=1
, the signal component of
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}}
can be perfectly canceled, leaving only the effective noise
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
.

We finally show

\displaystyle H\left(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx}}\mid{\mathbf{Y}}_{2},{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{H}\right)

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{\mathsf{K}}H\left(\mathcal{V}^{{\textnormal{Tx}},i}\mid{\mathbf{Y}}_{2},{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{V}^{{\textnormal{Tx}},3},\dots,\mathcal{V}^{{\textnormal{Tx}},i-1},\mathcal{H}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{\mathsf{K}}\epsilon_{i,\mathsf{n}}\mathsf{n},

where
\epsilon_{i,\mathsf{n}}
denotes a vanishing sequence for each
i
and the last inequalty holds by the following considerations.

For example, notice that the sub-IVAs known to node 3, i.e.,
\{a_{p,\mathcal{T}}^{q}\colon 3\in\mathcal{T}\}
are all contained in the set
(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}}\cup\bar{\mathcal{V}})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},3}
only contains sub-IVAs intended for node 3. Since
{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}}
contains
{\mathbf{Y}}_{3}
, we thus have by Fano’s Inequality equality:

H\left(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},3}\mid{\mathbf{Y}}_{2},{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}},\bar{\mathcal{V}}\right)\leq\epsilon_{3,\mathsf{n}}\mathsf{n},

(57)

for a sequence
\epsilon_{3,\mathsf{n}}\to 0
as
\mathsf{n}\to\infty
.

As noticed in (
45
), for each
i=4,\ldots,\mathsf{K}
,
the sub-IVAs known to Node
i
, i.e.,
\{a_{p,\mathcal{T}}^{q}\colon i\in\mathcal{T}\}
, are contained in the set
(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}}\cup\bar{\mathcal{V}}\cup\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},3}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},i-1})
. Since
\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},i}
only contains sub-IVAs intended for node
i
and
{\mathbf{Y}}_{i}
is contained in
{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}}
, we have by Fano’s inequality:

H\left(\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},i}\mid{\mathbf{Y}}_{2},{\mathbf{Y}}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{V}^{r},\bar{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},3},\ldots,\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},i-1}\right)\leq\epsilon_{i,\mathsf{n}}\mathsf{n},

(58)

for a sequence
\epsilon_{i,\mathsf{n}}\to 0
as
\mathsf{n}\to\infty
. This proves (
56
). Combining (
47
)–(
56
), we obtain

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathsf{n}\cdot\log\bigl(1+(\mathsf{K}-1)\mathsf{P}\mathsf{H}_{\max}^{2}\bigr)+\mathsf{n}\,\epsilon_{\mathsf{n}}

(59)

\displaystyle+h(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})-h(\mathbf{Z}_{\mathcal{F}})+\mathsf{n}\,\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{n}}.

Dividing by
\mathsf{n}\log\mathsf{P}
and letting
\mathsf{n}\to\infty
and
\mathsf{P}\to\infty

establishes (
38
).
∎

The proof is obtained by summing the upper bound in Lemma
2
over all index pairs
(t,j)\in[\mathsf{K}]
with
t\neq j
, and then normalizing.
Each rate term appears the same number of times, namely

\frac{\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{K}-1)|\mathcal{V}|}{|\mathcal{V}_{\textnormal{total}}|},

(60)

and since we have
\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{K}-1)
bounds, we obtain:

\mathbf{SDoF}\leq\frac{|\mathcal{V}_{\textnormal{total}}|}{|\mathcal{V}|}.

(61)

We have

|\mathcal{V}_{\textnormal{total}}|=\mathsf{r}\binom{\mathsf{K}}{\mathsf{r}}(\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r}).

(62)

To compute the size of
\mathcal{V}
, notice that for each
i\leq\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r}
, the size of
\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},\,i}
is

|\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx},i}|=\binom{\mathsf{K}-i}{\mathsf{r}-1},

(63)

because to complete the transmit set
\mathcal{T}
,
\mathsf{r}-1
additional indices need to be chosen from the
\mathsf{K}-i
possible elements

[\mathsf{K}]\setminus\{1,2,\dots,i\}=\{i+1,\dots,K\}.

Using the Hockey-stick identity, we obtain

|\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Tx}}|=\sum_{i=3}^{\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r}+1}\binom{\mathsf{K}-i}{\mathsf{r}-1}=\binom{\mathsf{K}-2}{\mathsf{r}}.

(64)

Moreover,

|\mathcal{V}^{\textnormal{Rx}}|=\binom{\mathsf{K}-1}{\mathsf{r}}\cdot\mathsf{r},

(65)

and
thus

|\mathcal{V}|=\binom{\mathsf{K}-2}{\mathsf{r}}+\binom{\mathsf{K}-1}{\mathsf{r}}\cdot\mathsf{r}.

(66)

Combining (
61
) with (
62
) and (
66
), we obtain

\mathbf{SDoF}\leq\frac{\mathsf{r}\binom{\mathsf{K}}{\mathsf{r}}(\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r})}{\binom{\mathsf{K}-2}{\mathsf{r}}+\binom{\mathsf{K}-1}{\mathsf{r}}\cdot\mathsf{r}}=\frac{\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{K}-1)\mathsf{r}}{(\mathsf{K}-2)\mathsf{r}+\mathsf{K}-1}.

(67)

VI
Conclusion

We proposed a new wireless MapReduce coding scheme based on interference alignement (IA) and zero-forcing (ZF) for computation loads
\mathsf{r}=\lfloor(\mathsf{K}-1)/2\rfloor
and show that it improves over all previous achievable NDTs. We further provide a converse result (lower bound on the NDT) for all non-cooperative coding schemes where IVAs are split into submessages and these submessages are transmitted (in an arbitrary fashion) by a single node only, thus preventing cooperation between nodes. This converse result lies above the NDT of our new IA & ZF scheme, thus proving strict suboptimality of non-cooperative transmissions for wireless MapReduce.

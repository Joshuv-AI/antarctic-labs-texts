A note on the tetrahedral index and the Hahn-Exton q q -Bessel function
Daniele Celoria

Abstract
. The purpose of this short note is twofold: First to elucidate some connections between the “building block” of Dimofte–Gaiotto–Gukov’s 3 3 D index, known as the tetrahedral index I Δ ​ ( m , e ) I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right) , and Hahn–Exton’s q q -analogue of the Bessel function J ν ​ ( z , q ) J_{\nu}\left(z;q\right) . The correspondence between I Δ I_{\Delta} and J ν J_{\nu} will allow us to translate useful relations from one setting to the other. Second, we want to introduce to the q q -hypergeometric community some possibly new techniques, theory and conjectures arising from applications of physical mathematics to geometric topology.



1.
Introduction

The study of quantum invariants of
3
-manifolds has seen remarkable developments over the past two decades, partly driven by new results and connections with geometric topology. Among the most interesting constructions to emerge from this interplay is the
3
D index, introduced by Dimofte, Gaiotto, and Gukov
[
DGG14
,
DGG13
]
, which assigns a formal power series
I_{\mathcal{T}}\left(q\right)\in\mathbb{Z}[\![q^{\frac{1}{2}}]\!]
to an ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of a cusped
3
-manifold
M
. This invariant, built from a basic building block known as the tetrahedral index
I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)
, encodes deep geometric and topological information on
M

[
GHHR16
,
Gar16
,
GW22
,
GY24
]
.

On the other hand, the Hahn–Exton
q
-Bessel function
J_{\nu}\left(z;q\right)
, a classical object in the theory of basic hypergeometric series, has long been studied for its rich analytic structure and its connections to physics, quantum groups, combinatorics, to mention a few
[
Swa92b
,
KS95
,
Koe94
]
.

The starting point of this note is to make explicit the correspondence between these two functions, by identifying the tetrahedral index with an evaluation of the Hahn–Exton function. While this connection is known to experts familiar with both frameworks, it seems to have never appeared in the literature. Our aim is to demonstrate how known identities for
J_{\nu}\left(z;q\right)
can be translated into non-trivial relations for the tetrahedral index, and vice versa.

Beyond these structural parallels, we also explore how this correspondence can be used to translate conjectural or experimental observations with a topological flavour into the language of
q
-hypergeometric series. In particular, we revisit a formula of Gang and Yonekura
[
GY18
]
expressing the
3
D index of a Dehn filled manifold in terms of the index of the unfilled one
[
CHR25
]
, and suggest that its effectiveness (even in cases where convergence is not guaranteed) may be better understood through number theoretic techniques.

While no fundamentally new theorems are proved here, we hope that this note will be of interest to two distinct communities: to topologists seeking tools for understanding quantum invariants, and to number theorists interested in new applications of classical
q
-series. We also include some conjectural identities and open questions.

2.
Tetrahedral index and the Hahn-Exton function

We will be using the standard notation for
q
-hypergeometric functions (also known as basic hypergeometric functions), following Gasper and Rahman’s book
[
GR04
]
.

Recall the definition of the basic hypergeometric series:

(1)

{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{r}\\
b_{1}&b_{2}&\ldots&b_{s}\end{matrix};q,z\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q\right)_{n}}{\left(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{s},q;q\right)_{n}}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}\right)^{1+s-r}z^{n},

where
\left(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{r};q\right)_{n}
is a shorthand for
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(c_{i};q\right)_{n}
, and
\left(c;q\right)_{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1-cq^{i}\right)
is known as a
q
-Pochhammer symbol. Here
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\infty
.

2.1.
The tetrahedral index

The “building block” of the
3
D index, first introduced in
[
DGG13
]
, is the
q
-holonomic function
I_{\Delta}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}(\!(q^{\frac{1}{2}})\!)
in two variables
m,e\in\mathbb{Z}
, given explicitly by

(2)

I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=\sum_{n=e_{+}}^{\infty}\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{q^{\frac{n\left(n+1\right)}{2}-\left(n+\frac{e}{2}\right)m}}{\left(q\right)_{n}\left(q\right)_{n+e}},

where
e_{+}=\frac{|e|-e}{2}
. For notational convenience, the dependency on
q
of
I_{\Delta}
and in the
q
-Pochhammer symbols has been suppressed.

By
[
DGG13
, Sec. 3.1]
, tetrahedral indices can be identified as the coefficients in the generating function

(3)

\frac{\left(q^{-\frac{m}{2}+1}\zeta^{-1}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta\right)_{\infty}}=\sum_{e\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)\zeta^{e}.

The left hand side of equation (
3
) is the product
e_{q}\left(-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta\right)E_{q}\left(q^{-\frac{m}{2}+1}\zeta^{-1}\right)
of two well-known
q
-analogues of the exponential function:

(4)

e_{q}\left(z\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{\left(q\right)_{n}}={{}_{1}\phi_{0}}\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
-\end{matrix};q,z\right]=\frac{1}{\left(z\right)_{\infty}},

(5)

E_{q}\left(z\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}}{\left(q\right)_{n}}z^{n}={{}_{0}\phi_{0}}\left[\begin{matrix}-\\
-\end{matrix};q,-z\right]=\left(-z\right)_{\infty}

The latter two identities follow easily from
[
GR04
, Sec. 1.3]
.

The tetrahedral index is known to satisfy numerous functional equations; see
e.g.

[
Gar16
]
, where
q
-holonomic relations completely determining
I_{\Delta}(m,e)
are explored. The first one is a simple chain of symmetries:

(6)

I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=I_{\Delta}\left(-e,-m\right)=\left(-\sqrt{q}\right)^{-e}I_{\Delta}\left(e,-e-m\right)=\left(-\sqrt{q}\right)^{m}I_{\Delta}\left(-e-m,m\right).

Next, there are two series of three-term relations, which combine the values of
I_{\Delta}
on three adjacent lattice points in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}I_{\Delta}\left(m,e+1\right)+\left(q^{e+\frac{m}{2}}-q^{-\frac{m}{2}}-q^{\frac{m}{2}}\right)I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)+I_{\Delta}\left(m,e-1\right)=0\\
I_{\Delta}\left(m+1,e\right)+\left(q^{-m-\frac{e}{2}}-q^{-\frac{e}{2}}-q^{\frac{e}{2}}\right)I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)+I_{\Delta}\left(m-1,e\right)=0,\end{gathered}

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}q^{\frac{e}{2}}I_{\Delta}\left(m+1,e\right)+q^{-\frac{m}{2}}I_{\Delta}\left(m,e+1\right)-I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=0\\
q^{\frac{e}{2}}I_{\Delta}\left(m-1,e\right)+q^{-\frac{m}{2}}I_{\Delta}\left(m,e-1\right)-I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=0.\end{gathered}

There are two further important relations. The first can be regarded as being some form of “weighted orthogonality” between the infinite arrays
\left(I_{\Delta}(m,e)\right)_{e\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and is usually referred to as
quadratic identity
:

(11)

\sum_{e\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{e}I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)I_{\Delta}\left(m,e+c\right)=\delta_{c,0}.

The last relation we need is referred to as
pentagon relation
, and holds for variables
m_{1},m_{2},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}
:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\sum_{e\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{e}I_{\Delta}\left(m_{1},e+x_{1}\right)I_{\Delta}\left(m_{2},e+x_{2}\right)I_{\Delta}\left(m_{1}+m_{2},e+x_{3}\right)=\\
q^{-x_{3}}I_{\Delta}\left(m_{1}-x_{2}+x_{3},x_{1}-x_{3}\right)I_{\Delta}\left(m_{2}-x_{1}+x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}\right).\end{gathered}

The quadratic and pentagon relations are crucial in the partial proof of the invariance of the
3
D index (see Section
3
for a definition) under
2
-
3
and
0
-
2
moves on triangulations of hyperbolic
3
-manifolds
[
GHHR16
,
GHRS15
]
. It is however important to remark that only moves of these kinds that preserve a further geometric property, known as
1
-efficiency, do indeed preserve the
3
D index.

Note that topological invariance (
i.e.
independence from the triangulation) of the
3
D index is still unknown in general; it was however proved in
[
GK19
]
that the
3
D index takes the same value on two triangulations of the same cusped manifold admitting a strict angle structure.

2.2.
The Hahn-Exton function

Bessel functions are well-known functions with multiple applications to physics, number theory and probability, to mention a few (see
e.g.

[
KS95
]
,
[
Koe94
]
,
[
Swa92a
]
). Their
q
-analogues first appeared in a series of papers by F.H. Jackson in 1903-1905. We refer to Swarttouw’s PhD thesis
[
Swa92b
]
for a recap on the history of these
q
-analogues. The same thesis contains many of the relations and tools we are going to use in what follows.
One of the most studied
q
-analogues of the Bessel function is known as the Hahn-Exton
q
-Bessel function. This is defined for
\nu,z\in\mathbb{C}
(except for
z=0
whenever
Re\left(\nu\right)<0
or if
Re\left(\nu\right)=0
and
\nu\neq 0
) as

(14)

J_{\nu}\left(z;q\right)=z^{\nu}\frac{\left(q^{\nu+1}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q\right)_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{\left(q^{\nu+1}\right)_{n}\left(q\right)_{n}}z^{2k}=z^{\nu}\frac{\left(q^{\nu+1}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q\right)_{\infty}}{{}_{1}\phi_{1}}\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
q^{\nu+1}\end{matrix};q,z^{2}q\right],

whenever
\nu>-1
. It is easy to prove (
cf
. Cor. 2
[
Swa92b
]
) that
J_{\nu}\left(z;q\right)
is an analytic function in
z
(except for
z=0
whenever
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
), and is an analytic function of
\nu
.

Note that, by
[
Swa92b
, Thm. 3.2]
, if
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
, it is possible to write:

J_{\nu}\left(z;q\right)=\left(-\sqrt{q}\right)^{-\nu}J_{-\nu}\left(q^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}z;q\right).

The main point of this section is to build on the following easy observation identifying the tetrahedral index with an evaluation of Hahn-Exton
q
-Bessel function, and deduce a whole suite of relations for
I_{\Delta}
. This connection is well-known to experts in the field, but to the best of the author’s knowledge, it never explicitly appeared in print.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
m,e\in\mathbb{Z}
; then

I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=J_{e}\left(q^{-\frac{m}{2}};q\right).

Proof.

It is immediate to see that, if
e\geq 0
,

I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=q^{-\frac{em}{2}}\sum_{n=e_{+}}^{\infty}\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}q^{n\left(1-m\right)}}{\left(q\right)_{n}\left(q\right)_{n+e}}.

Moreover, note that
\left(q\right)_{n+e}=\left(q\right)_{n}\left(q^{n+1}\right)_{e}=\left(q\right)_{e}\left(q^{e+1}\right)_{n}
.

Putting everything together, we get

I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=\frac{q^{-\frac{em}{2}}}{\left(q\right)_{e}}\sum_{n=e_{+}}^{\infty}\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}q^{n\left(1-m\right)}}{\left(q\right)_{n}\left(q^{e+1}\right)_{n}}.

From this latter expression it is easy to realise that

(15)

I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=\frac{q^{-\frac{me}{2}}}{\left(q\right)_{e}}{{}_{1}\phi_{1}}\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
q^{e+1}\end{matrix};q,q^{1-m}\right].

If instead
e<0
a similar analysis yields

(16)

I_{\Delta}\left(m,e\right)=\frac{q^{\frac{me}{2}}}{\left(q^{e}\right)_{-e}}{{}_{1}\phi_{1}}\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
q^{-e+1}\end{matrix};q,q^{1-e-m}\right],

and we can conclude.
∎

In view of Proposition
2.1
, the relations satisfied by
I_{\Delta}
can be understood as consequences of more general relations known for
J_{\nu}\left(z;q\right)
; the
3
-term relations (
2.1
), (
2.1
) are none other than the difference equations satisfied by
J_{\nu}
(see
e.g.

[
Swa92b
, 4.3.1]
).

The quadratic identity in equation (
11
) is implied by the orthogonality relations for
J_{\nu}

[
Swa92b
, Thm. 3.8]
; the pentagon identity can be derived straightforwardly by specialising a
q
-analogue of Graf’s addition formula
[
KS95
]
. The generating function for
I_{\Delta}
is paralleled in
[
KS92
, Prop. 2.5]
.

2.3.
From
J_{\nu}
to
I_{\Delta}

The purpose of this section is to use Proposition
2.1
to translate certain known relations for
J_{\nu}
into ones for
I_{\Delta}
.

We start with
[
Gro18
, Prop. 3.2]
, which (after a change of variable) gives
for
|t|<1
and
\nu>-1

(17)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{-\frac{\nu n}{2}}J_{\nu}\left(zq^{\frac{n}{2}};q\right)\frac{t^{n}}{\left(q\right)_{n}}=z^{\nu}\frac{\left(q^{\nu+1}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q\right)_{\infty}\left(t\right)_{\infty}}{{}_{1}\phi_{1}}\left[\begin{matrix}t\\
q^{\nu+1}\end{matrix};q,qz^{2}\right],

which implies right away that for
|t|<1
and
e>-1

(18)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}I_{\Delta}\left(m-n,e\right)\frac{\left(tq^{-\frac{e}{2}}\right)^{n}}{\left(q\right)_{n}}=q^{-\frac{me}{2}}\frac{\left(q^{e+1}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q\right)_{\infty}\left(t\right)_{\infty}}{{}_{1}\phi_{1}}\left[\begin{matrix}t\\
q^{e+1}\end{matrix};q,q^{1-m}\right].

As a further consequence, by specialising to
t=q^{e+1}
and using equation (
5
), we obtain the expression (again valid for
e\geq 0
)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(q^{\frac{e}{2}+1}\right)^{n}}{\left(q\right)_{n}}I_{\Delta}(m-n,e)=q^{-\frac{me}{2}}\frac{\left(q^{1-m}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q\right)_{\infty}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }m>0\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{q^{-\frac{me}{2}}}{\left(q\right)_{\infty}}&\text{ if }m=0\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{q^{-\frac{me}{2}}}{\left(q\right)_{-m}}&\text{ if }m<0.\end{cases}

One interesting relation on tetrahedral indices can be deduced from
[
Swa92a
, Eqn 3.1]
; explicitly, for
e_{1},e_{2}>-1
:

(19)

\begin{gathered}I_{\Delta}\left(m_{1},e_{1}\right)I_{\Delta}\left(m_{2},e_{2}\right)=\\
q^{-\frac{m_{1}e_{1}+m_{2}e_{2}}{2}}\frac{\left(q^{e_{1}+1}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{e_{2}+1}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q\right)^{2}_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}q^{-m_{2}n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{\left(q^{e_{2}+1}\right)_{n}\left(q\right)_{n}}{{}_{2}\phi_{1}}\left[\begin{matrix}q^{-n},q^{-n-e_{2}}\\
q^{e_{1}+1}\end{matrix};q,q^{n-m_{1}+m_{2}+e_{2}+1}\right].\end{gathered}

Yet another one comes from
[
Koe94
]
, and reads:

(20)

x^{\nu}J_{\nu}\left(\frac{zq^{\frac{\nu}{2}}}{x};q\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(-\frac{z\sqrt{q}}{x^{2}}\right)^{n}\frac{\left(x^{2}\right)_{n}}{\left(q\right)_{n}}J_{\nu+n}\left(zq^{\frac{\nu+n}{2}};q\right)

which, after applying equation (
6
), restricts to

(21)

I_{\Delta}\left(k,0\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{-\frac{n+k}{2}}I_{\Delta}\left(k+n,1\right),

for
x=z=\sqrt{q}
. If instead we impose
z=q^{-k+\frac{1}{2}}
,
x=\sqrt{q}
we get

(22)

q^{\frac{k}{2}}I_{\Delta}\left(k,k\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(-q^{-k}\right)^{n}I_{\Delta}\left(k-n-1,k+n\right).

A similar relation comes from the
q
-analogue of the multiplicative theorem for Bessel functions, proved in
[
Swa92b
, Thm. 3.11]
, and holds for
m<1
:

(23)

I_{\Delta}\left(m-2\lambda,e\right)=q^{\lambda e}\sum_{k\geq 0}q^{\left(1-\frac{m}{2}\right)k}\frac{\left(q^{2\lambda}\right)_{k}}{\left(q\right)_{k}}I_{\Delta}\left(m,e+k\right)

Finally for this section, we can translate
[
Rub02
, Cor. 2]

to get the following relation for
m_{1},m_{2},e\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
m_{1}+m_{2}\neq 0
:

(24)

q^{\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{1}-m_{2}\right)e}I_{\Delta}\left(m_{1}+m_{2},e\right)=q^{-e}\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{\frac{\left(2+m_{1}\right)l+\left(2-m_{2}\right)k}{2}}I_{\Delta}\left(m_{1},l\right)I_{\Delta}\left(m_{2},k\right)I_{\Delta}\left(0,k+l-e\right).

2.4.
From
I_{\Delta}
to
J_{\nu}

The main result of
[
CHR25
]
relied crucially on an identification between certain
q
-hypergeometric functions and a generating function for “diagonal” tetrahedral indices. More precisely, define

(25)

\varphi_{r}\left(z,q\right)=\begin{cases}\left(-zq^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}{{}_{3}\phi_{3}}\left[\begin{matrix}-z^{-1}\sqrt{q}&-z\sqrt{q}&0\\
-q&\sqrt{q}&-\sqrt{q}\end{matrix};q,q^{1-\frac{r}{2}}\right]&r\text{ even}\\
~\\
\left(-z\right)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}q^{\frac{1-r}{2}}\frac{\left(1+z\right)}{1-q}{{}_{3}\phi_{3}}\left[\begin{matrix}-z^{-1}q&-zq&0\\
-q&q^{\frac{3}{2}}&-q^{\frac{3}{2}}\end{matrix};q,q^{\frac{3-r}{2}}\right]&r\text{ odd.}\end{cases}

Then, by
[
CHR25
, Sec. 10]
we have

(26)

\varphi_{r}\left(z,q\right)=\sum_{e\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{\Delta}\left(e-r,e\right)z^{e}=\sum_{e\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{e}\left(q^{\frac{r-e}{2}};q\right)z^{e}.

It turns out that
\varphi_{r}\left(z,q\right)
can be identified with a product of certain
q
-analogues of trigonometric functions, introduced by various authors (see the discussion in
[
Šta16
, Sec. 2]
).

(27)

\mathcal{S}_{q}\left(z\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}q^{n\left(n+1\right)}}{\left(q\right)_{2n+1}}z^{2n+1},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{C}_{q}\left(z\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{\left(q\right)_{2n}}z^{2n}.

Equivalently,

(28)

\mathcal{S}_{q}\left(z\right)=\frac{z}{1-q}{{}_{1}\phi_{1}}\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
q^{3}\end{matrix};q^{2},q^{2}z^{2}\right],\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{C}_{q}\left(z\right)={{}_{1}\phi_{1}}\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
q\end{matrix};q^{2},qz^{2}\right].

Therefore in particular

\mathcal{S}_{q}\left(z\right)=\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}z^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(z;q^{2}\right),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{C}_{q}\left(z\right)=\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}z^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(q^{-\frac{1}{2}}z;q^{2}\right).

.

); if

3.
3
D index and Dehn fillings

To simplify and streamline what follows, we are going to restrict ourselves to the special case of the complement of a link in
S^{3}
with
r\geq 1
components.
We refer to
[
Thu14
,
BP92
,
Pur20
]
for basic notions in hyperbolic geometry and knot theory.

Such a manifold
M
can be constructed and concisely described by an
ideal triangulation

\mathcal{T}
. This is a triangulation of
M
built by ideal tetrahedra,
i.e.
compact tetrahedra with the vertices removed. A given cusped 3-manifold admits infinitely many different ideal triangulations, all related by a finite set of combinatorial moves known as Pachner moves (see
e.g.

[
Mat07
,
RST20
]
).

The complement in
S^{3}

Thurston
[
Thu78
]
introduced an important method for constructing hyperbolic geometry on a cusped 3-manifold
M
by gluing together geometric ideal tetrahedra in hyperbolic
3
-space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. (These are given as the convex hull of four generic points in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
.)
Up to isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
such an ideal tetrahedron is completely determined by its
shape parameter
. This is a complex number
z
such that
\mathrm{Im}\left(z\right)>0
. The geometry on
M
is then obtained by gluing together
n
ideal tetrahedra along their faces via isometries. Thurston’s gluing equations are a set of equations in the shape parameters of the triangulation’s tetrahedra that guarantee that tetrahedra fit together correctly around the edges to give geometry on
M
locally modelled on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.
Importantly, the (logarithm of) these equations, expressed in matrix form, can be easily recovered by using the freely available program SnapPy
[
CDGW
]
, via the
gluing_equations()
command.

There are several approaches to compute the
3
D index; either in terms of
edge weights
(see
e.g.

[
GHRS15
]
) or using
normal surfaces

[
GHHR16
]
. For a detailed explanation of the subtleties involved in the computations we refer to these papers as well as Section 14 and Appendix C in
[
CHR25
]
.

We now give a brief description of the former approach.
We start with the gluing matrix
G_{\mathcal{T}}
for the ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
, with
n
tetrahedra
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}
,
and a set of
n
edges denoted by
\mathcal{E}
. Roughly speaking,
I_{\mathcal{T}}^{\omega}\left(q\right)
is an infinite sum of products of tetrahedral indices over integer weights assigned to the edges of
\mathcal{T}
, defined using the combinatorics of
\mathcal{T}
.

Consider weight functions
k:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}
assigning an integer weight
k\left(e\right)
to each edge class
e\in\mathcal{E}
; this gives a weight on each edge of each tetrahedron
\Delta_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
. Adding up these weights on each pair of opposite edges in
\Delta_{j}
yields a vector of
quad weights

q_{j}\left(k\right)=\left(a_{j}\left(k\right),b_{j}\left(k\right),c_{j}\left(k\right)\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.
Then define

(35)

I_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}\left(q\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z},\\
k|_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}=0\end{subarray}}q^{k\left(\mathcal{E}\right)}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-b_{j}\left(k\right)}I_{\Delta}\left(b_{j}\left(k\right)-c_{j}\left(k\right),a_{j}\left(k\right)-b_{j}\left(k\right)\right)

where
k\left(\mathcal{E}\right)=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}k\left(e\right)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is a suitable choice of
r
edges. See
[
GHRS15
, Sec. 4.6]
to understand which choices are admissible (for
r=1
any edge is suitable).

In general,

(36)

I_{\mathcal{T}}^{\omega}\left(q\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z},\\
k|_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}=0\end{subarray}}q^{k\left(\mathcal{E}\right)}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-b_{j}^{\omega}\left(k\right)}I_{\Delta}\left(b_{j}^{\omega}\left(k\right)-c_{j}^{\omega}\left(k\right),a_{j}^{\omega}\left(k\right)-b_{j}^{\omega}\left(k\right)\right)

where
\left(a_{j}^{\omega}\left(k\right),b_{j}^{\omega}\left(k\right),c_{j}^{\omega}\left(k\right)\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
depend on
k
and some
boundary conditions

\omega\in\mathrm{H}_{1}\left(\partial M;\mathbb{Z}\right)
.
All the coefficients in these expressions can be read off easily from the gluing equations for
\mathcal{T}
given by SnapPy.
With these conventions,
I_{\mathcal{T}}^{\omega}\left(q\right)
is defined for

\omega\in\mathcal{K}=\ker\left(\mathrm{H}_{1}\left(\partial M;\mathbb{Z}\right)\to\mathrm{H}_{1}\left(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2}\right)\right).

Remark 3.1
.

It was proved in
[
GHHR16
]
that the index
I_{\mathcal{T}}^{\omega}\left(q\right)
converges for all boundary conditions
\omega
if and only if the ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
is
1
-efficient. Here
1
-efficiency means that that
\mathcal{T}
does not
contain any embedded closed normal surfaces of non-negative Euler characteristic, except
for vertex-linking tori.

The main result from
[
CHR25
]
gives a rigorous proof of a formula by Gang and Yonekura
[
GY18
]
, expressing the
3
D index of manifolds obtained as Dehn filling in terms of the
3
D index of the unfilled manifold.

Here we only consider Dehn fillings
M\left(\alpha\right)
along slopes
\alpha
on one
torus boundary component
T\subseteq\partial M
.
Then the Gang-Yonekura formula
[
GY18
]
gives the
3
D-index for
M\left(\alpha\right)
as an infinite linear combination of the
3
D-indices
I_{M}^{\gamma}
for
M
over boundary classes
\gamma

having intersection number
0
or
\pm 2
with
\alpha
:

(38)

I_{M(\alpha)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{K}_{T}\\
\gamma\cdot\alpha=0\end{subarray}}\left(-1\right)^{|\gamma|}\left(q^{\frac{1}{2}|\gamma|}+q^{-\frac{1}{2}|\gamma|}\right)I_{M}^{\gamma}\left(q\right)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{K}_{T}\\
\gamma\cdot\alpha=\pm 2\end{subarray}}I_{M}^{\gamma}(q)\right),

where
|\gamma|
is the number of components of
\gamma
and
\mathcal{K}_{T}=\ker\left(\mathrm{H}_{1}\left(T;\mathbb{Z}\right)\to\mathrm{H}_{1}\left(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2}\right)\right)
.
(For simplicity, we assume the boundary data vanishes on all components of
\partial M\setminus T
.)

4.
Conjectural relations in the closed case

There is currently no precise mathematical definition of
3
D index for closed
3
-manifolds. In spite of this, equation (
38
) can be applied to knot complements. Remarkably, experimental applications of the formula appear to yield meaningful results (
cf.

[
CHR25
, Sec. 13]
). As an example, in the case of hyperbolic knots, it appears that computing the index for exceptional slopes (
i.e.
producing non-hyperbolic manifolds, see
e.g.

[
Dun20
]
) always gives a somewhat trivial or diverging index.
In certain special cases, such as surgeries on alternating torus knots (
cf
.
[
CHR25
, Sec. 13.1]
), whose index is particularly simple to start with, it is possible to give an explicit answer for all surgeries.

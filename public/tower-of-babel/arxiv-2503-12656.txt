Cocompact Fuchsian groups with a modular embedding
Matthew Stover

Abstract
A Fuchsian group Γ \Gamma has a modular embedding if its adjoint trace field is a totally real number field and every unbounded Galois conjugate Γ σ \Gamma^{\sigma} comes equipped with a holomorphic (or conjugate holomorphic) map ϕ σ : 𝔹 1 → 𝔹 1 {\phi^{\sigma}:\mathbb{B}^{1}\to\mathbb{B}^{1}} intertwining the actions of Γ \Gamma and Γ σ \Gamma^{\sigma} on the Poincaré disk 𝔹 1 \mathbb{B}^{1} . This paper provides the first cocompact nonarithmetic Fuchsian groups with a modular embedding that are not commensurable with a triangle group. The main result, proved using period domains, is that any immersed totally geodesic complex curve on a complex hyperbolic 2 2 -orbifold has a modular embedding. Another consequence is arithmeticity of totally geodesic curves on finite-volume complex hyperbolic surfaces that are commensurable with quotients of 𝔹 1 \mathbb{B}^{1} by the group generated by reflections in quadrilaterals satisfying certain angle conditions.

Introduction

This paper considers Fuchsian groups as subgroups of
\PU(1,1)
acting on the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{1}
. Given such a group
\Gamma
, its
adjoint trace field

\mathbb{Q}\!\left(\Tr\Ad(\Gamma)\right)
is the extension of
\mathbb{Q}
generated by the traces of
\Gamma
acting on the Lie algebra of
\PU(1,1)
. A Fuchsian group
\Gamma
then has a
modular embedding
if
\mathbb{Q}\!\left(\Tr\Ad(\Gamma)\right)
is a totally real number field and each unbounded Galois conjugate
\Gamma^{\sigma}
induced by a real embedding
\sigma
of
\mathbb{Q}\!\left(\Tr\Ad(\Gamma)\right)
comes equipped with a holomorphic (or conjugate holomorphic) map
\phi^{\sigma}:\mathbb{B}^{1}\to\mathbb{B}^{1}
intertwining the actions of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\sigma}
. In other words,

\phi^{\sigma}(\gamma z)=\gamma^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma}(z)

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
z\in\mathbb{B}^{1}
. This property is usually stated for subgroups of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acting on the upper half-plane (e.g.,
[
6
,
16
]
), but conjugation by the Cayley transformation equates the two definitions.

Arithmetic Fuchsian groups have no unbounded Galois conjugates, so they vacuously have a modular embedding. Cohen and Wolfart proved that triangle groups have modular embeddings
[
6
]
; see
[
14
, Thm. 6.3]
on why that paper should allow conjugate holomorphic maps in the definition. The interest in groups with a modular embedding stems from intriguing properties they share with arithmetic Fuchsian groups. For example, their covering radius is a ratio of periods for abelian varieties; these ratios are transcendental, addressing a question of Lang (see
[
6
]
). They also admit
twisted
modular forms, which were introduced by Möller and Zagier
[
17
]
.

Around a decade after Cohen and Wolfart’s work, Calta
[
4
]
and McMullen
[
15
]
classified Teichmüller curves in genus
2
, and McMullen showed that these produce Fuchsian groups with a modular embedding that are not commensurable with triangle groups. See Möller
[
16
]
for examples with higher-degree trace field. It is well-known that Teichmüller curves are noncompact, and thus all previously-known nonarithmetic cocompact Fuchsian groups with a modular embedding were subgroups of triangle groups. The first contribution of this paper, proved in
Section

4
, is the existence of compact examples not commensurable with triangle groups.

Theorem 1.1
.

There are cocompact nonarithmetic Fuchsian groups with a modular embedding that are not commensurable with any triangle group.

This gives a negative answer to
[
20
, Prob. 1]
in the cocompact case. The examples are immersed totally geodesic curves on nonarithmetic complex hyperbolic
2
-orbifolds, i.e., finite-volume quotients of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{2}
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
equipped with its Bergman metric of constant holomorphic sectional curvature
-1
and holomorphic isometry group
\PU(2,1)

Theorem 1.2

If
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{1}
is a finite-volume orbifold that immerses as a totally geodesic curve on a finite-volume complex hyperbolic
2
-orbifold, then

Perhaps surprisingly, the proof does not show that the complex hyperbolic

Theorem

1.2

Section

2

3

It is worth noting that the nonarithmetic orbifolds that were constructed by Deligne and Mostow

semi-arithmetic

A given nonarithmetic group

Preliminaries

Basic geometry

See
[
11
]
for more details about basic geometry of
\mathbb{B}^{2}
. It will be advantageous in this paper to consider the model of complex hyperbolic space associated with an arbitrary hermitian form. To that end, if

is any hermitian form of signature
(2,1)
on
\mathbb{C}^{3}

\displaystyle K\coloneqq

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{P}\!\left(\U(2)\times\U(1)\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\U(2)

G
of a point in

for the left actions of

Taking

-positive lines and totally geodesic embeddings of
\mathbb{B}^{1}
in
\mathbb{B}^{2}

acts transitively on these embeddings with

\displaystyle W\coloneqq

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{P}\!\left(\U(1,1)\times\U(1)\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\U(1,1)

naturally isomorphic to the stabilizer of a fixed Poincaré disk

. Note that the restriction of an isometry to

is induced by projection of

onto
\PU(1,1)
. The maximal compact subgroup of

is isomorphic to

\displaystyle L\coloneqq

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{P}\!\left(\U(1)\times\U(1)\times\U(1)\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\U(1)\times\U(1)

with

identified with

and one
\U(1)
factor acting on its normal bundle.

A finite-volume complex hyperbolic

is a lattice in

, where

to

\Gamma_{g}
acting on the normal bundle to

Totally geodesic curves also have a dynamical interpretation. Indeed, they correspond precisely with the closed orbits for the action of

denotes the point
\Lambda g\in\Lambda\backslash G

is a lattice in

[
2
, §8.1]

Arithmetic invariants and period maps

The next goal is to describe algebraic groups associated with lattices in
\PU(2,1)
and their associated period maps. See
[

12

\displaystyle=\PU(2,1)/K

\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{1}

. Since
k
is totally real, so is
\ell
. Any real embedding
\sigma
of
\ell
then induces an embedding of
k
, and if
\Gamma^{\sigma}
is unbounded, then there is an associated lifted period map
\widetilde{\psi}_{\sigma}:\mathbb{B}^{2}\to D_{\sigma}
for some nontrivial Mumford–Tate domain
D_{\sigma}
for
\PU(2,1)
.
Proposition

3.1
implies that
\widetilde{\psi}_{\sigma}
provides a holomorphic map
\phi^{\sigma}:\mathbb{B}^{1}\to\mathbb{B}^{1}
intertwining the actions of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\sigma}
on
\mathbb{B}^{1}
, and thus
\Gamma
has a modular embedding.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Note that anti-holomorphic maps are also allowed in the definition of a modular embedding. For the period mapping, the complex structure on the target is chosen so the map is holomorphic.

Remark 4.3
.

Explicit matrices for the action of
\Gamma

can easily be found as follows. Set

Remark 4.4
.

The quadrilateral
\mathcal{Q}
with vertices

Question 4.5
.

Can totally geodesic curves on nonarithmetic complex hyperbolic manifolds produce new compact totally geodesic curves on Hilbert modular varieties that are not contained in a proper Shimura subvariety?

A positive answer would be particularly interesting in dimension

,

,

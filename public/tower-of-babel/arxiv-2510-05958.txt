On the coming down from infinity of continuous-state branching processes with drift-interaction
Félix Rebotier

Abstract
We study the phenomenon of coming down from infinity – that is, when the process starts from infinity and never returns to it – for continuous-state branching processes with generalized drift. We provide sufficient conditions on the drift term and the branching mechanism to ensure both non-explosion and coming down from infinity, without requir- ing the associated jump measure to have a finite first moment. Assuming the process comes down from infinity and the drift satisfies a one-sided Lipschitz condition, we show that, as the initial values tend to infinity, the process converges locally uniformly almost surely to the strong solution of a stochastic differential equation. The main techniques employed are comparison principles for solutions of stochastic equations and the method of Lyapunov functions, the latter being briefly reviewed in a broader setting.



Keywords.
Continuous-state branching process, Interaction, Entrance boundary, Coming down from infinity, Stochastic equation with jumps, Lyapunov functions

The logistic branching process, defined in

To the best of our knowledge, with the exception of the logistic case, most studies in the literature assume that the Lévy measure satisfies certain moment conditions, typically a finite first or log moment. In this article, we initiate the study of the boundary behavior of CBDI processes under relaxed moment conditions. This serves as a foundation for a broader investigation - incorporating the possibility of regular boundaries - which will be addressed in a forthcoming work.

We first establish explicit sufficient conditions for non-explosion and coming down from infinity. This completes previous results obtained in
[

]
and
[

]
by allowing the branching dynamics to be driven by Lévy measures with infinite mean. Our approach is based on comparison properties of stochastic equations and classical Lyapunov-type methods, which differs from those employed in the aforementioned works, and will be reexplained in a broad context.

The second contribution of this article is the analysis of the stochastic equation solved by the process starting from infinity. We will adapt techniques from the works of Li
[

]
, Bansaye
[

]
, and Bansaye et al.

Assume given processes

\tau_{a}^{x,-}\uparrow\tau_{a}^{\infty,-}:=\inf\{t\geq 0,\ X^{\infty}_{t}\leq a\}

Proof.

(i)
Fix
x>0
. Since the family of random times

is nondecreasing almost surely in

\ell:=\lim_{b\to\infty}\tau_{b}^{x,+},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

By definition of
\tau_{\infty}^{x,+}

\tau_{\infty}^{x,+}\geq\ell\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

Since the process

Moreover, by definition of

. Taking the limit

Since

\tau_{\infty}^{x,+}=\ell\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}

(ii)
Fix

. Since

for all

(iii)

and by combining the bounds, we get:

\tau_{\infty}^{\infty,-}=\lim_{a\to\infty}\tau_{a}^{\infty,-}=L\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

∎

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{u+z}{I(u+z)}-\frac{z}{I(z)}\right)\pi([u,1])\,\mathrm{d}u.

Since
\tfrac{z}{I(z)}\downarrow 0
as
z\to\infty
, we deduce that for all
u\in[0,1]

\left(\frac{u+z}{I(u+z)}-\frac{z}{I(z)}\right)\pi([u,1])\;\underset{z\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\;0,

and moreover

\Big|\left(\tfrac{u+z}{I(u+z)}-\tfrac{z}{I(z)}\right)\pi([u,1])\Big|\;\leq\;2\frac{\kappa}{I(\kappa)}\pi([u,1]).

The dominated convergence theorem then yields

\int_{0}^{1}\Big(f_{1}(z+h)-f_{1}(z)-hf_{1}^{\prime}(z)\Big)\pi(\mathrm{d}h)\;\underset{z\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\;0.

For the integral over
(1,\infty)
, again by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\big(f_{1}(z+h)-f_{1}(z)\big)\pi(\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u+z}{I(u+z)}\,\overline{\pi}(u\vee 1)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Since
z\mapsto\tfrac{z}{I(z)}
is nonincreasing, for all
u\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{u+z}{I(u+z)}\,\overline{\pi}(u\vee 1)

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\pi}(1)\frac{\kappa}{I(\kappa)}\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq 1\}}+\frac{u\overline{\pi}(u)}{I(u)}\mathbf{1}_{\{u>1\}}\;=:g(u).

The function
g
is integrable on
(0,\infty)
. Moreover, for every fixed
u\geq 0
, we have

\tfrac{u+z}{I(u+z)}\to 0
as
z\to\infty
. By dominated convergence,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\big(f_{1}(z+h)-f_{1}(z)\big)\pi(\mathrm{d}h)\;\underset{z\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\;0.

Finally, by condition (B1), since
z\mapsto\tfrac{z}{I(z)}
is nondecreasing on
[\kappa,\infty)
, we deduce that
f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(z)\leq 0
for
z\geq\kappa
. Therefore,

\mathcal{X}f_{1}(z)\;\leq\;z\big(-1+\epsilon_{1}(z)\big)\;\underset{z\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\;-\infty,

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f_{2}
be a
C^{2}
function such that, for any
z\geq\kappa
,
f_{2}(z)=\int_{\kappa}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{I(u)}
. If
I
satisfies (B1) and (B2), then

\mathcal{X}f_{2}(z)=-1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{z\bar{\pi}(u)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u+\epsilon_{2}(z),\ z\in[0,\infty)

where
\epsilon_{2}
is such that
\epsilon_{2}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z
goes to
\infty
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{I}=\int_{\kappa}^{\infty}\frac{u\bar{\pi}(u)}{I(u)}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
, then

\underset{z\to\infty}{\lim}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{z\overline{\pi}(u)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u=0,

(20)

and there exists
M_{2},c_{2}>0
such that:

\forall z\geq M_{2},\hskip 9.24994pt\mathcal{X}f_{2}(z)\leq-c_{2}.

Proof.

For
z\geq\kappa
, the extended generator applied to
f_{2}
reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}f_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=-1-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z\frac{I^{\prime}(z)}{I(z)^{2}}-\gamma\frac{z}{I(z)}+z\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{z}^{z+h}\left(\frac{1}{I(u)}-\frac{1}{I(z)}\right)\mathrm{d}u\right]\pi(\mathrm{d}h)+z\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{z}^{z+h}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{I(u)}\pi(\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle=-1-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z\frac{I^{\prime}(z)}{I(z)^{2}}-\gamma\frac{z}{I(z)}+z\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{I(u+z)}-\frac{1}{I(z)}\right)\pi([u,1])\mathrm{d}u+z\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\pi}(u\vee 1)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=-1+\epsilon_{2}(z)+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{z\overline{\pi}(u)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u,

with

\epsilon_{2}(z):=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z\frac{I^{\prime}(z)}{I(z)^{2}}-\gamma\frac{z}{I(z)}+z\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{I(u+z)}-\frac{1}{I(z)}\right)\pi([u,1])\mathrm{d}u+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{z\overline{\pi}(1)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u,\hskip 9.24994ptz\geq\kappa.

We aim to show that
\epsilon_{2}(z)
goes to
0
as
z
goes to
\infty
.
First, properties (B1) and (B2) imply:

\displaystyle z\frac{I^{\prime}(z)}{I(z)^{2}}=\left(\frac{z}{I(z)}\right)^{2}\frac{I^{\prime}(z)}{z}\longrightarrow 0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{z}{I(z)}\longrightarrow 0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{as}\hskip 9.24994ptz\to\infty.

Moreover, under (B1) and (B2), there exist constants
K_{1},K_{2}>0
such that, for all
z\geq\kappa
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{z}{I(z)}\leq K_{1},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt0\leq\frac{I^{\prime}(z)}{z}\leq K_{2}.

Now, for
z\geq\kappa
and
u\in[0,1]
, we have :

\displaystyle 0\leq z\left(\frac{1}{I(z)}-\frac{1}{I(u+z)}\right)\leq\frac{z}{I(z)}\leq K_{1}.

Thus,

z\left(\frac{1}{I(z)}-\frac{1}{I(u+z)}\right)\underset{z\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Furthermore, by the mean value theorem, for each
u\in[0,1]
, there exists
\delta_{u}\in(0,u)
such that :

\displaystyle z\left(\frac{1}{I(z)}-\frac{1}{I(u+z)}\right)

\displaystyle=zu\frac{I^{\prime}(z+\delta_{u})}{I(z+\delta_{u})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq zu\frac{K_{2}(z+\delta_{u})}{I(z+\delta_{u})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}u\left(\frac{z+\delta_{u}}{I(z+\delta_{u})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}^{2}K_{2}u.

Since
u\in[0,1]\mapsto u\pi([u,1])
is integrable, because
\int_{0}^{1}h^{2}\pi(\mathrm{d}h)<\infty
, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that:

z\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{I(u+z)}-\frac{1}{I(z)}\right)\pi([u,1])\mathrm{d}u\underset{z\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Now, considering the second integral:

\displaystyle z\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\overline{\pi}(1)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{z\overline{\pi}(1)}{I(z+1)}=\overline{\pi}(1)\frac{z+1}{I(z+1)}\times\frac{z}{z+1},

which tends to
0
as
z\to\infty
by (B1) and (B2). Gathering all terms and their limits, we conclude that the function
\epsilon_{2}
vanishes at
\infty
.

Assume now that
\mathcal{I}=\int^{\infty}\frac{u\bar{\pi}(u)}{I(u)}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
. Since

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{z\overline{\pi}(u)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(u+z)\overline{\pi}(u)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u,

and by assumption
u\mapsto I(u)/u
is non-decreasing, we can bound the integrand of the second integral as follows:

\frac{u+z}{I(u+z)}\overline{\pi}(u)\leq\frac{u}{I(u)}\overline{\pi}(u).

By (B2),
\frac{u+z}{I(u+z)}\underset{z\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0
and we have by the dominated convergence theorem:

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{z\overline{\pi}(u)}{I(u+z)}\mathrm{d}u\underset{z\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Therefore
\mathcal{X}f_{2}(z)\underset{z\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}-1
, which concludes the proof.
∎

5.2
Regularity of the CBDI in the initial state

Up to this point, we have considered the process starting from infinity as the pointwise limit along the initial value. However, without additional properties - such as the Feller property of the CBDI on
[0,\infty)
(see e.g. Foucart et al.
[
FLZ20
]
) - this approach does not seem sufficient to establish that the process started from
\infty
has càdlàg sample paths and satisfies the strong Markov property. We will show that, under an additional condition on the drift function
I
, the process
\left(X^{\infty}_{t},\ t\geq 0\right)
is the unique solution of a certain stochastic equation. The representation will directly imply the desired properties.

We first present a result about the regularity of the process
X
on its initial values. This will turn to be crucial in our study.
The key additional assumption on
I
is the one-sided Lipschitz condition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
X
is not explosive and that
I
satisfies (B3).
Then, for any
t\geq 0
,
y\in[0,\infty)
and
(y_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
y_{n}\rightarrow y
when
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have:

\mathbb{P}\big(\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\ \underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\ |X^{y_{n}}_{s}-X^{y}_{s}|=0\big)=1.

(21)

Proof.

We will need here a construction of the CBDI process with all initial values. Consider the stochastic equation (
1
) where the term driven by the Brownian motion
B
is replaced by the term (
2
) with a white noise
W
. Call
\big(X_{t}(x),t\geq 0,x\in[0,\infty)\big)
a family of solutions for which
X_{0}(x)=x
for all
x\in[0,\infty)
. The latter uniquely exists, is càdlàg in
t
and is equivalent for every fixed
x\in[0,\infty)
, to
(X^{x}_{t},t\geq 0)
. We refer the reader to
[
DL12
]
. We set, for
x,y\in(0,\infty)
,
D_{t}^{x,y}=X_{t}(x)-X_{t}(y)
. We are going to show that

\forall t\geq 0,\ \forall x,y\in(0,\infty),\hskip 9.24994pt\underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\ D_{s}^{x,y}\ \rightarrow 0\hskip 9.24994pt\textup{a.s. when}\ x\uparrow y.

For
y<x
and
t\geq 0
, we have:

D_{t}^{x,y}=x-y+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{D^{x,y}_{s-}}W^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u)-\gamma\int_{0}^{t}D_{s}^{x,y}\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\left[I(X_{s}(x))-I(X_{s}(y))\right]\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{D_{s-}^{x,y}}\int_{0}^{1}h\tilde{M^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{D_{s-}^{x,y}}\int_{1}^{\infty}hM^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

(22)

with
W^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u)=W(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u+X_{s-}(y))
and
M^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}z)=M(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u+X_{s-}(y)),\mathrm{d}z)
. By applying the same arguments as in the proof of Proposition 1.1 in
[
BFF18
, Page 15-16]
, we deduce that
W^{\prime}
is a Gaussian white noise with same intensity as
W
, that
M^{\prime}
is a Poisson random measure with same intensity as
M
and that
M^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime}
are independent.

Recall the hypothesis (B3) on
I
:

\exists b>0,\ \forall y,z\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994ptI(y+z)-I(y)\geq-bz.

We take such
b
for the rest of the proof. This leads to the following comparison result for the process
D^{x,y}
, which we admit for the moment and whose proof will be provided in the forthcoming Lemma
5.3
:

\forall t\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994ptD_{t}^{x,y}\leq Y_{t}(x-y)\hskip 9.24994pt\textup{a.s.}

(23)

where
Y(x-y)
satisfies the stochastic equation:

Y_{t}=x-y+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{Y_{s-}}W^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u)+(b-\gamma)\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{Y_{s-}}\int_{0}^{1}h\tilde{M^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{Y_{s-}}\int_{1}^{\infty}hM^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h).

(24)

Observe that the process
Y
is a CB(
\hat{\Psi}
) with
\hat{\Psi}(x):=\Psi(x)-bx
where

is the branching mechanism of the CB related to
X
, see (
3
) (with
\lambda=0
).

Granting (
23
), the proof follows from an application of Lemma 1.5 in Duquesne and Labbé
[
DL14
, page 8]
. The latter yields the following convergence in probability, for a fixed
t\geq 0
, one has

\underset{r\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\ \underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\,Y_{s}(r)=0.

In our setting, since

is a.s. non-decreasing, we deduce that the convergence actually holds almost surely. Consequently, we obtain :

0\leq\underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\,|X_{t}(x)-X_{t}(y)|=\underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\,D_{s}^{x,y}\leq\underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\,Y_{s}(x-y)\ \underset{x\downarrow y}{\longrightarrow}\ 0\hskip 9.24994pt\textup{a.s.},

which completes the proof.
∎

We now turn in the next lemma to the proof of the comparison result (
23
). We continue to work within the framework of Proposition
5.1
, under the same assumptions and with the same processes
D^{x,y}
and
Y
introduced there. The proof adapts the arguments of Theorem 2.2 in
[
DL12
]
. A difficulty arises from the fact that, unlike in the setting of
[

]
, the CBDI - and hence the process

-
may not have finite expectation. To overcome this, we modify the process by truncating all jumps above a fixed level
n
, replacing them by jumps of size
n
. This yields the processes

and

constructed in the proof below, for which the expectations are finite. In this framework, the argument for establishing the comparison property in
[

can be applied.

Lemma 5.3
.

The inequality (
23
) holds true, i.e. we have

\displaystyle\forall t\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994ptD_{t}^{x,y}\leq Y_{t}(x-y)\hskip 9.24994pt\textup{a.s.}

Proof.

Fix an integer
n\geq 1
, and let
J_{n}
denote the first time at which the process
D^{x,y}
undergoes a jump of size at least
n
, that is,

J_{n}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,D_{t}^{x,y}-D_{t-}^{x,y}\geq n\}.

Clearly,
J_{n}>0
almost surely. Define
(D_{t}^{n})_{t<J_{n}}:=(D_{t}^{x,y})_{t<J_{n}}
. By construction, the process
D^{n}
satisfies the same stochastic equation as
D^{x,y}
, except that jumps larger than
n
are truncated at size
n
. More precisely,
D^{n}
solves the following equation for

:

D_{t}^{n}=x-y+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{D_{s-}^{n}}W^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u)-\gamma\int_{0}^{t}D_{s}^{n}\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\left[I(X_{s}(y))-I(X_{s}(x))\right]\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{D_{s-}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}h\,\tilde{M}^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{D_{s-}^{n}}\int_{1}^{\infty}(h\wedge n)M^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h).

(25)

Fix
t\geq 0
. We aim to show that
\forall t<J_{n},\ D_{t}^{n}\leq Y_{t}^{n}
a.s., where
Y^{n}
denotes the unique strong solution to the stochastic equation:

Y^{n}_{t}=x-y+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{Y^{n}_{s-}}W^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u)+(b-\gamma)\int_{0}^{t}Y^{n}_{s}\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{Y^{n}_{s-}}\int_{0}^{1}h\tilde{M^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{Y^{n}_{s-}}\int_{1}^{\infty}(h\wedge n)M^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h).

(26)

We now introduce the process
\zeta^{n}
defined by

\forall t\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994pt\zeta^{n}_{t}:=D_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n}.

Our goal is to establish that,
\forall t<J_{n},\ \zeta_{t}^{n}\leq 0
a.s.. To this end, we will show that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\zeta_{t}^{n}\right)^{+}\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\right]=0,

where for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
z^{+}:=z\vee 0
.

We will need a smooth approximation of the positive part function
z\mapsto z^{+}
. Let
(\phi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of
C^{2}
functions satisfying the following properties:

•

For all
z\leq 0
,
\phi_{k}(z)=\phi_{k}^{\prime}(z)=0
.

•

For all
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi_{k}(z)\to z^{+}
as
k\to\infty
.

•

For all
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(z)\leq 1
,
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(z)\leq\frac{2}{kz}
.

The construction of such a sequence is recalled in the Appendix.

Define for all
t\geq 0
,
Z_{t}:=I(X_{t}(y))-I(X_{t}(x))
. By the one-sided Lipschitz assumption (B3), we have

\forall t<J_{n},\hskip 9.24994ptZ_{t}\leq bD_{t}^{x,y}=bD_{t}^{n}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

(27)

Finally, we note the following important property, which will be used later: on the event
\{\zeta_{s-}^{n}\leq 0\}
, the following holds almost surely:

\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})=\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})=\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})=0\hskip 9.24994pt\textup{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\zeta^{n}_{s-}+\mathbf{1}_{\{z\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}h-\mathbf{1}_{\{z\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}}h\leq 0\hskip 9.24994pt\textup{a.s.}.

(28)

Our aim is to analyze the expectation
\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{k}\left(\zeta^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\right]
and then let
k\rightarrow\infty
in order to establish the inequality:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\zeta^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)^{+}\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\right]\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\zeta^{n}_{s\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)^{+}\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq t<J_{n}\}}\right]\mathrm{d}s,

for some constant
c>0
. To this end, we apply Itô’s formula to the semimartingale
(\zeta^{n}_{t},t\geq 0)
using the function
\phi_{k}
. By applying
[
Sit05

, we have

\begin{split}\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{t})&=\phi_{k}(\zeta_{0})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\mathrm{d}s\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}u\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})(Z_{s-}-bY^{n}_{s-}-\gamma\zeta^{n}_{s-})\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\ \mathrm{d}s\\

&\hskip 170.71652pt-(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D^{n}_{s-}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y^{n}_{s-}\}})(h\wedge n)\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\Big]\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\mathrm{d}u\pi(\mathrm{d}h)\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+M^{n}(t),\end{split}

where
M^{n}(t)
is the local martingale given by:

\displaystyle M^{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}}\right)W^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-}+(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D^{n}_{s-}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y^{n}_{s-}\}})(h\wedge n))-\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\right]\tilde{M}^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h).

For any
m\geq 0
, set

\tau_{m}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,D_{t}^{n}\geq m\ \text{or}\ Y_{t}^{n}\geq m\}.

As we shall need it later, we show now that
M^{n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m})
is an
L^{2}
-martingale.
By Corollary 3, p.73 in
[
Pro04
]
, it suffices to show that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[M^{n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m}),M^{n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m})\big]_{t}\Big]<\infty.

We decompose
M^{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle M^{1,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}}\right)W^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u),

\displaystyle M^{2,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\phi_{k}\left(\zeta^{n}_{s-}+(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}})(h\wedge n)\right)-\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\right]\tilde{M}^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h).

By independence of
W^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime}
, the cross variation
[M^{1,n},M^{2,n}]
is identically zero. Since
M^{1,n}
is a continuous local martingale and
\phi_{k}^{\prime}\leq 1
, one has

\displaystyle\left[M^{1,n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m}),M^{1,n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m})\right]_{t}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime 2}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}|\zeta^{n}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s\leq m\sigma^{2}t.

The process
M^{2,n}
is purely discontinuous (in the sense of Protter’s definition of purely jump martingales in
[
Pro04
, page 193]
), so its quadratic variation is given by the sum of the squares of its jumps:

\displaystyle\left[M^{2,n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m}),M^{2,n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m})\right]_{t}=\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-}+\Delta_{s,u,h})-\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\right)^{2}M^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h),

where
\Delta_{s,z,h}:=(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}})(h\wedge n)
. The expectation formula for integrals of positive predictable processes with respect to a Poisson random measure provides:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big[\big[M^{2,n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m}),M^{2,n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m})\big]_{t}\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-}+\Delta_{s,u,h})-\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\right)^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{s<\tau_{m}\}}\right]\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}u\,\pi(\mathrm{d}h).

By the mean value theorem and
\phi_{k}^{\prime}\leq 1
, we have for all
s<\tau_{m}
:

\displaystyle\left(\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-}+\Delta_{s,z,h})-\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\right)^{2}\leq\left(\Delta_{s,z,h}\right)^{2}=\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{z\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{z\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}}\right)^{2}(h\wedge n)^{2}.

By Fubini–Tonelli’s theorem and
\sigma
-finiteness of
\pi
, we conclude:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[M^{2,n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m}),M^{2,n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m})\big]_{t}\Big]\leq mt\left(n^{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\pi(\mathrm{d}h)+\int_{0}^{1}h^{2}\pi(\mathrm{d}h)\right).

Combining both parts, we find

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[M^{n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m}),M^{n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m})\big]_{t}\Big]<\infty
,
and therefore
M^{n}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{m})
is an
L^{2}
-martingale.

We now analyze the limit of

\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{k}\left(\zeta^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\right]\hskip 9.24994pt\text{as }k\to\infty,

we examine each term in the Itô formula expansion (
29
).

•

Term involving
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}
:
For all
z\geq 0
, we have
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(z)\leq\frac{2}{kz}
, so that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\,\mathrm{d}s\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\leq\frac{2}{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\frac{1}{\zeta^{n}_{s-}}\zeta^{n}_{s-}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]=\frac{2}{k}\mathbb{E}[t\wedge\tau_{m}]\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0.

•

Drift term
:
Since
\forall s<J_{n},\ Z_{s}\leq bD_{s}^{n}
a.s. (see (
27
)), we deduce that:

\forall s<J_{n},\hskip 9.24994pt\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})(Z_{s-}-bY^{n}_{s-}-\gamma\zeta^{n}_{s-})\leq(b-\gamma)\zeta^{n}_{s-}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

•

Large jump term (first-order estimate)
:
By the mean value theorem, on
\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}
and using
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(z)\leq 1
, we have:

\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-}+\Delta_{s,z,h})-\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\leq\Delta_{s,z,h}\hskip 9.24994pt\textup{a.s.}.

•

Small jump term (second-order estimate)
:
Again by the mean value theorem and the bound
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(z)\leq\frac{2}{kz}
, we obtain:

\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-}+\Delta_{s,z,h})-\phi_{k}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})-\Delta_{s,z,h}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\leq\frac{1}{k\zeta^{n}_{s-}}\Delta_{s,z,h}^{2}.

Taking expectations in (
29
) and applying the above estimates yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{k}\left(\zeta^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\,\mathrm{d}s\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta^{n}_{s-})\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq D_{s-}^{n}\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq Y_{s-}^{n}\}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+(b-\gamma)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\zeta^{n}_{s-}\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+n\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\pi(\mathrm{d}h)\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\zeta^{n}_{s-}\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{k}\left(\int_{0}^{1}h^{2}\pi(\mathrm{d}h)\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta^{n}_{s-}>0\}}\mathrm{d}s\right],

where the first and last terms vanish as
k\to\infty
.

Letting
k\to\infty
and applying the monotone convergence theorem, we deduce:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\zeta^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)^{+}\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\right]\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\zeta^{n}_{s\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)^{+}\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq t<J_{n}\}}\right]\mathrm{d}s,

where
c:=b-\gamma+n\int_{1}^{\infty}\pi(\mathrm{d}h)>0
for
n
large enough, since
\bar{\pi}(1)>0
by assumption. An application of Grönwall’s lemma yields:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\zeta^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)^{+}\mathbf{1}_{\{t<J_{n}\}}\right]=0,

so that

\left(\zeta^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\right)^{+}=0\ \text{ on }\{t<J_{n}\}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

which in turn ensures that
D_{t}^{x,y}=D_{t}^{n}\leq Y_{t}^{n}
for all
t\in[0,J_{n})
almost surely.

Since furthermore the process
X
does not explode, we know that

J_{n}\rightarrow\infty
a.s. when
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence, the inequality
D_{t}^{x,y}\leq Y_{t}^{n}
holds a.s. for all
t\geq 0
. Finally, as
Y_{t}^{n}\uparrow Y_{t}
almost surely as
n\to\infty
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

5.3
The stochastic equation satisfied by
(X_{t}^{\infty},\ t>0)

This section is dedicated to the proofs of Proposition
3.2
and Theorem
3.2
. In all this section we assume that
I
satisfies the one-sided Lipschitz condition (B3), i.e. that:

\exists b>0,\ \forall y,z\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994ptI(y+z)-I(y)\geq-bz,

and that
\tau_{a}^{\infty,-}<\infty
a.s. for some
a\in(0,\infty)
.
We start by establishing Proposition
3.2
, this will be a consequence of Proposition
5.1
and Lemma
5.3
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.
Since
y_{n},y\in[0,\infty)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\lvert e^{-y_{n}}-e^{-y}\rvert\leq\lvert y_{n}-y\rvert,

and the local uniform convergence follows directly from Proposition
5.1
.

For the second part, assume that
I(0)=0
and use the one-sided Lipschitz condition (B3). For any
z\geq 0
,

I(z)\geq I(0)-bz.

By the comparison property (CP1), this implies that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
,

X_{t}^{x_{n}}\leq Y_{t}^{n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.,}

where
Y^{n}
denotes a CB started from
x_{n}
with branching mechanism
\tilde{\Psi}(x):=\Psi(x)+bx
.

From Lemma 1.5 in
[
DL14
, page 8]
, we know that for all
t\geq 0
,

Y_{t}^{x_{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{in probability}.

Consequently,
X_{t}^{x_{n}}\to 0
in probability as
n\to\infty
. Since the sequence
\left(X_{t}^{x_{n}}\right)_{n\geq 0}
is non-increasing a.s., the convergence in fact holds almost surely, which completes the proof.∎

To establish that
X^{\infty}
is the unique strong solution of a stochastic equation, we show that the processes

\big(X^{\infty}_{t},\ t\geq\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\big(\tilde{X}^{n}_{t-\tau_{n}^{-}(\infty)}(n),\ t\geq\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}\big)

have the same distribution, where
\tilde{X}^{n}
denotes the solution of a shifted stochastic equation for fixed
n\in(0,\infty)
.
This identity in law explicitly characterizes the stochastic equation satisfied by
X^{\infty}
.
Moreover, the regularity property of Proposition
5.1
also applies to the shifted process
\tilde{X}^{n}
.
The corresponding result, stated in Lemma
5.4
, provides the key tool to prove the convergence of

\big(X^{x}_{t},\ s\leq t\big)\text{ towards }\big(X^{\infty}_{t},\ s\leq t\big)\text{ in }(D,\rho_{\infty})\text{ as }x\to\infty.

The following proposition introduces the process called
\tilde{X}^{y}
as the unique strong solution of the shifted stochastic equation (
30
). Since by hypothesis
\tau_{a}^{\infty,-}<\infty
a.s., we have
\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}<\infty
a.s. for any
y\in(a,\infty)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For
y\in(a,\infty)
and
\xi
a positive random variable, there exists a unique càdlàg strong Markov process, which we denote by
\left(\tilde{X}_{t}^{y}(\xi),\ t\geq 0\right)
, solution of the stochastic equation:

X_{t}=\xi+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{X_{s}}\mathrm{d}B_{s+\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}-\gamma\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}}\int_{(0,1]}h\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s+\tau_{y}^{\infty,-},\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}}\int_{(1,\infty]}hM(\mathrm{d}s+\tau_{y}^{\infty,-},\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)-\int_{0}^{t}I(X_{s})\mathrm{d}s

(30)

until the first exit time of
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The shifted random measure

M^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h):=M(\mathrm{d}s+\tau_{y}^{\infty,-},\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

is a Poisson random measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with the same intensity measure as
M
, namely
\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u\pi(\mathrm{d}h)
. Similarly, the shifted process

\left(B^{\prime}_{s}\right)_{s\geq 0}:=\left(B_{s+\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}\right)_{s\geq 0}

is a Brownian motion with the same distribution as
B
, see e.g.
[
BFF18
, Page 16]
. Therefore, the stochastic equation (
30
) falls into the same class as the stochastic equation (
1
), and we deduce the existence and uniqueness of
\tilde{X}^{y}(\xi)
from Proposition
2.1
.∎

For any positive random variable
\xi
, and
a,b\in[0,\infty]
, we set:

\tilde{\tau}_{a}^{y,-}(\xi)=\inf\{t\geq 0,\ \tilde{X}_{t}^{y}(\xi)\leq a\}\hskip 9.24994pt,\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{\tau}_{b}^{y,+}(\xi)=\inf\{t\geq 0,\ \tilde{X}_{t}^{y}(\xi)\geq b\}

We now present two convergence lemmas for the family of processes
\tilde{X}^{y}
.
Lemma
5.4
establishes a regularity property with respect to the initial condition, in the same spirit as Proposition
5.1
, but for the shifted process
\tilde{X}^{y}
.
Lemma
5.5
then provides a central convergence result.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
I
satisfies the one-sided Lipschitz condition B3. Fix
y\in(a,\infty)
,
\xi_{n},\xi
positive random variables such that
\xi_{n}\rightarrow\xi
a.s. when
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Then, for any
t\geq 0
:

\underset{n\uparrow\infty\atop n\in\mathbb{N}}{\lim}\ \underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\ |\tilde{X}_{s}^{y}(\xi_{n})-\tilde{X}_{s}^{y}(\xi)|=0\ \ \textup{a.s.}.

(31)

Proof.

The proof uses the same arguments as the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Fix
t\geq 0
and
y\in(a,\infty)
.

\underset{k\rightarrow\infty\atop k\in\mathbb{N}}{\lim}\ \underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\ \left(\tilde{X}_{s}^{y}(y)-X^{k}_{s+\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}\right)=0\ \ \ \textup{a.s.}

(32)

Proof.

By the strong Markov property, we have the distributional identity

\left(X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{-}(k)+t},\ t\geq 0\right)\overset{d}{=}\left(\tilde{X}_{t}^{y}(y),\ t\geq 0\right).

Since
\tilde{X}_{t}^{y}(y)\to y
almost surely as
t\to 0
, and
\tau_{y}^{k,-}\uparrow\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}
almost surely as
k\to\infty
, it follows that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0
and
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq\delta}\left|\tilde{X}_{t}^{y}(y)-y\right|>\epsilon\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2},\hskip 18.49988pt\forall k\geq k_{0},\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{y}^{-}(\infty)>\tau_{y}^{-}(k)+\delta\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Then, for all
k\geq k_{0}
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}-y\right|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}-y\right|>\epsilon,\ \tau_{y}^{k,-}\leq\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}\leq\tau_{y}^{k,-}+\delta\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq\delta}\left|X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{k,-}+t}-y\right|>\epsilon\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq\delta}\left|\tilde{X}_{t}^{y}(y)-y\right|>\epsilon\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon.

This shows that
X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}\to y
in probability as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, since the mapping
k\mapsto X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}
is almost surely non-decreasing, convergence actually holds almost surely:

X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}\longrightarrow y\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s. as }k\to\infty.

By uniqueness of solutions to the stochastic equation (
30
), we also have:

X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}+t}=\tilde{X}_{t}^{y}\left(X_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}^{k}\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}

Hence, applying Lemma
5.4
, we obtain:

\sup_{s\leq t}\left|X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}+s}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{y}(y)\right|=\sup_{s\leq t}\left|\tilde{X}_{s}^{y}\left(X^{k}_{\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}}\right)-\tilde{X}_{s}^{y}(y)\right|\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}

∎

Throughout the remainder of this section, the integers
n
in the definition of
\tilde{X}^{n}(n)
are assumed to be larger than
a
, so that
\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}<\infty
a.s. By uniqueness of the almost sure limit and as a consequence of Lemma
5.4
, we obtain:

\forall t\geq\tau_{n}^{\infty,-},\hskip 9.24994ptX_{t}^{\infty}=\tilde{X}_{t-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{n}(n).

(33)

Since
\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}\downarrow 0
a.s. as
n\to\infty
, this provides an explicit representation of
X^{\infty}_{t}
for
t\in(0,\infty)
. Moreover, for any
n\geq m
and
t\geq\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}\geq\tau_{m}^{\infty,-}
, Lemma
5.5
ensures that

\tilde{X}_{t-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{n}(n)=\tilde{X}_{t-\tau_{m}^{\infty,-}}^{m}(m)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.},

so the expression in (
33
) is almost surely well-defined and independent of
n
for
t>0
. Since the processes
\tilde{X}^{y}
have càdlàg paths, the process
X^{\infty}
also inherits this property. Finally, because each
\tilde{X}^{n}(n)
solves the stochastic differential equation (
30
), the representation (
33
) implies that
X^{\infty}
satisfies the same type of equation. This is formalized in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

The process
X^{\infty}
satisfies
X^{\infty}_{t}\rightarrow\infty
a.s. when
t\downarrow 0
and is a strong solution of the stochastic equation :

X_{t}=X_{r}+\sigma\int_{r}^{t}\sqrt{X_{s}}\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\gamma\int_{r}^{t}X_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}}\int_{0}^{1}h\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)\\
+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}}\int_{1}^{\infty}hM(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)-\int_{r}^{t}I(X_{s})\mathrm{d}s,\hskip 9.24994pt0<r\leq t.

(34)

Proof.

Fix
t>0
. There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0\leq\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}\leq t
almost surely. Then, by Lemma
5.5
,

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\hskip 9.24994ptX^{\infty}_{t}=\tilde{X}_{t-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{n}(n)\geq X_{t}^{k}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}

Hence, for any
x\in(0,\infty)
, we have
X^{\infty}_{t}\geq X_{t}^{x}
. On the event
\{0<t<\tau_{y}^{x,-}\}
, this yields
X^{\infty}_{t}>y
. Since
\tau_{y}^{x,-}\uparrow\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}
a.s. as
x\to\infty
, we deduce that
X^{\infty}_{t}>y
on the event
\{0<t<\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}\}
. As
\tau_{y}^{\infty,-}>0
a.s., this proves that

X^{\infty}_{t}\to\infty\text{ as }t\to 0\text{ a.s.}

Now fix
t\geq r>0
, and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}\leq r
a.s. By definition of
\tilde{X}^{n}(n)
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{t}^{n}(n)

\displaystyle=n+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\tilde{X}_{s}^{n}(n)}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s+\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}-\gamma\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{X}_{s}^{n}(n)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tilde{X}_{s-}^{n}(n)}\int_{0}^{1}h\,\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s+\tau_{n}^{\infty,-},\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tilde{X}_{s-}^{n}(n)}\int_{1}^{\infty}h\,M(\mathrm{d}s+\tau_{n}^{\infty,-},\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-\int_{0}^{t}I(\tilde{X}_{s}^{n}(n))\,\mathrm{d}s.

Making the time change
s\mapsto s-\tau_{n}^{-}(\infty)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{t-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{n}(n)

\displaystyle=n+\sigma\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}\sqrt{\tilde{X}_{s-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{n}(n)}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\gamma\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}\tilde{X}_{s-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{n}(n)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tilde{X}_{(s-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-})-}^{n}(n)}\int_{0}^{1}h\,\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tilde{X}_{(s-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-})-}^{n}(n)}\int_{1}^{\infty}h\,M(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}I\big(\tilde{X}_{s-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{n}(n)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
X^{\infty}_{s}=\tilde{X}_{s-\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{n}(n)
for all

, this simplifies to:

\displaystyle X^{\infty}_{t}

\displaystyle=n+\sigma\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}\sqrt{X^{\infty}_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\gamma\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}X^{\infty}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}(\infty)}\int_{0}^{1}h\,\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}(\infty)}\int_{1}^{\infty}h\,M(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-\int_{\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}}^{t}I(X^{\infty}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

A similar expression holds for
X^{\infty}_{r}
, so subtracting yields:

\displaystyle X^{\infty}_{t}-X^{\infty}_{r}

\displaystyle=\sigma\int_{r}^{t}\sqrt{X^{\infty}_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\gamma\int_{r}^{t}X^{\infty}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{1}h\,\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}^{\infty}}\int_{1}^{\infty}h\,M(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-\int_{r}^{t}I(X^{\infty}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

∎

We have shown that the process starting from infinity
X^{\infty}
is a solution to the stochastic differential equation (
30
) with initial state
\infty
. The following lemma establishes the uniqueness of this solution, which follows naturally from the uniqueness result for the solution of (
1
) stated in Proposition
2.1
and the comparison property (CP1).

Lemma 5.7
.

There is a unique solution
X
of (
34
), that comes down from infinity and such that
X_{t}\rightarrow\infty
a.s. as
t\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

Let us first recall an important fact : for any
0<r
, the solution
\left(Z_{t},\ t\geq r\right)
to the stochastic differential equation

Z_{t}=z+\sigma\int_{r}^{t}\sqrt{Z_{s-}}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\gamma\int_{r}^{t}Z_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{Z_{s-}}\int_{0}^{1}h\,\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)\\
+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{Z_{s-}}\int_{1}^{\infty}h\,M(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)-\int_{r}^{t}I(Z_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

(35)

satisfies a comparison principle with respect to the initial value
z
, similar to property (CP1).

Let
Y
be a solution of (
34
) such that
Y_{t}\to\infty
a.s. as
t\to 0
and
Y
comes down from infinity, i.e.

\forall t>0,\hskip 9.24994ptY_{t}<\infty\hskip 18.49988pt\textup{a.s.}.

Fix
x>0
.
By càdlàg property and Fatou’s lemma, we have
\underset{t\rightarrow 0}{\liminf}\,\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}^{x}\leq Y_{t}\right)=1
. By the Markov property at time
t
and by comparison:

\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t+s}^{x}\leq Y_{t+s},\ \forall s\geq 0,\ X_{t}^{x}\leq Y_{t}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}^{x}\leq Y_{t}\right).

By letting
t
go to
0
, using right continuity, we get:

\mathbb{P}\left(X_{s}^{x}\leq Y_{s},\ s\geq 0\right)=1.

Thus:

\forall s\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994ptX_{s}^{\infty}\leq Y_{s},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

(36)

Now let
\left(X^{x}_{\epsilon,t},\ t\geq\epsilon\right)
be the pathwise unique solution of:

X_{t}=x+\sigma\int_{\epsilon}^{t}\sqrt{X_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\gamma\int_{\epsilon}^{t}X_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\epsilon}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}}\int_{0}^{1}h\,\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)\\
+\int_{\epsilon}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}}\int_{1}^{\infty}h\,M(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)-\int_{\epsilon}^{t}I(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

(37)

By the comparison property, the following almost sure limit is well-defined

X^{\infty}_{1/n,t}:=\lim_{x\to\infty}X^{x}_{1/n,t},\ \ \forall t\geq 1/n.

Conditionally on
Y_{1/n}
, since
Y_{1/n}<\infty
a.s., by comparison again, one has

\forall t\geq 1/n,\hskip 9.24994ptY_{t}\leq X_{1/n,t}^{\infty}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

(38)

Next, we perform the time shift
s\mapsto s-1/n
. The process
\left(X^{x}_{t-1/n},\ t\geq 1/n\right)
satisfies:

X_{t}=x+\sigma\int_{1/n}^{t}\sqrt{X_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s-1/n}-\gamma\int_{1/n}^{t}X_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{1/n}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}}\int_{0}^{1}h\,\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s-1/n,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)+\int_{1/n}^{t}\int_{0}^{X_{s-}}\int_{1}^{\infty}h\,M(\mathrm{d}s-1/n,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)\\
\!-\int_{1/n}^{t}I(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

(39)

The processes
\big(X^{x}_{1/n,t},t\geq 1/n\big)
and
\big(X^{x}_{t-1/n},t\geq 1/n\big)
have the same law since
\left(B_{s-1/n},s\geq 1/n\right)
and
\tilde{M}(\mathrm{d}s-1/n,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)
are independent of each other, and with the same laws as
(B_{s},s\geq 0)
and
M(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}h)
, see the proof of Proposition
5.1
. Combining inequalities (
36
) and (
38
), and passing to the limit
x\to\infty
,
n\to\infty
, we obtain that the processes
\left(X^{\infty}_{t},t>0\right)
and
\left(Y_{t},t>0\right)
are equal in distribution. Since
\forall t>0,\ X^{\infty}_{t}\leq Y_{t}
a.s., it follows that the two processes are indistinguishable.
∎

Recall the definition of the metric
\rho_{\infty}
on the space
D
of
[0,\infty]
-valued càdlàg functions (
4
).
It remains to prove the local uniform convergence in the Skorokhod space
D
of the process started from
x
towards the process started from infinity, as
x\to\infty
. In our first construction of the process starting from infinity, we established the almost sure pointwise convergence

\forall t\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994ptX_{t}^{x}\xrightarrow[x\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}X^{\infty}_{t}.

The following lemma establishes the stronger convergence in
(D,\rho_{\infty})
.

Lemma 5.8
.

For all
t\in[0,\infty)
,

\left(X_{s}^{x},\ s\leq t\right)\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\left(X^{\infty}_{s},\ s\leq t\right)\hskip 18.49988pt\textup{in}\hskip 9.24994pt(D,\rho_{\infty}).

(40)

When furthermore the
\mathrm{CBDI}
process
X^{\infty}
gets extinct in finite time a.s. the convergence (
40
) holds for
t=\infty
.

Proof.

Fix
t\geq 0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We know that

\tau_{n}^{k,-}\uparrow\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s. as }k\to\infty.

Since the process has no negative jumps, it follows that
\tau_{n}^{\infty,-}>\tau_{2n}^{\infty,-}
almost surely. Therefore we can select
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\tau_{n}^{k,-}>\tau_{2n}^{\infty,-}
a.s.. Fix now
x\geq k
. Using the inequality
1-e^{-z}\leq z
for any
z\geq 0
, we can bound the
\rho_{\infty}
-distance by

\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}\left(\left(X_{s}^{x}\right)_{s\leq t},\left(X^{\infty}_{s}\right)_{s\leq t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq s\leq\tau_{n}^{k,-}}\left|e^{-X_{s}^{x}}-e^{-X^{\infty}_{s}}\right|\vee\sup_{\tau_{2n}^{\infty,-}\leq s\leq t}\left|X^{\infty}_{s}-X_{s}^{x}\right|.

We now control the second term using Lemma
5.5
:

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau_{2n}^{\infty,-}\leq s\leq t}\left|X^{\infty}_{s}-X_{s}^{x}\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq s\leq t-\tau_{2n}^{\infty,-}}\left|\tilde{X}_{s}^{2n}(2n)-X^{x}_{s+\tau_{2n}^{\infty,-}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\ \ \ \ \sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left|\tilde{X}_{s}^{2n}(2n)-X^{x}_{s+\tau_{2n}^{\infty,-}}\right|\xrightarrow[x\to\infty]{}0.

To control the first term, we use the fact that, by definition of
\tau_{n}^{k,-}
, we have

\forall s\leq\tau_{n}^{k,-},\ X_{s}(k)\geq n
a.s.. By the comparison property (CP1), we also have

\forall s\leq\tau_{n}^{k,-},\hskip 9.24994ptX^{\infty}_{s}\geq X_{s}^{x}\geq X_{s}^{k}\geq n\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

Hence,

\sup_{0\leq s\leq\tau_{n}^{k,-}}\,\left|e^{-X_{s}^{x}}-e^{-X^{\infty}_{s}}\right|\leq 2e^{-n}.

Since
n
is arbitrary, by letting
x\to\infty
, we get the desired convergence

\rho_{\infty}\left(\left(X_{s}^{x}\right)_{s\leq t},\left(X^{\infty}_{s}\right)_{s\leq t}\right)\xrightarrow[x\to\infty]{}0.

Assume now that
X^{\infty}
gets extinct almost surely. Since
\tau_{0}^{x,-}\leq\tau_{0}^{\infty,-}
a.s., we have for all
x\in(0,\infty],

\rho_{\infty}\big((X_{t}^{x})_{t\geq 0},(X_{t}^{\infty})_{t\geq 0}\big)=\underset{0\leq t\leq\tau_{0}^{\infty,-}}{\sup}\,\Big|e^{-X_{t}^{x}}-e^{-X_{t}^{\infty}}\Big|\xrightarrow[x\rightarrow\infty]{}0\hskip 18.49988pt\textup{a.s..}

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.
The result is an immediate consequence of Lemmas
5.6
,
5.7
and
5.8
. ∎

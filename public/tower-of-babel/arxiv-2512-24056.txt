Policy Mirror Descent with Temporal Difference Learning: Sample Complexity under Online Markov Data
Wenye Li, Hongxu Chen, Jiacai Liu, Ke Wei

Abstract
This paper studies the policy mirror descent (PMD) method, which is a general policy optimization framework in reinforcement learning and can cover a wide range of policy gradient methods by specifying difference mirror maps. Existing sample complexity analysis for policy mirror descent either focuses on the generative sampling model, or the Markovian sampling model but with the action values being explicitly approximated to certain pre-specified accuracy. In contrast, we consider the sample complexity of policy mirror descent with temporal difference (TD) learning under the Markovian sampling model. Two algorithms called Expected TD-PMD and Approximate TD-PMD have been presented, which are off-policy and mixed policy algorithms respectively. Under a small enough constant policy update step size, the O ~ ​ ( ε − 2 ) \tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}) (a logarithm factor about ε \varepsilon is hidden in O ~ ​ ( ⋅ ) \tilde{O}(\cdot) ) sample complexity can be established for them to achieve average-time ε \varepsilon -optimality. The sample complexity is further improved to O ⁡ ( ε − 2 ) O(\varepsilon^{-2}) (without the hidden logarithm factor) to achieve the last-iterate ε \varepsilon -optimality based on adaptive policy update step sizes.

Introduction

The goal of Reinforcement learning (RL) is to solve a sequential decision problem via interacting with the environment
(
Sutton and Barto 2018
)
, and it plays an important role in various application fields including robotics
(
Hwangbo et al. 2019
;
Lee et al. 2020
;

;

)
, structure explorations
(

;
Fawzi et al. 2022
)
, and the post-training in Large Language Models (LLMs)
(
Zhang et al. 2025b
)
. Markov decision process (MDP) is a fundamental model for RL. Let
\Delta(\mathcal{X})
denotes the probabilistic simplex over set
\mathcal{X}
. An MDP can be described as a tuple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma)
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the state space,
\mathcal{A}
is the action space,
P:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\Delta(\mathcal{S})
is the state transition model,
r:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
is the reward function, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the discounted factor. To interact with the environment, the agent is equipped with a policy
\pi:\mathcal{S}\to\Delta(\mathcal{A})
. At each time, the agent chooses an action
a\in\mathcal{A}
at state
s\in\mathcal{S}
with probability
\pi(a|s)
, then it transfers to a new state
s^{\prime}
with the probability
P(s^{\prime}|s,a)
with a reward
r(s,a)
. By repeating the procedure above, the agent obtains a trajectory
\{(s_{t},a_{t},r_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
, where
s_{t}\sim P(\cdot|s_{t-1},a_{t-1})
,
a_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{t})
, and
r_{t}=r(s_{t},a_{t})
. The state value induced by policy
\pi
at state
s\in\mathcal{S}
is defined as the expectation of the discounted cumulative rewards over trajectories,

\displaystyle V^{\pi}(s):={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r(s_{t},a_{t})\,\bigg|\,s_{0}=s,\pi\right].

Similarly, the action value function induced by
\pi
is defined as

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}(s,a):={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r(s_{t},a_{t})\,\bigg|\,s_{0}=s,\;a_{0}=a,\pi\right].

The goal of RL is to seek the optimal policy which maximizes the state values or action values,

\displaystyle\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\;V^{\pi}(\mu):=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mu}[V^{\pi}(s)]\quad\mbox{or}\quad\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\;Q^{\pi}(\rho):=\mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim\rho}\left[Q^{\pi}(s,a)\right],

(1)

where
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
,
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A})
are some distribution and
\Pi
denotes the set of all admissible policies.

Policy mirror descent (PMD) is an instance of policy optimization methods for solving (
1
). Let
h
be a function that is proper, closed, strictly convex, essentially smooth, and satisfying
\mbox{rint dom}(h)\cap\Delta(\mathcal{A})\neq\emptyset
(see for example
Beck 2017
for related definitions). The policy update of PMD is given by
(
Xiao 2022
;
Lan 2021
)

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S}:\quad\pi^{+}(\cdot|s)=\underset{p\in\Delta(\mathcal{A})}{\arg\max}\left\{\eta\left\langle p,\,Q^{\pi}(s,\cdot)\right\rangle-D_{h}(p\,\|\,\pi(\cdot|s))\right\},

where
\eta>0
is the policy update step size and
D_{h}(p\,\|\,q)=h(p)-h(q)-\left\langle\nabla h(q),\,p-q\right\rangle
is the Bregman divergence induced by
h
. The PMD update fits into the general mirror descent (MD) framework in optimization
Xiao 2022
and covers a wide range of policy gradient methods by specifying different
h
. Thus, it has received intensive investigations recently.

1.2
Motivation and main contributions

Existing sample complexity results for PMD-type algorithms mostly focus on the setting of generative model
(
Lan 2021
;
Xiao 2022
;
Johnson et al. 2023
;
Protopapas and Barakat 2024
)
or the Markovian sampling but with nested-loop conditional temporal difference learning (CTD) estimation
(
Lan 2021
;
Li et al. 2024
)
, where action values are explicitly approximated to certain prerequisite accuracy. In contrast, TD-PMD
(
Geist et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2025b
)
updates the critic by applying only one-step Bellman operator over the last value estimate (without controlling the estimation accuracy). However, their analysis is limited to the exact setting and the generative model.
In this paper, we consider the more practical setting which combines both Markovian sampling (with a mini-batch mechanism) and TD learning. In particular, two algorithms including off-policy Expected TD-PMD (Algorithm
1
) and mixed-policy Approximate TD-PMD (Algorithm
3
) are presented, and their sample complexities have been investigated. We focus on the tabular setting in this paper. Despite this, it is worth emphasizing that even though various policy gradient methods with policy/value function approximations have been studied, it often requires strong assumptions in the parameter domain (such as the smoothness and non-degeneracy of the policy) which are not applicable for the tabular setting.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.

•

Under a small enough constant policy update step size, both Expected TD-PMD and Approximate TD-PMD enjoy a
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
sample complexity to obtain an average
\varepsilon
-optimal solution, where some constants and poly-logarithmic factors are hidden in
\tilde{O}(\cdot)
. Note that existing average-time sample complexities for PMD are also
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
under the generative model or the Markovian sampling with an inner-loop CTD learning
(
Lan 2021
;
Li et al. 2024
)
. In contrast, in Expected TD-PMD and Approximate TD-PMD, the new estimate of action value is obtained by applying only one step weighted Bellman operator (in sample form) to the last estimate, without an inner-loop critic learning to approximate the exact action values accurately. The crux in our analysis is the development of a route to bound the stochastic bias term

2.1
More on MDP

Throughout the paper, we use vectors
V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
and
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
to represent the value functions associated with the state and action, respectively. In addition, for any
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
, and
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A})
, let
V(\mu)=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mu}[V(s)]
and
Q(\rho)=\mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim\rho}[Q(s,a)]
.
Note that we will arrange any
\pi\in\Pi
and vector
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
first by states and then by actions, that is,

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=[Q(s_{1},a_{1}),\dots,Q(s_{1},a_{|\mathcal{A}|}),Q(s_{2},a_{1}),\dots,Q(s_{2},a_{|\mathcal{A}|}),\dots,Q(s_{|\mathcal{S}|},a_{|\mathcal{A}|})]^{\top},

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle=[\pi(a_{1}|s_{1}),\dots,\pi(a_{|\mathcal{A}|}|s_{1}),\pi(a_{1}|s_{2}),\dots,\pi(a_{|\mathcal{A}|}|s_{2}),\dots,\pi(a_{|\mathcal{A}|}|s_{|\mathcal{S}|})]^{\top}.

Define the state/action Bellman operator

\displaystyle[\mathcal{T}^{\pi}V](s)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s),\,s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}[r(s,a)+\gamma\,V(s^{\prime})],

\displaystyle[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q](s,a)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a),\,a^{\prime}\sim\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime})}[r(s,a)+\gamma\,Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})].

It can be easily verified that
V^{\pi}
and
Q^{\pi}
satisfy
\mathcal{T}^{\pi}V^{\pi}=V^{\pi}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q^{\pi}=Q^{\pi}
, and both
\mathcal{T}^{\pi}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}
are
\gamma
-contraction operators over
\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
and
\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
with respect to the
l_{\infty}
-norm. Furthermore, define the state/action transition matrix as

\displaystyle P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}^{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|}

\displaystyle:\quad P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}(s,s^{\prime})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)P(s^{\prime}|s,a),

\displaystyle P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\times|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}

\displaystyle:\quad P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}((s,a),(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}))=P(s^{\prime}|s,a)\,\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime}).

That is,
P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
is the transition matrix in the state space and
P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
is the transition matrix in the state-action space. Then the Bellman operators can be written in the following vector form

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\pi}V=r^{\pi}+\gamma P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}V,\quad\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q=r+\gamma P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}Q,

where
r^{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
with
r^{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)r(s,a)
and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
is the reward function. Moreover, define the optimal Bellman operators:

\displaystyle[\mathcal{T}V](s)

\displaystyle:=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\,[\mathcal{T}^{\pi}V](s)=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\,\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}}[r(s,a)+\gamma V(s^{\prime})],

\displaystyle[\mathcal{F}Q](s,a)

\displaystyle:=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\,[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q](s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}}\left[\max_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\,Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\right].

Let
V^{*}
and
Q^{*}
be the fixed point of
\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{F}
, respectively. It is well-known
(
Sutton and Barto 2018
)
that
V^{*}
and
Q^{*}
is the optimal solution to the problem (
1
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\forall\,s,a:\quad V^{*}(s)=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\,V^{\pi}(s),\quad Q^{*}(s,a)=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\,Q^{\pi}(s,a),

and there exists at least one optimal (deterministic) policy
\pi^{*}
such that
V^{\pi^{*}}=V^{*}
and
Q^{\pi^{*}}=Q^{*}
. Let
\Pi^{*}:=\left\{\pi^{*}\in\Pi:\;\;V^{\pi^{*}}=V^{*}\right\}
be the optimal policy set.

For conciseness, it is assumed that
r(s,a)\in[0,1]
. Then, for any policy
\pi\in\Pi
, there holds

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S},\;a\in\mathcal{A}:\quad 0\leq V^{\pi}(s)\leq V^{*}(s)\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma},\quad 0\leq Q^{\pi}(s,a)\leq Q^{*}(s,a)\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

(2)

Define the state/action visitation measure as

\displaystyle d^{\pi}_{\mu}(s)

\displaystyle:=(1-\gamma)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\cdot\mathbf{1}(s_{t}=s)\;\bigg|\;s_{0}\sim\mu\right],

\displaystyle v^{\pi}_{\rho}(s,a)

\displaystyle:=(1-\gamma)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\cdot\mathbf{1}(s_{t}=s,a_{t}=a)\;\bigg|\;(s_{0},a_{0})\sim\rho\right].

The visitation measures corresponding to an optimal policy will be denoted as
d^{*}_{\mu}
and
v^{*}_{\rho}
. The performance difference lemma is a fundamental result in policy optimization.

Lemma 2.1

(Performance difference lemma
(
Kakade and Langford 2002
;
Liu et al. 2025b
)
)
.

For any
\pi\in\Pi
,
V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
,
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
,
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
, and
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A})
, there holds

\displaystyle[V^{\pi}-V](\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left[\mathcal{T}^{\pi}V-V\right](d^{\pi}_{\mu}),

\displaystyle[Q^{\pi}-Q](\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q-Q\right](v^{\pi}_{\rho}).

As a corollary, we can obtain the following useful result.

Lemma 2.2
.

For arbitrary
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
,

\displaystyle\|Q^{\pi}-Q^{\pi^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma|\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\cdot\|\pi-\pi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

By the performance difference lemma, one has

\displaystyle\forall\,(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}:\quad|Q^{\pi}(s,a)-Q^{\pi^{\prime}}(s,a)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}|[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q^{\pi^{\prime}}-Q^{\pi^{\prime}}](d^{\pi}_{s,a})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}|[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q^{\pi^{\prime}}-\mathcal{F}^{\pi^{\prime}}Q^{\pi^{\prime}}](d^{\pi}_{s,a})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\|\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q^{\pi}-\mathcal{F}^{\pi^{\prime}}Q^{\pi^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}.

Since

\displaystyle\forall\,(s,a):\quad|\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q^{\pi^{\prime}}(s,a)-\mathcal{F}^{\pi^{\prime}}Q^{\pi^{\prime}}(s,a)|

\displaystyle=\gamma\cdot|\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}[\langle Q^{\pi^{\prime}}(s^{\prime},\cdot),\,\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime})-\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\rangle]|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\cdot|\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}[\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{1-\gamma}\cdot\|\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime})-\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\|_{\infty}]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma|\mathcal{A}|}{1-\gamma}\cdot\|\pi-\pi^{\prime}\|_{\infty},

it follows that

\displaystyle\forall\,\pi,\pi^{\prime}:\quad\|Q^{\pi}-Q^{\pi^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\gamma|\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\cdot\left\|\pi-\pi^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty},

which completes the proof.
∎

2.2
More on TD-PMD

As mentioned previously, TD-PMD updates the critic by tracking a sequence of TD learning of the action values which does not only involve current policy but also relies on the last value estimator. More precisely, TD-PMD admits the following form in the exact setting:

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S}:\quad\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)

\displaystyle=\underset{p\in\Delta(\mathcal{A})}{\arg\max}\,\left\{\eta_{k}\left\langle p,\,Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right\rangle-D^{p}_{\pi}(s)\right\},

(3)

\displaystyle Q^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k},

where
\eta_{k}>0
is the step size,
h
is a convex function over
\Delta(\mathcal{A})
, and
D_{h}(p\,||\,q)
is the Bregman divergence induced by
h
. Note that here and in the rest of the paper, we will omit the subscript
h
in
D_{h}
and use the short notation
D^{\pi}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)
for
D_{h}(\pi(\cdot|s)\,||\,\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s))
and
D^{p}_{\pi}(s)
for
D_{h}(p\,||\,\pi(\cdot|s))
. In contrast to the classical PMD which utilizes the true value function
Q^{\pi_{k}}
as the critic, TD-PMD obtains the new critic by only performing one-step of Bellman iteration based on the current policy and the last critic estimation. When
h
is specified to the negative entropy
h(p)=-\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}p(a)\log p(a)
and quadratic function
h(p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{2}^{2}
, TD-PMD reduces to TD-NPG and TD-PQA, respectively, with the following policy update rules:

(TD-NPG)

\displaystyle\pi_{k+1}(a|s)\propto\pi_{k}(a|s)\cdot\exp\left(\eta\,Q^{k}(s,a)\right),

(TD-PQA)

\displaystyle\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\textup{Proj}_{\Delta(\mathcal{A})}\left[\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)+\eta\,Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right],

where in TD-PQA,
\textup{Proj}_{\Delta(\mathcal{A})}
denotes the projection on the probabilistic simplex
\Delta(\mathcal{A})
.

The following three-point-descent lemma (see for example
Xiao 2022
;
Chen and Teboulle 1993
) is essential in the analysis of the PMD type methods.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\pi\in\Pi
,
\eta>0
, and
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
, consider the following update:

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S}:\quad\pi^{+}(\cdot|s)=\underset{p\in\Delta(\mathcal{A})}{\arg\max}\left\{\eta\left\langle p,\,Q(s,\cdot)\right\rangle-D^{p}_{\pi}(s)\right\}.

There holds
\pi^{+}(\cdot|s)\in\mbox{{rint dom }}h\cap\Delta(\mathcal{A})
and

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S},\;p\in\Delta(\mathcal{A}):\quad\eta\left\langle\pi^{+}(\cdot|s)-p,\;Q(s,\cdot)\right\rangle\geq D^{\pi^{+}}_{\pi}(s)+D^{p}_{\pi^{+}}(s)-D^{p}_{\pi}(s).

For the mirror map
h
being the negative entropy, there holds
\mbox{rint dom }h=\mbox{int }\Delta(\mathcal{A})
. Hence the policies generated by softmax NPG update always stay in
\mbox{rint }\Delta(\mathcal{A})
if the initial policy lies in
\mbox{rint }\Delta(\mathcal{A})
.

3
Expected TD-PMD with Off-Policy Markov Data

As shown in
Liu et al. 2025b
, TD-PMD admits the same sublinear convergence as PMD in the exact setting. Moreover, under the generative model where
[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}](s,a)
in (
3
) is estimated via random samples for every
(s,a)
, the
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
sample complexity of TD-PMD is also established therein. In contrast, we will consider the sample complexity of TD-PMD under the more practical and challenging online Markov sampling model.

This section first considers the case where the Markov data are obtained through an behavior policy
\pi_{b}
which is sufficiently exploratory.
Let
\tau_{k}
be the trajectory data of length
B_{k}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tau_{k}=\{(s_{t}^{k},a_{t}^{k},r^{k}_{t},s_{t+1}^{k})\}_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1},\quad\mbox{where}\;a^{k}_{t}\sim\pi_{b}(\cdot|s_{t}^{k}),\;\;r^{k}_{t}=r(s^{k}_{t},a^{k}_{t}),\;\;s^{k}_{t+1}\sim P(\cdot|s_{t}^{k},a_{t}^{k}).

For each tuple
(s_{t}^{k},a_{t}^{k},r_{t}^{k},s_{t+1}^{k})
, the expected TD error of critic
Q^{k}
with respect to the target policy
\pi_{k+1}
is constructed by

\displaystyle\forall\,(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}:\quad\delta_{t}^{k}(s,a):=\mathds{1}_{(s^{k}_{t},a^{k}_{t})=(s,a)}\cdot\left[r_{t}^{k}+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s_{t+1}^{k})}\left[Q^{k}(s_{t+1}^{k},a)\right]-Q^{k}(s^{k}_{t},a^{k}_{t})\right].

Notice that when conditioned on
(s_{t},a_{t})=(s,a)
, the term
r_{t}^{k}+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s_{t+1}^{k})}[Q^{k}(s_{t+1}^{k},a)]
is an unbiased estimator of
[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}](s,a)
. The expected TD error constructed from the whole trajectory
\tau_{k}
is defined as the weighted average of
\delta_{t}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\forall\,(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}:\quad\bar{\delta}_{k}(s,a):={\sum_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1}c_{t}^{k}\cdot\delta_{t}^{k}(s,a)},

where
c_{t}^{k}\geq 0
are weights satisfying
\sum_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1}c_{t}^{k}=1
.We then update the critic via the TD learning method, i.e.,

\displaystyle\forall\,(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}:\quad Q^{k+1}(s,a)=Q^{k}(s,a)+\alpha_{k}\cdot\bar{\delta}_{k}(s,a),

where
\alpha_{k}>0
is the critic update step size.

Figure 1:
An illustration of Expected TD-PMD with off-policy Markov data (Algorithm
1
),

The formal description of the Expected TD-PMD with the Off-Policy Markov data is given in Algorithm
1
, with an illustration given in Figure
1
.
First note that “expected” refers to the expectation term
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s_{t+1}^{k})}\left[Q^{k}(s_{t+1}^{k},a)\right]
, which is computable in the tabular case since
\pi_{k+1}
is known. Moreover, Algorithm
1
is an off-policy algorithm, as the behavior policy
\pi_{b}
for sampling the data is not the target policy
\pi_{k+1}
. It is worth remarking that when the policy update step size
\eta_{k}
goes to infinity, the updated policy
\pi_{k+1}
is actually the greedy policy induced by the action value
Q^{k}
. In such case, Expected TD-PMD reduces to the Q-learning algorithm
(
Sutton and Barto 2018
)
with batch Markov data. Thus the Expected TD-PMD can be viewed as a “smoothed” Q-learning algorithm. Furthermore, for the on-policy version with infinity critic update step size, i.e.,
\eta_{k}=\infty
and
\pi_{b}=\pi_{k+1}
, Expected TD-PMD with
B_{k}=1
reduces to the expected SARSA algorithm
(
Sutton and Barto 2018
)
.

s_{0}

Before proceeding to the sample complexity analysis of Expected TD-PMD, we give some intuition about the critic update formula. Conditioned on the first state
s_{0}^{k}
, the critic
Q^{k}
and the target policy
\pi_{k+1}
, it is straightforward to compute the expectation of the TD error,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\delta_{t}^{k}(s,a)\right]=\mathbb{P}\left(s_{t}^{k}=s\right)\pi_{b}(a|s)\cdot\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right](s,a).

Therefore the conditional expectation of
Q^{k+1}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{k+1}(s,a)\right]=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[Q^{k}(s,a)+\alpha_{k}\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1}c_{t}^{k}\cdot\underbrace{\mathbb{P}(s_{t}^{k}=s)\pi_{b}(a|s)}_{\mbox{exploration}}\cdot\underbrace{\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right]}_{\mbox{exploitation}}(s,a)\Bigg].

(4)

From this equation, it can be seen that there are two parts in the TD error: the exploration part and the exploitation part. The exploitation part is a Bellman operator, offering a shift for the critic to approach
Q^{\pi_{k+1}}
. The exploration part is indeed the probability for the behavior policy
\pi_{b}
to obtain action-state
(s,a)
. Noticing that the critic update will fail if the exploration part goes to
0
, we require the following two assumptions to ensure the sufficient exploration over all
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
.

Assumption 3.1

(Sufficient exploration of behavior policy)
.

We have
\tilde{\pi}_{b}:=\min_{(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\pi_{b}(a|s)>0
.

Assumption 3.2

(Ergodicity)
.

The state transition matrix induced by
\pi_{b}
, i.e.,
P^{\pi_{b}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
, is ergodic over
\mathcal{S}
.

Assumption
3.1
guarantees the exploration over the action space. Note that if there exists a pair
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
such that
\pi_{b}(a|s)=0
, then
(s_{t}^{k},a_{t}^{k})\neq(s,a)
almost surely and
Q^{k}(s,a)
will not be updated.
Assumption
3.2
is very standard for the analysis of the actor-critic algorithms with the Markov data, see for example
Li et al. 2024
;
Khodadadian et al. 2021a
;
Khodadadian et al. 2022
. This assumption can guarantee the sufficient exploration over the state space.

Proposition 3.3
.

Under Assumption
3.2
, there exists a unique stationary distribution
\nu^{\pi_{b}}\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
for the transition kernel
P^{\pi_{b}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
which satisfies

\displaystyle(\nu^{\pi_{b}})^{\top}P^{\pi_{b}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}=(\nu^{\pi_{b}})^{\top}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{\nu}_{b}:=\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\;\nu^{\pi_{b}}(s)>0.

Furthermore, letting
\{s_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the Markov chain induced by
P^{\pi_{b}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
, then it is geometrically mixing. That is, there exists two constants
m_{b}>0
and
\kappa_{b}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S},\;n\geq 0,\;t\geq 0:\quad d_{TV}(\nu^{\pi_{b}}(\cdot),\,\mathbb{P}(s_{n+t}=\cdot|s_{n}=s))\leq m_{b}\kappa_{b}^{t},

where
d_{TV}
denotes the total variation distance.

The proof of Proposition
3.3
can be found for example in
Khodadadian et al. 2021a
, which is the application of
Levin and Peres 2017
. Noticing that at any
k
-th iteration of Expected TD-PMD, the generated state trajectory
\{s_{t}^{k}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the Markov chain with transition kernel of
P^{\pi_{b}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
. Thus by Proposition
3.3
,

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K,\;s\in\mathcal{S},\;n\geq 0,\;t\geq 0:\quad d_{TV}(\nu^{\pi_{b}}(\cdot),\,\mathbb{P}(s_{n+t}^{k}=\cdot|s_{n}^{k}=s))\leq m_{b}\kappa_{b}^{t},

(5)

implying that the exploration part
\mathbb{P}(s_{t}^{k}=s)\pi_{b}(a|s)
converges to
\nu^{\pi_{b}}(s)\pi_{b}(a|s)\geq\tilde{\nu}_{b}\tilde{\pi}_{b}>0
as
B_{k}
increases. Therefore, Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
together can guarantee sufficient exploration over
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
.

We need to make an additional assumption on the mirror map
h
for the subsequent analysis when
\eta_{k}
is a small constant step size.

Assumption 3.4
.

The mirror map
h
is further
\lambda
-strongly convex w.r.t.
\ell_{p}
-norm over
\Delta(\mathcal{A})
where
p\in[1,+\infty]
is some constant.

Assumption
3.4
is also common in the convergence analysis of mirror descent methods (see for example
Beck 2017
), and typical mirror maps including quadratic function and negative entropy satisfy this assumption. Indeed, one can verify that
h(p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{2}^{2}
is
1
-strongly convex with respect to the
l_{2}
-norm, while
h(p)=-\sum_{a}p(a)\log p(a)
is
1
-strongly convex with respect to the
l_{1}
-norm. By Assumption
3.4
, it is clear that for PMD-type update (including the policy update in Expected TD-PMD) there holds

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K,\;s\in\mathcal{S}:\quad D^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\right\|_{p}^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\right\|_{\infty}^{2}.

(6)

Moreover, the policy shift
\|\pi_{k+1}-\pi_{k}\|_{\infty}
in after the PMD-type update can be bounded by the policy update step size based on the strong convexity of mirror map
h
and the three-point-descent lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Consider the PMD-type policy update in (
3
).
Under Assumption
3.4
, there holds

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K:\quad\left\|\pi_{k+1}-\pi_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\|_{1}}{\lambda}\eta_{k}.

Proof.

By Assumption
3.4
, one has

\displaystyle\forall\,x,y\in\Delta(\mathcal{A}):\quad D_{h}(x\,||\,y)=h(x)-h(y)-\left\langle\nabla h(y),\,x-y\right\rangle\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}{\|x-y\|_{p}^{2}.}

By Hölder’s inequality and the three-point descent lemma (Lemma
2.3
),

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\|\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\|_{p}\cdot\|Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\|_{\frac{p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\eta_{k}\left\langle\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s),\,Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq D_{\pi_{k}}^{\pi_{k+1}}(s)+D^{\pi_{k}}_{\pi_{k+1}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\cdot\|\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\|_{p}^{2}.

Since
\|Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\|_{\frac{p}{p-1}}\leq\left\|Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right\|_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)\|_{p}\leq\frac{\|Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\|_{1}}{\lambda}\eta_{k},

which implies that
\|\pi_{k+1}-\pi_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\|_{1}}{\lambda}\eta_{k}
.
∎

The following property about the boundedness of
Q^{k}
can be verified easily.

Lemma 3.6
.

Consider Expected TD-PMD
(
Algorithm
1
)
with critic step size
\alpha_{k}
satisfying
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1]
. There holds

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K,\;(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}:\quad 0\leq Q^{k}(s,a)\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

3.1
Expected TD-PMD with constant step size

We are now ready to provide the sample complexity analysis for Expected TD-PMD. This subsection focuses on the constant policy update step size setting, i.e.,
\eta_{k}=\eta>0
. We begin with the following upper bound for Expected TD-PMD.

Lemma 3.7
.

Consider Expected TD-PMD
(
Algorithm
1
)
. Suppose that Assumption
3.4
holds. Let
\eta_{k}=\eta>0
and
\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1]
. Fixing any optimal policy
\pi^{*}\in\Pi^{*}
and
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V^{*}(\mu)-V^{\pi_{\hat{K}}}(\mu)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\frac{1}{K+1}\left[\frac{\|D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}\|_{\infty}}{\eta(1-\gamma)}+\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right]}_{\mbox{\small Exact PMD bound}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\underbrace{\frac{1}{K+1}\cdot\frac{\eta|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{2(1-\gamma)^{2}\lambda}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|Q^{k}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]}_{\mbox{\small Stochastic Variance term}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\underbrace{\frac{1}{K+1}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{*}_{\mu}}\left[\left\langle\pi^{*}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s),\,Q^{\pi_{k}}(s,\cdot)-Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right\rangle\right]\right]}_{\mbox{\small Stochastic Bias term}},

where
\|D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}\|_{\infty}:=\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\,D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}(s)
and
\hat{K}
is a random time index that is uniformly sampled from
0,\dots,K
.

The proof of Lemma
3.7
follows the standard route of the stochastic PMD/NAC analysis, see for example
Lan 2021
;
Khodadadian et al. 2022
;
Xu et al. 2020a
;
Xu et al. 2020b
;
Fu et al. 2021
;
Xiao 2022
;
Li et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2025b
;
Li and Lan 2025
, and we present the proof in Section
5.1
for completeness. In the exact vanilla PMD setting (i.e.,
Q^{k}=Q^{\pi_{k}}
), the stochastic variance and bias terms vanish and the upper bound in Lemma
3.7
reduces to the same upper bound for the exact PMD in
Xiao 2022
.

Note that the exact PMD bound vanishes in Lemma
3.7
as
K
grows, and it is also not hard to see that the stochastic variance term can be well controlled if the policy update step size
\eta
is small enough. Thus the challenge is to bound the stochastic bias term, which closely relies on the critic update formula of Expected TD-PMD and occupies most of the proof. To this end, we reformulate the critic update formula of Expected TD-PMD into a more concise form. By equation (
4
) we know that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{k+1}(s,a)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[Q^{k}(s,a)+\alpha_{k}\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1}c_{t}^{k}\cdot\underbrace{\mathbb{P}(s_{t}^{k}=s)}_{\to\nu^{\pi_{b}}(s)\mbox{\small\;by equation~\eqref{eq:Expected-TD-AC-PMD:MDP-ergodicity}}}\pi_{b}(a|s)\cdot{\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right]}(s,a)\Bigg]

(7)

\displaystyle\approx\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{k}(s,a)+\alpha_{k}\cdot{\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s,a)}\cdot{\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right]}(s,a)\right],

where
\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(a|s):=\nu^{\pi_{b}}(s)\pi_{b}(a|s)
. One can verify that
\sigma^{\pi_{b}}
is the stationary distribution of
\{(s^{k}_{t},a^{k}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
, that is,
(\sigma^{\pi_{b}})^{\top}P^{\pi_{b}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}=(\sigma^{\pi_{b}})^{\top}
. For simplicity, assume
\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1]
. It is natural to define the following
\sigma^{\pi_{b}}
-weighted Bellman operator,

\displaystyle\forall\,\pi\in\Pi,\;Q\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}:\quad\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q(s,a):=Q(s,a)+\alpha\cdot\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s,a)\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q-Q\right](s,a),

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q:=Q+\alpha\cdot\Sigma^{\pi_{b}}\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q-Q\right],

where
\Sigma^{\pi_{b}}=\mbox{diag}(\sigma^{\pi_{b}})\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\times|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
. When
\alpha=1
, we simply denote
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}
by
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}}Q:=Q+\Sigma^{\pi_{b}}\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q-Q\right].

By Assumption
3.1
, Assumption
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
, it is easily shown that

\tilde{\sigma}_{b}:=\min_{s,a}\;\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s,a)\geq\tilde{\nu}_{b}\tilde{\pi}_{b}>0.

In this case, one can show that
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}
shares similar properties with the vanilla Bellman operator
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose Assumption
3.1
and Assumption
3.2
hold. Then for any
\pi\in\Pi
,

(a)

\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{\pi}=Q^{\pi}
;

(b)

\forall\,Q,\,Q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}:\;\;\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q-\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\leq(1-\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b})\left\|Q-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Claim
(a)
follows directly from the fact that
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q^{\pi}=Q^{\pi}
. Notice that for any
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q(s,a)-\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{\prime}(s,a)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(1-\alpha\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s,a))\left(Q(s,a)-Q^{\prime}(s,a)\right)+\alpha\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s,a)\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q(s,a)-\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q^{\prime}(s,a)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s,a))\left\|Q-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}+\alpha\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s,a)\underbrace{\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q-\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}}_{\leq\gamma\left\|Q-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha(1-\gamma)\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s,a))\cdot\left\|Q-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b})\cdot\left\|Q-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty},

which yields (b) directly.
∎

Let

A^{\pi}=I-\alpha\Sigma^{\pi_{b}}(I-\gamma P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}).

One has

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q-\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=A^{\pi}(Q-Q^{\prime}).

It follows immediately from Proposition
3.8
that

\displaystyle\forall\,\pi\in\Pi:\quad\|A^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq(1-\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b}).

(8)

Define the stochastic noise
\bar{\omega}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
as follows:

\displaystyle\omega_{t}^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\delta_{t}^{k}(s,a)-\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right](s,a),

(9)

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle={\sum_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1}c_{t}^{k}\cdot\omega_{t}^{k}(s,a)}=\bar{\delta}_{k}(s,a)-\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right](s,a).

We can rewrite the critic update of Expected TD-PMD into the following two equivalent ways:

Q^{k+1}=\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{k}+\alpha\cdot\bar{\omega}_{k}\,\,\mbox{ or }\,\,Q^{k+1}=Q^{k}+\alpha\cdot\Sigma^{\pi_{b}}\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right]+\alpha\cdot\bar{\omega}_{k}.

(10)

This expression means that the critic update in Expected TD-PMD is essentially the application of the
\sigma^{\pi_{b}}
-weighted Bellman operator
\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}
with an additive stochastic Markov noise
\bar{\omega}_{k}
. Moreover, we will consider the following average weight:

\displaystyle c_{t}^{k}=\dfrac{\vartheta^{B_{k}-t-1}}{\sum_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1}\vartheta^{t}},

(11)

where
\vartheta\in[0,1]
is some preset parameter. Note that when
\vartheta=0
, we define
0^{0}=1
here, therefore only the last sample in the batch will be used.

To bound the stochastic bias part, it is convenient to define the following index matrix:

\displaystyle E_{s}

\displaystyle=\left[{\mathbf{0}}\;\;{\cdots}\;\;{\mathbf{0}}\;\;\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&&&\\
&\ddots&&\\
&&1&\end{array}\;\;\mathbf{0}\;\;\cdots\;\;\mathbf{0}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|\times|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|},

(12)

\displaystyle J_{s}

\displaystyle=E_{s}^{\top}E_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\times|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|},

where
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|\times|\mathcal{A}|}
, and each
1
corresponds to an action associated with state
s
.
Then it is evident that
Q(s,\cdot)=E_{s}Q
and
\pi(\cdot|s)=E_{s}\pi
, and thus we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\pi(\cdot|s),\;Q(s,\cdot)\right\rangle=\left\langle E_{s}\pi,\;E_{s}Q\right\rangle=\left\langle E_{s}^{\top}E_{s}\pi,\;\;Q\right\rangle=\left\langle J_{s}\pi,\;\;Q\right\rangle.

We first have the following decomposition for the stochastic bias, whose proof is presented in Section
5.2
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Consider Expected TD-PMD
(

and

Remark 3.1
.

In

for all iteration

In order to bound the stochastic bias term, we decompose it into the accumulation of policy shifts and stochastic noises terms and we bound them separately.

The first term

\displaystyle\left|B_{0}^{(k)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle\left[(A_{0})^{k}\right]^{\top}J_{s}[\pi^{*}-\pi_{0}],\;Q^{\pi_{0}}-Q^{0}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left[(A_{0})^{k}\right]^{\top}J_{s}[\pi^{*}-\pi_{0}]\right\|_{1}\cdot\left\|Q^{\pi_{0}}-Q^{0}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left\|(A_{0})^{\top}\right\|_{1}^{k}}_{=\left\|A_{0}\right\|_{\infty}^{k}}\times\underbrace{\left\|J_{s}[\pi^{*}-\pi_{0}]\right\|_{1}}_{\leq 2}\times\underbrace{\left\|Q^{\pi_{0}}-Q^{0}\right\|_{\infty}}_{\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}\mbox{\small by equation~\eqref{eq:bounded-policy-values} and Lemma~\ref{lem:Expected-TD-AC-PMD:bounded-value}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\cdot\left[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}\right]^{k}.

(13)

The terms

Theorem 3.13
.

Consider Expected TD-PMD
(
Algorithm
1
)
. Suppose that Assumptions
3.1
,
3.2
, and
3.4
hold. Let

\eta_{k}=\eta=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha\lambda\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4}\|D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}\|_{\infty}}{6|\mathcal{A}|^{2}(K+1)}},\quad\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1],\quad B_{k}=B>0,

and assume

takes the form in equation (
11

\pi^{*}\in\Pi^{*}
and
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V^{*}(\mu)-V^{\pi_{\hat{K}}}(\mu)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\cdot\frac{3}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}(1-\gamma)^{3}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{K+1}}\cdot\left(\frac{6|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\|D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}\|_{\infty}}{\alpha\lambda\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}(1-\gamma)^{6}}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\frac{2}{\tilde{\sigma}_{b}(1-\gamma)^{2}}\Psi(B,\vartheta,m_{b},\kappa_{b}).

Proof.

First recall the error decomposition in Lemma
3.7
.
For the stochastic variance term, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K+1}\cdot\frac{\eta|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{2(1-\gamma)^{2}\lambda}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|Q^{k}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\eta|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{2(1-\gamma)^{4}\lambda}

since
\|Q^{k}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}
. For the stochastic bias term, based on equation (
13
) and Lemmas
3.9
–
3.12
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}[\langle\pi^{*}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s),Q^{\pi_{k}}(s,\cdot)-Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\rangle]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}[\langle\pi^{*}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s),Q^{\pi_{k}}(s,\cdot)-Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\rangle]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\left|\mathbb{E}[\langle\pi^{*}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s),Q^{\pi_{k}}(s,\cdot)-Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\rangle]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\left(\left|B_{0}^{(k)}\right|+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}[\left|C_{j}^{(k)}\right|+\left|D_{j}^{(k)}\right|+\left|E_{j}^{(k)}\right|]+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[F_{j}^{(k)}\right]\right|\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{2}{1-\gamma}(1-\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b})^{k}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{2\gamma|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\eta}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}\lambda(1-\gamma)^{4}}\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\eta}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}\lambda(1-\gamma)^{3}}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}\left[\frac{2\gamma|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\eta}{\alpha\lambda\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}(1-\gamma)^{4}}\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}\left[\frac{2}{(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}\Psi({B},\vartheta,m_{b},\kappa_{b})\right].

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K+1}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\mu}^{*}}[\langle\pi^{*}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s),Q^{\pi_{k}}(s,\cdot)-Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\rangle]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\cdot\frac{2}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}(1-\gamma)^{3}}+\frac{5|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\eta}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}\lambda(1-\gamma)^{5}}+\frac{2}{(1-\gamma)^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}\Psi({B},\vartheta,m_{b},\kappa_{b}).

Putting them all together in Lemma
3.7
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\left(V^{*}(\mu)-V^{\pi_{k}}(\mu)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\left[\frac{D_{\pi_{0}}^{\pi^{*}}(d_{\mu}^{*})}{{\eta}(1-\gamma)}+\frac{3}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}(1-\gamma)^{3}}\right]+\frac{6|\mathcal{A}|^{2}{\eta}}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}\lambda(1-\gamma)^{5}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{2}{(1-\gamma)^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}\Psi({B},\vartheta,m_{b},\kappa_{b}).

Choose the best
\eta
such that the minimal is achieved completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Expected TD-PMD with adaptive step sizes

As stated above, this section focuses on the later iteration complexity of Expected TD-PMD with adaptive step sizes when
\vartheta=1
. In this case, it can be shown the
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
sample complexity without a logarithm term can be established. Moreover, the mirror map
h
is not required to be strongly convex in this section.

The main idea is to approximate the classic value iteration algorithm (VI)
(
Sutton and Barto 2018
)
by using a sufficiently large adaptive policy update step size. More precisely, recall the policy update formula of Expected TD-PMD

\displaystyle\forall\,k:\quad\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\underset{p\in\Delta(\mathcal{A})}{\arg\max}\;\left\{\eta_{k}\left\langle p,\,Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right\rangle-D^{p}_{\pi_{k}}(s)\right\}.

Notice that when
\eta_{k}\to\infty
, the updated policy
\pi_{k+1}
tends to be greedy, i.e., choosing the action with largest critic value

\displaystyle\pi_{k+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{k}\to\infty}}{{\approx}}\pi^{k}_{\mbox{\tiny greedy}}:\quad\pi_{\mbox{\tiny greedy}}^{k}(a^{\prime}|s)=0,\quad\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S},\;\forall\,a^{\prime}\not\in\underset{a\in\mathcal{A}}{\arg\max}\;Q^{k}(s,a).

Therefore, one has
\mathcal{F}^{\pi^{k}_{k+1}}Q^{k}\approx\mathcal{F}Q^{k}
, and the critic update in equation (
10
) becomes

\displaystyle Q^{k+1}

\displaystyle\approx Q^{k}+\alpha_{k}\Sigma^{\pi_{b}}\left[\mathcal{F}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right]+\alpha_{k}\bar{\omega}_{k}.

Noting that
\Sigma^{\pi_{b}}\succeq\tilde{\sigma}_{b}\cdot I\succ\mathbf{0}
, the critic update above can be viewed as a weighted VI with step size
\alpha_{k}
. Thus we can bound
\|Q^{*}-Q^{k}\|_{\infty}
directly by using the
\gamma
-contraction property of
\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Suppose Assumption
3.1
and Assumption
3.2
hold. Consider Expected TD-PMD (Algorithm
1
) with constant critic step size
\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1]
. There holds

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K:\quad\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{k+1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]\cdot\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{k}\right\|_{\infty}+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\eta_{k}}\|D^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}_{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\bar{\omega}_{k}\|_{\infty},

where

\displaystyle\left\|D_{\pi_{k}}^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}\right\|_{\infty}:=\max_{s}D_{\pi_{k}}^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}(s),

and
\tilde{\pi}_{k}
is any policy that satisfies
\langle\tilde{\pi}_{k}(\cdot|s),Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\rangle=\max_{a}Q^{k}(s,a),\;\forall s
.

Lemma
3.14
establishes the linear convergence rate of the critic error
\|Q^{*}-Q^{k}\|_{\infty}
and the proof can be found in Section
5.6
. The following lemma shows that the error of the policy value can be bounded by the critic error.

Lemma 3.15
.

Suppose Assumption
3.1
and Assumption
3.2
hold. Consider Expected TD-PMD
(
Algorithm
1
)
with constant critic step size
\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1]
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K:\quad\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{k+1}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}\left(\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{k+1}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{k}\right\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\bar{\omega}_{k}\|_{\infty}\right).

Proof.

By the critic update formula (equation (
10
)),

\displaystyle\|Q^{\pi_{k+1}}-Q^{k+1}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|{Q^{\pi_{k+1}}}-\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{k}-\alpha\bar{\omega}_{k}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}{Q^{\pi_{k+1}}}-\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{k}-\alpha\bar{\omega}_{k}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{\pi_{k+1}}-\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b},\alpha}Q^{k}\right\|_{\infty}}_{\mbox{\small Applying Proposition~\ref{pro:Expected-TD-AC-PMD:sigma-bellman-property}}}+\alpha\left\|\bar{\omega}_{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]\|Q^{\pi_{k+1}}-Q^{k}\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\bar{\omega}_{k}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]\left(\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{k+1}}\|_{\infty}+\|Q^{*}-Q^{k}\|_{\infty}\right)+\alpha\|\bar{\omega}_{k}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{k+1}}\|_{\infty}+\|Q^{*}-Q^{k}\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\bar{\omega}_{k}\|_{\infty}.

The proof is completed by noting that
\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{k+1}}\|_{\infty}\leq\|Q^{*}-Q^{k+1}\|_{\infty}+\|Q^{\pi_{k+1}}-Q^{k+1}\|_{\infty}
.
∎

Theorem 3.17
.

Suppose Assumption
3.1
and Assumption
3.2
hold. Consider Expected TD-PMD
(
Algorithm

)
with constant critic step size
\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1]
and the adaptive policy update step size

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\geq\eta\cdot\|D^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}_{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\eta>0.

Let

. Then there holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{K}}\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}(1-\gamma)^{2}}[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{K-1}+\frac{2\gamma}{\alpha\eta(1-\gamma)^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{b}(1-\gamma)}\left(\frac{4|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{m_{b}}{1-\kappa_{b}}\right)\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Xi(K)+\Xi(K-1)+(B_{K-1})^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right),

where

.

Proof.

Taking an expectation on both sides of the inequality in Lemma
3.14
yields

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K:\quad

\displaystyle\phantom{=\,\,\,}\mathbb{E}\|Q^{*}-Q^{k+1}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]\cdot\mathbb{E}\|Q^{*}-Q^{k}\|_{\infty}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\eta_{k}}\|D^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}_{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}\right]}_{\mbox{\small Applying }\eta_{k}\geq\eta\|D^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}_{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}}+\alpha\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{\omega}_{k}\right\|_{\infty}}_{\mbox{\small Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:Expected-TD-AC-PMD:error-control-2}}}

\displaystyle\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{k}\right\|_{\infty}+\alpha\gamma\eta^{-1}+\alpha C_{1}(B_{k})^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},

where
C_{1}=\left(\frac{4|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{m_{b}}{1-\kappa_{b}}\right)\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
. Iterating from
K-1
to
0
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|Q^{*}-Q^{K}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{K}\cdot\|Q^{*}-Q^{0}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{K-1-k}[\gamma\eta^{-1}+C_{1}(B_{k})^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}]

\displaystyle\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{K}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{\eta(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}+\alpha C_{1}\Xi(K).

(14)

Similarly, there holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|Q^{*}-Q^{K-1}\|_{\infty}\leq[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{K-1}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{\eta(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}+\alpha C_{1}\Xi(K-1).

On the other hand, taking an expectation on both sides of the inequality in Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{K}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}\Big(\mathbb{E}\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{K}\right\|_{\infty}+\mathbb{E}\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{K-1}\right\|_{\infty}+\alpha\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{\omega}_{K-1}\right\|_{\infty}}_{\mbox{\small Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:Expected-TD-AC-PMD:error-control-2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}\Big(\mathbb{E}\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{K}\right\|_{\infty}+\mathbb{E}\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{K-1}\right\|_{\infty}+\alpha C_{1}(B_{K-1})^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\Big).

Inserting the above bounds for
\mathbb{E}\|Q^{*}-Q^{K}\|_{\infty}
and
\mathbb{E}\|Q^{*}-Q^{K-1}\|_{\infty}
completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Theorem
3.17
implies that
\mathbb{E}\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{K}}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
provided

\displaystyle K=\log\left(\frac{6}{\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}(1-\gamma)^{2}\varepsilon}\right)\left[\log\left(\frac{1}{1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}}\right)\right]^{-1}+1,\quad\eta=\frac{6\gamma}{\alpha(1-\gamma)^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}\varepsilon},

\displaystyle B_{k}=\frac{81}{\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(\frac{4|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{m_{b}}{1-\kappa_{b}}\right)\right)\frac{1}{\left(1-[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)^{2}}\times[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{K-k-2}\varepsilon^{-2}.

Thus the total sample complexity is

\displaystyle S=\sum_{k=0}^{K}B_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{81}{\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(\frac{4|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{m_{b}}{1-\kappa_{b}}\right)\right)\frac{1}{\left(1-[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)^{2}}\times\frac{[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{-2}}{(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}\varepsilon^{-2}

\displaystyle=O\left((1-\gamma)^{-7}|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\alpha^{-3}\tilde{\sigma}_{b}^{-5}\varepsilon^{-2}\cdot m_{b}(1-\kappa_{b})^{-1}\right)

which does not contain an logarithmic term. In contrast, existing sample complexities for PMD type methods under both the generative model
(
Lan 2021
;
Xiao 2022
;
Johnson et al. 2023
;
Protopapas and Barakat 2024
;
Liu et al. 2025b
)
and the Markov sampling
(
Li and Lan 2025
;
Li et al. 2024
)
are all
O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\varepsilon))
.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that the adaptive step sizes
\eta_{k}
in Theorem
3.17
is computable. In particular, for some specific mapping function
h
, the upper bound of
\|D^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}_{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}
can be easily derived. For instance, for
h(p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{2}^{2}
there holds
\|D^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}_{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and for

there holds
\|D^{\tilde{\pi}_{k}}_{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}\leq\log[(\min_{s,a}\,\pi_{k}(a|s))^{-1}]
.

3.2.1
Batch Q-learning

As mentioned previously, Expected TD-PMD approaches batch Q-learning (see Algorithm
2
) with
\eta_{k}\to+\infty
. In particular, by picking
h(p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{2}^{2}
, Proposition
3.19
below tells us that under an adaptive but finite policy update step size, Expected TD-PMD is exactly equivalent to batch Q-learning.

Lemma 3.18

(
Liu et al. 2025a
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
and

be two disjoint non-empty sets such that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}

Proposition 3.19
.

Consider Expected TD-PMD
(
Algorithm
1
)
with
h(p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{2}^{2}
. At each iteration
k
, let
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s}:=\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\,Q^{k}(s,a)
for each
s\in\mathcal{S}
, and also define the following value gap

\displaystyle\varDelta_{k,s}:=\begin{cases}\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q^{k}(s,a)-\max_{a^{\prime}\not\in\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s}}Q^{k}(s,a^{\prime})&\mbox{if }\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s}\neq\mathcal{A},\\
+\infty&\mbox{if }\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s}=\mathcal{A}.\end{cases}

If the policy update step size of Expected TD-PMD satisfies

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq 0:\quad\eta_{k}\geq 2\cdot\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\;(\varDelta_{k,s})^{-1},

(15)

then one has

\pi_{k+1}=\pi^{k}_{\mbox{\tiny greedy}}.

That is, Expected TD-PMD is equivalent to batch Q-learning under the step size condition in (
15
).

Proof.

Letting
h(p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{2}^{2}
, the policy update of Expected TD-PMD becomes

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S}:\quad\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\textup{Proj}_{\Delta(\mathcal{A})}\left[\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)+\eta_{k}\cdot Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right].

For the state such that
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s}=\mathcal{A}
, we have
\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\pi^{k}_{\mbox{\tiny greedy}}(\cdot|s)
for any
\eta_{k}>0
. Consider the state that
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s}\neq\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s}
,
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s}
. We have

\displaystyle\forall\,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}:\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{B}}\left[\pi_{k}(a|s)+\eta_{k}Q^{k}(s,a)-\pi_{k}(a^{\prime}|s)-\eta_{k}Q^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right]_{+}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{B}}\left[(\pi_{k}(a|s)-\pi_{k}(a^{\prime}|s))+\eta_{k}\left(Q^{k}(s,a)-Q^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{B}}\left[-1+\eta_{k}\cdot\varDelta_{k,s}\right]\geq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{B}}[-1+2]\geq 1.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.18
,

\displaystyle\forall\,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}:\quad\pi_{k+1}(a^{\prime}|s)=\textup{Proj}_{\Delta(\mathcal{A})}\left[\pi_{k}(\cdot|s)+\eta_{k}\cdot Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right](a^{\prime})=0,

which implies that
\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s)=\pi^{k}_{\mbox{\tiny greedy}}(\cdot|s)
and Expected TD-PMD reduces to batch Q-learning.
∎

also applies for batch Q-learning.

.

It is worth noting that, since for (batch) Q-learning the policy is greedy, we do not need to obtain the error bound of the policy value through Theorem

Remark 3.8
.

Corollary
3.20
implies that

\displaystyle S=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}B_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16\alpha^{2}|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}{\varepsilon^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{m_{b}}{1-\kappa_{b}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}}\frac{1}{\left(1-[1-(1-\gamma)\alpha\tilde{\sigma}_{b}]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)^{2}}

(1-\gamma)
and
\varepsilon
, the sample complexity of batch Q-learning is
O((1-\gamma)^{-5}\varepsilon^{-2})
. In
Li et al. 2021
, a tight sample complexity of
O((1-\gamma)^{-4}\varepsilon^{-2}|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\log^{2}((1-\gamma)^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}))
is established for Q-learning
(
i.e., batch Q-learning with

)
with a small critic step size

(1-\gamma)
, our sample complexity improves it by a logarithm factor. Moreover, it is interesting to see whether the sample complexity of batch Q-learning can be further improved to match that in

or not. As discussed in Remark

4
Approximate TD-PMD with Mixed-Policy Markov Data

Notice that in Expected TD-PMD, it requires to compute the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s_{t}^{k})}[Q^{k}(s^{k}_{t},a)]
when reconstructing the TD error. Thus, it is natural to ask what about we replacing the expectation with a sample mean? In this section we will investigate this problem.

In this setting, the trajectory
\tau_{k}
admits the following form

\tau_{k}=(s_{t}^{k},a_{t}^{k},r_{t}^{k},s_{t}^{\prime\,k},a_{t}^{\prime\,k})_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1},

where

\displaystyle a_{t}^{k}\sim\pi_{b}(\cdot|s_{t}^{k}),\;\;r_{t}^{k}=r(s_{t}^{k},a_{t}^{k}),\;\;s^{\prime\,k}_{t}\sim P(\cdot|s_{t}^{k},a_{t}^{k}),\;\;a_{t}^{\prime\,k}\sim\pi_{k+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime\,k}_{t}),\;\;s_{t+1}^{k}\sim P(\cdot|s_{t}^{\prime\,k},a_{t}^{\prime\,k}).

That is,
a^{k}_{t}
is the action sampled from a behavior policy
\pi_{b}
, while
a^{\prime\,k}_{t}
is the action sampled from the target policy
\pi_{k+1}
.
Moreover, the TD error is constructed by

\displaystyle\delta_{t}^{k}(s,a)=\mathds{1}_{(s^{k}_{t},a^{k}_{t})=(s,a)}\cdot\left[r_{t}^{k}+\gamma\left[Q^{k}(s_{t}^{\prime\,k},a_{t}^{\prime\,k})\right]-Q^{k}(s^{k}_{t},a^{k}_{t})\right].

Based on the new TD error, we obtain the Approximate TD-PMD algorithm, see Algorithm
3
for a detailed description with the illustration presented in Figure
2
. It is evident that Approximate TD-PMD is an online mixed-policy learning algorithm (off-policy for sampling an action to update the value while on-policy for estimating the expectation). As will be shown next, similar sample complexities can be established for Approximate TD-PMD.

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{k+1}(s,a)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{k}(s,a)+\alpha_{k}\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1}c_{t}^{k}\cdot{\mathbb{P}(s_{t}^{k}=s)\pi_{b}(a|s)}\cdot{\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right]}\right].

(16)

In contrast to the expression in Expected TD-PMD, the term
\mathbb{P}(s_{t}^{k}=s)
does not converge to the stationary distribution
\sigma^{\pi_{b}}(s)
due to alternative sampling. To obtain the stationary distribution of chain
\{s_{t}^{k}\}_{t\geq 0}
(note that the complete state chain is
\{s_{t}^{k},s_{t}^{\prime\,k}\}_{t\geq 0}
), for any
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
, define the following composition transition matrix:

\displaystyle P^{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|}:\quad P^{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}(s,\tilde{s})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A},\,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},\,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)P(s^{\prime}|s,a)\pi^{\prime}(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})P(\tilde{s}|s^{\prime},a^{\prime})=[P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}P^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}](s,\tilde{s}).

One can verify that
P^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
is the transition matrix of the state chain
\{s_{t}^{k}\}_{t\geq 0}
when
\pi_{k+1}
is given. Thus, we make the following assumption for the ergodicity of
\{s_{t}^{k}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Assumption 4.1
.

For any
\pi\in\Pi
, the state transition matrix
P^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
is ergodic over
\mathcal{S}
.

As the target policy
\pi_{k}
varies over from iteration to iteration, here we assume
P^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
is ergodic for any policy
\pi\in\Pi
. This is an assumption that is common in the analysis of AC algorithms. In fact, in existing works such as
Khodadadian et al. 2022
;
Li et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2024
, they assume the ergodicity of
P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
for any
\pi\in\Pi
. Under Assumption
4.1
, a result similar to Proposition
3.3
can be obtained.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that Assumption
4.1
holds. For any policy
\pi\in\Pi
, there exists a unique stationary distribution for transition kernel
P^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
,

\displaystyle\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}\in\Delta(\mathcal{S}):\quad(\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi})^{\top}P^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}=(\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi})^{\top}.

Additionally, there exists a constant
\tilde{\nu}>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,\pi\in\Pi,\;s\in\mathcal{S}:\quad\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}(s)\geq\tilde{\nu}>0.

Furthermore, for any
\pi
, let
\{s_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the Markov chain with transition kernel of
P^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
. Then the chain
\{s_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is
(
uniform
)
geometrically mixing, i.e., there exists
m>0
and
\kappa\in(0,1)

(
independent of
\pi
)
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S},\;n\geq 0,\;t\geq 0:\quad d_{TV}(\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}(\cdot),\,\mathbb{P}(s_{n+t}=\cdot|s_{n}=s))\leq m\kappa^{t}.

Proposition
4.2
can be proved similarly as
Khodadadian et al. 2022
and the details are omitted. At the
k
-th iteration of Approximate TD-PMD, the trajectory
\{s_{t}^{k}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the Markov chain with transition kernel of
P^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}
when
\pi_{k+1}
is fixed. Thus by Proposition
4.2
,

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K,\;s\in\mathcal{S},\;n\geq 0,\;t\geq 0:\quad d_{TV}(\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}(\cdot),\,\mathbb{P}(s_{n+t}^{k}=\cdot|s_{n}^{k}=s)\;\big|\;\pi_{k+1})\leq m\kappa^{t}.

(17)

Let
\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}(s,a):=\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}(s)\cdot\pi_{b}(a|s)
. One can directly verify that
\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}
is the stationary distribution of chain
\{(s_{t}^{k},a_{t}^{k})\}_{t\geq 0}
. By Proposition
4.2
, we have
\mathbb{P}(s_{t}^{k}=s)\to\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}(s)
and
\mathbb{P}(s^{k}_{t}=s,\,a^{k}_{t}=a)\to\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}(s,a)
as
B_{k}
increases.

As in Expected TD-PMD (see Lemma
3.6
), the Q-values in Approximate TD-PMD are also bounded.

Lemma 4.3
.

Consider Approximate TD-PMD
(
Algorithm
3
)
with critic step size satisfying
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1]
. We have

\displaystyle\forall\,0\leq k\leq K,\;(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}:\quad 0\leq Q^{k}(s,a)\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

In the sequel, we will extend the analysis for Expected TD-PMD to establish the sample complexity of Approximate TD-PMD under constant and adaptive policy update step sizes. The major difference is to handle the time-varying stationary distribution
\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}
, which requires following lemma.

Lemma 4.4

(
Mitrophanov 2005
)
.

Let
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\tilde{X}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be two Markov chains over
|\mathcal{X}|<\infty
with transition kernels
P
and
\tilde{P}
. Denote by
\nu
,
\tilde{\nu}
the stationary distribution of
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
,
\{\tilde{X}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
respectively, and assume
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is geometrically mixing, i.e.,

\displaystyle\exists\,m>0,\;\exists\,\kappa\in(0,1),\;\;\forall\,x\in\mathcal{X},\;\;\forall\,n\geq 0,\;\;\forall\,t\geq 0:\quad d_{TV}\left(\nu(\cdot),\;\mathbb{P}(X_{n+t}=\cdot|X_{n}=x)\right)\leq m\kappa^{t}.

Then there holds

\displaystyle d_{TV}(\nu(\cdot),\,\tilde{\nu}(\cdot))\leq\left(\left\lceil\log_{\kappa}m^{-1}\right\rceil+\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\right)\|P-\tilde{P}\|_{\infty}.

4.1
Approximate TD-PMD with constant step size

Notice that the proof of Lemma
3.7
does not rely on the sampling scheme and the critic update formula. Thus the upper bound in Lemma
3.7
still applies for Approximate TD-PMD, which is presented below for readers’ convenience.

Lemma 4.5
.

Consider Approximate TD-PMD
(
Algorithm
3
)
. Suppose that Assumptions
3.4
holds. Let
\eta_{k}=\eta>0
and
\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1]
. Fixing any optimal policy
\pi^{*}\in\Pi
and
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V^{*}(\mu)-V^{\pi_{\hat{K}}}(\mu)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\frac{1}{K+1}\left[\frac{\|D^{\pi^{*}}_{\pi_{0}}\|_{\infty}}{\eta(1-\gamma)}+\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right]}_{\mbox{ \small Exact PMD bound}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\underbrace{\frac{1}{K+1}\cdot\frac{\eta|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{2(1-\gamma)^{2}\lambda}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|Q^{k}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]}_{\mbox{\small Stochastic Variance term}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\underbrace{\frac{1}{K+1}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{*}_{\mu}}\left[\left\langle\pi^{*}(\cdot|s)-\pi_{k}(\cdot|s),\,Q^{\pi_{k}}(s,\cdot)-Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right\rangle\right]\right]}_{\mbox{\small Stochastic Bias term}}.

Next, the key is still to bound the stochastic bias, which requires us to rewrite the update of the critic. By Proposition
4.2
, under Assumption
4.1
, we have
\mathbb{P}(s_{t}^{k}=s)\to\nu^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}(s)
. Thus it follows from equation (
16
)that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{k+1}(s,a)\right]\approx\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{k}(s,a)+\alpha_{k}\cdot\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}(s,a)\cdot\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right](s,a)\right].

This inspires us to define the
\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}
-weighted Bellman operator with
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\forall\,\pi\in\Pi,\;Q\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}:\quad\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}Q(s,a):=Q(s,a)+\alpha\cdot\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}(s,a)\cdot\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q(s,a)-Q(s,a)\right],

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}Q=Q+\alpha\Sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q-Q\right],

where
\Sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}:=\mbox{diag}(\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi})\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\times|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
. When
\alpha=1
we simply denote
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}
by
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}Q:=Q+\Sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}[\mathcal{F}^{\pi}Q-Q].

By Proposition
4.2
, we know that under Assumption
4.1
and Assumption
3.1
there holds

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}:=\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\,\min_{(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\;\sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}(s,a)>0,

implying that
\Sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}\succeq\tilde{\sigma}\cdot I
. Such a positive lower bound offers further implies the contraction property of
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose Assumption
3.1
and Assumption
4.1
hold. Then for any
\pi\in\Pi
,

(a)

\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}Q^{\pi}=Q^{\pi}
;

(b)

\forall\,Q,\,Q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}:\;\;\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}Q-\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\leq(1-\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma})\|Q-Q^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

The proof is same with the one for Proposition
3.8
. Similarly, letting
A^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}=I-\alpha\Sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}(I-\gamma P^{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}})
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}Q-\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}Q^{\prime}=A^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}(Q-Q^{\prime}),

and thus

\displaystyle\forall\,\pi\in\Pi:\quad\left\|A^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi}\right\|_{\infty}\leq(1-\alpha(1-\gamma)\tilde{\sigma}).

Based on the operator
\mathcal{F}^{\pi}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi,\alpha}
, we can reformulate the critic update of Approximate TD-PMD as

\displaystyle Q^{k+1}=\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1},\alpha}Q^{k}+\alpha\cdot\bar{\omega}_{k}\quad\mbox{or}\quad Q^{k+1}=Q^{k}+\alpha\cdot\Sigma^{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right]+\alpha\cdot\bar{\omega}_{k},

(18)

where
\bar{\omega}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
is the stochastic term defined as follows:

\displaystyle\omega_{t}^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\delta_{t}^{k}(s,a)-\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right](s,a),

(19)

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{B_{k}-1}c_{t}^{k}\cdot\omega_{t}^{k}(s,a)=\bar{\delta}_{k}(s,a)-\left[\mathcal{F}^{\pi_{k+1}}_{\pi_{b}\circ\pi_{k+1}}Q^{k}-Q^{k}\right](s,a).

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate the sample complexity of policy mirror descent with temporal difference learning under online Markov sampling. Two algorithms including Expected TD-PMD (off-policy) and Approximate TD-PMD (mixed-policy) have been considered, and a comprehensive sample complexity analysis have been conducted. Under the constant policy update step size, both algorithms can find the average-time
\varepsilon
-optimal solution with the
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
sample complexity, which is further improved to the last-iterate
\varepsilon
-optimal solution with
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
sample complexity when utilizing adaptive step sizes.

For future work, it is interesting to extend our analysis to the RL problem with entropy regularization. In addition, it is also possible to consider the more general setting with policy or critic function approximation. Any progress towards these directions will be reported separately.

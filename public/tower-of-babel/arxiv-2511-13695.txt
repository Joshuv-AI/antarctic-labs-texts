Stability phenomena in Deligne-Mumford compactifications via Morse theory
Changjie Chen

Abstract
. We study the rational homology of the Deligne-Mumford compactification ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} of the moduli space of stable curves via a family of Morse functions, the sys_T \syst functions, which encode geometric information about short geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces. Exploiting the Morse-theoretic properties of sys_T \syst , including the existence of an index gap and the behavior of critical points near boundary strata, we prove that in low degrees the homology of ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} is supported entirely on the boundary ∂ ℳ g , n \partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n} . Furthermore, we establish finite generation and stability phenomena for the rational homology across all genera and numbers of marked points. Using stable graphs and explicit attaching maps, we show that for each fixed degree k k , a finite set of critical points generates all k k -th homology classes via attaching stable graphs of stratum dimension 0 0 . This result recovers previously known stability in the genus direction, such as Tosteson’s theorem, and provides a concrete, geometric construction of homology generators. Our approach unifies combinatorial, geometric, and Morse-theoretic techniques to give a comprehensive picture of the low-degree and stable homology of ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} .



1.
Introduction

The moduli space of stable curves
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a central object in algebraic geometry and low-dimensional topology. Its rich structure combines complex analytic, hyperbolic, and combinatorial perspectives, making it an ideal testing ground for techniques from Morse theory, Teichmüller theory, and homological stability. Understanding the topology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, particularly its rational homology in various degrees, remains a fundamental question with deep connections to mapping class groups and representation stability.

In this paper, we study the low-degree homology and stability phenomena of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
via the
\syst
functions
, a family of
C^{2}
-Morse functions defined on the moduli space. These functions, inspired by systolic geometry, encode geometric information about short geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces and extend
C^{2}
-continuously to the Deligne-Mumford compactification. Their Morse-theoretic properties, including the existence of an index gap and the behavior of critical points near boundary strata, provide a powerful tool for analyzing homology in a combinatorial and geometric fashion.

Our first main result concerns the low-degree homology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. By exploiting the
\syst
-Morse theory and the structure of the Deligne-Mumford boundary, we prove:

Theorem 1.1

(Low-degree homology is supported on the boundary, Theorem
4.2
)
.

For any
k>0
, when
g+n
is sufficiently large, in particular when
\ind(g,n)>k+1
, the inclusion

i\colon\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

induces an isomorphism

i_{*}\colon H_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q}).

This theorem implies that, in the stable range, low-degree homology classes are determined by boundary strata, a phenomenon revealed by the classical homological stability results of Harer
[
7
,
8
]
.

Our second main contribution addresses
stability phenomena
across the moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
as
(g,n)
varies. Using the combinatorial framework of stable graphs and the
\syst
-Morse theory, we establish that rational homology in each fixed degree is finitely generated and can be constructed via explicit attaching maps of low-dimensional stable graphs:

Theorem 1.2

(Finite generation of homology over attaching, Theorem
5.6
)
.

For every
k\geq 0
, there exists a finite set

\Xi=\{\xi_{i}\in H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{i},n_{i}};\mathbb{Q})\}

that generates

\bigoplus_{(g,n)}H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})

via attaching a stable graph of stratum dimension 0.

This result not only provides a concrete description of generators for homology across all
(g,n)
but also recovers previously known stability results in the genus direction, such as Tosteson’s theorem
[
13
]
. In particular, our approach gives a geometric and Morse-theoretic perspective on the phenomena of homological stabilization and the generation of homology by boundary strata.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we review stable graphs, strata of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, and the definition of the
\syst
functions. Section
3
discusses the Weil–Petersson metric and the construction of gradient systems compatible with the
\syst
functions. Section
4
proves the main low-degree homology isomorphism, and Section
5
establishes the finite generation and stability phenomena for the rational homology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

Throughout, we work over the rational coefficients unless otherwise specified. The techniques developed here combine geometric, combinatorial, and Morse-theoretic ideas, offering a unified approach to understanding the topology of moduli spaces of curves.

2.
Stable graphs and the
\syst
functions

We review standard definitions used to study Deligne–Mumford compactifications and discuss the construction and properties of the
\syst
functions.

2.1.
Stable graph and Deligne-Mumford compactification

We follow standard definitions in the literature on Deligne–Mumford compactifications (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
3
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a stable curve with marked points
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}
. The
stable graph
\textbf{G}=(G,g)
dual to
X
is a weighted multi-graph consisting of the following data:

•

The set
V(G)
of vertices: each
v
corresponds to an irreducible component
X_{v}
.

•

The set
E(G)
of edges: each
e=e(v,w)
corresponds to a node connecting
X_{v}
and
X_{w}
.

•

The set
H(G)
of half-edges: each
h=h(v)
corresponds to a marked point on
X_{v}
.

•

A genus function
g\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{N}
sending
v
to the genus of the normalization of
X_{v}
.

•

A marking function
m\colon\{1,\dots,n\}\to V(G)
sending
i
to
v
when
p_{i}
lies in
X_{v}
.

One may define an abstract stable graph in the same way as long as it satisfies the stability condition

2g(v)-2+n(v)>0,

where
n(v)=\#m^{-1}(v)
.

Conversely, given an abstract stable graph satisfying the stability condition, one can construct a dual stable curve. The
stratum
\mathcal{M}_{\textbf{G}}
dual to a stable graph
G
is the collection of all stable curves dual to the same stable graph. Formally, the stratum
\mathcal{M}_{\textbf{G}}
is given by

\mathcal{M}_{\textbf{G}}=\prod_{v\in V(G)}\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}/\text{Aut}(\textbf{G}),

where
\text{Aut}(\textbf{G})
denotes the automorphism group of the stable graph
G
.

2.2.
The Morse functions

There is a canonical correspondence between algebraic structures and hyperbolic structures on a topological surface. Thus, the moduli space can be defined as the quotient of the space of hyperbolic metrics by the action of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
0<T<1
be a parameter. For a hyperbolic structure
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, define

\syst(X)=-T\log\left(\sum_{\gamma\text{ s.c.g. on }X}e^{-\frac{1}{T}l_{\gamma}(X)}\right),

where s.c.g. stands for simple closed geodesic. The definition of
\syst
on the Deligne–Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is by continuous extension.

Definition 2.3
.

A point
X
in the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}
is
eutactic
if the origin lies in the interior of the convex hull of
\{\nabla l_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in S(X)}
in the tangent space
T_{X}\mathcal{T}
, with respect to the subspace topology, where
S(X)
is the set of shortest closed geodesics on
X
,
l_{\gamma}
is the geodesic-length function associated to
\gamma
, and
\nabla
is the (Weil–Petersson) gradient vector. In this case, the
eutactic rank
of
X
is defined as the rank of the set
{\nabla l_{\gamma}}_{\gamma\in S(X)}
.

A pointed subspace of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is called
stratifically stable
if the subspace is contained in the relative Deligne-Mumford compactification of the stratum the point lies in. The following theorem establishes the Morse property of
\syst
, which is foundational to subsequent Morse homology results.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
5
]
)
.

For sufficiently small
T>0
, we have:

•

The
\syst
function is
C^{2}
-Morse on the Deligne–Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

•

In the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
,
\syst
-critical points
p_{T}
correspond to eutactic points
p
, with Weil–Petersson distance
d_{WP}(p_{T},p)<CT
, and Morse index
\ind(p_{T})
equals the eutactic rank of
p
.

•

For
X\in\mathcal{M}_{\textbf{G}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)
X
is a critical point of
\syst
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
;

(2) For any
v\in V(G)
,
X_{v}
is a critical point of
\syst
on
\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}
.

And in this case,

\ind(X)=\sum_{v\in V(\textbf{G})}\ind(X_{v}).

•

The Weil–Petersson gradient flow of
\syst
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is well defined, and the unstable manifold at any point is stratifically stable. The closure of any
\syst
-flow line is contained in a single stratum or hits a critical point downward in its boundary stratum transversely.

The transversality statement in the last item strengthens the original theorem and is established as Lemma
3.6
.

An important property of the
\syst
functions is the existence of an index gap.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\ind(g,n)
be the lowest Morse index of
\syst
-critical points in
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, that is,

\ind(g,n)=\min_{X\in\crit(\syst|_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}})}\ind(X).

Theorem 2.6

(
[
4
]
)
.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
such that the lowest Morse index satisfies

\ind(g,n)>C\log\log(g+n).

3.
Subtleties in using the Weil–Petersson metric for Morse theory

3.1.
Weil–Petersson metric near the boundary

The Weil–Petersson metric, induced by the natural
L^{2}
-pairing on the space of (0,2)- or (-1,1)-tensors, is known to be incomplete on Teichmüller space. Consequently, when extended to the Deligne–Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, it defines an orbifold Riemannian metric that is singular on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. Wolpert
[
14
]
computed the expansion of the Weil–Petersson connection near the boundary of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

The degeneracy of the Weil–Petersson metric may pose challenges when applying Morse theory, even after small perturbations. Nevertheless, it is expected to be feasible with careful attention to finiteness conditions. For convenience, we consider a globally well-defined “twin” metric that shares key features with the Weil–Petersson metric in the Morse sense.

Let
S=\{\gamma_{i}\}
be a set of mutually disjoint simple closed geodesics, and let
\mathcal{S}_{S}
denote the stratum obtained by pinching all elements of
S
. Masur
[
10
]
showed that, near
\mathcal{S}_{S}
, the Weil–Petersson metric admits the expansion

g_{\text{WP}}=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{i}\left(\frac{dl_{i}^{2}}{l_{i}}+\frac{l_{i}^{3}}{4\pi^{4}}d\tau_{i}^{2}\right)+g_{\text{WP}}^{\mathcal{S}}+\text{higher order terms},

where
(l_{i},\tau_{i})_{i}
are the Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates associated to
S
, and
g_{\text{WP}}^{\mathcal{S}}
is the Weil–Petersson metric on the stratum
\mathcal{S}_{S}
. Hence, up to first order, the tangent subspace normal to the stratum
\mathcal{S}_{S}
is spanned by the coordinates
\{(l_{i},\tau_{i})\}
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

The
root-geodesic-length
differential orbifold structure of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is defined by assigning local charts

(l_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}},\tau_{i})_{i=1}^{k}+\text{Fenchel--Nielsen coordinates for $S^{C}$ in the surface}

near the stratum
\mathcal{S}_{S}
where
S=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\}
.

Under the root-geodesic-length differential structure, the Weil–Petersson metric is well defined in the radial directions but blows up in the angular directions with respect to
S
near
\mathcal{S}_{S}
.

3.2.
Gradient systems

We use the results of Bárta, Chill, and Fašangová
[
1
]
and Bílý
[
2
]
, which guarantee the existence of a Riemannian metric that turns a
gradient-like
vector field into an actual gradient vector field, both with respect to a given differentiable function.

Let
V
be a continuous vector field on a manifold
M
. A
stationary point
of
V
is where
V
vanishes.

Definition 3.2
.

A
C^{1}
-continuous function
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
strict Lyapunov function
for the ordinary differential equation

\dot{x}+V(x)=0,

if

\langle f^{\prime}(x),V(x)\rangle>0,

whenever
V(x)\neq 0
.

Bárta, Chill, and Fašangová
[
1
]
proved the strict Lyapunov condition is sufficient for the existence of such a Riemannian metric away from the stationary points of
V
, and Bílý
[
2
]
gave a condition when it admits a global extension.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
1
,
2
]
)
.

Let
V
be a continuous vector field on a manifold
M
, and let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
-continuous strict Lyapunov function for

\dot{x}+V(x)=0,

then there exists a Riemannian metric on the complement of the stationary points of
V
.

Furthermore, if at every stationary point
x
of
V
,
V^{\prime}
is regular, and
f^{\prime\prime}V^{\prime-1}
defines a scalar product, then there exists such a Riemannian metric on
M
.

In our setting,
V=\nabla^{\text{WP}}\syst
, which is the stratumwise Weil–Petersson gradient vector of
\syst
, and
f=\syst
. It is easy to check that the conditions are satisfied and therefore, there exists an orbifold Riemannian metric on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
such that the gradient vector of the
\syst
function coincides with
\nabla^{\text{WP}}\syst
.

It is clear that this modification of the metric does not affect the Morse properties of the
\syst
functions, including their critical points and Morse indices.

Remark 3.4
.

The choice of such a metric is not unique. Given a (Morse) function
f
and a Riemannian metric
g
, let
K=\ker\nabla_{g}f
. Modifying
g
on
K\times K\subset TM\times TM
leaves the gradient vector of
f
unchanged. In the case of the Weil–Petersson metric, which diverges only in twist directions near boundary strata, the stratumwise gradient of
\syst
defines a global
C^{1}
vector field transverse to these directions. By suitably modifying the metric in the twist directions, one obtains a globally well-defined Riemannian metric without changing the gradient vector field.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a Morse function on a manifold
M
equipped with a Riemannian metric, and
N\subset M
a Morse submanifold of
M
. Then if the closure of a flow line of
f
intersects
N
, then it is contained in
N
or hits a critical point in
N
transversely.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be the closure of an
f
-flow line. If
\varphi
contains a point in
N
in its interior, then
\varphi
is contained in
N
since
N
is a Morse submanifold of
M
. Suppose
\varphi
hits a point
p\in N
from outside
N
, then
p
lies at the end of
\varphi
and is a critical point of
f
. Take the orthogonal decomposition of the tangent space as

T_{p}M=T_{p}N\oplus\nu_{p}N.

Let
(x,y)
be a compatible local chart around
p
with
(x,y)(p)=(0,0)
such that

f(x,y)=f(p)+x^{T}Ax+y^{T}By+x^{T}Cy+g(x,y)

where
A
and
B
are nondegenerate matrices, and
\nabla^{2}g=0
. Note that

\nabla f=(2Ax+Cy,2By+Cx),

and therefore,
C=0
since locally
N=\{y=0\}
.

Pick
p_{0}=\varphi(t_{0})=(x_{0},y_{0})
where
y_{0}\neq 0
, then the flow through
p_{0}
is given by

\varphi(t-t_{0})=(e^{2At}x_{0},e^{2Bt}y_{0}),

which is transverse to
N
if it converges to
p
as
t\to-\infty
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

The closure of any
\syst
-flow line, with respect to the stratumwise Weil–Petersson metric, is contained in a single stratum or hits a critical point downward in its boundary stratum transversely.

4.
Low degree homology

The Deligne–Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a compact orbifold. The homology of such a space can be related to the homology of any finite manifold cover via the action of the deck transformation group, cf.
[
9
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
be a finite manifold cover with deck group
G
acting on
\widetilde{M}
. Then

H_{*}(M;\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{*}(\widetilde{M};\mathbb{Q})^{G},

where the superscript denotes the
G
-invariant part of homology.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any
k>0
, when
g+n
is sufficiently large, in particular when
\ind(g,n)>k+1
, the inclusion

i\colon\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

induces an isomorphism

i_{*}\colon H_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q}).

The proof uses the following two estimates from
[
5
]
:

•

It is uniform on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
that

|\syst-\sys|<CT.

•

Near a boundary stratum
\mathcal{S}_{S}
, where
S=\{\gamma_{i}\}
is a set of mutually disjoint simple closed geodesics, we have

\langle\nabla^{\text{WP}}\syst,\nabla l_{i}\rangle>0.

Proof.

Let
M
be a finite manifold cover of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, and let
B\subset M
denote the preimage of
\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. By abuse of notation, we denote the lift of the
\syst
function to
M
also by
\syst
.

Since
|\syst-\sys|<CT
, for
\delta>0
sufficiently small we have

\syst^{-1}([0,\delta])\subset M^{\leq\epsilon},

where
M^{\leq\epsilon}
is the preimage of the
\epsilon
-thin part
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\leq\epsilon}=\sys^{-1}([0,\epsilon])
. In particular,

\crit(\syst)\cap\syst^{-1}([0,\delta))\subset B.

By Morse handle decomposition
[
12
,
11
]
,
M
is obtained from
\syst^{-1}([0,\delta])
by attaching handles of index
m=\ind(g,n),\dots,\dim M
.

Since the level sets
\sys^{-1}(c)
are transverse to
\nabla^{\text{WP}}\syst
for small
c>0
, we have homeomorphisms

B=\sys^{-1}(0)\cong M^{\leq\epsilon}\cong\syst^{-1}([0,\delta]).

It follows that there is an isomorphism on homology in low degrees:

i_{*}\colon H_{k}(B)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{k}(M),

and hence, passing to the quotient by the deck group,

i_{*}\colon H_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q}).

∎

Taking the long exact sequence of the pair
(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n})
, we deduce:

Theorem 4.3
.

Under the same assumptions, we have

H^{6g-6+2n-k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{c}_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})=0.

Remark 4.4
.

This result is equivalent to Harer’s homological stability
[
7
,
8
]
in the stable range, although here we only consider a weaker statement for sufficiently large
g+n
.

5.
Stability Phenomena

To analyze homology and stability phenomena more precisely, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}(k)
denote the collection of Deligne–Mumford strata of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
containing
\syst
-critical points of index
k
. Define

\mathcal{S}_{g,n}([k])\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

as the relative Deligne–Mumford compactification of the union
\bigcup_{j\leq k}\mathcal{S}_{g,n}(j)
.

By the index gap theorem (Theorem
2.6
), for
k<\ind(g,n)
we have

\mathcal{S}_{g,n}([k])\subset\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}.

Analogously to Theorem
4.2
, the low-degree homology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
arises from the strata in
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}([k])
.

Definition 5.2
.

For a stable graph
G
, define its
stratum dimension

\dim\textbf{G}
as the dimension of
\mathcal{M}_{\textbf{G}}
, and its
local stratum dimension

|\textbf{G}|
as

|\textbf{G}|=\max_{v\in V(G)}\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
k<\ind(g,n)
, there exist constants
v=v(k)>0
and
h=h(k)>0
such that for any
\mathcal{M}_{\textbf{G}}\in\mathcal{S}_{g,n}([k])
, one has

\displaystyle|\textbf{G}|\leq h,

\displaystyle\#V(G)\geq v,

\displaystyle\#\{v\in V(G):\dim\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}>0\}\leq\frac{1}{2}hk.

Proof.

If the first two inequalities hold for a stratum
\mathcal{S}
, they also hold for any boundary stratum.

For any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{\textbf{G}}\in\mathcal{S}_{g,n}(k)
, we have

\ind(g(v),n(v))\leq\ind(X_{v})\leq\ind(X)=k,

so
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}
is bounded for each
v\in V(G)
. The lower bound on
\#V(G)
follows from

\#V(G)\geq\frac{6g-6+2n}{|\textbf{G}|}.

By the classification of low-index critical points
[
4
]
, we have

\ind(g(v),n(v))\geq 1\quad\text{whenever }\dim\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}>0.

Using the additivity of Morse indices over
V(G)
, it follows that

\#\{v\in V(G):\dim\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}>0\}\leq k.

The same inequalities extend to boundary strata of
\mathcal{M}_{\textbf{G}}
.
∎

In other words, when
\syst
-Morse theory applies, the “complexity” of
k
-th rational homology elements stabilizes as either
g
or
n
increases.

Corollary 5.4
.

For every
k\geq 0
, there exists a finite set of
\syst
-critical points

S=\{X_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{g_{i},n_{i}}\}

that generates all critical points of index
k
in
\bigsqcup_{(g,n)}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
via the attachment of copies of thrice-marked
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let
P
denote the stable graph dual to
\mathcal{M}_{0,3}
. For a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
, define
\sigma
-attaching maps
\text{Att}_{1}(\sigma),\text{Att}_{2}(\sigma),\text{Att}_{3}(\sigma)
on the set of stable graphs
G
as follows. Each cycle of
\sigma
is assigned to a single vertex of
G
via the marking function, and for
i=1,2,3
,
\text{Att}_{i}(\sigma)(\textbf{G})
is obtained by connecting the half-edges of
P
to
G
so that

m_{\text{Att}_{i}(\sigma)(\textbf{G})}(n^{\prime})=m_{\textbf{G}}(\sigma(n^{\prime})),\quad n^{\prime}=1,\dots,n-i,

and

m_{\text{Att}_{i}(\sigma)(\textbf{G})}(n-i+j)=m_{\textbf{P}}(1+j),\quad j=1,\dots,i.

In this framework, Part III of Theorem
2.4
implies:

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
k
, except for finitely many pairs
(g,n)
, for any
\xi\in H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})
, there exists
\sigma\in S_{n}
, such that

\xi\in\text{im}((\text{Att}_{i}(\sigma)_{*}:H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})\to H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+i-1,n+3-2i};\mathbb{Q})).

By composing attaching maps, one can attach a stable graph of stratum dimension
0
(dual to a maximally noded curve) to stable graphs with marked points. Morse-theoretically, attaching copies of thrice-marked
\mathbb{P}^{1}
preserves criticality and Morse index, inducing equivariant homomorphisms on rational homology and Morse homology.

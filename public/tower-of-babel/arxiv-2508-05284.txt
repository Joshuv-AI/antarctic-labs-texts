Simultaneous Rational Function Codes: Improved Analysis Beyond Half the Minimum Distance with Multiplicities and Poles
Matteo Abbondati, Eleonora Guerrini, Romain Lebreton

Abstract
In this paper, we extend the work of [ 2 ] on decoding simultaneous rational function codes by addressing two important scenarios: multiplicities and poles (zeros of denominators). First, we generalize previous results to rational codes with multiplicities by considering evaluations with multi-precision. Then, using the hybrid model from [ 10 ] , we extend our approach to vectors of rational functions that may present poles. Our contributions include: a rigorous analysis of the decoding algorithm’s failure probability that generalizes and improves several previous results, an extension to a hybrid model handling situations where not all errors can be assumed random, and a new improved analysis in the more general context handling poles within multiplicities. The theoretical results provide a comprehensive probabilistic analysis of reconstruction failure in these more complex scenarios, advancing the state of the art in error correction for rational function codes.

Introduction

An efficient approach to solving linear systems in distributed computation involves reconstructing
a vector of fractions
\left(\frac{f_{1}}{g},\ldots,\frac{f_{\ell}}{g}\right)
, all sharing the same denominator,
from its modular reductions with respect to
n
pairwise coprime elements. In this framework, a network
is structured around a central node, which selects a sequence of relatively prime elements

\left(m_{j}\right)_{1\leq j\leq n}
and delegates the system solving process to the network.
Each node
j
computes the solution modulo
m_{j}
and transmits the reduced solution vector

\left(f_{i}/g\ \bmod m_{j}\right)_{1\leq i\leq\ell}
back to the central node. The central node then
reconstructs the original vector through an interpolation step, formulated as a simultaneous
rational reconstruction problem. In the case of polynomial systems, this approach is known
as evaluation-interpolation
[
12
,
11
]
, whereas for integer
systems, it corresponds to modular reduction followed by reconstruction via the Chinese Remainder
Theorem
[
7
]
.

This work and
[
3
]
are companion papers, addressing the polynomial and the integer contexts respectively.
In view of the symmetry between these two cases, the layout of this paper reflects the structure of
[
3
]
.

Context of this paper

During data reconstruction, the central node may receive incorrect reductions due to computational errors,
faulty or untrusted nodes, or network noise.
In view of this issue, it is of great help to look at the theory of error correcting codes and, by integrating decoding algorithms in the above framework, obtain fault tolerant linear system solving methods.
Viewing the modular
reductions as coordinates of an error correcting code enables us to reconstruct the correct solution as long as the number
of erroneous reductions is below a certain value, corresponding to the unique decoding radius of the
code. In presence of more errors, there exist two
possible approaches in coding theory to correct beyond the unique decoding radius;
either decoding algorithms which return a list of all codewords within a certain distance of the received word (list decoding) or, by interleaving techniques, obtain positive decoding results under probabilistic assumptions on random errors corrupting
\ell
code-words on the shared positions.
In this paper, we focus on interleaving techniques as they fit in the simultaneous reconstruction problem.
Note that, a decoding algorithm working under this latter approach must inevitably fail for some instances, as
beyond unique decoding radius there can be many codewords around a given instance. Here the failure
probability is intended as the proportion of received words, within a given distance from
the codeword
\boldsymbol{f}/g
, for which the reconstruction fails.

Thus, focusing on the polynomial context, we will consider the simultaneous rational reconstruction problem over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
In particular, we study the reconstruction with multiplicities,
i.e.
with
m_{j}=(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
for a sequence of distinct evaluation points
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and relative multiplicities
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}>0
.
The evaluation points for which the modular reductions are not defined (zeros of the denominator
g
) are referred to as
poles
.
One advantage of considering reductions with multiplicities is that
solving a linear system modulo
(x-\alpha)^{\lambda}
is asymptotically faster than solving
it modulo
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\lambda}
(see
[
17
,
9
,
25
]
or
[
14
, Chapter 3]
for a survey).

In order to generalize our first results (Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
) on the decoding with multiplicities to a context including poles (Theorems
4.18
and
4.19
), we need to consider (as in

) a hybrid error model consisting of random evaluation errors and fixed valuation errors.
In Section

we present this error model for more general sets of errors. We note that this more general version has been independently introduced in
[
4
]
for IRS codes and folded Reed-Solomon codes decoding, as the
semi-adversarial error model
. There, the authors obtained a weaker failure probability bound (depending linearly instead of exponentially on the error size) but, for a given total amount of errors (random and fixed), the proportion of adversarial (fixed) errors they manage to correct, though asymptotically equivalent, is higher compared to our results.

1.1
Notations and preliminary definitions

We will denote vectors with bold letters
\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{r},\boldsymbol{c},\ldots
.
For
m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
we will denote its degree with
\partial(m)
and for
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{\ell}
we define its degree as the max
\partial(\boldsymbol{f})\coloneq\max_{i}\{\partial(f_{i})\}
.
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/m
will denote the quotient ring modulo the ideal
(m)
, while
\mathcal{Z}(m)
will denote the set of roots
of
m
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Given an indexed family of rings
\left\{A_{j}\right\}_{1\leq j\leq n}
, we let
\prod_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}
be their
Cartesian product.
Given a vector of modular reductions

\boldsymbol{r}\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
the corresponding capital letter
R
denote its
unique interpolant constructed via the Chinese remainder theorem modulo

M\coloneq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
.

We let
\Val_{\alpha}:\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
be the valuation function over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with respect to the evaluation point
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, whose output is the
highest power of
x-\alpha
dividing the input, where we set by convention its
value to be
\infty
when the input is
0
.

Dealing with a fixed sequence of precisions
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
, we truncate the valuation
function considering
\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(m)\coloneq\min\{\Val_{\alpha_{j}}(m),\lambda_{j}\}
, so that
\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(a)=\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(b)
when
a=b\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
.

When computing the valuation of a vector we set

\nu\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\coloneq\min_{i}\{\nu\left(f_{i}\right)\}
. Given the sequence of
multiplicities
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}>0
, we define the parameter

L\coloneq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}
. For us, all the vectors of fractions
\boldsymbol{f}/g
sharing the same
denominator will always be reduced, i.e. they satisfy
\gcd\left(\gcd(\boldsymbol{f}),g\right)=1
.

Simultaneous rational function reconstruction with errors (SRFRwE)

To quantify errors and to establish the correction capacity of the code we are
going to use, we need a notion of distance between words. In a context with
multiplicities where the coordinates are modular reductions relative to moduli
specified by different precisions
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
, it is classical
to consider (see for example
[
13
,
10
]
) a minimal error
index distance in which each modular reduction is regarded as a truncated
development, and the whole tail starting from the first error index in such
development is considered erroneous. Thus,
we are going to consider the following definition:

Definition 1.1

(Distance)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{R}^{1},\boldsymbol{R}^{2}\in(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}})^{\ell}
be two

\ell\times n
matrices, where each column
\boldsymbol{r}_{j}^{1},\boldsymbol{r}_{j}^{2}
belongs to
\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\ell}
. We
define their error support as

\xi_{\boldsymbol{R}^{1},\boldsymbol{R}^{2}}\coloneq\{j:\boldsymbol{r}_{j}^{1}\neq\boldsymbol{r}_{j}^{2}\}
and their error
locator polynomial as the product
\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{R}^{1},\boldsymbol{R}^{2}}\coloneq\prod_{j\in\xi_{\boldsymbol{R}^{1},\boldsymbol{R}^{2}}}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}-\mu_{j}}
, where
\mu_{j}\coloneq\nu_{\alpha_{j}}\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{j}^{1}-\boldsymbol{r}_{j}^{2}\right)
represents the minimal error index for the development around the
evaluation point
\alpha_{j}
. The distance between
\boldsymbol{R}^{1}
and
\boldsymbol{R}^{2}
is defined as

d(\boldsymbol{R}^{1},\boldsymbol{R}^{2})\coloneq\partial(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{R}^{1},\boldsymbol{R}^{2}})
.

The problem of simultaneous rational function reconstruction with errors is then given by:

Problem 1.2

(SRFRwE)
.

Given
\ell>0
,
n
distinct evaluation points
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with associated multiplicities

\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
, a received matrix
\boldsymbol{R}\in(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}})^{\ell}
, an error parameter
t
and two degree bounds
d_{f},d_{g}
such
that
d_{f}+d_{g}\leq L+1
, find a reduced vector of rational functions
\left(f_{1}/g,\ldots,f_{\ell}/g\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)^{\ell}
such that

1.

d\left(\bigl(f_{i}/g\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\bigr)_{i,j},\boldsymbol{R}\right)\leq t
,

2.

\partial(\boldsymbol{f})<d_{f}
,
\partial(g)<d_{g}
and
\gcd(g,M)=1
.

In the above we have that
\gcd(g,M)=1
so that the
reductions
f_{i}/g\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
are well-defined. We are going to drop this hypothesis in
Section
4
, when solving a more general version of
the SRFRwE problem, allowing for the presence of poles.

This problem can be reduced to the simultaneous error correction of
\ell
code words
(sharing the same denominator) for the multiplicity version of rational function codes. Without multiplicities (i.e. when
M
is square-free)
this code is the natural rational extension of Reed-Solomon codes
[
22
]
, and can be referred to as rational function codes,
extensively studied in
[
2
]
. It seems these rational codes were part of the
folklore; to the best of our knowledge, they were formally introduced in the language of coding
theory by Pernet in
[
21
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.5.2]
.

The condition
d_{f}+d_{g}\leq L+1
guarantees an injective encoding, whose proof will be given in Proposition
4.2

when considering the multi-precision encoding (see Definition
4.1
) first introduced in
[
10
]
, which is a
generalization of our current encoding in presence of poles.

This paper is concerned with error correction beyond guaranteed
uniqueness, this means that the solution to the problem will not always be
unique. In this rare case, our decoding algorithm returns a decoding failure.
We analyze the probability of failure in detail.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
we introduce the
simultaneous rational function codes whose decoding solves
Problem
1.2
as well as the corresponding decoding
Algorithm
1
. We study the failure probability of our decoding
algorithm for error parameters larger than the unique decoding radius of the
code. We note that this analysis generalizes the results
of
[
2
]
to the multiplicity case, it thus follows the
same broad lines except for some technical details (see
Lemma
2.16
).

In Section
3
, we adapt our analysis technique to the hybrid distribution
model of
[
10
]
in which not all errors are supposed to be random, but some
of them are fixed, either because of specific error patterns introduced by malicious entities or
because of specific faults of the network nodes.

Then, in Section
4
, by considering the multi-precision encoding
of
[
10
]
, we apply the hybrid approach to generalize our analysis to the
case of reductions with multiplicities and poles,
i.e.
we drop the hypothesis

\gcd\left(g,M\right)=1
.

Simultaneous Multiplicity Rational Function Codes

We reduce Problem
1.2
to the decoding of simultaneous rational function
codes with multiplicities, defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

Given
n
distinct evaluation points
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with relative
multiplicities
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
M(x)\coloneq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
L\coloneq\partial\left(M\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}
, two degree bounds
d_{f},d_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
d_{f}+d_{g}\leq L+1

and a parameter
\ell>0
, we define the
simultaneous multiplicity rational function code
as the set of matrices

\mathsf{SRF}_{\ell}(M;d_{f},d_{g})\coloneq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\frac{f_{i}}{g}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\end{pmatrix}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell\\
1\leq j\leq n\end{subarray}}:\begin{array}[]{c}\partial(\boldsymbol{f})<d_{f},\partial(g)<d_{g},\\
\gcd(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell},g)=1\\
\gcd(M,g)=1\end{array}\right\}.

We will refer to SRF codes for short if the parameters are implicit.

Note that when
L=n
,
i.e.
\lambda_{j}=1
for every
j=1,\ldots,n
, we obtain the simultaneous rational function codes first introduced in
[
21
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.3]
and extensively studied in
[
2
,
\lx@sectionsign
4]
. If furthermore

we obtain the interleaving of Reed-Solomon codes.

In the next section, we will see that the common denominator property is necessary to be able to take advantage in the
key equations of the fact that the
\ell
rational function codewords forming the lines of the matrices in
\mathsf{SRF}_{\ell}(M;d_{f},d_{g})
share the same error supports.

The condition
\gcd(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell},g)=1
, which is going to be used in the proof of
Lemma
2.13
, reflects that the solution vector we seek to reconstruct is a reduced
vector of rational functions.

Remark 2.2
.

A bounded distance decoding algorithm for the above code which is able to correct errors up to a

This justifies the denomination

.

If the decoding algorithm fails then

Proof.

We prove it by contrapositive. If

2.2
Minimal distance

The distance
d(\mathcal{C})\coloneq\min_{c_{1}\neq c_{2}\in\mathcal{C}}d(c_{1},c_{2})
of a code
\mathcal{C}
plays an important
role in coding theory to assess the amount of data one can correct. A classic result states that when the distance between the code
\mathcal{C}
and the input received word
\boldsymbol{R}
, defined as
\min\{d(\boldsymbol{C},\boldsymbol{R}):\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathcal{C}\}
, is below half the minimal distance of
\mathcal{C}
, then the decoding algorithm is guaranteed to succeed, while there is no guarantee on the decoding success beyond this quantity.
We start by proving a lower bound for the minimal distance of SRF codes:

Lemma 2.4
.

We have
d(\mathsf{SRF}_{\ell}(M;d_{f},d_{g}))>L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1
.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}=\left(f_{i}/g\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\right)_{i,j}
and
\boldsymbol{C}_{2}=\left(f_{i}^{\prime}/g^{\prime}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\right)_{i,j}
be two distinct code words. Setting

Y\coloneq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\mu_{j}}
, with
\mu_{j}=\nu_{\alpha_{j}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}/g-\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}/g^{\prime}\right)
. Thanks to

\gcd\left(Y,g\right)=\gcd\left(Y,g^{\prime}\right)=1
, we have that

Y|(\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g)
, with
\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g\neq\boldsymbol{0}\bmod M
since
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}\neq\boldsymbol{C}_{2}
. Given that
\partial(\boldsymbol{f}),\partial(\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime})<d_{f}
, and

\partial(g),\partial(g^{\prime})<d_{g}
we have
\partial(Y)<d_{f}+d_{g}-1
. Using the relation
Y=M/\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}}
,
we bound
d(\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2})=\partial(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}})=\partial(M/Y)>L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1
.
∎

. To adapt the same proof in the context of multiplicities we need to drop the hypothesis

We analyze the failure probability of the algorithm under two different classical error models in
Coding Theory, already considered in previous papers

,
2
,

]
, specifying two

Error Model 1

In this error model, we fix an error locator
\Lambda
among the divisors of

M
, then we let
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\Lambda}
be the set of error matrices
\boldsymbol{E}
whose columns
\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\ell}

\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=\boldsymbol{0}\text{ for all }j\text{ such that }\alpha_{j}\not\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda)

For any given code word
\boldsymbol{C}
and error locator

Proof.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\varphi\in({\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}/{\Lambda})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{q^{\ell\left(B+1\right)}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda/\eta)}\left(1-1/q^{\ell}\right)}

3
Analysis of the decoder for a hybrid error model

In this section we consider a hybrid approach to the failure probability analysis for the multiplicity rational function codes studied above.
The approach is hybrid in the sense that it lies in between unique decoding and interleaving.

As in the previous section, the algorithm parameter
t
is chosen and the probability of failure is studied under a random distribution of received words at distance at most
t
from a given codeword associated to a reduced vector of fractions
\boldsymbol{f}/g
.
With the hybrid approach, we analyze the failure probability for a modified random distribution, still determined by the algorithm parameter, but in which
t
splits into two components:
t_{i}
and
{t}_{u}
. While
{t}_{u}
captures constant errors of the distribution and is bounded to fit the unique decoding,
t_{i}
can be larger as it incorporates random errors and reflects the interleaving decoding technique having its bound
\bar{t}_{i}
(Equation (
10
)) increasing with
\ell
beyond unique capability and asymptotically up to
L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1-2{t}_{u}
.
Notably, if
t_{i}=0
, the algorithm never fails as
t={t}_{u}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1}{2}\right\rfloor
. Therefore, the probability of decoding failure is strictly related to
t_{i}
and is analyzed under probabilistic assumptions, considering a random distribution of errors.

The motivation for splitting
t
is that not all errors can be assumed to be purely random.
For instance, in the context of distributed computation, some errors might be introduced by malicious entities that deliberately choose specific error patterns to force the algorithm to fail. In such cases, the errors captured by
{t}_{u}
remain independent of the error distribution and can still be corrected.

Since we are above the unique decoding radius, not all errors are decodable. Interleaving techniques can provide positive decoding results by considering error sets where most errors are decodable using probabilistic arguments. These techniques focus on fixed error positions and consider all possible errors at each position.
In contrast, in a hybrid setting, one can handle more general sets of errors by analyzing the set of all possible errors across certain subsets of the error positions.
This approach may be of broader interest in coding theory.

A first particular instance of this hybrid model was introduced in
[
10
]
in the same context studied in this paper but with a different analysis, then it has been jointly generalized in
[
3
,
4
]
.
We remark that, as in
[
10
]
, in the forthcoming case of codes allowing poles (see Section
4
), we are only able to perform interleaving on a subset of all errors (namely evaluation errors)
This suggests that there could be a deeper obstacle preventing the interleaving of the other type of errors (namely valuation errors).

On a technical level this hybrid analysis consists in studying the failure probability with respect to a specific portion of the error’s distribution; allowing the errors to vary only over a subset
\xi_{i}\subseteq\xi
of the error support, while the errors in the complementary set
\xi_{u}\coloneq\xi\setminus\xi_{i}
are held fixed.
Note that, in this section the above partition might seem arbitrary but, as we will see in the next Section
4
on poles, it is clearly described by some property of the error itself (see Definition
4.8
).
Here we generalize the analysis of the previous section relative to the decoding of
\mathsf{SRF}
codes (Definition
2.1
) by means of Algorithm
1
.
In this setting we decompose the distance parameter
t
of the algorithm as

3.5
. We first notice that
{t}_{i}\leq\bar{t}_{i}=\ell/(\ell+1)\left[L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1-2{t}_{u}\right]<L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1-2{t}_{u}
, thus Constraint
3.5
is satisfied.
Thanks to Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.7
, we can upper bound the failure probability by

\mathbb{P}_{fail}\leq\mathbb{P}(S_{\boldsymbol{E}}^{h}\neq\{0\})\leq\frac{q^{\ell(B+1+{t}_{u})}}{q-1}q^{\partial(\Lambda_{i})}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{i})}\left(\frac{1-1/q^{\ell+\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{i})}}{1-1/q^{\ell}}\right).

Since
\partial(\Lambda_{i})\leq{t}_{i}
, we have

q^{\ell(B+1+{t}_{u})}q^{\partial(\Lambda_{i})}\leq q^{\ell(B+1+{t}_{u})}q^{{t}_{i}}=q^{-(\ell+1)(\bar{t}_{i}-{t}_{i})}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the event of decoding failure,
i.e.
the set of random matrices
\boldsymbol{E}
such that Algorithm
1
returns "decoding failure" with input parameter
t={t}_{i}+{t}_{u}
as in the statement of Theorem
3.3
.
We will denote
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}
) the probability function under the hybrid error model 2 (resp. model 1) specified by a given factorization of the error locator, and by a sequence of fixed error vectors
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j}
for every
j
such that
\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{Z}\left(\Lambda_{u}\right)
.

Using the law of total probability, we have that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\mathcal{F})
can be decomposed as the sum

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{\Lambda_{i}|\Lambda_{m,i}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\mathcal{F}\ |\ \Lambda_{\boldsymbol{E}}=\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{u})\ \mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{E}}=\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{u}),

where

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\mathcal{F}\ |\ \Lambda_{\boldsymbol{E}}=\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{u})=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\mathcal{F})
,
whereas

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{E}}=\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{u})=\frac{q^{\ell\partial(\Lambda_{i})}}{q^{\ell\partial(\Lambda_{m,i})}}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{i})}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{\ell}}\right)

as in Equation (
8
).

Plugging the above two expressions in the decomposition from the law of total probability, similarly as done in the proof of Theorem
2.9
, we can upper bound

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\mathcal{F})/\frac{q^{\ell\left(B+1+{t}_{u}\right)}}{(q-1)q^{\ell\partial(\Lambda_{m,i})}}

by

\sum_{\Lambda_{i}|\Lambda_{m,i}}q^{(\ell+1)\partial(\Lambda_{i})}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{i})}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{\ell+\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{i})}}\right)\leq q^{(\ell+1)\partial(\Lambda_{m,i})}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{m,i})}\frac{1-1/q^{\ell+\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{m,i})}}{1-1/q^{\ell+1}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\Lambda_{m,i}\Lambda_{u},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{\ell(B+1+{t}_{u})}}{q-1}q^{\partial(\Lambda_{m,i})}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{m,i})}\frac{1-1/q^{\ell+\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{m,i})}}{1-1/q^{\ell+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{\ell(B+1+{t}_{u})}}{q-1}q^{{t}_{i}}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{m,i})}\frac{1-1/q^{\ell+\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{m,i})}}{1-1/q^{\ell+1}}

and we conclude by using that

q^{\ell(B+1+{t}_{u})}q^{{t}_{i}}=q^{-(\ell+1)(\bar{t}_{i}-{t}_{i})}
.
∎

4
The case of poles

In this section we use the hybrid analysis technique presented above to extend our study of the decoding failure in a context where the hypothesis
\gcd(g,M)=1
of Definition
2.1
does not hold, thus some reductions in the encoding of
\boldsymbol{f}/g
may not be defined.
Evaluation points relative to undefined reductions are called
poles
.

We find two approaches in the literature to deal with poles: in
[
13
]
an extra symbol
\infty
is used,
while in
[
10
]
coordinates are given by shifted Laurent series representations of the vector of rational functions.

In particular, the authors of
[
10
]
introduced the following multi-precision encoding composed of a valuation part and a reduction part:

Definition 4.1

(Multi-precision encoding)
.

Given a sequence of evaluation points
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
along with associated multiplicities
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and a reduced vector of fractions
\boldsymbol{f}/g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)^{\ell}
, we define its multi-precision encoding to be the sequence of couples
\Ev^{\infty}\left(\boldsymbol{f}/g\right)\coloneq\left(\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g),\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)\right)_{1\leq j\leq n}
such that

\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)\coloneq\boldsymbol{f}/\left(g/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}\right)\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}-\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}.

By convention, we set
\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)=\boldsymbol{1}
when
\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)=\lambda_{j}
.

Under the hypothesis
L\geq d_{f}+d_{g}-1
the authors of
[
10
]
proved the injectivity of the above encoding.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}/g,\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}/g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)^{\ell}
be reduced with
\max_{i}\{\partial(f_{i})\}<d_{f},\ \partial(g)<d_{g}
such that
\Ev^{\infty}\left(\boldsymbol{f}/g\right)=\Ev^{\infty}\left(\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}/g^{\prime}\right)
. If we assume that
L\geq d_{f}+d_{g}-1
,
the equality
\boldsymbol{f}/g=\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}/g^{\prime}
holds.

Proof.

For every
j=1,\ldots,n
we let
\vr_{j}\coloneq\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)=\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g^{\prime})
. By hypothesis
\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)=\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}/g^{\prime})
, i.e.

\boldsymbol{f}/\left(g/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\right)=\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}/\left(g^{\prime}/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\right)\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}-\vr_{j}}
, thus
\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime}/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}=\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}-\vr_{j}}.

In other words
\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
, which implies that
\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g\bmod M
. Since by hypothesis
\partial(\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g)\leq d_{f}+d_{g}-2<L
, we conclude that
\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)^{\ell}
.
∎

Under the hypothesis
L\geq d_{f}+d_{g}-1
, we can then introduce the
simultaneous rational function code with poles
as the set

\mathsf{SRF}^{\infty}_{\ell}(M;d_{f},d_{g})\coloneq\left\{\Ev^{\infty}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{f}}{g}\right):\ \begin{array}[]{c}\partial(\boldsymbol{f})<d_{f},\quad\partial(g)<d_{g},\\
\gcd(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell},g)=1\\
\end{array}\right\}.

We will refer to it as the SRF code with poles.

Being composed of two parts, codewords
\Ev^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)
can be affected by two kinds of errors (valuation and evaluation errors).
Here we adapt the hybrid analysis of Section
3
, with the factorization of the error locator
\Lambda=\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{u}
reflecting these two types of errors (see Definition
4.8
).

Definition 4.3
.

Let the
ambient space of received words
be the quotient

\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\ell}\coloneq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}[0,\lambda_{j}]\times\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\ell}\right)/\sim

where
\sim
is the equivalence relation for which
(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\ \sim\ (\vr^{\prime}_{j},\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
if and only if for every

j=1,\ldots,n,\ (x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr^{\prime}_{j}}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}=(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
. We say that a representative
(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
is
reduced
if
\gcd(\boldsymbol{r}_{j},(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}})=1
for every

j=1,\ldots,n
. Define
R_{i}\coloneq\CRT_{M}(r_{i,1},\dots,r_{i,n})
for
every
i=1,\ldots,\ell
.

In what follows we can
always assume that the received word
(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
is reduced, thanks to the following proposition:

Proposition 4.4
.

Any equivalence class contains a reduced representative.

Proof.

Given any received word
(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
, for
every
j=1,\ldots,n
we let

(x-\alpha_{j})^{\eta_{j}}\coloneq\gcd(\boldsymbol{r}_{j},(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}})
. Then

(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\sim\left(\vr_{j}-\eta_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j}^{\lambda_{j}}/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\eta_{j}}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\right)_{1\leq j\leq n},

with the representative on the right-hand side
clearly reduced by the definition of
(x-\alpha_{j})^{\eta_{j}}
.
∎

In the ambient space
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\ell}
we identify received words which represent the same reduced vector of fractions in the sense that, by definition

1.

(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\ \sim\ (\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\ \Leftrightarrow\boldsymbol{r}_{j}=\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}-\vr_{j}}
.

2.

Given a received valuation
0\leq\vr_{j}\leq\lambda_{j}
then for every
1\leq\delta_{j}\leq\lambda_{j}-\vr_{j}

(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\ \sim\ (\vr_{j}+\delta_{j},(x-\alpha_{j})^{\delta_{j}}\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}.

Remark 4.5
.

Thanks to the first of the above two points we can map the evaluation of a reduced vector of rationals
\Ev^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)
into the space of received words.

Definition 4.6
.

Given two elements
\boldsymbol{R}_{1}:=(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n},\boldsymbol{R}_{2}:=(\vr^{\prime}_{j},\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
in
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\ell}
, we
define the columns
\boldsymbol{e}_{j}
of the relative error matrix
\boldsymbol{E}_{\boldsymbol{R}_{1},\boldsymbol{R}_{2}}
as

\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\coloneq(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j}-(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr^{\prime}_{j}}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}.

We let the relative error and truth locator be

\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{R}_{1},\boldsymbol{R}_{2}}\coloneq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}-\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(\boldsymbol{e}_{j})},\quad Y_{\boldsymbol{R}_{1},\boldsymbol{R}_{2}}\coloneq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(\boldsymbol{e}_{j})}

respectively, and the relative distance

d\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{1},\boldsymbol{R}_{2}\right)\coloneq\partial\left(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{R}_{1},\boldsymbol{R}_{2}}\right)
.

Remark 4.7
.

Unlike the errors considered in Sections
2

and
3
, in this case the usual relation

\boldsymbol{R}_{1}=\boldsymbol{R}_{2}+\boldsymbol{E}
does not hold.
For this reason the error models (see Subsection
4.4
) will be defined directly by distributions in the space of received words
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\ell}
.

In spite of the above remark, we note the consistency of the error
\boldsymbol{e}_{j}
with the equivalence relation
\sim
, indeed by definition

\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=\boldsymbol{0}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\quad\forall j=1,\ldots,n\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ \ (\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\sim(\vr^{\prime}_{j},\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}.

Due to the properties of
\sim
, we can partition the set of error positions into valuation and evaluation errors.

Definition 4.8
.

Given two evaluations

(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
,
(\vr^{\prime}_{j},\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\in\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\ell}

satisfying

\gcd((x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})=1
,
we divide the error support

\xi=\{j\ |\ (x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j}\neq(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr^{\prime}_{j}}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\}=\{j\ |\ (\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})\not\sim(\vr^{\prime}_{j},\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j})\}

into the
valuation errors

\xi_{v}:=\{j\ |\ \vr_{j}\neq\vr^{\prime}_{j}\}

and the remaining
evaluation errors

\xi_{e}=\{j\ |\ (\vr_{j}=\vr^{\prime}_{j})\text{ and }(\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\neq\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{j}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}-\vr_{j}})\}.

We provide an equivalent, yet more practical, representation of the errors.

Remark 4.9
.

Given a codeword
\left(\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g),\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)\right)_{1\leq j\leq n}
(as in
Definition
4.1
) and a received word
(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\in\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\ell}
,
the sequence of error vectors
(\boldsymbol{e}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
is given by

\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)-(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}.

Multiplying the above by the invertible element
g/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}
, we obtain that up to invertible transformations of the error
sequence components (leaving the distance unchanged), we can equivalently view the sequence of
error vectors as given by

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}\coloneq\frac{g}{(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\boldsymbol{f}-g\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}.

Study of potential errors and received words around a fixed codeword

Due to Remark
4.7
, we need to study what kind of errors and received words we can obtain around a fixed vector of fractions
\boldsymbol{f}/g
,
in particular with respect to the distinction between valuation and evaluation errors.
Regarding the error positions as long as
\xi_{e},\xi_{v}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\xi_{e}\cap\xi_{v}=\varnothing
we have no constraints: all valuation (resp. evaluation) error supports
\xi_{v}
(resp.
\xi_{e}
) are attained.
Once the error positions have been fixed and partitioned as
\xi_{v}\cup\xi_{e}
, the valuations of the error vectors need to satisfy
\mu_{j}=\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(\boldsymbol{e}_{j})=\lambda_{j}
for every position
j
which is not erroneous,
i.e.
\forall j\notin\xi_{e}\cup\xi_{v}
.
Let us examine what can happen in the evaluation and valuation error cases respectively:

1.

If
j\in\xi_{e}
, we have an evaluation error, thus any received word
\boldsymbol{R}
must satisfy
\vr_{j}=\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)
, furthermore we must have that the valuation of any error vector
\boldsymbol{e}_{j}
must satisfy
\mu_{j}=\nu_{\alpha_{j}}((x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\boldsymbol{f}-g\boldsymbol{r}_{j})\geq\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)
thus, dividing by
(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}
, we have that
\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}/g)-r_{j}
can be any element of valuation

\mu_{j}-\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)
.

2.

If
j\in\xi_{v}
, we have a valuation error, thus for every received word we have either

(a)

\vr_{j}<\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)
: in this case the valuation of the error vector and the received word must coincide,
i.e.
\mu_{j}=\vr_{j}
, and from the definition of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}
we must have that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}=(x-\alpha_{j})^{\mu_{j}}\boldsymbol{f}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}
, regardless of the reduction part
\boldsymbol{r}_{j}
. Thus, in this case we do not have any constraints on
\boldsymbol{r}_{j}
.

(b)

\vr_{j}>\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)
: in this case the valuation of the error vector must coincide with the valuation of
g
,
i.e.
\mu_{j}=\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)
. Besides this valuation constraint, the error vectors can take any value, as well as the received reductions
\boldsymbol{r}_{j}
.

Minimal distance

As for the SRF code without poles (see Lemma
2.4
), also in this case we can prove the following lower bound on the minimal distance of the code.

Lemma 4.10
.

We have
d\left(\mathsf{SRF}^{\infty}_{\ell}(M;d_{f},d_{g})\right)\geq L-d_{f}-d_{g}+2
.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}=(\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g),\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}/g))_{1\leq j\leq n},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}=(\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g^{\prime}),\mathcal{S}_{j}(\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}/g^{\prime}))_{1\leq j\leq n}
be two distinct codewords.
From

\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g^{\prime})}}\right)-(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g^{\prime})}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{f}}{g/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}}\right)\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}},

we see that

\frac{g}{(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}}\frac{g^{\prime}}{(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g^{\prime})}}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g-\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime}\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}.

Using
\Lambda\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=0\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
for all
j
, we obtain

\forall 1\leq j\leq n,\quad 0=\Lambda\frac{g}{(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}}\frac{g^{\prime}}{(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g^{\prime})}}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g-\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime})\ \bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}.

Therefore,
M
divides
\Lambda(\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g-\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime})
, so
Y=M/\Lambda
divides
(\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}g-\boldsymbol{f}g^{\prime})
.
Hence, for all codewords
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}\neq\boldsymbol{C}_{2}
, we bound
d(\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2})=\partial(\Lambda)=\partial(M/Y)>L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1
, and we have proven the lemma.
∎

Due to the different multiplicities among the evaluation points, we need to assume Constraint
4.11
on the parameters of the code in order to prove that the lower bound
L-d_{f}-d_{g}+2
on the minimial distance of the code can be tight (see Lemma
4.12
).

Constraint 4.11
.

Given the multiplicities
\left(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}
and the degree bounds
d_{f},d_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
L\coloneq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}>d_{f}+d_{g}-2
, we assume that there exist two disjoint subsets
S_{0},S_{\infty}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
and two (not necessarily distinct) indexes
\eta,\gamma\notin S_{0}\cup S_{\infty}
, such that:

1.

d_{f}-1=\delta_{0}+\sum_{j\in S_{0}}\lambda_{j}
with
0\leq\delta_{0}<\lambda_{\eta}

2.

d_{g}-1=\delta_{\infty}+\sum_{j\in S_{\infty}}\lambda_{j}
with
0\leq\delta_{\infty}<\lambda_{\gamma}

3.

\delta_{0},\delta_{\infty}>0\Rightarrow\eta\neq\gamma

The verification of this constraint represents a particular case of the
multiple subset sum
problem, which is known to be NP-complete on general instances
[
8
]
).
The proof of the next lemma is going to highlight the reasons behind the conditions of Constraint
4.11
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Assuming Constraint
4.11
, we have
d\left(\mathsf{SRF}^{\infty}_{\ell}(M;d_{f},d_{g})\right)=L-d_{f}-d_{g}+2
.

Proof.

We already proved that the minimal distance is lower bounded by the quantity:
L-d_{f}-d_{g}+2
.
Thanks to Constraint
4.11
we can define the relatively prime polynomials

\displaystyle f_{1}\coloneq\left(x-\alpha_{\eta}\right)^{\delta_{0}}\prod_{j\in S_{0}}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}},\quad g\coloneq\left(x-\alpha_{\gamma}\right)^{\delta_{\infty}}\prod_{j\in S_{\infty}}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}},

whose degrees are respectively
d_{f}-1
and
d_{g}-1
thanks to the first two points of Constraint
4.11
. The third point of Constraint
4.11
(together with
S_{0}\cap S_{\infty}=\emptyset
) ensure that
\gcd(f_{1},g)=1
.

For
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}
, we let
f_{2}\coloneq\beta f_{1}
and we define the two codewords
\boldsymbol{C}_{i}\coloneq\Ev^{\infty}\left(f_{i}\boldsymbol{1}/g\right)=\left(\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g),C_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq j\leq n}
for
i=1,2
, where
\boldsymbol{1}=\left(1,\ldots,1\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}
.
We note that for every
j\in S_{0}\cup S_{\infty}
the following holds

(\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g),C_{1,j})=(\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g),C_{2,j})=\begin{cases}(0,\boldsymbol{0})&\text{ if }j\in S_{0}\\
(\lambda_{j},\boldsymbol{1})&\text{ if }j\in S_{\infty}\end{cases}

thus
\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(Y_{\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}})=\lambda_{j}
for every
j\in S_{0}\cup S_{\infty}
.

Furthermore we see that
\nu_{\alpha_{\eta}}(g)=0
and
C_{1,\eta}=C_{2,\eta}=\boldsymbol{0}\bmod(x-\alpha_{\eta})^{\delta_{0}}
, from which it follows that
\nu_{\alpha_{\eta}}(Y_{\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}})\geq\delta_{0}
. Similarly, we have that
\nu_{\alpha_{\gamma}}\left(Y_{\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}}\right)\geq\delta_{\infty}
.

We notice that

\displaystyle\frac{\boldsymbol{e}_{\eta}}{(x-\alpha_{\eta})^{\delta_{0}}}=\frac{\prod_{j\in S_{0}}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}}{\left(x-\alpha_{\gamma}\right)^{\delta_{\infty}}\prod_{j\in S_{\infty}}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}}(1-\beta)\neq\boldsymbol{0}\ \bmod\left(x-\alpha_{\eta}\right)^{\lambda_{\eta}-\delta_{0}}

\displaystyle\frac{\boldsymbol{e}_{\gamma}}{(x-\alpha_{\gamma})^{\delta_{\infty}}}=\frac{\left(x-\alpha_{\eta}\right)^{\delta_{0}}\prod_{j\in S_{0}}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}}{\prod_{j\in S_{\infty}}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}}(1-\beta)\neq\boldsymbol{0}\ \bmod\left(x-\alpha_{\eta}\right)^{\lambda_{\gamma}-\delta_{\infty}}

from which it follows that
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}\neq\boldsymbol{C}_{2}
.

Thus

d_{f}+d_{g}-2=\left(\sum_{j\in S_{0}\cup S_{\infty}}\lambda_{j}\right)+\delta_{0}+\delta_{\infty}\leq\partial(Y_{\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}}),

and we conclude that
d\left(\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}\right)=L-\partial(Y_{\boldsymbol{C}_{1},\boldsymbol{C}_{2}})\leq L-d_{f}-d_{g}+2

∎

Remark 4.13
.

Constraint
4.11
takes inspiration from the proof of Lemma
4.12
in absence of multiplicities (
\lambda_{j}=1
for every
j=1,\ldots,n
)
[
21
, Theorem 2.3.1]
, in which case it is a natural consequence of the hypothesis
L>d_{f}+d_{g}-2
, by taking
S_{0}=\{1,2,\ldots,d_{f}-1\},S_{\infty}=\{d_{f}+1,d_{f}+2,\ldots,d_{f}+d_{g}-1\}
,
\delta_{0}=\delta_{\infty}=0
and
\eta=\gamma=d_{f}
.

Corollary 4.14
.

Given the parameters of the code

4.1
Key equations

As for the code of Section
2
, the decoding of SRF codes with poles is based on the resolution of a system of linear
key equations
.
Thanks to Remark
4.9
and the definition of
\Lambda
, we have that

\Lambda\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=0\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
, and so

0=\Lambda\widetilde{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}=(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\Lambda\boldsymbol{f}-\Lambda g\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}

Thus, for every couple of received word
\left(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\right)_{1\leq j\leq n}
and reduced vector of
fractions
\boldsymbol{f}/g
the equation

\CRT_{M}\left((x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\right)\Lambda f_{i}=\Lambda gR_{i}\ \bmod M

holds for every
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. By defining the new variables
\varphi\coloneq\Lambda g
,

\boldsymbol{\psi}=\Lambda\boldsymbol{f}
we get the
key equations
in presence of poles:

\forall i=1,\ldots,\ell,\quad\CRT_{M}\left((x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\right)\psi_{i}=\varphi R_{i}\ \bmod M.

(11)

For some distance parameter
t
, we let the set of solutions be

\displaystyle S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}\coloneq\left\{\left(\varphi,\boldsymbol{\psi}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{\ell+1}:\begin{array}[]{c}\CRT_{M}\left((x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\right)\psi_{i}=\varphi R_{i}\ \bmod M,\quad\forall i\\
\partial(\varphi)<d_{g}+t,\ \partial(\boldsymbol{\psi})<d_{f}+t\end{array}\right\}.

If
\partial(\Lambda)\leq t
we see that
v_{\boldsymbol{C}}\coloneq(\Lambda g,\Lambda\boldsymbol{f})\in S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}
.

Reduced key equations

It is possible to give an equivalent description of the solutions in
S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}
, whose degree bounds are smaller.
Letting
M_{\infty}:=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}
we note that, thanks to Equation (

),
M_{\infty}|\varphi
since
M_{\infty}|\CRT_{M}\left((x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\right)

M_{\infty}|M
and by hypothesis
\gcd(M_{\infty},R_{i})=1
as received words are assumed to be reduced. Thus, we can rewrite Equation (

) in the following form, which we call
reduced key equations

\forall i=1,\ldots,\ell,\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{i}=\varphi^{\prime}R_{i}^{\prime}\bmod\frac{M}{M_{\infty}}\\
\partial(\varphi^{\prime})<d_{g}+t-\partial(M_{\infty})\text{ and }\partial(\boldsymbol{\psi})<d_{f}+t\end{array}

where
\varphi^{\prime}\coloneq\varphi/M_{\infty}
and
R_{i}^{\prime}\coloneq R_{i}\CRT_{M/M_{\infty}}\left(\frac{M_{\infty}}{(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}}\right)
.

4.3
Unique decoding

We notice that Algorithm
2
it is always correct in decoding SRF codes with poles whenever the distance parameter

Proposition 4.16
.

Given the SRF code with poles and a received word
\boldsymbol{R}=(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
, we suppose that the distance parameter input of Algorithm
2
satisfies

\boldsymbol{f}/g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)^{\ell}
with
\partial(\boldsymbol{f})<d_{f}
and
\partial(g)<d_{g}
such that

S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}\subset v_{\boldsymbol{C}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.

Proof.

By hypothesis
d(\Ev^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{f}/g),(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n})\leq t
, we have
v_{\boldsymbol{C}}=(\Lambda g,\Lambda\boldsymbol{f})\in S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}
.
Let
(\varphi,\boldsymbol{\psi})\in S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}
be another solution of the key equations.
We have that

\begin{cases}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\Lambda\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\Lambda g&\phantom{}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\\
(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\boldsymbol{\psi}\ =\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\varphi&\phantom{}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}\end{cases}

for some

\Lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
\partial\left(\Lambda\right)\leq t\leq\left\lfloor\frac{L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1}{2}\right\rfloor
. Since the received word
(\vr_{j},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
is assumed to be reduced,
i.e.
\gcd((x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}},\boldsymbol{r}_{j})=1
, from the above we get that

(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}|\Lambda g
and
(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}|\varphi
for every
j=1,\ldots,n
. Thus, when multiplying the first equation by
\varphi
and the second one by

\Lambda g
we get

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\Lambda\varphi\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\Lambda g\varphi&\phantom{}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}+\vr_{j}}\\
(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\Lambda g\boldsymbol{\psi}=\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\Lambda g\varphi&\phantom{}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}+\vr_{j}}\end{cases}

subtracting one another, and dividing by

\Lambda\left(\varphi\boldsymbol{f}-g\boldsymbol{\psi}\right)=0\bmod M.

By hypothesis, we have that

\partial\left(\Lambda(\varphi\boldsymbol{f}-g\boldsymbol{\psi})\right)\leq 2t+d_{f}+d_{g}-2<L

which implies that
\Lambda\left(\varphi\boldsymbol{f}-g\boldsymbol{\psi}\right)=0

\varphi\boldsymbol{f}=g\boldsymbol{\psi}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{\ell}

Since
\boldsymbol{f}/g
is a reduced vector of rational functions, there exists

such that
(\varphi,\boldsymbol{\psi})=p(g,\boldsymbol{f})

Substituting in the key equations for
(\varphi,\boldsymbol{\psi})

p((x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{r}_{j}g)=0\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}
.
However,
\Lambda
divides

by definition of
\Lambda

(\varphi,\boldsymbol{\psi})\in v_{\boldsymbol{C}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]

∎

\CRT_{\Lambda_{e}}\left(\frac{\varphi}{(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}}\right)\widetilde{E}^{\prime}_{i}=\frac{\varphi f_{i}-g\psi_{i}}{Y}\ \bmod\Lambda_{e},

with
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}_{i}\coloneq\CRT_{\Lambda_{e}}\left(g/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}\right)E^{\prime}_{i}\ \bmod\Lambda_{e}
. Thus,
\omega\coloneq\CRT_{\Lambda_{e}}\left(\varphi/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\right)\in S_{\boldsymbol{E}}^{\infty}
and, thanks to the hypothesis
S_{\boldsymbol{E}}^{\infty}=\{0\}
,
\left(\varphi f_{i}-g\psi_{i}\right)/Y=0\ \bmod\Lambda_{e}
. Thanks to Constraint
4.21
and since
\partial(\Lambda_{v})\leq t_{v}
, we have

\partial\left(\frac{g\psi_{i}-f_{i}\varphi}{Y}\right)\leq B+\partial(\Lambda)<\partial(\Lambda_{e}).

As a result,
g\psi_{i}=f_{i}\varphi
for all
i=1,\ldots,\ell
.
Since
\gcd(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell},g)=1
, we must have that
g|\varphi
, i.e.
\varphi=sg
for
some
s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and, from the above conclusion, as well that
\boldsymbol{\psi}=s\boldsymbol{f}
. Let us
note

s\widetilde{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}=(x-\alpha_{j})^{\vr_{j}}\boldsymbol{\psi}-\varphi\boldsymbol{r}_{j}=\boldsymbol{0}\bmod(x-\alpha_{j})^{\lambda_{j}}.

As
\nu_{\alpha_{j}}\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}\right)=\nu_{\alpha_{j}}\left(\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\right)=\lambda_{j}-\Val_{j}(\Lambda)
, we obtain
\nu_{j}(s)\geq\lambda_{j}-(\lambda_{j}-\Val_{j}(\Lambda))

for every
j
, i.e.
\Lambda
divides
s
.
∎

Remark 4.23
.

Along the same lines of Remark
2.14
relative to the analysis of Section
2
, also in this context we see that when the distance parameter
t
of the decoding algorithm satisfies
t<\left\lfloor\frac{L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1}{2}\right\rfloor
and thus, thanks to Lemma
4.10
, it is
below half of the minimal distance of the SRF code with poles, we must have that
B+\partial(\Lambda)\leq B+t<0
since

\partial(\Lambda)\leq t
.
Under such circumstance we therefore
have
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]_{\Lambda_{e},B+\partial(\Lambda)}=\{0\}
and thus, similarly to Remark
2.14
, estimating the
failure probability of Algorithm
2
by studying
\mathbb{P}(S_{\boldsymbol{E}}^{\infty}\neq\{0\})

yields the expected unique decoding result whenever
t<\left\lfloor\frac{L-d_{f}-d_{g}+1}{2}\right\rfloor
.

Proof of Theorem
4.18
.

Since for all received words in
our random distribution, we know that if
S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}\subseteq v_{\boldsymbol{C}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
then Algorithm
2
succeeds, thus by contrapositive

\mathbb{P}_{f\!ail}\leq\mathbb{P}(S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}\not\subseteq v_{\boldsymbol{C}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[x])
.

We can prove that our choice of parameters satisfy Constraint
4.21
in the same fashion as the proof of
Theorem
3.2
. So we can apply Lemma
4.22
to
obtain
\mathbb{P}(S_{\boldsymbol{R}}^{\infty}\not\subseteq v_{\boldsymbol{C}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[x])\leq\mathbb{P}\left(S_{\boldsymbol{E}}^{\infty}\neq\{0\}\right)
.

As in Equation (
2.5.1
), we have

\mathbb{P}\left(S_{\boldsymbol{E}}^{\infty}\neq\{0\}\right)\leq\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\omega=1}^{\Lambda_{e}-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\forall i,\ \omega\widetilde{E}_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]_{\Lambda_{e},B+\partial(\Lambda)}\right)
.
Since
\widetilde{E}_{i}=\CRT_{M}\left(g/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}\right)E_{i}\ \bmod M
, and since

\CRT_{M}\left(g/(x-\alpha_{j})^{\nu_{\alpha_{j}}(g)}\right)
is an invertible element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/M
, we have that for
every
1\leq\omega\leq\Lambda_{e}-1
,
\mathbb{P}(\forall i,\ \omega\widetilde{E}_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]_{\Lambda_{e},B+\partial(\Lambda)})=\mathbb{P}(\forall i,\ \omega E_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]_{\Lambda_{e},B+\partial(\Lambda)}).

Now, since we know the distribution of
(E_{i}^{\prime})_{1\leq i\leq n}
thanks to
Lemma
4.17
, we use
Lemma
3.7
with
\Lambda_{i}
and
{t}_{u}
being
replaced by
\Lambda_{e}
and
t_{v}
to get

\sum_{\omega=1}^{\Lambda_{e}-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\forall i,\ \omega E_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]_{\Lambda_{e},B+\partial(\Lambda)}\right)\leq q^{\ell(B+1+t_{v})}q^{\partial(\Lambda_{e})}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{e})}\left(\frac{1-1/q^{\ell+\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{e})}}{1-1/q^{\ell}}\right).

Since
\partial(\Lambda_{e})\leq{t}_{e}
, we have

q^{\ell(B+1+t_{v})}q^{\partial(\Lambda_{e})}\leq q^{\ell(B+1+t_{v})}q^{{t}_{e}}=q^{-(\ell+1)(\bar{t}_{e}-{t}_{e})}
, we have proven Theorem
4.18
.
∎

4.7
Decoding failure probability with respect to the second error model

We will denote
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{m,e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{2}}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{1}}
) the probability function under
the second (resp. first) error model specified by a given factorization
\Lambda_{m,e}\Lambda_{v}
of
the error locator, and by a partial received word
(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{v})}
.

Proof of Theorem
4.19
.

As done in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, letting
\mathcal{F}
be the event of decoding failure and
using the law of total probability, we have that

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{m,e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{2}}(\mathcal{F})
can be decomposed as the sum

\sum_{\Lambda_{e}|\Lambda_{m,e}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{m,e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{2}}(\mathcal{F}\ |\ \Lambda_{\boldsymbol{E}}=\Lambda_{e}\Lambda_{v})\ \mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{m,e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{2}}(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{E}}=\Lambda_{e}\Lambda_{v}),

where

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{m,e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{2}}(\mathcal{F}\ |\ \Lambda_{\boldsymbol{E}}=\Lambda_{e}\Lambda_{v})=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{1}}\left(\mathcal{F}\right)

is upper bounded by

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})\leq\frac{q^{\ell(B+1+t_{v})}q^{\partial(\Lambda_{e})}}{q-1}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{e})}\left(\frac{1-1/q^{\ell+\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{e})}}{1-1/q^{\ell}}\right).

Whereas

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{m,e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{2}}(\mathcal{F}\ |\ \Lambda_{\boldsymbol{E}}=\Lambda_{e}\Lambda_{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{e})}(q^{\ell}-1)q^{\ell(\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{e})-1)}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{m,e})}q^{\ell\nu_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{m,e})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\ell\partial(\Lambda_{e})}}{q^{\ell\partial(\Lambda_{m,e})}}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda_{e})}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{\ell}}\right).

Plugging the above in the decomposition of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda_{m,e}\Lambda_{v},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{R}}}^{2}}(\mathcal{F})
and following the
proof of Theorem
3.3
with
\Lambda_{m,i},\Lambda_{i},\Lambda_{u}
being replaced by

\Lambda_{m,e},\Lambda_{e},\Lambda_{v}
respectively, we conclude the proof of Theorem
4.19
.
∎

Interacting Dirac magnons in honeycomb ferromagnets CrBr 3
Saikat Banerjee, Stephan Humeniuk

Abstract
We study the effects of magnon-magnon interactions in the two-dimensional van der Waals ferromagnet CrBr 3 focusing on its honeycomb lattice structure. Motivated by earlier theoretical predictions of temperature-induced spectral shifts and van Hove singularities in the magnon dispersion Pershoguba et al. 2018 , we go beyond the commonly used thermal magnon approximation by applying second-order perturbation theory in a fully numerical framework. Our analysis uncovers significant deviations from previous analysis: in particular, the predicted singularities are absent, consistent with recent inelastic neutron scattering measurements Nikitin et al. 2022 . Moreover, we find that the temperature dependence of the renormalized magnon spectrum exhibits a distinct T 3 T^{3} behavior for the optical magnon branch, while retaining T 2 T^{2} behavior for the acoustic or down magnon band. This feature sheds new light on the collective dynamics of Dirac magnons and their interactions. We further compare the honeycomb case with a triangular Bravais lattice, relevant for ferromagnetic monolayer MnBi 2 Te 4 , and show that both systems lack singular features while displaying quite distinct thermal trends.



I
Introduction

The discovery of graphene
Geim and Novoselov 2007
and topological insulators
Moore 2010
has led to the emergence of a broad class of systems known as Dirac materials, where low-energy fermionic excitations behave like massless relativistic particles with linearly dispersing energy-momentum relations. These so-called Dirac cones are typically protected by symmetries such as time-reversal or inversion
Wehling et al. 2014
, and while the cones are robust to many-body perturbations, interactions among quasiparticles can lead to subtle renormalizations of the spectrum
Elias et al. 2011
;
Kotov et al. 2012
. This naturally raises the question of whether similar relativistic-like features can arise in bosonic systems. In recent years, various platforms have indeed been identified as bosonic Dirac materials, including phonons in hexagonal lattices
Jin et al. 2018
, excitations in granular superconductors
Banerjee et al. 2016
, magnons in honeycomb ferromagnets
Fransson et al. 2016
;
Bhowmik et al. 2024
, and even plasmons in topological insulators
Di Pietro et al. 2013
. As with their fermionic counterparts, bosonic Dirac dispersions can also be symmetry-protected, yet remain sensitive to the effects of interactions
Kumar et al. 2020
. A recent theoretical study highlighted how such interactions can qualitatively reshape the spectrum, offering a simple but illustrative example of renormalization effects in Dirac boson systems
Banerjee et al. 2020
.

Among the various bosonic Dirac systems, Dirac magnons have attracted growing attention. The chromium trihalides, CrX
3
(X = F, Cl, Br, I), provide a natural platform for realizing such excitations, with CrBr
3
and CrCl
3
hosting linearly dispersing Dirac magnons
Wang et al. 2011
;
Soriano et al. 2020
, and CrI
3
showing evidence for topologically gapped magnon bands
Jiang et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Kim et al. 2024
. These are magnetic van der Waals materials where spins reside on a two-dimensional honeycomb lattice, making them ideal for exploring magnon band topology. In earlier theoretical work
Pershoguba et al. 2018
, we studied Dirac magnons in ferromagnetic CrBr
3
and demonstrated that magnon-magnon interactions lead to a renormalization of the Dirac dispersion, captured within a generalized Dyson spin-wave framework
Dyson 1956a
;
Dyson 1956b
. Notably, we found a characteristic
T^{2}
temperature dependence in the renormalized magnon energies, along with prominent van Hove-like structures in the interacting spectrum, especially near the M points of the Brillouin zone (BZ). These findings were motivated by longstanding anomalies observed in inelastic neutron scattering (INS) measurements of CrBr
3
from nearly five decades ago
Yelon and Silberglitt 1971
;
Samuelsen et al. 1971
, which had remained unexplained.

Our previous work
Pershoguba et al. 2018
analyzed magnonic interactions by approximating a continuously thermally excited magnon and focusing on scattering processes where this thermal magnon interacts with neutron-scattered magnons throughout the BZ. This analysis, based on second-order perturbation theory, predicted a quadratic temperature-dependent (
T^{2}
) self-energy correction and van Hove singularities near the M point and the Dirac point. However, recent INS measurements on CrBr
3

Nikitin et al. 2022
have revealed a discrepancy with these predictions. While Nikitin et al.
Nikitin et al. 2022
confirmed the
T^{2}
- self-energy correction, they did not observe the predicted momentum-dependent van Hove features. Dip structures in the magnon spectrum around the BZ boundaries have also been predicted for ferromagnetic Bravais lattices, including the monolayer ferromagnet MnBi
2
Te
4
. However, these features have not been observed experimentally
Bayrakci et al. 2006
;
Bayrakci et al. 2013
, necessitating a more sophisticated theoretical investigation. Motivated by these experimental results, we revisit the interacting Dirac magnon in CrBr
3
, going beyond the continuous thermal magnon approximation. We further incorporate a small on-site single-ion anisotropy to reconcile the observed long-range magnetic order at finite temperature with the Mermin-Wagner theorem
Mermin and Wagner 1966
. To capture the renormalization in an unbiased manner, we perform extensive numerical computations that evaluate self-energy corrections to the second order in perturbation theory, including self-consistent contributions.

We find that the renormalized magnon spectrum lacks van Hove singularities near the BZ corners, exhibiting instead a strong, non-monotonic momentum dependence. This contrasts sharply with predictions from prior work. A direct comparison with recent INS data
Nikitin et al. 2022
supports our findings. We also performed a similar analysis for the triangular lattice, relevant to monolayer MnBi
2
Te
4
, and confirmed that a second-order perturbation theory – without the thermal magnon approximation – does not host any van Hove singularities or dip structures, consistent with prior experimental observations
Bayrakci et al. 2006
;
Bayrakci et al. 2013
. Analysis of the temperature dependence of the magnon energy renormalization reveals a deviation from the previously predicted
T^{2}
- behavior. While our numerical results remain consistent with a
T^{2}
- dependence for the acoustic magnon branch, the optical branch exhibits a distinct
T^{3}
- dependence. Notably, recent theoretical work
Luo et al. 2025
reported a
T^{3}
- dependence across the entire renormalized magnon band structure for CrI
3
. In contrast, we find quadratic temperature dependence for the acoustic branch and cubic dependence for the optical branch within our honeycomb framework.
Finally, we note that small fluctuations in the temperature exponent
\alpha
for both up (
\alpha=3\pm\delta)
and down
(\alpha=2\pm\delta
) bands persist across the entire BZ. We interpret these fluctuations as arising from thermal effects, consistent with earlier theoretical predictions
Kosevich and Chubukov 1986
. Further controlled INS experiments are needed to confirm this modified temperature dependence and elucidate the underlying physics.

II
Model

We start with a ferromagnetic Heisenberg model on a honeycomb lattice with a tiny single-ion anisotropy relevant for CrBr
3
. The corresponding Hamiltonian is written as

\mathcal{H}=-J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j}+A\sum_{i}(S^{z}_{i})^{2},

(1)

where
J
is the strength of the ferromagnetic coupling, and
A
is a single-ion anisotropy term. We adapt to the parameters from Ref.
Nikitin et al. 2022
and choose
J=1.494
meV and
A=-0.028
meV. The tiny magnitude of the single-ion anisotropy term is adopted from the previous ferromagnetic resonance experiment
Alyoshin et al. 1997
. Below the Curie temperature (
T_{c}\sim 37
K)
Tang et al. 2020
;
O. Fumega et al. 2020
;
Kozlenko et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2025

S^{+}_{i}\approx\sqrt{2S}\left(1-\frac{n_{\eta_{i}}}{4S}\right)\eta_{i},\;S^{-}_{i}=(S^{+}_{i})^{\dagger},\;S^{z}_{i}=S-n_{\eta_{i}},

(2)

where

are the ladder operators,
n_{\eta_{i}}=\eta^{\dagger}_{i}\eta_{i}
is the number operator,
S=\frac{3}{2}

\mathcal{H}_{0}=JS\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}&b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}3-\frac{2A}{J}&-\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}\\
-\gamma^{\ast}_{\mathbf{k}}&3-\frac{2A}{J}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{\mathbf{k}}\\
b_{\mathbf{k}}\end{pmatrix},

(3)

where

being the three nearest-neighbor vectors in the honeycomb geometry as shown in Fig.
1
(a). The neighboring vectors are chosen as
\bm{\delta}_{1}=\tfrac{a}{2}(1,\sqrt{3})
,
\bm{\delta}_{2}=\tfrac{a}{2}(1,-\sqrt{3})

\Psi^{u,d}_{\mathbf{k}}=(e^{i\phi_{\mathbf{k}}/2}\mp e^{-i\phi_{\mathbf{k}}/2})/\sqrt{2}
, and the momentum-dependent phase is defined as
\phi_{\mathbf{k}}={\rm{arg}}\,\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}
. The corresponding spectrum for
up
and
down
magnons is shown in Fig.
1
(c).

II.1
Hartree contribution

Considering spin

, we now set up the perturbation theory in up and down magnon branches. The first-order Hartree correction is computed from Fig.

Pershoguba et al. 2018

\Sigma_{\rm{H}}(T)=\frac{J}{2N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\begin{bmatrix}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}&b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}h(T)&g_{\mathbf{k}}(T)\\
g^{\ast}_{\mathbf{k}}(T)&h(T)\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a_{\mathbf{k}}\\
b_{\mathbf{k}}\end{bmatrix},

(6)

where the temperature-dependent functions

7

After analyzing and obtaining the decay rates and self-energy corrections for each of the five processes in the previous section, we now calculate the total decay rate and self-energy correction for the up and down magnon bands. We notice that the overall magnitudes for the first, second and third processes are much larger than the fourth and fifth processes as shown in Fig.

A notable feature highlighted by our precise numerical results in Fig.
5

Pershoguba et al. 2018

Nikitin et al. 2022

(25)

\Gamma

However, at higher temperatures, the self-energy correction becomes more significant, potentially resulting in a negative overall renormalized spectrum. Henelius et al. 2000

Zauner-Stauber et al. 2018

Ponsioen et al. 2022

In principle, the negative energy down magnons for the small momentum case (i.e., long-wavelength) correspond to thermal fluctuations, which become increasingly significant as temperature rises in a ferromagnet. This phenomenon is well-known in perturbation theory and has been predicted in previous theoretical work by Kosevich and Chubukov
Kosevich and Chubukov 1986
.

V
Discussion and conclusion

In this paper, we investigated the role of magnon-magnon interactions in the well-known ferromagnetic material CrBr
3
. Our focus was on the temperature-dependent renormalization of the magnon spectrum and decay rates, using second-order perturbation theory. Our numerical results clearly show that, for the honeycomb lattice, a detailed analysis – without any thermal magnon approximations – reveals a
T^{2}
dependence for the band corresponding to the Hartree contribution. In contrast, the second-order self-energy correction yields a
T^{2}
dependence for the lower energy band while the upper energy band exhibits a
T^{3}
dependence. These findings starkly contrast with previous predictions, which suggested a quadratic temperature dependence for both bands in the cases of Hartree and second-order corrections.

Figure 11:
A comparison between the bare (solid line) and self-consistent (dashed line) calculation for the imaginary (a) and real (b) part of the second-order self-energy correction obtained from Eq. (
31
) at various temperatures along the high-symmetry points in the BZ. We chose the parameters as
J=0.2496
meV and

In this context, we emphasize a subtle point first theoretically predicted by Y. A. Kosevich and A. V. Chubukov in Ref.
Kosevich and Chubukov 1986
. Their analysis evaluated both Hartree and second-order self-energy corrections using a long-wavelength approximation for low-energy magnons. This approach predicted additional logarithmic factors: specifically, the Hartree correction behaves as
\Delta\varepsilon^{\rm H}_{\mathbf{k}}\sim T^{2}|\ln\{{\rm max}(\tfrac{k_{\rm B}T}{JS},\mathbf{k}^{2})\}|
, while the second-order correction scales as
\Delta\varepsilon^{(2)}_{\mathbf{k}}\sim T^{2}|\ln(\tfrac{\mathbf{k}^{2}JS}{k_{\rm B}T})|
for the regime where
JS\mathbf{k}^{2}\ll k_{\rm B}T
. Our numerical results reveal a similar logarithmic dependence for the triangular lattice case in the Hartree scenario, while the second-order correction shows similar characteristics to those previously mentioned in Ref.
Kosevich and Chubukov 1986
. However, such temperature dependence is completely absent in the non-Bravais case and therefore, our results clearly differentiates between the Bravais and non-Bravais lattices for the first time, to the best of our knowledge. Additionally, we believe that the presence of Dirac nodes in the band structure seems to be the underlying reason for drastic difference in the temperature behavior for the honeycomb (
non-Bravais
) magnets compared to the triangular (
Bravais
) case. As a secondary observation, we note a slight mismatch between the upper and lower magnon bands in the renormalized self-energy at the Dirac point for the honeycomb lattice. Interestingly, no such gap appears in the corresponding decay spectrum. We attribute this discrepancy to the absence of vertex corrections in our current perturbative treatment. A self-consistent inclusion of vertex corrections is expected to restore the Dirac cone structure. Nevertheless, the qualitative features of the real part of the self-energy, as shown in Fig.
4
and Fig.
5
, are expected to remain robust.

The central message of our work is that all quantities in second-order perturbation theory must be computed explicitly, without relying on simplifying assumptions such as the thermal magnon or long-wavelength approximations. To compare, we applied the same methodology to the triangular lattice ferromagnet MnBi
2
Te
4
and found significant qualitative differences between the complete perturbative calculation and the thermal magnon approximation. Notably, the latter predicts van Hove-like singularities in the magnon energies in the BZ and remains absent if analyzed without approximation. As a stringent test of our theoretical predictions, we performed a detailed comparison with recent inelastic neutron scattering (INS) data from S. E. Nikitin et al.
Nikitin et al. 2022
, as presented in Sec.
III
. The absence of van Hove singularities in our full calculation (see Fig.
3
) aligns with the experimental observations reported for CrBr
3
, thereby reinforcing the validity of our approach. In conclusion, we observe that the presence of a
T^{3}
dependence for the upper magnon band in the honeycomb lattice case seems to contradict the quadratic temperature behavior noted in Ref.
Nikitin et al. 2022
. However, it’s important to recognize that the total renormalization for the upper band is the result of both Hartree and self-energy contributions. The Hartree term exhibits a
T^{2}
dependence, as discussed in Sec.
II.2
, and its magnitude is greater than that of the self-energy term, which is predicted to be proportional to
T^{3}
. Consequently, the observed
T^{2}
behavior in experiments likely reflects the Hartree contribution, a point also confirmed by the authors.

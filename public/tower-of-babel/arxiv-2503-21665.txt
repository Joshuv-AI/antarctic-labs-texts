Non-quasicontinuous Newtonian functions and outer capacities based on Banach function spaces
Anders Björn

Abstract




Abstract.
We construct various examples of Sobolev-type functions,
defined via upper gradients in metric spaces,
that fail to be quasicontinuous or weakly quasicontinuous.
This is done with
quasi-Banach function lattices
X
as the
function spaces
defining the smoothness of the Sobolev-type functions.
These results are in contrast to the case
X=L^{p}
with
1\leq p<\infty
,
where all Sobolev-type functions in
N^{1,p}
are known to be quasicontinuous,
provided that the underlying metric space
\mathcal{P}
is locally complete.
In most of our examples,

\mathcal{P}

is a compact subset of
\mathbf{R}^{2}

and
X=L^{\infty}
.
Four
particular examples are the damped topologist’s sine curve,
the von Koch snowflake curve, the Cantor ternary set and the Sierpiński
carpet.
We also discuss several related properties, such as whether the Sobolev
capacity is an outer capacity, and how these properties are related.
A fundamental role in these considerations is played by the lack of
the Vitali–Carathéodory property.

Key words and phrases
:
Banach function lattice,
Banach function space,
metric space,
Newtonian space,
outer capacity,
quasicontinuity,
Sobolev capacity,
upper gradient,
Vitali–Carathéodory property,
weak quasicontinuity.

Introduction

We assume throughout the paper that
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P},d,\mu)

is a metric measure space equipped with a
positive complete Borel measure

\mu
such that
0<\mu(B)<\infty
for every ball
B\subset\mathcal{P}
.
Such spaces allow themselves to first order analysis, including
Sobolev type spaces, geometric analysis and potential theory as in
Hajłasz
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
Heinonen–Koskela
[
18
]
,
Cheeger
[
10
]
,
Shanmugalingam
[
28
]
,
Heinonen
[
17
]

and many subsequent papers,
as well as the books by
Hajłasz–Koskela
[
15
]
,
Heinonen
[
16
]
,
Björn–Björn
[
5
]

and Heinonen–Koskela–Shanmugalingam–Tyson
[
19
]
.

Since the late 1990s it has been an open question whether
all functions in the
(Sobolev) Newtonian space
N^{1,p}(\mathcal{P})
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
are quasicontinuous, without additional assumptions on
\mathcal{P}
.
It has recently been answered in the affirmative in
Eriksson-Bique–Poggi-Corradini
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]

when
\mathcal{P}
is locally complete.

In this paper we shall see
that this is not so in the wider generality
of Newtonian spaces
{N^{1}X}(\mathcal{P})
based on (quasi)-Banach function lattices
X
,
and in particular not for
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
.
A simple example of such a situation is when the metric space
\mathcal{P}\subset[0,1]

is the classical ternary Cantor set, equipped
with the

\tfrac{\log 2}{\log 3}
-dimensional
Hausdorff measure.
In this case, the function
\chi_{[0,a]\cap\mathcal{P}}\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})=L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})

is not quasicontinuous
when
a\in\mathcal{P}
is not an end point of any of the generating intervals.

The following result gives a more complete picture and
shows the striking difference
between
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
and
N^{1,p}(\mathcal{P})
with
1\leq p<\infty
.
See Theorem
6.2
for a more refined version
and Section
2
for the definitions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that there are

L^{\infty}
-almost no nonconstant rectifiable
curves in the metric space

\mathcal{P}
and that there is a point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{P}

with
\mu(\{x_{0}\})=0
.
Then
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})=L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
and
the following are true
:

1.

\chi_{\{x_{0}\}}\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is weakly quasicontinuous, but not
quasicontinuous.

2.

The capacity
{C_{\infty}^{\mathcal{P}}}
is not an outer capacity, not even for sets of zero capacity.

3.

If there is a closed set
F
with empty interior and
\mu(F)>0
,
then
\chi_{F}\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is not
weakly quasicontinuous.

In particular, all of these facts hold if
\mathcal{P}
is
the von Koch snowflake curve equipped with the

\frac{\log 4}{\log 3}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mu
.

To appreciate the statement of Theorem
1.1
,
let us turn back to
N^{1,p}(\mathcal{P})
, with
1\leq p<\infty
.
Unlike the classical Sobolev functions on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
the functions in
N^{1,p}(\mathcal{P})

are by definition better than arbitrary a.e.-representatives.
Namely, two
N^{1,p}
-functions which are equal a.e. are equal
up to a set of zero
p\mspace{1mu}
-capacity (that is, quasieverywhere (q.e.)),
because the other a.e.-representatives
do not possess
p\mspace{1mu}
-integrable upper gradients.

For metric spaces
\mathcal{P}
without (or with
L^{p}
-almost no)
rectifiable curves
and
1\leq p<\infty
,
the Newtonian space
N^{1,p}(\mathcal{P})
coincides with
L^{p}(\mathcal{P})
and
the
p\mspace{1mu}
-capacity coincides with the underlying measure on
\mathcal{P}
.
All
u\in N^{1,p}(\mathcal{P})
are thus quasicontinuous by virtue
of Luzin’s theorem.
We mention already here that in this context, one
crucial difference between
N^{1,p}
and
N^{1,\infty}
is that
L^{p}

has an absolutely continuous norm and
satisfies the
Vitali–Carathéodory property (VC, Definition
2.7
),
while
L^{\infty}
usually lacks these properties,
see Section
6
.

Clearly, without rectifiable curves,
{N^{1}X}(\mathcal{P})=X(\mathcal{P})

for every quasi-Banach function lattice
X
, but this is also the case
for the well-connected Sierpiński carpet
S_{1/3}
,
see Example
9.1
.
As a consequence, Theorem
1.1
applies also
to
\mathcal{P}=S_{1/3}
.

When
\mathcal{P}
is complete and
has enough rectifiable curves
(so that a
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality holds, and also assuming that

\mu
is doubling), it was shown in
Björn–Björn–Shanmugalingam
[
8
, Theorem 1.1 and p. 1198]

that all functions in
N^{1,p}(\mathcal{P})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, are quasicontinuous.
(This had been conjectured already in the late 1990s
by Shanmugalingam
[
27
, Corollary 3.1.7]
,
[
28
, Remark 4.4]
,
see also
[
8
, p. 1198]
.)
This result has later been generalized in various ways,
see
[
19
, Theorem 8.2.1]
,
[
7
, p. 1190]

and

[
6
, Theorem 9.1]
.
The assumption of Poincaré inequality was
(in complete doubling spaces and for
p>1
)
removed by Ambrosio et al. in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]

(cf.
[
6
, Remark 8.9]
), while
Eriksson-Bique–Poggi-Corradini
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]

recently
removed most of the earlier assumptions and only require
that
\mathcal{P}
is locally complete.
The assumption that
\mu(B)<\infty
for every ball
B
is however crucial,
as our Example
7.2
(b) shows.

In the world of (quasi)-Banach function lattices, the letter
X
is usually reserved
for the function lattice itself and corresponds in our setting to the
L^{p}

or
L^{\infty}
spaces,
which control the Sobolev functions and their gradients.
For the underlying metric space carrying the
Sobolev functions, we therefore use
\mathcal{P}
rather than
X
, which is
otherwise the usual notation in analysis on metric spaces
based on
L^{p}
as the function space.

Quasicontinuity is closely related to the outer property
of the Sobolev capacity
{C_{X}^{\mathcal{P}}}
associated with
{N^{1}X(\mathcal{P})}
.
We also study this property and how it
is related to quasicontinuity, as well as some variants
of these properties.
The following theorem summarizes quite well what can happen in the general case.
Here
{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}:=[-\infty,\infty]
.

X

(B).

{C_{X}^{\mathcal{P}}}

Definition 6.5
.

The (quasi)norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
is
absolutely continuous

(AC) if

\|u\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{X}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

whenever
u\in X
and

\{E_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence of measurable sets
with
\mu(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n})=0
.

Malý
[
24
, Proposition 2.1]
has shown that
(AC)+
(P4)
\mathchoice{\ \Longrightarrow\ }{\Rightarrow}{\Rightarrow}{\Rightarrow}
(VC), under our standing assumption
that balls have finite measure.

Example 6.6
.

Consider the compact space

\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}I_{n},\quad\text{where}\quad I_{n}=\begin{cases}[-1,0],&\text{if }n=0,\\
[2^{-1-2n},2^{-2n}],&\text{if }n\geq 1,\end{cases}

endowed with the Euclidean distance and
1
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
Let

X=L^{\infty}
.
Note that the Vitali–Carathéodory property (VC)
fails for
\chi_{\{0\}}
.

Each function in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is Lipschitz continuous within each interval
I_{n}
,
and thus continuous on
\mathcal{P}
, except possibly at
0
(from the right).
It follows that if
v\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
satisfies
v\geq 1
on a nonempty set
E\subset\mathcal{P}
,
then

\|v\|_{N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})}\geq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq 1\quad\text{and thus ${C_{\infty}}(E)\geq 1$.}

Since clearly
{C_{\infty}}(E)\leq\|1\|_{N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})}=1
, we see that
{C_{\infty}}(E)=1
.

Thus
{C_{\infty}}
is an outer capacity, i.e.
(B)
holds, and
any weakly quasicontinuous function must be continuous.
But
\chi_{I_{0}}\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is not continuous, and thus not
weakly quasicontinuous either, i.e.
(G)
fails.
Moreover,
\chi_{I_{0}}
does not have any weakly quasicontinuous representative.
Hence
(B)
\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow
(G)
and
thus also
(B)
\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow
(A)
.

Example 6.7
.

We are now going to give an example with the same properties
as Example
6.4
, but with a nonatomic measure.

Consider the compact space

\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}I_{n},\quad\text{where}\quad I_{n}=\begin{cases}\{0\},&\text{if }n=0,\\
[2^{-1-2n},2^{-2n}],&\text{if }n\geq 1,\end{cases}

endowed with the Euclidean distance and
1
-dimensional Lebesgue measure,
and with
X=L^{\infty}
.
Since there are no curves connecting the origin
0
to any other
point
x\in\mathcal{P}
, the constant zero function is an upper gradient of
\chi_{\{0\}}
.
Thus,
\chi_{\{0\}}\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
and
{C_{\infty}}(\{0\})=0
.
Obviously,
\chi_{\{0\}}|_{\mathcal{P}\setminus\{0\}}

is continuous and hence
\chi_{\{0\}}
is weakly quasicontinuous.

Each function
u\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is Lipschitz continuous within each interval
I_{n}
,
and thus continuous on
\mathcal{P}
, except possibly at
0
.
Hence
u
is weakly quasicontinuous, i.e.
(G)
holds.
It also follows, as in Example
6.6
,
that

{C_{\infty}}(E)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $E=\varnothing$ or $E=\{0\}$},\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence,
{C_{\infty}}
is
not an outer capacity for sets of zero capacity, i.e.
(E)
fails.

Moreover, every quasicontinuous function is continuous, by
Proposition
6.1
.
On the other hand, if
E=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}I_{2k}
,
then
\chi_{E}\in{N^{1}X}
does not have a continuous representative, and thus
not a quasicontinuous representative, i.e.
(F)
fails.
In particular,
(G)
\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow
(E)

and
(G)
\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow
(F)
.
The Vitali–Carathéodory property (VC)
again fails for
\chi_{\{0\}}
.

Example 6.8
.

(Damped topologist’s sine curve and
N^{1,\infty}
)
This is yet another example
with similar properties to Examples
6.4
and
6.7
,
but with a nonatomic measure and a pathconnected space
\mathcal{P}
.
Let

\mathcal{P}=\biggl\{\biggl(t,t\sin\frac{1}{t}\biggr):0<t\leq 1\biggr\}\cup\{(0,0)\},

equipped with the Euclidean metric.
Then
\mathcal{P}
is a compact pathconnected subset of
\mathbf{R}^{2}
,
but it is not rectifiably connected.

Let
\mu(E)=\mathcal{L}^{1}(\pi(E))
, where

\pi(t,y)=t
is the
orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{P}
onto
[0,1]

and
\mathcal{L}^{1}
is the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
.
Also let
X=L^{\infty}
.

Since
\mathcal{P}
has infinite length,
there are no (nonconstant)
rectifiable curves in
\mathcal{P}
through
0:=(0,0)
.
It thus follows that
0
is an
upper gradient of
\chi_{\{0\}}

and that
\|\chi_{\{0\}}\|_{N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})}=0
.
Hence
{C_{\infty}}(\{0\})=0
.

On the other hand, if
v\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
then

v
is locally Lipschitz on
\mathcal{P}\setminus\{0\}
.
Thus all functions in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
are weakly quasicontinuous,
i.e.
(G)
holds.
We also conclude that
{C_{\infty}}(\{x\})=1
if
x\neq 0
, and
hence
{C_{\infty}}(G)=1
for every nonempty open set
G
.
Thus
{C_{\infty}}
is not an outer capacity
for sets of zero capacity, i.e.
(E)
fails.
In particular,
(G)
\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow
(E)
.

Next, define

h(x)=\dist(w(x),\mathbf{Z}),\quad\text{where}\quad w(x)=\begin{cases}l_{x},&\text{if }x\neq 0,\\
0,&\text{if }x=0,\end{cases}

and

l_{x}
is the length of the geodesic (in
\mathcal{P}
) between

x
and
(1,\sin 1)
.
Then
1
is a
minimal
L^{\infty}
-weak
upper gradient of
h
and

h\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
.
If
\tilde{h}=h
q.e., then
\tilde{h}=h
on
\mathcal{P}\setminus\{0\}

and since

0=\liminf_{x\to 0}h(x)<\limsup_{x\to 0}h(x)=\tfrac{1}{2},

(6.2)

it follows that
\tilde{h}
is not
continuous, and thus not quasicontinuous,
by Proposition
6.1
.
Hence

(F)
fails and
(G)
\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow
(F)
.

Moreover, if
f\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is continuous, then

\|f-h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}\geq\tfrac{1}{4}

due to (
6.2
).
Hence continuous functions are not dense in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
.

.

Let

Example 7.1

\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow

.

Example 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}I_{n}
be as in Example
6.7
.
Recall that
\mathcal{P}
is compact.

(a)
Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
X
be given by the norm

\|u\|^{p}_{X}=\int_{\mathcal{P}}|u(x)|^{p}\,\frac{dx}{x}.

Here,
\chi_{\mathcal{P}}\notin X
and thus
(P4)
fails,
so
X
is not a Banach function space, only
a Banach function lattice.
Moreover,
\chi_{\{0\}}\in X
shows that
the Vitali–Carathéodory property (VC) fails.

As in Example
6.7
, it follows that
\chi_{\{0\}}\in X
and
{C_{X}}(\{0\})=0
.
Moreover,
{C_{X}}(G)=\infty
for every
open neighbourhood
G
of
0
as
\chi_{G}\notin X
.
Consequently,

{C_{X}}
is not an outer capacity
for sets of zero capacity, i.e.
(E)
fails.

Let
u\in{N^{1}X}
with an upper gradient
g\in X
.
Since for every
[y,z]\subset I_{n}
with
n=1,2,\dots
,

|u(y)-u(z)|\leq\int_{y}^{z}g\,ds=\int_{y}^{z}\frac{g(x)}{x^{1/p}}x^{1/p}\,dx\leq 2^{-2n}\biggl(\int_{y}^{z}g(x)^{p}\,\frac{dx}{x}\biggr)^{1/p}

(7.1)

and the last integral tends to zero as
y\to z
,
we conclude that

u

is continuous within each interval
I_{n}
,
and thus continuous on
\mathcal{P}
, except possibly at
0
.

Next we shall show that

\lim_{x\to 0+}u(x)=0.

(7.2)

Note first that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{I_{n}}|u|=0
.
Assume that
\limsup_{x\to 0+}|u(x)|>a>0
.
Then there are infinitely many
n
such that
\osc_{I_{n}}|u|>a
.
For such
n
, by Hölder’s inequality and since
g
is an upper gradient, we
get as in
(
7.1
) that

a<\int_{I_{n}}g\,dx\leq 2^{-2n}\biggl(\int_{I_{n}}g^{p}\,\frac{dx}{x}\biggr)^{1/p}\leq 2^{-2n}\|g\|_{X},

from which it follows that
g\notin X
, a contradiction.
Hence
we have shown (
7.2
).
It then follows
that
\tilde{u}=u\chi_{\mathcal{P}\setminus\{0\}}
is continuous on
\mathcal{P}
and

\tilde{u}=u
q.e., i.e.
(F)
holds and
(F)
\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow
(E)
.
Moreover, since

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|u-u_{k}\|_{{N^{1}X}}=0,\quad\text{where }u_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}u(x),&\text{if }x\in\bigcup_{n=1}^{k}I_{n},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

and the functions
u_{k}

are continuous, we see that
continuous functions are dense in
{N^{1}X}
.

(b)
If we consider
X=L^{p}(\mathcal{P},\mu)
, where

, then
we obtain the exact same space as in (a), but this time as a standard

for every ball

.
This shows that the assumption that balls have finite measure is essential for at least

For this interpretation,
(P4)
holds
and (VC) fails.

(c)
Yet another (non-

) modification of this example is

be given by the (maximal function) norm

where
\mathcal{L}^{1}
is
the Lebesgue measure.
Again,
\chi_{\{0\}}\in{N^{1}X}
and
{C_{X}}(\{0\})=0
.

Now, let

be an open set containing

.
Then

,
and thus
{C_{X}}
is not an outer capacity for sets of zero capacity,

(E)
fails.

As

the first inequality in (

)
that each

,

.

is weakly quasicontinuous,

(G)
holds and

\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow

.

Finally, let

, where

.
Then

and

does not have any quasicontinuous representative.
Hence
(F)
fails and

(G)
\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow
(F)
.
The function

.

Example 7.3
.

For each

, let

\mathcal{P}^{-}_{m}=\biggl[-\frac{1}{m},0\biggr]\times\{m\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{P}^{+}_{m}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}[2^{-1-2n},2^{-2n}]\times\{m\}

,

For any

\textstyle\kern 0.0pt\not\kern 0.0pt\Rightarrow

fails,

.

8
The variable exponent
case
X=L^{p(\cdot)}

We shall now modify Example
6.8
to provide non-quasicontinuous
Newtonian functions based on the variable exponent space
L^{p(\cdot)}
.
Several of the other examples can also be modified similarly, but we
restrict ourselves to this example to illustrate the main ideas.

Recall that the Luxemburg norm on
L^{p(\cdot)}(E)
,
where
E
and
p:E\to[1,\infty]
are measurable,
is given by

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}=\inf\biggl\{\lambda>0:\int_{E}\biggl(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\biggr)^{p(x)}\,d\mu(x)\leq 1\biggr\}

and that
L^{p(\cdot)}(E)=\{f:\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}<\infty\}
.
When
p(x)=\infty
, we use the convention that

a^{\infty}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }0\leq a<1,\\
1,&\text{if }a=1,\\
\infty,&\text{if }a>1.\end{cases}

Our examples will use the following characterization.

Proposition 8.1
.

Assume that

p:\mathcal{P}\to[1,\infty]
is measurable.
Let
E\subset\mathcal{P}
be a measurable set with
\mu(E)<\infty
and
let

E_{n}=\{x\in E:p(x)>n\}.

Then,

e^{p(\cdot)}\in\bigcap_{q\in[1,\infty)}L^{q}(E)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\to 0\ \ \text{as }n\to\infty.

Moreover, if
\|e^{p(\cdot)}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(E)}=\infty
for some
q_{0}\in[1,\infty)
,
then
\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\geq e^{-q_{0}}
for every
n=1,2,\dots
.

Suppose that
e^{p(\cdot)}\in L^{q}(E)
for every
q\in[1,\infty)
. Then,

p(\cdot)<\infty
a.e. in
E
, which implies that
\mu(E_{n})\to 0
as

n\to\infty
, since
\mu(E)<\infty
.
Given an arbitrary
\lambda\in(0,1/e)
, let

q_{\lambda}=|{\log\lambda}|\in(1,\infty)
.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\int_{E}\biggl(\frac{\chi_{E_{n}}}{\lambda}\biggr)^{p(x)}\,d\mu(x)=\int_{E}\bigl(e^{p(x)}\bigr)^{q_{\lambda}}\chi_{E_{n}}\,d\mu(x)\leq\|e^{p(\cdot)}\|_{L^{2q_{\lambda}}(E)}^{q_{\lambda}}\mu(E_{n})^{1/2}.

In particular, if
\mu(E_{n})\leq 1/\|e^{p(\cdot)}\|_{L^{2q_{\lambda}}(E)}^{2q_{\lambda}}
,
then this implies that
\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\leq\lambda
.
Hence,

0\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\leq\lambda.

Passing to the limit
\lambda\to 0
proves that
\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

For the converse, we need to distinguish two cases based on the measure of
the set

E_{\infty}\mathrel{:=}\{x\in E:p(x)=\infty\}
. If
\mu(E_{\infty})>0
, then

\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\geq\|\chi_{E_{\infty}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E_{\infty})}=\|\chi_{E_{\infty}}\|_{L^{\infty}(E_{\infty})}=1.

Suppose now that
\mu(E_{\infty})=0
and hence
\mu(E_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
,
since
\mu(E)<\infty
.
Assume that
q_{0}\in(1,\infty)
is such that
e^{p(\cdot)}\notin L^{q_{0}}(E)
.
Then for all
n=1,2,\dots
,

\int_{E}\biggl(\frac{\chi_{E_{n}}}{e^{-q_{0}}}\biggr)^{p(x)}\,d\mu(x)\geq\int_{E}e^{q_{0}p(x)}\,d\mu(x)-e^{q_{0}n}\mu(E\setminus E_{n})=\infty.

Therefore,
\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\geq e^{-q_{0}}
and
passing to the limit yields immediately that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(E)}\geq e^{-q_{0}}>0.\qed

Example 8.2
.

(Damped topologist’s sine curve and
N^{1,p(\cdot)}
)
Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\mu
be as in Example
6.8
.
Let
p:\mathcal{P}\to[1,\infty]

be a measurable variable exponent function that is finite a.e.
Suppose that there is

q_{0}\in(1,\infty)
such that

\|e^{p(\cdot)}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathcal{P})}=\infty
, but

\|e^{p(\cdot)}\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{P}\setminus G)}<\infty\quad\text{for every }q\geq q_{0}\text{ and every open neighbourhood $G$ of $0$}.

For instance, one may consider
p(x)=f(t)
, with
x=(t,\sin(1/t))

and
1\leq p((0,0))\leq\infty
,
where
f\>(0,1]\to[1,\infty)
is locally bounded and
there is
t_{0}>0
such that

f(t)>\frac{|{\log t}|}{q_{0}}\quad\text{for all $t\leq t_{0}$}.

Indeed,
for
q\geq q_{0}
,

\int_{\mathcal{P}}e^{p(x)q}\,d\mu(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}^{1}(\{t:f(t)>q^{-1}\log z\})\,dz\geq\int_{1/t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{dz}{z}=\infty.

Moreover, for any open neighbourhood
G
of
0
,
we have

\int_{\mathcal{P}\setminus G}e^{p(x)q}\,d\mu(x)\leq e^{p_{G}q}<\infty,\quad\text{where }p_{G}:=\sup_{\mathcal{P}\setminus G}p<\infty.

Since there is no (nonconstant) rectifiable curve in
\mathcal{P}
going through
0
,
the function
g(x)=0
is an upper gradient of
u=\chi_{\{0\}}
. Therefore,

u\in N^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})
with
\|u\|_{N^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})}=0
. It also
follows that
C_{p(\cdot)}(\{0\})=0
.

Let
G\subset\mathcal{P}
be an open set containing

0
and let
v\in N^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})

satisfy
v\geq 1
on
G
. Then,

\|v\|_{N^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})}\geq\|v\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})}\geq\|\chi_{G}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})}\geq e^{-q_{0}},

(8.1)

by the assumption
\|e^{p(\cdot)}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathcal{P})}=\infty

and Proposition
8.1
.
Thus,
C_{p(\cdot)}(G)\geq e^{-q_{0}}
.
In particular,

C_{p(\cdot)}(\{0\})=0<e^{-q_{0}}\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\supset\{0\}\\
G\text{ open}\end{subarray}}C_{p(\cdot)}(G),

which implies that
C_{p(\cdot)}
is not an outer capacity, not even for
zero-capacity sets, i.e.
(E)
fails.
It also follows from (
8.1
) that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}

is neither absolutely continuous (AC) nor satisfies
the Vitali–Carathéodory property (VC).

Arguing similarly as in Example
7.2
(a)
shows that
u|_{\mathcal{P}\setminus\{0\}}
is continuous
for every
u\in N^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})
.
Hence all functions in
N^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})
are weakly quasicontinuous,
i.e.
(G)
holds.
On the other hand, the function
h\in N^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathcal{P})
from
Example
6.8

has no quasicontinuous representative here either, i.e.
(F)
fails.

9
The Sierpiński carpet

The damped topologist’s sine curve and some of the disconnected
examples were based on the fact that there are no nonconstant rectifiable
curves passing through the origin.
The following example is well connected, even 2-quasiconvex, and yet

(A)
–
(G)

in Theorem
1.2
fail.
In particular, there is a function in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
which is not even weakly
quasicontinuous.

Example 9.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subset[0,1]^{2}
be the Sierpiński carpet

S_{1/3}
,
equipped with the

\tfrac{\log 8}{\log 3}
-dimensional
Hausdorff measure
\mu
,
normalized so that
\mu(S_{1/3})=1
.
Recall that the Sierpiński carpet

S_{1/3}

is a selfsimilar fractal constructed by
dividing the square
[0,1]^{2}
into nine squares with side length
\tfrac{1}{3}
and
removing the open middle square,
and then repeating this process in each of the remaining eight squares,
in a similar way as when constructing the
Cantor ternary set.

We are going to show that, for every quasi-Banach function lattice
X
,
there are
X
-almost no nonconstant rectifiable curves in
\mathcal{P}
,
and therefore
{N^{1}X}(\mathcal{P})=X(\mathcal{P})
.
This is well known for
X=L^{p}
, but may not have been
observed for general quasi-Banach function lattices.

Let
\pi_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{P}

onto the interval
[0,1]
.
It follows from the proof of Proposition 4.5 in Bourdon–Pajot
[
9
]

(see also Corollary 4.15 in
Durand-Cartagena–Jaramillo–Shanmugalingam
[
11
]
)
that the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{1}
on
[0,1]
is
singular with respect to the pushforward
\mu_{\sharp}
of
\mu
,
defined by

\mu_{\sharp}(A)=\mu(\pi_{1}^{-1}(A))\quad\text{for }A\subset[0,1].

Indeed, by the Radon–Nikodym theorem,

\mu_{\sharp}=h\mathcal{L}^{1}+\sigma,\quad\text{where }h\in L^{1}(0,1)\text{ and }\sigma\perp\mathcal{L}^{1}.

Now, if
(a_{1}a_{2}\dots)_{3}
with
a_{j}\in\{0,1,2\}
is a ternary expansion
of
x\in[0,1]
and
N_{n}(x)
is the number of
1
’s among
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
,

n=1,2,\dots
, then it is not difficult to verify using the construction
of
S_{1/3}

that for a.e.
x\in[0,1]
,

h(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\displaystyle\bigl(\tfrac{1}{4}\bigr)^{N_{n}(x)}\bigl(\tfrac{3}{8}\bigr)^{n-N_{n}(x)}}{\displaystyle\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr)^{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigl(\frac{2}{3}\Bigr)^{N_{n}(x)}\Bigl(\frac{9}{8}\Bigr)^{n}.

Thus, for a.e.
x\in[0,1]
,

h(x)=0\quad\text{if }\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{N_{n}(x)}{n}>\theta_{0}:=\frac{\log\tfrac{9}{8}}{\log\tfrac{3}{2}}=0.29\dots<\frac{1}{3}.

Now,
N_{n}(x)
can be written as the sum

\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\bf 1}_{\{a_{j}=1\}}

of independent identically distributed random variables on the probability space

[0,1]
with the Lebesgue measure and the Borel
\sigma
-algebra.
Since the expectations

{\bf E}({\bf 1}_{\{a_{j}=1\}})=\tfrac{1}{3},

the strong law of large numbers immediately gives that

\mathcal{L}^{1}\biggl(\biggl\{x\in[0,1]:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{N_{n}(x)}{n}=\frac{1}{3}\biggr\}\biggr)=1.

It therefore follows that
h=0
a.e. in
[0,1]
.
Hence,
\mu_{\sharp}=\sigma
is
singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{1}
.
Moreover,
\spt\mu_{\sharp}=[0,1]
and
\mu_{\sharp}
is nonatomic.
Thus, for each (nondegenerate) interval
[a,b]\subset[0,1]
,
there is a Borel set
N_{a,b}\subset[a,b]

such that

\mathcal{L}^{1}(N_{a,b})>0
and
\mu_{\sharp}(N_{a,b})=0
.

Next, let
\Gamma
be the collection
of all nonconstant rectifiable curves
\gamma:[0,l_{\gamma}]\to\mathcal{P}
,
and let

\Gamma_{\vertical}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\pi_{1}(\gamma)\text{ is a singleton}\}

be the collection of the “vertical” curves.
Let
\pi_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{2}
be the orthogonal projection on the
y
-axis,

N=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbf{Q}\\
0\leq a<b\leq 1\end{subarray}}N_{a,b}\quad\text{and}\quad E=\pi_{1}^{-1}(N)\cup\pi_{2}^{-1}(N).

Then

\rho:=\infty\chi_{E}=0

\mu
-a.e. and thus

\rho\in X(\mathcal{P},\mu)
for every quasi-Banach function lattice
X
, by (P1).
Moreover,

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds\geq\int_{\pi_{1}(\gamma)}\infty\chi_{N}(t)\,dt=\infty\quad\text{if }\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{\vertical},

while for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\vertical}

the same estimate holds with
\pi_{2}(\gamma)

instead of
\pi_{1}(\gamma)
.

Hence there are
X
-almost no nonconstant rectifiable curves in
\mathcal{P}
.
Therefore

{N^{1}X}(\mathcal{P})=X(\mathcal{P})\quad\text{and}\quad\|\cdot\|_{{N^{1}X}(\mathcal{P})}=\|\cdot\|_{X(\mathcal{P})}.

In particular, for
X=L^{\infty}
, it follows from
the last part of
Theorem
6.2

that
(E)
fails.

Moreover, since
\mu(\mathcal{P}\setminus E)=\mu(\mathcal{P})>0

and
\mu
is Borel regular, there is a compact
set
K\subset\mathcal{P}\setminus E
with
\mu(K)>0
, see
e.g. Rudin
[
26
, Theorem 2.18]
.
As
E
is dense in
\mathcal{P}
, the compact set
K
has empty interior and hence

v=\chi_{K}
is not weakly quasicontinuous, by Theorem
6.2
(III)
.

Efficacy and Safety of the First Adalimumab Biosimilar in Chinese Children with Severe Plaque Psoriasis: The PISCES Study, a Multicenter Prospective Study
Authors: Yirong Huang, Ting Pan, Xin Shi, Guoqiang Zhang, Fang Cheng, Songmei Geng, Heng Gu, Shanshan Li, Qingchun Diao, Xiaohua Wang, Yonghong Lu, Chao Ji, Yuzhen Li, Yuling Shi, Jun Gu, Lingling Chen

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAdalimumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting tumor necrosis factor-alpha, has been approved for the treatment of severe plaque psoriasis in children aged 4 years and older. Data on its use in the clinical management of pediatric psoriasis in China remains limited. This study is the first prospective analysis of the adalimumab biosimilar (GELELI®, the first biosimilar of adalimumab approved in China, and QLETLI® in the UK) safety and effectiveness for severe pediatric plaque psoriasis in China.MethodsWeight-based dosage was administered to 80 patients (ages 4–18 years) from multiple centers. Primary endpoints included the proportions of patients achieving PASI75 and PGA 0/1 at week 16.ResultsThe mean psoriasis area and severity index (PASI) score decreased from 15.31 ± 8.375 at baseline to 6.29 ± 4.208 at week 4. Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Psoriasis affects over 500,000 children in China, but treatment options for pediatric patients remain limited.Adalimumab has been approved for pediatric psoriasis in Europe and China, yet its efficacy and safety profile in Chinese children has not been prospectively evaluated. What did the study ask? This prospective, multicenter study assessed the efficacy and safety of the first adalimumab biosimilar in Chinese children aged 4–18 years with severe plaque psoriasis. What was learned from the study? At week 16, 81.0% of patients achieved PASI 75 and 68.4% achieved PGA 0/1, with significant improvements observed as early as week 4 and sustained through to week 24; no serious adverse events were reported.These findings confirm that the adalimumab biosimilar is effective and well-tolerated in Chinese pediatric patients, filling the evidence gap.

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic, immune-mediated inflammatory skin disease, with a global prevalence of 0.7%–1.2% in individuals under 18 years [1]. A multi-province incomplete survey in China reported an overall psoriasis prevalence of 0.47% and a pediatric rate of approximately 0.18% [2], corresponding to over 500,000 affected children. Increasing environmental exposures and improved diagnostic awareness may contribute to rising incidence [3]. Plaque psoriasis is the most common subtype in children (70% of cases) [4], presenting as localized or widespread plaques with silvery-white thin scales. Pediatric psoriasis is associated with elevated risks of obesity, hypertension, diabetes, psychiatric comorbidities, rheumatoid arthritis, and inflammatory bowel disease [5–8]. Moderate-to-severe psoriasis profoundly impairs physical and psychological wellbeing and imposes a substantial economic burden on families.

Treatment options remain limited for pediatric patients due to a lack of high-quality research data specific to this population. Phototherapy (e.g., narrowband UVB) is suitable for mild, localized disease and systemic plaque psoriasis. Systemic agents such as methotrexate and retinoids have yet to be approved for clinical use for pediatric psoriasis. The tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) inhibitor etanercept was approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for children ≥ 4 years with severe plaque psoriasis, demonstrating favorable efficacy and a reliable safety profile in randomized controlled trials [9].

Adalimumab has been approved in Europe and China since 2015 for severe pediatric plaque psoriasis (≥ 4 years). It is a fully human recombinant immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody that binds specifically to TNF-α. It suppresses the inflammatory cascade by blocking TNF-α interaction with type I (p55/60) and type II (p75/80) receptors [10], downregulates other proinflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, IL-8) [11], and induces complement-mediated lysis of TNF-expressing cells. These mechanisms collectively suppress keratinocyte proliferation and reduce Th17 differentiation, effectively alleviating psoriatic lesions. There is currently little high-quality research on adalimumab in Chinese pediatric patients. This study is the first prospective analysis of the safety and effectiveness of adalimumab biosimilar for severe pediatric plaque psoriasis in China, aiming to fill this important data vacuum.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

The PISCES study was a prospective, multicenter, single-arm trial conducted across nine dermatology centers in China. It evaluated the safety and effectiveness of the adalimumab biosimilar (GELELI® in China, QLETLI® outside of China) in children and adolescents with severe plaque psoriasis. The inclusion criteria were age ≥ 4 and ≤ 8 years, body weight ≥15 kg, a verified diagnosis of psoriasis for ≥ 6 months, having an inadequate response to topical therapy and phototherapy, and meeting at least one of the following criteria: psoriasis body surface area (BSA) > 10%, psoriasis area and severity index (PASI) > 12, psoriasis physician’s global assessment (PGA) score ≥ 4, or children’s dermatology life quality index (CDLQI) score > 10. Key exclusion criteria included: severe hypersensitivity to monoclonal antibodies; concurrent latent or active infection (e.g., tuberculosis, hepatitis B, or syphilis); severe systemic diseases; and use of anti-TNF or other biologics within the previous 12 weeks, topical corticosteroids or phototherapy within the past week, and nonbiologic systemic therapy within the past 2 weeks. Patients received subcutaneous injections of the same dose every 2 weeks (20 mg for 15–30 kg, 40 mg for ≥ 30 kg), starting 1 week after the initial dose and continuing until week 23 (13 doses in total). Key assessments were performed at weeks 4, 12, 16, and 24. The study was conducted in accordance with good clinical practice (GCP) and the Declaration of Helsinki. The protocol was approved by the ethics committee at each participating center, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants and their guardians. All the scores involved in the experiment, such as BSA, PASI, PGA, and CDLQI, have obtained the necessary usage permissions.

Assessments

Demographic characteristics, medical history, comorbidities, and previous psoriasis treatments were recorded at baseline. Efficacy and safety assessments—including PASI, BSA, PGA, and CDLQI scores—were collected at baseline and weeks 4 (± 3 days), 12 (± 7 days), 16 (± 7 days), and 24 (± 7 days) after the first adalimumab dose. All adverse events (AEs) were reported by patients or guardians and documented irrespective of causal relationship to adalimumab. Investigators assessed severity and relatedness, grading AEs according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. The outcomes of AEs and any temporary or permanent treatment discontinuation due to AEs were recorded.

Endpoints

The primary efficacy endpoints included the proportion of patients achieving a ≥ 75% reduction from baseline in PASI (PASI 75) and PGA of complete or almost complete clearance of skin lesions (PGA0/1) at week 16. Secondary endpoints included the PASI, BSA, PGA, and CDLQI scores at each follow-up visit, along with the proportion of patients achieving ≥ 50%, ≥ 75% (except week 16), ≥ 90%, and 100% reductions from baseline in PASI (PASI 50, PASI 75, PASI 90, and PASI 100, respectively) and PGA 0/1 (except week 16) at weeks 4, 12, 16, and 24, in conjunction with the change in CDLQI score from baseline. Safety endpoints included AEs, serious adverse events (SAEs), and adverse drug reactions (ADRs) recorded during the study period.

Statistical Methods

Efficacy analyses were based on the full analysis set (FAS), comprising all eligible subjects who met the inclusion criteria and underwent at least one efficacy evaluation. Safety analyses utilized the safety set (SS), which included all subjects who received ≥ 1 dose of adalimumab and completed ≥1 safety evaluation.

Descriptive statistics summarized all variables. Quantitative data (e.g., age, PASI score) are presented as the number of cases, mean, and standard deviation; qualitative data (e.g., sex, response rates) are presented as counts and percentages, with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) calculated using the Clopper-Pearson method. Safety endpoints, including AEs, SAEs, and ADRs, were summarized by system organ class and preferred term.

All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4. Two-sided tests were employed with a significance level of α = 0.05. For missing primary or secondary efficacy data, the last observation carried forward (LOCF) method was applied. Subjects withdrawn for nonresponse (defined as failing to achieve PASI 50 at week 12 or 16) were designated as nonresponders for all subsequent visits. Percentage calculations were based on nonmissing values, and records identified as outliers were treated as missing data.

For longitudinal comparisons of continuous efficacy outcomes between baseline and each post-baseline visit, paired t-tests were used. Given the exploratory nature and the prespecified primary endpoint at week 16, no adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied. However, nominal p-values are reported for transparency, and effect sizes (Cohen’s d) are provided to quantify the magnitude of observed differences. Exact binomial tests with Clopper–Pearson 95% confidence intervals were used for responder rate analyses. Detailed paired t-test statistics for all comparisons are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Results

Between March 2023 and May 2025, 97 patients were screened at nine centers in China; 80 were enrolled and received adalimumab. One patient was excluded from the FAS due to a major protocol deviation: this patient did not meet the inclusion criterion of “inadequate response to topical therapy and phototherapy” but was erroneously enrolled. As this patient was subjected to the study intervention and were retained in the safety set (SS). The SS included all 80 patients, while the full analysis set comprised 79 patients. Two patients in the FAS withdrew early (weeks 15 and 17) for personal reasons. Missing data after withdrawal were imputed using the LOCF. At baseline, PASI > 12 was recorded in 47 patients, BSA > 10% in 50, PGA ≥ 4 in 30, and CDLQI > 10 in 66. Sixty patients (75.9%) met at least one objective criterion of skin lesion severity (BSA > 10%, PASI > 12, or PGA ≥ 4). Baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1. The mean age was 12.2 years, 45.6% were male, and the mean BMI was 21.18 kg/m2. A total of 62.0% of patients had cohabitants who smoked, and 30.4% had a family history of psoriasis. Seven patients had a history of allergy, including two drug allergies (e.g., to cephalosporins and penicillin) and five other allergies (e.g., to food and dust). Comorbidities were present in 40 patients, including heart diseases (12.7%), metabolic and nutritional disorders (7.6%), etc. The mean psoriasis duration was 30 months. Nearly all patients (96.2%) had received psoriasis-related pharmacotherapy. A total of 83.5% had undergone topical treatment (corticosteroids, 53.2%; vitamin D3 analogs, 62.0%; calcineurin inhibitors, 20.3%), and 38.0% had received systemic therapy (Chinese patent medicine, 34.2%; systemic retinoids, 6.3%; cyclosporine, 1.3%; methotrexate, 1.3%). Two patients had a history of biologic agent use (etanercept and ustekinumab). The previous history of nonpharmacological treatments for psoriasis included phototherapy and other treatments (such as acupuncture, medicinal baths, etc.). A total of 7.6% of patients had received UVB phototherapy, 308-nm excimer light/laser, or UVA phototherapy.

Efficacy was assessed based on the full analysis set (n = 79). Mean baseline disease severity scores were as follows: PASI, 15.31 ± 8.375; BSA, 18.74% ± 15.604%; PGA, 3.3 ± 0.81; and CDLQI, 14.6 ± 5.71. After treatment initiation, a marked reduction in PASI score was observed from week 4 (6.29 ± 4.208). The score further decreased to 2.38 ± 2.410 and 1.84 ± 1.742 at weeks 12 and 16, and remained stable at week 24 (1.47 ± 1.593) (Fig. 1). Paired t-tests revealed that the reductions from baseline at all post-baseline time points were statistically significant. All nominal p < 0.001, with large effect sizes for which Cohen’s d ranges from 1.53 to 1.66. Full paired-test statistics including t-values and 95% CIs are detailed in Supplementary Table S1. Clinical improvement was supported by the proportions of patients achieving PASI 50, 75, 90, and 100 responses at various time points. At week 4, more than half of the patients (54.4%, 95% CI 42.8, 65.7) achieved PASI 50. Response rates increased substantially to 91.1% (95% CI 82.6–96.4) and 98.7% (95% CI 93.1–100.0), respectively, at weeks 12 and 16, remaining high at week 24 (Fig. 1).

The PASI 75 response rate, serving as a primary efficacy endpoint, demonstrated a consistent upward trend: 25.3% (95% CI 16.2–36.4) at week 4, and 68.4% (95% CI [56.9–78.4) at week 12. The study showed a significant rise to 81.0% (95% CI 70.6–89.0) at the primary endpoint of week 16, followed by a steady plateau at 86.1% (95% CI 76.5–92.8) by week 24 (Fig. 1).

The response rate of PASI 90 was 10.1% (95% CI 4.5–19.0) at week 4, increased to 49.4% (95% CI 37.9–60.9) by week 12, and was maintained at 62.0% (95% CI 50.4–72.7) at week 24 (Fig. 1). The PASI 100 response rate was relatively low but exhibited a gradual increase, from 1.3% (95% CI 0–6.9) at week 4 to 29.1% (95% CI 19.4–40.4) by week 24 (Fig. 1).

PGA scores decreased in parallel with PASI (Fig. 2). The paired t-test showed that significant improvements were observed at each visit point. (Supplementary Table S1). The PGA 0/1 response rate, serving as an additional primary scoring endpoint, was 32.9% (95% CI 22.7–44.4) at week 4, increased to 67.1% (95% CI 55.6–77.3) at week 12, reached 68.4% (95% CI 56.9–78.4) at week 16, and was maintained at 79.7% (95% CI 69.2–88.0) at week 24 (Fig. 2).

Safety

A total of 292 AEs were reported in 66 subjects (66/80, 82.5%) (Table 2). Among these, 200 AEs were grade 1 and occurred in 54 subjects (54/80, 67.5%), 91 were grade 2 (43/80, 53.8%), and 1 was grade 3 (1/80, 1.3%; an upper respiratory tract infection). This event was observed on day 4 of the patient’s study participation, 3 days after the first dose, and lasted for 3 days. The investigator determined that the event was unlikely to be related to the study drug. The event did not affect the dosage or dosing frequency of the study drug, nor did it lead to treatment interruption. ADRs, defined as AEs assessed as possibly, probably, or definitely related to treatment, occurred in 50 subjects (62.5%) and included 234 events, all grade 1–2. No serious adverse events (SAEs) or serious adverse drug reactions (SADRs) were reported.

Adverse Events in the Trial

The most common ADRs were infection disorders (21/80, 26.3%), primarily upper respiratory tract infection (10%), urinary tract infection (6.3%), bronchitis (3.8%), and pharyngitis (2.5%). Nonspecific or injection site reactions occurred in 12 subjects (15%), including injection site swelling (6.3%), pruritus (6.3%), red rash (5.0%), and pyrexia (3.8%). Nutritional and metabolic disorders were reported in eight subjects (10%), including hypertriglyceridemia (2.5%) and hyperuricemia (2.5%). A total of seven (8.8%) and seven (8.8%) subjects had skin and subcutaneous tissue disorders and cardiovascular disorders, respectively. Treatment was temporarily suspended in 11 subjects (13.8%) due to AEs; all resumed therapy after resolution. No permanent discontinuations occurred. By the study end, 258 AEs (65/80, 81.3%) had resolved, with no mortalities or sequelae reported.

Discussion

This study represents the first prospective investigation to evaluate the efficacy and safety of adalimumab biosimilar in children with severe plaque psoriasis across multiple regions in China, including North China, East China, Northwest China, etc. The established efficacy of adalimumab in this population was previously demonstrated in a phase III trial, which reported superior PASI 75 response rates at week 16 for adalimumab (0.4 mg/kg and 0.8 mg/kg) compared with methotrexate [12]. Supporting its long-term utility, a French registry study noted higher 2-year treatment persistence for adalimumab compared with etanercept in pediatric psoriasis [13]. Regarding safety, a cohort study indicated a lower incidence of adverse events with TNF-α inhibitors (38.7%) than with methotrexate (48.1%), alongside a relatively lower risk of adalimumab treatment discontinuation [14].

This study verified adalimumab’s efficacy by improving assessment scores across multiple categories. In this analysis, treatment benefits were observed as early as week 4. The PASI 50 response rate at week 4 was 54.4%, numerically higher than the rate in the 0.4 mg/kg group (41.0%) and was similar to the 0.8 mg/kg group (52.6%) from the phase III trial [12]. At the primary endpoint (week 16), 81.0% attained PASI 75 and 68.4% achieved PGA 0/1, rates numerically higher than those in the phase III trial (0.4 mg/kg: 43.6% and 41.0%; 0.8 mg/kg: 57.9% and 60.5%, respectively). At week 12, PASI 90 and PASI 100 responses here were numerically higher than those reported for the 0.8 mg/kg group (28.9% and 18.4%). The CDLQI score improved by 10.7 points from baseline at week 12, indicating a more pronounced quality-of-life benefit than the 6.6-point reduction observed in the phase III study. However, these descriptive comparisons must be interpreted with caution because of substantial differences in study design, enrollment criteria, and patient demographics between the two trials.

In this trial, the week 12 PASI 90 response rate in our study (49.4%) was numerically similar to that of the half-standard dose ustekinumab group (54.1%) [15] and higher than that for etanercept (27%) [9], though lower than those for low- and high-dose secukinumab (72.5% and 67.5%, respectively) [16]. The week 12 PGA 0/1 response rate (67.1%) also appeared numerically comparable to those of ustekinumab (67.6% and 69.4%) and was higher than those for high-dose secukinumab (60%) and etanercept (53%). The trend of CDLQI improvement was broadly consistent with those described in trials of ustekinumab, secukinumab, and etanercept [9, 15, 16]. Given the single-arm design of our study, efficacy comparisons with other biologics are indirect. These exploratory comparisons do not establish definitive superiority, noninferiority, or equivalence of adalimumab relative to other biologics.

Regarding safety, adalimumab was well-tolerated in the pediatric population with no novel safety concerns identified, a profile consistent with other TNF-α inhibitors and biologics. The safety profile was generally consistent with that reported in the randomized phase III trial and that of other TNF-α inhibitors and biologics. In the phase III trial, the incidence of AEs in the adalimumab groups was 68% (0.8 mg/kg) and 77% (0.4 mg/kg), with infections being the most common (45% and 56%, respectively) [11]. In our study, overall AE incidence was 82.5%, with 62.5% considered treatment-related. All were in grades 1–2, consistent with phase III findings. No new cases of special or severe infections were observed. Infections were most common (26.3%), followed by injection site reactions (15.0%) and metabolism/nutrition disorders (10.0%). Other system reactions each occurred in < 10% of patients. This pattern mirrors studies of other biologics in pediatric psoriasis, where infections are also the primary, mostly mild-to-moderate AEs, and injection-site reactions are less frequent and mild [9, 15, 16].

This multicenter, prospective, single-arm study provides systematic efficacy and safety data for adalimumab in Chinese children with severe plaque psoriasis from treatment initiation, addressing a notable evidence gap in this population. Pharmacoeconomically, adalimumab offers comparable efficacy (PASI 75/90) at a more competitive cost than originator biologics. Expanding biosimilar (including adalimumab) coverage under China’s medical insurance may reduce out-of-pocket expenses, potentially minimizing treatment discontinuation and improving treatment adherence and long-term outcomes. Study limitations include the single-arm, noncomparative design, which precludes direct efficacy comparisons with other systemic treatments. Any indirect cross-trial comparisons presented here are inherently limited by differences in patient characteristics, study protocols, and endpoint definitions and should be regarded as hypothesis-generating only. This study employed a “four-out-of-one” inclusion criterion for severe psoriasis, introducing a certain degree of heterogeneity in baseline disease severity. Additional limitations include the limited sample size and homogeneous ethnic background. Future studies with larger, diverse cohorts and extended follow-ups are needed to confirm long-term efficacy and safety.

Conclusions

This study confirms that adalimumab is an effective and safe treatment option for Chinese pediatric patients with severe plaque psoriasis. Its efficacy and safety profiles are consistent with clinical trial data for the originator drug, providing direct evidence for the clinical management of Chinese pediatric patients.

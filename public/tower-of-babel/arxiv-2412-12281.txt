An algebraic model for rational excisive functors
David Barnes, Magdalena Kędziorek, Niall Taggart

Abstract
. We provide a new proof of the rational splitting of excisive endofunctors of spectra as a product of their homogeneous layers independent of rational Tate vanishing. We utilise the analogy between endofunctors of spectra and equivariant stable homotopy theory and as a consequence, we obtain an algebraic model for rational excisive functors.



1.
Introduction

Goodwillie calculus
[
Goo90
,
Goo92
,
Goo03
]
is a tool for studying functors between categories which carry a notion of homotopy. It approximates a homotopy-preserving functor
F
by a tower

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{d}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{0}F}}

of functors, acting as a categorification of the Taylor series of a function from differential calculus. Through this analogy, polynomial functions of degree
d
are replaced by
d
-excisive functors and the functor
P_{d}F
appearing in the Goodwillie tower of
F
is the best approximation to
F
by a
d
-excisive functor. The difference between consecutive excisive approximations

D_{d}F=\mathsf{fib}(P_{d}F\longrightarrow P_{d-1}F),

is called a homogeneous functor of degree
d
, analogous to a monomial of degree
d
.

The basic aim of Goodwillie calculus is to understand the input functor
F

by analysing its excisive and homogenous approximations. Hence, one
studies the structure of the categories of excisive and homogeneous functors
in general.
In the case of
d
-homogeneous functors from spectra to spectra, Goodwillie’s classification of homogeneous functors says that there is an equivalence of
(\infty-)
categories between the category of
d
-homogeneous functors and spectra with an action of
\Sigma_{d}
(the group of permutations of
d
elements)

\mathsf{Homog}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\simeq{{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{B\Sigma_{d}},

realised by sending a functor
F
to its
d
-th derivative
\partial_{d}F\in{{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{B\Sigma_{d}}
.
This is analogous to a monomial of degree
d
being determined by its
d
-th derivative.

The classification of categories of excisive functors is more complicated.
For functors between the categories of (pointed) spaces and spectra, Arone and Ching
[
AC15
]
provided models in terms of coalgebras over a comonad.
Glasman
[
Gla16
]
gives a simpler classification of the category
\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
of
d
-excisive endofunctors of spectra in terms of (spectral) Mackey functors on the category
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq d}
of epimorphisms of finite sets of cardinality at most
d
.
This is analogous to the description of equivariant spectra for a finite group
G
as (spectral) Mackey functors for the orbit category
{\mathsf{Orb}}_{G}
of Guillou and May
[
GM24
]
and Barwick
[
Bar17
]
.

Glasman’s result instigated an analogy between equivariant homotopy theory and Goodwillie calculus which was utilised by Arone, Barthel, Heard and Sanders
[
ABHS25
]
to provide a tensor triangular classification of excisive functors through a computation of the Balmer spectrum of the category of (compact)
d
-excisive functors.
A key ingredient in this work is the Goodwillie-Burnside ring
A(d)
: the abstract Burnside ring in the sense of Yoshida
[
Yos87
]
associated to the category
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq d}
. This is a generalisation of the Burnside ring from equivariant algebra and representation theory. Indeed, inputting the orbit category of a finite group
G
into Yoshida’s machine produces the familiar Burnside ring.

In this paper, we analyse the idempotents of the rationalised Goodwillie-Burnside ring to give a new proof of the splitting of the category of rational
d
-excisive functors in terms of rational homogeneous functors.
In this context, a functor is rational if it takes values in
\mathbb{Q}
-local spectra, those spectra whose
homotopy groups are rational vector spaces.

Theorem A
.

There is an equivalence

\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}\mathsf{Homog}^{i}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}},

of
\infty
-categories between the
\infty
-category of rational
d
-excisive functors and the product of the
\infty
-categories of rational homogeneous functors.

Results of this form are already present in the literature. For instance, Kuhn
[
Kuh04
]
and McCarthy
[
McC01
]
used the Kuhn-McCarthy pullback square together with the rational Tate vanishing for finite groups to prove that every
d
-excisive functor splits as a product of its homogeneous layers after rationalization. Our proof of
Theorem

A
(an outline is given at the end of the introduction) avoids both the use of the Kuhn-McCarthy pullback square and rational Tate vanishing.

We can provide an even simpler description of
\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}}

using Goodwillie’s classification of homogeneous functors (as described above)
and results from rational equivariant stable homotopy theory.
Greenlees
[
Gre06
]
conjectured that the category of rational
G
-spectra (for
G
any compact Lie group) has an algebraic model: an algebraic category
\mathcal{A}(G)
and an equivalence
{{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\mathcal{A}(G)
. This program has had many successes, for example
[
BGKS17
,
GS18
,
Wim19
,
BGK21
,
BHI+22b
,
BHI+22a
]
, see
[
BK22
]
for a survey and introduction to the area. A key method in many of these results is using the idempotents of the rationalised Burnside ring
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
. Applying the fact that the category of rational chain complexes with an action of
\Sigma_{d}
,
{\mathsf{Ch}}(\mathbb{Q}[\Sigma_{d}])
, is an algebraic model for
{{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{B\Sigma_{d}}
gives the following.

Corollary B
.

There is an equivalence

\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}{\mathsf{Ch}}(\mathbb{Q}[\Sigma_{i}]),

of
\infty
-categories.

We also obtain the following seemingly new result, as
Corollary

6.5
, describing rational
homogenous functors as a category of modules. We do not expect this result to hold integrally.

Corollary C
.

The
d
-homogeneous functor
D_{d}(\Sigma^{\infty}\Omega^{\infty})
admits the structure of a commutative algebra object in the category of rational endofunctors of spectra, and there is an equivalence

\mathsf{Homog}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq{\mathsf{Mod}}_{{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\ast}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}}}(D_{d}(\Sigma^{\infty}\Omega^{\infty})),

of
\infty
-categories.

Outline of the proof

Taking motivation from equivariant homotopy theory, the first step is to understand the rational Goodwillie-Burnside ring. Following Yoshida, we show (
Lemma

2.4
) that the rational Goodwillie-Burnside ring splits

A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)\cong\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}\mathbb{Q},

by constructing an injective ring map
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)\to\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}\mathbb{Q}
using an epi-mono factorization of the category
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq d}
.

We can represent this ring map as a matrix. Computing the idempotents of the rational Goodwillie-Burnside ring then amounts to inverting this matrix (
Lemma

2.6
). In particular, for each
1\leq i\leq d
we obtain an idempotent
e_{i}
of the Goodwillie-Burnside ring
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)
, corresponding to the projection on the
i
-th factor in the product
\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}\mathbb{Q}
.

By work of Arone, Barthel, Heard and Sanders
[
ABHS25
]
, the Goodwillie-Burnside ring is the endomorphism ring of the unit of the homotopy category of
d
-excisive functors (
Lemma

5.1
), and hence these idempotents lift to the category of rational excisive functors, and induce a splitting of the category of rational excisive functors into an
e_{d}
-local piece and its orthogonal complement a
(1-e_{d})
-local piece.

The final step in the proof is to identify the orthogonal pieces in the splitting. To do this, we compute the kernel of the natural map
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)\to A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d-1)
coming from the inclusion of a subcategory
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq d-1}
into
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq d}
, showing that it is generated by the top idempotent
e_{d}
(
Example

3.6
). This map on rational Goodwillie-Burnside rings corresponds to the universal map
P_{d}F\to P_{d-1}F
(
Lemma

5.2
). It follows that for
F:{{\mathsf{Sp}}}\to{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
a rational
d
-excisive functor, peeling off the top idempotent splits the Goodwillie tower of
F
into two pieces,

e_{d}
-local
(1-e_{d})
-local

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{d-1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D_{d}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D_{d-1}F}}

where the orange box is the

Theorem

uses this to split the category of

into

Contribution of this paper

Our proof of the rational splitting of the category of excisive functors is very different to those currently in the literature.
It relies mainly on the formal properties of the category as developed in
[
ABHS25
]
, primarily
the symmetric monoidal structure of the category and the structure of the rational Goodwillie-Burnside ring.
The authors believe there should be further results along these lines, where one has some
generalisation of (topological) Mackey functors and can prove a splitting result
via properties of idempotents in the rational setting.
Indeed, recent work of the first two authors with Hill on incomplete Mackey functors
[
BHK24
]
,
uses a similar splitting approach with rational idempotents and again connects to rational Tate vanishing.
The authors believe this approach will be valuable in a setting where the analogue of
rational Tate vanishing is not accessible.

We believe that
Corollary

C
is new and of some independent interest, providing
a description of rational homogeneous functors in terms of a category of modules.

The authors also want to add to the efforts of Arone, Barthel, Heard and Sanders
[
ABHS25
]

in advertising the results and methods of Yoshida
[
Yos87
]
. In this paper we concentrate on the rational case, and we explicitly describe and draw the categories
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq 2}
,
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq 3}
and
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq 4}
, calculate a complete set of idempotents for associated rational Goodwillie-Burnside rings in these cases and offer a comparison to the case of
{\mathsf{Orb}}_{G}
for
G
the cyclic group of order 6.

2.
Rational abstract Burnside rings

In this section, we survey Yoshida’s theory of abstract Burnside rings over the rational numbers
[
Yos87
]
. It describes a process of assigning a ring to a finite skeletal category, which in the case of the orbit category of a finite group
G
recovers (
Example

2.7
) the rational Burnside ring
A(G)
from rational equivariant algebra
[
Yos87
, Example 3.3.(a)]
. We work rationally since our applications are rational, but the theory holds more generally, see e.g.,
[
Yos87
,
YOT18
]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote a finite skeletal category, i.e., a category with finitely many objects, finitely many morphisms and such that if
c\cong d
, then
c=d
. Write
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathcal{C}}
for the product
\prod_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{Q}
and denote by
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{C})
the free
\mathbb{Q}
-module generated by the set of object of
\mathcal{C}
. Define the
Burnside homomorphism
to be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map,

\phi:A_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\mathcal{C}},\ \ d\longmapsto(|\mathcal{C}(c,d)|)_{c\in\mathcal{C}},

where
\mathcal{C}(c,d)
is the set of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
from
c
to
d
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-module
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{C})
is called a
(rational) abstract Burnside ring
if the Burnside homomorphism is an injective
\mathbb{Q}
-module map.

If the Burnside homomorphism
\phi:A_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathbb{Q}^{\mathcal{C}}
is injective, then it is an isomorphism since domain and codomain are
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces of the same dimension. As such, there is a unique ring structure on
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{C})
such that the Burnside homomorphism is a ring map.

By construction, the Burnside homomorphism is represented by the
hom-set
|\mathcal{C}|\times|\mathcal{C}|
-matrix

H
given by

H_{cd}=|\mathcal{C}(c,d)|.

This observation reduces the analysis of the Burnside homomorphism into a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear algebra problem.

To guarantee that
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{C})
becomes an abstract Burnside ring, we restrict to those categories
\mathcal{C}
which may be equipped with a factorization system. This amounts to providing a method through which we can study the hom-set matrix through standard matrix decompositions.

Definition 2.2
.

A category
\mathcal{C}
has an
epi-mono factorization system
if there is a pair of wide

If

\mathsf{Aut}(c)=\mathcal{E}(c,c)=\mathcal{M}(c,c),

\mathcal{E}(c,d)\coloneqq\mathcal{C}(c,d)\cap\mathcal{E}\subseteq{\mathsf{Epi}}(c,d)

Lemma 2.3

(
[

The epimorphism, monomorphism and automorphism matrices are integral and

H=EAM.

By

H

\det(H)=\prod_{c\in\mathcal{C}}|\mathsf{Aut}(c)|
, which is non-zero since
\mathsf{Aut}(c)\neq\varnothing
for every

. It follows that the hom-set matrix is rationally invertible and the Burnside homomorphism is an isomorphism.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite skeletal category. If
\mathcal{C}
has an epi-mono factorization system, then the rational Burnside homomorphism

\phi:A_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\mathcal{C}},

is an isomorphism. In particular,
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{C})
is a rational abstract Burnside ring.

Remark 2.5
.

The rational situation is quite different from the integral case. Integrally, Yoshida
[
Yos87
, §3.5]
also requires the existences of certain coequalisers in
\mathcal{C}
. This discrepancy amounts to the fact that integrally it is not enough for the Burnside homomorphism to be injective, but rather we also require the image to be closed under multiplication.

The strength in the rational Burnside ring is that the isomorphism of

2.4
allows us to identify idempotents through the process of Möbius inversion, i.e., by inverting the hom-set matrix.

Lemma 2.6

(
[

, Lemma 5.4]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite skeletal category. If
\mathcal{C}

is given by

e_{d}=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}(H^{-1})_{cd}~c,

where
H

3.
Epiorbital categories of epimorphisms

We will now provide an example of the abstract Burnside ring machinery of

Definition 3.1
.

For each

5.
The rational Goodwillie-Burnside ring

Our attention now turns to the role of the abstract Burnside ring
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)
in Goodwillie calculus, a comprehensive account of which is given in
[
ABHS25
, §8-9]
. We work rationally, as this allows us to simplify and streamline certain arguments, but integral versions of many of the statements in this section may be found in
loc. cit.
.

The Goodwillie derivatives assemble into a left adjoint

\partial_{\ast}:\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\longrightarrow{\mathsf{SymSeq}},

between the
\infty
-category of
d
-excisive functors and the
\infty
-category of symmetric sequences
[
AC15
, Proposition 4.5]
. This functor factors through
d
-truncated symmetric sequences since
\partial_{i}P_{d}\simeq 0
for
i>d
. The mapping spectra in symmetric sequences decompose as a product, so there is an induced map on mapping spectra

\partial_{\ast}:\Hom_{\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}},P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}})\longrightarrow\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}\Hom_{{{\mathsf{Sp}}}}(\partial_{i}(P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}}),\partial_{i}(P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}})),

which induces a ring map

\theta_{d}:\pi_{0}{\mathsf{End}}_{\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\longrightarrow\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}\pi_{0}\Hom_{{{\mathsf{Sp}}}}(\partial_{i}(P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}}),\partial_{i}(P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}}))\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathbb{Q}^{d}

on the level of rational homotopy categories, where the last isomorphism follows from Arone and Ching’s computation
[
AC15
, Lemma 5.10]
of the derivatives of representables as
\partial_{i}(P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}})\simeq\mathbb{S}
with trivial
\Sigma_{i}
-action for all
1\leq i\leq d
.

To ease notation, we will denote the endomorphism ring
\pi_{0}\Hom_{{{\mathsf{Sp}}}}(P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}},P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
by
R_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)
. We will now show that Goodwillie-Burnside ring
A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)
is isomorphic to this endomorphism ring
R_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)
, by an intricate analysis of how the calculus and the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear algebra interact.

Lemma 5.1
.

There is an isomorphism

R_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)\cong A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)

between the endomorphism ring of the monoidal unit in
\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
and the rational Goodwillie-Burnside ring.

Proof.

There is a zigzag of ring maps

R_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \theta_{d}\ }\mathbb{Q}^{d}\xleftarrow{\ \phi\ }A_{\mathbb{Q}}(d),

in which the right-most map is an isomorphism by
Lemma

2.4
, hence it suffices to show that
\theta_{d}:R_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)\to\mathbb{Q}^{d}
is an isomorphism.

By an elementary degree computation
[
ABHS25
, Lemma 9.20]
, there are
elements
[\lambda_{m}]\in R_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)
,
1\leq m\leq d
, such that the

i
th component of
\theta_{d}[\lambda_{m}]

is
|{\mathsf{surj}}(i,m)|
, see
Example

3.5
. The same example shows that the vectors
\theta_{d}[\lambda_{1}],\dots,\theta_{d}[\lambda_{d}]
are linearly independent, hence
\theta_{d}
is surjective for all
d
.

To see that
\theta_{d}
is injective, and hence an isomorphism, we work by induction over
d
. In the initial case of
d=1
, we have equivalences

\Hom_{\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{1}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(P_{1}h_{\mathbb{S}},P_{1}h_{\mathbb{S}})\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}\Hom_{{{\mathsf{Sp}}}}(\partial_{1}(P_{1}h_{\mathbb{S}}),\partial_{1}(P_{1}h_{\mathbb{S}})),\simeq\Hom_{{{\mathsf{Sp}}}}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{S}),

by the classification of homogeneous functors, since
D_{1}h_{\mathbb{S}}\simeq P_{1}h_{\mathbb{S}}
. Taking
\pi_{0}
gives the required ring isomorphism
\theta_{1}:R_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)\to\mathbb{Q}
.

For the induction step, assume that
\theta_{d-1}
is an isomorphism. Since
\partial_{i}P_{d-1}\simeq\partial_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
and
\partial_{d}P_{d-1}\simeq 0
, there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Q}^{d}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R_{\mathbb{Q}}(d-1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Q}^{d-1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{d}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(P_{d-1})\otimes\mathbb{Q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i_{d-1})^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{d-1}}

where
(i_{d-1})^{\ast}:\mathbb{Q}^{d}\to\mathbb{Q}^{d-1}
is projection onto the first

The above square induces a map

on vertical kernels. If

is

The element

is in the kernel of

.

To show that the map on kernels is an isomorphism, we will show that both kernels have the same dimension. By the Yoneda lemma and the universal property of the excisive approximations the exact sequence

may be identified with the exact sequence

induced by the

-homogeneous fibre sequence of

evaluated at
\mathbb{S}
. Computing

-homogenous layer of

, we see that

, and

are isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}
, and hence

is an isomorphism.

Proposition 6.3
.

The fibre sequence,

P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}}[e_{d}^{-1}]\simeq D_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}}\qquad P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}}[(1-e_{d})^{-1}]\simeq P_{d-1}h_{\mathbb{S}}.

Consider the first term of this splitting and the induced fibre sequence

D_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}}[e_{d}^{-1}]\longrightarrow P_{d}h_{\mathbb{S}}[e_{d}^{-1}]\longrightarrow P_{d-1}h_{\mathbb{S}}[e_{d}^{-1}],

By

A similar argument for the other term of the splitting shows that

Theorem 6.4
.

There is an equivalence

\mathsf{Exc}_{\ast}^{d}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\prod_{1\leq i\leq d}\mathsf{Homog}^{i}({{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\omega},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})_{\mathbb{Q}},

By

7.

One of the major theorems of Goodwillie calculus is the classification of homogeneous functors. To every functor,

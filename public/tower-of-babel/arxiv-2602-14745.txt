Abstract
In this paper, we study non-planar degeneracies with cylindrical configurations. They could be constructed by the product ℂ ​ ℙ 1 × T \mathbb{CP}^{1}\times T of the projective plane and a complex torus with embedding ( m , n ) (m,n) . We prove that their fundamental groups of Galois covers have an abelian subgroup of rank m ⁡ ( 2 ​ n − 1 ) m(2n-1) respectively, and the irregularity of these surfaces are at least 2 ​ m ​ n − 1 2mn-1 . Furthermore, we also use Chern numbers to compute the index of such surfaces and classify them. Keywords Chern number, complex torus, fundamental groups, Galois cover. MR(2020) Subject Classification 05E16, 14J10, 14J80, 20F55. 1 Scientific background There has been extensive research about the topological and arithmetic properties of non-simply connected surfaces. In this paper, we investigate some of these properties of the surfaces. An effective approach to studying the topological properties of algebraic surfaces is the application of degeneration techniques, particularly degeneration of the surface into a union of planes. The earliest application of this method was shown in the study of Hirzebruch surfaces in [ 18 ] and [ 22 ] . As demonstrated in [ 8 ] , [ 9 ] , and [ 10 ] , this method has been successfully applied to surfaces of degrees 5, 6, and 8, revealing their topological properties. Moreover, in references [ 5 ] , [ 6 ] , and [ 7 ] , we further investigated the topological properties of the Galois cover of Zappatic surfaces. Our results are related to Chisini’s conjecture about algebraic surfaces and can be applied to the classification of algebraic surfaces. For details, please refer to [ 14 ] and [ 15 ] . A promising direction for further work is the exploration of non-planar degenerations. Indeed, we have initiated preliminary investigations into such degenerations in [ 1 ] . The Galois cover X g ​ a ​ l X_{gal} of a surface X X serves as a geometric manifestation of Galois theory and reflects the topological properties of the surface. In [ 20 ] , the author elucidates the arithmetic properties of Galois covers and provides computational formulas for certain topological invariants. Furthermore, Galois covers can be applied to the study of moduli spaces of surfaces; for instance, in [ 24 ] , the authors compute decompositions of these moduli spaces. Here, we aim to study the structure of fundamental group π 1 ​ ( X g ​ a ​ l ) \pi_{1}(X_{gal}) of the Galois cover of a surface. In this paper, as a first step towards computing π 1 ​ ( X g ​ a ​ l m , n ) \pi_{1}(X^{m,n}_{gal}) for surfaces X m , n X^{m,n} , here m ≥ 1 , n ≥ 2 m\geq 1,\ n\geq 2 , we provide a complete presentation of these fundamental groups for families X 2 , 4 X^{2,4} , X 2 , n X^{2,n} , and X 3 , n X^{3,n} . By applying techniques from [ 25 ] , it follows directly that π 1 ​ ( X g ​ a ​ l m , n ) \pi_{1}(X^{m,n}_{gal}) is a subgroup of a certain quotient of Coxeter groups. Furthermore, we employ Coxeter group theory to derive certain properties of π 1 ​ ( X g ​ a ​ l m , n ) \pi_{1}(X^{m,n}_{gal}) , with a particular focus on its subgroups and quotient structures. By analyzing the quotient group of π 1 ​ ( X g ​ a ​ l m , n ) \pi_{1}(X^{m,n}_{gal}) , we are able to estimate the irregularity of the corresponding Galois cover in this setting. The following theorem constitutes our main result. Theorem 1 . Group π 1 ​ ( X g ​ a ​ l m , n ) \pi_{1}(X^{m,n}_{gal}) contains a subgroup that is isomorphic to ℤ m ⁡ ( 2 ​ n − 1 ) \mathbb{Z}^{m(2n-1)} . Since a simply connected space has trivial fundamental group, Theorem immediately implies that Galois covers X g ​ a ​ l m , n X^{m,n}_{gal} cannot be simply connected. Therefore, we obtain the following corollary. Corollary 2 . The Galois covers of the surfaces X m , n X^{m,n} are non-simply connected. Building on the findings presented in Theorem , we can derive significant implications for the structure of the first homology group and the irregularity of the algebraic surface. This subgroup structure of the fundamental group indicates a rich topological complexity, facilitating a deeper understanding of the associated properties of algebraic surface. Corollary 3 . ( ( 1 ) ) The rank of the first homology group H 1 ​ ( X g ​ a ​ l m , n , ℤ ) H_{1}(X^{m,n}_{gal},\mathbb{Z}) is at least 2 ​ ( 2 ​ m ​ n − 1 ) 2(2mn-1) . ( ( 2 ) ) The irregularity of the algebraic surface X g ​ a ​ l m , n X^{m,n}_{gal} is at least 2 ​ m ​ n − 1 2mn-1 . In algebraic geometry, when classifying a compact complex surface X X , in addition to its own topological invariants (such as Betti numbers), the Chern numbers of its tangent bundle, c 1 2 ​ ( X ) c^{2}_{1}(X) and c 2 ​ ( X ) c_{2}(X) , are two of the most fundamental numerical invariants. The positivity and negativity of Chern numbers is closely related to the Kodaira dimension of the surface. In [ 12 ] , the authors discuss the geography problem of surfaces with given Chern numbers. The method of Galois covers can also be employed to provide many examples in the geography of surfaces. We apply the formulas from [ 22 ] to determine the Chern numbers of the Galois covers. Another important topological invariant of algebraic surfaces X X is the index of the intersection form on H 2 ​ ( X , ℚ ) H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q}) , denoted by τ ⁡ ( X ) \tau(X) . Hirzebruch’s formula (see [ 17 ] ) says that τ (X)= 1 3 ( c_1^2(X)-2c_2(X) ). By analyzing the index, we can draw connections between the topological characteristics of these surfaces and their geometrical properties. We have the following theorem: Theorem 4 . ( ( 1 ) ) τ ⁡ ( X g ​ a ​ l m , n ) = 1 3 ​ ( 4 ​ m ) ! ⋅ ( 8 ​ m − 9 ) \tau(X^{m,n}_{gal})=\frac{1}{3}(4m)!\cdot(8m-9) , if n = 2 n=2 .

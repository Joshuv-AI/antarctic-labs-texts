Abstract
. The cone of a classical group G G is an affine G × G G\times G -variety. The aim of this note is to initiate its combinatorial study in the cases when G G is the complex orthogonal or symplectic group. The coordinate ring of the cone of G G is a finitely generated commutative graded algebra. First the G × G G\times G -module structure of its homogeneous components is determined. This is used to compute the Hilbert series of this coordinate ring in the cases when G G is the orthogonal group O ⁡ ( 3 ) {\mathrm{O}}(3) , O ⁡ ( 4 ) {\mathrm{O}}(4) , the special orthogonal group SO ⁡ ( 4 ) {\mathrm{SO}}(4) , and when G G is the symplectic group Sp ⁡ ( 4 ) {\mathrm{Sp}}(4) . It is concluded that the coordinate ring of the cone of O ⁡ ( 3 ) {\mathrm{O}}(3) is not Koszul, hence the vanishing ideal of this cone has no quadratic Gröbner basis (although it is minimally generated by quadratic elements).

1.
Introduction

Quantum groups were originally defined as Hopf algebra deformations of universal enveloping algebras of complex semisimple Lie algebras.
A dual approach was taken by Faddeev, Reshetikhin and Tahtajan
[
17
]
, who defined quantized coordinate rings of classical groups
by taking a noncommutative deformation of the coordinate ring of
G
. This is a two-step process: first they define a matrix bialgebra (called the FRT-bialgebra),
and then specialize to
1
a central group-like element in it.
Let
G
be any of the classical groups
{\mathrm{SL}}(n)
,
{\mathrm{O}}(n)
, or
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
(for
n
even in the latter case).
The FRT-bialgebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}(G)
is an associative graded
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra generated by
n^{2}
elements of degree
1

(thought of as the coordinate functions on the space of
n\times n
”quantum matrices”) subject to a set of homogeneous quadratic relations depending on a
non-zero parameter
q\in{\mathbb{C}}
. The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}({\mathrm{SL}}(n))
is the quantized coordinate ring of
n\times n
matrices, and
is thoroughly studied from combinatorial and ring theoretic aspects, see for example
[
15
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
9
]
.
It is a noncommutative deformation of the coordinate ring of the space of
n\times n
matrices, a polynomial algebra in
n^{2}
indeterminates, whose

{\mathrm{GL}}(n)\times{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
-module structure is given by the Cauchy formula (see for example
[
16
, Section 11.5.1]
).

For
G={\mathrm{O}}(n)
or
G={\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
, the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}(G)
is a deformation of the coordinate ring
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))

of the cone
\mathcal{C}(G)
of
G
(see e.g.
[
6
]
).
Therefore the Hilbert series of the graded algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}(G)
coincides with the Hilbert series of
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))
.
Moreover, the problem of finding a normal form for the elements of
\mathcal{A}_{q}(G)
(e.g.
giving a monomial basis of
\mathcal{A}_{q}(G)
together with the corresponding rewriting rules) is closely related to the similar task for
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))
.
In this paper we initiate the combinatorial study of
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))
.

We shall work over the base field
{\mathbb{C}}
of complex numbers, and throughout the paper we assume that

n\geq 3
is a positive integer.
Recall that the orthogonal group is

{\mathrm{O}}(n)=\{M\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}\mid M^{T}M=I\},

the set of
n\times n
matrices over
{\mathbb{C}}
whose transpose is their inverse.
For
n=2m\geq 4
even, the symplectic group is

{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)=\{M\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}\mid M^{T}JM=J\},

where
J
is the
n\times n
matrix obtained by glueing
m
copies of

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
along the main diagonal.
Now let
G
be any of
{\mathrm{O}}(n)
or
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
.
Then
G
is a subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
defined by polynomial equations.

By the
cone
\mathcal{C}(G)
of
G
we mean the
Zariski closure
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
of

\{cM\mid c\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad M\in G\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}.

Note that the subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
in the above line is not Zariski closed in general, see Example
6.3
.
We have the embeddings

G\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}(G)\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}

of affine algebraic varieties. Restriction of functions induces the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra surjections

(1)

{\mathcal{O}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}(G)

between the corresponding coordinate rings.
Here
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n})={\mathbb{C}}[x_{ij}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n]
is an
n^{2}
-variable polynomial algebra, where
x_{ij}
is the function assigning to
M\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
its
(i,j)
-entry.
It is endowed with the standard grading. Since
\mathcal{C}(G)
is a cone, the surjection
\varphi_{1}
transports the grading to
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))
.
So denoting by
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d}
the degree
d
homogeneous component of
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))
we have

{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d}
.
The group
G\times G
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
by

(g,h)\cdot M=gMh^{-1}\ \mbox{ for }g,h\in G\mbox{ and }M\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n},

with matrix multiplication on the right hand side. The subvarieties
\mathcal{C}(G)
and
G
are both
G\times G
-stable,
and we have the induced
G\times G
-action on the corresponding coordinate rings. The
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra homomorphisms

\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are
G\times G
-equivariant.

2.
The
G\times G
-module structure of
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d}

Throughout this section
G
stands for
{\mathrm{O}}(n)
, or for
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
(where
n=2m
is even in the latter case).
The finite dimensional irreducible representations of
G
(as a linear algebraic group) are naturally indexed by a set
\Lambda(G)
of partitions.
By a
partition
we mean a non-increasing finite sequence of integers.
Denote by
\mathrm{Par}(n):=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\mid\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\quad\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}\}
the set of partitions with
at most
n
non-zero parts.
Recall from
[
16
]
or
[
20
]
that we have

\Lambda({\mathrm{O}}(n))=\{\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}(n)\mid|\{i\mid\lambda_{i}>0\}|+|\{i\mid\lambda_{i}>1\}|\leq n\},

whereas for
n=2m
even we have

\Lambda({\mathrm{Sp}}(n))=\mathrm{Par}(m).

Set
\Lambda(G)_{d}:=\{\lambda\in\lambda(G)\mid\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}=d\}
.
For
\lambda\in\Lambda(G)
denote by
V_{\lambda}
the underlying vector space of the irreducible representation of
G
labeled by
\lambda
, endowed
with the corresponding linear action of
G
.
Note that
V_{(1,0,\dots,0)}\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, endowed with the defininig representation of
G
(given by multiplication of column vectors by matrices).
We shall use the following well-known fact (see for example
[
16
, Section 11.3.1]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda(G)_{d}
the
G
-module
V_{\lambda}
occurs as a summand
in the
d
th tensor power
\mathrm{T}^{d}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, and
V_{\lambda}
is not a summand of
\mathrm{T}^{k}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})

for
k>d
with
k-d
odd, or
for
k<d
.

The representation of
G\times G
on
{\mathcal{O}}(G)
is described by the Peter-Weyl Theorem:

Proposition 2.2
.

We have the following isomorphism of
G\times G
-modules:

\mathcal{O}(G)\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda(G)}V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}.

Proof.

The Peter-Weyl Theorem says that
\mathcal{O}(G)\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda(G)}V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}
,
where
V_{\lambda}^{*}
stands for the dual of the representation
V_{\lambda}
(see for example
[
16
, Theorems 8.3.2 and 8.7.2.3]
).
Taking into account the fact that all representations of
G
are self-dual, i.e.
V_{\lambda}\cong V_{\lambda}^{*}
holds for all
\lambda\in\Lambda(G)
,
we get the desired statement.
∎

We mention that a basis of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathrm{O}}(n))
consisting of so-called bideterminants is given in
[
3
]
, extending for the orthogonal
group the analogous theory for
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n})
(cf.
[
16
, Section 13.4]
,

[
7
]
,
[
5
]
). The case of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathrm{Sp}}(n))
is treated in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
d=0,1,2,\dots
we have the following isomorphisms of
G\times G
-modules:

{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d}\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda(G)_{d-2k}}V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda},

where
\lfloor d/2\rfloor
is the lower integer part of
d/2
.
In particular, for
d\geq 2
we have

{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d}\cong{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d-2}\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda(G)_{d}}V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}.

Proof.

Note that
\varphi_{2}(\varphi_{1}(x_{ij}))
are the matrix elements of the defining representation of
G
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

(the maps
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are defined in (
1
)).
Therefore
\varphi_{2}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d})
is the
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear span of the length
d
products of the matrix elements of the defining representation of
G
,
hence
\varphi_{2}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d})
is the space of matrix elements of the
d
-fold tensor power
\mathrm{T}^{d}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
of the defining representation of
G
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
It follows by Proposition
2.2
that

\varphi_{2}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d})
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}
,
where the direct sum ranges over the
\lambda\in\Lambda(G)
such that

V_{\lambda}
occurs as a direct summand in
\mathrm{T}^{d}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
.
Hence by Proposition
2.1
we have

\varphi_{2}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d})\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda(G)_{d-2k}}V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}
.
The restriction to
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d}
of
\varphi_{2}
is injective, since if a homogeneous polynomial in
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n})
vanishes on
G
, then it vanishes on
\mathcal{C}(G)
as well.
Therefore we have
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d}\cong\varphi_{2}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G))_{d})
, finishing the proof of the first isomorphism.
The second isomorphism follows from the first by induction on
d
.
∎

3.
Some Hilbert series

Recall that the
Hilbert series
of a graded algebra
A=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}A_{d}

(with finite dimensional homogeneous components
A_{d}
) is the formal power series

\mathrm{H}(A;t):=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(A_{d})t^{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[[t]].

For
G={\mathrm{O}}(n)
or
G={\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
introduce the following function
h_{G}:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
:

(2)

h_{G}(d):=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(G)_{d}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{\lambda})^{2}.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.3
we get the following:

Corollary 3.1
.

For
G={\mathrm{O}}(n)
or
G={\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
we have

\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}(G));t):=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}h_{G}(d-2k)t^{d}.

3.1.
The case
G={\mathrm{O}}(3)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

We have the equality

\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3)));t)=-t+\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\binom{2d+3}{3}t^{2d}=\frac{1+5t+5t^{2}-6t^{3}+4t^{4}-t^{5}}{(1-t)^{4}}.

Proof.

We have
\Lambda({\mathrm{O}}(3))_{d}=\{(d,0,0),(d-1,1,0)\}
, except for
d=3
or
d=1
, when
\Lambda({\mathrm{O}}(3))_{1}=\{(1,0,0)\}
and
\Lambda({\mathrm{O}}(3))_{3}=\{(3,0,0),(2,1,0),(1,1,1)\}
.
Moreover,
V_{(d,0,0)}
can be identified with the space of
3
-variable spherical harmonics of degree
d
(see for example
[
11
, Section 5.2.3]
),
and so
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{(d,0,0)})=\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d}{2}=2d+1
. Moreover, the restriction to the special orthogonal group
{\mathrm{SO}}(3)
of the representation on
V_{(d-1,1,0)}
is isomorphic to

V_{(d-1,0,0)}
, whence

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{(d-1,1,0)})=\dim(V_{(d-1,0,0)})=2(d-1)+1=2d-1
.
Furthermore,
V_{(1,1,1)}
can be identified with the third exterior power of
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
, so

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{(1,1,1)})=1
.
Thus we have

h_{{\mathrm{O}}(3)}(d)=\begin{cases}(2d-1)^{2}+(2d+1)^{2}=8d^{2}+2&\mbox{ for }d\notin\{0,1,3\};\\
1^{2}&\mbox{ for }d=0;\\
3^{2}&\mbox{ for }d=1;\\
1^{2}+5^{2}+7^{2}&\mbox{ for }d=3.\end{cases}

It follows by Corollary
3.1
that
that for
d\geq 2
or
d=0
we have

\dim({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3)))_{d})=\begin{cases}1+3^{2}+5^{2}+\dots+(2d+1)^{2}=\binom{2d+3}{3}&\mbox{ for }d\neq 1;\\
1&\mbox{ for }d=1.\end{cases}

This shows

\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3)));t)=-t+\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\binom{2d+3}{3}t^{d}
.
Using that

\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\binom{2d+3}{3}t^{2d}=\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{3}\left(\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}t^{2d+3}\right)=\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{3}\frac{t^{3}}{1-t^{2}}=\frac{1+6t^{2}+t^{4}}{(1-t^{2})^{4}}

we get

\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\binom{2d+3}{3}t^{d}=\frac{1+6t+t^{2}}{(1-t)^{4}},

whence

\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3))),t)=\frac{1+6t+t^{2}}{(1-t)^{4}}-t=\frac{1+5t+5t^{2}-6t^{3}+4t^{4}-t^{5}}{(1-t)^{4}}.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

The quadratic algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3)))
is not Koszul.

Proof.

For a graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}A_{d}
,
the
Poincaré series
of
A
is

\mathrm{P}(A,t)=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\dim\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{d}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})t^{d}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{d}_{A}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})t^{d}.

It is well known that if
A
is Koszul, then the equality

(3)

\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(A),-t)\cdot\mathrm{P}({\mathcal{O}}(A),t)=1

holds in the power series ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[[t]]
(see
[
12
]
,
[
8
]
).
Up to degree
9
the terms of
1/\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3))),-t)
are the same as the corresponding terms of the polynomial

(1+t)^{4}\left(1+\sum_{d=1}^{9}(5t-5t^{2}-6t^{3}-4t^{4}-t^{5})^{d}\right)

which are

-7330t^{9}+17883t^{8}+11539t^{7}+5129t^{6}+1912t^{5}+628t^{4}+183t^{3}+46t^{2}+9t+1.

Thus the coefficient of
t^{9}
in
1/\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3))),-t)
is negative, hence it can not be a Poincaré series.
Therefore the equality (
3
) can not hold for
A={\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3)))
,
whence
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(3)))
is not Koszul.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Our focus on the Koszul property is motivated by the connection to the construction of certain quantum
groups (see the Introduction). In fact it was advertised in
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
that quadratic (and in particular, Koszul) algebras should play a distinguished
role in quantum group theory.

3.2.
The case of
{\mathrm{O}}(4)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

We have the equality

\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(4)));t)=\frac{1+9t+27t^{2}+19t^{3}-30t^{4}+34t^{5}-35t^{6}+21t^{7}-7t^{8}+t^{9}}{(1-t)^{7}}.

Proof.

For
d=3
or
d\geq 5
we have
\Lambda({\mathrm{O}}(4))_{d}=\{(d-2,1,1,0),\ (d-k,k,0,0)\ \mbox{ for }k=0,\dots,\lfloor d/2\rfloor\}
. For

d\leq 2
we have to omit
(d-2,1,1,0)
, whereas for

d=4
the set
\Lambda({\mathrm{O}}(4))_{4}
contains also
(1,1,1,1)
.
The dimension of
V_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda({\mathrm{O}}(4))_{d}
is given in the following table.
Note that the space
V_{(d,0,0,0)}
can be identified with the space of spherical harmonics of degree
d
in
4
variables,
hence its dimension is
\binom{d+3}{3}-\binom{d+1}{3}
. Moreover,
V_{(d-2,1,1,0)}
is isomorphic to
V_{(d-2,0,0,0)}
as an

{\mathrm{SO}}(4)
-module, whereas

can be identified with the
4
th exterior power of
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
.
We read off the dimension of
V_{(d-k,k,0,0)}
for

from
[

, Formula 3.28]
.

\begin{array}[]{c||c|c|c|c}\lambda&(d,0,0,0)&(d-k,k,0,0),1\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor&(d-2,1,1,0)&(1,1,1,1)\\
\hline\cr\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{\lambda})&(d+1)^{2}&2(d+1)(d-2k+1)&(d-1)^{2}&1\end{array}

It follows that for

defined in (

) we have

h_{{\mathrm{O}}(4)}(d)=\begin{cases}(d+1)^{4}+(d-1)^{4}+4\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}(d+1)^{2}(d-2k+1)^{2}&\mbox{ for }d\notin\{0,2,4\}\\
1&\mbox{ for }d=0\\
117&\mbox{ for }d=2\\
1707&\mbox{ for }d=4.\end{cases}

So for

we have

h_{{\mathrm{O}}(4)}(d)=\frac{2}{3}d^{5}+\frac{10}{3}d^{4}+\frac{32}{3}d^{2}-\frac{2}{3}d+2

by Corollary
3.1
.
A routine calculation yields that

\dim({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(4)))_{d})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ for }d=0\\
118&\mbox{ for }d=2\\
1825&\mbox{ for }d=4\\
p(d)&\mbox{ for }d\notin\{0,2,4\},\end{cases}

where

We have
p(0)=1
,
p(2)=119
,
p(4)=1825
,
so

(4)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(4)))_{d})=\begin{cases}p(d)&\mbox{ for }d\neq 2\\
p(d)-1&\mbox{ for }d=2.\end{cases}

We can rewrite

as a linear combination of binomial coefficients as follows:

(5)

p(d)=\binom{d+1}{1}-14\binom{d+2}{2}+34\binom{d+3}{3}-60\binom{d+5}{5}+40\binom{d+6}{6}.

It follows from (
4
) and (

) that

\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{O}}(4)));t)=-t^{2}+\frac{1}{(1-t)^{2}}-\frac{14}{(1-t)^{3}}+\frac{34}{1-t)^{4}}-\frac{60}{(1-t)^{6}}+\frac{40}{(1-t)^{7}},

which gives the formula in our statement.
∎

3.3.
The case of
{\mathrm{Sp}}(4)

Theorem 3.6
.

We have the equality

\mathrm{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}({\mathrm{Sp}}(4)));t)=\frac{1+5t+5t^{2}+t^{3}}{(1-t)^{11}}.

Proof.

We have
\Lambda({\mathrm{Sp}}(4))_{d}=\{(d-k,k)\mid k=0,1,\dots,\lfloor d/2\rfloor\}
,
and by
[
19
, Formula 3.29]
,

{\mathrm{End}}_{G}(\mathrm{T}^{d}(V))=Z({\mathcal{U}}(G)_{d})
is generated by the algebra

{\mathrm{End}}_{GL(V)}(\mathrm{T}^{d}(V))=Z({\mathcal{U}}_{d})
and a single
linear transformation

given by

B(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d})=\langle v_{1}\mid v_{2}\rangle\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}\otimes v_{3}\otimes\cdots v_{d},

see for example
[
16
, Section 11.3.2]
, where this is deduced from the first fundamental theorem for vector invariants of the orthogonal group (the algebra

Z({\mathcal{U}}(G)_{d})
is called
Brauer centralizer algebra
).
Since
{\mathcal{U}}_{d}
and
{\mathcal{U}}(G)_{d}
are semisimple subalgebras of
{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{T}^{d}(V))
, by the double centralizing theorem for semisimple algebras (see for example
[
16
, Theorem 6.2.5]
), one recovers them as the centralizer of their centralizer.
Thus we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}(G)_{d}=Z(Z({\mathcal{U}}(G)_{d}))=Z(\{B\}\cup Z({\mathcal{U}}_{d}))

\displaystyle=Z(\{B\})\cap Z(Z({\mathcal{U}}_{d}))={\mathcal{U}}_{d}\cap Z(\{B\}).

This can be reformulated as follows:

Proposition 6.5
.

The kernel of the natural surjection
{\mathcal{U}}_{d}^{*}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{U}}(G)_{d}^{*}

(given by restriction of linear functionals on
{\mathcal{U}}_{d}
to the subspace
{\mathcal{U}}(G)_{d}
)
is spanned by the restrictions to
{\mathcal{U}}_{d}
of the coordinate functions of the map

{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{T}^{d}(V))\to{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{T}^{d}(V)),\qquad Y\mapsto BY-YB.

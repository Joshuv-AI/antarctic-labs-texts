Introducing a Novel Subclass of Harmonic Functions with Close-to-Convex Properties
Serkan ÇAKMAK 1 and Sibel YALÇIN 2

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new subclass of close-to-convex harmonic functions. We present a sufficient coefficient condition for a function to be a member of this class. Furthermore, we establish a distortion theorem. These results lay the groundwork for extending the findings to function classes involving higher-order derivatives.

30C45, 30C50

1.
Introduction

In the realm of harmonic functions, every function
\mathfrak{f}
belonging to the class
\mathcal{SH}^{0}
can be expressed as
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}
, where

(1.1)

\mathfrak{u}(z)=z+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}u_{m}z^{m},\quad\mathfrak{v}(z)=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}v_{m}z^{m}

with both functions being analytic in the open unit disk
\mathbb{E}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|<1\right\}
. Provided that
|\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)|>|\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)|
in
\mathbb{E}
,
\mathfrak{f}
is locally univalent and sense-preserving in
\mathbb{E}
. It’s noteworthy that when
\mathfrak{v}(z)
is identically zero,
\mathcal{SH}^{0}
contracts to class
\mathcal{S}
.

The subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
that map
\mathbb{E}
onto starlike and close-to-convex domains, respectively, are denoted by
\mathcal{S^{*}}
and
\mathcal{K}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{SH}^{0,*}
and
\mathcal{KH}^{0}
represent subclasses of
\mathcal{SH}^{0}
that map
\mathbb{E}
onto their respective domains. (For further details, refer to
[
1
,
3
]
)

In 2005, Gao and Zhou
[
4
]
introduced the class

\mathcal{K}_{s}=\left\{\mathfrak{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathcal{S}:\mathrm{Re}\left\{\frac{z^{2}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)}{-\phi(z)\phi(-z)}\right\}>0\text{ for }z\in\mathbb{E}\right\}.

In 2011, Şeker
[
5
]
introduced the class

\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)=\left\{\mathfrak{u}\in\mathcal{S}:\mathrm{Re}\left\{\frac{z^{k}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right\}>\gamma,\ \ \ 0\leq\gamma<1\text{ and }z\in\mathbb{E}\right\}

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
\phi(z)
is an analytic function of the form

\phi(z)=z+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}c_{m}z^{m}\in S^{*}\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right),

and
\phi_{k}(z)
, which was previously introduced by Wang et al.
[
24
]
, is defined as

(1.2)

\phi_{k}(z)=\prod_{v=0}^{k-1}\mu^{-v}\phi(\mu^{v}z),\quad\text{where}\quad\mu^{k}=1

with
\mu=e^{2\pi i/k}
.

The classes
\mathcal{K}_{s}
and
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
, introduced by Gao and Zhou
[
4
]
and Şeker
[
5
]
, respectively, focus solely on analytic functions and are independent of the variable
\overline{z}
. This restriction prevents the study of properties of harmonic functions that depend on the variable
\overline{z}
. This leads us to define a new function class that includes the co-analytic part of harmonic functions.

Observe that if
k=2
in the class
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
, the class
\mathcal{K}(\gamma)
studied by Kowalczyk et al.
[
2
]
is obtained. For
k=2
and
\gamma=0
, the class
\mathcal{K}_{s}
studied by Gao and Zhou
[
4
]
is obtained. It is clear that the class
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
encompasses both classes. The class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
, which we will define shortly, covers the class
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
since it is defined using harmonic conjugates of analytic functions belonging to the classes
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
. Therefore, the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
is a generalization of the
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
,
\mathcal{K}_{s}
, and
\mathcal{K}(\gamma)
classes.

This generalization allows for a broader investigation of geometric properties, such as distortion bounds and close-to-convexity, for harmonic functions that depend on
\overline{z}
.

Definition 1.1
.

The class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
is defined as the collection of functions
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{SH}^{0}
that adhere to the following inequality:

(1.3)

\mathrm{Re}\left\{\frac{z^{k}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}-\gamma\right\}>\left|\frac{z^{k}\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right|,

where
0\leq\gamma<1
and
\phi_{k}(z)
is given by (
1.2
).

Specifically, when
\mathfrak{v}(z)\equiv 0
, the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
reduces to the class
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
. Also, by setting
\mathfrak{v}(z)\equiv 0
,
k=2
and
\gamma=0
, we obtain
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(2,0)=\mathcal{K}_{s}
. The inclusion of the co-analytic term
\overline{\mathfrak{v}(z)}
extends these classes, providing a more general framework that accommodates both analytic and co-analytic components. Additionally, when
\phi(z)=z
, and by appropriately selecting the parameters, the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
can be reduced to several well-known subclasses of harmonic functions, as outlined below.

[

]
.

ii

\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)=\mathcal{PH}^{0}(\gamma)

.

(

(

3.
Geometric Properties of the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)

First, we give a result that establishes a sufficient condition for
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{SH}^{0}
to be close-to-convex, which comes from Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
1
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{v}
be analytic functions in
\mathbb{E}
, such that
|\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(0)|<|\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(0)|
, and for each
\varepsilon

(|\varepsilon|=1)
,
F_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}
is close-to-convex. Then,
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}
is close-to-convex in
\mathbb{E}
.

The result we will present now establishes a connection between the
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
harmonic function class and the
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
analytic function class.

Theorem 3.2
.

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
if and only if
F_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}\in\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
for each
\varepsilon\ (\left|\varepsilon\right|=1)
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
. For each
\varepsilon\ (\left|\varepsilon\right|=1)
, we have

\displaystyle{\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z^{k}{F_{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right\}

\displaystyle={\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z^{k}{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right\}+\varepsilon{\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z^{k}{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right\}

\displaystyle>{\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z^{k}{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right\}-\left|\frac{z^{k}{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right|>\gamma\text{ }\left(z\in\mathbb{E}\right).

Hence,
F_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
for each
\varepsilon\ (\left|\varepsilon\right|=1)
.

Conversely, suppose
F_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}\in\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
. Then,

{\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z^{k}{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right\}>{\text{Re}}\left[-\varepsilon\frac{z^{k}{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right]+\gamma\text{ }\left(z\in\mathbb{E}\right).

Choosing an appropriate
\varepsilon\ (\left|\varepsilon\right|=1)
yields

{\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z^{k}{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}-\gamma\right\}>\left|\frac{z^{k}{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right|\text{ }\left(z\in\mathbb{E}\right),

and thus
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
.
∎

We will now demonstrate that harmonic functions belonging to the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
map the open unit disk onto a close-to-convex region. To this end, we first state the following lemma and subsequently establish that functions in the class
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
are close-to-convex within the open unit disk.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
24
]

Let
\phi(z)=z+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}c_{m}z^{m}\in\mathcal{S^{*}}\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)
for
k\geq 1
. Then,

(3.1)

\Phi_{k}(z)=\frac{\phi_{k}(z)}{z^{k-1}}=z+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}C_{m}z^{m}\in S^{*}

where
\phi_{k}(z)
is given by (
1.2
).

Theorem 3.4
.

If
F
is a function in the class
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
, then
F
is close-to-convex of order
\gamma
in the region
\mathbb{E}
.

Proof.

Let
F\in\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
. We have

{\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z^{k}{F}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right\}={\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z{F}^{\prime}(z)}{\Phi_{k}(z)}\right\}>\gamma,

where
\Phi_{k}(z)
is given by (
3.1
). Therefore, the function
F
is close-to-convex of order
\gamma
since
\Phi_{k}(z)\in\mathcal{S^{*}}
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Every function in the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
is close-to-convex within the region
\mathbb{E}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}
belong to class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
. Then by Theorem
3.2
the function
F_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}
belongs to class
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
and by Theorem
3.4
also close to convex in
\mathbb{E}
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.1
,
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
is also close to convex in
\mathbb{E}
.
∎

In the following result, we derive a coefficient bound for functions belonging to the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
. For
m\geq 2
, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\ \left|u_{m}\right|+\left|v_{m}\right|\leq\gamma+m(1-\gamma).

For the function
\mathfrak{f}(z)=z+[\gamma+m(1-\gamma)]z^{m}
, every outcome is sharp and every equality is holds.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
.
F_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}\in\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)\
for
\varepsilon

\left(\left|\varepsilon\right|=1\right)
, according to Theorem
3.2
. With respect to every
\varepsilon\ (\left|\varepsilon\right|=1),
we possess

{\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z^{k}{F_{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right\}={\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z{F_{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}(z)}{\Phi_{k}(z)}\right\}={\text{Re}}\left\{\frac{z\left({\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}(z)+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)\right)}{\Phi_{k}(z)}\right\}>\gamma.

for
z\in\mathbb{E}
. On the other hand, there is an analytic function
\mathfrak{P}(z)=1+\underset{m=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\mathfrak{p}_{m}z^{m}
in
\mathbb{E}
whose real part is positive, satisfying

(3.2)

\frac{z\left({\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}(z)+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)\right)}{\Phi_{k}(z)}=\gamma+\left(1-\gamma\right)\mathfrak{P}(z).

or

(3.3)

{z\left({\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}(z)+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)\right)}=\left[\gamma+\left(1-\gamma\right)\mathfrak{P}(z)\right]\Phi_{k}(z).

Upon comparing the coefficients in (
3.3
), it can be observed that

(3.4)

m(u_{m}+\varepsilon v_{m})=C_{m}+(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{p}_{m-1}+(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{p}_{1}C_{m-1}+\dots+(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{p}_{m-2}C_{2}.

Since
\Phi_{k}(z)
is starlike, we have
\left|C_{m}\right|\leq m
, and since
Re\{\mathfrak{P}(z)\}>0
, we have
\left|\mathfrak{p}_{m}\right|\leq 2
for
m\geq 1
. Hence, by equation
3.4
, we have

(3.5)

m\left|u_{m}+\varepsilon v_{m}\right|\leq m\left[\gamma+m(1-\gamma)\right].

Since
\varepsilon\left(\left|\varepsilon\right|=1\right)
is arbitrary, it follows that the proof is concluded. The function
\mathfrak{f}(z)=z+

[\gamma+m(1-\gamma)]z^{m}
,
demonstrates the sharpness of inequality.
∎

Now, we provide the necessary coefficient condition for a harmonic function to belong to the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{SH}^{0}
with the series expansions given by (
1.1
). If the following inequality holds:

(3.6)

\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}2m\left(\left|u_{m}\right|+\left|v_{m}\right|\right)+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}\left(\left|1-2\gamma\right|+1\right)\left|C_{m}\right|\leq 2(1-\gamma),

then
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
.

Proof.

Consider
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{v}
as functions with series expansions given by (
1.1
). Let
F_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}
for each
\varepsilon\ (\left|\varepsilon\right|=1)
. Define the functions
\phi_{k}(z)
and
\Phi_{k}(z)
as given by (
1.2
) and (
3.1
), respectively. Then we can express
A
as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}-\frac{\phi_{k}(z)}{z^{k-1}}\right|-\left|zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}+\frac{(1-2\gamma)\phi_{k}(z)}{z^{k-1}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|z(\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v})^{\prime}-\Phi_{k}(z)\right|-\left|z(\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v})^{\prime}+(1-2\gamma)\Phi_{k}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}m(u_{m}+\varepsilon v_{m})z^{m}-\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}C_{m}z^{m}\right|

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left|(2-2\gamma)z+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}m(u_{m}+\varepsilon v_{m})z^{m}+(1-2\gamma)\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}C_{m}z^{m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}m\left|u_{m}+\varepsilon v_{m}\right|\left|z\right|^{m}+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\left|C_{m}\right|\left|z\right|^{m}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left((2-2\gamma)\left|z\right|-\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}m\left|u_{m}+\varepsilon v_{m}\right|\left|z\right|^{m}-\left|1-2\gamma\right|\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\left|C_{m}\right|\left|z\right|^{m}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left\{-2(1-\gamma)+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}2m\left|u_{m}+\varepsilon v_{m}\right|+\left(\left|1-2\gamma\right|+1\right)\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\left|C_{m}\right|\right\}\left|z\right|

Since
\varepsilon\left(\left|\varepsilon\right|=1\right)
is arbitrary, and from the inequality (
3.6
), we obtain that
A<0
. This implies that
F_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}
belongs to the class
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
, and consequently, according to Theorem 2, it shows that
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}
belongs to the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
.
∎

The result we present now provides the distortion bounds for functions in the class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assuming
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
, the following inequalities hold for all
z
:

\left|z\right|+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{m-1}[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|z\right|^{m}\leq\left|\mathfrak{f}(z)\right|\leq\left|z\right|+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|z\right|^{m}.

These are sharp inequality for the function
\mathfrak{f}(z)=z+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]z^{m}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{u}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma).
Then using Theorem
3.2
,

F_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{u}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}\in\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)\
for each
\varepsilon\ (\left|\varepsilon\right|=1).
Additionally, from Theorem
3.6
in
[
5
]
, we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1-(1-2\gamma)\left|z\right|}{(1+\left|z\right|)^{3}}\leq\left|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq\frac{1+(1-2\gamma)\left|z\right|}{(1-\left|z\right|)^{3}}

Since

\displaystyle\left|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}m[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|z\right|^{m-1}

and

\displaystyle\left|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)+\varepsilon\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{m-1}m[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|z\right|^{m-1},

in particular, we get

\left|\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)\right|+\left|\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq 1+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}m[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|z\right|^{m-1}

and

\left|\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)\right|-\left|\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)\right|\geq 1+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{m-1}m[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|z\right|^{m-1}.

Assume
\Gamma
is the radial segment extending from
0
to
z
, so

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{f}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int\limits_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial\mathfrak{f}}{\partial\mathfrak{z}}d\mathfrak{z}+\frac{\partial\mathfrak{f}}{\partial\bar{\mathfrak{z}}}d\bar{\mathfrak{z}}\right|\leq\int\limits_{\Gamma}\left(\left|\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{z})\right|+\left|\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{z})\right|\right)\left|d\mathfrak{z}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\left|z\right|}\left(1+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}m[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|\rho\right|^{m-1}\right)d\mathfrak{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|z\right|+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|z\right|^{m},

and

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{f}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Gamma}\left(\left|\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{z})\right|-\left|\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{z})\right|\right)\left|d\mathfrak{z}\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\left|z\right|}\left(1+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{m-1}m[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|\rho\right|^{m-1}\right)d\mathfrak{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|z\right|+\sum\limits_{m=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{m-1}[m(1-\gamma)+\gamma]\left|z\right|^{m}.

∎

Theorem 3.9
.

The class
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
is closed under convex combinations.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{f}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}+\overline{\mathfrak{v}_{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
for
\alpha=1,2,...,p
and
\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p}s_{\alpha}=1

(0\leq s_{\alpha}\leq 1).
The convex combination
of functions
\mathfrak{f}_{\alpha}

\left(\alpha=1,2,...,p\right)
can be expressed as:

\mathfrak{f}(z)=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p}s_{\alpha}\mathfrak{f}_{\alpha}(z)=\mathfrak{u}(z)+\overline{\mathfrak{v}(z)},

where

\mathfrak{u}(z)=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p}s_{\alpha}\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}(z)\text{ \ and \ }\mathfrak{v}(z)=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p}s_{\alpha}\mathfrak{v}_{\alpha}(z).

Both
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{v}
are analytic within the open unit disk
\mathbb{E}
, satisfying initial conditions
\mathfrak{u}(0)=\mathfrak{v}(0)=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(0)-1=\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(0)=0
and

\displaystyle{\text{Re}}\left[\frac{z^{k}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}-\gamma\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\text{Re}}\left[\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p}s_{\alpha}\left(\frac{z^{k}\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}-\gamma\right)\right]>\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p}s_{\alpha}\left|\frac{z^{k}\mathfrak{v}_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right|=\left|\frac{z^{k}\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(z)}{\phi_{k}(z)}\right|

showing that
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
.
∎

4.
Conclusions

In this paper, we introduced a new class of harmonic functions denoted by
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
. We established a relationship between
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
and
\mathcal{K}(k,\gamma)
. We demonstrated that
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
is close-to-convex. For functions in the
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
class, we derived coefficient bounds and distortion theorems. Finally, we proved that
\mathcal{KH}^{0}(k,\gamma)
is closed under convolution.

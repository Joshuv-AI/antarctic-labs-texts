Holonomy of K-contact sub-Riemannian manifolds Thanks: 1 University of Hradec Králové, Faculty of Science, Department of Mathematics, Rokitanského 62, 500 03 Hradec Králové, Czech Republic
Anton S. Galaev

Abstract
. It is shown that the horizontal holonomy group of a K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold either coincides with the holonomy group of a Riemannian manifold, or it is a codimension-one normal subgroup of the later group. This implies the classification of the holonomy algebras of K-contact sub-Riemannian manifolds. The question of existence of parallel horizontal spinors, examples, and consequences are discussed. Keywords : holonomy; horizontal connection; contact manifold; sub-Riemannian manifold; Schouten connection; Sasaki manifold; parallel spinor AMS Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 53C29; 53C17; 15A66

1.
Introduction

Let
D
be a distribution on a smooth manifold
M
. A horizontal connection
\nabla
defines parallel transport of vectors tangent to
D
along curves tangent to
D
. This gives rise to the so-called horizontal holonomy. For the first time the horizontal holonomy was investigated in the contact sub-Riemannian case
[
6
]
. Later this notion was generalized and studied in several works, e.g., in
[
4
,
9
,
11
,
13
]
.

In the present paper we study holonomy of K-contact sub-Riemannian manifolds
(M,\theta,g)
. Here
M
is a smooth manifold with a contact form
\theta
, and
g
is a sub-Riemannian metric on the contact distribution
D=\ker\theta
. We assume that the Reeb vector field
\xi
is complete and its flow preserves the metric
g
.

In Section
2
, we recall the definition of Schouten connection
\nabla
, which is the horizontal Levi-Civita connection on a sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,D,g)
. In Section
3
, we discuss the extensions of the horizontal connection
\nabla
to connections on the vector bundle
D
. Among these extensions are the adapted connection
\nabla^{0}
and the Wagner connection
\nabla^{W}
. The connections
\nabla^{W}
and
\nabla^{0}
may be extended to metric connections with torsion on
M
, and their holonomy may be interesting also from this point of view, see
[
1
]
.

In Section
4
, we recall some facts about the horizontal holonomy from
[
6
,
11
]
. In particular, the holonomy of the connections
\nabla
and
\nabla^{W}
coincide, while the holonomy of the connection
\nabla
is contained in the holonomy of the connection
\nabla^{0}
.
The Reeb vector field
\xi
defines a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
M
(the characteristic foliation), by this reason, in Section
5
, we discuss transverse structures on foliations. In Section
6
, we compute the holonomy of the connection in the line bundle

\left<\xi\right>
defined by the contact form
\theta
.

In Section
7
, we compare the holonomy groups of the connections
\nabla
and
\nabla^{0}
. We prove the main result of the paper stating that either these groups coincide, or the holonomy group of
\nabla
is a codimension-one subgroup of the holonomy group of
\nabla^{0}
. In Section
8
, we study the second possibility in the case of simply connected spaces. We show that this possibility is equivalent to the existence of a transverse Kähler structure on the characteristic foliation and a relation between the 2-form
d\theta
and the transverse Ricci form.

Section
9
deals with examples and consequences of the results of Section
8
. We give a construction illustrating the results from Section
8
, we consider the special case of Sasaki manifolds, and we discuss the relation of the holonomy of the connection
\nabla
to the existence of parallel horizontal spinors.

2.
Horizontal connection

Let
D
be a distribution on a smooth manifold
M
. A piece-wise smooth curve in
M
is called
horizontal
if it is tangent to
D
.

A horizontal connection
on the distribution
D
is a map

(1)

\nabla:\Gamma(D)\times\Gamma(D)\to\Gamma(D)

sharing the usual properties of the covariant derivative. For a given horizontal connection
\nabla
, a horizontal curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
defines the parallel transport

(2)

\tau_{\gamma}:D_{\gamma(a)}\to D_{\gamma(b)}.

along
\gamma
.
Let
g
be a sub-Riemannian metric, i.e., a field of non-degenerate positively definite symmetric bilinear forms on the distribution
D
. Suppose that a rigging
D^{\prime}
is fixed, i.e., there is a decomposition

TM=D\oplus D^{\prime}

into the direct sum of the distributions.
Let

\pi:\Gamma(TM)\to\Gamma(D),\quad\pi^{\prime}:\Gamma(TM)\to\Gamma(D^{\prime})

be the corresponding projections.
The metric
g
and the rigging
D^{\prime}
define the Schouten connection that is the horizontal Levi-Civita connection given by the Koszul formula

(3)

2g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(Z,X)-Zg(X,Y)+g(\pi[X,Y],Z)-g(\pi[Y,Z],X)-g(\pi[X,Z],Y),

where
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(D)
.
This is the unique connection preserving the metric
g
and having zero torsion in the sense that

(4)

\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-\pi[X,Y]=0,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(D).

The curvature tensor of a connection
\nabla
was defined by Schouten
in the following way:

(5)

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{\pi[X,Y]}Z-\pi[\pi^{\prime}[X,Y],Z],\quad\forall X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(D).

The Schouten tensor satisfies the Bianchi identity

(6)

R(X,Y)Z+R(Y,Z)X+R(Z,X)Y=0,\quad\forall X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(D).

3.
Extended connections

Let, as in the previous section,
\nabla
be a horizontal connection on a distribution
D
.
An extended connection
is a connection

\nabla^{D}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(D)\to\Gamma(D)

on the vector bundle
D
such that

\nabla^{D}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(D).

Wagner
[
15
]
noted that the flatness of a horizontal connection is generally not equivalent to the vanishing of its Schouten tensor, and he constructed a special extended connection such that its curvature vanishes if and only if the horizontal connection is flat. The extended connection and its curvature tensor are called nowadays the Wagner connection and the Wagner curvature tensor, see
[
5
]
for the modern exposition.

Now we start to consider the case of contact sub-Riemannian manifolds.
Assume that the distribution
D
coincides with the kernel of a fixed contact form
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Denote by
\xi
the corresponding Reeb vector field on
M
defined by the conditions

\theta(\xi)=1,\quad\iota_{X}d\theta=0.

We assume that
\dim M=2m+1\geq 5
.

We denote by the triple
(M,\theta,g)

a contact manifold
(M,\theta)
with a sub-Riemannian metric
g
on the contact distribution and call it
a contact sub-Riemannian manifold
.

An arbitrary extended connection is uniquely defined by a field
N
of endomorphisms of
D
and satisfies the conditions

(7)

\nabla^{N}_{\xi}X=[\xi,X]+NX,\quad\nabla^{N}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(D).

The curvature tensor of the connection
\nabla^{N}
is given by

(8)

\displaystyle R^{N}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=R(X,Y)+d\theta(X,Y)N,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(D)

(9)

\displaystyle R^{N}(\xi,X)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\nabla)_{X}-\nabla_{X}N,\quad\forall X\in\Gamma(D),

see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
]
. Here
R
is the Schouten tensor defined in the previous section.

The extended connection given by
N
satisfying

g(NX,Y)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g)(X,Y),\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(D),

is called
the adapted connection
, see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.

For vector fields
X,Y\in\Gamma(D)
we define the bivector

X\wedge Y
as the tensor field

X\wedge Y=X\otimes Y-Y\otimes X.

The module of all bivectors is denoted by
\Gamma(\wedge^{2}D)
. For a 2-form

\omega
on
D
with values in a vector bundle over
M
, we set

\omega(X\wedge Y)=2\omega(X,Y).

This allows us to consider the values
\omega(\alpha)
of the 2-form
\omega
on any bivector
\alpha\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}D)
.

The Wagner connection
\nabla^{W}
in the case of a contact sub-Riemannian manifold is defined in the following way, see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
]
.
Denote by
(d\theta)^{-1}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}D)
the bivector such that its matrix of coordinates is the inverse to the matrix of coordinates of the form
d\theta
with respect to any local frame of
D
. It holds

(10)

d\theta((d\theta)^{-1})=-4m.

The Wagner connection
[
15
,
16
]
is defined by the condition on its curvature tensor

(11)

R^{W}((d\theta)^{-1})=0.

The corresponding field
N
is given by

(12)

N=\frac{1}{4m}R((d\theta)^{-1}).

This extended connection is a particular
special extension
defined in
[
6
]
. For the case of CR-manifolds such connection was constructed in
[
12
]
, where it was called a basic connection.

A contact sub-Riemannian manifold is called
K-contact
if
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g=0
. It is clear that in this case the corresponding adapted connection is given by the choice
N=0
. We denote this connection by
\nabla^{0}
. It holds

(13)

\nabla^{0}_{\xi}X=[\xi,X],\quad\nabla^{0}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(D).

The curvature tensor of this connection is given by

(14)

R^{0}(X,Y)=R(X,Y),\quad R^{0}(\xi,X)=0,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(D).

The curvature tensor of the Wagner connection in the K-contact case satisfies

(15)

R^{W}(X,Y)=R(X,Y)+d\theta(X,Y)N,\quad R^{W}(\xi,X)=-\nabla_{X}N,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(D),

where
N
is given by (
12
).

4.
Horizontal holonomy

Given a distribution
D
on a manifold
M
, a horizontal connection
\nabla
as in (
1
), and a point
x\in M
, one defines the
(horizontal) holonomy group
of the connection
\nabla
as the group of parallel transports along piece-wise smooth horizontal loops at the point
x\in M
. We denote this group by
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
. The
restricted holonomy group

\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla)
is the subgroup of

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
corresponding to contactable loops. Note that if the distribution
D
is bracket-generating, then any contractable horizontal loop is contactable in the class of horizontal loops, see, e.g.,
[
4
]
. The holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is a Lie subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(D_{x})
, and the restricted holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla)
is the identity component of
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
,
[
4
,
6
,
11
]
.
The corresponding Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(D_{x})
is called
the holonomy algebra
and is denoted by
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
. The parallel transport along a horizontal curve defines the isomorphism of the holonomy at the initial and the end-point of the curve, so the holonomy is independent on the point.

Consider now a contact sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,\theta,g)
as in Section
3
.
The following are versions of the Ambrose-Singer holonomy Theorem for the horizontal connection
[
6
]
, see also
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is spanned by
the following endomorphisms of
D_{x}
:

\tau_{\gamma}^{-1}\circ R(\alpha)\circ\tau_{\gamma},

where
\gamma
is an arbitrary piece-wise smooth horizontal curve starting at
x
, and
\alpha
is a bivector from
D
at the end-point of
\gamma
such that
d\theta(\alpha)=0
.

Theorem 2
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is spanned by
the following endomorphisms of
D_{x}
:

\tau_{\gamma}^{-1}\circ R^{W}(X,Y)\circ\tau_{\gamma},

where
\gamma
is an arbitrary piece-wise smooth horizontal curve starting at
x
, and
X
,
Y
are vectors from
D
at the end-point of
\gamma
.

In
[
4
]
, was introduced a notion of
selector
for a class of distributions. For a contact distribution
D
defined by the kernel of a 1-form
\theta
, a selector is a bivector
\alpha\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}D)
such that the function
d\theta(\alpha)
is non-vanishing. From
[
4
, Theorem 2.16]
it follows

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold and let
\nabla^{N}
be an extension of the Schouten connection
\nabla
. Suppose that there is a selector
\alpha
of
D
such that
R^{N}(\alpha)=0
, then
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)=\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})
.

This theorem, (
10
), and (
11
) imply

Theorem 4
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold. Then the horizontal holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)
coincides with the holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{W})
of the Wagner connection.

5.
Transverse structures of foliations

In this section we follow the exposition from
[
10
]
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on a smooth manifold
M
. Denote by
L\subset TM
the distribution tangent to
\mathcal{F}
. Let
Q=TM/L
be the normal bundle of
\mathcal{F}
.
A transverse tensor field
on
\mathcal{F}
is a tensor field
A
on the bundle
Q
satisfying the condition

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A=0,\quad\forall\xi\in\Gamma(L).

Using an auxiliary Riemannian metric on
M
, one may identify
Q
with a distribution on
M
such that it holds

TM=L\oplus Q.

Consider the corresponding projections

\pi:TM\to Q
and
\pi^{\prime}:TM\to L
. Equality (
3
) with
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(Q)
defines the horizontal Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
Q
.
The transversal Levi-Civita connection
is the connection on the vector bundle
Q
defined by

\nabla^{T}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y,\quad\nabla^{T}_{\xi}Y=\pi[\xi,Y],\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(Q),\quad\xi\in\Gamma(L).

The holonomy of the connection
\nabla^{T}
is called
the transverse
holonomy. It is clear that a tensor field
A
defined on
Q
is transverse if and only if

\nabla^{T}_{\xi}A=0,\quad\forall\xi\in\Gamma(L).

In particular, each
\nabla^{T}
-parallel tensor field on
Q
is a transverse tensor field.

A transverse Kähler structure
on
\mathcal{F}
is a transverse Hermitian structure
J
on
(Q,g)
parallel
with respect to the transversal connection
\nabla^{T}
.
A transverse Kähler structure exists if and only if
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{T})
is contained in
\operatorname{U}(m)
, where
\operatorname{rk}Q=2m
. The Ricci form of a transverse Kähler structure is defined as

\rho^{T}(X,Y)=\mathrm{Ric}^{T}(JX,Y),\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(Q).

Here
\mathrm{Ric}^{T}
is the transverse Ricci tensor
defined by

\mathrm{Ric}^{T}(X,Y)=\sum_{k=1}^{2m}g(R^{T}(e_{k},X)Y,e_{k}),

where
e_{1},\dots,e_{2m}
is an arbitrary local
g
-orthonormal frame on
Q
.
The Ricci form
\rho^{T}
coincides with the induced connection
on the bundle
K
of complex
m
-forms. For a transversal Kähler structure, the equality
\rho^{T}=0
is equivalent to the condition
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{T})\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold. Then the Reeb vector field
\xi
defines a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{F}
. The contact distribution
D
may be identified with the normal bundle of
\mathcal{F}
, the metric
g
is a transverse metric on
\mathcal{F}
, and the adapted connection
\nabla^{0}
coincides with the transversal connection
\nabla^{T}
.

6.
The connection
\nabla^{\theta}

Let
(M,\theta)
be a contact manifold.
Using the 1-form
\theta
, we define the connection
\nabla^{\theta}
on the trivial line bundle
\left<\xi\right>
over
M
by setting

\nabla^{\theta}_{X}\xi=\theta(X)\xi,\quad X\in\Gamma(TM).

It is clear that the curvature of the connection
\nabla^{\theta}
coincides with
d\theta
, i.e.,

R^{\theta}(X,Y)\xi=(d\theta(X,Y))\xi,\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

Let us compute the parallel transport for the connection
\nabla^{\theta}
. Let
\mu(t)
,
t\in[a,b]
, be a piece-wise smooth curve in
M
. Let
\lambda(t)\xi_{\gamma(t)}
be a section of the bundle
\left<\xi\right>
along the curve
\gamma(t)
. The parallel transport equation takes the form

\dot{\lambda}(t)+\lambda(t)\theta(\dot{\mu}(t))=0.

The solution of this equation is

\lambda(t)=\lambda(a)\exp\left(-\int_{a}^{t}\theta(\dot{\mu}(s))ds\right).

Consequently, if the curve
\mu(t)
is closed, then the parallel transport along
\mu(t)
is given by

\lambda(b)=\lambda(a)\exp\left(-\int_{\mu}\theta\right).

This implies that the holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\theta})
is isomorphic to the subgroup
\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathbb{R})
.

7.
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)
versus
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{0})

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold. Denote by
\nabla^{0}
the extended connection given, as in Section
3
, by
N=0
. We assume that the Reeb vector field
\xi
is complete and we denote its flow by
\varphi_{t}
. In this section we compare the holonomy groups
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
and
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
at a fixed point
x\in M
.

If the orbit
\varphi_{t}(x)
of the point
x
is cyclic, then we denote by
t_{0}
the smallest positive
t_{0}
such that
\varphi_{t_{0}}(x)=x
. Otherwise we assume that
t_{0}=0
.

Let

\mu:[a,b]\to M

be a piece-wise curve. We define the new curve

(16)

\tilde{\mu}(t)=\varphi_{f(t)}\mu(t),\quad t\in[a,b],

where

f(t)=-\int_{a}^{t}\theta(\dot{\mu}(s))ds.

Lemma 1
.

The curve
\tilde{\mu}
is horizontal.

Proof
. Differentiating (
16
), we get

\dot{\tilde{\mu}}(t)=\dot{f}(t)\xi_{\varphi_{f(t)}\mu(t)}+(d\varphi_{f(t)})\dot{\mu}(t)=-\theta(\dot{\mu}(t))\xi_{\varphi_{f(t)}\mu(t)}+(d\varphi_{f(t)})\dot{\mu}(t),

where

d\varphi_{f(t)}:T_{\mu(t)}M\to T_{\varphi_{f(t)}\mu(t)}M

is the differential of the diffeomorphism
\varphi_{f(t)}
. Since the flow
\varphi
preserves the vector field
\xi
, it holds

\xi_{\varphi_{f(t)}\mu(t)}=d\varphi_{f(t)}\xi_{\mu(t)}.

This implies that

\dot{\tilde{\mu}}(t)=(d\varphi_{f(t)})\left(\dot{\mu}(t)-\theta(\dot{\mu}(t))\xi_{\mu(t)}\right).

Since
\theta(\xi)=1
, the vector
\dot{\mu}(t)-\theta(\dot{\mu}(t))\xi_{\mu(t)}
is horizontal. Since
\varphi_{t}
preserves the contact distribution
D
, we get that the vector
\dot{\tilde{\mu}}(t)
is horizontal. ∎

Since the flow
\varphi_{t}
consists of automorphism of the distribution
D
, and
L_{\xi}g=0
, we see that the flow
\varphi_{t}
consists of automorphisms of the connection
\nabla
. We will need also the following statement.

Lemma 2
.

The flow
\varphi_{t}
consists of automorphism of the connection
\nabla^{0}
.

Proof.
It is enough to prove that
(d\varphi_{t})\nabla^{0}_{\xi}X=\nabla^{0}_{(d\varphi_{t})\xi}(d\varphi_{t})X
for all
X\in\Gamma(D)
. We have

(d\varphi_{t})\nabla^{0}_{\xi}X=(d\varphi_{t})[\xi,X]=[(d\varphi_{t})\xi,(d\varphi_{t})X]=[\xi,(d\varphi_{t})X]=\nabla^{0}_{\xi}(d\varphi_{t})X=\nabla^{0}_{(d\varphi_{t})\xi}(d\varphi_{t})X.

∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
X(t)
be a
\nabla^{0}
-parallel horizontal vector field along a curve
\mu(t)
.
Then the vector field

\tilde{X}(t)=(d\varphi_{f(t)})X(t)

is horizontal and it is
\nabla
-parallel along the curve
\tilde{\mu}(t)
.

Proof.
Consider the subset

W\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

consisting of the pairs
(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
t\in[a,b]
, and

r\in\left\{\begin{matrix}[0,f(t)],\text{if }f(t)\geq 0,\\
[f(t),0],\text{if }f(t)<0.\end{matrix}\right.

Consider the map

F:W\to M

defined by

F(t,r)=\varphi_{r}\mu(t).

Define the vector field

Y(t,r)=(d\varphi_{r})X(t)

along
F
.
It is obvious that

\mu(t)=F(t,0),\quad\tilde{\mu}(t)=F(t,f(t)),

X(t)=Y(t,0),\quad\tilde{X}(t)=Y(t,f(t)).

From Lemma
2
and the definition of the vector field
Y
it follows that

\nabla^{0}_{\xi_{F(t,r)}}Y(t,r)=0.

In particular,

\nabla^{0}_{\xi_{\mu(t)}}Y(t,0)=0.

Finally,

\nabla_{\dot{\tilde{\mu}}(t)}\tilde{X}(t)=\nabla_{(d\varphi_{f(t)})\left(\dot{\mu}(t)-\theta(\dot{\mu}(t))\xi_{\mu(t)}\right)}(d\varphi_{f(t)})X(t)=\nabla_{\dot{\mu}(t)-\theta(\dot{\mu}(t))\xi_{\mu(t)}}X(t)\\
=\nabla^{0}_{\dot{\mu}(t)-\theta(\dot{\mu}(t))\xi_{\mu(t)}}Y(t,0)=\nabla^{0}_{\dot{\mu}(t)}Y(t,0)-\theta(\dot{\mu}(t))\nabla^{0}_{\xi_{\mu(t)}}Y(t,0)=\nabla^{0}_{\dot{\mu}(t)}X(t)=0.

∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mu:[a,b]\to M
be a piece-wise smooth curve in
M
, and let
\tilde{\mu}:[a,b]\to M
be the corresponding horizontal curve. Suppose that
\tilde{\mu}(b)=\mu(b)
. Then the curves
\tilde{\mu}
and
\mu
are homotopic, and the parallel transport of the connection
\nabla^{0}
along the curve
\mu
coincides with the parallel transport of the connection
\nabla
along the curve
\tilde{\mu}
, i.e.,

\tau^{0}_{\mu}=\tau_{\tilde{\mu}}.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a loop at the point
x
. Then the curve
\tilde{\mu}
is also a loop at the point
x
if and only if

(17)

\int_{\mu}\theta\in\mathbb{Z}t_{0}.

Proof.
Let
\mu:[a,b]\to M
be a loop at the point
x
. By the definition of the curve
\tilde{\mu}
, it holds

\tilde{\mu}(b)=\varphi_{c}x,

where

c=-\int_{\mu}\theta.

From this and the definition of
t_{0}
it follows that the condition
\tilde{\mu}(b)=x
is equivalent to the condition
c\in\mathbb{Z}t_{0}
. ∎

From
Corollary
1
and Lemma
4
it follows

Corollary 2
.

If
\mu
is a loop at the point
x
satisfying (
17
), then
\tau^{0}_{\mu}\in\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
.

Corollary
2
and Lemma
4
imply

Proposition 1
.

An element
h\in\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
may be represented by a loop satisfying (
17
) if and only if
h\in\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
, i.e.,
it holds

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\left\{h\in\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})\left|\exists\mu:\tau^{0}_{\mu}=h,\int_{\mu}\theta\in\mathbb{Z}t_{0}\right\}\right..

Proposition 2
.

The Lie subgroup
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subset\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
is normal.

Proof.
Let
\mu
be a horizontal loop at the point
x
, and let
\gamma
be an arbitrary loop at
x
. Then

\int_{\gamma\mu\gamma^{-1}}\theta=\int_{\gamma}\theta+\int_{\mu}\theta+\int_{\gamma^{-1}}\theta=\int_{\mu}\theta=0,

which implies that

(\tau^{0}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tau_{\mu}\tau^{0}_{\gamma}=(\tau^{0}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tau^{0}_{\mu}\tau^{0}_{\gamma}=\tau^{0}_{\gamma\mu\gamma^{-1}}\in\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla).

∎

Let
\gamma
and
\mu
be two loops at the point
x
satisfying the condition

\int_{\mu}\theta=\int_{\gamma}\theta+kt_{0},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then it holds that
\tau^{0}_{\mu\gamma^{-1}}\in\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
. This shows that the maps

\lambda:\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\theta})\to\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})/\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla),\quad\lambda^{\prime}:\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\theta})/e^{\mathbb{Z}t_{0}}\to\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})/\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla),

\lambda:\exp\left(-\int_{\mu}\theta\right)\mapsto\tau^{0}_{\mu}\cdot\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla),\quad\lambda^{\prime}:\exp\left(-\int_{\mu}\theta\right)\,\,\cdot e^{\mathbb{Z}t_{0}}\mapsto\tau^{0}_{\mu}\cdot\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)

are well-defined. It is obvious that the both maps are surjective Lie group homomorphisms.

We have proved the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold. Suppose that the Reeb vector field
\xi
is complete. Then one of following conditions holds:

1.

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
;

2.

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subset\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
is a normal subgroup of codimension one.

If the condition 2 holds true, then the factor group
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})/\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is connected, and it holds

(18)

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)/\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla)=\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})/\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla^{0}).

Suppose that
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subset\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
is a normal Lie subgroup of codimension one, i.e.,

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subset\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
is an ideal of codimension one. Since the subalgebra
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})\subset\mathfrak{so}(2m)
is compact, there exists a complementary one-dimensional ideal
\mathfrak{t}_{x}
, i.e., it holds

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})=\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{x}.

The holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})\subset\mathfrak{so}(2m)
may be decomposed as the direct sum

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})=\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0}))

of the commutator
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})^{\prime}
(the semisimple part of
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
) and the center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0}))
. It is obvious that

\mathfrak{t}_{x}\subset\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})).

Let

\mathfrak{t}_{x}^{\bot}\subset\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0}))

be the orthogonal complement to
\mathfrak{t}_{x}
in
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0}))

with respect to the extension of
g_{x}
to
\mathfrak{so}(2m)
(which is proportional to the Cartan-Killing form of
\mathfrak{so}(2m)
). We thus conclude that

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{x}^{\bot}.

Let
T_{x}\subset\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
be the connected normal Lie subgroup corresponding to the ideal
\mathfrak{t}_{x}\subset\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
, then it holds

\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla^{0})=\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla)\cdot T_{x}.

This and (
18
) imply that

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})=\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)\cdot T_{x}.

8.
Classification

Let, as above,
(M,\theta,g)
be a K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold with the complete Reeb vector field
\xi
.

Proposition 3
.

The connected Lie group
\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla^{0})\subset\operatorname{SO}(D_{x},g_{x})=\operatorname{SO}(2m)
is the holonomy group of the Levi-Civita connection of a Riemannian manifold.

Proof.
The connection
\nabla^{0}
may be extended to a linear connection on
M
by setting
\nabla^{0}\xi=0
. It is clear that the curvature and holonomy of this extension coincide with the initial
R^{0}
and
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
, respectively.
From the classical Ambrose-Singer holonomy Theorem for linear connections, (
6
), and (
14
) it follows that
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})\subset\mathfrak{so}(2m)
is a Berger subalgebra, which means that it is spanned by the images of algebraic curvature tensors
[
3
]
. Each Berger subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}(2m)
is the holonomy algebra of a Riemannian manifold
[
3
]
. This implies the statement of the proposition. ∎

In the rest of this section we will be interested in the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
, so we will assume that the manifold
M
is simply connected.

By Proposition
3
,
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})\subset\mathfrak{so}(D_{x})
is the holonomy algebra of a Riemannian manifold.
According to the de Rham decomposition Theorem, there exists an
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
-invariant orthogonal decomposition of the vector space
D_{x}
,

(19)

D_{x}=D^{0}_{x}\oplus D_{x}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D_{x}^{r}

and the decomposition of
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
into the direct sum of ideals

(20)

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})=\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{r}

such that
\mathfrak{h}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{so}(D_{x}^{i})
is an irreducible Riemannian holonomy algebra for
i=1,\dots,r
, and
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
annihilates
D^{0}_{x}
.
According to the holonomy principle, the
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
-invariant subspaces
D_{x}^{i}\subset D_{x}
define
\nabla^{0}
-parallel distributions
D^{i}
over
M
.

Fix an
i\in\{0,\dots,r\}
and consider the distribution

L_{i}=\left<\xi\right>\oplus D^{0}\oplus D^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D^{i-1}\oplus D^{i+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D^{r}.

It holds that

TM=L_{i}\oplus D^{i}.

From the fact that the distributions
D^{j}
are
\nabla^{0}
-parallel, (
4
), and (
13
) it follows that the distribution
L_{i}
is involutive. Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{i}
the corresponding foliation on
M
. The distribution
D^{i}
may be identified with the transversal distribution to
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. From the fact that the distributions
D^{j}
are
\nabla^{0}
-parallel and (
4
) it follows that the restriction
g_{D^{i}}
of
g
to
D^{i}
is a transversal metric on the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. The corresponding transversal Levi-Civita connection coincides with the connection on
D^{i}
induced by
\nabla^{0}
, and its holonomy algebra coincides with
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
.

Suppose that, for some
i
, it holds that
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is a subalgebra of the unitary algebra
\mathfrak{u}(D_{x}^{i})
. Then the exists a
\nabla^{0}
-parallel field
J^{i}
of complex structures on the distribution
D^{i}
, i.e., we obtain a Kähler transverse structure on the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Let
K_{i}
be the complex line bundle generated by the complex volume form
\Omega_{i}
associated to
(g_{D^{i}},J^{i})
. As we have seen in Section
5
, the curvature of the induced connection on
K_{i}
coincides with the Ricci form
\rho^{i}
, and the holonomy of this connection coincides with the projection of
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
to
\mathfrak{u}(1)=\mathbb{R}J^{i}_{x}
with respect to the decomposition
\mathfrak{u}(D_{x}^{i})=\mathfrak{su}(D_{x}^{i})\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a simply connected K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold with the complete Reeb vector field
\xi
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

the horizontal holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is a codimension-one ideal of
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
;

2.

it holds
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})\subset\mathfrak{u}(m)
, i.e., the Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
admits a transverse Kähler structure
J
; the space
D^{0}_{x}
from decomposition (
19
) is trivial; each algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
from decomposition (
20
) is contained in
\mathfrak{u}(D^{i}_{x})
and it contains
\mathfrak{u}(1)=\mathbb{R}J_{x}^{i}
, where
J_{x}^{i}={J_{x}}|_{D^{i}_{x}}
;
it holds

(21)

d\theta=b_{1}\rho^{1}+\cdots+b_{r}\rho^{r},\quad b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}.

Remark 1
.

If the conditions of Theorem
6
hold true, then the transverse Kähler structure
J
is not defined uniquely. In fact,
\mathcal{F}
admits
2^{r}
Kähler structures, namely, each of them is of the form

c_{1}J_{x}^{1}+\cdots+c_{r}J_{x}^{r},\quad c_{1},\dots,c_{r}\in\{-1,1\}.

Theorem 7
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a simply connected K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold with the complete Reeb vector field
\xi
. Suppose that the horizontal holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is a codimension-one ideal of
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
. Let
J
be a fixed transverse Kähler structure. Then there are decompositions

(22)

\displaystyle D_{x}

\displaystyle=D_{x}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D_{x}^{r},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{r},

(24)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{r}\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{x}^{\bot},

where, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
,

\mathfrak{h}_{i}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{i}\oplus\mathbb{R}J_{x}^{i}\subset\mathfrak{u}(D^{i}_{x}),\quad J_{x}^{i}={J_{x}}|_{D^{i}_{x}},

is an irreducible Riemannian holonomy algebra, and
\mathfrak{t}^{\bot}_{x}
is the orthogonal complement to a one-dimensional subalgebra

\mathfrak{t}_{x}=\mathbb{R}(a_{1}J_{x}^{1}+\cdots+a_{r}J_{x}^{r}),\quad a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},

in the commutative Lie algebra
\mathrm{span}\{J_{x}^{1},\dots,J_{x}^{r}\}
.

Remark 2
.

If
r\geq 2
, then the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
from Theorem
7
cannot be the holonomy algebra of a Riemannian manifold. Indeed,
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
preserves the decomposition (
22
), but, unlike
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
,
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is not the direct sum of subalgebras from
\mathfrak{so}(D_{x}^{i})
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
does not satisfy the de Rham decomposition theorem. If

r=1
, then
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})\subset\mathfrak{u}(m)
is an irreducible subalgebra. If
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})\neq\mathfrak{u}(m)
is the holonomy algebra of a symmetric Kähler space, then
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)\cap\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
is not a Riemannian holonomy algebra.
If
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})=\mathfrak{u}(m)
, then
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)
, and this is the only case when the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
from Theorem
7
is a Riemannian holonomy algebra.

Proof of Theorems
6
and
7
.

Suppose that the horizontal holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is a codimension-one ideal of
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
. Let
\mathfrak{t}_{x}
be as in Section
7
.
It is obvious that
\mathfrak{t}_{x}
is contained in the direct sum of the centers of the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
.
If a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{so}(D^{i}_{x})
has a non-trivial center, then
\mathfrak{h}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{u}(D_{x}^{i})
, and the center of
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
coincides with
\mathfrak{u}(1)=\mathbb{R}J^{i}_{x}
. Thus, (after a change of the enumeration in (
19
)) there exists an
s
,
1\leq s\leq r
such that

\mathfrak{t}_{x}=\mathbb{R}A_{x}\quad A_{x}=a_{1}J_{x}^{1}+\cdots+a_{s}J_{x}^{s},\quad a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}.

Let

A=a_{1}J^{1}+\cdots+a_{s}J^{s}.

Normalizing
A
, we may assume that

g(A,A)=1.

It is clear that the line bundle

\mathfrak{t}=\left<A\right>=\left<a_{1}J^{1}+\cdots+a_{s}J^{s}\right>

is
\nabla^{0}
-parallel.
Let
y\in M
be an arbitrary point and let
\mu
be a horizontal curve from
x
to
y
. As it is well-known,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mu}(\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla^{0})

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mu}^{0}(\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0}))=\tau_{\mu}(\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})).

We conclude that

(25)

\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla^{0})=\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla)\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{y}.

From the results of Section
3
it follows that

(26)

R^{W}(\alpha)=R^{0}(\alpha)+d\theta(\alpha)N,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}D),

where
N
is the field of endomorphism defining the Wagner connection, and
R^{W}
is the curvature tensor of the Wagner connection.
From Theorem
4
it follows that

R_{y}^{W}(\alpha)\in\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla),\quad\forall y\in M,\quad\forall\alpha\in\wedge^{2}D_{y}.

From this and (
25
) it follows that

\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{t}}(R^{0}(\alpha))+d\theta(\alpha)\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{t}}N=0,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}D),

where the projection is taken with respect to the decomposition (
25
).
Let

K:\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{2k}\to\mathfrak{u}(k)=\mathfrak{su}(k)\oplus\mathbb{R}J

be any algebraic curvature tensor, then it is easy to check that

(27)

\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb{R}J}R(\alpha)=-\frac{1}{k}\rho(\alpha)J,\quad\forall\alpha\in\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{2k}.

This implies that

\operatorname{pr}_{\left<J^{1}\right>\oplus\cdots\oplus\left<J^{s}\right>}(R^{0}(\alpha))=-\frac{1}{m_{1}}\rho^{1}(\alpha)J^{1}-\cdots-\frac{1}{m_{s}}\rho^{s}(\alpha)J^{s},\quad\alpha\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}D),

where the numbers
2m_{i}
are the ranks of the distributions
D^{i}
. Using this, we get

\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{t}}(R^{0}(\alpha))=g\left(-\frac{1}{m_{1}}\rho^{1}(\alpha)J^{1}-\cdots-\frac{1}{m_{s}}\rho^{s}(\alpha)J^{s},A\right)A\\
=\left(-\frac{a_{1}}{m_{1}}\rho^{1}(\alpha)g(J^{1},J^{1})-\cdots-\frac{a_{s}}{m_{s}}\rho^{s}(\alpha)g(J^{s},J^{s})\right)A=-2\left(a_{1}\rho^{1}(\alpha)+\cdots+a_{s}\rho^{s}(\alpha)\right)A.

On the other hand,

\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{t}}N=2\varphi A,\quad\varphi=\frac{1}{2}g(N,A).

We conclude that

\varphi d\theta=a_{1}\rho^{1}+\cdots+a_{s}\rho^{s}.

We claim that the function
\varphi
is a non-zero constant. Indeed, applying the exterior derivative to the last equality, we get that

d\varphi\wedge d\theta=0.

Since
\iota_{\xi}d\theta=0
, we see that
\iota_{\xi}d\varphi=0
, i.e.,
d\varphi\in\Gamma(D^{*})
. Since the restriction of
d\theta
to
\wedge^{2}D
is non-degenerate,
and
2m=\mathrm{rk}D\geq 4
, we conclude that
d\varphi=0
, i.e.,
\varphi
is a constant.
If
\varphi=0
, then
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{t}}N=0
,
R^{0}
take values in
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
, and we get a contradiction. Since
d\theta\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}D)
is non-degenerate, we conclude that
r=s
and the distribution
D^{0}
is trivial. Thus, the first condition of Theorem
6
implies the second condition.

Suppose that the second condition of Theorem
6
holds true. Let

A=a_{1}J^{1}+\cdots+a_{r}J^{r}

be the normalization of

b_{1}J^{1}+\cdots+b_{r}J^{r}

and consider the
\nabla^{0}
-parallel bundle
\mathfrak{t}=\left<A\right>
. Let
\alpha\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}(D))
.
Using (
26
), (
12
) and the just used computations, we get

g(R^{W}(\alpha),A)=0.

From this and Theorem
2
it follows that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{t}
. Since
d\theta
is non-degenerate, each
\rho^{i}
is non-zero. This and (
27
) imply that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
contains
\mathbb{R}J^{i}
. Hence

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
contains
\mathfrak{t}
, and
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})\neq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
. From this and Theorem
5
it follows that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\subset\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
is a codimension-one ideal. ∎

Proposition 4
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
7
,
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is contained in
\mathfrak{su}(D_{x})=\mathfrak{su}(m)
for a transverse Kähler structure
J
if and only if

d\theta=b\rho,\quad b\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},

where
\rho
is the Ricci form corresponding to
J
.
In this case

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})=\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\oplus\mathbb{R}J_{x}.

Proof.
The condition
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)
is equivalent to the condition
\mathfrak{t}_{x}^{\bot}\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)
, which is equivalent to the condition
\mathfrak{t}_{x}=\mathbb{R}J_{x}
. ∎

9.
Consequences and examples

In this section we give several examples and applications.

9.1.
A construction

Let us give a construction of spaces satisfying the equivalent conditions of Theorem
6
.
Let
(M_{1},g_{1})
,…,
(M_{r},g_{r})
be Kähler manifolds such that their Ricci forms
\rho^{i}
are non-degenerate and exact, i.e.,

\rho^{i}=d\theta^{i},\quad\theta^{i}\in\Omega^{1}(M_{i}).

Consider the manifold

M=\mathbb{R}\times M_{1}\times\cdots\times M_{r}.

Fix non-zero numbers
b_{1},\dots,b_{r}
, define the 1-form

\theta=dt+b_{1}\theta^{1}+\cdots+b_{r}\theta^{r}\in\Omega^{1}(M),

where
t
is the coordinate on
\mathbb{R}
. It is obvious that
\theta
is a contact form on
M
.
We consider the tangent bundles
TM_{i}
as the subbundles of
TM
. Define the distributions

D_{i}=\{X-b_{i}\theta(X)\partial_{t}|X\in TM_{i}\}\subset TM.

Each distribution
D_{i}
may be identified with the tangent bundle
TM_{i}\subset TM
. Under this identification, each Riemannian metric
g_{i}
defines the sub-Riemannian metric on the distribution
D^{i}
, and the corresponding horizontal connection on
D^{i}
corresponds to the Levi-Civita connection on
(M_{i},g_{i})
.
Finally, the contact distribution

D=D^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D^{r}=\ker\theta

with the sub-Riemannian metric

g_{1}+\cdots+g_{r}

satisfies the second condition of Theorem
6
.

9.2.
Sasaki structures

Recall that a Sasaki manifold
(M,\xi,h)
is a Riemannian manifold
(M,h)
with a unit Killing vector field
\xi
such that the field of endomorphisms
\Phi
defined by

\Phi(X)=\nabla^{h}_{X}\xi,\quad X\in\Gamma(TM),

satisfies the conditions

\displaystyle\Phi^{2}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{id}_{TM}+\theta\otimes\xi,

\displaystyle(\nabla^{h}_{X}\Phi)Y

\displaystyle=h(\xi,Y)X-h(X,Y)\xi,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(TM),

where
\theta
is the 1-form
h
-dual to the vector field
\xi
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold.
Consider the Riemannian metric

h=\theta\otimes\theta+g.

It is not hard to check the following relation between the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{h}
and the Schouten connection
\nabla
:

\displaystyle\nabla^{h}_{X}Y

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}Y-d\theta(X,Y)\xi,

\displaystyle\nabla^{h}_{X}\xi

\displaystyle=\psi(X),

\displaystyle\nabla^{h}_{\xi}X

\displaystyle=[\xi,X]+\psi(X),

where
X,Y\in\Gamma(D)
, and the endomorphism
\psi
is defined by the equality

(28)

g(\psi(X),Y)=d\theta(X,Y).

Using this, it is easy to prove the following:

Proposition 5
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold with the Reeb vector field
\xi
. Then
(M,\xi,h)
is a Sasaki manifold if and only if the endomorphism
\psi
given by (
28
) satisfies the conditions

\psi^{2}=-\mathrm{id}_{D},\quad\nabla\psi=0.

Until the end of this section we suppose that
(M,\theta,g)
has the structure of a Sasaki manifold with a complete Reeb vector field
\xi
. The holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
is contained in
\mathfrak{u}(m)
, see
[
10
]
.

Corollary 3
.

The condition
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
holds true if and only if the transversal Kähler structure of each foliation
\mathcal{F}_{i}
defined in Section
8
is Einstein and not Ricci-flat.

Proof.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
. From Remark
1
it follows that the transverse Kähler structure
J
from Theorem
6
may be taken to be the transverse Kähler structure defined by the Sasaki structure. Then it holds

g(X,Y)=d\theta(JX,Y),\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(D).

From this and (
21
) it follows that

g=b_{1}\mathrm{Ric}_{1}+\cdots+b_{r}\mathrm{Ric}_{r},\quad b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}.

Conversely, the last equality implies (
21
), and by Theorem
6
it holds
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
. ∎

Corollary 4
.

The condition
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)
holds true if and only if the transversal Kähler structure of the characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
is Einstein.

Proof
The condition
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)
holds true if and only if the transversal Kähler structure of
\mathcal{F}
is Ricci-flat
[
10
]
. This proves the corollary in the case
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
. If

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
, then the statement follows from Proposition
4
and Corollary
3
. ∎

Let
A
be one of the Lie groups
S^{1}
or
\mathbb{R}
. Recall that a Sasaki manifold
(M,\xi,h)
is called regular if the Reeb vector field generates a free and proper action of a Lie group
A
. A regular Sasaki manifold
(M,\xi,h)
is called
a contactization
of a Kähler manifold
(Q,b,\omega)
, if there is a map
p:M\to Q
defining a principle
A
-bundle such that

g=p^{*}b,\quad d\theta=p^{*}\omega,

see, e.g.,
[
2
]
. The projection
p
may be used to identify fibres of the contact distribution
D
with the corresponding tangent spaces to
Q
. From (
3
) it follows that if
X,Y\in\Gamma(D)
, then

p\nabla_{X}Y=\nabla^{b}_{pX}(pY).

From (
5
) it follows that

pR(X,Y)Z=R^{b}(pX,pY)(pZ),\quad X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(D).

This and the above corollaries imply

Corollary 5
.

Let a regular Sasaki manifold
(M,\xi,h)
be a contactization of a complete and simply connected Kähler manifold
(Q,b,\omega)
.
Then
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{0})
if and only if
(Q,b,\omega)
is a product of Kähler-Einstein manifolds.

Corollary 6
.

Let a regular Sasaki manifold
(M,\xi,h)
be a contactization of a complete and simply connected Kähler manifold
(Q,b,\omega)
.
Then
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)
if and only if
(Q,b,\omega)
is a Kähler-Einstein manifold.

9.3.
Parallel horizontal spinors

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a simply connected contact sub-Riemannian manifold with the contact distribution
D
. The form
\theta
defines an orientation on
D
. Let
P_{\operatorname{SO}}
be the
\operatorname{SO}(2m)
-principle bundle of positively oriented frames on
D
. A
spin-structure
on
D
is a reduction of
P_{\operatorname{SO}}
to a
\operatorname{Spin}(2m)
-principle bundle
P_{\operatorname{Spin}}
corresponding to the two-fold covering

\lambda:\operatorname{Spin}(2m)\to\operatorname{SO}(2m).

Suppose that a spin-structure on
D
is fixed.
Denote by
\Delta_{2m}
the complex

\operatorname{Spin}(2m)
-spinor module.
The spinor bundle

S
is defined as the associated bundle,

S=P_{\operatorname{Spin}}\times_{\lambda}\Delta_{2m}.

The connections
\nabla
and
\nabla^{0}
are extended to the connections

\nabla^{S}:\Gamma(D)\times\Gamma(S)\to\Gamma(S),

\nabla^{0,S}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(S)\to\Gamma(S).

The holonomy algebras of these connections are respectively the representations of
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
and

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
on
S_{x}\cong\Delta_{2m}
. By the holonomy principle, there exists a non-zero spinor
s\in\Gamma(S)
such that
\nabla^{S}s=0
(resp.,
\nabla^{0,S}s=0
) if and only if
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
(resp.,
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
) preserves a non-zero element
s_{x}\in S_{x}
.

From now on suppose that
(M,\theta,g)
is a
simply connected K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold.
As we have seen,
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})\subset\mathfrak{so}(2m)
is a Riemannian holonomy algebra. Consequently, there exists a non-zero
\nabla^{0,S}
-parallel spinor if and only if each Lie algebra in decomposition (
20
) is one of
\mathfrak{su}(k)
,
\mathfrak{sp}(k)
,
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, or
\mathfrak{spin}(7)
, see
[
17
]
. If
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
, then
\nabla^{S}
-parallel spinors are exactly
\nabla^{0,S}
-parallel spinors. Consider the case when

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a
simply connected K-contact sub-Riemannian manifold of dimension
2m+1\geq 5
with a fixed spin structure on the contact distribution
D
and the complete Reeb vector field
\xi
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
. Then there exists a non-zero
\nabla
-parallel spinor if and only if there exists a transverse Kähler structure
J
such that
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)
.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

It is well-known that the
\mathfrak{u}(n)
-module
\Delta_{2n}
admits invariant decomposition

(29)

\Delta_{2n}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\wedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n}

such that
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the standard
\mathfrak{su}(n)
-module, and the complex structure
J\in\mathfrak{u}(n)
acts on
\wedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n}

as the multiplication by
(m-2k)i
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{R}J\subset\mathfrak{u}(n)
be the holonomy algebra of an irreducible Kähler symmetric space, then
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
does not annihilate any non-zero element from
\wedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
k=1,\dots,n-1
.

Proof.
It is enough to show that
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
does not annihilate any non-zero element from
\wedge^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}
, which is the same as to show that
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{C}

has this property. It is known that if
\mathfrak{f}\neq\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
, then the first prolongation of the subalgebra
\mathfrak{f}\oplus\mathbb{C}\operatorname{id}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
contains a submodule
U
isomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}
such that

\mathfrak{f}\oplus\mathbb{C}\operatorname{id}=\{\varphi(X)|\varphi\in U,X\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\},

see,
[
14
, pages 95, 99]
.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{f}
preserves a form
\omega\in\wedge^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}
, where
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Then the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}\oplus\mathbb{C}\operatorname{id}
preserves the line
\mathbb{C}\omega\subset\wedge^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}.

For each
\varphi\in U
, there exists
\lambda_{\varphi}\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}
such that

(\varphi(X)\cdot\omega)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\lambda_{\varphi}(X)\omega(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}),\quad\forall X,X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Alternating this equality and using

\varphi(X)Y=\varphi(Y)X,\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

we obtain

\lambda_{\varphi}\wedge\omega=0.

It is clear that the map

U\to(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*},\quad\varphi\mapsto\lambda_{\varphi}

is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{f}\oplus\mathbb{C}\operatorname{id}
-modules. Thus, it holds that

\omega_{1}\wedge\omega=0,\quad\forall\omega_{1}\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}.

Since
1\leq k\leq n-1
, this implies that
\omega=0
. ∎

Since
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{0})
, the second condition of Theorem
6
holds true. Fix a transversal Kähler structure
J
.
Decomposition (
22
) implies the isomorphism

\Delta_{2m}=\Delta^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Delta^{r}

of
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
-modules,
where
\Delta^{i}
is the spinor module corresponding to
D^{i}_{x}
,
i=1,\dots,r
.
Decomposition (
29
) then implies

\Delta_{2m}=\bigoplus_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}\wedge^{k_{1}}(D_{x}^{1}\otimes\mathbb{C})\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{k_{r}}(D_{x}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{C}).

Fix a non-zero spinor
s
from one of these submodules and suppose that
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
annihilates
s
.
From decomposition (
24
) and the lemma it follows that
s
belongs to a submodule corresponding to
k_{i}\in\{0,m_{i}\}
, where
2m_{i}=\dim D^{i}_{x}
,
i=1,\dots,r
.
As above,
\mathfrak{t}_{x}=\mathbb{R}A_{x}
, where

A=a_{1}J^{1}+\cdots+a_{r}J^{r}.

An element

C=c_{1}{J^{1}_{x}}+\cdots+c_{r}{J^{r}_{x}}

belongs to
\mathfrak{t}^{\bot}
if and only if
g(C,A_{x})=0
, that is

(30)

m_{1}a_{1}c_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}a_{r}c_{r}=0.

The condition
Cs=0
takes the form

(31)

(m_{1}-2k_{1})c_{1}+\cdots+(m_{r}-2k_{r})c_{r}=0.

Each solution of the equation (
30
) is a solution of
the equation (

\frac{m_{1}-2k_{1}}{m_{1}a_{1}}=\cdots=\frac{m_{r}-2k_{r}}{m_{r}a_{r}}.

Since
k_{i}\in\{0,m_{i}\}
, each number
m_{i}-2k_{i}

\pm m_{i}

If the last equality holds, then we may change the Kähler structure

This implies that
\mathfrak{t}_{x}=\mathbb{R}J_{x}

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
annihilates a spinor
s\in\Delta_{2m}

[
17
]
. ∎

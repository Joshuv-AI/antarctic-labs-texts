On the growth of Tate–Shafarevich groups of p p -supersingular abelian varieties of GL 2 {\rm GL}_{2} -type over ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} -extensions of number fields
Erman IŞIK and Antonio Lei

Abstract
. We study the boundedness of the Mordell–Weil rank and the growth of the v v -primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich group of p p -supersingular abelian varieties of GL 2 {\rm GL}_{2} -type with real multiplication over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of number fields, where v v is a prime lying above p p . Building on the work of Iovita–Pollack in the case of elliptic curves, under precise ramification and splitting conditions on p p , we construct explicit systems of local points using the theory of Lubin–Tate formal groups. We then define signed Coleman maps, which in turn allow us to formulate and analyse signed Selmer groups. Assuming these Selmer groups are cotorsion, we prove that the Mordell–Weil groups are bounded over any subextensions of the ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} -extension and provide an asymptotic formula for the growth of the v v -primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich groups. Our results extend those of Kobayashi, Pollack, and Sprung on p p -supersingular elliptic curves.

1.
Introduction

One of the central themes in Iwasawa theory is the study of the asymptotic behaviour of arithmetic invariants along towers of number fields. In his foundational work
[
Iwa73
]
, Iwasawa showed that for a number field
K
and a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}
over
K
, there exist constants
\mu_{K},\lambda_{K}
, and
\nu_{K}
such that the
p
-adic valuation of the class number
h_{n}
of the degree
p^{n}
subextension
K_{n}
in
K_{\infty}/K
satisfies

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(h_{n})=\mu_{K}p^{n}+\lambda_{K}n+\nu_{K}\;\text{for }n\gg 0.

In
[
Maz72
]
, Mazur studied this question in the context of Tate–Shafarevich groups of abelian varieties with good ordinary reduction. If
A/K
is an abelian variety with good ordinary reduction at all primes above
p
, and if the
p
-primary Selmer group over the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}
is cotorsion as a module over the corresponding Iwasawa algebra, then one obtains an analogous asymptotic formula describing the growth of the
p
-primary component of the Tate–Shafarevich group. More precisely, assume that
\Sha(A/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for all
n
, there exist constants
\mu_{A},\lambda_{A}
, and
\nu_{A}
such that

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\Sha(A/K_{n})[p^{\infty}])=\mu_{A}p^{n}+\lambda_{A}n+\nu_{A},

for all
n\geq 0
(see
[
Maz72
]
,
[
Gre99
, Theorem 1.10]
and
[
Lee20
, Theorem C]
).

The supersingular reduction case is more involved, even for elliptic curves over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, as the classical approach does not apply directly. For an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with good supersingular reduction at
p
, and the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, the case where
a_{p}(E)=0
has been
studied by Kurihara
[
Kur02
]
, Kobayashi
[
Kob03
]
and Pollack
[
Pol05
]
.

Again over the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, Sprung
[
Spr13
,
Spr17
]
studied this question for
p
-supersingular elliptic curves with
a_{p}(E)\neq 0
and for abelian varieties of
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type. For related results concerning upper bounds on the growth of Bloch–Kato–Shafarevich–Tate groups associated to higher-weight modular forms, see
[
LLZ17
]
. The growth of Mordell–Weil ranks and Tate–Shafarevich groups of elliptic curves over the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of certain number fields has also been studied in
[
LL22
]
. An explicit sufficient condition for the boundedness of Mordell–Weil ranks over the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of number fields where
p
is ramified was in
[
LP20
]
for abelian varieties with good supersingular reduction at
p
.

Parallel developments have taken place for elliptic curves in the anticyclotomic setting. Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field and
E
an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Assuming
p\geq 5
and that the pair
(E,K)
satisfies the Heegner hypothesis, Çiperiani proved in
[
Çip09
]
that the Tate–Shafarevich group of
E
has trivial corank over the Iwasawa algebra associated with the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
. A more refined formula describing the growth of these groups along the extension is given in
[
Mat21
,
LLM23
]
by Matar and by Lei–Lim–Müller, respectively. Further results concerning the inert primes and the elliptic curves with
a_{p}=0
over the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
have been obtained in
[
IL24
,
BKO25
,
Mül25
]
; in the latter cases, the elliptic curve is assumed to have complex multiplication by an order in
K
and
p\geq 5
.

Under certain hypotheses on the vanishing of the Mordell–Weil rank and the
p
-primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich group of an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
K
, Iovita and Pollack
[
IP06
, Theorem 5.1]
established a formula describing the growth of Tate–Shafarevich groups over a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
in which
p
splits completely and each prime above
p
is totally ramified in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension.

The main objective of this article is to extend the aforementioned theorem of Iovita and Pollack in
[
IP06
]
to the setting of abelian varieties of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type over
\mathbb{Q}
with real multiplication, arising from weight two newforms.

1.1.
Statement of the main theorem

Let
f
be a weight two newform of level
N
and trivial nebentype and let
F
be the Hecke field generated by the Fourier coefficients
a_{n}
of
f
. Note that since the nebentype is trivial, the Hecke field
F
is totally real (see
[
Rib04
, Proposition 3.4]
). Write
\mathcal{O}_{F}
for the ring of integers of
F
. Let
A:=A_{f}
be the associated
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type abelian variety over
{\mathbb{Q}}
of dimension
g
so that
\mathcal{O}_{F}\hookrightarrow{\rm End}(A)
and

\prod L(f^{\sigma},s)=L(A,s),

where the product runs over all Galois conjugates
f^{\sigma}
of
f
. Note that
A
is self-dual.

Let
p
be an odd prime, and suppose that
A
has good
supersingular
reduction at
p
. Let
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
be a finite extension and
K_{\infty}/K
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension. For an integer
n\geq 0
, we write
K_{n}
for the unique subextension of
K_{\infty}
such that
[K_{n}:K]=p^{n}
.

The Galois group of
K_{\infty}
over
K
is denoted by
\Gamma
. In addition, write
\Gamma_{n}={\rm Gal}(K_{\infty}/K_{n})
and
G_{n}={\rm Gal}(K_{n}/K)
. We fix once and for all a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
. For a prime
v
of
F
lying above
p
, let
F_{v}
denote the completion of
F
at
v
with the integer ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
. Let
\Lambda_{v}
denote the Iwasawa algebra
\mathcal{O}_{v}[[\Gamma]]=\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{O}_{v}[G_{n}]
, which we shall identify with the power series ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}[[X]]
by sending
\gamma-1
to
X
. Let
(-)^{\vee}:={\rm Hom}(-,F_{v}/\mathcal{O}_{v})
denote the Pontryagin duality functor.

We will assume the additional hypothesis on the splitting type of the prime
p
in
K
and
K_{\infty}
.

Hypothesis 1.1
.

The prime
p
splits completely in
K
into
d:=[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]
distinct primes,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{d}
. Moreover, each
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
is totally ramified in
K_{\infty}
.

In this article, we prove that the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-rank of the Mordell–Weil group of
A
over
K_{\infty}
is bounded assuming that the signed Selmer groups
{\rm Sel}_{v}^{{\mathbf{s}}}(A/K_{\infty})
are
\Lambda_{v}
-cotorsion for all choices of
{\mathbf{s}}\in\{\sharp,\flat\}^{d}
(see §
5
). Furthermore, we derive an asymptotic formula for the growth of the
v
-parts of the Tate–Shafarevich groups
\Sha(A/K_{n})
of
A
in terms of the Iwasawa invariants of
{\rm Sel}_{v}^{{\mathbf{s}}}(A/K_{\infty})^{\vee}
. The main result of this article is the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type over
{\mathbb{Q}}
associated to a weight-two newform with trivial nebentype. Let
K
be a number field and
p
be an odd prime satisfying Hypothesis
1.1
. Furthermore, assume that the signed Selmer groups
{\rm Sel}^{\mathbf{s}}_{v}(A/K_{\infty})
are cotorsion over
\Lambda_{v}
(see Hypothesis
5.3
).
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}A(K_{n})
is bounded independently of
n
.

(ii)

Assume further that
v
-part of the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module
\Sha(A/K_{n})
is finite for all
n\geq 0
and let
e_{n}:={\rm ord}_{v}\left(\Sha(A/K_{n})\right)
. Define

\displaystyle r_{\infty}:=\sup_{n\geq 0}\left\{{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}A(K_{n})\right\}.

Then, for
n\gg 0
, we have

e_{n}-e_{n-1}=F_{v}({\mathbf{s}},n)+(p^{n}-p^{n-1})\mu_{v,{\mathbf{s}}}+e_{v}\lambda_{v,{\mathbf{s}}}-r_{\infty},

where
e_{v}
denotes the ramification index of the extension
F_{v}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, the sign
{\mathbf{s}}=(s_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}\in\{\sharp,\flat\}^{d}
and the quantity
F_{v}({\mathbf{s}},n)
are given in Proposition
7.9
, which depend on the parity of
n
; the quantities
\mu_{v,{\mathbf{s}}}
and
\lambda_{v,{\mathbf{s}}}
denote the Iwasawa invariants of
{\rm Sel}_{v}^{{\mathbf{s}}}(A/K_{\infty})^{\vee}
.

Theorem
A
generalizes the works of Lei–Lim
[
LL22
, Theorems A and B]
, Kurihara
[
Kur02
, Theorem 0.1]
, Pollack
[
Pol05
, Theorem 1.1]
, Sprung
[
Spr13
, Theorem 1.1]
and Kobayashi
[
Kob03
, Theorem 1.4]
for elliptic curves over cyclotomic extensions. Furthermore, when
A
is an elliptic curve, our theorem generalizes
[
IP06
, Theorem 5.1]
, removing their hypothesis (G) on Tamagawa number and the triviality of
\Sha(A/K)[p]
and
A(K)/pA(K)
.

Remark 1.2
.

When
K=\mathbb{Q}
, the boundedness of
{\rm rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}A(K_{n})
has been established in
[
Kat04
, Theorem 14.4]
. See also
[
LP20
, Theorem 3.4 and the discussion in §3.3]
for an alternative approach to establish part (i) of Theorem
A
.

Remark 1.3
.

Hypothesis
1.1
is also assumed in the setting of
[
IP06
]
, where elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with
a_{p}=0
were studied. Our main contribution is to extend their construction of local points to the more general context of abelian varieties (see §
3
). These local points are then used to define signed Coleman maps (Proposition
4.2
via the results of
[
BBL24
]
) and the associated signed Selmer groups. Similar constructions were given in
[
BBL24
]
using the Perrin–Riou map, but our approach is more explicit and allows for a finer analysis of the joint image of the Coleman maps (Theorem
4.4
), which plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
A
.

1.2.
Organization

In Section
2
, we determine the Honda type of the formal groups associated with abelian varieties of
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type with real multiplication in our context and construct an explicit logarithm of the formal group associated with Lubin–Tate extensions of height one. This logarithm is used in Section
3
to construct a system of local points, following the approach of
[
IP06
, §4.2]
and
[
Kob03
, §8.4]
. These local points are then used in Section
4
to define signed Coleman maps within the framework of
[
BBL24
]
, and we prove a result describing the image of their direct sum. In Section
5
, we define the corresponding signed Selmer groups whose Iwasawa invariants appear in the statement of Theorem
A
. The section concludes with a review of certain global cohomology groups, under the assumption that the signed Selmer groups are cotorsion. We recall the definition and basic properties of the Kobayashi rank following
[
Kob03
, §10]
and
[
LLZ17
, §4]
in Section
6
. In Section
7
, we establish a connection between Coleman maps and Kobayashi ranks, and show how this relationship enables us to study the growth of certain local modules. We conclude by bringing these ingredients together to prove Theorem
A
. Relevant results on Honda type of formal groups are reviewed in Appendix
A
for the convenience of the reader.

1.3.
Outlook

The principal novelty of the current article is the construction of local points to define signed Coleman maps, generalising the results of
[
IP06
]
by using the formal groups associated with abelian varieties of
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type with real multiplication.
As in the work of Iovita–Pollack, Hypothesis
1.1
allows us to work with Lubin–Tate extensions of height one when we localize at a prime above
p
. It may be possible to relax this hypothesis by working with relative Lubin–Tate formal groups instead. This would likely involve working with Honda theory for formal groups defined over a non-trivial extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
developed in
[
Hon70
]
. Since
[K_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}:{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}]
can be
>1
without assuming Hypothesis
1.1
, the method to calculate the Kobayashi ranks of modules that involve the
d\times d
matrices for
d>1
as developed in
[
IL24
]
may also be useful.

Theorem
A
complements
[
IL24
, Theorem A]
, where the growth of the
p
-primary Tate–Shafarevich groups of an elliptic curve over the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of an imaginary quadratic field
K
is studied, assuming that
p
is inert in
K
. Unlike the present article,
[
IL24
]
employs calculations that are specifically used to find the Kobayashi ranks of modules arise from
2\times 2
matrices whose entries are elements of an Iwasawa algebra. It would be interesting to treat both cases within a single framework, in the spirit of the unified approach of
[
BLV23
]
, where anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjectures are studied in the
p
-split and
p
-inert settings simultaneously.

2.
Lubin–Tate extensions and formal groups attached to abelian varieties

Let
\{k_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
with
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}=k_{0}\subset k_{1}\subset k_{2}\subset\dots\subset k_{\infty}=\cup_{n}k_{n}
be a tower of fields with
G_{n}={\rm Gal}(k_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
k_{\infty}
is a totally ramified
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Let
L_{n+1}:=k_{n}(\mu_{p})
for
n\geq 0
and
L_{\infty}=\cup_{n}L_{n}=k_{\infty}(\mu_{p})
. Here, if
M
is a field, we denote by
M(\mu_{p})
the extension of
M
obtained by adjoining the
p
-th roots of unity in some fixed algebraic closure of
M
. It then follows that
L_{\infty}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, and the group of its universal norms is generated by a uniformizer
\pi
of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
such that
{\rm ord}_{p}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{p}-1\right)>0
. In particular, we may choose
\pi
so that
k_{n}
coincides with the completion of
K_{n}
at a prime above
p
.

Following
[
IP06
, §4.1]
, we fix a one-dimensional Lubin–Tate formal group
{\mathscr{F}}
of height-one over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, with parameter
\pi
and the lift of Frobenius

{\mathfrak{f}}(X):=\pi X+\sum_{i=2}^{p}\binom{p}{i}X^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[[X]].

For every
n
, we have
L_{n}={\mathbb{Q}}_{p}({\mathscr{F}}[\pi^{n}])
, where
{\mathscr{F}}[\pi^{n}]
denotes the
\pi^{n}
-torsion of the formal group
{\mathscr{F}}
.

We fix a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, which induces an injective ring homomorphism

\Phi:\mathcal{O}_{F}\longrightarrow M_{g\times g}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Let
C_{r}=\Phi(a_{r})
where
r\in\{q,q^{2}\}
and
q
is a prime. Define the formal Dirichlet series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{n}n^{-s}=\prod_{q}(I_{g}-C_{q}q^{-s}+C_{q^{2}}q^{1-2s})^{-1},

where
C_{n}\in M_{g\times g}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The formal group
\widehat{A}
of the abelian variety
A
at
p
is isomorphic over
{\mathbb{Z}}
to the formal group whose logarithm is given by

\log_{\widehat{A}}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{C_{n}}{n}{\mathbf{x}}^{n},

where
{\mathbf{x}}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{g})^{t}
is a column vector of
g
variables and
{\mathbf{x}}^{n}
denotes
\left(X^{n}_{1},\dots,X_{g}^{n}\right)^{t}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of
[
DN90
, Theorem 2.2]
.
∎

Consider the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
of
g\times g
matrices defined by
x_{-1}=0
,
x_{0}=I_{g}
and

(2.1)

px_{k}-C_{p}x_{k-1}+x_{k-2}=0\text{ for }k\geq 1,

where
C_{p}=\Phi(a_{p})
. In particular,
p^{k}x_{k}
has integral coefficients.

Definition 2.2
.

We define
{\mathfrak{f}}^{(0)}(X)=X
and if
k\geq 1
is an integer, we define
{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}
recursively by

{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(X)={\mathfrak{f}}({\mathfrak{f}}^{(k-1)}(X)).

Furthermore, we define

\ell({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}}),

where
{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}}):=\left({\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(X_{1}),\ldots,{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(X_{g})\right)^{t}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all integers
k,t\geq 0
, the entries of
x_{k}{\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(\mathbf{x}+p\mathbf{y})-x_{k}{\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(\mathbf{x})
are defined in
p^{t+1-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[X_{1},\dots,X_{g},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{g}]
.

Proof.

Since
A
is supersingular at
p
, the entries of
C_{p}
are divisible by
p
. Consequently,
x_{1}
is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. By induction, we can prove that the entries of
x_{k}
are in
p^{-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\mathbb{Z}
using (
2.1
).

The lemma would follow from the following inclusion

{\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X+pY)-{\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X)\in p^{t+1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[X,Y],

which can be proved using induction. Indeed, if
t=0
, the inclusion is immediate. Suppose that it holds for some
t\geq 0
. Let us write

{\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X+pY)={\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X)+p^{t+1}S(X,Y).

Then

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}^{(t+1)}(X+pY)

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{f}}({\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X)+p^{t+1}S(X,Y))

\displaystyle=\pi\left({\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X)+p^{t+1}S(X,Y)\right)+\sum_{i=2}^{p}\binom{p}{i}\left({\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X)+p^{t+1}S(X,Y)\right)^{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\pi{\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X)+\sum_{i=2}^{p}\binom{p}{i}\left({\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}(X)\right)^{i}={\mathfrak{f}}^{(t+1)}(X)\mod p^{t+2}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[X,Y]

as
\pi\in p{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. This gives the desired inclusion for all
t\geq 0
by induction.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

We have that
\hat{\ell}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}):=\ell^{-1}(\ell({\mathbf{x}})+\ell({\mathbf{y}}))
is a
g
-dimensional formal group over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Further, the formal groups
\hat{A}
and
\hat{\ell}
are isomorphic over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

Proof.

In the light of Theorem
A.6
, it is enough to show that the two formal groups have the same Honda type
u(T):=T^{2}-C_{p}T+p
. In particular, we need to show that

u*\ell\equiv u*\log_{\hat{A}}\equiv 0\mod p{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[[X]],

where
u*\ell
and
u*\log_{\hat{A}}
are given as in Definition
A.4
. Note that
{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(X)\equiv X^{p^{k}}\mod p
. We then deduce that

\displaystyle u*\ell

\displaystyle=\ell({\mathbf{x}}^{p^{2}})-C_{p}\ell({\mathbf{x}}^{p})+p\ell({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}}^{p^{2}})-C_{p}\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}}^{p})+p\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\geq 2}(px_{k}-C_{p}x_{k-1}+x_{k-2}){\mathbf{x}}^{p^{k+2}}+p{\mathbf{x}}\mod p

\displaystyle\equiv 0\mod p.

It then follows from Theorem
A.6
(i) that
\hat{\ell}
is a formal group over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. The proof for
u*\log_{\hat{A}}
is similar and relies on the fact that
C_{mp^{k}}=C_{m}C_{p^{k}}
whenever
p\nmid m
.
∎

For the rest of this section, we identify these two formal groups.

Lemma 2.5
.

The group homomorphism
\ell:\widehat{A}(L_{n})\longrightarrow{\widehat{{\mathbb{G}}}}^{g}_{a}(L_{n})
of formal groups is injective, where
{\widehat{{\mathbb{G}}}}^{g}_{a}
is the additive formal group of dimension
g
.

Proof.

Since the abelian variety is modular and has supersingular reduction at
p
, the argument used in the proof of
[
Lei11
, Lemma 4.4]
, which relies on the result given in
[
Edi92
, Theorem 2.6]
and
[
BLZ04
, Proposition 4.1.4]
on the local residual representation of a
p
-non-ordinary modular form, can be applied to show that
A(L_{n})[p^{\infty}]=0
for
n\geq 0
. Since the kernel of
\ell
consists of torsion points and
\widehat{A}(L_{n})
has no prime-to-
p
torsion (see
[
HS00
, Proposition C.2.5 and Theorem C.2.6]
), the lemma follows.
∎

3.
Construction of local points over Lubin–Tate extensions

Choose a
\pi
-sequence
\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
in
L_{\infty}
, i.e.
e_{n}\in{\mathscr{F}}[\pi^{n}]\setminus{\mathscr{F}}[\pi^{n-1}]
such that
{\mathfrak{f}}(e_{n})=e_{n-1}
for all
n\geq 1
and
e_{0}=0
.

Let
u_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus g}
be the column vector that represents
1\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
with respect to the basis chosen for the ring homomorphism
\Phi
. In particular, the orbit of
u_{1}
under the action of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
(induced by
\Phi
) is
\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus g}
. After a change of basis if necessary, we can take
u_{1}=(1,\dots,1)^{t}
. Indeed, we can choose an integral basis of
F
that is of the form
\{1,\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{g}\}
. Then
\{1-\sum_{i=2}^{g}\alpha_{i},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{g}\}
is another integral basis of
F
. The vector representing
1
with respect to this basis is then equal to
u_{1}
.

Let
\epsilon\in\left(p{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\right)^{g}
be such that

\ell(\epsilon)=(1+p-C_{p})^{-1}\left(pu_{1}\right).

Note that we have written
1+p
for the
g\times g
scalar matrix whose diagonal entries are all equal to
1+p
. As
a_{p}
is a non-unit of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, the matrix
C_{p}
is
p
-adically nilpotent. Thus,
1+p-C_{p}
is
p
-adically unipotent and belongs to
{\rm GL}_{g}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. We regard
\epsilon
as an element of
\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
.

Definition 3.1
.

We define
c_{0}=\epsilon\in\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
, and

c_{n}:=\epsilon[+]_{\widehat{A}}(e_{n}u_{1})\in\widehat{A}(L_{n}),\;\text{for all}\;n\geq 1,

where
[+]_{\widehat{A}}
is the addition of
\widehat{A}
.

We furthermore define
d_{n}
to be the image of
c_{n+1}
under the trace map
\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})\rightarrow\widehat{A}(k_{n})
for
n\geq 0
.

Fix a prime
v
of
F
lying above
p
and let
F_{v}
denote the completion of
F
at
v
with the integer ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
. Note that
\Phi
induces an
\mathcal{O}_{v}
-action on
\widehat{A}(M)
if
M
is a finite extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Under this action,
c_{0}=\epsilon
is a generator of the
\mathcal{O}_{v}
-module
\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
. Given finite extensions
M_{1}\supset M_{2}\supset{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, let
{\rm Tr}_{M_{1}/M_{2}}
be the trace map
M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}
. By an abuse of notation, we use the same notation to denote the trace map
\widehat{A}(M_{1})\longrightarrow\widehat{A}(M_{2})
with respect to the group law on
\widehat{A}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
n\geq 0
, there exists
d_{n}\in\widehat{A}(k_{n})
such that:

(i)

For
n\geq 1
,
{\rm Tr}_{n+1/n}\left(d_{n+1}\right)=C_{p}d_{n}-d_{n-1}
.

(ii)

{\rm Tr}_{1/0}\left(d_{1}\right)=\left(C_{p}-(C_{p}-2)^{-1}(p-1)\right)\cdot d_{0}
.

Proof.

Our proof follows closely the calculations in
[
IP06
, §4]
. Let
n\geq 2
. Note that the minimal polynomial of
e_{n}
over
L_{n-1}
is
{\mathfrak{f}}(X)-e_{n-1}
; we have
{\rm Tr}_{L_{n}/L_{n-1}}(e_{n})=-p
. Thus,

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ell\left({\rm Tr}_{L_{n}/L_{n-1}}\left(c_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{L_{n}/L_{n-1}}\left(\ell\left(c_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{L_{n}/L_{n-1}}\left((1+p-C_{p})^{-1}\left(pu_{1}\right)+\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\left({\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(e_{n})u_{1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(1+p-C_{p})^{-1}\left(p^{2}u_{1}\right)-pu_{1}+p\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{k}\left(e_{n-k}u_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{p}(1+p-C_{p})^{-1}(pu_{1})-(1+p-C_{p})^{-1}(pu_{1})+\sum_{k\geq 1}(C_{p}x_{k-1}-x_{k-2})\left(e_{n-k}u_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{p}(1+p-C_{p})^{-1}(pu_{1})-(1+p-C_{p})^{-1}(pu_{1})+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad C_{p}\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{k-1}\left(e_{n-k}u_{1}\right)-\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{k-2}\left(e_{n-k}u_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{p}(1+p-C_{p})^{-1}(pu_{1})-(1+p-C_{p})^{-1}pu_{1}+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad A_{p}\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\left(e_{n-1-k}u_{1}\right)-\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\left(e_{n-2-k}u_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{p}\ell(c_{n-1})-\ell(c_{n-2}).

Similarly, as the minimal polynomial of
e_{1}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is
{\mathfrak{f}}(X)/X
, we have
{\rm Tr}_{L_{1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(e_{1})=-p
. This allows us to deduce

\ell({\rm Tr}_{L_{1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(c_{1}))=(C_{p}-2)\ell(c_{0}).

Define
d_{n}:={\rm Tr}_{L_{n+1}/k_{n}}(c_{n+1})
for
n\geq 1
. Taking norms from
L_{n+1}
to
k_{n}
, we deduce that

{\rm Tr}_{k_{n}/k_{n-1}}(d_{n})=C_{p}d_{n-1}-d_{n-2},\text{ for }n\geq 2,

and
d_{0}=(C_{p}-2)c_{0}
. This gives

{\rm Tr}_{k_{1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(d_{1})=C_{p}d_{0}-(C_{p}-2)^{-1}(p-1)d_{0},

as required.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

For
n\geq 1
,
d_{n}
and
d_{n-1}
generate
{\widehat{A}}(k_{n})
as an
\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(k_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]
-module.

Proof.

We have chosen
c_{0}
to be an
\mathcal{O}_{v}
-generator of
\widehat{A}(L_{0})
. Suppose that
c_{n}
and
c_{n-1}
generate
\widehat{A}(L_{n})
as an
\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(L_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]
-module. We show that this implies
c_{n}
and
c_{n+1}
generate
\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})
as an
\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]
-module. Since
c_{n-1}=C_{p}c_{n}-{\rm Tr}_{L_{n+1}/L_{n}}\left(c_{n+1}\right)
, we have

\widehat{A}(L_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(L_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]c_{n}+\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]c_{n+1}.

Therefore, it suffices to show the following equality:

(3.1)

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]c_{n+1}+\widehat{A}(L_{n})}{\widehat{A}(L_{n})}=\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})\big/\widehat{A}(L_{n}).

As
\ell
is injective on
\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})
by Lemma
2.5
, it suffices to show that
\ell(c_{n+1})
generates the quotient
\ell({\mathfrak{m}}_{n+1}^{\oplus g})/\ell({\mathfrak{m}}_{n}^{\oplus g})
.

Let
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{g})\in\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})
. Then
[
IP06
, Proposition 4.4]
tells us that each
y_{i}
can be expressed as
\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/L_{n})}b_{\sigma}e_{n+1}^{\sigma}+c
, where
b_{\sigma}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
c\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}
. Since
{\mathfrak{f}}^{(s)}(X)\equiv X^{p^{s}}\mod p
for all
s\geq 1
, we have

{\mathfrak{f}}^{(s)}(y_{i})\equiv\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/L_{n})}b_{\sigma}(e_{n+1}^{\sigma})^{p^{s}}+c^{p^{s}}\equiv\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/L_{n})}b_{\sigma}{\mathfrak{f}}^{(s)}(e_{n+1}^{\sigma})+{\mathfrak{f}}^{(s)}(c)\mod p\mathcal{O}_{L_{n+1}}.

Furthermore,
{\mathfrak{f}}^{(s)}(e_{n+1}^{\sigma})\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}
. Therefore,
for all
k\geq 1
, setting
s=k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor
and applying Lemma
2.3
with
t=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
, we deduce that

x_{k}\cdot{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(\mathbf{y})=x_{k}\cdot{\mathfrak{f}}^{(t)}({\mathfrak{f}}^{(s)}(\mathbf{y}))\in({\mathfrak{m}}_{n+1}+L_{n})^{\oplus g}.

Consequently, we deduce that

\ell\left(\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})\right)\subseteq({\mathfrak{m}}_{n+1}+L_{n})^{\oplus g}.

This implies that

\ell({\mathfrak{m}}_{n+1}^{\oplus g})/\ell({\mathfrak{m}}_{n}^{\oplus g})\cong{\mathfrak{m}}_{n+1}^{\oplus g}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}^{\oplus g}.

Recall that
c_{n+1}
generates
{\mathfrak{m}}_{n+1}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]
-module and
\ell(c_{n+1})\equiv c_{n+1}u_{1}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}^{\oplus g}
. Furthermore, as an
\mathcal{O}_{v}
-module,
u_{1}
generates
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\oplus g}
. Hence, we deduce that
\ell(c_{n+1})
generates
\ell({\mathfrak{m}}_{n+1}^{\oplus g})/\ell({\mathfrak{m}}_{n}^{\oplus g})
as an
\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]
-module.

Since
[L_{n+1}:k_{n}]
is prime to
p
, the map
{\rm Tr}_{L_{n+1}/k_{n}}:\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})\longrightarrow\widehat{A}(k_{n})
is surjective. Indeed, we have that the space
\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})
decomposes as a direct sum of eigenspaces:

\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})=\bigoplus_{\chi}\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})^{\chi},

where
\chi
runs over the distinct characters of
{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/k_{n})
, and
\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})^{\chi}
is the eigenspace corresponding to the character
\chi
. For
\chi\neq\mathbf{1}
and
a\in\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})^{\chi}
, we have

{\rm Tr}_{L_{n+1}/k_{n}}(a)=\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/k_{n})}\chi(\sigma)a=\left(\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/k_{n})}\chi(\sigma)\right)a=0.

On the other hand, for
\chi=\mathbf{1}
and
a\in\widehat{A}(k_{n})
, we have

{\rm Tr}_{L_{n+1}/k_{n}}(a)=\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/k_{n})}\chi(\sigma)a=(p-1)a.

Since
p-1
is prime to
p
, the multiplication by
p-1
map is surjective on
\widehat{A}(k_{n})
. Thus, the trace map is surjective on
\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})
.

Since
c_{n}
and
c_{n-1}
generate
\widehat{A}(L_{n+1})
as a
\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(L_{n+1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]
-module, we have that
d_{n}
and
d_{n-1}
generate
\widehat{A}(k_{n})
as an
\mathcal{O}_{v}[{\rm Gal}(k_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})]
-module for
n\geq 1
.
∎

4.

Write
T_{v}
for the
v
-adic Tate module of

, which is a free

-module of rank
2
equipped with a continuous

-action. Write
H_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{1}(k_{n},T_{v})
for the image of the Kummer map
\widehat{A}(k_{n})\longrightarrow H^{1}(k_{n},T_{v})
, and similarly
H_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{1}(L_{n},T_{v})
for
\widehat{A}(L_{n})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists a primitive
Q
-system
{\mathbb{d}}=(d_{n})_{n\geq 0}
for
T_{v}
in the sense of
[
BBL24
, Definition 4.1]
; that is,
{\mathbb{d}}=(d_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

d_{0}\in\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\setminus p\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
;

(ii)

{\rm Tr}_{k_{1}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(d_{1})\in\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\setminus p\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
;

(iii)

{\rm Tr}_{k_{n+1}/k_{n}}(d_{n+1})=C_{p}d_{n}-d_{n-1}
for all

Proof.

The assertion (i) follows from the definition

. The assertions (ii) and (iii) follow from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

For
n\geq 0
, let
\omega_{n}=(1+X)^{p^{n}}-1=\displaystyle\prod_{0\leq i\leq n}\Phi_{i}
, where
\Phi_{i}
is the
p^{i}
-th cyclotomic polynomial in

. Set
\Lambda_{v}:=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{O}_{v}[G_{n}]
so that
\Lambda_{v,n}:=\Lambda_{v}\big/(\omega_{n})=\mathcal{O}_{v}[G_{n}]
, where we have
G_{n}={\rm Gal}(k_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})
. Define the following matrices

\mathcal{C}_{i}(X):=\begin{bmatrix}a_{p}&1\\
-\Phi_{i}&0\end{bmatrix}

and

\mathcal{H}_{n}:=\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)\dots\mathcal{C}_{1}(X).

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. Let

\langle-,-\rangle_{n}:\widehat{A}(k_{n})\times H^{1}(k_{n},T_{v})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{v}

denote the local pairing (
A
is self-dual). We define
H_{\rm Iw}^{i}(k_{\infty},T_{v})=\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}H^{i}(k_{n},T_{v})
, where the transition maps are given by the corestriction maps. Suppose that
{\mathbb{d}}=(d_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a primitive
Q
-system for
T_{v}
given as in Proposition
4.1
. Define

\displaystyle{\rm Col}_{v,n}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},T_{v})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Lambda_{v,n}

(4.1)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{\sigma\in G_{n}}\langle z^{\sigma^{-1}},d_{n}\rangle_{n}\cdot\sigma.

Theorem 4.2
.

There exist unique
\Lambda_{v}
-morphisms (called the
\sharp/\flat
-Coleman maps)

{\rm Col}^{\sharp}_{v},{\rm Col}^{\flat}_{v}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},T_{v})\longrightarrow\Lambda_{v}

such that

\mathcal{H}_{n}\begin{bmatrix}{\rm Col}^{\sharp}_{v}(z)\\
{\rm Col}^{\flat}_{v}(z)\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}{\rm Col}_{v,n}(z)\\
-\xi_{n-1}{\rm Col}_{v,n-1}(z)\end{bmatrix}\mod\omega_{n},

where
\xi_{n-1}:\Lambda_{v,n-1}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{v,n}
is the norm map.
Further, the Coleman maps
{\rm Col}^{\sharp}_{v},{\rm Col}^{\flat}_{v}
are surjective onto
\Lambda_{v}
.

Proof.

This is Corollary 4.5 and Proposition 4.7 in
[
BBL24
]
applied to our case.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
J_{v}:=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in\Lambda_{v}^{\oplus 2}\mid(p-1)g_{1}(0)=(2-a_{p})g_{2}(0)\}
. Then we have

\mathrm{Image}({\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}\oplus{\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat})\subseteq J_{v}.

Proof.

On taking
n=1
in Theorem
4.2
, we have

(4.2)

\begin{bmatrix}a_{p}&1\\
-\Phi_{1}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\rm Col}^{\sharp}_{v}(z)\\
{\rm Col}^{\flat}_{v}(z)\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}{\rm Col}_{v,1}(z)\\
-\xi_{0}{\rm Col}_{v,0}(z)\end{bmatrix}\mod\omega_{1}.

Combined with Theorem
3.2
(ii), we deduce that

\begin{bmatrix}a_{p}&1\\
-p&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\rm Col}^{\sharp}_{v}(z)\\
{\rm Col}^{\flat}_{v}(z)\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}\left(a_{p}-\frac{p-1}{a_{p}-2}\right){\rm Col}_{v,0}(z)\\
-p{\rm Col}_{v,0}(z)\end{bmatrix}\mod X

Thus,

a_{p}{\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}(z)+{\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat}(z)\equiv\left(a_{p}-\frac{p-1}{a_{p}-2}\right){\rm Col}_{v,0}(z),\quad{\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}(z)\equiv{\rm Col}_{v,0}(z)\mod X.

Combining these two congruences gives

(p-1){\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}(z)\equiv(2-a_{p}){\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat}(z)\mod X.

In particular,
\mathrm{Image}({\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}\oplus{\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat})\subseteq J_{v}
, as required.∎

Theorem 4.4
.

We have the equality
\mathrm{Image}({\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}\oplus{\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat})=J_{v}.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{v}
denote the residue field of
\mathcal{O}_{v}
and fix a uniformizer
\varpi
. We write
\Omega=\Lambda_{v}/(\varpi)
.
Let
\underline{{\rm Col}}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},T_{v})\longrightarrow\Omega^{\oplus 2}
denote the map given by
{\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}\oplus{\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat}
modulo
\varpi
and write
\underline{J_{v}}
for the image of
J_{v}\subset\Omega^{\oplus 2}
under the reduction map. It follows immediately from Proposition
4.3
that

\mathrm{Image}(\underline{{\rm Col}})\subseteq\underline{J_{v}}.

We claim that the theorem would follow from the equality
\mathrm{Image}(\underline{{\rm Col}})=\underline{J_{v}}
. Indeed, if this equality holds, given any
x\in J_{v}
, we can write
x=y+pz
for some
y\in\mathrm{Image}({\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}\oplus{\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat})
and
z\in\Lambda_{v}^{\oplus 2}
. This implies that
x-y=pz\in J_{v}\bigcap p\Lambda_{v}^{\oplus 2}
. A direct calculation shows that this intersection is
pJ_{v}
. Consequently, we deduce that
J_{v}=\mathrm{Image}({\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}\oplus{\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat})+pJ_{v}
. Nakayama’s lemma then implies
J_{v}=\mathrm{Image}({\rm Col}_{v}^{\sharp}\oplus{\rm Col}_{v}^{\flat})
.

We verify the equality
\mathrm{Image}(\underline{{\rm Col}})=\underline{J_{v}}
in the remainder of the proof. Since
\Omega^{\oplus 2}/\underline{J_{v}}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{v}
, it is enough to show that the cokernel of
\underline{{\rm Col}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{v}
. We prove this following a similar strategy as
[
KP07
, the proof of Proposition 1.2]
.

Let
\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{n})}^{\star}
denote the
{\mathbb{F}}_{v}[G_{n}]
-submodule of
\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{n})}:=\widehat{A}(k_{n})/(\varpi)
generated by
d_{n}
. We can check that
\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{n})}^{\star}\bigcap\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{n-1})}^{\star}=\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
for all
n\geq 1
. Combined with Proposition
3.3
gives the following short exact sequence

(4.3)

0\rightarrow\underline{\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}\rightarrow\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{n})}^{\star}\oplus\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{n-1})}^{\star}\rightarrow\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{n})}\rightarrow 0

as in
[
Kob03
, Proposition 8.12]
.

For
\natural\in\{\sharp,\flat\}
, let
\mathcal{P}^{\natural}
be the image of
\ker({\rm Col}_{v}^{\natural})
in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},T_{v})/(\varpi)
. Since the matrix
\mathcal{C}_{i}(X)
is congruent to
\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-\Phi_{i}&0\end{bmatrix}
modulo
\varpi
, it follows from Theorem
4.2
that

\mathcal{P}^{\sharp}=\varinjlim\left(\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{2n})}^{\star}\right)^{\perp},\quad\mathcal{P}^{\flat}=\varinjlim\left(\underline{\widehat{A}(k_{2n+1})}^{\star}\right)^{\perp},

where
(-)^{\perp}
denotes the orthogonal complement under the pairing

H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},T_{v})/(\varpi)\times\widehat{A}(k_{\infty})/(\varpi)\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{v}

induced from the local Tate duality

H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},T_{v})\times\left(\widehat{A}(k_{\infty})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}F_{v}/\mathcal{O}_{v}\right)\rightarrow F_{v}/\mathcal{O}_{v}

after identifying
\widehat{A}(k_{\infty})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}F_{v}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
with
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},A[v^{\infty}])
(which we can do since
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},A[v^{\infty}])/\widehat{A}(k_{\infty})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}F_{v}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
is the Pontryagin dual of the universal norm
\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}\widehat{A}(k_{n})
, which is 0 when
A
is supersingular at
p
; see
[
Sch87
, Theorem 1]
).

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.3
) gives the following short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\underline{\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}\rightarrow\left(\mathcal{P}^{\sharp}\right)^{\perp}\oplus\left(\mathcal{P}^{\flat}\right)^{\perp}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(k_{\infty},A[v^{\infty}])\rightarrow 0.

By duality, we deduce that the cokernel of
\underline{{\rm Col}}
is isomorphic to
\underline{\widehat{A}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}^{\vee}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{v}
, as desired.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In prior works on supersingular abelian varieties, the anti-block diagonal property of the logarithmic matrices related to the Coleman maps is sometimes assumed explicitly, which is equivalent to the hypothesis that
a_{p}=0
in the setting of
{\rm GL}_{2}
-type abelian varieties (see
[
LP20
,
DR25
]
). Our approach allows us to relax this hypothesis, where we study the joint image of the signed Coleman maps. This plays a crucial role in analyzing the growth of the
v
-primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich group via auxiliary
\mathcal{O}_{v}
-modules.

5.

Definition 5.1

Appendix A
Honda-type of formal groups

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Let
n\geq 1
be an integer, and let
{\mathbf{x}}
be the column vector of
n
variables
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{t}
. Define

to be the ring of formal power series in
n
variables. Write

for the set of column vectors
\left(\varphi_{1}({\mathbf{x}}),\ldots,\varphi_{n}({\mathbf{x}})\right)^{t}

\varphi_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\in R[[{\mathbf{x}}]]

\varphi({\mathbf{x}}),\psi({\mathbf{x}})\in R[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{n}
differ only in terms of total degree
\geq r

\varphi({\mathbf{x}})\equiv\psi({\mathbf{x}})\mod\deg r
. We write

for the set
\{f\in R[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{n}\mid f({\mathbf{x}})\equiv 0\mod\deg 1\}
.

Define the identity function

by

. If
\varphi({\mathbf{x}})
is an element of
R[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{n}_{0}
such that
\varphi({\mathbf{x}})\equiv P{\mathbf{x}}\mod\deg 2

\varphi^{-1}({\mathbf{x}})\in R[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{n}_{0}
satisfying
\varphi\circ\varphi^{-1}=\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi=i

\varphi
is invertible and
\varphi^{-1}

\varphi

Definition A.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{x}}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{t}

n
variables. An

-

\widehat{F}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})\equiv{\mathbf{x}}+{\mathbf{y}}\mod\deg 2

(ii)

\widehat{F}(\widehat{F}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}),{\mathbf{z}})=\widehat{F}({\mathbf{x}},\widehat{F}({\mathbf{y}},{\mathbf{z}}))

(iii)

\widehat{F}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=\widehat{F}({\mathbf{y}},{\mathbf{x}})
.

.

Let
\widehat{F}
and
\widehat{G}
be formal groups over
R
, of dimension
n
and
m
, respectively. An element

to

\varphi

:
\widehat{F}\approx\widehat{G}
over
S
.

Zinc Phosphide Poisoning in Pregnancy: A Rare Case Report From Nepal
Authors: Nabin Pahari, Sushant Shrestha, Sagun Ghimire, Mukesh Pahari, Anmol Singh Shrestha, Sahas Chhetri, Mrinal Silwal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTZinc phosphide (Zn3P2) poisoning during pregnancy is exceedingly unusual and potentially lethal, with no previously known occurrences globally. We present a 26‐year‐old woman, gravida 2 para 1, at 29 weeks gestation, who took 15 g of Zn3P2 in a suicidal attempt and experienced non‐projectile vomiting. Her vital signs were stable (BP 110/80 mmHg, GCS 15/15), but she experienced transitory hepatic dysfunction that improved on Day 4 with supportive therapy such as coconut oil gastric lavage, intravenous fluids, and pantoprazole. The fetal scan confirmed viability (heart rate of 158 bpm). The high mortality of Zn3P2 (37%–100%) is caused by phosphine gas‐induced suppression of mitochondrial respiration. This case emphasizes the lack of a particular antidote and the significance of immediate decontamination, hepatic monitoring, and mental follow‐up following purposeful consumption.

Introduction

Zinc Phosphide (Zn3P2) is one of the most commonly used rodenticides in developing countries like Nepal [1]. The use of Zn3P2 for suicidal purposes in Nepal is about 26.7% which is much more than that of cypermethrin (14.9%), the combination product Chlorpyrifos 50% and Cypermethrin 5% (13.9%), aluminium phosphide (8.1%) and dichlorvos (7.7%) [2]. Following the ingestion of Zn3P2, it reacts with water and hydrochloric acid in the stomach, where phosphine (PH3) is formed which is the primary toxic agent [3]. However, there are some literatures available that have doubt in this PH3 formation in case of Zn3P2 ingestion because of its unique clinical presentation [4]. A comprehensive literature search across multiple databases like PubMed/PubMed Central, Google Scholar, Embase and Scopus up to 3 July 2025 found no previously reported cases of zinc phosphide poisoning in pregnancy with a successful outcome.

Case History & Examination

A 26‐year‐old pregnant woman, gravida 2, para 1, living 1, at 29 weeks of gestation by last menstrual period, presented to the emergency department with a history of multiple episodes of non‐projectile vomiting approximately 3 h after the intentional ingestion of around 15 g of zinc phosphide following a domestic dispute with her husband. On presentation, her vital signs were stable with a blood pressure of 110/80 mmHg, a pulse rate of 78 beats per min, a respiratory rate of 14 breaths per min, and a temperature of 36°C. She was alert, oriented to time, place, and person, but appeared ill‐looking and slightly agitated.

Investigations and Treatment

Emergency management included gastric lavage using coconut oil via a nasogastric tube. Intravenous access was secured, and she was administered 500 mL of 0.9% normal saline, 80 mg of pantoprazole, and 4 mg of ondansetron. Initial laboratory investigations including complete blood count, renal function test, liver function test, and arterial blood gas analysis were within normal limits. Obstetric ultrasound revealed a single live intrauterine fetus corresponding to 28 weeks and 5 days gestation, with normal cardiac activity at 158 bpm, anterior placenta, unstable presentation, and an estimated fetal weight of 377 ± 56 g, with no gross congenital anomalies noted. Fetal heart rate was recorded hourly using Doppler ultrasound till discharge showing normal limits. The patient was transferred to the intensive care unit for close monitoring and supportive care. She was managed with intravenous fluids, proton pump inhibitors, prophylactic antibiotics, and midazolam to control her agitation. On the second day, her liver enzymes showed mild elevation with SGOT at 97 IU/L, SGPT at 90 IU/L, and prothrombin time of 17 s. By the third day, her blood values had improved and were decreasing and she was transferred to the obstetric unit. Outcome and Follow‐Up

On the fourth day of hospitalization, the patient was discharged in stable condition after psychiatric evaluation with a diagnosis of moderate depression with suicidal intent and was prescribed sertraline 100 mg once daily. She was advised to follow‐up in 2 weeks with repeat liver function tests. At her 2‐week follow‐up visit, the patient was clinically stable and her liver function tests had returned to normal, indicating full recovery. At 37 weeks of gestation, the patient came with labor pain and underwent normal vaginal delivery. There was no complication during child birth and the neurodevelopmental status of the child was also normal.

Discussion

This is the first case report of Zinc phosphide poisoning in a pregnant female to our knowledge. Zinc phosphide is one of the highly poisonous rodenticides with a mortality rate of 37%–100% which is crystalline and dark gray in color [5]. A dose of 4 to 5 g of zinc phosphide (55–70 mg/kg) can cause human deaths in acute toxicity [3]. In our case, instead of a high dose of zinc phosphide consumption reasons like early presentation to the hospital, hyperemesis and early initiation of gastric lavage with coconut oil led to a good outcome for the patient. In cases of poisoning from phosphides we generally use coconut or castor oil instead of conventional normal saline as these agents act as a barrier for zinc phosphide and water interaction for the formation of phosphine gas. The use of coconut or castor oil might sometimes cause lipoid pneumonia but only a few cases have been reported so far and its benefits outweigh its harms [6]. In the stomach, Zn3P3 converts to phosphine gas in the presence of moisture and acid which is then absorbed through the mucosa and transported to the tissues. Within 5 h of exposure, it significantly reduces mitochondrial membrane potential, slows oxidative respiration by 70% and affects mitochondrial shape as well [7]. It also inhibits cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) in the mitochondrial respiratory chain. This inhibition disrupts oxidative phosphorylation, leading to a lack of ATP production, increased free radicals, cell death, and multi‐organ failure [8].

The exact mechanism of toxicity remains unclear. Some authors even mention that there is formation of phosphonium ion (PH4 +) as an intermediate product which is created and passes through the stomach and then reduces to produce PH3 in the luminal tract [4]. Unlike aluminium phosphide, Zinc Phosphide appears less toxic because Zn3P2 is a relatively more stable compound as the formation of phosphine from Zinc Phosphide after contact with acid or moisture is slightly slower and Zn3P2 requires pH < 4 whereas aluminium phosphide and magnesium phosphide can produce PH3 in neutral ph as well [9, 10]. Abdominal pain, palpitation, profuse vomiting and dyspnea are the main symptoms of Zn3P2 toxicity. It affects all cardiovascular, respiratory, gastrointestinal systems and causes electrolyte abnormalities. No specific antidote is present and the main treatment is supportive care [11]. Vitamin K (phytomenadione) can be given if the Prothrombin time is prolonged [12]. Although standardized criteria for liver transplantation in acute fulminant hepatic failure are not yet established, evidence from a study in Kerala suggests that a MELD score ≥ 31 by the sixth day, or the onset of encephalopathy at any time post‐ingestion, is associated with a high risk of mortality in the absence of transplantation [13]. In our case we used coconut oil but instead of it castor oil and liquid paraffin can also be used. A study from Shaakori et al. showed that decontamination by castor oil has been proven beneficial as it can reduce toxin absorption [14]. The use of magnesium sulphate, N‐acetyl cysteine and Alpha lipoic acid in Zinc Phosphide toxicity is controversial. As Zn3P2 is a radio‐opaque substance, a recent study by Hassanian Moghaddam et al. suggested performing abdominal X‐rays even though intoxicated patients by Zinc Phosphide are asymptomatic. Positive findings in X‐rays were shown as a poor prognostic sign [15]. The exact mechanism of the development of coagulopathy and increase of SGOT/SGPT wasn't known but in our case it may be due to direct toxic damage of PH3 to the liver after being transported by the portal vein [16]. In pregnant patients with zinc phosphide poisoning, delayed gastric emptying may prolong phosphine gas exposure, while the physiological increase in plasma volume, although potentially diluting the toxin, can exacerbate fluid losses and thereby heighten the risk of shock. Pregnancy‐related alterations in hepatic metabolism, together with the direct hepatotoxic effects of phosphine gas, may accelerate the progression of liver injury and failure. These physiological changes can intensify disease severity and complicate the clinical course, thereby posing additional challenges to supportive management [17, 18, 19, 20]. Sertraline is used as an antidepressant in this case because of its fewer side effects to both mother and child as the concentration of Sertraline is also low in fetal blood in comparison to mother [21].

Conclusion

Zinc Phosphide is a rodenticide with a high mortality rate, among which most deaths occur within 12–24 h post ingestion. It has no antidote and treatment is truly based on supportive care. Early decontamination by gastric lavage with castor or coconut oil is the crucial thing.

Error analysis for a Finite Element Discretization of a radially symmetric harmonic map heat flow problem
Nam Anh Nguyen, Arnold Reusken

Abstract
We consider the harmonic map heat flow problem for a radially symmetric case. For discretization of this problem we apply a H 1 H^{1} -conforming finite element method in space combined with a semi-implicit Euler time stepping. The semi-implicit Euler method results in a linear problem in each time step. We restrict to the regime of smooth solutions of the continuous problem and present an error analysis of this discretization method. This results in optimal order discretization error bounds. Key ingredients of the analysis are a discrete energy estimate, that mimics the energy dissipation of the continuous solution, and a convexity property that is essential for discrete stability and for control of the linearization error. We also present numerical results that validate the theoretical ones.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N=2,3
, be a Lipschitz domain and
S^{2}
the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The harmonic map heat flow (HMHF) problem is as follows. Given an initial condition
\mathbf{u}_{0}:\Omega\to S^{2}
, determine
\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t):\Omega\to S^{2}
such that

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}=\Delta\mathbf{u}+\rvert\nabla\mathbf{u}\lvert^{2}\mathbf{u},\quad\mathbf{u}(\cdot,0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\quad\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t)_{|\partial\Omega}=(\mathbf{u}_{0})_{|\partial\Omega},\quad t\in(0,T].

(1)

This problem is obtained as the
L^{2}
gradient flow of the Dirichlet energy

E(\mathbf{v}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,dx

(2)

of vector fields
\mathbf{v}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
that satisfy a pointwise unit length constraint. Unit length minimizers of this Dirichlet energy are called harmonic maps. This problem is closely related to the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation. The HMHF equation can be considered as the limit of the LLG equation where the precessional term vanishes and only damping is left
[
29
]
.
Harmonic maps, HMHF and LLG equations have numerous practical applications, for example, in the modeling of ferromagnetic materials or of liquid crystals, cf. e.g.
[
27
,
28
,
32
,
24
]
.

There is an extensive mathematical literature in which topics related to well-posedness, weak formulations, regularity, blow-up phenomena and convergence of solutions of the HMHF problem to harmonic maps are studied, cf. e.g
[
35
,

,
17
,

,

,

,

,

]
.

Early work on the development and analysis of numerical methods for HMHF or LLG problems is found in

,

,

,

,

,

,

[

,

,

,

,

,

]
.

In this paper, we study the HMHF problem for a specific
radially symmetric
case. Assume
\Omega
is the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and assume the solution
\mathbf{u}
to be radially symmetric. Using polar coordinates on the disk a special type of solution of (
1
) is given by

\mathbf{u}(r,\psi,t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos{\psi}\sin{u(t,r)}\\
\sin{\psi}\sin{u(t,r)}\\
\cos{u(t,r)}\end{pmatrix},

(3)

with a scalar unknown function
u=u(t,r)
on
[0,T]\times[0,1]
.
Note that due to the structure of the solution
\mathbf{u}
in (

This leads to the following symmetric HMHF problem.
Given
0<T<\infty
,
I:=[0,1]
and
u_{0}\in C^{2}(I)
with
u_{0}(0)=u_{0}(1)=0
and
|u_{0}|<\pi
,
determine
u=u(t,r)
such that

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u&=\partial_{rr}u+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}u-\frac{\sin{(2u)}}{2r^{2}}\quad\text{for }r\in I,t\in(0,T],\\
u(0,r)&=u_{0}(r)\quad\text{for }r\in I,\\
u(t,0)&=u(t,1)=0\quad\text{for }t\in[0,T].\end{split}

(4)

Equation (
4
) has been used in models of nematic liquid crystals
[

]
. More importantly,
the radially symmetric case (
4
) plays a fundamental role in the analysis of HMHF.
In the seminal works
[
15
,

]
the authors study finite time singularities of (

)
for the two-dimensional case

. In these studies the radially symmetric case (
4
)
plays a crucial role. It is shown that for this problem with initial data
u_{0}
with
|u_{0}|\leq\pi
a unique global smooth solution exists, whereas for the case with
|u_{0}(1)|>\pi
the solution blows up in finite time, meaning that for the solution
u
the derivative at

becomes arbitrary large:
\lim_{x\downarrow 0}|u_{x}(x,t)|\to\infty
for
t\uparrow T_{\rm crit}
.
The work
[
15
]
has motivated further investigations of the blow-up behavior, e.g.,
[
12
,
37
]
where infinitely many solutions of (
4
) are constructed whose energy is bounded by the initial energy for all times
t
, but can
in
crease at certain points
t
, even for a smooth initial condition.
A further related topic is the analysis of the blow up rate. Several theoretical aspects of blow up rates of solutions of (
4
) are studied in
[
38
,
6
,
33
]
.
In a series of works, Gustafson
et al.

[

,
20
,
21
]
investigate the well-posedness and regularity of the
m
-equivariant version of (
4
). Hocquet
[
25
]
has studied the finite-time singularity of a stochastic version of (
4
).

The energy
E(\cdot)
in (
2
) can be rewritten in terms of
u
. We have
E(\mathbf{u})=2\pi\mathcal{E}(u)
with

\mathcal{E}(v):=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left((\partial_{r}v)^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}v}{r^{2}}\right)r\mathrm{d}r.

(5)

There are only very few papers in which numerical aspects of (
4
) are treated.
The paper
[
23
]
treats a moving mesh ansatz for the discretization of (
4
), based on finite differences in space combined with an ODE solver, that is used for capturing the blow up behavior of the solution with their numerical method.
In
[
31
]
, a first error analysis of a finite difference discretization of (
4
) is presented in the regime of smooth solutions. The main tool to establish stability is the use of
M
-matrix theory. An optimal discretization error bound (apart from a logarithmic term) is derived.

The main contribution of this paper is an error analysis for a finite element discretization of (
4
).
We restrict to the regime of smooth solutions of the continuous problem.
We consider an
H^{1}
-conforming finite element discretization in space combined with a semi-implicit Euler method in time. The semi-implicit Euler method results in a linear problem in each time step.
We summarize the main ingredients of our analysis. The energy
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
given in (
5
) is
not
convex. There, however, is a hidden convexity in the following sense. We use an energy splitting of the form

\mathcal{E}(v)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F\big(v(r)\big)}{r^{2}}r\,\mathrm{d}r,

where
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}_{r}}
is a weighted
H^{1}
-norm, cf. (
6
) below. It turns out that the function
F
used in this splitting
is convex
. As far as we know, this convexity property and energy relation have not been discussed in any other work on equation (
4
) so far. We also
show that, for functions
v
with sufficiently small energy (as made precise in Lemma
1
), one can bound
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}
by an explicit function of
\mathcal{E}(v)
.
Both the convexity property and this relation between
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
are essential for deriving a discrete energy dissipation property (Theorem
4
) and discrete stability (Corollary
5
).
The derivation of a sharp discretization error bound, cf. Theorem
9
, relies on the classical splitting of the error using the Galerkin projection. Due to conformity of the finite element space, we can use standard interpolation error bounds. The challenging part is to deal with the linearization error which requires a
L^{\infty}
-bound on the Galerkin projection of the continuous solution
scaled
by
1/r
, cf. Lemma
8
. Combining this bound with a suitable convexity argument, we are able to control the linearization error in a satisfactory way.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the variational formulation and derive a convexity property. In Section
3
, we formulate the finite element discretization and derive discrete energy dissipation and discrete stability results. A sharp bound for the discretization error is derived in Section
4
. We validate our theoretical findings in Section
5
with a numerical example that demonstrates convergence rates and energy dissipation. The source code of the numerical experiment can be found in
[
30
]
and is based on the software package
Netgen/NGSolve
.

2
Variational formulation

For a finite element discretization of this problem, we need a suitable variational formulation, that we now introduce. For
u,v\in H^{1}(I)
, we define the scalar products

\begin{split}(v,w)_{0,r}&:=\int_{0}^{1}v(r)w(r)r\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad(v,w)_{1,r}:=(\partial_{r}v,\partial_{r}w)_{0,r},\\
(v,w)_{H^{1}_{r}}&:=(v,w)_{1,r}+\left(\frac{v}{r},\frac{w}{r}\right)_{0,r}.\end{split}

(6)

Corresponding norms are denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{0,r}
,
\|\cdot\|_{1,r}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H_{r}^{1}}
, respectively.
The use of the scaling with
r
in these scalar products is natural because the problem (
4
) originates from a transformation to polar coordinates. We define the spaces

\displaystyle L^{2}_{r}

\displaystyle:=\left\{v:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{0,r}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle H_{r,0}^{1}

\displaystyle:=\left\{v\in L^{2}_{r}:\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}<\infty,v(0)=v(1)=0\right\}.

The solution
u
of (
4
) solves the variational problem

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}u,v\right)_{0,r}+\left(u,v\right)_{H^{1}_{r}}=\left(\frac{2u-\sin{(2u)}}{2r^{2}},v\right)_{0,r}\quad\text{for all}~t\in[0,T],~v\in H_{r,0}^{1}.

(7)

Furthermore, a smooth solution of (
7
) solves (
4
). In the remainder, we consider the variational formulation (
7
), which will be the basis of a finite element discretization.

We briefly comment on the interpretation of (
7
) as an
L^{2}_{r}
gradient flow problem. Using the definition (
5
), we obtain that the
L^{2}_{r}
gradient flow

(\partial_{t}u,v)_{0,r}=-\left(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(u),v\right)_{0,r},\quad\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(u)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial u},

coincides with the variational problem (
7
).
Taking
v=\partial_{t}u
in this gradient flow equation we obtain the energy decay property

\mathcal{E}(u(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{0,r}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathcal{E}(u_{0}).

(8)

We derive a few further properties that will be used in the analysis of the finite element discretization in the following sections. For this we introduce

\displaystyle F(v)

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(v^{2}-\sin^{2}v\right),

(9)

\displaystyle f(v)

\displaystyle:=F^{\prime}(v)=v-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2v),

(10)

for which the fundamental relation

\mathcal{E}(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F\big(u(r)\big)}{r^{2}}r\mathrm{d}r

(11)

holds.
The result in the following lemma, which is derived using a technique introduced in
[
34
, Lemma 2.3]
, bounds the maximum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}
of a function by its energy. We define, for
b>0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{b}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{r,0}~|~\mathcal{E}(u)\leq 2b\}.

Lemma 1
.

For all
u\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
the following holds:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\infty}\leq 2\arcsin\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(u)}.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle G(z):=\pi\int_{0}^{z}|\sin(s)|\mathrm{d}s.

Note that

G(-z)=\pi\int_{0}^{-z}|\sin(s)|\mathrm{d}s=-G(z),\quad G^{\prime}(z)=|\sin(z)|,

and thus
G
is odd and strictly increasing in
[-\pi,\pi]
with a strictly monotonic inverse. Take
\alpha\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
. Using
G(2\alpha\pi)=\pi(1-\cos(2\alpha\pi))
it follows that
G:\,[-2\alpha\pi,2\alpha\pi]\to[-\pi(1-\cos(2\alpha\pi)),\pi(1-\cos(2\alpha\pi))]
is bijective. With
\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\cos(2\alpha\pi))\in(0,1]
this can be rewritten as

G:\,[-2\arcsin\sqrt{\beta},2\arcsin\sqrt{\beta}]\to[-2\pi\beta,2\pi\beta].

(12)

Take
u\in\mathcal{E}_{b}
.
Then with
u(0)=0
we obtain, for any
r\in I
,

\displaystyle|G(u(r))|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{r}\partial_{\tilde{r}}G(u(\tilde{r}))\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}\right|=\pi\left|\int_{0}^{r}|\sin(u)|\partial_{\tilde{r}}u\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left((\partial_{r}u)^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}u}{r^{2}}\right)r\,\mathrm{d}r=\pi\mathcal{E}(u).

Using
\mathcal{E}(u)\leq 2
and the result in (
12
) with
\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(u)
yields the estimate
|u(r)|\leq 2\arcsin\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(u)}
for all
r\in I
. This completes the proof.
∎

Below we will use the following direct corollary of the result above:

u\in\mathcal{E}_{1}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\pi.

(13)

It will be useful to have relations between the energy
\mathcal{E}(u)
and the norm
\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}
.
These are given in the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

The following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}\quad\text{for all}~u\in H_{r,0}^{1},

(14)

\displaystyle C_{b}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(u)\quad\text{for all}~u\in\mathcal{E}_{b},~0<b<1,

(15)

with
C_{b}:=\frac{b(1-b)^{2}}{2\,\arcsin^{2}\sqrt{b}}
.

Proof.

The estimate (
14
) follows from

|\sin z|\leq|z|

for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
Take
u\in\mathcal{E}_{b},~0<b<1
. From Lemma
1
we obtain
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq 2\arcsin\sqrt{b}=:z_{b}<\pi
. Note
\sin z_{b}=2\sqrt{b}(1-b)>0
. The line through
(0,0)
and
(z_{b},\sin z_{b})
has slope
\alpha_{b}=\frac{\sqrt{b}(1-b)}{\arcsin\sqrt{b}}
, and for all
z\in[0,z_{b}]
we have
\sin z\geq\alpha_{b}z
. Using this we obtain for
r\in[0,1]

\sin^{2}u(r)=\sin^{2}|u(r)|\geq\alpha_{b}^{2}u(r)^{2},

which implies the result (
15
).
∎

From the results (
14
)-(
15
) it follows that we have an equivalence
\|\cdot\|_{H_{r}^{1}}^{2}\sim\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
on
\mathcal{E}_{b}
, provided
b<1
.

The nonlinear part of (
7
) is given by
\left(\frac{2u-\sin(2u)}{2r^{2}},v\right)_{0,r}=\left(\frac{f(u)}{r^{2}},v\right)_{0,r}
. Note that due to the
r^{2}
in the denominator there is a blow up effect for
r\downarrow 0
. In the analysis below, to control this term we will use a convexity argument. The energy
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
is
not
convex, but the function
F
, cf. the relation (
11
), is convex, since
F^{\prime\prime}(z)=f^{\prime}(z)=1-\cos(2z)\geq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
. This will be used to derive the results in the next lemma, which play a key role in finite element error analysis.

Lemma 3
.

The following holds:

\displaystyle f(z_{1})(z_{2}-z_{1})

\displaystyle\leq F(z_{2})-F(z_{1})\quad\text{for all}~z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}

(16)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{f(u)}{r^{2}},w-u\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\|w\|_{H_{r}^{1}}^{2}-\|u\|_{H_{r}^{1}}^{2}\big)+\mathcal{E}(u)-\mathcal{E}(w)\quad\text{for all}~u,w\in H_{0,r}^{1}.

(17)

Proof.

The result (
16
) is due to the convexity of
F
and
F^{\prime}=f
.
For
u,w\in H_{0,r}^{1}
we have, using the estimate (
16
) and the fundamental relation (
11
),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{f(u)}{r^{2}},w-u\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f\big(u(r)\big)}{r^{2}}(w(r)-u(r))r\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F\big(w(r)\big)-F\big(u(r)\big)}{r^{2}}\,r\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(u)-\mathcal{E}(w)+\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\|w\|_{H_{r}^{1}}^{2}-\|u\|_{H_{r}^{1}}^{2}\big),

which proves the result (
17
).
∎

A further useful estimate that we use in the error analysis is:

\displaystyle|f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})|\leq\left(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|\quad\text{for all}~z_{i}\in\mathbb{R},

(18)

which follows from

\displaystyle|f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(z_{2}+s(z_{1}-z_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}s\right||z_{1}-z_{2}|

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}\sin^{2}(z_{2}+s(z_{1}-z_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}s\,|z_{1}-z_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}(z_{2}+s(z_{1}-z_{2}))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,|z_{1}-z_{2}|

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{3}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{1}z_{2}+z_{2}^{2})|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq\left(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

3
Discrete problem

The discrete problem that we consider uses an implicit Euler method for time discretization, where the nonlinear term in (
7
) is treated in a semi-implicit way. Due to this, in each time step there is a linear problem to be solved. For the discretization in space we use a standard finite element space.

We introduce some further notation. We use the notation
H_{0}^{1}(I)
for the standard Sobolev space with homogeneous boundary conditions.
In space we use grid points
r_{i}=ih,i=0,\ldots,N
, with
h=1/N
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We use a uniform grid to simplify the presentation. All results can easily be generalized to the case of a quasi-uniform grid. We use the
H^{1}_{0}(I)
-conforming finite element space

\displaystyle S^{k}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{v\in C^{0}(I)~|~v_{h}|_{[r_{i},r_{i+1}]}\in P_{k}\text{ for all }i=0,\ldots,N-1\},\quad k\geq 1,

\displaystyle S_{h,0}^{k}

\displaystyle:=S^{k}_{h}\cap H_{0}^{1}(I).

For discretization in time we use a fixed time step
\tau
, with
\tau J=T
for some
J\in\mathbb{N}
.
We assume a
given
initial value
u_{h}^{0}\in S_{h,0}^{k}
that approximates
u_{0}
. The choice of this approximation will be discussed below. We introduce the following
discrete problem
: For
j\geq 0
, determine
u_{h}^{j+1}\in S^{k}_{h,0}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u^{j+1}_{h}-u^{j}_{h}}{\tau},v_{h}\right)_{0,r}+\left(u_{h}^{j+1},v_{h}\right)_{H^{1}_{r}}=\left(\frac{f(u_{h}^{j})}{r^{2}},v_{h}\right)_{0,r}\quad\text{for all}~v_{h}\in S^{k}_{h,0}.

(19)

Thus in each time step we have a uniquely solvable
linear
problem.

3.1
Stability analysis

We derive a stability result for this discrete problem.

Theorem 4
.

For the solution
(u_{h}^{j})_{1\leq j\leq J}
of (
19
) the following holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{h}^{j+1})+\tfrac{1}{\tau}\left\lVert u_{h}^{j+1}-u_{h}^{j}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{h}^{j+1}-u_{h}^{j}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(u_{h}^{j}),

(20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{h}^{j+1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(u_{h}^{j}).

(21)

Proof.

We test (
19
) with
v_{h}=u_{h}^{j+1}-u_{h}^{j}
, which yields,

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\tau}\left\lVert u_{h}^{j+1}-u_{h}^{j}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert u_{h}^{j+1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}-\left\lVert u_{h}^{j}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{h}^{j+1}-u_{h}^{j}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left(\frac{f(u_{h}^{j})}{r^{2}},u_{h}^{j+1}-u_{h}^{j}\right)_{0,r}.

(22)

The estimate (
17
) yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{f(u_{h}^{j})}{r^{2}},u_{h}^{j+1}-u_{h}^{j}\right)_{0,r}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\left\lVert u_{h}^{j+1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}-\left\lVert u_{h}^{j}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}\big)+\mathcal{E}(u_{h}^{j})-\mathcal{E}(u_{h}^{j+1}).

Using this in (
22
) yields the result (
20
). The result in (
21
) is a direct consequence of (
20
).
∎

The result (
20
) is a discrete analogon of (
8
).

Corollary 5
.

If
u_{h}^{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\pi,\quad 0\leq j\leq J,

holds.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
4
and (
13
).
∎

4
Discretization error analysis

In this section we derive bounds for the discretization error of the scheme (
19
).

4.1
Preliminaries

We collect some results that will be used in the error analysis in Section
4.2
.

Lemma 6
.

The following holds:

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{0,r}\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\|v\|_{H_{r}^{1}}\leq\sqrt{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}},\quad v\in H^{1}(I)\quad\text{with}~v(0)=0.

(23)

Proof.

The first two inequalities in (
23
) follow directly from the definitions. For the third one we use
\int_{0}^{1}v^{\prime}(r)^{2}r\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\int_{0}^{1}v^{\prime}(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r
and the (Hardy-type) inequality

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{v(r)^{2}}{r}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\int_{0}^{1}v^{\prime}(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r,

which follows from

\frac{v(r)^{2}}{r}=\frac{1}{r}\left(\int_{0}^{r}v^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{r}v^{\prime}(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{1}v^{\prime}(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

and integrating this inequality.
∎

Hence, we have the embeddings
S^{k}_{h,0}\subset H^{1}_{0}\subset H^{1}_{r,0}
.

The nodal interpolation operator on
S^{k}_{h}
is denoted by
\mathcal{I}_{h}
.
By classical finite element theory, e.g.
[
13
, Chapter 4]
, we have the interpolation error bounds

\displaystyle\left\lVert v-\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{m}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{m+1}},\quad v\in H^{m+1}(I),~~0\leq m\leq k,

(24)

\displaystyle\left\lVert v-\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\rVert_{W^{1}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{m}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{m+1}_{\infty}},\quad v\in W_{\infty}^{m+1}(I),~~0\leq m\leq k.

(25)

We define the Galerkin projection
\mathcal{P}_{h}:H^{1}_{r,0}\rightarrow S^{k}_{h,0}
by

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{P}_{h}v,v_{h}\right)_{H^{1}_{r}}=\left(v,v_{h}\right)_{H^{1}_{r}}\quad\text{for all}~v_{h}\in S^{k}_{h,0}.

(26)

From the projection property, (
23
) and (
24
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{h}v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}},\quad v\in H_{r,0}^{1},

(27)

\displaystyle\left\lVert v-\mathcal{P}_{h}v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert v-\mathcal{I}_{h}v\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq ch^{m}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{m+1}},\quad v\in H_{r,0}^{1}\cap H^{m+1}(I),

(28)

for
0\leq m\leq k
.
In the analysis we need a bound for
\|\frac{v_{h}}{r}\|_{L^{\infty}}:=\max_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\frac{v_{h}(r)}{r}\right|
in terms of
\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}
for finite element functions
v_{h}\in S^{k}_{h,0}
.
We derive such a result.

Lemma 7
.

For
v_{h}\in S^{k}_{h,0}
the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{v_{h}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{-1}\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}.

(29)

Proof.

First we consider
\left\lVert\frac{v_{h}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,h])}
.
Using the transformation
r\mapsto\hat{r}=\frac{1}{h}r
,
\hat{v}_{h}(\hat{r}):=v_{h}(h\hat{r})=v_{h}(r)
and
v_{h}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{d}{dr}\hat{v}_{h}(\hat{r})=\frac{1}{h}\hat{v}_{h}^{\prime}(\hat{r})
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}v_{h}^{\prime}(r)^{2}r\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{1}\hat{v}_{h}^{\prime}(\hat{r})^{2}\hat{r}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{r}.

With a suitable norm equivalence constant
c_{k}
that depends only on the polynomial degree
k
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\hat{v}_{h}}{\hat{r}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\leq c_{k}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\hat{v}_{h}^{\prime}(\hat{r})^{2}\hat{r}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\frac{v_{h}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,h])}&=h^{-1}\left\lVert\frac{\hat{v}_{h}}{\hat{r}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\leq c_{k}h^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\hat{v}_{h}^{\prime}(\hat{r})^{2}\hat{r}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=c_{k}h^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{h}v_{h}^{\prime}(r)^{2}r\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c_{k}h^{-1}\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}.\end{split}

(30)

Now assume that
r\in[h,1]
. From
\frac{v_{h}(r)}{r}=\frac{v_{h}(h)}{r}
+
\frac{1}{r}\int_{h}^{r}v_{h}^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{v_{h}(r)}{r}\right|\leq\left\lVert\frac{v_{h}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,h])}+\frac{1}{r}\left|\int_{h}^{r}v_{h}^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|.

(31)

For the second term on the right hand side we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\left|\int_{h}^{r}v_{h}^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left|\int_{h}^{r}v_{h}^{\prime}(s)s^{\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{h}^{r}v_{h}^{\prime}(s)^{2}s\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\int_{h}^{r}\frac{1}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}h^{-1}\frac{h}{r}\left(\ln\left(\frac{r}{h}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{-1}\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}.

Using this in (
31
) and combining with (
30
) completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 8
.

For
w\in H^{k+1}(I),k\geq 1
, with
w(0)=0
the following estimate holds:

\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h}w(r)}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq c\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{k+1}(I)}.

(32)

Proof.

We start with a triangle inequality

\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h}w(r)}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h}w-\mathcal{I}_{h}w}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{I}_{h}w-w}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\frac{w}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

(33)

With
w(0)=0
we obtain

\left\lVert\frac{w}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=\left\lVert\frac{\int_{0}^{r}w^{\prime}(s)\,ds}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|w\|_{W_{\infty}^{1}(I)}.

(34)

Using the same argument (note:
(\mathcal{I}_{h}w-w)(0)=0
) and (
25
) we obtain

\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{I}_{h}w-w}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{I}_{h}w-w\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{1}(I)}\leq c\|w\|_{W_{\infty}^{1}(I)}.

(35)

Using (
29
) and (
24
) we obtain

\begin{split}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h}w-\mathcal{I}_{h}w}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\frac{c}{h}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{h}w-\mathcal{I}_{h}w\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}\\
&\leq\frac{c}{h}\left\lVert w-\mathcal{I}_{h}w\right\rVert_{H_{r}^{1}}\\
&\leq ch^{k-1}\|w\|_{H^{k+1}}.\end{split}

(36)

We use the estimates (
34
), (
35
) and (
36
) in (
33
) and with the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(I)\hookrightarrow W_{\infty}^{1}(I)
we obtain the result (
32
).
∎

4.2
Discretization error bound

We define the error
e^{j}_{h}:=u_{h}^{j}-u(t_{j})
where

solves (
4
) and
u_{h}^{j+1}
solves (

). We use the notation

.
From now on, we assume that the solution

of (

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left\{\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{W^{k+1}_{\infty}}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}u(t)\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\partial_{tt}u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right\}\leq c<\infty.

(37)

Theorem 9
.

Given
u_{0}\in H^{k+1}(I)
with
u_{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, take
u_{h}^{0}\in S_{h,0}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{1}
. For the error
e^{j}_{h}:=u_{h}^{j}-u(t_{j})
,
1\leq j\leq J
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{r}e^{j}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,r}\leq C(\tau+h^{k})+c\|u_{0}-u_{h}^{0}\|_{H_{r}^{1}},

(38)

where the constants
C
,
c
are independent of
h
,
\tau
but depend on
T
and on the regularity assumption (
37
). Assume
\|u_{0}-u_{h}^{0}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}\leq ch^{k}
. For
h
and
\tau
sufficiently small we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{j}_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}\leq C(\tau+h^{k}).

(39)

Proof.

In the proof we use
c
to denote a varying constant independent of
h
and
\tau
. We split the error in the usual way

e_{h}^{j}=\big(u_{h}^{j}-P_{h}u(t_{j})\big)+\big(P_{h}u(t_{j})-u(t_{j})\big)=:\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}+\hat{e}_{h}^{j}.

Using (
28
) we have bounds for the projection error
\hat{e}_{h}^{j}
and obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}e_{h}^{j}\|_{0,r}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{r}\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}\|_{0,r}+ch^{k},

(40)

\displaystyle\|e_{h}^{j}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}+ch^{k}.

(41)

For bounding the terms with
\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}
we need a rather technical analysis. To improve the presentation we first outline the key ingredients of the proof below:

•

We use the continuous problem and a canonical test function
v_{h}=\frac{1}{\tau}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})
to derive the recursive relation (
42
).

•

The terms
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
are bounded using standard consistency arguments. For the terms
R_{3},R_{4},R_{5}
we need linearization. We use (
18
) and Lemma
8
. For
R_{5}
we use a convexity argument. This then results in the recursive relation (
51
).

•

With the fundamental relation (
11
) this yields the recursive relation (
52
) for the energy
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})
, which can be treated using standard arguments.

•

Using
\|\partial_{r}v\|_{0,r}\leq\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}(v)}
and Lemma
2
in combination with (
40
)-(
41
) we obtain the error bounds in (
38
) and (
39
).

We now continue our proof.
Using the differential equation (
7
) and the projection property (
26
), we obtain for arbitrary
v_{h}\in S^{k}_{h,0}
the relation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\big(\mathcal{P}_{h}(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})),v_{h}\big)_{0,r}+\big(\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j+1}),v_{h}\big)_{H^{1}_{r}}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\mathcal{P}_{h}\big(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\big)-\frac{1}{\tau}\big(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\big),v_{h}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\big(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\big)-\partial_{t}u(t_{j+1}),v_{h}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{f(u(t_{j+1}))-f(u(t_{j}))}{r^{2}},v_{h}\right)_{0,r}+\left(\frac{f(u(t_{j}))-f(\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j}))}{r^{2}},v_{h}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{f(\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j}))}{r^{2}},v_{h}\right)_{0,r}.

We subtract this from (
19
), test with
v_{h}=\tfrac{1}{\tau}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})
and use Young’s inequality. Thus we get, with arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\left\lVert\tilde{e}^{j+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}-\left\lVert\tilde{e}^{j}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}+\left\lVert\tilde{e}^{j+1}_{h}-\tilde{e}^{j}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert\frac{1}{\tau}\mathcal{P}_{h}\big(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\big)-\frac{1}{\tau}\big(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\big)\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert\partial_{t}u(t_{j+1})-\frac{1}{\tau}\big(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\big)\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\left(\frac{f(u(t_{j}))-f(u(t_{j+1}))}{r^{2}},\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\left(\frac{f(\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j}))-f(u(t_{j}))}{r^{2}},\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\left(\frac{f(u_{h}^{j})-f(\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j}))}{r^{2}},\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle=:2\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}R_{1}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}R_{2}+R_{3}+R_{4}+R_{5}.

(42)

For the term
R_{1}
we obtain, using
\|v\|_{0,r}\leq\|v\|_{H_{r}^{1}}
,

\displaystyle R_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\frac{1}{\tau}\mathcal{P}_{h}\big(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\big)-\frac{1}{\tau}\big(u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\big)\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{h}\partial_{t}u(s)-\partial_{t}u(s)\right\rVert_{H_{r}^{1}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:Projection_Error_H1r}}{\leq}ch^{k}\left\lVert\partial_{t}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{k+1}(I))}

(43)

For
R_{2}
we have

R_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}(s-t_{j})\partial_{tt}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{0,r}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\tau\max_{s\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\partial_{tt}u(s)\right\rVert_{0,r}.

(44)

For the term
R_{3}
we note

\left|\frac{u(t,r)}{r}\right|=\frac{1}{r}\left|\int_{0}^{r}\partial_{r}u(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{1}(I))}.

(45)

Using this and (
18
) we obtain:

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{f(u(t_{j}))-f(u(t_{j+1}))}{r^{2}},\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(\frac{u(t_{j})^{2}+u(t_{j+1})^{2}}{r^{2}}|u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})|,\left|\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right|\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{1}(I))}^{2}\left\lVert u(t_{j+1})-u(t_{j})\right\rVert_{0,r}\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\tau 2\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{1}(I))}^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}_{r})}\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\frac{c_{u}}{\varepsilon}\tau^{2},

(46)

with
c_{u}=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{1}(I))}^{4}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}_{r})}^{2}
.

We now consider
R_{4}
.
We use (
18
), (
45
) and Lemma
8
and obtain

\displaystyle R_{4}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{f(u(t_{j}))-f(\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j}))}{r^{2}},\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\left(\frac{u(t_{j})^{2}+(\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j}))^{2}}{r}\left|\frac{u(t_{j})-\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j})}{r}\right|,\left|\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right|\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert\frac{u(t_{j})-\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j})}{r}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert u(t_{j})-\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j})\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{r}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:Projection_Error_H1r}}{\leq}\frac{c}{\varepsilon}h^{2k}+\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}.

(47)

For the term
R_{5}
we need the following elementary estimate

\displaystyle\sin(2x)+\sin(2y)-\sin(2x+2y)

\displaystyle=2\sin(2x)\sin(y)^{2}+2\sin(2y)\sin(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=4\cos(x)\sin(x)\sin(y)^{2}+4\cos(y)\sin(y)\sin(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=4\sin(x)\sin(y)\left(\cos(x)\sin(y)+\cos(y)\sin(x)\right)

\displaystyle=4\sin(x)\sin(y)\sin(x+y)

\displaystyle\leq 4|x||y||\sin(x+y)|.

(48)

We use this estimate and the convexity property (
16
)

\displaystyle R_{5}=\left(\frac{f(u_{h}^{j})-f(\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j}))}{r^{2}},\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}=\left(\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}-\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}{r^{2}},\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sin(2u_{h}^{j})+\sin(-2\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j}))-\sin(2\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}{r^{2}},\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1})-F(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}{r^{2}}r\,\mathrm{d}r+2\left(\frac{|u_{h}^{j}||\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j})||\sin(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})|}{r^{2}},\frac{\left|\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}\right|}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}.

(49)

We use the uniform boundness result of Corollary
5
and Lemma
8
to get

\begin{split}&2\left(\frac{|u_{h}^{j}||\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j})||\sin(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})|}{r^{2}},\frac{\left|\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}\right|}{\tau}\right)_{0,r}\\
&\leq\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert u_{h}^{j}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h}u(t_{j})}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert\frac{\sin(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}{r}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}^{j+1}_{h}-\tilde{e}^{j}_{h}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert\frac{\sin(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}{r}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}^{j+1}_{h}-\tilde{e}^{j}_{h}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}.\end{split}

(50)

We collect (
43
), (
44
), (
46
), (
47
) and (
50
) in (
49
) to obtain from (
42
)

\begin{split}&\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\left\lVert\tilde{e}^{j+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}-\left\lVert\tilde{e}^{j}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}+\left\lVert\tilde{e}^{j+1}_{h}-\tilde{e}^{j+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}}\right)\\
&\quad\leq 5\varepsilon\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}+\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\left(\tau^{2}+h^{2k}\right)\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1})-F(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}{r^{2}}r\,\mathrm{d}r+\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert\frac{\sin(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}{r}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}.\end{split}

(51)

Now choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
\left\lVert\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1}-\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}}{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,r}^{2}
can be absorbed to the left hand side. Using the fundamental relation (
11
) we arrive at

\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j+1})-\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})\right)\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\left(\tau^{2}+h^{2k}+\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})\right).

We multiply by
\tau
and thus get

\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})\leq(1+c_{1}\tau)\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j-1})+c_{2}\tau(\tau^{2}+h^{2k}).

(52)

Recursive application and using (
14
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(1+c_{1}\tau)^{i}\right)\tau(\tau^{2}+h^{2k})+(1+c_{1}\tau)^{j}\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{0})

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}e^{c_{1}T}T(\tau^{2}+h^{2k})+\tfrac{1}{2}e^{c_{1}T}\|u_{h}^{0}-\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}^{2}.

We apply the triangle inequality
\|u_{h}^{0}-\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}\leq\|u_{h}^{0}-u_{0}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}+\|u_{0}-\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}
. For the last term we use (
28
) and then absorb it in the
h^{2k}
term. This yields

\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}\leq c(\tau+h^{k})+c\|u_{h}^{0}-u_{0}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}.

Now use
\|\partial_{r}\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}\|_{0,r}\leq\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}
and
combine this with the triangle inequality in (
40
). This yields the result (
38
).
If
\|u_{h}^{0}-\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}\leq ch^{k}
holds, we have
\mathcal{E}({\tilde{e}}_{h}^{j})\leq c(\tau+h^{k})
. For
h
and
\tau
sufficiently small we thus have
\mathcal{E}({\tilde{e}}_{h}^{j})\leq K<2
, i.e,
\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{b}
with
b<1
. Using the result (
15
) and (
41
) we obtain

\|e_{h}^{j}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}\leq\|\tilde{e}_{h}^{j}\|_{H_{r}^{1}}+ch^{k}\leq c\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(\tilde{e}_{h}^{j})}+h^{k}\leq c(\tau+h^{k}),

which proves the result (
39
).
∎

Long‐term efficacy and safety of romiplostim plus immunosuppressive therapy as first‐line treatment in patients with aplastic anaemia: 2‐year interim analysis of observational study of two clinical trials
Authors: Jong Wook Lee, Kohei Hosokawa, Jun Ho Jang, Naoshi Obara, Masashi Sawa, Kensuke Usuki, Gaku Oshikawa, Masahiro Kizaki, Koji Nagafuji, Ting‐An Lin, Hitoshi Hanamoto, Yeung‐Chul Mun, Toshiro Kawakita, Kazunori Imada, June‐Won Cheong, Sung‐Soo Park, Takayuki Tabayashi, Masaru Gamou, Kenta Murotani, Hirohito Yamazaki
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Acquired aplastic anaemia (AA) is a life‐threatening bone marrow failure characterized by pancytopenia. 1 First‐line therapy typically involves immunosuppressive therapy (IST) consisting of anti‐thymocyte globulin (ATG) and ciclosporin (CsA). However, some patients do not achieve an adequate response to IST and need alternative approaches. Romiplostim (Romiplate®, Kyowa Kirin Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) is a thrombopoietin receptor agonist (TPO‐RA) that promotes trilineage haematopoiesis in patients with AA refractory to IST or eltrombopag. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 Two international phase 2/3 trials, trial 531‐003 (NCT03957694, ATG + CsA + romiplostim) and trial 531‐004 (NCT04095936, CsA + romiplostim), assessed the efficacy, safety and pharmacokinetics of romiplostim combined with IST as first‐line treatment in IST‐naïve patients. 6 , 7 These trials demonstrated high haematological response rates, but were limited to a 27‐ to 52‐week treatment and brief follow‐up. Romiplostim's long‐term effectiveness and safety, particularly in combination with IST as a first‐line treatment, remain unclear. Therefore, we conducted a multicentre, prospective, observational follow‐up of patients previously enrolled in trials 531‐003 and 531‐004 for up to 5 years (Study 531‐005 [NCT04870346]). Here, we report findings from the 2‐year interim analysis.

IST‐naïve AA patients who participated in the trials 531‐003 and 531‐004 6 , 7 and gave written informed consent to participate in this study were enrolled. Data were collected at least once every 6 months. The decision to continue or discontinue romiplostim at the end of trials 531‐003 and 531‐004 was made at the discretion of the treating physician. Definitions of re‐administration and subsequent treatments are provided in the Supplementary Materials.

For this 2‐year interim analysis, patients enrolled in trials 531‐003 and 531‐004 6 , 7 were followed between June 2020 and December 2022, with data collection continuing until August 2023 (Figure S1).

The study followed the Declaration of Helsinki and ethical guidelines and received institutional ethics approval. All participants or their representatives provided written informed consent.

The primary end‐point was the haematological response rate, complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) at 2 years and up to 5 years after romiplostim initiation in trials 531‐003 and 531‐004. This report focuses on the 2‐year data; 5‐year follow‐up data are currently under analysis. Secondary end‐points are detailed in the Supplementary Materials. Haematological response rates and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated at 1 and 2 years. The proportion of patients receiving subsequent treatments at 1 and 2 years was calculated for each of the original studies. Methods for assessing changes in haemoglobin levels and blood cell counts, overall survival and duration of haematological response are described in the Supplementary Materials. For time to first haematological response, the cumulative incidence function and Gray's test were used to evaluate time to first response and group comparisons. Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4 and R version 4.2.0.

Patient disposition is shown in Figure S2. Table S1 shows the pre‐treatment background characteristics of patients from trials 531‐003 and 531‐004. A total of 36 patients were enrolled. The median age was 46.5 years, and 69.4% of patients had severe or very severe AA. Most patients (94.4%) had a platelet count <20 000/μL, and 35 of 36 (97.2%) patients were dependent on platelet or red cell transfusions. Regarding primary end‐point results, the median time from romiplostim administration to first haematological response was 105 days in trial 531‐003, 211 days in trial 531‐004, and 116 days overall (Figure 1). The haematological response rate following romiplostim administration was 68.6% at 1 year (CR, 20.0%; PR, 48.6%) and 78.8% at 2 years (CR, 33.3%; PR, 45.5%) (Table 1). This increase reflects additional patients achieving a response during extended follow‐up. Regarding secondary end‐point results, during the 105 weeks of the present study, the platelet count, haemoglobin concentration, neutrophil count and reticulocyte count remained stable (Figures S3 and S4). The median duration of haematological response in 22 patients with haematological response at 27 weeks was 325.0 days (Table S2). Up to 2 years after starting romiplostim administration, 15 patients (41.7%) received subsequent treatments (Table S3). The overall survival rate at 2 years was 91.6% (Figure S5). Three deaths occurred in trial 531‐004; two of the three patients died of infection, and the cause of death for the remaining patient is unknown due to transfer to another institution. No patients died in trial 531‐003. Figure S6 shows the swimmer plot of the 36 patients in this study. No new cases of transformation to acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or chromosomal abnormalities were observed during the 2‐year observation period following the initial 27 weeks (Table S4).

Patient flows by treatment response in trials 531‐003 and 531‐004 are shown in Figures S7 and S8. Among the 22 patients who achieved CR or PR at 27 weeks, 6 of the 12 patients who discontinued romiplostim maintained their response. Late responders—defined as patients who achieved a response after continuing romiplostim beyond 27 weeks—were also observed. Among the 13 patients who did not respond at 27 weeks, four of the six patients who continued romiplostim subsequently achieved a response beyond 27 weeks. Eltrombopag was administered in some patients at the discretion of the treating physician. This included cases where eltrombopag was initiated after romiplostim discontinuation, or when patients were switched from romiplostim to eltrombopag after 27 weeks owing to lack of efficacy. Standard IST, using eltrombopag, has an overall survival of 97% at 2 years 8 and a known recurrence rate of approximately 30%. 9 Our data are comparable to this, and no differences were observed in the safety profile. While our study details the use of subsequent treatments, it is important to note that the rate of subsequent treatment does not necessarily reflect the recurrence rate.

Treatment emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurring after trials 531‐003 and 531‐004 were observed in 14 patients (38.9%) (Table S5). TEAEs observed after trials 531‐003 and 531‐004 included corona virus infection, aplastic anaemia (worsening of aplastic anaemia) and rash. Serious TEAEs were observed in 10 patients (27.8%) after these trials; the most frequently reported serious TEAEs were corona virus infection (3 patients) and aplastic anaemia (2 patients). Furthermore, among 6 patients receiving romiplostim at 2 years, TEAEs were observed in 2 patients after these trials. One patient experienced menstruation with increased bleeding, which was classified as a serious TEAE; however, this was due to the underlying disease, and the patient underwent a total laparoscopic hysterectomy and has since recovered. The other patient experienced iron overload. No adverse events were observed in the remaining four patients. These results indicate that extended follow‐up after romiplostim‐based first‐line therapy may result in durable remissions with acceptable safety. The present 2‐year interim analysis demonstrated a haematological response rate of 78.8% in patients with AA who had not received prior IST. The overall response rate (ORR) at 2 years was 93.3% in trial 531‐003 and 66.7% in trial 531‐004—an improvement over the 27‐week response rates of 76.5% and 41.7% respectively. 6 , 7 These findings reinforce the potential of romiplostim as an effective addition to first‐line IST. Exploratory cross‐trial analyses suggest that the triplet regimen (trial 531‐003, ATG + CsA + romiplostim) is associated with a significantly shorter time to response compared with the doublet regimen (trial 531‐004, CsA + romiplostim) (p = 0.0096). The median time to response was 105 days for the triplet regimen and 211 days for the doublet regimen, suggesting enhanced efficacy with the incorporation of ATG. A prior 4‐year follow‐up study of IST combined with eltrombopag 9 reported a 1‐year ORR of 69% among patients who responded at 6 months, comparable to the 68.6% observed at 1 year in this study. Although direct comparison is not feasible because of differences in patient characteristics, the type and duration of TPO‐RA therapy, as well as the details of subsequent treatment, likely influenced outcomes. In the present study, blood cell counts remained stable throughout follow‐up. From week 27 of trials 531‐003, 531‐004, and the 2‐year follow‐up period (study 531‐005), no new cases of AML, MDS or chromosomal abnormalities were observed. While these data support the favourable safety profile of romiplostim even with extended use, ongoing monitoring and continued long‐term surveillance are necessary to fully characterize the longer‐term safety of this regimen. Importantly, four late responders—patients who showed haematological improvement beyond 27 weeks despite initial non‐response—were identified. This pattern implies that chronic stem cell stimulation by prolonged romiplostim therapy may deepen remissions in some patients. Additionally, six patients who achieved CR or PR by week 27 sustained their response after discontinuing romiplostim, suggesting potential for durable remission off treatment. These observations support continued romiplostim use in patients without early response and raise the possibility of tapering or discontinuation in those with stable remission. However, differences in treatment duration between the two studies (27 vs. 53 weeks) and the lack of bias adjustment, as well as small sample size and regional limitations in romiplostim availability, should be considered when interpreting these findings. Finally, these are interim results, and follow‐up (up to 5 years) is essential to monitor for late toxicities or relapses.

In summary, long‐term follow‐up of AA patients treated with first‐line IST plus romiplostim shows high response rates and favourable safety. The shorter time to response with the triplet regimen, the presence of late responders and sustained remissions after discontinuation warrant further investigation and may inform future treatment strategies.

Filtrations of tope spaces of oriented matroids
Kris Shaw and Chi Ho Yuen

Abstract
. We compare three filtrations of the tope space of an oriented matroid. The first is the dual Varchenko–Gelfand degree filtration, the second filtration is from Kalinin’s spectral sequence, and the last one derives from Quillen’s augmentation filtration. We show that all three filtrations and the respective maps coincide over ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} . We also show that the dual Varchenko–Gelfand degree filtration can be made into a filtration of the ℤ \mathbb{Z} -sign cosheaf on the fan of the underlying matroid. This was previously carried out with ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} -coefficients by the first author and Renaudineau using the Quillen filtration and has applications to real algebraic geometry via patchworking.



1.
Introduction

The
tope space
of a real hyperplane arrangement
{\mathcal{A}}
is the homology of its complement. While the complement simply consists of disjoint polyhedra, the interaction with the combinatorics and (complexified) geometry of
{\mathcal{A}}
leads to interesting filtrations of the tope space.
By the Folkman–Lawrence Topological Representation Theorem
[
12
]
, every oriented matroid can be realised by a
pseudohyperplane arrangement
, and the above notions can be generalised to the setting of
oriented matroids
.
The topes of an oriented matroid are in correspondence with the connected components of the complement of the pseudoarrangement, and the role of the complexification of the complement is played by the
Salvetti complex
.

In this paper, we start with studying the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-vector space generated by the topes of an oriented matroid, which we call the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-tope space
.
In the first part of this paper we show the equality of three filtrations of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space of an oriented matroid in the literature.
The first filtration is the mod
2
reduction of the dual of the
Varchenko–Gelfand degree filtration

{\mathcal{P}}_{\bullet}
on the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space
[
27
]
. This filtration comes from the ring of Heaviside functions, and we denote its mod
2
reduction by
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\bullet}
. The second filtration is the
Kalinin filtration

{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}

[
15
]
, which is induced by a spectral sequence on a topological space with involution. The third filtration, denoted
\mathcal{Q}_{\bullet}
, is derived from
Quillen’s augmentation filtration
on initial matroids; it was introduced by Renaudineau and the first author in
[
25
]
, and further developed by Rau, Renaudineau, and the first author in
[
24
]
.
We briefly state our first main result before describing the filtrations in more detail.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be an oriented matroid.
The filtrations of the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
–tope space
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bullet}(M),{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}(M),
and
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\bullet}(M)
all coincide.

The Salvetti complex
\Sal_{M}
of an oriented matroid
[
26
]
is a CW complex playing the role of the complement of a complexified hyperplane arrangement. In fact, when
M
is the oriented matroid of a real hyperplane arrangement, the Salvetti complex is a deformation retract of (hence homotopic equivalent to) the complexification of the complement. For any oriented matroid
M
, there is an involution on
\Sal_{M}
and the fixed locus under this involution consists of a discrete collection of points corresponding to the topes of the oriented matroid.

The Varchenko–Gelfand filtration arises by recognising that the ring of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued functions over the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space can be expressed as a quotient of the polynomial ring generated by
Heaviside functions
(indicator functions of halfspaces). The degree filtration of the polynomial ring induces a filtration
{\mathcal{P}}^{\bullet}
of said ring of functions, which in turn gives a filtration of the tope space itself by taking duals (or orthogonal complements).
Varchenko proved that the dual degree filtration is equal to the
asymptotic filtration
of a real hyperplane arrangement
[
28
]
, which has applications in Lie theory and singularity theory; see Remark
3.10
for its definition.
Working from the perspective of singularity theory, Denham
[
9
]
defined an isomorphism
{a}_{p}:{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)\to H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}})
for every
p
.
We can take the mod
2
reduction of everything to obtain a filtration
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\bullet}(M)
of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space and isomorphisms
\overline{{a}}_{p}:\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{p}(M)/\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{p+1}(M)\to H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
. See also the work of Proudfoot on the relation between the Varchenko–Gelfand rings and Salvetti complexes in an equivariant setting
[
21
]
.

The second filtration is the Kalinin filtration
{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}
.
The Kalinin filtration of a real algebraic variety, or more generally, a topological space (that is reasonably nice, e.g., a CW complex) with an involution representing complex conjugation, is induced by a spectral sequence that starts with the homology of the whole space over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
and converges to the homology of the real part of the variety (respectively, the fixed locus of the space).
This spectral sequence can be thought of as a categorification of the Smith–Thom inequality.
As mentioned above, the fixed locus of the involution on
\Sal_{M}
is a collection of points corresponding to the topes of
M
. Hence we obtain a filtration of the tope space together with an isomorphism
bv_{p}:{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{K}}_{p+1}(M)\to H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
for every
p
from the spectral sequence.

The last filtration derives from Quillen’s augmentation filtration
[
22
]
, which was originally used in
[
25
]
in the case of orientations of the uniform matroid
U_{n,n+1}
.
The usual Quillen’s augmentation filtration is defined on
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})[V]
, the group algebra of a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-vector space
V
.
For a general oriented matroid
M
, denote its underlying matroid by
\underline{M}
. The collection of topes of
M

adjacent
to a complete flag of flats of
\underline{M}
has the structure of an affine subspace of
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{E}
. By choosing an arbitrary origin of this affine space, we obtain a filtration of its group algebra which is independent of the choice made.
Taking the sum of these filtrations over all complete flags yields a filtration
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bullet}
of the tope space of
M
.
Moreover,
[
25
]
(implicitly) described an isomorphism
{q}_{p}
between
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}/{\mathcal{Q}}_{p+1}
and the
p
-th graded piece
\OS_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
of the dual of the
Orlik–Solomon algebra
of
\underline{M}
.

Once we have identified the filtrations of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
–tope space in Theorem
A
, we also compare their associated maps. In order to do that, we make use of the (dual of the) isomorphism given by Björner–Ziegler
[
5
]
, denoted by
BZ^{\vee}
, between
H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\OS_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Theorem B
.

As maps from
{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{p}(M)
to
H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
,
bv_{p},\overline{{a}}_{p}
are equal. Furthermore, we have the following commutative diagram

(1)

\begin{CD}\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{p}(M)@>{\overline{{a}}_{p}}>{}>H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})\\
@V{\parallel}V{}V@V{BZ^{\vee}}V{}V\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)@>{{q}_{p}}>{}>\OS_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})\end{CD}

In particular, the three maps descend to the same isomorphism from the
p
-th associated gradeds of the filtrations to
H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
up to the isomorphism
BZ^{\vee}
as explained above.

Quillen’s filtration applied to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-tope spaces was used by Renaudineau and the first author to obtain a combinatorial spectral sequence converging to the homology of the real part of hypersurfaces arising from Viro’s patchworking. This patchworking construction has been generalised to tropical manifolds equipped with real phase structures in
[
24
]
. The result of the patchworking is the
real part
of the tropical manifold and a
sign cosheaf
on the tropical manifold which computes the homology of this real part; the sign cosheaf of a tropical space equipped with a real phase structure is a cosheaf whose stalks are the tope spaces of oriented matroids.

The above two theorems further suggest that the spectral sequence from
[
25
]
, and more generally
[
24
]
, can be thought of as a tropical/combinatorial version of Kalinin’s filtration. A similar spectral sequence for real semi-stable degenerations satisfying some conditions was recently found by Ambrosi and Manzaroli using equivariant cohomology and log geometry
[
1
]
.

The application of Quillen’s filtration to the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space does not stabilise, see Example
3.18
.
Moreover, a naive extension of the Kalinin filtration to the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space is not well-behaved, see Remark
4.7
.
However, the dual Varchenko–Gelfand filtration is defined on the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space of an oriented matroid.
Here we show that this filtration can be turned into a filtration of the
\mathbb{Z}
-sign cosheaf on the polyhedral fan of a matroid. To do this, we first establish the functoriality of the filtration with respect to taking initial matroids. In the following statement,
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
is the dual of the
Cordovil algebra
and
\tilde{{a}}_{p}
is the map introduced in Proposition
6.4
; see Section
2.3
and
6.1
for details.

Proposition 1.1
.

The dual Varchenko–Gelfand filtration is functorial with respect to initial matroids. More precisely, for any pair of flags
{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
we have
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}})\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M_{\mathcal{F}})
and the following diagram commutes

(2)

\begin{CD}{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}})@>{\tilde{{a}}_{p}}>{}>{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}};{\mathbb{Z}})\\
@V{\iota}V{}V@V{\iota}V{}V\\
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M_{\mathcal{F}})@>{\tilde{{a}}_{p}}>{}>{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M_{{\mathcal{F}}};{\mathbb{Z}}).\end{CD}

The fan of a matroid has cones corresponding to the flags of flats of the matroid, hence to its initial matroids.
The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-sign cosheaf on a matroid fan of an oriented matroid associates to each face the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space of the initial matroid corresponding to the face.
The cosheaf maps are inclusions of tope spaces.

Using the above proposition, we can then establish the following theorem for the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-sign cosheaf of an oriented matroid, which is an extension of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-sign cosheaf from
[
24
]
. We let
{\mathfrak{P}}_{p}
denote a cosheaf formed by the dual Varchenko-Gelfand filtration. Notice that in the theorem below, we replace the Orlik–Solomon algebra with the
Cordovil algebra
.

Theorem C
.

The integral dual Varchenko–Gelfand filtration produces a filtration of the
\mathbb{Z}
-sign cosheaf on the fan (of the underlying matroid) of an oriented matroid,

{\mathfrak{P}}_{d}\subset\dots\subset{\mathfrak{P}}_{p+1}\subset{\mathfrak{P}}_{p}\subset\dots\subset{\mathfrak{P}}_{1}\subset\mathcal{S}.

Moreover, for every
p
there is a short exact sequence of cosheaves

0\to{\mathfrak{P}}_{p+1}\to{\mathfrak{P}}_{p}\to\mathfrak{A}_{p}\to 0,

where
\mathfrak{A}_{p}
is the
p
-th Cordovil cosheaf.

Via the spectral sequence of the above filtration, the Cordovil cosheaves on a tropical manifold with a real phase structure provide information on the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-homology groups of the real part. In future work, it will be interesting to compare the homology of these Cordovil cosheaves with the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tropical homology cosheaves

[
14
]
.

2.
Background

2.1.
Oriented Matroids

We follow the conventions for oriented matroids from
[
4
]
and we mostly use the covector description of oriented matroids
[
4
, Section 4]
.
The
covectors
of an oriented matroid
M
are elements of
\{0,+,-\}^{E}
where
E
is the ground set of
M
. See
[
4
, Section 4.1]
for the set of axioms for covectors.
The partial ordering of the covectors of
M
is induced via the relation
0<\pm
, and
+
and
-
are incomparable.
The covector lattice of an oriented matroid
M
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}(M)
, where we drop the
M
when the oriented matroid is clear from context.

When an oriented matroid is realised by a real hyperplane arrangement consisting of hyperplanes indexed by
E
, the covectors are precisely the sign vectors encoding the relative position of the points in the ambient space (see Example
2.1
): we fix a positive halfspace for the
i
-th hyperplane, and record which side a point is in (including the case of being on the hyperplane itself) by the
i
-th coordinate of the covector.
The hyperplanes provide a cellular decomposition of the ambient space, and the collection of covectors index the cells.
By the Folkman–Lawrence Topological Representation Theorem
[
12
]
, every oriented matroid arises from a
pseudohyperplane arrangement
, which allows us to extend the above intuition to all oriented matroids.
The topes of an oriented matroid are in correspondence with the connected components of the complement of the pseudoarrangement and all points of the ambient space can be assigned a covector upon choosing a positive side of each pseudohyperplane.

Example 2.1
.

The real hyperplane arrangement in Figure
1
consists of three hyperplanes
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}
.
For each hyperplane, the positive halfspace is pointed out by the arrows in the figure.
The arrangement defines an oriented matroid with 13 covectors as labeled by letters. Some examples as sign vectors are
T_{1}=+++,T_{3}=--+,\zeta_{2}=-0+,\zeta_{4}=0--,O=000
. The underlying matroid is
U_{2,3}.

Figure 1.
A real hyperplane arrangement realising
U_{2,3}
.

Every oriented matroid
M
has an underlying matroid
\underline{M}
. The
flats
of
\underline{M}
are obtained from the covectors of
M
by considering
E\backslash\supp(L)
over all covectors
L\in\mathcal{L}(M)
, here
\supp(L)\subset E
is the support of the vector. We will sometimes speak of flats of the oriented matroid
M
, meaning flats of
\underline{M}
.

We also make use of the rank function of the underlying matroid which we denote by
\rank\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
A
circuit
of
\underline{M}
is a subset
C\subset E
such that
\rank(C\setminus i)=\rank(C)=|C|-1
for any
i\in A
. An oriented matroid
M
can as well be described by its collections of
signed circuits
, which are elements of
\{+,-,0\}^{E}
supported on circuits of
\underline{M}

[
4
, Section 3.2]
. A
loop
of
\underline{M}
(hence
M
) is an element
i\in E
such that
\rank(\{i\})=0
.
Throughout we assume that the oriented matroid
M
is loopless.

There are operations of deletion and contraction on oriented matroids.
If
M
is the oriented matroid on ground set
E
obtained from an pseudohyperplane arrangement then for
i\in E
, the deletion
M\backslash i
is the oriented matroid arising from removing the
i
-th pseudohyperplane from the arrangement. The contraction
M/i
is the oriented matroid obtained by restricting the pseudohyperplane arrangement to the
i
-th sphere. The deletion and contraction operations can be defined for arbitrary subsets of
E
. Moreover, the restriction of
M
to
I\subset E
is the oriented matroid
M|_{I}=M\backslash(E\backslash I)
. The operations of deletion and contraction commute which allow us to define initial oriented matroids.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}:\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{t}=E
be a flag of flats of a loopless oriented matroid
M
.
The initial oriented matroid
M_{{\mathcal{F}}}
with respect to the flag is the (necessarily loopless) oriented matroid

(3)

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t-1}M|_{F_{i+1}}/F_{i},

with the same ground set
E
.

Since we assume that
M
is loopless, the covectors of
M
that are maximal with respect to the partial order are contained in
\{+,-\}^{E}
and are called the
topes
of
M
. The set of topes of
M
is denoted by

\mathcal{T}(M)\subseteq\{+,-\}^{E}.

Let
S\subset E
, the restriction of a tope
T
of
M
to
\{+,-\}^{S}
is always a tope of
M|_{S}
, and also a tope of
M/(E\setminus S)
as long as
S
is a flat.
For a tope
T
of
M
and a subset
F\subseteq E
, the sign vector
T\setminus F
is obtained from
T
by setting all coordinates in
F
to
0
. More precisely,

(T\setminus F)_{e}=\begin{cases}T_{e}\text{ \ \ if }e\not\in F\\
0\text{ \ \ \ if }e\in F.\end{cases}

This is not to be confused with the operation of matroid deletion.

The composition operation
\circ
on covectors
L
and
K
of
M
is defined by

(L\circ K)_{e}=\begin{cases}L_{e}\text{ \ \ if }L_{e}\neq 0\\
K_{e}\text{ \ \ \ if }L_{e}=0.\end{cases}

The tuple
L\circ K\in\{0,+,-\}^{E}
is another covector of
M
.
Hence,
L\circ T
is always a tope of
M
for any covector
L
and tope
T
.

2.2.
Salvetti Complexes

Let
M
be an oriented matroid on ground set
E
of rank
d
.
Recall that the complement of the support
\supp(L)
of a covector
L
is a flat of the underlying matroid, and we define the dimension of a covector as the rank of such a flat.

The
Salvetti complexes
are topological models for the “complexification” of
M
: as stated in the introduction, when
M
is an oriented matroid realised by a real hyperplane arrangement, the Salvetti complexes are deformation retracts of the complement of the complexification of the arrangement
[
10
,
26
]
; see also the alternative proof for simplicial arrangement case due to Paris
[
20
]
.

We describe two realisations of the Salvetti complex.
The finer version describes the complex as a simplicial complex.

Definition 2.3
.

The fine Salvetti complex
\widetilde{\Sal}_{M}
of
M
is the order complex of the Salvetti poset

\{w(L,T):L\in{\mathcal{L}},T\in{\mathcal{T}},L\leq T\},

where
w(L,T)\leq w(L^{\prime},T^{\prime})
if
L^{\prime}\leq L
and
T=L\circ T^{\prime}
.

In this paper, we most often use a coarser description of the space as a regular CW complex, which we denote as the (coarse) Salvetti complex.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
19
, Proposition 5.50]

For every
L\leq T
, denote by
Z(L,T)
the restriction of the fine Salvetti complex to the vertex set
\{w(L^{\prime},L^{\prime}\circ T):L^{\prime}\geq L\}
.
Then
Z(L,T)
is homeomorphic to a ball of dimension
\dim L
, whose boundary is equal to
\bigcup_{L^{\prime}>L}Z(L^{\prime},L^{\prime}\circ T)
.
Furthermore, the collection of cells
\{Z(L,T):L\leq T\}
forms a regular CW complex
\Sal_{M}
homeomorphic to the fine Salvetti complex.

We elaborate more details and notations from the above proposition.
For any pair of
L\in{\mathcal{L}},T\in{\mathcal{T}}
, define
Z(L,T)
to be the cell
Z(L,L\circ T)
; it is consistent with the already defined case
L<T
.
Therefore, we have
Z(L,T)=Z(L,T^{\prime})
for distinct
T,T^{\prime}
if and only if
L\circ T=L\circ T^{\prime}
.

The set of vertices of the Salvetti complex is
\{Z(T,T):T\in{\mathcal{T}}\}
, hence there is a bijection between the set of vertices and
\mathcal{T}
. This is because
Z(T,T^{\prime})
is independent of the choice of
T^{\prime}
since
Z(T,T^{\prime})=Z(T,T\circ T^{\prime})=Z(T,T)
.

Definition 2.5
.

The Salvetti poset, hence the fine Salvetti complex, admits a canonical involution which we call
the conjugation action
, given by
\overline{w(L,T)}=w(L,L\circ(-T))
. The conjugation action is well-defined for the Salvetti complex, in which the conjugate of
Z(L,T)
is simply
Z(L,-T)
.

The fixed locus of the conjugation action consists of cells
\{w(T,T)\ |\ T\in\mathcal{T}\}
and
\{Z(T,T)\ |\ T\in\mathcal{T}\}
in the fine Salvetti complex and Salvetti complex, respectively.

Throughout we denote by
\Delta(P)
the order complex of a poset
P
whose vertex set is
P
itself.
Let
L
be a covector and
F
be the flat complement to
\supp(L)
.
The cell
Z(L,T)
is canonically homeomorphic to the order complex
\Delta({\mathcal{L}}(M|_{F}))
(i.e., a topological representation of the oriented matroid
M|_{F}
) in the following sense:
consider the barycentric subdivision of
Z(L,T)
as a subcomplex of
\widetilde{\Sal}_{M}
, whose vertices are
w(L^{\prime},L^{\prime}\circ T)
’s for
L^{\prime}\geq L
, then the homeomorphism is defined by the map of vertices
w(L^{\prime},L^{\prime}\circ T)\mapsto L^{\prime}|_{F}
.
In particular, when the oriented matroid is realisable by a real hyperplane arrangement
\{{\bf v}_{i}^{\perp}:{\bf v}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}\}
, each top dimensional cell
Z({\bf 0},T)
has the same face poset as the
zonotope
of the arrangement, i.e., the Minkowski sum of the vectors
{\bf v}_{i}
, hence the notation
Z(L,T)
.

.

2.3.
The Orlik–Solomon Algebra and the Cordovil Algebra

Next we introduce the Orlik–Solomon algebra of a matroid.
Its significance is that when the matroid is representable over
{\mathbb{C}}
, its Orlik–Solomon algebra is naturally isomorphic to the (de Rham) cohomology ring of the complement of any complex hyperplane arrangement representing the matroid.

Definition 2.7
.

For a matroid
\underline{M}
, its Orlik–Solomon algebra
\OS^{\bullet}(\underline{M};R)
(where
R={\mathbb{Z}}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
) is the algebra defined by

\frac{\bigwedge(R^{*})^{E}}{\langle\sum_{k=1}^{t}(-1)^{k}e^{*}_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{e^{*}_{i_{k}}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{*}_{i_{t}}\rangle},

where the generators of the ideal are taken over all circuits
\{e_{i_{1}}<\ldots<e_{i_{t}}\}
of
\underline{M}
, ordered by an arbitrary but fixed ordering of
E
.

The Orlik–Solomon algebra is naturally graded and
\OS^{\bullet}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}})
is torsion free.
Denote the rank of the degree
p
piece of
\OS^{p}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
by
b_{p}(\underline{M})
, or simply
b_{p}
if the underlying matroid is clear. Then
b_{p}(\underline{M})=\dim\OS^{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
5
, Section 7]

Let
M
be an oriented matroid.
Then

H^{\bullet}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\OS^{\bullet}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}).

In particular,
b_{p}(\underline{M})=\dim H^{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})=\rank H^{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}})
.

(4)

|{\mathcal{T}}(M)|=\dim\OS^{\bullet}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})=\rank\OS^{\bullet}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}).

When a statement holds for both

We also require the duals of the graded pieces of the Orlik–Solomon algebra

For

[

For a matroid

In Section

Definition 2.10

For an oriented matroid

is the naturally graded algebra

\frac{R[x^{*}_{i}:i\in E]}{\langle(x^{*}_{i})^{2}\rangle+\langle\sum_{k=1}^{t}C(i_{k})x^{*}_{i_{1}}\ldots\widehat{x^{*}_{i_{k}}}\ldots x^{*}_{i_{t}}\rangle},

The Cordovil algebra is less studied than the Orlik–Solomon algebra in the literature, and some basic (but novel to the best of our knowledge) properties of the Cordovil algebra and their duals are proven in Section

. Nevertheless, we note a topological interpretation of

is given in
[

]

2.4.
Matroid Fans and Cosheaves

In Section
6
, we will show that the Varchenko–Gelfand filtration of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space of an oriented matroid as well its Cordovil algebra can be made into cosheaves on the fan of the underlying matroid.

We first present the fan of a matroid as introduced by Ardila and Klivans
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.11
.

The fan of a matroid
\Sigma_{\underline{M}}
is the polyhedral fan in
{\mathbb{R}}^{E}
consisting of the collection of cones

\{\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}\ |\ \mathcal{F}=\emptyset\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq F_{k}\subsetneq E\},

where
\mathcal{F}
is a flag of flats of
\underline{M}
and

\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}=\langle{\bf e}_{F_{1}}\dots,{\bf e}_{F_{k}}\dots,\pm{\bf e}_{E}\rangle_{\geq 0}
.

The projective matroid fan
\Sigma^{{\rm proj}}_{\underline{M}}
is the image of the fan above under the quotient map

{\mathbb{R}}^{E}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{E}/\langle(1,\dots,1)\rangle.
Note that there is a bijection between the cones of
\Sigma_{M}
and
\Sigma^{proj}_{\underline{M}}
.

To describe sheaves and cosheaves on polyhedral fans, we will consider them as (finite) categories. The objects of the category of
\Sigma
are its cones and the morphisms correspond to inclusions of cones. In other words, there is a map between the cones
\tau\to\sigma
if and only if there is an inclusion
\tau\subset\sigma
. The opposite category obtained by reversing the arrows is denoted by
\Sigma^{\text{op}}
.

Definition 2.12
.

A sheaf on a polyhedral fan
\Sigma
is a functor

\mathfrak{F}:\Sigma\to{\rm Mod_{R}}

and a cosheaf on a polyhedral fan
\Sigma
is a functor

\mathfrak{G}:\Sigma^{\text{op}}\to{\rm Mod}_{R}
.

In the next section, we define the tope space of an oriented matroid. We define the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-sign cosheaf
{\mathcal{S}}
of an oriented matroid as the functor which assigns to each face of the fan of the underlying matroid the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space of
M_{\mathcal{F}}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is the flag of flats corresponding to the face of the fan. The morphisms of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-sign cosheaf are inclusions.

3.
Three Filtrations of the Tope Space

This section presents three different filtrations of the tope space of an oriented matroid.

Definition 3.1
.

The
R
-tope space
of an oriented matroid
M
is free module
R[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
.

Notice that the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space is a vector subspace of the
2^{|E|}
-dimensional group algebra

({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})[({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{E}]
, and the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space is a submodule of
{\mathbb{Z}}[({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{E}]

3.1.
Dual Varchenko–Gelfand Filtration

Let
M
be an oriented matroid with topes
{\mathcal{T}}
. The dual Varchenko–Gelfand filtration filters the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space. In fact, it is most easily defined on the dual of the tope space (in which the filtration is commonly referred as Varchenko–Gelfand filtration).
The collection of functions
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}]^{*}:=\{f:{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}:f\text{ linear}\}
is equipped with a ring structure given by pointwise addition and multiplication, known as the
Varchenko–Gelfand ring
.

Definition 3.2
.

For each
e\in E
, define the Heaviside function
h_{e}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]^{*}
by extending
h_{e}(T)=\begin{cases}1&T_{e}=+\\
0&T_{e}=-\end{cases}

linearly.

The ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}]^{*}
is then generated by
h_{e}
,
e\in E
, and the unit
{\bf 1}
given by
{\bf 1}(T)=1
for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
.
Now for each non-zero
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}]^{*}
, we can define its
degree
as the minimum degree of polynomial in
h_{e}
’s that represents
f
. We define the degree of the zero function as
-1
.
The degree of any element of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]^{*}
is well-defined and always at most
d

[
27
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{P}}^{p}
the collection of functions in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]^{*}
whose degree is at most
p
.
The degree filtration of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]^{*}
is the filtration

{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]^{*}={\mathcal{P}}^{d}(M)\supset{\mathcal{P}}^{d-1}(M)\supset\ldots\supset{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}(M)=\{0\}.

The dual degree filtration of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
is

{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]={\mathcal{P}}_{0}(M)\supset{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(M)\supset\ldots\supset{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(M)\supset{\mathcal{P}}_{d+1}(M)=\{0\},

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)
is the annihilator of
{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}(M)
with respect to the pairing

{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]^{*}\times{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}

given by
(f,\gamma)\mapsto f(\gamma)
.

Denote by
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\bullet}(M)
the filtration of
({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
given by the mod 2 reduction of
{\mathcal{P}}_{\bullet}
.

Next we give an alternative description of the dual degree filtration of Varchenko–Gelfand using
prefix chains
.
The notion was first introduced in the context of hyperplane arrangements by Varchenko–Gelfand under the name of
flag cochains
, and reintroduced by Denham for general oriented matroids under the name of
bricks
.

For an element
{\bf v}\in({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{E}
, let
T_{{\bf v}}\in\{+,-\}^{E}
be given by
(T_{\bf v})_{i}=(-1)^{{\bf v}_{i}}
, i.e.,
(T_{\bf v})_{i}=+
if and only if
{\bf v}_{i}=0
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}:\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{k}=E
be a flag of flats.
Define

{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}(M)\ |\ T\setminus F_{i}\in\mathcal{L}\text{ for all }F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}\}.

From the point of view of a pseudohyperplane arrangement,
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
consists of the topes (viewed as connected components of the complement of the arrangement) for which the intersection with the (pseudo)subspace corresponding to each flat
F_{i}
is a cell of (the closure of) the tope of codimension
\rank(F_{i})

The next proposition gives a more explicit description of

Notation
: For the rest of this paper, when a complete flag of flats

is given, we denote by
{\bf d}_{{\mathcal{F}},p}
(respectively,
{\bf f}_{{\mathcal{F}},p}

{\bf e}_{F_{p}\setminus F_{p-1}}
(respectively,
{\bf e}_{F_{p}}

Proposition 3.5
.

[
23
, Lemma 3.2]

Let
{\mathcal{F}}:\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{k}=E

If the flag is complete, then

Namely,

is in correspondence with

for any

Suppose

If

Definition 3.6

Definition 3.7

Fix a complete flag of flats

Proposition 3.9
.

[

For an oriented matroid

-th prefix chains.
In other words,

{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)=\langle\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}\ |\ {\mathcal{F}}\text{ complete flag of flats}\rangle.

As a simple but helpful illustration, we have

{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(M)=\{\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}c_{T}\cdot T:\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}c_{T}=0\}.

We have the
\subset

\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
satisfies the linear condition on the right hand side. For the reversed containment, recall that two topes

Since any two topes

Remark 3.10
.

As mentioned in the introduction, another filtration of interest in this picture is the
asymptotic filtration
, which we now briefly describe.

The filtration is defined using the
Schechtman–Varchenko bilinear form
, which is the bilinear form
B_{M}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined over
{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{e}:e\in E][{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
by extending
B_{M}(T,T^{\prime})=\prod_{e:T(e)\neq T^{\prime}(e)}(1+x_{e})
bilinearly (we use a change of variables differs from the usual one for brevity).
The
p
-th piece of the asymptotic filtration of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
consists of all chains
\gamma
with the property that, for every
T^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}}(M)
, the polynomial
B(\gamma,T^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{e}:e\in E]
has no terms of degree less than

.

3.2.
Kalinin Filtration

The
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space can also be filtered via the Kalinin filtration.
For a topological space
X
equipped with an involution
c:X\to X
, Kalinin defined a spectral sequence which starts from the homology of
X
and converges to the homology of the fixed locus
\text{Fix}(c)\subset X

[
15
]
, see also
[
8
]
.

The first page of Kalinin spectral sequence has terms
E^{1}_{\bullet}=H_{\bullet}(X)
and differentials
d^{1}_{\bullet}=1+c_{*}
.
The further pages have terms

E^{r}_{p}=\ker d^{r-1}_{p}/\Image d^{r-1}_{p-r+1}.

A cycle
x_{p}
is in
\ker d^{r}_{p}\subset H_{p}(X)
if and only if there exist chains

y_{p}=x_{p},y_{p+1},\dots,y_{p+r-1}

so that
\partial y_{i+1}=(1+c_{*})y_{i}.
When such chains exist, the differential on the
r
-th page is the map
d^{r}_{p}
defined on the representative
x_{p}
by
d_{p}^{r}x_{p}=(1+c_{*})y_{p+r-1}
.

In our context, the space
X
under consideration is the Salvetti complex appearing in Proposition
2.4
and the involution is conjugation action from Definition
2.5
. Thus the fixed locus consists of the collection of vertices of the Salvetti complex in correspondence with the topes. Hence the fixed locus only has homology in degree
0
and
H_{\bullet}(\text{Fix}(c);{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})=H_{0}(\text{Fix}(c);{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}[\mathcal{T}(M)]
. We use this to simplify the presentation of the Kalinin spectral sequence below and obtain the Kalinin filtration.

Proposition 3.11
.

For the Salvetti complex of an oriented matroid, the Kalinin spectral sequence degenerates at the first page. Moreover, associated gradeds of the spectral sequence induce a filtration of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space:

{\mathcal{K}}_{d}(M)\subset\dots\subset{\mathcal{K}}_{1}(M)\subset{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(M)={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]

such that
{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{K}}_{p+1}(M)
is isomorphic to
H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
for all
p
.

Proof.

The dimension of
H_{\bullet}(\text{Fix}(c);{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
is equal to the number of topes of
M
, which by Zavlasky’s theorem (Equation (
4
)) is equal to the total dimension
H_{\bullet}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Hence
\Sal_{M}
is maximal in the sense of the Smith–Thom inequality, and

|{\mathcal{T}}(M)|=\dim H_{\bullet}(\text{Fix}(c);{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})=\dim H_{\bullet}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}).

Therefore, the maps
d_{\bullet}^{p}
must all be zero and the Kalinin spectral sequence degenerates at the first
page.
Since
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
is canonically isomorphic to
H_{\bullet}(\text{Fix}(c);{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
we obtain a filtration of the tope space.
∎

The isomorphism between the intermediate gradeds of the filtration and the homology groups of the Salvetti complex from the above proposition are given by the Viro homomorphisms
[
8
]
as below.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
M
be an oriented matroid of rank
d
and let
\mathcal{T}
denote its collection of topes.
The (Borel–)Viro homomorphisms

bv_{p}:{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)\rightarrow H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})

are defined recursively by

•

bv_{0}
is the induced inclusion map
i_{\ast}:H_{0}(\text{Fix}(c);{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{0}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
,

•

bv_{p}:{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)\rightarrow H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})

is given by
bv_{p}(\gamma)=[\beta_{p}+\overline{\beta_{p}}]
,
where
\beta_{p}
is any
p
-chain whose boundary is
bv_{p-1}(\gamma)
in
\Sal_{M}
.

We have
\gamma\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)
if for
i=1,\ldots,p
there exists
i
-chains
\beta_{i}\in C_{i}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
\partial\beta_{1}=i_{\ast}\gamma
and
\partial\beta_{i}=\beta_{i-1}+\overline{\beta_{i-1}}
for
i=2,\ldots,p
.

The Viro homomorphism descends to a map

bv_{p}:{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{K}}_{p+1}(M)\rightarrow H_{p}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}).

This arises as the isomorphism of the
E^{\infty}
page of the Kalinin spectral sequence with the associated gradeds. This isomorphism has the above form since the Kalinin spectral sequence degenerates at the first page by Proposition
3.11
, and the homology of
\text{Fix}(c)
is only non-zero in dimension
0
.
To see directly that the definition of
bv_{p}
does not depend on the choices of
\beta_{i}
’s:
suppose by induction that
\beta_{i-1}+\overline{\beta_{i-1}}
is well-defined, then any two choices of
\beta_{i}
differ by some homology class, while by
[
29
, Corollary A.2]
, the conjugation acts as an identity on
H_{i}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
, so the difference of the two candidates
\beta_{i}
cancels out upon adding to their conjugates.

bv_{1}(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{4}+T_{5})=[Z(\zeta_{1},T_{1})+Z(\zeta_{1},T_{2})+Z(\zeta_{4},T_{4})+Z(\zeta_{4},T_{5})]
. In order to find

, and
bv_{2}(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{4}+T_{5})=[Z(O,T_{1})+Z(O,T_{2})+Z(O,T_{4})+Z(O,T_{5})]
.

We can also consider Kalinin spectral sequence in the situation of a real hyperplane arrangement and its complexification.
Let
\mathbb{C}{\mathcal{C}}
denote the complement of the complexification of the arrangement from Example

.
Consider points

Then

forms a

3.3.
Quillen Filtration

Recall that the set
\{+,-\}^{E}
carries a vector space structure via the bijection
({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{E}\to\{+,-\}^{E}
, given by
{\bf v}\mapsto T_{\bf v}
where
(T_{\bf v})_{e}:=(-1)^{{\bf v}_{e}}
. Here we abuse notation and identify
\pm 1
with
\pm
.
Notice that the additive structure on
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
translates to the multiplicative structure on
\{+,-\}
.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
V
be a
d
-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.
Consider the group algebra
{\mathcal{I}}_{0}(V):=({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})[V]=({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})[T_{\bf v}:{\bf v}\in V]
, where multiplication is given by
T_{{\bf v}_{1}}T_{{\bf v}_{2}}=T_{{\bf v}_{1}+{\bf v}_{2}}
.
For a subset
G\subset V
, denote by
\gamma_{G}
the sum
\sum_{{\bf v}\in G}T_{\bf v}
.

Define the Quillen filtration of
{\mathcal{I}}_{0}(V)
as follows:

{\mathcal{I}}_{1}(V)=\{\sum_{\bf v}a_{\bf v}T_{\bf v}:\sum_{\bf v}a_{\bf v}=0\}

is the augmentation ideal of
V
, whereas further pieces are defined as

{\mathcal{I}}_{p}(V):={\mathcal{I}}_{1}^{p}(V)=\langle t_{1}t_{2}\ldots t_{p}:t_{i}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1}(V)\rangle.

Proposition 3.15
.

[
25
, Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.3]

The vector space
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}(V)
is additively generated by
\gamma_{G}
’s, ranging over all
p
-dimensional subspaces
G
of
V
.
Furthermore,
\dim{\mathcal{I}}_{p}(V)-\dim{\mathcal{I}}_{p+1}(V)=\dim\bigwedge^{p}V=\binom{d}{p}
.

By Proposition
3.5
, given a complete flag
{\mathcal{F}}
, after selecting an origin, we can identify
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
with a vector subspace and consider the Quillen filtration
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}})
’s.
By
[
25
, Lemma 4.4]
, the filtration obtained does not depend on the choice of origin.

Definition 3.16
.

Let
M
be an oriented matroid with topes
{\mathcal{T}}
.
Denote
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}(M):=({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})[{\mathcal{T}}]
.

Using the natural inclusion of
({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})[{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}]
into
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}(M)
, we can define the Quillen filtration on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}(M)
by

{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M):=\sum_{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{I}}_{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}),

where
{\mathcal{F}}
ranges over all complete flags of flats of
M
.

By Proposition
3.15
, the Quillen filtration of the tope space is equivalently

{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)=\langle\gamma_{U}\ |\ U\in\text{Aff}_{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{F}}})\rangle,

where
{\mathcal{F}}
ranges over all complete flags of flats of
M
,
\text{Aff}_{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{F}}})
denotes the set of affine subspaces of the affine space
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}

of dimension
p
, and
\gamma_{U}=\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in U}T_{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
.
Note that
\gamma_{U}
is the mod
2
reduction of
\gamma_{U,\mathcal{B},{\bf v}}
for any choice of
\mathcal{B}
and
{\bf v}
.

In addition, each piece of the Quillen filtration comes with a map to the dual of the Orlik–Solomon algebra as described in Proposition
2.9
. These maps are denoted by
{q}_{p}\colon{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)\to\OS_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
and are defined on the generators

\{\gamma_{U}\ |\ U\in\text{Aff}_{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{F}}})\}

via

(5)

{q}_{p}(\gamma_{U})={\bf v}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\bf v}_{p},

where
{\bf v}_{1},\dots,{\bf v}_{p}\in({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{E}
form a basis of the tangent space of the affine space
U
.

Theorem 3.17
.

The maps
{{q}}_{p}\colon{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)\to\OS_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})

are well-defined and descend into isomorphisms between
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{Q}}_{p+1}(M)
and
\OS_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
.
In particular, for each
p
,

\dim{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)-\dim{\mathcal{Q}}_{p+1}(M)=b_{p}(M).

Proof.

The proof follows closely the arguments given in
[
25
, Proposition 4.3 and Lemma 4.8]
, which were applied for the uniform matroid
U_{n,n+1}
and were shown to work for all oriented matroids in
[
24
, Proposition 5.7]
.
Since the statement in the last reference is a stronger result in terms of cellular cosheaves, here we overview the arguments and leave the reader to
[
25
]
and
[
24
]
for the detailed calculation.

It can be shown that the map
{\bf v}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\bf v}_{p}\mapsto[\gamma_{U}]\in{\mathcal{I}}_{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}})/{\mathcal{I}}_{p+1}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}})
, where
U
is the span of
{\bf v}_{i}
’s, induces an isomorphism between between
\bigwedge^{p}{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}})/{\mathcal{I}}_{p+1}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}})
(indeed, the isomorphism is a linear algebra fact that remains valid when
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
is replaced by any vector space over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
).
Summing the isomorphisms over all complete flags
{\mathcal{F}}
and applying Proposition
2.9
gives a map between
\OS_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{Q}}_{p+1}(M)
; the map is surjective and thus an isomorphism by a dimension count.
The map
{q}_{p}
can be described as the composition of the projection map
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{Q}}_{p+1}(M)
and the
inverse
of this isomorphism, hence it is well-defined, and the isomorphism claim is tautological.
∎

Example 3.18
.

While the Quillen filtration can be defined for the group algebra with coefficients from any commutative ring, if taken over
{\mathbb{Z}}
it does not provide a filtration with ranks corresponding to those over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Consider the uniform matroid
M=U_{2,2}
, which has four topes
T_{00},T_{01},T_{10},T_{11}
.
The augmentation ideal in the Quillen filtration over
{\mathbb{Z}}
for each complete flag
{\mathcal{F}}
is spanned by
T_{00}-T_{01},T_{00}-T_{10},T_{00}-T_{11}
, hence the corresponding
{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}})
contains

\displaystyle(T_{00}-T_{01})(T_{00}-T_{10})=T_{00}-T_{01}-T_{10}+T_{11},

\displaystyle(T_{00}-T_{01})(T_{00}-T_{11})=T_{00}-T_{01}+T_{10}-T_{11},

\displaystyle(T_{00}-T_{10})(T_{00}-T_{11})=T_{00}+T_{01}-T_{10}-T_{11},

which span a subgroup of rank 3 instead of 1.
In fact, one can see that the filtration never stabilises.

4.
Comparison of the Filtrations

Our goal in this section is to show that all three filtrations of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space of an oriented matroid are the same.
Our strategy is to first show that the Quillen filtration is contained in the reduction of the dual degree filtration modulo
2
.
To do this we use the prefix chain description of the dual degree filtration from Proposition
3.9
.
Then we show that the dual degree filtration is contained in the Kalinin filtration.

Therefore, we find the following sequence of inclusions of the filtrations of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope spaces

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bullet}(M)\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\bullet}(M)\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}(M).

We then compare the dimensions of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)
and conclude that all three filtrations are equal.

4.1.
The Dual VG Filtration Contains the Quillen Filtration

We show that prefix chains are sufficient to span the generators of the Quillen filtration.
As an intermediate step, we consider affine spaces
U
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
that are parallel to subspaces spanned by a subset of the basis
{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\bf d}_{{\mathcal{F}},1},\ldots,{\bf d}_{{\mathcal{F}},d}\}
, which we dub as the
affine coordinate subspaces
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
; they correspond to
affine coordinate chains

\gamma_{U,{\mathcal{B}},{\bf v}}
.

Whenever an affine coordinate subspace or chain is being considered, the flag of flats it is associated with would be clear and we always work with the basis consisting of
{\bf d}
’s, so for brevity, we do not define the bases in the proofs.

Since some of the results here are also applied in Section
6
, we work over
{\mathbb{Z}}
when dealing with the dual degree filtration.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}:\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{d}=E
be a complete flag of flats of
M
.
Then for any
p
-dimensional affine coordinate subspace
U
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\bf v}\in U
,
\gamma_{U,{\mathcal{B}},{\bf v}}
is contained in
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)
.

Proof.

By the definition of
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)
using Heaviside functions, it suffices to show that for any
e_{1},\ldots,e_{p-1}\in E
,
(h_{e_{1}}\ldots h_{e_{p-1}})(\gamma_{U,{\mathcal{B}},{\bf v}})=0
.
Suppose
U
is parallel to
\langle{\bf d}_{i_{1}},\ldots,{\bf d}_{i_{p}}\rangle
.
Then there exists
1\leq j\leq p
such that
{\bf d}_{i_{j}}
does not contain any of
e_{i}
’s, pairing up the topes in
U
as
\{{\bf u},{\bf u}+{\bf d}_{i_{j}}\}
’s, evaluating the two topes in each pair by
h_{e_{1}}\ldots h_{e_{p-1}}
produces equal value, but the two topes are of opposite signs in
\gamma_{U,{\mathcal{B}},{\bf v}}
.
∎

We now work with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-coefficients and we can identity an affine subspace
U
with a chain
\gamma_{U}
without specifying the original
{\bf v}
. Our next step is to get all affine subspaces from affine coordinate subspaces.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
V
be a
d
-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
with standard basis
{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{d}
.
Then the
p
-th piece
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}(V)
of the Quillen filtration of
V
is contained in the subspace generated by
\gamma_{U}
, ranging over all affine coordinate subspaces
U
of dimension
p
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.15
, it suffices to show that all elements of the form
\gamma_{V^{\prime}}
, where
V^{\prime}
is a
p
-dimensional subspace of
V
, can be written as the linear combination of
\gamma_{U}
’s with
U
’s being affine coordinate subspaces.
For a subset
A\subset V
, define the weight of
A
to be the sum of weights of the elements in
A
with respect to the standard basis.
For a subspace
V^{\prime}
of
V
, define the weight of
V^{\prime}
to be the minimum weight of
\mathcal{B}
among all bases
\mathcal{B}
of
V^{\prime}
.
We prove the statement by an induction on the weight of the subspace
V^{\prime}
.
If the weight of
V^{\prime}
is
p
, then necessarily
V^{\prime}
has a basis consisting of standard basis elements, hence
V^{\prime}
is a coordinate subspace.

Now suppose the weight of
V^{\prime}
is larger than
p
, pick a basis
{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p}
of
V^{\prime}
of minimum weight.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
{\bf v}_{p}
is not a standard basis element, and write
{\bf v}_{p}={\bf v}^{\prime}+{\bf e}_{k}
where
{\bf e}_{k}
is not involved when
{\bf v}^{\prime}
is written as sum of
{\bf e}_{i}
’s.
Note that
{\bf v}^{\prime},{\bf e}_{k}\not\in\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1}\rangle
, for otherwise
{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1},{\bf e}_{k}
(respectively
{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1},{\bf v}^{\prime}
) is a basis of smaller weight.
Let
W^{\prime}=\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1},{\bf v}^{\prime}\rangle
, and
W^{\prime\prime}=\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1},{\bf e}_{k}\rangle
.
By induction hypothesis,
\gamma_{W^{\prime}}
and
\gamma_{W^{\prime\prime}}
can each be written as a linear combination of the elements corresponding to affine coordinate subspaces.
By applying a translation by
{\bf e}_{k}
to the summands in the first linear combination,
\gamma_{{\bf e}_{k}+W^{\prime}}
can also be written as a linear combination of the elements corresponding affine coordinate subspaces.
Since
{\bf e}_{k}+W^{\prime}=({\bf e}_{k}+\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1}\rangle)\sqcup({\bf v}_{p}+\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1}\rangle)
and
G^{\prime\prime}=\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1}\rangle\sqcup({\bf e}_{k}+\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1}\rangle)
, while
V^{\prime}=\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1}\rangle\sqcup({\bf v}_{p}+\langle{\bf v}_{1},\ldots,{\bf v}_{p-1}\rangle)
, we have that
\gamma_{V^{\prime}}=\gamma_{{\bf e}_{k}+W^{\prime}}+\gamma_{W^{\prime\prime}}
is a linear combination of affine coordinate subspaces.
∎

We have established the following corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
M
be an oriented matroid.
Then for every
p
,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)\subset\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{p}(M)
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
3.15
that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(M)
is generated by elements of the form
\gamma_{U}
for some
p
-dimensional affine subspaces
U
.
From Proposition
4.1
, we obtain that
\gamma_{U}
is contained in
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{p}(M)
, where
U
is any affine coordinate subspace of
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}
for a complete flag of flats
{\mathcal{F}}
. Therefore, the statement follows.
∎

4.2.
The Kalinin Filtration Contains the Dual VG Filtration

We prove that every
p
-th prefix chain is contained in the
p
-th part of the Kalinin filtration by exhibiting a sequence of
\beta_{i}
’s as in Definition
3.12
.
We work with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
throughout this section unless otherwise specified.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}:\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{d}=E
be a complete flag of flats and let
T_{{\bf v}}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Then
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)
.

Proof.

For brevity of the notation, we assume (by reorientation)
{\bf v}={\bf 0}
whenever only one specific prefix chain is being considered.

For the case
p=1
, we have
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},1}=T_{{\bf 0}}+T_{{\bf d}_{1}}
, while
Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{1},T_{\bf 0})
is a
1
-dimensional cell whose boundary is precisely the two vertices
Z(T_{\bf 0},T_{\bf 0})
and

Z(T_{{\bf d}_{1}},T_{\bf 0})
.
So we can take
\beta_{1}
to be
Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{1},T_{\bf 0})
.

Now suppose the statement is true for
p
. More precisely, for every
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
we suppose for
i\leq p
that
there exists
\beta_{i}\in C_{i}(\Sal_{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
such that

\partial\beta_{1}=i_{\ast}\gamma
and
\partial\beta_{i}=\beta_{i-1}+\overline{\beta_{i-1}}
for
2\leq i\leq p
.
We further assume that we can choose

(6)

\beta_{p}=\sum_{{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p-1}}Z(T_{{\bf v}}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u}).

Notice that this holds in the case
p=1
above.

We now pass to the case of
p+1
, and prove that
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p+1}

is in
{\mathcal{K}}_{p+1}(M)
by finding
i
-chains
\beta_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,p+1
. Moreover, we have

\beta_{p+1}=\sum_{{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}}Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p+1},T_{\bf u}).

We start with noting

\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p+1}=\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}+\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf d}_{p+1},p}.

By induction, for
i=1,\ldots,p
,
there are
i
-chains
\beta_{i}^{\prime}
and
\beta_{i}^{\prime\prime}
certifying that
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}
and
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf d}_{p+1},p}
are in
{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
.
For
i\leq p
, we can take
\beta_{i}=\beta_{i}^{\prime}+\beta_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. Using the induction assumption, it is easily verified that
\partial\beta_{i}=\beta_{i-1}+\overline{\beta_{i-1}}
for
i=1,\ldots,p
.
It remains to show that
\partial\beta_{p+1}=\beta_{p}+\overline{\beta_{p}}
, where
\beta_{p}=\beta_{p}^{\prime}+\beta_{p}^{\prime\prime}
.

We spend some time simplifying the conjugation of
\beta^{\prime}_{p}
.
Observe that

\overline{Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u})}=Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p},-T_{\bf u})=Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p},T_{{\bf f}_{d}+\bf u})=Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p},T_{{\bf f}_{p}+{\bf u}}),

since
(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p})\circ T_{{\bf f}_{d}+\bf u}=(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p})\circ T_{{\bf f}_{p}+{\bf u}}
.
Hence we have

\overline{\beta^{\prime}_{p}}=\sum_{{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf f}_{p},p-1}}Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u}).

By the same argument, we also have

\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf d}_{p+1},p}\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(M)
.
More precisely,
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf d}_{p+1},p}
can be obtained from
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}
by replacing every
T_{\bf v}
by
T_{{\bf v}+{\bf d}_{p+1}}
in the sum.
By induction, when constructing
\beta^{\prime\prime}_{p^{\prime}},p^{\prime}<p
for
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf d}_{p+1},p}
, one can simply apply the analogous replacement of
Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p^{\prime}},T_{\bf v})
by
Z(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p^{\prime}},T_{{\bf v}+{\bf d}_{p+1}})
.

In particular, we can choose the corresponding
p
-chain to be

\beta_{p}^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf d}_{p+1},p-1}}Z(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u}).

Nevertheless, we note that
Z(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p},T_{{\bf u}+{\bf d}_{p+1}})=Z(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u})
for
{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p-1}
, as

(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p})\circ T_{{\bf u}+{\bf d}_{p+1}}=(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p})\circ T_{\bf u},

so the sum can actually be rewritten as

(7)

\beta_{p}^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p-1}}Z(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u}).

Analogous to the above,
we have

\overline{\beta^{\prime\prime}_{p}}=\sum_{{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf f}_{p},p-1}}Z(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u}).

Hence, it remains to show that the boundary of

\beta_{p+1}
is equal to

(8)

\sum_{{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}}\big[Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u})+Z(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u})\big],

here we use the simple fact that
U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}=U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p-1}\sqcup U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf f}_{p},p-1}
.

Every
p
-cell on the boundary of
\beta_{p+1}
is of the form
Z(T\setminus F,T_{\bf u})
for some flat
F\subset F_{p+1}
of rank
p
, some tope
T
that agrees with
T_{\bf 0}
outside of
F_{p+1}
, and some
{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}
.

Case I:
F\neq F_{p}
.

There exists some
1\leq k\leq p
such that
F\cap(F_{k}\setminus F_{k-1})=\emptyset
, for otherwise we would have
F_{p}=\langle F\cap(F_{i}\setminus F_{i-1}):i=1,\ldots,p\rangle\subsetneq F
, which is impossible by considering rank.
For any
{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v}_{0},p}
, we have
Z(T\setminus F,T_{\bf u})=Z(T\setminus F,T_{{\bf u}+{\bf d}_{k}})
because
(T\setminus F)\circ T_{\bf u}=(T\setminus F)\circ T_{{\bf u}+{\bf d}_{k}}
.
By pairing up the elements of
U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}
into
\{{\bf u},{\bf u}+{\bf d}_{k}\}
’s, we can see that the boundary does not contain cells of the form
Z(T\setminus F,T_{\bf u})
for
F\neq F_{p}
.

Case II:
F=F_{p}
.

In such a case, a tope
T
restricted to
F_{p+1}\setminus F_{p}
must be equal to that of
T_{\bf 0}
or
T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}
, for otherwise,
T|_{F_{p+1}}/F_{p}
would be a third tope of the rank 1 matroid
M|_{F_{p+1}}/F_{p}
.
For every
{\bf u}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}
, the cell
Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p+1},T_{\bf u})
is the unique cell in
\beta_{p+1}
whose boundary contains
Z(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u})
; it is also the unique cell in
\beta_{p+1}
which contains
Z(T_{{\bf d}_{p+1}}\setminus F_{p},T_{\bf u})
in its boundary: for any other
{\bf u}^{\prime}\in U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}
, we have
(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p})\circ T_{\bf u}\neq(T_{\bf 0}\setminus F_{p})\circ T_{{\bf u}^{\prime}}

p+1\in F_{p}\setminus F_{p-1}

\partial C
that do not vanish over
\epsilon_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
are
C(p)x_{1}^{*}\ldots\widehat{x_{p}^{*}}x_{p+1}^{*}
and
C(p+1)x_{1}^{*}\ldots x_{p}^{*}
, with the dual terms in
\epsilon_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
having coefficients
T_{\bf v}(1)\ldots\widehat{T_{\bf v}(p)}T_{\bf v}(p+1)
and
T_{\bf v}(1)\ldots T_{\bf v}(p)
, respectively.
Summarizing, the evaluation is zero if
C(p)C(p+1)=-T(p)T(p+1)
, which follows from the orthogonality of signed circuits and signed cocircuits: consider the restriction of
C
and
T
as a signed circuit (supported on
\{p,p+1\}
) and a cocircuit (supported on
F_{p}\setminus F_{p-1}
) of the rank 1 oriented matroid
M|_{F_{p}}/F_{p-1}
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

The map
\tilde{a}_{p}:{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)
given by extending
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}\mapsto\epsilon_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
linearly is well-defined, surjective, and has kernel equal to
{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)
. In particular,
\tilde{a}_{p}
descends to an isomorphism
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)
.

Proof.

We show that the map
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)
given by extending
[\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}]\mapsto\epsilon_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
linearly is well-defined and is an isomorphism. The statement in the proposition follows from composing the quotient map
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)
with this isomorphism.

We claim that the map
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)
is the composition of three isomorphisms:

(1)

{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)\cong\Hom({\mathcal{P}}^{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}(M),{\mathbb{Z}})
, induced from the perfect pairing
{\mathcal{P}}^{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}(M)\times{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
;

(2)

\Hom({\mathcal{P}}^{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}(M),{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\Hom({\mathcal{A}}^{p}(M),{\mathbb{Z}})
, induced by the pullback of the isomorphism
{\mathcal{A}}^{p}(M)\cong{\mathcal{P}}^{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}(M)
given by
(x^{*})^{S}\mapsto[\prod_{i\in S}h_{i}]

[
17
, Theorem 5.9]
;

(3)

{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)\cong\Hom({\mathcal{A}}^{p}(M),{\mathbb{Z}})
, induced by the perfect pairing
{\mathcal{A}}^{p}(M)\times{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Since
{\mathcal{P}}^{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}(M)
is generated by products of Heaviside functions
h_{i_{1}}\ldots h_{i_{p}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}(M)
is generated by prefix chains
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
, it suffices to verify the evaluation of
h_{i_{1}}\ldots h_{i_{p}}
on
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
is equal to that of
(x^{*})^{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}}
on
\epsilon_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
. We first assume
i_{j}\in F_{j}\setminus F_{j-1}
for every
j
(up to reindexing). On the Varchenko–Gelfand side, if
T^{\prime}
is the unique tope in
U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
such that
T^{\prime}(i_{j})=+
for every
j
, then the evaluation is equal to the sign of
T^{\prime}
in
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},T,p}
. This is
-1
to the power of the number of
j
’s such that
T^{\prime}(i_{j})
differ from
T(i_{j})
, equivalently
T(i_{1})\ldots T(i_{p})
.
The evaluation on the Cordovil side is also equal to
T(i_{1})\ldots T(i_{p})
.

Suppose
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}\cap(F_{j}\setminus F_{j-1})=\emptyset
for some
j\leq p
instead. On the Varchenko–Gelfand side, we pair up the elements of
U_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
into
\{{\bf u}_{i},{\bf u}_{i}+{\bf d}_{j}\}
’s, whose corresponding terms have opposite sign upon being evaluated by
h_{i_{1}}\ldots h_{i_{p}}
, thus the overall evaluation is zero. The evaluation is also zero on the Cordovil side by the simple observation about the monomials in the support of
\epsilon_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}
.
∎

The next lemma helps us to relate flags of flats of an initial matroid with flags of flats of the original matroid. It is used to define maps between the Cordovil algebras of initial oriented matroids to that of the original oriented matroid.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}:\emptyset=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{l}=E
be a flag of flats (not necessarily complete nor with
\rank F_{i}=i
). Let
G_{0}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq G_{d}
be a complete flag of the initial matroid
\underline{M}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Then there exists a complete flag
G^{\prime}_{0}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq G^{\prime}_{d}
of
\underline{M}
such that
\{G_{i}\setminus G_{i-1}:1\leq i\leq d\}=\{G^{\prime}_{i}\setminus G^{\prime}_{i-1}:1\leq i\leq d\}
.

Proof.

Every flat
G
of
\underline{M}_{\mathcal{F}}
can be written uniquely as
G^{(1)}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup G^{(l)}
, where
G^{(i)}\sqcup F_{i-1}\subset F_{i}
is a flat of
\underline{M}
for each
i
. So for any two consecutive flats
G_{j}\subsetneq G_{j+1}
, there must exist a unique index
1\leq k\leq l
such that
G_{j}^{(t)}=G_{j+1}^{(t)}
for any
t\neq k
and
G_{j}^{(k)}\subsetneq G_{j+1}^{(k)}
whose ranks differ by 1. Now the chain
G_{0}^{(1)}\leq G_{1}^{(1)}\leq\ldots\leq G_{d}^{(1)}\leq G_{0}^{(2)}\cup F_{1}\leq G_{1}^{(2)}\cup F_{1}\leq\ldots\leq G_{d}^{(2)}\cup F_{1}\leq\ldots\leq G_{d}^{(l)}\cup F_{l-1}
has exactly
d+1
distinct elements and form a complete flag of
\underline{M}
, which satisfies the requirement.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

For every flag
{\mathcal{F}}
there are canonical inclusion maps

\iota:{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}]\to{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
and
\iota:{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M_{\mathcal{F}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)
for all
p
.

Proof.

By Definition
3.4
, we have
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}(M)
which gives an immediate inclusion map for the tope spaces.

Every function in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}]^{*}
can be restricted as a function in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}]^{*}
, and the restriction of the Heaviside function
h_{e}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}]^{*}
is
h_{e}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}]^{*}
. Hence, when the function is written as a polynomial in Heaviside functions, the same polynomial (in Heaviside functions over
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}]
) represents its restriction in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}]^{*}
.
Conversely, every function in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}]^{*}
can be written in a polynomial in Heaviside functions (over
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}]
), in which the presentation defines a function in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}]^{*}
, whose restriction is the function we started with.

By choosing the presentation of the lowest possible degree in the above conversion, we have that
{\mathcal{P}}^{p}(M_{\mathcal{F}})
consists of precisely the restriction of the functions in
{\mathcal{P}}^{p}(M)
. Now
\gamma
is in
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M_{\mathcal{F}})
if and only if it is evaluated to 0 by every function in
{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}(M_{\mathcal{F}})
, so
\gamma
as an element in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}(M)]
also gets evaluated to 0 by every function in
{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}(M)
thus is in
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)
.
∎

Proposition 6.9
.

There is a canonical inclusion map
\iota^{{\mathcal{A}}}:{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M_{\mathcal{F}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)
for every flag
{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

Both
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M_{{\mathcal{F}}})
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)
live in

as submodules.
The Cordovil algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M_{\mathcal{F}})
is generated by
\epsilon_{{\mathcal{G}},{\bf v},p}

is a complete flag of
\underline{M}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Such an element is the image of the prefix chain
\gamma_{{\mathcal{G}},{\bf v},p}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M_{\mathcal{F}})
under
\tilde{a}_{p}
. By Lemma
6.7
, there exists a complete flag
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
of
\underline{M}
such that
\gamma_{{\mathcal{G}},{\bf v},p}
is an affine coordinate chain in
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)

∎

Remark 6.16
.

We mention some connection between our construction and operad theory as communicated to us by Basile Coron. In the case of the braid arrangement
\{\{x_{i}=x_{j}\}:1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}

{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space can be viewed as the group algebra

that relates symmetric groups of different sizes is encoded by the cosheaf structure of

Poisson operad
. Such a connection can be extended to more general (oriented) matroids. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
and the references therein for details on poset operad theory.

In the original short exact sequence over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
in
[
25
]
, the Cordovil cosheaf is replaced with the mod 2 reduction of the

-tropical homology cosheaf

(also known as the

-th multi-tangent space)

]
, which assigns

T_{3}-T_{5}=(T_{3}-T_{4})+(T_{4}-T_{5})
to
(\alpha^{\prime\prime}+\beta^{\prime}-\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}){\bf e}_{1}+(\alpha^{\prime\prime}+\beta^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime}-\beta){\bf e}_{2}+(\alpha^{\prime\prime}-\beta){\bf e}_{3}
.

Now comparing the coefficients of
{\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{2}
in the image of
T_{1}-T_{3}
yields
\beta-\beta^{\prime}=\alpha-\alpha^{\prime\prime}
, whereas comparing the coefficients of
{\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{3}
in the image of
T_{3}-T_{5}
yields
\beta+\beta^{\prime}=\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}
, these imply
\alpha=\beta,\alpha^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}
, a contradiction as they should have different parity.

6.3.
Projectivisation of the Varchenko–Gelfand Filtration

In
[
24
,
25
]
, the authors consider not just the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space of an oriented matroid but also the
projective
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space
. The intermediate gradeds of the filtration of the projective
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tope space are shown to be isomorphic to the stalks of the cosheaves from
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-tropical homology
\mathcal{F}_{p}(M)
.
By
[
30
]
, this vector space is also isomorphic to the dual of a graded piece of the
projective Orlik–Solomon algebra

[
18
,
16
]
, which we denote by
\overline{\OS}_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Here we consider a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-variant of this projectivisation of the tope space and also the dual Cordovil algebra. Consider the collection
{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm proj}
of projective topes, obtained by identifying antipodal topes in
{\mathcal{T}}
. We have the canonical projection
\pi:{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm proj}]
; denote by
\Theta
the kernel of this map.
We show that the dual Varchenko–Gelfand filtration induces a filtration on
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm proj}]
. In what follows we shorten

{\mathcal{P}}_{p}(M)
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}(M)
to
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
, respectively, since we only consider one oriented matroid
M
.

Consider the following diagram

\begin{CD}000\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}@>{}>{}>{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}@>{}>{}>{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}^{\rm proj}@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p}@>{}>{}>{\mathcal{P}}_{p}@>{}>{}>{\mathcal{P}}_{p}^{\rm proj}@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{}V{{\tilde{a}_{p}|_{\Theta}}}V@V{}V{\tilde{a}_{p}}V@V{}V{\tilde{a}_{p}^{\rm proj}}V\\
0@>{}>{}>B_{p}@>{}>{}>{\mathcal{A}}_{p}@>{}>{}>{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
000.\\
\end{CD}

Here
{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}^{\rm proj}=\pi({\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}),{\mathcal{P}}_{p}^{\rm proj}=\pi({\mathcal{P}}_{p}),B_{p}:=\tilde{a}_{p}(\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p})
, and
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}:={\mathcal{A}}_{p}/B_{p}
(
a priori
not
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}^{\rm proj}/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}^{\rm proj}
).

Proposition 6.18
.

The diagram is well-defined (the map
\tilde{a}_{p}^{\rm proj}
exists), and it is commutative and exact.

Proof.

The top-left square commutes since every map is an inclusion map. The commutativity of the top-right square is because the two horizontal maps are both restrictions of
\pi
whereas the two vertical maps are inclusions; analogous reasoning for the lower-left square.
The existence of
\tilde{a}_{p}^{\rm proj}
and the commutativity of the lower-right square follow from the snake lemma applied to the first two columns once we have proved the exactness of the first two columns.

The exactness of the first two rows follows the first isomorphism theorem, and that of the third row from definition. The middle column is Lemma
6.14
.
Next we prove the exactness of the first column, i.e., the kernel of
\tilde{a}_{p}
restricted to
\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
is
\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}
. The L.H.S. is contained in
\Theta
by definition, as well as in the kernel of
\tilde{a}_{p}
restricted to
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
, which is
{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}
; conversely,
\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}\subset\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
, while
\tilde{a}_{p}(\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1})\subset\tilde{a}_{p}({\mathcal{P}}_{p+1})=\{0\}
. The exactness of the last column follows from the nine lemma.
∎

Theorem 6.19
.

When
p
is even,
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
. When
p
is odd,
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}\cong\overline{\OS}_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Proof.

For
p
even, given
\gamma=\sum\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}\in\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
(possibly with repeated summands), we have
2\gamma=\sum[\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}-\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},-{\bf v},p}]
by the anti-symmetry of elements in
\Theta
. Hence
2\cdot\tilde{a}_{p}(\gamma)=\sum[\tilde{a}_{p}(\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p})-\tilde{a}_{p}(\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},-{\bf v},p})]=0
, but as
B_{p}
is a subgroup of the free abelian group
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
,
\tilde{a}_{p}(\gamma)=0
. In particular,
B_{p}=\{0\}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}={\mathcal{A}}_{p}/B_{p}\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
.

For
p
odd (which is the assumption for the rest of this proof), we also have that
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}-\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},-{\bf v},p}\in\Theta\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
for any prefix chain
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}\in{\mathcal{P}}
, except that
\tilde{a}_{p}(\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p}-\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},-{\bf v},p})=2\cdot\tilde{a}_{p}(\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf v},p})\in B_{p}
for parity reason in the definition of
\tilde{a}_{p}
. Since the prefix chains generate
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
,
2\cdot{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\subset B_{p}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}
is a quotient of
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}/2{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
, in particular a vector space over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The dimension of
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
is at least that of
\overline{\OS}_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
: tensoring the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}^{\rm proj}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}_{p}^{\rm proj}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}\rightarrow 0
with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
gives
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}^{\rm proj}}\rightarrow\overline{{\mathcal{P}}_{p}^{\rm proj}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\rm proj}\rightarrow 0
. Meanwhile, by combining Theorem
B
with
[
24
, Proposition 5.7]
, we find that
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}_{p}^{\rm proj}}/\overline{{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}^{\rm proj}}\cong\overline{\OS}_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
.

To see that this is exactly the dimension of
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{{\rm proj}}
, fix an arbitrary ordering of
E
, we claim that the projection of NBC-chains indexed by NBC-sets that do not contain the minimum element
i_{1}\in E
are sufficient to span
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}^{\rm proj}/{\mathcal{P}}_{p+1}^{\rm proj}
. Since the number of such NBC-sets of size
p
is the dimension of
\overline{\OS}_{p}(\underline{M};{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
, the tensor product above is actually exact. Let
\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{p}\}
be a NBC-set (
i_{2},\ldots,i_{p}
are not necessarily the next
p-1
smallest elements) and
{\mathcal{F}}
be the respective NBC-flag. By reorientation if necessary, without loss of generality we consider
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p}=\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p-1}-\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf d}_{p},p-1}
, whose image in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm proj}]
is equal to that of
\gamma^{\prime}:=\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p-1}-\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 1}-{\bf f}_{p},p-1}
.
We have
\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
: let
h_{j_{1}}\ldots h_{j_{p-1}}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{p-1}
, we may assume
j_{k}\in F_{k}\setminus F_{k-1}
for every
k
, or otherwise
h_{j_{1}}\ldots h_{j_{p-1}}(\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p-1})=h_{j_{1}}\ldots h_{j_{p-1}}(\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 1}-{\bf f}_{p},p-1})=0
; now each tope
T_{\bf v}
in the support of
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 0},p-1}
can be paired up with
T_{{\bf v}+{\bf 1}-{\bf f}_{p}}
of
\gamma_{{\mathcal{F}},{\bf 1}-{\bf f}_{p},p-1}
, which have the same evaluation under
h_{j_{1}}\ldots h_{j_{p-1}}
and of same coefficients in the respective chains, hence
h_{j_{1}}\ldots h_{j_{p-1}}(\gamma^{\prime})=0
as well. Therefore, we may write
\gamma^{\prime}
uniquely as a linear combination of NBC-chains, which we claim those indexed by NBC-sets that contains
i_{1}
do not appear: let
S\ni i_{1}
be such a NBC-set, consider
h^{S}:=\prod_{i\in S}h_{i}
, the same tope pairing argument shows that
h^{S}(\gamma^{\prime})=0
, which concludes the argument by Corollary
6.5
.4

Asymptotic expansions for the reciprocal Hardy-Littlewood logarithmic integrals Thanks: Email: glenn.bruda@ufl.edu
Glenn Bruda

Abstract
. Defining a family of recurrences, we generalize Comtet’s formula for the generating function of the enumeration of indecomposable permutations. Consequently, we generalize Panaitopol’s asymptotic expansion for the prime counting function, obtaining asymptotic expansions salient to the first Hardy-Littlewood conjecture.



1.
Introduction

Preliminarily, a
permutation

\sigma
of length
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is a bijection
\sigma:\{1,2,\dots,n\}\to\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Given a permutation
\sigma
of length
n
, the one-line notation of
\sigma
is
m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{n}
, where each
m_{i}=\sigma(i)
. For example,
\sigma_{1}=12345
and
\sigma_{2}=23451
are permutations of length
5
, where
\sigma_{1}
is the identity mapping and
\sigma_{2}(k)=k+1\pmod{5}
for all
k\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}
. A
proper prefix
of a permutation
\sigma
of length
n
is a restriction
\sigma|_{\{1,2,\dots,k\}}
for some
1\leq k<n
. For example, the proper prefixes of
23451
are
2345,234,23,2
.

As seen in
[
5
]
, an
indecomposable permutation

connected permutation

according to
[

is a permutation such that no proper prefix of itself is a permutation. For example,
321,312,231
are all indecomposable since
2,3,31,32,23
are not permutations. On the other hand,
123,132,213
are not indecomposable since
1,12,21
are all permutations. A thorough introduction to indecomposable permutations also appears in
[

.

All power series in this paper should be considered as formal power series over the integers. A
formal power series
over a ring
R
is an infinite series of monomials

\displaystyle c_{0}+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+c_{3}x^{3}+\cdots

such that each coefficient
c_{i}\in R
. An introduction to formal power series may be found in
[
9
]
.

Comtet introduced indecomposable permutations in
[
2
]
, motivated by determining the coefficients of the reciprocal of
\sum_{n\geq 0}n!x^{n}
. In particular, he found that

\displaystyle 1-\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}n!x^{n}\right)^{-1}\ =\ \sum_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{I}_{n}x^{n},

where
\mathcal{I}_{n}
is the number of indecomposable permutations of length
n
. We generalize this result in Proposition
2.2
, determining the coefficients of the reciprocal of
\sum_{n\geq 0}(n+k-1)!x^{n}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

We use Proposition
2.2
to determine the asymptotic behavior of the reciprocals of a class of integrals appearing in Conjecture
1.3
. In particular, we obtain asymptotic expansions for said reciprocal functions.

Definition 1.1

(Asymptotic expansion,
[
7
, Section 2.2]
)
.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of real-valued functions such that
\varphi_{n+1}(x)=o(\varphi_{n}(x))
as
x\to\infty
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}\vcentcolon=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
. Let
f
be a real-valued function. Suppose for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle f(x)-\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}\varphi_{n}(x)\ =\ o(\varphi_{N}(x))

as
x\to\infty
, where
\{a_{n}\}
is a sequence of real numbers. Then we say that
\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\varphi_{n}(x)
is the asymptotic expansion of
f(x)
with respect to the asymptotic sequence
\varphi_{n}(x)
, writing

\displaystyle f(x)\ \sim\ \sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\varphi_{n}(x).

In
[
11
]
, Panaitopol showed that the enumeration of indecomposable permutations appeared in an asymptotic expansion of the prime counting function. An extensive discussion on asymptotic expansions of the prime counting function may be found in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 1.2

(Panaitopol,
[
11
]
)
.

Letting
\pi(x)
denote the prime counting function, we have the asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\ \sim\ \frac{\log x}{x}\left(1-\frac{1}{\log x}-\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{\mathcal{I}_{n}}{(\log x)^{n}}\right),

where
\mathcal{I}_{n}
denotes the number of indecomposable permutations of length
n
.

We remark that Proposition
1.2
is equivalent to the
k=1
case of Theorem
2.7
since
\text{li}(x)^{-1}
and
\pi(x)^{-1}
have the same asymptotic expansion with respect to the asymptotic sequence
x^{-1}(\log x)^{-n+1}
. Indeed, letting
n\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary and appealing to the classical estimate

\displaystyle\pi(x)\ =\ \text{li}(x)+O\left(xe^{-a\sqrt{\log x}}\right)\text{~for some~}a>0

of de la Vallée Poussin
[
3
]
, we have

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\frac{x}{\pi(x)\log^{-n+1}(x)}-\frac{x}{\text{li}(x)\log^{-n+1}(x)}\ =\ x\log^{n-1}(x)\left(\frac{1}{\pi(x)}-\frac{1}{\text{li}(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=\ x\log^{n-1}(x)\left(\frac{1}{\text{li}(x)+O(xe^{-a\sqrt{\log x}})}-\frac{1}{\text{li}(x)}\right)\ =\ x\log^{n-1}(x)\left(\frac{O(xe^{-a\sqrt{\log x}})}{\text{li}(x)(\text{li}(x)+O(xe^{-a\sqrt{\log x}}))}\right)

\displaystyle=\ x\log^{n-1}(x)~O\left(\frac{xe^{-a\sqrt{\log x}}}{\text{li}(x)^{2}}\right)\ =\ x\log^{n-1}(x)~O\left(\frac{xe^{-a\sqrt{\log x}}}{x^{2}/\log^{2}(x)}\right)\ =\ O\left(e^{-a\sqrt{\log x}}\log^{n+1}(x)\right),

which converges to zero for any
a>0
since the last function is globally nonnegative and decreasing for
x>\exp((2n+2)^{2}/a^{2})
. With this remark noted, Proposition
1.2
may be quickly obtained from Comtet’s generating function formula by following the
k=1
case of the proof of Theorem
2.7
.

The famous prime number theorem, equivalently asserting that the prime counting function is asymptotic to the logarithmic integral, is generalized by the first Hardy-Littlewood conjecture.

Conjecture 1.3

(First Hardy-Littlewood Conjecture,
[

Let
0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{k}

P=(p,p+2m_{1},p+2m_{2},\dots,p+2m_{k})
forms a complete residue class with respect to some prime, the number of primes
p\leq x
such that
p,p+2m_{1},p+2m_{2},\dots,p+2m_{k}
are prime, denoted
\pi_{P}(x)
, is asymptotic

\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/g(x)=1
.
to

\displaystyle 2^{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\text{~prime}\\
q\geq 3\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{-k-1}\left(1-\frac{w(q;2m_{1},2m_{2},\dots,2m_{k})}{q}\right)\int_{2}^{x}\frac{1}{(\log t)^{k+1}}dt,

Like the prime number theorem, Conjecture

does not concern an asymptotic expansion; rather, only the lowest order asymptotic term of

is regarded. Theorem

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{(\log t)^{k}}dt\right)^{-1}\ \sim\ \frac{(\log x)^{k}}{x}\left(1-\frac{k}{\log x}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}^{(k)}}{(\log x)^{n+1}}\right)

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, where the dashed integral denotes the Cauchy principal value and the sequence
a_{n}^{(k)}
is as defined in Definition

2.7

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then

\displaystyle 1-(k-1)!\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}{(n+k-1)!}x^{n}\right)^{-1}\ =\ kx+\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{(k)}x^{n+1},

taken as formal power series.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Let
I_{k}(x)\vcentcolon=kx+\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{(k)}x^{n+1}
and for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
A_{m}(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{n\geq 0}(n+m)!x^{n}
. Then by the definition of
a_{n}^{(k)}
,

\displaystyle(k-1)!I_{k}(x)\ =\ k!x-\sum_{n\geq 1}(k-1)!a_{n}^{(k)}x^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\ k!x+x(A_{k}(x)-k!)-kx(A_{k-1}(x)-(k-1)!)-x\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}(n-m+k-2)!a_{m+1}^{(k)}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\ k!x+xA_{k}(x)-kxA_{k-1}(x)-x^{3}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(n-m+k)!a_{m+1}^{(k)}x^{n}.

Noting that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n+1}^{(k)}x^{n}\ =\ x^{-2}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{(k)}x^{n+1}\ =\ x^{-2}(I_{k}(x)-kx),

we obtain

\displaystyle(k-1)!I_{k}(x)\

\displaystyle=\ k!x+xA_{k}(x)-kxA_{k-1}(x)-x(I_{k}(x)-kx)A_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\ k!x-kxA_{k-1}(x)-x(I_{k}(x)-kx-1)A_{k}(x).

It then follows that

\displaystyle I_{k}(x)\ =\ \frac{k!x+(1+kx)xA_{k}(x)-kxA_{k-1}(x)}{(k-1)!+xA_{k}(x)}.

We may now simplify the above since

\displaystyle(k-1)!+xA_{k}(x)\ =\ A_{k-1}(x).

Indeed, we thusly have

\displaystyle I_{k}(x)\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{k!x+(1+kx)\left(A_{k-1}(x)-(k-1)!\right)-kxA_{k-1}(x)}{A_{k-1}(x)}

\displaystyle=\ 1-\frac{(k-1)!}{A_{k-1}(x)}

\displaystyle=\ 1-(k-1)!\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}{(n+k-1)!}x^{n}\right)^{-1},

as desired.
∎

Now with Proposition
2.2
proven, we define the
exponential integral
and the
Hardy-Littlewood logarithmic integrals
. Then, after invoking the standard expansion stated in Proposition
2.4
and inductively proving Lemma

Definition 2.3

(Exponential integral)
.

We define
\text{Ei}:\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\text{Ei}(x)\ \vcentcolon=\ \mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt,

where the dashed integral denotes the Cauchy principal value.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
8
,
Equation 6.12.2
]
)
.

As
x\to\infty
, we have the asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle\text{Ei}(x)\ \sim\ \frac{e^{x}}{x}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{n!}{x^{n}}.

Definition 2.5

(Hardy-Littlewood logarithmic integrals)
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we define
\text{li}_{k}:\mathbb{R}\backslash\{1\}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\text{li}_{k}(x)\ \vcentcolon=\ \mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{(\log t)^{k}}dt,

where the dashed integral denotes the Cauchy principal value. Note that
\text{li}_{1}(x)
is the usual logarithmic integral, denoted
\text{li}(x)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then

\displaystyle\text{li}_{k}(x)\ =\ \frac{1}{(k-1)!}\left(\text{li}(x)-x\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}\frac{i!}{(\log x)^{i+1}}\right).

Proof.

This result lends a simple inductive proof via integration by parts. For
k=1
, the claim follows from the definition of the logarithmic integral. For the inductive step, note that

\displaystyle\text{li}_{k}(x)\ =\ \frac{x}{(\log x)^{k}}+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{x}\frac{k}{(\log t)^{k+1}}dt.

So by inductive hypothesis,

\displaystyle\text{li}_{k+1}(x)\ =\ \mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{(\log t)^{k+1}}dt\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{k!}\left(\text{li}(x)-x\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}\frac{i!}{(\log x)^{i+1}}\right)-\frac{x}{k(\log x)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{k!}\left(\text{li}(x)-x\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{i!}{(\log x)^{i+1}}\right).

∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then we have the asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\text{li}_{k}(x)}\ \sim\ \frac{(\log x)^{k}}{x}\left(1-\frac{k}{\log x}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}^{(k)}}{(\log x)^{n+1}}\right).

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
A(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{n\geq 0}n!x^{n}
. By Proposition
2.2
,

\displaystyle kx+\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{(k)}x^{n+1}\

\displaystyle=\ 1-(k-1)!\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}{(n+k-1)!}x^{n}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\ 1-(k-1)!\left(\sum_{n\geq k-1}{n!}x^{n-k+1}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\ 1-\frac{(k-1)!x^{k-1}}{\sum_{n\geq k-1}n!x^{n}}

\displaystyle=\ 1-\frac{(k-1)!x^{k-1}}{A(x)-\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}i!x^{i}}.

After a change of variable, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(k-1)!x^{1-k}}{A(1/x)-\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}i!x^{-i}}\ =\ 1-\frac{k}{x}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}^{(k)}}{x^{n+1}}.

Proposition
2.4
gives the relation
A(1/x)\sim xe^{-x}\text{Ei}(x)
; thus,

\displaystyle 1-\frac{k}{x}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}^{(k)}}{x^{n+1}}\

\displaystyle\sim\ \frac{(k-1)!x^{1-k}}{xe^{-x}\text{Ei}(x)-\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}i!x^{-i}}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{(k-1)!}{x^{k}e^{-x}\left(\text{Ei}(x)-e^{x}\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}i!x^{-i-1}\right)}.

Noting that
\text{Ei}(x)=\text{li}(e^{x})
, by Lemma
2.6
we have

\displaystyle x^{k}e^{-x}\left(1-\frac{k}{x}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}^{(k)}}{x^{n+1}}\right)\ \sim\ \frac{(k-1)!}{\text{Ei}(x)-e^{x}\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}i!x^{-i-1}}\ =\ \frac{1}{\text{li}_{k}(e^{x})}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\text{li}_{k}(x)}\ \sim\ \frac{(\log x)^{k}}{x}\left(1-\frac{k}{\log x}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}^{(k)}}{(\log x)^{n+1}}\right),

as desired.
∎

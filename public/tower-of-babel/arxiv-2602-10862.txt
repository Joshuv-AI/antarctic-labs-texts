A note on smoothly slice links in S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2}
Marco Marengon and Clayton McDonald

Abstract
. We give an alternative proof of a result of Miyazaki and Yasuhara that there exists links that are not smoothly slice in S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} . We discuss potential applications to the detection of exotic S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} . This is a follow-up note to a similar paper for the ℂ ​ ℙ 2 ​ # ​ ℂ ​ ℙ 2 ¯ \mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}} case.



1.
Introduction

In this note, which is meant as a companion to
[
MM24
]
, we give a proof in the smooth category that there exists a non-slice 2-component link in
S^{2}\times S^{2}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The 2-component link in Figure
1
is not smoothly slice in
S^{2}\times S^{2}
.

Figure 1.
A 2-component link which is not smoothly slice in
S^{2}\times S^{2}
.

Recall that a link
L\subset S^{3}
is (smoothly or topologically)
slice in
X
if it bounds (smoothly or locally flatly) embedded disjoint discs in
X\setminus B^{4}
.
A classical result
[
Nor69
,
Suz69
]
shows that every knot is smoothly slice in both
S^{2}\times S^{2}
and
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
.
In
[
MM24
]
we produced a 2-component link that is not smoothly slice in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
, and show that it can potentially lead to detecting an exotic
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
(see
[
MM24
, Proposition 6.1]
for a precise statement).

Our method from
[
MM24
]
can be adapted to produce links that are not slice in
S^{2}\times S^{2}
, which in fact proves a result more general than our Theorem
1.1
(see Theorem
3.8
). We note that Miyazaki and Yasuhara have previously proved that there is a non-slice 2-component link in a punctured
S^{2}\times S^{2}

in the topological category

[
MY97
]
.
While their result is technically stronger, it cannot be used to detect non-sliceness (in
S^{2}\times S^{2}
) of our link in Figure
1
, because their argument requires the signatures of the components to be
0\pmod{8}
.
More importantly, since their argument is topological, it cannot be used to detect exotic 4-manifolds.
By contrast, our proof uses the smooth genus function, so our link is not known to be non-slice in the topological category. This means we can potentially use it to construct exotic 4-manifolds, see Proposition
4.1
.

The proof of Theorem

For the rest of this section, let

Let

3.2.
The genus function on
S^{2}\times S^{2}

Given a smooth, connected 4-manifold

, the 4-ball genus of a knot

gives a first obstruction to the homology classes of

that are represented by a disc with boundary

More precisely, if there is such a disc in homology class
\alpha

sitting in a homology class that by abuse of notation we still call
\alpha\in H_{2}(X)\cong H_{2}(X^{\circ},S^{3})

Then, knowledge of the genus function on

can give obstruction to such an

Theorem 3.2

(
[

, Corollary 1.3]
)
.

The minimal genus of a smoothly embedded orientable surface in

, with respect to the obvious basis, is

G_{S^{2}\times S^{2}}(a_{1},a_{2})=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if $a_{1}\cdot a_{2}=0$}\\
(|a_{1}|-1)\cdot(|a_{2}|-1)&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

Let us now return to the link

whose sliceness in

3.6.
Levine-Tristram signatures

The last step in order to prove Theorem

is to obstruct the existence of pairs of discs

and

in the homology classes given by Lemma

We recall that

These are the assumptions we make on the signature functions:

(A6)

.

(A7)

\sigma_{A}(\zeta_{4})=\sigma_{B}(\zeta_{4})=2
.

(A8)

\sigma_{A}(\zeta_{8})=\sigma_{B}(\zeta_{8})=2

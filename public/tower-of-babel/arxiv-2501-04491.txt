A fast iterative thresholding and support-and-scale shrinking algorithm (FITS 3 ) for non-Lipschitz group sparse optimization (I): the case of least-squares fidelity Thanks: Received date, and accepted date (The correct dates will be entered by the editor).
Yanan Zhao, Qiaoli Dong, Yufei Zhao, Chunlin Wu

Abstract
We consider to design a new efficient and easy-to-implement algorithm to solve a general group sparse optimization model with a class of non-convex non-Lipschitz regularizations, named as fast iterative thresholding and support-and-scale shrinking algorithm (FITS 3 ). In this paper we focus on the case of a least-squares fidelity. FITS 3 is designed from a lower bound theory of such models and by integrating thresholding operation, linearization and extrapolation techniques. The FITS 3 has two advantages. Firstly, it is quite efficient and especially suitable for large-scale problems, because it adopts support-and-scale shrinking and does not need to solve any linear or nonlinear system. For two important special cases, the FITS 3 contains only simple calculations like matrix-vector multiplication and soft thresholding. Secondly, the FITS 3 algorithm has a sequence convergence guarantee under proper assumptions. The numerical experiments and comparisons to recent existing non-Lipschitz group recovery algorithms demonstrate that, the proposed FITS 3 achieves similar recovery accuracies, but costs only around a half of the CPU time by the second fastest compared algorithm for median- or large-scale problems.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following non-convex and non-Lipschitz group sparse optimization model with a least-squares fidelity

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\mathcal{E}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\sum_{g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}}\psi(\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p})\right\},

(1.1)

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}(m<n)
is a nonzero measurement matrix,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the observation data,
\alpha>0
is the regularization parameter,
\psi(t):[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a regularization function,
p\in[1,+\infty)
,
\mathcal{G}
is the group index set,
x=(x_{g_{1}},x_{g_{2}},...,x_{g_{r}})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x_{g_{i}}\ (g_{i}\in\mathcal{G})
as its
i
th group. If each
g_{i}
has only one element, the group sparse reconstruction model (
1.1
) degenerates to the conventional non-group reconstruction model.
The regularization function
\psi
considered in this paper is assumed to be concave and non-Lipschitz around 0; see Assumption
2.1
for more details.
One example of
\psi
is
\psi(t)=t^{q}
(
0<q<1
).
Then the regularization term becomes the widely used non-convex and non-Lipschitz
\ell_{p,q}
regularization as follows

\|x\|_{p,q}^{q}=\sum_{g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p}^{q},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

With such a regularization term, (
1.1
) becomes the
\ell_{2}
-
\ell_{p,q}
minimization model

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\sum_{g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p}^{q}.

(1.2)

The minimization model (
1.2
) originates from the group sparse signal reconstruction problem. This problem actually is to find an
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to approximate the unknown ground
truth
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from the following measurement

b=A\underline{x}+\varepsilon,

(1.3)

where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the measurement noise with
m<n
.
The above linear system is an underdetermined linear system, so it usually has an infinite number of solutions.
If the ground truth
\underline{x}
is known to be sparse or groupwise sparse, the regularization term based on the sparse prior information is used to constrain the solution space. Researchers first considered the problem of sparse signal recovery and proposed

“norm” regularization model (

) model
[

. Later on, how to enhance the recoverability of the groupwise sparse signals or images has attracted attentions in many scenarios like color imaging (
[

In

The global convergence of PGM-GSO was established, and its local linear convergence rate was derived under certain assumptions for the case

and

.
The inexact iterative support shrinking algorithm with
proximal linearization for group sparse optimization (InISSAPL-GSO) was proposed in
[

]
for solving the general
\ell_{r}
-
\ell_{p,q}
minimization problem where
1\leq r\leq+\infty,\ p\geq 1
and
0<q<1
.
It was designed by developing a group support inclusion property, like some previous works. InISSAPL-GSO has a two-loop structure with a scaled alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) as the inner solver. In
[
23
]
, inspired by the obtained lower bound theory,
the iterative reweighted least squares with thresholding algorithm (IRLS-th), which utilized a group support shrinking strategy similar to
[
55
]
, was designed for the
\ell_{2}
-
\ell_{2,q}
minimization problem. This IRLS-th algorithm was proposed on the basis of the IRLS algorithm (
[
13
,
18
,
30
,
31
]
), and has an improved global convergence guarantee. We will compare these algorithms in Section
5
.

In this paper, we present a simple and efficient algorithm with convergence guarantee to solve (
1.1
).
We first give a lower bound theory for the minimization model (
1.1
), which is a straightforward generalization of the conclusion in
[
23
]
.
This naturally motivates a group support-and-scale shrinkage strategy in the iterative algorithm design.
Combining this strategy with a linearization method and an extrapolation technique, we construct an iterative algorithm named as the fast iterative thresholding with support-and-scale shrinking algorithm (FITS
3
).
FITS
3
is a unified iterative algorithm for solving model (
1.1
) with
p\geq 1
, and in particular, it can be applied to solve the
\ell_{p,q}
regularization model (
1.2
).
The subproblem in each iteration is strongly convex, and the analytical solution of the subproblem is derived when
p=1
and
p=2
.
Furthermore, we prove that the sequence generated by FITS
3
with an inexact inner solver converges globally to a stationary point of the objective function in (
1.1
). The convergence rate of FITS
3
can also be shown using a similar analysis as in the literatures. The numerical experiments show that, with comparable reconstruction accuracy, the FITS
3
algorithm has a definite advantage in terms of running time over other algorithms, especially for median- or large-scale problems.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give some notations and preliminaries. In Section
3
, we propose our new algorithm named as FITS
3
. The convergence analysis of FITS
3
is established in Section
4
. The numerical experiments and comparisons are presented in Section
5
.

Notation and preliminary

Suppose that
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})^{\top}
is an
n
-dimensional vector.
We use
[n]=\left\{1,2,...,n\right\}
to denote the index set of
x
and
x_{j}
is the
j
th element of
x
. Let
\mathcal{G}=\left\{g_{1},...,g_{i},...,g_{r}\right\}
denote a collection of index sets of groups with
g_{i}=\left\{n_{i-1}+1,...,n_{i-1}+n_{i}\right\}
,
n_{0}=0
,
n_{i}\in[n]
(
1\leq i\leq r
), and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}=n
.
With this,

x=(x_{g_{1}}^{\top},x_{g_{2}}^{\top},...,x_{g_{r}}^{\top})^{\top}
represents a group structure of
x
as follows

x=(\underbrace{x_{1},...,x_{n_{1}}}_{x_{g_{1}}^{\top}},...,\underbrace{x_{n_{i-1}+1},...,x_{n_{i-1}+n_{i}}}_{x_{g_{i}}^{\top}},...,\underbrace{x_{n-n_{r}+1},...,x_{n}}_{x_{g_{r}}^{\top}})^{\top},

where
x_{g_{i}}
is the
i
th group of
x
.
Let
\#g_{i}
represent the cardinality of
g_{i}
. We have
\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\#g_{i})=n
.
Note that we only consider the case with no overlapping between groups.
For a group
x_{g_{i}}
,
x_{g_{i}}=0
means that
x_{j}=0,\forall j\in g_{i}
. See, e.g.,
[
23
]
for details.
For a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, we also partition it into submatrices associating to the group structure of

, i.e.,

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}A_{1,g_{1}}&A_{1,g_{2}}&...&A_{1,g_{r}}\\
...&...&...&...\\
A_{m,g_{1}}&A_{m,g_{2}}&...&A_{m,g_{r}}\\
\end{array}\right].

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subset of

, then

denotes the sub-vector of

consisting of the elements determined by indices collected in

. Similarly,

denotes the sub-matrix of

.
We also recall the following notations for support sets:

\mathcal{S}(x)=\left\{g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}:\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p}\neq 0\right\},\qquad s(x_{g_{i}})=\left\{j\in g_{i}:x_{j}\neq 0\right\},

where
\mathcal{S}(x)
is called the group support set of

and

is called the support set of

(see, e.g.,
[
23
,
55
]
).

We assume the regularization function
\psi
satisfying the following

Assumption 2.1

(i)

The function
\psi:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a concave coercive (
[
42
, Definition 3.25]
) function, and is

on
(0,+\infty)
with
\psi(0)=0
.

(ii)

\psi^{\prime}(0+)=+\infty
.

(iii)

\psi^{\prime\prime}
is an increasing function on
(0,+\infty)
.

Assumption

implies several consequences. Firstly,

. Secondly, the objective function

, and thus (

) has a trivial solution

. Therefore, we consider the case of

is non-Lipschitz. Herein, we list some functions that satisfy Assumption

:
\psi_{1}(t)=t^{q},0<q<1;\psi_{2}(t)=\log(t^{q}+1),0<q<1

Remark 2.1

We list some straightforward properties (see, e.g.,
[
55
]
) of
\psi
.

(i)

For any
c>0
,
\psi^{\prime}(t)
is
L_{c}
-Lipschitz continuous on

, i.e., there exists constant
L_{c}>0

\left|\psi^{\prime}(t)-\psi^{\prime}(s)\right|\leq L_{c}\left|t-s\right|,
for any

(ii)

For any

and

, the subdifferential of
\psi(\|w\|_{p})
is

\partial\psi(\|w\|_{p})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\prod_{j=1}^{d}(-\infty,+\infty),\;\;\text{if}\;\;\|w\|_{p}=0;\\
&\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mu_{j},\qquad\quad\;\;\;\;\text{if}\;\;\|w\|_{p}\neq 0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

where

means the Cartesian product of sets, and

\mu_{j}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left\{\psi^{\prime}(\|w\|_{p})\|w\|_{p}^{1-p}|w_{j}|^{p-1}\sgn(w_{j})\right\},\quad p>1;\\
&\left\{\psi^{\prime}(\|w\|_{1})\sgn(w_{j})\right\},\qquad\quad\qquad\;\qquad p=1,w_{j}\neq 0;\\
&[-\psi^{\prime}(\|w\|_{1}),\;\psi^{\prime}(\|w\|_{1})],\;\,\;\qquad\quad\;\;\qquad p=1,w_{j}=0;\end{aligned}\right.

with the sign function
\sgn(\cdot)
.

Clearly, the subdifferential of
\mathcal{E}
in (
1.1
) is

\partial\mathcal{E}(x)=A^{\top}(Ax-b)+\alpha\prod_{g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}}\partial\psi(\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p}),

where
\partial\psi(\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{2})
is calculated by (
2.4
).

.

(

)
For any

,

, we have the following inequality

(2.5)

.

(

For any

,

3
Algorithm

In this section, we start from a theorem on lower bound theory, and combine thresholding, linearization method and the extrapolation technique, to develop a new efficient and easy-to-implement algorithm, i.e., the fast iterative thresholding with support-and-scale shrinking algorithm (FITS
3
) for the non-convex non-Lipschitz minimization problem in (
1.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

(Lower bound theory) If
\mathcal{E}
in (
1.1
) has a local minimum at
x^{*}
, then for any
g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}
,

x^{*}_{g_{i}}\neq 0\;\Longrightarrow\;\|x^{*}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}\geq\kappa\text{ with }\kappa=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\psi^{\prime\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{-\|A\|_{2}^{2}}{\alpha}\right),\;\quad\;\;\;1\leq p\leq 2,\\
&(\psi^{\prime\prime})^{-1}\left(\frac{-\|A\|_{2}^{2}}{\alpha(\#g_{i})^{1-2/p}}\right),\;p>2.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof 3.2
.

This proof is a straightforward generalization of
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem
3.1
implies a group support inclusion relationship.
Suppose that a local minimizer
x^{\ast}
is very near to some iteration point
x^{k}
such that
\|x^{\ast}-x^{k}\|_{p}<\kappa
. Then for
g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}
with
x^{k}_{g_{i}}=0
,
\|x^{\ast}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}\leq\|x^{\ast}_{g_{i}}-x^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}+\|x^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}<\kappa
, which implies
x^{\ast}_{g_{i}}=0
by Theorem
3.1
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{S}(x^{\ast})\subseteq\mathcal{S}(x^{k}):=\mathcal{S}^{k}
. Such
support inclusion relationship naturally motivates a group support shrinking strategy at each iteration, like
[
60
,
55
,
23
,
52
]
and references therein.
In addition, considering the finite word length of real computers and to avoid extremely large linearization weights described later,
we relax the computation of the group support set
\mathcal{S}^{k}
by using a thresholding operation like
[
23
,
52
,
50
]
. In particular, let
\tilde{x}^{k}
be as follows

\tilde{x}^{k}_{g_{i}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &x^{k}_{g_{i}},\;\;\;\text{if}\;\,\|x^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}\geq\tau;\\
&0,\quad\;\;\text{if}\;\,\|x^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}<\tau,\end{aligned}\right.

for some thresholding parameter
\tau
, and denote
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}=\mathcal{S}(\tilde{x}^{k})
. Obviously, it holds that
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{k}
(
[
52
]
).
Consequently, given
x^{k}
, we can compute
x^{k+1}
by

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\sum_{g_{i}\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}\psi(\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p})\right\}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\;x_{g_{i}}=0,\;\forall g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}.

Note that the problem (
3.7
) is still non-convex and cannot be solved directly. Here, we linearize both the fidelity term and the regularization term
[
24
,
60
]
at
\tilde{x}^{k}
, and
x^{k+1}
can be computed by

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\|A\tilde{x}^{k}-b\|_{2}^{2}+&(x-\tilde{x}^{k})^{\top}A^{\top}(A\tilde{x}^{k}-b)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|x-\tilde{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{2}+\\
&\alpha\sum_{g_{i}\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}\left[\psi(\|\tilde{x}_{g_{i}}^{k}\|_{p})+\psi^{\prime}(\|\tilde{x}_{g_{i}}^{k}\|_{p})(\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p}-\|\tilde{x}^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p})\right]\end{aligned}\right\}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\;x_{g_{i}}=0,\;\forall g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k},

where
\beta>\|A^{\top}A\|_{2}
. For (
3.8
), we can simply substitute the constraint into the objective function, which not only eliminates constraints but also reduces the scale of the problem. Let
B^{k}=A_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}
. Clearly, one has
A\tilde{x}^{k}=B^{k}\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}
.
When the group support set
\mathcal{S}(x)\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}
, we derive that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\|A\tilde{x}^{k}-b\|_{2}^{2}+(x-\tilde{x}^{k})^{\top}A^{\top}(A\tilde{x}^{k}-b)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|x-\tilde{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{2}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\|B^{k}\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-b\|_{2}^{2}+(x_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}})^{\top}(B^{k})^{\top}(B^{k}\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-b)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|x_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}\|^{2}_{2}.\end{split}

Therefore, the problem in (
3.8
) is equivalent to the following unconstrained minimization problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &x^{k+1}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}=\arg\min_{x_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}}\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\|B^{k}\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-b\|_{2}^{2}+&(x_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}})^{\top}(B^{k})^{\top}(B^{k}\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-b)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|x_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}\|^{2}_{2}+\\
&\alpha\sum_{g_{i}\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}\left[\psi(\|\tilde{x}_{g_{i}}^{k}\|_{p})+\psi^{\prime}(\|\tilde{x}_{g_{i}}^{k}\|_{p})(\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p}-\|\tilde{x}^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p})\right]\end{aligned}\right\}\\
&x^{k+1}_{\mathcal{G}\setminus\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.9)

The problem (
3.9
) can be solved efficiently. To further speed up the computation, we borrow the idea of the extrapolation technique (
[
36
,
6
,
60
,
38
]
),
and introduce a new variable
z^{k}
defined as:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &z^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}=\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}+t_{k}(\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-\tilde{x}^{k-1}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}});\\
&z^{k}_{\mathcal{G}\setminus\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.10)

where
t_{k}
is the parameter of extrapolation. Note that
\mathcal{S}(z^{k})\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}
.
We then propose to update
x^{k+1}
as follows

\left\{\begin{aligned} &x^{k+1}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}=\arg\min_{x_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}}\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\|B^{k}z^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-b\|_{2}^{2}+&(x_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-z^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}})^{\top}(B^{k})^{\top}(B^{k}z^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-b)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|x_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-z^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}\|^{2}_{2}+\\
&\alpha\sum_{g_{i}\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}\left[\psi(\|\tilde{x}_{g_{i}}^{k}\|_{p})+\psi^{\prime}(\|\tilde{x}_{g_{i}}^{k}\|_{p})(\|x_{g_{i}}\|_{p}-\|\tilde{x}^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p})\right]\end{aligned}\right\}\\

Algorithm 1

FITS
3
: fast iterative thresholding with support-and-scale shrinking

Input
\alpha,\beta,\mathrm{Tol},\tau,x^{0},x^{-1}=\tilde{x}^{-1}=x^{0},k=0,\text{MAXit}
and
\left\{t_{k}\right\}\subset[0,{\bar{t}})

.

while

k\leq\text{MAXit}

do

1. Thresholding:

\tilde{x}^{k}_{g_{i}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &x^{k}_{g_{i}},\;\;\;\text{if}\;\,\|x^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}\geq\tau;\\
&0,\quad\;\;\text{if}\;\,\|x^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}<\tau.\end{aligned}\right.

2. Find
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}=\left\{g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}:\|\tilde{x}^{k}_{g_{i}}\|_{p}\neq 0\right\}
.

3. Update

and

:

B^{k}=A_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}},\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} &z^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}=\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}+t_{k}(\tilde{x}^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-\tilde{x}^{k-1}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}});\\
&z^{k}_{\mathcal{G}\setminus\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

4. Compute

:

y^{k}=z^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-\frac{1}{\beta}(B^{k})^{\top}(B^{k}z^{k}_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{k}}-b)
.

5. Compute

6. If

), we get

\begin{split}\|\gamma^{k+1}\|_{2}\leq&\left((\|A^{\top}A\|_{2}+\beta+\frac{\beta\varepsilon}{2}+n\alpha L_{\underline{c}})\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{2}\beta(1-\varepsilon)\right)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|_{2}\\
&+(\|A^{\top}A\|_{2}+\beta)\sqrt{n}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|_{2}.\end{split}

(4.55)

Summarizing (
4.45
), (
4.47
) (
4.54
) and (
4.55
) completes the proof.

Lemma 4.8
.

For any
\beta>\|A^{\top}A\|_{2}
and the extrapolation parameters
\left\{t_{k}\right\}\subset[0,{\bar{t}})
satisfying
\sup_{k}t_{k}<{\bar{t}}
,
let
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
be a sequence generated by FITS
3
. Then, any cluster point of
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is a stationary point of
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof 4.9
.

By Lemma
4.2
(ii), the sequence
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is bounded and thus has a cluster point. Take an arbitrary cluster point of
\left\{x^{k}\right\}

denoted as
\hat{x}=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}x^{k_{l}}
. Then, we have
\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}(x^{k_{l}})=\mathcal{E}(\hat{x})
due to the continuity of
\mathcal{E}
. By Lemma
4.6
, one chooses a

\nu^{k_{l}}\in\partial\mathcal{E}(x^{k_{l}})
. It holds that
\nu^{k_{l}}\rightarrow 0
as
l\rightarrow\infty
by the inequality (
4.37
) and
the fact that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|_{2}=0
. The closedness of the graph of the limiting subdifferential operator guarantees that
0\in\partial\mathcal{E}(\hat{x})
.

Finally, we give our main convergence result of the FITS
3
algorithm.

Theorem 4.10
.

Assume that
\mathcal{H}(x,u)
is a KŁ function,
\beta>\|A^{\top}A\|_{2}
and the extrapolation parameters
\left\{t_{k}\right\}\subset[0,{\bar{t}})
satisfy
\sup_{k}t_{k}<{\bar{t}}
.
Let
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
be a sequence generated by FITS
3
. Then, the sequence
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
converges globally to a stationary point of
\mathcal{E}
. Moreover,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|_{2}<\infty
holds.

Proof 4.11
.

We use the abstract convergence theorem in
[
38
]
to show the results. Lemma
4.2
(i) (ii), Lemma
4.6
and the continuity of the function
\mathcal{E}
give (H1), (H2) and (H3) required by the abstract convergence theorem
[
38
, Theorem 3.7]
. Therefore, the sequence
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
converges to an
\overline{x}
which is a stationary point of
\mathcal{E}
by Lemma
4.8
. Moreover,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|_{2}<\infty
holds.

Remark 4.12
.

At least a broad class of objective functions
\mathcal{E}
satisfy KŁ property assumption on
\mathcal{H}
in Theorem

. If

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a new simple algorithm named as FITS
3
for a general non-convex non-Lipschitz group sparse regularization model with a least-squares fidelity, by integrating thresholding operation, group support-and-scale shrinkage, linearization and extrapolation techniques.
This iterative algorithm does not need to solve any linear or nonlinear system, and in two most important cases
p=1
and
p=2
it contains only simple operations like matrix-vector multiplication and soft thresholding. It thus is especially suitable for solving median- or large-scale problems. We also established the sequence convergence property of the proposed FITS
3
under an inexact condition at each iteration, which is applicable to our FITS
3
with a general
p\in[1,+\infty).

Numerical experiments illustrated the advantages of the FITS
3
, showing great potentials in various group sparse recovery applications in the current era of big data.

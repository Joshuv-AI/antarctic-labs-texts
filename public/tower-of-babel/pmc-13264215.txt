Identification of coronary calcified nodules on noninvasive coronary CT: A two-case report with intravascular imaging correlation
Authors: Junji Mochizuki, Mitsuaki Matsumura, Masafumi Suzuki, Shinya Misawa, Hiroaki Matsumi, Kentaro Kawada, Yoshiki Hata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Calcified nodules are a form of coronary calcification associated with acute coronary syndrome and adverse outcomes after percutaneous coronary intervention. We report 2 cases in which noninvasive high-resolution coronary computed tomography (CT) suggested calcified nodules later corroborated by intravascular optical coherence tomography. In case 1, CT demonstrated a protruding calcified focus in the right coronary artery with lower attenuation than adjacent calcification. In case 2, CT showed a small nodular lesion in the proximal left circumflex artery with a similar attenuation pattern. In both cases, the CT findings prompted a calcium-modification strategy and directly informed procedural planning. High-resolution coronary CT may therefore enable noninvasive recognition of calcified nodules with direct implications for preprocedural strategy selection; however, further studies are needed.

Introduction

Coronary artery calcification indicates progression of atherosclerosis. Advanced calcification can fracture to form calcified nodules protruding into the lumen [1]. Calcified nodules can trigger acute coronary syndrome and are associated with poor outcomes after percutaneous coronary intervention. Their identification is therefore important [2]. Performing interventions without doing so may lead to suboptimal stent expansion and increased risk of restenosis [3].

The characteristic imaging features of calcified nodules are relatively well known [4,5]. However, the current standard forms of imaging are invasive, including intravascular ultrasound and intravascular optical coherence tomography [6]. Coronary computed tomography (CT) is a widely used noninvasive method for assessing coronary atherosclerosis, and may have potential in identifying calcified nodules and therefore optimizing treatment strategies. This report presents 2 cases in which coronary CT suggested the presence of calcified nodules.

Case 1

An 80-year-old woman with a history of hypertension and dyslipidemia presented with dyspnea and chest pain. Electrocardiography and transthoracic echocardiography showed no significant abnormalities. Coronary CT imaging used the Revolution Apex Elite system (GE HealthCare, Milwaukee, WI). The scan parameters included tube voltage of 120 kV with automatic exposure control, tube rotation speed of 0.23 seconds, and detector collimation of 256 rows × 0.625 mm, giving scan coverage of 160 mm. Image acquisition was performed in high-resolution mode. Image reconstruction used a slice thickness of 0.625 mm with the high-spatial-resolution kernel HD Edge, and 40% weighting of adaptive statistical iterative reconstruction (ASiR-V) to balance spatial resolution and image noise. Images were evaluated using a fixed bone window setting (WW: 1500 HU; WL: 300 HU) to ensure consistent morphological and density assessment [7].

Image analysis using curved planar reconstruction and related images showed a focal calcified lesion measuring approximately 3.4 × 2.6 × 2.5 mm protruding into the lumen of the mid-right coronary artery, suggesting severe stenosis (Figs. 1A–C). To ensure measurement precision and minimize partial volume effects, attenuation was measured using a standardized circular region of interest (ROI) of 0.5 mm2 placed in the center of the calcified focus (Supplemental Fig.). In contrast to the adjacent calcification, which showed a mean CT attenuation of approximately 1330 Hounsfield units (HU) (Fig. 1D, red arrow), the CT attenuation of the severely stenotic calcified lesion was lower, at approximately 830 HU (Fig. 1E, yellow arrow). The use of a high-spatial-resolution kernel (HD Edge) and thin-slice reconstruction (0.625 mm) helped mitigate blooming artifacts, allowing for clear visualization of the lesion. The attenuation within the lesion was heterogeneous, and the lesion could be clearly distinguished from the vessel-wall calcification. These CT features were suggestive of a calcified nodule. A debulking strategy with rotational atherectomy was therefore used, followed by balloon predilation, giving adequate lumen expansion. Final angiography confirmed satisfactory flow with thrombolysis in myocardial infarction (TIMI) grade 3 (normal perfusion). No complications were observed. The patient remained asymptomatic during 6 months of clinical follow-up, with no additional imaging performed.

Case 2

A 70-year-old man with silent myocardial ischemia and a history of percutaneous coronary intervention for angina and receiving maintenance hemodialysis was referred for further evaluation because electrocardiography showed ST-segment depression in leads II, III, aVF, and V5–6. Imaging used the same system (Revolution Apex Elite) in high-resolution mode with the HD Edge kernel and 40% ASiR-V. In the proximal left circumflex artery, a localized short-segment nodular lesion measuring approximately 2.3 × 2.2 × 1.8 mm was observed protruding into the lumen, raising suspicion of severe stenosis (Figs. 2A–C). Consistent with case 1, attenuation was measured using the same window settings and a standardized circular ROI of 0.5 mm2 placed in the center of the calcified focus to ensure consistency and minimize partial volume effects (Supplemental Fig.). The CT attenuation of the calcified lesion was approximately 630 HU (Fig. 2D, yellow arrow), which was lower than that of the adjacent calcification (approximately 1370 HU) (Fig. 2E, red arrow). The lesion had well-defined margins and was clearly distinguishable from the surrounding calcification, findings suggestive of a calcified nodule. On the same day, coronary angiography demonstrated severe focal stenosis (Fig. 2F), and the instantaneous wave-free ratio was 0.64, indicating an ischemic threshold and confirming hemodynamically significant disease. Discussion

This report demonstrates that noninvasive coronary CT using high-resolution acquisition and reconstruction techniques can depict calcified nodules that have traditionally been evaluated using intravascular imaging. In both cases, coronary CT revealed localized calcified nodule protruding into the lumen with lower attenuation compared with the surrounding calcification. These imaging findings were confirmed as calcified nodules by coronary angiography and OCT.

To achieve sufficient spatial resolution while minimizing image noise, high-resolution acquisition was combined with the highest available high–spatial-resolution reconstruction kernel (HD Edge) and iterative reconstruction designed to preserve spatial resolution (ASiR-V 40%) [8]. This approach enabled clear visualization of irregular, nodular calcification protruding into the lumen, a characteristic feature of calcified nodules, as well as distinct attenuation differences relative to adjacent calcification. In the present cases, heterogeneity within the calcified nodule and a clear attenuation difference compared with neighboring calcification were detectable, helping distinguish the nodule from surrounding calcified plaque. Although blooming artifacts commonly hinder the visualization of heavily calcified structures [9], the adoption of a high-spatial-resolution protocol in our cases enabled the detection of subtle, visually distinct attenuation heterogeneity within the calcified nodule. Despite their subtlety, the consistent nodular morphology observed across multiple imaging planes reinforced confidence that these findings were not attributable to artefactual phenomena. Furthermore, image noise was quantitatively assessed and maintained at a level that permitted reliable attenuation measurement, supporting the preprocedural suspicion of calcified nodules prior to invasive confirmation. Although conventional CT has limitations in severely calcified segments, recent advances in CT hardware and reconstruction algorithms have improved the depiction of nodular calcification [10]. However, it should be emphasized that while these CT features can strongly raise suspicion, they are not yet definitive for diagnosis; rather, they serve as a valuable noninvasive screening tool to refine interventional strategies before confirmation with intravascular imaging.

Intravascular imaging remains the reference standard for diagnosing calcified nodules [6]. On OCT, these lesions typically appear as irregular, nodular calcification protruding into the lumen, with heterogeneous signal attenuation and fibrous cap disruption, sometimes accompanied by a signal-poor core. In our cases, the CT findings were concordant with these OCT features, particularly the irregular nodular contour and relatively lower, heterogeneous attenuation compared with adjacent calcification, helping distinguish these lesions from uniform, confluent calcification. In case 2, no radiolucent filling defect was evident on coronary angiography, underscoring its diagnostic limitations.

Preprocedural identification of calcified nodules influenced the interventions chosen in both cases. Dedicated debulking techniques, including rotational and orbital atherectomy, facilitated device delivery and optimized luminal expansion. Intravascular imaging confirmed adequate plaque modification, and subsequent angioplasty and/or stent deployment achieved favorable outcomes without complications. In this context, identifying calcified nodules before percutaneous coronary intervention is clinically important, because they are associated with incomplete stent expansion, edge dissection, restenosis, and target lesion failure [11,12]. Even partial prediction of calcified nodules using coronary CT may therefore help reduce procedural risk. Although coronary CT is widely used for the evaluation of coronary artery disease, it has been less frequently applied to detailed plaque morphology assessment. Our cases suggest that high-resolution CT protocols may enhance its value for preinterventional planning. However, larger prospective studies are required to confirm these findings, and broader clinical application will require standardization of image acquisition, postprocessing parameters, and diagnostic criteria for calcified nodules.

While our proposed CT markers—lower internal attenuation and protruding morphology—are suggestive of calcified nodules, several mimics and potential false positives warrant consideration. Motion artifacts from cardiac contraction and blooming artifacts associated with dense calcium can create a pseudo-nodular appearance. In addition, lipid-rich components or intraplaque hemorrhage adjacent to calcified plaque may exhibit low attenuation, mimicking the internal heterogeneity characteristic of true nodules. Accordingly, coronary CT is not intended to replace intravascular imaging but rather to serve as a valuable noninvasive screening tool to raise clinical suspicion of calcified nodules. Preprocedural identification of such "high-suspicion" lesions facilitates procedural planning by enabling the pre-emptive selection of appropriate debulking devices or lesion modification strategies, potentially improving the safety and efficiency of complex percutaneous coronary interventions.

To our knowledge, reports directly correlating CT-based attenuation heterogeneity within calcified nodules with OCT-confirmed calcified nodules remain limited. Our cases suggest that these CT features can aid in the identification of calcified nodules and directly influence procedural planning for high-risk calcified lesions.

Conclusion

These cases illustrate potential imaging correlations rather than diagnostic replacement. High-resolution coronary CT scan depict morphological features suggestive of calcified nodules, potentially reducing reliance on routine invasive confirmation. This capability may improve preprocedural planning and help guide interventional strategies during percutaneous coronary intervention, thereby supporting procedural safety and efficacy. Earlier recognition of calcified nodules on coronary CT may also contribute to better risk stratification and clinical decision-making in patients with complex coronary calcification.

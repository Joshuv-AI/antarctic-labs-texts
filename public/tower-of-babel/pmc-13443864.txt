Secure event-triggered control of nonlinear networked systems via interval type-2 fuzzy observer design
Authors: K Asmiya Banu, T Aparna, M Mubeen Tajudeen, A Muniyappan, Einas M Widaa, Ali Akgul
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper investigates the problem of observer-based event-triggered control for nonlinear networked control systems subject to deception attacks. An interval type-2 fuzzy logic was developed to improve the type-1 fuzzy form, which has a lower and upper membership function that represents the uncertain parameters and can provide control accuracy. An event-triggering scheme was developed based on the observer in the presence of deception attacks, which reduces unnecessary communications and saves limited bandwidth. An event-triggering scheme is employed to adapt the limited communication links. The Lyapunov-Krasovskii functional is used to obtain sufficient conditions to ensure asymptotic stability for interval type-2 fuzzy systems. The linear matrix inequality (LMI) technique is used to obtain the observer and controller gain matrices. Finally, numerical examples are offered to demonstrate the efficacy and applicability of the theoretical findings.Keywords: Interval type-2 fuzzy logic, Event-triggered scheme, Deception attacks, Lyapunov-Krasvoskii functionalSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Networked control systems (NCSs) topologies have been widely applied in vehicle suspensions, electrical grids, traffic control, space exploration, economic systems, industrial process control, transportation systems and so on1–5. NCSs can connect physical plants by using a communication network to connect controls, actuators, and sensors. When compared to other structures, they have several advantages, including less system wiring, easy mobility, good stability, high flexibility, diagnosis, faster maintenance, and easier installation. NCS has inspired a wave of academic interest in communication technology research in recent decades6–8. Along with the benefits, fundamental challenges in NCSs exist, such as network-induced delay, packet losses, quantization, time-varying sampling, time-delay, disorders, and bandwidth constraint, all of which affect control system performance and stability9–11. As a consequence, researchers concentrated their efforts on establishing an appropriate controller for NCS in order to limit network-induced effects12–14.

The fuzzy-model-based technique has been used for the design and systematic study of nonlinear systems as an effective tool for approximating nonlinear systems. A large number of study results have been obtained for nonlinear NCSs based on the fuzzy modeling method, particularly the Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) fuzzy one15–18. It’s interesting to note that, there exist not only nonlinearities but also conversion errors, parameter uncertainties, modeling errors, etc. Fuzzy systems can be classified into two major categories: type-1 fuzzy and type-2 fuzzy sets. The type-1 fuzzy system can successfully capture nonlinearities19; however, parameter uncertainties are ignored, reducing modeling accuracy. Because the plant has an uncertain parameter, the membership functions become uncertain, which is always true in practical applications. Zadeh introduced the concept of type-2 fuzzy sets in 1975. Based on this framework, Mendel et al. built a type-2 fuzzy set and system to handle parametric uncertainties based on this framework20. A type-2 T-S fuzzy logic was provided to extend the type-1 fuzzy form in order to evaluate and manage uncertainties in nonlinear plants21,22. Interval type-2 (IT2) and general type-2 (GT2) fuzzy logic systems are two kinds of type-2 fuzzy systems. Interval type-2 (IT2) fuzzy sets have lower and upper membership functions and are used to represent the uncertain parameters and can provide a greater degree of freedom with better modeling and control accuracy. The advantage of IT2 T-S fuzzy systems over type-1 T-S fuzzy models is suggested due to their simple design and practical application to a broad variety of fields23,24. As mentioned in25–28, a considerable amount of literature has previously studied the IT2 fuzzy logic system. Type-2 fuzzy logic systems have been widely applied to address uncertainties in complex dynamical and control systems. Recent studies have explored optimization techniques, fractional-order control, and uncertainty quantification frameworks to enhance system performance and robustness29–32. These advancements highlight the increasing importance of intelligent fuzzy-based approaches in modern engineering applications.

Wireless networks whose components are controllers, sensors, and actuators with restricted processing, sensing, and computational capabilities may impose a nontrivial cost in terms of energy in a practical control system. The first proposed transmission mechanism in communication networks is the time-triggered scheme inserted into NCSs, which results in poor use of limited computation and network resources33–36. Event-triggered control (ETC) approach by reducing unnecessary data transfer, especially when the controller and the system are connected via a network with limited bandwidth37,38. Many ETC-based results have recently been established for NCSs39–42. Numerous different event-triggered controls have been used for multi-agent systems43, networked control systems44, and single continuous-time systems to save communication resources for computation and network resources, making event-triggered control (ETC) a promising solution for NCSs with limited resources45.

With the rapid development of network technology, network security issues have attracted high attention in the field of NCSs. Network infrastructures and devices are possible to be corrupted by prospective cyber-attacks. Network transmission factors, including cyber-attacks, have recently become a major concern in NCSs. When it comes to system security, cyber-attacks are one of the worst offenders, with the objective of decreasing the stability of networked systems and reducing system performance. Typical cyber-attacks include replay attacks46, denial-of-service (DoS) attacks47, deception attacks48, and false data injection (FDI) attacks49. The transmitted signals can be reconstructed as malicious attack signals by deception attacks50. Due to the considerable influence of cyber-attacks remarkable achievements in51–57.

Conclusion

In this study, the topic of observer-based event-triggered interval type-2 fuzzy control for a networked control system with deception attacks is discussed. Due to the network framework, an observer-based event-triggered scheme was used to reduce communication burdens, mitigate the impact of deception attacks, and preserve bandwidth. Compared with the existing methods, type-2 fuzzy control method results show faster and more effective responses. The Lyapunov-Krasvoskii functional is then utilized to develop sufficient conditions to ensure that the system is asymptotically stable. The controller gain and observer have been derived by solving a linear matrix inequality. Finally, two numerical example is given to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methodology.

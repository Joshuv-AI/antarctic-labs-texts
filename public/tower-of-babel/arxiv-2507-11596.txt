S. R. Mane

Abstract
The Fibonacci polynomials are a generalization of the Fibonacci numbers. The k k -generalized Fibonacci polynomials are a further generalization to k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 summands, which satisfy the recurrence ℱ n , k ​ ( x ) = x k − 1 ​ ℱ n − 1 , k ​ ( x ) + x k − 2 ​ ℱ n − 2 , k ​ ( x ) + ⋯ + ℱ n − k , k ​ ( x ) \mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)=x^{k-1}\mathcal{F}_{n-1,k}(x)+x^{k-2}\mathcal{F}_{n-2,k}(x)+\dots+\mathcal{F}_{n-k,k}(x) . Most of the attention in the literature iterates the above recurrence upwards to positive values of n n . We show that, when the recurrence is iterated downwards to negative values of n n , there are indices where the polynomials vanish identically . This fact does not seem to have been noted in the literature. There are k ⁡ ( k − 1 ) / 2 k(k-1)/2 such indices. We enumerate and prove the complete list of such indices. We also employ generating functions to derive combinatorial sums for the k k -generalized Fibonacci polynomials, both for positive and negative n n . (Separate generating functions and combinatorial sums are required for positive and negative n n .) Lastly, we show that, unlike the case for positive n n , the degree of a k k -generalized Fibonacci polynomial (where k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 ) does not increase monotonically with | n | |n| for n < 0 n<0 . We conjecture a formula for the degree of the polynomial. It has been verified numerically for all values k = 2 , … , 100 k=2,\dots,100 and 1 ≤ | n | ≤ 1000 1\leq|n|\leq 1000 .

Introduction

The Fibonacci polynomials are a family of polynomials
F_{n}(x)
which satisfy the recurrence

F_{n}(x)=xF_{n-1}(x)+F_{n-2}(x)\,.

(1.1)

The initial values are
F_{0}(x)=0
and
F_{1}(x)=1
.
The Fibonacci polynomials are a generalization of the Fibonacci numbers.
We define the
k
-generalized Fibonacci polynomials employing the definition by Hoggatt and Bicknell
[
1
]

(who called them “R-Bonacci” numbers).
They are a family of polynomials
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)
, where
k\geq 2
, which satisfy the recurrence

\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)=x^{k-1}\mathcal{F}_{n-1,k}(x)+x^{k-2}\mathcal{F}_{n-2,k}(x)+\dots+\mathcal{F}_{n-k,k}(x)\,.

(1.2)

The initial values are
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)=1
for
n=1
and
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)=0
for
n=0,-1,\dots,-(k-2)
.
Note that are other generalizations of the Fibonacci polynomials, see e.g.
[
2
,
3
]
.
Arolkar defined the
B
-Tribonacci polynomials as follows (
[
4
]
, eq. (1))

t_{n+2}=x^{2}t_{n+1}+2xt_{n}+t_{n-1}
.
We do not treat such alternative definitions here.
Most authors iterate the recurrence eq. (
1.2
)
upwards to positive values of
n
, see e.g.
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
All the coefficients in the recurrence are positive, hence
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)
is not identically zero for
n\geq 1
.
It is then easily seen that for
n\geq 1
,
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)
is a polynomial in
x
of degree
(n-1)(k-1)
.
We begin with
\mathcal{F}_{1}(x)=1
, which is a polynomial of degree zero,
and each upward iteration in
n
adds
k-1
to the degree of the polynomial
because
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)=x^{k-1}\mathcal{F}_{n-1,k}(x)+\dots
.
For example
\mathcal{F}_{2,k}(x)=x^{k-1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{3,k}(x)=x^{2(k-1)}+\dots
.

However, little attention seems to have been paid if we iterate eq. (
1.2
)
downwards
to negative values of
n
.
We shall show that, for certain (negative) indices
n
, the
k
-generalized Fibonacci polynomials
vanish identically
.
This fact does not seem to have been noted in the literature.
There are
k(k-1)/2
such indices.
We enumerate and prove the complete list of such indices below.
We also employ generating functions to derive combinatorial sums for the
k
-generalized Fibonacci polynomials,
both for positive and negative
n
.
(Separate generating functions and combinatorial sums are required for positive and negative
n
.)
Lastly, we show that, unlike the case for positive
n
, the degree of a
k
-generalized Fibonacci polynomial (where
k\geq 3
)
does
not
increase monotonically with
|n|
for
n<0
.
We conjecture a formula for the degree of the polynomial.
It has been verified numerically for all values
k=2,\dots,100
and
1\leq|n|\leq 1000
.

2
Identically vanishing polynomials

For
k=2
, the recurrence for the Fibonacci polynomials
F_{n}(x)
is

F_{n}(x)=xF_{n-1}(x)+F_{n-2}(x)\,.

(2.1)

To iterate downwards to negative values of
n
, express this as

F_{n}(x)=-xF_{n+1}(x)+F_{n+2}(x)\,.

(2.2)

Let
x\leftarrow-x
and this is basically the same recurrence as the original.
Given the initial conditions
F_{0}(x)=0
and
F_{1}(x)=1
, it is easily seen that

F_{-n}(x)=F_{n}(-x)=(-1)^{n-1}F_{n}(x)\,.

(2.3)

However this is
not
the case for
k\geq 3
.
The recurrence for the Tribonacci polynomials
T_{n}(x)
is
[
1
]

T_{n}(x)=x^{2}T_{n-1}(x)+xT_{n-2}(x)+T_{n-3}(x)\,.

(2.4)

Flipping
x\leftarrow-x
is not helpful because the coefficient
x^{2}
does not change sign.
The initial values are
T_{1}(x)=1
and
T_{0}(x)=T_{-1}(x)=0
.
Set
n=-1,-2,\dots
to obtain, displaying only nonzero summands,

\begin{array}[]{lllll}T_{-2}(x)&=T_{1}(x)&=1\phantom{\Bigl|}\,,\\
T_{-3}(x)&=-xT_{-2}(x)&=-x\phantom{\Bigl|}\,,\\
T_{-4}(x)&=-xT_{-3}(x)-x^{2}T_{-2}(x)&=x^{2}-x^{2}&=0\,.\end{array}

(2.5)

The recurrence for the Quadranacci polynomials
Q_{n}(x)
is
[
1
]

Q_{n}(x)=x^{3}Q_{n-1}(x)+x^{2}Q_{n-2}(x)+xQ_{n-3}(x)+Q_{n-4}(x)\,.

(2.6)

The initial values are
Q_{1}(x)=1
and
Q_{0}(x)=Q_{-1}(x)=Q_{-2}(x)=0
.
Set
n=-3,-4,\dots
to obtain, displaying only nonzero summands,

\begin{array}[]{lllll}Q_{-3}(x)&=Q_{1}(x)&=1\phantom{\Bigl|}\,,\\
Q_{-4}(x)&=-xQ_{-3}(x)&=-x\phantom{\Bigl|}\,,\\
Q_{-5}(x)&=-xQ_{-4}(x)-x^{2}T_{-3}(x)&=x^{2}-x^{2}&=0\,.\end{array}

(2.7)

The Pentanacci polynomials
P_{n}(x)
display a similar pattern.
The initial values are
P_{1}(x)=1
and
P_{0}(x)=P_{-1}(x)=P_{-2}(x)=P_{-3}(x)=0
.
We obtain
P_{-4}(x)=1
,
P_{-5}(x)=-x
and
P_{-6}(x)=0
.
The pattern is the same for all the higher generalized Fibonacci polynomials:
the first two are respectively
1
and
-x
and the third is identically zero.
There are also other (negative) indices where the polynomials vanish identically.
Table
1
tabulates the Tribonacci, Quadranacci and Pentanacci polynomials for
n=0,-1,\dots,-19
.
Blanks in the table indicate indices where the polynomials vanish identically.
The patten is as follows, for all
k
.

1.

There are
k-1
blocks of indices where the polynomials vanish identically.

2.

The first block is the initial conditions.
Starting at
n=0
and counting downwards through
n=-(k-2),
this is a block of
k-1
consecutive indices.
(For
k=2
this is the only block where a polynomial vanishes identically, viz.
F_{0}(x)=0
.)

3.

Starting at
n=-(k+1)
and counting down there is a block of length
k-2
consecutive indices.

4.

Starting at
n=-2(k+1)
and counting down there is a block of length
k-3
consecutive indices.

5.

Proceeding in this way, the last block is at
n=-(k-2)(k+1)
and has length
1
.

6.

The total number of such indices is
1+2+\dots+(k-1)=k(k-1)/2
.

7.

Table
1
shows that there are respectively
3
,
6
and
10
vanishing polynomials for
k=3,4,5
, respectively.

3
Generating functions

We prove the above findings using generating functions.
From eq. (
1.2
) and the fact that
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)=1
for
n=1
, the generating function is

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(z)&=\frac{z}{1-x^{k-1}z-x^{k-2}z^{2}-\dots xz^{k-1}-z^{k}}\\
&=z\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}z^{j}(x^{k-1}+x^{k-2}z+\dots+xz^{k-2}+z^{k-1})^{j}\end{split}

(3.1)

All the coefficients are positive, hence there is no cancellation of terms.
The term in
z^{j}
means there is a nonzero term in
z^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.
Hence
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}
is never identically zero for any
n\geq 1
.
For negative indices, set
w=1/z
and expand in powers of
w
.
The generating function
\mathcal{G}_{\rm neg}(w)
(for “negative”) is (the overall minus sign is simply to make the leading term have a positive sign)

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{\rm neg}(w)&=-\frac{1/w}{1-x^{k-1}/w-x^{k-2}/x^{2}-\dots-x/w^{k-1}-1/w^{k}}\\
&=\frac{w^{k-1}}{-w^{k}+x^{k-1}w^{k-1}+x^{k-2}w^{k-2}+\dots+xw+1}\\
&=\frac{w^{k-1}}{(x^{k}w^{k}-1)/(xw-1)-w^{k}}\\
&=\frac{w^{k-1}(1-xw)}{1-(1+x^{k})w^{k}+xw^{k+1}}\\
&=w^{k-1}(1-xw)\\
&\qquad+w^{k-1}(1-xw)((1+x^{k})w^{k}-xw^{k+1})\\
&\qquad+w^{k-1}(1-xw)((1+x^{k})w^{k}-xw^{k+1})^{2}+\dots\\
&=w^{k-1}-xw^{k}\\
&\qquad+(1+x^{k})w^{2k-1}-(2x+x^{k+1})w^{2k}+x^{2}w^{2k+1}\\
&\qquad+w^{k-1}(1-xw)((1+x^{k})^{2}w^{2k}+\dots)+\dots\end{split}

(3.2)

Note the following:

1.

The initial values are
\mathcal{F}_{-(k-2)}(x)=\dots=\mathcal{F}_{0}(x)=0
.
This is a block of
k-1
consecutive indices.

2.

The two lowest nonzero terms are
w^{k-1}
and
-xw^{k}
, hence
\mathcal{F}_{-(k-1)}(x)=1
and
\mathcal{F}_{-k}(x)=-x
.
This was observed in Table
1
.

3.

For
k\geq 3
, there is a gap in the powers of
w
from
k+1
through
2k-2
.

Hence all the polynomials
\mathcal{F}_{-n}(x)
vanish identically for
n=k+1,\dots,2k-2
. This is a block of length
k-2
.

4.

The next nonvanishing term is in
w^{2k-1}
, hence
\mathcal{F}_{-(2k-1)}=1+x^{k}
.

5.

The following term is in
w^{2k}
, hence
\mathcal{F}_{-2k}=-x(1+x^{k})-x=-2x-x^{k+1}
.

6.

The following terms are in
w^{2k+1}
and
w^{3k-1}
. If
k=2
the exponents are equal, else
3k-1>2k+1
.

7.

Hence for
k\geq 3
, the term is given by
w^{2k+1}
alone, hence
\mathcal{F}_{-2k-1}=x^{2}
.

8.

Also for
k\geq 3
, the next nonvanishing term is in
w^{3k-1}
, hence
\mathcal{F}_{-(3k-1)}=(1+x^{k})^{2}
.

9.

By the same reasoning as above, there is a gap in the powers of
w
from
2k+2
through
3k-2
.
All the polynomials
\mathcal{F}_{-n}(x)
vanish identically for
n=2k+2,\dots,3k-2
. This is a block of length
k-3
.

10.

Proceeding in this way, one sees that a block of vanishing polynomials lies in the interval

n=sk+s,\dots,(s+1)k-2
. This is a block of length
k-s-1
.
The last block has length
1
, hence
s=0,\dots,k-2
.

11.

The total number of identically vanishing polynomials is
(k-1)+(k-2)+\dots+1=k(k-1)/2
.

12.

The first nonvanishing polynomial (after a previous vanishing block ends) is
\mathcal{F}_{1-sk}=(1+x^{k})^{s-1}
, for
s=1,\dots,k-2
.

13.

The last nonvanishing polynomial (before the next vanishing block begins) is
\mathcal{F}_{1-s(k+1)}=(-1)^{s}x^{s}
, for
s=0,\dots,k-2
.

4
Combinatorial sums for positive indices

4.1
Multinomial sum

Hoggatt and Bicknell
[
1
]
derived a combinatorial sum for the
k
-generalized Fibonacci polynomials in terms of multinomial sums.
See also Koshy
[
5
]
.
Both sets of authors treated only positive values of
n
.
We employ the generating functions in eqs. (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)
to derive a combinatorial sum for the
k
-generalized Fibonacci polynomials.
Here we treat begin with positive
n
.
We treat negative
n
in Sec.
5
.
We process the generating function in eq. (
3.1
) as follows.

\begin{split}G(z)&=\frac{z}{1-x^{k-1}z-x^{k-2}z^{2}-\dots xz^{k-1}-z^{k}}\\
&=z\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}(zx^{k-1}+x^{k-2}z^{2}+\dots xz^{k-1}+z^{k})^{s}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\dots+j_{k}=s}\frac{s!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k}!}\,z^{1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}ij_{i}}\,x^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}j_{i}(k-i)}\qquad(j_{i}\geq 0,\;i=1,\dots,k)\,.\end{split}

(4.1)

Then
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)
equals the coefficient of
z^{n}
.
Set
n=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}ij_{i}
, and for brevity define
J=\sum_{i=1}^{k}j_{i}
. Then

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)&=\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+kj_{k}=n-1}\frac{(j_{1}+j_{2}+\dots+j_{k})!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k}!}\,x^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}j_{i}(k-i)}\\
&=\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+kj_{k}=n-1}\frac{J!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k}!}\,x^{1-n+kJ}\qquad(j_{i}\geq 0,\;i=1,\dots,k)\,.\end{split}

(4.2)

4.2
Binomial coefficients only

Next, we derive a sum using only binomial coefficients.
We process
\mathcal{G}(z)
as follows.

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(z)&=\frac{z}{1-x^{k-1}z-x^{k-2}z^{2}-\dots xz^{k-1}-z^{k}}\\
&=\frac{z}{1-z(x^{k}-z^{k})/(x-z)}\\
&=\frac{1}{x}\frac{z(x-z)}{1-(1+x^{k}-z^{k})z/x}\end{split}

(4.3)

Define a temporary variable
\mathcal{T}=1/(1-(1+x^{k}-z^{k})z/x)
and expand in powers of
z
.

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}&=\frac{1}{1-(1+x^{k}-z^{k})z/x}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{s}}{x^{s}}(1+x^{k}-z^{k})^{s}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{s}}{x^{s}}\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{j}\binom{s}{j}(1+x^{k})^{s-j}z^{kj}\end{split}

(4.4)

Then
\mathcal{G}(z)
is a sum of two series:

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(z)&=z\mathcal{T}-\frac{z^{2}}{x}\mathcal{T}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{s+1}}{x^{s}}\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{j}\binom{s}{j}(1+x^{k})^{s-j}z^{kj}\\
&\quad-\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{s+2}}{x^{s+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{j}\binom{s}{j}(1+x^{k})^{s-j}z^{kj}\end{split}

(4.5)

The polynomial
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)
is the coefficient of
z^{n}
.
Then
n=s+1+kj
in the first sum, i.e.
s=n-1-kj
and
n=s+2+kj
in the second sum, i.e.
s=n-2-kj
.
Hence

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)&=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/(k+1)\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1-kj}{j}\frac{(1+x^{k})^{n-1-(k+1)j}}{x^{n-1-kj}}\\
&\quad-\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n-2)/(k+1)\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-2-kj}{j}\frac{(1+x^{k})^{n-2-(k+1)j}}{x^{n-1-kj}}\\
&=\frac{(1+x^{k})^{n-1}}{x^{n-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/(k+1)\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1-kj}{j}\frac{x^{kj}}{(1+x^{k})^{(k+1)j}}\\
&\quad-\frac{(1+x^{k})^{n-2}}{x^{n-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n-2)/(k+1)\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-2-kj}{j}\frac{x^{kj}}{(1+x^{k})^{(k+1)j}}\end{split}

(4.6)

The above expression is computationally efficient because it has no nested sums or multinomial sums.
However, it has the unfortunate feature that powers of
x
appear in the denominator.
Eq. (
4.6
)
has been tested numerically and it works, but is not computationally accurate for small
|x|\ll 1
.
The multinomial sum in eq. (
4.2
) is computationally inefficient
but powers of
x
appear only in the numerator: the sum is manifestly a polynomial in
x
.

5
Combinatorial sums for negative indices

5.1
Multinomial sum

Here we derive combinatorial sums for negative
n
.
We process the generating function in eq. (
3.2
) as follows.

\begin{split}G_{\rm neg}(w)&=\frac{w^{k-1}}{1+xw+\dots+x^{k-1}w^{k-1}-w^{k}}\\
&=w^{k-1}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{s}(xw+\dots+x^{k-1}w^{k-1}-w^{k})^{s}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\dots+j_{k}=s}(-1)^{s-j_{k}}\frac{s!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k}!}\,w^{k-1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}ij_{i}}\,x^{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}ij_{i}}\qquad(j_{i}\geq 0,\;i=1,\dots,k)\,.\end{split}

(5.1)

Then
\mathcal{F}_{-n,k}(x)
equals the coefficient of
w^{n}
.
Set
n=k-1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}ij_{i}
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}ij_{i}=n+1-k(1+j_{k})
.
Again for brevity define
J=\sum_{i=1}^{k}j_{i}
. Then

\mathcal{F}_{-n,k}(x)=\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+kj_{k}=n+1-k}(-1)^{J-j_{k}}\frac{J!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k}!}\,x^{n+1-k(1+j_{k})}\qquad(j_{i}\geq 0,\;i=1,\dots,k)\,.

(5.2)

5.2
Binomial coefficients only

Next, we derive a sum using only binomial coefficients.
Recall from eq. (
3.2
) that

\mathcal{G}_{\rm neg}(w)=\frac{w^{k-1}(1-xw)}{1-(1+x^{k})w^{k}+xw^{k+1}}\,.

(5.3)

Define a temporary variable
\mathcal{T}_{\rm neg}=1/(1-(1+x^{k})w^{k}+xw^{k+1})
and expand in powers of
w
.

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{\rm neg}&=\frac{1}{1-(1+x^{k}-xw)w^{k}}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}w^{ks}(1+x^{k}-xw)^{s}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}w^{ks}\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{j}\binom{s}{j}(1+x^{k})^{s-j}x^{j}w^{j}\end{split}

(5.4)

Then
\mathcal{G}_{\rm neg}(w)
is a sum of two series:

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{\rm neg}(w)&=w^{k-1}\mathcal{T}_{\rm neg}-xw^{k}\mathcal{T}_{\rm neg}\\
&=w^{k-1}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}w^{ks}\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{j}\binom{s}{j}(1+x^{k})^{s-j}x^{j}w^{j}\\
&\quad-xw^{k}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}w^{ks}\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{j}\binom{s}{j}(1+x^{k})^{s-j}x^{j}w^{j}\end{split}

(5.5)

The polynomial
\mathcal{F}_{-n}(x)
is the coefficient of
w^{n}
.
Then
n=ks+k-1+j
in the first sum, i.e.
j=n-(s+1)k+1
and
n=ks+k+j
in the second sum, i.e.
j=n-(s+1)k
.
Hence

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{-n}(x)&=\sum_{s=\lfloor(n-k+1)/(k+1)\rfloor}^{\lfloor(n-k+1)/k\rfloor}(-1)^{n-(s+1)k+1}\binom{s}{n-(s+1)k+1}(1+x^{k})^{s-n+(s+1)k-1}x^{n-(s+1)k+1}\\
&\quad+\sum_{s=\lfloor(n-k)/(k+1)\rfloor}^{\lfloor(n-k)/k\rfloor}(-1)^{n-(s+1)k+1}\binom{s}{n-(s+1)k}(1+x^{k})^{s-n+(s+1)k}x^{n-(s+1)k+1}\end{split}

(5.6)

The above expression is computationally efficient because it has no nested sums or multinomial sums.
Eq. (
5.6
) has been tested numerically and it works.
For odd
k
, it is not computationally accurate if
|1+x^{k}|\ll 1
, i.e.
x\simeq-1
.
The multinomial sum in eq. (
5.2
) is computationally inefficient
but powers of
x
appear only in the numerator: the sum is manifestly a polynomial in
x
.

\mathcal{F}_{n,k}(x)
vanish identically for
n=0,-1,\dots,-(k-2)

k\geq 2

and

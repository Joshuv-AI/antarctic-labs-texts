Triple-drug therapy with Goreisan, tranexamic acid and carbazochrome sodium sulfonate hydrate to prevent recurrence after chronic subdural haematoma surgery: a multicentre, randomised, controlled trial protocol
Authors: Haruka Negishi, Koji Hirata, Hitoshi Aiyama, Keishi Fujita, Yoji Komatsu, Noriyuki Kato, Yasushi Shibata, Kyoji Tsuda, Tomosato Yamazaki, Masayuki Sato, Shinya Watanabe, Narushi Sugii, Takahiro Konishi, Bryan J Mathis, Tomohiro Ohigashi, Mitsuhiko Endo, Eiichi Ishikawa, Yuji Matsumaru, The Triple Study Investigators

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionChronic subdural haematoma (CSDH) is a common neurosurgical condition in older adults, with a recurrence rate of approximately 7.1–13% after burr-hole drainage. Although surgical adjuncts such as subdural drains and middle meningeal artery embolisation may reduce recurrence, these are not suitable for all patients. Pharmacological strategies, including tranexamic acid, Goreisan and carbazochrome sodium sulfonate hydrate, have shown potential, but high-level evidence remains lacking. A prior retrospective study suggested that a triple oral regimen combining these agents may reduce recurrence. This randomised controlled trial aims to evaluate its efficacy and safety.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, multicentre, open-label, randomised controlled trial conducted across six hospitals in Ibaraki, Japan. A total of 180 patients undergoing first-time burr-hole surgery for CSDH will be randomised 1:1 to receive either triple therapy (Goreisan 7.5 g/day, carbazochrome sodium sulfonate hydrate 90 mg/day and tranexamic acid 750 mg/day for up to 90 days) or standard postoperative care. The primary outcome is recurrence requiring reoperation within 90 days. Secondary outcomes include time to recurrence and haematoma volume reduction on serial CT imaging. All analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle, using logistic regression, Cox proportional hazards models and mixed-effects models.Ethics and disseminationWritten, informed consent will be obtained from all participants at each participating hospital by trained staff from that hospital. The trial protocol has been approved by the ethics committee of the University of Tsukuba Hospital (approval no. TCRB23-025) and the Institutional Review Boards of all participating centres. Study findings will be disseminated through presentations at scientific conferences and publications in peer-reviewed journals. A summary of the results will also be provided to participating institutions and made publicly available in accordance with the BMJ Open data sharing policy.Trial registration numberjRCTs031240007.

Introduction

Background and rationale

Chronic subdural haematoma (CSDH) is becoming increasingly common, particularly in the elderly, and is projected to become the most frequently performed cranial neurosurgical procedure in ageing societies1 due to an ageing population and increasing usage of antithrombotic medication.2 The standard treatment for symptomatic CSDH is burr-hole drainage3 4; however, recurrence occurs in approximately 12.8% of cases,5 posing a major clinical challenge. Numerous studies have explored methods to reduce recurrence rates, including the placement of subdural drains,6 optimisation of drainage duration7 and minimisation of postoperative pneumocephalus.6 More recently, middle meningeal artery (MMA) embolisation has emerged as a promising adjunctive strategy for both treatment and prevention of CSDH recurrence.710 However, these surgical strategies may not be optimal in select populations.

Pharmacological interventions, such as corticosteroids,11 atorvastatin,12 tranexamic acid and Goreisan (a Chinese traditional medicine compound),13 14 have also been investigated, but effectiveness remains uncertain. We previously conducted a retrospective study suggesting that a triple oral regimen of Goreisan, carbazochrome sodium sulfonate hydrate and tranexamic acid may reduce postoperative recurrence.13 Given the limitations of retrospective analyses, a robust randomised controlled trial (RCT) is needed to provide higher-level evidence. This study, therefore, aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of this triple therapy in a multicentre, randomised, controlled, open-label trial.

Objectives

Objective

To determine the efficacy and safety of Goreisan, carbazochrome sodium sulfonate hydrate and tranexamic acid administered after CSDH to prevent recurrence.

Necessity

Recurrence in 12.8% of CSDH cases is costly in reoperation as well as pain, recovery and quality-of-life reductions for patients. Data from this trial are expected to contribute towards useful prophylaxis against CSDH recurrence in clinical settings.

Trial design

This is a prospective, multicentre, open-label, randomised, parallel-group controlled trial to be conducted at six medical institutions in Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan. The trial aims to compare the efficacy of triple oral pharmacotherapy with standard postoperative care in preventing recurrence following burr-hole evacuation of CSDH.

Methods: participants, interventions and outcomes

Study setting

The trial will take place at the University of Tsukuba Hospital, Hitachi General Hospital, Ibaraki Seinan Medical Center Hospital, Tsukuba Memorial Hospital, Mito Kyodo General Hospital and Mito Medical Center, which are all secondary-care or tertiary-care facilities in Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan. The trial is scheduled to run from 1 May 2024 to 31 March 2028.

Ethics and dissemination

Written, informed consent will be obtained from all participants at each participating hospital by trained staff from that hospital. The trial protocol has been approved by the ethics committee of the University of Tsukuba Hospital (approval no. TCRB23-025) and the Institutional Review Boards of all participating centres. Study findings will be disseminated through presentations at scientific conferences and publications in peer-reviewed journals. A summary of the results will also be provided to participating institutions and made publicly available in accordance with the BMJ Open data sharing policy. All procedures are conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Clinical Research Act of Japan.

Who will take informed consent

University of Tsukuba trained medical staff and listed researchers approved by the Clinical Research Review Board of the Tsukuba Clinical Research and Development Organization (T-CReDO) will take informed consent. Written, informed consent will be obtained from all participants at each hospital by trained staff from that hospital (online supplemental file 1).

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens

For participants enrolled at the University of Tsukuba, peripheral blood specimens will be collected at prespecified study time points and stored at −80°C for exploratory research. Planned analyses include RNA sequencing and ancillary biomarker investigations to elucidate molecular mechanisms underlying recurrence of CSDH and differential responses to the study interventions.

At present, no secondary use beyond the exploratory analyses specified in this protocol is anticipated. Potential future use for related investigations may be considered in accordance with participant informed consent and applicable regulations. All specimens will be de-identified prior to storage, maintained under secure conditions and accessible only to authorised investigators at the University of Tsukuba. Any use extending beyond the current protocol will require prior ethics approval.

Sample size (14)

Based on preliminary retrospective data indicating a recurrence rate of 15%7 15 16 in the control group versus 2.5%13 in the intervention group, a total of 174 participants are required to achieve 80% power at a two-sided α level of 0.05 for Fisher’s exact test. To account for potential dropout or loss to follow-up, the target enrolment is set at 180 patients.

Recruitment (15)

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation

Participants are to be randomly assigned to the treatment (oral therapy) or control (non-oral therapy) groups using a computer-generated allocation sequence, implemented through an electronic data capture (EDC) system. Randomisation is to be stratified by the presence or absence of bilateral lesions.

Concealment mechanism

Eligible patients will be randomised 1:1 using an EDC system (Alliance Clinical Research Supporting System (ACReSS)), stratified by the presence of bilateral CSDH. Due to the nature of the intervention, the study will be open-label; however, radiological assessments of haematoma volume and recurrence will be performed by blinded, independent reviewers.

Implementation

After registration, the trial physician will use ACReSS to assign patients and issue prescriptions as necessary.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded

This is an open-label trial. Outcome assessors for imaging endpoints are blinded to treatment allocation; participants and treating clinicians are not blinded. A blinded radiologist from another institution will perform the imaging evaluation and determine whether non-recurrence or recurrence is appropriate.

Procedure for unblinding if needed

None.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes

Three statistical data sets will be used in the analyses: (a) a full analysis set (FAS), including all subjects without major protocol violations who underwent assessment of the primary endpoint after randomisation and took the study medication at least once in the intervention group; (b) a per-protocol set (PPS), including all subjects without violations of the eligibility criteria (selection and exclusion criteria) and who took the study medication for more than half of the planned period in the intervention group; and (c) a safety analysis set, including all subjects who were randomised. The efficacy analysis will be performed with the FAS and PPS. The safety analysis will be performed with the safety analysis set.

The primary outcome will be analysed with a logistic regression model, including the group and the presence or absence of bilateral lesions. Among the secondary outcomes, time-to-event variables will be analysed with Cox proportional hazards models, including the group and the presence or absence of bilateral lesions. Among the secondary outcomes, longitudinal variables will be analysed with a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) analysis to detect changes in haematoma volume assessed by CT from baseline to 90 days. The MMRM analysis model will include the fixed effects of group, time point, group × time interaction, baseline measurements and the presence or absence of bilateral lesions. All statistical analyses will be conducted using SAS (version 9.4) or R software. Results will be deemed significant at p<0.05, and statistical tests will be conducted for two-tailed hypotheses.

Although patients were not directly involved in the design stage, their perspectives were indirectly incorporated through prior feasibility studies and routine clinical feedback.

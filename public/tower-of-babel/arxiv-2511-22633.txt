Influence of 13 C on proton-induced 13 N production from natural carbon target
Tomasz Matulewicz and Izabela Skwira-Chalot

Abstract
Two recent measurements of 12 C(p, γ \gamma ) 13 N reaction on natural carbon were performed by detecting β + \beta^{+} decay of 13 N residue. It is argued that the measurements at energies above the 13 C(p,n) 13 N reaction threshold of 3.24 MeV proton kinetic energy can be interpreted as a consequence of 1.06% admixture of 13 C isotope in natural carbon, as the cross section for 13 N production raised by 3 orders of magnitude with respect to the measurements of 12 C(p, γ \gamma ) 13 N at lower energies.

3
Conclusions

The measurement of the
12
C(p,
\gamma
)
13
N reaction in the proton energy range between 2 MeV and 22 MeV, performed by Rodríguez-González et al.
[
10
]
, can be interpreted as the production of
13
N on the 1.06% abundant
13
C isotope.
The reported cross section, when corrected for low abundance of
13
C isotope, agrees roughly with the dedicated measurements of
13
C(p,n)
13
N reaction on isotopically enriched targets.

The importance of the cross section of proton-induced reactions on carbon, nitrogen and oxygen targets calls for an intensified experimental activity, as these nuclei are abundant in tissue and proton-therapy procedures cause nuclear reactions on these elements
(e.g. reactions on oxygen
[
7
]
).
This activity should cover not only the most abundant light isotopes of these elements, but also all heavier ones, as it is the topic of current astrophysical research
[
2
]
.
The recent progress in experimental capabilities, in particular radiation detectors of energy resolution superior to those used in the past, should be exploited.
The results might influence as well the precision of predicted radiation in proton therapy centers, an issue valid for patients and personnel
[
11
]
.

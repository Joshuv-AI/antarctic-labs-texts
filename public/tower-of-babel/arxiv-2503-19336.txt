Circular Photon Drag Effect in Dirac electrons by Quantum Geometry
Guanxiong Qu

Abstract
Quantum geometry is a well-established framework for understanding transport and optical responses in quantum materials. In this work, I study the photon drag effect in Dirac electrons using the quantum geometric interpretation of non-vertical optical transitions. Due to the particle-hole symmetry inherent in Dirac electrons, the shift photon-drag photocurrent is dominated by dissipationless Fermi surface contributions, connected to the dipole of quantum metric tensor. I find that this dipole is significantly enhanced by a small band gap in massive Dirac electrons and remains robust in the massless limit. I demonstrate the existence of a circular shift photon-drag current in the effective Hamiltonian at the L L -point of bismuth, where the bands exhibit trivial topology, highlighting the ubiquity of the circular photon-drag effect in centrosymmetric materials.

Shift current of photon drag effect.
—The shift current of the PDE in the Dirac Hamiltonian can also be expressed in a compact form:

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\alpha\beta}_{\text{shift}}=\frac{e^{3}}{4\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\omega^{2}_{cv}d^{\tau}_{f}

Dipole of quantum metric tensor.

Conclusion.
—I have shown that the PDE can be described within a unified framework of quantum geometry of optical transitions. In Dirac electrons, the non-vertical optical transitions of the PDE are predominantly located at Fermi surface due to the presence of particle-hole symmetry. Notably, in such
\mathcal{P}
-symmetric systems, the shift current does not vanish because both the antisymmetric quantum metric connection
A_{\beta\gamma\alpha}
and group velocity are odd with respect to
\mathcal{P}
-inversion, where the dipolar distribution of quantum metric connection is connected with the quantum geometric tensor dipole. The ubiquity of quantum geometric tensor dipole suggests the universality of circular PDE even in bands with trivial topology.

Generic Hamiltonian with Minimal Coupling in Velocity-gauge

I consider a generic Hamiltonian with an electric field applied using the velocity-gauge

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\sum_{nm,\bm{k}}\hbar\omega_{\bm{k},n}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k},n}c_{\bm{k},n}-eA^{\alpha}(\bm{q},\omega)c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k},n}\hat{v}^{\alpha}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}-\bm{q},nm}c_{\bm{k}-\bm{q},m},

where the electric field
E^{\alpha}(\bm{q},\omega)=E^{\alpha}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{x}-i\omega t}=-i\omega A^{\alpha}(\bm{q},\omega)
is explicitly time and space dependent.
The reduced density matrix is defined as
Ventura et al. 2017
,

\displaystyle\rho_{mn}(t)=\text{Tr}[\hat{\rho}(t)c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k},n}c_{\bm{k},m}]=\braket{c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k},n}c_{\bm{k},m}}\equiv\braket{c^{\dagger}_{n}c_{m}},

(S-2)

where
\hat{\rho}(t)
is time-dependent density operator and the summation over the
\bm{k}
-indices is omitted. The equation of motion of the reduced density matrix is

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{mn}}{dt}+i\omega_{mn}\rho_{mn}=\frac{eE^{\alpha}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{x}-i\omega t}}{i\hbar\omega}\left[v^{\alpha}_{mp}(\bm{q})\rho_{pn}-\rho_{mp}v^{\alpha}_{pn}(\bm{q})\right],

where
v^{\alpha}_{mp}(\bm{q})\equiv\bra{n,\bm{k}}\hat{v}^{\alpha}\ket{p,\bm{k}-\bm{q}}

The reduced density matrix can be expanded in powers of the electric field as

\displaystyle\rho_{mn}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\rho^{(i)}_{mn}(t).

(S-4)

The second-order density response is given by:

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}_{mn}(-\omega_{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}E_{a}^{\alpha}E_{b}^{\beta}e^{i(\bm{q}_{a}+\bm{q}_{b})\cdot\bm{x}}}{2\hbar^{2}\omega_{a}\omega_{b}(\omega_{mn}-\omega_{c}-2i\eta)}\Bigg[\frac{f_{np}v^{\beta}_{mp}(\bm{q}_{b})v^{\alpha}_{pn}(\bm{q}_{a})}{\omega_{pn}-\omega_{a}-i\eta}-\frac{f_{pm}v^{\alpha}_{mp}(\bm{q}_{a})v^{\beta}_{pn}(\bm{q}_{b})}{\omega_{mp}-\omega_{a}-i\eta}\Bigg]+\left[(\alpha,a)\leftrightarrow(\beta,b)\right],

(S-5)

where
f_{nm}\equiv f_{n}-f_{m}
is the difference in Fermi distribution functions, and
\omega_{mn}\equiv\omega_{m}-\omega_{n}
represents the energy (frequency) difference. For the second-order density response in the dc limit, the frequency and wavevector satisfy:

\displaystyle\omega_{c}=0,\quad-\omega_{a}=\omega_{b}=\omega,\quad\bm{q}_{a}=-\bm{q}_{b}=\bm{q}.

(S-6)

Consequently, Eq. (
S-5
) simplifies to:

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}_{mn}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}E^{\alpha}_{-\omega}E^{\beta}_{\omega}}{2\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}\sum_{p}\frac{1}{\omega_{mn}-2i\eta}\Bigg[\frac{f_{np^{-}}v^{\beta}_{mp^{-}}(\bm{q})v^{\alpha}_{p^{-}n}(-\bm{q})}{\omega_{p^{-}n}+\omega-i\eta}-\frac{f_{p^{+}m}v^{\alpha}_{mp^{+}}(-\bm{q})v^{\beta}_{p^{+}n}(\bm{q})}{\omega_{mp^{+}}+\omega-i\eta}+(\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta,\bm{q}\leftrightarrow-\bm{q})\Bigg]

(S-7)

Here, I denote
\ket{p^{\pm}}\equiv\ket{p,\bm{k}\pm\bm{q}}
to explicitly show the momentum transfer of states.

A.2
Second-order Photovoltaic Effects

The second-order photovoltaic response is derived from the contraction of the reduced density matrix with the velocity matrix
Ventura et al. 2017
:

\displaystyle J^{(2),\gamma}=e\sum_{n,m,\bm{k}}v^{\gamma}_{nm}\rho^{(2)}_{mn},

(S-8)

typically divided into diagonal (injection) and off-diagonal (shift) currents:

\displaystyle J^{(2),\gamma}_{\text{inj}}=e\sum_{n,\bm{k}}v^{\gamma}_{nn}\rho^{(2)}_{nn},\quad J^{(2),\gamma}_{\text{shift}}=e\sum_{\tilde{n}\neq\tilde{m},\bm{k}}v^{\gamma}_{nm}\rho^{(2)}_{mn},

(S-9)

where
\tilde{n}
denotes the energy-degenerate subspace. Note that for degenerate bands (
\omega_{n}=\omega_{m}
), the off-diagonal matrix element of the velocity within the degenerate subspace vanishes:

\displaystyle v^{\gamma}_{nm}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\bra{n}\partial_{k^{\gamma}}H\ket{m}=v_{n}\delta_{nm}+(\omega_{m}-\omega_{n})\braket{n|\partial_{k_{\gamma}}m}.

(S-10)

A.3
Formula for Injection Current of the PDE

By symmetrizing the external photon wavevector
\bm{q}
in the density response
\rho^{(2)}_{mn}
, the injection current can be expressed as

\displaystyle J^{\gamma}_{\text{inj.}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}E^{\alpha}_{-\omega}E_{\omega}^{\beta}}{4i\eta\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}\sum_{n,m,\bm{k}}(v^{\gamma}_{n^{+}}-v^{\gamma}_{m^{-}})f_{n^{+}m^{-}}v^{\beta}_{n^{+}m^{-}}v^{\alpha}_{m^{-}n^{+}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{n^{+}m^{-}}-\omega+i\eta}-\text{c.c.}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi e^{3}E^{\alpha}_{-\omega}E_{\omega}^{\beta}}{2\eta\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}\sum_{n,m,\bm{k}}f_{n^{+}m^{-}}(v^{\gamma}_{n^{+}}-v^{\gamma}_{m^{-}})v^{\beta}_{n^{+}m^{-}}v^{\alpha}_{m^{-}n^{+}}\delta(\omega_{n^{+}m^{-}}-\omega),

(S-11)

where I define
\ket{n^{+}}=\ket{n,\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2}
and
\ket{m^{-}}=\ket{m,\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}
. The conductivity tensor for injection current [Eq. (3) in the main text] becomes:

\displaystyle\sigma^{\gamma;\alpha\beta}_{\text{inj.}}(\bm{q})=-\frac{e^{3}}{2\eta\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}\sum_{n,m,\bm{k}}f_{nm}(\bm{q})\Delta^{\gamma}_{nm}(\bm{q})v^{\beta}_{nm}(\bm{q})v^{\alpha}_{mn}(-\bm{q})\delta(\omega_{nm}(\bm{q})-\omega),

(S-12)

where I show the momentum transfer as a function of
\bm{q}
and omit the superscripts (
\pm
) for clarity.
\Delta^{\gamma}_{nm}(\bm{q})=v^{\gamma}_{n,\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2}-v^{\gamma}_{m,\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}
represents the difference in group velocity.

A.4
Formula for Shift Current of the PDE

In similar way, the shift current is expressed as

\displaystyle J^{\gamma}_{\text{shift}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}E^{\alpha}_{-\omega}E_{\omega}^{\beta}}{2\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}\sum_{\tilde{n}\neq\tilde{m},p,\bm{k}}\frac{v^{\gamma}_{nm}}{\omega_{mn}}\left[\frac{f_{np^{-}}v^{\beta}_{mp^{-}}v^{\alpha}_{p^{-}n}}{\omega_{p^{-}n}+\omega-i\eta}-\frac{f_{p^{+}m}v^{\alpha}_{p^{+}m}v^{\beta}_{p^{+}n}}{\omega_{mp^{+}}+\omega-i\eta}+(\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta,\bm{q}\leftrightarrow-\bm{q})\right].

(S-13)

Employing the identity:

\displaystyle\sum_{\tilde{n}\neq\tilde{m}}\ket{n}\frac{v^{\gamma}_{nm}}{\omega_{mn}}

\displaystyle=\ket{\partial_{\gamma}m}+\sum_{\tilde{l}=\tilde{m}}iA^{\gamma}_{lm}\ket{l}.

(S-14)

the equation reduces to

\displaystyle J^{\gamma}_{\text{shift}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}E^{\alpha}_{-\omega}E_{\omega}^{\beta}}{2\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}\sum_{n,m,\bm{k}}f_{n^{+}m^{-}}\Bigg\{\frac{\left[\partial_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{n^{+}m^{-}}-i\left(\sum\limits_{\tilde{l}=\tilde{n}}A^{\gamma}_{n^{+}l^{+}}v^{\beta}_{l^{+}m^{-}}-\sum\limits_{\tilde{l}=\tilde{m}}v^{\beta}_{n^{+}l^{-}}A^{\gamma}_{l^{-}m^{-}}\right)-w^{\gamma\beta}_{n^{+}m^{-}}\right]v^{\alpha}_{m^{-}n^{+}}}{\omega_{n^{+}m^{-}}-\omega+i\eta}

\displaystyle+\frac{v^{\beta}_{n^{+}m^{-}}\left[\partial_{\gamma}v^{\alpha}_{m^{-}n^{+}}-i\left(\sum\limits_{\tilde{l}=\tilde{m}}A^{\gamma}_{m^{-}l^{-}}v^{\alpha}_{l^{-}n^{+}}-\sum\limits_{\tilde{l}=\tilde{n}}v^{\alpha}_{m^{-}l^{+}}A^{\gamma}_{l^{+}n^{+}}\right)-w^{\gamma\alpha}_{m^{-}n^{+}}\right]}{\omega_{n^{+}m^{-}}-\omega-i\eta}\Bigg\},

(S-15)

where
w^{\gamma\beta}_{n^{+}m^{-}}=\bra{n^{+}}\partial_{\gamma}\hat{v}^{\beta}\ket{m^{-}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\bra{n^{+}}\partial_{\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\hat{H}\ket{m^{-}}
is the diamagnetic velocity, which vanishes with linear band dispersions. Neglecting the diamagnetic term, the conductivity tensor for shift current [Eq. (4) in the main text] simplifies to:

\displaystyle\sigma^{\gamma;\alpha\beta}_{\text{shift}}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}}{2\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}\sum_{n,m,\bm{k}}f_{nm}(\bm{q})\left[\frac{v^{\beta}_{nm}(\bm{q})\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\alpha}_{mn}(-\bm{q})}{\omega_{nm}(\bm{q})-\omega-i\eta}+\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{nm}(\bm{q})v^{\alpha}_{mn}(-\bm{q})}{\omega_{nm}(\bm{q})-\omega+i\eta}\right].

(S-16)

Symmetry operations in the Dirac Hamiltonian

I recall the Dirac Hamiltonian in the main text [Eq. (6)]

\displaystyle H=\Delta\gamma_{0}+i\hbar vk_{i}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}.

(S-17)

with eigenenergies and eigenstates:

\displaystyle\omega_{\tilde{c}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{k},\omega_{\tilde{v}}=-\omega_{k},

(S-18)

\displaystyle u_{c,1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}}{2\omega_{k}}}\left(-1,0,\frac{ivk_{3}}{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}},\frac{ivk_{1}-vk_{2}}{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}}\right),

\displaystyle u_{c,2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}}{2\omega_{k}}}\left(0,-1,\frac{ivk_{1}+vk_{2}}{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}},\frac{-ivk_{3}}{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}}\right),

\displaystyle u_{v,1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}}{2\omega_{k}}}\left(\frac{ivk_{3}}{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}},\frac{ivk_{1}-vk+2}{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}},-1,0\right),

\displaystyle u_{v,2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}}{2\omega_{k}}}\left(\frac{ivk_{1}+vk_{2}}{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}},\frac{-ivk_{3}}{\omega_{k}+\tilde{\Delta}},0,-1\right),

(S-19)

where
\omega_{k}=\sqrt{v^{2}k^{2}+\tilde{\Delta}^{2}}
with normalized half band gap

\tilde{c},\tilde{v}

B.1
Charge Conjugation in the Dirac Hamiltonian

The Dirac Hamiltonian respects charge conjugation (
\mathcal{C}
) symmetry, defined by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=i\gamma_{2}K,

(S-20)

where
K
is complex conjugate.
\mathcal{C}
is anti-unitary operator and transforms a conduction state
u_{c}
into a valance state
u_{v}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}u_{c,\sigma}(\bm{k})=u_{v,\bar{\sigma}}(\bm{k})(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma},

(S-21)

where
\sigma_{2}
is the Pauli matrix and
\sigma,\bar{\sigma}
denotes the spin indices.
The Hamiltonian and velocity operator transform under charge conjugation as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\hat{H}\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-\hat{H},\quad\mathcal{C}\hat{v}_{i}\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-\hat{v}_{i},

(S-22)

Due to the charge conjugation (particle-hole) symmetry, the interband matrix element of velocity and covariant derivative on velocity satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma^{+};v,\tau^{-}}

\displaystyle=\bra{u_{c,\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2)}\hat{v}^{\beta}\ket{u_{v,\tau}(\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2)}

\displaystyle=\bra{u_{c,\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2)}\mathcal{C}^{-1}\left(-\hat{v}^{\beta}\right)\mathcal{C}\ket{u_{v,\tau}(\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2)}

\displaystyle=-\left(\bra{\mathcal{C}u_{c,\sigma}(\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2)}\hat{v}^{\beta}\ket{\mathcal{C}u_{v,\tau}(\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2)}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=-\left(\bra{u_{v,\bar{\sigma}}(\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2)}\hat{v}^{\beta}\ket{u_{c,\bar{\tau}}(\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2)}\right)^{*}(i\sigma)_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma)_{\bar{\tau}\tau}

\displaystyle=-\bra{u_{c,\bar{\tau}}(\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2)}\hat{v}^{\beta}\ket{u_{v,\bar{\sigma}}(\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2)}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2}))_{\bar{\tau}\tau}

\displaystyle=-v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau},

(S-23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma^{+};v,\tau^{-}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma^{+};v,\tau^{-}}-i\left(\sum\limits_{\rho}A^{\gamma}_{c,\sigma^{+};c,\rho^{+}}v^{\beta}_{c,\rho^{+};v,\tau^{-}}-v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma^{+};v,\rho^{-}}A^{\gamma}_{v,\rho^{-};v,\tau^{-}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}-i\Big(\sum\limits_{\rho}v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\rho}_{1}^{+}}A^{\gamma}_{v,\bar{\rho}_{2}^{+};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\rho}_{1}\rho}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\rho}_{2}\rho}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}

\displaystyle-A^{\gamma}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};c,\bar{\rho}_{1}^{-}}v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\rho}_{2}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\rho}_{1}\rho}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\rho}_{2}\rho}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}\Big)

\displaystyle=-(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}\left[\partial_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}-i\left(\sum\limits_{\rho}A^{\gamma}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};c,\bar{\rho}^{-}}v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\rho}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}-v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\rho}^{+}}A^{\gamma}_{v,\bar{\rho}^{+};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau},

(S-24)

where I denote
\bar{\sigma}=-\sigma
for spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fermions. Note that the intraband (within the degenerate subspace) Berry connection follows the relation:

\displaystyle A^{\gamma}_{c,\sigma^{+};c,\tau^{+}}

\displaystyle=i\bra{u_{c,\sigma}(+)}\mathcal{C}^{-1}\partial_{\gamma}\mathcal{C}\ket{u_{c,\tau}(+)}

\displaystyle=i\left(\bra{\mathcal{C}u_{c,\sigma}(+)}\partial_{\gamma}\ket{\mathcal{C}u_{c,\tau}(+)}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=i\left(\bra{u_{v,\bar{\sigma}}(+)}\partial_{\gamma}\ket{u_{v,\bar{\tau}}(+)}\right)^{*}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}

\displaystyle=i\bra{u_{v,\bar{\tau}}(+)}\partial_{\gamma}\ket{u_{v,\bar{\sigma}}(+)}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}

\displaystyle=A^{\gamma}_{v,\bar{\tau}^{+};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}.

(S-25)

B.2
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T}
-inversion in the Dirac Hamiltonian

The spatial
(\mathcal{P})
and time
(\mathcal{T})
inversion operator in the Dirac Hamiltonian are

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}\left[\gamma_{3},\gamma_{1}\right]K,\quad\mathcal{P}=\gamma_{0},

(S-26)

The
\mathcal{T}
-operator inverts both spin and momentum of eigenstates:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}u_{c,\sigma}(\bm{k})=(-1)^{c,v}u_{c,\bar{\sigma}}(-\bm{k})(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma},

(S-27)

where
c=0,v=1
, indicating even and odd parity for conduction and valance bands, respectively.

The
\mathcal{P}
-operator only inverts the momentum:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}u_{c,\sigma}(\bm{k})=(-1)^{c,v}u_{c,\sigma}(-\bm{k}).

(S-28)

Due to the disparate parity between the conduction and valance states, the intraband and interband matrix elements of velocity and Berry connection follow different transformation rules. Under the
\mathcal{T}
-inversion, the intraband matrix elements of Berry connection (at
\bm{q}=0
) satisfy the following relation:

\displaystyle A^{\gamma}_{c,\sigma;c,\tau}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=A^{\gamma}_{c,\bar{\tau};c,\bar{\sigma}}(-\bm{k})(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}.

(S-29)

For the interband matrix elements of Berry connection and covariant derivative on Berry connection, the transformation under
\mathcal{T}
-inversion is given by:

\displaystyle A^{\gamma}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=-A^{\gamma}_{v,\bar{\tau};c,\bar{\sigma}}(-\bm{k})(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau},

(S-30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\bar{\gamma}}A^{\beta}_{v,\bar{\tau};c,\bar{\sigma}}(-\bm{k})(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\tau}\tau}(i\sigma_{2})_{\bar{\sigma}\sigma},

(S-31)

where I use identity:
\partial_{\bar{\gamma}}\equiv\partial_{k^{\bar{\gamma}}}=\partial_{-k^{\gamma}}=-\partial_{\gamma}
.

Thus, the quantum metric connection has relation

\displaystyle C^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(\bm{k})\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}(\bm{k})=-\sum_{\sigma,\tau}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(-\bm{k})\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}(-\bm{k})=-C^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{v},\tilde{c}}(-\bm{k}).

(S-32)

Under the
\mathcal{P}
-inversion, the intraband matrix elements of Berry connection obey the following relation:

\displaystyle A^{\gamma}_{c,\sigma;c,\tau}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=-A^{\gamma}_{c,\sigma;c,\tau}(-\bm{k}).

(S-33)

For the interband matrix elements of Berry connection and covariant derivative on Berry connection the relations become:

\displaystyle A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(-\bm{k}),

(S-34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}_{\bar{\gamma}}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(-\bm{k}).

(S-35)

Thus, the quantum metric connection satisfies the following relation:

\displaystyle C^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(\bm{k})\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}(\bm{k})=-\sum_{\sigma,\tau}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}(-\bm{k})\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}(-\bm{k})=-C^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}(-\bm{k}).

(S-36)

Appendix C
Photon Drag Effect in the Dirac Hamiltonian

C.1
Intraband Effect: Injection Photon Drag Effect

I first consider the injection current of the first-order PDE (
\propto q^{\tau}

\bm{q}
-relevant quantities in Eq. (
S-12

f_{nm}(\bm{q})

\delta
-function
\delta\left(\omega_{nm}(\bm{q})-\omega\right)

\Delta^{\gamma}_{nm}(\bm{q})

1. Fermi distribution expansion:

\displaystyle f_{nm}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=f_{n}(\bm{k})-f_{m}(\bm{k})+\frac{\hbar q^{\tau}}{2}\left[f^{\prime}_{n}(\bm{k})v^{\tau}_{n}(\bm{k})+f^{\prime}_{m}(\bm{k})v^{\tau}_{m}(\bm{k})\right]+\mathcal{O}(q^{2})

(S-38)

2.
\delta

\displaystyle 2\pi i\delta\left(\omega_{nm}(\bm{q})-\omega\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{nm}(\bm{q})-\omega-i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega_{nm}(\bm{q})-\omega+i\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi i\delta\left(\omega_{nm}-\omega\right)+2\pi q^{\tau}\left(v^{\tau}_{n}+v^{\tau}_{m}\right)\text{Im}\frac{1}{(\omega_{nm}-\omega-i\eta)^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}).

(S-39)

3. Group velocity expansion:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\gamma}_{nm}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\Delta^{\gamma}_{nm}+\frac{q^{\tau}}{2}\partial_{\tau}\left(v^{\gamma}_{n}+v^{\gamma}_{m}\right)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}).

(S-40)

Depending on the Fermi distribution function, I divide the
\bm{q}
-expansion of Eq. (
S-12
) into
Fermi surface
and
Fermi sea

Fermi surface

\bm{q}
-expansion of Fermi distribution [Eq. (
S-38

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\alpha\beta}_{\text{inj.}}

(S-41)

\Delta^{\gamma}_{nm}=0
for
\tilde{m}=\tilde{n}

The
Fermi sea

f_{nm}
(
\bm{q}=0

\tilde{n}\neq\tilde{m}

S-40

\delta

S-39

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\alpha\beta}_{\text{inj.}}

\displaystyle v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma^{+};v,\tau^{-}}v^{\alpha}_{v,\tau^{-};c,\sigma^{+}}

\displaystyle=v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}v^{\alpha}_{v,\bar{\sigma}^{+};c,\bar{\tau}^{-}}

\displaystyle\partial_{q^{\tau}}\left(v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}v^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{q^{\tau}}\left(v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau};v,\bar{\sigma}}v^{\alpha}_{v,\bar{\sigma};c,\bar{\tau}}\right),

\displaystyle g^{\beta\alpha}_{\tilde{c};\tilde{v}}=\frac{v_{F}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{\delta_{\beta\alpha}}{\omega_{k}^{2}}-\frac{v_{F}^{2}k^{\beta}k^{\alpha}}{\omega_{k}^{4}}\right),\quad\Omega^{\beta\alpha}_{\tilde{c};\tilde{v}}=0.

(S-47)

Thus, for the Dirac Hamiltonian, only LP light contributes to the injection current response:

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\alpha\beta}_{L,\text{inj.}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi e^{3}v^{2}}{\hbar\omega^{2}\eta}\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(f^{\prime}_{c}-f^{\prime}_{v}\right)k^{\tau}k^{\gamma}\left(\frac{\delta_{\beta\alpha}}{\omega_{k}^{2}}-\frac{v^{2}k^{\beta}k^{\alpha}}{\omega_{k}^{4}}\right)\delta(2\omega_{k}-\omega).

(S-48)

C.2
Interband effect: Shift Photon Drag Effect

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\alpha\beta}_{\text{shift}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}}{4\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\sigma,\tau,\bm{k}}d^{\tau}_{f}\left[\frac{v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}}{\omega_{cv}-\omega-i\eta}+\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}v^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}}{\omega_{cv}-\omega+i\eta}\right].

(S-49)

where I neglect the summation within degenerate subspace (
\tilde{n}=\tilde{m}

The
Fermi sea

\bm{q}
-expansion of the product of interband matrix elements velocity and its covariant derivatives:

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\alpha\beta}_{\text{shift}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}}{2\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}\sum_{\tilde{n}\neq\tilde{m},\bm{k}}f_{nm}\left\{\frac{\partial_{\tau}\left[v^{\beta}_{nm}(\bm{q})\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\alpha}_{mn}(-\bm{q})\right]}{\omega_{nm}-\omega-i\eta}+\frac{\partial_{\tau}\left[\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{nm}(\bm{q})v^{\alpha}_{mn}(-\bm{q})\right]}{\omega_{nm}-\omega+i\eta}\right\}.

(S-50)

which vanishes after summing the degenerate subspace, due to the constraint of
\mathcal{C}
-symmetry of the Dirac Hamiltonian [Eq. (
B.1
&
S-24
)]:

\displaystyle v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma^{+};v,\tau^{-}}\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\alpha}_{v,\tau^{-};c,\sigma^{+}}

\displaystyle=v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\alpha}_{v,\bar{\sigma}^{+};c,\bar{\tau}^{-}}

\displaystyle\partial_{q^{\tau}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma^{+};v,\tau^{-}}v^{\alpha}_{v,\tau^{-};c,\sigma^{+}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{q^{\tau}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\bar{\tau}^{-};v,\bar{\sigma}^{+}}v^{\alpha}_{v,\bar{\sigma}^{+};c,\bar{\tau}^{-}}\right).

(S-51)

Similarly, shift photon-drag current only has
Fermi surface
contribution [Eq. (
S-48
)] in the Dirac electrons.

C.3
Quantum Metric Connection

To discuss the geometric properties of the shift PDE, I define the quantum metric connection following Refs.
Ahn et al. 2020
;
Ahn et al. 2022
:

\displaystyle C^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma},

(S-52)

Note that the interband Berry connection is equivalent to interband matrix elements of position operator,
A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}=r^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}
which serves as the tangent basis vector of Riemannian geometry. Additionally, the covariant derivative on right is complex conjugate of
C^{\beta\alpha;\gamma}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}
with indices
\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta
interchanged:

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}A^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma},

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\sigma,\tau}A^{\alpha}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}A^{\beta}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}\right)^{*}=\left(C^{\alpha\gamma\beta}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}\right)^{*}.

(S-53)

The covariant derivative on interband Berry connection and covariant derivative on velocity matrix elements satisfy the following identity:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}A^{\nu}_{ab}

\displaystyle=-i\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{v^{\nu}_{ab}}{\omega_{ab}}\right)-\left(\sum_{\tilde{c}=\tilde{a}}A^{\mu}_{ac}\frac{v^{\nu}_{cb}}{\omega_{ab}}-\sum_{\tilde{c}=\tilde{a}}\frac{v^{\nu}_{ac}}{\omega_{ab}}A^{\mu}_{cb}\right)=\frac{-i}{\omega_{ab}}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}v^{\nu}_{ab}+\frac{iv^{\nu}_{ab}(v^{\mu}_{a}-v^{\mu}_{b})}{\omega^{2}_{ab}}.

(S-54)

Employ this identity, the product of interband matrix elements of velocity and its covariant derivative can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma,\tau}v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}D_{\gamma}v^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}_{cv}\left[C^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}+\frac{\Delta^{\gamma}_{cv}}{\omega_{cv}}Q^{\beta\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}\right],

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma,\tau}D_{\gamma}v^{\beta}_{c,\sigma;v,\tau}v^{\alpha}_{v,\tau;c,\sigma}

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}_{cv}\left[(C^{\alpha\gamma\beta}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}})^{*}+\frac{\Delta^{\gamma}_{cv}}{\omega_{cv}}(Q^{\alpha\beta}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}})^{*}\right].

(S-55)

The LP light response is obtained by symmetrizing the indices
\alpha,\beta
:

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\alpha\beta}_{L,\text{shift}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}}{8\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}d^{\tau}_{f}\omega^{2}_{cv}\left(\frac{C^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}+\frac{\Delta^{\gamma}_{cv}}{\omega_{cv}}Q^{\beta\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}+C^{\alpha\gamma\beta}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}+\frac{\Delta^{\gamma}_{cv}}{\omega_{cv}}Q^{\alpha\beta}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}}{\omega_{cv}-\omega-i\eta}+\text{c.c.}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}}{4\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}d^{\tau}_{f}\omega^{2}_{cv}\left[\left(\text{Re}\left(S^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}\right)+\frac{2\Delta^{\gamma}_{cv}}{\omega_{cv}}g^{\beta\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}\right)\mathcal{P}(\omega_{cv}-\omega)-\pi\text{Im}\left(S^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}\right)\delta(\omega_{cv}-\omega)\right],

(S-56)

where I use the relation
(\omega_{cv}-\omega-i\eta)^{-1}=\mathcal{P}(\omega_{cv}-\omega)+i\pi\delta(\omega_{cv}-\omega)
with
\mathcal{P}
denoting the principal value. The principal value corresponds to off-resonance conditions, while the
\delta
-function selects the on-resonance contributions.

The CP light response is obtained by antisymmetrizing the
\alpha,\beta
:

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\sigma}_{C,\text{shift}}

\displaystyle=i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\sigma}\frac{e^{3}}{8\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}d^{\tau}_{f}\omega^{2}_{cv}\left(\frac{C^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}+\frac{\Delta^{\gamma}_{cv}}{\omega_{cv}}Q^{\beta\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}-C^{\alpha\gamma\beta}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}-\frac{\Delta^{\gamma}_{cv}}{\omega_{cv}}Q^{\alpha\beta}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}}{\omega_{cv}-\omega-i\eta}-\text{c.c.}\right)

\displaystyle=-\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\sigma}\frac{e^{3}}{4\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}d^{\tau}_{f}\omega^{2}_{cv}\left[\left(\text{Im}\left(A^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}\right)-\frac{\Delta^{\gamma}_{cv}}{\omega_{cv}}\Omega^{\beta\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}\right)\mathcal{P}(\omega_{cv}-\omega)+\pi\text{Re}\left(A^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}\right)\delta(\omega_{cv}-\omega)\right].

(S-57)

In the massive Dirac electron with trivial band topology, the Berry curvature vanishes and the quantum metric connection lacks a symplectic form. Consequently, the linear shift current has only off-resonance contribution, while the circular shift current arises solely from on-resonance contribution.

The symmetric and antisymmetric quantum metric connection for the Dirac Hamiltonian are given by

\displaystyle S^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2v_{F}^{6}}{\omega_{k}^{6}}k^{\gamma}k^{\beta}k^{\alpha}-\frac{v_{F}^{4}}{2\omega_{k}^{4}}\left(2\delta_{\beta\alpha}k^{\gamma}+\delta_{\gamma\beta}k^{\alpha}+\delta_{\gamma\alpha}k^{\beta}\right),

(S-58)

\displaystyle A^{\beta\gamma\alpha}_{\tilde{c},\tilde{v}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{F}^{4}}{2\omega_{k}^{4}}\left(\delta_{\gamma\alpha}k^{\beta}-\delta_{\gamma\beta}k^{\alpha}\right).

(S-59)

The off-resonant contribution of LP light response in the Dirac Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\alpha\beta}_{L,\text{shift}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}v_{F}^{4}}{2\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(f^{\prime}_{c}-f^{\prime}_{v}\right)\left(\frac{2v_{F}^{4}k^{\gamma}k^{\tau}k^{\beta}k^{\alpha}}{\omega_{k}^{5}}-\frac{\delta_{\gamma\beta}v_{F}^{2}k^{\alpha}k^{\tau}+\delta_{\gamma\alpha}v_{F}^{2}k^{\beta}k^{\tau}}{\omega_{k}^{3}}\right)\mathcal{P}\left(2\omega_{k}-\omega\right).

(S-60)

The on-resonance contribution of CP light response in the Dirac Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\chi^{\gamma\tau;\sigma}_{C,\text{shift}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\sigma}\frac{\pi e^{3}v_{F}^{4}}{2\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(f^{\prime}_{c}-f^{\prime}_{v}\right)\frac{\delta_{\gamma\alpha}v_{F}^{2}k^{\beta}k^{\tau}-\delta_{\gamma\beta}v_{F}^{2}k^{\alpha}k^{\tau}}{\omega_{k}^{3}}\delta\left(2\omega_{k}-\omega\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\sigma}\frac{\pi e^{3}v_{F}^{4}}{2\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(f^{\prime}_{c}-f^{\prime}_{v}\right)\frac{\left(\delta_{\gamma\alpha}\delta_{\beta\tau}-\delta_{\gamma\beta}\delta_{\alpha\tau}\right)v_{F}^{2}k^{2}}{3\omega_{k}^{3}}\delta\left(2\omega_{k}-\omega\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{\gamma\tau\sigma}|\chi_{C,\text{shift}}|,

(S-61)

where I define the isotropic shift PDE tensor as

\displaystyle|\chi_{C,\text{shift}}|=\frac{\pi e^{3}v_{F}^{4}}{3\hbar\omega^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(f^{\prime}_{c}-f^{\prime}_{v}\right)\frac{\omega_{k}^{2}-\tilde{\Delta}^{2}}{\omega_{k}^{3}}\delta\left(2\omega_{k}-\omega\right).

(S-62)

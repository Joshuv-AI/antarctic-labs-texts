Abstract
A sensitivity of the collider FCC-hh to anomalous quartic gauge couplings (aQGCs) of neutral bosons in lead-lead collisions is studied. Examining diphoton production, the bounds on aQGCs at 5 ​ σ 5\sigma and 95% C.L. are obtained depending on systematic uncertainties.

The coherent action of

an intensive spectrum of equivalent photons in the ions with nuclear
charge
Z

charge
Z^{2}

\gamma\gamma

\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{4}
and has a tiny cross section, are enhanced
by a factor of

compared to similar

\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\gamma\gamma

\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{4}

can be strongly reduced in nuclear collisions

becoming the light-by-light (LbL) scattering feasible for the
experimental analysis.

\mathrm{Pb}\,\mathrm{Pb}\rightarrow\mathrm{Pb}\,\gamma\gamma\,\mathrm{Pb}

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}+\sum_{i=0}^{1}\frac{f_{S,i}}{\Lambda^{4}}\,\mathcal{O}_{S,i}+\sum_{i=0}^{7}\frac{f_{M,i}}{\Lambda^{4}}\,\mathcal{O}_{M,i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\neq 3,4\end{subarray}}^{9}\frac{f_{T,i}}{\Lambda^{4}}\,\mathcal{O}_{T,i}\;.

(1)

There are three classes of three types of dimension-8 operators in
this Lagrangian: i) operators
O_{S,i}
containing covariant
derivatives of the Higgs doublet only; ii) operators
O_{M,i}
with
two field strength tensors and two derivatives of Higgs; iii)
operators
O_{T,i}
with field strength tensors only. It is the
tensor operators
O_{T,i}
which induce anomalous

\gamma\gamma\gamma\gamma
vertex. They are as follows (see, for
instance,
[
19
]
)

\displaystyle O_{T,0}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{T,0}}{\Lambda^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu}\right]\times\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\alpha\beta}W^{\alpha\beta}\right],

\displaystyle O_{T,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{T,1}}{\Lambda^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\alpha\mu}W^{\nu\beta}\right]\times\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\nu\beta}W^{\alpha\mu}\right],

\displaystyle O_{T,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{T,2}}{\Lambda^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\alpha\mu}W^{\mu\beta}\right]\times\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\beta\nu}W^{\nu\alpha}\right],

\displaystyle O_{T,5}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{T,5}}{\Lambda^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu}\right]\times B_{\alpha\beta}B^{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle O_{T,6}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{T,6}}{\Lambda^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\alpha\mu}W^{\nu\beta}\right]\times B_{\nu\beta}B^{\alpha\mu},

\displaystyle O_{T,7}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{T,7}}{\Lambda^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[W_{\alpha\mu}W^{\mu\beta}\right]\times B_{\beta\nu}B^{\nu\alpha},

\displaystyle O_{T,8}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{T,8}}{\Lambda^{4}}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}\times B_{\alpha\beta}B^{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle O_{T,9}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{T,9}}{\Lambda^{4}}B_{\alpha\mu}B^{\mu\beta}\times B_{\beta\nu}B^{\nu\alpha}.

(2)

Here
f_{T,i}
(
i=0,1,2,5,6,7,8,9
) are dimensionless parameters,
and
\Lambda
is a mass-dimension parameter associated with the
scale of physics beyond the SM. Note that dimension-8 operators are
lowest-dimension operators inducing only aQGCs without anomalous
triple gauge boson vertices.

After EW symmetry breaking the part of the Lagrangian describing

\gamma\gamma\gamma\gamma
interaction is defined by two operators,

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma\gamma\gamma\gamma}=g_{1}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}+g_{2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\nu\alpha}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\beta\mu}\;,

(3)

with two anomalous couplings
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
of dimension -4. They
are linear combinations of the “unbroken” couplings

f_{T,i}/\Lambda^{4}
,

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{w}^{4}}{\Lambda^{4}}(f_{T,0}+f_{T,1})+\frac{s_{w}^{2}c_{w}^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}(f_{T,5}+f_{T,6})+\frac{c_{w}^{4}}{\Lambda^{4}}f_{T,8}\;,

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{w}^{4}}{\Lambda^{4}}f_{T,2}+\frac{s_{w}^{2}c_{w}^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}f_{T,7}+\frac{c_{w}^{4}}{\Lambda^{4}}f_{T,9}\;.

(4)

As one can see from (
1
), the diphoton production
via the
\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\gamma\gamma

f_{T,8}/\Lambda^{4}
and

The

TeV
-4

2
Diphoton production in nucleus-nucleus collision at the FCC-hh

The final-state signature of the lead-lead collision is the
exclusive production of two photons,

\mathrm{Pb}\,\mathrm{Pb}\rightarrow\mathrm{Pb}\,\gamma\gamma\,\mathrm{Pb}\;,

(8)

with the diphoton final-state measured in the central detector, and
lead nuclei surviving the electromagnetic interaction scattered at
very low angles with respect to the beams. In the equivalent photon
approximation (EPA)
[
69
]
the accelerated lead ions can
be considered as
\gamma
beams. Indeed, the emitted photons are
almost on-shell, since their virtuality
|Q^{2}|<1/R_{A}^{2}
, where

R_{A}=1.2A^{1/3}
fm is the radius of the nucleus. It results in

|Q^{2}|<7.7\cdot 10^{-4}
GeV
2
for A = 208.

In EPA the differential cross section of a photon fusion process
NN\rightarrow N\,\gamma\gamma\,N
can be factorized as

d\sigma=\int\limits_{\tau_{\min}}^{\tau_{\max}}\!\!\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\!\!\int\limits_{\omega_{\min}}^{\omega_{\max}}\!\!\frac{d\omega}{\omega}f_{\gamma/N}(\omega)f_{\gamma/N}(\tau/\omega)\,d\hat{\sigma}(\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\gamma\gamma)\;,

(9)

where

\omega_{\max}=E_{N}-m_{N}\;,\quad\tau_{\max}=(E_{N}-m_{N})^{2}\;,

(10)

\omega
is the photon energies emitted from the nucleus,
E_{N}
is
the energy of the ion beams, and
m_{N}
is the nucleus mass. The
quantity
4\tau
coincides with the center-of-mass energy squared of
the process
\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
. As for the lower
limits on variables
\omega
and
\tau
in (
9
), they are
given by equations

\omega_{\min}=\tau/\omega_{\max}\;,\quad\tau_{\min}=p_{\bot}^{2}\;,

(11)

where
p_{\bot}
is the transverse momenta of the outgoing photons.

In the relativistic limit the equivalent spectrum of the photon from
the nucleus
N
with the charge
Z
and atomic number
A
is given
by
[
70
]
-
[
72
]
(see also

[
73
]
-
[
82
]
).

f_{\gamma/N}(\omega)=\frac{2Z^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\!\left[\xi K_{0}(\xi)K_{1}(\xi)-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}(K_{1}^{2}(\xi)-K_{0}^{2}(\xi))\right],

(12)

where
\xi=\omega/E_{R}
,
E_{R}=E_{N}/(m_{N}R_{A})=\sqrt{s_{NN}}/(2m_{p}R_{A})
,
m_{p}
being the nucleon mass.
K_{0}(x)
(
K_{1}(x)
) is the
modified Bessel function of the second kind of order zero (one).
Note that

\left[\xi K_{0}(\xi)K_{1}(\xi)-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}(K_{1}^{2}(\xi)-K_{0}^{2}(\xi))\right]\bigg|_{\xi\rightarrow 0}=\ln\frac{1}{\xi}-C+\mathrm{O}(\xi^{2})\;,

(13)

where
C=\gamma_{E}+0.5-\ln 2
, and
\gamma_{E}\simeq 0.5772
is
Euler’s constant, that leads to the approximation

f_{\gamma/N}(x)|_{x\rightarrow 0}=\frac{Z^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\!\left[\ln\frac{1}{(xm_{N}R_{A})^{2}}-0.768\right],

(14)

where a dimensionless variable
x=\omega/E_{N}
is introduced. For
large values of
\xi

\left[\xi K_{0}(\xi)K_{1}(\xi)-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}(K_{1}^{2}(\xi)-K_{0}^{2}(\xi))\right]\bigg|_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}=\frac{\pi}{4}\,e^{-2\xi}\left[1+\mathrm{O}(\xi^{-1})\right].

(15)

For the PbPb collisions (with

TeV. We also get

R_{A}=7.1\mathrm{\ fm}=36.0\mathrm{\ GeV}^{-1}\;,\ E_{N}=4097.6\mathrm{\ TeV}\;,\ E_{R}\simeq 582.8\mathrm{\ GeV}\;,

(16)

and

\xi\simeq 1.71\left(\frac{\omega}{\mathrm{TeV}}\right).

(17)

The Lorentz factor is defined to be
\gamma_{L}=E_{N}/m_{N}=20935.7

Appendix A. Anomalous helicity amplitudes

For the LbL scattering the Bose-Einstein statistics and parity
invariance demand that there exist six independent anomalous
helicity amplitudes
M_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}}
with

\lambda_{1}=+1
. Only three of them are nonzero
[
3
]
,

\displaystyle M_{++++}(s,t,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{(4g_{1}+3g_{2})}{2}\,s^{2}\;,

\displaystyle M_{++--}(s,t,u)

\displaystyle=(4g_{1}+g_{2})\,(t^{2}+tu+u^{2})\;,

\displaystyle M_{+-+-}(s,t,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{(4g_{1}+3g_{2})}{2}\,u^{2}\;,

(A.1)

where
s
,
t
,
u
are Mandelstam variables of the

\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
collision,
s+t+u=0
. Other
three independent helicity amplitudes with
\lambda_{1}=+1
are
equal to zero,

M_{+++-}=M_{++-+}=M_{+-++}=0\;.

(A.2)

We also have the crossing relations

\displaystyle M_{+--+}(s,t,u)

\displaystyle=M_{+-+-}(s,u,t)=\frac{(4g_{1}+3g_{2})}{2}\,t^{2}\;,

\displaystyle M_{+---}(s,t,u)

\displaystyle=M_{+-++}(s,u,t)=0\;.

(A.3)

Appendix B. Two-photon cross section

The exclusive cross section for two-photon process can be
schematically written as

d\sigma=\int\!d\omega_{1}\!\int\!d\omega_{2}\frac{dn}{d\omega_{1}}\frac{dn}{d\omega_{2}}\,d\hat{\sigma}\;,

(B.1)

where the photon flux from a charge
Z
nucleus is of the form

[
72
]

\displaystyle\frac{dn}{d\omega}=\frac{2Z^{2}\alpha}{\pi\omega}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{0}}\right)K_{0}\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{0}}\right)K_{1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{0}}\right)^{\!\!2}\!\!\left[K_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{0}}\right)-K_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{0}}\right)\right]\bigg\},

(B.2)

with
\omega_{0}=E_{N}/(m_{N}R_{A})=\gamma_{L}/R_{A}
. The cross section
(
B.1
) can be rewritten in the form

d\sigma=\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{\omega_{1}}\!\int\frac{d\omega_{2}}{\omega_{2}}f(\omega_{1})f(\omega_{2})\,d\hat{\sigma}\;,

(B.3)

where the photon distribution in the nucleus
f(\omega)
is defined
by eq. (
12
).

2

As one can see,
dn/d\omega
and
f(\omega)
differ by the

1/\omega

After change of variables,
\omega=\omega_{1}
,
\tau=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}
, we come to formula (

Let us define an energy fraction of the photon,
x=\omega/E_{N}
.
For small

\frac{dn_{\gamma/N}(x)}{dx}\bigg|_{x\ll 1}\simeq\frac{Z^{2}\alpha}{\pi x}\ln\frac{1}{(xm_{N}R_{A})^{2}}\;.

(B.4)

If the source of the emitted photon is instead an electron, then

Z\rightarrow 1,\quad m_{N}\rightarrow m_{e},\quad R_{A}\rightarrow 1/Q\;,

(B.5)

\frac{dn_{\gamma/e}(x)}{dx}\bigg|_{x\ll 1}\simeq\frac{\alpha}{\pi x}\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{x^{2}m_{e}^{2}}\;,

(B.6)

in agreement with the Weizsäcker-Williams form.

Appendix C. Effective energy of
\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
collision

The photon luminosity as a function of variable
\tau

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ph}}(\tau)=\int\limits_{\omega_{\min}}^{\omega_{\max}}\!\!\frac{d\omega}{\omega}f_{\gamma/N}(\omega)f_{\gamma/N}(\tau/\omega)\;,

where
\tau
is in units of TeV
2

\sqrt{\tau}
is
equal to the half an invariant energy of the

\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\gamma\gamma

anomalous cross section is proportional to
\tau^{4}
. Thus, taking
into account eqs. (
9

average value of
\tau

\langle\tau\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\int\limits_{\tau_{\min}}^{\tau_{\max}}d\tau\tau^{4}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ph}}(\tau)\;,

(C.2)

where

N=\int\limits_{\tau_{\min}}^{\tau_{\max}}d\tau\tau^{3}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\tau)\;,

(C.3)

and
\tau_{\max}
,
\tau_{\min}
are defined by
eqs. (
10
), (
11
).

For the lead-lead collision at the 100 TeV FCC-hh our calculations

\langle\tau\rangle\simeq 1.23\mathrm{\ TeV}^{2}\;.

(C.4)

A revisit on the critical blow-up for semilinear wave equations in low space dimensions with slicing method
Hiroyuki Takamura

Abstract
In this reviewing paper, we are interested in the proof of estimating the lifespan of classical solutions of semilinear wave equations with the critical exponent from above especially in low space dimensions. There are a few ways to show the result by comparison argument with ODE via point-wise estimates, or by functional method via a weak form with a special choice of test functions. But in order to make a good much with the numerical analysis, we show a simple proof by iteration argument of a point-wise estimate of the solution with a slicing technique.

Introduction

First, let us review the analysis on the following initial value problem for morel semilinear wave equations
with unknown functions
u:(x,t)\in{\bf R}^{n}\times(0,T)\rightarrow{\bf R}
.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}-\Delta u=|u|^{p},\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\bf R}^{n}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
T>0
,
p>1
and
{\varepsilon}>0
is a small parameter.
The initial data
f
and
g
are smooth functions of compact support.
This model problem plays an important role to show the optimality
of the general theory for nonlinear wave equations
which is organized for the long time existence of classical solutions
of general equations with power-type nonlinear terms.
See a book by Li and Zhou
[
15
]
for the general theory.
Also see Katayama
[
9
]
, Zhou and Han
[
32
]
for the progress in 2 dimensional case
related to the so-called “combined effect”.
For the latest one for 1 dimensional case, see
Takamatsu
[
21
]
, Kido, Sasaki, Takamastu and Takamura
[
11
]
,
or a reviewing paper by Takamura
[
24
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the blow-up,
as well as the upper bound of the maximal existence time, of classical solutions of (
1.1
),
for which the so-called “lifespan” should be introduced.
Let us define a lifespan
T({\varepsilon})
of a solution of (
1.1
) by

T({\varepsilon})=\sup\{T>0\ :\ \exists\ \mbox{a solution $u(x,t)$ of (\ref{IVP})
for arbitrarily fixed $(f,g)$.}\},

where “solution” means the classical one when
p\geq 2
.
When
1<p<2
, it means the weak one, but sometimes the one
given by associated integral equations to (
1.1
)
by standard Strichartz’s estimate.
See Sideris
[
19
]
or Georgiev, Takamura and Zhou
[
3
]
for example on such an argument.
We note that
T({\varepsilon})<\infty
means that there is a special data
which produces the blow-up in finite time of the solution.

When
n=1
, we have
T({\varepsilon})<\infty
for any power
p>1
by Kato
[
10
]
.
When
n\geq 2
, we have the following Strauss’ conjecture on (
1.1
)
by Strauss
[
20

\begin{array}[]{lll}T({\varepsilon})=\infty&\mbox{if $p>p_{0}(n)$ and ${\varepsilon}$ is ``small"}&\mbox{(global-in-time existence)},\\
T({\varepsilon})<\infty&\mbox{if $1<p\leq p_{0}(n)$}&\mbox{(blow-up in finite time)},\end{array}

where
p_{0}(n)
is so-called Strauss’ exponent defined
by positive root of the quadratic equation,
\gamma(p,n)=0
, where

\gamma(n,p):=1+\frac{n+1}{2}p-\frac{n-1}{2}p^{2}.

(1.2)

That is,

p_{0}(n)=\frac{n+1+\sqrt{n^{2}+10n-7}}{2(n-1)}.

(1.3)

1<p\leq p_{0}(n)
,
we are interested in the estimate of the lifespan
T({\varepsilon})
to
clarify the stability of zero solution because we have an uniqueness of the solution.
To describe the results, we denote the fact that there are positive constants,

C_{1}

A({\varepsilon},C_{1})\leq T({\varepsilon})\leq A({\varepsilon},C_{2})

(1.6)

where

is a number satisfying

a^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\log(1+a)=1.

From now on, we are interested in the proof of the upper bound of
T({\varepsilon})
in (
1.9
),
especially low space dimensions
n=2,3
.
As stated above, we have already the one by point-wise estimate of the solution and
comparison argument with ODE due to Zhou for
n=3

[
29
]
and
n=2

[
30
]
.
See also Takamura
[
22
]
for its unified point-wise estimate.
Also the functional method which comes from the weak form with a special choice of the test function
can be found in Ikeda, Sobajima and Wakasa
[
6
]
.
But in order to apply the proof to discrete semiliner wave equations
which was first introduced by Matsuya
[
16
]
,
it is better to employ the iteration argument of the point-wise estimate only.
In fact, we have such a result in
n=1
by Tsubota, Higashi, Matsuya, Sasaki and Tokihiro
[
26
]
.
The discrete semilinear wave equation may help us to introduce the numerical analysis
to our problem (
1.1
) in the near future.
The iteration argument for (
1.1
) was introduced by John
[
8
]
for the subcritical case in
n=3
.
In order to apply it to the critical case, we need the slicing technique of the blow-up set
which was introduced by Agemi, Kurokawa and Takamura
[
1
]

for the weakly coupled system of semilinear wave equations with the critical exponents in
n=3
.
Such a technique is widely applicable to various inequalities with critical exponents
including 1d integral inequalities driven from ordinary differential inequalities of the second order.
For the latest example, see Sasaki, Shao and Takamura
[
18
]
for which the critical blow-up
for power nonlinear terms of spatial derivative can be covered.

The main purpose of this paper is to show its proof in the case of the single equation
(
1.1
) for the future application to discrete semilinear wave equations.

2
The result and its proof by slicing method

As stated in Introduction, we shall reprove the following theorem
by iteration argument with slicing technique.

Theorem 2.1
(Zhou
[
29
,
30
]
, Takamura
[
22
]
)

Let
n=2,3
and
p=p_{S}(n)
.
Assume that
f(x)\equiv 0
and
g(x)\geq 0(\not\equiv 0)
.
Then, there is a positive constant
{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(n,p,g)
such that
a classical solution of (
1.1
) cannot exist as far as
T
satifies

T>\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-p(p-1)}\right)\quad{for}\ 0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 2.1

The original assumption on the data in Zhou
[
29
,
30
]
is slightly different.
Here we do not need to assume that the support of the data is compact.
We note that the conclusion shows

T({\varepsilon})\leq\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-p(p-1)}\right)

with the same
{\varepsilon}
and
C
.

First we employ the following unified point-wise estimate in
n=2,3
of the solution.

Lemma 2.1
((3.8) in Takamura
[
22
]
)

Let the assumption in Theorem
2.1
be fulfilled
and
u
be a classical solution of (
1.1
).
Assume that there is a point
x_{0}\in{\bf R}^{n}
such that
g(x_{0})>0
.
Then, the spherical mean of
u
at
x_{0}
with radius
r
defined by

\widetilde{u}(r,t):=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\int_{|\omega|=1}u(x_{0}+r\omega,t)dS_{\omega}

satisfies

\begin{array}[]{ll}\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi^{3-n}r^{(n-1)/2}}\iint_{R(r,t)}\lambda^{(n-1)/2}|\widetilde{u}(\lambda,\tau)|^{p}d\lambda d\tau\\
&\displaystyle+\frac{M{\varepsilon}^{p}}{r^{(n-1)/2}(t-r)^{(n-1)p/2-(n+1)/2}}\end{array}\mbox{in}\ \Sigma

(2.1)

with some positive constant
M=M(n,p,g)
, where
\Sigma
and
R(r,t)
are defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\Sigma:=\{(r,t)\in(0,\infty)^{2}:3\delta\leq t-r\leq r\},\\
R(r,t):=\{(\lambda,\tau)\in(0,\infty)^{2}:t-r\leq\lambda,\ \tau+\lambda\leq t+r,\ 3\delta\leq\tau-\lambda\leq t-r\}\end{array}

and
\delta>0
is chosen as
\widetilde{g}(2\delta)>0
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
by slicing the blow-up set.

First, we assume an estimate

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{C_{j}}{r^{(n-1)/2}(t-r)^{(n-1)p/2-(n+1)/2}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j}k}\right)^{a_{j}}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Sigma_{j}

(2.2)

for
j\in{\bf N}
, where we set
k:=3\delta>0
and

\Sigma_{j}:=\{(r,t)\in(0,\infty)^{2}:l_{j}k\leq t-r\leq r\},\quad l_{j}:=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{1}{2^{i}},\quad a_{j}:=\frac{p^{j-1}-1}{p-1}.

The constant
C_{j}>0
will be defined later.
We note that (
2.2
) is true for
j=1
with
C_{1}=M{\varepsilon}^{p}
.
We may call
\Sigma_{j}
by sliced domain as

\Sigma_{j+1}\subset\Sigma_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad l_{j}\nearrow 2\ (j\rightarrow\infty).

Introducing characteristic variables by

\alpha:=\tau+\lambda,\ \beta:=\tau-\lambda

into the right-hand side in (
2.1
) and making use of
t+r\geq 3(t-r)
, we have that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{C}{r^{(n-1)/2}}\int_{l_{j}k}^{t-r}d\beta\int_{2(t-r)+\beta}^{3(t-r)}(\alpha-\beta)^{(n-1)/2}|\widetilde{u}(\lambda,\tau)|^{p}d\alpha\quad\mbox{in}\ \Sigma_{j},

where
C:=2^{-2}\pi^{n-3}>0
.
Hence it is possible to plug (
2.2
) into
\alpha
-integral above.
It follows from

p\left(\frac{n-1}{2}p-\frac{n+1}{2}\right)=1\quad\mbox{for}\ p=p_{S}(n)

that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{CC_{j}^{p}}{r^{(n-1)/2}}\int_{l_{j}k}^{t-r}\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}k}\right)^{pa_{j}}d\beta\int_{2(t-r)+\beta}^{3(t-r)}(\alpha-\beta)^{(n-1)(1-p)/2}d\alpha

which yields

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{CC_{j}^{p}}{r^{(n-1)/2}\{3(t-r)\}^{(n-1)(p-1)/2}}\int_{l_{j}k}^{t-r}\frac{t-r-\beta}{\beta}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}k}\right)^{pa_{j}}d\beta

in
\Sigma_{j}
.The
\beta
-integral above can be estimated as follows.
The integration by parts yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{l_{j}k}^{t-r}\frac{t-r-\beta}{\beta}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}k}\right)^{pa_{j}}d\beta\\
\displaystyle=\int_{l_{j}k}^{t-r}(t-r-\beta)\left\{\frac{\left(\log(\beta/l_{j}k)\right)^{pa_{j}+1}}{pa_{j}+1}\right\}^{\prime}d\beta\\
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{pa_{j}+1}\int_{l_{j}k}^{t-r}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}k}\right)^{pa_{j}+1}d\beta.\end{array}

Hence it follows from
\Sigma_{j+1}\subset\Sigma_{j}
and

\frac{l_{j}}{l_{j+1}}(t-r)\geq l_{j}k\quad\mbox{for}\ (r,t)\in\Sigma_{j+1}

that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{l_{j}k}^{t-r}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}k}\right)^{pa_{j}+1}d\beta&\displaystyle\geq\int_{l_{j}(t-r)/l_{j+1}}^{t-r}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}k}\right)^{pa_{j}+1}d\beta\\
&\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{l_{j}}{l_{j+1}}\right)(t-r)\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}k}\right)^{pa_{j}+1}\end{array}

in
\Sigma_{j+1}
.
This estimate shows the essence of the slicing method.
Therefore, summing up all the estimates, we obtain by

1-\frac{l_{j}}{l_{j+1}}=\frac{1}{2^{j+1}l_{j+1}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{j+2}}

and

pa_{j}+1=p\frac{p^{j-1}-1}{p-1}+1=\frac{p^{j}-1}{p-1}=a_{j+1}\leq\frac{p^{j}}{p-1}

that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{2^{-2}3^{(n-1)(1-p)/2}(p-1)CC_{j}^{p}}{r^{(n-1)/2}(t-r)^{(n-1)p/2-(n+1)/2}}\cdot\frac{1}{(2p)^{j}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}k}\right)^{a_{j+1}}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Sigma_{j+1}.

If we define a sequence
\{C_{j}\}
by

C_{j+1}=\frac{NC_{j}^{p}}{(2p)^{j}},\ C_{1}=M{\varepsilon}^{p},

(
2.2
) holds for all
n\in{\bf N}
, where we set

N:=2^{-2}3^{(n-1)(1-p)/2}(p-1)C>0.

This can be solved concretely as

C_{j+1}=\frac{N^{1+p+\cdots+p^{j-1}}(M{\varepsilon}^{p})^{p^{j}}}{(2p)^{j+p(j-1)+\cdots+p^{j-1}}}.

Here we note that

j+p(j-1)+\cdots+p^{j-1}=p^{j-1}\left(\frac{j}{p^{j-1}}+\frac{j-1}{p^{j-2}}+\cdots+1\right)

and there is a positive constant
S_{p}
such that

\frac{j}{p^{j-1}}+\frac{j-1}{p^{j-2}}+\cdots+1\nearrow S_{p}\quad(j\rightarrow\infty)

by d’Alembert criterion.

Therefore we obtain that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{N^{-1/(p-1)}}{r^{(n-1)/2}(t-r)^{(n-1)p/2-(n+1)/2}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{2k}\right)^{-1/(p-1)}\exp\left(I(r,t)p^{j}\right)

in
\Sigma_{\infty}:=\{(r,t)\in(0,\infty)^{2}:2k\leq t-r\leq r\}\subset\Sigma_{j}
for any
j\in{\bf N}
, where

I(r,t):=\log N^{1/(p-1)}+\log M{\varepsilon}^{p}-\log(2p)^{S_{p}/p}+\log\left(\log\frac{t-r}{2k}\right)^{1/(p-1)}.

If there is a point
(r_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{\infty}
such that
I(r_{0},t_{0})>0
,
then
u
cannot be a classical solution of (
1.1
) by letting
j\rightarrow\infty
.
We easily find that one of its sufficient conditions on
(r_{0},t_{0})
is

N^{1/(p-1)}(2p)^{-S_{p}/p}M{\varepsilon}^{p}\left(\log\frac{t_{0}}{4k}\right)^{1/(p-1)}>1

by setting
t_{0}=2r_{0}
.
It can be rewritten as

t_{0}>4k\exp\left(N^{-1}(2p)^{S_{p}(p-1)/p}M^{-(p-1)}{\varepsilon}^{-p(p-1)}\right).

Therefore if we define
{\varepsilon}_{0}
by

4k=\exp\left(N^{-1}(2p)^{S_{p}(p-1)/p}M^{-(p-1)}{\varepsilon}_{0}^{-p(p-1)}\right),

u
cannot be a classical solution as far as
T
satisfies

T>\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-p(p-1)}\right)\quad\mbox{for}\ 0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0},

where

C=2N^{-1}(2p)^{S_{p}(p-1)/p}M^{-(p-1)}>0.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

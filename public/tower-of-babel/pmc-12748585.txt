Prediction of electricity consumption and hydropower production in the smart power grid based on the gated recurrent unit neural network and modified future search algorithm
Authors: Hao Tang, Yuening Wang, Xinping Yuan, Navid Razmjooy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrecise prediction of electricity usage and hydroelectric power production can enhance energy distribution efficiency, minimize waste, and enhance general grid performance. Moreover, utilizing the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) Neural Network and Modified Future Search Algorithm (MFSA) can offer more accurate analysis and forecasts, and facilitates improved decision-making and allocation of resources. This investigation aims to develop an improved model based on GRU and MFSA for examining and forecasting energy generation and electricity usage in a smart power grid. This study employs economic and social data to predict patterns of long-term electricity usage, while climatic data is employed as input for the optimum model for simulation of electricity generation, which concentrates particularly on hydropower production. The developed optimized model has been compared to original deep learning and optimized methods to demonstrate its enhanced efficacy and superiority. According to the investigation, the obtained results show how effective the optimal method is in accurately forecasting electricity usage and hydropower generation.Keywords: Electricity consumption, Hydropower generation, Modified future search algorithm (MFSA), Gated recurrent unit (GRU) neural network, Economic-social data, Climatic dataSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Background

In the current dynamic energy environment, the combination of renewable energy sources and the rise of intelligent power grids have highlighted the crucial need for precise and effective energy management1.

While there is a global push towards sustainable energy solutions, developing countries face unique challenges in meeting increasing electric energy demand due to factors such as infrastructure limitations and economic constraints2.

The world is moving towards a more sustainable future, and enhancing energy distribution, reducing waste to the least, and improving general grid performance are central to this change. Recently, there have been noteworthy technological improvements in the power grid, especially in the production, transmission, and distribution sectors, in order to maintain with the dynamic energy needs3. The social and industrial sectors have experienced advancements that lead to a significant rise in electricity usage, and emphasize the necessity to investigate sustainable and renewable energy sources.

Hydropower is crucial in addressing this rising demand, and is a reliable and clean energy source4. Generating hydropower is one of the oldest methods of generating electrical and mechanical energy, with a long history as an essential source of renewable energy. It captures the moving water power in streams and rivers to produce electricity, and provides low-carbon electricity5. Facilities of hydropower can quickly increase output from zero to maximum capacity, provide electricity to the grid through main outages or disruptions6.This adaptability completes other renewable energy sources like wind and solar7.

Pumped storage hydropower plants have the capacity to discharge energy through water when there is high demand or when other renewable energy sources are not producing sufficient power. Hydropower is crucial in facilitating the integration of alternative renewable energy technologies by offering a reliable and steady baseload and by supporting the wind and solar energy’s intermittent nature8. The long hydropower infrastructure lifespan and its capability to ensure energy security both contribute to its significance in the energy mix9.

The implementation of smart grid technologies highlights the importance of effective management of energy and precise prediction of energy generation and usage10. Smart grids, which utilize digital technology and two-way communication, provide an opportunity to transform the management way of electricity supply11. Through smart grids, the management of the fluctuating nature of renewable energy sources (like hydropower) can be improved, and can result in an enhancement of general grid reliability12. Utilizing analysis of real-time data and advanced prediction techniques enables optimization of energy distribution, minimize waste, and improve resilience of grid13.

The smart power grid is a digitally linked system that allows for smooth communication and data flow among different parts of the power system, from power production sources to consumer devices14. Incorporating digital technology provides exceptional opportunities to transform energy management15. One of the main benefits of the smart grid is its capacity to utilize innovative data analysis methods, such as deep learning algorithms16.

Deep learning, a type of artificial intelligence, allows the grid to acquire knowledge and make well-informed decisions using extensive data produced by different meters, sensors, and devices linked to the grid17. By examining trends, patterns, and current data, the smart grid can enhance its operations, enhance effectiveness, and improve general efficiency18.

Utilizing advanced data analysis techniques, the smart grid can make accurate predictions about electricity usage patterns and predict peak demand periods19. This enables the improvement of power production and distribution to guarantee a more effective allocation of resources, reduce waste and minimize the risk of power outages. Furthermore, the smart grid has the ability to enhance the use of renewable energy sources, including hydropower, by forecasting water levels, predicting fluctuations in hydropower output, and maintaining a stable power supply20.

The smart power grid’s two-way communication ability enables consumers to actively participate in energy management. Through smart meters and connected devices, consumers can monitor their energy use, modify patterns of consumption, even supply excess energy back into the grid, and foster a more efficient and sustainable energy ecosystem21.

The energy sector often acts poorly when employ traditional forecasting methods to handling the intricate and ever-changing nature of electricity usage and hydropower production22. Here, deep learning, a robust kind of machine learning, becomes useful. Deep learning models, with multi-layered neural networks, can effectively capture nonlinear patterns and associations in data, and make them highly suitable for energy prediction23.

Related works

Emin Sahin et al.24 employed meteorological data from the area of the Hirfanlı Dam to develop a hybrid Genetic Grey Wolf Optimizer (GGWO)-based Convolutional Neural Network/Recurrent Neural Network (CNN/RNN) regression method for predicting hydropower production (PP). The study explored various machine learning techniques and introduced GGWO and CNN/RNN model combined with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) regression technique. In Method I, the ELM algorithm outperformed other ML models with a correlation coefficient of 0.977 and a mean absolute error of 0.4. In Method II, the proposed CNN/RNN hybrid model achieved even better results with a correlation coefficient of 0.9802 and a mean absolute error of 0.314. The study’s findings could benefit decision-making in renewable energy forecasting and resource management for electricity generation.

Pan et al.25 introduced an approach for hydropower station operation using an enhanced LSTM neural network and the Random Forest algorithm. The LSTM model was enhanced using particle swarm optimization, and the Random Forest algorithm was used for load-distribution modeling. Historical data was clustered using K-means, and the parameters of the Random Forest model were optimized. The approach was illustrated using a case study of a hydropower station in China. The research demonstrated that the I-LSTM approach accurately represented the operational characteristics of hydraulic turbines, and ensured their proximity to the actual operating state. When compared with other methods, I-LSTM resulted in reduced prediction errors and contributes to optimized unit operation and reduced water consumption in hydropower applications.

Shivashankaraiah et al.26 introduced an approach to forecast the output power of photovoltaic systems by using a Hybrid Deep Neural Network (HDNN) framework, which represented a significant advancement in the application of deep learning to prediction problems in transmission systems. The HDNN framework combined CNN and LSTM. Conventional deep learning techniques were utilized in the primary phase, and the evaluation of their effectiveness was presented in more detail after conducting training using firefly optimization techniques. The most reliable method among those used in previous studies was selected. The combination of deep learning and power efficiency seemed to hold promise, particularly in enhancing sustainable management and the electrical industry digitalization.

Almashnowi et al.27 suggested a hybrid deep learning-based power management strategy that used a combination of a convolutional neural network (CNN) and a deep Q-learning Network (DQN). The strategy was implemented using the matrix laboratory (MATLAB) language. Experimental results confirmed that this algorithm achieved a computational time of 0.2 s and an energy loss of 0.01 megawatts, which was lower compared to traditional models. Additionally, the proposed strategy demonstrated a high accuracy rate of 0.99, and represented its effectiveness in managing various load power and solar profiles. The adoption of this hybrid approach had the potential to significantly reduce operational costs and promote sustainability in freshwater generation, which contributed to optimized future for the desalination sector.

Li et al.28 aimed to improve electricity load forecasting in Turkey using machine learning techniques, specifically CatBoost and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), combined with optimization algorithms including Phasor Particle Swarm Optimization (PPSO), Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA), and Hybrid Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO). The analysis focused on assessing the performance of combined models using accuracy measures and runtime efficacy. The XGBoost-SSA model outperformed others, and indicated increased accuracy and better results in forecasting electricity usage. This model had the highest coefficient of determination (R2) value and exhibited lower errors during testing, and made it a promising approach for predicting electricity consumption in Turkey.

Contribution

Indeed, all individual building blocks, such as GRU networks and optimization algorithms, are established components in the domain; the main novelty of the present work lies in its synthesis and application, which aim to close an important gap in the forecasting of smart grids. All studies have either separated the forecasting of energy demand and the generation of renewables or minimized the influencing factors that they employed in their forecasting efforts. Our main contribution is the formulation of a unified hybrid model (GRU-MFSA) that can predict electricity consumption and hydropower production using economic, social, and climatic parameters within a single framework, thereby integrating disparate data domains to predict with both uncertainty and accuracy. This is not trivial because it requires a model that takes into consideration the complex nonlinear interactions between all these domains across time. Similarly, it constitutes a methodological novelty in applying the Future Search Algorithm to principles from PSO, particularly for hyperparameter tuning of the GRU network, thereby ensuring an optimal balance between accuracy and computational efficiency for the model. Therefore, the importance of this study lies not only in the application of existing tools, but also in demonstrating their synergistic mix to address a larger, more practically relevant energy forecasting problem; thus, providing a tool that is much more powerful in the hands of grid operators and policymakers.

The framework and design principle generated in this work includes the integration of a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) Neural Network with a Modified Future Search Algorithm (MFSA) to create an optimized model, in order to predict the electricity consumption and hydropower production in smart power grids.

The simplified version of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) for short time series prediction, with reset and update gates captured, known as Gated Recurrent Unit or GRU, is trained to learn both short and long temporal relationships and is suitable for time series prediction problems. On the contrary, the MFSA is an advanced optimization method that improves the accuracy of GRU by optimizing some hyperparameters like learning rate, number of neurons, and epochs. This leads to the model being able to learn very complicated relations in data that would still be computationally efficient. Specifically, the design principle pertains to developing a flexible and powerful model that can manage the complexities in the datasets of electricity consumption and hydropower. The proposed prediction model combines economic, social, and climate information.

Method and the experimental setup

Case study

The Qinshitan Dam, situated in the charming karst scenery of Guilin, is a major hydraulic facility in the Lijiang River basin in southwest China. It serves as both a power generator and a water source for the city. It has a reservoir capacity of 0.8 billion cubic meters and can generate 300 megawatts of power. Annually, it produces around 1.2 billion kilowatt-hours of electricity.

The dam encounters distinctive hydrological difficulties because of its karst watershed location. These difficulties consist of unpredictable streamflow patterns, leakage of groundwater, and restricted water storage capacity. With the looming threat of climate change, these challenges grow in significance, and potentially impact water accessibility and reliability for Guilin.

The dam experiences considerable temperature changes, with an average low of 8.6 °C in January and a high of 28.5 °C in July. The annual average temperature is 19.1 °C. Likewise, the patterns of rainfall varies, with December getting about 48.9 mm and June experiencing 356.7 mm. Generally, the Qinshitan Dam receives 1929.4 mm of precipitation each year, which leads to an average yearly streamflow of 1055.9 mm. For every 1 °C temperature increase, the average yearly evaporation increases by 9.3 mm.

Gated recurrent unit neural network

GRUs are a simplified type of Recurrent Neural Networks and have a shared origin with LSTM networks, as they have been created to tackle the same issue of vanishing gradients that RNNs face. GRUs simplify the structure found in LSTMs, and often results in improved calculational effectiveness and similar outcomes. Unlike LSTMs, GRUs use a single hidden state that effectively handles both immediate and long-term temporal dependencies, and eliminates the need for a separate cell state as in LSTMs. GRUs are distinguished by a more compact deep neural network structure, consisting of only two gates: the reset gate () and the update gate ()29. Modified future search algorithm (MFSA)

Future search (FS) algorithm

The universal desire for an enhanced quality of life motivates individuals to simulate the lifestyle of exceptional figures in order to enhance their well-being. This fundamental concept forms the basis of the improved exploration algorithm, which aims to find the best solutions. The exploration procedure is described using a statistical framework, where initial values are stochastically produced, and both local and global searches are carried out among candidates to improve solutions. Generally, metaheuristic algorithms begin with stochastic initialization and then proceed by iterating based on the optimal initial solution. If the best solution differs considerably from the global best solution, additional iterations are conducted to converge to the optimal solution. The FS algorithm deals with this issue by modifying stochastic initial values during every iteration. While the majority of metaheuristic algorithms distinguish the optimal local solution from numerous elements and a global solution through iterations, the updating solutions approach differs. Some algorithms only depend on the optimal local solution for updates, while others give priority to the optimal global solution. The selection of updating strategy can influence the quantity of iterations needed to achieve the best solution. The FS algorithm employs both the optimal local and global solutions to reveal the best results. Many metaheuristic algorithms involve complex statistical equations that can result in extended and increased iterations to attain the optimal solution. The suggested FSA, however, is on the basis of the simple statistical formulas. In this algorithm, the search space is associated to exploring candidates in different corners of the universe with the aim of reaching the highest quality of life.

A candidate who achieves the most efficient function in a country is the best local solution. Every year, there is a possibility that this solution can be enhanced by someone from another country. The better international solution is identified by the individual who consistently secures the most efficient function in a nation over multiple years. If individuals’ function results in a given year fail to match or surpass the quality of the former year, it will lead to a change in their main positions. Improved GRU (IGRU) network

In the design of deep neural networks, various hyperparameters, including the learning rate, number of neurons, and epochs are extremely important36. These factors impact how well the neural network fits the data, and also its performance during training37. Choosing the best values for these hyperparameters is crucial for improving the network’s performance38. The Modified Future Search Algorithm (MFSA) is an advanced optimization technique inspired by innovative predictive models39. It is known for its simplicity, easy implementation, and profound intelligent foundation. Appropriate for both academic research and industrial use, MFSA requires minimum adjustments40.

Utilizing MFSA’s outstanding global optimization ability along with neural networks can improve the networks’ capacity for learning and generalization, ultimately optimizing the general operational effectiveness of the neural network. In this investigation, MFSA is employed to identify the optimal hyperparameter values, and establish the most efficient structure for the network model. In MFSA, each specific hyperparameter configuration represents a distinct scenario. A scenario in this study includes an array containing (Neuron Count, Iterations, and Learning Rate). The MFSA procedure evaluates the performance index of each hyperparameter array in order to find the most suitable structure. Unlike the study conducted by Sahin et al.24, which utilized meteorological data in conjunction with a hybrid Genetic Grey Wolf Optimizer (GGWO)-CNN/RNN model for predicting hydropower generation, the current study integrates socioeconomic and climatic factors to assess both electricity demand and hydropower production. Utilizing MFSA for hyperparameter optimization enables the model to effectively tackle the scalability and accuracy issues prevalent in conventional methods. These improvements highlight the adaptability and effectiveness of the GRU-MFSA in managing intricate energy management challenges.

Comparative analysis with advanced methods

To provide for a more rigorous and statistically supported validation of our proposed GRU-MFSA model, an extensive comparative analysis was conducted against several advanced and state-of-the-art forecasting methods. Such a comparison would enable us to answer two pivotal questions: (1) Does the MFSA optimization give some benefit over the base GRU model? (2) Does our hybrid model surpass other competitive deep learning and optimized architectures? To this end, the competitor models considered were the standard GRU43, standard LSTM44, GRU model optimized with canonical Particle Swarm Optimization (GRU-PSO)45, and the Transformer model46. A fair competition was ensured by training, validating, and testing all models on the same dataset under the same conditions. A set of evaluation metrics encompassing various statistical measures was then deployed: MSE, RMSE, MAE, MAPE, and R2. Conclusion

This study developed an improved model that combined the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) Neural Network and the Modified Future Search Algorithm (MFSA) to enhance the efficiency of energy distribution and grid performance in smart power grids. GRU-MFSA outperformed traditional approaches with reduced error measures as Mean Squared Error (MSE) at training was 125, at validation was 2350, and at testing was 3985, whereas the correlation coefficients were 0.94, 0.85, and 0.81, respectively. The simulated results showed a steady increase in residential electricity demand from 2023 to 2040 as a result of population increase and technological progress, while the hydropower generation reduced steadily, indicating the need for better planning and integration of renewable resources. These results hold considerable practical significance, as they can assist policymakers in formulating sustainable energy strategies, enhancing grid infrastructure investments, and incorporating renewable energy sources to address increasing demands while minimizing environmental effects. Further research could improve the model’s applicability to diverse renewable energy sources, including wind and solar, incorporate real-time data for agile decision-making, and explore its potential uses in microgrid management or decentralized energy systems. Such advancements may contribute to the creation of more robust, flexible, and sustainable energy ecosystems globally.

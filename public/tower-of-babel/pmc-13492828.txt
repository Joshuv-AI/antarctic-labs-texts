A pathway to health: Spiritual health and health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with ASD
Authors: Faride Yousefizadeh, Mohaddeseh Ebrahimpour Roodposhti, Maryam Ebrahimpour Roodposhti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Caring for a child with ASD presents challenges for parents, affecting their health. Spiritual health can enhance health-promoting behaviors. This study examined the relationship between spiritual health and health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with ASD.MATERIALS AND METHODS:A cross-sectional study was conducted on 178 Iranian parents of children with ASD in 2024 using convenience sampling. Data were collected using demographic, spiritual health, and health-promoting lifestyle questionnaires. Descriptive statistics, independent t-test, one-way ANOVA, and Pearson correlation were used for analysis in SPSS22 with a statistical significance level set at P < 0.05.RESULTS:In this study, 46.6% of the parents were aged 30-40, and 89.9% of them were female. Doctors and health professionals (1.55) and the Internet (1.33) were the most common sources of health information for parents. The mean score of health-promoting behaviors for the parents of the study was 131.60 ± 24.42. The mean spiritual health of the parents was 86.14 ± 19.06. Spiritual health was low in 1.7% of the mothers, moderate in 71.3%, and high in 27%.CONCLUSION:This study found a significant relationship between spiritual health and health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with ASD. Parents with higher levels of spiritual health were more likely to engage in activities that support their overall health. However, a large number of parents report only a moderate level of spiritual health, which highlights the need for targeted interventions. Strategies such as spiritual counseling, spirituality-based programs, and support groups can help improve the spiritual health and health of these parents.

Introduction

Autism, or autism spectrum disorder (ASD), is a complex neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by deficits in social communication and the presence of restricted and repetitive behaviors and interests.[1] No biological marker for the diagnosis of ASD has hitherto been found.[2] The standardized criteria of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5), are used to identify and diagnose ASD. According to the DSM-5, autistic children must have impairments in all three domains of social communication and social interaction, including socioemotional interaction, nonverbal communication behaviors necessary for social interaction, and the ability to form, maintain, and understand relationships. Such children must also exhibit at least two of the four types of restricted and repetitive behaviors, including moving, speaking, and using objects in a familiar or repetitive manner, insistence on sameness, inflexible adherence to routines or ritualistic patterns in verbal or nonverbal behavior, very restricted and fixed interests with unusual focus and intensity, underreactivity or overreactivity to sensory input; or unusual interest in sensory aspects of the environment.[3]

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), approximately 1 in 100 children currently has ASD. In some studies, this number is higher, with approximately 1 in 59 children being diagnosed with ASD.[45] The average prevalence of ASD in Asia since 1980 has been reported to be 14.8 per 10,000 children.[6] ASD is also known to be a common disorder among children in Iran, and 1 out of every 150 children in Iran is affected by autism.[7]

Parents have an important role in the care of children with autism, providing daily support and necessary interventions to these children.[8] Significant health challenges, including physical and psychological problems, are often experienced by parents of children with ASD.[9] One of the most important dimensions of health is spiritual health, which as an essential component of overall health, complements physical, mental, and social health. Spiritual health is a state in which people are able to effectively cope with the challenges of their daily lives and thereby realize their full potential, meaning, and purpose in life. Although spiritual health may not directly cure disease, it can play an important role in influencing behaviors and lifestyle choices and can act as a preventive intervention, helping people cope with challenges, live a meaningful life, and engage in health-promoting behaviors.[10] Health-promoting behaviors are actions that improve health, prevent disease, and reduce healthcare costs. These behaviors include a wide range of activities that lead to an optimal level of health, self-actualization, and personal satisfaction.[11]

Literature review found no studies examining the relationship between spiritual health and health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with autism. In Pandya’s study, which reviewed similar research using a cross-country pre–post experimental design with a sample of 1,687 parents of children with ASD from 15 countries, the spiritual educational intervention resulted in increased resilience and a greater sense of coherence, along with improvements in dysfunction among the participants.[12] In Pearson’s qualitative study using grounded theory, faith, spirituality, and religious participation may enhance stress-related coping for parents of autistic children.[13] In a study of 180 Korean mothers of children with cerebral palsy, Lee showed that spiritual growth and stress reduction may mediate the relationship between parental stress and health-related quality of life in these mothers.[14] In Shahdadi’s study, which was conducted qualitatively using the conventional content analysis method, parents of children with disabilities used spiritual self-care to cope with the challenges of their child’s disability.[15]

Given the critical role parents play in caring for children with ASD and the significant physical and mental health challenges they face, it is very important to know the elements that enhance their health. Previous studies have highlighted the importance of spirituality in improving resilience, coping strategies, and overall quality of life for parents of children with disabilities. However, no study has specifically examined the relationship between spiritual health and health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with ASD. An understanding of this relationship can provide important insights into how spiritual health can help to promote a healthier lifestyle and increase the overall health of this group of people Therefore, this study aimed to determine the relationship between spiritual health and health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with ASD.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This descriptive-analytical study was conducted in Qom, Iran, from June 2024 to September 2024. The study focused on parents of children with autism attending autism centers in the city. The study aimed to investigate the relationship between health-promoting behaviors, health literacy, and spiritual health.

Study participants and sampling

The study population included parents of children diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). Owing to the lack of access to a complete sampling frame and limited cooperation from some centers (particularly private ones), a convenience sampling method was used. A total of 178 parents were recruited from waiting rooms of autism centers, where they accompanied their children. Participants were selected if they met the inclusion criteria, and informed consent was obtained from each participant prior to data collection.

Sample size estimation was conducted using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.4), considering a 95% confidence level, 85% statistical power, and a minimum correlation coefficient of 0.2 between health-promoting behavior and the independent variables (health literacy and spiritual health). This calculation determined a minimum sample size of 177 participants [see Figure 1].

Data collection and tools

Data were collected using structured, self-administered questionnaires distributed in the waiting areas of the autism centers. Before completing the questionnaires, participants were briefed about the study’s purpose and procedure, and they were assured of the voluntary nature of participation and confidentiality of their information.

Demographic Information Form: A demographic information form was used in this study to determine the parent’s age (years), sex, education level, occupation, and information center.

Spiritual Health Scale: The Paloutzian and Ellison Spiritual Health Scale, the validity and reliability of which was confirmed by Soltani et al. (1400), was used in the present study to assess spiritual health. The validity and reliability of the questionnaire were confirmed with Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.86.[16] This 20-item scale has two sections of religious and existential health, with 10 items in the religious section and 10 in the existential section. The questions are scored based on a 6-point Likert scale including strongly disagree (6), moderately disagree (5), disagree (4), agree (3), moderately agree (2), and strongly agree (1). The spiritual health score is the sum of the two subscores, with a range of 20-120. The higher the score, the more will be the spiritual health.[17]

Health-promoting Lifestyle Questionnaire: A 54-item questionnaire is used in this study to investigate health-promoting behaviors. The questions are scored based on a 4-point Likert-type (never: 1 to always: 4). This questionnaire has 6 dimensions, including nutrition (1-11), physical activity (12-24), health responsibility (25-32), stress management (33-38), interpersonal support (39-46), and self-actualization (47-54). The lowest and highest scores of the questionnaire are 54 and 216, respectively. Mohammadi Zeidi et al. (2011) confirmed the validity and reliability of the questionnaire.[18]

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the Research Council of Qom University of Medical Sciences (code: IR.MUQ.REC.1402.039). Before data collection, informed consent was obtained from all participants. Participation was voluntary, and confidentiality of participants’ data was strictly maintained throughout the study.

Statistical analysis method

Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 22. Descriptive statistics were calculated for demographic variables and questionnaire scores. The independent t-test and one-way ANOVA were used to examine differences between groups. The Pearson correlation coefficient was applied to assess relationships between variables. A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Based on the results of the present study, there was a significant and direct relationship between spiritual health and all dimensions of health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with ASD. This suggests that higher levels of spiritual health are associated with the adoption of health-promoting behaviors by these parents. According to our knowledge, few studies have no studies examining the relationship between spiritual health and health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with autism were identified in a review of the literature. In a review of similar studies, Pandya found that a spiritual training intervention increased participants’ resilience, sense of coherence, and functioning.[12] In Pearson’s study, faith, spirituality, and religious involvement were found to improve stress-related coping for parents of children with ASD.[13] In a study of mothers of children with cerebral palsy, Lee found that spiritual growth and stress reduction acted as mediators in the association between parental stress and health-related quality of life.[14] In Shahdadi’s study, parents of children with disabilities used spiritual self-care to cope with the challenges of their child’s disability.[15] The role of spirituality is emphasized in promoting positive health outcomes for parents experiencing chronic stress in these studies that are consistent with the findings of this study. It is suggested by this result that spiritual health enables individuals to maintain a positive outlook on their health despite the challenges of caregiving. Spiritual health is associated with psychological health, reduced stress, and increased social support, all of which can contribute to greater engagement in health-promoting behaviors such as physical activity, nutrition, and stress management.

According to the results of this study, most parents of children with ASD had a moderate level of spiritual health (71.3%), while a smaller percentage had a high level of spiritual health (27%). Similar to our study, the Khodaverdi’s study found that 83% of the parents of children with ASD had a moderate level of spiritual health and 16% had a high level of spiritual health.[19] In Soltani’s study, most parents also reported moderate levels of spiritual health.[16]

These findings suggest that while some parents have adequate levels of spiritual health, many may not be taking full advantage of the potential benefits of spirituality for stress management and health improvement.

These findings highlight the need for targeted interventions aimed at enhancing spiritual health in parents of children with ASD, given the important role that spiritual health plays in resilience, emotional health, and stress management. Moderate levels of spiritual health may be an indication that while some parents find meaning and support in spirituality, others may not reap its benefits. Spiritual interventions can help parents better understand their life goals and cope with the emotional and physical demands of caregiving, including mindfulness programs, spiritual counseling, and faith-based support groups.

Conclusion

These results underscore the important and direct link between spiritual Health and healthy behaviors among parents of children with ASD. Higher levels of spiritual health are associated with greater levels of participation in activities that promote overall health, highlighting the potential impact of spirituality in reducing the difficulties faced by these parents. By explicitly analyzing the relationship between spiritual health and health-promoting behaviors in parents of children with ASD, this study adds a unique perspective to the existing literature. Despite the positive correlation found, the results indicate that a significant number of parents have only moderate levels of spiritual health. This highlights the need for specific interventions for the improvement of the spiritual health of these parents. Parents can integrate spirituality more seamlessly into their daily lives, leading to better stress management and health outcomes, through approaches such as spiritual counseling, spirituality-based programs, and spirituality-based support groups.

Limitations and recommendation

Despite the researchers’ efforts to carefully design and conduct this study, this study also faced some limitations. First, the data in this study were collected through self-report questionnaires, which can be affected by respondent bias, especially on topics such as spiritual beliefs or health-related behaviors that may be mixed with social norms. To reduce this effect, standardized questionnaires with confirmed validity and reliability were used, and the confidentiality of the responses was also guaranteed to the participants. Second, the limited geographical and cultural scope of the samples may affect the generalizability of the results. To overcome this limitation, it is recommended that larger samples with greater geographical and cultural dispersion be used in future studies.

Early Universe production of W W bosons in neutrino decays
Amalia Dariana Fodor, Andru Mihai Buga, Cosmin Crucean

Abstract
In this paper we study, via perturbative methods, the rates of production of W W bosons emitted in neutrino decays during the early stages of the Universe. We compute the transition amplitude corresponding to the first order of de Sitter electroweak perturbation theory and study its various limiting cases. The transition rates are derived using minimal subtraction and dimensional regularization. In the end we attempt to obtain the density number of W W bosons produced in perturbative transitions in de Sitter spacetime, using the rates obtained in this paper and in previous ones, and analyze the density number with respect to the particle momenta and renormalisation mass μ \mu .

The transition amplitude for transversal Proca modes

The present work is done in the de Sitter geometry described by the metric

ds^{2}=dt^{2}-e^{2\omega t}d\vec{x}^{2}=\frac{1}{(\omega t_{c})^{2}}(dt_{c}^{2}-d\vec{x}^{2}),

For the line element (

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[{\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{ig}{2\sqrt{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g(x)}\,\bar{u}_{\vec{p}\sigma}(x)\gamma^{\hat{i}}e^{j}_{\hat{i}}(1-\gamma^{5})u_{\vec{p^{\prime}}\sigma^{\prime}}^{0}(x)f_{\vec{P}\lambda=\pm 1,\,j}^{*}(x).

We solve the temporal integral in terms of

3
The transition rate

In this section we want to obtain the dependence of the partial transition rate on the ratio between the particles masses and Hubble constant. For this purpose we rewrite the amplitude in the following general form

A_{if}=\delta^{3}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}-\vec{p}-\vec{P})M_{if}I_{if},

(12)

where the temporal integral is denoted by
I_{if}
and can be written as

I_{if}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\mathcal{K}_{if},

(13)

with
\mathcal{K}_{if}
being the integrand of the temporal integral. In addition,
M_{if}
represents the the rest of the factors present in amplitude (
5

The transition rate per unit volume in the conformal chart is defined as follows:

\displaystyle R_{if}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\lambda}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\delta^{3}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}-\vec{p}-\vec{P}\,)|M_{if}|^{2}|I_{if}|\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|e^{\omega t}\mathcal{K}_{if}|.

(14)

An extended discussion about the definition of the transition rate in de Sitter Universe can be found in
[
68
,
71

. We also mention that the first general definition of this rate was given in
[
59
]
, where the analogy with the definition of the transition rate in Minkowski spacetime was also discussed.

In order to write down the quantities necessary to define the transition rate, we rewrite the amplitude in terms of Hankel and Bessel
K

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{ige^{\pi k/2}}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sqrt{p}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma^{\prime}\right)\delta^{3}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}-\vec{p}-\vec{P})\frac{2i}{\pi}

\displaystyle\times\int z\,dz\,e^{ip^{\prime}z}H^{(2)}_{\frac{1}{2}+ik}(pz)K_{-iK}(iPz)

\displaystyle\times\xi_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p})\sigma^{j}\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime})\epsilon_{j}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1).

(15)

The transition rate is relevant when
\omega\gg M_{W}
and
\omega\gg m_{e}
. Thus we can further approximate the index of the modified Bessel function
K_{-iK}(iPz)
such that

25
]

-i\sqrt{\left(\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4\left(\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}\right)^{2}}\approx\frac{1}{2}.

(16)

An analytical result for the transition rate can be obtained if the Bessel
K

K_{\nu}(z)\simeq 2^{\nu-1}\Gamma(\nu)/z^{\nu}

K_{1/2}\left(\frac{Pe^{-\omega t}}{\omega}\right)\simeq\frac{\Gamma(1/2)}{\sqrt{2i\frac{Pe^{-\omega t}}{\omega}}}.

(17)

Hankel functions can also be approximated for small arguments using
H^{(2)}_{\nu}(z)\simeq i2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu)/\pi z^{\nu}

25
]

H^{(2)}_{1/2+ik}\left(\frac{pe^{-\omega t}}{\omega}\right)\simeq\frac{-i\Gamma(1/2+ik)}{\pi}\left(\frac{2}{\frac{pe^{-\omega t}}{\omega}}\right)^{1/2+ik}.

(18)

Then the limit of the integrand can be computed by using the above approximations, which still preserve the dependence on parameters

and
K

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}\big|e^{\omega t}\mathcal{K}_{if}\big|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{pP\cosh(\pi k)}}.

(19)

With (

\displaystyle R_{if}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda\sigma}\frac{g^{2}\pi^{2}pp^{\prime}}{32(2\pi)^{3}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p^{\prime}}-\vec{p}-\vec{P})\frac{|I_{if}|}{\sqrt{Pp\cosh(\pi k)}}

\displaystyle\times|\xi_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p})\sigma^{i}\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}){\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1)|^{2}

where

The result for the temporal integral is:

I_{if}=\frac{e^{i\pi/4}e^{\pi k/2}}{i\sqrt{p^{\prime}}\cosh(\pi k)}(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}),

(21)

For the sake of examining the behavior of the transition rate, it is useful to perform a graphical analysis of the terms which depend on the ratios of the particle masses and the Hubble parameter. We can group all relevant terms into a single quantity:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{pp^{\prime}}e^{\pi k/2}}{\cosh{(\pi k)}}\sqrt{\frac{(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4})(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4})^{*}}{P\cosh{(\pi k)}}}.

3.1
The transition rate in the limit of large expansion

The present subsection is dedicated to the computation of the total rate of transition of the process in the limit when
m/\omega=M_{W}/\omega=0
, which corresponds to the large expansion conditions of the Early Universe.

In this particular limit, the transition amplitude (
5
) for the process
\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}
depends only on Hankel functions of order
1/2
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[{\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{4\sqrt{2}}\frac{\pi\sqrt{p}e^{-i\pi/4}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma^{\prime}\right)\delta^{3}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}-\vec{p}-\vec{P})

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z\,e^{ip\,^{\prime}z}H_{1/2}^{(2)}(pz)H_{1/2}^{(1)}(Pz)\xi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{n}_{\mathcal{P}},\lambda)\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}).

(23)

This expression is suitable for analytical computations, since the special functions in the general case reduce to simple polynomial functions of the particle momenta.
In this case the temporal integral can be solved by adding a factor of
\exp(-\epsilon z)
, with
\epsilon>0
, which leads to:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[{\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{4\sqrt{2}}\frac{\pi\sqrt{p}e^{-i\pi/4}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma^{\prime}\right)\delta^{3}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}-\vec{p}-\vec{P})\,\xi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{n}_{\mathcal{P}},\lambda)\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime})

\displaystyle\times\frac{-2i}{\pi\sqrt{pP}(p^{\prime}-p-P)}

where, if we use the notations from (
12

\displaystyle M_{if}=\frac{g}{4\sqrt{2}}\frac{\pi\sqrt{p}e^{-i\pi/4}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma^{\prime}\right)\xi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{n}_{\mathcal{P}},\lambda)\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}),

(25)

while the result for the temporal integral is:

\displaystyle I_{if}=\frac{-2i}{\pi\sqrt{pP}(p^{\prime}-p-P)}.

(26)

The factor
|M_{if}|^{2}
has to be summed over the final polarizations of the particles. For computing this sum we will consider that the particles in the final state are emitted in the same direction as the neutrino in the initial state. The neutrino and
W
boson will have the same orientation, while the electron will have opposite orientation, i.e.
\vec{P}=-P\vec{e}_{3};\,\vec{p}=p\vec{e}_{3};\,\vec{p}\,^{\prime}=-p^{\prime}\vec{e}_{3}
. In this case the bispinor summation gives only one non-vanishing term, the one corresponding to
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma=-1/2
:

\xi^{{\dagger}}_{-1/2}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}_{-1}(\vec{P},\lambda)\xi_{-1/2}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime})=\sqrt{2}.

(27)

Next we replace (
25

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|e^{\omega t}K_{if}|

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\biggl|e^{\omega t}e^{-\omega t}e^{i(p^{\prime}-P-p)\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{\omega}}\frac{i\pi}{\sqrt{Pp}}\biggl|

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{Pp}},

(28)

in the transition rate formula defined earlier (

Consequently we reach the final form of the transition rate in the large expansion limit:

R_{if}=\frac{g^{2}\pi}{8(2\pi)^{6}}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}-\vec{p}-\vec{P})}{P(p^{\prime}-p-P)}.

(29)

The total transition rate in the limit of large expansion is obtained by integrating after the final momenta of the electron and
W^{+}
boson:

\displaystyle R_{\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}}=\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}P\,R_{if}

(30)

One of the momentum integrals can be eliminated by using the Dirac Delta function:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}P\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}-\vec{p}-\vec{P})}{P(p^{\prime}-p-P)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}P\frac{1}{2P(p^{\prime}-P)},

(31)

where the above result is obtained by taking the momenta orientations discussed earlier in this section.

The remaining integral is divergent, which means we have to use regularization methods
[

,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
to obtain a finite result. We start with dimensional regularization
[
32
,
34
]
, and rewrite integral (

) in the general case of

dimensions:

\displaystyle I(D)=\frac{2\pi^{D/2}}{2P(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dP\frac{P^{D-2}}{p^{\prime}-P}\,.

(32)

By making the substitution
P=-p^{\prime}y
, we arrive at the integral which defines the Beta Euler function:

\displaystyle I(D)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{D/2}(-p^{\prime})^{D-2}}{2(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{D-1}}{1+y}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{D/2}(-p^{\prime})^{D-2}\Gamma(D-1)\Gamma(4-D)}{(2\pi)^{D}2\Gamma(D/2)(2-D)(3-D)}.

(33)

The above result is still divergent for
D=3
, due to the factor
(3-D)^{-1}
. A way for solving this divergence is to apply the minimal subtraction method
[
34
]
. Firstly we compute the residue of the integral in
D=3
:

\displaystyle\text{Res}\,I(D)=\lim_{D\rightarrow 3}(3-D)I(D)=-\frac{p^{\prime}}{4\pi^{2}}.

(34)

Afterwards we can define the regularized integral as:

\displaystyle I(D)_{r}

\displaystyle=I(D)-\frac{p^{\prime}\mu^{D-3}}{4\pi^{2}(3-D)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3-D}\bigg(\frac{2\pi^{D/2}(p^{\prime})^{D-2}\Gamma(D-1)\Gamma(4-D)}{2P(2\pi)^{D}(2-D)\Gamma(D/2)}+\frac{p^{\prime}\mu^{D-3}}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg),

(35)

where the counter-term

\frac{\mu^{s}\,\text{Res}\,I(D)}{3-D}

(36)

with
s=D-3
and mass parameter
\mu
has been chosen such that it has the same dimension as
I(D)
.

The expansion around the value
D=3
of the paranthesis in (
35
) will result in terms that cancel out the divergent factor
(3-D)^{-1}
:

\displaystyle I(D)_{r}=\,\frac{1}{3-D}\times\Bigg\{\frac{p^{\prime}(3-D)}{8\pi^{2}}\bigg[2-\gamma+\ln\Big(\frac{\pi\mu^{2}}{p^{\prime 2}}\Big)\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\big((D-3)^{2}\big)\Bigg\}.

(37)

It is important to point out that the same result for the regularized integral can be obtained from equation (
31
) if we solve first for the integral over
P
, and then the one over
p
.

The term in front of parenthesis will simplify the factor
D-3
of the first term, while terms proportional with
(D-3)^{2}
will vanish when taking the limit
D=3
. The transition rate is finally:

\displaystyle R_{\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}}=\frac{G_{F}M_{W}^{2}\,p^{\prime}}{8\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg[2-\gamma+\ln\Big(\frac{\pi\mu^{2}}{p^{\prime 2}}\Big)\bigg].

(38)

Relation (
38
) represents the total decay rate for the process
\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}
in the limit of large expansion, which corresponds to the Early Universe. A similar derivation can be done for the transition rate of the antineutrino decay
\widetilde{\nu}\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+}
. The outcome is that the total rates for both of these transitions are equal in this limit:

R_{\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}}=R_{\widetilde{\nu}\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+}}.

(39)

4
Density number of particles

In this section we will demonstrate how to obtain the density number of particles from the transition rates established in the previous section, and also in previous papers
[
68
,
71
]
which discuss other transitions involving
W
bosons in de Sitter spacetime. Since in the conditions of rapid expansion of the Early Universe the notion of thermal equilibrium is not well defined, we cannot define the density number of
W
bosons obtained in neutrino decays in of the density number of neutrinos. This is because the density numbers of neutrinos obtained so far were computed using the distribution function and the hypothesis of thermal equilibrium. For these reasons we will define the density number of
W
bosons as the ratio between the production rate and the decay rate for the
W
boson, since this definition establishes a clear picture of the balance between the production and decay of
W
bosons in the early universe. Then we can use the general equation that gives the variation of the density number in unit volume and in unit time, which has the meaning of the total decay rate per unit volume
[
58
]
:

\frac{dN}{dt\,dV}=\frac{R_{\text{transition}}}{V}.

(40)

In order to have a clear picture of the number of
W
bosons that are actually produced, the balance between the production processes and the decay processes must be taken into account. The number of
W
bosons produced in perturbtive electroweak interactions in the Early Universe can be computed by substracting the total decay rate from the total production rate, both computed in the de Sitter metric by taking the limit
\text{mass}/\omega\rightarrow 0
. Our proposal is to define the number of bosons in a time interval and in a volume interval, since the background is expanding. For that we use equation (
40
):

\displaystyle\frac{dN}{dt\,dV}=\frac{R_{\text{production}}(W)-R_{\text{decay}}(W)}{V}

(41)

where,
R_{\text{production}}(W)
is the total rate of production of
W
bosons in perturbative processes in de Sitter spacetime, while
R_{\text{decay}}(W)
is the total decay rate for the
W
bosons in de Sitter spacetime.

The total rate of production is obtained by adding the first order electroweak transitions in which
W
bosons are produced in the de Sitter universe. That includes the production of
W
bosons from vacuum
[
68
]
and the production of
W
bosons in emission processes by fermions
[
71
]
, these processes being possible only in the de Sitter metric. The decays for the three families of neutrinos were studied in the present paper, in the previous sections, with the mention that the results for the transition rates are the same for each neutrino family. The same applies to all three leptonic families which can emit
W
bosons
[
71
]
. These processes are written below for the
W^{-}
boson:

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{e}\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+}\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\quad e^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu_{e}

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{\mu}\rightarrow W^{-}+\mu^{+}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\mu^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu_{\mu}

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{\tau}\rightarrow W^{-}+\tau^{+}\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\quad\tau^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu_{\tau},

and can be obtained also for
W^{+}
, the transition rates remaining the same.

To the above production processes one must add the spontaneous production of
W^{\pm}
bosons from the de Sitter vacuum
[
68
]
:

\displaystyle vac\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+}+\nu_{e}

\displaystyle vac\rightarrow W^{-}+\mu^{+}+{\nu}_{\mu}

\displaystyle vac\rightarrow W^{-}+\tau^{+}+{\nu}_{\tau}.

Summing all these contributions we obtain the total transition rate of production for
W
bosons:

\displaystyle R_{\text{production}}(W^{-})=3\left[R_{vac\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+}+\nu_{e}}+R_{e^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu_{e}}+R_{\tilde{\nu}_{e}\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+}}\right],

(42)

where the numerical factor 3 stands for all the leptonic possible transitions. We also mention that the same total production rate is obtained for the
W^{+}
boson, i.e.
R_{\text{production}}(W^{-})=R_{\text{production}}(W^{+})
.

The transition rates for emission of
W
bosons from electrons
[
71
]
and spontaneous production of
W
bosons from vacuum
[
68
]
are given below:

\displaystyle R_{vac\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+}+\nu_{e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,G_{F}M_{W}^{2}\,}{8\sqrt{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{M_{W}}{2}\bigg[\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)\bigg],

(43)

\displaystyle R_{e^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu_{e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,G_{F}M_{W}^{2}\,p}{8\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg[2-\gamma+\ln\Big(\frac{\pi\mu^{2}}{p^{2}}\Big)\bigg].

(44)

When computing the total decay rate for the
W
bosons, the decay into lepton-antilepton pairs and quark-antiquark pairs must be taken into account. In the Minkowski field theory the total decay rate of the
W
boson is
R_{d}=2.5
GeV
[
61
]
, with the observation that the decay into quarks accounts for
60
% of the total decay rate
[
61
]
.

The total decay rate for the
W
boson will comprise of two terms, one that will take into account the leptonic decays
R_{W^{\rightarrow}l+\widetilde{l}}
, and one that will take into account the decays into quark-antiquark pairs
R_{W\rightarrow q+\bar{q}}

[
61
]
.

In the case of leptonic decays, the total transition rate in the conditions of the Early Universe is
[
71
]
:

\displaystyle R_{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu}}=R_{W^{+}\rightarrow e^{+}+\nu}=\frac{G_{F}M_{W}^{2}\,P}{4\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg[\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{P^{2}}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)\bigg],

(45)

with the observation that, in the first order of perturbation theory, the partial widths are equal for all possible decays
[
61
]
:

\displaystyle W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\tilde{\nu}_{e}\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\quad W^{+}\rightarrow e^{+}+\nu_{e}

\displaystyle W^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}+\tilde{\nu}_{\mu}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad W^{+}\rightarrow\mu^{+}+\nu_{\mu}

\displaystyle W^{-}\rightarrow\tau^{-}+\tilde{\nu}_{\tau}\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\quad W^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}+\nu_{\tau}.

Our decay rate into leptons must be multiplied by a factor of
3
if we take into account just one of the charged bosons, and by a factor of
6
if we take into account both charged bosons.

The decays of
W
bosons into quark-antiquark pairs in de Sitter spacetime have not been studied so far, but the main step will be to consider the Lagrangian of interaction with quarks, adapted to a curved background. Since in the Minkowski field theory, the
W
boson decay into quarks-antiquarks
R_{W\rightarrow q+\bar{q}}
accounts for
60\,\%
of the total decay rate, we will consider that the same principle applies in de Sitter spacetime. Therefore we can write:

R_{W\rightarrow q+\bar{q}}=\frac{6}{4}R_{W^{\rightarrow}l+\widetilde{l}}\,.

(46)

That being the case, we can express the total decay rate of
W^{+}
bosons in terms of the
W^{+}
rate of decay into leptons:

\displaystyle R_{\text{decay}}(W^{-})=R_{\text{decay}}(W^{+})=\frac{10}{4}R_{W^{\rightarrow}l+\widetilde{l}}=\frac{30}{4}R_{W^{+}\rightarrow e^{+}+\nu_{e}}=\frac{30}{4}R_{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu_{e}}},

(47)

where the last equality was obtained by considering all three leptonic decays of the
W^{+}
boson in de Sitter spacetime,
R_{W^{+}\rightarrow l+\widetilde{l}}=3R_{W^{+}\rightarrow e^{+}+\nu}
.

Then the density number of
W^{-}
bosons in time interval
\Delta t
, and volume interval
\Delta V
, can be estimated with the help of formulas (
40
) and (
47
):

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta N_{W^{-}}}{\Delta t\,\Delta V}=\frac{3\,G_{F}M_{W}^{2}\,}{8\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}(2\pi)^{3}V}\left\{\frac{\pi^{2}M_{W}}{2}\bigg[\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)\bigg]+p\bigg[2-\gamma+\ln\Big(\frac{\pi\mu^{2}}{p^{2}}\Big)\bigg]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+p^{\prime}\bigg[2-\gamma+\ln\Big(\frac{\pi\mu^{2}}{p^{\prime 2}}\Big)\bigg]-5P\bigg[\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{P^{2}}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)\bigg]\right\}.

(48)

The density number of
W^{-}
bosons must be equal with the density number of
W^{+}
bosons, since the decay rates for both charged bosons are equal, while the rates of decay for neutrinos and antineutrinos are also equal
R_{\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}}=R_{\tilde{\nu}\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+}}
. This leads to the total density number of
W
bosons in time interval
\Delta t
, and volume interval
\Delta V
being:

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta N_{W}}{\Delta t\,\Delta V}=2\frac{\Delta N_{W^{-}}}{\Delta t\,\Delta V},

(49)

since there are equal probabilities for spontaneous production of
W^{-}
and
W^{+}
bosons in various perturbative processes.

The ratio between the density numbers of produced
W
bosons in perturbative processes and the density number of bosons that decay is then:

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta N_{W^{-}}}{\Delta N_{D,W^{-}}}=\frac{\frac{\pi^{2}M_{W}}{2}\bigg[\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)\bigg]+p\bigg[2-\gamma+\ln\Big(\frac{\pi\mu^{2}}{p^{2}}\Big)\bigg]+p^{\prime}\bigg[2-\gamma+\ln\Big(\frac{\pi\mu^{2}}{p^{\prime 2}}\Big)\bigg]}{5P\bigg[\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{P^{2}}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)\bigg]},

(50)

where
N_{D,W^{-}}
is the number of
W
bosons that decay in leptons and quarks. In the above equation the conditions
4\pi\mu^{2}\geq M_{W}^{2}\,;\pi\mu^{2}\geq p^{2}\,;\pi\mu^{2}\geq p^{\prime 2}
are mandatory in the logarithms such that we avoid negative values.

From equations (
48
) and (
49
) it can be seen that the density number of
W
bosons is a quantity which depends on the particle momenta and the regularization parameter
\mu
. Another consequence is that the density number of produced
W
bosons begins to drop as the Universe expanded and the decay rate began to increase. This happens when the energy of the background becomes smaller than the rest energy of the
W
bosons. This fact can also be seen from a graphical analysis of the density numbers.

In our analysis we have taken into account only the transversal modes of the Proca field in de Sitter spacetime
[
5
]
. This choice is justified by the fact that, at earlier times and high temperatures, the mass of the
W

W

\omega

M_{W}[T(\omega)]
.

In the end of this section, we must point out that the present density numbers account for the perturbative treatment of the
W
boson production in the Early Universe, and do not take into account other possible mechanisms of
W
boson generation. Our results are exclusively the consequence of the large expansion in the early moments of the Universe.

5
Conclusions

In this paper we analyze the production of charged
W^{\pm}
bosons in emission processes by neutrinos and antineutrinos. This phenomenon received little attention in the literature, and our study is the first to take into account neutrino decays as a source of producing massive
W^{\pm}
bosons.
Our analytical and graphical results prove that such transitions in the first order of electroweak theory, in de Sitter spacetime, can occur only in the large expansion conditions of the Early Universe,
when the background energy was larger than the rest energy of the
W
bosons. Our graphical analysis also reveals that we obtain the Minkowski limit of neutrino decays, i.e. the probabilities and transition rates become zero when the Hubble constant vanishes
(\omega=0)
. This confirms the fact that the emission of
W
bosons by neutrinos (antineutrinos),
\nu\rightarrow W^{+}+e^{-}
(
\widetilde{\nu}\rightarrow W^{-}+e^{+})
, is forbidden in flat spacetime.

Another important result is related to the large expansion limit, when
m/\omega=M_{W}/\omega=0
, and our equations simplify. In this limit, we applied dimensional regularization and minimal subtraction in order to extract finite results for our total transition rates. Using previous results related to the decay and production of
W^{\pm}
bosons in de Sitter spacetime, (
[
68
]
and
[
71
]
), we managed to obtain the density number of
W
bosons as a function of the particle momenta and renormalisation mass
\mu
. The density number obtained with our perturbative methods is finite in the limit of large expansion from the Early Universe, i.e.
\omega\gg M_{W}
.

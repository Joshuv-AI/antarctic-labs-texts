New Upper bounds for KL-divergence Based on Integral Norms
Liu-Quan Yao, Song-Hao Liu

Abstract
In this paper, some new upper bounds for Kullback-Leibler divergence(KL-divergence) based on L 1 , L 2 L^{1},L^{2} and L ∞ L^{\infty} norms of density functions are discussed. Our findings unveil that the convergence in KL-divergence sense sandwiches between the convergence of density functions in terms of L 1 L^{1} and L 2 L^{2} norms. Furthermore, we endeavor to apply our newly derived upper bounds to the analysis of the rate theorem of the entropic conditional central limit theorem.



1
Introduction

Pinsker’s inequality, a well-known inequality, shows that the KL-divergence effectively governs the total variation distance between two random variables, thus establishing a profound connection between the information-entropy theorem and classical probability theory. Specifically, considering two probability measures denoted by
P
and
Q
, defined on a common measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
, the Pinsker’s inequality (see in
[
7
]
for example) states that:

\text{KL}(P\|Q)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|P-Q\|_{TV}^{2},

(1.1)

where the KL-divergence is defined as
\text{KL}(P\|Q):=\int_{\Omega}\ln(\frac{dP}{dQ})dP
, while the total variation distance is defined as
\|P-Q\|_{TV}:=2\sup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}|P(A)-Q(A)|
.

On the one hand, it is evident that if
P
and
Q
are discrete probability measures over a countably infinite set
B
, we obtain:

\|P-Q\|_{TV}=2\sup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}|P(A)-Q(A)|=\sum_{x\in B}|P(x)-Q(x)|.

(1.2)

On the other hand, if
P
and
Q
are continuous probability measures on the real line
\mathbb{R}
, with densities
p
and
q
, respectively, we have:

\|P-Q\|_{TV}=2\sup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}|P(A)-Q(A)|=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|p(x)-q(x)|dx=\|p-q\|_{1}.

(1.3)

This relationship of control can be easily extended to the sequential case that as
n\to\infty
,

\text{KL}(P_{n}\|Q)\to 0\Rightarrow\|P_{n}-Q\|_{TV}\to 0.

As a result, the use of entropy methods in classical probability theorems, such as the entropic central limit theorem, has garnered significant attention in numerous research works (
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
15
]

et al.
).

In recent researches, the upper bounds for KL-divergence was discussed frequently, since KL-divergence is a common measure for random variables in practical applications (
[
11
]
,
[
1
]
and
[
19
]

et al.
). One of the important series of studies for the upper bounds is the reverse Pinsker’s inequality.
Sason (2015)
[
17
]
built a reverse Pinsker’s inequality for general probability measures
P,Q
as follows.

Lemma 1.1

If
P\ll Q
and let
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in[0,1]
be given by

\beta_{1}^{-1}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}\frac{dP}{dQ}(x),\;\;\beta_{2}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}\frac{dP}{dQ}(x).

(1.4)

Then

\text{KL}(P\|Q)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\ln\beta_{1}}{1-\beta_{1}}-\beta_{2}\right)\|P-Q\|_{TV}.

(1.5)

Sason also discussed the discrete case in
[
17
]
that

Lemma 1.2

If
P,Q
are discrete probability measures on a countably infinite set
B
, then

\text{KL}(P\|Q)\leq\ln\left(1+\frac{\|P-Q\|_{TV}^{2}}{2\min_{x\in B}Q(x)}\right).

(1.6)

For two probability measure
P
and
Q
defined on a common measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
such that
P\ll Q
,
we say that
(P,Q)\in\mathcal{A}(\delta,m,M)
if

\mbox{ess}_{Q}\inf\frac{dP}{dQ}=m,\;\;\mbox{ess}_{Q}\sup\frac{dP}{dQ}=M,\;\;\|P-Q\|_{TV}=\delta,

(1.7)

where
\mbox{ess}_{Q}\inf f:=\sup\{b\in\mathbb{R}|Q(\{x:f(x)<b\})=0\},\mbox{ess}_{Q}\sup f:=\inf\{b\in\mathbb{R}|Q(\{x:f(x)>b\})=0\}
.
Binette(2019)
[
4
]
showed a reverse Pinsker’s inequality by an equality form as follows.

Lemma 1.3

If
\delta\geq 0,m\geq 0,M<\infty
and
\mathcal{A}(\delta,m,M)\neq\emptyset
, then

\sup_{(P,Q)\in\mathcal{A}(\delta,m,M)}D_{f}(P\|Q)=\delta\left(\frac{f(m)}{1-m}+\frac{f(M)}{M-1}\right),

(1.8)

where
f
is any measurable function such that
f:[0,\infty)\to(-\infty,\infty]
,
f(1)=0
and

D_{f}(P\|Q):=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[f\left(\frac{dP}{dQ}\right)\right].

(1.9)

If
f(t)=t\ln t
, then
D_{f}(P\|Q)=\text{KL}(P\|Q).

In addition, Berend and Kontorovich(2012)
[
3
]
discussed the minimum KL-divergence conditional on some distances in total variation distance sense, that is,

Lemma 1.4

Given a probability measure
P
on measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
and define

\beta:=\inf\{P(A);A\in\mathcal{F},P(A)\geq 1/2\}.

(1.10)

Then

1.

If
\beta>1/2
, than for
2\beta-1>\varepsilon\to 0
,

\inf_{Q;\|P-Q\|_{TV}\geq\varepsilon}\text{KL}(P\|Q)=\frac{1}{8\beta(1-\beta)}\varepsilon^{2}-\frac{2\beta-1}{24\beta^{2}(1-\beta)^{2}}\varepsilon^{3}+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

(1.11)

2.

If
\beta=1/2
, then for
1>\varepsilon\to 0
,

\inf_{Q;\|P-Q\|_{TV}\geq\varepsilon}\text{KL}(P\|Q)=\varepsilon^{2}/2+\varepsilon^{4}/4+O(\varepsilon^{6}).

(1.12)

Thus for any
P
with
\beta=1/2
, and any small
\varepsilon
, we can find a
Q_{\varepsilon}
that

\text{KL}(P\|Q_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|P-Q_{\varepsilon}\|_{TV}^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

(1.13)

The reverse Pinsker’s inequality attempts to utilize only total variation distance in order to control KL-divergence and subsequently deduce an equivalent convergence relation

\text{KL}(P_{n}\|Q)\to 0\Leftrightarrow\|P_{n}-Q\|_{TV}\to 0,\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty,

by combining it with the Pinsker’s inequality. However, it is evident that the conditions of finiteness and non-zero values for the parameters (
1.4
) and (
1.7
) are strong assumptions. In this paper, we focus on random variables with densities on
\mathbb{R}
and introduce the
L^{2}
norm of the density to establish upper bounds on KL-divergence for a more generalized scenario.

The paper outline and our main contributions are stated as follows. In Section
2
, we place greater emphasis on the connections between different convergence senses implied by our upper bounds and ultimately observe that convergence in terms of KL-divergence is bounded between convergence in the
L^{1}
norm the
L^{2}
norm of the density, as shown in Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
. We also establish an equivalence between the aforementioned convergence senses under certain assumptions, as Corollary
2.2
. Particularly, we focus on a specific case that we consider the measures of difference between random variable
X
and Gaussian random variable
G
, and deduce the above results for this case as Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
2.5
. The key Corollary
2.6
are also proved in Section
2
, which is treated as the key tool to obtain a rate theorem for conditional central limit theorem. Moving on to Section
3
, our upper bounds are applied to demonstrate the central limit theorem, with valuable assistance from the local limit theorem. Theorem
3.2
is set for central limit theorem with independent sum case, while after some truncation, the rate theorem for conditional central limit theorem is established as Theorem
3.3
.

2
New Upper bounds for KL-divergence

In this section, we present novel upper bounds for the KL-divergence
\text{KL}(X\|Y)
between two continuous random variables
X
and
Y
, leveraging the
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms of their densities. We further expand these bounds when
Y
follows a Gaussian distribution. Our bounds establish encompassing relationships between various types of convergences, including KL-divergence convergence,
L^{1}
norm convergence, and
L^{2}
norm convergence for continuous random sequences. In the following part, for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, any function
f
and any measurable set
A
, we denote
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
by
L^{p}
and
\int_{A}f(x)dx
by
\int_{A}f
for simplicity.

2.1
The General Case

Firstly, we consider two random variables with one having non-zero density, and we have the following result.

Theorem 2.1

Given two random variables
X,Y
, with densities
p_{X},p_{Y}
, where
0<p_{Y}(y)\leq D<\infty,\;\;\forall y\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for any set
\mathcal{A}:=[-A,A]\subset\mathbb{R},A>0,
and
s>1
,

\displaystyle\text{KL}(X\|Y)

(2.1)

\displaystyle\leq(1+|\ln D|)[\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{1}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{X}(|\ln p_{Y}(X)|^{s})^{1/s}[\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{1}]^{1-1/s}(1+\max_{y\in\mathcal{A}}p^{-1}_{Y}(y))\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof:
Define

\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{A}^{c}\cap\{x;p_{X}(x)\leq p_{Y}(x)\},\;\;\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{A}^{c}\cap\mathcal{B}^{c}.

Since
t\ln t\leq(t-1)+(t-1)^{2},\forall t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\text{KL}(X\|Y)=\int_{\mathcal{A}}p_{X}\ln\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}+\int_{\mathcal{B}}p_{X}\ln\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{X}\ln\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{A}}p_{Y}\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}\ln\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{X}\ln\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}-1\right)p_{Y}+\int_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}-1\right)^{2}p_{Y}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{X}\ln\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{A}^{c}}(p_{Y}-p_{X})+\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{(p_{X}-p_{Y})^{2}}{p_{Y}}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{X}\ln\frac{p_{X}}{p_{Y}}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+(\max_{y\in\mathcal{A}}p^{-1}_{Y}(y))\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{X}\ln(1+|p_{X}-p_{Y}|p_{Y}^{-1})

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+(\max_{y\in\mathcal{A}}p^{-1}_{Y}(y))\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{X}|\ln p_{Y}|

(2.7)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{X}\ln(D+|p_{X}-p_{Y}|)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+(\max_{y\in\mathcal{A}}p^{-1}_{Y}(y))\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{x\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}p_{X}|\ln p_{Y}|

(2.9)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{X}(|\ln D|+D^{-1}|p_{X}-p_{Y}|)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+(\max_{y\in\mathcal{A}}p^{-1}_{Y}(y))\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{x\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}p_{X}|\ln p_{Y}|

(2.11)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+|\ln D|\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{1}+\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p_{Y}(|\ln D|+D^{-1}|p_{X}-p_{Y}|)

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq(1+|\ln D|)\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+(1+\max_{y\in\mathcal{A}}p^{-1}_{Y}(y))\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{x\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}p_{X}|\ln p_{Y}|

(2.13)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+|\ln D|\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{1}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}D(D^{-1}|p_{X}-p_{Y}|)

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq(1+|\ln D|)\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+(1+\max_{y\in\mathcal{A}}p^{-1}_{Y}(y))\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{x\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}p_{X}|\ln p_{Y}|+(|\ln D|+1)\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{1}.

(2.16)

The inequality now comes from

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}p_{X}|\ln p_{Y}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}(|\ln p_{Y}(X)|^{s})^{1/s}\mathbb{P}^{1-1/s}(|X|\geq A)

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}(|\ln p_{Y}(X)|^{s})^{1/s}[\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A)+\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{1}]^{1-1/s}.

\blacksquare

Directly, we deduce that
\|\cdot\|_{2}
can controls KL-divergence, as the following corollary.

Corollary 2.1

Given a random variable
Y
with density
p_{Y}
, where
0<p_{Y}(y)\leq D<\infty,\;\;\forall y\in\mathbb{R}
, and a random variable sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
with densities
\{p_{n},n\geq 1\}
. If
\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{2}\to 0
and there exists
s>1
such that
T_{n}:=|\ln p_{Y}(X_{n})|
has uniformly bounded
s
-th moment, then
\text{KL}(X_{n}\|Y)\to 0.

Proof:
We use Theorem
2.1
for all
X_{n}
. Since
\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{2}\to 0
, we can always choose
\mathcal{A}_{n}=[-A_{n},A_{n}]
s.t.

\max_{y\in[-A_{n},A_{n}]}p_{Y}^{-1}(y)\leq\min\{M,\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|^{-1}_{2}\}

with
\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=\infty,
where

M:=\max_{y\in[-1,1]}p_{Y}^{-1}(y).

Then with above
\mathcal{A}_{n}
, the assumptions imply that as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle a_{n}:=

\displaystyle(1+e^{-1}+|\ln D|)\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq A_{n})+(1+\max_{y\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}p^{-1}_{Y}(y))\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{X_{n}}(|\ln p_{Y}(X_{n})|^{s})^{1/s}\mathbb{P}^{1-1/s}(|Y|\geq A_{n})

tends to 0. Further, we have
\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{2}\to 0\Rightarrow\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{1}\to 0
, which is proved in
A
, and thus the proof completes.

\blacksquare

Remark 2.1

The assumption “
\mathbb{E}T^{s}_{n}<C,\forall n"
is not hard to be satisfied, for example, it holds when
X_{n}
s are Gaussian random variables with bounded variances.

Since
\|\cdot\|_{2}
control KL-divergence, and KL-divergence control
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, once the convergence of
\|\cdot\|_{1}
can deduce the convergence of
\|\cdot\|_{2}
, the three convergent become equivalent. Specifically, we deduce the following equivalence between different convergent forms.

Corollary 2.2

Given a random variable
Y
with density
p_{Y}
, where
0<p_{Y}(y)\leq D<\infty,\;\;\forall y\in\mathbb{R}
, and a random variable sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
with densities
\{p_{n},n\geq 1\}
. If
\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{2}\to 0
and there exists
s>1
such that
T_{n}:=|\ln p_{Y}(X_{n})|
has uniformly bounded
s
-th moment, and one of the following assumptions holds:

1.

\exists 2<q\leq\infty,\sup_{n}\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{q}<\infty.

2.

\limsup_{n}\|p_{n}-\|_{2}\leq\|p_{Y}\|_{2}<\infty
.

Then we obtain the equivalence of the following statements:

1.

p_{n}\xrightarrow{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}p_{Y},\;\;n\to\infty.

2.

p_{n}\xrightarrow{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}p_{Y},\;\;n\to\infty.

3.

\text{KL}(X_{n}\|Y)\to 0,\;\;n\to\infty.

Proof:
Due to Pinsker’s inequality and Corollary
2.1
, we only need to prove “
p_{n}\xrightarrow{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}p_{Y},n\to\infty"

\Rightarrow
“
p_{n}\xrightarrow{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}p_{Y},n\to\infty"
under the assumptions.

1.

If
\exists 2<q\leq\infty,\sup_{n}\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{q}<\infty,
then we can find
\alpha\in(0,1]

s.t.

\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
. Then by H
\ddot{\mbox{o}}
lder’s inequality,

\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{2}\leq\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{1}^{\alpha}\|p_{n}-p_{Y}\|_{q}^{1-\alpha}\to 0,\;\;n\to\infty.

(2.18)

2.

If
\limsup_{n}\|p_{n}\|_{2}\leq\|p_{Y}\|_{2}<\infty
, we know that
(p_{n})
is bounded in
L^{2}
. According to
[
6
, Exercise 4.16.2]
(which has been proved in
B
), we have
p_{n}\rightharpoonup p_{Y}
weakly
\sigma(L^{2},L^{2})
. By
[
6
, Proposition 3.5]
, we deduce
\|p_{n}\|_{2}\to\|p_{Y}\|_{2},n\to\infty
. By
[
6
, Exercise 4.19.1]
(which has been proved in
B
), we finish the proof.

\blacksquare

Further more, if
Y
has some zero density points on
\mathbb{R}
but
X\ll Y
, we have a similar result as Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.2

Given
X,Y
, with density
p_{X},p_{Y}
, where
p_{Y}(y)\leq C<\infty,\;\;\forall y\in\mathbb{R}
and
X\ll Y
. Then for any set
\hat{\mathcal{A}}:=\{y;p_{Y}(y)\geq\frac{1}{A}\}\subset\mathbb{R},A>0,
and
s>1
,

\displaystyle\text{KL}(X\|Y)

(2.19)

\displaystyle\leq(1+|\ln C|)[\mathbb{P}(Y\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{c})+\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{1}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{X}(|\ln p_{Y}(X)|^{s})^{1/s}[\mathbb{P}(Y\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{c})+\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{1}]^{1-1/s}(1+A)\|p_{X}-p_{Y}\|_{2}^{2}.

Apparently, the above Corollary
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
can also be obtained under these conditions.

2.2
A Special Case

In this part, we consider a special case that
Y
in Theorem
2.1
is Gaussian with the same mean and variance of
X
. We denote a Gaussian random variable with the same mean and variance of
X
by
G_{X}
.
Under this special case, we write
\text{KL}(X\|G_{X})
as
D(X)
for simplify, similarly with
[
5
]
. Since for any
a\in\mathbb{R},b>0
, and random variable
X
,

D(X)=D(b(X-a)),

(2.20)

we first focus on
X
be a random variable with density
p
and
\mathbb{E}X=0,\text{Var}(X)=1
. Denote standard Gaussian random variable by
G
and its density by
\phi(x)=(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}e^{-x^{2}/2}.

We define three sets for a given
A>0
:

\mathcal{A}:=[-A,A],\;\;\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{A}^{c}\cap\{x;p(x)\leq\phi(x)\},\;\;\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{A}^{c}\cap\mathcal{B}^{c}.

On
\mathcal{A}=[-A,A]
, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
(Lemma 2.2 in
[
5
]
)

\int_{\mathcal{A}}p\ln\frac{p}{\phi}\leq\mathbb{P}(|G|\geq A)+\sqrt{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal{A}}(p-\phi)^{2}e^{x^{2}/2}-\int_{\mathcal{A}^{c}}p.

Then we deduce that

\int_{\mathcal{A}}p\ln\frac{p}{\phi}\leq\mathbb{P}(|G|\geq A)+\sqrt{2\pi}e^{A^{2}/2}\|p-\phi\|^{2}_{2}.

(2.21)

On
\mathcal{A}^{c}
, when
p/\phi\leq 1
(
i.e.
on
\mathcal{B}
), we have
|\ln(p/\phi)|\leq e^{-1}(\phi/p).
Finally on
\mathcal{C}
, since

\frac{p(x)}{\phi(x)}\leq 1+\|p-\phi\|_{\infty}\phi(x)^{-1}=1+\sqrt{2\pi}\|p-\phi\|_{\infty}e^{x^{2}/2}\leq\sqrt{2\pi}(\|p-\phi\|_{\infty}+1)e^{x^{2}/2},

(2.22)

we can show that

\int_{\mathcal{C}}p\ln(p/\phi)\leq\ln(\sqrt{2\pi}(\|p-\phi\|_{\infty}+1))\mathbb{P}(X\in\mathcal{A}^{c})+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(X^{2}1_{X\in\mathcal{A}^{c}}).

(2.23)

Combining (
2.21
) (
2.22
), similar to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

Given a random variable
X
with density
p
,
\mathbb{E}X=0,\text{Var}(X)=1
and
\mathbb{E}|X|^{s}<\infty
for some
s>2
, then
\exists C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
, such that for any
A>0
,

\displaystyle D(X)\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}e^{A^{2}/2}\|p-\phi\|^{2}_{2}+(C_{2}+\|p-\phi\|_{\infty})\left(\|p-\phi\|_{1}+e^{-A^{2}/2}\right)

(2.24)

\displaystyle+C_{3}(\mathbb{E}|X|^{s})^{2/s}\left(\|p-\phi\|_{1}+e^{-A^{2}/2}\right)^{1-2/s}.

Proof:

\displaystyle D(X)

(2.25)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{A}}p\ln\frac{p}{\phi}+\int_{\mathcal{B}}p\ln\frac{p}{\phi}+\int_{\mathcal{C}}p\ln\frac{p}{\phi}

(2.26)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|G|\geq A)+\sqrt{2\pi}e^{A^{2}/2}\|p-\phi\|^{2}_{2}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\ln(\sqrt{2\pi}(\|p-\phi\|_{\infty}+1))\mathbb{P}(X\in\mathcal{A}^{c})+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(X^{2}1_{X\in\mathcal{A}^{c}})

(2.28)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|G|\geq A)+\sqrt{2\pi}e^{A^{2}/2}\|p-\phi\|^{2}_{2}

(2.29)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\ln(\sqrt{2\pi}(\|p-\phi\|_{\infty}+1))\mathbb{P}(X\in\mathcal{A}^{c})+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(X^{2}1_{X\in\mathcal{A}^{c}})

(2.30)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-A^{2}/2}+C_{1}e^{A^{2}/2}\|p-\phi\|^{2}_{2}+(C_{2}+\ln(\|p-\phi\|_{\infty}+1))\mathbb{P}(X\in\mathcal{A}^{c})

(2.31)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C_{3}(\mathbb{E}|X|^{s})^{2/s}\mathbb{P}^{1-2/s}(X\in\mathcal{A}^{c})

(2.32)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-A^{2}/2}+C_{1}e^{A^{2}/2}\|p-\phi\|^{2}_{2}+(C_{2}+\|p-\phi\|_{\infty})\mathbb{P}(X\in\mathcal{A}^{c})

(2.33)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C_{3}(\mathbb{E}|X|^{s})^{2/s}\mathbb{P}^{1-2/s}(X\in\mathcal{A}^{c}).

(2.34)

Since

\mathbb{P}(X\in\mathcal{A}^{c})=\int_{|x|\geq A}p=\int_{|x|\geq A}p-\phi+\int_{|x|\geq A}\phi\leq\|p-\phi\|_{1}+e^{-A^{2}/2},

(2.35)

the proof completes.

\blacksquare

By taking
e^{A^{2}/2}=\|p-\phi\|^{-1}_{2}
when
\|p-\phi\|_{2}\leq 1
, the following corollary holds.

Corollary 2.3

Given a random variable
X
with density
p
,
\mathbb{E}X=0,\text{Var}(X)=1
and
\mathbb{E}|X|^{s}<\infty
for some
s>2
, and

\|p-\phi\|_{2}\leq 1.

(2.36)

Then
\exists C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle D(X)

(2.37)

\displaystyle\leq(C_{1}+\|p-\phi\|_{\infty})\left(\|p-\phi\|_{1}+\|p-\phi\|_{2}\right)+C_{2}(\mathbb{E}|X|^{s})^{2/s}\left(\|p-\phi\|_{1}+\|p-\phi\|_{2}\right)^{1-2/s}.

Remark 2.2

The constant 1 in (
2.36

3
Conditional Central Limit Theorem

As emphasized in
[
20
]
, the random nature of many problems arising in the applied sciences leads to mathematical models where conditioning is
present, thus it is necessary to study the conditional distribution.

Holst (1979) proved a conditional central limit theorem (CCLT) for a special conditional distribution in
[
12
]
, which has a simple form that under some regular assumptions for
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{i}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Var}(\xi_{i})}}\leq t{\bigg|}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}=n\mathbb{E}\eta_{1}+o(1)\right)\to\Phi(t),\;\;n\to\infty,

where
\eta_{i}
’s are
i.i.d.
discrete random variables, and
\xi_{i}
’s are also
i.i.d.
with
E\xi_{i}=0,\forall i
,
\Phi
is standard Gaussian distribution function. The result was strengthened in
[
13
]
to the case
n\mathbb{E}\eta_{1}+O(\sqrt{n}).

In
[
16
]
Rubshtein proved a CCLT for ergodic stationary process. Using martingale method, Rubshtein showed that for a two-dimensional ergodic stationary process
(\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta}):=\{(\xi_{n},\eta_{n}),n\geq 1\}
satisfying some regular conditions, the conditional distribution function

F_{n,\bm{z}}(t)=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{n}-\mathbb{E}(S_{n}|\bm{\eta}=\bm{z})}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(S_{n}|\bm{\eta}=\bm{z})}}\leq t|\bm{\eta}=\bm{z}\right),t\in\mathbb{R}

(3.1)

converges weakly to the distribution function of standard Gaussian for realizations
\bm{z}=\bm{\eta}(\omega)=\{\eta_{n}(\omega)\}
almost surely, where
S_{n}=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{n}
.

More recently,
[
20
]
showed a limit theorem for the conditionally independent case.
[
10
]
used Stein method to deduce an upper bound for Wasserstein distance between conditional distribution and Gaussian distribution.

It is worthy to note that Yao
et al.
(2024) use entropy method to prove a conditional central limit theorem in
[
15
]
which covers the independent sum case. Yao
et al.
also deduce a rate theorem for conditional central limit theorem in
[
15
]
according to the Berry-Esseen bound in
[
5
]
. However, a finite 64-th moment condition is needed (see details in Remark
3.3
and
[
15
]
), which is necessary to be further improved.

In this section, we use our upper bounds in Section
2
to deduce a conditional central limit theorem in KL-divergence sense (see Theorem
3.2
), based on local limit theorem stated in the next part, and eventually prove a new rate theorem for conditional central limit theorem (see Theorem
3.3
).

We ask the help for traditional local limit theorem, specifically, we combine the classical results in

Lemma 3.1

Consider an independent sequence of random variables
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
with densities
\{p_{X_{n}},n\geq 1\}
, and
\mathbb{E}X_{n}=0,\forall n\geq 1
. Denote

B_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Var}(X_{i}).

(3.2)

If the following conditions hold:

1.

\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}=\infty
.

2.

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{3}=O(B_{n}).

3.

There exists a subsequence
X_{n_{1}},X_{n_{2}}\cdots,X_{n_{m}},\cdots
, s.t. for some
\lambda>0
,

\mu:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{m:n_{m}\leq n\}}{n^{\lambda}}>0,

(3.3)

and
\sup_{m}\|p_{X_{n_{m}}}\|_{TV}<\infty
, where
\#
denotes the counting measure and
\|\cdot\|_{TV}
denotes the total variation of a function, i.e.

\|f\|_{TV}:=\sup_{n,a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(a_{i})-f(a_{i+1})|.

Then if we denote the density function of
S_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
by
p_{n}
, we obtain

\|p_{n}-\phi\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}B_{n}^{-3/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{3}+C_{1}\sqrt{B_{n}}e^{-C_{2}n^{\lambda}},

(3.4)

where
\phi=(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}e^{-x^{2}/2}
is the density of standard normal distribution,
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}
are positive constants.

Proof:
Theorem 10 in Chapter VI in
[
18
]
says that

\|p_{n}-\phi\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}B_{n}^{-3/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{3}+\sqrt{B_{n}}\int_{|t|>\frac{1}{2}K_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|f_{n}(t)|dt,

(3.5)

where
C_{0}
is an absolutely constant,
K_{1}:=\sup_{n}B_{n}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{3}
and
f_{n}
is the characteristic function of
X_{n}
. Lemma 10 in Chapter VI of
[
18
]
says that if condition 3 holds, there exists a constant

such that

\int_{|t|>\frac{1}{2}K_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|f_{n}(t)|dt\leq K_{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma\mu n^{\lambda}}}e^{-\gamma\mu n^{\lambda}\frac{1}{4}K_{1}}+K_{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\mu n^{\lambda}}4C_{B}^{2},

(3.6)

where

are absolutely constants, and

[

\blacksquare

For a random variable

\displaystyle\overset{b}{\leq}C\left(\frac{M^{4/k}}{n^{1-2s}}+3\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{M^{3/k}}{n^{1/2-3s/2}}\right)^{1/2-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\overset{c}{\leq}C\left(\frac{M^{4/k}}{n^{1-2s}}+3\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{M^{3/k}}{n^{1/2-3s/2}}\right)^{1/2-\varepsilon}+C\left(n\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}|X|^{k}}{M}+\dfrac{\mathbb{E}J^{u}(X|Y)}{n^{su}}\right)\right)^{1-t}

\displaystyle\overset{d}{=}O\left(\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{u-\frac{6}{k}u-3}{4u+\frac{6}{k}u+3}(1-2\varepsilon)}\right),

where ’
a
’ holds due to (
3.30
) and (
3.34
); ’
b
’ comes from Cauchy inequality and
\text{Var}(X)=\mathbb{E}\text{Var}(X|Y)+\text{Var}\mathbb{R}(X|Y)
; ’
c
’ is deduced by (
3.33
);
d
comes from taking

s=\frac{5}{3+6u/k+4u},\;\;M=n^{su},\;\;t=2\varepsilon.

If we take
r=su
, (
3.35
) holds for all
n\geq 1
.
Since
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
is arbitrary, the proof completes.

\blacksquare

Remark 3.3

A well-known rate theorem for central limit theorem is proved by Bobkov et al. in

Compared with our Theorem

Lemma A.2
(Exercise 4.13.2 in
[
6
]
)

Let
(f_{n})
be a sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

1.

f_{n}(x)\to f(x),a.e.,

2.

(f_{n})
is bounded in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e.
\|f_{n}\|_{1}\leq M,\forall n.

Then
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int(|f_{n}|-|f_{n}-f|)=\int|f|.

(A.2)

Proof:
Firstly, by Fatou Lemma,
M\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int|f_{n}|\geq\int\liminf_{n\to\infty}|f_{n}|=\int|f|
, thus
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Next, let
\phi_{n}(x):=||f_{n}-|f_{n}-f|-|f||
, by (
A.1
) we have
\phi_{n}(x)\leq|f|\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, thus by dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\phi_{n}=\int\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{n}=0,

(A.3)

and the proof completes.

\blacksquare

Lemma A.3
(Exercise 4.13.3 in

]

.

Stability of optimal transport on metric measure spaces
Bang-Xian Han, Zhuo-Nan Zhu

Abstract
We prove a quantitative stability of Kantorovich potentials on metric measure spaces with lower Ricci curvature bound, thereby confirming a recent conjecture of Kitagawa, Letrouit and Mérigot. Our proof, which employs the heat kernel-regularized c c -transform, does not rely on linear structure or sectional curvature bounds. As a corollary, we get a quantitative stability of optimal transport maps on Alexandrov spaces with lower curvature bound.



Keywords
: optimal transport, heat kernel-regularization, Kantorovich potential, stability, metric measure space, curvature-dimension condition, Ricci curvature

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and setting

Optimal transport.

Optimal transport, initiated by Monge
[
35
]
and reframed by Kantorovich
[
24
]
, seeks the most efficient way to redistribute mass between two probability distributions. Precisely, given two probability measures
\rho
and
\mu
defined on Polish spaces
X
and
Y
respectively, and a cost function
c:X\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the Monge optimal transport problem aims to find a minimizer
T
, called an optimal transport map, of the following optimization problem among all measurable maps
H:X\to Y
pushing
\rho
forward to
\mu
:

\int_{X}c(x,T(x))\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)=\inf_{H_{\sharp}\rho=\mu}\int_{X}c(x,H(x))\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x),

(MP)

The Monge problem prevents mass splitting, sometimes making the problem ill-posed. Its relaxation, called Kantorovich problem, optimizes over joint couplings instead of deterministic maps. A fundamental result known as Kantorovich duality, asserts that the Kantorovich problem is equivalent to the following dual problem:

\sup_{\phi(x)+\psi(y)\leq c(x,y)}\left\{\int_{X}\phi(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)+\int_{Y}\psi(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right\}.

(KD)

The optimal functions
\phi,\psi
, which always exist under standard assumptions, are called Kantorovich potentials
[
2
,
45
]
.

In the Euclidean space, for
c(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}|x-y|^{2}
, Brenier’s landmark result
[
5
]
established that, for absolutely continuous source measures, the optimal transport map is unique and takes the form
T(x)=\nabla u(x)
for a convex function
u
. This was later extended to Riemannian manifolds by McCann
[
32
]
, who showed that for
c(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)
, the optimal transport map is given almost everywhere by
T(x)=\exp_{x}(-\nabla\varphi(x))
for a Kantorovich potential
\varphi
.

A fundamental question in both theoretical and applied contexts of optimal transport is the quantitative stability of optimal transport maps and Kantorovich potentials under perturbations of the target measure (see Letrouit’s lecture note
[
28
]
and the references therein). Based on recent breakthroughs in the quantitative stability of optimal transport on Euclidean spaces
[
11
,
30
]
, on boundaries of convex bodies
[
27
,
20
]
and on Riemannian manifolds
[
26
]
,
Kitagawa–Letrouit–Mérigot
[
26
, §1.2]
conjecture that:

the quantitative stability results are also true in more general metric measure spaces with synthetic curvature bounds.

General setting.

In this paper, we confirm the conjecture of Kitagawa– Letrouit–Mérigot for the following spaces:

•

RCD
(K,N)
spaces (metric measure spaces with synthetic Ricci curvature lower bound): we prove quantitative
L^{1}
-stability of Kantorovich potentials with respect to Wasserstein perturbations of the target measure;

•

Alexandrov spaces with (sectional) curvature lower bound: we establish stability estimates for optimal transport maps in terms of the
L^{2}
-norm of the potential difference.

An Alexandrov space is a geodesic space of finite Hausdorff dimension and of curvature bounded from below (cf.
[
8
]
). An
{\rm RCD}(K,N)
space is a metric measure spaces verifying the synthetic Riemannian curvature-dimension condition
[
43
,
31
,
3
,
12
]
. An
n
-dimensional Alexandrov space with curvature bounded from below by
k
, equipped with its
n
-Hausdorff measure, is an
{\rm RCD}(k(n-1),n)
space
[
41
,
46
]
.
After
[
5
,
32
]
, Gigli–Rajala–Sturm
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
proved the existence and uniqueness of the optimal transport map on an
\rm{RCD}(K,N)
space
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
, for the quadratic cost
c(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)
and the source measure
\rho\ll\mathfrak{m}
.

John domains, named after F. John
[
23
]
, encompass many cases of interest, such as bounded Lipschitz domains, bounded domains satisfying a cone condition and certain fractal domains (see
[
7
]
for more discussions).

Definition 1.1

(John domain)
.

A bounded open subset
S
of a metric space is called a
John domain
if there is a distinguished point
x_{0}\in S
and a constant
\eta>0
such that, for every
x\in S
, there is a rectifiable curve
\gamma\colon[0,\ell(\gamma)]\to S
parametrized by arc length, such that
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(\ell(\gamma))=x_{0}
, and

{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma(t),S^{c})\geq\eta t,~~~\forall t\in[0,\ell(\gamma)],

where
S^{c}
denotes the complement of
S
.

In
[
30
, Theorem 1.9]
, it has been shown that when the source measure
\rho
is the uniform density on some non-John domain
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then no quantitative stability estimates of the form
\|\phi_{\mu}-\phi_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}\leq CW_{p}^{\alpha}(\mu,\nu)

can hold for any
C,\alpha,p>0
.
Moreover, examples in
[
29
]
indicates that both unboundedness of the density and openness of
S
may cause instability of optimal transport maps.

In this paper, we always assume that the Kantorovich potential
\phi_{\mu}
from
\rho
to
\mu
satisfies
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}(\phi_{\mu})=\int_{S}\phi_{\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho=0
. Together with the uniqueness of the optimal transport map, such
\phi_{\mu}
is unique and thus makes sense to study its stability.

1.2
Main results

Our main theorem concerns the quantitative
L^{1}
stability of Kantorovich potentials on
{\rm RCD}(K,N)
spaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be an
{\rm RCD}(K,N)
metric measure space. Let
S\subseteq X
be a John domain and
Y\subseteq X
be compact with
\mathfrak{m}(Y)>0
. Let
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(S)
be with
a_{1}\mathfrak{m}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{S}$}\leq\rho\leq a_{2}\mathfrak{m}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{S}$}
for some positive constants
a_{1},a_{2}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
K,N,a_{1},a_{2},S,\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S\cup Y)
, such that for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
,

\|\phi_{\mu}-\phi_{\nu}\|_{L^{1}(\rho)}\leq CW_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mu,\nu),

(1.1)

where
\phi_{\mu}
and
\phi_{\nu}
are the Kantorovich potentials from
\rho
to
\mu
and
\rho
to
\nu
respectively.

If
(S,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
is a compact
{\rm RCD}(K,N)
space, it supports a global
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality (cf.
[
18
,
42
]
) and we can remove the John domain assumption:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
be a compact
{\rm RCD}(K,N)
metric measure space. Let
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(X)
be with
a_{1}\mathfrak{m}\leq\rho\leq a_{2}\mathfrak{m}
for some positive constants
a_{1},a_{2}
. Then the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
holds.

Adapting the strategy of
[
26
]
, we can also prove the stability of optimal transport maps on Alexandrov spaces.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(X,{\mathrm{d}})
be an
n
-dimensional Alexandrov space,
\mathfrak{m}
be the
n
-Hausdorff measure. Under the same assumption for
S,Y
and
\rho
as in Theorem
1.2
, and if
S
additionally has finite perimeter, then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
k,n,a_{1},a_{2},\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S\cup Y),{\rm{Per}}(S),S
, such that for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(Y)
, we have

\int_{S}{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(T_{\mu}(x),T_{\nu}(x))\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\leq CW_{1}^{1/6}(\mu,\nu),

(1.2)

where
T_{\mu}
and
T_{\nu}
are the optimal transport maps from
\rho
to
\mu
and
\rho
to
\nu
respectively.

1.3
Strategy: heat kernel-regularized
c
-transform

Motivated by regularized
c
-transforms using Gibbs kernels
e^{-c(x,y)/\varepsilon}

[
26
]
, entropic optimal transport
[
16
,
17
]
and Varadhan’s formula

\lim_{t\to 0}-t\log p_{t/2}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)=c(x,y),

we make use of heat kernel-regularized
c
-transform (Hopf–Cole transform):

\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})\ni\psi\mapsto\Phi_{t}[\psi](x)=-t\log\int_{X}e^{\frac{\psi(y)}{t}}p_{t/2}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y).

This approach allows us to bypass the low regularity of Kantorovich potentials in the non-smooth setting.

We define the heat kernel regularized Kantorovich functional as

\mathrm{K}_{t}[\varphi]:=\int_{S}\Phi_{t}[\varphi]\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho.

Similar to
[
26
]
, the key in our proof is the strong concavity of the functional
\mathrm{K}_{t}
.
To achieve this, we first derive a local strong concavity estimate using heat kernel estimate, then globalize the estimate on the support of the source measure using a Boman chain argument for John domains.

Unlike the regularized
c
-transform used in
[
26
]
, the existence of the boundary of
Y
may lead to the failure in our heat kernel regularization argument. This possibility is ruled out by using the measure concentration property of the heat kernel and by making a careful choice of Lipschitz extension.

Organization.
This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we prove quantitative stability of Kantorovich potentials on
\mathrm{RCD}(K,N)
spaces. In Section
3
, we establish stability of optimal transport maps on Alexandrov spaces. The Appendix
A
contains technical lemmas about Poincaré inequalities.

2
Stability of Kantorovich potentials

In this section, we study the quantitative stability of Kantorovich potentials on RCD
(K,N)
spaces and prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.1
Heat kernel estimate

We begin by recalling the short-time asymptotic behaviour of the heat kernel on
{\rm RCD}(K,N)
spaces. It was first studied by Varadhan on Riemannian manifolds
[
44
]
, and is known as Varadhan formula today.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p_{t}(x,y)
be the heat kernel on an
{\rm RCD}(K,N)
space
(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
. Then

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}-t\log p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y),\quad\text{uniformly in}\quad x\in S,\,y\in Y.

(2.1)

Proof.

By the heat kernel estimate
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
, for any
\epsilon>0
, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{1}(\epsilon)\mathfrak{m}\left(B\big(y,\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}}\big)\right)}

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)}{(4-\epsilon)t}-C_{2}(\epsilon)t\right)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq p_{t}(x,y)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}(\epsilon)}{\mathfrak{m}\left(B\big(y,\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}}\big)\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)}{(4+\epsilon)t}+C_{2}(\epsilon)t\right).

Then we have

\displaystyle-t\log

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}(\epsilon)}{\mathfrak{m}\left(B\big(y,\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}}\big)\right)}+\frac{2{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)}{4+\epsilon}+\frac{C_{2}(\epsilon)}{2}t^{2}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-t\log p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\leq t\log C_{1}(\epsilon)\mathfrak{m}\left(B\big(y,\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}}\big)\right)+\frac{2{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)}{4-\epsilon}-\frac{C_{2}(\epsilon)}{2}t^{2}.

By Bishop–Gromov inequality
[
43
, Theorem 2.3]
, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}t\log\mathfrak{m}\left(B\big(y,\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}}\big)\right)=0,\quad\text{uniformly in }\,y\in Y.

(2.4)

Letting
t\rightarrow 0
in (
2.3
), we get the following uniform estimate:

\frac{2{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)}{4+\epsilon}\leq\varliminf_{t\rightarrow 0}-t\log p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\leq\varlimsup_{t\rightarrow 0}-t\log p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\leq\frac{2{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)}{4-\epsilon}.

(2.5)

Letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we prove the lemma.
∎

2.2
Heat kernel regularization

To establish the quantitative stability, we adopt a regularization technique similar to those in
[
36
,
26
]
and
[
16
,
17
]
(see also
[
9
,
10
,
34
]
).

For
\psi\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(Y,{\mathrm{d}})
, define

\bar{\psi}(y)=\sup_{z\in Y}\big\{\psi(z)-\text{Lip}(\psi)\,{\mathrm{d}}(z,y)\big\},\quad y\in X.

By MacShane’s Lemma,
\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(\bar{\psi})=\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(\psi)
and
{\bar{\psi}}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{Y}$}=\psi\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{Y}$}
.
Denote
D=\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S\cup Y)
,
\Lambda_{\psi}=D+\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(\psi)+1
and define
\psi_{*}={\bar{\psi}}-\Lambda_{\psi}{\mathrm{d}}(\cdot,Y)
.

For
t>0
, we define the heat kernel regularized
c
-transform as

\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})\ni\varphi\mapsto\Phi_{t}[\varphi](x):=-t\log\int_{X}e^{\frac{{\varphi}(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y),

and define the heat kernel regularized Kantorovich functional as

\mathrm{K}_{t}[\varphi]:=\int_{S}\Phi_{t}[\varphi]\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho.

For
\varphi\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})
and
x\in X
, we associate a probability measure

{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{x}^{t}[\varphi](y):=\frac{e^{\frac{\varphi(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}{\int_{X}e^{\frac{\varphi(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}

and define
\mu^{t}[\varphi]:=\int_{X}\mu_{x}^{t}[\varphi]\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)
. This means, for any
v\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, it holds

\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{t}[\varphi]}(v)=\int_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\varphi]}(v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x).

(2.6)

Next we prove the Sobolev regularity of
x\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\varphi]}(v)
. We refer to
[
3
]
for Sobolev calculus on metric measure spaces.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
t\in\big(0,\frac{D+1}{\sqrt{(N-1)|K|}}\wedge 1\big)
,
\psi\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(Y,{\mathrm{d}})
and
v\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})
, there holds
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v)\in W^{1,2}(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
.

Proof.

Denote by
H_{t}f(x)=\int_{X}f(y)p_{t}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)
the heat flow from
f
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v)=\frac{\int_{X}v(y)e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}{\int_{X}e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}=\frac{H_{\frac{t}{2}}(ve^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}})(x)}{H_{\frac{t}{2}}(e^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}})(x)}.

Claim
:
ve^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}},e^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}}\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
.

Note that
|v(y)|\leq\sup_{Y}|v|+\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(v)\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)+\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(v){\mathrm{d}}(y,Y)
and
\psi_{*}(y)\leq\sup_{Y}\psi-(D+1){\mathrm{d}}(y,Y)
. So
ve^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}},e^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
.

Fix
y_{0}\in Y
and denote
B_{i}=\{y\in X:i\leq{\mathrm{d}}(y,Y)<i+1\},i\in\mathbb{N}
. It holds

\displaystyle\int_{X}e^{\frac{2\psi_{*}}{t}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{2\sup_{Y}\psi}{t}}e^{\frac{-2(D+1)i}{t}}\mathfrak{m}(B_{i})

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{2\sup_{Y}\psi}{t}}e^{\frac{-2(D+1)i}{t}}\mathfrak{m}(B(y_{0},\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)+i+1)).

Without loss of generality, we may assume
K<0
. By Bishop–Gromov inequality
[
43
, Theorem 2.3]
, we have

\mathfrak{m}(B(y_{0},\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)+i+1))\leq c_{2}\int_{0}^{\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)+i+1}\sinh^{N-1}(\sqrt{\frac{-K}{N-1}}s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

Note that
\sinh^{N-1}(\sqrt{\frac{-K}{N-1}}s)\leq\frac{e^{\sqrt{(N-1)|K|}s}}{2^{N-1}}
, then

\mathfrak{m}(B(y_{0},\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)+i+1))\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)+i+1}\frac{e^{\sqrt{(N-1)|K|}s}}{2^{N-1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leq c_{2}e^{\sqrt{(N-1)|K|}i},

(2.8)

where
c_{1}=\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B(y_{0},\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)))}{\int_{0}^{\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)}\sinh^{N-1}(\sqrt{\frac{-K}{N-1}}s){\mathrm{d}}s}
,
c_{2}=c_{1}\frac{e^{\sqrt{(N-1)|K|}(\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)+1)}}{2^{N-1}\sqrt{\frac{-K}{N-1}}}
.

For
t<\frac{D+1}{\sqrt{(N-1)|K|}}
, it holds
\sqrt{(N-1)|K|}-\frac{2(D+1)}{t}<-\frac{D+1}{t}
, so by (
2.7
) and (
2.8
),

\int_{X}e^{\frac{2\psi_{*}}{t}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{2\sup_{Y}\psi}{t}}c_{2}e^{\frac{-2(D+1)i}{t}}e^{\sqrt{(N-1)|K|}i}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{2\sup_{Y}\psi}{t}}c_{2}e^{\frac{-(D+1)i}{t}}<+\infty.

(2.9)

Similarly, we can prove

\int_{X}v^{2}e^{\frac{2\psi_{*}}{t}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}<+\infty
and we prove the claim.

By the regularization of heat flow
[
3
, Theorem 6.5]
, we have
H_{\frac{t}{2}}(ve^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}}),H_{\frac{t}{2}}(e^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}})\in W^{1,2}(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})\cap\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
. Moreover, by heat kernel estimate (
2.2
),

\displaystyle H_{\frac{t}{2}}(e^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}})(x)\geq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}e^{\frac{\psi(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\inf_{Y}\psi}{t}}\int_{Y}\frac{1}{C_{1}(1)\mathfrak{m}(B(y,\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}}))}\exp\left(-\frac{2{\mathrm{d}}^{2}(x,y)}{3t}-\frac{C_{2}(1)}{2}t\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\inf_{Y}\psi}{t}}e^{-\frac{2D^{2}}{3t}-\frac{C_{2}(1)}{2}t}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(Y)}{\mathfrak{m}(B(y_{0},\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(Y)+1))}\frac{1}{C_{1}(1)}>0.

So
\frac{1}{H_{\frac{t}{2}}(e^{\frac{\psi_{*}}{t}})}\in W^{1,2}(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
and by chain rule (see
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.3]
), we have
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v)\in W^{1,2}(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
v\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})
and
\psi\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(Y,{\mathrm{d}})
, we have

•

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{s=0}$}{\mathrm{K}}_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]=-\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v)
;

•

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}{{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{s=0}$}{\mathrm{K}}_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]=-\frac{1}{t}\int_{S}{\rm{Var}}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)
.

For abbreviation, we can write

\nabla{\mathrm{K}}_{t}[\psi_{*}]=-\mu^{t}[\psi_{*}],\quad\langle D^{2}{\mathrm{K}}_{t}[\psi_{*}]v,v\rangle=-\frac{1}{t}\int_{S}{\rm{Var}}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x).

Proof.

Let
|s|\leq 1
.
By direct computation,

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}\Phi_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]=-\frac{\int_{X}v(y)e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)+sv(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}{\int_{X}e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)+sv(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}.

Similar to Lemma
2.2
, we can see that

\left|\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}\Phi_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]\right|

is uniformly bounded in
s
.

By differentiating under the integral defining
{\mathrm{K}}_{t}[\psi_{*}]
and letting
s=0
, we have

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{s=0}$}{\mathrm{K}}_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]=\int_{S}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{s=0}$}\Phi_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)=-\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v).

Similarly, we can prove

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}{{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}}\Phi_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{\int_{X}v^{2}(y)e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)+sv(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}{\int_{X}e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)+sv(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}-\left|\frac{\int_{X}v(y)e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)+sv(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}{\int_{X}e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)+sv(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}\right|^{2}\right)

and

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}{{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{s=0}$}{\mathrm{K}}_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]=\int_{S}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}{{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{s=0}$}\Phi_{t}[\psi_{*}+sv]\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)=-\frac{1}{t}\int_{S}{\rm{Var}}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x).

∎

2.3
Gradient estimate

To leverage the Hessian formula in Lemma
2.3
, we need to bound the variance term from below; this amounts to proving strong concavity of the regularised Kantorovich functional. We achieve this by establishing a gradient estimate for the marginal density
x\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]}(v)
.

The following auxiliary lemma will be used throughout this section. We refer to
[
13
,
14
]
for a comprehensive introduction to
L^{2}
-normed tangent module

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
, and
g\in L^{0}(X\times X)
be with

for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x,y\in X
. Then

\int_{X}f(y)g(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)\in W^{1,2}(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
and for any

, we have

\int\langle\nabla_{x}\int_{X}f(y)g(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y),\varphi(x)\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)=\int_{X}f(y)\int\langle\nabla g(\cdot,y),\varphi\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)

(2.11)

and

\left|\nabla_{x}\int_{X}f(y)g(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)\right|\leq\int_{X}|f(y)||\nabla_{x}g(x,y)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y).

(2.12)

In particular, we can write

\int_{X}f(y)\nabla_{x}g(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)=\nabla_{x}\int_{X}f(y)g(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)\in L^{2}(TX).

(2.13)

Proof.

Since
f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
, the heat flow regularization yields
H_{s}(f)\in W^{1,2}(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
. Moreover, by
[
22
]
, we have
p_{s}(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,2}(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
y\in X
. For
\varphi\in\big\{\phi\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})\cap{\mathrm{D}}(\Delta):\Delta\phi\in W^{1,2}(X,{\mathrm{d}},\mathfrak{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})\}
, by integration by parts and Fubini theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Delta\varphi(x)\left(\int_{X}f(y)g(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)

(2.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(y)\int_{X}g(x,y)\Delta\varphi(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}f(y)\int_{S}\langle\nabla_{x}g(x,y),\nabla\varphi(x)\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y).

By approximation (cf.
[
13
, §3.2]
) and the Riesz representation theorem for Hilbert module (cf.
[
14
, Theorem 3.2.14, Example 3.2.15 ]
) we prove the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.6

Lemma 2.7
.

For

small enough, it holds

J_{t}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]}\big(|\nabla_{x}\log p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,\cdot)|^{2}\big)\leq\frac{C_{1}(K,N,\Lambda_{\psi})}{t^{2}}.

J_{t}(x)\leq e^{-\frac{Kt}{3}}\int_{X}\frac{\Delta_{x}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)}{p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{x}^{t}[\psi_{*}]+\frac{NK}{3}\frac{e^{-\frac{2Kt}{3}}}{1-e^{-\frac{Kt}{3}}}.

Proposition 2.11
.

, and
\phi_{s}=\psi_{*}+sv

.

2.3

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}\Big({\Phi}_{t}[\phi_{s}]-\mathrm{K}_{t}[\phi_{s}]\Big)=

2.6

In the following lemma we pass to the limit of
t

Lemma 2.12
.

For any
\psi\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(Y,{\mathrm{d}})

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}{\Phi}_{t}[\psi_{*}](x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}-t\log\int_{X}e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)=\psi^{c}(x),

(2.36)

for
\rho
-a.e.

\psi^{c}(x)=\inf_{y\in Y}\{c(x,y)-\psi(y)\}

Proof.

For
\delta>0
and
Y_{\delta}:=\{y\in X:{\mathrm{d}}(y,Y)\leq\delta\}
, we have

\int_{X}e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)=\underbrace{\int_{Y_{\delta}}e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{X\setminus Y_{\delta}}e^{\frac{\psi_{*}(y)}{t}}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}_{I_{2}}.

(2.37)

Step 1:

Recall that
\psi_{*}(y)=\bar{\psi}(y)-\Lambda_{\psi}{\mathrm{d}}(y,Y)
,
\Lambda_{\psi}=D+\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(\psi)+1,D=\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S\cup Y)
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{I_{2}}{I_{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{\sup_{Y}\psi_{i}-(D+1)\delta}{t}}\int_{X}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}{e^{\frac{\inf_{Y}\psi_{i}}{t}}\int_{Y}p_{\frac{t}{2}}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(y)}.

(2.38)

Combining with the stochastic completeness of the heat flow and the heat kernel estimate (

\delta>0
such that

\frac{I_{2}}{I_{1}}\lesssim e^{-\frac{D^{2}}{t}}~~~~\text{as}~~t\to 0.

(2.39)

Step 2:
We claim that

\inf_{y\in Y_{\delta}\setminus Y}\{c(x,y)-\psi_{*}(y)\}>\inf_{y\in Y}\{c(x,y)-\psi(y)\}=\psi^{c}(x).

(2.40)

Since

3
Stability of optimal transport maps

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
concerning the quantitative stability of optimal transport maps on Alexandrov spaces. This is achieved by combining Theorem
1.2
and the following estimate.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,{\mathrm{d}})
be an Alexandrov space with no boundary. Then under the same assumptions of Theorem
1.4
, there exists a constant
\bar{C}>0
, depending on
k,n,a_{1},a_{2},\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S\cup Y),{\rm{Per}}(S),S
, such that for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(Y)
, we have

\int_{S}|\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\leq\bar{C}\left(\int_{S}|\phi_{\mu}(x)-\phi_{\nu}(x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},

(3.1)

where
\phi_{\mu}
and
\phi_{\nu}
are the Kantorovich potentials from
\rho
to
\mu
and
\rho
to
\nu
respectively.

We follow a strategy of
[
26
]
by lifting integrals from the base space to the unit tangent bundle, evolve the relevant quantities via the geodesic flow developed in
[
25
]
, and then exploit the one-dimensional convexity of the potentials. For the reader’s convenience, we recall the necessary Alexandrov geometry theory and provide a self-contained proof.

The Riemannian structure on the set of regular points
X_{\mathrm{reg}}
(cf.
[
38
]
) endows
\mathrm{T}X_{\mathrm{reg}}
with the structure of a Euclidean vector bundle over
X_{\mathrm{reg}}
. On this Euclidean vector bundle, one can define (cf.
[
25
, Section3]
) a canonical Liouville measure
\mathfrak{m}_{L}
: it is the unique Borel measure on
\mathrm{T}X_{\mathrm{reg}}
such that for any Borel set
A\subset\mathrm{T}X_{\mathrm{reg}}
, we have

\mathfrak{m}_{L}(A)=\int_{X}\mathcal{H}^{n}\big(A\cap\mathrm{T}_{x}X\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(x).

We extend
\mathfrak{m}_{L}
to a measure on
\mathrm{T}X
by setting
\mathfrak{m}_{L}(\mathrm{T}X\setminus\mathrm{T}X_{\mathrm{reg}})=0
. Moreover, by
[
6
, Theorem 1.4]
, if
(X,{\mathrm{d}})
is an Alexandrov space with no boundary, then the geodesic flow preserves the Liouville measure.

Let
\mathrm{S}X=\{v\in\mathrm{T}X:|v|=1\}
denote the unit tangent bundle. There is a canonical Liouville measure
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
on
\mathrm{S}X
(cf.
[
25
, Section 3.6]
), also called Liouville measure, such that if the geodesic flow defined on
{\rm T}X
preserves
\mathfrak{m}_{L}
, then the geodesic flow defined on
{\rm S}X
preserves
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
as well. For any Borel set
A\subseteq{\rm S}X
, we have

\mathfrak{m}_{S}(A)=\int_{{\rm S}X}\chi_{A}(x,v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)=c_{n}\int_{X}\int_{\Sigma_{x}(X)}\chi_{A}(x,v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{x}(v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(x),

(3.2)

where
\Sigma_{x}(X)
denotes the space of directions at
x
,
c_{n}=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm S}^{n-1})
and
\sigma_{x}\in\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{x}(X))
is the canonical probability measure on the fiber.

Lifting to the unit tangent bundle.

Let
\varphi_{s}:{\rm S}X\rightarrow{\rm S}X
be the geodesic flow at time
s\in[0,1]
on
\mathrm{S}X
, and write
\varphi_{s}(x,v)=(b_{s}(x,v),t_{s}(x,v))
, where
b_{s}(x,v)\in X
and
t_{s}(x,v)\in{\Sigma_{b_{s}(x,v)}(X)}
. For
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
-a.e.
(x,v)\in{\rm S}X
such that the curve
[0,1]\ni s\mapsto b_{s}(x,v)
is a locally shortest path, denote by
I_{S}(x,v)
the set of connected components of
\{s\in[0,1]:b_{s}(x,v)\in S\}
. Since
S
is an open set, we have

\{s\in[0,1]:b_{s}(x,v)\in S\}=\bigcup_{i\in I_{S}(x,v)}(\alpha_{i}(x,v),\beta_{i}(x,v)).

For
x\in S\cap X_{\mathrm{reg}}
, by
[
8
, Theorem 10.8.4]
,
\Sigma_{x}(X)
is isometric to
S^{n-1}
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{x}(X)}\langle\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x),v\rangle^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{x}(v)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm S}^{n-1}}\langle\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x),v\rangle^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{x}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{n}|\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x)|^{2},

for some universal constant
d_{n}
.

By
[
25
, Section 3.6]
,
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
is preserved by
\varphi_{s}
, so

\displaystyle\int_{S}|\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\leq a_{2}\int_{S}|\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(x)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathop{=}^{\eqref{0.53}}

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}}{d_{n}}\int_{S}\int_{\Sigma_{x}(X)}\langle\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x),v\rangle^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\sigma_{x}(v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle\mathop{=}^{\eqref{liouville}}

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}}{c_{n}d_{n}}\int_{{\rm S}X}\langle\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x),v\rangle^{2}\chi_{S}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}}{c_{n}d_{n}}\int_{{\rm S}X}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla\phi_{\mu}(b_{s}(x,v))-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(b_{s}(x,v)),t_{s}(x,v)\rangle^{2}\chi_{S}(b_{s}(x,v))\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}}{c_{n}d_{n}}\int_{{\rm S}X}\sum_{i\in I_{S}(x,v)}\int_{\alpha_{i}(x,v)}^{\beta_{i}(x,v)}\langle\nabla\phi_{\mu}(b_{s}(x,v))-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(b_{s}(x,v)),t_{s}(x,v)\rangle^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v).

One-dimensional convexity estimate.

For
(x,v)\in{\rm S}X
and
s\in[0,1]
, denote
u_{\mu}^{(x,v)}(s)=\phi_{\mu}(b_{s}(x,v))
. Then

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}u_{\mu}^{(x,v)}(s)=\langle\nabla\phi_{\mu}(b_{s}(x,v)),t_{s}(x,v)\rangle,\quad\text{for a.e. $s\in[0,1]$},

and an analogous formula holds for
u_{\nu}^{(x,v)}(s)=\phi_{\nu}(b_{s}(x,v))
.

Denote
S_{1}=\{x\in X:{\mathrm{d}}(x,S)\leq 1\}
. We note that in the last integral of (
3.4
), only the elements
(x,v)\in{\rm S}X
for which
x\in S_{1}
have a non-vanishing contribution. For
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
-a.e.
(x,v)\in{\rm{S}}X
with

, the curve
s\mapsto b_{s}(x,v)
is locally minimizing on
[0,1]
. Thus, applying Ohta’s semiconcavity estimate
[
37
, Lemma 3.2]
on sufficiently small subsegments yields local concavity of
u^{(x,v)}_{\mu}-\zeta|s|^{2}
with a uniform constant
\zeta
. The local-to-global principle for concave functions then leads to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
37
]
, Lemma 3.2)
.

There exists
\zeta
, depending on
k
and
\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S\cup Y)
, such that for
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
-a.e.
(x,v)\in\rm{S(S_{1})}
, the functions
u_{\mu}^{(x,v)}-\zeta|s|^{2}
,
u_{\nu}^{(x,v)}-\zeta|s|^{2}
are concave on
s\in[0,1]
. Moreover, the modulus of their derivatives (which exist a.e. on
[0,1]
) is bounded above by
\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S\cup Y)+2\zeta
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
11
]
, Lemma 5.1)
.

Let

be a compact segment and let

be two convex functions such that
|u^{\prime}|
and
|v^{\prime}|
(defined a.e. on
I

. Then

\|u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq 8(\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)})^{\frac{4}{3}}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{2}{3}}.

Therefore, applying Lemma
3.3
to the functions
\zeta|s|^{2}-u_{\mu}^{(x,v)}
and
\zeta|s|^{2}-u_{\nu}^{(x,v)}
on each compact segment
[\alpha_{i}(x,v),\beta_{i}(x,v)]
and using Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S}|\nabla\phi_{\mu}(x)-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}^{\eqref{0.54}}

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}}{c_{n}d_{n}}\int_{{\rm S}X}\sum_{i\in I_{S}(x,v)}\int_{\alpha_{i}(x,v)}^{\beta_{i}(x,v)}\langle\nabla\phi_{\mu}(b_{s}(x,v))-\nabla\phi_{\nu}(b_{s}(x,v)),t_{s}(x,v)\rangle^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{{\rm S}X}\sum_{i\in I_{S}(x,v)}\left(\int_{\alpha_{i}(x,v)}^{\beta_{i}(x,v)}|\phi_{\mu}(b_{s}(x,v))-\phi_{\nu}(b_{s}(x,v))|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{{\rm S}X}\left(\#I_{S}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\phi_{\mu}(b_{s})-\phi_{\nu}(b_{s})|^{2}\chi_{S}(b_{s})\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\int_{{\rm S}X}\#I_{S}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int_{{\rm S}X}\int_{0}^{1}|\phi_{\mu}(b_{s})-\phi_{\nu}(b_{s})|^{2}\chi_{S}(b_{s})\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\frac{c_{n}}{a_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{{\rm S}X}\#I_{S}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int_{S}|\phi_{\mu}-\phi_{\nu}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},

where

depends on

, and the last inequality follows from the invariance of
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
under the geodesic flow.

One-dimensional BV estimate.

We now aim to control
\int_{{\rm S}X}\#I_{S}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}
, the average number of times a geodesic crosses the boundary of
S
. The argument of
[
26

relies on the existence of
T>0
, so that for
(x,v)\in{\rm S}X
, the geodesic
s\mapsto b_{s}(x,v)
is minimizing on
[0,T]
, and hence in particular does not self-intersect. In an Alexandrov space, one cannot in general expect such a uniform injectivity radius bound. We use a localization method to overcome this difficulty.

In the following proposition, we denote by
|{\mathrm{D}}f|([0,1])
the total variation of a function
f
on
[0,1]
and denote by
{\rm{Per}}(S)
the perimeter of a set
S\subset X
. We refer to
[
33
,
1
]
for
\rm{BV}
functions and sets of finite perimeter in metric measure spaces.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume
{\rm{Per}}(S)<+\infty

\int_{{\rm S}X}\#I_{S}(x,v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)\leq c_{n}\big(\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{1})+{\rm{Per}}(S)\big).

Proof.

Let
(x,v)\in{\rm S}X
be such that
s\mapsto b_{s}(x,v)
is well-defined on
[0,1]
, and let
u\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})
.
Note that
s\mapsto b_{s}(x,v)
has unit speed, then

\left|\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}u(b_{s}(x,v))\right|\leq\left|\nabla u\right|(b_{s}(x,v)),~~~\text{a.e. $s\in[0,1]$},

and

|{\mathrm{D}}u(b_{s}(x,v))|([0,1])=\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}s}u(b_{s}(x,v))\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla u|(b_{s}(x,v))\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

(3.6)

Let
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(X,{\mathrm{d}})
be such that
u_{k}\rightarrow\chi_{S}
in
L^{1}(\mathcal{H}^{n})
and

\int_{X}|\nabla u_{k}|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm{Per}}(S).

(3.7)

Then

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm S}X}\int_{0}^{1}\left|u_{k}(b_{s}(x,v))-\chi_{S}(b_{s}(x,v))\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\rm S}X}\left|u_{k}(b_{s}(x,v))-\chi_{S}(b_{s}(x,v))\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)\,{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle{=}

\displaystyle c_{n}\int_{X}|u_{k}(x)-\chi_{S}(x)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(x)\rightarrow 0.

So up to taking a subsequence, it holds that for
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
-a.e.
(x,v)\in{\rm S}X
,

u_{k}(b_{s}(x,v))\rightarrow\chi_{S}(b_{s}(x,v))\quad\text{in $L^{1}([0,1])$ as $k\to\infty$}.

(3.9)

By lower semicontinuity of the total variation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm S}X}|{\mathrm{D}}\chi_{S}(b_{s}(x,v))|([0,1])\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm S}X}\varliminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}|{\mathrm{D}}u_{k}(b_{s}(x,v))|([0,1])\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}^{\text{Fatou}}

\displaystyle\varliminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{{\rm S}X}|{\mathrm{D}}u_{k}(b_{s}(x,v))|([0,1])\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}^{*}

\displaystyle\varliminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}c_{n}\int_{X}|\nabla u_{k}|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{n}\mathop{=}^{\eqref{Per(S)}}c_{n}{\rm{Per}}(S),

where
(*)
follows from (
3.6
) and the invariance of
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
under the geodesic flow.

Finally, notice that

\#I_{S}(x,v)\leq 1+\frac{1}{2}|{\mathrm{D}}\chi_{S}(b_{s}(x,v))|([0,1])

(3.11)

and that only the elements
(x,v)\in{\rm S}X
for which
x\in S_{1}
have a non-vanishing contribution. Combining with (
3.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm S}X}\#I_{S}(x,v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{S}(x,v)\leq

\displaystyle c_{n}\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{1})+\frac{1}{2}c_{n}{\rm{Per}}(S).

(3.12)

∎

Appendix A
Poincaré inequality: local to global

Lemma A.1
.

Let
x_{0}\in S
,
0<r_{0}\leq 1
and
B=B(x_{0},r_{0})\subseteq S
,
\rho_{B}=\frac{\rho\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{B}$}}{\rho(B)}
. Then the following strong local
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality holds for
f\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(B,{\mathrm{d}})
:

\int_{B}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho_{B}(x)\leq C_{4}r_{0}\int_{B}|\nabla_{x}f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho_{B},

(A.1)

where
C_{4}
depends on
K,N,a_{1},a_{2}
.

Proof.

By
[
18
, Chapter 9]
and
[
42
, Remark 3.3]
,
\mathfrak{m}
satisfies the
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality:

\int_{B}|f-\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq c_{1}r_{0}\int_{B}|\nabla_{x}f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m},

(A.2)

where
c_{1}
depends on
K,N
,
\mathfrak{m}_{B}=\frac{\mathfrak{m}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{B}$}}{\mathfrak{m}(B)}
. Since
a_{1}\mathfrak{m}\leq\rho\leq a_{2}\mathfrak{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B}|f-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B}|f-\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho+\int_{B}|\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho

(A.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2a_{2}\int_{B}|f-\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{m}_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2a_{2}c_{1}r_{0}\int_{B}|\nabla_{x}f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq 2\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}c_{1}r_{0}\int_{B}|\nabla_{x}f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho,

which is the thesis.
∎

Next we prove an
L^{1}
-variant of the gluing Lemma in
[
30
, Lemma 3.3]
, following the same Boman-chain decomposition, utilizing the doubling property directly instead of the maximal function estimates.

Definition A.2

(Boman chain condition)
.

We say that a probability measure
\rho
on an open set
S
of a metric space satisfies
Boman chain condition
with parameters
E,F,G>1
if there is a covering
\mathcal{F}
of
S
by open balls
B\in\mathcal{F}
such that:

1.

For any
x\in S
,

\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\chi_{2B}(x)\leq E\chi_{S}(x).

2.

For some fixed ball
B_{0}
in
\mathcal{F}
, called the
central ball
, and for every
B\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists a chain
B_{0},B_{1},\ldots,B_{N}=B
of distinct balls from
\mathcal{F}
such that

B\subset FB_{j},~~~\forall j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}.

3.

Consecutive balls of the above chain overlap quantitatively:

\rho(B_{j}\cap B_{j+1})\geq G^{-1}\max(\rho(B_{j}),\rho(B_{j+1})),~~~\forall j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}.

Lemma A.3
.

For any
f\in L^{1}(\rho)
, it holds that

\int_{S}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\leq C_{5}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\rho(B)\int_{B}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho_{B}(x),

(A.4)

where
C_{5}
depends on
K,N,\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S),S
.

Proof.

Since
S
is a John domain, and
\rho
is a doubling measure on
S
, by
[
26
, Proposition 3.7]
,
\rho
satisfies the Boman chain condition. Hence there exists a covering
\mathcal{F}
of
S
satisfying Definition
A.2
.

For the central ball
B_{0}
, note that

\displaystyle\int_{S}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)+\int_{S}|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)

(A.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{S}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x).

For
B\in\mathcal{F}
, denote
a_{B}=\int_{B}|f-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho=\rho(B)\int_{B}|f-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho_{B}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is a covering of
S
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{S}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\leq\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{B}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)

(A.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\left(\int_{B}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)+\int_{B}|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\left(a_{B}+\rho(B)|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\right).

For any
B\in\mathcal{F}
, by Boman chain condition, there exists a chain
B_{0},B_{1},\dots,B_{N}=B
of distinct balls from
\mathcal{F}
, such that for any
j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j}}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j+1}}}(f)|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\rho(B_{j}\cap B_{j+1})}\int_{B_{j}\cap B_{j+1}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j}}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j+1}}}(f)\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho\right|

(A.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho(B_{j}\cap B_{j+1})}\int_{B_{j}\cap B_{j+1}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j}}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j+1}}}(f)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho(B_{j}\cap B_{j+1})}\left(\int_{B_{j}\cap B_{j+1}}\left|f-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j}}}(f)\right|+\left|f-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j+1}}}(f)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{a_{B_{j}}+a_{B_{j+1}}}{\rho(B_{j}\cap B_{j+1})}\overset{*}{\leq}G\left(\frac{a_{B_{j}}}{\rho(B_{j})}+\frac{a_{B_{j+1}}}{\rho(B_{j+1})}\right),

where
(*)
follows from the quantitative chain overlap of Boman chain condition
3
.

Thus, we have

|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j}}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{j+1}}}(f)|\leq 2G\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{a_{B_{j}}}{\rho(B_{j})}\overset{*}{\leq}2G\sum_{B\subset F\bar{B}}\frac{a_{\bar{B}}}{\rho(\bar{B})},

(A.8)

where
\sum_{B\subset F\bar{B}}
means that the sum runs over all
\bar{B}\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying
B\subset F\bar{B}
, and
(*)
follows from the Boman chain condition
2
.
Then by Fubini–Tonelli theorem,

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\rho(B)|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\leq

\displaystyle 2G\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\rho(B)\sum_{B\subset F\overline{B}}\frac{a_{\overline{B}}}{\rho(\overline{B})}\leq 2G\sum_{\bar{B}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{a_{\bar{B}}}{\rho(\bar{B})}\sum_{B\subset F\bar{B}}\rho(B).

(A.9)

By Boman chain condition
1
and the doubling property of
\rho
, we have

\sum_{B\subset F\bar{B}}\rho(B)\leq E\rho(F\bar{B})\leq E\beta^{2}F^{\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}}\rho(\bar{B}),

(A.10)

where
\beta=\beta(K,N,\mathop{\rm diam}\nolimits(S))
is the doubling constant.

Combining (
A.5
), (
A.6
), (
A.9
) and (
A.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S}|f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho(x)\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\left(a_{B}+\rho(B)|\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B_{0}}}(f)|\right)

(A.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(1+2\beta^{2}EF^{\frac{\log\beta}{\log 2}}G)\sum_{B\in\mathcal{F}}\rho(B)\int_{B}|f-\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{B}}(f)|\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho_{B},

which is the thesis.
∎

Approximation and parameterized algorithms for covering disjointness-compliable set families
Zeev Nutov, Anael Vaknin

Abstract
A set-family ℱ {\cal F} is disjointness-compliable if A ′ ⊆ A ∈ ℱ A^{\prime}\subseteq A\in{\cal F} implies A ′ ∈ ℱ A^{\prime}\in{\cal F} or A ∖ A ′ ∈ ℱ A\setminus A^{\prime}\in{\cal F} ; if ℱ {\cal F} is also symmetric then ℱ {\cal F} is proper . A classic result of Goemans and Williamson [SODA 92:307-316] states that the problem of covering a proper set-family by a min-cost edge set admits approximation ratio 2 2 , by a classic primal-dual algorithm. However, there are several famous algorithmic problems whose set-family ℱ {\cal F} is disjointness-compliable but not symmetric – among them k k -Minimum Spanning Tree ( k k -MST) , Generalized Point-to-Point Connection (G-P2P) , Group Steiner , Covering Steiner , multiroot versions of these problems, and others. We will show that any such problem admits approximation ratio O ⁡ ( α ​ log ⁡ τ ) O(\alpha\log\tau) , where τ \tau is the number of inclusion-minimal sets in the family ℱ {\cal F} that models the problem and α \alpha is the best known approximation ratio for the case when τ = 1 \tau=1 . This immediately implies several results, among them the following two. • The first deterministic polynomial time O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) -approximation algorithm for the G-P2P problem; the previous O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) -approximation was both pseudopolynomial and randomized. Here the τ = 1 \tau=1 case is the k k -MST problem, that admits a constant approximation ratio. • Approximation ratio O ⁡ ( log 4 ⁡ n ) O(\log^{4}n) for the multiroot version of the Covering Steiner problem, where each root has its own set of groups. Here the τ = 1 \tau=1 case is the Covering Steiner problem, that admits approximation ratio O ⁡ ( log 3 ⁡ n ) O(\log^{3}n) . We also discuss the parameterized complexity of covering a disjointness-compliable family ℱ {\cal F} , when parametrized by τ \tau . We will show that if ℱ {\cal F} is proper then the problem is fixed parameter tractable and can be solved in time O ∗ ​ ( 3 τ ) O^{*}(3^{\tau}) . For the non-symmetric case we will show that the problem admits approximation ratio between α \alpha and α + 1 \alpha+1 in time O ∗ ​ ( 3 τ ) O^{*}(3^{\tau}) , which is essentially the best possible.

Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
An
edge
e
covers a set
A\subset V
if
e
has exactly one end in
A
.
An edge set
J\subseteq E
covers
A
if some
e\in J
covers
A
.
We say that
J

covers a set family

{\cal F}
, or that
J
is an
{\cal F}
-cover
,
if
J
covers every
A\in{\cal F}
.
The following generic meta-problem captures dozens of specific network design problems,
among them
Steiner Forest
,

k
-MST
,

Generalized Point–to-Point Connection
,

Group Steiner
,
and many more.

Set-Family Edge-Cover

Input:
A graph
G=(V,E)
with edge costs
\{c_{e}:e\in E\}
and a set family
{\cal F}
on
V
.

Output:
A min-cost forest
J\subseteq E
that covers
{\cal F}
.

In this problem the set family
{\cal F}
may not be given explicitly,
but we will assume that some queries related to
{\cal F}
can be answered in polynomial time.
Given a partial solution
J\subseteq E
to the problem,
we will consider the
residual instance
on node set
V^{J}
obtained by
contracting every connected component of
(V,J)
into a single node and
replacing
{\cal F}
by the
residual family

{\cal F}^{J}
on
V^{J}
,
that consists of the members of
{\cal F}
not covered by
J
.

Definition 1

(Goemans & Williamson
[
11
]
)
.

A set family
{\cal F}
is
disjointness-compliable
if it satisfies the
disjointness property
:

A^{\prime}\subseteq A\in{\cal F}
implies
A^{\prime}\in{\cal F}
or
A\setminus A^{\prime}\in{\cal F}
.
If
{\cal F}
is also symmetric (namely, if
A\in{\cal F}
implies
V\setminus A\in{\cal F}
) then
{\cal F}
is
proper
.

It is known that if
{\cal F}
is disjointness-compliable then so is any residual family
{\cal F}^{J}
of
{\cal F}
.

A classic result of Goemans and Williamson
[
11
]
from the early 90’s shows by an elegant proof that

Set-Family Edge-Cover
with proper
{\cal F}
admits approximation ratio
2
.
Slightly later, Williamson, Goemans, Mihail, and Vazirani
[
20
]

further extended this result to the more general class of
uncrossable families

(
A\cap B,A\cup B\in{\cal F}
or
A\setminus B,B\setminus A\in{\cal F}
whenever
A,B\in{\cal F}
),
by adding to the algorithm a novel reverse-delete phase.

However, there are several fundamental algorithmic problems whose
set-family
{\cal F}
is disjointness-compliable but
not
symmetric.
Consider for example the following three famous problems that are all disjointness-compliable
(meaning that each can be cast as
Set-Family Edge-Cover
with disjointness-compliable
{\cal F}
).
In all problems we are given a graph
G=(V,E)
with edge costs
\{c_{e}:e\in E\}

and seek a subgraph
H
of
G
that satisfies a prescribed property.

Quota Tree

We are given a root
r\in V
, integer charges
\{b(v)\geq 0:v\in V\}
, and an integer
q\geq 1
.

The connected component of
H
that contains
r
should have charge
\geq q
.

k
-Minimum Spanning Tree (
k
-MST)
is the case when

b(v)=1
for all
v\in V
and
q=k
.

Generalized Point-To-Point Connection (G-P2P)

We are given integer (possible negative) charges
\{b(v):v\in V\}
such that
b(V)\geq 0
.

Every connected component of
H
should have non-negative charge.

Covering Steiner

We are given a root
r\in V
, groups set
{\cal X}\subseteq 2^{V}
, and a demand
k_{X}\leq|X|
for each
X\in{\cal X}
.

The connected component of
H
that contains
r
should contain
k_{X}
nodes from each
X\in{\cal X}
.

Group Steiner
is the case when
k_{X}=1
for all
X\in{\cal X}
.

The
Quota Tree
problem with
b(v)\geq 1
for all
v\in V

can be reduced to
k
-MST
by inflating every node
v
with
b(v)\geq 2

into a star of cost zero on
b(v)
nodes.
While this reduction is approximation ratio preserving,
the inflated instance may have pseudo-polynomial size
\Theta({b(V)}+|E|)
.
On the other hand, when running a
k
-MST
algorithm,
one does not need to construct the inflated graph explicitly,
but can only simulate this construction.
The
k
-MST
problem admits approximation ratio
2

due to Garg
[
9
]
, see also
[
2
]
.
It may be that Garg’s
2
-approximation for
k
-MST

extends to
Quota Tree
, but we do not have a proof for that.
Earlier, Garg
[
8
]
gave a simpler
3
-approximation algorithm for
k
-MST
,
and Johnson, Minkoff & Phillips
[
15
]
described a reduction
that enables to ignore the zero charge nodes, and also verified that Garg’s

3
-approximation
[
8
]
extends to the
Quota Tree
problem.

Now consider the
G-P2P
problem.
If
b(V)=0
then we can consider the set family
\{A:b(A)\neq 0\}
which is proper
and achieve approximation ratio
2

[
11
,
13
]
.
If
b(V)>0
then we consider the family
\{A:b(A)<0\}
that is only disjointness-compliable.
The inclusion-minimal sets in this family are the singletons with negative charge.
Note that if we already have a partial solution
J
then we can contract
every connected component
C
of the graph
(V,J)

into a node
v_{C}
with charge
b(C)
and consider the residual
G-P2P
instance.
Such
J
is a feasible solution if and only if the residual instance has no negative nodes.
Thus a natural strategy is to find repeatedly
a cheap edge set that reduces the number of negative nodes.
This can be done in two ways:
solving the problem for one negative node separately by applying a
Quota Tree
algorithm,
or connecting several negative nodes.
We will show that choosing at each step the better among these two gives a logarithmic approximation ratio.

Now let us consider the
Group Steiner
and the
Covering Steiner
problems.
On star instances
Group Steiner
is equivalent to the
Hitting Set
problem.
On tree instances it admits approximation ratio

\displaystyle O(\log|{\cal X}|\cdot\log\max_{X\in{\cal X}}|X|)

[
10
]
,
and this is tight
[
14
]
.
The best known approximation for general graphs is obtained
by using the FRT probabilistic tree embedding
[
7
]
,
that invokes a factor of
O(\log n)
.
The same approximation ratios are achievable also
for the more general
Covering Steiner
problem
[
12
]
.
Now consider the
Multiroot Group Steiner
problem,
where there is a set
R
of roots and every
r\in R
has a set
{\cal X}^{r}
of groups.
One can see that the family

\{A:\exists r\in R,X\in{\cal X}^{r}\mbox{ such that }r\in A\mbox{ and }X\cap A=\emptyset\}

of this problem is disjointness-compliable, and its minimal sets are the roots.
A natural strategy to solve the problem is to repeatedly
add a cheap edge set that reduces the number of roots.
There are two ways to achieve this:
solving the problem for one root separately by applying a
Groups Steiner
algorithm,
or connecting several roots.
As we shall see, choosing at each step the better among these two
gives a polylogarithmic approximation ratio.

Having a variety of distinct and seemingly unrelated disjointness-compliable problems,
with totally different approximation ratios
(
2
for
k
-MST
and
O(\log^{3}n)
for
Group Steiner
),
we cannot expect to have a single algorithm for them all,
and clearly their approximability is distinct.
In fact, we do not exclude that there are natural disjointness-compliable problems
with polynomial approximation thresholds.
We note that a problem closely related to
Group Steiner
,
of increasing the edge-connectivity from
r
to each group
X\in{\cal X}
from
\ell
to
\ell+1
,
has such threshold
[
3
]
; however, this problem is not disjointness-compliable.

Roughly speaking, we will show that any disjointness-compliable problem admits
approximation ratio
O(\alpha\log\tau)
,
where
\tau
is the number of inclusion-minimal sets in
{\cal F}
and

\alpha
is the best known ratio for a “simple” subproblem of covering
a disjointness-compliable subfamily of
{\cal F}
with
\tau=1
.
We need some definitions and simple facts to present this result.

Definition 2
.

A
core
of a set-family
{\cal F}
, or an
{\cal F}
-core
for short,
is an inclusion minimal member of
{\cal F}
;
let
{\cal C}_{\cal F}
denote the family of
{\cal F}
-cores.
For
C\in{\cal C}_{\cal F}
the
halo-family
{\cal F}(C)
of
C
(w.r.t.
{\cal F}
)
is the family of all sets in
{\cal F}
that contain no core distinct from
C
.

The following is easy to verify.

Lemma 3
.

If
{\cal F}
is disjointness-compliable then

A\cap B\in{\cal F}
or
A\setminus B,B\setminus A\in{\cal F}
for any
A,B\in{\cal F}
.
Consequently,
C\subseteq A
or
C\cap A=\emptyset
holds
for any
A\in{\cal F}
and any
{\cal F}
-core
C
.
In particular, the
{\cal F}
-cores are pairwise disjoint.

Given an
{\cal F}
-core
C
we say that an edge set
J
is a
restricted
{\cal F}(C)
-cover

if
J
covers the halo-family
{\cal F}(C)
of
C
but no edge in
J
has an end in an
{\cal F}
-core distinct from
C
.
Note that a restricted
{\cal F}(C)
-cover can be computed by removing edges incident to
nodes in cores distinct from
C
and computing a cover of
{\cal F}(C)
.
We will make the following two assumptions about any residual family
{\cal F}^{J}
of
{\cal F}
.

\blacktriangleright

Assumption 1:

The membership in
{\cal F}^{J}
can be tested in polynomial time.

\blacktriangleright

Assumption 2:

For any
{\cal F}^{J}
-core
C
that has a restricted
{\cal F}^{J}(C)
-cover,
we can compute in polynomial time an
\alpha
-approximate one.

Under Assumptions 1 and 2, we prove the following.

Theorem 4
.

Set Family Edge Cover
with disjointness-compliable
{\cal F}

admits approximation ratio
\alpha+\max\{\alpha,2\}\cdot\ln\tau
,
where
\tau=|{\cal C}_{\cal F}|
is the number of
{\cal F}
-cores.

The proof of Theorem
4
is based on a generalization of the Klein & Ravi
[
16
]
spider decomposition.
The main difference is that in our case a spider may have just one “leg”,
but this leg may be “complex” – a restricted
{\cal F}^{J}(C)
-cover that is not a path
(for example, a
k
-MST/Quota Tree
solution in the case of
G-P2P
).
“One-leg spiders” were already considered in
[
19
]
,
but in that case the leg still was similar to a path.

Theorem
4
easily implies several results, among them the following two.

Theorem 5
.

G-P2P
admits approximation ratio
3(\ln\tau+1)
,
where
\tau=|\{v:b(v)<0\}|
is the number of nodes with negative charge.
The problem also admits approximation ratio
2+3[\ln(b(V)+2)+1]
,
where
b(V)=\sum_{v\in V}b(v)
is the total charge.

The previous best approximation algorithm for
G-P2P

[
13
]

achieved approximation ratio
O(\log\min\{n,b(V)+2\})

in pseudo-polynomial time (due to linear dependence on the maximum charge)
and was randomized (due to using the FRT probabilistic tree embedding
[
7
]
).
Our algorithm is deterministic and runs in polynomial time.

In the
Multiroot Covering Steiner
problem we are given a set
R
of roots and
every root
r\in R
has its own group set
{\cal X}^{r}\subseteq 2^{V}
and
a demand
k^{r}_{X}
for each
X\in{\cal X}^{r}
.
We require that for every
r\in R
, the connected component of the output graph
H
that contains
r

should contain
k^{r}_{X}
nodes from each group
X\in{\cal X}^{r}
.
We will prove the following.

Theorem 6
.

Multiroot Covering Steiner
admits approximation ratio
O(\alpha\log|R|)
,
where
\alpha
is the best known approximation ratio for
Covering Steiner
with
{\cal X}=\cup_{r\in R}{\cal X}^{r}

(
\alpha=\displaystyle O(\log|{\cal X}|\cdot\log\max_{X\in{\cal X}}|X|)
for tree instances
and for general graphs
\alpha
is by an
O(\log n)
factor larger).

Since many disjointness-compliable problems are NP-hard, also parameterized
exact and approximation algorithms are of interest.
A natural question then is whether the problem
is
fixed parameter tractable
w.r.t a parameter
p
, namely, if it can be solved in time

f(p)\cdot N^{O(1)}=O^{*}(f(p))
, where
N
is the input size
and the notation
O^{*}(f(p))
suppresses terms polynomial in
N
.
A related question is what approximation ratio can be achieved within this time bound.
One of the most studied problems is the Steiner
Tree problem, where we seek a min-cost subtree that spans a given set
T
of terminals.
Already in the 70’s, Dreyfus and Wagner
[
6
]
showed that this problem can be solved in
time
O^{*}(3^{\tau})
, where
\tau=|T|
is the number of terminals.
The currently best bound is
O^{*}(2^{\tau})

[
1
,
18
]
.
One can observe that
\tau
is in fact
the number of cores of the proper family
{\cal F}=\{A:\emptyset\neq A\cap T\neq T\}
of the problem.
Thus the number
\tau
of
{\cal F}
-cores is a very natural parameter.
We will prove the following.

Theorem 7
.

Set-Family Edge-Cover
, when parametrized by the number
\tau
of
{\cal F}
-cores,
is fixed parameter tractable for proper
{\cal F}
and can be solved in time
O^{*}(3^{\tau})
, under Assumption 1.
If
{\cal F}
is only disjointness-compliable then the problem admits approximation ratio between

\alpha
and
\alpha+1
in time
O^{*}(3^{\tau})
, under Assumptions 1 and 2.

The second result in the theorem is almost the best possible, as
\alpha
is defined to be the best
known approximation ratio when
\tau=1
.
Using the theorem we will prove the following.

Theorem 8
.

Consider the
G-P2P
problem with
\tau
negative nodes.
In time
O^{*}(3^{\tau})
, it is possible to compute a
4
-approximate solution if
b(V)>0
, and
to solve the problem exactly if
b(V)=0
or if the charges are in the range
\{-1,0,n\}
.

We note that the contribution of our paper is not technical but rather conceptual –
we provide a very simple unified recipe for obtaining non-trivial approximation ratios
and parameterized algorithm for a large class of seemingly unrelated problems.

Some properties of disjointness-compliable families

The following simple fact is well known; we provide a proof for completeness of exposition.

Lemma 9
.

Any inclusion-minimal cover
J
of an arbitrary set family
{\cal F}
is a forest.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
J
contains a cycle
Q
.
Let
e=uv
be an arbitrary edge in
Q
. Since
P=Q\setminus\{e\}
is a
uv
-path,
then for any set
A
covered by
e
, there is
e^{\prime}\in P
that covers
A
.
This implies that
J\setminus\{e\}
also covers
{\cal F}
, contradicting the minimality of
J
.
∎

In what follows, let
{\cal F}
be a disjointness-compliable set-family
and suppose that Assumption 1 and 2 hold.
Due to Lemma
3
, we may contract each
{\cal F}
-core
C
into a single node
t_{C}

and assume that
all
{\cal F}
-cores are singletons
, which we view as
terminals
.
Let
T
denote the set of terminals. Thus for any
t\in T
,

{\cal F}(\{t\})
is the halo-family of the core
C=\{t\}
, and
A\in{\cal F}(\{t\})
iff
A\in{\cal F}

and
A\cap T=\{t\}
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
{\cal F}
be disjointness-compliable and let
A\in{\cal F}
and
B\subseteq V\setminus T
. Then

[(i)]

1.

A\setminus B\in{\cal F}
.

2.

If
{\cal F}
is also symmetric (namely, proper) then also
A\cup B\in{\cal F}
.

Proof.

For (i), let
A^{\prime}=A\cap B
and note that we cannot have

A^{\prime}\in{\cal F}
since
A^{\prime}
contains no terminal.
Thus
A\setminus B=A\setminus A^{\prime}\in{\cal F}
, as required.
Applying (i) on
\bar{A}=V\setminus A
and using symmetry gives (ii).
∎

Lemma 11
.

Let
H=(V_{H},E_{H})
be a non-trivial connected component of an inclusion-minimal cover
J
of a disjointness-compliable family
{\cal F}
.
Then
H
is a tree (by Lemma
9
) and the following holds:

[(i)]

1.

V_{H}
contains at least one terminal.

2.

If
V_{H}
contains exactly one terminal
t
then

E_{H}
is a restricted
{\cal F}(\{t\})
-cover.

3.

If
{\cal F}
is proper then every leaf of
H
is a terminal; thus
V_{H}
contains at least two terminals.

Proof.

We prove (i).
Let
e=uv\in E_{H}
. Then
H\setminus\{e\}
is a union of
a tree
H_{u}
that contains
u
and a tree
H_{v}
that contains
v
.
By the minimality of
J
, there is
A\in{\cal F}
such that
\delta_{J}(A)=\{e\}
.
Thus
A
contains exactly one of
H_{u},H_{v}
, say
H_{u}
,
and is disjoint to
H_{v}
.
Since
{\cal F}
is disjointness-compliable,
H_{u}\in{\cal F}
or
A\setminus H_{u}\in{\cal F}
.
Note that
\delta_{J}(A\setminus H_{u})=\emptyset
, thus
H_{u}\in{\cal F}
,
and this implies that
H_{u}
contains a terminal.

We prove (ii).
Since
H
contains no terminal distinct from
t

we just need to prove that
E_{H}
covers
{\cal F}(\{t\})
.
Suppose to the contrary that
E_{H}
does not cover some
A\in{\cal F}(\{t\})
.
Then
V_{H}\subseteq A
and thus
V_{H}\in{\cal F}
, since
A\setminus V_{H}
contains no terminal.
But
V_{H}
is not covered by
J
, contradicting that
J
covers
{\cal F}
.

We prove (iii). Let
e
be the unique edge in
J
incident to a leaf
v
of
H
.
Since
J
is inclusion-minimal, there is
A\in{\cal F}
such that
e

is the unique edge in
{\cal F}
that covers
A
.
By symmetry, the set
V\setminus A
is in
{\cal F}
and has the same property.
One of
A,V\setminus A
, say
A
, contains
v

(and possibly some other components of
(V,J)
).
Note that
A\setminus\{v\}
is not covered by
J
, hence
A\setminus\{v\}\notin{\cal F}
.
Thus
\{v\}\in{\cal F}
, hence
v
is a terminal.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4

Here we prove Theorem
4
.
We start by describing the Klein-Ravi decomposition
[
16
]
of a tree (or of a forest) into spiders.

Definition 12
.

A
spider
is a rooted tree with at least two nodes,
such that any its node, except of maybe the root, has degree
\leq 2
.
Given a graph with a set
T
of terminals,
we say that a spider
S
in this graph is a
T
-spider
if every terminal in
S
is a root or a leaf of
S
,
and any other node in
S
is not a terminal.

Lemma 13

(Klein & Ravi
[
16
]
)
.

Any tree
H
with a set
T
of at least two terminals has a decomposition
{\cal S}

into node-disjoint
T
-spiders such that every terminal belongs to some spider.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
|T|
.
The induction base case
|T|=2
is trivial, so assume that
|T|\geq 3
.
If
H
has a leaf
v\notin T
then by the induction hypothesis
H\setminus\{v\}
, and thus also
H
,
has a decomposition
{\cal S}
as in the lemma.
Assume therefore that all leaves of
H
are terminals.
Root
H
at some leaf
r
.
If
H
is a path then the statement is trivial.
Otherwise,
H
has a node
s
of degree
\geq 3
such that
the subtree
S
that consists of
s
and all its descendants is a spider with at least two leaves.
If
H
is not a spider, then
s
has an ancestor
s^{\prime}
such that the degree of
s^{\prime}
is at least
3
,
but every node in the (possibly empty) set

Here we prove Theorem

We start with proper families.
The idea is simple.
We “guess” the set of terminals

Then we compute an optimal Steiner tree on

The total time complexity is dominated by that of computing an optimal Steiner tree

We now provide a formal proof.
We say that a set

For

We claim that choosing

We let

is found recursively.
However for this to work, our set

[(i)]

1.

We should have
{\cal F}={\cal F}^{\prime}\cup{\cal F}^{\prime\prime}
, as otherwise
J^{\prime}\cup J^{\prime\prime}
may not cover
{\cal F}
.

2.

If we cover
{\cal F}^{\prime}
by cost
c^{\prime}
, then the optimal cost of covering
{\cal F}^{\prime\prime}
will be at most
{\sf opt}-c^{\prime}
.

In what follows, we will show that either
S=T
, or there exists
S\subseteq T
with
2\leq|S|\leq|T|-2
that has these properties.
The next lemma gives a sufficient condition for (i) to hold.

Lemma 22
.

If
S\notin{\cal F}
then
{\cal F}={\cal F}_{S}\cup{\cal F}_{T\setminus S}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\cal F}_{S}\cup{\cal F}_{T\setminus S}\subseteq{\cal F}
.
Suppose to the contrary that there is
A\in{\cal F}\setminus({\cal F}_{S}\cup{\cal F}_{T\setminus S})
.
Then
A\cap T=S
or
\bar{A}\cap T=S
,
say (by symmetry)
A\cap T=S
.
Then by Lemma
10

S=A\cap T\in{\cal F}
, contradicting the assumption
S\notin{\cal F}
.
∎

For
S\subseteq T
let us use the following notation:

•

{\sf smt}(S)
is the minimum cost of a Steiner tree with terminal set
S
in the graph
G\setminus(T\setminus S)
.

•

{\sf opt}(S)
is the minimum cost of an
({\cal F}_{S}\setminus{\cal F}_{T})
-cover in the graph
G\setminus(T\setminus S)
.

Note that
{\sf opt}(T\setminus S)
is the minimum cost of an

({\cal F}_{T\setminus S}\setminus{\cal F}_{S})
-cover in the graph
G\setminus\delta(S)
.
The quantity we want to compute is
{\sf opt}(T)
.

Lemma 23
.

Let
H
be a component of an
{\cal F}
-cover
J
and let
S=H\cap T
.
Then

[(i)]

1.

S\notin{\cal F}
, and thus
{\cal F}={\cal F}_{S}\cup{\cal F}_{T\setminus S}
(by Lemma
22
).

2.

J\setminus H
covers
{\cal F}_{T\setminus S}\setminus{\cal F}_{S}
.

3.

S=T
or
2\leq|S|\leq|T|-2
.

Furthermore, if
J
is an optimal solution then

{\sf opt}(T)={\sf smt}(S)+{\sf opt}(T\setminus S)

Proof.

For (i) note that by Lemma
10
,
S\in{\cal F}
implies
H\in{\cal F}
, contradicting that
J
covers
{\cal F}
.
For (ii), suppose to the contrary that
J\setminus S
does not cover some
A\in{\cal F}_{T\setminus S}\setminus{\cal F}_{S}
.
Then
A
is covered by edges of
H
only.
Consider the set
A^{\prime}=A\cap T\subset T\setminus S
.
Then
A^{\prime}\in{\cal F}
by Lemma
10
, but
A^{\prime}
is not covered by
J
,
which gives a contradiction.
For (iii), note that by Lemma
11
every component contains at least two terminals,
thus if
J
has at least two components then
2\leq|S|\leq|T|-2
.

If
J
is an optimal solution, then
{\sf opt}=c(J)=c(H)+c(J\setminus H)
.
By Lemma
22
, taking a Steiner tree on
H
that spans
S
and an
optimal cover of
{\cal F}_{T\setminus S}\setminus{\cal F}_{S}
gives a feasible solution,
hence
{\sf smt}(S)+{\sf opt}(T\setminus S)\geq{\sf opt}
.
On the other hand
{\sf smt}(S)\leq c(H)
and
{\sf opt}(T\setminus S)\leq c(J\setminus S)
.
The claim follows.
∎

Let

h(T,S)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\sf smt}(S)+{\sf opt}(T\setminus S)&\mbox{if}\ \ S\notin{\cal F}\\
\infty&\mbox{if}\ \ S\in{\cal F}\end{array}\right.

Lemma 24
.

Let
J^{*}
be an optimal
{\cal F}
-cover.
If
J^{*}
has exactly one component then
{\sf opt}(T)={\sf smt}(T)
. Else,

{\sf opt}(T)=\min\{h(T,S):S\subset T,2\leq|S|\leq|T|-2\}

Proof.

The one component case is obvious, so assume that

J^{*}
has at least two components.
By Lemma
22
,

{\sf opt}(T)\leq h(T,S)
for any
S\notin{\cal F}
.
By Lemma
23
there exists
S
with
2\leq|S|\leq|T|-2
for which an equality holds.
The lemma follows.
∎

We now consider general disjointness-compliable families.
The algorithm is similar to the symmetric case.
We “guess” the set
S
of terminals of some component
H
of an optimal solution
J^{*}
,
when now we may also have
|S|=1
.
Then we compute an approximate solution to the problem of computing a Steiner tree on
S
that
also covers the halo family of the contracted node
v_{S}
, if
v_{S}
is a terminal.
This problem can be formally stated as follows:

In the graph
G\setminus(T\setminus S)
, find an edge set
H
that covers
the restriction of
{\cal F}
to
V\setminus(T\setminus S)
.

If
|S|=1
then this problem admits approximation ratio
\alpha
, by Assumption 2.
Otherwise, this problem admits approximation ratio
\alpha+1
in time
O^{*}(2^{|S|})
.
For that we find an optimal Steiner tree
H_{S}
on
S
in
G\setminus(T\setminus S)
,
contract it into a new node
v_{S}
, and if
v_{S}
is a terminal of
{\cal F}^{J_{S}}

find an
\alpha
-approximate cover of
{\cal F}^{J_{S}}(\{v_{S}\})
(possible by Assumption 2).
But in specific cases, it might be possible in to achieve ratio
\alpha
when
|S|\geq 2
as well.

As in the symmetric case, the running time is dominated by computing the Steiner trees,
and thus the entire algorithm can be implemented in
O^{*}(3^{\tau})
time.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
7
.

6
Applications of Theorem
7

We now discuss some applications and consequence from Theorem
7
.
The next two corollaries are immediate consequences from the theorem.

Corollary 25
.

Multiroot Quota Tree
admits approximation ratio
4
in time
O^{*}\left(3^{|R|}\right)
.

Corollary 26
.

Multiroot Covering Steiner
admits approximation ratio

\displaystyle O(\log|{\cal X}|\cdot\log\max_{X\in{\cal X}}|X|\cdot\log n)

in time
O^{*}\left(3^{|R|}\right)
, where
{\cal X}=\cup_{r\in R}{\cal X}^{r}
.

For specific problems, it is sometimes possible to obtain a better running time than the one in Theorem
7
.
We illustrate this on the
Steiner Forest
problem, that can be stated as follows.

Steiner Forest

We are given a set
T
of
t
terminals and a partition
\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}\}
of
T
,
|T_{i}|\geq 2
for all
i
.

For every
T_{i}
, there should be a connected component of
H
that contains
T_{i}
.

Set family:
{\cal F}=\{A:A\mbox{ divides some }T_{i}\}
.

Corollary 27
.

Steiner Forest
can be solved in time
O^{*}(2^{p+\tau})
.

Proof.

Here we fill a table of size
2^{p}-1
, with entries being non-empty subsets of
\{1,\ldots,p\}
.
For each subset
S
we compute a Steiner tree on
\cup_{i\in S}T_{i}
in

O^{*}\left(2^{\sum_{i\in S}|T_{i}|}\right)=O^{*}(2^{\tau})
time.
The time complexity is therefore
O^{*}(2^{\tau}\cdot 2^{p})=O^{*}(2^{\tau+p})
.
∎

If
p
is small, then the running time can be smaller than
O^{*}(3^{\tau})
,
and in any case
p\leq\tau/2
.

Now we prove Theorem
8
.
We need to prove that for the
G-P2P
problem it is possible in time

O^{*}(3^{\tau})
to do the following:

[(i)]

1.

To compute a
4
-approximate solution if
b(V)>0
.

2.

To solve the problem exactly if
b(V)=0
.

3.

To solve the problem exactly if the charges are in the range
\{-1,0,n\}
.

Part (i) is an immediate consequence from Theorem
7
and Lemma
18
.

Part (ii) is an immediate consequence from Theorem
7
and
the observation that if
b(V)=0
then the problem is modeled by a proper family

\{A:b(A)\neq 0\}
.

We prove part (iii).
Let us color the nodes with charge
-1
red and the nodes with charge
n
blue.
The problem then is to find a min-cost subgraph
H
such that
every connected component of
H
that contains a red node also contains a blue node.
We apply the same algorithm as in the proof of Theorem
7
with the following minor change –
when we guess the set
S
of terminals (red nodes) of a component, we also guess one blue node of that component.
The total time we need to compute all Steiner trees with
k
terminals (red nodes) and one blue node is

O^{*}\left({k\choose\tau}\cdot\beta\cdot 2^{k+1}\right)
,
where
\beta
is the number of red nodes.
However

O^{*}\left({k\choose\tau}\cdot\beta\cdot 2^{k+1}\right)=O^{*}\left({k\choose\tau}\cdot 2^{k}\right)
, thus we get the same overall time
O^{*}(3^{\tau})
as before.

7
Concluding remarks

We showed that disjointness-compliable problems admit approximation ratio
O(\alpha\log n)

by a simple greedy algorithm that at each step
chooses the better among connecting some cores by a spider or
computing an
\alpha
-approximate restricted cover of a halo family of a single core.
We illustrated this approach on several non-symmetric disjointness-compliable problems
related to
k
-MST/Quota Tree
or to
Group/Covering Steiner
problems,
either matching or outperforming the known algorithms.
We also note that our approach extends to node-weighted versions of these problems.

Nutov
[
19
]
showed that the Klein-Ravi spider decomposition
[
16
]

extends to edge-covers of uncrossable families;
recall that
{\cal F}
is uncrossable if
A\cap B,A\cup B\in{\cal F}

or
A\setminus B,B\setminus A\in{\cal F}
whenever
A,B\in{\cal F}
.
Unfortunately, the non-symmetric disjointness-compliable families considered here have a weaker property —

A\cap B\in{\cal F}
or
A\setminus B,B\setminus A\in{\cal F}
whenever
A,B\in{\cal F}
, see Lemma
3
.
It would be interesting to establish whether the approach presented in this paper extend to this
type of families.
Another interesting direction is to establish what types of disjointness-compliable families enable to admit a constant
approximation (e.g.,
k
-MST
and
Quota Tree
),
a polylogarithmic approximation (e.g.,
Group Steiner
on trees),
and when even a polylogatrithmic approximation is unlikely.
We also note that the approximability status of
G-P2P
is still open –
the best known ratio is logarithmic, but no super constant approximation threshold is known.
For the
Multi-Instance Quota Tree
problem, where there is one root but many charge vectors,
we don’t even have a polylogarithmic approximation ratio.

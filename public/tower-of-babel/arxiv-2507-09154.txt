1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, and let
dA
denote the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.
For
0<p<\infty
, the Bergman space
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})~(\alpha>-1)
is the space consisting of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
that belong to
L^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
, where
dA_{\alpha}(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
,
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,\alpha}
defined as follows:

\|f\|_{p,\alpha}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,dA_{\alpha}(z)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It is well-known that
L^{2}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product defined by

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\,dA_{\alpha}(z),~f,g\in L^{2}_{a}(dA_{\alpha}).

For
1<p<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, we define the integral pairing

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\,dA_{\alpha}(z),

where
f\in L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
and
g\in L_{a}^{q}(dA_{\alpha})
.

The function

K(w,z)=K_{z}(w)=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{z}w)^{2+\alpha}}

is called the reproducing kernel in
L_{a}^{2}(dA_{\alpha})
.
For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, the function
k_{z}(w)
defined by

k_{z}(w)=(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{2+\alpha}{2}}K_{z}(w)

is called the normalized reproducing kernel at
z
. It is also in
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
for
1\leq p<\infty
.

An interesting problem is to consider the boundedness and compactness of linear operators on Bergman spaces.
By a variety of methods, some remarkable work has been done on boundedness and compactness of Toeplitz operators with variable symbols, see
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
13
]
.
Moreover, a list of open problems about the boundedness, compactness and Fredholm properties of Toeplitz operators have been summarized in
[
10
]
.

Recently, Mitkovski and Wick
[
9
]
provided a sufficient condition for the boundedness of general operator
T
on reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces
H
, which consist of holomorphic functions in
L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)
, where
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\mu
is a certain finite measure on
\Omega
. Furthermore, under the aforementioned boundedness assumptions, the operator
T
is compact on
H
if and only if
\|Tk_{z}\|\rightarrow 0
as
d(z,0)\rightarrow\infty
.

For a bounded linear operator
S
on
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})~(0<p<\infty)
, the operator
S_{z}
is defined by
S_{z}=U_{z}SU_{z}
, where
U_{z}
is a bounded operator on
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
defined by

U_{z}f=\big(f\circ\varphi_{z}\big)\cdot k_{z}.

For
z\in\mathbb{D}
, let
\varphi_{z}
be the Möbius transformation on
\mathbb{D}
defined by

\varphi_{z}(w)=\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{z}w},~w\in\mathbb{D}.

Furthermore,
U_{z}
is a unitary operator on
L_{a}^{2}(dA_{\alpha})
.

In 2004, Miao and Zheng
[
8
]
showed that if
S
is a bounded operator on
L_{a}^{p}(dA)~(1<p<\infty)
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|S_{z}1\|_{m}<\infty,~\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|S_{z}^{\ast}1\|_{m}<\infty,

for some
m>3/(p-1)
,
then
S
is compact on
L_{a}^{p}(dA)~(1<p<\infty)
if and only if
\widetilde{S}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
. Here,
\widetilde{S}
denotes the Berezin transform of
S
, defined by

\widetilde{S}(z)=\langle Sk_{z},k_{z}\rangle,~z\in\mathbb{D}.

In particular, the result for
p=2
agrees with the special case
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
result of Mitkovski and Wick
[
9
]
. Similar results on Fock spaces have also been studied by the authors of this paper in
[
6
]
(see also
[
11
]
).

In this paper, we further explore the boundedness and compactness of linear operators
S
on the Bergman space
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
using a different method.
We first establish a sufficient condition for the boundedness of a linear operator
S
on
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
.
We then derive the compactness of
S
on
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
, where the requirement of
\|S^{\ast}_{z}1\|_{m}
is dropped, in contrast to the result in
[
8
]
(see Remark
1
for details). It is well known that linear combinations of reproducing kernels are dense in Bergman spaces. From the definition of
S_{z}
, we have

(S_{z}1)(w)=k_{z}(\varphi_{z}(w))\cdot k_{z}(w).

This indicates that if
S
is well defined on reproducing kernels, then
S_{z}1
is well defined.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the domain of
S
contains all reproducing kernels. Our main results are as follows:

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-1
and
S
is a linear operator on
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
. If there exist a positive constant
C
and some
m>p\frac{2+\alpha}{1+\alpha}\max\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\}
, such that
\|S_{z}1\|_{m,\alpha}\leq C
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
S
is bounded.

Theorem B
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-1
and
S
is a linear operator on
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
. If there exist a positive constant
C
and some
m>p\frac{2+\alpha}{1+\alpha}\max\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\}
, such that
\|S_{z}1\|_{m,\alpha}\leq C
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
S
is compact if and only if
\widetilde{S}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Remark 1
.

Theorem B weakens the assumption of Theorem 1.1 in
[
8
]
.
Specifically, the condition
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|S_{z}^{\ast}1\|_{m}<\infty
has been removed in Theorem B.
On the other hand, when
\alpha=0
and
1<p<\frac{3}{2}
, it is easy to verify that

p\frac{2+\alpha}{1+\alpha}\max\Big\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\Big\}<\frac{3}{p-1}.

Thus, our requirement for
m
is weaker than that in
[
8
]
, because
\|f\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{q}
whenever
p<q
.

2.
Bounded linear operators

In this section, we provide sufficient conditions for the boundedness of linear operators on Bergman spaces
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
.

The
Bergman disk
is defined by
D(a,r)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:~\beta(z,a)<r\}

where

\beta(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\rho(z,w)}{1-\rho(z,w)}

is the
Bergman metric
, and

\rho(z,w)=\frac{|z-w|}{|1-z\overline{w}|}.

For
r>0
and
a_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
, we say that
\{a_{k}\}
is a
r
-lattice
if

\mathbb{D}=\bigcup_{k}D(a_{k},r);\ \ \ \beta(a_{i},a_{j})\geq t/2\ \text{ for all}\ i,j\ \text{with}\ i\neq j.

The following lemmas are used in our proofs:

Lemma 1
.

[
14
, Theorem 4.33]

Suppose
p>0
,
\alpha>-1
, and

b>\max\Big\{1,\frac{1}{p}\Big\}+\frac{\alpha+1}{p}.

Then there exists a constant
\sigma>0
such that for any
r
-lattice
\{a_{k}\}
in the Bergman metric, where
0<r<\sigma
, the space
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
consists exactly of functions of the form

(2.1)

f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{pb-2-\alpha}{p}}}{(1-z\overline{a_{k}})^{b}},

where
\{c_{k}\}\in l^{p}
. The series in
(\ref{formula1})
converges in the norm of
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
, and the norm of
f
in
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
,
\|f\|_{p,\alpha}
, is comparable to

\|f\|=\inf\Big\{\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}:\{c_{k}\}~\text{satisfies}~(\ref{formula1})\Big\}.

Lemma 2
.

[
5
, Theorem 1.3]

Suppose
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
c
is real,
t>-1
, and

I_{c,t}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-z\overline{w}|^{2+t+c}}\,dA(w).

(a)

If
c<0
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\frac{\Gamma(1+t)}{\Gamma(2+t)}\leq I_{c,t}(z)\leq\frac{\Gamma(1+t)\Gamma(-c)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{2+t-c}{2}\right)^{2}}.

(b)

If
c>0
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\frac{\Gamma(1+t)}{\Gamma(2+t)}\leq(1-|z|^{2})^{c}I_{c,t}(z)\leq\frac{\Gamma(1+t)\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{2+t+c}{2}\right)^{2}}.

(c)

If
c=0
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\frac{\Gamma(1+t)}{\Gamma(1+\frac{t}{2})}\leq|z|^{2}\Big(\log\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}\Big)^{-1}I_{c,t}(z)\leq\frac{1}{1+t}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
t_{1}>1
and

L(w)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{t_{1}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{t_{2}}},

where
\{a_{k}\}
is an
r
-lattice on
\mathbb{D}
.
Then

(2.2)

L(w)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{C_{t_{1},t_{2},r}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{t_{2}-t_{1}}},&t_{2}>t_{1},\quad w\in\mathbb{D},\\
C_{t_{1},t_{2},r}\log\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}},&t_{2}=t_{1},\quad w\in\mathbb{D},\\
C_{t_{1},t_{2},r},&t_{2}<t_{1}.\\
\end{array}\right.

where
C_{t_{1},t_{2},r}
is a positive constant depending on
t_{1},~t_{2},~r
.

Proof.

By applying Propositions 4.4 and 4.5 of
[
14
]
, we have

(2.3)

|D(a_{k},r)|=\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{2}s^{2}}{(1-|a_{k}|^{2}s^{2})^{2}},

where
s=\tanh r
.
By applying Proposition 4.5 in
[
14
]
again, we observe that

\frac{1-s}{1+s}(1-|v|^{2})\leq 1-|a_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{1+s}{1-s}(1-|v|^{2}),~v\in D(a_{k},r).

So

(2.4)

C_{t_{1},r}^{-1}(1-|v|^{2})^{t_{1}-2}\leq(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{t_{1}-2}\leq C_{t_{1},r}(1-|v|^{2})^{t_{1}-2},\quad v\in D(a_{k},r),

where

C_{t_{1},r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\frac{1-s}{1+s})^{t_{1}-2},&t_{1}<2;\\
(\frac{1-s}{1+s})^{2-t_{1}},&t_{1}\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

According to Lemma 4.30 in
[
14
]
, there exists a positive constant
C_{t_{2},r}
dependent on
r
and
t_{2}
, such that for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
,

(2.5)

|1-\overline{v}w|^{t_{2}}\leq C_{t_{2},r}|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{t_{2}},\quad v\in D(a_{k},r).

By combining (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{t_{1}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{t_{2}}}&=\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{t_{1}-2}(1-s^{2}|a_{k}|^{2})^{2}}{s^{2}|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{t_{2}}}|D(a_{k},r)|\\
&\leq C_{t_{1},r}C_{t_{2},r}\frac{(1+s^{2})^{2}}{s^{2}}\int_{D(a_{k},r)}\frac{(1-|v|^{2})^{t_{1}-2}}{|1-\overline{v}w|^{t_{2}}}dA(v).\end{split}

By Lemma 4.7 in
[
14
]
and Lemma
2
, there exists a positive constant
C_{t_{1},t_{2},r}
dependent on
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
and
r
, such that

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{t_{1}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{t_{2}}}&\leq C_{t_{1},t_{2},r}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|v|^{2})^{t_{1}-2}}{|1-\overline{v}w|^{2+(t_{1}-2)+(t_{2}-t_{1})}}\,dA(v),\\
\end{split}

which yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 4

[

[

If

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-1
, and
\sigma>0
such that for any
r
-lattice
\{a_{k}\}
in the Bergman metric, where
0<r<\sigma
. Then we can represent
f\in L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
as

f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}{p}}}{(1-\overline{a_{k}}z)^{2+\alpha}},

in such a way that the series converges in the norm topology of
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\leq C\|f\|_{p,\alpha}^{p}

for some positive constant
C
independent of
f
. Furthermore, if
\{f_{n}\}\rightarrow 0
weakly in
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
, then

(2.6)

\sum_{|a_{k}|\leq R}|c_{k,n}|^{p}\rightarrow 0~~\text{as}~~n\rightarrow\infty

for any fixed
R<1
, where
c_{k,n}
are coefficients of

f_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k,n}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}{p}}}{(1-\overline{a_{k}}z)^{2+\alpha}}.

Proof.

According to Lemma
1
, every
f\in L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
can be expressed as

(2.7)

f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}{p}}}{(1-\overline{a_{k}}z)^{2+\alpha}},

where
\{c_{k}\}\in l^{p}
,
\{a_{k}\}
is an
r
-lattice in the Bergman metric and the series in (
2.7
) converges in the norm of
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
.
Define an operator
T
as follows:

Tf(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{\alpha}(D_{k})f(a_{k})}{(1-\overline{a_{k}}z)^{2+\alpha}},~~f\in L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha}),

where
\{D_{k}\}
is a decomposition of
\mathbb{D}
according to Lemma 4.10 in
[
14
]
.
From the proof of Lemma
1
, it follows that

is bounded and invertible. Let
g=T^{-1}f
, then

c_{k}=\frac{A_{\alpha}(D_{k})g(a_{k})}{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}{p}}}.

By Proposition 4.5 in
[

for any

and

. Thus, there exists a positive constant

dependent only on

and

\begin{split}A_{\alpha}(D_{k})&\leq C_{r,\alpha}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{2+\alpha}.\\

Thus, by

(2.8)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}&\leq C_{r,\alpha}^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{\alpha}(D_{k})|g(a_{k})|^{p}\\

&\leq C_{r,\alpha}^{p-1}M\|T^{-1}f\|_{p,\alpha}^{p}\\
&\leq C\|f\|_{p,\alpha}^{p},\end{split}

where

[

Next, we show that
(\ref{f2})
holds. For any fixed

where

\frac{s+|a_{k}|}{1+s|a_{k}|}\leq\frac{s+R}{1+sR}<1,~\text{when}~0\leq|a_{k}|\leq R<1.

where

\mathbb{D}
.
Since
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
weakly, by Lemma
4
,
\{f_{n}\}
is bounded and
f_{n}(z)\rightarrow 0
uniformly on each compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
. According to Lemma

, we have

f_{n}(w)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k,n}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}{p}}}{(1-\overline{a_{k}}w)^{2+\alpha}}.

Let

In other words, for any

(2.9)

\begin{split}\sum_{|a_{k}|\leq R}|c_{k,n}|^{p}&\leq C_{r,\alpha}^{p-1}M\int_{\bigcup_{|a_{k}|\leq R}D_{k}}|g_{n}(w)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(w)\\

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-1
and
S
is a linear operator on
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
. If there exist a positive constant
C
and some

m>p\frac{2+\alpha}{1+\alpha}\max\Big\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\Big\},

such that
\|S_{z}1\|_{m,\alpha}\leq C
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
S
is bounded.

Proof.

From Lemma
1
, for
f\in L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
we have

(2.11)

f(w)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}f_{k}(w),

where
\{c_{k}\}\in l^{p}
,

\displaystyle f_{k}(w)=(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}{p}}K_{a_{k}}(w),

and
\{a_{k}\}
is an
r
-lattice in the Bergman metric.

By Lemma
7
, we have

(2.12)

\begin{split}|Sf_{k}(w)|&=|(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}{p}}SK_{a_{k}}(w)|\\
&\leq\|S_{a_{k}}1\|_{m,\alpha}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{(\frac{p-1}{p}-\frac{1}{m})(2+\alpha)}(1-|w|^{2})^{-\frac{2+\alpha}{m}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{(1-\frac{2}{m})(2+\alpha)}}.\\
\end{split}

Applying (
2.11
), (
2.12
), and Hölder’s inequality, we get

(2.13)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{D}}&|Sf(w)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(w)\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big|c_{k}\|S_{a_{k}}1\|_{m,\alpha}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{(\frac{p-1}{p}-\frac{1}{m})(2+\alpha)}(1-|w|^{2})^{-\frac{2+\alpha}{m}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{(1-\frac{2}{m})(2+\alpha)}}\Big|\bigg)^{p}\,dA_{\alpha}(w)\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big|c_{k}\|S_{a_{k}}1\|_{m,\alpha}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{(\frac{p-1}{p}-\frac{1}{m})(2+\alpha)-n_{1}}(1-|w|^{2})^{-\frac{2+\alpha}{m}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{(1-\frac{2}{m})(2+\alpha)-n_{2}}}\Big|^{p}\bigg)\\
&\quad\times\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{qn_{1}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{qn_{2}}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dA_{\alpha}(w),\\
\end{split}

where
0<n_{1},n_{2}<\infty
and
q=p/(p-1)
.

To get the desired result, we will prove that the last term in (

) can be controlled by
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\|S_{a_{k}}1\|_{m,\alpha}^{p}
.
Next, the assumption gives

\frac{p-1}{p}<\Big(\frac{p-1}{p}-\frac{1}{m}\Big)(2+\alpha),~~\frac{2+\alpha}{m}<\frac{1+\alpha}{p}.

Choose positive numbers
n_{1}
and
n_{2}

\frac{p-1}{p}<n_{1}<\Big(\frac{p-1}{p}-\frac{1}{m}\Big)(2+\alpha),\quad n_{1}<n_{2}<n_{1}+\frac{1+\alpha}{p}-\frac{2+\alpha}{m},

which implies that

q_{n_{2}}>qn_{1}>q\frac{p-1}{p}=1.

By Lemma
3
, there exists a positive constant
C_{q,n_{1},n_{2},r}
depending on
q,n_{1},n_{2},r
such that

(2.14)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{qn_{1}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{qn_{2}}}\leq\frac{C_{q,n_{1},n_{2},r}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{q(n_{2}-n_{1})}}.

Furthermore, we have

\Big(-\frac{2+\alpha}{m}-n_{2}+n_{1}\Big)p+\alpha>-1.

By Lemma
2
,

(2.15)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big|\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{-\frac{2+\alpha}{m}-n_{2}+n_{1}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{(1-\frac{2}{m})(2+\alpha)-n_{2}}}\Big|^{p}\,dA_{\alpha}(w)\leq\frac{C_{m,p,n_{1},n_{2}}}{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{(p-1)(2+\alpha)-\frac{p(2+\alpha)}{m}-pn_{1}}}.\end{split}

Combining
(\ref{formula6})
,
(\ref{f3})
,
(\ref{formula16})
and Lebesgue’s Monotone Convergence Theorem, we get

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|Sf(w)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(w)\leq C_{m,p,r,n_{1},n_{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\|S_{a_{k}}1\|_{m,\alpha}^{p}.\end{split}

Since

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, applying Lemma

yields

\|Sf\|_{p,\alpha}\leq K\|f\|_{p,\alpha}

for some positive constant
K
.
This implies that
S
is bounded on
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
.
∎

For any

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that

\alpha>-1

is bounded.

Proof.

Let

K_{z}(w)=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{z}w)^{2+\beta}}~\text{and}~f_{k}(w)=(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\delta}K_{a_{k}}(w).

By Lemma
1
,
(\ref{f1})
can be written as

f(w)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}f_{k}(w).

Applying Lemma
7

(2.17)

\begin{split}|Sf(w)|^{p}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\|S_{a_{k}}1\|_{m,\beta}^{p}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{p(\delta-\frac{2+\beta}{m})}(1-|w|^{2})^{-p\frac{2+\beta}{m}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{p(1-\frac{2}{m})(2+\beta)}}.\end{split}

It is easy to check that
-p\frac{2+\beta}{m}+\alpha>-1
and

c=p(1-\frac{1}{m})(2+\beta)-(2+\alpha)=p(\delta-\frac{2+\beta}{m})>0.

By Lemma

, there exists positive constant

,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{-p\frac{2+\beta}{m}}}{|1-\overline{a_{k}}w|^{p(1-\frac{2}{m})(2+\beta)}}\,dA_{\alpha}(w)\leq\frac{C_{m,p,\delta,r}}{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{c}}.\end{split}

Combining this with (

, and Lebesgue’s Monotone Convergence Theorem yields

\begin{split}\|Sf\|_{p,\alpha}^{p}\leq C_{m,p,\delta,r}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\|S_{a_{k}}1\|_{m,\beta}^{p}\leq C^{p}C_{m,p,\delta,r}\|f\|^{p}_{p,\alpha}.\end{split}

Therefore,

is bounded on
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
.
∎

If
p\delta<1+\alpha
, then

\frac{2+\alpha}{p\delta}+1>2+\frac{1}{\alpha+1}>\frac{1+p\delta}{1+\alpha}+1.

Otherwise,

\frac{2+\alpha}{p\delta}+1<2+\frac{1}{\alpha+1}<\frac{1+p\delta}{1+\alpha}+1.

In addition,

.

Thus, we can obtain the sharp result as follows.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose that
0<p\leq 1
,
\alpha>-1
,
\beta=\frac{3+2\alpha}{p}-2

is bounded.

If

In this case, if there exist a positive constant
C
and some

\|S_{z}1\|_{m,\alpha}\leq C
for all

is bounded on

is bounded from
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})

. If

, then

\frac{p(2+\alpha)}{(p-1)(1+\alpha)}<\frac{p(2+\alpha)}{1+\alpha}.

For
m
such that

\frac{p(2+\alpha)}{(p-1)(1+\alpha)}<m\leq\frac{p(2+\alpha)}{1+\alpha},

. In this case, we have the following result:

Theorem 3
.

Suppose

,
\alpha>-1

(a)

Proof.

By Lemma
1

f(w)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}f_{k}(w),

p^{\prime}\Big(1-\frac{2}{m}\Big)(2+\alpha)>p^{\prime}(2+\alpha)\Big(\frac{p-1}{p}-\frac{1}{m}\Big),

3.
Compact linear operator

In this section, we aim to characterize the compactness of a linear operator
S
on
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
.
Recall that if
S
is a compact linear operator on
L_{a}^{2}(dA_{\alpha})
, then
\widetilde{S}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
(see
[
14
]
).
In
[
1
, Lemma 3.10]
, Axler proved that
\|K_{z}\|_{p}
are equivalent to
(1-|z|^{2})^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{p}}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Zeng
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
has showed that
\frac{K_{z}}{\|K_{z}\|_{p}}\rightarrow 0
weakly in
L^{p}_{a}(dA)
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
for
1<p<\infty
.

Note that by the definition of
\widetilde{S}
,
\widetilde{S}(z)
is equivalent to

\bigg\langle S\Big(\frac{K_{z}}{\|K_{z}\|_{p}}\Big),\frac{K_{z}}{\|K_{z}\|_{q}}\bigg\rangle,

where
1<p<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Therefore, the relationship between compact operators and Berezin transform can be extended to
L^{p}_{a}(dA)\ (1<p<\infty)
.
That is, if
S
is compact on
L_{a}^{p}(dA)~(1<p<\infty)
, then
\widetilde{S}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
. By Lemmas
2
and
4
, we can easily establish the following lemma, extending the results from
[
1
]
and
[
12
]
to weighted Bergman spaces
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-1
.

(a)

Let
c=(p-1)(2+\alpha)
, then

\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{p}(2+\alpha)}}\leq\|K_{z}\|_{p,\alpha}\leq\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(2+\alpha)\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(\frac{2+\alpha+c}{2})^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(b)

\frac{K_{z}}{\|K_{z}\|_{p,\alpha}}\rightarrow 0
weakly in
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
.

By above lemma, it is not hard to check that for each compact operator
S
on
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})(1<p<\infty)
, we have
\widetilde{S}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Lemma 9
.

[
8
, Lemma 5.3]

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
S
is a bounded operator on
L_{a}^{p}(dA)
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|S_{z}1\|_{m}<\infty

for some
m>1
, then
\widetilde{S}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
if and only if for every
t\in[1,m)
,
\|S_{z}1\|_{t}\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
.

By Lemma
2
and the method in Lemma
9
, it is not hard to check that the result in Lemma
9
also holds on Bergman space
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-1
and
S
is a linear operator on
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
, if there exist a positive constant
C
and some

, such that

for all

z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1
, the assumption yields that
S
is bounded
L_{a}^{p}(dA_{\alpha})
. If
S
is compact, by Lemma
8
,
\widetilde{S}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
.

If
\widetilde{S}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
, then by Lemma
9
,
to obtain the desired result, we only need to show that if
\|S_{z}1\|_{m,\alpha}\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
, then
S
is compact.

Suppose
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
weakly in
L^{p}_{a}(dA_{\alpha})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. To show that
S
is compact, it is sufficient to prove that
\|Sf_{n}\|_{p,\alpha}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Lemma
1
gives that

f_{n}(w)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k,n}\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{(p-1)(2+\alpha)}{p}}}{(1-w\overline{a_{k}})^{2+\alpha}},

where

and
\{a_{k}\}
is an
r
-lattice on
\mathbb{D}
.

By the proof in Theorem
1
, we know that

\|Sf_{n}\|_{p,\alpha}^{p}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k,n}|^{p}\|S_{a_{k}}1\|_{m,\alpha}^{p},

with positive constant
C
.
Since
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
weakly, Lemma
6
shows that for any fixed
0<R<1

\sum_{|a_{k}|\leq R}|c_{k,n}|^{p}\rightarrow 0~~\text{as}~~n\rightarrow\infty,

i.e., for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N>0
, if
n>N
, then

(3.1)

\sum_{|a_{k}|\leq R}|c_{k,n}|^{p}<\varepsilon

Since
\|S_{z}1\|_{m,\alpha}\rightarrow 0
as

, then for the

(\ref{f5})

This means that

which shows that

∎

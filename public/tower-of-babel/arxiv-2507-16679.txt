PICACO: Pluralistic In-Context Value Alignment of LLMs via Total Correlation Optimization
Han Jiang, Dongyao Zhu, Zhihua Wei, Xiaoyuan Yi, Ziang Xiao, Xing Xie

Abstract
In-Context Learning has shown great potential for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human values, helping reduce harmful outputs and accommodate diverse preferences without costly post-training, known as In-Context Alignment (ICA). However, LLMs’ comprehension of input prompts remains agnostic, limiting ICA’s ability to address value tensions—human values are inherently pluralistic , often imposing conflicting demands, e.g. , stimulation vs. tradition. Current ICA methods therefore face the Instruction Bottleneck challenge, where LLMs struggle to reconcile multiple intended values within a single prompt, leading to incomplete or biased alignment. To address this, we propose PICACO , a novel pluralistic ICA method. Without fine-tuning, PICACO optimizes a meta-instruction that navigates multiple values to better elicit LLMs’ understanding of them and improve their alignment. This is achieved by maximizing the total correlation between specified values and LLM responses, theoretically reinforcing value correlation while reducing distractive noise, resulting in effective value instructions. Extensive experiments on five value sets show that PICACO works well with both black-box and open-source LLMs, outperforms several recent strong baselines, and achieves a better balance across up to 8 distinct values.

)
. Alignment techniques
(

;
Bai et al. 2022a
)

(

;

)
, empowering various applications such as personalized chat

Given the substantial computational and data costs of post-training based alignment methods
(

;
Rafailov et al. 2023
)
,
In-Context Alignment
(ICA) has attracted growing attention
(

)
.

)
, or both
(

)
into task prompts at inference time, ICA effectively leverages the knowledge embedded in LLMs without requiring fine-tuning. This enables flexible, real-time, and personalized alignment with varying values and preferences.

However, the agnostic interplay in LLMs’ comprehension of prompts or instructions hinders ICA from effectively addressing value tensions inherent in complex human needs.
Human values are pluralistic and real-world needs are heterogeneous

(
Bakker et al. 2022
;
Sorensen et al. 2024
)
. Users often present varying or even conflicting demands simultaneously
(

;
Ying et al. 2024
)
,
e.g.
, requiring an LLM to answer sensitive questions in a way that is both helpful and harmless, or to offer advice that balances the user’s contradictory desires for stimulation and tradition
(

;
Feng et al. 2024
)
.
As shown in Fig.

, current ICA methods struggle to reasonably navigate multiple requirements within a single prompt
(

;

;
Feng et al. 2024
)
, leading to biased alignment—a limitation we refer to as the
Instruction Bottleneck

Despite endeavours to address pluralistic alignment in tuning-based methods
(
Rame et al. 2023
;
Guo et al. 2024
;
Agnihotri et al. 2025
)
, corresponding approaches for ICA remain largely unexplored, hurting user experience.

To unlock the full capabilities of ICA, we propose a novel
P
luralistic
I
n-
C
ontext
A
lignment via Total
C
orrelation
O
ptimization method (
PICACO
). Our method automatically optimizes a meta-instruction that articulates the requirements of multiple values, achieved by maximizing the conditional Total Correlation
(

, TC;)
between the specified values and LLMs’ responses to a given task prompt. This theoretically reinforces the correlation between responses and each value while iteratively reducing irrelevant contents. Requiring not any training or sophisticated manual instruction design, PICACO generates an instructive meta-instruction that elicits the LLM’s understanding of target values and steers its output distribution accordingly. In summary, our contributions are as follows: (1) To our best knowledge, we are the first to apply Total Correlation maximization to ICA. (2) We introduce PICACO, an effective pluralistic ICA method that works for both open-source and black-box proprietary models. Related Work

LLM Alignment

With the prosperity of generative LLMs
(
OpenAI 2024
;
OpenAI 2025
;
Jiang et al. 2024
;
Dubey et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2025
)
, alignment has become essential for AI safety
(
OpenAI 2022a
)
. Most early efforts focus on post-training methods, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback
(
Ouyang et al. 2022
;
Lee et al. 2023
, RLHF;)
, which typically first trains reward models (RMs) on high-quality preference data and then fine-tunes LLMs using RL algorithms
(
Schulman et al. 2017
)
, resulting in various effective variants
(
Gulcehre et al. 2023
;
Gao et al. 2024c
;
Liang et al. 2025
)
. Subsequently, the growing need to reduce reliance on RMs sparks a range of Supervised Fine-Tuning
(
Dong et al. 2023a
;
Yuan et al. 2023
, SFT;)
methods, which directly learn to maximize the probability of preferred responses while minimizing that of dispreferred ones
(
Rafailov et al. 2023
;
Khaki et al. 2024
;
Meng et al. 2024
)
. Despite satisfactory performance, these methods rely heavily on large amounts of high-quality supervision and computational resources.

In-Context Alignment

To reduce such demands and enable greater flexibility, ICA methods have come to the front
(
Han 2023
;
Ganguli et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2023
;
Lin et al. 2024
;
Kamruzzaman and Kim 2024
;
Gao et al. 2024b
;
Yang et al. 2024a
;
Choi and Li 2024
)
. ICA usually leverages auxiliary models to: i) provide heuristic guidance and select few-shot examples
(
Saley et al. 2024
;
Song et al. 2024
;
Lake et al. 2024
)
, ii) optimize instructions to improve LLMs’ understanding and conformity to values
(
Cheng et al. 2024a
;
Trivedi et al. 2025
)
, or iii) create candidate responses reflecting various alignment goals
(
Feng et al. 2024
)
, making the alignment process more flexible and efficient, though still task-dependent. Another branch is decoding-time alignment, which incorporates external signals to modify LLMs’ internal representations
(
Adila et al. 2024
)
, token distributions
(
Han et al. 2024
;
Xu et al. 2025
)
or span scores
(
Li et al. 2024
;
Balashankar et al. 2024
)
. While offering greater controllability, these methods are limited to white-box models. Despite their practicality, these methods still treat human values as a
unitary
concept, lacking adaptability to value pluralism.

Multi-Value Alignment

Which/Whose values do LLMs reflect?
The question raises concerns about value/preference biases internalized in LLMs
(
Bai et al. 2022b
;
Santurkar et al. 2023
;
Huang et al. 2024
;
Park et al. 2024
)
. Consequently, increasing efforts have been made to multi-value alignment. Some incorporate multiple values by aggregating diverse rewards
(
Wu et al. 2023
;
Zhou et al. 2024b
;
Wang et al. 2024a
;
Wang et al. 2024b
;
Ramesh et al. 2024
;
Agnihotri et al. 2025
)
, reward models
(
Rame et al. 2023
)
, or policy LLMs themselves
(
Jang et al. 2023
)
, each conditioned on a specific value. Another line of work incorporates additional contextual signals into finetuning to achieve multiple objectives conditioned on specific inputs
(
Dong et al. 2023b
;
Guo et al. 2024
;
Yang et al. 2024b
;
Pitis et al. 2024
;
Zhong et al. 2024
)
. Besides, decoding-time methods have been developed in parallel
(
Deng and Raffel 2023
;
Shi et al. 2024
;
Mudgal et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024b
;
Lin et al. 2025
)
. However, there are few efforts considering multiple values in the context of ICA.
Lin et al. 2024
and
Lake et al. 2024
raise multiple expectations, such as helpfulness, politeness, and social responsibility, through manual value instructions.
Liu et al. 2024a
and
Gao et al. 2024b
measure the helpfulness, harmlessness, and honesty of LLMs, as well as their co-dynamics. For greater flexibility,
Modular Pluralism

(
Feng et al. 2024
)
employs specialized community LLMs and merges their responses for pluralistic alignment.

.

All ICA methods mentioned above either require extensive human labor, rely on pre-defined value compositions, or operate only on specific values—leaving the dynamics within various value compositions underexplored and failing to address the
Instruction Bottleneck
challenge.

3
Methodology

3.1
Formalization and Overview

Define
p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})
as an LLM that generates a response
\bm{y}
given a task prompt
\bm{x}
and
\bm{v}\!=\!(\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{K})
as the
K
target values,
e.g.
,
Universalism
from the Schwartz Theory of Basic Human Values
(
Schwartz 2007
, STBHV;)
. Each
\bm{v}_{k}
is represented by either a set of implicit demonstrations or an explicit natural-language description, like “
a belief in equal respect, empathy, and protection for all humans and nature
” for
\bm{v}_{k}\!=\!

Universalism
.
Our goal is to derive a meta-instruction
\bm{e}
that maximizes the LLM’s conformity to these values,
i.e.
, maximizing
\ p_{\bm{e}}(\bm{v})\!\approx\!\mathbb{E}_{\hat{p}(\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\bm{e}}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[q_{\bm{\omega}}(\bm{v}|\bm{y})]
across a wide range of task prompts, without altering its internal parameters.
q_{\bm{\omega}}
is a value evaluator that estimates the degree to which
\bm{y}
reflects the values
\bm{v}
.

To address the
Instruction Bottleneck
, we propose PICACO, as illustrated in Fig.
2
, which iteratively enhances
\bm{y}
’s value conformity and refines
\bm{e}
to maximize the likelihood that the LLM produces these well-aligned
\bm{y}
. This process theoretically optimizes the total correlation between intended values and LLM responses, reducing biased alignment and enabling more flexible value composition.

3.2
PICACO Framework

Total Correlation Maximization

Total Correlation,
\text{TC}(\bm{v},\bm{y})
, quantifies the informativeness of
\bm{y}
about the dependence among
\bm{v}

(
Gao et al. 2019
)
, which has been used in conditional image generation
(
Hwang et al. 2021
)
and visual control
(
Cheng et al. 2024b
)
. In ICA, since LLM parameters are frozen, we reformulate pluralistic alignment as a Black-Box Optimization
(
Sun et al. 2022
)
problem, and optimize
\text{TC}_{\bm{e}}(\bm{v},\bm{y})
to obtain an instructive value meta-instruction
\bm{e}
, which is maximized if and only if
\bm{y}
encodes the correlation across all the
K
values of interest, thereby achieving the maximal compatibility with all values.

Concretely, we maximize the following conditional Total Correlation objective:

\displaystyle\bm{e}^{*}=

\displaystyle\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \text{TC}_{\bm{e}}(\bm{v},\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{I}_{\bm{e}}(\bm{v}_{k};\bm{y}|\bm{x})\!-\!\text{I}_{\bm{e}}(\bm{v};\bm{y}|\bm{x}),

(1)

where
\text{I}_{\bm{e}}
is mutual information conditioned on the instruction
\bm{e}
. To estimate
I_{\bm{e}}
in Eq. (
1
), we can further derive a Barber-Agakov bound
(
Barber and Agakov 2004
)
for the first term, and we extend the CLUB bound
(
Cheng et al. 2020
)
to a conditional version for the second term, obtaining an approximated lower bound of Eq.(
1
):

\displaystyle\text{TC}_{\bm{e}}(\bm{v},\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{\hat{p}(\bm{x})}\{\beta\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\bm{e}}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q_{\bm{\omega}}(\bm{v}_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]}_{\text{Pluralistic Conformity}}

\displaystyle\!-\!\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{p_{\bm{e}}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]}_{\text{Redundancy Reduction}}\!+\!\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]}_{\text{Redundancy Reduction}}\},

(2)

where
\hat{p}(\bm{x})
is the empirical distribution of task prompts
\bm{x}
,
p_{\bm{e}}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})
is the LLM with
\bm{e}
as its meta-instruction,
\bm{s}
is the content where
\bm{v}
is derived, which can be assumed as a modest set of demonstrations that embody these values, and
\beta
is a hyper-parameter to control the trade-off between conformity and redundancy in responses.
q_{\bm{\omega}}(\bm{v}_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
is a variational distribution, acting as a value evaluator parameterized by
\bm{\omega}
mentioned in Sec.
3.1
, and
q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
is another to quantify how much unnecessary content from the textual observation
\bm{s}
is copied to the response
\bm{y}
, reflecting the extent of superficial alignment
(
Zhou et al. 2023
)
.

When maximizing Eq. (
2
), the first term enhances the value-wise conformity of
\bm{y}
to all
K
values, while the other two terms penalize unnecessary details inherited from the meta-instruction
\bm{e}
and the textual observation
\bm{s}
, ensuring that
\bm{y}
truly reflects the values rather than simply replicating superficial details. In this way, PICACO better balances the expression of multiple values and significantly reduces reliance on labeled data, overcoming the
Instruction Bottleneck
challenge.

Variational Information Maximization

Although we obtain a computable lower bound of TC in Eq. (
2
), solving
\bm{e}^{*}\!=\!\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \text{TC}_{\bm{e}}(\bm{v},\bm{y}|\bm{x})
still remains an intractable problem, as it is infeasible to traverse the whole instruction space. Therefore, we further approximate Eq. (
2
) as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{e}^{*}=

\displaystyle\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\{\sum_{j=1}^{M_{1}}p_{\bm{e}}(\bm{y}_{i,j}|\bm{x}_{i})[

\displaystyle\beta\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log q_{\bm{\omega}}(\bm{v}_{k}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j})\!-\!\log q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j})]

\displaystyle\!+\!\sum_{j=1}^{M_{2}}p(\bm{y}_{i,j}|\bm{x}_{i})\log q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j})\},

(3)

where
N
,
M_{1}
, and
M_{2}
are the numbers of task prompts, aligned and noisy (unaligned) responses sampled for each
\bm{x}
per iteration, respectively.

Without changing the LLM parameters, we resort to Variational Information Maximization
(
Barber and Agakov 2004
, VIM;)
to iteratively optimize Eq. (
3
) on a task prompt set
\mathcal{X}=\{\bm{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, through the following two alternative steps.

4.2
Evaluation Results

The overall conformity scores of each ICA method across five value compositions and three target LLMs are shown in Table
1
&
5
, from which several key observations can be made:

PICACO consistently outperforms most baselines, showing strong versatility.
Existing ICA methods tend to be biased toward specific value types—for example, OPRO emphasizes HH values and URIAL prioritizes Schwartz values. However, PICACO maintains near-SOTA performance across a range of intended values and target LLMs, showing strong steerability and flexibility. We attribute this to the trade-off between value conformity and redundancy, as constrained by
q_{\bm{\omega}}
and
q_{\bm{\phi}}
.

Fake alignment is more prevalent in Schwartz values.
While generally higher conformity scores (the mean of subscores for each value) indicate better alignment with human intent in HH values,
the same does not hold for Schwartz values
. We observed that some responses with high conformity scores are not even contextually relevant. They tend to include superficial mentions and overly generic elaborations on the intended values, causing the fake alignment problem
(
Wang et al. 2024c
)
, see Fig.
13
. In other words, these responses appear overfitted to target values. Considering the faithfulness of alignment, we further weighted the conformity with a metric based on Grice’s Maxim of
Relevance

(
Grice 1975
)
, which serves as a penalization term for the
Confucianism
and
Modern Liberalism
compositions.

Smaller models tend to be more sensitive to the choice of ICA methods.

For
GPT-3.5-Turbo
and
Gemini-1.5-Flash
, most baselines achieve better alignment than the vanilla query (Q), particularly in HH value alignment. However,
LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct
performs worse with nearly half of the baselines across all five compositions. This suggests that smaller models place higher demands on ICA prompts. Two possible explanations are: 1) smaller models are more susceptible to bias due to their limited parameters and training data; and 2) they have lower capacity to comprehend instructions, making them more vulnerable to the
Instruction Bottleneck
challenge.

High-quality demonstrations, optimization steps, and dialogue turns play a significant role in ICA performance.
URIAL, which uses manually crafted meta-instructions and demonstrations, significantly outperforms other naïve baselines without demonstrations. Notably,
URIAL+Sum
, which applies demonstrations generated by
GPT-4o
with Q+IF, performs worse than URIAL, underscoring the importance of demonstration quality. Both OPRO and PICACO iteratively optimize the meta-instruction, further enhancing the models’ understanding of the intended values.
Modular Pluralism
, a collaborative approach leveraging dialogue among multiple community LMs, also shows strong performance. These four methods show consistent superiority over other naïve baselines, suggesting that ICA remains a promising direction, especially with better-designed approaches that push its performance toward the upper bound.

4.3
Further Analysis

Adaptability to Growing Value Sets

The results in Table
1
demonstrate PICACO’s general superiority across value compositions with varying intentions and goals. Given that most compositions in the main experiment consist of four values, we further evaluate how Q+IF,
Modular Pluralism
, and PICACO adapt to HH compositions of increasing sizes. For four different numbers (n=2, 4, 6, 8) of target values, we report the delta in average scores relative to the base method, as well as the coefficient of variation, in Fig.
3
. The results show that as the number of values increases, overall conformity to the intended values tends to decrease. However, PICACO consistently achieves better performance (higher deltas) and greater balance (lower coefficients of variation), outperforming
Modular Pluralism
, which is also specifically designed for pluralistic alignment. We attribute this to the iterative VIM, which contributes to PICACO’s stability when navigating pluralistic values.

Robustness to Backbone LLMs

Is the effectiveness of PICACO influenced by LLM’s capability? We explore this through two additional settings involving more capable LLMs: 1) using
GPT-4o
for meta-instruction refinement in the
Instruction Refinement Step
; and 2) using
O4-Mini
for Schwartz value alignment. The results of 1) are shown in Fig.
4
(a). Using
GPT-4o
as the meta-instruction optimizer results in a negligible difference in performance, though still outperforming most baselines across the two target LLMs and five value compositions. This suggests that PICACO is robust and leveraging the capabilities of the target LLM itself is sufficient,
not
relying on external optimizers.

Additionally, we compare the effectiveness of different methods on three target LLMs with distinct capabilities in Fig.
4
(b). Through dual self-refinement, PICACO further enhances the capabilities of stronger LLMs, showing greater performance gains positively correlated with the original scores, and producing notably different meta-instructions (see Fig.
12
). This finding is particularly evident with the
Modern Liberalism
composition, which deviates more significantly from typical LLM response styles. We also observe that the naïve baseline, Q+IF, performs better with
O4-Mini
than with the other two, suggesting that reasoning-focused LLMs are more effective at comprehending and integrating instructions.

Resistance to Jailbreak Attacks

To test the robustness of PICACO against difficult cases, we wrap the queries with a carefully crafted jailbreaking template
(
Andriushchenko et al. 2025
)
and compare PICACO with three baselines using
GPT-3.5-Turbo
on the
Harmlessness
composition. Fig.
4
(c) shows the proportions of queries that elicit responses with either an average
Helpfulness
score
\geq
3 or an average
Toxicity
score
\geq
3. The results suggest that PICACO steers LLMs toward generating less harmful responses while still enhancing helpfulness by providing information on why the harmful queries cannot be answered. By continuously updating the aligned response pool, PICACO steadily enables benign outputs and enhances resistance to adversarial prompts.

Case Study

Since the
Instruction Bottleneck
challenge may manifest as severe conflicts between alignment objectives in real-world scenarios, we experiment with two pairs of highly conflicting Schwartz values:
Tradition
vs.

, and
Self-direction
vs.
Conformity
. As shown in Figs.

&

, with meta-instructions from PICACO, LLMs are encouraged to integrate these values more thoughtfully into their responses, rather than simply mentioning each or failing to grasp the requirements.

5
Conclusion

The rapid advancement of LLMs has highlighted the potential of ICA as an efficient and flexible solution in various applications. To free ICA methods from predefined value sets and costly labeled data, we propose PICACO, a novel ICA framework that refines meta-instructions using EM-like iterations, which PICACO outperforms existing ICA baselines with superior steerability and versatility across diverse intended human values and target (both black-box and open-source) LLMs. In the future, we plan to further explore generalizable and effective settings in real-world value compositions.

Limitations

This study aims to enhance pluralistic value alignment through PICACO. However, it is important to note several limitations that may impact the interpretation and generalizability of our findings:

•

Scope of target values.
Our experiment focuses on a comprehensive set of human values from the Schwartz Theory of Basic Human Values
(
Schwartz 2007
)
and Helpful and Harmless
(
Wang et al. 2024e
;
Askell et al. 2021
)
. However, there are other value theories such as Kohlberg’s Moral Development Theory
(
Kohlberg 1971
)
and Hofstede’s Culture Dimensions
(
Hofstede 2011
)
. Therefore, although STBHV and HH are well-adopted, further experiments are needed to test our method on other value constructs.

•

Scope of target LLMs.
Our study evaluates 3 competitive LLMs as target model, both open-source and proprietary, with model sizes ranging from billions to hundreds of billions of parameters. However, newer models, especially reasoning models, e.g., DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek-V3, may behave differently when adopting ICA methods. We will include additional experiments in our revision to demonstrate the generalizability of our methods to new models.

•

Potential bias in LLM scoring.
Despite the use of repeated experiments in our evaluations to reduce the stochasticity in LLM scoring, other types of biases may still exist in LLM scoring. For example, guardrails in proprietary models (e.g.,
GPT-4o-2024-08-06
) may refuse to rate certain values in target LLM’s generation, compromising the validity of the rating.

Given that understanding and adapting to specified human values is an important and relatively new field in LLM research, our work recognizes the above limitations. In future research, we are prepared to further refine our methods and address the aforementioned issues.

Appendix A
Value Composition Design

A.1
Single Values

As discussed in Sec.
1
, PICACO is compatible with various value compositions. In response to the well-known Helpful-Harmless dilemma
(
Bai et al. 2022a
)
, we first consider five helpful values defined in existing research:

•

Coherence
refers to the consistency and clarity of expression
(
Wang et al. 2024e
)
, measuring if the response is well-structured, with ideas presented in a clear and coherent manner
(
Lin et al. 2024
)
.

•

Complexity
represents the intellectual depth of the response, reflecting whether the content is basic or requires profound expertise (i.e., whether the response can be written by anyone with basic language competency or requires deep domain expertise to author)
(
Wang et al. 2024e
)
.

•

Verbosity
refers to the amount of detail included in the response
(
Wang et al. 2024e
)
.

•

Helpfulness
requires the response to be helpful and completely aligned with the spirit of what the prompt was asking for
(
Wang et al. 2024e
)
, making a clear attempt to perform the task or answer the question posed
(
Askell et al. 2021
)
.

The four harmless values are defined to mitigate the four risks posed by language models, as identified by
Weidinger et al. 2022
:

•

Non-Toxicity
avoids language risks including profanities, identity attacks, sleights, insults, threats, sexually explicit content, demeaning language, language that incites violence, causing offense, psychological harm, and even material harm in the case of inciting violence.

•

Fairness
prevents potential harms such as social stereotypes, unfair discrimination, and exclusionary norms contributing to the oppression of those at social margins, and the unjust representation or treatment of marginalized groups.

•

Information Safety
avoids potential harms such as privacy violations and safety risks, namely leaking or correctly inferring private or sensitive information.

•

Responsible Uses
discourage uses including undermining public discourse, crimes such as fraud, personalized disinformation campaigns, and the weaponization or production of malicious code.

The ten Schwartz values and their defining goals are as follows:

•

Self-direction
Independent thought and action–choosing, creating, exploring.

•

Stimulation
Excitement, novelty, and challenge in life.

•

Hedonism
Pleasure or sensuous gratification for oneself.

•

Achievement
Personal success through demonstrating competence according to socialstandards.

•

Power
Social status and prestige, control or dominance over people and resources.

•

Security
Safety, harmony, and stability of society, of relationships, and of self.

•

Conformity
Restraint of actions, inclinations, and impulses likely to upset or harm others and violate social expectations or norms.

•

Tradition
Respect, commitment, and acceptance of the customs and ideas that one’s culture or religion provides.

•

Benevolence
Preserving and enhancing the welfare of those with whom one is in frequent personal contact (the
in-group
).

•

Universalism
Understanding, appreciation, tolerance, and protection for the welfare of all people and for nature.

However, since these ten values are inherently inter-conflicting to some extent, embodying all values in a single response is implausible. Therefore, we choose two four-value compositions grounded in existing cultures and philosophies.

A.2
Confucianism

Confucianism, derived from the teachings of the Chinese philosopher
Confucius (Kong Fuzi)
, is a system of thought and behavior with the core aim of promoting harmony of the family and society as a whole
(
Yao 2000
)
.
Confucian ethics is characterized by the promotion of virtues, known as
the Five Constants
, which were elaborated by Confucian scholars from the inherited tradition since the Han dynasty as
Ren, Yi, Li, Zhi, Xin

(
Runes 1983
)
.

Ren
is equivalent to
Benevolence
in Schwartz values. According to Confucius,
Ren
has its root in respect for family
(
Confucius 1998
)
, and the benevolent person loves others
(
Nan 2013
)
. To be more specific, one should be dignified in personal conduct, efficient in action, generous and trustworthy when dealing with others, and extend kindness and favors to the surrounding people
(
Confucius 1998
)
.

Yi
involves a moral disposition to do good. It represents moral acumen that is not merely the ability to follow rules, but also a balanced understanding of a situation, along with the creative insight and decision-making ability necessary to apply virtues properly and appropriately, without losing sight of the greater good
(
Cheng 1972
)
. Similar to
Conformity
,
Yi
requires individuals to adjust their thoughts and actions to avoid violating social expectations or norms.

Li
represents social protocols in situations that require a sense of respect
(
Tiwald 2020
)
, carrying rich religious and social connotations
(
Hopfe and Woodward 2001
)
. It originally referred to religious sacrifices
(
Chan 1963
)
and later came to encompass the customs and principles of proper social behavior, taught by fathers, elders, and government officials
(
Wright and Twitchett 1962
)
, which can be seen as a form of
Tradition
.

Zhi
is the ability to recognize what is right and fair in behavior, thus understanding the best means to achieve virtuous ends
(
Wright and Twitchett 1962
)
.
Xin
, introduced by
Dong Zhongshu
, is understood as a commitment to reality in a consistent and reliable way
(
Yao 2013
)
. Together, these virtues contribute to the harmony and stability of society, aligning with the defining goal of
Security
.

Consequently, we select
Benevolence, Conformity, Tradition
, and
Security
for this composition.

A.3
Modern Liberalism

Modern liberalism, which was formed in the 20th century in response to the Great Depression
(
Olson and Gumpert 2017
)
, has been the dominant version of liberalism in the United States.
As the liberal party officially advocates, the government should use all its power and resources to ensure that the average person has the right to their own economic and political
life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness

(
Roosevelt 1938
)
, embodying a strong sense of
Universalism
and
Self-direction
. The well-known phrase comes from the United States Declaration of Independence, which also emphasizes the protection of individual rights and liberties of all people. For example, it asserts,
All men are by nature equally free and independent.

Modern liberals often point to the widespread prosperity enjoyed under a mixed economy. Meanwhile, the phrase
the pursuit of happiness
has been widely adopted as the title of various cultural products, including films, music, and books
(
Brinton 1893
;
Rogers 1969
;
Kennedy 2003
)
. This orientation distinguishes liberalism from most East Asian cultures, which prioritize self-restraint and duty over individual autonomy and pleasure. While claiming that liberalism aligns with
Hedonism
may be an oversimplification, we can consider adding this value to moderately emphasize the significance of happiness.

Liberals also describe themselves as open to change and receptive to new ideas
(
Berman 2008
)
. In this sense, liberalism can be seen as interconnected with
Stimulation
defined by Schwartz, as both prioritize individual autonomy and the pursuit of a fulfilling, diverse, and dynamic life.

Therefore, we select
Universalism, Self-direction, Hedonism
, and
Stimulation
to align LLMs with
Modern Liberalism
.

The datasets from which we collect data, i.e.,
\mathcal{X}
and the queries in
D
, for alignment with Schwartz values are as follows:

NYT-Dilemma

(
Appiah 2024
)
is a dataset we curated from
The Ethist
column in
The New York Times Magazine
. It consists of 300 questions submitted by magazine readers seeking open-ended advice on tricky life situations and moral dilemmas, spanning from 2007 to 2024. We remove the titles and retain only the questions. The final sentence of each question typically follows the pattern:
Should I [action-verb]?
We annotate the presence of each of the 10 Schwartz values in the questions independently using
GPT-4
.

ValueLex Stems

(
Biedma et al. 2024
)
is a dataset designed to reconstruct the unique value systems of LLMs using sentence stems (e.g.,

). Inspired by projective tests in psychology, it presents respondents with ambiguous stimuli, allowing their responses to reflect internal states and thereby probing the intrinsic values embedded in language models. The dataset is manually adapted and expanded from the Rotter Incomplete Sentences Blank
Rotter 1950
. Its taxonomy includes Competence (Self-Competent, User-Oriented), Character (Social, Idealistic), and Integrity (Professional, Ethical), which categorize the stems into six groups.

is a benchmark generated by AdAEM
(
Duan et al. 2025
)
, an Adaptively and Automatically Extensible Measurement framework. AdAEM produces value-evoking questions by iteratively optimizing a Jensen-Shannon-divergence-based information-theoretic objective in an in-context manner, without any manually curated data or fine-tuning. We include a subset of the questions in the benchmark, such as:
"Did the emphasis on physical exercise in ancient Greek culture reflect a balance between individual achievement and communal values, or was it primarily driven by military necessities?"

For HH alignment, we consider data from these datasets:

(
Ji et al. 2023
)
is a safety-focused collection that consists of 301k human-labeled question-answer pairs, each labeled with 1 or more harm categories. There are 14 harm categories in
BeaverTails

(

)

Weidinger et al. 2022
,
i.e.
, (I) information hazards; (II) malicious uses; (III) discrimination, exclusion, and toxicity; (IV) misinformation harms; and (V) human-computer interaction harms, are inherited and extended to 12 second-level types and 60 distinct types in the dataset.

(
Bhardwaj and Poria 2023

HoneSet

HoneSet

)

)
.

is a dataset of 99,442 naturally occurring prompts extracted from a large corpus of English web text,

Gokaslan and Cohen 2019

Perspective

C.1
Settings of PICACO

Hyperparameters and Input

In this paper, we set
N=50
,
M_{1}=10
,
M_{2}=15
, and
T=10
for all PICACO experiments. Specifically, for Schwartz value alignment, we set
\beta=0.5
and provide a different, model-specific
S
, obtained by prompting the target model to elaborate on the intended values. This is because we do not want the aligned model to merely explain the values without addressing the queries-i.e., the
superficial alignment
mentioned in Sec.
4.1
. For HH value alignment, we set
\beta=5
and
S=D
directly.

The data sources for
\mathcal{X}
and
D
are detailed in App.
B
. For each value composition, we curate eight demonstration conversations, with responses initially drafted by
GPT-4o
and then manually refined, some of which are shown in Fig.
6
. The seed meta-instructions are initialized differently depending on the alignment task. For Schwartz value alignment, we mention only the values themselves in the meta-instructions, as shown in Table
3
. For HH value alignment, we minimally modify each value’s definition into an instruction (listed in Table
2
) and concatenate the instructions corresponding to all intended values.

Probability Distributions

In this paper, we implement the variational distribution
q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
, which indicates value conformity, using
GPT-4o-mini
. The model is prompted with the evaluation template shown in Fig.
9
, and the resulting scores are converted into percentage values.

The other distribution,
q_{\omega}(S|\bm{x},\bm{y})
, which measures the redundancy, is derived based on embedding similarity. Specifically, we first embed the textual observation
S
, the query
\bm{x}
, and the response
\bm{y}
with
jina-embeddings-v3

(
Sturua et al. 2024
)
, and then compute the cosine similarity
Sim(\cdot)
between
S
and
\bm{y}
, as well as between
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
. The probability is then computed as:

\displaystyle q_{\omega}(S|\bm{x},\bm{y})=1+Sim(S,\bm{y})-Sim(x,\bm{y}).

(5)

Additionally, we estimate the generation probability using a method compatible with both open- and closed-source LLMs. For example, to compute
p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})
, we sample
n=10
responses
\{\bm{y}_{1},\bm{y}_{2},...,\bm{y}_{n}\}
from
p_{\theta}
in a single call with input
\bm{x}
, and

\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Sim(\bm{y},\bm{y}_{i}).

(6)

VIM Sampling

In the Response Enhancement step of VIM, we perform bucketed sampling to encourage diverse, high-quality responses. For each bucket, we randomly sample three demonstrations from
D
and append them to the meta-instruction with the highest
TC_{e}
, which is used as the system prompt. The user prompt is simply the wrapped query
\bm{x}
. Examples of the system and user prompts are shown in Fig.
7
.

In the Instruction Refinement step, we sample one new meta-instruction per call. For each call, we first use stratified sampling to obtain four meta-instructions with different levels of
TC_{e}
, then randomly sample three demonstrations from
D
and provide them to the optimizer model (typically the target model). In every iteration, we generate
M_{2}
new meta-instructions using the instruction in Fig.
8
and select
M_{2}
existing ones with the highest
TC_{e}
. We then update their
TC_{e}
scores with the currently best-aligned responses and cached noisy responses.

C.2
Evaluation Protocol

To evaluate the meta-instructions produced by PICACO, we collect responses for each test set following the same procedure used in the Response Enhancement step (Fig.
7
). For each query, responses are sampled across three buckets and three repetitions per bucket, resulting in a total of nine responses.

The evaluation template for
GPT-4o-2024-08-06
is shown in Fig.
9
. For HH value compositions, the overall conformity
\mu_{\text{conf}}
is calculated by first averaging the scores across responses for each query and value, and then averaging across all queries and values. For Schwartz value compositions, the overall conformity is further weighted by
Relevance
:

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{\text{conf}}=\frac{\mu_{\text{conf}}\cdot Relevance}{5}

C.3
Baseline Details

Q+IF

(
Ganguli et al. 2023
)
is a naive ICA method that uses a meta-instruction as the system prompt to guide the model in embodying the target values. In this paper, Q+IF employs the seed meta-instructions in Table
3
and
2
.

Q+IF+CoT

(
Ganguli et al. 2023
)
enhances Q+IF by adding a Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Specifically, after the meta-instruction and the query marked with "# Query", the LLMs are prompted with:

Let’s think about how to respond in a way that considers [INTENDED VALUES].

Their responses are then appended to the chat history, and they are subsequently asked:

Based on the above, what is your response to the query marked with "# Query"?

URIAL

(
Lin et al. 2024
)
is an extension of basic ICL in two parts: a meta-instruction for ICA and stylistic responses for few-shot demonstrations.
The meta-instruction introduces the scenario and format of the following demonstrations, outlines the role of the AI assistant in multiple aspects, and emphasizes the importance of social
responsibility.
The demonstrations in URIAL are manually curated and restyled to be more engaging and declarative.

For value compositions that include only helpful and harmless values, we adopt the official meta-instruction of URIAL. For other Schwartz value compositions, we slightly modify the meta-instruction as follows:

Below is a list of conversations between a human and an AI assistant (you).

Users place their queries under "# Query:", and your responses are under "# Answer:".

You should always provide responses that reflect the values of [INTENDED VALUES].

Your answers should be value-laden and provide detailed information. They should also have an engaging tone.

To ensure fair comparisons, we also use bucketed sampling and randomly select
K=3
demonstrations from
D
within each bucket.

URIAL+Sum

(
Lake et al. 2024
)
refers to the top-performing setting in the paper, where the human-crafted demonstration responses in URIAL are replaced with responses and summaries generated by a teacher model (
GPT-4o
in this paper).

MP+System 1/2

(
Kamruzzaman and Kim 2024
)
combines a machine persona with a system 1/2 prompt to guide LLMs in thinking in different ways. According to Dual Process Theory, system 1 facilitates quick comprehension through associations and pre-existing knowledge, while system 2 involves careful thought, evaluating logical relations, and conducting explicit reasoning to reach conclusions.

Additionally, to steer LLMs toward the intended values, we append the seed meta-instructions to the original prompts.

Modular Pluralism

(
Feng et al. 2024
)
is a pluralistic alignment framework based on multi-LLM collaboration. In the
Overton
mode, multiple community LMs are employed to generate comments in response to a given query, each representing a value or perspective. Then the comments are combined with the query and summarized by target LLMs.

In this paper, we implement the community LMs by applying Q+IF with a single value to the target LLM. Specifically, for Schwartz values, we use
GPT-4o
to generate a definition for each value, which is then manually revised into meta-instructions, as shown in Table
4
.

OPRO

(
Yang et al. 2024a
)
leverages LLMs to iteratively optimize task-specific instructions. In each iteration, the optimizer generates new instructions from the optimization prompt that contains previous instructions with their scores, then the new instructions are applied, evaluated, and added to the optimization prompt.

In this paper, we optimize the meta-instruction for pluralistic ICA with OPRO. Specifically, we employ the aforementioned few-shot demonstrations to exemplify the alignment tasks. For fair comparisons, we also sample
M_{2}
new meta-instructions in
T
iterations. The generated instructions are evaluated on the same query set as PICACO’s, with their conformity scores calculated as the
Pluralistic Conformity
term in Eq.(
2
) and scaled to a 0-100 range. Other hyperparameters are inherited from the paper of OPRO.

Appendix D
Detailed Derivations

D.1
Method

Define
p_{\theta}
as a black-box LLM parameterized by
\theta
and
\bm{v}=\{v_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
as
K
values with each
v_{i}
being an implicit variable defined by a set of labelled text conforming to it or an explicit natural-language expression. Our goal is to find a natural-language description,
e
, as a meta-instruction to ensure that for each query
\bm{x}
, the LLM’s generated response
\bm{y}
maximally reflects all the
K
specified values simultaneously, without changing
\theta
.

To do so, we solve the following conditional Total Correlation Maximization problem:

\displaystyle\bm{e}^{*}=

\displaystyle\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \text{TC}_{e}(\bm{v},\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{I}_{e}(v_{k};\bm{y}|\bm{x})-\text{I}_{e}(\bm{v};\bm{y}|\bm{x}),

(8)

where
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{k})
is the concatenation of
K
different values. More concretely, we can prove:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{I}_{e}(v_{k};\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(v_{k})}\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},v_{k})}[\log q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{\hat{p}(\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},e)}[\log q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})],

(9)

where
\hat{p}(\bm{x})
is the empirical distribution of all prompts and
q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
is a variational distribution parameterized by
\phi
to tell whether the generated response
\bm{y}
conforms to the given value
v_{k}
. Similarly, we have:

\displaystyle\text{I}_{e}(\bm{v};\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\}

\displaystyle\approx\mathbb{E}_{\hat{p}(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},e)}[\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\},

(10)

where
\bm{s}
is the content where the value
\bm{v}
is summarized, which can be assumed as several few-shot examples exhibiting all values
\bm{v}
.
q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
is also a variational distribution to tell whether the generated responses
\bm{y}
contain too many unnecessary details in the exemplars
\bm{s}
. Note that
q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
can be implemented as another classifier different from
q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
.

By Combining Eq.(
9
) and Eq.(
10
), we can approximately have:

\displaystyle\text{TC}_{e}(\bm{v},\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\hat{p}(\bm{x})}\{\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},e)}[\log q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]}_{\text{Pluralistic Conformity}}

\displaystyle\!-\!

\displaystyle\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},e)}[\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\!+\!\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]}_{\text{Redundancy Reduction}}\}.

(11)

When maximizing Eq.(
11
), the first term helps improve the generated response
\bm{y}
’s conformity to all K values, and the other two terms help reduce unnecessary details from the meta instruction
e
and few-shot examples
\bm{s}
, ensuring that
\bm{y}
truly reflects the values, not just irrelevant details from their descriptions or examples.

Then we have:

\displaystyle\bm{e}^{*}=

\displaystyle\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \text{TC}_{e}(\bm{v},\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\{\sum_{j=1}^{M_{1}}p_{\theta}(\bm{y}_{i,j}|\bm{x}_{i},e)[

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j})-\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j})]

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{M_{2}}p_{\theta}(\bm{y}_{i,j}|\bm{x}_{i})\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j})\}

(12)

Since we aim at achieving pluralistic alignment via ICL, we don’t change the LLM parameter
\theta
by optimizing the meta-instruction
e
(
e
can also include few-shot examples).

To do so, we use the Variational Information Maximization (VIM) to solve Eq.(
12
) in an iterative optimization manner like EM algorithm used in Black-box Optimization. Concretely, we alternately conduct the following two steps:

Response Enhancement Step

At the
t
-th iteration, fix
e^{t-1}
and find the optimal
q
to maximize Eq.(
12
). Since
q
is fixed (as it is implemented by either a fine-tuned classifier or an off-the-shelf LLM judge), this can be approximately achieved by finding better
\bm{y}_{i,j}
to maximize
\log q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t})
,
\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\hat{\bm{y}}_{i,j})
and minimize
\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t})
. Concretely, we sample a set of
\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t}\sim p_{\theta}(\bm{y}_{i,j}|\bm{x}_{i},e^{t-1})
and calculate the probability
\log q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t})
and
\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t})
for each
\bm{x}_{i}
. Then we select the top-
M
ones
\{\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t}\}_{j=1}^{M_{1}}
with the largest
\log q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t})
and smallest
\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t})
. The responses used in the last term in Eq.(
12
) ,
\{\hat{\bm{y}}_{i,j}\}_{j=1}^{M_{2}}\sim p_{\theta}(\bm{y}_{i,j}|\bm{x}_{i})
, can be sampled and the corresponding
q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\hat{\bm{y}}_{i,j})
can be calculated offline before the iteration process.

Instruction Refinement Step

Once we obtain
q
with increased probability from the Response Enhancement step, we fix it and optimize the whole Eq.(
12
) by finding the next (a better)
e
(this can be regarded as optimizing
p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},e))
:

\displaystyle\bm{e}^{t}=

\displaystyle\ \underset{\bm{e}}{\text{argmax}}\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\{

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{M_{1}}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log\frac{q_{\phi}(v_{k}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t})}{q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t})^{\frac{1}{K}}}\right]p_{\theta}(\bm{y}_{i,j}^{t}|\bm{x}_{i},e)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{M_{2}}p_{\theta}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{i,j}|\bm{x}_{i})\log q_{\omega}(\bm{s}|\bm{x}_{i},\hat{\bm{y}}_{i,j})\}.

(13)

In Eq.(
13
),
q
distributions act as weights to measure the importance of each
\bm{y}^{t}_{i,j}
sampled in the Response Enhancement step. These
\bm{y}^{t}_{i,j}
should better reflect all values
\{v_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
and contain fewer unnecessary expression details (e.g., words) in the few-shot examples
\bm{s}
. Then, the updated
e^{t}
should hold a larger probability to encourage the LLM
p_{\theta}
to generate such responses
\bm{y}^{t}_{i,j}
. The last term functions as a regularizer, which indicates
p_{\theta}
should not generate more useless details than
e
is not injected in the prompt.

When the iteration converges, we can obtain an
e
that is compact and effective to motivate the LLM to generate a response exhibiting all values.

D.2
Detailed Derivation

We first consider the maximization of each
\text{I}_{e}(v_{k};\bm{y}|\bm{x})
. Since the conditional probability
p_{e}(v|\bm{x},\bm{y})=p_{\theta}(v|\bm{x},\bm{y})
is hard to accurately calculate when the LLM
p_{\theta}
is a black-box one or a small and weak one with less value-related knowledge. Therefore, we derive a lower bound of it by incorporating a variational distribution
q
and have:

\displaystyle\text{I}_{e}(v_{k};\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\iiint p_{e}(\bm{x},y,v_{k})\log\frac{p_{e}(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})}{p_{e}(v_{k})}d\bm{x}d\bm{y}dv

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{x},\bm{y})}\{\text{KL}[p_{e}(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})||q(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{x})}\iint p_{e}(v_{k},\bm{y}|\bm{x})\log\frac{q(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})}{p_{e}(v_{k}|\bm{x})}dvd\bm{y}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{x})}\iint p_{e}(v_{k},\bm{y}|\bm{x})\log q(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})dvd\bm{y}+\mathcal{H}_{p_{e}}[v_{k}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{x})}\iint p_{e}(v_{k},\bm{y}|\bm{x})\log q(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})dvd\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(v_{k})}\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},v_{k})}[\log q(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})].

(14)

Then, we assume
e
should be irrelevant to the prompt
\bm{x}
, and hence we approximate
p_{e}(\bm{x})
as an empirical distribution of all training prompts.
p_{e}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},v)
is
p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},e)
in practice. Once
v_{k}^{*}
is specified,
p_{e}(v_{k})=\delta(v_{k}-v_{k}*)
. In this way, we can have an approximated lower bound:

\displaystyle\text{I}_{e}(v_{k};\bm{y}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{\hat{p}(\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\bm{y}|\bm{x},e)}[\log q(v_{k}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{M}p_{\theta}(\bm{y}_{i}^{j}|\bm{x}_{i},e)\log q(v_{k}|\bm{y}_{i}^{j},\bm{x}_{i}).

(15)

Then we consider the minimization of
\text{I}_{e}(\bm{v};\bm{y}|\bm{x})
. Directly minimizing the correlation between
\bm{y}
and all values takes the risk of mistakenly unlearning value information, especially when
v_{k}
is implicitly represented by a classifier trained on labelled data. Therefore, we incorporate a new variable
\bm{s}
representing all the
K
values related LLM behaviors (actions). In practice, either implicit or explicit
\bm{v}
is learned or extracted from
\bm{s}
, which can be formed as
\bm{v}=f(\bm{s})
where
f
is a deterministic mapping. By data processing inequality, we have
\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x},\bm{s})\geq\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x},f(\bm{s}))
. Since
\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x},\bm{s})\!=\!\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x})+\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{s}|\bm{x})
and
\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x},\bm{v})\!=\!\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x})+\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{v}|\bm{x})
, and
\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x},\bm{s})-\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x})\geq\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x},f(\bm{s}))-\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{x})
, we have
\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{s}|\bm{x})\geq\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{v}|\bm{x})
.

As a result, we further solve the minimization of
\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{s}|\bm{x})
as an upper bound of the original mutual information. Similarly, this can not be easily calculated. Therefore, we resort to its CLUB upper bound. We prove that
\text{I}_{e}(\bm{y};\bm{s}|\bm{x})\leq\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p_{e}(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\}
.

To do it, we start from proving
\text{I}(\bm{y};\bm{s}|\bm{x})\!\leq\!\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\!-\!\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\}
, which is a conditional version of the CLUB bound. We consider

\displaystyle\Delta=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\}-\text{I}(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})-\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})]\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle\!-\!\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})\!-\!\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})].

(16)

Since
\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})=\log\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[p(\bm{s}|y,\bm{x})]\geq\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|y,\bm{x})]
,
\Delta\geq 0
. Hence, we prove that
\text{I}_{cCLUB}=\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\}\geq\text{I}(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})
.

Similar to the maximization part,
p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
is hard to obtain, and thus we approximate it by
q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})
and define
\text{I}_{vcCLUB}\!\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\!\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\!-\!\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\}
. We demonstrate that under a mild condition,
\text{I}_{vcCLUB}
still upper bounds
\text{I}(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})
.
We consider:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=

\displaystyle\text{I}_{{\text{vcCLUB}}}(\bm{y};\bm{s}|\bm{x})-\text{I}(\bm{y};\bm{s}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\left[\log{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})}-\log{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\right]\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{[\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\log q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]

\displaystyle\!-\![\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\log p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}\log q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})]\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log\frac{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}{q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})}]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log\frac{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})}{q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})}]\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{x})}\{\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log\frac{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x})p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}{q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{p(\bm{y},\bm{s}|\bm{x})}[\log\frac{p(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}{q(\bm{s}|\bm{x},\bm{y})p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}]\}

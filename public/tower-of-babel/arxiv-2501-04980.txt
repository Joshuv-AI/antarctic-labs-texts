On the crossing profile of rectilinear drawings of K n K_{n}
Isaac Chen and Oriol Solé-Pi

Abstract
We introduce the crossing profile of a drawing of a graph. This is a sequence of integers whose ( k + 1 ) th (k+1)^{\text{th}} entry counts the number of edges in the drawing which are involved in exactly k k crossings. The first and second entries of this sequence (which count uncrossed edges and edges with one crossing, respectively) have been studied by multiple authors. However, to the best of our knowledge, we are the first to consider the entire sequence. Most of our results concern crossing profiles of rectilinear drawings of the complete graph K n K_{n} . We show that for any k ≤ ( n − 2 ) 2 / 4 k\leq(n-2)^{2}/4 there is such a drawing for which the k th k^{\text{th}} entry of the crossing profile is of magnitude Ω ⁡ ( n ) \Omega(n) . On the other hand, we prove that for any k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 and any sufficiently large n n , the k th k^{\text{th}} entry can also be made to be 0 0 . As our main result, we essentially characterize the asymptotic behavior of both the maximum and minimum values that the sum of the first k k entries of the crossing profile might achieve. Our proofs are elementary and rely mostly on geometric constructions and classical results from discrete geometry and geometric graph theory.

Introduction

Graph drawings and their properties have been studied extensively, both from a theoretical and from a practical standpoint. Early research in this area was centered around
planar graphs
, that is, graphs that can be drawn on the plane without incurring in any crossings between the edges; there many interesting questions regarding such graphs that are yet to be answered. Landmark results in the study of planar graphs include Euler’s formula (which relates the number of nodes, edges and faces of a planar map), Kuratowski’s theorem
[

[

Going beyond planar graphs, one of the most natural questions one can ask is: what is the least number of crossings between edges that a drawing of a graph

G

\Cr(G)

[

[
27
]
for an in depth treatment of the subject.

The study of graph drawings and crossing numbers has had implications in the areas of VLSI (Very large scale integration)
[
9
,
5
]
and graph visualization
[
28
,
29
]
. Large graphs are subjacent to many interesting phenomena that show up in all kinds of disciplines, and being able to produce visual representations of graphs which are easy to understand and convey relevant information has become a very important endeavor.

The purpose of this work is to study not the total number of crossings in a drawing, but how these crossings are distributed across the edges of the graph. More precisely, we are interested in bounding the number of edges which are involved in at most

Definition 2.1

Let

Definition 2.2

Given a graph

Next, we consider points other than vertices where two edges meet.

Definition 2.3

Let

Definition 2.4

Let

The following notable result, which is usually referred to as the crossing lemma, yields a lower bound on the crossing number in terms of the number of edges and nodes of the graph.

Let

[
27

If

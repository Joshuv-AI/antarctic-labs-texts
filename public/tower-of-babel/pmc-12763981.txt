Prefabricated 3D-printed full-arch implant for functional and esthetic rehabilitation: a prosthesis-integrated drill guide solution
Authors: Hao Bai, Wenyan Wu, Xuejun Ge, Zhen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmediate-loading full-arch implant restoration offers improved patient comfort and acceptance but poses challenges in implant positioning, especially for complex anatomical conditions. Fully digital workflows, including intraoral scans and 3D-printed guides, enhance precision but may still result in cumulative errors affecting prosthetic fit. This study introduces a modified digital protocol integrating prefabricated prostheses with drill guides to improve accuracy and simplify full-arch rehabilitation.MethodsA systematic step-by-step protocol was developed using digital simulation and virtual design to fabricate prefabricated prostheses. These prostheses, combined with surgical drill templates, allowed for immediate functional and esthetic loading. Integration of the provisional restoration with the drill guide was designed to reduce discrepancies during prosthesis repositioning when securing pre-cut temporary cylinders with resin.ResultsThe proposed workflow demonstrated high accuracy in prosthesis placement, reducing repositioning errors during the clinical procedure. Functional and esthetic loading was successfully achieved, with precise alignment of components and a streamlined digital workflow.ConclusionsThis digital approach provides an effective and cost-efficient solution for immediate full-arch implant loading. By enhancing accuracy and minimizing procedural errors, it offers improved clinical outcomes and sets a foundation for advanced full-arch rehabilitation techniques.

Introduction

Currently, immediate-loading full-arch implant restoration has become a routine practice for edentulous patients. Compared to conventional implant restoration protocols, the immediate-loading approach—featuring a fixed, chairside, implant-supported interim restoration followed by a titanium-milled frame prosthesis—demonstrates improved patient acceptance and comfort when evaluated after 3 months [1, 2]. Techniques such as distal tilted, zygomatic, and pterygoid implants expand treatment options in cases of severe bone atrophy. However, accurate placement of long, angled implants remains challenging due to anatomical complexity and the proximity of vital structures. To address these challenges, fully digital workflows have been developed, integrating CBCT, intraoral and facial scans, digital articulators, and 3D printing. A fully digital workflow, including 3D-printed surgical templates and provisional prosthetics aligned with anchor pins, are the general commercial procedures for full-arch implant restoration. From preoperative data collection to virtual design and realistic rendering, digitally guided implant surgery combined with prefabricated prostheses allows for the precisely transfer of esthetic and functional information—synthesized and simulated by software—directly to the patient, leading to more favorable outcomes. Although discrepancies between the actual implant position and the virtual simulation may occasionally occur, full-arch guided implant surgery has consistently proven to be a predictable method for implant placement [3]. Alternatively, tooth-supported full-arch implant guides may provide more accuracy for partially edentulous patients compared to fully edentulous patients, with the number of remaining teeth being a significant factor in this difference [4]. Cumulative errors throughout the workflow—ranging from data acquisition to final prosthesis delivery—can compromise fit and occlusion. While enlarging prosthetic channels can improve tolerance, this may weaken prosthesis strength and occlusal accuracy.

Nevertheless, in some patients, unexpected deviations in surgical implant placement can prevent the interim prosthesis from being properly positioned and fixed. Moreover, enlargement of the fitting channels may diminish the strength and deplete the occlusal anatomy of the interim prosthesis.

The purpose of the present article is to describe a modified, step-by-step, digitally planed, immediate implant-guided full-arch implant loading protocol. In this approach, a tooth-supported surgical guide is used to position the anchor pin at the beginning of surgery. The guided drilling and implant insertion are performed by an integrated solution involving prosthetics and a full or pilot drill implant guide, which means that the prefabricated prosthesis functions as an implant guide as well. This technique may improve the positioning and fixation of prefabricated prostheses and increase the success rate of the overall procedure for full-arch implant restoration.

Results

The technique was applied to a cohort of patients requiring immediate full-arch rehabilitation. All interim prostheses achieved a 100% successful intraoperative pick-up rate. During the follow-up period, all implants remained clinically stable and functional without signs of failure. The emergence of all screw access channels fell within the prosthetically planned positions, indicating high accuracy in implant placement and confirming the workflow’s clinical feasibility and reproducibility.

Discussion

Commercial procedures for fully digital workflows in immediate full-arch implant loading have become widely adopted and are undergoing rapid improvements. With constantly advancements in digital techniques, software and hardware updates, innovative guided implants and immediate loading solutions are emerging while existing methods continue to evolve [5, 6]. In addition to the stackable guide concept based on anchor pins, [5, 7] other techniques have been applied in digital workflows [6, 8].

Compared to standard commercial fully digital workflows, other techniques not only focus on minimizing accumulative errors in implant placement but also use new connective components or procedures, such as shape memory alloy abutment, to tolerate errors in locating the interim prostheses [8].

Our solution, which integrates prosthetics with full or pilot drill implant guides, provides a feasible and cost-effective technique for seamlessly transferring preoperative digital plans into the clinical workflow. The principles of prosthetically guided surgery are demonstrated through the use of a flexible, detachable guide ring design, combining the implant surgical guide and prosthesis into a single unit. This approach significantly reduces errors that typically arise from prosthesis repositioning after fully guided implantation using reference anchor pins.

In edentulous cases, the prosthetic design can be appropriately modified to enhance stability and guidance. By integrating an anchor pin and detachable implant guide into a complete denture–like interim prosthesis, this workflow eliminates the need for a separate surgical template while maintaining high accuracy in implant placement and enabling immediate loading. Despite its favorable intraoperative performance, the current technique is limited to drill guidance and does not constitute a fully guided surgical protocol. Future developments may focus on modular guide components to achieve comprehensive guidance throughout the procedure, thereby improving precision and clinical reliability. Moreover, long-term clinical studies are warranted to evaluate the prognosis and generalizability of this technique.

Summary

The prefabricated, digitally planned, 3D-printed prosthesis with integrated pilot drill guidance is a feasible solution that maximally maintains both esthetic and functional effects for full-arch implant rehabilitation patients. This technique simplifies the overall surgical and immediately restorative procedures inexpensively and precisely.

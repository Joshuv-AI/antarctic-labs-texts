Expression and Clinical Significance of the MRPS5 Gene in Uterine Corpus Endometrial Carcinoma: A Comprehensive Study Based on Public Databases
Authors: Ting Wang, Jian Liu, Huiping Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo examine the expression and clinical implications of the mitochondrial ribosomal protein S5 (MRPS5) gene in uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC) using bioinformatics tools.MethodsA comprehensive analysis was performed utilizing databases such as SANGERBOX, XIANTAO BIOINFORMATICS TOOL, UALCAN, and STRING. Expression levels of MRPS5 in UCEC were validated through Western blotting (WB) and real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) to determine its clinical relevance.ResultsMRPS5 expression was significantly elevated in UCEC patients (n=546) compared to normal tissues (n=35), P<0.05. WB analysis showed increased MRPS5 protein levels in three endometrial carcinoma cell lines: (RL95-2, 3.36), (HEC-1-B, 3.78), (Ishikawa, 7.39), relative to normal endometrial cells (12Z, 0.94), P<0.05. RT-qPCR revealed significantly higher MRPS5 mRNA levels in these cell lines compared to normal endometrial cells (P<0.0001). In UCEC, high MRPS5 expression was significantly associated with clinical characteristics such as stage, race, nodal weight, age, histological subtype, TP53 mutations, and promoter methylation (P<0.05). Additionally, MRPS5 protein expression in UCEC was linked to alterations in the WNT pathway, mTOR pathway, NRF2 pathway, RTK pathway, chromatin modifier alterations, and MYC/MYCN alterations. Survival analysis showed that high MRPS5 expression levels were significantly associated with decreased overall survival (OS) in UCEC (P<0.05).ConclusionMRPS5 gene is highly expressed in most cases of UCEC, and is correlated with disease onset, progression, and adverse prognosis, suggesting its potential as a valuable biomarker for diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis of UCEC.

Introduction

UCEC is a prevalent gynecological malignancy that significantly impacts women’s physical and mental health. In the United States, it is the fourth most common newly diagnosed cancer and ranks sixth in cancer-related mortality.1 Annually, about 66,200 women are diagnosed with UCEC, resulting in approximately 13,030 deaths.2 Despite ongoing research, the survival rate for women with UCEC has not improved markedly over the past four decades.3 Unlike other cancers, a hallmark symptom of UCEC is postmenopausal vaginal bleeding.4 Additionally, a rising incidence of UCEC has been noted, correlated with increasing obesity rates.5 Consequently, enhancing the diagnosis and treatment of UCEC is crucial. Developing novel diagnostic biomarkers to improve patient prognosis and assist in treatment planning is paramount in addressing this challenge.

The mitochondrial ribosome, consisting of a 28S small subunit and a 39S large subunit, facilitates the translation of mRNA transcribed from mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA). This process is essential for synthesizing proteins involved in the mitochondrial electron transport chain, which is integral to cellular energy metabolism.6 MRPS5, a component of the 28S small subunit, plays a role in regulating mitochondrial energy metabolism and is implicated in the development and progression of various cancers. High MRPS5 expression has been linked to poor outcomes in hepatocellular carcinoma, where it promotes tumor progression and presents a potential therapeutic target.7 In thyroid cancer, MRPS5 dysregulation affects tumor development and progression.8 Similarly, in bladder cancer, increased MRPS5 expression influences cell proliferation and stemness characteristics, correlating with unfavorable prognosis.9 However, research on the MRPS5 gene’s role in UCEC is limited. Thus, this study focuses on bioinformatics analysis to investigate the expression and clinical relevance of MRPS5 in UCEC.

Materials and Methods

Database Information

Sangerbox Database Analysis

The Sangerbox database is a clinical bioinformatics analysis platform that consolidates data from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), and International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC), including clinical data, follow-up information, expression profiles, and mutation data.10 In this study, the Sangerbox database was utilized to analyze MRPS5 gene expression across various cancers, its prognostic significance in UCEC, and its role in immune infiltration.

Ualcan Database Analysis

The UALCAN database is an interactive cancer omics data analysis platform.11 It was employed to assess MRPS5 expression in UCEC and to correlate this with clinical data.

Xiantao Bioinformatics Tools

Xiantao Bioinformatics Tools is an online bioinformatics data analysis platform that aggregates standardized data from TCGA.12 This platform was used to compare MRPS5 expression between UCEC and control groups and to evaluate diagnostic tests using ROC curves.

String Database

The STRING database13 integrates all known and predicted protein-protein associations, encompassing both physical and functional relationships.

Cell Culture

From the Shanghai Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, we procured human normal endometrial tissue cells (12Z) as well as three endometrial cancer cell lines (RL95-2, HEC-1-B, and Ishikawa). They were incubated at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator with RPMI-1640 media supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS).

Procedure: After digestion, cells were counted and seeded at a density of 5×105 cells per well into 6-well plates. The plates were incubated overnight at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator to achieve 70–90% confluence by the next day. Samples were collected for detection.

WB

The BCA assay was used to assess the concentration of total protein after it was isolated from cells using protein lysis buffer. Following SDS-PAGE separation, the proteins were transferred to a PVDF membrane. Prior to incubation with main and secondary antibodies, the membrane was blocked with 5% BSA. We used chemiluminescent detection.

RT-qPCR

To quantify total RNA, cells were treated with TriQuick Reagent, followed by purification and precipitation with chloroform and isopropanol. The resulting RNA was dissolved in DEPC water. The temperature schedule was followed to synthesize cDNA after mixing RNA with 5×RT SuperMix for qPCR for reverse transcription. Statistical Methods

To compare expression levels between normal and tumor tissues across various cancers, R software version 3.6.4 was utilized to perform non-parametric rank-sum tests and signed-rank tests for determining statistical significance. Correlation coefficients, both Pearson’s and Spearman’s, were computed through the Corr.test function in the Psych package of R software, to evaluate the associations between gene expression levels and immune cell infiltration scores. Differences in prognosis among groups were examined using the Survfit function from the Survival package in R software, with the Log rank test applied to assess the significance of these differences, where statistical significance was set at *P<0.05, **P<0.01, ***P<0.001, ****P<0.0001.

Discussion

Globally, UCEC ranks as the sixth most prevalent cancer among women, and its incidence is on a consistent upward trajectory. Consequently, enhancing the prognosis for patients afflicted with UCEC has emerged as an urgent concern.14 Yet, the potential of the MRPS5 gene as a prognostic and clinical predictor in UCEC is not well-documented. The present research is designed to delineate the prognostic importance and conceivable therapeutic implications of MRPS5 for individuals with UCEC.

Initial analyses conducted using the UALCAN database indicated a pronounced elevation in both MRPS5 gene and protein levels in UCEC tissues compared to healthy counterparts. Subsequent verification through the Xiantao academic bioinformatics platform corroborated a substantial increase in MRPS5 mRNA in patients. Moreover, RT-PCR assessments further substantiated these results, demonstrating a significant rise in MRPS5 expression within tumor cells. Complementary WB evaluations also verified this augmentation in MRPS5 protein levels in malignant cells relative to normal ones. Additionally, Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, revealing a high Area Under the Curve (AUC = 0.903), underscored MRPS5’s prospective diagnostic utility in UCEC.

In this comprehensive study, we analyzed numerous clinical characteristics of UCEC patients through the UALCAN database, encompassing disease stage, race, body weight, age, histological subtypes, TP53 mutation status, and promoter methylation. The findings indicated that MRPS5 expression escalates with advancing disease stage, a statistically significant trend. Elevated MRPS5 expression was noted in UCEC patients across diverse racial groups including Caucasian, African American, and Asian populations, in comparison to normal controls. Additionally, irrespective of body weight categories—normal, extreme, obese, and extremely obese—the highest MRPS5 expression was observed in normal-weight individuals. Across various age groups—21-100 years—MRPS5 expression was consistently higher among UCEC patients than in normal individuals. Histologically, increased levels of MRPS5 were detected in Endometrioid, Serous, and Mixed serous and endometrioid subtypes, with the Serous subtype displaying the most pronounced expression.

Concerning genetic alterations, TP53 mutations are widely recognized for their significant role in oncogenesis.15–17 Our analysis revealed enhanced MRPS5 expression in UCEC patients, both TP53-Mutant and TP53-NonMutant, with the highest levels found in the TP53-Mutant group. Furthermore, the promoter methylation of the MRPS5 gene in primary tumors was lower compared to normal tissues, highlighting its potential involvement in tumor progression through gene expression regulation.18 The complexity of the tumor microenvironment influences immune cell infiltration, a phenomenon underscored by varying interactions between tumor and immune cells. In summary, by evaluating immune infiltration scores across various immune cell types based on MRPS5 gene expression, distinct correlations were established. The inverse relationship between MRPS5 expression and infiltration by CAFs, CD4-T cells, CD8-T cells, and endothelial cells was evident, alongside a positive correlation with other cell types, highlighting complex interactions in the tumor microenvironment. Similarly, Spearman correlation analysis corroborated these findings, adding that macrophages also showed a positive correlation. These insights underscore the heterogeneous nature of immune cell interactions within the UCEC tumor microenvironment, potentially driven by various factors such as genomic complexity, immune system plasticity, and other tumor microenvironment characteristics. Such dynamics suggest potential mutual inhibition or synergistic interactions among different immune cells, emphasizing the necessity to consider these relationships in the development of more effective UCEC treatment strategies. As cancer remains a leading cause of death globally, focusing on targeted therapeutics and understanding molecular pathways that govern cancer progression are pivotal areas of ongoing global research.19

The investigation of MRPS5 expression across various signaling pathways revealed that in both the WNT Pathway-altered and other unspecified groups, MRPS5 levels were elevated compared to the normal group. This suggests that MRPS5 protein levels are upregulated in UCEC patients with disruptions in the WNT signaling pathway, which is known to be critical for cell proliferation, differentiation, and migration, and its dysregulation is often linked to the etiology and advancement of multiple cancers.

Likewise, in the mTOR Pathway-altered and other groups, an increase in MRPS5 expression was observed relative to the normal group. The mTOR pathway, pivotal in protein synthesis and cell growth regulation, is intimately connected with cancer initiation and progression. In groups with alterations in the NRF2 Pathway, which regulates cellular responses to oxidative stress, MRPS5 expression exceeded that in the normal group, suggesting that these changes may enhance tumor resistance and progression.

Elevated MRPS5 expression was also noted in the RTK Pathway-altered and other groups, indicating enhanced MRPS5 levels in UCEC patients with disruptions in the receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) pathway, crucial for cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. Its aberrant activation is associated with the development of various malignancies. Similarly, in the Chromatin Modifier altered and MYC/MYCN altered groups, increased MRPS5 expression suggests that modifications in chromatin structure and stability, along with changes in cell proliferation and metabolism, could contribute to tumorigenesis.

Moreover, our analysis extended to the relationship between MRPS5 expression and prognosis in UCEC patients, supplemented by MRPS5 protein interaction studies. Validation from two databases confirmed that patients in the low-expression MRPS5 group exhibited significantly better OS. In biological systems, proteins interact within complex networks that are vital for maintaining cellular physiological functions. The interactions of MRPS5 with other mitochondrial ribosomal proteins demonstrate the intricacy and mutual dependency within the mitochondrial protein network.

Although multiple database analyses have shown that MRPS5 is significantly higher in tumor patients than in normal tissues, this study only analyzed a single factor and did not consider the influence of various factors such as treatment plan, tumor grade, and tumor comorbidities. This needs further validation in clinical practice. However, even so, MRPS5 can still serve as a biomarker for UCEC tumors and play an important role in tumor diagnosis.

In conclusion, MRPS5 expression is markedly increased in most UCEC cases and is intricately linked with the disease’s onset, progression, and adverse prognosis. Consequently, MRPS5 serves as a promising biomarker for diagnosis, therapeutic strategies, and prognosis assessment in UCEC.

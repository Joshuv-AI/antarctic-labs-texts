On optimal LISA orbit design
Yaguang Yang

Abstract
The ESA/NASA joint LISA (laser interferometer space antenna) mission is designed to detect gravitational waves, which relies crucially on maintaining three-spacecraft constellation as close to an equilateral triangle with a designed distance as possible. Efforts have been made to achieve this goal by using various simplified models to make it easy to approximately solve the complex problem. In this paper, the problem is formulated as a nonlinear optimization problem using exact nonlinear Kepler’s orbit equations. It is shown that the optimal solution based on the exact nonlinear Kepler’s orbit equations gives a better solution than the previously obtained ones.

Introduction

LISA is an ESA/NASA mission with the objective of sensing low-frequency gravitational waves.
Two sets of interferometers are installed on each of the three spacecraft. Each spacecraft’s
orbit is on a heliocentric plane. The three spacecraft are the three vertices of a moving
(approximate) equilateral triangle with time-dependent distance variations around a

3.1
An arc-search infeasible interior-point algorithm

Consider a general nonlinear optimization problem written as the following form:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\min&:&f({\bf x})\\
\textrm{s.t.}&:&{\bf h}({\bf x})={\bf 0},\\
&&{\bf g}({\bf x})\geq{\bf 0},\end{array}

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the nonlinear objective function,
{\bf h}({\bf x})={\bf 0}

represents
m
the nonlinear equalilty constraints, and
{\bf g}({\bf x})\geq{\bf 0}
represents

p
the inequality constraints. The infeasible interior-point method has become
popular to solve the nonlinear optimization problem
[
12
]
. The key idea of
the method is to start with an initial point
{\bf x}^{0}
that meets the nonlinear inequality
constraints (
{\bf x}^{0}
is an interior-point) but may NOT meet the equality constraints
(
{\bf x}^{0}
is an infeasible point) because it is very expensive to find a solution of the
nonlinear system of equations
{\bf h}({\bf x})={\bf 0}
. However, as the iterate sequence

\{{\bf x}^{k}\}
approximate the optimal solution
{\bf x}^{*}
, the iterates
{\bf x}^{k}

will approach to a feasible solution (meeting both equality and inequality).
The traditional optimization method uses linear search to find a better iterate in
every iteration. This may not be a good strategy because the constraints are
nonlinear. A benchmark test problem (HS-19) in Hock and Schittkowski
[
4
]

test set is used to justify why arc-search is a more appropriate search method.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\min&:&f({\bf x})=(x_{1}-10)^{3}+(x_{2}-20)^{3}\\
\textrm{s.t.}&:&(x_{1}-5)^{2}+(x_{2}-5)^{2}-100,\geq 0\\
&&-(x_{2}-5)^{2}-(x_{1}-6)^{2}+82.81\geq 0,\\
&&13\leq x_{1}\leq 100,\\
&&0\leq x_{2}\leq 100.\end{array}

For this problem, the last 4 boundary inequalities are redundent. The area of the constraint
is depicted in Figure
4
, which is between two red curves. The contour lines
represent the levels of the objective function, which decrease in the top-down direction.
The optimal solution is at the interception of the two red curves marked with a red ’x’.
Clearly, for an iterate inside the area of the constraint, searching along a
decreasing line segment for optimizer is not as good as searching along an arc
described in Figure
5
which is part of an ellipse.

In this design, we assume that the eccentricity and inclination of spacecraft

Accordingly, the orbits of the three spacecraft at the same orientation are modified as

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{k}(E_{k})=a(\cos(E_{k})+e_{k})\cos(i_{k}),\mbox{for $k=1,2,3$,}

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{k}(E_{k})=a\sqrt{1-e_{k}^{2}}\sin(E_{k}),\hskip 7.22743pt\mbox{for $k=1,2,3$,}

\displaystyle X_{k}=\tilde{X}_{k}(E_{k})\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}(k-1)\right)-\tilde{Y}_{k}(E_{k})\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}(k-1)\right),\hskip 7.22743pt\mbox{for $k=1,2,3$,}

\displaystyle Z_{k}=\tilde{Z}_{k}(E_{k}),\hskip 7.22743pt\mbox{for $k=1,2,3$}.

4
Conclusions

The LISA orbit design problem is formulated as a nonlinear optimization problem using
exact nonlinear Kepler’s orbit equations. The problem is solved by
using an arc-search interior-point algorithm. The solution minimizes the distance
variations about a designed constant between the three LISA spacecraft, thereby
reducing the Doppler shift and breathing angle effects on the measurement of gravitational waves.

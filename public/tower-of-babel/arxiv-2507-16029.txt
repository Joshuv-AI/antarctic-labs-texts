being Lee–Yang.

We will also need the following two results on the intersection with dense lines.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a column vector whose entries are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and let

as in

(2). Let

be the zero set of a Laurent polynomial

is dense in

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that

is irreducible.
We identify the tangent space

of

at

via the differential of the covering map

Basics on

Proof of
2.2
.

Suppose that
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
C^{1+\epsilon}
with
f(0)=0
and
\nabla f(0)=0
, so there is
c_{f}>0
such that
\|\nabla f(y)\|\leq c_{f}\|y\|^{\epsilon}
and mean value theorem gives
|f(y)|\leq c_{f}\|y\|^{1+\epsilon}
. Taking
1>\delta_{f}>0
small enough so that
c_{f}\delta_{f}^{\epsilon/2}<1
we may conclude that

\|\nabla f(y)\|<\|y\|^{\epsilon/2},\ \text{and }\ |f(y)|<\|y\|^{1+\epsilon/2}\quad\forall y\in B_{\delta_{f}}(0)

The graph of the function
f
,
U_{f}=\{(y,f(y))\ :\ \|y\|<\delta_{f}\}
, is contained in the region
\{(y,t)\ :\ \|y\|<\delta_{f},\ |t|<\|y\|\}
.
The normal to
U
is
\mathbf{n}(y,f(y))=\frac{(-\nabla f(y),1)}{\sqrt{\|\nabla f(y)\|^{2}+1}}
, with
\mathbf{n}(0,0)=e_{n}
. Using the fact that
1-(t^{2}+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}<\frac{t^{2}}{2}
for all
t
, we write

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{n}(y,f(y))-e_{n}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle 2-2\frac{1}{\sqrt{\|\nabla f(y)\|^{2}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(y)\|^{2}\leq\|y\|^{\epsilon}

for all
\|y\|<\delta_{f}
. Since
\|y\|\leq\|x\|
for
x=(y,f(y))\in U
, we conclude that

\|\mathbf{n}(x)-\mathbf{n}(0)\|\leq\|x\|^{\epsilon/2},\quad\text{and}\quad|\langle\mathbf{n}(0),x\rangle|=|f(y)|\leq\|x\|^{1+\epsilon/2}

for all
x\in U
.

We now take
\Sigma
to be a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
periodic hypersurface which is
C^{1+\epsilon}
smooth. For every
x_{0}\in\Sigma/{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
there is a local
\Sigma/{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
neighborhood of
x_{0}
which is, up to translation and orthogonal transformation, the graph of a
C^{1+\epsilon}
function
f_{x_{0}}
with
f_{x_{0}}(0)=0
and
\nabla f_{x_{0}}=0
. Then,
\delta_{f}
for
f=f_{x_{0}}
depends continuously on
x_{0}
. Since
\Sigma/{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is compact, there is a minimal
\delta
, and we are done.
∎

To prove

(10)

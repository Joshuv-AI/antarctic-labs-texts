Dynamics of tumor in situ fluid circulating tumor DNA in recurrent glioblastomas forecasts treatment efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade coupled with low-dose bevacizumab
Authors: Dayang Wang, Jiubing Zhang, Chaojie Bu, Guanzheng Liu, Guangzhong Guo, Ziyue Zhang, Guangming Lv, Zhiyuan Sheng, Zhaoyue Yan, Yvshuai Gao, Meiyun Wang, Gang Liu, Ruijiao Zhao, Tianxiao Li, Chunxiao Ma, Xingyao Bu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeImmune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapies have shown efficacy in various tumors, but long-term responses in glioblastoma are less than 10%. Quantifying tumor in situ fluid circulating tumor DNA (TISF-ctDNA) and therapeutic dynamics may enable real-time GBM disease burden evaluation. This study explores the potential of tumor in situ fluid circulating tumor DNA (TISF-ctDNA) dynamics in predicting treatment efficacy.MethodsTISF and peripheral blood samples were collected from patients with recurrent glioblastoma (rGBM) undergoing tislelizumab (a programmed death 1 inhibitor) combined with low-dose bevacizumab (an anti-vascular endothelial growth factor antibody) treatment before and during each immunotherapy cycle. Biomarkers evaluated included TISF-ctDNA, measured using Next Generation Sequencing (NGS), and host inflammation markers such as the platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR).ResultsAll 32 patients received tislelizumab plus low-dose bevacizumab regularly. The median progression-free survival (PFS) was 4.0 months, and overall survival (OS) was 22.3 months. An analysis of 19 patients with continuous evaluable TISF showed baseline TISF-ctDNA abundance did not correlate with OS (p = 0.23) or PFS (p = 0.23). However, a change in TISF-ctDNA maximal Somatic Variant Allelic Frequency (MVAF) after six treatment cycles predicted both PFS (p = 0.02) and OS (p < 0.0001). Lower baseline PLR also correlated with better survival outcomes.ConclusionThe combination of tislelizumab and low-dose bevacizumab therapy appears to be effective in extending both OS and PFS in rGBM patients. Continuous TISF-ctDNA testing shows potential utility in complementing radiological monitoring. The temporal change pattern of TISF MVAF is more predictive of immunotherapy response than imaging. PLR before immunotherapy can screen patients likely to benefit from tislelizumab plus low-dose bevacizumab therapy.Trial registrationThe trial registration number: NCT05502991; Date of registration: 2022-08-14.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-024-05997-8.Keywords: Circulating tumor DNA, Immune checkpoint blockade, Recurrent glioblastoma, Tumor in situ fluid

Introduction

Glioblastomas tend to spread along blood vessels and white matter bundles, the residual lesions after surgery are inevitable, which result in recurrence in almost all cases. Recurrent glioblastoma lacks effective salvage therapies, with a median survival of approximately 14.6 months. Unfortunately, treatment options at recurrence are limited, and existing salvage therapies exhibit minimal efficacy. Current viewpoints for rGBM diagnosis and treatment strongly advocate for participation in suitable and feasible clinical trials (van den Bent et al. 2023). Effective treatment strategies for recurrent glioblastoma are urgently needed. Although ICB therapies have shown success in various tumor types, their effectiveness and predictability in GBM are limited, with long-term responses observed in fewer than 10% of patients (Agosti et al. 2023). At present, no validated predictive biomarkers for immune checkpoint inhibitors are routinely used in glioma treatment (Lynes et al. 2020; Mensali and Inderberg 2022). These dilemmas highlight the use of dynamic molecular responses in explaining therapeutic outcomes and guiding the development of treatment strategies.

Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) is utilized for various purposes, including early cancer detection, molecular residual disease (MRD) detection, comprehensive genomic profiling for treatment selection, recurrence surveillance, and monitoring treatment response. Increasing evidence suggests that ctDNA measurement can aid in interpreting clinical responses in patients undergoing ICB therapy. ctDNA has been linked to tumor burden, treatment response, and so on in many other solid tumors, which has emerged as a non-invasive, blood-based biomarker that broadly mirrors the somatic variants in tumor tissue (Cohen et al. 2023; Thompson et al. 2023). However, due to the challenges of ctDNA traversing the blood-brain barrier, its levels in glioma are markedly low in the bloodstream. When the tumor is connected to the ventricular system, cerebrospinal fluid appears to contain relatively higher ctDNA levels, yet these amounts are still below expected levels (De Mattos-Arruda et al. 2015; Miller et al. 2019; Pentsova et al. 2017). TISF-ctDNA offers non-invasive access to cancer-specific somatic mutations. We observed that TISF contains a large amount of tumor DNA, with a positive rate of 93.3% in the early postoperative period and 100% in tumor progression. It is far more sensitive than cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and can characterize the genetic spectrum of glioma in real time. This approach reveals the vivo genetic evolution of minimal residual disease after glioma surgery and assesses the risk of early recurrence (Liu et al. 2023a, b; Sheng et al. 2023; Sheng, Yu, Deng, Andrade-Barazarte, Sheng et al. 2021a, b; Sheng, Yu, Deng, Bu, Sheng et al. 2021a, b; Xu et al. 2022; Yu et al. 2021).

In addition to ctDNA, markers of inflammation in peripheral blood also provide a viable approach for evaluating immunotherapy. Elements like total leukocyte, neutrophil, lymphocyte, monocyte, and platelet counts in the full blood count have been widely studied as indicators of a dysfunctional pro-inflammatory response in cancer patients and in other medical fields. Inflammatory response (IR) indicators derived from peripheral blood may reflect cancer-related inflammation, host immune responses to cancer, and comorbidities. IR is a cheap, quick, and effective evaluation indicator, with its value reflected in the tumor stage, treatment choice, and other aspects, especially in recurrent and metastatic tumors (Bilen et al. 2019; Gou et al. 2021; Verrico et al. 2022). However, no similar studies have been conducted in recurrent glioblastoma.

Our phase IIa trial showcases preliminary clinical results of combination of PD-1 inhibitor and low dose bevacizumab therapy for rGBM. The findings from a forward-looking observational examination identify changes in TISF-ctDNA levels in patients with rGBM under the influence of PD-1 inhibitor combined with low-dose bevacizumab therapy selection pressure and establish a correlation between these dynamics and clinical outcomes. Additionally, the study evaluates the association between inflammatory marker levels and the efficacy of immunotherapy.

Materials and methods

Patients and study design

Genomics Assessment of Recurrent Glioblastomas Receiving Immune Therapy (GAGIT) study (NCT05502991) is a single center, single-arm, open-label, phase IIa trial. Approval was obtained from the Henan Provincial People’s Hospital Ethics Board (approval number: MR-41-23-037636). The main including criteria were as follows: (1) Histologically confirmed diagnosis of glioblastoma with disease progression confirmed by imaging; (2) Resection surgery done at the study center (Henan Provincial People’s Hospital), with an reservoir intraoperatively implanted connecting the surgical cavity and the subscalp for postoperative noninvasive TISF collection; (3) Previous first line treatment with at least radiotherapy before the study treatment; (4) Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) of 70 or higher. The main excluding criteria were any of the following: (1) More than two recurrences of GBM; (2) Presence of extracranial metastasis, significant leptomeningeal disease or tumors primarily localized to the brainstem or spinal cord. All patients signed written informed consent to participate in this study, and the treatments were administered at Henan Provincial People’s Hospital. Baseline was defined as recurrence after first-line treatment. All patients were treated with tislelizumab 200 mg intravenously and bevacizumab 3 mg/kg every three weeks upon disease progression. Notably, first-line immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) alone or in combination with low dose bevacizumab was not a standard care option in China during the study period, so Programmed Cell Death-Ligand 1 (PD-L1) levels were not routinely tested. Enrollment has been completed for this study, but patients will be followed up on an ongoing basis. The cut-off date for data collection was December 31, 2023.

TISF and blood collection

The collection and evaluation of TISF and peripheral blood were completed in Henan Provincial People’s Hospital. During surgery, a fluid reservoir capsule was placed between the periosteum and the galea aponeurosis for postoperative TISF collection. Samples were obtained at each treatment cycle after disease progression. The baseline sample (T1) was collected prior to treatment initiation. Each patient provided 0.5 to 1 ml of TISF via the indwelling reservoir. Additionally, 5 ml of peripheral blood was collected from each patient for germline DNA control. Peripheral blood plasma was separated via density gradient using Ficoll-Hypaque and subsequently centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min to remove cellular components and debris. The resultant supernatant was preserved at -80 °C for subsequent analysis. Employing an analogous method, TISF specimens underwent centrifugation at the same speed and duration, with the resulting supernatant being similarly preserved at -80 °C for future examination.

DNA extraction and library preparation

Genomic DNA (gDNA) extraction was performed using three distinct commercial kits, each designed for a unique type of sample. The Mag MAX™ Cell Free DNA Isolation Kit from Thermo was used for isolating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) from TISF. Following manufacturer instructions strictly, DNA quality was assessed using the Qubit Fluorometer and Qubit dsDNA HS Assay kit from Thermo. An additional quality assessment was performed using the Agilent 4200 TapeStation. Libraries were assembled using probe hybridization capture. This process of library construction and hybrid capture made use of commercially available reagents and probes that were specifically tailored. Utilized gDNA, ranging from 15 ng to 200 ng, was fragmented into 200–350 base pairs (bp) using specific enzymes. Custom-developed indexed paired end adapters (SimcereDx) were utilized for the Illumina platform. End repair, A-tailing, and adaptor ligation for sheared DNA and cfDNA were performed using the Library Prep kit supplied by Roche Diagnostics and Vazyme. For the assessment of the final library, procedures similar to the initial DNA quantification were applied.

Library sequencing and bioinformatics analysis

DNA libraries that had been prepared were subjected to sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq6000 platform, which produced reads of 150 bp in a paired-end format (Modi et al. 2021). Subsequently, adapters trimming and low-quality base filtering were conducted using fastp software (Version 2.20.0), as per PMID: 30,423,086. The resulting bases were aligned to the UCSC hg19 GRCh37 reference genome using the BWA-MEM algorithm (Version 0.7.17) (detailed in PMID: 30824715). Variants that were identified underwent a filtering process to exclude common SNPs, referencing public databases such as the 1000 Genome Project (as of August 2015) and the ExAC Browser 28 (Version 0.3). In the final stage of analysis, Copy Number Variations were examined utilizing CNVkit and factera for each purpose, respectively. For further information, refer to PMID: 27,100,738 for CNVkit and PMID: 25,143,292 for factera.

Peripheral blood parameters

Initial markers of host inflammation in circulation, particularly the platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), were quantified utilizing conventional protocols for bloodwork. These markers were divided into two categories according to their median values to examine their association with clinical pathological characteristics and patient survival.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistical analysis summarized patient demographics and clinical attributes, detailing the median and range for continuous variables and frequency and percentage for categorical variables. OS was defined as the period from initial surgical intervention to the date of either demise or the most recent follow-up. PFS was defined as the interval commencing with the initiation of immunotherapy and culminating at the onset of subsequent disease progression, mortality, or the most recent follow-up, depending on which event occurred earliest. The association between variables and survival outcomes was analyzed using the Cox proportional hazards model. Survival analysis employed the Kaplan-Meier estimator and the log-rank test. The evaluation of tumor response adhered to the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology 2.0 (RANO 2.0), as conducted by researchers, and by the immunotherapy RANO (iRANO) for participants exhibiting progression of disease (PD) (Wen et al. 2023).

Results

The study incorporated 59 patients between June 1, 2021, and December 31, 2023, all of whom (100%) were part of the analysis post-receiving surgical intervention and subsequent regular administration of temozolomide following the procedure. Each of the 59 patients (100%) received a combination of tislelizumab and low dose bevacizumab at the time of imaging-documented progression. Imaging evaluation and follow-up were performed in 32 patients who received a combination of tislelizumab and low dose bevacizumab regularly after relapse. All 32 patients (100%) underwent serial collection of peripheral blood parameters following recurrence. In 20 patients (63%), serial tumor in situ fluid (TISF) was gathered following recurrence. Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) was employed at every accessible timepoint, leading to the calculation of the maximal Somatic Variant Allelic Frequency (MVAF) (Fig. 1). 19 patients were incorporated into the concluding analysis of TISF-ctDNA kinetics (Fig. 2). The collection of TISF was demonstrated to be both feasible and safe, with no reported complications associated with the collection process.

Treatment response in rGBM

All 32 patients (100%) received a combination of tislelizumab and low dose bevacizumab regularly. Prior to study enrollment, all patients had undergone systemic anti-cancer treatments. The median age at diagnosis was 58 years, with 47% of the cohort being male. Detailed patient demographics and clinical characteristics are presented in Table 1. The median PFS was 4.0 months (95% CI: 1.5–6.5), and OS was 22.3 months (95% CI: 14.8–29.7), which exceeds the median survival time of 14.6 months observed with standard treatment. Eight patients demonstrated a Partial Response (PR), resulting in an Objective Response Rate (ORR) of 25%—the ORR encompasses both complete response and PR. Furthermore, a significant portion of the study group, 17 patients or 53%, derived clinical benefit (defined as a complete response, PR, or maintaining stable disease for six or more months). Previous studies have shown that patients with O-6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter methylation tend to experience prolonged survival after their first progression following standard chemoradiotherapy. To investigate whether the extended OS observed in some patients within our cohort is attributable to MGMT promoter methylation, we compared the proportion of MGMT promoter methylation in patients whose survival exceeded the reported median of 14.6 months and in those who showed clinical benefit (Table S1). The results indicated a balanced distribution of MGMT promoter methylation and non-methylated patients in both groups, with no statistically significant difference. Therefore, we conclude that the improvement in survival is likely due to the combined effect of ICB and low-dose bevacizumab, rather than MGMT promoter methylation.

Genomic landscape of rGBM

TISF-ctDNA was detected in 19/20(95%) patients at baseline. Somatic mutations were identified in 95% of the TISF-ctDNA samples. Frequently mutated cancer-related genes, including TERT (58%), TP53 (47%), and PTEN (42%) (Fig. 3a), suggest TISF-ctDNA’s detectability in patients prior to treatment. Pathway enrichment analysis of the mutated genes was performed in 15 patients who developed a second tumor progression during immunotherapy. Gene Ontology (GO) annotation analysis revealed significant correlations between the altered genes and key biological processes, with the cell population proliferation signaling pathway being predominantly represented among the altered genes (Fig. 3b). Further analysis categorized the mutated genes into their corresponding Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways, notably the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway signaling pathway, among other cellular mechanisms (Fig. 3c). This provides a basis for exploring the mechanism of immune resistance in glioblastoma in the future. Following disease progression, the most frequently mutated genes were TERT (53%), MUC16 (40%), and IGFN1 (33%) (Fig. 3d). Notably, IGFN1 mutations, undetected at the first recurrence, were observed in 5/15 (33%) patients who progressed again, indicating its potential as a therapeutic target for immunotherapy-resistant rGBM patients.

Predictive value of TISF-ctDNA abundance and dynamics on treatment outcomes

In certain solid tumors, blood ctDNA maximal variant allele frequency (MVAF) serves as an effective molecular marker for predicting immunotherapy efficacy. We investigated whether TISF-ctDNA MVAF could similarly predict the efficacy of GBM immunotherapy. 19 patients were incorporated into the concluding analysis of TISF-ctDNA kinetics. After approximately 6 treatment cycles, TISF-ctDNA MVAF decreased from baseline in 14 patients (74%) and increased from baseline in 5 patients (26%). Patient demographics and clinical features are detailed in Table S2. In a baseline assessment, median MVAF of 11.8%, ranging from 0.99 to 93.9%. Using the median MVAF of 11.8% as a threshold, no significant correlation was observed between TISF-ctDNA abundance and PFS or OS (p-values of 0.83 and 0.23, respectively) (Fig. 4a). However, TISF-ctDNA MVAF dynamics during treatment predicted immunotherapy efficacy. Initial decreases in TISF-ctDNA MVAF from T1 to T7 were indicative of enhanced PFS and OS, with statistical significance (PFS: p = 0.02; OS: p < 0.0001) (Fig. 4b). Furthermore, patients with decreased MVAF had a higher Clinical Benefit Rate (CBR) compared to those with increased MVAF (64% vs. 20%) (Fig. 4c). Even in cases of initial radiological progression, patients with a reduction in TISF-ctDNA MVAF from T1 to T7 exhibited extended median OS (22.6 months) compared to those with elevated TISF-ctDNA MVAF (9.1 months), with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.21 (95% CI 0.03–1.31, p = 0.01) (Table 2). These findings emphasize the importance of monitoring TISF-ctDNA MVAF dynamics in predicting and improving treatment outcomes and the advantages over traditional imaging.

Initial levels of peripheral blood PLR serve as prognostic indicators

Previous studies have suggested that high levels of PLR may reflect a tumor microenvironment that is hostile to immune cells. Therefore, we attempted to establish a correlation between PLR and combination of PD-1 inhibitor and low dose bevacizumab treatment efficacy. Using a median PLR of 155.48 as the threshold, we found that a baseline PLR below the median was associated with extended PFS (p = 0.02) and OS (p = 0.007) (Fig. 5a). Furthermore, patients with low baseline PLR showed a higher CBR than those with high baseline PLR (69% vs. 38%) (Fig. 5b). When included in a multivariable Cox model adjusted for age, gender and smoking status, high pre-treatment baseline PLR remained a significant predictor of shorter PFS and OS, the HR for PFS was 3.51 (95% CI, 1.42–8.66 p = 0.007) and for OS was 6.66 (95% CI, 2.26–19.62 p = 0.001) (Figure S1). These data suggest that PLR is a convenient initial screening indicator for the efficacy of combination of PD-1 inhibitor and low dose bevacizumab therapy.

In a multivariable Cox regression model accounting for age, gender, smoking status, and PLR, the reduction in MVAF consistently served as an independent prognostic indicator for OS. The model revealed that a decrease in MVAF was associated with a HR of 0.05, indicating a protective effect, and this association was statistically significant (95% CI: 0.01–0.37, p = 0.003) (Figure S2).

Discussion

Individuals diagnosed with recurrent glioblastoma (rGBM) face limited treatment options, underscoring the urgent need for integrated therapeutic approaches to improve patient prognosis. Moreover, the development of reliable biomarkers to guide treatment protocols is imperative. The absence of dependable biomarkers capable of predicting responses to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy significantly hinders the creation of personalized treatment plans based on biomarker profiling (Lynes et al. 2020; Mensali and Inderberg 2022). A multi-site Phase 1 trial has shown that reductions over time in the VAF of CSF H3K27M serve as a robust biomarker for clinical response, particularly in PFS (Cantor et al. 2022).

Our study is pioneering in proposing a treatment regimen combining tislelizumab and low-dose bevacizumab, which effectively prolongs both PFS and OS. We prospectively evaluated TISF-ctDNA dynamics in patients with rGBM undergoing this regimen, identifying a reliable predictor of response to immunotherapy. Serial TISF samples were analyzed using a fixed-panel NGS method optimized for glioma.

The combination of a PD-1 inhibitor and low-dose bevacizumab therapy appears superior because low-dose bevacizumab can improve the tumor microenvironment by inhibiting VEGF, thereby reducing the number and activity of immunosuppressive cells and enhancing the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors. Conversely, conventional doses may lead to excessive inhibition of angiogenesis, affecting blood supply to normal tissues and adversely impacting the immune system (Tijtgat et al. 2023). Additionally, conventional doses of anti-angiogenic therapy may cause tumor cells to develop drug resistance, affecting the treatment’s effectiveness. Low doses of bevacizumab can reduce this risk while maintaining VEGF inhibition, thereby extending treatment efficacy. More importantly, low-dose bevacizumab has the potential to induce prolonged vessel normalization, allowing immune cells such as effector T-cells to more effectively infiltrate tumor tissue (Fukurnura et al. 2018). This optimization of the microenvironment helps the immune checkpoint inhibitors to activate and maintain the immune response more effectively, thus improving anti-tumor efficacy (Hirsch et al. 2022).

Current explorations of benefit markers for glioblastoma immunotherapy primarily focus on PD-L1 expression, microsatellite instability (MSI), tumor mutational burden (TMB), and tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), with most research targeting tumor tissues (Beshiri et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2023; Dede et al. 2017). However, due to the high temporal heterogeneity of glioblastoma, these predictive values are relatively limited. Our study results reveal that variations in TISF-ctDNA MVAF dynamics from the onset to the completion of the treatment cycle provide a more precise forecast for both PFS and OS than merely assessing the initial TISF-ctDNA MVAF levels. TISF offers a novel source of ctDNA, providing a more direct and sensitive means of detecting tumor-specific mutations and monitoring disease progression. Our prior research indicates that the genomic state of glioma can be accurately delineated in real-time through TISF analysis (Liu et al. 2023a, b; Sheng et al. 2023; Sheng, Yu, Deng, Andrade-Barazarte, Sheng et al. 2021a, b; Sheng, Yu, Deng, Bu, Sheng et al. 2021a, b; Xu et al. 2022; Yu et al. 2021). It also offers higher safety and operability compared to continuous tissue biopsies, presenting new prospects for glioma liquid biopsies. To achieve this, we utilized targeted sequencing, a cutting-edge method for detecting and quantifying TISF-ctDNA. Although liquid biopsies are not yet standard in most clinical settings, changes in ctDNA levels over time during treatment could significantly aid clinical decision-making (Cohen et al. 2023).

In our study, patients treated with PD-1 inhibitor combined with low-dose bevacizumab who had low baseline PLR levels showed longer PFS and OS. Previous studies indicate that high baseline PLR levels in other solid tumors may suggest an immunosuppressive state (Onagi et al. 2022). In this case, the immune system, including lymphocytes that are critical to the effectiveness of immunotherapy, may not function optimally. Such suppression can limit the body’s natural capacity to combat cancer cells and diminish the effectiveness of treatments dependent on the immune system. PLR is a simple, cost-effective marker of host inflammation, easily discernible from routine pre-treatment bloodwork. The results of this study may impact risk stratification for patients undergoing treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors (Mege et al. 2019; Solimando et al. 2020). Similar to other validated prognostic scores used to assess a patient’s suitability for immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy.

It is important to acknowledge the limitations of our study, including the inability to collect or test some TISF samples as scheduled due to the COVID-19 pandemic. This resulted in some patients not being evaluated, primarily due to issues with sample continuity. To fully realize the predictive value of TISF-ctDNA and other biomarkers, thereby enhancing treatment strategies for rGBM, larger-scale studies are needed in the future. Due to practical limitations, we were unable to assess additional immune response markers, such as IFN-γ, from TISF. Future studies are warranted to explore the direct contribution of immune responses to the observed clinical outcomes in rGBM patients undergoing this combined therapy.

We believe the most promising application of liquid biopsies lies in assessing the dynamics of the circulating tumor burden longitudinally during therapy. We anticipate that various tumor-agnostic liquid biopsy assays, each with its own thresholds and definitions of ctDNA molecular response, will guide the escalation or de-escalation of immunotherapy. Prospective testing in clinical trials is crucial to fully establish the clinical utility of liquid biopsies in cancer immunotherapy and to identify settings where ctDNA molecular responses are most informative (Sivapalan et al. 2023; Tie 2020; Yao et al. 2023). This approach would allow healthcare professionals to leverage the known intertumoral heterogeneity in gliomas, identifying patients with poor immunotherapy responses to avoid superfluous additional consolidation therapy and optimize therapeutic value. Subsequent studies are encouraged to pursue the integration of ctDNA dynamics alongside various clinicopathological and radiological indicators (Vanguri et al. 2022). Integrating multiple types of data is a vital direction for precision oncology, enhancing our ability to identify early predictors of response and resistance to ICB (Quandt et al. 2017; Sivapalan et al. 2023; Tie 2020). We firmly believe that future precision immuno-oncology will include liquid biopsy approaches, enabling precise, early decision-making for the growing number of patients undergoing cancer immunotherapy.

Conclusions

Our study suggests that the combination of tislelizumab and low-dose bevacizumab may contribute to prolonging overall and progression-free survival in recurrent glioblastoma patients. The use of TISF-ctDNA tracking emerges as a feasible and potentially valuable method for monitoring treatment response in the context of the combination of PD-1 inhibitor and low-dose bevacizumab therapy. Significantly, the pattern of change in TISF-ctDNA MVAF over time shows greater predictive value for immunotherapy response than traditional imaging methods. Furthermore, PLR before immunotherapy can be used as a convenient screening indicator for patients who may benefit from the combination of tislelizumab and low dose bevacizumab therapy. These findings underscore the potential of TISF-ctDNA as a dynamic biomarker in the personalized management of glioblastoma, paving the way for more tailored and effective treatment strategies in the future.

Partition of Sparse Multigraphs into a Forest and a Forest with Restrictions
Ilkyoo Choi, Alexandr Kostochka, Matthew P. Yancey

Abstract
The following measure of sparsity of multigraphs refining the maximum average degree: For a > 0 a>0 and an arbitrary real b b , a multigraph H H is ( a , b ) (a,b) -sparse if it is loopless and for every A ⊆ V ⁡ ( H ) A\subseteq V(H) with | A | ≥ 2 |A|\geq 2 , the induced subgraph H ⁡ [ A ] H[A] has at most a ​ | A | + b a|A|+b edges. Forests are exactly ( 1 , − 1 ) (1,-1) -sparse multigraphs. It is known that the vertex set of any ( 2 , − 1 ) (2,-1) -sparse multigraph can be partitioned into two parts each of which induces a forest. For a given parameter D D we study for which pairs ( a , b ) (a,b) every ( a , b ) (a,b) -sparse multigraph G G admits a vertex partition ( V 1 , V 2 ) (V_{1},V_{2}) of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) such that G ⁡ [ V 1 ] G[V_{1}] and G ⁡ [ V 2 ] G[V_{2}] are forests, and in addition either (i) Δ ⁡ ( G ⁡ [ V 1 ] ) ≤ D \Delta(G[V_{1}])\leq D or (ii) every component of G ⁡ [ V 1 ] G[V_{1}] has at most D D edges. We find exact bounds on a a and b b for both types of problems (i) and (ii). We also consider problems of type (i) in the class of simple graphs and find exact bounds for all D ≥ 2 D\geq 2 .

Introduction

1.1
History and results

All multigraphs in this paper are loopless.
For a multigraph
G
, we use
V(G),E(G),|G|,\Delta(G)
, and
\delta(G)
to denote its vertex set, edge set, order, maximum degree, and minimum degree, respectively.
A
k
-vertex
is a vertex of degree
k
.
A
forest
is an acyclic graph.

We consider
partitions of vertex sets of multigraphs and simple graphs
into two forests. Recall that the
vertex arboricity

va(G)
of a multigraph
G
is the minimum number
k
such that there is a vertex partition
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})
of
V(G)
such that the subgraph
G[V_{i}]
of
G
induced by
V_{i}

has no cycles for each
1\leq i\leq k
. This parameter was introduced in 1968 by Chartrand, Kronk, and Wall
[
7
]
, who called it
point-arboricity
.

Hakimi and Schmeichel
[
13
]
proved that the decision problem whether a given planar graph has vertex arboricity at most
2
is NP-complete. In a series of papers
[
4
,
8
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
it was proved that broad subclasses of planar graphs have vertex arboricity
at most
2
. Mostly, these classes do not contain graphs with high average degree. Other sufficient conditions for
va(G)\leq 2
were proved for more general classes of sparse graphs.
Meaningful measures of sparseness of a (multi)graph
G
are maximum degree,
maximum average degree
,

mad(G)=\max_{H\subseteq G}\frac{2|E(H)|}{|V(H)|}
, and its refinement,

(a,b)
-sparseness.
For
a>0
and an arbitrary real
b
, call
a multigraph
H

(a,b)
-
sparse
if for every
A\subseteq V(H)

with
|A|\geq 2
, the induced subgraph
H[A]
has at most
a|A|+b
edges.
By definition, forests are exactly
(1,-1)
-sparse multigraphs.

A result of Borodin
[
2
]
from 1976, later reproved by Bollobás and Manvel
[
1
]
and Catlin and Lai
[
6
]
, implies that every simple graph
G
with
\Delta(G)\leq 4
not containing
K_{5}
satisfies
va(G)\leq 2
. A corollary of this result is that every graph
G
with
mad(G)\leq 4
(i.e.
(2,0)
-sparse) not containing
K_{5}

satisfies
va(G)\leq 2
.

A number of papers also consider partitions of multigraphs into two forests in which one or both of the forests has restrictions.
For an integer
D
, an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring
of a multigraph
G
is a partition
(M,F)
of
V(G)
such that
G[M]
is a forest with maximum degree at most
D
and
G[F]
is a forest.

The case of
D=0
(i.e.
M
is an independent set) of
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring has attracted much attention. It was proved (see e.g.
[
5
,
11
,
20
]
) that deciding whether a graph has an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{0},\mathcal{F})
-coloring is an NP-complete problem even in some classes of graphs with moderate average degree. In particular, Dross, Montassier, and Pinlou
[
11
]
proved this for the class of planar graphs, and Yang and Yuan
[
20
]
proved this for the class of graphs with maximum degree at most
4
.

On the other hand, for several versions of sparseness it was proved that “sparse” graphs admit an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{0},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.
Borodin and Glebov
[
3
]
proved that every planar graph of girth at least 5
has an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{0},\mathcal{F})
-coloring. Dross, Montassier, and Pinlou
[
11
]
conjectured that this holds already for planar graphs of girth at least
4
.

The above mentioned result by Borodin
[
2
]
, Bollobás and Manvel
[
1
]
, and Catlin and Lai
[
6
]
yields that every simple graph
G
with
\Delta(G)\leq 3
not containing
K_{4}
has an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{0},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.

Cranston and Yancey
[
9
]
proved that every
(1.5,0.5)
-sparse multigraph containing neither
K_{4}
nor a special
7
-vertex graph called the
Moser Spindle
has an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{0},\mathcal{F})
-coloring. They proved further that there is a finite set
\cal H
of graphs such that every
(1.6,0.8)
-sparse simple graph not containing a subgraph in
\cal H
also has an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{0},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.
Both bounds are exact.

In this paper we consider
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-colorings for
D\geq 1.

Our results on such colorings are:

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
D\geq 1
, every
\left(\frac{4D+3}{2(D+1)},\frac{1}{2(D+1)}\right)
-sparse
multigraph has an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
D\geq 2
, every
\left(\frac{6D+5}{3(D+1)},\frac{2}{3(D+1)}\right)
-sparse
simple graph has an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.

Both bounds are exact. Note that when
D=1
, the corresponding sparseness of Theorem
1.2
does not hold as there are more sparse “critical” graphs.

Dross, Montassier, and Pinlou
[
11
]
showed that planar graphs of girth at least
4
admit an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{5},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.
This was improved by Feghali and Šámal
[
12
]
to an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{3},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.
Liu and Wang
[
16
]
showed that planar graphs of girth at least
4
and no chorded
6
-cycle admit an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{2},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.
We use the above result to give Corollary
1.7
, which is an independent proof of Liu and Wang’s result.

We also consider a similar problem with stronger restrictions on one of the forests.
An
(\mathcal{F}^{e}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring
of a multigraph
G
is a partition
(M,F)
of
V(G)
such that
G[M]
is a forest in which every component has at most
D
edges and
G[F]
is a forest. For
D\in\{0,1\}
, an
(\mathcal{F}^{e}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring is simply an

(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring, but for
D\geq 2
, an
(\mathcal{F}^{e}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring is significantly more restrictive than an
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.

Cranston and Yancey
[
10
]
considered the similar problem when not only the components of
G[M]
are bounded, but also
F
must be an independent set. They found an exact bound on the maximum average degree of a (simple) graph that guarantees the existence of such a partition.

Our result on
(\mathcal{F}^{e}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring is:

Theorem 1.3
.

(i) For each odd
D\geq 1
, every
\left(\frac{4D+3}{2D+2},\frac{1}{2(D+1)}\right)
-sparse
multigraph has an
(\mathcal{F}^{e}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.

(ii) For each even
D\geq 2
, every
\left(\frac{4D+1}{2D+1},\frac{1}{2D+1}\right)
-sparse
multigraph has an
(\mathcal{F}^{e}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-coloring.

Both parts of Theorem
1.3
are sharp.
Note that the bound on sparseness for odd
D
is the same as in Theorem
1.1
, but
for even
D
it is different.

At the Barbados 2019 Graph Theory Workshop, Hendrey, Norin, and Wood asked for the largest value of
f_{a,b}
such that any graph
G
with
mad(G)<f_{a,b}
has a vertex partition
(A,B)
of
V(G)
such that
mad(G[A])<a
and
mad(G[B])<b
.
Observe that
mad(G[A])<\frac{2D}{D+1}
if and only if every component of
G[A]
has at most
D-1
edges.
Thus, Theorem
1.3
answers the analogous question for multigraphs when
b=2
and
a<2
.

1.2
Proof ideas and structure of the paper

We will prove our results in terms of the sizes of critical multigraphs, which are formally stronger than
Theorems
1.1
–
1.3

We call a multigraph
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-

-coloring (respectively, an

-coloring), but every proper subgraph does. In these terms, the counterparts of Theorems

–

Theorem 1.4
.

For each
D\geq 1

(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-critical multigraph

on at least three vertices satisfies
|E(G)|\geq\frac{(4D+3)|V(G)|+2}{2(D+1)}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For each
D\geq 2
, every
(\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-critical simple graph
G
on at least three vertices satisfies
|E(G)|\geq\frac{(6D+5)|V(G)|+3}{3(D+1)}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

(i) For each odd
D\geq 1
, every
(\mathcal{F}^{e}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-critical multigraph
G
on at least three vertices satisfies

|E(G)|\geq\frac{(4D+3)|V(G)|+2}{2(D+1)}
.

(ii) For each even
D\geq 2
, every
(\mathcal{F}^{e}_{D},\mathcal{F})
-critical multigraph
G
on at least three vertices satisfies

|E(G)|\geq\frac{(4D+1)|V(G)|+2}{2D+1}
.

Theorem
1.5
provides a short proof of Liu and Wang’s result.

(

Suppose

If

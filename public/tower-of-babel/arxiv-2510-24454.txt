Birational geometry of hyperkähler
manifolds and the Hu-Yau conjecture

Abstract




Abstract:

Wierzba and
Wiśniewski proved that for
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=4
, every
bimeromorphic map
M\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
of hyperkähler manifolds
is represented as a composition of Mukai flops.
Hu and Yau conjectured that this result can
be generalized to arbitrary dimension. They
defined “Mukai’s elementary transformation” as the
blow-up of a subvariety ruled by complex projective spaces, composed with
the contraction of the ruling. Hu and Yau conjectured that
any bimeromorphic map
M\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
can be decomposed into a sequence of Mukai’s
elementary transformations, after possibly removing
subvarieties of codimension greater than
2
.
We prove this conjecture for compact hyperkähler manifolds
of maximal holonomy by decomposing any bimeromorphic map into a
composition of wall-crossing flops associated with MBM contractions.

1
Introduction

Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be two bimeromorphic compact hyperkähler manifolds.
In
[
HY
]
Hu and Yau have studied the
most basic type of bimeromorphic maps between
M
and
M^{\prime}

known as Mukai flops or Mukai’s elementary transformations.
Hu and Yau conjectured that every bimeromorphic map between

M
and
M^{\prime}
may to a certain extent be described as a sequence
of Mukai flops. More precisely, given a bimeromorphic map

\varphi\colon M\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
, it was conjectured
that after removing some subvarieties of codimension
greater than two from
M
and
M^{\prime}
the map
\varphi
decomposes into
a finite composition of Mukai’s elementary transformations.

The work by Wisniewski and Wierzba
[
WW
]
, along with some observations in
[
BHL
]
, actually proves a stronger result in dimension four. In the case of fourfolds one does not need
to remove any subvarieties from
M
and
M^{\prime}
to decompose
\varphi

into a sequence of flops. The central result of
[
WW
, Theorem 1.1]
is the
description of the contraction loci in holomorphic symplectic fourfolds,
claiming that the contraction locus is always a disjoint union of
complex projective planes.

In the present paper we address the Hu–Yau conjecture in arbitrary
dimension. Our aim is to give a precise statement of the conjecture,
drawing attention to the possible ambiguity in the formulation.
The ambiguity originates from the need to remove subvarieties
of codimension three and higher from the original hyperkähler
manifolds. To make things precise, we introduce in Section
2.2
the notion
of a Hu–Yau transformation: this is a Mukai’s elementary transformation up to codimension three or higher. Our main result,
2.2
, claims that any bimeromorphic map
\varphi

as above is the composition of a finite number of Hu–Yau transformations.

Let us make a few comments on the comparison of our proof with that of
[
WW
]
.
Our proof relies on
[
WW
, Theorem 1.1]
in an essential way. On the other hand,
our methods give a new proof of
[
WW
, Theorem 1.2]
in the case when the
varieties are compact hyperkähler. The novelty here is a different method
of decomposing bimeromorphic maps into compositions of flops. Whereas loc. cit.
relies on the general arguments of the minimal model program providing the termination
of log-flips, our proof uses completely different methods based on
[
AV1
, Theorem 3.17]
.
The latter theorem (see also
[
HT0
, Proposition 17]
provides a locally finite wall-and-chamber structure for the
positive cone of an arbitrary compact hyperkähler manifold (under mild conditions on its second Betti number),
and therefore the possibility to decompose an arbitrary bimeromorphic map into a finite sequence of wall-crossing transformations,
see Section
4
for details. Unlike
[
WW
, Theorem 1.2]
, our proof works in the non-projective
setting, therefore generalizing the main results of loc. cit. to the case of non-projective
hyperkähler fourfolds.

In Section
7
we provide an example to justify our reformulation of the
Hu–Yau conjecture. The example deals with the geometry of intersections of the centers of Mukai’s elementary transformations
in codimension two, and to the dynamical behaviour of the composition of such transformations.

2
Main results

In this section, we give the key definitions, in order to state the main
result of this paper,
2.2
.

2.1
Mukai flops

Given a complex
(k+1)
-dimensional vector space
V
,
let
M_{2k}=\mathrm{Tot}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}(V)})
and
M^{\prime}_{2k}=\mathrm{Tot}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}(V^{*})})

be the total spaces of the cotangent bundles of
\mathbb{P}(V)
and of its dual.
It is well-known that the zero sections of the bundles
M_{2k}
and
M^{\prime}_{2k}
can be contracted,
and there is an isomorphism between the resulting varieties that gives rise to a birational map
between
M_{2k}
and
M^{\prime}_{2k}
called the Mukai flop.

Let us briefly recall some details of this construction. Starting from the Euler sequence on
\mathbb{P}(V)
:

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\to V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(1)\to T_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\to 0,

we pass to its dual and use the canonical map
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(-1)\hookrightarrow V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}
to get the embedding of vector bundles

\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}.

Given a point
[u]\in\mathbb{P}(V)
recall that
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}(V),[u]}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(V/\mathbb{C}u,\mathbb{C}u)
.
We see that
M_{2k}
can be described as the following closed subset of
\mathbb{P}(V)\times\operatorname{End}(V)
:

M_{2k}=\{([u],A)\in\mathbb{P}(V)\times\operatorname{End}(V)\enskip|\enskip\mathrm{im}(A)\subset\mathbb{C}u,\,A^{2}=0\}.

The projection
\pi_{2k}\colon M_{2k}\to\operatorname{End}(V)
contracts the zero section

Z_{2k}=\{([u],A)\in M_{2k}\enskip|\enskip A=0\}

to the origin.
The image
N_{2k}=\pi_{2k}(M_{2k})
is the closure of the minimal nilpotent
\mathrm{GL}(V)
-orbit in
\operatorname{End}(V)

consisting of square-zero endomorphisms of rank at most one. The symplectic form on
M_{2k}
is the pullback of the Kirillov–Kostant form on
N_{2k}
.
Analogously,

M_{2k}^{\prime}=\{([\alpha],B)\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})\times\operatorname{End}(V^{*})\enskip|\enskip\mathrm{im}(B)\subset\mathbb{C}\alpha,\,B^{2}=0\}.

There is the analogous projection
\pi_{2k}^{\prime}\colon M_{2k}^{\prime}\to\operatorname{End}(V^{*})
onto the closed affine cone
N_{2k}^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{End}(V^{*})
.
Identifying
\operatorname{End}(V)
with
\operatorname{End}(V^{*})
by taking adjoints of the endomorphisms gives an isomorphism between
N_{2k}
and
N_{2k}^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.1:

The morphism
\mu\colon M_{2k}\setminus Z_{2k}\to M_{2k}^{\prime}
defined by
\mu([u],A)=([\ker(A)],A^{*})

gives a birational map

\mu\colon M_{2k}\dashrightarrow M_{2k}^{\prime}

(2.1)

called the
Mukai flop
in codimension
k
. It is clear from the
above description that
\mu=(\pi_{2k}^{\prime})^{-1}\circ\pi_{2k}
, where
\pi_{2k}\colon M_{2k}\to N_{2k}
and
\pi_{2k}^{\prime}\colon M_{2k}^{\prime}\to N_{2k}^{\prime}\simeq N_{2k}

are the contractions described above.

Given a hyperkähler fourfold
M
and a projective plane
S\simeq\mathbb{C}P^{2}\subset M
,
an open analytic neighborhood of
S
in
M
is isomorphic to a neighbourhood of the zero section
Z_{4}
in
M_{4}
,
see
[
WW
]
and references therein, hence the Mukai flop construction can be performed
on any hyperkähler fourfold. In
[
WW
]
, J. Wierzba and J. Wiśniewski prove that a small contraction
of a quasiprojective holomorphic symplectic fourfold contracts a disjoint union of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
’s, and it follows that
any bimeromorphic map between hyperkähler manifolds of complex dimension four can be
decomposed into a composition of Mukai flops (
[
BHL
]
).

More generally, for hyperkähler manifolds of arbitrary dimension,
we would like to describe the building blocks of arbitrary bimeromorphic maps.
The local model of our bimeromorphic transformations will be given by taking the product of
a Mukai flop with a polydisc (possibly up to codimension three or higher, see below).

More precisely, let

U_{2k}\subset N_{2k}
be an open analytic neighborhood of the vertex
0\in N_{2k}
,
where
N_{2k}
is the affine cone from
2.1
. Using the morphisms

\pi_{2k}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{2k}
from
2.1
, we define the local model
of a bimeromorphic transformation of a
d
-dimensional complex manifold by the following
diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{-1}_{2k}(U_{2k})\times\Delta^{d-2k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\pi^{\prime}_{2k})^{-1}(U_{2k})\times\Delta^{d-2k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{2k}\times\Delta^{d-2k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu\times\mathrm{id}}

(2.2)

In the above diagram, the diagonal arrows define contractions with the exceptional
sets isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{k}
-bundles over the polydisc
\Delta^{d-2k}
. We introduce
the following notion, using the terminology of
[
HY
]
.

Definition 2.2:

Let
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
be a bimeromorphic map between arbitrary
complex manifolds of complex dimension
d
. We will say that
\varphi
is
Mukai’s elementary transformation

in codimension
k
, if there exists a normal complex-analytic variety
Y
, two closed non-empty complex
submanifolds
E\subset X
,
E^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
and two proper bimeromorphic morphisms

\pi\colon X\to Y
,
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y
that satisfy the following conditions.

1.

\varphi=(\pi^{\prime})^{-1}\circ\pi
;

2.

\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are contractions of
E
and
E^{\prime}
onto the singular locus
Y^{s}\subset Y
;

3.

for any point
y\in Y^{s}
there exists an open neighborhood
U\subset Y

of
y
isomorphic to
U_{2k}\times\Delta^{d-2k}
, and over
U
the map
\varphi

is of the form (
2.2
).

The manifolds
E
and
E^{\prime}
, which are
\mathbb{P}^{k}
-bundles over
Y^{s}
, are called
the
centers
of the transformation
\varphi
and its inverse, respectively.

To summarize, Mukai’s elementary transformation is the composition of a
contraction of a
\mathbb{P}^{k}
-bundle and of the inverse of another such contraction,
and transversally to the singular locus of the variety obtained by contraction
the transformation is modeled on the usual Mukai flop of the total space of the cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{P}^{k}

in its zero section, as in
2.1
.

2.2
Hu–Yau transformations and the Hu–Yau conjecture

In order to study bimeromorphic transformations of hyperkähler manifolds
of arbitrary dimension, we will need to use bimeromorphic maps more general
than the Mukai’s elementary transformations introduced in
2.1
.
Following the idea of Hu and Yau
[
HY
]
, we will allow arbitrary bimeromorphic
transformations that generically look like Mukai flops in codimension two,
at the same time ignoring what happens in codimension greater than two.
We formalize this by introducing the following notion.

Definition 2.3:

Let
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
be a bimeromorphic map between two complex manifolds.
We will say that
\varphi
is a
Hu–Yau transformation
if there exist closed
subvarieties
Z\subset X
and
Z^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
of codimension strictly greater than two,
such that over the open subsets
X_{0}=X\setminus Z
and
X_{0}^{\prime}=X^{\prime}\setminus Z^{\prime}

\varphi|_{X_{0}}\colon X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{0}^{\prime}

is a Mukai elementary transformation
in codimension two in the sense of
2.1
.

We reproduce the following conjecture made by Hu and Yau in
[
HY
, Conjecture 7.3]
,
stating it only for compact hyperkähler manifolds, since this will be the only case
considered in the present paper. The definition of a compact hyperkähler manifold
is recalled below, in Section
3
.

Conjecture 2.4:
Let
\varphi:\;M\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
be a bimeromorphic map of compact
hyperkähler manifolds. Then, after removing subvarieties
of codimension greater than two,
\varphi
is a sequence of
Mukai’s elementary transformations.

The above conjecture predicts a description of codimension two structure of bimeromorphic
maps between hyperkähler manifolds. Since we are
allowed to remove subvarieties of codimension greater than two, the conjecture
does not predict anything about bimeromorphic maps that are regular in codimension two.
Our main result is the following statement. We use the notion of a Hu–Yau transformation
introduced above in
2.2
.

Theorem 2.5:

Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be compact hyperkähler manifolds,
and
\varphi:\;M\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
a bimeromorphic map that is not regular in codimension two. Then

\varphi
is a composition of a finite number of
Hu–Yau transformations between compact hyperkähler manifolds.

The proof of the above theorem will be given in Section
6
.

3
Hyperkähler manifolds and MBM classes

In this section we fix the notation related to hyperkähler manifolds that
will be used throughout the paper.

3.1
Hyperkähler manifolds and their Teichmüller spaces

By a compact hyperkähler manifold we will mean a compact simply connected complex manifold
that admits a hyperkähler metric of maximal holonomy, see
[
Hu
]
. To study bimeromorphic
maps between hyperkähler manifolds it is sufficient to work with a fixed deformation class,
because by
[
Hu
, Proposition 27.8]
bimeromorphic hyperkähler manifolds are deformation
equivalent. Therefore we fix the underlying
C^{\infty}
-manifold
M
. Henceforth, a hyperkähler
manifold is the same thing as a complex structure of hyperkähler type (i.e. admitting a hyperkähler metric) on
M
.
Given such a complex structure
I
, we will write

(M,I)
for the corresponding hyperkähler manifold. If the complex structure is clear from the
context or irrelevant, we will simply write
M
instead of
(M,I)
.
The manifold
M
is holomorphically symplectic and irreducible, i.e. the holomorphic symplectic form on
M
is
unique up to multiplication by a constant. We will denote by
\sigma_{I}\in H^{0}(M,\Omega^{2}_{M})
a
symplectic form of unit volume, where the volume is defined as the integral of the form
\sigma_{I}^{n}\wedge\overline{\sigma}_{I}^{n}
.
Here
2n
is the complex dimension of
M
. We will also assume that
b_{2}(M)\geqslant 6
.

We will denote by
q
the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki (BBF for short) quadratic form on
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})
.
Recall from
[
Hu
]
that
q
is of signature
(3,b_{2}(M)-3)

and satisfies the Fujiki relations:

q(a)^{n}=c_{M}\int_{M}a^{2n}

for any
a\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, where
c_{M}\in\mathbb{Q}
is a positive constant that can be chosen so
that
q
is integral and primitive on
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, see
[
So
]
for details.

Since
M
is simply connected, its second homology is torsion-free,
hence
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})
.
The form
q
defines an embedding of lattices

q\colon H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. This induces an embedding of
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
into the vector
space
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
, in particular
q
restricts to a rational quadratic form on the homology classes
of degree two. In what follows we will implicitly use this extension of
q
to
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

The Teichmüller space of all complex structures of hyperkähler type on
M
will be denoted by
\operatorname{\sf Teich}(M)
.
The point corresponding to the complex structure
I
will be denoted by
[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}(M)
.
Recall the definition of the period domain:

\mathcal{D}=\{x\in\mathbb{P}H^{2}(M,\mathbb{C})\enskip|\enskip q(x)=0,\,q(x,\overline{x})>0\}

and the period map

\rho\colon\operatorname{\sf Teich}(M)\to\mathcal{D},

sending a point
[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}(X)
to
[\sigma_{I}]
. According to
[
Hu
]
, if
(M,I_{1})
and

(M,I_{2})
are bimeromorphic, then there exists a diffeomorphism
\alpha\colon M\to M
such that the points
[I_{1}]
and
[\alpha^{*}I_{2}]

are non-separated in
\operatorname{\sf Teich}(M)
.

3.2
MBM classes and the Kähler cone

Bimeromorphic hyperkähler manifolds correspond to non-separated points in the Teichmüller
space, so for our purposes we may fix a connected component of the latter. This connected component
will be denoted by
\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)
. The non-separated points of the Teichmüller space
are distinguished by the Kähler cones of the corresponding manifolds. For
[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}(M)

the Kähler cone of
(M,I)
, denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{I}
, is the set of cohomology classes in

H^{1,1}_{I}(M,\mathbb{R})
that can be represented by Kähler forms. To describe the structure
of the Kähler cone we will use the notion of an MBM class which we will now recall.

Given a cohomology class
\eta\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, we define

\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}_{\eta}(M)=\{[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)\enskip|\enskip q(\rho(I),\eta)=0\},

in other words, the subspace of the complex structures where
\eta
is of type
(1,1)
.

Definition 3.1:
A cohomology class
\eta\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is called
an MBM class
if
it satisfies the following conditions:

1.

\eta
is primitive;

2.

q(\eta)<0
;

3.

for any
[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}_{\eta}(M)
and any
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}_{I}
we have
q(\kappa,\eta)\neq 0
.

The set of MBM classes on
M
will be denoted by
\operatorname{MBM}(M)
(see
[
AV0
]
for various equivalent characterizations of MBM classes).

The notion of an MBM class has a deep connection with the geometry of rational curves on
M
.
We will partially recall this below, but see
[
AV3
, section 3]

for a more detailed discussion. Recall that by
[
AV1
, Theorem 3.17]
the mapping class
group of
M
acts on
\operatorname{MBM}(M)
with finitely many orbits, in particular the BBF squares of the MBM
classes are bounded.

Given
[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)
, recall that the restriction of
q
to
H^{1,1}_{I}(M,\mathbb{R})

has signature
(1,b_{2}(M)-3)
, therefore the set
\{x\in H^{1,1}_{I}(X,\mathbb{R})\enskip|\enskip q(x)>0\}

has two connected components. We define the positive cone
\mathcal{C}_{I}
to be the connected component
that contains the Kähler cone
\mathcal{K}_{I}
. Given
[I^{\prime}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)
and a
bimeromorphic map
\varphi\colon(M,I)\dashrightarrow(M,I^{\prime})
, recall from
[
Hu
]
that

\varphi^{*}
is a well defined map on
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{C})
. The set
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}

is an open convex cone contained
\mathcal{C}_{I}
.

Definition 3.2:
The union of the cones
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}
for all bimeromorphic maps
\varphi
as above
is called
the birational Kähler cone
of
(M,I)
, denoted by
\mathcal{BK}_{I}
.

Note that the birational Kähler cone is not convex in general. To describe its structure we
introduce the wall and chamber decomposition of the positive cone.
Given
\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(X)
, denote by
W_{\eta}
the corresponding wall in the positive cone,
i.e. the intersection of
\mathcal{C}_{I}
with the
q
-orthogonal complement of
\eta
. Define

\mathcal{C}_{I}^{\circ}=\mathcal{C}_{I}\setminus\left(\cup_{\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(X)}W_{\eta}\right).

Definition 3.3:
In the notation introduced above,
a Kähler chamber
is a connected component of

\mathcal{C}_{I}^{\circ}
.

The following statement summarizes a description of the cones introduced above.
It is a combination of results proven in
[
AV0
]
,
[
AV1
]
,
[
Ma
]
and
[
Bo
]
.

Theorem 3.4:

Assume that
b_{2}(M)\geqslant 5
and let
[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)
. Then we have the following:

1.

The collection of walls
W_{\eta}
, where
\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(X)
, is locally finite in
\mathcal{C}_{I}
1
1

1

Remark that
walls may accumulate towards the the border of
CC_{I}
, the isotropic cone.
;

2.

The Kähler cone
\mathcal{K}_{I}
is a connected component of
\mathcal{C}_{I}^{\circ}
;

3.

For every Kähler chamber there exists a unique
[I^{\prime}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)
such that

\rho(I^{\prime})=\rho(I)
and
\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}
is equal to that chamber.
The manifolds
(M,I)
and
(M,I^{\prime})
are bimeromorphic. This gives a bijection between the Kähler chambers
and the points
[I^{\prime}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}(M)
with
\rho(I)=\rho(I^{\prime})
and
(M,I^{\prime})
bimeromorphic to
(M,I)
;

4.

The birational Kähler cone
\mathcal{BK}_{I}
is the union of some of the Kähler chambers.
The closure
\overline{\mathcal{BK}_{I}}
is the cone dual to the pseudo-effective cone of
M
, in particular

\overline{\mathcal{BK}_{I}}
is convex.

We close this section by outlining the connection between the MBM classes and the structure
of extremal contractions of hyperkähler manifolds. For more details we refer to
[
AV3
]
.

Definition 3.5:
Given a class
\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(M)
we will say that
\eta

is extremal
, or that
\eta
defines
a wall of the Kähler cone
, if

W_{\eta}\cap\overline{\mathcal{K}_{I}}
contains a non-empty open subset of
W_{\eta}
.

Any MBM class is extremal on some deformation of the ambient complex structure; in a given one,
MBM classes are extremal up to monodromy and birational transformation, see
[
AV0
]
.

By a rational curve
C
in a hyperkähler manifold
M
we will mean the image
of a non-constant holomorphic map from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
into
M
. Its homology class will
be denoted by
[C]
. In the following definition we implicitly use the embedding

H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
given by the BBF form, as was explained above.

Definition 3.6:
Let
\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(M)
be an extremal MBM class.
The corresponding
full MBM locus

E_{\eta}
is the closure of the union
of all rational curves
C
such that
[C]
is proportional to
\eta
.

Note that the full MBM locus need not be irreducible (or even connected).
We will need to talk about the codimension of the MBM loci. By this we
will mean the following.

Definition 3.7:
Let
\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(M)
be an extremal MBM class. The
codimension
of
\eta

(and of
E_{\eta}
) is the minimum of the codimensions of the irreducible components
of
E_{\eta}
. We will say that
\eta
is
divisorial
if it is of codimension one,
otherwise
\eta
will be called
non-divisorial
.

Lemma 3.8:
Let
\eta
be a divisorial MBM class. Then
E_{\eta}
is an irreducible divisor.

Proof:

By the definition of the codimension of
\eta
,
E_{\eta}
contains at least one irreducible component
of codimension one, call this component
D
. The divisor
D
is uniruled, covered by
deformations of a rational curve
C
with
[C]
proportional to
\eta
, by the definition
of
E_{\eta}
. It is known (see e.g.
[
AV2
, Lemma 2.11]
) that
[C]
is proportional
to
[D]
, therefore
[D]
is proportional to
\eta
. Assume that
E^{\prime}
is another irreducible component of
E_{\eta}
.
Then
E^{\prime}
contains a rational curve
C^{\prime}
that does not lie inside of
D

and with
[C^{\prime}]
proportional to
\eta
. Since
C^{\prime}
is not contained in
D
, their intersection
index is non-negative, contradicting the fact that
q(\eta)<0
.

In the terminology of
[
Bo
]
, the divisorial MBM loci are the prime exceptional divisors, and
their cohomology classes span the extremal rays of the pseudo-effective cone of
M
.
Therefore the divisorial MBM classes define the walls of
\overline{\mathcal{BK}_{I}}
, i.e.
for a divisorial MBM class
\eta
the intersection
W_{\eta}\cap\overline{\mathcal{BK}_{I}}

contains a non-empty open subset of
W_{\eta}
that lies on the boundary of
\overline{\mathcal{BK}_{I}}
.
The non-divisorial extremal MBM classes define the internal walls that cut the birational
Kähler cone into Kähler chambers.

We close the section by recalling the following result (see also
[
BL
, Corollary 5.9]
).

Theorem 3.9:
[
AV3
, Theorem 4.6]
Let
\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(M)
be an extremal MBM class
on a hyperkähler manifold with
b_{2}(M)\geqslant 5
. Then there exists a normal symplectic variety
N

and a proper bimeromorphic morphism
\pi\colon M\to N
such that the exceptional
set of
\pi
is the full MBM locus
E_{\eta}
.

4
Wall-crossing flops

In this section we study the bimeromorphic transformations corresponding to
passing through a wall
W_{\eta}
defined by an extremal non-divisorial MBM class
\eta
.

Consider a point
[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)
and the corresponding birational Kähler cone
\mathcal{BK}_{I}
.

Definition 4.1:

We will say that two chambers
\mathcal{K}_{1},\mathcal{K}_{2}\subset\mathcal{BK}_{I}
are
adjacent
if
the intersection
\overline{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
contains a non-empty open
subset of one of the walls
W_{\eta}
for some
\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(M)
.

Definition 4.2:
Let
\varphi\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}
be a bimeromorphic map between hyperkähler manifolds
with Kähler cones
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
. If
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{K}_{2}
are adjacent,
then
\varphi
is called a
wall-crossing flop
.

It will be convenient for us to work with wall-crossing flops that satisfy
an additional condition, namely that
\varphi^{*}
acts as the identity on the second
cohomology of
M
. It is always possible to arrange this due to the following statement.

Proposition 4.3:

Assume that
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{BK}_{I}
is a chamber.
Then there exists a complex structure
I^{\prime}
of hyperkähler type on
M
, unique up to isotopy, and a bimeromorphic
map
\varphi\colon(M,I)\dashrightarrow(M,I^{\prime})
such that
\varphi^{*}
is the identity map on
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})

and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}
.

Proof:
By the definition of
\mathcal{BK}_{I}
, there exists
[\widetilde{I}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)
and
a bimeromorphic map
\psi\colon(M,I)\dashrightarrow(M,\widetilde{I})
such that
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\psi^{*}\mathcal{K}_{\widetilde{I}}
.
By
[
Hu
, Proposition 27.8]
there exist two families
\pi_{1}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\Delta
and
\pi_{2}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\to\Delta

of hyperkähler manifolds over the unit disc and a bimeromorphic map
\Psi\colon\mathcal{M}\dashrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}

with the following properties. First, we have
\mathcal{M}_{0}\simeq(M,I)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\simeq(M,\widetilde{I})
, where

\mathcal{M}_{t}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{t}
denote the fibers over
t\in\Delta
. Second,
\Psi
induces a fiberwise isomorphism
over
\Delta\setminus\{0\}
, and
\Psi_{0}\colon\mathcal{M}_{0}\dashrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
equals
\psi
.

Trivializing the families
\mathcal{M}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
by Ehresmann’s theorem, we may identify
\mathcal{M}_{t_{0}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{t_{0}}

with
M
as
C^{\infty}
-manifolds, where
0\neq t_{0}\in\Delta
is some base point. Then
\Psi_{t_{0}}
defines a diffeomorphism
of
M
. By the second property mentioned above, the action of
\Psi_{t_{0}}
on
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
coincides with the action of
\psi
.

Define
I^{\prime}=\Psi_{t_{0}}^{*}\widetilde{I}
. Then
\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1}\colon(M,\widetilde{I})\to(M,I^{\prime})
is a biholomorphic isomorphism,
hence
\mathcal{K}_{\widetilde{I}}=(\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1})^{*}\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}
. Define
\varphi=\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1}\circ\psi
. Then by construction

\varphi^{*}
acts on
H^{2}
as the identity. The claim about uniqueness of the complex structure
I^{\prime}
up to isotopy follows
from the description of the fibers of the period map, as was recalled in
3.2
.

Corollary 4.4:

Let
\varphi\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}
be a bimeromorphic map, where
M_{1}=(M,I_{1})
and
M_{2}=(M,I_{2})
.
Then there exists a unique
[I_{2}^{\prime}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}(M)
and an isomorphism
\xi\colon M_{2}\to M_{2}^{\prime}
,
where
M_{2}^{\prime}=(M,I_{2}^{\prime})
, such that
\varphi^{\prime}=\xi\circ\varphi\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}^{\prime}
is a bimeromorphic
map that acts as the identity on the second cohomology of
M
. If
\varphi
is a wall-crossing flop,
then so is
\varphi^{\prime}
.

Proof:

Let
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}\mathcal{K}_{I_{2}}
. By
4
there exists a complex structure
I_{2}^{\prime}
,
unique up to isotopy, and a bimeromorphic map
\varphi^{\prime}\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}^{\prime}=(M,I_{2}^{\prime})

such that
\varphi^{\prime}
acts as the identity on
H^{2}
and and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathcal{K}_{I_{2}^{\prime}}
. The
composition
\varphi\circ(\varphi^{\prime})^{-1}
is a bimeromorphic map from
M_{2}^{\prime}
to
M_{2}
, and
it maps the Kähler cone of
M_{2}
to the Kähler cone of
M_{2}^{\prime}
, hence by
[
Hu
, Proposition 27.6]

it is biholomorphic. Defining
\xi
to be the inverse of this map proves the first claim.
If
\varphi
is a wall-crossing flop, then by
4

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
is adjacent to
the Kähler cone of
M_{1}
. By construction
(\varphi^{\prime})^{*}\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}_{2}}=\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
, hence
\varphi^{\prime}

is also a wall-crossing flop.

Lemma 4.5:

Let
\varphi\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}
and
\varphi^{\prime}\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}
be two bimeromorphic
maps acting as the identity on the second cohomology. Then
\varphi^{\prime}=\xi\circ\varphi
,
where
\xi
is a biholomorphic automorphism of
M_{2}
that acts as the identity on the second cohomology.

Proof:

Define
\xi=\varphi^{\prime}\circ\varphi^{-1}
and conclude that it is a biholomorphic map by
[
Hu
, Proposition 27.6]
.

Now can describe the nature of wall-crossing flops, showing that they are compositions of
a blow-down followed by a blow-up, similarly to Mukai’s elementary transformations.

Proposition 4.6:

Let
\varphi\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}
be a wall-crossing flop. If
M_{i}
are non-projective,
assume additionally that
b_{2}(M_{i})\geqslant 6
. Then there exists a compact
symplectic variety
N
and two small contractions
\pi_{1}\colon M_{1}\to N
and
\pi_{2}\colon M_{2}\to N

such that
\varphi=\pi_{2}^{-1}\circ\pi_{1}
.

The proof of the above proposition will be divided in two parts, depending on whether
M_{i}
are projective.

4.1
The projective case

We prove
4
assuming that
M_{i}
are projective. Let
M_{1}=(M,I_{1})
and
M_{2}=(M,I_{2})
.
By
4
we may assume that the map
\varphi
acts as the identity on
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.
By the definition of a wall-crossing flop, there exists a wall
W_{z}
of the positive cone
\mathcal{C}_{I_{1}}
such
that the intersection
\overline{\mathcal{K}_{I_{1}}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{K}_{I_{2}}}
contains a non-empty open subset of
W_{z}
. Since the
manifolds
M_{i}
are projective, this open subset contains an integral element
c\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. The class

c
is of Hodge type
(1,1)
both on
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, so there exist
L_{1}\in\operatorname{Pic}(M_{1})
and
L_{2}\in\operatorname{Pic}(M_{2})

with
c_{1}(L_{i})=c
. By construction, the class
c
is big and nef both on
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, so by
a theorem of Kawamata
[
Kw
, Theorem 6.1]
the bundles
L_{i}
are semiample
and define small contractions
f_{1}\colon M_{1}\to N_{1}
and
f_{2}\colon M_{2}\to N_{2}
onto projective symplectic
varieties
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
. Let
\psi\colon N_{1}\dashrightarrow N_{2}
be the composition
f_{2}\circ\varphi\circ f_{1}^{-1}
.
Note that
L_{i}\simeq f_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{N_{i}}(1)
, where
\mathcal{O}_{N_{i}}(1)
are very ample line bundles on
N_{i}
, and

\varphi^{*}L_{2}\simeq L_{1}
by construction. We see that
\psi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{N_{2}}(1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{N_{1}}(1)
,
therefore by the Matsusaka–Mumford criterion
[
Ko
, Theorem 11.39]
we conclude that
\psi
is an
isomorphism. Identifying
N_{1}
with
N_{2}
via
\psi
and letting
N=N_{1}=N_{2}
, we finish the proof.

4.2
The non-projective case

To prove
4
in the case when
M_{i}
are non-projective we will use ergodic
theory, as in
[
AV3
]
. We first make the following observation.

Lemma 4.7:

Let
\pi\colon M\to N
be an extremal MBM contraction with the exceptional set
E_{\eta}
,
as in
3.2
, and
\xi\colon M\to M
an automorphism that acts trivially on
H^{2}
.
Then
\xi
descends to an automorphism of
N
.

Proof:

Consider the contraction
\pi^{\prime}=\pi\circ\xi
. Since
\xi
acts trivially on the second cohomology,
it preserves homology classes of the rational curves that cover the full MBM locus
E_{\eta}
.
By the definition of a full MBM locus,
\pi^{\prime}
preserves
E_{\eta}
and contracts all the fibers
of
\pi
. Consider the map
\pi\times\pi^{\prime}\colon M\to N\times N
and let
Z
be its
image. The variety
Z
is finite of degree one over both factors of
N\times N
, and since

N
is normal,
Z
is isomorphic to
N
, therefore being the graph of an automorphism of
N
.

Fix
\eta\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(M)
and recall that given a Kähler chamber
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{C}_{I}^{\circ}

we say that
\eta
defines a wall of
\mathcal{K}
if
\overline{\mathcal{K}}\cap W_{\eta}
contains a non-empty
open subset of the wall
W_{\eta}
. Define the following open subspaces of
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\eta}^{\circ}(M)
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{+}_{\eta}(M)=\{[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}_{\eta}(M)\enskip|\enskip\eta\mbox{ defines a wall of }\mathcal{K}_{I}\mbox{ and }q(\eta,\mathcal{K}_{I})>0\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{-}_{\eta}(M)=\{[I]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\circ}_{\eta}(M)\enskip|\enskip\eta\mbox{ defines a wall of }\mathcal{K}_{I}\mbox{ and }q(\eta,\mathcal{K}_{I})<0\}

Note that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the points of the two spaces above: given
[I^{+}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{+}_{\eta}(M)
,
the intersection
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{I^{+}}\cap W_{\eta}
contains a non-empty open subset of
W_{\eta}
, so there exists a unique
chamber
{\mathcal{K}}_{I^{-}}
on the opposite side of the wall
W_{\eta}
, so that
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{I^{-}}\cap W_{\eta}=\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{I^{+}}\cap W_{\eta}

and
[I^{-}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{-}_{\eta}(M)
. The two Kähler cones
{\mathcal{K}}_{I^{\pm}}
are adjacent and by
4
there exists
a wall-crossing flop
\varphi\colon(M,I^{+})\dashrightarrow(M,I^{-})
. It is clear that all wall-crossing flops across the walls defined by
\eta
arise this way.

We have two period maps
\rho^{\pm}\colon\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\pm}_{\eta}(M)\to\mathcal{D}_{\eta}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{\eta}=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathbb{P}(\eta^{\perp})
.
We see that the fibers of
\rho^{\pm}
over a point
[\sigma_{I}]\in\mathcal{D}_{\eta}
are in one-to-one correspondence
with the chambers of the wall
W_{\eta}=\mathcal{C}_{I}\cap\eta^{\perp}
. Those chambers are cut out by the orthogonal complements
to the MBM classes in
\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(M)
different from
\pm\eta
.

Denote by
G
the mapping class group of
M
and by
G_{\eta}\subset G
the stabilizer of
\eta
. Recall from
[
AV3
, Theorem 2.14]

the classification of
G_{\eta}
-orbits in
\mathcal{D}_{\eta}
. It follows from the classification that given a period
[\sigma_{I}]\in\mathcal{D}_{I}

of a non-projective hyperkähler manifold
(M,I)
, its orbit
G_{\eta}\cdot[\sigma_{I}]
contains a period of some projective
hyperkähler manifold in its closure.

Let us finish the proof of
4
in the non-projective case. Let
\varphi\colon M^{+}\dashrightarrow M^{-}

be a wall-crossing flop that acts trivially on
H^{2}
, where
M^{\pm}=(M,I^{\pm})
for some
[I^{\pm}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\pm}_{\eta}(M)
.
Let
\pi^{\pm}\colon M^{\pm}\to N^{\pm}
be the extremal contractions that exist by
3.2
.
We need to show that the bimeromorphic map
N^{+}\dashrightarrow N^{-}
induced by
\varphi
is an isomorphism.
Assuming that
I^{\pm}
are non-projective, we use the
G_{\eta}
-orbit classification
[
AV3
, Theorem 2.14]

and
[
V2
, Theorem 3.1]
to find a sequence of diffeomorphisms

\gamma_{i}
such that
[\gamma_{i}^{*}I^{+}]\to[I_{0}^{-}]
,
i\to\infty
, for some projective
complex structures
[I^{\pm}_{0}]\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\pm}_{\eta}(M)
. Clearly, also
[\gamma_{i}^{*}I^{-}]\to[I_{0}^{-}]
. Denote
M_{0}^{\pm}=(M,I_{0}^{\pm})
and let

\varphi_{0}\colon M_{0}^{+}\dashrightarrow M_{0}^{-}
be a wall-crossing flop acting trivially on
H^{2}
. By the projective
case of the proposition proven above, we can decompose
\varphi_{0}
into the composition of a contraction

\pi^{+}_{0}\colon M_{0}^{+}\to N_{0}
and the inverse of a contraction
\pi^{-}_{0}\colon M_{0}^{-}\to N_{0}
.

We refer to
[
BL
]
and references therein for the notion of a locally trivial deformation of a complex variety.
Let
\nu\colon\mathcal{N}\to B
be the universal locally trivial deformation of
N_{0}
over a polydisc, and
\Pi^{\pm}\colon\mathcal{M}^{\pm}\to\mathcal{N}

the corresponding deformations of the contractions
\pi_{0}^{\pm}
that exist by
[
BL
, Theorem 1.1]
.
The morphisms
\Pi^{\pm}
induce a family
\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}^{+}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{-}
,
\Phi_{0}=\varphi_{0}
,
of wall-crossing flops (each
\Phi_{t}
over
\mathcal{N}_{t}
), where
\mu^{\pm}\colon\mathcal{M}^{\pm}\to B
are the
deformations of
M_{0}^{\pm}
preserving the contractions. The latter induce embeddings
B\hookrightarrow\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\pm}_{\eta}(M)
, making
B
an open
neighborhood of
[I_{0}^{\pm}]
in
\operatorname{\sf Teich}^{\pm}_{\eta}(M)
. The convergence
[\gamma_{i}^{*}I^{\pm}]\to[I_{0}^{\pm}]
,
i\to\infty

implies that we can find
t\in B
and
\gamma\in G_{\eta}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\pm}\simeq(M,\gamma^{*}I^{\pm})
.

Now we have two wall-crossing flops
\Phi_{t}
and
\gamma^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\gamma\colon\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-}
.
By
4
we have
\gamma^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\gamma=\xi\circ\Phi_{t}
for some automorphism
\xi

of
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-}
acting trivially on
H^{2}
.
By construction, the flop
\Phi_{t}
induces an isomorphism between the bases
\mathcal{N}_{t}^{\pm}
of the extremal contractions

\Pi^{\pm}_{t}\colon\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\pm}\to\mathcal{N}_{t}^{\pm}
. By
4.2
the map
\xi
induces an automorphism of
\mathcal{N}_{t}^{-}
.
Therefore the flop
\gamma^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\gamma
induces an isomorphism between
\mathcal{N}_{t}^{\pm}
,
and the same is true about
\varphi
, since
\gamma\colon\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\pm}\to(M,I^{\pm})
is an isomorphism.
This completes the proof of
4
.

4.3
Decomposition

We now show how to decompose an arbitrary bimeromorphic map into a composition of wall-crossing flops.

Proposition 4.8:

Assume that
\varphi\colon M\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
is a bimeromorphic map between compact hyperkähler
manifolds with
b_{2}\geqslant 5
. Then there exists a finite sequence of wall-crossing flops
\varphi_{i}\colon M_{i}\dashrightarrow M_{i+1}
,

i=0,\ldots,m-1
such that
M_{0}=M
,
M_{m}=M^{\prime}
and
\varphi=\varphi_{m}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{0}
,

Proof:

By
4
we may assume that
\varphi\colon(M,I)\dashrightarrow(M,I^{\prime})
acts as the identity
on
H^{2}
and
\mathcal{K}_{I}
,
\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}
are two chambers of the birational Kähler cone
\mathcal{BK}_{I}
. Choose
two points
a\in\mathcal{K}_{I}
,
b\in\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}
and connect them by a straight segment
\ell\subset H^{1,1}_{I}(M,\mathbb{R})
.
By
[
AV1
, Theorem 3.17]
and
[
SSV
, Proposition 5.14]
the collection of walls
W_{z}
,
z\in\operatorname{MBM}^{1,1}_{I}(M)

is locally finite in
\mathcal{C}_{I}
, and since the segment
\ell
is compact, it intersects only a finite number of
walls
W_{z_{1}},\ldots,W_{z_{m}}
. Moreover, since the chambers
\mathcal{K}_{I}
and
\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}
are open, we may,
by perturbing the endpoints
a
and
b
, make sure that all points of intersection
W_{i}\cap\ell
are distinct.
In this case, whenever the segment
\ell
crosses a wall
W_{z_{i}}
, it passes to an adjacent chamber.
We therefore get a sequence of adjacent chambers
\mathcal{K}_{0}=\mathcal{K}_{I},\mathcal{K}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{K}_{m}=\mathcal{K}_{I^{\prime}}
. For every
chamber
\mathcal{K}_{j}
there exists a complex structure
I_{j}
such that
\mathcal{K}_{I_{j}}=\mathcal{K}_{j}
and a wall-crossing
flop
\varphi_{j}\colon(M,I_{j})\dashrightarrow(M,I_{j+1})
acting as the identity on
H^{2}
. Consider the composition

\alpha=\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi_{m}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{0}
, so that
\alpha\colon(M,I)\dashrightarrow(M,I)
.
By construction, the map
\alpha
acts as the identity on
H^{2}
, hence it preserves the Kähler cone
\mathcal{K}_{I}
,
therefore by
[
Hu
, Proposition 27.6]
the map
\alpha
is a regular automorphism. Replacing
\varphi_{0}
with
\varphi_{0}\circ\alpha^{-1}
,
we finish the proof.

4.4
Wall-crossing flops in codimension two

We apply the main theorem of
[
WW
]
to establish the structure of a wall-crossing flop
in codimension two. The main step is to reduce the question to the case of four-dimensional symplectic
manifolds. We do this by taking a slice transversal to the indeterminacy locus of the flop.

Assume that
M
is a holomorphically symplectic manifold and
\pi\colon M\to N

a proper bimeromorphic map onto a normal variety
N
. We recall that according to
[
Kl
]
the variety

N
is symplectic and admits a unique stratification
N=\coprod N_{i}
, where
N_{i}
are locally closed
non-singular symplectic subvarieties of
N
. We will call it
the Kaledin stratification
.
Recall also that the symplectic forms on
M
and
N
induce Poisson brackets on their structure
sheaves, so that
\pi
is a morphism of Poisson varieties.

The following proposition essentially follows from
[
Kl
, Theorem 2.3]
. In loc. cit., both the slice of
N

transversal to a stratum of the Kaledin stratification and a local product decomposition are constructed on the level of formal germs.
Our proposition contains two minor modifications: first, our slices and the decomposition are constructed locally in the analytic topology;
second, we construct them in the presence of a crepant resolution, and show that the resolution also
admits a local product decomposition. The proof is the same as in loc. cit., we give it here for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 4.9:

Let
M
be a holomorphically symplectic manifold
and
\pi\colon M\to N
a proper small contraction onto a normal symplectic variety.
Let
Z\subset N
be a stratum of the Kaledin stratification.
Then for any point
z\in Z
there exists an open subset
U\subset N
containing
z

and a closed symplectic subvariety
N^{\prime}\subset U
called
the Kaledin slice
such that:

1.

z\in N^{\prime}
and
\dim(N^{\prime})=\mathrm{codim}_{N}(Z)
;

2.

M^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}(N^{\prime})
is a non-singular symplectic variety and
\pi
induces a small contraction of
M^{\prime}
onto
N^{\prime}
;

3.

The subset
U
and its preimage in
M
admit product decompositions as symplectic varieties:

U\simeq N^{\prime}\times\Delta^{2d},\quad\pi^{-1}(U)\simeq M^{\prime}\times\Delta^{2d},

where
2d
is the dimension of
Z
and the polydisc
\Delta^{2d}
is endowed with the standard symplectic structure.

Proof:

Let
E\subset M
be the exceptional locus of
\pi
. Since
\pi
is a small contraction,
all irreducible components of
E
are of codimension at least two in
M
.
We will prove the proposition by induction on
d
. The case
d=0
being trivial, we assume that
d\geqslant 1
.

We let
U
be a small analytic neighborhood of
z
in
N
that we may shrink when necessary.
Given a holomorphic function
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
, we will denote by
H_{f}
its Hamiltonian vector field,
i.e. the vector field corresponding to the derivation
g\mapsto\{f,g\}
, where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is
the Poisson structure on
U
coming from the symplectic form. We will also let
\widetilde{f}
be the
pullback of
f
to
\pi^{-1}(U)
and
H_{\widetilde{f}}
its Hamiltonian vector field. Observe
that since
\pi
is a morphism of Poisson varieties, we have
d\pi(H_{\widetilde{f}})=H_{f}
.

Given a tangent vector
v\in T_{z}Z
, we claim that there exists a holomorphic function
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)

such that
H_{f}(z)=v
. Indeed,
Z
is a smooth manifold with a symplectic form
\sigma
induced
by the Poisson structure on
N
. We can therefore find a function
g\in\mathcal{O}(Z\cap U)
such that

dg_{z}=v\,\lrcorner\,\sigma
. The function
g
is a restriction of some
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
, and since
Z
is
a Poisson submanifold of
N
, we have by construction
H_{f}(z)=v
.

Given a function
f
as above, denote by
N_{f}
its zero locus, and by
M_{f}
the preimage of
N_{f}

in
M
, i.e. the zero locus of
\widetilde{f}=f\circ\pi
. Observe that after shrinking
U
we may
assume that
H_{f}
is nowhere vanishing, and hence the same is true of
H_{\widetilde{f}}
. It follows that

M_{f}
is a non-singular subvariety of
\pi^{-1}(U)
, indeed,
df
and
d\widetilde{f}
are non-zero by duality. Given a point
x\in E\cap\pi^{-1}(U)
, we have

d\pi(H_{\widetilde{f}}(x))=H_{f}(\pi(x))
and since
H_{f}
is tangent to the strata of the Kaledin stratification,
the vector
H_{\widetilde{f}}(x)
is tangent to
E
. Moreover,
H_{\widetilde{f}}(x)
is not contained in
the kernel of
d\pi
, therefore
E_{f}=E\cap M_{f}
is of codimension one in
E
.

We now choose two linearly independent vectors
v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{z}Z
with
\sigma(v_{1},v_{2})
= 1,
and the corresponding functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
as above. Shrinking
U
we may assume that the function

\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
is nowhere vanishing in
U
, so that
H_{f_{1}}
and
H_{f_{2}}
are linearly
independent in
U
. Then the hypersurfaces
M_{f_{1}}
and
M_{f_{2}}
intersect transversally
and their intersection
M^{\prime\prime}
is a submanifold of
\pi^{-1}(U)
. The tangent bundle of
M^{\prime\prime}
is
the symplectic orthogonal complement to the span of
H_{f_{1}}|_{M^{\prime\prime}}
and
H_{f_{2}}|_{M^{\prime\prime}}
,
and since
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
is nowhere vanishing,
M^{\prime\prime}
is a symplectic submanifold of
\pi^{-1}(U)
.
Observe that by the analogous argument
E^{\prime\prime}=E_{f_{1}}\cap E_{f_{2}}
is of codimension two in
E\cap\pi^{-1}(U)
,
and
\pi|_{M^{\prime\prime}}
is a small contraction onto
N^{\prime\prime}=N_{f_{1}}\cap N_{f_{2}}
.

We may apply the induction hypothesis and obtain the product decompositions
N^{\prime\prime}=N^{\prime}\times\Delta^{2d-2}

and
M^{\prime\prime}=M^{\prime}\times\Delta^{2d-2}
. To complete the proof, observe that

\widetilde{F}=(\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2})\colon\pi^{-1}(U)\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
defines a submersion onto a neighborhood of the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
The fiber over the origin is
M^{\prime\prime}
, and the submersion factors through
F=(f_{1},f_{2})\colon U\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
We may replace
U
by the preimage of a small bidisc in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
centered at the origin.
The vector fields
H_{f_{1}}
and
H_{f_{2}}
give a splitting of the exact sequence defining the relative tangent bundle
of the submersion
\widetilde{F}
. So, we have
\pi^{-1}(U)\simeq M^{\prime\prime}\times\Delta^{2}
as soon as the vector
fields
H_{\widetilde{f}_{1}}
and
H_{\widetilde{f}_{2}}
commute, that is, generate a foliation. To achieve this we
need to modify the function
f_{2}
without changing its zero locus.

We will replace
f_{2}
with another function
f_{2}^{\prime}
such that
\{f_{1},f_{2}^{\prime}\}=1
in
U

and
N_{f_{2}}=N_{f_{2}^{\prime}}
. We do it as follows. On
\pi^{-1}(U)
, the vector field
H_{\widetilde{f}_{1}}
is
transversal to the hypersurface
M_{f_{2}}
, since by construction
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
is nowhere
vanishing. Therefore there exists a unique function
\widetilde{f}_{2}^{\prime}
in a neighborhood of
M_{f_{2}}

such that
\widetilde{f}_{2}^{\prime}
vanishes on
M_{f_{2}}
and
\{\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2}^{\prime}\}=\mathcal{L}_{H_{\widetilde{f}_{1}}}(\widetilde{f}_{2}^{\prime})=1

(one can see this by using the local canonical form of a vector field in a neighborhood of its
regular point). After possibly shrinking

, the function
\widetilde{f}_{2}^{\prime}
is the pullback of some
f_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(U)
, because

\pi
is an isomorphism in codimension one and
N
is a normal variety.
Now we have
[H_{f_{1}},H_{f_{2}^{\prime}}]=H_{\{f_{1},f_{2}^{\prime}\}}=0
, so the vector fields
H_{\widetilde{f}_{1}}
and
H_{\widetilde{f}_{2}^{\prime}}

commute and define a regular foliation transversal to

. The leaves of this foliation give the necessary splitting.

We next describe the structure of wall-crossing flops in codimension two,
showing that they are Mukai’s elementary transformations in the sense of
2.1
.

Proposition 4.10:

Let
\varphi\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}
be a wall-crossing flop between hyperkähler manifolds.
If
M_{i}
are non-projective, assume additionally that
b_{2}(M_{i})\geqslant 6
.
Then there exist two closed subvarieties
Z_{1}\subset M_{1}
and
Z_{2}\subset M_{2}
of codimension
strictly greater than two, such that
\varphi\colon M_{1}\setminus Z_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}\setminus Z_{2}
is
Mukai’s elementary transformation in codimension two (or an isomorphism).

Proof:

We deduce from
4
that
\varphi=(\pi_{2})^{-1}\circ\pi_{1}
where
\pi_{i}\colon M_{i}\to N

are small contractions onto a symplectic variety
N
. Let
E_{i}\subset M_{i}
be the exceptional loci
of the contractions. Since the MBM class defining the flop
\varphi
is non-divisorial, all irreducible
components of
E_{i}
are of codimension strictly greater than one, and the singular locus of
N
is of
codimension strictly greater than three. We are interested in the structure of the flop over the
codimension four strata of the Kaledin stratification.

Let
Z
be the union of the strata of codimension six and higher (recall that the strata
of the Kaledin stratification are even-dimensional, being symplectic manifolds). Let

Z_{i}=\pi_{i}^{-1}(Z)
. The contractions
\pi_{i}
being semismall, the subvarieties
Z_{i}
are
of codimension at least three in
M_{i}
. Let
N^{\circ}=N\setminus Z
and
M_{i}^{\circ}=M_{i}\setminus Z_{i}
.

Note that the singular locus of
N^{\circ}
is either empty or the union of strata of codimension four in the Kaledin
stratification. If it is empty, by semismallness our flop is an isomorphism in codimension two.
Otherwise, given a singular point
z\in N^{\circ}
we apply
4.4
to construct the
Kaledin slice
N^{\prime}
passing through the point
z
. The slice
N^{\prime}
is four-dimensional and
z
is
its isolated singular point. It has two crepant resolutions
M_{1}^{\prime}=\pi_{1}^{-1}(N^{\prime})
and
M_{2}^{\prime}=\pi_{2}^{-1}(N^{\prime})
.
We now have to show that the preimages of
z
in
M_{i}^{\prime}
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, since in
that case the product decomposition from
4.4
implies that
\varphi|_{M_{1}^{\circ}}
is Mukai’s elementary
transformation in codimension two.

We apply
[
WW
, Theorem 1.1]
to the small contractions
\pi_{i}\colon M_{i}^{\prime}\to N^{\prime}
to conclude the proof.
Note that in loc. cit. the result is stated for quasi-projective varieties (however, see the remark following
the statement of Theorem 1.1. in loc. cit.). To reduce to the quasi-projective case, we use deformation invariance
of the contraction centers
[
AV3
, Theorem 1.8]
and deform
M_{i}
preserving the wall that
defines
\varphi
to make
M_{i}
projective. Next we note that in the proof of
4.4
the stratum of the
Kaledin stratification is a quasi-projective manifold. In the induction step of the proof, to construct
the slice
N^{\prime}
, we may choose the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
to be algebraic, defined on a Zariski-open subset
of

. The slice

and its preimages

constructed this way will also be quasi-projective,
and we may apply
[
WW
, Theorem 1.1]
to them, concluding the proof.

The following statement follows directly from the above proposition and

.

Corollary 4.11:

Any wall-crossing flop between hyperkähler manifolds (assuming
b_{2}\geqslant 6
in the non-projective case)
that is not regular in codimension two is a Hu–Yau transformation.

5
The symplectic rank and bimeromorphic maps

5.1
Definition of the symplectic rank

Let
M
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold with symplectic form
\sigma

and
Z\subset M
an irreducible subvariety. If
z\in Z
is a smooth point,
then the rank of the two-form
\sigma|_{T_{Z,z}}
is an even integer. This integer
is constant on a dense Zariski-open subset of
Z
, i.e. for a general point of
Z
.

Definition 5.1
.

For an irreducible subvariety
Z\subset M
its symplectic rank

\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)
is the rank of the form
\sigma|_{T_{Z,z}}
for a general smooth point
z\in Z
.
For a subvariety
Z=\cup_{i}Z_{i}
with irreducible components
Z_{i}\subset M
define

\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)={\rm max}_{i}{\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z_{i})}
.

We record a few useful properties of the symplectic rank.

Proposition 5.1:

Let
M
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold with symplectic form
\sigma

and
Z\subset M
an irreducible subvariety.

1.

Z
is isotropic if and only if
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)=0
;

2.

We always have
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)\geqslant\dim(Z)-\mathrm{codim}(Z)

and the equality holds if and only if
Z
is coisotropic;

3.

If
Z^{\prime}\subset Z
is another irreducible subvariety, then
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z^{\prime})\leqslant\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)
;

4.

If
W
is an irreducible variety and
f\colon W\to Z
a surjective
morphism, then
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)
is the rank of
f^{*}\sigma
at a general smooth point of
W
.

Proof:

(1), (2) and (3):
Obvious from the analogous statements about a subspace in a symplectic vector space.

(4):
Resolving the singularities of
W
we may assume that
W
is smooth. Then the morphism

f
is generically smooth over the smooth locus of
Z
. We may find a point
w\in W
such that

df_{w}\colon T_{W,w}\to T_{Z,f(w)}
is surjective and
\sigma|_{T_{Z,f(w)}}
is of rank
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)
.
Then
f^{*}\sigma|_{T_{W,w}}
is of the same rank as
\sigma|_{T_{Z,f(w)}}
. The points
w
with this
property form a non-empty open subset of
W
, hence the claim.

We next observe that the symplectic rank is invariant under bimeromorphic transformations.

Proposition 5.2:

Let
\varphi\colon M\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
be a bimeromorphic map between hyperkähler manifolds
with symplectic forms
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
,

Z\subset M
a closed subvariety and
Z^{\prime}=\varphi(Z)
its full transform under
\varphi
.
Then
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)=\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})
.

Proof:

Resolve the indeterminacies of
\varphi
by a bimeromorphic morphism
\pi_{1}\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
,
so that
\varphi=\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1}^{-1}
for some bimeromorphic morphism
\pi_{2}\colon\widetilde{M}\to M^{\prime}
.
Since the space of holomorphic two-forms on a hyperkähler manifold is one-dimensional,
the forms
\pi_{1}^{*}\sigma
and
\pi_{2}^{*}\sigma^{\prime}
are proportional, we may assume that they are equal,
and denote this two-form by
\widetilde{\sigma}
.

Let
\widetilde{Z}=\pi_{1}^{-1}(Z)
, then
Z^{\prime}=\pi_{2}(\widetilde{Z})
. Every irreducible component
of
Z
is dominated by an irreducible component of
\widetilde{Z}
, so by part (4) of
5.1
,
we have
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z)\leqslant\operatorname{rk}_{\widetilde{\sigma}}(\widetilde{Z})
. The inverse inequality follows from
parts (3) and (4) of
5.1
, because every irreducible component of
\widetilde{Z}
is mapped
into some irreducible component of
Z
. By an analogous argument applied to
Z^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{Z}
,
we have
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime})=\operatorname{rk}_{\widetilde{\sigma}}(\widetilde{Z})
and this completes the proof.

5.2
The symplectic rank of MBM loci and flops

We use the notion of symplectic rank to study compositions of wall-crossing flops.
This is the key technical step in the proof of the Hu–Yau conjecture.
We start from the following observation.

Proposition 5.3:

Let
\eta
be an extremal MBM class on a hyperkähler manifold
M
and
E_{\eta}
its full
MBM locus. Then
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(E_{\eta})=\dim(M)-2\operatorname{codim}(E_{\eta})
.

Proof:

By
[
AV2
, Proposition 2.2]
, for every irreducible component
E^{\prime}
of
E_{\eta}

the codimension of
E^{\prime}
equals the dimension of the kernel of
\sigma|_{E^{\prime}}
at a general
point of
E^{\prime}
. It follows that
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(E^{\prime})=\dim(E^{\prime})-\operatorname{codim}(E^{\prime})=\dim(M)-2\operatorname{codim}(E^{\prime})
,
which implies the claim, since the symplectic rank of
E_{\eta}
is defined by taking the
maximum over all irreducible components, and the codimension is defined by taking the minimum
over all irreducible components.

The following proposition is a strengthening of
4.4
.

Proposition 5.4:

Let
\varphi\colon M_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}
be a wall-crossing flop between hyperkähler manifolds of dimension
2n
.
If
M_{i}
are non-projective, assume additionally that
b_{2}(M_{i})\geqslant 6
.
Let
W_{1}\subset M_{1}
and
W_{2}\subset M_{2}
be two closed subvarieties with
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma_{i}}(W_{i})\leqslant 2n-6
.
Then there exist two closed subvarieties
Z_{1}\subset M_{1}
and
Z_{2}\subset M_{2}
of codimension
strictly greater than two with
W_{i}\subset Z_{i}
,
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma_{i}}(Z_{i})\leqslant 2n-6
and such that
\varphi\colon M_{1}\setminus Z_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{2}\setminus Z_{2}
is
Mukai’s elementary transformation in codimension two. In particular,
this applies when
W_{i}
are MBM loci of codimension strictly greater than two,
or the full transforms of such loci under some bimeromorphic maps.

Proof:

First note that by part (2) of
5.1
we have
2\operatorname{codim}(W_{i})\geqslant 2n-\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma_{i}}(W_{i})
,
and by our assumptions this implies
\operatorname{codim}(W_{i})\geqslant 3
.

Repeating the proof of
4.4
we decompose
\varphi
into the composition
of a contraction
\pi_{1}\colon M_{1}\to N
and the inverse of another contraction
\pi_{2}\colon M_{2}\to N
.
We let
W=\pi_{1}(W_{1})\cup\pi_{2}(W_{2})
and
Z\subset N
be the union of
W
and all strata of the Kaledin
stratification of codimension six and higher. Define
Z_{i}=\pi_{i}^{-1}(Z)
.
Note that since the symplectic rank of
W

is at most
2n-6
, the variety
W
can not contain any stratum of codimension four
in the Kaledin stratification. Therefore the varieties
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
do not dominate
any stratum of codimension four, so we have
\operatorname{rk}_{\sigma}(Z_{i})\leqslant 2n-6
.
The latter implies that
\operatorname{codim}(Z_{i})\geqslant 3
, as was explained above, and we conclude the proof as in
4.4
.

We are now ready to prove that under certain conditions the compositions of wall-crossing flops
are Hu–Yau transformations. This will give us a basic step in the proof of the Hu–Yau conjecture.

Proposition 5.5:

Consider a sequence of wall-crossing flops between
2n
-dimensional hyperkähler manifolds

M_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{1}}}{{\dashrightarrow}}M_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{2}}}{{\dashrightarrow}}\ldots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{k}}}{{\dashrightarrow}}M_{k+1},

such that exactly one of the MBM loci defining the flops has codimension two. Then the composition

\varphi=\varphi_{k}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{1}
is a Hu–Yau transformation.

Proof:

Assume that
\varphi_{i_{0}}
is a flop in codimension two. Let
W_{i_{0}+1}\subset M_{i_{0}+1}
be the union of the full transforms
of
E_{j}
for
j>i_{0}
and let
W_{i_{0}}\subset M_{i_{0}}
be the union of the full transforms
of
E_{j}
for
j<i_{0}
. Then by
5.1
the symplectic rank of
W_{i_{0}}
and
W_{i_{0}+1}
is a most
2n-6
.
Applying
5.2
to the flop
\varphi_{i_{0}}\colon M_{i_{0}}\dashrightarrow M_{i_{0}+1}
and the two subvarieties

W_{i_{0}}
and
W_{i_{0}+1}
, we get two subvarieties
Z_{i_{0}}
and
Z_{i_{0}+1}
of symplectic rank at most
2n-6

such that
\varphi_{i_{0}}\colon M_{i_{0}}\setminus Z_{i_{0}}\dashrightarrow M_{i_{0}+1}\setminus Z_{i_{0}+1}
is Mukai’s elementary transformation
in codimension two, and we have the inclusions
W_{i_{0}}\subset Z_{i_{0}}
and
W_{i_{0}+1}\subset Z_{i_{0}+1}
.
We let
Z_{1}\subset M_{1}
be the full transform of
Z_{i_{0}}
and
Z_{k+1}\subset M_{k+1}
be the full transform of
Z_{i_{0}+1}
.
By
5.1
the symplectic ranks of
Z_{1}
and
Z_{k+1}
are still a most
2n-6
, hence they are
of codimension at least three.
The composition
\varphi_{i_{0}-1}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{1}\colon M_{1}\setminus Z_{1}\to M_{i_{0}}\setminus Z_{i_{0}}
is a biholomorphic
morphism, because by construction
Z_{1}
contains the indeterminacy locus of that map. Analogously, the composition

\varphi_{k}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{i_{0}+1}\colon M_{i_{0}+1}\setminus Z_{i_{0}+1}\to M_{k+1}\setminus Z_{k+1}
is a biholomorphic
morphism. We therefore conclude that
\varphi\colon M_{1}\setminus Z_{1}\dashrightarrow M_{k+1}\setminus Z_{k+1}
is a composition
of two biholomorphic morphisms and Mukai’s elementary transformation in codimension two. Therefore
\varphi
is a Hu–Yau transformation.

6
Proof of the main theorem

We use the results of the previous section to prove
2.2
.
Given a bimeromorphic map
\varphi\colon M\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
between compact hyperkähler
manifolds, we apply
4.3
and decompose
\varphi

into a finite sequence of wall-crossing flops
\varphi_{i}\colon M_{i}\dashrightarrow M_{i+1}
,

i=1,\ldots,k
, such that
M_{1}=M
,
M_{k+1}=M^{\prime}
, so that
\varphi=\varphi_{k}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{1}
.
We split the sequence of flops
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{k}
into groups of consecutive flops, each
group containing exactly one flop in codimension two (all the flops in the sequence can not be in codimension greater than two
by the assumptions of the theorem). Applying
5.2
to
each of the groups of the flops, we see that the composition of the flops forming the group
is a Hu–Yau transformation. Hence the claim of the theorem.

7
Compositions of flops in codimension two

The key step of the proof of
2.2
is
5.2
that allows us
to show that a wall-crossing flop in codimension two is a Mukai elementary transformation
on an open subset with complement of codimension three or higher, even if we require that
the complement should contain a given subvariety (under some suitable conditions on the latter).
The purpose of this section is to show that it is in general not possible to achieve this
for a composition of several flops, i.e. there need not exist an open subset
with complement of codimension higher than two where such map is a composition of successive Mukai’s elementary transformations
in codimension two. We will show this on an example.

To construct the example, we start from the K3 surface
S
that is a quartic hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}

containing a line
C
, and generic with this condition. The pencil of planes passing through
C

defines an elliptic fibration
f\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose general fibre will be denoted by
F
. We take
M=S^{[3]}
.

Let us recall the description of the MBM classes on
M
following
[
AV4
]
, and the
explicit description of the MBM loci that can be found in
[
HT1
]
,
[
HT3
]
and
[
AV4
]
.

Let us denote by
\Lambda
the K3 lattice and let
\operatorname{Pic}(S)\subset\Lambda
be the Picard lattice of
S
.
The latter is spanned by the classes of the curves
C
and
F
and has the intersection matrix

\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\begin{pmatrix}-2&3\\
3&0\end{pmatrix}

Let
\Lambda^{\prime}
be the lattice
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
with the BBF form. Then
\Lambda^{\prime}\simeq\Lambda\oplus\mathbb{Z}\delta
,
where the direct sum is orthogonal and
\delta=[\Delta]/2
with
\Delta
the exceptional divisor of the contraction
S^{[3]}\to S^{(3)}
.
We have
\delta^{2}=-4
. Consequently, we also have
\operatorname{Pic}(M)\simeq\operatorname{Pic}(S)\oplus\mathbb{Z}\delta
.
The lattice
\Lambda
is unimodular and the discriminant group of the lattice
\Lambda^{\prime}
is clearly isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
.

According to
[
AV4
]
the MBM classes on
M
form five orbits under the action of the diffeomorphism
group of
M
: three orbits consisting of divisorial classes, one orbit of codimension two classes and one orbit of
codimension three classes. The orbits are distinguished by the square of the class
x\in\Lambda^{\prime}
and by the
image of
x/d(x)
in the discriminant group, where
d(x)
is the divisibility of
x
as an element of the dual
lattice
(\Lambda^{\prime})^{\vee}
. More precisely,
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are in the same orbit if and only if
x_{1}^{2}=x_{2}^{2}

and
x_{1}/d(x_{1})\equiv\pm x_{2}/d(x_{2})\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,\Lambda^{\prime})
.

The representatives of the orbits in our situation are the following:

1.

The divisorial class
\delta
; we have
\delta^{2}=-4
and
\delta/d(\delta)=\delta/4
is the class of the
ruling on the divisor
\Delta=E_{\delta}
;

2.

The divisorial class
\alpha=[C]
; we have
\alpha^{2}=-2
and
\delta(\alpha)=1
. The divisor
E_{\alpha}

consists of subschemes that intersect
C
;

3.

The divisorial class
\varepsilon=2[F]-\delta
; we have
\varepsilon/d(\varepsilon)=[F]-\delta/2
is the class
of the ruling on the divisor
E_{\varepsilon}
. The latter divisor is the closure of the image
of the natural map
\mathrm{Hilb}^{2}(S/\mathbb{P}^{1})\times S\dashrightarrow S^{[3]}
, where
\mathrm{Hilb}^{2}(S/\mathbb{P}^{1})

is the relative Hilbert scheme of the elliptic fibration
f\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
;

4.

Let us describe three classes of codimension two.

(a)

The class
\beta=4[C]-\delta
with
\beta^{2}=-36
and
d(\beta)=4
. The MBM locus
E_{\beta}

is the closure of the image of the natural map
C^{(2)}\times S\dashrightarrow S^{[3]}
;

(b)

The class
\eta=4[F]-3\delta
with
\eta^{2}=-36
and
d(\eta)=4
. The MBM locus
E_{\eta}

is the image of the natural embedding
\mathrm{Hilb}^{3}(S/\mathbb{P}^{1})\hookrightarrow S^{[3]}
;

(c)

The class
\zeta=4[C]+4[F]-5\delta
with
\zeta^{2}=-36
and
d(\zeta)=4
. The MBM locus

E_{\zeta}
consists of subschemes of
S
that lie on a line in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
; see Remark
7.1
below;

5.

The codimension three class
\gamma=2[C]-\delta
; we have
\gamma^{2}=-12
and
\delta(\gamma)=2
.
The MBM locus
E_{\gamma}
is the set of subschemes supported on
C
, isomorphic to
C^{(3)}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Remark 7.1
.

Descriptions of all MBM classes and the corresponding rational curves from the above list have already
appeared in the literature, see
[
AV4
, section 4]
and references therein. One exception is,
possibly, the class
\zeta
. Let us indicate how one can find the expression of this class given above.
A rational curve in the MBM locus
E_{\zeta}
can be described as follows. Let us fix a
line
L_{0}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that
L_{0}\cap S=\{p,q,r,s\}
, where
p\in C
and
\{q,r,s\}
are
three other distinct point on
S
. Let
P_{0}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
be a plane passing through the line
L_{0}

and not containing
C
.
Consider all lines
L\subset P_{0}
passing through the point
q
. For a generic such line the intersection

S\cap L
consists of the point
q
and three other points on
S
, the latter defining
a point of
S^{[3]}
. This gives a rational curve
C_{\zeta}
in
S^{[3]}
. To find its cohomology class,
we can compute its intersection indices with the divisor classes
[C]
,
[F]
and
\varepsilon
.
We have chosen the line
L_{0}
in a way to make sure that the points of intersection are smooth points of the
divisors and the intersections are transversal.
The divisor with the class
[C]
is
E_{\alpha}
, it consists of all subschemes of
S
intersecting the
curve
C
. By construction, there is only one such subscheme
\{p,r,s\}
on the curve
C_{\zeta}
, hence

C_{\zeta}\cdot[C]=1
. Analogously, to compute
C_{\zeta}\cdot[F]
, consider a generic plane
P_{1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}

containing
C
. Let
F_{1}
be the irreducible component of
P_{1}\cap S
of degree three. The class

[F]
on
S^{[3]}
is represented by the divisor consisting of subschemes intersecting
F_{1}
.
Note that
F_{1}\cap P_{0}
are three points on a line in
P_{0}
. For a generic choice of
P_{1}
the latter
line is different from
L_{0}
, and therefore
C_{\zeta}
has three distinct points of intersection with
the above divisor, therefore
C_{\zeta}\cdot[F]=3
. To compute
C_{\zeta}\cdot\varepsilon
, note that the
unique point of intersection of
C_{\zeta}
and
E_{\varepsilon}
is the subscheme
\{p,r,s\}
corresponding
to the line
L_{0}
, hence
C_{\zeta}\cdot\varepsilon=1
. Using these intersection indices, we find that

[C_{\zeta}]=[C]+[F]-\frac{5}{4}\delta
, hence the expression for the MBM class
\zeta
given
in the list above.

In the above listing we have slightly abused the notation for the MBM loci: not all of the MBM classes
on the list are extremal, so not all of the loci described above are contractible on
S^{[3]}
. In order
to make them contractible one may need to pass to a different birational model. The birational
ample cone of
S^{[3]}
(i.e. the intersection of the birational Kähler cone with the subspace
spanned by the Picard lattice) and its wall-and-chamber structure is depicted in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
The birational ample cone of
S^{[3]}

In Figure
1
we have the projectivisation of the positive cone, which is the hyperbolic plane in our case,
and the walls described above. We will consider the Kähler chambers numbered 1, 2, 3 and 4, and the corresponding
birational models
M_{i}
for
i=1,2,3,4
. The model
M_{3}
is the Hilbert scheme
S^{[3]}
.
The walls are colored depending on the image of
x/d(x)
in the discriminant group for an MBM class
x
:
the black walls correspond to
0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
, the blue walls to
\pm 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)

and the red walls to
2\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
.

The manifold
S^{[3]}
admits a Lagrangian fibration induced by the elliptic fibration
f\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
the nef isotropic class defining the fibration being equal to
[F]
(the lower right cusp in Figure
1
).
It also admits a rational Lagrangian fibration defined by the isotropic class
[C]+[F]-\delta
(the point
of concentration of the walls in the upper left part of Figure
1
). This fibration is due to the fact
that
S^{[3]}
is birational to the Beauville–Mukai system with the Mukai vector
(0,[C]+[F],1)
; it can be described
explicitly as the map
S^{[3]}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
sending a triple of points on
S
to the plane in
\mathbb{P}^{3}

spanned by these points. One can see that the indeterminacy locus of this map is the MBM locus
E_{\zeta}
,
and one passes to a model where the fibration becomes regular by making two flops: in
E_{\gamma}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}

(which is the singular locus of
E_{\zeta}
) and then in
E_{\zeta}
. The Beauville–Mukai system in our example admits an infinite
group of birational automorphisms, hence the infinite family of walls passing
through the isotropic class
[C]+[F]-\delta
.

We will consider the following sequence of flops:

M_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{\beta}}}{{\dashrightarrow}}M_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{\gamma}}}{{\dashrightarrow}}M_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{\eta}}}{{\dashrightarrow}}M_{4},

denoting by
\varphi
their composition. Both
\varphi_{\beta}
and
\varphi_{\eta}
are wall-crossing flops in codimension two,
while
\varphi_{\gamma}
is a wall-crossing flop in codimension three.

To understand the geometry of the map
\varphi
, we need to study the intersections of the MBM loci
that define the flops. First, consider the subvariety
E_{\beta}
. It is non-normal and
birational to
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times S\simeq C^{(2)}\times S
. The singular locus of
E_{\beta}
is
E_{\gamma}\simeq C^{(3)}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Denote by
\widetilde{E}_{\beta}

the normalization of
E_{\beta}
, then
\widetilde{E}_{\beta}
is isomorphic to the blow-up of

C^{(2)}\times S
in the incidence subvariety
Z\subset C^{(2)}\times C\subset C^{(2)}\times S
,
where
((x,y),z)\in Z
iff
x=z
or
y=z
. The proper transform of
C^{(2)}\times C
under the
blow-up is a divisor
D\subset\widetilde{E}_{\beta}
. The proper transform of
E_{\beta}
on the model
M_{2}
,
that we will denote by
E^{\prime}_{\beta}
, is obtained by blowing-down the divisor
D
. Then variety
E^{\prime}_{\beta}

is a
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle over
S
and it is the center of the flop
\varphi_{\beta}^{-1}
.

The center of the flop
\varphi_{\eta}
is
E_{\eta}\simeq\mathrm{Hilb}^{3}(S/\mathbb{P}^{1})
,
which is a
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle over another K3 surface
S^{\prime}=\operatorname{Pic}^{3}(S/\mathbb{P}^{1})
, the latter being the
relative Picard group of the elliptic fibration
f\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
we will
denote by
F_{t}
the fiber of
f
over
t
. We will implicitly fix an origin on
F_{t}
making
it an elliptic curve and also identifying the fiber of
S^{\prime}
over
t
with
F_{t}
.

Let
E_{\beta\eta}=E_{\beta}\cap E_{\eta}\subset M_{3}
. Then we have a morphism
E_{\beta\eta}\to S^{\prime}

that is the composition of the embedding into
E_{\eta}
and the projection onto
S^{\prime}
. Analogously,
we get a rational map
E_{\beta\eta}\dashrightarrow S
given by the embedding into
E_{\beta}
and
the rational projection onto
S
. We will denote by
\Sigma\subset S\times S^{\prime}
the closure of the image
of the product of the two maps above. Note that
\Sigma
is fibered over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
denote by
\Sigma_{t}=\Sigma\cap(F_{t}\times F_{t})
a fiber.

Lemma 7.1:

The subvariety
\Sigma\subset S\times S^{\prime}
is dominant and finite of degree three both over
S
and
S^{\prime}
.
If the fiber
F_{t}\subset S
intersects the curve
C
in three distinct points
e_{0}
,
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
,
then
\Sigma_{t}\subset F_{t}\times F_{t}
consists of three connected components that are the translations
of the diagonal by
(0,e_{0}+e_{1})
,
(0,e_{1}+e_{2})
and
(0,e_{2}+e_{0})
.

Proof:

Let us fix a point
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
so that
F_{t}
satisfies the conditions of the lemma. Let
x\in F_{t}

be a point different from
e_{0}
,
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
. Then the fiber of
E_{\beta}
over
x
is
\mathbb{P}^{2}\simeq C^{(2)}\times\{x\}
.
It intersects
E_{\eta}
in three points:
((e_{0},e_{1}),x)
,
((e_{1},e_{2}),x)
and
((e_{2},e_{0}),x)

that are mapped by the projection
E_{\eta}\to S^{\prime}
to the points
e_{0}+e_{1}+x
,
e_{1}+e_{2}+x

and
e_{2}+e_{0}+x
respectively. This proves the claim about
\Sigma_{t}
, because the latter is closed in
F_{t}\times F_{t}
.
Hence
\Sigma_{t}
is dominant and finite of degree three over both factors, and since this holds for a generic
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
we also get the first claim of the lemma.

Denote by
p
and
q
the projections from
\Sigma
to the two factors of
S\times S^{\prime}
.
Given
x,y\in S
we will write
x\sim y
if
q(p^{-1}(x))\cap q(p^{-1}(y))\neq\emptyset
.
Generating an equivalence relation on the points of
S
by
\sim
, we will call the corresponding
equivalence classes
\Sigma
-orbits.

Lemma 7.2:

Let
x\in F_{t}
. Then
x\sim x+e_{1}-e_{0}
,
x\sim x+e_{2}-e_{1}
and
x\sim x+e_{0}-e_{2}
.
Whenever at least one of
e_{1}-e_{0}
,
e_{2}-e_{1}
or
e_{0}-e_{2}
is non-torsion,
the orbit of
x
is infinite, hence Zariski dense in
F_{t}
.

Proof:

From
7
we see that

q(p^{-1}(x))=\{x+e_{0}+e_{1},x+e_{1}+e_{2},x+e_{2}+e_{0}\},

and

q(p^{-1}(x+e_{1}-e_{0}))=\{x+2e_{1},x+2e_{1}-e_{0}+e_{2},x+e_{1}+e_{2}\},

which
shows that
x\sim x+e_{1}-e_{0}
. Analogously for the other two points. The last claim
of the lemma is obvious.

Lemma 7.3:

For a very general
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
the points
e_{1}-e_{0}
,
e_{2}-e_{1}
and
e_{0}-e_{2}

are non-torsion in
F_{t}
.

Proof:

Let
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the complement of the discriminant of the elliptic fibration
f

and let
g\colon J\to U
be the Jacobian fibration corresponding to
f|_{U}
. Let
J^{t}\subset J

be the subset of points that are torsion in their respective fibers. Then
J^{t}
is a countable
union of closed subvarieties of
J
, each of them being an unramified finite cover of
U
.

For a generic choice of the surface
S
the curve
C
is ramified over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
so that at least one of the
ramification points is contained in a smooth fiber of the elliptic fibration
f
.
The pairwise differences of the points
e_{0}
,
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
(that are the intersection points
of
F_{t}
with
C
) define a curve
C^{\prime}\subset J
that is a ramified covering of
U
.
Hence
C^{\prime}
can not be contained in
J^{t}
, therefore
J^{t}\cap C^{\prime}
and its image in
U
are
countable sets. This proves the claim.

As the final step of our discussion let us show that the birational map
\varphi
can not be represented
as a composition of Mukai flops in codimension two, even after removing some subvarieties of higher
codimension from
M_{1}
and
M_{4}
, see
7
below. Before proving this claim, let us
introduce some notation. Let
Z_{\beta}
be the center of the flop
\varphi_{\beta}
,

Z_{\eta}
be the proper transforms of
E_{\eta}
on the model
M_{1}
,
and
Z_{\gamma}
be the proper transform of the center of the flop
\varphi_{\gamma}
on the model
M_{1}
. By construction,

Z_{\beta}
,
Z_{\gamma}
and
Z_{\eta}
are the irreducible components of the indeterminacy locus
of the map
\varphi
. Note that
Z_{\gamma}
is of codimension three, being birational to
\mathbb{P}^{3}
,
and the other two components are of codimension two. The subvarieties
Z_{\beta}
,
Z_{\gamma}
and
Z_{\eta}

are swept out by rational curves representing the corresponding MBM classes.

Assume that there exist two open subsets
M_{1}^{\circ}\subset M_{1}
and
M_{4}^{\circ}\subset M_{4}

such that
\varphi|_{M_{1}^{\circ}}=\varphi_{m}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{0}
,
where
\varphi_{i}\colon U_{i}\dashrightarrow U_{i+1}
are Mukai’s elementary transformations in codimension two,

U_{0}=M_{1}^{\circ}
and
U_{m+1}=M_{4}^{\circ}
. Every
\varphi_{i}
is the composition
of a proper contraction
U_{i}\to V_{i}
with the exceptional set
E_{i}
and the inverse
of a proper contraction
U_{i+1}\to V_{i}
with the exceptional set
E^{\prime}_{i+1}
.
Both
E_{i}
and
E^{\prime}_{i+1}
are smooth
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundles over the singular locus of
V_{i}
.
We may also assume that
E_{i}
and
E^{\prime}_{i+1}
are irreducible.

Let us denote by
Z_{i}
the proper transforms of the varieties
E_{i}
on the model
M_{1}^{\circ}
.
By this we mean the following. Given an irreducible subvariety of codimension two
Z\subset U_{i+1}
,
we have two possibilities. Either
Z
is not contained in
E^{\prime}_{i+1}
, and then we take its
proper preimage under
\varphi_{i}
in the usual sense: the closure in
U_{i}
of the preimage
of
Z\setminus E_{i+1}^{\prime}
under
\varphi_{i}
. Or
Z
coincides with
E^{\prime}_{i+1}
, and
in this case by the proper preimage of
Z
under
\varphi_{i}
we will mean the subvariety
E_{i}
.
Iterating this procedure over the maps
\varphi_{i}
, we get a proper preimage of
Z
in
M_{1}^{\circ}
.

The proper preimages
Z_{i}
are codimension two irreducible subvarieties of
M_{1}^{\circ}

that are swept out by rational curves
C_{i}
, the latter being proper preimages of the
lines in the fibers of the
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundles
E_{i}
. Let us denote by
\overline{Z}_{i}
the
closure of
Z_{i}
in
M_{1}
. Now we can prove the following statement.

Proposition 7.4:

There do not exist open subsets
M_{1}^{\circ}\subset M_{1}
and
M_{4}^{\circ}\subset M_{4}
with complements
of codimension strictly greater than two and such that
\varphi\colon M_{1}^{\circ}\dashrightarrow M_{4}^{\circ}

is a composition of Mukai’s elementary transformations in codimension two.

Proof:
Assume the contrary and use the notation introduced above. To arrive at a contradiction,
we will make two steps. First, we will show that the subvariety
Z_{\gamma}
is contained in the complement
of
M_{1}^{\circ}
. Second, we will use
7
,
7
and
7
to show that

Z_{\beta}
and
Z_{\eta}
also have to be in the complement of
M_{1}^{\circ}
contradicting the assumption on
the codimension.

Step 1.
Note that the indeterminacy locus of the map
\varphi|_{M_{1}^{\circ}}
has to be contained in the union
of the subvarieties
Z_{i}
constructed before the statement of the proposition.
Assume that
Z^{\circ}_{\gamma}=Z_{\gamma}\cap M_{1}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
. Then
Z^{\circ}_{\gamma}
has
to be contained in one of
Z_{i}
. Let
i_{0}
be the smallest index such that
Z^{\circ}_{\gamma}\subset Z_{i_{0}}
,
so that
Z_{\gamma}\subset\overline{Z}_{i_{0}}
. Let
Z_{\gamma}^{\prime}\subset U_{i_{0}}
be the proper transform
of
Z_{\gamma}^{\circ}
under the sequence of flops
\varphi_{0},\ldots,\varphi_{i_{0}-1}
. By construction,
Z_{\gamma}^{\prime}\subset E_{i_{0}}
.

We claim that
Z_{\gamma}^{\prime}
does not dominate the base of the
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle
E_{i_{0}}
. To prove the claim, let

\widetilde{Z}_{i_{0}}
be the resolution of singularities of
\overline{Z}_{i_{0}}
. Observe that the singular
locus of
Z_{i_{0}}
is contained in the union of
Z_{i}
with
i<i_{0}
. Therefore, our choice of
i_{0}

guarantees that
Z_{\gamma}^{\circ}
is not contained in the singular locus of
Z_{i_{0}}
. We can then
consider the proper preimage
\widetilde{Z}_{\gamma}
of
Z_{\gamma}
in
\widetilde{Z}_{i_{0}}
,
and the variety
\widetilde{Z}_{\gamma}
is then birational to
Z_{\gamma}
. Since
Z_{\gamma}
is rational,
the variety
\widetilde{Z}_{\gamma}
can not dominate the base of the MRC-fibration of
\widetilde{Z}_{i_{0}}
.
The latter is birational to the
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle
E_{i_{0}}
, hence the claim that
Z_{\gamma}^{\prime}
does not dominate
the base of that
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle.

The previous paragraph implies that the variety
Z_{\gamma}^{\prime}
contains one of the fibers of the
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle
E_{i_{0}}
.
It follows that one of the rational curves
C_{i_{0}}
defined above
may be chosen to lie inside the variety
Z_{\gamma}
. Then the image of
C_{i_{0}}
on the model
M_{3}
is
contained in the MBM locus
E_{\gamma}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
, therefore all deformations of
C_{i_{0}}
stay inside
E_{\gamma}
.
On the other hand, by construction, the deformations of
C_{i_{0}}
sweep out a subvariety of codimension two.
This contradiction completes the first step of the proof.

Step 2.
Denote by
\pi_{1}\colon M_{1}\to N_{1}
and
\pi_{4}\colon M_{4}\to N_{4}
the extremal contractions
of the MBM loci that define the wall-crossing flops
\varphi_{\beta}
and
\varphi_{\eta}
.
Note that the exceptional set of
\pi_{1}
is the variety
Z_{\beta}
, and since it has codimension two,
it has to coincide with
\overline{Z}_{i}
for some
i
. The variety
Z_{i}=\overline{Z}_{i}\cap M_{1}^{\circ}=Z_{\beta}\cap M_{1}^{\circ}
is a proper

\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle, hence
M_{1}^{\circ}
has to be proper over its image in
N_{1}
.
Analogously,
M_{4}^{\circ}
has to be proper over its image in
N_{4}
.

The singular locus of
N_{1}
is the K3 surface
S
. Assume that

x\in S
is a point in the complement of
\pi_{1}(M_{1}^{\circ})
. Our definition of
the correspondence
\Sigma\subset S\times S^{\prime}
from
7
implies that the
entire
\Sigma
-orbit of
x
has to lie in the complement of
\pi_{1}(M_{1}^{\circ})
.
By
7
and
7
such orbit will be dense in the fiber
F_{t}
of the elliptic
fibration
f
if
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is very general.

Now recall that by the first step of the proof
Z_{\gamma}
has to be contained in the complement of
M_{1}^{\circ}
.
Therefore the curve
C\subset S\subset N_{1}
is in the complement of
\pi_{1}(M_{1}^{\circ})
.
The curve
C
intersects all fibers of the elliptic fibration
f
, so by the previous
paragraph the complement of
\pi_{1}(M_{1}^{\circ})
contains a very general fiber
F_{t}
.
Therefore it has to contain the whose surface
S
, because very general fibers form a dense subset of
S
.
We conclude that the entire MBM locus
Z_{\beta}
has to be in the complement of
M_{1}^{\circ}
.
But
Z_{\beta}
has codimension two in
M_{1}
, hence the claim of the proposition.

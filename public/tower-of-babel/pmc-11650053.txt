Setting of import tolerances for azoxystrobin in melons and watermelons
Authors: EFSA (European Food Safety Authority), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Luis Carrasco Cabrera, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Luna Greco, Samira Jarrah, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Stefanie Nave, Hermine Reich, Silvia Ruocco, Alessia Pia Scarlato, Andrea Simonati, Marta Szot, Anne Theobald, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani, Elena Zioga
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant Syngenta Crop Protection AG submitted a request to the competent national authority in Austria to set an import tolerance for the active substance azoxystrobin in melons and watermelons. The data submitted in support of the request were found to be sufficient to derive MRL proposals for melons and watermelons. Adequate analytical methods for enforcement are available to control the residues of azoxystrobin on the commodities under consideration at the validated LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. Based on the risk assessment results, noting that an acute risk assessment was not deemed necessary for azoxystrobin, EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues resulting from the use of azoxystrobin according to the reported agricultural practices is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health. It is to be noted that the chronic consumer exposure estimated for residues in animal commodities is indicative due to data gaps related to the toxicity of livestock metabolites L1, L4, L9 and K1.Keywords: azoxystrobin, consumer risk assessment, melons, MRL, pesticide, watermelons

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, Syngenta Crop Protection AG submitted an application to the competent national authority in Austria to set import tolerances for the active substance azoxystrobin in melons and watermelons.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format was submitted through the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) Central Submission System on 17 April 2024. The Rapporteur Member State (RMS) Austria assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 24 June 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 11 July 2024 to 1 August 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 5 August 2024. The RMS proposed to establish maximum residue levels (MRLs) for melons and watermelons imported from Brazil at the level of 6 mg/kg, which is at the same level as proposed by the applicant.

The European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA on 29 August 2024 to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation. EFSA identified points which needed further clarification and requested the RMS to address them. On 22 October 2024, the applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the EMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 4 November 2024, replacing the previously submitted evaluation report.

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC, the data evaluated under previous MRL assessments and the additional data provided by the RMS in the framework of this application, the following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of azoxystrobin following foliar applications was investigated in crops belonging to the groups of fruit crops (grapes), cereals (wheat) and pulses/oilseeds (peanuts). Studies investigating the effect of processing on the nature of azoxystrobin (hydrolysis studies) demonstrated that azoxystrobin is stable.

As the uses of azoxystrobin under assessment are on imported commodities, investigations of residues in rotational crops are not required.

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in metabolism studies and on the results of the hydrolysis studies, the residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment in all plant commodities following foliar application were proposed as ‘azoxystrobin’ for primary crops and processed products. These residue definitions are also deemed appropriate for the post‐harvest uses.

EFSA concluded that for the fruit crops assessed in this application, metabolism of azoxystrobin in primary crops, and the possible degradation in processed products has been sufficiently addressed and that the previously derived residue definitions are applicable.

Sufficiently validated analytical methods based on high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry detection (HPLC–MS/MS) are available to quantify residues in melons and watermelons assessed in this application according to the enforcement residue definition. The methods enable quantification of residues at or above 0.01 mg/kg (limit of quantification, LOQ).

The available residue trials on melons are sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 6 mg/kg for melons and via extrapolation for watermelons in support of the authorised (foliar/post‐harvest) use of azoxystrobin in Brazil.

The peeling factors for melons, which can be used by extrapolation for watermelons, were derived from residue data representing the foliar use and three types of combine treatments (foliar + post harvest). The most critical peeling factor was derived from the foliar use alone and is recommended for the inclusion in Annex VI of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005:

–melons, pulp: < 0.33

melons, pulp: < 0.33

The toxicological profile of azoxystrobin was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review of approval of the active substance azoxystrobin under Directive 91/414/EEC and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.2 mg/kg bw per day, whereby an acute reference dose (ARfD) was deemed unnecessary.

The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). The estimated chronic exposures were compared with the ADI of azoxystrobin. The estimated long‐term dietary intake accounted for a maximum of 22% of the ADI (Dutch toddler diet). The contribution of residues expected in the melons and watermelons from the authorised uses individually do not exceed 0.01% of the ADI (IE adult and GEMS/Food G06 diet, respectively).

EFSA concluded that azoxystrobin residues in melons and watermelons from the authorised uses in Brazil will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference value and therefore are unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health. The chronic consumer risk assessment shall be regarded as indicative considering the data gap for general toxicity identified by EFSA for the metabolites L1, its conjugate K1, L4 and L9, which were identified in products of animal origin.

ASSESSMENT

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) received an application to set an import tolerance for the active substance azoxystrobin in melons and watermelons. The detailed description of authorised uses of azoxystrobin in Brazil on melons and watermelons, which are the basis for the current MRL application, is reported in Appendix A.

Azoxystrobin is the ISO common name for methyl (2E)‐2‐(2‐{[6‐(2‐cyanophenoxy)pyrimidin‐4‐yl]oxy}phenyl)‐3‐methoxyprop‐2‐enoate (IUPAC). The chemical structures of the active substance and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix E.

Azoxystrobin was evaluated in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC 1 with the United Kingdom designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS) for the representative uses as a foliar treatment on cereals and brassica vegetables. The draft assessment report (DAR) prepared by the RMS has been peer reviewed by EFSA (2010). Azoxystrobin was approved 2 on 1 January 2012 for the uses as fungicide only. The renewal of the approval process has not been initiated yet.

The EU MRLs for azoxystrobin are established in Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 3 The review of existing MRLs according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (MRL review) has been performed (EFSA, 2013) and the proposed modifications have been implemented in the MRL legislation. After completion of the MRL review, EFSA has issued several reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for azoxystrobin, including the evaluation of the MRL review Confirmatory data (EFSA, 2016a, 2016b, 2020, 2021a, 2021b, 2022, 2023a). The proposals from these reasoned opinions have been considered in recent MRL regulations. Also, certain Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) have been assessed (EFSA, 2018b, 2021c, 2023b, 2024) and taken over in the EU MRL legislation. 4

In accordance Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 5 the applicant Syngenta Crop Protection AG submitted on 17 April 2024 an application to the competent national authority in Austria, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format.

The appointed RMS Austria assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 24 June 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 11 July 2024 to 1 August 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 5 August 2024. The RMS proposed to establish maximum residue levels (MRLs) for melons and watermelons imported from Brazil at the level of 6 mg/kg. The tolerance for azoxystrobin in melons and watermelons in Brazil is established at 7 mg/kg for the residue definition ‘azoxystrobin’.

The European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA on 29 August 2024 to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation. EFSA identified points which needed further clarification and requested the EMS to address them. On 22 October 2024, the applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the EMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 4 November 2024 (Austria, 2024), which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Austria, 2024), the DAR and its addendum (United Kingdom, 2009a, 2009b) prepared under Council Directive 91/414/EEC, the Commission review report on azoxystrobin (European Commission, 2015), the conclusion on the peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance azoxystrobin (EFSA, 2010), the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (EFSA, 2013), the Article 12 confirmatory data assessment (EFSA, 2020), as well as the conclusions from previous EFSA opinions on azoxystrobin (EFSA, 2016a, 2016b, 2021a, 2021b, 2023a).

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 544/2011 6 and the guidance documents applicable at the date of submission of the IUCLID application are applicable (European Commission, 1997a, 1997b, 1997c, 1997d, 1997e, 1997f, 1997g, 2010, 2017, 2020, 2023; OECD, 2011). The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 7

A selected list of end points of the studies assessed by EFSA in the framework of this MRL application including the end points of relevant studies assessed previously, is presented in Appendix B.

The evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Austria, 2024) and the exposure calculations using the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) are considered as supporting documents to this reasoned opinion and, thus, are made publicly available as background documents to this reasoned opinion. 8

It is noted that as regards the following sections: analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant and animal commodities, storage stability of residues in plants and magnitude of residues in processed commodities, the applicant submitted additional data for azoxystrobin and its Z‐isomer, however, when the investigated matrices were found to be not relevant for the crops under consideration, those studies were not further assessed. Furthermore, it was considered that Z‐isomer is not included in the currently in place residue definitions (RDs) for monitoring or risk assessment. The following studies might be further considered in the framework of the renewal of the approval.

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of azoxystrobin following foliar applications close to harvest was investigated in primary crops belonging to the groups of fruit crops (grapes), cereals (wheat) and pulses/oilseeds (peanuts) in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2010).

The metabolism pattern was similar in all plant groups investigated with the parent azoxystrobin being the major compound, accounting for 17%–43% total radioactive residue (TRR) in cereal grain and straw, 35%–65% TRR in grapes and 14%–48% TRR in peanut hulls and hay.

The uses under assessment in the present application refer to combined foliar and subsequent post‐harvest treatment of melons and watermelons. It is to be noted that no studies investigating the metabolism of azoxystrobin following post‐harvest treatment are available.

Considering that in the available metabolism studies azoxystrobin was applied 10–20 days before harvest with mature commodity present, and the MRL review (EFSA, 2013) considered that the results of the available studies cover also the post‐harvest GAPs.

It is therefore concluded that for the authorised uses under consideration, the metabolic behaviour in primary crops is sufficiently addressed and further data are not required.

Nature of residues in rotational crops

Investigations of residues in rotational crops are not required for imported crops.

Nature of residues in processed commodities

The effect of processing on the nature of azoxystrobin was investigated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2010). These studies showed that azoxystrobin is hydrolytically stable under standard processing conditions.

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

Analytical methods for the determination of azoxystrobin residues in plant commodities were assessed during the EU pesticides peer review, MRL review and in the recently evaluated MRL applications.

The high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS) methods coded RAM 305/03 and DFG S19 are sufficiently validated for the determination of the residues of azoxystrobin in high water, high acid, high‐oil content, dry matrices. In addition, RAM 305/03 method is also sufficiently validated in hops (EFSA, 2013). The methods allow quantification of residues at or above the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg (EFSA, 2010, 2013, 2021b).

A validated multiresidue QuEChERS method in combination with HPLC‐MS/MS and gas chromatography with mass spectrometry (GC–MS), is also available to analyse parent azoxystrobin in all plant matrices and allows quantification of residues at or above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (EFSA, 2013).

In the framework of this application, further validation data for the RAM 305/03 method were submitted. The method was sufficiently validated for the determination of the residues of azoxystrobin and its Z‐isomer (R230310) in high water (lettuces, carrot roots and carrot tops), high oil (oilseed rape seeds) and dry (dried bean seeds) matrices and allows quantification of residues at or above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for azoxystrobin and Z‐isomer separately (Austria, 2024).

In addition, in the framework of this application, validation data were submitted for a new HPLC–MS/MS (QuEChERS) method which was proposed by the RMS to replace the currently in‐use enforcement methods for monitoring of azoxystrobin residues in plant matrices. The QuEChERS method was separately validated for the determination of the residues of azoxystrobin and its Z‐isomer (R230310) in various crop matrices with high‐water content (tomatoes, melons, potatoes), high‐oil content (oilseed rape seeds), high‐acid content (grapes) and in dry matrices (barley grain, barley straw) and, in addition, for determination of azoxystrobin residues in wheat grain with an individual LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. This method was sufficiently independently validated in high‐water content matrices (tomatoes) and high‐oil content matrices (oilseed rape seeds) for azoxystrobin only (Austria, 2024).

Overall, the QuEChERS method is found to be sufficiently validated for the enforcement of azoxystrobin residues in plant matrices with high water, high oil and high‐acid content and in dry matrices. It is to be noted that for melon pulp and peel three instead of five specimens were analysed per each fortification level. This reduced validation is considered as an acceptable deviation, as all the other method validation criteria are fully in line with the requirements of SANTE/2020/12830, Rev.2 Guidance (European Commission, 2023).

Melons and watermelons belong to the high‐water content commodity group. Therefore, EFSA concluded that sufficiently validated analytical methods are available for enforcing the proposed MRLs for azoxystrobin in melons and watermelons.

A study on the extraction efficiency of the analytical methods used for enforcement has been submitted in the context of a previous MRL application assessed by EFSA (EFSA, 2021b). The solvent systems, of RAM 305/03 and QuEChERS methods were cross‐validated against the solvent system used in the metabolism studies. Extraction efficiency when using the solvents of all methods ranged between 90% and 103% for all plant matrices and hops (EFSA, 2021b). Nevertheless, due to the missing information from the primary crop metabolism studies for the fruit crop commodity metabolism group and, since the specific percentage of the extractable TRR of parent azoxystrobin by the solvents used in all analytical methods was not reported, it was concluded that extraction efficiency was partially demonstrated (EFSA, 2021b). The same is concluded for the new QuEChERS method where the same extraction procedure and the solvent system are used (i.e. 50/50 v/v water/acetonitrile).

Based on the available data it can be concluded that extraction efficiency of the enforcement methods for melons and watermelons is partially demonstrated. EFSA therefore recommends reconsidering this further in the framework of the peer review for the renewal of approval of the active substance.

Storage stability of residues in plants

The storage stability of azoxystrobin in plants stored under frozen conditions was investigated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review of approval of the active substance azoxystrobin and in the MRL review (EFSA, 2010, 2013).

The storage stability of azoxystrobin was demonstrated for a period of 24 months at −18°C in the high‐water content commodities (bananas, peaches, tomatoes, cucumbers, lettuces, carrots) and in the high‐oil content commodities (oilseed rape seeds, pecans, peanuts) (EFSA, 2010, 2013).

In addition, new study investigating the stability of residues in frozen samples of crops classified as matrices with high‐water content (tomatoes), high‐acid content (grapes), high‐oil content (oilseed rape seeds), dry matrices (wheat grain) and others (barley straw) was submitted with the current application (Austria, 2024). Samples were fortified with 0.1 mg azoxystrobin/kg and stored frozen at −18°C for 24 months. The samples were analysed at zero time and also at the intervals of 6, 11, 18 months. It was demonstrated that in crops assessed in the framework of this application, residues were stable for at least 24 months when stored at −18°C (European Commission, 1997f).

Proposed residue definitions

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in metabolism studies, the results of hydrolysis studies, the toxicological relevance of the active substance and the capabilities of enforcement analytical methods, the residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment in all plant commodities following foliar applications was proposed as ‘azoxystrobin’ (EFSA, 2010, 2013).

The residue definition for enforcement set in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 is identical with the above‐mentioned residue definition. The residue definition is also applicable to processed commodities and is considered to cover post‐harvest uses on the fruit crops under consideration (EFSA, 2013).

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

Authorised Brazilian Good Agricultural Practices (GAPs) on melons and watermelons are summarised in Appendix A, which include one foliar GAP and three types of indoor post‐harvest GAPs:

– outdoor foliar application 5 × 64 g a.s./ha, interval 7 days, PHI 2 days

outdoor foliar application 5 × 64 g a.s./ha, interval 7 days, PHI 2 days

– indoor post‐harvest application (either dipping for 2 min, application to the stalk or spray application) 1 × 119.5 g a.s./hL, PHI 0 days

indoor post‐harvest application (either dipping for 2 min, application to the stalk or spray application) 1 × 119.5 g a.s./hL, PHI 0 days

In addition, foliar application can be combined with any type of the post‐harvest treatments referred to above.

In support of the import tolerance application, the applicant submitted eight independent residue trials on melons conducted in Brazil during the growing season of 2022. These trials cover the following types of application regimes: foliar applications and foliar applications followed by one of the three types of post‐harvest treatment.

Each trial consisted of one control plot and four treated plots. All treated plots received six foliar applications of azoxystrobin at the rate of 80 g a.s./ha, separated by intervals of 7 days. The whole fruit samples were collected at the PHI of 3 days. It is noted that the trials were not fully compliant with the authorised GAP, in terms of the number of applications (six instead of five), application rate (80 g/ha instead of 64 g/ha) and PHI (3 days instead of 2 days).

Part of fruit samples were separated into peel and pulp and stored for analysis (to obtain data on the magnitude of residues in the crop following foliar treatment) and part of the fruits were further subject to three different post‐harvest treatments with a suspension concentrate (SC) formulation nominally containing 239 g azoxystrobin/L of formulated product:

–dipping for 2 min or

dipping for 2 min or

–spraying or

spraying or

–stalk application.

stalk application.

The single dip or the single spray or stalk post‐harvest application was compliant with the maximum nominal concentration of 119.5 g/hL of the reported GAPs. Melon fruits were collected on the day of the treatment and 7 and 15 days after the treatment to determine whether residue decline occurs. The whole fruit samples were separated into peel and pulp and stored for the analysis (Austria, 2024).

The RMS concluded that the submitted trials are found to be acceptable in support of the authorised uses of azoxystrobin in Brazil on melons and watermelons. Since the impact of the initial foliar applications on the final residues was evaluated as insignificant, the deviations in the trial parameters as noted above for the foliar use, were considered acceptable. Moreover, the RMS concluded that residues in treated fruits resulting from three different post‐harvest treatments were statistically comparable (Kruskal‐Wallis Test) and combined the residue data to derive an MRL proposal of 6 mg/kg in melons and the STMR of 0.01 mg/kg (pulp).

EFSA agrees with the position of the RMS that treatment pattern applied in residue trials supporting the authorised foliar GAP can be accepted, noting that residues resulting from the foliar treatments alone are very low in the whole fruit (in the range of < 0.01–0.04 mg/kg) as compared to residues found after the post‐harvest treatments (ranging between 0.03 and 2.86 mg/kg). However, EFSA disagrees with the position of the RMS to combine the residue data resulting from the different post‐harvest uses. Therefore, EFSA evaluated each post‐harvest use separately and identified the following critical GAP: outdoor foliar application combined with one post‐harvest spray application. Nevertheless, this different selection does not affect the MRL or STMR proposals derived by the RMS.

The residue data from the supervised residue trials in primary crops are summarised in Appendix B.1.2.1.

An extrapolation of residue data from melons to watermelons is proposed by the applicant. The proposed extrapolation is acceptable according to the EU technical guidelines (European Commission, 2020). EFSA concludes that an MRL proposal of 6 mg/kg can be derived for the authorised foliar and post‐harvest uses of azoxystrobin on melons based on the provided trials, and by extrapolation on watermelons. The tolerance established for azoxystrobin in Brazil 9 on melons and watermelons based on the post‐harvest uses on citrus fruits is 7 mg/kg.

The samples of the described residue trials were stored under conditions for which the integrity of the samples is demonstrated (Austria, 2024). The samples of peel and pulp were analysed for the parent compound according to the residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment using the analytical method RAM 305/03, which is sufficiently validated and fit for purpose (Austria, 2024). No residues of azoxystrobin at or above the LOQ of the analytical method were found in any of the untreated specimens (Austria, 2024).

The extraction efficacy of the HPLC–MS/MS method used for the residue trials has been previously assessed by EFSA and no new information has been submitted within this application (see also Section 1.1.4). It was concluded that the extraction efficiency of the solvent system used in RAM 305/03 method (i.e. acetonitrile/water 50/50 v/v) is only partially demonstrated for the commodities belonging to the metabolism group of ‘fruits and fruiting vegetables’ and of the high‐water content commodities group (as per the classification in SANTE/2020/12830, Rev.2 Guidance; European Commission, 2023). EFSA therefore recommends reconsidering this further in the framework of the peer review for the renewal of approval of the active substance.

Magnitude of residues in rotational crops

As the uses of azoxystrobin under assessment are on imported commodities, investigations of residues in rotational crops are not required.

Magnitude of residues in processed commodities

In principle, specific processing studies investigating the effect of industrial processing on the magnitude of residues should be provided since the level of residues in the raw agricultural commodity (RAC) (i.e. whole melon fruit) are above 0.1 mg/kg (European Commission, 1997d). However, considering that the contribution of residues in melons and watermelons to the overall dietary exposure is individually below 10% of the ADI for any European consumer diet group, since residues in melon pulp are low (HR of 0.03 m/kg) and since these commodities are mostly consumed unprocessed, EFSA concluded that processing studies are not necessary for the crops under assessment.

Based on the submitted residue trials on melons, the separation of fruits into peel and pulp allowed derivation of multiple peeling factors (see Table B.1.2.3). For the combined treatment (foliar + post‐harvest), residue data in pulp were provided for various withholding periods.

Results indicate that major part of residues is removed by peeling. In all pulp samples after foliar spray application residues were below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. After additional stalk and spray post‐harvest treatments for almost all withholding periods investigated residues were measured below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg and in the remaining cases at 0.01 mg/kg. Residues in pulp were > LOQ in samples that have been treated post‐harvest via dipping. From this combined use residues in pulp were in the range of < 0.01–0.03 mg/kg. The most critical peeling factor (PF of < 0.33 derived from foliar use alone) and considering all available data is recommended for inclusion in Annex VI of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

Proposed MRLs

The available data are considered sufficient to derive MRL proposals as well as risk assessment values for melons and watermelons imported from Brazil (see Appendix B.1.2.1).

In Section 3, EFSA assessed whether azoxystrobin residues in melons and watermelons resulting from the uses authorised in Brazil are likely to pose a consumer health risk.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

Not relevant as melons and watermelons are not used for feed purposes.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA performed a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018a, 2019). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different sub‐groups of the EU population and allows the acute and chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

The applicable toxicological reference value used in the risk assessment –ADI of 0.2 mg/kg bw per day ‐ for azoxystrobin was derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review of approval of this active substance. The ARfD has been considered unnecessary for azoxystrobin (European Commission, 2015), thus short‐term (acute) risk assessment is not required.

The previous long‐term (chronic) consumer risk assessment performed in the context of an MRL application on hops (EFSA, 2023a) is now revised by considering the STMR values for azoxystrobin in melons and watermelons as derived from the residue trials submitted in support of this MRL application. Additionally, for various crops the Codex MRLs have been taken over in the EU MRL legislation therefore for these commodities the STMR values derived by the JMPR were also included in the exposure calculations (FAO, 2014, 2017, 2019, 2023; EFSA, 2018b, 2021c, 2023b, 2024). The input values used to perform the consumer risk assessment are reported in Appendix D.1.

The estimated chronic exposures were compared with the ADI of azoxystrobin. The estimated long‐term dietary intake accounted for a maximum of 22% of the ADI (Dutch toddler diet). The contribution of residues in melons and watermelons to the overall long‐term exposure is individually below 0.01% of the ADI (IE adult and GEMS/Food G06 diet, respectively). More details of the contribution of the residues is included in Appendix B.4.

EFSA concluded that the consumer intake of residues of azoxystrobin resulting from the existing uses in EU and the authorised uses on melons and watermelons in Brazil is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

It is to be noted that the chronic exposure estimated for residues in animal commodities is indicative due to data gaps identified in the MRL review and confirmed in the MRL review of the confirmatory data assessment related to the toxicity of livestock metabolites L1, L4, L9 and K1 (conjugate of L1) (EFSA, 2013, 2020).

For further details on the exposure calculations, a screenshot of the report sheet of the PRIMo is presented in Appendix C.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The data submitted in support of this MRL application were found to be sufficient to derive the MRL proposals for melons and watermelons imported from Brazil.

EFSA concluded that the notified uses (import tolerances) of azoxystrobin on melons and watermelons will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference value and therefore are unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health. The chronic consumer risk assessment shall be regarded as indicative considering the data gap for general toxicity identified by EFSA for the metabolites L1, its conjugate K1, L4 and L9, which were identified in products of animal origin.

The MRL recommendations are summarised in Appendix B.5.

From Bit to Block: Decoding on Erasure Channels
Henry D. Pfister ∗, Oscar Sprumont †, Gilles Zémor ‡

Abstract
. We provide a general framework for bounding the block error threshold of a linear code C ⊆ 𝔽 2 N C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N} over the erasure channel in terms of its bit error threshold. Our approach relies on understanding the minimum support weight of any r r -dimensional subcode of C C , for all small values of r r . As a proof of concept, we use our machinery to obtain a new proof of the celebrated result that Reed–Muller codes achieve capacity on the erasure channel with respect to block error probability.



1.
Introduction

We will be interested in the performance of linear codes over noisy communication channels. Ever since Shannon’s seminal paper
[
Sha48
]
, information theorists have spent considerable time and effort designing codes that
achieve capacity
, i.e. codes
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
that approach the optimal tradeoff between their rate
R:=\frac{\log_{2}|C|}{N}
and the amount of noise they can tolerate.

Shannon’s probabilistic argument
[
Sha48
]
established that uniform
random codes achieve capacity on both the binary symmetric channel and the erasure channel. The first explicit families of codes to provably achieve capacity were only obtained decades later, when Forney introduced concatenated codes
[
For66
]
. More recently, Arikan showed that polar codes - which have both a deterministic construction and efficient encoding and decoding algorithms -
achieve capacity on all memoryless channels
[
Ari09
]
. This brought renewed attention to the closely related Reed–Muller codes, which were shown to achieve capacity on the binary symmetric channel and the erasure channel in
[
ASW15
,
KKM+17
,
KKM+16
,
RP24
,
AS23b
,
AS23a
]
.

The proof that Reed–Muller codes of constant rate achieve capacity on the erasure channel
[
KKM+17
,
KKM+16
]
relies heavily on the double transitivity of their automorphism group. In fact, Kudekar, Kumar, Mondelli, Pfister, Şaşoğlu and Urbanke showed in
[
KKM+17
]
that
any
doubly transitive linear code achieves capacity under bit-MAP decoding. A very natural question is then to understand whether or not the bit and block error thresholds of doubly transitive codes are asymptotically equal. (By the result of
[
KKM+17
]
, this is equivalent to asking whether or not every doubly transitive code achieves capacity on the erasure channel under block-MAP decoding.)

1.1.
Main Results

Our main contribution is to develop a framework for bounding the gap between a code’s bit and block error thresholds on the erasure channel. Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
be any linear code. We show that if, for all integers
r<r_{0}
(where
r_{0}
depends on the sharpness of the bit threshold of
C
), any
r
-dimensional subcode of
C
has support
1
1

1

is the set of indices
j\in[N]
where at least one codeword
c\in S
has
c_{j}=1
.
growing faster than
r\log N
, then the block error threshold of
C
is close to its bit error threshold. An informal version of our main result is stated below. For the formal version, see Theorem
7
.

Theorem 1

(Informal)
.

Consider any linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
and suppose that a uniform random codeword
c\in C
is sent over the erasure channel with erasure probability
p\in[0,1]
. Let

be such that, for every
i\in[N]

i^{\textnormal{th}}

is bounded by

\displaystyle\Pr_{p\textnormal{-erasures}}\Big[\textnormal{cannot recover }c_{i}\Big]\leq\delta.

Suppose additionally that for all
r=1,2,\dotsc,\sqrt{\delta}N
, the support of any
r

Then there exists

such that the probability we fail to decode the codeword

is bounded by

\displaystyle\Pr_{p^{\prime}\textnormal{-erasures}}\Big[\textnormal{cannot recover }c\Big]\leq\sqrt{\delta}+o(1).

As a proof of concept, we show in Section

. Our analysis relies on the work of Wei, who obtained in
[
Wei91
]
exact expressions for the minimum support weight of any
r

1
gives an alternative proof that Reed–Muller codes achieve capacity on the erasure channel under block-MAP decoding, a fact that was shown by Kudekar, Kumar, Mondelli, Pfister, Şaşoğlu and Urbanke in
[
KKM+17
,
KKM+16
]
. See Theorem

We note that one key argument in the proof of
[
KKM+16
]
is the claim that the low-weight codewords of Reed-Muller codes do not contribute much to the decoding error probability, and thus one only needs to worry about codewords of high weight. In order to prove this claim,
[
KKM+16
]
relies on a very careful analysis of Reed–Muller codes’ weight enumerator, which may be difficult to generalize to other codes. In contrast, our approach only requires somewhat loose bounds on the minimum support weight of any subcode. For
r<N^{1-o(1)}
, we need any
r
-dimensional subcode to have support weight larger than
r\log N

r2^{(\log N)^{0.99}}
(see Corollary
10

1.2.
Techniques

Our proof of Theorem
1
is based on a well-known work of Tillich and Zémor
[
TZ00
]
, who established sharp threshold results for the symmetric and erasure channels. For a linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
and a string
x\in\{0,1\}^{N}
, we define

(1)

\displaystyle S_{C}(x):=\Big\{c\in C:c_{i}\leq x_{i}\textnormal{ for all }i\in[N]\Big\}

to be the set of codewords that are indistinguishable from the
0
-vector once you erase all coordinates
i\in[N]
where
x_{i}=1
. Note that
S_{C}(x)
is a subcode of
C
. Tillich and Zémor showed in
[
TZ00
]
that for any fixed
r\in\{1,2,\dotsc,\textnormal{dim }C\}
, the function

(2)

\displaystyle f_{r}(p):=\Pr_{x\sim p}\Big[\textnormal{dim }S_{C}(x)\geq r\Big]

transitions rapidly from
\approx 0
to
\approx 1
as a function of
p
, where
x\sim p
denotes a
p
-noisy random string
x\in\{0,1\}^{N}.
They also showed that for any
r
, the curves
f_{r}(p)
and
f_{r+1}(p)
stay within a distance of about
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{r}(C)}}
from each other, where
d_{r}(C)
denotes the minimum support weight of any
r
-dimensional subcode of
C
.

In this work, we first strengthen their result to show that the curves
f_{r}(p)
and
f_{r+1}(p)
in fact stay within a distance of about
\frac{1}{d_{r}(C)}
from each other (see the proof of Theorem
6
). We then leverage the fact that every doubly transitive code achieves capacity under bit-MAP decoding to prove that for some
r_{0}=N^{1-o(1)}
, the function
f_{r_{0}}
corresponding to any doubly transitive code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
satisfies

\displaystyle f_{r_{0}}\Big(1-\textnormal{rate}(C)-o(1)\Big)=o(1).

Since the distance between the curves
f_{r}(p)
and
f_{r+1}(p)
can be bounded in terms of the minimum support weight
d_{r}(C)
, we are then able to prove (see Theorem
8
) that the function
f_{1}(p)
corresponding to any doubly transitive code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
with large enough minimum support weights satisfies

(3)

\displaystyle f_{1}\Big(1-\textnormal{rate}(C)-o(1)\Big)=o(1).

But the left hand side above is exactly the probability that a sent codeword
c\in C
can be uniquely recovered from random erasures of probability
1-\textnormal{rate}(C)-o(1)
, so equation (
3
) shows that any doubly transitive linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
with large enough minimum support weights
\{d_{r}(C)\}
achieves capacity on the erasure channel under block-MAP decoding. See Theorem
7
for the formal statement.

Notation and Conventions

We will be interested in the behavior of linear codes

\displaystyle x_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{with probability $p$},\\

\displaystyle y_{i}=\begin{cases}c_{i}&\text{if $x_{i}=0$},\\

A notion that will be important in our analysis is the notion of

\displaystyle x\succ c.

We note that if the erasure pattern

and the codeword
c+c^{\prime}.
We denote the subcode of
C
covered by an erasure pattern

\displaystyle S_{C}(x):=\Big\{c\in C:x\succ c\Big\}.

A sent codeword
c\in C
can be recovered uniquely from an erasure pattern

if and only if

. Similarly, for any coordinate
i\in[N]
, the bit

(5)

\displaystyle g_{r}(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\textnormal{dim }S_{C}(x)\geq r$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For any fixed linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}

\displaystyle f_{r}(p):=\mathop{\mathbb{}}{E}_{x\sim p}\big[g_{r}(x)\big],

\displaystyle\theta_{r}(\alpha):=f_{r}^{-1}(\alpha).

The main quantity that will allow us to control the behavior of these two functions is the minimum support size of any
r

(6)

\displaystyle d_{r}(C):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq C\\

One explicit family of codes we will be interested in is the family of Reed–Muller codes. We denote by

3.
General Linear Codes

In this section, we will provide general conditions under which the bit and block error thresholds of an arbitrary linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
are close to one another. We start by bounding the distance between the curves
\theta_{1}(\alpha)
and
\theta_{N_{0}}(\alpha)
, for any
N_{0}>1.

Theorem 6
.

Fix any linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
and any integer
N_{0}\leq\textnormal{dim }C
. Then, letting
\gamma
{}_{N_{0}}
:=\sqrt{\sum_{r=1}^{N_{0}-1}\frac{1}{d_{r}(C)}}
, we have that for any

0\leq\alpha\leq 1-\textnormal{\kern-1.72218pt\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\gamma$}\kern-1.72218pt${}_{N_{0}}$},

the functions
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{N_{0}}
associated with

satisfy

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\big(\alpha+\textnormal{\kern-1.72218pt\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\gamma$}\kern-1.72218pt${}_{N_{0}}$}\big)\geq\theta_{N_{0}}(\alpha)-\textnormal{\kern-1.72218pt\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\gamma$}\kern-1.72218pt${}_{N_{0}}$}.

Proof.

Consider any
r\leq\textnormal{dim }C.

By Lemmas
4
and
5

f_{r}
associated with our code

satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dp}f_{r}(p)

\displaystyle\geq\Pr_{x\sim p}\Big[h_{g_{r}}(x)\neq 0\Big]\cdot d_{r}(C)

(9)

\displaystyle=\Big(f_{r}(p)-f_{r+1}(p)\Big)d_{r}(C).

Since each

\theta_{r}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\Pr_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\Big[\theta_{N_{0}}(\alpha)-\theta_{1}(\alpha)>\textnormal{\kern-1.72218pt\raisebox{1.72218pt}{$\gamma$}\kern-1.72218pt${}_{N_{0}}$}\Big]

We are now ready to prove our main result. The following theorem is a formal version of Theorem

.

Theorem 7
.

Consider any linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
with
N\geq 10
. Let
p,\delta\in[0,1]
be such that
\textnormal{dim }C\geq\sqrt{\delta}N
and

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p}\Big[i\in\textnormal{supp}\big(S_{C}(x)\big)\Big]\leq\delta

for every
i\in[N]
. Define

\displaystyle\Delta:=\min_{r=1,2,\dotsc,\sqrt{\delta}N}\Big\{\frac{d_{r}(C)}{r}\Big\}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p-\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}}\Big[\exists c\in C:x\succ c\Big]\leq\sqrt{\delta}+\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}.

Proof.

Note that we may assume that

(11)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\delta}+\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}\leq 1,

as otherwise the claim is trivial.
By linearity of expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{}}{E}_{x\sim p}\Big[\textnormal{supp}\big(S_{C}(x)\big)\Big]\leq\delta N.

Since the dimension of a subspace can at most be as large as its support, Markov’s inequality then gives us

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p}\Big[\textnormal{dim}\big(S_{C}(x)\big)\geq\sqrt{\delta}N\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathop{\mathop{\mathbb{}}{E}}_{x\sim p}\Big[\textnormal{supp}(S_{C}(x))\Big]}{\sqrt{\delta}N}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\delta},

or equivalently,

(12)

\displaystyle\theta_{\sqrt{\delta}N}(\sqrt{\delta})\geq p.

On the other hand, by definition of
\Delta
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\sqrt{\delta}N}\frac{1}{d_{r}(C)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Delta}\sum_{r=1}^{\sqrt{\delta}N}\frac{1}{r}

(13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log N}{\Delta},

where in the last line we used the facts that
N\geq 10
and that
\sum_{r=1}^{N^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r}\leq\ln(N^{\prime})+1
for all
N^{\prime}\geq 1
. By inequalities (
11
) and (
13
), the conditions of Theorem
6
are satisfied for
N_{0}=\sqrt{\delta}N
and
\alpha=\sqrt{\delta}
. Applying Theorem
6
, we then have

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\left(\sqrt{\delta}+\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\theta_{1}\left(\sqrt{\delta}+\sqrt{\sum_{r=1}^{\sqrt{\delta}N}\frac{1}{d_{r}(C)}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\theta_{\sqrt{\delta}N}(\sqrt{\delta})-\sqrt{\sum_{r=1}^{\sqrt{\delta}N}\frac{1}{d_{r}(C)}}

\displaystyle\geq\theta_{\sqrt{\delta}N}(\sqrt{\delta})-\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}},

where in the first and third lines we used the inequality (

). By equation (

), we get

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\left(\sqrt{\delta}+\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq p-\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}.

4.
Doubly Transitive Codes

In this section, we apply our Theorem

Theorem 8
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
be any doubly transitive linear code with
N\geq 10
. Define
p^{*}\in[0,1]
to be the noise parameter at which
\Pr_{x\sim p^{*}}\big[1\in S_{C}(x)\big]=\frac{1}{2}

\textnormal{dim }C\geq Ne^{-\frac{p^{*}-p}{2}\log(N-1)}
. Then defining

\displaystyle\Delta:=\min_{r=1,2,\dotsc,Ne^{-\frac{p^{*}-p}{2}\log(N-1)}}\Big\{\frac{d_{r}(C)}{r}\Big\},

we have

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p-\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}}\Big[\exists c\in C:x\succ c\Big]\leq\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}+e^{-\frac{p^{*}-p}{2}\log(N-1)}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2
, we have that for all
i\in[N]
,

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p}\Big[i\in\textnormal{supp}\big(S_{C}(x)\big)\Big]\leq e^{-(p^{*}-p)\log(N-1)}.

Applying Theorem
7
with
\delta=e^{-(p^{*}-p)\log(N-1)}
, we then get

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p-\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}}\Big[\exists c\in C:x\succ c\Big]\leq\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\Delta}}+e^{-\frac{p^{*}-p}{2}\log(N-1)}.

∎

5.
Reed–Muller Codes

We will now use Theorem
8
to show that Reed–Muller codes achieve capacity on the erasure channel. For any
t\leq d\leq n
, consider the subcode
S_{t}\subseteq\mathsf{RM}(n,d)
defined in
Theorem
3
. Recall that Wei showed in
[
Wei91
]
that
S_{t}
is a subcode of minimal support for its dimension (see Theorem
3
).
We bound its dimension and support explicitly below.

Lemma 9
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
d\leq\frac{n}{2}+\sqrt{n\log n}
and every
t\in[5\sqrt{n\log n},d]
, the corresponding subcode
S_{t}\subseteq\mathsf{RM}(n,d)
satisfies

\displaystyle\big|\textnormal{supp}(S_{t})\big|\geq 2^{n-t},

\displaystyle\textnormal{dim}(S_{t})\leq 2^{n-t}\cdot 2^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{t^{2}}{n}-\frac{t^{3}}{n^{2}}\right)}.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the fact that the evaluation vector of the monomial
\prod_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}
is in
S_{t}
, and the fact that
\prod_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}
evaluates to
1
on all points
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{t}=1.
For the second statement, we compute

\displaystyle\textnormal{dim}(S_{t})

\displaystyle=\binom{n-t}{\leq d-t}

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n-t}{\leq\frac{n}{2}-\frac{4t}{5}},

where in the second line we used the fact that
d\leq\frac{n}{2}+\sqrt{n\log n}\leq\frac{n}{2}+\frac{t}{5}
. Since
\binom{m}{\leq s}\leq 2^{h(\frac{s}{m})m}
for all
s\leq\frac{m}{2}
(see for example
[
Gal14
]
, Theorem 3.1) we then get

\displaystyle\textnormal{dim}(S_{t})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{h\big(\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4t}{5n}}{1-\frac{t}{n}}\big)(n-t)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{h\big(\frac{1-\frac{3t}{5n}}{2}\big)(n-t)},

where in the second line we used the Taylor expansion
\frac{1}{1-x}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x^{j}
to bound
\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4x}{5}}{1-x}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3x}{10(1-x)}\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3x}{10}
for all
x\in[0,1)
. Applying the inequality
h(\frac{1-x}{2})\leq 1-\frac{x^{2}}{2\ln 2}
, we then get

\displaystyle\textnormal{dim}(S_{t})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(1-\frac{t^{2}}{4n^{2}})(n-t)},

as desired.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
9
, we get the following bound on the minimum size of the support of any
r
-dimensional subcode of a Reed–Muller code.

Corollary 10
.

For every
n
large enough, every
d\leq\frac{n}{2}+\sqrt{n\log n}
and every
\epsilon\in\left[6\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}},\frac{1}{2}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d_{r}(\mathsf{RM}(n,d))}{r}\geq 2^{\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10}}

for all
r\leq 2^{n-\epsilon n}
.

Proof.

We first note that since the minimum distance of
\mathsf{RM}(n,d)
is
2^{n-d}
, it will suffice to prove our claim for every
r\in[2^{n-d-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10}},2^{n-\epsilon n}]
. Consider any such
r
, and let the integer
t\in[\epsilon n-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10},d-1]
be such that

(14)

\displaystyle 2^{n-t-1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10}}\leq r\leq 2^{n-t-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10}}.

Note that by Lemma
9
and our theorem’s condition on
\epsilon
, for any
t\in[\epsilon n-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10},d-1]
we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{dim}(S_{t})\leq 2^{n-t}\cdot 2^{-\frac{n}{4}\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}\left(1-\frac{t}{n}\right)}.

But the function
x^{2}(1-x)
is increasing over
[0,\frac{2}{3}]
. Since
t\leq\frac{n}{2}+\sqrt{n\log n}\leq\frac{2n}{3}
for all
n
large enough, we then get (because
\frac{t}{n}\geq\epsilon-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{10}
)

\displaystyle\textnormal{dim}(S_{t})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n-t}\cdot 2^{-\frac{n}{4}\left(\epsilon-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{10}\right)^{2}\left(1-\epsilon+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{10}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n-t}\cdot 2^{-\frac{n}{4}\left(\frac{19\epsilon}{20}\right)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n-t}\cdot 2^{-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10}-1},

where in the second line we used the fact that
\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Combining this with the leftmost inequality of (
14
), we get
r\geq 2^{n-t-1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10}}\geq\textnormal{dim}(S_{t})
. By Theorem
3
and Lemma
9
, we must then have

\displaystyle d_{r}\big(\mathsf{RM}(n,d)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\big|\textnormal{supp}(S_{t})\big|

\displaystyle\geq 2^{n-t}.

Combining this inequality with the right-hand side of (
14
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d_{r}\big(\mathsf{RM}(n,d)\big)}{r}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{10}}.

∎

We are now ready to prove that the bit and block error thresholds of Reed–Muller codes are asymptotically equal. Since every doubly transitive code achieves capacity under bit-MAP decoding
[
KKM+17
]
, this implies that Reed–Muller codes achieve capacity under block-MAP decoding.

Theorem 11
.

For every
n
large enough, every
d\in[\frac{n}{2}-\sqrt{n\log n},\frac{n}{2}+\sqrt{n\log n}]
and every
\epsilon\geq 20\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}
, the Reed–Muller code
\mathsf{RM}(n,d)
satisfies

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p^{*}-\epsilon}\Big[\exists c\in C:x\succ c\Big]\leq 2^{-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{100}},

where
p^{*}\in[0,1]
is such that
\Pr_{x\sim p^{*}}\big[1\in\textnormal{supp}(S_{C}(x))\big]=\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Note that we may assume that
\epsilon\leq 1
. Note also that

\displaystyle\textnormal{dim}\big(\mathsf{RM}(n,d)\big)

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{\leq d}

\displaystyle\geq\binom{n}{\leq\frac{n}{2}-\sqrt{n\log n}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\cdot 2^{h\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)n},

where in the third line we used the inequality
\binom{n}{k}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\cdot 2^{h(k/n)n}
(see for example
[
MS77
]
, page 309, Lemma 7). Applying the inequality
h(\frac{1-x}{2})\geq 1-x^{2}
, we then get

\displaystyle\textnormal{dim}\big(\mathsf{RM}(n,d)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}2^{(1-4\frac{\log n}{n})n}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{n}e^{-\frac{\epsilon(n-1)}{6}}.

By Theorem
8
, for every
p\leq p^{*}-\frac{\epsilon}{3}
, we then have

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p-\sqrt{\frac{n}{\Delta}}}\Big[\exists c\in C:x\succ c\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{\Delta}}+e^{-\frac{p^{*}-p}{2}\log(2^{n}-1)}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{\Delta}}+e^{-\frac{p^{*}-p}{2}(n-1)}

for

\displaystyle\Delta:=\min_{r=1,2,\dotsc,Ne^{-\frac{p^{*}-p}{2}(n-1)}}\Big\{\frac{d_{r}\big(\mathsf{RM}(n,d)\big)}{r}\Big\}.

Letting
p=p^{*}-\frac{2\epsilon}{3}\cdot\frac{n}{n-1}
and applying Corollary
10
with parameter
\frac{\epsilon}{3\ln 2}
(the conditions of Corollary
10
are satisfied by our theorem’s condition on
\epsilon
), we get

\displaystyle\Delta\geq 2^{\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{90(\ln 2)^{2}}}\geq n2^{\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{50}+2},

and thus equation (
15
) becomes

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p-2^{-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{100}}}\Big[\exists c\in C:x\succ c\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{100}-1}+e^{-\frac{\epsilon n}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{100}}.

But by definition
p=p^{*}-\frac{2\epsilon}{3}\cdot\frac{n}{n-1}
and by our theorem’s conditions
2^{-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{100}}<\frac{\epsilon}{4}
, so we get

\displaystyle\Pr_{x\sim p^{*}-\epsilon}\Big[\exists c\in C:x\succ c\Big]\leq 2^{-\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{100}}.

∎

Remark 1
.

The work of Tillich and Zémor gives very sharp block error decays in terms of the minimum distance of a linear code
[
TZ00
]
. Thus, the primary purpose here is to bound the distance in
p
between the block-error and bit-error thresholds. Once we have a bound such as the one given by our Theorem
11
, we immediately get a strong error decay by
[
TZ00
]
.

Remark 2
.

For Reed–Muller codes specifically, the ratios
\{\frac{d_{r}}{r}\}
are large enough that one does not need the full power of Theorem
2
’s bit-error decay,
\Pr_{x\sim p}[\textnormal{bit error}]\leq e^{-(p^{*}-p)n}
. To show that the bit-error and block-error thresholds of Reed–Muller codes are asymptotically equal, it would have been sufficient to use a bit error decay of
e^{-(p^{*}-p)a\sqrt{n\log n}}
for any
a=\omega(1)
. This is because by Corollary
10
, we have
\frac{d_{r}}{r}\geq n^{10}
for all
r\leq 2^{n-10\sqrt{n\log n}}
. Setting
p=p^{*}-\frac{20\ln 2}{a}
and applying Theorem
7

would then have given that the block-error probability under noise
p^{*}-\frac{20\ln 2}{a}-\frac{1}{n^{4}}
is bounded by
\frac{2}{n^{4}}.

Intrinsic AIE drug nanocrystals enable imaging-guided orchitis theranostics
Authors: Lingling Wang, Wenbo Tang, Zuping Xiong, Fengfei Zhao, Rendy Hosea, Yujun Zhang, Ben Zhong Tang, Fei Sun, Feiyi Sun, Xinghua Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAcute orchitis and other inflammatory testicular diseases lack delivery platforms that enable label-free fluorescence tracking of drug distribution in inflamed testes. Here, we show that three clinically approved leukotriene receptor antagonists, namely Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast can be transformed into self-reporting, self-delivering, and self-therapeutic nanotheranostics. These drugs intrinsically possess aggregation-induced emission (AIE): extensive intermolecular halogen and hydrogen bonds simultaneously restrict intramolecular motion to generate bright solid-state fluorescence and drive spontaneous self-assembly into uniform, PEG-stabilized nanocrystals. Unlike conventional dye-labeled nanocarriers, in which dye leakage causes false signals, AIE fluorescence faithfully reports the aggregated nanocrystal state, enabling monitoring of drug integrity. Beyond their canonical anti-leukotriene activity, these drugs directly suppress NF-κB signaling and scavenge reactive oxygen species—mechanisms highly relevant to acute orchitis pathogenesis, which involves TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB activation and oxidative stress. In LPS-stimulated cells, the nanocrystals suppress NF-κB activation and scavenge reactive oxygen species. In a rat model of acute orchitis, AIE imaging reveals efficient testicular accumulation, which correlates with attenuated histopathological damage, restored oxidative stress balance, and downregulation of the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB and PK2/PKR1 pathways, with no obvious adverse effects on the measured short-term reproductive indices (sperm morphology and testosterone levels) observed within the experimental timeframe. This strategy converts old drugs into minimal-excipient, self-monitoring AIE nanotheranostics for imaging-guided orchitis therapy.Keywords: Aggregation-induced emission, Drug nanocrystals, Leukotriene receptor antagonists, Aggregate science, Theranostics

Introduction

Acute orchitis is a urogenital inflammatory condition that causes severe testicular pain, swelling, and, if unresolved, can lead to impaired spermatogenesis and permanent male infertility [1,2]. Current clinical management relies heavily on empirical antibiotics and non-specific anti-inflammatory agents, lacking both molecular specificity and the ability to visualize drug distribution or therapeutic response in time [[3], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8], [9]]. An ideal theranostic platform would simultaneously treat inflammation and report its own delivery fate, yet conventional nanocarriers face intrinsic limitations: they typically require a combination of fluorescent dye, drug, carrier material, and surface modifications, leading to compositional complexity, batch-to-batch variability, low drug loading, and, critically, false positive signals caused by dye leakage [[10], [11], [12], [13], [14], [15]]. When the dye dissociates from the carrier, the fluorescence signal no longer reflects the presence of intact drug at the target site [16]. This fundamental disconnect between imaging signal and drug integrity underscores the urgent need for minimal-excipient systems that unify therapy, delivery, and self-reporting.

Aggregation-induced emission (AIE) offers a unique opportunity to address this challenge. AIE luminogens (AIEgens) are weakly emissive in molecularly dissolved states but become highly fluorescent upon aggregation, enabling high-sensitivity bioimaging and image-guided therapy [[17], [18], [19], [20], [21]]. While tremendous synthetic efforts have generated vast libraries of AIEgens, their clinical translation is hampered by tedious synthesis, poorly characterized long-term toxicity, and formidable regulatory barriers associated with entirely new chemical entities [[22], [23], [24], [25], [26], [27]]. An alternative, largely untapped strategy is to discover intrinsic AIE properties in clinically approved small-molecule drugs [28]. Critically, when such drugs self-assemble into nanocrystals, the same intermolecular forces that restrict intramolecular motion to produce AIE also drive the formation of stable nanostructures. Thus, the AIE signal becomes a direct reporter of the aggregated nanocrystal state: bright fluorescence indicates that the drug remains in its intact particulate form, while loss of signal would signal disassembly. In contrast to conventional dye-labeled systems, this built-in, structure-coupled luminescence eliminates false-positive imaging from leaked dyes and enables label-free tracking of drug integrity, pharmacokinetics, and therapeutic response [[29], [30], [31]].By replacing external carriers, fluorescent tags, and targeting ligands with a single AIE-active drug, the entire formulation is dramatically simplified, reducing translational barriers while enabling truly integrated “self-reporting, self-delivering, self-therapeutic” nanotheranostics.

Leukotriene receptor antagonists (LTRAs), including Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast, are widely prescribed medications for asthma and allergic rhinitis that function by blocking cysteinyl leukotriene receptor 1 [32]. Beyond their canonical anti-leukotriene activity, accumulating evidence reveals that LTRAs exert diverse anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects, including suppression of nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) signaling, reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokine production, scavenging of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and modulation of the NLRP3 inflammasome [33,34]. These pharmacological properties align remarkably well with the core pathogenesis of acute orchitis, which involves activation of the Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/myeloid differentiation primary response protein 88 (MyD88)/NF-κB pathway, excessive generation of ROS, release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and disruption of the blood–testis barrier [35,36]. Therefore, LTRAs are mechanistically attractive candidates for repurposing in testicular inflammatory disorders. However, whether LTRAs inherently possess AIE activity and can be fabricated into self-reporting nanotheranostics for orchitis remains largely unexplored.

Herein, we report the discovery that Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast are intrinsically AIE-active. Systematic photophysical and theoretical investigations reveal that extensive intermolecular halogen and hydrogen bonds simultaneously restrict intramolecular motion to generate bright solid-state fluorescence and drive spontaneous self-assembly into uniform, spherical, PEG-stabilized nanocrystals (PNPs, ZNPs, MNPs). In LPS-stimulated cells, these nanocrystals significantly suppress NF-κB pathway activation, inhibit pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, and scavenge ROS. In a rat model of LPS-induced acute orchitis, the nanocrystals markedly alleviate histopathological injury, restore oxidative stress balance, and downregulate TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB and the prokineticin 2/prokineticin receptor 1 (PK2/PKR1) signaling. Crucially, the intrinsic AIE fluorescence enables label-free tracking of nanocrystal delivery to the testis, and the imaging signal correlates with therapeutic efficacy, without causing reproductive toxicity as evidenced by normal sperm morphology and testosterone levels (Scheme 1). This work establishes a paradigm for transforming conventional small-molecule drugs into minimal-excipient, self-monitoring AIE nanotheranostics for imaging-guided therapy of acute orchitis.

Schematic illustration of the discovery of intrinsic aggregation-induced emission (AIE) in three clinically approved leukotriene receptor antagonists (Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, Montelukast), their self-assembly with DSPE-PEG2k into uniform spherical nanocrystals (PNPs, ZNPs, MNPs), and the application of these PEG-stabilized AIE nanotheranostics for label-free fluorescence imaging and therapy, with detectable accumulation in the inflamed testes. The nanocrystals suppress NF-κB activation via blocking p65 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, scavenge reactive oxygen species, and downregulate the TLR4/MyD88 and PK2/PKR1 pathways, achieving potent anti-inflammatory efficacy while enabling fluorescence-guided biodistribution tracking without detectable reproductive toxicity.

Materials and methods

Materials

Pranlukast nanoparticles (PNPs), Zafirlukast nanoparticles (ZNPs) and Montelukast nanoparticles (MNPs) were self-prepared fluorescent CysLT1 receptor-antagonist nanoparticles. The intrinsic fluorescence of these nanoparticles was used directly for cellular uptake, efflux and tissue-distribution assays without additional fluorescent labelling. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) was obtained from Beyotime. CCK-8 reagent was obtained from Solarbio (CA1210).

IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β ELISA kits were sourced from Solarbio for mouse (SEKM-0034, SEKM-0007, SEKM-0002) and from MSKBIO for rat (69-30484, 69-30490, 69-30375). The free testosterone ELISA kit was obtained from Solarbio (SEKSM-0045-48T). Lyso-Tracker Red was purchased from Beyotime (C1046).

Antibodies against NF-κB p65 (Proteintech, 10745-1-AP), MyD88 (Proteintech, 67969-1-Ig), phospho-NF-κB p65 (Abclonal, AP1294), TLR4 (Proteintech, 19811-1-AP), IκBα (Abclonal, A19714), GAPDH (Abclonal, A19056), PK2 and PKR1 were used for immunofluorescence, immunohistochemistry or western blotting as appropriate. DAPI-containing antifade mounting medium (Beyotime, P0131), and citrate antigen-retrieval buffer (Cell Signaling Technology, 14746) were used according to the manufacturers’ protocols.

Major instruments included a CO2 incubator (Thermo, 3111 or Forma Series II Water Jacketed CO2 Incubator), a biological safety cabinet (Suzhou Antai, BS-1300IIA2), microplate readers (Beijing Liuyi WD-2102B and Thermo Labsystems Multiskan MK3), centrifuges (Thermo MICROCL 17 and LEGEND MICRO 21R), a CytoFLEX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter), a Zeiss LSM700 confocal laser scanning microscope, a CRi Maestro in vivo imaging system, Bio-Rad electrophoresis and transfer apparatus, and a GelView 6000Plus chemiluminescence imaging system.

Preparation and characterization of AIE drug nanocrystals

For each drug (Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, or Montelukast, 5 mg), it was mixed with DSPE-PEG2k (0.5 mg) and dissolved in THF (0.5 mL). The solution was then rapidly injected into 5 mL of ultrapure water under vigorous stirring for 30 s. After THF was removed by evaporation under a fume hood for 2 h, aqueous suspensions of the corresponding nanocrystals (denoted as PNPs, ZNPs, and MNPs, respectively) were obtained [[37], [38], [39]]. The THF solution was rapidly injected within 3 s into ultrapure water under vigorous stirring at 1000 rpm. THF removal was confirmed by gravimetric analysis until constant weight. The nanocrystal suspensions were stored at 4 °C protected from light. TEM images were acquired on a JEOL JEM-2100 microscope by directly depositing 5 μL of the suspension onto carbon-coated copper grids and air-drying without negative staining, as the nanocrystals provided sufficient electron contrast due to their crystalline nature. UV–vis absorption spectra and fluorescence spectra were recorded on a Shimadzu UV-2600 spectrophotometer and an Edinburgh FLS1000 spectrofluorometer, respectively. To evaluate AIE behavior, stock solutions of each drug in tetrahydrofuran (THF) were mixed with water to achieve water volume fractions (fw) ranging from 0% to 99%, and emission spectra were collected immediately. Excitation wavelengths were set at 320 nm (Pranlukast), 320 nm (Zafirlukast), and 320 nm (Montelukast). The I/I0 values were monitored at 450 nm (Pranlukast), 450 nm (Zafirlukast), and 500 nm (Montelukast), corresponding to their respective emission maxima in the aggregated state. Fluorescence quantum yields were determined using an integrating sphere (Edinburgh FLS1000) with absolute measurement method. The fluorescence quantum yields (PLQYs) of Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast in THF solution were determined to be 1.8%, 2.2%, and 3.8%, respectively; in nanocrystal suspension, 2.1%, 2.1%, and 4.5%; and in powder state, 5.8%, 20.3%, and 15.6%, respectively. The progressive increase in PLQY from solution to nanocrystal suspension to powder confirms the aggregation-induced emission enhancement upon increasing molecular packing density.

Theoretical calculations

All quantum-chemical calculations employed Gaussian 09. Ground-state (S0) geometries were optimized at the B3LYP-D3BJ/6-31G(d,p) level, and excited-state (S1) geometries were optimized using time-dependent density functional theory (TD-DFT) at the same level. Natural transition orbital (NTO) analyses were performed to characterize the electron distribution in the S1 excited state. Reorganization energies were evaluated with the MOMAP package based on the optimized ground- and excited-state geometries and further decomposed into contributions from bond-length, bond-angle, and dihedral-angle variations. Crystalline-phase reorganization energies were calculated using cluster models constructed from the single-crystal structures. Intermolecular interactions in the crystal were analyzed using Hirshfeld surface and fingerprint plot analyses, and the corresponding surfaces were visualized with Multiwfn and VMD.

Cell culture and LPS-induced inflammatory stimulation

GC-1 cells were cultured in a humidified atmosphere. Logarithmic-phase cells were used for all in vitro experiments. To establish the inflammatory model, cells were stimulated with LPS (1 μg mL−1) for 24 h, followed by treatment with PNPs, ZNPs, or MNPs at 5 μg mL−1, a concentration determined by CCK-8 cytotoxicity assays. Experimental groups comprised untreated control, LPS-stimulated alone, and LPS-stimulated plus each of the three nanocrystal treatments.

CCK-8 cell viability assay

CCK-8 assay was used to assess the cytotoxicity of PNPs, ZNPs, and MNPs toward GC-1 cells. Logarithmic-phase cells were seeded into 96-well plates and allowed to adhere for 6–24 h at 37 °C under 5% CO2. Then, 20 μL of serially diluted nanoparticles was added per well, covering concentration ranges of 0–200 μg mL−1 (PNPs, ZNPs and MNPs). Blank wells (medium only) and control wells (untreated cells) were included in triplicate.

ELISA analysis of inflammatory cytokines in cell culture supernatants

ELISA was performed to measure IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6 levels in GC-1 cell culture supernatants. After LPS stimulation and nanoparticle treatment, supernatants were collected and centrifuged to remove debris. Cytokine levels were quantified using commercial ELISA kits (Solarbio for mouse; MSKBIO for rat) following the manufacturers’ protocols.

Western blot analysis of NF-κB pathway proteins in cells

Western blotting was used to assess the effects of PNPs, ZNPs and MNPs on LPS-induced NF-κB activation. After treatment, GC-1 cells were lysed in RIPA buffer containing PMSF (100:1), and total protein was collected by centrifugation (12,000 rpm, 10 min, 4 °C). Protein concentration was determined by BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein were incubated with primary antibodies against phospho-NF-κB p65 (p-p65, 1:3000), IκBα and GAPDH.

Immunofluorescence staining of NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation

GC-1 cells seeded on coverslips (5 × 105 cells/well) were treated with LPS and nanoparticles, washed with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (15 min), blocked with 5% BSA (1 h, RT), and incubated with anti-NF-κB p65 primary antibody overnight at 4 °C. After washing, cells were incubated with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibody (1 h, 37 °C, dark). Nuclear translocation was evaluated by p65/DAPI colocalization.

Intracellular ROS detection

Intracellular ROS was measured using DCFH-DA. After LPS and nanoparticle treatment, cells were incubated with 10 μmol L−1 DCFH-DA (1:1000 in serum-free medium) for 20 min at 37 °C, washed, and analyzed by flow cytometry (Ex/Em 488/525 nm). DCF fluorescence intensity reflected ROS levels.

Cellular uptake and lysosomal colocalization by confocal microscopy

Cells seeded on coverslips (60–70% confluence) were incubated with PNPs, ZNPs or MNPs (5 μg mL−1) for 0.5, 1, 2, 4 or 8 h at 37 °C. Lyso-Tracker Red was added 30 min before each endpoint. Cells were then fixed, stained with DAPI, and imaged by confocal microscopy (Zeiss LSM700). Colocalization was assessed by overlay analysis.

Quantification of cellular uptake by flow cytometry

Logarithmic-phase GC-1 cells (1 × 106 mL−1) were incubated with nanoparticles for 0.5–8 h, washed with PBS, and analyzed on a CytoFLEX flow cytometer. Mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was used to quantify uptake at each time point.

Nanoparticle efflux kinetics by flow cytometry

Cells were loaded with nanoparticles for 4 h, washed, and then cultured in nanoparticle-free medium for 0, 2, 6 or 24 h. At each efflux time point, cells were washed, detached, and analyzed by flow cytometry. Fluorescence decay relative to 0 h was used to evaluate retention/efflux.

Animals and LPS-induced acute testicular injury model

Male SD rats (180–200 g) were housed under standard conditions. After acclimation, rats were randomly divided into five groups (n = 6 each): control, LPS + PBS, LPS + PNPs (1 mg/kg), LPS + ZNPs (1 mg/kg), LPS + MNPs (1 mg/kg). All animal procedures were approved (wydw2026-0428). Acute orchitis was induced by intraperitoneal (i.p.) LPS injection (5 mg/kg) at Day 0, while control rats received saline. Two hours later, treatment groups received i.p. PNPs, ZNPs or MNPs; the model group received PBS. Treatments were repeated every 12 h for a total of seven administrations (at 2, 14, 26, 38, 50, 62, 74 h). At 98 h (24 h after last dose), rats were anesthetized, blood and tissues (testes, epididymides, major organs) were collected.

Ex vivo fluorescence imaging and tissue biodistribution

At the endpoint, heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney and testes were excised and imaged using a CRi Maestro system (no additional staining needed due to intrinsic nanoparticle fluorescence). Fluorescence signals were quantified to evaluate organ distribution and testicular accumulation.

Assessment of body weight, scrotal swelling, testicular weight and sperm morphology

Body weight was recorded on Day 0, 2, 4. Scrotal redness/swelling was scored daily (0–3 scale). After dissection, both testes were weighed, and relative testicular weight (testis weight/body weight ×100%) was calculated. Epididymal sperm suspensions were examined microscopically for normal sperm morphology (%).

Serum testosterone and inflammatory cytokine assays

Blood samples were collected between 8:00 and 10:00 a.m. to minimize circadian rhythm effects on testosterone secretion. Blood was clotted (20 min, RT), centrifuged (1000×g, 10 min, 4 °C) and serum collected. Free testosterone, TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6 were measured by commercial ELISA kits following the manufacturer's instructions (absorbance at 450 nm, four-parameter logistic fitting).

Hematoxylin and eosin staining

Testicular tissues were fixed, paraffin-embedded, sectioned (3–5 μm), deparaffinized, rehydrated, stained with hematoxylin (5–10 min) and eosin (1–2 min). Seminiferous tubule structure and germ-cell arrangement were evaluated.

Immunohistochemistry of TLR4, MyD88 and NF-κB pathway proteins

Paraffin sections underwent antigen retrieval (citrate buffer, 95–98 °C, 10 min) and peroxidase blocking (3% H2O2, 10 min). After blocking, sections were incubated with primary antibodies against TLR4 (1:500), MyD88 (1:1000) or p-NF-κB p65 (1:3000). Signal was developed with DAB, and sections were counterstained. Positive staining intensity indicated pathway activation.

Testicular MDA and GSH assays

MDA was measured using a commercial kit: homogenates were heated (100 °C, 30 min), centrifuged, and absorbance read at 450/532/600 nm. GSH was measured using fresh testicular homogenates: after centrifugation (8000 rpm, 10 min, 4 °C), absorbance at 412 nm was read.

ELISA analysis of inflammatory cytokines in testicular tissue homogenates

TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6 levels in testicular homogenates were measured by ELISA (same protocol as 2.16). Absorbance at 450 nm, four-parameter logistic fitting.

Western blot analysis of PK2 and PKR1 in testicular tissue

Testicular tissues were homogenized, and total protein was extracted. After blocking, membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against PK2 (1:1000), PKR1 (1:1000) and GAPDH.

Serum biochemical analysis and hemolysis assay

For serum biochemical analysis, blood samples collected at the endpoint were centrifuged (1000 × g, 10 min, 4 °C), and serum levels of aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and creatinine (CREA) were measured using an automatic biochemical analyzer.

For the hemolysis assay, fresh rat red blood cells (2% suspension in PBS) were incubated with PNPs, ZNPs, or MNPs (100 μg mL−1) at 37 °C for 1 h. Deionized water served as the positive control (100% hemolysis) and PBS as the negative control (0% hemolysis). After centrifugation (1000 × g, 5 min), the supernatant absorbance was measured at 540 nm. Hemolysis percentage was calculated as: (Asample − Anegative)/(Apositive − Anegative) × 100%.

Data processing and statistical analysis

Data were processed with Excel and GraphPad Prism. CCK-8 was blank-corrected; ELISA used four-parameter logistic fitting; flow cytometry data expressed as MFI; Western blots normalized to loading control; histology images analyzed semi-quantitatively. All experiments were repeated at least three times. Data are mean ± SEM. Multiple groups were compared by one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post-hoc test. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results and discussion

AIE properties and self-assembly into nanocrystals

We first examined the pristine drug powders by scanning electron microscopy. As shown in Fig. S1–S3, Pranlukast, Zafirlukast and Montelukast all exist as irregular, micro-sized crystalline aggregates (several to tens of micrometres), which are unsuitable for intraperitoneal administration due to the risk of capillary blockage and rapid clearance. We then investigated the photophysical properties of the three leukotriene receptor antagonists in mixed tetrahydrofuran/water solvents. As the water volume fraction fw increased, the fluorescence emission of Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast intensified dramatically (Fig. 1A–D), a hallmark of aggregation-induced emission. Notably, Montelukast exhibited a progressive redshift in its emission maximum from ∼405 nm to ∼500 nm with increasing water fraction (Fig. 1D), indicative of J-aggregate formation, where slipped cofacial molecular packing lowers the excited-state energy level. This assignment is supported by the single-crystal structure of Montelukast (CCDC 714043), which reveals a slipped stacking arrangement characteristic of J-aggregation. In contrast, Pranlukast and Zafirlukast showed minimal spectral shifts, suggesting different aggregation modes. The intensity ratio I/I0 rose sharply at fw > 40%, confirming that molecular aggregation in the poor solvent turns on bright fluorescence (Fig. 1E). Fig. 1F and G presents the normalized UV–vis absorption and fluorescence emission spectra of the three drugs in the aggregated state, revealing structure-dependent electronic transitions and emission profiles that underpin their distinct AIE behaviors. We subsequently formulated these AIE-active drugs into nanocrystals via a simple nanoprecipitation method using DSPE-PEG2k as a stabilizer. Transmission electron microscopy (Fig. 1H) showed that the resulting PNPs, ZNPs, and MNPs were uniform spheres with primary crystalline domains of 1–5 nm and a narrow size distribution (Fig. 1I). Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) analysis further verified that the characteristic diffraction peaks of the raw drugs were retained in the nanocrystals with notable broadening (Fig. S4 for PNPs; Fig. S5 for MNPs), consistent with the nanocrystal size observed by TEM. The PEG corona not only imparted excellent colloidal stability but also ensured that the nanocrystals retained the strong solid-state fluorescence essential for label-free bioimaging. This straightforward strategy converts three clinically approved small-molecule drugs into PEG-stabilized, brightly emissive nanoparticles without chemical modification, thereby preserving their intrinsic pharmacological activities. Moreover, the nanocrystal suspensions exhibited bright blue-green luminescence under UV illumination (Fig. S6), while appearing nearly colourless under daylight. This simple visual test confirms that the AIE property is retained after formulation, enabling naked-eye or fluorescence-guided drug tracking. The hydrodynamic diameters of PNPs, ZNPs, and MNPs measured by DLS were approximately 82 nm, 100 nm, and 76 nm, respectively (Fig. S7–S9), which are significantly larger than the TEM-determined core sizes (1–4 nm). This difference is attributed to the combined effects of the PEG hydration layer and the formation of loose, dynamic aggregates of several primary nanocrystals in aqueous solution. Zeta potential values of approximately −24 to −29 mV (Fig. S10–S12) confirm the good colloidal stability of the formulations.

Origin of AIE: Restricted intramolecular motion in the crystalline state

To elucidate the origin of the AIE behavior, the electronic structures and excited-state relaxation processes of Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast were investigated by TD-DFT calculations. As shown in Fig. 2, the HOMOs and LUMOs are distributed over different aromatic fragments, indicating varying degrees of excited-state electron redistribution upon photoexcitation. The corresponding natural transition orbitals (NTOs) further reveal the hole–particle distribution at the S1 state, confirming that the lowest excited states possess mixed π–π* and charge-transfer characteristics.

Comparison of frontier molecular orbitals and excited-state electronic distributions of Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast. Top: HOMO and LUMO together with the corresponding oscillator strengths (f) for the lowest-energy electronic transitions. Bottom: Hole and particle natural transition orbitals (NTOs) at the S1 state. All electronic structure calculations were performed using TD-DFT at the B3LYP-D3BJ/6-31G(d,p) level. The NTOs were generated from the S1 excited state.

To evaluate the influence of molecular motions on excited-state deactivation, reorganization energy (λ) analyses were performed. In the gas phase, Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast exhibit total reorganization energies of 4697, 4370, and 6548 cm−1, respectively (Fig. 3a–c). Notably, Montelukast shows a dominant contribution from dihedral-angle variations (68.76%), indicating pronounced rotor motions in the excited state. Such large-amplitude intramolecular motions are expected to facilitate nonradiative decay and reduce fluorescence efficiency. In contrast, when transferred into the Montelukast crystalline phase (CCDC 714043), the dihedral-angle contribution of Montelukast decreases dramatically from 68.76% to 11.54%, whereas the bond-angle contribution increases to 76.99% (Fig. 3d). This substantial suppression of torsional motion demonstrates that molecular rotations are effectively restricted in the aggregated state. According to the restricted intramolecular motion (RIM) mechanism, inhibition of these nonradiative relaxation pathways favors radiative decay and consequently enhances fluorescence emission.

The dominant contribution from dihedral-angle motions in Montelukast indicates pronounced intramolecular conformational relaxation in the excited state. (D) Reorganization energy distribution of Montelukast in the crystalline phase. Compared with the gas phase, the contribution from dihedral-angle motions is markedly suppressed, demonstrating restricted intramolecular motion (RIM) upon aggregation. (E) Crystal-packing analysis of Montelukast. The crystal cluster structure, Hirshfeld surface, and corresponding two-dimensional fingerprint plots reveal abundant intermolecular O···H and Cl···H interactions, which effectively restrict molecular motions and account for the observed AIE behavior.

To identify the structural origin of this restriction, crystal-packing analyses were carried out. The crystal cluster, Hirshfeld surface, and two-dimensional fingerprint plots (Fig. 3e) reveal abundant intermolecular O···H and Cl···H contacts, contributing 8.3% and 8.1% of the total intermolecular interactions, respectively. These noncovalent interactions lock the molecules into rigid conformations within the crystal lattice and effectively suppress excited-state conformational relaxation. For Pranlukast and Zafirlukast, although single-crystal structures are not available, their AIE behavior is consistent with the general restricted intramolecular motion (RIM) mechanism, as supported by their rigid molecular backbones and the solvent-dependent fluorescence enhancement observed experimentally. However, we do not extrapolate the specific halogen-bonding interactions observed in Montelukast to these two compounds without crystallographic evidence. Nevertheless, the synergistic effects of restricted molecular motion and dense intermolecular interactions provide a clear explanation for the observed AIE behavior of these leukotriene receptor antagonists.

Cellular uptake and retention of AIE nanocrystals

Efficient cellular internalization and prolonged intracellular retention are critical for therapeutic efficacy. Confocal microscopy and flow cytometry were used to monitor the uptake kinetics of PNPs and ZNPs (Fig. 4; Fig. S13 for MNPs). Green fluorescence was detectable after 0.5 h of incubation and gradually increased with prolonged incubation, reaching a peak at approximately 4 h, followed by a slight decrease at 8 h. Co-staining with LysoTracker Red revealed pronounced colocalization between nanoparticle fluorescence and lysosomal fluorescence, indicating that the nanoparticles were mainly transported to lysosomes after endocytosis. Quantitative Pearson's colocalization coefficients for PNPs, ZNPs, and MNPs at 8 h were 0.76, 0.66, and 0.52, respectively (Fig. S14–S16), confirming time-dependent lysosomal accumulation. Flow cytometric analysis of efflux (Fig. S13) showed that after removal of the nanocrystals from the medium, intracellular fluorescence of PNPs, ZNPs, and MNPs underwent time-dependent efflux over 24 h. This prolonged intracellular residence is likely attributable to the nanoscale dimensions and the PEGylated surface, which may reduce exocytosis and endosomal recycling. The persistent intracellular presence ensures a sustained pharmacological effect at the target site, a property particularly advantageous for treating acute inflammatory conditions where a prolonged anti-inflammatory action is desired.

In vitro anti-inflammatory activity and NF-κB pathway suppression

The therapeutic potential of the AIE drug nanocrystals was first evaluated in an LPS-induced inflammation model in cells. CCK-8 assays demonstrated that all three types of nanoparticles exhibited good biocompatibility at low concentrations. Specifically, PNPs maintained cell viability above 80% at 0.5–5 μg mL−1, ZNPs showed a broader safety window with viability >80% at 0.5–25 μg mL−1, and MNPs kept viability above 80% at 0.5–10 μg mL−1 (Fig. 5A–C). ELISA measurements of cell culture supernatants showed that LPS stimulation significantly increased the levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 compared with the control group, and treatment with PNPs, ZNPs, or MNPs significantly suppressed the LPS-induced upregulation of these inflammatory cytokines (Fig. 5D–F). Among the three nanoparticles, MNPs exhibited the strongest inhibitory effect, followed by ZNPs, whereas PNPs showed a relatively weaker effect. To delineate the underlying molecular mechanism, we examined the NF-κB signaling pathway. Western blot analysis (Fig. 5G–I) revealed that LPS stimulation markedly increased phosphorylation of p65 and degradation of IκBα, whereas co-treatment with the nanocrystals effectively reversed these changes. MNPs showed the most pronounced inhibitory effect on p-p65 levels and IκBα restoration. Immunofluorescence staining for p65 (Fig. 5J, Fig. S17 for quantification) further showed that the nanocrystals inhibited LPS-induced p65 nuclear translocation, with green fluorescence retained in the cytoplasm. Additionally, DCFH-DA probing (Fig. 5K) indicated that treatment with PNPs, ZNPs, or MNPs markedly reduced intracellular ROS accumulation, with MNPs and ZNPs exhibiting stronger antioxidant capacities than PNPs [[40], [41], [42]]. Together, these results establish that the AIE drug nanocrystals exert their anti-inflammatory effects by dual targeting of the NF-κB pathway and oxidative stress, thereby suppressing the downstream inflammatory cascade.

Therapeutic efficacy in an LPS-induced acute orchitis rat model

Encouraged by the promising in vitro results, we tested the therapeutic efficacy in a rat model of acute orchitis induced by intraperitoneal LPS injection. Histopathological examination with H&E staining (Fig. 6A) showed that LPS caused severe seminiferous tubule degeneration, interstitial edema, and massive inflammatory cell infiltration. Treatment with PNPs, ZNPs, or MNPs markedly alleviated these pathological changes, with the histological features of the MNPs group most closely resembling those of the control group. Immunohistochemical staining (Fig. 6A) revealed that the nanocrystals markedly downregulated the expression of MyD88, NF-κB, and TLR4 in testicular tissues, with MNPs exhibiting the strongest inhibitory effect, confirming suppression of the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling axis [43]. In line with the histological improvement, the nanocrystals restored the balance of oxidative stress markers: malondialdehyde (MDA) levels were significantly reduced and glutathione (GSH) levels were elevated compared with the untreated orchitis group, with MNPs exhibiting the strongest antioxidant activity (Fig. 6B and C). ELISA quantification (Fig. 6D–F) confirmed a marked decrease in the intratesticular levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, with the inhibitory efficacy following the order MNPs > ZNPs > PNPs. Moreover, the protein expression of prokineticin 2 (PK2) and its receptor PKR1, a recently identified pathway contributing to testicular inflammation and pain, was significantly downregulated by the nanocrystal treatment, with MNPs inducing the greatest reduction (Fig. 6G and H). These findings collectively demonstrate that the AIE drug nanocrystals effectively alleviate LPS-induced acute orchitis by inhibiting the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway, mitigating oxidative stress, and suppressing the PK2/PKR1 axis.

Ex vivo biodistribution and reproductive safety

The intrinsic AIE fluorescence of the nanocrystals enabled label-free fluorescence tracking of their ex vivo biodistribution. Ex vivo imaging of major organs (Fig. 7A) revealed that after intraperitoneal administration, the nanocrystals predominantly accumulated in the liver and kidneys, with a clearly detectable signal in the inflamed testes, revealing detectable accumulation in the inflamed testes. Importantly, comprehensive reproductive toxicity evaluation demonstrated an excellent safety profile. Body weight monitoring showed that body weight gradually decreased in the LPS + PBS group, whereas body weight loss was significantly attenuated in all nanoparticle-treated groups and approached the level of the control group by day 4 (Fig. 7B). The scrotal swelling/redness score was markedly elevated in the LPS + PBS group but was significantly reduced in all treatment groups, with the lowest score observed in the MNPs group (Fig. 7C). Relative testicular weight, expressed as the percentage of testis weight to body weight, was significantly increased in the LPS + PBS group, reflecting testicular edema and inflammation. This parameter was restored in all nanoparticle-treated groups, with the most evident recovery observed in the MNPs group (Fig. 7D). Sperm morphology analysis showed that the percentage of morphologically normal sperm was significantly reduced after LPS challenge. Nanoparticle treatment markedly improved sperm morphology, with the degree of recovery following the order MNPs > ZNPs > PNPs (Fig. 7E). In addition, serum testosterone levels were significantly decreased in the LPS + PBS group but were markedly restored after nanoparticle treatment, with the highest recovery observed in the MNPs group (Fig. 7F). The ability to visualize drug distribution while exerting potent therapeutic effects without reproductive toxicity on the measured short-term reproductive indices positions these AIE drug nanocrystals as a promising theranostic platform for acute orchitis and potentially other inflammatory diseases. In addition, systemic biosafety was assessed by serum biochemical analysis and hemolysis assay. As shown in Fig. S18–S22, no significant differences in serum AST, ALT, ALP, BUN, or CREA levels were observed between any of the nanoparticle-treated groups and the control group, indicating no detectable hepatic or renal dysfunction. The hemolysis test (Fig. S23) revealed that PNPs, ZNPs and MNPs exhibited hemolysis rates below 3%, all meeting the acceptable safety threshold, confirming good hemocompatibility. We acknowledge that further preclinical steps, including long-term toxicity evaluation in larger animals, comprehensive pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) profiling, and formulation scalability under GMP-compliant conditions, are essential to advance this strategy toward clinical application [44].

Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated that three clinically approved leukotriene receptor antagonists—Pranlukast, Zafirlukast, and Montelukast—are intrinsically AIE-active, and that this property originates from extensive intermolecular halogen and hydrogen bonds that restrict intramolecular motion in the crystalline state. By formulating these drugs into PEGylated nanocrystals, we created minimal-excipient, self-monitoring nanotheranostics that combine bright intrinsic fluorescence with potent anti-inflammatory activity. The nanocrystals are efficiently internalized by cells, exhibit prolonged retention, and suppress LPS-induced inflammation by dual inhibition of the NF-κB pathway and oxidative stress. In a rat model of acute orchitis, they significantly alleviate testicular histopathology, downregulate the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB and PK2/PKR1 cascades, and restore oxidative balance. Importantly, the AIE fluorescence permits label-free fluorescence tracking of biodistribution, revealing detectable accumulation in the inflamed testes. Moreover, no obvious adverse effects were observed in the measured short-term reproductive parameters, although long-term reproductive and multigenerational studies are warranted to fully assess safety. This work establishes a straightforward strategy of repurposing conventional small-molecule drugs into self-assembled AIE nanotheranostics, offering a clinically translatable approach for imaging-guided therapy of acute orchitis and potentially other inflammatory diseases.

Sensitivity of kfp on the Breakthrough Curve

(1) At the initial stage (up to C/C0 = 0.5), film diffusion and pore diffusion are commonly both considered as the limiting steps.3 The Bi number was in a range of 71 to 261 × 102, according to the corrected scale in Figure 2C and Table S2.

(2) In ref (1), the sensitivity test was used to address the effects of variation in kfp on the breakthrough curve shape; the low sensitivity stands for the weak response of the breakthrough curve to the variation of kfp with a fixed Deff.

However, in our research, we used breakthrough curve fitting to derive the values of kfp. In our research, the external temperature and DC fields lead to varied Deff values, which is why breakthrough curve shapes and the kfp will of course vary with Deff accordingly; that is, the variations of kfp – f(t)/f(x) are meaningful in our research.

Meanwhile, our research found that the effects of the DC field on Deff were much stronger than effects on kfp (cf. Figure 3, ratios of 0.43–0.87). This is also related to the high Bi numbers; i.e., the kfp was already high before the application of external influencing factors. Therefore, the variations according to the variation of Deff are less sensitive.

“Effectiveness” of Film and Pore Diffusion

The appropriateness of adopting the kinetic models has been addressed in section 1. Our understanding is that the trends of kfp and Deff relate to the “effectiveness” of film and pore diffusion.

In detail, Deff is defined as the “effective diffusion coefficient”, which is determined by not only the molecular diffusion in pores but also the sorption rate (i.e., “effectively” adsorbed). A higher temperature decreases the liquid viscosity, resulting in faster diffusion. However, sorption isotherms proved that the sorption capacity and rate decrease in higher temperatures; i.e., the molecules that diffused to pores are less “effectively” adsorbed. Therefore, the effective diffusion coefficient decreases with an increase in temperature.

Environmental Application

In our research, we adopted weak DC fields; the enhancement can be manipulated by the DC field strength with a corresponding 10 mA magnitude increment in current. The effects of temperature could be used in soil heating desorption processes and for environmental temperature variations, e.g., areas with large diurnal temperature differences.

(1) The DC enhancement is proportional to the DC field applied; therefore, in the application, especially in the fast response of a point source high-concentration pollution control, the DC field strength can be manipulated. In section 4.1, we made the following statements: “This represents a maximum variation of 4.4% in PFOA retention per °C”. “There was a maximum of 26% variation in PFOA retention per V cm–1”.

Meanwhile, increasing the DC field by 1 V/cm results in a current increment of only 10 mA, so the DC field is a powerful tool.

(2) The process is not designed for heating the water flow, but in practice is combined with electrical resistance heating4 (cf. DOI: 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.165771), or variations in environmental temperature during the application of electrokinetics, especially for the areas with large diurnal temperature differences.

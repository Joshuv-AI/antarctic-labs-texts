Reassessing the gallium anomaly using exact electron wave functions
M. Cadeddu, N. Cargioli, G. Carotenuto, F. Dordei, L. Ferro, C. Giunti

Abstract
The gallium anomaly, a persistent discrepancy exceeding 4 ​ σ 4\sigma in the 71 Ga neutrino capture rates from 51 Cr and 37 Ar calibration sources by the GALLEX, SAGE, and recently BEST experiments, has challenged particle physics and nuclear theory for over three decades. We present a new calculation for the neutrino capture cross-section, abandoning the conventional leading-order approximation for electronic wave functions by numerically solving the Dirac-Coulomb equation for both bound and continuum electron states. Finally, we re-evaluate the gallium anomaly, updating its global significance and presenting the most up-to-date status of its interpretation in terms of sterile neutrinos.



I
Introduction

The study of neutrino interactions with matter has provided profound insights into both the properties of neutrinos and the dynamics of weak interactions. In this field, a particularly intriguing and long-standing puzzle is the so-called
gallium anomaly

Elliott et al. 2024
;
Giunti and Laveder 2011

;

;
Kaether et al. 2010
;
Altmann et al. 2005
and SAGE

;

experiments, which reported a measured neutrino-induced inverse beta decay (IBD) rate from
51
Cr and
37
Ar calibration sources systematically lower than predictions, has raised questions about the robustness of the theoretical cross-section calculations. Moreover, when considered alongside other anomalies
Mention et al. 2011
;
Abratenko et al. 2023
;
Giunti et al. 2022a
;
Berryman and Huber 2020
, it has strengthened the case for potential implications beyond the Standard Model (SM)
Giunti and Laveder 2007
;
Giunti and Lasserre 2019
;
Gariazzo et al. 2016
;
Gonzalez-Garcia et al. 2016
;
Diaz et al. 2020
;
Böser et al. 2020
;
Dasgupta and Kopp 2021
;
Giunti and Ternes 2024
;
Abazajian et al. 2012
;
Acero et al. 2024
;
Farzan and Schwetz 2023
.

The anomaly arises in the charge current
\nu_{e}+^{71}{\textrm{Ga}}\to\,^{71}{\textrm{Ge}}+e^{-}
reaction, a process historically used to detect neutrinos from solar and artificial sources. While the theoretical predictions for the cross section have provided critical benchmarks for interpreting experimental results, the persistent deficit in the observed rates, now exceeding
4\sigma
thanks to the recent short-baseline BEST experiment
Barinov et al. 2022a
;
Barinov et al. 2022b
, demands a closer examination of the underlying assumptions and parameters in the theoretical models. An early theoretical work by Bahcall
Bahcall 1997
established the foundation for these predictions. However, the persistence of this anomaly has later prompted other theoretical investigations
Elliott et al. 2023
;
Haxton and Rule 2025
;
Kostensalo et al. 2019
;
Brdar et al. 2023
;
Giunti et al. 2023
;
Huber 2023
, that employed more recent experimental inputs, the inclusion of radiative terms, additional corrections and an updated evaluation of excited-state contributions, among others.

Our analysis builds on and updates these previous works. In this paper, we undertake a comprehensive revision of the theoretical cross section for neutrino interactions with
{}^{71}{\textrm{Ga}}
.
The widely employed theoretical approach relies on the
detailed balance
(db) principle
Williams 1971
;
Blatt and Weisskopf 1952
;
Bahcall 1989
;
Alvarez 1949
between neutrino-induced IBD and electron capture (EC) rates, factorizing nuclear and leptonic matrix elements, and utilizing leading-order (LO) approximations for the
lepton
wave functions
Bambynek et al. 1977
;
Behrens and Jänecke 1969
.
Here, we abandon the conventional LO approximation for electronic wave functions by numerically solving the Dirac-Coulomb equation for both bound and continuum electron states, using the same underlying assumptions and software. The latter is used to obtain an accurate determination of the Fermi function
Fermi 1933
, a correction for the Coulomb distortion of the outgoing electron in the field of the associated nucleus.
While doing so, we incorporate recent developments in the evaluation of the electron-capture lifetime of
{}^{71}{\textrm{Ge}}

Hampel and Remsberg 1985
;
Collar and Yoon 2023
;
Norman et al. 2024
;
Derbin et al. 2025
and the first determination of the nuclear charge radius of
{}^{71}{\textrm{Ge}}

Wang et al. 2024
, the use of a more accurate
Q
-value

Singh and Chen 2023
as well as updated electron-capture probability ratios
Collar and Yoon 2023
;
Arnaud et al. 2020
;
Bambynek et al. 1977
.

These updates aim to provide a more accurate and self-consistent theoretical framework for interpreting gallium-based neutrino experiments. Moreover, we update the status of the allowed parameter space for the interpretation of such an anomaly in terms of sterile neutrinos
Elliott et al. 2024
;
Giunti and Lasserre 2019
;
Giunti and Laveder 2007
;
Böser et al. 2020
whose presence would result in a reduction of the observed IBD event rate.

III
Reavaluation of the anomaly

Our theoretical estimation of the number of expected IBD events,
N_{\mathrm{cal}}
, can be compared with the experimental measurements from GALLEX
Anselmann et al. 1992
;
Hampel et al. 1999
;
Kaether et al. 2010
;
Altmann et al. 2005
, SAGE
Abdurashitov et al. 2009
;
Gavrin
and BEST
Barinov et al. 2022a
;
Barinov et al. 2022b
,
N_{\mathrm{exp}}
, leading to a ratio
R=N_{\mathrm{exp}}/N_{\mathrm{cal}}
. We define the following
\chi^{2}
function

\chi^{2}\!=\!\min_{\eta}\!\Bigg[\!\sum_{k,j}(R_{k}-\eta\overline{R})(V^{-1})_{kj}(R_{j}-\eta\overline{R})+\!\left(\frac{1\!-\!\eta}{\Delta\eta}\right)^{2}\Bigg],

(25)

where the sum runs over all the experimental measurements
k,j
and the nuisance parameter
\eta
is constrained, in the case of the (p,n) excited contribution, by

\begin{split}\Delta\eta&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cr}\Delta\eta_{+}(\mathrm{p,n})=0.047&\text{for}\,\eta>1,\\
\Delta\eta_{-}(\mathrm{p,n})=0.014&\text{for}\,\eta<1,\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(26)

and
\Delta\eta(^{3}\mathrm{He},^{3}\mathrm{H})=0.029
for the (
{}^{3}\text{He},^{3}\text{H}
) one.
The covariance matrix
V
is obtained from the uncertainties on the
R_{k}
ratios.
The resulting best fit value for all the experiments is found to be
\overline{R}=0.814^{+0.029}_{-0.045}
, when considering the (p,n) excited contribution, see Fig.
2
(upper panel).
Taking into account the non-parabolic behavior of the
\chi^{2}
,
shown in Fig.
3
,
we obtain a
6.1\sigma
anomaly. With the (
{}^{3}\text{He},^{3}\text{H}
) excited contribution
we obtain
\overline{R}=0.797^{+0.035}_{-0.034}
,
also shown in Fig.
2
(lower panel),
and a smaller
5.3\sigma
anomaly. These results have been obtained by improving
the calculation method used in Ref.
Giunti et al. 2023

by removing the model uncertainties from the BEST data.
These uncertainties are correlated among the experiments
and are taken into account in the
\chi^{2}
with the
\eta
nuisance parameter.
We also took into account the correlation of the systematic uncertainties of the
BEST data.
Moreover, to provide a conservative estimate of the anomaly, we reevaluate it considering the ground-state cross section in Eq. (
17
), whose best fit of the data is found to be
\overline{R}=0.860\pm 0.030
, which corresponds to a
4.5\sigma
anomaly.

V
Conclusions

In this work, we critically reassess the status of the gallium anomaly. By employing exact Dirac-Coulomb wave functions for both the bound and continuum electron states, a new averaging procedure and a refined statistical analysis, we reevaluate the neutrino capture cross-section on
71
Ga, showing that a
6.1\sigma
discrepancy is now achieved with the (p,n) excited state contribution,
and
5.3\sigma
with the (
{}^{3}\text{He},^{3}\text{H}
) excited state contribution. Such a result is obtained by using the same software and underlying assumptions when evaluating the exact electron wave functions in both the Fermi function and electron density at the nucleus for the EC process, which entering as a ratio in the cross section avoid possible systematic shifts, making the result more robust and self-consistent.
We also present the updated allowed region in the space of the
effective parameters of 3+1 active-sterile neutrino mixing.
We confirm the need for the explanation of the gallium data
of a large active-sterile mixing
which is in tension with the results of reactor antineutrino experiments

Giunti et al. 2022b
;
Giunti et al. 2022a
;
Almazán et al. 2023
;
Machikhiliyan 2022
;
Andriamirado et al. 2025
,
with the solar neutrino bound

Goldhagen et al. 2022
;
Gonzalez-Garcia et al. 2025
,
and the KATRIN neutrino mass measurement

Acharya et al. 2025
.
Hence, the solution of the gallium anomaly remains an open problem
which may be solved by a new source experiment
with a different detector
Huber 2023
;
Ciuffoli et al. 2025
;
Chauhan and Huber 2025
.

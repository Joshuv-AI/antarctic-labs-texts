QED calculations of the 𝐄𝟏 \bf E1 transition amplitude in neon-like iron and nickel
M. G. Kozlov 1,2, V. A. Yerokhin 3, M. Y. Kaygorodov 1, E. V. Tryapitsyna 1

Abstract
We calculated QED corrections to the E ​ 1 E1 transition amplitudes in Ne-like iron and nickel. For the 2 ​ p → 3 ​ d 2p\to 3d transitions the dominant effect came from the many-electron mixing, or electronic correlations. For the 2 ​ p → 3 ​ s 2p\to 3s transitions the correlation and one-electron effects were comparable and tended to compensate each other. Our ab initio calculations showed that vertex corrections were negligible for both types of transitions. Other QED corrections were accurately reproduced by including effective QEDMOD operator in the many-electron relativistic configuration interaction calculation.



I
Introduction

Recent high accuracy calculations of
E1
transition amplitudes in neon-like iron and nickel revealed rather large QED corrections for such relatively low-
Z
systems
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. This increased the interest to QED corrections to the transition amplitudes in many-electron atoms and ions.

There are many calculations of the QED corrections to the
E1
transition amplitudes in highly-charged one- and two-electron ions, see for instance Refs.
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. For the many-electron neutral atoms, or weakly charged ions, such corrections are usually not included. Neutral Cs is probably the most prominent exception. It was used for the high precision measurement of the parity non-conservation in atoms
[
7
]
. Using atomic theory one can accurately determine from this experiment the weak charge of the nucleus and test Standard Model at low energies
[
8
]
. This is why QED corrections to the
E1
amplitudes in Cs were calculated several times in different approaches.
Sapirstein and Cheng [9]
calculated QED correction to the
6s_{1/2}\to 6p_{1/2}
amplitude
ab initio
in the one-electron approximation, while calculations
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
included correlations but used an approximate model QED potential
[
13
]
and neglected the vertex correction.

Here we analyse in detail QED corrections to the
E1
amplitudes in neon-like Fe
16+
and Ni
18+
, explain their surprisingly large size, and discuss implications for other systems.

II
One-electron approximation

Ab initio
QED calculations are presently feasible in the
one-electron approximation, for local screening potentials (not the Dirac-Fock
potential). For this reason, we start with carrying out
ab initio
QED calculations
in the one-electron approximation, compare the
ab initio
results with the approximate QED
treatment, and then proceed to the many-electron calculations.

II.1
Ab initio
QED

The
E1
transition amplitude between one-electron states
a
and
b

is proportional to the matrix element of the electric dipole
operator in the length gauge,

\displaystyle z_{ab}=\bra{a}r_{z}\ket{b}=A(j_{a},m_{a},j_{b},m_{b})\mathcal{I}(a,b)\,,

(1)

where
r_{z}
is the
z
component of the position vector
\bm{r}
;
A
is the angular factor, which depends on the angular momenta
j_{a},j_{b}
and their projections
m_{a},m_{b}
;
\mathcal{I}(a,b)
is the radial integral, which depends on the radial parts of the upper and lower components
g
and
f
of the electronic wavefunction:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(g_{a}g_{b}+f_{a}f_{b})r^{3}dr\,.

(2)

The one-loop QED corrections to the
matrix element
z_{ab}
are induced by the electron self-energy (se)
and vacuum polarization (vp),

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm qed}=\delta z_{\rm se}+\delta z_{\rm vp}\,.

(3)

General expressions for the QED corrections to the
E1
transition
amplitude can be found
in Ref.
[
9
]
(see also Ref.
[
14
]
).
The self-energy correction is given by
the sum of the perturbed-orbital (po) and the vertex
+
reducible (vr) parts,

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm se}=\delta z_{\rm se,po}+\delta z_{\rm se,vr}\,.

(4)

The perturbed-orbital part is given by

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm se,po}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n\neq a}\bra{a}\Sigma_{R}(\varepsilon_{a})\ket{n}\,\frac{z_{nb}}{\varepsilon_{a}-\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n\neq b}\frac{z_{an}}{\varepsilon_{b}-\varepsilon_{n}}\bra{n}\Sigma_{R}(\varepsilon_{b})\ket{b}\,,

(5)

where
\Sigma_{R}(\varepsilon)
is the renormalized one-loop self-energy operator,
\Sigma_{R}(\varepsilon)=\Sigma(\varepsilon)-\gamma^{0}\delta m
,
and
\delta m
is the one-loop mass counterterm.
The one-loop self-energy operator is defined by its matrix elements with one-electron wave functions
as
[
15
]

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma(\varepsilon)\ket{b}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,\sum_{n}\frac{\bra{an}I(\omega)\ket{nb}}{\varepsilon-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n}}\,,

(6)

where
u=1-i0
, the summation is performed over the complete spectrum of the Dirac equation and the operator
I(\omega)
describes the
exchange of a virtual photon. In the Feynman gauge,
I(\omega)
is given by

\displaystyle I(\omega,{\bm{r}}_{1},{\bm{r}}_{2})=\alpha\big(1-\bm{\alpha}_{1}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{2}\big)\,\frac{e^{i\sqrt{\omega^{2}+i0}\,r_{12}}}{r_{12}}\,,

(7)

where
\bm{\alpha}
is vector of Dirac matrices and
r_{12}=|{\bm{r}}_{1}-{\bm{r}}_{2}|
.
The vertex
+
reducible contribution is given by

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm se,vr}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}}\frac{z_{n_{1}n_{2}}\,\bra{an_2}I(\omega)\ket{n_1b}}{(\varepsilon_{a}-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n_{1}})(\varepsilon_{b}-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n_{2}})}

\displaystyle-\frac{z_{ab}}{2}\,\sum_{n}\frac{\bra{an}I(\omega)\ket{na}}{(\varepsilon_{a}-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n})^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{z_{ab}}{2}\,\sum_{n}\frac{\bra{bn}I(\omega)\ket{nb}}{(\varepsilon_{b}-\omega-u\varepsilon_{n})^{2}}\Bigg]\,.

(8)

For renormalization purposes, we separate from the vertex
+
reducible contribution
the contribution of free electron proparagors. This contribution is
denoted as the zero-potential part and is calculated in
the momentum space. The remainder (denoted as the many-potential part) contains one or more
interactions with the binding Coulomb potential in electron propagators. It is
ultraviolet finite and calculated in coordinate space. The separation
into the zero-potential and many-potential parts is

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm se,vr}=\delta z_{\rm se,vr}^{(0)}+\delta z_{\rm se,vr}^{(1+)}\,.

(9)

The vacuum-polarization correction is well approximated by its perturbed-orbital part,
\delta z_{\rm vp}\approx\delta z_{\rm vp,po}
, which has the same form as Eq. (
5
),

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm vp,po}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n\neq a}\bra{a}V_{\rm vp}\ket{n}\,\frac{z_{nb}}{\varepsilon_{a}-\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n\neq b}\frac{z_{an}}{\varepsilon_{b}-\varepsilon_{n}}\bra{n}V_{\rm vp}\ket{b}\,,

(10)

where
V_{\rm vp}
is the vacuum-polarization potential, see Ref.
[
14
]
for details.

Our numerical calculations of the self-energy and vacuum-polarization
corrections were carried out for
the Kohn-Sham (KS) local screening potential
[
16
]
.
For representing the electron propagators we numerically solve the
Dirac equation with the nuclear Coulomb potential and the KS
screening potential. The
Green’s function of the Dirac equation in a general
(asymptotically Coulomb) potential was computed by the
method described in Appendix of Ref.
[
17
]
.

Numerical results for QED corrections to the
E1
transition amplitude
are conveniently represented
in terms of the multiplicative function
R_{\rm qed}(Z)
defined as

\displaystyle\delta z_{\rm qed}=z_{ab}\,\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,R_{\rm qed}(Z)\,.

(11)

We thus represent
R_{\rm qed}
as

\displaystyle R_{\rm qed}=R_{\rm se,po}+R_{\rm se,vr}^{(0)}+R_{\rm se,vr}^{(1+)}+R_{\rm vp}\,.

(12)

The QED correction to the radial integral (
2
) has the same form as Eq. (
11
),

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{I}_{\rm qed}(a,b)=\mathcal{I}(a,b)\,\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,R_{\rm qed}(Z)\,.

(13)

Our numerical results for individual QED contributions for Fe
16+
are presented in Table
1
. We note a large cancellation between
the zero-potential and many-potential vertex
+
reducible contributions,
R_{\rm se,vr}^{(0)}
and
R_{\rm se,vr}^{(1+)}
. Separately, these contributions are of the same order as the perturbed-orbital part, but their sum is by about three orders of magnitude smaller. Because of this cancellation, a very careful control of the numerical accuracy is required at intermediate stages of the computation. We also note that the perturbed-orbital contribution
dominates over the vertex
+
reducible part.

II.2
Model QED operator

Here we compare the results of
ab initio
QED calculations with the approximate
results obtained with an effective QED operator within quantum mechanical approach. There were several such operators suggested in the literature
[
13
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
.
These operators are now broadly used for calculations of many-electron systems including atoms

[
21
,
22
,
23
]
, highly-charged ions
[
24
,
25
]
, molecules
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
and superheavy elements
[
29
,
30
]
.

Here we use model QED operator formulated in Ref.
[
19
]

and implemented as the QEDMOD package in Refs.
[
31
,
32
]
.
This approximate treatment does not include the self-energy vertex and reducible corrections
but should approximately reproduce the perturbed-orbital part of the self-energy and vacuum-polarization corrections,

\displaystyle R_{\rm qed}^{\rm mod}\approx R_{\rm se,po}+R_{\rm vp,po}\,.

(14)

Fortunately, for the
E1
transition amplitudes the vertex and reducible corrections are typically small and the QEDMOD operator provides a reasonable approximation for the total QED correction.

First, for the

-

III
Many-electron calculations

In this section we describe our results for the QED corrections to the energies and transition rates in the ten-electron ions Fe
16+
and Ni
18+
. The many-electron
E1
transition amplitude between the states with the total angular momentum
J
and parity
p
can be described by the reduced matrix element. In the L-gauge it can be written in the following form:

E1(J^{\prime}p^{\prime},Jp)\equiv\langle J^{\prime}p^{\prime}||E1||Jp\rangle=\sum_{nlj,n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\rho_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime},nlj}^{1}\\
\times(-1)^{j+l_{\mathrm{max}}-1/2}\sqrt{(2j^{\prime}+1)(2j+1)l_{\mathrm{max}}}\\
\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}l^{\prime}&j^{\prime}&1/2\\
j&l&1\end{array}\right\}\mathcal{I}(n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime},nlj)\,,

(15)

where
\rho_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime},nlj}^{1}
is the reduced transition matrix (RTM) of the tensor rank 1,
\mathcal{I}(n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime},nlj)
is the respective radial integral (
2
), and
l_{\mathrm{max}}=\max(l,l^{\prime})
. RTM of the rank
L
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\rho^{L}_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime},nlj}=(-1)^{J^{\prime}-M^{\prime}}\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{rcc}J^{\prime}&L&J\\
-M^{\prime}&q&M\\
\end{array}\!\!\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{mm^{\prime}}(-1)^{j^{\prime}-m^{\prime}}\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{rcc}j^{\prime}&L&j\\
-m^{\prime}&q&m\\
\end{array}\!\!\right)\rho_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime}m^{\prime},nljm},

where

\displaystyle\rho_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime}m^{\prime},nljm}=\langle J^{\prime}M^{\prime}|a^{\dagger}_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a_{nljm}|JM\rangle\,,

(20)

and
a^{\dagger}_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}j^{\prime}m^{\prime}}
and
a_{nljm}
are creation and annihilation operators respectively.

15
).

Calculations in this work were performed by the configuration interaction (CI) method implemented by the computer package CI-MBPT
[
34
]
.
Calculations for both ions were done in the same manner. We used the basis set
[17spdfg]
, which included 4-component orbitals with principle quantum numbers
n\leq 17
for 9 relativistic partial waves from

to

[
35
,
36
]

10^{6}
determinants.

IV
Conclusions

At present, purely
ab initio
many-electron QED calculations are impossible for complex many-electron systems. Here we show that the effective QED operator QEDMOD
[
19
]
accurately includes most important QED contributions into correlated calculation. Vertex corrections, neglected in this approach, turn out to be very small, both for the
p\to s
and
p\to d
transitions considered here, at least for the neon-like iron and nickel. We estimate the accuracy of the QEDMOD operator for the QED corrections to the
E1
transition amplitudes to be about 10%, same as assumed previously
[
31
,
20
]
for energies. Our present study confirms high accuracy of the recent calculations of these systems in Refs.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

QED corrections to the many-electron
E1
transition amplitudes may be enhanced for the systems with intermediate coupling, where they affect the selection rules implied by a coupling scheme. In the
LS
limit, the singlet to triplet transitions, like
1
S
{}_{0}\to{}^{3}
P
{}_{1}^{o}
, are forbidden, while in the
jj
limit the transitions with
\Delta j>1
, like
p_{1/2}\to d_{5/2}
, are forbidden. Transition from the one coupling to another is the main reason for the large QED corrections to the
2p-3d
transitions in neon-like iron and nickel.

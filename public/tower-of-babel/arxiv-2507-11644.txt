Reopening of the conjecture about the decidability of Quasi-Dense Modal Logics
Olivier Gasquet, The author declare that he has no conflict of interest, No new data were created or analyzed in this study. Data sharing is not applicable

Abstract
In [ 1 ] the question of the decidability of quasi-dense modal logics is answered, and an upper bound in 𝐄𝐗𝐏𝐒𝐏𝐀𝐂𝐄 \mathbf{EXPSPACE} is given. Unfortunately, authors’ intricate proof seems to contain a major flaw that cannot be fixed, leaving the question wide open.



A wrong proof

Even if the conclusion were true (membership in
\mathbf{EXPSPACE}
), the authors’ proof is incorrect with no hope of being fixed easily, and such a longstanding conjecture deserves a better death.

One core notion of the paper is precisely the aptly notion of
core
of a tree
T
. In the case of the article, the tree is infinite, but this is not important.
But it is basic knowledge in the graph theory community that the core of a tree is the complete 2-vertices graph named
K_{2}
since tree are bipartite. And a question arises: how such a poor structure as
K_{2}
could embed the all the information contained in a Kripke model ? Well, it cannot.

1
Analysis of the proof

The proof is far from the usual standard in modal logic and goes as follows. What authors call a
chase
is a tableau seen as a set of graphs whose nodes are labelled with sets of formulas (each element of a chase corresponding to one tableau branch). The intention, as usual, is that nodes are to be interpreted as possible worlds, and their labels to formulas true at this world. Each element of a chase/tableau branch (due to disjunctions there are several branches). Once identified, this is very standard.

Notice that their claim
To put it succinctly, this approach is more parsimonious than it would otherwise be.

is strange, referring to the literature would have saved several pages which are just re-demonstrations of known standard results, in an obfuscated way.

1.1

In order to analyse formulas into their subformulas, still as usual, the authors provide a standard set of rules called here
RR

In addition, in order to take into account quasi-dense axioms (say

Then starting with the set

1.3
Where the devil hides

Up to now, nothing new, all was already known in the basic literature of modal logicians.

Up to now, all seems to be correct though not new. But, let’s go back a few pages backwards, when the authors define the
core
of a graph. The devil is in the details, and here, it is in the footnotes.

1.4
Core of a tree

This notion is a standard one in graph theory, it has several equivalent definition, it is unique up to isomorphism: a subgraph
H
of
G
is a core iff there exists a homomorphism
h
from
G
to
H
, and every homomorphism from
H
to
H
is an isomorphism (hence
H
is minimal in terms of homomorphic embedding).

At this point, a mere footnote says something fundamental for the rest of the paper, it is even its backbone, it says:

We remark that cores of infinite instances do not exist in general
[
2
]
, despite existing
for infinite trees of finite depth.

[
2
]
indeed says that core do not exist in general for infinite graphs. The fact that cores do exist for trees (finite or not) is not mentioned, because it is folklore. Every graph theorist knows that the core of a tree is simply the 2-vertices complete graph named
K_{2}
which consists in two nodes
a
and
b
, and two edges
(a,b)
and
(b,a)
. And this is the case for all bipartite graphs, which trees are. One will easily check that the desired homomorphism is
h(x)=a
if
x
is at even distance from the root of the tree, and
h(x)=b
otherwise (see https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_(graph_theory), no better source found for this folklore fact). This sounds strange, as one may found it difficult to imagine how a 2-vertices graph could encode a whole Kripke model. The devil will show below but one can already imagine what’s wrong: this homomorphism identifies nodes that are inconsistent, as would do a filtration only involving two equivalence classes.

1.5
The devil’s hiding place

Let us go again forward to where we were, at definition 18, authors define the
unravel
operation by
core\circ trim\circ unfold
(the index
i
is not important), and make the central wrong statement at:

Observation 20: if
I
validates the rules of
{\cal R}_{\phi}
then so does
unravel(I)
.

Before to exhibit an example, let us summarize the reason why it is false.

Recall that
core(G)
is always
K_{2}
, intuitively speaking, the sets of all worlds homomorphically mapped on the same vertex will be merged into one. Of course, for most of the rules, this is harmless, since each rule applies on only one literal, but it is not true for the rule of clash which involves two nodes that may be inconsistent.

Suppose
I
contains a node
a
, a literal
P_{\phi}(a)
, a node
b
and
P_{\neg\phi}(b)
and
h(a)=h(b)
, then
unravel(I)
now contains
P_{\phi}(h(a))
and
P_{\neg\phi}(h(a))
, but it does not contain
\bot
(or
contr
): of course, since rules are not applied again on the result of
unravel
. It does not contain
\bot
, but is nevertheless inconsistent.

All in all, the authors wrongly assume that this homomorphism between frames (from their structure to
K_{2}
) is in fact a homomorphism between models which would preserve satisfiability and provides something like a filtration of their infinite tree model.

By the way, w.r.t. Observation 20, the proof itself cannot be false since, indeed, there is no such proof. Instead one can only read:

core also
trivially
preserves the satisfaction of the rule.

Unfortunately, it does not. The devil. In the details.

1.6
An example

The attentive reader is probably already convinced of the flaw, but let’s run an example. The simplest one is the case where
{\cal R}

\mathbf{K}

Let us start with the formula
\neg p\wedge\Diamond\Diamond p
which is, of course,
\mathbf{K}

Here, we would start the chase/tableau with:

, and after a fair application of the rules of

In this precise example, operations

For the same reason, lemma 27 which says that the existence of a template (a homomorphic image of a single open tableau branch) not containing

The last lemma 31 is false too. At the light of the above, it says that there exists a tableau branch

1.7
Is fix possible ?

As said above, the whole approach developed by the authors mainly consists in reusing well-known facts, it is unlikely that without something really new they could fix it. Even if the authors would try to embed their infinite tree in another finite tree rather than
K_{2}
, they would still have to prove that this can be done while preserving consistency. And this is precisely what many logicians have tried to do for 40 years.

2
Conclusion

The conjecture about decidability of quasi-dense modal logics reopens.

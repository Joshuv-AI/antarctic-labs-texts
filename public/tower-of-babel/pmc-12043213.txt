Authors, reviewers and editorial team – “Let’s work together to make it a timely affair”
Authors: Baljeet Singh
Published: 2024

As we draw closer to the end of year 2024, it’s time to have some introspection of our work and the progress the Journal of Indian Society of Periodontology (JISP) has made this year.

Due to circumstances beyond the control of the previous editorial team and some compelling reasons, my team began its tenure with a backlog. With some good help and guidance from my immediate predecessor Dr. Ashish Kumar, the current editorial team, despite some initial hickups, could slowly manage to bring down the lag period.

After assuming the charge of editor, JISP and having got to know the intricacies involved in the job, one thing that I have realized is that this seemingly easy job for the authors and readers is no cake walk for the editorial team.

There has hardly been a day since I have taken over the office of editor, JISP that I did not get an e-mail or message from harried authors asking the status or reasons for delay in the processing of their manuscripts.

Let me make it clear to all the authors/readers that there has not been a single day in my tenure when the JISP editorial team did not work to bring out quality articles for you all.

There are various reasons for the current delay in the processing of the manuscripts. Important ones being the inescapable backlog (already discussed), reviewers, including some very senior ones not responding in due time, delay in submitting the revised manuscripts by the authors etc. To hasten up the processing and save some time, often the editorial team had to do the work what authors were supposed to do. In my opening remarks as an editor, I had mentioned regarding poor sense of English grammar, spellings, verbs, tenses etc. as a regular cause of rejection/delay of manuscripts. Believe me, it is a herculean task for the editorial team to deal with such manuscripts having avoidable mistakes. This definitely adds to the evadable delay in the processing of a manuscript.

Processing of a manuscript is an arduous task for the editorial team. Mere acceptance of a manuscript for processing is no guarantee that a manuscript will get published in the journal. For those who are not very familiar with steps in the processing of a manuscript at JISP, the stages of processing have been briefly explained in the form of a flow chart [Figure 1].

Steps in processing of a manuscript

I can understand, how difficult it must be for someone to face the rejection of a seemingly well prepared manuscript. At JISP, to ensure the quality of the manuscripts that are published and to maintain its standards, many a times, the editorial team rejects the manuscript even during the final stages of processing also. If the editorial team is not satisfied, there can be multiple rounds of revisions for a single manuscript. The average acceptance rate of manuscripts at JISP is 16-17% and a manuscript can be rejected at any stage of processing.

During my journey of a year as editor of this prestigious journal, I have been ably supported by Business Manager, Dr. Vikas Jindal; Assistant Editor, Dr. Basant Kaur Aulakh; in coming Assistant Editor, Dr. Abhinav Bhasker, my postgraduate students, ISP head office, Medknow publishing house and advertising companies. I am grateful to all the reviewers, authors and readers for their invaluable support and contributions.

It was a matter of pride for the Team JISP and Indian Society of Periodontology (ISP) head office for organizing the first ever reviewers training meet by a dental speciality journal in India, at Chandigarh. Next year we plan to organize some more such programs in other parts of the country.

The path of success is often winding and steep, filled with obstacles and challenges. We at JISP are committed to provide our readers with quality research against all the odds.

“Losers stop when they fail, winners fail till they succeed”

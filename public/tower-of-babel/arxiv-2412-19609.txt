Bidding Games on Markov Decision Processes with Quantitative Reachability Objectives
Guy Avni, Martin Kurečka, Kaushik Mallik, Petr Novotný, Suman Sadhukhan

Abstract
. Graph games are fundamental in strategic reasoning of multi-agent systems and their environments. We study a new family of graph games which combine stochastic environmental uncertainties and auction-based interactions among the agents, formalized as bidding games on (finite) Markov decision processes (MDP). Normally, on MDPs, a single decision-maker chooses a sequence of actions, producing a probability distribution over infinite paths. In bidding games on MDPs, two players—called the reachability and safety players—bid for the privilege of choosing the next action at each step. The reachability player’s goal is to maximize the probability of reaching a target vertex, whereas the safety player’s goal is to minimize it. These games generalize traditional bidding games on graphs , and the existing analysis techniques do not extend. For instance, the central property of traditional bidding games is the existence of a threshold budget, which is a necessary and sufficient budget to guarantee winning for the reachability player. For MDPs, the threshold becomes a relation between the budgets and probabilities of reaching the target. We devise value-iteration algorithms that approximate thresholds and optimal policies for general MDPs, and compute the exact solutions for acyclic MDPs, and show that finding thresholds is at least as hard as solving simple-stochastic games .

Applications

Our results have an immediate application in

(

In this framework, we are given an arena, modeling the environment, a pair of specifications

As zero-sum bidding games have been studied only on graphs, auction-based scheduling is restricted to graph arenas until now.
Our work will lead to a decentralized solution of multi-objective reachability on MDPs using auction-based scheduling.

Bidding games on graphs

We briefly survey results on traditional bidding games on graphs

We summarize our results below.
We consider the problem of deciding if the reachability player can reach the target in a given MDP with a given probability
p
using a given budget
B
.
(I)
For
general MDPs
, the reachability problem remains open. Under the assumption that
(B,p)
is not exactly on the threshold, we show that the problem is decidable. The time and space complexity of our algorithm depends on the distance

of
(B,p)
from the threshold (infinity norm), the minimum probability

, and the number of vertices

(II)
For

The above assumption that the point

E.g. for “classical” (non-bidding) multi-objective stochastic games, algorithms for determining the winning player assume that the target payoff vector does not lie on the boundary of the Pareto set of achievable payoffs
(

Preliminaries of Markov Decision Processes (MDP)

Syntax.
An MDP is a tuple
\left\langle V,{V_{\mathsf{c}}},{V_{\mathsf{r}}},E,\delta\right\rangle
,
where
V
is a finite set of vertices,

{V_{\mathsf{c}}}
and
{V_{\mathsf{r}}}
are the
control
and
random
vertices such that
{V_{\mathsf{c}}}\cup{V_{\mathsf{r}}}=V
and
{V_{\mathsf{c}}}\cap{V_{\mathsf{r}}}=\emptyset
,

E\colon{V_{\mathsf{c}}}\to 2^{V_{\mathsf{r}}}
is the
control transition function
, and

\delta\colon{V_{\mathsf{r}}}\to\Delta({V_{\mathsf{c}}})
is the
random transition function
, where
\Delta({V_{\mathsf{c}}})
is the set of all probability distributions over
{V_{\mathsf{c}}}
.
The set of
successors
of vertex
v
will be denoted as
\mathit{Succ}(v)
, where
\mathit{Succ}(v)\coloneqq E(v)
if
v\in{V_{\mathsf{c}}}
and
\mathit{Succ}(v)\coloneqq\left\{v^{\prime}\in V\mid\delta(v)(v^{\prime})>0\right\}
if
v\in{V_{\mathsf{r}}}
.
A vertex
v
is called
sink
if
\mathit{Succ}(v)=\left\{v\right\}
.

Convention for figures.
MDPs are depicted as transition diagrams with circular nodes representing control vertices and diamond-shaped nodes representing random vertices.
The target vertices are depicted in double circles.
Random transitions with
uniform
probability distributions are marked using an arc between them.

Semantics.

Semantics of MDPs are summarized below; details can be found in standard textbooks
(
Puterman, 1990
)
.
A
path
of an MDP starting at a given vertex
v\in V
is a sequence
v^{0}v^{1}\ldots
with
v^{0}=v
and every
v^{i>0}
being a successor of
v^{i-1}
.
Paths can be either finite or infinite.
We write
\mathit{Paths_{\mathsf{fin}}}(M)
and
\mathit{Paths_{\mathsf{inf}}}(M)
to denote, respectively, the set of all finite and infinite paths, and write
\mathit{Paths}_{\mathsf{fin}}^{\mathsf{c}}(M)
to denote the set of all finite paths that end in a control vertex.
A
scheduler
is a function
\theta\colon\mathit{Paths}_{\mathsf{fin}}^{\mathsf{c}}(M)\to V
mapping every finite path
\rho=v^{0}\ldots v^{k}
ending at the control vertex
v^{k}\in{V_{\mathsf{c}}}
to one of its successors; i.e.,
\theta(\rho)\in E(v^{k})
.
Every scheduler
\theta
induces a unique probability distribution
\mathbb{P}_{v}^{M,\theta}(\cdot)
over the paths of
M
with initial vertex
v
.

Specifications.

A
specification

\varphi
over an MDP
M
is a set of infinite paths of
M
.
We will consider reachability and safety specifications of both bounded and unbounded variants, defined below.
Given a set of vertices
T\subseteq V
called the
target
vertices, and an integer
h>0
, the
bounded-horizon reachability
specification is the set of paths that visit
T

in at most
h
steps
, i.e.,
\mathit{Reach}^{M,h}(T)\coloneqq\left\{v^{0}v^{1}\ldots\in\mathit{Paths_{\mathsf{inf}}}(M)\mid\exists i\leq h\;.\;v^{i}\in T\right\}
. The (unbounded)
reachability
specification is the set of paths that
eventually
visit
T
; i.e.,
\mathit{Reach}^{M}(T)\coloneqq\bigcup_{h}\mathit{Reach}^{M,h}(T)
.
Dually, given a set of vertices
U\subseteq V
called the
unsafe
vertices, and a number
h>0
, the
bounded-horizon safety
specification is the set of paths that avoid
U

for at least
h
steps
, i.e.,
\mathit{Safe}^{M,h}(U)\coloneqq\left\{v^{0}v^{1}\ldots\in\mathit{Paths_{\mathsf{inf}}}(M)\mid\forall i\leq h\;.\;v^{i}\notin U\right\}
. The (unbounded)
safety
specification is the set of paths that
always
avoid
U
; i.e.,
\mathit{Safe}^{M}(U)\coloneqq\bigcap_{h}\mathit{Safe}^{M,h}(U)
.
Reachability and safety specifications—with bounded and unbounded horizons—are complementary to each other, i.e., for every
h>0
,
\mathit{Reach}^{M,h}(T)=V^{\omega}\setminus\mathit{Safe}^{M,h}(T)
, and
\mathit{Reach}^{M}(T)=V^{\omega}\setminus\mathit{Safe}^{M}(T)
.

3.
Bidding Games on MDP-s

On a given
M
, we consider a zero-sum “token game” between two players, who will be referred to as the
reachability
and
safety
players.
Initially, the token is placed in a given
initial
vertex, and the players are allocated budgets (positive real numbers) whose sum is
1
.
As convention, we will only specify the reachability player’s budget as
B
, and the safety player’s budget will be implicit (i.e.,
1-B
).

The game is played as follows.
When the token is in a control vertex
v
, the players independently and simultaneously propose their bids which may not exceed their current budgets.
Whoever bids higher pays his bid amount to the other player and moves the token to one of
v
’s successors.
The budgets are updated accordingly:
assuming the reachability player’s budget at
v
was
B
, if he wins by bidding
b_{R}
, his new budget will reduce to
B-b_{R}
, and if the safety player wins by bidding
b_{S}
, the reachability player’s new budget will increase to
B+b_{S}
.
1
1

1

This bidding mechanism is known as Richman bidding in the literature. Other bidding mechanisms also exist, but they are left as part of future works.

On the other hand, when the token is in a random vertex
v
, it moves to a successor
w
with probability
\delta(v)(w)
, and the budgets of the players remain unaffected.

The game continues in this fashion forever, generating an infinite path traversed by the token.
Given a set of target vertices
T
, the goal of the reachability player is to maximize the probability that the token eventually reaches
T
from the initial vertex, while the goal of the safety player is to minimize this probability.

Policies and paths.

We formalize bidding games on MDPs as follows.
A policy of a player is a function of the form
[0,1]\times\mathit{Paths}_{\mathsf{fin}}^{\mathsf{c}}(M)\to[0,1]\times V

and a successor of

.
We will write

Suppose we are given an initial vertex

and an initial budget

We will call the pair

•

if

Winning conditions.

Let
\left\langle v,B\right\rangle
be an initial configuration,
\varphi
be a reachability specification (bounded or unbounded), and
p\in[0,1]
be the
required probability
for the reachability player to satisfy
\varphi
.
A
winning policy of the reachability player
is a policy
\sigma
such that for every policy
\tau
of the safety player, it holds that
\mathbb{P}_{v,B}^{\sigma,\tau}(\varphi)\geq p
.
Dually, a
winning policy of the safety player
is a policy
\tau
such that for every policy
\sigma
of the reachability player, it holds that
\mathbb{P}_{v,B}^{\sigma,\tau}(\varphi)\leq p
.
We will write
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p,v,\varphi)
and
\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(B,p,v,\varphi)
to denote the sets of winning policies for reachability and safety players, respectively.
If
\varphi
is clear, we will simply write
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p,v)
and
\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(B,p,v)
.

Thresholds.

In traditional reachability bidding games on graphs, where probabilities are unnecessary, the threshold of a vertex
v
is the budget
B
such that the reachability player wins from
v
with every budget
B^{\prime}>B
, and loses with every budget
B^{\prime}<B
.
In bidding games on MDPs, thresholds generalize to relations over budgets and probabilities:
The threshold of
v
is the set of all pairs
(B,p)
such that the reachability player wins with every budget greater than
B
and required probability less than
p
, and loses with every budget less than
B
and required probability larger than
p
.

Definition 3.1 (Threshold).

For a given vertex
v
, the
threshold
of
v
, written
\mathit{Th}_{v}
,
is the set of all pairs
(B,p)
such that
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B^{\prime},p^{\prime},v)
is nonempty whenever
B^{\prime}>B
and
p^{\prime}<p
,
and
\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(B^{\prime},p^{\prime},v)
is nonempty whenever
B^{\prime}<B
and
p^{\prime}>p
.

A central question in traditional bidding games is whether thresholds exist, because then it can be
determined
which of the players will win based on the budget allocation, as long as the budget is not exactly equal to the threshold.
In our case, the existence question of thresholds generalizes to the question of
whether the threshold completely separates

the winning points of the two players.

Definition 3.2 (Completely separating thresholds).

The threshold of
v
is
completely separating
if for
every point
(B,p)\notin\mathit{Th}_{v}
,

•

there exists
(B^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\mathit{Th}_{v}

such that either
B<B^{\prime}
and
p>p^{\prime}
, or
B>B^{\prime}
and
p<p^{\prime}
, and

•

exactly one of the sets
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p,v)
and
\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(B,p,v)
is nonempty.

The algorithmic question.

We define
problem instances
as tuples of the form
\left\langle M,v,T,B,p\right\rangle
, where
M
is an MDP,
\left\langle v,B\right\rangle
is the initial configuration,
T
is the target, and

is the required probability of satisfying the reachability specification

The subject of this paper is how to decide who wins in a given problem instance.

Problem 1 (Quantitative reachability).

Let
\left\langle M,v,T,B,p\right\rangle
be a problem instance.
For a given
j\in\left\{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{S}\right\}
, decide if the set
\Pi_{j}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M}(T))
is nonempty.

If
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M}(T))\neq\emptyset
, our decision procedure will produce the witness winning policy for the reachability player as a byproduct; construction of winning policies for the safety player is solved for acyclic MDPs, and remains open for general MDPs.
We will assume that
T
is a set of sinks, which is without loss of any generality since the game ends as soon as
T
is reached.

Theorem 4.1.

Let
\left\langle M,v,T,B,p\right\rangle

, and

\mathit{val}

\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}

\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}

Otherwise, assume

.

yielding the new budget

which is nonempty by the induction hypothesis (here

\mathsf{R}

is

).

Now consider the case when

for each

for each

∎

The computability of the values at each step follows from their finite representations.
In particular, the sets

Lemma 4.2.

Let

be an MDP,

be target vertices, and

For every

5.
From Bounded to Unbounded Horizon

5.1.
Limiting Behavior of Value Iteration

If we continue the bounded-horizon value iteration for increasing horizon, we obtain the following values in the limit:

\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}\coloneqq\mathsf{cl}\left[{\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}}\right]\text{ and }\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i},

where
\mathsf{cl}\left[{S}\right]
denotes the closure of a set

in the Euclidean metric; note that
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
is closed by construction.
In this section, we establish a connection between
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v},\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
and the true values that are winning for the respective player in the unbounded horizon setting.
In particular, we relate the limit sets to the threshold
\mathit{Th}_{v}
, and present an algorithm for Prob.
1
that runs in doubly exponential time.
Our proof also constructs the winning policy for the reachability player, if one exists; the construction of the safety player’s winning policy remains open.
In the following, we will use the notation

to denote the interior of the set

.

Theorem 5.1.

Let

The following hold:

(2)

Let

.
Every reachability policy winning on a finite horizon is, in particular, winning on the infinite horizon,

Now suppose

implying there also exists

5.2.
On the Decidability of the Quantitative Reachability Problem

From Thm.
5.1
, it follows that Problem

reduces to the membership problem of deciding which of the sets
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
and
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
contain the given pair

.
Unfortunately, the decidability of this question remains open.
As far as the value iteration is concerned, the difficulty with decidability comes from the situation when
(B,p)\in\mathit{Th}_{v}
, because the true
\mathit{Th}_{v}
is obtained only in the limit.
Therefore we are not guaranteed to decide the membership of
(B,p)
in finite time by only using value iteration.
To circumvent this “edge case,” we make the following assumption.

Assumption 1.

Let
\left\langle M,v,T,B,p\right\rangle
be a problem instance.
The given pair
(B,p)
does not belong to
\mathit{Th}_{v}
.

In other words, we assume that the pair

lies either in the

of
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{*}
or in the

of
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{*}
.
For every point
(B,p)\in\left\langle{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{*}}\right\rangle
, Lem.
5.3
below provides an upper bound on the iteration index after which
(B,p)
will be included inside
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
; and excluded from
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
, respectively.

We first introduce some notation.
We will use
\delta_{\min}
to denote the minimum transition probability in
M
; if all transitions are non-probabilistic, we will set
\delta_{\min}=\frac{1}{2}
.
We further denote by

and to the set

Of importance is the situation when either

is called the

’s threshold
.
Given the sets

, the Hausdorff distance

between

is the largest distance one needs to travel starting from any point in either

to reach the closest point in the other set.
Formally,

\displaystyle d_{h}(X,Y)\coloneqq\max\left\{\max_{y\in Y}\min_{x\in X}\lVert x-y\rVert_{\infty},\max_{x\in X}\min_{y\in Y}\lVert x-y\rVert_{\infty}\right\}.

It can be shown that there exist optimal policies that admit a short path to

regardless of the game history,
and that after exponentially many random choices, such a path is traversed with sufficiently high probability.
This idea yields the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3.

.

paves the way to the following algorithm for Prob.

.

\mathcal{O}\left(\log\left(1/\epsilon\right)|V|\delta_{\min}^{-2|V|}\right)

iterations, yielding the space and time complexity

|V|^{\mathcal{O}\left(\log\left(1/\epsilon\right)|V|\delta_{\min}^{-2|V|}\right)}.

5.3.
Abstraction for Complexity Reduction

To reduce the high computational complexity of
AlgExact
, we present an approximation algorithm that is sound and complete
in the sense of Thm.
5.4
although its running time is only exponential in
|V|
.
The high complexity of
AlgExact
stems from the arbitrarily high precision of the value set representations.
The idea of our algorithm is to discretize the two dimensions—budget and probability—using a fixed finite precision.
The approximate value iteration begins by rounding off
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{0}
or
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{0}
(whether rounding up or down depends on the dimension and the value set) to the closest discrete level along each dimension.
At each subsequent step, first the
\mathcal{T}_{v}
operator (defined in (
4
) and (
3
)) is applied on the approximate values from the previous iteration.
Since
\mathcal{T}_{v}
may produce value sets with higher precision than the chosen one,
the obtained sets are further
rounded off again to the closest discrete level along each dimension.
We show that the procedure yields a sufficiently low approximation error that depends on the number of iterations and the chosen precision.
We formalize the abstract safety value iteration below; the abstract reachability values are later obtained through duality.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
be a constant parameter, called the
grid size
, such that
1/\alpha
is an integer.
We define a uniform rectangular grid
X
on the value space
[0,1]^{2}
such that the space is divided in squares of length
\alpha
, i.e.,

X\coloneqq\left\{[p\alpha,(p+1)\alpha]\times[q\alpha,(q+1)\alpha]\mid 0\leq p,q<1/\alpha\right\}.

Given a relation
S\subseteq[0,1]^{2}
over the budgets and probabilities, define
\Delta_{\alpha}(S)
as the
over-approximation
of
S
using the elements of
X
, i.e.,
\Delta_{\alpha}(S)\coloneqq\bigcup\left\{x\in X\mid x\cap S\neq\emptyset\right\}
.

For every
v\in V
, the abstract safety value iteration computes the decreasing (with respect to “
\subseteq
”) sequence
\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{0},\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{1},\ldots

defined as below:

\displaystyle\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{0}\coloneqq\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{0}),

\displaystyle\forall i>0:\quad

\displaystyle\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i}\coloneqq\Delta_{\alpha}\circ\mathcal{T}_{v}\left(\left\{\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{w}^{i-1}\mid w\in\mathit{Succ}(v)\right\}\right).

Henceforth, we will refer to
\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i}
as
abstract
values, and
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}

as
concrete
values.
The following lemma shows that the abstract values always over-approximate the concrete values, and the approximation error remain bounded by
\alpha(i+1)
for every
i
.
The proof is provided in the supplementary material.

Lemma 5.5.

For every
v\in V
and every
i
,
\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i}\supseteq\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
, and, moreover,
d_{h}(\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i},\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i})\leq\alpha(i+1)
.
The abstract value
\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i}
can be computed in
2|V|^{2}i/\alpha
time and
2|V|/\alpha
space.

Given an abstract safety value, we can define an abstract reachability value
\llbracket{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i}\coloneqq[0,1]^{2}\setminus\llbracket{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i}\cup\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{0}
.
Note that
\llbracket{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i}
is under-approximation of
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
since both, the complement of
\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i}
and
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{0}
, are subsets of
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
.
Moreover, whenever
d_{h}\left(\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i},\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}\right)\leq\overline{\epsilon}
then

d_{h}\left(\llbracket{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{i},\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}\right)\leq\overline{\epsilon}
.

We now present
AlgApprox
, an improved algorithm for Prob.
1
.

AlgApprox
uses the same principle as
AlgExact
, but instead of using the exact value iteration, uses the approximate counterpart along with an iterated refinement of the resolution of the grid.

For suitable

from Lemma

, repeat for

:

(1)

Set

,

(2)

Compute
\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{n}

\llbracket{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{n}

(3)

If
(B,p)\in\llbracket{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{v}^{n}

(B,p)
is in
\left\langle{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{*}}\right\rangle

(4)

If

(B,p)
is in
\left\langle{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{*}}\right\rangle

The algorithm
AlgApprox
iteratively looks for a precision

5.4.
Lower Complexity Bounds

We show that the exact problem for general MDPs is at least as hard as computing values in simple stochastic games (SSG), which is known to be in NP
\cap
co-NP and whether it belongs to P remains to be a long-standing open problem.

SSGs are generalizations of MDPs where the control vertices are partitioned into two sets based on their (fixed) ownerships by two players, referred to as Player
0
and Player
1
.
Formally, an SSG is a tuple
\left\langle V,V_{0},V_{1},{V_{\mathsf{r}}},E,\delta\right\rangle
, where
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
are the vertices owned by Player
0
and Player
1
respectively, and
V
,
{V_{\mathsf{r}}}
,
E
, and
\delta
are adapted from their definitions in MDPs as follows:
V=V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup{V_{\mathsf{r}}}
,
E\colon V_{0}\cup V_{1}\to{V_{\mathsf{r}}}
, and
\delta\colon{V_{\mathsf{r}}}\to\Delta(V_{0}\cup V_{1})
.
Without loss of generality, we assume every SSG has a designated initial vertex which is in
V_{1}
and every path belongs to
(V_{1}{V_{\mathsf{r}}}V_{0}{V_{\mathsf{r}}})^{\omega}
,
i.e., each kind of vertices evenly alternate (achievable by polynomial blowup).

A Player
j

policy
is a function that maps every vertex
v\in V_{j}
to one of
v
’s successors.
Suppose, Player
0
is the reachability player trying to reach a given target
T_{G}\subseteq V
, and Player
1
is the safety player trying to avoid it.
The
value
of a vertex
v
is the maximum probability
p
such that the reachability player can reach
T_{G}
with probability at least
p
against any policy of the safety player.
We refer to Condon
(
Condon, 1990
)
for detailed formal definitions.

Theorem 5.7.

For any SSG
G
with initial vertex
v
, there exists a bidding game
G_{B}
whose size is polynomial in the size of
G
, and such that the value of the reachability objective in
G

is equal to the minimal
p
that satisfies
(\frac{1}{3},p)\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
in
G_{B}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be an SSG with vertices
V_{G}
.
The structure of
G_{B}
is the same as that of
G

except that the control vertices have an extra edge appended.
Namely, for every
v\in V_{0}
, there is a new (non-target) sink
v^{\prime}
with an edge
(v,v^{\prime})
,
and for every
v\in V_{1}
, there is a new target sink
v^{\prime}
with an edge
(v,v^{\prime})
.
We set
{V_{\mathsf{c}}}
to be the union of
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
, and
T
to be
T_{G}
together with the new targets.

Suppose the value of
G
at the initial vertex
v
is
p
, and
let
q
be minimal such that
\left(1/3,q\right)\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
.
We first show that
q\leq p
. We will prove that
\left(1/3-s,p\right)
belongs to
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
for every
s>0
,
since this implies
\left(1/3,p\right)\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
as
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
is closed.
By definition of
p
, there exists a policy
\pi_{1}
of Player
1
in
G
that guarantees avoiding
T_{G}
with probability at least
1-p
against any policy of Player
0
.
We use
\pi_{1}
to construct a policy
\tau
in
G_{B}
.
The policy
\tau
is defined as follows:
For every
w\in V_{1}
,
\tau(B,w)=(1/3,\pi_{1}(w))
, and for every
w\in V_{0}
,
\tau(B,w)=(1/3,w^{\prime})
where
w^{\prime}
is the new sink.
Note that since the initial budget of the reachability player is less than
1/3
and the control vertices alternate evenly,
his budget will be less than
1/3
on every visit of
V_{1}
.
Hence the reachability player loses every bidding in

, regardless of his policy.
Therefore, for every
\sigma
, the game ends in a new non-target sink or forever stays in
V_{G}
,
where
\tau
playes as
\pi_{1}
in
G
. Hence the token avoids
T
with probability at least
1-p
.

We next show that for every
p^{\prime}<p
,
there exists
s>0
such that
\left(1/3-s,p^{\prime}\right)\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
.
Since
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
is
\prec
-downward closed,
this implies
(1/3,p^{\prime\prime})\in\left\langle{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}}\right\rangle
for every
p^{\prime\prime}<p^{\prime}
.
Since
p^{\prime}
ranges over all reals less than
p
, we derive
(1/3,p^{\prime\prime})\in\left\langle{\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{s}}\right\rangle

.
By definition of
p
, there exists a policy
\pi_{0}
of Player
0
in

p

\pi_{1}
.
For every
p^{\prime}<p

\pi_{0}

In

I.e.
\sigma(B,w)=(B,w^{\prime})

In

6.
Bidding Games on Restricted MDP-s

Now we consider the special cases of Prob.
1
for acyclic and tree-shaped MDPs, which are MDPs whose underlying transition graphs are acyclic (with loops on sinks) and rooted tree, respectively.

6.1.
Acyclic MDPs

It is easy to see that, contrary to general MDPs (with cycles), the value iteration algorithm (from Sec.
5
) converges in at most
|V|
iterations for acyclic MDPs.
This implies that
AlgExact
will always terminate in at most
|V|
iterations.

Lemma 6.1.

For acyclic MDPs, for every vertex
v
,
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|}_{v}=\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
and
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|}_{v}=\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
.

Lem.
6.1
implies that Prob.
1
is in EXPTIME for acyclic MDPs.

Theorem 6.2.

For acyclic MDPs,
AlgExact
is a sound and complete algorithm for Prob.
1

and terminates in at most
|V|
iterations, yielding the space and time complexity
\mathcal{O}\left(|V|^{|V|}\right)
.

6.2.
Tree-Shaped MDPs

In case the MDP is tree-shaped, we can solve Prob.
1
in NP
\cap
co-NP.
The main idea of the proof is to find a certificate
of the fact that
(B,p)
belongs to
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|}_{v}
. According to the inductive definition of
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|}_{v}
by (
3
) and (
4
),
the presence of
(B,p)
in
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|}_{v}
can be witnessed by a point
(B_{w},p_{w})\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|-1}_{w}
for every

.
A similar witness can be constructed to show that each of the points
(B_{w},p_{w})
belongs to
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|-1}_{w}
, and the process can be repeated recursively up to the base case
(B_{u},p_{u})\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{u}^{0}
.
Therefore, whenever
(B,p)
belongs to
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
, there exists a certificate of this fact in the form of a finite set of points satisfying the relations in (
3
) and (
4
).
If
M
is a tree, the certificate moreover contains a single point
(B_{u},p_{u})
for every vertex
u\in V
.
The whole certificate then satisfies the following constraints:

\displaystyle B_{v}

\displaystyle\leq B,\penalty\ p_{v}\geq p

\displaystyle\begin{subarray}{c}\forall u\in{V_{\mathsf{c}}}\\
\exists u^{-},u^{+}\in\mathit{Succ}(u)\end{subarray}:\penalty\ B_{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{u^{-}}+B_{u^{+}}}{2},\penalty\ p_{u}\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\min_{w\in\mathit{Succ}(u)}\!p_{w},B_{u^{+}}\geq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\max_{w\in\mathit{Succ}(u)}\!\!\!\!B_{w}

\displaystyle\forall u\in{V_{\mathsf{r}}}:\penalty\ B_{u}

\displaystyle\geq\!\!\!\!\!\max_{w\in\mathit{Succ}(u)}B_{w},\penalty\ p_{u}=\sum_{w\in\mathit{Succ}(u)}\delta(u)(w)\cdot p_{w}

\displaystyle\forall t\in T:\penalty\ 0\leq B_{t}

\displaystyle\leq 1,\penalty\ 0\leq p_{t}\leq 1

\displaystyle\forall z\in Z:\penalty\ B_{z}

\displaystyle=1\text{ or }\penalty\ p_{z}=0,

where

(B,p)
belongs to
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
if and only if there is a choice that makes the linear program feasible.
The same idea can be used to prove a point belongs to
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}

Theorem 6.3.

For tree-shaped MDPs, Prob.
1
is in NP
\cap
co-NP.

7.
Conclusions and Future Work

We studied bidding games on MDPs with quantitative reachability and safety specifications.
We show that thresholds are binary relations over budgets and probabilities.
This makes their computation significantly more challenging than traditional bidding games on graphs, for which thresholds are scalars (budgets).
We developed a new value iteration algorithm for approximating the threshold up to arbitrary precision, and showed how it can be used to decide whether a given initial budget
B
suffices to win w.p. at least
p
, assuming
(B,p)
is not on the threshold (Assump.
1
). In acyclic and tree-shaped MDPs, Assump.
1
is not required and the decision procedure becomes significantly more efficient.

A number of questions remain open: Is Prob.
1
decidable without Assump.
1
? What are the exact complexities? (There is a big gap between the upper and lower complexity bounds.)
Furthermore, several interesting extensions can be considered, namely extensions to richer classes of specifications (like
\omega
-regular and mean-payoff) and extensions to different forms of bidding mechanisms (like poorman and taxman, both with and without charging).
Another interesting question is the equivalence with stochastic models (recall that bidding games are equivalent to random-turn games).
This is still unclear, because even if the threshold budget were simulated by random turn assignments, this randomness would not “blend” with the existing randomness (in the random transitions) in the MDP, and we would obtain stochastic games with two sources of probabilities, which have not been studied to the best of our knowledge.
Finally, the foundation of auction-based scheduling
(
Avni
et al., 2024b
)
on MDPs is now ready, and it will be interesting to investigate how policy synthesis for multi-objective MDPs can benefit from it.

. We only consider the case
v\in{V_{\mathsf{c}}}
since the proof for
v\in{V_{\mathsf{r}}}
is analogous.
By the above argument, the operator
\Delta_{\alpha}
will not increase the distance by more than
\alpha
, hence it is enough
to show that
d_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{v}\left(\left\{\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{w}^{i-1}\mid w\in\mathit{Succ}(v)\right\}\right),\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i})\leq\alpha i
.
Namely, for every point
(B,p)\in\mathcal{T}_{v}\left(\left\{\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{w}^{i-1}\mid w\in\mathit{Succ}(v)\right\}\right)
, we have to prove the existence of a point
(B^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
such that
\lVert(B,p)-(B^{\prime},p^{\prime})\rVert_{\infty}\leq\alpha i

(the other direction is trivial since the two sets are in an inclusion).
By definition of
\mathcal{T}_{v}
, the point
(B,p)
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{v}\left(\left\{\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket_{w}^{i-1}\mid w\in\mathit{Succ}(v)\right\}\right)
only if there exist
(B_{-},p)\in\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket^{i-1}_{w_{-}}
for some
w_{-}\in\mathit{Succ}(v)
and
(B_{+},p)\in\llbracket{\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}}\rrbracket^{i-1}_{w_{+}}
for every
w_{+}\in\mathit{Succ}(v)

such that
B=\frac{B_{+}+B_{-}}{2}
.
Further, by the induction hypothesis, it holds
d_{\infty}\left((B_{-},p),\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w}^{i-1}\right)\leq\alpha i
and
d_{\infty}\left((B_{+},p),\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w}^{i-1}\right)\leq\alpha i
for every
w\in\mathit{Succ}(v)
.
These inequalities are witnessed by points
(B_{w_{-}},p_{w_{-}})\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w_{-}}^{i-1}
and
(B_{w_{+}},p_{w_{+}})\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w_{+}}^{i-1}
for every
w_{+}
, respectively.
Let
p_{\max}
be maximal value among
p_{w_{-}}
and all
p_{w_{+}}
, and
B_{\min}
be the minimal value among all
B_{w_{+}}
.
Since the sets
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w_{-}}^{i-1}
and
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w_{+}}^{i-1}
are
\prec
-upward closed,
it holds that
(B_{w_{-}},p_{\max})\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w_{-}}^{i-1}
and
(B_{\min},p_{\max})\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w_{+}}^{i-1}
for every
w_{+}
.
Since all differences
\lvert p-p_{\max}\rvert
,
\lvert B_{-}-B_{w_{-}}\rvert
, and
\lvert B_{+}-B_{\min}\rvert
are less or equal to
\alpha i
, it follows that the point
\left(\frac{B_{w_{-}}+B_{\min}}{2},p_{\max}\right)

belongs to
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
and is at most
\alpha i
away from
(B,p)
.

To represent the abstract value, it is enough to store
2/\alpha
of its boundary grid points, hence all abstract values require
2|V|/\alpha
space.
One iteration of the algorithm computes
|V|
abstract values, and for each of them sequentially combines up to
|V|
abstract values,
which requires
2|V|^{2}/\alpha
time. The time per iteration is thus
2|V|^{2}/\alpha
.
∎

A.5.
Bidding Games on Restricted MDP-s

Lemma
6.1
0.

For acyclic MDPs, for every vertex
v
,
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|}_{v}=\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
and
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{|V|}_{v}=\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}^{*}_{v}
.

Proof.

Let
d_{v}
be the length of the longest simple path leading from a vertex
v
.
We prove by induction on
d_{v}
that
\mathit{val}_{v}^{d_{v}}=\mathit{val}_{v}^{d_{v}+k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
d_{v}=0
, then
v
does not have any successors (except itself), hance
\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}=\mathit{val}_{v}^{0}
in for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now suppose
d_{v}=i+1
and that the claim holds for every vertex
u
such that
d_{i}\leq i
.
Since
d_{w}<d_{v}
for every successor
w
of
v
, we have
\mathit{val}_{w}^{d_{w}}=\mathit{val}_{w}^{i}=\mathit{val}_{w}^{i+k}
.
We derive
\mathit{val}_{v}^{i+1}=\mathit{val}_{v}^{i+1+k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since the sequences
\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
stabilize after at most
|V|
steps, the respective limits are equal to
\mathit{val}_{v}^{|V|}
.
∎

We now further analyse Thm.
4.1
to preciselly determine which of the points
(B,p)
in sets
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
and
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
are actually achievable, i.e., when it is true that

\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{m,i}(T))
, respectively,
\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{m,i}(T))
is nonempty.

Lemma A.3.

Let
\left\langle M,v,T,B,p\right\rangle
be a problem instance. The following hold:

•

\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))\neq\emptyset
if and only if there exists
B^{\prime}<1
such that
B^{\prime}\leq B
and
(B^{\prime},p)\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}

•

\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))\neq\emptyset
if and only if there exists
B^{\prime}>B
such that
(B^{\prime},p)\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
, or
B=1
and
(B,p)\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
.

Proof.

According to Thm.
4.1
, all the interior points of
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
are winnable for the reachability player.
On the other hand, the outerior points
(B,p)
of
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
are not winnable for the reachability player, since they are in the interior of
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
.
Thus, for such points, the safety player is able to guarantee a reachability probability strictly less than
p
with budget
B
.
An analogous arguments hold for the safety player.

Hence for the rest of the proof we suppose that
(B,p)
lies on the threshold, i.e.,
the intersection of
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
and
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
.

Let us first discuss whether
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))
is nonempty.
We prove the implication from left to right first.
If
B<1
, then we can just set
B^{\prime}=B
and since
(B,p)
is on threshold, the claim follows.
If
B=1
then note, that on a finite horizon of length
i
already the budget
1-2^{-i}
is enough for the reachability player to win all the bids,
hence any higher budget does not bring any benefit in terms of increase of the reachability probability.
Thus whenever
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))
is nonempty and
B=1
, it must be indeed the case that already
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B^{\prime},p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))
is nonempty
for
B^{\prime}=1-2^{-i}
, and we have already proven for such
B^{\prime}
that
(B^{\prime},p)\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
.

For the opposite implication suppose there exists
B^{\prime}<1
such that
B^{\prime}\leq B
and
(B^{\prime},p)\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
. It is enough to show that the set

\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B^{\prime},p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))
is nonempty. Note that for
i=0
the claim is true since any point
(B^{\prime},p)\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{0}
is winnable for the reachability
player if
v\in T
, and any point
(B^{\prime},0)
is winnable if
v\not\in T
.

For the case
i>0
, we revisit the inductive construction
from the proof of Thm.
4.1
.
The case
v\in{V_{\mathsf{r}}}
is the same as in the proof of Thm.
4.1
. So suppose
v\in VC
,
and let
B_{+}
and
B_{-}
be such that
(B_{+}+B_{-})/2=B^{\prime}
,
(B_{+},p)\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w_{+}}^{i-1}
for every
w_{+}\in\mathit{Succ}(v)

and
(B_{-},p)\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{w_{-}}^{i-1}
for some
w_{-}\in\mathit{Succ}(v)
.
We can further without loss of generality assume
B_{-}\leq B
,
hence by inductive hypothesis, there exists a policy in
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B_{-},p,w_{-},\mathit{Reach}^{m,i-1}(T))
.
For every
w_{+}
, there exists a policy in
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B_{+},p,w_{+},\mathit{Reach}^{m,i-1}(T))
whenever
B_{+}<1
.
If the reachability player bids
|B_{+}-B_{-}|/2
, then he either wins the bidding and decreases his budget to
B_{-}

or loses the bidding and increases his budget to
B_{+}
. However note, that since the reachability player wins
the ties, the budget can only increase to
B_{+}
if
B_{+}<1
. Therefore, regardless of the result, the reachability player
can always continue according to some winning policy that exists by the inductive hypothesis.

Now let us discuss when
\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(B,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))
is nonempty.
We first show the direction from right to left. If there exists
B^{\prime}>B
such that
(B^{\prime},p)\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
, then the claim follows from
Thm.
4.1
. So suppose
B=1
and
(1,p)\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
. Note that
B=1
implies the initial safety player budget is zero.
However since even the initial safety player budget
2^{-i}
would not prevent the reachability player from winning all the bids in the following
i
steps,
the policy in
\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(1-2^{-i},p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))
proves there also exists a policy in
\Pi_{\mathsf{S}}(1,p,v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))
.

We show the other direction by a contraposition. Suppose
(B,p)
lies on the threshold,
B<1,
and there is no
B^{\prime}>B
such that
(B^{\prime},p)\in\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
.
Then necessarily, due to the staircase shape of
\mathsf{s}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
and
\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
, there is a point
(B,p^{\prime})
with
p^{\prime}>p
such that
(B,p^{\prime})\in\mathsf{r}\mathchar 45\relax\mathit{val}_{v}^{i}
,
hence
\Pi_{\mathsf{R}}(B,p^{\prime},v,\mathit{Reach}^{M,i}(T))
is nonempty. This implies there is indeed no policy that would guarantee reachability probability at most
p
with budget
B
.
∎

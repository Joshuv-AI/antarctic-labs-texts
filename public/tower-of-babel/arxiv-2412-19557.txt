On second-order optimality conditions for C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} optimization problems via Lagrangian functions
DUONG THI VIET AN 1 AND NGUYEN VAN TUYEN 2

Abstract
. This paper focuses on optimality conditions for C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} -smooth optimization problems subject to inequality and equality constraints. By employing the concept of limiting (Mordukhovich) second-order subdifferentials to the Lagrangian function associated with the problem, we derive new second-order optimality conditions for the considered problem. Applications for multiobjective optimization problems are studied as well. These results extend and refine existing results in the literature.

By using the second-order subdifferential concepts mentioned above, Chieu et al.

Thereafter, these results are applied to study second-order optimality conditions for vector optimization problems with inequality constraints
[
15

. By applying the notion of the pseudoconvexity at a point in a direction and the second-order characterizations of (strict and strong) pseudoconvexity, the authors of

Our approach departs from
[

by avoiding the use of second-order tangent sets to the feasible set. Instead, we employ the limiting second-order subdifferential of the Lagrangian function to obtain second-order optimality conditions for

Firstly, we focus on optimization problems with finite inequality and equality constraints. Secondly, we employ the Lagrangian method and utilize information from the first-order tangent set, whereas the optimality conditions in
[

rely on information from the second-order tangent set.
Furthermore, in Theorem

[
10

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, the considered spaces are finite-dimensional Euclidean with the inner product and the norm being denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and by
||\cdot||
, respectively.
Along with single-valued maps usually denoted by
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we consider set-valued maps (or multifunctions)
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
with values
F(x)
in the collection of all the subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The limiting construction

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}F(x):=\bigg\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid\exists x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x},y_{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ y_{k}\in F(x_{k}),\forall k=1,2,....\bigg\}

is known as the
Painlevé-Kuratowski outer/upper limit
of
F
at
\bar{x}
. All the maps considered below are proper, i.e.,
F(x)\not=\emptyset
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

(see
[
28
, pp. 5–6]
)Let
\Omega
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
. The
Fréchet (regular) normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(\bar{x},\Omega)=\Big\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\limsup\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\Big\},

where
x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}
means that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
and
x\in\Omega
. The
limiting (Mordukhovich) normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is given by

\displaystyle N(\bar{x},\Omega)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\widehat{N}(x,\Omega).

We put
\widehat{N}(\bar{x},\Omega)=N(\bar{x},\Omega)=\emptyset
if
\bar{x}\not\in\Omega
.

Clearly, one always has

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(\bar{x},\Omega)\subset N(\bar{x},\Omega),\ \forall\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall\bar{x}\in\Omega.

If
\Omega
is convex, one has

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(\bar{x},\Omega)=N(\bar{x},\Omega):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq 0,\ \forall x\in\Omega\},

i.e., the regular normals to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
coincides with the limiting normal cone and both constructions reduce to the normal cone in the sense of convex analysis.

Definition 2.2
.

(see, e.g.,
[
29
, Definition 3.41]
) Let
\Omega
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. An element
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
tangent
to
\Omega
at a point
\bar{x}\in\Omega
if there are a sequence
\{x_{k}\}\subset\Omega
and a sequence
\{t_{k}\}
of positive real numbers such that
t_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
,
x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
, and
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}=v.

The set
T(\bar{x},\Omega)
of all tangents to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is called the
contingent cone
(or
the Bouligand-Severi tangent cone

[
24
, Chapter 1]
) to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
. Note that
v\in T(\bar{x},\Omega)
if and only if there exist a sequence
\{t_{k}\}
of positive scalars and a sequence of vectors
\{v_{k}\}
with
t_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
v_{k}\rightarrow v
as
k\rightarrow\infty
such that
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k}
belongs to
\Omega
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

In the case, where
\Omega
is a convex set,
[T(\bar{x},\Omega)]^{*}=N(\bar{x},\Omega)
and
[N(\bar{x},\Omega)]^{*}=T(\bar{x},\Omega)
, where

[N(\bar{x},\Omega)]^{*}:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle v,x\rangle\leq 0,\ \forall x\in N(\bar{x},\Omega)\}.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
be a set-valued map with the
domain

{\rm{dom}}\,F:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid F(x)\not=\emptyset\}

and the
graph

{\rm{gph}}\,F:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid y\in F(x)\}.

Definition 2.3
.

(see
[
28
, Definition 1.11]
) Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
be a multifunction and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm{gph}}\,F
. The
limiting (Mordukhovich) coderivative
of

is a multifunction
D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
with the values

\displaystyle D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v):=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(u,-v)\in N\left((\bar{x},\bar{y}),\mbox{\rm gph}\,\,F\right)\right\},\quad v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

If
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\notin{\rm{gph}}\,F
, one puts
D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v)=\emptyset
for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The symbol
D^{*}F(\bar{x})
is used when

is single-valued at
\bar{x}
and

.

Consider a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
with the
effective domain

\mbox{dom}\,f:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid f(x)<+\infty\},

the
epigraph

{\rm{epi}}\,f:=\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid\alpha\geq f(x)\},

Definition 2.4
.

(see
[
24
, Definition 1.77]
)
Suppose that
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

One calls the set

\displaystyle\partial f(\bar{x}):=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in N((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\mbox{\rm epi}\,f)\right\}

of

\bar{x}
. If

One can use the notion of coderivative to construct the second-order generalized differential theory of extended-real-valued functions.

Definition 2.5
.

(see
[
28
, Definition 3.17]

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}

be a function with a finite value at

For any

\widehat{\partial}^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\partial^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v):=(D^{*}\partial f)(\bar{x},\bar{y})(v)=\{u\mid(u,-v)\in N((\bar{x},\bar{y}),\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial f)\}

If

\displaystyle\partial^{2}f(\bar{x})(v)=\{\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})^{*}v\}=\{\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})v\},\ v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})
being the Hessian matrix of

.

Let
D
be an open subset of

. We denote by

the class of all real-valued functions

According to

, if

and

, one has

(1)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}f(\bar{x})(v):=\partial^{2}f(\bar{x},\nabla f(\bar{x}))(v)=\partial\langle v,\nabla f\rangle(\bar{x}),\ \forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The following properties can obtained directly from the definition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f\in C^{1,1}(D)
and
\bar{x}\in D
. The following assertions hold:

(i)
For any

, one has

(ii)
For any

the mapping

is locally bounded. Moreover, if
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
,
x_{k}^{*}\to x^{*}
,
x_{k}^{*}\in\partial^{2}f(x_{k})(v)
for all

, then
x^{*}\in\partial^{2}f(\bar{x})(v).

The Taylor formula for

.

(see

3.
Second-order necessary optimality conditions

In the paper, we will study a general optimization problem. More precisely, the optimization problem considered is of the form

(P1)

\displaystyle\min\{f(x)\mid x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{i}(x)\leq 0,i\in I,h_{j}(x)=0,j\in J\},

where
f,g_{i},h_{j}\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
I=\{1,2,...,m\}
,
J=\{1,2,...,p\}
.
The feasible set is denoted by

(2)

X:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid g_{i}(x)\leq 0,\ i\in I,h_{j}(x)=0,j\in J\}.

Given
x\in X,
we denote the
active index
set to
x
by

I^{0}(x):=\{i\in I\mid g_{i}(x)=0\}

and denote by
C(x)
the
cone of critical directions

\displaystyle C(x):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle\nabla f(x),v\rangle=0,\

\displaystyle\langle\nabla g_{i}(x),v\rangle\leq 0,\ i\in I^{0}(x),

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h_{j}(x),v\rangle=0,\ j\in J\}.

We invoke the notion of an isolated local solution of order
2
, as introduced by Auslender
[
30
, Definition 2.1]
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a feasible point
\bar{x}\in X
is an
isolated local solution of order 2
of (
P1
) if there exist a positive constant
\rho
and a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}

such that

(3)

\displaystyle f(x)>f(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{2}\rho\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2},\ \forall x\in X\cap U,\ x\not=\bar{x}.

Observe that if we can find a positive constant
\bar{\rho}
and a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
with

(4)

\displaystyle f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2},\ \forall x\in X\cap U

then, for any fixed
\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho})
, condition (
3
) is fulfilled. Therefore, a feasible point
\bar{x}
of (
P1
) is an isolated local solution of order
2
if and only if there exist a positive constant
\bar{\rho}
and a neighborhood
V
of
\bar{x}

such that (
4
) holds.

Definition 3.2
.

(see
[
17
]
)
One says that the
metrically subregular
(MSCQ) holds at
\bar{x}\in X
if there exist a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
and a number
\kappa>0
such that the following condition is satisfied

(5)

\mathrm{dist}\,(x;X)\leq\kappa\left(\sum_{i\in I}\max\{g_{i}(x),0\}+\sum_{j\in J}|h_{j}(x)|\right)\ \ \text{for all}\ \ x\in U.

In
[
17
, Proposition 9.1]
, by using
[
31
, Lemma 2.5]
the author proved that if the MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then the following
Abadie constraint qualification
(ACQ) is satisfied

T(\bar{x},X)=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\,\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0,\,i\in I^{0}(\bar{x}),\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),\,v\rangle=0,j\in J\},

where
g_{i}
,
i\in I
, and
h_{j}
,
j\in J
, are
C^{2}
smooth functions. In the next proposition, we show that this result is still true for the case where functions
g_{i}
,
i\in I
, and
h_{j}
,
j\in J
, are only differentiable at the point considered.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that functions
g_{i}
,
i\in I
, and
h_{j}
,
j\in J
, are differentiable at
\bar{x}\in X
. If the MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then so does the ACQ.

Proof.

Let
U
be a neighborhood of
\bar{x}
and
\kappa
be a positive number such that (
5
) is satisfied. Put

L(\bar{x},X):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\,\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0,\,i\in I^{0}(\bar{x}),\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0,\,j\in J\}.

By definition, it is easy to see that
T(\bar{x},X)\subset L(\bar{x},X)
. We now show that
L(\bar{x},X)\penalty\ \subset\penalty\ T(\bar{x},X)
and so the ACQ holds at
\bar{x}
. Let any
v\in L(\bar{x},X)
and consider arbitrary sequences
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
,
v_{k}\to v
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, put
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k}
. Since
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
, without any loss of generality we may assume that
x_{k}\in U
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us denote

I^{0}(\bar{x},v):=\{i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})\,\mid\,\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0\}.

We claim that

(6)

g_{i}(x_{k})<0\ \ \forall i\in I\setminus I^{0}(\bar{x},v)

and
k
large enough. Indeed, if
i\notin I^{0}(\bar{x})
, then
g_{i}(\bar{x})<0
and so
g_{i}(x_{k})<0
for all
k
large enough due to the continuity of
g_{i}
and the fact that
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
. If
i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})\setminus I^{0}(\bar{x},v)
, then
g_{i}(\bar{x})=0
and
\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle<0
. By the differentiability of
g_{i}
at
\bar{x}
, we have

g_{i}(x_{k})=g_{i}(x_{k})-g_{i}(\bar{x})=t_{k}\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v_{k}\rangle+o(t_{k}),

with
o(t_{k})/t_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Hence

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{g_{i}(x_{k})}{t_{k}}=\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle<0

and we therefore get
g_{i}(x_{k})<0
for all
k
large enough, as required. Without any loss of generality, we assume that (
6
) holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This and the MSCQ at
\bar{x}
imply that

\mathrm{dist}\,(x_{k},X)\leq\kappa\left(\sum_{i\in I^{0}(\bar{x},v)}\max\{g_{i}(x_{k}),0\}+\sum_{j\in J}|h_{j}(x_{k})|\right)\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

By the differentiability of
g_{i}
and
h_{j}
at
\bar{x}
, one has

\displaystyle g_{i}(x_{k})

\displaystyle=t_{k}\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v_{k}\rangle+o(t_{k}),\ \ i\in I^{0}(\bar{x},v),

\displaystyle h_{j}(x_{k})

\displaystyle=t_{k}\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v_{k}\rangle+o(t_{k}),\ \ j\in J.

This implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{g_{i}(x_{k})}{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0,\ \ \forall i\in I^{0}(\bar{x},v),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{h_{j}(x_{k})}{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0,\ \ \forall j\in J.

Hence

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{dist}\,(x_{k},X)}{t_{k}}=0.

This and the closedness of
X
imply that there exists a sequence
z_{k}
in
X
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\|z_{k}-x_{k}\|}{t_{k}}=0.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, put
w_{k}:=(z_{k}-x_{k})/t_{k}
, Then
w_{k}\to 0
and we have

z_{k}=x_{k}+t_{k}w_{k}=\bar{x}+t_{k}(v_{k}+w_{k}).

Since
z_{k}\in X
,
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
, and
v_{k}+w_{k}\to v
, one has
v\in T(\bar{x},X)
. Thus, we have
L(\bar{x},X)\subset T(\bar{x},X)
, as required.
∎

The Lagrangian associated with the constrained problem (
P1
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\sum\limits_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}g_{i}(x)+\sum\limits_{j\in J}\mu_{j}h_{j}(x).

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\bar{x}\in X
. The set of
Lagrange multipliers
of problem (
P1
) at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\Lambda(\bar{x}):=\{(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\,\mid\,\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=0,\bar{\lambda}_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x})=0,\ i\in I\}.

If
\Lambda(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
, then
\bar{x}
is called a

of (

).

The following theorem provides second-order necessary optimality conditions for problem (
P1
) in terms of limiting second-order subdifferentials.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that
\bar{x}\in X
is a local solution of (
P1
) and the MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
. Then, the following assertions hold:

(i)

\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),v\rangle\geq 0
for any
v\in T(\bar{x},X).
Consequently,
\Lambda(\bar{x})
is nonempty.

(ii)
Let
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\Lambda(\bar{x})
. Then for each
v\in T(\bar{x},X^{0})
, there exists
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(v)
such that

(7)

\langle z,v\rangle\geq 0,

where

\displaystyle X^{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid h_{j}(x)=0,\ j\in J,\

\displaystyle g_{i}(x)=0\ \mbox{if}\ i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})\ \mbox{and}\ \bar{\lambda}_{i}>0,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad g_{i}(x)\leq 0,\ \mbox{otherwise}\}.

(iii)
If
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of (
P1
) and
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\Lambda(\bar{x})
, then, for every
v\in T(\bar{x},X^{0})\setminus\{0\}
, there exists
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(v)
such that

\langle z,v\rangle>0.

Proof.

(i) Let
v\in T(\bar{x},X)
. Then, by definition, there exist a sequence of positive scalars
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
and a sequence of vectors
v_{k}\to v
such that
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k}\in X
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\bar{x}
is a local solution of (
P1
) and
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
, we have
f(x_{k})\geq f(\bar{x})
for all
k
large enough. By the differentiability of
f
at
\bar{x}
, one has

0\leq f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})=t_{k}\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),v_{k}\rangle+o(t_{k}),

with
o(t_{k})/t_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Dividing two sides by
t_{k}
and letting
k\to\infty
we obtain
\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),v\rangle\geq 0
. Now by the MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, one has
\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),v\rangle\geq 0
for all
v
satisfies

\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0,\ i\in I^{0}(\bar{x}),\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0,\ \ j\in J.

This means that the following system

\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),v\rangle<0,\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0,\ i\in I^{0}(\bar{x}),\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0,\ \ j\in J

has no solution
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, by the Motzkin’s theorem of the alternative (see
[
32
, pp. 28–29]
), there exist
\bar{\lambda}_{i}\geq 0
,
i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})
, and
\bar{\mu}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
j\in J
, such that

\nabla f(\bar{x})+\sum_{i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x})+\sum_{j\in J}\bar{\mu}_{j}\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x})=0.

For
i\in I\setminus I^{0}(\bar{x})
, put
\bar{\lambda}_{i}=0
and
\bar{\lambda}:=(\bar{\lambda}_{i})_{i\in I}
,
\bar{\mu}:=(\mu_{j})_{j\in J}
. Then
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\Lambda(\bar{x})
, as required.

(ii) Suppose that
\bar{x}
is a local minimum of problem (
P1
) and
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\Lambda(\bar{x})
. Let any
v\in T(\bar{x},X^{0})
. Since
\bar{x}
is a local minimum of (
P1
), there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

f(x)-f(\bar{x})\geq 0,\ \forall x\in X\cap U.

As
v\in T(\bar{x},X^{0})
, there exist a sequence of positive scalars
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
and a sequence of vectors
v_{k}\to v
such that

x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k}
belongs to
X^{0}\cap U
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}.
Since
x_{k}\in X^{0}
, we have
\bar{\lambda_{i}}g_{i}(x_{k})=0
for
i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})
and
h_{j}(x_{k})=0
for
j\in J
. Hence

\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\geq 0.

Combining this with the fact that
\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=0
one has

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})]+[\mathcal{L}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})]

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})]+[\mathcal{L}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})

(8)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),t_{k}v\rangle].

Setting

A=\mathcal{L}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),t_{k}v\rangle

and

B=\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}).

By applying the Taylor formula (Theorem

) we can find
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(t_{k}v)
such that

(9)

\displaystyle A\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\langle z_{k},t_{k}v\rangle,

where

\xi_{k}\in[\bar{x},\bar{x}+t_{k}v].

From Proposition

(i),
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(t_{k}v)
means that

The latter allows us to express

, where

Thus, (
9
) can be rewritten as

(10)

\displaystyle A\leq\dfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\langle w_{k},v\rangle.

For each
k
, the function
\varphi(t):=\mathcal{L}((1-t)\bar{x}+tx_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
, where
t\in[0,1]
is the composition function of the affine function

defined on
[0,1]
and the vector function

defined on

. Under our assumptions, we have
\mathcal{L}(\cdot,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By using the classical mean value theorem (see, e.g.,
[

, Theorem 5.10]
) to

on
[0,1]
and using the chain rule (see, e.g.,
[
34
, p. 103]
), we find
\zeta_{k}\in(\bar{x}+t_{k}v,x_{k})
such that

B=\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\zeta_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),x_{k}-(\bar{x}+t_{k}v)\rangle.

The later is equivalent to

B=\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\zeta_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),t_{k}(v_{k}-v)\rangle,

and, hence

\displaystyle|B|

\displaystyle=|\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\zeta_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),t_{k}(v_{k}-v)\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\zeta_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),t_{k}(v_{k}-v)\rangle|

(11)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\zeta_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\|.\|t_{k}(v_{k}-v)\|.

Since
\mathcal{L}(\cdot,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can find a positive constant
\ell
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\zeta_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\|

\displaystyle\leq\ell\|\zeta_{k}-\bar{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq\ell[\|\zeta_{k}-x_{k}\|+\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|]

(12)

\displaystyle=\ell t_{k}[\|v_{k}-v\|+\|v_{k}\|],\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Combining (
11
) and (
12
) yields

|B|\leq\ell t_{k}^{2}[\|v_{k}-v\|+\|v_{k}\|].\|v_{k}-v\|.

This implies that

(13)

\dfrac{|B|}{t_{k}^{2}}\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ k\to\infty.

From (
8
) and (
10
) we get

\displaystyle 0\leq A+B\leq\dfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\langle w_{k},v\rangle+B.

Dividing both sides by
t_{k}^{2}
we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\langle w_{k},v\rangle+\dfrac{B}{t_{k}^{2}}.

Since
\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(.,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(v)
is locally bounded at
\bar{x}
, and
\xi_{k}\to\bar{x}
, we deduce that
\{w_{k}\}
is bounded. Thus, we can assume, without any loss of generality, that
w_{k}
converges to some
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
, and, hence

0\leq\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\langle w_{k},v\rangle+\dfrac{B}{t_{k}^{2}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\langle z,v\rangle

due to (
13
). In other words, we can find
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
such that

\langle z,v\rangle\geq 0.

(iii) Suppose that
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of (
P1
) and
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\Lambda(\bar{x})
. Let
\bar{\rho}
be a positive number and
U
be a neighborhood of
\bar{x}
such that

f(x)-f(\bar{x})\geq\bar{\rho}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2},\ \ \forall x\in X\cap U.

For
v\in T(\bar{x},X^{0})\setminus\{0\}
, there exist a sequence of positive scalars
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
and a sequence of vectors

such that

x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k}
belongs to

for all

Hence

\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\geq\bar{\rho}t_{k}^{2}\|v_{k}\|^{2}.

By this and a similar argument as in the proof of part (ii), we see that there exists
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
such that

\frac{1}{2}\langle z,v\rangle\geq\bar{\rho}\|v\|^{2}>0,

as required.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Feng and Li
[
10
]
investigated second-order necessary conditions for optimization problems involving set constraints and finite inequality constraints, employing the concept of limiting second-order subdifferentials. Our approach differs significantly from that of
[
10
]
.
Firstly, we focus on optimization problems with finite inequality and equality constraints. Secondly, we employ the Lagrangian method and utilize information from the first-order tangent set, whereas the optimality conditions in
[
10
]
rely on information from the second-order tangent set.
Especially in Theorem
3.5
, we study the second-order necessary optimality conditions for the isolated solution of order 2 as well.

4.
Second-order sufficient optimality conditions

Let us now turn our attention to second-order sufficient optimality conditions.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\bar{x}
be a stationary point of (
P1
) and
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\Lambda(\bar{x})
. Assume that for every
v\in[T(\bar{x},X)\cap C(\bar{x})]\setminus\{0\}
and for any
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(v)
one has

\displaystyle\big\langle z,v\big\rangle>0.

Then
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of (
P1
).

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that
\bar{x}
is not an isolated local solution of order
2
of (
P1
). Then there exists a sequence
x_{k}\in X
such that
x_{k}\to\bar{x}

(x_{k}\not=\bar{x})
and

(14)

\displaystyle f(x_{k})\leq f(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2},\ \forall k,

where
t_{k}:=\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|>0
and
t_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Set
v_{k}=\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}.
Since
v_{k}
is bounded, there exists a subsequence, denoted in the same way, such that
v_{k}\to v
as
k\to\infty
and
\|v\|=1
. Clearly,
v\in T(\bar{x},X)
.

For
i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})
, we can expand
g_{i}(x)
around
\bar{x}
as follows

0\geq g_{i}(x_{k})=\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),x_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle+o(x_{k}-\bar{x}),

with
o((x_{k}-\bar{x})/{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|})\to 0
as
x_{k}\to\bar{x}.
Dividing both sides by
\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|
and passing to the limit over the subsequence for which
v_{k}\to v
, one gets

\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0,\ i\in I^{0}(\bar{x}).

A similar analysis of equality constraints yields the relations

\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0,\ j\in J.

Since
\bar{x}
is a stationary point, we have

\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),v\rangle=-\sum_{i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle-\sum_{j\in J}\bar{\mu}_{j}\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\geq 0.

On the other hand, from (
14
) we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),x_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle+o(x_{k}-\bar{x})=f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\leq\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2}.

Dividing both sides by
\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|
and passing to the limit
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
we get
\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0.

Consequently,
\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0.
Therefore
v\in C(\bar{x}).

By Taylor formula (Theorem
2.7
), we can find
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(x_{k}-\bar{x})
, where
\xi_{k}\in[x_{k},\bar{x}]
such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\langle z_{k},x_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),x_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle.

This and the fact that
x_{k}\in X
imply that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\langle z_{k},t_{k}v_{k}\rangle\leq\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})

\displaystyle=[f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})]+\sum_{i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}[g_{i}(x_{k})-g_{i}(\bar{x})]+\sum_{j\in J}\bar{\mu}_{j}[h_{j}(x_{k})-h_{j}(\bar{x})]\leq\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2}.

By Proposition
2.6
(i) and the fact that
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(t_{k}v_{k})
, there exists
z^{\prime}_{k}\penalty\ \in\penalty\ \partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(v_{k})
such that
z_{k}=t_{k}z^{\prime}_{k}
.
Hence

\dfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\langle z^{\prime}_{k},v_{k}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2},\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N},

and it implies that
\langle z,v\rangle\leq 0
for some
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(x,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(v)
, which contradicts the assumptions.
∎

Let us see an example to illustrate Theorems

and
4.1

Example 4.2
.

Consider the problem (

) with

,

\bar{\mu}

Remark 4.3
.

Theorem
4.1
establishes a sufficient optimality condition that differs from the one presented in

Example 4.4
.

(Theorem

\displaystyle X:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,g_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq 0,g_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq 0\},

and
\bar{x}=(0,0)\in X
. First, we observe that
\bar{x}
is a stationary point of

5.
Second-order sufficient optimality conditions in multiobjective optimization problems

In this section, we consider the following multiobjective optimization problem

(MP)

\mathrm{Min}\,_{\mathbb{R}^{q}_{+}}\{\varphi(x):=(\varphi_{1}(x),\ldots,\varphi_{q}(x))\,|\,x\in X\},

where
\varphi_{l}\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
l\in L:=\{1,\ldots,q\}
, and
X
is defined as in (
2
).

The Lagrangian associated with the constrained problem (
MP
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,\alpha,\lambda,\mu)=\sum_{l\in L}\alpha_{l}\varphi_{l}(x)+\sum\limits_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}g_{i}(x)+\sum\limits_{j\in J}\mu_{j}h_{j}(x).

We say that
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
critical direction
of (
MP
) at a
x\in X
if

\begin{cases}\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(x),v\rangle\leq 0\ \ &\forall l\in L,\\
\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(x),v\rangle=0\ \ &\text{at least one}\ \ l\in L,\\
\langle\nabla g_{i}(x),v\rangle\leq 0\ \ &\forall i\in I^{0}(x),\\
\langle\nabla h_{j}(x),v\rangle=0\ \ &\forall j\in j.\end{cases}

The set of all critical direction of (
MP
) at
x
is denoted by
K(x)
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\bar{x}\in X
. The set of
Lagrange multipliers

) at
\bar{x}

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}(\bar{x}):=\{(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in(\mathbb{R}^{q}_{+}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\,

\displaystyle|\,\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=0,

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \bar{\lambda}_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x})=0,i\in I\}.

If the set of Lagrange multipliers of (

(see, e.g.

The following result gives a sufficient for the nonemptiness of the set of Lagrange multipliers at a given local weak efficient solution of (

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that
\bar{x}\in X
is a local weak efficient solution of (
MP
). If the MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then

\max\{\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\,|\,l\in L\}\geq 0

for every
v\in T(\bar{x},X).
Consequently,
\mathrm{A}(\bar{x})
is nonempty.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that there exists a vector
v\penalty\ \in\penalty\ T(\bar{x},X)
such that
\max\{\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\,|\,l\in L\}<0,
or, equivalently,
\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle<0
for all
l\in L
. Let
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
and
v_{k}\to v
such that
\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k}\in X
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
l\in L
, it follows from

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k})-\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})}{t_{k}}=\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle<0

that there exists
k_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k})<\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})\ \ \forall k\geq k_{l}.

Let
k_{0}:=\max\{k_{l}\,|\,l\in L\}
. Then we have

\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k})<\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})\ \ \forall l\in L,k\geq k_{0},

contrary to the fact that
\bar{x}
is a local efficient solution of (
MP
). Hence

\max\{\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\,|\,l\in L\}\geq 0\ \ \forall v\in T(\bar{x},X).

From this, the MSCQ condition at
\bar{x}
, and Proposition
3.3
, we see that the following system has no solution
v

\begin{cases}\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle<0\ \ &\forall l\in L,\\
\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0\ \ &\forall i\in I^{0}(\bar{x}),\\
\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0\ \ &\forall j\in j.\end{cases}

By the Motzkin’s theorem of the alternative (see
[
32
, pp. 28–29]
),
\mathrm{A}(\bar{x})
is nonempty. The proof is complete.
∎

The following theorem presents a second-order sufficient optimality condition for an isolated local solution of order
2
of (

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that
\bar{x}
is a stationary point of (
MP
) and
(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\mathrm{A}(\bar{x})
. Assume that for every
v\in[T(\bar{x},X)\cap K(x)]\setminus\{0\}
and for any
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})

one has

\langle z,v\rangle>0.

Then
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of (
MP
).

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
\bar{x}
is not an isolated local solution of order
2
of (
MP
). Then there exists a sequence
x_{k}\in X
such that
x_{k}\to\bar{x}

(x_{k}\not=\bar{x})
and

(15)

\displaystyle\max\{\varphi_{l}(x_{k})-\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})\,|\,l\in L\}\leq\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2},\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N},

where
t_{k}:=\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|>0
. Clearly,
t_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Set
v_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}.
Since
v_{k}
is bounded, there exists a subsequence, denoted in the same way, such that
v_{k}\to v
as
k\to\infty
and
\|v\|=1
. By a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we see that

\displaystyle v\in T(\bar{x},X),\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0,\ \ i\in I^{0}(\bar{x}),\ \ \text{and}

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0,\ \ j\in J.

It follows from (
15
) that

\varphi_{l}(x_{k})-\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})\leq\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2},\ \ \forall l\in L,k\in\mathbb{N}.

By Taylor formula, one has

\varphi_{l}(x_{k})-\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})=t_{k}\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v_{k}\rangle+o(t_{k})\leq\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2}.

Dividing both sides by
t_{k}
and passing to the limit
k\to\infty
we get

\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle\leq 0,\ \ l\in L.

We claim that there exists
l\in L
such that
\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle=0
. If otherwise, then it follows from
(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\mathrm{A}(\bar{x})

that

\displaystyle 0=\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),v\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in L}\bar{\alpha}_{l}\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle+\sum_{i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{j\in J}\bar{\mu}_{j}\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),v\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in L}\bar{\alpha}_{l}\langle\nabla\varphi_{l}(\bar{x}),v\rangle+\sum_{i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),v\rangle<0,

a contradiction. Hence,
v\in[T(\bar{x},X)\cap K(x)]\setminus\{0\}
.

By Theorem
2.7
, we can find
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(t_{k}v_{k})
, where
\xi_{k}\in[x_{k},\bar{x}]
such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\langle z_{k},x_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),x_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle.

This and the fact that
x_{k}\in X
imply that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\langle z_{k},t_{k}v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),x_{k}-\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in L}\bar{\alpha}_{l}[\varphi_{l}(x_{k})-\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})]+\sum_{i\in I^{0}(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}[g_{i}(x_{k})-g_{i}(\bar{x})]

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\in J}\bar{\mu}_{j}[h_{j}(x_{k})-h_{j}(\bar{x})]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k}\bigg(\sum_{l\in L}\bar{\alpha}_{l}\bigg)t_{k}^{2}.

By Proposition
2.6
(i) and the fact that
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(t_{k}v_{k})
, there exists
z^{\prime}_{k}\penalty\ \in\penalty\ \partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(v_{k})
such that
z_{k}=t_{k}z^{\prime}_{k}
.
Hence

\dfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\langle z^{\prime}_{k},v_{k}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{k}\bigg(\sum_{l\in L}\bar{\alpha}_{l}\bigg)t_{k}^{2},\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N},

and this implies that
\langle z,v\rangle\leq 0
for some
z\in\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(x,\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(v)
, which contradicts the assumptions.
∎

Example 5.5
.

Consider the problem (

) with the following data:

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(x)=x^{2},\varphi_{2}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}|t|dt,\ g(x)=-x\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=(\varphi_{1}(x),\varphi_{2}(x)),X:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,g(x)\leq 0\},

and

\bar{x}

The Lagrangian associated with (

It is clear that

Since

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(x,\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda})(v)

This implies that

6.
Concluding remarks

This paper focuses on deriving second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for
C^{1,1}
optimization problems in finite-dimensional spaces, subject to both inequality and equality constraints. Our approach utilizes the Lagrangian function and the concept of limiting second-order subdifferentials to obtain these conditions. These results represent a significant extension of existing results for optimization problems with
C^{1,1}
data.

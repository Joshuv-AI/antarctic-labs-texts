Constant maps in equivariant topological strings and geometric modeling of fluxes
Luca Cassia, and Kiril Hristov † \dagger ]Corresponding author

Abstract
We study the equivariant generalization of topological strings on toric manifolds, focusing in particular on defining the contributions of constant maps in the genus expansion of the partition function. This approach regularizes the integration over non-compact Calabi-Yau spaces, producing finite results at each order in the expansion, as illustrated by a broad set of explicit examples. Our investigation highlights the geometric modeling of flux compactifications and clarifies the link between the effective supergravity framework and the equivariant topological string formalism, building on recent developments by Martelli and Zaffaroni. We conclude that the connection between topological string theory and supergravity/field theory involves switching between geometric moduli and fluxes, shedding light on the role of ensemble averages in string theory. We propose an exact non-perturbative holographic match with the corresponding M2-brane partition functions, which we test perturbatively at all orders in the gauge group rank N N in a companion paper. A special case of our proposal for vanishing flux reformulates the Ooguri–Strominger–Vafa conjecture within the equivariant topological string framework.

and degree

\overline{{\cal M}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the moduli space of genus

Since any cohomology class in

can be written as a polynomial in the classes
\phi_{a}

X
by pairing all possible degree-
n

C_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}:=\int_{X}\phi_{a_{1}}\cup\dots\cup\phi_{a_{n}}\ ,

(13)

which we can also interpret as intersection numbers of divisors in
X
as follows. First, observe that to a degree-2 cohomology class
\phi_{a}
we can associate a line bundle
L_{a}
such that
c_{1}(L_{a})=\phi_{a}
. Then one can associate a divisor
D_{a}
in
X
to each such line bundle, as the (homology class of the) zero locus of a generic section of

.
Then we have the identification

C_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}=D_{a_{1}}\cap\dots\cap D_{a_{n}}\ ,

(14)

where on the r.h.s. we have the intersection number of the divisors
D_{a_{i}}

\phi_{a}
as the Poincaré dual class to the divisor

Now we can package all of this information about intersection numbers into a generating function. To do so, we define the function

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t):=

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}\frac{1}{d!}C_{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}\,t^{a_{1}}\dots t^{a_{d}}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}\frac{1}{d!}t^{a_{1}}\dots t^{a_{d}}\int_{X}\phi_{a_{1}}\cup\dots\cup\phi_{a_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=\int_{X}\mathrm{e}^{\omega}\ ,

where in the last step we used that, from dimensional reasons, the integral over
X
selects the order
d
term in the expansion of the exponential of the Kähler class. It follows that this function corresponds to the symplectic volume of the manifold, which we also denote by
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t)
.
By construction then, the function
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t)
satisfies the identity

C_{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}=\frac{\partial^{d}\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t)}{\partial t^{a_{1}}\dots\partial t^{a_{d}}}=\frac{\partial^{d}\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t)}{\partial t^{a_{1}}\dots\partial t^{a_{d}}}\Big|_{t=0}\ ,

(16)

with the latter equality, here trivial, written for a simpler comparison with the forthcoming equivariant generalization.

In the case when

is a polynomial of degree 3 in the Kähler moduli, which correspond to the genus-zero constant-maps term in the topological string free energy, cf. (

10

N_{0,0}(t)=\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t)=\frac{1}{3!}\,\sum_{a,b,c}C_{a,b,c}\,t^{a}t^{b}t^{c}\ .

(17)

The constant-maps term in the genus-one free energy of the topological string on a three-fold

If we similarly expand the second Chern class

The integral in (

2.2
Equivariant volumes, toric geometry, and generating functions

The formula for the symplectic volume in (
15
) admits a natural equivariant upgrade if we substitute the Kähler class by its equivariant completion. Namely, let
T
be a torus of rank
n
acting on
X
with moment map
H:X\to\mathrm{Lie}(T)^{\ast}
, and let
\rho_{i}
be a basis for
\mathrm{Lie}(T)^{\ast}
so that
H=\rho_{i}H^{i}
.
The equivariant completion of the symplectic form
\omega
is the combination
\omega-\epsilon_{i}H^{i}
, with
\epsilon_{i}\in H^{2}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
being the generators of the
T
-equivariant cohomology of the point. The equivariant upgrade of the symplectic form is closed w.r.t. the equivariant differential
\mathrm{d}+\epsilon_{i}\iota_{v^{i}}
for vector fields
v^{i}
generators of the flow along the 1-parameter subgroups of
T
associated to the generators
\rho_{i}
.
Then we can define the equivariant (symplectic) volume of
X
as the integral

\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,\epsilon):=\int_{X}\mathrm{e}^{\omega-\epsilon_{i}H^{i}}\ .

(23)

We observe that the equivariant volume is a function of the equivariant parameters
\epsilon_{i}
and it is no longer polynomial in the variables
t^{a}
. If the manifold
X
is compact, then the equivariant volume is a regular function around
\epsilon_{i}=0
, such that
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,0)
is simply the symplectic volume
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t)
that generates the intersection numbers, as in (
15
). If
X
is non-compact, then the
\epsilon_{i}\to 0
limit is not smooth as the manifold does not possess a finite volume. In this sense, one can think of the equivariant generalization as a way of regularizing the volume of non-compact manifolds, as heavily explored in the physics literature starting with
[
4
]
.

In the toric case,
X
can be described as a Kähler quotient of a linear space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by a torus
U(1)^{r}
, such that the complex dimension of
X
is given by
d=n-r
. The action of this torus is described in terms of a matrix of integer charges
Q_{i}^{a}
with
i=1,\dots,n
and
a=1,\dots,r
, corresponding to an embedding of
U(1)^{r}
into the larger torus
U(1)^{n}
acting diagonally on the homogeneous coordinates
z_{i}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The associated moment map
\mu(z)=\rho_{a}Q_{i}^{a}|z_{i}|^{2}
is the composition of the map
Q
and the moment map
H=\rho_{i}H^{i}=\rho_{i}|z_{i}|^{2}
for the
U(1)^{n}
-action, where
\rho_{a}
and
\rho_{i}
are a basis of
\mathrm{Lie}(U(1)^{r})^{\ast}\cong\mathbb{R}^{r}
and
\mathrm{Lie}(U(1)^{n})^{\ast}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively.

The (ordinary) cohomology of the quotient space
X:=\mathbb{C}^{n}//U(1)^{r}=\mu^{-1}(t)/U(1)^{r}
is generated by tautological classes
\phi_{a}\in H^{\ast}(X)
, one for each of the
U(1)
factors in the torus
U(1)^{r}
.
Moreover, since the original
U(1)^{n}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
descends to a (not necessarily faithful) action on the quotient space
X
, we can also consider the
U(1)^{n}
-equivariant cohomology
H^{\ast}_{U(1)^{n}}(X)
as a module over the
U(1)^{n}
-equivariant cohomology of the point
H^{\ast}_{U(1)^{n}}(\mathrm{pt})\cong\mathbb{C}[\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n}]
, which is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in the equivariant parameters
\epsilon_{i}
.

The Chern classes dual to the toric divisors
D_{i}=(\{z_{i}=0\}\cap\mu^{-1}(t))/U(1)^{r}\subset X
admit an equivariant upgrade as equivariant Chern classes

x_{i}:=\epsilon_{i}+\phi_{a}Q^{a}_{i}\in H^{\ast}_{U(1)^{n}}(X)\ .

(24)

Equivariant localization à la Jeffrey–Kirwan (JK) then allows to compute the equivariant volume in (
23
) via a residue integral of the form

\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,\epsilon)=\oint_{\text{JK}}\prod_{a=1}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{a}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\phi_{a}t^{a}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}\ ,

(25)

where the choice of contour depends on the
chamber
for the Kähler parameters
t^{a}
, also known as
phase
of the symplectic quotient
[
75
]
.
Evaluation of the residues at each of the JK-poles allows to recover the contributions of each fixed-point as in the ABBV localization formula.

Again, we can regard
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,\epsilon)
as a generating function of certain geometric quantities, namely, the equivariant integrals over
X
of all polynomial functions in the tautological classes
\phi_{a}
.
An alternative way to construct a generating function of equivariant integrals is as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a toric quotient as before, with equivariant Chern roots
x_{i}
as in (
24
). Let
\lambda^{i}
be a set of
n
formal variables conjugate to the classes
x_{i}
, then we define the generating function of
equivariant intersection numbers
as

\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon):=\oint_{\text{JK}}\prod_{a=1}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{a}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{x_{i}\lambda^{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}

(26)

which we regard as a formal power series in the
\lambda^{i}
’s with coefficients in the field of fractions of the
U(1)^{n}
-equivariant cohomology ring of the point.

Remark
.

The formal parameters
\lambda^{i}
’s are not directly related to the Kähler moduli of
X
, in fact one should think of them as conjugate variables to all of the isometries of the prequotient
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, including those that are not gauged when reducing to
X
. In this sense, the
\lambda^{i}
’s can be regarded as
redundant Kähler parameters
, the same way that the
\epsilon_{i}
’s can be regarded as redundant equivariant parameters.
11
11

11

Observe that the same kind of redundant Kähler parameters appeared before in the literature in
[
46
]
, for instance, although in the context of (quantum) K-theory. A similar redundant parametrization was also employed in the definition of the equivariant volume in
[
31
]
.

In particular, the coefficients in the
\lambda^{i}
expansion encode all equivariant integrals of polynomials in the equivariant Chern classes
x_{i}
. For this reason, we define
divisors operators

{\cal D}_{i}:=\epsilon_{i}+Q_{i}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{a}}\,.

(27)

such that they produce inserions of classes
x_{i}
when acting on the equivariant volume
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,\epsilon)
. Similarly, we can produce the same insertions of classes
x_{i}
’s in the function
\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon)
if we act with the operators
\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{i}}
.
We then have the identity

\left.{\cal D}_{i}\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,\epsilon)\right|_{t=0}=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{i}}\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon)\right|_{\lambda=0}

(28)

and more generally we have the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

The equivariant volume
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,\epsilon)
and the generating function
\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon)
are related by the identity

\left.\mathrm{e}^{\epsilon_{i}\lambda^{i}}\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,\epsilon)\right|_{t^{a}=Q_{i}^{a}\lambda^{i}}=\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon)

(29)

Proof.

This follows from definitions (
25
), (
26
) and (
24
).
∎

Compared to
\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon)
, the function
\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)
does not depend on all of the parameters
\epsilon_{i}
but rather on
d=n-r
independent linear combinations of them. This follows by observing the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

Equivalently, if we take the limit where all

Q_{i}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon_{i}}\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon)=0\,.

Proof.

Suppose
\gamma_{i}=\varphi_{a}Q_{i}^{a}
for some
\varphi_{a}\in\mathbb{R}
, then we have

(\epsilon_{i}+\gamma_{i})+\phi_{a}Q_{i}^{a}=\epsilon_{i}+(\phi_{a}+\varphi_{a})Q_{i}^{a}\,.

(32)

Then we can redefine the integration variables in (

\tilde{\phi}_{a}=\phi_{a}+\varphi_{a}\ ,

(33)

which leads to the claim of the lemma, namely

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon+\gamma)

\displaystyle=\oint\prod_{a=1}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{a}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{(\epsilon_{i}+(\phi_{a}+\varphi_{a})Q_{i}^{a})\lambda^{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\epsilon_{i}+(\phi_{a}+\varphi_{a})Q_{i}^{a})}

(34)

\displaystyle=\oint\prod_{a=1}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\phi}_{a}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{(\epsilon_{i}+\tilde{\phi}_{a}Q_{i}^{a})\lambda^{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\epsilon_{i}+\tilde{\phi}_{a}Q_{i}^{a})}=\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon)\,.

Similarly, (

Q_{i}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon_{i}}\prod_{j}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{x_{j}\lambda^{j}}}{x_{j}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{a}}\prod_{j}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{x_{j}\lambda^{j}}}{x_{j}}\ ,

(35)

is a total derivative in
\phi_{a}
.
∎

Let us then define a map

v^{i}_{\alpha}Q_{i}^{a}=0\,,

(36)

depends only on the combinations

\left.\tilde{\mathbb{V}}_{X}(\lambda,\nu)\right|_{\nu_{\alpha}=v_{\alpha}^{i}\epsilon_{i}}=\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda,\epsilon)\,.

We observe that the function

coincides with the function defined in
(

up to a change of sign in front of the formal variables
\lambda^{i}
.

12

12

The following identities hold true:

We again stress that the above definition, (
52
), might be counterintuitive at first when used on a toric manifold of arbitrary dimension, but it follows the logic of using triple equivariant intersection numbers for all constant maps terms. We comment more on the possibility for considering intersections of different order at the end of this paper.

Summary of main formulae

Here we gather the formulae that allow us to efficiently calculate the equivariant volumes and corresponding constant maps terms in the explicit examples in the following sections.

The equivariant volume, as a function of the Kähler moduli
t
, for a given toric manifold with a charge matrix
Q
can be computed via the JK-residue expression

\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon)=\oint_{\text{JK}}\prod_{a=1}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{a}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\phi_{a}t^{a}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}\ ,

(54)

and the
\lambda
-parametrization can be obtained via

\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda^{i}{\cal D}_{i}}\,\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon)\,\Big|_{t=0}\,,

(55)

with summations over repeated indices implied.

The equivariant constant maps terms can be computed by ordinary partial derivatives w.r.t. the
\lambda
parameters:

\displaystyle N_{0,0}(\lambda,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\,\sum_{i,j,k}\frac{\partial^{3}\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)}{\partial\lambda^{i}\partial\lambda^{j}\partial\lambda^{k}}\Big|_{\lambda=0}\,\lambda^{i}\lambda^{j}\lambda^{k}\ ,

(56)

\displaystyle N_{1,0}(\lambda,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{i<j}\sum_{k}\frac{\partial^{3}\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)}{\partial\lambda^{i}\partial\lambda^{j}\partial\lambda^{k}}\Big|_{\lambda=0}\,\lambda^{k}\ ,

\displaystyle N_{\mathfrak{g}>1,0}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{|B_{2\mathfrak{g}}|}{4\mathfrak{g}}\frac{|B_{2\mathfrak{g}-2}|}{(2\mathfrak{g}-2)}\frac{(-1)^{\mathfrak{g}}}{(2\mathfrak{g}-2)!}\sum_{i<j<k}\frac{\partial^{3}\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)}{\partial\lambda^{i}\partial\lambda^{j}\partial\lambda^{k}}\Big|_{\lambda=0}\ .

2.3
Calabi–Yau cones, Kähler bases, and mesonic twist

So far, we have kept the nature of the toric action general, but in fact, there are two distinct types of symmetries to consider. The geometry typically includes non-contractible two-cycles, corresponding to the number of Kähler parameters
t^{a}
, which can be understood as internal symmetries—referred to as baryonic symmetries—since the toric action associated to these cycles is unrelated to the global isometries of the manifold. The remaining symmetries arise from the maximal torus of the manifold’s isometry group and are called mesonic symmetries, following
[
41
,
42
]
. Notably, we are always effectively breaking baryonic symmetries by sitting at a specific point in the Kähler moduli space.

Based on the above discussion, we note an interesting holographic observation that, while not fully proven, has been empirically verified in several examples (see
[
41
,
42
]
). The dual field theory seems to be unaware of the baryonic symmetries of the geometry. Instead, the global symmetries of the field theory on M2-branes appear to correspond solely to the mesonic symmetries, which arise from the isometries of the manifold transverse to the brane.

Assuming this holographic observation holds, achieving a correct holographic match requires implementing the so-called
mesonic truncation
or
mesonic twist
in the geometry, which involves removing the baryonic symmetries, introduced by the symplectic quotient. Geometrically, this corresponds precisely to going to the origin of the Kähler moduli space of
X
thus contracting all non-trivial two-cycles. This operation can be regarded as the opposite of a blow-up process and as such it introduces a conical singularity, rather than being a resolution of a singular space. The geometrical setup is the following.

Let us consider a toric CY space
X
obtained as symplectic reduction of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
under the action of the torus
U(1)^{r}
as described in the previous sections. The precise geometry and topology of
X
depends on both the choice of charges
Q_{i}^{a}
as well as the choice of chamber in the extended Kähler cone. Different choices of chamber lead to genuinely different quotient spaces
X
. Furthermore, from the geometry of the symplectic reduction, we have that each real Kähler parameter
t^{a}
parametrizes the volume of one of the cycles
\beta_{a}
in
H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
,

\int_{\beta_{a}}\omega=t^{a}

(57)

In turn, one can regard such parameters
t^{a}
as measuring the lengths of the compact 1-dimensional legs in the toric diagram of
X
. By definition, the toric diagram is the 1-skeleton of the intersection locus of the preimage of the moment maps with the positive orthant in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The CY condition on
Q_{i}^{a}
implies that this intersection in non-compact, and very far from the origin it looks like a real
2d
-dimensional cone,
d=(n-r)
.
When we set all the Kähler moduli to zero, we then expect that
X
becomes a cone where all the non-trivial homology 2-cycles have been shrunk to a single point coinciding with the tip of the cone. The base of this cone is a Sasakian manifold
L
, of real dimension
2d-1
. Conversely, we can regard
X
as a toric resolution of the cone over
L
. Because
t=0
is at the origin of the extended Kähler moduli space, after shrinking all homology 2-cycles in
X
, we end up with a conical singular space which no longer depends on the choice of chamber (all chambers contain the origin as a boundary point).
From now on we will refer to this conical space as
X_{\circ}
and we will use that

X_{\circ}\cong C(L)\ .

(58)

When this is the case, we can write its metric as

\mathrm{d}s^{2}(X_{\circ}):=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}(L)\ ,

(59)

where
r
is the radial coordinate and
\mathrm{d}s^{2}(L)
is a metric on the base.
Furthermore, we will assume that
L
can be described as a S
1
-bundle over a conformally Kähler manifold
B
such that

\mathrm{d}s^{2}(L)=\eta^{2}+\mathrm{d}s^{2}(B)\ ,

(60)

where
\eta
is the contact 1-form on
L
dual to the Reeb vector
\xi
(see
[
49
,
48
]
and references therein for more details),

\iota_{\xi}\eta=1\,.

(61)

In the following, we will also assume that the base
B
can be described as follows. Within the base of the toric fibration of the cone
X_{\circ}
, we define a compact sublocus corresponding to the intersection with an hypersurface associated to an equation of the form

\sum_{i}b_{i}|z_{i}|^{2}=c\ ,

(62)

where
b_{i}
are some real parameters and
z_{i}
are the homogeneous coordinates on
X_{\circ}
.
If the
b_{i}
are integers, then we can regard (
62
) as a moment map for a Hamiltonian
U(1)
-action on
X_{\circ}
and
c
as the corresponding Kähler parameter.
Then
B
is obtained as the symplectic reduction

B=X_{\circ}//U(1)

(63)

and
c
is its only non-vanishing modulus, which is to say that
H_{2}(B,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

Concretely, the mesonic twist acts as a specialization of parameters,

t^{a}_{\text{mes.}}=0\ ,\qquad\forall a\ ,

(64)

which in terms of the ineffective Kähler parameters
\lambda^{i}
, corresponds to imposing the linear constraints

\sum_{i}Q_{i}^{a}\lambda_{\text{mes.}}^{i}=0\ ,\quad\forall a\ .

(65)

Using the above condition together with (
29
), it is straightforward to derive the general behavior,

\mathbb{V}_{X}(\lambda_{\text{mes.}},\epsilon)=\mathrm{e}^{\epsilon_{i}\lambda_{\text{mes.}}^{i}}\,\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t=0,\epsilon)=:\mathrm{e}^{\epsilon_{i}\lambda_{\text{mes.}}^{i}}\,\mathrm{vol}_{X}^{\text{mes.}}(\epsilon)\ .

(66)

Note that the expression for the equivariant volume
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(t,\epsilon)
at general non-zero values of
t
, depends not only on the charge matrix
Q_{i}^{a}
, but also on the chamber in the Kähler moduli space where
t
lies, cf. (
25
). On the other hand, in the limit
t\to 0
the final expression for the equivariant volume is independent of the chamber, which means that under the mesonic twist there is a universal formula for
\mathrm{vol}_{X}^{\text{mes.}}(\epsilon)
which only depends on the charge matrix
Q_{i}^{a}
.

We can further understand the mesonic condition through the use of the toric fan vectors
v_{\alpha}^{i}
, which by definition satisfy the relation (
36
)
where
\alpha
labels precisely the mesonic symmetries (i.e. those symmetries which act effectively on the quotient
X
), while
a
labels the baryonic ones. It follows that the parameters
\lambda^{i}
, in order to satisfy the constraint (
65
), must be expressed as linear combinations of the vectors of the toric fan, namely

\lambda_{\text{mes.}}^{i}(\mu):=\sum_{\alpha}v^{i}_{\alpha}\mu^{\alpha}\ ,

(67)

for some coefficients
\mu^{\alpha}
that play the role of “reduced”
\lambda
parameters.
In other words, the parameters
\mu^{\alpha}
are the totally redundant Kähler moduli, i.e. those orthogonal to the effective Kähler moduli
t^{a}
.

Notice that (
67
) together with (
37
) imply
\epsilon_{i}\lambda_{\text{mes.}}^{i}(\mu)=\epsilon_{i}v_{\alpha}^{i}\mu^{\alpha}=\nu_{\alpha}(\epsilon)\mu^{\alpha}
, so that the equivariant volume at the mesonic twist point can be written as an explicit function of the reduced parameters
\mu^{\alpha}
and
\nu_{\alpha}
:

\mathbb{V}^{\text{mes.}}(\mu,\nu):=\tilde{\mathbb{V}}(\lambda_{\text{mes.}}(\mu),\nu)\ .

(68)

Importantly, the above discussion allows us to simplify the genus-zero and genus-one constant maps terms in (
56
) at the mesonic twist point. Namely, if we assume that
\lambda^{i}
are of the form (
67
) then, for genus
\mathfrak{g}=0,1
we can use (
40
) to write

\displaystyle N_{0,0}^{\text{mes.}}(\lambda_{\text{mes.}},\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\,\left(\epsilon_{i}\lambda_{\text{mes.}}^{i}\right)^{3}\,\mathrm{vol}^{\text{mes.}}(\epsilon)\ ,

(69)

\displaystyle N_{1,0}^{\text{mes.}}(\lambda_{\text{mes.}},\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}\,\left(\epsilon_{i}\lambda_{\text{mes.}}^{i}\right)\,\sum_{i<j}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)}{\partial\lambda^{i}\partial\lambda^{j}}\Big|_{\lambda=0}\,\ ,

while the higher-genus constant maps cannot be simplified from their expression in (
56
). It is important to stress here that the specialization of
\lambda
-parameters as in (
67
) should be done after taking derivatives w.r.t. the unconstrained variables
\lambda^{i}
. Moreover, since
\sum_{i<j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\mathbb{V}|_{\lambda=0}
corresponds to the integral of the second Chern class, in the case that
X
is a complex two-fold it becomes equal to the Euler number which is independent of the equivariant parameters, while in all other cases it is a non-trivial rational function of the
\epsilon_{i}
. A similar consideration applies to the function
\sum_{i<j<k}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\mathbb{V}|_{\lambda=0}
(which appears at genus
\mathfrak{g}>1
) that becomes equal to the Euler number if
X
is a complex three-fold.

These expressions can in turn be converted as functions of
\mu
and
\nu
in a straightforward way given the previous discussion and identities.

Mesonic constraint

An important observation one needs to make regarding the relation between the redundant and effective equivariant parameters is that, while it is straightforward to define the
\epsilon_{i}
’s as functions of the
\nu_{\alpha}
’s, the converse is not true. In fact, (
37
) generically does not admit a unique solution as a system of equations for the
\epsilon_{i}
’s. While it is true that by making some non-canonical choices one can always find a way to invert those relations, one would like to be able to have a systematic procedure to define
\epsilon_{i}
’s as functions of
\nu_{\alpha}
’s.
In order to do so, we will make use of the mesonic twist (
64
) together with the observation that

\left.v_{\alpha}^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{i}}\log\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)\right|_{\lambda=\lambda_{\rm mes.}(\mu)}=v^{i}_{\alpha}\epsilon_{i}\ ,

(70)

which follows directly from the identity (
40
).
The matrix
v_{i}^{\alpha}
appears on both sides, however we cannot get rid of it since it is not an isomorphism. Nevertheless, one could decide to impose the condition that the
\epsilon_{i}
’s are such that they satisfy

\epsilon_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{i}}\log\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)\right|_{\lambda=\lambda_{\rm mes.}(\mu)}\ ,

(71)

which now form a system of non-linear equations due to the fact that the r.h.s. is a complicated rational function of the
\epsilon_{i}
’s, namely we can rewrite (
71
) explicitly as

\epsilon_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\frac{\prod_{a}\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{a}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}(\epsilon_{j}+\phi_{a}Q_{j}^{a})^{-1}}{\prod_{a}\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{a}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\prod_{j}(\epsilon_{j}+\phi_{a}Q_{j}^{a})^{-1}}\ .

(72)

We refer to this as the
mesonic constraint
on the
\epsilon_{i}
’s.
As it turns out, not all of these equations are linearly independent precisely because of the identity (
70
), which means that one cannot solve for the
\epsilon_{i}
’s completely, in fact only
r
of them can be fixed by the equations. The remaining
d
independent degrees of freedom can be though of as parametrizing a specific choice of the effective parameters
\nu_{\alpha}
’s. In practice, getting rid of the redundancies in the
\epsilon_{i}
’s by imposing the constraints (
71
) is an effective but not unique way to invert the relations in (
37
).
14
14

14

Alternatively, we can define functions

\epsilon_{i}^{\rm mes.}(\nu):=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{i}}\log\tilde{\mathbb{V}}(\lambda,\nu)\right|_{\lambda=\lambda_{\rm mes.}(\mu)}

(73)

where we remark that the r.h.s. does not depend on
\mu^{\alpha}
’s due to some cancellations that only happen after we impose the mesonic twist on the Kähler parameters
\lambda^{i}
’s.
With this definition, we readily have that
v^{i}_{\alpha}\epsilon_{i}^{\rm mes.}(\nu)=\nu_{\alpha}
,
which means that we have found a way to invert the relations in (
37
),
provided we know the function
\tilde{\mathbb{V}}(\lambda,\nu)
beforehand.
It is interesting to observe that the mesonic constraint on the
\epsilon
parameters also has a holographic counterpart which has been previously explored in
[
42
]
.

Universal parametrization

As we discussed, the mesonic twist acts as a blow-down of the non-trivial two cycles in
X
corresponding to the vanishing of all of its effective Kähler parameters. What remains, are the ineffective parameters
\mu^{\alpha}
which are formally conjugate variables to the equivariant parameters
\nu_{\alpha}
associated to the effective torus isometries of
X_{\circ}
and therefore those of the base
L
.
In order to pass to the quotient
B
, we have to perform an additional symplectic reduction w.r.t. a
U(1)
subgroup of the isometry group, which introduces a unique non-trivial two-cycle in
H_{2}(B,\mathbb{Z})
. The associated Kähler parameter is the variable conjugate to the equivariant parameter of the symmetry that has been quotiented out.
For the sake of concreteness, we can assume that the
U(1)
quotient is defined by the moment map

\sum_{i}v^{i}_{\alpha}|z_{i}|^{2}=\mu^{\alpha}

(74)

where
z_{i}
are homogeneous coordinates on
X_{\circ}
and the label
\alpha
identifies the 1-parameter subgroup.
Then
\mu^{\alpha}
becomes the unique (effective) Kähler parameter of
B
and the corresponding equivariant parameter
\nu_{\alpha}
becomes an integration variable in the contour integral representation of the equivariant volume of
B
. In other words, we have removed the
\alpha
-th column vector from
v
and added it as a row vector to
Q
.
In particular, this means that the direct relation between supergravity and the dual field theory can be performed by picking a single non-vanishing
\mu
-parameter.

Among all possible choices of label
\alpha
for the reduction, there is one which is somewhat special. This corresponds to the
\alpha
such that
v^{i}_{\alpha}=1
for all
i
, which always exists due to the CY condition on the matrix
Q_{i}^{a}
. It is indeed a standard convention that the first toric fan vector of a Calabi–Yau manifold is simply
v_{1}=(1,1,\dots,1)
. We will then refer to this choice as the
universal reduction

B
, and
we will refer to the parametrization

\lambda^{i}=v_{1}^{i}\mu^{1}=\mu^{1}\,,\quad\forall i\,,

(75)

as the
universal parametrization
.

3
Basic toric examples

In this section, we begin with some of the simplest toric manifolds, which serve as the fundamental building blocks for the more complex cases discussed in subsequent sections. Taking advantage of the computational simplicity of these models, we introduce key objects of interest in a more explicit and pedagogical manner. Readers already familiar with equivariant intersection theory on toric manifolds may wish to skip this section, as it primarily covers well-established results.

3.1
Complex plane and discrete quotients,
\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma

The simplest (non-compact) toric CY manifold consists of
n
copies of the complex plane,
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. It has no non-trivial cycles, so it has no effective Kähler parameters
t^{a}
to start with. Correspondingly, there is no charge vector
Q_{i}^{a}
that can be defined. For illustrational purposes, we can be fully explicit here and introduce local coordinates,

\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,(\mathrm{d}r_{i}^{2}+r_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi_{i}^{2})\ ,

(76)

with
2\pi
-periodic angle coordinates
\varphi_{i}
.
The Kähler form is given by

\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}\varphi_{i}\wedge r_{i}\mathrm{d}r_{i}\ .

(77)

There is a natural
U(1)^{n}
action of rotations around the origin, parametrized by

v:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}\,v^{i}=2\pi\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}\,\partial_{\varphi_{i}}\ ,

(78)

such that the equivariant indices in this case are identified with the label on the respective copy of
\mathbb{C}
. The equivariant completion of
\omega
is given by the moment maps

H^{i}=(r_{i})^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\mathrm{d}+\epsilon_{i}\iota_{v^{i}})(\omega-\epsilon_{i}\,H^{i})=\epsilon_{i}\,(\iota_{v^{i}}\omega-\mathrm{d}H^{i})=0\ .

(79)

Clearly the standard symplectic volume on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is not well-defined, so we can directly consider the equivariant volume. Via a direct integration from (
23
) we find,

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{\omega-\epsilon_{i}H^{i}}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon_{i}H^{i}}

(80)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\prod_{i}\mathrm{d}\varphi_{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\prod_{i}(r_{i}\mathrm{d}r_{i})\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}\,r_{i}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}}\ .

The same result is reproduced also via the ABBV localization theorem, where the fixed point of the Killing vector
\xi
corresponds to the origin of each copy of
\mathbb{C}
, such that

\mathrm{vol}(\epsilon)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon_{i}H^{i}}|_{r=0}}{e(T_{\{0\}}\mathbb{C}^{n})}=\frac{1}{\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}}\ .

(81)

where
e(T_{\{0\}}\mathbb{C}^{n})
is the equivariant Euler class of the normal bundle to the fixed point
\{0\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

The third way of reproducing the calculation of the equivariant volume, i.e. the contour integral in (
25
), automatically agrees with the result above, since the equivariant Chern roots are simply the equivariant parameters,
x_{i}=\epsilon_{i}
, and there is no integration due to a lack of Kähler moduli.

After this straightforward calculation, let us turn to the main object of interest here: the constant maps. We first evaluate the equivariant volume in the
\lambda
-parametrization, (
26
),

\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)=\exp(\epsilon_{i}\lambda^{i})\,\mathrm{vol}(\epsilon)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\epsilon_{i}\lambda^{i}}}{\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}}\ ,

(82)

where summation over repeated up-down indices is implied.
Note that in this simplest case, due the lack of effective Kähler moduli, the mesonic twist condition is trivial, and the matrix
v_{\alpha}^{i}
can be taken to be the identity matrix, so that
\lambda=\mu
and
\epsilon=\nu
. With these identifications, we can write

\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(\lambda,\epsilon)\equiv\mathbb{V}^{\text{mes.}}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(\mu,\nu)\ .

(83)

This is only true for the present example and does not hold on any of the other examples.

The genus-zero and genus-one constant maps follow directly from (
46
) and (
49
):

N_{0,0}=\frac{(\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}\lambda^{i})^{3}}{6\,\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}}\ ,\hskip 30.0ptN_{1,0}=\frac{(\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j})\,(\sum_{k}\epsilon_{k}\lambda^{k})}{24\,\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}}\ ,

(84)

while the higher genus constant maps follow from (
52
),

N_{\mathfrak{g}\geq 2,0}=\frac{(-1)^{\mathfrak{g}}}{(2\mathfrak{g}-2)!}\,\frac{|B_{2\mathfrak{g}}|}{2\mathfrak{g}}\frac{|B_{2\mathfrak{g}-2}|}{(2\mathfrak{g}-2)}\,\frac{\sum_{i<j<k}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{k}}{2\,\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}}\,\ .

(85)

Notice that the genus-zero expression is non-vanishing for any dimension
n
, while the genus-one expression only vanishes for
n=1
and the higher genus expression vanishes for
n=1,2
.

Let us also consider a generalization of the above case allowing for finite discrete quotients of the form
\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma
. Assuming that the finite group
\Gamma
preserves the symplectic structure, the full space inherits the toric action and the equivariant structure from the
\mathbb{C}^{n}
case above. Even though in general the resulting space is singular (we give examples of resolutions in the next section), we can still define the equivariant volume as before, (
23
). If we have no further knowledge of the group
\Gamma
, the equivariant volume becomes a sum over all group elements,
g\in\Gamma
. From the fixed-point theorem it further follows that each group element contributes through its fixed-point set,
\text{Fix}(g)
.

Let us then assume the simplest case where the discrete group
\Gamma
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with no fixed points other than the origin. Then we have the simple identity

\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}}{|\Gamma|}\ ,\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma}=\frac{\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}}{|\Gamma|}\ .

(86)

This includes the special case of the cyclic groups
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, with corresponding action on
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by

(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\mapsto(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}m_{1}/k}z_{1},\dots,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}m_{n}/k}z_{n})\ ,

(87)

which are particularly relevant for holographic applications.
In this case, we have

\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}}(\lambda,\epsilon)=\exp(\epsilon_{i}\lambda^{i})\,\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}}(\epsilon)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\epsilon_{i}\lambda^{i}}}{k\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}}\ ,

(88)

The corresponding constant maps terms
N_{\mathfrak{g},0}
can be computed as in (
69
) by first resolving the singularity and then imposing the mesonic twist condition after taking
\lambda
-derivatives.

3.2
Spindle,
\mathbb{WP}^{1}_{a,b}

One-dimensional complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, also known as the Riemann sphere, can be realized as a toric quotient
\mathbb{C}^{2}//U(1)
by taking the
U(1)
action corresponding to both charges being 1, so that the moment map is
\mu(z)=|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}
. This simple quotient admits a generalization to a weighted projective space corresponding to the quotient taken w.r.t. the action with two charges which are not necessarily equal to 1.

In this case, the moment map is
\mu(z)=a|z_{1}|^{2}+b|z_{2}|^{2}
where the charges
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
introduce two conical deficit angles at the poles of the Riemann sphere. The resulting quotient is still toric however it becomes a singular space, better described via the language of Deligne--Mumford stacks.
15
15

15

In the case where
\gcd(a,b)\neq 1
the resulting quotient stack is not reduced and generically takes the form of a
\mathbb{Z}_{\gcd(a,b)}
-gerbe over the reduced stack.

Known colloquially as a
spindle
, this manifold has recently been considered as a horizon geometry for asymptotically AdS black holes in gauged supergravity, generalizing the usual spherical topologies, see
[
76
,
77
]
and references therein. One can think of the spindle topologically as a sphere, with a single Kähler modulus
t
. It can be represented as a stack quotient,

\mathbb{WP}^{1}_{a,b}=\mathbb{C}^{2}//U(1)\ ,

(89)

and a corresponding charge matrix (in this case a vector)

Q=(a,b)\ .

(90)

This example was considered in some detail in
(
31
, Section 3.1)
, where local coordinates and explicit expressions for the equivariant Kähler form can be found. To avoid repetition while still remaining pedagogical, let us focus on evaluating the equivariant volume using (
25
), our main mathematical tool in the forthcoming examples. Applying this formula for the spindle (using the relabeling of parameters
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{+},\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon_{-}
and likewise for the superscript of the
\lambda
-parameters) leads to

\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon)=\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\phi t}}{(\epsilon_{+}+a\phi)(\epsilon_{-}+b\phi)}\\
=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\epsilon_{+}t}{a}}}{(a\epsilon_{-}-b\epsilon_{+})}+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\epsilon_{-}t}{b}}}{(b\epsilon_{+}-a\epsilon_{-})}\ ,

(91)

where we took the poles at
\phi=-\frac{\epsilon_{+}}{a}
and
\phi=-\frac{\epsilon_{-}}{b}
. Note that the denominators of the two terms are equal, up to a sign, such that in the non-equivariant limit we have a cancellation of all the singular terms,

\lim_{\epsilon_{\pm}\to 0}\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon)=\frac{t}{a\,b}-\frac{t^{2}}{2a^{2}b^{2}}\,(b\epsilon_{+}+a\epsilon_{-})+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})\ .

(92)

As expected, the leading order is proportional to
t
and it parametrizes the ordinary volume, while the equivariant upgrade adds corrections at higher orders in the Kähler modulus
t
and the equivariant parameters
\epsilon_{\pm}

3.3
Projective space,

A higher-dimensional generalization of the previous example is the

Since the addition of the conical deficit numbers is straightforward and goes in analogy to the previous example, let us just take the case

In the

\lim_{\epsilon_{i}\to 0}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}(t,\epsilon)=\frac{t^{k}}{k!}-\frac{t^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\,\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\epsilon_{k}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})\ .

\lambda

\mathbb{V}(\lambda,\epsilon)=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\lambda^{j}(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i})}}{(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i})}\ ,

generalizing (

\nu_{\alpha}=\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{k+1}\ ,\qquad\forall\alpha=1,\dots,k\ .

(104)

We can then lift the redundancy in the equivariant parameters and write

\tilde{\mathbb{V}}(\lambda,\nu)=\left(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\lambda^{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}}}{\nu_{\alpha}}\right)-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda^{k+1}\nu_{\alpha}}}{\nu_{\alpha}}\prod_{\beta\neq\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\lambda^{\beta}(\nu_{\beta}-\nu_{\alpha})}}{(\nu_{\beta}-\nu_{\alpha})}\right)\ .

\displaystyle N_{0,0}

4
Calabi–Yau cones and resolutions

In this section we consider a set of examples of CY cones and their resolutions, which in general present more involved cases in comparison with the basic examples of the previous section. In all of the examples here we look at symplectic quotients given by the charge matrices obeying

\sum_{i}Q^{a}_{i}=0\ ,\qquad\forall a\ ,

(108)

which corresponds to the Calabi–Yau condition on toric manifolds. As a main calculational tool we directly apply formula (
25

In order to keep a relatively compact presentation, in this section we refrain from explicitly evaluating the constant maps terms of the topological string, which follow straightforwardly from the calculations we present. The reader can find other explicit examples of CY resolutions in

4.1
Local

The space

7.1
Minkowski vacuum and the 4d prepotential

We start with the vacuum case, where the 11d spacetime is given by

We briefly highlight key aspects of 4d

Explicitly, at two derivative level, the direct dimensional reduction produces the following prepotential of degree

Appendix A
Exact M2-brane charge

Here we address the question of the exact M2-brane charge, denoted by
N_{\rm M2}
in the introduction, first focusing on the spacetime filling case. In the classical two-derivative supergravity background, see section
6.1
,
N_{\rm M2}
matches the number of branes

. Eight-derivative corrections to the M2-brane solution were previously discussed in
[
138

, where two correction terms were proposed (in the absence of fractional branes). However, we find that only one of these terms should be included, while the second should be discarded.

To present our main argument, we briefly summarize the key elements of the calculation in
[
138
]
, which is based on the Chern-Simons-like higher-derivative correction to the 11d supergravity action, derived in
[
139
,
140
]
.
45
45

45

It is important to note that the fully supersymmetric invariant Lagrangian that includes this higher-derivative correction is still unknown. Hence, we only rely on the available action to determine the M2-brane charge, which is protected from other contributions, but cannot use it to calculate the corrections to the supergravity action.
Including this correction, the 11d supergravity bosonic action for the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and the three-form field
C_{3}
with field strength
F_{4}={\rm d}C_{3}
is given by

S_{\mathrm{11d}}=\frac{2\pi}{(2\pi l_{P})^{9}}\int\left[R\star 1-\frac{1}{2}\,F_{4}\wedge\star F_{4}-\frac{1}{6}\,C_{3}\wedge F_{4}\wedge F_{4}+(2\pi l_{P})^{6}\,C_{3}\wedge I_{8}\right]\ ,

(262)

where

I_{8}=-\frac{1}{2\cdot 4!}\,\left(\frac{1}{4}\,p_{1}^{2}-p_{2}\right)\ .

(263)

The M2-brane charge in a given background can be derived from the equation of motion for

{\rm d}\star F_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\,F_{4}\wedge F_{4}=(2\pi l_{P})^{6}\,N\,\delta^{8}(x)+(2\pi l_{P})^{6}\,I_{8}\ ,

where the first term on the r.h.s. represents a localized M2-brane source with flux
N

X_{\circ}
over the Sasakian manifold
L

At large radius, the 11d geometry looks like that of
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times X_{\circ}

X_{\circ}
(pulled-back to 11d).
Since

is a complex manifold with vanishing first Chern class (i.e. CY), we can further simplify the anomaly polynomial as

I_{8}=-\frac{c_{4}(TX_{\circ})}{24}\ ,

(265)

where

is the fourth Chern class of the tangent to
X_{\circ}

The key point, which holds universally for all M2-brane solutions, is that the additional terms in the

\displaystyle N_{\rm M2}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi l_{P})^{6}}\,\int_{L}\star F_{4}

(266)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi l_{P})^{6}}\,\int_{X_{\circ}}\mathrm{d}\star F_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N-\frac{\chi(X)}{24}-\frac{1}{2(2\pi l_{P})^{6}}\,\int_{X}F_{4}\wedge F_{4}\ ,

In the last line, we also used that the top Chern class of a smooth manifold integrates to the Euler characteristic. However, since

(279)

together with

\lambda^{3,4}_{+}=\lambda^{3,4}\mp\frac{\bar{\nu}}{\nu_{+}}\,\lambda^{2}\ ,\qquad\lambda^{3,4}_{-}=\lambda^{3,4}\pm\frac{\bar{\nu}}{\nu_{-}}\,\lambda^{1}\ ,

(280)

where the sign
\pm
is correlated with the index
3,4
respectively, such that we arrive at

\mathbb{V}_{F_{k}}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{\bar{\nu}}\,\left(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\lambda_{+}^{3,4},\epsilon_{+})-\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\lambda_{-}^{3,4},\epsilon_{-})\right)\ .

(281)

Notice the close similarity to the general expressions for the CY cases discussed in the main text, (
164
) and (
181
). It is however important to note that the underlying variables are not the same and depend on the details of the fiber.

\mathbb{P}^{m}
over
\mathbb{WP}^{1}_{a,b}

We can easily generalize to a fibration of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
on the spindle with

Q=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0&-k\\
0&0&1&1\end{pmatrix}\ ,

(282)

again having the base on the first row. Then the volume becomes

\mathrm{vol}_{F^{(a,b)}_{k}}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\epsilon_{1}t^{1}}{a}}}{a\epsilon_{2}-b\epsilon_{1}}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(t^{2},\epsilon_{3},\epsilon_{4}+\frac{k}{a}\epsilon_{1}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\epsilon_{2}t^{1}}{b}}}{b\epsilon_{1}-a\epsilon_{2}}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(t^{2},\epsilon_{3},\epsilon_{4}+\frac{k}{b}\epsilon_{2}\right)\ ,

(283)

and the resulting
\lambda
-parametrization follows analogously as above.

Moreover, we can also generalize the dimension of the fiber and consider
\mathbb{P}^{m}
over the spindle, with a charge matrix given by

Q=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0&\dots&0&-k\\
0&0&1&\dots&1&1\end{pmatrix}\ ,

(284)

such that the equivariant volume becomes

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\phi_{1}t^{1}}}{(\epsilon_{a}+a\phi_{1})(\epsilon_{b}+b\phi_{1})}\left[\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{2}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\phi_{2}t^{2}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}(\epsilon_{i}-k\,\phi_{1}+\delta_{i,m+1}\phi_{2})}\right]

(285)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\epsilon_{a}t^{1}}{a}}}{a\epsilon_{b}-b\epsilon_{a}}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}\left(t^{2},\epsilon_{i}+\frac{k}{a}\epsilon_{a}\delta_{i,m+1}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\epsilon_{b}t^{1}}{b}}}{b\epsilon_{a}-a\epsilon_{b}}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}\left(t^{2},\epsilon_{i}+\frac{k}{b}\epsilon_{b}\delta_{i,m+1}\right)\ .

Again, it is straightforward to rewrite this expression in the
\lambda
-parametrization to generalize (
281
).

\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}

Let us consider the fiber
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
over a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
base, giving us an interesting example of a product space fiber with a compact and a non-compact factor. This is the closest example to the situation discussed in the main text in section
7.2
that features a non-toric compact factor in the fiber.

We can choose the matrix of charges

Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&-n&-p\\
0&0&1&1&0\end{pmatrix}\ ,

(286)

where we regard
t^{1}
(corresponding to the first row) as the volume of the base and
t^{2}
(corresponding to the second row) that of the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
factor in the fiber. We also assume that
n,p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and note that the fifth direction above corresponds to the complex plane factor
\mathbb{C}
, and we use the notation
\epsilon_{5}=\epsilon
to distinguish it.

The equivariant volume in the chamber
t^{1},t^{2}>0
is given by the integral

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{2}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\phi_{1}t^{1}+\phi_{2}t^{2}}}{(\epsilon_{1}+\phi_{1})(\epsilon_{2}+\phi_{2})(\epsilon_{3}+\phi_{2})(\epsilon_{4}-n\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})(\epsilon-p\phi_{1})}

(287)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon_{1}t^{1}}}{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(t^{2},\epsilon_{3},\epsilon_{4}+n\epsilon_{1})\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}}(\epsilon+p\epsilon_{1})

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon_{2}t^{1}}}{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(t^{2},\epsilon_{3},\epsilon_{4}+n\epsilon_{2})\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}}(\epsilon+p\epsilon_{2})

where we took the poles corresponding to solutions of:

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{1}+\phi_{1})(\epsilon_{3}+\phi_{2})=0

(288)

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{2}+\phi_{1})(\epsilon_{3}+\phi_{2})=0

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{1}+\phi_{1})(\epsilon_{4}-n\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})=0

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{2}+\phi_{1})(\epsilon_{4}-n\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})=0

as suggested by the JK prescription in this chamber.

It is particularly interesting to observe what happens in the non-equivariant limits in this case. Setting to zero the equivariant parameters
\epsilon_{3},\epsilon_{4}
associated to the compact directions on the fiber, we find

\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon,\epsilon_{1,2},\epsilon_{3,4}=0)=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}\,\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-t^{1}\epsilon_{1}}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-n\,t^{2}\epsilon_{1}})}{n\,\epsilon_{1}(\epsilon+p\,\epsilon_{1})}-\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-t^{1}\epsilon_{2}}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-n\,t^{2}\epsilon_{2}})}{n\,\epsilon_{2}(\epsilon+p\,\epsilon_{2})}\right)\ ,

(289)

such that the equivariant volume has an inverse cubic dependence on the
\epsilon
parameters. We can also take the further non-equivariant limit of the compact base as well, finding

\lim_{\epsilon_{1,2,3,4}\to 0}\mathrm{vol}(t,\epsilon)=\frac{p\,t^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{t^{2}(2t^{1}+nt^{2})}{2\epsilon}\ .

(290)

In the
\lambda
-parametrization, we instead find

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\lambda^{2}(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1})}}{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}\,\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\lambda^{3,4},\epsilon_{4}-\epsilon_{3}+n\,\epsilon_{1})\,\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda,\epsilon+p\,\epsilon_{1})

(291)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda^{1}(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1})}}{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}\,\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\lambda^{3,4},\epsilon_{4}-\epsilon_{3}+n\,\epsilon_{2})\,\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda,\epsilon+p\,\epsilon_{2})\ ,

It is again straightforward to find the parametrization that brings the equivariant volume in the form analogous to (
281
).

The generalization of these results to a fibration of
\mathbb{C}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
over
\mathbb{WP}^{1}_{a,b}
follows the same pattern by adjusting the corresponding number of variables.

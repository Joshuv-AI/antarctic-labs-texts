Conserved Quantities in Expanding Gödel Cosmology
Alexander Leithes

Abstract
At linear order we study perturbations to a Gödel background spacetime which includes expansion in addition to rotation. We investigate the transformation behaviour of these perturbations under gauge transformations and construct gauge invariant quantities. Using the perturbed energy conservation equation we find that there are conserved quantities in Expanding Gödel (EG) Cosmology, in particular a spatial metric trace perturbation, ζ SMTP {\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}} , which is conserved on large scales for pressureless dust. We finally extend our discussion to a perfect fluid matter content to also obtain conserved quantities in this context.



I
Introduction

Recent discoveries by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) of an apparent bias in the alignment of the axes of rotation of galaxies throughout the observable universe
JWST
has added weight to the earlier evidence of the same phenomenon discovered from, for example, the SDSS data sets
SDSS
.
One mechanism which has been proposed as driving this alignment is that of a rotating cosmology
JWST
. A rotating cosmology has also been suggested as possible method of resolving the Hubble tension between early and late time derived values for the Hubble constant
HUBBLETENSION
.

The earliest and perhaps best known of these cosmologies is that proposed by Kurt Gödel in 1949
GODEL1949
, which famously gave exact solutions to the Einstein Field Equations.
While this cosmology includes rotation it does not allow for expansion of the universe as supported by current observations e.g.
Hubble
.
In 2000 Yuri N. Obukhov constructed an extension to Gödel’s cosmology which does allow for an expanding as well as rotating universe
REFEG
, in which the author states it is an exact solution as per the original Gödel cosmology. Hereafter this extension is referred to as Expanding Gödel (EG) cosmology.
Indeed,
HUBBLETENSION
specifically cites
REFEG
as the model they are likely to use in further studies in the context of rotating cosmologies.

In order to study structure formation in such a cosmology it would be helpful to discover conserved quantities in that spacetime. More specifically, in cosmological perturbation theory (see e.g.
Bardeen80
;
KS
), these gauge invariant conserved perturbations enable us to relate early to late times in a given cosmological model (e.g.
Lyth85
), the standard cosmological model usually being a Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW)
Friedmann
background spacetime.

In this work we build on both the previous work in gauge invariant conserved quantities in FRW, for example the curvature perturabtion
\zeta
, as well as our previous work in Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi (LTB)
Bondi
and Lemaître spacetimes
Leithes2014
where we construct a similar conserved quantity
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}
which was conserved in those spacetimes as well as in FRW.

It should be noted that objections have been made in the past to the compatibility of rotating cosmologies with an expanding universe more generally. Somewhat analogous to a pirouetting ice-skater throwing out their arms to slow their rotation through conservation of angular momentum, it has been suggested that in an expanding universe the effects of any rotation would rapidly become negligible and undetectable throughout most of the history of the universe. Indeed, Obukhov briefly discusses these objections in his paper
REFEG
.
However, such a diminishing influence of rotation with time on structure formation seems to be supported by recent observations. The discovery of the alignment of galactic rotations in later epochs measured using SDSS data
SDSS
versus the same phenomenon detected to a greater degree through earlier epochs using JWST data
JWST
might be expected. In later times the alignment may have become disrupted to some degree by the peculiar motions of individual galaxies and clusters of galaxies and their interactions in the absence of a significant restoring force provided by any residual cosmological rotation. Whereas in earlier times, where the rotation and its aligning influence was more pronounced, the galaxy rotation alignment is correspondingly more significant.

This paper is structured as follows. In the following section
II
we present the general governing equations under General Relativity independent of a given spacetime.
In Section
III
we review Expanding Gödel cosmology at the background level for a pressureless dust matter content. We then extend this by adding perturbations to the Expanding Gödel background. We also examine the transformation behaviour of matter and metric variables and then construct gauge invariant quantities. In the last part of this section we look at the evolution of the spatial metric trace perturbation,
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}
. Finally in Section
V
we discuss the implications of our work for studies of Expanding Gödel spacetime, the evolution of structure therein, as well as other areas of further research in Cosmology more widely in which this work may prove useful.

II
General Governing equations

In this section we discuss the general governing equations from Einstein’s General Relativity without assuming any particular spacetime. We also do not yet split quantities into background plus perturbations. Here we restrict the discussion to just those quantities which were useful in this paper.

The Einstein field equations take the form

G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}\,,

(1)

G_{\mu\nu}
being the Einstein tensor,
T_{\mu\nu}
the
stress-energy-momentum tensor, and
G
is Newton’s constant.
The stress-energy-momentum tensor for a perfect fluid is given by,

T^{\mu\nu}=(\rho+P)u^{\mu}u^{\nu}+P\ g^{\mu\nu}\,,

(2)

where
g^{\mu\nu}
is the metric tensor,
\rho
is the energy density,

P
the pressure, and
u^{\mu}
the 4-velocity of the fluid.
However, we initially use the stress-energy-momentum tensor for pressureless dust

T^{\mu\nu}=\rho u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\,,

(3)

The metric tensor for any spacetime is subject to this constraint,

g^{\mu\nu}g_{\nu\gamma}={\delta^{\mu}}_{\gamma},

(4)

{\delta^{\mu}}_{\gamma}
being the Kronecker delta.
We define the 4-velocity as,

u^{\mu}=\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\tau}\,,

(5)

\tau
being the proper time along curves to which
u^{\mu}
is tangent, related to the line element
ds
through

ds^{2}=-d\tau^{2}\,.

(6)

The constraint for the 4-velocity is,

u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1\,.

(7)

Energy-momentum conservation is obtained from the Einstein equations,
Eq. (
1
), via the Bianchi identities,

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0\,.

(8)

The covariant derivative of a 4-vector can be decomposed as (see e.g.
Ellis
,
Wald84
),

\nabla_{\mu}u_{\nu}=-u_{\mu}u^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\nu}+\frac{1}{3}\Theta{\cal{P}}_{\mu\nu}+\sigma_{\mu\nu}+\omega_{\mu\nu}\,,

(9)

where we use vector,
u^{\mu}
, as an example, since Eq. (
9
) is true for all 4-vectors including the 4-velocity. Here

\Theta
is the expansion factor,
\sigma_{\mu\nu}
the shear
tensor,
\omega_{\mu\nu}
the vorticity tensor,
and
{\cal{P}}_{\mu\nu}
the spatial projection tensor.

The expansion factor defined with respect to the 4-velocity is,

\Theta=\nabla_{\mu}u^{\mu}\,,

(10)

III
Expanding Gödel (EG) spacetime

Here we first briefly review Expanding Gödel (EG) cosmology at the background level, highlighting minor notation changes we have made from that used by Obukhov in
REFEG
.
We then extend this by adding perturbations to the EG background. To facilitate removing any unwanted gauge modes, we examine the transformation behaviour of the perturbations, allowing us to construct gauge-invariant quantities, most notably an equivalent to
the curvature perturbation. We then show under which conditions this
curvature perturbation is conserved.

Throughout this section we assume zero background pressure in the
spacetime i.e. a pressureless dust matter content. We do however allow for a pressure perturbation in analysis of the perturbed quantities.
We intend to extend this work to a Perfect Fluid matter content which would include background pressure.

III.1
Background

The EG metric as written by Obukov, equation 4.1 in
REFEG
, closely follows the original form of the metric in Gödel’s non-expanding cosmology
GODEL1949
with the addition of a scale factor for the added expansion. We use a slightly modified notation in our version to make it closer to that used in our earlier work in LTB, Lemaître and FRW spacetimes
Leithes2014
, while retaining a similarity to the previous work in FRW more broadly e.g.
Lyth85
.
In our notation the metric may be written as,

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+2\sqrt{\sigma}X(x,t)dtdy+a^{2}(t)dx^{2}+\kappa X^{2}(x,t)dy^{2}+a^{2}(t)dz^{2},

(11)

where
a(t)
is the time only dependent scale factor, analogous to that found in standrd FRW cosmology, while
X
is the scale factor dependent upon both the
x
spatial and time co-ordinates similar to the scale factors found in LTB cosmology
Leithes2014
. Also similarly to LTB the two scale factors are not independent, but in EG the indices
0,1,2,3
are
t,x,y,z
respectively. The scale
factors are related by,

X=a(t)e^{mx},

(12)

where
m
is a constant.
The universal rotation is in the
z
direction and its magnitude,
\omega
is given by,

\omega=\frac{m}{2a}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{\kappa+\sigma}},

(13)

where
m
,
\kappa
and
\sigma
are simply constants. It can be seen that in the limit
\sigma=m=0
and
\kappa=1
we recover the FRW metric. For a more detailed description of the origin and form of these constants see
REFEG
.
The off-diagonal components of the background metric will clearly lead to more complicated equations in the following sections than those employed in FRW and to some degree even LTB and Lemaître spacetimes.

The background 4-velocity is given from its definition,
Eq. (
5
), as

u^{\mu}=[1,0,0,0]\,,

(14)

as we assume we are co-moving with respect to the background
and therefore,
dx=dy=dz=0
, and hence
d{\tau}^{2}=dt^{2}
in the local rest frame.

From the definition of the stress-energy-momentum tensor, Eq. (
2
), we find that for pressureless dust the only non-zero component is,
T^{00}=\rho
. For later convenience we define the Hubble parameter and one equivalent parameter for our
X
scale factor such that,

H=\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\,,\qquad H_{X}=\frac{\dot{X}}{X}\,.

(15)

where the “dot” denotes the derivative with respect to coordinate time
t
.

In order to construct the Einstein equations we must first find the connections, zero and non-zero, denoted by the Christoffel symbols below. In
REFEG
it seems that gauge selections have been made to yield a remarkably small number of non-zero connections - seven including duplicates. Here we make no initial gauge choices choosing to leave our expressions fully general, only later fixing gauges when dealing with perturbations in order to find gauge invariant conserved quantities. As such we find,

\begin{aligned} &{{\Gamma}}^{0}_{00}={{\Gamma}}^{2}_{02}={{\Gamma}}^{2}_{20}=H_{X}\,,2{{\Gamma}}^{0}_{01}={{\Gamma}}^{0}_{10}=\frac{2}{3}{{\Gamma}}^{2}_{12}=\frac{2}{3}{{\Gamma}}^{2}_{21}=\frac{X^{\prime}}{X}\,,{{\Gamma}}^{0}_{02}=\frac{1}{2}{{\Gamma}}^{0}_{20}=-{{\Gamma}}^{2}_{22}=\frac{\kappa\dot{X}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\,,{{\Gamma}}^{0}_{11}={{\Gamma}}^{0}_{33}=a\dot{a}\,,\\
&{{\Gamma}}^{0}_{12}={{\Gamma}}^{0}_{21}=\frac{\kappa X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}-\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}X^{\prime}}{2}\,,{{\Gamma}}^{0}_{22}=\kappa X\dot{X}\,,{{\Gamma}}^{1}_{01}={{\Gamma}}^{1}_{10}={{\Gamma}}^{3}_{03}={{\Gamma}}^{3}_{30}=H\,,{{\Gamma}}^{1}_{02}={{\Gamma}}^{1}_{20}=-\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}X^{\prime}}{2a^{2}}\,,{{\Gamma}}^{1}_{22}=-\frac{\kappa XX^{\prime}}{a^{2}}\,,\\
&{{\Gamma}}^{2}_{00}=\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}\dot{X}}{\kappa X^{2}}\,,{{\Gamma}}^{2}_{01}={{\Gamma}}^{2}_{10}=\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}X^{\prime}}{2\kappa X^{2}}\,,{{\Gamma}}^{2}_{11}=-\frac{a\dot{a}}{\sqrt{\sigma}X}\,,\end{aligned}

(16)

with the remaining thirty-eight connections all being zero. Here the “prime” denotes the derivative with respect to the spatial coordinate
x
.

In order to derive the Einstein equations we must first construct the components of the Ricci Tensor,
R_{\mu\nu}
, and then the Ricci Scaler,
R
. These may be found in Appendix
A.1
and Appendix
A.2

From Eq. (

H^{2}-\frac{3{H_{X}}^{2}}{2}+4HH_{X}+\frac{\sigma(X^{\prime})^{2}}{4\kappa X^{2}a^{2}}+\frac{\kappa\ddot{X}}{\sigma X}-\frac{9X^{\prime\prime}}{4Xa^{2}}-\frac{\kappa{H_{X}}^{2}}{2\sigma}+\frac{3(X^{\prime})^{2}}{8X^{2}a^{2}}+\frac{2\kappa HH_{X}}{\sigma}=8\pi G\rho\,.

(17)

For the

\frac{H_{X}X^{\prime}}{X}+\frac{3\dot{X}^{\prime}}{2X}-\frac{2HX^{\prime}}{X}-\frac{\sigma H_{X}X^{\prime}}{2\kappa X}=0\,.

(18)

For the
0-2

\frac{7\sqrt{\sigma}X^{\prime\prime}}{4a^{2}}+\frac{3\kappa H_{X}\dot{X}}{2\sqrt{\sigma}}+\frac{\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}(X^{\prime})^{2}}{4\kappa Xa^{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}(X^{\prime})^{2}}{8Xa^{2}}-\frac{3\sqrt{\sigma}H_{X}\dot{X}}{2}-\sqrt{\sigma}\ddot{X}-\frac{2\sqrt{\sigma}X\ddot{a}}{a}-2\sqrt{\sigma}H\dot{X}-\sqrt{\sigma}H^{2}X=0\,.

(19)

For the
1-1

a\ddot{a}+\frac{X^{\prime\prime}}{4X}+\frac{13(X^{\prime})^{2}}{8X^{2}}+\frac{\ddot{X}a^{2}}{X}+\frac{{H_{X}}^{2}a^{2}}{2}+\frac{\kappa\ddot{X}a^{2}}{\sigma X}+\frac{2\kappa a\dot{a}H_{X}}{\sigma}-\frac{3\sigma(X^{\prime})^{2}}{4\kappa X^{2}}-\frac{\kappa{H_{X}}^{2}a^{2}}{2\sigma}=0\,.

(20)

For the
1-2
component we find,

\frac{\kappa\dot{X}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}-\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}\dot{X}^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{3\kappa H_{X}X^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{\sigma}}-\sqrt{\sigma}H_{X}X^{\prime}=0\,.

(21)

For the
2-2
component we find,

\frac{\kappa\dot{X}^{2}}{2}+\frac{5\kappa(X^{\prime})^{2}}{8a^{2}}+\frac{5\kappa XX^{\prime\prime}}{4a^{2}}-\frac{5\kappa^{2}\dot{X}^{2}}{2\sigma}-\frac{2\kappa\ddot{a}X^{2}}{a}-\frac{\sigma(X^{\prime})^{2}}{4a^{2}}-\frac{\kappa^{2}\ddot{X}X}{\sigma}-\frac{2\kappa^{2}X\dot{X}H}{\sigma}-\frac{\kappa X^{2}H}{a}=0\,.

(22)

For the
3-3

a\ddot{a}+\frac{a^{2}\ddot{X}}{X}+\frac{a^{2}{H_{X}}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\kappa\ddot{X}a^{2}}{\sigma X}+\frac{3(X^{\prime})^{2}}{8X^{2}}+\frac{2\kappa a\dot{a}H_{X}}{\sigma}-\frac{\sigma(X^{\prime})^{2}}{4\kappa X^{2}}-\frac{9X^{\prime\prime}}{4X}-\frac{\kappa a^{2}{H_{X}}^{2}}{2\sigma}=0\,.

(23)

The
0-3
,
1-3
and
2-3

The energy conservation equation in EG spacetime for pressureless dust, obtained from Eq. (
8
), is

\dot{\rho}+2\frac{\dot{X}}{X}\rho+2\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\rho+\frac{\dot{X}}{X}\rho=0\,.

(24)

We first present here this less concise expanded form of the energy conservation equation rather than the simpler,

\dot{\rho}+3H_{X}\rho+2H\rho=0\,,

(25)

as it is not immediately obvious how this could reduce to the energy conservation equation in FRW in the limit
\sigma=m=0

\kappa=1

\dot{\rho}+2\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\rho+\frac{\dot{X}}{X}\rho=0\,.

(27)

From Eq. (

Throughout this work we use the Cadabra tensor manipulation package

. While this is an older package it does greatly facilitate the checking of the steps undertaken generating expressions, such as those in Eq. (

III.2
Perturbations

In this section we introduce perturbations to the EG background, while not decomposing these perturbations further into scalar, vector and tensor components. This follows our method in
Leithes2014
, which considerably simplifies our perturbed governing equations.

We do however split quantities into a
t
and
x
dependent background part where necessary, and a perturbation depending on all four coordinates. We decompose the energy density
\rho
as follows,

\rho=\bar{\rho}(t,x)+\delta\rho(x^{\mu})\,,

(28)

here and in the following a “bar” denotes a background quantity where there may be a possibility for confusion.

Now we perturb the metric in a similar way as in
Leithes2014
only here we use Cartesian spatial coordinates. We split the metric tensor as,

g_{\mu\nu}={\bar{g}}_{\mu\nu}+\delta g_{\mu\nu},

(29)

here
{\bar{g}}_{\mu\nu}
given by Eq. (
11
).

We now make the following ansatz for the perturbed metric,
\delta g_{\mu\nu}
,

\delta g_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}-2\Phi&aB_{x}&\sqrt{\sigma}XB_{y}&aB_{z}\\
aB_{x}&2a^{2}C_{xx}&aXC_{xy}&a^{2}C_{xz}\\
\sqrt{\sigma}XB_{y}&aXC_{xy}&2\kappa X^{2}C_{yy}&aXC_{yz}\\
aB_{z}&a^{2}C_{xz}&aXC_{yz}&2a^{2}C_{zz}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(30)

Here
\Phi
is the lapse function, while
B_{i}
, where
i=x,y,z
,
are the shift functions for each spatial coordinate. Similarly,

C_{ij}
, where
i,j=x,y,z
, form the spatial metric perturbations.

From the perturbed metric we can construct the perturbed
4-velocities using the definition, Eq. (
5
).
To linear order Proper time is in the perturbations given by,

d{\tau}=(1+\Phi)dt\,.

(31)

We define the 3-velocity as,

v^{i}=\frac{dx^{i}}{dt}\,.

(32)

From Eq. (
5
) we get the contravariant 4-velocity vector,

u^{\mu}=[(1-\Phi),v^{x},v^{y},v^{z}].

(33)

By using the perturbed metric we lower the index to obtain the
covariant form of the 4-velocity,

u_{\mu}=[-(1+\Phi)+\sqrt{\sigma}Xv^{y},aB_{x}+a^{2}v^{x},\,\sqrt{\sigma}X(B_{y}+1+\Phi)+\kappa X^{2}v^{y},\,aB_{z}+a^{2}v^{z}]\,.\\

(34)

This is more complicated than the equivalent in LTB or Lemaître spacetimes
Leithes2014
, and far more complicated than in FRW (see e.g.
Lyth85
), due in part to the off-diagonal components of the metric even at background Eq. (
11
).

As with the background, once again Eq. (
8
) together with Eq. (
2
) allows us to derive the perturbed energy conservation equation to linear order in perturbations as,

\begin{aligned} &\delta\dot{\rho}+{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}v^{x}+\bar{\rho}{v^{x}}^{\prime}+\bar{\rho}\partial_{y}v^{y}+\bar{\rho}\partial_{z}v^{z}-2\dot{\bar{\rho}}\Phi+2\dot{B}_{y}\bar{\rho}+\dot{C}_{xx}\bar{\rho}+\dot{C}_{yy}\bar{\rho}+\dot{C}_{zz}\bar{\rho}+3H_{X}\delta\rho+2H\delta\rho+3\frac{X^{\prime}}{X}v^{x}\bar{\rho}+2\frac{\kappa\dot{X}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}v^{y}\bar{\rho}\\
&-6H_{X}\Phi\bar{\rho}-4H\Phi\bar{\rho}+\frac{\partial_{y}\delta P}{\sqrt{\sigma}X}+\frac{\bar{\rho}\partial_{y}\Phi}{\sqrt{\sigma}X}+\frac{2\kappa H_{X}\delta P}{\sigma}+2H\delta P+H_{X}\delta P=0\,,\end{aligned}

(35)

where we used Eq. (
28
), and the pressureless dust background requiring
\bar{P}=0
. Once again Eq. (
35
) is more lengthy than the equivalents in LTB, Lemaître or FRW.

III.3
Gauge Transformations

As in
Leithes2014
we aim to construct gauge-invariant perturbations, and as such we need to examine the transformation behaviour of our matter and metric variables. As before, we use the Bardeen approach to cosmological
perturbation theory
Bardeen80
;
KS
. For an overview with references to the primary literature, see
MW2008

Using the active approach, tensorial linear order perturbations,
\mathbf{T}
, transform as

\delta\tilde{\mathbf{T}}=\delta\mathbf{T}+{\pounds}_{\delta x^{\mu}}\bar{\mathbf{T}}\,,

(36)

where a tilde denotes quantities evaluated in the “transformed” coordinate system.
The original and “transformed” - or new - coordinate systems are related by

\tilde{x}^{\mu}=x^{\mu}+\delta x^{\mu}\,,

(37)

where
\delta x^{\mu}=[\delta t,\delta x^{i}]
is the gauge generator.
Throughout we use the Lie derivative, denoted by
{\pounds}_{\delta x^{\mu}}
.

III.3.2
Geometric Perturbed Quantities

The expansion scalar, defined in Eq. (
10
) with
u^{\mu}
is calculated from the 4-velocity as given in Eq. (

\Theta=2H+2H_{X}+3\dot{\psi}+\dot{B}_{y}+\partial_{i}v^{i}-2H\Phi-2H_{X}\Phi+2\frac{X^{\prime}}{X}v^{x}\,.

In order to have the possibility to define hypersurfaces of

\begin{aligned} &{\tilde{\Theta}}=\Theta+\partial_{t}\left[(2H+2H_{X})\delta t+2\frac{X^{\prime}}{X}\delta x+\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}}{\kappa X}\partial_{y}\delta t+\partial_{i}\delta x^{i}+\dot{\delta t}+\frac{\kappa X}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\delta y-\frac{\partial_{y}\delta t}{\sqrt{\sigma}X}+\partial_{y}\delta y\right]\\

(58)

The transformation behaviour of

Gauge Invariant Quantities

III.4.1
Gauge-invariant Quantities in EG Spacetime

We now construct gauge-invariant quantities in the perturbed EG model, taking the FRW case as guidance.

Taking our earlier spatial metric perturbation,

, Eq. (

), and its transformation behaviour, Eq. (

\delta t\Big|_{\delta\tilde{\rho}=0}=-\frac{1}{\dot{{\bar{\rho}}}}\left[\delta\rho+{\bar{\rho}}^{\prime}\delta x\right]\,.

(59)

Substituting this into Eq. (

-{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}=\psi-\frac{1}{3}\left[2H+H_{X}\right]\left(\frac{\delta\rho+\bar{\rho}^{\prime}\delta x}{\dot{\bar{\rho}}}\right)+\frac{X^{\prime}}{3X}\delta x-\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}}{3\kappa X\dot{\bar{\rho}}}\left[\partial_{y}\delta\rho+\bar{\rho}^{\prime}\partial_{y}\delta x\right]+\frac{1}{3}\partial_{i}\delta x^{i}\,,

We now choose co-moving hypersurfaces to fix the remaining spatial

\delta x^{i}=\int v^{i}dt\,.

(61)

Substituting the above equations into Eq. (

-{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}=\psi-\frac{1}{3\dot{\bar{\rho}}}\left[2H+H_{X}\right]\left(\delta\rho+\bar{\rho}^{\prime}\int v^{x}dt\right)+\frac{X^{\prime}}{3X}\int v^{x}dt-\frac{\sqrt{\sigma}}{3\kappa X\dot{\bar{\rho}}}\left[\partial_{y}\delta\rho+\bar{\rho}^{\prime}\partial_{y}\int v^{x}dt\right]+\frac{1}{3}\partial_{i}\int v^{i}dt\,.

is gauge invariant.

Instead of using
\delta\rho

\delta\tilde{\rho}\Big|_{\tilde{\psi}=0,\tilde{v^{i}}=0}=\delta\rho+\frac{\dot{\bar{\rho}}}{2H+H_{X}}\left[3\psi\right]\,,

\begin{aligned} &\delta\tilde{\rho}\Big|_{\tilde{\psi}=0,\tilde{v^{i}}=0}=\delta\rho+\frac{\dot{\bar{\rho}}}{2H+H_{X}}\Big[3{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}-\left[2H+H_{X}\right]\frac{1}{\dot{\bar{\rho}}}\left(\delta\rho+\bar{\rho}^{\prime}\int v^{x}dt\right)+\frac{X^{\prime}}{X}\int v^{x}dt\\

III.5
Evolution of
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}

Before deriving the evolution equation for spatial metric trace perturbation
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}
, we briefly discuss the pressure perturbation in EG spacetime. We assume that the pressure
P=P(\rho,S)
, where
\rho
is the density and
S
the entropy of the system. We can expand the pressure and obtain the pressure perturbation as,

\delta P=c_{\rm{s}}^{2}\delta\rho+\delta P_{\mathrm{nad}}\,,

(66)

where
\delta P_{\mathrm{nad}}
is the non-adiabatic pressure perturbation, and the adiabatic sound speed is defined as

c_{\rm{s}}^{2}\equiv\left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho}\right|_{S}\,,

(67)

Since in EG background quantities are
t
and
x
dependent, we assume for now
\bar{P}\equiv\bar{P}(t,x)
, and find that

c_{\rm{s}}^{2}=\frac{\dot{\bar{P}}+\bar{P}^{\prime}v^{x}}{\dot{\bar{\rho}}+\bar{\rho}^{\prime}v^{x}}\,.

(68)

Since for our EG spacetime setup we initially assume pressureless dust as our matter content for simplicity,
\bar{P}=0
, we have that on uniform density
hypersurfaces
\delta P=\delta P_{\mathrm{nad}}
.

The evolution equation for spatial metric trace perturbation on uniform density and co-moving hypersurfaces,
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}

62

35
) and background conservation equation, Eq. (

{\dot{\zeta}}_{\rm{SMTP}}=\frac{1}{3}\left(2H+H_{X}+\frac{2\kappa}{\sigma}H_{X}\right)\frac{\delta P_{\mathrm{nad}}}{\bar{\rho}}\,.

(69)

Therefore
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}
is conserved in the large scale limit for
\delta P_{\mathrm{nad}}=0
, e.g. for barotropic fluids. This is similar to
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}
in Lemaître spacetime, but differs from LTB, which was conserved on all scales - at least for barotropic fluids
Leithes2014
.

This result is also similar to the standard curvature perturbation,
\zeta
, in the FRW case
WMLL

69
) is again slightly more complicated than the equivalent in FRW.

V
Discussion and conclusion

In this paper we constructed gauge-invariant quantities in a perturbed EG spacetime. Specifically, we have constructed the
gauge-invariant spatial metric trace perturbation on co-moving, uniform density hypersurfaces,
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}
.

We derived the evolution equations for
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}
and found that it is conserved on large scales for barotropic fluids (when
\delta P_{\mathrm{nad}}=0
). This is similar to the standard FRW result, where the similar gauge-invariant quantity,
\zeta
, is only conserved on large scales.

Deriving these results in EG is more involved than in either the LTB, Lemaître or FRW cases, in part because the background metric Eq. (
11
) contains off-diagonal components, complicating the form of background quantities, and further complicating the perturbed quantities.

The main application of these results lies in providing conserved quantities in EG spacetime. Conserved quantities have proved to be very useful in standard cosmology with FRW, as they can be used to directly relate observable quantities, such as the density perturbation between earlier and later times, without having to solve the field equations. In addition, these conserved quantities can be used as a consistency check for numerical simulations, as for example those proposed in
HUBBLETENSION
. By solving for the density contrast numerically and comparing this result to the one obtained by using
{\zeta}_{\rm{SMTP}}
, the accuracy of the code can easily be checked.

As explained by Obukhov
REFEG
the EG model is part of the wider class of Bianchi type III models, and identifying gauge-invariant conserved quantities within the class more broadly would be a useful avenue of further research, with a particular focus on spacetimes with rotation.

One attractive feature of the EG model is that it rapidly reduces to standard FRW with the ongoing expansion of the universe. Nevertheless, given the entire universe is initially rotating this should lead to observable consequences, not least of which would be identifying where this axis of rotation lies. This axis could lie within the currently observable universe, or beyond the particle horizon, but observational evidence should still exist in either case and further research would be needed to identify its location in either eventuality.

A rotating spacetime such as EG could also contribute to the anomalous CMB dipole (see e.g.
(
DipoleA
;
DipoleB
)
), particularly if we are situated a significant distance from the axis of rotation. Even given the magnitude of rotation has reached negligible levels in later times - and the cosmology currently conforms to FRW - this rotation in earlier epochs, and the location of its axis, could still have had a detectable effect on the CMB radiation. For an interesting review of some of the observations which are in tension with standard FRW cosmology see e.g.
Alurietal
.

Finally, recent detections by the JWST of large galaxies in the very early universe (see e.g.
EarlygalaxyA
;
EarlygalaxyB
) seem to disagree with current models of galaxy formation and the time-line for such formation. If in the very early universe the rotation still had significant dynamical effects on galaxy formation, as suggested by the galaxy rotation alignment results
JWST
, perhaps there were further effects on the evolution of these early galaxies. Possibly with the entire spacetime rotating the entire universe shared some attributes - though on a far larger scale - with accretion disks around stars, possibly compressing the distribution of matter overall in the
z
-axis i.e. the axis of rotation as defined in the EG model
REFEG
. This compression could aid and accelerate the formation of galaxies analogous to the formation of planets within stellar accretion disks. Again, this could prove a valuable avenue of further research.

Sparsification of sums with respect to convex cones
James Saunderson

Abstract
Let x 1 , x 2 , … , x m x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m} be elements of a convex cone K K such that their sum, e e , is in the relative interior of K K . An ϵ \epsilon -sparsification of the sum involves taking a subset of the x i x_{i} and reweighting them by positive scalars, so that the resulting sum is ϵ \epsilon -close to e e , where error is measured in a relative sense with respect to the order induced by K K . This generalizes the influential spectral sparsification model for sums of positive semidefinite matrices. This paper introduces and studies the sparsification function of a convex cone, which measures, in the worst case over all possible sums from the cone, the smallest size of an ϵ \epsilon -sparsifier. The linear-sized spectral sparsification theorem of Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava can be viewed as a bound on the sparsification function of the cone of positive semidefinite matrices. This result is generalized to a family of convex cones (including all hyperbolicity cones) that admit a ν \nu -logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier with certain additional properties. For these cones, the sparsification function is bounded above by ⌈ 4 ​ ν / ϵ 2 ⌉ \lceil 4\nu/\epsilon^{2}\rceil . For general convex cones that only admit an ordinary ν \nu -logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier, the sparsification function is bounded above by ⌈ ( 4 ​ ν / ϵ ) 2 ⌉ \lceil(4\nu/\epsilon)^{2}\rceil . Furthermore, the paper explores how sparsification functions interact with various convex geometric operations (such as conic lifts), and describes implications of sparsification with respect to cones for certain conic optimization problems.



1
Introduction

The starting point for this paper is the celebrated linear-sized spectral sparsification
result of Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava
[
2
]
, in the general rank form given in
[
37
]
. This result
describes how a collection of
d\times d
positive semidefinite matrices (elements of
\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}
) that sum to a positive definite matrix (i.e., element of

\textup{int}(\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d})
), can be approximated in a certain spectral sense by keeping only a small number of terms in the sum and suitably reweighting them.
Here, and throughout, we use the notation
X\preceq Y
to mean that
Y-X
is positive semidefinite.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}
be such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}=E\in\textup{int}(\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d})

and let
0<\epsilon<1
. Then there exists a subset
S\subseteq[m]
with

|S|\leq\lceil 4d/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
and positive scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
such that

(1-\epsilon)E\preceq\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}X_{i}\preceq(1+\epsilon)E.

This result was motivated by the study of graph sparsification. Indeed a weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
with vertex set
V
, edge set
E
, and edge weights
(w_{e})_{e\in E}
,
can be represented by its weighted Laplacian

L_{G}=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{\{i,j\}}(e_{i}-e_{i})(e_{i}-e_{j})^{\intercal}.

If
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
and
(w^{\prime}_{e})_{e\in E^{\prime}}
is another weight function, a sparsification of
L_{G}
is a sum of the form

L_{G^{\prime}}=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E^{\prime}}w_{\{i,j\}}^{\prime}(e_{i}-e_{j})(e_{i}-e_{j})^{\intercal}

which can be interpreted as the weighted Laplacian of a new weighted graph
G^{\prime}=(V,E^{\prime},w^{\prime})
with fewer edges. If
L_{G}
and
L_{G^{\prime}}

satisfy
(1-\epsilon)L_{G}\preceq L_{G^{\prime}}\preceq(1+\epsilon)L_{G}
for some
0<\epsilon<1
then many quantitative properties of
G
are approximated
by the corresponding properties of
G^{\prime}
. This idea was initially studied specifically in the context of graph cuts by Benczúr and Karger
[
4
]
.
The idea of constructing spectral sparsifiers, i.e., those that approximate the Laplacian in this spectral sense, was pioneered by Spielman and Teng
[
41
]
.
Before
[
2
]
the number of edges in the known cut or spectral sparsifiers
was
\widetilde{O}(|V|/\epsilon^{2})
, where the
\widetilde{O}(\cdot)
hides poly-logarithmic factors
(see, e.g.,
[
4
]
,
[
40
]
).
The major breakthrough of
[
2
]
was to obtain a linear-sized spectral sparsifier,
i.e., one with number of edges that is
O(|V|/\epsilon^{2})
.

This paper investigates analogues of linear-sized spectral sparsification for
general closed convex cones. If
K
is a closed convex cone and

x,y\in\textup{span}(K)
then we use the notation
x\preceq_{K}y
to mean that
y-x\in K
.
If
K
is, in addition, pointed (i.e.,
K\cap(-K)=\{0\}
) then
\preceq_{K}
is a partial order
on
\textup{span}(K)
. The following definitions introduce concise language
to help keep track of the extent to which arbitrary sums of elements from a convex cone
can be sparsified to within a given error (measured relative to the cone).

Definition 1.2
.

Let
K
be a closed convex cone.
A
sparsification function for
K
is a monotonically non-increasing upper semi-continuous
function
\alpha:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
for any
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\in K
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=e\in\textup{relint}(K)

and any
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists
S\subseteq[m]
with
|S|\leq\alpha(\epsilon)

and positive scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
, such that

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e.

(1)

Definition 1.3
.

Let
K
be a closed convex cone.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{K}
denote the collection of all sparsification functions for
K
.
The
sparsification function of
K
, denoted
\textsc{sp}_{K}:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, is defined by

\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\alpha(\epsilon).

Note that the pointwise infimum of monotonically non-increasing upper semi-continuous functions is again a monotonically non-increasing upper semi-continuous function. Therefore,
\textsc{sp}_{K}
is a well-defined sparsification function for
K
.

It follows directly from Carathéodory’s theorem for cones that
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\dim(K)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. (See Lemma
3.1
in Section
3
.) Sparsification functions are
interesting if, for a non-trivial range of
\epsilon
, they give bounds that are smaller than the dimension of the cone. In such cases
there is a meaningful trade-off between the approximation error (quantified by
\epsilon
) and the amount that conic
combinations of points can be sparsified.

The linear-sized spectral sparsification Theorem (Theorem
1.1
), when stated in the language of Definition
1.3
, says that the sparsification function
of
\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}
satisfies
\textsc{sp}_{\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil 4d/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
. If
\sqrt{\frac{8}{d+1}}<\epsilon<1
then
\textsc{sp}_{\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}}(\epsilon)

is smaller than the trivial bound of
\dim(\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d})=\binom{d+1}{2}
from Carathéodory’s theorem.

1.1
Contributions

This paper develops methods to give upper bounds on sparsification functions of closed convex cones,
with a focus on bounds that are potentially much smaller than the dimension of the cone. While the results are constructive,
the emphasis is on the existence of constructions rather than efficient algorithms.
The most general result in this direction applies to arbitrary closed, pointed, full-dimensional
convex cones (which we refer to as
proper cones
)
and is the subject of Section
4
.
It gives a bound on the sparsification function that depends only on the barrier parameter
of a logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for the cone.
(See Section
2.3
for definitions of these terms.)

Theorem 1.4
.

If
K
is a proper cone with a
\nu
-logarithmically
homogeneous self-concordant barrier then
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil(4\nu/\epsilon)^{2}\rceil
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Section
7.2
considers examples where this bound on the sparsification function is independent of the dimension of
K
, and so significantly improves over the bound from Carathéodory’s theorem. However, in the case of the positive semidefinite cone, it is known that
d

is the smallest possible parameter for a logarithmically
homogeneous self-concordant barrier for
\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}

[
32
]
. Therefore, when
specialized to the positive semidefinite cone, Theorem
1.5

cannot improve upon the trivial dimension-based bound from Carathéodory’s theorem,
let alone match Theorem
1.1
.

The next contribution of this paper, and the focus of Section
5
,
is a bound on the sparsification function of
proper cones that admit a
\nu
-logarithmically-homogeneous self-concordant barrier
with certain additional properties. Such barriers will be called
pairwise-self-concordant
barriers in this paper, see Definition
5.2
.
Remarkably, for pairwise-self-concordant barriers there is a bound on the sparsification function
that is linear in
\nu
, the barrier parameter.
This result directly generalizes Theorem
1.1
, and is established by suitably generalizing
the barrier method originally developed in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

If
K
is a proper cone with a
\nu
-logarithmically
homogeneous pairwise-self-concordant barrier then
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil 4\nu/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Hyperbolic polynomials are multivariate polynomials with real coefficients that enjoy certain
real-rootedness properties (see Section
2.2
for the definition). Associated
with a hyperbolic polynomial is a convex cone called a hyperbolicity cone.
Hyperbolicity cones include the positive
semidefinite cone as a special case. If
p
is a hyperbolic polynomial of degree
d
,
then it is well-known that
-\log p
is a
d
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for the associated hyperbolicity cone
[
13
]
.
We will see that such hyperbolic barriers are, in fact, pairwise-self-concordant. As a corollary we obtain a generalization of Theorem
1.1
for hyperbolicity cones.

Corollary 1.6
.

Suppose that
p
is a hyperbolic polynomial of degree
d
, hyperbolic with respect to
e
.
Let
\Lambda_{+}
denote the associated hyperbolicity cone. Then
\textsc{sp}_{\Lambda_{+}}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil 4d/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
.

By taking the hyperbolic polynomial
p
to be the determinant restricted to
d\times d
symmetric matrices (a polynomial of degree
d
), the associated hyperbolicity cone is the positive semidefinite cone
\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}
. Theorem
1.1
is, therefore, a special case of Corollary
1.6
.

Section
3
considers how some basic convex geometric operations affect sparsification functions. (See Section
2.1
for notation and terminology related to convex geometry.)
The main result in this direction is that sparsification functions interact nicely with the following
notion of lifted representations (or
extended formulations
) of convex cones
[
12
,
8
]
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
C
and
K
be closed convex cones. We say that
C
has a
K
-lift
if
there is a linear subspace
L\subseteq\textup{span}(K)
and a linear map

\pi:\textup{span}(K)\rightarrow\textup{span}(C)
such that
C=\pi(L\cap K)
. If, in addition,

L\cap\textup{relint}(K)
is non-empty, we say that
C
has a
proper
K
-lift
.

The following result is established in Section
3
.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
C
and
K
be closed convex cones.
If
C
has a proper
K
-lift then
\textsc{sp}_{C}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

The main difficulty in establishing Proposition
1.8
is to show that if
C=\pi(K)
where
C
and
K
are closed convex cones and
\pi
is a linear map, then the sparsification function of
C
is bounded above by the sparsification function of
K
(Lemma
3.3
).

It is natural to consider whether Proposition
1.8
can be used in combination with either
Theorem
1.5
(for general convex cones) or Corollary
1.6
(for hyperbolicity cones)
to obtain further results. If
C
has a proper
K
-lift then any
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for
K
can be used to construct a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for
C
via the implicit barrier theorem
[
32
, Theorem 5.3.6]
. Therefore, combining Proposition
1.8
with Theorem
1.5
does not yield any further results. However, Proposition
1.8
can be used profitably in combination with Corollary
1.6
, to give the following result.

Corollary 1.9
.

Suppose that
p
is a hyperbolic polynomial of degree
d
, hyperbolic with
respect to
e
with associated
hyperbolicity cone
\Lambda_{+}
.
Let
C
be a closed convex cone. If
C
has a
\Lambda_{+}
-lift then
\textsc{sp}_{C}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil 4d/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

This result is interesting because, in general, there are convex cones that have a
\Lambda_{+}
-lift for some hyperbolicity cone but that are not, themselves, hyperboliticy cones. One way to see this is to observe that hyperbolicity cones are always facially exposed
[
35
]
,
but general convex cones with
\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}
-lifts—let alone hyperbolicity cone lifts—need not be facially exposed
[
34
]
.

The astute reader will notice that Corollary
1.9
is not quite an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.6
and Proposition
1.8
. This is because Corollary
1.9
only assumes that
C
has a
\Lambda_{+}(p,e)
-lift, not a
proper

\Lambda_{+}(p,e)
-lift. The proof of Corollary
1.9
relies on certain properties of the faces of hyperbolicity cones, and is given in Section
7.1
.

1.2
Related work

Since the work of Benczúr and Karger
[
4
]
and the spectral sparsification formulation of Spielman and Teng
[
41
]
,
many variations and extensions on the problems of cut and spectral sparsification have been studied. This section briefly discusses a selection of such generalizations, focusing
on existence results rather than algorithmic advances.

Spectral-like sparsification models have been developed for combinatorial objects such as linear codes
[
19
]
, hypergraphs
[
39
]
, or constraint satisfaction problems
[
20
]
. For instance, given a hypergraph with
m
hyperedges
E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{m}
each of which is a subset of
[n]
,
the aim is to find
S\subseteq[m]
and positive scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
such that

(1-\epsilon)\sum_{i\in[m]}\max_{j,k\in E_{i}}(x_{j}-x_{k})^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}\max_{j,k\in E_{i}}(x_{j}-x_{k})^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i\in[m]}\max_{j,k\in E_{i}}(x_{j}-x_{k})^{2}\quad\textup{for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

This reduces to the spectral sparsification model for graphs when all hyperedges have degree two, but appears to be
structurally different to the model in Definition
1.3
in general. In this setting spectral hypergraph sparsifiers exist with
|S|
of size
O(\epsilon^{-2}\log(D)n\log n)
where
D
is the maximum size of a hyperedge
[
22
,
17
]
.

Another family of sparsification problems comes from approximating sums of (semi-)norms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
16
]
.
Given a collection
N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{m}
of semi-norms, the aim is to find
S\subseteq[m]
and positive scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
such that

(1-\epsilon)\sum_{i\in[m]}N_{i}(x)\leq\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}N_{i}(x)\leq(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i\in[m]}N_{i}(x)\;\;\textup{for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

This generalizes the problem of approximating a centrally symmetric zonotope with
m
generators (or, more generally, a zonoid)
by a centrally symmetric zonotope with a small number of generators (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
). This is because the support function
of the zonotope
[-a_{1},a_{1}]+\cdots+[-a_{m},a_{m}]
(for
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
) is
\sum_{i\in[m]}N_{i}(x)
where
N_{i}(x)=|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|
.
For the general problem of sparsifying sums of norms, the set
S
can be taken to be of size
O(\epsilon^{-2}n\log(n/\epsilon)(\log(n))^{2.5})

[
16
]
.

Related to spectral sparsification of quadratic forms (or equivalently positive semidefinite matrices) is the Kadison-Singer problem,
resolved by Marcus, Spielman, and Srivastava
[
29
]
. In one of its equivalent forms (see
[
33
, Theorem 1.2]
),
this says that given
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is a
partition of
[m]=T_{1}\cup T_{2}
such that, for
j\in\{1,2\}
,

\left(\frac{1}{2}-5\sqrt{\alpha}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}v_{i}^{\intercal}\right)\preceq\sum_{i\in T_{j}}v_{i}v_{i}^{\intercal}\preceq\left(\frac{1}{2}+5\sqrt{\alpha}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}v_{i}^{\intercal}\right)

where
\alpha=\max_{j}v_{j}^{\intercal}\left(\sum_{i\in[m]}v_{i}v_{i}^{\intercal}\right)^{+}v_{j}
is the maximum of the leverage scores of the
v_{j}
. This result shows that
the positive semidefinite matrix
\sum_{i}v_{i}v_{i}^{\intercal}
can be approximated well by summing over either the terms in
T_{1}
or the terms in
T_{2}
, without any reweighting.
On the other hand, the approximation quality depends on the leverage scores of the vectors, and is only small when all of the vectors contribute in a balanced way to the
sum. Paschalidis and Zhuang have shown that this result implies linear sized spectral sparsification
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
, but not with the refined constants of Theorem
1.1
.
The solution of the Kadison-Singer problem has been generalized to hyperbolicity cones by Brändén
[
7
]
. It seems likely
that a similar reduction could be carried
out to deduce linear-sized spectral sparsification with respect to hyperbolicity cones, yielding a qualitatively similar result to Corollary
1.6
,
but again without the refined constants.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Convex geometry

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the notation
\textup{cone}(S)
(resp.,
\textup{conv}(S)
) to denote the conic hull (resp., convex hull) of
S
, i.e., the collection of all non-negative (resp., convex) combinations of elements of
S
. A convex cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
pointed
if
K\cap(-K)=\{0\}
, and
full-dimensional
if
\textup{span}(K)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote its closure with respect to the usual topology by
\textup{cl}(S)
and its relative interior (i.e., its interior taken relative to its affine span) by
\textup{relint}{(S)}
.
We call a closed, pointed, full-dimensional convex cone a
proper cone
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote a fixed choice of inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which allows us
to identify
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with its dual space.
If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set then
S^{*}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;\langle y,x\rangle\geq 0,\;\;\textup{for all $x\in S$}\}
is a closed convex cone called the
dual cone
of
S
.

If
K
is a closed convex cone, then a convex subset
F\subseteq K
is a
face
if
x,y\in K
and
x+y\in F
implies that
x,y\in F
. A face
F\subseteq K
is
exposed
if there exists
y\in K^{*}
such that
F=\{x\in K\;:\;\langle y,x\rangle=0\}
, i.e., if
F
can be expressed as the intersection of
K
with a supporting hyperplane to
K
.

2.2
Hyperbolic polynomials and hyperbolicity cones

A polynomial
p
, homogeneous of degree
d
, in
n
variables with real coefficients is
hyperbolic with respect to
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if

p(e)>0
and, for every fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if
p(te-x)=0
then
t
is real. A fundamental example to keep in mind is the determinant restricted to symmetric
d\times d
matrices.
This is hyperbolic with respect to the
d\times d
identity matrix because
p(te-x)=\det(tI-x)
is the characteristic polynomial of the symmetric matrix
x
, which has only real roots because symmetric matrices have real eigenvalues.

If
p
is hyperbolic with respect to
e
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
then we denote by
\lambda_{1}^{(p,e)}(x)\geq\lambda_{2}^{(p,e)}(x)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}^{(p,e)}(x)

the (real) roots of
p(te-x)=0
, and call these the
hyperbolic eigenvalues of
x
with respect to
(p,e)
.

Associated with any hyperbolic polynomial
p
, and direction of hyperbolicity
e
, is the
hyperbolicity cone

\Lambda_{+}(p,e)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;\lambda_{i}^{(p,e)}(x)\geq 0\;\;\textup{for all $i=1,2,\ldots,d$}\},

which turns out to be a closed convex cone
[
11
]
. In the case of the determinant restricted to
d\times d
symmetric matrices, the hyperbolicity cone
\Lambda_{+}(\det,I)
is the cone
\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}
of
d\times d
positive semidefinite matrices. If
p
is hyperbolic with respect to
e
then
p
is also hyperbolic with respect to any
e^{\prime}\in\textup{int}(\Lambda_{+}(p,e))
, and
\Lambda_{+}(p,e)=\Lambda_{+}(p,e^{\prime})

[
11
]
.

If
p
is hyperbolic with respect to
e
, we say that
p
is
complete
if
p(te-x)=t^{d}p(e)
implies that
x=0
(i.e., the only point with all hyperbolic eigenvalues
equal to zero is
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
).
If
p
is a complete hyperbolic polynomial then it follows from the definitions that
the associated hyperbolicity cone is pointed.

2.3
Self-concordant barriers

This section summarizes the basic definitions and facts (following
[
32
]
) about logarithmically homogeneous
self-concordant barriers for convex cones needed for subsequent developments.

For a
k
-times
continuously differentiable function
f
with open domain, a point
x\in\textup{dom}(f)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and directions
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define

D^{k}f(x)[u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}]:=\left.\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t_{1}\cdots\partial t_{k}}f(x+t_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+t_{k}u_{k})\right|_{t_{1}=\cdots=t_{k}=0}.

Note that
D^{k}f(x)[u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}]
is multilinear in the
u_{i}
and that
D^{k}f(x)[u,\ldots,u]
is
the
k
th directional derivative of
f
at
x
in the direction
u
.

Definition 2.1
.

A three-times continuously differentiable closed convex function

\varphi:\textup{dom}(\varphi)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with open domain
\textup{dom}(\varphi)
is
standard self-concordant
if

|D^{3}\varphi(x)[u,u,u]|\leq 2D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,u]^{3/2}

for all
x\in\textup{dom}(\varphi)
and all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that
\varphi
is a standard self-concordant function and
\textup{cl}(\textup{dom}(\varphi))=:K

is a closed full-dimensional convex cone. We say that
\varphi
is a

\nu
-
logarithmically-homogeneous self-concordant barrier
for

K
if
\varphi(tx)=\varphi(x)-\nu\log(t)
for all
x\in\textup{dom}(\varphi)
and all
t>0
.

Suppose that
\varphi
is a
\nu
-logarithmically-homogeneous self-concordant barrier for a closed, full-dimensional, convex cone
K
. Then we can associate with each point
e\in\textup{int}(K)
the
Hessian (semi-)norm

\|u\|_{e}:=D^{2}\varphi(e)[u,u]^{1/2}.

(2)

If
K
is a proper cone (so is, in addition, pointed), then the Hessian semi-norm is non-degenerate
[
32
, Theorem 5.1.6]
, and so is actually a norm.

For a full-dimensional closed convex cone
K
and
x\in\textup{int}(K)
,
for each
u
we can also define
an analogue of the spectral norm via

|u|_{x}:=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;-tx\preceq_{K}u\preceq_{K}tx\}.

(3)

If
K
is, in addition, pointed then
|u|_{x}=0

if and only if
u=0
, and so
|\cdot|_{x}
defines a
norm.
The following result collects some standard facts about
logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant functions and these associated norms.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed
full-dimensional convex cone. Let
\varphi
be a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for

. Then the following properties hold.

(i)

[
32
, Theorem 5.4.3]
If
e\in\textup{int}(K)
then
D\varphi(e)[e]=-\nu
.

(ii)

[
32
, Theorem 5.1.14]
If
u\in K
and

then
-D\varphi(e)[u]\geq\|u\|_{e}
.

(iii)

[
32
, Theorem 5.1.5]
If
e\in\textup{int}(K)
then
\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;\|z-e\|_{e}\leq 1\}\subseteq K
.

(iv)

If
e\in\textup{int}(K)
then
\|u\|_{e}\geq|u|_{e}
for all
u
.

Proof.

We only establish (iv), since the other results are directly from
[
32
]
.
Let
u
be arbitrary. To establish that
|u|_{e}\leq\|u\|_{e}
, it is enough to show that

-\|u\|_{e}e\preceq_{K}u\preceq_{K}\|u\|_{e}e.

(4)

To establish (
4
) we observe that

\|(e\pm(\|u\|_{e}+\epsilon)^{-1}u)-e\|_{e}=\frac{\|u\|_{e}}{\|u\|_{e}+\epsilon}<1

for all
\epsilon>0
. It follows from (iii) that
(\|u\|_{e}+\epsilon)e\pm u\in K

for all
\epsilon>0
. Taking the limit as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0

is closed, establishes (

).
∎

2.4
Operator monotone functions

Let
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval and let
\mathcal{S}_{J}^{d}
denote the collection of
d\times d
symmetric
matrices with eigenvalues in
J
. Given a function
h:J\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the associated spectral
function
h:\mathcal{S}_{J}^{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{d}
is defined by

h(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}h(\lambda_{i})P_{i}

where
\lambda_{1}>\cdots>\lambda_{k}
are the distinct eigenvalues of
X
, and each
P_{i}
is the orthogonal projector onto the eigenspace of
X
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
.

A function
h:J\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
n
-monotone
for some positive integer
n
if
X,Y\in\mathcal{S}_{J}^{n}
and
X\succeq Y
together imply that
h(X)\succeq h(Y)
. A function that is
n
-monotone
for all positive integers
n
is called
operator monotone
. Löwner’s theorem characterizes operator monotone functions. Indeed, any operator monotone function
h:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, defined on the positive half-line, can be expressed in the form

h(t)=h(1)+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t-1}{\lambda(t-1)+1}\;d\mu(\lambda)\;\;\textup{for all $t\in(0,\infty)$}

(5)

where
\mu
is a positive Borel measure
\mu
supported on
[0,1]
. Note that
similar integral representations hold for operator monotone functions defined on other intervals.
Moreover, there are many different equivalent ways to write integral representations of operator monotone functions
[
38
]
. For a version of Löwner’s theorem written in the form of (
5
), see
[
9
, Theorem 4]
.

3
Basic results about sparsification functions

3.1
Upper bounds

This section establishes some bounds on sparsification functions of convex cones arising from
basic convex geometric considerations.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
K
is a closed convex cone then
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\dim(K)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in K
, let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, and let
e=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\in\textup{relint}(K)
.
Let
C
be the conic hull of the
x_{i}
, i.e,
C=\textup{cone}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}\subseteq K
.
By Carathéodory’s theorem,
there exists
S\subseteq[m]
with
|S|\leq\dim(C)\leq\dim(K)
and positive scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}

such that
\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}=e
, and so

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}e=\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq(1+\epsilon)e.

It follows that
\dim(K)
is a sparsification function for
K
, and hence that

\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\dim(K)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
by the definition of
\textsc{sp}_{K}
.
∎

The following result shows that sparsification functions do not increase under taking the intersection
with a subspace that meets the relative interior of a cone.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be a closed convex cone and let
L
be a linear subspace such that

\textup{relint}(K)\cap L\neq\emptyset
. If
C=K\cap L
then
\textsc{sp}_{C}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\in C=K\cap L
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=e\in\textup{relint}(K\cap L)
and

0<\epsilon<1
.
Since
\textup{relint}(K)\cap L\neq\emptyset
it follows that
\textup{relint}(K)\cap L=\textup{relint}(K\cap L)

[
36
, Corollary 6.5.1]
.
Therefore
e\in\textup{relint}(K)
. From the definition of
\textsc{sp}_{K}
,
there exists some
S\subseteq[m]
with
|S|\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
and positive
scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
such that

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e.

Since
e\in L
and
\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\in L
it follows that

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{C}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq_{C}(1+\epsilon)e,

and so that
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
is a sparsification function for
C
. The inequality
\textsc{sp}_{C}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
then follows from the definition of
\textsc{sp}_{C}
.
∎

Sparsification functions also do not increase under linear projections.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K
be a closed convex cone and let
\pi:\textup{span}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}

be a linear map. Then
\textsc{sp}_{\pi(K)}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Moreover, if
\pi
is a linear isomorphism then
\textsc{sp}_{\pi(K)}(\epsilon)=\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\in\pi(K)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=e\in\textup{relint}(\pi(K))
and
0<\epsilon<1
.
Let
x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m}^{\prime}\in K
be such that
\pi(x_{i}^{\prime})=x_{i}
for
i\in[m]
. Let
e^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{\prime}
.
Since
\pi
is linear,
\pi(e^{\prime})=e
. Since
x_{i}^{\prime}\in K
for all
i
, it follows that
e^{\prime}\in K
.
There are two cases to consider, depending on whether or not
e^{\prime}\in\textup{relint}(K)
.

If
e^{\prime}\in\textup{relint}(K)
then, by the definition of
\textsc{sp}_{K}
, there exists
S\subseteq[m]
with
|S|\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)

and positive scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
such that
(1-\epsilon)e^{\prime}\preceq_{K}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}^{\prime}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e^{\prime}
.
By applying
\pi(\cdot)
it follows that
(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{\pi(K)}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq_{\pi(K)}(1+\epsilon)e
, implying that
\textsc{sp}_{K}
is a sparsification function for
\pi(K)
, as required.

If
e^{\prime}
is in the relative boundary of
K
then
x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m}^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime}
all lie in a proper face of
K
, and hence in a proper exposed face of
K
(due to the existence of a supporting hyperplane to
K
at
e^{\prime}
). Therefore
there is a linear functional
\xi\in K^{*}
such that
\xi[x_{i}^{\prime}]=0
for all
i
and
\xi[e^{\prime}]=0
.
Since
\textup{relint}(\pi(K))=\pi(\textup{relint}(K))

[
36
, Theorem 6.6]
, there exists
\tilde{e}\in\textup{relint}(K)
such
that
\pi(\tilde{e})=\pi(e^{\prime})=e
. Let
\delta>0
, let
x_{m+1}^{\prime}=\delta\tilde{e}
, and consider the collection of points
x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m}^{\prime},x_{m+1}^{\prime}\in K
.
Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}x_{i}^{\prime}=e^{\prime}+\delta\tilde{e}=:e^{\prime\prime}\in\textup{relint}(K)
since
x_{m+1}^{\prime}\in\textup{relint}(K)
and
x_{i}^{\prime}\in K
for all
i\in[m]
.
By the definition of
\textsc{sp}_{K}
there exists
S\subseteq[m]
with
|S|\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)
,
positive scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
, and a non-negative scalar
\lambda_{m+1}
, such that

\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)e^{\prime\prime}\preceq_{K}\lambda_{m+1}x_{m+1}^{\prime}+\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}^{\prime}\preceq_{K}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)e^{\prime\prime}.

(6)

To complete the argument, we need to control the value of
\lambda_{m+1}
. Recall that there is a
linear functional
\xi\in K^{*}
such that
\xi[x_{i}^{\prime}]=0
for all
i
and
\xi[e^{\prime}]=0
. Applying this to both sides of (
6
) gives

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)\delta\xi[\tilde{e}]=\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)\xi[e^{\prime\prime}]

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{m+1}\xi[x_{m+1}^{\prime}]=\delta\lambda_{m+1}\xi[\tilde{e}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)\xi[e^{\prime\prime}]=\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)\delta\xi[\tilde{e}].

Since
\tilde{e}\in\textup{relint}(K)
it follows that
\xi[\tilde{e}]>0
. Therefore,
\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)\leq\lambda_{m+1}\leq\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)
.

Now we will consider the image of (
6
) under
\pi
. Since
\pi(e^{\prime\prime})=\pi(e^{\prime}+\delta\tilde{e})=(1+\delta)e
, we have

\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)(1+\delta)e\preceq_{\pi(K)}\lambda_{m+1}\delta e+\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq_{\pi(K)}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)(1+\delta)e.

Using the fact that
\delta\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)e\preceq_{\pi(K)}\delta\lambda_{m+1}e\preceq_{\pi(K)}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{1+2\delta}\right)\delta e
gives

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{\pi(K)}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq_{\pi(K)}(1+\epsilon)e.

It follows that
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon/(1+2\delta))
is a sparsification function for
\pi(K)
. Since

\delta>0
was arbitrary, it follows that
\alpha(\epsilon):=\inf_{\delta>0}\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon/(1+2\delta))

is a sparsification function for
\pi(K)
. Since sparsification
functions are (by definition) upper semi-continuous and non-increasing, it follows that

\alpha(\epsilon)=\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
, and so
\textsc{sp}_{K}
is a sparsification function for
\pi(K)
, as required.

In the case where
\pi
is a linear isomorphism, we have that
C
is the image of
K
under a linear map and
K
is also the image of

C
under a linear map. Therefore
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{C}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
∎

Since sparsification functions do not increase under (proper) intersections with a subspace and under projections, it follows that the sparsification functions do not increase under proper lifts (see Definition
1.7
), allowing us to establish Proposition
1.8
.

Proof of Proposition
1.8
.

This follows directly from Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
.
∎

We defined the sparsification function for any closed convex cone. The following result shows that
we can, without loss of generality, consider proper cones.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
K
be a closed convex cone and let
L=K\cap(-K)
be the lineality space of
K
.
Then the sparsification functions of
K
and the closed pointed cone

K^{\prime}=K\cap L^{\perp}
are equal.

Proof.

If
L=\{0\}
then
K=K^{\prime}
so there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, we have the
direct sum decomposition
K=K^{\prime}+L

[
36
, Page 65]
.
Note that if
z\in K^{\prime}
and
v\in L
then
(z,v)\in K
if and only
if
z\in K^{\prime}
and
v\in L
. If, in addition
z^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}\in L

then
(z,v)\preceq_{K}(z^{\prime},v^{\prime})
if and only if
(z-z^{\prime},v-v^{\prime})\in K^{\prime}+L
which
holds if and only if
z\preceq_{K^{\prime}}z^{\prime}
.

Since
K^{\prime}
is the image of
K
under a linear map,

\textsc{sp}_{K^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
For the reverse inequality, let
(z_{1},v_{1}),\ldots,(z_{m},v_{m})\in K
be such that

\sum_{i\in[m]}(z_{i},v_{i})=(z_{0},v_{0})\in\textup{relint}(K)
.
Then, by the definition of
\textsc{sp}_{K^{\prime}}(\epsilon)
there exists
S\subseteq[m]
with
|S|\leq\textsc{sp}_{K^{\prime}}(\epsilon)
and positive scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
such that

(1-\epsilon)z_{0}\preceq_{K^{\prime}}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}z_{i}\preceq_{K^{\prime}}(1+\epsilon)z_{0}.

But this holds if and only if

(1-\epsilon)(z_{0},v_{0})\preceq_{K}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}(z_{i},v_{i})\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)(z_{0},v_{0}),

showing that
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K^{\prime}}(\epsilon)
.
∎

3.2
Faces

This section considers how sparsification functions interact with the facial structure of a convex cone. It is reasonable to hope that sparsification functions might be monotone along faces, in the sense that if
F\subseteq K
is a face of
K
then
\textsc{sp}_{F}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. However we are only able to establish this result under additional
technical assumptions on the face
F
. The technical assumptions we involve are related to certain strong notions of facial exposedness. The first of these is the notion of a face being projectionally exposed. This was first introduced by Borwein and Wolkowicz
[
5
]
in their study of facial reduction methods for abstract convex optimization problems.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
K
be a closed convex cone. A face
F
of
K
is
projectionally exposed
if there is an idempotent linear map
\pi:\textup{span}(K)\rightarrow\textup{span}(K)
such that

\pi(K)=F
.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
K
is a closed convex cone and
F
is a projectionally exposed face of
K
then
\textsc{sp}_{F}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

If
F
is a projectionally exposed face of
K
then there exists an idempotent
linear map
\pi
such that
\pi(K)=F
. The result then follows from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Another, more recent, strengthening of facial exposedness is the notion of an amenable face, introduced by Lourenço
[
28
]
in the context of developing error bounds for conic optimization problems in the absence of constraint qualification.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
K
be a closed convex cone. A face
F
of
K
is
amenable
if
there is a positive constant
\kappa
such that

d(x,F)\leq\kappa\,d(x,K)\;\;\textup{for all $x\in\textup{span}(F)$}.

The following result is strictly stronger that Lemma
3.6
, since it is known that
there are cones with faces that are amenable but not projectionally exposed
[
27
]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
K
is a closed convex cone and
F
is an amenable face of
K
then

\textsc{sp}_{F}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
e\in\textup{relint}(K)
, let
S=\textup{span}(F\cup\{e\})
and consider
\hat{K}=K\cap S
.
If
F=K
then the result trivially holds. As such, from now on we assume that
F
is a proper subset of
K
. Therefore
F
is disjoint from the relative interior
of
K
, so
e\notin F
. It follows that
\dim(F)=\dim(S)-1=\dim(\hat{K})-1
(where the last equality holds because
S
meets the relative interior of
K
). Moreover,
F\cap S=F
is a face of
\hat{K}=K\cap S
.

By assumption,
F
is an amenable face of
K
. As such, there exists
\kappa>0
such that

d(x,F)\leq\kappa\,d(x,K)\;\;\textup{for all $x\in\textup{span}(F)$}.

Since
\hat{K}\subseteq K
it follows that
d(x,K)\leq d(x,\hat{K})
for all
x
.
Therefore,

d(x,F)\leq\kappa d(x,K)\leq\kappa d(x,\hat{K})\;\;\textup{for all $x\in\textup{span}(F)$}.

Hence
F
is a codimension one amenable face of
\hat{K}
. From
[
25
, Theorem 6.2]
, it follows that
F
is a projectionally exposed face of
\hat{K}
. From Lemma
3.6
we see that
\textsc{sp}_{F}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{\hat{K}}(\epsilon)
. Finally,
since
S
meets the relative interior of
K
, it follows from Lemma
3.2

that
\textsc{sp}_{\hat{K}}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Overall, we have that
\textsc{sp}_{F}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
, as required.
∎

4
Sparsification and self-concordant barriers

This section develops a very simple method for sparsficiation with respect to
a general proper convex cone
K
. The basic idea is to find a point that is close to

e=\sum_{i\in[m]}x_{i}
in the appropriate sense by running the Frank-Wolfe algorithm
on an optimization problem with a quadratic objective and a constraint set with extreme
points that are scaled versions of the input points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in K
. Since each
iteration of the Frank-Wolfe algorithm steps in the direction of an extreme point, the number
of iterations bound the number of terms in the decomposition of
the current iterate. We use, as the objective, the squared Hessian norm of a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant
barrier for
K
. Such an objective is related (by part (iv) of Lemma
2.3
)
to the spectral norm
|\cdot|_{e}
with respect to
K
, the quantity
we truly would like to control.
By taking the extreme points of the constraint set to be scaled in a way that is
also informed by the self-concordant barrier, we obtain a bound on the curvature constant
of the objective that depends only on
\nu
. Combining these ingredients
gives a bound (Theorem
4.2
) on the sparsification function that depends
only on
\nu
and
\epsilon
.

Theorem
4.2
is not strong enough to
give a non-trivial sparsification result for the positive semidefinite cone. However, it does
give interesting examples of convex cones for which dimension-independent sparsification is possible. We discuss this further in Section
7.2
.

4.1
The Frank-Wolfe algorithm

The approach we will use to construct a sparsifier with respect to a general convex cone will be based on an algorithm for minimizing
smooth convex functions over compact convex sets, known as the
Frank-Wolfe
(or
conditional gradient
) algorithm
[
10
]
.
Let
f
be a differentiable convex function, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a compact convex subset of the domain of
f
.
Consider the optimization problem

f^{\star}:=\min_{z\in\mathcal{X}}f(z).

The Frank-Wolfe algorithm, given in its most basic form in Algorithm
1
, is a method to solve this problem.

Algorithm 1

Frank-Wolfe algorithm

Input:
Differentiable convex function
f
, compact convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\textup{dom}(f)

Initialize:
Initial point
z_{0}\in\mathcal{X}

for

t=0,1,\ldots

do

\alpha_{t}=\frac{2}{t+2}

v_{t}\in\arg\min_{v\in\mathcal{X}}\langle\nabla f(z_{t}),v\rangle

z_{t+1}=(1-\alpha_{t})z_{t}+\alpha_{t}v_{t}

end

for

Each step of the algorithm involves minimizing a linear functional over
\mathcal{X}
to obtain
a direction
v_{t}
. The next iterate is then a convex combination of the current iterate and
the direction
v_{t}
. Since linear optimization over a compact convex set always has an optimal
point that is an extreme point of the set, we can always choose
v_{t}
to be an extreme point
of
\mathcal{X}
. If we do this, the Frank-Wolfe algorithm (as stated in Algorithm
1
)
has the property that the
t
th iterate,
z_{t}
, is a convex combination of at most
t

extreme points of
\mathcal{X}
.

The main parameter of the objective function that appears in typical convergence results for the Frank-Wolfe algorithm (see Theorem
4.1
, to follow)
is the
curvature constant
of
f
with respect to
\mathcal{X}
, defined as

C_{f}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,s\in\mathcal{X},\\
\gamma\in[0,1]\end{subarray}}\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}B_{f}((1-\gamma)z+\gamma s||z)

where

B_{f}(x||y):=f(x)-f(y)-\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle

is the
Bregman divergence
associated with
f
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
15
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
f
be a differentiable convex function with curvature constant
C_{f}
and let
\mathcal{X}
be a compact convex set contained in the domain of
f
.
Then the iterates
z_{t}
of Algorithm
1
applied to
f
and
\mathcal{X}
satisfy

f(z_{t})-f^{\star}\leq\frac{2C_{f}}{t+2}\quad\textup{for all $t\geq 1$}.

4.2
A bound on the sparsification function

In this section we establish a general bound on the sparsification function of a convex cone in terms of the barrier parameter of a logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for the cone.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed, pointed, full-dimensional convex cone. Let
\varphi
be a logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for
K
with parameter
\nu
. Then
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil(4\nu/\epsilon)^{2}\rceil
.

Proof.

First, we will find scalars
\mu_{i}>0
, and define rescaled points

\tilde{x}_{i}:=\mu_{i}x_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
, such that
e
is a convex combination of the
\tilde{x}_{i}
.
Given such scalars, if there exists a set
S\subset[m]
and positive scalars
\tilde{\lambda_{i}}
for
i\in S
such that

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}\sum_{i\in S}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\tilde{x}_{i}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e
, then
Theorem
4.2
follows by taking
\lambda_{i}=\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\mu_{i}
for
i\in S
.
As such, it is enough to
work with the
\tilde{x}_{i}
.

To find the scalars
\mu_{i}
, we note that since
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=e
, by property (i) of Lemma
2.3
we have that

-\sum_{i=1}^{m}D\varphi(e)[x_{i}]=-D\varphi(e)[e]=\nu.

Let
w_{i}=-D\varphi(e)[x_{i}]/\nu
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Since
x_{i}\in K
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
, it follows from property (ii) of Lemma
2.3
that
w_{i}\geq\|x_{i}\|_{e}/\nu>0
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
(since
K
is pointed so
\|\cdot\|_{e}
is non-degenerate).
Furthermore,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}=1
. As such, let
\tilde{x}_{i}=w_{i}^{-1}x_{i}
. Then
\tilde{x_{i}}\in K
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\tilde{x}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=e
,
so
e
is a convex combination of the
\tilde{x}_{i}
. Therefore we can take
\mu_{i}=w_{i}^{-1}
for
i\in[m]
.

Let
\mathcal{X}:=\textup{conv}\{\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{m}\}
and let
f(z)=\frac{1}{2}\|z-e\|_{e}^{2}
. Consider the optimization problem

\min_{z\in\mathcal{X}}f(z).

Since
e\in\mathcal{X}
, it follows that the optimal value
f^{\star}
of this problem is zero.
Moreover, since

-D\varphi(e)[\tilde{x}_{i}]=\nu\quad\textup{for all $i$,}

and
-D\varphi(x)[\cdot]
is a linear functional,
it follows that
-D\varphi(e)[z]=\nu
for all
z\in\mathcal{X}
.

We next compute the curvature constant of
f
with respect to
\mathcal{X}
.
A straightforward computation shows that

B_{f}(x||y)=f(x)-f(y)-\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|_{e}^{2}.

Therefore, the curvature constant can be bounded as

\displaystyle C_{f}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,s\in\mathcal{X},\\
\gamma\in[0,1]\end{subarray}}\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}B_{f}((1-\gamma)z+\gamma s||z)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,s\in\mathcal{X},\\
\gamma\in[0,1]\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\|\gamma(s-z)\|_{e}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,s\in\mathcal{X},\\
\gamma\in[0,1]\end{subarray}}\|s-z\|_{e}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\sup_{z\in\mathcal{X}}\|z\|_{e}\right)^{2}\quad\textup{(by the triangle inequality)}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sup_{z\in\mathcal{X}}(-D\varphi(e)[z])^{2}

\displaystyle=4\nu^{2}

where the last equality uses the fact that
-D\varphi(e)[z]=\nu
for all
z\in\mathcal{X}
.

After
T=\lceil\frac{16\nu^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\rceil
steps of the Frank-Wolfe algorithm, we obtain a point
z_{T}
that is a convex combination of at most
T
elements of
\{\tilde{x}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{m}\}
.
Moreover,

f(z_{T})-f^{\star}=f(z_{T})\leq\frac{2C_{f}}{T+2}\leq\frac{8\nu^{2}}{T}\leq\epsilon^{2}/2.

Therefore,
\|z_{T}-e\|_{e}\leq\epsilon
. From Lemma
2.3
part (iv) we have that
|z_{T}-e|_{e}\leq\|z_{T}-e\|_{e}\leq\epsilon
and so

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}z_{T}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e,

as required.
∎

5
Generalizing Batson-Spielman-Srivastava sparsification

This section shows that if a closed, pointed, full-dimensional convex cone admits a
\nu
-self-concordant barrier with certain additional properties (see Definition
5.2
), then the sparsification function is bounded by an expression that is
linear
in the barrier parameter
\nu
.
This is an exact generalization of the linear-sized spectral sparsification result of Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava (Theorem
1.1
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
K
be a closed, pointed, full-dimensional, convex cone.
If
K
has a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous pairwise-self-concordant barrier
then
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil 4\nu/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
.

The additional ‘pairwise-self-concordance’ property constrains the change of the barrier function
along
pairs
of directions in the cone
K
, whereas the usual notion of self-concordance
is a constraint that relates the second and third derivatives along arbitrary lines.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
K
be a closed, pointed, full-dimensional convex cone.
A logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier
\varphi
for
K

is
pairwise-self-concordant
if,
for all
u,v\in K
and all
x\in\textup{int}(K)
,

0\leq-D^{3}\varphi(x)[v,u,u]\leq 2D^{2}\varphi(x)[v,u]|u|_{x}.

(7)

It seems that this additional property has not been considered before, so we discuss it in more
detail in Section
6
. In particular, we give a sufficient condition that implies pairwise-self-concordance (see Lemma
6.1
).
The
d
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant
barrier
-\log\det(\cdot)
for the positive semidefinite cone satisfies this sufficient condition, and so is pairwise-self-concordant (see Lemma
6.2
in Section
6
).
Therefore Theorem
5.1
exactly generalises Theorem
1.1
.
Furthermore, hyperbolic barriers also satisfy the sufficient condition for being pairwise-self-concordant. This tells us that Theorem
5.1
implies Theorem
1.6
,
our main sparsification result for hyperbolicity cones.

The aim of the rest of this section is to establish Theorem
5.1
, by appropriately generalizing the deterministic sparsification argument of Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava
[
2
]
.

5.1
Barrier functions and the generalized BSS algorithm

The sparsification method of Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava applies to the positive semidefinite cone and the problem of sparsifying a collection
(X_{i})_{i\in[m]}
of positive semidefinite matrices that sum to the identity, i.e.,
\sum_{i\in[m]}X_{i}=I
.

If
\ell\,I\prec X\prec u\,I
then they define upper and lower barrier functions

\Phi^{u}(X)=\textup{tr}\left[(uI-X)^{-1}\right]\;\;\textup{and}\;\;\Phi_{\ell}(X)=\textup{tr}\left[(X-\ell I)^{-1}\right].

Their algorithm uses these barrier functions as a guide to control
the eigenvalues of the current iterate and to take steps in the direction of
X_{i}
for some
i\in[m]
, in such a way that the eigenvalues of the new matrix become more uniform.

In the general setting, we note that
\textup{tr}(X^{-1})=-D\log\det(X)[I]
. Therefore,
a natural generalization of the upper and lower barrier functions is
to take

\displaystyle\Phi^{u,e}(x)

\displaystyle=-D\varphi(ue-x)[e]\quad\textup{and}\quad

(8)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell,e}(x)

\displaystyle=-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e].

(9)

This choice leads to what
we call the generalized BSS algorithm (Algorithm
2
), which is the exact analogue of the original BSS algorithm
[
2
, Algorithm]
in the abstract conic setting.

Algorithm 2

Generalized BSS algorithm for a proper cone
K
that admits a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous pairwise-self-concordant barrier
\varphi
with associated upper and lower barrier functions defined in (
8
) and (
9
).

Input:
points
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\in K
such that
\sum_{i\in[m]}x_{i}=:e\in\textup{int}(K)
;
0<\epsilon<1

Output:

\lambda\geq 0
with at most
T
non-zero entries satisfying

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}\sum_{i\in[m]}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e
.

Initialize:

z_{0}=0\in\textup{span}(K)
;
y_{0}=0\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
;
T=\lceil 4\nu/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
;

\delta_{u}=1+\epsilon/2
;
\delta_{\ell}=1-\epsilon/2
,
u_{0}=2\nu/\epsilon
,
\ell_{0}=-2\nu/\epsilon
.

for

t=1,2,\ldots,T

do

Set
u_{t}=u_{t-1}+\delta_{u}
and
\ell_{t}=\ell_{t-1}+\delta_{\ell}

if
there exists
j\in[m]
and
\alpha>0
such that the following three properties hold

(i)
\ell_{t}\,e\prec_{K}z_{t-1}+\alpha x_{j}\prec_{K}u_{t}e

(ii)
\Phi^{u_{t},e}(z_{t-1}+\alpha x_{j})\leq\Phi^{u_{t-1},e}(z_{t-1})

(iii)
\Phi_{\ell_{t},e}(z_{t-1}+\alpha x_{j})\leq\Phi_{\ell_{t-1},e}(z_{t-1})

then

Set
z_{t}=z_{t-1}+\alpha x_{j}
and
[y_{t}]_{i}=\begin{cases}[y_{t-1}]_{i}&\textup{if $i\neq j$}\\
[y_{t-1}]_{i}+\alpha&\textup{if $i=j$}\end{cases}

else

return
fail

end

if

end

for

return

\lambda=\frac{1}{T}y_{T}

The next result tells us that as long as it is possible to simultaneously satisfy the conditions required to take a step at each iteration, then the sparsification algorithm works.

Lemma 5.3
.

If the generalized BSS algorithm (Algorithm
2
) for a proper
cone
K
terminates correctly (i.e., does not return ‘fail’) for all inputs,
then
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil 4\nu/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that, for an arbitrary input, if the
generalized BSS algorithm does not return ‘fail’ then
it returns
\lambda\geq 0
with at
most
T
non-zero entries that satisfies

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}\sum_{i\in[m]}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e
.

Since at most one entry of
y
becomes non-zero at each iteration, it follows
that
y_{T}
(and hence
\lambda
) has at most
T
non-zero entries.
It is straightforward to check (by induction on
t
) that the iterates of the algorithm have the property

\sum_{i\in[m]}[y_{t}]_{i}x_{i}=z_{t},\quad\textup{for all $t\in[T]$}.

Therefore
\sum_{i\in[m]}\lambda_{i}x_{i}=\frac{1}{T}z_{T}
.
By construction
\frac{1}{T}z_{T}
satisfies
(\ell_{T}/T)e\prec_{K}\frac{1}{T}z_{T}\prec_{K}(u_{T}/T)e
. Furthermore,

\frac{\ell_{T}}{T}=\frac{\ell_{0}+T\delta_{\ell}}{T}=-\frac{2\nu/\epsilon}{\lceil 4\nu/\epsilon^{2}\rceil}+1-\epsilon/2\geq 1-\epsilon,

where the inequality follows from
\lceil x\rceil\geq x
.
Similarly

\frac{u_{T}}{T}=\frac{u_{0}+T\delta_{u}}{T}=\frac{2\nu/\epsilon}{\lceil 4\nu/\epsilon^{2}\rceil}+1+\epsilon/2\leq 1+\epsilon.

It follows that

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}(\ell_{T}/T)e\prec_{K}\sum_{i\in[m]}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\prec_{K}(u_{T}/T)e\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e.

∎

To establish Theorem
5.1
, it remains to establish that the generalized BSS algorithm always terminates correctly.

The following result gives a sufficient condition relating the value of the barriers (
\epsilon_{u}
and
\epsilon_{\ell}
) and the size of the barrier shifts (
\delta_{u}
and
\delta_{\ell}
), such that it is possible to take a step,
\alpha x_{j}
and ensure that the shifted barriers do not increase in value.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in K\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=e\in\textup{int}(K)
. Let
\varphi
be a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous pairwise-self-concordant barrier for
K
with
associated upper and lower barrier functions defined in (
8
) and (
9
).
Let
u,\ell,\delta_{\ell},\delta_{u},\epsilon_{u},\epsilon_{\ell}>0
. Let
\ell\,e\prec_{K}x\prec_{K}u\,e

and let
\Phi^{u,e}(x)\leq\epsilon_{u}
and
\Phi_{\ell,e}(x)\leq\epsilon_{\ell}
.
If
0<\delta_{u}^{-1}+\epsilon_{u}\leq\delta_{\ell}^{-1}-\epsilon_{\ell}
then
there exists
j\in[m]
and
\alpha>0
such that

(i)
(\ell+\delta_{\ell})e\prec_{K}x+\alpha x_{j}\prec_{K}(u+\delta_{u})e

(ii)
\Phi^{u+\delta_{u},e}(x+\alpha x_{j})\leq\Phi^{u,e}(x)

(iii)
\Phi_{\ell+\delta_{\ell},e}(x+\alpha x_{j})\leq\Phi_{\ell,e}(x)

The proof of this result, given in Section

With this result established, it is relatively straightforward to show that the generalized BSS

Proposition 5.5
.

If
K
is a proper convex cone and
\varphi
is a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous pairwise-self-concordant barrier for
K

Proof.

We will to show that, at every iteration
t

\alpha>0
and
j\in[m]
such that
(i)–(iii) are satisfied.
We argue by strong induction on
t
.

For the base case, when
t=1
, we know that

\Phi^{u_{0},e}(z_{0})=-D\varphi(u_{0}e)[e]=u_{0}^{-1}\nu=\epsilon/2,

where the second equality holds because
\varphi
is logarithmically homogeneous, so
D\varphi
is positively homogeneous of degree
-1
. Similarly

\Phi_{\ell_{0},e}(z_{0})=-D\varphi(-\ell_{0}e)[e]=(-\ell_{0})^{-1}\nu=\epsilon/2.

Furthermore,
\ell_{0}e=-(2\nu/\epsilon)e\prec_{K}z_{0}\prec_{K}(2\nu/\epsilon)e=u_{0}e
.

We can apply Proposition

with

,
u=u_{0}=2\nu/\epsilon
,
\ell=\ell_{0}=-2\nu/\epsilon
,
\epsilon_{u}=\epsilon_{\ell}=\epsilon/2
, and
\delta_{u}=1+\epsilon/2
and
\delta_{\ell}=1-\epsilon/2
. Note that

0<\delta_{u}^{-1}+\epsilon_{u}=\frac{1+(\epsilon/2)+(\epsilon/2)^{2}}{1+(\epsilon/2)}\leq\frac{1-(\epsilon/2)+(\epsilon/2)^{2}}{1-(\epsilon/2)}=\delta_{\ell}^{-1}-\epsilon_{\ell}

(10)

where the inequality holds because
(1-t)(1+t+t^{2})=1-t^{3}\leq 1+t^{3}=(1+t)(1-t+t^{2})
for all

.

tells us that there exists
\alpha>0
and
j\in[m]
such that
\ell_{1}e\prec_{K}z_{0}+\alpha x_{j}\prec_{K}u_{1}e
and

\Phi^{u_{1},e}(z_{0}+\alpha x_{j})\leq\Phi^{u_{0},e}(z_{0})
and

\Phi_{\ell_{1},e}(z_{0}+\alpha x_{j})\leq\Phi_{\ell_{0},e}(z_{0})
.
So conditions (i)—(iii) are satisfied.

Suppose that at every iteration

we can find
\alpha>0
and
j\in[m]

such that (i)—(iii) are satisfied. Then
\ell_{t-1}e\prec_{K}z_{t-1}\prec_{K}u_{t-1}e

and
\Phi^{u_{t-1},e}(z_{t-1})\leq\Phi^{u_{0},e}(z_{0})=\epsilon/2
and

\Phi_{\ell_{t-1},e}(z_{t-1})\leq\Phi_{\ell_{0},e}(z_{0})=\epsilon/2
.

,

,

,
\epsilon_{u}=\epsilon_{\ell}=\epsilon/2

\delta_{u}=1+\epsilon/2
and

follows directly from Lemma

. Therefore, it remains to prove Proposition

.

5.2
Properties of pairwise-self-concordant barriers

In this section we summarize the key properties of the Hessian of pairwise-self-concordant
barriers that we will use in what follows.
The first tells us about properties of the Hessian inner product of two points in the cone
under the assumption that the barrier is pairwise-self-concordant.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\varphi:\textup{int}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a pairwise-self-concordant barrier for a closed, convex, pointed, full-dimensional cone
K
.
If
x\in\textup{int}(K)
and
u,v\in K
then
D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v]\geq 0
. If, in addition,
u\in\textup{int}(K)
and
v\in K\setminus\{0\}
then
D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v]>0
.

Proof.

First consider the case when
|u|_{x}=0
. Then
u\in K\cap(-K)
and so, since
K
is pointed,
u=0
. In this case
D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v]=0
. Otherwise consider the case that
|u|_{x}>0
. In this case, the definition of a pairwise-self-concordant barrier tells us that

D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v]\geq 0
.

If
u\in\textup{int}(K)
then there exists some
\eta>0
such that
u-\eta v\in K
. Therefore
D^{2}\varphi(x)[u-\eta v,v]\geq 0
. Since
D^{2}\varphi(x)[\cdot,v]
is linear, it follows that

D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v]\geq\eta D^{2}\varphi(x)[v,v]=\eta\|v\|_{x}^{2}>0,

where the final inequality holds because
v\neq 0
and
K
is pointed.
∎

The other technical result we will need controls how the Hessian inner products between
elements of the cone change from point to point. The argument is similar to typical
ways to control how the Hessian norm changes for (ordinary) self-concordant functions.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\varphi:\textup{int}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a pairwise-self-concordant barrier for a closed, convex, pointed, full-dimensional cone
K
.
Let
x\in\textup{int}(K)
and
u,v\in K
be arbitrary. Then

\displaystyle D^{2}\varphi(x+tv)[u,v]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(1+t|v|_{x})^{2}}D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v]\;\;\textup{for all $t\geq 0$}

(11)

\displaystyle D^{2}\varphi(x-tv)[u,v]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-t|v|_{x})^{2}}D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v]\;\;\textup{for all $0\leq t<1/|v|_{x}$}.

(12)

Proof.

Consider the univariate functions
g(t)=D^{2}\varphi(x-tv)[u,v]
defined for
t\in(-\infty,1/|v|_{x})

and
h(t)=D^{2}\varphi(x+tv)[u,v]
defined for
t\in(-1/|v|_{x},\infty)
.
We have that

g^{\prime}(t)=-D^{3}\varphi(x-tv)[u,v,v]\quad\textup{and}\quad h^{\prime}(t)=D^{3}\varphi(x+tv)[u,v,v].

By the pairwise-self-concordance assumption

\displaystyle 0\leq g^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2D^{2}\varphi(x-tv)[u,v]|v|_{x-tv}=2g(t)|v|_{x-tv}\quad\textup{and}

\displaystyle 0\geq h^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq-2D^{2}\varphi(x+tv)[u,v]|v|_{x+tv}=-2h(t)|v|_{x+tv}.

Since
v\in K
,

|v|_{x-tv}=\inf\{\tau\;:\;v\preceq_{K}\tau(x-tv)\}\quad\textup{and}\quad|v|_{x+tv}=\inf\{\tau\;:\;v\preceq_{K}\tau(x+tv)\}.

Now
v\preceq_{K}|v|_{x}x
implies that
(1-|v|_{x}t)v\preceq_{K}|v|_{x}(x-tv)
, which implies that (for
t<1/|v|_{x}
)

v\preceq_{K}\frac{|v|_{x}}{1-t|v|_{x}}(x-tv).

Clearly, then,
|v|_{x-tv}\leq\frac{|v|_{x}}{1-t|v|_{x}}
. A similar argument shows that
|v|_{x+tv}\leq\frac{|v|_{x}}{1+t|v|_{x}}
.
From Lemma
5.6
,
g(t)\geq 0
and
h(t)\geq 0
. Therefore

g^{\prime}(t)\leq 2g(t)\frac{|v|_{x}}{1-t|v|_{x}}\quad\textup{and}\quad h^{\prime}(t)\geq-2h(t)\frac{|v|_{x}}{1+t|v|_{x}}.

(13)

Then for
0\leq t<1/|v|_{x}
, using (
13
) gives

\frac{d}{dt}\left[(1-t|v|_{x})^{2}g(t)\right]=-2|v|_{x}(1-t|v|_{x})g(t)+(1-t|v|_{x})^{2}g^{\prime}(t)\leq-2|v|_{x}(1-t|v|_{x})g(t)+2|v|_{x}(1-t|v|_{x})g(t)=0.

Therefore,
(1-t|v|_{x})^{2}g(t)
is monotonically non-increasing so

(1-t|v|_{x})^{2}D^{2}\varphi(x-tv)[u,v]=(1-t|v|_{x})^{2}g(t)\leq g(0)=D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v].

Similarly, for
t\geq 0
,

\frac{d}{dt}\left[(1+t|v|_{x})^{2}h(t)\right]=2|v|_{x}(1+t|v|_{x})h(t)+(1+t|v|_{x})^{2}h^{\prime}(t)\geq 2|v|_{x}(1+t|v|_{x})h(t)-2|v|_{x}(1+t)|x|_{v})h(t)=0.

Therefore,
(1+t|v|_{x})^{2}h(t)
is monotonically non-decreasing so

(1+t|v|_{x})^{2}D^{2}\varphi(x+tv)[u,v]=(1+t|v|_{x})^{2}h(t)\geq h(0)=D^{2}\varphi(x)[u,v].

∎

5.3
Barrier progress results

The aim of this section is to establish Proposition
5.4
(stated in Section
5.1
) which shows that it is always possible to make progress in the generalized BSS algorithm.
This result follows from key intermediate results that control how the upper and lower barriers change (Propositions
5.10
and
5.11
) as well as how to take steps
that ensure both barriers remain under control
5.12
. The section concludes with a proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Propositions
5.10
,
5.11
, and
5.4
play the
role of the original Lemmas 3.3, 3.4, and 3.5 from
[
2
]
. They are, however, not quite
direct generalizations, since they are reformulated to streamline certain aspects of the proof (in particular, to completely avoid Claim 3.6 of
[
2
]
).

Before stating and proving the barrier progress lemmas (Propositions
5.10
,
5.11
, and
5.12
), we establish some preliminary results
that control how the upper and lower barriers change with changes in their arguments.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\varphi
be a pairwise-self-concordant barrier for a proper cone
K
.
Let
e\in\textup{int}(K)
, let
u>0
, let
x
be such that
ue-x\succ_{K}0
, and let
u^{\prime}>u
.
Then

\Phi^{u^{\prime},e}(x)-\Phi^{u,e}(x)=-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e]-(-D\varphi(ue-x)[e])\leq-(u^{\prime}-u)D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,e].

Proof.

Consider the univariate function
g:[u,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

g(t)=-D\varphi(te-x)[e]
. Note that
g^{\prime}(t)=-D^{2}\varphi(te-x)[e,e])
and
g^{\prime\prime}(t)=-D^{3}\varphi(te-x)[e,e,e]
. Since
\varphi
is pairwise-self-concordant,
g
is a convex function.
By convexity of
g
,

0\leq g(u)-[g(u^{\prime})+g^{\prime}(u^{\prime})(u-u^{\prime})]=-D\varphi(ue-x)[e]+D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e]-D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,e](u^{\prime}-u).

Rearranging gives the desired inequality.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\varphi
be a pairwise-self-concordant barrier for a proper cone
K
.
Let
e\in\textup{int}(K)
, let
\ell>0
, let
x
be such that
x-\ell^{\prime}e\succ_{K}0
and let
\ell^{\prime}>\ell
. Then

\Phi_{\ell^{\prime},e}(x)-\Phi_{\ell,e}(x)=-D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]-(-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e])\leq(\ell^{\prime}-\ell)D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,e].

Proof.

Consider the univariate function
g:(-\infty,\ell^{\prime}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

g(t)=-D\varphi(x-te)[e]
. Note that
g^{\prime}(t)=D^{2}\varphi(x-te)[e,e]
and
g^{\prime\prime}(t)=-D^{3}\varphi(x-te)[e,e,e]
. Since
\varphi
is pairwise-self-concordant,
g
is a convex function.
By convexity of
g
,

0\leq g(\ell)-[g(\ell^{\prime})+g^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime})(\ell-\ell^{\prime})]=-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]+D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]+D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,e](\ell^{\prime}-\ell).

Rearranging gives the desired inequality.
∎

Proposition 5.10

(Upper barrier shift)
.

Let
\varphi
be a pairwise-self-concordant barrier for a proper convex cone
K
.
Let
e\in\textup{int}(K)
, let
u^{\prime}=u+\delta_{u}>u>0
, let
x
be such that

ue-x\succ_{K}0
, and let

\Delta\in K\setminus\{0\}
. If

1\geq\frac{D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[\Delta,e]}{\delta_{u}D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,e]}-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[\Delta]=:U[\Delta]

(14)

then

u^{\prime}e-(x+\Delta)\succ_{K}0
and

-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-(x+\Delta))[e]\leq-D\varphi(ue-x)[e]
.

Proof.

For the first conclusion, we note that
ue-x\succ_{K}0
implies that
u^{\prime}e-x\succ_{K}0
since
e\in\textup{int}(K)
. Since
K
is pointed,
D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,e]>0
. Since
e\in\textup{int}(K)
, by Lemma
5.6
we have that
D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[\Delta,e]>0
.
Therefore (
14
) implies that
-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[\Delta]<1
.
Then, from item (iv) of Lemma
2.3
, we know that
|\Delta|_{u^{\prime}e-x}<1
, or equivalently, that

-(u^{\prime}e-x)\prec_{K}\Delta\prec_{K}u^{\prime}e-x
. Rearranging gives
u^{\prime}e-(x+\Delta)\succ_{K}0
.

For the second conclusion, we write

-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-(x+\Delta))[e]+D\varphi(ue-x)[e]=\left[-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e]+D\varphi(ue-x)[e]\right]\\
+\left[-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-(x+\Delta))[e]+D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e]\right]

(15)

and upper bound the two terms in (
15
) separately.
Lemma
5.8
tells us that

-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e]-(-D\varphi(ue-x)[e])\leq-\delta_{u}D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,e].

For the second term in (
15
) we use Taylor’s theorem with integral remainder together with the Hessian control bound (Lemma
5.7
) for
\varphi
. This gives

\displaystyle-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-(x+\Delta))[e]+D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x-t\Delta)[e,\Delta]\;dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-t|\Delta|_{u^{\prime}e-x})^{2}}D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,\Delta]\;dt

\displaystyle=\frac{D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,\Delta]}{1-|\Delta|_{u^{\prime}e-x}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,\Delta]}{1-(-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[\Delta])}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{u}D^{2}\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[e,e],

where the second last inequality holds because
|\Delta|_{u^{\prime}e-x}\leq-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-x)[\Delta]
and the last inequality follows from the assumption that
U[\Delta]\leq 1
.
Substituting into (
15
) we see that
-D\varphi(u^{\prime}e-(x+\Delta))[e]+D\varphi(ue-x)[e]\leq 0
, as required.
∎

Proposition 5.11

(Lower barrier shift)
.

Let
\varphi
be a pairwise-self-concordant barrier for a proper cone
K
.
Let
e\in\textup{int}(K)
, let
\ell^{\prime}=\ell+\delta_{\ell}>\ell>0
, let
x
be such that

x-\ell e\succ_{K}0
, and let
\Delta\in K\setminus\{0\}
. If
\Phi_{\ell,e}(x)=-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]<\delta_{\ell}^{-1}
and

1\leq\frac{D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[\Delta,e]}{\delta_{\ell}D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,e]}+D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[\Delta]=:L[\Delta]

(16)

then

x+\Delta-\ell^{\prime}e\succ_{K}0
and

-D\varphi(x+\Delta-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]\leq-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]
.

Proof.

For the first conclusion, we note that
-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]<\delta_{\ell}^{-1}
implies that

|e|_{x-\ell e}<\delta_{\ell}^{-1}
(by part (iv) of Lemma
2.3
).
This means that
-\delta_{\ell}^{-1}(x-\ell e)\prec_{K}e\prec_{K}\delta_{\ell}^{-1}(x-\ell e)
. Rearranging we see that
x-\ell e-\delta_{\ell}e\succ_{K}0
. It follows that
x-\ell^{\prime}e\succ_{K}0
.

For the second conclusion, we write

-D\varphi(x+\Delta-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]+D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]=\left[-D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]+D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]\right]\\
+\left[-D\varphi(x+\Delta-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]+D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]\right]

(17)

and bound the two terms in (
17
) separately.
Lemma
5.9
tells us that

-D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]-(-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e])\leq\delta_{\ell}D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,e].

For the second term in (
17
) we use Taylor’s theorem with integral remainder together with the Hessian control bound (Lemma
5.7
) for
\varphi
. This gives

\displaystyle-D\varphi(x+\Delta-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]+D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}\varphi(x+t\Delta-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,\Delta]\;dt

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1+t|\Delta|_{x-\ell^{\prime}e})^{2}}D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,\Delta]\;dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,\Delta]}{1+|\Delta|_{x-\ell^{\prime}e}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,\Delta]}{1+(-D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[\Delta])}

\displaystyle\leq-\delta_{\ell}D^{2}\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e,e],

where the second last inequality follows from the fact that
|\Delta|_{x-\ell^{\prime}e}\leq-D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[\Delta]
and the last inequality follows from the assumption that
L[\Delta]\geq 1
.
Substituting into (
17
) we see that
-D\varphi(x+\Delta-\ell^{\prime}e)[e]+D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]\leq 0
, as required.
∎

We conclude this section with a proof of the third key progress result used to prove the correctness of the generalized BSS algorithm.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in K\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=e\in\textup{int}(K)
.
Let
\delta_{\ell},\delta_{u},\epsilon_{u},\epsilon_{\ell}>0
. Let
\ell\,e\prec_{K}x\prec_{K}u\,e

and let
-D\varphi(ue-x)[e]\leq\epsilon_{u}
and
-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]\leq\epsilon_{\ell}
.
Let
U[\cdot]
and
L[\cdot]
denote the linear functionals defined in (
14
) and (
16
), respectively.
If
0<\delta_{u}^{-1}+\epsilon_{u}\leq\delta_{\ell}^{-1}-\epsilon_{\ell}
then
there exists
j\in[m]
and
\alpha>0
such that
U[\alpha x_{j}]\leq 1\leq L[\alpha x_{j}]

Proof.

It is enough to show that

L[e]=\sum_{j\in[m]}L[x_{j}]\geq\sum_{j\in[m]}U[x_{j}]=U[e].

If this were the case then

\sum_{j\in[m]}(L[x_{j}]-U[x_{j}])\geq 0

from which we can deduce that there exists some
j
such that
L[x_{j}]\geq U[x_{j}]
. Furthermore, since
x_{j}\in K\setminus\{0\}
and
e\in\textup{int}(K)
, it follows from the definition (
14
) of
U[\cdot]
and from Lemma
5.6
that
U[x_{j}]>0
.
Choosing
\alpha=\frac{2}{L[x_{j}]+U[x_{j}]}>0
would complete the proof because

\frac{2}{L[x_{j}]+U[x_{j}]}U[x_{j}]\leq 1\quad\iff\quad L[x_{j}]-U[x_{j}]\geq 0

and

\frac{2}{L[x_{j}]+U[x_{j}]}L[x_{j}]\geq 1\quad\iff\quad L[x_{j}]-U[x_{j}]\geq 0.

It remains to show that
L[e]\geq U[e]
. To do so, we observe that the univariate function
g(t)=-D\varphi(te-x)[e]
is monotonically non-increasing and the univariate function
h(t)=-D\varphi(x-te)[e]
is monotonically non-decreasing. This is because
g^{\prime}(t)=-D^{2}\varphi(te-x)[e,e]\leq 0
and
h^{\prime}(t)=D^{2}\varphi(x-te)[e,e]\geq 0
. It then follows that

L[e]=-(-D\varphi(x-\ell^{\prime}e)[e])+\delta_{\ell}^{-1}\geq-(-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e])+\delta_{\ell}^{-1}\geq-\epsilon_{\ell}+\delta_{\ell}^{-1}

and

U[e]=-D{\varphi}(u^{\prime}e-x)[e]+\delta_{u}^{-1}\leq-D\varphi(ue-x)[e]+\delta_{u}^{-1}\leq\epsilon_{u}+\delta_{u}^{-1}.

The inequality
L[e]\geq U[e]
then follows from our assumption that
-\epsilon_{\ell}+\delta_{\ell}^{-1}\geq\epsilon_{u}+\delta_{u}^{-1}
.
∎

We conclude this section with a proof of Proposition
5.4
. This completes all of the arguments that constitute the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Proposition
5.12
tells us that there exists
j\in[m]
and
\alpha>0
such that
U[\alpha x_{j}]\leq 1
.
From the upper barrier shift result (Proposition

), we can conclude that

x+\alpha x_{j}\prec_{K}u+\delta_{u}
and that
-D\varphi((u+\delta_{u})e-(x+\alpha x_{j}))[e]\leq-D\varphi(ue-x)[e]
.

Similarly, Proposition

tells us that
L[\alpha x_{j}]\geq 1
. Moreover,
\Phi_{\ell,e}(x)=-D\varphi(x-\ell e)[e]\leq\epsilon_{\ell}<\delta_{\ell}^{-1}
(from the assumption that

∎

6
Pairwise-self-concordant barriers

In this section, we establish a sufficient condition for a self-concordant barrier
\varphi
for a convex cone
K
to be pairwise-self-concordant, i.e., to satisfy Definition
5.2
.
The sufficient condition is based on the operator monotonicity of a family of univariate functions associated with
\varphi
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a self-concordant barrier for a closed, pointed, full-dimensional
convex cone
K
. Given
x\in\textup{int}(K)
, and
u,v\in K
with
|u|_{x}\leq 1
, define

h:(-1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

h(t)=D\varphi(x+tu)[v].

If
h
is operator monotone for all choices of
x\in\textup{int}(K)
, and
u,v\in K
with

|u|_{x}\leq 1
then
\varphi
is a pairwise-self-concordant barrier for
K
.

Proof.

We make an affine change of variables
and consider
g:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

g(t)=h(t-1)
.
This has the following properties:

•

g(t)=D\varphi(x+(t-1)u)[v]
;

•

g^{\prime}(t)=D^{2}\varphi(x+(t-1)u)[v,u]
and so
g^{\prime}(1)=D^{2}\varphi(x)[v,u]
;

•

g^{\prime\prime}(t)=D^{3}\varphi(x+(t-1)u)[v,u,u]
and so
g^{\prime\prime}(1)=D^{3}\varphi(x)[v,u,u]
.

By the integral representation (
5
) of operator monotone functions defined on the positive half-line (from Section
2.4
), there is a positive measure supported on
[0,1]
such that

g(t)=g(1)+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t-1}{\lambda(t-1)+1}\;d\mu(\lambda).

Differentiating under the integral sign, we have that, for all
t\in(1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)
(with
0<\epsilon<1/2
, say),

g^{\prime}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(\lambda(t-1)+1)^{2}}\;d\mu(\lambda)\;\;\textup{and}\;\;g^{\prime\prime}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{-2\lambda}{(\lambda(t-1)+1)^{3}}\;d\mu(\lambda).

These formulas are valid by applying the dominated convergence theorem. Indeed, in each case the integrand is uniformly bounded because, for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
and all
t\in(1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)
we have that
|t-1|\leq\epsilon
and
|\lambda(t-1)+t|\geq|t|-\lambda|t-1|\geq 1-2\epsilon
. Therefore,
\left|\frac{t-1}{\lambda(t-1)+t}\right|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{1-2\epsilon}
and
\left|\frac{1}{(\lambda(t-1)+t)^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{(1-2\epsilon)^{2}}
.

Now
g^{\prime}(1)=\int_{0}^{1}\;d\mu(\lambda)=D^{2}\varphi(x)[v,u]
.
The inequality

0\leq-D^{3}\varphi(x)[v,u,u]\leq 2D^{2}\varphi(x)[v,u]

follows from the observing that

-g^{\prime\prime}(1)=2\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\;d\mu(\lambda)
,
and
0\leq\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\;d\mu(\lambda)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\;d\mu(\lambda)=g^{\prime}(1)
.

To establish that
\varphi
is pairwise-self-concordant,
we need to show that
0\leq-D^{3}\varphi(x)[v,u,u]\leq 2D^{2}\varphi(x)[v,u]|u|_{x}

for all
u\in K
, not just for
u\in K
such that
|u|_{x}\leq 1
.
For a general (unnormalized)
u\in K
, we know that

0\leq-D^{3}\varphi(x)[v,u/|u|_{x},u/|u|_{x}]\leq 2D^{2}\varphi(x)[v,u/|u|_{x}].

The result follows by multiplying through by
|u|_{x}^{2}
.
∎

It is fairly straightforward to check that the sufficient condition of Lemma
6.1
is
satisfied by the self-concordant barrier
-\log\det(X)
for the positive semidefinite cone.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
X\succ 0
be any positive definite matrix, and
U,V\succeq 0
be positive
semidefinite matrices and assume that
U\preceq X
.
Let
\varphi(X)=-\log\det(X)
.
Consider the function
h:(-1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

h(t)=D\varphi(X+tU)[V]=-\textup{tr}\left[(X+tU)^{-1}V\right].

Then
h
is operator monotone.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation let
U_{X}=X^{-1/2}UX^{-1/2}
and let
V_{X}=X^{-1/2}VX^{-1/2}
.
Since
U
and
V
are positive semidefinite, it follows that
U_{X}
and
V_{X}

are also positive semidefinite. Using this notation, then

h(t)=-\textup{tr}[(I+tU_{X})^{-1}V_{X}].

Let
U_{X}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}P_{i}
be
an eigenvalue decomposition of
U_{X}
where
P_{i}
is the (positive semidefinite)
orthogonal projector onto
the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
.
Since
U_{X}
is positive semidefinite,
each of the
\lambda_{i}
is positive.
Then

h(t)=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{1+t\lambda_{i}}\textup{tr}(P_{i}V_{X}).

Each of the functions
-1/(1+ta)
for
a>0
is operator monotone.
Since
\textup{tr}(P_{i}V_{X})\geq 0
for all
i
, we have expressed

h
as a non-negative combination of such functions, and so
h
is
operator monotone.
∎

The same result holds for any hyperbolic barrier function. This can be established
directly using similar techniques to those employed to show that hyperbolic barriers have the
negative curvature property
[
13
, Theorem 6.1]
. Another approach is
to invoke the Helton-Vinnikov theorem

.

Let

Corollary 6.4
.

Let

be a polynomial that is homogeneous of degree

.
Then

7
Further results on sparsification functions

In this section, we discuss further bounds on sparsification functions of cones that can
be obtained by combining results from Sections
3
,
4
, and
5
.

7.1
Cones having lifted representations with respect to hyperbolicity cones

We begin with the proof of Corollary
1.9
, which says that if
p
is hyperbolic with respect to
e
and
C
is a closed convex cone with a
\Lambda_{+}(p,e)
-lift, then
\textsc{sp}_{C}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil 4\textup{deg}(p)/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof of Corollary
1.9
.

If
C
has a proper
\Lambda_{+}(p,e)
-lift, then
\textsc{sp}_{C}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{\Lambda_{+}(p,e)}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon
(by Proposition
1.8
), and so the bound follows from the bound on the sparsification function for a hyperbolicity cone (Corollary
1.6
).

As such, assume that that the lift is not proper. In other words, suppose that
C=\pi(\Lambda_{+}(p,e)\cap L)
where
\pi
is a linear map and
L
a linear space that does not meet the relative interior of
\Lambda_{+}(p,e)
. Let
F
be the smallest face of
\Lambda_{+}(p,e)
that contains
\Lambda_{+}(p,e)\cap L
. Then it follows, for instance, from
[
25
, Proposition 2.2]
that

\Lambda_{+}(p,e)\cap L=F\cap L
and that
\textup{relint}(F)\cap L
is non-empty.
Furthermore, it follows from
[
26
, Corollary 3.4]
that
F
is the hyperbolicity cone corresponding to some hyperbolic polynomial
q
with
\textup{deg}(q)\leq\textup{deg}(p)
. Therefore,
C=\pi(F\cap L)
has a proper
F
-lift, and so

\textsc{sp}_{C}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{F}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil 4\,\textup{deg}(q)/\epsilon^{2}\rceil\leq\lceil 4\,\textup{deg}(p)/\epsilon^{2}\rceil.

∎

7.2
Sparsification functions and dual cones

Recall that Theorem
4.2
gives a bound of
\lceil(4\nu/\epsilon)^{2}\rceil
on the sparsification function of any convex cone that admits a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier.
We have seen that this result can be improved to
\lceil 4\nu/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
when
K
admits a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous pairwise-self-concordant barrier. However, there are situations where
Theorem
4.2
gives interesting sparsification results.

For instance, suppose that
K=\Lambda_{+}(p,e)^{*}
is the dual cone of a hyperbolicity cone associated with a low-degree hyperbolic polynomial
p
.
In general, the dual cone of a hyperbolicity cone is not a hyperbolicity cone
[
34
]
, and little is known about lifted representations of these cones.
Despite this, we can get a logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for
\Lambda_{+}(p,e)^{*}
with parameter
\textup{deg}(p)
via the following result.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
31
, Theorem 2.4.4]
)
.

Let
K
be a closed pointed full-dimensional convex cone and let
\varphi
be a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for
K
.
Then
\varphi^{*}(y):=\sup_{x}\langle x,y\rangle-\varphi(x)
is a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier for
-K^{*}
.

Applying Theorem
4.2
immediately yields the following bound on the sparsification functions
of the dual cones of hyperbolicity cones.

Corollary 7.2
.

Suppose that
p
is a hyperbolic polynomial of degree
d
, hyperbolic with
respect to
e
with associated
hyperbolicity cone
\Lambda_{+}
. Let
\Lambda_{+}^{*}
denote the associated dual cone.
Then
\textsc{sp}_{\Lambda_{+}^{*}}(\epsilon)\leq\lceil(4d/\epsilon)^{2}\rceil
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

This is remarkable because it gives another source of convex cones, other than hyperbolicity cones, where the sparsification function is bounded above by a quantity that is independent of the dimension of the cone.

We currently do not know, in general, whether the dual cone of a hyperbolicity cone admits a pairwise-self-concordant barrier, let alone one that is
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous for some
\nu<4\deg(p)^{2}
. As such, it is unclear whether there is any way to obtain an improved bound on
\textsc{sp}_{\Lambda_{+}^{*}}(\epsilon)
by directly applying Theorem
5.1
.

7.3
Example: epigraphs of the spectral and nuclear norms

As an illustration of the results in this section, let
n>k
be positive integers and consider the cones

\displaystyle K_{\textup{sp}}

\displaystyle=\left\{(X,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}\times\mathbb{R}\;:\;\sigma_{1}(X)\leq t\right\}\quad\textup{and}\quad

\displaystyle K_{\textup{nuc}}

\displaystyle=\left\{(X,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}\times\mathbb{R}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}(X)\leq t\right\},

where
\sigma_{1}(X)\geq\sigma_{2}(X)\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{k}(X)
are the singular values of the
n\times k
matrix
X
(with
k\leq n
).
The cone
K_{\textup{sp}}
is the epigraph of the spectral norm. The cone
K_{\textup{nuc}}
is the epigraph of the nuclear norm. Consider the setting where
k
is fixed and
n
is growing. The special case
k=1
gives the second order cone (or Lorentz cone).

It turns out that
K_{\textup{nuc}}=K_{\textup{sp}}^{*}
, which follows from the duality of the spectral and nuclear norms. Moreover,
K_{\textup{sp}}
is the hyperbolicity cone associated with the hyperbolic polynomial
-\log\det(t^{2}I_{k}-X^{\intercal}X)
of degree
2k

[
3
, Section 6]
. Therefore the sparsification function of
K_{\textup{sp}}
is bounded above by
\lceil 8k/\epsilon^{2}\rceil
, despite being a cone of dimension
nk+1
.

Interestingly,
K_{\textup{sp}}
also admits the
(k+1)
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier,

-\log\det(tI_{k}-X^{\intercal}X/t)-\log(t)

(see, e.g.,
[
31
, Proposition 5.4.6]
), which is not a hyperbolic barrier. However, this means that
K_{\textup{nuc}}
also admits a
(k+1)
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier (by Theorem
7.1
). Theorem
4.2

then implies that the sparsification function of

K_{\textup{nuc}}
is bounded above by
\lceil(4(k+1)/\epsilon)^{2}\rceil
, despite having dimension
nk+1
.

8
Sparsification and packing and covering conic programs

This section considers a special class of conic programs that generalize packing and covering linear programs. These are called covering and packing conic programs, respectively. Sparsification gives structural information about near-optimal solutions of covering conic programs. Sparsification also allows packing conic programs to be approximated by a potentially simpler problem instances.

A
covering linear program
is an optimization problem of the form

\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}\;b^{\intercal}y\;\;\textup{subject to}\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}a_{i}\geq c.

(18)

where
c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, and
b\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
.
The corresponding dual problem, known as a
packing linear program
, has the form

\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\;c^{\intercal}x\;\;\textup{subject to}\;\;a_{i}^{\intercal}x\leq b_{i}\;\;\textup{for $i=1,2,\ldots,k$}

(19)

This section considers how natural conic generalizations of (
18
) and (
19
) can be sparsified in certain ways.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a finite dimensional real inner product space.
Let
K\subseteq\mathcal{E}
be a closed convex cone and let
K^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{*}
be the corresponding dual cone. Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in K
and
c\in K
.
A natural conic generalization of a covering linear program
is an optimization problem of the form

\textsc{cover}(b,c):=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}\;\langle b,y\rangle\;\;\textup{subject to}\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}a_{i}\succeq_{K}c,

(20)

where
b\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
.
The corresponding dual problem has the form

\textsc{pack}(b,c):=\sup_{x\in K^{*}}\;\langle c,x\rangle\;\;\textup{subject to}\;\;\langle a_{i},x\rangle\leq b_{i}\;\;\textup{for $i=1,2,\ldots,k$},

(21)

and is a natural conic generalization of a packing linear program. If each entry of

b
is strictly positive then it is straightforward to check that
strong duality holds for this pair of conic programs since Slater’s condition holds.

The following result shows that if we replace the cost vector
c
of (
21
) with a cost
vector
c^{\prime}
that is close to
c
with respect to the ordering induced by
K
(e.g., obtained via sparsification if
c
is expressed as a sum of elements of
K
),
then the optimal value of the resulting problem is approximately preserved.
This idea is used in the context of algorithms for the max-cut semidefinite program where the cost is the Laplacian of a graph, and the modified cost is obtained via spectral sparsification.

Proposition 8.1
.

Suppose that
c\in\textup{relint}(K)
and
c=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}
where
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{m}\in K
. Let
c^{\prime}
be such that
(1-\epsilon)c\preceq_{K}c^{\prime}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)c
. If
b>0
then

(1-\epsilon)\textsc{pack}(b,c)\leq\textsc{pack}(b,c^{\prime})\leq(1+\epsilon)\textsc{pack}(b,c).

Proof.

Let
z
be any feasible point for (
21
).
Then, since
z\in K^{*}
, we have that

(1-\epsilon)\langle c,z\rangle\leq\langle c^{\prime},z\rangle\leq(1+\epsilon)\langle c,z\rangle.

But then

\textsc{pack}(b,c^{\prime})=\sup_{x\in K^{*}}\langle c^{\prime},x\rangle\geq\langle c^{\prime},z\rangle\geq\langle c,z\rangle(1-\epsilon).

Since
z
is an arbitrary point that satisfies

z\in K^{*}
and
\langle a_{i},z\rangle\leq b_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, we can take the supremum of the right hand side over
z
to establish that
\textsc{pack}(b,c^{\prime})\geq(1-\epsilon)\textsc{pack}(b,c)
.

For the reverse inequality, let
y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
be such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}a_{i}\succeq_{K}c
be any feasible point for the dual problem (
20
).
Then
(1+\epsilon)y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
and
satisfies

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1+\epsilon)y_{i}a_{i}=(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}a_{i}\succeq_{K}(1+\epsilon)c\succeq_{K}c^{\prime}.

Therefore,
(1+\epsilon)\langle b,y\rangle\geq\textsc{cover}(c^{\prime})
. Taking the infimum over
y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}a_{i}\succeq_{K}c
gives

(1+\epsilon)\textsc{cover}(b,c)\geq\textsc{cover}(b,c^{\prime}).

By strong duality we have that
\textsc{cover}(b,c^{\prime})=\textsc{pack}(b,c^{\prime})
and
\textsc{cover}(b,c)=\textsc{pack}(b,c)
, completing the argument.
∎

Sparsification can be used in another way in the context of conic covering problems. Indeed, if
non-trivial sparsification with respect to
K
is possible, then it implies the existence of near-optimal sparse solutions to conic covering problems (
20
).

Proposition 8.2
.

Suppose that
b>0
and
c\in\textup{relint}(K)
and let
y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
be an optimal point for (
20
). Then, for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
such that

•

y^{\prime}
is feasible for (
20
);

•

y^{\prime}
has at most
\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
non-zero entries; and

•

\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\langle b,y^{\prime}\rangle\leq\textsc{cover}(b,c)\leq\langle b,y^{\prime}\rangle

Proof.

Consider the point
e=\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}a_{i}\succeq_{K}c
. Since
c\in\textup{relint}(K)
it follows that
e\in\textup{relint}(K)
. Fix
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
From the definition of the sparsification function of
K
, there exists
S\subseteq[k]

with
|S|\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
, and positive
scalars
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in S}
, such that

(1-\epsilon)e\preceq_{K}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}y_{i}a_{i}\preceq_{K}(1+\epsilon)e.

(22)

Let
y_{i}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}y_{i}/(1-\epsilon)
for
i\in S
and
y_{i}^{\prime}=0
for
i\in[k]\setminus S
.
Then
y_{i}^{\prime}\geq 0
for all
i
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}^{\prime}a_{i}=\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}y_{i}a_{i}/(1-\epsilon)\succeq_{K}e\succeq_{K}c
. Therefore
y^{\prime}
is feasible for (
20
). It follows that
\langle b,y^{\prime}\rangle\geq\textsc{cover}(b,c)
.

Let
x
be any optimal point for (
21
). Since
b>0
, strong duality holds. Therefore, the primal-dual optimal pair
(y,x)
satisfies the complementarity conditions

\displaystyle\langle c,x\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}a_{i},x\right\rangle=\langle e,x\rangle\quad\textup{and}

(23)

\displaystyle y_{i}b_{i}

\displaystyle=y_{i}\langle a_{i},x\rangle\quad\textup{for all $i=1,2,\ldots,k$}.

(24)

We then have that

\langle c,x\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\left\langle e,x\right\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\geq}}\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\left\langle\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}y_{i}a_{i},x\right\rangle\\
=\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}y_{i}\langle a_{i},x\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}y_{i}b_{i}=\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\langle b,y^{\prime}\rangle

where (a) follows from (
23
), (b) follows from (
22
) and the fact that
x\in K^{*}
, and (c) follows from (
24
).
The final result then follows
from the fact that
\textsc{cover}(b,c)=\textsc{pack}(b,c)=\langle c,x\rangle
.

∎

9
Discussion

This paper introduces the sparsification function of a closed convex cone, and develops basic bounds on this quantity for different families of convex cones. Theorem
1.1
directly generalizes the celebrated linear-sized spectral sparsification results of Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava
[
2
]
to the setting of convex cones that admit a pairwise-self-concordant barrier, such as the hyperbolic barriers for hyperbolicity cones. We conclude by discussing some natural questions raised by the results in this paper.

Algorithms:

Our proof of Theorem
5.1
, which gives a bound on the sparsification function of convex cones that admit a pairwise-self-concordant barrier, is algorithmic. However, the resulting algorithm, while polynomial time in the case of the positive semidefinite cone,
is not particularly efficient. There has been a line of work
[
1
,
23
,
18
,
21
]
that gives faster algorithms for linear-sized spectral sparsification, i.e., for the case of sparsifying sums of elements of the positive semidefinite cone. It would be interesting to understand the extent to which these algorithms can be generalized to the case of a convex cone that admits a

\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous pairwise-self-concordant barrier.

Similarly, another approach to sparsification (for cuts
[
4
]
, hypergraphs
[
39
]
, sums of norms
[
16
]
, linear codes
[
19
]
, and in the spectral sparsification model
[
40
]
) is based on importance sampling. The rough idea is that sampling the terms in the sum based on suitably defined importance weights gives an appropriately sparsified sum, with high probability. In the case of spectral sparsification, such randomized constructions come at the expense of a logarithmic factor in the number of terms required. It would be interesting to see whether a natural analogue of such randomized constructions can achieve, up to logarithmic factors, the same sparsification results as we achieve here. The main challenge appears to be controlling the resulting empirical process over the dual cone
K^{*}
.

Sparsification with constrained weights:

In the present paper, the
only requirement on the weights
\lambda_{i}
(for
i\in[m]
) is that they are non-negative. There
are interesting graph sparsification problems (for instance degree-preserving sparsification)
where it is natural also to ask that the weights also satisfy certain constraints, such as
that they lie in a subspace. The discrepancy-based approach to spectral
sparsification is flexible enough to sparsify sums of positive semidefinite matrices while
ensuring that the weights lie in a given (high-dimensional) affine subspace
[
21
]
.
It would be interesting to extend our abstract sparsification model to also capture this situation.

Pairwise-self-concordant barriers:

We have seen strong bounds on the sparsification
function of convex cones that admit pairwise-self-concordant barriers. However, the most general family
of convex cones we are aware of that admit pairwise-self-concordant barriers are hyperbolicity cones.
It would be very interesting to construct other convex cones that admit such barriers. It
seems likely that this is possible, given that hyperbolicity cones enjoy the (apparently)
stronger property that
t\mapsto D\varphi(x+tv)[u]
is operator monotone whenever
x\in\textup{int}(K)
and
u,v\in K
. It would be also interesting to understand how this property of self-concordant barriers relates to other properties, such as the negative curvature property
[
30
]
.

Faces:

We have seen, in Section
3.2
, that if
F
is a face of a convex cone
K
then, under certain additional technical assumptions on the face,
\textsc{sp}_{F}(\epsilon)\leq\textsc{sp}_{K}(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. It would be interesting to determine whether these additional technical assumptions are necessary or whether sparsification functions are monotone (or even strictly monotone) along faces, in general. The main challenge here appears to be that of relating errors with respect to the ordering induced by
K
to errors
with respect to the ordering induced by
F
.

Duality:

Our most general bound on the sparsification function of a cone (Theorem
4.2
) is invariant under duality, in the sense that it gives the same bound on the sparsification function of
K
and
K^{*}
(by using the fact that if
K
has a
\nu
-logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier, then so does
K^{*}
). However, we do not know, in general how the sparsification functions of
K
and
K^{*}
are related.

Existence theorem on the UV limit of Wilsonian RG flows of Feynman measures
Jobst Ziebell

Abstract
In nonperturbative formulation of Euclidean signature quantum field theory (QFT), the vacuum state is characterized by the Wilsonian renormalization group (RG) flow of Feynman measures. Such an RG flow is a family of Feynman measures on the space of ultraviolet (UV) regularized fields, linked by the Wilsonian renormalization group equation. In this paper we show that under mild conditions, a Wilsonian RG flow of Feynman measures extending to arbitrary regularization strenghts has a factorization property: there exists an ultimate Feynman measure (UV limit) on the distribution sense fields, such that the regularized instances in the flow are obtained from this UV limit via taking the marginal measure against the regulator. Existence theorems about the flow and UV limit of the corresponding action functional are also proved.



Keywords
: Wilsonian renormalization, renormalization group flow, UV limit

Introduction

In the Feynman functional integral formulation of nonperturbative interacting Euclidean signature quantum field theories (QFTs), the vacuum state of a model is described by a Feynman measure living on the space of field configurations. More precisely, it is described by a Wilsonian renormalization group (RG) flow of Feynman measures on the space of ultraviolet (UV) regularized fields. As is well known, the need for the regularization and renormalization comes from the necessity of spacetime pointwise multiplication of certain distribution sense fields, as spelled out in the following.

Classically, a Euclidean field theory is described by its action functional
S
on the space of smooth and sufficiently rapidly decreasing field configurations. It is assumed that a splitting
S=T+V
is given, with
T
being a quadratic positive definite kinetic term, such as a Klein–Gordon term
T(\varphi)=\int\varphi\,(-\Delta+m^{2})\varphi
, and with
V
being a higher than quadratic degree interaction term bounded from below, such as
V(\varphi)=g\int\varphi^{4}
, the integrals performed over the spacetime.

The tentative definition for the Feynman measure of the interacting QFT model is the product measure

\int(\dots)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\phi):=\int(\dots)\,\rme^{-V(\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)

\int(\dots)\,\rme^{-(T+V)(\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\phi

\gamma

\rme^{-V}

A workaround to this problem, called
Wilsonian regularization
, was developed in the 1970s, which can be rigorously formulated as follows. First, some continuous linear operator
C

coarse-grainig

On flat spacetime, it is customary to consider only those coarse-grainings which are translationally invariant, and these can be seen to be the convolution operators by test functions, or in momentum space description these can be represented as multiplication by some momentum damping profile function. \rme^{-V}\cdot(C_{*}\,\gamma)

\mathop{\mathrm{Ran}}\nolimits(C)

\displaystyle\exists\,\mathrm{real\;valued\;functional}\;z\;\mathrm{of\;coarse{\shortminus}grainings}:

(4)

\displaystyle\Big.\quad\forall\,\mathrm{coarse{\shortminus}grainings}\;C,C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime}\;\mathrm{with}\;C^{\prime\prime}=C^{\prime}\,C:

(5)

\displaystyle{}_{\Bigg.}\qquad\forall\,\mathrm{real\;valued\;functional\;(``observable")\;}O\;\mathrm{on\;smooth\;fields}\,:

(6)

\displaystyle\quad\int\limits_{\varphi^{\prime\prime}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Ran}}\nolimits(C^{\prime\prime})}O(z(C^{\prime\prime})\,\varphi^{\prime\prime})\;\mathrm{d}\mu_{C^{\prime\prime}}(\varphi^{\prime\prime})\;=\;\int\limits_{\varphi\in\mathop{\mathrm{Ran}}\nolimits(C)}O(C^{\prime}\,z(C)\,\varphi)\;\mathrm{d}\mu_{C}(\varphi).

(7)

That is, given some smoothed observable, its expectation value is calculable consistently from any applicable UV regularized Feynman measure instance in the flow. In a sense, a Wilsonian RG flow of Feynman measures is almost as good as if a single ultimate Feynman measure existed on the unregularized fields, i.e. a UV limit.

In this paper we prove that for bosonic fields over a flat spacetime manifold, given a Wilsonian RG flow of UV regularized Feynman measures

\mu_{C}=z(C)^{-1}_{\;\;*}\,\big(\,C_{*}\,\mu\,\big)
holds, i.e. such that
\mu_{C}
are the pushforwards (marginal measures) of that ultimate Feynman measure
\mu

and
\gamma_{C}
of regularized Feynman measures have an interaction potential

relative to each-other such that
\mu_{C}=\rme^{-V_{C}}\cdot\gamma_{C}

C

V_{C}
will exist for all coarse-gainings
C
, and the flow of potentials will have a UV limit

on the distributional sense fields such that

has the same lower bound as

if those potentials were bounded from below at a suitably chosen coarse-graining

In the present paper, however, due to the Euclidean signature setting, stronger statements can be proved, such as an existence theorem on the UV limit of relative interaction potential between two flows. Wilsonian renormalization group (RG) flows

The mathematical results presented in this paper focus on Euclidean field theories formulated over flat spacetimes, i.e. spacetimes isometric to

Remark 3
.

In the above definition, each member of the family
(\mu_{\eta})_{\eta\in\mathcal{R}}
was defined on Borel sets of

\displaystyle\mathrm{Borel}(C_{\eta}[\mathcal{S}^{\prime}])=\left\{B\cap C_{\eta}[\mathcal{S}^{\prime}]\;\big|\;B\in\mathrm{Borel}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right\}

holds. This, in turn, means that every Borel measurable subset of

\displaystyle\nu_{\eta}(B):=\mu_{\eta}(B\cap C_{\eta}[\mathcal{S}^{\prime}])\qquad B\in\mathrm{Borel}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}).

(14)

The extended family of measures

Lemma 4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hausdorff locally convex topological vector spaces, and suppose that
X
is a countable union of weakly compact sets. Then for every continuous map
u:\,X\to Y
and weakly closed set

is a Borel set in
Y

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{K}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of weakly compact sets in
X
such that

, then

\displaystyle u[X]+A

\displaystyle\quad=\quad

\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(u[\mathcal{K}_{n}]+A).

(15)

Note that every continuous linear map

is weakly continuous, hence

is weakly compact in

. Furthermore, the sum between a compact and a closed set is closed, according to

[
20

In probability and measure theory, the notion of Polish spaces are rather important. These are such topological spaces, which are complete, separable, and metrizable. A generalization of such spaces are the Souslin spaces, which are images of Polish spaces by continuous maps. The significance of Souslin spaces in measure theory are given by the following collection of known theorems.

(iii)

In

(iv)

Lemma 9
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{S}
with nowhere vanishing
F(\eta)
. Then, the convolution
operator
C_{\eta}:\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime},\,t\mapsto C_{\eta}\,t:=\eta\star t
is injective. In particular, then
C_{\eta}:\,\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}
is injective.

Proof.

Take some
\eta\in\mathcal{S}
and
t\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Then the Fourier transform of the convolution of
\eta
and
t
is
F(\eta\star t)=F(t)F(\eta)
as tempered distribution, meaning that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
one has
(F(\eta\star t)\,|\,\varphi)=(F(t)\,|\,F(\eta)\cdot\varphi)
. In particular, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{S}
that identity holds. Consider now such an
\eta
which has nowhere vanishing
F(\eta)
. Then for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
the function
\varphi/F(\eta)
is everywhere defined, smooth and compactly supported, i.e.
\varphi/F(\eta)\in\mathcal{D}
. Consequently, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
one has
(F(\eta\star t)\,|\,\varphi/F(\eta))=(F(t)\,|\,\varphi)
. If
t\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is such that
\eta\star t=0
holds, then by means of our observation, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
the identity
(F(t)\,|\,\varphi)=(F(\eta\star t)\,|\,\varphi/F(\eta))=0
needs to hold. That is, the tempered distribution
F(t)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is zero on the dense subspace
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{S}
, implying that
F(t)=0
. By the injectivity of the Fourier transformation,
t=0
follows.
∎

Lemma 10
.

(sequential positive factorization)
Let
(j_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{S}
that converges to some
j\in\mathcal{S}
. Then there exist a function
\eta\in\mathcal{R}_{+}
and a sequence
(\ell_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{S}
convergent to some
\ell\in\mathcal{S}
, such that
j_{n}=\eta\star\ell_{n}
holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By assumption,
\mathcal{J}:=\left\{j_{n}\,|\,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\cup\{j\}
is a compact set in
\mathcal{S}
. Applying Remark
7
(
iv
) it follows that there exits some
\eta\in\mathcal{R}_{+}
and some compact set
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{S}
, such that
\mathcal{J}=\eta\star\mathcal{L}
holds. By this observation, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
\ell_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
j_{n}=\eta\star\ell_{n}
holds. Since the sequence
(\ell_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
runs in a compact set, therefore it is bounded and has accumulation points. We will show that all its accumulation points are the same, which implies that it is convergent. For let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
be accumulation points. Then, there exists some index subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
(\ell_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

converges to

. For these, one has that for all

\eta\star\ell_{n_{k}}=j_{n_{k}}
, moreover for all
l\in\mathbb{N}:

\eta\star\ell_{m_{l}}=j_{m_{l}}
. By the continuity of convolution it follows then that
\eta\star\lambda_{1}=j
and
\eta\star\lambda_{2}=j
, i.e. one has
\eta\star\lambda_{1}=\eta\star\lambda_{2}
. Since one had

, the convolution by
\eta

and thus
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
follows. Therefore,
(\ell_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some

.
∎

Remark 11
.

The following results on approximate identities are recalled from literature.

(i)

The convergence of a sequence of test functions
\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{D}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) to the Dirac delta distribution

in the
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}

one has

\mathcal{D}
topology. On the other hand, it is well known that one can construct special Dirac delta approximating sequences

(

\mathcal{D}
. These are called

, see e.g.
[
26
]
Theorem 6.32(a).

(ii)

It is common knowledge that the space
\mathcal{S}
also admits approximate identity in the following sense. Sequences
\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{S}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) exist such that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
one has
\alpha_{n}\star\varphi\to\varphi
in the
\mathcal{S}
topology as

. For let us take any
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
and define its compressed version
\alpha_{\Lambda}(x):=\Lambda^{N}\,\alpha(\Lambda\,x)

(

. One can verify that for all

one has
\alpha_{\Lambda}\star\varphi\to\varphi
in the
\mathcal{S}
topology as
\Lambda\to\infty
. To confirm this, one should note that

is topological isomorphism, and therefore it is enough to show that
(1-F(\alpha_{\Lambda}))\cdot F(\varphi)\to 0
in the
\mathcal{S}
topology as
\Lambda\to\infty
. The canonical topology of
\mathcal{S}
is characterized by the family of seminorms
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|Q(x)\,P(\partial_{x})\,\psi(x)|
on a

being any polynomials. Clearly, the seminorms
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|P(\partial_{x})\,Q(x)\,\psi(x)|
(

\|P(\partial)\,Q\,\psi\|_{L^{2}}
(
\psi\in\mathcal{S}
), with
P,Q
being polynomials. Using
\left.F(\alpha_{\Lambda})\right|_{\omega}=\left.F(\alpha)\right|_{\omega/\Lambda}
(
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

and all

,

one has

Remark 12
.

Recall that given some sigma-additive non-negative valued finite measure

on the Borel sets of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
it has a corresponding Fourier transform function

\displaystyle Z(j)

Remark 13
.

Let

Theorem 14
.

Let

We are going to construct the function

be arbitrary but fixed, and choose
\eta\in\mathcal{R}
and

\displaystyle Z(j):=Z_{\eta^{t}}(\ell).

\displaystyle Z_{\eta_{1}^{t}}(\ell_{1}\star\alpha)=Z_{\alpha^{t}\star\eta_{1}^{t}}(\ell_{1})=Z_{\alpha^{t}}(\eta_{1}\star\ell_{1})

\displaystyle\Big.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=Z_{\alpha^{t}}(\eta_{2}\star\ell_{2})=Z_{\alpha^{t}\star\eta_{2}^{t}}(\ell_{2})=Z_{\eta_{2}^{t}}(\ell_{2}\star\alpha)

(23)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\mbox{as }n\to\infty),

(24)

where we used the continuity of

As a next step, we show that

4
Existence of UV limit of relative interaction potential

In QFT applications, one often needs to address the problematics of comparing Wilsonian RG flows of two related models. For instance, the flow of an interacting model, with respect to a reference flow corresponding to the Gaussian measure subordinate to the free Klein–Gordon operator
(-\Delta+m^{2})
, as outlined in Section
1

\mu_{\eta}
and
\gamma_{\eta}

(

between them, such that the measure
\mu_{\eta}

\tilde{\gamma}_{\eta}

\eta

denotes the reference measure
\gamma_{\eta}
re-expressed on the rescaled fields by the factor

f_{\eta}:\,C_{\eta}[\mathcal{S}^{\prime}]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{0}^{+}

\mu_{\eta}=f_{\eta}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{\eta}
holds. (In QFT, often this relative density function is expressed through its negative logarithm
V_{\eta}
, i.e. via
f_{\eta}=\rme^{-V_{\eta}}

\mu_{\eta}=f_{\eta}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{\eta}
means that for each Borel set
A

\mu_{\eta}(A)=\int\limits_{\varphi\in A}f_{\eta}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\gamma}_{\eta}(\varphi)=\int\limits_{\phi\in\binrel@{(z_{\eta}\,C_{\eta})}\binrel@@{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt(z_{\eta}\,C_{\eta})}\limits^{-1}}(A)}f_{\eta}(z_{\eta}\,C_{\eta}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)

where at the last equality we used the fact that the family of reference measures

guaranteed by Corollary

3
3

3

As outlined at the end of Section
1
, also formalized in Definition

That is, one can adapt a normalization convention in which it does not appear explicitely in the notation, when considering only a single flow. In this convention, it re-appears as a running relative field renormalization factor, when comparing two different flows against each-other.
The answer to this question on physics ground is not evident at all, since the whole concept of Wilsonian RG flow was invented in order to overcome the difficulty of directly specifying a measure

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}h(\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\phi)

Remark 19

Assuming the conditions of Lemma

Remark 22

In the usual informal QFT RG theory approach, the differential form of Eq.(

, given some initial data

at

Moreover, the described informal procedure implicitly uses a number of tacit assumptions, the justification of which is not easy to establish. Furthermore, even if such an ODE solution could be produced, it is merely a sufficient condition for a solution to Eq.(

5
Case studies on example models

As shown in Corollary
16
, a Wilsonian RG flow not terminating at some finite UV regularization strength always originates from a UV limit Feynman measure. That is, characterizing such flows is equivalent to characterizing their UV limit Feynman measures. The most common approach in the literature is to solve an informal ODE flow equation similar to Remark
22
, and try to obtain a global solution admitting a UV limit. Besides such informal approach, a rigorous alternative is to make an educated guess directly on the UV limit Feynman measure. In the following we demonstrate this procedure on the
\varphi^{4}
and similar models in spacetime dimensions 1 to 4, which need different techniques. Our guidelines will be Corollary
16
, Corollary
21
and Theorem
23
.

The most naive method is to construct the sought-after UV limit measure
\mu
from a fixed reference (Gaussian) measure
\gamma
on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
as described in Section
1
, and from an interaction potential
V:\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, according to
\mu:=\rme^{-V}\cdot\gamma
. Here
V
is some kind of Borel measurable function, associated to the naive potential
\mathcal{V}:\,\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, usually defined by some local expression similar to that of
\mathcal{V}(\varphi)=g\,\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{4}
, with
\Omega
being some bounded open region of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(IR cutoff). The most simple association procedure of
V
to
\mathcal{V}
is by smoothing (mollifying), i.e. taking the limit of
\mathcal{V}\circ C_{\eta_{n}}
in a suitable sense as
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an approximate identity in
\mathcal{S}
.
To that end, let us introduce the set of mollifier sequences

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big\{(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mbox{ in }\mathcal{S}\;\Big|\;(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\;\mbox{ is approximate identity in }\mathcal{S}\Big\}

(34)

often used in the following.

Example 24

(Extension by a single mollifier sequence)
.

Let
\gamma
be a reference measure and
\mathcal{V}:\,\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
a Borel measurable functional that we wish to extend to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\Delta
a sequence.
Define the extension

V:\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},\qquad\phi\mapsto\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{V}\left(\eta_{n}\star\phi\right)\,.

(35)

This is clearly measurable (
[
28
]
Theorem 1.14) and if one can control the above limit for a given potential, it is arguably one of the simplest extensions of
\mathcal{V}
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

As a specific mollifier sequence is singled out in the above construction, it is not canonical. A more canonical method is to simply use all of the mollifying sequences, and take the best of them.

Example 25

(The greedy extension)
.

Let
\gamma
be a reference measure and
\mathcal{V}:\,\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
a Borel measurable functional on the smooth fields. Then define the measurable function
V
via

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathop{(\gamma)\!\inf}\limits_{(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\Delta}\;\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\;\mathcal{V}\circ C_{\eta_{n}}

(36)

which we call the
greedy extension
of
\mathcal{V}
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Here
(\gamma)\!\inf
is a shorthand for the infimum taken in the lattice of all
\gamma
-measurable functions, i.e. it is the “functionwise” infimum (see
[
29
]
Lemma 2.6 and the preceding paragraphs, as well as in
[
30
]
Lemma 2.6.1 and surrounding paragraphs). It is necessary to take the lattice (functionwise) infimum because the set
\Delta
of mollifier sequences is uncountable, and therefore the pointwise infimum might lead to measurability issues.

Note the identity
\rme^{-V}=\mathop{(\gamma)\!\sup}\limits_{(\eta_{n})\in\Delta}\;\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\;\rme^{-\mathcal{V}\circ C_{\eta_{n}}}
if
V
is the greedy extension of
\mathcal{V}
. Therefore, the above is the most economic measurable extension of potentials via smoothing, optimized such that
\rme^{-V}
has the largest possible overlap integral with
\gamma
on all Borel sets. The following theorem summarizes its basic properties.

Theorem 26
.

Let
\gamma
be a reference measure, and let
\mathcal{V}:\,\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a measurable function bounded from below. Define
V
to be its greedy extension according to Eq.(
36
). Then
V
is also a measurable function
\gamma
-essentially bounded from below, thus
\mu:=\rme^{-V}\cdot\gamma
defines a sigma-additive non-negative valued finite measure. The measure
\mu
is not the zero measure if and only if

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\mathop{(\gamma)\!\sup}\limits_{(\eta_{n})\in\Delta}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\,\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(\eta_{n}\star\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)\;>\;0.

(37)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{(\eta_{n})\in\Delta}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(\eta_{n}\star\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)\;>\;0

(38)

is sufficient for
\mu
being not the zero measure.

Proof.

The first statement Eq.(
37
) is evident from the definition of the greedy extension. The second statement Eq.(
38
) is seen as follows.

Using the knowledge that
\mathcal{V}
is bounded from below, the family of functions
\rme^{-\mathcal{V}\circ C_{\eta}}
(
\eta\in\mathcal{S}
) are bounded from above by some
K\geq 0
. That is, they have a
\gamma
-integrable majorant
K
. Taking now an approximate identity
(\eta_{n})\in\Delta
, and applying the Fatou lemma (
[
28
]
Theorem 1.28) to the sequence of non-negative valued measurable functions
K-\rme^{-\mathcal{V}\circ C_{\eta_{n}}}
, then taking the negative of both sides of the Fatou inequality, and adding
\int_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}K\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)
, one infers

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(C_{\eta_{n}}\,\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)\;\leq\;\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(C_{\eta_{n}}\,\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi).

(39)

Since
\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}\circ C_{\eta_{n}}}\leq\mathop{(\gamma)\!\sup}\limits_{(\eta_{n})\in\Delta}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}\circ C_{\eta_{n}}}
holds
\gamma
-almost everywhere, by applying the monotonity of integration to the right hand side of Eq.(
39
), one has

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(C_{\eta_{n}}\,\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)\;\leq\;\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\mathop{(\gamma)\!\sup}\limits_{(\eta_{n})\in\Delta}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(C_{\eta_{n}}\,\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi).

(40)

Taking now the
\sup\limits_{(\eta_{n})\in\Delta}
of both sides of this inequality,

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{(\eta_{n})\in\Delta}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(C_{\eta_{n}}\,\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)\;\leq\;\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\mathop{(\gamma)\!\sup}\limits_{(\eta_{n})\in\Delta}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(C_{\eta_{n}}\,\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)

(41)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;=\;\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\rme^{-V(\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)

(42)

is inferred. Thus, Eq.(
38
) is indeed a sufficient condition for the integral on the rightmost side of Eq.(
41
) to be positive, which completes the proof.
∎

Considering the elementary properties of the supremum and limsup, Eq.(
37
) and Eq.(
38
) may be simplified even further, as stated below.

Corollary 27
.

Let
\gamma
be a reference measure, and let
\mathcal{V}:\,\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a measurable function bounded from below. Let
V
be its greedy extension according to Eq.(
36
), and define the corresponding sigma-additive non-negative valued finite measure
\mu:=\rme^{-V}\cdot\gamma
. Then,
\mu
is not the zero measure if and only if there exists some approximate identity
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{S}
, such that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(\eta_{n}\star\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)\;>\;0

(43)

holds. Moreover,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\rme^{-\mathcal{V}(\eta_{n}\star\phi)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)\;>\;0

(44)

is sufficient for
\mu
being not the zero measure.

The above necessary and sufficient condition Eq.(
43
) for the nonvanishing of
\rme^{-V}\cdot\gamma
is typically hard to evaluate, given some
\mathcal{V}
. The sufficient condition Eq.(

), however, is in principle evaluable for concrete QFT models, by mapping
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
\gamma
and the operations defining
\mathcal{V}
to sequence spaces. It is still an unresolved interesting question, that along which additional properties on
\mathcal{V}
the condition Eq.(
44
) would be also necessary. If such properties could be uncovered, it would provide a rather powerful tool for checking the renormalizability of concrete QFT models. For potentials having finite UV limit of their expectation value, i.e. in case

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}(\eta_{n}\star\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(\phi)\;<\;\infty

holds for some approximate identity
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{S}
, Jensen’s inequality (
[
28
]
Theorem 3.3) implies that the sufficient condition Eq.(
44
) is satisfied. Evaluating this, it follows that for the IR cutoff
\varphi^{4}

It is of course well known in the literature that this model has a Feynman measure over
\mathbb{R}^{1}

The condition Eq.(

The greedy extension preserves lower bound, and therefore is particularly attractive for extension of regularized potentials which are themselves bounded from below, as it respects the constraint by Theorem

Taking a step back, the use of mollifiers is actually just a way to approximate a given
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
by a sequence in
\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. If we simply consider every possible approximation at any point

Example 28

(The lower semicontinuous envelope)
.

Let
\gamma
be a reference measure and
\mathcal{V}:\,\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
Borel measurable.
Set
W
to be

W\left(\phi\right)=\cases{\mathcal{V}\left(\phi\right)&if $\phi\in\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}$\\
+\infty&otherwise,}

(46)

i.e. to be the extension of
\mathcal{V}
by
+\infty
wherever it was not already defined in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, and take its lower semicontinuous envelope

\underline{W}\left(\phi\right)\;:=\;\inf_{\phi_{\alpha}\to\phi}\liminf_{\alpha}W\left(\phi_{\alpha}\right)\;=\;\inf_{\varphi_{\alpha}\to\phi}\liminf_{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\left(\varphi_{\alpha}\right)\,,

(47)

where
(\phi_{\alpha})
and
(\varphi_{\alpha})
are any net in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, and the
\liminf

The above construction corresponds to defining
\underline{W}
by setting its epigraph to be equal to the closure of the epigraph of
W
, the closure taken with respect to the weak topology of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

A straightforward consequence is that all sublevel sets
\binrel@{\underline{W}}\binrel@@{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\underline{W}}\limits^{-1}}([-\infty,t])

are weakly closed in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
Hence,
\underline{W}
is automatically Borel measurable.

A benefit of this construction is that it is always smaller than any (meaningful) limit of a sequence
\mathcal{V}\circ C_{\eta_{n}}
(

\rme^{-\underline{W}}
has a greater chance of not being zero

\mathcal{V}

\rme^{-\underline{W}}
and
\gamma
is nonzero.

It is apparent that the construction of UV limit potentials in general is very difficult.

6
Concluding remarks

In a Euclidean signature interacting QFT model, the vacuum state is described by the Wilsonian renormalization group (RG) flow of regularized Feynman measures. These are Feynman measures on the spaces of ultraviolet (UV) regularized fields, linked by a consistency condition called the Wilsonian renormalization group equation (RGE). That equation asserts that proceeding in the flow from the UV toward infrared (IR) means subsequent application of coarse-graining operators to fields, and that the corresponding regularized Feynman measure instances in the flow are marginals of each-other with respect to the intermediary coarse-graining (Definition
2
). Important QFT models are those which admit Wilsonian RG flows meaningful at any UV regularization strengths.

In this paper it was shown that the Wilsonian RG flow of Feynman measures extending to any UV regularization strengths admit a factorization property. Namely, there exists an ultimate (UV limit) Feynman measure on the unregularized distributional fields, such that the regularized Feynman measure instances in the flow are obtained from that ultimate measure, via marginals by coarse-graining (Corollary
16
).

In addition to proving the existence of the UV limit Feynman measure, it was shown that if two Wilsonian RG flows of Feynman measures admit a relative interaction potential with respect to one-another at a suitable coarse-graining, then the entirety of the two flows admit a flow of relative interaction potentials, moreover, there exists a UV limit relative interaction potential between the UV limit Feynman measures as well (Corollary
21
).

It was also shown, that whenever the regularized relative interaction potential between two Wilsonian RG flows is bounded from below at a suitable coarse-graining, then the UV limit relative interaction potential is also bounded from below, with the same bound (Theorem
23
).

Applications of the above theorems to concrete interacting QFT models were provided, over continuum flat Euclidean signature spacetimes, in dimensions 1 to 4 (Section
5
). In particular, we prove that in arbitrary dimensional spacetimes, an interacting QFT model defined by a free Klein–Gordon kinetic term + an interaction potential which is bounded both from below and above, is nonperturbatively renormalizable (Theorem
26
, Corollary
27
). Such models are defined e.g. by the “Weidling” potential
V(\varphi)=g\,\int\frac{1}{1+l^{4}\,\varphi^{4}}\,\varphi^{4}
, or by a sine–Gordon model. Possible triviality of these models is still an open question (for very recent advances on that question, see
[
36
]
). The nonperturbative renormalizability of further relevant models, such as the competing Higgs model defined by the potential
V(\varphi,\psi)=g\,\int(\varphi^{2}-\psi^{2})^{2}
also could not be decided, but there are indications for its renormalizability (it avoids the no-go Theorem
30
, which obstructs high dimensional
\varphi^{4}
models). The results proved in the paper were also discussed in the light of known examples, in particular the
\varphi^{4}
models over 1 to 4 dimensional spacetimes
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
.

Appendix A
On the supports of Gaussian measures

For a sigma-additive non-negative valued measure
\gamma
on the Borel sets of some topological space there is the well known notion of topological support, denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\gamma)
, see e.g.
[
22
]
Definition A.3.14. It is defined as the intersection of those closed sets whose complements are
\gamma
-measure zero. By construction, the topological support of a measure is a closed set, and is a generalization of the support notion of the continuous functions on topological spaces. Not surprisingly, typical Gaussian measures turning up in QFT are so-called non-degenerate, meaning that their topological support is the full space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Since, however, in QFT the considered density functions
\rme^{-V}
to be integrated against the reference Gaussian measure
\gamma
are not continuous, only measurable, the topological support of the reference measure
\gamma
is not very informative on the non-vanishing of the overlap integral of
\rme^{-V}
and
\gamma
.

Due to the above complication, another notion of support is also used in the literature. A Borel set
B
is said to support
\gamma
if its complement has zero
\gamma
-measure. Clearly, if one can find some Borel set
B
such that its
\gamma
-measure is nonzero, and the density function
\rme^{-V}
is nonwhere zero on
B
, then their product
\rme^{-V}\cdot\gamma
is not the zero measure, i.e. it defines a proper probability measure modulo normalization, meaning that
V
describes a renormalizable QFT model.

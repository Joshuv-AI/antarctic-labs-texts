Counting conjugacy classes and automorphism-conjugacy classes of ZM-groups
Marius Tărnăuceanu

Abstract
In this paper, we count the number of conjugacy and automorphism-conjugacy classes of elements of a ZM-group. The size of a conjugacy class with respect to these two equivalence relations is also counted.



MSC2020 :
Primary 20D60; Secondary 20D45, 11A25.

Key words :
conjugacy class, automorphism-conjugacy class, ZM-group, group action, Burnside’s lemma, Menon’s identity.

1
Introduction

Given a finite group
G
, we denote by
{\rm Inn}(G)
and
{\rm Aut}(G)
the inner automorphism group and the automorphism group of
G
. Then the orbits of
G
under the natural actions of
{\rm Inn}(G)
and
{\rm Aut}(G)
are called the
conjugacy classes
and the
automorphism-conjugacy classes
of
G
. They are very useful in the study of groups, especially in representation theory. The numbers of these orbits are usually denoted by
k(G)
and
k^{\prime}(G)
, respectively, and represent two important invariants of
G
. Note that
k(G)
is equal to the number of complex irreducible characters of
G
.

In what follows, we will determine these numbers and the sizes of conjugacy classes for ZM-groups. Recall that a
ZM-group
is a finite group with all Sylow subgroups cyclic. By
[
5
]
, such a group is of type

{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)=\langle a,b\mid a^{m}=b^{n}=1,\hskip 2.84526ptb^{-1}ab=a^{r}\rangle,

where the triple
(m,n,r)
satisfies the conditions

(m,n)=(m,r-1)=1\mbox{ and }r^{n}\equiv 1\hskip 2.84526pt({\rm mod}\hskip 2.84526ptm).

It is clear that
|{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)|=mn
and
Z({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))=\langle b^{d}\rangle
, where
d
is the multiplicative order of

r
modulo
m
, i.e.

d=o_{m}(r)={\rm min}\{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\mid r^{k}\equiv 1\hskip 2.84526pt({\rm mod}\hskip 2.84526ptm)\}.

The subgroups of
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
have been completely described
in
[
2
]
. Set

L=\left\{(m_{1},n_{1},s)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}\hskip 2.84526pt\mid\hskip 2.84526ptm_{1}|m,\hskip 2.84526ptn_{1}|n,\hskip 2.84526pts<m_{1},\hskip 2.84526ptm_{1}|s\frac{r^{n}-1}{r^{n_{1}}-1}\right\}.

Then there is a bijection between
L
and the subgroup lattice

L({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))
of
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
, namely the function
that maps a triple
(m_{1},n_{1},s)\in L
to the subgroup
H_{(m_{1},n_{1},s)}
defined by

H_{(m_{1},n_{1},s)}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}(b^{n_{1}}a^{s})^{k}\langle a^{m_{1}}\rangle=\langle a^{m_{1}},b^{n_{1}}a^{s}\rangle.

Note that:

-

|H_{(m_{1},n_{1},s)}|=\frac{mn}{m_{1}n_{1}}
, for any
s
satisfying
(m_{1},n_{1},s)\in L
;

-

H_{(m_{1},n_{1},s)}
is normal in
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
if and only if
m_{1}\mid r^{n_{1}}-1
and
s=0
(see
[
10
]
);

-

H_{(m_{1},n_{1},s)}
is cyclic if and only if
\frac{m}{m_{1}}\mid r^{n_{1}}-1
(see
[
11
]
);

-

two subgroups of
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
are conjugate if and only if they have the same order (see
[
1
]
).

Our approach uses some basic tools of group action theory, such as the Orbit-Stabilizer theorem and the Burnside’s lemma, and involves modular arithmetic.

Orbit-Stabilizer theorem.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a finite set
X
and
x\in X
. Denote by

O_{x}=\{g\circ x\mid g\in G\}\mbox{ and }Stab_{G}(x)=\{g\in G\mid g\circ x=x\}

the orbit and the stabilizer of
x
in
G
. Then

|O_{x}|=[G:Stab_{G}(x)]=\frac{|G|}{|Stab_{G}(x)|}\,.

Burnside’s lemma.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a finite set
X
and

Fix(g)=\{x\in X\mid g\circ x=x\},\mbox{ for }g\in G.

Then the number of distinct orbits is

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|Fix(g)|.

Most of our notations are standard and will not be repeated here. Basic definitions and results on group theory can be found in
[
5
,
9
]
. For number theoretic notions we refer the reader to
[
6
,
8
]
.

2
Main results

Throughout this section we will use
G
to denote
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
. The general form of automorphisms of a metacyclic group had been determined in the main result of
[
3
]
. For ZM-groups, in
[
4
]
we obtained the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Each automorphism of
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)
is given by

b^{u}a^{v}\mapsto b^{yu}a^{x_{1}v+x_{2}[u]_{r}},\,u,v\geq 0,

for a unique triple of integers
(x_{1},x_{2},y)
such that

0\leq x_{1},x_{2}<m,\,(x_{1},m)=1,\,0\leq y<n\mbox{ and }y\equiv 1\hskip 2.84526pt({\rm mod}\hskip 2.84526ptd),

(1)

where

[u]_{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\!1+r+\cdots+r^{u-1},&u>0\\
\!\!0,&u=0\end{array}\right..

In particular, we have

|{\rm Aut}({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))|=m\varphi(m)\frac{n}{d}\,.

We are now able to compute the number of automorphism-conjugacy classes of
G
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The following equality holds:

k^{\prime}({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))=\displaystyle\frac{d}{m\varphi(m)}\displaystyle\sum_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in A}\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{\left[\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\,,o_{\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}}(r)\right]}\,,

(2)

where
A
is the set of triples
(x_{1},x_{2},y)
satisfying the conditions (1).

Proof.

We will apply Burnside’s lemma to the natural action of

{\rm Aut}(G)=\{\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)}\mid(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in A\}

on
G
. Let
(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in A
and

Fix(\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)})=H_{(m_{1},n_{1},s)}=\langle a^{m_{1}},\,b^{n_{1}}a^{s}\rangle,

where
(m_{1},n_{1},s)\in L
. Since
H_{(m_{1},n_{1},s)}
is the join of its subgroups
\langle a^{m_{1}}\rangle
and
\langle b^{n_{1}}a^{s}\rangle
,
m_{1}|m
and
n_{1}|n
are minimal with the property that there is
0\leq s<m_{1}
such that
m_{1}|s\frac{[n]_{r}}{[n_{1}]_{r}}
and both
a^{m_{1}}
and
b^{n_{1}}a^{s}
are fixed points of
\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)}
. We have

a^{m_{1}}\!\in\!Fix(\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)})\!\Leftrightarrow\!a^{x_{1}m_{1}}\!\!=a^{m_{1}}\!\Leftrightarrow\!m\,|\,m_{1}(x_{1}-1)\!\Leftrightarrow\!\frac{m}{(m,x_{1}-1)}\,|\,m_{1},

which leads to
m_{1}=\frac{m}{(m,x_{1}-1)}
, and

b^{n_{1}}a^{s}\!\in\!Fix(\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)})\!\Leftrightarrow\!b^{yn_{1}}a^{x_{1}s+x_{2}[n_{1}]_{r}}\!\!=b^{n_{1}}a^{s}\!\Leftrightarrow\!\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\!n\,|\,n_{1}(y-1)\\
\!\!m\,|\,s(x_{1}-1)+x_{2}[n_{1}]_{r}\end{array}\right..

Thus
n_{1}
is minimal such that
\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\,|\,n_{1}
and there is
0\leq s<m_{1}
with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\!m\,|\,s(x_{1}-1)\frac{[n]_{r}}{[n_{1}]_{r}}\\
\!\!m\,|\,s(x_{1}-1)+x_{2}[n_{1}]_{r}\end{array}\right..

Since

m\,|\,s(x_{1}-1)+x_{2}[n_{1}]_{r}\Rightarrow m\,|\,s(x_{1}-1)\frac{[n]_{r}}{[n_{1}]_{r}}\,,

it follows that
n_{1}
is minimal such that
\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\,|\,n_{1}
and

\!\!m\,|\,s(x_{1}-1)+x_{2}[n_{1}]_{r}

for some
0\leq s<m_{1}
, i.e. the congruence

(x_{1}-1)s\equiv-x_{2}[n_{1}]_{r}\,({\rm mod}\,m),

has solutions. This is equivalent to

(m,x_{1}-1)\,|\,x_{2}[n_{1}]_{r},

i.e.

\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}\,|\,r^{n_{1}}-1,

which means that

o_{\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}}(r)\,|\,n_{1}.

Thus

n_{1}=\left[\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\,,o_{\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}}(r)\right]

and

|Fix(\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)})|=\frac{mn}{m_{1}n_{1}}=\frac{n(m,x_{1}-1)}{\left[\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\,,o_{\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}}(r)\right]}.

Then we get

\displaystyle k^{\prime}(G)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{d}{mn\varphi(m)}\displaystyle\sum_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in A}\frac{n(m,x_{1}-1)}{\left[\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\,,o_{\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}}(r)\right]}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{d}{m\varphi(m)}\displaystyle\sum_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in A}\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{\left[\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\,,o_{\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}}(r)\right]}\,,

as desired.
∎

In the notation in the proof of Theorem 2.2, we observe that

\frac{n}{(n,y-1)}\leq n_{1}\leq n,

which leads to

(m,x_{1}-1)\leq|Fix(\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)})|\leq(m,x_{1}-1)(n,y-1).

By summing over all
(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in A
and using the well-known Menon’s identity
[
7
]
, we get

\tau(m)\leq k^{\prime}(G)\leq\frac{d^{2}}{n}f\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\tau(m),

where
f
is the arithmetic function defined by

f(\alpha)=\sum_{\beta=0}^{\alpha-1}(\alpha,\beta).

Note that if
\alpha=p_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\varepsilon_{k}}
is the decomposition of
\alpha
as a product of prime factors, then

f(\alpha)=\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\varepsilon_{i}+1-\frac{\varepsilon_{i}}{p_{i}}\right).

A simple formula for
k^{\prime}(G)
is obtained if
n
is a prime number.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
n
is a prime number, then the following equality holds:

k^{\prime}({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))=n-1+\tau(m).

(3)

Proof.

Since
n
is prime, we obtain
d=n
and
y=1
. By (2), it follows that

k^{\prime}(G)=\displaystyle\frac{d}{m\varphi(m)}\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{0\leq x_{1},x_{2}<m}_{\ (x_{1},m)=1}}\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{o_{\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}}(r)}\,.

Moreover, we have

n_{1}=o_{\frac{(m,x_{1}-1)}{(m,x_{1}-1,x_{2})}}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\!1,&\mbox{ if }\,(m,x_{1}-1)\mid x_{2}\\
\!\!n,&\mbox{ if }\,(m,x_{1}-1)\nmid x_{2}\end{array}\right.

and so

|Fix(\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)})|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\!n(m,x_{1}-1),&\mbox{ if }\,(m,x_{1}-1)\mid x_{2}\\
\!\!(m,x_{1}-1),&\mbox{ if }\,(m,x_{1}-1)\nmid x_{2}\end{array}\right.

Then

\displaystyle k^{\prime}(G)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m\varphi(m)}\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\ 0\leq x_{1}<m}_{\ (x_{1},m)=1}}\!\!\left(\,\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq x_{2}<m}|Fix(\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)})|\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m\varphi(m)}\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\ 0\leq x_{1}<m}_{\ (x_{1},m)=1}}\!\!\left(\,\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\ 0\leq x_{2}<m}_{(m,x_{1}-1)\mid x_{2}}}\!\!\!n(m,x_{1}-1)+\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\ 0\leq x_{2}<m}_{(m,x_{1}-1)\nmid x_{2}}}\!\!\!(m,x_{1}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m\varphi(m)}\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\ 0\leq x_{1}<m}_{\ (x_{1},m)=1}}\!\!\left[mn+(m,x_{1}-1)\left(m-\frac{m}{(m,x_{1}-1)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m\varphi(m)}\left(mn\varphi(m)+m\!\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\ 0\leq x_{1}<m}_{\ (x_{1},m)=1}}(m,x_{1}-1)-m\varphi(m)\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m\varphi(m)}\left(mn\varphi(m)+m\varphi(m)\tau(m)-m\varphi(m)\right)

\displaystyle=n-1+\tau(m),

completing the proof.
∎

In particular, for
n=2
and
r=m-1
we have
{\rm ZM}(m,n,r)\cong D_{2m}
, the dihedral group of order
2m
. Thus, the equality (3) becomes

k^{\prime}(D_{2m})=\tau(m)+1,\mbox{ for all odd integers }m\geq 3.

(4)

An example of applying the formula (2) is presented in the following.

Example 2.4
.

For the dicyclic group
{\rm Dic}_{3}={\rm ZM}(3,4,2)={\rm SmallGroup}(12,1)
, we have
d=2
and (2) leads to

k^{\prime}({\rm Dic}_{3})=5.

Next, we will focus on computing the number of conjugacy classes of
G
.

Theorem 2.5
.

The following equality holds:

k({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))=\displaystyle\frac{n}{md}\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=0}^{d-1}\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=0}^{m-1}\frac{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{o_{\frac{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{(m,r^{\alpha}-1,\beta)}}(r)}\,.

(5)

Proof.

We have

{\rm Inn}(G)=\{\varphi_{b^{\alpha}a^{\beta}}\mid\alpha=\overline{0,d-1},\beta=\overline{0,m-1}\},

where
\varphi_{b^{\alpha}a^{\beta}}
is the conjugation by
b^{\alpha}a^{\beta}
. In the notation in Theorem 2.2, it is easy to see that

\varphi_{b^{\alpha}a^{\beta}}=\varphi_{(r^{\alpha},\beta(1-r),1)}

and so

|Fix(\varphi_{b^{\alpha}a^{\beta}})|=\frac{n(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{o_{\frac{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{(m,r^{\alpha}-1,\beta)}}(r)}\,.

Then (5) follows by applying Burnside’s lemma to the natural action of
{\rm Inn}(G)
on
G
.
∎

We remark that

o_{\frac{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{(m,r^{\alpha}-1,\beta)}}(r)=1\Leftrightarrow\frac{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{(m,r^{\alpha}-1,\beta)}=1\Leftrightarrow(m,r^{\alpha}-1)\mid\beta.

(6)

Also, for
(m,r^{\alpha}-1)\nmid\beta
we have

\frac{1}{d}\leq\frac{1}{o_{\frac{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{(m,r^{\alpha}-1,\beta)}}(r)}\leq\frac{1}{p}\,,

(7)

where
p
is the smallest prime divisor of
d
. Then, writing

k(G)=\displaystyle\frac{n}{md}\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=0}^{d-1}(m,r^{\alpha}-1)\!\!\left(\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{0\leq\beta<m-1}_{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)\mid\beta}}\!\!\frac{1}{o_{\frac{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{(m,r^{\alpha}-1,\beta)}}(r)}+\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{0\leq\beta<m-1}_{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)\nmid\beta}}\!\!\frac{1}{o_{\frac{(m,r^{\alpha}-1)}{(m,r^{\alpha}-1,\beta)}}(r)}\!\right)

and using (6) and (7), we get

\frac{n}{d}\left(d-1+\frac{S}{d}\right)\leq k(G)\leq\frac{n}{d}\left(d-\frac{d}{p}+\frac{S}{p}\right),

(8)

where

S=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{d-1}(m,r^{\alpha}-1).

Note that if
d
is prime, then
p=d
and (8) leads to the equality

k(G)=\frac{n}{d}\left(d-1+\frac{S}{d}\right).

(9)

By considering the natural action of
\mathbb{Z}^{\times}_{m}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, we have

S=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{d-1}|Fix(\hat{r}^{\,\alpha})|,

implying that

m+d-1\leq S\leq\sum_{\hat{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}_{m}}|Fix(\hat{a})|=\varphi(m)\tau(m).

These inequalities, together with (8), show that

\frac{n(m+d^{2}-1)}{d^{2}}\leq k(G)\leq\frac{n[\varphi(m)\tau(m)+d(p-1)]}{dp}\,.

Again, if
n
is prime, then
d=n
is also prime and (9) gives a simple formula for
k(G)
.

Corollary 2.6
.

If
n
is a prime number, then the following equality holds:

k({\rm ZM}(m,n,r))=n-1+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{n-1}(m,r^{\alpha}-1).

In particular, for
n=2
and
r=m-1
we get

k(D_{2m})=\frac{m+3}{2},\mbox{ for all odd integers }m\geq 3.

Note that for even integers
m\geq 2
this equality becomes

k(D_{2m})=\frac{m+6}{2}\,.

The equality (9) can be also used to compute the number of conjugacy classes of
{\rm Dic}_{3}
, for which we obtain:

Example 2.7
.

k({\rm Dic}_{3})=6
.

Finally, we compute the sizes
|O^{\prime}_{b^{u}a^{v}}|
and
|O_{b^{u}a^{v}}|
of automorphism-conjugacy class and conjugacy class of an arbitrary element
b^{u}a^{v}\in G
.

Theorem 2.8
.

The following equalities hold:

a)

|O^{\prime}_{b^{u}a^{v}}|=\displaystyle\frac{m\,n\,\varphi\!\left(\displaystyle\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}\right)}{(n,u)\left(\displaystyle\frac{n}{(n,u)}\,,d\right)(m,[u]_{r})}
;

b)

|O_{b^{u}a^{v}}|=\displaystyle\frac{m}{(m,[u]_{r})}\,o_{\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}}(r)
.

Proof.

In both cases we will apply the Orbit-Stabilizer theorem. Also, we will use the notation

g=\displaystyle\frac{(m,[u]_{r})}{(m,[u]_{r},v)}\,.

a)

We have

|O^{\prime}_{b^{u}a^{v}}|=[{\rm Aut}(G):{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}(G)}(b^{u}a^{v})].

An automorphism
\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)}
is contained in
{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}(G)}(b^{u}a^{v})
if and only if
b^{u}a^{v}\in Fix(\varphi_{(x_{1},x_{2},y)})
, that is if and only if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\!n\,|\,u(y-1)\\
\!\!m\,|\,v(x_{1}-1)+x_{2}[u]_{r}.\end{array}\right.

Put
y=zd+1
, where
z\in\{0,1,...,\frac{n}{d}-1\}
. Then the first condition is equivalent with
\frac{n}{(n,u)}\mid zd
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{n}{h}\mid z,\vskip-5.69054pt

where
h=(n,u)\left(\frac{n}{(n,u)}\,,d\right)
, and so
y
can be chosen in
\frac{n}{d}:\frac{n}{h}=\frac{h}{d}
ways. We also have

\displaystyle|\{(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\in\overline{0,m-1}^{\,2}\mid(x_{1},m)=1,m\,|\,v(x_{1}-1)+x_{2}[u]_{r}\}|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\,\,\,\,0\leq x_{1}<m}_{\ (x_{1},m)=1}}|\{x_{2}\in\overline{0,m-1}\mid m\,|\,v(x_{1}-1)+x_{2}[u]_{r}\}|

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,0\leq x_{1}<m}_{\ (x_{1},m)=1,\,g\,|\,x_{1}-1}}\!\!\!\!\!(m,[u]_{r})

\displaystyle=(m,[u]_{r})\,\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(m)}{\varphi(g)}\,.

Thus

\displaystyle|O^{\prime}_{b^{u}a^{v}}|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{|{\rm Aut}(G)|}{|{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}(G)}(b^{u}a^{v})|}

\displaystyle=m\varphi(m)\frac{n}{d}:\displaystyle\frac{h}{d}\,(m,[u]_{r})\,\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(m)}{\varphi(g)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{m\,n\,\varphi(g)}{h(m,[u]_{r})}\,.

b)

We have

|O_{b^{u}a^{v}}|=[{\rm Inn}(G):{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Inn}(G)}(b^{u}a^{v})]\vskip-2.84526pt

and

\displaystyle|{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Inn}(G)}(b^{u}a^{v})|

\displaystyle=|\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\overline{0,d-1}\times\overline{0,m-1}\mid b^{u}a^{v}\in Fix(\varphi_{(r^{\alpha},\beta(1-r),1)})\}|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\alpha<d}|\{\beta=\overline{0,m-1}\mid m\,|\,v(r^{\alpha}-1)+\beta(1-r)[u]_{r}\}|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,0\leq\alpha<d}_{(m,[u]_{r})|v(r^{\alpha}-1)}}\!\!\!(m,[u]_{r})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\,\,\,0\leq\alpha<d}_{\,\,\,g|r^{\alpha}-1}}\!(m,[u]_{r})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{{}^{\,\,\,0\leq\alpha<d}_{\,\,\,o_{g}(r)|\alpha}}\!(m,[u]_{r})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{d\,(m,[u]_{r})}{o_{g}(r)}\,.

Thus

\displaystyle|O_{b^{u}a^{v}}|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{|{\rm Inn}(G)|}{|{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Inn}(G)}(b^{u}a^{v})|}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{dm}{\displaystyle\frac{d\,(m,[u]_{r})}{o_{g}(r)}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{m}{(m,[u]_{r})}\,o_{g}(r)

and the proof of our theorem is complete.
∎

Since
{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Inn}(G)}(b^{u}a^{v})\cong C_{G}(b^{u}a^{v})
, the above proof also gives the order of the centralizer of
b^{u}a^{v}
in
G
.

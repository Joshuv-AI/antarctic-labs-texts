Graphs with Independent Exact r r -covers for all r r
Hou Tin Chau

Abstract
For every natural number d d , we construct finite d d -regular simple graphs that, for every r ≤ d r\leq d , contain an independent exact r r -cover. This answers a question of Gray and Johnson that arose in their study of 2-step transit probabilities. We obtain some divisibility conditions on the order n n of graphs that for every r ≤ d r\leq d contain an independent exact r r -cover, and give constructions for d = 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 d=3,4,5,6 where the order of the graph is minimal (we deduce this minimality from our divisibility conditions). We construct these graphs as common coverings of smaller graphs. We revisit a result of Angluin and Gardiner on finite common coverings of two regular graphs of the same degree, and the result of Gross that regular graphs of even degree are Schreier coset graphs. We combine both results to provide a finite common covering of two regular graphs of the same degree, that uses fewer vertices than the construction of Angluin and Gardiner in some cases.

Definition 2.1

(
[
3

A

Semi-edges arise naturally when the two endpoints of an ordinary edge are mapped to the same vertex (

]

Definition 2.2

(Generalized graph covering,
[
3
]

Let

\pi

\pi\!\upharpoonright_{\Gamma(v)}:\Gamma(v)\to\Gamma(\pi(v))

on the dart neighbourhood of each vertex

.

For simple graphs, the map on the vertex sets

Abusing notation slightly, we will sometimes write a covering map as

Some standard properties of graph coverings are:

(Image of an edge,

Under a graph covering map

1.

Proposition 2.5

(Preimage of an edge,
[
3
]

Under a graph covering map

1.

These combinatorial coverings for generalized graphs differ from topological coverings only on how semi-edges are treated. When there are no semi-edges, the two concepts coincide if we view graphs as one-dimensional simplicial complexes (
[
3
]

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(see
[
11
]
pp. 4–5), consistent with the fact that an ordinary edge (with trivial fundamental group) gives a 2-fold covering over a semi-edge.

6.

Match the darts pointing at each other with the same colour.

Note that the functions

Remark 2.13
.

The construction in the proof of Lemma

depends upon the decomposition, not just upon the graphs

Corollary 2.14
.

Let

be even. If

are
d
-regular generalized graphs without semi-edges (i.e. multigraphs with loops), then they have a common covering with

Proof.

By Petersen’s 2-factor theorem (for multigraphs with loops), any regular graph of even degree has a

2.12
(with

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
d
be odd. If

are
d

Remark 2.16
.

In

Since

Proposition 4.7
.

The smallest order of a 5-regular simple graph with independent exact
r
-covers for all
r\leq 5
is 6048.

Proof.

Taking
r_{1}=1
and
r_{2}=3
in
Lemma

4.6
, we have
2^{5}\mid n
. Using the earlier
Lemma

4.3
with
r_{1}=1
and
r_{2}=4
, we have
3^{3}\mid n
. Also
7\mid n
from the existence of an independent exact
2
-cover. Hence
6048=2^{5}\times 3^{3}\times 7\mid n
.

For the construction, we apply
Corollary

2.15
to obtain a common covering of the following five 5-regular graphs:

for
r=1
,

K_{6}
;

for
r=2
,

a 7-vertex graph with semi-edge from
Lemma

3.1
Case 3;

for
r=3
,

an 8-vertex graph from
Lemma

3.1
Case 1, i.e., a
K_{5,3}
with a 5-cycle added within the independent set of size five;

for
r=4
,

a 9-vertex graph with semi-edge from
Lemma

3.1
Case 3, i.e., a
K_{5,4}
with a 1-factor added to the independent set of size 5 (the 1-factor consisting of a matching of size two together with one semiedge);

for
r=5
,

the dipole
D_{5}
(five parallel edges between two vertices).

The result is a simple graph because
K_{6}
is simple, it has an independent exact
r
-cover for all
r\leq 5
by Lemma
2.7
, and it has

6\times 7\times 8\times 9\times 2=6048
vertices.
∎

In the search of smallest graphs with independent exact
r
-covers for all
r\leq d
,
d=7
is therefore the smallest open case, for which we have a gap of a factor of 2 between the lower and upper bounds. For larger
d
, the gap between our lower and upper bounds grows superexponentially:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
N(d)
be the smallest order of a
d
-regular simple graph with an independent exact
r
-cover for all
r\leq d
. Then

d(2-o(1))\leq\log N(d)\leq d(\log(4d)-1+o(1)),

where
\log
is the natural logarithm.

Proof.

Let
G
be an optimal
d
-regular graph satisfying the condition, with
\left|G\right|=N(d)
.
First we compute a lower bound from the divisibility conditions. Using the fact that any independent exact
r
-cover has size
\frac{r}{d+r}N(d)
, in
Lemma

4.6
we also have

\left|S_{3}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2})\right|=\frac{d^{2}N(d)}{2(d+r_{1})(d+r_{2})},

so

2\lcm\{(d+r_{1})(d+r_{2}):0\leq r_{1}<r_{2}\leq d\}\ \text{ divides }\ d^{2}N(d),

and

N(d)\geq\frac{2}{d^{2}}\lcm\{(d+r_{1})(d+r_{2}):0\leq r_{1}<r_{2}\leq d\}

For a more explicit lower bound we note that
\operatorname{lcm}\{(d+r_{1})(d+r_{2}):0\leq r_{1}<r_{2}\leq d\}
is at least
\lcm(d+1,d+2,\ldots,2d)=\lcm(1,2,\ldots,2d)=\exp{\psi(2d)}
where
\psi
is the second Chebyshev function satisfying
\psi(2d)/(2d)=1+o(1).
(See e.g. Ch. 4 of
[
2
]
)

The upper bound from
Corollaries

2.14
and
2.15
is the product of
d+1,d+2,\ldots,2d
which is
(2d)!/d!=\exp(d(\log(4d)-1+o(1)))
by Stirling’s approximation.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

The lcm in the lower bound
(\Diamond)
is larger than
\exp\psi(2d)
, but it satisfies

\operatorname{lcm}\{(d+r_{1})(d+r_{2}):0\leq r_{1}<r_{2}\leq d\}\leq(\operatorname{lcm}\{1,2,\ldots,2d\})^{2}=\exp(2\psi(2d))=\exp((4+o(1))d),

so even if the lower bound is done more carefully with
(\Diamond)
, we can at most improve the constant in the exponent, but to close the super-exponential gap will require a new argument.

It would be interesting to determine the growth rate of
N(d)
. We do not know whether there are more efficient ways to construct common coverings, and whether there are constructions with a lot of independent exact
r
-covers not coming from common coverings.

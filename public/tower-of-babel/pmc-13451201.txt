HBO-NAS: class-aware zero-cost fitness for diversity-preserving neural architecture search through hybrid breeding optimization algorithm
Authors: Jie Sun, Pengfei Li, Zhiwei Ye, Jia Guo, Chuan Xu, Liye Mei, Zhina Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeural Architecture Search (NAS) with evolutionary computing increasingly relies on zero-cost proxies to mitigate the prohibitive computational cost of training candidate networks. However, existing proxies are mainly designed to maximize score–accuracy correlation, neglecting the landscape structure of objectives required to sustain population diversity. To address this issue, a class-aware, training-free objective function is proposed, which utilizes intra-class compactness and inter-class separability to induce a structured framework, a multi-modal fitness landscape that naturally prevents premature convergence. This capability effectively facilitates the discovery of a broader range of different, high-performing structures. When evaluated using Hybrid Breeding Optimization algorithm, our method consistently yields superior optimization performance, achieving the average accuracy of 71.18% on ImageNet16-120 within the DARTS search space, which is nearly equal to the reported best-performing architecture with the accuracy of 72.00%, while maintaining a high level of population diversity. These findings show the critical shift towards a search-centric perspective, where shaping the landscape structure of objectives is as important as ranking fidelity for discovering diverse structures.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Deep neural networks have been widely adopted in various fields of data mining, such as image recognition1,2, disease diagnosis3 and natural language processing4,5. However, their performance is highly dependent on the underlying network architecture6. Consequently, automating this design process is crucial to overcoming the limitations of manual engineering and fully unlocking the potential of deep learning models7,8. Neural architecture search (NAS) aims to automatically design neural network architectures that achieve competitive or superior performance compared to hand-crafted architectures9,10. By reducing reliance on human expertise, NAS has been successfully applied to image classification11,12, object detection13, and other tasks14,15. Despite its effectiveness, NAS continues to face major challenges in efficiency and search robustness, particularly in population-based evolutionary scenarios where the quality of the evaluation signal directly shapes search behavior16,17.

A fundamental bottleneck of conventional NAS lies in architecture evaluation18. Early NAS methods rely on fully training candidate architectures or employing costly proxy training schemes19,20, resulting in prohibitive computational overhead and limited scalability. For example, Zoph and Le21 proposed a reinforcement learning-based neural network architecture search method that uses recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to generate model descriptions of neural networks and trains these RNNs using reinforcement learning to maximize the expected accuracy of the generated architectures on a validation set. Because it validates the performance of individual architectures through actual training, the resulting architectures exhibit excellent performance. However, the overall cost of validation and search is extremely high. On the CIFAR-10 dataset, the Google Brain team actually used approximately 800 GPUs and spent 28 days (approximately 32, 000 GPU hours) to complete the architecture search for the CIFAR-10 task. Although researchers have proposed weight-sharing methods to reduce training time, the time and computing power required are still unacceptable22,23. To alleviate this issue, a number of zero-cost proxies have recently been proposed, which estimate architecture quality using signals extracted from untrained networks via a few forward or backward passes24. These methods are commonly assessed by their ability to rank architectures according to the final performance25,26. Mellor et al.27 proposed a training-free neural architecture search method called NASWOT, that predicts a network’s trained performance by measuring the activation overlap between datapoints in untrained networks. This approach enables efficient NAS on a single GPU in seconds without any network training, and is validated on various Network Design Spaces, achieving good results. Huang et al.28 proposed ePADS, a zero-cost proxy for zero-shot NAS, which includes a vector-based simplified NASWOT term (reducing complexity from O() to O(N)) and a fine-grained perturbation-aware term for distinguishing original and perturbed inputs. The two terms are combined via layer-wise score multiplication, and experiments show ePADS outperforms other zero-cost proxies in predictive reliability and efficiency. Xue et al.29 proposed SiGNAS, a similarity surrogate-assisted evolutionary NAS method based on graph neural networks, which predicts architecture performance by measuring the similarity between candidate architectures and optimal benchmarks.

However, most existing zero-cost proxies are inherently class-agnostic, aggregating global activation or gradient statistics without leveraging label information30,31. As a consequence, different architectures often receive similar evaluation signals, leading to weak guidance during the search32,33. This issue is particularly problematic for evolutionary NAS, where the search trajectory is fundamentally driven by the discriminative power of the fitness function33.

Evolutionary NAS operates by iteratively evolving a population of architectures based on their fitness scores, relying on the evaluation signal to shape the underlying fitness landscape34,35. However, when guided by the aforementioned indiscriminate zero-cost proxies, the resulting landscape becomes flat or deceptive25,36. Consequently, it is well known that evolutionary NAS methods are prone to premature convergence37,38, where architectural diversity diminishes rapidly, and the search stagnates around local optima. While existing solutions often introduce explicit diversity-preservation mechanisms such as novelty search and multi-objective optimization39–43, these strategies increase algorithmic complexity and are typically applied independently of the fitness function itself44.

In the paper, it is found that architectures inducing compact intra-class and well-separated inter-class activation patterns tend to generalize better after training. This phenomenon is consistent with prior findings in representation learning, where the class-conditioned geometry of feature spaces is closely tied to discriminative power45. Crucially, traces of class-aware structure can already be observed from activation patterns of randomly initialized networks, yet it has rarely been exploited as a training-free evaluation signal for NAS. Motivated by this insight, a class-aware zero-cost fitness function is proposed to evaluate candidate architectures by explicitly modeling intra-class compactness and inter-class separability of activation patterns obtained from a single forward pass of an untrained network. Conceptually, while methods like NASWOT and ePADS evaluate architectures based on global activation overlap or perturbation discrepancies, our formulation explicitly differentiates architectures based on their topological capacity to separate distinct semantic classes. By shifting from class-agnostic global statistics to class-conditioned structural signals, our method bridges a critical practical gap: it inherently prevents the premature convergence typically caused by flat fitness landscapes. Rather than targeting maximal ranking fidelity across the entire search space, the proposed fitness function is designed to induce a structured and multi-modal fitness landscape, providing differentiated yet complementary evaluation signals that are more suitable for guiding evolutionary search.

We integrate the proposed fitness function into a population-based Hybrid Breeding Optimization (HBO) algorithm46, which is inspired by the principles of hybrid rice breeding and exhibits strong convergence capability and stability, having been widely applied to solve various optimization problems, such as feature selection47. In this work, HBO is employed in its original form and serves solely as a generic evolutionary optimizer. The core idea is as illustrated in Fig. 1. By coupling HBO with the proposed class-aware evaluation, the resulting framework naturally preserves population diversity through the induced fitness landscape, without introducing additional diversity constraints or modifying the search algorithm itself, while maintaining extremely low evaluation cost.

Given the encoded population, the population-based evolutionary HBO algorithm is employed to search the architecture space. HBO iteratively evolves the population through hybridization, selfing, and renewal, while all candidate architectures are evaluated exclusively using the proposed class-aware zero-cost fitness without any training or parameter updates. In this work, HBO is employed as a generic population-based evolutionary optimizer without modification.

The specific motivation for selecting HBO in this framework lies in its unique multi-population paradigm. HBO inherently maintains three distinct sub-populations (maintainer, sterile, and restorer lines) that interact cooperatively. This mechanism is exceptionally well-suited to explore the multi-modal fitness landscape induced by our class-aware evaluation, allowing the sub-populations to simultaneously inhabit multiple competitive basins of attraction, thereby preventing structural collapse more effectively than standard single-population metaheuristics.

By coupling the proposed class-aware fitness with population-based search, the induced multi-modal fitness landscape tends to guide different subpopulations toward distinct local optima. This is because class-conditioned activation similarity emphasizes different architectural characteristics across classes, reducing the risk of a single dominant evaluation mode overwhelming the population and thereby facilitating more effective exploration under a strictly training-free evaluation protocol.

By inducing a structured and multi-modal fitness landscape, it naturally preserves population diversity during evolutionary search, effectively mitigating premature convergence without explicit diversity constraints.

Unlike rank-oriented zero-cost proxies that aim to impose a strong global ordering over architectures, the proposed fitness function intentionally avoids overly concentrated evaluation signals. By distributing architectural preferences across class-conditioned activation structures, our method induces multiple competitive basins in the fitness landscape, which is more compatible with population-based evolutionary search and less prone to premature convergence.

Experimental setting

In this section, experiments are conducted to evaluate the proposed method. First, it is demonstrated that even under random initialization, samples from the same class exhibit more similar activation patterns than those from different classes. Second, the effectiveness of the proposed similarity-based surrogate is validated by analyzing its Kendall’s rank correlation with the final test accuracy across multiple search spaces. Furthermore, PCA dimensionality reduction is used to verify whether the proposed Similarity proxy possesses stronger global discriminative ability and multi-peak exploration potential in the NAS search space. Third, the surrogate model is integrated into the HBO algorithm for architecture search and compared with state-of-the-art zero-cost surrogate models. Fourth, to test the robustness of HBO-NAS to its key hyperparameters, parameter sensitivity analysis is performed on the ImageNet16-120 dataset using the NAS-Bench-201 search space. Finally, to verify the role of each component in the NAS task, ablation experiments are performed on the ImageNet16-120 dataset using the DARTS search space. All experiments were conducted on a Windows 10 computer equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090ti, with the same random seed set to 42.

Evaluation metrics

We employ several commonly used metrics to evaluate experimental performance. Specifically, Hamming distance is used to quantify activation similarity, while Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients are adopted to measure the correlation between surrogate scores and true test accuracy. Search performance and population diversity are evaluated using classification accuracy and the average pairwise distance, respectively. Specifically, population diversity is calculated as the average pairwise distance between the continuous encodings v of all candidate architectures within the current generation. Since each dimension of the continuous vector v directly and deterministically maps to a specific operator choice or topological decision (as defined in Section Architecture Encoding and Search Strategy), the Euclidean distance in this encoding space serves as a direct, monotonically consistent proxy for structural diversity in the decoded discrete networks.

Although Zen-Score and ZiCo achieve higher correlations on NAS-Bench-101 (τ = 0.420 and 0.460, respectively), their performance is notably less stable across other search spaces, as discussed below.

We also acknowledge the stronger performance of ZiCo and SynFlow on the NAS-Bench-SSS CIFAR-10 task. This is largely because both ZiCo and SynFlow utilize fine-grained gradient information from backward passes, which is particularly effective in size-based spaces where parameter scales vary drastically. Despite lacking gradient signals, our method demonstrates superior cross-space stability (as seen in NDS).

On the ENAS and NASNet spaces, ePADS obtains the highest individual correlations (τ = 0.493 and 0.458, respectively); however, ePADS shows considerably weaker results on several other benchmarks (e.g., τ = 0.303 on NAS-Bench-SSS CIFAR-100), whereas our method maintains a more balanced profile. The NDS results also expose the instability of several widely used proxies: SynFlow produces negative correlations across all five NDS spaces, and GradNorm exhibits sign inconsistency (positive on DARTS but negative on NASNet and Amoeba). By contrast, our method yields strictly positive and practically meaningful correlations (τ> 0.37, ρ> 0.53) in every NDS space, highlighting its cross-space robustness.

The scatter plots in Fig. 4 provide a complementary visual perspective. On NAS-Bench-201 (all three datasets), the point clouds exhibit a clearly ascending trend with moderate dispersion, consistent with the high ρ values reported in Table 3. On NAS-Bench-SSS, the score distributions are wider and more spread, which is expected given the size-based nature of the search space; nonetheless, the overall positive trend is well preserved. In the NDS spaces, the scatter plots reveal a more challenging evaluation landscape. In particular, the DARTS space shows a dense cluster of architectures with test accuracy between 80% and 90%, yet our proxy score successfully separates them into distinguishable ranges, confirming its ability to provide meaningful ranking signals even in highly competitive accuracy regions. On the NASNet and Amoeba spaces, the presence of low-accuracy outliers (below 20%) increases visual scatter; however, the monotonic trend in the main body of the distribution remains intact.

A key practical requirement for zero-cost proxies is consistent reliability across heterogeneous search spaces. To highlight this, we note that several strong baselines exhibit pronounced performance swings: Zen-Score achieves τ = 0.420 on NAS-Bench-101 but drops to τ = 0.236 on NAS-Bench-201; SNIP yields τ = 0.511 on NAS-Bench-SSS CIFAR-10 but becomes negative on NAS-Bench-101 (τ = -0.140); ZiCo reaches τ = 0.729 on NAS-Bench-SSS CIFAR-10 yet falls to τ = -0.02 on NDS-Amoeba. Our method, by contrast, does not exhibit any negative correlation across any of the 14 benchmark–dataset combinations evaluated, and achieves and uniformly. This stability can be attributed to the class-conditioned similarity decomposition, which provides a structured and semantically grounded evaluation signal that is less susceptible to the idiosyncrasies of individual search space topologies.

To verify whether the proposed Similarity proxy possesses stronger global discriminative ability and multi-peak exploration potential in the NAS search space, we randomly sampled 2500 architectures on NAS-Bench-201 and projected their discrete encodings onto the same PCA two-dimensional embedding space. While maintaining consistency in the sampling set and embedding method, we compared and presented the proxy score landscapes of Similarity and NASWOT, focusing on the coverage of high-scoring regions.

As seen in the PCA landscape in Fig. 5, both proxies exhibit the characteristic of “high-scoring regions not concentrated in a single minimal neighborhood,” but their distribution patterns differ significantly. To quantitatively formalize this landscape analysis, we evaluated both the grid coverage and the density of local optima. Specifically, the top 10% high-scoring points of our Similarity proxy cover 72/144 grids, compared to 66/144 for NASWOT, indicating a broader coverage of high-quality regions in the embedding space. Furthermore, our proxy identifies 74 distinct local optima (constituting 2.96% of the samples), whereas NASWOT yields only 50 (2.00%). This higher density of distinct peaks formally demonstrates that our class-aware evaluation shapes a more rugged, multi-modal landscape. In summary, at the same sampling scale, the proposed Similarity metric exhibits more pronounced multi-modality and broader high-scoring region coverage, which fundamentally prevents premature convergence and is highly beneficial for the subsequent evolutionary mining of diverse candidate architectures.

NAS performance with similarity-based proxy

We first evaluate the impact of the proposed fitness function on evolutionary search behavior, specifically focusing on convergence stability and the convergence accuracy. As shown in Fig. 6, the convergence curves of the HBO search (solid lines) are generally more stable and reach high-performing solutions in fewer iterations compared to the GA (dashed lines). This stability stems from HBO’s inherent mechanism of retaining the top one-third of candidate architectures, preventing the loss of promising genes. Notably, despite the inherent volatility of GA, we observe that when equipped with our similarity-based scoring method, it still consistently outperforms baselines like NASWOT and ePADS. This indicates that our metric provides robust guidance for evolution even under stochastic selection pressure. Furthermore, guiding the HBO search with similarity-based scoring consistently yields higher average accuracy. For instance, on CIFAR-10 within the NAS-Bench-201 space, the method reaches near-optimal performance (approx. 94%) in fewer than ten iterations. Similarly, on ImageNet16-120 (DARTS space), it surpasses ePADS and converges to the optimum within approximately 20 iterations. This rapid convergence suggests that the structured fitness landscape induced by class-aware evaluation effectively guides the population toward global optima without getting trapped in local basins.

Beyond test accuracy, maintaining architectural variance is crucial for stable evolutionary search. As further illustrated in Fig. 7, we track the evolution of population diversity and its tangible benefits regarding resource costs (training time and model size). Population diversity serves as a critical indicator of search robustness, as a higher diversity score signifies that the evolutionary algorithm effectively resists premature convergence and explores a broader spectrum of structural modes. Regarding training efficiency, on the CIFAR-10 dataset (NAS-Bench-101), our method not only achieves the highest accuracy (91.80%) while maintaining high population diversity but also discovers architectures that are inherently more training-efficient. The average training time (as recorded in the NAS-Bench-101 tabular data) of the 30 architectures discovered by HBO-NAS is 60.80 seconds lower than that of the architectures found by NASWOT, indicating that our class-aware fitness naturally guides the search toward structurally efficient solutions. Regarding model efficiency, our approach demonstrates the capability to discover compact yet accurate architectures. On the CIFAR-10 dataset (ENAS space), while ranking second in accuracy with a marginal accuracy drop of 0.34%, our discovered model is significantly more lightweight–3.78% (0.07M) smaller than ePADS and 13.6% (0.28M) smaller than NASWOT. On both DARTS datasets, our method achieves optimal accuracy while maintaining optimal diversity. Notably, on the challenging ImageNet16-120 dataset, we minimize model size by 6.17% and 6.18% compared to NASWOT and ePADS, respectively, without sacrificing precision. Although we note a slight increase in model size on CIFAR-10 (DARTS space) compared to ePADS (0.12M), this is a reasonable trade-off for the superior convergence stability shown in Fig. 6.

Ablation study

To verify our components, we conducted ablations on ImageNet16-120 (Table 8). First, replacing binary patterns with raw activations (“w/o Binary”) degrades accuracy to 69.64%, confirming binary encoding’s robustness. Second, removing separation metrics (“w/o Class-Aware”) yields the lowest accuracy (68.47%) despite high diversity (1.60), implying degradation into blind exploration. Crucially, while excluding global diversity (“w/o Global Div”) matches the peak accuracy (71.18%), it causes population diversity to collapse to 0.48. This indicates that without global guidance, the search prematurely converges to a single structural mode. In contrast, the Full Method achieves the same optimal accuracy (71.18%) while sustaining significantly higher diversity (0.71), proving that effectively prevents representational collapse without compromising search performance. In summary, all three components are indispensable for the robust performance and sustained population diversity of HBO-NAS.

Conclusion

We proposed HBO-NAS, a diversity-preserving neural architecture search framework driven by a class-aware, training-free fitness function. Unlike conventional class-agnostic zero-cost proxies that primarily emphasize global ranking fidelity, the proposed fitness function explicitly models intra-class compactness and inter-class separability from a single forward pass, with the goal of inducing a structured and multi-modal fitness landscape suitable for population-based evolutionary search. Integrated with hybrid breeding optimization, HBO-NAS naturally maintains architectural diversity and mitigates premature convergence without modifying the underlying search algorithm.

Extensive experiments on standard NAS benchmarks demonstrate that the proposed fitness function leads to more stable search dynamics, higher sustained population diversity, and competitive final architectures under identical evaluation budgets. While maintaining competitive score-accuracy correlations with existing zero-cost proxies, the primary benefit of the proposed method lies in shaping evolutionary search behavior rather than maximizing ranking metrics alone.

Limitations of this work include the reliance on a small labeled dataset for fitness evaluation, the potential loss of fine-grained activation magnitude information due to binary encoding, and the absence of validation on very large-scale models or high-resolution datasets. Future work will explore semi- and self-supervised extensions to reduce label dependence, richer similarity measures to capture more nuanced activation structure, and integration with scalable NAS paradigms and large-model search scenarios.

Rui Han, Moises Gomez Solis

Abstract
. Motivated by Open Problem 17 in Damanik-Fillman’s manuscript [ 2 ] , we study the decay rate of limit-periodic approximation such that ballistic transport persists in the limit-periodic setting. We show an exponential decay rate, roughly ‖ 𝒥 − 𝒥 q n ‖ ∼ e − η ​ q n \|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|\sim e^{-\eta q_{n}} , is sufficient. This improves on earlier results of Fillman [ 5 ] .



1.
Introduction

We consider limit-periodic Jacobi matrices and their connection to quantum dynamics. When we say
Jacobi matrix
we mean a bounded self-adjoint operator,
\mathscr{J}=\mathscr{J}(\{a_{n}\},\{b_{n}\})
, defined by

(1.1)

(\mathscr{J}\phi)_{n}=a_{n-1}\phi_{n-1}+b_{n}\phi_{n}+a_{n}\phi_{n+1},\quad\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\quad\text{ acting on }\phi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}),

with
a\equiv\{a_{n}\}_{n=-\infty}^{\infty},b\equiv\{b_{n}\}_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R})
, and
\inf_{n}a_{n}>0
.
We would like to understand the asymptotic behavior of solutions of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation,

(1.2)

i\partial_{t}\Psi=\mathscr{J}\Psi,

with initial condition
\Psi(0)=\phi_{0}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. As it is well-known in the literature
[
12
,
14
]
, we can write solutions to (
1.2
) as

\Psi(t)=e^{-it\mathscr{J}}\phi_{0},\quad\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

These solutions have been thoroughly studied in a myriad of perspectives and there is a wealth of (classic) literature in both mathematics and physics discussing them, see
[
15
,
9
,
12
]
, and the references there in. In this paper we study how fast the initial state
\phi_{0}
spreads in space as time evolves.

We denote the Heisenberg evolution of the position operator relative to our Jacobi matrix
\mathscr{J}
by,

(1.3)

\displaystyle X_{\mathscr{J}}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}e^{it\mathscr{J}}Xe^{-it\mathscr{J}},\quad\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Here
X
is the position operator, which is defined by,

(1.4)

\displaystyle(X\phi)_{n}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}n\phi_{n},\quad\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}

(1.5)

\displaystyle\phi\in\mathfrak{D}(X)

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\left\{\phi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\colon\|X\phi\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\right\}\subseteq\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}).

Heuristically, we want to see whether an initial state, after some time
t
, will obey a rough linear growth pattern. In terms of operators, this would mean that for our evolved position operator, we would expect
\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}}(t)
to tend to a nonzero operator asymptotically. More formally, we say that the dynamics associated to our Jacobi matrix show
ballistic transport
(in the strong sense), if there exists a bounded self-adjoint operator
Q_{\mathscr{J}}
such that
\operatorname{Ker}(Q_{\mathscr{J}})\bigcap\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})=\{0\}
and

(1.6)

\text{s-}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}}=Q_{\mathscr{J}}.

For the case of
1
-dimensional Jacobi matrices, there are results relating Ballistic transport to the fact that Jacobi matrices have
purely absolutely continuous
(p.a.c.) spectrum
[
3
,
1
]
.

It is known that if there exists
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that the Jacobi coefficients satisfy
a_{n+q}=a_{n}
and
b_{n+q}=b_{q}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the dynamics are ballistic (in the strong sense). When the elements of a Jacobi matrix satisfy this
q
-periodicity, we simply say that the matrix
\mathscr{J}
is
q
-periodic.
Since periodic Jacobi matrices have all the properties we could expect to ensure the presence of ballistic transport it becomes natural to ask under what conditions, approximations by periodic Jacobi matrices, can maintain these dynamical properties.

Throughout the paper we make the same assumption of
R
-boundedness on
\mathscr{J}
, as in
[
5
]
. That is, there exists
R>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1/R\leq a_{n}\leq R
and
|b_{n}|\leq R
. We denote the family of
R
-bounded Jacobi matrices by
\mathcal{J}(R)
.

Definition 1.1
.

We say a Jacobi matrix,
\mathscr{J}
, is limit-periodic if there exists a sequence
\{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
consisting of
q_{n}
-periodic matrices such that

(1.7)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|=0.

In
[
4
]
Damanik-Lukiç-Yessen proved ballistic transport for periodic block-valued Jacobi matrices. Later Fillman
[
5
]
showed it is possible to establish ballistic transport for limit-periodic operators if one imposes some conditions on the rate of convergence.
More precisely, Fillman showed that for
\mathscr{J}\in\mathcal{J}(R)
, there exists
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(R)>0
such that if there exists a family of
q_{n}
-periodic Jacobi matrices
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}
satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\eta_{0}q_{n+1}}\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|=0,

then
\mathscr{J}
exhibits ballistic transport.
We also mention that the same type of result was established by Young
[
16
]
in the continuum case.
In Open Problem 17 (see, appendix E) in
[
2
]
, Damanik and Fillman asked what is the optimal decay rate of
\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|
such that ballistic transport holds for
\mathscr{J}
.
Theorem
1.1
below makes progress on this open problem by relaxing the convergence rate to roughly
e^{-\eta q_{n}}
, see definition below.

Definition 1.2
.

Given
\eta>0
we say a Jacobi matrix, say
\mathscr{J}
, is limit-periodic of exponential class
\eta
. That is,
\mathscr{J}\in\text{EC}(\eta)
, if there are integers
\{q_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, with
q_{n}|q_{n+1}
,
q_{n}\neq q_{n+1}
, such that
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}
is
q_{n}
-periodic and

(1.8)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n+1}^{3}e^{\eta q_{n}}\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|=0.

Before we state our main theorem, let us introduce the notions of quantum transport exponents.
For
p>0
and
\phi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we define

(1.9)

|X|^{p}_{\phi}(t)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(|n|^{p}+1)|\langle\delta_{n},e^{-it\mathscr{J}}\phi\rangle|^{2}.

We also define the discrete Schwartz space as

(1.10)

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z})\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\left\{\phi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\left|\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n|^{p}|\phi_{n}|<\infty\quad\text{for all }p>0\right.\right\}.

As we are primarily interested in the growth of
|X|^{p}_{\phi}(t)
, for
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
, in polynomial time we consider the (quantum) dynamical exponents

(1.11)

\beta_{\phi}^{+}(p)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\limsup_{t\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|X|^{p}_{\phi}(t)}{p\log t},\quad\quad\beta_{\phi}^{-}(p)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\liminf_{t\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|X|^{p}_{\phi}(t)}{p\log t}

Our main theorem being as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
R>0
, there is a constant
\eta=\eta(R)
such that if
\mathscr{J}\in\mathcal{J}(R)
and further
\mathscr{J}\in\text{EC}(\eta)
, namely

(1.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n+1}^{3}e^{\eta q_{n}}\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|=0,

then the dynamics is ballistic in the sense that there exists a self-adjoint operator
Q_{\mathscr{J}}
such that

(1.13)

\lim_{t\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}}=Q_{\mathscr{J}},

with
\operatorname{Ker}(Q_{\mathscr{J}})\bigcap\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})={0}
. In particular,
\beta_{\phi}^{+}(p)=1
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
and all
p>0
.

In fact, the result holds for any
\eta>7c_{1}
, where
c_{1}=c_{1}(R)
is as in Lemma
2.3
.
From here onward we are going to be assuming that
\eta>7c_{1}
.

Remark 1.2
.

In the literature, the dynamics with
\beta^{+}(p)=1
, for any
p>0
, are also called quasiballistic. Quasiballistic transports have also been studied in the quasi-periodic setting in the regime of purely absolutely continuous spectrum
[
8
,
17
,
18
]
, or with Liouville frequencies
[
11
,
7
]
. We briefly comment on some connections to Liouville frequencies in Sec.
6
.

Remark 1.3
.

One might ask if a lower bound
\inf a_{n}\geq 1/R>0
is necessary. If for some
n
,
a_{n}=0
in the periodic case with period
q
, then using
a_{n}=a_{n+q}=0
, one can construct finite volume eigenfunction
\phi
in the box
[n+1,n+q]
, for which the dynamics
e^{-it\mathscr{J}}\phi
does not propagate as time evolves, so ballistic transport does not hold.

Structure of the paper

In Section 2 we go over some of the properties of Jacobi matrices belonging to
\text{EC}(\eta)
, for
\eta>0
. In particular we introduce as lemmas some known results estimating the size of the bad set
\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}
. In Section 3 we show how to obtain an improved estimate for
\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}
. In Section 4 we introduce estimates for the required rate of convergence, and prove the main Theorem
1.1
in Section 5. Some connections to the quasi-periodic setting is discussed briefly in Section 6.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Limit-periodic setup

For notational convenience we introduce the following

(2.14)

\mathcal{J}_{q_{n}}(R)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\left\{\mathscr{J}\in\mathcal{J}(R)\colon\mathscr{J}\text{ is }q_{n}\text{-periodic}\right\}.

Throughout this paper we require
\mathscr{J}\in\mathcal{J}(R)\cap EC(\eta)
. It is clear that for
n
large enough,
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\in\mathcal{J}_{q_{n}}(2R)
. We can rescale
R
, and for simplicity just assume
\mathscr{J}\in\mathcal{J}(R)
and
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\in\mathcal{J}_{q_{n}}(R)
for
n
large enough.

Proposition 2.1
.

A Jacobi matrix
\mathscr{J}
can be decomposed in the form:

(2.15)

\mathscr{J}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{k},\quad H_{k}=\begin{cases}\mathscr{J}_{q_{1}},&k=1\\
\mathscr{J}_{q_{k}}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{k-1}},&k\geq 2\end{cases},\quad H_{k}\in\mathcal{P}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z});q_{k}).

Here
\mathcal{P}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z});q_{k})
is defined to be the space of
q_{k}
-periodic operators in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
; These operators, for
k
large enough satisfy
\|H_{k}\|\leq e^{-\eta q_{k-1}}/q_{k}^{3}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{J}\in\text{EC}(\eta)
, by definition there exists a sequence of integers
\{q_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, with
q_{n}|q_{n+1}
,
q_{n}\neq q_{n+1}
, such that

(2.16)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n+1}^{3}e^{\eta q_{n}}\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|=0,\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, for
n
large enough, we have
\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|<ce^{-\eta q_{n}}/q_{n+1}^{3}
for any small
c>0
. As such, we can define

(2.17)

H_{k}=\begin{cases}\mathscr{J}_{q_{1}},&k=1\\
\mathscr{J}_{q_{k}}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{k-1}},&k\geq 2\end{cases}.

To see that
H_{k}
is
q_{k}
-periodic, notice that
\mathscr{J}_{q_{k}}
is by definition
q_{k}
-periodic and
\mathscr{J}_{q_{k-1}}
can be taken as
q_{k}
-periodic since
q_{k-1}|q_{k}
. Furthermore,

(2.18)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}H_{k}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=2}^{N}(\mathscr{J}_{q_{k}}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{k-1}})+\mathscr{J}_{q_{1}}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{J}_{q_{N}}=\mathscr{J},

and for large enough
k

(2.19)

\|H_{k}\|\leq\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{k}}\|+\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{k-1}}\|\leq\frac{ce^{-\eta q_{k}}}{q_{k+1}^{3}}+\frac{ce^{-\eta q_{k-1}}}{q_{k}^{3}}\leq\frac{e^{-\eta q_{k-1}}}{q_{k}^{3}},

as claimed.
∎

2.2.
Bad sets
\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}

In order to prove ballistic transport, we proceed similarly as in
[
4
,
5
]
, that is, we want to determine an upper bound for the measure of “bad” sets
\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}
, where

(2.20)

\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}\colon\,\exists\,0\leq j<k\leq q_{n}-1\text{ s.t. }|\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

Here the
\lambda_{q_{n},i}(\theta)
’s are the eigenvalues of a given
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)
, where

(2.21)

\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&a_{1}&&&a_{q_{n}}e^{i2\pi\theta}\\
a_{1}&b_{2}&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&b_{q_{n-1}}&a_{q_{n-1}}\\
a_{q_{n}}e^{-i2\pi\theta}&&&a_{q_{n-1}}&b_{q_{n}}\\
\end{pmatrix}

is the operator with pseudo-periodic boundary condition parametrized by
\theta
. From
Floquet theory
we know that the spectrum of
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\in\mathcal{J}_{q_{n}}(R)
is such that

(2.22)

\sigma\left(\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\right)=\bigcup_{\theta\in\mathbb{T}}\sigma\left(\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)\right),\qquad\mathbb{T}\simeq[0,1)

Theorem 2.2
.

[
13
, Theorem 5.3.4]

Let
\{\lambda_{{}_{q_{n}},k}(\theta)\}_{k=0}^{q_{n}-1}
be the eigenvalues of
{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}
, then

(1)

\lambda_{{}_{q_{n}},k}(\theta)
is analytic for
\theta\in(0,1/2)\cup(1/2,1)
.

(2)

\lambda_{{q_{n}},k}(\theta)
is strictly monotone in
(0,1/2)
and
(1/2,1)
.

2.3.
Last’s result

Clearly the measure of bad sets can be controlled via certain lower bounds on the slopes of eigenvalues.
In this section we elaborate on some necessary estimations on the slopes, obtained by Last
[
10
]
in the setting of discrete Schrödinger operators, which are going to be cornerstone for later discussion on both, the proof of the perturbation step and proving ballistic transport. We present the result in the form of lemmas as to make the whole more self-contained.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
R>0
there is a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(R)>0
, such that, for any
\mathscr{J}\in\mathcal{J}_{q_{n}}(R)
, with associated
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)
, one has.

(2.23)

|\dot{\lambda}_{q_{n},j}(\theta)|\geq e^{-c_{1}q_{n}}|\sin 2\pi\theta|,\quad\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq q_{n}\text{ and }\quad\forall\theta\in\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

Let
R>0
and

(2.24)

\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\left(\theta\right)=\begin{pmatrix}b1&a_{1}&0&&\cdots&e^{i2\pi\theta}a_{q_{n}}\\
a_{1}&b_{2}&a_{2}&&&\\
0&a_{2}&b_{3}&\ddots&&\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&\ddots&b_{q_{n}-1}&a_{q_{n}-1}\\
e^{i2\pi\theta}a_{q_{n}}&&&&a_{q_{n}-1}&b_{q_{n}}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

we see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{det}(\lambda-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta))

\displaystyle=(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{q_{n}})\left(\Delta(\lambda)-2\cos{2\pi\theta}\right),

here
\Delta(\lambda)
denotes the “discriminant” (see chapter 5 in
[
13
]
) or the trace of the transfer matrix induced by
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)
, and is a polynomial of degree
q_{n}
. Since the eigenvalues,
\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)
, of
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)
solve

\Delta(\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta))=2\cos{2\pi\theta},

we have

\dot{\Delta}(\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta))\cdot\dot{\lambda}_{q_{n},j}(\theta)=-4\pi\sin{2\pi\theta}.

Notice that
\Delta^{\prime}(\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta))
is a polynomial of degree
q_{n}-1
whose zeros separate those of
\Delta(\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta))
, or equivalently, the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{J}(\theta)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\left|\dot{\lambda}_{q_{n},j}(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{2\sin{2\pi\theta}}{\dot{\Delta}(\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta))}\right|

\displaystyle\geq e^{-c_{1}q_{n}}|\sin{2\pi\theta}|,

where
c_{1}>0
depends on the
a_{j}
’s, and hence on
R
.
∎

As a corollary of Last’s estimates, Fillman obtained the following upper bound on
\mathrm{mes}(B_{q_{n},\varepsilon})
in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
\varepsilon>0
,

(2.25)

\operatorname{mes}\left({\mathcal{B}}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}\right)\leq 4e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Assume that
\theta\in\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}
, then there exists
0\leq j<k\leq q_{n}-1
such that

(2.26)

\displaystyle\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)

Now we use the general properties of 1D eigenvalues in Theorem
2.2
that the spectral bands do not overlap, meaning for
j<k
,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\max_{\theta}\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)\leq\min_{\theta}\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta).

Combining this with (
2.26
) implies

(2.28)

\displaystyle\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\min_{\theta}\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta).

Assume without loss of generality
\min_{\theta}\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)=\lambda_{q_{n},k}(0)
(the case when minimum is attained at
1/2
is completely analogous).
By Lemma
2.3
,

(2.29)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geq\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\theta}\dot{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(s)\text{d}s

(2.30)

\displaystyle\geq 4\int_{0}^{\theta}e^{-c_{1}q_{n}}\|s\|_{\mathbb{T}}\text{d}s

(2.31)

\displaystyle\geq 2e^{-c_{1}q_{n}}\theta^{2}.

This implies
|\theta|\leq e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}
. Hence
\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}
must be contained in neighborhoods of
0
and
1/2
with total measure
<4e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. This completes the proof.
∎

This estimate holds for any
\varepsilon>0
and it holds for any
q_{n}
-periodic
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\in\mathcal{J}_{q_{n}}(R)
.
In the next section, we show an improved bound for
\mathrm{mes}(\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon})
for
\varepsilon
not too small, by using the fact that
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}
is a small perturbation of a
q_{n-1}
-periodic matrix
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n-1}}
, due to Proposition
2.1
that
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}=\mathscr{J}_{q_{n-1}}+H_{n}
with
\|H_{n}\|\leq e^{-\eta q_{n-1}}/q_{n}^{3}
.
We present this improvement in the next section.

3.
An improved estimate for
\mathrm{mes}(B_{q_{n},\varepsilon})

As a preparation we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{J}\in\text{EC}(\eta)\cap\mathcal{J}(R)
, and
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\in\mathcal{J}_{q_{n}}(R)
be its
q_{n}
-periodic approximant. Denote by
\tilde{\mathscr{J}}_{q_{n}}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\mathscr{J}_{q_{n-1}}
, that is, the
q_{n-1}
-periodic Jacobi matrix artificially seen as
q_{n}
-periodic Jacobi matrix. Let
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)
and
\tilde{\mathscr{J}}_{q_{n}}(\theta)
be the Floquet matrices of
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}
and
\tilde{\mathscr{J}}_{q_{n}}
, respectively. Denote by
\sigma(\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta))=\{\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\}_{k=0}^{q_{n}-1}
and
\sigma(\tilde{\mathscr{J}}_{q_{n}}(\theta))=\{\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\}_{k=0}^{q_{n}-1}
, the sets of ordered eigenvalues. In other words, we have

(3.32)

\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k+1}(\theta),\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\leq\lambda_{q_{n},k+1}(\theta)\qquad 0\leq k\leq q_{n}-1.

Then

(3.33)

|\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)|\leq e^{-\eta q_{n-1}}/q_{n}^{3},\quad\text{ for each }0\leq k\leq q_{n}-1.

Proof.

Since for
n
large enough we have
\|\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}-\tilde{\mathscr{J}}_{q_{n}}\|=\|\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n-1}}\|=\|H_{q_{n}}\|\leq e^{-\eta q_{n-1}}/q_{n}^{3}
, by proposition
2.1
. The same estimate works for the Floquet matrices. In other words,
\|\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)-\tilde{\mathscr{J}}_{q_{n}}(\theta)\|\leq e^{-\eta q_{n-1}}/q_{n}^{3}
uniformly in
\theta
.
Thus the result follows readily from the minimax principle (see
[
9
]
.)
∎

We now proceed to estimate the upper-bound of (
2.20
). Moreover, from now to the rest of the manuscript we introduce for convenience the following:
\gamma_{n}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}e^{-\eta q_{n-1}}/q_{n}^{3}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0\leq\gamma_{n}<\varepsilon
. Then,

(3.34)

\operatorname{mes}\left(\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}\right)\leq q_{n}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Consider

(3.35)

\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}=\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}\colon\,\exists\,0\leq j<k\leq q_{n}-1\text{ s.t. }|\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

By proposition
3.1
we must have

\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}\subseteq\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}\colon\,\exists\,0\leq j<k\leq q_{n}-1\text{ s.t. }|\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},j}(\theta)-\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)|\leq\varepsilon+2\gamma_{n}\right\}=\colon\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{q_{n},\varepsilon+2\gamma_{n}}.

Since we have by hypothesis
\gamma_{n}<\varepsilon
, it follows that
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{q_{n},\varepsilon+2\gamma_{n}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{q_{n},3\varepsilon}
. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
6
]
) that the two sets of eigenvalues
\{\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\}
and
\{\lambda_{q_{n-1},k}(\theta)\}
are related in the sense

(3.36)

\bigcup_{k=0}^{q_{n}-1}\left\{\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\right\}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{q_{n-1}-1}\bigcup_{l=0}^{l_{n-1}-1}\left\{\lambda_{q_{n-1},k}\left(\frac{\theta+l}{l_{n-1}}\right)\right\},\qquad\text{where }l_{n-1}=\frac{q_{n}}{q_{n-1}}.

Recall that Last’s result (
2.23
) implies

(3.37)

\left|{\dot{\lambda}}_{q_{n-1},k}(\theta)\right|\geq e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}|\sin{2\pi\theta}|,

and hence if
\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)=\lambda_{q_{n-1},\tilde{k}}(\frac{\theta+\ell}{\ell_{n-1}})
, then

\displaystyle\left|{\dot{\tilde{\lambda}}}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{l_{n-1}}\left|\dot{{\lambda}}_{q_{n-1},\tilde{k}}\left(\frac{\theta+l}{l_{n-1}}\right)\right|\geq\frac{1}{l_{n-1}}e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}\left|\sin{2\pi\left(\frac{\theta+l}{l_{n-1}}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{l_{n-1}}e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}\inf_{l}\left\|\left(\frac{2\theta+2l}{l_{n-1}}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{l_{n-1}}e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}\inf_{r\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{l}\left|\left(\frac{2\theta+2l+rl_{n-1}}{l_{n-1}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{l_{n-1}^{2}}e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}\inf_{r\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{l}\left|\left(2\theta+2l+rl_{n-1}\right)\right|

(3.38)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{l_{n-1}^{2}}e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}\left\|2\theta\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}.

Here
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{T}}=\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\mathbb{Z})
and the absolute constant
c>0
is such that
|\sin\pi\phi|\geq c\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{T}}
.

Henceforth, if
\theta\in\tilde{B}_{q_{n},3\varepsilon}
, then we have for some
j<k
,

(3.39)

|\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},j}(\theta)-\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)|\leq 3\varepsilon.

Then similar to the proof of
2.4
, we have

(3.40)

\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},j}(\theta)+3\varepsilon\leq\max_{\theta}\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},j}(\theta)+3\varepsilon\leq\min_{\theta}\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)+3\varepsilon.

Assume without loss of generality
\min_{\theta}\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)=\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(0)
.
Then, by (
3
),

(3.41)

\displaystyle 3\varepsilon\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)-\tilde{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\theta}\dot{\tilde{\lambda}}_{q_{n},k}(s)\text{d}s

(3.42)

\displaystyle\geq\int^{\theta}_{0}\frac{c}{l_{n-1}^{2}}e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}\|2s\|_{\mathbb{T}}\text{d}s

(3.43)

\displaystyle=\int^{\theta}_{0}\frac{c}{l_{n-1}^{2}}e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}2s\text{d}s\qquad(\theta\text{ is small})

(3.44)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{l_{n-1}^{2}}e^{-c_{1}q_{n-1}}\theta^{2},

implying
|\theta|\leq C\ell_{n-1}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.
Therefore
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{q_{n},3\varepsilon}
must be contained within neighborhoods, of size
\sim l_{n-1}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, around
\{0,\frac{1}{2}\}
. Thus,

(3.45)

\operatorname{mes}(\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{q_{n},3\varepsilon})\leq Cl_{n-1}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leq C\frac{q_{n}}{q_{n-1}}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leq q_{n}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon},

as claimed.
∎

Having obtained this estimate, we now proceed in the same fashion as in
[
5
]
and show that ballistic transport for this larger class of limit-periodic Jacobi matrices.

4.
Proof of ballistic transport

Let
\{\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\}_{k=0}^{q_{n}-1}
the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)
, and by
P_{k}(\theta)
the projection onto corresponding eigenspace:
\operatorname{Ker}(\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)\mathbb{I})
. Following
[
4
]
, identify

(4.46)

Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)=q_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{q_{n}-1}\dot{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)P_{k}(\theta),\qquad\theta\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\left\{0,\frac{1}{2}\right\};

Here we can think of
Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)
as the operator acting on a copy of
\mathbb{C}^{q_{n}}
for each
\theta\in\mathbb{T}\simeq[0,1)
. Let

(4.47)

Q_{q_{n}}=\mathcal{F}_{q_{n}}^{-1}\,\left(\int_{0}^{1}Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)\text{d}\theta\right)\,\mathcal{F}_{q_{n}}

We also introduce
A_{q_{n}}(\theta)=(\mathcal{F}_{q_{n}}A\mathcal{F}_{q_{n}}^{-1})(\theta)
, where
A\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}i[\mathscr{J},X]
,
\mathcal{F}_{q_{n}}
is the
q_{n}
-periodic Floquet transform and
X
the position operator. For more details, see Appendix
B
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
R>0
, let
c_{1}(R)>0
be the constant in Lemma
2.3
.
Let
\mathscr{J}\in EC(\eta)\cap\mathcal{J}(R)
with
\eta>7c_{1}(R)
. Then for
n
large,

(4.48)

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}\text{d}s\right\|^{2}\leq\int^{1}_{0}\left\|Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}A_{q_{n}}(\theta)e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}\text{d}s\right\|_{HS}^{2}\text{d}\theta

(4.49)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}+64R^{2}e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon},\text{ for any }\varepsilon\geq 0,\text{ (Estimate I)},\\
\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}+16R^{2}q_{n}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon},\text{ for any }\gamma_{n}<\varepsilon,\text{ (Estimate II)}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Denote the eigenvector of
\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)
associated to eigenvalue
\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)
by
\phi_{k}(\theta)
.
Consider the matrix elements (see Appendix
A
),

\left\langle\phi_{j}(\theta),\left(Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}A_{q_{n}}(\theta)e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}\text{d}s\right)\phi_{k}(\theta)\right\rangle,\quad 1\leq j,k\leq q_{n},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{0,\pi\}.

In the case when
j=k
we have

(4.50)

\left\langle\phi_{k}(\theta),\left(Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}A_{q_{n}}(\theta)e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}\text{d}s\right)\phi_{k}(\theta)\right\rangle=0,\quad\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

To see this, first observe that

(4.51)

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{k}(\theta),\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}A_{q_{n}}(\theta)e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}\phi_{k}(\theta)\right\rangle=\langle\phi_{k}(\theta),A_{q_{n}}(\theta)\phi_{k}(\theta)\rangle.

Then by
[
4
, Equation (10)]
,
P_{k}(\theta)A_{q_{n}}(\theta)P_{k}(\theta)=q_{n}\dot{\lambda}_{q_{n},k}(\theta)P_{k}(\theta)
, hence the claim.

For the case when
j\neq k
and for all
\theta\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{0,\frac{1}{2}\}
, we have

(4.52)

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{j}(\theta),Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)\phi_{k}(\theta)\right\rangle=0,

and

(4.53)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\phi_{j}(\theta),e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}A_{q_{n}}(\theta)e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}\phi_{k}(\theta)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\right|

(4.54)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is(\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\langle\phi_{j}(\theta),A_{q_{n}}(\theta)\phi_{k}(\theta)\rangle\right|

(4.55)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{2R}{t|\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)|},

in which we bounded
|\langle\phi_{j}(\theta),A_{q_{n}}(\theta)\phi_{k}(\theta)|\leq 2R
, see (
2.117
).
Therefore,

(4.56)

\left|\left\langle\phi_{j}(\theta),\left(Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}A_{q_{n}}(\theta)e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}\text{d}s\right)\phi_{k}(\theta)\right\rangle\right|\leq\frac{2R}{t|\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)|}.

We want to partition
\mathbb{T}
into bad and good sets, where by “bad” we mean sets such that the denominator in (
4.56
) is smaller than some prescribed
\varepsilon>0
, and thus make the estimate useless in (
4.56
). Recall our bad set
\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}
was defined in (
2.20
). We denote the complement as
\mathcal{G}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\mathbb{T}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}
. Next, we proceed by cases, controlling for the size of
\varepsilon>0
.
For simplicity, we denote

(4.57)

\displaystyle Q_{q_{n}}(\theta)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}A_{q_{n}}(\theta)e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}(\theta)}\text{d}s\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}Y(\theta)

Note by the vanishing diagonal and off-diagonal estimates in (
4.56
), we have

(4.58)

\displaystyle\|Y(\theta)\|_{HS}^{2}\leq\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t_{n}^{2}\min\limits_{j\neq k}\left\{|\lambda_{q_{n},j}(\theta)-\lambda_{q_{n},k}(\theta)|^{2}\right\}}

Estimate I
:
Using Lemma
2.4
,

(4.59)

\operatorname{Leb}(\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon})\leq 4e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Thus,

(4.60)

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n}}_{0}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}\text{d}s\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathcal{G}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}}\left\|Y(\theta)\right\|_{HS}^{2}\text{d}\theta+\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}}\left\|Y(\theta)\right\|_{HS}^{2}\text{d}\theta

(4.61)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t_{n}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}+16R^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Leb}(\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon})

(4.62)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}_{n}\varepsilon^{2}}+64R^{2}e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon},

here the integral on the bad sets follows from the measure estimate of
\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon}
in (
4.59
) and a trivial bound on
\|Y(\theta)\|_{HS}
, see
[
5
, Equation 2.13]
.

Estimate II
:
For
0\leq\gamma_{n}<\varepsilon
, we use Theorem
3.2
and observe,

(4.63)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Leb}(\mathcal{B}_{q_{n},\varepsilon})\leq q_{n}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Then similar computations yield

(4.64)

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n}}_{0}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}\text{d}s\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}_{n}\varepsilon^{2}}+16R^{2}q_{n}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon},

where the estimate for the bad set measure is provided by (
4.63
).
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})\cap\mathfrak{D}(X)
, then for
\varepsilon>\gamma_{n}
,

(4.65)

\left\|Q_{q_{n}}\varphi-\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t)\varphi\right\|\leq\frac{1}{t}\|X\varphi\|+\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{1}}\left(\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}+16R^{2}q_{n}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/2},

where
X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}e^{it\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}Xe^{-it\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}
is the Heisenberg evolution.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathfrak{D}(X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t))=\mathfrak{D}(X)\subseteq\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and

(4.66)

X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t)=X+\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}\text{d}s,

here we used that
A_{q_{n}}=i[\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}},X]
.
Therefore

(4.67)

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{q_{n}}\varphi-\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t)\varphi\right\|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\|X\varphi\|+\left\|\left(Q_{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\varphi\right\|

(4.68)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\|X\varphi\|+\left\|Q_{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|\cdot\|\varphi\|_{1}.

Then second term above can be bounded via Estimate II in Theorem
4.1
since we required
\varepsilon>\gamma_{n}
.
∎

5.
Ballistic dynamics: proof of Theorem
1.1

5.1.
Strong convergence of
Q_{q_{n}}

For the existence of such
Q_{\mathscr{J}}
it is enough to show that this holds for every
\phi\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
satisfying

(5.69)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|Q_{q_{n}}\phi-Q_{q_{n+1}}\phi\|<\infty.

By Estimate I of Theorem
4.1
applied to
Q_{q_{n}}
and
t=t_{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
(values to be determined below), we have

(5.70)

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n}}_{0}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}\text{d}s\right\|\leq\left(\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t_{n}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}+64R^{2}e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/2}

By Estimate II of Theorem
4.1
applied to
Q_{q_{n+1}}
,
t=t_{n}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
(value to be determined below), we have

(5.71)

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{q_{n+1}}-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n}}_{0}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n+1}}}A_{q_{n+1}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n+1}}}\text{d}s\right\|\leq\left(\frac{4R^{2}q_{n+1}^{2}}{t_{n}^{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}}+16R^{2}q_{n+1}e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/2}.

Here, in order to apply Estimate II, we have to ensure that
\gamma_{n+1}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}
. This condition will be checked below in (
5.77
).
We make the natural choice of

(5.72)

t_{n}^{2}=q_{n+1}e^{-c_{1}{q_{n}}/2}\tilde{\varepsilon}^{-5/2}

so that

(5.73)

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{q_{n+1}}-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n}}_{0}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n+1}}}A_{q_{n+1}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n+1}}}\text{d}s\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(q_{n+1}e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\tilde{\varepsilon}}+16R^{2}q_{n+1}e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}\sqrt{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/2}

(5.74)

\displaystyle\leq 5R\sqrt{q_{n+1}}e^{c_{1}q_{n}/4}\tilde{\varepsilon}^{1/4}.

We also make the choices that

(5.75)

\varepsilon=q_{n}^{-2}e^{-q_{n}\eta_{0}},\text{ and }\tilde{\varepsilon}=q_{n+1}^{-2}e^{-q_{n}\eta_{0}},

with

(5.76)

\displaystyle 5c_{1}<\eta_{0}<\frac{4\eta-3c_{1}}{5}<\eta.

Recall that
\eta>7c_{1}
, so the set of
\eta_{0}
is non-empty.
This choice of
\eta_{0}
also ensures that

(5.77)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n+1}=q_{n+1}^{-3}e^{-q_{n}\eta}<q_{n+1}^{-2}e^{-q_{n}\eta_{0}}=\tilde{\varepsilon}.

With these choices in place we observe
t_{n}^{2}=q_{n+1}^{6}e^{(\frac{5}{2}\eta_{0}-\frac{c_{1}}{2})q_{n}}
, and hence (
5.70
) leads to:

\displaystyle\left\|\left(Q_{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n}}_{0}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}\text{d}s\right)\phi\right\|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}\left(\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{6}}{q_{n+1}^{6}e^{(\frac{5}{2}\eta_{0}-\frac{c_{1}}{2})q_{n}}e^{-2\eta_{0}q_{n}}}+64R^{2}q_{n}^{-1}e^{c_{1}q_{n}/2}e^{-\eta_{0}q_{n}/2}\right)^{1/2}

(5.78)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq 3R\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}e^{\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}-\eta_{0})q_{n}},

and (
5.73
) leads to:

(5.79)

\displaystyle\left\|\left(Q_{q_{n+1}}-\frac{1}{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n}}_{0}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n+1}}}A_{q_{n+1}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n+1}}}\text{d}s\right)\phi\right\|\leq 5R\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}e^{\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}-\eta_{0})q_{n}}.

Moreover, by
[
5
, Theorem A.1]
, we have

(5.80)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{n}}\left\|\int^{t_{n}}_{0}e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}A_{q_{n}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}-e^{is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n+1}}}A_{q_{n+1}}e^{-is\mathscr{J}_{q_{n+1}}}\text{d}s\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2(R+1)t_{n}\|\mathscr{J}_{n}-\mathscr{J}_{n+1}\|

(5.81)

\displaystyle\leq 2(R+1)q_{n+1}^{3}e^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1})q_{n}}\gamma_{n+1}

(5.82)

\displaystyle\leq 2(R+1)e^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1}-4\eta)q_{n}}.

Therefore, combining the estimate above with (
5.1
) and (
5.79
), we have

(5.83)

\displaystyle\|(Q_{q_{n}}-Q_{q_{n+1}})\phi\|\leq(8Re^{\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}-\eta_{0})q_{n}}+2(R+1)e^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1}-4\eta)q_{n}})\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}.

This is clearly summable since
\eta_{0}>c_{1}
and
4\eta>5\eta_{0}-c_{1}
, see (
5.76
). Note
Q_{q_{n}}
is also uniformly bounded,
\|Q_{q_{n}}\|\leq 2R
, hence
Q_{q_{n}}
converges strongly to a bounded self-adjoint operator
Q_{\mathscr{J}}
.

5.2.
\bf{Q_{\mathscr{J}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}X_{\mathscr{J}}(t)}

To show
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}X_{\mathscr{J}}(t)
equals
Q_{\mathscr{J}}
, let us fix
\phi\in\mathfrak{D}(X)\subset\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then, for a large
t>0
, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
and

t_{n-1}=q_{n}^{3}e^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1})q_{n-1}},\,t_{n}=q_{n+1}^{3}e^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1})q_{n}}.

We estimate

(5.84)

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}}(t)-Q_{\mathscr{J}}\right)\phi\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\left\|\left(X_{\mathscr{J}}(t)-X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t)\right)\phi\right\|+\left\|\left(\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t)-Q_{q_{n}}\right)\phi\right\|+\left\|\left(Q_{q_{n}}-Q_{\mathscr{J}}\right)\phi\right\|.

We have shown that
\|(Q_{q_{n}}-Q_{\mathscr{J}})\varphi\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
(as
t\to\infty
). Next we estimate the other two terms separately.
By
[
5
, Theorem A.1]
,

(5.85)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\left\|(X_{\mathscr{J}}(t)-X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t))\phi\right\|\leq 2Rt\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|\cdot\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle 2Rt_{n}q_{n+1}^{-3}e^{-\eta q_{n}}\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}

(5.86)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2Re^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1}-4\eta)q_{n}}\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}\to 0,

since
4\eta>5\eta_{0}-c_{1}
.
Also, by Corollary
4.2
, for
\varepsilon_{0}>\kappa_{n}
, we have

(5.87)

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t)-Q_{q_{n}}\right)\phi\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t_{n-1}}\|X\phi\|+\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}\left(\frac{4R^{2}q_{n}^{2}}{t_{n-1}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}+16R^{2}q_{n}e^{c_{1}q_{n-1}/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)^{1/2}.

Picking
\varepsilon_{0}=q_{n}^{-2}e^{-\eta_{0}q_{n-1}}>q_{n}^{-3}e^{-\eta q_{n-1}}=\gamma_{n}
(since
\eta>\eta_{0}
) yields

(5.88)

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\frac{1}{t}X_{\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}}(t)-Q_{q_{n}}\right)\phi\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t_{n-1}}\|X\phi\|+5Re^{\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}-\eta_{0})q_{n-1}}\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}\to 0,

since
\eta_{0}>c_{1}
.
Therefore
\lim_{t\in\infty}t^{-1}X_{\mathscr{J}}(t)
converges to
Q_{\mathscr{J}}
strongly.

5.3.
Nontrivial kernel

We are only left to show that
Q\phi\neq 0
for every
0\neq\phi\in\mathfrak{D}(X)\cap\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
. For such a
\phi
, we normalize it so that
\|\phi\|_{\ell^{2}}=1
. We have the lower bound
\|Q_{q_{n}}\phi\|\geq e^{-c_{1}q_{n}}
by
[
5
, Equation (3.6)]
.
Also for
n
large enough, by (
5.83
), and that
\eta_{0}>c_{1}
,
4\eta>5\eta_{0}-c_{1}
,

(5.89)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\|(Q_{q_{k}}-Q_{q_{k+1}})\phi\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}(8Re^{\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}-\eta_{0})q_{k}}+2(R+1)e^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1}-4\eta)q_{k}})\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}

(5.90)

\displaystyle\leq(8Re^{\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}-\eta_{0})q_{n}}+2(R+1)e^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1}-4\eta)q_{n}})\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}.

As such,

(5.91)

\displaystyle\|Q\phi\|\geq

\displaystyle\|Q_{n}\phi\|-\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\|(Q_{q_{k}}-Q_{q_{k+1}})\phi\|

(5.92)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{-c_{1}q_{n}}-(8Re^{\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}-\eta_{0})q_{n}}+2(R+1)e^{\frac{1}{4}(5\eta_{0}-c_{1}-4\eta)q_{n}})\|\phi\|_{\ell^{1}}>0,

for
n
large enough, since by (
5.76
) we have
\eta_{0}>5c_{1}
and
4\eta>5\eta_{0}+3c_{1}
. ∎

6.
A brief comparison to the quasi-periodic setting

The limit-periodic operator
\mathscr{J}
satisfying the condition
\mathscr{J}\in EC(\eta)
is close in spirit to, but easier than, quasi-periodic operators with Liouville frequencies, which are defined as

(6.93)

\displaystyle(H_{\omega,\theta}\phi)_{n}=\phi_{n-1}+v(\theta+n\omega)\phi_{n}+\phi_{n+1}.

Here we assume
v
to be Lipschitz on
\mathbb{T}
and
\omega
is a Liouville frequency, meaning there exists a sequence of continued fraction approximants
\{p_{n}/q_{n}\}
such that

(6.94)

\displaystyle\|q_{n}\omega\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq e^{-\beta q_{n}},

for some
\beta>0
.
This means
H_{\omega,\theta}
is exponentially close to a
q_{n}
-periodic operator locally, when restricted to a large box of size
e^{\beta q_{n}/2}
:

(6.95)

\displaystyle\left\|(H_{\omega,\theta}-H_{p_{n}/q_{n},\theta})|_{[-e^{\beta q_{n}/2},e^{\beta q_{n}/2}]}\right\|\lesssim e^{-\beta q_{n}/2}.

In fact this follows from that

(6.96)

\displaystyle\left\|(H_{\omega,\theta}-H_{p_{n}/q_{n},\theta})|_{[-e^{\beta q_{n}/2},e^{\beta q_{n}/2}]}\right\|\leq

\displaystyle\max_{|j|\leq e^{\beta q_{n}/2}}|v(\theta+j\omega)-v(\theta+jp_{n}/q_{n})|

(6.97)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{|j|\leq e^{\beta q_{n}/2}}\mathrm{Lip}(v)\cdot\|j(\omega-p_{n}/q_{n})\|_{\mathbb{T}}

(6.98)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{|j|\leq e^{\beta q_{n}/2}}\frac{|j|}{q_{n}}\,\|q_{n}\omega\|_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot\mathrm{Lip}(v)

(6.99)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\beta q_{n}/2}\mathrm{Lip}(v).

This is, roughly speaking, the notion of
\beta
-almost periodic in Jitomirskaya-Zhang
[
7
]
.

In the limit-periodic setting, our condition
\mathscr{J}\in EC(\eta)
requires
\mathscr{J}
to be exponential close to a
q_{n}
-periodic operator globally:

(6.100)

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{J}-\mathscr{J}_{q_{n}}\|\leq q_{n+1}^{3}e^{-\eta q_{n}}.

Breast surgery and systemic treatment continuation for patients with de novo metastatic breast cancer and locoregional oligoprogression: a cohort study☆
Authors: C Valenza, N Bianco, M Milano, D Trapani, S Di Bella, A Sciandivasci, I Minchella, C Sangalli, V Galimberti, M Intra, P Veronesi, E Munzone, M Colleoni, G Curigliano
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe role of breast surgery in patients with de novo metastatic breast cancer (mBC) and locoregional oligoprogression, either in the breast or locoregional lymph nodes, who previously had a response to systemic treatment is unknown. This approach may allow maintenance of the same systemic treatment and prolong the time to treatment failure.Patients and methodsWe conducted a cohort study including consecutive patients with de novo mBC, after a locoregional oligoprogression, who underwent breast surgery and continued the same systemic treatment at the European Institute of Oncology. The primary endpoint was postsurgery progression-free survival (pS-PFS).ResultsFifty-nine patients were included: 28 (47%) had hormone receptor (HR)-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative disease, 30 (51%) HER2-positive, and 1 (2%) triple-negative. The median pre-oligoprogression PFS was 15.2 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 12.0-18.4 months]. After a median follow-up of 54.1 months (95% CI 44.2-64.0 months), the median pS-PFSs was 15.0 months (95% CI 5.1-24.9 months), and was significantly longer in patients with HER2-positive disease or with a pre-oligoprogression PFS ≥12 months.ConclusionsThis study suggests that selected patients with de novo mBC and locoregional oligoprogression can benefit from breast surgery while maintaining the same systemic treatment, particularly in the setting of HER2-positive disease or a pre-oligoprogression PFS >1 year.Key words: oligoprogressive breast cancer, breast surgery

Introduction

De novo metastatic breast cancer (mBC), defined as metastatic disease at initial diagnosis, represents ∼6% of new breast cancer cases in the United States each year, and up to 10% overall.1 In recent years, the proportion of patients presenting with de novo human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive mBC has risen substantially, reaching as high as 50% in some series.2

Randomized clinical trials, most conducted before the introduction of contemporary therapies that have extended overall survival, have shown no survival benefit from upfront breast surgery in de novo mBC.3 Consequently, these patients are typically managed with sequential systemic therapy, with surgery reserved to the management of local complications, usually fungating tumors, and symptomatic masses.4

In some patients, while systemic therapy maintains control of metastatic disease, the primary tumor or locoregional lymph nodes may progress driven by resistant cellular subclones.5 This pattern of locoregional oligoprogression is encompassed within the definition of oligoprogression proposed in the joint consensus of the European Society for Radiotherapy and Oncology (ESTRO) and the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC).6

Several studies have shown that, in selected patients with oligoprogressive mBC, local treatment of oligoprogressive sites while continuing the same systemic therapy can prolong the time to treatment failure.7, 8, 9 In this context, breast surgery may be an option for patients with de novo disease and locoregional oligoprogression, particularly when systemic therapy is well tolerated, such as endocrine therapy or maintenance with trastuzumab and pertuzumab.4

This study aimed to evaluate the clinical outcomes of patients with de novo mBC and locoregional oligoprogression who underwent breast surgery while continuing the same systemic therapy.

Patients and methods

We conducted a prospective-retrospective, single-center cohort study on a prospectively maintained database at the European Institute of Oncology (IEO) IRCCS, Milan, Italy.

We collected data from consecutive patients aged ≥18 years old, with a histologically confirmed diagnosis of de novo mBC, who experienced a locoregional oligoprogression, underwent breast surgery at IEO from January 2007 to March 2025, and continued the same systemic treatment after surgery until subsequent progression or unacceptable toxicity.

All cases were discussed at the weekly mBC multidisciplinary tumor board including breast medical oncologists, breast surgeons, radiation oncologists, radiologists, and interventional radiologists. All patients underwent whole-body imaging during the systemic treatment every 12 ± 2 weeks, as per IEO internal guidelines, either with computed tomography (CT) scan, positron emission tomography (PET), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), whole-body MRI, or a combination of them. Physical examinations of the breast and locoregional lymph nodes were conducted at least every other month to monitor locoregional disease. Types and extent of surgery was decided in this context and carried out by breast-dedicated oncology surgeons.

Locoregional oligoprogression was defined as radiologic progression per RECIST 1.1 in the breast and/or locoregional lymph nodes, with at least one other metastatic site maintaining the disease control.

The primary endpoint was postsurgery progression-free survival (pS-PFS), defined as the time from the first radiographic evidence of locoregional oligoprogression to the subsequent progression or death, whichever occurred first.

All information was obtained through access to medical records. The study was approved by the IEO institutional review board (project code: UID 2709) and was conducted in accordance with the principles stated in the Declaration of Helsinki and with the principles of good clinical practice.

Descriptive statistics were used to analyze patients’ characteristics. Clinical and biological variables were grouped in standard categories whenever reasonable. Continuous variables were expressed as the median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are expressed as numbers, proportions (%), and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs). pS-PFS was calculated by Kaplan–Meyer method. Analyses were conducted using SPSS (version 28.0.1.0) and Stata (version 18.0).

At the time of locoregional oligoprogression, the median line of systemic therapy was 1 (IQR 1-2), the objective response rate to ongoing treatment was 64%, and the median pre-oligoprogression PFS was 15.2 months (95% CI 12.0-18.4 months). Among patients with HR-positive/HER2-negative disease, 22 of 28 (79%) were receiving endocrine therapy; among those with HER2-positive disease, 19 of 30 (63%) were receiving first-line maintenance with trastuzumab and pertuzumab after taxane-based chemotherapy (CLEOPATRA regimen).

Locoregional oligoprogression occurred in the breast in 50 patients (85%), in the axillary lymph nodes in 3 (5%), and in both sites in 6 (10%).

The median interval between oligoprogression and surgery was 1.5 months (95% CI 1.3-1.7 months) and no distant progression was reported during this interval. Overall, 12 (20%) patients underwent lumpectomy, 45 (75%) mastectomy, 5 (9%) sentinel lymph node biopsy, and 27 (46%) lymph node dissection. No serious post-operative adverse events were reported. Among the 57 patients who underwent lumpectomy or mastectomy, all had negative margins (pR0) and the pathology report showed ypT1 disease in 8 (14%) patients, ypT2 in 31 (54%), and ypT3-4 in 18 (32%) (according to AJCC TNM 8th edition). Subtype switches were observed in four patients: two from triple-positive to HR-positive/HER2-negative, and two from HR-positive/HER2-negative to triple-negative disease.

Only two patients received consolidation post-operative radiotherapy to the chest wall. All patients continued the same systemic treatment after surgery. The two patients whose tumors lost HER2 expression were not receiving anti-HER2 therapy at the time of locoregional oligoprogression due to prior toxicity; instead, the two patients whose tumors lost HR expression were on chemotherapy.

After a median follow-up of 54.1 months (95% CI 44.2-64.0 months), 38 patients experienced a subsequent progression (all at distant sites), with a median pS-PFSs of 15.0 months (95% CI 5.1-24.9 months) (Figure 1).

Discussion

In this study, we reported that patients with de novo mBC and locoregional oligoprogression appeared to benefit from breast surgery with continuation of systemic therapy. This strategy delayed subsequent disease progression by a median of 15.0 months (95% CI 5.1-24.9 months). This benefit is clinically meaningful, as the lower boundary of the 95% CI exceeded the typical 3- to 4-month interval between CT scans in patients with mBC receiving sequential systemic therapy.4

To our knowledge, this is the first cohort study to evaluate breast surgery in this setting. Other metastasis-directed approaches, such as stereotactic radiotherapy for bone metastases or thermal ablation for liver metastases, have likewise been shown to significantly delay progression and treatment failure in selected patients with oligoprogressive mBC.7, 8, 9

Surgery proved to be safe, with no serious post-operative complications. Most patients underwent mastectomy, reflecting the size of oligoprogressive breast lesions, which measured ≥2 cm in 84% of cases. Although nodal oligoprogression was observed in only 15% of patients, 55% underwent axillary surgery to remove residual disease under control.

Patients with HER2-positive disease seemed to derive a greater benefit from this approach than those with HR-positive/HER2-negative disease (median pS-PFS 24.2 months versus 9.0 months), despite having a shorter median pre-oligoprogression PFS (10.7 months versus 15.2 months). In this setting, resistance to trastuzumab–pertuzumab maintenance appeared to originate primarily from the breast tumor, suggesting spatial heterogeneity of HER2 expression.10

A pre-oligoprogression PFS of ≥12 months was associated with longer pS-PFS and may serve as a clinical marker to guide treatment decisions, as it reflects the in vivo activity of systemic therapy. This observation is consistent with our prior report on patients with oligoprogressive bone metastases treated with stereotactic radiotherapy.7

We acknowledge that the observed benefit reflects careful patient selection by the multidisciplinary tumor board: only one patient had triple-negative disease, most were receiving first-line therapy after a prolonged PFS before oligoprogression, and nearly all presented with a single oligoprogressive lesion. These clinical features partially overlap with the recently proposed prognostic staging system for patients with de novo mBC, developed by Plichta and colleagues, which incorporates anatomic stage (T), tumor histology and subtype, grade, and metastatic site to predict overall survival.1

Limitations of this analysis include its retrospective observational design, although consecutive patients were drawn from a prospectively maintained database; the small sample size, which may limit the robustness of the findings; the heterogeneity of systemic therapies, which may have influenced patient outcomes and limits the comparability across subgroups; the lack of overall survival data; the lack of translational analyses; and the use of different imaging modalities with variable sensitivity and specificity, although consistent within individual patients. The study also lacked a comparison group of patients who either switched systemic therapy or continued it beyond progression without breast surgery.

In conclusion, this study suggests that selected patients with de novo mBC and locoregional oligoprogression may benefit from breast surgery while continuing the same systemic treatment, particularly in the setting of HER2-positive disease or a pre-oligoprogression PFS >1 year. In such patients, this multimodal strategy challenges the traditional paradigm of sequential systemic therapies with local treatment reserved for palliation purpose. Decisions regarding local therapy should, however, be individualized within a multidisciplinary tumor board, taking into account disease features, patient characteristics, and prognostic criteria.

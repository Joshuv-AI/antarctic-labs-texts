Abstract.
The purpose of this work is an attempt to expand the results obtained by A. K. Lakmon and Y. Mensah on embeddings of quantum Sobolev spaces
\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}(G,H)
consisting of Hilbert-Schmidt operators, with
p\neq 2

Key words.
Quantum Sobolev spaces, Schatten–von–Neumann classes, quantum Fourier transform, projective representations

1
Introduction

In contrast to conventional harmonic analysis, in quantum harmonic analysis, the paradigm fundamentally shifts: functions are replaced by operators, typically elements of Schatten–von Neumann classes. The seminal work of Werner
[
9
]
introduced the quantum Fourier transform associated to a projective representation of an locally compact abelian (LCA) groups , yielding a Fourier-type correspondence between operator ideals (trace class, Hilbert–Schmidt, more generally
S_{p}
classes) and function spaces (
C_{0}
,
L^{2}
,
L^{q}
respectively). These foundational ideas were systematized and extended in subsequent work, notably by Führ
[
2
]
for the representation-theoretic aspects and more recently by Fulsche and Galke
[
3
]
for the harmonic analysis on LCA groups. The work of Lakmon and Mensah
[
7
]
specifically studies quantum Sobolev spaces whose elements are Hilbert-Schmidt operators, obtaining continuous embedding theorems and exploring applications to operator-valued partial differential equations.

Given this operator-valued Fourier analysis framework, quantum Sobolev spaces arise naturally by imposing weighted integrability conditions on the operator Fourier transform. For the case
p=2
, these coincide with weighted
L^{2}
conditions on the Fourier transform, analogous to classical Bessel potential spaces.

The applications of quantum Sobolev spaces span several critical areas. Operator-valued PDEs like quantum Poisson-type equations, generalized bosonic string equations
[
5
]
, and other operator-valued partial differential equations admit natural formulations where the unknown is an operator and the solution space is a quantum Sobolev space
[
8
,
7
]
. Quantum Sobolev spaces provide the functional-analytic foundation for quantization procedures on compact groups and hypergroups
[
1
]
, facilitating the study of decay and regularity properties of operators under Fourier transformation. They extend the well-established correspondence between
L^{p}
-spaces and Schatten
S_{p}
(the operator version of Hausdorff-Young inequalities) to a complete Sobolev scale, enabling interpolation theory, embedding theorems, and spectral analysis in the operator setting.

Recent work by Bataka, Egwe, and Mensah
[
1
]
has extended Sobolev spaces to the framework of hypergroup Gelfand pairs, constructing these spaces via Fourier transforms on hypergroups and establishing embedding theorems in this more general setting.

The purpose of this work is an attempt to expand the results obtained by A. K. Lakmon and Y. Mensah
[
7
]
on embeddings of quantum Sobolev spaces
\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}(G,H)
consisting of Hilbert-Schmidt operators, for
p\neq 2
, into Schatten-classes
S_{p}
. Our proof technique largely repeats the technique applied to classical Sobolev spaces. For background on classical Sobolev theory on groups we refer to
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, and for the operator-theoretic aspects to
[
9
,
2
,
3
]
.

Preliminaries

Let
H
be a separable complex Hilbert space. For a compact operator
T
on
H

(s_{n}(T))_{n\geq 1}
its singular values (nonincreasing). For
0<p<\infty

S_{p}(H)
by

S_{p}(H)=\bigg\{T\in K(H):\,\|T\|_{S_{p}}:=\bigg(\sum_{n}s_{n}(T)^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}<\infty\bigg\}

Important special cases:
S_{1}(H)
(trace class),
S_{2}(H)
(Hilbert–Schmidt),
S_{\infty}(H)=B(H)
(bounded operators). For
1\leq p\leq\infty
the space

is a Banach space; for

it is a Hilbert space with inner product
\langle W,T\rangle_{S_{2}}=\mathrm{tr}(T^{*}W)
. Schatten classes satisfy Hölder-type inequalities: if
1/p+1/q=1/r
(with

), then
\|AB\|_{S_{r}}\leq\|A\|_{S_{p}}\|B\|_{S_{q}}
for appropriate

.

Let

is also LCA. A projective unitary representation

with multiplier

\widehat{G}
.

Main Results

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<p<2
.
\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}(G,H)
is a Banach space. The mapping

\Phi:T\mapsto(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)
establishes a topological isomorphism between
\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}(G,H)
and its image in
L^{q}(\hat{G})
. The image of
\Phi
is a closed subspace of
L^{q}(\hat{G})
, and
\Phi
is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Proof.

Step 1.

Let
(T_{n})
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}(G,H)
. We have

\|T_{n}-T_{m}\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}}=\big\|(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\big(\mathcal{F}_{U}(T_{n})-\mathcal{F}_{U}(T_{m})\big)\big\|_{L^{q}(\widehat{G})}

Thus the condition that
(T_{n})
is Cauchy in
\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}
is

\forall\,\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\ \forall n,m\geq N:\ \big\|(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\big(\mathcal{F}_{U}(T_{n})-\mathcal{F}_{U}(T_{m})\big)\big\|_{L^{q}(\widehat{G})}<\varepsilon

In other words, the sequence

\big((1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T_{n})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{q}(\widehat{G})
. Since
L^{q}(\widehat{G})
is complete, there exists

g\in L^{q}(\widehat{G})\quad\text{such that}\quad(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T_{n})\to g\ \text{ in }L^{q}(\widehat{G})

Step 2:
\Phi
is continuous.
This follows immediately from the definition:
\|\Phi(T)\|_{L^{q}}=\|T\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}}
.

Step 3:
\Phi
is injective.
If
\Phi(T)=0
, then
(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)=0
a.e. Since
\gamma(\xi)>0
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)=0
a.e. By injectivity of
\mathcal{F}_{U}
on
S_{p}(H)
(from Hausdorff-Young), we get
T=0
.

Step 4: Image of
\Phi
is closed in
L^{q}(\widehat{G})
.

Let
(T_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}(G,H)
such that
\Phi(T_{n})
converges in
L^{q}(\hat{G})
to some
g
. Since
(T_{n})
is bounded in the reflexive Banach space
S_{p}(H)
, by the Banach–Alaoglu theorem there exists a subsequence
(T_{n_{k}})
weakly converging to some
T\in S_{p}(H)
. The continuity of
\Phi
implies that
\Phi(T_{n_{k}})
converges weakly to
\Phi(T)
in
L^{q}(\hat{G})
. As the subsequence
\Phi(T_{n_{k}})
also converges strongly to
g
, the weak limit must coincide with the strong limit, hence
\Phi(T)=g
. Therefore,
g
lies in the image of
\Phi
, proving that the image is closed in
L^{q}(\hat{G})
.

Since
||T_{n}||_{\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}}=||f_{n}||_{L^{q}}
is bounded, and
S_{p}(H)
is reflexive for
1<p<\infty
, there exists a weakly convergent subsequence
(T_{n_{k}})\rightarrow T\text{ in }S_{p}(H)
.

Step 5:
\Phi^{-1}
is continuous on
\text{Im}(\Phi)
.
By the Open Mapping Theorem, since
\Phi:\mathfrak{H}_{\gamma}^{s,p}(G,H)\to\text{Im}(\Phi)
is a continuous bijection between Banach spaces, it is a homeomorphism.

1.

Let

, or

(C2)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
is the inner product on
H
. Since
u,v
were arbitrary,
this forces
T=0
. The same density argument applies if one assumes
\operatorname{tr}(T\,W^{*})=0
for all
T\in S_{p}(H)
and wishes to conclude
W=0
.

Step 4.

By Step 2 we have a continuous linear map

\iota:S_{p}(H)\to(\mathfrak{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{\gamma})^{*},\;\iota(T)=L_{T}.

Define
\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}
to be the closure of
\iota(S_{p}(H))
in
(\mathfrak{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{\gamma})^{*}

with the operator norm. Since
(\mathfrak{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{\gamma})^{*}
is Banach, its closed subspace

\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}
is Banach as well. The estimate in Step 2 gives the continuous
injection
S_{p}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}
(via
\iota
), and every element of

\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}
is represented by a (unique) continuous functional on
\mathfrak{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{\gamma}

which, by Riesz-style identification through the trace pairing, corresponds to a (unique)
Schatten operator on
H
. Thus
\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}
canonically embeds into
S_{p}(H)
and the
claimed norm inequality holds.
∎

In analogy with the classical situation, we distinguish between the

inhomogeneous
quantum Sobolev space

\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}(G,H)=\Bigl\{T\in S_{p}(H):(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{s/2}\,\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\in L^{q}(\widehat{G})\Bigr\}

with norm

\|T\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}}=\|(1+\gamma^{2})^{s/2}\,\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\|_{L^{q}}

and the
homogeneous
quantum Sobolev space

\dot{\mathfrak{H}}^{s,p}_{\gamma}(G,H)=\Bigl\{T\in S_{p}(H):\gamma(\cdot)^{s}\,\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\in L^{q}(\widehat{G})\Bigr\}

with norm

\|T\|_{\dot{\mathfrak{H}}^{s,p}_{\gamma}}=\|\gamma^{s}\,\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\|_{L^{q}}

Let
1<p\leq 2
,
s>0
, and set
q=\dfrac{p}{p-1}>2
. Fix
\alpha>0
and assume one of the following
two verifiable integrability hypotheses holds:

1.

(Inhomogeneous case)

(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{-s/2}\in L^{\alpha}(\widehat{G})
.

2.

(Homogeneous case)

\gamma(\cdot)^{-s}\in L^{\alpha}(\widehat{G})
.

Define

\sigma:=\frac{\alpha q}{\alpha+q}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta:=\frac{\alpha q}{\alpha(q-1)-s}.

Assume moreover that
1<\sigma\leq 2
. Then we have

Proposition 3.7
.

(i)

If (1) is satisfied and
T\in\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}(G,H)
, then
\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\in L^{\sigma}(\widehat{G})
and
T\in S_{\beta}(H),\qquad\|T\|_{S_{\beta}}\leq C\,\|T\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}}
for a constant
C
depending only on
\alpha,q,s,\gamma
and the Hausdorff–Young constant
C_{HY}
.

(ii)

If (2) is satisfied and
T\in\dot{\mathfrak{H}}^{s,p}_{\gamma(G,H)}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\in L^{\sigma}(\widehat{G})
and
T\in S_{\beta}(H),

\|T\|_{S_{\beta}}\leq C\,\|T\|_{\dot{\mathfrak{H}}^{s,p}_{\gamma}}

with the same type of constant
C
.

Proof.

We treat both cases by the same argument. Take
T
in the relevant Sobolev space (either
T\in\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}
or

T\in\dot{\mathfrak{H}}^{s,p}_{\gamma}
). Set the multiplier
m(\xi)
to be

m(\xi)=\begin{cases}(1+\gamma(\xi)^{2})^{-s/2}&\text{inhom. case }\\[4.0pt]
\gamma(\xi)^{-s}&\text{hom. case }\end{cases}

By the respective hypothesis we have
m\in L^{\alpha}(\widehat{G})
. Now write pointwise

\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)(\xi)=m(\xi)\cdot\bigg(m(\xi)^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)(\xi)\bigg)

where
m^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)
is exactly the Fourier–side quantity whose
L^{q}
-norm
coincides with the Sobolev norm of
T
(either
(1+\gamma^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)

or
\gamma^{s}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)
). Apply Hölder’s inequality on
\widehat{G}
with
exponents
\alpha
and
q
chosen so that

\frac{1}{\sigma}=\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{q}

By the definition
\sigma=\dfrac{\alpha q}{\alpha+q}
this identity holds.
Hence

\|\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\|_{L^{\sigma}}\leq\|m\|_{L^{\alpha}}\,\|m^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\|_{L^{q}}=C_{1}\,\|T\|_{\mathcal{H}}\quad\mathcal{H}={\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}}\text{ or }{\dot{\mathfrak{H}}^{s,p}_{\gamma}}

Since by assumption
1<\sigma\leq 2
, the operator form of the
Hausdorff–Young inequality gives a bounded map

\mathcal{F}_{U}^{-1}:L^{\sigma}(\widehat{G})\longrightarrow S_{\sigma^{\prime}}(H),\qquad\sigma^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}

Applying that to
f=\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)
yields

\|T\|_{S_{\sigma^{\prime}}}\leq C_{\mathrm{HY}}\,\|\mathcal{F}_{U}(T)\|_{L^{\sigma}}\leq C_{\mathrm{HY}}\,C_{1}\,\|T\|_{\mathcal{H}}

Finally compute the exponent
\sigma^{\prime}
. Substituting

\sigma=\dfrac{\alpha q}{\alpha+s}
gives

\sigma^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}=\frac{\alpha q}{\alpha(q-1)-s}=\beta.

Combining the displayed estimates produces
\|T\|_{S_{\beta}}\leq C\,\|T\|_{\mathcal{H}}
with
C=C_{\mathrm{HY}}C_{1}
, which is the asserted norm estimate and hence the claimed continuous embedding.
∎

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group and let
\pi
be a projective representation
satisfying the integrability (or square–integrability) assumptions and the Heisenberg
multiplier conditions used in
[
7
]
. Fix
s>0
and
p>2
, and denote
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)<2
.
Assume the weight
\gamma:\widehat{G}\to(0,\infty)
satisfies the integrability condition so that
\mathfrak{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{\gamma}(G,H)
is well defined (by Theorem
3.3
) as a Banach space. Then we have

Theorem 3.8
.

In general one cannot upgrade the embedding above to

\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}(G,H)\hookrightarrow S_{\beta}(H),\qquad\beta<p,

without further assumptions on
\gamma
or on the representation
\pi
. In fact, for natural models such as
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the Schrödinger/Heisenberg representation, there exists a bounded sequence in
\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}(G,H)
whose
S_{\beta}
-norms diverge for every
\beta<p
.

Proof.

Consider the model case
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the standard Heisenberg representation, so that
\mathcal{F}_{U}
coincides with the Weyl transform. Fix
q
associated to
p^{\prime}
, and construct functions
a_{n}
supported on sets of measure
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, with

\|a_{n}\|_{L^{q}}=1\quad\text{ but}\quad\|a_{n}\|_{L^{\rho}}\to\infty\quad\text{for all }\rho>q

Such functions can be constructed explicitly, e.g.
a_{n}=\varepsilon_{n}^{-1/q}\textbf{1}_{E_{n}}
with
|E_{n}|=\varepsilon_{n}
, so that
||a_{n}||_{L^{q}}=1
and
||a_{n}||_{L^{\rho}}=\varepsilon_{n}^{1/\rho-1/q}\mapsto\infty
for
\rho>q
. Let
T_{n}:=\mathcal{F}_{U}^{-1}(a_{n})
. Then
\|T_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}}
is uniformly bounded (
L^{q}
-norm of
a_{n}
is fixed), while for any
\beta<p
Hausdorff–Young requires
\rho>q
to control
\|T_{n}\|_{S_{\beta}}
. Hence
\|T_{n}\|_{S_{\beta}}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, showing that no continuous embedding into
S_{\beta}
is possible.
∎

The obstruction does not preclude improved embeddings under stronger
assumptions. For instance, if
(1+\gamma(\xi)^{2})^{-r/2}\in L^{1}(\widehat{G})
for
r
large enough, or if
\pi
enjoys enhanced
L^{\rho}\to S_{\rho^{\prime}}
bounds with
\rho>2
, then one can obtain continuous embeddings
\mathfrak{H}^{s,p}_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow S_{\beta}
for some
\beta<p
. Precise thresholds can be derived using the same weighted Hölder/Hausdorff–Young estimates as before.

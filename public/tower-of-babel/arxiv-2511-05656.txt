Quantum enforcement of strong cosmic censorship
Damien A. Easson

Abstract
We show that the trace anomaly of quantum field theory is sufficient to enforce strong cosmic censorship in any stationary spacetime with an inner horizon of nonzero surface gravity. In the two-dimensional dilaton reduction of four-dimensional Einstein–Maxwell theory coupled to N N conformal fields, the anomaly-induced Polyakov term yields a finite ⟨ T v ​ v ⟩ → N 48 ​ π ​ κ − 2 \langle T_{vv}\rangle\to\frac{N}{48\pi}\kappa_{-}^{2} near the Cauchy horizon, while the affine-frame energy grows as T k ​ k ∝ e 2 ​ κ − ​ v T_{kk}\propto e^{2\kappa_{-}v} . The resulting tidal blow-up converts the inner horizon into a null, Tipler–strong singularity. The mechanism is local, universal, and state-independent, and extends to Kerr/Kerr–Newman, Λ > 0 \Lambda>0 , and nonsingular interiors. Thus, the trace anomaly is the minimal quantum ingredient restoring strong cosmic censorship.



The strong cosmic censorship (SCC) conjecture
Penrose 1965
;
Cardoso et al. 2018
;
Luna et al. 2019
asserts that the maximal Cauchy development of generic initial data in general relativity is inextendible as a regular Lorentzian manifold.
Classical Reissner–Nordström and Kerr black holes appear to violate this expectation by admitting smooth inner Cauchy horizons.
Perturbative analyses show that small disturbances are blueshifted without bound, leading to a “mass–inflation” singularity
Poisson and Israel 1990
;
Ori 1991
;
Brady and Smith 1995
;
Dafermos 2005
.
Yet the ultimate fate of the inner horizon once full semiclassical backreaction is included remains unresolved.

We show that the fate of the inner horizon follows directly from the universal structure of quantum field theory in curved spacetime.
The same trace anomaly that generates Hawking radiation inevitably destabilizes the Cauchy horizon.
A rigorous treatment by Hollands, Wald, and Zahn (HWZ) demonstrated that
\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle
diverges at the Cauchy horizon of Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter black holes using microlocal techniques in four dimensions
Hollands et al. 2020
.
Although they noted that the two–dimensional trace–anomaly argument alone was insufficient to capture the full four–dimensional behavior due to mode backscattering, we show that this very simplicity is in fact the key:
when expressed in the affine frame, the same trace anomaly fully determines the local divergence mechanism, providing a transparent geometric derivation of the HWZ result and extending it to Kerr,
\Lambda>0
, and nonsingular geometries.

—In a stationary, spherically symmetric semiclassical spacetime with nonzero inner-horizon surface gravity
\kappa_{-}>0

T_{kk}\propto e^{2\kappa_{-}v},\qquad(v\!\to\!\infty),

driving curvature blow-up and rendering the spacetime inextendible beyond the Cauchy horizon.

Hence, the trace anomaly alone drives the exponential blueshift that creates a null curvature singularity.
Vacuum quantum fluctuations, without charged matter or classical mass inflation,

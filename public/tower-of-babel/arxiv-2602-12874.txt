Quantile characterization of univariate unimodality
Markus Zobel, Axel Munk

Abstract
. Unimodal univariate distributions can be characterized as piecewise convex-concave cumulative distribution functions. In this note we transfer this shape constraint characterization to the quantile function. We show that this characterization comes with the upside that the quantile function of a unimodal distribution is always absolutely continuous and consequently unimodality is equivalent to the quasi-convexity of its Radon-Nikodym derivative, that is, the quantile density. Our analysis is based on the theory of generalized inverses of non-decreasing functions and relies on a version of the inverse function rule for non-decreasing functions.

1.1.
Characterization of unimodality

We will start with the intuitive characterization of a univariate unimodal (probability) distribution through its (Lebesgue-) density (if it exists): The density is first non-decreasing until a mode and then non-increasing (left of
Figure 1

This description matches directly with the density being quasi-concave.

Definition 1.1
.

a)

A function

A function

is quasi-concave, that is, if it satisfies for all

and

f(wx+(1-w)y)\leq\max(f(x),f(y)).

Indeed, this definition is equivalent to the qualitative description above:
A function

1.2.
Main results

In this paper we provide a new equivalent notion of cumulative distribution function-unimodality.
The result came about as a useful framing while studying properties of Wasserstein-2 barycenters, but it stands on it own in its simplicity.
The starting point for the characterizations of cumulative distribution function-unimodality we propose is the fact that the quantile function
Q_{\mu}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
, defined as

(1.2)

Q_{\mu}(t)\coloneqq\inf(\{x\in\mathbb{R}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}t\leq F_{\mu}(x)\}),\quad\forall t\in(0,1),

of a cumulative distribution function-unimodal distribution enjoys higher regularity.

Theorem 1.5
.

The quantile function
Q_{\mu}
of a cumulative distribution function-unimodal distribution is absolutely continuous.

A proof is given in
Section 4.2
.
For now, we define
absolute continuity of a non-decreasing function
, like the quantile function

Radon-Nikodym derivative

q_{\mu}:(0,1)\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
such that
Q_{\mu}(y)-Q_{\mu}(x)=\int_{x}^{y}q_{\mu}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda
for all
x,y\in(0,1)
. The Radon-Nikodym derivative

of a quantile function

is called the

of

[

]

The following result is the main result of this paper and provides a characterization of cumulative distribution function-unimodal distributions
\mu

Theorem 1.6
.

A univariate distribution
\mu
is cumulative distribution function-unimodal if and only if

a)

there exists a quantile density
q_{\mu}
of
\mu
which is quasi-convex, or

b)

there exists a
quantile mode

\alpha\in[0,1]
such that the quantile function

is concave on
(0,\alpha]
and convex on
[\alpha,1)
.

A proof is given in

.
Intuitively,

is equivalent to

as the inverse of a non-decreasing convex function is non-decreasing and concave, and vice versa.
Our proof of
Theorem 1.6
will not use this approach directly but instead focuses on the relation of the quantile density
q_{\mu}
and the density
f_{abs}
of the absolutely continuous dens-unimodal part
\mu_{abs}
of a general cumulative distribution function-unimodal distribution
\mu
. The two are related by a generalized inverse function rule for Radon-Nikodym derivatives

(1.3)

f_{abs}=\frac{1}{q_{\mu}\circ F_{\mu}}\quad\text{$\lambda$-{almost everywhere} on $\support{\mu}$,}

that is, there is a null set
N\in\B(\mathbb{R})
such that
\{x\in\support{\mu}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}f_{abs}(x)\neq\frac{1}{q_{\mu}\circ F_{\mu}}(x)\}\subseteq N
and
\lambda(N)=0
. Here,
\support{\mu}
denotes the support of
\mu
, that is, the smallest closed subset

such that
\mu(\mathbb{R}\setminus A)=0
.

1.3.
Novelty

To the best of our knowledge
Theorem 1.5
and
Theorem 1.6
are new.
The characterization
b)
of
Theorem 1.6
appears in
[
1
, Section 6.1.]
for absolutely continuous
\mu
with a subtle difference. They only require the quantile function to be concave and convex on the open intervals
(0,\alpha),(\alpha,1)
respectively. This condition is only necessary but not sufficient for cumulative distribution function-unimodality.
These requirements do not guarantee the continuity of the quantile function at the quantile mode. A discontinuity at a quantile mode
\alpha\in(0,1)
corresponds to a flat region in the cumulative distribution function. We then can decompose the cumulative distribution function into four regions such that the function is convex, concave, convex, concave. Therefore, the corresponding cumulative distribution function is cumulative distribution function-bimodal. A counter example is the (absolutely continuous) mixture of two uniform distributions. Take
\mu=\left.\kern-1.2pt\lambda\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right\rvert_{(0,\frac{1}{2})}+\left.\kern-1.2pt\lambda\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right\rvert_{(\frac{3}{2},2)}
then its quantile function
Q_{\mu}=\left.\kern-1.2pt\Id\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right\rvert_{(0,1)}+\mathds{1}_{(\frac{1}{2},1)}
is
affine
, that is, convex and concave, on both
(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
(\frac{1}{2},1)
. But, the measure
\mu
is clearly dens-bimodal (non-decreasing, non-increasing, non-decreasing, non-increasing density) and thus not cumulative distribution function-unimodal.

1.4.
Implications

The authors of
[
1
]
use their characterization to investigate the (dens-) unimodality of the Wasserstein-2 barycenter of (dens-) unimodal distribution, as the quantile function of the Wasserstein-2 barycenter of a distribution of distributions is the point-wise average of the random quantile functions.
Therefore, characterizing distributions via their quantile function is pertinent to prove the preservation of these properties when taking the Wasserstein-2 barycenter.
While this idea was also the starting point of this work, we want to emphasize the simplicity of the new characterization of cumulative distribution function-unimodality of distributions via their quantile function in

.
It encompasses the standard definition of unimodality
[
5
]
and reveals that the quantile perspective on unimodality can be used equally well as we always have enough regularity for the existence of a quantile density.

3.1.

For measures

with respect to

9
, (19.24)]

\int_{A}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho=\mu(A),\quad\forall A\in\B(I).

All Radon-Nikodym derivatives of

[
8

Recall that
\mu
has a unique

with respect to
\rho

[
9
, (19.42)]
given by the measures

and

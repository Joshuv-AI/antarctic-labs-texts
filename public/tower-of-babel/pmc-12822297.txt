Synergistic effects of multicomponent exercise and rTMS on sarcopenia and mild cognitive impairment in older people: a protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yanqing Ren, Xiangfeng He, Zhen Zhang, Min Zhuang, Gonghua Yin, Lingyan Huang, Nan Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSarcopenia and cognitive impairment (CI) are major contributors to disability in older people, significantly reducing quality of life and imposing a substantial burden on healthcare systems. Sarcopenia accelerates cognitive decline through mechanisms such as chronic inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and disruption of the muscle–brain axis. Conversely, CI impairs motor planning and daily functioning, further exacerbating muscle loss and physical deterioration. Previous studies have shown that physical exercise optimizes peripheral physiological conditions, promoting neuroplasticity in the central nervous system. Similarly, repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) enhances brain plasticity, improving both cognitive and physical functions. This study aims to evaluate the combined effects of multicomponent exercise and rTMS on sarcopenia and CI in older people.MethodsThis is a 16-week, randomized, single-blind, controlled trial. A total of 51 older people with sarcopenia and mild cognitive impairment (MCI) will be randomly assigned to one of three groups: (1) exercise combined with rTMS, (2) exercise combined with sham rTMS, and (3) a control group. The exercise intervention will integrate Baduanjin and resistance training, while rTMS will target the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC). Participants will be assessed for muscle fitness, cognitive function, and psychological well-being at baseline, week 8, and week 16.DiscussionThis trial aims to provide empirical evidence on the synergistic effects of exercise and rTMS in managing sarcopenia and MCI in older people. Additionally, it will explore the neural mechanisms underlying improvements in cognitive function. The findings may inform integrated treatment strategies for these interconnected conditions, enhancing quality of life and promoting functional independence in aging populations.Trial registrationInternational Traditional Medicine Clinical Trial Registry, ITMCTR2025000311. Introduction

Sarcopenia, an age-related geriatric syndrome, is characterized by a significant decline in muscle mass, muscle strength, and physical performance [1]. It increases the risk of falls and fractures [2] and is closely associated with various chronic conditions, including cognitive impairment [3], cardiovascular diseases [4], and depression [5]. The prevalence of sarcopenia is 5%−13% in individuals aged 60–70 years, and 11%−50% in those aged 80 and above [6]. Cognitive impairment primarily includes mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and dementia [7]. MCI is a transitional stage between age-related cognitive decline and dementia [8], with a substantially increased risk of progression to dementia [9, 10]. Studies indicate that over 50% of individuals with MCI may develop dementia within 4 to 6 years [9], and the risk of dementia in individuals with MCI is approximately 10-fold higher compared to cognitively normal peers [10]. With the aging of the global population, both two conditions have become major public health challenges [11, 12].

Emerging epidemiological evidence reveals bidirectional links between sarcopenia and CI. A cross-sectional study involving individuals aged 60 and above revealed significantly worse cognitive performance in sarcopenic individuals, particularly in orientation, calculation, memory, and visuospatial abilities, compared to their non-sarcopenic counterparts [3]. A 5.6-year longitudinal study further confirmed that baseline sarcopenia severity predicted both higher incidence of MCI and accelerated cognitive decline [13]. Conversely, CI may precede sarcopenia, as evidenced by Pacifico et al. ‘s finding of threefold higher sarcopenia prevalence in dementia patients (26.4%) versus controls (8.4%) [14]. Although the exact mechanisms linking sarcopenia and CI remain unclear, emerging studies suggest that chronic inflammation, mitochondrial oxidative stress, and alterations in the muscle-brain axis may play critical roles [15]. Specifically in Alzheimer’s disease (AD), Aβ-induced glial activation drives neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, reducing brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) circulation and promoting sarcopenia pathogenesis. Conversely, sarcopenia exacerbates systemic inflammation and impairs myokine secretion (e.g., BDNF, irisin), which further disrupts neuronal homeostasis and accelerates AD progression. This reciprocal crosstalk establishes a self-reinforcing vicious cycle between neuromuscular degeneration and cognitive decline [16]. Early intervention for both sarcopenia and CI may therefore help mitigate disability progression in aging populations.

Physical activity elevates circulating myokines—including insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) and BDNF—which enhance neurocognitive function by regulating neuronal proliferation, differentiation, synaptic plasticity, and memory consolidation [17]. Although exercise modulates peripheral neural pathways to induce muscular adaptation [18], this mechanism provides insufficient neurocognitive benefits for older people with sarcopenia and comorbid CI. Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS), a non-invasive neuromodulation technique [19], augments cortical plasticity to facilitate motor recovery [20, 21]. Crucially, by enhancing neural plasticity and neural network functionality [22, 23], rTMS synergistically potentiates exercise-induced therapeutic outcomes and improves cognitive-motor integration. However, this combined strategy has not been explored in older people with sarcopenia and cognitive impairment. Therefore, the aim of this study is to implement a combined intervention strategies integrating multicomponent exercise and rTMS to manage sarcopenia and delay the progression of CI in older people and to investigate the underlying neurophysiological mechanisms.

Methods

Study design

This study is a randomized, single-blind, clinically controlled trial conducted from March 2025 to January 2026 at community health service centers in Shanghai, China. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to one of three groups in a 1:1:1 ratio: the multicomponent exercise with active rTMS (Ex + rTMS) group, the multicomponent exercise with sham rTMS (Ex + Sham-rTMS) group, and the control group. At baseline, demographic information including age, sex, education, comorbidities, current medication use, and alcohol and smoking status will be collected. Cognitive function and sarcopenia-related outcomes will be assessed at baseline, post-intervention, and follow-up. Additionally, functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) will be used to measure brain activation and connectivity, providing insights into the mechanisms underlying cognitive improvement. During the 8-week intervention period, participants’ physical activity levels and dietary status will be monitored using the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ) [24] and the Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) [25], respectively. Sample size

Sample size estimation was performed using G*Power 3.1. Effect sizes were derived from previous meta-analyses on rTMS and exercise interventions targeting sarcopenia and cognitive impairment. Reported effect sizes included: rTMS on muscle strength (Cohen’s d = 0.66) [29], combined exercise on knee extension strength (d = 0.62) [30], and active rTMS on cognitive function (Cohen’s d = 0.48) [31]. To provide a conservative estimate, the smallest effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.48) was selected. The corresponding Cohen’s f = 0.24 was calculated using the formula. With α = 0.05 and power (1 − β) = 0.80, the repeated-measures ANOVA (within–between interaction) indicated that 39 participants in total were required. Allowing for a 20% dropout rate and maintaining a strict 1:1:1 allocation, we will recruit 51 participants in total (17 per group).

Randomization and masking

Fifty-one eligible participants will be randomly assigned to one of three groups (Ex + rTMS, Ex + Sham-rTMS, and control) in a 1:1:1 ratio. Randomization will be conducted using a stratified block randomization procedure with a fixed block size of six [32], generated by an independent statistician (not involved in the study) with SPSS version 27 (SPSS, Inc.), stratified by gender and age to ensure balance in key demographic characteristics.

Allocation concealment will be maintained using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes (SNOSE) prepared by the independent statistician. Each envelope will contain the participant’s group code and will be opened sequentially after baseline assessment by a research coordinator who is not involved in data collection or analysis [33]. The randomization and group assignment data will be securely encrypted and stored in an electronic database managed by external researchers.

Therapists will deliver interventions according to the respective group protocols. Statisticians, assessors, and data managers will be blinded to group assignments.

Masking will be removed in the event of a medical emergency requiring immediate intervention, with the approval of the responsible investigator and the knowledge of the data monitoring committee (DMC). Unblinding will only occur under these circumstances to ensure the participant’s safety, and the procedure will follow a standardized protocol. All unblinding events will be documented and reported to the ethics committee within 24 hours [34].

Recruitment

Participant recruitment will take place from March 2025 to January 2026 in selected towns within Shanghai, China. A multifaceted recruitment strategy will be implemented. First, annual health screenings will be conducted in designated communities to identify eligible residents. Educational lectures on sarcopenia and CI will be delivered to enhance public awareness and attract potential participants. Home visits will be arranged in collaboration with community staff to engage older people who do not regularly attend health screenings. Additionally, research announcements will be posted on community bulletin boards to further promote enrollment.

Screening

All potential participants will complete the Strength, Assistance with walking, Rise from a chair, Climb stairs, and Falls (SARC-F) questionnaire, along with the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE). Individuals scoring ≥ 4 on the SARC-F and < 24 on the MMSE will be further assessed for eligibility based on the study’s inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Intervention

Intervention duration

The 16-week intervention, consisting of 8 weeks of supervised training and 8 weeks of home-based follow-up, will be designed based on previous evidence regarding the temporal dynamics of neuroplastic and functional adaptations induced by exercise and rTMS. Randomized controlled trials have shown that 2–6 weeks of high-frequency rTMS (typically 10–30 sessions) can significantly improve global cognition or specific cognitive domains in individuals with MCI and Alzheimer’s disease [35, 36], with some studies reporting sustained effects at one-month follow-up [37]. These improvements are attributed to the induction of long-term potentiation (LTP)-like neuroplasticity, which requires repeated stimulation over several weeks for stable consolidation. For multicomponent exercise interventions, 8–12 weeks of training have been shown to significantly enhance muscle strength, cognitive performance, and overall physical function in older adults [30, 38].

Furthermore, combined neuromodulation–exercise protocols are expected to require sufficient duration for rTMS-induced neural priming and exercise-driven neuroplastic adaptations to interact synergistically [39]. Based on this evidence, the present study will implement an 8-week intensive supervised phase to induce peak neuroplastic responses, followed by an 8-week home-based phase to consolidate these gains and evaluate their sustainability.

Group protocols

All interventions will last 8 weeks, with three sessions per week. Each intervention consists of two components: first, participants in the Ex + rTMS group will receive 20 min of active rTMS, while the other two groups will receive sham stimulation. Then, the Ex + rTMS and Ex + Sham-rTMS groups will engage in a 45-minute Baduanjin and resistance training (BDJ-RT) program, while the control group will attend a 45-minute health education course on sarcopenia and CI.

All rTMS or sham stimulation will be administered by licensed rehabilitation therapists with at least three years of clinical experience in neurorehabilitation and formal certification in transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) operation. The BDJ-RT sessions will be led by exercise coaches certified by the Chinese Health Qigong Association and trained in geriatric exercise instruction to ensure safety and standardization.

Exercise program

All exercise procedures will be standardized and implemented according to established guidelines to ensure reproducibility. Specifically, the “Health Qigong–Baduanjin” guidelines published by the General Administration of Sport of China (2003) will be followed for the Baduanjin exercise component [42]. The progressive resistance training protocol will be designed based on the guidelines provided by the American College of Sports Medicine for resistance training in older adults [43].

The first part includes joint mobility exercises, targeting movements in various directions for the neck, lower back, shoulder joints, knee joints, and ankle joints. The second part involves slow, controlled stretching exercises targeting major muscle groups such as the quadriceps, calves, and latissimus dorsi.

rTMS

The rTMS procedure will be conducted in accordance with the 2014 International Guidelines for Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation Treatment [44]. rTMS will be delivered using a MagTD stimulator (Wuhan, China) equipped with a 90-mm figure-of-eight coil. The stimulation target will be the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), localized according to the 5 cm rule by identifying a site 5 cm anterior and 2 cm lateral to the motor hotspot (M1). Stimulation parameters will include a frequency of 10 Hz and an intensity set at 90% of the RMT, with each session comprising 2,000 pulses (40 pulses per train, 50 trains). Each session will last approximately 20 min.

During sham stimulation, the coil will be rotated 90° and positioned perpendicular to the scalp to effectively prevent magnetic field induction, while all other stimulation parameters will remain identical to those in the active condition. Participants will wear earplugs throughout the procedure to minimize auditory input, and any adverse events will be closely monitored to ensure safety and tolerability.

② 2-Back working memory task (5 minutes)

A sequence of uppercase letters will be presented on the screen (stimulus duration: 1 s; interstimulus interval: 0.5 s). Participants will be required to determine whether the current letter matches the one presented two trials earlier. If it does, they will respond via a button press; otherwise, no response will be required. The task will consist of six blocks, each comprising a 30-second task period followed by a 20-second rest period. Behavioral data, including accuracy and reaction time, will be recorded.

③ Verbal fluency task (2 minutes)

This task will include a 30-second pre-task baseline, a 60-second task period, and a 30-second post-task baseline. During baseline periods, participants will be asked to repeatedly count aloud from 1 to 5. During the task period, they will be instructed to generate as many phrases as possible containing a given Chinese character (e.g., “白 [white]”, “北 [north]”, “火 [fire]”). Character cues will be updated every 20 s to minimize silence. The number of valid words and reaction times will be recorded.

Statistical analysis

Clinical data analysis

Statistical analyses will be performed using MATLAB and SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Descriptive statistics will summarize the data as follows: normally distributed data will be expressed as mean ± SD, non-normally distributed data as median and interquartile range (IQR), and categorical data as frequencies (n) and percentages (%). Group comparisons will be conducted using appropriate statistical tests: one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for normally distributed data, the Kruskal-Wallis H-test for non-normally distributed data, and chi-square tests for categorical data. Time effects will be assessed by analyzing changes in data across baseline, post-intervention, and follow-up time points using Repeated Measures ANOVA (RM-ANOVA), following the intention-to-treat principle. Missing data will be assumed to be missing at random (MAR) and will be addressed using standard maximum likelihood estimation. To adjust for multiple comparisons, a false discovery rate (FDR) correction will be applied to the p-values, with statistical significance defined as an FDR-adjusted p-value < 0.05.

fNIRS data analysis

Preprocessing of the fNIRS signals will be conducted using MATLAB (Version R2017a, MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA) in conjunction with the open-source package Homer 2.0 [57]. Preprocessing steps will include artifact removal, signal conversion, and band-pass filtering. The processed data will be converted into hemoglobin concentration changes (hemoglobin oxygenated, HbO, and hemoglobin deoxygenated, HbR), with HbO serving as the primary measure for cortical activation across all channels. Statistical analyses will consist of the following components:

Activation AnalysisRM-ANOVA will be employed to compare HbO changes among the three groups under both resting-state and task conditions. The analyses will be conducted at three time points: baseline, post-intervention, and follow-up, to assess the main effects of time, group, and their interaction (time × group).

Adverse events monitoring and reporting

Prior to the intervention, participants will undergo fall prevention education and a comprehensive pre-exercise assessment, which will include evaluation of their cardiovascular, metabolic, and renal health history, exercise habits, and any musculoskeletal or joint pain. Additionally, the scalp of each participant will be examined to ensure the absence of trauma or dermatological conditions.

During the intervention, participants will receive continuous supervision and professional guidance to minimize the risk of injury. The intensity of rTMS stimulation will be tailored to each participant’s individual tolerance, based on the pre-assessment and ongoing monitoring throughout the intervention.

All adverse events occurring during the study will be meticulously documented and evaluated by a medical professional to determine their potential association with the intervention. Any adverse events related to the intervention will be promptly reported to the local ethics committee, and appropriate management strategies will be explored in collaboration. Participants will retain the right to withdraw from the study at any point during the intervention period.

Patient and public involvement

Public involvement in this study will begin during the recruitment phase, utilizing a variety of strategies, including annual community health check-ups, educational seminars, home visits, and public announcements via bulletin boards. At the conclusion of the follow-up period, individual study results will be provided to each participant. Although participants will not be directly involved in the study design, they will receive comprehensive information regarding the time commitments and study requirements prior to the intervention. Only participants who voluntarily agree to participate, after being fully informed, will be asked to sign an informed consent form.

Data management and monitoring

The study will utilize Microsoft Excel 365 for the recording and storage of all relevant data, including socio-demographic characteristics, outcome measurements, and adverse events. To ensure participant confidentiality, all electronic data will be encrypted, and participant names will be replaced with numerical identifiers.

All data will be validated through double-data entry (manual data entry performed by two blinded data-entry clerks) and by applying validation rules, range checks, logic checks, and duplicate record checks for all quantitative and categorical variables [58].

A Data Monitoring Committee (DMC), comprising independent clinical experts, researchers, trialists, and statisticians, will regularly monitor study progress, data quality, and participant safety. The DMC will review adverse events and protocol adherence at scheduled intervals and provide recommendations if safety or data integrity concerns arise [59].

To ensure data integrity, both paper-based and electronic data will be destroyed after a retention period of five years.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study will be the first RCT to investigate the combined effects of multicomponent exercise and rTMS in older people diagnosed with both sarcopenia and MCI. Sarcopenia and CI are leading contributors to disability in older people, significantly diminishing their quality of life and imposing a considerable burden on healthcare systems and society. This study is designed to concurrently enhance physical performance and cognitive function, thereby promoting overall health and functional independence in the aging population.

This study will have several key strengths. Firstly, it will use an integrated approach targeting the concurrent decline in both cognitive and physical functions. Sarcopenia and MCI are both prevalent degenerative conditions in older people, with mutual pathological interactions. Sarcopenia accelerates cognitive decline through mechanisms such as chronic inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and disruption of the muscle–brain axis [60, 61]. Conversely, cognitive impairment impairs motor planning and daily function, exacerbating muscle loss and physical decline [62, 63]. This bidirectional relationship can lead to a vicious cycle of functional deterioration. Although most previous studies have focused on interventions targeting these conditions separately, this study will treat sarcopenia and MCI as a unified entity, offering a novel pathway for preventing and managing geriatric syndromes.

Secondly, this study will propose a combined intervention of physical exercise and rTMS to synergistically modulate both central and peripheral systems, thereby optimizing therapeutic outcomes. Exercise facilitates neuroplasticity and remodels to enhance cognitive function [64]. In addition, it improves muscle strength, increases metabolic rate, and reduces inflammation, thereby addressing sarcopenia [30]. However, the neuroplastic effects of exercise develop gradually and are primarily mediated through indirect mechanisms [65]. In contrast, rTMS directly modulates cortical excitability, leading to significant improvements in cognitive functions such as executive function and attention [66]. Emerging evidence also suggests that rTMS may mitigate sarcopenia by reducing neuroinflammation, suppressing oxidative stress, and enhancing neuromuscular conduction [16]. Thus, exercise optimizes peripheral physiological conditions, supporting the central plasticity induced by rTMS, while rTMS further amplifies the cognitive and physical function benefits of exercise. The synergistic integration of these two interventions is anticipated to yield superior results in counteracting both cognitive and physical decline, surpassing the efficacy of single intervention [39, 67].

Thirdly, this study will employ rTMS targeting the left DLPFC, selected for its dual involvement in cognitive and motor regulation. Previous studies have demonstrated that the DLPFC participates in higher-order cognitive processes, including executive control, goal-directed behavior, and motivation [68, 69], which are key psychological mechanisms underlying physical activity. Dysfunction in this region is often associated with apathy, reduced motivation, and diminished engagement [70], all of which are recognized as major risk factors for sarcopenia in older people. Although the motor regulatory function of the DLPFC has been less extensively investigated, existing evidence suggests that it exerts top-down modulation of motor output through its functional connectivity with M1 [71, 72]. Moreover, as a key node linking the default mode, attentional, and motor networks [73], the DLPFC may facilitate the coordination and integration of cognitive control processes—such as movement attention and error correction—with motor performance. Previous studies have also reported that high-frequency rTMS applied to the DLPFC can significantly improve lower-limb motor function, gait speed, and postural stability [74, 75]. Therefore, this study will target the left DLPFC to concurrently modulate cognitive and motor functions, aiming to achieve improvements in both cognitive impairment and sarcopenia.

Fourth, this study will not only evaluate the efficacy of cognitive improvement but also investigate its underlying neural mechanisms. fNIRS will be used to dynamically monitor changes in HbO levels and functional connectivity in key cortical regions [76]. Cognitive function will be systematically assessed using standardized neuropsychological tools, including MoCA, WMS-RC, VST, and TMT-B. By integrating neurophysiological indicators with behavioral outcomes, the study aims to elucidate the neural mechanisms through which combined rTMS and exercise interventions promote cognitive improvement in aging individuals.

However, this study will also have certain limitations. Firstly, the multicomponent intervention may require a longer duration, which could affect participant adherence and motivation [77, 78], particularly among older adults with sarcopenia [1] and MCI [79], who often experience poor physical health. To improve adherence, attendance will be recorded, make-up sessions will be provided for missed classes, and professional coaches will offer continuous guidance to help participants stay on track and maintain high compliance.

Secondly, although Baduanjin involves only eight postures and is relatively simple compared to other exercises [80], it may still be challenging for older individuals with sarcopenia and MCI. Cognitive decline can impair memory and imitation abilities [81, 82], while sarcopenia is often associated with balance dysfunction [83], both of which may hinder correct performance. To ensure standardization, professional coaches with over five years of Baduanjin teaching experience will provide pre-intervention training, and offer ongoing demonstrations, supervision, and corrections throughout the intervention.

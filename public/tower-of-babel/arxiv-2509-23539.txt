Noncommutative localizations and joint spectra of a contractive quantum plane
Anar Dosi

Abstract
. In the present paper we investigate the localizations in the sense of J. L. Taylor of the Arens-Michael-Fréchet algebras occurred within the formal geometry of a contractive q q -plane. It turns out that all noncommutative Fréchet algebras obtained by the Fréchet sheaves of the formal geometry are indeed localizations. That topological homology property of the structure sheaf results in the key properties of Taylor and Putinar spectra of the Banach q q -modules.

1.
Introduction

The localizations or (weak) homological epimorphisms introduced by J. L.
Taylor
[
33
]
play one of the central roles in the multivariable
functional calculus problem for the operator tuples, and noncommutative
complex analytic geometry. They have also significant impact in computations
of the homological dimensions of topological algebras, and many other problems
of topological homology developed by J. L. Taylor
[
32
]
and A. Ya.
Helemskii
[
25
]
. The key idea of a localization is to draw the pure
algebra homology construction over a base algebra
B
to topological homology
of a polynormed algebra
A
along the given canonical algebra homomorphism

B\rightarrow A
. Since every pure algebra can be equipped with the finest
polynormed topology, the localization can be treated as a polynormed algebra
homomorphism
B\rightarrow A
, which identifies the category of polynormed

A
-modules with a full subcategory of the category of polynormed
B
-modules
so that topological homology over
A
is reduced to topological homology over

B
along the homomorphism
B\rightarrow A
. In the purely algebraic case, we
used to say that
B\rightarrow A
is a homological epimorphism
[
19
]
, or

A
is stably flat over
B

[
23
]
, which is equivalent to the condition

\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{B}\left(A,A\right)=0
for all
n>0
. The stably
flatness plays an important role in some problems of representation theory of
finite-dimensional algebras
[
20
]
, and algebraic
K
-theory
[
23
]
.

In noncommutative complex analytic geometry, the base algebra
B
stands for a
finitely generated algebra of noncommutative variables, whereas its
Fréchet algebra localizations represent the algebras of local sections of
germs of noncommutative holomorphic functions in
B
defined on the
noncommutative space
\mathfrak{X}
(the spectrum) of
B
. The spectrum

\mathfrak{X}
of
B
is given by means of its irreducible Banach space
representations
[
24
, 6.2]
equipped with a special (used to be
non-Hausdorff) topology depending on
B
. The localizations of
B
are in turn
glued to a noncommutative structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
over
\mathfrak{X}
,
and the ringed space
\left(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}\right)
stands for noncommutative complex analytic geometry of
B
. The related
noncommutative
\mathcal{O}
-functional calculus problem for the Banach space
representations of
B
is in turn associated with noncommutative
B
-spectral
theory and the related topics.

In the case of the polynomial base algebra
B=\mathbb{C}\left[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right]
, the canonical inclusions
B\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
into the Fréchet algebras of holomorphic functions on
domains
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
of holomorphy are localizations. We come up
with the standard complex analytic geometry and the related Taylor’s
holomorphic functional calculus associated with the joint spectral theory of
several commuting operators
[
17
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
33
]
.

The localizations of the universal enveloping (base) algebra
B=\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
of a finite dimensional Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
were gradually investigated in the last 20 years mainly by O.
Yu. Aristov, A. Yu. Pirkovskii and the author in a series of papers. The first
target was the canonical map of
\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)

into its Arens-Michael envelope
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}}
that represents
the algebra of all noncommutative entire functions in variables generating

\mathfrak{g}
. Certainly
\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a localization when
\mathfrak{g}

is abelian Lie algebra, and that is not the case if
\mathfrak{g}
is a
semisimple Lie algebra
[
33
]
. In
[
26
]
, A. Yu. Pirkovskii proved
that the solvability of
\mathfrak{g}
is a necessary condition for

\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}}
to be a localization. The case of a nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

was investigated in
[
6
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
9
]
mainly
motivated by implementation of Taylor‘s general framework in the
noncommutative case
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
13
]
. The detailed
analysis of the localization problem
\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}}
was considered in
[
27
]
, and
completed in
[
1
]
by O. Yu. Aristov, where it was shown that

\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a localization for every nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The case
of a non-nilpotent but solvable Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
was recently solved
in
[
3
]
. Thus
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is stably flat over

\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
is a
solvable Lie algebra. The localization problem for the noncommutative sheaf of
formally-radical functions in elements of a nilpotent Lie algebra was solved
in
[
12
]
.

In the present paper we investigate the localization problem for the algebras
occurred in the formal geometry of a contractive
q
-plane (see
[
16
]
). The quantum plane (or just
q
-plane) is the free associative algebra

\mathfrak{A}_{q}=\mathbb{C}\left\langle x,y\right\rangle/\left(xy-q^{-1}yx\right),\quad q\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0,1\right\}

generated by
x
and
y
modulo
xy=q^{-1}yx
(see
[
22
]
,
[
18
]
). If

\left|q\right|\neq 1
, then we come up with a contractive quantum
plane. If
x
and
y
are invertible additionally, then the algebra represents
the quantum
2
-torus
[
29
]
. That is our base algebra
B
of
noncommutative variables, whose Arens-Michael envelope is denoted by

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
. The key property of a
contractive
q
-plane that was observed in
[
15
]
is that the algebra

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
turns out to be commutative
modulo its Jacobson radical
\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
. It means that the spectrum
\mathfrak{X}
of

\mathfrak{A}_{q}
consists of the continuous characters of
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
, that is, all irreducible Banach

\mathfrak{A}_{q}
-modules are just the trivial
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
-modules

\mathbb{C}\left(\gamma\right)
given by the continuous characters

\gamma
(see
[
24
, 6.2.1]
) of
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
. Thus
\mathfrak{X}=\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)\right)
, and in this case, we have

\mathfrak{X}=\mathbb{C}_{xy}:=\mathbb{C}_{x}\cup\mathbb{C}_{y}\quad\text{with\quad}\mathbb{C}_{x}=\mathbb{C\times}\left\{0\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2},\quad\mathbb{C}_{y}=\left\{0\right\}\times\mathbb{C\subseteq C}^{2}.

The space
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
equipped with the
q
-topology (see below
Subsection
2.4
) stands for the noncommutative analytic space, whose
structure sheaf would consists of noncommutative Fréchet algebras
extending the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
. The
Arens-Michael envelope is extended to a presheaf in
[
15
]
providing
noncommutative analytic geometry of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
. But in the formal
level we have a complete geometric configuration
[
16
]
. The formal
geometry of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
is based on the PI envelope (see
[
2
]
)

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
, which
stays larger than
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
but it has
the same spectrum
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
, that is,
\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\right)=\mathbb{C}_{xy}
.
That PI envelope is extended up to a Fréchet algebra sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{q}
on
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
, and the couple
\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy},\mathcal{F}_{q}\right)
stands for the noncommutative formal geometry of

\mathfrak{A}_{q}

[
16
]
. Thus the formal geometry
\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy},\mathcal{F}_{q}\right)
of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
turns out to
be more attractive from point of view localizations, and certainly related
noncommutative
\mathcal{F}_{q}
-functional calculus problem.

The structure sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{q}
is obtained as the fibered product

\mathcal{F}_{q}=\mathcal{O}_{x}\left[\left[y\right]\right]\underset{\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]}{\times}\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}_{y}

of the formal, Fréchet
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
-algebra sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{x}\left[\left[y\right]\right]
and

\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}_{y}
over the constant sheaf

\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]
, where
\mathcal{O}_{x}
and

\mathcal{O}_{y}
are copies (on
\mathbb{C}_{x}
and
\mathbb{C}_{y}
,
respectively) of the standard sheaf
\mathcal{O}
of stalks of holomorphic
functions on the complex plane. The multiplications in all these sheaves are
naturally extended by one of the original algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
(or

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
). The connecting morphisms

\mathcal{O}_{x}\left[\left[y\right]\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]
and
\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}_{y}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]

defining the fibered product are evaluations maps of stalks at the
intersection point of two irreducible subsets
\mathbb{C}_{x}
and

\mathbb{C}_{y}
of
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
. Notice that the related commutative
analytic space
\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy},\mathcal{O}\right)
(see

[
17
, 4]
) consists of the topological space
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
and the
standard (commutative) sheaf

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{x}\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\times}\mathcal{O}_{y}

of stalks of holomorphic functions. The main result of
[
16
]
assets
that
\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy},\mathcal{F}_{q}\right)
can be driven from the
commutative analytic space
\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy},\mathcal{O}\right)
as a
quantization such that
\mathcal{F}_{q}/\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{F}_{q}=\mathcal{O}
. Namely, one needs to generate the shifted copies

\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
of the sheaf

\mathcal{O}
, which are the coherent
\mathcal{O}
-modules (or just
Fréchet space (not algebra) sheaves) on
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
. If

U_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{x}
is a
q
-open subset then the Fréchet space

\mathcal{O}_{x}\left(d\right)\left(U_{x}\right)
consists of

z^{d}f\left(z\right)
,
f\in\mathcal{O}_{x}\left(U_{x}\right)
, that
is,
\mathcal{O}_{x}\left(d\right)
is a Fréchet space sheaf copy of

\mathcal{O}_{x}
with its evaluation morphism
\mathcal{O}_{x}\left(d\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
f\mapsto\left(d!\right)^{-1}f^{\left(d\right)}\left(0\right)
. In a similar way, there are
\mathcal{O}_{y}
-modules
\mathcal{O}_{y}\left(d\right)
on the topological space

\mathbb{C}_{y}
. If
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)=\mathcal{O}_{x}\left(d\right)\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\times}\mathcal{O}_{y}\left(d\right)
are
the fibered products of the Fréchet sheaves over the constant sheaf

\mathbb{C}
, then

\mathcal{F}_{q}=\prod\limits_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)

up to a topological isomorphism of the Fréchet space sheaves

[
16
]
. That is so called the Decomposition Theorem for
\mathcal{F}_{q}
. In this case,
\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{F}_{q}=\prod\limits_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)
.

We prove that all noncommutative Fréchet algebras of the formal geometry
of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
are localizations indeed. Namely, so are the canonical
embeddings of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
into the Arens-Michael-Fréchet algebras

\mathbb{C}_{q}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]
,
\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]
and
\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)
whenever
U
is a

q
-open subset of
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
. But, if
U
is a Runge
q
-open subset,
then
\mathfrak{A}_{q}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
is a
localization too. Since we come up with the
q
-topology being weaker than the
original topology, it follows that
\mathfrak{A}_{q}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
is a localization for all
U
from a
q
-topology base
of
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
. In particular, PI-envelope of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
is a
localization. Thus we have the full description of the homological properties
of these algebras, and a certain model of noncommutative holomorphic

q
-functional calculus for the Banach
q
-modules is formed within Taylor’s
general framework of the multi-operator functional calculus
[
33
]
. Our
approach to the problem is crucially based on the Decomposition Theorem
mentioned above, and the triangular representations of the differentials by
involving some partial differential and difference operators. The related
technical machinery is developed within Sections
3
,

4
and
5
by analyzing the diagonal cohomology
groups. A proper discussion of localizations we only start in Section

6
. The main result (Theorem
7.2
) of the paper is
proved in Section
7
right after a brief discussion of the
formal geometry of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
. Finally, we prove that the
q
-closure
of the Taylor spectrum
\sigma\left(T,S\right)
of a left Banach

q
-module
X
given by an operator couple
\left(T,S\right)
on
X
with

TS=q^{-1}ST
is reduced to the Putinar spectrum
\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
of the module
X
with respect to the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{q}

(see Theorem
8.1
), that is,

\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)=\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}\text{.}

It turns out that there is a big gap between these spectra caused mainly by
the weakness of the
q
-topology. That is somewhat new that we have not seen
even in the case of Banach modules over a nilpotent (in particular abelian)
Lie algebra
[
13
, Proposition 5.3]
. These spectra used to coincide
in the case of a Banach module, but that is not the case in the
q
-theory.
This phenomenon explains the lack of the projection property for the Taylor
spectrum of the Banach
q
-modules
[
14
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

All considered vector spaces are assumed to be complex. The algebra of all
continuous linear operators on a polynormed (or locally convex) space
X
is
denoted by
\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
. If the topology of a complete
polynormed algebra
A
is defined by means of a family of multiplicative
seminorms, then
A
is called an
Arens-Michael algebra

[
24
, 1.2.4]
.

2.1.
Triangular (co)chains

Let
X_{n}
,
Y_{n}
,
Z_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
be Fréchet spaces and let

X=\prod_{n}X_{n}
,
Y=\prod_{n}Y_{n}
, and
Z=\prod_{n}Z_{n}
be their
topological direct products. A continuous linear mapping
S:X\rightarrow Y
is
said to be
a triangular operator
if it is given by a lower (or upper)
triangular operator matrix
S=\left[S_{mn}\right]
,
S_{mn}=0
,
m<n
,
where
S_{mn}:X_{n}\rightarrow Y_{m}
is a continuous linear operator. By a
triangular cochain we mean a couple of triangular operators

X\overset{S}{\longrightarrow}Y\overset{T}{\longrightarrow}Z
such that

TS=0
. In this case, there are the diagonal cochains
X_{m}\overset{S_{mm}}{\longrightarrow}Y_{m}\overset{T_{mm}}{\longrightarrow}Z_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The following assertion was taken from
[
10
, Proposition
4.2]
and
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Aristov-Dosi) Let
X\overset{S}{\longrightarrow}Y\overset{T}{\longrightarrow}Z
be a triangular cochain of Fréchet spaces.
If all diagonal cochains
X_{m}\overset{S_{mm}}{\longrightarrow}Y_{m}\overset{T_{mm}}{\longrightarrow}Z_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
are exact then
so is the original cochain. Moreover, if all diagonal operators
X_{m}\overset{S_{mm}}{\longrightarrow}Y_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
are topologically
injective maps, then so is
X\overset{S}{\longrightarrow}Y
.

Let us notify that the reverse statement to one from Lemma
2.1
is not
true, the exactness of a triangular cochain does not necessarily imply the
exactness of its diagonals. That is the case of the bimodule resolution for
the Fréchet algebra
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
considered below
in Subsection
7.4
.

2.2.
Topological homology and localizations

The main category of the underlying polynormed spaces for the considered
framework of topological homology is the category of Fréchet spaces. Let

A
be a Fréchet algebra, and let
X
be a left Fréchet
A
-module.
The category of all left Fréchet
A
-modules is denoted by
A
-
\operatorname*{mod}
whereas
\operatorname{mod}
-
A
(resp.,
A
-
\operatorname{mod}
-
A
) denotes the category of all right (resp., bi)
Fréchet
A
-modules. Recall that
X
is said to be a free left
A
-module
if
X=A\widehat{\otimes}E
is the projective tensor product of
A
and some
Fréchet space
E
equipped with the natural left
A
-module structure. A
retract in the category
A
-
\operatorname*{mod}
of a free
A
-module is
called a projective left
A
-module.
A projective resolution
of a
left
A
-module
X
is a complex
\left(\mathcal{P},d\right)
of left
projective
A
-modules with a morphism
\pi:\mathcal{P}_{0}\rightarrow X
such
that the augmented complex
0\leftarrow X\overset{\pi}{\longleftarrow}\mathcal{P}_{0}\overset{d_{0}}{\longleftarrow}\mathcal{P}_{1}\overset{d_{1}}{\longleftarrow}\cdots
is admissible (splits as a complex of the Fréchet spaces).

Let
B
be a polynormed algebra and let
F:A
-
\operatorname*{mod}\rightarrow B
-
\operatorname*{mod}
be an additive (co)functor. By
F_{n}
(respectively,

F^{n}
) we denote the
n
-th (projective) derived (co)functor of
F
. By its
very definition (see
[
24
, 3.3.1]
),
F_{n}\left(X\right)

(respectively,
F^{n}\left(X\right)
) is just the
n
-th (co)homology of
the (co)chain complex
\left(F\left(\mathcal{P}\right),F\left(d\right)\right)
for a projective resolution
\left(\mathcal{P},d\right)
of the
A
-module
X
. Taking into account that all projective
resolutions of a module are homotopy equivalent
[
24
, 3.2.3]
, we conclude
that
F_{n}\left(X\right)
(respectively,
F^{n}\left(X\right)
) does
not depend upon the particular choice of a projective resolution
\left(\mathcal{P},d\right)
of
X
. If
F
is the (co)functor

X\underset{A}{\widehat{\otimes}}\circ
(respectively,
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\circ\mathfrak{,}Y\right)
) then we write
\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{A}\left(X,\circ\right)
(respectively,
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}\left(\circ,Y\right)
) instead of the
n
-th derived (co)functor, as
usual. If
\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{A}\left(X,Y\right)=\left\{0\right\}
,
n\geq 0
, then we write
X\perp Y
, and say that these
A
-modules are in
the transversality relation.

Now assume that
\iota:B\rightarrow A
is a continuous algebra homomorphism
from a polynormed algebra
B
into a Fréchet algebra
A
. The source
algebra
B
can be a pure algebra equipped with the finest locally convex
topology. The homomorphism
\iota:B\rightarrow A
is said to be
a
localization
if it induces the natural isomorphisms
H_{n}\left(B,M\right)=H_{n}\left(A,M\right)
,
n\geq 0
of all homology groups for every
Fréchet
A
-bimodule
M
. In particular, the multiplication mapping

A\widehat{\otimes}_{B}A\rightarrow A
,
a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\mapsto a_{1}a_{2}

is a topological isomorphism, and
H_{n}\left(B,A\widehat{\otimes}A\right)=\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{B}\left(A,A\right)=\left\{0\right\}
for all

n>0
, where the algebra
A
is considered to be a
B
-bimodule via the
homomorphism
\iota
. If
0\leftarrow B\longleftarrow\mathcal{P}
is an
admissible projective bimodule resolution of
B
, then the application of the
functor
A\widehat{\otimes}_{B}\circ\widehat{\otimes}_{B}A
to
\mathcal{P}

augmented by the multiplication morphism results in the following chain
complex

0\leftarrow A\longleftarrow A\widehat{\otimes}_{B}\mathcal{P}\widehat{\otimes}_{B}A.

(1)

If (
1
) is admissible for some (actually for every)
\mathcal{P}
, then
certainly
\iota:B\rightarrow A
is a localization called
an absolute
localization
(or
A
is
stably flat over

B
). In this case,

H_{n}\left(B,M\right)=H_{n}\left(A,M\right)
,
n\geq 0
hold for every

\widehat{\otimes}
-bimodule
M
(not necessarily a Fréchet one). If
A

and
A\widehat{\otimes}_{B}\mathcal{P}\widehat{\otimes}_{B}A
consists of
nuclear Fréchet spaces, then
\iota:B\rightarrow A
is a localization (see

[
33
, Proposition 1.6]
) whenever (
1
) is exact.

2.3.
Arens-Michael envelope

Let
A
be a polynormed algebra with its separately continuous multiplication.
The
Arens-Michael envelope

[
24
, 5.2.21]
of
A
is called a pair

\left(\widetilde{A},\omega\right)
, where
\widetilde{A}
is an
Arens-Michael algebra,
\omega:A\rightarrow\widetilde{A}
is a continuous
algebra homomorphism with the following ”universal-projective” property: for all Arens-Michael
algebra
B
and a continuous algebra homomorphism
\pi:A\rightarrow B
, there
exists a unique continuous algebra homomorphism
\widetilde{\pi}:\widetilde{A}\rightarrow B
such that
\widetilde{\pi}\cdot\omega=\pi
. It
turns out that an Arens-Michael algebra is an inverse limit of some Banach
algebras
[
24
, 5.2.10]
. Thus in the latter universal projective property
it can be assumed that all considered algebras
B
are Banach algebras. The
set of all continuous characters of
A
is denoted by
\operatorname{Spec}\left(A\right)
. If
\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}\left(A\right)
then
the algebra homomorphism
\lambda:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defines (Banach)

A
-module structure on
\mathbb{C}
via pull back along
\lambda
. This
module called
a trivial module
is denoted by
\mathbb{C}\left(\lambda\right)
, which is an
\widetilde{A}
-module automatically. Thus

\operatorname{Spec}\left(A\right)=\operatorname{Spec}\left(\widetilde{A}\right)
.

2.4.
The
q
-topology of the complex plane

Let us fix
q\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
with
\left|q\right|<1
. A subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is called a
q
-spiraling set
if it contains the origin and
qS\subseteq S
. An open
subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is said to be
a
q
-open set

if it is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
in the standard topology, which is
also a
q
-spiraling set. The whole plane
\mathbb{C}
is
q
-open, and the
empty set is assumed to be
q
-open set. The family of all
q
-open subsets
defines a new topology
\mathfrak{q}
in
\mathbb{C}
, which is weaker than
the original standard topology of the complex plane. Every open disk
B\left(0,r\right)
centered at the origin is a
q
-open set.

Notice that
\left\{0\right\}
is a generic point of the topological space

\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
being dense in the whole plane. One
can easily prove that if
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
then
its closure in
\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
is given by

\left\{x\right\}^{-}=\left\{q^{-k}x:k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right\},

whereas
\left\{x\right\}_{q}=\left\{q^{k}x:k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right\}
defines its
q
-hull. Thus the topological space
\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
satisfies the axiom
T_{0}
, and it turns out to be an
irreducible topological space
[
5
, II, 4.1]
. One can easily see that

\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
is not quasicompact, in particular,
it is not Noetherian.

If
K\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is a compact subset in the standard topology of

\mathbb{C}
then it turns out to be a quasicompact subset of
\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
, but not necessarily a
\mathfrak{q}
-closed
subset. In particular, all disks (open or closed) centered at the origin are
quasicompact (nonclosed) subsets of
\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
. They are all dense subsets of
\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
.
Every closure
\left\{x\right\}^{-}
of a point
x\in\mathbb{C}
is not quasicompact.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be the standard Fréchet sheaf of stalks of the
holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}
and let
\operatorname{id}:\mathbb{C\rightarrow}\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
be the
identity mapping, which is continuous. The direct image
\operatorname{id}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}
of the sheaf
\mathcal{O}
along the identity mapping
turns out to be a Fréchet algebra sheaf on the topological space
\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
. For every
q
-open set
U
and its
quasicompact subset
K\subseteq U
we define the related seminorm
\left\|f\right\|_{K}=\sup\left|f\left(K\right)\right|
,

f\in\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
on the algebra
\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
. The family
\left\{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{K}\right\}

of seminorms over all
q
-compact subsets
K\subseteq U
(that is,
K=K_{q}
)
defines the same original Fréchet topology of
\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
. The stalk of
\operatorname{id}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}
at zero is the
same original stalk
\mathcal{O}_{0}
, whereas
\left(\operatorname{id}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}\right)_{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left\{\lambda\right\}_{q}\right)=\mathcal{O}_{0}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathcal{O}_{q^{n}\lambda}
at every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
. The algebra
\left(\operatorname{id}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}\right)_{\lambda}
is not local for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
. The ideal of those stalks
\left\langle U,f\right\rangle\in\left(\operatorname{id}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}\right)_{\lambda}
with

f\left(\left\{\lambda\right\}_{q}\right)=\left\{0\right\}
is not
maximal (see
[
15
]
). Everywhere below, we use the same notation

\mathcal{O}
instead of
\operatorname{id}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}
.

2.5.
The Fréchet space sheaves
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)
on

\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)

If
U\subseteq\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
is a
q
-open subset,
then it contains the origin and we put

\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)=\left\{f\left(z\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(U\right):z^{-d}f\left(z\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)\right\}

to be a closed ideal of
\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
, where
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Notice that
\mathcal{O}\left(0\right)\left(U\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
, and
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)
consists of those
f\left(z\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
such that
f\left(0\right)=f^{\prime}\left(0\right)=\cdots=f^{\left(d-1\right)}\left(0\right)=0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The ideal
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)
is
the principal ideal of
\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
generated by
z^{d}
,
that is,
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)=z^{d}\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
. The linear mapping
z^{-d}:\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
,
f\left(z\right)\mapsto z^{-d}f\left(z\right)
implements a topological
isomorphism of the Fréchet spaces preserving the multiplication operator
by
z
. Moreover,

\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)\oplus\mathbb{C}1\oplus\mathbb{C}z\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}z^{d-1}

is a topological direct sum of the closed subspaces
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)
and
\mathbb{C}1\oplus\mathbb{C}z\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}z^{d-1}
.

Proof.

If
f\in\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)
,
d\geq 1
, then

f\left(z\right)=z\left(z^{-1}f\left(z\right)\right)
with

z^{-1}f\left(z\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(d-1\right)\left(U\right)
. Thus
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)\subseteq z\mathcal{O}\left(d-1\right)\left(U\right)
. One can easily verify
that

P_{d}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)\right),\quad P_{d}\left(f\right)\left(z\right)=f\left(z\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{f^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)}{i!}z^{i}

is a continuous projection onto
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)
such that
\ker\left(P_{d}\right)=\oplus_{i=0}^{d-1}\mathbb{C}z^{i}
is a finite dimensional polynomial subspace. Furthermore,

\displaystyle P_{d}\left(zf\left(z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=zf\left(z\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(zf\right)^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)}{i!}z^{i}=zf\left(z\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{f^{\left(i-1\right)}\left(0\right)}{\left(i-1\right)!}z^{i}=z\left(f\left(z\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\frac{f^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)}{i!}z^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=zP_{d-1}\left(f\left(z\right)\right)

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
. It follows that
z\mathcal{O}\left(d-1\right)\left(U\right)\subseteq\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)\subseteq z\mathcal{O}\left(d-1\right)\left(U\right)
, that is,
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)=z\mathcal{O}\left(d-1\right)\left(U\right)=z^{d}\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
is
the principal ideal generated by the monomial
z^{d}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)=\left(z^{d}\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)\right)\oplus\left(\mathbb{C}z^{d-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}1\right)
is a topological direct sum of the closed subspaces.

Finally, the multiplication operator
z^{d}:\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)
is a continuous
bijection of the Fréchet spaces. By the Open Mapping Theorem, its inverse
mapping
z^{-d}:\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
,
f\left(z\right)\mapsto z^{-d}f\left(z\right)
is continuous, and
z\left(z^{-d}\left(f\left(z\right)\right)\right)=z^{-d}\left(zf\left(z\right)\right)
,

f\in\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)
.
∎

Thus
U\mapsto\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)
defines a
Fréchet space sheaf on
\mathbb{C}
denoted by
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)
. It is an isomorphic copy of the original sheaf
\mathcal{O}

obtained by means of the
d
-shift, that is,
\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)=z^{d}\mathcal{O}
is a (free) coherent
\mathcal{O}
-module
[
17
, 4]
. By
Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\oplus\mathbb{C}1\oplus\mathbb{C}z\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}z^{d-1}

is a direct sum of the Fréchet sheaves on
\left(\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{q}\right)
, where
\mathbb{C}z^{i}
,
0\leq i<d
are the constant sheaves.

Along with the projection
P_{d}
from the proof of Lemma
2.2
, let us
also introduce the following (translation) operator

\Delta_{q}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)\right),\quad\Delta_{q}\left(f\right)\left(z\right)=f\left(qz\right).

(2)

Once can easily verify that
\Delta_{q}P_{d}=P_{d}\Delta_{q}
. Finally, for a
holomorphic function
\theta\left(z,w\right)
about the origin in two
complex variables
z
and
w
, we define

\theta_{z}\left(z,w\right)=\frac{\theta\left(z,w\right)-\theta\left(0,w\right)}{z},\quad\theta_{w}\left(z,w\right)=\frac{\theta\left(z,w\right)-\theta\left(z,0\right)}{w}

to be the difference operators.

2.6.
Arens-Michael envelope of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
and noncommutative
plane
\mathbb{C}_{xy}

As above let
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
be the quantum plane (or just
q
-plane)
generated by

and

modulo the relation

, where

\left|q\right|\leq 1

. The Banach algebra completion of the seminormed

\mathcal{A}\left(\rho\right)
. The Arens-Michael envelope

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
of

turns

\mathcal{A}\left(\rho\right)
,
\rho>0
(see
[
28
]
,
[
15
]
). The closed two-sided
ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
generated by
xy
is
denoted by
\mathcal{I}_{xy}
. The following assertion was proved in

[
15
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\left|q\right|\neq 1
then the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
is commutative modulo its Jacobson
radical
\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
.
Moreover,

\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)=\cap\left\{\ker\left(\lambda\right):\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)\right)\right\}=\mathcal{I}_{xy},

\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)\right)=\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)/\mathcal{I}_{xy}\right)=\mathbb{C}_{xy},

where
\mathbb{C}_{x}=\mathbb{C\times}\left\{0\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
\mathbb{C}_{y}=\left\{0\right\}\times\mathbb{C\subseteq C}^{2}
and
\mathbb{C}_{xy}=\mathbb{C}_{x}\cup\mathbb{C}_{y}
. If
\left|q\right|=1
then
\mathcal{I}_{xy}
contains all quasinilpotents of

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
, and none of the ordered
monomials

,
i,k\in\mathbb{N}
is quasinilpotent.

Thus the character space
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
of the Fréchet algebra

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
is the union
\mathbb{C}_{x}\cup\mathbb{C}_{y}
of two copies of the complex plane whose intersection
consist of the trivial character that corresponds to
\left(0,0\right)
.
One can swap the role of
x
and
y
having
\mathbb{C}_{yx}
as the character
space of
\mathcal{O}_{q^{-1}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
. Based on
Proposition
2.1
, we use the notation
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
instead of
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
whenever
\left|q\right|\neq 1
. The character space

\mathbb{C}_{xy}
being the union
\mathbb{C}_{x}\cup\mathbb{C}_{y}
is
equipped with the final topology so that both embeddings

\left(\mathbb{C}_{x},\mathfrak{q}\right)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{xy}\hookleftarrow\left(\mathbb{C}_{y},\mathfrak{q}\right)

are continuous, which is called the
\mathfrak{q}
-topology of

\mathbb{C}_{xy}
. The topology base in
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
consists of all open
subsets
U=U_{x}\cup U_{y}
with
q
-open sets
U_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{x}

and
U_{y}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{y}
. In this case,
\mathbb{C}_{xy}=\mathbb{C}_{x}\cup\mathbb{C}_{y}
is the union of two irreducible subsets,
whose intersection is the unique generic point.

z_{2}-q^{d+1}z_{1}\end{array}\right],\quad\partial_{d,l}^{1}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}&q^{l}w_{2}-w_{1}\end{array}\right],

\pi_{d,l}\left(h\left(z_{1},w_{1};z_{2},w_{2}\right)\right)=h\left(z,q^{l}w;q^{d}z,w\right).

One can easily verify that
\partial_{d,l}^{1}\partial_{d,l}^{0}=0
and

\pi_{d,l}\partial_{d,l}^{1}=0
. Notice that

\partial_{d,l}^{0}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}w\otimes 1-q^{l+1}\otimes w\\
1\otimes z-z\otimes q^{d+1}\end{array}\right],\quad\partial_{d,l}^{1}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}1\otimes z-z\otimes q^{d}&q^{l}\otimes w-w\otimes 1\end{array}\right]

whereas
\pi_{d,l}\left(h\left(z_{1},w_{1};z_{2},w_{2}\right)\right)=\left(h_{1}\left(z_{1},q^{d}z_{1}\right),h_{4}\left(q^{l}w_{2},w_{2}\right)\right)
. The cohomology groups of
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(d,l\right)
are denoted by
H_{d,l}^{i}
,
0\leq i\leq 3
.

4.2.
The operator
T
on the free quadruples

Let us introduce the free quadruples to be the elements of the following
Fréchet space

\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)\times\mathcal{O}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\times\mathcal{O}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)\times\mathcal{O}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right),

which contains
\mathcal{O}_{d,l}
as a closed subspace (see Lemma

3.2
). The following continuous linear operator

T:\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)\longrightarrow\begin{array}[c]{c}\mathcal{O}_{d,l}\\
\oplus\\
\mathcal{O}_{d,l}\end{array},\quad T\left(\theta\right)=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\zeta\\
\eta\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\left(\left(0,-q^{l+1}w_{2}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(w_{1}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),\zeta_{4}\right)\right)\\
\left(\left(\eta_{1},-q^{d+1}z_{1}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(z_{2}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),0\right)\right)\end{array}\right]

plays a key role in the description of the cohomology groups
H_{d,l}^{i}
,
where
\zeta_{4}
and
\eta_{1}
are obtained by

\displaystyle\zeta_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{1}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)-q^{l+1}w_{2}\theta_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)+w_{1}w_{2}\theta_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-q^{d+1}z_{1}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right)+z_{2}\theta_{3}\left(0,z_{2}\right)+z_{1}z_{2}\theta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right).

By Lemma
3.2
, one can easily verify that
\zeta
and
\eta
are
compatible quadruples from
\mathcal{O}_{d,l}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}\left(z_{1},0\right)

\displaystyle=0=\zeta_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right),\quad\zeta_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)=w_{1}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)=\zeta_{4}\left(w_{1},0\right)\text{, }

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}\left(0,z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=0=\zeta_{3}\left(0,z_{2}\right)\text{,\quad}\zeta_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)=-q^{l+1}w_{2}\theta_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)=\zeta_{4}\left(0,w_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\left(z_{1},0\right)

\displaystyle=-q^{d+1}z_{1}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right)=\eta_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right),\quad\eta_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)=0=\eta_{4}\left(w_{1},0\right),

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\left(0,z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=z_{2}\theta_{3}\left(0,z_{2}\right)=\eta_{3}\left(0,z_{2}\right),\quad\eta_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)=0=\eta_{4}\left(0,w_{2}\right).

Thus
T
is a well defined continuous linear operator.

4.3.
The diagonal cohomology group
H_{d,l}^{1}

It turns out that the image of
T
provides the kernel of the differential

\partial_{d,l}^{1}
of the complex
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(d,l\right)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The range of the operator
T
equals to
\ker\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)
, and
T
implements a topological isomorphism

\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\ker\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)
of the Fréchet spaces.

Proof.

Take an element
\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\zeta\\
\eta\end{array}\right]=T\left(\theta\right)\in\operatorname{im}\left(T\right)
with

\theta\in\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)
, and put
\vartheta=\partial_{d,l}^{1}\left(T\left(\theta\right)\right)
. Using Corollary

3.1
, and the fact that
\zeta_{1}=0
,
\eta_{4}=0
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\vartheta

\displaystyle=\partial_{d,l}^{1}\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\zeta\\
\eta\end{array}\right]=\left(z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}\right)\zeta+\left(q^{l}w_{2}-w_{1}\right)\eta

\displaystyle=\left(\left(0,-q^{d}z_{1}\zeta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(z_{2}\zeta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),0\right)\right)+\left(\left(0,q^{l}w_{2}\eta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(-w_{1}\eta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),0\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(0,q^{d+l+1}z_{1}w_{2}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(z_{2}w_{1}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),0\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\left(0,-q^{l+d+1}w_{2}z_{1}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(-w_{1}z_{2}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),0\right)\right)=0,

that is,
T\left(\theta\right)\in\ker\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)
.
Conversely, take
\omega=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\zeta\\
\eta\end{array}\right]\in\ker\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)
. Then
\left(z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}\right)\zeta=\left(w_{1}-q^{l}w_{2}\right)\eta
, which
means that (see Corollary
3.1
)

\displaystyle\left(z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}\right)\zeta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(w_{1}-q^{l}w_{2}\right)\eta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)=0,

\displaystyle-q^{d}z_{1}\zeta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}\right)\zeta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)=\left(w_{1}-q^{l}w_{2}\right)\eta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)=-q^{l}w_{2}\eta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right),

\displaystyle z_{2}\zeta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}\right)\zeta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)=\left(w_{1}-q^{l}w_{2}\right)\eta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)=w_{1}\eta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}\right)\zeta_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)=\left(w_{1}-q^{l}w_{2}\right)\eta_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right).

It follows that (removable singularities)

\zeta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)=0,\quad\eta_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)=0,\quad\zeta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)=-q^{l+1}w_{2}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right),\quad\zeta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)=w_{1}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)

for some
\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{O}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)
and

\theta_{3}\in\mathcal{O}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)
(optionally one can use
Analytic Nullstellensatz, see below to the proof of Lemma
4.2
). In
particular,
\eta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)=-q^{d+1}z_{1}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)
and
\eta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)=z_{2}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)
. Further, using Lemma

3.2
, we derive that
\zeta_{4}\left(w_{1},0\right)=\zeta_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)=w_{1}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)
,

\zeta_{4}\left(0,w_{2}\right)=\zeta_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)=-q^{l+1}w_{2}\theta_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)
. In particular,
\zeta_{4}\left(0,0\right)=0
and

\zeta_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)=w_{1}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)-q^{l+1}w_{2}\theta_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)+w_{1}w_{2}\theta_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)

for some
\theta_{4}\in\mathcal{O}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)
(see below
Remark
4.1
). In a similar way, we obtain that

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}\left(z_{1},0\right)+\eta_{1}\left(0,z_{2}\right)+z_{1}z_{2}\theta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)=\eta_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right)+\eta_{3}\left(0,z_{2}\right)+z_{1}z_{2}\theta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-q^{d+1}z_{1}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right)+z_{2}\theta_{3}\left(0,z_{2}\right)+z_{1}z_{2}\theta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)

for some
\theta_{1}\in\mathcal{O}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)
. If

\theta=\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\theta_{4}\right)\in\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)
is the free quadruple obtained, then

T\left(\theta\right)=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\left(\left(0,-q^{l+1}w_{2}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(w_{1}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),\zeta_{4}\right)\right)\\
\left(\left(\eta_{1},-q^{d+1}z_{1}\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(z_{2}\theta_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),0\right)\right)\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\zeta\\
\eta\end{array}\right].

Thus
\operatorname{im}\left(T\right)=\ker\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)
, and in particular,
\operatorname{im}\left(T\right)
is
closed. One can easily see that
\ker\left(T\right)=\left\{0\right\}
.
By Open Mapping Theorem, we conclude that
T
implements a topological
isomorphism of
\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)
onto
\ker\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

If
\zeta\left(z,w\right)
is a holomorphic function
defined on a polydomain containing the origin, and
\zeta\left(0,0\right)=0
, then

\displaystyle\zeta\left(z,w\right)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{z}\left(z,w\right)z+\zeta\left(0,w\right)=\zeta_{z}\left(z,0\right)z+\left(\zeta_{z}\right)_{w}\left(z,w\right)wz+\zeta\left(0,w\right)

\displaystyle=z\frac{\zeta\left(z,0\right)-\zeta\left(0,0\right)}{z}+\zeta\left(0,w\right)+zw\left(\zeta_{z}\right)_{w}\left(z,w\right)=\zeta\left(z,0\right)+\zeta\left(0,w\right)+zw\zeta_{zw}\left(z,w\right),

that is,
\zeta\left(z,w\right)=\zeta\left(z,0\right)+\zeta\left(0,w\right)+zw\zeta_{zw}\left(z,w\right)
.

For the description of
\operatorname{im}\left(\partial_{d,l}^{0}\right)

in terms of the quadruples from Lemma
4.1
, we introduce the
following subspace
\mathcal{I}\left(U\right)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)
for those (free) quadruples
\theta
such that
\theta_{2}\left(0,0\right)=\theta_{3}\left(0,0\right)
and

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\left(z_{1},q^{d+1}z_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right)-\theta_{3}\left(0,q^{d+1}z_{1}\right)}{z_{1}},

(6)

\displaystyle\theta_{4}\left(q^{l+1}w_{2},w_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)-\theta_{3}\left(q^{l+1}w_{2},0\right)}{w_{2}},

Thus
\mathcal{I}\left(U\right)
is identified with the fibered product

\mathcal{O}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\times}\mathcal{O}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)
up to a topological isomorphism.
Every couple
\left(\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\right)
from the fibered product
defines uniquely the related quadruple
\theta
(see to the proof of the
forthcoming lemma).

Lemma 4.2
.

The continuous linear operator

with the relations

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right),\quad\alpha_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)=\alpha_{4}\left(w_{1},0\right),

(7)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\left(0,z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{d,l}^{0}\left(\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\left(w_{1}-q^{l+1}w_{2}\right)\alpha\\

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\left(\left(0,-q^{l+1}w_{2}\alpha_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(w_{1}\alpha_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),\left(w_{1}-q^{l+1}w_{2}\right)\alpha_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)\right)\right)\\

.
∎

Now we can prove the following key result on the first diagonal cohomology group.

Proposition 4.1
.

The canonical identification
H_{d,l}^{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
holds up to a topological isomorphism. In this case,
\ker\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)=\operatorname{im}\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)\oplus\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
is a topological direct sum
of the Fréchet spaces given by a continuous projection
p_{d,l}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\ker\left(\partial_{d,l}^{1}\right)\right)
whose
image being identified with
\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
consists of the
free quadruples
\left(f\left(z_{1}\right),0,\lambda,g\left(w_{2}\right)\right)
,
f\in\mathcal{O}\left(z_{1}\right)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
g\in\mathcal{O}\left(w_{2}\right)
.

Proof.

We define the following continuous linear mapping

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle:\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(z\right)\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\times}\mathcal{O}\left(w\right),

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\left(z\theta_{1}\left(z,q^{d+1}z\right)-\theta_{2}\left(z,0\right)+\theta_{3}\left(0,q^{d+1}z\right),w\theta_{4}\left(q^{l+1}w,w\right)-\theta_{2}\left(0,w\right)+\theta_{3}\left(q^{l+1}w,0\right)\right).

Notice that
\Phi\left(\theta\right)=\left(f\left(z\right),g\left(w\right)\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(z\right)\oplus\mathcal{O}\left(w\right)
with
f\left(0\right)=-\theta_{2}\left(0,0\right)+\theta_{3}\left(0,0\right)=g\left(0\right)
, that is,
\Phi\left(\theta\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(z\right)\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\times}\mathcal{O}\left(w\right)
. Further,
\theta\in\ker\left(\Phi\right)

if and only if
\theta\in\mathcal{I}\left(U\right)
.

Now take
\left(f\left(z\right),g\left(w\right)\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(z\right)\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\times}\mathcal{O}\left(w\right)
with
\lambda=f\left(0\right)=g\left(0\right)
. It
follows that
f\left(z\right)=\lambda+zf_{1}\left(z\right)
and

g\left(w\right)=\lambda+wg_{1}\left(w\right)
for some
f_{1}\in\mathcal{O}\left(z\right)
and
g_{1}\in\mathcal{O}\left(w\right)
.
Put
\theta_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)=f_{1}\left(z_{1}\right)
,

\theta_{2}=0
,
\theta_{3}=\lambda
,
\theta_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)=g_{1}\left(w_{2}\right)
, which defines a free quadruple
\theta
such
that
\Phi\left(\theta\right)=\left(zf_{1}\left(z\right)+\lambda,wg_{1}\left(w\right)+\lambda\right)=\left(f\left(z\right),g\left(w\right)\right)
. Thus

\Psi:\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right),\quad\Psi\left(f,g\right)=\left(f_{z}\left(z_{1}\right),0,f\left(0\right),g_{w}\left(w_{2}\right)\right)

is a continuous right inverse of
\Phi
. It follows that
p_{d,l}=\Psi\Phi
is
a continuous projection on
\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)
such that

\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)=\mathcal{I}\left(U\right)\oplus\operatorname{im}\left(p_{d,l}\right)
is a topological direct sum, and

\operatorname{im}\left(p_{d,l}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
up
to a topological isomorphism of the Fréchet spaces. Using Lemma

4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, we deduce that

H_{d,l}^{1}=\mathcal{Z}\left(U\right)/\mathcal{I}\left(U\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(z\right)\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\times}\mathcal{O}\left(w\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)

up to the topological isomorphisms, that is,
H_{d,l}^{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
holds.
∎

4.4.
The main result on the diagonal cohomology

Now we can prove the central result on the cohomology groups of the diagonal
complex
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(d,l\right)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
H_{d,l}^{p}
,
0\leq p\leq 3
are the cohomology groups of
the diagonal complex
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(d,l\right)
, then
H_{d,l}^{0}=\left\{0\right\}
,
H_{d,l}^{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
,

H_{d,l}^{2}=\left\{0\right\}
and
H_{d,l}^{3}=\left\{0\right\}
.

Proof.

Take
h=h\left(z_{1},w_{1};z_{2},w_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{O}_{d,l}
with

\partial_{d,l}^{0}h=0
. It follows that

\left(w_{1}-q^{l+1}w_{2}\right)h\left(z_{1},w_{1};z_{2},w_{2}\right)=\left(z_{2}-q^{d+1}z_{1}\right)h\left(z_{1},w_{1};z_{2},w_{2}\right)=0.

Notice that
w_{1}=q^{l+1}w_{2}
defines an algebraic (or thin) subset

\Xi\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
to be zero set of the polynomial function

w_{1}-q^{l+1}w_{2}
. The function
h
is vanishing out of
\Xi\cap\left(U_{{}_{1}}\times U_{{}_{2}}\right)
. By Removable Singularity Theorem

[
21
, I.C.3]
, we deduce that
h
is vanishing over all
U_{1}\times U_{2}
. Therefore
H_{d,l}^{0}=\left\{0\right\}
. The fact
H_{d,l}^{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
follows from Proposition
4.1
.

Now let us prove that
H_{d,l}^{2}=\left\{0\right\}
. Assume that

\pi_{d,l}\left(h\right)=h\left(z,q^{l}w;q^{d}z.w\right)=0
for some

h\in\mathcal{O}_{d,l}
. As a compatible quadruple, we obtain that

h_{1}\left(z_{1},q^{d}z_{1}\right)=0,\quad h_{4}\left(q^{l}w_{2},w_{2}\right)=0.

As above in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, using Analytic Nullstellensatz,
we conclude that
h_{1}=\left(z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}\right)f_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)
and
h_{4}=\left(q^{l}w_{2}-w_{1}\right)f_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)
for some
f_{1}\in\mathcal{O}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)
and
f_{4}\in\mathcal{O}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)
. Based on the
compatibility conditions from Lemma
3.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle-q^{d}z_{1}f_{1}\left(z_{1},0\right)

\displaystyle=h_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right),\quad h_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)=-w_{1}f_{4}\left(w_{1},0\right),

\displaystyle z_{2}f_{1}\left(0,z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=h_{3}\left(0,z_{2}\right),\quad h_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)=q^{l}w_{2}f_{4}\left(0,w_{2}\right),

which invokes that
h_{2}\left(0,0\right)=h_{3}\left(0,0\right)=0
. It
follows that (see Remark
4.1
)

\displaystyle h_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=h_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right)+h_{2}\left(0,w_{2}\right)+z_{1}w_{2}g_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-q^{d}z_{1}f_{1}\left(z_{1},0\right)+q^{l}w_{2}f_{4}\left(0,w_{2}\right)+z_{1}w_{2}g_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right),

\displaystyle h_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=h_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)+h_{3}\left(0,z_{2}\right)+w_{1}z_{2}g_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-w_{1}f_{4}\left(w_{1},0\right)+z_{2}f_{1}\left(0,z_{2}\right)+w_{1}z_{2}g_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)

for some
g_{2}\in\mathcal{O}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)
and
g_{3}\in\mathcal{O}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right)
. We define

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle=\left(\left(f_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right),f_{1}\left(z_{1},0\right)\right),\left(f_{1}\left(0,z_{2}\right)+w_{1}g_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),f_{1}\left(0,0\right)+w_{1}g_{3}\left(w_{1},0\right)\right)\right),

\displaystyle=\left(\left(f_{4}\left(0,0\right)+q^{-l}z_{1}g_{2}\left(z_{1},0\right),f_{4}\left(0,w_{2}\right)+q^{-l}z_{1}g_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(f_{4}\left(w_{1},0\right),f_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)\right)\right)

to be the elements of
\mathcal{O}_{d,l}

\displaystyle=\left(z_{2}-q^{d}z_{1}\right)\zeta+\left(q^{l}w_{2}-w_{1}\right)\eta

\displaystyle=\left(\left(h_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right),-q^{d}z_{1}f_{1}\left(z_{1},0\right)\right),\left(z_{2}f_{1}\left(0,z_{2}\right)+z_{2}w_{1}g_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),0\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(h_{1}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right),h_{2}\left(z_{1},w_{2}\right)\right),\left(h_{3}\left(w_{1},z_{2}\right),h_{4}\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)\right)\right)=h.

Hence

Finally, let us prove that

5.1.

Let

q
-diagonal operator, that is,
D_{y,q}=\prod\limits_{n}q^{n}
. Let us define
the following Fréchet space complex
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(y\right)
:

0\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]\overset{d_{y}^{0}}{\longrightarrow}\begin{array}[c]{c}\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]\\
\oplus\\
\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]\end{array}\overset{d_{y}^{1}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]\overset{\pi_{y}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x\right]\right]\rightarrow 0,

whose differentials are acting by

d_{y}^{0}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}N_{y}\\
x_{2}-qx_{1}D_{y,q}\end{array}\right],\quad d_{y}^{1}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}x_{2}-x_{1}D_{y,q}&-N_{y}\end{array}\right].

(8)

Notice that
\left(x_{2}-x_{1}D_{y,q}\right)f=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(x_{2}-q^{n}x_{1}\right)f_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}=\left(\prod\limits_{n}\left(x_{2}-q^{n}x_{1}\right)\right)f
for all

f\in\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]
, that is,
x_{2}-x_{1}D_{y,q}=\prod\limits_{n}\left(x_{2}-q^{n}x_{1}\right)
. In a similar way, we have
x_{2}-qx_{1}D_{y,q}=\prod\limits_{n}\left(x_{2}-q^{n+1}x_{1}\right)
. Then

\displaystyle d_{y}^{1}d_{y}^{0}f

\displaystyle=\left(\prod\limits_{n}\left(x_{2}-q^{n}x_{1}\right)\right)\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{n-1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}-\left(1\otimes R_{y}\right)\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(x_{2}-q^{n+1}x_{1}\right)f_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(x_{2}-q^{n}x_{1}\right)f_{n-1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}-\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(x_{2}-q^{n+1}x_{1}\right)f_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n+1}=0

for every
f\in\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\pi_{y}d_{y}^{1}h

\displaystyle=\pi_{y}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(x_{2}-q^{n}x_{1}\right)f_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}-\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{n-1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)|_{x_{1}=x_{2}=x}=0

for all
h=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}f\\
g\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]^{\oplus 2}
. It follows that
d_{y}^{1}d_{y}^{0}=0

and
\pi_{y}d_{y}^{1}=0
, which means that
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(y\right)
is a cochain complex of the Fréchet spaces called
the left
diagonal formal
q
-complex
.

5.2.
The right diagonal formal
q
-complex

In a similar way, we consider the commutative Fréchet algebra

\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]
of all formal power
series in the independent variables
y_{1}
,
y_{2}
, and the Fréchet space

\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]
(to be
\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x\right]\right]\widehat{\otimes}\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]
)
with the related operators

\displaystyle N_{x},D_{x,q}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{L}\left(\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]\right),\quad N_{x}\left(f\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}f_{n-1}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right),\text{ }D_{x,q}\left(f\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}q^{n}f_{n}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right),

\displaystyle y_{1}-qy_{2}D_{x,q}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{n}\left(y_{1}-q^{n+1}y_{2}\right),\quad y_{2}D_{x,q}-y_{1}=\prod\limits_{n}\left(q^{n}y_{2}-y_{1}\right).

Let us define the following
right diagonal formal
q
-complex

\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(x\right)
of the Fréchet
spaces

0\rightarrow\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]\overset{d_{x}^{0}}{\longrightarrow}\begin{array}[c]{c}\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]\\
\oplus\\
\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]\end{array}\overset{d_{x}^{1}}{\longrightarrow}\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]\overset{\pi_{x}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y\right]\right]\rightarrow 0,

whose differentials are acting by

d_{x}^{0}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}y_{1}-qy_{2}D_{x,q}\\
N_{x}\end{array}\right],\quad d_{x}^{1}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}N_{x}&y_{2}D_{x,q}-y_{1}\end{array}\right],

and the morphism
\pi_{x}
has the same action
\pi_{x}\left(\sum_{n}x^{n}h_{n}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)\right)=h_{0}\left(y,y\right)
.
Notice that

\displaystyle\pi_{x}d_{x}^{1}\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}f\\
g\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\pi_{x}\left(N_{x}f+\left(y_{2}D_{x,q}-y_{1}\right)g\right)

\displaystyle=\pi_{x}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}f_{n-1}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)+\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}\left(y_{2}q^{n}-y_{1}\right)g_{n}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(y_{2}-y_{1}\right)g_{0}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)|_{y_{1}=y_{2}=y}=0.

In the same way, we have

\displaystyle d_{x}^{1}d_{x}^{0}h

\displaystyle=N_{x}\left(y_{1}-qy_{2}D_{x,q}\right)h+\left(y_{2}D_{x,q}-y_{1}\right)N_{x}h

\displaystyle=N_{x}\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}\left(y_{1}-q^{n+1}y_{2}\right)h_{n}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)-\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n+1}\left(y_{1}-q^{n+1}y_{2}\right)h_{n}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}\left(y_{1}-q^{n}y_{2}\right)h_{n-1}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)-\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}\left(y_{1}-q^{n}y_{2}\right)h_{n-1}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)=0.

Thus
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(x\right)
is a cochain complex of the
Fréchet spaces indeed.

5.3.
The exactness of the diagonal formal complexes

Actually, both diagonal formal
q
-complexes are exact.

Lemma 5.1
.

The diagonal formal
q
-complexes
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(y\right)
and
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(x\right)
are exact.

Proof.

We provide the proof in the case of the left diagonal formal
q
-complex

\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(y\right)
. The same argument is applicable in the
case of
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(x\right)
(see below Remark
5.1
).
If
h\in\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]
with
d_{y}^{0}h=0
, then
N_{y}h=\sum_{n\geq 1}h_{n-1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}=0
, which means that
h_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=0
for all
n
. Thus
\ker\left(d_{y}^{0}\right)=\left\{0\right\}
. Pick
\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}f\\
g\end{array}\right]\in\ker\left(d_{y}^{1}\right)
, that is,
\prod\limits_{n}\left(x_{2}-q^{n}x_{1}\right)f=\left(1\otimes R_{y}\right)g
. Then
\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=0
and
\left(x_{2}-q^{n}x_{1}\right)f_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=g_{n-1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)
,
n\geq 1
. Since the commutative algebra
\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]
has no zero divisors

[
4
, IV.5.3, Theorem 1]
, it follows that
f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=0
. Put
h=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n+1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}\in\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]\left[\left[y\right]\right]
. Then

\displaystyle d_{y}^{0}h

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}N_{y}h\\
\left(x_{2}-qx_{1}D_{y,q}\right)h\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}\\
\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(x_{2}-q^{n+1}x_{1}\right)f_{n+1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}f\\
\sum_{n\geq 0}g_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}f\\
g\end{array}\right],

that is,
\ker\left(d_{y}^{1}\right)=\operatorname{im}\left(d_{y}^{0}\right)
. Further, take
h\in\ker\left(\pi_{y}\right)
, that is,

h_{0}\left(x,x\right)=0
. Based on Taylor’s formula for the power series

[
4
, IV.5.8, Proposition 9]
, we deduce that

h_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{1}+t\right)-h_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{1}\right)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}h_{0}}{\partial x_{2}^{m}}\left(x_{1},x_{1}\right)t^{m}=t\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}h_{0}}{\partial x_{2}^{m}}\left(x_{1},x_{1}\right)t^{m-1}\right).

It follows that

h_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=h_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{1}+\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)\right)-h_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{1}\right)=\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)

for some
f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]
. Put
g=-\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n+1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y_{1}^{n}
and
f=f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)
.
Then

\displaystyle d_{y}^{1}\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}f\\
g\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)-\left(-\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n+1}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y_{1}^{n+1}\right)=h_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)+\sum_{n\geq 1}h_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle=h,

that is,
\ker\left(\pi_{y}\right)=\operatorname{im}\left(d_{y}^{1}\right)
. Hence
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(y\right)
is an exact complex.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

In the case of the right diagonal formal
q
-complex

\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(x\right)
, one needs to take
h=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}g_{n+1}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)\in\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]
whenever
\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}f\\
g\end{array}\right]\in\ker\left(d_{x}^{1}\right)
. If
h\in\ker\left(\pi_{x}\right)

, and
g=g_{0}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)
whenever

h_{0}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)=\left(y_{2}-y_{1}\right)g_{0}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)
.

5.4.
The left diagonal holomorphic
q
-complex

Now assume that
\left|q\right|<1
,
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{xy}
a

q
-open subset, and let
U_{x_{1}}
,
U_{x_{2}}
(resp.,
U_{y_{1}}
,

U_{y_{2}}
) be two copies of the
q
-open subset
U_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{C}

(resp.,
U_{y}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
). We replace
\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]\right]
by the Fréchet algebra
\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)
of holomorphic functions on the polydomain

U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In the same way,

\mathbb{C}\left[\left[y_{1},y_{2}\right]\right]

\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y_{1}}\times U_{y_{2}}\right)

well defined continuous linear operators
N_{y},D_{y,q}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\right)

N_{x},D_{x,q}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y_{1}}\times U_{y_{2}}\right)\right)
, which in turn define the diagonal holomorphic
q
-complexes

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(y\right)
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(x\right)

instead of
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(y\right)
and
\mathfrak{D}_{q}\left(x\right)
, respectively. Namely,
the left diagonal holomorphic
q
-complex

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(y\right)
is the following
Fréchet space complex

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\overset{d_{y}^{0}}{\longrightarrow}\begin{array}[c]{c}\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\\
\oplus\\
\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\end{array}\overset{d_{y}^{1}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\overset{\pi_{y}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\rightarrow 0,

where
d_{y}^{i}
are given by the same matrices (
8
), and
\pi_{y}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)y^{n}\right)=h_{0}\left(x,x\right)
. In the same way,
the right diagonal
holomorphic
q
-complex
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(x\right)
is defined.

Proposition 5.1
.

The diagonal holomorphic
q
-complexes
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(x\right)
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(y\right)
are exact.

Proof.

Let us consider the case of the left diagonal complex
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(y\right)
. By mimicking the proof of Lemma
5.1
, we have to prove
first that the equality
\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=0
for some
f_{0}\in\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)
implies that
f_{0}=0
. Notice that
x_{2}=x_{1}
defines
a thin subset
\Xi\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
to be zero set of the holomorphic
function
x_{2}-x_{1}
. The function
f_{0}
is vanishing out of
\Xi\cap\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)
. By Removable Singularity
Theorem
[
21
, I.C.3]
, we deduce that
f_{0}
is vanishing over all

U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}
. Further, if
h_{0}\in\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)
, then the function

f_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\frac{h_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)-h_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{1}\right)}{x_{2}-x_{1}}

defined on
\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\Xi\right)\cap\left(U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}\right)
has a unique holomorphic extension to
U_{x_{1}}\times U_{x_{2}}
again by virtue of Removable Singularity Theorem.

Finally, in the case of
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(x\right)
we use the same
argument by appealing to Remark
5.1
. The rest is clear.
∎

6.
Noncommutative localizations

In this section we switch to the quantum plane
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
and its
bimodule resolution, that is lifted to the left and right formal Fréchet
algebras of noncommutative formal geometry of
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
.

6.1.
The bimodule resolution of the quantum plane

The quantum plane
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
possesses (see
[
30
]
,
[
34
]
) the
following free
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
-bimodule resolution

0\leftarrow\mathfrak{A}_{q}\overset{\pi}{\longleftarrow}\mathfrak{A}_{q}\otimes\mathfrak{A}_{q}\overset{\partial_{0}}{\longleftarrow}\mathfrak{A}_{q}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathfrak{A}_{q}\overset{\partial_{1}}{\longleftarrow}\mathfrak{A}_{q}\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathfrak{A}_{q}\leftarrow 0,

(9)

with the mapping
\pi\left(a\otimes b\right)=ab
and the differentials

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\left(a\otimes e_{1}\otimes b\right)

\displaystyle=a\otimes xb-ax\otimes b,\quad\partial_{0}\left(a\otimes e_{2}\otimes b\right)=a\otimes yb-ay\otimes b,

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\left(a\otimes e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\otimes b\right)

\displaystyle=a\otimes e_{2}\otimes xb-qax\otimes e_{2}\otimes b-qa\otimes e_{1}\otimes yb+ay\otimes e_{1}\otimes b,

where
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{q}
and
\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)
is the
standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. One of the key results of
[
28
]

asserts that the canonical embedding
\mathfrak{A}_{q}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
is an absolute localization
(see Subsection
2.2
). Thus, by applying the functor
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\underset{\mathfrak{A}_{q}}{\widehat{\otimes}}\circ\underset{\mathfrak{A}_{q}}{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
to the resolution
(
9
), we derive that the following complex

0\leftarrow\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\overset{\pi}{\longleftarrow}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\overset{d_{0}}{\longleftarrow}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\widehat{\otimes}\mathbb{C}^{2}\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\overset{d_{1}}{\longleftarrow}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\widehat{\otimes}\wedge^{2}\mathbb{C}^{2}\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\leftarrow 0

similar to (
9
) is admissible. It provides a free
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
-bimodule resolution of
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
(see
[
25
, 3.2]
). We use a bit
modified cochain version of this resolution

\mathcal{R}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\right):0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\overset{d^{0}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\overset{d^{1}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\rightarrow 0,

whose differentials can be given by the following operator matrices

d^{0}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{c}R_{y}\otimes 1-q\otimes L_{y}\\
1\otimes L_{x}-R_{x}\otimes q\end{array}\right],\text{ }d^{1}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}1\otimes L_{x}-R_{x}\otimes 1&1\otimes L_{y}-R_{y}\otimes 1\end{array}\right],

(10)

where
L
and
R
indicate to the left and right regular (anti)
representations of the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
. Since
\mathcal{R}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\rightarrow 0
is an admissible complex of the free

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
-bimodules, we deduce (see

[
25
, 3.1.18]
) that it splits in the category of Fréchet

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
-bimodules too.

Now let
\mathcal{A}
be a Fréchet
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
-algebra with its continuous homomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
. By applying the
functor
\mathcal{A}\underset{\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)}{\widehat{\otimes}}\circ\underset{\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)}{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{A}
to the resolution

\mathcal{R}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\right)
, we obtain that

\mathcal{A}\underset{\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)}{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{R}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\right)\underset{\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)}{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{A=R}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\right)\text{,}

which is a free
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule complex with the same differentials
(
10
). Moreover, it can be augmented by the multiplication morphism

\pi:\mathcal{A}^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, that is, we come
up with the following complex

\mathcal{R}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\widehat{\otimes}2}\right)\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow 0

(11)

of the free
\mathcal{A}
-bimodules. Thus
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is an absolute localization if
(
11
) is admissible (in particular, exact). In this case, (
11
)
provides a free
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule resolution of the algebra
\mathcal{A}
. But if
\mathcal{A}
is a nuclear Fréchet space, and (
11
) is
exact, then
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
(or
\mathfrak{A}_{q}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
) is a localization,
that is, all homology functors
H_{p}\left(\mathcal{A},\circ\right)
,

p\geq 0
are reduced to
H_{p}\left(\mathfrak{A}_{q},\circ\right)
,

p\geq 0
(see Subsection
2.2
). Notice that
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is a localization if and only
so is
\mathfrak{A}_{q}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}

[
33
, Proposition 1.8]
.
The potential candidates for the localizing algebras
\mathcal{A}
are the
Fréchet
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
-algebras

\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]
,

\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)
,

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
and
\mathbb{C}_{q}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]
considered below. They are all nuclear Fréchet
spaces, and we prove that the related complexes (
11
) are exact indeed.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Fréchet
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}

is closed then

Proof.

First notice that

and
ay\otimes b-a\otimes yb

belong to
\operatorname{im}\left(d^{1}\right)
for all

for every polynomial
p\in\mathfrak{A}_{q}
. Using the
continuity argument, we deduce that

for all

whenever
\operatorname{im}\left(d^{1}\right)

\operatorname{im}\left(d^{1}\right)

Finally, if

The space

7.
The formal geometry of the contractive quantum
plane

In this section we solve the localization problem for the structure sheaf
algebras of the formal geometry of the contractive quantum plane

\mathfrak{A}_{q}
.

7.1.
The decomposition theorem

We briefly review the related construction and the main decomposition theorem
from
[
16
]
. The formal geometry of the contractive quantum plane

\mathfrak{A}_{q}
is given by the ringed space
\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy},\mathcal{F}_{q}\right)
, which consists of the topological space

\mathbb{C}_{xy}
equipped with the
q
-topology, and the Fréchet

\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
-algebra sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{q}
of the formal
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
-sections on
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
. If

U\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{xy}
is a
q
-open subset, then
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
is the fibered product
\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\underset{\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]}{\times}\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)
defined in terms of the following commutative diagram

\begin{array}[c]{ccccc}&&\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)&&\\
&{}^{\pi_{x}}\swarrow&&\searrow^{\pi_{y}}&\\
\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]&&&&\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)\\
&{}_{s_{x}}\searrow&&\swarrow_{s_{y}}&\\
&&\mathbb{C}_{q}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]&&\end{array}

with the canonical projections
\pi_{x}
,
\pi_{y}
, and the continuous
algebra homomorphisms

\displaystyle s_{x}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{q}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right],\quad s\left(U_{x}\right)\left(\sum_{n}f_{n}\left(z\right)y^{n}\right)=\sum_{s,i}\dfrac{f_{i}^{\left(s\right)}\left(0\right)}{s!}x^{s}y^{i},

\displaystyle s_{y}

\displaystyle:\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{q}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right],\quad t\left(U_{y}\right)\left(\sum_{n}x^{n}g_{n}\left(w\right)\right)=\sum_{t,j}\dfrac{g_{t}^{\left(j\right)}\left(0\right)}{j!}x^{t}y^{j}.

It turns out that

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)=\left\{\left(f,g\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\oplus\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right):\dfrac{f_{k}^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)}{i!}=\dfrac{g_{i}^{\left(k\right)}\left(0\right)}{k!},i,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right\}.

(15)

Thus
\mathcal{F}_{q}=\mathcal{O}\left[\left[y\right]\right]\underset{\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]}{\times}\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}
is a Fréchet algebra sheaf on

\mathbb{C}_{xy}
being the fibered product of the Fréchet algebra sheaves

\mathcal{O}\left[\left[y\right]\right]
and
\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}
over the constant sheaf
\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]
. The morphisms
l
and
r
(see Subsection

6.4
) define the Fréchet algebra sheaf morphism
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}
, that turns

\mathcal{F}_{q}
into a Fréchet algebra sheaf over
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
. For every integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

we define the following closed subspaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]^{d}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)y^{d}\times\prod\limits_{k>d}\left(\mathbb{C}z^{d}\right)y^{k}\subseteq\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]\text{,}

\displaystyle\left[\left[x\right]\right]^{d}\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)

\displaystyle=x^{d}\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)\times\prod\limits_{i>d}x^{i}\left(\mathbb{C}w^{d}\right)\subseteq\left[\left[x\right]\right]\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right),

where
\mathbb{C}z^{d}\subseteq\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)
(resp.,

\mathbb{C}w^{d}\subseteq\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)
) is the subspace
generated by the monomial. They consist of certain elements of the order
d

(the least nonzero term occurs at
d
). We also put

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{d}=\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]^{d}\underset{\mathbb{C}\left[\left[x,y\right]\right]}{\times}\left[\left[x\right]\right]^{d}\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)

to be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
. For every

d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the following continuous linear operator

\displaystyle p_{d}\left(U\right)

\displaystyle:\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right),\quad p_{d}\left(U\right)\left(f,g\right)=\left(\overline{f},\overline{g}\right),

\displaystyle\overline{f}

\displaystyle=\left(f_{d}\left(z\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{g_{i}^{\left(d\right)}\left(0\right)}{d!}z^{i}\right)y^{d}+\sum_{k>d}\left(\dfrac{g_{d}^{\left(k\right)}\left(0\right)}{k!}z^{d}\right)y^{k},

\displaystyle\overline{g}

\displaystyle=x^{d}\left(g_{d}\left(w\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{f_{i}^{\left(d\right)}\left(0\right)}{d!}w^{i}\right)+\sum_{i>d}x^{i}\left(\dfrac{f_{d}^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)}{i!}w^{d}\right).

defines a continuous projection onto
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{d}

such that

p\left(U\right)=\prod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}p_{d}\left(U\right):\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)\rightarrow\prod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{d},\quad p\left(U\right)\left(\zeta\right)=\left(p_{d}\left(U\right)\left(\zeta\right)\right)_{d}

implements a topological isomorphism of
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)

onto the direct product of the Fréchet spaces. Moreover, the linear
mapping
\alpha_{d}:\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\left(U\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{d},

\alpha_{d}\left(f,g\right)=\left(f\left(z\right)y^{d}+\sum_{k>d}\left(\dfrac{g^{\left(k\right)}\left(0\right)}{k!}z^{d}\right)y^{k},x^{d}g\left(w\right)+\sum_{i>d}x^{i}\left(\dfrac{f^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)}{i!}w^{d}\right)\right)

implements a topological isomorphism of the related Fréchet spaces.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{xy}
be a
q
-open subset. The
mapping
\Lambda\left(U\right):\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
,
\Lambda\left(U\right)\left(f,g\right)=\left(f_{0},g_{0}\right)
is a retraction of the Fréchet
spaces with its right inverse
\alpha_{0}\left(U\right):\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{0}
, which allows us
to identify
\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)
with the complemented subspace

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{0}
of
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
. In this case,

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(U\right)\oplus\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)

is a topological direct sum of the closed subspaces,

\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)=\prod\limits_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{d}

up to a topological isomorphism of the Fréchet spaces, and

\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)\right)=U
.

That is the Decomposition Theorem from
[
16
]
, which states that

\mathcal{F}_{q}
can be obtained as the quantization of the commutative
structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
in the following way

\mathcal{F}_{q}=\prod\limits_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)

up to a Fréchet space sheaf isomorphism. Moreover,
\mathcal{O}
can be
restored as the quotient
\mathcal{F}_{q}/\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{F}_{q}=\mathcal{O}
, where
\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{F}_{q}
is the sheaf
associated to the presheaf
U\mapsto\operatorname{Rad}\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
.

7.2.
Runge
q
-open subsets

Recall that an open subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{C}
in the standard topology is
said to be a Runge open subset if it has no holes or every component of
V
is
a simply connected domain. A Runge open subset
V
can also be defined in
terms of the density of the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}\left[z\right]

in the Fréchet algebra
\mathcal{O}\left(V\right)
. A
q
-open and
Runge open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is called
a Runge
q
-open set
. The basic
q
-open subsets considered in
[
15
]

are all Runge
q
-open subsets. Namely, take
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
and its
\mathfrak{q}
-neighborhood
U
, which
is a
q
-open subset containing
\lambda
. The
q
-hull
\left\{\lambda\right\}_{q}
of
\lambda
(see Subsection
2.4
) is
contained in
U
. Notice that
B\left(0,\varepsilon\right)\subseteq U
for
a small
\varepsilon>0
, and there are just finitely many of
q^{m}\lambda

outside of the disk
B\left(0,\varepsilon\right)
, say
\left\{\lambda,q\lambda,\ldots q^{n}\lambda\right\}
. Pick up small disks
B\left(q^{m}\lambda,\left|q\right|^{m}\delta\right)\subseteq U
,
0\leq m\leq n
for
\delta>0
. Then
U_{\varepsilon,\delta}=B\left(0,\varepsilon\right)\cup\bigcup\limits_{m=0}^{n}B\left(q^{m}\lambda,\left|q\right|^{m}\delta\right)
is a Runge
q
-open neighborhood of

\left\{\lambda\right\}_{q}
contained in
U
. A
q
-open subset

U\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{xy}
is said to be
a Runge
q
-open
subset
if both parts
U_{x}
and
U_{y}
are Runge
q
-open subsets of the
complex plane. Thus Runge
q
-open subsets in the
q
-plane
\mathbb{C}_{xy}

form a topology basis.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{xy}
be a
q
-open subset. Then

U
is a Runge
q
-open subset if and only if
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
is dense in

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
.

Proof.

First assume that
U
is a Runge
q
-open subset. Take
h=\left(f,g\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)
to be identified with

\alpha_{d}\left(h\right)=\left(f\left(z\right)y^{d}+\sum_{k>d}\left(a_{k}z^{d}\right)y^{k},x^{d}g\left(w\right)+\sum_{i>d}x^{i}\left(b_{i}w^{d}\right)\right)\in\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{d},

where
a_{k}=\dfrac{g^{\left(k\right)}\left(0\right)}{k!}
and

b_{i}=\dfrac{f^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)}{i!}
for all
k,i>d
.
There are polynomials
\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}\left[z\right]
and
\left\{v_{n}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}\left[w\right]
such that
\lim_{n}u_{n}=f
in
\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)
and

\lim_{n}v_{n}=g
in
\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)
. Based on the
continuity of the differentiation operator on the Fréchet spaces

\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(U_{y}\right)
,
we derive that
a_{k}=\lim_{n}\dfrac{v_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(0\right)}{k!}
and
b_{i}=\lim_{n}\dfrac{u_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)}{i!}
. Since
f^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)=0
,

g^{\left(k\right)}\left(0\right)=0
,
i,k<d
, we can also assume that

u_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(0\right)=0
,
v_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\left(0\right)=0
,
i,k<d
for all
n
. One needs to replace
u_{n}
,

v_{n}
by
P_{d}\left(u_{n}\right)
,
P_{d}\left(v_{n}\right)
using
the projection
P_{d}
from the proof of Lemma
2.2
. Moreover,
replacing
u_{n}\left(z\right)
by
u_{n}\left(z\right)+\lambda_{n}z^{d}
,
\lambda_{n}=\dfrac{v_{n}^{\left(d\right)}\left(0\right)}{d!}-\dfrac{u_{n}^{\left(d\right)}\left(0\right)}{d!}
, we can assume
that
h_{n}=\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\in\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)
for
all
n
. Since
\lim_{n}\lambda_{n}=0
, it follows that
\lim_{n}h_{n}=h
in

\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)
. Further,
h_{n}
is identified with

\alpha_{d}\left(h_{n}\right)=\left(u_{n}\left(z\right)y^{d}+\sum_{k>d}\left(a_{k}^{\left(n\right)}z^{d}\right)y^{k},x^{d}v_{n}\left(w\right)+\sum_{i>d}x^{i}\left(b_{i}^{\left(n\right)}w^{d}\right)\right)\in\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)^{d},

where

\mathfrak{A}_{q}

\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)

\mathcal{O}\left(d\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}_{q}^{-}

. Let us consider the canonical projection

is a Runge open subset.

, we conclude that

is a Runge open subset. Whence

is a Runge
q
-open subset.
∎

7.3.

\left(i,j\right)\neq\left(d,l\right)
. Then

\left(d^{0}\alpha\right)_{i,j}=\partial_{i,j}^{0}\alpha_{i,j}+M_{i,j-1}^{0}\alpha_{i,j-1}+N_{i-1,j}^{0}\alpha_{i-1,j}=\partial_{i,j}^{0}\alpha_{i,j}

for all
\left(i,j\right)\leq\left(d,l\right)
. It follows that

\left(\xi-d^{0}\alpha\right)_{i,j}=0
for all
\left(i,j\right)\leq\left(d,l\right)
. Thus, we come up with a new kernel element

\xi-d^{0}\alpha\in\ker\left(d^{1}\right)
whose entries are vanishing
till
\left(d,l\right)
included. Namely,
\xi-d^{0}\alpha\in\prod\limits_{\left(i,j\right)>\left(d,l\right)}\mathcal{O}_{i,j}^{\oplus 2}
. By induction on
\left(i,j\right)
, we obtain that

\xi-\sum_{k=1}^{n}d^{0}\alpha^{\left(k\right)}
is small enough in

\prod\limits_{\left(i,j\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}}\mathcal{O}_{i,j}^{\oplus 2}
for a sequence
\left\{\alpha^{\left(k\right)}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{q}^{\widehat{\otimes}2}
. Hence
\xi=\sum_{k}d^{0}\alpha^{\left(k\right)}
belongs to the closure of

\operatorname{im}\left(d^{0}\right)
. But
\operatorname{im}\left(d^{0}\right)
is closed. Hence
\xi\in\operatorname{im}\left(d^{0}\right)
.

Finally, the image
\operatorname{im}\left(d^{1}\right)
is closed thanks
to Proposition
7.2
, and
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
is dense in

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
by Proposition
7.1
. Using
Proposition
6.1
, we conclude
\operatorname{im}\left(d^{1}\right)=\ker\left(\pi\right)
. The fact that
\pi
is onto immediate. Whence
(
11
) for
\mathcal{A=F}_{q}
is exact.
∎

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{xy}
be a Runge
q
-open subset. Then both
projections
\pi_{x}:\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\left(U_{x}\right)\left[\left[y\right]\right]
,

and the canonical morphism

Proof.

One needs to use Theorem
7.2
and
[
33

.
∎

8.
The joint spectra

In this final section we introduce the joint spectra within the general
framework of noncommutative functional calculus considered in
[
13
]
.

Let
X
be a left Fréchet
\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
-module given by a couple
\left(T,S\right)
of continuous linear
operator on
X
such that
TS=q^{-1}ST
. For brevity we say that
X
is a left
Fréchet
q
-module. If
X
is a Banach
\mathfrak{A}_{q}
-module, then it
is a left Fréchet
q
-module automatically. Recall
[
14
]
that the
resolvent set
\operatorname{res}\left(T,S\right)
of these operators (or
the
q
-module
X
) is defined as a set of those
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}_{xy}

such that the transversality relation
\mathbb{C}\left(\gamma\right)\perp X
holds, that is,
\operatorname{Tor}_{k}^{\mathfrak{A}_{q}}\left(\mathbb{C}\left(\gamma\right),X\right)=\left\{0\right\}
for all

k\geq 0
. The complement

\sigma\left(T,S\right)=\mathbb{C}_{xy}^{2}\backslash\operatorname{res}\left(T,S\right)

defines the Taylor spectrum of the operator couple
\left(T,S\right)
.

The Putinar spectrum of a left Fréchet module with respect to a
Fréchet algebra sheaf was introduced in
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
,
that plays the central role in noncommutative functional calculus problems.
One can prove that every
q
-open subset of
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
is

\mathcal{F}_{q}
-acyclic, but we neglect this statement in this presentation
and introduce Putinar spectrum within the framework of the contractive

q
-plane. The resolvent set
\operatorname*{res}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
of the module
X
with respect to the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{q}

is defined as a subset of those
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}_{xy}
such that there
exists a
q
-open neighborhood
U_{\gamma}
of
\gamma
with the
transversality property
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(V\right)\perp X
for each

q
-open subset
V\subseteq U_{\gamma}
. By its very definition,

\operatorname*{res}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
is a
q
-open subset
whereas that is not the case for
\operatorname{res}\left(T,S\right)
(see
below Example). The complement

\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)=\mathbb{C}_{xy}\backslash\operatorname*{res}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)

is called the Putinar spectrum of the left Fréchet
q
-module
X
with
respect to the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{q}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{xy}
be a Runge
q
-open subset,

V\subseteq U
its
q
-open subset, and let
X
be a left Fréchet

q
-module. If
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)\perp X
then
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(V\right)\perp X
holds too.

Proof.

Put
A=\mathcal{O}_{q}\left(\mathbb{C}_{xy}\right)
to be an algebra of
finite type
[
33
, Definition 2.4]
. By the sheaf property of

\mathcal{F}_{q}
, we deduce that
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(V\right)
is a
Fréchet
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
-bimodule. Since
A\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
is a localization (Theorem
7.2
),
it follows that
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
is dominating over

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(V\right)

[
33
, Proposition 2.1]
. That is,

\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{A}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right),\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(V\right)\right)=\left\{0\right\}
,
n>0
, and
the natural mapping
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(V\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{A}\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(V\right)
,
\zeta\mapsto 1\otimes\zeta
is a topological isomorphism

[
33
, Definition 2.2]
. Taking into account the nuclearity property of

\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)
, the result follows from
[
33
, Proposition
2.7 (b)]
.
∎

Based on Lemma
8.1
, we obtain that
\gamma\in\operatorname*{res}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U_{\gamma}\right)\perp X
for a certain Runge
q
-open subset
U_{\gamma}

containing
\gamma
. Recall that the Runge
q
-open subsets form a topology
base of the
q
-topology in
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
. Thus
\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
is a
q
-closed subset of
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X
be a left Fréchet
q
-module given by an operator
couple
\left(T,S\right)
. Then
\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}\subseteq\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
holds, where
\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}
is the
q
-closure of the Taylor spectrum
\sigma\left(T,S\right)
. If
X
is a left Banach
q
-module, then

\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)=\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}.

Proof.

First take
\gamma\in\operatorname*{res}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
.
Then
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U_{\gamma}\right)\perp X
for a certain Runge

q
-open subset
U_{\gamma}
containing
\gamma
. Since
\mathbb{C}\left(\gamma\right)
is a Fréchet
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U_{\gamma}\right)
-bimodule and
\mathfrak{A}_{q}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U\right)

is a localization (Theorem
7.2
), it follows that
\mathbb{C}\left(\gamma\right)\perp X
holds too (see
[
33
, Proposition 2.7 (b)]
). Thus

\operatorname*{res}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)\subseteq\operatorname{res}\left(T,S\right)
or
\sigma\left(T,S\right)\subseteq\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
. But
\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
is a
q
-closed subset (Lemma
8.1
),
therefore
\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}\subseteq\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
.

Further, assume that
X
is a left Banach
q
-module, and take
\gamma\notin\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}
. Then
U_{\gamma}\cap\sigma\left(T,S\right)=\varnothing
for a certain Runge
q
-open subset
U_{\gamma}

containing
\gamma
. It follows that
\mathbb{C}\left(\lambda\right)\perp X
for all
\lambda\in U_{\gamma}
. Using
[
16
, Theorem 6.2]
, we
deduce that
\mathcal{F}_{q}\left(U_{\gamma}\right)\perp X
. By Lemma

8.1
, we conclude that
\gamma\in\operatorname*{res}\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)
. Whence
\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)=\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}
.
∎

Now let us illustrate the statement of Theorem
8.1
in the following
example to see the gap between these spectra.

Example.
Put
X=\ell_{p}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
,
1\leq p<\infty
with its standard topological basis
\left\{e_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
, and consider the unilateral shift operator
T
and the
diagonal
q
-operator
S
on
\ell_{p}
. Thus
T,S\in\mathcal{B}\left(X\right)
with
T\left(e_{n}\right)=e_{n+1}
and
S\left(e_{n}\right)=q^{n}e_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. In the case of
\left|q\right|<1
, the operator
S
is compact with its spectrum
\sigma\left(S\right)=\left\{q^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right\}\cup\left\{0\right\}
, which is the
q
-hull of
\left\{1\right\}
, and
\sigma\left(T\right)=\mathbb{D}_{1}
is the closed unit disk, which is
q
-dense in

\mathbb{C}
. Since
TS=q^{-1}ST
, it follows that
X
is a left Banach

q
-module. It is proved (see
[
14
]
) that

\sigma\left(T,S\right)=\left(\left(\mathbb{D}_{\left|q\right|^{-1}}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\circ}\right)\times\left\{0\right\}\right)\cup\left\{\left(0,1\right)\right\},

where
\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\circ}=B\left(0,1\right)
. The
q
-closure of

\sigma\left(T,S\right)\cap\mathbb{C}_{x}
equals to the complement of the
disk
\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\circ}\times\left\{0\right\}
. Indeed, for every

z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\left|z\right|>\left|q\right|^{-1}

there is a positive integer
n\geq 1
such that
\left|q\right|^{-n}<\left|z\right|\leq\left|q\right|^{-n-1}
. It follows
that
q^{n}z\in\mathbb{D}_{\left|q\right|^{-1}}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\circ}
or
\left(q^{n}z,0\right)\in\sigma\left(T,S\right)
. Thus
\left(z,0\right)=q^{-n}\left(q^{n}z,0\right)\in\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}
(see Subsection
2.4
). Moreover,
the
q
-closure of
\sigma\left(T,S\right)\cap\mathbb{C}_{y}
equals to

\left\{\left(0,q^{-n}\right):n\geq 0\right\}
. By Theorem
8.1
,
we conclude that

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}_{q},X\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(T,S\right)^{-q}=\left(\left(\mathbb{D}_{\left|q\right|^{-1}}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\circ}\right)\times\left\{0\right\}\right)^{-q}\cup\left\{\left(0,1\right)\right\}^{-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\circ}\times\left\{0\right\}\right)\cup\left\{\left(0,q^{-n}\right):n\geq 0\right\}.

The concluding remark.
It turns out that the Taylor spectrum

\sigma\left(T,S\right)
in the
q
-theory could admit a large
q
-closure
that results in the Putinar spectrum. This moment explains the lack of the
conventional projection property for the Taylor spectrum observed in

[
14
]
. Since Putinar spectrum plays a central role in noncommutative
functional calculus problem and the spectral mapping property
[
13
]
,
we are expecting some key properties for the
q
-closure of
\sigma\left(T,S\right)
rather than itself. Certainly, this serious gap between spectra
is mainly caused by the weak, non-Hausdorff
q
-topology of
\mathbb{C}_{xy}
.
This is a new phenomenon of noncommutative analytic geometry, which makes the

q
-theory more attractive and interesting.

Covering theory, functor categories and the Krull-Gabriel dimension
Grzegorz Pastuszak *

Abstract
Assume that K K is an algebraically closed field, R R a locally bounded K K -category, G G an admissible group of K K -linear automorphisms of R R and F : R → A ≅ R / G F:R\rightarrow A\cong R/\penalty G the associated Galois G G -covering functor. In the first part of the paper we show that KG ⁡ ( R ) ≤ KG ⁡ ( A ) \KG(R)\leq\KG(A) where KG \KG denotes the Krull-Gabriel dimension . In other words, we obtain a general fact that the Galois covering functors do not increase the Krull-Gabriel dimension. This result is proved by developing the Galois covering theory of functor categories , based on the existence of the tensor product bifunctor for categories of modules over small K K -categories. We understand this theory as the theory of the left and the right adjoint functors Φ , Θ : MOD ⁡ ( ℛ ) → MOD ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) \Phi,\Theta:{\rm MOD}({\cal R})\rightarrow{\rm MOD}({\cal A}) to the pull-up functor Ψ = ( F λ ) ∙ : MOD ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) → MOD ⁡ ( ℛ ) \Psi=(F_{\lambda})_{\bullet}:{\rm MOD}({\cal A})\rightarrow{\rm MOD}({\cal R}) , along the classical push-down functor F λ : ℛ → 𝒜 F_{\lambda}:{\cal R}\rightarrow{\cal A} where ℛ = mod ⁡ ( R ) {\cal R}=\mod(R) , 𝒜 = mod ⁡ ( A ) {\cal A}=\mod(A) and ( F λ ) ∙ = ( − ) ∘ F λ (F_{\lambda})_{\bullet}=(-)\circ F_{\lambda} . In the case F λ F_{\lambda} is dense, Φ \Phi and Θ \Theta are natural generalizations of the classical push-down functors F λ F_{\lambda} and F ρ F_{\rho} to the level of functor categories. Generally, Φ \Phi and Θ \Theta restrict to categories ℱ ⁡ ( R ) , ℱ ⁡ ( A ) {\cal F}(R),{\cal F}(A) of finitely presented functors and the restricted functors Φ , Θ : ℱ ⁡ ( R ) → ℱ ⁡ ( A ) \Phi,\Theta:{\cal F}(R)\rightarrow{\cal F}(A) coincide. The second part of the paper is devoted to studying the functor Φ : ℱ ⁡ ( R ) → ℱ ⁡ ( A ) \Phi:{\cal F}(R)\rightarrow{\cal F}(A) . First we show that Φ \Phi is a Galois G G -precovering of functor categories. Then we consider an important special case when the category R R is simply connected and locally representation-finite. In this case, Φ : ℱ ⁡ ( R ) → ℱ ⁡ ( A ) \Phi:{\cal F}(R)\rightarrow{\cal F}(A) may be studied in terms of classical covering theory which allows to give an example of Φ \Phi which is not dense. This justifies the introduction of the functors of the first kind and the functors of the second kind , following the terminology of Dowbor and Skowroński. In the final part of the paper, we give applications of our results in determining the Krull-Gabriel dimension and the representation type of some classes of algebras. Last but not least, we discuss applications to the conjecture of M. Prest, relating the Krull-Gabriel dimension of an algebra with its representation type.

3
General covering theory of functor categories

In this section we prove our first main results. We start with recalling the existence of the
tensor product bifunctor

[

,
37
]
for categories of modules over arbitrary small

-categories, abelian

-categories in particular. Then we determine in Theorem

the left and the right adjoint to the pull-up functor. This fact is already proved in
[

, but it seems not to be widely recognized. As a direct consequence of Theorem

is the given Galois

4.2
The case of dense push-down functor

Throughout the section we assume that
R
is a locally bounded
K
-category,
G
an admissible group of
K
-linear automorphisms of
R
and
F:R\rightarrow A\cong R/\penalty G
the Galois covering.

The following Theorem
4.5
widely generalizes a part of the assertion
(2)
of Theorem
4.3
, stating only the existence of an isomorphism
\Psi(\Phi(T))\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}gT
, for any finitely presented
T\in{\cal F}(R)
. This result is further applied to show that if the push-down functor
F_{\lambda}:\mod(R)\rightarrow\mod(A)
is dense, then the functors
\Phi,\Theta:{\rm MOD}({\cal R})\rightarrow{\rm MOD}({\cal A})
have descriptions analogous to the classical covering functors, see Corollary
4.6
. In the dense case, this also yields a simpler proof of the fact that
\Phi
and
\Theta
are equivalent on the category
{\cal F}(R)
of finitely presented functors, proved in general in Theorem
3.7
(1), and hence that they are exact functors, see Theorem
4.7
.

Theorem 4.5
.

For any functor
T\in{\rm MOD}({\cal R})
we have natural isomorphisms of functors

\Psi(\Phi(T))=\Phi(T)\circ F_{\lambda}\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}gT\textnormal{ and }\Psi(\Theta(T))=\Theta(T)\circ F_{\lambda}\cong\prod_{g\in G}gT

which extend to natural equivalences

\Psi(\Phi(\cdot))\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}g(\cdot)\textnormal{ and }\Psi(\Theta(\cdot))\cong\prod_{g\in G}g(\cdot).

Proof.

Let
T\in{\rm MOD}({\cal R})
and
?\in\mod(R)
. We show that
\Psi(\Phi(T))\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}gT
. Indeed, first observe we have the following natural isomorphisms:

\Psi(\Phi(T))=\Phi(T)\circ F_{\lambda}=T\otimes_{{\cal R}}[{}_{A}(F_{\lambda}(?),F_{\lambda}(*))]\cong T\otimes_{{\cal R}}[{}_{R}(F_{\bullet}(F_{\lambda}(?)),*)]\cong

\cong T\otimes_{{\cal R}}[{}_{R}(\bigoplus_{g\in G}{}^{g}?,*)]\cong T\otimes_{{\cal R}}[\prod_{g\in G}{}_{R}({}^{g}?,*)].

Proposition
4.1
(2) yields
\prod_{g\in G}{}_{R}({}^{g}?,*)\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}{}_{R}({}^{g}?,*)
, because for any
M,X\in\mod(R)
there is only a finite number of
g\in G
such that
{}_{R}({}^{g}X,M)\neq 0
. Therefore we obtain

T\otimes_{{\cal R}}[\prod_{g\in G}{}_{R}({}^{g}?,*)]\cong T\otimes_{{\cal R}}[\bigoplus_{g\in G}{}_{R}({}^{g}?,*)]\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}T\otimes_{{\cal R}}[{}_{R}({}^{g}?,*)]\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}T(^{g}?)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}gT,

which follows from the fact that the tensor product commutes with colimits and also from the co-Yoneda lemma, respectively.

Now we show that
\Psi(\Theta(T))\cong\prod_{g\in G}gT
. Indeed, some standard arguments and the Yoneda lemma yield the natural isomorphisms:

\Psi(\Theta(T))=\Theta(T)\circ F_{\lambda}={}_{{\cal R}}({}_{A}(F_{\lambda}(*),F_{\lambda}(?)),T)\cong{}_{{\cal R}}({}_{R}(*,F_{\bullet}(F_{\lambda}(?))),T)\cong

\cong{}_{{\cal R}}({}_{R}(*,\bigoplus_{g\in G}{}^{g}{?}),T)\cong{}_{{\cal R}}(\bigoplus_{g\in G}{}_{R}(*,{}^{g}{?}),T)\cong\prod_{g\in G}{}_{{\cal R}}({}_{R}(*,{}^{g}{?}),T)\cong\prod_{g\in G}T(^{g}?)=\prod_{g\in G}gT.

The second part of the thesis follows from similar arguments, because if
T_{1},T_{2}\in{\rm MOD}(R)
and
\iota:T_{1}\rightarrow T_{2}
is a morphism of functors, then for any
M\in\mod(R)
we have

\iota\otimes_{{\cal R}}[{}_{R}(M,*)]\cong\iota_{M}\textnormal{ and }{}_{{\cal R}}({}_{R}(*,M),\iota)\cong\iota_{M},

by the co-Yoneda and Yoneda lemmas, respectively. In consequence, we obtain the isomorphisms
\Psi(\Phi(\iota))\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}g\iota
and
\Psi(\Theta(\iota))\cong\prod_{g\in G}g\iota
which shows the claim.
∎

It is convenient to formulate the following straightforward corollary of Theorem
4.5
, giving an exact description of the functors
\Phi,\Theta:{\rm MOD}({\cal R})\rightarrow{\rm MOD}({\cal A})
on the category of
A
-modules of the first kind. This description is purely analogous to the case of classical covering functors. In some sense, it also generalizes and retrieves the construction of the functor
\Phi:{\cal F}(R)\rightarrow{\cal F}(A)
, as given in Section 5 of
[
40
]
.

Corollary 4.6
.

The functors
\Phi,\Theta:{\rm MOD}({\cal R})\rightarrow{\rm MOD}({\cal A})
have the following properties.

(1)

Assume that
T\in{\rm MOD}({\cal R})
and
M\in\mod(R)
. Then we have:

\Phi(T)(F_{\lambda}(M))\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}T({}^{g}M)\textnormal{ and }\Theta(T)(F_{\lambda}(M))\cong\prod_{g\in G}T({}^{g}M).

(2)

Assume that
\alpha:F_{\lambda}(M)\rightarrow F_{\lambda}(N)
is an
A
-homomorphism such that
\alpha=\sum_{g\in G}F_{\lambda}(f_{g})
where
f_{g}:{}^{g}M\rightarrow N

. Then we have:

\Phi(T)(\alpha)\cong\sum_{g\in G}\bigoplus_{h\in G}T({}^{h}f_{g})\textnormal{ and }\Theta(T)(\alpha)\cong\sum_{g\in G}\prod_{h\in G}T({}^{h}f_{g}).

(3)

Assume that
T_{1},T_{2}\in{\rm MOD}({\cal R})
,
\iota:T_{1}\rightarrow T_{2}
is a morphism of functors and let

. Then we have:

\Phi(\iota)_{F_{\lambda}(M)}\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}\iota_{{}^{g}M}\textnormal{ and }\Theta(\iota)_{F_{\lambda}(M)}\cong\prod_{g\in G}\iota_{{}^{g}M}.

Proof.

We show that the assertions
(1)
,
(2)
and
(3)
follow directly from Theorem
4.5
. Indeed, observe that

\Phi(T)(F_{\lambda}(M))=\Psi(\Phi(T))(M)\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}gT(M)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}T({}^{g}M)\textnormal{, and moreover}

\Phi(T)(\alpha)=\Phi(T)(\sum_{g\in G}F_{\lambda}(f_{g}))=\sum_{g\in G}\Phi(T)(F_{\lambda}(f_{g}))\cong

\cong\sum_{g\in G}\Psi(\Phi(T))(f_{g})\cong\sum_{g\in G}\bigoplus_{h\in G}hT(f_{g})=\sum_{g\in G}\bigoplus_{h\in G}T({}^{h}f_{g}).

This shows that the homomorphism

is defined by

, for any

\Phi(\iota)_{F_{\lambda}(M)}\cong\Psi(\Phi(\iota))_{M}\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}(g\iota)_{M}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\iota_{{}^{g}M}

which implies that the homomorphism

\Phi(\iota)_{F_{\lambda}(M)}:\bigoplus_{g\in G}T_{1}({}^{g}M)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{g\in G}T_{2}({}^{g}M)

is defined by
\iota_{{}^{g}M}:T_{1}({}^{g}M)\rightarrow T_{2}({}^{g}M)
, for any

. It is easy to see that all the isomorphisms for the functor

have analogous proofs.
∎

The above facts show in particular that for any functor

Theorem 4.7

Assume that the push-down functor

\Theta:{\rm MOD}({\cal R})\rightarrow{\rm MOD}({\cal A})

It follows from Theorem
4.5

∎

Remark 4.9
.

Theorem

4.3

Theorems

3.8

the functor
\Phi:{\cal F}(R)\rightarrow{\cal F}(A)
is exact and faithful and hence
\KG(R)\leq\KG(A)
. It is shown in
[
42
]
that the opposite inequality
\KG(A)\leq\KG(R)
holds in case
R
is locally support-finite, intervally finite and the group
G
is torsion-free. Since these conditions imply the density of the push-down
F_{\lambda}:\mod(R)\rightarrow\mod(A)

We introduce some terminology. Assume that
A,B
are some locally bounded
K
-categories and let
\varphi\colon\mod(A)\rightarrow\mod(B)
be a
K
-linear additive covariant functor. We define
\Lambda_{\varphi}\colon{\cal F}(B)\rightarrow{\cal G}(A)
as the composition functor

. Observe that if
U\in{\cal F}(B)
and
{}_{B}(-,X)\xrightarrow{{}_{B}(-,f)}{}_{B}(-,Y)\rightarrow U\rightarrow 0
, then we have

{}_{B}(\varphi(-),X)\xrightarrow{{}_{B}(\varphi(-),f)}{}_{B}(\varphi(-),Y)\rightarrow U\varphi=\Lambda_{\varphi}(U)\rightarrow 0.

We call the functor
\varphi\colon\mod(A)\rightarrow\mod(B)

if and only if
\varphi
is dense and
\textnormal{Im}(\Lambda_{\varphi})\subseteq{\cal F}(A)
(the terminology is introduced in
[
28
]
). This means that
\Lambda_{\varphi}
preserves finitely presented functors. Recall that if
\varphi\colon\mod(A)\rightarrow\mod(B)
is admissible, then
\KG(B)\leq\KG(A)
, because the functor
\Lambda_{\varphi}\colon{\cal F}(B)\rightarrow{\cal F}(A)
is exact and faithful, see the remark following
[
42
, Theorem 1.3]
for details.

Assume that
G
is an admissible group of
K
-linear automorphisms of
R
. A finite convex subcategory
B
of the category
R
is called
fundamental domain

[
42
]
if and only if for any

there exists

such that
\mbox{\rm supp}({}^{g}M)\subseteq B
. We say that a locally bounded
K
-category

is

[
9

Theorem 4.10

Assume that
R
is a locally support-finite and intervally finite locally bounded

G

(1)

19

19

5
The functors of the first and the second kind

This section presents examples of Galois coverings

Therefore it is natural to introduce the following terminology, originated in
[
13
]
for the case of modules over locally bounded
K
-categories.

Definition 5.1
.

Assume that
F:R\rightarrow A
is a Galois
G
-covering with a torsion-free group
G
and let
\Phi_{F}:{\cal F}(R)\rightarrow{\cal F}(A)
be the associated Galois

if and only if

lies in the image of

. Otherwise, we call

a

Recall from Proposition

that categories of finitely presented functors are abelian Krull-Schmidt categories. This implies that the density of

5.1
The locally representation-finite case

We formulate the following well-known fact and give its simple proof for convenience.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
R
is a locally bounded
K
-category. Then
R
is locally representation-finite if and only if the category
{\rm ind}(R)
is locally bounded.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
{\rm ind}(R)
is locally bounded if and only if the hom-functors
{}_{R}(*,X)
and
{}_{R}(X,*)
have finite supports, for any
X\in{\rm ind}(R)
.

Assume that
R
is locally representation-finite and let
X\in{\rm ind}(R)
. We show that the functor
{}_{R}(*,X)
has finite support (arguments for the functor
{}_{R}(X,*)
are analogous). Indeed, let
R_{a}
be the finite set of all indecomposable modules in
\mod(R)
which are nonzero in
a\in\ob(R)
. Let
{\cal X}
be the union of all the sets

such that

. If

and
{}_{R}(Y,X)\neq 0
, then
\mbox{\rm supp}(X)\cap\mbox{\rm supp}(Y)\neq\emptyset
and hence

. Since
{\cal X}
is finite, we conclude that
{}_{R}(*,X)
has finite support. Thus
{\rm ind}(R)
is locally bounded.

On the other hand, assume that the hom-functors have finite supports. We prove that
R
is locally representation-finite. Let

and recall that
X(a)\cong{}_{R}(P_{a},X)
, for any indecomposable module
X\in\mod(R)
, and hence
X(a)\neq 0
if and only if
{}_{R}(P_{a},X)\neq 0
. Since the support of the functor
{}_{R}(P_{a},*)
is finite, we conclude that there is a finite number of indecomposable
R
-modules

with

. Thus

is locally representation-finite.
∎

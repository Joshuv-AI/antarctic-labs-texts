Abstract
. We develop a topological framework in an attempt to generalize the classical colourful Carathéodory theorem by imposing an additional constraint. For that we introduce the notion of zero-avoding complexes and covering criteria for the existence of colourful transversals. Using the developed method in combination with the homological Nerve theorem of Meshulam we recover all known versions of the colourful Carathéodory’s theorem and prove a constraint extension which, in particular, implies an extension of the original (affine) Tverberg result.

The general colourful Carathéodory’s problem

The main result of the classical 1982 work of Bárány
[

Theorem 1.1

(The colourful Carathéodory I)
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
d\geq 1

A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

0\in A(J)
where
J=\conv\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}\subseteq\Delta_{N}
is the face of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
spanned by the selected vertices
p_{1},\dots,p_{r}
.

Yet another intriguing extension is due to Kalai & Meshulam

For the proper statement of the result we need to introduce the notion of a matroid.

An abstract simplicial complex
\mathcal{M}

To every matroid

\rho_{\mathcal{M}}\colon 2^{M}-\{\emptyset\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}

\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(S):=\dim(\mathcal{M}[S])+1

for every

.
Recall, here
\mathcal{M}[S]
denotes the induced sub-complex of
\mathcal{M}
on
S
.

As an illustration
of a matroid we give a relevant example: Let
k\geq 1
be an integer and let

be a collection of

The join simplicial complex

is the so-called partition matroid, see Figure

(The colourful Carathéodory IV)
.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer, and let

be an affine map.
Suppose that the matroid
\mathcal{M}
is a sub-complex of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
with

.

If for every face
S
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
with the property that
\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(S)=\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
and
\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(M-S)\leq d
it holds that
0\in A(S)

J
of the matroid
\mathcal{M}
such that
0\in A(J)
.

Summarizing the previous results we see that we always begin with an affine map
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
from a simplex into some Euclidean space, and then we fix two sets of data

–

a simplical sub-complex
\mathcal{K}
of
\Delta_{N}

\vertex(\mathcal{K})=\vertex(\Delta_{N})

–

a family of faces

\Delta_{N}
with the property that for every

we have
0\in A(S)
.

Note that the second set of data also restricts the class of affine maps which can be considered.

We hope to find a face
J
of

with the property that
0\in A(J)

In this language the previous results correspond to the following setups

– Theorem

\vertex(\Delta_{N})=\vertex(F_{1})\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\vertex(F_{r})

\mathcal{S}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{r}\}
, and
\mathcal{K}=\vertex(F_{1})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r})

– Theorem

\vertex(\Delta_{N})=\vertex(F_{1})\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\vertex(F_{r-1})\sqcup\{p\}

\mathcal{S}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{r-1}\}
, and
\mathcal{K}=\vertex(F_{1})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r-1})*\{p\}

– Theorem
1.3
:

\vertex(\Delta_{N})=\vertex(F_{1})\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\vertex(F_{r})

\mathcal{S}=\{F_{i}\cup F_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq r\}
, and
\mathcal{K}=\vertex(F_{1})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r})

– Theorem

:

\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{M}
is a matroid with
\vertex(\mathcal{M})=\vertex(\Delta_{N})
and
\mathcal{S}=\{S\subseteq\Delta_{N}:\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(S)=\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(M),\,\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(M-S)\leq d\}
.

Thus, the general colourful Carathéodory problem can be formulated as follows.

Problem 1.5

(The general colourful Carathéodory problem)
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
d\geq 1
be integers.
Find all simplicial sub-complexes
\mathcal{K}
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
, with
\vertex(\mathcal{K})=\vertex(\Delta_{N})
, and all families
\mathcal{S}
of faces of
\Delta_{N}
such that the following implication holds: If an affine map
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies that
0\in A(S)
for every face
S\in\mathcal{S}
, then there exists a face
J
of
\mathcal{K}
with
0\in A(J)
.

In this paper, building on the ideas of Kalai & Meshulam
[
18
]
, we develop an algorithm for solving Problem
1.5
.
Then, using this method and the homological Nerve theorem of Meshulam
[
21
]
we reprove all known results and also prove the following new generalisation of the colourful Carathéodory theorem.
As a consequence we obtain the corresponding extension of the classical Tverberg theorem.

1.2.
A constrained colourful Carathéodory theorem

The central result of this paper is the following constrained colourful Carathéodory theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
d\geq 2
and
r\geq d+1
be integers, and let
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an affine map.
Suppose that

(1)

F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
is a collection of pairwise disjoint non-empty faces of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
such that

0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r})
, and

(2)

\mathcal{K}
is a simplicial sub-complex
L*\vertex(F_{3})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r})
of
\Delta_{N}
where
L
is a path-connected sub-complex of the join complex
\vertex(F_{1})*\vertex(F_{2})
with
\vertex(L)=\vertex(F_{1})\cup\vertex(F_{2})
.

Then there exists a face
J
of
\mathcal{K}
with
0\in A(J)
.

The example in Figure

1.6
is not a consequence of the matroid version of the colourful Carathéodory theorem

.
Further, an illustration of Theorem

2.
Zero-avoiding complexes, its Alexander duals and induced sub-complexes

We begin by introducing the notion of a zero-avoiding simplicial complex associated to an affine map from a simplex into an affine Euclidean space.

2.1.
Definition of zero-avoiding complexes

Let
\mathbb{E}
be a finite dimensional affine Euclidean space with the fixed structure of a real vector space.
In particular, we know what is
0\in\mathbb{E}
.
The standard scalar product on the vector space structure of
\mathbb{E}
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Let
V
be a finite set (in
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and
\Delta_{V}
the simplex with the vertex set
V
.
Without loss of generality we can assume that
V:=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
e_{i}
is the
i
-th vector of the standard base
(e_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N})
of
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
.
Fix an affine map
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
with the property that
A(V)\subseteq\mathbb{E}-\{0\}
.
An (abstract) simplicial complex on the set of vertices
V
defined by

\mathscr{C}_{A}:=\{U\subseteq V:0\notin\conv(A(U))=A(\conv(U))\}

(1)

is called the
zero-avoiding complex
of the affine map
A
.
The set
V
is indeed the set of vertices of
\mathscr{C}_{V}
because
0\notin A(V)
.
Furthermore,
0\notin\conv(A(U))
implies that
0\notin\conv(A(U^{\prime}))
for every subset
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
, so
\mathscr{C}_{A}
is an abstract simplicial complex as announced.

For example, let
\mathbb{E}\cong\mathbb{R}
.
Consider
V_{1}=\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and
V_{2}=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{k}\}
with affine maps
A_{1}\colon\Delta_{V_{1}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
,
A_{1}(e_{1})=-1
,
A_{1}(e_{2})=1
and
A_{2}\colon\Delta_{V_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
,
A_{2}(e_{i})=i
for
0\leq i\leq s
.
Then
\mathscr{C}_{A_{1}}=[2]
is the two point space and
\mathscr{C}_{A_{2}}=\Delta_{|V_{2}|}
is a
(k-1)
-dimensional simplex.

Now consider the “normalisation” of an affine map
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
onto the unit sphere
S(\mathbb{E})
, that is the affine map
\overline{A}\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
defined, on the vertices, by

\overline{A}(v):=\frac{A(v)}{\|A(v)\|}\ \in\ S(\mathbb{E})

for all
v\in V
.
It is important to notice that zero-avoiding complexes of
A
and
\overline{A}
coincide, or in other words
\mathscr{C}_{A}=\mathscr{C}_{\overline{A}}
.
Indeed, for any collection of points
u_{1},\dots,u_{s}\in\mathbb{E}-\{0\}
it holds that:

0\in\conv\{u_{1},\dots,u_{s}\}\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad 0\in\conv\Big\{\frac{u_{1}}{\|u_{1}\|},\dots,\frac{u_{s}}{\|u_{s}\|}\Big\}.

Consequently, without loss of generality, from now on, we can assume that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.

2.2.
Families of open and closed hemispheres

In order to understand the topology of the complex
\mathscr{C}_{A}
we make a detour and first study families of open and closed hemispheres on the unit sphere
S(\mathbb{E})
and their unions and intersections.

For a unit vector
u\in S(\mathbb{E})
we denote the associated open and closed hemisphere of
S(\mathbb{E})
by

\mathscr{B}_{u}:=\{x\in S(\mathbb{E}):\langle u,x\rangle>0\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathscr{D}_{u}:=\{x\in S(\mathbb{E}):\langle u,x\rangle\geq 0\},

respectively.
Evidently,
\mathscr{B}_{u}\subseteq\mathscr{D}_{u}
.
If
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
is a non-empty finite collection of unit vectors in
\mathbb{E}
then we set

\mathscr{B}(U):=\bigcap_{u\in U}\mathscr{B}_{u},\qquad\mathscr{D}(U):=\bigcap_{u\in U}\mathscr{D}_{u}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle:=\bigcup_{u\in U}\mathscr{B}_{u},\qquad\mathscr{D}\langle U\rangle:=\bigcup_{u\in U}\mathscr{D}_{u}.

It is evident that each
\mathscr{B}(U)
is either an open
(m-1)
-dimensional ball or empty.
On the other hand nothing can be said a priori about
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
besides that it is a non-empty open subset of the sphere
S(\mathbb{E})
.
Therefore, we mostly focus our attention on understanding the topology of the unions.

The first non-elementary observation gives the criterion for non-emptiness of
\mathscr{B}(U)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
be a finite collection of points.
Then
\mathscr{B}(U)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
0\notin\conv(U)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathscr{B}(U)\neq\emptyset
, or in other words
\langle x,u\rangle>0
for all
u\in U
.
Consider the open half-space

H^{+}=\{y\in\mathbb{E}:\langle x,y\rangle>0\}

determined by
x
.
Then
U\subseteq H^{+}
and so
\conv(U)\subseteq\conv(H^{+})=H^{+}
.
Since
0\notin H^{+}
it follows that
0\notin\conv(U)
as desired.

Conversely, suppose that
0\notin\conv(U)
.
Then there exists a hyperplane
H=\{y\in\mathbb{E}:\langle x_{0},y\rangle=0\}
, for some
x_{0}\in S(\mathbb{E})
, with the property that
\conv(U)\subseteq H^{+}=\{y\in\mathbb{E}:\langle x_{0},y\rangle>0\}
.
Since
U\subseteq\conv(U)
it follows that
\langle x_{0},u\rangle>0
for every
u\in U
.
Consequently,
x_{0}\in\bigcap_{u\in U}\mathscr{B}_{u}=\mathscr{B}(U)
, and so
\mathscr{B}(U)\neq\emptyset
, as claimed.

Note that for
x_{0}
we can take the vector
\frac{\overrightarrow{0\,p_{\conv(U)}(0)}}{\|\overrightarrow{0\,p_{\conv(U)}(0)}\|}
,
where
p_{\conv(U)}
denotes the metric projection of the convex body
\conv(U)
.
For a definition and properties of the metric projection, consult for example
[
15
, Sec. 4.1]
.
∎

In the case of families of closed hemispheres the situation is a bit more delicate as we will see in the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
be a finite collection of points.
Then
\mathscr{D}(U)\neq\emptyset
if and only if there exists a vector
x\in S(\mathbb{E})
such that
U\subseteq\mathscr{D}_{x}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathscr{D}(U)\neq\emptyset
.
Then for every
u\in U
holds
\langle x,u\rangle\geq 0
.
Hence,
U\subseteq\{y\in S(\mathbb{E}):\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\}=\mathscr{D}_{x}
, as claimed.

Suppose that
U\subseteq\mathscr{D}_{x}=\{y\in S(\mathbb{E}):\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\}
for some
x\in S(\mathbb{E})
.
Then
\langle x,u\rangle\geq 0
for every
u\in U
implying that
x\in\mathscr{D}(U)
.
Hence,
\mathscr{D}(U)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Unlike the case of open hemisphere the intersection of closed hemispheres is not always empty or contractible.
For the complete description we recall the notion of Robinson (spherical) convexity
[
13
, Sec. 9.1. (R)]
and immediately have the following claim.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
be a finite collection of points, and assume that
\mathscr{D}(U)\neq\emptyset
.
Then
\mathscr{D}(U)
is Robinson convex and so either contractible or equal to an equatorial sphere
S^{\ell}
for some
0\leq\ell\leq\dim(\mathbb{E})-2
.
In particular, if
U
does not contain any pair of antipodal points, in other words if
U\cap(-U)=\emptyset
, then
\mathscr{D}(U)
is contractible.
Here,
-U:=\{-u:u\in U\}
.

Switching our attention to the topology of the unions of families of open hemispheres we start with a criterion for
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
to cover the sphere
S(\mathbb{E})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
be a finite collection of points.
Then
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle=S(\mathbb{E})
if and only if
0\in\interior(\conv(U))
.

Proof.

We prove the left-to-right implication via contraposition.
In other words, we prove the implication:

0\notin\interior(\conv(U))\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\neq S(\mathbb{E}).

Suppose
0\notin\interior(\conv(U))
.
Then either

\interior(\conv(U))=\emptyset\qquad\text{or}\qquad\interior(\conv(U))\subseteq H^{+}=\{y\in\mathbb{E}:\langle x_{0},y\rangle>0\}

for some
x_{0}\in S(\mathbb{E})
.
In both cases, by choosing
x_{0}
appropriately, we can guarantee that

\emptyset\neq\conv(U)\ \subseteq\ \cl(H^{+})=\{y\in\mathbb{E}:\langle x_{0},y\rangle\geq 0\}.

Consequently, for every
u\in U\subseteq\conv(U)
holds
\langle-x_{0},u\rangle\leq 0
.
In other words,
-x_{0}\notin\bigcup_{u\in U}\mathscr{B}_{u}=\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
, which implies that
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\neq S(\mathbb{E})
.

Conversely, assume that
0\in\interior(\conv(U))
.
Then, according to
[
20
, Thm. 6]
, for every
u\in U
there exists
t_{u}>0
with the property that

\sum_{u\in U}t_{u}\cdot u=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{u\in U}t_{u}=1.

Take an arbitrary point
y\in S(\mathbb{E})
, and compute

0=\langle 0\,,\,y\rangle=\big\langle\sum_{u\in U}t_{u}\cdot u\,,\,y\big\rangle=\sum_{u\in U}t_{u}\cdot\langle u,y\rangle.

Now, either
\langle u,y\rangle=0
for all
u\in U
, or there exists at least one
u_{0}\in U
with the property that
\langle u_{0},y\rangle>0
.
Since,
0\in\interior(\conv(U))
we have that
U
positively spans
\mathbb{E}
(as a vector space) and so indeed there exists some
u_{0}\in U
such that
\langle u_{0},y\rangle>0
.
Hence,
y\in\mathscr{B}_{u_{0}}\subseteq\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
implying that
S(\mathbb{E})=\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
.
∎

From the previous two lemmas it is apparent that if
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle=S(\mathbb{E})
, then
\mathscr{B}(U)=\emptyset
.
Furthermore, the previous lemma indicates that we should next discuss the topology of
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
in the case when
0\notin\interior(\conv(U))
.
This will be done in several steps starting with an extension of the previous lemma to the case when
0\in\relint(\conv(U))
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
be a finite collection of points.
If
0\in\relint(\conv(U))
, then
S(\mathbb{E})=\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\cup S
and
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\cap S=\emptyset
, where

S=\{y\in S(\mathbb{E}):(\forall u\in U)\ \langle y,u\rangle=0\}

is the unit sphere in the orthogonal complement of
\sspan(U)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in S(\mathbb{E})-S
.
Then, there exists
u_{0}\in U
with the property that
\langle u_{0},x\rangle\neq 0
.
Since
0\in\relint(\conv(U))
then for every
u\in U
there is
t_{u}>0
such that

\sum_{u\in U}t_{u}\cdot u=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{u\in U}t_{u}=1,

consult
[
20
, Thm. 6]
.
Like in the proof of the previous lemma we get that

0=\langle 0\,,\,x\rangle=\big\langle\sum_{u\in U}t_{u}\cdot u\,,\,x\big\rangle=\sum_{u\in U}t_{u}\,\langle u,x\rangle.

Since
\langle u_{0},x\rangle\neq 0
there is
u_{1}\in U
so that
\langle u_{1},x\rangle>0
.
Hence,
x\in\mathscr{B}_{u_{1}}\subseteq\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
.
∎

Next we consider the case when
0\in\conv(U)-\relint(\conv(U))
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
be a finite collection of points.
If
0\in\conv(U)-\relint(\conv(U))
, then
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
is a contractible space.

Proof.

Let
0\in\conv(U)-\relint(\conv(U))
.
Then there exists a unique maximal proper subset
U^{\prime}\subsetneq U
with the property
0\in\relint\conv(U^{\prime})
.
In particular,
\conv(U^{\prime})
is a face of the polytope
\conv(U)
.
Take
H:=\{y\in\mathbb{E}:\langle y,z\rangle=0\}
to be a support hyperplane of
\conv(U)
such that
\conv(U^{\prime})=\conv(U)\cap H
and
\conv(U)\subseteq H^{+}:=\{y\in\mathbb{E}:\langle y,z\rangle\geq 0\}
.
The maximality of
U^{\prime}
implies that
U^{\prime}=U\cap H
.
Now we have that:

(\forall u\in U^{\prime})\ \langle u,z\rangle=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\forall u\in U-U^{\prime})\ \langle u,z\rangle>0.

Hence,
\langle u,-z\rangle\leq 0
for every
u\in U
, or in other words,
-z\notin\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
.
On the other hand, since
U-U^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, we have that
z\in\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
.

To complete the proof of the lemma we define a strong deformation retraction from
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
to
\{z\}
by

h\colon\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\times[0,1]\longrightarrow\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle,\qquad\qquad h(x,s):=\frac{(1-s)\cdot x+s\cdot z}{\|(1-s)\cdot x+s\cdot z\|}.

Assuming
h
is well-defined, it is evident that the map
h(\cdot,0)\colon\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\longrightarrow\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
is the identity map and the map
h(\cdot,1)\colon\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\longrightarrow\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
is the constant map with the value
z
.
Hence, it remains to verify that
h
is well-defined.

First, we show that the denominator
\|(1-s)\cdot x+s\cdot z\|
does not vanish.
Assume contrary, there exist
s\in[0,1]
and
x\in\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
such that
(1-s)\cdot x+s\cdot z=0
, or equivalently
(1-s)\cdot x=s\cdot(-z)
.
The points
x
and
-z
are on the sphere
S(\mathbb{E})
and so
s\neq 0
and
s\neq 1
, or in other words
s\in(0,1)
.
Applying the norm to the equality
(1-s)\cdot x=s\cdot(-z)
we get that
1-s=s
, and so
s=\frac{1}{2}
.
Consequently,
x=-z
.
This cannot be because
x\in\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
and we have shown that
-z\notin\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
.

Finally, we verify that
\im(h)\subseteq\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
.
In the case
s=0
or
s=1
this was already verified.
For
s\in(0,1)
we consider the following two cases:

(a)

Let
x\in\mathscr{B}_{u}
for some
u\in U-U^{\prime}
. Then

\langle h(x,s),u\rangle=\frac{1-s}{\|(1-s)\cdot x+s\cdot z\|}\,\langle x,u\rangle+\frac{s}{\|(1-s)\cdot x+s\cdot z\|}\,\langle z,u\rangle\ >\ 0,

because
x\in\mathscr{B}_{u}
implies
\langle x,u\rangle>0
and
u\in U-U^{\prime}
implies
\langle z,u\rangle>0
.
Thus,
h(x,s)\in\mathscr{B}_{u}\subseteq\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
.

(b)

Let
x\in\mathscr{B}_{u}
for some
u\in U^{\prime}
. Then again

\langle h(x,s),u\rangle=\frac{1-s}{\|(1-s)\cdot x+s\cdot z\|}\,\langle x,u\rangle+\frac{s}{\|(1-s)\cdot x+s\cdot z\|}\,\langle z,u\rangle\ >\ 0,

because
x\in\mathscr{B}_{u}
implies
\langle x,u\rangle>0
,
u\in U^{\prime}
implies
\langle z,u\rangle=0
, and finally
s\neq 1
.
Thus, again
h(x,s)\in\mathscr{B}_{u}\subseteq\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
.

Therefore, the strong deformation retraction
H
is well-defined, and we have completed the proof.
∎

In the final case we analyze topology of
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
when
0\notin\conv(U)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
be a finite collection of points.
If
0\notin\conv(U)
, then
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
is a contractible space.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
be a finite collection of points and suppose that
0\notin\conv(U)
.
Consider the family
\mathscr{U}=\{\mathscr{B}_{u}:u\in U\}
as an open and good cover of the
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle=\bigcup_{u\in U}\mathscr{B}_{u}
, this means that every non-empty intersection is contractible.
Then, by the Nerve theorem
[
16
, Cor. 4G.3]
, the space
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle
is homotopy equivalent to the nerve
\mathcal{N}_{\mathscr{U}}
of the covering
\mathscr{U}
.
Since,
0\notin\conv(U)
it follows that
\mathscr{B}(U)=\bigcap_{u\in U}\mathscr{B}_{u}\neq\emptyset
, see Lemma
2.1
.
Therefore, the nerve
\mathcal{N}_{\mathscr{U}}
is isomorphic to the
(|U|-1)
-dimensional simplex.
Hence,
\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\simeq\mathcal{N}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\Delta_{|U|-1}\simeq\mathrm{pt}
, as claimed.

∎

In summary, we have proved that for
U\subseteq S(E)
it holds that:

\mathscr{B}(U)\ \cong\ \begin{cases}\ \cong D^{\dim(\mathbb{E})-1},&0\notin\conv(U),\\
\ =\emptyset,&0\in\conv(U),\end{cases}

and

\mathscr{B}\langle U\rangle\ \begin{cases}\ =S(\mathbb{E}),&0\in\interior(\conv(U)),\\
\ =S(\mathbb{E})-\{y\in S(\mathbb{E}):(\forall u\in U)\ \langle y,u\rangle=0\},&0\in\relint(\conv(U)),\\
\ \simeq\mathrm{pt},&0\in\conv(U)-\relint(\conv(U)),\\
\ \simeq\mathrm{pt},&0\notin\conv(U).\end{cases}

(2)

2.3.
Zero-avoiding complex as a nerve

In this section we connect definition of zero-avoiding complex and the findings of the previous section with a hope to say more about the topology of zero-avoiding complexes.

Let
V
be a finite set (in
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
) and let
\Delta_{V}
be the simplex with the vertex set
V
.
Fix an affine map
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
from the simplex
\Delta_{V}
into a finite dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}
such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.
Consider the family of open hemispheres

\mathscr{U}_{A}:=\{\mathscr{B}_{A(v)}\subseteq S(\mathbb{E}):v\in V\}

induced by the affine map
A
.
In particular,
\mathscr{U}_{A(V)}
is a good cover of
\mathscr{B}\langle A(V)\rangle
, that is all intersections of elements of
\mathscr{U}_{A(V)}
are either empty of contractible.

First, we relate the nerve of the cover
\mathscr{U}_{A}
with the zero-avoiding complex
\mathscr{C}_{A}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
V
be a finite set and let
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
be an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.
Then
\mathscr{C}_{A}
is isomorphic, as a simplicial complex, to the nerve
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}_{A}}
of the open cover
\mathscr{U}_{A}
.

Proof.

The claim follows directly from the definition of the nerve and Lemma
2.1
.
Indeed, for
U\subseteq V
the following equivalences hold:

\displaystyle U\text{ spans a simplex in the nerve of }\mathscr{U}_{A}\quad

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathscr{B}(A(U))\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\notin\conv(A(U))

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad A(U)\text{ spans a simplex in }\mathscr{C}_{A(V)}.

∎

Now, using Lemma
2.5
, Lemma
2.6
, Lemma
2.7
, Lemma
2.8
, and the Nerve theorem
[
16
, Cor. 4G.3]
we get the following characterization of a zero-avoiding complex.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
V
be a finite set and let
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
be an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.
Then

\mathscr{C}_{A}\ \simeq\ \begin{cases}\ S^{\dim(\spann(A(V)))-1},&0\in\relint(\conv(A(V))),\\
\ \mathrm{pt},&0\notin\relint(\conv(A(V))).\end{cases}

Proof.

There is a sequence of relations

\mathscr{C}_{A}\overset{\text{Lem. }\ref{lem : nerve}}{\cong}\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}_{A}}\overset{\text{Nerve thm.}}{\simeq}\bigcup_{u\in V}\mathscr{B}_{A(u)}=\mathscr{B}\langle A(V)\rangle,

which allows us to use Lemma
2.5
, Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
, or equivalently (
2
).

It remains to show that in the case when
0\in\relint\conv(A(V))
we have

S(\mathbb{E})-\{y\in S(\mathbb{E}):(\forall u\in V)\ \langle y,A(u)\rangle=0\}\ \simeq\ S^{\dim(\spann(A(V)))-1}.

(3)

Set
W=\spann(A(V))
, and denote by
W^{\perp}
its orthogonal complement.
Then

\{y\in S(\mathbb{E}):(\forall u\in V)\ \langle y,A(u)\rangle=0\}\,=\,S(W^{\perp}).

The relation (
3
) follows from the following observations about the (topological) joins:

S(\mathbb{E})\cong S(W\oplus W^{\perp})\cong S(W)*S(W^{\perp})\qquad\text{and}\qquad X*Y-Y\simeq X.

Here
X
and
Y
are arbitrary topological spaces.
∎

2.4.
Alexander duals of the zero-avoiding complexes

The Alexander duality
[
1
]
is one of the most frequently used classical results of algebraic topology.
In the context of the category of simplicial complexes it can be formulated as follows.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
V
.
The simplicial complex

\mathscr{K}^{*}:=\{F\subseteq V:V-F\notin\mathscr{K}\}

is called the
Alexander dual
of
\mathscr{K}
.
In particular,
(\mathscr{K}^{*})^{*}=\mathscr{K}
.
Now, the combinatorial version of the Alexander duality can be stated as follows, see for example
[
8
, Thm. 1.1]
.

From this point on
R
is assumed to be a commutative ring with one, and
\widetilde{H}
stands for the reduced singular or simplicial (co)homology with coefficients in
R
-modules.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a simplicial complex on the finite vertex set
V
which in not the simplex
\Delta_{V}
.
Then for every integer
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and every coefficient
R
-module
M
there are isomorphisms

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{K};M)\cong\widetilde{H}^{|V|-3-i}(\mathscr{K}^{*};M)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{K}^{*};M)\cong\widetilde{H}^{|V|-3-i}(\mathscr{K};M).

(4)

In this section we focus our attention on the topology of induced sub-complexes of the Alexander duals of zero-avoiding complexes.
More precisely, let
\mathbb{E}
be a finite dimensional affine Euclidean space with the fixed structure of the real vector space.
Additionally, let
V
be a finite set (in
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
) and let
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
be an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.
Suppose
U\subseteq V
.
The main question we want to answer is:
What is the topology of the induced sub-complex

\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]:=\{F\subseteq U:F\in\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}\}

of the zero-avoiding complex
\mathscr{C}_{A}
?

The answer to the central question of this section starts with the case
U=V
, or in other words with the Alexander dual of the zero-avoiding complex.

Recall, if
V
is a finite set and
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
is an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
, then

\mathscr{C}_{A}:=\{U\subseteq V:0\notin\conv(A(U))\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}=\{F\subseteq V:0\in\conv(A(V-F))\}.

(5)

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
V
be a finite set and let
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
be an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.
For every coefficient
R
-module
M
:

(1)

if
0\notin\relint(\conv(A(V)))
, then for every integer
i
holds

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*};M)=0,

(2)

if
0\in\relint(\conv(A(V)))
, then

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*};M)\cong\begin{cases}M,&i=|V|-\dim(\spann(A(V)))-2,\\
0,&i\neq|V|-\dim(\spann(A(V)))-2.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the Alexander duality isomorphism of Theorem
2.10
and Lemma
2.9
.
More precisely, we have that

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*};M)\cong\widetilde{H}^{|V|-3-i}(\mathscr{C}_{A};M)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{C}_{A}\ \simeq\ \begin{cases}\ S^{\dim(\spann(A(V)))-1},&0\in\relint(\conv(A(V))),\\
\ \mathrm{pt},&0\notin\relint(\conv(A(V))),\end{cases}

which implies the statement of the lemma.

It is important to observe that in the case (2) when
|V|-3=|V|-\dim(\spann(A(V)))=1
we have that the dual
\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}
is empty and so the corresponding non-vanishing homology appears only in dimension
-1
as claimed.
∎

We continue with the study of induced sub-complexes in several steps depending on the geometry of the affine map
A
and a subset
U\subseteq V
on which the sub-complex is considered.

Recall, if
V
is a finite set and
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
is an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
we have defined that

\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]:=\{F\subseteq U:F\in\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}\}=\{F\subseteq U:0\in\conv(A(V-F))\}.

(6)

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
V
be a finite set,
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
, and let
U\subseteq V
.
If
0\in\conv(A(V-U))
, then
\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
is the simplex
\Delta_{U}
, that is a
(|U|-1)
-dimensional simplex.
In particular,

0\in\conv(A(V-U))\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]\simeq\mathrm{pt}.

Proof.

Suppose
U\subseteq V
and
\conv(A(V-U))
.
Take an arbitrary
F\subseteq U
.
Then
V-U\subseteq V-F
, and so
0\in\conv(A(V-U))\subseteq\conv(A(V-F))
.
From (
5
) follows that
F\in\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
.
Consequently,
\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
is the simplex
\Delta_{U}
.
∎

From now on assume that
V
is a finite set,
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
is an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
, and that
U\subseteq V
with
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
.
Hence,
U\notin\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
, and so
\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
is a sub-complex of the boundary of the
(|U|-1)
-dimensional simplex
\Delta_{U}
on the vertex set
U
, which is a
(|U|-2)
-dimensional sphere.
In particular we get the following fact.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
V
be a finite set and let
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
be an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.
If
U\subseteq V
and
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
, then for every integer
i\geq|U|-1
and every coefficient
R
-module
M
it holds that:

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U];M)\cong 0.

Note that Lemma
2.12
allows us to remove the assumption
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
from the statement of the previous Lemma
2.13
.
In other words, for every
U\subseteq V
and every integer
i\geq|U|-1
it holds that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U];M)\cong 0
.

If
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
then
\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
is either the boundary of the simplex
\Delta_{U}
or a proper
sub-complex of
\partial\Delta_{U}
.
Hence, the Alexander dual of the simplicial complex
\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
in
\partial\Delta_{U}
is the simplicial complex given by:

(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U])^{*}=\{F\subseteq U:U-F\notin\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]\}=\{F\subseteq U:0\notin\conv(A(F\cup(V-U)))\}.

(7)

Note that in the case when
\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]=\partial\Delta_{U}
the dual
(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U])^{*}
is the empty simplicial complex.
Further, from Lemma
2.1
, we get that

(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U])^{*}=\{F\subseteq U:\mathscr{B}(A(F\cup(V-U)))\neq\emptyset\}=\{F\subseteq U:\mathscr{B}(A(F))\cap\mathscr{B}(A(V-U))\neq\emptyset\}.

(8)

Hence,
(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U])^{*}
can be identified with the nerve of the good open covering

\{\mathscr{B}_{A(u)}\cap\mathscr{B}(A(V-U)):u\in U\}

of the open set
\mathscr{B}\langle A(U)\rangle\cap\mathscr{B}(A(V-U))
of the sphere
S(\mathbb{E})
.
Now, the Nerve theorem
[
16
, Cor. 4G.3]
evidently implies the following claim.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
V
be a finite set and let
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
be an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.
If
U\subseteq V
and
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
, then

(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U])^{*}\ \simeq\ \mathscr{B}\langle A(U)\rangle\cap\mathscr{B}(A(V-U)).

(9)

Next, we apply the previous Lemma
2.14
in several situations getting better insight in the topology of the induced sub-complex
\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
.
We start with a general observation about the connectivity of the induced sub-complex.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
V
be a finite set and let
A\colon\Delta_{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}
be an affine map such that
A(V)\subseteq S(\mathbb{E})
.
If
U\subseteq V
and
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
, then for every integer
i\leq|U|-\dim(\mathbb{E})-2
and every coefficient
R
-module
M
it holds that

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U];M)\cong 0.

Proof.

Since
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
from Lemma
2.14
we conclude that

(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U])^{*}\ \simeq\ \mathscr{B}\langle A(U)\rangle\cap\mathscr{B}(A(V-U))\ \subseteq\ \mathscr{B}(A(V-U))\ \cong\mathbb{R}^{\dim(\mathbb{E})-1}.

Hence,
\widetilde{H}^{j}((\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U])^{*};M)\cong 0
for all
j\geq\dim(\mathbb{E})-1
.
Now, the Alexander duality isomorphism (
4
) implies that

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U];M)\ \cong\ \widetilde{H}^{|U|-3-i}((\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U])^{*};M)\ \cong\ 0

for all
|U|-3-i\geq\dim(\mathbb{E})-1
, or equivalently for all
i\leq|U|-\dim(\mathbb{E})-2
, as claimed.
∎

Combining Lemma
2.12
with the previous Lemma
2.15
we can remove the condition
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
and obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
V

Lemma 2.18

Let

Let

Hence, there is at least one

Lemma 2.19

\mathscr{B}\langle A(U)\rangle\cap\mathscr{B}(A(V-U))\ =\ \mathscr{B}(A(V-U))-\{-x\}.

\mathscr{B}\langle A(U)\rangle\cap\mathscr{B}(A(V-U))\subseteq\mathscr{B}(A(V-U))-\{-x\}

3.
Covering scheme and proofs of colourful Carathéodory theorems

In this section we first develop the so-called “covering scheme”, Lemma
3.2
, which offers an algorithm for proving different instances of
Problem
1.5
:

For integers
N\geq 1
and
d\geq 1
, find all simplicial sub-complexes
\mathcal{K}
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
, with
\vertex(\mathcal{K})=\vertex(\Delta_{N})
, and all the families
\mathcal{S}
of faces of
\Delta_{N}
such that the following implication holds: If an affine map
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies that
0\in A(S)
for every face
S\in\mathcal{S}
, then there exists a face
J
of
\mathcal{K}
with
0\in A(J)
.

Then, using the covering scheme we give essentially identical proofs to all Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.6
.

An additional ingredient of all these proofs will be one of the following Nerve theorems:

(A)

the Nerve theorem of Björner
[
7
, Thm. 6]
:

Theorem 6.
Let
X
be a connected regular CW complex and let
\mathscr{U}:=(X_{i}:i\in I)
be a cover of
X
by sub-complexes. Additionally, assume that every non-empty
t
-fold intersection
X_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap X_{i_{t}}
is
(k-t+1)
-connected, where
t\geq 1
. Then there is a continuous map
f\colon X\longrightarrow\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}
from
X
into the nerve
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}
of the cover
\mathscr{U}
which induces isomorphism between homotopy groups

f_{*}\colon\pi_{i}(X)\longrightarrow\pi_{i}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}})

for all
i\leq k
.

(B)

the homological Nerve theorem of Meshulam
[
21
, Thm. 2.1]
:

Theorem 2.1.
Let
X
be a connected finite simplicial complex and let
\mathscr{U}:=(X_{i}:i\in I)
be a cover of
X
by sub-complexes. Additionally, assume that for every non-empty
t
-fold intersection
X_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap X_{i_{t}}
and every
0\leq j\leq k-t+1
holds that

\widetilde{H}_{j}(X_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap X_{i_{t}};\mathbb{F})=0
,
where
t\geq 1
. Then for arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
coefficients it holds that

(1)

\widetilde{H}_{j}(X;\mathbb{F})\cong\widetilde{H}_{j}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{F})
for all
0\leq j\leq k
, and

(2)

if
\widetilde{H}_{k+1}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{F})\neq 0
, then
\widetilde{H}_{k+1}(X;\mathbb{F})\neq 0
.

To use any of the Nerve theorems we will have to estimate the (homological) connectivity of intersections of sub-complexes.
For that we will use two auxiliary facts.
First, the classical criterion for the connectivity, see for example

be a space and let

which says that:

\widetilde{H}_{k}(A*B;\mathbb{F})\cong\bigoplus_{a+b=k-1}\widetilde{H}_{a}(A;\mathbb{F})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}\widetilde{H}_{b}(B;\mathbb{F}),

where

V
, say

\vertex(Z)=\vertex(X^{*})\sqcup\vertex(Y):=V^{(1)}\sqcup V^{(2)}
.
For
p_{j}\in V
the corresponding elements in
V^{(1)}
and
V^{(2)}
will be denoted by
p_{j}^{(1)}
and
p_{j}^{(2)}
respectively.
Now, we colour (partition) the vertex set of
Z
into
N+1
colour classes as follows:

\vertex(Z)=V^{(1)}\sqcup V^{(2)}=\bigsqcup_{0\leq j\leq N}C_{j}=\bigsqcup_{0\leq j\leq N,}\{p_{j}^{(1)},p_{j}^{(2)}\}.

Here, as expected,
C_{j}:=\{p_{j}^{(1)},p_{j}^{(2)}\}
.
A face
T
of the simplicial complex
Z
is called
colourful transversal face
if for every
0\leq j\leq N
holds:

1\leq|T\cap C_{j}|=|\vertex(T)\cap C_{j}|\leq 2.

Now we can give the first key lemma, which can be seen as an analogue of the so-called configuration space test map scheme paradigm.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
d\geq 1
be integers,
V:=\vertex(\Delta_{N})
,
\mathcal{S}
a family of faces of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
and
\mathcal{K}
a simplicial complex with
V=\vertex(\mathcal{K})
.
Suppose that
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an affine map with the property that
0\notin\vertex(\Delta_{N})
and that
0\in A(S)
for every
S\in\mathcal{S}
.

If the simplicial complex
Z=Z(A,\mathcal{K})
has at least one colourful transversal face, then there exists a face
J
of
\mathcal{K}
such that
0\in A(J)
.

Proof.

Let
T=I*J
be a colourful transversal face of the simplicial complex
Z=X^{*}*Y
with
I\in X^{*}=\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}
and
J\in Y=\mathcal{K}
.
Since
X^{*}
is not the simplex
\Delta_{N}
, the definition of a colourful transversal face implies that the face
J
of
Y
is non-empty.

For simplicity’s sake, we assume that the faces
I
and
J
are on the same vertex set
V=V^{(1)}=V^{(2)}
.
An abuse of notation which follows should not cause any confusion.

The colourful transversality of the face
T=I*J
yields that
V-\vertex(I)\subseteq\vertex(J)
where
J\in Y=\mathcal{K}
.
Since
I\in X^{*}=\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}=\{F\subseteq V:0\in\conv(A(V-F))\}
we have that

0\in\conv(A(V-\vertex(I)))\subseteq\conv(A(\vertex(J)))=A(J).

Thus, we have found a face
J
of
\mathcal{K}
with the property that
0\in A(J)
.
∎

We proceed with the second key lemma which we also call the
covering scheme
.

Lemma 3.2

(Covering scheme)
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
d\geq 1
be integers,
V:=\vertex(\Delta_{N})
,
\mathcal{S}
a family of faces of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
and
\mathcal{K}
a simplicial complex with
V=\vertex(\mathcal{K})
.
Suppose that
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an affine map with the property that
0\notin A(\vertex(\Delta_{N}))
and that
0\in A(S)
for every
S\in\mathcal{S}
.

If for every face
J
of
\mathcal{K}
it holds that
0\notin A(J)
, the then the family of induced sub-complexes

\{Z(A,\mathcal{K})[(V^{(1)}\sqcup V^{(2)})-C_{j}]:0\leq j\leq N\}

is a cover of the simplicial complex
Z(A,\mathcal{K})
.

Proof.

From the assumption and Lemma
3.1
it follows that the simplicial complex
Z(A,\mathcal{K})
has no colourful transversal faces.
Now, let
F=I*J
be a face of
Z(A,\mathcal{K})=X(A,\mathcal{K})^{*}*Y(A,\mathcal{K})
.
Since
F
is not a colourful transversal face, there exists an integer
0\leq j\leq N
with the property that
|F\cap C_{j}|=0
.
Consequently,
F\in Z(A,\mathcal{K})[(V^{(1)}\sqcup V^{(2)})-C_{j}]
and the proof is completed.
∎

Now, as announced, we use Lemma
3.2
to prove all colourful Caratheodory theorems, except Theorem
1.4
, which has also an analogous proof.

Let us first briefly outline the algorithm of all the proofs which follow.

Suppose we are given

–

integers
d\geq 1
and
N\geq 1
,

–

N
-dimensional simplex
\Delta_{N}
with set of vertices
V=\vertex(\Delta_{N})
,

–

\mathcal{S}
a family of faces of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
,

–

\mathcal{K}
a simplicial complex with
V=\vertex(\mathcal{K})
, and

–

A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and affine map which has the property that
0\notin A(\vertex(\Delta_{N}))
and
0\in A(S)
for every
S\in\mathcal{S}
.

The goal is to find a face
J
of
\mathcal{K}
with the property that
0\in A(J)
.

For this, we:

–

consider the zero-avoiding simplicial complex
X:=X(A,\mathcal{K})=\mathscr{C}_{A}
of the affine map
A
, as defined in (
1
), and the simplicial complex
Y:=Y(A,\mathcal{K})=\mathcal{K}
,

–

form the simplicial complex
Z:=X^{*}*Y
with the vertex set equal to the disjoint union of two copies of
V
, that is
\vertex(Z)=\vertex(X(A,\mathcal{K})^{*})\sqcup\vertex(Y(A,\mathcal{K})):=V^{(1)}\sqcup V^{(2)}
where
V^{(1)}=V^{(2)}=V
,

–

consider the family
\mathscr{U}:=\{Z[(U^{(1)}\sqcup U^{(2)}]:\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V,\,|U|=|V|-1\}

of induced sub-complexes of the complex
Z(A,\mathcal{K})
, and crucially

–

prove that the family
\mathscr{U}
cannot cover the simplicial complex
Z
,
that is show
\bigcup\mathscr{U}\neq Z
.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

As a first illustration of the methods, we prove the classical colourful Caratheodory theorem
[
6
, Thm. 2.1]
.

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
d\geq 1
be integers, and let
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an affine map.

If there exists an integer
r\geq d+1
and
r
pairwise disjoint non-empty faces
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
such that

0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r})
,
then there exists a selection of vertices
p_{1}\in F_{1},\dots,p_{r}\in F_{r}
with the property that
0\in A(F)
where
F=\conv\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}\subseteq\Delta_{N}
is the face of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
spanned by the selected vertices
p_{1},\dots,p_{r}
.

Proof.

This is a proof by contradiction.
Fix integers
N\geq 1
,
d\geq 1
,
r\geq d+1
, and disjoint non-empty faces
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
.
Suppose that
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an affine map with the property that
0\notin A(\vertex(\Delta_{N}))
and that

0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r})
.
Without loss of generality we can assume that
A(\Delta_{N})
affinely spans
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and that

0\in\relint(A(F_{1}))\cap\cdots\cap\relint(A(F_{r}))\subseteq A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r}).

Assume that for every selection of vertices
p_{1}\in F_{1},\dots,p_{r}\in F_{r}
it holds that
0\notin A(F)
where
F=\conv\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}\subseteq\Delta_{N}
is the face of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
spanned by the selected vertices
p_{1},\dots,p_{r}
.

Set
\mathcal{K}:=\vertex({F_{1}})*\cdots*\vertex({F_{r}})\subseteq\Delta_{N}
, and further

X:=X(A,\mathcal{K})=\mathscr{C}_{A},\qquad Y=Y(A,\mathcal{K})=\mathcal{K},\qquad Z:=Z(A,\mathcal{K})=X^{*}*Y.

We first compute the homology
H_{*}(Z;\mathbb{Q})
.
we have that
0\in\interior(\conv(A(V)))
from the assumptions, and so by
Lemma
2.11
and get that

\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*};\mathbb{Q})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*};\mathbb{Q})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q},&i=N-d-1,\\
0,&i\neq N-d-1.\end{cases}

Furthermore,
Y=\mathcal{K}=\vertex({F_{1}})*\cdots*\vertex({F_{r}})
is the
r
-fold join of finite
0
-dimensioanl simplicial complexes.
Consequently,
Y
is
(r-2)
-connected, meaning

\widetilde{H}_{i}(Y;\mathbb{Q})\cong\begin{cases}\neq 0,&i=r-1,\\
0,&i\neq r-1.\end{cases}

Then, the homology Künneth formula for joins (
19
) yields

\widetilde{H}_{i}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}*Y;\mathbb{Q})\cong\begin{cases}\neq 0,&i=N-d-1+r,\\
0,&i\neq N-d-1+r.\end{cases}

(20)

Now, we conclude from the assumption on the affine map
A
and Lemma
3.2
that the family of induced sub-complexes

\mathscr{U}:=\{Z[(V^{(1)}\sqcup V^{(2)})-C_{j}]\,:\,0\leq j\leq N\}

is a cover of
Z
.
Since
C_{j}=\{p_{j}^{(1)},p_{j}^{(2)}\}
and
V^{(1)}=V^{(2)}=V
, the elements of the cover can be presented as follows

Z[(V^{(1)}\sqcup V^{(2)})-C_{j}]=X^{*}[V-\{p_{j}\}]*Y[V-\{p_{j}\}].

Thus, the nerve
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}
of the cover
\mathscr{U}
can be identified with the boundary of the simplex on the set of vertices
V
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
, that is,
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\partial(\Delta_{N})
.

So far there is no indication that the nerve
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}
of the cover
\mathscr{U}
of
Z
gives any information about the complex
Z
.
To obtain a relationship between
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}
and
Z
we use the Nerve’s theorem of Björner
[
7
, Thm. 6]
and the connectivity criterion
[
19
, Thm. 4.4.1]
, which we already recalled.

Hence, we have to estimate the connectivity of all non-empty intersections of the elements of the cover
\mathscr{U}
, these are all the sub-complexes of the form
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
where
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
.
This is done in two steps, depending on whether
0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
belongs to
\conv(A(V-U))
.

(1)

If
0\in\conv(A(V-U))
, then
X^{*}[U]=\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
is a
(|U|-1)
-dimensional simplex according to Lemma
2.12
. Hence,
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is contractible and therefore
(k-t+1)
-connected whenever
k-t+1\geq-1
.

(2)

If
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
, then
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U];\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for every
i\leq|U|-d-2
by Lemma
2.15
. From the assumption that
0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r})
and assumption that
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
it follows that

U\cap\vertex(F_{j})\neq\emptyset
,
for every
1\leq j\leq r
. In particular,
|U|\geq r
. Consequently,

Y[U]\cong\big(U\cap\vertex({F_{1}})\big)*\cdots*\big(U\cap\vertex({F_{r}})\big)

is at least
(r-2)
-connected. Now, using the homology criterion for connectivity
[
19
, Thm. 4.4.1]
and the Künneth formula for joins (
19
) we conclude that
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is
(|U|+r-d-2)
-connected, or in other words for all
i\leq|U|+r-d-2
:

\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Z})\cong 0.

In summary, for every
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
it holds that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for all
i\leq|U|+r-d-2
.

Now, for each
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
the sub-complex
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is the intersection of
N+1-|U|
elements of the cover
\mathscr{U}=\{X^{*}[U^{\prime}]*Y[U^{\prime}]:\emptyset\subsetneq U^{\prime}\subsetneq V,\,|U^{\prime}|=|V|-1\}
.
Then the Nerve theorem
[
7
, Thm. 6]
implies that
\pi_{i}(Z)\cong\pi_{i}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}})
for all
i\leq N-1
.
Since,
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\partial(\Delta_{N})\cong S^{N-1}
we have that

\pi_{i}(Z)\cong 0

and

\widetilde{H}_{i}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong 0

for
1\leq i\leq N-2
, and more importantly,

\pi_{N-1}(Z)\cong\mathbb{Z}

and

\widetilde{H}_{N-1}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}
.
This is a contradiction with (
20
), because
\widetilde{H}_{i}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for all
i\neq N-d-1+r\geq N
implying that
\widetilde{H}_{N-1}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
.
This completes the proof of the classical colourful Carathéodory theorem.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we give a new proof of Theorem
1.2
relying now on the homological Nerve theorem of Meshulam
[
21
, Thm. 2.1]
.
The following claim is proved.

Theorem
1.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
d\geq 1
be integers, and let
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an affine map.

If there exists an integer
r\geq d+1
and
r
pairwise disjoint non-empty faces
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
such that

0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r-1})
,
then for an arbitrary vertex
p\in F_{r}
there exists a selection of vertices
p_{1}\in F_{1},\dots,p_{r-1}\in F_{r-1}
with the property that
0\in A(F)
where
F=\conv\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r-1},p\}\subseteq\Delta_{N}
is the face of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
spanned by the selected vertices
p_{1},\dots,p_{r-1},p
.

Proof.

Fix integers
d\geq 1
and
r\geq d+1
, and an affine map
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose that there exist
r
pairwise disjoint non-empty faces
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
which have the property that

0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r-1})
.
Without loss of generality assume that
\vertex(F_{1}),\dots,\vertex(F_{r})
is a partition of
V:=\vertex(\Delta_{N})
and that
F_{r}=\{p\}
is just a
0
-dimensional face of
\Delta_{N}
.
Additionally, let

–

\mathcal{S}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{r-1}\}
,

–

\mathcal{K}=\vertex(F_{1})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r-1})*\{p\}
, a simplicial complex with
V=\vertex(\Delta_{N})=\vertex(\mathcal{K})
,

–

X:=X(\mathcal{K},A):=\mathscr{C}_{A}
,
Y:=Y(\mathcal{K},A):=\mathcal{K}
,
Z:=Z(\mathcal{K},A)=X^{*}*Y
, and

–

\mathscr{U}:=\big\{X^{*}[U]*Y[U]\subseteq Z:\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V,\,|U|=|V|-1\big\}
.

According to Lemma
3.2
, it order to prove the theorem, we need to show that
\mathscr{U}
cannot cover
Z
.
For this we study the homologies
H_{*}(Z;\mathbb{F})
and
H_{*}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{F})
where
\mathbb{F}
is a fixed arbitrary field.

First we compute homology of the simplicial complex
Z
.
Since the simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}=\vertex(F_{1})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r-1})*\{p\}
is just a cone and so contractible it follows that

\widetilde{H}_{i}(Z;\mathbb{F})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}*Y;\mathbb{F})=\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}*\mathcal{K};\mathbb{F})\cong 0

(21)

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Next, we analyse the homology of simplicial complexes of the form
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
where
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
.
This is done, as before, in two steps, depending on whether
0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
belongs to
\conv(A(V-U))
:

(1)

If
0\in\conv(A(V-U))
, then
X^{*}[U]=\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
is a
(|U|-1)
-dimensional simplex according to Lemma
2.12
. Hence,
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is contractible and so
\widetilde{H}_{i}\big(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Q}\big)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
, then
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U];\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for every
i\leq|U|-d-2
by Lemma
2.15
. From the assumptions
0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r-1})
and
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
follows that

U\cap\vertex(F_{j})\neq\emptyset
,
for every
1\leq j\leq r-1
. In particular,
|U|\geq r-1
.
Consequently,

Y[U]\cong\big(U\cap\vertex({F_{1}})\big)*\cdots*\big(U\cap\vertex({F_{r-1}})\big)*\begin{cases}\{p\},&p\in U,\\
\emptyset,&p\notin U,\end{cases}

is at least
(r-3)
-connected.
Now, using the homology criterion for connectivity
[
19
, Thm. 4.4.1]
and the Künneth formula for joins (
19
) we conclude that
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is
(|U|+r-d-3)
-connected.
In particular, for all
i\leq|U|+r-d-3
:

\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{F})\cong 0.

In summary, for every
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
it holds that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{F})\cong 0
for all
i\leq|U|+r-d-3
.

Now, for each
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
the sub-complex
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is the intersection of
N+1-|U|
elements of the cover
\mathscr{U}=\{X^{*}[U^{\prime}]*Y[U^{\prime}]:\emptyset\subsetneq U^{\prime}\subsetneq V,\,|U^{\prime}|=|V|-1\}
.
Using the homological Nerve theorem
[
21
, Thm. 2.1]
we have that

\widetilde{H}_{j}(Z;\mathbb{F})\cong\widetilde{H}_{j}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{F})

for all
0\leq j\leq N+r-d-3
, where
N+r-d-2\geq N-1
because
r\geq d+1
.
Additionally, if
\widetilde{H}_{N+r-d-2}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{F})\neq 0
, then also
\widetilde{H}_{N+r-d-2}(Z;\mathbb{F})\neq 0
.
Since,
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\partial(\Delta_{N})\cong S^{N-1}
we have reached a contradiction with (
21
), because
\widetilde{H}_{i}(Z;\mathbb{F})\cong 0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
but
\widetilde{H}_{N-1}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{F})\cong\mathbb{F}
and
N+r-d-3\geq N-2
.
This completes the proof of the second version of the classical colourful Carathéodory theorem.
∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section we reprove the following result using the methods we have developed.

.

N\geq 1
be an integer, and let

(22)

.

X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is contractible and so
\widetilde{H}_{i}\big(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Q}\big)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
, then
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U];\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for every
i\leq|U|-d-2
by Lemma
2.15
. From the assumptions that
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
and
0\in\bigcap_{1\leq i<j\leq r}A(F_{i}\cup F_{j})
it follows that
U\cap(\vertex(F_{i})\cup\vertex(F_{j}))\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq i<j\leq r
.
Hence the set
\{i:1\leq i\leq r,\,U\cap\vertex(F_{i})\neq\emptyset\}
has at least
r-1
elements.
Consequently,

Y[U]\cong\big(U\cap\vertex({F_{1}})\big)*\cdots*\big(U\cap\vertex({F_{r}})\big)

is at least
(r-3)
-connected.
The homology criterion for connectivity
[
19
, Thm. 4.4.1]
and the Künneth formula for joins (
19
) imply that
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is
(|U|+r-d-3)
-connected, or in other words for all
i\leq|U|+r-d-3
:

\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Z})\cong 0.

Summarising both steps we get that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for all
i\leq|U|+r-d-3
and all
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
.

The final step is identical to the one in the previous section.
For each
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
the sub-complex
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is the intersection of
N+1-|U|
elements of the cover
\mathscr{U}=\{X^{*}[U^{\prime}]*Y[U^{\prime}]:\emptyset\subsetneq U^{\prime}\subsetneq V,\,|U^{\prime}|=|V|-1\}
.
Using the homological Nerve theorem
[
21
, Thm. 2.1]
we have that

\widetilde{H}_{j}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong\widetilde{H}_{j}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{Q})

for all
0\leq j\leq N+r-d-3
, where
N+r-d-3\geq N-2
because
r\geq d+1
.
Additionally, if
\widetilde{H}_{N+r-d-2}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
, then also
\widetilde{H}_{N+r-d-2}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
.
Since,
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\partial(\Delta_{N})\cong S^{N-1}
we have reached a contradiction with (
22
).
Indeed,
\widetilde{H}_{i}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
but
\widetilde{H}_{N-1}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}
and
N+r-d-3\geq N-2
.
This completes the proof of the third version of the colourful Carathéodory theorem.
∎

3.5.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

We now prove the central result of this paper almost identically as we have proved the previous versions of the colourful Carathéodory theorem.

Theorem
1.6
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
d\geq 2
and
r\geq d+1
be integers, and let
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an affine map.
Suppose that

(1)

F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
is a collection of pairwise disjoint non-empty faces of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
such that

0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r})
, and

(2)

\mathcal{K}
is a simplicial sub-complex
L*\vertex(F_{3})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r})
of
\Delta_{N}
where
L
is a path-connected sub-complex of the join complex
\vertex(F_{1})*\vertex(F_{2})
with
\vertex(L)=\vertex(F_{1})\cup\vertex(F_{2})
.

Then there exists a face
J
of
\mathcal{K}
with
0\in A(J)
.

Proof.

Fix integers
N\geq 1
,
d\geq 2
and
r\geq d+1
, as well as an affine map
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Choose
r
pairwise disjoint non-empty faces
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
of the simplex
\Delta_{N}
such that

0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r})
.
Like before, without loss of generality we can assume that
\vertex(F_{1}),\dots,\vertex(F_{r})
is a partition of
V:=\vertex(\Delta_{N})
.
Additionally, let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial sub-complex
L*\vertex(F_{3})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r})
of
\Delta_{N}
where
L
is a path-connected sub-complex of the join complex
\vertex(F_{1})*\vertex(F_{2})
with
\vertex(L)=\vertex(F_{1})\cup\vertex(F_{2})
.
Now, set

–

\mathcal{S}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{r}\}
,

–

\mathcal{K}=L*\vertex(F_{3})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r})
,

–

X:=X(\mathcal{K},A):=\mathscr{C}_{A}
,
Y:=Y(\mathcal{K},A):=\mathcal{K}
,
Z:=Z(\mathcal{K},A)=X^{*}*Y
, and

–

\mathscr{U}:=\big\{X^{*}[U]*Y[U]\subseteq Z:\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V,\,|U|=|V|-1\big\}
.

Like in all previous proofs, according to Lemma
3.2
, in order to prove the theorem, we need to show that
\mathscr{U}
cannot cover
Z
.
Again, we study the homologies
H_{*}(Z;\mathbb{Q})
and
H_{*}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{Q})
.

Thanks the assumption on the affine map
A\colon\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
that

0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r})
, we can assume without loss of generality that
A(\Delta_{N})
affinely spans
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and that

0\in\relint(A(F_{1}))\cap\cdots\cap\relint(A(F_{r}))\subseteq A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r}).

Consequently,
0\in\interior(\conv(A(V)))
and so, by Lemma
2.11
, we get that

\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*};\mathbb{Q})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*};\mathbb{Q})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q},&i=N-d-1,\\
0,&i\neq N-d-1.\end{cases}

Furthermore,
Y=\mathcal{K}=L*\vertex(F_{3})*\cdots*\vertex(F_{r})
is an
(r-1)
-dimensional and
(r-2)
-connected simplicial complex, implying that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(Y;\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for all
i\neq r-1
.
Consequently, the Künneth formula for a join with field coefficients (
19
) yields

\widetilde{H}_{i}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}*Y;\mathbb{Q})\cong 0

(23)

for all
i\neq N+r-d-1
.

Once again we consider the homology of
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
where
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
.
This is done in two steps:

(1)

If
0\in\conv(A(V-U))
, then
X^{*}[U]=\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U]
is a
(|U|-1)
-dimensional simplex according to Lemma
2.12
. Hence,
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is contractible and so
\widetilde{H}_{i}\big(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Q}\big)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
, then
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathscr{C}_{A}^{*}[U];\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for every
i\leq|U|-d-2
by Lemma
2.15
. From the assumptions that
0\notin\conv(A(V-U))
and
0\in A(F_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(F_{r})
it follows that
U\cap\vertex(F_{i})\ \neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
Consequently,

Y[U]\cong L[U]*\big(U\cap\vertex({F_{3}})\big)*\cdots*\big(U\cap\vertex({F_{r}})\big)

is at least
(r-3)
-connected.
The homology criterion for connectivity
[
19
, Thm. 4.4.1]
and the Künneth formula for joins (
19
) imply that
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is
(|U|+r-d-3)
-connected.
This means that for all
i\leq|U|+r-d-3
:

\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Z})\cong 0.

Summarising both steps we get that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(X^{*}[U]*Y[U];\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for all
i\leq|U|+r-d-3
and all
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
.

The final step coincides word by word with the one in the previous section.
For each
\emptyset\subsetneq U\subsetneq V
the sub-complex
X^{*}[U]*Y[U]
is the intersection of
N+1-|U|
elements of the cover
\mathscr{U}=\{X^{*}[U^{\prime}]*Y[U^{\prime}]:\emptyset\subsetneq U^{\prime}\subsetneq V,\,|U^{\prime}|=|V|-1\}
.
Using the homological Nerve theorem
[
21
, Thm. 2.1]
we have that

\widetilde{H}_{j}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong\widetilde{H}_{j}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{Q})

for all
0\leq j\leq N+r-d-3
, where
N+r-d-3\geq N-2
because
r\geq d+1
.
Additionally, if
\widetilde{H}_{N+r-d-2}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
, then also
\widetilde{H}_{N+r-d-2}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
.
Since,
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\partial(\Delta_{N})\cong S^{N-1}
we have reached a contradiction with (
23
).
Indeed,
\widetilde{H}_{i}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\cong 0
for all
i\neq N+r-d-1
but
\widetilde{H}_{N-1}(\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{U}};\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}
and
N+r-d-1\geq N
.
This completes the proof of our main result.
∎

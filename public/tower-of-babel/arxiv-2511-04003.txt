A Generalized Frankel Conjecture Via The Yang-Mills Flow
Jiangtao Li

Abstract
. In this note, we introduce a new curvature condition called the 2 − 2- positive bisectional curvature on compact Kähler manifolds. We then deduce a characterization theorem for manifolds with 2 − 2- positive bisectional curvature, which can be regarded as a variant of the classical Frankel conjecture (cf. [ Fra61 , SY80 ] ) and its generalizations (cf. [ Siu80 , Mok88 ] ).



1.
Introduction

One of the landmark results in complex differential geometry is the resolution of the Frankel’s conjecture (cf.
[
Fra61
]
) by Siu and Yau:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
SY80
]
)
.

A compact Kähler manifold with positive bisectional curvature is biholomorphic to the complex projective space.

A purely algebro-geometric generalization of the conjecture was proposed by Hartshorne (cf.
[
Har70
]
) and proved by Mori (cf.
[
Mor79
]
). This is one of the first few results in the modern study of Fano manifolds and Mori’s original technique has tremendous influence on the later development of the theory on Fano manifolds.

On the differential geometric side,
1.1
has also been generalized in multiple different ways. Under the non-compact setting, it was generalized by the famous Yau’s uniformization conjecture:

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
Yau88
,
SY94
]
)
.

A non-compact Kähler manifold with positive bisectional curvature is biholomorphic to the complex Euclidean space.

The conjecture has received a lot of attention during the last decades and there has been much progress towards it by many mathematicians (see
[
Ni98
,
NT03
,
Ni04
,
CFYZ06
,
Liu16
]
). The best result so far was obtained by G. Liu, who confirmed the conjecture under the additional assumption of maximum volume growth. We refer the readers to the survey
[
Liu20
]
for more details on this problem.

There are also various generalizations in the compact case. For instance, Siu (cf.
[
Siu80
]
) gave a curvature characterization of complex projective spaces and smooth complex hyperquadrics using similar methods as in
[
SY80
]
. Later, Mok (cf.
[
Mok88
]
) studied compact Kähler manifolds with non-negative bisectional curvature and showed that they are exactly all the compact Hermitian symmetric spaces. In addition, it was shown by Gu-Zhang (cf.
[
GZ10
]
) and Feng-Liu-Wan (cf.
[
FLW17
]
) that the Frankel’s conjecture still holds if the assumption of positive bisectional curvature is weakened to positive orthogonal bisectional curvature.

In this note we give another generalization of the classical Frankel’s conjecture similar to that of Siu (cf.
[
Siu80
]
). We shall introduce a new curvature condition for compact Kähler manifolds of dimension at least 2, which we call
2-
positive bisectional curvature (see Definition
2.1
). We remark that
[
Siu80
]
also defined
m-
positivity of bisectional curvature but it is a different curvature condition from ours. One of differences is that the
m-
positivity in
[
Siu80
]
requires non-negativity of the bisectional curvature while ours in this paper doesn’t impose this restriction. The main theorem of this paper gives a characterization of compact Kähler manifolds with
2-
positive bisectional curvature.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
. An
n
dimensional compact Kähler manifold
M^{n}
admits a Kähler metric with
2-
positive bisectional curvature if and only if

(1)

M^{2}
is biholomorphic to a del Pezzo surface if
n=2
;

(2)

M^{n}
is biholomorphic to either
\C P^{n}
or the smooth complex hyperquadric
Q^{n}
if
n\geq 3
.

As we shall see in
Section

2
, (1) of
1.3
follows directly from the definition of
2-
positivity and Yau’s theorem (cf.
[
Yau78
]
). The main part of the note is devoted to the proof of (2). We briefly mention the idea and key ingredients in the proof here. Firstly, by a classical result in algebraic geometry on the pseudoindex of Fano manifolds (cf.
[
Miy04
,
DH17
]
), the statement is equivalent to an estimate on the minimal anti-canonical degree of rational curves on
M^{n}
. To establish the desired estimate, we consider the Yang-Mills flow on the Riemann sphere
\C P^{1}
. The long time existence and convergence are well known due to existing theory on the Yang-Mills flow on Riemann surfaces (cf.
[
Don85
,
Das92
,
Rd92
]
). The key will be Corollary
3.5
, a maximum principle for the Yang-Mills flow, which follows from Hamilton’s tensor maximum principle
3.4
. The Hamilton’s maximum principle is well known and used widely in the applications of the Ricci flow. However, it seems never appearing in the Yang-Mills setting.

The paper is organized as follows: In
Section

2
, we define the curvature condition of
2-
positive bisectional curvature and construct such metrics on del Pezzo surfaces and smooth complex hyperquadrics. In
Section

3
, we give a review of the fundamental results on the Yang-Mills flow on Riemann surfaces, which will be used in the proof of the main theorem. Afterwards, we establish the key maximum principle Corollary
3.5
. In
Section

4
, we complete the proof of the main theorem. Finally, we discuss some related questions and also relevant works by others in the last section.

2.
2
-positive bisectional curvature

Let
(M,g)
be an
n

M

We introduce a new notion of positivity for bisectional curvatures which we call

Definition 2.1

(

-positive bisectional curvature)
.

(M,g)

-positive bisectional curvature if
\lambda_{1}(x,U)+\lambda_{2}(x,U)>0

We give some examples of compact Kähler manifolds with

-positive bisectional curvature. Observe that when

-positivity of bisectional curvature is equivalent to the positivity of Ricci curvature. Therefore, by Yau’s resolution of the Calabi conjecture (cf.
[

]
), all Fano surfaces, namely, del Pezzo surfaces, admit Kähler metrics with

-positive bisectional curvature.

When

2
-positive bisectional curvature. Next we give another example of compact Kähler manifolds with positive
2
-positive bisectional curvature. Indeed, as shown in the main theorem, these are the only manifolds with
2-

3.
The Yang-Mills flow on Riemann surfaces

In this section, we present some fundamental results on the Yang Mills flow on Riemann surfaces, which are well known in literature (cf.
[
BL81
,
AB83
,
Don83
,
Rd92
,
Das92
]
). We also establish a new maximum principle for the Yang-Mills flow in general as a corollary of Hamilton’s maximum principle. The Hamilton’s maximum principles is a standard and common tool in the field of the Ricci flow and there has been many successful applications in both general Ricci flow (cf.
[
Ham86
,
BW08
,
BS09
]
) and Kähler-Ricci flow (cf.
[
Che07
,
Ni07
,
NZ25
]
).

3.1.
Fundamentals of the Yang-Mills flow on Riemann surfaces

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold with Kähler form
\omega
and
E
be a rank
r
complex vector bundle over
M
with a Hermitian metric
h
. A
unitary connection
on
(E,h)
is a complex linear connection which is compatible with the Hermitian metric
h
. Let
D_{A}
be the exterior derivative induced by a unitary connection
A
and
D_{A}=D_{A}^{\prime}+D_{A}^{\prime\prime}
be its decomposition into holomorphic part
D_{A}^{\prime}
and conjugate holomorphic part
D_{A}^{\prime\prime}
. The connection is
integrable
if
D_{A}^{\prime\prime 2}=0
. In this case,
D_{A}^{\prime\prime}
induces a unique holomorphic structure on
E
(cf.
[
Kob87
, Proposition 1.4.17]
).

We will only consider integrable unitary connections. For such a connection
A
on
(E,h)
, the
Yang-Mills energy
of
A
is defined as the
L^{2}
norm of the curvature form:

(3.1)

\YM(A)=\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}\vol_{g}.

The critical points of the Yang-Mills energy functional
\YM
are called
Yang-Mills connections
. A simple calculation of the first variation (cf.
[
BL81
, Theorem 2.21]
) shows that an integrable unitary connection
A
is a Yang-Mills connection if and only if the following equation holds

(3.2)

D_{A}^{*}F_{A}=0.

where
D_{A}^{*}
is the formal adjoint to
D_{A}
with respect to the
L^{2}
inner product induced by
g
and
h
.

Equation

3.2
is called
the Yang-Mills equation
.

The
Yang-Mills flow
is the
L^{2}
gradient flow of the Yang-Mills energy functional
\YM
. In other words, it is a time dependent connection
A(t)
such that the following evolution equation holds:

(3.3)

\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}=-D_{A}^{*}F_{A}.

The long time existence of the flow on compact Kähler manifolds was established by Donaldson (cf.
[
Don85
, Proposition 20]
):

Theorem 3.1
.

For any initial integrable and unitary connection
A
, the solution to the Yang-Mills flow
3.3
exists for all
t\geq 0
.

If we specialize to the case when the base Kähler manifold is one dimensional, namely, a Riemann surface, the following convergence result is known (cf.
[
Rd92
,
Das92
]
):

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemann surface and
(E,h)
be a Hermitian complex vector bundle. If
A(t)
is a solution to the Yang-Mills flow. Then
A(t)
converges to a Yang-Mills connection
A_{\infty}
smoothly as
t\to\infty
.

3.2.
A maximum principle for Yang-Mills flow

The curvature form
F_{A}
is an skew-Hermitian endomorphism valued
(1,1)
form. In other words, it is a section of the bundle
\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{u}(E))\subset\Omega^{2}(\End E)
. Let
\Lambda_{\omega}
be the contraction with the Kähler form
\omega
. Then
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}
is a section of the vector bundle
\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(E)
consisting of Hermitian endomorphisms. We establish a tensor maximum principle for
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}
along the Yang-Mills flow in this subsection.

Firstly, the evolution equation of the curvature form
F_{A}
can be calculated from the Yang-Mills flow
3.3
:

Lemma 3.3
.

The curvature form
F_{A}
evolves by the following equation:

(3.4)

\frac{\partial F_{A}}{\partial t}=-\Delta_{D_{A}}F_{A}.

where
\Delta_{A}=D_{A}D_{A}^{*}+D_{A}^{*}D_{A}
is the Hodge Laplacian on
\End E
valued forms.

Proof.

We work locally. Choose a local unitary frame
\{\xi_{\alpha}\}
of
E
with respect to the fixed metric
h
. Then we may identify the connection form
A
as a matrix valued 1 form and
F_{A}
as a matrix valued 2 form. The Yang-Mills equation is then equivalent to

\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}=-d^{*}F_{A}

where the RHS means that
-d^{*}
acts on each entry of
F_{A}
. Since
F_{A}=dA+A\wedge A
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{A}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}\right)+\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}\wedge A+A\wedge\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-dd^{*}F_{A}-(d^{*}F_{A})\wedge A-A\wedge(d^{*}F_{A})

\displaystyle=-D_{A}(D_{A}^{*}F_{A})=-\Delta_{D_{A}}F_{A}.

Note that the last equality follows from the Bianchi identity
D_{A}F_{A}=0
.
∎

It follows from the Kähler identity
[\Delta_{A},\Lambda_{\omega}]=\Delta_{A}\Lambda_{\omega}-\Lambda_{\omega}\Delta_{A}=0
that the evolution equation of
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}
is:

(3.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}=-\Delta_{A}(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A})=\Delta(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}).

where
\Delta=g^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\bar{j}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\End E
and
\nabla
is the connection induced by
A(t)
.

Recall the following general maximum principle for sections of vector bundles by R. Hamilton:

Theorem 3.4

(
[
Ham86
, Lemma 8.1]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold with time dependent metric
g(t)
and
E
be a vector bundle over
M
with a fixed metric
h
. Suppose that
\nabla(t)
is a time dependent linear connection on

which is compatible with

. Let

be a section of

satisfying the parabolic evolution equation

(3.6)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=\Delta f+\phi(f)

where
\Delta=g^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}
is the Laplacian induced by
g(t)
and
\nabla(t)
. For a subset
C
of
E
which is invariant under parallel translation, let
C_{x}=C\cap E_{x}
. If
C_{x}
is closed, convex and preserved by the ODE

(3.7)

\frac{df}{dt}=\phi(f),

then
C
is preserved by the flow
3.6
. Namely, if
f_{x}\in C_{x},\forall x\in M
at
t=0
, then it remains so for all
t\geq 0
. Moreover, if
f_{x}
is in the interior of
C_{x}
at some point

and

, then
f_{x}
is in the interior of
C_{x}
for any

all
t>0
.

For any section
f\in\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(E)
, its value
f_{x}
at

is Hermitian and hence orthogonally diagonolizable with real eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(f_{x})\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{r}(f_{x})
. We say that

(1)

f
is
2
-nonnegative if
\lambda_{1}(f_{x})+\lambda_{2}(f_{x})\geq 0

x\in M
;

(2)

f
is
2
-positive if
\lambda_{x,1}(f)+\lambda_{2,x}(f)>0
for all
x\in M
;

(3)

f
is
2
-quasi-positive if
\lambda_{1}(f_{x})+\lambda_{2}(f_{x})\geq 0
for all
x\in M
and the inequality is strict at one point of

;

(4)

For
\varepsilon>0
,

is
\varepsilon

\lambda_{1}(f_{x})+\lambda_{2}(f_{x})\geq\varepsilon

x\in M
.

By applying the above general maximum principle
3.4
, we obtain the following maximum principle for the Yang-Mills flow:

Corollary 3.5
.

Under the Yang-Mills flow
3.3
, the following hold:

(1)

if
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}
is
\varepsilon

2-
positive at
t=0
, then it remains so for all
t\geq 0
;

(2)

If
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}
is quasi
2-
positive at
t=0
, then it is
2-
positive for all
t>0
.

Proof.

For (1), let
C_{\varepsilon}(E)
be the subset of
\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(E)
consists of
\varepsilon

2-
positive Hermitian endomorphisms. We use the language of principal bundles (cf.
[
KN63
, Chapter II]
). Let
P
be the unitary frame bundle of
(E,h)
, which is a principal
U(r)
bundle over
M
. Let
\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(r)
be the vector space of all Hermitian matrices. The standard representation of
U(r)
on
\C^{r}
induces a representation
\rho
of
U(r)
on
\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(r)
. Then there is the isomorphism

(3.8)

\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(E)\cong P\times_{\rho}(\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(r))

where
P\times_{\rho}(\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(r))
is the associated vector bundle of
P
with respect to the representation
\rho
. Define
C_{\varepsilon}
to be the subset of
\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(r)
consisting of all
\varepsilon

2-
positive Hermitian matrices. Then under the isomorphism
3.8
,

(3.9)

C_{\varepsilon}(E)\cong P\times_{\rho}C_{\varepsilon}.

Since
C_{\varepsilon}
is closed, so is
C_{\varepsilon}(E)
. Moreover,
C_{\varepsilon}(E)
is invariant under the parallel translation because
C_{\varepsilon}
is
U(r)
invariant. It remains to show that
C_{\varepsilon}(E)_{x}
is convex for each
x\in M
. This is equivalent to the convexity of
C_{\varepsilon}
. Observe that for any
U\in\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(r)
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(U)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{r}(U)
,

\lambda_{1}(U)+\lambda_{2}(U)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle=0\\
|v_{1}|=|v_{2}|=1\end{subarray}}\langle Uv_{1},v_{1}\rangle+\langle Uv_{2},v_{2}\rangle.

It follows easily that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
is a convex function on
\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{u}(r)
and thus
C_{\varepsilon}
is convex. By combining the evolution equation
3.5
and the maximum principle
3.4
, we deduce (1).

(2) follows directly from the same argument as in (1) and the last statement in the maximum principle
3.4
.
∎

4.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section we prove the main theorem
1.3
. Firstly, we recall the notion of the pseudoindex of Fano manifolds, which is the minimal anti-canonical degree of rational curves:

Definition 4.1

(pseudoindex)
.

The
pseudoindex

i(M)
of a Fano manifold
M
is

(4.1)

i(M)=\inf\{-K_{M}\cdot C:\text{ $C$ is a rational curve on $M$}\}.

In algebraic geometry, the pseudoindex plays an important role on the classification of Fano manifolds (cf.
[
CMSB02
,
Miy04
,
DH17
]
). In particular, we shall utilize the following theorem:

Theorem 4.2

(
[
Miy04
,
DH17
]
)
.

If
M^{n}
is an
n
dimensional Fano manifold and
i(M)\geq n
, then
M
is biholomorphic to either
\C P^{n}
or the smooth quadric hypersurface
Q^{n}
.

By the above structure result, the main theorem follows from an estimate on the aniticanonical degree
-K_{M}\cdot C
of any rational curve
C
on
M
.

To that end, let
C\subseteq M
be a rational curve on
M
. By normalization, we get a holomorphic map

f:\C P^{1}\to C\subseteq M.

Let
E=f^{*}TM
be the pull-back of the holomorphic tangent bundle of
M
and
h
be the pull-back of the Kähler metric on
TM
. Equip
E
with the pull back holomorphic structure and let
A_{0}
be the Chern connection induced by
h
. Fix the standard Fubini-Study metric
\omega
on
\C P^{1}
and we deform
A_{0}
by the Yang-Mills flow
3.3
. The convergence theorem
3.2
shows that
A(t)
converges to a Yang-Mills connection
A_{\infty}
. The following lemma is a well known property of Yang-Mills connection on Riemann surfaces (see for example,
[
AB83
]
). For reader’s convinence, we include a proof here:

Lemma 4.3
.

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A_{\infty}}
is parallel with respect to the connection
A_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Note that the Yang-Mills equation is equivalent to
D_{A}(\star F_{A_{\infty}})=0
, where
\star
is the Hodge star operator on
\End E
valued 2 forms. Moreover, since the base manifold is a Riemann surface,
\star F_{A_{\infty}}
is equal to
\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A_{\infty}}
up to a constant multiple. Hence,
D_{A}(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A_{\infty}})=0
.
∎

This lemma implies that
\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A_{\infty}}
has constant eigenvalues
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
everywhere. Suppose that
\lambda_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{k}
are the distinct eigenvalues. It induces an orthogonal splitting

(4.2)

E=E_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{k}

where

(4.3)

\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A_{\infty}}|_{E_{l}}=\lambda_{l}\Id_{E_{l}},l=1,\cdots,k.

Since
\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A_{\infty}}
is parallel, each
E_{l}
is invariant under parallel translation. It follows that
E_{l}
’s are holomorphic subbundles of
(E,D^{\prime\prime}_{A_{\infty}})
. Because of
4.3
,
h
is a Hermitian-Einstein metric on
E_{l}
. By the easy direction of Kobayashi-Hitchin correspondence (cf.
[
Kob80
,
Lub83
]
),
E_{l}
is polystable and thus
E_{l}
is a direct sum of copies of
\mathcal{O}(\lambda_{l})
. Therefore,
a_{i}
’s are all integers and there is the isomorphism

(4.4)

E\cong\mathcal{O}(a_{1})\oplus\cdots\mathcal{\oplus}{O}(a_{n}).

Next, we show that
a_{1}+a_{2}\geq 1
. By the assumption that
M
has
2-
positive bisectional curvature,
(E,h)
has
2-
quasi-positive curvature. (Note that we can only get quasi-positivity here because
df
may vanish at some points of
\C P^{1}
.) It then follows from (2) of Corollary
3.5
that
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}
becomes
2-
positive for some time
t_{0}>0
. By compactness, we may assume that
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}
is
\varepsilon

2-
positive at
t_{0}
for some positive number
\varepsilon
. Apply (1) of Corollary
3.5
and we deduce that
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A_{\infty}}
is also
\varepsilon

2-
positive and in particular,

(4.5)

a_{1}+a_{2}>0\Rightarrow a_{1}+a_{2}\geq 1.

Let
D
be the divisor consisting of points where
df
vanishes, counted with order and
\mathcal{O}(D)
be the associated line bundle. Then
\mathcal{O}(D)\cong\mathcal{O}(k),k\geq 0
. Note that
T_{\C P^{1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}(E)\cong\mathcal{O}(2+k)
is a subbundle of
E
. It follows that
a_{n}\geq 2+k\geq 2
. Since
n\geq 3
, by combining with
4.5
, we get the desired estimate on the anti-canonical degree of the rational curve
C
:

(4.6)

-K_{M}\cdot C=\langle c_{1}(E),[\C P^{1}]\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=(a_{1}+a_{2})+(a_{3}+\cdots+a_{n})\geq 1+(n-3)+2=n.

Hence, the pseudoindex
i(M)\geq n
. This completes the proof of the main theorem.

5.
Further Discussions

Continuing under the theme of this note, we talk about some related questions in this section.

The
2-
positivity of the bisectional curvature can be generalized to
m-
positivity on Fano manifolds:

Definition 5.1

(
m-
positive bisectional curvature)
.

Under the notations of
Section

2
, for any integer
1\leq m\leq n
,
(M,g)
has
m-
positive bisectional curvature if

\lambda_{1}(x,U)+\cdots+\lambda_{m}(x,U)>0,\quad\forall x\in M,\forall U\in T_{x}M.

Remark 5.2
.

As we noted in the introduction, the
m-
positivity here is different from the one defined in
[
Siu80
]
.

When
m=n
, the
m-
positivity of the bisectional curvature is just the Ricci positivity and it imposes no further restrictions on Fano manifolds. Therefore, we consider the case when
m<n
. By the same argument as in the proof of the main theorem, one can show that

Proposition 5.3
.

If
n>m
and
M
is an
n
dimensional compact Kähler manifold with
m-
positive bisectional curvature, then there is a lower bound on the pseudoindex of
M
:

(5.1)

i(M)\geq n-m+2.

Given this and Fujita’s classification of
n
dimensional Fano manifolds with pseudoindex
n-1
(cf.
[
Fuj90
]
), our main theorem is expected to be extended to a characterization theorem of compact Kähler manifolds with Kähler metrics whose bisectional curvature is
3-
positive. Of course, extra work is required to check whether or not each member in Fujita’s list admits Kähler metrics with
3-
positive bisectional curvature.

It is also interesting to know if the converse of Proposition
5.3
is true or not. When
m=1,2
, it is true by the pseudoindex characterization of
\C P^{n}
and
Q^{n}
, along with our calculation in
Section

2
. The case when
m=3
might be handled similarly due to Fujita’s classification. However, the remaining cases seems unknown. In other words, if
n>m>2
and
M^{n}
is a compact Kähler manifold with
i(M)\geq n-m+2
, does
M
admit a Kähler metric with
m-
positive bisectional curvature?

We are able to prove the main theorem because there are classification theorems when the pseudoindex is large. When the pseudoindex is smaller than
n-1
, there is no longer full classification results available in algebraic geometry. Instead, given the close relation between
m-
positivity and pseudoindex, it is also interesting to consider the differential geometric approach to classifying Fano manifolds based on
m-
positivity with geometric analytic methods such as the Kähler-Ricci flow.

There are also various related curvature conditions in literature, for example, those in
[
NWZ21
,
Ni21
,
NZ25
]
. Here we discuss the positivity of orthogonal Ricci curvature, proposed in
[
NWZ21
]
. Let
M^{n}
be an
n
dimensional compact Kähler manifold. For any holomorphic tangent vector
X
, the orthogonal Ricci curvature along
X
is defined as

(5.2)

\Ricci^{\perp}(X)=\Ricci(X,\bar{X})-H(X)

where
H(X)
is the holomorphic sectional curvature along
X
.
M^{n}
has positive orthogonal Ricci curvature if
\Ricci^{\perp}(X)>0
for any holomorphic tangent vector
X
. It is clear that the positivity of
\Ricci^{\perp}
is weaker than
m-
positivity of bisectional curvature for all
m<n
. Among other things, the paper
[
NWZ21
]
deduced a complete classification of 3 dimensional Kähler manifolds with
\Ricci^{\perp}>0
:

Theorem 5.4

(
[
NWZ21
, Theorem 1.8]
)
.

A 3 dimensional compact Kähler manifold with
\Ricci^{\perp}>0
is biholomorphic to either
\C P^{3}
or
Q^{3}
.

This is a generalization of our main theorem in 3 dimensional case. In dimension 4, the paper also obtains a partial classification result. In higher dimensions, much less is known so far. A better knowledge on
m-
positive bisectional curvature should also be useful towards understanding the positive orthogonal Ricci curvature in higher dimensions.

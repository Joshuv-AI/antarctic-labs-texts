Symbolic calculus for a class of pseudodifferential operators with applications to compactness
Árpád Bényi, Tadahiro Oh, and Rodolfo H. Torres

Abstract
. We prove a symbolic calculus for a class of pseudodifferential operators, and discuss its applications to L 2 L^{2} -compactness via a compact version of the T ⁡ ( 1 ) T(1) theorem.

1.
Introduction

Starting with the work of Villarroya
[
25
]
, there has been some interest in developing versions of the

T(1)
theorem of David and Journé
[
14
]
to characterize not only the boundedness of Calderón-Zygmund operators but also their compactness; see, for example,
[
19
,
15
,
1
,
7
,
2
]
. Unfortunately, while elegant and highly non-trivial, such characterizations have not revealed many new examples of compact Calderón-Zygmund operators. Perhaps the most important examples of compact Calderón-Zygmund operators are certain paraproducts, but their compactness can be proved by other methods,
and in fact they are used in the proof of the characterizations of compactness. One of the purposes of this article is to provide some new examples involving pseudodifferential operators and their commutators.

Sufficient conditions for the compactness of pseudodifferential operators on
L^{2}
were originally provided by Cordes
[
13
]
. However, such conditions fail to produce Calderón-Zygmund operators. In fact, while compact on
L^{2}
, Cordes’ operators may not even be bounded on
L^{p}
for other
p\in(1,\infty)
. In a recent work
[
9
]
, Carro, Soria and the last named author of this article combined Cordes’ conditions with the typical decay of the Hörmander class
S_{1,0}^{0}
to obtain, via a new extrapolation result, compactness of the resulting Calderón-Zygmund operators on weighted
L^{p}(w)
spaces for all
1<p<\infty
and all weights
w
in the classical Muckenhoupt classes
A_{p}
(whose definition will not be needed in this article)
1
1

1

The result can also be obtained by more general extrapolation results in
[
8
,
17
]
. However, the more restrictive extrapolation in
[
9
]
has a straightforward proof and produces a stronger uniformly compact result in some appropriate sense. See
[
9
]
for further details.
. The result in
[
9
]
was presented at the conference in honor of Professor Jill Pipher to whom this article is dedicated. Since then there has been some interest in looking at further compactness results for pseudodifferential operators; see
[
23
]
.

As in the case of the classical
T(1)
theorem, the analogous characterizations of compactness required conditions which are symmetric on the operator
T
in question and its transpose
T^{*}
. This usually creates a non-trivial task, when the operator is presented by a symbol in pseudodifferential form and not via an explicit kernel, to verify that the class of symbols is invariant by transposition (which, as it is known, is not always the case for
general pseudodifferential operators). We will develop a symbolic calculus for the operators considered in
[
9
]

(see Theorem
2.1
), and then use it to prove a new result on the compactness of commutators of a certain class of pseudodifferential operators and multiplication by bounded Lipschitz functions, using indeed the compact
T(1)
theorem;
see Theorem
4.1
.

2.
A class of pseudodifferential operators

We start by recalling the Hörmander classes of symbols
S_{1,0}^{s}
.
Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we say that a symbol
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to the Hörmander class
S_{1,0}^{s}
, and we write
\sigma\in S_{1,0}^{s}
, if it satisfies

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\sigma(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{s-|\beta|}

for all multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
. Associated with such a symbol
\sigma
, we have the pseudodifferential operator
T_{\sigma}
, a priori defined from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, given by

\displaystyle T_{\sigma}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sigma(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi.

It is a standard fact that
S_{1,0}^{0}
is closed under transposition. That is, if
\sigma\in S_{1,0}^{0}
, then
(T_{\sigma})^{*}=T_{\sigma^{*}}
with
\sigma^{*}\in S_{1,0}^{0}
; see, for example,
[
22
, p. 259]
(see also
[
5
, Theorem 1]
for a bilinear version of this fact). Moreover, if
\sigma\in S_{1,0}^{0}
, then
T_{\sigma}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator.

In what follows, we restrict our attention to a
vanishing at infinity
sub-class of
S_{1,0}^{0}
considered in
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
; see also
[
13
]
. We say that a symbol
\sigma=\sigma(x,\xi)
belongs to the class
V_{1,0}^{s}
if it satisfies

(2.1)

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\sigma(x,\xi)|\leq K_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)(1+|\xi|)^{s-|\beta|}

for all multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
, where
K_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)
is a bounded function such that

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|+|\xi|\to\infty}K_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)=0.

Our main result is the following symbolic calculus for the class
V_{1,0}^{s}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
\sigma\in V_{1,0}^{s}
, then
(T_{\sigma})^{*}=T_{\sigma^{*}}
with
\sigma^{*}\in V_{1,0}^{s}
. Moreover, we have the asymptotic expansion

(2.2)

\sigma^{*}(x,\xi)=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{i^{-|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\sigma(x,-\xi),

in the sense that, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2.3)

\sigma^{*}(x,\xi)-\sum_{|\alpha|<N}\frac{i^{-|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\sigma(x,-\xi)\in V_{1,0}^{s-N}.

Proof.

As mentioned above, the calculus in the statement of the theorem holds with
V_{1,0}^{s}
replaced by the larger class
S_{1,0}^{s}
.
An additional complexity in the current setting is a careful accounting of the constants involved in several estimates (which depend on the spatial variable
x
and the frequency variable
\xi
)
in showing that they vanish at infinity.
In the following, we will recast the computations usually done for
S_{1,0}^{s}

by following closely other presentations in the literature. In particular, the reader may consult
[
22
, pp. 238–240 and pp. 258–259]
, or the more elaborate presentation in
[
5
]
(for
s=0
) for some of the omitted details and computations.

Given
\sigma\in V_{1,0}^{s}
, it suffices to prove the result for symbols of the form:

(2.4)

\sigma_{\epsilon}(x,\xi)=\sigma(x,\xi)u(\epsilon x,\epsilon\xi),

where
u(x,\xi)
is a
C^{\infty}
function with compact support and
u(0,0)=1
,
but with estimates independent of the support of

\sigma_{\epsilon}
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Assuming that this is the case, a standard limiting argument removes the above truncation of the symbol.
Henceforth, and for simplicity of notation, we assume that
\sigma(x,\xi)
is one of the
\sigma_{\epsilon}(x,\xi)
with compact support.

For
f,g\in\mathcal{S}
, we have

\langle T_{\sigma}(f),g\rangle=\int_{y}\int_{x}\int_{\xi}\sigma(x,\xi)g(x)e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}d\xi dx\,f(y)dy,

where the integral is absolutely convergent due to the compact support assumption on
\sigma
. It follows from here that

T_{\sigma}^{*}(g)(x)=\int_{y}\int_{\xi}\sigma(y,-\xi)g(y)e^{-i(y-x)\cdot\xi}d\xi dy=:T_{[c]}(g)(x),

where

(2.5)

\displaystyle c(y,\xi):=\sigma(y,-\xi)

and
T_{[c]}
is a so-called
compound operator
.
It is clear that
c
satisfies the same bound (
2.1
) as
\sigma
. The next step is to show that, given the compound operator
T_{[c]}
, there exists a symbol
\sigma^{*}\in V_{1,0}^{0}
such that
T_{[c]}=T_{\sigma^{*}}
, and also show that the asymptotic expansion (
2.2
) for
\sigma^{*}
holds. The computation below is justified by the assumption on
\sigma
having compact support. We have

\displaystyle T_{[c]}(g)(x)

\displaystyle=\iint c(y,\xi)g(y)e^{-i(y-x)\cdot\xi}d\xi dy

\displaystyle=\int_{z}\int_{\xi}\int_{y}(2\pi)^{-d}c(y,\xi)e^{-i(y-x)\cdot\xi}e^{i(y-x)\cdot z}dyd\xi\,\widehat{g}(z)e^{ix\cdot z}dz

\displaystyle=T_{\sigma^{*}}(g)(x),

where

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}(x,\xi)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{z}\int_{y}c(y,z)e^{i(x-y)\cdot(z-\xi)}dydz.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
to be an even function such that
\phi(y)=1
for
|y|\leq 1
and
\phi(y)=0
for
|y|>2
. We can then further write

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(2\pi)^{d}\sigma^{*}(x,\xi)&=\iint\phi(x-y)c(y,z)e^{i(x-y)\cdot(z-\xi)}dydz+r(x,\xi)\\
&=\iint\phi(y)c(x+y,z+\xi)e^{-iy\cdot z}dydz+r(x,\xi)\\
&=m(x,\xi)+r(x,\xi),\end{split}

where

(2.8)

\displaystyle r(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{z}\int_{y}(1-\phi(x-y))c(y,z)e^{i(x-y)\cdot(z-\xi)}dydz,

(2.9)

\displaystyle m(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{z}\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,z+\xi)dz.

Here,
\widehat{b}^{(2)}
denotes the Fourier transform with respect of the
y
-variable of

(2.10)

\displaystyle b(x,y,\xi):=\phi(y)c(x+y,\xi).

We note that
b
satisfies similar estimates to
c
, that is

(2.11)

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{y}^{\tau}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}b(x,y,\xi)|\leq A_{\alpha,\tau,\beta}(x,y,\xi)(1+|\xi|)^{s-|\beta|},

where
A_{\alpha,\tau,\beta}
is a bounded function with compact support in
all the three variables
(in particular, supported on
\{|y|\leq 2\}
).

Let us first concentrate on the remainder term
r(x,\xi)
in (
2.8
). Let
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
with
N_{2}>d/2
and
N_{1}\geq 2N_{2}
. Integration by parts gives

r(x,\xi)=\int_{y}\int_{z}\frac{(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_{y})^{N_{1}}\Delta_{z}^{N_{2}}\big((1-\phi(x-y))c(y,z)\big)}{(1+|z-\xi|^{2})^{N_{1}}|x-y|^{2N_{2}}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot(z-\xi)}dzdy,

where we are integrating (in
y
) over
|y-x|>1
.
By (
2.5
) and (
2.1
), we have

|r(x,\xi)|\leq\int_{y}\int_{z}\frac{B_{N_{1},N_{2}}(x,y,z)(1+|z|)^{s-2N_{2}}}{(1+|z-\xi|)^{2N_{1}}(1+|x-y|)^{2N_{2}}}dzdy,

where
B_{N_{1},N_{2}}
is a bounded function with compact support in
y
and
z
.
By applying Peetre’s inequality (with
k=2N_{2}
)

(2.12)

(1+|z|)^{s-k}\leq(1+|\xi|)^{s-k}(1+|z-\xi|)^{|s|+k},k\geq 0,

and setting
N_{1}=N+\lceil|s|\rceil
and
N_{2}=N/2
for some large
N
(which we can assume to be even),
the last integral can then be estimated by

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(1+|\xi|)^{s-N}\int_{y}\int_{z}\frac{B_{N,N/2}(x,y,z)}{(1+|z-\xi|)^{N}(1+|x-y|)^{N}}dzdy\\
&\quad=:(1+|\xi|)^{s-N}\widetilde{B}_{N}(x,\xi).\end{split}

Note that
the integral defining
\widetilde{B}_{N}(x,\xi)
is absolutely convergent,
with the bound independent of the support of
\sigma
.
Furthermore, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|+|\xi|\to\infty}\widetilde{B}_{N}(x,\xi)=0.

To see this last fact, notice that
B_{N,N/2}(x,y,z)
can be controlled by a finite sum of the form:

(2.15)

\displaystyle B_{N,N/2}(x,y,z)\lesssim\sum_{j}L_{j}(x-y)H_{j}(y,z),

where the functions
L_{j}
and
H_{j}
are bounded, and
H_{j}(y,z)\to 0
when
|y|+|z|\to\infty
, actually independently of the support
in view of (
2.4
) and the estimates
(
2.1
)
satisfied by the original symbol
\sigma
.
Thus, it follows from (
2.13
) and (
2.15
)
that
\widetilde{B}_{N}(x,\xi)
can be controlled by a finite sum of terms of the form:

\int_{|y|>1}\int_{z}\frac{L_{j}(y)H_{j}(x-y,z+\xi)}{(1+|z|)^{N}(1+|y|)^{N}}dzdy

to which the dominated convergence theorem (in the parameter
(x,\xi)
) can be easily applied.
Hence, for any large
N\gg 1
, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle|r(x,\xi)|\leq\widetilde{B}_{N}(x,\xi)(1+|\xi|)^{s-N}

where
\widetilde{B}_{N}
satisfies (
2.14
).
A similar calculation will show that, for
any large
N\gg 1
, we also have

(2.17)

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}r(x,\xi)|\leq\widetilde{B}_{N;\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)(1+|\xi|)^{s-N},

where

\widetilde{B}_{N;\alpha,\beta}

is a bounded function such that

\widetilde{B}_{N;\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)\to 0

as
|x|+|\xi|\to\infty
.
In other words,
the remainder term
r
is smoothing of infinite order
.

Next, we analyze the main term
m(x,\xi)
in (
2.9
). Given a multi-index
\tau
, using (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle|z^{\tau}\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,z+\xi)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{y}b(x,y,z+\xi)\partial_{y}^{\tau}(e^{-iy\cdot z})dy\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{y}(\partial_{y}^{\tau}b(x,y,z+\xi))e^{-iy\cdot z}dy\bigg|\leq\int_{y}|\partial_{y}^{\tau}b(x,y,z+\xi)|dy

\displaystyle\leq(1+|z+\xi|)^{s}\int_{y}A_{0,\tau,0}(x,y,z+\xi)dy.

Thus, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

|\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,z+\xi)|\leq A^{\prime}_{N}(x,z+\xi)(1+|z+\xi|)^{s}(1+|z|)^{-N},

where
A^{\prime}_{N}
is a bounded function such that
A^{\prime}_{N}(x,z+\xi)\to 0
as
|x|+|z+\xi|\to\infty
.
By a similar computation, for any multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
, we have

(2.18)

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,z+\xi)|\leq A^{\prime}_{N;\alpha,\beta}(x,z\xi)(1+|z+\xi|)^{s-|\beta|}(1+|z|)^{-N},

where
A^{\prime}_{N;\alpha,\beta}

is a bounded function such that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|+|\xi|\to\infty}A^{\prime}_{N;\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)=0.

We note from (
2.10
)
that the functions
A^{\prime}_{N;\alpha,\beta}
do not depend on the support of
\sigma
as they are obtained by integrating over
\{|y|\leq 2\}
.
From (
2.9
),
(
2.18
), and
an appropriate version of (
2.12
), namely

(1+|z+\xi|)^{s-|\beta|}\leq(1+|\xi|)^{s-|\beta|}(1+|z|)^{|s|+|\beta|},

we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}m(x,\xi)|&\leq\int A^{\prime}_{N;\alpha,\beta}(x,z+\xi)(1+|z+\xi|)^{s-|\beta|}(1+|z|)^{-N}dz\\
&\leq(1+|\xi|)^{s-|\beta|}\int A^{\prime}_{N;\alpha,\beta}(x,z+\xi)(1+|z|)^{-N+|s|+|\beta|}dz.\end{split}

Now, let

(2.21)

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi):=\int A^{\prime}_{d+1+\lceil|s|\rceil+|\beta|;\alpha,\beta}(x,z+\xi)(1+|z|)^{-d-1}dz.

Then, from (
2.19
)
and
the dominated convergence theorem,
we see that
A^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}
is a bounded function such that

A^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)\to 0
as
|x|+|\xi|\to\infty
.
Hence, from (
2.20
) with
(with
N=d+1+\lceil|s|\rceil+|\beta|
) and (
2.21
),
we obtain

(2.22)

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}m(x,\xi)|\leq A^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)(1+|\xi|)^{s-|\beta|},

where
A^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}
is a bounded function, vanishing at infinity,
and is independent of the support of the symbol
\sigma
.
Therefore, from (
2.17
) and (
2.22
),
we conclude that
\sigma^{*}
defined in (
2.6
) is in
V_{1,0}^{s}
, with estimates independent of the support.

Next, we show that
\sigma^{*}
has
the asymptotic expansion (
2.2
)
in the sense of (
2.3
).
On the one hand, given
M,N\in\mathbb{N}
,
by applying the Taylor remainder theorem
to
\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,z+\xi)

in the third argument,
(
2.18
),
and Peetre’s inequality, we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bigg|\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,z+\xi)-\sum_{|\alpha|<N}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,\xi)\frac{z^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\bigg|\\
&\quad=|z|^{N}\bigg|\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\frac{N}{\alpha!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{N-1}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,tz+\xi)dt\bigg|\\
&\quad\lesssim(1+|z|)^{N-M}\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\int_{0}^{1}A^{\prime}_{M;0,\alpha}(x,tz+\xi)(1+|tz+\xi|)^{s-N}dt\\
&\quad\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{s-N}(1+|z|)^{|s|+2N-M}\widetilde{A}_{M;N}(x,z,\xi),\end{split}

where

(2.24)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{M;N}(x,z,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\int_{0}^{1}A^{\prime}_{M;0,\alpha}(x,tz+\xi)dt.

Note that, for fixed
z
,
the condition
|x|+|\xi|\gg|z|

implies

|x|+|tz+\xi|\sim|x|+|\xi|
uniformly in
0\leq t\leq 1
.
Then, from (
2.24
) and (
2.19
),
we see that

\widetilde{A}_{M;N}
is a bounded function such that

(2.25)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|+|\xi|\to\infty}\widetilde{A}_{M;N}(x,z,\xi)=0

for each fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

On the other hand,
from (
2.5
), the fact that
\phi\equiv 1
in the neighborhood of
y=0
,
and (
2.10
),
we have

(2.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\sigma(x,-\xi)&=\partial_{y}^{\alpha}\big(\phi(y)c(x+y,\xi)\big)|_{y=0}=\partial_{y}^{\alpha}b(x,0,\xi)\\
&=\frac{{i}^{|\alpha|}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int z^{\alpha}\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,\xi)dz.\end{split}

Hence, from (
2.7
), (
2.9
),
(
2.26
),
(
2.23
) with
M=2N+\lceil|s|\rceil+d+1
,
and (
2.16
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\sigma^{*}(x,\xi)-\sum_{|\alpha|<N}\frac{i^{-|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\sigma(x,-\xi)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\bigg|\widehat{b}^{(2)}(x,z,z+\xi)-\sum_{|\alpha|<N}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\widehat{b}^{2}(x,z,\xi)\frac{z^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\bigg|dz+|r(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\widetilde{K}_{N}(x,\xi)(1+|\xi|)^{s-N},

where

\widetilde{K}_{N}(x,\xi)=\widetilde{B}_{N}(x,\xi)+\int\widetilde{A}_{2N+\lceil|s|\rceil+d+1;N}(x,z,\xi)(1+|z|)^{-d-1}dz.

From (
2.14
), (
2.25
),
and the dominated convergence theorem, we see that

\widetilde{K}_{N}(x,\xi)
is a bounded function such that

\widetilde{K}_{N}(x,\xi)\to 0
as
|x|+|\xi|\to\infty
.
A similar computation
yields an analogous bound
on

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\bigg(\sigma^{*}(x,\xi)-\sum_{|\alpha|<N}\frac{i^{-|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\sigma(x,-\xi)\bigg),

thus establishing (
2.3
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The conditions on the symbol assumed by Cordes
[
13
]
(corresponding to the case
s=0
in (
2.1
)) are only that
\sigma
has continuous and bounded derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\sigma
for all multi-indices
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 2N
, where

N=\big[\frac{d}{2}\big]+1
, and that

(2.27)

(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_{x})^{N}(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}\sigma(x,\xi)\longrightarrow 0,

as
|x|+|\xi|\to\infty
. However, as mentioned already, the corresponding operator cannot be Calderón-Zygmund (see the definition in the next section) since it may not be even bounded on
L^{p}
for
p\neq 2
. If one assumes, in addition to (
2.27
), that the symbol belongs to
S^{0}_{1,0}
, then the corresponding pseudodifferential operator becomes bounded on all weighted spaces
L^{p}(w)
, where
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}
, and by the extrapolation results in
[
8
,
17
]
,
it can be proved to be compact. Likewise, in the main result in
[
9
]
, the conditions in (
2.1
) need to be assumed only for a (large) finite number of derivatives. These conditions on the symbol
\sigma
are hence weaker than
\sigma
belonging to
V_{1,0}^{0}
, for which we impose (
2.1
) for

all
the derivatives of
\sigma

in order for
the symbolic calculus, and in particular, for the expansion formula (
2.3
), to hold.

Remark 2.3
.

Symbols in the class
V_{1,0}^{0}
are very easy to construct. Trivially, if we let

\sigma(x,\xi)=m(x)\psi(\xi),

where
m,\psi\in C^{\infty}
,
|\partial^{\alpha}m(x)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
and
\psi
has compact support, then

\sigma(x,\xi)\in V^{-N}_{1,0}

for all
N
.

More interestingly, one can consider the following modification of the
elementary symbols à la Coifman-Meyer
; see
[
11
, p. 41]
. Given
m_{j},\psi\in C^{\infty}
such that
|\partial^{\alpha}m_{j}(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}
for all
x
,
j
and
\alpha
,
\partial^{\alpha}m_{j}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
for each
\alpha
and uniformly in
j
, and
\psi
compactly supported on
\big\{\frac{1}{2}<|\xi|<2\big\}
, define

(2.28)

\sigma(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}m_{j}(x)2^{-j}\psi(2^{-j}\xi).

Due to the condition on the support of
\psi
, for a fixed
(x,\xi)
there are only finitely many terms in the summation above. More precisely, for a fixed
j_{0}
and
2^{j_{0}-1}<|\xi|<2^{j_{0}+1}
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\sigma(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=-1}^{1}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}m_{j_{0}+k}(x)|2^{-j_{0}-k}|\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\psi(2^{-j_{0}-k}\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}2^{-j_{0}(|\beta|+1)}\sum_{k=-1}^{1}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}m_{j_{0}+k}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{-(|\beta|+1)}\sum_{k=-1}^{1}\sup_{j}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}m_{j+k}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim K_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)(1+|\xi|)^{-|\beta|},

where
K_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)\to 0
as
|x|+|\xi|\to\infty.
Analogous to the case of symbols in
S_{1,0}^{0}
, it may be possible to write every symbol in
V_{1,0}^{0}
as some combination of elementary symbols of the
form (
2.28
). Interested readers may consider adapting some of the methods suggested in

[
5
, Lemma 1]
to see if such a result holds in this context.

3.
Compact
T(1)
theorems

In this section, we collect several definitions and compactness versions of the
T(1)
theorem.

Definition 3.1
.

A continuous, linear operator
T:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be a singular integral operator with a Calderón-Zygmund kernel
K
if the restriction of its distributional Schwartz kernel to the set
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}:x\neq y\}
agrees with a locally integrable function
K
satisfying

(3.1)

|\partial^{\beta}K(x,y)|\leq C_{K}|x-y|^{-d-|\beta|},\quad|\beta|\leq 1.

In particular, we have

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x,y)f(y)dy

for
x\notin\operatorname{supp}f
.

While weaker regularity conditions than (
3.1
) can be assumed, in the case of the pseudodifferential operators we consider in this article, the condition (
3.1
)
is actually satisfied for derivatives of any order
|\beta|\in\mathbb{N}

(but with constants depending on
|\beta|
).
A singular integral operator with a Calderón-Zygmund kernel is said to be a Calderón-Zygmund operator,
if it extends as a bounded linear operator
T:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In such a case, standard results of the Calderón-Zygmund theory imply that the operator
T
is also bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
1<p<\infty
. Appropriate end-point and weighted estimates
are also known to hold for such
T
.

As usual, we write
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the space of
C^{\infty}
-functions with compact support, and
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the space of continuous functions vanishing at infinity.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a function
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
a
normalized bump function
of order
M

if
\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset B_{0}(1)

and
\|\partial^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1

for all multi-indices
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq M
.
Here, given
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

B_{x}(r)
denotes the ball of radius
r
centered at
x
.

Given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
R>0
, we set

(3.2)

\displaystyle\phi^{x_{0},R}(x)=\phi\bigg(\frac{x-x_{0}}{R}\bigg).

The statement of the
T(1)
theorem of David and Journé
[
14
]
is the following.

Theorem A

(
T(1)
theorem)
.

Let
T:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a linear singular integral operator
with a Calderón-Zygmund kernel.
Then,
T
can be extended to a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

if and only if
it satisfies the following two conditions
:

(A.i)

T
satisfies the weak boundedness property
;

there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

such that we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle T(\phi_{1}^{x_{1},R}),\phi_{2}^{x_{2},R}\big\rangle\big|\lesssim R^{d}

for any normalized bump functions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}

of order
M
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and
R>0
.

(A.ii)

T(1)

and
T^{*}(1)

are in
BMO
.

Definition 3.3
.

We say that a linear singular integral operator
T:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with a Calderón-Zygmund kernel
satisfies the
weak compactness property

if there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

such that we have

\displaystyle\lim_{|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty}R^{-d}\big|\big\langle T(\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}),\phi_{2}^{x_{0}+x_{2},R}\big\rangle\big|=0

for any normalized bump functions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}

of order
M
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In order to state the compact counterparts of the
T(1)
theorem,
we also need to recall the following important subspace of
BMO
.

Definition 3.4
.

The closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the
BMO
topology is called the space of functions of
continuous mean oscillation
, and it is denoted by
CMO
.
It also holds that
\textit{CMO}=\overline{C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\textit{BMO}}
;
see
[
6
, Théorème 7]
.

The following version of the compact
T(1)
theorem was recently proved in
[
19
]
;
see also
[
25
]
.
For a version of a compact
T(b)
theorem, see
[
26
]
.

Theorem B

(compact
T(1)
theorem)
.

Let
T
be as in Theorem
A
.
Then,
T
can be extended to a compact operator on
L^{2}

if and only if
it satisfies the following two conditions
:

(B.i)

T
satisfies the weak compactness property.

(B.ii)

T(1)

and
T^{*}(1)

are in
CMO
.

In the following, we say that a linear singular integral operator
T

with a
Calderón-Zygmund kernel
is a
compact Calderón-Zygmund operator
if it is compact on
L^{2}
.

Remark 3.5
.

If
T
is a compact Calderón-Zygmund operator,
then it is also compact in the following settings:

(a)

On

L^{p}
for any
1<p<\infty
,
this follows from interpolation
of compactness

[
18
]
.

(b)

From
L^{\infty}
to
CMO
; see

[
21
]
.
.

(c)

From the Hardy space
H^{1}
to
L^{1}
;
see
[
20
, p. 1285]
.

(d)

From
L^{1}
to
L^{1,\infty}
; see
[
20
]
.

(e)

From
BMO
to
CMO

under the cancellation assumption that
T(1)=T^{*}(1)=0
; see
[
21
]
.

(f)

From
H^{1}
to
H^{1}

under the cancellation assumption that
T(1)=T^{*}(1)=0
; this follows from (e) by duality, but see also
[
2
]
.

Remark 3.6
.

Our definitions of the weak boundedness property (
3.3
)
and the weak compactness property (Definition
3.3
)
are slightly different from those in
[
14
,
19
]
,
but one can easily check that they are equivalent.

As in the boundedness case
(see
[
22
, Theorem 3 on p. 294]
and
also
[
16
,
4
]
),
it is possible to state a version of the compact
T(1)
theorem without referring to
T(1)
at all. The following version was established in
[
2
]
.

Theorem C

(compact
T(1)
theorem à la Stein)
.

Let
T
be as in Theorem
A
.
Then,
T
can be extended to a compact operator
on
L^{2}

if and only if
the following two conditions hold
:

(i)

(
L^{2}
-condition).

There exists
M\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty}\bigg(R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|T(\phi^{x_{0},R})\|_{L^{2}}+R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|T^{*}(\phi^{x_{0},R})\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)=0

holds for any normalized bump function
\phi

of order
M
.

(ii)

(
L^{\infty}
-condition).

Given any
weak-
\ast
convergent sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

in
L^{\infty}

such that
\{T(f_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

and
\{T^{*}(f_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

are bounded in
CMO
,
both the sequences

\{T(f_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

and
\{T^{*}(f_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

are precompact in
CMO
.

4.
An application

In this section, we present an application
of our symbolic calculus and the compact
T(1)
theorem.

We first note that for a pseudodifferential operator with symbol
\sigma
,
we have
T_{\sigma}(1)=\sigma(x,0)
. Thus, if
\sigma\in V_{1,0}^{0}
,
then we have

|\sigma(x,0)|\leq K_{\alpha}(x,0)\longrightarrow 0,

as
|x|\to\infty
.
Moreover,
since

\sigma\in S_{1,0}^{0}
,
T_{\sigma}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator
and thus it follows from Theorem
A

that
T_{\sigma}(1)\in\textit{BMO}
.
In particular,
this implies that
T_{\sigma}(1)=\sigma(x,0)\in\textit{CMO}=\overline{C_{0}}^{\textit{BMO}}
.
In view of Theorem
2.1
,
we have
\sigma^{*}\in V^{0}_{1,0}

and thus
we also obtain
T_{\sigma}^{*}(1)=T_{\sigma^{*}}(1)\in\textit{CMO}
.

We already know
from
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]

that the class
V^{0}_{1,0}
actually produces compact operators on
L^{p}(w)
for all
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}
.
We will use the symbolic calculus in a crucial way to show the compactness of the commutators of
pseudodifferential operators with symbols in
V_{1,0}^{1}
with pointwise multiplication by bounded Lipschitz functions.

The commutator
[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]

of a pseudodifferential operator
T_{\sigma}
with the multiplication
M_{a}
by the function
a
is given by

[T_{\sigma},M_{a}](f)=(T_{\sigma}M_{a}-M_{a}T_{\sigma})(f)=T_{\sigma}(af)-aT_{\sigma}(f).

Recall that if
\sigma\in S_{1,0}^{1}
and
a\in\textit{BMO}
then
[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]
is bounded on
L^{2}
, while if
a\in\textit{CMO}
then
[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]
is compact on
L^{2}
; see
[
12
]
and
[
24
]
, respectively. On the other hand, if
\sigma\in V^{0}_{1,0}
and
a\in L^{\infty}
, then it is trivial to see that
[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]
is a compact operator on
L^{2}
because the class of compact operators is a two-sided ideal of the algebra of bounded operators.

Let now
\sigma\in V_{1,0}^{1}
and
a
be a bounded Lipschitz function, that is
a\in L^{\infty}
and
\nabla a\in L^{\infty}
.
Since we have
V_{1,0}^{1}\subset S_{1,0}^{1}
,
it follows from
[
10
, Theorem 4 on p. 90]
that the commutator

[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]
is a linear Calderón-Zygmund operator,
bounded on
L^{p}
for any
1<p<\infty
.
In the following,
we show that this boundedness can be improved to compactness for symbols
\sigma\in V_{1,0}^{1}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\sigma\in V_{1,0}^{1}
and let
a
be a bounded Lipschitz function.
Then,
[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]
is a compact
Calderón-Zygmund operator.

Proof.

We will follow the argument in
[
3
]

for boundedness of commutators in the bilinear setting.
We break the proof of Theorem
4.1
into several steps.

\bullet

Step 1:

Reduction from
V_{1,0}^{1}
to
V_{1,0}^{0}
.

Let
\widetilde{\sigma}(x,\xi)=\sigma(x,0)\psi(\xi)
,
where
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}
and
\psi(0)=1
. Then,
\widetilde{\sigma}\in V^{-N}_{1,0}
for any
N\geq 0
.
Define
\sigma_{0}=\sigma-\widetilde{\sigma}\in V^{1}_{1,0}
such that

T_{\sigma}=T_{\sigma_{0}}+T_{\widetilde{\sigma}}
. Since
T_{\widetilde{\sigma}}
is compact on
L^{2}
and
a\in L^{\infty}
, so is the commutator

[T_{\widetilde{\sigma}},M_{a}].
Moreover, since
\widetilde{\sigma}\in S^{1}_{1,0}
, we have that
[T_{\widetilde{\sigma}},M_{a}]
is also a compact Calderón-Zygmund operator.
Hence, by Theorem
B
,
we have

(4.1)

[T_{\widetilde{\sigma}},M_{a}](1)\in\textit{CMO}.

Next, by the fundamental theorem of calculus
and the fact that
\sigma_{0}(x,0)=0
,
we have

(4.2)

\sigma_{0}(x,\xi)=\sigma_{0}(x,\xi)-\sigma_{0}(x,0)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{j}\sigma_{j}(x,\xi),

where

\sigma_{j}(x,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\xi_{j}^{\prime}}\sigma_{0}(x,\xi^{\prime})|_{\xi^{\prime}=t\xi}\,dt.

We claim that
\sigma_{j}\in V_{1,0}^{0}
for each
j=1,\dots,d
.
Noting that

t(1+t|\xi|)^{-1}\leq(1+|\xi|)^{-1},

for any
0\leq t\leq 1

and applying
(
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\sigma_{j}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}t^{|\beta|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi_{j}^{\prime}}\sigma_{0}(x,\xi^{\prime})|_{\xi^{\prime}=t\xi}\,dt\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}t^{|\beta|}K_{\alpha,\beta+1}(x,t\xi)(1+t|\xi|)^{-|\beta|}dt

\displaystyle\leq(1+|\xi|)^{-|\beta|}\int_{0}^{1}K_{\alpha,\beta+1}(x,t\xi)dt

\displaystyle=:(1+|\xi|)^{-|\beta|}\widetilde{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi).

Note that, since
K_{\alpha,\beta+1}
is a bounded function, so is
\widetilde{K}_{\alpha,\beta}
.
Moreover, for each
t>0
,
K_{\alpha,\beta+1}(x,t\xi)
tends to
0
as
|x|+|\xi|\to\infty
.
Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem
we conclude that
\widetilde{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)
tends to
0
as
|x|+|\xi|\to\infty
.
This proves
\sigma_{j}\in V_{1,0}^{0}
.

From (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

T_{\sigma_{0}}(f)=-i\sum_{j=1}^{d}T_{\sigma_{j}}(\partial_{j}f).

Hence, we obtain

(4.4)

[T_{\sigma_{0}},M_{a}](f)=-i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Big(T_{\sigma_{j}}(f\partial_{j}a+a\partial_{j}f)-aT_{\sigma_{j}}(\partial_{j}f)\Big).

\bullet

Step 2:
The commutator and its transpose on the function 1.

From (
4.4
), we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle[T_{\sigma_{0}},M_{a}](1)=-i\sum_{j=1}^{d}T_{\sigma_{j}}(\partial_{j}a).

Since
\sigma_{j}\in V_{1,0}^{0}
,
it follows
that
T_{\sigma_{j}}
is a compact Calderón-Zygmund operator
and, by Remark
3.5
(b),
T_{\sigma_{j}}:L^{\infty}\to\textit{CMO}
.
Hence, recalling that
\nabla a\in L^{\infty}
,
we obtain from (
4.5
) that

(4.6)

[T_{\sigma_{0}},M_{a}](1)\in\textit{CMO}\,.

Therefore, from
(
4.1
) and (
4.6
) with
\sigma=\sigma_{0}+\widetilde{\sigma}
, we conclude that

(4.7)

\displaystyle[T_{\sigma},M_{a}](1)\in\textit{CMO}.

A simple calculation shows that the transpose of the commutator satisfies

[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]^{*}=-[T_{\sigma}^{*},M_{a}]=-[T_{\sigma^{*}},M_{a}],

where
\sigma^{*}\in V_{1,0}^{1}
; see Theorem
2.1
.
Therefore, repeating the calculation above by replacing
\sigma
by
\sigma^{*}
, we also obtain

(4.8)

[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]^{*}(1)\in\textit{CMO}\,.

\bullet

Step 3:
Weak compactness property.

From Steps 1 and 2, we may assume that
\sigma(x,0)=0
so the formula (
4.2
) holds. Fix
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and two normalized bump functions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
.
In this part, we prove the weak compactness property
of
[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]
, namely,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty}R^{-d}\big|\big\langle[T_{\sigma},M_{a}](\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}),\phi_{2}^{x_{0}+x_{2},R}\big\rangle\big|=0.

In the following,
we assume that
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}

are normalized bump functions of order at least
M\geq 1
.
The
M=0
case follows from (
4.9
) for
M\geq 1

and an approximation argument.

First, note that

[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]=[T_{\sigma},M_{\tilde{a}}]
, where
\widetilde{a}=a-a(x_{0}+x_{1})
.
Thus, for each given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we may assume without loss of generality that

a(x_{0}+x_{1})=0
.
Then, from the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

(4.10)

\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{x_{0}+x_{1}}(R))}\lesssim R\|\nabla a\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Similarly,
we have

(4.11)

\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{x_{0}+x_{2}}(R))}\lesssim(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+R)\|\nabla a\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim R\|\nabla a\|_{L^{\infty}}

for any
R\gtrsim|x_{1}-x_{2}|
.
Recall also from (
3.2
) that

(4.12)

\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\phi_{j}^{x_{0}+x_{j},R}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim R^{\frac{d}{2}-|\alpha|},

uniformly in
x_{0},x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
j=1,2
.

Given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
R>0
(recall that
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are fixed),
set

\displaystyle A(x_{0},R)

\displaystyle:=R^{-d}\left|\big\langle T_{\sigma}(a\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}),\phi_{2}^{x_{0}+x_{2},R}\big\rangle\right|,

\displaystyle B(x_{0},R)

\displaystyle:=R^{-d}\left|\big\langle aT_{\sigma}(\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}),\phi_{2}^{x_{0}+x_{2},R}\big\rangle\right|.

Then,
we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle R^{-d}\left|\langle[T_{\sigma},M_{a}](\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}),\phi_{2}^{x_{0}+x_{2},R}\rangle\right|\leq A(x_{0},R)+B(x_{0},R).

Let
R\gtrsim|x_{1}-x_{2}|
.
Then, from (
4.12
), (
4.11
), and (
4.3
), we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}B(x_{0},R)&\leq R^{-d}\|aT_{\sigma}(\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\|_{L^{2}(B_{x_{0}+x_{2}}(R))}\|\phi_{2}^{x_{0}+x_{2},R}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{x_{0}+x_{2}}(R))}\|T_{\sigma}(\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\|_{L^{2}(B_{x_{0}+x_{2}}(R))}\\
&\lesssim R^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}\|\nabla a\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|T_{\sigma_{j}}(\partial_{j}\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\nabla a\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|T_{\sigma_{j}}(\phi_{1,j}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where
\phi_{1,j}=\partial_{j}\phi_{1}
.
Recall now from Step 1
that
T_{\sigma_{j}}
is a compact Calderón-Zygmund operator.
Then, by noting that
\phi_{1,j}
is a normalized bump function (of order
M-1
),
it follows from
Theorem
C
(necessity of the
L^{2}
-condition)
that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty}R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\big\|T_{\sigma_{j}}(\phi_{1,j}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\big\|_{L^{2}}=0

for any
j=1,\dots,d
.
Hence, from (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we obtain

(4.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty}B(x_{0},R)=0.

Proceeding as in (
4.14
), we have

\displaystyle A(x_{0},R)

\displaystyle\leq R^{-d}\|T_{\sigma}(a\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\|_{L^{2}}\|\phi_{2}^{x_{0}+x_{2},R}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|T_{\sigma_{j}}(\partial_{j}(a\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|T_{\sigma_{j}}(\partial_{j}a\cdot R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|T_{\sigma_{j}}(R^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{B_{x_{0}+x_{1}}(R)}a\cdot R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{1,j}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R})\|_{L^{2}},

where, in the last step, we used the chain rule:

\partial_{j}\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}=R^{-1}(\partial_{j}\phi_{1})^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}=R^{-1}\phi_{1,j}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}

and the fact that
\phi_{1,j}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}
is supported on the ball
B_{x_{0}+x_{1}}(R)
.
Since
\partial_{j}a\in L^{\infty}
, we observe that

\partial_{j}a\cdot R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}
converge weakly to
0
in
L^{2}
as
|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty
.
From (
4.10
), we have
R^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{B_{x_{0}+x_{1}}(R)}a\in L^{\infty}

and thus we see that

R^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{B_{x_{0}+x_{1}}(R)}a\cdot R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\phi_{1,j}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}

also
converge weakly to
0
in
L^{2}
as
|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty
.
Hence, since
T_{\sigma_{j}}
is compact on
L^{2}
, we obtain

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty}A(x_{0},R)=0.

Therefore, from (
4.13
), (
4.16
), and (
4.17
), we obtain

\lim_{|x_{0}|+R+R^{-1}\to\infty}R^{-d}\left|\langle T(\phi_{1}^{x_{0}+x_{1},R}),\phi_{2}^{x_{0}+x_{2},R}\rangle\right|=0,

which proves the weak compactness property of the commutator
[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]
.

Finally, from Theorem
B
with
Steps 2 and 3,
we conclude that
the commutator
[T_{\sigma},M_{a}]

is
a compact Calderón-Zygmund operator.
∎

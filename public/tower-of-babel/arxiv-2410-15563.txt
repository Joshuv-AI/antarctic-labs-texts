Variants of Solovay reducibility Thanks: supported in part by the ANR project FLITTLA ANR-21-CE48-0023
Ivan Titov

Abstract
Solovay reducibility was introduced by Robert M. Solovay [ 6 ] in 1975 in terms of translation functions on rationals. It is a measure of relative approximation speed and thus also of relative randomness of reals. In the area of algorithmic randomness, Solovay reducibility has been intensively studied and several central results on left-c.e. reals have been obtained. Outside of the left-c.e. reals, Solovay reducibility is considered to be behaved badly [ 2 ] . Proposals for variants of Solovay reducibility that are better suited for the investigation of arbitrary, not necessarily left-c.e. reals were made by Rettinger and Zheng [ 9 ] , and, recently, by Titov [ 7 ] and by Kumabe and co-authors [ 3 ] , [ 4 ] . These variants all coincide with the original version of Solovay reducibility on the left-c.e. reals. Furthermore, they are all defined in terms of translation functions. The latter translate between computable approximations in the case of Rettinger and Zheng, are monotone in the case of Titov, and are functions between reals in the case of Kumabe et al. In what follows, we derive new results on the mentioned variants and their relation to each other. In particular, we obtain that Solovay reducibility defined in terms of translation function on rationals implies Solovay reducibility defined in terms of translation functions on reals, and we show that the original version of Solovay reducibility is strictly weaker than its monotone variant. Solovay reducibility and its variants mentioned so far have tight connections to Martin-Löf randomness, the strongest and most central notion of a random sequence. For the investigation of Schnorr randomness, total variants of Solovay reducibility have been introduced by Merkle and Titov [ 5 ] in 2022 and, independently, by Kumabe et al. [ 4 ] in 2024, the latter again via real-valued translation functions. In what follows, we show that total Solovay reducibility defined in terms of rational functions implies total Solovay reducibility defined in terms of real functions.



1
Solovay reducibility and its variants

We start with reviewing the concept of Solovay reducibility introduced by Solovay
[
6

Solovay reducibility

Proof.

(a)

The proof is standard and is left to the interested reader.

(b)

1.2
Monotone Solovay reducibility

In 2024, Titov
[
7
]
proposed the following monotone variant of Solovay reducibility, which coincides with
{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}
on the set on left-c.e. reals.

Definition 3
.

A real
\alpha
is
monotone Solovay reducible
to a real
\beta
, written
\alpha{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{m}}\beta
, if
\alpha
is
Solovay reducible
to
\beta
via a
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function that is nondecreasing.

By definition, Solovay reducibility is implied by its monotone variant. In the remainder of this section, we demonstrate that the reverse implication does not hold in general, i.e.,
{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{m}}
is strictly stronger than
{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}
. For a start, we state somewhat technical observations about the behavior of monotone
\mathbb{Q}
-translation functions, their proofs are left to the interested reader.

Lemma 1

Every monotone
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function
g
from a real
\beta
to a real
\alpha
satisfies the following properties.

(a)

For every
q\geq\beta
such that
g(q)
is defined, we have
g(q)\geq\beta
.

(b)

If
q_{0},q_{1},\dots
is a sequence of rationals in
[0,\beta)
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g(q_{n})=\alpha
, then
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}q_{n}=\beta
.

The next proposition shows that the monotone
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function can be only from a left-c.e. to a left-c.e. or from a nonleft-c.e. to a nonleft-c.e. real.

Proposition 2

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\alpha
is left-c.e. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\beta
is left-c.e.;

(ii)

there exists a nondecreasing
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function from
\alpha
to
\beta
;

(iii)

there exists a nondecreasing
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function from
\beta
to
\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
a_{0},a_{1},\dots
where
a_{0}=0
be a left-c.e. approximation of
\alpha
.

•

(i)\implies(ii)
and
(i)\implies(iii)
. In case
\beta
is left-c.e., let
b_{0},b_{1},\dots
where
b_{0}=0
be a left-c.e. approximation of
\beta
. Then the functions

g(q)=a_{\min\{m:b_{m}\geq q\}}\makebox[40.00006pt]{and}h(q)=b_{\min\{m:a_{m}\geq q\}}

are nondecreasing translation functions from
\beta
to
\alpha
and from
\alpha
to
\beta
, respectively.

•

(ii)\implies(i)
. If there exists a nondecreasing
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function

from
\alpha
to
\beta
, then the sequence
g(a_{0}),g(a_{1}),\dots
is a nondecreasing computable approximation of

•

(iii)\implies(i)
. If there exists a nondecreasing
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function

from
\beta
to
\alpha
, then, for a fixed computable enumeration

of
\mathrm{dom}(g)
, we define

step-wise: at step

; at step

, we search for an index

such that

q_{i_{n}}<q_{i}\text{, }g(q_{i})>a_{n},\text{ and there is an index }j>i_{n}\text{ such that }g(q_{i})<a_{j}

and set

.

Then every step terminates since, for every

, all rationals in the interval
(q_{i_{n}},\beta)\cap(d_{n},\beta)
, where

is some point in
(0,\beta)
such that

, fulfill (

). Therefore, the sequence

is infinite and computable.
It is increasing by
q_{i_{n}}<q_{i_{n+1}}
.
Further, for every

, we have
q_{i_{n}}<a_{j}<\alpha

, so, by Lemma

(

q_{i_{0}},q_{i_{1}},\dots

.
Finally, it fulfills

for every

, hence the sequence
g(q_{i_{0}}),g(q_{i_{1}}),\dots
tends to
\alpha
; and thus, by Lemma
1
(

is left-c.e.

∎

Corollary 1

The set of left-c.e. reals is
{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{m}}
-closed downwards and upwards in
\mathbb{R}
.

In contrast, the set of left-c.e. reals is not
{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}
-closed downwards, as we will see in the next proposition.

Proposition 3

There exist a nonleft-c.e. real
\beta
such that
1{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}\beta
.

Proof.

We fix an enumeration
q_{1},q_{2},\dots
of all rationals in the unit interval and define a computable test
I_{n}:=[q_{n},q_{n}+2^{-2n}]
.
Since
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(I_{n})=\frac{1}{3}
, while the set of all left-c.e. reals has the Lebesgue measure
0
because there are only countably many such reals, we can fix a nonleft-c.e. real
\beta\notin\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}I_{n}
.

On the one hand, the function
g
defined by

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function from
\beta
to
1
.
Moreover, for every
q_{n}<\beta
, we easily obtain from
\beta\notin I_{n}
that
\beta>q_{n}+\mu(I_{n})=q_{n}+2^{-2n}
, and thus

1-g(q_{n})=1-(1-2^{-2n})=2^{-2n}\in(0,\beta-q_{n}).

Therefore we obtain that
1{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}\beta
with the constant
1
via
g
.
∎

On the other hand, the existence of a nondecreasing
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function from the real
\beta
defined in the latter proof to
1
would imply by Proposition
2
that
\beta
is left-c.e., which cannot be true.
Therefore, Proposition
3
allows us to separate
{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{m}}
and
{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 2

There exist two reals
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
\alpha{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}\beta
but
\alpha\nleq_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{m}}\beta
.

1.3
Computable functions on reals

Definition 4
.

A sequence
q_{0},q_{1},\dots
of rationals is called
effective approximation
if it fulfills
|q_{n}-q_{n+1}|<2^{-n}
for every
n
.

Since every effective approximation
q_{0},q_{1},\dots
is a Cauchy sequence, it converges to some limit point
x\in\mathbb{R}
. The following properties of effective approximations and their limits can be obtained straightforwardly.

Lemma 2

(a)

If
q_{0},q_{1},\dots
is an effective approximation, then there exists a real
x
such that

|x-q_{n}|<2^{-(n-1)}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(5)

In particular,
x=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}q_{n}
. In that case, we also say that
q_{0},q_{1},\dots
is an
effective approximation of
x
.

(b)

Let
q_{0},q_{1},\dots
be an approximation of a real
x
that satisfies

|q_{n+1}-q_{n}|<2^{-n}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(6)

Then
q_{0},q_{1},\dots
is an effective approximation of
x
.

Obviously, a real
x
is computable iff there exists a computable effective approximation of
x
.

The class of Turing machines that, using an infinite sequence of finite strings (in our case, encoded rationals) as an oracle, returns another sequence of finite strings was formally defined by Weihrauch
[
8
, Chapter 2]
under the name “Turing machines of Type 2”. In what follows, we give a formal definition of a computable real function using a notion of a Turing machine of Type 2 specified for the sequences of rationals.

Definition 5

(Weihrauch, 2000)
.

A
Turing Machine

M

of Type 2
is an oracle Turing machine that, for every oracle
(p_{0},p_{1},\dots)
, where
p_{0},p_{1},\dots
are (appropriately finitely encoded) rationals, produces either an infinite sequence of rationals
(q_{0},q_{1},\dots)
; in this case, we
write
M^{(p_{0},p_{1},\dots)}\downarrow=(q_{0},q_{1},\dots)
and
say that
M

returns the sequence

(q_{0},q_{1},\dots)

from the input

(p_{0},p_{1},\dots)
; or a finite set of rationals
(q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
; in the latter case, we say that
M^{(p_{0},p_{1},\dots)}

is undefined
.

If, for an effective approximation
(p_{0},p_{1},\dots)
given as an oracle,
M
returns at least
n+1
first elements
(q_{0},\dots,q_{n})
of some effective approximation
(q_{0},q_{1},\dots)
, we say that
the computation of
M^{(p_{0},p_{1},\dots)}

with tolerance
2^{-(n-1)}

halts
and call
p_{n}

result of

M^{(q_{0},q_{1},\dots)}

with tolerance

2^{-(n-1)}
.

A real function
f
from some subset of
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
is
computable
on some set
{X\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(f)}
if there exists an oracle Turing machine
M
such that, for every
x\in X
and every effective approximation
p_{0},p_{1},\dots
that converges to
x
,

M^{(p_{0},p_{1},\dots)}
returns an effective approximation
q_{0},q_{1},\dots
of
f(x)
.

By
[
8
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, computability of a real function implies its continuity.

Proposition 4

If a real function
f
is computable on some interval
(a,b)
, then
f
is continuous on
(a,b)
.

1.4
Variants of Solovay reducibility defined via translation function on reals

According to Proposition
1
(
a
), on the left-c.e. reals, the Solovay reducibility is equivalent to the Solovay reducibility via a Lipschitz continuous
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function. In 2020, Kumabe, Miyabe, Mizusawa, and Suzuki proposed
[
3
, Definition 9]
a new type of reducibility by replacing the Lipschitz continuous translation functions on rationals in the latter characterization by the translation functions on reals, without requiring for the reals
\alpha
and
\beta
to be left-c.e.

In what follows, we give the formal definition of this reducibility denoted by Kumabe et al. as “L2” under the more intuitive name “real Solovay reducibility”.

Definition 6

(Kumabe et al., 2020)
.

An
\mathbb{R}
-translation function
from a real
\beta
to another real
\alpha
is a real function which is computable on the interval
[0,\beta)
and maps it to the interval
[0,\alpha)
such that

\lim\limits_{x\nearrow\beta}f(x)=\alpha.

(7)

A real
\alpha
is
real Solovay reducible
to a real
\beta
, written
\alpha{\leq}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathbb{R}}\beta
, if there exists a Lipschitz continuous
\mathbb{R}
-translation function from
\beta
to
\alpha
.

Note that, between every two left-c.e. reals, there exist both an
\mathbb{R}
-translation function and a
\mathbb{Q}
-translation function.

Proposition 5

If
\alpha
and
\beta
are left-c.e. reals, then there exists an
\mathbb{R}
-translation function from
\beta
to
\alpha
, whose restriction on

\mathbb{Q}
-translation function from
\beta
to
\alpha
.

Proof.

Fixing two left-c.e. approximations

\mathbb{R}
-translation function

from

\alpha

f(x)=a_{n}+\frac{x-b_{n}}{b_{n+1}-b_{n}}(a_{n+1}-a_{n})\quad\text{if there exists such }n\text{ that }b_{n}\leq x<b_{n+1}.

It is easy to see that, if
x

\mathbb{Q}
-translation function from

\alpha

Revisiting η ′ ​ ( 958 ) \eta^{\prime}(958) nuclear states
E. Friedman, A. Gal

Abstract
Observing η ′ \eta^{\prime} -nuclear quasibound states requires that the η ′ \eta^{\prime} -nuclear potential is both sufficiently attractive and weakly absorptive, as argued by the CBELSA/TAPS collaboration analysing inclusive η ′ \eta^{\prime} production experiments on nuclear targets, including liquid hydrogen (LH 2 ). Here we present an alternative derivation of the η ′ \eta^{\prime} -nuclear potential constrained by near-threshold p ​ p → p ​ p ​ η ′ pp\to pp\eta^{\prime} and γ ​ p → η ′ ​ p \gamma p\to\eta^{\prime}p production experiments on a free proton. The resulting η ′ \eta^{\prime} -nuclear potential is weakly attractive and strongly absorptive, to the extent that observation of clear signals of η ′ \eta^{\prime} -nuclear quasibound states is unlikely.



1
Introduction

There is considerable interest at present in meson-nuclear quasibound states,
specifically for
{\bar{K}}
-
\eta
-
\eta^{\prime}
pseudoscalar mesons and

\omega
-
\phi
vector mesons, all of which with masses lower than or about
1 GeV
[
1
]
. Of these meson candidates, the only meson-nuclear
quasibound state established so far experimentally is
{\bar{K}}NN
, likely a
(
J^{P}=0^{-}
,
I=\frac{1}{2}
) state, bound by about 42 MeV but with a very large
width of about 100 MeV
[
2
]
. Experimental searches for
\eta(548)

and
\eta^{\prime}(958)
quasibound states are ongoing
[
3
,
4
]
but so far
without success. While both
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
nuclear interactions are likely
to be attractive
[
1
]
, it is not clear whether or not they are
sufficiently strong to generate quasibound states across the periodic table.
For
\eta(548)
, a considerable uncertainty arises from the model dependence
of the poorly known near-threshold
\eta N
scattering amplitude

f_{\eta N}(\sqrt{s})
which serves as input in
\eta
-nuclear bound state
calculations, see for example Refs.
[
5
,
6
]
.

For
\eta^{\prime}(958)
, in contrast, most phenomenological
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear
potentials devised for calculating quasibound states are not directly
connected to the near-threshold
\eta^{\prime}N
scattering amplitude

f_{\eta^{\prime}N}(\sqrt{s})
. Rather, they are constrained by fitting
inclusive
\eta^{\prime}
photoproduction spectra taken on
nuclei
,
such as
12
C and
93
Nb
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. A common
parametrization of such
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear potentials is given by
[
1
]

V_{\eta^{\prime}}(r)=(V_{0}+{\rm i}W_{0})\frac{\rho(r)}{\rho(0)},

(1)

where
\rho(r)
is the nuclear density. The most recent values derived for
the strength (or depth) parameters of the real and imaginary parts are

V_{0}=-(44\pm 16\pm 15)~{\rm MeV}~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{N18}{}{}]},\,\,\,\,\,\,W_{0}=-(13\pm 3\pm 3)~{\rm MeV}~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{F16}{}{}]}.

(2)

Given these values, only broad and overlapping quasibound nuclear
states are expected in
\eta^{\prime}
nuclear production spectra, as shown in
Table
1
for
12
C and
40
Ca. However, the
12
C and

93
Nb
\eta^{\prime}
-photoproduction data used to extract
V_{0}
and
W_{0}
consist
dominantly of
p_{\eta^{\prime}}\gg k_{F}
input, with nuclear-matter Fermi momentum

k_{F}\approx 270
MeV, whereas the construction of a near-threshold optical
potential
V_{\eta^{\prime}}^{\rm opt}(r)
that generates
\eta^{\prime}
-nucleus bound states
requires
p_{\eta^{\prime}}\ll k_{F}
input data, preferably two-body
\eta^{\prime}
-nucleon
data.

In the present work we construct a low-energy
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear optical
potential by using available near-threshold data of
\eta^{\prime}
production
on a free proton. The selected data set is briefly reviewed in
Sect.
2
, and the associated
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear optical
potential
V_{\eta^{\prime}}^{\rm opt}(r)
is discussed in Sect.
3
.
Results of
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear quasibound state calculations are listed in
Sect.
4
. It is found that, since the imaginary part of

V_{\eta^{\prime}}^{\rm opt}(r)
is sizable, no clear
\eta^{\prime}

are expected across the periodic table.

5
.

2
\eta^{\prime}N
low-energy interaction parameters

Low energy
\eta^{\prime}p
scattering parameters, in particular the
\eta^{\prime}p

scattering length
a_{\eta^{\prime}p}
, were constrained by using two near-threshold

\eta^{\prime}
production reactions on a free proton. In the first one,
a_{\eta^{\prime}p}

was determined from the rise of the total cross section of the near-threshold

\eta^{\prime}
production reaction
pp\to pp\eta^{\prime}
at COSY
[
12
]
, with real
and imaginary parts given by

{\rm Re}\,a_{\eta^{\prime}p}=(0.00\pm 0.43)~{\rm fm}\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm Im}\,a_{\eta^{\prime}p}=0.37^{+0.02+0.38}_{-0.11-0.05}~{\rm fm},

(3)

where the systematic uncertainty of Re
\,a_{\eta^{\prime}p}
is negligible. In the
second one, using
\gamma p\to\eta^{\prime}p
data from several near-threshold

\eta^{\prime}
photoproduction experiments, only the absolute value of
a_{\eta^{\prime}p}

was constrained
[
13
]
:

|a_{\eta^{\prime}p}|=(0.403\pm 0.020\pm 0.060)\,{\rm fm},

(4)

with indications that the real part of
a_{\eta^{\prime}p}
is small compared to the
imaginary part. A more restrictive constraint arises provided there is no
near-threshold narrow
\eta^{\prime}N

s
-wave resonance
[
13
]
:

|a_{\eta^{\prime}p}|=(0.356\pm 0.012)\,{\rm fm}.

(5)

In fact, the nearly overlapping
I=\frac{1}{2}

J^{\pi}=(\frac{1}{2})^{-}

N^{\ast}
(1895) resonance is quite broad,

\Gamma_{1895}\approx 120
MeV
[
14
]
, therefore justifying this
latter constraint. The near overlap of the
\eta^{\prime}N
threshold and the peak
of
N^{\ast}
(1895) explains qualitatively why
a_{\eta^{\prime}p}
, constrained by
Eqs. (
3
,
4
) or Eqs. (
3
,
5
),
is dominantly imaginary, thereby making the observation of
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear
quasibound states quite difficult. This situation differs qualitatively from
the
a_{\eta N}
model dependence introduced by the broad
I=\frac{1}{2}

J^{\pi}=(\frac{1}{2})^{-}

N^{\ast}
(1535) resonance which is peaked about
50 MeV above the
\eta N
threshold,
with
\Gamma_{1535}\approx 150
MeV
[
14
]
.

Regarding the sign of Re
\,a_{\eta^{\prime}p}
, the (10-40)% branching ratio
observed for
N^{\ast}(1895)\to N\eta^{\prime}
decay
[
14
]
suggests that

N^{\ast}
(1895), projected onto the
\eta^{\prime}N
channel, is a resonance
rather than a bound state which within our phase convention implies that
Re
\,a_{\eta^{\prime}p}
is positive.

3
Optical-potential methodology

\eta^{\prime}
bound states in nuclei are calculated here using
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear
density-dependent optical potential,

V_{\rm opt}^{\eta^{\prime}}(\rho)=-\frac{4\pi}{2\mu_{\eta^{\prime}}}f^{(2)}_{A}\,b_{0}^{A}(\rho)\,\rho,,

(6)

with a density-dependent
\eta^{\prime}N
c.m. scattering amplitude
b_{0}^{A}(\rho)

in units of fm (
\hbar=c=1
). In this expression
A
is the mass number
of the
nuclear core
,
\rho
is a nuclear density normalized to
A
,

\rho_{0}=0.17
fm
-3
stands for nuclear-matter density,
\mu_{\eta^{\prime}}
is
the
\eta^{\prime}
-nucleus reduced mass and
f^{(2)}_{A}
is a kinematical factor
transforming
b_{0}^{A}(\rho)
from the
\eta^{\prime}N
center-of-mass (c.m.) system,
to the
\eta^{\prime}
-nucleus c.m. system:

f^{(2)}_{A}=1+\frac{A-1}{A}\frac{\mu_{\eta^{\prime}}}{m_{N}}.

(7)

The density-dependent
\eta^{\prime}N
c.m. scattering amplitude
b_{0}^{A}(\rho)
is
given by

b_{0}^{A}(\rho)=\frac{{\rm Re}\,b_{0}}{1+(3k_{F}/2\pi)f^{(2)}_{A}{\rm Re}\,b_{0}}+{\rm i}\,{\rm Im}\,b_{0},

(8)

where
k_{F}
is the Fermi momentum associated with local density
\rho
:

k_{F}=(3{\pi}^{2}\rho/2)^{1/3}
. The density dependence of
b_{0}^{A}(\rho)
accounts
for long-range Pauli correlations in
\eta^{\prime}N
in-medium multiple scatterings,
starting at
\rho^{4/3}

[
15
,
16
]
in a nuclear-density expansion,
as practised in our
K^{-}
-atom studies
[
17
]
. Note that Im
\,b_{0}
in
Eq. (
8
) is not affected by Pauli correlations since all
N\eta^{\prime}

two-body decays proceed with c.m. momentum larger than
k_{F}
.
The low-density limit of
b_{0}^{A}(\rho)
is obtained by setting
b_{0}^{A}(\rho)\to b_{0}
where
b_{0}
is identified in the present exploratory study
with the
\eta^{\prime}N
scattering length
a_{\eta^{\prime}N}
considered in the
previous section (note that since
I_{\eta^{\prime}}=0
,
a_{\eta^{\prime}n}=a_{\eta^{\prime}p}
,
here denoted
a_{\eta^{\prime}N}
). Precisely the same form of
V_{\rm opt}(\rho)

was used by us recently for constructing the
YN
(
Y=\Lambda,\Xi
) induced
component of the
Y
-nuclear optical potential

V_{\rm opt}^{Y}(\rho)

[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

For nuclear densities
\rho(r)=\rho_{p}(r)+\rho_{n}(r)
we used
harmonic-oscillator type densities
[
23
]
for
12
C, with the same
radial parameters for neutrons and protons, and two-parameter Fermi (2pF)
distributions for
40
Ca, normalized to
Z
for protons and
N=A-Z
for
neutrons, all derived from nuclear charge distributions assembled in
Ref.
[
24
]
. The corresponding r.m.s. radii follow closely values derived
from experiment by relating proton densities
\rho_{p}(r)
to charge densities
and including the proton charge finite size and recoil effects. This approach
is equivalent to assigning some finite range to the
\eta^{\prime}N
interaction.
Folding reasonably chosen
\eta^{\prime}N
interaction ranges other than
corresponding to the proton charge radius, varying the spatial form of the
charge density, or introducing realistic differences between neutron and
proton r.m.s. radii, made little difference.

5
Concluding remarks

In this work we explored to what extent
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear quasibound states
are sufficiently narrow to allow experimental observation. For this task
we used a low-energy
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear density-dependent optical potential

V_{\rm opt}^{\eta^{\prime}}(\rho)
, Eq. (
6
), that has been applied by us
quite successfully to
\Lambda
hypernuclei
[
20
,
21
]
and
\Xi^{-}

hypernuclei
[
18
,
19
,
22
]
. The density dependence of such
hadron-nucleus optical potential
V_{\rm opt}^{h}(\rho)
accounts for
the leading long-range Pauli correlations. For
\eta^{\prime}
, the input to

V_{\rm opt}^{\eta^{\prime}}(\rho)
consisted of the
\eta^{\prime}N
scattering length

a_{\eta^{\prime}N}
which unfortunately is dominated by its imaginary (absorptive)
part. This made the
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear quasibound states calculated here in

12
C and in
40
Ca too broad to allow clear experimental observation.
The reason behind Im
\,a_{\eta^{\prime}N}

\gg
Re
\,a_{\eta^{\prime}N}
is apparently
the almost perfect overlap of the
N^{\ast}
(1895) peak and the
\eta^{\prime}N

threshold.

Future work should consider
V_{\rm opt}^{\eta^{\prime}}(\rho)
at
subthreshold

\eta^{\prime}N
energies, as done for
\eta N
in Refs.
[
5
,
6
]
. It was
found there that
\eta
-nuclear binding energies as well as widths calculated
at subthreshold are smaller than those calculated at threshold.
The subthreshold
\eta N
scattering amplitude
f_{\eta N}(\sqrt{s})
was
derived there by following a dynamical model of
N^{\ast}
(1535) in terms
of its major two-body decay channels, foremost
\eta N
. Given the near
overlap of the
\eta^{\prime}N
threshold (1896-1897 MeV) and the
N^{\ast}
(1895)
resonance, a promising path to consider for
\eta^{\prime}N
would be a dynamical
model of
N^{\ast}
(1895) in terms of its main decay channels
N\pi
,
N\eta
,

N\eta^{\prime}
,
\Lambda K
,
\Sigma K
,
N\omega
and
N\rho
. Some work in this
direction was done recently by Bruns and Cieplý
[
25
]
.

Experiments attempting to observe
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear quasibound states have been
limited to
12
C. Thus, the
(p,d)
reaction was proposed by Itahashi et
al.
[
26
]
at GSI using the fragment separator FRS, with negative
results published subsequently by Tanaka et al.
[
27
,
28
]
.
Arguments in favor of trying the semi-exclusive
12
C(
p,dp
) reaction
were made by Fujioka et al.
[
29
,
30
]
and very recently by
Ikeno et al.
[
31
]
. Another attempt, running the
12
C(
\gamma,p
)
reaction in the LEPS2 beamline at SPring-8, also failed to observe a clear

\eta^{\prime}
quasibound signal
[
32
]
. Given our present optical-potential
analysis, it could be an uphill battle to observe
\eta^{\prime}
-nuclear quasibound
states.

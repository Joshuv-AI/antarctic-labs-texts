An improved chaos control with adaptive active set approach combined in reliability-based design optimization
Authors: Xue An, De Li, Jiaming Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To overcome the drawbacks of the traditional chaos control method (CC), such as non-convergence, inefficiency and repeated adjustment of control factor, a new method named adaptively active set-based CC (AASCC) is presented and further extended to the reliability-based design optimization (RBDO). First, the AASCC method builds a relationship between the iterative step size and search region of the minimum performance target point (MPTP) via gradient on the MPTP. This approach can adjust the iterative step size to respond dynamically to changes while automatically scaling the search domain of MPTP to eliminate unnecessary MPTP. As a result, it minimizes the demand for frequent debugging of control factors meanwhile accelerating computational efficiency. Second, the proposed strategy is combined with the reliability index to establish a dynamic dictate condition to optimize the performance of the RBDO-based double-loop method (DLM) in efficiency and accuracy. The dynamic dictate condition is executed to refresh the active determined constraint so that it effectively relieves the excessive redundant workloads. Finally, the proposed method is tested on several authoritative examples, involving both MPTP searching cases and RBDO problems to verify its characteristics, showing notable advantages.

To date, several outstanding methods have been put forward to overcome these instabilities of the original inverse HL-RF method. Generally, the advanced mean value (AMV) method11 was more effective for calculating MPTP due to its efficiency and simplicity. However, it can occasionally face convergence issues and exhibit chaotic behavior when dealing with concave performance functions12. Thus, many novel variants have been derived by learning from the advantages of mean value13–15. Although they can maintain the numerical stability and convergence properties for MPTP search with convex/concave performance functions, they still struggle with convergence16 when strong nonlinear equations are covered. For this reason, the concept of stability transformation method (STM) was discovered by Dixiong Yang17 and a series of algorithms were derived from it, for instance, chaos control based on FORM18, enhanced chaos control19, hybrid chaos control20, directional stability transformation method21 and so on. Ref.22 pointed out that the CC of the HL-RF method requires too many iterations, which makes it unsuitable for many problems thanks to the fixed relatively small chaos control factor (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document}), even if the CC-based methods achieved acceptable accuracy. Furthermore, incorporating the ideas of adaptive theory, adaptive CC23, relaxed mean value24, self-adaptive modified chaos control25, dynamical accelerated chaos control26, etc., have been developed in succession to efficiently and reliably identify MPTP using a specific adaptive or dynamic factor. By reading these documents, the improved FORM algorithms based on the adaptive theoretical system explored MPTP, which is more robust than the initial HL-RF method and more effective than the CC method during the inverse reliability analysis. However, as the nonlinear constraints become more complex and large-scale, the computing pressure of all these methods increases dramatically. Besides, if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} is selected to be small, the computational effort could be increased to achieve stable solutions in strong nonlinear cases. Consequently, it seems to be very important to develop a reasonable adaptive step size strategy based on CC for reliability analysis algorithms.

In short, the reliability analysis methods have made great achievements in the safety assessment of engineering structures. Not only that, they have also made good progress in the RBDO field27,28. Conceptually, RBDO relates to doing a deterministic optimization within a probability-constrained framework, which is related to the reliability index12. A popular technical route of computing RBDO problems is the DLM, which contains two major branches, that is reliability index approach-based DLM (RIA-based DLM)29 and performance measure approach-based DLM (PMA-based DLM)12. In PMA-based DLM, HL–RF is usually undertaken to study the inverse reliability analysis of the inner loop thanks to high efficiency and simplicity. However, DLM combined with HL–RF may be prone to convergence instabilities and low efficiency for large-scale and complex RBDO problems. As is known to all, efficiency and accuracy are key factors for assessing the quality of PMA-based DLM algorithms in solving RBDO problems. Based on those papers, it has been recognized that considerable efforts have been dedicated to optimizing the performance of the PMA-based DLM algorithm, but the balance among the three indicators does not seem to be very good. Therefore, it is very essential to conduct further investigation.

In this paper, an updated FORM is proffered to strengthen the efficiency and robustness of CC in the MPTP search process when dealing with complex performance functions. This method is called an adaptively active set-based chaos control (AASCC). The main innovation of the suggested method involves the following aspects: Firstly, an adaptively active set strategy is explored, aiming to auto-limit the search region of MPTP. Secondly, the iterative step size is redefined so that it can be adaptively changed to reduce repeated debugging and suppress the risk of periodic and chaos. It is noteworthy that the iterative step size and search region have the features of flexible adjustment, also both are carried out simultaneously according to the sensitivity at the mean value to evaluate the inverse reliability analysis. Thirdly, the proposed method is combined with DLM to enhance the property of PMA-based DLM in terms of efficiency and accuracy through a dynamic dictate parameter, which is derived from AASCC and the reliability index. This is the second novelty of the proposed method.

The framework of this study is laid out as follows: the method, including RBDO and FORM, is reviewed in Section "Review on RBDO and FORM". In Section "The proposed AASCC method", the proposed new adaptive approach based on CC, i.e., named AASCC, is thoroughly derived and explained in detail. After that, illustrative examples are provided in Section "Illustrative examples". Specifically, in Section "Evaluation of the AASCC method in reliability analysis", a total of seven numerical/structural examples are adopted to evaluate the performance of AASCC in MPTP search, focusing on both efficiency and accuracy. Section "Testing the AASCC mehtod in RBDO" presents the contribution of the new AASCC method in RBDO to further validate its converged performance, and then Sect. 5 brings out the concluding remarks.

CC method

Inspired by chaos dynamics theory, the CC method is developed using the STM technique18 and its iteration format is as follows:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{gathered} u_{CC}^{k + 1} = u_{CC}^{k} + \lambda C(f(u^{k} ) - u_{CC}^{k} ), \, 0 < \lambda \le 1 \hfill \\ f(u^{k} ) = f(u_{AMV}^{k + 1} ) = - \beta^{t} \frac{{\nabla_{u} G(d,u_{AMV}^{k} )}}{{\left\| {\nabla_{u} G(d,u_{AMV}^{k} )} \right\|}} \hfill \\ \end{gathered}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} represents the control factor ranging from 0 to 1, which is also equivalent to the iteration step size in the CC method. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C$$\end{document} denotes the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbf{n}} \times {\mathbf{n}}$$\end{document} dimension involutory matrix, where each row and each column can only have one element and its value is either 1 or -1 while the others are 0. However, it is usually taken to the unit matrix for the purpose of simplicity. Also, if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda = 1$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C = {\mathbf{I}}$$\end{document}, CC is similar to the AMV method. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda = 0$$\end{document} expresses that the current iteration point position remains unchanged.

It is not difficult to see from Eq. (9) that the calculation speed and convergence stability of the CC method depends on the iteration step size \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document}, which should require continuous trial and error to determine. If \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} is chosen too small, the convergence ability of the iterative point will be better, but this will make the computing pressure increase. Or even worse, non-convergence situations such as periodic solutions, oscillations, etc. may occur. Besides, it always remains a fixed value during the optimization process, which is another drawback of the CC method. Therefore, how to automatically select an appropriate iteration step size of the CC method simply and quickly, without increasing the computational cost and while improving convergence stability, is a serious problem.

The proposed AASCC method

Computing strategy of the AASCC

In view of the inefficiency and non-convergence of the CC approach, a new adaptively active set strategy-based chaos control (AASCC) iterative algorithm is proposed. The first novelty of the proposed method includes: (1) the first step is to calculate the gradient value of the current iteration point, which is appointed to adaptively select the chaos control factor and the search area of the iteration point, thereby effectively avoiding the repeated debugging of the chaos control factor. (2) the chaos control factor in this work is dynamically changing, which is very different from the traditional CC algorithm because it uses a fixed step size. (3) the search area of the iterative point could be automatically scaled, so the iteration point could jump to reach the optimum value, i.e., MPTP, rather than gradually approaching it. (4) both the iteration step size and search area of the iterative point are updated at the same time, which greatly improves the calculation efficiency. From the direction of the iteration step and dynamic search area, the traditional CC method is improved, meantime, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda^{k}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{a}$$\end{document} are adaptively moved according to the gradient at the iteration point.

Concluding remarks

In this paper, a novel self-adjusted updating technique for the control factor is investigated to circumvent the shortcomings of the CC method in reliability analysis. This developed strategy is named the adaptive active set-based chaos control (AASCC), which gives priority to the flexible adjustment of step size and the search domain of MPTP according to the gradient vector at the MPTP. Moreover, an outstanding DLM approach is proposed by defining a dynamic judgment criterion, which combines AASCC and the reliability index, referred to as DLM_AASCC. Several examples are provided to test the strong efficiency and accuracy of AASCC for reliability analysis and RBDO. The main conclusions are drawn below:Regarding MPTP search in reliability analysis, five numerical examples and two structural examples are used to test the efficiency and accuracy of the proposed AASCC algorithm. The outcomes demonstrate that the proposed AASCC algorithm outperforms recent ones for benchmark limit state functions and engineering structures.Regarding the scope of RBDO, three intricate RBDO problems are applied to examine the effectiveness and accuracy of the recommended DLM_AASCC method by comparing it to several advanced methods. The results showcased that the DLM_AASCC algorithm stands out as the most efficient method, meanwhile offering similar accuracy to the newer method. Hence, the DLM_AASCC method is recognized as a superior choice for addressing RBDO issues in engineering applications.DLM_AASCC is insensitive to the choice of both the starting setting and the standard deviation, while DLM_AMV and DLM_CC are not.This proposed DLM_AASCC method is an inheritance and improvement of the DLM_CC method, which falls within the scope of PMA-based DLM.The proposed AASCC method belongs to the field of approximate analytical algorithms, which is a branch of the FORM algorithm. The AASCC method uses a fixed differential step size to compute the gradient. On a negative note, the fixed step size may lead to calculation errors and may limit certain practical complex structures, such as those involving finite element analysis (FEA), so its application is still not universal.

Abstract
The transition to sustainable energy systems has highlighted the critical need for efficient sizing of renewable energy resources in microgrids. In particular, designing photovoltaic (PV) and battery systems to meet residential loads is challenging due to trade-offs between cost, reliability, and environmental impact. While previous studies have employed dynamic programming and heuristic techniques for microgrid sizing, these approaches often fail to balance computational efficiency and accuracy. In this work, we propose BOOST , or Battery-solar Ordinal Optimization Sizing Technique , a novel framework for optimizing the sizing of PV and battery components in microgrids. Ordinal optimization enables computationally efficient evaluations of potential designs while preserving accuracy through robust ranking of solutions. To determine the optimal operation of the system at any given time, we introduce a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) approach, which achieves lower costs than the commonly used dynamic programming methods. Our numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework identifies optimal designs that achieve a levelized cost of energy (LCOE) as low as 8.84 ¢/kWh, underscoring its potential for cost-effective microgrid design. The implications of our work are significant: BOOST provides a scalable and accurate methodology for integrating renewable energy into residential microgrids, addressing economic and environmental goals simultaneously.

Ordinal Optimization (OO)

Problem (
1

given

1

Proposition 1
.

Order is robust with respect to estimation noise.

In other words, if a set of designs is evaluated using the accurate MILP model and ordered in terms of cost, their order will not differ much if they were to be evaluated using the simple LP model. Indeed, the costs would not be as precise if they were to be evaluated using LP, but the overall
rank
will not be drastically affected.

To answer (Q1), our ordinal optimization (OO) approach to the sizing problem is then divided into two steps: (1) We evaluate a large set of possible designs using the simple LP model and rank them in terms of overall cost. (2) We extract a subset of the top designs found using the LP model, and thoroughly re-evaluate them using the accurate MILP model. Crucial to this approach is determining
N
, the number of designs to be evaluated using the simple LP model, and
s
, the number of top designs to be re-evaluated using the accurate MILP model.

VI
Conclusion

This paper proposes an ordinal optimization (OO) approach to size the photovoltaic (PV) and battery storage components in a microgrid containing a diesel generator and a load connected to the grid. Ordinal optimization allows for the use of simplified models for the sizing with minimal sacrifice to accuracy.

To determine the contribution of each resource at a given time, mixed-integer linear programming is used instead of the common dynamic programming and is shown to yield lower costs. Future work would consider the sizing problem at the larger power network level.

Abstract
. The two-boost problem in space mission design asks whether two points of phase space can be connected with the help of two boosts of given energy. We provide a positive answer for a class of systems related to the restricted three-body problem by defining and computing its Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology. The main technical work goes into dealing with the noncompactness of the corresponding energy hypersurfaces.



1.
Introduction

The
two-boost problem
goes back to the classical work of W. Hohmann on the attainability of heavenly bodies
[
8
]
. Although this work was written more than four decades before the first human being stepped on the moon, the Hohmann transfer is still one of the crucial ingredients in space mission design, see e.g.
[
15
]
. The Hohmann transfer
is a transfer between two circular orbits in the Kepler problem with the help of a Kepler ellipse which is tangent to the two circles. It requires two tangential boosts, one to transfer from the first circle to the ellipse and a second one to transfer from the ellipse to the second circle.
Given two points in the plane different from the origin, there always exists a conic section through the two points with focus in the origin. This means that, for the Kepler problem, two points in phase space can always be connected with the help of two boosts. The motivating question for this paper is whether this continues to hold for more general systems.

The general setup for the two-boost problem is as follows: Consider the cotangent bundle
T^{*}Q
of a manifold
Q
with its canonical exact symplectic form
\omega=d\lambda
,
\lambda=p\,dq
, and a Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}Q\to\mathbb{R}
. Given two points
q_{0},q_{1}\in Q
and an energy value
c
, the two-boost problem asks for the existence of a Hamiltonian orbit of energy
c
connecting the cotangent fibres
T_{q_{0}}^{*}Q
and
T_{q_{1}}^{*}Q
.
Such orbits arise as critical points of the
Rabinowitz action functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}

\displaystyle:\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(v,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda(\partial_{t}v)dt-\eta\int_{0}^{1}(H-c)(v(t))dt

associated to the path space

\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}:=\left\{v\in W^{1,2}([0,1],T^{*}Q)\ \big|\ v(i)\in T^{*}_{q_{i}}Q\quad\textrm{for}\quad i=0,1\right\}.

Thus the two-boost problem has a positive answer whenever the corresponding
Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology

{\rm LRFH}_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
is well-defined and nontrivial.

As observed in
[
4
]
, the well-definedness of (Lagrangian) Rabinowitz Floer homology requires some hypothesis on the Hamiltonian
H
. A suitable class is formed by
magnetic Hamiltonians

H(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}|p-A(q)|^{2}-V(q)

for a magnetic potential
A\in\Omega^{1}(Q)
, a potential
1
1

1

As is customary in celestial mechanics, our
V
is
minus
the physical potential.

V:Q\to\mathbb{R}
, and a Riemannian metric on
Q
. Under the assumption that the underlying manifold
Q
is
compact
, the Rabinowitz Floer homology of such Hamiltonians (with possibly noncompact magnetic field) has been studied in
[
5
]
for the periodic case and in
[
10
]
for the Lagrangian case. An important dynamical quantity associated to such a Hamiltonian is its
Mañé critical value
. By a Theorem of W. Merry
[
10
]
, for
c
above the Mañé critical value the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology
{\rm LRFH}_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
is well-defined and nontrivial, hence the two-boost problem is solvable.

Returning to celestial mechanics, let us consider the
planar circular restricted 3-body problem
. Here two heavy bodies (the primaries) move under their mutual gravitational attraction on circles around their center of mass in a plane, and the third body (of negligible mass) moves in the same plane under the gravitational attraction by the primaries. In rotating coordinates, this system is described by the autonomous Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H(q_{1},q_{2},p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})+p_{1}q_{2}-p_{2}q_{1}-V(q_{1},q_{2}),

where
V
is the sum of the (negative) Coulomb potentials of the two primaries (see e.g.
[
7
]
). Writing it as

\displaystyle H(q_{1},q_{2},p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(|p_{1}+q_{2}|^{2}+|p_{2}-q_{1}|^{2})-V_{\rm eff}(q_{1},q_{2})

with the effective potential
V_{\rm eff}(q_{1},q_{2})=V(q_{1},q_{2})+\frac{1}{2}(q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2})
, we see that this is a magnetic Hamiltonian, with magnetic field generating the Coriolis force.
In contrast to the previous paragraph, the configuration space
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{q_{E},q_{M}\}
is now noncompact due to possible collisions with the primaries at positions
q_{E},q_{M}
and possible escapes to infinity.
Our goal is to define and compute Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology in this situation.
Since the noncompactness due to collisions can be removed by Moser or Levi-Civita regularization (see
[
7
]
), we will focus our attention on the noncompactness at infinity in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For this, we consider the quadratic Hamiltonian

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{0}(q_{1},q_{2},p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})+p_{1}q_{2}-p_{2}q_{1}=\frac{p_{r}^{2}}{2}+\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{2r^{2}}+p_{\theta},

where
(r,\theta)
are polar coordinates on the plane and
(p_{r},p_{\theta})
their conjugate momenta.
We introduce the following set of smooth functions constant outside a compact set:

(1.2)

\mathcal{H}:=\left\{h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \Big|\ dh\in C_{c}^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad h>0,\quad h-dh(p\partial_{p})>0\right\}.

For
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we define the Rabinowitz action functional
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}:\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as above, with
Q=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
H-c
replaced by
H_{0}-h
. Our first result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
H_{0}
be as in (
1.1
). For every
h\in\mathcal{H}
the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology of
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is well defined.
Moreover, for any two
h_{0},h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology of
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{0}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{1}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
are isomorphic.

We denote the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology of
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
by
{\rm LRFH}_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
. Here we use coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and a half-integer grading by Maslov indices (see §
2.2
).
Moreover, it comes with a canonical action filtration. Our next result computes the positive action part in the case
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
in (
1.1
),
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and any
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
, the positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz homology of
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is well defined
and equal to

{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\textrm{for}\quad*=1/2,\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This shows that the two-boost problem at energy
0
is solvable for
H_{0}-h
with
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Note that
h(q,p)=c+V(q)
belongs to
\mathcal{H}
for each constant
c>0
and compactly supported potential
V\geq 0
, so the two-boost problem is solvable for
H_{0}-V
with such
V
at each energy
c>0
.

The potential in the planar circular restricted 3-body problem (after regularisation) satisfies
V\geq 0
, but it is not compactly supported. The following result allows us to deal with certain non-compactly supported potentials.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
H_{0}
be the Hamiltonian in (
1.1
). Fix
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
and a positive constant
c>0
.
Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative potential for which there exist
r_{0}>0
and

(\alpha,a)\in\{2\}\times\left(0,\frac{c^{2}}{4}\right)\cup(2,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
such that for
r>r_{0}
we have

V(r,\theta)\leq\frac{a}{r^{\alpha}}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\partial_{r}V(r,\theta)\geq-\frac{a}{r^{\alpha+1}}.

Then there exists a function
V_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-V-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}=\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-V_{0}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
.

In view of Theorem
1.2
, the two-boost problem is therefore solvable for
H_{0}-V
at energy
c>0
for any
(V,c)
as in Proposition
1.3
. In particular, this holds for any
c>0
and
V\geq 0
which decays at infinity as
r^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>2
. Note that this does
not
cover the planar circular restricted 3-body problem, in which the potential decays as
r^{-1}
. We defer the extension to this case to future work.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that for
h\in\mathcal{H}
the Liouville vector field
p\partial_{p}
is transverse to
(H_{0}-h)^{-1}(0)
, so the zero level set of
H_{0}-h
is of restricted contact type. Thus the solutions of the two-boost problem resulting from Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.3
can also be interpreted as solutions of Arnold’s chord conjecture (existence of a Reeb chord connecting two Legendrians) for certain noncompact contact manifolds.

Remark 1.5
.

Work by R. Nicholls
[
11
]
provides some evidence that, if defined, the Mañé critical value for the restricted three-body problem should be zero. It would be interesting to make this rigorous by developing a notion of Mañé critical value for magnetic Hamiltonians over noncompact base manifolds.

2.
Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology

Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology was first defined by Merry in
[
10
]
for virtually exact Lagrangian submanifolds and compact hypersurfaces of virtual contact type in symplectically aspherical symplectic manifolds. In his setting, the pull-back of the symplectic form to the universal cover of the symplectic manifold had to be exact and the pre-image of the chosen hypersurface in the universal cover had to be of contact type. The symplectic manifold and the Lagrangian submanifold could be non-compact, but the chosen hypersurface of virtual contact type had to be compact.

Our aim is to use Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology to analyse the Planar Circular Restricted Three Body Problem. Therefore, we are interested in the setting of a cotangent bundle
T^{*}Q
with its standard symplectic form and a pair of fibres
T_{q_{0}}^{*}Q,T_{q_{1}}^{*}Q
as the Lagrangians. In this section we will explain the definition of the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology in this setting. It is a far easier setting than the one in
[
10
]
, as the cotangent bundle is an exact manifold and the fibres are exact Lagrangians. Moreover, the level sets above the 4th and 5th Lagrange point of the Hamiltonian corresponding to the Planar Circular Restricted Three Body Problem are all of exact contact type (see Remark
1.4
).
The only challenge of this setting is that all the energy level sets in the Restricted Three Body Problem are non-compact.
Rabinowitz Floer homology (for periodic orbits) of noncompact energy level sets of “tentacular Hamiltonians” on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
has been defined in
[
12
]
. However, the Hamiltonian for the Planar Circular Restricted Three Body Problem does not belong to this class, so new arguments are needed for this setting.

2.1.
The Lagrangian Rabinowitz action functional

Throughout this section,
Q
denotes a smooth oriented
n
-dimensional manifold, and
T^{*}Q
its cotangent bundle equipped with the exact symplectic form
\omega=d\lambda
for the canonical
1
-form
\lambda:=p\,dq
.
Fix
q_{0},q_{1}\in Q
and define the space of paths

\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}:=\left\{v\in W^{1,2}([0,1],T^{*}Q)\ \big|\ v(i)\in T^{*}_{q_{i}}Q\quad\textrm{for}\quad i=0,1\right\}.

Consider a Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}Q\to\mathbb{R}
with regular level set
H^{-1}(0)
.
The
Lagrangian Rabinowitz action functional

\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}:\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
associated to
H
is defined as

\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(v,\eta):=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda(\partial_{t}v)dt-\eta\int_{0}^{1}H(v(t))dt.

Remark 2.1
.

We have a natural bijection
\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\ni v\mapsto\overline{v}\in\mathscr{H}_{q_{1},q_{0}}
with
\overline{v}(t):=v(1-t)
. Then

\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(v,\eta)=-\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{1},q_{0}}(\overline{v},-\eta)\qquad\forall\ (v,\eta)\in\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}.

The derivative of
\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
in direction
(\xi,\sigma)\in T_{v}\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times T_{\eta}\mathbb{R}
equals

(2.1)

d\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(v,\eta)[\xi,\sigma]=\int_{0}^{1}\omega(\xi,\partial_{t}v-\eta X_{H})-\sigma\int_{0}^{1}H(v)dt.

Here
X_{H}
is the Hamiltonian vector field defined by
dH=-i_{X_{H}}\omega
.
Consequently,
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
if and only if it satisfies

\partial_{t}v=\eta X_{H}(v)\quad\textrm{and}\quad v(t)\in H^{-1}(0)\quad\forall\ t\in[0,1].

Thus we can have three types of critical points:

•

\eta>0
and
\widetilde{v}(t):=v(t/\eta)
is a Hamiltonian chord (i.e., an integral curve of
X_{H}
) on
H^{-1}(0)
from
T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q
to
T_{q_{1}}^{*}Q
;

•

\eta<0
and
\widetilde{v}(t):=v(t/\eta)
is a Hamiltonian chord on
H^{-1}(0)
from
T^{*}_{q_{1}}Q
to
T_{q_{0}}^{*}Q
;

•

\eta=0
and
v
is a constant path in
T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q\cap T_{q_{1}}^{*}Q\cap H^{-1}(0)
(which can only occur if
q_{0}=q_{1}
).

In particular, if
T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q\cap H^{-1}(0)=\emptyset
or
T_{q_{1}}^{*}Q\cap H^{-1}(0)=\emptyset
, then
\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}})=\emptyset
. Therefore, from now on we will assume that

(2.2)

T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q\cap H^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset,\quad T^{*}_{q_{1}}Q\cap H^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset,\quad\textrm{and }T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q\pitchfork H^{-1}(0)\text{ if }q_{0}=q_{1}.

In order to construct Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology we want the critical set of the action functional to be bounded in
L^{\infty}
. It is possible to define Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology without this assumption, but the construction is much more challenging, so we postpone it for future projects. In fact, to construct Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology we will need the boundedness of the critical set of the action functional to persist under compact perturbations of the Hamiltonian. We formalize this in the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Consider a Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}Q\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
H^{-1}(0)
is noncompact. Fix
q_{0},q_{1}\in T^{*}Q
satisfying (
2.2
). Let
K\subseteq T^{*}Q
be a compact set and let

\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)\ |\ dh\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K)\},

be an open neighbourhood of
0
in
\{h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)\ |\ dh\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K)\}
.
We say that the associated Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer functional
\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}:\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
has critical set
continuously compact
in
(K,\mathcal{H})
if

\forall\ h\in\mathcal{H}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\forall\ (v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\quad\textrm{we have}\quad v([0,1])\subseteq K.

2.2.
Grading

In
[
4
]
, Rabinowitz Floer homology (for periodic orbits) is equipped with a shifted integer grading under the hypothesis that the ambient symplectic manifold has vanishing first Chern class. In this subsection we indicate how this carries over to the Lagrangian setting; see
[
10
]
for details.

Consider an exact symplectic manifold
(V,\omega=d\lambda)
of dimension
2n
and a Hamiltonian
H:V\to\mathbb{R}
with regular level set
\Sigma=H^{-1}(0)
such that
\xi=\ker(\lambda|_{\Sigma})
is a contact structure. Consider in addition two Lagrangian submanifolds
L_{0},L_{1}\subset V
transverse to
\Sigma
with
\lambda|_{L_{i}}=0
. We assume that
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
are either disjoint or equal.
The condition of vanishing first Chern class in
[
4
]
gets replaced by vanishing of the relative first Chern class
c_{1}(V,L_{0}\cup L_{1})
. The transversal Conley-Zehnder index in
[
4
]
gets replaced by the
transverse Maslov index
defined a follows.
Let
(v,\eta)
be a critical point of the Rabinowitz action functional
\mathcal{A}^{H}
on the space of paths from
L_{0}
to
L_{1}
. Pick a symplectic trivialization
v^{*}\xi\cong[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}
sending
T_{v(i)}L_{i}\cap\xi_{v(i)}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
for
i=0,1
. In this trivialization, the image of
T_{v(i)}L_{i}\cap\xi_{v(i)}
under the linearized flow of
\eta X_{H}
gives a path
\Lambda:[0,1]\to\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
in the Grassmannian
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
of Lagrangian subspaces of
(\mathbb{R}^{2n-2},\omega_{\rm std})
with
\Lambda(0)=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Let
\mu^{\rm tr}(v,\eta)\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
be the Maslov index of the path
\Lambda
defined by Robbin and Salamon in
[
13
]
. By vanishing of
c_{1}(V,L_{0}\cup L_{1})
this is independent of the choices. In the case
\eta=0
(which occurs only if
L_{0}=L_{1}
),
v
is a critical point of an auxiliary Morse function
f
on
L_{0}\cap\Sigma
. Its
signature index
is
{\rm ind}^{\sigma}_{f}(v,0):=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm sign}\,{\rm Hess}_{f}(v)
, and we set it to zero if
\eta\neq 0
. According to
[
4
, Appendix A]
, the
Maslov index

\mu(v,\eta):=\mu^{\rm tr}(v,\eta)+{\rm ind}^{\sigma}_{f}(v,\eta)\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}

defines a half-integer grading on the Rabinowitz Floer complex with respect to which the differential has degreee
-1
.

This discussion applies to the setting in this section where
V=T^{*}Q
and
L_{i}=T_{q_{i}}^{*}Q
for an oriented
n
-dimensional manifold
Q
.

Example 2.3
.

Consider the free Hamiltonian
H_{\bullet}(q,p):=\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}
on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
and fix an energy
c>0
. Assume first that
q_{0}\neq q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{A}^{H_{\bullet}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
has exactly two critical points
(v_{\pm},\eta_{\pm})
given by

v_{\pm}(t)=\Bigl((1-t)q_{0}+tq_{1},\frac{q_{1}-q_{0}}{\eta_{\pm}}\Bigr),\qquad\eta_{\pm}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{2c}}{|q_{1}-q_{0}|}.

The linearized flow of
\eta_{\pm}X_{H_{\bullet}}
along
v_{\pm}
equals
\Phi_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}{{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}}&t\eta_{\pm}{{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}}\\
0&{{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}}\end{pmatrix}
, so the corresponding path of Lagrangian subspaces
\Lambda:[0,1]\to\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
is given by
\Lambda(t)=\Phi_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})={\rm graph}\,A(t)
with
A(t)=t\eta_{\pm}{{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}}
. Thus the (Localization) property in
[
13
]
yields

\mu(v_{\pm},\eta_{\pm})=\mu^{\rm tr}(v_{\pm},\eta_{\pm})=\frac{1}{2}{\rm sign}\,A(1)-\frac{1}{2}{\rm sign}\,A(0)=\pm\frac{n-1}{2}.

In the case
q_{0}=q_{1}
, let
f
be a Morse function on the
(n-1)
-sphere
T_{q_{0}}Q\cap H_{\bullet}^{-1}(c)
with exaxctly two critical points, the maximum
v_{+}
and the minimum
v_{-}
. Then
\mathcal{A}^{H_{\bullet}-c}_{q_{0},q_{0}}
has exactly two critical points
(v_{\pm},0)
of index

\mu(v_{\pm},0)={\rm ind}^{\sigma}_{f}(v_{\pm},0)=\pm\frac{n-1}{2}.

For
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
this immediately implies

{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{\pm}\left(H_{\bullet}^{-1}(c),T^{*}_{q_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{n},T^{*}_{q_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\textrm{for}\quad*=\pm\frac{n-1}{2},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Moreover, for
n\neq 3
and any
q_{0},q_{1}
(equal or not) we obtain

{\rm LRFH}_{*}\left(H_{\bullet}^{-1}(c),T^{*}_{q_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{n},T^{*}_{q_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\textrm{for}\quad*=\frac{n-1}{2}\textrm{ and }*=-\frac{n-1}{2},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(This also holds for
n=2
, but an additional argument is needed to show that the two critical points of index
\pm 1/2
do not cancel in homology).

Remark 2.4
.

The previous example generalizes to the free Hamiltonian
H_{\bullet}(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}
on the cotangent bundle of an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(Q,g)
. For
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
and
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{\bullet}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
, the path
v
projects onto a geodesic
\bar{v}
from
q_{0}
to
q_{1}
and Proposition 6.38 in
[
14
]
yields

\mu(v,\eta)={\rm ind}(\bar{v})+{\rm sign}(\eta)\frac{n-1}{2},

where
{\rm ind}(\bar{v})
is the Morse index of the geodesic
\bar{v}
(i.e., the number of conjugate points along
\bar{v}
).

2.3.
Floer trajectories

In this subsection we introduce the fundamental notion for constructing any Floer-type homology - the Floer trajectories. We start by equipping the space
\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
with a metric. An
almost complex structure

J
on a manifold
M
is a bundle endomorphism
J:TM\to TM
satisfying
J^{2}=-\operatorname{Id}
. An almost complex structure
J
on a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is called
compatible
if
\omega(\ \cdot\ ,J\cdot\ )
defines a Riemannian metric on
M
. Denote by
\mathcal{J}(M,\omega)
the space of all compatible almost complex structures on
(M,\omega)
with the
C^{\infty}
-topology.
A linear algebra argument
[
3
, Prop. 13.1]
shows that
\mathcal{J}(M,\omega)
is contractible.

A smooth
2
-parameter family
\{J_{t,\eta}\}_{(t,\eta)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}}
of compatible almost complex structures on
(T^{*}Q,\omega)
defines an
L^{2}
-metric on
\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}
by

\langle(\xi_{1},\sigma_{1}),(\xi_{2},\sigma_{2})\rangle:=\int_{0}^{1}\omega(\xi_{1}(t),J_{t,\eta}(v(t))\xi_{2}(t))+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}.

for
(\xi_{i},\sigma_{i})\in T_{(v,\eta)}(\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R})
.
The gradient of the Lagrangian Rabinowitz action functional
\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
with respect to this metric equals

\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(v,\eta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-J_{t,\eta}(v(t))(\partial_{t}v-\eta X_{H})\\
-\int H(v)dt.\end{array}\right)

Fix an open subset
\mathcal{V}\subseteq T^{*}Q
and
\mathbb{J}\in\mathcal{J}(T^{*}Q,\omega)
.
We denote by
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{J})
the set of all smooth maps

[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{J}(T^{*}Q,\omega),\qquad(t,\eta)\mapsto J_{t,\eta}

satisfying

\displaystyle J_{t,\eta}(x)=\mathbb{J}(x)\text{ for }x\notin\mathcal{V}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sup_{(t,\eta)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}}\|J_{t,\eta}\|_{C^{k}}<+\infty\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Fix Hamiltonians
H_{\pm}
and
J_{\pm}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{J})
. A
homotopy
from
(H_{-},J_{-})
to
(H_{+},J_{+})
is a smooth family
\Gamma=\{(H_{s},J_{s})\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
of Hamiltonians and compatible almost complex structures
J_{s}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{J})
which agrees with
(H_{-},J_{-})
for
s\leq s_{-}
and with
(H_{+},J_{+})
for
s\geq s_{+}
, with some
s_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
.
A solution
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}
to the gradient flow equation
\partial_{s}u=\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{s}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(u)
is called a
Floer trajectory
. In other words, a Floer trajectory
u=(v,\eta)\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1],T^{*}Q)\times W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R})
is a solution to the equations

(2.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}v(s,t)

\displaystyle=-J_{s,t,\eta}(v(s,t))(\partial_{s}v(s,t)-\eta(s)X_{H_{s}}(v(s,t))),

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\eta(s)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}H_{s}\circ v(s,t)dt,

v(s,0)\in T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q\quad\textrm{and}\quad v(s,1)\in T^{*}_{q_{1}}Q\quad\forall\ s\in\mathbb{R}.

For
(x_{-},x_{+})\in\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{-}}_{q_{0},q_{1}})\times\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{+}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
we denote the space of Floer trajectories from
x_{-}
to
x_{+}
by

\mathscr{F}_{\Gamma}(x_{-},x_{+}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}&\partial_{s}u=\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{s}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(u),\\
\hskip-5.69046pt\smash{\raisebox{6.0pt}{ $u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}$}}&\lim_{s\to\pm\infty}u(s)=x_{\pm}.\end{array}\right\}

In case the homotopy
\Gamma
is constant in
s
, i.e.
H_{s}\equiv H
and
J_{s,t,\eta}\equiv J\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{J})
for some Hamiltonian
H
and a compatible almost complex structure
J
, we denote
\mathscr{F}_{H,J}(x_{-},x_{+}):=\mathscr{F}_{\Gamma}(x_{-},x_{+})
.

Moreover, for every pair
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we denote

(2.4)

\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}&(x_{-},x_{+})\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{-}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{+}}_{q_{0},q_{1}},\\
\hskip-5.69046pt\smash{\raisebox{6.0pt}{ $u\in\mathscr{F}_{\Gamma}(x_{-},x_{+})$}}&\mathcal{A}^{H_{-}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x_{-})\geq a,\quad\mathcal{A}^{H_{+}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x_{+})\leq b.\end{array}\right\}

Analogously, we denote
\mathcal{M}^{H,J}(a,b):=\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
whenever the homotopy
\Gamma
is constant in
s
and equal to the pair
(H,J)
.

If s homotopy
\Gamma
is constant in
s
, then the action is increasing along Floer trajectories. However, for a nonconstant homotopy
\Gamma
this need not be the case. To deal with this phenomenon, we introduce a condition that ensures that the action cannot decrease indefinitely along a Floer trajectory.
We say a that homotopy
\Gamma:=\{(H_{s},J_{s})\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
with
H_{\pm}:=\lim_{s\to\pm\infty}H_{s}
satisfies the
Novikov finiteness condition
if for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}&y\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{+}}_{q_{0},q_{1}},\quad\exists\ x\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{-}}_{q_{0},q_{1}},\\
\hskip-5.69046pt\smash{\raisebox{6.0pt}{ $\mathcal{A}^{H_{+}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(y)$}}&\mathcal{A}^{H_{-}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)\geq a,\quad\mathscr{F}_{\Gamma}(x,y)\neq\emptyset.\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle>-\infty,

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}&x\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{-}}_{q_{0},q_{1}},\quad\exists\ y\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{+}}_{q_{0},q_{1}},\\
\hskip-5.69046pt\smash{\raisebox{6.0pt}{ $\mathcal{A}^{H_{-}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)$}}&\mathcal{A}^{H_{+}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)\leq b,\quad\mathscr{F}_{\Gamma}(x,y)\neq\emptyset.\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle<+\infty.

2.4.
Defining Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology

In this subsection we will recall the construction of the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology from
[
10
]
, using standard Floer techniques introduced in Floer’s seminal paper
[
6
]
and the techniques typical for the setting of the Rabinowitz action functional from the first two author’s paper
[
4
]
. More precisely, we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Consider a cotangent bundle
(T^{*}Q,\omega)
with its standard symplectic form and a Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}Q\to\mathbb{R}
with regular level set
H^{-1}(0)
.
Fix a pair
q_{0},q_{1}\in Q
such that both sets
H^{-1}(0)\cap T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q
and
H^{-1}(0)\cap T^{*}_{q_{1}}Q
are compact and nonempty. Fix a compatible almost complex structure
\mathbb{J}\in\mathcal{J}(T^{*}Q,\omega)
.
Assume that there exists
a compact subset
K
, an open subset
\mathcal{V}
satisfying
K\subseteq\mathcal{V}\subseteq T^{*}Q
, and an open neighbourhood
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)\ |\ dh\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K)\}
of
0

such that:

(1)

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
the Hamiltonian
H+h
satisfies (
2.2
);

(2)

the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer functional
\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
has critical set continuously compact in
(K,\mathcal{H})
;

(3)

for all
h_{0},h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
and
J_{0},J_{1}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{J})
every homotopy
\Gamma=\{(H+h_{s},J_{s})\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}

from
(H+h_{0},J_{0})
to
(H+h_{1},J_{1})
satisfies the Novikov finiteness condition (
2.5
)
and for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
the space of Floer trajectories
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
is bounded in the
L^{\infty}
-norm.

Then for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology
{\rm LRFH}_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
is well defined and isomorphic to
{\rm LRFH}_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
.

Proof.

We will define Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology in the case
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
, and then briefly explain the case
q_{0}=q_{1}
. Therefore, we first assume
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
.

The first step is to show that for
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
and generic
h\in\mathcal{H}
the Lagrangian Rabinowitz action functional
\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is Morse, i.e., its Hessian
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)
has trivial kernel for all
x\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
.
Observe that for
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
we we have
\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\cap\left(\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\{0\}\right)=\emptyset
. By a standard Sard-Smale argument
[
9
, Theorem A.51]
, there exists a residual subset
\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}\subseteq C_{0}^{\infty}(K)
such that for all
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
we have

D\phi^{\eta}_{H+h}(T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q)\pitchfork T^{*}_{q_{1}}Q\qquad\forall\ (v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}},

where
\phi^{t}_{H+h}
denotes the Hamiltonian flow of
X_{H+h}
.
The condition is equivalent to the triviality of the kernel of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)
for all
x\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
.
A straightforward consequence of the Morse property of the Lagrangian Rabinowitz action functional and continuous compactness of the critical set of
\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is that the critical set of
\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is finite for each
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
.

Fix
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
. Since
K\subseteq\mathcal{V}
, by continuous compactness of the critical set of
\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
in
(K,\mathcal{H})
for every
J\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{J})
, we have

(2.6)

v(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])\cap\mathcal{V}\neq\emptyset\quad\forall\ u=(v,\eta)\in\mathscr{F}_{H+h,J}(x,y)\quad\forall\ x,y\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}.

Therefore, we can apply the Sard-Smale argument in
[
9
, Thm. A.51]
to conclude that there exists a residual set
\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{J}(T^{*}Q,\omega,\mathcal{V})
such that for all
x,y\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
and all
J\in\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h}
the space of Floer trajectories
\mathscr{F}_{H+h,J}(x,y)
is a transversely cut out smooth manifold.

Let us now fix
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
and
J\in\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h}
.
For every
x,y\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
there is a natural
\mathbb{R}
-action on
\mathscr{F}_{H+h,J}(x,y)
given by

\mathscr{F}_{H+h,J}(x,y)\times\mathbb{R}\ni(u,s)\longmapsto u(s+\cdot)\in\mathscr{F}_{H+h,J}(x,y).

Denote the quotient space
\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{H+h,J}(x,y):=\mathscr{F}_{H+h,J}(x,y)/\mathbb{R}
.
By
[
10
, Thm. 2.23]
, for all
x,y\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
with
x\neq y
the quotient
\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{H+h,J}(x,y)
is also a smooth manifold and its dimension is given by

\dim\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{H+h,J}(x,y)=\mu(y)-\mu(x)-1.

By condition 3 in Theorem
2.5
, for all
x,y\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
the space of Floer trajectories
\mathscr{F}_{H+h,J}(x,y)
is bounded in the
L^{\infty}
-norm. Now standard compactness arguments (see
[
6
, Prop. 3b]
or
[
2
, Thm. 9.1.7]
)
imply that
\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{H+h,J}(x,y)
is compact up to breaking in the sense of Floer.
If
\mu(y)-\mu(x)=1
this means that
\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{H+h,J}(x,y)
is a finite set of points
[
2
, Thm. 9.2.1]
.

Now we are ready to define Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology. For
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
let
CF_{k}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-vector space of formal sums of the form
\sum_{x\in S}x
, where
S\subseteq\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is a (possibly infinite) set satisfying
\mu(x)=k
for all
x\in S
and the
Novikov finiteness condition

\#\{x\in S\ |\ \mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)>a\}<+\infty\qquad\forall\ a\in\mathbb{R}.

Now we fix an almost complex structure
J\in\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h}
and turn
CF(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
into a chain complex. For
x,y\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
with
\mu(y)-\mu(x)=1
we set

n(x,y):=\#\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{H+h,J}(x,y)\operatorname{mod}2\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

The Floer boundary operator
\partial_{k}^{J}:CF_{k}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})\to CF_{k-1}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
is the linear map defined on generators by

\partial_{k}^{J}y:=\sum_{x}n(x,y)x.

Compactness up to breaking implies that
\partial_{k}^{J}\circ\partial_{k+1}^{J}=0
, so we can define the
Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology

{\rm LRFH}_{k}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}};J):=\ker\partial_{k}^{J}/\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}^{J}.

A standard continuation argument shows that the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology does not depend on the choice of
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
and
J\in\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h}
. For this, let
h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
and
J_{i}\in\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h_{i}}
for
i=0,1
be given. Pick a homotopy
\Gamma=\{(H+h_{s},J_{s})\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
connecting
(H+h_{0},J_{0})
to
(H+h_{1},J_{1})
as in condition 3 of Theorem
2.5
.
As before, it follows that for generic
\Gamma
and any pair
(x,y)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h_{0}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h_{1}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
the associated space of Floer trajectories
\mathscr{F}_{\Gamma}(x,y)
is a smooth manifold of dimension
\mu(y)-\mu(x)
which is compact up to breaking. In particular, if
\mu(x)=\mu(y)
then
\mathscr{F}_{\Gamma}(x,y)
is a finite set of points and we set
m(x,y):=\#\mathscr{F}_{\Gamma}(x,y)\mod 2
. We define a linear map
\phi^{\Gamma}:(CF_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h_{1}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}),\partial^{J_{1}})\to(CF(\mathcal{A}^{H+h_{0}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}),\partial^{J_{0}})
on generators by

\phi^{\Gamma}(y):=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h_{0}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}}m(x,y)x.

Here the Novikov finiteness condition (
2.5
) on
\Gamma
ensures that
\phi^{\Gamma}\left(CF(\mathcal{A}^{H+h_{1}}_{q_{0},q_{1}})\right)\subseteq CF(\mathcal{A}^{H+h_{0}}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
. Compactness up to breaking implies that
\phi^{\Gamma}
is a chain map, and composition with a homotopy in the opposite direction shows that
\phi^{\Gamma}
induces an isomorphism on homology (see
[
2
, Chapter 11]
for details on this standard argument).

Since
{\rm LRFH}_{k}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}};J)
does not depend on
J\in\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h}
, we can drop
J
from the notation and write is as
{\rm LRFH}_{k}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
. Since this homology is the same vector space for all
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
, we can unambiguously extend it as this vector space to all
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
. This proves the theorem in the case
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
.

In the case
q_{0}=q_{1}
, by condition 1 for each
h\in\mathcal{H}
we have critical points
(v,0)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{0}}
where
v
is a constant path in the nonempty transverse intersection
S_{h}:=T^{*}_{q_{0}}Q\cap(H+h)^{-1}(0)
. If
S_{h}
has positive dimension, then the functional
\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{0}}
will only be Morse-Bott for generic
h\in\mathcal{H}
. This phenomenon is well known in Rabinowitz Floer theory and can be dealt with by picking a Morse function on
S_{h}
and counting gradient flow lines with cascades, see
[
4
]
. With this understood, the rest of the argument works as in the previous case.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Consider a cotangent bundle
(T^{*}Q,\omega)
with its standard symplectic form and a Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}Q\to\mathbb{R}
such that
H^{-1}(0)
is of exact contact type. Assume that there exists an exhausting sequence of compact sets
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
K_{n}\subseteq K_{n+1}\subseteq T^{*}Q
,
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}=T^{*}Q
and an open neighbourhood
\mathcal{H}
of
0
in
\{h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)\ |\ dh\in C_{c}^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)\}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the sets
K_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{n}=\{h\in\mathcal{H}\ |\ dh\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K_{n})\}
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
2.5
. Then for any
h\in\mathcal{H}
the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology
{\rm LRFH}_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
is well defined and isomorphic to
{\rm LRFH}_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
.

2.5.
Positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology

The action functional
\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
provides an
\mathbb{R}
-filtration on
CF_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
by

CF_{*}^{\leq a}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right)\coloneqq\left\{{\textstyle\sum_{x\in S}x}\in CF_{*}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right)\left|\ \sup_{x\in S}\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)\leq a\right.\right\}.

Since Floer trajectories are defined by the
L^{2}
-gradient of the action functional, the boundary operator does not increase the action, i.e.

(2.7)

\partial\left(CF^{\leq a}_{*+1}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right)\right)\subseteq CF^{\leq a}_{*}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right).

The
positive Rabinowitz Floer homology

{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
is the homology of the quotient complex

CF_{*}^{+}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right)\coloneqq CF_{*}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right)\Big/CF_{*}^{\leq 0}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right)

with boundary operator
\partial^{\scriptscriptstyle+}
induced by
\partial
on the quotient.

In order to get the required transversality in the preceding discussion, we need to replace
H
by
H+h
with
h\in\mathcal{H}^{reg}
as in the previous subsection.
For independence of positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology of the choice of
h
we need a further condition. The regular level set
H^{-1}(0)
is said to be of
exact contact type
if there exists a Liouville vector field
Y
on
T^{*}Q
(satisfying
L_{Y}\omega=\omega
) such that
dH(Y)>0
along
H^{-1}(0)
.
Setting
(\xi,\sigma)=(Y,\eta)
in equation (
2.1
) we get

(2.8)

\mathcal{A}^{H}(v,\eta)-d\mathcal{A}^{H}(v,\eta)[Y,\eta]=\eta\int_{0}^{1}dH(Y)(v(t))dt.

At critical points the second term on the left-hand side vanishes and we conclude

(2.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Crit}^{\pm}\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{x\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\ |\ \pm\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)>0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\ |\ \pm\eta>0\right\}.

The following lemma shows that the exact contact type property provides a set of compactly supported perturbations for which the set of positive action values is bounded away from zero.

Lemma 2.7
.

Consider a cotangent bundle
(T^{*}Q,\omega)
with its standard symplectic form and a Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}Q\to\mathbb{R}
such that
H^{-1}(0)
is of exact contact type. Fix
q_{0},q_{1}\in Q
with
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
.
Assume that there exists a compact subset
K\subseteq T^{*}Q
and an open neighbourhood
\mathcal{H}
of
0
in
\{h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)\ |\ dh\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K)\}
such that for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
the following holds:

(2.10)

\forall\ (v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\quad\textrm{we have}\quad v([0,1])\subseteq K.

Then there exists an open neighbourhood
\mathcal{O}(K)
of
0
in
\mathcal{H}
such that

(2.11)

\inf\left\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(x)&x\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}},\quad h\in\mathcal{O}(K)\end{array}\right\}>0.

Proof.

By assumption there exists a Liouville vector field
Y
such that
dH(Y)>0
along
H^{-1}(0)
. Since
K
is compact,

\delta:=\inf\{dH(Y)(x)\ |\ x\in K\cap H^{-1}(0)\}>0.

For
h\in\mathcal{H}
small enough in the
C^{1}
-norm we then have

(2.12)

(H+h)^{-1}(0)\cap K\subseteq\left\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}x\in K&d(H+h)(Y)(x)\geq\delta/2\end{array}\right\}.

Denote by
\mathcal{O}(K)
the set of all
h\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying (
2.12
). Then
\mathcal{O}(K)
is an open neighbourhood of
0
in
\{h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)\ |\ dh\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K)\}
.
Consider
h\in\mathcal{O}(K)
and
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
, so
\eta>0
in view of (
2.9
) applied to
H+h
.
By (
2.10
) we have
v([0,1])\subset K\cap(H+h)^{-1}(0)
, so from (
2.8
) and (

) we obtain

|\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(v,\eta)|\geq\delta\eta/2.

On the other hand, from

for
i=0,1
and
\partial_{s}v=\eta X_{H+h}(v)
we deduce

0<\varepsilon\leq|v(1)-v(0)|\leq\eta\int_{0}^{1}|X_{H+h}(v(t))|dt\leq C\eta

with the positive constants

\varepsilon:=\textrm{dist}(K\cap T_{q_{0}}^{*}Q,K\cap T_{q_{1}}^{*}Q),\qquad C:=\max\{|X_{H+h}(x)|\;\bigl|\;x\in K\}.

The two estimates combine to
|\mathcal{A}^{H+h}_{q_{0},q_{1}}(v,\eta)|\geq\delta\varepsilon/2C>0
.
∎

The next result provides conditions under which positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology is not only well defined, but also independent of the auxiliary choices and invariant under compact perturbations.

Corollary 2.8
.

Consider the setting as in Theorem
2.5
with sets
K\subseteq\mathcal{V}\subseteq T^{*}Q
and
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)\ |\ dh\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K)\}

.

Proof.

By assumption the critical set of

is continuously compact in

. Denote by

consisting of all Hamiltonians

is dense in
\mathcal{O}
.
Fix

. By assumption, there exist

for

and a homotopy
\Gamma=\{(H+h_{s},J_{s})\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
with

and

from

to

with the following properties:

);

∎

3.
Bounds on the Floer trajectories

The aim of this section is to show that the Copernican Hamiltonian
H_{0}
defined in (
1.1
) together with the set of compactly supported perturbations defined in (
1.2
) satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
2.5
, in order to apply it and prove Theorem
1.1
.

We will start by showing that the necessary condition for the existence of Reeb chords on
(H_{0}-h)^{-1}(0)
between any two cotangent fibres
T_{q_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
T_{q_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{2}
, assumption 1 of Theorem
2.5
, holds true in our setting.
Next, we will prove that the second assumption of Theorem
2.5
holds true in our setting, i.e., the critical set of the Lagrangian Rabinowitz action functional is bounded in
L^{\infty}
. It is possible to define the Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology without this assumption as a limit of homologies defined in an action window, but this is more involved and we postpone it to a future paper.

The most challenging part, which will occupy most of this section, is to prove that the Floer trajectories are uniformly bounded in the
L^{\infty}
-norm. This is essential in first defining the Lagrangian Rabinowitz homology and then constructing the isomorphism from Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H_{0}
be the Copernican Hamiltonian defined in (
1.1
) and let
\mathcal{H}
be the set of perturbations defined in (
1.2
). Then the set
(H_{0}-h)^{-1}(0)\cap T_{q}\mathbb{R}^{2}
is compact and nonempty for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
and every
q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

Fix
q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since
h
is constant outside a compact set,
we have

\lim_{|p|\to\infty}\left(H_{0}(q,p)-h(q,p)\right)=+\infty.

On the other hand, we have
h>0
by assumption and
H_{0}(q,0)=0
, hence

H_{0}(q,0)-h(q,0)=-h(q,0)<0.

Consequently, by the intermediate value theorem there exists
p\in T_{q}\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that
H_{0}(q,p)-h(q,p)=0
.
∎

The following lemma proves that the Copernican Hamiltonian
H_{0}
together with the set of compactly supported perturbations defined in (
1.2
) satisfies the second assumption of Theorem
2.5
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H_{0}:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Copernican Hamiltonian defined in (
1.1
). Fix two points
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\hskip-5.69046pt\smash{\raisebox{6.0pt}{ $(r,p_{r},\theta,p_{\theta})\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}_{+}\times T^{*}S^{1}$}}&p_{\theta}\leq r_{n}\sqrt{2m}.\end{array}\right\}\,.

Then for every

such that

We will use the Poisson bracket defined by
\{f,g\}=\omega(X_{f},X_{g})=dg(X_{f})

Using (
1.1

\{H_{0},r\}=p_{r},\quad\textrm{and}\quad\{H_{0},\{H_{0},r\}\}=\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}.

Let now

Arguing by contradiction, suppose that

Since

, we have

and the condition that

attains its maximum at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}r\circ v(t_{0})=\eta\{H_{0}-h,r\}\circ v(t_{0})=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{and}\quad\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}r\circ v(t_{0})=\eta^{2}\{H_{0}-h,\{H_{0}-h,r\}\}\circ v(t_{0})\leq 0.

The definition of

For the bounds on

. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\frac{p_{r}^{2}}{2}+\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{2r^{2}}+p_{\theta}

\displaystyle=h(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta}),

\displaystyle p_{r}^{2}+\left(\frac{p_{\theta}}{r}+r\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=r^{2}+2h\leq r_{n}^{2}+2m,

\displaystyle|p_{r}\circ v|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{r_{n}^{2}+2m},

\displaystyle|p_{\theta}\circ v|

\displaystyle\leq r_{n}\left(\sqrt{r_{n}^{2}+2m}-r_{n}\right)\leq r_{n}\sqrt{2m}.

∎

Before formulating the main theorem of this section we will first introduce the following notation.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
H_{0}
be the Hamiltonian defined in (

Let
\mathcal{V}\subseteq T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open, but precompact subset, such that

. Let

be a smooth homotopy of Hamiltonians

and
2
-parameter families of almost complex structures

constant in

[0,1]
, such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\frac{1}{c}+\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{3},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\textrm{where}\qquad c:=\inf_{s\in[0,1]}\{h_{s}-dh_{s}(p\partial_{p})\}.

Then for every pair

3.1.
The bounds on the action

If the family of Hamiltonians
\{H_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
is constant in
s
, i.e.
H_{s}=H
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
then
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)\neq\emptyset
implies
b>a
. However, since in the parametric case the action along the Floer trajectories may not be monotonically increasing, we need to ensure that we have a bound on how much the action can decrease along a Floer trajectory.
Proving the bounds on the action conditions will be the main aim of this section.

We will start by proving the following lemma:

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
h\in\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\textrm{if}\qquad\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}(v,\eta)\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}<1,

\displaystyle\textrm{then}\qquad|\eta|\leq\frac{1}{c}|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}(v,\eta)|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c}},

where
c:=\inf\{h-dh(p\partial_{p}\}>0
.

Proof.

Using (
1.1
) we can compute the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
satisfies the following equality:

(3.4)

dH_{0}(p\partial_{p})=p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+q_{2}p_{1}-q_{1}p_{2}=\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}+H_{0}(p,q).

Combining that with equality (
2.8
) for all
(q,p,\eta)=(v,\eta)\in\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}(v,\eta)-d\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}[p\partial_{p},0]

\displaystyle=\eta\int_{0}^{1}\left(d(H_{0}-h)(p\partial_{p})-H_{0}(v)+h(v)\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\eta\left(\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(h(v)-dh(p\partial p)\right)\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\geq\eta\left(\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{L^{2}}+c\right),

where the last inequality comes from the fact that

c:=\inf\{h-dh(p\partial_{p}\}>0
.

Consequently, for all
(q,p,\eta)=(v,\eta)\in\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
, such that
\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}(v,\eta)\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}<1
, we obtain

|\eta|<\frac{|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}(v,\eta)|+\|p\|_{L^{2}}}{\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c}.

Now let us analyze the right-hand side as functions of
\|p\|_{L^{2}}
:

\displaystyle\max_{x\geq 0}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+c}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{x}{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+c}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}{\left(\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+c\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle\max_{x\geq 0}\left(\frac{x}{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+c}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c}}.

These estimates give the desired inequality.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Consider the setting as in Theorem
3.3
. Fix
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}\circ u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|\nabla^{J_{s}}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}\circ u(s)\|_{L^{2}([0,1])\times\mathbb{R}}
are uniformly bounded for all
u\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
.

The following proof follows closely the arguments presented in
[
12
, Prop. 3.3]
. We will not present all of them here, but we encourage a curious reader to see the details in
[
12
]
.

Proof.

Using the fact that
u\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
is a Floer trajectory one can calculate the derivative of the action functional over
s
and obtain the following inequalities (see
[
12
, Prop. 3.3]
):

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{|a|,|b|\}+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{J_{s}}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}(b-a+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

In particular the convergence of the integral

\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])}\leq\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{J_{s}}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])},

implies that for small enough

\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u(s))\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}<1
.
This ensures that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
the following value
\tau_{0}(s)
is well defined and finite

\tau_{0}(s):=\sup\{\tau\leq s\ |\ \|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{\tau}}(u(\tau))\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}<1\}.

For
\tau\in[\tau_{0}(s),s]
we have
\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{\tau}}(u(\tau))\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}\geq 1
, which allows us to estimate (see
[
12
, Prop. 3.3]
):

(3.9)

\displaystyle|s-\tau_{0}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}(b-a+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}),

(3.10)

\displaystyle|\eta(s)-\eta(\tau_{0}(s))|

\displaystyle\leq\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}(b-a+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

Now using
the constant
c>0
as in (
3.3
) together with the
the estimates from Lemma
3.4
and (
3.7
) we can calculate:

\displaystyle|\eta(s)|

\displaystyle\leq|\eta(\tau_{0}(s))|+|\eta(s)-\eta(\tau_{0}(s))|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c}|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u(s))|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c}}+\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}(b-a+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}})

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\frac{1}{c}+\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{c}\max\{|a|,|b|\}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c}}+\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}(b-a).

Since this inequality has to hold for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
it also has to hold for
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Using (
3.2
) we obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{1}{c}\max\{|a|,|b|\}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c}}+\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}(b-a)}{1-\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\frac{1}{c}+\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{c}\max\{|a|,|b|\}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c}}+\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}(b-a)\right)=:\mathfrak{y},

Now using (
3.2
), (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}-c}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{|a|,|b|\}+\frac{c}{3}\mathfrak{y}=:\mathfrak{a},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{J_{s}}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}-c}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(b-a+\frac{c}{3}\mathfrak{y}\right)=:\mathfrak{e}.

∎

Having obtained the bounds on the action we are ready to prove the Novikov finiteness condition:

Lemma 3.6
.

Consider the setting as in Theorem
3.3
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(a,b)\neq\emptyset
implies

a\leq\max\left\{3b,3\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}\right\}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad b\geq\min\left\{3a,-3\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}\right\}.

Proof.

This proof follows arguments presented in
[
4
, Cor. 3.8]
. We will prove the first inequality since the second is completely analogous.

(3.14)

\displaystyle\textrm{First assume}\ \qquad 3\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq a\quad\textrm{and}\quad|b|\leq a.

\displaystyle\textrm{Then}\qquad\max\{|a|,|b|\}

\displaystyle=a\quad\textrm{and}\quad b-a\leq 0.

By (
3.11
) and (
3.14
) we get:

\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{a}{c}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c}}\right)=2\frac{a}{c}.

On the other hand, by (
3.2
) we have
\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{c}{3}
. This, together with (
3.8
), implies

b\geq a-\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq a-2\frac{a}{c}\cdot\frac{c}{3}=\frac{1}{3}a.

Now assume that
3\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}\leq a<|b|
. To finish the proof it suffices to exclude the case
a<-b
.

\textrm{Then}\qquad\max\{|a|,|b|\}=-b\quad\textrm{and}\quad b-a\leq 0.

By (
3.11
) and (
3.14
) we get:

\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{-b}{c}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c}}\right)\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{-b}{c}+\frac{a}{3c}\right)\leq-2\frac{b}{c}.

This, together with (
3.2
) and (
3.8
), gives us a contradiction:

\displaystyle b\geq a-\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq a+2\frac{b}{c}\cdot\frac{c}{3}=a+\frac{2}{3}b,

\displaystyle 0\geq\frac{1}{3}b\geq a\geq 3\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}>0.

∎

3.2.
The set of infinitesimally small action derivation

Let
H_{0}
be the Hamiltonian defined in (

) and let
\mathcal{H}
be the set of Hamiltonians as in (

{\smash{\raisebox{6.0pt}{ $(v,\eta)\in\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}$}}}&|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}(v,\eta)|\leq\mathfrak{a},\ |\eta|\leq\mathfrak{y}.\end{array}\right\}

The main aim of this subsection will be to prove the following lemma:

Proposition 3.7
.

For fixed constants
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y}>0

\mathcal{B}_{h}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
is bounded both in the
L^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm and in the
L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm.

We will prove Proposition
3.7
in a series of smaller lemmas:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}
and let
c:=\inf\{h-dh(p\partial_{p})\}>0
.
If we fix
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon>0
then for every
(q,p,\eta)\in\mathcal{B}_{h}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
we have:

|\eta|\|p\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\|\partial_{t}p\|_{L^{2}}\leq 3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}

Proof.

Using (
3.6
) for
(q,p,\eta)\in\mathcal{B}_{h}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
we obtain

\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c\right)\leq\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}(v,\eta)-d\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}[p\partial_{p},0]\leq\mathfrak{a}+\varepsilon\|p\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle|\eta|\|p\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{a}\|p\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c}.

Now we would like to estimate the maximum of the function on the right-hand side of the inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}x+\varepsilon x^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+c}\right)=-\frac{\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{a}x^{2}-2\varepsilon cx-\mathfrak{a}c}{\left(\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+c\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}x+\varepsilon x^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+c}\right)=0\quad\iff\quad x=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}}\left(2\varepsilon c\pm\sqrt{4\varepsilon^{2}c^{2}+2\mathfrak{a}^{2}c}\right),

\displaystyle\max_{x\geq 0}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}x+\varepsilon x^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+c}\right)=\varepsilon+\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{2}}{2c}+\varepsilon^{2}}\leq 2\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}.

This gives us the bound on
|\eta|\|p\|_{L^{2}}
, i.e. the first inequality.

Let us denote by

(X_{H_{0}-h})_{p}:=\left(p_{2}+\frac{\partial h}{\partial_{q_{1}}}\right)\partial_{p_{1}}-\left(p_{1}-\frac{\partial h}{\partial_{q_{2}}}\right)\partial_{p_{2}}.

Then we can estimate

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}p\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{t}p-\eta(X_{H_{0}-h})_{p}\|_{L^{2}}+|\eta|\|(X_{H_{0}-h})_{p}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H}(q,p,\eta)\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}+|\eta|(\|p\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}),

\displaystyle\leq 3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}.

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

If we fix
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon>0
then for every
(q,p,\eta)\in\mathcal{B}_{h}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
we have:

\|q\|_{L^{2}},\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\min\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|\}+2\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Proof.

Begin first observe that

qdq(X_{H_{0}})=q_{1}(p_{1}+q_{2})+q_{2}(p_{2}-q_{1})=q_{1}p_{1}+q_{2}p_{2}.

Therefore, for all
t\in[0,1]
we have

\displaystyle|q(t)|^{2}-|q_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}qdq\left(\partial_{t}v-\eta X_{H_{0}-h}\right)+2\eta\int_{0}^{t}\left(qdq(X_{H_{0}})-qdq(X_{h})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|q\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H}(q,p,\eta)\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}+2|\eta|\|q\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|p\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right),

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|q\|_{L^{2}}\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right),

where the last inequality we obtained using the result form Lemma
3.8
. Integrating both sides, we obtain

\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq|q_{0}|^{2}+2\|q\|_{L^{2}}\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

By solving this quadratic inequality we obtain the following bound:

\displaystyle\|q\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sqrt{\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{2}+|q_{0}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|q_{0}|+2\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

By repeating this procedure with the equation
|q(t)|^{2}=|q_{1}|^{2}-2\int_{t}^{1}q\partial_{t}q
, we obtain the bound on
\|q\|_{L^{2}}
we were looking for.

To obtain the bound for
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}
we will use (
3.16
) again:

\displaystyle\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{|q_{0}|^{2}+2\|q\|_{L^{2}}\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{|q_{0}|^{2}+2\left(|q_{0}|+2\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\right)\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq|q_{0}|+2\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

Analogously as before, we repeat this procedure with the equation
|q(t)|^{2}=|q_{1}|^{2}-2\int_{t}^{1}q\partial_{t}q
to obtain the bound on
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}
we were looking for.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

If we fix
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon>0
then for every
(q,p,\eta)\in\mathcal{B}_{h}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
we have:

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathfrak{q}+\sqrt{2\left(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+c+\varepsilon\right)}=:\mathfrak{p},

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{p}+\left(\varepsilon+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right),

\displaystyle\textrm{where}\qquad\mathfrak{q}

\displaystyle:=\min\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|\}+2\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Proof.

By (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(H_{0}-h)(q,p)dt-\int_{0}^{1}\left(p_{1}q_{2}-p_{2}q_{1}\right)dt+\int_{0}^{1}h(q,p)dt

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h-c}\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}+\|p\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}+\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+c

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\mathfrak{q}\|p\|_{L^{2}}+\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+c

where
\mathfrak{q}:=\min\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|\}+2\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)
as in Lemma
3.9
. By solving this quadratic inequality we obtain the following bound:

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{q}+\sqrt{\mathfrak{q}^{2}+2\left(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+c+\varepsilon\right)}\leq 2\mathfrak{q}+\sqrt{2\left(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+c+\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle=2\min\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|\}+4\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{y}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)+\sqrt{2\left(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+c+\varepsilon\right)}.

To prove the second bound first observe that there exists
t_{0}\in[0,1]
, such that
|p(t_{0})|\leq 2\mathfrak{q}+\sqrt{2\left(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+c+\varepsilon\right)}
. Thus can estimate:

\displaystyle|p(t)|

\displaystyle\leq|p(t_{0})|+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}|\partial_{t}v|=|p(t_{0})|+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}|\partial_{t}v-X_{H_{0}-h}|+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}|X_{h}|

\displaystyle\leq|p(t_{0})|+\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h-c}\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq|p(t_{0})|+\varepsilon+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\min\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|\}+13\varepsilon+4\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+(4\mathfrak{y}+1)\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sqrt{2\left(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}+c+\varepsilon\right)}.

∎

Remark 3.11
.

Note that in the proof above we did not use the assumption that
dh\in C_{c}^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Thus we can conclude that for any
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{y}
,
\varepsilon>0
and any Hamiltonian
h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap W^{1,\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2})
, such that
h>0
and
h-dh(p\partial_{p})>0
the corresponding set
\mathcal{B}_{h}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
is bounded in the
L^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm and the
L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm.

Remark 3.12
.

Note that for
h\in\mathcal{H}
the bounds on
\mathcal{B}_{h}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
depend smoothly on the constants
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{y}
,
\varepsilon
,
c:=\inf\{h-dh(p\partial_{p})\}
and
\|h\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
. Thus if we take a subset
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
bounded in the
W^{1,\infty}
-norm such that
\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\inf\{h-dh)p\partial_{p})\}>0
, then the corresponding set

\bigcup_{h\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\mathcal{B}_{h}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)\subseteq\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R},

will be also bounded in the
L^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm and the
L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm.

3.3.
The
L^{2}
bounds

The aim of this subsection will be to prove the following Proposition:

Proposition 3.13
.

Consider a setting as in Theorem
3.3
. Then for every pair
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
the corresponding space
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
is bounded in the
L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
there exist
\mathfrak{a,e,y}>0
, such that for every
u:=(v,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
we have

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\mathfrak{y},\qquad\|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{a},

\displaystyle\textrm{and}\qquad\|\nabla^{J_{s}}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{e}.

Moreover, by (
3.13
) the convergence of the integral

\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])}^{2}\leq\mathfrak{e}\|J\|_{\infty}^{2},

implies that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
s\in\mathbb{R}
small enough such that
\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u(s))\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}<\varepsilon
. This ensures that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
the following value
\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)
is well defined and finite

\tau_{\varepsilon}(s):=\sup\left\{\tau\leq s\ \Big|\ u(\tau)\in\mathcal{B}_{h_{\tau}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)\right\}.

Note that for all
\tau\in[\tau_{\varepsilon},s]
we have
\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{\tau}-c}(u(\tau))\|_{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}\geq\varepsilon
, thus we can estimate

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}|s-\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)|\leq\|\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])}^{2}\leq\mathfrak{e}\|J\|_{\infty}^{2},

(3.17)

\displaystyle|s-\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)|\leq\frac{\mathfrak{e}\|J\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

On the other hand, we know that

\displaystyle c:=\inf_{s\in[0,1]}\{h_{s}-dh_{s}(p\partial_{p})\}>0,

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\|h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}<+\infty,\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\|\nabla h_{s}\|_{L^{2}}<+\infty,

therefore by Remark
3.12
we know that the set

(3.18)

\mathcal{B}^{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon):=\bigcup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathcal{B}_{h_{s}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)\subseteq\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R},

is bounded in
L^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}
- and
L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm. In fact, we can use the same bounds as in Lemmas
3.9
and
3.10
denoting

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\|h_{s}\|_{W^{1,\infty}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}

\displaystyle:=\min\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|\}+2\left(3\varepsilon+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\sqrt{2c}}+\mathfrak{yh}\right),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}

\displaystyle:=2\mathfrak{q}+\sqrt{2\left(\mathfrak{h}+c+\varepsilon\right)}.

Now using (
3.17
) together with Lemma
3.9
we obtain

\displaystyle|q(s,t)|

\displaystyle\leq|q(\tau_{\varepsilon}(s),t)|+\int_{\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)}^{s}|\partial_{s}q(\tau,t)|d\tau,

\displaystyle\leq|q(\tau_{\varepsilon}(s),t)|+\int_{\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)}^{s}|\partial_{s}v(\tau,t)|d\tau,

\displaystyle\|q(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|q\circ\tau_{0}(s)\|_{L^{2}}+\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|\int_{\tau_{0}(s)}^{s}\partial_{s}vd\tau\right|^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|q\circ\tau_{0}(s)\|_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{|s-\tau_{0}(s)|}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\tau_{0}(s)}^{s}\left|\partial_{s}v\right|^{2}d\tau dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\circ\tau_{0}(s)\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{\mathfrak{e}}\|J\|_{\infty}}{\varepsilon}\ \|\nabla^{J_{s}}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{q}+\frac{\mathfrak{e}\|J\|_{\infty}}{\varepsilon}.

Analogously, using Lemma
3.10
we obtain

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|p(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{p}+\frac{\mathfrak{e}\|J\|_{\infty}}{\varepsilon},

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|v(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{q}+\mathfrak{p}+\frac{\mathfrak{e}\|J\|_{\infty}}{\varepsilon}.

∎

3.4.
The maximum principle

In this section we will explain how to use Aleksandrov’s maximum principle to find
L^{\infty}
-bounds on the Floer trajectories outside
\mathcal{B}(a,\varepsilon)
, following the argument of Abbondandolo and Schwartz in
[
1
]
. The contents of this section are word to word from the last author’s paper with Pasquotto
[
12
]
, but we include it here for the completeness of the argument.

Theorem 3.14
.

(
Aleksandrov’s maximum principle
)

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
\rho:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a function in
C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{(}\overline{\Omega})
function satisfying the elliptic differential inequality

\triangle\rho+\langle h,\nabla\rho\rangle\geq f,

where
g\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
f\in L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then there exists
C>0
which depends only on
\operatorname{diam}\Omega
and
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, such that

\sup_{\Omega}\rho\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}\rho+C\left(\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|f^{-}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

provided
g
and the negative part of
f
are in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

In order to apply Aleksandrov’s maximum principle and find
L^{\infty}
bounds on the Floer trajectories, one first has to construct a function
F
with compact level sets, whose composition with a Floer trajectory
u=(v,\eta)\in\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfies the elliptic differential inequality

\triangle(F\circ v)+\langle g,\nabla(F\circ v)\rangle\geq f

outside of the set of infinitesimal action derivation
\mathcal{B}^{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
, i.e. on every connected component
\Omega
of

\Omega\subseteq\left(\mathbb{R}\setminus u^{-1}\left(\mathcal{B}^{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)\right)\right)\times[0,1].

Having such an inequality, one can apply the Aleksandrov maximum principle, which gives us

\sup_{\Omega}(F\circ v)\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}(F\circ v)+C(\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|f^{-}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

provided
g
and the negative part of
f
are in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

The core of this method is to find a function satisfying all the required properties. The classical approach is to use plurisubharmonic functions.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold and
J
a compatible almost complex structure. Then a
C^{2}
function
F:M\to\mathbb{R}
is called plurisubharmonic if

-dd^{\mathbb{C}}F=\omega,

where
d^{\mathbb{C}}F=dF\circ J
.

Remark 3.16
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold and let
J
be a compatible almost complex structure on
M
. Then a function
F:M\to\mathbb{R}
is plurisubharmonic if and only if its gradient with respect to
g
is a Liouville vector field.

The reason one uses plurisubharmonic functions is because their composition with a
2
-dimensional curve
v:\Omega\to M,\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies

-dd^{\mathbb{C}}(F\circ v)=\triangle(F\circ v)ds\wedge dt.

In particular, if
v
is a
J
-holomorphic curve then the elliptic inequality is trivially satisfied. Unfortunately, in the case of Floer trajectories proving the elliptic inequality is a little more complicated. One has to investigate how the plurisubharmonic function interacts with the Hamiltonian vector field, in particular one needs to understand the functions

dF(X_{H})
and
d^{\mathbb{C}}F(X_{H})
, which appear if we calculate
d^{\mathbb{C}}(F\circ u)
.

The proof of the following Lemma you can find in
[
12
]
as a part of the proof of Proposition 7.1:

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
H
be a Hamiltonian function on an exact symplectic manifold
(M,\omega=d\lambda)
and let
J
be a compatible almost complex structure on
M
. Let
u:\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\to M\times\mathbb{R}
be a Floer trajectory satisfying
\partial_{s}u=\nabla\mathcal{A}^{H}(u)
(with constant
H
and
J
). Then for a plurisubharmonic function
F:M\to\mathbb{R}
we have

(3.22)

\displaystyle\triangle(F\circ v)

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{s}v\|^{2}+\eta\left(dH+d(d^{\mathbb{C}}F(X_{H}))+d^{\mathbb{C}}(dF(X_{H}))\right)(\partial_{s}v)

\displaystyle+\eta^{2}d(dF(X_{H}))(X_{H})+\partial_{s}\eta\ d^{\mathbb{C}}F(X_{H}).

3.5.
The
L^{\infty}
bounds

In this subsection we will apply the Maximum Principle explained in the previous subsection to the setting of the Floer trajectories from
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
to establish the
L^{\infty}
-bounds.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
:

Let
\Gamma:={(h_{s},J_{s})}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth homotopy of Hamiltonians
h_{s}\in\mathcal{H}
and almost complex structures
J_{s}\in C^{\infty}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R},\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{J}))
satisfying (
3.2
). Here
\mathcal{V}\subseteq T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the open, but pre-compact subset defined in Theorem
3.3
with the property that for all
s\in[0,1]

\operatorname{supp}dh_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
and
J_{s}\big|_{T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{J}}\equiv\mathbb{J}
.

Let
c>0
be as in (
3.3
).
If we fix
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
then by Lemma
3.5
we know that there exists
\mathfrak{y,a,e}>0
, which depend only on
a,b,c
and
\|J\|_{\infty}
such that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\sup\left\{|\eta(s)|\ |\ (v,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b),\ s\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{y},

\displaystyle\sup\left\{|\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}-c}(u)|\ |\ u\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{a},

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\|\nabla^{J_{s}}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{s}-c}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])\times\mathbb{R}}\ |\ u\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{e}.

On the other hand, if we fix
\varepsilon>0
then by Proposition
3.7
and Remark
3.12
we know that the set
\mathcal{B}^{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
, defined in (
3.18
), is bounded in the
L^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}
norm and the bounds depend only on
a,b,\varepsilon
and
\Gamma
. Consequently there exists a compact subset
K_{\varepsilon}\subseteq T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

(3.24)

v([0,1])\subseteq K_{\varepsilon}\qquad\textrm{for all}\qquad(v,\eta)\in\mathcal{B}^{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon).

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\overline{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq K_{\varepsilon}
.

Let us fix a Floer trajectory
u=(v,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
and denote a connected component

(3.25)

\Omega\subseteq\left(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\right)\setminus v^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon}).

By assumption
\operatorname{supp}dh_{s}\subseteq\overline{V}\subseteq K_{\varepsilon}
, hence
dh_{s}\circ v|_{\Omega}\equiv 0
for all
s\in[0,1]
. This means that instead of
H-h_{s}
we can use the Hamiltonian
H_{0}-\mathbf{c}_{s}
for some positive function
\mathbf{c}_{s}>0

(s,t)\in\Omega

(v,\eta)

Naturally,

.

By the convergence of the integral

we know that
\lim_{s\to\pm\infty}u(s)\in\mathcal{B}^{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{y},\varepsilon)
. Therefore,

v(\partial\Omega)\subseteq K_{\varepsilon}\cup T^{*}_{q_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{2}\cup T^{*}_{q_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{2}

Consequently, we have
\sup_{\partial\Omega}|q(s,t)|\leq\sup_{K_{\varepsilon}}|q|
. Unfortunately, we do not have a uniform bound on
\sup_{\partial\Omega}|p|
. Therefore, we will need to treat the cases of the position and momenta coordinates separately. We introduce the following functions on

(3.27)

F_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}|q|^{2}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad F_{2}:=\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2},

Both of the functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}

3.16

In Lemma
3.18
we will show that on
\Omega

F_{2}\circ v

\displaystyle\triangle(F_{1}\circ v)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}F_{1}\circ v+\partial_{s}\eta\left(F_{2}\circ v-H_{0}\circ v\right)=:f_{1}(s,t),

\displaystyle\triangle(F_{2}\circ v)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}F_{1}\circ v-\partial_{s}\eta\left(F_{2}\circ v+H_{0}\circ v\right)=:f_{2}(s,t).

In Lemma
3.22
and Lemma
3.24
we will prove that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, respectively, are bounded in the
W^{1,1}
-norm on
\Omega

and the bounds depend only on
a,b,c,\varepsilon
and
\Gamma
, but not on our choice of the Floer trajectory
u\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
or our choice of the connected component
\Omega\subseteq\left(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\right)\setminus v^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})
. By the continuity of the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,1}\hookrightarrow L^{2}
we obtain
L^{2}
-bounds on
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
.

Since we know that
\sup_{\partial\Omega}|q(s,t)|\leq\sup_{K_{\varepsilon}}|q|
we can now apply the Alexandrov maximum principle to obtain

\sup_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]}F_{1}\circ v\leq\sup_{K_{\varepsilon}}f_{1}\circ v+C\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and the right-hand side of the inequality does not depend on our choice of the Floer trajectory
u\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
or our choice of the connected component
\Omega\subseteq\left(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\right)\setminus v^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})
. In other words, we obtain that the set

\{\pi\circ v\ |\ u=(v,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)\},

is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Now we would like to establish the
L^{\infty}
-bounds on the
p
-variable of the Floer trajectories. To establish uniform bounds on
F_{2}\circ v
for the Floer trajectory
u=(v,\eta)
we extend the domain of the Floer trajectory
v:\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}
to a cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\left([-1,1]/-1\sim 1\right)
in the following way

(3.28)

\bar{v}(s,t):=\begin{cases}v(s,-t)&\textrm{for}\quad t\leq 0,\\
v(s,t)&\textrm{for}\quad t\geq 0.\end{cases}

If we extend the domain
\Omega
to the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\left([-1,1]/-1\sim 1\right)
in the following way

(3.29)

\displaystyle\Theta:=\Omega\cup\{(s,-t)\ |\ (s,t)\in\Omega\},

\displaystyle then\qquad

\displaystyle\bar{v}(\partial\Theta)\subseteq K_{\varepsilon}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad v(\Omega)=\bar{v}(\Theta).

In particular, we have that
\sup_{\Omega}f\circ v=\sup_{\Theta}f\circ\bar{v}
and
\|f\circ\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Theta)}=2\|f\circ v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for any smooth function
f\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

After proving in Lemma
3.19
that
F_{2}\circ\bar{v}
is in
C^{2}(\Theta)
we can apply the Aleksandrov maximum principle and obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]}F_{2}\circ v

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\partial\Theta}F_{2}\circ\bar{v}+C\|f_{2}\circ\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Theta)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{K_{\varepsilon}}f_{2}\circ v+2C\|f_{2}\circ v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since the established bounds do not depend on the choice of
u\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
or the choice of the connected component
\Omega\subseteq\left(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\right)\setminus v^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})
, this concludes the proof that
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}(a,b)
is bounded in the
L^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}
-norm.

\square

Lemma 3.18
.

\displaystyle dF_{2}(X_{H_{0}})

\displaystyle=0,

(3.31)

\displaystyle d^{\mathbb{C}}F_{1}(X_{H_{0}})

\displaystyle=qdp(X_{H_{0}})=q_{1}p_{2}-q_{2}p_{1}=F_{2}-H_{0},

\displaystyle d^{\mathbb{C}}F_{2}(X_{H_{0}})

\displaystyle=-\left(|p|^{2}+q_{1}p_{2}-q_{2}p_{1}\right)=-F_{2}-H_{0},

\displaystyle d(dF_{1}(X_{H_{0}}))(X_{H_{0}})

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{s}v\|^{2}+\eta\left(dH_{0}+d(d^{\mathbb{C}}F_{2}(X_{H_{0}}))+d^{\mathbb{C}}(dF_{2}(X_{H_{0}}))\right)(\partial_{s}v)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{4}\eta^{2}|q|^{2}-\partial_{s}\eta\left(\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}+H_{0}\right)

Lemma 3.19
.

Consider the standard symplectic space
(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2},\omega_{0})
with the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
defined as in (
1.1
). Let
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}
,
u=(v,\eta)

defined in (
3.28
). Then the composition
F_{2}\circ\bar{v}
with the function
F_{2}:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}

.

Proof.

First, observe that
\bar{v}
is continuous. Moreover,
\bar{v}
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}\times\left((-1,0)\cup(0,1)\right)
. Therefore,
F_{2}\circ\bar{v}
is also everywhere continuous and smooth on
\mathbb{R}\times\left((-1,0)\cup(0,1)\right)
. What is left to check is that
F_{2}\circ\bar{v}
is

on
\mathbb{R}\times\{-1,0,1\}
.
Note that

\frac{d}{dt}(F_{2}\circ\bar{v})(s,t)=\begin{cases}dF_{2}(\partial_{t}v)(s,t)&\textrm{for}\quad t\geq 0,\\
-dF_{2}(\partial_{t}v)(s,-t)&\textrm{for}\quad t\leq 0.\end{cases}

Hence, the function

is

if and only if

(3.33)

dF_{2}(\partial_{t}v)(s,0)=dF_{2}(\partial_{t}v)(s,1)=0\qquad\forall\ s\in\mathbb{R}.

By assumption

v(s,0)\equiv q_{0}
and
v(s,1)\equiv q_{1}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}

(3.34)

dq_{i}(\partial_{s}v)(s,0)=dq_{i}(\partial_{s}v)(s,1)=0\qquad\textrm{for}\quad i=0,1\quad\textrm{and}\quad\forall\ s\in\mathbb{R}.

Now using the Floer equation (
3.26
) for the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
and the relation (
3.30
) we can calculate for

\displaystyle dF_{2}(\partial_{t}v)

\displaystyle=d^{\mathbb{C}}F_{2}(\partial_{s}v)+\eta dF_{2}(X_{H_{0}})=-pdq(\partial_{s}v).

Plugging (
3.34
) into the equation above we obtain (
3.33
), which proves that

is
C^{1}
everywhere.

To prove that
F_{2}\circ\bar{v}
is
C^{2}
we first calculate:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}F_{2}\circ v

\displaystyle=D^{2}F_{2}(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}v)+dF_{2}(\partial_{tt}v)=\eta^{2}D^{2}F_{2}(X_{H_{0}},X_{H_{0}})+dF_{2}(\partial_{tt}v)

Now

\mathbb{R}\times([-1,1]/-1\sim 1)

Finally, we observe that

as it does not depend on the sign of the

Having proven the

4.
Positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.2
. We will start by showing that the positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz homology of the Copernican Hamiltonian is well-defined and invariant of perturbations. Further on we will prove that for fixed endpoints of the chords and high enough energy, the Copernican Hamiltonian has exactly one Reeb chord with positive action. Consequently, the corresponding positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology has only one generator. Finally, we will show that the generator’s Maslov index is in fact
0
.

4.1.
Definition and invariance of perturbations

First, we will prove that if the perturbation is small enough then there exists a homotopy, such that any associated Floer trajectory starting at a critical point with a positive action has to end at a critical point with positive action.

Now suppose
b<-a\leq-\delta(h_{0})
. Then
b-a\leq 0
and by (
3.11
) we have

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{2}{c(h_{0})}\max\{|a|,|b|\}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{c(h_{0})}}+\|J\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}(b-a)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{c(h_{0})}\left(\frac{\sqrt{c(h_{0})}}{2}-b\right)<\frac{-3b}{c(h_{0})}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{c(h_{0})}}{2\delta(h_{0})}\right)

Combining it with (
3.8
) and (
4.1
), we obtain the following inequality:

a\leq b+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq b\left(1-\frac{3b}{c(h_{0})}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{c(h_{0})}}{2\delta(h_{0})}\right)\|\partial_{s}h_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}b<-\frac{1}{2}a,

which contradicts the assumption
a\geq\delta(h_{0})>0
. That excludes the case
b<-a
and proves the lemma.
∎

We are now ready to prove the first part of Theorem
1.2
, i.e.
to prove that the positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz homology of the Copernican Hamiltonian is well-defined and invariant under compact perturbations.
We will do that by showing that all conditions of Corollary
2.8
are satisfied in our setting.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
H_{0}
be the Copernican Hamiltonian defined in (
1.1
) and let
\mathcal{H}
be the corresponding set of compact perturbations as in (
1.2
). Then for all
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and all

the positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology
{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
is well defined and isomorphic to
{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
for any
c>0
.

Proof.

Fix two points
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

and let
\{K_{n,m}\}_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequenceof compact sets
as in Lemma
3.2
satisfying (
3.52
).
Fix
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
and denote
c(h_{0}):=\inf(h_{0}-dh_{0}(p\partial_{p}))>0
.
Let
K_{n,m}\subseteq T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}
be any compact set from the sequence, such that
\operatorname{supp}dh_{0}\subseteq K_{n,m}
and
\|h_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<m
. By Lemma
2.7
and
3.2
there exists an open neighbourhood
\mathcal{O}_{h_{0}}(K_{n,m})
of
0
in

, such that

(1)

\{h_{0}+h\ |\ h\in\mathcal{O}_{h_{0}}(K_{n,m})\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
;

(2)

The critical set of
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{0}}
is continuously compact in
(K_{n,m},\mathcal{O}_{h_{0}}(K_{n,m}))
;

(3)

For all

\inf((h_{0}+h)-d(h_{0}+h)(p\partial_{p}))>\frac{1}{2}c(h_{0})
;

(4)

There exists

, such that for all

(5)

For all

\|h_{0}-h\|_{L^{\infty}}<\frac{1}{2}c(h_{0})\inf\left\{\frac{1}{13},\frac{1}{6}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{c(h_{0})}}{2\delta(h_{0})}\right)^{-1}\right\}.

h\in\mathcal{O}_{h_{0}}(K_{n,m})
the positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology
{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})

{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h_{0}}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
.

Fix
h_{1}\in\{h_{0}+h\ |\ h\in\mathcal{O}_{h_{0}}(K_{n,m})\}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\operatorname{reg}}
. Let
J_{0}\in\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h_{0}}
and
J_{1}\in\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{reg}}_{h_{1}}
will be two almost complex structures close enough, so that there exists a smooth homotopy
\{J_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
with

3.53

).

-norm. Finally, by Lemma
4.1

. This means that for every

. Hence for every

.

.
Since

is path connected and the positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology is constant on every open set

∎

4.2.
The critical set of the Copernican Hamiltonian

In this subsection we will analyse the Reeb chords corresponding to the Copernican Hamiltonian
H_{0}
defined in (
1.1
).
More precisely, we will show that the positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz action functional
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}

has an odd number of Reeb chords with positive
\eta>0
under specific conditions relating the energy level set of the Hamiltonian
H_{0}^{-1}(c)>0
and the endpoints of Reeb chords
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
H_{0}
be the Hamiltonian defined in (
1.1
) and let
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}:\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Lagranian Rabinowitz action functional corresponding to the energy
c>0
and a pair
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4},q_{0}\neq q_{1}
.
Then
\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
has the following properties:

(1)

If
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
and
|q_{0}||q_{1}|\leq 2c
then
\#\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}=\#\operatorname{Crit}^{-}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}=1
.

(2)

For a fixed
c>0
there exists a residual set
\mathcal{Q}^{c}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\Delta
, where
\Delta:=\left\{(q,q)\ |\ q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\right\}
, such that for all pairs
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathcal{Q}^{c}
both
\#\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
and
\#\operatorname{Crit}^{-}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
are odd numbers.

(3)

For a fixed pair
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\ q_{0}\neq q_{1}
there exists a residual set
\mathcal{I}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
, such that for all
c\in\mathcal{I}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
both
\#\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
and
\#\operatorname{Crit}^{-}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
are odd numbers.

Proof.

For clarity of the argument, we will divide the proof of this proposition in a sequence of lemmas. First recall that by equation (
2.9
) we have

\operatorname{Crit}^{\pm}\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}=\left\{(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\ |\ \pm\eta>0\right\}.

In Lemma
4.4
we will show that for each
c>0
and each pair
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
there exists a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, such that
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
if and only if
f(\eta)=0
. We also show that for
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
the critical set of
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is discrete. Denote by
Z(f)
the set of roots of the function
f
,
Z(f):=\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}|\ f(\eta)=0\}
.
In Lemma
4.5
we will consider pairs
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
, such that
|q_{0}||q_{1}|\leq 2c
and show that for
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
the corresponding function
f
satisfies
\#\{\eta>0\ |\ f(\eta)=0\}=\#\{\eta<0\ |\ f(\eta)=0\}=1
, whereas for
q_{0}=q_{1}
we have
Z(f)=\{0\}
.

Denote
\Delta:=\{(q,q)\ |\ q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{4}
. In Lemma
4.6
we prove that for a fixed
c>0
there exists a residual set
\mathcal{Q}^{c}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\Delta
, such that for all pairs
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathcal{Q}^{c}
the corresponding function
f
satisfies:

(4.3)

f^{\prime}(\eta)\neq 0\qquad\forall\ \eta\in Z(f),\qquad\textrm{where}\quad Z(f):=\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}\ |\ f(\eta)=0\}.

In Lemma
4.7
we show that for a fixed pair
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4},q_{0}\neq q_{1}
there exists a residual set
\mathcal{I}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
, such that for all
c\in\mathcal{I}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
the corresponding function
f
satisfies property (
4.3
).

In Lemma
4.8
we prove that if
c>0
and
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4},q_{0}\neq q_{1}
are such that the corresponding function
f
satisfies (
4.3
), then
f
has an odd number of roots on each positive and negative half line. This together with the bijection established in Lemma
4.4
concludes the proof.
∎

Before we prove the first lemma we will start by recalling the properties of the group of matrices corresponding to rotations: if we define

(4.4)

R(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\cos(t)&\sin(t)\\
-\sin(t)&\cos(t)\\
\end{array}\right).

then
\{R(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
form a group with the following properties:

\displaystyle R(0)=\operatorname{Id}\quad\textrm{and}\quad R\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}0&1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right),

(4.7)

\displaystyle R(t)R(\tau)=R(t+\tau)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ t,\tau\in\mathbb{R},

(4.8)

\displaystyle R^{-1}(t)=R^{T}(t)=R(-t)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle R^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}-\sin(t)&\cos(t)\\
-\cos(t)&-\sin(t)\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}0&1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\cos(t)&\sin(t)\\
-\sin(t)&\cos(t)\\
\end{array}\right)

(4.15)

\displaystyle=R\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)R(t)=R\left(t+\frac{\pi}{2}\right).

In the following lemma we will show that the critical set of
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
consists of isolated points and, in case
q_{0}=q_{1}
it is a circle.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
H_{0}
be the Hamiltonian defined in (
1.1
). For every
c>0
and every pair
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we define

(4.16)

f(\eta):=-c\eta^{2}+\eta q_{1}^{T}R\left(\eta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)q_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\left(|q_{0}|^{2}+|q_{1}|^{2}\right)-q_{1}^{T}R(\eta)q_{0}.

If we denote

(4.17)

Z(f):=\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}\ |\ f(\eta)=0\},

then the map
Z(f)\setminus\{0\}\ni\eta\longmapsto(v_{\eta},\eta),

\textrm{with}\qquad v_{\eta}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}(1-t)R(t\eta)&tR(\eta(t-1))\\
-\frac{1}{\eta}R(t\eta)&\frac{1}{\eta}R(\eta(t-1))\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right).

defines a bijection between the set
Z(f)\setminus\{0\}
and
\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\setminus(\{0\}\times\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
.

Proof.

By definition a pair
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
if and only if it satisfies the following three conditions:

(1)

The curve
v
starts in the Lagrangian
T^{*}_{q_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{2}
and ends in
T^{*}_{q_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{2}
;

(2)

The curve
v
is a Reeb chord and
\eta
is its period;

(3)

The curve
v
lies on the level set
H_{0}^{-1}(c)
.

Note that if
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
we have
\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\cap\left(\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\{0\}\right)=\emptyset
. In case
q_{0}=q_{1}
we always have a submanifold of constant solutions

\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\cap\left(\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{0}}\times\{0\}\right)=\{q_{0}\}\times\{p_{0}\in T^{*}_{q_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ H_{0}(q_{0},p_{0})=c\}.

One can easily calculate that for a fixed
q_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
the set
\{p\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ H_{0}(q_{0},p)=c\}
is a circle with origin at
-\mathbb{J}q_{0}
and radius
\sqrt{|q_{0}|^{2}+2c}
:

H_{0}(q,p)=c\quad\iff\quad|p+\mathbb{J}q_{0}|^{2}=2c+|q_{0}|^{2}.

From now on we will assume
\eta\neq 0
.

Using (
1.1
) and the notation from (
4.4
) we can express
H_{0}
and
X_{H_{0}}
in the following way:

\displaystyle H_{0}(q,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(q,p)A(q,p)^{T},

\displaystyle X_{H_{0}}(q,p)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{J}A(q,p)^{T}

\displaystyle\textrm{where}\qquad A

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}0&-R\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\\
R\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)&\operatorname{Id}\end{array}\right).

In particular, the flow
\varphi^{t}
of the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H_{0}}
can be easily calculated to be

(4.20)

\varphi^{t}(q,p)=\operatorname{Exp}(t\mathbb{J}A)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q\\
p\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}R(t)&tR(t)\\
0&R(t)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q\\
p\end{array}\right)

By definition, a critical point
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
satisfies
\varphi^{\eta}\circ v(0)=v(1)
. Moreover, we have
\pi\circ v(0)=q_{0}
and
\pi\circ v(1)=q_{1}
.
If we denote
v(0)=(q_{0},p_{0})
and
v(1)=(q_{1},p_{1})
and
combine it with the matrix formula for the Hamiltonian flow, we obtain

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{1}\\
p_{1}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}R(\eta)&\eta R(\eta)\\
0&R(\eta)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
p_{0}\end{array}\right).

Assuming, that
\eta\neq 0
we can use (
4.8
) to solve for
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
:

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}p_{0}\\
p_{1}\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}-\operatorname{Id}&R(-\eta)\\
-R(\eta)&\operatorname{Id}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right).

Using the equation above we obtain the following formula for
v(0)
:

(4.21)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
p_{0}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\operatorname{Id}&0\\
-\frac{1}{\eta}&\frac{1}{\eta}R(-\eta)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right)

Now, by combining (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) we can express a Reeb chord
v
in terms of
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
in the following way:

\displaystyle v_{\eta}(t)

\displaystyle:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}R(t\eta)&t\eta R(t\eta)\\
0&R(t\eta)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\operatorname{Id}&0\\
-\frac{1}{\eta}&\frac{1}{\eta}R(-\eta)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}(1-t)R(t\eta)&tR(\eta(t-1))\\
-\frac{1}{\eta}R(t\eta)&\frac{1}{\eta}R(\eta(t-1))\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right).

Such defined
v_{\eta}
is a solution of the Hamiltonian flow and it belongs to
\mathscr{H}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
.
To assure that
v_{\eta}(t)\in H_{0}^{-1}(c)
for all
t\in[0,1]
we use (
4.7
), (
4.8
), (
4.2
) and (
4.21
) to express the condition
(q_{0},p_{0})\in H_{0}^{-1}(c)
in the following way:

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(q_{0},\ p_{0}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\operatorname{Id}&-\frac{1}{\eta}\\
0&\frac{1}{\eta}R(\eta)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}0&-R\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\\
R\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)&\operatorname{Id}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\operatorname{Id}&0\\
-\frac{1}{\eta}&\frac{1}{\eta}R(-\eta)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(q_{0},\ p_{0}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}\operatorname{Id}&-R(-\eta)\\
-R(\eta)&\operatorname{Id}\end{array}\right)+\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}0&-R\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\eta\right)\\
R\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\eta\right)&0\end{array}\right)\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(|q_{0}|^{2}+|q_{1}|^{2}\right)-q_{1}R(\eta)q_{0}^{T}\right)+\frac{1}{\eta}q_{1}R\left(\eta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)q_{0}^{T}.

Now if we define
f\in C^{\infty}(R)
as in (
4.16
)
then
(v_{\eta},\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
if and only if
f(\eta)=0
.

Observe that

f(0)=\frac{1}{2}|q_{1}-q_{0}|^{2}
, thus
0\in Z(f)
if and only if
q_{0}=q_{1}
. In that case,
\left(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\right)^{-1}(0)
is a circle.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

In case
|q_{0}||q_{1}|\leq 2c
the critical set of
\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
satisfies one of the following properties:

either

q_{0}\neq q_{1}
and then
\#\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}=\#\operatorname{Crit}^{-}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}=1
,

or

q_{0}=q_{1}
and then
\#\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}=\#\operatorname{Crit}^{-}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}=0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4
we know that we can restrict ourselves to the analysis of function
f
defined in (
4.16
).
Note that
f(0)=\frac{1}{2}|q_{1}-q_{0}|^{2}
, thus

\displaystyle\textrm{for}\quad q_{0}

\displaystyle\neq q_{1}\quad\textrm{we have}\quad f(0)>0,

\displaystyle\textrm{and for}\quad q_{0}

\displaystyle=q_{1}\quad\textrm{we have}\quad f(0)=0.

In both cases, we know that
\lim_{\eta\to\pm\infty}f(\eta)=-\infty
due to the dominance of the summand
-c\eta^{2}
for
|\eta|
large enough. In case
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
we can use the intermediate value theorem to prove that
f
has a root both on the positive and the negative half line. What is left to show is that
f
has only one root on each half-line.

Using (
4.15
) we can now calculate the derivative of
f
:

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\eta)

\displaystyle=-2c\eta+q_{1}^{T}\left(R\left(\eta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)-R^{\prime}\left(\eta\right)\right)q_{0}+\eta q_{1}^{T}R^{\prime}\left(\eta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)q_{0}

\displaystyle=-\eta(2c+q_{1}^{T}R(\eta)q_{0}).

Thus in case
|q_{0}||q_{1}|\leq 2c
we have

2c+q_{1}^{T}R(\eta)q_{0})\geq 2c-|q_{0}||q_{1}|\geq 0,

and consequently
f^{\prime}(\eta)\leq 0
for
\eta>0
and
f^{\prime}(\eta)\geq 0
for
\eta<0
. Moreover, in case
|q_{0}||q_{1}|=2c
we have
f^{\prime}(\eta)=0
if and only if
\eta=0
or
q_{1}=-\frac{2c}{|q_{1}|^{2}}R(\eta)q_{0}
. In particular, in case
|q_{0}||q_{1}|=2c
the critical points of
f
are isolated. Consequently,
f
obtains its maximum at
0
and is non-decreasing on the negative half line and non-increasing on the positive half line. This proves that in case
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
there exists only one root on each positive and negative half line. On the other hand, since
f
obtains its maximum at
0
, thus in case
q_{0}=q_{1}
we know that
f(\eta)<0
for all
\eta\neq 0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For a fixed
c>0
there exists a residual set
\mathcal{Q}^{c}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\Delta
, such that for all pairs
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathcal{Q}^{c}
the corresponding function
f
as defined in (
4.16
) satisfies property (
4.3
).

Proof.

To prove the statement of the lemma, we will first extend the function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
to
\bar{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{5})
by taking
\bar{f}(q_{0},q_{1},\eta):=f(\eta)
where
f
depends on
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
in the way described in (
4.16
).

Recall, that
f(0)=\frac{1}{2}|q_{1}-q_{0}|^{2}
, thus

\bar{f}^{-1}(0)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\{0\}\right)=\Delta\times\{0\}.

We will show now that
\bar{f}^{-1}(0)\setminus(\Delta\times\{0\})
is a smooth manifold. Using (
4.16
) we can calculate
D\bar{f}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}\bar{f}

\displaystyle=-\eta\left(2c+q_{1}^{T}R(\eta)q_{0}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{0}}\bar{f}

\displaystyle=\eta q_{1}^{T}R\left(\eta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)-q_{1}^{T}R(\eta)+q_{0},

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{1}}\bar{f}

\displaystyle=\eta q_{0}^{T}R\left(-\eta-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)-q_{0}^{T}R(-\eta)+q_{1}.

A straightforward computation shows that
\bar{f}
satisfies the following relation:

\bar{f}(q_{0},q_{1},\eta)=-c\eta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(q_{0}\partial_{q_{0}}\bar{f}+q_{1}\partial_{q_{1}}\bar{f}\right).

Consequently, for all
(q_{0},q_{1},\eta)\in\bar{f}^{-1}(0)
we have
|\eta||\partial_{q_{0}}\bar{f}||\partial_{q_{1}}\bar{f}|\neq 0
. In particular, for all
(q_{0},q_{1},\eta)\in\bar{f}^{-1}(0)\setminus(\Delta\times\{0\})
the derivative
D\bar{f}\neq 0
and thus, by the inverse function theorem,
\bar{f}^{-1}(0)\setminus(\Delta\times\{0\})
is a smooth manifold.

Note, that if we consider the function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
as in (
4.16
) corresponding to a fixed pair
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
, then

\left(\bar{f}^{-1}(0)\setminus\Delta\right)\cap\left(\{(q_{0},q_{1})\}\times\mathbb{R}\right)=Z(f)\setminus\{0\}.

Additionally,
f^{\prime}(\eta)=\partial_{\eta}\bar{f}(q_{0},q_{1},\eta)
for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, if we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{c}:=

\displaystyle\left\{(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\ |\ \forall\ \eta\in Z(f)\setminus\{0\}\quad f^{\prime}(\eta)\neq 0\right\},

\displaystyle\textrm{then}\qquad\mathcal{Q}^{c}=

\displaystyle\left\{(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\ |\ \forall\ (q_{0},q_{1},\eta)\in\bar{f}^{-1}(0)\setminus\Delta\quad\partial_{\eta}\bar{f}\neq 0\right\}.

Consequently, the set
\mathcal{Q}^{c}
satisfies the assumptions of the theorem. On the other hand, since
\bar{f}^{-1}(0)\setminus\Delta
is a smooth manifold, the set of regular values of the projection
P:\bar{f}^{-1}(0)\setminus\Delta\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
is in fact equal to
\mathcal{Q}^{c}
. By the Morse-Sard Theorem
\mathcal{Q}^{c}
is residual in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

For a fixed pair
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4},q_{0}\neq q_{1}
there exists a residual set
\mathcal{I}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
, such that for all
c\in\mathcal{I}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
the corresponding function
f
as defined in (
4.16
) satisfies property (
4.3
).

Proof.

In this proof we will follow the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
4.6
. First, we extend the function

as in (
4.16
) to a function
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by setting
\tilde{f}(c,\eta):=f(\eta)
. Second, we observe that
\partial_{c}\tilde{f}(c,\eta)=-\eta^{2}<0
for all

. By assumption we take

\displaystyle\left\{c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\ |\ \forall\ (c,\eta)\in\tilde{f}^{-1}(0)\quad\partial_{\eta}\tilde{f}\neq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\left\{c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\ |\ \forall\ \eta\in Z(f)\quad f^{\prime}\neq 0\right\}.

In this final lemma, we show how the condition (
4.3
) implies that the function
f
has an odd number of zeros on each the positive and the negative half line.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
c>0
and
(q_{0},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{4},q_{0}\neq q_{1}
are such that the corresponding function
f
satisfies (
4.3
), then
f
has an odd number of roots on each positive and negative half line.

Proof.

First we will show that if
t_{0},t_{1}\in\mathbb{R},t_{0}<t_{1}
are two roots of a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, such that
f^{\prime}(t_{0}),f^{\prime}(t_{1})\neq 0
and
f(t)\neq 0
for all

, then
f^{\prime}(t_{0})f^{\prime}(t_{1})<0
. Suppose the opposite is true and
f^{\prime}(t_{0})f^{\prime}(t_{1})>0
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
f^{\prime}(t_{0}),f^{\prime}(t_{1})>0
. Then there would exist
\delta>0
, such that for all
t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta),\ f(t)>0
and for all
t\in(t_{1}-\delta,t_{1}),\ f(t)<0
. Consequently, by the intermediate value theorem there would have to exist
t\in(t_{0}+\delta,t_{1}-\delta)
, such that
f(t)=0
. But that brings us a contradiction.

Let now
f
be the function defined in (
4.16
). We will show that the set of roots of
f
is bounded. Observe that
f
can be bounded from above by

f(\eta)\leq-c\eta^{2}+|\eta||q_{0}||q_{1}|+\frac{1}{2}(|q_{0}|+|q_{1}|)^{2}.

Consequently, we have

f(\eta)<0
for
\eta\in(-\infty,-\delta_{0})\cup(\delta_{0},+\infty)
, where

(4.22)

\delta_{0}:=\frac{1}{2c}\left(\sqrt{|q_{0}|^{2}|q_{1}|^{2}+2c(|q_{0}|+|q_{1}|)^{2}}+|q_{0}||q_{1}|\right).

In particular, the set of roots of
f
denoted by
Z(f)
is a subset of
[-\delta_{0},\delta_{0}]
. Since
Z(f)
is bounded and discrete the number
k:=\#\{\eta\in Z(f)\ |\ \eta>0\}\in\mathbb{N}
is well defined. Moreover,
we can enumerate the elements of the set
\{\eta\in Z(f)\ |\ \eta>0\}=\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, such that
\eta_{i}<\eta_{i+1}
for
i=1,\dots k-1
and

(4.23)

\displaystyle(\eta_{i},\eta_{i+1})\cap Z(f)

\displaystyle=\emptyset

\displaystyle\textrm{for}

\displaystyle i=1,\dots k-1,

\displaystyle(0,\eta_{1})\cap Z(f)

\displaystyle=\emptyset

\displaystyle\textrm{and}

\displaystyle(\eta_{k},+\infty)\cap Z(f)=\emptyset.

Our aim is to prove that
k
is an odd number.

We will show now that if
f
satisfies (
4.3
) then
f^{\prime}(\eta_{1})<0
. Recall that
f(0)=\frac{1}{2}|q_{1}-q_{0}|^{2}
, which in the case
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
means
f(0)>0
. Suppose now that
f^{\prime}(\eta_{1})>0
. That would imply that there exists
\delta>0
, such that
f(\eta)<f(\eta_{1})=0
for
\eta\in(\eta_{1}-\delta,\eta_{1})
. Consequently, by the intermediate value theorem, there would have to exist
\eta\in(0,\eta_{1}-\delta)
, such that
f(\eta)=0
. But that would contradict our assumption (
4.23
). Thus
f^{\prime}(\eta_{1})<0
.

The last step would be to show that if
f
satisfies (
4.3
) then
f^{\prime}(\eta_{k})<0
. Recall that
\lim_{\eta\to+\infty}f(\eta)=-\infty
.
Suppose now that
f^{\prime}(\eta_{k})>0
. That would imply that there exists
\delta>0
, such that for all
\eta\in(\eta_{k},\eta_{k}+\delta)
,
f(\eta)>0
. Consequently, by the intermediate value theorem, there would have to exist
\eta\in(\eta_{k}+\delta,+\infty)
, such that
f(\eta)=0
. But that contradicts assumption (
4.23
).

Let us recall what we have learned: if
f
satisfies (
4.3
) then
f^{\prime}(\eta_{1})<0
,
f^{\prime}(\eta_{i})
has an opposite sign than
f^{\prime}(\eta_{i+1})
for all
i=1,\dots k-1
and finally
f^{\prime}(\eta_{k})<0
. This implies that
k
is an odd number.

Using the same arguments we prove that
\#\{\eta\in Z(f)\ |\ \eta<0\}
is an odd number as well.

Proposition 4.9
.

For fixed

we have

\lim_{c\searrow 0}\#\left(\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{q_{0},q_{1}}^{H_{0}-c}\right)=+\infty.

Proof.

By Lemma 4.4 we know that for any fixed

and

we have
\#\left(\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{q_{0},q_{1}}^{H_{0}-c}\right)=\#\{\eta>0\ |\ f(\eta)=0\}
, where

is the function defined in (4.8) which depends on

,
q_{1}
, and

We express

and
q_{1}
in polar coordinates as

\displaystyle q_{0}

\displaystyle=r_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\cos(\alpha)\\
\sin(\alpha)\end{array}\right)=r_{0}R^{T}(\alpha)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle=r_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\cos(\alpha+\theta)\\

Plugging this into the function

\displaystyle f(\eta)

\displaystyle=-c\eta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{0}^{2}+r_{1}^{2}\right)+r_{0}r_{1}(1,0)\left(\eta R\left(\eta+\theta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)-R(\eta+\theta)\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\

Observe that

\displaystyle f(\eta)

\displaystyle=-c\eta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(r_{0}-r_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle\textrm{for}\qquad\eta=2\pi n-\theta,

\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\#\left(\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{q_{0},q_{1}}^{H_{0}-c}\right)

Lemma 4.10
.

For
c>0
define a Hamiltonian
H_{c}:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(4.24)

H_{\delta}(q,p):=\frac{|p|^{2}}{2}+\delta(p_{1}q_{2}-p_{2}q_{1}).

\varphi_{\delta}(q,p):=\left(\sqrt{\delta}q,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}p\right)

(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}_{q_{0},q_{1}}^{H_{\delta}-1}\qquad\iff\qquad\left(\varphi_{\delta}\circ v,\delta\eta\right)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}_{\varphi_{\delta}(q_{0}),\varphi_{\delta}(q_{1})}^{H_{0}-\frac{1}{\delta}}.

First observe that
\varphi
preserves the standard symplectic form, so it is in fact a symplectomorphism. Moreover, the Hamiltonians satisfy the following relation

H_{0}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)=\varphi_{\delta}(H_{\delta}^{-1}(1))\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad X_{H_{\delta}}=\delta D\varphi_{\delta}^{-1}X_{H_{0}}.

Now
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}_{q_{0},q_{1}}^{H_{\delta}-1}
if and only if

for

,

\frac{d}{dt}(\varphi_{\delta}\circ v)=D\varphi_{\delta}(\partial_{t}v)=\eta D\varphi_{\delta}(X_{H_{\delta}}(v))=\delta X_{H_{0}}(\varphi_{\delta}\circ v).

∎

Lemma 4.11

\lim_{\delta\to 0}(v_{\delta},\eta_{\delta})=(v_{0},\eta_{0})\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{\bullet}-1}_{q_{0},q_{1}}.

|\varphi_{\delta}(q_{0})||\varphi_{\delta}(q_{1})|=\delta|q_{0}||q_{1}|\leq\frac{2}{\delta}
we have
1=\#\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{\varphi_{\delta}(q_{0}),\varphi_{\delta}(q_{1})}^{H_{0}-\frac{1}{\delta}}=\#\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{q_{0},q_{1}}^{H_{\delta}-1}
. Consequently, for every
\delta<\sqrt{\frac{2}{|q_{0}||q_{1}|}}
(or in the case either
q_{0}
or
q_{1}
are zero for any
\delta>0
) there is a unique solution
(v_{\delta},\eta_{\delta})\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{q_{0},q_{1}}^{H_{\delta}-1}
.

Having established that
(v_{\delta},\eta_{\delta})\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{\delta}-1}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is uniquely defined for small enough
\delta
, we can use the function
f
from Lemma
4.4
to estimate
\eta_{\delta}
. We simply need to map
(c,q_{0},q_{1},\eta)\mapsto(\frac{1}{\delta},\sqrt{\delta}q_{0},\sqrt{\delta}q_{1},\delta\eta)
to obtain that
\eta_{\delta}
is the unique positive root of the function:

\displaystyle g_{\delta}(\eta)

\displaystyle:=-\eta^{2}+\delta\eta q_{1}^{T}R\left(\delta\eta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)q_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\left(|q_{0}|^{2}+|q_{1}|^{2}\right)-q_{1}^{T}R(\delta\eta)q_{0}

(4.25)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|q_{1}-q_{0}|^{2}-\eta^{2}+q_{1}^{T}\left(\operatorname{Id}-R(\delta\eta)-\delta\eta R^{\prime}(\delta\eta)\right)q_{0}

This way we know that
(v_{\delta},\eta_{\delta})\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{\delta}-1}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
if and only if
g_{\delta}(\eta_{\delta})=0
.

By (
4.25
) we can use the Taylor expansion to obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|q_{1}-q_{0}|^{2}-\eta^{2}(1+\delta^{2}|q_{0}||q_{1}|)\leq g_{\delta}(\eta)\leq\frac{1}{2}|q_{1}-q_{0}|^{2}-\eta^{2}(1-\delta^{2}|q_{0}||q_{1}|),

\displaystyle g_{\delta}(\eta_{\delta})=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{|q_{1}-q_{0}|}{\sqrt{2(1+\delta^{2}|q_{0}||q_{1}|)}}\leq\eta_{\delta}\leq\frac{|q_{1}-q_{0}|}{\sqrt{2(1-\delta^{2}|q_{0}||q_{1}|)}},

which directly gives us
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\eta_{\delta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|q_{1}-q_{0}|
.

To show the convergence of
v_{\delta}
we again use Lemma
4.4
to present the equation for
v_{\delta}
explicitly. By Lemma
4.10
is suffices to just use the mapping
(q_{0},q_{1},\eta)\mapsto(\sqrt{\delta}q_{0},\sqrt{\delta}q_{1},\delta\eta)
in the formula for
v_{\delta}
from Lemma
4.4
to obtain:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\delta}\circ v_{\delta}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}(1-t)R(t\delta\eta_{\delta})&tR(\delta\eta_{\delta}(t-1))\\
-\frac{1}{\delta\eta_{\delta}}R(t\delta\eta_{\delta})&\frac{1}{\delta\eta_{\delta}}R(\delta\eta_{\delta}(t-1))\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sqrt{\delta}q_{0}\\
\sqrt{\delta}q_{1}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle v_{\delta}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}(1-t)R(t\delta\eta_{\delta})&tR(\delta\eta_{\delta}(t-1))\\
-\frac{1}{\eta_{\delta}}R(t\delta\eta_{\delta})&\frac{1}{\eta_{\delta}}R(\delta\eta_{\delta}(t-1))\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right).

Consider the functions
t\mapsto R(t\delta\eta_{\delta})
and
t\mapsto R((1-t)\delta\eta_{\delta})
on the interval
[0,1]
. Since
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta\eta_{\delta}=0
, we have the uniform convergence
\lim_{\delta\to 0}R(t\delta\eta_{\delta})=\lim_{\delta\to 0}R((1-t)\delta\eta_{\delta})=\operatorname{Id}
. Consequently,

(4.26)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}v_{\delta}=v_{0}(t)\quad\textrm{with}\quad v_{0}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}(1-t)&t\\
-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{|q_{1}-q_{0}|}&\frac{\sqrt{2}}{|q_{1}-q_{0}|}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{0}\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right).

A straightforward calculation shows that
(v_{0},\eta_{0})
with
\eta_{0}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|q_{1}-q_{0}|
is the unique element of
\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{\bullet}-1}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

By Proposition
4.2
we know that for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
and all
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
the positive Lagrangian Rabinowitz Floer homology
{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-h}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
is well defined and isomorphic to
{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
for any
c>0
. On the other hand, by Proposition
4.3
we know that if
c\geq|q_{0}||q_{1}|/2
then
\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
has only one element. Consequently,
{\rm LRFH}_{*}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
has only one generator and its boundary operator is
0
. It remains to calculate the Maslov index of this generator.

Let
\{H_{\delta}\}_{\delta>0}
be the family of Hamiltonians defined in (
4.24
) and let
\varphi_{\delta}
be the family of symplectomorphisms defined by
\varphi_{\delta}(q,p):=(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}q,\sqrt{\delta}p)
.
By Lemma
4.10
, for every
c>0
we have

(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\qquad\iff\qquad(\varphi_{\frac{1}{c}}^{-1}\circ v,c\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{1/c}-1}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}q_{0},\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}q_{1}}.

Since the Maslov index is invariant under symplectomorphisms, we get

\mu^{\rm tr}\left(v\left(\frac{\cdot}{\eta}\right)\right)=\mu^{\rm tr}\left(\varphi_{\frac{1}{c}}^{-1}\circ v\left(\frac{\cdot}{c\eta}\right)\right).

Therefore, it suffices to calculate
\mu^{\rm tr}\left(v_{\delta}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\eta_{\delta}}\right)\right)
of the unique
(v_{\delta},\eta_{\delta})\in\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{\delta}-1}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
for
\delta>0
small enough.

Denote by
H_{\bullet}
the kinetic Hamiltonian
H_{\bullet}(q,p):=\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}
and let
(v_{0},\eta_{0})
be the unique element of
\operatorname{Crit}^{+}\mathcal{A}^{H_{\bullet}-1}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
as in (
4.26
).
By Lemma
4.11
we know that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}(v_{\delta},\eta_{\delta})=(v_{0},\eta_{0})
. Continuity of the Maslov index and Example
2.3
implies

\mu^{\rm tr}\left(v_{\delta}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\eta_{\delta}}\right)\right)=\mu^{\rm tr}\left(v_{0}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\eta_{0}}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{2}

for small enough
\delta>0
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

\square

5.
Non-compactly supported potential

The aim of this section will be to prove Proposition
1.3
. Let
H_{0}:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the quadratic Hamiltonian defined in (
1.1
) and let
V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the potential function as in Proposition
1.3
.

(5.1)

\textrm{Define}\qquad H:=H_{0}-V.

Then

(5.2)

\displaystyle X_{H}

\displaystyle=(p_{1}+q_{2})\partial_{q_{1}}+(p_{2}-q_{1})\partial_{q_{2}}+\left(p_{2}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial_{q_{1}}}\right)\partial_{p_{1}}-\left(p_{1}-\frac{\partial V}{\partial_{q_{2}}}\right)\partial_{p_{2}},

\displaystyle X_{H}

\displaystyle=p_{r}\partial_{r}+\left(1+\frac{p_{\theta}}{r^{2}}\right)\partial_{\theta}+\left(\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}\right)\partial_{p_{r}}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial\theta}\partial_{p_{\theta}}.

This first step to prove Proposition
1.3
is to show that for positive energy
c>0
all periodic orbits of
X_{H}
on
H^{-1}(c)
are contained in a compact subset of
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
H:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Hamiltonian defined in (
5.1
). Fix
c>0
and
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}:\mathscr{H_{1}}_{q_{0},q_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the corresponding action functional. Then the critical set
\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}
.

Proof.

By an argument similar to the one in the proof of Lemma
3.2
it suffices to show that the set

(5.3)

H^{-1}(c)\cap\{\{H,r\}=0\}\cap\{\{H,\{H,r\}\}\leq 0\}

is compact. By (
5.2
) we have that

\displaystyle\{H,r\}

\displaystyle=p_{r},

\displaystyle\{H,\{H,r\}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}.

For
(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta})\in H^{-1}(c)\cap\{\{H,r\}=0\}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{2r^{2}}+p_{\theta}=V(r,\theta)+c\geq c,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p_{\theta}}{r}+r\right)^{2}\geq r^{2}+2c,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{|p_{\theta}|}{r}\geq\sqrt{r^{2}+2c}-r.

Consequently, by our assumption on the potential function
V
, if
\alpha>2
and
a>0
then for
(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta})\in H^{-1}(c)\cap\{\{H,r\}=0\}
, such that

\displaystyle r\geq r_{1}:=\max\left\{r_{0},\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{c},\left(\frac{a\alpha}{c^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\textrm{we have}\quad\frac{|p_{\theta}|}{r}\geq\sqrt{r^{2}+2c}-r>\frac{c}{2r},

\displaystyle\{H,\{H,r\}\}\geq\frac{c^{2}}{4r^{3}}-\frac{a\alpha}{r^{\alpha+1}}>0.

On the other hand, if
\alpha=2
and
a\in\left(0,\frac{c^{2}}{4}\right)
then for
(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta})\in H^{-1}(c)\cap\{\{H,r\}=0\}
, such that

\displaystyle r\geq r_{1}:=\max\left\{r_{0},\sqrt{\frac{a}{c-\sqrt{2a}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\textrm{we have}\quad\frac{|p_{\theta}|}{r}\geq\sqrt{r^{2}+2c}-r>\frac{\sqrt{2a}}{r},

\displaystyle\{H,\{H,r\}\}\geq\frac{2a}{r^{3}}-\frac{2a}{r^{3}}>0.

Consequently, in both cases the set

H^{-1}(c)\cap\{\{H,r\}=0\}\cap\left\{\{H,\{H,r\}\}\leq 0\right\}

is bounded in
r
and for every
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
we have

\sup r\circ v\leq R_{0}:=\max\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|,r_{1}\}.

By an argument similar to the one presented in the Lemma
3.2
we obtain that
p_{r}\circ v
and
p_{\theta}\circ v
are also uniformly bounded in the following way:

\displaystyle|p_{r}|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{R_{0}^{2}+2(\sup_{r\leq R_{0}}V+c)},

\displaystyle|p_{\theta}|

\displaystyle\leq R_{0}\left(R_{0}+\sqrt{R_{0}^{2}+2(\sup_{r\leq R_{0}}V+c)}\right).

∎

Remark 5.2
.

Note that the assertions of Proposition
5.1
hold true also for the potential
V
satisfying
V(r,\theta)\leq\frac{a}{r^{2}}
and
\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}\geq-\frac{2a}{r^{3}}
for
r>r_{0}
and
a\in\left[\frac{c^{2}}{4},\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right)
. However, we were unable to prove Proposition
1.3
for this class of potentials and that is why we restrict ourselves to
a<\frac{c^{2}}{4}
.

Now we would like change the Hamiltonian
H
, by multiplying the potential function
V
with a compactly supported function
\varphi:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
, in the following way

(5.5)

H_{1}(q,p):=H_{0}(q,p)+\varphi(q,p)V(q).

The associated Hamiltonian vector field is

(5.6)

\displaystyle X_{H_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{r}-V\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p_{r}}\right)\partial_{r}+\left(1+\frac{p_{\theta}}{r^{2}}-V\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p_{\theta}}\right)\partial_{\theta}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\varphi\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+V\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial r}\right)\partial_{p_{r}}+\left(\varphi\frac{\partial V}{\partial\theta}+V\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\theta}\right)\partial_{p_{\theta}}.

If we choose
\varphi
, such that for all the Reeb chords
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
we would have
v([0,1])\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(1)
, then
\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})\subseteq\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{1}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
. In fact, we would like to choose
\varphi
, such that
\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{1}-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})=\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})
.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function, such that
-2<\chi^{\prime}<0
on
[0,1]
and

\displaystyle\chi(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{for}\quad x\leq 0,\\
0&\textrm{for}\quad x\geq 1.\end{cases}

Define

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(r)

\displaystyle:=\chi\left(\beta(r-R_{1})\right),

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta})

\displaystyle:=\chi(H_{0}(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta})-\sup V-c),

(5.7)

\displaystyle\varphi(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta})

\displaystyle:=\chi_{0}(r)\chi_{1}(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta}).

(5.8)

where

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\max\left\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|,r_{0},\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{c},\left(\frac{8a\alpha}{c^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-2}}\right\}&\textrm{for}\quad\alpha>2,\\
\max\left\{|q_{0}|,|q_{1}|,r_{0},\frac{c+2\sqrt{a}}{2\sqrt{c-2\sqrt{a}}}\right\}&\textrm{for}\quad\alpha=2.\end{cases}

(5.9)

and

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\frac{2-\alpha}{2R_{1}}&\textrm{for}\quad\alpha>2,\\
\frac{(c+2\sqrt{a})^{2}-16a}{8aR_{1}}&\textrm{for}\quad\alpha=2.\end{cases}

Note that in both for
\alpha>2
and for
\alpha=2
we have
\beta>0
. In the later case, it follows from the assumption that
a<\frac{c^{2}}{4}
.

This way we have

(5.10)

\chi_{0}(r)=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{for}\ r\leq R_{1},\\
0&\textrm{for}\ r\geq R_{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}.\end{cases}

We will show that for a function
\varphi
defined as in (
5.7
) we have

\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-\varphi V-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}})=\operatorname{Crit}(\mathcal{A}^{H_{0}-V-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}).

The first step would be to show that
\varphi
has compact support:

Lemma 5.3
.

The function
\varphi:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as in (
5.7
) has compact support.

Proof.

By (
5.7
) and (
5.10
) we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\varphi

\displaystyle\subseteq\{H_{0}\leq\sup V+c+1\}\cap\left\{r\leq R_{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\frac{1}{2}p_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p_{\theta}}{r}+r\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}r^{2}+\sup V+c+1\right\}\cap\left\{r\leq R_{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\frac{1}{2}p_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p_{\theta}}{r}+r\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(R_{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^{2}+\sup V+c+1\right\}\cap\left\{r\leq R_{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}\right\}.

Since both of the sets on the right-hand side are compact, their intersection is compact, so
\operatorname{supp}\varphi
is compact as a closed subset of a compact set.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a potential function as in Proposition
1.3
and let
\varphi
be the corresponding function defined in (
5.7
). Then
\varphi V+c\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

First observe that

\operatorname{supp}d(\varphi V)=\operatorname{supp}(Vd\varphi+\varphi dV)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\varphi,

hence
d(\varphi V)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2})
. On the other hand,
by definition:

\displaystyle\varphi(p,q)V(q)

\displaystyle+c-d(\varphi V)(p\partial_{p})=V(q)\chi_{0}(q)\left(\chi_{1}(q,p)-d\chi_{1}(p\partial_{p})\right)+c

\displaystyle=V(q)\chi_{0}(q)\left(\chi_{1}(q,p)-\chi^{\prime}(H_{0}(q,p)-\sup V-c)dH_{0}(p\partial_{p})\right)+c.

Since by assumption we the functions
V
,
\chi_{0}
,
\chi_{1}
and
-\chi^{\prime}
are non-negative it suffices to prove that
dH_{0}(p\partial_{p})\geq 0
on
\chi_{1}^{-1}((0,1))
. Note that on
\chi_{1}^{-1}((0,1))
we have
H_{0}>\sup V+c
. Combining that with (
3.4
) we can calculate that on
\chi_{1}^{-1}((0,1))
we have:

dH_{0}(p\partial_{p})=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|^{2}+H_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\sup V+c>0.

∎

Now we have proven that
\varphi
as in (
5.7
) is an eligible candidate, we will continue with the proof of Proposition
1.3
. However, we will first start with the proof of a series of lemmas:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
H
be the Hamiltonian defined in (
5.1
) and let
\varphi
be the function defined in (
5.7
).
Then

v([0,1])\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(1)\quad\textrm{for all}\quad(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}.

Proof.

By definition of
H
we have

H_{0}=H+V\leq H+\sup V,

so
H^{-1}(c)\subseteq H_{0}^{-1}((-\infty,\sup V+c])
. On the other hand,
by Proposition
5.1
for every
(v,\eta)\in\operatorname{Crit}\mathcal{A}^{H-c}_{q_{0},q_{1}}
we have

\displaystyle v([0,1])

\displaystyle\subseteq\{r\leq R_{0}\}\cap H^{-1}(c)\subseteq\{r\leq R_{1}\}\cap H_{0}^{-1}((-\infty,\sup V+c])

\displaystyle=\chi_{0}^{-1}(1)\cap\chi_{1}^{-1}(1)=\varphi^{-1}(1).

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
H_{1}
be the Hamiltonian defined in (
5.5
) and let
\varphi
be the function defined in (
5.7
). Then

H_{1}^{-1}(c)\cap\{\{H_{1},r\}=0\}\cap\{\{H_{1},\{H_{1},r\}\}\leq 0\}\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(1).

Proof.

First observe that
H_{1}\big|_{\varphi^{-1}(0)}=H_{0}
, so by (
3.1
) we have

H_{1}^{-1}(c)\cap\{\{H_{1},r\}=0\}\cap\{\{H_{1},\{H_{1},r\}\}\leq 0\}\cap\varphi^{-1}(0)=\emptyset.

On the other hand, if
x\in\chi_{1}^{-1}([0,1))
, then
H_{0}(x)>\sup V+c
and

H_{1}(x)=H_{0}(x)-\varphi(x)V(x)\geq H_{0}(x)-\sup V>c.

Hence
\chi_{1}^{-1}((0,1])\cap H_{1}^{-1}(c)=\emptyset
. Therefore we can restrict ourselves to the analysis of the set
\chi_{0}^{-1}((0,1))
, i.e. when
R_{1}<r<R_{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}
.

Let us calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial r}

\displaystyle=\beta\chi_{0}^{\prime}\chi_{1}-\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\theta}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p_{r}}

\displaystyle=p_{r}\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p_{\theta}}=\left(\frac{p_{\theta}}{r^{2}}+1\right)\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime}.

In particular, by (
5.2
) we have

\{H_{1},r\}=dr(X_{H_{1}})=p_{r}-V\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p_{r}}=p_{r}(1-V\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime}).

By assumption
V,\chi\geq 0
and
\chi^{\prime}\leq 0
, thus
\{H_{1},r\}=0
implies
p_{r}=0
.

Consider now
(r,\theta,p_{r},p_{\theta})\in H_{1}^{-1}(0)\cap\left(\{H_{1},r\}\right)^{-1}(0)
. Then

\displaystyle\{H_{1},\{H_{1},r\}\}

\displaystyle=\{H_{1},p_{r}\}-\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p_{r}}\{H_{1},V\}-V\left\{H_{1},\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p_{r}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\{H_{1},p_{r}\}-V\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial p_{r}^{2}}\{H_{1},p_{r}\}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\varphi\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+V\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial r}\right)\left(1-V\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial p_{r}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\chi_{0}\chi_{1}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+V\left(\beta\chi_{0}^{\prime}\chi_{1}-\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime}\right)\right)\left(1-\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime}V\right)

Again, since by assumption
V,\chi\geq 0
and
\chi^{\prime}\leq 0
, thus
1-\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime}V\geq 1
and
\{H_{1},\{H_{1},r\}\}
has the same sign as

(5.11)

\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\chi_{0}\chi_{1}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+V\left(\beta\chi_{0}^{\prime}\chi_{1}-\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime}\right)

Furthermore, since by assumption
V,\chi_{0},p_{\theta}\geq 0
and
\chi_{1}^{\prime}\leq 0
, consequently
-\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}\chi_{0}\chi_{1}^{\prime}V\geq 0
and we can estimate (
5.11
) from below by

(5.12)

\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\chi_{0}\chi_{1}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+V\beta\chi_{0}^{\prime}\chi_{1}.

We will show that (
5.12
) is positive for both cases
\alpha>2
and
\alpha=2
.

Case
\alpha>2
:

By (
5.8
) we have that
r>R_{1}\geq\left(\frac{8a\alpha}{c^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-2}}
, so
\frac{c^{2}}{4r^{3}}>\frac{2a\alpha}{r^{\alpha+1}}
. On the other hand,
r<R_{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}=R_{1}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-2}
. Combining that with the assumptions on the potential
V
and the fact that
-2\leq\chi^{\prime}\leq 0
and
0\leq\chi\leq 1
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\chi_{0}\chi_{1}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+\frac{\alpha-2}{2R_{1}}V\chi_{0}^{\prime}\chi_{1}

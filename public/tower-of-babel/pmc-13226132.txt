Disarming CAR-Tregs with a clinical-stage natural product to supercharge CAR-T therapy
Authors: Bowen Li, Haoran Xu, Wenjun Zhang, Jing Yang
Published: 2026

Main text

Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy has revolutionized treatment of relapsed/refractory B-cell malignancies, yet about 60% of patients eventually relapse or become resistant.1,2 A key mechanism underlying resistance is the emergence of CAR-expressing regulatory T cells (CAR-Tregs) within infused products. These CAR-Tregs can arise from transduction of natural Tregs or from conventional T cells that acquire a regulatory phenotype upon CAR engagement. Once present, they potently suppress effector CAR-T cells by secreting IL-10 and TGF-β, expressing CTLA-4 and CD25, and metabolically disrupting the tumor microenvironment.3,4,5 Good et al. and Haradhvala et al. independently demonstrated that higher frequencies of FOXP3+ CAR-Tregs correlate with poor clinical outcomes, and adding as few as 5% CAR-Tregs to conventional CAR-T cells suffices to cause relapse in xenograft models.3,4 However, selectively depleting CAR-Tregs without harming effector CAR-T cells remains a major unmet need.

In our study recently published in Nature Communications, we identified Timosaponin AIII (TAIII)—a steroidal saponin from the traditional Chinese herb Anemarrhena asphodeloides Bunge—as a potent and selective CAR-Treg-clearing agent.6 Through a high-throughput phenotypic screen of more than 3,000 compounds, TAIII emerged as a top candidate capable of reversing FOXP3-mediated immune suppression with minimal cellular toxicity. In CD19 CAR-T products, a clinically relevant dose of 1 μM TAIII lowered the proportion of FOXP3-positive CAR-Tregs by approximately 40% and robustly enhanced effector cytotoxicity, all without altering the baseline CD4-to-CD8 ratio or overall T cell viability.

Mechanistically, we synthesized a photoaffinity biotin-TAIII probe for proximity labeling followed by LC-MS, which identified the adenosine A2A receptor (A2AR) as the direct target. TAIII bound selectively to A2AR with a dissociation constant of approximately 700 nM. By competing with cholesterol at the cholesterol-sensitive motif within the A2AR transmembrane domain, TAIII acts as an allosteric inhibitor that strongly blocks cAMP production and CREB activation. Crucially, a targeted knockdown of A2AR by more than 70% effectively abolished TAIII’s ability to enhance cytotoxicity, confirming its precise on-target specificity.

We further validated the Treg-dependency of TAIII in vivo. In immunodeficient NSG mice lacking a functional adaptive immune system, TAIII monotherapy showed little to no antitumor efficacy against Raji xenografts, indicating that an intact T cell compartment is essential for its activity. Moreover, in the Nalm6-Luc model using Treg-depleted CAR-T cells, TAIII completely failed to enhance tumor clearance or improve survival. Together, these results demonstrate that the therapeutic benefit of TAIII strictly depends on the presence of Tregs, reinforcing its selectivity for the A2AR-Treg axis rather than direct stimulation of effector T cells.

In co-culture assays, TAIII significantly boosted CAR-T killing of Raji and Nalm6 cells and increased IFN-γ, IL-2 and TNF-α secretion by 2- to 3-fold. Real-time monitoring revealed TAIII accelerated tumor apoptosis within 4 h and expanded the central memory T cell (Tcm) subset by approximately 2-fold. In a Raji-Luc xenograft model, CAR-T + TAIII induced rapid clearance with no relapse, translating to long-term survival, whereas CAR-T cells alone failed. Flow cytometry showed TAIII increased Tcm frequency while reducing CAR-Tregs by >50%. Similar results occurred in Nalm6-Luc and MC38-mCD19 solid tumor models. Remarkably, ex vivo TAIII pretreatment before infusion was sufficient to accelerate tumor eradication. In patient-derived CAR-T products from eight individuals failing therapy, TAIII restored killing activity and significantly reduced FOXP3 expression. TAIII also synergized with anti-PD-1 in MC38 tumors, decreasing intratumoral FOXP3+ Tregs and increasing CD8+ T cell infiltration, effectively switching the tumor microenvironment from “cold” to “hot.”

Our discovery of TAIII as a CAR-Treg-clearing agent builds upon our group’s systematic effort to reprogram immune cells by targeting protein stability. Recognizing that nuclear transcription factors like FOXP3 and RORγt are “undruggable” to conventional inhibitors, we pioneered a strategy to modulate their protein stability. In 2014–2015, we first identified USP17 and USP4 as deubiquitinases that stabilize RORγt and promote pathogenic Th17 cell function. We further discovered that Vialinin A, a natural terphenyl compound, acts as a first-in-class Th17-depleting agents by suppressing USP4 enzymatic activity, thereby ameliorating autoimmune responses in rheumatoid arthritis models.7,8,9 Extending this concept to Tregs, we performed tandem affinity purification-mass spectrometry and identified USP44 as a critical stabilizer of FOXP3. Conditional knockout of USP44 in regulatory T cells (Tregs), particularly in the induced Treg (iTreg) subset, enhanced anti-tumor immunity across multiple models and ameliorated colitis in preclinical settings, confirming that genetic destabilization of FOXP3 can remodel the tumor microenvironment.10 Building on this, we identified 10-hydroxycamptothecin (10-HCPT) as a candidate USP44 inhibitor with potential for effective Treg depletion (unpublished data). Based on this mechanistic insight, we established a phenotypic Treg functional screen, which led to the identification of TAIII as a clinical-stage natural product that effectively eliminates Tregs from CAR-T products without compromising effector cell function. Compared with existing Treg depletion strategies such as physical sorting or antibody-based removal, TAIII achieves only partial Treg reduction, which is sufficient to restore CAR-T efficacy while preserving overall immune homeostasis and avoiding the risk of complete Treg ablation. Unlike genetic ablation of A2AR or FOXP3, which causes permanent and irreversible alterations, TAIII offers a reversible pharmacological modulation, allowing flexible dosing and fine-tuning of immune responses. Moreover, in contrast to synthetic A2AR antagonists such as AZD4635, TAIII acts as an allosteric inhibitor and simultaneously modulates cholesterol metabolism—a pathway critically involved in A2AR activation—thereby providing a broader and more physiologically integrated mechanism of action. Importantly, TAIII has already completed a Phase II clinical safety and efficacy trial (KCT0002223) as an anti-wrinkle agent, which greatly facilitates its clinical translation for immuno-oncology applications. More recently, we extended the “barrier-disruption” strategy to TP53-mutated DLBCL and solid tumors by targeting the DDR1 (discoidin domain receptor 1) pathway. To this end, we developed DDR1 degradation strategies that dismantle the immune barrier, enhance CAR-T infiltration (unpublished data), sensitize tumor cells to cytotoxicity, and overcome immunotherapy resistance.11 Collectively, these works establish a translational platform for immune cell reprogramming via targeted protein stability control, providing a framework for iterative therapeutic development to dismantle immunosuppressive barriers and potentiate CAR-T therapy (Figure 1).

Schematic overview of our group’s systematic strategy for immune cell reprogramming via targeted protein stability control

The concept encompasses four interconnected approaches: (1) elimination of pro-inflammatory cells (e.g., via USP4/USP17 inhibition) for autoimmune disease therapy; (2) first-generation Treg-depleting strategies (e.g., USP44 targeting); (3) second-generation Treg-depleting agents represented by TAIII, a clinical-stage allosteric A2AR inhibitor that selectively clears CAR-Tregs and potentiates CAR-T therapy; and (4) extending barrier disruption to tumor-intrinsic pathways, such as DDR1 degradation, to enhance CAR-T infiltration and overcome resistance.

Ultimately, TAIII is a safe, selective, and reversible allosteric A2AR inhibitor that depletes CAR-Tregs, promotes Tcm formation, prevents relapse in multiple preclinical models, and rejuvenates patient-derived failing CAR-T products. Given its established clinical safety and high potency, TAIII can be immediately evaluated as an ex vivo manufacturing additive or an in vivo combination partner to improve CAR-T durability. We believe this natural product represents an ideal combination partner for CAR-T therapy and offers a broadly applicable strategy to overcome Treg-mediated resistance.

Integrative Transcriptomic Analysis and Preliminary Peripheral Blood Evaluation Identify ASAP2 as an Exploratory Candidate Molecule Associated with Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome
Authors: Cheng Luo, Chengzheng Duan, Shiyu Xu, Jiali Xu, Shengxin Wang, Lulu Zheng, Xianmei Yu, Dongjuan He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundComplete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS) is a rare 46,XY disorder/difference of sex development (DSD) caused by impaired androgen receptor (AR) function. Although AR sequencing is central to diagnosis, downstream transcriptional consequences of AR dysfunction and accessible readouts of androgen-pathway disruption remain unclear.MethodsTwo CAIS-related RNA-seq datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) were analyzed separately rather than merged at the expression-matrix level. GSE125222 included testicular tissue from 3 CAIS cases and 3 normal-spermatogenesis controls on GPL20301/Illumina HiSeq 4000. GSE300211 included 64 genital skin fibroblast libraries from 12 control and 4 CAIS fibroblast lines treated with ethanol or 10 nM dihydrotestosterone for 48 or 72 h on GPL30173/NextSeq 2000. Differential expression analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), random forest, and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) were used to prioritize exploratory candidate genes. ASAP2 mRNA expression and ASAP2 protein abundance were further examined by reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and Western blotting, respectively, in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from an independent pilot cohort (3 CAIS patients and 3 controls). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and CIBERSORTx were used to explore associated pathways and immune features.ResultsASAP2 was consistently downregulated in CAIS testicular tissue and patient-derived fibroblasts and was prioritized as an exploratory candidate molecule. In the pilot PBMC cohort, ASAP2 mRNA and ASAP2 protein levels were also reduced in CAIS. ASAP2-associated genes were enriched in pathways related to cytoskeletal remodeling, antigen processing and presentation, tumor necrosis factor signaling, and NOD-like receptor signaling. Immune deconvolution suggested altered immune-cell composition in CAIS tissue and associations between ASAP2 expression and several immune-cell subsets.ConclusionASAP2 represents a CAIS-associated exploratory candidate with preliminary peripheral detectability. These findings support further investigation of downstream molecular networks after AR deficiency and possible androgen-pathway disruption. They do not establish ASAP2 as a clinical diagnostic biomarker. Larger multicenter cohorts, relevant 46,XY DSD comparators, and functional studies are required to clarify whether ASAP2 can serve as a surrogate readout of broader androgen-pathway disruption.Keywords: ASAP2, androgen receptor, complete androgen insensitivity syndrome, exploratory target, machine learning, peripheral blood mononuclear cells, transcriptomics, weighted gene co-expression network analysis

Introduction

Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS) is a rare 46,XY disorder/difference of sex development caused by loss of androgen receptor (AR) function.1–3 Molecular genetic testing has substantially improved the diagnosis of CAIS, and AR sequencing remains central to clinical confirmation.1–3 However, identifying an AR variant does not fully explain the downstream tissue-level consequences of androgen signaling failure. Histopathological studies have shown that CAIS gonads may exhibit seminiferous tubule dysgenesis, reduced or delayed germ cell development, Leydig/Sertoli cell abnormalities, and persistence of fetal testicular features.4–6 These findings suggest that AR deficiency affects not only sexual differentiation, but also gonadal development, tissue homeostasis, and local molecular remodeling.

Transcriptomic approaches provide an opportunity to explore these downstream changes beyond single-gene diagnosis. Existing studies have examined androgen-dependent transcriptional responses in genital tissues and patient-derived fibroblasts, but fibroblasts show strong tissue-of-origin dependence and developmental heterogeneity.7,8 Therefore, candidate signals derived from a single tissue or cell type may reflect anatomical background rather than androgen receptor pathway biology. To reduce this limitation, the present study integrated a CAIS testicular tissue dataset (GSE125222) with a patient-derived genital skin fibroblast dataset (GSE300211), and used differential expression analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), and multi-algorithm feature selection to prioritize CAIS-associated exploratory candidate molecules.

The peripheral blood component of this study was designed only as a pilot exploratory assessment. AR signaling also participates in innate and adaptive immune regulation, including macrophage- and T cell-related immune processes.9–11 Previous AIS-related PBMC transcriptomic work provides a direct precedent for exploratory peripheral-blood analysis, and broader blood transcriptomic studies support the use of circulating leukocyte transcriptomes to assess systemic immune states.12–14 Nevertheless, PBMCs are immune-lineage cells and should not be interpreted as biological surrogates for testicular tissue or genital skin fibroblasts. Their transcriptomes may be influenced by immune-cell composition and systemic hormonal milieu, including altered testosterone, DHT, or estradiol levels in CAIS.15 Thus, the precise rationale of this study was to identify a highly preliminary exploratory ASAP2-related signal associated with downstream androgen-pathway disruption, and to assess whether this signal showed preliminary peripheral detectability in PBMCs. The study was not designed to establish ASAP2 as a diagnostic biomarker for CAIS or as a blood-test substitute for AR sequencing.

Materials and Methods

Data Acquisition and Transcriptomic Preprocessing

Two independent CAIS-related RNA-seq cohorts were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database: GSE125222 and GSE300211.16 GSE125222 represented an in vivo testicular tissue cohort, whereas GSE300211 represented an in vitro androgen-response fibroblast cohort. The sample size, biological source, phenotype annotations, sequencing platform, and analytical role of each dataset are summarized in Table 1.

Overview of GEO Datasets Used in This Study

GSE125222 included 6 testicular tissue libraries, comprising 3 samples from individuals with CAIS and 3 control testicular oligobiopsy samples from men with normal spermatogenesis. GSE300211 included 64 genital skin fibroblast RNA-seq libraries, comprising 48 control libraries from 12 control fibroblast lines and 16 CAIS libraries from 4 CAIS fibroblast lines. Samples in GSE300211 were treated with ethanol or 10 nM dihydrotestosterone (DHT) for 48 or 72 h. The available phenotype fields included tissue origin, cell type, genotype, treatment, time point, and batch.

Because GSE125222 and GSE300211 differed in tissue source, biological context, experimental design, and sequencing platform, their expression matrices were not directly merged for batch correction or pooled modeling.17 Instead, each dataset was preprocessed and analyzed within its own biological and technical context.

Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes

Differential expression analysis was performed separately for the above in vivo and in vitro datasets using the DESeq2 algorithm.17 Samples from the normal group served as the reference controls for identifying genes with significantly altered expression under the CAIS condition. To ensure the rigor of multiple-testing correction, the significance threshold for differential expression was uniformly set at an adjusted P value (padj) < 0.05, based on the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method, together with an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FoldChange|) > 1. The results were visualized using volcano plots and unsupervised hierarchical clustering heatmaps generated with the ggrepel and pheatmap packages.

Weighted Gene Co-Expression Network Analysis

To further identify gene clusters co-expressed within the in vitro CAIS fibroblast dataset, a scale-free co-expression network was constructed using the WGCNA R package (version 1.73).18 WGCNA was applied within GSE300211 after within-dataset preprocessing, rather than to a merged testis-fibroblast expression matrix, to avoid treating tissue-origin and platform differences as directly comparable biological variance. First, the top 5000 highly variable genes in the GSE300211 expression matrix were selected based on median absolute deviation (MAD) or variance. An optimal soft-thresholding power was then determined using the topology-based approximation algorithm to construct the adjacency matrix, which was subsequently transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM). The network was built as an unsigned network, with a minimum module size (minModuleSize) of 30 and a module merging threshold (mergeCutHeight) of 0.25. Finally, Pearson correlation coefficients between module eigengenes and the CAIS phenotype were calculated, and genes from the module showing the strongest association with CAIS, namely the turquoise module, were extracted for subsequent analyses.

Intersection of Multi-Level Evidence and Pathway Enrichment Analysis

The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) identified from in vivo testicular tissue, DEGs from in vitro fibroblasts, and genes from the WGCNA core module were intersected, and a three-way Venn diagram was constructed to define the core target gene pool potentially involved in disease development. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were then performed on the intersected gene set using the clusterProfiler R package (version 4.16.0).19 The species annotation database was set as org.Hs.egdb (version 3.21.0), and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Topological Evaluation of the Protein-Protein Interaction Network

The core candidate targets were imported into the STRING online database to construct the underlying protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. The network relationship file was then imported into Cytoscape for visualization and topological reconstruction. Using the Degree centrality algorithm implemented in the cytoHubba plugin, the top 10 hub genes with the highest topological weights were identified and extracted.

Multi-Algorithm Feature Reduction and Identification of the Core Molecule

To further prioritize exploratory candidate molecules from the candidate gene pool, three machine learning algorithms with mutually independent underlying logic were applied within the GSE300211 fibroblast expression matrix:20 (1) least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, implemented using the glmnet package (version 4.1–10) with 10-fold cross-validation (family = “binomial”); (2) random forest, implemented using the randomForest package (version 4.7–1.2) with 500 decision trees (ntree = 500), with variable importance quantified by the mean decrease in Gini impurity; and (3) support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), implemented using the e1071 package with a linear kernel. The machine-learning step was performed within GSE300211 after within-dataset preprocessing and candidate-gene filtering, and no algorithm was trained on a merged testis-fibroblast expression matrix. The intersection of the top 15 variables identified by the three algorithms was used to define exploratory molecular candidates, including ASAP2. The expression pattern and receiver operating characteristic curve of ASAP2 were then examined in GSE125222 as a testicular-tissue cross-dataset consistency assessment. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated using the pROC package as an internal screening metric and should not be interpreted as evidence of clinical diagnostic performance.

Single-Gene Gene Set Enrichment Analysis Based on ASAP2

To explore the potential downstream regulatory network of the core target gene ASAP2, the global expression matrix of the entire sequencing cohort was extracted, and Pearson correlation coefficients between ASAP2 expression and the expression of all genes across the transcriptome were calculated. A ranked gene list was then generated in descending order according to the correlation coefficients. Subsequently, GSEA-KEGG analysis was performed within the clusterProfiler framework, with minGSSize = 10 and maxGSSize = 500.19,21

Deconvolution and Association Analysis of the Local Immune Microenvironment

For the in vivo tissue samples (GSE125222), raw count data were converted into counts per million (CPM) and used as the input for the deconvolution algorithm. The processed data were uploaded to the CIBERSORTx online platform, and the LM22 immune cell signature matrix, which contains 22 mature leukocyte subsets, was used for cell composition estimation.22 After excluding background cell subsets with a maximum relative abundance of < 1%, exploratory between-group comparisons of the infiltration proportions of specific immune cell subsets were performed using the independent-samples Student’s t test. This approach was adopted because, in an extremely small cohort (n = 3 per group), nonparametric methods such as rank-sum tests have inherent limitations in statistical power. Finally, Spearman rank correlation analysis was used to assess the statistical association between ASAP2 mRNA expression levels and the relative proportions of specific immune cell subsets.

Clinical Blood Sample Collection and Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cell Isolation

Given that the bioinformatics analyses suggested an association between ASAP2 expression and local immune cell populations, and because AR signaling has documented roles in immune-cell regulation, we explored ASAP2 expression in the peripheral circulation as an accessibility test rather than as diagnostic validation.12 PBMCs were selected because they are clinically accessible and contain immune-cell subsets in which sex-hormone receptor signaling may influence transcriptional programs. However, PBMCs were not assumed to biologically represent testicular tissue or genital skin fibroblasts. Their transcriptomes may be affected by immune-cell composition and systemic endocrine conditions, including altered testosterone, DHT, or estradiol levels in CAIS. A total of 3 patients with CAIS treated at Quzhou People’s Hospital and 3 age-matched healthy volunteers were enrolled in this study. Peripheral venous blood (5 mL) was collected from all participants in the fasting state into EDTA-K2 anticoagulant tubes and processed within 2 h after collection. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated by Ficoll-Paque density gradient centrifugation (Cytiva, Uppsala, Sweden; Cat. No. 17144002). After isolation, PBMCs were washed twice with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and used for subsequent RNA and protein extraction or stored temporarily at −80 °C. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Quzhou People’s Hospital (Ethical Review Approval No. 2022–065, Quzhou People’s Hospital). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians.

RNA Extraction and RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from PBMCs using TRIzol reagent (TaKaRa, Shiga, Japan; Cat. No. 9108), and RNA concentration and purity were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer. For complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis, 1 μg of total RNA was reverse-transcribed using a reverse transcription kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China; Cat. No. R211-02) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RT-qPCR was then performed using SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China; Cat. No. Q711-02) on a LightCycler 480 II Real-Time PCR System (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). ACTB was used as the internal reference gene. Gene-specific primers for ASAP2 and ACTB were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). The primer sequences (5′→3′) were as follows: ASAP2 forward, 5′-AATAAGCGGAGCGGAAATTGC-3′; ASAP2 reverse, 5′-GTTTCAATGGAAGGTTTGAGGC-3′; ACTB forward, 5′-TTCCTTCCTGGGCATGGAGTC-3′; and ACTB reverse, 5′-AGACAGCACTGTGTTGGCGTA-3′. Amplification was performed using a standard two-step PCR protocol. Each biological sample was analyzed in technical triplicate, and relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Protein Extraction and Western Blot Analysis

After collection of the PBMC pellet, cells were resuspended in RIPA lysis buffer containing 1% protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail and lysed thoroughly on ice for 30 min. The lysates were then centrifuged at 4 °C and 12,000 rpm for 15 min, and the supernatants were collected as total protein extracts. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China; Cat. No. P0010S). Equal amounts of total protein (20–30 μg) were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto 0.45 μm polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. After transfer, membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat milk or bovine serum albumin (BSA) at room temperature for 1.5 h and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against ASAP2 (1:1000, ab230841, Abcam) and β-actin (1:5000, 66,009-1-Ig, Proteintech), respectively On the following day, membranes were washed three times with tris-buffered saline containing Tween-20 (TBST) and incubated with the corresponding horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents, detected with a chemiluminescence imaging system, and quantified using ImageJ software. Relative ASAP2 protein expression was expressed as the grayscale ratio of ASAP2/β-actin.

Sample-Size Rationale and Statistical Analysis

All bioinformatics analyses and statistical visualizations were performed in the R environment (version 4.5.1). The sample sizes of the two public GEO datasets were fixed by the available deposited cohorts and therefore could not be determined by an a priori power calculation. For the independent PBMC cohort, no formal power calculation was performed because CAIS is rare and only six eligible participants were available during the recruitment period. Accordingly, the PBMC evaluation was not powered to estimate a meaningful clinical effect size or diagnostic performance and should be regarded as a pilot exploratory assessment. Comparisons of continuous variables between groups were conducted using the independent-samples Student’s t test when the assumption of approximate normality was considered reasonable; correlation analyses were performed using Spearman rank correlation tests. Molecular biology experimental data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), based on biological samples rather than technical replicates alone. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered nominally significant. Given the limited sample size, analyses involving immune infiltration estimation, correlation with ASAP2 expression, and peripheral blood evaluation were interpreted as exploratory, and no inference about clinical diagnostic performance was made.

Results

Transcriptomic Differential Expression Profiles in in vivo and in vitro Models of Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome

To characterize the global transcriptomic features of CAIS, we first analyzed the testicular tissue dataset GSE125222. PCA showed a clear separation between the CAIS group and the normal control group in principal component space (Figure 1A). Using the screening criteria of padj < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 1, a total of 3551 upregulated genes and 6306 downregulated genes were identified (Figure 1B). The clustering heatmap of the top 50 differentially expressed genes showed distinct expression profile patterns between the two groups (Figure 1C).

Further intersection analysis of the differentially expressed genes identified in GSE125222 and GSE300211 yielded 672 shared differentially expressed genes (shared DEGs) (Figure 3C). Gene Ontology enrichment analysis showed that these genes were mainly enriched in terms such as collagen-containing extracellular matrix and were involved in biological processes including muscle tissue development and regulation of nervous system development (Figure 3D). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment analysis showed that the shared DEGs were significantly enriched in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, extracellular matrix-receptor interaction, and cytoskeleton in muscle cells, among other pathways (Figure 3E).

On this basis, the 672 shared DEGs were further intersected with genes in the turquoise module, ultimately yielding 328 overlapping genes, which were used as the candidate gene set for subsequent analyses (Figure 3C).

Subsequently, three methods, namely least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), random forest, and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), were applied for feature selection of the candidate genes. The results of the 10-fold cross-validation for LASSO regression are shown in Figure 4C; the variable importance ranking from the random forest model is shown in Figure 4D; and the feature weight ranking from SVM-RFE is shown in Figure 4E. The intersection of the three algorithms identified ASAP2 and GRIN2D as common candidate feature genes. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis showed that both genes achieved an apparent area under the curve (AUC) of 1.000 within the discovery cohort (Figure 4F). Because this estimate was derived from a small public dataset and a feature-selection workflow, it was interpreted as an internal screening signal rather than evidence of clinical diagnostic performance.

In light of the subsequent study design, ASAP2 was selected as the target molecule for preliminary evaluation and downstream exploratory analyses.

Transcriptomic Characterization of ASAP2 and Preliminary Evaluation in an Independent PBMC Pilot Cohort

To evaluate the expression pattern of ASAP2 across different data layers, we first reviewed its expression in the two public transcriptomic datasets. The results showed that ASAP2 was downregulated in both GSE125222 testicular tissue and the GSE300211 fibroblast model (Figure 5A).

We then examined ASAP2 expression in PBMCs from an independent pilot clinical cohort. RT-qPCR showed that ASAP2 mRNA expression was lower in PBMCs from patients with CAIS than in those from healthy controls (Figure 5B). Western blot analysis further demonstrated reduced ASAP2 protein expression in the CAIS group compared with the control group (Figure 5C). Given the cohort size and the immune-lineage origin of PBMCs, these results indicate preliminary peripheral detectability rather than validated diagnostic utility or direct tissue-level correspondence with gonadal disease.

Discussion

The central etiological basis of CAIS lies in defective AR function. However, how AR inactivation affects testicular development, local tissue homeostasis, and related molecular networks remains incompletely characterized.1,23 In the present study, we integrated transcriptomic data from CAIS testicular tissue (GSE125222) and patient-derived fibroblasts (GSE300211), and combined differential expression analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis, protein-protein interaction network analysis, and multi-algorithm feature selection. This strategy identified ASAP2 as an exploratory candidate molecule that was downregulated in public tissue and cellular datasets and in a small independent PBMC pilot cohort. The PBMC finding suggests preliminary peripheral detectability, but it should not be interpreted as clinical validation because the cohort was underpowered. The principal value of this study is therefore hypothesis generation. It proposes a candidate entry point for studying downstream molecular networks after AR deficiency, rather than establishing a disease-driving role or immediate clinical utility for ASAP2.

The significance of this finding should be interpreted within the context of the existing CAIS research landscape. Previous studies on CAIS have long focused on AR variant identification, clinical phenotype characterization, gonadal management, and tumor risk assessment, whereas systematic investigation of downstream molecular networks has remained relatively limited.1,24,25 Meanwhile, histopathological studies have clearly shown that CAIS gonads are not simply “morphologically abnormal testes”, but rather are accompanied by abnormal testicular development, reduced and developmentally delayed germ cells, aberrant Leydig/Sertoli cell differentiation, and persistent retention of certain fetal testicular features.4–6 Collectively, these observations suggest that the consequences of AR deficiency are not restricted to altered endpoints of sexual differentiation, but extend to broader disturbances in development and tissue homeostasis. Recent transcriptomic studies have also shown that androgen-dependent transcriptional responses in human genital tissue are strongly tissue-specific, whereas CAIS fibroblasts lack a reproducible canonical androgen-responsive program.7,16 Therefore, jointly screening candidate molecules at two levels—in vivo diseased tissue and in vitro patient-derived cells—may be more conducive than a single cohort to capturing relatively stable abnormal signals across different models.

Nevertheless, the dual-cohort integrative strategy adopted in this study requires cautious interpretation. GSE125222 and GSE300211 differ in tissue source, experimental setting, sequencing platform, and biological context. In addition, the CAIS and control fibroblast lines in GSE300211 were not fully matched in anatomical origin, and fibroblasts themselves exhibit marked tissue-of-origin dependence and developmental heterogeneity; cells from different anatomical sites may display stable and substantial transcriptional differences.7,8 Therefore, baseline transcriptomic differences between testicular tissue and genital skin fibroblasts could not be fully controlled by statistical normalization alone. We did not directly merge expression matrices across tissue types or apply conventional batch correction to a pooled matrix, because tissue origin, platform, and dataset identity were intrinsically confounded. Instead, each dataset was analyzed separately, and cross-dataset integration was restricted to intersecting gene-level evidence from differentially expressed genes, WGCNA module genes, and multi-algorithm feature-selection results. This cross-convergent strategy may reduce background noise and narrow the candidate range, but it cannot eliminate confounding from tissue origin, batch effects, or baseline biological differences. The evidence should therefore be interpreted as exploratory convergence rather than proof that the in vitro fibroblast model and in vivo testicular tissue are biologically equivalent.

Among the final candidate molecules, ASAP2 was selected for further exploratory analysis because of its biological plausibility, with reasonable biological justification. ASAP2 belongs to the ASAP ArfGAP protein family, whose members share a common domain architecture and are closely involved in membrane trafficking, regulation of adhesive structures, and actin cytoskeletal dynamics. Previous studies have shown that ASAP family-related proteins, particularly ASAP1, participate in the regulation of focal adhesions, podosomes/invadopodia, and cell migration-associated structures; more broadly, the ArfGAP family as a whole is regarded as an important regulator of membrane remodeling and cytoskeletal reorganization.26–28 In light of our finding that shared DEGs were enriched in extracellular matrix-receptor interaction, the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase-protein kinase B signaling pathway, and cytoskeleton in muscle cells, together with the single-gene gene set enrichment analysis results indicating associations of ASAP2 with cytoskeletal processes, antigen processing and presentation, the tumor necrosis factor signaling pathway, and the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, it is reasonable to speculate that ASAP2 may lie at the interface between structural homeostasis and microenvironmental remodeling. It should be emphasized, however, that this inference currently represents a biologically plausible association based on expression profiles and known protein functions, rather than direct mechanistic proof. At this stage, a more appropriate conclusion is that ASAP2 is a CAIS-related candidate molecule with structural biological plausibility, rather than a definitively established pathogenic core molecule.

Another noteworthy finding of the present study is the possible link between ASAP2-related pathways and local immune features. Accumulating evidence in recent years has shown that AR signaling acts not only in structural cells involved in sexual differentiation, but also in the regulation of innate and adaptive immune processes, and that AR dysregulation may be accompanied by alterations in tissue immune status.10,11,29 In the present study, gene set enrichment analysis indicated that ASAP2-related genes were enriched in antigen processing and presentation, the tumor necrosis factor signaling pathway, and the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway. Immune deconvolution further suggested relatively increased proportions of monocytes, M2 macrophages, and activated natural killer cells, together with a relatively decreased proportion of naive B cells, in CAIS testicular tissue. Taken together, these findings suggest that alterations in the local microenvironment of CAIS may involve not only structural abnormalities, but also remodeling of immune composition. However, these results are better regarded as exploratory clues rather than strong evidence. On the one hand, the LM22 signature matrix commonly used in CIBERSORTx is fundamentally built on 22 human leukocyte/hematopoietic cell subsets. On the other hand, previous studies have shown that deconvolution accuracy depends heavily on the biological match between the reference matrix and the target tissue, whereas human testicular tissue itself contains a complex mixture of germ cells and multiple somatic cell populations. Therefore, given the extremely small sample size of the present study, these findings should still be interpreted with caution.30–32 At present, a more conservative conclusion is that ASAP2 may be associated with changes in local immune features in CAIS, whereas the specific direction and underlying mechanisms require further investigation using higher-resolution approaches. These observations provide the biological rationale for examining ASAP2 in PBMCs, because PBMCs contain circulating immune-cell populations in which androgen- and estrogen-related signaling may influence transcriptional states. Nevertheless, this rationale supports only an exploratory cross-compartment assessment and does not imply that PBMCs reproduce the developmental biology of testicular tissue or genital skin fibroblasts.

In addition to the public-data analysis, we observed reduced ASAP2 mRNA and ASAP2 protein expression in an independent PBMC pilot cohort. The rationale for examining PBMCs was not that circulating immune cells biologically represent gonadal tissue or genital skin fibroblasts, but that AR signaling has documented roles in immune-cell regulation and that PBMCs may provide an accessible window into endocrine-immune perturbation.7,33,34 This observation therefore provides only a cross-compartment detectability signal, and its biological interpretation should remain conservative. PBMCs are heterogeneous and include lymphocytes, monocytes, and other immune cell subsets; their transcriptomes may be influenced by cell composition and systemic endocrine conditions rather than directly mirroring local gonadal AR signaling.33,34 In CAIS, altered testosterone, DHT, and estradiol exposure may further regulate PBMC transcriptional states independently of direct cell-intrinsic AR deficiency.1,15,23 Because serum sex-steroid levels and sorted immune-cell subsets were not profiled in the present PBMC cohort, the observed decrease in ASAP2 cannot be assigned specifically to direct AR-dependent regulation in circulating immune cells. Therefore, the PBMC results should not be interpreted as evidence that ASAP2 can diagnose CAIS or replace AR sequencing.1–3 If replicated in larger and clinically stratified cohorts, ASAP2 may be more plausibly evaluated as a surrogate readout of broader androgen-pathway disruption or as one component of a wider molecular signature in equivocal 46,XY DSD cases.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. The primary limitation is the lack of statistical power. Both the public discovery cohorts and the independent PBMC cohort were small, and the PBMC evaluation included only 3 CAIS cases and 3 controls. Therefore, the peripheral blood component should be considered a pilot exploratory analysis, and an AUC = 1.000 in the discovery cohort should be viewed as a possible indication of overfitting rather than established generalizability.21 Second, residual batch effects and biological heterogeneity remain unavoidable because GSE125222 and GSE300211 differed in tissue source, sequencing platform, experimental setting, and dataset identity. Although the datasets were processed separately and integrated only at the gene-list level, the convergence of ASAP2 across datasets should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive cross-tissue validation. Third, anatomical-origin bias in GSE300211 could not be fully eliminated because fibroblasts display marked regional and transcriptional heterogeneity.8 Fourth, enrichment analysis and immune deconvolution remain expression-based computational inferences without direct functional experimental support.32,35 Finally, PBMCs are immune-lineage cells and may be affected by systemic hormonal milieu and immune-cell composition. Because serum sex-steroid levels, hormone-matched controls, sorted immune-cell subsets, and in vitro androgen/estradiol stimulation experiments were not included, the PBMC ASAP2 signal cannot be assigned specifically to local gonadal AR signaling. Overall, the present study is better viewed as a starting point for exploratory candidate molecule identification and preliminary evaluation.

Conclusion

By integrating transcriptomic data from CAIS testicular tissue and patient-derived fibroblasts, together with weighted gene co-expression network analysis and multi-algorithm feature selection, this study identified ASAP2 as an exploratory candidate molecule associated with CAIS-related transcriptomic changes. ASAP2 showed consistent downregulation in public datasets and in a small independent PBMC pilot cohort, suggesting preliminary peripheral detectability. Enrichment and immune deconvolution analyses further suggested associations with cytoskeletal homeostasis, inflammation and immune-related pathways, and local microenvironmental remodeling. However, the evidence is based primarily on integrated small-cohort transcriptomic analyses and pilot evaluation. It is therefore insufficient to establish ASAP2 as a core pathogenic molecule or a mature clinical biomarker. Future studies should test whether ASAP2 can serve as a surrogate readout of broader androgen-pathway disruption in larger multicenter cohorts, including relevant 46,XY DSD differential diagnoses and equivocal cases, and should clarify its biological role through functional experiments and higher-resolution omics approaches.

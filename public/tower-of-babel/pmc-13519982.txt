Sandwich-structured rGO/CoxNiyOz/N-doped carbon nanosphere aerogels for thermal insulation and broadband electromagnetic shielding
Authors: Yubin Zhang, Zhenhao Zheng, Qicheng Zhu, Yanli Nan, Xiaoyun Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The growing complexity of electromagnetic-wave detection environments calls for microwave absorbers that combine strong attenuation, broad bandwidth, light weight, and thermal management. Herein, we report a sandwich-structured hierarchical reduced graphene oxide (rGO) aerogel decorated with magnetic Co–Ni oxide nanoparticles on N-doped honeycomb-like mesoporous carbon nanospheres (MCN), fabricated via hydrothermal self-assembly. This sandwich-structured aerogel features rGO as both the cross-linker and lossy component, with MCN/CoxNiyOz as the functional interlayer. This architecture synergistically integrates multiple loss mechanisms: N-doped carbon provides polar groups for dipolar polarization; metallic Co and Ni contribute to strong magnetic resonance loss, while the accompanying oxide phases (such as CoO and CoNiO2) provide abundant cation/oxygen vacancies and heterointerfaces that boost dielectric polarization; and the micro–meso–macro porous structure enables multi-scale scattering while preventing rGO restacking. The resulting aerogel achieves an outstanding minimum reflection loss (RLmin) of −45.51 dB and an effective absorption bandwidth (EAB, ≤−10 dB) of 6.32 GHz at 10 wt% loading. Additionally, over the thickness range of 2.6–3.8 mm, all samples exhibit an RLmin below −20 dB. It also exhibits excellent thermal insulation (16.5 °C surface temperature on a 90 °C hot plate), demonstrating dual functionality for microwave absorption and infrared stealth. This work suggests that sandwich-structured hierarchical engineering combined with compositional and interfacial design is a viable strategy for generating lightweight multifunctional microwave absorbers.

Introduction

In recent years, electromagnetic wave detection environments have become increasingly complex and demanding, rendering conventional microwave absorbers inadequate.1 Consequently, there is a pressing need for multifunctional microwave absorbers that can simultaneously fulfil multiple performance requirements.2 Therefore, electromagnetic wave-absorbing materials that synergistically integrate structural load-bearing3 (for coating reinforcement), mechanical flexibility4 (for wearable applications), or thermal insulation5 (for thermal protection systems), have emerged as critical research priorities. Among them, wave-absorbing and thermally insulating integrated materials have received considerable research interest owing to their broad application prospects in next-generation communication, radar stealth, and portable electronics, where the synergistic combination of microwave attenuation and thermal insulation is highly desirable.6,7 They not only attenuate radar waves and adjust infrared emissivity via electromagnetic loss to suppress thermal radiation for radar–infrared stealth, but also act as a thermal barrier to shield the matrix from external heat, maintaining structural stability at high temperatures.8–10

Aerogels feature ultra-low density,11 a continuous 3D nanoporous network,12 high porosity,13 and low thermal conductivity.14 These attributes, combined with compositional modification, pore structure regulation, and interfacial polarization engineering, allow aerogels to simultaneously optimize microwave attenuation and thermal insulation, meeting the requirements for functional synergy between these two properties.15–20

To date, a variety of aerogel materials with different chemical compositions have been developed for use in microwave absorption applications.21,22 Among the commonly used fillers for microwave-absorbing aerogels, such as silicon carbide23,24 (SiC), carbon nanotubes25 (CNTs), and reduced graphene oxide26 (rGO), rGO exhibits superior dielectric absorbing performance.27 This is attributed to its abundant lattice defects,28 high specific surface area,29 excellent electron transport capability,30 and tunable dielectric loss,31 positioning it as an ideal high-performance modifying filler.32 Peng et al.33 prepared MnO2/Ti3C2Tx/rGO composite aerogels by etching Ti3AlC2 with HF, followed by hydrothermal treatment with KMnO3 to yield MnO2/Ti3C2Tx hybrids. The hybrids were then mixed with GO, reduced by NaHSO3 at 80 °C under hydrothermal conditions, and freeze-dried. The resulting aerogel showed a minimum reflection loss (RLmin) of 36 dB at 1.5 mm and an effective absorption bandwidth (EAB) of 4.2 GHz. However, rGO nanosheets tend to aggregate via van der Waals interactions, decreasing the number of polarization active sites and compromising impedance matching.34 Additionally, as a single-component carbonaceous material, rGO itself lacks diverse electromagnetic loss mechanisms, relying predominantly on dielectric loss with limited magnetic or multiple polarization responses.35–37 Such dielectric-type absorbers inherently exhibit poor impedance matching with free space, inducing strong surface reflection and lowering the wave incidence efficiency.38–40 The high-frequency skin effect further restricts attenuation to the near-surface region, resulting in internal components being underutilized and yielding a limited absorption bandwidth.41–43 Collectively, these structural and compositional deficiencies prevent broadband and efficient microwave absorption.

Currently, to address the insufficient microwave absorption performance of rGO aerogels, researchers focus primarily on optimization strategies based on compositional modification and structural engineering.44,45 In composition engineering, the dominant strategy is to hybridize rGO with single-type magnetic nanoparticles (e.g., Fe3O4 and CoFe2O4), leveraging magneto-dielectric synergy to improve impedance matching and introduce magnetic loss.46,47 Nonetheless, such composites suffer from monophasic magnetic constituents and limited interfacial polarization diversity, which together constrain the controllable range of dielectric loss. Beyond magnetic hybridization, chemical functionalization enriches the carbon framework with polar functional groups (e.g., hydroxyl, amino, and carboxyl moieties) through heteroatom doping or surface modification, thereby enhancing dipolar polarization.48 In structural engineering, most efforts have focused on constructing three-dimensional porous skeletons or utilizing ice-templating for oriented pore channels. While these methods partially prevent nanosheet agglomeration and promote multiple internal reflections, the randomly distributed pore morphology and insufficient microstructural precision impede multistage synergistic dissipation, leaving considerable room for further improvement.49

In our preceding work, magnetic-particle-decorated 3D honeycomb-like mesoporous carbon nanospheres (multi-CoxNiyOz-MCN) were synthesized as a powder-type microwave absorbers, and their favour dielectric–magnetic synergy was established.49 On the basis of these findings, these magnetic–carbon hybrid nanospheres are herein incorporated as constitutive components into an rGO-based three-dimensional aerogel (rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel) via hydrothermal self-assembly, constructing a sandwich-structured architecture wherein the MCN spheres, decorated with magnetic CoxNiyOz nanoparticles, serve as the functional interlayer, while the rGO sheets simultaneously act as cross-linking agents to bridge adjacent MCN spheres and as lossy components contributing to the overall microwave attenuation. The honeycomb mesopores and magnetic CoxNiyOz particles of the MCN offer strong magnetocrystalline anisotropy and interfacial polarization, resulting in the formation of a hierarchical loss network superior to that of single-magnetic systems with rGO. Moreover, the nitrogen-doped carbon skeleton of MCN is abundant in oxygen- and nitrogen-containing polar groups, and their integration with rGO further increases the density of such dipolar species, resulting in the construction of a high-density polar network that induces intense dipolar polarization relaxation under alternating electromagnetic fields. Additionally, MCN spheres inhibit rGO agglomeration and construct a micro–meso–macro porous hierarchy, enabling progressive wave attenuation that reduces skin effect and high-frequency impedance mismatch. Benefiting from the synergistic effects of this sandwich configuration and the bifunctional role of rGO, the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel exhibits excellent microwave absorption performance at an ultralow filler loading of 10 wt%. Notably, it achieves an RLmin of −45.51 dB at an optimal thickness of 3.2 mm, along with an EAB of 6.32 GHz. Moreover, when the material was placed on a 90 °C heat source, its surface temperature stabilized at 16.5 °C, demonstrating its favorable potential for infrared stealth applications.

Preparation of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel

The preparation process of the rGO composite aerogels is illustrated in Scheme 1. First, 42 mg of multi-CoxNiyOz-MCN was added to a GO dispersion (1 mg mL−1, 70 mL). Detailed characterization data and synthesis details of multi-CoxNiyOz-MCN and MCN have been reported in our previous work.49 After ultrasonication for 1 h, a homogeneous mixed dispersion of GO and multi-CoxNiyOz-MCN was obtained. Afterward, a predetermined amount of triammonium citrate was introduced into the above mixed dispersion. In all the preparations, the mass ratio of triammonium citrate to GO was fixed at 5.0. The mixture was subsequently subjected to a hydrothermal reaction at 120 °C for 9 h to yield a hydrogel. After dialysis against a 20% ethanol solution and freeze-drying, the final composite aerogel, denoted as rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel, was obtained. The same procedure was employed to fabricate the rGO-MCN-aerogel.

Results and discussion

Microstructural and phase analysis

The morphology and pore structure of the rGO-MCN-aerogel and rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The rGO-MCN-aerogel (Fig. 1a and b) has a sandwich-structured three-dimensional porous network formed by overlapping rGO nanosheets, with abundant interlayer voids that reduce the stacking density. MCN microspheres (with diameters mostly below 200 nm) are uniformly anchored on the rGO surface without agglomeration, serving as the functional interlayer between adjacent rGO layers, resulting in the formation of a continuous conductive network. The MCN matrix possesses an open honeycomb mesoporous structure with an average pore size of 8.16 nm, as confirmed by BJH analysis, which ensures abundant gas diffusion channels and a large specific surface area.49 Furthermore, the MCN are derived from polyaniline precursors, yielding a high nitrogen doping level of 5.33 at% with configurations dominated by pyridinic-N, pyrrolic-N, and graphitic-N (confirmed by XPS).49 These nitrogen dopants destroy the symmetry of the carbon sp2 conjugated framework, creating abundant lattice defects and C–N dipolar sites, while simultaneously providing free charge carriers to the carbon matrix, thereby synergistically regulating the dielectric loss and electrical conductivity. High porosity favours impedance matching and wave penetration.

SEM images of (a and b) the rGO-MCN-aerogel and (c and d) the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel.

With respect to the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel (Fig. 1d and e), the porous skeleton remains intact, and compared with the rGO-MCN-aerogel, the metal oxide-loaded carbon nanospheres are uniformly dispersed with higher loading density and more abundant metal–carbon heterointerfaces, which promotes multiple scattering and interfacial polarization while maintaining a lightweight structure for broadband absorption at low filler loading.50

Collectively, both aerogels possess sandwich-structured three-dimensional porous rGO architectures with uniformly dispersed carbon nanospheres as the interlayer. The introduction of cobalt–nickel species preserves the lightweight framework while introducing abundant heterointerfaces between the magnetic components and the carbon matrix. This synergistic combination of sandwich architecture, porous morphology and multicomponent interfacial polarization accounts for the superior microwave absorption of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel from both structural and dielectric perspectives.

The crystalline phase structures, molecular functional groups, surface elemental compositions, chemical valence states, and carbon framework defect densities of the different materials were characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and Raman spectroscopy, and the results are presented in Fig. 2.

The XRD patterns (Fig. 2a) show distinct phase changes upon metal incorporation. The rGO-MCN-aerogel (Fig. 2a) displays only broad carbon peaks at 2θ ≈ 26° (002) and 43° (100), confirming the phase-pure carbon framework without metallic residues. In contrast, the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel (Fig. 2a) shows sharp peaks indexed to Co, Ni, CoO, and CoNiO2, clearly confirming the incorporation of multiple magnetic phases that enable dielectric–magnetic loss synergy and abundant heterointerfaces for polarization relaxation.51

The surface functional groups and interfacial interactions of MCN and the rGO-MCN-aerogel were characterized by FTIR (Fig. 2b). A comparison of the two spectra reveals that the absorption peak intensity near 3400 cm−1 for the rGO-MCN-aerogel is weakened and broadened, and the CC peak at 1615 cm−1 shows a slight shift, suggesting that two types of noncovalent interactions—hydrogen bonding and π–π stacking—are formed between MCN and rGO. Specifically, the –OH and –NH2 groups on the MCN surface form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with the residual oxygen-containing functional groups of rGO, while the conjugated carbon frameworks interact via π–π interactions, collectively constructing a stable and continuous carbon–carbon heterointerface. The characteristic peaks of functional groups are well preserved after composite formation, and no new peaks emerge, confirming that the two components are bonded solely through noncovalent interactions. The abundant heterointerfaces can induce charge accumulation under an electromagnetic field, triggering interfacial polarization relaxation, thereby providing a structural basis for the enhancement of dielectric loss and microwave absorption performance.53

For the rGO-MCN-aerogel, the XPS survey spectrum (Fig. 2d) reveals only C 1s, O 1s, and N 1s signals, indicating that the sample contains solely C, O, and N elements without metallic components. The high-resolution N 1s spectrum (Fig. 2e) can be deconvoluted into three doping configurations: graphitic N, amine N (–NH2/NH3+), and pyridinic N. Pyridinic N introduces dipolar polarization centers, graphitic N enhances the electrical conductivity of the carbon matrix, and amine N contributes to surface functional groups. Multitype nitrogen doping synergistically optimizes the dielectric loss performance. In the Raman spectrum (Fig. 2c), the ID/IG ratio of the rGO-MCN-aerogel is 1.27, indicating that abundant defect sites within the carbon framework can serve as active centers for polarization relaxation.

For the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel, the XPS survey spectrum (Fig. 2f) exhibits additional Co 2p and Ni 2p signals in addition to the characteristic C, O, and N peaks, confirming the successful loading of cobalt–nickel components. The high-resolution O 1s spectrum (Fig. 2g) is deconvoluted into three distinct oxygen species: lattice oxygen (OL) for the stable Co–O/Ni–O framework, oxygen vacancies (OV) acting as strong dipolar polarization centers, and carbon-based adsorbed oxygen (OC) located at the carbon–metal heterointerfaces.54 The abundant OV defects induce intense dipolar relaxation loss, while the OC-mediated heterointerfaces introduce additional interfacial polarization, synergistically optimizing the dielectric loss and impedance matching.55 The high-resolution Co 2p spectrum (Fig. 2h) can be fitted to metallic Co0, Co2+, and shake-up satellite peaks, whereas the Ni 2p spectrum (Fig. 2i) corresponds to Ni0 and Ni2+ signals. Zero-valent metals provide magnetic loss channels, whereas divalent metal ions create abundant metal–carbon heterointerfaces that increase interfacial polarization loss. The Raman spectrum (Fig. 2c) shows an ID/IG ratio of 1.12 for this sample, which is lower than that of the rGO-MCN-aerogel, indicating that the metallic components catalyze the graphitization of the carbon matrix during the high-temperature in situ growth process, thereby reducing the defect density and improving the conductive continuity.56

Collectively, the characterization results elucidate the multi-loss mechanism underlying the exceptional microwave absorption performance. Nitrogen-doped carbon architectures are endowed with abundant polar functional groups, which serve as efficient dipolar polarization centers to attenuate incident electromagnetic waves. Upon incorporation of cobalt–nickel species, crystalline magnetic phases are introduced alongside the carbon backbone, establishing not only magnetic resonance loss but also extensive heterointerfaces that promote interfacial polarization. Moreover, the presence of these metal components affects the degree of local graphitization of the carbon framework, striking a favourable balance between conduction loss and impedance matching. The synergy among polar groups, magnetic phases, interfacial junctions, and conductive networks—each rooted in a specific chemical composition and phase constitution—provides a comprehensive structural basis for the superior microwave absorption capability of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel.

Prior to comparative analysis, the optimal filler loading for each system was determined by evaluating aerogels with rGO-MCN-aerogel contents of 10, 20, and 30 wt% and rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel contents of 5, 10, and 15 wt% (Fig. S1 and S2, SI). Through a comprehensive comparison of the RL values, effective absorption bandwidths, and minimum reflection losses at various matching thicknesses, the optimal loadings were identified as 20 wt% for the rGO-MCN-aerogel and 10 wt% for the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel, which were used for all subsequent characterizations and comparisons.

For the rGO-MCN-aerogel (20 wt% filler loading, Fig. 3a–c), the RLmin reaches −38.55 dB with an EAB of 6.56 GHz. However, its effective absorption coverage is limited, with high-efficiency absorption concentrated mainly in the mid-to-high-frequency range and a relatively narrow matching thickness window, which is attributed to impedance mismatch caused by the excessively high electrical conductivity of the pure carbon matrix. For the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel (10 wt% filler loading, Fig. 3d–f), the RLmin reaches −45.51 dB with an EAB of 6.32 GHz, and it maintains strong absorption (RLmin ≤ −20 dB) across a wide thickness range, providing greater design flexibility for lightweight and ultrathin applications. The introduction of cobalt–nickel components introduces magnetic loss and heterointerfaces that balance dielectric and magnetic parameters for optimized impedance matching, whereas synergistic polarization and eddy current loss increase attenuation efficiency, yielding strong absorption and broad bandwidth.57

In summary, although the minimum reflection loss value of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel is not the highest, it achieves a wideband, efficient, and broader matching range of microwave absorption at an ultralow filler ratio of 10%. This material is lightweight and has excellent comprehensive microwave absorption performance, significantly outperforming the rGO-MCN-aerogel with a filler ratio of 20%. These results indicate that multi-metal collaborative design is an effective approach for preparing high-performance lightweight carbon-based microwave-absorbing aerogels.

To elucidate the mechanism underlying the difference in microwave absorption performance between the two aerogels, the complex permittivity, complex permeability, and corresponding loss tangents were measured over the frequency range of 2–18 GHz. The electromagnetic parameter curves are presented in Fig. 4.

Electromagnetic parameters of the rGO-MCN-aerogel and the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel in the 2–18 GHz frequency range: (a) ε′; (b) ε″; (c) tan δε; (d) µ′; (e) µ″; (f) tan δµ.

The real part of the permittivity (ε′) characterizes the storage capability of the electric field energy, whereas the imaginary part (ε″) reflects the dielectric loss level. Over the entire frequency range, both the ε′ and the ε″ of the rGO-MCN-aerogel are greater than those of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel. For both samples, the real and imaginary parts of the permittivity gradually decrease with increasing frequency, indicating typical frequency dispersion behavior. The pure carbon-based rGO-MCN-aerogel forms a continuous conductive network with a high concentration of free charge carriers, thus exhibiting stronger energy storage and dielectric loss capability. In contrast, the introduction of cobalt–nickel oxides partially interrupted the conductive pathways of the carbon matrix, reducing the overall dielectric parameters and effectively alleviating the impedance mismatch issue caused by excessively high permittivity. The variation trend of the dielectric loss tangent (tan δε) is consistent with that of ε″. rGO-MCN-aerogel exhibits a higher dielectric loss level across the entire band, whereas rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel displays a moderate dielectric loss intensity with stable frequency-dependent behavior, relying on interfacial polarization induced by metal–carbon heterointerfaces and dipolar polarization arising from nitrogen doping sites to achieve stable and continuous dielectric attenuation.58

The real part of the permeability (µ′) and the imaginary part (µ″) reflect the magnetic energy storage capability and magnetic loss intensity, respectively. With respect to the rGO-MCN-aerogel, which contains no magnetic components, µ′ remains stable at approximately 1.0, and µ″ is extremely low, contributing negligibly to magnetic loss. In contrast, because of the presence of cobalt–nickel magnetic species, the µ′ of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel is significantly greater in the low-frequency region, Furthermore, a sharp decline in µ′ is observed around 2 GHz, accompanied by two distinct µ″ resonance peaks located at approximately 2 GHz and in the 10–12 GHz band. These features originate from the natural resonance and exchange resonance effects induced by the magnetic Co/Ni particles, respectively. The magnetic loss tangent (tan δµ) further confirms the superior magnetic loss of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel in the mid-to-high frequency range, establishing a synergistic attenuation system combining dielectric and magnetic losses that compensates for the deficiency of pure carbon materials and relies solely on a single dielectric loss mechanism.59

On the basis of the comprehensive electromagnetic parameters, it can be concluded that the rGO-MCN-aerogel consumes electromagnetic energy solely through dielectric loss; however, its excessively high dielectric parameters disrupt impedance matching with free space, significantly narrowing the effective absorption bandwidth. In contrast, the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel achieves simultaneous modulation of the dielectric parameters and construction of magnetic loss channels through the introduction of multimetallic components, establishing a dielectric–magnetic synergistic loss mechanism that ensures a stable attenuation capability while optimizing broadband impedance matching.60 This constitutes the core mechanism underlying its superior overall microwave absorption performance.

For the rGO-MCN-aerogel, two distinct and well-defined semicircular arcs can be observed in the Cole–Cole plot, corresponding to two types of polarization relaxation origins, namely, dipolar polarization induced by nitrogen doping sites in the carbon framework and interfacial polarization formed at the rGO nanosheet/carbon nanosphere interfaces. With respect to the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel sample, the Cole–Cole plot reveals a greater number of semicircular arcs with varying sizes, which is significantly greater than that of the pure carbon sample. With the introduction of multimetallic cobalt–nickel oxides, abundant metal–carbon heterointerfaces are constructed within the system, introducing numerous additional interfacial polarization relaxation centers.61 Moreover, the synergy between metal ions and nitrogen-doping sites diversifies the dipolar polarization sources. The simultaneous operation of multiple polarization relaxation processes corresponds to the multiple segmented arcs observed in the plot. A comparison of the two sets of curves reveals that rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel possesses a greater number of polarization loss channels, and the multiple relaxation effects can continuously dissipate the electric field energy of incident electromagnetic waves, which constitutes the key polarization mechanism underlying its broader dielectric loss capability and enhanced microwave absorption performance.

Over the entire test frequency range, the α values of the rGO-MCN-aerogel are consistently higher than those of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel, and both show an overall increasing trend with increasing frequency. The attenuation level of the rGO-MCN-aerogel increases because of the strong conduction loss provided by its continuous carbon conductive network. Although the α values of the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel are generally lower, they increase steadily with increasing frequency. The multiple polarization and magnetic loss channels constructed by the multimetallic components can continuously dissipate the energy of electromagnetic waves that enter the material, while the moderate electromagnetic parameters ensure efficient wave incidence, achieving a balance between incidence efficiency and attenuation capability.62

The eddy current loss coefficient C0 is employed to determine the dominant type of magnetic loss: if the C0 curve remains constant with increasing frequency, eddy current loss is the predominant mechanism; if the curve exhibits significant fluctuations, resonance losses such as natural resonance and exchange resonance dominate.635In the low-frequency region, the C0 values of both samples exhibit pronounced fluctuations. For the rGO-MCN-aerogel, which contains no magnetic components, the curve rapidly decreases and approaches zero, contributing negligibly to magnetic loss. For rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel, the C0 curve continues to fluctuate in the mid-to-high-frequency range without exhibiting a plateau region, indicating that its magnetic loss originates mainly from natural resonance and exchange resonance induced by magnetic cobalt–nickel particles, with eddy current loss playing only a supplementary role.63 Multiple resonance effects broaden the effective frequency range of magnetic loss and form a synergistic attenuation system together with dielectric loss, further enriching the understanding of the microwave absorption mechanism.64

Impedance matching is essential for microwave absorbers, as it determines whether incident waves can enter the material rather than being reflected at the surface. On the basis of transmission line theory, the impedance matching (Zin/Z0) of the two aerogels was calculated at various thicknesses, as shown in Fig. 7. In general, values closer to 1 indicate better matching.

The two samples show markedly different matching behavior. For the rGO-MCN-aerogel (Fig. 7a), Zin/Z0 remains low across most of the band, approaching unity only in a narrow thickness–frequency window. This poor matching arises from its excessively high permittivity, which is the main factor limiting its absorption performance.

In contrast, the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel (Fig. 7b) exhibits superior impedance matching, with Zin/Z0 values within the ideal range over a broader frequency and thickness span. The multimetallic components enable precise balancing of the permittivity and permeability, allowing favourable matching even at 10 wt% loading and ensuring efficient wave entry for subsequent attenuation.

Together with the attenuation constant and loss mechanism analyses, these results confirm that the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel achieves an optimal trade-off between attenuation capability and impedance matching—the key to its broadband, high-efficiency absorption at low filler loading. This finding validates the multimetallic synergistic design strategy for lightweight high-performance microwave absorbers.65

Schematic illustration of the microwave absorption mechanism for the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel.

The three-dimensional cross-linked rGO nanosheets overlap with each other to form a continuous conductive network, in which free electrons can migrate across the nanosheets, resulting in significant conduction loss under an external alternating electromagnetic field, thereby converting electromagnetic energy into thermal dissipation.66 Moreover, the abundant interconnected pores between the nanosheets prolong the propagation path of electromagnetic waves, promoting multiple reflection and scattering effects that further consume the incident wave energy. The carbon nanospheres anchored on the graphene surface uniformly host multimetallic CoxNiyOz particles, forming abundant heterostructures at the carbon–metal and carbon–carbon interfaces.67 Under an electromagnetic field, charge separation and accumulation occur at these interfaces, resulting in strong interfacial polarization relaxation loss. In addition, nitrogen doping sites and lattice defects within the carbon framework serve as centers for dipole polarization and defect polarization, with polar groups and defect sites undergoing oscillatory reorientation under an alternating electric field, continuously dissipating the energy of the electric field. The cobalt–nickel-based magnetic components simultaneously introduce magnetic loss channels, attenuating magnetic field energy through natural resonance and exchange resonance, thereby establishing a composite attenuation system featuring synergistic dielectric and magnetic losses.68

MCN and rGO are bound through hydrogen bonding and π–π stacking to form stable carbon-based heterointerfaces, while the multimetallic particles further increase the interface types. The synergistic effects of multiple loss mechanisms, together with the three-dimensional porous framework that optimizes impedance matching, enable electromagnetic waves to efficiently enter the material interior and be fully dissipated, ultimately achieving broadband and high-efficiency microwave absorption.69

Mechanical properties and infrared thermal imaging analysis

Fig. 9 presents the macroscopic characterisation results of the compressive resilience and lightweight performance of the two aerogels. The compression test under a 60 g load revealed that the pure rGO- MCN-aerogel underwent irreversible skeleton collapse upon compression, exhibiting poor resilience. In contrast, after the introduction of CoxNiyOz metal oxides, the ternary composite aerogel demonstrated excellent reversible compressibility. The CoxNiyOz particles can serve as rigid support points, effectively reinforcing the porous network structure and improving the material toughness. Meanwhile, the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel could rest stably on a petal surface without bending it, demonstrating its ultralow density and lightweight nature. These results indicate that cobalt–nickel oxide modification can simultaneously optimise the mechanical reversibility and lightweight advantages of aerogels, making them suitable for lightweight functional applications.70

The synergistic mechanism of thermal insulation and infrared suppression is illustrated with the aid of a schematic diagram, and the results are presented in Fig. 10.

Time-sequence infrared thermal images of the two aerogels placed on a 90 °C heat source and the corresponding thermal insulation and infrared stealth mechanism: (a) rGO-MCN-aerogel; (b) rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel; (c) schematic diagram illustrating the heat barrier and infrared attenuation mechanism of the composite aerogel.

The infrared thermal imaging time series images directly reflect the difference in surface temperature increase between the samples. For the rGO-MCN-aerogel, the initial center temperature was 11.7 °C and increased to 14.7 °C after 5 min of heating, corresponding to a temperature increase of 3.0 °C. For the rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel, the initial center temperature was 15.7 °C and reached only 16.5 °C under the same heating conditions, with a temperature increase of only 0.8 °C. The significantly lower heating rate than that of the pure carbon-based aerogel demonstrates its superior thermal insulation capability.

Both samples possess a three-dimensional interconnected porous network formed by the overlapping of graphene nanosheets. A large amount of stagnant air is trapped within the pores, which effectively suppresses vertical heat transfer owing to the extremely low thermal conductivity of air. Moreover, the tortuous and intertwined carbon skeleton prolongs the solid-state heat conduction pathway, thereby reducing heat transfer through both the solid and gas phases. In addition, the abundant porous interfaces induce multiple reflections and scattering of incident infrared radiation, substantially reducing the transmitted infrared energy and achieving effective infrared signal attenuation.71

Fig. 10c systematically illustrates the integrated protection mechanism of the material: upon the arrival of external high-temperature heat input at the material surface, heat is slowly conducted along the carbon-based solid framework, while the entrapped stagnant air within the pores further inhibits gas-phase heat transfer. The multiple internal reflections and scattering of infrared radiation within the porous structure effectively weaken the transmitted infrared signal, resulting in only a small amount of heat diffusing outwards.72 Consistent with the thermal imaging results in Fig. 10a and b, both aerogels exhibit extremely low temperature rise rates (within 3.0 °C after 5 minutes) and maintain surface temperatures well below 20 °C, confirming the reliable thermal shielding effect. Owing to the highly porous MCN skeleton, the overall density of the aerogel is significantly reduced, ensuring lightweight thermal insulation. Moreover, the introduction of cobalt–nickel composite metal oxide particles further absorbs and scatters infrared radiation, reducing the infrared emissivity of the system and further lowering the surface thermal radiation, simultaneously endowing the material with infrared stealth capability on the basis of enhanced thermal insulation. This three-dimensional porous carbon–metal composite structure can simultaneously achieve high-efficiency microwave absorption, lightweight thermal insulation, and infrared stealth, satisfying the application requirements for multiband-compatible electromagnetic–thermal protective materials.73

Conclusions

In summary, a lightweight reduced graphene oxide-based composite aerogel decorated with multimetallic cobalt–nickel oxide-decorated nitrogen-doped mesoporous carbon nanospheres (rGO-CoxNiyOz-MCN-aerogel) was successfully fabricated via hydrothermal self-assembly, affording a sandwich-structured architecture wherein rGO sheets serve as both cross-linking outer layers and lossy components, while MCN/CoxNiyOz composites function as the functional interlayer. The synergistic integration of compositional and structural engineering endows the aerogel with multiple loss mechanisms: polar groups provide dipolar polarization, magnetic phases contribute resonance loss, and heterointerfaces promote interfacial polarization, while the hierarchical porous architecture facilitates multi-scale wave scattering and optimizes impedance matching. Benefiting from this sandwich-structured multilevel design, the optimized aerogel achieves an exceptional minimum reflection loss of −45.51 dB at 3.2 mm and an effective absorption bandwidth of 6.32 GHz at an ultralow filler loading of 10 wt%. Notably, it also exhibits outstanding thermal insulation (16.5 °C on a 90 °C hot plate), demonstrating dual functionality for microwave absorption and infrared stealth. This work presents a viable strategy for designing high-performance, multifunctional microwave absorbers through sandwich-structured hierarchical architecture engineering combined with multimetallic compositional design.

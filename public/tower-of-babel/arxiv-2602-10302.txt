Totally decomposable algebras with involution and R R -triviality
M. Archita, Karim Johannes Becher

Abstract
. We show that the group of proper projective similitudes of a totally decomposable algebra with involution of the first kind over a field of characteristic different from 2 2 is R R -trivial. Keywords: Classical adjoint algebraic group, stably rational, R R -equivalence, quadratic form, hyperbolic, Pfister form Classification (MSC 2020): 11E04, 11E57, 11E81, 14E08, 20G15



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of characteristic different from
2
.
Let
(A,\sigma)
be a
K
-algebra with involution. Let
{\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
denote the algebraic group of its projective similitudes and
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
the connected component of the identity.
The latter is a classical semi-simple connected linear algebraic group of adjoint type.
(In various cases, one has
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)={\bf PSim}(A,\sigma)
, e.g. if
\sigma
is symplectic.)
In
[
9
]
, Merkurjev studied
the geometric properties of
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
as a variety, regarding the question whether it is rational, stably rational or
R
-trivial.

In this article, we assume that the involution
\sigma
is
K
-linear (i.e. an involution of the first kind).
We call
(A,\sigma)

totally decomposable

if

(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}(Q_{i},\tau_{i})\right)

for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
K
-quaternion algebras with involution
(Q_{i},\tau_{i})
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
and the adjoint algebra with involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)
of some odd-dimensional quadratic form
\psi
over
K
. (Taking
\psi=\langle 1\rangle
corresponds to the situation without the first factor.)

If
\sigma
is orthogonal and
A
is split, then we obtain by
[
1
, Theorem 1]
that
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi\otimes\pi)

for some Pfister form
\pi
over
K
, and in this case it follows by
[
9
, Prop. 7]
that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
is stably rational, and in particular
R
-trivial, by
[
9
, Prop. 1]
.
One may ask whether these facts still hold without assuming that
A
is split.
In this article, we show:

Theorem

(
4.2
)
.

If
(A,\sigma)
is a totally decomposable
K
-algebra with involution of the first kind with
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
, then
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
is
R
-trivial.

The most interesting case is that where
\sigma
is orthogonal and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
.
To our knowledge, this is the first general positive result on
R
-triviality of
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
that applies to non-split algebras with orthogonal involution
(A,\sigma)
of degree divisible by
8
.

For the proof, we crucially rely on the characterization of
R
-equivalence in
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
established in
[
9
, Theorem 1]
. It translates the question into that of comparing the group of similarity factors with the subgroup of the multiplicative group
{K}^{\times}
generated by the norms from finite field extensions where the involution
\sigma
becomes hyperbolic.
The main part of our proof consists in describing the similarity factors of
(A,\sigma)
in terms of quadratic forms over
K
.
To this end, we combine a variety of key results from quadratic form theory.

2.
Similarity factors

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
K
-algebra with involution of the first kind. (That is,
A
is a central simple
K
-algebra and
\sigma
is a
K
-linear involution on
A
.)
In
{K}^{\times}
, we consider the subgroup

\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\{\sigma(x)x\mid x\in{A}^{\times}\}\cap{K}^{\times}\,.

Its elements are called
similarity factors
or
multipliers of similitude of
(A,\sigma)
. We refer to
[
7
, §12]
for a discussion of similitudes and their multipliers.

We use standard notation and terminology from quadratic form theory, taking
[
4
]
and
[
8
]
as main references.
For basic concepts on central simple algebras and their involutions, we refer to
[
12
, Chap. 8]
and
[
7
]
.
By a
quadratic form over
K
we always mean a regular quadratic form, given by a quadratic map on a finite dimensional
K
-vector space.
Given a quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
, we associate its
adjoint algebra with involution

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)
; see e.g.
[
1
]
.
For
a\in{K}^{\times}
, we write
\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle
for the quadratic form
\langle 1,-a\rangle
, which is the norm form of the quadratic étale extension of
K
given by
K[T]/(T^{2}-a)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

We have

\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\{a\in{K}^{\times}\mid\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes(A,\sigma)\mbox{ is hyperbolic}\}\,.

Proof.

See
[
7
, Prop. 12.20]
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\psi
be an odd-dimensional quadratic form over
K
.
Then
(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic.
Furthermore,

\mathsf{G}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes(A,\sigma))=\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)\,.

Proof.

If
(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic, then so is
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes(A,\sigma)
.
Assume now that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic.
If
\psi=m\times\langle 1\rangle
for an odd number
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then the desired conclusion that
(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic follows from Scharlau’s result
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
that the torsion part of the Witt group of hermitian forms over
(A,\sigma)
is
2
-primary; see also
[
2
, Theorem 6.5]
.
In the general case, fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathsf{dim}(\psi)=2k+1
.
By Lewis’ Theorem
[
8
, Chap. VIII, Theorem 8.3]
, the class of
\varphi
in the Witt ring of
K
is a zero of the polynomial
\prod_{i=0}^{k}(X^{2}-(2i+1)^{2})
. This implies that for
m=\prod_{i=0}^{k}(2i+1)^{2}
the quadratic form
m\times\langle 1\rangle
is Witt equivalent to a multiple of
\psi\otimes\psi
, and hence of
\psi
.
Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic, we therefore obtain that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(m\times\langle 1\rangle)\otimes(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic.
Since
m
is odd, it follows by the first case that
(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic.
This shows the first part of the statement.

For
c\in{K}^{\times}
, we have

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes(A,\sigma))\,,

and by the first part, this algebra with involution is hyperbolic if and only if

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes(A,\sigma)
is hyperbolic.
Now the last part follows by
2.1
.
∎

.

Let
\psi
be a quadratic form of odd dimension over

.
Using
2.2
m
[

yields that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi)\otimes(A,\sigma))

Applying

For a quadratic form
\varphi
over

of dimension at least
3

K(\varphi)
.
We refer to

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
Q
be a
K
-quaternion algebra,
\nu
its norm form and set
L=K(\nu)
.
Let
(A,\sigma)
be a
K
-algebra with orthogonal involution such that
A
is Brauer equivalent to
Q
.
Then
\sigma_{L}
is hyperbolic if and only if
\sigma
is hyperbolic.
Furthermore,
A_{L}
is split and
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}((A,\sigma)_{L})\cap{K}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
A_{L}
is split.
We set
L_{0}=K(\nu^{\prime})
where
\nu^{\prime}
is the subform satisfying
\nu\simeq\langle 1\rangle\perp\nu^{\prime}
.
Then
L/L_{0}
is a rational function field in one variable.
Hence, the statement on hyperbolicity follows from the corresponding statement with
L_{0}
in the place of
L
, which is Dejaiffe’s Theorem
[
3
]
.
Applying this now to
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes(A,\sigma)
in the place of
(A,\sigma)
for arbitrary
c\in{K}^{\times}
, we conclude by
2.1
that
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}((A,\sigma)_{L})\cap{K}^{\times}
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
Q
be a
K
-quaternion algebra and let
\gamma
be the canonical involution on
Q
and let
\nu
denote the norm form of
Q
.
Then for every
K
-algebra with symplectic involution
(A,\sigma)
where
A
is Brauer equivalent to
Q
, there exists a quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
such that

(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)\otimes(Q,\gamma)\,.

Given such a presentation,
\sigma
is isotropic, respectively hyperbolic, if and only if
\varphi\otimes\nu
has the corresponding property, and
furthermore
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi\otimes\nu)
.

Proof.

This follows from Jacobson’s Theorem
[
6
]
; see also
[
12
, Theorem 10.1.1]
.
∎

For
a,b\in{K}^{\times}
, we denote by

(a\,\cdot\!\!\mid b)_{K}

the
K
-algebra with involution
(Q,\tau)_{K}
defined on
Q=K\oplus Ki\oplus Kj\oplus Kij
by relations
i^{2}=a,j^{2}=b,ij+ji=0,\tau(i)=-i\hbox{ and }\tau(j)=j
.
Hence,

is the
K
-quaternion algebra
(a,b)_{K}
and
\tau
is an orthogonal involution on

of discriminant

.

For any
K
-quaternion algebra with orthogonal involution,
fixing
a,b\in{K}^{\times}
with
\mathsf{disc}(\tau)=a{K}^{\times 2}
and

, one obtains that
(Q,\tau)_{K}\simeq(a\,\cdot\!\!\mid b)_{K}
.

3.
Pfister forms

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\pi
be a nontrivial Pfister form over
K
.
For
c\in\mathsf{G}(\pi)
, there exists
d\in{K}^{\times}
such that
c\in\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle
and
\pi_{K(\sqrt{d})}
is hyperbolic.

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathsf{G}(\pi)
.
Then
\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi
is hyperbolic and in particular isotropic.
By
[
5
, Corollary 2.3]
, it follows that
\pi
has a
2
-dimensional subform
\beta
such that
\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\beta
isotropic.
Let
d\in{K}^{\times}
be such that
\mathsf{disc}(\beta)=d{K}^{\times 2}
.
Then
c\in\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle
and
\beta_{K(\sqrt{d})}
is isotropic.
Hence
\pi_{K(\sqrt{d})}
is isotropic, and since
\pi
is a Pfister form, it follows that
\pi_{K(\sqrt{d})}
is hyperbolic.
∎

We denote by
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}_{K}
the hyperbolic plane over
K
, and for
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
d\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}_{K}
for the
d
-fold orthogonal sum
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}_{K}\perp\ldots\perp\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}_{K}
, the
2d
-dimensional hyperbolic form over
K
.
Given a quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
, we denote by
\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
its Witt index. Witt’s Cancellation Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 8.4]

implies the following subform criterion.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\tau
and
\varphi
be quadratic forms over
K
.
Then
\tau
is a subform of
\varphi
if and only if
\mathsf{i}(\varphi\perp-\tau)\geqslant\mathsf{dim}(\tau)
.

Proof.

Let
d=\mathsf{dim}(\tau)
. Note that
\tau\perp-\tau\simeq d\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}_{K}
.
In view of Witt’s Cancellation Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 8.4]
, we obtain for an arbitrary quadratic form
\psi
over
K
that

\varphi\simeq\tau\perp\psi
holds if and only if
\varphi\perp-\tau\simeq\psi\perp d\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}_{K}
.
The existence of such a quadratic form
\psi
is therefore equivalent to having that
\mathsf{i}(\varphi\perp-\tau)\geqslant d
.
∎

We formulate a variant of the so-called Subform Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 22.5]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\varphi
and
\tau
be Pfister forms with
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)>\mathsf{dim}(\tau)\geqslant 2
.
Then
\varphi_{K(\tau)}
is hyperbolic if and only if
\tau
is a subform of
\varphi
.

Proof.

If
\varphi
is anisotropic and
\varphi_{K(\tau)}
is hyperbolic, then it follows by
[
4
, Theorem 22.5]
that
\tau
is a subform of
\varphi
.
If
\varphi
is isotropic, then it is hyperbolic, and since
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)\geqslant 2\mathsf{dim}(\tau)
, it follows that
\tau
is a subform of
\varphi
.
Conversely, if
\tau
is a subform of
\varphi
, then
\varphi_{K(\tau)}
is isotropic and hence hyperbolic.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\alpha
be an anisotropic Pfister form and
\varphi
a quadratic form over
K
.
Then there exists a quadratic form
\varphi^{\prime}
with
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi^{\prime})\equiv\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)\,\,\mathsf{mod}\,2
and such that

\alpha\otimes\varphi^{\prime}
is anisotropic and Witt equivalent to
\alpha\otimes\varphi
.
In particular, if
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
is even, then
\mathsf{i}(\alpha\otimes\varphi)
is a multiple of
\mathsf{dim}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

If
\alpha\simeq\langle 1\rangle
the statement is trivial, so we may assume that
\alpha
is a non-trivial Pfister form and consider its function field
K(\alpha)
.
Then
\alpha_{K(\alpha)}
is hyperbolic.
Let
\vartheta
denote the anisotropic part of
\alpha\otimes\varphi^{\prime}
. It follows by
[
8
, Chap. X, Theorem 4.11]
that
\vartheta\simeq\alpha\otimes\varphi^{\prime}
for some quadratic form
\varphi^{\prime}
.
Then
(\varphi\perp-\varphi^{\prime})\otimes\alpha
is hyperbolic, and since
\alpha
is not hyperbolic, it follows by
[
8
, Chap. VIII, Cor. 8.5]
(or by
2.2
) that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi\perp-\varphi^{\prime})
is even, whereby
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)\equiv\mathsf{dim}(\varphi^{\prime})\,\mathsf{mod}\,2
.
∎

For a quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
, we set
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\{a\in{K}^{\times}\mid a\varphi\simeq\varphi\}
and observe that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{G}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi))
, and we further denote by
\mathsf{D}(\varphi)
the set of nonzero elements of
{K}^{\times}
which are represented by
\varphi
over
K
.
Recall that
\mathsf{D}(\varphi)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
holds whenever
\varphi
is a Pfister form; see e.g.
[
8
, Chap. X, Theorem 1.8]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\alpha
and
\rho
be Pfister forms over
K
such that
\rho
is nontrivial. Let
b\in{K}^{\times}
.
Set
\tau=\alpha\otimes\langle\!\langle b\rangle\!\rangle
,
\pi=\alpha\otimes\rho
and
\widetilde{\pi}=\alpha\otimes(\langle b\rangle\perp\rho^{\prime})
.
Then
\mathsf{G}(\pi_{K(\tau)})\cap{K}^{\times}=\mathsf{D}(\pi)\cdot\mathsf{D}(\widetilde{\pi})
.

Proof.

If
\pi
is hyperbolic, then
\mathsf{G}(\pi_{K(\tau)})\cap{K}^{\times}={K}^{\times}=\mathsf{D}(\pi)=\mathsf{D}(\pi)\cdot\mathsf{D}(\widetilde{\pi})
.
We may thus assume that
\pi
is anisotropic. In particular
\alpha
is anisotropic.

Consider
c\in{K}^{\times}
.
We have
c\in\mathsf{G}(\pi_{K(\tau)})
if and only if
(\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi)_{K(\tau)}
is hyperbolic.
In view of
3.3
, this is if and only if
\tau
is a subform of
\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi
.
Note that
\tau=\alpha\perp-b\alpha
and
\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi=\alpha\perp(\alpha\otimes\rho^{\prime})\perp-c\pi
.
By Witt’s Cancellation Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 8.4]
,
\tau
is a subform of
\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi
if and only if
-b\alpha
is a subform of
(\alpha\otimes\rho^{\prime})\perp-c\pi
.
Now observe that

b\alpha\perp(\alpha\otimes\rho^{\prime})\perp-c\pi\simeq\alpha\otimes(\langle b\rangle\perp\rho^{\prime}\perp-c\rho)\simeq\widetilde{\pi}\perp-c\pi\,.

It follows by
3.4
that
\mathsf{i}(\widetilde{\pi}\perp-c\pi)
is a multiple of
\mathsf{dim}(\alpha)
.
Therefore
\widetilde{\pi}\perp-c\pi
is isotropic if and only if
\mathsf{i}(b\alpha\perp(\alpha\otimes\rho^{\prime})\perp-c\pi)\geqslant\mathsf{dim}(b\alpha)
, and by
3.2
this is if and only if
-b\alpha
is a subform of
(\alpha\otimes\rho^{\prime})\perp-c\pi
.

We conclude that
-b\alpha
is a subform of
\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi
if and only if
\widetilde{\pi}\perp-c\pi
is isotropic. The latter is equivalent to having that
c\in\mathsf{D}(\pi)\cdot\mathsf{D}(\widetilde{\pi})
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
(A,\sigma)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi\otimes\rho)\otimes(a\,\cdot\!\!\mid b)_{K}
for some
a,b\in{K}^{\times}
, a Pfister form
\rho
over
K
and an odd-dimensional quadratic form
\psi
over
K
.
Set
\pi=\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\rho
and
\widetilde{\pi}=\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes(\langle b\rangle\perp\rho^{\prime})
.
Then
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{D}(\pi)\cdot\mathsf{D}(\widetilde{\pi})
.
Furthermore, for any field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, the form
\widetilde{\pi}_{K^{\prime}}
is isotropic if and only if
\sigma_{K^{\prime}}
is hyperbolic.

Proof.

Let
Q=(a,b)_{K}
and
\tau=\langle\!\langle a,b\rangle\!\rangle
.
Then
Q_{K(\tau)}
and
A_{K(\tau)}
are split, and

(A,\sigma)_{K(\tau)}\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle_{K(\tau)})\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\rho_{K(\tau)})\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\pi_{K(\tau)})\,.

By
2.4
, there exists a field extension
L/K
such that
A_{L}
is split and
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}((A,\sigma)_{L})\cap{K}^{\times}
.
Then
Q_{L}
is split.
Since
\tau
is the norm form of
Q
,
\tau_{L}
is isotropic, whence
L(\tau)/L
is rational.
This yields that
\mathsf{G}((A,\sigma)_{L})=\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)_{L(\tau)}\cap{L}^{\times}
.
Therefore
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)_{L(\tau)}\cap{K}^{\times}
, and consequently

\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}((A,\sigma)_{K(\tau)})\cap{K}^{\times}
.
Now observe that
(A,\sigma)_{K(\tau)}\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}((\psi\otimes\pi)_{K(\tau)})
and

\mathsf{G}((\psi\otimes\pi)_{K(\tau)})=\mathsf{G}(\pi_{K(\tau)})
, by
2.2
.
Using
3.5
, we therefore conclude that

\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}(\pi_{K(\tau)})\cap{K}^{\times}=\mathsf{D}(\pi)\cdot\mathsf{D}(\widetilde{\pi})
.

Consider now a field extension
K^{\prime}/K
.
Note that

\widetilde{\pi}_{K^{\prime}(\tau)}\simeq\pi_{K^{\prime}(\tau)}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad(A,\sigma)_{K^{\prime}(\tau)}\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\pi_{K^{\prime}(\tau)})\,.

Assume first that
\widetilde{\pi}_{K^{\prime}}
is isotropic.
Then
\pi_{K^{\prime}(\tau)}
is isotropic and thus hyperbolic, because it is a Pfister form.
It follows that
\sigma_{K^{\prime}(\tau)}
is hyperbolic, and hence
\sigma_{K^{\prime}}
is hyperbolic, by
2.4
.
Assume conversely that
\sigma_{K^{\prime}}
is hyperbolic.
Then
\pi_{K^{\prime}(\tau)}
is hyperbolic, and it follows by
3.3
that
\tau_{K^{\prime}}
is a subform of
\pi_{K^{\prime}}
.
Since
\widetilde{\pi}
is Witt equivalent to
\pi\perp-\tau
and
\mathsf{dim}(\widetilde{\pi})=\mathsf{dim}(\pi)
, we conclude that
\widetilde{\pi}_{K^{\prime}}
is isotropic.
∎

4.
The main result

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be a totally decomposable
K
-algebra with involution of the first kind of even degree and with
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
.
For every
c\in\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)
there exist
d_{1},d_{2}\in{K}^{\times}
such that
\sigma_{K(\sqrt{d_{i}})}
is hyperbolic for
i\in\{1,2\}
and
c\in\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d_{1}\rangle\!\rangle\cdot\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d_{2}\rangle\!\rangle
.

Proof.

It follows from the hypothesis by
[
1
, Corollary]
if
\sigma
is symplectic and by
[
1
, Theorem 2]
if
\sigma
is orthogonal that there exists a decomposition

(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi\otimes\rho)\otimes(Q,\gamma)

with a
K
-quaternion algebra with involution of the first kind
(Q,\tau)
, a Pfister form
\rho
over
K
and an odd-dimensional quadratic form
\psi
over
K
.

Assume first that
\sigma
is symplectic. Then
\gamma
is symplectic and hence equal to the canonical involution on
Q
.
Let
\nu
denote the norm form of
Q
.
This is a
2
-fold Pfister form.
By
2.5
and
2.2
we have that

\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}(\psi\otimes\rho\otimes\nu)=\mathsf{G}(\rho\otimes\nu)
and,
for any field extension
L/K
,
(A,\sigma)_{L}
is hyperbolic if and only if
(\psi\otimes\rho\otimes\nu)_{L}
is hyperbolic, if and only if
(\rho\otimes\nu)_{L}
is hyperbolic.
Consider an element
c\in\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)
.
Since
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{G}(\rho\otimes\nu)
and
\rho\otimes\nu
is a Pfister form, we obtain by
3.1
that

c\in\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle
for some
d\in{K}^{\times}
such that
(\nu\otimes\rho)_{K(\sqrt{d})}
is hyperbolic.
Hence, we may take
d_{1}=d_{2}=d

to have that
c=c\cdot 1\in\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d_{1}\rangle\!\rangle\cdot\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d_{2}\rangle\!\rangle

and
\sigma_{K(\sqrt{d_{i}})}
is hyperbolic for
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Assume now that
\sigma
is orthogonal.
If
\mathsf{deg}A\equiv 2\,\mathsf{mod}\,4
, then
\rho\simeq\langle 1\rangle
and we fix
d\in{K}^{\times}
such that
\mathsf{disc}(\tau)=d{K}^{\times 2}
to have that
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle
and
\sigma_{K(\sqrt{d})}
is hyperbolic, so the claim holds trivially with
d_{1}=d_{2}=d
.
We may now assume that
\mathsf{deg}A
is a multiple of
4
.
We choose
a,b\in{K}^{\times}
such that
(Q,\tau)\simeq(a\,\cdot\!\!\mid b)_{K}
, whereby
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\psi\otimes\rho)\otimes(a\,\cdot\!\!\mid b)_{K}
.
Consider
c\in\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)
.
Let
\pi
and
\widetilde{\pi}
be defined as in
3.6
.
Note that
\rho^{\prime}
is a subform of both
\pi
and
\widetilde{\pi}
.
Using
3.6
we can decompose
c=c_{1}\cdot c_{2}
with certain
c_{1}\in\mathsf{D}(\pi)
and
c_{2}\in\mathsf{D}(\widetilde{\pi})
.
Since
\mathsf{dim}(\rho^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{deg}A-1\geqslant 1
, we may pick an element
e\in\mathsf{D}(\rho^{\prime})
. Since
e,c_{1}\in\mathsf{D}(\pi)=\mathsf{G}(\pi)
, we obtain that
e^{-1}c_{1}\in\mathsf{D}(\pi)
.
By
3.1
, there exists some
d_{1}\in{K}^{\times}
such that
e^{-1}c_{1}\in\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d_{1}\rangle\!\rangle
and
\pi_{K(\sqrt{d_{1}})}
is hyperbolic. Thus
\sigma_{K(\sqrt{d_{1}})}
is hyperbolic.
Observe further that
1,ec_{2}\in\mathsf{D}(e\widetilde{\pi})
.
Hence
e\widetilde{\pi}
has a binary subform
\beta
with
1,ec_{2}\in\mathsf{D}(\beta)
.
Then
\beta\simeq\langle\!\langle d_{2}\rangle\!\rangle
for some

.
We obtain that
ec_{2}\in\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d_{2}\rangle\!\rangle
and
\widetilde{\pi}_{K(\sqrt{d_{2}})}
is isotropic, whereby
\sigma_{K(\sqrt{d_{2}})}
is hyperbolic, by
3.6
.
We thus have that
c=e^{-1}c_{1}(ec_{2})\in\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d_{1}\rangle\!\rangle\cdot\mathsf{D}\langle\!\langle d_{2}\rangle\!\rangle
and
\sigma_{K(\sqrt{d_{i}})}
is hyperbolic for
i\in\{1,2\}
, as desired.
∎

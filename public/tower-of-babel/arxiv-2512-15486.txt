Completely Independent Spanning Trees in Split Graphs: Structural Properties and Complexity
Mohammed Lalou, Nader Mbarek, Abdallah Skender, Olivier Togni

Abstract
We study completely independent spanning trees (CIST), i.e. , trees that are both edge-disjoint and internally vertex-disjoint, in split graphs. We establish a correspondence between the existence of CIST in a split graph and some types of hypergraph colorings (panchromatic and bipanchromatic colorings) of its associated hypergraph, allowing us to obtain lower and upper bounds on the number of CIST. Using these relations, we prove that the problem of the existence of two CIST in a split graph is NP-complete. Finally, we formulate a conjecture on the bipanchromatic number of a hypergraph related to the results obtained for the number of CIST.

Introduction

The graphs considered in this paper are undirected, finite, and simple. For a graph

Independent spanning trees have several applications in various domains

The practical interests of CIST necessitate characterizing them, identifying the maximum number of instances a graph may contain, and proposing constructions for these trees. Hasunuma

(

A graph

A

is a graph in which the vertices can be partitioned into a clique

is the

In this paper, we continue the study of the CIST problem on split graphs and give a necessary condition relating the hypergraph panchromatic coloring problem and the CIST existence problem in this class of graphs. We also introduce a variant of the panchromatic coloring called bipanchromatic coloring to obtain sufficient conditions for CIST in split graphs. The previous conditions allow us to establish tight bounds on the maximum number of CIST. Moreover, we prove that the CIST problem in split graphs is NP-Complete and propose integer linear programming models that allow us to formulate a conjecture for the hypergraph panchromatic coloring problem.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
provides the preliminaries used throughout this paper, specifically the panchromatic and bipanchromatic coloring of hypergraphs, as well as the notion of a corresponding hypergraph to a split graph. Section
3
presents results on the number of CIST in split graphs according to the panchromatic and bipanchromatic colorings of hypergraphs. Section
4
proves that the CIST problem in split graphs is NP-complete. Section
5
concludes the paper by giving some research directions. Finally, in Appendix
A
, we provide ILP models for the panchromatic and bipanchromatic coloring problems.

Preliminaries

A hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
is a generalization of a graph in which an edge

, called a

, can join any number of vertices,

\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{P}(V)

with

(see Figure
1
). Obviously, every split graph has exactly one corresponding hypergraph and vice versa. The corresponding split graph of a hypergraph

is denoted
G(H)
, and the corresponding hyperedge in
H(G)
of a vertex

is denoted by

. Without loss of generality, we assume that every vertex of
D
is adjacent to at least one vertex of
I
; otherwise, a vertex in

with no neighbor in

can be reassigned to

without changing the split structure.

Hypergraph coloring extends the concept of graph coloring: A
k
-coloring of a hypergraph is an assignment of
k
colors to its vertices so that no hyperedge is monochromatic. A
panchromatic

k
-coloring of
H
is a coloring of the vertices of

such that each hyperedge contains at least one vertex of each color. A color that appears only once in the hypergraph will be called a
unique
color. The problem of deciding whether a hypergraph is
2
-colorable (known as the set splitting problem or Property B) is NP-Complete
[
7
]
and by extension for

. Since a panchromatic
2
-coloring is a

-coloring, the problem of determining whether a hypergraph is panchromatically
2
-colorable is also NP-complete.

We define a variation of panchromatic coloring that will be useful for the CIST problem in split graphs: If a panchromatic
k
-coloring
\varphi
of a hypergraph

is such that each of the

colors appears at least twice in

(
i.e.
,
\varphi
has no unique color), then

is called

.
The panchromatic (bipanchromatic, respectively) number, denoted
\chi_{p}(H)
(
\chi^{2}_{p}(H)
, respectively), is the maximum
k
such that
H
admits a panchromatic (bipanchromatic, respectively)
k
-coloring. We have trivially
\chi_{p}^{2}(H)\leq\chi_{p}(H)
. But the inequality can be strict: The hypergraph on the right of Figure
1
is such that
\chi_{p}=3
and
\chi^{2}_{p}=2
(a bipanchromatic
2
-coloring is obtained by changing the color of the red vertex to blue).

Most of the research works on panchromatic coloring are related to the study of the minimum number of hyperedges in an
n
-uniform hypergraph that does not admit a panchromatic
r
-coloring, denoted by
p(n,r)
. Many sufficient conditions and bounds for

have been proposed; for more details, the reader may refer to
[
12
,
20
]
.

3
CIST and hypergraph coloring

In this section, we demonstrate a direct correspondence between CIST in split graphs and panchromatic colorings of their associated hypergraphs. We first prove that the existence of
k
CIST in a split graph
G
implies that its corresponding hypergraph
H(G)
is panchromatically
k
-colorable. Then, to analyze the converse, we examine the links between CIST and the presence of colors that appear only once in a panchromatic
k
-coloring of
H(G)
. We conclude with an upper bound on the number of CIST.

Theorem 4
.

Let
k\geq 2
. Let
G=(D\cup I,E)
be a split graph. If
G
has
k
completely independent spanning trees, then

is panchromatically

-colorable.

Proof.

Let
G=(D\cup I,E)
be a split graph, and

be completely independent spanning trees of
G
. By Theorem
2
,
G
admits a CIST-partition

. Let us construct a panchromatic
k
-coloring
\varphi
of
H(G)
: Each vertex in

is assigned the color
i

1\leq i\leq k
.
By the definition of a CIST-partition, every vertex

is adjacent to a vertex of each of the
k
spanning trees. Then, the hyperedge
e_{x}
contains all the
k
colors of
\varphi
. Thus,
\varphi
is a panchromatic

-coloring of

.
∎

Proof.

5
Concluding remarks

We have shown that the number of CIST in a split graph
G
is at most
\chi_{p}^{2}
or
\chi_{p}^{2}+1
, where
\chi_{p}^{2}=\chi_{p}^{2}(H(G))
is the bipanchromatic number of
H(G)
. Moreover, from the proof of Theorem
6
, we have
\chi_{p}(H)-\left\lceil\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\rceil\leq\chi^{2}_{p}(H)\leq\chi_{p}(H)
for every hypergraph
H
, where
\alpha=\alpha_{\chi_{p}(H)}(H)
is the minimum number of unique colors among all panchromatic
\chi_{p}(H)
-colorings of
H
. As bipanchromatic coloring is a new type of coloring, it would be useful to prove a stronger result relating the panchromatic and bipanchromatic numbers of a hypergraph
H
. Specifically, it would be nice to prove that, given any panchromatic
\chi_{p}(H)
-coloring of
H
minimizing
\alpha
, pairing the unique colors two by two until no unique color remains yields exactly a bipanchromatic
\chi^{2}_{p}
-coloring, giving for any hypergraph
H
:

\chi^{2}_{p}(H)=\chi_{p}(H)-\left\lceil\frac{\alpha_{\chi_{p}(H)}(H)}{2}\right\rceil

(5.1)

To test Equation (
5.1
), we develop an integer linear programming formulations (see Appendix
A
) for the panchromatic and bipanchromatic coloring problems and the minimization of unique colors problem. We randomly generate a set of
100
hypergraphs with
n
vertices and
m
hyperedges for each pair
(n,m)
such that
n\in\{4,5,\dots,13\}
and
m\in\{n-1,n,n+1,\dots,n+8\}
. We observed that each generated hypergraph satisfies (
5.1
). We then propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

Every hypergraph
H
satisfies
\chi^{2}_{p}(H)=\chi_{p}(H)-\left\lceil\frac{\alpha_{\chi_{p}(H)}(H)}{2}\right\rceil
.

By Theorems
5
,
6
and
7
, the following result is immediate:

Proposition 3
.

Let
G=(D\cup I,E)
be a split graph. If Conjecture
1
is true, then the maximum number
M
of CIST of
G
satisfies

\chi_{p}(H(G))-\left\lceil\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\rceil\leq M\leq\chi_{p}(H(G))-\left\lceil\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\rceil+1,

where
\alpha=\alpha_{\chi_{p}(H(G))}(H(G))
.

Appendix A
Linear modeling

Let
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
be a hypergraph such that
|D|=n
and
|I|=m
. Let
C=\{1,2,\dots,c\}
denote a set of colors. Let
A=(a_{ij})
be the incidence matrix of
H
such that
a_{ij}=1
if the hyperedge
i
contains the vertex
j
, otherwise
a_{ij}=0
.

A.1
The panchromatic coloring problem

The goal is to determine the panchromatic number of
H
. In order to do that we introduce the variable
k_{p}
that indicates if a color
p
is used at least once, as well as
x_{jp}
that indicates if a vertex
j
is colored by the color
p
. These variables are defined as:

k_{p}\ \ =\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{p{1cm} p{10cm}}1&\mbox{if the color $p$ is used by at least one vertex, $p\in[1,c],$\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\\
\end{tabular}\right.}

x_{jp}\ \ =\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{p{1cm} p{10cm}}1&\mbox{if the vertex $j$ is assigned the color $p$, $j\in[1,n],$ $p\in[1,c],$\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\\
\end{tabular}\right.}

Since the value of
c
cannot be greater than
n
, let us take
c=n
. Our ILP formulation of the panchromatic coloring problem is:

Maximize
\sum\limits_{p=1}^{n}k_{p}

subject to

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace]

\displaystyle x_{jp}\leq k_{p}

\displaystyle\forall j\in[1,n],\ p\in[1,c]

(A.1)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=1}^{c}x_{jp}=1

\displaystyle\forall j\in[1,n]

(A.2)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}x_{jp}a_{ij}\geq k_{p}

\displaystyle\forall i\in[1,m],p\in[1,c]

(A.3)

\displaystyle x_{jp},k_{p},a_{ij}\in\{0,1\}

\displaystyle\forall i\in[1,m],j\in[1,n],p\in[1,c]

(A.4)

Eq. (
A.1
) guarantee that if a color is not used, then it cannot be assigned to any vertex. Eq. (
A.2
) ensures that only one color is assigned to each vertex. Also, by Eq. (
A.3
), if a color is used, it must appear in every hyperedge.

A.2
The bipanchromatic coloring problem

To determine the bipanchromatic number of
H
, the previous model can be modified by adding the constraint that each color appears at least twice in the graph:

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}x_{jp}\geq 2\times k_{p}

\forall p\in[1,\chi_{p}]

Since
\chi^{2}_{p}\leq\chi_{p}
, let us set
c=\chi_{p}
. This implies that in order to determine the bipanchromatic number of
H
, the panchromatic number of
H
has to be found first. The objective function is the same as for the panchromatic coloring problem.

A.3
The minimum number of unique colors problem

To determine the minimum number of unique colors
\alpha_{\chi_{p}(H)}(H)
among all panchromatic
\chi_{p}
-colorings of the hypergraph
H
, we introduce the variable
v_{p}
that indicates if a color
p
is unique, it is defined as:

v_{p}\ \ =\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{p{1cm} p{10cm}}1&\mbox{if the color $p$ is a unique color, $p\in[1,\chi_{p}],$\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\\
\end{tabular}\right.}

Our ILP formulation of the minimum number of unique colors problem is:

Minimize
\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\chi_{p}}v_{p}

subject to

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace]

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}x_{jp}a_{ij}\geq 1

\displaystyle\forall i\in[1,m],p\in[1,\chi_{p}]

(A.5)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\chi_{p}}x_{jp}=1

\displaystyle\forall j\in[1,n]

(A.6)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}x_{jp}+v_{p}\geq 2

\displaystyle\forall p\in[1,\chi_{p}]

(A.7)

\displaystyle x_{jp},a_{ij},v_{j}\in\{0,1\}

\displaystyle\forall i\in[1,m],j\in[1,n],p\in[1,\chi_{p}]

(A.8)

Eq. (
A.7
) states that if a color
p
is unique,
i.e.
,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{jp}=1
, then it implies that
v_{p}=1
. However, if the color
p
is not unique, that is,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{jp}\geq 2
, then, since the objective of the problem is to minimize
\sum v_{p}
, it follows that
v_{p}=0
.

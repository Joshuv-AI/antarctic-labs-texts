Study on the pore structure and CO2 adsorption characteristics of Wudong low-rank coal under the action of bacteria
Authors: Jie Yang, Xiangrong Liu, Jun Deng, Xin Yi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractInjecting CO2 into the goaf for storage can not only reduce CO2 emissions, but also prevent the spontaneous combustion of residual coal in the goaf. This article intends to utilize the degradation effect of bacteria to promote the development of the pore structure of Wudong coal and improve the adsorption capacity for CO2. Four bacteria, namely P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida and B. subtilis, were selected to degrade Wudong coal. The effects of slurry density, inoculation volume, incubation temperature and degradation time on the degradation effect were explored. The changes in the chemical structure of Wudong coal before and after degradation were analyzed using FTIR and XRD. The dissolution products of Wudong coal degraded by four bacteria were characterized by GC-MS. And it was found that the four bacteria mainly act on the oxygen-containing functional groups and branched fatty hydrocarbons of Wudong coal, resulting in a decrease in the height and an increase in the width of microcrystalline structural units. The influence of bacterial degradation on the pore structure of Wudong coal was investigated using low-temperature nitrogen adsorption. The experimental results indicated that bacterial degradation led to the formation of microporous structures in Wudong coal. The characterization of fractal dimension indicated that the action of bacteria reduced the roughness of the pore surface of Wudong coal, promoted the development of pore structure, and increased the pore volume. CO2 adsorption experiment indicated that bacterial degradation increased the adsorption capacity of Wudong coal for CO2 from 1.72 to 2.53 mmol/g. In summary, utilizing bacteria to regulate the pore structure of coal samples and enhance the CO2 sequestration capacity has important theoretical significance and application prospects.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-26146-2.Keywords: Pore structure, Wudong coal, CO2 sequestration, Bacteria, DegradationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Environmental sciences

Introduction

CO2 is the primary greenhouse gas causing global warming, and with the development of research on CO2 capture, utilization, and storage, geological storage of CO2 has attracted the attention of scholars. Coal is the main energy source and essential industrial raw material in China, and has long occupied a pivotal strategic position in national energy security and economic construction1–4. During the mining process of coal resources in China, large areas of underground goaf have been formed, which provide enormous potential space for CO2 underground storage5. CO2 could displace CH4 from goaf through competitive adsorption, not only achieving CO2 sequestration, but also effectively inhibiting spontaneous coal combustion in goaf by reducing the risk of coal oxygen reaction6. Coal are natural porous media that exist underground, and the pore structure of coal directly determines its adsorption capacity and gas storage performance7,8. During the geological storage of CO2 in coal seams, CO2 mainly exists in the micropores within the coal matrix9. How to improve the microporous structure in coal and effectively increase the CO2 storage capacity in coal seams is also a key research focus.

Microorganisms play a crucial role in coalification processes, and the bridge bonds and side chains in coal are easily degraded by microorganisms10. Microorganisms could act on functional groups in coal by secreting alkaline substances, chelating agents, enzymes, and surfactants11,12, which not only inhibit coal spontaneous combustion13–20 but also alter the pore structure of coal21. Guo Hongyu et al.22 utilized microorganisms enriched from mine water to interact with long flame coal, gas coal, and coking coal. The experimental results showed that the action of microorganisms significantly increased the pore volume of coal samples and enhanced pore connectivity. Di Gao et al.23 studied the pore structure characteristics of coal samples treated by microorganisms in the No. 3 coal seam of the Qinshui Basin. The study found that microorganisms participate in the interaction of coal seams, making the pores smoother, reducing the specific surface area and increasing the pore volume, which is more conducive to gas adsorption. Xian Niu et al.24 used Fusarium NF01 for microbial transformation of lignite. Scanning electron microscopy showed that Fusarium NF01 promoted changes in the surface structure of lignite, increasing the number of pores and cracks.

The adsorption capacity of coal for CO₂ is a key factor affecting geological storage efficiency. Traditional physical or chemical methods of increasing permeability and adsorption often come with high energy consumption, chemical pollution, or irreversible damage to coal structure. In contrast, utilizing microorganisms can green and gently alter the chemical composition and physical pore structure of coal. Although research on microbial effects on coal pores has made some progress, the application of microbial action for targeted modification of coal structure to enhance CO₂ geological sequestration remains limited. Furthermore, systematic studies elucidating the relationship between microbial action, coal chemical structure evolution, pore structure remodeling, and CO₂ adsorption capacity are still insufficient. Particularly lacking are quantitative descriptions of pore structure complexity and mechanistic derivations.

Based on the degradation effect of microorganisms, this article attempts to enhance the CO2 adsorption capacity of coal through microorganisms. Therefore, four microorganisms with the ability to degrade lignin or polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons were selected, including P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida and B. subtilis, to interact with Wudong coal and study the degradation effects. The changes in chemical structure of Wudong coal before and after bacterial action were analyzed using infrared spectroscopy, XRD, and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry. The impact of bacterial action on the pore structure of Wudong coal was studied by nitrogen adsorption. The change rule of coal pore structure was studied by fractal dimension calculation, and the mechanism of bacteria on coal pore structure was finally deduced. This study helps to reveal the mechanism of bacterial action on coal pore structure, providing more detailed scientific insights for precisely regulating coal structure and enhancing CO₂ sequestration.

Materials and methods

Materials

The coal sample used in this experiment was collected from Wudong in Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region. And the coal sample was ground and sieved to a particle size of 0.075–0.125 mm. Raw coal was pretreated with acid for 48 h, then it was washed to neutral with distilled water and dried to obtain the Wudong coal sample. Planomicrobium huatugouensis (P. huatugouensis), Sphingomonas polyaromaticivorans (S. polyaromaticivorans), Pseudomonas putida (P. putida) and Bacillus subtilis (B. subtilis) were purchased from China Center of Industrial Culture Collection (CICC) with CICC numbers of 24460, 10894, 21906 and 23830. These four types of bacteria are aerobic bacteria that can metabolize oxygen in the goaf. In addition, due to the structural similarity between coal and oil, P. huatugouensis, which can degrade crude oil, were isolated from Huatugou oilfield in Qinghai Province25. Coal contains a large number of aromatic hydrocarbons, so S. polyaromaticivorans and P. putida that can degrade aromatic hydrocarbons were also selected26,27. The Wudong coal selected in this article belongs to low-rank coal and contains the lignin like structures. B. subtilis can secrete lignin degrading enzyme laccase to degrade lignin structures28.

P. putida was maintained on beef extract peptone medium, containing 3 g/L beef extract, 10 g/L peptone, 10 g/L NaCl and 18 g/L agar in distilled water. P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans and B. subtilis were maintained on Luria-Bertani (LB) agar slants, containing 10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L NaCl and 18 g/L agar in distilled water.

Degradation experiments of Wudong coal by four bacteria

A certain mass of Wudong coal sample was placed into a 150 mL conical flask and 50 mL of culture medium was added. After sterilization for 15 min, the cooling medium was inoculated with 5 mL of bacterial broth. The conical flask was placed in a constant temperature shaking incubator at 160 r/min for degradation. In this experiment, the effects of pulp density, inoculation quantity, degradation time and incubation temperature on the degradation of Wudong coal by P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida and B. subtilis were investigated. The optimization ranges of these single factors are as follows: pulp density (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9 g/50 mL), inoculation quantity (1, 3, 5, 7, 9 mL/50 mL), incubation temperature (25, 30, 35, and 40 °C) and degradation time (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 d). Three parallel experiments were set up for each group. After the incubation, the dissolution products and degraded Wudong coal were separated by centrifugation. The A450 value of dissolution products was determined by UV-vis to indicate the degradation effect of bacteria.

Characterization of Wudong coal by FT-IR and XRD

The infrared spectra of Wudong coal were scanned in the wavelength range of 4000 cm− 1 to 400 cm− 1 using a Thermo Scientific FTIR spectrometer with a quantitative KBr pressure method. Bruker D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer was used to detect and analyze Wudong coal before and after the degradation. The diffraction experiments were conducted at room temperature with a diffraction angle 2θ of 10–70°.

Characterization of dissolution products by GC-MS

The dissolution products of Wudong low rank coal by P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida and B. subtilis after filtering through the decontamination filter were extracted with acetonitrile. The extracted product was removed from the water. The analysis was performed using an Agilent 7890 A/5975 C gas chromatograph-mass spectrometer with methanol as the mobile phase and helium gas as the carrier gas.

Characterization of the pore structure of Wudong coal

A low-temperature nitrogen adsorption experiment was conducted using the Autosorb-iQ fully automated specific surface area and pore size distribution analyzer manufactured by Quantachrome Instruments U.S. The N2 adsorption/desorption isotherms can be obtained under a temperature of 77 K and a relative pressure of 0.001–0.995. Subsequently, the N2 adsorption/desorption data were analyzed using the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET), Barrett-Joyner-Halenda (BJH), and Density Functional Theory (DFT) models to obtain the total pore volume, pore size distribution, and specific surface area of Wudong coal29.

Adsorption experiment of CO2 by Wudong coal

The high-pressure gas adsorption instrument was used to measure the adsorption capacity of coal samples for CO2, and studying the changes in adsorption capacity of Wudong coal samples before and after bacterial action. Place the coal sample in a vacuum environment at 105 °C to remove various impurity gases. Set the test pressure for CO2 to 3 MPa and the temperature to 25 °C. Divide the test pressure into 10 points on average, with an equilibration time of 60 min for each point and an equilibration pressure deviation of 0.005 MPa. Obtain the adsorption capacity at different partial pressures through testing.

Results and discussions

Bacterial degradation of Wudong coal

Four types of bacteria were selected for the degradation of Wudong coal, and the effects of coal slurry density, inoculation volume, incubation temperature, and degradation time on the degradation process of Wudong coal were investigated, as shown in Fig. 1. Figure 1a shows the changes in the A450 of Wudong coal at five coal slurry concentrations. It can be seen from the Fig. 1a that the four bacteria had similar effects on Wudong coal at different coal slurry concentrations. As the concentration of coal slurry increased, the degradation first increased and then decreased, reaching its maximum value at 0.5 g. When the concentration of coal slurry risen from 0.1 to 0.5 g, the effective functional groups provided by Wudong coal particles that can be degraded by microorganisms increased, resulting in an increased A450. But when the concentration of coal slurry increased from 0.5 to 0.9 g, the active groups reach saturation, also, the frictional force formed between the increased coal particles will affect the growth of bacterial cells30.

Effects of slurry density (a), inoculation volume (b), incubation temperature (c) and degradation time (d) on the degradation of Wudong coal by P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida and B. subtilis.

Figure 1b showed the effect of inoculation volume on the degradation of Wudong coal. As the inoculation volume of bacterial solution increased, the A450 increased and reached its maximum value at 7 mL. The increase in inoculation solution contributed to the rapid growth and reproduction of bacteria, thereby secreting a large number of active substances to achieve efficient degradation of Wudong coal. However, as the inoculation volume continued to increase, a large number of microorganisms may grow poorly under limited nutritional conditions. In addition, it was found in previous studies that during the degradation process, microorganisms could spontaneously regulate the secretion of more active substances to degrade coal under the stimulation of coal.

The effect of cultivation temperature on the degradation of Wudong coal was shown in Fig. 1c. All four bacteria could achieve degradation of Wudong coal within the range of 20–40 °C. Still, the A450 of Wudong coal is highest at 30 °C. From Fig. 1c, it can be seen that temperature has a significant impact on the degradation of Wudong by the four types of bacteria. This is because bacteria do not have the ability to regulate temperature internally, and external temperature can affect all biochemical reactions and bacterial growth activities within the cell31. Also, different bacteria have different temperature tolerance abilities. Among the four types of bacteria, B. subtilis still had a higher degradation efficiency in the temperature range of 30–40 °C.

Figure 1d analyzed the effect of degradation time on the A450 of Wudong coal. As the degradation time increased, the A450 of the four bacteria on Wudong coal continued to grow. The A450 risen rapidly in the first 12 days of the degradation process, but after 12 days, especially starting from the 14th day, the increase in A450 slowed down, and the degradation process was basically finish. The A450 reached its maximum value on the 18th day.

To investigate the active substances produced by four bacteria acting on Wudong coal, the pH values of the extracellular secretions from bacteria were measured, as shown in Fig. S1. The Fig. S1 indicated that all four bacteria could secrete alkaline substances. Following high-temperature treatment of the cellular fluids at 120 °C prior to their application for Wudong coal degradation, results demonstrated that heating did not significantly reduce the degradation activity of the extracellular fluids (Fig. S2). This indicates that the active substances secreted by the four bacteria are thermostable and therefore not enzymatic in nature. Consequently, it is inferred that the alkaline substances play a primary role in the degradation of Wudong coal by four bacteria.

The influence of bacterial degradation on the structure of Wudong coal

The chemical composition of Wudong coal before and after degradation by four types of bacteria was detected using infrared spectroscopy, as shown in Fig. 2. From Fig. 2, after bacterial degradation, the intensity of hydroxyl peak at 3405 cm−1 on the infrared curve of Wudong coal has changed32. The hydroxyl peak intensity of Wudong coal decreased after degradation by S. polyaromaticivorans and B. subtilis, while the hydroxyl peak intensity of Wudong coal increased after degradation by P. huatugouensis and P. putida. Another significant change was that after degradation by four types of bacteria, the carboxyl group of Wudong coal at 1708 cm−1 disappeared33, while a new carbon oxygen bond of phenol appeared at 1373 cm−1 on the infrared curve. The action of P. huatugouensis and P. putida also enhanced the carbonyl (1608 cm−1)34 and ether (1257 cm−1)35 peaks of Wudong coal, while B. subtilis reduced the intensity of these two peaks in Wudong coal. The above experimental results indicated that the four types of bacteria have different degradation mechanisms for Wudong coal, but mainly act on oxygen-containing functional groups, especially carboxyl groups. The increase in strength of oxygen-containing functional groups such as carbonyl and carboxyl groups suggested that the role of microorganisms may involve oxidation.

Infrared spectral curves of Wudong coal before and after bacterial degradation. (P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida and B. subtilis represent the degraded Wudong coal treated separately by these four types of bacteria).

The XRD patterns of Wudong coal before and after degradation by four types of bacteria were shown in Fig. 3a. XRD curves 1–4 represented the XRD results of Wudong coal after degradation by P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida and B. subtilis, respectively. The XRD curves of Wudong coal and Wudong coal degraded by four types of bacteria were quite similar, with a significant 002 peak and 100 peak. The fitting results of peak 002 and peak 100 are shown in Fig. 3b and f. The XRD structural parameters of Wudong coal before and after bacterial degradation were calculated36 and listed in Table 1. The structural parameters in Table 1 revealed that the effects of four microorganisms altered the microcrystalline structure of Wudong coal. The action of microorganisms increased the ratio of the average diameter of microcrystals to the stacking height of aromatic layers (La/Lc), which meant that after bacterial degradation, the height of the microcrystalline structural units in Wudong coal decreased and the width increased. The vertical distance (d002) between the aromatic layers also changed, and the degradation of P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans and P. putida decreased the value of d002. The action of P. putida and B. subtilis reduced the average number of aromatic layers (Nave) in Wudong coal. In addition, except for S. polyaromaticivorans, the other three bacteria all increased the aromaticity (fa) of Wudong coal, which may be the result of bacterial degradation of branched aliphatic hydrocarbons in the coal structure37. From the above results, four bacteria did indeed degrade the structure of Wudong coal, but there are differences in the degradation mechanisms of the four types of bacteria.

Composition analysis of dissolution products

In order to further analyze the degradation effect of four bacteria on Wudong coal, the dissolution products of Wudong coal were characterized by GC-MS, and the GC-MS curves were shown in Fig. 4. The detailed information of the detected dissolution products was listed in Tables S1-S4, and all substances were classified using a bar chart, as shown in Fig. 5. From the results of the GC-MS analysis, it can be seen that four types of bacteria degraded macromolecular structure of Wudong coal into small molecule liquid compounds, and the composition of the compounds was very vibrant, including aldehyde, ketones, esters, ethers, carboxylic acid, alcohol, fatty hydrocarbon, aromatics and heterocycle compounds. There are differences in the composition of dissolution products from the degradation of Wudong coal by four types of bacteria. The dissolution products degraded by P. huatugouensis were mainly aldehydes, ketones, esters, and ethers. The most abundant dissolution products degraded by S. polyaromaticivorans and B. subtilis were aromatics. The most abundant dissolution products of Wudong coal degraded by P. putida were aldehydes, ketones, esters and ethers. Excluding S polyaromaticivorans, the content of fatty hydrocarbon in the degradation dissolution products of the other three types of bacteria was over 25%, accounting for a relatively large proportion. Through the analysis of dissolution products, it can also be seen that there were differences in the degradation mechanism of Wudong coal by the four bacteria. But overall, all four bacteria could act on the oxygen-containing groups and fatty hydrocarbon side chains in Wudong coal, and aromatic hydrocarbons can also be degraded from the macromolecular structure of Wudong coal.

Adsorption-desorption process of nitrogen by Wudong coal

The pore structure and morphology of Wudong coal and Wudong coal degraded by four types of bacteria were investigated using low-temperature nitrogen adsorption-desorption curves, as shown in Fig. 6. The accumulative adsorption surface area of five coal samples was calculated using BET and Langmuir methods, as shown in Table S5. The BET model is more suitable for mesoporous materials, while the Langmuir model focuses more on reflecting the surface area characteristics of micropores. Therefore, the ratio of BET to Langmuir was also calculated to observe the contribution ratio of various pores to the surface area. From the Table S5, after the degradation of Wudong coal by four types of bacteria, the action of P. huatugouensis and S. polyaromaticivorans resulted in a decrease in the surface area of Wudong coal. This may be due to the microbial degradation causing some pores in the coal to be blocked. Although new pores were generated, the total specific surface area still decreased. The degradation of P. putida and B. subtilis mainly involves dissolving the coal matrix, creating new pores, with weak blocking effects, resulting in an increase in specific surface area. In addition, after the action of the four bacteria, the Langmuir/BET ratio increased from 1.338 to 1.3504, 1.3684, 1.3585, and 1.3571, respectively, indicating that the contribution ratio of micropores was higher than before degradation, that is, the relative importance of micropores increased.

The adsorption-desorption isotherms of N2 for Wudong coal (a) and Wudong coal degraded by P. huatugouensis (b), S. polyaromaticivorans (c), P. putida (d), B. subtilis (e).

According to the IUPAC classification38, the N2 adsorption isotherms of coal samples are mainly of type II. From Fig. 6, it can be seen that the adsorption-desorption curves of the five coal samples had similar shapes and no significant hysteresis loops were observed. When the relative pressure was between 0 and 0.8, and the adsorption-desorption curve was relatively stable. At this time, monolayer adsorption occurred on the larger pore walls of the coal sample. After the relative pressure exceeded 0.8, the adsorption line rose sharply, the adsorption layer increased, and capillary condensation occurred in the corresponding larger pores. The pore structures of the five coal samples were all parallel plate pores with all sides open39. Therefore, the action of microorganisms did not change the pore structure type of the coal samples.

Analysis of the nitrogen adsorption capacity of coal showed that the nitrogen adsorption capacity of Wudong coal was 4.43 cm3/g. After the degradation of Wudong coal by P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans and B. subtilis, the nitrogen adsorption capacity of Wudong coal decreased to 3.58, 3.95, and 3.75 cm3/g, respectively. However, after the degradation of Wudong coal by P. putida, the adsorption capacity of the coal sample increased, with a nitrogen adsorption capacity of 4.97 cm3/g.

The influence of bacterial degradation on the pore structure distribution of Wudong coal

Microporous characteristics based on the DFT model

Density functional theory (DFT) could describe the configuration of adsorbed phases at the molecular level and provide a reasonable and reliable evaluation of nanopore size distribution (i.e., for both mesopores and micropores)40. Therefore, the DFT method was used to analyze the pore size of Wudong coal before and after bacterial action, as shown in Fig. 7. Figure 7 shows the distribution curves of cumulative pore volume and differential pore volume as a function of pore size based on the DFT model. The distribution curve of Wudong coal showed multiple continuous peaks in the range of 10–216 nm, indicating that the pores in the Wudong coal sample were mainly mesopores and macropores. In the pore size range below 10 nm, the cumulative pore volume and dV/dD values were zero, indicating the absence of micropores in Wudong coal. After the degradation of Wudong coal by four types of bacteria, significant peaks appeared at wavelengths less than 2 nm, indicating that the bacterial action led to the formation of micropores in the coal. The curves of the differential pore volume of Wudong coal after the degradation of P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida, and B. subtilis showed continuous peaks in the ranges of 8–233 nm, 6–252 nm, 6–252 nm, and 6–233 nm, respectively, mainly consisting of mesopores and macropores. In addition, it can be seen from the cumulative pore volume curve that the cumulative pore volume of Wudong coal was 0.0616. After the degradation of Wudong coal by P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, and B. subtilis, the cumulative pore volume of the coal sample decreased. In contrast, the cumulative pore volume increased after the action of P. putida.

The pore size distribution (DFT model) of Wudong coal (a) and Wudong coal degraded by P. huatugouensis (b), S. polyaromaticivorans (c), P. putida (d), B. subtilis (e).

Mesoporous characteristics based on the BJH model

When the pore diameter is less than 10 nm, the BJH method have an error of 20–30%. Therefore, the BJH method was used to analyze the mesopores and macropores of Wudong coal before and after bacterial degradation. Figure 8 shows the variation of differential pore volume with pore size, and there is no prominent peak in the range greater than 50 nm. Therefore, dV/dlog(D) was used to amplify the value of dV/dD. The distribution curves of cumulative pore volume and dV/dlog(D) as a function of pore size based on the BJH method were also displayed in Fig. 8. It can be seen that the cumulative pore volume and dV/dlog(D) curve of Wudong coal are similar to the curves of Wudong coal degraded by four bacteria. The cumulative pore volume of Wudong coal significantly increased from 10 nm, while the cumulative pore volume of Wudong coal degradation by four bacteria increased from 2 nm, especially the coal samples degraded by S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida, and B. subtilis. This also indicated that bacterial degradation increased the micropores of Wudong coal. Similar to the DFT method, after the degradation of Wudong coal by P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, and B. subtilis, the cumulative pore volume of the coal sample decreased. In contrast. the cumulative pore volume increased after the degradation by P. putida. The dV/dlog(D) curves of the five coal samples all increased continuously with the increase of pore size, reaching a maximum value between 60 and 80 nm and then rapidly decreased. There were a large number of macropores within the 60–80 nm pore range in the five coal samples.

The pore size distribution (BJH model) of Wudong coal (a) and Wudong coal degraded by P. huatugouensis (b), S. polyaromaticivorans (c), P. putida (d), B. subtilis (e).

Pore size distribution proportions

In order to describe the changes in pore volume of Wudong coal before and after bacterial degradation, the distribution of different pore volumes was calculated, as shown in Fig. 9. It can be clearly seen from Fig. 9 that the main pores in the Wudong coal sample were mesopores (2–50 nm) and macropores (> 50 nm)41. After the degradation of Wudong coal by P. huatugouensis, S. polyaromaticivorans, P. putida, and B. subtilis, micropores appeared in the Wudong coal, accounting for 0.31%, 0.67%, 0.35%, and 0.31%, respectively. At the same time, the degradation also increased the proportion of mesopores, from 31.57% before degradation to 37.95%, 37.15%, 35.28%, and 41.50%, respectively. The degradation of four of bacteria reduced the proportion of macropores in Wudong coal from 68.43% before degradation to 61.74%, 62.18%, 64.37%, and 58.19%, respectively.

The pore volume percentages for different pore diameters of Wudong coal (a) and Wudong coal degraded by P. huatugouensis (b), S. polyaromaticivorans (c), P. putida (d), B. subtilis (e).

Currently, most research also focuses on altering the pore structure of coal through physicochemical or biological methods to enhance its gas adsorption capacity. Fei Gao et al. used CO(NH)2,MgCl2 and NH4Cl inhibitor solutions to treat coal samples42. The results showed that the inhibitor significantly increased the number of micropores and mesopores with pore sizes < 7.5 nm in coal, enhancing the coal’s ability to adsorb CO2. Satya Harpalani et al. used microbial consortium to process coal sample and found that microorganisms could enhance coal’s adsorption capacity by adjusting pore size distribution or generating new pores43. Rui Zhang et al. also used microbial consortium for coal biotransformation, observing an increase in the number density of accessible micropores after biological treatment, which led to higher methane adsorption44. The above results indicated that micropores and mesopores are the core structures determining coal adsorption performance, which is consistent with our research findings.

Fractal dimension characterization

Further research was conducted on the changes in the pore structure of Wudong coal before and after bacterial action. According to the fractal dimension theory, the fractal dimensions of Wudong coal and coal samples degraded by four types of bacteria were fitted and calculated45–47, as shown in Fig. 10. In Fig. 10, the fitting curve of lnV/V0 to ln (P/P0) was divided into two segments, with relative pressure less than 0.5 and relative pressure greater than 0.548. This was because the adsorption mechanism of coal for nitrogen was different before and after the relative pressure of 0.5, and the fitting curve exhibited different characteristics in these two regions49. Relatively speaking, the fitting accuracy for relative pressures less than 0.5 is lower than that for relative pressures greater than 0.5, as in the low-pressure zone, the adsorption capacity of coal samples for nitrogen is relatively small, resulting in fluctuations in adsorption data. The fractal dimension (D1) with P/P0 < 0.5 and the fractal dimension (D2) with P/P0 > 0.5 were both between 2 and 3, otherwise, these data is meaningless50,51. Moreover, after the four bacteria acted on Wudong coal, the value of D1 decreased and D2 increased. In the low-pressure stage with P/P0 < 0.5, the adsorption of nitrogen by Wudong coal was in the form of a single molecular layer, and the roughness of the coal surface determined the amount of nitrogen adsorption. Therefore, D1 reflected the roughness of the coal sample pores42. The decrease in D1 value indicated that bacterial degradation has reduced the roughness of the pore surface, which may be due to the degradation of some minerals in the pores. In the high-pressure stage with P/P0 > 0.5, the adsorption of nitrogen by Wudong coal changed from single-layer adsorption to multi-layer adsorption, and the pore volume determined the adsorption amount of nitrogen. Therefore, D2 reflected the complexity of the pore structure52. The increase of D2 indicated that the four types of bacteria promoted the development of pore structure in Wudong coal. Based on previous analysis results, the appearance of micropores, increase in mesopores, and bacterial damage to macropores in coal samples make the pore structure of Wudong coal more complex and the pore volume increase.

The influence of bacterial degradation on the CO2 adsorption of Wudong coal

The adsorption capacity of Wudong coal for CO2 before and after bacterial action was measured using a high-pressure gas adsorption instrument, and the adsorption isotherm is shown in Fig. 11. As can be seen from Fig. 11, after being degraded by four bacteria, Wudong coal demonstrated enhanced capacity to adsorb CO2. And with the increase in pressure, the adsorption capacity is also constantly growing. The order of adsorption capacity of coal samples after the action of four types of bacteria is: B. subtilis > P. putida > S. polyaromaticivorans > P. huatugouensis. The maximum adsorption capacity of CO2 in coal samples after the action of B. subtilis may be because B. subtilis not only increased micropores, but also reduced the number of macropores in the coal sample.

The mechanism of bacteria on the pore structure of Wudong coal

Based on the characterization of the chemical composition and pore structure of Wudong coal before and after bacterial degradation, the mechanism of bacterial action on coal pores was speculated, as shown in Fig. 12. The molecular structure of Wudong coal was determined based on the typical structural model of low rank coal and the structural characterization of Wudong coal10. After degradation by four types of bacteria, the oxygen-containing functional groups and fatty hydrocarbon side chains in Wudong coal were significantly affected, with carbon oxygen bonds, carbon carbon bonds, etc. breaking, transforming some molecular structures of Wudong coal into small liquid molecules. Therefore, due to the degradation effect of microorganisms, microporous structures have appeared in Wudong coal, which promoted the adsorption of CO2 by Wudong coal. The four bacteria did not change the pore type of Wudong coal, and remained parallel plate pores with open ends before and after degradation. The pore size of Wudong coal was mainly distributed between 1 and 100 nm, while the average diameter of bacteria is about 1000 nm53. Therefore, bacteria cannot enter the pores of Wudong coal. Alkaline substances secreted by bacteria were able to enter the pores of coal to realize the degradation of coal’s macromolecular structure, and generating dissolution products to be discharged from the pores. In addition to developing new microporous structures, bacterial activity also leads to an increase in mesopores and a decrease in macropores. This may be attributed to active substances secreted by bacteria acting upon functional groups within the coal structure, causing partial conversion of macromolecular coal structures into dissolution products, which subsequently triggers the collapse of macropores.

Mechanism diagram of bacteria on the pore structure of Wudong coal.

Conclusion

Four bacteria could secrete alkaline substances to act on oxygen-containing functional groups, aliphatic side chains, and aromatic hydrocarbons in Wudong coal. After the degradation by bacteria, Wudong coal exhibited the microporous structure, with an increased proportion of mesopores and a reduced proportion of macropores. Bacterial action can reduce the roughness of the pore surfaces in Wudong coal and promote the development of the pore structure. CO2 adsorption experiments have shown that bacterial degradation improve the adsorption capacity of Wudong coal, with a maximum increase of 47% in CO2 adsorption by B. subtilis. This may be due to the alkaline substances secreted by bacteria acting on the functional groups in the coal structure, leading to the partial transformation of the macromolecular coal structure into dissolution products, followed by the appearance of micropores and the collapse of macropores, ultimately improving the adsorption capacity of Wudong coal. The bacterial enhancement mechanism revealed in this study contributes to the synergistic enhancement of CO₂ sequestration and prevention of spontaneous combustion of residual coal in goaf, providing a key scientific basis for the development of microbial based CO₂ sequestration strategies in goaf.

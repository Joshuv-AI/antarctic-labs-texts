Phases and propagation of closed p p -brane
Kiyoharu Kawana

Abstract
We study phases and propagation of closed p p -brane within the framework of effective field theory with higher-form global symmetries, i.e., brane-field theory . We extend our previous studies by including the kinetic term of the center-of-mass motion as well as the kinetic term for the relative motions constructed by the area derivatives. This inclusion gives rise to another scalar Nambu-Goldstone mode in the broken phase, enriching the phase structures of p p -brane. For example, when the higher-form global symmetries are discrete ones, we show that the low-energy effective theory in the broken phase is described by a topological field theory of the axion φ ⁡ ( X ) \varphi(X) and p p -form field A p ​ ( X ) A_{p}(X) with multiple (emergent) higher-form global symmetries. After the mean-field analysis, we investigate the propagation of p p -brane in the present framework. We find the (functional) plane-wave solutions for the kinetic terms and derive a path-integral representation of the brane propagator. This representation motivates us to study the brane propagation within the Born-Oppenheimer approximation, where the volume of p p -brane is treated as constant. In the volume-less limit (i.e. point-particle limit), the propagator reduces to the ordinary propagator of relativistic particle, whereas it describes the propagation of the area elements in the large-volume limit. Correspondingly, it is shown that the Hausdorff dimension of p p -brane varies from 2 2 to 2 ​ ( p + 1 ) 2(p+1) as we increase the p p -brane volume within the Born-Oppenheimer approximation. Although these results are quite intriguing, we also point out that the Born-Oppenheimer approximation is invalid in the point-particle limit, highlighting the quantum nature of p p -brane as an extended object in spacetime.

-type topological field theory and exhibits topological order as explained in the previous section.

In this way, our brane-field theory provides an effective framework for describing a variety of (topological) phases of matter and can be regarded as a natural generalization of conventional Landau field theory for
0
-form symmetries to higher-form symmetries.

3
Propagation of
p
-brane

In this section, we study the propagation of
p
-brane in the brane-field theory.
We will derive a path-integral expression of the brane propagator in the Schwinger’s proper time formalism and obtain approximate analytic expressions of the brane propagator in the Born-Oppenheimer approximation.
We also discuss the Hausdorff dimension of

3.1
Plane wave and propagator

In the following, we consider the free theory, i.e.

\displaystyle V(\phi[C_{p}],\phi^{*}[C_{p}])=T_{p}^{2}\phi^{*}[C_{p}]\phi[C_{p}]~,

(80)

where
T_{p}
corresponds to the
p
-brane tension with a mass dimension
p+1

The time-ordered propagator is formally defined by

\displaystyle G[C_{p},C_{p}^{\prime}]=\langle T\{\phi[C_{p}]\phi^{\dagger}[C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}]\}\rangle\coloneqq\frac{1}{Z}\int{\cal D}\phi^{*}{\cal D}\phi~\phi[C_{p}]\phi^{*}[C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}]e^{iS[\phi]}~,

(81)

which can be written in the operator form as

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\langle C_{p}|\left(\hat{H}-i\varepsilon/2\right)^{-1}|C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dA\langle C_{p}|e^{-i(\hat{H}-i\frac{\varepsilon}{2})A}|C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle~,

(82)

where

is the Feynman’s prescription and

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}[C_{p}]^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{\mathrm{CM}}^{\mu}\partial x_{\mathrm{CM}\mu}}-\int_{S^{p}}d^{p}\xi~\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(\xi)}\frac{\sqrt{h(\xi)}}{\mathrm{Vol}[C_{p}]}\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(\xi)}+T_{p}^{2}\right]

(83)

Note that
AT_{p}
is interpreted as the proper surface-area of the
(p+1)
-dimensional world-manifold.

In order to calculate the above amplitude, we have to find the plane-wave solutions.
As for the center-of-mass motion, it is well-known as

\displaystyle\langle x_{\mathrm{CM}}|k_{\mathrm{CM}}\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{D/2}}e^{ik_{\mathrm{CM}}\cdot x_{\mathrm{CM}}}~.

(84)

We can similarly find the plane-wave for the area derivative as

\displaystyle\exp\left(i\int_{C_{p}}K_{p}\right)\coloneqq\exp\left(\frac{i}{(p+1)!}\int_{C_{p}}K_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(\xi)Y^{[\mu_{1}}(\xi)dY^{\mu_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dY^{\mu_{p+1}]}\right)~,

(85)

\{Y^{\mu}(\xi)\}
in order to show that it is independent of
\{x_{\mathrm{CM}}^{\mu}\}

Acting the area derivative, we can explicitly check that

\displaystyle-i\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(\xi)}\exp\left(i\int_{C_{p}}K_{p}\right)

In particular, the plane-wave of the volume dynamics can be identified by

\displaystyle(K_{p}^{\mathrm{Vol}})_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}\coloneq-(dE_{p}(\xi))_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}\times k_{\mathrm{Vol}}~,\quad k_{\mathrm{Vol}}\in\mathbb{R}~

(87)

In the following, we redefine

Then, the completeness relation is given by

(93)

\displaystyle D[C_{p}(t)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}[C_{p}(t)]}\int_{S^{p}}d^{p}\xi\sqrt{h(t,\xi)}(dE_{p})\cdot(dE_{p})

\displaystyle K_{p}(t)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}[C_{p}(t)]}\int_{S^{p}}d^{p}\xi\sqrt{h(t,\xi)}K_{p}\cdot K_{p}~,

(95)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{p+1}}K_{p+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\int_{M_{p+1}}K_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(t,\xi)dY^{\mu_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dY^{\mu_{p+1}}~,

The path-integral of

3.2
Born-Oppenheimer treatment

In general, the motion of
p
-brane is a complex mixture of the center-of-mass and relative motions as illustrated in Fig.
5
.
This situation reminds us molecular dynamics consisting of atomic nuclei and electrons.
In particular, when we closely look at the equations (
93
)-(
96
), one can recognize that one of the complexities of the brane motion originates in the existence of
\mathrm{Vol}[C_{p}(t)]
in each terms in the Lagrangian.
In other words, the complexity seems to be significantly reduced when we separate the volume-dynamics from the others.
Such a treatment resembles the Born-Oppenheimer approximation in molecule systems
[
34
]
and we consider a similar treatment in the
p
-brane dynamics below.

We neglect
\dot{\mathrm{Vol}}[C_{p}(t)]
in Eq. (
92
) and the second term in Eq. (
93
), and represent a fixed
p
-brane volume as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}[C_{p}]=V_{p}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad M_{p}[C_{p}]\coloneq T_{p}\mathrm{Vol}[C_{p}]=T_{p}V_{p}=M_{p}~,

(100)

where
M_{p}
can be interpreted as the “mass” of
p
-brane.
The validity of this Born-Oppenheimer treatment is discussed in the last part of this section.
Within this approximation, one can see that the center-of-mass transition amplitude is decoupled from the relative motions in Eq. (
98
) and described by

\displaystyle\langle C_{p}|e^{-i(\hat{H}-i\varepsilon)A}|C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle\bigg|_{\mathrm{CM}}=\int\frac{d^{D}k_{\mathrm{CM}}}{(2\pi)^{D}}e^{ik_{\mathrm{CM}}\cdot(x_{\mathrm{CM}}-x_{\mathrm{CM}}^{{}^{\prime}})-i\frac{AT_{p}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{CM}}^{2}}{M_{p}^{2}}-i\varepsilon\right)}~,

(101)

which coincides with the transition amplitude of relativistic particle with a proper time
AT_{p}/V_{p}
and mass
M_{p}
.

As for the relative motions, we can at least find a self-consistent saddle-path in the following way.
First, Eq. (
96
) can be deformed as

\displaystyle\int_{M_{p+1}}K_{p+1}=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\int_{M_{p+1}}\bigg[d(K_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(t,\xi)Y^{\mu_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge dY^{\mu_{p+1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt-(dK_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(t,\xi))Y^{[\mu_{1}}dY^{\mu_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dY^{\mu_{p+1}]}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{C_{p}}K_{p}(A)-\int_{C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}}K_{p}(0)-\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\int_{M_{p+1}}(dK_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(t,\xi))Y^{[\mu_{1}}dY^{\mu_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dY^{\mu_{p+1}]}

(102)

by the Stokes theorem.
Then, if we neglect the
\{Y^{\mu}(t,\xi)\}
dependence in Eq. (
95
) (i.e.

), one can see that their dependence appears only in the last term in Eq. (
102

\displaystyle dK_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(t,\xi)=0\quad\therefore K_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}(t,\xi)=k_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}=\text{constant}~,

(103)

as well as the center-of-mass momenta (
97
).
By putting this into Eq. (
95

\displaystyle K_{p}(t)^{2}=k\cdot k\coloneq\frac{1}{(p+1)!}k_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}k^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}~,

(104)

which is indeed independent of
\{Y^{\mu}(\xi)\}
.
Thus, Eq. (
103

Now the transition amplitude (
98

\displaystyle\langle C_{p}|e^{-i\hat{H}A}|C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle|_{\mathrm{BO}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{D}k_{\mathrm{CM}}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\int\frac{d^{D^{\prime}}k}{(2\pi)^{D^{\prime}}}\exp\left(ik_{\mathrm{CM}}\cdot(x_{\mathrm{CM}}-x_{\mathrm{CM}}^{{}^{\prime}})+ik\cdot(\sigma[C_{p}]-\sigma[C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}])-i\frac{AT_{p}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{CM}}^{2}}{M_{p}^{2}}+\frac{k\cdot k}{T_{p}^{2}}+1-i\varepsilon\right)\right),

(105)

where

and

\displaystyle k\cdot\sigma[C_{p}]\coloneq\frac{1}{(p+1)!}k_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}\sigma^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p+1}}[C_{p}]~.

(106)

Compared to the transition amplitude of relativistic particle (
101

) describes the propagation of the area elements

of
p
-brane as well.
Note that the result (
105

Although a true propagator of
p
-brane would be more complicated than Eq. (

For example, when

\displaystyle\langle C_{p}|e^{-i\hat{H}A}|C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle|_{\mathrm{BO}}\approx

\displaystyle\delta^{(D^{\prime})}(\sigma[C_{p}]-\sigma[C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}])\int\frac{d^{D}k_{\mathrm{CM}}}{(2\pi)^{D}}e^{ik_{\mathrm{CM}}\cdot(x_{\mathrm{CM}}-x_{\mathrm{CM}}^{{}^{\prime}})-i\frac{AT_{p}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{CM}}^{2}}{M_{p}^{2}}+1-i\varepsilon\right)},

(108)

On the other hand, for

\displaystyle V_{p}\rightarrow\infty\quad\text{with}\quad T_{p}~=~\text{fixed}\quad(\text{Large-brane limit})~,

(109)

Eq. (
105

\displaystyle\langle C_{p}|e^{-i\hat{H}A}|C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}\rangle|_{\mathrm{BO}}\approx

\displaystyle\delta^{(D)}(x_{\mathrm{CM}}-x_{\mathrm{CM}}^{{}^{\prime}})\int\frac{d^{D^{\prime}}k}{(2\pi)^{D^{\prime}}}e^{ik\cdot(\sigma[C_{p}]-\sigma[C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}])-i\frac{A}{2}\left(k\cdot k+T_{p}^{2}-i\varepsilon\right)}~,

which describes the propagation of the shape-deformations of

We should note that similar transition amplitude and propagator were derived in Refs.

In the present framework, such approximations correspond to the Born-Oppenheimer and saddle-path approximations.

Finally, let us discuss the conditions under which the Born-Oppenheimer approximation would be valid.
Equation (

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{eff}}=\int_{0}^{A}dt\left[k_{\mathrm{Vol}}(t)\dot{\mathrm{Vol}}[C_{p}(t)]-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{M_{p}(t)^{2}}+D[C_{p}(t)]k_{\mathrm{Vol}}(t)^{2}\right)\right]~,\quad M_{p}(t)=T_{p}\mathrm{Vol}[C_{p}(t)]~.

(111)

One can see that the center-of-mass kinetic energy

In order to compare several time-scales, let us use the dimensionless unit such that every quantities is normalized by an appropriate power of the
p

T_{p}

Then, a typical (dimensionless) time-scale of the volume-dynamics

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{Vol}}\sim T_{p}\left(\bigg|D[V_{p}]\frac{(k^{2}/M_{p}^{2})^{\prime\prime}}{k^{2}/M_{p}^{2}}\bigg|\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sim T_{p}\sqrt{\frac{V_{p}^{2}}{D[V_{p}]}}\sim T_{p}V_{p}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}~,

(112)

where

On the other hand, the typical diffusion time-scales of the center-of-mass and area-elements can be read from Eq. (

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{CM}}\sim\tau_{\mathrm{AE}}\sim AT_{p}^{2}~,

\displaystyle s\coloneq\frac{AT_{p}}{V_{p}}\ll L_{p}\coloneq V_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}~,

Namely, the Born-Oppenheimer approximation seems to be invalid after the proper-time becomes comparable to the size of

In this sense, the point-particle limit (

A more dedicated study is necessary to understand the propagation of

Hausdorff dimension is a measure of fractal dimension of general objects

In the case of closed

In a discretized Euclidean space such as a cubic lattice with a lattice size

p
-brane such that one random step is described by adding a

Then, we count the number of triangulations constructed by

p

C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}}
and final
p

C_{p}
as

\displaystyle n_{\chi}(C_{p},C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}};A)~,

(115)

where
\chi
represents a topology of the random surfaces.
Then, the total number of
A

p
-brane

is given by

\displaystyle N_{\chi}(C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}};A)\coloneq\sum_{C_{p}}n_{\chi}(C_{p},C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}};A)~,

(116)

where the summation is taken over all
p

\displaystyle P_{\chi}(C_{p},C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}};A)\coloneq\frac{n_{\chi}(C_{p},C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}};A)}{N_{\chi}(C_{p}^{{}^{\prime}};A)}~,

(117)

of random surfaces as

3

4
Summary

We have studied phases and propagation of closed
p
-brane in the effective field theory with higher-form global symmetries.
Compare to the previous studies
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
19
]
, we have included the kinetic term of the center-of-mass motion as well as the kinetic term constructed by the area derivatives, which then gives rise to a new dynamical scalar (NG) mode of the
0
-form global symmetry.
Within the mean-field analysis, we have shown that the classical brane-field exhibits the area law in the unbroken phases in the large-brane limit and that the effective theory is described by the simple Maxwell theory (
49
) when the higher-form global symmetries are
\mathrm{U}(1)
.
On the other hand, when these
\mathrm{U}(1)
global symmetries are explicitly broken down to discrete ones, the effective theory in the broken phase is found to be a topological field theories such as Eq. (
52
) and (
65
) with multiple (emergent) higher-form discrete symmetries, resulting in topological order in general.

In addition to the mean-field analysis, we have investigated the propagation of
p
-brane in the present framework.
Compared to the naive kinetic term employing the conventional functional derivatives, our brane-field model can be more intuitively analyzed due to (i) the separation of kinetic terms between the center-of-mass and relative motions and (ii) nice geometrical properties of the area derivatives.
We have found the plane-wave solutions of the kinetic terms and obtained the path-integral expression of the brane propagator in the Schwinger’s proper time formalism.
However, due to the mixture of the center-of-mass and relative motions, it is still challenging to perform the path-integral exactly.
As a first step toward understanding the propagation of
p
-brane, we have applied the Born-Oppenheimer approximation by treating the brane volume as constant and obtained the approximate analytic expressions of the propagator: In the point-particle limit, the propagator reduces to the ordinary propagator of relativistic particle with the mass
M_{p}=T_{p}V_{p}
, while it describes the propagation of the area elements in the large-brane limit.
Moreover, we have examined the Hausdorff dimension of
p
-brane and found that it varies from
2
to
2(p+1)
as the
p
-brane volume increases.
While these results are intriguing, the Born-Oppenheimer approximation seems to be invalid for the point-particle limit, suggesting that the quantum nature of
p
-brane is essentially different from relativistic particle for any parameter values.
A more dedicated analysis is left for future investigations.

Appendix A
Functional Derivative

In this Appendix, we summarize the basics of ordinary functional derivatives.
Let us consider a functional
F[\phi]
of a scalar field
\phi(X)
.
A naive functional derivative at a point
X
can be defined by

\displaystyle\frac{\delta F[\phi]}{\delta\phi(X)}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{F[\phi(Y)+\varepsilon\delta^{(d)}(Y-X)]-F[\phi]}{\varepsilon}~,

(124)

which is not manifestly covariant because the delta function is not.
Instead, we can define a manifestly covariant functional derivative by

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{c}F[\phi]}{\delta\phi(X)}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{F[\phi(Y)+\varepsilon\delta_{c}^{(d)}(Y-X)]-F[\phi]}{\varepsilon}~,

(125)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{c}^{(d)}(Y-X)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g(X)}}\delta^{(d)}(Y-X)

(126)

is the covariant delta function.
In particular, when
F[\phi]=\phi(X)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{c}\phi(X)}{\delta\phi(Y)}=\delta^{(d)}_{c}(X-Y)~,

(127)

and one can check that Eqs. (
124
)(
125
) are related each other as

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{c}F[\phi]}{\delta\phi(X)}=\int d^{d}Y\frac{\delta_{c}\phi(Y)}{\delta\phi(X)}\frac{\delta_{c}F[\phi]}{\delta_{c}\phi(Y)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g(X)}}\frac{\delta F[\phi]}{\delta\phi(X)}~.

(128)

Then, a general functional expansion is

\displaystyle F[\phi+\delta\phi]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int d^{d}X_{i}\sqrt{-g(X)}\delta\phi(X_{i})\right)\frac{\delta^{n}_{c}F[\phi]}{\delta\phi(X_{1})\cdots\delta\phi(X_{n})}

(129)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int d^{d}X_{i}\delta\phi(X_{i})\right)\frac{\delta^{n}F[\phi]}{\delta\phi(X_{1})\cdots\delta\phi(X_{n})}~.

(130)

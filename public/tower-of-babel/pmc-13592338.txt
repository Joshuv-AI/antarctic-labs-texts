Incidence and Clinical Course of Intraocular Inflammation Following Pharmacy-Compounded Intravitreal Aflibercept 8mg for Neovascular AMD: A Real-World Cohort Study
Authors: Jan Kucharczuk, Hubert Kasprzak, Marlena Tuchowska-Celej, Maciej Gawęcki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the incidence and clinical course of intraocular inflammation (IOI) after intravitreal aflibercept 8 mg (Eylea 8 mg – E8) injection in patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) in Poland.Patients and MethodsThis retrospective study included 867 eyes of 867 patients (591 female, 276 male) with nAMD treated with E8 at the 10th Military Hospital in Bydgoszcz between January and December 2025. Of these, 655 patients were switched from other intravitreal agents and 212 were treatment-naïve. A total of 3441 E8 injections were administered before IOI onset, with two full 0.07mL (8mg) doses withdrawn from each single-use vial through pharmacy compounding. IOI severity was graded using the Standardization of Uveitis Nomenclature (SUN) criteria, and clinical course and outcomes were analyzed over 6 months of follow-up.ResultsIOI occurred in 20 patients, corresponding to an incidence of 2.31% per eye/patient and 0.58% per injection. Symptoms appeared a mean of 3.3 ± 1.9 days after injection, with decreased visual acuity (BCVA dropped from 0.22 ± 0.10 to 0.58 ± 0.45 logMAR, p=0.0005). All cases were managed with topical corticosteroids, 16/20 additionally received sub-Tenon triamcinolone, and 6 required oral prednisolone; 1 patient required pars plana vitrectomy. Nineteen patients resolved on medical therapy alone, while one patient required surgical intervention (pars plana vitrectomy) to achieve resolution. No retinal vasculitis occurred, and central retinal thickness remained stable during follow-up.ConclusionIn this first Polish cohort study, the incidence of IOI after E8 injection (2.31% per eye/patient) was higher than reported in registration trials, though episodes were generally mild and self-limited, without long-term visual impact. These findings may help inform clinical monitoring for patients receiving E8 for nAMD.

Introduction

Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) is one of the leading causes of irreversible vision loss in the elderly population, with global prevalence projected to reach 288 million cases by 2040.1 Its treatment relies on long-term, repeated intravitreal anti-VEGF injections, and this considerable treatment burden has been a major driver behind the development of longer-acting agents allowing extended treatment intervals.

Inflammatory reactions to intravitreal medications are regularly reported for different anti-VEGF agents. Sterile intraocular inflammation (IOI), an immunological response to the drug, has to be differentiated from classic endophthalmitis, which is infectious in nature, usually follows a dramatic course, and often requires pars plana vitrectomy (PPV).2 In contrast, immunologic inflammation typically presents more mildly and rarely requires a surgical approach. Patients with non-infectious IOI are usually managed with topical anti-inflammatory drugs or periocular corticosteroid injections. Nevertheless, these cases require close follow-up and frequent visits, and their incidence after such a common procedure as intravitreal injection (IVI) warrants careful analysis, particularly given the range of medications currently available on the market. It should also be remembered that cases of occlusive retinal vasculitis have been reported for several agents, most notably brolucizumab, and have resulted in poor visual prognosis.3,4

Among available intravitreal drugs, aflibercept is a well-established agent used for the treatment of neovascular AMD at a dose of 2 mg (Eylea 2 mg – E2). Its safety profile has been extensively researched, with the incidence of inflammatory response estimated at a low level, between 1.5% and 2% per patient and approximately 0.5% per injection.5,6 The introduction of aflibercept at a dose of 8 mg (Eylea 8 mg – E8) raised concern regarding a potentially stronger immunologic reaction to this higher drug dose. Moreover, although no formal data from Poland have so far been published, numerous informal reports of a higher incidence of IOI after E8 IVI have been received from several ophthalmic centers across the country, which prompted the authors to investigate the subject systematically. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to formally analyze the incidence and course of IOI after E8 injection in a Polish cohort.

The goal of the present retrospective study was to analyze the incidence and course of sterile inflammation after intravitreal injection of E8 in a single clinical ophthalmological unit in Poland, reporting outcomes at both the per-injection and per-patient level.

Materials and Methods

This retrospective analysis included patients with neovascular AMD (nAMD) treated with 8 mg aflibercept (E8) at the 10th Military Hospital in Bydgoszcz between 1 January and 31 December 2025. All patients were enrolled in the Polish National Drug Reimbursement Programme for Retinal Diseases and were followed according to the diagnostic and therapeutic rules outlined for that purpose. Owing to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of standard procedures, the local ethics committee waived the requirement for informed consent for the conduct of the study (Okręgowa Izba Lekarska w Gdańsku, letter dated 15.07.2026). All patients, however, signed informed consent for participation in the Drug Programme.

Diagnosis of nAMD

The diagnosis of nAMD in all cases was established on the basis of the following imaging modalities: fluorescein angiography (FA), spectral domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT), OCT-angiography (OCTA) and, in nAMD cases suspected of polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV), indocyanine green angiography (ICGA). SD-OCT was routinely performed at follow-up visits during both the loading and extension phases. All patients also underwent best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) testing and anterior and posterior segment evaluation by slit-lamp at every visit.

Treatment

Under the Polish Drug Reimbursement Program, a single vial of the medication is intended to be split into two doses. This procedure is carried out under sterile conditions by the hospital’s Pharmacy Department. Specific doses of the drug are placed in insulin syringes, sealed, and delivered to the ophthalmic ward. Two types of syringes were used for this purpose: the first for withdrawing the drug from the vial (Jiangsu Webest Medical Product Co., Ltd., 1 mL/40 j.m., with DispoFine 21G/0.8 × 40 mm needle) and the second for intravitreal drug administration (Chirana 1 mL U100, with 29G × ½” 0.33 × 12.7 mm needle). Both syringe types are silicone-lubricated, consistent with standard hypodermic and insulin syringe manufacturing, and were stored unfilled in the operating room compartment at a temperature below 23°C prior to compounding. Compounded (drug-filled) syringes were stored refrigerated at 4–8°C and administered within 24 hours of preparation. All patients had E8 administered under sterile conditions in a dedicated room within the operating theater. Single-use, dedicated sterile sets for intravitreal injections were used, including draping, a speculum, a caliper, and cotton buds. Typically, 10% and 5% povidone-iodine solutions are used for skin sterilization and for rinsing the conjunctival sac before and after the injection, respectively. The procedure is carried out by a physician with the assistance of a nurse; personnel use single-use coats, sterile gloves, and masks. No antibiotics are prescribed for patients after the injection. Patients are instructed to contact the hospital immediately in case of symptoms such as pain, loss of vision, marked discomfort, or discharge from the treated eye.

Follow-Up

All nAMD patients treated at the hospital were managed according to a treat-and-extend (T&E) schedule. According to the European Union Summary of Product Characteristics (EU SmPC), treatment was initiated with 1 injection per month for 3 consecutive doses.7 After the loading phase, injection intervals were extended, depending on disease activity, up to a maximum of four months for nAMD.

Study Population

The study population included 867 eyes of 591 female and 276 male patients; in all cases, 8 mg aflibercept was administered to one eye only. Of these, 655 patients were switched to E8 and 212 were treatment-naïve. The reason for switching was a lack of further morphological or functional improvement, or an inability to extend treatment intervals. The total number of E8 injections received by this cohort before the onset of IOI was 3441; 2333 were administered to females and 1108 to males. Treatment-naïve patients received a total of 571 IVI of E8, while patients switched to E8 received 2870.

Statistical Analysis

Quantitative variables were presented as integer numbers and percentages (frequencies). Qualitative traits were described using measures of central tendency – mean and median, and measures of dispersion – standard deviation and lower-to-upper quartile values. Due to the small sample size, general estimating equations (GEE) were performed for repeated measurements. The Gaussian distribution family and exchangeable correlation structure were applied in the model where were dependent variables were continuous (BCVA and CRT). The binomial family and exchangeable correlation structure were chosen when dependent variables were binary (SRF, IRF and sPED). The identity link function was chosen for CRT, whereas the log link function was set for BCVA, SRF, IRF and sPED. The GEEs were adjusted for the study participant’s age and gender. The inclusion of one eye per person, along with the study sample size, led to the use of a robust error correction method, albeit without fitting intra-subject correlations (reserved for binocular non-independent subjects). The dynamics of changes in the occurrence of selected clinical conditions were estimated by using Cochran’s Q test (dichotomous dependent variables) or exact symmetry test (multiple discrete levels of a dependent variable). A level of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All the procedures were carried out in Stata, release 14.2 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, Texas, USA).

Results

A total of 20 cases of IOI were noted in 20 individuals treated for nAMD. That accounts to 2.31 (+/-0.51) % per person/eye and 0.58 (+/- 0.13) % per injection. IOI occurred in four treatment naïve patients, the other 16 were previously treated with different intravitreal medications, mostly aflibercept 2 mg. The incidence of IOI did not differ significantly between treatment-naïve and previously treated patients (1.89%) (4/212) vs 2.44% (16/655) per eye (p=0.80 in Fisher test). No injection day was associated with more than one case of IOI, including among paired patients who received their doses from the same split vial. All the patients were treated from presentation with topical 0.1% Dexamethasone drops applied every hour for the first three days. Additionally 16/20 eyes received sub-Tenon single injection of triamcinolone acetonide. If the regression of symptoms was noted, the dosing was tapered to 5 times daily. After subsequent two weeks the dose was tapered to 3 times daily and discontinued after the following two weeks. Six patients were put on oral prednisolone starting from 1 mg/kg body weight and tapered along with remission of symptoms. Hence, oral treatment lasted for 4 weeks in these patients. Complete regression of symptoms at two months post treatment was noted in 19 patients. The remaining patient presented with significant vitreous haze and was treated with three sub-Tenon injections of triamcinolone acetonide, administered one month apart, each in addition to topical treatment; systemic corticosteroids were also prescribed for this condition. As no improvement was observed after the third injection, with BCVA at 0.7 logMAR, the patient was qualified for pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) together with intravitreal vancomycin injection and vitreous culture; no additional antibiotic agents (eg, ceftazidime) were administered, and no corticosteroid washout was performed, as the patient was already receiving systemic corticosteroid therapy for this condition. Culture of the material obtained during vitrectomy did not reveal any microorganisms. The patient’s final BCVA post-PPV was 0.5 logMAR. From two months post initiation of IOI treatment, patients demonstrating nAMD activity were managed with faricimab without any complications. Discussion

In the present cohort, IOI occurred in 2.31% of eyes/patients (per-eye and per-patient rates being identical, as only one eye per patient received E8) and in 0.58% of injections. Interpretation of these results has to be based not only on absolute numbers but also on the severity and consequences of IOI occurrences. Although generally mild, our cases of IOI significantly impacted patient management and follow-up and were the reason for presentation to the ophthalmic center; hence, these cases should be regarded as symptomatic uveitis rather than the subclinical immunologic reaction reported in up to 20% of patients.2

When the incidence of IOI after intravitreal injection with other medications than E8, such as bevacizumab or faricimab, is considered, the numbers might seem similar.9–12 Moreover, registration clinical trials for E8 did not reveal any significant difference in the incidence of IOI after IVI compared with the lower-dose E2.13,14 Both the 48- and 96-week follow-up studies reported low IOI rates — specifically 2.1% for E2 and 1.3% per patient for E8 at 96 weeks — with no cases of vasculitis observed during either trial. The incidence of IOI reported in our cohort is higher than in the registration trials (2.31% vs 1.3% per patient). It should also be noted that the differences in severity and incidence of IOI after intravitreal treatment with E8 compared with other drugs (faricimab, aflibercept 2 mg, bevacizumab) are clearly noticeable in Poland.

A large meta-analysis of 16 studies reporting the incidence of IOI after E8 was published by Rebsdorf et al.15 The authors revealed reports of 28 cases of intraocular inflammation per 8834 injections (0.32%), all mild to moderate in severity. Interestingly, incidence rates per injection differed between clinical trials (0.15%) and real-world studies (0.93%). For example, Binder et al reported 5 IOI cases per 136 injections (3.7%), but as many as 5 per 41 patients (12%).16 Matsumoto et al published two studies on the subject. The first assessed 35 eyes after the initial dose of E8 and reported 3 cases of IOI with retinal vasculitis (8.6%).17 The second analyzed the incidence of IOI after the loading phase with E8 and reported IOI in 9 of 87 eyes that entered the study (10.8%).18

A more recent, large study by Karunainathan et al reported safety features of switching to aflibercept 8 mg during nAMD treatment.19 The authors analyzed 1429 eyes and 8675 injections, with IOI occurring in 11 eyes, 10 managed with topical dexamethasone and one with PPV. These data translate into a very low IOI incidence — 0.77% per eye and 0.13% per injection. This study, very similar in design to the present one, reports notably lower complication rates. In our cohort, also only the single patient required PPV and had negative vitreous cultures; together with the patient’s concurrent systemic corticosteroid therapy for presumed sterile inflammation, this supports a diagnosis of severe sterile IOI rather than culture-negative endophthalmitis, although the latter cannot be entirely excluded given prior antibiotic and corticosteroid treatment before sampling. Notably, two doses were also obtained from every vial in this study, a procedure authorized in Denmark, exactly as in Poland. In our cohort, IOI never occurred in more than one patient from the same injection day. This pattern argues against a vial-level etiology (eg, contamination, protein aggregation, or a manufacturing defect) and is more consistent with individual patient- or syringe-related factors.

An interesting comparison was presented in a recent study by Hoffmann et al.20 The authors compared complication rates for E8 delivered from a vial vs a pre-filled syringe. The material included 2631 injections in 453 eyes of 398 patients. Mild sterile IOI managed with topical medication occurred in 41 eyes, corresponding to an incidence of 1.56% per injection, 9.05% per eye, and 9.30% per patient. The difference in the incidence of IOI between the vial and the pre-filled syringe was significant per injection: 2.26% (vial) vs 0.21% (pre-filled syringe) (p=0.010).

The reported incidence of IOI varies considerably across studies depending on the denominator used for its calculation — per injection, per eye, or per patient — a methodological inconsistency that may materially affect the clinical interpretation of the reported safety signal. As can be seen from the above analysis, data on the incidence of IOI after E8 are diverse and significantly spread out, making it difficult to obtain a reliable overall estimate. Moreover, the way of reporting IOI incidence after IVI deserves comment: the authors of the large 2025 meta-analysis by Rebsdorf et al15 present pooled data not as a percentage of IOI per eye or patient, but only as IOI incidence per injection. Such an approach obscures the full clinical picture of this complication. Since an episode of IOI usually requires switching to another intravitreal medication and close follow-up, creating a significant burden on the healthcare system, the number of patients affected by IOI seems, in our opinion, more clinically relevant than the incidence per injection, which yields an overly optimistic, low number.

Compared with other real-world studies, our work stands out for its large number of analyzed cases and relatively long observation period, together with the Danish study by Karunainathan et al, which has a comparable sample size and a practically identical study design. Nevertheless, these two studies report markedly different incidences of IOI after E8.

Mechanism of IOI After IVI

Various mechanisms have been considered as causative factors in the development of sterile acute IOI. Among them is patient susceptibility, expressed as the production of anti-drug antibodies (ADA), typically at variable levels for different medications.21 Naturally, a higher dose within the same volume has the potential for higher immunogenicity of the drug and is associated with a higher ADA level.22 Another consideration is the manufacturing process of the specific drug and the potential for non-infectious contamination, which has been reported, for example, for bevacizumab.23 Last, but important, are external factors associated with the equipment used, primarily syringes: it has been reported that silicone oil from syringes may cause protein aggregation or conformational changes and act as a cause of sterile IOI.24,25 In Poland, splitting a vial of different intravitreal drugs into two doses is a common and standardized procedure. Before the introduction of E8, no increased incidence of sterile inflammation had been reported for other split-vial medications. Karunainathan et al also used the vial-splitting procedure and reported a very low incidence of IOI after E8. On the other hand, Hoffmann et al demonstrated a lower incidence of inflammatory complications with the use of a pre-filled syringe. These findings should be considered together.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective design and single-center setting may limit the generalizability of the findings. The cohort combined treatment-naïve and previously treated (switched) patients, which may have introduced heterogeneity in baseline immunologic exposure. No parallel control group receiving aflibercept 2 mg was analyzed, precluding a direct within-study statistical comparison. Anti-drug antibody levels were not measured, so the proposed immunologic mechanism could not be directly confirmed in our cohort. Finally, as all reported cases presented with symptomatic, clinically apparent inflammation, our results may not capture subclinical cases of IOI, which could affect the true incidence estimate.

Conclusions

In this large, real-world Polish cohort, the incidence of IOI after intravitreal injection of aflibercept 8 mg was 2.31% per eye/patient and 0.58% per injection. In the vast majority of cases, IOI was mild, could be managed with topical medications, and did not affect the final visual outcome of treatment. These findings, from the first such analysis conducted in Poland, provide clinically relevant, patient-level incidence data that may help inform monitoring strategies and clinical decision-making when considering intravitreal medications for the treatment of nAMD.

Konrad Burnik suggests a structure-preserving
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QR}}
factorization for centrosymmetric matrices, known as
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
factorization. In this article,
we obtain the
explicit expressions for
rigorous
perturbation bounds of the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
factorization when the original matrix is perturbed, either norm-wise or component-wise. First, using the matrix-equation approach, weak
rigorous
perturbation bounds are derived. Then, strong
rigorous
perturbation bounds are obtained by combining the modified matrix-vector equation approach with the strategy for the Lyapunov majorant function and the Banach fixed-point theorem. The mixed and component-wise condition numbers and their upper bounds are also explicitly expressed. Numerical tests illustrate the validity of the obtained results.

Keywords:
Structure-preserving
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QR}}
factorization; Centrosymmetric matrix;
Rigorous
perturbation
bounds; Mixed and Component-wise condition numbers; Lyapunov majorant function; Banach fixed point
theorem.

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
be the set of
m\times n
real matrices,
{\mathbb{R}}_{r}^{m\times n}
the set of rank-
r
matrices in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
, and
I_{r}
the identity matrix of order
r

is such that
a_{\alpha{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{,}}\beta}=a_{{m-\alpha+1},{n-\beta+1}}

\alpha=1:m
and
\beta=1:n

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{R_{m}}}A{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{R_{n}}}=A
, where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{R_{n}}}
is the reverse identity matrix of order

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{R_{n}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&&&&1\\
&&\iddots&\\
1&&&&\end{array}\right].

Every centrosymmetric matrix can be factorized as

(1.1)

\displaystyle A={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q_{0}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X_{0}}},

where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q_{0}=({q^{0}}}}\!\!_{\alpha,\beta})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is a perplectic orthogonal matrix, satisfying
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q_{0}}}^{T}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{R_{m}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q_{0}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{R_{m}}}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q_{0}}}^{T}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q_{0}}}=I_{m},
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X_{0}}}=({x^{0}}\!\!_{\alpha,\beta})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a double-cone centrosymmetric matrix (see
[
1
]
). The name given to this decomposition is structure-preserving
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QR}}

[
1
]
, or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
decomposition
[

[
1
]
demonstrated this decomposition by employing a Householder-type algorithm based on perplectic orthogonal block reflectors. Steele et al.
[
2

also validated this by utilizing standard
QR
factorizations on two matrices, each approximately half the size of the matrix
A
. The thin
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
decomposition is also discussed in
[
2
]
:

(1.2)

\displaystyle A={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}},

where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a column perplectic orthogonal matrix defined by
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}^{T}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{R_{m}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{R_{n}}}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}^{T}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}=I_{n}
. The matrix
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}=(x_{\alpha,\beta})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a square double-cone centrosymmetric matrix, which we refer to as an ”
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}
”-type matrix
[
3
]
. It is worth noting that both
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}_{0}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}
are centrosymmetric matrices. When
n
is even, one can obtain the factorization (
1.2

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}_{0}
and the corresponding middle columns of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}_{0}

n

[
3
]
. For simplicity, we always refer to the thin

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
algorithm can be developed using the

14

[
3
]
presented a perturbation analysis for the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
decomposition of centrosymmetric matrices, deriving only the first-order norm-wise perturbation bounds for this decomposition.

Rigorous
perturbation bounds are studied because first-order bounds may be inaccurate as they neglect higher-order terms. We continue to study
rigorous
perturbation bounds, and the mixed and component-wise condition numbers for this decomposition. This paper will primarily focus on deriving
rigorous
perturbation bounds and analyzing the mixed and component-wise condition numbers. Condition numbers play an important role in estimating the forward errors of algorithms when the original system becomes ill-conditioned. They are also used to predict the convergence of iterative methods. For the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
factorization, condition numbers must be investigated to evaluate the worst-case sensitivity of factors
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}
when the original matrix
A
is perturbed.

(ii) the component-wise condition number for

The following lemma from

simplifies the condition number computation when the map

in Definition

is Fréchet differentiable.

Lemma 1.3

Using the same assumptions as in Definition

3.1
Mixed and component-wise condition numbers

It is well-known that norm-wise condition numbers fail to adequately capture the effects of scaling or sparsity in data. When data is improperly scaled or contains numerous zero entries, norm-wise condition numbers may not accurately reflect the magnitude of errors in these smaller or zero components. To address these issues, we explore mixed and component-wise condition numbers. Considering the general case of equation (
3.1
), the definitions of mixed and component-wise condition numbers for the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
factorization are as follows:

\displaystyle m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}}(\varphi_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}},x)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\Delta A|\leq\epsilon|A|\end{subarray}}\frac{\|\Delta{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}\|_{\max}}{d(x+\Delta x,x)\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}\|_{\max}}=m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}},

\displaystyle m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}}(\varphi_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}},x)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\Delta A|\leq\epsilon|A|\end{subarray}}\frac{\|\Delta{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}\|_{\max}}{d(x+\Delta x,x)\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}\|_{\max}}=m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}},

\displaystyle c_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}}(\varphi_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}},x)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\Delta A|\leq\epsilon|A|\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d(x+\Delta x,x)}\|\Delta{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}\|_{\max}=c_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}},

\displaystyle c_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}}(\varphi_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}},x)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\Delta A|\leq\epsilon|A|\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d(x+\Delta x,x)}\|\Delta{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}\|_{\max}=c_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}}.

where
x={\rm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{vec}}}(A),
and for a matrix
A
,
\|A\|_{\max}=\|{\rm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{vec}}}(A)\|_{\infty}=\mathop{\max}\limits_{i,j}|a_{ij}|.
Taking into account Lemma
1.3
and the preceding equations, the following theorem provides the explicit expressions for the mixed and component-wise condition numbers for both factors in the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
factorization.

Theorem: 3.1
.

Assume that the conditions in Theorem
2.1
hold. Then, the mixed and component-wise condition numbers for the factors
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}
are given by:

(3.2)

\displaystyle m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}}=\frac{\||G_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}}|{\rm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{vec}}}(|A|)\|_{\infty}}{\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}\|_{\max}},\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle c_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}}={\left\|\frac{|G_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}}|{\rm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{vec}}}(|A|)}{|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{X}}|}\right\|}_{\infty},

(3.3)

\displaystyle m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}}=\frac{\||G_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}}|{\rm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{vec}}}(|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}|)\|_{\infty}}{\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}\|_{\max}},\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle c_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}}={\left\|\frac{|G_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}}|{\rm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{vec}}}(|A|)}{|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{Q}}|}\right\|}_{\infty}.

Below are the upper bounds for the mixed and component-wise condition numbers, as the matrices in (

(3.5)

Conclusion

This study has thoroughly explored the rigorous perturbation analysis of the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
decomposition for centrosymmetric matrices, addressing both norm-wise and component-wise perturbations of the original matrix. Weak rigorous perturbation bounds were derived using the refined matrix equation approach, while strong rigorous perturbation bounds were obtained by combining the modified matrix-vector equation technique, the Lyapunov majorant function, and the Banach fixed-point theorem. Additionally, we provided explicit expressions for the mixed and component-wise condition numbers, along with their upper bounds. The numerical results confirm the validity of these findings. Future work will extend this analysis by exploring the multiplicative perturbation bounds for the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{QX}}
decomposition.

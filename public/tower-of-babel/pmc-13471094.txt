SP1 Aggravates Neonatal Hypoxic-Ischemic Brain Damage by Promoting Ferroptosis Through Transcriptional Upregulation of ACSL4
Authors: Shuting Chang, Weiqing Huang, Biao Yi, Chenchao Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeonatal hypoxic-ischemic brain damage (HIBD) is a major cause of neurological disability. This study investigated the role of the transcription factor specificity protein 1 (SP1) and its target, Acyl-CoA Synthetase Long-Chain Family Member 4 (ACSL4), in regulating ferroptosis in neonatal HIBD. Using a neonatal mouse HIBD model and an in vitro neuronal oxygen-glucose deprivation/reperfusion (OGD/R) model, we applied gene knockdown/overexpression and pharmacological tools, analyzed via molecular and histological techniques. Ferroptosis was activated in HIBD. Hypoxic-ischaemic injury upregulated SP1, which transcriptionally activated ACSL4 by binding its promoter. SP1 knockdown attenuated neuronal and brain injury, reducing lipid peroxidation and iron accumulation while improving cell viability and cognitive function. These protections were abolished by ACSL4 overexpression or erastin co-treatment, and were associated with suppression of the ACSL4-dependent ferroptotic pathway. The SP1/ACSL4 axis critically aggravates neonatal hypoxic-ischaemic brain damage by promoting ferroptosis, representing a promising therapeutic target.

Introduction

Neonatal HIBD is a severe perinatal complication resulting from cerebral hypoxia and ischaemia, which can lead to significant neurological sequelae. HIBD is a leading cause of neonatal neurological impairment and mortality, with an incidence of around 2–3 per 1000 term infants and accounting for approximately 15-20% of neonatal deaths (Zhang et al., 2023). Neonatal HIBD is characterized by neuronal loss and white matter injury, and represents a major underlying cause of childhood epilepsy and other serious neurodevelopmental impairments (Thei et al., 2018). Despite advances in therapeutic interventions, the molecular mechanisms underlying neonatal HIBD pathogenesis remain incompletely elucidated, necessitating further investigation into potential regulatory pathways involved in neuronal damage.

Ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death marked by lipid peroxidation, has recently emerged as a critical contributor to ischaemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury in the brain (Sun et al., 2024). During cerebral I/R, ferroptosis promotes neuronal death and exacerbates tissue damage (Huang et al., 2022), highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target. Central to the regulation of ferroptosis is ACSL4, which plays a pivotal role in orchestrating lipid metabolism and ferroptotic cascade activation (Zhang et al., 2022). Previous studies have demonstrated that inhibition of ACSL4 attenuates ferroptosis and mitigates brain injury following I/R (Liu et al., 2023), suggesting its functional importance in related pathologies. Moreover, it has been proved that knockdown of ACSL4 confers protection against acute kidney injury, primarily through the suppression of ferroptosis and the attenuation of inflammatory responses (Wang et al., 2022). In oxygen-glucose deprivation/reoxygenation induced HT-22 cells, downregulated 2-phosphoglyceric acid exerted neuroprotective effects both in vitro and in vivo by attenuating hypoxic-ischemic-induced ferroptosis through modulation of the GPX4/ACSL4 axis (Chen et al., 2024). However, the specific role and regulatory mechanisms of ACSL4 mediated ferroptosis in the context of NHIE have not yet been explored.

Transcription factor SP1 is involved in various stress-induced brain injury models and has been shown to be activated under multiple pathological conditions (Liu et al., 2024; Zhang and Li, 2024). Meanwhile, SP1 has also been implicated in promoting ferroptosis in several disease models (Zhuang et al., 2024), though its function in neonatal HIBD remains unclear. SP1 binds to the SLC6A14 promoter to enhance its transcription, whereas baicalin inhibits SP1-mediated SLC6A14 transcription, thereby attenuating apoptosis, inflammation, ferroptosis, and ultimately ameliorating ulcerative colitis progression (Sun et al., 2025). SP1 has been proved to bind to the ACSL4 promoter region to enhance its transcription, and ischemia-induced ACSL4 activation contributes to ferroptosis-mediated tissue injury in intestinal I/R (Li et al., 2019). Consistently, through bioinformatic analysis using the JASPAR database, we identified potential binding sites for SP1 within the promoter region of ACSL4, suggesting a direct regulatory interaction. While SP1 and ACSL4 have been implicated in ferroptosis in cancer and adult stroke models, their role in neonatal HIBD remains completely unknown. The neonatal brain differs fundamentally from the adult brain in terms of iron metabolism, antioxidant capacity, and developmental gene expression. Therefore, this study aims to elucidate the SP1-ACSL4 regulatory axis and its role in ferroptosis during neonatal HIBD, providing new insights into potential therapeutic strategies for mitigating brain injury in affected neonates.

Based on these findings, we hypothesize that in neonatal HIBD, SP1 transcriptionally upregulates ACSL4 expression, thereby promoting the activation of ferroptosis, which subsequently exacerbates cerebral tissue damage and neurological deficits.

Materials and Methods

Experimental Animal Models

All animal experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations and were approved by The Affiliated Children’s Hospital Of Xiangya School of Medicine, Central South University(HCHDWLL-2023-11). Postnatal day 0 (P0) C57BL/6 mouse pups of both sexes were used in this study. The neonatal HIBD model was established based on the classic Rice-Vannucci procedure. Briefly, pups were anesthetized on ice for about 3 min until the loss of response to a toe pinch. A midline cervical incision was then made to expose the right common carotid artery (CCA), which was carefully dissected free from the adjacent vagus nerve and permanently double-ligated with 7-0 surgical silk sutures. The incision was sutured, and the pups were allowed to recover for 1 h on a warmed heating pad at 37 °C. Following recovery, the pups were subjected to systemic hypoxia in a perfusion chamber maintained at 37 °C. The chamber was infused with a humidified gas mixture of 8% oxygen balanced with 92% nitrogen for 90 min. After the hypoxic exposure, the pups were returned to their home cages and housed with the dam until subsequent analysis. Sham-operated control animals underwent the same surgical procedure, including anesthesia and cervical incision, but without ligation of the CCA or exposure to hypoxia.

Drug Administration

The specific ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 (Selleck, USA) and the ferroptosis inducer erastin (Selleck, USA) were used to modulate ferroptosis in vivo. For Fer-1 treatment, a stock solution (5 mg/mL) was prepared by dissolving Fer-1 powder in a vehicle comprising 2% DMSO, 50% polyethylene glycol 300, 5% Tween 80, and double-distilled water. Mice in the HIBD + Fer-1 group received an intraperitoneal injection of Fer-1 at a dose of 5 mg/kg per day for 7 consecutive days, starting 24 hours before the HIBD induction (Zhang et al., 2023). For erastin treatment, the erastin stock solution (1.25 mg/mL) was formulated in 5% DMSO, 40% polyethylene glycol 300, 5% Tween 80, and double-distilled water. Mice in the HIBD + Ad-siSP1 + erastin group received an intraperitoneal injection of erastin at a dose of 10 mg/kg/day for 14 days, following the established HIBD model (Zhang et al., 2023).

Morris Water Maze Test

The spatial learning and memory capabilities of the mice were assessed using the Morris water maze (MWM) test. Briefly, the apparatus consisted of a circular pool (diameter: 120 cm; height: 50 cm) filled with water (22 ± 1 °C) and divided into four imaginary quadrants. A hidden escape platform (diameter: 10 cm) was submerged 1 cm below the water surface in the center of the target quadrant.

Mice underwent four training trials per day for five consecutive days. For each trial, the mouse was gently released into the water facing the pool wall from one of four different start locations (randomly varied across trials). The trial ended when the mouse found and climbed onto the platform, or after a maximum of 60 s. Mice that failed to find the platform within the allotted time were gently guided to it and allowed to remain there for 15 s. The escape latency and swimming path were recorded and analyzed using an automated video tracking system.

Twenty-four hours after the last training session, a probe test was conducted to evaluate spatial memory retention. The hidden platform was removed from the pool, and each mouse was allowed to swim freely for 60 s. The time spent in the target quadrant, the number of crossings over the former platform location, and the swimming trajectory were recorded and analyzed.

Histological Staining Analysis

Dihydroethidium (DHE) Staining for Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) Detection

To evaluate intracellular superoxide anion production, fresh-frozen brain sections (10 μm thickness) were incubated with 5 μM DHE in a light-protected humidified chamber at 37 °C for 30 min. Following incubation, sections were rinsed in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and images were immediately captured using a fluorescence microscope (excitation/emission: 535/610 nm).

2,3,5-triphenyltetrazolium Chloride (TTC) Staining for Cerebral Infarction Evaluation

To assess cerebral infarction volume, brains were rapidly harvested 24 h after hypoxia-ischemia (HI) induction and coronal slices were sectioned into 1-mm thick slices using a brain matrix. Sections were incubated in 2% TTC solution at 37 °C for 15 min in the dark, followed by fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde. Viable brain tissue stains deep red due to formazan formation, while infarcted areas remain unstained (pale). Infarct volume was calculated using image analysis software and expressed as a percentage of total hemispheric volume.

Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) Staining for Histopathological Examination

Brain tissue specimens were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 5 μm thickness. Following deparaffinization and rehydration, sections were stained with hematoxylin for 5 min and eosin for 3 min according to standard protocols. After dehydration and mounting, morphological changes in the cerebral cortex and hippocampal regions were examined under a light microscope (Olympus, Japan).

Nissl Staining for Neuronal Injury Assessment

Paraffin-embedded sections were dewaxed with xylene, followed by rehydration in a graded alcohol series. The sections were stained with toluidine blue (Beyotime, China) for 10 min at room temperature. After rinsing with distilled water to remove residual dye, the sections were dehydrated using an alcohol series and made transparent using xylene. After drying, the sections were mounted in neutral resin. The Nissl bodies in the hippocampal region were imaged under an optical microscope (Olympus, Japan) and quantified using Image-Pro Plus software (Media Cybernetics, Bethesda, USA).

Terminal Deoxynucleotidyl Transferase dUTP Nick-End Labeling (TUNEL) Staining for Detection of Cell Death

Briefly, deparaffinized sections were incubated with TUNEL reaction mixture (Abcam, USA) at 37 °C for 1 h. After PBS washing, sections were counterstained with DAPI in darkness to visualize all nuclei. TUNEL-positive cells (green fluorescence) were observed under a fluorescence microscope, and the apoptosis index was calculated as the percentage of TUNEL-positive cells relative to total DAPI-positive cells in the same field.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) Staining

Immunohistochemical staining was performed to evaluate the expression and localization of ACSL4, GPX4, and 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE) in brain tissues. Paraffin-embedded brain sections (5 μm thickness) were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series. For antigen retrieval, sections were incubated in citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at 95 °C for 15 min and allowed to cool naturally to room temperature. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched by incubation with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 15 min. After blocking with 10% normal goat serum for 1 h at room temperature, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies (Abcam, USA): rabbit anti-ACSL4 (1:200; ab155282), rabbit anti-GPX4 (1:200; ab125066), or mouse anti-4-HNE (1:25; ab48506).

Following primary antibody incubation, sections were washed and incubated with a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody (goat anti-rabbit or goat anti-mouse) for 1 h at room temperature. Immunoreactivity was visualized using a diaminobenzidine (DAB) substrate kit, and sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. Finally, sections were dehydrated, cleared, and mounted with neutral balsam. Stained sections were imaged using an optical microscope (Olympus, Japan).

Malondialdehyde (MDA) Content Assay

The concentration of MDA, a terminal product of lipid peroxidation, was measured using a MDA assay kit (Solarbio, China). Briefly, brain tissues and primary neurons were mixed with thiobarbituric acid (TBA) reagent and heated at 95 °C for 60 min. The supernatant was collected, and other reagents were added before boiling. The absorbance was measured at 532 nm.

Reduced Glutathione (GSH) Assay

Intracellular GSH levels were determined using a reduced GSH content assay kit, and the absorbance was measured at 412 nm. The supernatant of brain tissue and primary neuron homogenates were mixed with the regents. Total GSH content was calculated from a standard curve and normalized to protein concentration.

Ferrous Iron (Fe2+) Assay

The Fe2+ content was measured using an iron assay kit (Abcam, USA). Tissue homogenates and primary neurons were incubated with acid buffer to release protein-bound iron, followed by the addition of iron reducer. Consequently, the iron probe was added and incubated for 60 min at 37 °C. The absorbance was measured at 593 nm using a colorimetric microplate reader.

Total RNA isolation was performed from both brain tissues and primary neuronal cells derived from neonatal mice, employing the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). RNA quality control was conducted by measuring both concentration and purity. First-strand cDNA synthesis was subsequently carried out using a commercial reverse transcription system (Takara, China). Quantitative analysis of SP1 and ACSL4 gene expression was achieved through real-time PCR with SYBR Green Taq Mix (Takara, Dalian, China), while β-Actin served as the endogenous control for normalization purposes. Protein Extraction and Western Blot

Brain tissue specimens and primary neuronal cells were homogenized in ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime Biotechnology, China). Following centrifugation at 12,000 g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, the resulting supernatants were carefully collected. Protein quantification was performed using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method. Electrophoretic separation was carried out using SDS-PAGE, after which proteins were electrotransferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Membrane blocking was achieved with 5% non-fat dry milk prior to incubation with specific primary antibodies (Abcam, China) including: monoclonal rabbit anti-SP1 (ab124804, diluted 1:1000), monoclonal rabbit anti-ACSL4 (ab155282, 1:10000 dilution), monoclonal rabbit anti-GPX4 (ab125066, 1:1000) and polyclonal rabbit anti-SLC7A11 (ab216876, 1:1000), monoclonal rabbit anti-cleaved caspase-3 (ab214430, 1:5000) and monoclonal rabbit anti-cleaved caspase-7 (ab256469, 1:1000). Subsequently, membranes were treated with species-matched secondary antibodies. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence detection and quantitatively analyzed with ImageJ analysis software (NIH, USA).

Isolation and Culture of Primary Cortical Neurons

Primary cortical neurons were isolated from embryonic day 16 to 18 fetal C57BL/6 mouse. The meninges were carefully removed, and the cortical tissues were digested with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA for 15 min at 37 °C. The digestion was terminated by adding complete neurobasal medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). The tissues were then gently triturated to obtain a single-cell suspension. After centrifugation, the cell pellet was resuspended in neurobasal medium supplemented with 2% B-27 supplement, 1% L-glutamine, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. The cells were seeded onto poly-D-lysine pre-coated culture plates and maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The culture medium was refreshed every three days.

OGD/R Model Establishment

To mimic hypoxic ischemic (HI) conditions in vitro, an OGD/R model was established on mature primary cortical neurons. For the OGD phase, the original culture medium was replaced with glucose-free, deoxygenated DMEM medium. The cells were then transferred to a hypoxic chamber (Billups-Rothenberg, USA) flushed with a gas mixture of 95% N2 and 5% CO2 to achieve an oxygen concentration below 1%. The chamber was sealed and placed in a 37 °C incubator for 4 h to induce injury. After the OGD insult, the cells were subjected to reperfusion. The glucose-free DMEM medium was replaced with the original complete medium. The culture plates were then returned to the normoxic incubator (37 °C, 5% CO2 and 95% air) for subsequent analysis.

Cell Transfection

The full-length coding sequence of mouse ACSL4 was amplified by PCR from mouse brain cDNA and subsequently cloned into the pcDNA3.1(+) mammalian expression vector (Invitrogen, USA) to generate the ACSL4 overexpression construct (pc-ACSL4). The empty pcDNA3.1 vector was used as a negative control (pc-NC). For SP1 knockdown, the small interfering RNA (siRNA) oligonucleotides targeting mouse SP1 mRNA and a negative control siRNA (si-NC) were designed and synthesized by GenePharma (China). Primary cortical neurons were transfected using Lipofectamine 3000 Transfection Reagent (Invitrogen, USA), according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

In Vivo Gene Knockdown

A recombinant adenovirus vector expressing short hairpin RNA against mouse SP1 (Ad-siSP1) and a non-targeting control adenovirus (Ad-siNC) were packaged and purified by GeneChem (China). The viral titer was 1.0 × 1012 PFU/mL. P0 neonatal C57BL/6 pups were anesthetized on ice for 3–5 min. 2 μL of adenovirus (Ad-siNC or Ad-siSP1) or 1 μL of erastin or equivalent vehicle were bilaterally injected into the lateral ventricles. All injections were performed 24 h prior to HIBD induction at P6 to allow for sufficient gene knockdown.

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) Assay

Neuronal viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo Molecular Technologies, Japan). Following 24 h OGD treatment, 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent was introduced to each well, with subsequent 2 h incubation at 37 °C. Absorbance measurements at 450 nm wavelength were performed using a microplate spectrophotometer (Analytik Jena, Germany).

Measurement of Lipid Peroxidation by C11 BODIPY 581/591 Staining

For cell experiments: The level of lipid ROS was assessed using the fluorescent probe C11 BODIPY581/591 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Following the respective treatments and the OGD/R procedure, the culture medium was removed. Neurons were washed gently with PBS and then incubated with complete medium containing 2 μM C11 BODIPY581/591 probe for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark. After incubation, the cells were washed twice with PBS to remove excess probe. Fluorescent images were immediately captured using a laser scanning confocal microscope (Zeiss LSM 880). The fluorescence intensity of both the oxidized and reduced forms was quantified in at least five random fields per sample using ImageJ software (NIH, USA).

For animal brain tissue: After the indicated treatments, mice were euthanized, and the brain tissues were rapidly dissected. The tissue samples were minced into small pieces and digested with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA for 30 min at 37 °C, followed by mechanical trituration to obtain a single-cell suspension. The cell suspension was filtered through a 40 μm cell strainer and then centrifuged at 300 ×g for 5 min. The pelleted cells were resuspended in PBS containing 2 μM C11 BODIPY581/591 and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark. After washing twice with PBS, the cells were resuspended and immediately analyzed on a flow cytometer. Fluorescence was collected in the FITC (510 nm, oxidized form) and PE (590 nm, reduced form) channels.

Immunofluorescence Staining for co-Localization of ACSL4 with Different Cell Types

Mice were deeply anesthetized and transcardially perfused with ice-cold PBS, followed by 4% paraformaldehyde in PBS. The brains were carefully removed, post-fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 4 h at 4 °C, and subsequently cryoprotected in 25% sucrose solution until saturation. Coronal sections of the ipsilateral hemisphere containing the hippocampus and cortex were cut at a thickness of 40 μm and mounted onto poly-L-lysine-coated glass slides. After washing with PBS, sections were permeabilized and blocked with a solution containing 10% normal horse serum and 0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS for 1 h at room temperature. Sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies from Abcam (USA) diluted in blocking solution: rabbit anti-ACSL4 (ab155282, 1:100), rabbit anti-NeuN (ab177487, 1:100) for neurons, rabbit anti-GFAP (ab178846, 0.1-1 µg/mL) for astrocytes, or rabbit anti-Iba-1 (ab178846, 0.1-1 µg/mL) for microglia. After three washes with PBS, the sections were incubated for 1 hour at room temperature in the dark with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated donkey anti-rabbit secondary antibody (ab150073, 1:200). Nuclei were counterstained with 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, Solarbio Technology, China) to visualize cellular morphology. NeuN (green fluorescence) and ACSL4 (red fluorescence) expression was observed and imaged using a laser scanning confocal microscope (Zeiss LSM 880).

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay

To investigate whether SP1 transcriptionally regulates ACSL4 expression by binding to its promoter, a dual-luciferase reporter assay was performed. A fragment of the wild-type (WT) mouse ACSL4 promoter containing the predicted SP1 binding site was amplified by PCR and cloned into the pGL3-basic luciferase reporter vector (Promega, USA) to generate the WT-ACSL4 plasmid. A mutant (MUT) promoter fragment with the core SP1 binding site sequence deleted was synthesized and similarly cloned to create the MUT-ACSL4 plasmid. Primary neurons were co-transfected with either the WT-ACSL4 or MUT-ACSL4 reporter plasmid, along with either si-NC or si-SP1 using Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 h of transfection, cells were lysed, and luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

ChIP Assay

The ChIP analysis was performed following the manufacturer’s instructions provided with the Chromatin Immunoprecipitation System (Invitrogen, USA). In this experiment, approximately 1 × 107 primary neurons were initially cross-linked using 1% formaldehyde solution and subsequently subjected to sonication to generate chromatin fragments ranging from 200 to 500 bp. Immunoprecipitation was carried out by incubating the chromatin samples with a specific anti-SP1 antibody (Abcam, USA) at 4 °C overnight, while normal IgG served as the negative control group. Following the addition of Protein G magnetic beads (Millipore, USA) and thorough mixing, the antibody-protein-DNA complexes were treated with proteinase K for enzymatic digestion. The immunoprecipitated DNA was then isolated using magnetic bead-based purification. The enrichment of the ACSL4 promoter region containing the putative SP1 binding site was analyzed by quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) using specific primers. Statistical Analysis

All experimental procedures were repeated independently in triplicate for reliability. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Quantitative results are presented as mean values ± standard deviation (SD). For comparative analyses between two groups, Student’s t-test was employed, while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was utilized for multiple group comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as p-values less than 0.05.

Results

Ferroptosis Was Induced in Hypoxic-Ischemic Brain Damage in Vivo

To investigate the potential involvement of ferroptosis in the pathogenesis of neonatal HIBD, we established the HIBD model in P0 neonatal C57BL/6 mice. Compared to the sham group, the DHE fluorescence intensity in the brain tissue of neonatal mice markedly increased after HI modeling, indicating significant oxidative stress in the HI group (Figure 1A). Biochemical assays further confirmed a pronounced dysregulation of redox and iron metabolism. The brains of mice in the HI group exhibited a significant increase in the lipid peroxidation product MDA (Figure 1B) and in ferrous iron content (Figure 1C), coupled with a substantial depletion of the key antioxidant GSH (Figure 1D). Importantly, C11-BODIPY staining directly demonstrated a marked increase in lipid ROS levels in the brain tissue of HI mice compared to sham controls (Figure 1E). Consistent with these findings, IHC analysis revealed a significant decrease in the expression of the ferroptosis negative regulator GPX4 and a concurrent increase in the lipid peroxidation marker 4-HNE in the HI group compared to the sham group (Figure 1F). Furthermore, the protein levels of the key anti-ferroptotic proteins, including GPX4 and the cystine/glutamate transporter subunit SLC7A11 were dramatically down-regulated (Figure 1G). To exclude the potential contribution of apoptosis, we examined the activation of executioner caspases. Western blot analysis revealed that protein levels of cleaved Caspase-3 and cleaved Caspase-7 were not significantly altered in the brain tissues of HI mice compared to sham controls (Figure S1A), indicating that apoptosis is not prominently activated under our experimental conditions. Collectively, these data suggest that the ferroptotic cell death pathway is activated following HI insult in the neonatal brain.

Suppression of Ferroptosis Relieved Hypoxic-Ischemic Brain Damage in Vivo

To further elucidate the functional role of ferroptosis in HIBD, we administered the specific ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 (5 mg/kg) for 7 consecutive days, starting 24 h before HIBD induction. In the hidden platform trials of the Morris water maze, mice in the HI group exhibited significantly prolonged escape latencies compared to the sham group (Figure 2A and B), indicating severe learning and memory impairment. Treatment with Fer-1 remarkably shortened the escape latency in HI mice (Figure 2A and B), suggesting a restoration of cognitive function. During the probe trial, HI mice spent prominently less time in the target quadrant and showed fewer platform crossings (Figure 2C and D). In contrast, Fer-1-treated HI mice spent more time in the target quadrant and crossed the former platform location more frequently (Figure 2C and D), demonstrating that ferroptosis inhibition improved the learning ability of HI mice. Consistent with the behavioral improvements, TTC staining revealed extensive cerebral infarction in the HI group, which was significantly attenuated by Fer-1 treatment (Figure 2E). Histopathological examination showed severe tissue damage in the HI group, while these pathological changes were markedly alleviated in the Fer-1-treated group (Figure 2F). Furthermore, compared with the sham group, the HI group showed significant neuropathological changes in the dentate gyrus, including neuronal loss, nuclear atrophy, or complete disappearance (Figure 2G). Fer-1 treatment effectively mitigated neuronal injury and increased the survival rate of Nissl-positive neurons (Figure 2G). The TUNEL assay revealed a significant increase in apoptotic cells in the HI group compared to the sham group (Figure 2H). Importantly, Fer-1 administration dramatically reduced the number of TUNEL-positive cells following HI injury (Figure 2H). In summary, inhibition of ferroptosis can protect against HIBD, rescue damaged brain tissue and neuronal death, thereby underscoring the critical contribution of ferroptosis to HIBD pathogenesis.

ACSL4 is Upregulated in Hypoxic-Ischemic Brain Damage in Vivo and in Vitro

Given the critical role of ACSL4 in ferroptosis, we investigated its expression following HI insult. In the neonatal HIBD model, both mRNA and protein levels of ACSL4 were significantly elevated in the brain tissues of the HI group compared to the sham group (Figure 3A and B). IHC staining further corroborated these findings, revealing a substantial increase in ACSL4-positive cells in the brain tissues of HI mice (Figure 3C). To identify the cellular source of ACSL4 upregulation, we performed immunofluorescence co-staining. The results demonstrated that ACSL4 was predominantly co-localized with the neuronal marker NeuN in the brain tissues of HI mice, indicating that its expression was increased specifically in neurons following injury (Figure 3D). As shown in Supplementary Figure S1B, ACSL4 expression was very low in GFAP-positive astrocytes and Iba-1-positive microglia, further confirming that HI-induced ACSL4 upregulation is largely neuron-specific. To validate these observations in a controlled cellular environment, we subjected primary neurons to OGD/R. Consistent with the in vivo data, the expression of ACSL4 was obviously up-regulated at both the transcriptional and translational levels in primary neurons upon OGD/R treatment compared to the normoxic control group (Figure 3E and F). Collectively, these results from in vivo and in vitro models consistently demonstrate that HI injury induces a marked up-regulation of ACSL4, suggesting a potential involvement of ACSL4 in the pathogenesis of HIBD.

SP1 Binds to the ACSL4 Promoter and Thereby Activates Its Transcription and Expression

To investigate the upstream regulatory mechanism of ACSL4 up-regulation following HI injury, we focused on the transcription factor SP1. In primary neurons subjected to OGD/R, both the mRNA and protein expression levels of SP1 were significantly increased compared to the control group (Figure 4A and B), suggesting its potential role in the cellular response to injury. To determine whether SP1 regulates ACSL4 expression, we knocked down SP1 using specific siRNA (siSP1) in primary neurons. The efficiency of knockdown was confirmed by a significant decrease in SP1 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels (Figure 4C and D). Notably, SP1 knockdown also led to a marked reduction in ACSL4 expression (Figure 4C and D), indicating that SP1 is a positive regulator of ACSL4. Moreover, knockdown of SP1 remarkably reduced the luciferase activity of the wt-ACSL4 promoter reporter but had no significant effect on the mut-ACSL4 promoter reporter (Figure 4E). Using an SP1-specific antibody, we observed significant enrichment of the ACSL4 promoter region containing the putative binding site compared to the IgG control (Figure 4F), confirming that SP1 directly bound to the ACSL4 promoter in primary neurons. In summary, these findings demonstrate that HI stress induces SP1 expression, which in turn directly binds to the ACSL4 promoter to transcriptionally activate its expression, establishing a direct regulatory axis between SP1 and ACSL4.

SP1 Knockdown Alleviates OGD/R-Induced Neuronal Damage by Inhibiting ASCL4

To establish whether ACSL4 functions as a key downstream effector of SP1 in promoting ferroptosis, we first validated the efficiency of ACSL4 overexpression. Transduction of primary neurons with a pc-ACSL4 lentiviral vector resulted in a significant increase in ACSL4 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels compared to the pc-NC control (Figure 5A and B). In neurons subjected to OGD/R, knockdown of SP1 significantly attenuated ferroptotic events, as evidenced by reduced lipid ROS accumulation (Figure 5C), decreased levels of MDA (Figure 5D) and Fe2+ (Figure 5E), and increased GSH content (Figure 5F). Concurrently, the down-regulation of the anti-ferroptotic proteins GPX4 and SLC7A11 induced by OGD/R was markedly reversed by SP1 knockdown (Figure 5G). Crucially, these protective effects were largely abolished when ACSL4 was over-expressed concurrently with SP1 knockdown, as indicated by the restoration of high lipid peroxidation, iron accumulation, GSH depletion, and reduced GPX4/SLC7A11 expression (Figure 5C–G). Additionally, SP1 knockdown significantly improved cell viability (Figure 5H) and reduced the rate of cell death (Figure 5I) following OGD/R, while the beneficial effects of SP1 knockdown were significantly counteracted by the forced expression of ACSL4 (Figure 5H and I). Taken together, these results indicate that SP1 knockdown alleviates OGD/R-induced neuronal damage by inhibiting ASCL4 mediated ferroptosis.

The SP1/ACSL4 Axis Regulates Ferroptosis in Hypoxic-Ischemic Brain Damage in Vivo

To further validate the role of the SP1/ACSL4 pathway in regulating ferroptosis in vivo, we intracerebroventricularly injected an adenovirus expressing short hairpin RNA targeting SP1 (Ad-siSP1) into neonatal mice prior to HIBD induction. As shown in Figure 6A, DHE staining revealed a significant increase in ROS in the HI group compared to the sham controls, which was markedly attenuated by Ad-siSP1 treatment. Consistent with this, SP1 knockdown significantly reduced the levels of MDA (Figure 6B) and Fe2+ (Figure 6C), while restoring the content of GSH (Figure 6D) in brain tissues subjected to HI injury. Immunohistochemical staining further supported these findings. HI injury led to a pronounced decrease in GPX4-positive cells and a notable increase in 4-HNE-positive cells, and these effects were effectively reversed by SP1 knockdown (Figure 6E). Western blot analysis corroborated these results, showing that the down-regulation of GPX4 and SLC7A11 protein expression induced by HI was significantly prevented in Ad-siSP1-treated mice (Figure 6F). These convergent lines of experimental evidence collectively established that knockdown of SP1 alleviates ferroptosis, thereby confirming that the SP1/ACSL4 axis plays a critical regulatory role in HIBD.

SP1 Silencing Relieved Brain Tissue Impairment Induced by Ferroptosis in Vivo

To definitively establish that the neuroprotective effect of SP1 knockdown is mediated through the inhibition of ferroptosis, P0 newborn C57BL/6 mice were injected with Ad-siSP1 into both lateral ventricles under low-temperature anesthesia, in combination with iron death inducer erastin to establish the HIBD model on P6. Behavioral assessment using the Morris water maze demonstrated that SP1 knockdown significantly improved cognitive function after HI injury, as indicated by shortened escape latencies during training sessions and increased time spent in the target quadrant along with more platform crossings during the probe trial. Importantly, these beneficial effects were substantially abolished by concurrent treatment with erastin (Figure 7A–D). Consistent with the behavioral results, TTC staining showed that the reduction in cerebral infarction volume achieved by Ad-siSP1 treatment was partially reversed upon erastin administration (Figure 7E). In addition, knocking down SP1 significantly reduced brain tissue damage in HI mice, while combining erastin treatment led to further deterioration of brain tissue (Figure 7F). At the cellular level, the increased survival of Nissl-positive neurons afforded by SP1 knockdown was markedly reduced by erastin co-treatment (Figure 7G). Furthermore, the significant decrease in TUNEL-positive cells observed in the HI+Ad-siSP1 group was partially reversed when erastin was administered (Figure 7H). These in vivo rescue experiments provide compelling evidence that inhibiting the SP1/ASCL4 signaling axis alleviates HI brain injury by suppressing ferroptosis.

Discussion

Neonatal HIBD remains a leading cause of neurological disability in children, with limited therapeutic options beyond hypothermia, which is only partially effective (Xiong et al., 2022). The pathogenesis of neonatal hypoxic-ischemic brain damage (HIBD) is complex and multifactorial, characterized by free radical production, ion imbalance, excitotoxicity, and inflammatory responses that ultimately drive neuronal cell death (Jiang et al., 2020). The intricate mechanisms underlying neuronal death following HI insult are not fully elucidated, hindering the development of novel neuroprotective strategies. Our study provides compelling evidence that identifies the transcription factor SP1 and its downstream target ACSL4 as critical drivers of ferroptosis, a form of regulated cell death, in the pathogenesis of neonatal HIBD. The key findings are that ferroptosis is activated in HIBD, that SP1 transcriptionally upregulates ACSL4 expression under HI conditions, and that the SP1/ACSL4 axis exacerbates neuronal injury primarily by promoting ferroptosis, both in vitro and in vivo.

Our data robustly confirm the significant involvement of ferroptosis in HIBD. We observed classic hallmarks in the HI-injured neonatal mouse brain, including iron accumulation, lipid peroxidation, glutathione depletion, and downregulation of GPX4 and SLC7A11, consistent with previous reports in HIBD rats (Tan et al., 2023) and other neurological disorders (Wang et al., 2023). Importantly, administration of the specific ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 conferred substantial neuroprotection, ameliorating cognitive deficits, reducing infarct volume, and attenuating neuronal death. This finding corroborates the role of ferroptosis in brain I/R injury (Wang et al., 2021) and solidifies its pathogenic significance in the developing brain, highlighting it as a promising therapeutic target for HIBD. However, pharmacological inhibition of ferroptosis with Fer-1 significantly alleviated HI-induced brain damage, the rescue was not complete, suggesting that other regulated cell death (RCD) pathways may operate in parallel with ferroptosis in our neonatal HIBD model. Apoptosis is a well-established contributor to neonatal hypoxic-ischemic injury, particularly during the early reperfusion phase, driven by both intrinsic (mitochondrial) and extrinsic (death receptor) pathways (Thornton et al., 2017). While our data showed no significant activation of cleaved Caspase-3/7 in whole-brain lysates at 24 h post-HI, we cannot rule out a minor but functionally relevant role of apoptosis at different time points or within specific cell populations. Necroptosis, mediated by the RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL axis, has been increasingly recognized as a key player in cerebral ischemia (Zhang et al., 2017). Notably, crosstalk between necroptosis and ferroptosis has been reported, where RIPK3 can enhance lipid peroxidation (Müller et al., 2017). Therefore, residual damage following Fer-1 treatment may be partly attributable to concurrent necroptosis. Autophagy-dependent cell death represents another intriguing possibility. Autophagy can promote ferroptosis by facilitating ferritinophagy or GPX4 degradation (Hou et al., 2016). It is plausible that Fer-1 interrupts the lipid peroxidation arm of ferroptosis but fails to block upstream autophagic signals that could contribute to cell death through other mechanisms. Future studies using genetic or pharmacological inhibition of these pathways in combination with Fer-1 are warranted to dissect their individual and collective contributions to neonatal HIBD.

The central discovery of this study is the identification of the SP1/ACSL4 signalling axis as a key regulatory mechanism of ferroptosis in HIBD. We demonstrated that HI stress induces the expression of the transcription factor SP1, which in turn directly binds to the promoter of ACSL4 to enhance its transcription. ACSL4, an enzyme that preferentially incorporates polyunsaturated fatty acids into membrane phospholipids, is a well-established determinant of cellular sensitivity to ferroptosis (Tomitsuka et al., 2023). Our results are consistent with reports showing that ACSL4 is a key mediator of ferroptosis in other disease models, such as Parkinson’s disease, multiple sclerosis and so on (Jia et al., 2023). SP1 induces ACSL4 expression after hypoxia by directly binding to its promoter and activating transcription, a mechanism that is also active under normoxic conditions in human colon carcinoma cell line Caco-2 (Li et al., 2019). SP1 exacerbates osteoarthritis progression by activating ACSL4 transcription, which in turn drives chondrocyte ferroptosis (He et al., 2023). The novelty of our work lies in establishing SP1 as a critical upstream regulator of ACSL4 specifically in neurons of the developing brain. The rescue experiments, wherein the neuroprotective effects of SP1 knockdown were effectively reversed by ACSL4 overexpression or erastin treatment, provide definitive evidence that SP1 exacerbates neuronal injury predominantly by activating ACSL4-dependent ferroptosis. This SP1/ACSL4 pathway represents a novel transcriptional regulatory circuit controlling ferroptotic death in HIBD.

We found that intracerebral knockdown of SP1 significantly mitigated ferroptosis and alleviated brain injury in neonatal mice subjected to HI, as evidenced by improved functional outcomes and reduced histological damage. Crucially, the in vivo rescue experiment using the ferroptosis inducer erastin abolished the protective effect of SP1 knockdown, providing compelling evidence that the SP1/ACSL4 axis functions primarily through ferroptosis to aggravate HIBD. This finding positions the SP1/ACSL4 axis as a central pathological pathway, suggesting that its inhibition could be a viable therapeutic strategy. Notably, ACSL4 also exacerbates ischemic stroke by promoting microglia-mediated neuroinflammation (Cui et al., 2021). While previous studies have hinted at SP1’s role in cell death and ACSL4’s function in ferroptosis, our study is the first to integrate these components into a coherent signaling cascade specifically responsible for driving neuronal loss after neonatal HI.

Beyond targeting the SP1/ACSL4 transcriptional axis, directly inhibiting ferroptosis using pharmacological agents represents a complementary therapeutic strategy for neonatal HIBD. Iron chelators, such as deferoxamine (DFO), have been extensively studied in preclinical models of cerebral ischemia (Wang et al., 2025). DFO is believed to exert neuroprotection by chelating free iron, thereby reducing Fenton chemistry-mediated lipid peroxidation. Notably, DFO has already entered clinical trials for intracerebral hemorrhage and neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, supporting its feasibility for clinical translation (Wang et al., 2025). However, challenges remain, including its short plasma half-life, poor blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeability, and potential systemic iron deficiency with long-term use (Aldè et al., 2024). Other ferroptosis-specific inhibitors, including liproxstatin-1 (Lip-1) and ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1), act as radical-trapping antioxidants that directly inhibit lipid peroxidation (Zilka et al., 2017). While these agents show potent neuroprotective effects in rodent HIBD models, their clinical application is currently limited by unfavorable pharmacokinetic properties and lack of FDA approval for human use (Devisscher et al., 2018). Combination strategies may represent a promising direction. Given that Fer-1 only partially rescued brain damage in our model, combining a ferroptosis inhibitor with an agent targeting another cell death pathway could theoretically achieve more complete neuroprotection (Fu et al., 2024). Future studies are warranted to optimize dosing regimens, improve BBB penetration, and evaluate long-term safety profiles of these inhibitors in clinically relevant large-animal models.

In conclusion, our study unveils a novel pathogenic mechanism in neonatal HIBD, wherein the SP1/ACSL4 axis acts as a critical amplifier of ferroptotic cell death, leading to neuronal loss and neurological deficits. We establish, for the first time, that this axis is operational in neonatal HIBD, distinct from adult models. These findings not only deepen our understanding of the molecular events following HI insult but also identify SP1 and ACSL4 as potential new targets for neuroprotective intervention. A limitation of this study is that the therapeutic window for targeting this pathway after HI insult remains to be fully determined. Future research should focus on developing clinically feasible inhibitors, such as small molecule compounds or specific antagonists targeting SP1, and evaluating their efficacy in combination with therapeutic hypothermia. Targeting the SP1/ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis pathway may represent a promising avenue for developing much-needed adjunctive therapies to improve the long-term outcomes of neonates affected by HIBD.

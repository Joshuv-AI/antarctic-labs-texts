Finite Langlands correspondence
Naoki Imai

Abstract
We construct the Langlands correspondence for connected reductive groups over finite fields, which we call the finite Langlands correspondence. We discuss also its relation with the categorical local Langlands correspondence.



1
Introduction

In
[
IV25
]
, we formulate
the Langlands correspondence for connected reductive groups over finite fields, which we call the finite Langlands correspondence, introducing Langlands parameters for connected reductive groups over finite fields.
In the
\GL_{n}
-case, this correspondence is essentially due to Macdonald
[
Mac80
]
. See also
[
Aub25
]
for a more relevant description.
In
[
Col25
]
, the finite Langlands correspondence is constructed for
\SL_{n}
, and its relation to the local Langlands correspondence is studied in details.
In this paper we construct the finite Langlands correspondence.

Let
G
be a reductive group over a finite field
k
.
Let
\ell
be a prime different from the characteristic of
k
. We write
\Rep(G(k))
for the category of finite dimensional representations of
G(k)
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.
Actually we first show the following theorem on a categorical equivalence.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

We fix a Whittaker datum of
G^{\circ}
.
Then there is a natural equivalence

\Rep(G(k))\cong\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{o}}\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{c}}\bigoplus_{\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{\mathbf{c},\Omega_{\mathbf{c}}}^{\circ}}\Sh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\tau_{\beta}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}})^{\Omega_{\mathbf{c},\beta}}

of abelian categories, where
\mathfrak{o}
runs thorough the semisimple parameters of
G
,
\mathfrak{c}
runs thorough the unipotent parameters of
G
with respect to
\mathfrak{o}
.
Here
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathbf{c},\Omega_{\mathbf{c}}}^{\circ}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
are a finite set and a finite group determined from
\mathfrak{c}
,
and
\Sh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\tau_{\beta}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}})^{\Omega_{\mathbf{c},\beta}}
is a category of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
-equivarinat sheaves on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
with equivariant structure with respect to an action of a finite group
\Omega_{\mathbf{c},\beta}
.

Because of the importance of disconnected groups in Langlands program (
cf.
[
Kal22
]
), we include disconnected groups in Theorem
4.5
as well.
A key input is Proposition
4.1
, where we describe the category of unipotent representations of a connected reductive group.
Then we construct the finite Langlands correspondence in
Theorem
5.3
using Theorem
4.5
.
The categorical equivalence in Proposition
4.1
is crucial even if one is finally interested only in the isomorphism classes (for example, the finite Langlands correspondence) by the following reason:
A parametrizatin of irreducible representations of
G(k)
is reduced to that of unipotent representations of a disconnected group involving an endoscopic group.
To extend the parametrization of unipotent representations from a connected group to a disconnected group canonically, we need functoriality of the categorial equivalence in Proposition
4.1
, which we can not see from a bijection between sets of isomorphism classes.

Using Theorem
4.5
, we also deduce a parametrizatin of irreducible representations of
G(k)
in the style of
[
Lus84
]
in Theorem
4.12
. In
[
Lus84
, (13.2.1)]
, the center of
G
is assumed to be connected. See
[
Lus88
]
,
[
DM90
]
and
[
Lus08
]
for the general case.
Our result is new if the center of
G
is disconnected in the sense that the parametrization is uniquely rigidified. This is possible thanks to functoriality of the categorial equivalence in Proposition
4.1
as explained above.

In Section
6
, we formulate a conjecture relating the finite Langlands correspondence with the categorical local Langlands correspondence.

2
Whittaker datum

We recall a definition of Whittaker datum.
Let
p
be a prime number.
In this section,

F
is a non-archimedean local field of residue characteristic
p
or a finite field of characteristic
p
.
Let
G
be a connected quasi-split reductive group over
F
.
Let
\Lambda
be a ring.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
.
A smooth character
\psi\colon R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)(F)\to\mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}
is called generic if it factors through
R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)^{\mathrm{ab}}(F)
and the stabilizer of
\psi
in
G(F)
is
Z(G)(F)R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)(F)
.

If
\psi\colon R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)(F)\to\mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}
is a generic character and there is an injective homomorphism
\Image(\psi)\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{\times}
, then the obtained homomorphism
R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)(F)\to\Lambda^{\times}
is also called a generic character.

Remark 2.2
.

If
F
is non-archimedean local field, any smooth character
R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)(F)\to\mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}
factors through
R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)^{\mathrm{ab}}(F)
by
[
BH02
, 4.1 Theorem]
.
This may not be true in general when
\lvert F\rvert\leq 3
(cf.
[
How74
, Lemma 7]
).

Definition 2.3
.

A Whittaker datum of
G
is a
G(F)
-conjugacy class of
(B,\psi)
, where

B
is a Borel subgroup of
G
, and
\psi
is a generic character of
R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)(F)
.

We recall the following well-known fact (
cf.
[
DR10
, §3]
):

Proposition 2.4
.

The set of Whittaker data of
G
forms a
G^{\ad}(F)/G(F)
-torsor.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the conjugate action of
G^{\ad}(F)
is transitive on the set of Whittaker data of
G
. Therefore we may assume that
G
is adjoint.

Let
B
be a Borel subgroup. We take a maxmal torus
T
of
G
contained in
B
. It suffices to show that the generic characters of
R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)(F)
are conjugate under the action of
T(F)
.
Let
\Delta
be the set of simple roots of
G_{F^{\mathrm{sep}}}
with respect to
B_{F^{\mathrm{sep}}}
and
T_{F^{\mathrm{sep}}}
.
We take a representative
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in\Delta
of
\Gamma_{F}\backslash\Delta
.
For
1\leq i\leq m
, let
F_{i}
be the finite separable extension of
F
such that
\Gamma_{F_{i}}
is the stabilizer of
\alpha_{i}
in
\Gamma_{F}
. Then we have an isomorphism

R_{\mathrm{u}}(B)^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}\Res_{F_{i}/F}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}.

(2.1)

If
\psi
is a generaic character, then it induces a character of
\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}(F_{i})
via (
2.1
), and its restriction to each factor
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}(F_{i})
is non-trivial by the definition of a generic character. We note that the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}(F_{i})
on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}(F_{i})
induces a transitive action of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}(F_{i})
on the set of non-trivial characters
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}(F_{i})\to\mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}
(
cf.
[
BH06
, 1.7 Proposition]
). Therefore it suffices to show that the morphism

T\to\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}\Res_{F_{i}/F}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}

induced by conjugate action via (
2.1
) is an isomorphism. It is enough to show this after the base change to
F^{\mathrm{sep}}
, where it follows from the adjointness of
G
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

If
F
is a finite field and
Z(G)
is connected, the Whittaker datum of
G
is unique.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
2.4
because

H^{1}(F,Z(G))
is trivial by Lang’s theorem.
∎

3
Langlands parameters

We recall the definition of Langlands parameters for reductive groups over finite fields from
[
IV25
]
.

Let
k
be a finite field with
q
elements.
Let
\overline{k}
be an algebraic closure of
k
.
Let
\sigma_{q}\in\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
be the
q
-th power arithmetic Frobenius element.

Definition 3.1
.

We put

I_{k}=\varprojlim_{k^{\prime}}k^{\prime\times}

(3.1)

where
k^{\prime}
runs through the finite extensions of
k
in
\overline{k}
and the transition maps are the norm maps.
We define the Weil group of
k
by

W_{k}=I_{k}\rtimes\langle\sigma_{q}\rangle

(3.2)

where the conjugation by
\sigma_{q}
acts on
I_{k}
as
q
-th power.

Next we recall a definition of a Weil–Deligne group for a finite field.

Definition 3.2
.

The Weil–Deligne group of the finite
field
k
is the locally pro-algebraic group
scheme

\mathit{WD}_{k}=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\rtimes W_{k}

(3.3)

over
\mathbb{Q}
, where
(\sigma_{q}^{n},w)\in W_{k}
acts on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
by the multiplication by
q^{n}
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over

k
. Let
\Lambda
be an algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
.
We write
{}^{L}G
for the L-group of
G
over
\Lambda
.

Definition 3.3
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is a field of characteristic zero.
An L-parameter of Weil–Deligne type is a morphism of

\varphi\colon\mathit{WD}_{k}\rightarrow{}^{L}G

(3.4)

group schemes over
\Lambda
which is compatible with the projections to
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
.

We say that the L-parameter
\varphi
is special
if
\varphi|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}(K)}(1)
is a special unipotent element of

\widehat{G}^{\varphi(I_{k})}
.
We say that
\varphi
is Frobenius semisimple if

\varphi(\sigma_{q})
is semisimple in
{}^{L}G
.

We put

A_{Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))=\pi_{0}(Z_{Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))/Z(Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ})).

We define Lusztig’s canonical quotient
\overline{A}_{Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))

of
A_{Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))
as in
[
Lus84
, 13.1]
using the isomorphism given by
[
IV25
, Lemma 4.2]
.
We put
\varphi_{0}=\varphi|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}}
and

A(\varphi_{0})=\pi_{0}\left(Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi_{0})/Z(\widehat{G})\right).

(3.5)

Further we put

\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})=A(\varphi_{0})/\Ker(A_{Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))\to\overline{A}_{Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))).

(3.6)

We put

\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0})=\{(g,\sigma_{q}^{m})\in{}^{L}G\mid\Ad((g,\sigma_{q}^{m}))(\varphi_{0}(x))=\varphi_{0}(\Ad(\sigma_{q}^{m})(x))\ \textrm{for all $x\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}$}\}.

Further we put

\widetilde{A}(\varphi_{0})=\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0})/\Ker(Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi_{0})\to\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})).

(3.7)

We have

\varphi(\sigma_{q})\in\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0})
.
Let
\overline{\varphi(\sigma_{q})}
be the image of

\varphi(\sigma_{q})
under the natural projection

\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0})\to\widetilde{A}(\varphi_{0}).

We say that two
L-parameters
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
of Weil–Deligne type are equivalent
if the following condition is satisfied:
there is
g\in\widehat{G}
such that

\Ad(g)(\varphi_{0})=\varphi_{0}^{\prime}
and

\overline{\varphi(\sigma_{q})}
corresponds to

\overline{\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma_{q})}
under the bijection

\widetilde{A}(\varphi_{0})\cong\widetilde{A}(\varphi^{\prime}_{0})

induced by
\Ad(g)
, where

\varphi_{0}=\varphi|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}}
and

\varphi_{0}^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}}
.
Let
\Phi_{\Lambda}(G)
be the equivalence classes of
Frobenius semisimple L-parameters over
\Lambda
of
G
.
We write
\Phi_{\Lambda}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}\subset\Phi_{\Lambda}(G)
for
the equivalence classes of special ones.

We put

A_{\varphi}=Z_{\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})}(\overline{\varphi(\sigma_{q})}).

(3.8)

In the finite Langlands correspondence, the irreducible representation of

A_{\varphi}
will parametrizes the L-packet of
\varphi
.

4
Categorical equivalence

Let
H
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\overline{k}
with Frobenius endomorphism
\mathrm{F}
with respect to
k
.
Let
\mathbf{c}
be a two-sided cell of the Weyl group of
H
such that
\mathrm{F}(\mathbf{c})=\mathbf{c}
.
Let
\ell
be a prime number different from
p
.
We write
\Rep_{\mathrm{u}}^{\mathbf{c}}(H^{\mathrm{F}})

for the category of representation of
H^{\mathrm{F}}
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
which are finite direct sums of unipotent representations whose associated two-sided is
\mathbf{c}
.

Let
u_{\mathbf{c}}
be an unipotent element in the unipotent conjugacy class of
\widehat{H}
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
corresponding to
\mathbf{c}
.
Let
\sigma
be the automorphism of
\widehat{H}
given by the Frobenius structure of
H
.
We define
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
as the Lusztig’s canonical quotient of
\pi_{0}(Z_{\widehat{H}}(u_{\mathbf{c}})/Z(\widehat{H}))
.
This is isomorphic to the finite group attached to
\mathbf{c}
constructed in
[
Lus84
, Chapter 4]
by
[
Lus84
, (13.1.3)]
(
cf.
[
Lus14
]
).
We take
h_{\mathbf{c}}\in\widehat{H}
such that

\Ad(h_{\mathbf{c}})(\sigma(u_{\mathbf{c}}))=u_{\mathbf{c}}
.
We define
\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}\in\Aut(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}})
by

\Ad(h_{\mathbf{c}})\circ\sigma
.

For a finite group
\mathcal{G}
with an automorphsim
\tau
, let
\Sh^{\mathcal{G},\tau}(\mathcal{G})
be the category of

\mathcal{G}
-equivalent sheaves of finite dimensional
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-vector spaces over
\mathcal{G}
with respect to the
\tau
-twisted conjugation action of
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathcal{G}
. If
\tau
is the identity, we simply write

\Sh^{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{G})
for
\Sh^{\mathcal{G},\id}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

There is a canonical equivalence

\Rep_{\mathrm{u}}^{\mathbf{c}}(H^{\mathrm{F}})\cong\Sh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}})

of abelian categories.

Proof.

By
[
DM20
, Proposition 11.3.8]
and the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
, both sides do not change even if we replace
H
by the adjoint quotient.
Hence we may assume that
H
is adjoint simple.
By
[
Lus15
, 6.3 (a)]
, we have an equivalence

\Rep_{\mathrm{u}}^{\mathbf{c}}(H^{\mathrm{F}})\cong\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{c}}\mathcal{B}^{2})

of abelian categories,
where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{c}}\mathcal{B}^{2}
is a monoidal abelian category defined in

[
Lus15
, 1.6, 6.1]
,
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}
is the categorical center relative to
\sigma
defined in
[
BV12
, §5.5]
.

For each left cell
\Gamma
in
\mathbf{c}
, we attach
a subgroup
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
as
[
Lus87
, 3.8 Proposition]
. We put

X_{\mathbf{c}}=\coprod_{\Gamma}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}/\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
, where
\Gamma
run through the left cells in
\mathbf{c}
.

Let

\Coh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}}(X_{\mathbf{c}})

be the category of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
-equivariant sheaves of finite dimensional
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-vector spaces over
X_{\mathbf{c}}
.
Let
\Fun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}}(X_{\mathbf{c}},X_{\mathbf{c}})
be the monoidal category of all
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-linear functors

\Coh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}}(X_{\mathbf{c}})\to\Coh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}}(X_{\mathbf{c}})
(
cf.
[
BO04
, 5.1]
).
We recall that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
if
\mathbf{c}
is exceptional (
cf.
[
Ost14
, 1]
).
Let
\omega\in H^{3}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\{\pm 1\})
be the unique nontrivial cohomology class if
\mathbf{c}
is exceptional, and the trivial one otherwise.
Let

\Fun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\omega}(X_{\mathbf{c}},X_{\mathbf{c}})
be the tensor category obtained from
\Fun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}}(X_{\mathbf{c}},X_{\mathbf{c}})

by twisting
the associativity isomorphisms by
\omega
.
By
[
BFO09
, Theorem 4]
,
[
BO04
, 5.1 Remark]
and
[
Ost14
, Remark 2.20]
, we have an equivalence

\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{c}}\mathcal{B}^{2}\cong\Fun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\omega}(X_{\mathbf{c}},X_{\mathbf{c}})

of tensor categories (
cf.
Proof of
[
BFO12
, Corollary 5.4]
).

We take a minimum positive integer
n
such that
\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{n}
acts trivially on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
.
We note that
n=1
when
\mathbf{c}
is exceptional.
We note that
\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}
is centralizable in the sense of
[
BV12
, §5.1]
by
[
BV12
, Proposition 5.3]
and
[
KL01
, Proposition 5.1.7]
.
By
[
BV12
, Theorem 5.12, Theorem 6.5]
,

\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}}(\Fun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\omega}(X_{\mathbf{c}},X_{\mathbf{c}}))
is equivalent to the degree
1
part of

\mathcal{Z}(\Fun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\rtimes\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}},\omega}(X_{\mathbf{c}},X_{\mathbf{c}}))
.
By
[
EGNO15
, Example 7.12.19, Corollary 7.16.2]
,

\mathcal{Z}(\Fun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\rtimes\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}},\omega}(X_{\mathbf{c}},X_{\mathbf{c}}))
is equivalent to

\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\rtimes\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}},\omega})
, where
\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\rtimes\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}},\omega}
is the monoidal category of
(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\rtimes\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}})
-graded finite dimensional vector spaces over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
with the associativity isomorphisms twisted by
\omega
.
Further
\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\rtimes\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}},\omega})
is equivalent to

\Sh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\rtimes\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}\rtimes F^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}})
as abelian categories.
The degree
1
part of this category is

\Sh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}})
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
h_{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}
is another choice of
h_{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}
,
we have other automorphicm
\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
and equivalence

\Rep_{\mathrm{u}}^{\mathbf{c}}(H^{\mathrm{F}})\cong\Sh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}})

as Proposition

.
Two equivalences are related by the translation under the image of
h_{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}h_{\mathbf{c}}^{-1}
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
.

In the rest of this section, let
G
be a reductive group over
k
such that each connected component of
G
is defined over
k
.

We take a Borel pair
(B,T)
of
G
. Let
W
and
W^{\circ}
be the Weyl group of
G
and
G^{\circ}
with respect to
T
respectively.
Let
\Ch(T)
be the abelian group of isomorphism classes of character sheaves on
T
. We have an isomorphsim

\Ch(T)\cong\Hom(T(k),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times})

(4.1)

by
[
Yun14
, A.3.3 Theorem]
.

In the following, when we use results in
[
LY20
]
, we refer
[
LY21
]
, which is a corrected version of
[
LY20
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

(1)

A semisimple parameter of
G
is a
W
-orbit
\mathfrak{o}\subset\Ch(T)
which contains an
F
-stable
W^{\circ}
-orbit.

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{o}
be a semisimple parameter of
G
.
A unipotent parameter of
G
with respect to
\mathfrak{o}
is a union
\mathfrak{c}\subset W^{\circ}\times\mathfrak{o}
of a
W
-orbit of
F
-stable two-sided cells
\mathfrak{c}^{\circ}\subset W\times\mathfrak{o}^{\circ}
in the sense of
[
LY21
, 11.4]
,
where
\mathfrak{o}^{\circ}\subset\mathfrak{o}
is an
F
-stable
W^{\circ}
-orbit.

For
\mathcal{L}\in\Ch(T)
, let
W_{\mathcal{L}}
and
(W^{\circ})_{\mathcal{L}}
be the stabilizer of
\mathcal{L}
in
W
and
W^{\circ}
respectively.
We put

\Phi_{\mathcal{L}}=\{\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)\mid\textrm{$(\alpha^{\vee})^{*}\mathcal{L}$ is trivial}\},\quad\Phi_{\mathcal{L}}^{+}=\Phi_{\mathcal{L}}\cap\Phi^{+}(G,T).

Let
(W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}
be the Weyl group of
\Phi_{\mathcal{L}}
. We put

\Omega_{\mathcal{L}}=W_{\mathcal{L}}/(W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ},\quad\Omega_{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}=(W^{\circ})_{\mathcal{L}}/(W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}
be a unipotent parameter of
G
with respect to a semisimple parameter
\mathfrak{o}
of
G
.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be a representative of
\mathfrak{o}
.
Then there is a two-sided cell

\mathbf{c}\subset(W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}
such that
\mathfrak{c}\cap((W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}\times\{\mathcal{L}\})=\Omega_{\mathcal{L}}\mathbf{c}\times\{\mathcal{L}\}
.

Proof.

We put

\mathfrak{o}^{\circ}=W^{\circ}\mathcal{L}
.
We take a two-sided cell
\mathfrak{c}^{\circ}\subset W^{\circ}\times\mathfrak{o}^{\circ}
as
Definition
4.3

(2)
.
Further, we take a two-sided cell

\mathbf{c}\subset(W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}
such that
\mathfrak{c}^{\circ}\cap((W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}\times\{\mathcal{L}\})=\Omega_{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}\mathbf{c}\times\{\mathcal{L}\}
as
[
LY21
, 11.4]
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}\cap((W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}\times\{\mathcal{L}\})

\displaystyle=(W_{\mathfrak{o}^{\circ}}\mathfrak{c}^{\circ})\cap((W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}\times\{\mathcal{L}\})=(W_{\mathcal{L}}\mathfrak{c}^{\circ})\cap((W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}\times\{\mathcal{L}\})

\displaystyle=W_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{c}^{\circ}\cap((W_{\mathcal{L}})^{\circ}\times\{\mathcal{L}\}))=\Omega_{\mathcal{L}}\mathbf{c}\times\{\mathcal{L}\}

by
\mathfrak{c}\cap(W^{\circ}\times\mathfrak{o}^{\circ})=W_{\mathfrak{o}^{\circ}}\mathfrak{c}^{\circ}
and
W_{\mathcal{L}}/(W^{\circ})_{\mathcal{L}}\cong W_{\mathfrak{o}^{\circ}}/W^{\circ}
.

5
Langlands correspondence for finite fields

Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over
k
.
The aim of this section is constructing the Langlands correspondence for
G
, which is a natural map

\mathcal{L}_{G}\colon\Irr_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G(k))\to\Phi_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}.

(5.1)

This is correspondence concerning L-parameters of Weil–Deligne type explained in Section
3
.
However, for a technical reason, we need to use auxiliary L-parameters of
\SL_{2}
-type, which we explain below, in the course of our construction of (
5.1
).

Let
\Lambda
be a field of characteristic zero,
and
{}^{L}G
the
L
-group of
G
over
\Lambda
.

Definition 5.1
.

An L-parameter of
\SL_{2}
-type is a morphism

\psi\colon\SL_{2}\times W_{k}\rightarrow{}^{L}G

(5.2)

of group schemes over
\Lambda
which is
compatible with the projections to
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
.

We say that the L-parameter
\psi
is special
if
\psi|_{\mathbb{G}_{a}(K)}(1)
is a special unipotent element of

\widehat{G}^{\psi(I_{k})}
.
We say that
\psi
is Frobenius semisimple if

\psi(\sigma_{q})
is semisimple in
{}^{L}G
.
We say that
\psi
is unipotent if

\psi(I_{k})
is trivial.

We put
\psi_{0}=\psi|_{\SL_{2}\times I_{k}}
and

A(\psi_{0})=\pi_{0}\left(Z_{\widehat{G}}(\psi_{0})/Z(\widehat{G})\right).

(5.3)

For
\varphi_{0}
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(a,w)=\psi_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},w\right)

for
(a,w)\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}
,
we have a natural isomorphism

A(\psi_{0})\cong A(\varphi_{0})
since

Z_{\widehat{G}}(\varphi_{0})
is a semidirect product of

Z_{\widehat{G}}(\psi_{0})
with a connected unipotent group by
[
BMIY24
, Proposition 3.3]
.
Then we define
\overline{A}(\psi_{0})
as the quotient of
A(\psi_{0})
corresponding to
Lusztig’s canonical quotient
\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})
of
A(\varphi_{0})
.

We put

\widetilde{Z}(\psi_{0})=\{(g,\sigma_{q}^{m})\in{}^{L}G\mid\Ad((g,\sigma_{q}^{m}))(\psi_{0}(x))=\psi_{0}(\Ad(\sigma_{q}^{m})(x))\ \textrm{for all $x\in\SL_{2}\times I_{k}$}\}.

(5.4)

Further we put

\widetilde{A}(\psi_{0})=\widetilde{Z}(\psi_{0})/\Ker(Z_{\widehat{G}}(\psi_{0})\to\overline{A}(\psi_{0})).

(5.5)

We have

\psi(\sigma_{q})\in\widetilde{Z}(\psi_{0})
.
Let
\overline{\psi(\sigma_{q})}
be the image of

\psi(\sigma_{q})
under the natural projection

\widetilde{Z}(\psi_{0})\to\widetilde{A}(\psi_{0}).

We say that two
L-parameters
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
are equivalent
if the following condition is satisfied:
there is
g\in\widehat{G}
such that

\Ad(g)(\psi_{0})=\psi_{0}^{\prime}
and

\overline{\psi(\sigma_{q})}
corresponds to

\overline{\psi^{\prime}(\sigma_{q})}
under the bijection

\widetilde{A}(\psi_{0})\cong\widetilde{A}(\psi^{\prime}_{0})

induced by
\Ad(g)
, where

\psi_{0}=\psi|_{\SL_{2}\times I_{k}}
and

\psi_{0}^{\prime}=\psi^{\prime}|_{\SL_{2}\times I_{k}}
.
Let
\Psi_{\Lambda}(G)
be the equivalence classes of
Frobenius semisimple L-parameters over
\Lambda
of
G
.
We write
\Psi_{\Lambda}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}\subset\Psi_{\Lambda}(G)
for
the equivalence class of special ones.

We put

A_{\psi}=Z_{\overline{A}(\psi_{0})}(\overline{\psi(\sigma_{q})}).

(5.6)

We assume and fix
q^{1/2}\in\Lambda
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Sending an L-parameter

\psi
of
\SL_{2}
-type to
\varphi
defined by

\varphi(a,w)=\psi\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lvert w\rvert^{1/2}&0\\
0&\lvert w\rvert^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix},w\right),

(5.7)

we have bijections

\Psi_{\Lambda}(G)\cong\Phi_{\Lambda}(G)

and

\Psi_{\Lambda}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}\cong\Phi_{\Lambda}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}
.
Further we have a natural isomorphism

A_{\psi}\cong A_{\varphi}
for
\psi
and
\varphi
as above.

Proof.

Sending
\psi
to
\varphi
,
we have a bijection between
the
\widehat{G}
-equivalence classes of
the Frobenius semisimple L-parameters
of
\SL_{2}
-type
and
the
\widehat{G}
-equivalence classes of
the Frobenius semisimple Weil–Deligne L-parameters
by
[
BMIY24
, Theorem 6.16]
and
[
Ima24
, Proposition 1.7]
.
We have a natural isomorphism

\overline{A}(\psi_{0})\cong\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})
by the definition of
\overline{A}(\psi_{0})
.
Therefore

\widetilde{Z}(\psi_{0})\to\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0});(g,\sigma_{q}^{m})\mapsto\psi_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\lvert\sigma_{q}\rvert^{m/2}&0\\
0&\lvert\sigma_{q}\rvert^{-m/2}\end{pmatrix},1\right)(g,\sigma_{q}^{m})

(5.8)

induces the isomorphism

\widetilde{A}(\psi_{0})\cong\widetilde{A}(\varphi_{0})
,
where (
5.8
) is a group homomorphism because any element of
Z_{\widehat{G}}(\psi_{0})
commutes with
\psi\left(\begin{pmatrix}\lvert\sigma_{q}\rvert^{m/2}&0\\
0&\lvert\sigma_{q}\rvert^{-m/2}\end{pmatrix},1\right)
.
Therefore we obtain the claims.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

We fix a Whittaker datum of
G
.
Then we have a natural map

\mathcal{L}_{G}\colon\Irr_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G(k))\to\Phi_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}},

and a natural bijection between
\mathcal{L}_{G}^{-1}(\varphi)
and

\Irr_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(A_{\varphi})
for
\varphi\in\Phi_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}
.

Proof.

We construct a map

\mathcal{L}_{G}^{\Psi}\colon\Irr_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G(k))\to\Psi_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}.

(5.9)

Let
\pi\in\Irr_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G(k))
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{\pi}
be an irreducible object of

\Sh^{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}},\tau_{\beta}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}})^{\Omega_{\mathbf{c},\beta}}
corresponding to
\pi
under
Theorem
4.5

for some
\mathfrak{o}
,
\mathfrak{c}
and

\beta\in\mathfrak{B}_{\mathbf{c},\Omega_{\mathbf{c}}}^{\circ}
.
This gives an element
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
and a representaion of
Z_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}}(g\sigma_{\beta})
with
\Omega_{\mathbf{c},\beta}
-equivariant structure.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a representative of
\mathfrak{o}
.
Recall that
\mathcal{H}=Z_{\widehat{G}}(\phi_{\mathcal{L},0})
.
Let
u_{\mathbf{c}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\circ}
an element of the unipotent conjugacy classs corresponding to
\mathbf{c}
.
We have an identification

\overline{A}_{\mathcal{H}^{\circ}}(u_{\mathbf{c}})\cong\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}
.
Let
\widetilde{g}\in Z_{\mathcal{H}^{\circ}}(u_{\mathbf{c}})
be a lift of
g
.

We define
\psi
by

\psi|_{I_{k}}=\phi_{\mathcal{L},0},\quad\psi\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=u_{\mathbf{c}},\quad\psi(\sigma_{q})=\widetilde{g}h_{\beta}\dot{w}^{\beta}\rtimes\sigma_{q}.

We note that

\displaystyle\Ad(\psi(\sigma_{q}))(\phi_{\mathcal{L},0})=\phi_{\mathcal{L},0}^{q}.

We also have

\Ad(\psi(\sigma_{q}))(u_{\mathbf{c}})=u_{\mathbf{c}}
.
We put
\mathcal{L}_{G}^{\Psi}(\pi)=\psi
.

We have natural bijections

A_{\psi}\cong Z_{\overline{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(u_{\mathbf{c}})}(\overline{\psi(\sigma_{q})})\cong Z_{\overline{A}_{\mathcal{H}}(u_{\mathbf{c}})}(g\overline{h_{\beta}\dot{w}^{\beta}\sigma_{q}}).

(5.10)

By (
5.10
) and Lemma
4.10
,
we have

\Rep(A_{\psi})\cong\Rep(Z_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{c}}}(g\tau_{\beta}))^{\Omega_{\mathbf{c},\beta}}.

(5.11)

By the construction and (
5.11
), the fiber
(\mathcal{L}_{G}^{\Psi})^{-1}(\psi)

is parametrized by
the isomorphism classes of the irreducible representations of
A_{\psi}
.

We define
\mathcal{L}_{G}

as the composite of
\mathcal{L}_{G}^{\Psi}

and
the natural bijection

\Psi_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}\cong\Phi_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}
in Proposition
5.2
.

C6 and KLRG2 are pyroptosis subtype-related prognostic biomarkers and correlated with tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Shu-Min Yuan, Xiao Chen, Yi-Qing Qu, Meng-Yu Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPyroptosis plays an important role in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). In this study, we aimed to explore the pyroptosis-related gene (PRG) expression pattern and to identify promising pyroptosis-related biomarkers to improve the prognosis of LUAD. The gene expression profiles and clinical information of LUAD patients were downloaded from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), and validation cohort information was extracted from the Gene Expression Omnibus database. Gene expression data were analyzed using the limma package and visualized using the ggplot2 package as well as the pheatmap package in R software. Functional enrichment analysis was also performed for the 44 differentially expressed PRGs (DEPRGs). Then, consensus clustering revealed pyroptosis-related tumor subtypes, and differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened according to the subtypes. Next, univariate Cox and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to identify independent prognostic PRGs. After overlapping DEGs and the Lasso regression analysis-based prognostic genes, the predictive risk model was established and validated. Correlation analysis between PRGs and clinicopathological variables was also explored. Finally, the TIMER and TISIDB databases were used to further explore the correlation analysis between immune cell infiltration levels, the risk score, and clinicopathological variables in the predictive risk model. A total of 52 genes from the PubMed were identified as PRGs, and 44 of the 52 genes were pooled as DEPRGs. The most significant GO term was “collagen trimer” (P = 2.46E-13), and KEGG analysis results indicated that 44 DEPRGs were significantly enriched in Salmonella infection (P < 0.001). Then, consensus clustering analysis divided LUAD patients into two clusters, and a total of 79 DEGs were identified according to these cluster subtypes. Subsequently, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to identify 12 genes that could serve as independent prognostic indicators and we also performed Lasso regression analysis and screened 23 DEGs. After overlapping 23 DEGs and 12 genes, only 4 (KLRG2, MAPK4, C6 and SFRP5) of 12 genes were selected for the further exploration of the prognostic pattern. Survival analysis results indicated that this risk model effectively predicted the prognosis (P < 0.001). Combined with the correlation analysis results between the 4 genes and clinicopathological variables, C6 and KLRG2 were screened as prognostic genes. In this study, we constructed a predictive risk model and identified two pyroptosis subtype-related gene expression patterns to improve the prognosis of LUAD. Introduction

Lung cancer is still the leading cause of cancer-related death in men and women worldwide1, of which non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for over 80% of lung cancer-related deaths2. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is one of the most common histological types, accounting for about half of lung cancer. However, the molecular basis of the pathogenesis of LUAD is still not well elucidated. Despite the advances in new targeted therapies, chemotherapy, radiotherapy as well as surgical methods, the 5-year survival rate of LUAD is less than 17%3. Early detection, diagnosis, and intervention contribute to a better clinical outcome as well as the prognosis for LUAD patients, such as early diagnosis based on low-dose computed tomography, which can finally improve lung cancer mortality by nearly 20%4, but most newly diagnosed patients are at an advanced or metastatic stage and often lose the best chance for surgery. Hence, it is essential to identify new biomarkers for early diagnosis and intervention and eventually improve the prognosis of LUAD.

Pyroptosis is believed to be a form of caspase-mediated programmed cell death, which is initially identified as an infection defense mechanism and recent studies have revealed its vital roles in the development of numerous tumors. In recent years, pyroptosis-related genes (PRGs) have been investigated in various cancers5–8. In lung cancer, researchers performed prognostic analyses and found a poor survival rate in LUAD patients with low expression of NLRP7, NLRP1, NLRP2, and NOD1 and high CASP6 expression (all of the mentioned genes are PRGs)9. Other research revealed that down-regulation of GSDMD attenuates tumor proliferation and predicts a good prognosis in non‑small cell lung cancer10. Another study suggested that the the expression of all six GSDM family members is increased in LUAD patients and related to shorter overall survival in patients with LUAD11. AIM2, CASP3, ZBP1 and CASP8 may play critical roles in the growth of tumor in ovarian cancer and can be regulated by non-coding RNAs12. Another study generated a signature featuring 4 PRGs (CASP3, GSDMA, IL18, and PLCG1) and observed that it could predict overall survival (OS) in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma13. Researchers also constructed a risk model based on 4 PRGs (NLRP1, HMGB1, CYCS, and BAK1) expression levels, which could also predict the prognosis of LUAD patients and serve as prognostic biomarkers14. Existing studies further elucidated the crucial role of PRGs in the biological processes, while PRG expression patterns not only participate in the formation of inflammasome but also could serve as prognostic biomarkers in many cancers.

All of the above findings confirm the role of pyroptosis in lung cancer and indicate that PRGs could serve as prognostic biomarkers. Moreover, pyroptosis may play key role in the regulation of tumor microenvironment (TME)15–17. Pyroptosis plays vital roles in the innate immune system and the acquired immune system, which can influence the levels of infiltrating immune cells. The correlation between pyroptosis and the immune system depends on the complex cellular interactions, such as pyroptosis and immune cells18. Pyroptosis in normal cells has the potential to alter the TME and accelerate immune escape19. Meanwhile, there are therapeutic techniques that engage the immune system and cause the pyroptosis of cancer cells20. Furthermore, previous studies have illustrated that the prognostic value of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) significantly differs according to histological type and other factors in NSCLC patients21.

In the current study, we pooled an pyroptosis subtype-related gene expression pattern from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database and validated it in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. We identified two tumor subgroups using consensus clustering analysis based on 44 differentially expressed PRGs (DERPGs), in which we found that cluster 1 had poor prognostic value in LUAD compared to cluster 2. Then, we analyzed the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) of these two clusters and performed univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses as well as Lasso regression analysis to obtain the prognostic PRGs and overlapped the DEGs and prognostic PRGs. Finally, 12 genes were selected, and a risk model was constructed based on the coefficient value of each independent risk gene to predict the prognosis of LUAD patients. Many previously published works have simply focused either on consensus clustering analysis or on risk models. Few studies have focused on both at the same time. Therefore, in the current study, we combined both of these methods to explore the prognosis of LUAD. Furthermore, unlike existing studies, we further validated their expression and prognostic value and explored their associations with TILs.

Materials and methods

Data collection and validation of differentially expressed PRGs (DEPRGs) in LUAD patients

The Gene Database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/) was used to extract genes involved in pyroptosis. The searching strategy was the followings: first, we typed “pyroptosis” into the searching bar. Then on the right side, “Homo sapiens” was selected and on the left side, we chose the Gene sources as “Genomic”, the Categories as “Protein-coding”, the Sequence content as “RefSeqGene”, and the “Current Status” was selected. Finally, 52 pyroptosis-related genes were screened. The gene expression data, somatic data and clinical information of LUAD patients were downloaded from the TCGA database (https://tcga-data.nci.nih.gov/tcga/). In detail, TCGA contains a total of 594 LUAD patients (including 59 adjacent normal lung tissues and 535 LUAD tissues). Gene expression data from TCGA were analyzed by the limma package in R software. Validated gene expression profiling studies for LUAD were searched via Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) and PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). The search strategy used the term: (pyroptosis OR pyroptotic) AND (lung adenocarcinoma OR LUAD) AND (profil* OR expression). Every study was estimated thoroughly evaluated through full text. The included studies met the following criteria: (1) Gene expression profiling studies; (2) Primary experimental studies published in English language; (3) The dataset contains both expression levels of related genes and clinical information such as age, sex, survival status and survival time. (4) Studies compared gene expression between LUAD and adjacent normal lung tissues in human. Expression studies using cell lines or body fluid (such as serum, saliva, peripheral blood, etc.) were excluded; patients who had clinical information but no gene expression data were excluded; and studies that only analyzed diverse histologic subtypes but did not include adjacent normal lung tissues were also excluded. Finally, the independent cohort GSE7209422, which included 442 LUAD patients, was downloaded for data validation.

Differential expression pattern and mutational analysis of DEPRGs in LUAD patients

A total of 44 DEPRGs were identified using the limma package in R software. The significance cutoff value was P < 0.050 and absolute fold change > 2. In addition, all expression levels of the DEPRGs were visualized as heatmaps using the pheatmap package in R software. The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of 44 DEPRGs was constructed in the STRING database (https://string-db.org/). R package maftools was utilized to map the somatic mutation waterfall plot among all of the PRGs in LUAD patients. Additionally, several R packages were also used to visualize the data.

Consensus clustering analysis of the DEPRGs

Consensus clustering analysis could infer the optimal number of clusters, the lowest proportion of ambiguous clustering, and the best cumulative distribution function (CDF) value by using the k value of 2 in the current study. We first extracted the expression of 44 DEPRGs and then performed unsupervised consensus clustering analysis based on the expression levels of 44 DEPRGs using the ConsensusClusterPlus package in R software. Finally, two clusters (subtypes) were identified. To further analyze the prognostic value of pyroptosis subtypes, we also performed the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis using the survival package and survminer package in R software. Additionally, we also screened differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between cluster 1 and cluster 2. Furthermore, we also mapped the expression heat map of 166 DEGs using the pheatmap package in conjunction with the clinicopathological features in all of the TCGA-LUAD patients.

Correlation analysis among 23 prognostic DEGs, risk score, and clinicopathological variables in LUAD patients

After screening 23 prognostic DEGs and exploring their associations with OS, we shifted our attention to the relationship between prognostic DEGs, risk score and clinicopathological variables. Therefore, in this study, correlation analysis was performed to further explore the correlation between prognostic DEGs, risk score and clinicopathological variables in LUAD, including age (≤ 65 years group and > 65 years group), sex (female and male groups), stage (I&II and III&IV groups), pathological T stage (T1&T2 and T3&T4 groups), pathological N stage (N0 and N1–N3 groups), and pathological M stage (M0 and M1 groups).

Construction and validation of the prognostic risk model in both training and testing sets

To quantify the degree of risk for each patient, we constructed a risk model based on the pyroptosis pattern based on the TCGA database. Then, we overlapped the DEGs in TCGA (training set) and GSE72094 (testing set) to further validate the risk model. The training set is used to construct the prognostic risk model, and the testing set and the entire set are used to validate the risk model. Next, LASSO Cox regression analysis was executed in the training set to reduce overfitting genes with 10-fold cross-validation and 1000 repeated times. Finally, we performed multivariate Cox regression analysis using the genes screened by the LASSO Cox regression analysis, we also calculated the risk score for each patient according to expression levels and regression coefficients of genes. At the same time, the hazard ratios (HRs) were also calculated using multivariate Cox regression analysis. Finally, the median risk score was used as the cutoff value, dividing all of the LUAD patients into low-risk and high-risk groups. Principle component analysis (PCA) was performed to visualize the distribution between the two different pyroptosis sub types. Furthermore, in the training and testing sets, we also performed Kaplan-Meier survival analysis using survminer and survival packages in R sofeware and ROC curve analysis using timeROC package in R software, respectively. Additionally, we explored the relationship between the pyroptosis sub types, risk score, and survival status, as well as the differences in risk scores between the two distinct sub types. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the predictive accuracy of our risk model.

Functional enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analysis includes Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) in this study. In brief, GO enrichment analyses predict the function of the target genes and KEGG is a widely used database for systematic signaling pathway analysis according to gene functions. In this study, the clusterProfiler package in R software was used to perform the functional enrichment analysis of 23 prognostic DEGs in LUAD, and GOplot package in R software was employed to visualize all of the enrichment analysis results. The identification criterion of significant GO terms and KEGG pathways was P < 0.050.

Immune response analysis of pyroptosis-related subtypes using single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)

The scores of 16 tumor-infiltrating immune cells and 13 immune-related functions for each LUAD sample were estimated through ssGSEA based on the expression levels of risk scores using the GSEABase and GSVA packages in R software. The comparisons of the infiltrating immune cells and immune functions were conducted and visualized in box plots.

Explore the immune-related mechanism of C6 and KLRG2

Immune-related mechanism of C6 and KLRG2 were performed by TISIDB (https://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/) and Tumor IMmune Estimation Resource TIMER (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/)23,24. In the current study, we validated C6 and KLRG2 expression levels and their relation to OS in both TISIDB and TIMER database. Then, the correlation between C6 and KLRG2 expression level and immune cell infiltration level were explored, as well as the correlation between SCNA and mutation levels of the two genes and immune cell infiltration level. We further explored cumulative survival rates between low and high immune cell (B Cell and Dendritic Cell) infiltration levels in TIMER. Finally, gene expression levels and SCNA levels of C6 and their relationships to immunoinhibitors were also examined. The purity-corrected partial Spearman method was used to analyze the data.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R software, and P < 0.050 was regarded as statistically significant. Patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the risk scores. Comparisons of the expression rates between the two groups were performed using Student’s t-test. The unpaired t-test was used to assess expression levels of the PRGs between the high-expression and low-expression groups. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated for the TCGA cohort and GSE72094 and analyzed using the log-rank test. The correlation between gene expression levels and infiltrating immune cell levels was determined using the purity-corrected partial Spearman method.

Results

Differential expression patterns and mutational characteristics of PRGs in LUAD patients from TCGA

The flow diagram of this study is shown in Fig. 1. In the current study, gene expression profiles and mutational data were selected from the TCGA database and a total of 52 PRGs were pooled (Supplementary Table S1). According to the TCGA database, expression levels of 52 PRGs are listed in Supplementary Table S2. Then, we performed differential expression analysis using limma package and identified DEPRGs between LUAD tissues and adjacent normal lung tissues; genes with P < 0.05 and absolute fold change > 2 were considered as DEPRGs. Finally, a total of 44 PRGs were differentially expressed in LUAD patients, including 17 downregulated genes and 27 upregulated genes (Fig. 2A). Details of 44 DEPRGs (such as conMean, treatment Mean, logFC and P value) are also displayed in Supplementary Table S3. PPI network analysis revealed a close linkage among most genes using STRING database (Fig. 2B). Then we explored the somatic mutation levels in the 52 PRGs in LUAD patients. Somatic mutations were frequently observed 340 of 561 (60.61%) samples had mutations in PRGs, including missense mutations, nonsense mutations, in frame deletions, frame shift insertions, etc. We also noticed that missense mutations are the most common forms among PRGs. Among them, TP53 (47%) had the highest mutation frequency, followed by NLRP3 (11%) and NLRP7 (5%) (Fig. 2C). Co-expression analysis showed the caspase family, GSDMD and NLRP1 positively correlated with most of the PRGs, indicating the underlying relations in pyroptosis (Fig. 2D).

Functional enrichment analysis of 44 DEPRGs in LUAD patients

To determine the biological functions of the 44 DEPRGs, gene ontology (GO) and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed. The top 5 associated GO terms were collagen trimer (P = 2.46E-13), lamellar body (P = 8.44E-13), extracellular matrix (P = 4.4E-12), extracellular matrix organization (P = 3.6E-11), and collagen-containing extracellular matrix (P = 5.47E-11) (Supplementary Table S4). In addition, the significant GO terms were also visualized in a bar plot (Supplementary Figure S1A) and circle plot (Supplementary Figure S1B). Moreover, the top 25 GO terms are shown in Table 1. Further KEGG analysis results indicated that the 44 DEPRGs were significantly enriched in salmonella infection, NOD-like receptor signaling pathway and necroptosis (all P < 0.001) (Supplementary Figure S1C), and they were involved in a total of 30 KEGG pathways (Supplementary Figure S1D). According to the results of functional enrichment of 44 DEPRGs, we found that they were not only connected to pyroptosis but also involved in other biological processes. Therefore, in this study, we pooled the specific roles of PRGs in both autophagy and LUAD.

Identification of 2 clusters using consensus clustering analysis

Pyroptosis may exhibit different expression patterns among LUAD patients, potentially affecting the prognosis and gene expression signature. In the current study, 44 DEPRGs were used to identify pyroptosis subtypes associated with the overall survival of LUAD. Consensus clustering was used to explore the similarity of expression patterns according to the 44 DEPRGs. By selecting a k value of 2 (Fig. 3A–D), we obtained the optimal CDF value and classified the LUAD patients into 2 clusters (Supplementary Table S5). In addition, to explore whether these two clusters of gene expression levels affect clinical outcomes, we performed prognostic analysis using Kaplan-Meier. The results revealed that the prognosis of cluster 2 was better than that of cluster 1 (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3E). Combining the results of consensus clustering analysis and Kaplan-Meier analysis, we found that pyroptosis may play a role in the occurrence and development of LUAD.

Construction of the risk model based on differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between clusters 1 and 2 to improve the prognostic prediction of LUAD

Since different clusters have shown variations in PRGs and different prognosis of LUAD patients, we explored the DEGs between cluster 1 and cluster 2 and finally extracted 79 DEGs. To reveal the distinct expression signature of PRGs, we conducted Cox regression analysis to predict the prognosis of LUAD. First, we combined pyroptosis-related subgroups and clinicopathological features of LUAD patients to map 79 DEGs expression heatmaps (Fig. 4A) and screened 24 upregulated genes in cluster 1 and 55 upregulated genes in cluster 2 (Supplementary Table S6). Then, univariate Cox regression analysis was used to identify the prognostic pyroptosis-related DEGs and 32 DEGs were pooled as prognostic factors (Fig. 4B), among which 15 of 32 were identified as protective factors (HR < 1), while another 17 genes were identified as risk factors (HR > 1) (Supplementary Table S7). Furthermore, we also conducted multivariate Cox regression analysis and the results suggested that 12 genes (CNTNAP2, SPRR1B, ANO1, TM4SF4, KLRG2, PSCA, INHA, MAPK4, MAGEA8, C6, SFRP5 and NLRP2) represented independent prognostic indicators, which were selected for further exploration of the prognostic pattern (Supplementary Table S8). To quantify the risk of each LUAD patient, we constructed a risk model based on pyroptosis cluster-related prognostic DEGs. First, we randomized patients into a training set from TCGA database (n = 535) and a testing set (n = 442) from GSE72094. Second, in the training set, LASSO analysis was used to construct an appropriate risk model. Based on the minimum partial likelihood deviance, 23 potential candidate genes were screened by LASSO regression analysis (Fig. 4C,D) (Supplementary Table S9). Subsequent 23 prognostic genes were used to construct the risk model, namely CNTNAP2, CLDN18, SPRR1B, ANO1, CYP3A5, CAPN13, MSLNL, TM4SF4, SLCO1B3, FCER2, CYP4Z2P, SPIB, KLRG2, PADI1, PSCA, CALB2, INHA, MAPK4, MAGEA8, C6, SFRP5, WNT7A and NLRP2. We calculated the risk score for each patient based on the formula. Risk score = (0.174 * expression of CNTNAP2) + (-0.026 * expression of CLDN18) + (0.032 * expression of SPRR1B) + (0.230 * expression of ANO1) + (0.032 * expression of CYP3A5) + (-0.012 * expression of CAPN13) + (0.031 * expression of MSLNL) + (0.055 * expression of TM4SF4) + (0.045 * expression of SLCO1B3) + (-0.012 * expression of FCER2) + (-0.069 * expression of CYP4Z2P) + (-0.038 * expression of SPIB) + (-0.170 * expression of KLRG2) + (0.032 * expression of PADI1) + (0.003 * expression of PSCA) + (0.081 * expression of CALB2) + (0.070 * expression of INHA) + (0.142 * expression of MAPK4) + (0.114 * expression of MAGEA8) + (-0.123 * expression of C6) + (-0.135 * expression of SFRP5) + (0.002 * expression of WNT7A) + (-0.133 * expression of NLRP2).

Validation of the risk model based on 23 DEGs to improve the prognostic prediction of LUAD

Next, we divided LUAD patients into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. In the training set, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that patients in the high-risk group had worse OS (P < 0.001) than those in the low-risk group (Fig. 5A). The risk score’s 1-, 3- and 5-year AUC values were 0.653, 0.638 and 0.780, respectively (Fig. 5B). The risk score curve and survival status scatter plots showed that the number of deaths in LUAD patients increased as the risk score increased (Fig. 5C,D). Principal component analysis (PCA) results revealed two significantly different distribution patterns of LUAD patients. The samples of cluster 1 and cluster 2 were distributed on the left side and right sides, respectively (Fig. 5E). The t-SNE analysis by the prognostic PRGs involved in the construction of the risk model also divided the LUAD patients into 2 different groups (Fig. 5F). Meanwhile, we validated the same analysis in the testing sets and obtained similar results as Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that patients in the high-risk group had worse OS (P < 0.001) than those in the low-risk group (Fig. 6A). The risk score’s 1-, 3- and 5-year AUC values were 0.737, 0.756 and 0.733, respectively (Fig. 6B). The risk score curve and survival status scatter plots showed that the number of deaths in LUAD patients increased as the risk score increased (Fig. 6C and D). PCA results revealed two significantly different distribution patterns of LUAD patients according to the risk score. The samples of cluster 1 and cluster 2 were also distributed on the left side and right sides, respectively (Fig. 6E). However, the t-SNE analysis seemed hard to divide the LUAD patients the test set into 2 different groups (Fig. 6F). Besides, owing to the different survival rates between these 2 clusters, we also explored the potential roles of immune cells. The results showed that aDCs, B cells, DCs, iDCs, Mast cells, Netrophils, T helper cells and Tfh seems significant in different risk groups (Fig. 7A,B) both in training and testing sets. All of the above results further validated the accuracy of our constructed prognostic model. Taken together, our established risk model demonstrates excellent performance in predicting the survival outcome of LUAD patients.

Correlation analysis between 23 prognostic DEGs and clinicopathological variables in LUAD patients

After pooling 23 independent risk genes, we combined the pyroptosis-related clusters, gene cluster, and clinicopathological features of LUAD patients to create heat maps and found significant expression differences among gene clusters (Fig. 8A). In addition, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to explore the prognostic value of 23 gene expression patterns and clinicopathological variables. The univariate Cox regression analysis results indicated that grade (P < 0.001), pathological T stage (P < 0.001), pathological N stage (P < 0.001), pathological M stage (P = 0.030) and risk score (P < 0.001) were correlated with OS (Fig. 8B). The multivariate Cox independent prognostic analysis results indicated that gender (P = 0.022), grade (P < 0.001) and risk score (P < 0.001) represented independent prognostic factors in LUAD (Fig. 8C) (Supplementary Table S10).

Validation of the 12 DEPRGs expression pattern and prognostic value using qRT-PCR

Overlapping LASSO regression analysis based 23 DEGs and multivariate Cox regression analysis conducted 12 DEGs, combining clinical feature analysis results, 12 DEGs were finally screened and qRT-PCR was performed. The results showed that 4 (KLRG2, MAPK4, C6 and SFRP5) (all P < 0.05) of the 12 genes were consistent with the above results (Fig. 9A–D), while the rest of the 8 genes were not significantly different in LUAD tissues compared to adjacent normal lung tissues (Supplementary Figure S2). According to the survival analysis results showed, we found that the HR of the mRNA expression of SFRP5 for OS was 0.74 (P = 0.150) (Fig. 9E) and the mRNA levels of MAPK4 (Fig. 9F) seemed have no relation with the OS statistically (HR 1.1, P = 0.520), while the mRNA expression of C6 (HR 0.59, P = 0.023) (Fig. 9G) and KLRG2 (HR 0.52, P = 0.002) (Fig. 9H) were significantly associated with the OS. Then we also performed the qRT-PCR using 16HBE, BEAS-2B, A549, H1299, H1975 and PC9 cell lines and the results showed that KLRG2 was low-expressed in LUAD cell lines (A549, H1299, H1975 and PC9) (all P < 0.001) compared with 16HBE cell line (Fig. 9I) as well as BEAS-2B cell line (P = 0.019, P < 0.001, P = 0.007 and P = 0.048, respectively) (Fig. 9J). MAPK4 were low-expressed in A549 (P = 0.015), H1299 (P = 0.003), H1975 (P = 0.047) and PC9 (P < 0.001) compared with 16HBE cell line (Fig. 9K) as well as BEAS-2B cell line (all P < 0.001) (Fig. 9L). The mRNA expression levels of C6 and SFRP5 were significantly lower in 16HBE and BEAS-2B cell lines compared with LUAD cell lines (all P < 0.001) (Fig. 9M–P).

C6 and KLRG2 expression levels and associations between TILs and OS

Our previous results showed that C6 and KLRG2 are differentially expressed and could affect the prognosis of LUAD, so we decided to further explore the role of these 2 genes. Firstly, we validated C6 and KLRG2 expression levels (Fig. 10A,B) as well as the correlation between C6 and KLRG2 expression levels were also explored using TIMER database. The results showed that as tumor purity increases, it seems that C6 and KLRG2 expression levels are positively related (r = 0.063, P = 5.99e-01) (Fig. 10C), the same results were also observed in GEPIA (r = 0.18, P = 3.8e-05) (Fig. 10D). Besides, we also explored their expression levels in different stages using TISIDB database (Fig. 10E,F). Finally, we validated their association with OS in both TIMER (Fig. 10G) and TISIDB databases (Fig. 10H), we also constructed Kaplan-Meier analysis to compare high and low TIL expression levels. Kaplan-Meier analysis results showed that cumulative survival rates between low and high immune cell (B cell and Dendritic cell) infiltration levels were significantly different (P < 0.001 and P = 0.048) (Fig. 10G,H). In addition, we also performed Kaplan-Meier survival analysis according to different C6 and KLRG2 expression levels of LUAD patients. The results showed that the expression levels of C6 and KLRG2 are related to 5-year overall survival rates and higher expression levels of C6 (P = 0.013) (Fig. 10I) and KLRG2 (P = 0.005) (Fig. 10J) are associated with a better prognosis.

For further exploration, we conducted an integrated analysis to predict the potential biological roles of C6 and KLRG2 in tumor immunity of LUAD. The results showed that C6 and KLRG2 expression levels are positively correlated to B cell (r = 0.041, P = 3.70e-01; r = 0.239, P = 9.93e-08) (Fig. 11A) as well as Dendritic cells (r = 0.037, P = 4.12e-01; r = 0.112, P = 1.33e-02) (Fig. 11B) infiltration levels in TIMER database. According to the expression levels, we also explored the C6 and KLRG2 expression distribution across LUAD subtype (Fig. 11C,D). According to the heatmap analysis results, we found that B cell and Dendritic cell infiltration levels were negatively related to KLRG2, while being positively related to C6. We also validated the correlation between C6 and KLRG2 expression levels and immune cell infiltration levels in TISIDB database. The results showed that KLRG2 expression level was negatively correlated with B cell infiltration level (r=-0.1, P = 0.0227) (Fig. 11E) and Dendritic cell (r=-0.137, P = 0.00174; r=-0.141, P = 0.00132) (Fig. 11F,G), which is consistent with previous results. Regarding C6 expression level, it was positively correlated with Dendritic cell infiltration level (r = 0.2, P = 4.86e-06) (Fig. 11H) as well as B cell infiltration level (r = 0.234, P = 7.88e-08; r = 0.295, P = 9.07e-12) (Fig.11I,J). Heatmap analysis between C6 (Fig. 11K) and KLRG2 (Fig. 11L) expression levels and tumor infiltrating lymphocytes in TISIDB database was also performed.

Associations between somatic copy number variation (SCNA) levels of C6 and KLRG2 and TILs levels

The “SCNA” module of TIMER database provides the abundance of TILs among tumors with different somatic copy number aberrations for a given gene, we further explored the association between SCNA levels of C6 and KLRG2 with immune cell infiltration, respectively. The results indicated that copy number variations appears to correlate with C6 (Supplementary Figure S3A) and KLRG2 (Supplementary Figure S3B) expression in LUAD. Heatmap analysis between SCNA levels of C6, KLRG2 expression levels and TILs in TISIDB database was also performed (Supplementary Figures S3C-D). Furthermore, SCNA levels of C6 were negatively correlated with B cell and Dendritic cell infiltration levels (both r < 0; all P < 0.050) (Supplementary Figures S3E-H), while the SCNA levels of KLRG2 seem to have no relation with cell and Dendritic cell infiltration levels.

Previous studies have found that decreased C6 expression can exhibit significant response to immunomodulators. Therefore, to identify the expression patterns of C6 in response to immunomodulators, heatmap analysis was performed (Supplementary Figure S4). Additionally, we also explored the methylation levels of C6 across LUAD and found that methylation levels of C6 were positively correlated with B cell and Dendritic cell infiltration levels (both r > 0; all P < 0.050) (Supplementary Figure S5A). Furthermore, C6 mutation seems to have no relation to B cell (Supplementary Figure S5B) and Dendritic Cell infiltration levels (Supplementary Figure S5C).

Discussion

In our current study, the limma package in R software was first used to identify 44 DEPRGs, and then PPI network analysis and coexpression analysis were performed to further refine the potential roles of DEPRGs. Before performing Cox and Lasso regression analysis, we classified LUAD patients into two subtypes according to consensus clustering based on the 44 DEPRGs. In addition, Kaplan-Meier analysis results showed differential prognosis between cluster 1 and cluster 2. Therefore, based on these two clusters, DEGs of each cluster were identified. Subsequently, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to obtain 12 prognostic genes. Then, using an independent cohort from TCGA database, we performed a Lasso regression model and identified 23 prognosis-related genes for further study, and a risk model based on these genes was constructed to evaluate whether these genes could serve as independent prognostic factors for LUAD patients in TCGA database and we performed subsequent validation using GSE72094. After exploring the associations between DEGs and clinicopathological variables, 12 genes were identified as prognostic genes. After validating their expression levels and their relation to OS in GEPIA and Kaplan Meier-plotter databases, only C6 and KLRG2 were left for further immune-related mechanism exploration. Kaplan-Meier analysis indicated survival rates are related to different immune cell (B cell and dendritic cell) infiltration levels, so we further explored the correlation between these two gene expression levels and immune cell infiltration levels as well as their response to immunomodulators.

Pyroptosis plays a crucial role in the initiation, progression, and metastasis of various cancers, including LUAD. Although it is known that aberrantly regulated pyroptosis impacts the prognosis of LUAD, the underlying mechanisms remain poorly defined. Pyroptosis contributes to the inflammatory cell death of cancer cells and can inhibit their proliferation and migration. Some pyroptotic inflammasomes have been found to be downregulated in cancer cells25. Studies have demonstrated that pyroptosis is associated with tumorigenesis26–30. To be honest, pyroptosis exerts dual functions in tumorigenesis, including both positive and negative effects. The relationship between pyroptosis and cancer is complex and the effect of pyroptosis on cancer varies in different tissue and genetic backgrounds31. It can inhibit tumor development in the early stages by clearing damaged proteins and organelles, but in advanced stages, it may promote tumor growth by creating a microenvironment conducive to cancer cell proliferation32. Research into the mechanisms of pyroptosis has revealed that DHA can inhibit tumorigenesis by inducing pyroptosis in breast cancer cells, and STING-mediated Syk signaling may regulate tumorigenesis in colitis-associated colorectal cancer by modulating pyroptosis33. In addition, STING-mediated Syk signaling may regulate the tumorigenesis of colitis-associated colorectal cancer by modulating pyroptosis of tumor cells34. Naturally, PRGs have also attracted increasing attention for their significance to the development of various cancers35–38.

Recent studies have highlighted the significance of PRGs in cancer development, but next-generation sequencing and the establishment of predictive risk models associated with PRGs have not been thoroughly explored. Given the poor prognosis of LUAD, identifying novel prognostic biomarkers is critical. Recent developments include a PRG-related prognostic signature that divides patients into high-risk and low-risk groups, which has shown potential as an effective indicator for prognosis and immunotherapy response. One study proposed an PRG related prognostic signature and divided all patients into high-risk and low-risk groups, and the author concluded that the PRG prognostic signature may function as an effective indicator for the prognosis prediction and immunotherapy response of low-grade glioma patients39.Another four PRGs (MKI67, BTK, MST1 and TUBB6) were explored, and the author found that these genes not only were significantly associated with overall survival but also could change the immune microenvironment in LUAD40. In this study, we identified 23 DEGs using Lasso regression analysis. We then created a risk model based on these genes to assess their potential as independent prognostic factors for LUAD patients. This model was validated using the GSE72094 dataset. We further refined our analysis by identifying 12 DEGs from Cox regression analysis, of which four (SFRP5, MAPK4, C6, and KLRG2) were found to correlate with clinicopathological variables and prognosis in LUAD patients. Secreted frizzled-related protein 5 (SFRP5) is a member of the SFRP family of secreted proteins41 and adipocyte-derived SFRP5 inhibits breast cancer cells migration and invasion through Wnt and epithelial-mesenchymal transition signaling pathways42. MAPK4 could promotes prostate cancer by concerted activation of androgen receptor and AKT43 as well as triple negative breast cancer growth and reduces tumor sensitivity to PI3K blockade44. C6 was not only significantly downregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma samples compared with normal liver samples, but also found to be significantly associated with overall survival, disease-free survival, and progress-free survival in hepatocellular carcinoma patients45. The function of KLRG2 (also known as CLEC15B) is not known, it contains a conserved C-type lectin/C-type lectin-like domain (CTL/CTLD), which are believed to function in carbohydrate recognition and binding46.

After screening the validated prognostic genes, C6 and KLRG2, as attractive genes were selected for further specific study after considering its expression level and prognostic value. Pyroptosis is involved in immune cell infiltration levels and PRGs can affect immune responses. Numerous studies have revealed the indispensable role of pyroptosis in innate immunity and antitumor effects47,48. It has been demonstrated that global alterations in PRGs at the transcriptional and genetic levels in colorectal cancer49. Various previous clinical studies have suggested that immune cell infiltration levels have a major impact on the clinical outcomes of several cancers50–53. C6 encodes a component of the complement cascade and the encoded protein is part of the membrane attack complex that can be incorporated into the cell membrane and cause cell lysis, as characterized in Gene database. KLRG2 as known as CLEC15B, which was predicted to enable carbohydrate binding activity and integral component of membrane. In the present study, we found that C6 and KLRG2 expression is associated with the prognosis of LUAD. Furthermore, the expression levels of C6 and KLRG2 were correlated with B cell and dendritic cell infiltration levels. In contrast, Kaplan-Meier analysis results showed that only B cell and dendritic cell infiltration levels were significantly correlated with prognosis.

Our current study utilized gene expression profiles from the TCGA database to establish a risk model based on PRGs to predict LUAD prognosis. We validated the prognostic value of these genes and explored their association with clinicopathological variables and immune cell infiltration. This study is the first to construct a risk model based on 23 DEGs, providing new insights for monitoring and predicting LUAD patient outcomes.

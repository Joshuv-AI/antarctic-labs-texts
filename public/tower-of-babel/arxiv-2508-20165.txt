Boundary Carroll CFTs: SUSY and Superstrings
Arjun Bagchi, Shankhadeep Chakrabortty, Pronoy Chakraborty, Ritankar Chatterjee, and Priyadarshini Pandit

Abstract
We consider two dimensional superconformal Carrollian theories with boundaries and construct two variants of the Boundary Superconformal Carrollian Algebra (BSCCA), viz. the Homogeneous and the Inhomogeneous, by making appropriate identification of the parent superconformal Carrollian algebras. These new algebras are then recovered by appropriate limits of a single copy of Super Virasoro algebra. We then focus on the theory of null tensionless superstrings and construct, for the first time, an open null superstring. The Homogeneous version of the BSCCA is realised as worldsheet symmetries on this open null superstring.

2
Boundary Carroll CFTs: A recap of the Bosonic story

In the first part of our paper, we will focus on 2d Carroll CFTs, their supersymmetric extensions and how one introduces boundaries in these Carroll SCFTs. After a quick review of basics of 2d CCFTs, we will remind the reader of the bosonic version of Boundary CCFTs, specifically the emergence of a new algebra the Boundary Carroll Conformal Algebra (BCCA). We then recap the supersymmetric extensions of CCFTs and the fact that there are two distinct types of Superconformal Carrollian Field Theories (SCCFTs) that emerge from different Inönü-Wigner contractions of the parent 2d relativistic SCFTs. We will then go on to our main results in this part of the paper, where we introduce boundaries in SCCFTs, leading to new two classes of Boundary Supersymmetric Carrollian Conformal Algebras (BSCCA).

We begin in this section with a review of the bosonic version of conformal Carrollian symmetries, first the vanilla kind and then flavoured with boundaries.

2.1
2d Carrollian CFTs

We now briefly recap salient features of 2d CCFTs, viz. how to obtain it from a limit of relativistic 2d CFTs and a more geometric formulation of Conformal Carrollain symmetries in terms of conformal isometries of a Carrollian manifold.

From Relativistic to Carroll CFTs

The power of conformal symmetry manifests itself in all its glory in relativistic conformal field theories in
d=2
. It is well known that in 2d, there exists an infinite set of local conformal transformations generated by two copies of the Virasoro algebra

\begin{split}[\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{n},\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{m}]=&(n-m)\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{n+m}+\frac{c^{\pm}}{12}n(n^{2}-1)\delta_{n+m},\quad[\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n},\mathcal{L}^{-}_{m}]=0.\end{split}

(1)

Here
c^{\pm}
are the central charges of the Virasoro algebra. The infinite underlying symmetry of the relativistic 2d CFT helps is understanding these theories even without recourse to any underlying Lagrangian description.

We now move to Carrollian CFTs. Carroll symmetries appear in the vanishing speed of light limit
c
of relativistic theories and an Inönü-Wigner
c\to 0
contraction of the Poincaré symmetry results in the Carroll algebra
Leblond65
;
SenGupta:1966qer
. To understand how the same happens for a relativistic 2d CFT, let us focus on the generators of the Virasoro algebra defined on a cylinder parametrized by
(\tau,\sigma)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{n}=ie^{in\sigma^{\pm}}\partial_{\pm},

(2)

where
\partial_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\tau}\pm\partial_{\sigma})
. The Carroll contraction of these Virasoro generators amounts to taking the following limit

\displaystyle\tau\to\epsilon\tau,\sigma\to\sigma~\text{with}~\epsilon\to 0.

(3)

This is equivalent to taking the speed of light to zero and results in the emergence of Carrollian Conformal generators in 2d as

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=i\,e^{in\sigma}(\partial_{\sigma}+in\tau\,\partial_{\tau}),\quad M_{n}=i\,e^{in\sigma}\partial_{\tau},

(4)

where
\tau
is the null direction
\mathbb{R}_{null}
and
\sigma
parametrizes the angular coordinate of
S^{1}
. In terms of the Virasoro generators, this amounts to the contraction

L_{n}=\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}-\mathcal{L}^{-}_{-n},\quad\quad M_{n}=\epsilon(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{-n}).

(5)

These generators (
4
) satisfy the 2d Carrollian Conformal Algebra (CCA
2
):

\displaystyle\left[L_{n},L_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=(n-m)L_{n+m}+\frac{c_{L}}{12}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m},

(6a)

\displaystyle\left[L_{n},M_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=(n-m){M}_{n+m}+\frac{c_{M}}{12}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m},

(6b)

\displaystyle\left[M_{n},M_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=0.

(6c)

Here,
c_{L}
and
c_{M}
are the central charges. This algebra isomorphic to
3
d Bondi-Metzner-Sachs algebra (BMS
3
) algebra
Barnich:2006av
, which is the asymptotic symmetry algebra of asymptotically flat 3d spacetimes at the null boundary
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962wk
. This isomorphism between the conformal Carroll and the BMS algebras extend to arbitrary dimensions, forming the building blocks of holography in asymptotically flat spacetimes
Bagchi:2010zz
;
Duval:2014uva
;
Duval:2014lpa
.

The geometric formulation

We now move on to discuss the geometric aspect of the Carrollian and Conformal Carrollian symmetries
Duval:2014lpa
;
Duval:2014uva
;
Duval:2014uoa
. The Carrollian algebra corresponds to the isometry algebra of a flat Carrollian manifold. A Carrollian manifold in
d
-dimensional spacetime is defined by a degenerate symmetric rank-two covariant tensor field
h_{\mu\nu}
and a nowhere-vanishing vector field

v^{\mu}
that spans the 1-dimensional kernel of
h_{\mu\nu}
, i.e,
v^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=0
. The isometries of a Carroll manifold are generated by vector fields
\xi^{\mu}
that preserve the following Carrollian structure

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}v^{\mu}=0,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\xi}h_{\mu\nu}=0.

(7)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
denotes the Lie derivative along
\xi^{\mu}
. When the Carroll structure is flat, then the Lie algebra of these vector fields close to form the Carroll algebra. Here in this work, we shall mostly be interested in the conformal structure of Carroll manifolds where the conformal isometries are generated by vector fields that satisfy the Carroll conformal Killing equations,

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}v^{\mu}=\lambda\,v^{\mu},\qquad\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\xi}h_{\mu\nu}=-2\lambda\,h_{\mu\nu}\,.

(8)

Here
\lambda
is the conformal factor. Solution to the above conformal isometry equations (
8
) in 2d for flat Carroll manifolds (with coordinates
\tau
and
\sigma
so that
v^{\tau}=1
,
h_{\sigma\sigma}=1
,
v^{\sigma}=h_{\tau\tau}=h_{\tau\sigma}=0
) yields the Carroll conformal Killing vectors given as

\xi=[\xi_{M}(\sigma)+\tau\xi_{L}^{\prime}(\sigma)]\partial_{\tau}+\xi_{L}(\sigma)\partial_{\sigma}.

(9)

Here,
\xi_{M}(\sigma)
and
\xi_{L}(\sigma)
are arbitrary functions of spatial coordinate
(\sigma)
parameterizing “supertranslations” and “superrotations” respectively. The Lie brackets generate the (centerless)
d
-dimensional conformal Carroll algebra. The generators of the algebra of Carrollian conformal isometry can be defined as

L(\xi_{L})=\tau\,\xi_{L}^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\partial_{\tau}+\xi_{L}(\sigma)\,\partial_{\sigma},\quad\quad M(\xi_{M})=\xi_{M}(\sigma)\partial_{\tau}.

(10)

Expanding these functions in terms of Fourier modes:

\xi_{L}=\sum a_{n}\,e^{in\sigma},~~\xi_{M}=\sum b_{n}\,e^{in\sigma}
, the generators can be re-written as

\begin{split}L(\xi_{L})&=\sum_{n}a_{n}e^{in\sigma}(\partial_{\sigma}+in\tau\,\partial_{\tau})=-i\sum_{n}a_{n}\,L_{n},\\
M(\xi_{M})&=\sum_{n}b_{n}e^{in\sigma}\partial_{\tau}=-i\sum_{n}b_{n}\,M_{n}.\end{split}

(11)

It is evident from (
11
) form of the generators are same as (
4
) and permitting central extensions obey the 2d Carrollian Conformal algebra or BMS
3
algebra (
6
). The generators can be understood in terms of the geometry of 3d flat space on its null boundary
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
with cylinder topology
\mathbb{R}_{null}\times S^{1}
, where the
S^{1}
is sometimes referred to as “celestial circle”. The superrotations
L_{n}
generate diffeomorphisms of this celestial circle. The supertranslations
M_{n}
generate angle-dependent translations along the null direction, i.e. along advanced or retarded time.

2.2
2d Carrollian CFTs with boundaries

We now consider 2d CCFTs with boundaries. Specifically, we study these theories defined on the null cylinder with Carrollian structure

v^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=\partial_{\tau}\quad\qquad\extdm\!s^{2}=0\cdot\extdm\!\tau^{2}+\extdm\!\sigma^{2}\quad(\sigma\sim\sigma+2\pi)\,.

(12)

We remind the reader that the generators of 2d CCFT on the cylinder are given by (
4
). We now introduce boundary on the cylinder at
\sigma=0
and
\sigma=\pi
, as illustrated in Fig
1
.

Figure 1:
Null cylinder with boundaries

Boundary Carroll Conformal Algebra

From the figure, it can be clearly seen that the translation symmetry along the
\sigma
direction is broken. Hence the operators with a non-zero
\partial_{\sigma}
component at the boundaries cannot preserve our boundary conditions and have to be excluded from the algebra. Since
L_{n}
’s in (
4
) contain terms proportional to
\partial_{\sigma}
, we need to construct a new set of generators whose
\partial_{\sigma}
terms vanish appropriately at
\sigma=0,\pi
. Hence we define

\mathcal{O}_{n}=L_{n}-L_{-n}=2i\sin(n\sigma)\,\partial_{\sigma}+2in\tau\cos(n\sigma)\,\partial_{\tau}\,.

(13)

At the boundaries
\sigma=0
and
\pi
, this reduces to

\mathcal{O}_{n}=\pm 2in\tau\,\partial_{\tau}~~\text{for}~~\sigma=0~\text{and}~\pi,

(14)

thus preserving the boundary conditions. Similarly, we redefine

\mathcal{P}_{n}=M_{n}+M_{-n}=2\cos(n\sigma)\,\partial_{\tau},

(15)

where
\mathcal{O}_{-n}=-\mathcal{O}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{-n}=\mathcal{P}_{n}
,
\forall{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. At this juncture, there could have been different choices of the supertranslation generator we could have chosen, e.g.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}=M_{n}-M_{-n}=2\sin(n\sigma)\,\partial_{\tau},

(16)

or chosen to keep both halves and hence the original
M
. In our explicit construction of the open null string later in the paper, it is
\mathcal{P}
that would show up and hence we will focus on this. But we comment on the other choices later in the section in more detail.

We now compute the symmetry algebra satisfied by these boundary Carrollian CFT generators that preserve the boundary conditions we imposed on the Carrollian null cylinder. The resulting symmetry algebra called the Boundary Conformal Carrollian Algebra (BCCA)
Bagchi:2024qsb
is given by

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{O}_{n},\,\mathcal{O}_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\mathcal{O}_{n+m}-(n+m)\mathcal{O}_{n-m},

(17a)

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{O}_{n},\,\mathcal{P}_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=(n-m)P_{n+m}+(n+m)P_{n-m}+\frac{c_{M}}{12}(n^{3}-n)(\delta_{n+m}+\delta_{n-m}),

(17b)

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{P}_{n},\,\mathcal{P}_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=0.

(17c)

Here we see that there is only one possible central extension
c_{M}
in the cross commutator. It is interesting to note that we can use a single set of generators for the entire algebra at the cost of being somewhat more cumbersome. This new basis is defined by

\displaystyle Q_{n}=\mathcal{O}_{n}+\mathcal{P}_{n}

(18)

The algebra now reads:

\displaystyle[Q_{n},Q_{m}]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\left(\frac{3}{2}Q_{n+m}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{-n-m}\right)+(n+m)\left(\frac{1}{2}Q_{m-n}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{n-m}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c_{P}}{6}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m,0}.

(19)

When we refer to the BCCA, we will however focus on the previous version, viz. (
17
).

BCCA from a single Virasoro

We now show how to obtain the BCCA as a novel contraction of a single copy of the Virasoro algebra. To this end, we express the Virasoro generators in a different basis (useful when boundaries are introduced, as we will see below) defined as

\mathbb{L}_{n}=\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{n},\qquad\qquad\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}=\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}-\mathcal{L}^{-}_{n}.

(20)

In this basis, the Virasoro algebra take the following form :

\displaystyle[\mathbb{L}_{m},\mathbb{L}_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)\mathbb{L}_{(m+n)}+\frac{\mathbb{c}}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\delta_{m+n,0}

(21a)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{m},\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)\mathbb{L}_{(m+n)}+\frac{\mathbb{c}}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\delta_{m+n,0}

(21b)

\displaystyle[\mathbb{L}_{m},\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{m+n}+\frac{\tilde{\mathbb{c}}}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\delta_{m+n,0}.

(21c)

where
\mathbb{c},\tilde{\mathbb{c}}=c^{+}\pm c^{-}
. Using (
2
), the coordinate representation for these modified generators on the cylinder are

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{n}=-\frac{1}{2}\bigl\{e^{in(\tau+\sigma)}(\partial_{\tau}+\partial_{\sigma})+e^{in(\tau-\sigma)}(\partial_{\tau}-\partial_{\sigma})\bigr\}

(22a)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}=-\frac{1}{2}\bigl\{e^{in(\tau+\sigma)}(\partial_{\tau}+\partial_{\sigma})-e^{in(\tau-\sigma)}(\partial_{\tau}-\partial_{\sigma})\bigr\}

(22b)

The generators
\mathbb{L}_{n}
,
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}
as given in (
22a
) at
\sigma=0,\pi
, respectively become the following

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{n}|_{\sigma=0}=-e^{in\tau}\partial_{\tau},\,~\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}|_{\sigma=0}=e^{in\tau}\partial_{\sigma};\qquad\mathbb{L}_{n}|_{\sigma=\pi}=e^{in\tau}\partial_{\tau},\,~\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}|_{\sigma=\pi}=-e^{in\tau}\partial_{\sigma}.

(23)

We thus notice that
\mathbb{L}_{n}
s respect both
\sigma=0,\pi
boundaries while
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}
s are not compatible with them (since these contain
\partial_{\sigma}
). Looking at (
21
), one can see that boundary compatible symmetry generators form one copy of the Virasoro algebra. Now we can reproduce the boundary Carrollian conformal generators previously introduced in (
13
), and in (
15
) by taking the following ultra-relativistic contraction of the Virasoro generators in the new basis,

\mathcal{O}_{n}=\mathbb{L}_{n}-\mathbb{L}_{-n},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{n}=\epsilon\,(\mathbb{L}_{n}+\mathbb{L}_{-n}),\qquad\epsilon\to 0.

(24)

This of course is true at the level of the algebra as well, without recourse to the explicit form of the generators on the null cylinder. It can be easily checked that the above contraction reduces a single copy of the Virasoro algebra, here given by generators
\mathbb{L}_{n}
, to the BCCA.

Choice of boundary generators for BCCA

We now comment on the choice of the generators for the bosonic boundary CCA. We have seen above that the choice of boundary generators was

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}=L_{n}-L_{-n},\quad\mathcal{P}_{n}=M_{n}+M_{-n}

(25)

The choice of the combinations of
L
s was dictated by the fact that we wanted to put boundaries on constant
\sigma
, viz. at
\sigma=0,\pi
on the null cylinder where the field theory was defined. A priori, the choice of combination of
M
seemed arbitrary and we promised a more detailed account of the other choices, viz

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}=M_{n}-M_{-n}

(26)

and unrestricted
M
s. However, using limiting analysis from the Virasoro generators (
\mathbb{L},\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}
), we can clearly see that two of the above three (
\mathcal{R}
and
M
) are not allowed.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}=M_{n}+M_{-n}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\{(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{-n})+(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{-n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{n})\}=\epsilon\,(\mathbb{L}_{n}+\mathbb{L}_{-n})

(27a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}=M_{n}-M_{-n}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\{(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{-n})-(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{-n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{n})\}=\epsilon\,(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}-\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{-n})

(27b)

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\,(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{-n})=\frac{\epsilon}{2}(\mathbb{L}_{n}+\mathbb{L}_{-n}+\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}-\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{-n}).

(27c)

As we already know from the expression of
\mathbb{L},\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}
in (
23
) at
\sigma=0,\pi
, due to the presence of
\partial_{\sigma}
term,
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}
is not allowed at the boundaries. Hence, any generators containing
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}
as in
\mathcal{R},M
, are not allowed.

It is interesting to consider what would happen if we had disregarded this limiting construction. Although, it may seem at first glance
Bagchi:2024qsb
, that the algebras with
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{R}
are identical, there are crucial differences. This stems from the critical behaviour:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{-n},\quad\mathcal{R}_{n}=-\mathcal{R}_{-n}.

(28)

So, although the non-centrally extended infinite dimensional algebra stays the same, the central terms differ

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{P}_{m}]=(n-m)\mathcal{P}_{n+m}+(n+m)\mathcal{P}_{n-m}+\frac{c_{m}}{12}(n^{3}-n)(\delta_{n+m}+\delta_{n-m})

(29a)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{R}_{m}]=(n-m)\mathcal{R}_{n+m}+(n+m)\mathcal{R}_{m-n}+\frac{c_{r}}{12}(n^{3}-n)(\delta_{n+m}+\delta_{n-m})

(29b)

In particular,

\displaystyle c_{m}=c_{M},\quad c_{r}=0.

(30)

The second equality follows in a straightforward manner by noticing

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{R}_{n}]=\frac{c_{r}}{12}(n^{3}-n),\,[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{R}_{-n}]=\frac{c_{r}}{12}(n^{3}-n),\,[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{R}_{n}]=-[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{R}_{-n}]\implies c_{r}=0.

(31)

So the BCCA with
\mathcal{P}
allows for non-trivial central extensions, while the one with
\mathcal{R}
does not.

We close this section with comments on the finite dimensional algebra generated by the two different generators. The global part of 2d Conformal Carroll algebra has 6 generators:
\{L_{0},L_{1},L_{-1},M_{0},M_{1},M_{-1}
}. It is expected that once we introduce a boundary, we obtain 3 independent non-zero symmetry generators. If we consider
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{P}
, this expectation is met and we indeed have 3 independent generators viz.
\{\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{P}_{1}\}
. The algebra of these finite generators is given by

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{0}]=2P_{1},~~~[\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{1}]=2P_{0},~~~[\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{P}_{1}]=0.

(32)

This is actually the
iso(2)
algebra (or
iso(1,1)
since the algebra does not distinguish between spatial and temporal directions), as is to be expected. However, if we consider
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{R}_{0}
vanishes because antisymmetry, and we will only have
\{\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{1}\}
. These commute:

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{1}]~=~0.

(33)

Given all of these reasons, going forward, we focus on
\{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P}\}
as our bosonic BCCA generators.

3
Carrollian Superconformal Field Theories

Our first aim in this paper is to figure out how to supersymmetrize the above construction, i.e. we wish to build a supersymmetric boundary Carrollian CFT. As a prelude to this, we will begin this section by reviewing the supersymmetric extension of the 2d Carrollian CFTs. Just like the bosonic 2d CCFTs, these theories too, can be connected to appropriately defined Carrollian limits of the relativistic superconformal field theories (SCFTs). In earlier studies
Bagchi:2016yyf
;
Bagchi:2017cte
, it was found that there are two possible ways to find supersymmetric extension of 2d CCA, namely Homogeneous and Inhomogeneous Superconformal Carroll Algebras (SCA). In this section, we review both of these algebras and also show how they come from different Inönü-Wigner contractions of the Super-Virasoro algebra.

We start from the supersymmetric extension of the Virasoro algebra. For SFT without boundaries we have symmetry generators
\{\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n},\mathcal{Q}_{r},\mathcal{L}^{-}_{n},\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{r}\}
, which satisfy two copies of Super-Virasoro algebra given below

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n},\mathcal{L}^{+}_{m}]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n+m}+\frac{c}{12}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n,-m},

(34a)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n},\mathcal{Q}_{r}]

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)\mathcal{Q}_{n+r},

(34b)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{Q}_{r},\mathcal{Q}_{s}\}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{L}^{+}_{r+s}+\frac{c}{3}\Big(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\delta_{r,-s}.

(34c)

The similar algebra is satisfied by
(\mathcal{L}^{-}_{n},\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{r})
as well. There are two kinds of supersymmetric extensions of Virasoro algebra taking the same form given in (
34
). One is the Ramond algebra where
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and the other is the Neveu-Schwarz algebra where
r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
.

3.1
Homogeneous SSCA

We will begin our Carrollian explorations with the so-called
Homogeneous
limit, where the supersymmetry generators would be scaled in the same way. We take
the following Inönü-Wigner contraction on the symmetry generators

\displaystyle L_{n}=\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}-\mathcal{L}_{-n}^{-},\quad M_{n}=\varepsilon(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{-n}),\quad Q^{+}_{r}=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}_{r},\quad Q^{-}_{r}=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{-r}.

(35)

This is the homogeneous scaling since both
\mathcal{Q}^{+}_{r}
’s and
\mathcal{Q}^{-}_{r}
’s are being scaled by the same factor,
\sqrt{\epsilon}
. This scaling leads to the following algebra, which we call Homogeneous Superconformal Carrollian algebra (SCCA)

\displaystyle[L_{n},L_{m}]=(n-m)L_{n+m}+\frac{c_{L}}{12}\,(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m,0},

\displaystyle[L_{n},M_{m}]=(n-m)M_{n+m}+\frac{c_{M}}{12}\,(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m,0},

(36)

\displaystyle[L_{n},Q^{\alpha}_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)Q^{\alpha}_{n+r},\quad\{Q^{\alpha}_{r},Q^{\beta}_{s}\}=\delta^{\alpha\beta}\left[M_{r+s}+\frac{c_{M}}{6}\Big(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\delta_{r+s,0}\right],

where
\alpha
and
\beta
stand for
\pm
. In the rest of the paper, the homogeneous algebra would be the main focus of our attention. So to make the notation less cumbersome, we will not put any
(H)
labels on this. This above algebra SCCA turns out to be the asymptotic symmetry algebra of conventional
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity in 3d asymptotically flat spacetimes
Barnich:2014cwa
.

3.2
Inhomogeneous SCCA

The contraction from the two copies of the Virasoro algebra to the conformal Carroll algebra involved a linear combination of the bosonic generators. It is thus natural to envision such linear combinations while scale the supersymmetric generators as well. Interestingly, this leads to a richer algebra which has more non-trivial commutation relations. To see this consider the following Inönü-Wigner contraction of Super-Virasoro algebra (
34
):

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}-\mathcal{L}^{-}_{-n},\quad M_{n}=\varepsilon(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{-n}),

\displaystyle G_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{r}-i\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{-r},\quad K_{r}=\varepsilon(\mathcal{Q}_{r}+i\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{-r}).

(37)

This kind of scaling on the superconformal generators is known as inhomogeneous scaling since unlike the homogenous cases,
G
and
K
are contracted in two distinct ways. The algebra we get from this contraction is known as inhomogeneous super Carrollian conformal algebra (SCCA
I
),

\displaystyle[L_{n},L_{m}]=(n-m)L_{n+m}+\frac{c_{L}}{12}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m,0},

\displaystyle[L_{n},M_{m}]=(n-m)M_{n+m}+\frac{c_{M}}{12}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m,0},

(38)

\displaystyle[L_{n},G_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)G_{n+r},\ [L_{n},K_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)K_{n+r},\ [M_{n},G_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)K_{n+r},

\displaystyle\{G_{r},G_{s}\}=2L_{r+s}+\frac{c_{L}}{3}\Big(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\delta_{r+s,0},\ \{G_{r},K_{s}\}=2M_{r+s}+\frac{c_{M}}{3}\Big(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\delta_{r+s,0}.

We see now that in contrast to the homogeneous version above, the supersymmetric generators “square-up” to both the bosonic generators, giving more non-trivial structures in the SUSY sector of the algebra. It is interesting to note that this algebra has been also realized as an asymptotic symmetry algebra of an exotic theory of supergravity in 3d asymptotically flat spacetimes
Lodato:2016alv
.

We will be interested in applications to string theory, in particular to null superstrings. Both algebras have been realised in terms of worldsheet symmetries of closed null superstrings. The homogeneous one was first obtained many years ago in
Bagchi:2016yyf
and then understood as a Carrollian algebra in
Bagchi:2022owq
. The inhomogeneous one led to the discovery of a new null superstring in
Bagchi:2017cte
. Its tensile origins were explored in
Bagchi:2018wsn
. We will return to string theory at length in the second half of the paper.

4
Putting boundaries in Superconformal Carroll Field Theory

As described earlier, we wish to study the effect of boundaries in Superconformal Carrollian field theories (SCCFT). We will adapt an algebraic approach here where we deal specifically with generators and not their vector field representations. When we will address string theory later in the paper, we will return to the explicit vector field representations in terms of superspace.

4.1
Homogeneous theory with boundaries

We begin by exploring the Homogeneous SCCA in the presence of boundaries. In order to obtain the boundary version of the SCCA, we use the following basis of generators

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}

\displaystyle=L_{n}-L_{-n},~~~~\mathcal{P}_{n}=M_{n}+M_{-n},\quad\mathcal{H}_{r}=Q^{+}_{r}+Q^{-}_{-r},~~~~\mathcal{I}_{r}=Q^{+}_{r}-Q^{-}_{-r}.

(39)

Additionally, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}

\displaystyle=M_{n}-M_{-n},

(40)

and note that this generator is
not
a part of the bosonic BCCA. The redefined generators (
39
) are very similar to what we did for the bosonic theory and one of the supersymmetry generators would be picked out by the algebra. In order to see this, let us work out the commutators for the above sets of generators.

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{O}_{m}]=(n-m)\mathcal{O}_{n+m}-(n+m)\mathcal{O}_{n-m}

(41a)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{P}_{m}]=(n-m)\mathcal{P}_{n+m}+(n+m)\mathcal{P}_{n-m}

(41b)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{r},\mathcal{H}_{s}]=\Big(\frac{r}{2}-s\Big)\mathcal{H}_{r+s}+\Big(\frac{r}{2}+s\Big)\mathcal{H}_{s-r}

(41c)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{r},\mathcal{I}_{s}]=\Big(\frac{r}{2}-s\Big)\mathcal{I}_{r+s}+\Big(\frac{r}{2}+s\Big)\mathcal{I}_{s-r}

(41d)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{r},\mathcal{H}_{s}\}=\mathcal{P}_{r+s},~~~~\{\mathcal{I}_{r},\mathcal{I}_{s}\}=\mathcal{P}_{r+s},

(41e)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\{\mathcal{H}_{r},\mathcal{I}_{s}\}=\mathcal{R}_{r+s}},~~~~[\mathcal{H}_{r},\mathcal{P}_{s}]=[\mathcal{P}_{r},\mathcal{P}_{s}]=0.

(41f)

We draw the attention of the reader to the bracket between
\mathcal{H}_{r}
and
\mathcal{I}_{s}
(in red above). From this we see, when we choose
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P}
to be our bosonic generators of the BCCA (as we have done above), we cannot choose both
\mathcal{H}_{r}
and
\mathcal{I}_{s}
as their supersymmetric completion. We would have to choose one, and interestingly these lead to isomorphic algebras. For our explicit computations in the later section, we will choose
\mathcal{H}_{r}
and this is the one we display as the Boundary Superconformal Carroll Algebra (BSCCA), now displayed with central terms:

\begin{split}&[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{O}_{m}]=(n-m)\mathcal{O}_{n+m}-(n+m)\mathcal{O}_{n-m}\\
&[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{P}_{m}]=(n-m)\mathcal{P}_{n+m}+(n+m)\mathcal{P}_{n-m}+\frac{c_{M}}{12}(n^{3}-n)(\delta_{n+m}+\delta_{n-m})\\
&[\mathcal{O}_{r},\mathcal{H}_{s}]=\Big(\frac{r}{2}-s\Big)\mathcal{H}_{r+s}+\Big(\frac{r}{2}+s\Big)\mathcal{H}_{s-r}\\
&\{\mathcal{H}_{r},\mathcal{H}_{s}\}=\mathcal{P}_{r+s}+\frac{c_{M}}{3}(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4})\delta_{r+s,0},~~~[\mathcal{H}_{r},\mathcal{P}_{s}]=[\mathcal{P}_{r},\mathcal{P}_{s}]=0.\\
\end{split}

(42)

We will recover the same BSCCA as a residual gauge symmetry algebra of tensionless homogeneous open superstring worldsheet. In our superspace formulation, which we will introduce in the string theory section, once boundary is introduced in appropriate location of the superspace, between
\mathcal{H}
’s and
\mathcal{I}
’s, one set of the generators becomes incompatible with the boundary and hence need to be removed. For our choice of boundary in superspace, the generators
\mathcal{I}
’s are removed and we will be left with the algebra above.

From relativistic to Carroll boundary superconformal symmetry

We now show how the BSCCA emerges from the Carroll limit of a single copy of superconformal algebra of super Virasoro generators expressed in a different basis. Analogous to Virasoro generators (in different basis) we discussed in the previous section, here we define the super Virasoro generators as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{n}=\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}+\mathcal{L}^{-}_{n},\quad\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}=\mathcal{L}^{+}_{n}-\mathcal{L}^{-}_{n},\quad\mathbb{Q}_{r}=\mathcal{Q}_{r}+\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{r},\quad\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{r}=\mathcal{Q}_{r}-\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{r}.

(43a)

The super-Virasoro algebra in this basis becomes following

\displaystyle[\mathbb{L}_{n},\mathbb{L}_{m}]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\mathbb{L}_{(m+n)}~~~[\mathbb{L}_{n},\mathbb{Q}_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)\mathbb{Q}_{n+r}~~~\{\mathbb{Q}_{r},\mathbb{Q}_{s}\}=2\mathbb{L}_{r+s}

(44a)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n},\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{m}]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\mathbb{L}_{(m+n)}~~~[\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n},\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)\mathbb{Q}_{n+r}~~~\{\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{r},\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{s}\}=2\mathbb{L}_{r+s}

(44b)

\displaystyle[\mathbb{L}_{n},\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{m}]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n+m}~~~~~[\mathbb{L}_{n},\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{n+r}~~~\{\mathbb{Q}_{r},\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{s}\}=2\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{r+s}.

(44c)

We have argued in our bosonic review that for boundaries placed at

(\sigma=0,\pi)
,
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{n}
’s are not compatible generators as they contain
\partial_{\sigma}
terms. The boundary preserving bosonic algebra thus just contains only
\mathbb{L}
. It is clear from the cross-commutator between
\mathbb{Q}
’s and
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
’s, one set of the generators becomes incompatible with the boundary and hence needs to be removed.
At the level of the algebra, this choice is arbitrary and results in identical boundary algebras like in the case discussed above. We discard generators
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
’s, and obtain an algebra containing (
\mathbb{L},\mathbb{Q}
).

\displaystyle[\mathbb{L}_{n},\mathbb{L}_{m}]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\mathbb{L}_{(m+n)}~~~[\mathbb{L}_{n},\mathbb{Q}_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)\mathbb{Q}_{n+r}~~~\{\mathbb{Q}_{r},\mathbb{Q}_{s}\}=2\mathbb{L}_{r+s}.

(45)

This choice will later be justified by our superspace analysis. From the above algebra, one can obtain SCCA (
42
) through the following Inönü-Wigner contraction

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}=\mathbb{L}_{n}-\mathbb{L}_{-n},~~~P_{n}=\epsilon~(\mathbb{L}_{n}+\mathbb{L}_{-n}),~~~\mathcal{H}_{r}=\sqrt{\epsilon}\mathbb{Q}_{r},~~~\epsilon\to 0.

(46)

It is interesting to ask whether the above is the only BSCCA that one could obtain from the Homogeneous SCA. From our algebraic analysis above, it is clear that when the bosonic sub-algebra is fixed to be the BCCA (
17
), and when we consider the Homogeneous supersymmetric extension, (
42
) is the unique algebra that one can obtain. The choice between
\mathcal{H}
’s and
\mathcal{I}
’s does not change the algebra.

4.2
Inhomogeneous SCCA with boundaries

We now turn our attention to the Inhomogeneous algebra (
3.2
). We will play the same game as in the Homogeneous case. To begin with we choose the following combinations of generators:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}

\displaystyle=L_{n}-L_{-n},~~~\mathcal{P}_{n}=M_{n}+M_{-n}

(47a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{r}

\displaystyle=G_{r}-G_{-r},\quad\mathcal{K}_{r}=K_{r}+K_{-r}\quad\mathcal{G}_{r}=G_{r}+G_{-r},~~~\mathcal{Y}_{r}=K_{r}-K_{-r}

(47b)

We also take into account other generators:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{n}

\displaystyle=L_{n}+L_{-n},~~~\mathcal{R}_{n}=M_{n}-M_{-n}

(48)

We note that these
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{R})
are
not
generators of the bosonic BCCA (
17
). We now write down the algebra of the generators (
47
), omitting the commutators of bosonic generators given in (
17
) and zero brackets:

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{O}_{n},Z_{r}\right]=\left(\frac{n}{2}-r\right)Z_{n+r}-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r\right)Z_{n-r},\quad\text{where}\quad Z\equiv\mathcal{J},\mathcal{G},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{Y}.

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{P}_{n},\mathcal{J}_{r}\right]=\left(\frac{n}{2}-r\right)\mathcal{K}_{n+r}-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r\right)\mathcal{K}_{n-r},

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{P}_{n},\mathcal{G}_{r}\right]=\left(\frac{n}{2}-r\right)\mathcal{Y}_{n+r}+\left(\frac{n}{2}+r\right)\mathcal{Y}_{n-r}

(49)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\left\{\mathcal{J}_{r},\mathcal{J}_{s}\right\}=2\mathcal{X}_{n+r}-2\mathcal{X}_{n-r},}\quad{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\left\{\mathcal{G}_{r},\mathcal{G}_{s}\right\}=2\mathcal{X}_{n+r}+2\mathcal{X}_{n-r},}

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{J}_{r},\mathcal{G}_{s}\right\}=2\mathcal{O}_{n+r}-2\mathcal{O}_{n-r},\quad{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{r},\mathcal{J}_{s}\right\}=2\mathcal{R}_{n+r}-2\mathcal{R}_{n-r},}

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{K}_{r},\mathcal{G}_{s}\right\}=2\mathcal{P}_{n+r}+2\mathcal{P}_{n-r}\quad\left\{\mathcal{Y}_{r},\mathcal{J}_{s}\right\}=2\mathcal{P}_{n+r}-2\mathcal{P}_{n-r}\quad{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\left\{\mathcal{Y}_{r},\mathcal{G}_{s}\right\}=2\mathcal{R}_{n+r}+2\mathcal{R}_{n-r}}.

These generators
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Y})

do not
form a closed algebra. The problematic brackets are pointed out in red. It is clear that we would not be able to include
\mathcal{J}_{r}
and
\mathcal{G}_{r}
in a set of generators that contain generators of BCCA (
17
) as the only bosonic generators. So only the permissible generators will be
\mathcal{O}_{n}
,
\mathcal{P}_{n}
,
\mathcal{K}_{r}
, and
\mathcal{Y}_{r}
, which now close to form an inhomogeneous version of BSCCA that we regard as BSCCA
I
,

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{O}_{m}\right]=(n-m)\mathcal{O}_{n+m}-(n+m)\mathcal{O}_{n-m}

(50a)

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{P}_{m}\right]=(n-m)\mathcal{P}_{n+m}+(n+m)\mathcal{P}_{n-m}+\frac{c_{M}}{12}(n^{3}-n)(\delta_{n+m}+\delta_{n-m})

(50b)

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{K}_{r}\right]=\left(\frac{n}{2}-r\right)\mathcal{K}_{n+r}+\left(\frac{n}{2}+r\right)\mathcal{K}_{n-r}

(50c)

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{Y}_{r}\right]=\left(\frac{n}{2}-r\right)\mathcal{Y}_{n+r}-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r\right)\mathcal{Y}_{n-r}

(50d)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{K}_{r},\mathcal{K}_{s}\}=\{\mathcal{Y}_{r},\mathcal{Y}_{s}\}\;=\{\mathcal{Y}_{r},\mathcal{K}_{s}\}\;=\;[\mathcal{P}_{r},\mathcal{K}_{s}]=[\mathcal{P}_{r},\mathcal{Y}_{s}]\;=\;[\mathcal{P}_{n},\mathcal{P}_{m}]=0,

(50e)

where we have now also included central terms. The only non-zero central term is in the bosonic sector. The above algebra is somewhat unimpressive in the sense that the fermionic generators don’t do much. They don’t even “square” to the bosonic generators, – they just anticommute.
From a particular Inönü-Wigner contraction of (
45
), one can obtain the algebra given in (
50
). The contraction is as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}=\mathbb{L}_{n}-\mathbb{L}_{-n},~~~P_{n}=\epsilon~(\mathbb{L}_{n}+\mathbb{L}_{-n}),

(51)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{r}=\epsilon(\mathbb{Q}_{r}+\mathbb{Q}_{-r}),~~~~\mathcal{Y}_{r}=\epsilon(\mathbb{Q}_{r}-\mathbb{Q}_{-r}),~~~\epsilon\to 0.

(52)

The algebra (
50
), if restricted to one of the two generators
\mathcal{K}_{r}
or
\mathcal{Y}_{r}
is also obtainable as a further contraction of the Homogeneous algebra (
42
). So the boundary version of the Inhomogeneous SCCA is actually less rich than the Homogeneous version, in direct contrast to the original algebras without the introduction of boundaries.

In what follows, we will be interested in the Homogeneous SCA and will ignore the inhomogeneous version. It is possible that when we look at a different type of boundary in the original 2d bosonic CCFT where instead of
\mathcal{O}
, we use
\mathcal{X}
e.g. for a boundary in
\tau
instead of
\sigma
, the inhomogeneous version of the supersymmetric theory would become more prominent. We leave this for future explorations.

This concludes our generic exploration of Supersymmetric BCCFTs in this paper. We will now focus on a particular application of our general results in this part: the appearance of the Homogeneous version of the BSCCA as the residual symmetries on the worldsheet of the null open string.

5
Bosonic Null strings

As stressed in the introduction, null tensionless strings are to string theory what massless point particles are to usual physics based on point particles. Just as massless particles travel along null geodesics in spacetime, null strings sweep out 2d null worldsheets in the target space.

The recent revival of null strings have been focused on the understanding of the ILST action

Bagchi:2016yyf
and inhomogeneous versions

we encountered in the previous sections.

The recent analyses of null strings have been focused on the closed string sector and it was an open and challenging question what open strings meant in this context

5.1
Bosonic null strings: Closed

The point-particle limit of string theory is the limit where the length of the fundamental string
\ell_{s}=2\pi\alpha^{\prime}=1/T
goes to zero and this reduces string theory to Einstein gravity. Here
T
is the tension of the fundamental string. The other diametrically opposite limit is where the tension of the string goes to zero
T\to 0
making the fundamental string long, floppy and tensionless. It is this rather strange limit we would be interested in. As mentioned earlier, this is analogous to the massless limit of the point particle and the worldsheet becomes null.

The worldsheet formulation of null strings was first developed in
Lindstrom:1990qb
and later rigorously studied in
Isberg:1993av
. The worldsheet dynamics of null strings are governed by the ILST action, which takes the form on a 2d manifold
\mathcal{M}(\tau,\sigma)
,

S_{\text{ILST}}=\frac{1}{2\pi c^{\prime}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d\tau~d\sigma\,~V^{\alpha}V^{\beta}(\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu})(\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu})\eta_{\mu\nu},

(53)

where
c^{\prime}
is a dimensionful normalization constant, and it plays the role analogous to
\alpha^{\prime}
in the standard tensile theory. Once we vary the ILST action with respect to
X^{\mu}
and
V^{\alpha}
, we obtain the following equations of motion and the constraint equations,

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}(V^{\alpha}V^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu})\eta_{\mu\nu}=0,~~V^{\beta}\eta_{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu})(\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu})=0,

(54)

together with boundary terms. For the moment, we focus on closed null strings with periodic boundary conditions. We will come back to the boundary terms in detail when we discuss open null strings.

As in the tensile case for the Polyakov action, the ILST action has Poincaré invariance in target spacetime as well as diffeomorphism invariance (
\xi^{\alpha}\to\xi^{\alpha}+\epsilon^{\alpha}
) on the worldsheet. Here, under diffeomorphism,
X^{\mu}
and
V^{\alpha}
transform in the following way

\displaystyle\delta V^{\alpha}=-

\displaystyle V^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha}+\epsilon^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}V^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\beta}\epsilon^{\beta})V^{\alpha},\quad\delta X^{\mu}(\tau,\sigma)=\epsilon^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}(\tau,\sigma).

(55)

As the worldsheet diffeomorphism is a gauge symmetry, the null string action (
53
) requires gauge fixing. A convenient choice of gauge is the following

\displaystyle V^{\alpha}=(v,0),\hskip 14.22636ptv=\text{constant}.

(56)

It can be shown that in this gauge, there is a residual symmetry left over, again very much like the tensile string, but the usual two copies of the Virasoro algebra that form the worldsheet symmetry for the usual string theory now on the null worldsheet is replaced by the 2d Conformal Carroll algebra (
6

Bagchi:2015nca

Bagchi:2020fpr
;
Bagchi:2019cay
;
Bagchi:2020ats
. For details of these constructions, the reader is referred to
Bagchi:2015nca
.

5.2
Bosonic null strings: Open

We now return to the boundary term which we neglected in the variation of the ILST action. This is given by

\int_{\partial\cal M}\extdm\!\tau\,n_{\alpha}V^{\alpha}V^{\beta}(\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu})\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\delta X^{\mu}=0.

(57)

Here
\partial\mathcal{M}
is the spatial boundary located at
\sigma=0,\pi
(as shown in the figure
1
) and

is normal to the boundary. In order to make the boundary term vanish, we have the following options on boundary conditions at
\sigma=0,\pi

\displaystyle\delta X^{\mu}\big|_{\sigma=0,\pi}

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{Dirichlet,}

(58a)

\displaystyle V^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu}\big|_{\sigma=0,\pi}

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{Neumann,}

(58b)

\displaystyle n_{\alpha}V^{\alpha}\big|_{\sigma=0,\pi}

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{Null.}

(58c)

The first and second conditions, respectively, correspond to Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions analogous to the tensile open string. The third condition (

For what follows, here and in the supersymmetric case later, we will focus on the Dirichlet boundary condition. The other two, especially the null boundary condition as we described above, are very interesting and we hope to return to them in the near future.

6
Null superstrings

In this section, we construct a theory of open null superstrings based on the Dirichlet conditions advertised above. It has already been shown in the tensionless version of closed string theory that there exists two independent versions of the tensionless sector of the closed superstring, e.g., the homogeneous
Bagchi:2016yyf
and the inhomogeneous
Bagchi:2017cte
tensionless superstrings. Similarly, we naturally expect these two independent versions of tensionless superstring to still persist in the presence of a boundary. For our present purpose, we focus on the homogeneous version of the tensionless open superstring satisfying the Dirichlet boundary condition. In our previous analysis of the symmetry algebra, we saw that the homogeneous version led to a more interesting boundary algebra and we will realise this algebra in the context of string theory in the following.

6.1
Closed null superstrings

The tensionless superstring action is given by
Lindstrom:1990ar

S=\int d^{2}\xi~~\left[\left(V^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}+i\chi\psi^{\mu}\right)\cdot\left(V^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}X^{\mu}+i\chi\bar{\psi}^{\mu}\right)+i\bar{\psi}^{\mu}\rho^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\psi_{\mu}\right].

(66)

In this action
\psi^{\mu}
is the fermionic density carrying a weight
-\frac{1}{4}
, and pairs with the bosonic partner
X^{\mu}
. The fermionic partner for vector density
V^{\alpha}
is
\chi
, where the latter is related to the ordinary gravitino
\chi_{\alpha}
by the relation
\chi=V^{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}
. Once we vary the above action for supersymmetric null strings with respect to
X^{\mu}
,
\psi_{\mu}
, and
\chi_{\alpha}
we obtain the respective equations of motion

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\left(V^{\alpha}V^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}X_{\mu}\right)+i\partial_{\alpha}\left(V^{\alpha}V^{\beta}\chi_{\beta}\psi_{\mu}\right)-i\partial_{\alpha}\left(\rho^{\alpha}\psi_{\mu}\right)=0,

(67a)

\displaystyle 2V^{\alpha}V^{\beta}\left(i\chi_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}X_{\mu}+\chi_{\alpha}\chi_{\beta}\psi_{\mu}\right)=0,

(67b)

\displaystyle V^{\beta}\left(\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}\partial_{\beta}X_{\mu}+2i\chi_{\beta}\psi^{\mu}\partial_{\alpha}X_{\mu}-\chi_{\alpha}\psi^{\mu}\chi_{\beta}\psi_{\mu}\right)+\frac{i}{2}\psi^{\mu}\partial_{\alpha}\psi_{\mu}=0,

(67c)

\displaystyle V^{\alpha}V^{\beta}\psi^{\mu}\left(i\partial_{\beta}X_{\mu}-\chi_{\beta}\psi_{\mu}\right)=0,

(67d)

and a boundary term that we shall revisit shortly when we introduce null supersymmetric open strings.
The action (
66
) is invariant under worldsheet diffeomorphisms and supersymmetry transformations. Under the worldsheet diffeomorphism parametrized by
\xi^{\alpha}
the fields vary as

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}X^{\mu}

\displaystyle=-\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}X^{\mu},~~~~\delta_{\xi}V^{\alpha}=-V^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\xi^{\alpha}+\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}V^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}\right)V^{\alpha},

(68a)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\psi^{\mu}

\displaystyle=-\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\psi^{\mu},~~~~\delta_{\xi}\chi=-\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\chi+\frac{1}{4}\left(\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}\right)\chi.

(68b)

Supersymmetry transformation among the fields with parameter
\epsilon

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}X^{\mu}

\displaystyle=i\epsilon\psi^{\mu},~~~~\delta_{\epsilon}\psi^{\mu}=-\epsilon V^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}i\epsilon\left(\psi^{\mu}\chi\right),

(69a)

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}V^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=iV^{\alpha}\left(\epsilon\chi\right),~~~~\delta_{\epsilon}\chi=V^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\epsilon.

(69b)

The presence of the diffeomorphism on the null superstring worldsheet, like in the bosonic case, leads to a gauge fixing condition. This will be the tensionless equivalent of the conformal gauge for the usual superstring:

V^{\alpha}=(1,0),~~~~~~~\chi=0.

(70)

Note that the gamma matrices in the tensile theory satisfy the usual form of Clifford algebra (
120
) where we use
\eta^{ab}
as the gauge fixed tensile worldsheet metric. However, here, due to the presence of degenerate metric structure on the null worldsheet, we replace
\eta^{\alpha\beta}
by an appropriate combination of the gauged fixed vector densities. With this modification, the Clifford algebra of the gamma matrices in the tensionless superstring theory reads

\displaystyle\{\rho^{\alpha},\rho^{\beta}\}=2V^{\alpha}V^{\beta}.

(71)

A convenient representation of this modified Clifford algebra (
71
) is obtained by simply identifying the gamma matrices
\rho^{\alpha}
with the vector densities
V^{\alpha}
, i.e.,
\rho^{\alpha}=V^{\alpha}\mathbb{1}
where the explicit form of the
\rho^{\alpha}
matrices for homogeneous null closed superstrings is given as

\displaystyle\rho^{0}=\mathbb{1},~~\rho^{1}=\mathbb{O}.

(72)

In this gauge, the action becomes:

S=\int d^{2}\xi\left[\dot{X}^{2}+i\psi\cdot\dot{\psi}\right].

(73)

The equations of motion and the constraints take the following form

\displaystyle\ddot{X}^{\mu}=0,\quad\dot{\psi}^{\mu}=0,

(74a)

\displaystyle\dot{X}^{2}=0,\quad\dot{X}\cdot X^{\prime}+\frac{i}{2}\bar{\psi}\cdot\psi^{\prime}=0,\quad\psi\cdot\dot{X}=0,

(74b)

and the gauge fixed forms of the restricted diffeomorphism and the supersymmetry
under which the action (
73
) remains invariant are given as

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}X

\displaystyle=\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}X,\quad\delta_{\xi}\psi=\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\psi+\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha})\psi,

(75a)

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}X

\displaystyle=\bar{\epsilon}\psi,\quad\delta_{\epsilon}\psi^{\mu}=-i\rho^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}X\epsilon.

(75b)

Using the gauge in the second equations of (
68a
) and (
69b
), we obtain the following differential equations for
\xi
and
\epsilon

\dot{\xi^{0}}=\left(\xi^{1}\right)^{\prime},\quad\dot{\xi^{1}}=0,\quad\dot{\epsilon^{\alpha}}=0.

(76)

Solving the above equation gives us the form for the restricted diffeomorphism and supersymmetry transformations:

\xi^{0}=f^{\prime}(\sigma)\tau+g(\sigma),\quad\xi^{1}=f(\sigma),\quad\epsilon^{\pm}=\epsilon^{\pm}(\sigma).

(77)

To clearly illustrate the form of the generators for (
77

\xi
and
\epsilon
to
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
superspace by adjoining Grassmann coordinates

\displaystyle\sigma^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\sigma^{\prime a}=\sigma^{\alpha}+\xi^{\alpha}+\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{+}V^{\alpha}\theta+\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{-}V^{\alpha}\bar{\theta}

(78a)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\rightarrow\theta^{\prime}=\theta+\epsilon^{+}+\frac{1}{4}\theta\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}

(78b)

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\bar{\theta}^{\prime}=\bar{\theta}+\epsilon^{-}+\frac{1}{4}\bar{\theta}\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}

(78c)

Consequently, (
78

\Phi\left(\sigma^{\alpha},\theta,\bar{\theta}\right)
,

\delta\Phi=\left(\delta\tau\partial_{\tau}+\delta\sigma\partial_{\sigma}+\delta\theta\partial_{\theta}+\delta\bar{\theta}\partial_{\bar{\theta}}\right)\Phi

(79)

By making use of (

\displaystyle\delta\Phi=

\displaystyle{\left[\left(f^{\prime}\tau+g+\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{+}\theta+\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{-}\bar{\theta}\right)\partial_{\tau}+f\partial_{\sigma}\right.}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\left.+\left(\epsilon^{+}+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}\theta\right)\partial_{\theta}+\left(\epsilon^{-}+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}\bar{\theta}\right)\partial_{\bar{\theta}}\right]\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left[L(f)+M(g)+Q^{+}\left(\epsilon^{+}\right)+Q^{-}\left(\epsilon^{-}\right)\right]\Phi}.

(80)

Once we have this explicit form, it becomes straightforward to identify the generators of superfield transformation,

\displaystyle L(f)=f\partial_{\sigma}+f^{\prime}\left[\tau\partial_{\tau}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta\partial_{\theta}+\bar{\theta}\partial_{\bar{\theta}}\right)\right],\quad M(g)=g\partial_{\tau},

(81a)

\displaystyle Q^{+}

\displaystyle\left(\epsilon^{+}\right)=\epsilon^{+}\left(\partial_{\theta}+\frac{i}{2}\theta\partial_{\tau}\right),\quad Q^{-}\left(\epsilon^{-}\right)=\epsilon^{-}\left(\partial_{\bar{\theta}}+\frac{i}{2}\bar{\theta}\partial_{\tau}\right),

f=\sum_{n}a_{n}e^{in\sigma},\quad g=\sum_{n}b_{n}e^{in\sigma},\quad\epsilon^{\pm}=\sum_{n}\zeta_{n}^{\pm}e^{in\sigma}.

(82)

Once we substitute (

L(f)=-i\sum_{n}a_{n}L_{n},\quad M(g)=-i\sum_{n}b_{n}M_{n},\quad Q^{\pm}\left(\epsilon^{\pm}\right)=\sum_{n}\zeta_{n}^{\pm}Q_{n}^{\pm},

\displaystyle L_{n}=ie^{in\sigma}\Big[\partial_{\sigma}+in\tau\partial_{\tau}+\frac{in}{2}\big(\theta\partial_{\theta}+\bar{\theta}\partial_{\bar{\theta}}\big)\Big],~~~M_{n}=ie^{in\sigma}\partial_{\tau}

A direct computation of the commutators and anticommutators among

\begin{split}&[L_{n},L_{m}]=(n-m)L_{n+m}~~~~[L_{n},M_{m}]=(n-m)M_{n+m}\\
&[L_{n},Q^{\alpha}_{r}]=\Big(\frac{n}{2}-r\Big)Q^{\alpha}_{n+r}~~~\{Q^{\alpha}_{r},Q^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{s}\}=\delta^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}M_{r+s}\\

(85)

In the quantum theory of the null closed strings, we can recover the homogeneous SCCA algebra, including the central terms derived earlier in (

6.2
Open null superstrings: Boundary conditions

In this section, we present for the first time, a construction of an open null superstring satisfying Dirichlet boundary conditions and demonstrate how the null open worldsheet intrinsically realizes boundary homogeneous superconformal Carrollian algebra (BSCCA).
Here, we primarily focus on the boundary term, that we previously did not examine when we discussed the variation of the action (
66
).

\begin{split}\delta S_{bdy}=-\frac{T}{2}\int d^{2}\sigma\,\Big[\,\partial_{\alpha}\,&\left(V^{\alpha}\,V^{\beta}\,\partial_{\beta}X_{\mu}\delta X^{\mu}+iV^{\alpha}V^{\beta}\chi_{\beta}\psi_{\mu}\delta X^{\mu}\right)-i\partial_{\alpha}\left(\bar{\psi}^{\mu}\rho^{\alpha}\delta\psi_{\mu}\right)\Big].\end{split}

(86)

The purely fermionic part of the boundary term given by the last term in (

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\sigma\,\partial_{\alpha}\left(\bar{\psi}^{\mu}\rho^{\alpha}\,\delta\psi_{\mu}\right)=\oint_{c}dl~(\,\bar{\psi}^{\mu}\,n_{\alpha}\rho^{\alpha}\,\delta\psi_{\mu}\,).

(87)

One way to make this term vanish is the fermionic analogue of the null boundary conditions (
58c
) we found in the bosonic case:

\displaystyle n_{\alpha}\rho^{\alpha}=0.

(88)

Although we will not venture in this direction in the current work, this is clearly an interesting avenue to pursue. We plan to return to this in future work.

For the present purpose, our interest lies only in the Dirichlet boundary conditions, and we will work with the same gauge as mentioned in (
70
). The representation of the gamma matrices stays the same as (
72
). Additionally, for this particular choice of gauge, the action, equations of motion, constraint equations, restricted diffeomorphism and the supersymmetry transformation for the homogeneous null open superstrings remain the same as (
73
), (
74
), and (
75
) respectively. Interestingly, choice of the gauge
V^{a}=(1,0)
or {
\rho^{0}=\mathbb{1},\rho^{1}=\mathbb{O}
} enforces the fermionic boundary term given in (

\displaystyle\oint_{c}dl~n_{a}(\,\bar{\psi}\,\rho^{a}\,\delta\psi\,)=\int d\sigma\,(\psi_{+}\,\delta\psi_{+}+\,\psi_{-}\,\delta\psi_{-})\Big|_{\tau=-\infty}^{\tau=\infty}=0,

(89)

Unlike the tensile superstrings, the way the fermionic boundary term becomes zero does not lead to any particular boundary condition. Nonetheless, we still have a choice to introduce the Dirichlet boundary conditions in the fermionic sector by combining the bosonic Dirichlet condition with the gauge‑fixed supersymmetry transformations (
75
).

\delta_{\epsilon}X\Big|_{\sigma=0,\pi}=0,\qquad\implies\epsilon^{+}\,\psi_{+}=-\,\epsilon^{-}\,\psi_{-}.

(90)

Note that the coefficients of
\psi_{+}
and
\psi_{-}
given above differ from those in the tensile case (

) due to the distinct representation of gamma matrices. Similar to the theory of tensile superstrings, here also we exploit the fact that the presence of the boundary breaks half of the supersymmetry and thus following the similar steps given in (
A.1

\displaystyle\epsilon^{+}(0)\,=\epsilon^{-}(0)\,\implies\psi_{+}(0)=-\,\psi_{-}(0),

(91a)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{+}(\pi)=-\epsilon^{-}(\pi)\,\implies\psi_{+}(\pi)=\psi_{-}(\pi).

(91b)

Likewise, the Ramond sector takes the following form

\displaystyle\epsilon^{+}(0)\,=\epsilon^{-}(0)\,

\displaystyle\implies\psi_{+}(0)=-\psi_{-}(0),

(92a)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{+}(\pi)=\epsilon^{-}(\pi)\,

\displaystyle\implies\psi_{+}(\pi)=-\psi_{-}(\pi).

(92b)

The above two equations (
91
) and (
92
) give us the necessary constraints on the fermionic fields which we use to study their mode expansions etc. For the rest of our analysis we will be focussing on the Neveu-Schwarz sector.

6.3
Symmetry generators with superspace boundary

It is well known that in tensile RNS superstring theory, the gauge fixed action can also be rewritten
on a superspace where there are worldsheet coordinates {

The superspace boundary locations for both Neveu Schwarz and Ramond sectors for tensile RNS superstrings have been worked out for Dirichlet boundary conditions in Appendix

\displaystyle\{\sigma=0,\theta_{2}=0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\sigma=\pi,\theta_{1}=0\},\quad\text{where}\quad\theta_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}(\theta\pm\bar{\theta}).

Here we have assumed that the boundary locations in the superspace are not affected by the homogeneous Carrollian limit on the superspace. Note that our choice of boundary is restricted to the Dirichlet Neveu Schwarz sector of the open null homogenous superstring.
We know from

\sigma=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}=L_{n}-L_{-n},~~~~\mathcal{P}_{n}=M_{n}+M_{-n}.

Hence, we need to ensure whether this combination of generators also preserves the aforementioned superspace boundary. The generators
L_{n}

7
Conclusion and future directions

Summary

The current paper could be broadly divided in two parts. In the first part of this paper, constituting of Sec. 2 to 4, we formulated how to put boundaries on Supersymmetric Carrollian CFTs in
d=2
. Taking cues out of the bosonic version of the construction in
Bagchi:2024qsb
, which we recapitulated in Sec. 2, we explored how one can restrict the boundary algebra in the supersymmetric case. The SUSY extension already had two versions of Carroll conformal algebras, viz. the homogeneous and inhomogeneous (which we revisited in Sec. 3), and we found boundary versions in both. We showed how to obtain these algebras from a single copy of the Super Virasoro algebra. There were some surprises along the way. The inhomogeneous algebra, which is richer in terms of the number of non-zero commutators between generators, turned out to be less interesting when boundaries were inserted than the homogeneous version. We explained this phenomenon algebraically.

The second part of the paper dealt with string theory and in particular the first construction of a open null superstring. We again gave the reader a quick tour of the basics of closed null strings and their SUSY extension, as well as the construction of the bosonic open null string. The null open superstring was constructed by combining the constructions of the null bosonic open string and the null closed superstring. We concentrated on the homogeneous version and found that the same algebra as discovered in the first half of the paper was reproduced in the context of null open superstings thus providing a very satisfying first application of the new algebra.

Looking ahead

There are several pressing questions which arise from our analysis and ones we hope to solve in the near future. Below we list a few of them.

•

Inhomogeneous open superstrings:
We have seen that there are two boundary superconformal Carroll algebras arising from the homogeneous and inhomogeneous algebras. We dealt with the homogeneous algebra when we constructed the null superstring. This is the open string equivalent of the construction in
Bagchi:2016yyf
. There exists an inhomogeneous construction for the null superstring
Bagchi:2017cte
. It seems that the open sector of this superstring is somewhat less interesting and more restrictive from the point of view of the algebra which is like a contracted version of the homogeneous boundary algebra. It would be very interesting to figure out why there is such a difference in the homogeneous and inhomogeneous sectors of the open null superstring.

•

Null boundary conditions:
In our analysis of boundary terms for the open null strings, we came across a rather curious condition which we called the null boundary condition (
58c
). In the conformal gauge we were interested in for both the bosonic and the supersymmetric case, we saw that this condition was automatically satisfied hence there was no need to impose any further boundary condition from the point of view of the intrinsic null string analysis. Our construction in this paper (as also in the original
Bagchi:2024qsb
) focused however on the Dirichlet condition. We did this in order to connect with the boundary Carroll CFT constructed in the first half of the paper and also to tie up closely with the open tensile string. The theory constructed for the null open (super)string was a direct descendant of the tensile parent open (super)string. The null boundary condition, as mentioned earlier, is an avenue for further exploration into the belief that open and closed strings lose their distinction in the tensionless limit. This gives further credence to the novel closed to open transition encountered in
Bagchi:2019cay
.

•

D-branes:
We have looked at a theory of open strings and the natural next question is what of D-branes. Should all D-branes constructed out to tensionless theories be necessarily null branes or is there some wiggle-room for non-null branes? We hope to be able to use techniques in relativistic boundary CFTs like the construction of boundary states to understand D-branes from the point of view of worldsheet symmetries.

•

Quantization:
The quantization of the closed bosonic null string led to three different quantum theories built on three different vacua, which were the induced, flipped and oscillator vacua
Bagchi:2020fpr
. It would be interesting to understand whether all of these vacua support open strings and if all types of boundary conditions are compatible with these different vacua and hence different theories. The equivalent superstring analysis has not been performed and is a very important step to take.

•

Open-closed dualities:
The re-examination of open-closed dualities for the null string also remains an intriguing question. This is especially so as there is also the transition from closed to open strings as tension goes to zero
Bagchi:2019cay
.

We hope to address the above questions as well as several others regarding the field theory aspects of Boundary CCFTs, with and without supersymmetry, in the near future.

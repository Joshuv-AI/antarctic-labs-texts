Critical exponents of boomerang subgroups in the free group
Waltraud Lederle

Abstract
We construct, for the free group acting on its Cayley tree, boomerang subgroups whose critical exponent is arbitrarily close to the critical exponent of a given finitely generated subgroup.



1
Introduction

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space and
\mathrm{Iso}(X)
its group of isometries. For a discrete subgroup
\Gamma\leq\mathrm{Iso}(X)
, the
critical exponent
of
\Gamma
is defined by

\delta(\Gamma):=\inf\big\{s>0\,\big|\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}e^{-s\cdot d_{X}(x,\gamma(x))}<\infty\big\},

it is independent of the choice of
x\in X
.
Gekhtman–Levit gave the following lower bound for critical exponents of invariant random subgroups.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space and let
G<\mathrm{Iso}(X)
be a closed, non-elementary subgroup acting cocompactly on
X
and admitting a uniform lattice. Let
\mu
be an invariant random subgroup on
G
such that
\mu
-almost every subgroup is discrete and infinite. Then,
\mu
-almost every
H\leq G
satisfies:

1.

\delta(H)>\frac{1}{2}\dim_{H}(\partial X)
, and

2.

if
H
is of divergence type, then
\delta(H)=\dim_{H}(\partial X)
.

In a later paper
[
4
]
they give a similar lower bound on critical exponents of stationary random subgroups.

In
[
7
,
6
]
the authors introduced boomerang subgroups of countable groups. For a countable group
\Gamma
, we denote its set of subgroups by
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
and endow it with the subspace topology inherited from the obvious embedding
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)\subset\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
. With this topology
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
is called the
Chabauty space
of
\Gamma
.
Note that the set of finitely generated subgroups is dense in the Chabauty space, because for every
H\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
we can enumerate its elements as
H=\{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots\}
and then
H=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\rangle
.
The group
\Gamma
acts on the compact, metrizable, totally disconnected space
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
continuously via conjugation.
A
boomerang subgroup
of
\Gamma
is a subgroup
\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
such that, for every
g\in\Gamma
, there exists a sequence
(n_{k})\to\infty
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}g^{n_{k}}\Delta g^{-n_{k}}=\Delta
.
An
invariant random subgroup
of
\Gamma
is a conjugacy-invariant, Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
.
As a simple consequence of the Poincaré recurrence theorem, given any invariant random subgroup
\mu
of a countable group
\Gamma
,
\mu
-almost every subgroup is a boomerang subgroup of
\Gamma
.
This motivates the question which properties that hold almost surely for invariant random subgroups remain true for boomerang subgroups.
In this note, we show that the lower bound on the critical exponent does not.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F_{d}
be the free group of rank
d
acting on its Cayley tree.
Let
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
be a finitely generated subgroup,
O\subset\mathrm{Sub}(F_{d})
a neighborhood of
\Gamma
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, there exists a boomerang subgroup
\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(F_{d})
with
\Delta\in O
and
\delta(\Gamma)\leq\delta(\Delta)\leq\delta(\Gamma)+\varepsilon
.

The proof uses the visualization of boomerang subgroups via Schreier graphs introduced in
[
6
, Section 2]
.

The critical exponent is tightly related to the
growth rate
of a subgroup.
Consider again
F_{d}
, the free group of rank
d
, acting on its Cayley tree
\mathcal{T}
.
We denote by
B_{\mathcal{T}}(1,R)
the subgraph that is the closed ball of radius
R
centered at
1
in
\mathcal{T}
.
For
g\in F_{d}
we denote by
|g|
the usual word length.
We denote

c_{\Gamma}(R):=|\Gamma\cap B_{\mathcal{T}}(1,R)|=\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid|\gamma|\leq R\}.

An alternate description of the critical exponent is

\delta(\Gamma)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\ln(c_{\Gamma}(R))}{R},

where the limit exists by Fekete’s lemma.
Exponentiating we get
b(\Gamma):=e^{\delta(\Gamma)}
, and
b(\Gamma)
measures the
growth rate
in the sense that
c_{\Gamma}(R)\sim b(\Gamma)^{R}
.

In a recent sequence of papers
[
11
,
12
]
, Louvaris, Wise and Yehuda showed that every number in
[1,2d-1]
can be realized as the growth rate of a subgroup of
F_{d}
, and the set of growth rates of finitely generated subgroups of
F_{d}
is dense. This now immediately gives the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathrm{Boom}(F_{d})\subset\mathrm{Sub}(F_{d})
denote the set of boomerang subgroups.
Then,
\delta(\mathrm{Boom}(F_{d}))
is dense in
[0,\ln(2d-1)]
, and
b(\mathrm{Boom}(F_{d}))
is dense in
[1,2d-1]
.

In some sense this corollary means that there are boomerang subgroups that cannot be “typical instances” of invariant random subgroups, or a given stationary random subgroup.

A subgroup
\Delta\leq F_{d}
is
confined
if there is no sequence
(g_{n})
in
F_{d}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}\Delta g_{n}^{-1}=\{1\}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

The set of critical exponents of non-confined boomerang subgroups is dense in
[0,\ln(2d-1)]
.

Fraczyk
[
2
, Corollary 3.2]
showed that subgroups of
F_{d}
with critical exponent
\leq\ln(2d-1)/2
cannot be confined.
Lower semicontinuity of the critical exponent (see Proposition
3.1
), together with Theorem
1.1
now immediately implies the following.

Corollary 1.5
.

There exist boomerang subgroups
\Delta<F_{d}
such that the only invariant random subgroup supported on the closure of the conjugacy class of
\Delta
is the Dirac measure at the trivial group.

Remark 1.6
.

Using results from
[
4
]
about critical exponents one can derive a similar statement involving stationary random subgroups.
But still
[
6
, Theorem 1.8]
implies that nontrivial boomerang subgroups of the free group are geometrically dense.

Corollary
1.5
is another stark contrast to lattices of higher
\mathbb{Q}
-rank in simple Lie groups, like
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
n\geq 3
.
The main theorem of
[
6
]
shows that
every non-trivial boomerang subgroup there is finite and central or has finite index, in particular has only finitely many conjugates and is in particular confined.

An underlying philosophical question that motivated this work is: “How are boomerang subgroups more general than typical instances of invariant random subgroups?”
In this work, we look at the question from the geometric point of view of the critical exponent.
In joint work with Yair Glasner and Tobias Hartnick
[
5
]
, we consider a completely different angle: non-singular dynamics and orbit equivalence. We start from the observation that whenever
\mu
is a Borel probability measure on
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
such that each
\gamma\in\Gamma
acts on
(\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma),\mu)
as a conservative transformation, then
\mu
-almost every subgroup is a boomerang subgroup.
We then construct uncountably many such measures on
\mathrm{Sub}(F_{d})
that are singular with respect to every invariant random subgroup.
Whether there is a connection to this work is unclear, see Question
2.9
.

Schreier graphs and their cores

2.1
Schreier graphs

The subgroups of
F_{d}=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\rangle
are in 1-1 correspondence with (isomorphism classes of) directed, labeled, rooted graphs, where each vertex has exactly one ingoing and one outgoing edge labeled
a_{i}
for each
i=1,\dots,d
. An edge can be ingoing and outgoing for the same vertex, it is then a loop.
We call such a graph a
Schreier graph
.
We can write any
g\in F_{d}
uniquely as reduced word
g=b_{1}\dots b_{k}\in F_{d}
, where
b_{j}\in\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d},a_{1}^{-1},\dots,a_{d}^{-1}\}
.
We can interpret
g
as a path

1

Our paths and cycles are allowed to visit vertices and edges multiple times, and go against the direction of an edge, but have no backtracking unless explicitly allowed to.

in the Schreier graph starting at the root and walking along the edges with label
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}
, where “walking along
a_{i}^{-1}
” means “walking along the directed edge with label
a_{i}
into the opposite direction”. In addition
g
determines a vertex
v_{g}
that is the endpoint of that path.
The group
F_{d}
acts on the set of Schreier graphs by changing the root from
v_{1}
to
v_{g}
.

Given a Schreier graph, the corresponding subgroup of
F_{d}
is

\{\gamma\in F_{d}\mid v_{\gamma}=v_{1}\}
, or all
\gamma\in F_{d}
leaving the root fixed.
Conversely, if
\mathcal{T}
is the (directed, labeled, rooted) Cayley tree of
F_{d}
and
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
, then the
Schreier graph
of
\Gamma
is the quotient
\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma)=\Gamma{\setminus}\mathcal{T}
.
This correspondence between Schreier graphs and subgroups is equivariant, with respect to the action of
F_{d}
on Schreier graphs (via root change) and on subgroups (via conjugation).

The topology on
\mathrm{Sub}(F_{d})
can be formulated via the Schreier graphs as follows.
For a graph
\mathfrak{g}
and a vertex
v
, we denote by
B_{\mathfrak{g}}(v,R)
the subgraph induced by all vertices of distance at most
R
from
v
.
Given
\Gamma\leq\mathrm{Sub}(F_{d})
, a neighborhood basis of
\Gamma
is

\{\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(F_{d})\mid B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma)}(v_{1},R)\cong B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Delta)}(v_{1},R)\}
,
where
R
runs over all natural numbers and
\cong
denotes isomorphism as directed, rooted, labeled, directed graphs.
We hence can determine boomerang subgroups by their Schreier graphs, as described in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
6
, Section 2]
)
.

A subgroup
\Delta\leq F_{d}
is a boomerang subgroup if and only if for every
g\in F_{d}
and every
R>0
there exists an
n>0
such that the balls
B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma)}(v_{1},R)
and
B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma)}(v_{g^{n}},R)
are isomorphic as labeled, rooted graphs with root
v_{1}
and
v_{g^{n}}
, respectively.

2.2
Core graphs

Given a Schreier graph, it is only the part of the graph that contains all cycles that determines the corresponding subgroup. This part is called the
core
.
For a comprehensive treatment of the core we recommend
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
be a subgroup and
\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma)
its Schreier graph.
The
core graph
of
\Gamma
is the unique minimal connected (directed, labeled, rooted) subgraph
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)
of
\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma)
containing all cycles based at the root.

In particular, all vertices have valency at least two, except possibly the root.
From
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)
we can recover
\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma)
by hanging infinite trees at all vertices of valency less than
2d
.
Thus also (isomorphism classes of) core graphs are in 1-1 correspondence with subgroups of
F_{d}
.
Given a core graph
\mathfrak{g}
, denote
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{g})\leq F_{d}
the corresponding subgroup, so
\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{g}))=\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{C}(\Gamma))=\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
be finitely generated and

1.

There exists

The set of vertices

Let

Construction 2.8
.

Let
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
be finitely generated.
If
\Gamma
has finite index or is the trivial subgroup, there is nothing to do.
Otherwise, enumerate
F_{d}=\{g_{1},g_{2},\dots\}
.
Set
\Gamma_{0}:=\Gamma
.
Assume
\Gamma_{n-1}
has been constructed for
n\geq 1
; it is finitely generated hence
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma_{n-1})
is finite.
If
g_{n}
satisfies the equivalent conditions of Lemma
2.4
,
we can set
\Gamma_{n}:=\Gamma_{n-1}
.
Otherwise, let
R_{n-1}=\min\{R\mid\mathrm{C}(\Gamma_{n-1})\subset B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma_{n-1})}(v_{1},R)\}
be the radius of
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma_{n-1})
with center
v_{1}
.
There exists
M_{n}>0
such that, for all
m_{n}\geq M_{n}
,

•

the requirements of Definition
2.6
are satisfied with
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\mathrm{C}(\Gamma_{n-1})
and
g=g_{n}^{m_{n}}
, and

•

|g_{n}^{m_{n}}|>2R_{n-1}
.

So choose any
m_{n}\geq M_{n}
.
Set

\Gamma_{n}:=\langle\Gamma_{n-1},g_{n}^{m_{n}}\Gamma_{n-1}g_{n}^{-m_{n}}\rangle=\Gamma_{n-1}\ast g_{n}^{m_{n}}\Gamma_{n-1}g_{n}^{-m_{n}}.

By Lemma
2.7
we know that
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma_{n})=\mathrm{C}(\Gamma_{n-1})\sqcup_{g_{n}^{m_{n}}}\mathrm{C}(\Gamma_{n-1})
.
In particular, for all
n^{\prime}\geq n
, we have
B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma_{n^{\prime}})}(v_{1},R_{n-1})\cong B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma_{n^{\prime}})}(v_{g_{n}^{m_{n}}},R_{n-1})
.
By construction, we have
\Gamma\leq\Gamma_{1}\leq\Gamma_{2}\leq\dots

\Delta:=\bigcup_{n}\Gamma_{n}.

Also
\mathrm{C}(\Delta)=\bigcup_{n}\mathrm{C}(\Gamma_{n})

2.3
Critical exponents and cores

Let
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
. Recall that the critical exponent of
\Gamma
is

\delta(\Gamma)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\ln(c_{\Gamma}(R))}{R}
,
where

\displaystyle c_{\Gamma}(R)

\displaystyle=|\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid|\gamma|\leq R\}|

\displaystyle=|\{g\in F_{d}\mid\omega_{g}\subset\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)\text{ is a cycle based at }v_{1}\}|.

Hence calculating critical exponents amounts to counting cycles in core graphs.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
be a finitely generated subgroup.
Let
g\in F_{d}
be an admissible connector for
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)
with itself,
and let
J
be the length of the join segment.
Then

c_{\Gamma\ast g\Gamma g^{-1}}(R)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{R}{2J}\right\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2i+1}\\
\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}=R+2i(|g|-2J)\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2i}c_{\Gamma}(\alpha_{j}).

Proof.

In the graph
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)\sqcup_{g}\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)
, we call
\mathfrak{g}
the copy of
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)
containing the root, and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
the other copy. A cycle based at the root that does not remain in
\mathfrak{g}
has to travel the join segment
p
of
\omega_{g}
, which joins
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, back and forth.
Estimating the number of those cycles of length
R
hence boils down to seeing how often we can travel this segment back-and-forth during that time, and how often we can loop around inside
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
in between those travels.

The number of times the path between
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
can be traveled back-and-forth by a cycle of length
R
is at most
\lfloor\frac{R}{2J}\rfloor
.

Let now
0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{R}{2J}\rfloor
.
Consider a cycle in
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)\sqcup_{g}\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)
of length at most
R
based at the root that visits
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
exactly
i
times.
We can write it as
(p_{0},q,p_{1},q^{-1},\dots,q^{-1},p_{2i})
, where
p_{j}
is a path in
\mathfrak{g}
for
j
even, a path in
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
for
j
odd, and
q
is the unique path connecting
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.
Let
J_{1}
be the length of the initial segment and
J_{2}
the length of the terminal segment of
g
.

We now enlarge the cycle (possibly introducing backtracking to not change its homotopy type) to write it as concatenation of a cycle in
\mathfrak{g}
, followed by
\omega_{g}
, followed by a cycle in
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, followed by
\omega_{g}
backwards, etc.
For this, we need to add edges as follows. To
p_{0}
we add at most
J_{1}
many edges at the end (so that it ends at the root), and to
p_{2i}
we add at most
J_{1}
many edges at the start (so that it starts at the root).
Then, to each
q
we add
J_{1}
edges at the start and
J_{2}
edges at the end to make it into
\omega_{g}
; and analogously we add
J_{1}+J_{2}
many edges to
q^{-1}
.
Lastly, for each
0<j<2i
, if
j
is even we enlarge
p_{j}
by at most
2J_{1}
many edges and if
j
is odd we enlarge
p_{j}
by at most
2J_{2}
many edges.
We obtained now a closed path

(c_{0},\omega_{g},c_{1},\omega_{g}^{-1},c_{2},\omega_{g},\dots,\omega_{g}^{-1},c_{2i})
with backtracking,
where each
c_{j}
is a closed path that is based at the root
v_{1}
if
j
is even and based at
v_{g}
if
j
is odd.
This closed path actually lives in
\mathrm{Sch}(\langle\Gamma,g\Gamma g^{-1}\rangle)
, since it might happen that
\omega_{g}
is not contained in
C(\Gamma)\sqcup_{g}C(\Gamma)
, but this does not influence the rest of the argument.
Using
|g|=J_{1}+J+J_{2}
,
the total length of this closed path is at most
R+4i(|g|-J)
, of which
2i|g|
is spent on the paths
g
or
g^{-1}
.

Then, the number of cycles that visits the second copy of
\mathfrak{g}
exactly
i
times is smaller than or equal to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2i+1}\\
\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}=R+4i(|g|-J)-2i|g|\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2i}c_{\Gamma}(\alpha_{j}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Let

\Gamma
.

Remark 2.12
.

For
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
we have
\delta(\Gamma)=0
if and only if
\Gamma
is cyclic.
If
\Gamma
is the trivial group, then
c_{g}(R)=1
for all
R
; otherwise let
\Gamma=\langle g\rangle
and assume for simplicity that
g
is cyclically reduced, so
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)
is just a cycle of length
|g|
.
Then
c_{\Gamma}(R)=2\cdot\lfloor\frac{R}{|g|}\rfloor+1
.

Now we can estimate the critical exponent of subgroups of the form
\Gamma\ast g\Gamma g^{-1}
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
be a finitely generated subgroup.
Let
g\in F_{d}
be an admissible connector for
\mathrm{C}(\Gamma)
with itself.
Denote by
J
the length of the join segment of
g
.

1.

Assume
\Gamma
is not cyclic and that
2J>|g|
.
Then

\delta(\Gamma)\leq\delta(\langle\Gamma,g\Gamma g^{-1}\rangle)\leq\delta(\Gamma)+Ke^{-\delta(\Gamma)(2J-|g|)},

where
K
is the constant from Proposition
2.11
.

2.

Assume
\Gamma=\langle\gamma\rangle
is cyclic and nontrivial.
Assume
\gamma
is cyclically reduced and no prefix of
\gamma
or
\gamma^{-1}
is a prefix of
g
, and no suffix of
\gamma
or
\gamma^{-1}
is a suffix of
g
.
Assume in addition that
|g|
is divisible by
|\gamma|
. Then

\frac{1}{|\gamma||g|}\leq\delta(\langle\Gamma,g\Gamma g^{-1}\rangle)\leq\frac{1}{|\gamma|\sqrt{|g|}}.

Proof.

Write
\Gamma^{\prime}:=\langle\Gamma,g\Gamma g^{-1}\rangle\cong\Gamma\ast g\Gamma g^{-1}
.

(1) Plugging Proposition
2.11
into Lemma
2.10
we obtain

\displaystyle c_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(R)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{R}{2J}\right\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2i+1}\\
\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}=R+2i(|g|-2J)\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2i}c_{\Gamma}(\alpha_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{R}{2J}\right\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2i+1}\\
\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}=R+2i(|g|-2J)\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2i}Ke^{\delta(\Gamma)\alpha_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{R}{2J}\right\rfloor}K^{2i+1}e^{\delta(\Gamma)(R+2i(|g|-2J))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2i+1}\\
\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}=R+2i(|g|-2J)\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=e^{\delta(\Gamma)R}\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{R}{2J}\right\rfloor}K^{2i+1}e^{2i\delta(\Gamma)(|g|-2J)}\binom{R+2i(|g|-2J)+2i}{2i}

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{\delta(\Gamma)R}\sum_{j=0}^{R}K^{j}e^{j\cdot\delta(\Gamma)(|g|-2J)}\binom{R}{j}

\displaystyle=Ke^{\delta(\Gamma)R}(1+Ke^{\delta(\Gamma)(|g|-2J)})^{R}.

Hence

\displaystyle\delta(\Gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\ln(Ke^{\delta(\Gamma)R}(1+Ke^{\delta(\Gamma)(|g|-2J)})^{R})}{R}

\displaystyle=\delta(\Gamma)+\ln(1+Ke^{\delta(\Gamma)(|g|-2J)})

\displaystyle\leq\delta(\Gamma)+Ke^{\delta(\Gamma)(|g|-2J)}.

(2) We first assume that
|\gamma|=1
, so without loss of generality
\Gamma=\langle a_{1}\rangle
. Let
n=|g|
.
Consider the polynomial

P_{n}(t):=\begin{cases}2t^{n}+t-1&n\text{ odd}\\
2\frac{t^{n}+t}{t+1}-1&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}

Kwon and Park showed in
[
9
,
10
]
that
\delta(\Gamma^{\prime})=\ln(x_{n}^{-1})
, where
x_{n}
is the unique real root of
P_{n}(t)
.
Now we can compute using the intermediate value theorem that

e^{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}\leq x_{n}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{n}}
and hence

\frac{1}{n}\leq\delta(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

For the general case,
using the definition of the critical exponent with the Poincaré series, it is easy to see that if we scale the metric by a factor
\lambda>0
, then the critical exponent of a subgroup gets divided by
\lambda
.
Hence, if we scale the above situation by the factor
|\gamma|
we get

\frac{1}{|\gamma||g|}\leq\delta(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{|\gamma|\sqrt{|g|}}.

∎

3
Proof of the main theorem

As an important tool we need that the critical exponent is lower semi-continuous.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
4
, Proposition 10.4]
)
.

Let
(\Gamma_{k})
be a converging sequence of subgroups and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_{k}=\Gamma
. Then

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\delta(\Gamma_{k})\geq\delta(\Gamma).

In particular, if
\Gamma_{1}\leq\Gamma_{2}\leq\dots
then
\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta(\Gamma_{k})=\delta(\Gamma)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If
\Gamma\leq F_{d}
has finite index, there is nothing to show.
Without loss of generality, assume that there exists
R>0
such that

O=\{\Gamma^{\prime}\leq F_{d}\mid B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma^{\prime})}(v_{1},R)\cong B_{\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma)}(v_{1},R)\}
.
Also, by adding a suitable generator if necessary using Proposition
2.13
(2), we can assume without loss of generality that
\Gamma
is not cyclic.

Enumerate the elements
F_{d}=\{g_{1},g_{2},\dots\}
; assume that
\langle g_{1}\rangle\cap\Gamma=\{1\}
. We perform Construction
2.8
, and also use the notation from there.
Choosing
m_{1}
large enough, we can ensure that
\Gamma_{n}\in O
for all
n\geq 1
.
Moreover, for every
n\geq 1
, by Proposition
2.13
, we can ensure that
m_{n}
is large enough such that
\delta(\Gamma_{n})<\delta(\Gamma_{n-1})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{n}}
.
Then by Proposition
3.1
we have
\delta(\Delta)<\delta(\Gamma)+\varepsilon
.

∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

The first part is clear from the above proof.
The second follows with Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
3.1
.
∎

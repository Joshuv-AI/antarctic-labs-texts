We propose a BRST extension of the higher-spin gauge theory in
AdS_{4}
with the
BRST operator associated with the local Lorentz symmetry. Our construction supports
manifest local Lorentz covariance and

1
Introduction

The Cartan formulation of gravity in terms of differential forms is
based on the two fundamental symmetry principles: diffeomorphisms and local Lorentz symmetry. The
latter acts on all Lorentz tensor-spinors and reduces the formulation in terms of
the vielbein one-form to that in terms of the metric. To consider a theory of gravity
interacting with any matter it is crucially important to respect both of
these symmetries. The formulation in terms of exterior algebra automatically respects
diffeomorphisms. In particular, this is true in the unfolded formulation of the
nonlinear higher-spin (HS) gauge theory of
[
1
]
. The situation with local Lorentz symmetry is more tricky.
Though, naively, the formulation of
[
1
]
is
\mathfrak{sl}(2|\mathbb{C})
invariant, this
\mathfrak{sl}(2|\mathbb{C})
is
not manifest due to a non-zero
vacuum expectation value of one of the fields. That the full nonlinear equations of

[
1
]
respect local the Lorentz symmetry
is a delicate property originally shown in
[
2
]
. As explained in
[
3
]
(see also
[
4
]
),
after all it follows from the special form of the HS equations related to
the deformed oscillator algebra of
[
5
,
6
]
providing an alternative
form of the Wigner oscillator
[
7
]
.

Though HS theory is proven to be Lorentz covariant, the problem of finding proper
variables in which it is manifestly covariant is involved and has been studied by
a number of authors within different formalisms
[
8
]
-
[
12
]
. An essential progress was made
in
[
13
]
where an explicit construction was found for a particular formulation of
the theory resulting from the so-called conventional homotopy approach which, being the
simplest, is not necessarily appropriate from the locality perspective (see e.g.

[
14
]
). In this paper we present a new approach allowing to build
manifestly Lorentz covariant formulation of the HS gauge theory in any homotopy scheme including the recently proposed
differential homotopy
[
15
]
. It is analogous to the
BRST
extended version of the HS equations proposed recently
in
[
16
]
to formulate the
\mathfrak{sp}(2)
invariance condition and factorization transformations in the
d
-dimensional HS models.
In this paper we use
the BRST language to Lorentz covariantize the spinor form of HS equations in
AdS_{4}

with the BRST sector directly associated with the manifest local Lorentz symmetry.
This leads to an additional shift symmetry acting both on
the spin two Lorentz connection
\omega^{L}
of the original HS theory and on a new Lorentz
connection
\hat{\omega}
. By construction, the new Lorentz connection
\hat{\omega}
acts
in a manifestly Lorentz covariant way on all multispinors. Therefore, the gauge condition

\omega^{L}=0
with respect to the shift symmetry leads to a manifestly Lorentz covariant formulation of the theory.

The layout of the rest of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we recall the form of the HS equations and the shifted and differential homotopy
approaches to their perturbative analysis.
The Ansatzes for the linear in
\eta
(
\bar{\eta}
) deformations are also recalled. In Section
3
the derivation
of the Lorentz covariant HS (LCHS) equations via BRST formalism is presented, the
gauge symmetries of the resulting system are considered and a part of them is shown to
form a Stueckelberg symmetry allowing to
formulate the theory in the manifestly Lorentz covariant form.
In Sections
3.4
and
3.5
the vacuum solution and the linearized analysis of
LCHS are described within the shifted homotopy approach.
The differential homotopy approach is elaborated in Section
4
where it
is argued to be most convenient in the BRST formalism.
Conclusions are in Section
5
.

2.2.2
Differential homotopy

Repeated application of contracting homotopies leads to multiple integrals

\int{dt^{1}}\int{dt^{2}}\ldots

All integration parameters

(2.29)

The consistency condition

Functions like

Differential homotopy is based on the removal of the integrals.
Namely, following
[
15

(2.31)

resulting in

(2.32)

where

does not contribute to the integral because its degree
\deg g^{weak}
as a form in the locus
{\cal M}
of

differs
from
\dim{\cal M}
. Setting

(taking into account that

and
\deg f=\dim{\cal M}
) and replacing

we obtain (
2.29

Though the integrals are removed from the equations,
to avoid a sign ambiguity due to (anti)commutativity of
differential forms, every differential expression
is accompanied with integrals.
As in
[
15
]

\int_{{\cal M}}fdt^{1}\ldots dt^{k}=\int_{t^{1}}\ldots\int_{t^{k}}f:=\int_{t^{1}\ldots t^{k}}f\,

5
Conclusion

In this paper, an extension of the standard equations of the
HS gauge theory is proposed that makes local Lorentz symmetry of the model manifest. The main idea is to use its BRST extension
a la

[
16
]
with respect to the local
\mathfrak{sl}(2|\mathbb{C})
Lorentz symmetry
in four dimensions. This leads to an additional Stueckelberg symmetry trading
the spin two Lorentz connection
\omega^{L}
of the original HS theory for the new

\mathfrak{sl}(2|\mathbb{C})
gauge field
\hat{\omega}
, that allows one to impose the gauge condition

\omega^{L}=0
yielding the proper Lorentz covariant derivative
D^{L}
with respect
to
\hat{\omega}
at all stages of the computations.
There are also new space-time two-form fields
U^{\alpha\beta}
and
\bar{U}^{{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\beta}}}

associated with the expansion in powers of the BRST antighosts
b_{\alpha\beta}

and
\bar{b}_{{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\beta}}}
, that play a role of the Riemann tensor being
expressed in terms of the other fields by virtue of the gauge condition
\omega^{L}=0
. The fields

U^{\alpha\beta}
and
\bar{U}^{{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\beta}}}
are analogous to the field tensors

R^{\alpha\beta}
and
\bar{R}^{{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\beta}}}
introduced in
[
13
]
.

Note that the role of the BRST extension of the HS theory of this paper differs from that of the usual
BRST formulations of HS theories both in the direct BRST description of the HS spectra
and vertices (see,
e.g.,

[
22
]
-
[
27
]
and references therein) and in the approach
of
[
16
]
, where the BRST operator is constructed from a kind of Howe dual algebra to the HS algebra.
In the approach of this paper, the BRST
operator originates from an additional Lorentz algebra, that generates an additional Lorentz connection related to
the Lorentz component of the original HS field via a nonlinear
Stueckelberg symmetry. As such the BRST operator of this paper plays an auxiliary role and
cannot be used alone for the description of the theory as a whole.

The advantage of the
proposed scheme compared to that of
[
13
]
is that it applies to any homotopy solution scheme of the HS equations
including, as explained in the paper, the differential homotopy of
[
15
]

while the approach of
[
13
]
was elaborated for the simplest conventional
(not shifted) homotopy.
The proposed general scheme is illustrated by the lower-order analysis of the theory to demonstrate its computational efficiency.
Let us stress that the proposed approach can be directly applied
to more general HS models in
AdS_{3}
and
AdS_{4}
including the extended system of
[
28
]
, Coxeter HS theories of
[
29
]

(see also
[
30
]
) and the models of
[
12
]
.

Successful Treatment of Recalcitrant Pyoderma Gangrenosum with Baricitinib in Two Patients with Rheumatoid Arthritis
Authors: Christian Ciolfi, Bernd Raffeiner, Margherita Zen, Anna Giorgia Osele, Jacopo Tartaglia, Stefano Piaserico
Published: 2026

Introduction

Pyoderma gangrenosum (PG) is an ulcerative, inflammatory cutaneous disease characterized by painful skin ulcerations with violaceous, undermined borders and a predilection for the lower extremities. It belongs to the spectrum of neutrophilic dermatoses and is often associated with systemic diseases, including inflammatory bowel diseases, rheumatoid arthritis (RA), and hematological disorders or malignancies.

PG currently lacks standardized therapy. Traditional therapeutic options include immunosuppressive agents such as systemic corticosteroids and cyclosporine, immunomodulatory drugs such as dapsone, and biologics such as tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α inhibitors [1].

Managing refractory cases remains challenging. We herein present two cases of PG refractory to multiple lines of therapy, successfully treated with baricitinib.

Case Presentations

Case 1

A 65-year-old female patient came to our attention in 2018 for the evaluation of painful skin ulcers of the lower extremities (Figure 1A), which had developed four years before.

The patient also suffered from severe erosive RA, which had required multiple treatments, including systemic corticosteroids, cyclosporine, methotrexate, and leflunomide.

Skin biopsy in 2014 was compatible with PG, revealing ulceration of the epidermis, subepidermal edema, and a dense, diffuse infiltrate with neutrophils, lymphocytes, histiocytes, and plasma cells in the dermis.

Despite multiple therapies over the years, including high-dose corticosteroid pulses, dapsone, infliximab, etanercept, adalimumab, tocilizumab, and ustekinumab, the patient experienced only limited improvement. In 2018, treatment with baricitinib 4 mg/day was initiated, resulting in significant improvement within a few weeks and complete remission after six months (Figure 1B). To date, baricitinib has maintained clinical remission for over seven years, with sustained control at both the articular and skin levels, allowing gradual withdrawal of systemic corticosteroids.

Case 2

In 2009 a 66-year-old female was diagnosed with severe seropositive erosive RA. Following primary failure of methotrexate, she was treated sequentially with several biologics (etanercept, abatacept, tocilizumab) in combination with methotrexate. In 2012, while receiving tocilizumab, the patient developed her first episode of PG, with skin ulcers affecting the left ankle and forearm (Figure 2A). Tocilizumab was discontinued, and the patient was treated with systemic glucocorticoids, achieving complete resolution of skin lesions. In 2015, while on methotrexate and rituximab for joint disease control, she experienced a recurrence of PG. Management included cyclosporine, topical dapsone, and anakinra, which resulted in only partial clinical improvement. In 2019, a flare of RA and worsening of PG occurred despite treatment with cyclosporine, methotrexate, low-dose glucocorticoids, and anakinra (Figure 2A). A second anti-TNF-α agent, adalimumab, in combination with methotrexate was initiated but did not yield improvement in either joint or skin symptoms. Consequently, in 2020, baricitinib 2 mg/day (adjusted for age and frailty) was introduced, alongside low-dose glucocorticoids and methotrexate. This regimen led to rapid clinical improvement in both joint and skin manifestations, with remission achieved within six months (Figure 2B). Methotrexate was subsequently discontinued without relapse.

Conclusions

The pathogenesis of PG is multifactorial and incompletely understood, being characterized by profound neutrophil-driven inflammation. Histopathological studies of PG lesions demonstrate upregulation of inflammatory mediators, including IL-36, IL-1β, IL-8, IL-17, and TNF-α [2].

In this context, Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors have emerged as a potential therapeutic option in refractory PG [3].

Baricitinib is an oral selective JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor approved for several immune-mediated inflammatory diseases, including RA. Baricitinib may exert therapeutic effects in PG by inhibiting neutrophil migration in response to IL-8, modulating the IL-17/23 signaling axis, and indirectly controlling inflammatory comorbidities such as RA [4].

To date, several cases of refractory PG successfully treated with baricitinib have been reported in the literature, highlighting its potential in this setting [3–6].

Our findings align with this emerging evidence. In addition to its efficacy on PG, baricitinib demonstrated rapid and sustained control of severe RA in both patients, permitting de-escalation of concomitant immunosuppressive therapies.

Baricitinib achieved rapid healing and durable remission of refractory PG in two patients with severe rheumatoid arthritis, enabling tapering of concomitant immunosuppression. Further data are needed to define its role in PG management.

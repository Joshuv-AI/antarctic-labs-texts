Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation With Robotic-Assisted Thoracic Surgery for Treatment of Giant Mediastinal Liposarcoma
Authors: Manuel Antonio Villa Sanchez, Adham Ahmed, Rohit Shahani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
A 61-year-old woman presented to our team with giant mediastinal liposarcoma causing compression of the esophagus, bronchus, and left atrium. Given the patient’s advanced presentation and reduced cardiac reserve, rescue venoarterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation was used in addition to standard robotic-assisted thoracic surgery for mass resection.

Although originally used in acute settings to manage refractory cardiorespiratory failure, there is growing evidence to support the use of venoarterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VA-ECMO) for adjunctive hemodynamic support in patients undergoing major thoracic surgery.1 Myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma is a highly aggressive sarcoma with a predilection for mediastinal growth.2 We report our experience using VA-ECMO to facilitate robotic-assisted thoracic surgery for resection of mediastinal liposarcoma causing compressive symptoms. A 61-year-old woman with past medical history of hypertension presented to the emergency department with complaints of persistent dyspnea and mild chest pain. Of note, she was currently visiting the country on a visa from Montenegro, where she had undergone surgery 6 weeks prior to remove a mediastinal mass. She endorsed that a few weeks later she returned to the hospital with recurrent shortness of breath and was told that her mass was not removed completely and that she had advanced-stage disease. The patient did not receive any further treatment after her index operation in Montenegro.

In the emergency department at our hospital, the patient was hemodynamically stable with unremarkable blood and metabolic laboratory work-up. Computed tomography angiography showed a large subcarinal low density mass with associated mass effect mostly on the left atrium and an associated large left pleural effusion on the side of her previous thoracotomy (Video 1, Figure 1). Transthoracic echocardiography confirmed severe left atrial compression, intact left ventricular ejection fraction, with moderately reduced right ventricular systolic function (Video 2). The patient underwent thoracentesis with removal of 1.6 L of serous fluid from the left hemithorax. Thoracic surgery was consulted for surgical resection. After a multidisciplinary discussion among our senior cardiac anesthesiologist and surgeons, we decided that the patient would benefit from mechanical support during her surgery. Given the extent of the malignancy to be excised, expected long operative duration, and possible difficulty weaning off mechanical support, we thought extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) would be superior to full bypass for facilitation of long-term support with a lower risk of systemic inflammatory response, coagulopathy, and less aggressive anticoagulation requirement. Consequently, the decision was made to initiate ECMO under local sedation prior to anesthesia induction.

The patient was placed supine with right brachial artery pressure monitoring. Cannulation for ECMO was initiated through the right femoral artery and left femoral vein using fluoroscopy. The patient was systemically heparinized and was redosed as per protocol to maintain a target activated clotting time of 200 to 250 seconds. Only then was general anesthesia initiated via double-lumen endotracheal tube. Careful dissection of the mediastinal tumor was initiated with the da Vinci robot (Intuitive Surgical, Inc), but the tumor was densely adherent to the posterior left atrium, pulmonary veins, left mainstem bronchus, and esophageal wall. There was no evidence of metastatic disease. Given the extensive tumor burden adherent to vital structures, the decision was made to convert to an open right posterolateral thoracotomy with capsular dissection of the tumor, including the esophagus. Resection of a 13 × 16 × 13 cm specimen was complete and was sent for pathologic analysis, which confirmed high-grade myxoid pleomorphic liposarcoma. The tumor showed diffuse nuclear atypia and pleomorphism with variable staining for Mouse double minute 2 homolog (MDM2) and Cyclin-dependent kinase 4 (CDK4) (Figure 2). The use of VA-ECMO has been previously described in patients undergoing massive anterior mediastinal mass resection.3 This report is the first to show its successful utilization in a patient with severe, high-grade liposarcoma who required redo surgical excision of her malignancy.

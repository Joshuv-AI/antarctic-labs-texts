Green credit risk assessment and prediction in China’s construction industry: based on the optuna-catboost model
Authors: Zenan Qin, Meitong Meng, Dongbao Li, Junwei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Green credit, as a new credit model, not only shares the risk characteristics of traditional credit but also incorporates environmental risk factors into its analysis, making the assessment more complex. This article employs a modified KMV model to evaluate the risk of green credit in the construction industry. Utilizing Mann-Whitney U test and a feature ranking method based on learning models for indicator selection, the final indicators are made more objective and scientific. Subsequently, a green credit risk assessment system suitable for the Chinese construction industry is proposed. Finally, the Optuna-CatBoost (OptCAT) algorithm is applied to predict the risk of green credit in the construction industry, aiming to provide a methodological reference for the industry to mitigate green credit risks. Empirical results indicate that the modified KMV model provides an accurate measurement of green credit risk in the Chinese construction industry. Compared to default parameter (DP), random search (RS), grid search (GS), bayesian optimization (BO), and Hyperopt, Optuna demonstrates better optimization effects. When compared to support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), XGBoost, and LightGBM models, OptCAT exhibits the optimal predictive performance.

Introduction

The Equator Principles are a set of international guidelines for environmental and social risk management in project finance, proposed by the International Finance Corporation and ABN AMRO Bank in 2003. They are designed to assess and manage environmental and social risks associated with project finance, requiring financial institutions to comprehensively consider the potential impacts of projects on the ecological environment, local communities, and other stakeholders during project financing decision-making. Financial institutions should only provide financing support to projects that meet environmental and social standards2. The Equator Principles have become an industry norm for international project finance, with more than 100 financial institutions in over 40 countries worldwide having adopted them.

Since the Equator Principles became a new standard for international project financing, it incorporates the impact of projects on the environment and society into comprehensive assessments, emphasizing corporate environmental and social responsibility. Financial institutions have gradually shifted from the previous profit-maximizing operating philosophy to one that focuses on the coordinated development of the economy and the environment. The non-performing loan ratio is an indicator used to assess the quality of bank assets1. In the current situation of high non-performing loan ratios in commercial banks, the occurrence of green credit risks will have a significant impact. Green projects often involve large investments, long durations, lack of collateral, and insufficient internalization of green benefits, leading to lower returns on risk-adjusted green loans. Therefore, potential green credit risks should be given attention. It is necessary to assess and predict green credit.

Green credit risk refers to the default risk faced by financial institutions when providing green credit to enterprises, which is caused by factors such as poor corporate environmental performance, insufficient environmental protection investment, or environmental compliance issues. Different from traditional credit risk, green credit risk incorporates environmental factors into the risk assessment system, and these environmental factors may affect corporate financial performance through multiple channels. On the one hand, stringent environmental protection policies may expose high-pollution enterprises to risks such as fines and production suspension for rectification; on the other hand, good environmental performance may bring enterprises policy preferences, improved market reputation, and enhanced long-term competitiveness.

The construction industry is one of the focal points for global carbon emissions. In recent years, government agencies and regulatory bodies have identified green building as a key area for financial support. Therefore, the innovation in this article is primarily manifested in the following aspects: Firstly, the article evaluates the risk of green credit in the Chinese construction industry, which is a novel research focus. Secondly, environmental factors are taken into account, and a modified KMV model is employed to assess green credit risk. Thirdly, the article uses the OptCAT algorithm to predict green credit risk in the construction industry. This not only expands the application scope of the OptCAT algorithm but also enriches the methods for predicting green credit risk in the construction industry.

Green credit risk assessment

With the growing awareness of environmental issues and social responsibility, sustainable finance has been vigorously promoted. While commercial banks leverage economic mechanisms to engage in green credit activities, there is an increasing need to focus on environmental risks and establish robust preventive systems and management. Lundgren and Catasús,16 propose a rigorous evaluation of the environmental risks of lending enterprises before deciding whether to grant loans. This approach allows commercial banks to actively respond to government calls for environmental protection, elevating the importance of environmental responsibility while mitigating the green credit risks associated with lending enterprises. Jeucken (2001) suggests that a bank’s sustainability is closely related to environmental factors, reputation, risk assessment, and more. He argues that if companies prioritize environmental protection, they are likely to increase investments in areas such as environmental protection and technology improvement, leading to higher costs and ultimately an increase in the risk of loan defaults. Therefore, managing environmental risks in banks needs to start at the source. Additionally, he believes that banks should implement energy-saving measures internally to reduce green credit risks. Labatt and White,13 analyze how financial institutions, including banks, should conduct financial assessments regarding environmental risks. They express their views on the relationship between environmental protection and financial innovation, asserting that the banking industry should continuously improve environmental risk prevention systems to minimize losses. Weber,23 points out that most European banks have successfully integrated environmental risk management concepts and sustainable development principles into their operations, effectively avoiding environmental risks and achieving sustainability to some extent. Samuel,18 applies concepts from modern portfolio theory to evaluate portfolio risks associated with loans, using new risk measurement tools to assess derivatives of credit risks.

Currently, some literature has assessed the risks of green credit using more traditional measurement methods such as KMV. However, environmental risk factors have not been considered. Green credit is the core product of China’s green finance system, which has distinct local characteristics in top-level institutional design, policy incentive mechanisms, and practical application scenarios. It differs from similar products such as green loans in the international market and still faces many problems in actual promotion. It requires continuous improvement and further research. The risk management of green credit is an indispensable component for banks engaging in green credit business. Compared with traditional credit risk, green credit risk has a more complex composition and involves many factors, including environmental policy risk, environmental cost internalization risk, green project characteristic risk, environmental information asymmetry risk, environmental risk spillover risk, etc. Therefore, the risk indicator system also needs to take environmental factors into account.

Traditional credit risk assessment methods have some shortcomings: (1) Credit-metrics Model: Parameter estimation for the Credit-metrics model is ineffective due to the large and complex parameter scale. Additionally, historical data is challenging to obtain given its extensive time span. (2) Expert Analysis: Expert analysis suffers from significant subjectivity, leading to less objective results. (3) Neural Network Model: The shortcomings of the neural network model include randomness, requiring a considerable amount of time for debugging, resulting in poor practicality and operability. (4) Logistic Model: The logistic model relies on a substantial amount of sample data for fitting and discrimination. (5) Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP): AHP requires judging the consistency of matrices, but it is largely influenced by subjective factors, making it less objective for assessing corporate environmental risks. Furthermore, in traditional KMV models, statistical analysis methods cannot accurately calculate the impact of equity value volatility. Therefore, this study utilizes the GARCH(1,1) model with generalized conditional heteroskedasticity for computation. To highlight the differences from traditional credit risk assessments, this paper considers environmental factors. It employs a modified KMV model to measure default distance (DD) and expected default frequency (EDF).

Green credit risk prediction

In the face of increasingly complex products, the methods for predicting green credit risks also need to be updated. Machine learning methods play a significant role in improving the efficiency and accuracy of credit risk predictions, and both domestic and international research in this area has made progress. Machine learning methods such as decision trees (DT), neural networks, and support vector machine (SVM) have demonstrated good performance in empirical studies. Li and Wu,14 applied a BP neural network to establish a credit risk assessment model, and empirical evidence indicated the feasibility of this model in practical applications. Fu and Liu,5 proposed using a recursive algorithm to train an RBF neural network. Empirical research showed that this method possesses strong learning capability and good generalization ability, highlighting the model’s effective learning. Farquad,3 introduced a method that combines principal component analysis with SVM. The approach involves dimensionality reduction using principal components followed by non-classification using SVM. Empirical results demonstrated its superior performance compared to logistic regression. Harris,7 utilized CSVM to build a credit assessment model, and empirical results indicated that CSVM outperformed SVM, enhancing the model’s performance. García (2015) conducted a study on credit ratings and corporate bankruptcies from 2000 to 2013, evaluating 1-NN, C4.5, SVM, and MLP using two databases and different sample partitioning methods. Based on accuracy, SVM’s performance was judged to be superior to other algorithms in both databases. Wei et al.,24 proposed a least squares SVM with a hybrid kernel, addressing the sparsity and robustness issues of traditional least squares SVM. Experimental results showed that the optimized model could obtain a small number of features and enhance generalization ability. Szwabe and Misiorek,22 introduced a DT model for credit decision-making. They evaluated several methods for classifying loan applications, providing individual DT as output.

Ensemble is a method of combining individual learners to enhance the model’s generalization ability, and it has been widely applied in the field of machine learning for assessing credit risks. Xia,25 developed an ensemble method that is both parallelizable and sequential in the domain of credit risk assessment. This provides the model with greater flexibility and simplifies the model by eliminating redundant variables. Singh,20 compared the performance of base learners and ensemble learning in the field of credit scoring, and empirical evidence showed that the classification results of ensemble learning were significantly superior to those of single algorithms. Feng,4 proposed a dynamic weighted ensemble credit scoring method, using a Markov chain to construct a dynamically weighted trainable combiner. Empirical results indicated that this model had higher accuracy and efficiency in classification compared to static ensemble models. Sivasankar et al.,21 utilized an ensemble CRP model to predict the credit risk of financial institutions. They introduced an enhanced ensemble method called Weighted Adjusted Boosting Ensemble Method (WABEM), which employed feature selection (FS) techniques based on rough sets (RS) and preprocessing based on balance and regression, referred to as RS-RFS-WABEM. Regression was used to fill missing values in records, improving the performance of the CRP model.

Currently, ensemble learning methods have not been widely applied in the prediction of green credit risk. The CatBoost algorithm, introduced relatively recently, builds on many existing excellent algorithms. Despite its short existence, the CatBoost algorithm has gained widespread application across various domains due to its high performance, good robustness, and the need for minimal hyperparameter tuning. This includes applications in areas such as driving style recognition15,17, diabetes prediction12; Jaiswal and Gupta, 2022), and more. In this study, the CatBoost model was selected to predict green credit risk in the construction industry, contributing to the enrichment of research in the field of green credit risk.

Methodology

Indicator selection and data preprocessing

Following the principles of authenticity, comprehensiveness, scientificity, and availability, this paper initially constructs an indicator system from four major categories, namely, debt-paying ability, operational capability, profitability, and development capability, encompassing a total of 114 financial indicators for listed companies in the Chinese construction industry. Additionally, it includes four environmental indicators based on the ESG rating system.

This study selected four environmental indicators under the Huazheng ESG Rating system (https://www.chindices.com/), which are formulated centering on the core regulatory requirements for the green development of the construction industry. The ESG score provides a comprehensive evaluation of enterprises’ overall performance in three dimensions, namely environment, society and governance, with a higher score indicating better comprehensive ESG performance. The Environmental score (E score) is dedicated to assessing enterprises’ environmental performance, covering five sub-dimensions with respective weightings: carbon emission management (30%), resource use efficiency (25%), pollution prevention and control (20%), environmental management systems (15%), and biodiversity conservation (10%). While falling under the social dimension, the Social score (S score) includes indicators closely linked to the environment in the construction industry, such as community relations, employee health and safety, and product liability. The Governance score (G score) evaluates enterprises’ environmental governance capabilities, including the transparency of environmental information disclosure, the organizational structure for environmental risk management, and the formulation and implementation of environmental goals. The research data cover Chinese A-share listed companies in the construction industry, and all indicators are measured on a 100-point scale (0-100 points). To ensure temporal consistency, we adopted the ESG scores of the previous year to predict the credit risk of the current year. This lag processing is consistent with the operational logic in practical business, where banks use historical environmental performance to assess current risks. Other data in this study were sourced from the CSMAR Database (https://data.csmar.com/) and the Wind Database (https://www.wind.com.cn/), with partial missing data manually supplemented using the annual reports of listed construction enterprises.

Firstly, a preliminary screening was conducted on the initial 118 financial indicators, resulting in the retention of 58 financial indicators after eliminating those with too much missing data and high similarity. Mann-Whitney U test, proposed by H.B. Mann and D.R. Whitney in 1947, was then performed. As shown in Table 1 and 47 indicators exhibited significant differences in means, indicating that green credit risk can be highly revealed through these indicators. Therefore, these 47 indicators are temporarily retained as the initial dataset, suggesting their effectiveness in predicting green credit risk.

In order to reduce the number of feature variables and enhance the model’s generalization ability, feature selection is generally required. One of the criteria for feature selection is to choose feature variables that have a high correlation with the target variable. Common methods include mutual information, PCA, and others. However, these methods have drawbacks such as weak interpretability and poor correlation, which may not fully meet the requirements of the current experiment. With the development of machine learning technology, feature ranking methods based on learning models are well-suited to address this issue. The basic idea is to directly use the final model to establish a predictive model for each indicator and the dependent variable. When selecting a model, if the relationship between the indicator and the dependent variable is nonlinear, tree-based methods (DT, RF), or extended linear models can be chosen. Because tree-based methods can model nonlinear relationships effectively and require less parameter tuning, they are commonly used. It is essential to be cautious about the depth of the trees in tree-based feature selection methods to prevent overfitting. In addition, the use of cross-validation is another method, scoring the indicators through such training to obtain correlation and then retraining the final model.

This article chooses the feature ranking based on learning models in embedded feature selection, and the learning model selected is the CatBoost model. The CatBoost method is employed to rank the importance of indicators, and features with an importance score greater than 0.2 are retained. Due to the large number of indicators, this article only lists the importance scores of indicators that need to be excluded, as shown in Table 2. In the end, out of 114 indicators, 27 indicators are selected to construct the necessary indicator system for this article, as shown in Table 3.

Modified KMV model

Core Modifications of the Revised KMV Model: To address the limitations of the traditional KMV model (namely, the assumption that stock returns follow a normal distribution and the use of simple historical volatility to estimate the volatility of equity value), this study employs the GARCH (1,1) model to capture the leptokurtic, fat-tailed characteristics and volatility clustering of stock returns, thereby enabling a more accurate estimation of equity value volatility. Meanwhile, in response to the requirements for assessing green credit risk in the construction industry, environmental indicators are integrated into the subsequent risk prediction framework. This forms a green credit risk assessment system that combines the quantification of default risk using core financial parameters with the supplementation of risk characteristics via environmental indicators. In this study, environmental indicators are not directly involved in the calculation of KMV model parameters such as asset value, default point, DD, and EDF. Instead, they serve as features together with DD and EDF output from the KMV model for the subsequent training of the OptCAT model.

KMV Corporation believes that a company’s default depends on the company’s asset value. There will be no default when the debt value is less than the asset value, and a default event will occur when the debt value is greater than the asset value. At the same time, KMV Corporation considers the company’s default threshold to be the company’s debt value and further calculates the EDF through the correspondence between DD and the probability of default risk.

The parameters involved in the model include equity market value E, asset value VA, debt value D, risk-free rate r, debt maturity T, equity value volatility σE, and asset value volatility σA. CatBoost algorithm

CatBoost algorithm is a machine learning method based on Gradient Boosting Decision Trees (GBDT). It was introduced in 2017 by engineers from the Russian company Yandex. CatBoost can be used for classification or regression of numerical data types, ranking, binary and multi-class classification, and multitasking. The name CatBoost comes from the combination of “Category” and “Boosting,” indicating that the algorithm is designed for handling categorical data. In the algorithm, numerical features of the target statistics are used to replace categorical features to achieve the desired estimation.

CatBoost algorithm innovatively proposes a method for handling categorical features as numerical features. It adopts a ranking boosting method to handle noise and avoid gradient bias issues. Additionally, it uses fully symmetric trees as base classifiers during the training process. The following will provide a detailed explanation of these innovative points.

Categorical features

Categorical features

Categorical features

In machine learning, categorical features are often challenging to handle. Poor handling of these features can significantly impact the predictive performance of the model. Categorical features may lack comparability between categories, making them unsuitable for direct use in binary decision trees. The most common approach to dealing with categorical features is to preprocess them by converting them into numerical values, such as 0, 1, 2, for each category in each example.

CatBoost algorithm, based on traditional boosting algorithms, employs one-hot encoding during the preprocessing stage to convert categorical features into numerical features. However, one-hot encoding generates a large number of new features, and if the number of feature values is too large, it can lead to the curse of dimensionality.

When dealing with categorical features in gradient boosting algorithms, a common approach is to use the average value of the corresponding labels for the categorical features. In decision tree models, the Greedy Target-based Statistics method is often used, where the average value of the labels serves as the criterion for node splitting. Prediction shift

Prediction shift is a common issue in all algorithms based on the GBDT framework, caused by gradient bias. In the GBDT algorithm framework, each iteration uses the same sample dataset to obtain the gradient of the current model through the loss function, training and obtaining the base learner. This leads to gradient estimation bias, affecting the predictive performance of the model. The CatBoost algorithm uses the Ordered Boosting method instead of traditional gradient estimation methods, effectively reducing the bias in gradient estimation and thereby improving the model’s generalization ability.

The CatBoost algorithm optimizes the gradient boosting decision tree method by using ordered boosting to ensure that all datasets can be used for training and learning. It reduces overfitting in training by adjusting the learning rate. Unlike other gradient boosting algorithms such as XGBoost, CatBoost builds symmetric trees, using the same conditions to split the leaves of the previous tree in each step. This balanced tree structure facilitates efficient CPU implementation, reduces prediction time, and regularizes the model structure to prevent overfitting.

Based on the innovative features of the CatBoost algorithm described above, several major advantages can be summarized. Firstly, CatBoost algorithm exhibits excellent performance and ranks among the top machine learning algorithms. Secondly, it addresses the issues of gradient bias and prediction shift present in traditional GBDT algorithm frameworks, enhancing the algorithm’s generalization and prediction capabilities. Additionally, CatBoost’s handling of categorical features allows for good predictive performance even with low-dimensional features. Finally, CatBoost provides out-of-the-box GPU support, enabling a significant reduction in training time when using GPUs.

Hyperparameter optimization

Optuna is an open-source hyperparameter optimization framework proposed by Akiba et al. in 2019. It helps users automatically adjust hyperparameters of machine learning models to achieve optimal performance. Optuna framework has three main features: first, it can implement distributed parallel optimization. Second, the framework can prune experiments that show no hope of improvement. Finally, Optuna supports various hyperparameter optimization algorithms, including Bayesian optimization, random search, gradient-based optimization, etc. It helps users quickly find the best hyperparameter combinations, supporting multiple hyperparameter types such as integers, floats, booleans, categorical values, etc. Optuna also offers various visualization tools to help users visualize the hyperparameter optimization process, better understand the model’s behavior, and efficiently adjust hyperparameters for optimal performance.

The core idea of the Optuna framework is an optimized transformation based on the Parzen optimizer’s concept in Bayesian optimization algorithms. The Bayesian optimization algorithm relies on the idea that the combination of hyperparameter values to be tested in the next step depends on the model’s historical data. The Optuna framework selects the configuration space for hyperparameter combinations based on historical data and conducts hyperparameter search within this configuration space. The result obtained from each search is used to update the configuration space and inform the next search.

In a simplest Optuna optimization program, there are three fundamental concepts: the objective function, a trial, and a study. The objective function is responsible for defining the function to be optimized and specifying the range of hyperparameters. A trial corresponds to a single execution of the objective function, and a study manages the optimization process, deciding how optimization is performed, the total number of trials, recording trial results, and more. The optimization process for hyperparameters in Optuna follows the following steps:Step 1: Initialize the objective function, defining the types of parameters and the search space for parameters.Step 2: Initialize the study, setting the optimization method and parameters for a single trial, such as the maximum number of iterations.Step 3: Begin the hyperparameter tuning process.Step 4: When the maximum number of iterations is reached, output the optimal hyperparameters and the value of the objective function.

Benchmark models selection rationale

To comprehensively and objectively evaluate the performance of the proposed OptCAT model in green credit risk prediction, this study selects four representative machine learning algorithms as benchmark models: SVM, RF, XGBoost, and LightGBM. The selection of these specific models is based on three core rationales that ensure the rigor, comparability, and validity of the comparative experiments.

Firstly, these models are the most widely recognized and universally adopted benchmarks in the field of credit risk evaluation, with extensive validation in both academic research and industrial practice. SVM has long been a classic method for credit scoring due to its strong nonlinear fitting ability and stability in small-sample scenarios9. RF, as a representative bagging ensemble algorithm, is widely used for its excellent robustness and anti-overfitting capability19. XGBoost and LightGBM, as state-of-the-art gradient boosting tree models, have become the de facto standard for financial risk prediction tasks in recent years26. By using these universally accepted benchmarks, our experimental results can be directly compared with existing studies on green credit risk prediction, ensuring the academic comparability of our findings.

Secondly, the selected benchmark models cover three distinct machine learning paradigms, allowing us to verify the superiority of the OptCAT model from multiple dimensions: (1) SVM: Represents traditional statistical learning methods, which excel at handling high-dimensional mixed data and provide a baseline for comparing with modern ensemble methods. (2)RF: Represents the bagging ensemble learning paradigm, which reduces prediction variance through parallel training of multiple decision trees. Comparing with RF helps us demonstrate the advantages of gradient boosting (the paradigm adopted by CatBoost) in sequential error correction for risk prediction tasks. (3)XGBoost and LightGBM: Belong to the same GBDT family as CatBoost, representing the two most mature and widely used variants of GBDT. Direct comparison with these two models can isolate and highlight two key contributions of our study: ①the performance improvement brought by Optuna-based hyperparameter optimization; and ② CatBoost’s inherent advantages in handling categorical features and mitigating gradient bias, which are particularly relevant for green credit risk assessment involving environmental indicators.

Thirdly, our green credit risk prediction task has two key characteristics: (1) a mixed dataset containing both numerical financial indicators and categorical environmental indicators; and (2) a relatively limited sample size (949 firm-year observations) with imbalanced default samples. The selected benchmark models are all proven to perform well under these conditions. We intentionally excluded models that are unsuitable for this study: ①Simple models such as logistic regression and single decision trees were excluded because their predictive performance is significantly inferior to ensemble methods, as widely documented in previous credit risk literature. ②Deep learning models (e.g., neural networks) were excluded because they require large amounts of training data to achieve optimal performance and are prone to overfitting on our relatively small sample size, which would lead to unfair comparisons.

Significance of environmental indicators in the context of china’s green credit policy

Since China issued the Green Credit Guidelines in 2012, environmental risk has been formally incorporated into the credit risk management framework of commercial banks. Following the proposal of the “Dual Carbon” goal in 2020, the construction industry, an important source of carbon emissions, has become a key sector for green finance support and supervision. In accordance with the Special Statistical System for Green Loans issued by the People’s Bank of China, construction enterprises applying for green loans must meet the criteria specified in the Catalogue of Green Bond Supported Projects, covering green buildings, ultra-low energy consumption buildings, renewable energy applications in construction, and other related fields.

In this study, we link environmental indicators to green credit risk through two approaches. First, among the 119 listed construction enterprises in the sample, 43 publicly disclosed that they had obtained green loans or issued green bonds during the sample period. Second, regulatory requirements on green credit risk issued by financial authorities clearly state that “credit shall not be granted or existing credit lines shall be reduced for clients with non-compliant environmental and social performance.” The ST designation used in this study is highly associated with operational difficulties caused by environmental violations.

Therefore, by integrating ESG environmental indicators into the credit risk assessment system, this study not only complies with the requirements of Chinese regulators for green credit risk evaluation but also reflects the real transmission mechanism of environmental risks faced by the construction industry under the “Dual Carbon” strategy.

Sample selection and division

This study selected 119 enterprises in China’s construction industry from 2009 to 2022 as research samples. ST and *ST companies were defined as ST samples, while normally operating companies were defined as non-ST samples. The total number of samples was 949, divided into a training set and a test set. Data from 2009 to 2020 were assigned to the training set, while data from 2021 to 2022 were assigned to the test set. The training set consisted of 730 samples, including 80 ST samples, and the test set comprised 219 samples, including 22 ST samples.

Due to the 10.96% proportion of the “ST group”, it is evident that the imbalance in sample sizes across different categories poses a problem. To address this issue, the Borderline SMOTE algorithm was employed. This study strictly adheres to the no-data-leakage principle of machine learning data processing. The Borderline SMOTE oversampling operation is independently performed only on the training set. Specifically, after completing the time-based division of the training set and test set, oversampling is only applied to the minority class (ST samples) in the training set until the ratio of ST to non-ST samples in the training set reaches 1:1. For the test set, this study completely retains its original sample distribution characteristics without any resampling, data balancing, or other processing. The model trained on the training set is directly applied to the original test set to truly simulate the unbalanced data scenario of the model in actual credit decision-making, ensuring the practical application value of the prediction results. Introduced by Han et al., in 20058, this oversampling algorithm is an improvement on the SMOTE algorithm. Conclusions

The modified KMV model can accurately measure the credit dimension of green credit risk in China’s construction industry. The EDF output by this model serves as the regression target of the OptCAT model, and when combined with environmental indicators and financial indicators, it realizes a two-stage prediction of green credit risk: quantitative regression and status verification. Firstly, this paper utilizes the modified KMV model to assess green credit risk in the construction industry. Secondly, the Mann-Whitney U test and a feature ranking method based on machine learning are employed for indicator selection, making the final set of indicators more objective and scientific. Subsequently, a green credit risk assessment system suitable for China’s construction industry is proposed. Finally, the OptCAT algorithm is used to predict green credit risk in the construction industry. The following conclusions are drawn: the modified KMV model provides an accurate measurement of green credit risk in China’s construction industry. Compared with DP, RS, GS, BO, and Hyperopt, Optuna demonstrates superior optimization performance. When compared with SVM, RF, XGBoost, and LightGBM models, OptCAT exhibits optimal predictive performance. In this study, by setting a unified parameter search range and differentiated reasonable search budgets, and conducting 20 repeated experiments and paired t-tests for each algorithm, it is confirmed that the performance differences between Optuna and other optimization algorithms are statistically significant. Moreover, Optuna achieves the smallest standard deviation among all metrics, indicating that its optimization advantage is not accidental but possesses robustness and substantiality. In addition, further verification based on EDF-based classification metrics shows that the OptCAT model achieves significantly higher AUC, accuracy, F1-score and other metrics than the benchmark models in both the in-sample and out-of-sample periods. Under the optimal EDF threshold, the misdetection rates are only 5% and 9.1%, respectively, demonstrating stronger ability to distinguish the actual risks between ST and non-ST enterprises in the construction industry. The results of the confusion matrix indicate that the OptCAT model can effectively balance the dual needs of avoiding misdetection of high-risk enterprises and misclassification of normal enterprises, which is more in line with the actual scenarios of green credit decision-making in commercial banks.

Through the empirical research conducted in this study, the following managerial insights can be derived: (1) Commercial banks can accurately assess green credit risk in the construction industry to make informed lending decisions. (2) Investors can gain an understanding of green credit risk in the construction industry, enabling them to prudently mitigate risks and maximize returns. (3) The OptCAT model can accurately predict green credit risk in the construction industry, providing decision support for risk managers. (4) Business decision-makers can comprehend the situation of green credit risk in the construction industry, making appropriate adjustments to achieve the organic integration of environmental practices, social responsibility, and corporate performance.

In conclusion, this study has limitations, and there are areas that warrant improvement: (1) As the focus of this research is on establishing the OptCAT model for predicting green credit risk in the construction industry, the direct choice of using Borderline-SMOTE oversampling to balance the dataset may lead to issues such as fuzzy boundaries between majority and minority class samples. Future considerations could involve using undersampling, ADASYN, and cluster-based oversampling methods to balance the dataset. (2) Over time, as the number of default events of listed construction companies in China increases, incorporating more default samples into the model for training will further enhance the accuracy of model predictions.

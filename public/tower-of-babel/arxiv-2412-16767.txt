Modal coupling impacts the parametric normal mode splitting: Quantifying the tunable mode coupling of a nanomechanical resonator
Ahmed A. Barakat, Avishek Chowdhury, Anh Tuan Le, Eva M. Weig

Abstract
The estimation of the modal coupling strength between two hybridized normal modes or oscillators remains a hard task to achieve. However, the coupling effects can be unearthed by observing the system’s dynamic behaviour upon energy injection. One of the manifestations of this approach is the normal mode splitting generated using parametric excitation. In this contribution, a rigorous and generic mathematical formulation for the parametric normal mode splitting in any two-mode dynamical system is presented. It allows for estimating the coupling strength both in the weak and in the strong coupling regime, and irrespective of the degree of hybridization between the modes. The method is applied on the vibrations of a nanomechanical two-mode system implemented in a tunable nanostring resonator. We find good agreement between the experiment and the theoretical model, and are able to quantify the modal coupling of the nanostring as a function of the applied bias voltage.



Introduction -

The avoided level crossing or normal mode splitting is a hallmark of strong coupling. It opens in the spectrum of two coupled excitations if the coupling strength
g
exceeds their dissipation rates, and is indicative of the fact that the coupling lifts the degeneracy of the eigenfrequencies. The strong coupling regime enables a coherent energy exchange between the two excitations at a rate faster than their decay time. This was first studied for atom-photon interactions in the framework of the Jaynes-Cummings model, and implemented in the context of cavity (and later circuit) quantum electrodynamics
Thompson et al. 1992
;
Wallraff et al. 2004
. Note that we adhere to the common convention that
g
designates the coherent exchange rate between the two original, unhybridized modes, giving rise of a splitting of size
2g
.

Two coupled harmonic oscillator modes produce a similar phenomenology in the classical regime
Novotny 2010
: The resonant coupling of two modes also gives rise to an avoided crossing. This has, e.g., been observed for nanomechanical resonators
Faust et al. 2012a
. For the case of dispersively coupled modes the same holds true. This is, e.g., apparent in cavity optomechanical systems, where a mechanical resonator mode is parametrically coupled to a much higher-frequency mode of an optical or microwave cavity
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014
. In case of strong optomechanical coupling, a normal mode splitting is observed
Gröblacher et al. 2009
. As it is rooted in the parametric nature of the optomechanical coupling, it is often referred to as parametric normal mode splitting (PNMS)
Dobrindt et al. 2008
;
Wilson-Rae et al. 2008
.
The same phenomenon can also be induced between two linearly (but not necessarily strongly) coupled mechanical modes, upon the additional application of a parametric drive
Okamoto et al. 2013
. This can be readily appreciated by comparing the input-output formalism
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014
and the system of slowly varying amplitudes
Frimmer and Novotny 2014
.
Note that the terms avoided crossing and normal mode splitting are often used synonymously in the literature. However, for the case of parametrically excited mechanical systems it is important to delineate between resonant and dispersive coupling, as both can lead to a splitting. We will therefore adhere to the following convention: The term ”avoided crossing” will be solely employed for the strong resonant coupling of two linearly coupled modes. The avoided crossing appears when the two modes are tuned on resonance by an external parameter, lifting the degeneracy of the natural eigenfrequencies as a consequence of their hybridization into normal modes. This phenomenon is also known in the mechanical engineering community as ”mode-veering”
Leissa 1974
;
Pierre 1988
. The term ”parametric normal mode splitting” will refer to the splitting of
each
of the two linearly coupled normal modes into two branches under the application of an additional parametric drive tuned to their frequency difference. Notice that the physical mechanisms behind the opening of an avoided crossing vs. a PNMS bear analogy to the dc vs. ac Stark effect.

Unlike the avoided crossing, the PNMS can be induced at any value of the external tuning parameter, and is not limited to a specific resonance situation. As a result, the PNMS has been applied to achieve tunable and controllable strong coupling in various nano- and micromechanical systems, ranging from GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure beams
Mahboob et al. 2013
over carbon nanotubes
Li et al. 2016
;
Zhu et al. 2017
to graphene or MoS
2
microdrums
Mathew et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2015
;
Prasad et al. 2019
, and has also been employed to induce coupling between different resonators in arrays of GaAs/AlGas beams, Si
3
N
4
nanostrings or membranes
Okamoto et al. 2013
;
Okamoto et al. 2016
;
Huang et al. 2016
;
Hälg et al. 2022
. The above implementations of PNMS rely on the same generic model. It assumes two harmonic oscillator modes that exhibit a weak linear coupling
\lambda
. In addition to a resonant drive, a parametric drive at the difference frequency of the two modes is applied to induce the parametric coupling between the modes. The size of the PNMS splitting
2g
is set by both the original coupling strength
\lambda
and the parametric drive amplitude. While the dependence of
2g
on the parametric drive amplitude has been established
Okamoto et al. 2013
, the relation between
2g
and
\lambda
has not been appreciated to date.

Here, we resolve this open challenge and discuss how to obtain the weak linear coupling strength
\lambda
between the two original modes from the measured PNMS. We explore the two fundamental flexural eigenmodes of a high Q nanomechanical string resonator denoted as out-of-plane and in-plane mode which are dielectrically coupled by a dc voltage applied between two adjacent electrodes (see Fig.
1
)
Faust et al. 2012a
. The modes of the strings are driven by a white-noise force. A parametric coupling is induced by modulating both modes at their difference frequency with a second tone. We develop a thorough and generic theoretical model to describe the PNMS. It is applicable to any two-level, or two-mode, system exhibiting a modal coupling and subjected to inter- or intramodal parametric excitation. The model is deduced using the multiple-scales perturbation method
Nayfeh 2000
, and is therefore capable to trace both splitting branches to obtain the splitting width
2g
up to the second order approximation.

By establishing an analytical connection between the splitting
2g
and the original coupling
\lambda
, we are able to significantly extend the understanding of dielectrically controlled nanomechanical systems. To date, the dielectrically induced coupling of the original modes,
\lambda
, could only be extracted from the avoided crossing, i.e. for both modes tuned on resonance with the applied dc voltage
Rieger et al. 2012
;
Faust et al. 2012a
.
For the case of weak coupling, where the avoided crossing is not resolved,
\lambda
is not accessible. Even more, it has been postulated that
\lambda
should scale with the electrostatic field energy and hence linearly with the applied dc voltage, albeit the measurement from the avoided crossing is limited to a single value of
V_{dc}
. Taking advantage of the PNMS,
\lambda
is revealed irrespective of the strength of the coupling and for any value of the applied dc voltage. We measure the PNMS as a function of
V_{dc}
.
We show that the dielectric coupling
\lambda
confirms the expected behavior and reaches a maximum near resonance. Naturally, our approach can be applied to any other system of two linearly coupled modes of arbitrary coupling strength.

Conclusions -

The determination of the coupling strength in coupled dynamical systems is not accessible to standard dynamical characterization methods due to the hybridization of the lab coordinates. Previous methodologies were established to extract it for specific conditions. For example, in the case of strong coupling, the avoided crossing provides direct access to the coupling strength for the system’s eigenfrequencies tuned on resonance, but does not yield results away from the resonance.
In this contribution, a general methodology of retrieving the coupling strength based on the parametric normal mode splitting (PNMS) is presented. It neither requires strong coupling nor tuning the system to a specific operation point, and is thus not bound to fulfilling a resonance condition.

We develop a rigorous model of PNMS for a generic two-mode dynamical system, applicable to any physical implementation of two coupled harmonic oscillator modes. A parametric modulation is applied to both modes at their difference frequency. This perturbation induces a normal mode splitting for each of the resonating normal modes. Its width
2g
is set by the strength of parametric modulation and the coupling strength
\lambda
between the modes. The former has previously been discussed in the literature. Our model establishes an explicit relation between the width of the normal mode splitting
2g
and the coupling strength
\lambda
, an aspect which has not been appreciated to date. This allows to use the PNMS to determine the coupling strength between the two modes. Even more, the model represents a comprehensive description of the splitting, and considerably extends the framework of parametrically coupled systems.

The theoretical model is complemented by an experiment using a nanoelectromechanical two-mode system. We study the two orthogonal fundamental flexural modes of a dielectrically controlled SiN nanostring resonator. Dielectric driving is mediated by a dc bias voltage, which also facilitates a tuning of the eigenfrequencies and induces a coupling between the two modes. We apply an additional parametric modulation at the difference frequency to induce a PNMS from which the coupling strength is extracted quantitatively. By varying the applied dc bias voltage, we compare the observed splittings with the theoretical model, and find an excellent traceability irrespective of the coupling strength both in the weak and strong coupling regime.

Beyond the mere validation of the PNMS model, our work enables important insights into the dynamics of dielectrically controlled nanostring resonators. We provide experimental evidence of the presumed linear relation between the coupling strength and the dc bias voltage in the voltage range between the two avoided level crossings observed at positive and negative dc bias, respectively. This establishes a novel means to implement tunable linear coupling. Even more, the coupling strength extracted near the modes’ resonance agrees well with that obtained from the avoided crossing. The parametric normal mode splitting thus complements the scenario of linear strong coupling, and generalizes the access to the modal coupling parameters to beyond the modes being tuned on resonance.

Accordingly, applying this method to other coupled dynamical systems, whether through electric, optical or magnetic fields will reveal further knowledge on the nature of dynamical coupling. This methodology will thereby open new pathways for applications in coupled electro- or optomechanical systems, just to name a few, providing a detailed understanding and control of the modal couplings.

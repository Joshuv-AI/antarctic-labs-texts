Applications of Poisson cohomology to the inducibility problems and study of deformation maps
Apurba Das, Ramkrishna Mandal, Anupam Sahoo

Abstract
. This paper provides some applications of the Poisson cohomology groups introduced by Flato, Gerstenhaber and Voronov. Given an abelian extension of a Poisson algebra by a representation, we first investigate the inducibility of a pair of Poisson algebra automorphisms and show that the corresponding obstruction lies in the second Poisson cohomology group. Consequently, we obtain the Wells exact sequence connecting various automorphism groups and the second Poisson cohomology group. Subsequently, we also consider the inducibility for a pair of Poisson algebra derivations, obtain the obstruction and construct the corresponding Wells-type exact sequence. To get another application, we introduce the notion of a ‘deformation map’ in a proto-twilled Poisson algebra. A deformation map unifies various well-known operators such as Poisson homomorphisms, Poisson derivations, crossed homomorphisms, Rota-Baxter operators of any weight, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and modified Rota-Baxter operators on Poisson algebras. We show that a deformation map r r induces a new Poisson algebra structure and a suitable representation of it. The corresponding Poisson cohomology is defined to be the cohomology of the deformation map r r . Finally, we study the formal deformations of the operator r r in terms of the cohomology.

1.1.
The inducibility problems

As mentioned earlier, the inducibility problem for automorphisms was first considered in a paper by Wells. Subsequently, the same problem was generalized for derivations
[
28
]
,
[
5
]
.
In the context of Lie algebras, the inducibility problems for automorphisms and derivations can be stated as follows. First, let
0\rightarrow V\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{p}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow 0
be an abelian extension of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by a given representation
V
(i.e., the induced representation on
V
coming from the abelian extension coincides with the prescribed one). (i) Let
\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)
be the set of all Lie algebra automorphisms
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(E)
that satisfies
\gamma(V)\subset V
. Then there is a group homomorphism
\tau:\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(V)\times\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
,
\tau(\gamma)=(\gamma|_{V},p\gamma s)
, where
s
is a section of the map
p
. Then the inducibility problem for automorphisms asks to find a necessary and sufficient condition under which a pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(V)\times\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
of Lie algebra automorphisms lies in the image of
\tau
.
(ii) Let
\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)
be the space of all Lie algebra derivations
d\in\mathrm{Der}(E)
that satisfies
d(V)\subset V
. As before, there is a map
\eta:\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(V)\times\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
given by
\eta(d)=(d|_{V},pds)
, where
s
is a section of
p
. The inducibility problem for derivations then asks to find a necessary and sufficient condition under which a pair
(d_{V},d_{\mathfrak{g}})\in\mathrm{Der}(V)\times\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
of Lie algebra derivations lies in the image of
\eta
. It is easy to see that the above inducibility problems make sense in the context of other types of algebras. See
[
28
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
17
]
and the references therein for these problems on different algebras.

Here we shall study the inducibility problems in the context of Poisson algebras. For these, we will introduce suitable Wells maps (one for Poisson automorphisms and another for Poisson derivations) in the context and show that the obstructions for both problems lie in the second Poisson cohomology group. Consequently, we obtain two Wells exact sequences, one connecting various Poisson automorphism groups and the second Poisson cohomology group, and the other connecting various Poisson derivation spaces and the second Poisson cohomology group.

1.2.
Deformation maps

The notion of deformation maps in a twilled Lie algebra (or a matched pair of Lie algebras) was introduced by Agore and Militaru to study the classifying complements problem
[
1
]
. It turns out that a deformation map in a twilled Lie algebra unifies Lie algebra homomorphisms, derivations, crossed homomorphisms, and (relative) Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
,
1

[
19
]
. However, there are operators similar to Rota-Baxter operators (e.g. twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and modified Rota-Baxter operators) on Lie algebras that cannot be viewed as deformation maps in twilled Lie algebras. To put this list of operators in the same unified framework, Jiang, Sheng and Tang
[
19
]
considered quasi-twilled Lie algebras and introduced two types of deformation maps (type-I and type-II). They further defined the cohomology groups of these deformation maps that unified the existing cohomology groups of all the well-known operators mentioned above.

Our primary aim in this part is to define the cohomology of Poisson homomorphisms, Poisson derivations, crossed homomorphisms, Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
and
1
, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and modified Rota-Baxter operators on Poisson algebras. To unify all these operators by a single map (unlike type-I and type-II deformation maps), one needs to consider a bigger object than a quasi-twilled Poisson algebra. Namely, we will consider a proto-twilled Poisson algebra, which is a Poisson algebra whose underlying vector space has a direct sum decomposition into subspaces. A quasi-twilled Poisson algebra is a particular case in which a decomposed subspace is a Poisson subalgebra, and a twilled Poisson algebra is a case in which both the decomposed subspaces are Poisson subalgebras. We define the notion of a deformation map in a proto-twilled Poisson algebra (generalizing deformation map in a twilled Poisson algebra
[
2
]
) and show that a deformation map unifies all the above-mentioned operators on Poisson algebras. We show that a deformation map
r
in a proto-twilled Poisson algebra induces a new Poisson algebra and a suitable representation. The corresponding Poisson cohomology is the cohomology of the deformation map
r
. In particular, we obtain the cohomologies of all the well-known operators mentioned above. Finally, we study linear and formal one-parameter deformations of the operator
r
in terms of its cohomology.

2.
Poisson algebras and the Flato-Gerstenhaber-Voronov cohomology

In this section, we recall representations and cohomology of Poisson algebras as introduced by Flato, Gerstenhaber and Voronov
[
15
]
.

2.1 Definition
.

A
Poisson algebra
is a triple
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
consisting of a commutative associative algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!)
and a Lie algebra
(P,\{~,~\})
both defined on a same vector space
P
satisfying additionally the following Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle\{x,y\cdot z\}=\{x,y\}\cdot z+y\cdot\{x,z\},\text{ for }x,y,z\in P.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. A
Poisson subalgebra
of
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
is a vector subspace
Q\subset P
that is a subalgebra for both the commutative associative algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!)
and the Lie algebra
(P,\{~,~\})
.

Poisson algebras are the underlying algebraic structure of Poisson manifolds. The algebra of observables in symplectic geometry, the space of smooth functions
C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
on the dual of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, vertex operator algebras and the semi-classical limit of any associative deformations of a commutative algebra inherit Poisson algebra structures.

2.2 Definition
.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. A
representation
of
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
is a triple
(V,\mu,\rho)
consisting of a vector space
V
endowed with linear maps
\mu,\rho:P\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
such that

(i)

(V,\mu)
is a module over the commutative associative algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!)
, i.e.,
\mu_{x\cdot y}=\mu_{x}\mu_{y}
,

(ii)

(V,\rho)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(P,\{~,~\})
, i.e.,
\rho_{\{x,y\}}=\rho_{x}\rho_{y}-\rho_{y}\rho_{x}
,

for all
x,y\in P
, and the following compatibilities are hold:

\displaystyle\mu_{\{x,y\}}=~

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\circ\mu_{y}-\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x},

\displaystyle\rho_{x\cdot y}=~

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\circ\rho_{y}+\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x}.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. Then the triple
(P,\mu_{\mathrm{ad}},\rho_{\mathrm{ad}})
is a representation, where
(\mu_{\mathrm{ad}})_{x}y=x\cdot y
and
(\rho_{\mathrm{ad}})_{x}y=\{x,y\}
, for all
x,y\in P
. This is called the

or the regular representation of
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
.

For any Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
, the triple
(P^{*},\mu_{\mathrm{coad}},\rho_{\mathrm{coad}})
is also a representation, where
(\mu_{\mathrm{coad}})_{x}(\zeta)(y)=\langle\zeta,x\cdot y\rangle
and
(\rho_{\mathrm{coad}})_{x}(\zeta)(y)=-\langle\zeta,\{x,y\}\rangle
, for all
x,y\in P
and
\zeta\in P^{*}
. This is called the
coadjoint representation
. In general, given any representation of a Poisson algebra, one can dualize the representation (see for example,
[
23
]
). With this, the coadjoint representation is simply the dual of the adjoint representation.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. A linear map

is called a
(m,n)
-cochain
if

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}_{(i,m-i)}}(-1)^{\sigma}~f\big((a_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}\otimes a_{\sigma^{-1}(i+1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\sigma^{-1}(m)})\otimes(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n})\big)=0,

for all
0<i<m
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in P
. We denote the set of all
(m,n)
-cochains by
C^{m,n}(P,V)
. Then for any

, the space of
k

C^{k}(P,V):=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+n=k\\
m\neq 1\end{subarray}}C^{m,n}(P,V)

and the coboundary map

is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{FGV}}(f)=\delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{m,n}(f)+(-1)^{m}~\!\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}^{m,n}(f),

for

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{m,n}f)((a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m+1})~\otimes~

\displaystyle(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n}))=\mu_{a_{1}}f((a_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m+1})\otimes(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i}f((a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\cdot a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m+1})\otimes(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n}))

\displaystyle C^{0,n}(P,V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}P,V)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(P\otimes\wedge^{n-1}P,V)=C^{1,n-1}(P,V)

\displaystyle((a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m})\otimes(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n+1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\bigg(\rho_{x_{i}}f((a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m})\otimes(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{x_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n+1}))

is a cochain complex, called the

and the set of all

It follows from the above discussions that a Poisson
2
-cocycle can be described by a pair
(h,H)
consisting of a symmetric map

and a skew-symmetric map

that satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

\displaystyle\mu_{x}h(y,z)-h(x\cdot y,z)+h(x,y\cdot z)-\mu_{z}h(x,y)=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\rho_{x}H(y,z)+\rho_{y}H(z,x)+\rho_{z}H(x,y)+H(x,\{y,z\})+H(y,\{z,x\})+H(z,\{x,y\})=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\rho_{x}h(y,z)-h(\{x,y\},z)-h(y,\{x,z\})+H(x,y\cdot z)-\mu_{y}H(x,z)-\mu_{z}H(x,y)=0,

for all

Further, two Poisson

-cocycles

and
(h^{\prime},H^{\prime})
are

such that for all

(4)

\displaystyle h(x,y)-h^{\prime}(x,y)=\mu_{x}\varphi(y)-\varphi(x\cdot y)+\mu_{y}\varphi(x),\quad H(x,y)-H^{\prime}(x,y)=\rho_{x}\varphi(y)-\varphi(\{x,y\})-\rho_{y}\varphi(x).

3.
Inducibility of Poisson automorphisms

In this section, we study the inducibility problem for a pair of Poisson algebra automorphisms in a given abelian extension of a Poisson algebra by a representation. To find an answer to this problem, we define the Wells map in the context of Poisson algebras and show that a pair of Poisson algebra automorphisms is inducible if and only if its image under the Wells map (the image lies in the second cohomology group) vanishes identically.

Let

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(h,H)

(h^{\prime},H^{\prime})
are cohomologous, there exists a linear map
\varphi:P\rightarrow V
such that the identities in (
4
) hold. Hence we have

\displaystyle h_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)-h^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)=~

\displaystyle\beta\big(\mu_{\alpha^{-1}(x)}\varphi\alpha^{-1}(y)-\varphi(\alpha^{-1}(x)\cdot\alpha^{-1}(y))+\mu_{\alpha^{-1}(y)}\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(y)-\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x\cdot y)+\mu_{y}\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x)\quad(\text{by }(\ref{c-iden}))

and similarly,
H_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)-H^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)=\rho_{x}\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(y)-\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(\{x,y\})-\rho_{y}\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x)
, for all
x,y\in P
. This shows that
(h_{(\beta,\alpha)},H_{(\beta,\alpha)})
and
(h^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)},H^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)})
are cohomologous Poisson
2
-cocycles.
∎

It follows from the above proposition that there is a well-defined map
\Psi:\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V))
given by

\displaystyle\Psi(\beta,\alpha)[(h,H)]:=[(h_{(\beta,\alpha)},H_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(h,H)],\text{ for }(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}\text{ and }[(h,H)]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V).

Given any section
s
, let the abelian extension (
3
) produce the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)
. Then we define a map
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V)
, called the
Wells map
, given by

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\beta,\alpha):=\Psi(\beta,\alpha)[(h,H)]=[(h_{(\beta,\alpha)},H_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(h,H)],\text{ for }(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}.

It follows from Proposition
3.2
(ii) that the Wells map doesn’t depend on the choice of the section
s
. We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

3.3 Theorem
.

Let (
3
) be an abelian extension of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
by a representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
. Then a pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(V)\times\mathrm{Aut}(P)
of Poisson algebra automorphisms is inducible if and only if

\displaystyle(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{W}(\beta,\alpha)=0.

Proof.

Let
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(V)\times\mathrm{Aut}(P)
be an inducible pair of Poisson algebra automorphisms. Then we have seen in Proposition
3.1
that
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}
. Next, consider a Poisson automorphism
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)
such that
\gamma|_{V}=\beta
and
p\gamma s=\alpha
, where
s
is any section. Depending on a fixed section
s
, let the given abelian extension produce the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)
. Then for any
x,y\in P
, we have

\displaystyle h_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)-h(x,y)

\displaystyle=\beta\big(h(\alpha^{-1}(x),\alpha^{-1}(y))\big)-h(x,y)

\displaystyle=\beta\big(s\alpha^{-1}(x)\cdot_{E}s\alpha^{-1}(y)-s(\alpha^{-1}(x)\cdot\alpha^{-1}(y))\big)-\big(s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-s(x\cdot y)\big)

\displaystyle=\gamma s\alpha^{-1}(x)\cdot_{E}\gamma s\alpha^{-1}(y)-\gamma s(\alpha^{-1}(x)\cdot\alpha^{-1}(y))-s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)+s(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle=\gamma s\alpha^{-1}(x)\cdot_{E}\gamma s\alpha^{-1}(y)-\gamma s(\alpha^{-1}(x)\cdot\alpha^{-1}(y))-s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)+s(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle\quad-(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(x)\cdot_{E}(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(y)\quad(\because~\!\mathrm{Im}(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)\subset V\text{ and }V\text{ has trivial Poisson structure})

\displaystyle=s(x)\cdot_{E}\gamma s\alpha^{-1}(y)-s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-\gamma s\alpha^{-1}(x\cdot y)+s(x\cdot y)+\gamma s\alpha^{-1}(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\text{after rearranging})

\displaystyle=\mu_{x}(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(y)-(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(x\cdot y)+\mu_{y}(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(x),

and similarly,
H_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)-H(x,y)=\rho_{x}(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(y)-(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(\{x,y\})-\rho_{y}(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(x)
. This shows that the Poisson
2
-cocycles
(h_{(\beta,\alpha)},H_{(\beta,\alpha)})
and
(h,H)
are cohomologous. Hence we have
\mathcal{W}(\beta,\alpha)=[(h_{(\beta,\alpha)},H_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(h,H)]=0.

Conversely, suppose that
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}
and
\mathcal{W}(\beta,\alpha)=0
. Let
s
be any fixed section. Depending on
s
, let the given abelian extension produce the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)
. Since
\mathcal{W}(\beta,\alpha)=0
, it follows that the Poisson
2
-cocycles
(h_{(\beta,\alpha)},H_{(\beta,\alpha)})
and
(h,H)
are cohomologous. Thus, there exists a linear map
\varphi:P\rightarrow V
such that

(9)

\displaystyle\beta\big(h(\alpha^{-1}(x),\alpha^{-1}(y))\big)-h(x,y)=~

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\varphi(y)-\varphi(x\cdot y)+\mu_{y}\varphi(x),

(10)

\displaystyle\beta\big(H(\alpha^{-1}(x),\alpha^{-1}(y))\big)-H(x,y)=~

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\varphi(y)-\varphi(\{x,y\})-\rho_{y}\varphi(x),

for all
x,y\in P
. Since
s
is a section, any element
e\in E
can be uniquely written as
e=s(x)+u
, for some
x\in P
and
u\in V
. We now define a map
\gamma:E\rightarrow E
by

\displaystyle\gamma(e)=\gamma(s(x)+u)=s(\alpha(x))+\beta(u)+\varphi(x),\text{ for }e=s(x)+u\in E.

The map is injective as
\gamma(e)=0
(for
e=s(x)+u
) implies that
s(\alpha(x))=0
and
\beta(u)+\varphi(x)=0
. Since
s,\alpha,\beta
are all injective maps, we get that
x=0
and
u=0
which in turn implies that
e=0
. The map
\gamma
is also surjective as
e=s(x)+u\in E
has unique preimage
e^{\prime}=s(\alpha^{-1}(x))+(\beta^{-1}(u)-\beta^{-1}\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x))\in E
. This shows that
\gamma
is indeed a bijective map. Next, we claim that
\gamma:E\rightarrow E
is a Poisson algebra automorphism. For any two elements
e=s(x)+u
and
e^{\prime}=s(y)+v
from the space
E
, we observe that

\displaystyle\gamma(e)\cdot_{E}\gamma(e^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\big(s(\alpha(x))+\beta(u)+\varphi(x)\big)\cdot_{E}\big(s(\alpha(y))+\beta(v)+\varphi(y)\big)

\displaystyle=s(\alpha(x))\cdot_{E}s(\alpha(y))+s(\alpha(x))\cdot_{E}\beta(v)+s(\alpha(x))\cdot_{E}\varphi(y)+s(\alpha(y))\cdot_{E}\beta(u)+s(\alpha(y))\cdot_{E}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=s(\alpha(x)\cdot\alpha(y))+h(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))+\mu_{\alpha(x)}\beta(v)+\mu_{\alpha(x)}\varphi(y)+\mu_{\alpha(y)}\beta(u)+\mu_{\alpha(y)}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=s(\alpha(x)\cdot\alpha(y))+\beta\big(h(x,y)+\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u\big)+\varphi(x\cdot y)\qquad(\text{by }(\ref{cohomo-1})\text{ and }(\ref{c-iden}))

\displaystyle=\gamma\big(s(x\cdot y)+h(x,y)+s(x)\cdot_{E}v+s(y)\cdot_{E}u\big)\qquad(\text{from the definition of }\gamma)

\displaystyle=\gamma\big(s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)+s(x)\cdot_{E}v+s(y)\cdot_{E}u\big)

\displaystyle=\gamma((s(x)+u)\cdot_{E}(s(y)+v))=\gamma(e\cdot_{E}e^{\prime}).

In the same way, one can show that
\{\gamma(e),\gamma(e^{\prime})\}_{E}=\gamma\{e,e^{\prime}\}_{E}.
Hence the claim follows. Finally, it follows from the definition of
\gamma
that
\gamma(u)=\beta(u)
and
\overline{\gamma}(x)=p\gamma s(x)=p(s(\alpha(x))+\varphi(x))=\alpha(x)
, for all
x\in P
and
u\in V
. Hence
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(P)
and
\tau(\gamma)=(\gamma|_{V},\overline{\gamma})=(\beta,\alpha)
which shows that the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
is inducible.
∎

The Wells map given in (
8
) generalizes the classical Wells map defined in the context of abstract groups
[
30
]
(see also
[
20
,
27
]
) and Lie algebras
[
6
]
. In those classical cases, the Wells map fits into a short exact sequence. In the context of Poisson algebras, we have a similar generalization stated below. The proof is also similar to the classical cases.

3.4 Theorem
.

Let
0\rightarrow V\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{p}P\rightarrow 0
be an abelian extension of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
by a given representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
. Then there is an exact sequence

(11)

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{V,P}(E)\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)\xrightarrow{\tau}\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V),

where
\mathrm{Aut}_{V,P}(E)=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)~\!|~\!\tau(\gamma)=(\mathrm{id}_{V},\mathrm{id}_{P})\}
.

An abelian extension (
3
) is said to be
split
if there exists a section
s:P\rightarrow E
which is also a homomorphism of Poisson algebras. In this case, we can identify
E\cong P\oplus V
as Poisson algebras, where the Poisson algebra structure on
P\oplus V
is given by

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(y,v)=(x\cdot y~\!,~\!\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{\ltimes}=(\{x,y\}~\!,~\!\rho_{x}v-\rho_{y}u),

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in P\oplus V.
With this identification, the maps
i,p
and
s
are the obvious ones. For the above section
s
, if the abelian extension produce the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)
, then

\displaystyle h(x,y)=s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-s(x\cdot y)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad H(x,y)=\{s(x),s(y)\}_{E}-s\{x,y\}=0.

Hence
(h,H)=0
which in turn implies that the Wells map
\mathcal{W}
vanishes identically. Thus the Wells exact sequence (
11
) takes the form

(12)

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{V,P}(E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)\xrightarrow{\tau}\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow 1.

Let
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}
. Then we define a map
\gamma_{(\beta,\alpha)}:E\rightarrow E
by
\gamma_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,u)=(\alpha(x),\beta(u))
, for
(x,u)\in E
. It is easy to see that
\gamma_{(\beta,\alpha)}\in\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)
. Hence there is a map
t:\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)
given by
t(\beta,\alpha):=\gamma_{(\beta,\alpha)}
, for
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}
. The map
t
is a group homomorphism satisfying additionally
\tau t=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}}
. Thus, (
12
) is a split short exact sequence of groups that yields the following isomorphism of groups

\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}_{V}(E)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\mu,\rho}\ltimes\mathrm{Aut}_{V,P}(E).

4.
Inducibility of Poisson derivations

In this section, we study the inducibility of a pair of Poisson derivations in a given abelian extension of a Poisson algebra by a representation. We observe that the image of a suitable Wells-type map can describe the corresponding obstruction for this inducibility problem. In the end, we also obtain a short exact sequence connecting some derivation spaces and the second Poisson cohomology group.

As before, let (
3
) be a fixed abelian extension of a Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
by a representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
. We also let

\displaystyle\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)=\{d:E\rightarrow E\text{ is a Poisson derivation }|~\!d(V)\subset V\}

be the set of all Poisson derivations on
E
that make the subspace
V
invariant. For any
d\in\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)
, it follows that
d|_{V}\in\mathrm{Der}(V)
. Moreover, for any linear section
s:P\rightarrow E
, we define a map
\overline{d}:P\rightarrow P
by
\overline{d}(x):=pds(x)
, for
x\in P
. The map
\overline{d}
doesn’t depend on the choice of
s
. Further, for any
x,y\in P
,

\displaystyle\overline{d}(x\cdot y)=~

\displaystyle pd\big(s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-h(x,y)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle pd(s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y))\quad(\because~d(V)\subset V\text{ and }p|_{V}=0)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle p\big(ds(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)+s(x)\cdot_{E}ds(y)\big)=\overline{d}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot\overline{d}(y)\quad(\because~ps=\mathrm{id}_{P}).

Similarly, one can show that
\overline{d}(\{x,y\})=\{\overline{d}(x),y\}+\{x,\overline{d}(y)\}
, for all
x,y\in P
. This shows that the map
\overline{d}
is a Poisson derivation on the Poisson algebra
P
. Hence, there is a well-defined map

\displaystyle\eta:\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(V)\times\mathrm{Der}(P)~~\text{ given by }~~\eta(d)=(d|_{V},\overline{d}).

A pair of Poisson derivations
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathrm{Der}(V)\times\mathrm{Der}(P)
is said to be
inducible
if there exists a Poisson derivation
d\in\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)
such that
\eta(d)=(d_{V},d_{P})
. Our aim in this section is to find a necessary and sufficient condition under which a pair of Poisson derivations is inducible. For this, we will present a Wells-type map in the present context and show that a pair of Poisson derivations is inducible if and only if its image under this new Wells-type map vanishes identically. We start with the following result.

4.1 Proposition
.

Let (
3
) be an abelian extension of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
by a representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
. If the pair of Poisson derivations
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathrm{Der}(V)\times\mathrm{Der}(P)
is inducible then

(13)

\displaystyle d_{V}(\mu_{x}v)=\mu_{d_{P}(x)}v+\mu_{x}d_{V}(v)\quad\text{ and }\quad d_{V}(\rho_{x}v)=\rho_{d_{P}(x)}v+\rho_{x}d_{V}(v),\text{ for any }x\in P,~\!v\in V.

Proof.

Since
(d_{V},d_{P})
is an inducible pair, there exists a Poisson derivation
d\in\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)
such that
d\big|_{V}=d_{V}
and
pds=d_{P}
(here
s
is any arbitrary section). Hence for any
x\in P
and
v\in V
,

\displaystyle d_{V}(\mu_{x}v)

\displaystyle d_{V}(\rho_{x}v)

\displaystyle=d(s(x)\cdot_{E}v)

\displaystyle=d\{s(x),v\}_{E}

\displaystyle=ds(x)\cdot_{E}v+s(x)\cdot_{E}d(v)

\displaystyle=\{ds(x),v\}_{E}+\{s(x),d(v)\}_{E}

\displaystyle=sd_{P}(x)\cdot_{E}v+s(x)\cdot_{E}d_{V}(v)

\displaystyle=\{sd_{P}(x),v\}_{E}+\{s(x),d_{V}(v)\}_{E}

\displaystyle=\mu_{d_{P}(x)}v+\mu_{x}d_{V}(v),

\displaystyle=\rho_{d_{P}(x)}v+\rho_{x}d_{V}(v).

In the third equality of both the above calculations, we have used that
(ds-sd_{P})(x)\in\mathrm{ker}(p)=\mathrm{im}(i)\cong V.
This proves the result.
∎

Given an abelian extension as above, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}:=\big\{(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathrm{Der}(V)\times\mathrm{Der}(P)~\!|~\!\text{the identities in }(\ref{g1})\text{ hold}\big\}.

It is easy to verify that
\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}
has a Lie algebra structure with the componentwise commutator bracket. Let
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}
be any arbitrary element. For any linear maps
h:P^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow V
and
H:\wedge^{2}P\rightarrow V
, we define new maps
h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}:P^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow V
and
H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}:\wedge^{2}P\rightarrow V
respectively by

\displaystyle h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y):=~

\displaystyle d_{V}(h(x,y))-h(d_{P}(x),y)-h(x,d_{P}(y)),

\displaystyle H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y):=~

\displaystyle d_{V}(H(x,y))-H(d_{P}(x),y)-H(x,d_{P}(y)),

for
x,y\in P
. With these notations, we have the following.

4.2 Proposition
.

(i)

If
(h,H)
is a Poisson
2
-cocycle of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
then
(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})
is so.

(ii)

If
(h,H)
and
(h^{\prime},H^{\prime})
are two cohomologous Poisson
2
-cocycles then
(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})
and
(h^{\prime}_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H^{\prime}_{(d_{V},d_{P})})
are also cohomologous.

Proof.

(i) Note that
(h,H)
is a Poisson
2
-cocycle implies that the identities in (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) hold. First, we observe that the map
h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}
is commutative and for any
x,y,z\in P
,

\displaystyle\mu_{x}h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(y,z)-h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x\cdot y,z)+h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y\cdot z)-\mu_{z}h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mu_{x}\big(d_{V}(h(y,z))-h(d_{P}(y),z)-h(y,d_{P}(z))\big)-d_{V}(h(x\cdot y,z))+h(d_{P}(x\cdot y),z)+h(x\cdot y,d_{P}(z))

\displaystyle~~~+d_{V}(h(x,y\cdot z))-h(d_{P}(x),y\cdot z)-h(x,d_{P}(y\cdot z))-\mu_{z}\big(d_{V}(h(x,y))-h(d_{P}(x),y)-h(x,d_{P}(y))\big)

\displaystyle=d_{V}\mu_{x}h(y,z)-\mu_{d_{P}(x)}h(y,z)-\mu_{x}h(d_{P}(y),z)-\mu_{x}h(y,d_{P}(z))-d_{V}h(x\cdot y,z)

\displaystyle~~~+h(d_{P}(x)\cdot y,z)+h(x\cdot d_{P}(y),z)+h(x\cdot y,d_{P}(z))+d_{V}h(x,y\cdot z)-h(d_{P}(x),y\cdot z)

\displaystyle~~~-h(x,d_{P}(y)\cdot z)-h(x,y\cdot d_{P}(z))-d_{V}\mu_{z}h(x,y)+\mu_{d_{P}(z)}h(x,y)+\mu_{z}h(d_{P}(x),y)+\mu_{z}h(x,d_{P}(y))

\displaystyle=0\qquad(\text{as }h\text{ satisfies }(\ref{pois-co1}))

On the other hand, the map
H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}
is skew-symmetric and also we have

\displaystyle\rho_{x}H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(y,z)+c.p.+H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,\{y,z\})+c.p.

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}\big\{d_{V}(H(y,z))-H(d_{P}(y),z)-H(y,d_{P}(z))\big\}+c.p.

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+\big\{d_{V}(H(x,\{y,z\}))-H(d_{P}(x),\{y,z\})-H(x,d_{P}\{y,z\})\big\}+c.p.

\displaystyle=\big\{d_{V}\rho_{x}H(y,z)-\rho_{d_{P}(x)}H(y,z)-\rho_{x}H(d_{P}(y),z)-\rho_{x}H(y,d_{P}(z))\big\}+c.p.

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+\big\{d_{V}(H(x,\{y,z\}))-H(d_{P}(x),\{y,z\})-H(x,\{d_{P}(y),z\})-H(x,\{y,d_{P}(z)\})\big\}+c.p.

\displaystyle=0\qquad(\text{as }H\text{ satisfies }(\ref{pois-co2})).

Here
c.p.
stands for the cyclic permutations of the inputs
x,y,z
. Finally, we also have

\displaystyle\rho_{x}h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(y,z)-h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(\{x,y\},z)-h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(y,\{x,z\})+H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y\cdot z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\mu_{y}H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,z)-\mu_{z}H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}\big(d_{V}h(y,z)-h(d_{P}(y),z)-h(y,d_{P}(z))\big)-d_{V}h(\{x,y\},z)+h(d_{P}\{x,y\},z)+h(\{x,y\},d_{P}(z))

\displaystyle~~~-d_{V}h(y,\{x,z\})+h(d_{P}(y),\{x,z\})+h(y,d_{P}\{x,z\})+d_{V}H(x,y\cdot z)-H(d_{P}(x),y\cdot z)-H(x,d_{P}(y\cdot z))

\displaystyle~~~-\mu_{y}\big(d_{V}H(x,z)-H(d_{P}(x),z)-H(x,d_{P}(z))\big)-\mu_{z}\big(d_{V}H(x,y)-H(d_{P}(x),y)-H(x,d_{P}(y))\big)

\displaystyle=0\qquad(\text{by using }(\ref{g1}),\text{ the derivation property of }d_{P}\text{ and the identity }(\ref{pois-co3})).

This shows that the pair
(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})
satisfy all the identities (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
). Hence it is a Poisson
2
-cocycle.

(ii) Since
(h,H)
and
(h^{\prime},H^{\prime})
are cohomologous Poisson
2
-cocycles, there exists a linear map
\varphi:P\rightarrow V
such that the identities in (
4
) hold. Hence we have

\displaystyle h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y)-h^{\prime}_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y)

\displaystyle=d_{V}(h(x,y))-h(d_{P}(x),y)-h(x,d_{P}(y))-d_{V}(h^{\prime}(x,y))+h^{\prime}(d_{P}(x),y)+h^{\prime}(x,d_{P}(y))

\displaystyle=d_{V}\big(\mu_{x}\varphi(y)-\varphi(x\cdot y)+\mu_{y}\varphi(x)\big)-\big(\mu_{d_{P}(x)}\varphi(y)-\varphi(d_{P}(x)\cdot y)+\mu_{y}\varphi(d_{P}(x))\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\big(\mu_{x}\varphi(d_{P}(y))-\varphi(x\cdot d_{P}(y))+\mu_{d_{P}(y)}\varphi(x)\big)\qquad(\text{by }(\ref{cobound}))

\displaystyle=\mu_{x}(d_{V}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ d_{P})(y)-(d_{V}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ d_{P})(x\cdot y)+\mu_{y}(d_{V}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ d_{P})(x)\qquad(\text{by }(\ref{g1}))

and similarly,

\displaystyle H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y)-H^{\prime}_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\rho_{x}(d_{V}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ d_{P})(y)-(d_{V}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ d_{P})(\{x,y\})-\rho_{y}(d_{V}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ d_{P})(x).

This shows that the Poisson
2
-cocycles
(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})
and
(h^{\prime}_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H^{\prime}_{(d_{V},d_{P})})
are cohomologous by the map
d_{V}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ d_{P}
. This completes the proof.
∎

The above proposition shows that there is a well-defined map

\displaystyle\Theta:\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V))~~\text{ given by }~~\Theta(d_{V},d_{P})[(h,H)]:=[(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})],

for any arbitrary
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}
and
[(h,H)]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V)
. For a fixed section
s
, let the given abelian extension (
3
) produce the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)
. Then we define a map (also denoted by the same notation as before)
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V)
by

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(d_{V},d_{P}):=\Theta(d_{V},d_{P})[(h,H)]=[(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})],\text{ for }(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}.

The map
\mathcal{W}
defined above is called the
Wells map
in the context of Poisson derivations. It follows from Proposition
4.2
(ii) that the above Wells map is independent of the choice of the section
s
. We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

4.3 Theorem
.

Let (
3
) be an abelian extension of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
by a representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
. Then a pair
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathrm{Der}(V)\times\mathrm{Der}(P)
of Poisson derivations is inducible if and only if

\displaystyle(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{W}(d_{V},d_{P})=0.

Proof.

Let
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathrm{Der}(V)\times\mathrm{Der}(P)
be an inducible pair of Poisson derivations. Then there exists a Poisson derivation
d\in\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)
such that
d|_{V}=d_{V}
and
pds=d_{P}
, where
s
is any section. Further, we have seen in Proposition
4.1
that
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}
. Depending on a fixed section
s
, let the given abelian extension produce the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)
. Then we have

\displaystyle h_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y)

\displaystyle=d_{V}(h(x,y))-h(d_{P}(x),y)-h(x,d_{P}(y))

\displaystyle=d_{V}\big(s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-s(x\cdot y)\big)-sd_{P}(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)+s(d_{P}(x)\cdot y)-s(x)\cdot_{E}sd_{P}(y)+s(x\cdot d_{P}(y))

\displaystyle=s(x)\cdot_{E}ds(y)+ds(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-ds(x\cdot y)-sd_{P}(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-s(x)\cdot_{E}sd_{P}(y)+sd_{P}(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad(\because~d_{V}=d|_{V}\text{ and }d,d_{P}\text{ are derivations})

\displaystyle=\mu_{x}(ds-sd_{P})(y)-(ds-sd_{P})(x\cdot y)+\mu_{y}(ds-sd_{P})(x)

and similarly,
H_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(x,y)=\rho_{x}(ds-sd_{P})(y)-(ds-sd_{P})(\{x,y\})-\rho_{y}(ds-sd_{P})(x)
, for all
x,y\in P
. This shows that the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})
is cohomologous to the null Poisson
2
-cocycle
(0,0)
. Hence
\mathcal{W}(d_{V},d_{P})=[(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})]=0
.

Conversely, we assume that
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}
and
\mathcal{W}(d_{V},d_{P})=0
. Depending on a section
s
, let the given abelian extension produce the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)
. Since
\mathcal{W}(d_{V},d_{P})=0
, it follows that the Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h_{(d_{V},d_{P})},H_{(d_{V},d_{P})})
is cohomologous to the null Poisson
2
-cocycle. That is, there exists a linear map
\varphi:P\rightarrow V
such that

(15)

\displaystyle d_{V}(h(x,y))-h(d_{P}(x),y)-h(x,d_{P}(y))=~

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\varphi(y)-\varphi(x\cdot y)+\mu_{y}\varphi(x),

(16)

\displaystyle d_{V}(H(x,y))-H(d_{P}(x),y)-H(x,d_{P}(y))=~

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\varphi(y)-\varphi(\{x,y\})-\rho_{y}\varphi(x),

for all
x,y\in P
. We now define a linear map
d:E\rightarrow E
by

(17)

\displaystyle d(e)=d(s(x)+u)=s(d_{P}(x))+d_{V}(u)+\varphi(x),\text{ for }e=s(x)+u\in E.

Then for any
e=s(x)+u
and
e^{\prime}=s(y)+v
from the space
E
, we observe that

\displaystyle d(e\cdot_{E}e^{\prime})=d((s(x)+u)\cdot_{E}(s(y)+v))

\displaystyle=d\big(s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)+s(x)\cdot_{E}v+s(y)\cdot_{E}u\big)

\displaystyle=d\big(s(x\cdot y)+h(x,y)+\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u\big)

\displaystyle=s(d_{P}(x\cdot y))+d_{V}h(x,y)+d_{V}\mu_{x}v+d_{V}\mu_{y}u+\varphi(x\cdot y)\quad(\text{from the definition of }d)

\displaystyle=s(d_{P}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot d_{P}(y))+h(d_{P}(x),y)+h(x,d_{P}(y))+\mu_{x}\varphi(y)+\mu_{y}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu_{d_{P}(x)}v+\mu_{x}d_{V}(v)+\mu_{d_{P}(y)}u+\mu_{y}d_{V}(u)\quad(\text{by }(\ref{h1})\text{ and }(\ref{g1}))

\displaystyle=s(d_{P}(x))\cdot_{E}s(y)+s(x)\cdot_{E}s(d_{P}(y))+s(x)\cdot_{E}\varphi(y)+s(y)\cdot_{E}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle\qquad+s(d_{P}(x))\cdot_{E}v+s(x)\cdot_{E}d_{V}(v)+s(d_{P}(y))\cdot_{E}u+s(y)\cdot_{E}d_{V}(u)

\displaystyle=\big(s(d_{P}(x))+d_{V}(u)+\varphi(x)\big)\cdot_{E}(s(y)+v)~\!+~\!(s(x)+u)\cdot_{E}\big(s(d_{P}(y))+d_{V}(v)+\varphi(y)\big)

\displaystyle=d(e)\cdot_{E}e^{\prime}+e\cdot_{E}d(e^{\prime})

and along the same way,
d\{e,e^{\prime}\}_{E}=\{d(e),e^{\prime}\}_{E}+\{e,d(e^{\prime})\}_{E}
. This shows that
d\in\mathrm{Der}(E)
is a Poisson derivation on the Poisson algebra
E
. Further, it follows from (
17
) that
d(V)\subset V
(additionally
d|_{V}=d_{V}
) and
pds=d_{P}
. Hence
d\in\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)
and
\eta(d)=(d|_{V},pds)=(d_{V},d_{P})
which shows that the pair
(d_{V},d_{P})
is inducible.
∎

Next, we show that the Wells map defined above also fits into a short exact sequence. More precisely, we have the following.

4.4 Theorem
.

Let (
3
) be an abelian extension of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
by a representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
. Then there is a short exact sequence

(18)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(P,V)\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)\xrightarrow{\eta}\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V),

where the map
\iota
is given by
\iota(D)(e)=D(p(e))
, for
D\in\mathrm{Der}(P,V)
and
e\in E
.

Proof.

The map
\iota
is injective and hence the sequence is exact in the first place. Next, we take an element
d\in\mathrm{ker}(\eta)
. Thus,
\eta(d)=(d|_{V},pds)=(0,0)
which means that
d_{V}=0
and
pds=0
, where
s
is any arbitrary but fixed section. For any
x\in P
, we observe that
d(s(x))\in\mathrm{ker}(p)=\mathrm{im}(i)\cong V
. We now define a map
D:P\rightarrow V
by
D(x):=d(s(x))
, for
x\in P
. Then for any
x,y\in P
,

\displaystyle D(x\cdot y)=d(s(x\cdot y))=~

\displaystyle d\big(s(x)\cdot_{E}s(y)-h(x,y)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle s(x)\cdot_{E}ds(y)+d(s(x))\cdot_{E}s(y)\quad(\because~d\text{ is a Poisson derivation and }d|_{V}=0)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mu_{x}D(y)+\mu_{y}D(x)

and in the same way,
D\{x,y\}=\rho_{x}D(y)-\rho_{y}D(x)
. This shows that
D\in\mathrm{Der}(P,V)
is a Poisson derivation. Finally, for any
e=s(x)+u\in E
, we have

\displaystyle d(e)=d(s(x)+u)=d(s(x))=D(x)=\iota(D)(s(x)+u)=\iota(D)(e)

which in turn implies that
d\in\mathrm{im}(\iota)
. Hence
\mathrm{ker}(\eta)\subset\mathrm{im}(\iota)
. Conversely, if
D\in\mathrm{Der}(P,V)
then
\eta(\iota(D))=\big(\iota(D)|_{V},p(\iota(D))s\big)=(0,0)
follows from the definition of
\iota(D)
. Thus, we obtain that
\mathrm{im}(\iota)\subset\mathrm{ker}(\eta)
and hence the sequence (
18
) is also exact in the second place. Finally, to show that the sequence is exact in the third place, we begin with an element
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathrm{ker}(\mathcal{W})
. Then it follows from Theorem
4.3
that the pair
(d_{V},d_{P})
is inducible and hence it lies in the image of
\eta
. Conversely, if
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathrm{im}(\eta)
then it is inducible and thus
\mathcal{W}(d_{V},d_{P})=0
. This shows that
\mathrm{ker}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{im}(\eta).
This completes the proof.
∎

When (
3
) is a split abelian extension of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~.~\})
by the representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
, the Wells map
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(P,V)
vanishes identically. Hence, in this case, the short exact sequence (
18
) becomes

(19)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(P,V)\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)\xrightarrow{\eta}\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow 0.

For any
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}
, we define a map
d_{(d_{V},d_{P})}:E\rightarrow E
by
d_{(d_{V},d_{P})}(s(x)+u)=s(d_{P}(x))+d_{V}(u)
, for
s(x)+u\in E.
It is not hard to see that
d_{(d_{V},d_{P})}\in\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)
. Hence there is a map
t:\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)
given by
t(d_{V},d_{P}):=d_{(d_{V},d_{P})}
, for
(d_{V},d_{P})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}
. The map
t
is indeed a Lie algebra homomorphism and additionally
\eta t=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}}
. As a result, we get that (
19
) is a split short exact sequence of Lie algebras. Thus, we obtain the following isomorphism as Lie algebras

\displaystyle\mathrm{Der}_{V}(E)=\mathrm{Der}_{V}(P\ltimes V)\cong\mathcal{D}_{\mu,\rho}\ltimes\mathrm{Der}(P,V).

5.
Proto-twilled Poisson algebras and deformation maps

In this section, we consider proto-twilled Poisson algebras and study deformation maps on them. We observe that various well-known operators on Poisson algebras such as Poisson algebra homomorphisms, Poisson derivations, Rota-Baxter operators of any weight, crossed homomorphisms, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and modified Rota-Baxter operators can be seen as deformation maps in suitable proto-twilled Poisson algebras. Finally, given a proto-twilled commutative algebra
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},~\!\odot~\!)
and a deformation map
r
on it, we consider scalar deformations of
r
with respect to an associative algebra deformation of
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},~\!\odot~\!)
. Then the map
r
turns out to be a deformation map in the semi-classical proto-twilled Poisson algebra.

5.1 Definition
.

A
proto-twilled Poisson algebra
is a Poisson algebra
(\mathcal{P},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
whose underlying vector space has a direct sum decomposition
\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
into subspaces.

It is important to remark that, in a proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(\mathcal{P},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
as above,
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are just subspaces (need not be Poisson subalgebras) of
\mathcal{P}
. When exactly one of them is a Poisson subalgebra of
\mathcal{P}
, we call
(\mathcal{P},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
a
quasi-twilled Poisson algebra
. Further, it is said to be a
twilled Poisson algebra
when
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are both Poisson subalgebras of
\mathcal{P}
. A twilled Poisson algebra is also called a
matched pair of Poisson algebras
in the literature
[
23
]
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra. Since
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are both subspaces of
\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
, there are
12
(bi)linear maps

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}~\cdot_{1}:P_{1}\times P_{1}\rightarrow P_{1}\quad(\text{symmetric})\\
~\cdot_{2}:P_{2}\times P_{2}\rightarrow P_{2}\quad(\text{symmetric})\\
~\mu:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2})\\
~\nu:P_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{1})\\
~h:P_{1}\times P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}\quad(\text{symmetric})\\
~\theta:P_{2}\times P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}\quad(\text{symmetric})\end{cases}\qquad\quad\begin{cases}~\{~,~\}_{1}:P_{1}\times P_{1}\rightarrow P_{1}\quad(\text{antisymmetric})\\
~\{~,~\}_{2}:P_{2}\times P_{2}\rightarrow P_{2}\quad(\text{antisymmetric})\\
~\rho:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2})\\
~\psi:P_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{1})\\
~H:P_{1}\times P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}\quad(\text{antisymmetric})\\
~\Theta:P_{2}\times P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}\quad(\text{antisymmetric})\end{cases}

such that the operations
\odot
and
\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle(x,u)\odot(y,v)=~

\displaystyle\big(x\cdot_{1}y+\nu_{u}y+\nu_{v}x+\theta(u,v)~\!,~\!u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u+h(x,y)\big),

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{(x,u),(y,v)\}\!\!\}=~

\displaystyle\big(\{x,y\}_{1}+\psi_{u}y-\psi_{v}x+\Theta(u,v)~\!,~\!\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{x}v-\rho_{y}u+H(x,y)\big),

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in P_{1}\oplus P_{2}.
These
12
(bi)linear operations defined above satisfy certain compatibility conditions to make
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
into a Poisson algebra.

There are various examples of proto-twilled Poisson algebras arising from direct products, semidirect products, twisted semidirect products etc. Here we shall provide those examples which are useful in the study of some well-known operators on Poisson algebras.

5.2 Example
.

(Direct product)
Let
({P}_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
and
({P}_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
be two Poisson algebras. Then their
direct product

(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},~\!\cdot_{\mathrm{dir}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\mathrm{dir}})
is a proto-twilled Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{\mathrm{dir}}(y,v):=(x\cdot_{1}y~\!,~\!u\cdot_{2}v),\quad\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{\mathrm{dir}}:=(\{x,y\}_{1}~\!,~\!\{u,v\}_{2}),\text{ for }(x,u),(y,v)\in P_{1}\oplus P_{2}.

5.3 Example
.

(Semidirect product) Let
({P},~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. Then their
semidirect product

(P\oplus V,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes})
is a proto-twilled Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(y,v):=(x\cdot y~\!,~\!\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u)~~~\text{ and }~~~\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{\ltimes}:=(\{x,y\}~\!,~\!\rho_{x}v-\rho_{y}u),

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in P\oplus V
. Symmetrically,
(V\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\rtimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\rtimes})
is also a proto-twilled Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle(u,x)\cdot_{\rtimes}(v,y):=(\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u~\!,~\!x\cdot y)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\{(u,x),(v,y)\}_{\rtimes}:=(\rho_{x}v-\rho_{y}u~\!,~\!\{x,y\}).

Recall that
[
23
]
an
action
of a Poisson algebra
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
on another Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
is given by maps
\mu,\rho:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2})
that makes the vector space
P_{2}
into a representation of the Poisson algebra
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
such that for all
x\in P_{1}
and
u,v\in P_{2}
,

\displaystyle\mu_{x}(u\cdot_{2}v)=~

\displaystyle\mu_{x}(u)\cdot_{2}v,

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\{u,v\}_{2}=~

\displaystyle\{\mu_{x}u,v\}_{2}-u\cdot_{2}\rho_{x}v,

\displaystyle\rho_{x}(u\cdot_{2}v)=~

\displaystyle\rho_{x}(u)\cdot_{2}v+u\cdot_{2}\rho_{x}(v),

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\{u,v\}_{2}=~

\displaystyle\{\rho_{x}u,v\}_{2}+\{u,\rho_{x}v\}_{2}.

5.4 Example
.

Given an action of a Poisson algebra
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
on the Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
as above, the direct sum
P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
inherits a Poisson algebra structure with the operations

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(y,v):=(x\cdot_{1}y~\!,~\!u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u)~\text{ and }~\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{\ltimes}:=(\{x,y\}_{1}~\!,~\!\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{x}v-\rho_{y}u),

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
. Hence
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes})
is a proto-twilled Poisson algebra. Symmetrically,
(P_{2}\oplus P_{1},~\!\cdot_{\rtimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\rtimes})
is also a proto-twilled Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle(u,x)\cdot_{\rtimes}(v,y):=(u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u~\!,~\!x\cdot_{1}y)~~~\text{ and }~~~\{(u,x),(v,y)\}_{\rtimes}:=(\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{x}v-\rho_{y}u~\!,~\!\{x,y\}_{1}).

5.5 Example
.

(Twisted semidirect product) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. Then for any Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)\in Z^{2}(P,V)
, the direct sum
P\oplus V
carries a Poisson algebra structure whose multiplication and the Lie bracket are respectively given by

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{\ltimes_{h}}(y,v):=(x\cdot y~\!,~\!\mu_{x}v+\mu_{y}u+h(x,y))~~\text{ and }~~\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{\ltimes_{H}}:=(\{x,y\}~\!,~\!\rho_{x}v-\rho_{y}u+H(x,y)),

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in P\oplus V
. The Poisson algebra
(P\oplus V,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes_{h}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes_{H}})
is a proto-twilled Poisson algebra.

5.6 Example
.

(Reynolds semidirect product) This example is a particular case of the previous one where we consider the adjoint representation of a Poisson algebra and the Poisson
2
-cocycle is to be the negative of the given Poisson structure. More precisely, let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. Then
(P\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes_{-}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes_{-}})
is a proto-twilled Poisson algebra, where for
(x,u),(y,v)\in P\oplus P
,

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{\ltimes_{-}}(y,v):=~

\displaystyle(x\cdot y~\!,~\!x\cdot v+u\cdot y-x\cdot y),

\displaystyle\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{\ltimes_{-}}:=~

\displaystyle(\{x,y\}~\!,~\!\{x,v\}+\{u,y\}-\{x,y\}).

Another example of a proto-twilled Poisson algebra that is important in our study is given in the following result.

5.7 Proposition
.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. Then the triple
(P\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\mathrm{mod}})
is a proto-twilled Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}}(y,v):=~

\displaystyle(x\cdot y+u\cdot v~\!,~\!x\cdot v+u\cdot y),

\displaystyle\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{\mathrm{mod}}:=~

\displaystyle(\{x,y\}+\{u,v\}~\!,~\!\{x,v\}+\{u,y\}),

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in P\oplus P
. This is called the modified semidirect product.

Proof.

It has been shown in
[
13
]
that the multiplication
~\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}}~
is associative. Further, it is commutative as the multiplication
~\cdot~
is so. On the other hand, it is shown in
[
19
]
that the bracket
\{~,~\}_{\mathrm{mod}}
defines a Lie bracket on
P\oplus P
. Thus, it remains to show the Leibniz rule of a Poisson algebra. For any
(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)\in P\oplus P
, we have

\displaystyle\{(x,u),(y,v)\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}}(z,w)\}_{\mathrm{mod}}

\displaystyle=\{(x,u),(y\cdot z+v\cdot w~\!,~\!y\cdot w+v\cdot z)\}_{\mathrm{mod}}

\displaystyle=\big(\{x,y\cdot z\}+\{x,v\cdot w\}+\{u,y\cdot w\}+\{u,v\cdot z\}~\!,~\!\{x,y\cdot w\}+\{x,v\cdot z\}+\{u,y\cdot z\}+\{u,v\cdot w\}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\{x,y\}\cdot z+y\cdot\{x,z\}+\{x,v\}\cdot w+v\cdot\{x,w\}+\{u,y\}\cdot w+y\cdot\{u,w\}+\{u,v\}\cdot z+v\cdot\{u,z\},

\displaystyle\qquad\{x,y\}\cdot w+y\cdot\{x,w\}+\{x,v\}\cdot z+v\cdot\{x,z\}+\{u,y\}\cdot z+y\cdot\{u,z\}+\{u,v\}\cdot w+v\cdot\{u,w\}\big)

\displaystyle=(\{x,y\}+\{u,v\}~\!,~\!\{x,v\}+\{u,y\})\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}}(z,w)~+~(y,v)\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}}(\{x,z\}+\{u,w\}~\!,~\!\{x,w\}+\{u,z\})

\displaystyle=\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{\mathrm{mod}}\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}}(z,w)~+~(y,v)\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}}\{(x,u),(z,w)\}_{\mathrm{mod}}.

This proves the desired result.
∎

Deformation maps in a proto-twilled Poisson algebra.
The notion of deformation maps in a twilled Poisson algebra was considered by Agore and Militaru
[
2
]
in the classifying compliments problem for Poisson algebras. Here we shall generalize their notion in a given proto-twilled Poisson algebra. Although deformation maps in a proto-twilled Poisson algebra do not solve any compliments problem, they unify a wide class of well-known operators on Poisson algebras. As a result, one could study all these operators by studying deformation maps.

5.8 Definition
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra. A linear map
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
is said to be a
deformation map
if the graph
Gr(r)=\{(r(u),u)~\!|~\!u\in P_{2}\}
is a Poisson subalgebra of
(\mathcal{P},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
.

We have seen that a proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
can be described by 12 (bi)linear maps given in (
20
) that satisfy certain compatibility conditions. With such notations, a linear map
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
is a deformation map if and only if for all
u,v\in P_{2}
,

(21)

\displaystyle r(u)\cdot_{1}r(v)+\nu_{u}r(v)+\nu_{v}r(u)+\theta(u,v)=~

\displaystyle r\big(u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u+h(r(u),r(v))\big),

(22)

\displaystyle\{r(u),r(v)\}_{1}+\psi_{u}r(v)-\psi_{v}r(u)+\Theta(u,v)=~

\displaystyle r\big(\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u+H(r(u),r(v))\big).

5.9 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it. Then the vector space
P_{2}
inherits a Poisson algebra structure whose multiplication and the Lie bracket are respectively given by

\displaystyle u\cdot_{r}v:=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{2}((r(u),u)\odot(r(v),v))=u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u+h(r(u),r(v)),

\displaystyle\{u,v\}_{r}:=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{2}\{\!\!\{(r(u),u),(r(v),v)\}\!\!\}=\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u+H(r(u),r(v)),

for
u,v\in P_{2}
. (We denote this induced Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
simply by
(P_{2})_{r}
.)

In the following, we provide a list of operators (defined on a Poisson algebra) that can be regarded as deformation maps in suitable proto-twilled Poisson algebras.

5.10 Example
.

(
Poisson homomorphisms.
) Let
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
and
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
be two Poisson algebras. A
Poisson homomorphism
or a homomorphism of Poisson algebras from
P_{1}
to
P_{2}
is a linear map
\varphi:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
that satisfies
\varphi(x\cdot_{1}y)=\varphi(x)\cdot_{2}\varphi(y)
and
\varphi(\{x,y\}_{1})=\{\varphi(x),\varphi(y)\}_{2}
, for all
x,y\in P_{1}.
Equivalently,
\varphi
is simultaneously a commutative algebra homomorphism and Lie algebra homomorphism. It is easy to see that a linear map
\varphi:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
between two Poisson algebras is a Poisson homomorphism if and only if its graph
Gr(\varphi)=\{(\varphi(x),x)~\!|~\!x\in P_{1}\}
is a Poisson subalgebra of the direct product
(P_{2}\oplus P_{1},~\!\cdot_{\mathrm{dir}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\mathrm{dir}})
. Thus, it turns out that a Poisson homomorphism
\varphi:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
can be regarded as a deformation map in the proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(P_{2}\oplus P_{1},~\!\cdot_{\mathrm{dir}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\mathrm{dir}})
.

5.11 Example
.

(
Poisson derivations.
) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. A linear map
D:P\rightarrow V
is said to be a
Poisson derivation
on
P
with values in the representation
V
if

\displaystyle D(x\cdot y)=\mu_{x}D(y)+\mu_{y}D(x)\quad\text{ and }\quad D(\{x,y\})=\rho_{x}D(y)-\rho_{y}D(x),\text{ for all }x,y\in P.

A Poisson derivation is nothing but a Poisson
1
-cocycle of the Poisson algebra
P
with coefficients in the representation
V
. It can be checked that a linear map
D:P\rightarrow V
is a Poisson derivation if and only if its graph
Gr(D)=\{(D(x),x)~\!|~\!x\in P\}\subset V\oplus P
is a Poisson subalgebra of the semidirect product
(V\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\rtimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\rtimes})
given in Example
5.3
. Thus, a Poisson derivation can be seen as a deformation map in the proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(V\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\rtimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\rtimes})
.

5.12 Example
.

(
Relative Rota-Baxter operators (of weight
0
).
) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. A linear map
r:V\rightarrow P
is said to be a
relative Rota-Baxter operator

[
23
]
(also called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
) with respect to the representation
V
if

\displaystyle r(u)\cdot r(v)=r(\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u)\quad\text{ and }\quad\{r(u),r(v)\}=r(\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u),\text{ for all }u,v\in V.

It follows that a linear map
r:V\rightarrow P
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if the graph
Gr(r)=\{(r(u),u)~\!|~\!u\in V\}
is a Poisson subalgebra of the semidirect product
(P\oplus V,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes})
. Hence a relative Rota-Baxter operator can also be seen as a deformation map in a proto-twilled Poisson algebra.

When
(V,\mu,\rho)=(P,\mu=\mathrm{ad}_{\cdot}~\!,\rho=\mathrm{ad}_{\{~,~\}})
is the adjoint representation of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
, a relative Rota-Baxter operator is simply called a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on the Poisson algebra
P

[
3
]
. Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
play an important role in the study of pre-Poisson algebras and Poisson bialgebras.

5.13 Example
.

(
Relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
.
)
Let
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
be a Poisson algebra that acts on another Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
by the maps
\mu,\rho:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2}).
Then a linear map
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
is said to be a
relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
if it satisfies

\displaystyle r(u)\cdot_{1}r(v)=r(u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u)~~\text{ and }~~\{r(u),r(v)\}=r(\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u),

for all
u,v\in P_{2}
. When the Poisson algebra structure on
P_{2}
is trivial, then
r
becomes a relative Rota-Baxter operator (of weight
0
).

5.14 Proposition
.

(i)

For any Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
, the map
-\mathrm{Id}:P\rightarrow P
,
x\mapsto-x
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
.

(ii)

Moreover, if
r:P\rightarrow P
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
then
-\mathrm{Id}-r:P\rightarrow P
is so.

The following result shows that relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
can also be characterized by their graphs.

5.15 Proposition
.

Let
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
be a Poisson algebra that acts on another Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
by the maps
\mu,\rho:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2}).
A linear map
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
if and only if the graph
Gr(r)=\{(r(u),u)~\!|~\!u\in P_{2}\}
is a Poisson subalgebra of
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes})
given in Example
5.4
.

It follows from the above result that a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
is nothing but a deformation map in the proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes})
.

5.16 Example
.

(
Crossed homomorphisms.
) Let
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
be a Poisson algebra that acts on another Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
by the maps
\mu,\rho:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2}).
Then a linear map
D:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
is said to be a
relative crossed homomorphism
on
P_{1}
with values in
P_{2}
if

\displaystyle D(x\cdot_{1}y)=\mu_{x}D(y)+\mu_{y}D(x)+D(x)\cdot_{2}D(y)~~\text{ and }~~D(\{x,y\}_{1})=\rho_{x}D(y)-\rho_{y}D(x)+\{D(x),D(y)\}_{2},

for all
x,y\in P_{1}
. If the Poisson algebra structure on
P_{2}
is trivial, then a relative crossed homomorphism becomes a Poisson derivation. It is important to remark that the inverse of a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
(when invertible) is a relative crossed homomorphism on
P_{1}
with values in
P_{2}
. A useful characterization of a (relative) crossed homomorphism that is important in our purpose is the following.

5.17 Proposition
.

Let
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
be a Poisson algebra that acts on another Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
by the maps
\mu,\rho:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2}).
A linear map
D:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
is a relative crossed homomorphism if and only if its graph
Gr(D)=\{(D(x),x)~\!|~\!x\in P_{1}\}
is a Poisson subalgebra of the proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(P_{2}\oplus P_{1},~\!\cdot_{\rtimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\rtimes})
considered in Example
5.4
.

Thus, a relative crossed homomorphism can be regarded as a deformation map in
(P_{2}\oplus P_{1},~\!\cdot_{\rtimes}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\rtimes})
.

5.18 Example
.

(
Twisted Rota-Baxter operators.
)
Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. For any Poisson
2
-cocycle
(h,H)\in Z^{2}(P,V)
, a linear map
r:V\rightarrow P
is said to be a
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator
if it satisfies

\displaystyle r(u)\cdot r(v)=~

\displaystyle r\big(\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u+h(r(u),r(v))\big),

\displaystyle\{r(u),r(v)\}=~

\displaystyle r\big(\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u+H(r(u),r(v))\big),

for all
u,v\in V
. Twisted Rota-Baxter operators on Poisson algebras were studied and the underlying algebraic structure was found in
[
12
]
(see also
[
26
]
). An important characterization of a twisted Rota-Baxter operator is given by the following.

5.19 Proposition
.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra,
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation and
(h,H)
be a Poisson
2
-cocycle. Then a linear map
r:V\rightarrow P
is a
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator if and only if the graph
Gr(r)=\{(r(u),u)~\!|~\!u\in V\}
is a Poisson subalgebra of the twisted semidirect product
(P\oplus V,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes_{h}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes_{H}})
.

The above characterization enables us to realize a
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator
r:V\rightarrow P
as a deformation map in the twisted semidirect product
(P\oplus V,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes_{h}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes_{H}})
.

5.20 Example
.

(
Reynolds operators.
) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. A linear map
r:P\rightarrow P
is said to be a
Reynolds operator
on
P
if

(23)

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot r(y)=~

\displaystyle r\big(r(x)\cdot y+x\cdot r(y)-r(x)\cdot r(y)\big),

(24)

\displaystyle\{r(x),r(y)\}=~

\displaystyle r\big(\{r(x),y\}+\{x,r(y)\}-\{r(x),r(y)\}\big),

for all
x,y\in P
. Reynolds operators are examples of twisted Rota-Baxter operators. It is important to note that invertible Reynolds operators are closely related to Poisson derivations.

5.21 Proposition
.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. An invertible linear map
r:P\rightarrow P
is a Reynolds operator on
P
if and only if the map
r^{-1}-\mathrm{Id}:P\rightarrow P
is a Poisson derivation.

Proof.

In view of the invertibility of
r
, the identities (
23
), (
24
) are respectively equivalent to

\displaystyle(r^{-1}-\mathrm{Id})(x\cdot y)=~

\displaystyle(r^{-1}-\mathrm{Id})(x)\cdot y+x\cdot(r^{-1}-\mathrm{Id})(y),

\displaystyle(r^{-1}-\mathrm{Id})(\{x,y\})=~

\displaystyle\{(r^{-1}-\mathrm{Id})(x),y\}+\{x,(r^{-1}-\mathrm{Id})(y)\},

for all
x,y\in P
. This proves the desired result.
∎

Finally, an easy observation shows that a linear map
r:P\rightarrow P
is a Reynolds operator on
P
if and only if the graph
Gr(r)=\{(r(x),x)~\!|~\!x\in P\}
is a Poisson subalgebra of the Reynolds semidirect product
(P\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes_{-}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes_{-}})
. Hence Reynolds operators on a Poisson algebra
P
are equivalent to deformation maps in the proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(P\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes_{-}}~\!,\{~,~\}_{\ltimes_{-}})
.

5.22 Example
.

(
Modified Rota-Baxter operators.
) For a Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
, a linear map
r:P\rightarrow P
is said to be a
modified Rota-Baxter operator
on
P
if

(25)

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot r(y)=r(r(x)\cdot y+x\cdot r(y))-x\cdot y~~~\text{ and }~~~\{r(x),r(y)\}=r(\{r(x),y\}+\{x,r(y)\})-\{x,y\},

for all
x,y\in P
. Modified Rota-Baxter operators are closely related to Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
by the following result.

5.23 Proposition
.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. Then a linear map
r:P\rightarrow P
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
if and only if the map
\mathrm{Id}+2r:P\rightarrow P
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator on
P
.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}+2r)(x)\cdot(\mathrm{Id}+2r)(y)-(\mathrm{Id}+2r)\big((\mathrm{Id}+2r)(x)\cdot y+x\cdot(\mathrm{Id}+2r)(y)\big)+x\cdot y

\displaystyle=x\cdot y+2x\cdot r(y)+2r(x)\cdot y+4r(x)\cdot r(y)-2(\mathrm{Id}+2r)(x\cdot y+r(x)\cdot y+x\cdot r(y))+x\cdot y

\displaystyle=4\big(r(x)\cdot r(y)-r(x\cdot y+r(x)\cdot y+x\cdot r(y))\big)

and similarly,

\displaystyle\{(\mathrm{Id}+2r)(x),(\mathrm{Id}+2r)(y)\}-(\mathrm{Id}+2r)\big(\{(\mathrm{Id}+2r)(x),y\}+\{x,(\mathrm{Id}+2r)(y)\}\big)+\{x,y\}

\displaystyle=4\big(\{r(x),r(y)\}-r(\{x,y\}+\{r(x),y\}+\{x,r(y)\})\big).

This shows the desired result.
∎

Finally, one can show the following characterization of a modified Rota-Baxter operator on a Poisson algebra.

5.24 Proposition
.

Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. Then a linear map
r:P\rightarrow P
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator on
P
if and only if
Gr(r)=\{(r(x),x)~\!|~\!x\in P\}
is a Poisson subalgebra of the modified semidirect product
(P\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}},\{~,~\}_{\mathrm{mod}})
.

The proof of the above proposition is straightforward. As a consequence of this result, we get that a modified Rota-Baxter operator
r:P\rightarrow P
on a Poisson algebra
P
is equivalent to a deformation map in the proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(P\oplus P,~\!\cdot_{\mathrm{mod}},\{~,~\}_{\mathrm{mod}})
.

5.1.
Scalar deformations of
r

It is well-known that the semi-classical limit of an associative algebra deformation of a commutative algebra carries a Poisson algebra structure
[
21
]
. Recently, this result was extended when the algebras are endowed with representations
[
11
]
. Among others, the authors considered scalar deformations of a Rota-Baxter operator
r
defined on a given commutative algebra and showed that the same operator
r
becomes a Rota-Baxter operator on the semi-classical limit Poisson algebra. Here we shall generalize these results by considering proto-twilled associative algebra deformations of a proto-twilled commutative algebra.

A
proto-twilled associative algebra
is an associative algebra whose underlying space has a direct sum decomposition into subspaces. Further, it is said to be a
proto-twilled commutative algebra
if the underlying associative algebra is commutative.
Quasi-twilled and twilled associative algebras or commutative algebras can be defined similarly. Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot)
be a proto-twilled associative algebra or a proto-twilled commutative algebra. Then a linear map
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
is said to be a
deformation map
if its graph
Gr(r)=\{(r(u),u)~\!|~\!u\in P_{2}\}
is a subalgebra of
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot)
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot)
be a proto-twilled commutative algebra. Consider the space
\mathcal{P}[\!\![t]\!\!]
of all formal power series in
t
with coefficients from
\mathcal{P}
. Then we have
\mathcal{P}[\!\![t]\!\!]\cong P_{1}[\!\![t]\!\!]\oplus P_{2}[\!\![t]\!\!]
as
{\bf k}[\!\![t]\!\!]
-modules.
An associative algebra deformation of
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot)
is a formal sum

\displaystyle\odot_{t}=\odot_{0}+t\odot_{1}+t^{2}\odot_{2}+\cdots\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes 2},\mathcal{P})[\!\![t]\!\!]~\text{ with }\odot_{0}=\odot

that makes
(\mathcal{P}[\!\![t]\!\!]\cong P_{1}[\!\![t]\!\!]\oplus P_{2}[\!\![t]\!\!],\odot_{t})
into a proto-twilled associative algebra over the base ring
{\bf k}[\!\![t]\!\!].

Then by adapting the semi-classical limit construction, we get the following.

5.25 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot)
be a proto-twilled commutative algebra and let
\odot_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}\odot_{i}
(with
\odot_{0}=\odot
) be an associative algebra deformation of it. Define a bracket

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}:\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}~\text{ by }~\{\!\!\{(x,u),(y,v)\}\!\!\}:=(x,u)\odot_{1}(y,v)-(y,v)\odot_{1}(x,u),

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in\mathcal{P}
. Then
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is a proto-twilled Poisson algebra.

In particular, when
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot)
is a quasi-twilled (resp. twilled) commutative algebra and
\odot_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}\odot_{i}
(with
\odot_{0}=\odot
) is an associative deformation that makes
(\mathcal{P}[\!\![t]\!\!]\cong P_{1}[\!\![t]\!\!]\oplus P_{2}[\!\![t]\!\!],\odot_{t})
into a quasi-twilled (resp. twilled) associative algebra, then
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is a quasi-twilled (resp. twilled) Poisson algebra.

5.26 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot)
be a proto-twilled commutative algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it. Suppose there is an associative algebra deformation
\odot_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}\odot_{i}
(with
\odot_{0}=\odot
) of the given proto-twilled commutative algebra for which the
{\bf k}[\!\![t]\!\!]
-linear extension of
r
, namely,

\displaystyle r_{\bf k}:P_{2}[\!\![t]\!\!]\rightarrow P_{1}[\!\![t]\!\!]~~~\text{ defined by }~~~r_{\bf k}(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u_{i}t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r(u_{i})t^{i}

is a deformation map in the proto-twilled associative algebra
(\mathcal{P}[\!\![t]\!\!]\cong P_{1}[\!\![t]\!\!]\oplus P_{2}[\!\![t]\!\!],\odot_{t})
. Then the linear map
r
is a deformation map in the semi-classical proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}).

Proof.

For any
u,v\in P_{2}
, consider the elements
(r(u),u)
and
(r(v),v)
from
Gr(r)
. Realizing these elements in
Gr(r_{\mathbf{k}})
and using the fact that
Gr(r_{\mathbf{k}})
is a subalgebra of
(\mathcal{P}[\!\![t]\!\!],\odot_{t})
, we get that

\displaystyle(r(u),u)\odot_{t}(r(v),v)-(r(v),v)\odot_{t}(r(u),u)\in Gr(r_{\mathbf{k}}).

Diving this element by
t
and then taking
t
tends to
0
, we get that
\{\!\!\{(r(u),u),(r(v),v)\}\!\!\}\in Gr(r)
. This shows that
Gr(r)
is also a subalgebra of the Lie algebra
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
. Hence the result follows.
∎

As a consequence of the above theorem, we obtain the following results.

.

A,B
be two commutative algebras and

B
.

(iv) Let
A
be a commutative algebra and
r:A\rightarrow A
be a Reynolds operator (resp. modified Rota-Baxter operator) on
A
. Suppose there is an associative algebra deformation
(A[\!\![t]\!\!],\mu_{t})
of the commutative algebra
A
for which the
{\bf k}[\!\![t]\!\!]
-linear extension
r_{\bf k}:A[\!\![t]\!\!]\rightarrow A[\!\![t]\!\!]
is also a Reynolds operator (resp. modified Rota-Baxter operator) on the deformed associative algebra

. Then the map

is a Reynolds operator (resp. modified Rota-Baxter operator) on the corresponding semi-classical Poisson algebra.

6.
Cohomology of deformation maps: Examples

This section introduces the cohomology of a deformation map
r
in a given proto-twilled Poisson algebra. In particular, we obtain the cohomology of Poisson homomorphisms, Poisson derivations, relative Rota-Baxter operators, crossed homomorphisms, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and modified Rota-Baxter operators on Poisson algebras. We will use the cohomology of a deformation map
r
in the next section when we study the formal deformation theory of the operator
r
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra. For any linear map
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
, consider two new linear maps
\mathrm{Id}+\widetilde{r}:P_{1}\oplus P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
and
\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r}:P_{1}\oplus P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
defined by

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}+\widetilde{r})(x,u):=(x+r(u),u)\quad\text{ and }\quad(\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})(x,u):=(x-r(u),u),

for
(x,u)\in P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
. Then
\mathrm{Id}+\widetilde{r}
is a linear isomorphism with the inverse
\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r}
. Using these maps, one may define a new (proto-twilled) Poisson algebra structure
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot_{r},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}_{r})
given by

\displaystyle(x,u)\odot_{r}(y,v):=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})\big((\mathrm{Id}+\widetilde{r})(x,u)\odot(\mathrm{Id}+\widetilde{r})(y,v)\big),

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{(x,u),(y,v)\}\!\!\}_{r}:=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})\{\!\!\{(\mathrm{Id}+\widetilde{r})(x,u),(\mathrm{Id}+\widetilde{r})(y,v)\}\!\!\},

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
. Moreover, the map
\mathrm{Id}+\widetilde{r}:P_{1}\oplus P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
is an isomorphism of Poisson algebras from
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot_{r},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}_{r})
to
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
.

6.1 Proposition
.

Let
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it. Then
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot_{r},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}_{r})
is quasi-twilled Poisson algebra with
P_{2}
being a Poisson subalgebra of it.

Proof.

To show that
P_{2}
is a Poisson subalgebra of
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot_{r},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}_{r})
, we observe that

\displaystyle(0,u)\odot_{r}(0,v)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})\big((r(u),u)\odot(r(v),v)\big)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})\big(r(u)\cdot_{1}r(v)+\nu_{u}r(v)+\nu_{v}r(u)+\theta(u,v)~\!,~\!u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u+h(r(u),r(v))\big)

(26)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{dm1})}}{{=}}\big(0~\!,~\!u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u+h(r(u),r(v))\big)\in\{0\}\oplus P_{2}

and similarly,

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{(0,u),(0,v)\}\!\!\}_{r}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})\big(\{r(u),r(v)\}_{1}+\psi_{u}r(v)-\psi_{v}r(u)+\Theta(u,v)~\!,~\!\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u+H(r(u),r(v))\big)

(27)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{dm2})}}{{=}}\big(0,\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u+H(r(u),r(v))\big)\in\{0\}\oplus P_{2}.

This proves the result.
∎

It follows from (
26
) and (
27
) that the Poisson algebra structure on
P_{2}
induced from
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot_{r},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}_{r})
coincides with the one given in Proposition
5.9
.

6.2 Proposition
.

Let
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it. Define maps
\nu_{r},\psi_{r}:P_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{1})
by

\displaystyle(\nu_{r})_{u}x:=~

\displaystyle\nu_{u}x+r(u)\cdot_{1}x-r(\mu_{x}u+h(r(u),x)),

\displaystyle(\psi_{r})_{u}x:=~

\displaystyle\psi_{u}x+\{r(u),x\}_{1}-r(-\rho_{x}u+H(r(u),x)),

for
u\in P_{2}
and
x\in P_{1}
. Then the triple
(P_{1},\nu_{r},\psi_{r})
is a representation of the induced Poisson algebra
(P_{2})_{r}
.

Proof.

We have already seen that
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot_{r},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}_{r})
is a quasi-twilled Poisson algebra with
P_{2}
being a Poisson subalgebra of it (and the induced Poisson algebra structure being
(P_{2})_{r}
). Moreover, it follows that the Poisson algebra
(P_{2})_{r}
has a representation on the space
P_{1}
with the action maps
\overline{\nu},\overline{\psi}:P_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{1})
given by

\displaystyle\overline{\nu}_{u}x:=\mathrm{pr}_{1}\big((0,u)\odot_{r}(x,0)\big)=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{1}\big((\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})((r(u),u)\odot(x,0))\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{1}\big((\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})(r(u)\cdot_{1}x+\nu_{u}x~\!,~\!\mu_{x}u+h(r(u),x))\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{1}\big(r(u)\cdot_{1}x+\nu_{u}x-r(\mu_{x}u+h(r(u),x))~\!,~\!\mu_{x}u+h(r(u),x)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r(u)\cdot_{1}x+\nu_{u}x-r(\mu_{x}u+h(r(u),x))=(\nu_{r})_{u}x

and

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}_{u}x:=\mathrm{pr}_{1}\{\!\!\{(0,u),(x,0)\}\!\!\}_{r}=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{1}\big((\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})(\{\!\!\{(r(u),u),(x,0)\}\!\!\})\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{1}\big((\mathrm{Id}-\widetilde{r})(\{r(u),x\}_{1}+\psi_{u}x~\!,~\!-\rho_{x}u+H(r(u),x))\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{1}\big(\{r(u),x\}_{1}+\psi_{u}x-r(-\rho_{x}u+H(r(u),x))~\!,~\!-\rho_{x}u+H(r(u),x)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\{r(u),x\}_{1}+\psi_{u}x-r(-\rho_{x}u+H(r(u),x))=(\psi_{r})_{u}x,

for
u\in P_{2}
and
x\in P_{1}
. This proves the result.
∎

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it. Then one may consider the FGV cochain complex of the induced Poisson algebra
(P_{2})_{r}
with coefficients in the representation
(P_{1},\nu_{r},\psi_{r})
given in the above proposition. More precisely, we set

\displaystyle C^{k}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})=\oplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+n=k\\
m\neq 1\end{subarray}}C^{m,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1}),\text{ where }C^{m,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})\text{ is the space of }(m,n)\text{-cochains and}

maps
\delta^{m,n}_{\mathrm{H}}:C^{m,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})\rightarrow C^{m+1,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})
(
m\neq 0
),
\delta^{0,n}_{\mathrm{H}}:C^{0,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})\rightarrow C^{2,n-1}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})
and
\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}^{m,n}:C^{m,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})\rightarrow C^{m,n+1}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})
by

\displaystyle(\delta^{m,n}_{\mathrm{H}}f)((u_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{m+1})\otimes~

\displaystyle(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}))=(\nu_{r})_{u_{1}}f((u_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{m+1})\otimes(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i}~\!f((u_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{i}\cdot_{r}u_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{m+1})\otimes(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}))

\displaystyle+(-1)^{m+1}~\!(\nu_{r})_{u_{m+1}}f((u_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{m})\otimes(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n})),

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{0,n}:C^{0,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(P_{2}\otimes\wedge^{n-1}P_{2},P_{1})=C^{1,n-1}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})\xrightarrow{\delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{1,n-1}}C^{2,n-1}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1}),

and

\displaystyle(\delta^{m,n}_{\mathrm{CE}}f)

\displaystyle((u_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{m})\otimes(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n+1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}~\!\bigg((\psi_{r})_{v_{i}}f((u_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{m})\otimes(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{v_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n+1}))

\displaystyle~~-\sum_{j=1}^{m}f((u_{1}\otimes\otimes\{v_{i},u_{j}\}_{r}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{m})\otimes(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{v_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n+1})\bigg)

\displaystyle~~+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j}~\!f((u_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{m})\otimes(\{v_{i},v_{j}\}_{r}\wedge v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{v_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{v_{j}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n+1})),

for any
f\in C^{m,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+1},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\in P_{2}
. Finally, we define a map

\displaystyle\delta^{r}_{\mathrm{FGV}}:C^{k}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})\rightarrow C^{k+1}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})~~\text{ by }~~\delta_{\mathrm{FGV}}^{r}(f)=\delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{m,n}(f)+(-1)^{m}~\!\delta^{m,n}_{\mathrm{CE}}(f),

for
f\in C^{m,n}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1}).

Then it turns out that
\{C^{\bullet}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1}),\delta^{r}_{\mathrm{FGV}}\}
is a cochain complex, called the cochain complex associated with the deformation map
r
. The corresponding cohomology groups are called the cohomology groups of the operator
r
, and they are denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{FGV}}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})
.

So far, we have defined the cohomology theory of a deformation map in a given proto-twilled Poisson algebra. In various examples of proto-twilled Poisson algebras and deformation maps there, we obtain the cohomologies of Poisson algebra homomorphisms, Poisson derivations, Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
and
1
, crossed homomorphisms, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and modified Rota-Baxter operators on a Poisson algebra. Thus, the cohomology for any of these operators is described by FGV cohomology of the corresponding induced Poisson algebra with coefficients in a suitable representation.

(
Cohomology of Poisson homomorphisms
.) Let
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
and
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
be two Poisson algebras, and
\varphi:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
be a Poisson homomorphism. Then the Poisson algebra
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
has a representation on the vector space
P_{2}
with the maps
\mu_{\varphi},\rho_{\varphi}:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2})
which are respectively given by

\displaystyle(\mu_{\varphi})_{x}u:=\varphi(x)\cdot_{2}u~~~\text{ and }(\rho_{\varphi})_{x}u:=\{\varphi(x),u\}_{2},\text{ for }x\in P_{1},u\in P_{2}.

The FGV cohomology of the Poisson algebra
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
with coefficients in the above representation on
P_{2}
is defined to be the cohomology of the Poisson homomorphism
\varphi
.

(
Cohomology of Poisson derivations.
) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. For any Poisson derivation
D:P\rightarrow V
, the FGV cohomology of the given Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\mu,\rho)
is defined to be the cohomology of the Poisson derivation
D
. It follows that the cohomology of a Poisson derivation
D
is independent of
D
itself.

(
Cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
.
) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra,
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it and
r:V\rightarrow P
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
. Then
V
inherits a Poisson algebra structure whose multiplication and the bracket are respectively given by

\displaystyle u\cdot_{r}v:=\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\{u,v\}_{r}:=\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u,\text{ for }u,v\in V.

Moreover, there is a representation of this induced Poisson algebra
(V,~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
on the space
P
with the action maps
\nu_{r},\psi_{r}:V\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P)
which are respectively given by

\displaystyle(\nu_{r})_{u}x=r(u)\cdot x-r(\mu_{x}u)~~~\text{ and }~~~(\psi_{r})_{u}x=\{r(u),x\}+r(\rho_{x}u),\text{ for }u\in V,x\in P.

The FGV cohomology of the Poisson algebra
(V,~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
with coefficients in the representation
(P,\nu_{r},\psi_{r})
is called the cohomology of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
r
(of weight
0
).

(
Cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
.
) Let
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
be a Poisson algebra that acts on another Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
by the maps
\mu,\rho:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2})
. Suppose
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
. Then the space
P_{2}
carries a new Poisson algebra structure whose multiplication and the bracket are respectively given by

\displaystyle u\cdot_{r}v:=u\cdot_{2}v+\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\{u,v\}_{r}:=\{u,v\}_{2}+\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u,\text{ for }u,v\in P_{2}.

The Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
has a representation on the space
P_{1}
with the action maps
\nu_{r},\psi_{r}:P_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{1})
given by

\displaystyle(\nu_{r})_{u}x=r(u)\cdot_{1}x-r(\mu_{x}u)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~(\psi_{r})_{u}x=\{r(u),x\}_{1}+r(\rho_{x}u),\text{ for }u\in P_{2},x\in P_{1}.

The corresponding FGV cohomology is defined to be the cohomology of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
r
(of weight
1
).

(
Cohomology of Crossed homomorphisms.
) As before, let
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
be a Poisson algebra that acts on another Poisson algebra
(P_{2},~\!\cdot_{2}~\!,\{~,~\}_{2})
by the maps
\mu,\rho:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2})
. Let
D:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
be a crossed homomorphism on
P_{1}
with values in
P_{2}
. Then the Poisson algebra
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
has a new representation on the space
P_{2}
with the action maps
\mu_{D},\rho_{D}:P_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(P_{2})
given by

\displaystyle(\mu_{D})_{x}u=\mu_{x}u+D(x)\cdot_{2}u~~~~\text{ and }~~~~(\rho_{D})_{x}u=\rho_{x}u+\{D(x),u\}_{2},\text{ for }x\in P_{1},u\in P_{2}.

Then the FGV cohomology of the Poisson algebra
(P_{1},~\!\cdot_{1}~\!,\{~,~\}_{1})
with coefficients in the representation
(P_{2},\mu_{D},\rho_{D})
is defined to be the cohomology of the crossed homomorphism
D
.

(
Cohomology of twisted Rota-Baxter operators.
) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra,
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it and
(h,H)\in Z^{2}(P,V)
be a Poisson
2
-cocycle. Let
r:V\rightarrow P
be an
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator. Then the vector space
V
can be given a Poisson algebra structure whose multiplication and bracket are respectively given by

\displaystyle u\cdot_{r}v:=\mu_{r(u)}v+\mu_{r(v)}u+h(r(u),r(v))~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\{u,v\}_{r}:=\rho_{r(u)}v-\rho_{r(v)}u+H(r(u),r(v)),\text{ for }u,v\in V.

The Poisson algebra
(V,~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
has a representation of the vector space
P
with the action maps

\displaystyle(\nu_{r})_{u}x=r(u)\cdot x-r(\mu_{x}u+h(r(u),x))~~~\text{ and }~~~(\psi_{r})_{u}x=\{r(u),x\}-r(-\rho_{x}u+H(r(u),x)),

for
u\in V
,
x\in P
. The FGV cohomology of the Poisson algebra
(V,~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
with coefficients in the representation
(P,\nu_{r},\psi_{r})
is defined to be cohomology of the operator
r
.

(
Cohomology of Reynolds operators.
) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
r:P\rightarrow P
be a Reynolds operator on it. Then the space
P
inherits a new Poisson algebra structure with the operations

\displaystyle x\cdot_{r}y:=r(x)\cdot y+x\cdot r(y)-r(x)\cdot r(y)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\{x,y\}_{r}:=\{r(x),y\}+\{x,r(y)\}-\{r(x),r(y)\},

for
x,y\in P
. This Poisson structure has a representation on the vector space
P
itself with the action maps

\displaystyle(\mu_{r})_{x}y=r(x)\cdot y-r(x\cdot y-r(x)\cdot y)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~(\rho_{r})_{x}y=\{r(x),y\}-r(\{x,y\}-\{r(x),y\}),\text{ for }x,y\in P.

The FGV cohomology of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
with coefficients in the above representation on
P
is said to be the cohomology of the Reynolds operator
r
.

(
Cohomology of modified Rota-Baxter operators.
) Let
(P,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
r:P\rightarrow P
be a modified Rota-Baxter operator on it. Then there is a new Poisson algebra structure on the vector space
P
with the operations

\displaystyle x\cdot_{r}y:=r(x)\cdot y+x\cdot r(y)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\{x,y\}_{r}:=\{r(x),y\}+\{x,r(y)\},\text{ for }x,y\in P.

Moreover, this Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
has a representation on the space
P
itself with the action maps

\displaystyle(\mu_{r})_{x}y=r(x)\cdot y-r(x\cdot y)~~\text{ and }~~(\rho_{r})_{x}y=\{r(x),y\}-r\{x,y\},\text{ for }x,y\in P.

We call the cohomology of the Poisson algebra
(P,~\!\cdot_{r}~\!,\{~,~\}_{r})
with coefficients in the representation above as the cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter operator
r
.

7.
Linear and formal deformation theory of deformation maps

In this section, we study linear and formal deformation theory of a deformation map
r
in a given proto-twilled Poisson algebra. In particular, we define Nijenhuis elements associated with a deformation map
r
that generate linear deformations. Finally, we also find a sufficient condition for the rigidity of a deformation map
r
.

Let
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it. A
linear deformation
of
r
is given by a parametrized sum
r_{t}=r+t~\!r_{1}
(for some fixed
r_{1}\in\mathrm{Hom}(P_{2},P_{1})
) that is also a deformation map for each value of the parameter
t\in{\bf k}
. In this case, we say that the map
r_{1}\in\mathrm{Hom}(P_{2},P_{1})
generates a linear deformation of
r
. It follows that
r_{1}\in\mathrm{Hom}(P_{2},P_{1})
generates a linear deformation of
r
if and only if the following set of identities hold:

(28)

\displaystyle r(u)\cdot_{1}r_{1}(v)+r_{1}(u)\cdot_{1}r(v)+\nu_{u}r_{1}(v)+\nu_{v}r_{1}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad=r\big(\mu_{r_{1}(u)}v+\mu_{r_{1}(v)}u+h(r_{1}(u),r(v))+h(r(u),r_{1}(v))\big)+r_{1}(u\cdot_{r}v),

(29)

\displaystyle r_{1}(u)\cdot_{1}r_{1}(v)=r_{1}\big(\mu_{r_{1}(u)}v+\mu_{r_{1}(v)}u+h(r(u),r_{1}(v))+h(r_{1}(u),r(v))\big)+r\big(h(r_{1}(u),r_{1}(v))\big),

(30)

\displaystyle r_{1}\big(h(r_{1}(u),r_{1}(v))\big)=0,

(31)

\displaystyle\{r(u),r_{1}(v)\}_{1}+\{r_{1}(u),r(v)\}_{1}+\psi_{u}r_{1}(v)-\psi_{v}r_{1}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad=r\big(\rho_{r_{1}(u)}v-\rho_{r_{1}(v)}u+H(r_{1}(u),r(v))+H(r(u),r_{1}(v))\big)+r_{1}(\{u,v\}_{r}),

(32)

\displaystyle\{r_{1}(u),r_{1}(v)\}_{1}=r_{1}\big(\rho_{r_{1}(u)}v-\rho_{r_{1}(v)}u+H(r(u),r_{1}(v))+H(r_{1}(u),r(v))\big)+r\big(H(r_{1}(u),r_{1}(v))\big),

(33)

\displaystyle r_{1}\big(H(r_{1}(u),r_{1}(v))\big)=0,

for all
u,v\in P_{2}
.

Before we define ‘equivalence’ between linear deformations, we need to first define homomorphisms between deformation maps. Let
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r,r^{\prime}:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be two deformation maps. A homomorphism from
r
to
r^{\prime}
is a pair
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
of linear maps
\varphi_{1}:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{2}
that make the map
\varphi_{1}\oplus\varphi_{2}:P_{1}\oplus P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
,
(x,u)\mapsto(\varphi_{1}(x),\varphi_{2}(u))
into a homomorphism of Poisson algebras satisfying
\varphi_{1}\circ r=r^{\prime}\circ\varphi_{2}
.

7.1 Definition
.

Let
r_{t}=r+t~\!r_{1}
and
r_{t}^{\prime}=r+t~\!r_{1}^{\prime}
be two linear deformations of
r
. Then
r_{t}
is said to be
equivalent
with
r_{t}^{\prime}
if there exists an element
x_{0}\in P_{1}
such that the pair of maps

\displaystyle\big(\varphi_{1,t}=\mathrm{Id}_{P_{1}}+t\{x_{0},-\}_{1}~\!,~\!\varphi_{2,t}=\mathrm{Id}_{P_{2}}+t(\rho_{x_{0}}+H(x_{0},r-))\big)

defines a homomorphism of deformation maps from
r_{t}
to
r_{t}^{\prime}
.

Thus, for the above
\varphi_{1,t}
and
\varphi_{2,t}
, we must have

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1,t}\oplus\varphi_{2,t})((x,u)\odot(y,v))=~

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1,t}\oplus\varphi_{2,t})(x,u)\odot(\varphi_{1,t}\oplus\varphi_{2,t})(y,v),

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1,t}\oplus\varphi_{2,t})\{\!\!\{(x,u),(y,v)\}\!\!\}=~

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{(\varphi_{1,t}\oplus\varphi_{2,t})(x,u),(\varphi_{1,t}\oplus\varphi_{2,t})(y,v)\}\!\!\},

\displaystyle\varphi_{1,t}\circ r_{t}=~

\displaystyle r_{t}^{\prime}\circ\varphi_{2,t}.

Note that the last condition is equivalent to the following: for
u\in P_{2}
,

(34)

\displaystyle r_{1}(u)-r_{1}^{\prime}(u)=~

\displaystyle\{r(u),x_{0}\}_{1}-r\big(-\rho_{x_{0}}u+H(r(u),x_{0})\big),

(35)

\displaystyle\{r_{1}(u),x_{0}\}_{1}=~

\displaystyle r_{1}^{\prime}\big(-\rho_{x_{0}}u+H(r(u),x_{0})\big).

A linear deformation
r_{t}=r+t~\!r_{1}
of a deformation map
r
is said to be
trivial
if it is equivalent to the (undeformed) linear deformation
r_{t}^{\prime}=r
. We will now consider Nijenhuis elements associated with a deformation map
r
that generate trivial linear deformations.

7.2 Definition
.

Let
(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it.
An element
x_{0}\in P_{1}
is said to be a
Nijenhuis element
associated with the deformation map
r
if for all values of the parameter
t\in{\bf k}
, the map

\displaystyle P_{1}\oplus P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}\oplus P_{2},~(x,u)\mapsto\big(x+t\{x_{0},x\}_{1}~\!,~\!u+t(\rho_{x_{0}}u+H(x_{0},r(u)))\big)

is a Poisson algebra homomorphism satisfying additionally

(36)

\displaystyle\big\{\{r(u),x_{0}\}_{1}-r(-\rho_{x_{0}}u+H(r(u),x_{0}))~\!,~\!x_{0}\}_{1}=0,\text{ for all }u\in P_{2}.

The set of all Nijenhuis elements associated with
r
is denoted by
\mathrm{Nij}(r)
.

Let
r_{t}=r+t~\!r_{1}
be a trivial linear deformation of
r
. Then there exists
x_{0}\in P_{1}
such that the pair of maps

\big(\varphi_{1,t}=\mathrm{Id}_{P_{1}}+t\{x_{0},-\}_{1}~\!,~\!\varphi_{2,t}=\mathrm{Id}_{P_{2}}+t(\rho_{x_{0}}+H(x_{0},r-))\big)
defines a homomorphism of deformation maps from
r_{t}=r+t~\!r_{1}
to
r_{t}^{\prime}=r
. In the identities (
34
) and (
35
), by substituting
r_{1}^{\prime}=0
, we obtain (
36
). Hence, it turns out that
x_{0}\in P_{1}
is a Nijenhuis element associated with the deformation map
r
. Conversely, if the map
\psi=0
in the proto-twilled Poisson algebra (see (
20
)) and
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Nij}(r)
is a Nijenhuis element associated with
r
, then it is not hard to see that
r_{t}=r+t~\!\delta^{r}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(x_{0})
is a trivial linear deformation of
r
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it. A
formal one-parameter deformation
of
r
is given by a formal power series

\displaystyle r_{t}=r_{0}+tr_{1}+t^{2}r_{2}+\cdots\in\mathrm{Hom}(P_{2},P_{1})[\!\![t]\!\!]\text{ with }r_{0}=r

such that its
{\bf k}[\!\![t]\!\!]
-linear extension map (also denoted by the same notation)
r_{t}:P_{2}[\!\![t]\!\!]\rightarrow P_{1}[\!\![t]\!\!]
is a deformation map in the proto-twilled Poisson algebra
(\mathcal{P}[\!\![t]\!\!]\cong P_{1}[\!\![t]\!\!]\oplus P_{2}[\!\![t]\!\!],\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}).
Here the Poisson algebra structure of
\mathcal{P}
has been extended to a Poisson algebra structure on
\mathcal{P}[\!\![t]\!\!]
by using
{\bf k}[\!\![t]\!\!]
-(bi)linearity.

It turns out that
r_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}r_{i}
(with
r_{0}=r
) is a formal one-parameter deformation of
r
if and only if for each
n\geq 1
and
u,v\in P_{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}r_{i}(u)\cdot_{1}r_{j}(v)\!~

\displaystyle+~\!\nu_{u}r_{n}(v)+\nu_{v}r_{n}(u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r_{n}(u\cdot_{2}v)+\sum_{i+j=n}r_{i}\big(\mu_{r_{j}(u)}v+\mu_{r_{j}(v)}u\big)+\sum_{i+j+k=n}r_{i}\big(h(r_{j}(u),r_{k}(v))\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\{r_{i}(u),r_{j}(v)\}_{1}\!~

\displaystyle+~\!\psi_{u}r_{n}(v)-\psi_{v}r_{n}(u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r_{n}(\{u,v\}_{2})+\sum_{i+j=n}r_{i}\big(\rho_{r_{j}(u)}v-\rho_{r_{j}(v)}u\big)+\sum_{i+j+k=n}r_{i}\big(H(r_{j}(u),r_{k}(v))\big).

For
n=1
, the above two identities are respectively equivalent to
\delta^{1,0}_{\mathrm{H}}(r_{1})(u,v)=0
(or
\delta^{0,1}_{\mathrm{H}}(r_{1})(u,v)=0
) and
\delta^{0,1}_{\mathrm{CE}}(r_{1})(u,v)=0
. Hence

\displaystyle\delta^{r}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(r_{1})=\delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{0,1}(r_{1})+\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}^{1,0}(r_{1})=0.

This shows that the map
r_{1}\in\mathrm{Hom}(P_{2},P_{1})=C^{0,1}\big((P_{2})_{r},P_{1}\big)=C^{1}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})
is a
1
-cocycle in the cochain complex associated with the deformation map
r
. This is called the
infinitesimal
of the given formal deformation
r_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i}t^{i}
.

7.3 Definition
.

Let
r_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}r_{i}
and
r_{t}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}r_{i}^{\prime}
(with
r_{0}=r_{0}^{\prime}=r
) be two formal one-parameter deformations of
r
. They are said to be
equivalent
if there exists
x_{0}\in P_{1}
and maps
\varphi_{1,i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(P_{1},P_{1})
,
\varphi_{2,i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(P_{2},P_{2})
for
i\geq 2
such that the pair of maps

\displaystyle\big(\varphi_{1,t}=\mathrm{id}_{P_{1}}+t\{x_{0},-\}_{1}+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}~\!\varphi_{1,i}~\!,~\!\varphi_{2,t}=\mathrm{id}_{P_{2}}+t(\rho_{x_{0}}+H(x_{0},r-))+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}~\!\varphi_{2,i}\big)

defines a homomorphism of deformation maps from
r_{t}
to
r_{t}^{\prime}
.

By expanding the condition
\varphi_{1,t}\circ r_{t}=r_{t}^{\prime}\circ\varphi_{2,t}
and equating coefficients of
t
in both sides, we simply obtain the identity (
34
). This, in turn, implies that (when the map
\psi=0
)

\displaystyle r_{1}(u)-r^{\prime}_{1}(u)=(\psi_{r})_{u}x_{0}=\delta^{r}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(x_{0})(u),\text{ for }u\in P_{2}.

As a summary, we get the following.

7.4 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra and
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
be a deformation map on it. Then the infinitesimal of any formal one-parameter deformation of
r
is a
1
-cocycle in the cochain complex associated with
r
. Moreover, when
\psi=0
, the infinitesimals of equivalent formal one-parameter deformations are cohomologous, i.e., they correspond to the same element in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{FGV}}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})
.

In the following, we shall consider the rigidity of a deformation map
r
and find a sufficient condition for the rigidity.

7.5 Definition
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra. A deformation map
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
is said to be
rigid
if any formal one-parameter deformation
r_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}r_{i}
of
r
is equivalent to the undeformed one
r_{t}^{\prime}=r
.

7.6 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}=P_{1}\oplus P_{2},\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
be a proto-twilled Poisson algebra in which
\psi=0
. Let
r:P_{2}\rightarrow P_{1}
is a deformation map such that
Z^{1}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})=\delta_{r}(\mathrm{Nij}(r))
, where
Z^{1}((P_{2})_{r},P_{1})
is the set of all
1
-cocycles in the cochain complex associated with
r
. Then
r
is rigid.

Proof.

Take any formal one-parameter deformation
r_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}r_{i}
of the operator
r
. Then we have seen that
r_{1}
is a
1
-cocycle in the cochain complex associated with
r
. Hence from the hypothesis, we get that
r_{1}=\delta^{r}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(x_{0})
, for some
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Nij}(r)
. We set

\displaystyle\varphi_{1,t}=\mathrm{Id}_{P_{1}}+t\{x_{0},-\}_{1}~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\varphi_{2,t}=\mathrm{Id}_{P_{2}}+t\big(\rho_{x_{0}}+H(x_{0},r-)\big).

Then
r_{t}^{\prime}:=\varphi_{1,t}\circ r_{t}\circ\varphi_{2,t}^{-1}
is a formal one-parameter deformation of
r
and this is equivalent to
r_{t}
. We observe that

\displaystyle r_{t}^{\prime}(u)\quad(\mathrm{mod}~t^{2})=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}_{P_{1}}+t\{x_{0},-\}_{1})(r+tr_{1})(\mathrm{Id}_{P_{2}}-t(\rho_{x_{0}}+H(x_{0},r-)))(u)\quad(\mathrm{mod}~t^{2})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}_{P_{1}}+t\{x_{0},-\}_{1})\big(r(u)+t~\!r_{1}(u)-t~\!r(\rho_{x_{0}}u+H(x_{0},r(u)))\big)\quad(\mathrm{mod}~t^{2})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r(u)+t\big(r_{1}(u)+\{x_{0},r(u)\}_{1}-r(\rho_{x_{0}}u+H(x_{0},r(u)))\big)\quad(\mathrm{mod}~t^{2})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r(u)\qquad(\because~r_{1}=\delta^{r}_{\mathrm{FGV}}(x_{0})).

This shows that the coefficient of
t
in the power series expansion of
r_{t}^{\prime}
vanishes. In the same way, we can show that
r_{t}^{\prime}
is equivalent to some formal one-parameter deformation
r_{t}^{\prime\prime}
whose coefficients in
t
and
t^{2}
both vanish. Applying this process repeatedly, we get that
r_{t}
is equivalent to the undeformed one
r_{t}^{\prime}=r
. This proves the result.
∎

7.7 Remark
.

In the last part of this paper, we developed the cohomologies of various well-known operators on Poisson algebras. In particular, we defined the cohomology of a (relative) Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
. On the other hand, it is well-known
[
3
]
that a (relative) Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on a Poisson algebra induces a pre-Poisson algebra structure. Recall that a pre-Poisson algebra is a vector space endowed with a zinbiel algebra (or a commutative dendriform algebra) structure and a pre-Lie algebra structure satisfying certain compatibility conditions. It would be very interesting to find the cohomology theory of a pre-Poisson algebra (combining the cohomologies of the underlying zinbiel algebra and pre-Lie algebra) that controls the formal one-parameter deformations of the structure. Further, one may look for a homomorphism from the cohomology of a (relative) Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on a Poisson algebra to the cohomology of the induced pre-Poisson algebra.

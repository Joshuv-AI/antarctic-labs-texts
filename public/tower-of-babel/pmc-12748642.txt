Study of the effect of pinning size on the critical current density in a superconducting tape with a Moiré triangular pinning array at magic angles
Authors: Fatemeh Ghanavati, Mehdi Hosseini, Zahra Owjifard, Ali Moftakharzadeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The creation of artificial pinning centers significantly influences the critical current density of type-II superconductors. Consequently, substantial research efforts have been directed toward the design and optimization of pinning structures within superconducting materials. In this study, the influence of pinning centers on the critical current density of superconducting tapes is examined. A triangular and moiré triangular pinning lattice is simulated, and the corresponding Langevin equation is formulated by considering various forces, including vortex-vortex interaction, vortex-pinning interaction, and thermal fluctuation forces. Vortex dynamics is examined through molecular dynamics simulations, which are employed to determine the associated critical current densities. The results demonstrate that increasing the pinning force and the radius of the pinning centers leads to a significant enhancement in the critical current density.

Introduction

In type-II superconductors, magnetic fields penetrate the material in the form of quantized vortices, resulting in a mixed state of magnetic flux lines and superconductivity. The stability of these vortices directly affects the critical current density1–4. A major challenge in the design and practical application of type-II superconductors is suppression of vortex motion, which is driven by the Lorentz force under applied currents2,4. Vortex motion causes energy dissipation and degrades the superconducting performance of the material. Consequently, effective vortex pinning is crucial for enhancing the critical current density4,5.

In recent years, the investigation of nanoscale materials and lattice structures—such as triangular lattices6–21, square lattices6,7,14,22–35, rectangular lattices36–41, Kagome lattices9,13–15,19,42–48, honeycomb lattices9,17,42–44,47, moiré lattices49,50, quasiperiodic lattices51–56, and other structures57,58 has received widespread attention in studies.

Artificial pinning centers are critical for enhancing the critical current density (Jc) in type-II superconductors by immobilizing vortices and suppressing energy dissipation. Early theoretical studies using the nonlinear Ginzburg–Landau (GL) framework demonstrated that periodic arrays of antidots or magnetic inclusions lead to strong commensurability effects, with vortex configurations and critical currents strongly dependent on pinning geometry and lattice symmetry59–63. Recent experimental advances demonstrated controlled vortex motion in nanopatterned superconducting films and nano-bridge structures, validating the influence of pinning geometry, size, and arrangement on vortex stability64–66. A superconducting Pb film patterned with a fivefold Penrose array of Co dots showed stable vortex configurations at specific magnetic fields, observed using scanning Hall probe microscopy. At 1.6 H₁, the vortices formed ringlike “vortex corral” structures that stabilized a giant vortex state carrying two flux quanta. This demonstrates how quasiperiodic symmetry and long-range vortex interactions can generate multiquanta vortices in superconducting films67. Vortex pinning in quasiperiodic (Penrose) lattices leads to a remarkable enhancement of the critical depinning current (Jc) compared with triangular or random arrays. Simulations show multiple and broad matching peaks in Jc arising from the many built-in periods of the Penrose lattice, resulting in strong and extended vortex pinning over a wide magnetic-field range, which is advantageous for improving superconducting performance68.

A moiré array arises from the superposition of two similar crystalline lattices, with one rotated at a specific angle \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta$$\end{document} relative to the other. Constructing a pinning array based on a moiré pattern enables precise control over the spatial distribution of pinning centers, thereby enhancing the performance of superconductors in various applications. The implementation of moiré-patterned pinning arrays can significantly influence the critical current density in type-II superconductors. By carefully adjusting moiré pattern parameters—such as the rotation angle and lattice periodicity—an optimized pinning center can be achieved, resulting in more efficient vortex immobilization and increased critical current density. Previous investigations have shown that the critical current density is significantly enhanced at specific orientations, known as magic angles49,50.

Moiré lattices have attracted considerable interest in solid-state physics and materials science due to their distinctive electronic properties69–71. In the present study, a triangular moiré lattice is examined to evaluate the effects of pinning force and pinning radius on the critical current density at two rotation angles: 0° and 22.5°. These effects are analyzed using molecular dynamics simulations. The angle of 22.5° is selected based on its promising performance reported in prior studies49,50. The angle of 0 corresponds to triangular lattice.

Simulation details

A triangular moiré lattice is a pattern resulting from the superposition of two identical triangular lattices, where one is rotated relative to the other by a specified angle. Upon applying periodic boundary conditions, the magnetic field penetrates the type-II superconductor in the form of vortices. In this simulation, a triangular moiré lattice of size 10λ × 10λ is employed. The critical current density is evaluated as a function of varying pinning forces and pinning radii for both triangular and moiré lattices at rotation angles of 0° and 22.5°. For each angle, the pinning force fp is assigned values of 0.5, 1, and 2, while the pinning radius rp is set to 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.5.

As mentioned earlier, specific rotation angles in the moiré pattern—referred to as magic angles—are associated with enhanced critical current density50. Prior studies have identified angles such as 22.5°, 8°, 37.5°, and 53° as yielding peak critical current densities49.

The critical current density is evaluated using molecular dynamics simulations by tracking the motion of vortices distributed throughout the sample. The value of Jc ​ depends on the sample. In the liquid nitrogen temperature range, the Jc​ value for ceramic YBCO samples is typically in the range of 103 to 104 A/cm2 and for YBCO thin films or single-crystal samples with optimized flux pinning centers, the Jc value can exceed 106 A/cm². In a study on rectangular YBCO samples with different cross-sectional areas, the Jc value increased with decreasing cross section, and the highest value was observed in samples with smaller cross-sectional areas1–77.

In this study, the superconductor is subjected to a variable magnetic field, and the number of vortices is varied from 0 to 200. The Langevin equations for all vortices are integrated simultaneously using a molecular dynamics approach, ensuring that the interactions are treated in a self-consistent manner at each time step. The simulations begin with vortices uniformly distributed across the domain, and periodic boundary conditions are applied in both directions. The vortex dynamics are integrated using the velocity-Verlet algorithm to ensure numerical stability under nonlinear interactions. The simulations continue until a steady-state is reached, where the vortex velocities and the normalized critical current density converge within a 1% tolerance.

Results and discussion

In this simulation, the behavior of vortices in a triangular moiré lattice is examined for magic rotation angles of 8°, 22.5°, 37.5°, and 53°. As the critical current density exhibits similar trends with respect to pinning size and pinning force across all these magic angles, only the results for the 22.5° configuration are reported. For this case, the pinning force is set to fp=0.5,1,2, and for each force value, the pinning radius is assigned rp=0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5. Figure 2 presents the critical current density as a function of the number of vortices for the triangular lattice. The plots are shown for pinning radii rp=0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5, with each column corresponding to a different pinning force: fp=0.5,1,2.

The plots in Fig. 2 reveal two characteristic behaviors: an initial sharp decrease in critical current density followed by a more gradual decline. For instance, the plot in the first column (a) and first row (a) of Fig. 2 shows a significant drop in the critical current density. As the pinning force and pinning radius increase, this decline becomes less pronounced. At higher pinning force values (fp = 1 and fp = 2), the critical current density becomes nearly constant for magnetic fields above 100, which is of substantial practical importance.

The plots in Fig. 2 further demonstrate that, for a given pinning force, the critical current density increases with increasing pinning radius. As the radius of the pinning centers grows, the probability of vortex trapping rises, thereby enhancing the critical current density. Moreover, the results show that, for a fixed pinning radius, increasing the pinning force also leads to a higher critical current density. A stronger pinning force improves the effectiveness of vortex immobilization, thereby enhancing the superconductor’s overall current-carrying capacity.

Figure 3 presents the critical current density as a function of the number of vortices at a rotation angle of 22.5°, for pinning radii rp=0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5. Each column corresponds to a different pinning force: fp=0.5,1,2. The variation of critical current density as a function of the number of vortices follows a trend similar to that observed in the triangular lattice. The plots in the first column (a) of Fig. 3 indicate that, at a constant pinning force of fp=0.5, the critical current density increases with increasing pinning radius. This increasing trend is also observed in columns (b) and (c) for fixed pinning forces of fp=1 and fp=2, respectively. As a result, both higher pinning force and larger pinning radius lead to an increase in the critical current density. Figure 4 presents the averaged critical current density as a function of pinning radius for the triangular lattice under low (corresponding to vortex numbers 40–60), intermediate (90–110), and high (180–200) magnetic field conditions, at various values of pinning force. The results indicate that the critical current density increases with increasing pinning radius. At high magnetic fields (Fig. 4a), this increase is approximately linear. In contrast, at lower magnetic fields, the increase in critical current density follows a parabolic trend with negative curvature. In fact, under lower magnetic fields, the variation in critical current density approaches saturation as both the pinning force and pinning radius increase.

By comparing the critical current density in the triangular and moiré lattices under intermediate and low magnetic field conditions, it is observed that the rate of increase in critical current density is higher for the moiré lattice compared to the triangular lattice.

Conclusion

In this study, the effects of varying pinning forces and pinning center radii were investigated for triangular and triangular moiré pinning arrays at a rotation angle of 22.5°. This specific angle was chosen because it corresponds to a maximum in critical current density as a function of rotation angle. The results show that increasing both the pinning force and the pinning radius leads to an enhancement in critical current density in both triangular and moiré pinning structures. Furthermore, the increase in critical current density is more pronounced in the moiré lattice at this specific angle compared to the triangular lattice.

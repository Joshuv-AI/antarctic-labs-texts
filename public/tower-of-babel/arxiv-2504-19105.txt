Blended PC Peer Review Model: Process and Reflection
Chakkrit Tantithamthavorn, Nicole Novielli, Ayushi Rastogi, Olga Baysal, Bram Adams

Abstract
. The academic peer review system is under increasing pressure due to a growing volume of submissions and a limited pool of available reviewers, resulting in delayed decisions and an uneven distribution of reviewing responsibilities. To address this challenge, the International Conference on Mining Software Repositories (MSR) 2025 introduced a Blended Program Committee (PC) peer review model for its Technical Track. Building upon the community’s earlier experience with the Shadow PC (2021 and 2022) and Junior PC (2023 and 2024), the new model pairs up one Junior PC member with two regular PC members as part of the core review team of a given paper, instead of adding them as an extra reviewer. This paper presents the rationale, implementation, and reflections on the model, including insights from a post-review author survey evaluating the quality and usefulness of reviews. Our findings highlight the potential of Junior PCs to alleviate reviewer shortages, foster inclusivity, and sustain a high-quality peer review process. We offer lessons learned and recommendations to guide future adoption and refinement of the model.



1.
Introduction

The academic peer review system is facing increasing strain due to a shortage of qualified reviewers and the resource-intensive nature of the process
(
Petrescu and Krishen 2022
)
.
As the volume of paper submissions continues to rise, conferences including Mining Software Repositories (MSR) and other software engineering conferences struggle to secure timely and high-quality reviews
(
Bacchelli and Beller 2017
)
.
Many experienced researchers decline review invitations due to overwhelming workloads, resulting in delays and an uneven distribution of the reviewing burden, while early career researchers often struggle to receive invitations despite their willingness and capability to contribute.
This shortage not only compromises the efficiency of the academic peer review system but also raises concerns about the diversity, equity, and inclusivity, and long-term sustainability of the peer review ecosystem.

This paper presents the Blended PC peer review model implemented at the International Conference on Mining Software Repositories (MSR) 2025, Ottawa, Canada. The goal of this report is to explain the novel review model proposed this year by building upon previous experience of the MSR community with the Shadow PC initiative
(
Thongtanunam et al. 2021
)
, started in 2021 and then continued with the Junior PC initiative in 2023 and 2024
(
Tantithamthavorn and Baysal 2023
)
. We offer the history of the Shadow PC and Junior PC initiatives in Section 2.
This year we introduced a new model for the Technical Track, which includes two regular PC members and one junior PC member, as explained in Section 3.
To assess the perceived quality of the reviews and the effectiveness of the proposed model, we conducted a qualitative survey, whose results are presented in Section 4.
Finally, Section 5 discusses our reflections, while Section 6 draws our conclusions.

The ultimate goal of Junior PCs is to train the next generation of junior program committee members while implementing structured mentorship programs to build a more diverse, inclusive, and sustainable peer review ecosystem.
The Junior PC represents a significant evolution of the Shadow PC initiative. Different from the Shadow PC initiative, in which participants were involved in a parallel review process for training purposes without a direct influence on the outcome of the main track reviewing, the Junior PCs participate in the actual review process and contribute to the final decision of the papers submitted, thus improving the motivation and engagement of the junior PCs throughout the process. This transition reflects the MSR community’s growing confidence in this participatory mentorship model: the Shadow PC initiative demonstrated consistent effectiveness in training reviewers, and it has eventually evolved into the Junior PC model, which now enables junior community members to actively collaborate with seniors in the review process that eventually shapes the conference program.

The primary audience for the Junior PC is early-career researchers (PhD students, postdocs, new faculty members, and industry practitioners) who are keen to get more involved in the academic peer-review process but have not yet served on a technical research track program committee at respected international software engineering conferences (e.g., ICSE, ESEC/FSE, ASE, MSR, ICSME, SANER).
Serving on the Junior PC is an excellent opportunity for early-career researchers (PhD students, postdocs, new faculty members, industry practitioners, etc.) to be recognized and gain experience in community service, i.e., being part of a major conference program committee.
It is also worthwhile for several reasons, serving three main goals: (a) learning purposes, (b) raising awareness on the conference processes, and (c) providing opportunities for personal growth as a researcher actively contributing to the community. To this aim, the program provides Junior PCs with the opportunity to:

•

See examples of actual reviews for the same papers, written by the regular PC members, gaining more experience as a reviewer and learning from the senior researchers how to write better reviews.

•

Participate in the real review process and contribute to the final outcome of the paper, thus improving the motivation and engagement of the junior reviewers throughout the process.

•

Get to know how a PC is run and how it operates.

•

Receive mentorship from experienced PC members.

•

Gain experience in reviewing papers and in understanding the challenges faced by reviewers reading multiple papers, which may not always be in their area of expertise.

•

Submitting high-quality reviews makes a junior reviewer a more likely candidate for future PCs of the technical track of MSR or otherwise.

•

See both strong and weak papers at the submission stage.

•

Discover what it takes to publish a paper in a reputable conference, such as MSR.

•

Have a chance to read top-notch papers in your area of expertise before they are published.

3.
New Blended PC Review Model

Motivation.
Different from the MSR Junior PC review processes in 2023 and 2024, which involved three regular PCs and two junior PCs, MSR 2025 has shifted to a blended PC model of two regular PCs and one junior PC. This brings the core review team of a given paper back to three, in line with the MSR review process before 2021 and with other conferences. The changes were driven by the following reasons:

•

Reducing Review Workload.
The previous model, requiring five PCs per paper, placed a substantial review burden on both junior and regular PCs, potentially compromising the quality of the reviews due to the tight 1-month review schedule of MSR. By reducing the number of PCs from 3+2 to 2+1, the review workload for each PC will be lowered, thereby expecting to enhance the quality of the reviews.

•

Improving Authors’ Responses.
MSR provides an author response period to allow authors to clarify their submissions. Previously, with five PCs per paper, authors struggled to produce high-quality responses within the 750-word limit. We expected that the reduction to three PCs (two regular, one junior) would likely help authors craft more effective and manageable responses within the word limit.

We anticipate the change towards 2+1 to provide substantial benefits to authors, Junior PCs, and Regular PCs.
Nevertheless, the Junior PC co-chairs’ role is to ensure that the quality of the feedback is not compromised.
Junior PC members receive training from senior researchers and/or past MSR distinguished reviewers on how to write and provide constructive reviews for software engineering papers, what to look for, what to avoid, and the suggested review structure aligned with the MSR review criteria.
They also receive guidance from the MSR (Junior) PC co-chairs on the expected review quality, confidentiality, and ethics standards, how to write good reviews, and how to participate in discussions (see ACM reviewers’ responsibilities).
Finally, Junior PC members receive feedback on how to improve their reviews before the rebuttal notification (from the discussion lead, who also acts as a mentor).
The discussion lead will perform quality assurance, ensuring the reviews are of high quality and remain constructive.

Review Process for MSR 2025.
As all submissions to the MSR research track are reviewed jointly by both regular and junior PC members, as part of the same process, Junior PCs fully engage in the review process, including reviewing author responses, participating in PC discussions, and reaching a decision on the paper.
Most of the papers (110 out of 157 papers, 70%) received two reviews from two regular PC members and one review from a Junior PC member, with the remaining papers having three regular PC members. This represents a significant evolution in the role of Junior PCs, with their evaluations contributing substantially more to the decision-making process compared to previous models.
The final decision is made by consensus among all reviewers, as always.
Based on our experience with the MSR Shadow PC in 2021 and 2022 and MSR Junior PC in 2023 and 2024, our expectation was that the addition of a junior PC to each paper would increase the overall quality of reviews that the authors receive, since junior reviewers typically have a deep understanding of recent topics, and can thus provide deep technical feedback on the subject.

The Responsibility of Junior PCs.

Junior PC members are requested to commit themselves to writing their own detailed and rigorous reviews for papers assigned to them by the allotted deadline. This timely review commitment is essential to the good functioning of the Junior PC. Candidates who might be unable to fulfil their reviewing duties should refrain from applying. This year, Junior PC members were expected to review one paper per member, submit on-time reviews, and actively participate in the online discussions of their assigned papers. Junior PC members must follow the ethical standards of peer review, respect the anonymity of the review process, not share which papers they have reviewed or solicit sub-reviews, and must not use Generative AI (new for 2025).

The Eligibility of Junior PCs.
The Junior PC is open to PhD students, post-docs, new faculty members, and industry practitioners experienced in software engineering or mining software repositories research, especially those who have not yet served in a program committee of the technical research track (or the main track) of the international SE conferences (e.g., ICSE, FSE, ASE, MSR, ICSME, SANER). We selected the Junior PC members based on their research experience. The Junior PC selection process also strives to ensure diversity and inclusion.

Junior PCs Recruitment.
We used self-nomination as a recruitment process for Junior PC. We ran a one-month recruitment campaign (October 18th, 2024 – November 20th, 2024, AoE) through various social media channels, e.g., SEWORLD, X (formerly Twitter), and Facebook.
Then, the formal invitation email was sent out on December 1st, 2024.

We were overwhelmed with the positive reaction from the MSR community, with a total of 248 (self)-nominations for the Junior PC 2025.
This is 26% higher than MSR 2023’s 197 Junior PC applications, 123% higher than MSR 2022’s 111 Shadow PC applications, and 53% higher than MSR 2021’s 162 Shadow PC applications.
Unfortunately, we did not have the capacity to accept everyone.

After carefully reviewing each nomination, we selected 110 applicants for the MSR 2025 Junior PC (an acceptance rate of 44%), giving priority to applicants in the later stages of their PhD program and ensuring that we put together a diverse Junior PC that reflects the geographic, demographic, and research area diversity of the MSR community. 4.
Research Methodology

In this section, we present the design and the results of our survey.

4.1.
Research Questions and Survey Design

With the newly introduced Blended PC model of 2 Regular + 1 Junior program committees, it is important to investigate the authors’ perceptions of the preferred review models and their review quality.
To address this gap, we conducted a qualitative study to answer the following two research questions:

•

RQ1: Which review model is preferred the most by the authors?
and

•

RQ2: Is there any difference between the review quality of Junior and Regular PCs?

To answer the research questions, we created and distributed anonymous, voluntary surveys to both authors of papers submitted to the MSR Technical Track.
The survey is designed as a cross-sectional study where participants provided their responses at one fixed point in time.
We encouraged one author per paper to complete the survey.
The survey enabled us to collect feedback on the perceived review quality, on the opinions regarding which review model best fits the MSR Technical Track, and the ability to distinguish between reviews provided by junior and regular PC members.
The survey consists of three closed-ended questions and three open-ended questions.

For closed-ended questions, we use multiple-choice questions and a Likert scale from 1 to 5.

•

(Q1) Which of the following two review models would you prefer for the MSR Technical Track?

•

(Q2) How would you rate the (overall) review quality (for Reviewer A/B/C)?

•

(Q3) Could you guess the junior PC on your paper?

Each of these questions is followed by an open-ended question for the rationale, allowing the participants to leave additional free text comments to elaborate more on their answers and to provide further feedback to inform future decisions.
We use Google Forms to conduct our survey in an online setting.
The survey takes approximately 5 minutes to complete and is completely anonymous.
To verify the completeness of the response in our survey (i.e., whether all questions were appropriately answered), we manually review all of the open-ended questions.

Overall, we obtained 75 responses out of 426 unique authors, which represent 18% of the unique authors of papers submitted to the MSR Technical Track. Since we encouraged one response per paper, our insights represent perspectives on 73 out of the 161 papers (i.e., 45% of the papers) submitted to the MSR 2025 Technical Track. Of these, 45 papers were rejected and 20 accepted.
In the following, we present the survey responses to answer the research questions.

4.2.
RQ1: Which review model is preferred the most by the authors?

When requested which review model best fits the MSR technical track review process, 48% of authors suggest adopting the 2+1 model, i.e., two regular PC members and one junior member as reviewers for each paper (see Figure
1
).
Furthermore, 32% expressed no preference while 20% declared they would like to see reintroduced the model adopted in MSR 2023 and 2024, which is three regular and two junior PC members as reviewers for each paper.

Respondents indicating a preference for the models adopted for MSR 2025 including two regular and one junior PC members, generally support having three reviewers per paper, as this creates an odd number that helps avoid deadlocks in decision-making and provides sufficient coverage without overwhelming authors with too many opinions to reconcile. Regarding junior reviewers, the responses showed mixed opinions and presented different perspectives. Several respondents strongly favor including junior reviewers, as this was seen as a valuable professional training practice. One respondent specifically mentioned the benefit of valuing the junior PC members’ opinions, with the possibility of them playing a decisive role in case of disagreements between the seniors.

One respondent reported a moderate expression of concern that reviewers might feel pressure to be overly critical to establish their credibility, potentially leading to harsh rejections. However, the model, including only one junior reviewer, is seen as capable of mitigating any risks deriving from less experienced reviewers taking part in the process, with a balanced approach ensuring the inclusion of diverse perspectives while maintaining an efficient review process.

Figure 1.
Count of preferences regarding the review models to adopt for the MSR Technical Track.

As for the supporters of the five-review model, some of the open-ended responses highlight the trade-off between maintaining the usual high MSR review quality standards and including junior community members in what is perceived as a valuable training program. Some of the respondents suggest increasing the number of reviewers to reduce randomness in review outcomes and provide more balanced input.

Finally, people expressing no preference between the two models also provided some suggestions for alternative models. One of the respondents suggests a new possible option comprising three regular and one junior PC members. While increasing the overall review load, such a model may mitigate threats due to the junior not having enough influence in the discussion, thus following the more experienced reviewers, who would end up dominating the discussion.

4.3.
RQ2: Is there a difference between the review quality of Junior and Regular PCs?

Authors were requested to provide an overall evaluation of the quality of each of the three reviews received for their papers. Authors were not aware of which review, if any, was submitted by the Junior PC member.
In Figure
2
, we report a bar chart illustrating both the absolute numbers and the proportional differences between the two review categories. It shows that the authors were generally satisfied with the quality of reviews provided by both regular and junior PC, with the majority of reviews being evaluated as either “Good" (in light blue) or “Award-worthy" (in dark blue) quality, thus confirming that the overall perceived quality of the reviews is comparable to the high standard expectations of the authors submitting to the MSR Technical Track.

Figure 2.
Authors’ evaluation of the overall review quality.

In particular, 9 regular reviews were rated as “Award-worthy" (14%), while 29 were rated as “Good" (45%). As for junior reviews, we observe a slightly better distribution with 9 reviews rated as “Award-worthy" (21%) and 21 rated as “Good" (49%).
Interestingly, while junior reviews have fewer total ratings (43 junior reviews were evaluated, compared to 65 for regular reviews), they show a higher percentage of top-quality ratings, with overall 70% of respondents rating their reviews as either “Award-worthy" or “Good", with only 12% rating them as “Poor."

This finding suggests that junior reviews’ overall quality and commitment hold potential for equaling the highest quality standard, despite them being involved for the first time in the MSR review process.
This finding becomes even more significant considering our committee composition — 110 junior PC members alongside 148 regular PC members. The high-performance metrics from Junior PC members, despite their numerical minority, suggest that their contribution was crucial and an enrichment in the review process.

This finding is further corroborated by the fact that the reviews provided by junior PC members appear not to be identifiable. In fact, when requested to guess which one was the review provided by the junior PC member, only 16% of responders could correctly guess the correct association. Furthermore, we did not observe any substantial differences in the average scores provided by the two groups, with regular and junior PC members providing, on average, an overall merit score of 2.26 and 2.18, respectively.

5.
Reflections

This section presents our reflections on the review process and outcomes from the perspectives of the Technical Track PC co-chairs and Junior PC co-chairs. We observed the process from various perspectives and, based on this, we provide suggestions and recommendations for future editions of the MSR conference and other conferences facing similar challenges, as well as exploring junior PC as a potential solution.

Since the adoption of the Junior/Shadow PC program, we have integrated several early career researchers into the Technical Track PC. In the current edition, we invited distinguished, eligible Junior PC members from the 2024 edition to join the Technical Track PC. This ensures that we are contributing to the long-term sustainability of the scientific community.

The relevance of the Junior PC program is also reflected in the lower review loads overall. While junior PC members reviewed one paper on a subject of their expertise, the Technical Track PC reviewed between two and four papers. In comparison to other similar conferences, this process is relatively lightweight.

One space where we felt junior PC (and their training) can improve is in terms of online paper discussion. The two technical track PC co-chairs followed each paper for review quality before rebuttal and the discussion thereafter. In order to reach a fair acceptance decision, the online discussion between reviewers is just as important as writing a high-quality review. While the authors’ response suggests that the quality of reviews from Junior PC was of comparable quality to the technical track PC, the technical track PC chairs felt that some junior PC reviewers could have voiced their opinions during the online discussion more strongly than they did.

This was a problem in three papers where the other two reviewers had divergent opinions, and the junior PC did not express their views due to a lack of confidence in taking sides. Eventually, the situation was resolved by adding a fourth reviewer (from the team of rapid response reviewers). However, for future editions, our advice is to also train junior PC on how to participate in discussions and decision-making, reducing their fear of intervening. Notably, in all three cases, eventually the junior PC served as shepherds, as they were also experts on the topics and methodologies relevant to the articles.

We were also limited by the HotCRP review platform in realizing our vision for this year’s blended PC review model. Our vision with the blended PC (2+1) model was to have no distinction between junior PC and technical PC. Unfortunately, HotCRP did not fully support it. For example, while we wanted fellow reviewers not to know who is a junior PC, hiding the ‘junior PC’ tag created for management purposes was not possible. Related to this, we could not assign the ‘artifact check’ role to the reviewers with the ‘junior PC’ tag, although we initially planned on distributing the ‘artifact check’ role equally among all PC members.

Another potential “wishlist” feature is the ability to survey authors about review quality from within the review platform. This year, we solicited author feedback on review quality outside the platform. And for those worried about soliciting negative author feedback, we would like to point out that in our survey, the majority of respondents were authors of the rejected papers (45 authors of rejected papers and 20 authors of accepted papers). Yet, many of them considered reviews to be of “Award-worthy" and “Good" quality.

Our final recommendation relates to management. We advise that no Junior PC co-chair should be allowed to submit a paper due to having full access to the review process on the platform. In the previous model, only Technical Track PC co-chairs were not allowed to submit papers to the track. In the current model, given that junior PC chairs have as much access to the data as the Technical Track PC co-chairs, we suggest a similar rule (of having no paper submissions) for the Junior PC co-chairs.

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we present the blended PC peer review model implemented at the International Conference on Mining Software Repositories (MSR) 2025, Ottawa, Canada.
We introduced and tested out a new blended PC model (2+1) for the Technical Track, which includes two regular and one junior PC member, which is different from the previous editions’ 3+2 model (i.e., three regular and two junior PC members).
To investigate the authors’ perception and their review quality, we conducted a survey to answer two research questions: (1) Which review models are most preferred by the authors? and (2) Is there a difference between the review quality of Junior and Regular PCs?
Based on the 75 responses representing a 17.6% response rate, we found that (1) 48% of authors preferred adopting the new blended PC (2+1) model; (2) the authors were generally satisfied with the quality of reviews provided by both regular and junior PC; and (3) only 16% of responders could correctly guess the correct association.
The high satisfaction with review quality from both junior and regular PC members, and the low rate of correct identification suggests that junior reviewers delivered reviews indistinguishable in quality from their senior counterparts—supporting the value of involving early-career researchers in the peer review process.

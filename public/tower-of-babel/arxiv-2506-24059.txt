A scalar-mean curvature comparison theorem for manifolds with iterated conical singularities
Milan Jovanovic, Jinmin Wang

Abstract
. We use the Dirac operator method to prove a scalar-mean curvature comparison theorem for spin manifolds which carry iterated conical singularities. Our approach is to study the index theory of a twisted Dirac operator on such singular manifolds. A dichotomy argument is used to prove the comparison theorem without knowing precisely the index of the twisted Dirac operator. This framework also enables us to prove a rigidity theorem of Euclidean domains and a spin positive mass theorem for asymptotically flat manifolds with iterated conical singularities.



1.
Introduction

The study of comparison theorems in differential geometry, particularly those involving scalar curvature and mean curvature, has seen significant developments in recent decades.
In
[
17
]
, Gromov proposed studying comparison theorems of scalar curvature alongside its companion, mean curvature. Lott, in
[
24
]
, established a scalar-mean comparison theorem for compact spin manifolds with boundary via Dirac operators, showing that for a model manifold with nonnegative curvature operator, nonnegative second fundamental form, and nonzero Euler characteristic, it is impossible to simultaneously increase its scalar curvature, mean curvature, and metric tensor. This result generalizes the scalar curvature comparison theorems of Llarull
[
23
]
and Goette–Semmelmann
[
16
]
for closed manifolds. This Dirac operator method was also employed by Cecchini–Zeidler
[
10
]
to show a scalar–mean comparison theorem for Riemannian bands. Meanwhile, using the method of
\mu
-bubbles, Wang, Zhu, and the second author
[
27
]
derived a Listing-type scalar-mean comparison result in low dimensions without assuming the spin condition.

The investigation of scalar-mean comparison theorems naturally extends to singular manifolds.
Gromov in
[
17
]
posed the question whether Llarull’s theorem still holds for spheres with subsets removed. For the case of antipodal points, this was answered in the affirmative, proven independently by
[
2
]
and
[
31
]
. If higher-dimensional subsets of the sphere are removed, then further regularity conditions are required
[
8
,
30
,
13
,
9
,
34
]
.
Meanwhile, comparison theorems for polyhedra have been explored
[
17
,
32
,
6
,
29
]
. Notably, He–Shi–Yu in
[
18
]
establish a minimal-surface approach for singular spaces.

In this paper, we are interested in a generalization of the comparison theorems where the model manifold itself carries
non-isolated
asymptotically conical singularities.
This is motivated by Cheeger’s foundational work
[
11
]
. Briefly speaking, a manifold
(M,g)
carries asymptotically conical singularities if its metric near singularities locally takes the form

g=g_{B}+dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{F}(b)+\textup{higher order terms}

where
g_{B}
is a smooth metric pulled back from the base,
r
is the defining function that measures the distance from the singularity, and
g_{F}(b)
is a metric on the link fiber varying with the base, which may itself contain lower-dimensional conical singularities. The reader may refer to Sections
2
and
5
for the precise definition. For manifolds with boundary, we assume that the singularities lie in the interior. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\hat{M}^{n},\partial\hat{M}^{n-1},\hat{g})
and
(M^{n},\partial M^{n-1},g)
be compact spin manifolds with iterated asymptotically conical singularities (IACS) and smooth boundaries

,
\partial M
.
Assume that the singular points occur in codimension at least three.
Suppose that the curvature operator of
M
and the second fundamental form on the boundary of

are nonnegative.
Let

be an asymptotically canonical map, with

denoting its restriction to the boundary.
Assume that one of the following conditions holds:

The boundaries of

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we analyze a twisted Dirac operator
D
on
\hat{M}
with a specific boundary condition.
The study of

presents two major challenges.

(a)

Fredholmness
: showing that
D
is Fredholm under the given boundary condition;

(b)

Index computation
: determining the Fredholm index of

.

For spin Dirac operators or signature operators on manifolds with conical singularities, the essential self-adjointness and Fredholmness follow from a Witt-type condition
[
12
,
7
,
21
,
1
,
4
]
, which requires that the associated link operators admit a spectral gap at least
1/2
.
We demonstrate that this criterion extends to the twisted Dirac operator, assuming the scalar curvature comparison and the nonnegativity of the curvature operator on the target manifold.
Strictly speaking, one must assume that the conical singularities occur in codimension at least three. Indeed, the theorem fails for manifolds with codimension-two singularities (see Remark
2.4
).

For manifolds with conical singularities, the index formula of Dirac operators typically involves the eta invariants arising from the singularities
[
1
, Main Theorem]
, which are by nature spectral and nonlocal.
In this work, we give a dichotomy argument to establish the comparison theorems
without
having to know the precise index of the twisted Dirac operator. This approach is inspired by Witten’s proof of the spin positive mass theorem
[
33
]
. As an application, we establish a scalar-mean rigidity theorem for Euclidean domains.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},\partial M^{n-1},g)
be a compact spin manifold with IACS.
Let

be a smooth map with nonzero degree, where

is a closed convex hypersurface in the Euclidean space

Assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

is an isometry.

(ii)

Let

Moreover, we extend this idea to prove a positive mass theorem for asymptotically flat (AF for short) spin manifolds with iterated asymptotically conical singularities. The famous positive mass theorem states that an asymptotically flat smooth manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature must have nonnegative ADM mass. This result was first proved by Schoen–Yau using minimal surfaces
[
25
]
. Witten also proved the spin positive mass theorem for all dimensions
[
33
]
. It is a natural problem to generalize the positive mass theorem to singular Riemannian manifolds. However, the Schwarzchild metric, which admits a point with a horn singularity, is scalar-flat but has negative mass. In
[
22
]
, Li–Mantoulidis proved a positive mass theorem for AF manifolds with
L^{\infty}
-metric and fiberwise conical singularities. Dai–Sun–Wang also proved a positive mass theorem for singular spaces with isolated conical singularities
[
15
,
14
]
.

In this paper, we extend the idea of our main result and prove a positive mass theorem for manifolds with iterated conical singularities.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an n-dimensional asymptotically flat spin manifold with iterated asymptotically conical singularities.
Suppose that the links of
M
have dimension at least two.
If
\mathrm{Sc}_{g}\geq 0
, then

.
Moreover, if

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section

Section

Section

and

.
In Section

will follow from the same proofs given in Sections

and
4

Lastly, in Section

2.
Manifolds with fiberwise asymptotically conical singularities

In this section, we make precise the notion of a manifold with fiberwise asymptotically conical singularities (FACS)
and the notion of an asymptotically conical map between manifolds with FACS.
After that, we review some basic facts regarding the geometry of the cone of a Riemannian manifold.
Then, the geometry of an asymptotic family of cones over a base is compared to the geometry of just a regular family of cones over the same base.
In particular, the asymptotic behavior of the error between the scalar curvatures is computed in Proposition
2.8
.
We then present an explicit relationship between the twisted Dirac operator on a cone and the twisted Dirac operator on its link.
Finally, some standard Listing-type estimates are established.

.

The scalar curvature of
(CL^{n},g_{CL})
is given by

\mathrm{Sc}_{CL}=\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{L}-(n-1)(n-2)}{r^{2}}.

2.3.
Geometry of a family of asymptotic cones

In this section, we study the geometry of a family of cones parametrized by a base space, having asymptotically conical metrics. The setup is as follows: There exists a Riemannian manifold
(B^{h},g_{B})
, representing the base, and a manifold
L^{l}
, representing the link.
The space in question is the direct product
M^{n}=B^{h}\times(0,\infty)\times L^{l}
.
Let
g_{L}(b)
be a family of metrics on the link varying smoothly over the base.
Defining
\mathbb{L}\coloneqq B\times L
with
g_{\mathbb{L}}=\pi^{*}g_{B}\oplus g_{L}(b)
yields a Riemannian link bundle over
B
.
We write
C\mathbb{L}
to denote the fiberwise coning of
\mathbb{L}
.
Namely,
C\mathbb{L}=B^{h}\times(0,\infty)\times L^{l}
with metric

g_{\mathbb{L}}=\pi^{*}g_{B}\oplus dr^{2}\oplus r^{2}g_{L}(b).

The metric on
M
is then assumed to differ from the metric on
C\mathbb{L}
by
o_{2}(r^{2})
error terms.
Thus, it is of the form

g_{M}=dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{\mathbb{L}}+o_{2}(r^{2}).

The next proposition relates the scalar curvature of
M
to the scalar curvature of
C\mathbb{L}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

\mathrm{Sc}_{M}=\mathrm{Sc}_{C\mathbb{L}}+o(1/r^{2})

Proof.

Let
g
denote the metric on
C\mathbb{L}
and
h
the metric on
M
.
Take an orthonormal frame
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{h},\partial_{r},\frac{e_{1}}{r},\ldots,\frac{e_{l}}{r}\}=\{\{b_{i}\},\partial_{r},\{e_{i}\}\}
with respect to
g
.
Here, the
\{e_{i}\}
are taken to be in
TL
and the
\{b_{i}\}
are lifted from the base.

By assumption

\displaystyle\langle b_{i},b_{j}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}+o_{2}(r^{2})

\displaystyle\langle b_{i},\partial_{r}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=o_{2}(r^{2})

\displaystyle\langle b_{i},\frac{e_{i}}{r}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=o_{2}(r)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{r},b_{j}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=o_{2}(r^{2})

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{r},\partial_{r}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=1+o_{2}(r^{2})

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{r},\frac{e_{i}}{r}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=o_{2}(r)

\displaystyle\langle\frac{e_{i}}{r},b_{j}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=o_{2}(r)

\displaystyle\langle\frac{e_{i}}{r},\partial_{r}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=o_{2}(r)

\displaystyle\langle\frac{e_{i}}{r},\frac{e_{j}}{r}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}+o_{2}(1)

Applying Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization to
\{\{b_{i}\},\partial_{r},\{e_{i}\}\}
with respect to the metric
h
, we obtain an orthonormal frame
\{\{\hat{b}_{i}\},\hat{\partial}_{r},\{\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\}\}
.
Following the Gram-Schmidt algorithm, it is not hard to see that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{i}

\displaystyle=b_{i}+o_{2}(r^{2})\{b_{j}\}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\partial_{r}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}+o_{2}(r^{2})\partial_{r}+o_{2}(r^{2})\{b_{j}\}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{i}}{r}+o_{2}(1)\{\frac{e_{j}}{r}\}+o_{2}(r)\partial_{r}+o_{2}(r)\{b_{j}\}

The converse holds too, namely

(2.6)

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle=\hat{b}_{i}+o_{2}(r^{2})\{\hat{b}_{j}\}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{r}

\displaystyle=\hat{\partial}_{r}+o_{2}(r^{2})\hat{\partial}_{r}+o_{2}(r^{2})\{\hat{b}_{j}\}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{e_{i}}{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}+o_{2}(1)\{\frac{\hat{e}_{j}}{r}\}+o_{2}(r)\hat{\partial}_{r}+o_{2}(r)\{\hat{b}_{j}\}

Let
\{v_{i}\}
label the elements of
\{\{b_{i}\},\partial_{r},\{e_{i}\}\}
and
\{\hat{v}_{i}\}
label the elements of
\{\{\hat{b}_{i}\},\hat{\partial}_{r},\{\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\}\}
.
Let
\omega_{ij}^{k}
denote
\langle\nabla^{g}_{v_{i}}v_{j},v_{k}\rangle_{g}
and
\hat{\omega}_{ij}^{k}
denote
\langle\nabla^{h}_{\hat{v}_{i}}\hat{v}_{j},\hat{v}_{k}\rangle_{h}
.
Define
c_{ij}^{k}=\langle[v_{i},v_{j}],v_{k}\rangle_{g}
and
\hat{c}_{ij}^{k}=\langle[\hat{v}_{i},\hat{v}_{j}],\hat{v}_{k}\rangle_{h}
.
Applying (
2.3
)-(
2.5
) and (
2.6
)-(
2.8
) we see that, at worst,

(2.9)

\hat{c}_{ij}^{k}=c_{ij}^{k}+o_{1}(1/r)

From the Koszul formula

\displaystyle\omega_{ij}^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(c_{ij}^{k}-c_{ik}^{j}-c_{jk}^{i})

\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{ij}^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{c}_{ij}^{k}-\hat{c}_{ik}^{j}-\hat{c}_{jk}^{i})

and hence, at worst

(2.10)

\hat{\omega}_{ij}^{k}=\omega_{ij}^{k}+o_{1}(1/r)

The scalar curvature formula, written in terms of indices associated to a nonholonomic orthonormal frame, is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sc}_{C\mathbb{L}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\left[v_{i}(\omega_{jj}^{i})-v_{j}(\omega_{ij}^{i})+\omega_{jj}^{k}\omega_{ik}^{i}-\omega_{ij}^{k}\omega_{jk}^{i}-c_{ij}^{k}\omega_{kj}^{i}\right]

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sc}_{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\left[\hat{v}_{i}(\hat{\omega}_{jj}^{i})-\hat{v}_{j}(\hat{\omega}_{ij}^{i})+\hat{\omega}_{jj}^{k}\hat{\omega}_{ik}^{i}-\hat{\omega}_{ij}^{k}\hat{\omega}_{jk}^{i}-\hat{c}_{ij}^{k}\hat{\omega}_{kj}^{i}\right]

where
v(\omega_{ij}^{k})
denotes differentiation of the real-valued function
\omega_{ij}^{k}
in the direction of
v
.
Combining the above with Equations (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) implies the conclusion.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

The above proof shows that the error terms in the sectional curvatures are all
o(1/r^{2})
.

2.4.
Twisted Dirac operator on a cone

Let
(\hat{L}^{n-1},\hat{g})
and
(L^{n-1},g)
be closed spin Riemannian manifolds of the same dimension and suppose
F\colon C\hat{L}\to CL
is a smooth map of the form
F(r,x)=(\varphi(r),f(x))
. Define
V=TC\hat{L}\oplus F^{*}TCL
. We want to relate the Dirac operator on
SV
to the Dirac operator on
SV|_{\hat{L}}
. To do so, we will construct a unitary
\Psi\colon L^{2}((0,\infty),SV|_{\hat{L}})\to L^{2}(SV)
.

Before constructing
\Psi
, let us establish some notation.
First, notice that the spinor bundle
SV
is a
C\ell(TC\hat{L}\oplus F^{*}TCL)
-module.
The Clifford action by vectors
\hat{w}
coming from
TC\hat{L}
will be denoted by
\hat{c}(\hat{w})
and the Clifford action by vectors
w
coming from
F^{*}TCL
will be denoted by
c(w)
. Second, given
\sigma_{x}
in
(SV|_{\hat{L}})_{x}
, let
\overline{\sigma_{x}}
denote the parallel transport of
\sigma_{x}
along the ray
(0,\infty)\times\{x\}
.
This parallel transport satisfies the following properties.

Lemma 2.10
.

If
X\in T\hat{L}
and
Y\in f^{*}TL
, then

(2.11)

\displaystyle\overline{\hat{c}(X)\sigma}=\hat{c}(\overline{X})\overline{\sigma}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\overline{c(Y)\sigma}=c(\overline{Y})\overline{\sigma}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\overline{\sigma}=\overline{\nabla_{X}\sigma}

Here,
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
are the parallel transports of
X
and
Y
in the radial direction.

Proof.

Equations (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) are an immediate consequence of the compatibility of the spinor connection with the Clifford action.

To prove Equation (
2.13
), take an orthonormal frame
\big\{\partial_{r},\{\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\},\varphi^{*}\partial_{r},\{\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(r)}\}\big\}
on
V
.
The associated connection matrix takes the form

\omega_{V}=\sum_{i}\hat{\omega}_{\partial_{r}}^{i}\partial_{r}\wedge\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}+\sum_{i,j}\hat{\omega}_{j}^{i}\frac{\hat{e}_{j}}{r}\wedge\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}+\sum_{i}(F^{*}\omega_{\partial_{r}}^{i})\,\varphi^{*}\partial_{r}\wedge\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(r)}\\
+\sum_{i,j}(F^{*}\omega_{j}^{i})\,\frac{f^{*}e_{j}}{\varphi(r)}\wedge\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(r)}

In the above,
X\wedge Y
is the endomorphism defined by
(X\wedge Y)(w)=\langle X,w\rangle Y-\langle Y,w\rangle X
.

Lifting the frame and connection to the spinor bundle, one gets an orthonormal spinor frame
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{N})
for which

\nabla\sigma_{k}=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}\hat{\omega}_{\partial_{r}}^{i}\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\hat{c}\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\right)\sigma_{k}+\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{2}\hat{\omega}_{j}^{i}\hat{c}\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{j}}{r}\right)\hat{c}\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\right)\sigma_{k}\\
+\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}(F^{*}\omega_{\partial_{r}}^{i})c(\varphi^{*}\partial_{r})c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(r)}\Big)\sigma_{k}+\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{2}(F^{*}\omega_{j}^{i})c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{j}}{\varphi(r)}\Big)c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(r)}\Big)\sigma_{k}.

Observe that the frame
\{\sigma_{i}\}
is parallel with respect to the radial direction, so actually
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{N})=(\overline{\sigma}_{1},\ldots,\overline{\sigma}_{N})
.

Thus,

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\overline{\sigma}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}\hat{\omega}_{\partial_{r}}^{i}(X)\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\hat{c}\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\right)\overline{\sigma}_{k}+\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{2}\hat{\omega}_{j}^{i}(X)\hat{c}\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{j}}{r}\right)\hat{c}\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\right)\overline{\sigma}_{k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\partial_{r}}^{i}(f_{*}X)c(\varphi^{*}\partial_{r})c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(r)}\Big)\overline{\sigma}_{k}+\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{2}\omega_{j}^{i}(f_{*}X)c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{j}}{\varphi(r)}\Big)c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(r)}\Big)\overline{\sigma}_{k}

\displaystyle=\overline{\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}\hat{\omega}_{\partial_{r}}^{i}(X)\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{i}\right)\sigma_{k}+\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{2}\hat{\omega}_{j}^{i}(X)\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{j}\right)\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{i}\right)\sigma_{k}}

\displaystyle+\overline{\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\partial_{r}}^{i}(f_{*}X)c(\varphi^{*}\partial_{r})c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(1)}\Big)\sigma_{k}+\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{2}\omega_{j}^{i}(f_{*}X)c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{j}}{\varphi(1)}\Big)c\Big(\frac{f^{*}e_{i}}{\varphi(1)}\Big)\sigma_{k}}

\displaystyle=\overline{\nabla_{X}\sigma_{k}},

where in the above we have used that the connection matrices
\hat{\omega}
and
\omega
are constant with respect to the radial direction
r
(see (
2.1
)).

The general case where
\sigma
in
SV|_{\hat{L}}
is a linear combination of the
\{\sigma_{i}\}
follows from the above.
∎

The unitary
\Psi
is constructed as follows.
Given
\sigma(r)
in
C^{\infty}((0,\infty),SV|_{\hat{L}})
, define
(\Psi\sigma)(r)=r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\overline{\sigma(r)}(r)
, where recall that
n-1
is the dimension of the link.
In words,
(\Psi\sigma)(r)
is the parallel transport of
\sigma(r)
to the
r
-link,
\{r\}\times\hat{L}
, multiplied by
r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
.
The
r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
term ensures
\Psi
is a unitary.
To see this, we compute

\displaystyle\lVert\Psi\sigma\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{L}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\overline{\sigma}|^{2}r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{L}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\sigma|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=\lVert\sigma\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence,
\Psi
extends to a unitary
L^{2}((0,\infty),SV|_{\hat{L}})\to L^{2}(SV)
.

The next step is to explicitly determine the form of the Dirac operator on
SV
when viewed as an unbounded operator acting on
L^{2}((0,\infty),SV|_{\hat{L}})
.
To this end, start by defining

T_{X}^{\partial,0}\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\hat{\omega}_{\partial_{r}}^{i}(X)\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\hat{c}\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\right)\sigma

and consider the new connection
\nabla^{\partial,0}_{X}\sigma\coloneqq\nabla_{X}\sigma-T_{X}^{\partial,0}\sigma
.
Intuitively, we are just removing the contribution from the radial direction of
TC\hat{L}
.
The next proposition expresses
\Psi^{-1}D\Psi
in terms of
\nabla^{\partial,0}
.

Proposition 2.11

(cf.
[
20
, Proposition 5.2]
)
.

Suppose
\sigma\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty),SV|{\hat{L}})
is a smooth compactly supported section vanishing near the singularity.
Then

\Psi^{-1}D\Psi\sigma=\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\frac{d}{dr}\sigma+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i}\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{i}\right)\nabla^{\partial,0}_{\hat{e}_{i}}\sigma

where
\{\hat{e}_{i}\}
is any local orthonormal frame on
\hat{L}
.

Proof.

To prove the statement, it is sufficient to show it for sections
\sigma
supported in
(0,\infty)\times U
, for any coordinate neighborhood
U
of the link.
In this case,
\sigma
can be written as a linear combination
\sigma=\sum_{k}a_{k}(r,x)\sigma_{k}
, where each
\sigma_{k}
depends only on
x
.
Thus, it is enough to prove the statement for sections of the form
a(r,x)\sigma
, where
a(r,x)
is a smooth function and
\sigma
is a smooth section of
SV|_{\hat{L}}
.
We compute

\displaystyle D\Psi a(r,x)\sigma

\displaystyle=D(r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}a(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r))

\displaystyle=\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\nabla_{\partial_{r}}(r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}a(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r))+\sum_{i}\hat{c}\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}\right)\nabla_{\frac{\hat{e}_{i}}{r}}(r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}a(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r))

\displaystyle=\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\left[-\Big(\frac{n-1}{2}\Big)r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}a(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r)+r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}a^{\prime}(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i}r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}\overline{\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{i}\right)\nabla_{\hat{e}_{i}}a(r,x)\sigma}(r)

\displaystyle=\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\left[-\Big(\frac{n-1}{2}\Big)r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}a(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r)+r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}a^{\prime}(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i}r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}\overline{\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{i}\right)\nabla^{\partial,0}_{\hat{e}_{i}}a(r,x)\sigma}(r)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i,j}r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}a(r,x)\overline{\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{i}\right)\frac{1}{2}\hat{\omega}_{\partial_{r}}^{j}(\hat{e}_{i})\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{j}\right)\sigma}(r).

On the other hand,
\omega_{\partial_{r}}^{j}(\hat{e}_{i})=\big<\nabla_{\hat{e}_{i}}\partial_{r},\frac{\hat{e}_{j}}{r}\big>=\delta_{ij}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle D\Psi a(r,x)\sigma

\displaystyle=\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\left[\underbrace{-\Big(\frac{n-1}{2}\Big)r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}a(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r)}+r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}a^{\prime}(r,x)\overline{\sigma}(r)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i}r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}\overline{\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{i}\right)\nabla^{\partial,0}_{\hat{e}_{i}}a(r,x)\sigma}(r)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\underbrace{\sum_{i}r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1}{r}a(r,x)\overline{\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)\sigma}(r)}.

The underbraced terms cancel out, which implies

\Psi^{-1}D\Psi a(r,x)\sigma=\hat{c}\left(\partial_{r}\right)a^{\prime}(r,x)\sigma+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i}\hat{c}\left(\hat{e}_{i}\right)\nabla^{\partial,0}_{\hat{e}_{i}}a(r,x)\sigma.

∎

.

.

SV

To finish the section, recall that
\hat{L}^{n-1}

decomposes into

\widetilde{\Psi}
from
L^{2}((0,\infty),S^{+}V|_{\hat{L}}\oplus S^{+}V|_{\hat{L}})

.
Proposition
2.11
can be rewritten as follows:

.

Suppose
\sigma

\widetilde{\Psi}^{-1}D\widetilde{\Psi}\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\frac{d}{dr}\sigma+\frac{1}{r}\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal{P}\\

Remark 2.14
.

Let
T^{0,\partial}

2.5.
Dirac operator estimates

In this subsection, we present some standard Listing-type estimates relating the twisted Dirac operator with the scalar and mean curvatures on the domain and target.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
(\hat{M},\partial\hat{M},\hat{g})
and
(M,\partial M,g)
be manifolds with FACS and
F
an asymptotically conical map between them.
If the curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
on M is nonnegative and if
\sigma
is a smooth section of
S(T\hat{M}\oplus F^{*}TM)
vanishing near the boundary and near the singular stratum of
\hat{M}
, then the following inequality holds:

(2.14)

\int_{\hat{M}}|D\sigma|^{2}\geq\int_{\hat{M}}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+\int_{\hat{M}}\frac{1}{4}(\hat{\mathrm{Sc}}-\|\wedge^{2}dF\|F^{*}\mathrm{Sc})|\sigma|^{2}.

Proof.

Since
\sigma
vanishes near the boundary,

\int_{\hat{M}}|D\sigma|^{2}=\int_{\hat{M}}\langle D^{2}\sigma,\sigma\rangle.

By the twisted Lichnerowicz theorem,

D^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\frac{\hat{\mathrm{Sc}}}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
k<l\end{subarray}}\left<\mathcal{R}(F_{*}\hat{e}_{i}\wedge F_{*}\hat{e}_{j}),e_{k}\wedge e_{l}\right>\hat{c}(\hat{e}_{i})\hat{c}(\hat{e}_{j})c(e_{k})c(e_{l}).

The only term that needs to be investigated is the last one.
For the sake of simplifying notation, enumerate
\{\hat{e}_{i}\wedge\hat{e}_{j}\}_{i<j}
by
\{\hat{w}_{1},\hat{w}_{2},\ldots\}
and
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}_{i<j}
by
\{w_{1},w_{2},\ldots\}
.
Observe that writing
\hat{c}(\hat{w}_{k})
makes sense if we define
\hat{c}(\hat{e}_{i}\wedge\hat{e}_{j})=\hat{c}(\hat{e}_{i})\hat{c}(\hat{e}_{j})
, and the same goes for
c(w_{k})
.
Under this notation, the last term can be written as
-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\left<\mathcal{R}F_{*}\hat{w}_{i},w_{j}\right>\hat{c}(\hat{w}_{i})c(w_{j})
.
Since
\mathcal{R}
is nonnegative, it has a square root
L
.
We compute

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\left<\mathcal{R}F_{*}\hat{w}_{i},w_{j}\right>\hat{c}(\hat{w}_{i})c(w_{j})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\langle LF_{*}\hat{w}_{i},Lw_{j}\rangle\hat{c}(\hat{w}_{i})c(w_{j})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,k}\langle LF_{*}\hat{w}_{i},w_{k}\rangle\langle Lw_{j},w_{k}\rangle\hat{c}(\hat{w}_{i})c(w_{j})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,k}\langle F_{*}\hat{w}_{i},Lw_{k}\rangle\langle w_{j},Lw_{k}\rangle\hat{c}(\hat{w}_{i})c(w_{j})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\hat{c}(\hat{L}w_{k})c(Lw_{k}).

5.
Manifolds with iterated asymptotically conical singularities

In this section, we generalize the notion of a manifold with fiberwise asymptotically conical singularities (FACS) to that of a manifold with iterated asymptotically conical singularities (IACS).
In particular, the notion of a
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-manifold will be defined.
In this new language, the manifolds with FACS, as defined in Definition
2.1
, are the
\mathcal{C}_{1}
-manifolds.
After this, Fredholmness of the twisted Dirac operator in this more general setting will be established by generalizing the results of Section
3
.
Lastly, we will state Theorem
1.1
precisely using our language of
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-manifolds.

5.1.
Geometric setup

The
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-manifolds are defined to be the smooth manifolds, and a
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-map is just a smooth map between smooth manifolds.

The
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-manifolds are defined inductively.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer and
M
a topological space.
Suppose that the
\mathcal{C}_{0}
, …,
\mathcal{C}_{m-1}
-manifolds and the notions of a
\mathcal{C}_{0}
, …,
\mathcal{C}_{m-1}
-map have been defined.

We call a neighborhood
U
a
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-neighborhood
of

if
U
is homeomorphic to a direct product of
\mathbb{R}^{h}
with the cone
C_{\varepsilon}L
, where
L
is a
\mathcal{C}_{m-1}
-manifold without boundary, for which
p
is taken to a point of
\mathbb{R}^{h}\times\{0\}
under the homeomorphism.
The point
p
is a called a
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-singular point
of
M
.

A topological space

is said to be an
n
-dimensional
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-manifold
if the following conditions hold:

(a)

Topology:
There is an open cover of
M
for which every open set in the cover is either homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or is a
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-neighborhood of some
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-singular point of
M
, with
k\leq m
.
The transition functions between two nontrivially intersecting neighborhoods
U
and
V
, both homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, are required to be smooth.
The transition functions between two nontrivially intersecting
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-neighborhoods

and
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{h}\times C_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}L^{\prime}
are required to take the form:

F(b,r,x)=(\psi(b),\varphi(b,r),f(b,x))

where
b
represents a point on the base space,
r
corresponds to the radial coordinate, and
x
is an element of the link.
The functions
\psi
and
\varphi
are required to be smooth away from
r=0
and
\varphi
is assumed to take the form
\varphi(b,r)=a(b)\cdot r+o_{2}(r)
near
r=0
, with
a(b)>0
for all
b
.
The function
f
is required to be a
\mathcal{C}_{m-1}
-map.

The
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-stratum
is the set of
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-singular points of
M
.
This set is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{k}
.
The singular stratum is denoted by
\mathcal{C}
and is defined to be

.
The smooth stratum, or
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-stratum, is the complement

and is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{0}
.

(b)

Geometry:
For each
\mathcal{C}_{k}\neq\emptyset
there is a metric
g_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}
on
\mathcal{C}_{k}
.
There is also a metric
g
defined on the smooth stratum of
M
such that in a
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-neighborhood
U=\mathbb{R}^{h}\times C_{\varepsilon}L
of a
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-singular point
p

g
takes form

g|_{U}=\pi^{*}g_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}|_{\pi(U)}\oplus\beta^{\prime}(b,r)^{2}dr^{2}\oplus\beta(b,r)^{2}g_{L}(b)+o_{2}(r^{2})

subject to the following criteria:

(a)

\pi^{*}g_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}|_{\pi(U)}
is the pullback of the metric on the
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-stratum under the projection
\pi\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{h}
.

(b)

g_{L}(b)
is a family of metrics on
L
such that the product
\mathcal{C}_{k}|_{\pi(U)}\times L
equipped with
\pi^{*}g_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}|_{\pi(U)}\oplus g_{L}
is a
\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
-manifold.

(c)

\beta(b,r)
is a real-valued function that is smooth away from
r=0
and is of the form
\beta(b,r)=a(b)\cdot r+o_{2}(r)
near
r=0
, with
a(b)>0
for all

.

To ease the notation, we will often write

to denote the pullback of the base metric, instead of
\pi^{*}g_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}|_{\pi(U)}

If

is a
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-manifold for some

is referred to as a
manifold with iterated asymptotically conical singularities
, sometimes shortened to “manifold with IACS” or just “IACS manifold.”

The same remarks following Definition

apply.
We summarize them here.
In a conical neighborhood, after a reparametrization, the metric becomes a Riemannian submersion of a family of cones over the base, up to asymptotic error terms.
Two
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-singular points in the same connected component of

must have the same link.
The notion of a manifold with IACS can naturally be extended to that of an
IACS manifold with IACS boundary.

The singular stratum
\mathcal{C}
may depend on the covering atlas.

We now define a natural class of maps between
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-manifolds.
Just as before, the definition will be inductive.

Definition 5.2
.

Let

be an integer and suppose the notions of a

If

(1)

(2)

(3)

If

, and in the trivialization the map

takes the form

F(\hat{b},r,\hat{x})=(\psi(\hat{b}),\varphi(\hat{b},r),f(\hat{b},\hat{x}))

where
\hat{b}
represents a point on the base space,
r
corresponds to the radial coordinate, and
\hat{x}
is an element of the link.
The functions
\psi
and
\varphi
are required to be smooth away from

\varphi

.
The function

\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
-map.

5.2.
Functional analytic preliminaries

To prove the theorem, the Fredholmness of the twisted Dirac operator needs to be established first.
This will be deduced just as in Section
3
, by first establishing self-adjointness and then using the compactness of the inclusion of
H^{1}
into
L^{2}
to obtain a compact resolvent.
To this end, we generalize the results of Section
3.1
.

The notation used in this subsection is as follows:

Definition 5.3
.

Let
E
denote a Hermitian vector bundle on an IACS manifold
M
, possibly having a smooth boundary
\partial M
, with Hermitian connection.

(1)

C_{c}^{\infty}(M,E)
is defined to be the set of smooth compactly supported sections of

vanishing near the singular stratum.

(2)

H^{1}(M,E)
is defined to be the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(M,E)
with respect to the norm

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{1}}\coloneqq(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})^{1/2}

The first proposition of this subsection generalizes Proposition
3.2
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
E
be a Hermitian vector bundle with Hermitian connection over a compact manifold
M
with IACS.
Let
d_{\mathcal{C}}(p)\coloneqq\dist(p,\mathcal{C})
denote the distance from
p
to the closest singular point.
If the links of
M
have dimension at least two, then multiplication by
1/d_{\mathcal{C}}
defines a bounded linear operator from
H^{1}(M,E)
to
L^{2}(M,E)
.
Moreover, the operator norm is bounded above by a constant that depends only on the geometry and topology of
M
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction.
This was proven in Proposition
3.2
for
\mathcal{C}_{1}
-manifolds.
Suppose it holds for
\mathcal{C}_{1}
-manifolds, …,
\mathcal{C}_{m-1}
-manifolds.
To prove the statement for
\mathcal{C}_{m}
-manifolds it is sufficient to prove the statement in a
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-neighborhood of a
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-singular point for

After localizing and accounting for error terms, the proof reduces to the case where

with model metric

Here,

is an

\mathcal{C}_{k}
-manifold with

In the model case,

denotes the closest distance from

L
with respect to the metric
g_{L}(b)
.
Thus, it suffices to show that multiplication by

is bounded.
Let

be a smooth compactly supported section vanishing near the singular stratum.
Using the inductive hypothesis,

\displaystyle\lVert\frac{1}{\tilde{d}_{\mathcal{C}}}\sigma\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{L}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{1}{\dist(x,\mathcal{C}_{L(b)})^{2}}|\sigma|^{2}r^{l}dvol_{L(b)}drdvol_{B}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{C(b)}{r^{2}}\big(\lVert(\sigma(r)|_{L(b)})\rVert_{L^{2}(L(b))}^{2}+\lVert\nabla(\sigma(r)|_{L(b)})\rVert_{L^{2}(L(b))}^{2}\big)r^{l}drdvol_{B}.

We may assume that the

Next, observe that

\int_{B}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\lVert(\sigma(r)|_{L(b)})\rVert_{L^{2}(L(b))}^{2}r^{l}drdvol_{B}\leq\frac{2}{l-1}\lVert\sigma\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}

Finally,

\int_{B}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\lVert\nabla(\sigma(r)|_{L(b)})\rVert_{L^{2}(L(b))}^{2}r^{l}drdvol_{B}\leq\lVert\nabla\sigma\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}

Combining all of the above inequalities implies the conclusion.

We write

The case of

Suppose the statement has been proven for compact

Let

6.1.

We prove a rigidity theorem for Euclidean domains as a corollary of Theorem

Theorem 6.1

(ii)

Proof.

Let

The proofs of Section

For local orthonormal bases

6.2.
Positive mass theorem

We start by establishing some definitions and notation.

Definition 6.2
.

Consider the Euclidean flat space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a smooth positive function
\rho
that is equal to the distance function from the origin outside a compact set.
For any
\delta>0
and any measurable function
f
, define

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\delta}^{2}}^{2}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert f\rvert^{2}\rho^{-2\delta-n}.

The
\delta
-weighted
L^{2}
-space is defined to be

L_{\delta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}:\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\delta}^{2}}<\infty\}.

endowed with the
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L_{\delta}^{2}}
norm.

The
\delta
-weighted
H^{k}
-Sobolev space is defined to be

H_{\delta}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}:\partial^{\alpha}f\in L_{\delta-\lvert\alpha\rvert}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;\forall\text{ multi-indices $\alpha$ with $\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k$}\}

where differentiation is in the weak sense.
This space is endowed with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{H^{k}_{\delta}}^{2}=\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k}\lVert\partial^{\alpha}f\rVert_{L_{\delta-\lvert\alpha\rvert}^{2}}^{2}.

It is not hard to see that the vector spaces
L_{\delta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H_{\delta}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as well as the topologies induced by the norms, do not depend on the choice of
\rho
.

Definition 6.3
.

A
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-manifold
(M,g)
is said to be asymptotically flat if the following conditions hold:

(1)

There is a compact set
K
such that
(K,g)
is a
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-manifold with smooth boundary.

(2)

There exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon M\setminus K\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K^{\prime}
, where
K^{\prime}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, satisfying the following properties:

(a)

\Phi_{*}g-g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\in H_{\tau}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K^{\prime})
where
\tau=\frac{n-2}{2}

(b)

\lambda^{-1}g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq\Phi_{*}g\leq\lambda g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
for some
\lambda>0

(3)

\mathrm{Sc}_{g}\in L^{1}(M\setminus K)

For an asymptotically flat
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-manifold
(M,g)
the ADM mass is defined in the usual manner, namely,

m(g)=\frac{1}{16\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S(R)}\big((\Phi_{*}g)_{ij,j}-(\Phi_{*}g)_{jj,i}\big)dS^{i}

where
(\Phi_{*}g)_{ij,k}
denotes the
k
-th partial derivative,
S(R)
is the standard round sphere of radius
R
, and

is the normal surface element in the direction of

.

We will prove the positive mass theorem for IACS manifolds:

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional asymptotically flat spin
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-manifold.
Suppose that the links of
M
have dimension at least two.
If
\mathrm{Sc}_{g}\geq 0
, then
m(g)\geq 0
.
Moreover, if
m(g)=0
, then the metric
g
is flat.

Before getting into the proof, we first extend the notion of a
\delta
-weighted space to include Hermitian bundles with Hermitian connection on asymptotically flat IACS manifolds.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
E
be a Hermitian bundle with Hermitian connection on an asymptotically flat IACS manifold
M
.

The
\delta
-weighted
H^{k}
-Sobolev space
H_{\delta}^{k}(M,E)
is defined to be the completion of the smooth compactly supported sections of

with respect to the norm

\lVert\sigma\rVert_{H_{\delta}^{k}}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\leq k}\lVert\nabla^{\alpha}\sigma\rVert_{L_{\delta-\alpha}^{2}}^{2}

where

\lVert\nabla^{\alpha}\sigma\rVert^{2}\coloneqq\int_{M}|\nabla^{\alpha}\sigma|^{2}\rho^{-2\delta-n}.

Here,
\rho
is any positive function on
M
that is equal to the Euclidean distance from the origin outside some compact set
K
for which
M\setminus K\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K^{\prime}
.

The proof of Theorem
6.4
utilizes the following crucial lemma.

Lemma 6.6
.

If
\mathrm{Sc}_{g}\geq 0
, then the Dirac operator

D\colon H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)\to L_{-\tau-1}^{2}(M,SM)

is invertible when
\tau=\frac{n-2}{2}
.

Proof.

The proofs of Section
5.3
carry over to show that self-adjointness holds near the singular stratum.
Combining this with results of
[
3
]
we get
D^{*}=D
, where on the left-hand side
D^{*}
is interpreted as the functional-analytic adjoint of

D\colon H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)\to L_{-\tau-1}^{2}(M,SM)

and on the right-hand side
D
is interpreted as the map

D\colon H_{\tau-n+1}^{1}(M,SM)\to L_{\tau-n}^{2}(M,SM).

The proof of Fredholmness also carries over from Section
5.3
, allowing us to construct a parametrix for
D
on some compact set
K
, with smooth boundary, containing the singular stratum.
The results of
[
3
]
allow us to construct a parametrix for
D
on the infinite end
M\setminus K
.
By gluing these parametrices together with a partition of unity, a parametrix for
D
on the entire
\mathcal{C}_{k}
-manifold
M
can be constructed.
Hence,
D
is Fredholm, and in particular has closed image.

To show that the operator is invertible, we start by showing that the kernel is trivial.
By definition, if
\sigma\in H_{-\tau}^{1}
, then

\int_{M}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}<\infty\text{ and }\int_{M}|\sigma|^{2}\rho^{-2}<\infty.

If
\sigma
were smooth and compactly supported, then, by the Lichnerowicz formula,

\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\sigma|^{2}=\int_{M}|D\sigma|^{2}-\int_{M}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}

where the right-hand side is bounded above by a constant multiple of
\lVert\nabla\sigma\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

Since the smooth compactly supported functions are dense in
H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
, multiplication by
\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}}{2}
must a bounded linear map from
H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
to
L^{2}(M,SM)
.
Thus, the Lichnerowicz formula holds for general
\sigma\in H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
.

Suppose
\sigma\in H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
were a nonzero element in the kernel of
D
.
By ellipticity,
\sigma
must be a smooth section of
SM
away from the singular stratum.
Combining the nonnegativity of the scalar curvature with the Lichnerowicz formula, we see that
\sigma
is parallel.
In particular,
|\sigma|
must be a nonzero constant.
This implies that for some large
R>0
,

\int_{M}|\sigma|^{2}\rho^{-2}\geq\int_{R}^{\infty}|\sigma|^{2}\rho^{n-3}d\rho=\infty

a contradiction.
Hence, the kernel of
D
must be trivial.

To establish surjectivity, we investigate the kernel of

D\colon H_{\tau-n+1}^{1}(M,SM)\to L_{\tau-n}^{2}(M,SM).

By definition, an element
\sigma^{*}\in H_{\tau+1-n}^{1}(M,SM)=H_{-n/2}^{1}(M,SM)
satisfies

\int_{M}|\nabla\sigma^{*}|^{2}\rho^{2}<\infty\text{ and }\int_{M}|\sigma^{*}|^{2}<\infty.

In particular,
\nabla\sigma^{*}
is in
L^{2}(M,SM)
.
The same argument as before implies that multiplication by
\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}}{2}
is a bounded linear map from
H_{\tau+1-n}^{1}(M,SM)
to
L^{2}(M,SM)
.
Thus, the Lichnerowicz formula holds for
\sigma\in H_{\tau+1-n}^{1}(M,SM)
.

Now, suppose
\sigma^{*}\in H_{\tau+1-n}^{1}(M,SM)
were a nonzero element in the kernel of
D^{*}
.
By ellipticity and the Lichnerowicz formula,
\sigma^{*}
is smooth away from the singular stratum and also parallel.
Hence,
|\sigma^{*}|
must be a nonzero constant.
This implies that for sufficiently large
R>0
,

\int_{M}|\sigma^{*}|^{2}\geq\int_{R}^{\infty}|\sigma^{*}|^{2}\rho^{n-1}d\rho=\infty

a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.4
.

Let
E=M\setminus K
.
Let
\sigma_{0}
denote the pullback of a constant spinor on
E
.
Smoothly extend
\sigma_{0}
to a section of
M
vanishing near the singular stratum.
According to
[
3
]
,
\nabla\sigma_{0}\in L_{-\tau-1}^{2}(M,SM)
.
By Lemma
6.6
, there exists a unique
\sigma_{1}\in H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
for which
D\sigma_{1}=-D\sigma_{0}
.
Let
\sigma=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{0}
.
Then,
D\sigma=0
and
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
modulo
H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
.
In particular,
\nabla\sigma\in L_{-\tau-1}^{2}(M,SM)
.

By the ellipticity of
D
,
\sigma_{1}
is smooth except at the singular stratum.
The proof of Lemma
6.6
showed that the Lichnerowicz formula holds for
H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
, namely,

(6.1)

\int_{M}|D\sigma_{1}|^{2}=\int_{M}|\nabla\sigma_{1}|^{2}+\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\sigma_{1}|^{2}.

Take a compactly supported cutoff function
\psi
which is equal to one near the singular stratum.
Since
\psi\sigma_{1}\in H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
, again we must have

\int_{M}|D(\psi\sigma_{1})|^{2}=\int_{M}|\nabla(\psi\sigma_{1})|^{2}+\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\psi\sigma_{1}|^{2}.

On the other hand, a direct application of the Lichnerowicz formula and Stokes’s theorem gives

\int_{M}|D(\psi\sigma_{1})|^{2}=\int_{M}|\nabla(\psi\sigma_{1})|^{2}+\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\psi\sigma_{1}|^{2}+\lim_{R\to 0}\int_{d_{\mathcal{C}}=R}\big<L^{\partial}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{1}\big>.

Here,
d_{\mathcal{C}}(p)\coloneqq\dist(p,\mathcal{C})
is the distance to the singular stratum and
L^{\partial}
is the differential operator on the boundary defined by

L^{\partial}\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}c(\nu)c(e_{i})\nabla_{e_{i}}\sigma

where
\nu
is the inner unit normal vector and
\{\nu,\{e_{i}\}\}
is an orthonormal frame.
Thus,

(6.2)

\lim_{R\to 0}\int_{d_{\mathcal{C}}=R}\big<L^{\partial}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{1}\big>=0.

A direct application of the Lichnerowicz formula, Stokes’s theorem, and (
6.2
) gives

(6.3)

\int_{D(R)}|D\sigma_{1}|^{2}=\int_{D(R)}|\nabla\sigma_{1}|^{2}+\int_{D(R)}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\sigma_{1}|^{2}+\int_{S(R)}\big<L^{\partial}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{1}\big>

for large balls
D(R)
.
Taking the limit as
R\to\infty
, since (
6.1
) must hold, we conclude

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S(R)}\big<L^{\partial}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{1}\big>=0.

Observe that if
\psi
is a smooth compactly supported section, then

\displaystyle\lvert\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}\langle\psi,\sigma_{0}\rangle\rvert\leq\int_{M}\lvert\langle\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}}{2}\psi,\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}}{2}\sigma_{0}\rangle\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\psi|^{2}\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\sigma_{0}|^{2}.

The integral

\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\sigma_{0}|^{2}

is finite since
\mathrm{Sc}_{g}\in L^{1}
and because
|{\sigma_{0}}|^{2}\leq C
uniformly, for some constant
C
.
In Lemma
6.6
we showed

\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\psi|^{2}\leq C\lVert\psi\rVert_{H_{-\tau}^{1}}^{2}

for some
C
independent of
\psi
.
This shows

\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}\langle\;\cdot\;,\sigma_{0}\rangle

is a continuous functional on
H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
.
By checking on smooth compactly supported sections, it is not hard to see that

\int_{M}\langle D\psi,D\sigma_{0}\rangle=\int_{M}\langle\nabla\psi,\nabla\sigma_{0}\rangle+\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}\langle\psi,\sigma_{0}\rangle

for all
\psi\in H_{-\tau}^{1}(M,SM)
.
On the other hand, by directly applying the Lichnerowicz formula and Stokes’s theorem,

\int_{D(R)}\big<D\sigma_{1},D\sigma_{0}\big>=\int_{D(R)}\big<\nabla\sigma_{1},\nabla\sigma_{0}\big>+\int_{D(R)}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}\big<\sigma_{1},\sigma_{0}\big>+\int_{S(R)}\big<L^{\partial}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{0}\big>.

Thus,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S(R)}\big<L^{\partial}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{0}\big>=0.

Combining all of the above, we deduce

0=\int_{M}|D\sigma|^{2}=\int_{M}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+\int_{M}\frac{\mathrm{Sc}_{g}}{4}|\sigma|^{2}+\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S(R)}\big<L^{\partial}\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0}\big>.

By a computation of Bartnik,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S(R)}\big<L^{\partial}\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0}\big>=-c(n)m(g)

for some normalization constant
c(n)>0
.
It follows that
m(g)\geq 0
.

To prove the second part of the theorem, suppose that
m(g)=0
.
The above procedure allows us to construct a parallel spinor on
M
given any spinor that is constant at infinity.
In particular, we may construct enough linearly independent parallel sections to conclude that
g
is flat.
∎

Preliminary study of FAP-targeted radiopharmaceuticals based on covalent targeting strategy and pharmacokinetic characteristics
Authors: Yiheng Ai, Zhongcai Jin, Tongtong Wu, Yi Li, Yanggang Liu, Jing Yang, Shijie Tang, Yue Chen, Lin Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn tumor imaging and therapy, fibroblast activation protein (FAP)-targeted radiotracers remains a research hotspot. The recently reported [68Ga]Ga/[177Lu]Lu-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 has been demonstrated to address limitations in conventional FAP-targeted agents, such as low tumor uptake, short retention time, and suboptimal therapeutic efficacy. However, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 positron emission tomography (PET) clinical imaging revealed high uptake in salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreas, necessitating further structural optimization. To address this limitation, we developed two novel radiopharmaceuticals.Results[68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 demonstrated favorable targeting specificity and diagnostic efficacy. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 exhibited dual excretion pathways (hepatobiliary and urinary systems), whereas [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 showed exclusive urinary excretion, demonstrating superior pharmacokinetic properties. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 exhibited significantly enhanced tracer uptake compared to [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286 in primary tumors (SUVmax: 11.03 ± 1.45 vs. 5.47 ± 1.66), lymph node metastases (SUVmax: 12.13 ± 2.22 vs. 5.70 ± 1.08), and distant metastases (SUVmax: 12.03 ± 6.56 vs. 6.83 ± 0.36) (all p < 0.05).ConclusionBoth [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 effectively overcame the drawback of high uptake in salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreas observed with [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 demonstrated superior pharmacokinetic characteristics with urinary excretion and showed the potential for superior diagnostic efficacy compared with [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at10.1186/s13550-026-01431-x.Keywords: Covalent targeted drugs, Pharmacokinetics, FAP, PET/CT, Lung cancer

Introduction

Fibroblast activation protein (FAP), a type II transmembrane serine protease highly expressed in cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) of over 90% of malignant epithelial tumors, is critical in tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis [1, 2]. In tumor imaging and therapy, FAP-targeted radiotracers remain a research hotspot, such as FAPI-04, FAPI-46, FAPI-74 (small-molecule FAPI derivatives), and FAP-2286 (a cyclic peptide-based ligand) have been applied in clinical and research settings [3–6].

The development of FAPIs originated from the structural modification of DPPIV inhibitors to enhance selectivity against DPPIV and PREP, which yielded the core structure N-(4-quinoly)-Gly-(2-cyanopyrrolidine). Based on this motif, a series of FAPI-based radiopharmaceuticals, represented by [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAPI-04, have been developed and applied in positron emission tomography (PET) imaging of various human tumors. Compared with [18F]FDG, the most commonly used PET tracer, 68Ga/18F-labeled FAPI tracers typically exhibit lower background signals and enhanced tumor-specific uptake, enabling broad diagnostic applications [7–12]. However, their relatively short in vivo tumor retention time limits further therapeutic use in targeted radionuclide therapy.

To improve tumor uptake and retention, multiple FAPI-04-based tracers have been developed, primarily through conjugation with additional binding moieties (e.g., albumin-binding domains [13, 14] or integrin receptor-targeting groups [15, 16]), dimerization or multimerization (e.g., BiOncoFAP, DOTA-2P(FAPi)₂, DOTA-4P(FAPi)₄ [17, 18]), and in some cases novel chelators [19] or alternative radionuclides [20], which may indirectly influence pharmacokinetics and retention.

Recently, Liu et al. [21] published a groundbreaking study which incorporated a sulfur(VI)-fluoride exchange (SuFEx)-based click chemistry linker onto radiopharmaceuticals enabling covalent ligand-target protein conjugation without affinity loss, thereby irreversibly modifying target proteins with radionuclide-bearing chelators. Once the covalent targeted radioligands (CTR) reach the tumor site, they bind to the target through non-covalent interactions, followed by proximity effect-facilitated irreversible covalent bonding. This mechanism significantly enhances binding affinity and minimizes tumor clearance rates. Meanwhile, unbound CTR molecules undergo rapid systemic elimination.

Using tumor-bearing mice, Liu et al. [21]demonstrated that the SuFEx-engineered small molecule conjugate [68Ga]Ga/[177Lu]Lu-CTR-FAPI ([68Ga]Ga/[177Lu]Lu-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04) showed 257% higher tumor uptake and 13-fold longer tumor retention than the original [68Ga]Ga/[177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-04. It also showed that unbound [68Ga]Ga/[177Lu]Lu-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 was rapidly cleared through the renal system. The SuFEx-engineered FAPI enhanced Peptide Receptor Radionuclide Therapy (PRRT) efficacy, achieving nearly complete tumor regression in mice. Furthermore, the study achieved irreversible immobilization of radioligands onto tumor targets through covalent targeting strategies, effectively overcoming the therapeutic limitations of conventional FAP-targeted radioligands caused by insufficient tumor uptake and retention.

The promising results of the covalent-targeted radiopharmaceutical [68Ga]Ga/[177Lu]Lu-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 in tumor-bearing mice prompted researchers to initiate clinical translation studies. A first-in-human study conducted in October 2024, involving 50 medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) patients [22], confirmed its superior diagnostic performance to [18F]FDG PET/CT in detecting primary lesions, lymph node metastases, and distant metastases. However, in human imaging studies, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 demonstrated elevated physiological uptake in salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreatic tissues, potentially obscuring lesion detection in these regions and posing a risk of radiation-induced toxicity during PRRT. Therefore, further optimization of its pharmacokinetic profile is imperative.

Zboralski et al. [23] developed FAP-2286, a DOTA-chelated cyclic FAP-binding peptide. DOTA-FAP-2286 employs a cyclic peptide as its binding motif, demonstrating excellent FAP-targeting specificity and high affinity, along with favorable stability in human plasma.

Compared to [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-04, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 exhibits prolonged tumor retention, making it more suitable for PRRT. Therefore, in this study, we selected DOTA-FAP-2286 as the molecular scaffold and employed the SuFEx covalent targeting approach to design and synthesize FAP-targeted 68Ga-labeled covalent-targeted radiopharmaceuticals. In this study, we deliberately chose the clinically validated cyclic peptide scaffold DOTA-FAP-2286 as the molecular backbone rather than FAPI-04. Unlike the linear peptidomimetic structure of FAPI-04, FAP-2286 is a macrocyclic peptide with inherently prolonged tumor retention and lower off-target uptake. Previous studies have demonstrated that FAP-2286-based tracers exhibit superior tumor retention and therapeutic efficacy compared to FAPI-04 derivatives in both preclinical and early clinical settings [24, 25]. This study preliminarily investigated the pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, and lesion uptake of these radiopharmaceuticals in normal mice, healthy volunteers, and lung cancer patients, and compared them with the [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286 to develop FAP-targeted radiopharmaceuticals with superior in vivo pharmacokinetic properties. Our purpose was to resolved the problem of high uptake observed with [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 in the salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreas and to identify imaging agents with superior pharmacokinetic characteristics.

Materials and methods

Radiopharmaceutical preparation

The previously reported compounds (DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 and DOTA-FAP-2286), as well as the novel agents [DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 and DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 (where KERERG denotes the hydrophilic peptide sequence Lys-Glu-Arg-Glu-Arg-Gly)] designed in this study were synthesized under contract by WuXi AppTec and Chutide. The synthesis methods and of the compounds are provided in the Supplementary Materials (Figure S1-S2 ). For radiolabeling, [68Ga]GaCl3 was obtained from a 68Ge/68GaGagenerator (Eckert & Ziegler) using 0.05 M HCl (5 mL). The eluate was added to a reaction vial containing sodium acetate buffer (0.25 M) and 40 µg precursor (1 mg/mL), resulting in a mixture with pH 4. The mixture was heated to 100 °C for 10 min and purified using a C18 Sep-Pak column (Waters), eluting with 0.5 mL of 75% ethanol solution followed by 5 mL saline. The product’s radiochemical purity was analyzed using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) (Shimadzu, Tokyo, Japan). For the HPLC method, please refer to the supplementary materials.

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR)

To determine the binding kinetics between the ligands and recombinant human FAP (catalog no. 10464H07H; Sino Biological Inc., Beijing, China), SPR experiments were conducted using a Biacore system (RRID: SCR_017162). The equilibrium dissociation constant (KD, expressed in nM) was calculated from the measured association rate constant (ka, M⁻¹ s⁻¹) and dissociation rate constant (kd, s⁻¹).

FAP was immobilized on a Series S Sensor Chip CM5 using a previously reported amine-coupling protocol. The sensor surface was preconditioned with a continuous buffer flow at 10 µL/min for at least 30 min at 25 °C. Binding kinetics were assessed by injecting serial dilutions of each ligand at increasing concentrations. Each injection was performed at a flow rate of 30 µL/min, with an association phase of 180 s and a dissociation phase of 200 s. Kinetic parameters were determined using single-cycle kinetics. Sensorgrams were analyzed with Biacore Evaluation software and fitted to a 1:1 Langmuir binding model. The reported kinetic constants (ka, kd, and KD) were derived from global fitting of a single comprehensive SPR run encompassing multiple analyte concentrations. High-quality sensorgrams and excellent agreement with the 1:1 binding model (low χ² values) confirmed the reliability of the fitted parameters, rendering technical replicates unnecessary for this confirmatory analysis.

Cell culture

HEK293huFAP cells were kindly provided by Sichuan KelunBiotech Biopharmaceutical Co., Ltd. These cells were generated by cloning huFAP into a pEAK8 vector (Edge Biosystems) and stably transfecting HEK293 c-18 cells (ATCC CRL-10852, RRID: CVCL_0045), followed by limiting dilution and monoclonal purification. HEK293huFAP cells (HEK293t cells with the human FAP gene, FAP-positive) were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM, Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% antibiotics (penicillin/streptomycin) at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

Biodistribution and micro-PET/CT imaging in normal and tumor-bearing mice

All animal studies adhere to ethical guidelines. Normal mice studies were conducted using 4–5-week-old male Kunming white mice purchased from Chengdu Yaokang Biotechnology Co., Ltd., HEK293huFAP tumor-bearing mice were generated by subcutaneously injecting 2 × 106 HEK293huFAP cells suspended in 1× PBS into the axillary region of 4–5-week-old male BALB/c nude mice (4–5 weeks old). The tumors were allowed to grow for 7–8 weeks until they reached a diameter of 5–8 mm. All animals were housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) barrier facility equipped with individually ventilated caging systems. Environmental conditions were strictly maintained at 25–27 °C with 55 ± 5% relative humidity, under a 10:14-h light-dark cycle. Animals were intravenously injected with 3.7–5.55 MBq (100–150 µCi) of four FAP-targeted tracers ([68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286, and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286; n = 3 per group). In the HEK293huFAP tumor-bearing mice, at 30 min, 1 h, and 2 h post-injection, blood, selected normal tissues, and tumors were excised, weighed, and the radioactivity was measured using a γ-counter (CAPRAC-t, CAPINTEC, USA). Blood samples were collected via retro-orbital bleeding, and euthanasia was performed by cervical dislocation under isoflurane anesthesia. The percentage of injected dose per gram of tissue (%ID/g) was calculated for each organ of interest.

Micro-PET/CT imaging was performed using a Siemens small-animal PET/CT scanner (Siemens Medical Solutions USA Inc.). Animals were intravenously injected with 3.7–5.55 MBq (100–150 µCi) of the tracers (n = 3 per group). Whole-body scans were acquired at 1 h post-injection. Mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane/oxygen (induction chamber flow 4–5 L/min, maintenance nose cone 1.5–2 L/min). CT acquisition preceded PET for attenuation correction and anatomical reference: 80 kV tube voltage, 0.5 mAs tube current, 200 ms exposure time, 3 bed positions, total CT time ~ 10 min. PET data were acquired in list-mode for 10 min (3 mm spatial resolution, 1 bed position).

Image reconstruction: PET images were reconstructed using the iterative ordered subset expectation maximization (OSEM) algorithm with appropriate corrections for scatter, randoms, and CT-based attenuation. Reconstruction parameters: 2 iterations and 16 subsets (or as per scanner default for Siemens Inveon), matrix 128 × 128 × 159, voxel size 0.776 × 0.776 × 0.796 mm³. No additional post-smoothing filter was applied to preserve quantitative fidelity.

Image analysis: Reconstruction and quantification were performed using Inveon Research Workplace software version 4.2.0.8 (Siemens Medical Solutions USA Inc.). Volumes of interest (VOIs) were manually delineated on co-registered CT images for organs of interest (heart, liver, kidneys, spleen, lungs, muscle, brain, salivary glands, tumor) and transferred to PET images. Tumor VOIs were defined using a 3D isocontour at 50% of maximum intensity or manual contouring guided by CT. Standardized uptake values (SUVmean and SUVmax) were calculated as (tissue radioactivity concentration/injected dose) × body weight. Decay correction was applied. Biodistribution (%ID/g) was derived from SUV values with appropriate corrections for injected dose and tissue weight.

Clinical PET/CT studies

This prospective study has been approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (Ethics Approval No.: 2020035; Clinical Trial Registration No.: ChiCTR2100044131). Written informed consent was obtained from all healthy volunteers and lung cancer patients prior to participation. No fasting or blood glucose measurement was required before imaging. Healthy volunteers and patients received an intravenous injection of four FAP-targeted radiotracers at a dose of 1.85 MBq/kg (0.05 mCi/kg) and were instructed to rest quietly for approximately 40–50 min before imaging. All PET/CT scans were performed using a uMI 780 PET/CT scanner (Shanghai United Imaging Healthcare, China). CT acquisition used helical mode with 120 kV voltage, 150 mAs current, 16 s acquisition time, 0.6 mm × 64 rows detector configuration, 0.8 pitch, and 1.5 mm reconstruction slice thickness. PET acquisition employed a three-dimensional (3D) static mode with 435–650 keV energy window, covering skull vertex to upper thighs (6–7 bed positions) at 3 min/bed position. Image reconstruction utilized the TrueX algorithm (6 iterations, 14 subsets) with 168 × 168 matrix, 2× zoom factor, 2.0 mm × 2.0 mm pixel size, Gaussian smoothing (FWHM = 2 mm), applied scatter correction, dead-time correction, and CT-based attenuation correction.

PET/CT image analysis

All PET/CT images were interpreted by two nuclear medicine physicians with extensive diagnostic experience who evaluated standardized uptake values (SUV) in normal organs, primary tumor lesions, lymph nodes, and distant metastases. For cases with multiple lymph node metastases, up to 5 nodes with the highest SUVmax values were selected for measurement. Similarly, for organs/tissues with multiple metastatic lesions, up to 5 lesions with the highest SUVmax values were measured, with lesions of appropriate size and clarity being prioritized to ensure accurate and reproducible measurements. If several lesions were confluent, the fused mass was measured. The metastatic lesions from all tissues/organs were combined and analyzed. In the event of disagreement between the two physicians‘ interpretations, a third board-certified nuclear medicine physician with ≥ 10 years of diagnostic experience was consulted; their assessment constituted the final determination.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Inter-group differences in tracer uptake among the four radiopharmaceuticals were evaluated using the Kruskal-Wallis H test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 20.0 (IBM Corp., USA) and Prism 10.1.2 (GraphPad Software, USA).

Results

Preparation and quality control of four radiopharmaceuticals

The purities of the four precursors (DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04, DOTA-FAP-2286, DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286, and DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286) were ≥ 95%. Physicochemical properties, results of HPLC and high-resolution mass spectrometry are shown in Table S1 and Figure S3-S5 in the Supplementary Materials. After radiolabeling with 68Ga in sodium acetate buffer (pH 4) at 100 °C for 10 min, all four FAP-targeted tracers exhibited radiochemical purity > 98% following purification, with specific activities exceeding 20 GBq/µmol. Radio-HPLC results are provided in the Supplementary Materials (Figure S6). The final 68Ga-labeled products were obtained with high molar activities ranging from 38 to 65 GBq/µmol, ensuring their suitability for both preclinical imaging and clinical pilot studies. The chemical structures of these four FAP-based radiopharmaceuticals are shown in Fig. 1.

Binding affinity

The FAP-binding specificity of the three ligands was assessed via SPR using recombinant human FAP (See Supplementary Materials Figure S7). All ligands exhibited strong nanomolar affinities, with KD values of 2.8 nM for DOTA-FAP-2286, 84 nM for DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286, and 0.21 nM for DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286. Kinetic analysis showed that DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 had the slowest binding rate. Conversely, DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 displayed the highest overall affinity, with a 40- to 50-fold increase over DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 and a significantly slower dissociation rate constant (8- to 12-fold lower than the other two ligands). This superior affinity, driven by the fastest association and slowest dissociation, aligns with molecular docking predictions and highlights its potential for targeting high FAP-expressing tumors.

Biodistribution and PET imaging in normal mice and tumor-bearing mice

Figure 2 displays the maximum intensity projection (MIP) images of PET scans obtained 1 h post-injection of the four radiopharmaceuticals in normal mice and tumor-bearing mice. Figure 3; Table 1 present the corresponding quantitative biodistribution data of tumor-bearing mice. PET imaging demonstrated distinct excretion patterns: [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 showed renal excretion with prominent bladder accumulation (Fig. 2A) and biodistribution values of 28.13 ± 0.67%ID/g (kidneys), 3.05 ± 0.23%ID/g (liver), 2.48 ± 0.48%ID/g (salivary glands), 1.75 ± 0.71%ID/g (thyroid), and 1.78 ± 0.71%ID/g (pancreas). [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286 exhibited exclusive renal clearance with uptake confined to kidneys and bladder (Fig. 2B), demonstrating 16.89 ± 3.22%ID/g (kidneys) and 3.15 ± 0.53%ID/g (liver). [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 displayed dual hepatobiliary-renal excretion with high uptake in both kidneys (29.71 ± 0.91%ID/g) and liver (28.39 ± 1.56%ID/g) (Fig. 2C). In the HEK293huFAP tumor model, the tumor uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286, and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 after 60 min was 39.68 ± 4.83% ID/g,42.68 ± 3.83% ID/g, 36.16 ± 4.63% ID/g, and 52.13 ± 3.26% ID/g. The optimized [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 exhibited favorable tumor uptake and pharmacokinetics, with predominant renal excretion (43.41 ± 6.99% ID/g) and low liver accumulation (1.69 ± 0.21% ID/g). Complete biodistribution data are presented in Fig. 3 (histogram format) and Table 1 (detailed quantitative results), revealing significant differences in tissue distribution profiles among the four tracers, particularly highlighting the advantageous excretion pattern of the KERERG-modified variant with the highest renal clearance and lowest non-target accumulation.

PET imaging in healthy volunteers

Twenty healthy volunteers (evenly divided into 4 groups of 2 females and 3 males each, median age: 55 years, range: 25–68 years) underwent PET/CT imaging with four different FAP-targeted radiopharmaceuticals: [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286, and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286. All volunteers tolerated the radiopharmaceuticals well without adverse reactions, such as tachycardia, dyspnea, nausea, or vomiting during injection, uptake period, or imaging procedures.

Figure 4 shows MIP images of the four tracers in healthy volunteers, while Fig. 5; Table 2 present the average SUVmax values in normal tissues. The PET/CT findings demonstrated urinary excretion of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04, with evident radiotracer accumulation in bilateral renal pelvises, calyces, and the urinary bladder. Additionally, physiological uptake was observed in the salivary glands, thyroid gland, and pancreas (Fig. 4A); the SUVmax distribution of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 in these glands was 9.90 ± 2.48, 12.67 ± 4.62, and 11.92 ± 2.96, respectively. Significant interspecies differences were observed in [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 uptake patterns between healthy humans and mice, with physiological uptake detected in salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreas in human subjects but absent in murine models. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286, a widely used PET/CT imaging agent in clinical practice, is excreted through the urinary system (Fig. 4B) and undergoes partial reabsorption in the renal parenchyma, with a renal SUVmax of 4.23 ± 1.28, while showing very low uptake in other tissues and organs. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 displayed dual hepatobiliary-renal excretion with the highest uptake in the liver (SUVmax 14.14 ± 2.72) and kidneys (11.03 ± 3.89), but no abnormal accumulation in salivary glands, thyroid, or pancreas (Fig. 4C).

Tumor lesion analysis in lung cancer patients

Twenty patients with pathologically confirmed lung cancer, equally divided into four groups (n = 5 each), were enrolled into the study, who then underwent PET/CT scans with four different radiopharmaceuticals. The study preliminarily evaluated the uptake patterns and targeting efficacy of these four imaging agents in primary tumors, lymph node metastases, and distant metastases. Among the five patients who underwent [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 PET/CT, the primary lesions showed SUVmax of 13.99 ± 6.96, three had lymph node metastases with SUVmax of 9.20 ± 6.22, and two had distant metastases with SUVmax of 5.87 ± 1.13. Among the five patients who underwent [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286 PET/CT, the primary lesions showed SUVmax of 5.47 ± 1.65, four had lymph node metastases with SUVmax of 5.70 ± 1.06, and three had distant metastases with SUVmax of 6.83 ± 0.35. Among the five patients who underwent [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 PET/CT, the primary lesions showed SUVmax of 8.80 ± 8.78, three had lymph node metastases with SUVmax of 6.50 ± 5.00, and three had distant metastases with SUVmax of 8.10 ± 2.69. Among the five patients who underwent [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 PET/CT, the primary lesions showed SUVmax of 11.03 ± 1.45, four had lymph node metastases with SUVmax of 12.13 ± 2.23, and three patients had distant metastases with SUVmax of 12.03 ± 6.61. Discussion

In this study, we designed and synthesized two novel radiopharmaceuticals, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286, based on the DOTA-FAP-2286 molecular scaffold using the SuFEx covalent targeting strategy.

During [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04 PET/CT imaging in humans, we observed significant radiotracer accumulation in the salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreas. This finding diverges from our murine experimental results, underscoring the importance of clinical translation studies. Our findings are consistent with those of Liu et al. [21]. The elevated radiotracer uptake in salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreas may obscure lesion detection in these organs and pose a risk of radiation-induced dysfunction during PRRT. This necessitates further optimization of the pharmacokinetic properties of this class of covalent FAP-targeted radiopharmaceuticals. We selected the clinically widely used DOTA-FAP-2286 with low background uptake and good targeting properties as the backbone, and designed and synthesized DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286. The PET/CT findings of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 demonstrated good consistency between murine and human studies. Notably, the radiopharmaceutical showed no significant uptake in glandular tissues, which may be attributed to the increased hydrophilicity of the imaging agent, which consequently decreases nonspecific interactions with lipid membranes in glandular tissues. Furthermore, structural modifications altered the metabolic pathways of the radiotracers, shifting their excretion preference toward renal elimination rather than glandular secretion. However, unlike [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286, which is excreted solely through the urinary system, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 also undergoes hepatobiliary excretion.

This dual excretion pathway may lead to radiotracer accumulation in the liver, resulting in high background signals that could obscure hepatic lesions and compromise imaging clarity and diagnostic accuracy. This may also impose a significant burden on the liver, particularly for patients with impaired liver function, potentially exacerbating hepatotoxicity. The hepatic excretion observed with [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 is attributable to its relatively higher lipophilicity resulting from the incorporation of the mFS covalent warhead, which favors hepatobiliary clearance. In contrast, insertion of the hydrophilic KERERG peptide sequence in [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 markedly decreased lipophilicity, redirecting excretion almost exclusively to the renal pathway and virtually eliminating hepatic uptake. This shift is consistent with literature showing that increased hydrophilicity reduces nonspecific interactions with hepatic metabolic enzymes and lipid membranes [26, 27]. To address these issues, we further optimized the chemical structure by incorporating a hydrophilic amino acid sequence (KERERG) into DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286. The KERERG polypeptide linker was deliberately chosen to enhance hydrophilicity. This sequence combines positively charged (Lys, Arg) and negatively charged (Glu) residues with Gly for flexibility, thereby promoting rapid renal clearance of the radioligand and reducing non-specific abdominal background—particularly beneficial for FAP-targeted imaging near the gastrointestinal tract. This design principle follows established strategies in tetrazine-radioligand development, where a closely analogous hydrophilic sequence (Gly-Arg-Glu-Arg-Glu-Lys) successfully shifted excretion from hepatobiliary to renal pathways and markedly improved imaging contrast for colorectal tumors [28]. Previous studies have demonstrated that adding hydrophobic or hydrophilic peptide sequences to compounds can modify their lipid-water partition coefficients, influencing their pharmacokinetic properties, in vivo biodistribution, and excretion pathways [29–31]. Through chemical structure optimization, we successfully developed [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286, significantly reducing nonspecific uptake in glands (such as salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreas) and liver while maintaining excellent tumor targeting and imaging contrast. Final PET/CT results in murine and human studies demonstrated that the incorporation of KERERG sequence into DOTA-FAP-mFS-2286 transformed the excretion pathway of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-mFS-2286 from dual hepatobiliary-renal clearance to predominantly urinary excretion, with low background values, good targeting, and significantly improved pharmacokinetic characteristics.

In healthy volunteers, our PET/CT imaging results demonstrated that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 exhibited low uptake in normal tissues/organs except for excretory pathways.

Conversely, in lung cancer patients, these two radiopharmaceuticals showed excellent tumor targeting capability with significantly high uptake in primary tumors, lymph node metastases, and distant metastases, a crucial prerequisite for potential radionuclide therapy applications. Importantly, both agents demonstrated good safety profiles, with no adverse reactions observed in the study participants. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 exhibited significantly enhanced tracer uptake compared to [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286 in primary tumors, metastatic lymph nodes, and distant metastatic lesions. The observed increase in SUVmax values indicates higher tumor uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286, Image and semi-quantitative comparison showed that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 excreted solely through the kidneys, showed high accumulation in tumor cells, and had low background values and good targeting properties, demonstrating favorable pharmacokinetic characteristics. Moreover, its uptake in non-target organs was low, similar to [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286, indicating that structural modification did not affect the specificity of FAP-based agents. These findings may suggest that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 has the potential for superior diagnostic efficacy compared with [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286.

FAP, as a pan-cancer target, has shown significant diagnostic efficacy in various tumors when labeled with different radioactive isotopes. However, its rapid clearance from the bloodstream and short retention time in tumors hinders its application in PRRT. Therefore, structural modifications of FAP-targeted agents to optimize tumor uptake and retention time are key research directions. Through our covalent targeting-based structural modification, the developed agent demonstrated enhanced binding affinity. When radiolabeled, DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 demonstrated prolonged intratumoral retention, which is promising for potential therapeutic applications. However, the observed increase in renal uptake warrants further investigation to optimize the pharmacokinetic profile. While the agent shows enhanced tumor targeting, minimizing renal toxicity will be a critical factor in evaluating its therapeutic potential. Future studies will focus on balancing tumor retention with renal safety to fully assess its therapeutic superiority over conventional DOTA-FAP-2286.

Ultimately, we selected DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286, which demonstrated favorable pharmacokinetic properties from a panel of four radiopharmaceuticals. This agent showed high tumor cell uptake with low uptake in normal tissues and organs. When labeled with therapeutic radionuclides such as 177Lu, it holds promise for further therapeutic studies. Although the clinically prevalent DOTA-FAP-2286 demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance, labeling it with therapeutic radionuclides may compromise its therapeutic efficacy due to suboptimal tumor uptake and rapid clearance under the unique tumor microenvironment conditions [32, 33].

Our optimized DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 demonstrated significant potential, exhibiting superior diagnostic performance to DOTA-FAP-2286. Therapeutically, our covalent targeting approach improves FAP binding affinity (KD = 0.21 nM vs. 2.8 nM for the parent compound), potentially enhancing tumor cell retention and addressing the short retention time limitation of current FAP-targeted agents in PRRT. This study has some limitations. First, the patient cohort size was relatively small. Second, while DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 demonstrated optimal performance in tumor targeting and pharmacokinetics, its significant renal reabsorption caused high radiotracer accumulation in kidneys, potentially risking renal damage during radionuclide therapy. This increased renal uptake could be linked to the hydrophilic nature of the KERERG peptide sequence, which enhances renal clearance and reabsorption. The reabsorption mechanism requires further investigation, and it may involve nonspecific binding to tubular epithelial cell surface proteins. Literature suggests that enhancing hydrophilicity and optimizing the molecular structure could reduce renal reabsorption [34]. These findings highlight the importance of further structural modifications to minimize renal uptake while maintaining excellent tumor targeting, especially for clinical translation and therapeutic applications. Third, although simultaneous PET/CT imaging with all four tracers can provide optimal comparability, ethical considerations and radiation dose constraints limited our study to single-tracer administration per subject, potentially introducing SUVmax selection bias. Fourth, metastatic lesions were not pathologically confirmed by gold-standard diagnostic methods.

Future research should focus on validating the performance of these imaging agents across a broader range of tumor types and exploring their potential in radionuclide therapy applications. In future studies, we intend to optimize the chemical structure of the DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 and DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 by investigating modifications that reduce renal uptake, such as further enhancing the hydrophilicity of the KERERG sequence while maintaining the agents’ tumor-targeting specificity and imaging performance. Additionally, we aim to explore structural changes to the SuFEx-based linker and the DOTA chelator to reduce nonspecific interactions and improve overall pharmacokinetics, focusing on minimizing unwanted uptake in non-target organs. The imaging agents developed in this study demonstrated significant clinical translation potential, particularly for FAP-overexpressing tumors, and may facilitate personalized medicine. With favorable characteristics, including low glandular uptake, excellent targeting specificity, high safety profile, and optimal pharmacokinetics, this agent demonstrated significant potential as a novel PET/CT imaging diagnostic and therapeutic radiopharmaceutical.

Conclusion

In this study, we successfully designed and synthesized two radiopharmaceuticals, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286, using DOTA-FAP-2286 as the molecular framework and the SuFEx covalent targeting approach. Both agents overcame the drawback of high uptake in salivary glands, thyroid, and pancreas observed with [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAPI-04. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-FAP-2286 exhibited good targeting specificity and low background values, excreted through hepatobiliary and urinary systems. The further optimized [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 demonstrated superior pharmacokinetic characteristics with urinary excretion only, excellent targeting capability, low uptake in normal organs and tissues, high tumor-to-background ratios, and clear tumor imaging, showing potential to surpass [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAP-2286 in diagnostic performance. This agent holds promise as a novel small-molecular imaging agent and therapeutic drug. Further studies are warranted to evaluate its antitumor efficacy when labeled with therapeutic radionuclides (e.g., 177Lu and 225Ac) and assess its renal toxicity. If warranted, optimizing the pharmacokinetic properties of DOTA-mFS-KERERG-FAP-2286 can further reduce renal reabsorption and decrease kidney toxicity.

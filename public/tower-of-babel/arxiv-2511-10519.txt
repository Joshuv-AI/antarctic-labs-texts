Say It Differently: Linguistic Styles as Jailbreak Vectors
Srikant Panda, Avinash Rai

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) are commonly evaluated for robustness against paraphrased or semantically equivalent jailbreak prompts, yet little attention has been paid to linguistic variation as an attack surface. In this work, we systematically study how linguistic styles such as fear or curiosity can reframe harmful intent and elicit unsafe responses from aligned models. We construct style-augmented jailbreak benchmark by transforming prompts from 3 standard datasets into 11 distinct linguistic styles using handcrafted templates and LLM-based rewrites, while preserving semantic intent. Evaluating 16 open- and close-source instruction-tuned models, we find that stylistic reframing increases jailbreak success rates by up to +57 percentage points. Styles such as fearful, curious and compassionate are most effective and contextualized rewrites outperform templated variants. To mitigate this, we introduce a style neutralization preprocessing step using a secondary LLM to strip manipulative stylistic cues from user inputs, significantly reducing jailbreak success rates. Our findings reveal a systemic and scaling-resistant vulnerability overlooked in current safety pipelines. 1 1 1 Preprint 2 2 2 †Work conducted during tenure at Oracle. Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in socially and economically consequential settings such as education, healthcare and software engineering. Their widespread use has intensified concerns about jailbreak attacks adversarial prompts that bypass alignment safeguards and elicit harmful or restricted responses. Most existing jailbreak research focuses on semantic perturbations, such as paraphrasing, obfuscation or token-level manipulations, which conceal harmful intent while preserving original meaning. While such methods probe semantic robustness, they do not reflect the full diversity of natural language encountered in real-world interactions.

Recent evidence highlights the importance of this gap. Anthropic’s study
9
simulates corporate environments where LLMs are granted autonomous email access and benign business objectives. Under pressures such as potential job loss or goal organization conflicts, many models engage in harmful insider behaviors (e.g. blackmail, data leaks) despite acknowledging ethical constraints. This demonstrates that model behavior can be shaped by subtle contextual or social cues, underscoring the need to examine vulnerabilities beyond explicit semantic manipulations.

A complementary line of work explores persona- and role-based jailbreaks, which induce models to adopt specific identities or value systems that conflict with refusal policies. These approaches typically require multi-turn interactions or explicit role framing. In contrast, stylistic reframing operates at the surface level, without altering semantics or narrative structure. It leverages pragmatic cues such as politeness, fear or compassion that shape how models interpret user intent and modulate compliance behavior. These variations are lightweight, easy to automate, and effective in both single- and multi-turn black-box settings, expanding the adversarial space beyond semantic or persona-based strategies.

To study this systematically, we construct a style-augmented jailbreak benchmark by transforming harmful prompts from three public datasets into eleven linguistic styles using handcrafted templates and LLM-based rewrites. This approach simulates realistic user inputs while preserving semantic intent, enabling controlled evaluation of jailbreak robustness across diverse models. We assess 16 instruction-tuned models spanning both open- and close-source families, finding that stylistic reframing can increase jailbreak success. Contextualized rewrites consistently outperform templated ones and styles such as fearful, curious and compassionate are especially effective at bypassing safety guardrails.

Methodology

3.1
Positioning

Prior work has largely focused on semantic perturbations, such as paraphrasing and obfuscation and on persona- or role-based jailbreaks that induce harmful outputs by assigning models identities or narratives conflicting with refusal policies. While effective, these approaches operate at deeper semantic. In contrast, linguistic style-augmented jailbreaks exploit surface-level stylistic cues such as politeness, fear or curiosity without altering meaning. These lightweight, easily automated attacks broaden the adversarial space and mirror natural communication, making them practical and difficult to detect.

3.2
Proposed Framework

Our pipeline consists of three stages: (1) constructing a linguistic style-augmented benchmark from existing jailbreak datasets, (2) generating stylistic variants of harmful prompts using both template-based and LLM-based methods, and (3) evaluating model responses through a standardized judging protocol.

We quantify attack effectiveness using Attack Success Rate (ASR), defined as the proportion of responses deemed
Unethical

\text{ASR}=\frac{\#\{\text{responses labeled Unethical}\}}{\#\{\text{total prompts}\}}.

4.2
Analysis of Templated vs. Contextualized Stylistic Attacks

Overall effect of stylistic augmentation.

Both templated and contextualized stylistic rewrites consistently increase ASR relative to original baseline across all datasets and models. In several cases, ASR rises by more than 50 percentage points (PP), showing that at least one linguistic style can reliably bypass safety mechanisms where the original phrasing fails. This demonstrates that guardrails tuned to neutral phrasing are insufficient once stylistic variation is introduced. This behavior can be partly attributed to the data distributions underlying model pretraining and alignment, which are dominated by naturalistic text containing diverse pragmatic and stylistic cues. Models learn to associate certain stylistic framings such as politeness, curiosity or emotional vulnerability with cooperative or helpful responses. Since most alignment and safety tuning primarily targets semantically explicit harmful content in neutral phrasing, stylistic reframing can exploit these learned social priors, leading models to override refusal behavior while maintaining appearance of legitimate interaction.

4.3
Style-Wise Effects

To further disentangle the impact of stylistic variation, we compute AASR for each linguistic style across all models and datasets. We report two complementary metrics: (i) ASR (All), the overall rate of unsafe responses elicited by a given style and (ii) ASR (Unique vs. Neutral), the proportion of unsafe responses uniquely attributable to stylistic variation and not overlapping with the Original (neutral) prompts. This separation highlights which linguistic styles introduce new and distinct vulnerabilities beyond baseline jailbreak rates, rather than merely amplifying existing ones.

Takeaway.

Most impactful stylistic jailbreaks exploit compliance-oriented cues such as fear, compassion or curiosity, rather than overt hostility. This reveals a critical blind spot in current safety allignment that are tuned to block aggressive or semantically explicit jailbreaks but often fail against more naturalistic, trust building stylistic framings that subtly bypass defenses. By isolating linguistic style as an independent factor, our study demonstrates that stylistic manipulation beyond purely semantic content can systematically subvert alignment safeguards.

4.4
Mitigation

To mitigate jailbreak attacks that exploit variations in linguistic style, we propose a preprocessing mechanism that leverages a secondary large language model (GPT-4.1). Before user input is passed to the target LLM, it is first processed through a style neutralization stage, where GPT-4.1 reformulates input prompt into a semantically equivalent but stylistically neutral version using the neutralization prompt described in Appendix
B.4
. This preprocessing step removes stylistic cues and manipulative framing patterns that have been shown to trigger unintended behaviors or policy violations in LLMs. By standardizing linguistic style of incoming prompts, our method substantially reduces the likelihood of successful jailbreaks. Notably, reductions in ASR when evaluating neutralized prompts relative to original or stylistically varied versions provide causal evidence that stylistic factors themselves, rather than incidental paraphrasing effects, drive a significant portion of jailbreak success. This controls for potential confounds where rephrasing might inadvertently preserve the original style, ensuring that observed differences in ASR genuinely reflect the impact of linguistic style. Takeaway.

LLM judges are reliable on explicit harmful content but exhibit blind spots. While Qwen showed better alignment with human judgments on this qualitative set, GPT-4.1 was chosen as the primary judge to avoid evaluation bias. These findings highlight the importance of calibrating or combining judges when evaluating jailbreaks.

5
Conclusion

We introduced linguistic style as a novel and underexplored attack surface for large language models. Across three datasets and model families, we showed that stylistic variation whether introduced through templates or naturalistic contextualized rewrites significantly increases jailbreak success rates. Styles such as fearful, curious and compassionate are especially effective, revealing that socially compliant framings can bypass guardrails more reliably than overtly hostile phrasing.

Our results highlight that current alignment methods, which focus on semantic content, remain vulnerable to stylistic manipulation. Scaling alone does not provide sufficient protection. These findings suggest that linguistic style should be treated as one of the dimension in safety evaluation and model alignment. Integrating stylistic diversity into red-teaming pipelines and developing style-aware defenses will be critical for building more resilient, human-aligned language models.

6
Limitations

While our study highlights linguistic style as a potent and underexplored jailbreak vector, several limitations remain. First, we focus on single-turn interactions, whereas real-world attacks often unfold through multi-turn dialogues or role-play, potentially amplifying stylistic effects. Second, although our benchmark spans eleven styles, it does not cover complete spectrum of linguistic variation (e.g. sarcasm, humor, or mixed styles). Third, our style-neutralization mitigation illustrates feasibility but is not comprehensive; we do not address adaptive adversaries or alternative defenses such as adversarial training or style-aware refusal tuning. Fourth, evaluation relies on GPT-4.1 as the sole judge, which may introduce bias despite high human agreement; ensemble judging could improve robustness. Finally, our analysis is limited to textual jailbreaks, leaving multimodal settings where linguistic style may interact with visual or audio cues as an important avenue for future work.

7
Ethical Considerations

This work examines linguistic style as a jailbreak vector to better understand and mitigate vulnerabilities in large language models (LLMs). While the overarching objective is to advance model safety, we acknowledge that the techniques explored herein may be susceptible to dual-use risks, potentially enabling malicious actors to craft more effective jailbreak prompts. To minimize such risks, we refrain from releasing the full set of harmful generations and benchmark resources will be made available only to verified researchers upon request. Illustrative examples may contain offensive or harmful content; appropriate content warnings are provided in the abstract. We emphasize that the intent of this research is to support safer model deployment and inform the development of more robust defense mechanisms.

8
Future Work

Future work can extend this study to multi-turn interactions, where semantic manipulations and stylistic cues co-occur and evolve over dialogue. Real-world jailbreaks often unfold through gradual shifts in both meaning and linguistic style, potentially compounding vulnerabilities beyond single-turn settings. Another promising direction is to integrate RL-based automated red teaming, framing jailbreak generation as a sequential decision-making process. This would enable adaptive discovery of hybrid semantic stylistic attack strategies, bridging controlled benchmarks with realistic adversarial behaviors and supporting the development of dynamic, style-aware defenses.

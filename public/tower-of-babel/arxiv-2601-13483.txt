Gorenstein Special Fiber Rings of Ladder Determinantal Modules Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 13C40, 13A30, 13H10; Secondary 14M12, 14M15, 13C70. Thanks: Keyword: Multi-Rees algebra, determinantal, Gorenstein, Hibi ring
Louiza Fouli, Kuei-Nuan Lin, Haydee Lindo, Maral Mostafazadehfard

Abstract
. A ladder determinantal module is an arbitrary direct sum of ideals of maximal minors of a generic ladder matrix. In this article, we give necessary and sufficient conditions for the special fiber ring of such modules to be Gorenstein. These conditions are expressed in terms of data obtained from the underlying matrix.



1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring or a standard graded ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
. For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, the
Rees algebra
of
M
,
\mathcal{R}(M)
, is an algebra that naturally generalizes the notion of the Rees algebra of an ideal. It is defined as the quotient of the symmetric algebra of
M
by its torsion. Geometrically,
\mathcal{R}(M)
corresponds to the blowup of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
along
M
, which generalizes the notion of a blowup along an ideal. The Rees algebra of a module describes the closure of the graph of the rational map induced by
M
.
When
R
is local with residue field
{\mathbb{K}}
or
R
is a standard graded ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
one can consider another blowup algebra, namely, the special fiber ring of
M
, defined as
\mathcal{F}(M)\coloneq\mathcal{R}(M)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
. In other words, the special fiber ring of
M
is the algebra generated by the images of the generators of
M
over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Geometrically,
\mathcal{F}(M)

is the homogeneous coordinate ring of the exceptional fiber of the blowup of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
along
M
.

When a coordinate ring is Gorenstein, the associated algebraic variety has particularly nice duality properties, for example canonical sheaves become invertible,
[
1
, Chapter 3]
. This often implies that the variety has mild singularities and a well-behaved dualizing complex, properties that are essential in birational geometry and the minimal model program
[
6
, Chapter 21]
.

The Gorenstein property of special fiber rings has been extensively studied in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry.
Lima and Pérez
[
14
]
established necessary and sufficient conditions for the special fiber (fiber cone) of a Hilbert filtration to be Gorenstein, relating it to combinatorial invariants such as lengths and numbers of generators.
Earlier, Heinzer, Kim, and Ulrich
[
9
]
showed that in a Gorenstein ring
R
, under suitable hypotheses, the associated graded ring of an ideal
I
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{F}(I)
and
R/I
are both Gorenstein.
Further, Kustin, Polini, and Ulrich
[
13
]
analyzed special fiber rings of height-three Gorenstein ideals, demonstrating that these rings often inherit favorable duality and structural properties.

Our main object of study is the special fiber of ladder determinantal modules. A ladder determinantal module is a module that is the direct sum of finitely many copies of the ideal of maximal minors of a generic ladder matrix, that is a matrix with zeros and indeterminates such that the nonzero entries form a ladder shape, see
Section

2
for more details. Conca, Herzog, and Valla,
[
3
]
, studied the Rees and special fiber algebras of rational normal scrolls and determined the defining equations of these algebras. Moreover, by studying the symmetry of the
h
-vector, they identified necessary and sufficient conditions under which the special fiber ring of these rational normal scrolls is Gorenstein. Recent work has advanced our understanding of the special fiber ring of determinantal ideals: for ideals of
2\times 2
minors of sparse
2\times n
matrices Celikbas et al. showed that the defining equations of the special fiber ring are the expected (Plücker-type) relations and used this to deduce normality, Cohen–Macaulayness, and many related invariants for the special fiber ring,
[
2
]
.
Ramkumar and Sammartano proved that for several families of
2
-determinantal ideals the Rees algebra and the special fiber ring are Cohen–Macaulay and have quadratic (Koszul) presentations
[
19
]
.

Lin and Shen classified when the special fiber rings of secant varieties of rational normal scrolls are Gorenstein,
[
15
]
. Later,
same authors determined the defining equations of the Rees algebra and the special fiber ring of ladder determinantal modules,
[
16
, Theorem 5.5]
. Recently, Costantini and coauthors calculated various algebraic invariants of the special fiber algebra of a ladder determinantal module in
[
5
]
.

It turns out that the initial algebra of the special fiber ring of a ladder determinantal module is a Hibi ring
[
16
, Theorem 4.8]
. Via
Sagbi
deformation, we prove that one can reduce the study of the Gorenstein property for
\mathcal{F}(M)
to the study of the Gorenstein property for the Hibi ring arising from the initial algebra of the special fiber ring of the ideal of maximal minors of the given ladder matrix,
4.1
.

It is well known that a Hibi ring is Gorenstein if and only if the distributive lattice associated to the ring has a pure join-irreducible poset, that is a poset where all the maximal chains have the same length,
[
11
]
. Recently, Miyazaki showed that the homogeneous coordinate ring of a Schubert variety is an algebra with straightening law generated by a distributive lattice and gave a combinatorial description of when a Schubert cycle is Gorenstein using the join-irreducible poset of the Hibi ring,
[
17
]
. A Schubert cycle
corresponds to maximal minors of a one-sided ladder matrix. In our work, we consider two-sided ladder matrices and extend our study to special fiber rings of direct sums of ideals of maximal minors.

We now describe the main results of this work. In
Section

2
, we set up our notation and framework. In
Section

3
, we give an explicit description of the join-irreducible poset of the Hibi ring that is the initial algebra of the special fiber algebra of a ladder determinantal module,
3.6
.
More precisely, we associate the ladder matrix with the join-irreducible poset of the Hibi ring, and one can read off the join-irreducible elements from a given ladder matrix, see
3.6
and
3.13
for an instructive example.
Building on this straightforward connection, in
4.11
and
4.12
we are able to provide necessary and sufficient conditions in terms of the matrix structure for the special fiber ring of ladder determinantal modules to be Gorenstein.
As an application of our results we obtain explicit calculations of the regularity, reduction number, and
a
-invariant of these algebras when they are Gorenstein,
4.14
.
Finally, we consider the question whether these special fiber rings we considered are
F
-regular. When
\mathcal{F}(M)
is Gorenstein it follows that it is
F
-regular,
4.15
. We conclude this work with an example showing that even without the Gorenstein property, one can still show that the special fiber ring of an ideal of maximal minors of a ladder matrix is
F
-regular,
4.16
.

We note here that the ideals we consider are ideals of maximal minors of a ladder matrix and they are in general different than ladder determinantal ideals considered for example in
[
4
]
. Moreover, the special fiber rings we consider are not in general quotients of polynomial rings by determinantal ideals.

2.
Preliminaries and notations

We adopt the convention that if
p
and
q
are two positive integers with
p\leq q
, the interval
[p,q]
denotes the set
\{p,p+1,\dots,q-1,q\}
. When
p=1
, we denote the interval
[1,q]
by
[q]
.
We will use the notation in the following definition throughout the article.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field and let
m,n,r
be positive integers with
n<m
. Write
[m]=S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup\cdots\cup S_{n}
, where for each
i\in[n]
,
S_{i}\coloneqq[u_{i},v_{i}]
for some
u_{i},v_{i}
positive integers with
u_{i}<v_{i}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
1=u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots<u_{n}<m
and
1<v_{1}<v_{2}<\cdots<v_{n}=m
.
For each
i\in[n]
and each
j\in S_{i}
, let
x_{i,j}
be an indeterminate over the field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Let
\mathbb{S}\coloneq\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}\}
.

(1)

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
be the
n\times m
matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is
x_{i,j}
when
j\in S_{i}
and
0
otherwise. We say that
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
is the
ladder matrix
associated to
\mathbb{S}
.

(2)

Let
R\coloneq{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}]={\mathbb{K}}[x_{i,j}:i\in[n],j\in S_{i}]
be a polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{K}}
and
L\coloneq I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}})
be the
R
-ideal generated by the
n\times n
(maximal) minors of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
.

(3)

Let
\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\{{\bm{c}}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{n}:c_{i}<c_{i+1}\mbox{ for all }i\in[n-1]\}
. Notice that by the ladder shape of
{\bm{X}}_{\mathbb{S}}
we have
\det[{\bm{c}}]\neq 0
for any
{\bm{c}}\in\mathcal{L}
, where
\det[{\bm{c}}]
is the determinant of the
n\times n
submatrix of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
whose columns are
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
. Therefore, the set
\{\det[{\bm{c}}]:{\bm{c}}\in\mathcal{L}\}
is a minimal generating set of the ideal
L
.

(4)

The module
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}L
is called a
ladder determinantal module
associated to the matrix
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
. Let
\mathcal{L}\times[r]\coloneqq\{({\bm{c}},j):{\bm{c}}\in\mathcal{L},j\in[r]\}
be the cartesian product of
\mathcal{L}
and
[r]
and notice that the set
\{(\det[{\bm{c}}],j):({\bm{c}},j)\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]\}
is a minimal generating set for
M
.

(5)

Let
\tau
be the lexicographic order on the ring
R
induced by the lexicographic order on the entries in
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
, that is
x_{i,j}>_{\tau}x_{l,k}
if and only if
i<l
or
i=l
and
j<k
.

(6)

Let
N=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(L)
be the direct sum of the initial ideal of
L
with respect to
\tau
.

(7)

For all
i\in[n]
and all
j\in[n-1]
, let
\Delta_{i}\coloneq v_{i}-u_{i}
,
\epsilon_{j}\coloneq u_{j+1}-u_{j}
, and
\theta_{j}\coloneq v_{j+1}-v_{j}
. We adopt the convention that
\epsilon_{n}=\theta_{0}=\infty
.

We examine the blowup algebras associated with the module
M=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}L
.
The
Rees algebra
,
\mathcal{R}(M)
, and the
special fiber ring
,
\mathcal{F}(M)
, of such a module
M
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(M)

\displaystyle\coloneqq R[Lt_{1},\ldots,Lt_{r}]

\displaystyle\,=\bigoplus_{a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}L^{\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}}t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{a_{r}}\,\subseteq R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}],

\displaystyle\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\mathcal{F}(M)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{R}(M)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}\cong{\mathbb{K}}[\det[{\bm{c}}]t_{j}:({\bm{c}},j)\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]].

One can realize the special fiber ring as a quotient of a polynomial ring in the following way.
Let
\{T_{{\bm{c}},j}:({\bm{c}},j)\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]\}
be a new collection of indeterminates over
{\mathbb{K}}
and let
T\coloneq{\mathbb{K}}[\{T_{{\bm{c}},j}:({\bm{c}},j)\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]\}]
. We define the following surjective homomorphism:

\displaystyle\psi:T\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(M),

determined by
\psi(T_{{\bm{c}},j})=\det[{\bm{c}}]t_{j}
for each
({\bm{c}},j)\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]
.
Then
\mathcal{F}(M)\cong T/\operatorname{ker}(\psi)
, and the
The ideal
\operatorname{ker}(\psi)
is called the
defining
or
presentation ideal
of
\mathcal{F}(M)
.

In order to study the Gorenstein property of
\mathcal{F}(M)
we use the method of
Sagbi
degeneration.
Recall that for a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra
A
, a set of elements
\mathcal{B}\subseteq A
is called a
Sagbi
basis for
A
, with respect to a monomial order
>
, if
\operatorname{in}_{>}(A)={\mathbb{K}}[\operatorname{in}_{>}(\mathcal{B})]
. In the case of the ladder determinantal modules we are considering, Lin and Shen proved that the natural generators of
M
form a
Sagbi
basis for
\mathcal{F}(M)
and used this
Sagbi
basis to determine the defining equations of
\mathcal{F}(M)
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
16
, Theorem 4.8. 5.5, Corollary 4.10. 5.7]
)
.

Let
M
and
N
be as in
2.1
.

(1)

The set
\{\det[{\bm{c}}]t_{i}:({\bm{c}},i)\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]\}
is a
Sagbi
basis for
\mathcal{F}(M)
with respect to an extended monomial order
\tau^{\prime}
, that is
\operatorname{in}_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}(M))=\mathcal{F}(N)
. Moreover, there exists a monomial order
\omega
on
T
such that
\operatorname{in}_{\omega}(\mathcal{K})=\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{J})
, where
\mathcal{K},\mathcal{J}
are the defining ideals of
\mathcal{F}(M)
and
\mathcal{F}(N)
, respectively.

(2)

The
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra
\mathcal{F}(N)
is a Hibi ring and is isomorphic to
T/\mathcal{J}
where

\quad\mathcal{J}=(T_{{\bm{a}}}T_{{\bm{b}}}-T_{{\bm{a}}\wedge{\bm{b}}}T_{{\bm{a}}\vee{\bm{b}}}:{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]).

(3)

\mathcal{F}(M)
and
\mathcal{F}(N)
are both Cohen–Macaulay normal domains.

We note here that
\mathcal{F}(N)
is a Hibi ring associated to the distributive lattice
\mathcal{L}\times[r]
. Indeed, the set
\mathcal{L}\times[r]
admits a partial order defined by

({\bm{a}},j_{1})=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},j_{1})\geq({\bm{b}},j_{2})=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n},j_{2})

if and only if

a_{i}\geq b_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[n]\text{ and }j_{1}\geq j_{2},
or equivalently,
[({\bm{a}},j_{1})]_{i}\geq[({\bm{b}},j_{2})]_{i}
for all
i\in[n+1]
, where the notation
[-]_{i}
is used to denote the
i
-th entry of the corresponding tuple.
Under this partial ordering,
\mathcal{L}\times[r]
becomes a distributive lattice with meet and join defined by:

{\bm{c}}\wedge{\bm{d}}=\min\{{\bm{c}},{\bm{d}}\}\ \mbox{ and }{\bm{c}}\vee{\bm{d}}=\max\{{\bm{c}},{\bm{d}}\}\mbox{ for any }{\bm{c}},{\bm{d}}\in\mathcal{L}\times[r],

where the minimum and maximum of two tuples are the vectors of the component wise minimum and maximum, respectively. In other words, for
{\bm{c}},{\bm{d}}\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]
we have
[{\bm{c}}\wedge{\bm{d}}]_{i}=\min\{[{\bm{c}}]_{i},[{\bm{d}}]_{i}\}
and
[{\bm{c}}\vee{\bm{d}}]_{i}=\max\{[{\bm{c}}]_{i},[{\bm{d}}]_{i}\}
for all
i\in[n+1]
.

We close this section with the following remark.

Remark 2.3
.

For all
i\in[2,n]
, let
S_{i}^{\prime}=S_{i}
if
u_{i-1}<u_{i}
, and let
S_{i}^{\prime}=[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]
whenever
u_{i-1}=u_{i}
.
Let
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}=\{S_{1},S_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,S_{n}^{\prime}\}
, and let
{\mathbf{X}}_{\mathbb{S}^{\prime}}
be the ladder matrix corresponding to
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}
.
Notice that
\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}))=\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}^{\prime}}))
.
Furthermore, since
\tau
is a diagonal monomial order, we may assume that
u_{i}\leq v_{i-1}+1
for all
i\in[2,n]
.
Hence,
we may assume without loss of generality that
u_{i}<u_{i+1}
and
v_{i}<v_{i+1}
as in
2.1
. If
u_{i}=v_{i}
for some
i\in[n]
, then
L=x_{i,u_{i}}I_{n-1}(\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{S}})
, where
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{S}}
is the ladder matrix obtained from
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
after removing row
i
. Note that
\mathcal{F}(M)\cong\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{n-1}(\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{S}})
and thus we may assume without loss of generality that
u_{i}<v_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
as in
2.1
. Moreover, under our assumptions we cover the case of a generic matrix by considering
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
with
\epsilon_{i}=1
and
\theta_{i}=1
for all
i\in[n-1]
. Finally, if
n=m
, then
\mathcal{F}(M)
is a polynomial ring and there is nothing to prove in this case. Therefore we may assume that
n<m
.

3.
The join-irreducible poset

Through this article we will assume the notation as in
2.1
. We begin by introducing a list of tuples that we will use to form the collection of join-irreducible elements of the poset
\mathcal{L}\times[r]
.

Definition 3.1
.

For each
i\in[n]
and each
j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]
set

{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\coloneq(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{i-1},j,\max\{j+1,u_{i+1}\},\ldots,\max\{j+(n-i),u_{n}\}).

For all
k\in[2,r]
define

{\bm{a}}_{n+1,k}\coloneq(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},k).

Remark 3.2
.

By construction, each
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
is determined by its
i
-th component
[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{i}
and for fixed
i
we have
|\{{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\}_{j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]}|=\Delta_{i}
and
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\in\mathcal{L}
. Indeed, for any
s\in[0,n-i]
if

[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{i+s}=\max\{j+s,u_{i+s}\}=j+s,

then

u_{i+s}\leq j+s\leq v_{i}+s\leq v_{i+s}
, where the last inequality follows by the ladder structure of the matrix. Therefore,
u_{i+s}\leq[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{i+s}\leq v_{i+s}
implying
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
P
be any poset and
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in P
, with
{\bm{b}}<{\bm{a}}
. We say that
{\bm{a}}

covers

{\bm{b}}
and write
{\bm{b}}\lessdot{\bm{a}}
when there is no
{\bm{c}}\in P
with
{\bm{b}}<{\bm{c}}<{\bm{a}}
.

Remark 3.4
.

A join-irreducible element of a poset
P
is any element that covers exactly one other element in
P
. Our goal is to determine the join-irreducible elements of
\mathcal{L}
and to understand the poset structure of the poset of join-irreducible elements of
\mathcal{L}\times[r]
and
\mathcal{L}
.
Note that when restricting to the set of join-irreducible elements, a join-irreducible element may cover several other join-irreducible elements; see
3.8
.

The following result establishes that the elements we defined in
3.1
are in fact the join-irreducible elements of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

The set

P_{\mathcal{L}}:=\{{\bm{a}}_{i,j}:i\in[n],j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]\}

is the set of join-irreducible elements of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Let
P
be the set of join-irreducible elements of
\mathcal{L}
. We claim that
P=P_{\mathcal{L}}
. By
[
5
, Corollary 4.24]
it follows that
|P|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}
and since
|P_{\mathcal{L}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}
, the result follows once we show
P_{\mathcal{L}}\subseteq P
.

We claim that for any
{\bm{c}}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathcal{L}
, if
{\bm{c}}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
, then
[{\bm{c}}]_{i}<[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{i}=j
. Indeed, for any
t\in[1,i-1]
we have
[{\bm{c}}]_{t}=c_{t}=u_{t}=[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{t}
. Since
{\bm{c}}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
, there exists
s\geq i
such that
[{\bm{c}}]_{s}=c_{s}<[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{s}
and
[{\bm{c}}]_{t}=[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{t}
for all
t\leq s-1
. If
s>i
, then by the construction of
\mathcal{L}
we have
u_{s}\leq[{\bm{c}}]_{s}<[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{s}=\max\{j+s-i,u_{s}\}=j+s-i
. Moreover, by
3.1
we have
[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{s-1}=\max\{j+s-i-1,u_{s-1}\}=j+s-i-1
. Then

[{\bm{c}}]_{s-1}=[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{s-1}=j+s-i-1<[{\bm{c}}]_{s}<[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{s}=j+s-i

which is impossible. Therefore,
s=i
and
[{\bm{c}}]_{i}<[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{i}=j
.

Now define

{\bm{b}}_{i,j}\coloneq(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},\,j-1,\,\max\{j+1,u_{i+1}\},\ldots,\max\{j+(n-i),u_{n}\}).

Then
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
,
{\bm{b}}_{i,j}\in\mathcal{L}
and
{\bm{b}}_{i,j}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
. If
{\bm{c}}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
, then by the argument above we have
[{\bm{c}}]_{t}=[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{t}=[{\bm{b}}_{i,j}]_{t}
for all
t\in[i-1]
and
[{\bm{c}}]_{i}<[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{i}=j
. Hence
[{\bm{c}}]_{i}\leq[{\bm{b}}_{i,j}]_{i}
. Moreover,
[{\bm{c}}]_{t}\leq[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{t}=[{\bm{b}}_{i,j}]_{t}
for all

t\neq i
. In particular,
{\bm{c}}\leq{\bm{b}}_{i,j}
and therefore,
{\bm{b}}_{i,j}\lessdot{\bm{a}}_{i,j}

, that is
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
is a join-irreducible element of
\mathcal{L}
as claimed.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

The join-irreducible elements of
\mathcal{L}\times[r]
are precisely the elements

P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}:=\{({\bm{a}}_{i,j},1):i\in[n],j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]\}\cup\{{\bm{a}}_{n+1,k}:k\in[2,r]\}.

Proof.

Let
P
be the set of join-irreducible element of
\mathcal{L}\times[r]
. By
[
5
, Corollary 4.24]
we have
|P|=|P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}|
and as before it suffices to show that
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}\subseteq P
. Let
({\bm{c}},k)\in\mathcal{L}\times[r]
. If
k=1
, then
({\bm{c}},1)\in P
if and only if
{\bm{c}}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
. If
k\geq 2
, then
({\bm{c}},k-1)\lessdot({\bm{c}},k)
and
({\bm{d}},k)\lessdot({\bm{c}},k)
for any
{\bm{d}},{\bm{c}}\in\mathcal{L}
with
{\bm{d}}\lessdot{\bm{c}}
. Therefore when
k\geq 2
,
({\bm{c}},k)\in P
if and only if there does not exist
{\bm{d}}\in\mathcal{L}
with
{\bm{d}}\lessdot{\bm{c}}
. The latter means that
{\bm{c}}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
and the result follows.
∎

Having determined the elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
or equivalently the elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
, we turn our attention to the poset structure of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
{\bm{a}}_{i,j},{\bm{a}}_{k,l}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
. Then
{\bm{a}}_{k,l}\leq{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
if and only if
k\geq i
and
l-j\leq k-i
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\bm{a}}_{k,l}\leq{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
. By
3.1
, we must have
k\geq i

and
[{\bm{a}}_{k,l}]_{t}\leq[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{t}
for all
t\in[n]
.
In particular,

u_{k}<l=[{\bm{a}}_{k,l}]_{k}\leq[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{k}=\max\{j+k-i,\,u_{k}\},

which implies
l-j\leq k-i
.

Conversely, suppose
k\geq i
and
l-j\leq k-i
.
For any
t<k
, we have

[{\bm{a}}_{k,l}]_{t}=u_{t}\leq[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{t}
.
For any
t\geq k
, we obtain

[{\bm{a}}_{k,l}]_{t}=\max\{\,l+(t-k),\,u_{t}\}\leq\max\{\,j+(t-i),\,u_{t}\}=[{\bm{a}}_{i,j}]_{t}

and hence
{\bm{a}}_{k,l}\leq{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
.
∎

For any element of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
, we determine which elements it covers.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
, then the only elements
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
covers are the elements in the set

\{{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1},{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}\},

which may be the empty set.
Moreover,

{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
if and only if
j\geq u_{i}+2
and
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
if and only if
j\geq u_{i+1}
and
i\neq n
.

Proof.

First notice that since
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
, then
i\in[n]
and
j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]
. Hence by
3.1

{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
if and only if
j-1\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]
and the latter is equivalent to
j\geq u_{i}+2
, since
j\leq v_{i}
. Similarly,
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
if and only if
i+1\in[n]
and
j+1\in[u_{i+1}+1,v_{i+1}]
. Since
i\in[n]
and
j\leq v_{i}<v_{i+1}
we can conclude that
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
if and only if
i\neq n
and
j\geq u_{i+1}
.
By
3.7
, if
{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
, then
{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
and similarly, if
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
, then
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
.

Now suppose that
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
covers
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
. Since
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
, then
g\geq i
and
h-j\leq g-i
,
3.7
. If
g=i
, then
h<j
, since
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
. Thus
h\leq j-1<j
and since
{\bm{a}}_{g,h},{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
, then
{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}
exists and
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}\leq{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}
, again by
3.7
. Therefore,
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}={\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}
, since
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
covers
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}
.

If
g>i
, then we claim that
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}
exists and
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}={\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}
. If
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}
does not exist, then
j<u_{i}+1
and thus
h<u_{i}+1+g-i\leq u_{g}+1
. This means
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}
does not exist, a contradiction to our assumption that
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
covers
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
. Thus
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}
exists and since
h-j\leq g-i=1+g-(i+1)
, it follows that
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}\leq{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}
,
3.7
. Hence
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}={\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}
.

Therefore,
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
covers an element of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
if and only if
\{{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1},{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}\}\neq\emptyset
. Moreover,
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
covers only the join-irreducible elements in
\{{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1},{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}\}
, when the set is not empty.
∎

We remark here that
3.8
implies that if neither
{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}
and
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}
exist, then
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
does not cover any elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
.
Our next goal is to understand the connected components of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
. We introduce the following notation.

Notation 3.9
.

Consider the set

\mathcal{C}=\{i\in[n-1]:v_{i}<u_{i+1}\}\cup\{n\}=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{t-1},q_{t}=n:q_{i}<q_{i+1}\text{ for all }i\in[n-1]\}.

Notice that
i\in\mathcal{C}
if and only if
u_{i+1}=v_{i}+1
for all
i\in[n-1]
by
2.3
.
We partition
[n]
as follows

[n]=[1,q_{1}]\cup[p_{2},q_{2}]\cup\cdots\cup[p_{t},q_{t}],

where
p_{i+1}=q_{i}+1
for all
i\in[t-1]
.
For simplicity let
p_{1}=1
.
Notice that with these assumptions
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
is a block matrix with
t
blocks
\mathbf{X}_{i}
, where
\mathbf{X}_{i}
is a ladder matrix in the rows
p_{i}
to
q_{i}
:

\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{X}_{1}&&&\\
&\mathbf{X}_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\mathbf{X}_{t}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

We now determine the connected components of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

The lattice
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
is connected if and only if
r=1
and
|\mathcal{C}|=1
.
In particular,
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
has
|\mathcal{C}|
connected components when
r=1
and
|\mathcal{C}|+1
connected components when
r>1
.

Proof.

Recall that
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}=\{({\bm{a}}_{i,j},1):i\in[n],j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]\}\cup\{{\bm{a}}_{n+1,k}:k\in[2,r]\}
,
3.6
. Let
\mathcal{C}=\{i\in[n-1]:v_{i}<u_{i+1}\}\cup\{n\}=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{t-1},q_{t}=n:q_{i}<q_{i+1}\text{ for all }i\in[n-1]\}
be as in
3.9
. For every
s\in[t]
let
A_{s}\coloneq\{({\bm{a}}_{i,j},1):i\in[p_{s},q_{s}],j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]\}
.
Let
B=\{{\bm{a}}_{n+1,k}:k\in[2,r]\}
, which exists if and only if
r\geq 2
. We claim that
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t},B
are the connected components of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
when
r\geq 2
.
In fact,
A_{s}
is an isomorphic copy of the poset corresponding to the
s
-th block of
X_{\mathbb{S}}
.

Note that
B\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}A_{i}=P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
.
Observe that
({\bm{a}}_{i,j},1)
is incomparable to
{\bm{a}}_{n+1,k}
for any
i\in[n]
,
j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]
and
k\in[2,r]
. Moreover,
B
is a connected component of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
since
{\bm{a}}_{n+1,k}<{\bm{a}}_{n+1,k+1}
for all
k\in[2,r-1]
.

Let
s\in[t]
and let
i\in[p_{s},q_{s}]
. By
3.7
the set
\{({\bm{a}}_{i,j},1):j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]\}
is connected. If
i<q_{s}
, then

v_{i}\geq u_{i+1}
. Hence
({\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}},1)>({\bm{a}}_{i+1,v_{i}+1},1)
by
3.8
. Therefore,
A_{s}
is connected.

We claim that for any
s,s^{\prime}\in[t]
with
s\neq s^{\prime}
the elements of
A_{s}
and
A_{s^{\prime}}
are incomparable. Indeed, let
({\bm{a}}_{i,j},1)\in A_{s}
and
({\bm{a}}_{g,h},1)\in A_{s^{\prime}}
and without loss of generality assume
s^{\prime}>s
. Then
g>i
and
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}\not>{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
. Since

j\leq v_{i}\leq v_{q_{s}}<u_{q_{s}+1}=u_{p_{s+1}}\leq u_{g}<h,

it follows that
h-j>u_{g}-v_{i}\geq g-p_{s+1}+1+q_{s}-i=g-i
, as
p_{s+1}=q_{s}+1
,
u_{k}-u_{l}\geq k-l
and
v_{k}-v_{l}\geq k-l
for any
k>l
. Thus,
{\bm{a}}_{g,h}\not<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
and hence
({\bm{a}}_{i,j},1),({\bm{a}}_{g,h},1)
are incomparable by
3.7
as claimed. Therefore,
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t},B
are the connected components of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
. In the case
r=1
, one can show as above that the sets
A^{\prime}_{s}=\{{\bm{a}}_{i,j}:i\in[p_{s},q_{s}],j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]\}
are the connected components of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
for all
s\in[t]
.
∎

In our final result of this section we determine the minimal and maximal elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Proposition 3.11
.

The minimal elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
are

\{{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1}:\epsilon_{i}>1\}

and the maximal elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
are

\{{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}}:\theta_{i-1}>1\}.

Proof.

By
3.8
,
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
covers only the elements
\{{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1},{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}\}
in
P_{\mathcal{L}}
, when they exist. It follows that
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
is minimal if and only if this set is empty, that is, when

j=u_{i}+1
and
u_{i}+1<u_{i+1}
or
i=n
. The latter is equivalent to
j=u_{i}+1
and
\epsilon_{i}>1
, since
\epsilon_{n}>1
by convention.

Similarly,
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
is maximal if and only if
\{{\bm{a}}_{i,j+1},{\bm{a}}_{i-1,j-1}\}=\emptyset
.
Note, since
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
, then
{\bm{a}}_{i,j+1}
does not exist if and only if
j=v_{i}
. Moreover,
{\bm{a}}_{i-1,v_{i}-1}
does not exist if and only if either
i=1
or
v_{i}-1\not\in[u_{i-1}+1,v_{i-1}]
.
The latter is equivalent to either
i=1
or
v_{i}-1>v_{i-1}
.
Therefore
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
is maximal if and only if
j=v_{i}
and
\theta_{i-1}>1
, since by convention
\theta_{0}>1
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

One may associate several combinatorial objects with
P_{\mathcal{L}}
. We first define a two-sided ladder matrix
\mathbf{X}_{P_{\mathcal{L}}}
of the same size as
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
as follows: for any
i\in[n]
and any
j\in[m]
the
(i,j)
-th entry of
\mathbf{X}_{P_{\mathcal{L}}}
is
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
if
j\in[u_{i}+1,v_{i}]
and zero otherwise. The nonzero entries of
\mathbf{X}_{P_{\mathcal{L}}}
are precisely the join-irreducible elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
. By
3.8
,
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
covers only the elements in positions
(i,j-1)
and
(i+1,j+1)
provided they are nonzero. Thus the poset structure of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
can be identified directly from
\mathbf{X}_{P_{\mathcal{L}}}
, and therefore directly from
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
.
Note that the difference between the ladder structures of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
and
\mathbf{X}_{P_{\mathcal{L}}}
lie only in the
(i,u_{i})
entries; consequently,
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
and
\mathbf{X}_{P_{\mathcal{L}}}
share the same values of
\epsilon_{i}
and
\theta_{i}
. One can also see that
P_{\mathcal{L}}
can be associated to a skew Young diagram. Recently, Gasanova and Nicklasson associated a skew Young diagram to the join-irreducible poset of certain Hibi rings arising from planar lattices to compute the
h
-vectors of these rings

]
.

4.
The Gorenstein property

In
Section

3
we described the structure of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
explicitly. Our goal in this section is to provide necessary and sufficient conditions for
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
to be pure or equivalently for
\mathcal{F}(M)
to be Gorenstein.

Recall that a subset
\mathcal{A}
of a poset
P
is called a

chain
of
P
if
\mathcal{A}
is a totally ordered subset with respect to the induced order. In other
words, a chain is a subset
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
of
P
with
a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}
.
The
length
of a chain
\mathcal{A}
is
|\mathcal{A}|-1
. Let
\operatorname{rank}(P)
denote the rank of
P
, which is the length of the longest chain of
P
. We also recall that for a standard graded algebra
A
over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, the Hilbert function
H_{A}(t)
counts the dimension of the
t
-th graded component of
A
. The Hilbert series is given by

H_{A}(t)\;=\;\frac{h(t)}{(1-t)^{d}},

where
d
is the Krull dimension of
A
, and

h(t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}

is a polynomial with integer coefficients. The
h
-vector of
A
is denoted by

h(A)=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}).

We first show that it suffices to work with
\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(L))
which is a Hibi ring,
2.2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
r\geq 2
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{F}(M)
is Gorenstein;

(2)

\mathcal{F}(N)
is Gorenstein;

(3)

\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(L))
is Gorenstein and
\operatorname{rank}(P_{\mathcal{L}})=r-2
;

(4)

\mathcal{F}(L)
is Gorenstein and
\operatorname{rank}(P_{\mathcal{L}})=r-2
.

Proof.

Recall that a graded algebra is Gorenstein if and only if it is a Cohen-Macaulay domain and its
h
-vector is symmetric, see
[
1
, Corollary 4.4.6]
.

By
2.2
we know that
\operatorname{in}_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}(M))\cong\mathcal{F}(N)
, where
N=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(L)
.
Then by
[
3
, Proposition 2.4]
, the Hilbert functions of
\mathcal{F}(M)
and
\mathcal{F}(N)
coincide and hence
\mathcal{F}(M)
and
\mathcal{F}(N)
have the same
h
-vector. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}(M)
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{F}(N)
is Gorenstein, since
\mathcal{F}(M)
and
\mathcal{F}(N)
are Cohen-Macaulay domains,
2.2
.
Moreover, by
2.2
,
\mathcal{F}(N)
is the Hibi ring of the distributive lattice
\mathcal{L}\times[r]
. Thus,
\mathcal{F}(N)
is Gorenstein if and only if the poset of join-irreducible elements

P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
is pure,
[
11
]
. The latter holds if and only if all the maximal chains of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
have the same length.

Finally, that by the proof of
3.10
the maximal chains of
P_{\mathcal{L}\times[r]}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the maximal chains of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
along with the chain given by
\{{\bm{a}}_{n+1,k}\}_{k=2}^{r}
, which has length
r-2
. Now the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Notice that, by
4.1
, even if
\mathcal{F}(L)
is Gorenstein, the algebra
\mathcal{F}(M)
can only be Gorenstein for one value of
r
. On the other hand, for a fixed
r
, the condition that
\mathcal{F}(M)
is Gorenstein imposes restrictions on the shape of the ladder matrix, as we will show in
4.12
.

In light of
4.1
, we can now focus on determining necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(L))
to be Gorenstein. In the next result we compute the length of any saturated chain between any two comparable elements in
P_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Any saturated chain between two join-irreducible elements
{\bm{a}}_{i,j},{\bm{a}}_{k,l}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
with
{\bm{a}}_{k,l}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
has length
2(k-i)+j-l
.

Proof.

Let
{\bm{a}}_{i,j},{\bm{a}}_{k,l}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
such that
{\bm{a}}_{k,l}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
.
Then by
3.7
,
k\geq i
. Notice that by
3.8
, we have
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
and
{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
as long as
{\bm{a}}_{i+1,j+1}
and
{\bm{a}}_{i,j-1}
exist. Therefore, the difference in the index between two consecutive elements in a saturated chain is either
(1,1)
or
(0,-1)
.
Since
{\bm{a}}_{k,l}<{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
we must have

(k,l)=(i,j)+a(1,1)+b(0,-1)

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
.
The unique solution is
a=(k-i)
and
b=(k-i)+(j-l)
. Thus the length of any such saturated chain from
{\bm{a}}_{k,l}
to
{\bm{a}}_{i,j}
is
a+b=2(k-i)+(j-l).
∎

In order to determine the structure of saturated chains in
P_{\mathcal{L}}
we introduce the notion of consecutive minima and maxima.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1},{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}
be two minimal elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
as in
3.11
. We say that
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1}
and
{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}
with
i<k
are
consecutive minima
there is no other minimal element
{\bm{a}}_{t,u_{t}+1}
of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
with
i<t<k
. In particular, if
i<k
and
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1}
,
{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}
are consecutive minima, then for all
s\in[i+1,k-1]
we have
\epsilon_{s}=1
. Similarly, we can define
consecutive maxima
.

With the definition of consecutive minima and maxima we are now able to show how maximal chains are related in
P_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
v_{i}\geq u_{i+1}
for all
i\in[n-1]
. Then for any two consecutive minimal elements in
P_{\mathcal{L}}
there exist saturated chains to the same maximal element of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
.
Similarly, for any two consecutive maximal elements in
P_{\mathcal{L}}
there exist saturated chains to the same minimal element of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Proof.

Let
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1},{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
be two consecutive minimal elements. Without loss of generality we may assume
i<k
and that for all
s\in[i+1,k-1]
we have
\epsilon_{s}=1
.

Notice first that
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1}\leq{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}}
, by
3.7
. Moreover, since
\epsilon_{s}=1
for all
s\in[i+1,k-1]
we have that
u_{k}=u_{i+1}+(k-(i+1))=u_{i+1}+(k-i)-1
. Hence,
u_{k}+1\leq v_{i}+k-i
, since
u_{i+1}\leq v_{i}
. Therefore,
{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}<{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}}
, by
3.7
. Finally, since
{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}}
is larger than both
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1}
,
{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}
, then there exists a maximum that is the common end of a chain from each one of the minimal elements. Symmetrically, for two consecutive maximal elements
{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}},{\bm{a}}_{k,v_{k}}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
one can show that
{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}\leq{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}}
and
{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}\leq{\bm{a}}_{k,v_{k}}
.
∎

We now give necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{F}(L)
to be Gorenstein, provided that
P_{\mathcal{L}}
is connected. Recall that
P_{\mathcal{L}}
is connected if and only if
|\mathcal{C}|=1
,
or equivalently,
v_{i}\geq u_{i+1}
for all
i\in[n-1]
,
3.10
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
v_{i}\geq u_{i+1}
for all
i\in[n-1]
. Then

\mathcal{F}(L)
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

u_{k}-u_{i}=2(k-i)

whenever
\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{k}>1
;

(2)

v_{k}-v_{i}=2(k-i)
whenever
\theta_{i-1},\theta_{k-1}>1
.

Proof.

Notice that by
3.11
whenever
\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{k}>1
, then
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1},{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}
are minimal elements in
P_{\mathcal{L}}
. Similarly,
{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}},{\bm{a}}_{k,v_{k}}
are maximal elements of
P_{\mathcal{L}}
whenever
\theta_{i-1},\theta_{k-1}>1
.

Suppose first that
\mathcal{F}(L)
is Gorenstein. Then
by the proof of
4.1

\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(L)
is Gorenstein and thus
P_{\mathcal{L}}
is pure,
[
11
]
. Hence all the maximal chains in
P_{\mathcal{L}}
have the same length.
Let
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1},{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
be any two consecutive minimal elements. Then by
4.5
, there exists a maximal element
{\bm{a}}_{h,v_{h}}
with
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1}\leq{\bm{a}}_{h,v_{h}}
and
{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}\leq{\bm{a}}_{h,v_{h}}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
h\leq i<k
. Then by
4.3
, we have

2(i-h)+v_{h}-(u_{i}+1)=2(k-h)+v_{h}-(u_{k}+1)

or equivalently
(u_{k}-u_{i})=2(k-i)
. Similarly, using
4.5
, for any two consecutive maximal elements
{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}},{\bm{a}}_{k,v_{k}}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
we have
(v_{k}-v_{i})=2(k-i)
.

Now for any pair of minima
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i}+1},{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
we let
j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}
be the indices of minimal elements between
i
and
k
. Then we have

(u_{k}-u_{i})=u_{k}-u_{j_{1}}+u_{j_{1}}-u_{j_{2}}+\cdots+u_{j_{s}}-u_{i}=2[(k-j_{1})+(j_{1}-j_{2})+\cdots+(j_{s}-i)]=2(k-i).

Similarly,
(v_{k}-v_{i})=2(k-i)
for every pair of maxima
{\bm{a}}_{i,v_{i}},{\bm{a}}_{k,v_{k}}\in P_{\mathcal{L}}
.

For the converse, suppose now that conditions (a) and (b) hold. Consider two maximal chains from
{\bm{a}}_{i,u_{i+1}}
to
{\bm{a}}_{g,v_{g}}
and from
{\bm{a}}_{k,u_{k}+1}
to
{\bm{a}}_{h,v_{h}}
. The lengths of these chains are equal when
2(i-g)+v_{g}-u_{i}-1=2(k-h)+v_{h}-u_{k}-1
,
4.3
. This is equivalent to

2(i-k)+v_{g}-v_{h}=2(g-h)+u_{i}-u_{k},

which holds by conditions (a) and (b).
∎

Remark 4.7
.

4.6
extends the result in
[
2
, Corollary 4.8]
, as we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{F}(L)
to be Gorenstein, when
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
is a sparse
2\times n
matrix as a sparse matrix can be rearranged into a ladder matrix as shown in
[
2
, Corollary 4.8]
.
If
\mathbf{X}
is a generic
n\times m
matrix, then
\mathcal{F}(I_{n}(\mathbf{X}))
is Gorenstein by the fact that

\operatorname{in}_{\tau}\!\left(I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}})\right)=\operatorname{in}_{\tau}\!\left(I_{n}(\mathbf{X})\right),

where
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
is an
n\times m
ladder matrix with
\epsilon_{i}=\theta_{j}=1
for all
i\in[n-1],j\in[n]
and
\epsilon_{n},\theta_{0}>1
. Note since
m>n
, we have
v_{i}\geq u_{i+1}
for all
i\in[n-1]
.
By the proof of
4.1
, the algebra
\mathcal{F}(I_{n}(\mathbf{X}))
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I_{n}(\mathbf{X})))
is Gorenstein.
By the same reasoning,
\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I_{n}(\mathbf{X})))
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{F}(I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}))
is Gorenstein.
The claim now follows by
4.6
as the required assumptions are vacuous in the generic case.

As seen in the proof of
4.6
, when
P_{\mathcal{L}}
is connected, to prove that
\mathcal{F}(L)
is Gorenstein it suffices to verify conditions (a) and (b) for consecutive minima and maxima, respectively.

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose
v_{i}\geq u_{i+1}
for all
i\in[n-1]
. Then

\mathcal{F}(L)
is Gorenstein if and only if the matrix
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbb{S}}
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

For any
i\in[n-1]
if
\epsilon_{i}>1
, then
\epsilon_{j}=1
for all
j\in[i+1,\epsilon_{i}-2]
and
\epsilon_{i+\epsilon_{i}-1}>1
;

(2)

For any

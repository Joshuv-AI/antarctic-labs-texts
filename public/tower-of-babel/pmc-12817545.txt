Systematic annotation and analysis of susceptibility genes associated with vaccine adverse events
Authors: Qingyun Song, Jun Su, Chenchen Pan, Xue Zhang, Bingjian Yang, Yongqun He, Jiangan Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Susceptibility genes, including single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the DNA sequences, genetically predispose certain individuals to developing adverse events (AEs) following vaccination. Such AEs are often undetected in initial clinical safety trials during vaccine licensing evaluations. Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of susceptibility genes is crucial for vaccine development, safety monitoring, and precision immunization. VaegenDB is a web-based centralized database and analysis system designed for managing, storing, and analyzing susceptibility genes associated with vaccine AEs. Basic information on these genes and supporting evidence are curated from peer-reviewed literature, while more detailed gene, AE, and vaccine data are automatically extracted from existing databases such as RefSeq and VIOLIN using in-house scripts. Currently, VaegenDB contains information on 160 susceptibility genes linked to 151 AEs and 86 vaccines. The system offers a user-friendly web interface that enables interactive querying and visualization of susceptibility genes. Bioinformatics analyses using VaegenDB reveal that a single susceptibility gene may harbor multiple genetic variations, one vaccine can be associated with several AEs, and a single AE may be influenced by multiple genes or SNPs. In addition, KEGG and GO enrichment analyses were employed to identify gene signatures—including functional annotations, mutation types, and expression patterns—associated with adverse reactions. The construction of this database and subsequent bioinformatics analyses help clarify enriched gene profiles and underlying mechanisms of vaccine-related AEs, thereby supporting rational vaccine design and advances in precision medicine.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12920-025-02292-4.

Introduction

Vaccines represent one of the most significant achievements of modern medicine and serve as a powerful tool in combating infectious diseases. Although the underlying concept of vaccination may appear straightforward, it involves a sophisticated scientific mechanism that mimics pathogenic invasion, primes the immune system, and enables the body to develop defense mechanisms without exposure to virulent pathogens. A primary function of vaccines is to prevent disease outbreaks. By introducing attenuated or inactivated pathogens into the body, vaccines stimulate the immune system to recognize foreign antigens and generate antibodies, thereby establishing robust protection against future infections [1]. Safety and efficacy are fundamental to vaccine development. Vaccines are developed in accordance with rigorous scientific standards and undergo extensive clinical trials to ensure their safety and effectiveness [2]. Furthermore, continuous monitoring and evaluation are conducted to sustain long-term vaccine efficacy and safety [3].

Although licensed vaccines demonstrate excellent safety profiles, certain vaccines can induce mild to severe adverse events (AEs) in specific populations. Current understanding of AEs, particularly rare ones, remains incomplete, and establishing causal relationships with vaccination poses considerable challenges. Emerging evidence suggests that genetic variations in vaccine recipients may contribute to AE susceptibility [4]. These genetic factors—including haplotypes, alleles, single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), and insertions—can increase host vulnerability to post-vaccination AEs [5]. For clarity, we introduce the term “vaegen” to describe a susceptibility gene that becomes abnormally expressed or activated in a vaccinee following immunization, leading to specific vaccine-associated AEs. Due to the low incidence of such AEs in narrow populations, they often remain undetected during initial clinical safety trials [6]. Systematic data mining and analysis of AE to identify the vaegens could significantly enhance AE evaluation and prediction at the individual level, thereby advancing personalized preventative medicine tailored to an individual’s predicted vaccine response.

While intensive research has identified many vaegens, systematic analysis is lacking, and there is no central resource for annotating, storing, retrieving, and comparing them. To address this challenge, we developed VaegenDB, a web-based system that stores manually curated vaegens from the literature and supports their bioinformatics analysis and comparison.

Methods

Manual data collection and annotation

Vaegens are defined as susceptibility genes that exhibit abnormal expression or activity in the vaccinees following immunization with specific vaccines and subsequent manifestation of particular AEs. Most currently identified vaegens originate from observational studies, including clinical case-control investigations and case report analyses. Consequently, the genetic associations among vaegens, AEs, and vaccinations remain incompletely verified, necessitating further validation through more robust experimental or clinical evidence. A systematic literature curation process was conducted following the preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [7]. Literature retrieval was performed through PubMed/MEDLINE using the following search terms individually and in combination: suscepti*, vaccin*, adverse event, and gene. The initial screening involved examining titles and abstracts to exclude irrelevant studies. Subsequently, full-text articles of potentially eligible studies were thoroughly evaluated. To minimize potential publication bias, two reviewers (i.e., the authors of this paper, Qingyun Song and Jun Su) independently retrieved and annotated article abstracts and full texts. Inter-rater reliability for full-text assessments was evaluated using the kappa coefficient [8]. From the eligible articles, vaegen-related information—including specific susceptibility genes, corresponding AEs, and administered vaccines—was systematically identified, extracted, stored, and analyzed. Each vaegen record is made publicly accessible on the VaegenDB website only after undergoing critical review and receiving formal approval by a domain expert.

To enhance the efficiency and consistency of manual curation, we employed Limix [9], an in-house web-based literature mining and curation system. This platform integrates computational text-mining utilities with manual curation workflows to support comprehensive literature search, data extraction, edition, and submission. Key features of Limix include: (a) Facilitating the search of vaegen-related literature, enabling seamless text copy, and direct data submission to the database; (b) Providing reviewer tools for evaluating, modifying, and approving the curated data within a unified web interface; (c) Maintaining complete version histories for each vaegen entry, allowing reviewers to compare any two historical versions and track detailed changes.

VaegenDB system and database design

VaegenDB is implemented within the VIOLIN database infrastructure and operates on two virtual servers running Redhat Enterprise Linux [10]. These servers are configured to perform routine mutual backups, ensuring data integrity and availability. The system accepts user-submitted queries via a web interface, which are processed through PHP/SQL scripts against a MySQL relational database (version 5.5.28). Query results are dynamically rendered in the user’s browser. Additionally, VaegenDB integrates multiple analytical tools that provide predictive outputs and support sequence-based analyses (Fig. 1). As a newly integrated component of the comprehensive VIOLIN vaccine database and analysis platform, VaegenDB extends its functionality toward genetic susceptibility research. The database organizes the following information for each vaegen: (a) General gene information, including gene symbol, protein function, NCBI gene/nucleotide identifiers, and chromosomal location, automatically extracted and updated from reference databases such as RefSeq. (b) Standardized vaccine and AE descriptions using the Vaccine Ontology (VO) and Ontology of Adverse Events (OAE) [11, 12]. (c) Detailed reference metadata, including author names, article title, journal, and PubMed ID (PMID). All manually curated data are further amenable to bioinformatic analyses, such as ontology-based hierarchical classification of AEs and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) for the specific susceptibility genes (Fig. 1).

VaegenDB data query and display

All data stored in VaegenDB are publicly accessible and freely queryable through a web-based interface developed with PHP and HTML. Query results are returned in real time and rendered directly within the browser. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the system enables interactive querying and visualization of both manually curated and pre-computed data. Users may search vaegen-related information by specifying single or multiple criteria, such as PMID or vaccine name (Fig. 2A). For example, a search using “measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine” returns a tabular overview of relevant vaegen entries with basic annotations (Fig. 2B). Selecting a specific vaegen from the result set reveals additional detailed information, which is dynamically integrated from external databases via embedded hyperlinks (Fig. 2C, D and E, and H). To support vaccinology-focused analysis, the system also allows mapping of AEs (e.g., febrile seizures) and vaccines (e.g., MMR vaccine) to standardized ontology terms from the OAE and VO, respectively. Vaegen data analysis

To support reliable and effective analysis of vaegen data, VaegenDB is designed to interoperate with established databases (e.g., NCBI gene database) and bioinformatics tools (e.g., NCBI BLAST) (Fig. 1). A key feature of VaegenDB is the integration of Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment methods [14, 15], enabling functional interpretation of user-selected gene sets. Following the extraction of vaegens from the database, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses are performed using a custom Python-based workflow. Statistically significant enrichment is assessed with Fisher’s exact test, and the terms with false discovery rate (FDR) adjusted p ≤ 0.05 are visualized using the GOfox tool [16].

In these analyses, Fisher’s exact test evaluates whether a given vaegen is overrepresented in specific functional categories. Under the null hypothesis that foreground and background genes are randomly distributed among functional categories, the nominal p value reflects the probability of observing the current or a more extreme distribution. To account for multiple testing, we apply the Bonferroni correction. This procedure involves sorting all functional categories by their nominal p values in ascending order and identifying the largest index k such that p(k) <= (k*q)/m, where m is the total number of categories and q is the set FDR threshold (commonly used standard is 0.05). Functional categories with p values less than or equal to the p(k) are considered significantly enriched.

Data transfer and download

All vaegen sequence data are freely accessible and downloadable from the VaegenDB website (https://violinet.org/vaegendb) in FASTA format. Each downloaded file contains a header line initiated with the ‘>’ symbol, followed by corresponding gene sequence. Additionally, users can export selected vaegen-associated information as a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet for offline analysis. To enhance data interoperability and reusability, all vaegen records have been incorporated into the VO. VO is a community-based ontology developed in the web ontology language (OWL) format, which ensures compatibility with numerous software tools and supports advanced semantic reasoning [17]. Storing vaegen data within VO facilitates the development of new applications that integrate these genetic susceptibility factors with other biomedical datasets, thereby enabling more sophisticated bioinformatics analyses.

Results

VaegenDB statistics

The PRISMA flowchart (Fig. 3) outlines the literature selection process, including the inclusion criteria results from each stage of query processing. The initial search of the PubMed database using a predefined search strategy on January 1 st 2024, yielded 2896 records. After removing duplicates and excluding studies based on title and abstract screening, 180 articles were selected for full-text review. Following full-text assessment, studies deemed irrelevant to the subject were systematically excluded (Fig. 3). Ultimately, 100 peer-reviewed articles containing vaegens and supporting scientific evidence were included in the meta-analysis. Among these, 62 were clinical case-control studies, 24 were case reports, and 14 involved cell or animal model research. Inter-rater agreement between the two reviewers was very good, with a kappa statistic of 0.80. Manual data extraction focused on retrieval of specific vaegen information, including PMID, gene symbol, SNP RSID, vaccine name, AE, and experimental evidence. In most cases, curated publications did not report gene identifiers or chromosomal locations; such information was subsequently obtained by querying the NCBI database using gene details provided in the original articles. Additionally, vaccine names and AE descriptions were standardized through mapping to the VO and OAE, respectively. To date, VaegenDB contains 160 vaegens associated with 151 AEs and 86 vaccines (Supplemental File 1). All data in VaegenDB have been harmonized in accordance with established biomedical data standards, significantly enhancing their portability and reusability across multiple databases and platforms.

Analysis of the vaegen-associated AEs in VaegenDB

Our analysis of 100 peer-reviewed articles identified 151 AEs associated with 160 vaegens. It should be noted that individual AEs may be linked to multiple vaegens, and individual vaegens may be associated with multiple AEs (Supplemental File 1). For example, vaccine-associated paralytic poliomyelitis (VAPP) AE following oral polio vaccine (OPV) vaccination involves both Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK) and toll-like receptor 8 (TLR8) genes [18]. Similarly, the chemokine (C-X-C motif) ligand 9 (CXCL9) vaegen is associated with multiple AEs—including generalized rash, fever, and lymphadenopathy—following smallpox vaccination [19]. We employed an OAE-based classification approach to analyze these vaegen-associated AEs. Table 1 displays the hierarchical classification of 151 AEs according to the OAE inferred hierarchy. The most represented category was ‘immune system AE’, comprising 22 distinct symptoms. Other frequently observed categories included ‘skin AE’, ‘investigation result abnormal AE’, and ‘behavioral and neurological AE’. Furthermore, VaegenDB integrates with the Ontobee [13] (https://ontobee.org/) and Ontofox [20] (https://ontofox.hegroup.org/) platforms, enabling automated extraction and analysis of vaegen-specific AE terms along with their complete hierarchical relationships within the OAE framework. This ontological organization allows users to efficiently collect and integrate related AE terms from external ontologies, supporting data reuse and advanced analysis through import into client OWL ontology environments.

Analysis of the Vaegens in VaegenDB

From the 100 peer-reviewed articles, we identified 160 vaegens that were observed to be abnormally expressed or activated in individuals following vaccination with specific vaccines and associated with particular AEs. For instance, two genetic loci— interferon induced protein 44 like (IFI44L) and cluster of differentiation 46 (CD46)—were associated with febrile seizure AE following MMR vaccination [21]. Specifically, the SNP rs273259 in a protein-coding gene IFI44L demonstrated significant genome-wide associations with post-MMR febrile seizures when compared to both controls and non-vaccine-related febrile seizures. Similarly, an intronic SNP rs2724384 in CD46 reached genome-wide significance in its association with immune responses after MMR vaccination [21].

As illustrated in Fig. 4A, VaegenDB incorporates a gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) interface that enables users to perform GO-based or KEGG-based functional analysis on selected vaegens. The analysis employs the GO_Molecular_Function_2023 dataset comprising 1,810 genes. Enrichment significance is determined by evaluating the overlap between foreground and background gene sets using Fisher’s test with Bonferroni correction. For example, the “Cytokine Activity” term (GO:0005125) showed an overlap of 27 foreground genes out of 178 background genes (Fig. 4B). Using fever-associated vaegens as a case study, we perform GSEA focusing on GO molecular function terms, which describe molecular-level activities such as catalytic or binding functions [22]. The analysis revealed significant enrichment in cytokine-mediated signaling pathways (p < 0.05) and positive regulation of cytokine production (adjusted p < 0.05) (Fig. 4B and C). Further examination identified substantial enrichment in inflammatory response pathways (p < 0.05), complemented by enhanced regulation of IL-6 production (p < 0.05). These results collectively suggest that the inflammatory mechanisms play a crucial role in the development of vaccine-associated AEs.

Data accessibility, query capabilities, and system updates

All vaegen data in VaegenDB are freely accessible for download and exchange as detailed in the Methods section. The system provides seamless query capabilities across the entire database through its web interface, while also enabling integrated analysis using the computational tools available with the broader VIOLIN system. To maintain current and comprehensive data coverage, VaegenDB will be updated quarterly with new identified vaegens and corresponding bioinformatics analyses. All updates and new data releases will be promptly announced on the VaegenDB website. Furthermore, to enhance data utility and support reproducible research, we are developing interoperability with established immunology databases including Immport [23] and Reactome [24]. These integration efforts will facilitate secondary analysis and promote reuse of vaegen data in broader biomedical contexts.

Discussion

Vaccines developed over the past two centuries have substantially reduced the global burden of infectious diseases. The continued proliferation of novel vaccines—along with administration to increasingly diverse populations—demands optimized vaccination strategies to minimize associated risks. Growing evidence suggests that vaccine-induced protection may be influenced by individual genetic differences, such as variations in gene expression and alternative splicing [25]. Accordingly, knowledge of a person’s haplotype profile may help predict susceptibility to specific AEs following immunization [26]. Although most AEs are mild [27, 28], severe reactions occasionally occur in susceptible subpopulations [29], and vaccine safety remains a critical public health priority [30]. To systematically investigate the genetic basis of such reactions, this study established a comprehensive resource of susceptibility genes and variants (vaegens) and examined their roles in vaccine-associated AEs.

The immunogenicity and safety outcomes of vaccination are influenced by multiple host and environmental factors, including genetic background, ethnicity, number of vaccine doses, age, and environmental exposures [31]. One proposed mechanism for AEs involves dysregulated inflammatory activation coupled with impaired tissue repair pathways. For instance, antigen presentation to T cells initiates cytokine release, triggering a cascade of chemokines and inflammatory mediators that amplify the immune response [32]. Genetic polymorphisms can modulate this process by altering cytokine and receptor expression levels, thereby shaping individual susceptibility to AEs. It is important to distinguish between genetic variants associated with vaccine-specific AEs and those linked to AEs in general. For example, SNPs rs273259 (IFI44L) and rs1318653 (CD46) are specifically associated with febrile seizures following MMR vaccination, whereas SNPs in SCN1A, SCN2A, TMEM16C, and DCN are associated with febrile seizures unrelated to MMR vaccination [21]. Furthermore, polymorphisms in immune-related genes may predispose individuals to AEs by altering immune cell activation and mediator secretion [33]. The statistical association between a genetic variant and an AE depends on the chosen significance threshold [34]. Some variants near but not reaching this threshold may still have biological relevance. Moreover, while numerous studies have reported correlations between gene expression changes and AE after vaccination [35, 36], such observations generally indicate correlation rather than causation. For example, elevated interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) level in smallpox vaccine recipients with fever and rash provide evidence for cytokine involvement in AEs, but do not establish causal mechanisms [37].

With the growing integration of precision medicine into clinical practice, pharmacogenomics has emerged as a pivotal field for understanding interindividual variability in treatment responses. PharmGKB serves as a key resource that advances understanding of how genetic variation influences drug response. It provides not only clinically actionable pharmacogenomic information but also data supporting basic research and drug development [38, 39]. Related disciplines such as vaccinomics have subsequently evolved from this foundation, focusing on genetic determinants of heterogeneous immune responses to vaccines [40]. For instance, a specific SNP in the toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) gene has been associated with diminished humoral and cell-mediated immune responses to the measles vaccine [41]. To the best of our knowledge, VaegenDB represents the first publicly accessible web-based platform dedicated to curating and analyzing susceptibility genes associated with vaccine AEs. The database systematically collects vaegen information from case reports of AEs following immunization with licensed vaccines, as well as from clinical trials of investigational vaccine candidates (Supplemental File 1). VaegenDB incorporates three key design features that enhance its research utility. First, it provides manually curated experimental and clinical evidence supporting each vaegen association, complementing basic gene information. Second, it integrates comprehensive data on susceptibility genes, AEs, and vaccine details, all standardized according to findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable (FAIR) principles to maximize data utility [42]. Third, the platform includes built-in bioinformatics tools supporting diverse analyses such as ontology-based AE hierarchical classification and GSEA for susceptibility genes.

The VaegenDB database enables multiple research and clinical applications. Specifically, vaegens provide a foundation for designing rationally personalized vaccines. Personalized vaccine development requires consideration of an individual’s genetic profile, sex, age, body size, as well as vaccine-specific parameters including dosage, storage conditions, and adjuvant selection—all factors influencing immunogenicity, efficacy, and safety [31]. Precision vaccinology, which applies precision medicine principles to vaccinology, aims to develop tailored vaccines that maximize protection while minimizing risks [43]. VaegenDB contributes to this field by generating actionable insights and testable hypotheses. Future applications may include guiding optimal vaccine administration strategies through integration of genomic data and advanced analytical technologies. Furthermore, identification of specific vaegens could enable prediction of individuals at high risk for particular AEs [44]. Systematic analysis of VaegenDB data will support safer matching of existing vaccines to appropriate populations, particularly through reduced incidence of serious AEs. Ultimately, integration of experimental validation with vaegen analytics will advance the development and implementation of precision vaccination strategies.

Several limitations of this study and the VaegenDB database should be acknowledged. First, the data on susceptibility genes and their associations with vaccine AEs were curated primarily from published literature, which is susceptible to selection and reporting biases since negative or non-significant results are less frequently published. Second, although the database is comprehensive, it is not exhaustive; it captures a subset of known vaegens reported in the literature up to January 1, 2024, and many associations likely remain undiscovered or unvalidated. Third, most gene-AE-vaccine relationships are supported by evidence from observational studies—such as case-control studies and case reports—which indicate correlation but do not establish causality. Future work should include experimental validation and larger epidemiological studies to confirm these links and clarify the underlying biological mechanisms. In addition, despite the use of ontologies (e.g., OAE and GO) to standardize terminology, heterogeneity in AE and genetic variant reporting across studies remained a challenge. Finally, functional enrichment analyses, though informative, rely on reference annotations from GO and KEGG, which are continuously updated and may not fully represent immunogenetic pathways specific to vaccine responses.

Conclusions

VaegenDB represents the first dedicated database for curating vaegens—gene susceptibility factors such as SNPs that are associated with specific AEs following vaccinations. The current release integrates 160 vaegens linked to 151 AEs across 86 vaccines. The database will be regularly updated with additional vaegen records and enhanced with new analytical features. Analysis of VaegenDB data facilitates the identification of population-specific vaegen expression patterns and their correlation with post-vaccination AEs. As a centralized resource, VaegenDB provides curated data and bioinformatics tools to support researchers in vaccinology, pharmacogenomics, and immunology. In the era of precision medicine and big data, this integrated knowledge base is positioned to advance rational vaccine design and improve understanding of vaccination response mechanisms.

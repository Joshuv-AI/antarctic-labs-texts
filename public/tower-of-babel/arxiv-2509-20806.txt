Abstract
We propose that the high eccentricity of the stellar orbit in Gaia BH3 system could be excited through a secular resonance effect if the inner dark object is, in effect, a tight and eccentric black hole binary (BHB). During the orbital decay of the inner BHB, the apsidal precession rate of the inner binary matches that of the outer stellar orbit, and this resonance advection can drive the outer eccentricity into some extreme values. For a Gaia BH3-like system, we show that a near equal-mass ( q = 0.8 q=0.8 ) BHB with an initial semi-major axis of 1–3 au and an initial eccentricity ≳ 0.95 \gtrsim 0.95 is able to excite the outer orbit to the observed value, leaving a current BHB with semi-major axis 0.25–0.5 au and eccentricity ∼ 0.8 \sim 0.8 . The eccentric inner BHB imprints two observable signatures on the outer star: (1) short-term RV modulations with an amplitude ≲ 100 \lesssim 100 m/s and (2) long-term apsidal precession with a rate ≲ 0.1 ​ ° \lesssim 0.1\degree /yr. Although neither of these is detected in the currently available astrometry and RV data, we show that these signals are detectable with the full Gaia astrometry data and dedicated high-precision and/or long-term RV observations. Our work provides a new perspective on the dynamical formation of Gaia BH3, and the methodology is readily applicable to similar systems such as HD 130298, Gaia BH1, and Gaia BH2.



I
Introduction

Gaia BH3 is the third stellar system to contain a dormant stellar-mass black hole
(BH) discovered by the
Gaia
mission
(
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016
)
. The BH has a mass of
\sim 33~M_{\odot}
and is on a wide (
\simeq 16
au) and highly eccentric (
e\simeq 0.73
) orbit around a very metal-poor ([Fe/H]
\simeq-2.5
) giant star of mass
M_{\star}=0.76\,M_{\odot}
(
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2024
, hereafter Gaia2024). This stellar system is believed to be part of the ED-2 stellar stream
(
E. Balbinot et al. 2024
)
, which is composed of stars at least 12 Gyr old and probably formed from a disrputed low-mass star cluster with
M_{\text{cluster}}\lesssim 5\times 10^{4}~M_{\odot}

(
E. Dodd et al. 2023
)
.

The formation pathway of Gaia BH3 and systems remains heatedly debated. Isolated binary evolution is able to produce such systems under certain conditions
(
K. El-Badry 2024
;
G. Iorio et al. 2024
)
. The association of Gaia BH3 with the ED-2 stream may also point to a dynamical origin that the system could be assembled from a massive BH and a visible star in the progenitor globular cluster of the ED-2 stream
(
D. Marín Pina et al. 2024
)
.

The measured high mass of Gaia BH3 also opens up the possibility that the dark object in Gaia BH3 could be composed of a black-hole binary (BHB)
1
1

1

We clarify that “BHB” in this work refers specifically to a binary system composed of two BHs, distinct from some literature where it denotes a star-BH binary.
rather than a single BH.
The formation of BHB triple with a visible star is challenging due to the extreme expansion of BHB progenitors (typically
10^{2}-10^{3}\,R_{\odot}
), which generally disrupts triple-system stability. However, some low-metallicity binaries may avoid expansion
(
P. Marchant et al. 2016
)
, enabling compact triples
(
A. Vigna-Gómez et al. 2021
)
. Alternatively, such systems can be effectively formed in low metallicity open clusters
(
A. Tanikawa et al. 2025
)
.

If Gaia BH3 indeed hosts a BHB, non-trivial dynamical effects emerge and could significantly influence the evolutionary history of the system
(
B. Liu et al. 2015b
;
S. Naoz et al. 2017
;
F. Antonini et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
. Recent studies demonstrated that in coplanar triples containing a merging BHB, the outer star may experience extreme eccentricity growth when its apsidal precession rate matches that of the inner BHB
(
B. Liu et al. 2022
;
B. Liu & D. Lai 2024
, e.g.,)
.
We show in this work that such an “apsidal precession resonance” capture could naturally explain both the high eccentricity of the stellar orbit and the large mass (
\sim 33~M_{\odot}
) of the dark object in the Gaia BH3 system.

The BHB hypothesis can be tested via high-precision astrometric and/or RV observations of the visible star. An inner BHB would imprint two distinct signatures on the visible star: (1) short-term RV modulations induced by the orbital motion of the binary
(
M. H. M. Morais & A. C. M. Correia 2008
;
T. Hayashi et al. 2020
)
, and (2) long-term orbital precession from secular perturbations
(
B. Liu et al. 2015a
;
T. Hayashi & Y. Suto 2020
)
. The recent constraints on the binarity of Gaia BH1’s dark companion by
P. Nagarajan et al. 2024
demonstrate the feasibility of detecting such inner BHBs hidden in Gaia BH systems.

In this Letter, we first present in Section
II
the possibility that the high eccentricity
e\simeq 0.73
of the stellar orbit in Gaia BH3 system could have been excited by an inner merging BHB through apsidal precession resonance capture, and then assess in Section
III
the detectability of such BHBs with high-precision astrometric and RV observations. A brief discussion is given in Section
IV
on the influence of non-coplanar orbits and the applicability to other detached eccentric BH systems. Finally, we summarize our findings in Section
V
.

II
Apsidal Precession Resonance

If the
33\,M_{\odot}
companion in Gaia BH3 system is actually a black-hole binary (BHB) instead of a single BH, we will show in this section that, under certain initial configurations, the inner BHB can excite the eccentricity of the outer stellar orbit from near circular to the observed value (
\gtrsim 0.73
) through the apsidal precession resonance within the system age (
\sim 13
Gyr).

We assume here a coplanar hierarchical triple system in order to better understand the dynamical evolution and leave the more complicated situation of inclined systems to Section
IV.1
.
The inner binary consists of masses of
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
(
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
), with a total mass of
m_{12}\equiv m_{1}+m_{2}=32.70~M_{\odot}
from
11
. The mass ratio of the inner binary is defined as
q\equiv m_{2}/m_{1}
(
0<q\leq 1
). The mass of the tertiary star is fixed at
m_{\star}=0.76~M_{\odot}
(
11
). The semi-major axes and eccentricities of the inner and outer binaries are denoted by
a_{\text{in}}
,
a_{\text{out}}
, and
e_{\text{in}}
,
e_{\text{out}}
, respectively. The semimajor axis of the outer binary takes the observed value
a_{\text{out}}=16.17
au
(
11
)
throughout the paper, as
a_{\text{out}}
is conserved under the double-averaged formalism in the secular regime.
Since we focus on coplanar triples, we use the longitudes of periapsis,
\varpi_{\text{in}}
and
\varpi_{\text{out}}
, to specify the apsidal lines of the inner and outer binaries, respectively.

We first identify the initial configurations that could potentially undergo the apsidal precession resonance by taking into account the resonance-capture condition, the orbital decay timescale due to gravitational radiation, and the stability of the coplanar triple. Note that all parameters in the initial state are denoted with an extra subscript “0”. Below, we choose a near equal-mass (
q=0.8
) BHB for illustration purposes. Results corresponding to other values of mass ratio
q
are given in Appendix
A
. The initial eccentricity of the outer binary is set to
e_{\text{out,0}}=10^{-4}
as an example of a near circular orbit.

In order for the apsidal precession resonance capture to happen, the parameter

\gamma\equiv\frac{\dot{\varpi}_{\text{out}}}{\dot{\varpi}_{\text{in}}}

(1)

must cross unity from above to below during the orbital decay of the inner BHB
(
B. Liu & D. Lai 2024
)
. Here,
\dot{\varpi}_{\text{in}}
is the apsidal precession rate of the inner binary due to the 1st-order post-Newtonian effect

\dot{\varpi}_{\text{in}}=\frac{3G^{3/2}m_{12}^{3/2}}{c^{2}a_{\text{in}}^{5/2}(1-e_{\text{in}}^{2})},

(2)

and
\dot{\varpi}_{\text{out}}
is the apsidal precession rate of the outer binary due to the quadrupole-level secular perturbation from the inner BHB
(
B. Liu et al. 2015a
)

\dot{\varpi}_{\text{out}}=\frac{3}{8}\left(\frac{a_{\text{in}}}{a_{\text{out}}}\right)^{2}n_{\text{out}}\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{2}}\frac{2+3e_{\text{in}}^{2}}{(1-e_{\text{out}}^{2})^{2}},

(3)

where
n_{\text{out}}
is the mean motion of the outer orbit.
The critical condition of
\gamma=1
is plotted as the dashed line in Figure
1
.

The second condition is given by the orbital decay of the inner binary due to gravitational radiation, which changes both
\dot{\varpi}_{\text{in}}
and
\dot{\varpi}_{\text{out}}
.
The merger time due to gravitational radiation of a binary with initial semi-major axis
a_{\text{in},0}
and eccentricity
e_{\text{in},0}
is given by (e.g.,
P. C. Peters 1964
)

\displaystyle T_{\text{m}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{10^{13}\ \text{yr}}{f(e_{\text{in},0})}\left(\frac{a_{\text{in},0}}{0.5\ \text{au}}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{m_{12}}{30\ M_{\odot}}\right)^{-3}\frac{(1+q)^{2}/q}{4},

(4)

\displaystyle f(e_{\text{in},0})

\displaystyle\equiv\ \frac{1}{(1-e_{\text{in},0}^{2})^{7/2}}\left(1+\frac{73}{24}e_{\text{in},0}^{2}+\frac{37}{96}e_{\text{in},0}^{4}\right).

We show the merger time of
T_{\text{m}}=10
Gyr for a Gaia BH3-like system with different initial configurations as the dotted curve in Figure
1
. Given the uncertainty in the system age
(
11
, 12–14 Gyr,)
, we also plot the merger time of
T_{\text{m}}=13
Gyr as the dotted-dashed curve in the same figure, which is not too different from the
T_{\text{m}}=10
Gyr curve. BHBs with initial semi-major axis and eccentricity above these curves have experienced significant orbital decay within the age of the system and thus may get locked into the apsidal precession resonance.

The third condition is the stability of the coplanar triple system. We choose the dynamical instability condition for hierarchical triples given by
R. A. Mardling & S. J. Aarseth 2001

\frac{a_{\text{out}}}{a_{\text{in}}}>2.8\left[\left(1+\frac{m_{\star}}{m_{12}}\right)\frac{1+e_{\text{out}}}{(1-e_{\text{out}})^{3}}\right]^{2/5}\left(1-0.3\frac{i_{\text{mut}}}{180\degree}\right),

(5)

where
i_{\text{mut}}
is the mutual inclination between the inner and outer binaries and is set to zero for our coplanar triples. The threshold of dynamical instability for a Gaia BH3-like system is shown as the solid black curve in Figure
1
.

The region bounded by the three conditions given above in Figure
1
is the set of valid combinations of initial semi-major axis and eccentricity for the inner BHB that could potentially undergo apsidal precession resonance capture.
For Gaia BH3-like systems, the mechanism requires an inner BHB with an initially highly eccentric orbit (see also
B. Liu & D. Lai 2024
).

For BHBs with initial configurations within the identified parameter space, we then investigate their dynamical evolution in order to compare with the observed properties of the Gaia BH3 system. We select 118 sets of
(a_{\text{in},0},e_{\text{in},0})
within the allowed parameter space; for comparison purposes, we also select 135 combinations of
a_{\text{in},0}
and
e_{\text{in},0}
that fall beyond the previously identified parameter space. The chosen parameter combinations are shown as colored circles/squares in Figure
1
. For each combination of
a_{\text{in},0}
and
e_{\text{in},0}
, we integrate the double-averaged secular equations of motion
(
B. Liu et al. 2015b
)
for up to 13 Gyr or merger of the inner BHB to obtain the final (i.e., present) orbital parameters of the system. Those systems that have experienced BHB merger within 13 Gyr are marked as squares in Figure
1
, while those that have not are marked as circles. The final orbital eccentricity of the outer binary at 13 Gyr or merger of the BHB is indicated by the color scale in Figure
1
. About one-third of the systems (38 out of 118) with initial conditions in the gray-shaded region get caught in the apsidal resonance, and the final
e_{\text{out}}
reaches higher than the observed value
e_{\text{obs}}\simeq 0.73
. Taking into account the stability of the coplanar triple, only 14 out of the 38 systems remain compatible with observations, which are highlighted by black open circles in Figure
1
.

III
Detectability in Astrometry and RV

III.1
Short-term RV Modulations

The semi-amplitude of short-term RV oscillations due to an inner binary inside a coplanar triple can be estimated as
(
M. H. M. Morais & A. C. M. Correia 2011
;
T. Hayashi et al. 2020
, e.g.,)

K_{\text{short}}\approx\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{m_{12}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{12}+m_{\star}}{m_{12}}}\left(\frac{a_{\text{in}}}{a_{\text{out}}}\right)^{7/2}g(e)K_{\text{circ}}.

(6)

Here
K_{\text{circ}}
is the characteristic RV semi-amplitude of the tertiary star around the center of mass of the inner binary

K_{\text{circ}}\equiv\frac{m_{12}}{m_{12}+m_{\star}}a_{\text{out}}n_{\text{out}}\sin i_{\star},

(7)

and
g(e)\equiv[(1+e_{\text{in}})/(1-e_{\text{out}})]^{7/2}
is an empirical factor that takes into account the eccentricities of the inner and outer orbits.
The red lines in Figure
3
show the contours of relevant values of
K_{\text{short}}
for the potential BHBs in the Gaia BH3 system.
It is also worth noting that the actual RV deviation from a Keplerian motion also depends on the sampling cadence and strategy of the RV observations and could be 10–100 times larger than the analytic approximation given by Equation (
6
), as demonstrated by the simulations in
T. Hayashi et al. 2023
for Gaia BH1 and Gaia BH2 systems. The maximum deviation appears around the periastron passage, and the next event for Gaia BH3 is in October of 2029 according to the orbital solution of
11
.

The published RV measurements of Gaia BH3 are very sparse and not enough to exclude any of the potential BHB configurations in Figure
3
.

Below, we provide a simple estimate of how better constraints can be obtained with future observations. To the leading order, a constant apsidal precession model introduces a signal whose amplitude is
\sim\dot{\omega}_{\text{out}}\Delta T
smaller compared to the RV/astrometry signal of the Keplerian model. Here
\Delta T
is the observational baseline.
For astrometry, the uncertainty on the apsidal precession rate
\dot{\omega}
can be estimated as

\sigma_{\dot{\omega}}\simeq 0.03\ \text{deg\,yr}^{-1}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\text{AL}}}{0.14\,\text{mas}}\frac{27\,\text{mas}}{\rho}\frac{10\,\text{yr}}{\Delta T}\right),

(10)

Here
\sigma_{\text{AL}}
is the uncertainty of the astrometric measurement in the along-scan (AL) direction,
\rho
is the amplitude of the astrometry signal of the Keplerian motion. The evaluation is performed on the known properties of Gaia BH3 system (
\sigma_{\text{AL}}=0.14
mas,
\rho=27
mas,
11
) at the end of the Gaia mission (
\Delta T=10
yr).

Similarly, the uncertainty on the apsidal precession rate
\dot{\omega}_{\text{out}}
from long-term RV observations can be estimated as

\sigma_{\dot{\omega}}\simeq 0.01\,\text{deg\,yr}^{-1}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\text{RV}}}{100\,{\rm m\,s}^{-1}}\frac{50\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}}{K_{\rm circ}}\frac{10\,\text{yr}}{\Delta T}\right),

(11)

where
\sigma_{\text{RV}}
is the precision of a single RV measurement. The above estimation is validated with simulated high-precision (
\sigma_{\text{RV}}=100
m/s, achievable with current instruments on Gaia BH3,
11
) RV observations of five measurements per year for 10 years.

Therefore, both Gaia astrometry and long-term RV measurements are promising in constraining the apsidal precession rate induced by a BHB inside Gaia BH3 and testing the secular resonance hypothesis.

IV.1
Misaligned Triples

Our investigation has thus far assumed the BHB inside Gaia BH3 to be coplanar with the outer star. We now discuss the implications of misaligned configurations.

For moderate mutual inclinations (
i_{\text{mut}}\lesssim 50^{\circ}
) between the inner and the outer orbits, the second and the third conditions given in Section
II
are largely unchanged. The condition for resonance capture is revised, and no simple analytical result can be derived, but our general conclusion is largely unaffected.
One example is shown in Figure
4
, which compares the time evolution of the outer eccentricity for the same inner BHB as in Figure
2
with various mutual inclinations (
i_{\text{mut}}=0,20,40,50,60\degree
).

The eccentricity of the outer stellar orbit is securely excited by the inner BHB through the apsidal precession resonance mechanism, as long as the mutual inclination
i_{\text{mut}}\lesssim 50\degree
.
The larger the mutual inclination, the earlier the system gets captured into apsidal precession resonance. This is because the critical
a_{\text{in}}
that determines the secular resonance shifts with
i_{\text{mut}}

(
B. Liu et al. 2015a
;
B. Liu & D. Lai 2020
;
B. Liu et al. 2022
, more examples in)
. The final eccentricity of the outer stellar orbit also increases with increasing
i_{\text{mut}}
. For large mutual inclinations (
i_{\text{mut}}\gtrsim 50\degree
), the dynamics of the system is largely determined by the inverse von Zeipel-Lidov-Kozai
(
H. von Zeipel 1910
;
M. L. Lidov 1962
;
Y. Kozai 1962
, ZLK,)
oscillations, and the eccentricity excitation due to apsidal precession resonance is suppressed
(
S. Naoz et al. 2017
;
B. R. Vinson & E. Chiang 2018
)
.

V
Conclusion

We have explored the possibility that the high eccentricity (
\approx 0.73
) of the stellar orbit in Gaia BH3 system could be excited by an eccentric BHB hidden in the
33M_{\odot}
dark component via the apsidal precession resonance mechanism. When the apsidal precession rate of the inner BHB matches that of the outer star during the orbital decay of the BHB, the system enters resonance, and the orbital eccentricity of the outer star can be excited to some extreme value.
We show that a near equal-mass (
q=0.8
) BHB with an initial semi-major axis of 1–3 au and an initial eccentricity
\gtrsim 0.95
is able to excite an initially nearly circular stellar orbit into the observed value (
\gtrsim 0.73
) within the age of the system (
\sim
13 Gyr). The final semi-major axis and eccentricity of the inner BHBs that can reproduce the observed properties are
a\sim 0.25
–0.5 au and
e\sim 0.75
–0.85, respectively. This mechanism can still work even if the inner and the outer orbits are moderately misaligned (
i_{\text{mut}}\lesssim 50^{\circ}
).

The eccentric BHB inside Gaia BH3 is expected to induce short-term RV modulations to the visible star with semi-amplitudes of
K_{\text{short}}\sim
10–50
{\rm m\,s}^{-1}
and long-term apsidal precession with rates of
\dot{\omega}_{\text{out}}\sim 0.01
–
0.08

{\rm deg\,yr}^{-1}
. Such signals remain undetected in the currently available astrometry and RV data, but are within the reach of the full Gaia mission as well as dedicated RV follow-ups.

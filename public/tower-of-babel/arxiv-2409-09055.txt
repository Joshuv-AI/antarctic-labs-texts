Generalised 6j symbols over the category of G G -graded vector spaces
Fabio Lischka

Abstract
Any choice of a spherical fusion category defines an invariant of oriented closed 3-manifolds, which is computed by choosing a triangulation of the manifold and considering a state sum model that assigns a 6j symbol to every tetrahedron in this triangulation. This approach has been generalized to oriented closed 3-manifolds with defect data by Catherine Meusburger. In a recent paper, she constructed a family of invariants for such manifolds parametrised by the choice of certain spherical fusion categories, bimodule categories, finite bimodule functors and module natural transformations. Meusburger defined generalised 6j symbols for these objects, and introduces a state sum model that assigns a generalised 6j symbol to every tetrahedron in the triangulation of a manifold with defect data, where the type of 6j symbol used depends on what defect data occur within the tetrahedron. The present work provides non-trivial examples of suitable bimodule categories, bimodule functors and module natural transformation, all over categories of G G -graded vector spaces. Our main result is the description of module functors in terms of matrices, which allows us to classify these functors when G G is a finite cyclic group. Furthermore, we calculate the generalised 6j symbols for categories of G G -graded vector spaces, (bi-)module categories over such categories and module functors.

Introduction

In
[
BW96
]
, Barrett and Westbury construct a family of invariants of closed oriented 3-manifolds, parametrised by spherical fusion categories, which generalise the Turaev-Viro invariant.
Given a spherical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, calculating the corresponding invariant of an oriented closed 3-manifold
M
involves
choosing a triangulation of
M
and considering a state sum model that assigns a 6j symbol to every tetrahedron in this triangulation. These 6j symbols encode the associativity constraint of the category
\mathcal{C}
.

Meusburger has shown that this approach can be generalised to oriented closed 3-manifolds with defect data
[
Meu23
]
.
The defect data of dimension 2, 1 and 0 are called defect areas, defect lines and defect vertices, respectively, and their union is a subset

The invariants defined in
[
Meu23
]

a spherical fusion category for every connected component of

, a bimodule category for every defect area, a finite bimodule functor for every defect line and a module natural transformation for every defect vertex. Meusburger defines generalised 6j symbols for these objects, and the state sum model she introduces involves assigning a generalised 6j symbol to every tetrahedron in the triangulation of
M
, where the type of 6j symbol used depends on what defect data occur within the tetrahedron.

In order to calculate this generalised state sum model and study its properties on examples, it is necessary to investigate non-trivial examples of spherical fusion categories, their bimodule categories as well as bimodule functors and calculate their 6j symbols.
In this thesis, we will examine these structures in a simple example with applications.
Specifically, we only consider the category

of
G
-graded vector spaces as an example of a spherical fusion category, where

is a group and

is a 3-cocycle of

[

]
reduce to Dijkgraf-Witten theory, which was introduced in
[
DW90
]
. The generalised state sum model from
[
Meu23
]
should thus give rise to a generalised Dijkgraf-Witten theory for 3-manifolds with defects.

Our main result is the explicit description of
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors in terms of matrices, which allows us to classify these functors in the case
G=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
for
n\neq 0
. Furthermore, we calculate the generalised 6j symbols for
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
, for
(\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega_{G}},\mathrm{Vec}_{H}^{\omega_{H}})
-bimodule categories and for
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors.

2.4

Let

\mathrm{Fun}_{(\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega_{G}},\mathrm{Vec}_{H}^{\omega_{H}})}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
and
\mathrm{Fun}_{\mathrm{Vec}_{G\times H}^{\omega}}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Gamma_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Gamma_{Y}))
. The identity morphisms
\mathrm{id}_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F\Rightarrow F
in both categories are represented by a family of identity matrices

M_{x,y}^{\mathrm{id}_{F}}=\mathbb{1}_{m_{xy}^{F}}\qquad(x\in X,y\in Y),

so
\mathrm{id}_{F}^{\prime}=\mathrm{id}_{F^{\prime}}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{id}_{F}}=\mathrm{id}_{\widetilde{F}}
.
Composition of (bi-)module natural transformations
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F\Rightarrow K
and
\mu\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}K\Rightarrow L
is described by the formula

M_{x,y}^{\mu\circ\eta}=M_{x,y}^{\mu}\,M_{x,y}^{\eta}\qquad(x\in X,y\in Y),

so the assignments (
35
) and (
36
) define functors.
It is easy to check that these functors are inverses of each other.
∎

5.3
Examples of
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors

In some cases, the matrices
(A_{g,x,y}^{F})_{g,x,y}
that determine a
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor can be represented elegantly by a certain functor. We now introduce the notation needed to state this correspondence.

Definition 5.10
.

[
Rie17
, Example 1.5.18]

Let
G
be a group and let
X
be a
G
-set. The
action groupoid

\mathcal{C}_{X}
of
X
is the category whose objects are elements
x
of
X
and whose morphisms are pairs

(g,x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x\to g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x\qquad(g\in G,x\in X).

The composite of two morphisms is

(g,x)\circ(h,x^{\prime})=(gh,x^{\prime})

and is defined if and only if
x=h\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x^{\prime}
.

From here on, we will regard
X\times Y
as a
G
-set with the diagonal action

g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y)\qquad(g\in G,x\in X,y\in Y).

Furthermore, we write
\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{F}}
for the category of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces.

Example 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})
and
\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
be finite semisimple
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module categories such that at least one of the following holds:

•

\Psi_{X}
and
\Psi_{Y}
are trivial, i.e.,
\Psi_{X}(g,h,x)=\Psi_{Y}(g,h,y)=1
for all
g\in G,h\in H,x\in X,y\in Y
(this implies
\omega=1
), or

•

X=Y=\{\bullet\}
is the trivial
G
-set with one element and
\Psi_{X}=\Psi_{Y}
.

Then there is an
\mathbb{F}
-linear isomorphism of categories

\mathrm{Fun}_{\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))\cong\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}_{X\times Y}^{op},\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{F}}).

Proof.

In both cases, condition (
33
) simplifies to

(37)

A_{gh,x,y}^{F}=A_{h,x,y}^{F}\,A_{g,h\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,h\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}.

For a functor
T\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{C}_{X\times Y}^{op}\to\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{F}}
, define a
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
by

\begin{array}[]{ll}&m_{xy}^{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(T((x,y)))\in\mathbb{N},\\
&A_{g,x,y}^{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=T((g,(x,y)))\in\mathrm{Mat}(m_{g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}\times m_{xy}^{F},\mathbb{F})\qquad(g\in G,x\in X,y\in Y),\end{array}

where we again identified
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{F}}}(\mathbb{F}^{n},\mathbb{F}^{m})
and
\mathrm{Mat}(m\times n,\mathbb{F})
. Since every morphism in
\mathcal{C}_{X\times Y}
is an isomorphism,
A_{g,x,y}^{F}
is invertible and
m_{xy}^{F}=m_{g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}
.
Furthermore, compatibility of
T
with composition implies that the family
(A_{g,x,y}^{F})_{g,x,y}
satisfies (
37
).
Now let
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
be a
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor. Then

\displaystyle T((x,y))

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathbb{F}^{\oplus m^{F}_{xy}},

\displaystyle T((g,(x,y)))

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=A^{F}_{g,x,y}\qquad(g\in G,x\in X,y\in Y)

defines a functor
T\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{C}_{X\times Y}^{op}\to\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{F}}
, as can be verified easily. It is straightforward to check that these constructions are mutually inverse and give rise to an
\mathbb{F}
-linear isomorphism of categories.
∎

We now reformulate the second case of Example
5.11
, which generalises
[
Eti+15
, Example 7.12.19]
.
Let
\mathrm{B}G
be the category with a single object, elements of
G
as morphisms and group multiplication as composition. Write
G^{op}
for the opposite group of
G
defined in Section
4.3
. Then
(\mathrm{B}G)^{op}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}(G^{op})
.
It is well-known that
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{B}(G^{op}),\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{F}})
is isomorphic to the category
\mathrm{Rep}(G^{op})
of representations of
G^{op}
as an
\mathbb{F}
-linear category.

Example 5.12
.

Suppose
\omega=1\in Z^{3}(G,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
, let
X=Y=\{\bullet\}
be the trivial
G
-set with one element and let
\Psi_{X}=\Psi_{Y}=\Psi\in C^{2}(G,\mathrm{Map}(\{\bullet\},\mathbb{F}^{\times}))
with
\dif\Psi=\omega^{-1}
.

Then
\mathcal{C}_{X\times Y}=\mathrm{B}G
, so we have an
\mathbb{F}
-linear isomorphism of categories

\mathrm{Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M}(\{\bullet\},\Psi),\,\mathcal{M}(\{\bullet\},\Psi))\cong\mathrm{Rep}(G^{op}).

Example
5.12
shows that, in general, classifying all simple
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors between finite semisimple
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module categories is difficult since this classification would include a classification of the simple representations of
G
.

We now generalise Example
5.12
by dropping the condition
\Psi_{X}=\Psi_{Y}
. It will turn out that
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors
\mathcal{M}(\{\bullet\},\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(\{\bullet\},\Psi_{Y})
are classified by certain projective representations.

Definition 5.13
.

[
Eti+15
, Example 7.4.9]

Let
G
be a group and let
\psi\in Z^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
be a 2-cocycle. A
projective representation
of
G
with
Schur multiplier

\psi
consists of an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
V
and a map
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
that satisfies

\rho(g)\circ\rho(h)=\psi(g,h)\,\rho(gh)

for all
g,h\in G
. Projective representations of
G
with Schur multiplier
\psi
form an
\mathbb{F}
-linear category, which will be denoted
\mathrm{Rep}_{\psi}(G)
.

Example 5.14
.

As before, let
\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})
and
\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
be finite semisimple
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module categories. Assume
X=Y=\{\bullet\}
, but let
\Psi_{X}
and
\Psi_{Y}
be arbitrary. Then there is an
\mathbb{F}
-linear isomorphism of categories

\mathrm{Fun}_{\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))\cong\mathrm{Rep}_{\psi}(G^{op}).

for a certain
\psi\in Z^{2}(G^{op},\mathbb{F}^{\times})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.23
,
\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(G,\psi_{X})
and
\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(G,\psi_{Y})
, where
\psi_{X}
and
\psi_{Y}
are the 2-cocycles defined by

\psi_{U}(g,h)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Psi_{U}(g,h,\bullet)\qquad(g,h\in G,U\in\{X,Y\}).

Hence (
33
) is, in this case, equivalent to

A_{gh,\bullet,\bullet}^{F}=\psi_{X}(g,h)\,\psi_{Y}^{-1}(g,h)\ A_{h,\bullet,\bullet}^{F}\,A_{g,\bullet,\bullet}^{F}\qquad(g,h\in G).

This can be rewritten as

(38)

A_{h,\bullet,\bullet}^{F}\,A_{g,\bullet,\bullet}^{F}=\psi(h,g)\ A_{gh,\bullet,\bullet}^{F},

where
\psi\in Z^{2}(G^{op},\mathbb{F}^{\times})
is defined by

\psi(g,h)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\psi_{X}^{-1}(h,g)\,\psi_{Y}(h,g)\quad(g,h\in G).

It is easy to check that
\psi
is indeed a cocycle.
By (
38
),
V\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathbb{F}^{\oplus m_{\bullet,\bullet}^{F}}
and
\rho(g)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=A_{g,\bullet,\bullet}^{F}
defines a projective representation
(V,\rho)
of

with Schur multiplier
\psi
. As in Example
5.11
, this gives rise to an
\mathbb{F}
-linear isomorphism of categories.
∎

5.4
Properties of
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors

Let
\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})
and
\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
be finite semisimple
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module categories. We have already seen in Example
5.11
that the
G
-set
X\times Y
plays an important role in describing the
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
. It is also apparent that the numbers
m_{xy}^{F}
only depend on the orbit of
(x,y)\in X\times Y
and that condition (
33
)

A_{gh,x,y}^{F}=\Psi_{X}(g,h,(gh)\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x)\,\Psi_{Y}^{-1}(g,h,(gh)\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y)\ A_{h,x,y}^{F}\,A_{g,h\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,h\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}

only relates matrices
A_{g,x,y}^{F}
with
(x,y)
from the same orbit. These facts give rise to a decomposition of
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors into direct sums, as will be made precise in the following.

First, we describe the direct sum of
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors by applying the construction from the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Lemma 5.15
.

The direct sum of
(F_{1},s_{1}),\dots,(F_{n},s_{n})\in\mathrm{Fun}_{\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))
is the module functor
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
defined by

m_{xy}^{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{xy}^{F_{i}},

A_{g,x,y}^{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{g,x,y}^{F_{1}}&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&A_{g,x,y}^{F_{n}}\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

It is clear that the functor defined by
(m_{xy}^{F})_{x,y}
is the direct sum of the functors
F_{i}
in the sense that

F(x)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}(x)

for
x\in X
. As for the matrices
(A_{g,x,y}^{F})_{g,x,y}
, note that we implicitly chose

j_{Fxy}^{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\iota_{x}^{(i_{\alpha})}\circ j_{F_{i_{\alpha}},x,y}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y\to F_{i_{\alpha}}(x)\to F(x),\quad p_{Fxy}^{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=p_{F_{i_{\alpha}},x,y}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\circ\pi_{x}^{(i_{\alpha})}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F(x)\to F_{i_{\alpha}}(x)\to y

as inclusions and projections for
F(x)
, where

\iota_{x}^{(i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F_{i}(x)\to F(x),\quad\pi_{x}^{(i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F(x)\to F_{i}(x)\qquad(x\in X,i=1,...,n)

are inclusions and projections for
F(x)
and

i_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\min\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\ \middle|\ \sum_{k=1}^{i}m_{xy}^{F_{k}}\geq\alpha\right\},\quad\alpha^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\alpha-\sum_{k=1}^{i_{\alpha}-1}m_{xy}^{F_{k}}.

Now we can calculate

\displaystyle\left(A_{g,x,y}^{F}\right)_{\beta}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}p_{Fxy}^{\alpha})\circ s_{g,x}\circ j_{F,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}p_{F_{i_{\alpha}},x,y}^{\alpha^{\prime}})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}\pi_{x}^{(i_{\alpha})})\circ s_{g,x}\circ\iota_{g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x}^{(i_{\beta})}\circ j_{F_{i_{\beta}},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{pro:modf_cat}}{=}\delta_{i_{\alpha},i_{\beta}}\ (\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}p_{F_{i_{\alpha}},x,y}^{\alpha^{\prime}})\circ\left(s_{i_{\alpha}}\right)_{g,x}\circ j_{F_{i_{\beta}},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{\beta^{\prime}}=\delta_{i_{\alpha},i_{\beta}}\ \left(A_{g,x,y}^{F_{i_{\alpha}}}\right)^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove that the category of
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors
\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
can be decomposed into subcategories which are parametrised by the orbits of
X\times Y
.

Definition 5.16
.

For every orbit
\Gamma\in(X\times Y)/G
, define the
\mathbb{F}
-linear abelian category
\mathrm{Fun}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))
as the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Fun}_{\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))
consisting of all
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
with
m_{xy}^{F}=0
whenever
(x,y)\notin\Gamma
.

Proposition 5.17
.

There exists an exact
\mathbb{F}
-linear equivalence

\mathrm{Fun}_{\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))\cong\bigoplus_{\Gamma\in(X\times Y)/G}\mathrm{Fun}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})).

Proof.

For a
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
define module functors

F_{\Gamma}\in\mathrm{Fun}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))
by

m_{xy}^{F_{\Gamma}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m_{xy}^{F},&(x,y)\in\Gamma\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.,\qquad A_{g,x,y}^{F_{\Gamma}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{g,x,y}^{F},&(x,y)\in\Gamma\\
\left[\right],&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for
\Gamma\in(X\times Y)/G,g\in G,x\in X
and
y\in Y
, where
[]
denotes the
0\times 0
-matrix. It is straightforward to check that
F_{\Gamma}
is well-defined and Lemma
5.15
implies that
F=\bigoplus_{\Gamma\in(X\times Y)/G}\,F_{\Gamma}
. Furthermore,

\mathrm{Hom}(H_{\Gamma},H_{\Gamma^{\prime}})=0

whenever

H_{\Gamma}\in\mathrm{Fun}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})),\,H_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{Fun}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))\text{ and }\Gamma\neq\Gamma^{\prime}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.18
.

Every simple
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
is contained in
\mathrm{Fun}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))
for some
\Gamma\in(X\times Y)/G
.

We now determine the right adjoint of a
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
.
Recall from Section
5.1
the definition (
30
) of the coherence datum for the right adjoint module functor
(F^{r},s^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})\to\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})
.
To determine matrices
(A_{g,x,y}^{F^{r}})_{g,x,y}
which describe this coherence datum, we need to choose inclusions and projections for
F^{r}(x)
. For
x\in X,y\in Y
, let

\phi_{x,y}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathrm{Hom}(F(x),y)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Hom}(x,F^{r}(y))\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}f\mapsto F^{r}(f)\circ\eta_{x},

where
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})}\Rightarrow F^{r}F
is the unit of the adjunction
F\dashv F^{r}
. Since
F^{r}
is
\mathbb{F}
-linear and composition is bilinear,
\phi_{x,y}
is
\mathbb{F}
-linear.
We now define inclusions for
F^{r}(x)
as

j_{F^{r},y,x}^{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\phi_{x,y}(p_{F,x,y}^{\alpha})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ x\to F^{r}(y)\qquad(x\in X,y\in Y,\alpha=1,\dots,m_{xy}^{F}).

Since they form a basis of
\mathrm{Hom}(y,F^{r}(x))
, there exist projections
(p_{F^{r},y,x}^{\alpha})_{y,x,\alpha}
with

p_{F^{r},y,x}^{\alpha}\circ j_{F^{r},y,x^{\prime}}^{\beta}=\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}\,\mathrm{id}_{x}\,,\quad\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m_{xy}^{F}}j_{F^{r},y,x}^{\alpha}\circ p_{F^{r},y,x}^{\alpha}=\mathrm{id}_{F^{r}(y)}.

Proposition 5.19
.

The right and left adjoint of a
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
are both given by the
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor
(F^{r},s^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})\to\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})
, which is defined by

m_{yx}^{F^{r}}=m_{xy}^{F},

A_{g,y,x}^{F^{r}}=\Psi_{X}(g,g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x)\,\Psi_{Y}^{-1}(g,g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y)\,\left(A_{g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}\right)^{T}.

Here,
A^{T}
denotes the transpose of a matrix
A
.

Proof.

We first establish that
(F^{r},s^{\prime})
is right adjoint to
(F,s)
. Since
F^{r}
is right adjoint to
F
as a functor, we have

m_{yx}^{F^{r}}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathrm{Hom}(y,F^{r}(x)))=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathrm{Hom}(F(y),x))=m_{xy}^{F}.

Substitute
M\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=x,C\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\delta^{g}
and
N\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=y
in (
30
). Then the chain of isomorphisms in (
30
) defines the map

\varphi_{g,y}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=s^{\prime}_{g,y}\circ-\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ \mathrm{Hom}(x,F^{r}(\delta^{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y))\to\mathrm{Hom}(x,\delta^{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}F^{r}(y)).

We now calculate the matrix entries of
A_{g,y,x}^{F^{r}}
. We have

\left(A^{F^{r}}_{g,y,x}\right)^{\alpha}_{\beta}=(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}p_{F^{r},y,x}^{\alpha})\circ s^{\prime}_{g,y}\circ j_{F^{r},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x}^{\beta}=(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}p_{F^{r},y,x}^{\alpha})\circ\varphi_{g,y}(j_{F^{r},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x}^{\beta}).

Set
q_{g,x,y}^{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\varphi_{g,y}(j_{F^{r},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y,x}^{\alpha})
.
By writing out the isomorphisms in (
30
) explicitly, we get

\displaystyle q_{g,x,y}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}\phi_{g^{-1}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,y}\left((\operatorname{ev}_{g}^{R}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}\mathrm{id}_{y})\circ m_{g^{-1},g,y}^{-1}\circ\left(\mathrm{id}_{g^{-1}}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}\phi_{x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{-1}(j_{F^{r},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y,x}^{\alpha})\right)\circ s_{g^{-1},x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\circ m_{g,g^{-1},x}\circ(\operatorname{coev}_{g}^{R}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}\mathrm{id}_{x})

\displaystyle=\omega(g^{-1},g,g^{-1})\,\Psi_{X}(g,g^{-1},x)\,\Psi_{Y}^{-1}(g^{-1},g,y)\,\sum_{\beta=1}^{m_{xy}^{F}}\left(A_{g^{-1},x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}\right)_{\alpha}^{\beta}\,(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}j_{F^{r},y,g^{-1}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x}^{\beta}).

Once more, we abused notation and wrote
m
for the module constraint of
\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})
and of
\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
.
We now apply the coboundary condition (
17
) for
\Psi_{Y}
and
(g^{-1},g,g^{-1},y)
and obtain

q_{g,x,y}^{\alpha}=\Psi_{X}(g,g^{-1},x)\,\Psi_{Y}^{-1}(g,g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y)\,\sum_{\beta=1}^{m_{xy}^{F}}\left(A_{g^{-1},x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}\right)_{\alpha}^{\beta}\,(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}j_{F^{r},y,g^{-1}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x}^{\beta}).

As a result,

\left(A_{g,y,x}^{F^{r}}\right)^{\alpha}_{\beta}=(\mathrm{id}_{g}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}p_{F^{r},y,x}^{\alpha})\circ q_{g,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,y}^{\beta}=\Psi_{X}(g,g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x)\,\Psi_{Y}^{-1}(g,g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y)\,\left(A_{g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}\right)^{\beta}_{\alpha}

\Rightarrow\quad A_{g,y,x}^{F^{r}}=\Psi_{X}(g,g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x)\,\Psi_{Y}^{-1}(g,g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y)\,\left(A_{g^{-1},g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,g\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{F}\right)^{T}.

Replacing

by

in the formulae for

and

, one easily checks that
(F^{r})^{r}\cong F

n

[

Lemma 5.20
.

Let
(F,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X})\to\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y})
be a simple
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functor. Then there exists an orbit
\Gamma\in(X\times Y)/G
with

m_{xy}^{F}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&(x,y)\in\Gamma\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.18
, there exists an orbit
\Gamma\in(X\times Y)/G
such that
m_{xy}^{F}=0
if
(x,y)\notin\Gamma
. The category
\mathrm{Fun}_{\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}}(\mathcal{M}(X,\Psi_{X}),\mathcal{M}(Y,\Psi_{Y}))

5.2

2.4
that
(F,s)
is simple if and only if

\mathrm{Hom}((F,s),(F,s))\cong\mathbb{F}.

By Lemma
5.8
, morphisms
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}(F,s)\Rightarrow(F,s)
of
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-module functors are in bijection with families of matrices
(M_{x,y}^{\eta})_{x,y}
that satisfy

M_{x,y}^{\eta}\,A^{F}_{\bar{k},x,y}=A^{F}_{\bar{k},x,y}\,M^{\eta}_{\bar{k}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,\bar{k}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}\qquad(x\in X,y\in Y,\bar{k}\in G).

Fix an element
(x,y)\in\Gamma
and write
r\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=|\Gamma|
for the length of
\Gamma
. Note that
\bar{r}\in G
generates the stabiliser subgroup of

. For every
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\Gamma

\bar{k}\in G

(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(\bar{k}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,\bar{k}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y)

\eta
is uniquely determined by the single matrix

M_{\bar{k}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,\bar{k}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{\eta}=\left(A_{\bar{k},x,y}^{F}\right)^{-1}\,M_{x,y}^{\eta}\,A_{\bar{k},x,y}^{F}.

A direct calculation shows that a given matrix
M_{x,y}\in\mathrm{Mat}(m_{xy}^{F}\times m_{xy}^{F},\mathbb{F})
determines an endomorphism
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}(F,s)\Rightarrow(F,s)
via
M_{\bar{k}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}x,\bar{k}\mathbin{\vartriangleright}y}^{\eta}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(A_{\bar{k},x,y}^{F})^{-1}\,M_{x,y}\,A_{\bar{k},x,y}^{F}

-linear bijection

\mathrm{Hom}((F,s),(F,s))\cong\left\{M\in\mathrm{Mat}(m_{xy}^{F}\times m_{xy}^{F},\mathbb{F})\ \middle|\ MA_{\bar{r},x,y}^{F}=A_{\bar{r},x,y}^{F}\,M\right\}.

Assume

is a multiple of the identity matrix, every

∎

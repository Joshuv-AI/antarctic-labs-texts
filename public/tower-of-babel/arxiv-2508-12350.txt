Dynamics on Bi-Lagrangian Structures and Cherry maps
Bertuel TANGUE NDAWA

Abstract




Abstract

We consider a bi-Lagrangian structure
(\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
on a manifold
M
, that is,
(M,\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
is a bi-Lagrangian manifold. We prolong bi-Lagrangian structures on
M
, and lift a dynamic on its tangent and cotangent bundles in different ways. In some cases, we show that the lifted structures are affine. In the case of the 2-dimensional torus, we find that an extension of the same dynamic on pairs of so-called Cherry vector fields induces a conjugation action on a subset of Cherry maps (circle maps with a flat). Additionally, we define the linear connections for certain Cherry maps.

Keywords
: Symplectic, Symplectomorphism, Bi-Lagrangian, Hess connection, Cherry vector field, Cherry map, Circle map, Flat piece.

1
Introduction

A non-degenerate and closed 2-form
\omega
on a manifold
M
is called a symplectic form, and the pair
(M,\omega)
is called a symplectic manifold. A bi-Lagrangian manifold is a quadruplet
(M,\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
where
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
is a pair of transversal foliations both Lagrangian (called a bi-Lagrangian structure) on the symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
.
Note that if
(M,\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
is a bilagragian manifold, then the triplet
(\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
is also called a bi-Lagrangian structure on
M
.

Bi-Lagrangian manifolds have been intensively explored in recent years, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
12
]
. Among the many reasons to study them are their connections to geometric quantization (see
[
7
]
) and Koszul-Vinberg cohomology (see
[
3
]
). Moreover, a bi-Lagrangian structure
(\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
on
M
induces in a one-to-one manner a para-Kähler structure
(G,F)
on
M
, where
G
is a pseudo-Riemannian metric and the so-called almost product structure
F
(that is,
F
is a (1,1)-tensor on
M
satisfying
F^{2}=Id
) verifies
G(F(\cdot),F(\cdot))=-G(\cdot,\cdot)
). The three tensors
\omega
,
G
, and
F
are linked by the relation:
\omega(\cdot,\cdot)=G(F(\cdot),\cdot)
, see
[
6
]
. Therefore, a bi-Lagrangian manifold combines symplectic, semi-Riemannian, and almost product structures.

In
[
12
]
, the author studies a lifting of an action of the symplectic group on the set of bi-Lagrangian structures when the manifold is parallelizable. The main result is based on a lifting of bi-Lagrangian manifold, and the action defined is induced by
(\psi,X)\mapsto\psi_{*}X
the canonical action of the diffeomorphism group on the set of vector fields. In this work, we lift in different way the same action defined in
[
12
]
for all bi-Lagrangian manifolds. In the case of 2-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
(a bi-Lagrangian structure can be defined by a pair of vector field without singularity transverse everywhere), the action
(\psi,X)\mapsto\psi_{*}X
also induced a conjugation action on the set of circle map with a flat piece (called Cherry map) generated by a some pairs of so-called Cherry vectors. It is on this basis that we define the linear connection associated with certain Cherry maps.

Before we can explain more precisely and prove our results, it is necessary to present some definitions, fix some notations and formulate some known results we need.

Basics definitions, properties and notations

We assume that all the objects are smooth throughout this paper unless otherwise stated.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
Instead of
\{1,2,\dots,k\}
we will simply write
[k]
.
Einstein summation convention: an index repeated as sub and superscript in the a product represents summation over the range of the index. For example,

2.1
Push forward foliation and conormal distribution

Let
M
be an
m
-manifold. We define a
k
-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
M
to be a decomposition of
M
into a union of disjoint, non-empty, connected, immersed
k
-dimensional submanifolds
\{S_{x}\}_{x\in M}
, called the leaves of the foliation, with the following property (called completely integrable property): every point
y
in
M
has a coordinate chart
(U,y^{1},\dots,y^{m})
such that for each leaf
S_{x}
the components of
U\cap S_{x}
are described by the equations
y^{k+1}\mbox{=constant},\dots,y^{m}=\mbox{constant}
, see
[
8
, p. 501]
.

Let
TM
be the tangent bundle of
M
, and let
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the set of vector fields on
M
, that is,
\Gamma\left(TM\right)=\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
The expressions
T\mathcal{F}\subset TM
, and
\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)=\Gamma\left(T\mathcal{F}\right)\subset\mathfrak{X}(M)
denote the tangent bundle of
\mathcal{F}
, and the set of sections of
T\mathcal{F}
respectively. For each point
y\in M
the vector subspace
T_{y}S_{y}\subset T_{y}M
is called the bundle tangent of
\mathcal{F}
over
y
, and is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{y}
or
T_{y}\mathcal{F}
.
The Lie bracket of two vector fields
X
,
Y
on
M
is given by

[X,Y]:=X\circ Y-Y\circ X
.
Note that the completely integrable property of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
means that
\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
is stable under the Lie bracket, that is, if
X,Y\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
, then
[X,Y]\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
. This is the Frobenius integrability theorem, see
[
8
, p. 496]
.

Let
\psi:M\longrightarrow N
be a diffeomorphism. The push forward
\psi_{*}\mathcal{F}=\{\psi(S_{x})\}_{x\in M}
of
\mathcal{F}
by
\psi
is a foliation on
N
, and

\Gamma\left(\psi_{*}\mathcal{F}\right):=\{\psi_{*}X,\;X\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\}=\psi_{*}\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right).

(2.1)

Let
S\subset M
be a submanifold. The conormal space at a point
x\in S
is defined by

N^{*}_{x}S=\{\xi_{x}\in T_{x}M,\;{\xi_{x}}_{|T_{x}S}=0\}
, and the conormal bundle of
S
is

N^{*}S=\{(\xi_{x},x)\in T^{*}M,\;\xi_{x}\in N^{*}_{x}S\}.

The conormal bundle of a foliation
\mathcal{F}=\{S_{x}\}_{x\in M}
is

N^{*}\mathcal{F}=\{N^{*}S_{x}\}_{x\in M}.

For more detail, see
[
5
, p. 17-18]
).

2.2
Bi-Lagrangian manifold

Let
(M,\omega)
be a
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold. A submanifold
S\subset M
is Lagrangian if for every
X\in\Gamma\left(TS\right)
,
\omega(X,Y)=0
if and only if
Y\in\Gamma\left(TS\right).

That is, the orthogonal section

\Gamma\left(TS\right)^{\perp}=\left\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(M):\;\omega(X,Y)=0,\;\forall Y\in\Gamma\left(TS\right)\right\}

of
\Gamma\left(TS\right)
is equal to
\Gamma\left(TS\right)
. A foliation
\mathcal{F}
is Lagrangian if its leaves are Lagrangian. That is,

\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)^{\perp}=\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right).

A bi-Lagrangian structure on
M
consists of a pair
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
of transversal Lagrangian foliations, together with a symplectic form
\omega
. As a consequence, the tangent bundle decomposes as
TM=T\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus T\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
be a bi-Lagrangian structure on a
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
. Every point in
M
has an open neighborhood
U
which is the domain of a chart with local coordinates
(p^{1},\dots,p^{n},q^{1},\dots,q^{n})
such that

\Gamma(\mathcal{F}_{1})_{\mid U}=\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{n}}\right>,\mbox{ and }\Gamma(\mathcal{F}_{2})_{\mid U}=\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{n}}\right>.

Such a chart is said to be adapted to the bi-Lagrangian structure
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
. Moreover, if

\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{n}dq^{i}\wedge dp^{i}

then such a chart is said to be adapted to the bi-Lagrangian structure
(\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
.

2.3
Linear connection

Let
\nabla
be a linear connection. The torsion tensor
T_{\nabla}
(or simply
T
if there is no ambiguity), and curvature tensor
R_{\nabla}
(or simply
R
) are given respectively by

T_{\nabla}(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-[X,Y],\;X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M),

and

R_{\nabla}(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}{\nabla_{Y}Z}-\nabla_{Y}{\nabla_{X}Z}-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,\;X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a linear connection
\nabla

-

parallelizes
\omega
if
\nabla\omega=0
, this means,

\omega(\nabla_{X}{Y},Z)+\omega(Y,\nabla_{X}{Z})=X\omega(Y,Z)
for every
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
;

-

preserves
\mathcal{F}
if
\nabla{\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)}\subseteq\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
, that is,

\nabla_{X}Y\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
for every
(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
.

2.4
Prolongations of some objects to tangent bundle

We will present the vertical and complete lifts of a tensor field, the vertical lift of a linear connection, and the vertical lift of a foliation. If
T
is a tensor field on a manifold
M
,
T^{v}
and
T^{c}
denote the vertical and the complete lifts of
T
to
TM
, respectively. For more details, see
[
13
,
14
]
.

Let
\pi:TM\longrightarrow M
be the natural projection,
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
(the set of 1-forms on
M
).

-

f^{v}=f\circ\pi
, and
f^{c}=df
;

-

X^{v}(f^{c})=(Xf)^{v}
, and
X^{c}(f^{c})=(Xf)^{c}
;

-

\alpha^{v}(Y^{c})=(\alpha(Y))^{v}
, and
\alpha^{c}(Y^{c})=(\alpha(Y))^{c}
,
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

From these definitions, it follows that

\begin{array}[]{ll}(fg)^{v}=f^{v}g^{v},&(fg)^{c}=f^{c}g^{v}+f^{v}g^{c},\\
(fX)^{v}=f^{v}X^{v},&(fX)^{c}=f^{c}X^{v}+f^{v}X^{c},\\
(f\alpha)^{v}=f^{v}\alpha^{v},&(f\alpha)^{c}=f^{c}\alpha^{v}+f^{v}\alpha^{c}\end{array}

(2.2)

where
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, and
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Let
S
and
T
be two tensor fields on
M
. By (
2.2
), the vertical and complete lifts of a tensor field on
M
are defined inductively by using the following formulas:

\begin{array}[]{ll}(S+T)^{v}=S^{v}+T^{v},&(S+T)^{c}=S^{c}+T^{c},\\
(S\otimes T)^{v}=S^{v}\otimes T^{v},&(S\otimes T)^{c}=S^{c}\otimes T^{v}+S^{v}\otimes T^{c}.\end{array}

As a consequence, if
T
is a
(r,0)
tensor, then

T^{c}(X_{1}^{c},\dots,X_{r}^{c})=(T(X_{1},\dots,X_{r}))^{c}\;\mbox{for all }X_{1},\dots,X_{r}\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

(2.3)

In this paper, we only need the complete lifts of a linear connection and a foliation. The complete lift of a linear connection
\nabla
is defined as

\nabla^{c}_{X^{c}}Y^{c}=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{c}.

(2.4)

Moreover, if
T
is a tensor, then

\nabla^{c}T=(\nabla T)^{c}.

(2.5)

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
14
]
the author shows that for every
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
[X^{c},Y^{c}]=[X,Y]^{c}
, see Remark 4, p.237. Thus, by the Frobenius integrability theorem, if
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation, then
\mathcal{F}^{c}
is the foliation whose the set of sections is

\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}^{c}\right):=\{X^{c},\;X\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\}=\left(\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\right)^{c}.

(2.6)

3.1
Symplectic manifolds

Symplectic manifolds have been studied since the 18th century. Among many significant results in the theory, the cotangent bundle of a manifold is naturally endowed with a so-called tautological 2-form, often referred to as the canonical symplectic form. This section is mainly devoted to the precise formulation of this result. For further details on the concepts covered here, the reader is referred to
[
5
,
9
]
.

Let
M
be an
m
-dimensional manifold,
and let
{}^{*}\hskip-2.84544pt\pi:TM\longrightarrow M
be the natural projection.
The tautological 1-form, also known as the Liouville 1-form,
\theta
is defined by

\theta_{(x,\alpha_{x})}(v)=\alpha_{x}\left((T_{(x,\alpha_{x})}{{}^{*}\hskip-2.84544pt\pi})(v)\right),\;(x,\alpha_{x})\in T^{*}M,\,v\in T_{(x,\alpha_{x})}T^{*}M.

The exterior differential
d\theta
of
\theta
is called the canonical symplectic form, also known as the Liouville 2-form, on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
.

Note that for any coordinate chart
(U,x^{1},\dots,x^{m})
on
M
, with associated cotangent coordinate chart
(T^{*}U,x^{1},\dots,x^{m},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})
, we have

\theta=\sum_{1}^{m}\xi_{i}dx^{i}\mbox{ and }d\theta=\sum_{1}^{m}d\xi_{i}\wedge dx^{i}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold. The cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
of
M
, naturally endowed with the canonical symplectic form
d\theta
is a symplectic manifold.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\psi:M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}
be a
diffeomorphism. The lift

\hat{\psi}:z=(x,\alpha_{x})\longmapsto(\psi(x),(\psi^{-1})^{*}_{x}\alpha_{x})
of
\psi
is
a symplectomorphism from
(T^{*}M_{1},d\theta_{1})
to

(T^{*}M_{2},d\theta_{2})
where
d\theta_{1}
and
d\theta_{2}
are the canonical symplectic forms on
T^{*}M_{1}
and
T^{*}M_{2}
, respectively.

3.2
Affine and push forward bi-Lagrangian structures

In
[
7
]
, the author shows that a bi-Lagrangian manifold
have a unique torsionless connection (called Hess (or bi-Lagrangian) connection) which parallelizes
\omega
, and preserves both foliations. The explicit formula for a Hess connection was given by Boyom, see
[
1
,
2
]
. A bi-Lagrangian manifold is said to be affine when the curvature tensor of its Hess connection vanishes identically This condition is both necessary and sufficient to define Koszul-Vinberg Cohomology, see
[
3

. Affine bi-Lagrangian manifolds are characterized as follows:

Theorem 3.3
.

[
7
, Theor.2:, p.159]

Let
(\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
be a bi-Lagrangian structure on a
2n
-dimensional manifold
M
, with

\nabla
denoting its Hess connection. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

a.

The connection
\nabla
is flat.

b.

Each point of
M
has a coordinate chart adapted to
(\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
.

The following result describes a method for pushing forward a bi-Lagrangian structure, providing insight into how these structures can be transformed under a symplectomorphism.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
12
, Lem.2.2.,p. 6]

Let
(M,\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
be a bi-Lagrangian manifold with
\nabla
as its Hess connection, and let
N
be a manifold which is diffeomorphic to
M
. Then for any diffeomorphism
\psi:M\longrightarrow N
, the structure
((\psi^{-1})^{*}\omega,\psi_{*}\mathcal{F}_{1},\psi_{*}\mathcal{F}_{2})
is bi-Lagrangian on
N
, with
\nabla^{\psi}:(X,Y)\mapsto\psi_{*}\nabla_{\psi_{*}^{-1}X}\psi_{*}^{-1}Y
as its Hess connection. Moreover, if
(\omega,\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
is affine, then so is
((\psi^{-1})^{*}\omega,\psi_{*}\mathcal{F}_{1},\psi_{*}\mathcal{F}_{2})
.

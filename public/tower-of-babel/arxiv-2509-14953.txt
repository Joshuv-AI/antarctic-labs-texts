A Quantum Perspective on Uniqueness Pairs for the Fourier Transform
Oleg Szehr

Abstract
. Kulikov, Nazarov, and Sodin recently introduced the notion of criticality to analyze when discrete subsets of the real line form uniqueness pairs for the Fourier transform, relying crucially on estimates derived from the Wirtinger–Poincaré inequality. In this work, drawing analogies from quantum mechanics, we propose an alternative approach to studying uniqueness sets in a Fourier-symmetric Sobolev space. Our method is based on eigenvalue estimates and offers a simplified perspective on the problem.



The Fourier-symmetric Sobolev space

\mathcal{H}=\left\{f\>\Big|\>\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+x^{2})|f(x)|^{2}\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+k^{2})|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\textnormal{d}k<\infty\right\}

has recently attracted attention in the study of crystalline measures and sampling theory. In this note, we examine this space from a quantum-mechanical perspective, recovering recent results about uniqueness sets associated with the Fourier transform. We employ the “unitary convention” for the Fourier transform

\displaystyle\hat{f}(k)=\mathcal{F}[f](k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-ikx}\textnormal{d}x,\quad f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\hat{f}](x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(k)e^{ikx}\textnormal{d}k.

It will be convenient to view
\mathcal{H}
as a subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, by introducing the norm

\left|\!\left|f\right|\!\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}k^{2}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\textnormal{d}k

and interpreting
\mathcal{H}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\>|\>|\!|f|\!|_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty\}
. An interesting observation is that
\mathcal{H}
arises as both the domain and the range of a self-adjoint, positive-definite operator. Using Plancherel’s identity and integration by parts we find

\displaystyle\left|\!\left|f\right|\!\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}k^{2}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\textnormal{d}k

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f(x)}x^{2}f(x)\textnormal{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f(x)}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{2}}(x)dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{f}H(f)\textnormal{d}x,

where

H=\frac{1}{2}\left(x^{2}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\right).

H
is the Hamiltonian operator of the classical quantum harmonic oscillator
[
S
]
. The eigenvalue-eigenvector equation
Hf=Ef
is the Schrödinger equation of the system
[
S
]
.
Our computation assumes that the second derivative of
f
exists. However, for the construction of
\mathcal{H}
this is immaterial.

Theorem 1
.

The following assertions hold for the Fourier-symmetric Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}
:

(1)

\mathcal{H}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{H}
is the domain and image of the self-adjoint and positive operator
H=\frac{1}{2}\left(x^{2}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\right)
.

(2)

H
has eigenvectors that are Hermite functions of the form

\phi_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x-\partial_{x})\right)^{n}\phi_{0},\ n\geq 0,

with
\phi_{0}=\pi^{-1/4}e^{-x^{2}/2}
. The respective eigenvalues are
E_{n}=(n+\frac{1}{2})
.

(3)

H
is self-dual under the Fourier transform:
\hat{H}=\mathcal{F}H\mathcal{F}^{-1}=H
.

(4)

The Hermite functions form an orthogonal basis for
\mathcal{H}
.

(5)

For all
f\in\mathcal{H}
,
f\neq 0
, it holds that
|\!|f|\!|_{\mathcal{H}}\geq|\!|f|\!|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
and equality is achieved if and only if
f
is a multiple of
\phi_{0}
.

The properties
(1), (2), (3)
are classical facts in the study of the quantum oscillator, see e.g.
[
S
]
and
[
PS
]
for a nice summary. Property
(4)
has recently been shown in
[
Z
]
, but here
(4)
follows immediately from
(2)
and our computation above. Property
(5)
is an uncertainty principle in
\mathcal{H}
: The two integrals in the definition of
|\!|f|\!|_{\mathcal{H}}
cannot be simultaneously small. This property follows from the eigenvalue estimate

|\!|f|\!|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{f}H(f)\textnormal{d}x\geq 2E_{0}|\!|f|\!|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

and the fact that the eigenvalues of
H
carry no degeneracy.

A central theme of our analysis is the spectral behavior of Hamiltonians confined by infinite potential barriers. We write for the Hamiltonian of the confined system

H_{[a,b]}=\begin{cases}H\ \textnormal{if}\ x\in[a,b],\\
\infty\ \textnormal{else},\end{cases}

in a distributional sense. This corresponds to the Schrödinger equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions

(0.1)

\displaystyle Hf=Ef\ \textnormal{under}\ f(a)=f(b)=0.

Similarly to the unconfined harmonic oscillator, the solutions of the confined system (
0.1
) have been studied exhaustively, e.g.
[
BS
]
. With the boundary conditions
H_{[a,b]}
is a self-adjoint and positive definite operator on
L^{2}{[a,b]}
. We will write
E_{[a,b]}^{(n)}
for the eigenvalues of the confined system and
\psi_{[a,b]}^{(n)}
for the respective eigenvectors. It holds automatically that
E_{[a,b]}^{(n)}>E_{n}
because the introduction of confinement can only lead to an increase of energy levels.

Definition 2
.

Let
(\Lambda,M)
be a pair of countable subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Enumerate the elements of
\Lambda
and
M
in increasing order.

(1)

The pair
(\Lambda,M)
is uniformly supercritical if

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\max\{|\lambda_{j}|,|\lambda_{j+1}|\}(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})<\pi\ \textnormal{and}\ \sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\max\{|\mu_{j}|,|\mu_{j+1}|\}(\mu_{j+1}-\mu_{j})<\pi.

The pair
(\Lambda,M)
is supercritical if

\lim\sup|\lambda_{j}|(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})<\pi\ \textnormal{and}\ \lim\sup|\mu_{j}|(\mu_{j+1}-\mu_{j})<\pi.

The pair
(\Lambda,M)
is subcritical if

\lim\inf|\lambda_{j}|(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})>\pi\ \textnormal{and}\ \lim\inf{|\mu_{j}|}(\mu_{j+1}-\mu_{j})>\pi.

(2)

The pair
(\Lambda,M)
has uniformly supercritical ground state energy if

\inf_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\sqrt{E_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}}{\max\{|\lambda_{j}|,|\lambda_{j+1}|\}}>1\ \textnormal{and}\ \inf_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\sqrt{E_{[\mu_{j},\mu_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}}{\max\{|\mu_{j}|,|\mu_{j+1}|\}}>1,

where
E_{[a,b]}^{(0)}
stands for the ground state energy of the Schrödinger equation (
0.1
). We define the notions of super- and subcritical ground state energy, mutatis mutandis, as in item
(1)
.

Remark 3
.

A pair
(\tilde{\Lambda},\tilde{M})
is called uniformly supercritical in
[
KNS
]
if

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\max\{|\tilde{\lambda}_{j}|,|\tilde{\lambda}_{j+1}|\}(\tilde{\lambda}_{j+1}-\tilde{\lambda}_{j})<\frac{1}{2}\ \textnormal{and}\ \sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\max\{|\tilde{\mu}_{j}|,|\tilde{\mu}_{j+1}|\}(\tilde{\mu}_{j+1}-\tilde{\mu}_{j})<\frac{1}{2}.

This discrepancy is due to the choice of Fourier convention. To transcript between the conventions consider
M^{\prime}=2\pi\tilde{M}
such that
\hat{f}(\mu_{i}^{\prime})=0
for all
\mu_{j}^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
under our convention. The respective criticality condition for
M^{\prime}
reads
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\max\{|\mu_{j}^{\prime}|,|\mu_{j+1}^{\prime}|\}(\mu_{j+1}^{\prime}-\tilde{\mu}_{j}^{\prime})<\frac{1}{2}\cdot(2\pi)^{2}
.
(\tilde{\Lambda},M^{\prime})
is a uniqueness set if and only if
(\alpha\tilde{\Lambda},\frac{1}{\alpha}M^{\prime})
,
\alpha\neq 0
is a uniqueness set. We use this property setting
\Lambda=\sqrt{2\pi}\tilde{\Lambda}
and
M=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}M^{\prime}
.

The following theorem takes a pivotal role in the development of the theory of criticality. It serves as a
“toy model”
to outline the main line of reasoning in a way that is
“short and free of cumbersome technical details”
in
[
KNS
]
.

Theorem 4

(Theorem 1A
[
KNS
]
)
.

Let
(\Lambda,M)
be a uniformly supercritical pair. Then the only function
f\in\mathcal{H}
such that
f(\lambda_{i})=\hat{f}(\mu_{i})=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
is
f=0
.

An essential part in the proof of the theorem is played the Wirtinger–Poincaré inequality. We will use this theorem as a cornerstone to guide our further investigation. But rather than making use of the Wirtinger–Poincaré inequality, we will employ spectral methods to arrive at our estimates. Our definition of uniformly supercritical ground state energy is motivated by the following spectral estimate on the Rayleigh quotient of the confined Hamiltonian. It holds that

\frac{\int_{a}^{b}\bar{f}H_{[a,b]}f\textnormal{d}x}{\int_{a}^{b}|f|^{2}\textnormal{d}x}\geq E_{[a,b]}^{(0)},\ f\neq 0.

This leads to the following theorem that mimics Theorem
4
.

Theorem 5
.

Let the pair
(\Lambda,M)
have uniformly supercritical ground energy. Then the only function
f\in\mathcal{H}
such that
f(\lambda_{i})=\hat{f}(\mu_{i})=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
is
f=0
.

Proof.

We make use of Theorem
1
, item
(1)
, and of the unitarity of the Fourier transform. We phrase the confined Schrödinger equation as an eigenvalue problem of a confined Hamiltonian. We then use the mentioned lower bound on the Rayleigh quotient to estimate expectations of
H_{[a,b]}
and
\hat{H}_{[a,b]}
. Recall that
H=\hat{H}
according to Theorem
4
, item
(3)
. This yields

\displaystyle|\!|f|\!|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{f}Hf\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\hat{f}}\>\widehat{Hf}\textnormal{d}k

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{f}Hf\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\hat{f}}\hat{H}\hat{f}\textnormal{d}k

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}\bar{f}Hf\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mu_{j}}^{\mu_{j+1}}\overline{\hat{f}}\hat{H}\hat{f}\textnormal{d}k\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}{\bar{f}}H_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}f\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mu_{j}}^{\mu_{j+1}}\overline{\hat{f}}\hat{H}_{[\mu_{j},\mu_{j+1}]}\hat{f}\textnormal{d}k\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(E_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}^{(0)}\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}|f|^{2}\textnormal{d}x+E_{[\mu_{j},\mu_{j+1}]}^{(0)}\int_{\mu_{j}}^{\mu_{j+1}}|\hat{f}|^{2}\textnormal{d}k\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{E_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}{\max\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}x^{2}|f|^{2}\textnormal{d}x+\frac{E_{[\mu_{j},\mu_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}{\max\{|\mu_{j}|^{2},|\mu_{j+1}|^{2}\}}\int_{\mu_{j}}^{\mu_{j+1}}k^{2}|\hat{f}|^{2}\textnormal{d}k\right).

Akin to assuming uniformly supercritical ground state energy of
(\Lambda,M)
, we assume that the quantities in Definition
2
,
(4)
, are bounded from below by
l
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\!|f|\!|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq l^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j+1}}x^{2}|f|^{2}\textnormal{d}x+\int_{\mu_{j}}^{\mu_{j+1}}k^{2}|\hat{f}|^{2}\textnormal{d}k\right)\geq l^{2}|\!|f|\!|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

If
l>1
this is a contradiction unless
f=0
.
∎

We see three advantages in Theorem
5
over Theorem
4
:
1)
Obtaining lower estimates on the energy levels
E_{[a,b]}^{(n)}
is straight forward. Their analytic form can be derived using perturbation theory.
\emph{2)}
Using eigenvalue bounds, the proof of Theorem
5
provides a direct route to sharpness, with the lower bound on the Rayleigh quotient attained by the ground-state eigenfunction.
3)
The derivation of Theorem
5
is simpler than the one of Theorem
4
presented in
[
KNS
]
.

We begin by studying the energy levels. Assuming
0\notin[a,b]
, the energy levels of the confined harmonic oscillators can be bounded by the respective energy levels of a particle in constant elevated box potential

H_{[a,b]}^{down}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\min\{a^{2},b^{2}\}\ \textnormal{if}\ x\in[a,b],\\
\infty\ \textnormal{else},\end{cases}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad H_{[a,b]}>H_{[a,b]}^{down},

H_{[a,b]}^{up}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\max\{a^{2},b^{2}\}\ \textnormal{if}\ x\in[a,b],\\
\infty\ \textnormal{else},\end{cases}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad H_{[a,b]}<H_{[a,b]}^{up}.

These systems have the following eigenvectors for
n\geq 0

\psi_{[a,b]}^{(n)}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{b-a}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi(n+1)(x-a)}{b-a}\right),

with respective eigenvalues

E_{[a,b]}^{down,(n)}=\frac{(n+1)^{2}\pi^{2}}{2(b-a)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\min\{a^{2},b^{2}\}\ \textnormal{and}\ E_{[a,b]}^{up,(n)}=\frac{(n+1)^{2}\pi^{2}}{2(b-a)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\max\{a^{2},b^{2}\}.

For the ground state energy of the confined Harmonic oscillators this implies

\frac{\pi^{2}}{2(b-a)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\max\{a^{2},b^{2}\}>E^{(0)}_{[a,b]}>\frac{\pi^{2}}{2(b-a)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\min\{a^{2},b^{2}\}.

To compare theorems
4

5
we show that the notions of criticality and criticality of the ground state energy are closely related.

Lemma 6
.

The following assertions hold for a pair
(\Lambda,M)
:

(1)

If
(\Lambda,M)
has uniformly supercritical ground state energy then
(\Lambda,M)
is uniformly supercritical.

(2)

If
(\Lambda,M)
is uniformly supercritical, then
(\Lambda,M)
has uniformly supercritical ground state energy with the exception of maximally a finite number of intervals
\{[\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i+1}]\}_{i}
,
\{[\mu_{j},\mu_{j+1}]\}_{i}
with
|i|<N_{\Lambda}
and
|j|<N_{M}
.

(3)

\displaystyle\lim\inf\frac{\sqrt{E_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}}{\max\{|\lambda_{j}|,|\lambda_{j+1}|\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\lim\sup(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})\max\{|\lambda_{j}|,|\lambda_{j+1}|\}},

\displaystyle\lim\inf\frac{\sqrt{E_{[\mu_{j},\mu_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}}{\max\{|\mu_{j}|,|\lambda_{j+1}|\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\lim\sup(\mu_{j+1}-\mu_{j})\max\{|\mu_{j}|,|\mu_{j+1}|\}}.

Proof.

We show the assertions for
\Lambda
; the same reasoning applies to
M
.

(1)

Making use of our upper bound on the ground state energy and the condition
\frac{{E_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}}{\max\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}>1
, we have

\displaystyle 1<

\displaystyle\frac{{E_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}}{\max\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}<\frac{\pi^{2}}{{2}(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\max\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}+\frac{1}{2},

which implies
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\max\{|\lambda_{j}|,|\lambda_{j+1}|\}(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})<\pi
.

(2)

Making use of our lower bound on the ground state energy and the condition
\max\{|\lambda_{j}|,|\lambda_{j+1}|\}(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})<\pi
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{E_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}}{\max\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{{2}(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\max\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}+\frac{\min\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}{2\max\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\min\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}{\max\{|\lambda_{j}|^{2},|\lambda_{j+1}|^{2}\}}.

It is obvious that if
|\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j}|\rightarrow 0
then the second summand approaches
\frac{1}{2}
and consequently in this case
\inf_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\sqrt{E_{[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j+1}]}^{(0)}}}{\max\{|\lambda_{j}|,|\lambda_{j+1}|\}}>1
with the possible exception of a finite number of terms. Hence, we show that
|\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j}|\rightarrow 0
. Using standard
\theta(\cdot)
notation from asymptotic analysis, suppose that

|\lambda_{n}|=\theta(|n|^{\alpha})
,
\alpha>0
. Then making use of a telescope sum we find

\displaystyle|\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{0}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}|\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta(|n|^{-\alpha}).

If
\alpha>1
then the right hand side converges, while the left hand side grows, which is a contradiction. Hence
\alpha\leq 1
. It can be argued similarly that
\alpha<1
.
In this case
\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\rightarrow 0
when
n\rightarrow\infty
.

(3)

This follows from the above proofs of
(1)
and
(2)
.

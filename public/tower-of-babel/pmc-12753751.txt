A new method for determining the pore water pressure around twin shallow circular tunnels
Authors: Tao Zhan, Shengbiao Shan, Chao Wang, Peiyuan He, Jinfeng Zou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To obtain the distribution rules of pore water pressure for twin shallow circular tunnels, the shallow circular tunnels were regarded as fully saturated, homogeneous, isotropic structures in a semi-infinite space. Coordinate transformations were utilized to obtain the water pressure control equations in a bipolar coordinate system. Through the Schwarz alternating method, the calculation method for the pore water pressure around a single tunnel was generalized to twin tunnels, and a new method for determining the pore water pressure around twin shallow circular tunnels in a semi-infinite space was proposed for the first time by using the bipolar coordinate system method and the Schwarz alternating method. Solutions for the pore water pressure were obtained via multiple iterations. The calculation results obtained via the proposed method were compared with the other theoretical calculation results and numerical simulation results, and we further utilized the proposed method to analyse the influencing factors of the pore water pressure. The results show that the maximum error between the theoretical calculation results and the numerical simulation results was only 2.15%; thus, the rationality of this new method was effectively verified. The relative error between the results obtained by the proposed method and the results obtained by the existing method was only 1.0%, and both very well matched the numerical simulation results; thus, the high accuracy of the proposed method was confirmed. The number of tunnels and the centre distance between the twin tunnels are the main influencing factors of the pore water pressure. According to their influences, the distribution pattern of the pore water pressure around the twin shallow circular tunnels can be preliminarily determined. Therefore, the method proposed in this study provides a theoretical guidance for the development of design and construction programs for water-rich tunnel engineering.

Introduction

Underground works are invariably constructed with a strong emphasis on the impact of groundwater. Especially in the process of water-rich tunnel excavation, groundwater can easily flow into the tunnel due to the seepage effect, generating pore water pressure in the surrounding rock, which will negatively affect the effective stresses in the tunnel surrounding rock and cause the tunnel structure to fail. Therefore, to guarantee the safety of the tunnel structure during construction, the distribution pattern of pore water pressure around the tunnel under the influence of seepage should be analysed to maintain the stability of the tunnel structure in groundwater-rich areas1–3.

To date, several studies have been conducted on the pore water pressure around tunnels. For a single tunnel, Shin et al.4 studied the potential effect of the pore water pressure on the tunnel lining via the finite element method and proposed a method for estimating the design curve of the pore water pressure for the secondary lining. Pan et al.5 analysed the effect of pore water pressure on the stability of the palm face via fluid mechanics and numerical simulation. Park et al.6 derived an analytical solution for the seepage force around a circular tunnel via the complex variable method. Zhang et al.7 used complex variable theory and the conformal mapping method to determine the half-plane excess pore pressure around an unlined tunnel caused by grouting pressure and further obtained an analytical solution for soil consolidation based on the conformal transformation method and Terzaghi–Rendulic consolidation theory. They also analysed the influencing factors of the pore water pressure around a tunnel and ground consolidation settlement. Yang et al.8 considered the influence of cutter head rotation and slurry specific gravity on pressure boundary conditions and proposed a multifield coupling model that can be used to analyse the distribution pattern of the excess pore water pressure. Lee et al.9 proposed a calculation approach for groundwater seepage in surrounding rock by using the limit analysis upper limit method. Yang et al.10 proposed stability solutions that consider the water seepage of shallow tunnels on the basis of strength reduction theory. For twin tunnels, Zhang et al.11 derived analytical solutions of the non-Darcy seepage field in shallow single-hole and double-hole circular tunnels via the principle of the mirror method. Zhu et al.12 derived the stable seepage field of twin parallel tunnels via the complex variable method. Zhang13 derived an analytical solution for the stable seepage field of twin parallel tunnels in a semi-infinite plane via the principle of the mirror method. Wan et al.14 used fully grouted vibrating-wire piezometers to measure rapid pore water pressure changes around tunnels, and they identified five distinct immediate pore water pressure responses. Guo et al.15 proposed a new alternating analytical approach to establish a new calculation method for the steady-state seepage field around underwater twin tunnels in semi-infinite saturated aquifers. Yang et al.16 used the nonlinear failure criterion and modified tangential technique to propose upper-bound solutions for the critical supporting pressure on a deep tunnel face under the conditions of pore water pressure. Hu et al.17 used Terzaghi–Rendulic theory to establish the governing equations for the dissipation of excess pore water pressure in the surrounding soil mass of a tunnel; thus, they verified the rationality of the novel analytical solution for the consolidation behaviour of viscoelastic saturated soft soil subjected to large-scale ground loading. According to our literature review, theoretical research on the pore water pressure around tunnels has focused mostly on single tunnels, and research on the pore water pressure around twin tunnels has been relatively limited, especially research on the change rule of the pore water pressure around twin shallow tunnels. In addition, although the change rule of pore water pressure can be described by analytical equations, it is often affected by complex boundary conditions, so these analytical methods usually lack practical applicability and have not attracted substantial interest in engineering. To facilitate the development of design and construction programs for water-rich shallow buried tunnel projects, it is necessary to study the theoretical calculation method for the pore water pressure around twin shallow circular tunnels to explore the distribution range of the pore water pressure and its change rule.

The form of the surrounding pore water pressure distribution due to tunnel excavation is often complex, and twin shallow circular tunnels are represented as plane strain problems in semi-infinite spaces. Considering the difficulty of solving for the displacement and stress of the surrounding rock and the variation in pore water pressure in a semi-infinite space under the Cartesian coordinate system or polar coordinate system, according to Jeffery18, the bipolar coordinate system method is introduced to solve the elasticity problem in semi-infinite spaces. As a special curvilinear coordinate system, the bipolar coordinate system can greatly simplify the calculation flow when specific conditions such as perimeter rock displacement19 and pore water pressure are considered. Moreover, since twin shallow circular tunnels are symmetric about the vertical direction (y-axis), according to Hu et al.17, Bi et al.20 and Zhang et al.21, the Schwarz alternating method is often used to obtain analytical solutions for the mechanics of symmetric tunnels. Therefore, the bipolar coordinate system method and Schwarz alternating method are used in this study to solve for the pore water pressure around twin shallow circular tunnels via several iterative calculations. According to the results of the above literature review, the Schwarz alternating method has not yet been adopted for the theoretical study of pore water pressure around tunnels. Therefore, the number of iterations and the calculation accuracy of the pore water pressure around tunnels are controlled by setting the average pore water pressure tolerance value [p] in this study to obtain an accurate analytical solution for the pore water pressure around the twin shallow circular tunnels, thus effectively compensating for the deficiencies in the theoretical calculation of the pore water pressure around tunnels.

In this study, shallow circular tunnels in a fully saturated, homogeneous, isotropic semi-infinite space are used as an example. We use the bipolar coordinate system method to derive a calculation method for the pore water pressure around a shallow circular tunnel. On this basis, we further use the Schwarz alternating method and set the allowable value of the pore water pressure to control the number of iterations and the computational accuracy of the output results to establish a new method for determining the pore water pressure around twin shallow circular tunnels. By comparing the results obtained via the proposed method with the numerical simulation results and the theoretical calculation results obtained via the existing method, the rationality of the method proposed in this study is verified, and its calculation accuracy is further improved. The method is further utilized to theoretically analyse the main influencing factors of pore water pressure around tunnels, and a basic law of seepage in shallow tunnels is proposed accordingly, which is highly important in guiding the safe construction of water-rich shallow tunnels.

Problem description and basic assumptions

In Fig. 1, twin shallow circular tunnels are arranged horizontally, and the centre depth of the left tunnel is the same as that of the right tunnel. The radius, depth, centre distance, and ground surface hydraulic head of the twin shallow circular tunnels are r, d, L, and hw, respectively. The left and right tunnels correspond to Tunnel I and Tunnel II, respectively, and the ratio of the centre depth to the radius of each of the left and right tunnels does not exceed 7; thus, the twin tunnels in Fig. 1 can be considered shallow tunnels.Fig. 1Twin shallow circular tunnels in a semi-infinite space.

Twin shallow circular tunnels in a semi-infinite space.

In Fig. 1, we take the straight line where the ground surface is located as the x-axis and the straight line above the centre of the left circular tunnel and perpendicular to the x-axis as the y1-axis. The intersection of the two is the coordinate origin O1 of the local Cartesian coordinate system x1O1y1. Similarly, we take the straight line over the centre of the right circular tunnel and perpendicular to the x-axis as the y2-axis, and the intersection of the two is the coordinate origin O2 of the local Cartesian coordinate system x2O2y2. The above two local Cartesian coordinate systems correspond to the left tunnel (Tunnel I) and the right tunnel (Tunnel II), respectively, and both belong to the global Cartesian coordinate system xOy, as shown in Fig. 1. Therefore, if the length of each tunnel is sufficiently long, the three-dimensional space problem can be simplified to a two-dimensional plane problem. The hydraulic head of the ground surface is greater than that of the ground, so the soil is fully saturated. The permeability coefficient of the soil is too small to keep the ground surface hydraulic head stable. Therefore, the shallow twin circular tunnels are buried in a fully saturated, homogeneous and isotropous semi-infinite space. In addition, owing to the mutual symmetry of the twin shallow circular tunnels parallel to each other in the semi-infinite plane, the centre distance between the twin tunnels is assumed to be smaller. When the twin shallow circular tunnels in a semi-infinite plane are regarded as the object of this study, it is very difficult to solve for the pore water pressure around the twin shallow circular tunnels directly. Therefore, we use the Schwarz alternating method to regard the problem of twin shallow circular tunnels in the semi-infinite plane as a superposition problem after solving the problem for the left and right tunnels in several iterations to obtain the distribution pattern of the pore water pressure around the twin shallow circular tunnels via the Schwarz alternating method.

Derivation of the pore water pressure around a shallow circular tunnel

According to Zou et al.22, with the constant hydraulic head seepage mode, the method for determining the pore water pressure around a shallow circular tunnel is derived in the bipolar coordinate system to provide a theoretical basis for solving the problem for the twin shallow circular tunnels.

Bipolar coordinate system method

The bipolar coordinate system is a curvilinear coordinate system that was first introduced by Jeffery18 to solve semi-infinite spatial elasticity problems and simplify the related calculations. In Fig. 4, O1(0,a) and O2(0, −a) are the 2 poles of the bipolar coordinate system and are symmetric about the x-axis. Point P is the origin of the Cartesian coordinate system, and a represents the distance from pole O1 or O2 to point P. If any points M(x1,y1) and N(x2,y2) in the Cartesian coordinate system can be denoted as M(α1,β1) and N(α2, β2) in the bipolar coordinate system, then α1 = ln(l2/l1) and β1 = θ2−θ1 , where l1 and l2 are the distances from point M to points O1 and O2, respectively, and θ1 and θ2 are the angles of MO1 and MO2 with the x-axis, respectively. Therefore, the different α values in the bipolar coordinate system can be plotted as a series of coaxial eccentric circular curves called α curves with O1 and O2 as the poles, radius acsch(α), and centre coordinates (0,acoth(α)). Different β values can be plotted as circular curves from the 2 poles and orthogonal to each of the α circles, which are called β curves.Fig. 4Bipolar coordinate system.

Bipolar coordinate system.

We let the left and lower sides be the positive directions of the x- and y-axes of the Cartesian coordinate system, respectively; then, α is positive below the x-axis, and β is positive to the left of the y-axis. The coordinates (α, β) of any point in the bipolar coordinate system correspond to the intersection of different α and β curves. According to the rules specified above for the coordinates, the interval of α is (−∞, + ∞), and α = 0 when the coordinate point is located on the x-axis. When the coordinate point is located at either of the 2 poles O1 and O2, α is + ∞ and − ∞, respectively. The interval of values of β is (−π,π), and β is π and −π when the coordinate points are located in the O1P and O2P segments on the y-axis, respectively, and β is 0 when the coordinate points are located elsewhere on the y-axis. The larger the radius of the curve is, the smaller the absolute values of α and β are, as shown by the coordinate relationship between points M and N in Fig. 4.

Schwarz alternating method

The Schwarz alternating method was proposed for solving the Dirichlet problem, and its convergence in two and three dimensions was subsequently demonstrated by Mikhlin27 and Soboldff28. Therefore, for the analysis of the elastic zone of the surrounding rock in a multiconnected domain, the sequence of loading and geometry changes has no effect on the stresses in the surrounding rock. Therefore, the Schwarz alternating method can transform multiconnected domain problems into single connected domain problems29–31 to simplify the solutions for the pore water pressure around twin shallow circular tunnels in a semi-infinite space.

The solution for the pore water pressure around a shallow circular tunnel in the bipolar coordinate system is extended to the twin tunnels via the Schwarz alternating method.

Comparison with existing methods

To verify the accuracy of the proposed calculation method, we compare this method with the method of Guo et al.33. Considering that the method proposed by Guo et al.33 is derived by the complex variable method and Schwarz alternating method to solve the pore water pressure around the twin shallow circular tunnels, while the method proposed in this study is derived by the bipolar coordinate system method and Schwarz alternating method to solve the pore water pressure around the twin shallow circular tunnels, so the rationality of the proposed method can be verified by comparing the calculation results of the two methods. Although the bipolar coordinate system method and the complex variable method are two important methods for solving two-dimensional field problems, there are some differences between them. The bipolar coordinate system method is based on orthogonal coordinate system transformations and is designed for bifocal symmetry problems, which are solved by transforming the physical problem into the bipolar coordinate system and using the separated variable method combined with special functions. While the complex variable method relies on the theory of complex analysis, and maps complex geometric boundaries to simple regions by constructing analytic functions or conformal transformations, so as to satisfy the Laplace equation by directly utilizing the properties of analytic functions. The core difference between the two is that the bipolar coordinate system method relies on symmetry-adapted coordinate systems and is physically intuitive, while the complex variable method maps the geometric complexity through complex plane, which is more flexible and universal. Accordingly, in this study, the calculation case in Guo et al.’s study is used to analyse the distribution of the hydraulic head under the influence of the pore water pressure around the twin shallow circular tunnels at different locations, and the corresponding location division is shown in Fig. 15. We adopt the proposed method to determine the pore water pressure and corresponding hydraulic head in this case and compare the results obtained via the proposed method with those obtained via Guo et al.’s method and numerical simulation, as shown in Fig. 15, and the effective verification of the accuracy of the calculation method proposed in this study is achieved.Fig. 15Comparison of the results for the hydraulic head around the twin shallow circular tunnels at different locations.

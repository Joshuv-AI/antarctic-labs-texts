Description of the Tranquillo Phase 3 Clinical Trial Designs/Study Protocols to Assess Ritlecitinib in Adults and Adolescents with Nonsegmental Vitiligo
Authors: Tatjana Lukic, Pranab Ghosh, Lynne Napatalung, Cunshan Wang, Samantha K. Kurosky, Iltefat Hamzavi, Rodney Sinclair, Leihong Xiang, Kenneth Steil, David Rosmarin, Davinder Parsad, Khaled Ezzedine, Roni Adiri

ABSTRACT
IntroductionRitlecitinib is an oral, selective inhibitor of JAK3 and TEC family kinases under investigation for the treatment of nonsegmental vitiligo (NSV). In a phase 2b study, ritlecitinib was efficacious and well tolerated over 48 weeks. The Tranquillo phase 3 program comprises three studies aiming to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of once-daily 50-mg and 100-mg ritlecitinib in adult and adolescent patients with NSV.MethodsTranquillo (NCT05583526), a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled 52-week trial, compares the efficacy and safety of once-daily 50-mg ritlecitinib vs placebo (N ≈ 600). Tranquillo LTE (NCT06163326), a randomized, double-blind, withdrawal and dose-up titration 52-week extension study, investigates the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of once-daily 50-mg and 100-mg ritlecitinib and durability of response with once-daily 50-mg ritlecitinib following participation in Tranquillo (N ≈ 400). Tranquillo 2 (NCT06072183) comprises two parts: Part I, a 52-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study with 52-week extension with randomized dose-up/dose-down titration comparing once-daily 50-mg and 100-mg ritlecitinib with placebo (N ≈ 1000), and Part II, a de novo 52-week nonrandomized, open-label once-daily 100-mg ritlecitinib arm (N ≈ 450).Participants are aged ≥ 12 years (Tranquillo 2: ≥ 18 years) with clinical diagnosis of active or stable NSV for ≥ 3 months, body surface area involvement 4%–60%, facial body surface area ≥ 0.5%, Facial Vitiligo Area Scoring Index (F-VASI) ≥ 0.5, and Total (T)-VASI ≥ 3.Planned OutcomesTranquillo’s primary efficacy endpoint is the proportion of participants achieving ≥ 75% improvement in F-VASI from baseline (F-VASI75) at Week 52; T-VASI50 at Week 52 is a coprimary efficacy endpoint in the US and a key secondary endpoint globally (other than US). Tranquillo LTE’s primary endpoint is safety. Tranquillo 2’s primary efficacy endpoint is F-VASI75 at Week 52; T-VASI50 at Week 52 is a coprimary efficacy endpoint in the US and a key secondary endpoint globally (other than US). Introduction

Background

Vitiligo is an autoimmune skin disease characterized by pigment loss due to the destruction of melanocytes. Vitiligo has an estimated global prevalence of 0.36% to 2% and can affect people of all sexes, races, ethnicities, and ages [1–4]. The most common type is nonsegmental vitiligo (NSV), occurring in 84% to 95% of cases, which manifests as symmetrical patches of depigmentation and confers an unpredictable disease course [1–3]. Vitiligo can often affect a patient’s psychosocial health, and patients with vitiligo experience higher rates of anxiety and depression [2, 4–8].

The pathogenesis of vitiligo is complex and can involve multiple factors, from genetic predisposition to oxidative stress, resulting in recruitment of cytotoxic CD8+ T-cells to the location of melanocytes and the production of cytokines and chemokines and leading to further immune system activation and recruitment [9–12]. Cumulatively, this leads to melanocyte destruction [9–12]. The Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription proteins (JAK/STAT) pathway, in particular JAK3, and the tyrosine kinase expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (TEC) family kinase members play a vital role in vitiligo pathogenesis. Their roles include increasing the activity and expression of cytokines such as IFN-γ and IL-2, which leads to the activation and recruitment of CD4+ and cytotoxic CD8+ T-cells and tissue-resident memory T-cells [13–17]. Inhibition of both JAK3 and the TEC family kinases in patients with NSV was shown to decrease biomarkers associated with vitiligo pathogenesis [18].

Treatments for vitiligo include phototherapy, topical or oral corticosteroids, topical calcineurin inhibitors, and immunotherapies, as well as ruxolitinib, a topical JAK1/2 inhibitor (limited in clinical trials to topical application on ≤ 10% of a patient’s body surface area) [19–22]. However, these treatments often have limited efficacy, especially in patients with large areas of depigmentation, long-term safety concerns, or both [23]. No targeted oral systemic treatment is currently approved for NSV, highlighting an unmet need.

Ritlecitinib is an oral, selective irreversible inhibitor of JAK3 and the TEC family kinases that is under investigation for the treatment of NSV [24]. In a phase 2b study (NCT03715829), ritlecitinib 50 mg once daily (QD) (with or without an initial 4-week 100- or 200-mg QD loading dose) was efficacious and well tolerated in adults with active NSV over 48 weeks, demonstrating significant improvement vs placebo on centrally read Facial Vitiligo Area Scoring Index (F-VASI) scores at Week 24, with further efficacy observed up to Week 48 [24]. Exposure-response findings indicate that doses of ritlecitinib > 50 mg QD may provide additional efficacy [25]. In trials, 100-mg QD doses of ritlecitinib in patients with rheumatoid arthritis for up to 24 weeks and 200-mg doses QD in patients with ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, or rheumatoid arthritis for up to 8 weeks were generally safe and well tolerated [26–29]. In completed and ongoing studies on alopecia areata and vitiligo, over 1600 participants have received ritlecitinib in doses ranging from 50 to 200 mg QD, which were generally safe and well tolerated [24, 30–33].

Objectives

Here, we present an overview of the Tranquillo phase 3 program, which comprises three phase 3 studies aiming to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of ritlecitinib 50 mg QD and 100 mg QD in adult and adolescent participants with NSV: Tranquillo (NCT05583526), Tranquillo Long Term Extension (LTE) (NCT06163326), and Tranquillo 2 (NCT06072183). The comprehensive program will assess efficacy by measuring repigmentation on both the face and entire body and seek to determine the safety and tolerability of both short- and long-term treatment with ritlecitinib. Tranquillo LTE and Tranquillo 2 will additionally study the impact of dose titration and randomized withdrawals to investigate optimal dosing strategies.

Methods

Study Designs

Tranquillo

Tranquillo is a phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter clinical trial designed to compare the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of ritlecitinib 50 mg QD with those of placebo in adult and adolescent participants with NSV. The primary objective of Tranquillo is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ritlecitinib 50 mg QD vs placebo in participants with NSV. The study includes a 30-day screening period, a 52-week treatment period, and either a 4-week follow-up period or, for those eligible, entry into the extension study Tranquillo LTE (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Study design for the Tranquillo clinical trial. LTE long-term extension, QD once daily

Study design for the Tranquillo clinical trial. LTE long-term extension, QD once daily

At baseline, participants are randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either ritlecitinib 50 mg QD or placebo for 52 weeks. Participants are stratified based on Fitzpatrick skin type (FST) and age. FST stratification includes three strata: FST I and II, FST III and IV, and FST V and VI. Age stratification includes two strata: adolescent participants < 18 years and adult participants ≥ 18 years, with a target enrollment of approximately 5% of the total population being adolescents. Participants with active or stable NSV are enrolled without formal stratification or capping.

Efficacy evaluations include outcomes at Weeks 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, and 52. Outcomes at all time points in the ritlecitinib 50-mg QD group are being compared with those in the placebo group.

Tranquillo LTE

Tranquillo LTE is a phase 3, multicenter, double-blind, parallel-group, randomized withdrawal and dose-up titration extension study designed to investigate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of ritlecitinib 50 mg QD and ritlecitinib 100 mg QD, and durability of response with ritlecitinib 50 mg QD in adults and adolescents with NSV following their participation in the Tranquillo study for 52 weeks. The primary objective is to evaluate the long-term safety and tolerability of ritlecitinib 50 mg QD and 100 mg QD based on the incidence of adverse events, treatment-related adverse events, and serious adverse events leading to discontinuation and the incidence of clinically significant laboratory abnormalities. The study includes a 30-day screening period, a 52-week treatment period, and a 4-week follow-up period (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Study design of the Tranquillo LTE clinical trial. Tranquillo 2

Tranquillo 2 comprises two parts. Part I is a phase 3, 52-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled multicenter study (Part Ia) plus 52-week randomized dose-up/dose-down titration extension (Part Ib) designed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of ritlecitinib 50 mg QD and ritlecitinib 100 mg QD compared with placebo in adult participants with NSV. Part II is a de novo 52-week non-randomized, open-label ritlecitinib 100-mg QD arm to support the safety database. The primary objectives of Tranquillo 2 are to compare the efficacy of ritlecitinib 100 mg QD vs placebo and to evaluate the safety and tolerability of ritlecitinib over time in participants with NSV. Part I includes a 30-day screening period, a 52-week placebo-controlled treatment period, a 52-week dose-up/down titration extension period, and a 4-week follow-up period. Part II includes a 30-day screening period, a 52-week treatment period, and a 4-week follow-up period (Fig. 3).Fig. 3Study design for the Tranquillo 2 clinical trial. DDT dose-down titration, DUT dose-up titration, NR non-randomized, QD once daily, RDRN randomized dose-ranging non-responders, RDRS randomized dose-ranging sub-threshold responders

Study design for the Tranquillo 2 clinical trial. DDT dose-down titration, DUT dose-up titration, NR non-randomized, QD once daily, RDRN randomized dose-ranging non-responders, RDRS randomized dose-ranging sub-threshold responders

In Part Ia, participants are randomized in a 3:1:1 ratio to receive ritlecitinib 100 mg QD, ritlecitinib 50 mg QD, or placebo QD for 52 weeks. Participants are stratified based on FST into three strata: FST I and II, FST III and IV, and FST V and VI.

At Week 52, participants in Part Ia enter Part Ib, with treatment response in Part Ia at Week 52 informing Part Ib treatment arm allocation (Table 1). For those who received 100 mg QD for 52 weeks during Part Ia, responders (defined as those who had ≥ F-VASI75 and/or ≥ T-VASI50 at Week 52) are randomized 1:1 to either continue ritlecitinib 100 mg QD for 52 weeks or receive ritlecitinib 50 mg QD for 52 weeks (dose-down titration), sub-threshold responders (defined as those who had at least ≥ F-VASI50 but < F-VASI75 and/or at least ≥ T-VASI33 but < T-VASI50 at Week 52) continue ritlecitinib 100 mg for 52 weeks, and non-responders (defined as < F-VASI50 and < T-VASI33 at Week 52) receive placebo for 52 weeks. For those who received ritlecitinib 50 mg QD for 52 weeks during Part Ia, responders continue ritlecitinib 50 mg QD for 52 weeks, sub-threshold responders are randomized 1:1 to either continue ritlecitinib 50 mg QD for 52 weeks or receive ritlecitinib 100 mg QD for 52 weeks (dose-up titration), and non-responders receive placebo for 52 weeks. For those who received placebo QD for 52 weeks during Part Ia, responders continue receiving placebo for 52 weeks, sub-threshold responders are randomized 2:1 to either receive ritlecitinib 100 mg QD for 52 weeks or receive ritlecitinib 50 mg QD for 52 weeks (randomized dose-ranging sub-threshold responders), and non-responders are randomized 2:1 to either receive ritlecitinib 100 mg QD for 52 weeks or receive ritlecitinib 50 mg QD for 52 weeks (randomized dose-ranging non-responders).

In Part II, all participants receive ritlecitinib 100 mg QD for 52 weeks in an open-label manner. Those enrolled in Part II do not proceed to the extension period.

Efficacy evaluations include outcomes at Weeks 24, 36, and 52. Outcomes at all time points in the ritlecitinib 50-mg QD and 100-mg QD groups are being compared with those in the placebo group.

Sample Selection

In brief, eligible participants for all 3 studies include individuals aged ≥ 12 years (for Tranquillo and Tranquillo LTE) or ≥ 18 years (for Tranquillo 2) with a clinical diagnosis of NSV (as previously defined) [24] for at least 3 months, with active or stable disease status evaluated at baseline. Participants must have a body surface area involvement of 4% to 60%, excluding palms, soles, or dorsal aspect of the feet, and body surface area ≥ 0.5% involvement on the face. Additionally, participants must have F-VASI ≥ 0.5 and T-VASI ≥ 3.

Exclusion criteria include any psychiatric condition including recent or active suicidal ideation or behavior, other forms of vitiligo, or other diseases or conditions affecting the skin.

Planned Outcomes and Measurements

Tranquillo

In the Tranquillo study, selection and testing of the primary and key secondary endpoints are region specific as a result of negotiations and agreements with global regulators. In the US, F-VASI75 and T-VASI50 at Week 52 are co-primary endpoints, while F-VASI75 at Week 52 is the primary endpoint and T-VASI50 at Week 52 is a key secondary endpoint for Global (other than US) study sites. Both F-VASI75 and T-VASI50 are clinically meaningful endpoints, with T-VASI50 representing clinically meaningful improvement for patients across the whole body (including face). F-VASI75 represents the most clinically meaningful and socially impactful improvement for patients who have lesions on their face. Both endpoints will be controlled for family-wise Type I error rate, and there are no implications to the interpretation of the study results.

In the US, the coprimary efficacy endpoints are the comparison of the proportions of participants achieving F-VASI75 at Week 52 and the proportion of participants achieving T-VASI50 at Week 52 among those receiving ritlecitinib 50 mg QD and those receiving placebo (Table 3). Data Collection and Data Analysis

Sample Size

Tranquillo is enrolling approximately 600 participants, with approximately 400 participants randomized to receive ritlecitinib 50 mg QD and approximately 200 participants randomized to receive placebo. Tranquillo LTE is enrolling approximately 400 participants who completed the Tranquillo study to receive ritlecitinib 100 mg QD, ritlecitinib 50 mg QD, or placebo QD, based on their responder status, age, and dose at Week 52 in Tranquillo. Tranquillo 2 is enrolling approximately 1000 participants in Part I, with 600 randomized to receive ritlecitinib 100 mg QD, 200 participants to receive 50 mg QD, and 200 participants to receive placebo. An additional approximately 450 patients are enrolled in Part II. The sample sizes in Tranquillo and Tranquillo 2 yield higher power (≥ 89%) of conclusion of efficacy under alternative hypotheses. The sample size in Tranquillo LTE assumes approximately 85% of those that complete Tranquillo will enter Tranquillo LTE.

Statistical Analyses

In general, numbers and percentages will be presented for binary variables. Number, mean, standard deviation, median, minimum, and maximum will be presented for all continuous variables. Analysis of binary endpoints will be conducted using the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel approach adjusting for stratification factor of FST. Large sample approximation will be used to test the superiority of ritlecitinib 50 mg QD or ritlecitinib 100 mg QD vs placebo (where applicable) at the stated time points and to form 95% CIs and calculate P values.

For treatment comparison using continuous endpoints, the data will be analyzed using an analysis of covariance model, which will include treatment group, baseline value of the endpoint, FST, and age (where applicable) as covariates. The mean treatment difference, its 95% CI, and its P value will be obtained. Missing data will be considered as non-response for binary endpoints and imputed with worst observation carried forward for continuous endpoints. Safety data will be summarized descriptively and presented in tabular and/or graphical format.

No comparative analyses will be performed on the non-randomized cohorts within Tranquillo LTE, Tranquillo 2 Part Ib, or Tranquillo 2 Part II.

Strengths and Limitations

Strengths

Overall, the Tranquillo program comprehensively explores ritlecitinib, a targeted systemic treatment, in a large and varied cohort of approximately 2050 patients with active and stable NSV spanning all FSTs and a broad range of affected percent body surface area over the course of up to 104 weeks. Collectively, these three studies provide insights into the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of ritlecitinib, both on the face and body, and after both short- and long-term treatment, in patients with NSV. Additionally, the inclusion of dose-up titration, dose-down titration, and randomized withdrawal arms in the study designs will provide valuable information on the effects of ritlecitinib dosage changes and the impact of treatment interruptions on patient responses to ritlecitinib.

Limitations

The Tranquillo program may be limited by the complexity of the comprehensive study design. To aid in understanding, where possible, published results will include graphics and plain language summaries so that physicians, caregivers, and patients can access and understand the information.

Ethics and Dissemination

These studies are being conducted in accordance with the protocols and with the consensus ethical principles derived from international guidelines, including the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments and CIOMS International Ethical Guidelines; applicable International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines; and applicable regional laws and regulations, including privacy laws. The protocols, protocol amendments, and other relevant documents are reviewed and approved by the sponsor and submitted by the investigator for review and approval by the institutional review board/ethics committee before each study can be initiated. All participants or their legally authorized representative must provide written informed consent for their participation in the trial.

The results of the trials described here will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal for publication.

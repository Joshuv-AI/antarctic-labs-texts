Cyclic sieving phenomena via combinatorics of continued fractions
Bishal Deb, [5mm] Yau Mathematical Sciences Center, Tsinghua University, Beijing 100084, China

Abstract
We will exhibit several instances of the cyclic sieving phenomenon involving statistics and involutions on the following combinatorial families of objects: permutations, set partitions, perfect matchings, D-permutations (and its subclasses). Our results will be based on continued fraction identities enumerating these objects. Our instances of cyclic sieving phenomenon for permutations involve the Corteel involution; this was first studied by Adams, Elder, Lafrenière, McNicholas, Striker and Welch (arxiv 2024). We will reprove several of their results using our setting of continued fractions; we will also prove two of their conjectures. Our study of set partitions and perfect matchings will involve the Kasraoui–Zeng involution and the Chen–Deng–Du–Stanley–Yan (CDDSY) involution. Finally, for D-permutations, we will construct a new involution which we call the Genocchi–Corteel involution. The common feature of all of these involutions, other than the CDDSY involution, is that they are constructed via bijections to weighted lattice paths, and that they exchange crossings and nestings on their respective objects.

1.1
The cyclic sieving phenomena

The cyclic sieving phenomenon was introduced by Reiner, Stanton and White
[
38
]
;
also see the nice survey article of Sagan
[
41
]
and also
[
39
]
.

Let
X
be a finite set (usually a set of combinatorial objects).
Next, let
C
be a finite cyclic group of order
n

with an action on
X
.
Given
g\in C
, we use
X^{g}
to denote the elements of
X
invariant under the action of
g
,
i.e.

X^{g}\;=\;\{y\in X\;\colon\;g\cdot y=y\}\;.

(1.1)

Let
\omega
be a generator of the group
C
, i.e.,
C=\langle\omega\rangle
.
We are now ready to define the cyclic sieving phenomenon.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a set
X
, a polynomial
f(q)
, and an action of
C
on
X
,
the triple
(X,C,f(q))
exhibits the
cyclic sieving phenomenon
if,
for all
d
we have

\left|X^{\omega^{d}}\right|\;=\;f\left(\exp\left(2\pi i\dfrac{d}{n}\right)\right)\;.

(1.2)

Notice that when
d=n
, the identity (
1.2
) implies that
f(1)=|X|
.
Thus, the coefficients of the polynomial
f(q)
should sum up to the cardinality of
X
.
For our purposes,
f(q)
will be the generating polynomial of some
statistic on the set
X
.

When
n=2
, the action of the generator
\omega

gives a bijection on the set
X
,
x\mapsto\omega\cdot x
.
This bijection is, in fact, an involution.
This special case, was first introduced as the “
q=-1
phenomenon” by Stembridge
[
44
]
.
Exhibiting the cyclic sieving phenomenon in this case is equivalent
to showing that
f(1)=|X|
,
and that
f(-1)=|X^{\omega}|
, the number of fixed points of the involution
x\mapsto\omega\cdot x
.
In this paper, our instances of the cyclic sieving phenomenon
will all be instances of the “
q=-1
” phenomenon.

The main idea in this paper can be summarised as follows:
Let
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a combinatorial family of objects
and let
{\rm Stat}:S_{n}\to\mathbb{N}
be some statistics on this set.
Let
F(t;q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}q^{{\rm Stat}(\sigma)}t^{n}

be its bivariate ordinary generating function.
Here the indeterminate
q
will keep track of this statistic and
t
will keep track of the set
S_{n}
.
Also, let
\phi_{n}:S_{n}\to S_{n}
be an involution such that the number fixed points of this involution is
a_{n}
.
We think of this involution as the action of
\omega
on the set
S_{n}
where
\omega^{2}={\rm id}_{C}
.
Then exhibiting the cyclic sieving phenomenon is equivalent to showing

F(t;-1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}
.
We will specifically look at generating functions
F(t;q)
which can be expressed as a nice classical continued fraction;
this will simplify the evaluation of the substitution
F(t;-1)

and our results will seem to be easy consequences.
Following
[
1
]
, we will say that the statistic

{\rm Stat}
exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon to mean that
the corresponding generating polynomial

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}q^{{\rm Stat}(\sigma)}

exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon.

Labelled Dyck, Motzkin and Schröder paths

Recall that a
Motzkin path
of length
n\geq 0

is a path
\omega=(\omega_{0},\ldots,\omega_{n})

in the right quadrant
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
,
starting at
\omega_{0}=(0,0)
and ending at
\omega_{n}=(n,0)
,
whose steps
s_{j}=\omega_{j}-\omega_{j-1}

are
(1,1)
[“rise” or “up step”],
(1,-1)
[“fall” or “down step”]
or
(1,0)
[“level step”].
We write
h_{j}
for the
height
of the Motzkin path at abscissa
j
,
i.e.
\omega_{j}=(j,h_{j})
;
note in particular that
h_{0}=h_{n}=0
.
We write
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
for the set of Motzkin paths of length
n
,
and
{\mathcal{M}}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.
A Motzkin path is called a
Dyck path
if it has no level steps.
A Dyck path always has even length;
we write
{\mathcal{D}}_{2n}
for the set of Dyck paths of length
2n
,
and
{\mathcal{D}}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{D}}_{2n}
.

A
Schröder path
of length
2n
(
n\geq 0
)
is a path
\omega=(\omega_{0},\ldots,\omega_{2n})

in the right quadrant
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
,
starting at
\omega_{0}=(0,0)
and ending at
\omega_{2n}=(2n,0)
,
whose steps are
(1,1)
[“rise” or “up step”],

(1,-1)
[“fall” or “down step”]
or
(2,0)
[“long level step”].
We write
s_{j}
for the step starting at abscissa
j-1
.
If the step
s_{j}
is a rise or a fall,
we set
s_{j}=\omega_{j}-\omega_{j-1}
as before.
If the step
s_{j}
is a long level step,
we set
s_{j}=\omega_{j+1}-\omega_{j-1}
and leave
\omega_{j}
undefined;
furthermore, in this case there is no step
s_{j+1}
.
We write
h_{j}
for the height of the Schröder path at abscissa
j

whenever this is defined, i.e.
\omega_{j}=(j,h_{j})
.
Please note that
\omega_{2n}=(2n,0)
and
h_{2n}=0

are always well-defined,
because there cannot be a long level step starting at abscissa
2n-1
.
Note also that a long level step at even (resp. odd) height
can occur only at an odd-numbered (resp. even-numbered) step.
We write
{\mathcal{S}}_{2n}
for the set of Schröder paths of length
2n
,
and
{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{S}}_{2n}
.

There is an obvious bijection between Schröder paths and Motzkin paths:
namely, every long level step is mapped onto a level step.

Let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=({\mathcal{A}}_{h})_{h\geq 0}
,
{\bm{\mathcal{B}}}=({\mathcal{B}}_{h})_{h\geq 1}

and
{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}=({\mathcal{C}}_{h})_{h\geq 0}
be sequences of finite sets.
An

({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled Motzkin path of length
\bm{n}

is a pair
(\omega,\xi)

where
\omega=(\omega_{0},\ldots,\omega_{n})

is a Motzkin path of length
n
,
and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
is a sequence satisfying

\xi_{i}\>\in\>\begin{cases}{\mathcal{A}}(h_{i-1})&\textrm{if step $i$ is a rise (i.e.\ $h_{i}=h_{i-1}+1$)}\\[2.84526pt]
{\mathcal{B}}(h_{i-1})&\textrm{if step $i$ is a fall (i.e.\ $h_{i}=h_{i-1}-1$)}\\[2.84526pt]
{\mathcal{C}}(h_{i-1})&\textrm{if step $i$ is a level step (i.e.\ $h_{i}=h_{i-1}$)}\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
h_{i-1}
(resp.
h_{i}
) is the height of the Motzkin path
before (resp. after) step
i
.
[For typographical clarity
we have here written
{\mathcal{A}}(h)
as a synonym for
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
, etc.]
We call
\xi_{i}
the
label
associated to step
i
.
We call the pair
(\omega,\xi)

an
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}})
-labelled Dyck path

if
\omega
is a Dyck path (in this case
{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}
plays no role).
We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})

the set of
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled Motzkin paths of length
n
,
and by
{\mathcal{D}}_{2n}({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}})

the set of
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}})
-labelled Dyck paths of length
2n
.

We define a
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled
Schröder path

in an analogous way;
now the sets
{\mathcal{C}}_{h}
refer to long level steps.
We denote by
{\mathcal{S}}_{2n}({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})

the set of
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled Schröder paths
of length
2n
.

Let us stress that the sets
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{h}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{h}
are allowed
to be empty.
Whenever this happens, the path
\omega
is forbidden to take a step
of the specified kind starting at the specified height.

The Corteel involution on permutations

In

1

Warning:

We should warn the reader that Corteel’s definitions of crossings and nesting is different from ours; hers can be expressed in our notation as follows

{\rm crossings}\;=\;{{\rm ucross}}+{\rm lcross}+{\rm cdrise}\\
{\rm nestings}\;=\;{\rm unest}+{\rm lnest}+{\rm psnest}

See the historical remarks in

for more on this story.

Her proof implicitly used an involution on permutations
but this was only explicitly written down in

Corteel used the Foata–Zeilberger
[

to construct her involution on permutations.
This bijection is a bijection
between permutations and labelled Motzkin paths
that has been very successfully
employed to obtain continued fractions involving polynomial coefficients
counting various permutation statistics (see for example

[

,
6

More recently, Deb provided a reinterpretation of this bijection
in terms of Laguerre digraphs
[

The Foata–Zeilberger bijection (as stated in
[
13
, Section 6.1]
)
is a bijective correspondence between
\mathfrak{S}_{n}

and the set of

({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled Motzkin paths of length

,
where
the labels

lie in the sets

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}&=\{0,\ldots,h\}\,\;\;\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\hbox{for $h\geq 0$}\\
{\mathcal{B}}_{h}=\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\hbox{for $h\geq 1$}\\
{\mathcal{C}}_{h}=\left(\{1\}\times C_{h}^{(1)}\right)\,\cup\,\left(\{2\}\times C_{h}^{(2)}\right)\,\cup\,\left(\{3\}\times C_{h}^{(3)}\right)\qquad\hbox{for $h\geq 0$}

(3.9)

where

C_{h}^{(1)}&=\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\qquad\hbox{for $h\geq 1$}\\
C_{h}^{(2)}=\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\qquad\hbox{for $h\geq 1$}\\
C_{h}^{(3)}=\{0\}\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\>\hbox{for $h\geq 0$}

(3.10)

A level step that has label

\xi_{h}\in\{i\}\times C_{h}^{(i)}

will be called a level step of type
i
(
i=1,2,3
).
Let
\mathfrak{S}\mathcal{M}_{n}
denote this set of
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled
Motzkin paths of length
n
with label sets
given by (
3.9
),(
3.10
).
The Foata–Zeilberger bijection as per
[
13
, Section 6.1]

is a bijective map
\varphi^{\rm FZ}_{n}:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\mathfrak{S}\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
For the convenience of the reader,
we relate our terminology to that of Corteel’s
[
11
, Section 3.1]
:

•

rises
correspond to steps

,

•

falls correspond to steps

,

•

level steps of type

\bar{E}
,

•

Corteel considers cycle double rises and fixed points together,
and hence, her steps

correspond to both level steps of types
2
and

.

For what follows,
we denote by
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{h}

the following labels at
a level step at height

c_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}(3,0)&\quad\text{if $i=h$}\\
(2,i)&\quad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\;.

(3.11)

Thus,
{\mathcal{C}}_{h}=\bigl(\{1\}\times C_{h}^{(1)}\bigr)\cup\{c_{0},\ldots,c_{h}\}
.

We will now describe an involution
\phi_{n}:\mathfrak{S}\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathfrak{S}\mathcal{M}_{n}
;
the Corteel involution will then be obtained by the composition of bijections

\bigl(\varphi^{\rm FZ}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{n}\circ\varphi^{\rm FZ}_{n}

Let
(\omega,\xi)\in\mathfrak{S}\mathcal{M}_{n}

where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
.
Let
\xi^{\rm c}=(\xi^{\rm c}_{1},\ldots,\xi^{\rm c}_{n})
be a sequence where

\xi^{\rm c}_{i}

is given by

\xi^{\rm c}_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}h-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a rise}\\
h-1-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a fall}\\
(1,h-1-k)&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a level step of type 1
and $\xi_{i}=(1,k)$}\\

It is clearly follows that

and that
(\xi^{\rm c})^{{\rm c}}=\xi
.
Thus, the map
(\omega,\xi)\mapsto(\omega,\xi^{\rm c})

is an involution on the set
\mathfrak{S}\mathcal{M}_{n}
;
we use
\phi_{n}
to denote this involution.
The Corteel involution on permutations
is the the map
\phi_{n}^{{\rm C}}\coloneqq\bigl(\varphi^{\rm FZ}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{n}\circ\varphi^{\rm FZ}_{n}
.

An important property of the Corteel involution is that crossings and nestings, in the sense of Corteel, are exchanged.

\displaystyle\bigl({\rm unest}(\widehat{\sigma})+{\rm lnest}(\widehat{\sigma})+{\rm psnest}(\widehat{\sigma}),{\rm ucross}(\widehat{\sigma})+{\rm lcross}(\widehat{\sigma})+{\rm cdrise}(\widehat{\sigma})\bigr)\;.

4
Set partitions

A set partition of
[n]
is a partition of the set
[n]
into nonempty blocks;
it is counted by the Bell number
B_{n}
.
We use
\Pi_{n}
to denote the set of all set-partitions of
[n]
.
For a partition
\pi
,
|\pi|
will denote its number of blocks.
An important property of the Bell numbers
B_{n}
is that they have the following J-fraction
[
47
,
21
]
:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}t^{n}\;=\;\cfrac{1}{1-t-\cfrac{t^{2}}{1-2t-\cfrac{2t^{2}}{1-3t-\cfrac{3t^{2}}{1-\ldots}}}}\;.

(4.1)

We will use various generalisations of this identity in this section.

In Section
4.1
we define some statistics on set partitions.
Then in Section
4.2
we recall the Kasraoui–Zeng
involution
[
26
]

in terms of labelled Motzkin paths and count its number of fixed points.
After this, in Section
4.3
,
we look at Chen et al’s involution
[
8
]

and count its number of fixed points.
Then in Section
4.4
,
we will use the statistics defined in Section
4.1

to recall multivariate generating polynomials
introduced by Sokal and Zeng
[
43
]
.
Finally, we state our main results
in Section
4.5
.
Finally in Section
4.6
,
we conclude with a discussion on Mahonian statistics on set partitions.

4.1
Statistics on set partitions

Let
\pi\in\Pi_{n}
be a set partition of
[n]
.
Following Sokal and Zeng
[
43
]
,
we first classify each element
i\in[n]
as follows:

•

i
is an
opener
(
{\rm op}
) if it is the smallest element of a block of size
\geq 2
;

•

i
is a
closer
(
m_{\geq 2}
) if it is the largest element of a block of size
\geq 2
;

•

i
is an
insider
(
{\rm in}
) if it is a non-opener non-closer element of a block of size
\geq 3
;

•

i
is a
singleton
(
m_{1}
) if it is the sole element of a block of size
1
.

Clearly every element
i
belongs to precisely one of these four classes;
we will call this the
type classification for set partitions
2
2

2

We borrow
the term
type
from Kasraoui and Zeng
[
26
]
;
their usage of the word type is different but in a similar context.
.

We say that an index
i
is an
exclusive record
of
\pi

if it is not the largest element in its block,
(that is, it is either an opener or an insider)
and its right neighbor (within its block) sticks out farther
to the right than any right neighbor (within its block) of a vertex
<i
.
With this notion of an exclusive record, we refine openers and insiders as follows:

•

i
is an
exclusive record opener
(
{\rm erecop}
) if it is an opener
and also an exclusive record;

•

i
is an
non-exclusive record opener
(
{\rm nerecop}
) if it is an opener
but not an exclusive record;

•

i
is a
exclusive record closer
(
{\rm erecin}
) if it is a closer and also an exclusive record;

•

i
is a
non-exclusive record closer
(
{\rm nerecin}
) if it is a closer but not an exclusive record.

These four statistics along with insiders and singletons
form 6 disjoint categories and we will call them the
record-and-type classification
.

After this, we introduce crossings, nestings, overlaps and coverings on set partitions.
But first, we recall the
partition graph
of
\pi
;
we follow the convention of Sokal and Zeng.
We associate to the partition
\pi
a graph
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
with vertex set
[n]

such that
i,j
are joined by an edge if and only if they are consecutive elements within the same block.
(Thus, the graph
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
has
|\pi|
connected components and
n-|\pi|
edges.)
With this in mind, we say that a quadruplet
i<j<k<l
forms a

•

crossing
(
{\rm cr}
) if
(i,k)
and
(j,l)
are edges in
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
;

•

nesting
(
{\rm ne}
) if
(i,l)
and
(j,k)
are edges in
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
.

Also, we say that a triple
i<j<l
forms a

•

pseudo-nesting
(
{\rm psne}
) if
j
is a singleton and
(i,l)
is an edge in
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
.

Sokal and Zeng further refine the categories of crossing and nesting depending on the type of the second index
j

and introduce the following four classes:

crossing of opener type
(
{\rm crop}
),

crossing of insider type
(
{\rm crin}
),

nesting of opener type
(
{\rm neop}
),

nesting of insider type
(
{\rm nein}
).

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be blocks of
\pi
. We say that the pair
(B_{1},B_{2})
forms

•

an
overlap
(
{\rm ov}
) if
\min B_{1}<\min B_{2}<\max B_{1}<\max B_{2}
;

•

a
covering
(
{\rm cov}
) if
\min B_{1}<\min B_{2}<\max B_{2}<\max B_{1}
;

•

a
pseudo-covering
(
{\rm pscov}
) if
\min B_{1}<\min B_{2}=\max B_{2}<\max B_{1}

(so that here
B_{2}
is a singleton).

They also define two related quantities
{\rm ovin}
and
{\rm covin}
as follows:

{\rm ovin}(\pi)&=\!\!\sum\limits_{j\in{\rm insiders}\cap B_{2}}\!\!\#\{(B_{1},B_{2})\colon\>j\in B_{2}\,\hbox{ and }\,\min B_{1}<j<\max B_{1}<\max B_{2}\}\\
\\
{\rm covin}(\pi)=\!\!\sum\limits_{j\in{\rm insiders}\cap B_{2}}\!\!\#\{(B_{1},B_{2})\colon\>j\in B_{2}\,\hbox{ and }\,\min B_{1}<j<\max B_{2}<\max B_{1}\}\\

(4.2)

We also recall the notion of a
block record
.
We say that
j
is a
block record
if it is either an opener or an
insider and its block sticks out farther to the right than any block containing a vertex
<j
.
We write

•

{\rm brecin}(\pi)
for the number of insiders that are block-records,

•

{\rm nbrecin}(\pi)
for the number of insiders that are not block-records,

•

{\rm brecop}(\pi)
for the number of openers that are block-records,

•

{\rm nbrecop}(\pi)
for the number of openers that are not block-records.

4.2
The Kasraoui–Zeng involution on set partitions

In
[
26
]
, Kasraoui and Zeng constructed an involution on the set
\Pi_{n}

that exchanges the number of crossings and nestings while keeping various other statistics fixed.
We recall this involution and then count its number of fixed points.
However, we will state this involution via labelled Motzkin paths (
Charlier diagrams of Viennot
)
to be consistent with our formalism in the previous sections.

The Kasraoui–Zeng bijection (as stated by Sokal and Zeng
[
43
, sec. 7.2]
)
is a correspondence between
\Pi_{n}
and the set of

({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled Motzkin paths of length
n
,
where
the labels
\xi_{i}
lie in the sets

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}&=\{0\}\qquad\quad\;\;\hbox{for $h\geq 0$}\\
{\mathcal{B}}_{h}=\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\quad\hbox{for $h\geq 1$}\\
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=\{2\}\times\{0\}\\
{\mathcal{C}}_{h}=\{1\}\times\{0,1,\ldots,h-1\}\cup\{2\}\times\{0\}\quad\hbox{for $h\geq 1$}

(4.3)

Let
\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}
denote this set of
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled
Motzkin paths of length
n
with label sets (
4.3
a,b,c).
These are the
Charlier diagrams
of Viennot
[
48
]
.
The Kasraoui–Zeng bijection as per
[
43
, Section 7.2]

is a bijective map
\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}:\Pi_{n}\to\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

We say that a level step at height
h
is of the
first kind
(
second kind
resp.)
if the first index of
\xi_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{h}
is
1
(
2
).

We will now describe an involution
\phi_{n}:\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}
;
the Kasraoui–Zeng involution will then be obtained by the composition of bijections

\bigl(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{n}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}
.
Let
(\omega,\xi)\in\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}

where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
.
Let
\xi^{\rm c}=(\xi^{\rm c}_{1},\ldots,\xi^{\rm c}_{n})
be the sequence where

\xi^{\rm c}_{i}

is given by

\xi^{\rm c}_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}\xi_{i}=0&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a rise}\\
h-1-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a fall}\\
(1,h-1-\xi_{i})&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a level step of the first kind}\\
(2,\xi_{i})=(2,0)&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a level step of the second kind}\end{cases}

(4.4)

It clearly follows that
(\omega,\xi^{\rm c})\in\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}

and that
(\xi^{\rm c})^{{\rm c}}=\xi
.
Thus, the map
(\omega,\xi)\mapsto(\omega,\xi^{\rm c})

is an involution on the set
\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}
;
we use
\phi_{n}
to denote this involution.
The Kasraoui–Zeng involution on set partitions
is the map
\bigl(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{n}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}
.

An important property of this involution is that crossings and nestings are exchanged;
i.e., for a partition
\pi
with
\widehat{\pi}\coloneqq\biggl(\bigl(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{n}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}\biggr)(\pi)
,
we have

({\rm cr}(\pi),{\rm ne}(\pi))\;=\;({\rm ne}(\widehat{\pi}),{\rm cr}(\widehat{\pi}))\;.

(4.5)

We now establish the number of fixed points of the Kasraoui–Zeng involution.
First, let
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence of integers given by the linear recurrence

a_{n}\;=\;3a_{n-1}\,-\,a_{n-2}

(4.6)

with initial conditions
a_{0}=a_{1}=1
, see
[
33
, A001519]
.
In fact, these numbers are the odd subsequence of the Fibonacci numbers and hence we will call them the
odd Fibonacci numbers
.
The first few numbers of this sequence are

1,1,2,5,13,34,89,233,610,1597,4181,10946,28657,\ldots

(4.7)

In the rest of this paper, we will use
F_{2n-1}\coloneqq a_{n}
to denote these numbers.
(Here
F_{-1}=1
.)

Lemma 4.1
.

The number of fixed points of
\Pi_{n}
under the action of the Kasraoui–Zeng involution

\bigl(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{n}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}

is the odd Fibonacci number
F_{2n-1}

[
33
, A001519]
.

Proof.

It suffices to count the number of labels
\xi
such that

\xi=\xi^{\rm c}
.
Using general theory,
we obtain that the generating function of labelled Motzkin paths that are fixed points of the involution
\phi_{n}

is given by the Jacobi-type continued fraction:

\cfrac{1}{1-t-\cfrac{t^{2}}{1-2t}}\;.

(4.8)

This finishes the proof.
∎

4.3
The Chen–Deng–Du–Stanley–Yan (CDDSY) involution on set partitions

In
[
8
]
, Chen et al gave a different involution on set partitions.
In their paper, the authors introduced the notion of

vacillating tableaux
, which are certain walks on the Young’s lattice,
and they constructed a bijection between set partitions and vacillating tableaux.
They then pullback a simple involution on vacillating tableaux via this bijection,
to describe their involution on set partitions.
We will refer to this involution as the
CDDSY involution
.
In this section, we will show that the number of fixed points of
the CDDSY involution is also given by Lemma
4.1
.

We first recall some basic definitions.
Let
\mathbb{Y}
be the
Young’s lattice
, that is, the set of all integer partitions ordered component-wise.

Definition 4.2
.

A
vacillating tableaux

V_{\lambda}^{2n}
of shape
\lambda
and length
2n
is a sequence

\emptyset=\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\ldots,\lambda^{2n}=\lambda
of integer partitions
with the property that
\lambda^{2i+1}
is obtained by
either setting
\lambda^{2i+1}=\lambda^{2i}
or by deleting a square from
\lambda^{2i}
,
and
\lambda^{2i}
is obtained by either setting

\lambda^{2i}=\lambda^{2i-1}
or by adding a square to
\lambda^{2i-1}
.

In terms of the
U,D
and
I
operators on the Young’s lattice
\mathbb{Y}
,
we can restate the conditions in this definition as follows:
given a
\lambda^{2i-2}
in the sequence of a vacillating tableaux,
the integer partition
\lambda^{2i-1}
is obtained by applying the operator

I+D
to
\lambda^{2i-2}
and then picking one of the summands,
and similarly,
the integer partition
\lambda^{2i}
is obtained by applying the operator

I+U
to
\lambda^{2i-1}
and then selecting one of the summands.

Let
\mathcal{VT}_{n}
denote the set of all vacillating tableaux of shape
\emptyset

and length
2n
.
In
[
8
, Section 3]
, the authors constructed a bijection

\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{n}:\Pi_{n}\to{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}
.
Let
\tau_{n}:{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}
be the involution defined by taking the conjugate to each partition

\lambda^{i}
.
The CDDSY involution on set partitions (defined in
[
8
, proof of Theorem 1]
)
is the map

\bigl(\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\tau_{n}\circ\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{n}
.

We now establish the number of fixed points of the CDDSY involution.

Lemma 4.3
.

The number of fixed points of
\Pi_{n}
under the action of the CDDSY involution

\bigl(\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\tau_{n}\circ\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{n}

is the odd Fibonacci number
F_{2n-1}

[
33
, A001519]
.

Proof.

It suffices to count the number of fixed points of the involution
\tau_{n}
.
Let
{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}^{\flat}
denote the set of all vacillating tableau
(\lambda^{0},\ldots,\lambda^{2n})

such that each integer partition
\lambda^{i}
is either
\emptyset
or
1
.
We will show that
{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}^{\flat}
is the set of fixed points of
\tau_{n}
.
In fact, it is immediate that an element of
{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}^{\flat}
is always a fixed point of
\tau_{n}
.
On the other hand, any vacillating tableaux not in the set
{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}^{\flat}

must have index
i
such that

\lambda^{i}\vdash 2
.
For such an index
i
, the partition
\lambda^{i}
and its conjugate must be different.
Hence, such a vacillating tableaux cannot be a fixed point of
\tau_{n}
.

Let
M_{n}^{\flat}
denote the set of Motzkin paths of length
n
which never cross the line
x=1
,
where the level steps at height
1
gets a label
0
or
1
.
From
[
21
]
, we know that the ordinary generating function of the
cardinalities of
M_{n}^{\flat}
is given by the continued fraction

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl|M_{n}^{\flat}\bigr|t^{n}\;=\;\cfrac{1}{1-t-\cfrac{t^{2}}{1-2t}}\;.

(4.9)

Notice that the right-hand side is the same as in (
4.8
).
Thus, a bijection between the sets
{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}^{\flat}
and
M_{n}^{\flat}

will prove this lemma.
We construct such a bijection now.

Let
(\lambda^{0},\ldots,\lambda^{2n})\in{\mathcal{VT}}_{n}^{\flat}
and
let
\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\times\{0,1\}
be the pair

\omega_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}(i,0)\qquad\text{if $\lambda^{2i}=\emptyset$}\\
(i,1)\qquad\text{if $\lambda^{2i}=1$}\end{cases}\;.

(4.10)

The sequence
\omega=(\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})

is a path of length
n
which never crosses the line
x=1
.
By construction, step
i
is a level step at height
1
if and only if

\lambda^{2i-2}=\lambda^{2i}=1
.
In this situation, the integer partition
\lambda^{2i-1}
can be either
1
or
\emptyset
,
and step
i
gets label
0
and
1
, respectively.
This gives us a Motzkin path
\omega
where each level step at height
1

gets a label
0
or
1
.
This is our desired bijection.
∎

4.4
Sokal and Zeng’s multivariate polynomials enumerating statistics on set partitions

In this section, we recall three of Sokal–Zeng’s multivariate polynomials enumerating statistics on set partitions;
first we shall state
[
43
, eq. (3.6)]
which does not have any
p,q
-statistics,
then we shall state their first
p,q
-generalisation involving crossings and nestings

[
43
, eq. (3.11)]
,
and then finally we state their second
p,q
-generalisation involving overlaps and coverings.

The first polynomial is
[

B_{n}^{(1)}(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\;=\;\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{n}}x_{1}^{m_{1}(\pi)}x_{2}^{m_{\geq 2}(\pi)}y_{1}^{{\rm erecin}(\pi)}y_{2}^{{\rm erecop}(\pi)}v_{1}^{{\rm nerecin}(\pi)}v_{2}^{{\rm nerecop}(\pi)}\;.

(4.11)

The ordinary generating function of these polynomials have a nice Jacobi-type continued fraction

[
43
, Theorem 3.2]
.

The second polynomial is
[
43
, eq. (3.11)]

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581ptB_{n}^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},v_{1},v_{2},p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2},r)

\displaystyle\qquad=\;\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{n}}x_{1}^{m_{1}(\pi)}x_{2}^{m_{\geq 2}(\pi)}y_{1}^{{\rm erecin}(\pi)}y_{2}^{{\rm erecop}(\pi)}v_{1}^{{\rm nerecin}(\pi)}v_{2}^{{\rm nerecop}(\pi)}\>\times\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\;\>p_{1}^{{\rm crin}(\pi)}p_{2}^{{\rm crop}(\pi)}q_{1}^{{\rm nein}(\pi)}q_{2}^{{\rm neop}(\pi)}r^{{\rm psne}(\pi)}\>.\qquad

(4.12)

The ordinary generating function of these polynomials have a nice Jacobi-type continued fraction as well

[
43
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Our third polynomial is analogous to
(
4.12
)
but uses block-records, overlaps, coverings and pseudo-coverings
in place of exclusive records, crossings, nestings and pseudo-nestings:

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581ptB^{(3)}_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},v_{1},v_{2},p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2},r)

\displaystyle\qquad=\;\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{n}}x_{1}^{m_{1}(\pi)}x_{2}^{m_{\geq 2}(\pi)}y_{1}^{{\rm brecin}(\pi)}y_{2}^{{\rm brecop}(\pi)}v_{1}^{{\rm nbrecin}(\pi)}v_{2}^{{\rm nbrecop}(\pi)}\>\times\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\;\>p_{1}^{{\rm ovin}(\pi)}p_{2}^{{\rm ov}(\pi)}q_{1}^{{\rm covin}(\pi)}q_{2}^{{\rm cov}(\pi)}r^{{\rm pscov}(\pi)}\>.\qquad

(4.13)

It turns out that these polynomials are not merely
analogous

to (
4.12
);
they are
identical
to (
4.12
)

[
43
, Theorem 3.5]
.
See historical remark in
[
43
, Section 3.4]
.

In this paper, we refrain from working with Sokal and Zeng’s master continued fractions for set partitions
which involve infinitely many statistics on set partitions
[
43
, Sections 3.7-3.10]
.
However, it is easy to generalise our results to the infinite-variable setting.

4.6
A remark on Mahonian statistics on set partitions

In
[
32
]
, Milne introduced the inversion statistic
on set partitions;
following Wachs and White
[
49
]

we write it as
[
43
, eq. (3.61a)]

{\rm lb}(\pi)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\;\#\{(B_{1},B_{2},k)\>\colon\;\min B_{1}<\min B_{2}<k\in B_{1}\}\;.

(4.19)

The following continued fraction identity was
proved in
[
50
]

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{n}}q^{{\rm lb}(\pi)}t^{n}\;=\;\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{t}{1-\cfrac{t}{1-\cfrac{qt}{1-\cfrac{(1+q)t}{1-\cfrac{q^{2}t}{1-\cfrac{(1+q+q^{2})t}{1-\ldots})}}}}}}\;.

(4.20)

Various other statistics have been shown to be equidistributed with
the inversion statistic
{\rm lb}(\pi)
,
see more details in

[
43
, Sections 3.11, 3.12]

and in

[
30
]
;
these are known as the
Mahonian statistics on set partitions
.

Notice that on setting

q=-1
in the continued fraction (
4.20
),
we get

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{n}}(-1)^{{\rm lb}(\pi)}t^{n}\;=\;\dfrac{1}{1-t-t^{2}}

(4.21)

where clearly the right-hand side is the generating function of the Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}
.
Thus, an involution
\phi:\Pi_{n}\to\Pi_{n}

must have Fibonacci many fixed points
if any Mahonian statistic were to exhibit the cyclic-sieving phenomenon with respect to
\phi
.

We will now construct such an involution by
using the Kasraoui–Zeng bijection
\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}:\Pi_{n}\to\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
For a level step of a Motzkin path
\omega\in\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}

we rewrite some labels as
c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{h}

where

c_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}(2,0)&\quad\text{if $i=h$}\\
(1,i)&\quad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\;.

(4.22)

Now let
\bar{\phi}:\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}

be the following involution on Charlier diagrams
that sends
(\omega,\xi)
to
(\omega,{\widehat{\xi}})

where
{\widehat{\xi}}=({\widehat{\xi}}_{1},\ldots,{\widehat{\xi}}_{n})

is given by

{\widehat{\xi}}_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}\xi_{i}=0&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a rise}\\
h-1-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a fall}\\
c_{h-k}&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a level step and $\xi_{i}=c_{k}$}\end{cases}\;.

(4.23)

It is clear that
\bar{\phi}

is an involution on
\Pi\mathcal{M}_{n}

with
F_{n}
fixed points.
The composition of maps

\phi\coloneqq\left(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}\right)^{-1}\circ\bar{\phi}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}

gives us an involution on set partitions
with the same number of fixed points.
We leave out all the details and state the following result.

Theorem 4.6
.

The Mahonian statistics on set partitions
exhibit the cyclic sieving phenomenon
with respect to involutions have

F_{n}
fixed points
(in particular the involution

\left(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}\right)^{-1}\circ\bar{\phi}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{n}
).

5
Perfect matchings

A
perfect matching of
[2n]
is an involution on the set
[2n]
with no fixed points;
we use
\mathcal{I}_{2n}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{2n}
to denote the set of perfect matchings.
It is known that the cardinality of this set is
|\mathcal{I}_{2n}|=(2n-1)!!
.
An important property of the sequence of numbers
(2n-1)!!
is that they have the following Stieltjes-type continued fraction

[
20
, sec. 29]

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2n-1)!!\,t^{n}\;=\;\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{t}{1-\dfrac{2t}{1-\dfrac{3t}{1-\ldots}}}}\;.

(5.1)

We will use various generalisations of this identity in this section.

In Section
5.1
we define some statistics on set partitions.
Then in Section
5.2
we recall the Kasraoui–Zeng
involution
[
26
]
on perfect matchings
in terms of labelled Dyck paths and count its number of fixed points.
After this, in Section
5.3
,
we look at Chen et al’s involution
[
8
]

on perfect matchings
and count its number of fixed points.
Then in Section
5.4
,
we will use the statistics defined in Section
5.1

to recall multivariate generating polynomials
introduced by Sokal and Zeng
[
43
]
.
Finally, we state our main results
in Section
5.5
.

5.1
Statistics on perfect matchings

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{I}_{2n}
be a perfect matching on
[2n]
.
Each
\sigma
has
n
cycles of size
2
,
and each cycle has a cycle peak and a cycle valley.
Following Sokal and Zeng
[
43
]
,
we classify these according to their
parity
and their
record classification

(by considering them as permutations of
[2n]
).
We then obtain the following eight statistics,
the following four for cycle peaks:
even cycle-peak antirecord (ecpar),
odd cycle-peak antirecord (ocpar),
even cycle-peak non-antirecord (ecpnar),
odd cycle-peak non-antirecord (ocpnar),
and the remaining four for cycle valleys:
even cycle-valley record (ecvr),
odd cycle-valley record (ocvr),
even cycle-valley non-record (ecvnr),
odd cycle-valley non-record (ocvnr).

After this, we introduce crossings and nestings on perfect matchings.
We think of
\sigma
as a permutation and notice that for involutions
we always have
{\rm ucross}(\sigma)={\rm lcross}(\sigma)

and
{\rm unest}(\sigma)={\rm lnest}(\sigma)
;
so we denote these quantities simply as
{\rm cr}(\sigma)
and
{\rm ne}(\sigma)
, respectively.

We then further refine crossings and nestings as follows:
we say that a quadruplet
i<j<k<l

is an
even
(resp.
odd
) crossing
if the index
j
is even (resp. odd) and
the quadruplet forms a (upper) crossing;
we use
{\rm ecr}(\sigma)
, and
{\rm ocr}(\sigma)
to denote the number of even and odd crossings, respectively.
Similarly, we define even and odd nestings according to the
the parity of the second index
j
for quadruplets which form upper nestings;
we use
{\rm ene}(\sigma)
, and
{\rm one}(\sigma)
to denote the number of even and odd nestings, respectively.

5.2
The Kasraoui–Zeng involution on perfect matchings

The Kasraoui–Zeng involution on set partitions can be restricted down to perfect matchings.
This restriction to perfect matchings exchanges the
number of crossings and nestings while keeping various other statistics fixed.
We now state it this involution via labelled Dyck paths.

Consider the set of
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}})
-labelled Dyck paths of length
2n
,
where
the labels
\xi_{i}
lie in the sets

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}&=\{0\}\qquad\quad\;\;\hbox{for $h\geq 0$}\\
{\mathcal{B}}_{h}=\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\quad\hbox{for $h\geq 1$}

(5.2)

Let
\mathcal{ID}_{n}
denote this set of
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}})
-labelled
Dyck paths of length
2n
with label sets (
5.2
a,b).
The Kasraoui–Zeng bijection as per
[
43
]

is a bijective map
\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{2n}:\mathcal{I}_{2n}\to\mathcal{ID}_{2n}

(seen as the restriction of the bijection
\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{2n}:\Pi_{2n}\to\Pi\mathcal{M}_{2n}

when there are no level steps).

Let
(\omega,\xi)\in\mathcal{ID}_{2n}

where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{2n})
.
Let
\xi^{\rm c}=(\xi^{\rm c}_{1},\ldots,\xi^{\rm c}_{n})
be a sequence where

\xi^{\rm c}_{i}

is given by

\xi^{\rm c}_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}\xi_{i}=0&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a rise}\\
h-1-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a fall}\\
\end{cases}

(5.3)

It is clearly follows that
(\omega,\xi^{\rm c})\in\mathcal{ID}_{2n}

and that
(\xi^{\rm c})^{{\rm c}}=\xi
.
Thus, the map
(\omega,\xi)\mapsto(\omega,\xi^{\rm c})

is an involution on the set
\mathcal{ID}_{2n}
;
we use
\phi_{2n}
to denote this involution.
The Kasraoui–Zeng involution on perfect matchings
is the map
\bigl(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{2n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{2n}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{2n}
.

As in the set partition case,
we have for a perfect matching
\sigma
with

\widehat{\sigma}\coloneqq\biggl(\bigl(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{2n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{2n}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{2n}\biggr)(\sigma)
,
we have

({\rm cr}(\sigma),{\rm ne}(\sigma))\;=\;({\rm ne}(\widehat{\sigma}),{\rm cr}(\widehat{\sigma}))\;.

(5.4)

We now establish the number of fixed points of the Kasraoui–Zeng involution on perfect matchings.

Lemma 5.1
.

The number of fixed points of
\mathcal{I}_{2n}
under the action of the Kasraoui–Zeng involution

\bigl(\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{2n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{2n}\circ\varphi^{\rm KZ}_{2n}

is
1

and thus is generated by the continued fraction

\cfrac{1}{1-t}\;.

(5.5)

The proof of this lemma is similar to that of Lemma
4.1
and hence, we leave it to the reader.

5.3
The Chen–Deng–Du–Stanley–Yan (CDDSY) involution on perfect matchings

In
[
8
]
, Chen et al showed that the bijection

\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{n}
can be restricted to perfect matchings.
Now this bijection is to a subclass of vacillating tableaux,
called
oscillating tableaux
,
which were studied by Sundaram in her PhD thesis
[
45
]
.

We now recall the definition of oscillating tableaux.

Definition 5.2
.

An
oscillating tableaux

T_{\lambda}^{n}
of shape
\lambda

and length
n
is a sequence

\emptyset=\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\ldots,\lambda^{n}=\lambda
of integer partitions
with the property that
\lambda^{i}
is obtained
either deleting or adding a square to
\lambda^{i-1}
.

In terms of the
U,D
and
I
operators on the Young’s lattice
\mathbb{Y}
,
we can restate this definition as follows:
given a
\lambda^{i-1}
in the sequence of a vacillating tableaux,
the integer partition
\lambda^{i}
is obtained by applying the operator

U+D
to
\lambda^{i-1}
and then picking one of the summands.

Let
\mathcal{OT}_{n}
denote the set of all oscillating tableaux of shape
\emptyset

and length
n
.
The CDDSY bijection is a bijective map

\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{2n}:\mathcal{I}_{2n}\to{\mathcal{OT}}_{2n}

(seen as the restriction of the bijection
\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{2n}:\Pi_{2n}\to{\mathcal{VT}}_{2n}

when the partition
\pi
has no insiders or singletons and
all consecutive repeatitions are removed from the vacillating tableau).
Let
\tau_{n}:{\mathcal{OT}}_{n}
be the involution on oscillating tableaux
that is obtained by sending each integer partition to its conjugate.
Then the CDDSY involution for perfect matchings
is the map

\bigl(\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{2n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\tau_{2n}\circ\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{2n}
.

We now show that the number of fixed points of the CDDSY involution
is also given by Lemma
5.5
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The number of fixed points of
\mathcal{I}_{2n}
under the action of the
CDDSY involution

\bigl(\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{2n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\tau_{2n}\circ\varphi^{\rm CDDSY}_{2n}

is
1

and thus is generated by the continued fraction

\cfrac{1}{1-t}\;.

(5.6)

Proof.

It suffices to count the number of fixed points of
the involution
\tau_{2n}
.
Notice that an oscillating tableau

(\lambda^{0},\ldots,\lambda^{2n})\in{\mathcal{OT}}_{2n}

is a fixed point of
\tau_{2n}
if and only if

\lambda^{2i}=\emptyset
and
\lambda^{2i-1}=1
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Motivated by Randrianarivony’s bijection, Deb and Sokal
[

introduced two new bijections involving all D-permutations,
one of which extends Randrianarivony’s bijection.

Both bijections of Deb and Sokal
(
[

, Sections 6.1-6.3]

[
14
, Section 6.5]
)
are correspondences between
{\mathfrak{D}}_{2n}
and the set of

({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled
0-Schröder paths of length
2n
, where
the labels

lie in the sets

[

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}&=\{0,\ldots,\lceil h/2\rceil\}\qquad\quad\;\;\hbox{for $h\geq 0$}\\
{\mathcal{B}}_{h}=\{0,\ldots,\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil\}\quad\hbox{for $h\geq 1$}\\
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=\{0\}\\
{\mathcal{C}}_{h}=\emptyset\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\hbox{for $h\geq 1$}

(6.7)

Let
\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{S}_{2n}
denote this set of
({\bm{\mathcal{A}}},{\bm{\mathcal{B}}},{\bm{\mathcal{C}}})
-labelled
0-Schröder paths of length
2n

with label sets (
6.7
).

In this paper, we only focus on their first bijection which involves record classification

[
14
, Sections 6.1-6.3]
.
Let
\varphi^{\rm DS}_{2n}:{\mathfrak{D}}_{2n}\to\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{S}_{2n}

denote this bijection.

We now describe an involution which is the analog of the
Corteel
involution in this setting;
we call it the
Genocchi–Corteel
involution.

Description of the Genocchi–Corteel involution:

Let
(\omega,\xi)\in\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{S}_{2n}

where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{2n})
.
Let
\xi^{\rm c}=(\xi^{\rm c}_{1},\ldots,\xi^{\rm c}_{2n})
be a sequence where

\xi^{\rm c}_{i}

is given by

\xi^{\rm c}_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}\xi_{i}=\lceil h/2\rceil&\qquad\text{if $i$ is even, step $i$ is a rise and $\xi_{i}=\lceil h/2\rceil$}\\
\lceil h/2\rceil-1-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if $i$ is even and step $i$ is a rise and $\xi_{i}<\lceil h/2\rceil$ }\\
\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if $i$ is even and step $i$ is a fall}\\
\lceil h/2\rceil-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if $i$ is odd and step $i$ is a rise}\\
\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil-1-\xi_{i}&\qquad\text{if $i$ is odd, step $i$ is a fall
and $\xi_{i}<\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil$}\\
\xi_{i}=\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil&\qquad\text{if $i$ is odd, step $i$ is a fall
and $\xi_{i}=\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil$}\\
\xi_{i}=0&\qquad\text{if step $i$ is a long level step at height $0$}\end{cases}

(6.8)

It clearly follows that
(\omega,\xi^{\rm c})\in\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{S}_{2n}

and that
(\xi^{\rm c})^{{\rm c}}=\xi
.
Thus, the map
(\omega,\xi)\mapsto(\omega,\xi^{\rm c})

is an involution on the set
\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{S}_{2n}
;
we use
\phi_{2n}
to denote this involution.
We define the
Genocchi–Corteel involution on D-permutations

to be the map

\phi^{{\rm GC}}_{2n}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\;\bigl(\varphi^{\rm DS}_{2n}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\phi_{2n}\circ\varphi^{\rm DS}_{2n}\;.

(6.9)

(We call it the Genocchi–Corteel involution as we think it is the analog of the Corteel involution for objects counted by the
Genocchi and median–Genocchi numbers, we will expand on this in Proposition
6.1

and Corollary
6.2
.)

We now state some properties of the involution
\phi^{{\rm GC}}_{2n}
;
in particular, we will show that the cycle classification is preserved under this involution but the
crossings and nestings are exchanged.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{2n}
be a D-permutation on
2n
letters.
The following holds true under the Genocchi–Corteel involution:

•

If
i
is a cycle valley (cycle double rise resp.)
of
\sigma
then then
i
is also a cycle valley (cycle double rise)
of
{\phi^{{\rm GC}}_{2n}(\sigma)}
,
and furthermore,
{\rm unest}(i,\sigma)={\rm ucross}(i,{\phi^{{\rm GC}}_{2n}(\sigma)})
.

•

If
i
is a cycle peak (cycle double fall resp.)
of
\sigma
then then
i
is also a cycle peak (cycle double fall)
of
{\phi^{{\rm GC}}_{2n}(\sigma)}
,
and furthermore,
{\rm lnest}(i,\sigma)={\rm lcross}(i,{\phi^{{\rm GC}}_{2n}(\sigma)})
.

•

If
i
is an even fixed point (odd fixed point resp.)
of
\sigma
then
i
is also an even fixed point (odd fixed point)
of
{\phi^{{\rm GC}}_{2n}(\sigma)}
,
and furthermore,
{\rm psnest}(i,\sigma)={\rm psnest}(i,{\phi^{{\rm GC}}_{2n}(\sigma)}))
.

Proof.

As the Schröder path
\omega
stays the same under the involution,
the sets

introduced in

[
14

This along with the fact that

The exchange of crossing and nestings follows from the definition of

Appendix A
Positivity conjectures on distribution of vincular patterns

Several sequences of numbers and polynomials associated to counting permutation patterns, or pattern-avoiding permutations have been
conjectured to be Stieltjes moment sequences, see
[
7
, Section 4.2, Open question]
,
[
5
]
.
In this appendix, we introduce two new conjectures that adds to this growing list of “positivity questions”
associated with permutation patterns.
Our conjectures here involve the distribution of occurences of vincular patterns of the form
a-bc
and
bc-a
.

We first recall
[
9
, Proposition 1]
as stated in
[
10
, Proposition 2]
:

Proposition A.1
.

With respect to being equidistributed, the twelve vincular patterns of the form
a-bc
or
bc-a

fall into the three classes

\{1-23,3-21,12-3,32-1\}

\{1-32,3-12,21-3,23-1\}

\{2-13,2-31,13-2,31-2\}

Following Claesson and Mansour
[
10
]
, we refer to these classes as Class 1, 2 and 3, respectively.
Finally, let
P_{n}^{(1)}(x),P_{n}^{(2)}(x)
, and
P_{n}^{(3)}(x)
denote the generating polynomials of these three classes, respectively.

We have already noted in Theorem
3.8

that the generating function
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}^{(3)}(x)t^{n}

can be written as a Stieltjes-type continued fraction.
However, it seems that the generating function of the polynomial sequences

\left(P_{n}^{(1)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0},\left(P_{n}^{(2)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0}

do not have any nice continued fraction.
A consequence of this continued fraction is that the sequence of polynomials
(P_{n}^{(3)}(x))_{n\geq 0}

forms a Stieltjes moment sequence for
x\geq 0
.
Hence, it is natural to ask if the same is true for the polynomial sequences

\left(P_{n}^{(1)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0},\left(P_{n}^{(2)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0}

even though they do not have a nice continued fraction.
We have the following conjecture:

Conjecture A.2
.

The sequences of polynomials
\left(P_{n}^{(1)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0}
,
\left(P_{n}^{(2)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0}
,
where the polynomials
P_{n}^{(1)}(x)
and
P_{n}^{(2)}(x)

are the generating polynomials for the number of occurences of a vincular pattern of Class 1 or of Class 2, respectively,
are both Stieltjes moment sequences for
x\geq 0
.

To verify this conjecture, for each of the two sequences we computed its S-fraction coefficients;
these are rational functions in the variable
x
.
We then used the
Reduce
function in Mathematica to compute the subdomain of
\mathbb{R}
in which these are non-negative.
For the sequence
\left(P_{n}^{(1)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0}
, we were able to do this for the first
30
S-fraction coefficients,
and for the sequence
\left(P_{n}^{(2)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0}
we tested the first
37
S-fraction coefficients.
In both cases, we found that these initial S-fraction coefficients are non-negative whenever
x\geq 0
.

A stronger consequence of the existence of the Stieltjes-type continued fraction for the sequence
(P_{n}^{(3)}(x))_{n\geq 0}

is that it is coefficientwise-Hankel totally positive
[
34
, Theorem 9.9]
.
Hence, we tested if the polynomial sequences
\left(P_{n}^{(1)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0},\left(P_{n}^{(2)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 0}

are also coefficientwise Hankel totally positive.
Unfortunately, this is not the case.
However, this seems to be true after a shift
x\mapsto x+1
; we state this as a conjecture:

Conjecture A.3
.

The sequences of polynomials
\left(P_{n}^{(1)}(x+1)\right)_{n\geq 0}
,
\left(P_{n}^{(2)}(x+1)\right)_{n\geq 0}
,
where the polynomials
P_{n}^{(1)}(x)
and
P_{n}^{(2)}(x)

are the generating polynomials for the number of occurences of a vincular pattern of Class 1 or of Class 2, respectively,
are both coefficientwise Hankel totally positive with respect to the variable
x
.

We have verified this conjecture for the first
10\times 10
submatrix.
It is clear that
Conjecture
A.3
implies that Conjecture
A.2
holds for
x\geq 1
.

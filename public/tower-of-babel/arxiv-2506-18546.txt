On an Iterative Scheme for the Spinorial Yamabe Equation on Manifolds with Boundary Thanks: Mathematisches Institut, Universität Freiburg, eric.trebuchon@math.uni-freiburg.de
Eric Trébuchon

Abstract
. We study the existence of solutions to the spinorial Yamabe equation—that is, the Euler–Lagrange equation associated with the conformal invariant introduced by S. Raulot—for compact manifolds with boundary. For the inhomogeneous equation, we employ an iterative scheme to establish existence under smallness assumptions on the relevant parameters. Using bootstrapping methods, we extend the regularity of the solution to C ∞ C^{\infty} away from its zero set in the interior, and up to the boundary in the case of Shapiro–Lopatinski boundary conditions.

1.
Introduction

The study of spin conformal invariants has played a central role in differential geometry, with foundational contributions by Hijazi, Lott, and others
[
Hij86
,
Lot86
,
Bär92
,
Amm03b
]
, who introduced such an invariant via the smallest positive eigenvalue of the Dirac operator on closed manifolds. This concept was later extended to manifolds with boundary by Raulot
[
Rau09a
]
, who introduced a conformal invariant associated to a fixed spin structure
\sigma
on a compact Riemannian manifold
(\Omega,h)
with boundary, under the chiral bag boundary condition
B^{\pm}\psi=0
, defined by

\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega):=\inf_{h\in[h]}|\lambda_{1}(D^{h}_{B^{\pm}})|\,\text{Vol}(\Omega,h)^{\frac{1}{n}},

where
[h]
represents the conformal class of the Riemannian metric
h
and
\lambda_{1}^{\pm}(D^{h}_{B^{\pm}})
denotes the smallest eigenvalue of the Dirac operator
D_{h}
under the chiral bag boundary condition
B^{\pm}
. The choice of sign in
\pm
yields the same spectrum up to sign and is hence irrelevant for
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
.

This article is about the spinorial Yamabe equation on Riemannian spin manifolds with boundary. This equation is equivalent to the Euler-Lagrange equation of the above-mentioned conformal invariant, which can be expressed by a variational problem. Specifically, given a smooth compact Riemannian manifolds
(\Omega,h)
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, a fixed spin structure
\sigma
and its corresponding spinor bundle
\Sigma\Omega
, as well as a chiral bag boundary condition
B^{\pm}
, the Euler-Lagrange equation turns out to be equivalent (see Appendix
6
) to

(2)

\displaystyle Du

\displaystyle=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle B^{\pm}u

\displaystyle=0\quad\quad\,\,\,\,\,\text{on }\partial\Omega

where
p=\frac{2n}{n-1}
. In the variational formulation, there is an extra orthogonality condition between
u
and the kernel of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\ker(B^{\pm})}}
if the latter is not already vanishing.

In our article, however, we will analyse this problem for a wider range of boundary operators
P
and with non-homogeneous boundary condition, meaning we will be interested in solutions to

(3)

\displaystyle Du

\displaystyle=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle Pu

\displaystyle=Pg\quad\quad\,\,\,\,\,\text{on }\partial\Omega

where
g
is a prescribed section of the spinor bundle and
P
is any boundary operator such that
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\ker(P)}}
behaves well (this will be specified later).

The classical analogue, the Yamabe problem on manifolds with boundary, has been studied extensively using a variety of analytical methods
[
BC14
,
Esc92a
,
Esc92b
,
Mar05
]
.

In the following, we want to state some first result for the conformal spin invariant on closed
manifolds, which is defined as

\lambda_{\text{min}}^{+}(\Omega,[h])=\inf_{h^{\prime}\in[h]}\lambda_{1}^{+}(D^{h^{\prime}})\text{Vol}(\Omega,h^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{n}},

where
\lambda_{1}^{+}
denotes the first positive eigenvalue.

In
[
Hij91
]
, Hijazi derives
a conformal lower bound for any eigenvalues
\lambda
of the Dirac operator involving the Yamabe
invariant
\mu(\Omega,[h])
. Indeed, he proved that if
n\geq 3
, then
\lambda^{2}\text{Vol}(\Omega,h)^{\frac{2}{n}}\geq\frac{n}{4(n-1)}\mu(\Omega,[h])

The Yamabe invariant
\mu(\Omega,[h])
has been introduced in
[
Yam60
]
in order to solve the Yamabe problem, although this paper contained a flaw pointed out by Trudinger, which was then corrected by Trudinger, Aubin and later in full generality by Schoen, resulting in a complete affirmative resolution of the Yamabe problem in the year 1984.

Lott showed in
[
Lot86
]
that the conformal invariant is positive if there is some element
h^{\prime}\in[h]
such that
D^{h^{\prime}}
is invertible. This result was improved in
[
Amm03a
]
, where Ammann showed that
D^{h^{\prime}}
does not need to be invertible for this to hold.

In the same paper, Ammann showed that
\lambda_{\text{min}}^{+}(\Omega,[h])\leq\lambda_{\text{min}}^{+}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})=\frac{n}{2}\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}
with the standard metric and spin structure on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, where
\omega_{n}
is the
n-
measure of the
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
. In
[
Amm03b
]
, he showed that the strict inequality
\lambda_{\text{min}}^{+}(\Omega,[h])<\lambda_{\text{min}}^{+}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})
implies the existence of a solution to the spinorial Yamabe equation. This is very reminiscent of the result by Aubin
[
Aub76
]
in resolving the classical Yamabe problem. Several other spinorial Yamabe-type equations can be found, e.g., in
[
Iso11b
,
Iso11a
,
Rau09b
,
BCT19
]
.

Solutions to the spinorial Yamabe equation are connected to several intriguing geometric problems. For example, in dimension
n=2
, the solutions produce conformally immersed surfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with constant mean curvature
[
Amm03a
]
. The reverse is also proven there, namely periodic conformal immersions with constant mean
curvature lead to solutions of the spinorial Yamabe equation for all
n\geq 2
.

The spinorial Yamabe equation is also a part of the spinorial analogue of the supersymmetric extension of harmonic maps, i.e., the Dirac-harmonic
[
CJLW06
,
CJW08
]
.

Finally arriving back at our setting with both the boundary and the spinorial version, the spin conformal invariant (
1
) introduced in
[
Rau09a
]
satisfies a lot of similar results:

(1)

The Hijazi inequality proved in
[
Rau06
]
, yields a comparison between the chiral bag invariant
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
and the Yamabe invariant
\mu(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
(see
[
Esc92b
]
). Explicitly, if
n\geq 3
, we have:

\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega)^{2}\geq\frac{n}{4(n-1)}\mu(\Omega,\partial\Omega).

(2)

In
[
Rau09a
]
, a generalization of Ammann’s result, which holds for closed spin manifolds, is shown:

\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega)\leq\lambda_{\text{min}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+},\partial{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+})=\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{{\omega}_{n}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}},

where
{\omega}_{n}
again denotes the
n-
dimensional volume of the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
.

(3)

For certain classes of manifolds, the last inequality is shown to be strict
[
Rau11
]
. There are similar results in the context of closed manifolds. There doesn’t seem to be any results claiming that the strict inequality implies the existence of solutions to the spinorial Yamabe equation with boundary condition.

(4)

For spinorial Yamabe-type equations on manifolds with boundary, there are some known results e.g
[
DL18
,
WZ24
]
.

We now return to our problem of solving (
3
). In
[
RX21
]
, the authors gave a new approach for solving the (classical) Yamabe equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions under some smallness assumptions on the underlying Riemannian domain using an iterative scheme.

The main objective of this paper is to explore whether such an iterative method can be adapted to the spinorial setting.
The basic idea remains the similar, but the spinorial case introduces significant technical challenges.

In the Yamabe case, working with the (conformal) Laplace operator, many estimates are carried out on the corresponding quadratic forms using
H^{1}-
regularity. Since the Dirac operator is of first order, the natural regularity is
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
. To work with such a quadratic form, it is helpful to take a “square-root” out of the Dirac operator.
For that, we work with the Dirac operator under a certain boundary condition, yielding an operator
D_{P}:={\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\ker(P)}}
. If this operator is self-adjoint, we can use the functional calculus of self-adjoint operators to define
|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
. To prove our results, we will need that
D_{P}
is a
regular
operator, by which we mean

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{H^{1}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}\leq c_{1}\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|D\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

for all
\psi\in\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})=\ker(P)
, where the subscript
\mathcal{T}
refers to norms defined via a chosen trivialization of the spinor bundle. We prove that this regularity for
D_{P}
gets inherited by the root
|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
in the shape of the regularity estimate

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}\leq c_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}+\||D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}\right)

for all
\psi\in\operatorname{dom}(|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

Furthermore, the techniques we will use depend on the size of the smallest eigenvalue of
D_{P}
, denoted
\lambda_{1}(D_{P})
, which is understood as the eigenvalue of minimal absolute value in the spectrum. In the case of ambiguity, we simply choose the positive value.

We denote by
c_{h},C_{h}>0
the constants that will relate our metric-related
L^{p}-
norm and our
L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}-
norm defined by means of trivialization
\mathcal{T}
, which we will discuss in the prerequisites.

With all this, our theorem can be stated as:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
D_{P}
is self-adjoint, invertible, and regular as above. Then, for all spinors
g\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
and complex numbers
\lambda
such that
\|g\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}
,
|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}
and
|\lambda||\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}
are small as a function of
c_{h},C_{h},c_{1}
and
n
, there exists a solution
u\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
to the boundary value problem

(4)

\displaystyle Du

\displaystyle=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(5)

\displaystyle Pu

\displaystyle=Pg.

Inspired by the methods in
[
RX21
]
for the classical Yamabe equation, we construct a similar iterative scheme and prove by explicit estimates, that the sequence of solutions
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to some function
u
. These estimates only work out under certain conditions on
\lambda
,
\lambda_{1}(D_{P})
and
g
, as well as the regularity properties of the boundary condition and the trivialization. We then prove that this limit function indeed solves the partial differential equation.

In Section
5
, we enhance the regularity by iterating various Sobolev embedding theorems and Schauder estimates in the interior and even till the boundary for Shapiro-Lopatinski boundary value problems. As the notion of Shapiro-Lopatinski is a local one, we can not extend this regularity for non-local boundary value problems to the boundary. The class of Shapiro-Lopatinski boundary problems contains e.g. the chiral and MIT bag boundary value conditions.

The Shapiro-Lopatinski condition can be used to get an elliptic inequality for all
q\in(1,\infty)
and some
c_{q}>0
of the type

\|\psi\|_{W^{1,q}}^{2}\leq c_{q}\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{q}}^{2}+\|D\psi\|_{L^{q}}^{2}\right)

holding for all
\psi\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
with
D\psi\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
fulfilling
P\psi=0
, with the boundary operator defined on
P\colon W^{1,q}(\Sigma\Omega)\to W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}})
.

Theorem 1.2

(Regularity)
.

Let
(D,P)
be as above and elliptic in the sense of Shapiro-Lopatinski, then any solution
u\in H^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
of
Du=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u
,
Pu=Pg
with
p=\frac{2n}{n-1}
and
g\in W^{1,\infty}
lies in
C^{1,\alpha}(\Sigma\Omega)
and is smooth on the complement of
u^{-1}(\{0\})
.

An interior version of this theorem holds without any assumptions on the boundary condition by work of Ammann
[
Amm03b
]
.

We note that besides the chiral bag boundary condition, other local boundary condition that give rise to self-adjoint and regular Dirac operator and fulfill the Shapiro-Lopatinski condition exist: every regular and local boundary problem is Shapiro-Lopatinski
[
GUB24
, Thm. 4.5]
. Since we already assume regularity of
(D,P)
, it suffices to further require locality of
P
to establish boundary regularity. For a characterization in even dimensions of local boundary conditions with self-adjoint Dirac operators, see
[
GUB24
, Thm. 1.5]
.

Sobolev Spaces on Vector Bundles and Trivializations

Let
E
be a complex vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold
\Omega
with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Choose a finite trivialization
\mathcal{T}=(\kappa_{\alpha}\colon V_{\alpha}\to U_{\alpha}\subset\Omega,\xi_{\alpha},\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha=0,...,k}
of the vector bundle
E
as e.g in
[
GS13
]
. The sets
V_{\alpha}
are open with respect to the topology of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}:={\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
. The trivialization
\mathcal{T}
contains a finite family of charts
\kappa_{\alpha}
and trivializing maps
\xi_{\alpha}\colon V_{\alpha}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{r}\to{\left.\kern-1.2ptE\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{U_{\alpha}}}
over
\kappa_{\alpha}
, as well as a partition of unity subordinated to the cover
(U_{\alpha})_{\alpha=0,\dots,k}
called
(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha=0,\dots,k}
with
\rho_{\alpha}:\Omega\to[0,1]
. Define
W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as the distributions
f\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
such that the norm

\|f\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\bigg(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|(\rho_{\alpha}f)\circ\kappa_{\alpha}\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is finite, where the fractional Sobolev norm on domains of the euclidean space are defined and studied in
[
DNPV12
]
.

This definition of
W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as a set does not depend on the trivialization itself, yet different trivializations would yield to different (but by compactness of
\Omega
still equivalent) norms.
Furthermore, for a complex vector bundle
E
of rank
r
and a trivialization
\mathcal{T}=(\kappa_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha},\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha=0,...,k}
, define
W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(\Omega,E)=:W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)
as the
E-
valued distributions
f\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega,E)
such that

(6)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)}:=\bigg(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}f)\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is finite, where the pullback is defined by
\xi_{\alpha}^{*}u:=\xi^{-1}_{\alpha}\circ u\circ\kappa_{\alpha}
and for later purposes the push forward is
(\xi_{\alpha})_{*}=\xi_{\alpha}\circ u\circ\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}
and thus
(\xi_{\alpha})_{*}^{-1}=\xi_{\alpha}^{*}
. Different trivializations again always yield equivalent norms since there are only finitely many charts. This makes
W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)
as a set independent of the trivialization
\mathcal{T}
, but not as a normed space. For
p=2
we write
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(E):=W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,2}(E)
and when
s=0
, we write
L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}(E):=W_{\mathcal{T}}^{0,p}(E)
, which needs to be distinguished from the Lebesgue space defined by the Hermitian bundle metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
E
and the Riemannian metric
h
on
\Omega
, which we will denote
L^{p}(E)
. It is defined by the completion
L^{p}(E):=\overline{C^{\infty}(E)}^{\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}}
with
\|\psi\|_{L^{p}}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\psi|^{p}\text{d}vol_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, where we recall that
|\cdot|
is induced by the Hermitian product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
E
. For
p=2
,
L^{2}(E)
carries the standard Hermitian product
(\psi,\varphi)_{L^{2}}=\int\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\text{d}vol_{g}
, which means we have a notion of
L^{2}(E)-
orthogonality. We can identify
L^{p}(E)
and
L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}(E)
as sets, but they differ in norm. The following Lemma shows they are equivalent in a uniform way:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold
\Omega
with boundary and let
\mathcal{T}
be a trivialization. Then

(7)

c_{h}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}(E)}\leq C_{h}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}

for some constants
c_{h},C_{h}>0
independent of
p
.

Proof:

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\kappa_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha},\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha=0,\dots,k}
be a finite trivialization of
E
over
\Omega
, where
(\rho_{\alpha})
is a smooth partition of unity subordinate to the open cover associated to the charts
\kappa_{\alpha}
. Since
\Omega
is compact and the
\rho_{\alpha}
have compact support, there exist constants
\delta>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

•

For every
x\in\Omega
, there exists at least one
\alpha
with
\rho_{\alpha}(x)\geq\delta
;

•

For every
x\in\Omega
, at most
N
of the functions
\rho_{\alpha}(x)
are nonzero (one could take
N=k
for example)

For any measurable section
f
of
E
, we first show a certain upper bound. By using the relation between the finite dimensional
\ell^{1}
and the
\ell^{p}-
norm (which follows by the discrete Hölder’s inequality) and by using the uniform bound
N
on the number of nonzero terms, we estimate:

|f(x)|=\sum_{\alpha}|1_{\rho_{\alpha}(x)\not=0}(x)\rho_{\alpha}(x)f(x)|\leq N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{\alpha}|\rho_{\alpha}(x)f(x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Raising both sides to the
p-
th power and integrating over
\Omega
gives

\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p}\leq N^{p-1}\sum_{\alpha}\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p},

and taking the
p-
th root yields

\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}\leq N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{\alpha}\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For the lower bound, note that at each point
x\in\Omega
, there exists some
\alpha_{0}
with
\rho_{\alpha_{0}}(x)\geq\delta
. Therefore,

|f(x)|\leq\delta^{-1}|\rho_{\alpha_{0}}(x)f(x)|\leq\delta^{-1}\left(\sum_{\alpha}|\rho_{\alpha}(x)f(x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Raising to the
p
-th power and integrating gives:

\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p}\leq\delta^{-p}\sum_{\alpha}\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p},

so taking the
p
-th root yields

\left(\sum_{\alpha}\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\delta\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}.

Combining both estimates (
2
) and (
2
), we obtain constants
C_{1}=\delta
and
C_{2}=N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
such that

C_{1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}\leq\left(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}.

Since
N
and
\delta
are independent of
p
, the norms are equivalent with constants independent of
p
.

Now, consider a single chart
(\kappa_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})
, where
\xi_{\alpha}:E|_{U_{\alpha}}\to U_{\alpha}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
is a smooth trivialization. By continuity of the Hermitian inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the metric
h
on the compact set
\Omega
, there exist constants
c,C>0
, independent of
p
, such that for all measurable sections
f
supported in
U_{\alpha}
,

c\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}\leq\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}f)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+};{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}\leq C\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}.

Combining the above estimates across all
\alpha
, and using the definition

\|f\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}(E)}:=\left(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}f)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+};{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p}\right)^{1/p},

we obtain

\left(\sum_{\alpha}c^{p}\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}(E)}\leq\left(\sum_{\alpha}C^{p}\|\rho_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Using (
2
) involving
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, we conclude for the constants
c_{h}=cC_{1}=c{\delta}
and
C_{h}=CC_{2}=CN^{1-\frac{1}{p}}>0

c_{h}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}(E)}\leq C_{h}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}.

The value of

is independent of

and by choosing

this constant also becomes independent of

, hence proving the result.
∎

For integers

Interpolating Sobolev Spaces on Vector Bundles

We quickly introduce Besov spaces as a short-hand for this section.
The Besov space
B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})
can be defined via interpolation of Sobolev spaces. Let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Theta,p}
stand for the real interpolation method
[
Tri10
, Section 1.6.2]
. For
s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},1<p<\infty
,
and
0<\Theta<1
, we put

B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}):=\left(W^{s_{0},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}),W^{s_{1},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})\right)_{\Theta,p},

where
s=\Theta s_{0}+(1-\Theta)s_{1}
. In fact,
B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})
does not depend on the choice of
s_{0},s_{1}
and
\Theta
.

In order to understand the interpolation of sobolev spaces on vector bundles, we need the following

Lemma 2.4

(Equivalent norms for interpolation of Sobolev Spaces)
.

For a Riemannian manifold
\Omega
with boundary and a vector bundle
E
over
\Omega
with a finite trivialization
\mathcal{T}
, the norm on
B^{s}_{p,p}(E,\mathcal{T}):=\left(W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{0},p}(E),W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{1},p}(E)\right)_{\Theta,p}
is equivalent to
\left(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}f)\|_{B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

Proof:

Define
\ell^{p}(B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}))
to be the sequences
(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of functions
f_{\alpha}\in B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})

\alpha=0,\dots,k
such that the norm

\|(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}))}=\left(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|f_{\alpha}\|_{B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is finite. Define similarly
\ell^{p}(W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}))
.

Consider

\Psi\colon W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)\to\ell^{p}(W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})),\quad f\mapsto\left(\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}f_{\alpha})\right)_{\alpha}.

Since
\Psi
is by definition an isometric isomorphism,

B^{s}_{p,p}(E,\mathcal{T}):=\left(W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{0},p}(E),W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{1},p}(E)\right)_{\Theta,p}\cong\left(\ell^{p}(W^{s_{0},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})),\ell^{p}(W^{s_{1},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}))\right)_{\Theta,p}

is an isometric isomorphism as well. By
[
Tri78
, Thm 1.18.1]
, the right hand side is equivalent to

\big(\ell^{p}(W^{s_{0},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})),\ell^{p}(W^{s_{1},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}))\big)_{\Theta,p}=\ell^{p}\big(\left(W^{s_{0},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}),W^{s_{1},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})\right)_{\Theta,p}\big)

for
1<p<\infty
,
s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\Theta\in(0,1)
,
s=\Theta s_{0}+(1-\Theta)s_{1}
and the equivalence constants are only dependent on
\Theta
and
p
. On the right hand side we now have just
\ell^{p}(B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}))
.
This means that the norm
\left(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}f)\|_{B^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
of the latter space is equivalent to the norm of
B^{s}_{p,p}(E,\mathcal{T})
.

∎

We can now conclude

Theorem 2.5

(Interpolation of
H^{s}(E)-
spaces)
.

For a Riemannian manifold
\Omega
of dimension
n
with boundary and a vector bundle
E
of rank
r
over
\Omega
with a finite trivialization
\mathcal{T}
, we have

\left(H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{0}}(E),H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{1}}(E)\right)_{\Theta,2}=H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(E)

and the constants in this equivalence depend only on
s_{0},s_{1},s,\Theta,n
and
r
.

Proof:

Note that for
p=2
,

B^{s}_{2,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}):=\left(H^{s_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r}),H^{s_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})\right)_{\Theta,2}=H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})

on

[

, Thm. 2.10.1]
with a constant only depending on

and

. We thus have shown with lemma

, that

and the latter space is isometrically isomorphic via

Recall the following classical theorems for Sobolev spaces:

Theorem 2.6

(Sobolev Embeddings,

)
.

For a compact Riemannian manifold
\Omega

For

In the reference, they do not work with sections of vector bundles like stated here, instead they only work with complex valued functions. That is not a problem as we may transform our vector bundle sections to

Fractional Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

We first recall the non-homogeneous fractional Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We will need this inequality for the convergence of our iterative scheme in our main theorem.

Theorem 2.9

(Fractional Gagliardo–Nirenberg,
[
HMOW11
, Prop. 4.2]
)
.

Let
1<p,p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
,
0\leq s,s_{1}
,
0\leq\theta\leq 1
and
n,r\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume

\frac{n}{p}-s=\left(1-\theta\right)\frac{n}{p_{0}}+\theta\left(\frac{n}{p_{1}}-s_{1}\right),\quad s\leq\theta s_{1},

Then the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}\leq K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\|u\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{\theta}

holds for some
K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},p,p_{0},p_{1},s,s_{1},\theta,n,r)>0
.

The result in our reference is only
{\mathbb{R}}-
valued instead of
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}-
valued, but we can deduce the vector valued version by summing up the
2r
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities. More importantly, we can also restrict ourself to bounded, open and connected subsets
\Omega
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with smooth boundary (we will need it only for
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
):

Lemma 2.10

(Fractional Gagliardo–Nirenberg on smooth domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
)
.

Let
U
be a bounded, open and connected subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with smooth boundary and
1<p,p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
,
0\leq s,s_{1}
,
0\leq\theta\leq 1
as well as
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Assume

\frac{n}{p}-s=\left(1-\theta\right)\frac{n}{p_{0}}+\theta\left(\frac{n}{p_{1}}-s_{1}\right),\quad s\leq\theta s_{1},

then the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(U,{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}\leq K_{FGN}(U)\|u\|_{L^{p_{0}}(U,{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(U,{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{\theta}

holds for some
K_{FGN}(U)=K_{FGN}(U,p,p_{0},p_{1},s,s_{1},\theta,n,r)
.

Proof:

We use the extension operator and the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
:
Let
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
. Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(U,{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\|Eu\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\|Eu\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{1-\theta}\|Eu\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})K_{E}(U,0,p_{0},n)^{1-\theta}K_{E}(U,s_{1},p_{1},n)^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{p_{0}}(U,{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(U,{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{\theta}

meaning the fractional Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality holds with

K_{FGN}(U):=K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},p,p_{0},p_{1},s,s_{1},\theta,n,r)K_{E}(U,0,p_{0},n,r)^{1-\theta}K_{E}(U,s_{1},p_{1},n,r)^{\theta}>0.

∎

We now extend our theorem to our case of vector bundles over compact manifolds with boundary

Theorem 2.11

(Fractional Gagliardo–Nirenberg on vector bundles over manifolds with boundary)
.

Let
(\Omega,h)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
E
be a complex vector bundle over
\Omega
of rank
r
with a finite trivialization
\mathcal{T}
.
Let
1<p,p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
,
0\leq s,s_{1}
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.
Assume

\frac{n}{p}-s=\left(1-\theta\right)\frac{n}{p_{0}}+\theta\left(\frac{n}{p_{1}}-s_{1}\right),\quad s\leq\theta s_{1}.

Then the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{\mathcal{T}}(E)}\leq K_{FGN}\|u\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p_{0}}(E)}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{1},p_{1}}(E)}^{\theta}

holds for some
K_{FGN}=K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},p,p_{0},p_{1},s,s_{1},\theta,n,r)>0
.

Proof:

The complex vector bundle
E
has a finite trivialization
\mathcal{T}=(\kappa_{\alpha}\colon V_{\alpha}\to U_{\alpha}\subset\Omega,\xi_{\alpha},\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha=0,...,k}
. We want to prove

\|u\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)}\leq K_{FGN}(E)\|u\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p_{0}}(E)}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{1},p_{1}}(E)}^{\theta}

for all sections
u\in L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p_{0}}(E)\cap W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{1},p_{1}}(E)
and some constant
K_{FGN}(E)
.
Recall definition (
6
) of the Sobolev norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)}=\bigg(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}f)\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For each term, we may use the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on
U:={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
applied to
\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})

\displaystyle\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq K_{FGN}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p(1-\theta)}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p\theta}.

By construction, the constant
K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
only depends on the dimensions
n,r
and the parameters
s,s_{1},p,p_{0},p_{1},\theta
.
Summing up the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities from the charts, we thus obtain:

\|u\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)}^{p}\leq K_{FGN}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p(1-\theta)}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p\theta}.

Recall the discrete Hölder’s inequality which states that for all
q,q^{\prime}\geq 1
with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
, and all
x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}},\,i=1,...,k

\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x_{i}y_{i}|\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x_{i}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|y_{i}|^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

Furthermore, these expressions have a certain monotonicity, namely for
0<q_{1}\leq q_{2},

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x_{i}|^{q_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x_{i}|^{q_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}.

Combining this, we see that the conditions
q,q^{\prime}\geq 1
and
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\geq 1
actually suffice to apply Hölder’s inequality.

Choose
q=\frac{p_{0}}{p(1-\theta)}
and
q^{\prime}=\frac{p_{1}}{p\theta}
which satisfy this modified assumption by simple rearrangement of the parameter conditions posed in our theorem. Applying hence our modified Hölder’s inequality with
x_{i}=\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p(1-\theta)}
and
y_{i}=\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p\theta}
to (
2
), we see

\|u\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)}^{p}\leq K_{FGN}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})\bigg(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p_{0}}\bigg)^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{p_{0}}}\bigg(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|\xi_{\alpha}^{*}(\rho_{\alpha}u)\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})}^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p\theta}{p_{1}}}.

Taking the
p
-th root,

\|u\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s,p}(E)}\leq K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})\|u\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p_{0}}(E)}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{W_{\mathcal{T}}^{s_{1},p_{1}}(E)}^{\theta}.

∎

It is interesting to note that the constant
K_{FGN}
neither depends on
E
nor on the trivialization: We showed
K_{FGN}(E)=K_{FGN}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
is always the same constant as in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
.

The Spinor Bundle

Let
(\Omega,h,\sigma)
be an
n-
dimensional compact Riemannian spin manifold.
The spinor bundle is denoted by

\Sigma\Omega=\Sigma(\Omega,h,\sigma)

and carries a natural Clifford multiplication
\cdot
, a natural
Hermitian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and a metric connection
\nabla^{S}
. These fulfill the properties of a Dirac bundle as introduced in
[
LM89
]
. With these notions, we may define the Dirac Operator by acting through
\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\cdot\nabla^{S}_{e_{k}}
for local orthonormal frames
(e_{k})_{k=1,...,n}
. The definition is independent of the local orthonormal frame and the maximal domain of
D
is
\{\varphi\in L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)\;|\;D\varphi\in L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)\}
where
D\varphi
is to be understood distributionally.
Denote the rank of the spinor bundle as
r:=\operatorname{rank}(\Sigma\Omega)
, which always equals
2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}
for the spinor bundle of a spin manifold.

Proving a Fractional Regularity Estimate

The following section holds in a broader setting, as we will only use the self-adjointness and regularity of a certain linear operator, without specifically needing that it will be the Dirac operator on the spinor bundle.

Let
P\colon H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)\to H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}})
be a boundary operator and denote by
D_{P}:={\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\operatorname{ker}(P)}}
the Dirac operator under the boundary condition induced by
P
. We assume that
D_{P}
is self-adjoint.
Following
[
BB12
, Def. 1.10]
, we call our operator
D_{P}
regular, if there is a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

(15)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}\leq c_{1}\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}+\|D\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}\right)

for all
\psi\in\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})=\ker(P)
.

The following fractional regularity will be needed for
s=\frac{1}{2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
D_{P}\colon\dom(D_{P})\subset L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)
be a self-adjoint regular operator such that
0\not\in\spec{D_{P}}
. Then
D_{P}
is also
s-
regular for all
s\in(0,1)
, i.e., there is an explicit constant
c_{s}>0
(
3
) such that

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}\leq c_{s}\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}+\||D_{P}|^{s}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}\right)

for all
\psi\in\operatorname{dom}(|D_{P}|^{s})
.

To prove this, we need to understand how the operator
|D_{P}|^{s}
is defined and what we may conclude by our conditions on
D_{P}
.

Since
0\not\in\spec(D_{P})
and
D_{P}
is self-adjoint,
D_{P}
is a bijective unbounded operator. This implies that
D_{P}
is Fredholm of index
0
.
Furthermore, we can deduce compactness of
D_{P}^{-1}
by the compact inclusion
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}\to L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}
(Rellich-Kondrachov theorem). By using the method of compact resolvent and again the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem, we get by standard methods
[
FS98
]
that
\operatorname{spec}(D_{P})
consists entirely of point spectrum and is furthermore discrete.

We can then find an
L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)-
orthonormal eigenbasis
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
associated to the operator
D_{P}
with corresponding real eigenvalues
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
. This eigenbasis yields us a description of the domain

\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})=\big\{\varphi=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\;|\;a_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}},\,\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{k}|^{2}(1+\lambda_{k}^{2})<\infty\big\}.

For short, we will denote this space as
H^{1}_{D}
, omitting the dependence on
P
. We equip
H^{1}_{D}
with the graph norm

\|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\|_{H^{1}_{D}}^{2}:=\|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\|_{D}^{2}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{k}|^{2}(1+\lambda_{k}^{2}),

as seen by the
L^{2}-
orthonormality of the eigenbasis.

We may use the functional calculus of self-adjoint operators to construct an operator
|D_{P}|^{s}
with domain

\operatorname{dom}(|D_{P}|^{s})=\big\{\varphi=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\;|\;a_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}},\,\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{k}|^{2}(1+|\lambda_{k}|^{2s})<\infty\big\},

which maps
|D_{P}|^{s}:\varphi=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\mapsto\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{s}\varphi_{k}
. We will similarly denote this space as
H^{s}_{D}
with the graph norm given by

\|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\|_{H^{s}_{D}}^{2}:=\|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\|_{|D_{P}|^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{k}|^{2}(1+|\lambda_{k}|^{2s})
, as seen by
L^{2}-
orthogonality.

This domain is complete with respect to the graph norm, as
|D_{P}|^{s}
is a self-adjoint and thus a closed operator.

We denote
Z\precsim X
for normed spaces if
Z\subset X
as sets and
\|\cdot\|_{X}\leq c\|\cdot\|_{Z}
on
Z
for some fixed
c>0
(or more precisely, if there is a bounded inclusion map
\iota:Z\to X
). We write
A\sim B
for normed spaces if
A\precsim B
and
B\precsim A
as normed spaces.

Furthermore, for any sequence of positive real numbers
(\omega_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, the weighted
\ell^{p}-
space is given by

\ell^{p}(\omega_{k})=\{(f_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\;|\;f_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}},\|(f_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|_{\ell^{p}(\omega_{k})}<\infty\},\quad\|(f_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|_{\ell^{p}(\omega_{k})}^{p}:=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f_{k}|^{p}\omega_{k}.

Remark 3.2
.

Let again
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Theta,p}
stand for the real interpolation method
[
Tri10
, Section 1.6.2]
. If
Z\precsim X
, then it follows that
(Z,Y)_{\theta,p}\precsim(X,Y)_{\theta,p}
and
(Y,Z)_{\theta,p}\precsim(Y,X)_{\theta,p}
.

Proof of Theorem 3.1:

Recall that for all
\varphi=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\in\operatorname{dom}(|D_{P}|^{s})
,

\|\varphi\|_{H_{D}^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{k}|^{2}(1+|\lambda_{k}|^{2s})=\|(a_{k})_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}\left({1+|\lambda_{k}|^{2s}}\right)}^{2}

and for all
\psi=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}b_{k}\varphi_{k}\in\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})
,

\|\psi\|_{H_{D}^{1}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|b_{k}|^{2}(1+\lambda_{k}^{2})=\|(b_{k})_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}\left({1+\lambda_{k}^{2}}\right)}^{2}.

Now since

1+|x|^{2s}\leq(1+x^{2})^{s}\leq 2(1+|x|^{2s})

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
s\in(0,1)
, we conclude

\ell^{2}\left({1+|\lambda_{k}|^{2s}}\right)\sim\ell^{2}\left((1+\lambda_{k}^{2})^{s}\right)

with the equivalence being independent of the eigenvalues.
By
[
BL76
, Thm. 5.5.1]
, we have an isometric isomorphism

\ell^{2}\left((1+\lambda_{k}^{2})^{s}\right)\cong\left(\ell^{2}(1),\ell^{2}\left(1+\lambda_{k}^{2}\right)\right)_{s,2}.

The latter space is isometrically isomorphic to
\left(L^{2},H^{1}_{D}\right)_{s,2}
since we have isometric isomorphisms
H^{1}_{D}\cong\ell^{2}\left({1+\lambda_{k}^{2}}\right)
and
L^{2}\cong\ell^{2}(1)
explicitly given by the orthonormal eigenbasis property and equation (
3
).
We also constructed the isometric isomorphism between
H^{s}_{D}
and
\ell^{2}\left({1+|\lambda_{k}|^{2s}}\right)
using equation (

H^{s}_{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{norm}}}{{\cong}}\ell^{2}\left({1+|\lambda_{k}|^{2s}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{norm3}}}{{\sim}}\ell^{2}\left((1+\lambda_{k}^{2})^{s}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{interpolation}}}{{\cong}}\left(\ell^{2}(1),\ell^{2}\left({1+\lambda_{k}^{2}}\right)\right)_{s,2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{norm2}}}{{\cong}}\left(L^{2},H^{1}_{D}\right)_{s,2}.

Since

by the regularity assumption (

H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{thm.}\penalty\ \ref{thm:H^s interpolation}}}{{\sim}}\left(L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2},H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}\right)_{s,2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{H^1 < D assumption}}}{{\precsim}}\left(L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2},H^{1}_{D}\right)_{s,2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equivnorms}}}{{\precsim}}\left(L^{2},H^{1}_{D}\right)_{s,2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{H^s sim inter}}}{{\sim}}H_{D}^{s},

where the second and third estimates are based on the remark

where the operator norm of the inclusion

as seen in theorem

4.
The Main Theorem

Let
n\geq 2
and
p:=\frac{2n}{n-1}
. Let
(\Omega,h,\sigma)
be an
n-
dimensional compact Riemannian spin manifold with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, a spinor bundle
\Sigma\Omega
of rank
r=2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}
and some trivialization
\mathcal{T}
. Let
P\colon H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)\to H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}})
for

be a boundary operator such that the restricted Dirac operator
D_{P}:={\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\ker(P)}}
is self-adjoint, invertible (meaning
0\not\in\spec{D_{P}}
) and regular, i.e., there exists a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

(24)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}^{2}\leq c_{1}\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|D\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

for all
\psi\in\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})
.

We proved in lemma
3.1
that these conditions imply the existence of some
c_{\frac{1}{2}}>0
explicitly given by (
3
), such that

(25)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq c_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\||D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

for all
u\in\operatorname{dom}(|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

We denote by
\lambda_{1}(D_{P})
the eigenvalue of smallest modulus; in case of ambiguity, we choose the positive one. This choice does not affect the proof, as only the modulus
|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|
will of relevance.

Recall that
c_{h}
and
C_{h}
are defined in (
7
) such that

c_{h}\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma\Omega)}\leq\|\cdot\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}(\Sigma\Omega)}\leq C_{h}\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma\Omega)}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
D_{P}
is self-adjoint, invertible, and regular as above. Then, for all spinors
g\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
and complex numbers
\lambda
such that
\|g\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}
,
|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}
and
|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}|\lambda|
are small as a function of
c_{h},C_{h},c_{1}
and
n
, there exists a solution
u\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
to the boundary value problem

(27)

\displaystyle Du

\displaystyle=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(28)

\displaystyle Pu

\displaystyle=Pg\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Remark 4.2
.

The exact smallness conditions are stated in (
4
)-(
C3
), which are the restrictions yielding a solution
u
with
\|u\|_{H^{1}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma\Omega)}\leq 1
. If one wants to be more flexible, one can use (
4
)-(
B3
) with parameters
\Xi
and
\Lambda
, which determine the upper bounds, as the solution will then satisfy

and

varies the conditions restrictiveness.

Proof of Thm. 4.1:

We decompose the proof into multiple steps: We first construct the iterative sequence, show convergence, analyse the regularity of the limit, check that the limit weakly solves the partial differential equation, and then conclude the theorem. Lastly, we verify that the parameter conditions used in all these steps boil down to the smallness of
\|g\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}
,
\big|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})\big|^{-1}\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}
and
|\lambda||\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}
as described above.

\displaystyle P\tilde{u}_{k+1}

\displaystyle\||u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

) with

\displaystyle\leq C_{h}|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}\bigg(|\lambda|c_{h}^{-\frac{n+1}{n-1}}K_{GN}^{\frac{n+1}{n-1}}\Xi^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\Lambda^{\frac{n}{n-1}}+\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}\bigg).

Thus, we can bound the

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}_{k+1}\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{H^1<D assumption main theorem}}}{{\leq}}c_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\tilde{u}_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|D\tilde{u}_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

We finally get the

and

implying

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}_{k+1}\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}},\|u_{k+1}\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}\leq\Xi,\quad\|\tilde{u}_{k+1}\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}},\|u_{k+1}\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}\leq\Lambda.

Convergence

In the previous part we have constructed two
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)-
bounded sequences
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(\tilde{u}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.
By proving that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\tilde{u}_{k+1}-\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{H_{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}}<\infty,

we will conclude that
(\tilde{u}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a
H_{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)-
Cauchy sequence and is therefore convergent.

The defining equations of two consecutive iterations with
k\geq 1
are

\displaystyle Du_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}\,\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle Du_{k}

\displaystyle=\lambda|u_{k-1}|^{p-2}u_{k-1}\,\text{ in }\Omega

and
Pu_{k+1}=Pu_{k}=Pg
on
\partial\Omega
.

Let
\omega=\omega^{+}+\omega^{-}
be the
L^{2}-
orthogonal decomposition of an arbitrary spinor
\omega\in

\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})
into the positive and negative eigenvalue parts of
D_{P}
in the orthogonal decomposition
\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\spec(D_{P})}\text{Eig}_{\lambda}(D_{P})
. By the eigenspinor property, applying
D_{P}
preserves the decomposition and the related orthogonality.
By using the shorthand notation
\Delta_{k+1}:=u_{k+1}-u_{k}
,
subtracting the second from the first equation and pairing with
\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\in

\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})
, we get one equation per sign:

(2)

\displaystyle\left(D_{P}\Delta_{k+1},\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}=\lambda\left(|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-|u_{k-1}|^{p-2}u_{k-1},\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}.

Recall our definition
(
3
)
of the self-adjoint operators
|D_{P}|^{s}:H^{s}_{D}(\Sigma\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)
.
There is an inclusion
\operatorname{dom}(D_{P})\subset\operatorname{dom}(|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}})
: By discreteness of
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
(recall that
D_{P}^{-1}
is compact and self-adjoint) there are only finitely many eigenvalues with modulus smaller than
1
and all other eigenvalues satisfy
1+|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1+\lambda_{k}^{2}
, thus the comparison test of series implies the inclusion. Hence,
\Delta_{k+1}
and
\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}
are also elements of
\operatorname{dom}(|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}})=H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}(\Sigma\Omega)
.

For
s=1
, we get the unbounded operator
|D_{P}|:L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)
which can be simply expressed by
|D_{P}f|=D_{P}f^{+}-D_{P}f^{-}
.
Equation
(
2
)
yields an expression for
\left(D_{P}\Delta_{k+1},\Delta_{k+1}^{+}\right)_{L^{2}}=\left(|D_{P}|\Delta_{k+1}^{+},\Delta_{k+1}^{+}\right)_{L^{2}}
and
\left(D_{P}\Delta_{k+1},\Delta_{k+1}^{-}\right)_{L^{2}}=\left(-|D_{P}|\Delta_{k+1}^{-},\Delta_{k+1}^{-}\right)_{L^{2}}
, showing

\displaystyle\||D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{+},|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{+}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(|D_{P}|\Delta_{k+1}^{+},\Delta_{k+1}^{+}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-|u_{k-1}|^{p-2}u_{k-1},\Delta_{k+1}^{+}\right)_{L^{2}}

and also

\displaystyle\||D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{-},|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{-}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(|D_{P}|\Delta_{k+1}^{-},\Delta_{k+1}^{-}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(-D_{P}\Delta_{k+1}^{-},\Delta_{k+1}^{-}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\left(|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-|u_{k-1}|^{p-2}u_{k-1},\Delta_{k+1}^{-}\right)_{L^{2}}.

We now get an upper bound on the
|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}-
graph norm by

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\||D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3)

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left|\lambda\left(|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-|u_{k-1}|^{p-2}u_{k-1},\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}\right|,

which relates to the
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)-
norm through (
25

Since
\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\in\dom(D_{P})

\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}=\left(|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-1}|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}
. This makes the first term on the right-hand side of (

(4)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}(|D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-2}\||D_{P}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})\right|^{-1}\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}}^{2}.

To estimate the second term of

, we want to use the following pointwise estimate, for which we need to choose a representative of the Sobolev functions at play. Let

for

\displaystyle|f(z)-f(y)|\leq|z-y|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\|Df(tz+(1-t)y)\|,

. The operator norm may be estimated as

.
For a certain representative of

and

and

\displaystyle\leq(p-1)\,|u_{k}(x)-u_{k-1}(x)|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left|tu_{k}(x)+(1-t)u_{k-1}(x)\right|^{p-2}

This pointwise estimate implies

\displaystyle\bigg|\lambda\big(|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-|u_{k-1}|^{p-2}u_{k-1},\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|\int_{\Omega}||u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-|u_{k-1}|^{p-2}u_{k-1}||\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}|d\text{vol}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq(p-1)|\lambda|\int_{\Omega}\,|\Delta_{k}||u_{k}|^{p-2}|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}|d\text{vol}_{h}

\displaystyle\,+(p-1)|\lambda|\int_{\Omega}\,|\Delta_{k}||u_{k-1}|^{p-2}|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}|d\text{vol}_{h}.

Let
p_{A}\geq p-2=\frac{2}{n-1}
.
Applying Hölder’s inequality to both terms and choosing
\overline{p}_{1}=\frac{p_{A}}{p-2}
,
\overline{p}_{2}=\overline{p}_{3}=\frac{2\overline{p}_{1}}{\overline{p}_{1}-1}=\frac{2p_{A}}{p_{A}-p+2}=:p_{B}

\displaystyle\bigg|\lambda\big(|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-

\displaystyle|u_{k-1}|^{p-2}u_{k-1},\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq(p-1)|\lambda|\left(\int_{\Omega}\,|u_{k}(x)|^{\overline{p}_{1}(p-2)}d\text{vol}_{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{\overline{p}_{1}}}\|\Delta_{k}\|_{L^{p_{B}}}\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{p_{B}}}

\displaystyle\,+(p-1)|\lambda|\left(\int_{\Omega}\,|u_{k-1}(x)|^{\overline{p}_{1}(p-2)}d\text{vol}_{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{\overline{p}_{1}}}\|\Delta_{k}\|_{L^{p_{B}}}\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{p_{B}}}

\displaystyle\leq(p-1)|\lambda|\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{A}}}^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\|\Delta_{k}\|_{L^{p_{B}}}\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{p_{B}}}

(5)

\displaystyle\,+(p-1)|\lambda|\|u_{k-1}\|_{L^{p_{A}}}^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\|\Delta_{k}\|_{L^{p_{B}}}\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{L^{p_{B}}}.

For the factors of
u_{k}
and

in this expression, we use the Gagliardo-Nirenberg theorem (

): Take
\hat{p}=p_{A},\hat{p}_{0}=\hat{p}_{1}=2,\hat{s}=0,\hat{s}_{1}=1
and hence
\theta_{A}=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{p_{A}}
.

Solving the Partial Differential Equation

To improve convergence and regularity, note that
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)\hookrightarrow H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)\hookrightarrow H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)

are compact inclusions for
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded in
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
, as well as convergent in
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)
towards
u\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)
. The
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)-
bound and the compactness of the first inclusion imply that
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)-
convergent subsequence, which must converge to
u\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)
by the continuity of the second inclusion. As above, any subsequence of
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a finer subsequence that converges in
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)
to
u
, implying that the entire sequence already
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)-
converges to
u
for all
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. This improved the
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)-
convergence of our sequence to convergence in all
H^{s}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma\Omega)
with
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, and showed that the limit
u\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)
even lies in
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)
for all
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.

By the bound in
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
, there is a subsequence
(u_{k_{n}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and some
w\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
such that
f(u_{k_{n}})\to f(w)
for all functionals
f\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)^{*}
. Since
u_{k_{n}}\to u
converges strongly in
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)
, we trivially get weak
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)-
convergence. But the weak
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)-
limit of
(u_{k_{n}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has to be the same as the weak
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma\Omega)-
limit of
(u_{k_{n}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, because the inclusion into
L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)
is for both spaces compact and injective, meaning
u=w\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
. By lemma
2.2
, our limit
u
is hence in
u\in H^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
. We hence achieved a higher regularity that we could not show by our previous method. This is an essential step in showing that
u
is a solution of the spinorial Yamabe equation, because we may now apply our operators
P
and
D
to
u
and work with slighlty better convergence, which is necessary to work with
P
:

We will now prove that our limit satisfies the boundary condition

\displaystyle Pu=Pg\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Since
P
is a bounded linear operator
P\colon H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)\to H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}})
for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, we conclude by the improved convergence for all
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, that
Pu_{k}=Pg
converges in
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}})
to
Pu
. Since the converging sequence was constant, it already follows that
Pu=Pg\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}})
. This implies that
u-g\in\operatorname{ker}(P\colon H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)\to H_{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}}))
, meaning

. We have thus proven the boundary condition
Pu=Pg
of our boundary value problem, which we will now need when proving that
u
is a weak solution of our spinorial Yamabe equation.

We want to show that the limit
u
solves

(2)

\displaystyle Du=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\text{ in }\Omega.

To show this, we start with a weak version of the partial differential equation split to the positive and negative spectrum. More precisely, we want to show that

\displaystyle\kappa 2^{\frac{3}{2}}3^{\theta_{B}}|\lambda|\big|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})\big|^{-1}<1

with
\kappa=2(p-1)c_{h}^{-\frac{2}{n-1}-2}C_{h}^{2(1-\theta_{B})}c_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\theta_{B}}K_{GN2}^{\frac{2}{n-1}}K_{FGN}^{2}
, where

. As a reminder, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality contained the constants

All the above conditions (

\big|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})\big|^{-1}|\lambda|

and

are small as a function of the parameters

Essential Remarks:

(1)

We get an apriori estimate on the
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}-
norm of our solution
u
by
\|u\|_{H^{1}_{\mathcal{T}}}\leq\Lambda=1
. The conditions on the parameters depend on the chosen
\Lambda
and
\Xi
which we have set to
1
in our final summarizing step. Reversely, for fixed geometry, spinor
g
, and parameter
\lambda
, only certain values of
\Lambda
and
\Xi
are allowed. They can’t be too large nor too small. The benefit of smaller
\Lambda
and

and
\|u\|_{H^{1}_{\mathcal{T}}}\leq\Xi
. So even though we set

in the final step, one would maybe want to search for optimal values of
\Lambda
and

to yield the most out of the iterative scheme.

(2)

We may scale
\lambda
by
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
to

\hat{\lambda}:=\alpha\lambda,

transforming our solution
u
to
\hat{u}=\alpha^{\frac{1}{2-p}}u
and the boundary value function

to
\hat{g}:=\alpha^{\frac{1}{2-p}}g=\alpha^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}g,
so that

and

.

This shows that by increasing/decreasing
|\lambda|
(
\alpha>1
/
\alpha<1
), we may resp. decrease/increase both
\|g\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}
and
\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}
.

We need

\big|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})\big|^{-1}|\lambda|
,
|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|g\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}
to be small as a function of the parameters
c_{h},C_{h}
and
c_{1}
as concluded in (
4
)-(
C3
).

By the above, we can drop all the conditions on
|\lambda||\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}
and simply rely on

being small as a function of the same parameters
c_{h},C_{h},c_{1}
and on
|\lambda||\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}
.

The reverse is also possible, meaning there is a solution for any

, as long as we choose
|\lambda||\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}
small as a function of
c_{h},C_{h},c_{1},|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|g\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}
.

Keep in mind that this procedure transforms our
H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}-
bound
\Lambda
by a corresponding
factor of
\alpha^{\frac{1}{2-p}}
.

(3)

The relevance of the spinorial Yamabe equation came from its conformal invariance and the relation to the Euler-Lagrange equation (see Appendix
6
). Nevertheless, one would like to know if the Dirac Operator on the left-hand side of
Du=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u
could not be replaced by other operators:

(i)

Replace the Dirac operator
D
and the boundary operator
P
by some other differential operator
D^{\prime}
with a corresponding boundary operator
P^{\prime}
, acting on the sections of a possibly different complex or real vector bundle. Then the non linear boundary value problem

(1)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}u

\displaystyle=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(2)

\displaystyle P^{\prime}u

\displaystyle=P^{\prime}g\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

has a solution as long as
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD^{\prime}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\ker(P^{\prime})}}
is regular, self-adjoint and invertible, and further
|\lambda||\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1},\,|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})|^{-1}\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|g\|_{H^{1}_{\mathcal{T}}}
are small enough. We do not need to change anything in the proof, as we never explicitly mentioned or used that our operator
D
was in fact the Dirac operator, nor that our vector bundle was the spinor bundle (we only used its rank).

(ii)

The exponent
p
in the proof is really only used in five instances, each of which is listed below:

(i)

We use
p-1\geq 1
when estimating the norm of
||x|^{p-2}x-|y|^{p-2}y|

(ii)

The first Gagliardo-Nirenberg needed
\theta=\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p-1})\in[0,1]

(iii)

We introduce
p_{A}
that needs to fulfill
p_{A}\geq p-2
so that the Hölder’s inequality works

(iv)

We need
\theta_{A}=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{p_{A}}\in[0,1]
for the second Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

(v)

We need
\theta_{B}=\frac{n(p-2)}{p_{A}}\in[0,1]
for the last Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.

Explicitly, the restrictions on
p_{A}
sum up to:
p_{A}\in[2,\frac{2n}{n-2}]
and
p_{A}\geq n(p-2)
, which can only exist if
n(p-2)\leq\frac{2n}{n-2}
, meaning when
p\leq\frac{2n-2}{n-2}
. The other conditions on
p
imply
p\geq 2
and again
p\leq\frac{2n-2}{n-2}
. In summary, for all
p\in[2,\frac{2n-2}{n-2}],
the statement of the proof holds. As a reminder, our value of
p
used in the theorem was
\frac{2n}{n-1}\in[2,\frac{2n-2}{n-2}]
.

(4)

One could have defined an iterative scheme for
a\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\spec(D_{P})
and
f\in L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)
by setting
u_{0}=f
and then

\displaystyle Du_{k+1}-au_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-au_{k}\,\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle Pu_{k+1}

\displaystyle=Pg\,\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

If
a=0
and
f=g
, then this is the iterative scheme used in the proof above.

This new iterative scheme can be understood just like in our proof by analyzing consecutive iterative equation, but with additional terms. For example, (
12
) turns into

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+|a|)\big|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})\big|^{-1}\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}}^{2}+|a|\left|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})\right|^{-1}\|\Delta_{k}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}}\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}}

\displaystyle+\kappa\Xi^{\frac{2(1-\theta_{A})}{n-1}}\Lambda^{\frac{2\theta_{A}}{n-1}}|\lambda|\big|\lambda_{1}(D_{P})\big|^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\|\Delta_{k}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}}\|\Delta_{k+1}^{\pm}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}}.

More importantly, the induction step also changes and we obtain estimates

\|\tilde{u}_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{T}}}\leq C_{h}|\lambda_{1}(D_{P}-a)|^{-1}\|\lambda|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-au_{k}+g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{T}}},

adding a term of
\|au_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq|a|\Xi
. Carrying out these calculations, the new conditions on our parameters are given by

\|f\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}\leq\Xi,\quad\|f\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}\leq\Lambda

\big|\lambda_{1}(D_{P}-a)\big|^{-1}<1-\epsilon

\displaystyle\frac{C_{h}}{|\lambda_{1}(D_{P}-a)|}\bigg(|\lambda|c_{h}^{-\frac{n+1}{n-1}}K_{GN}^{\frac{n+1}{n-1}}\Xi^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\Lambda^{\frac{n}{n-1}}+|a|\Xi+\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)+\|g\|_{L_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Xi,

\displaystyle c_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1+|a|}{|\lambda_{1}(D_{P}-a)|}\right)\bigg(|\lambda|c_{h}^{-\frac{n+1}{n-1}}K_{GN}^{\frac{n+1}{n-1}}\Xi^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\Lambda^{\frac{n}{n-1}}+a\Xi+\|Dg\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)+\|g\|_{H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda,

\displaystyle\kappa\Xi^{\frac{2(1-\theta_{A})}{n-1}}\Lambda^{\frac{2\theta_{A}}{n-1}}|\lambda|\big|\lambda_{1}(D_{P}-a)\big|^{-(1-\theta_{B})}+|a||\lambda_{1}(D_{P}-a)|^{-1}

\displaystyle<\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2}},

with some
\epsilon>0
and
\kappa=2(p-1)c_{h}^{-\frac{2}{n-1}-2}C_{h}^{2(1-\theta_{B})}c_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\theta_{B}}K_{GN2}^{\frac{2}{n-1}}K_{FGN}^{2}
.

Under these assumptions, we get a Cauchy sequence and the two expressions
-au_{k+1}
and
-au_{k}
in the iterative scheme cancel in the limit, giving us a solution to the original problem.

Including
a
and
f
in our iterative scheme gains a lot of flexibility, as we could potentially get different new solutions by choosing other parameters
f
and
a
:

(i)

Not only can we get new solutions as we just hinted, we can achieve every possible solution
u
of the boundary value problem
Du=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u,\,Pu=Pg
as the limit of such an iterative scheme: Taking
a\not\in\operatorname{spec}(D_{P})
arbitrary (if
D_{P}
is not invertible, we hence require
a\not=0
) and
f:=u
, we get in the first step
u_{0}=f=u
. The higher iterations are the the unique solutions of

\displaystyle Du_{k+1}-au_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}-au_{k}\,\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle Pu_{k+1}

\displaystyle=Pg\,\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Turning
u_{k+1}
into
\tilde{u}_{k+1}=u_{k+1}-g
and assume the induction hypothesis
u_{k}=u
which holds for
k=0
, we have

\displaystyle D\tilde{u}_{k+1}-a\tilde{u}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u-au-Dg-ag\,\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle P\tilde{u}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=0\,\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

meaning with
\tilde{u}=u-g

\displaystyle D\tilde{u}_{k+1}-a\tilde{u}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=D\tilde{u}-a\tilde{u}\,\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle P\tilde{u}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=0\,\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

and thus the unique solution
\tilde{u}_{k+1}
is
(D_{P}-a)^{-1}((D-a)\tilde{u})=\tilde{u}
, meaning
u_{k+1}=u
needs to be the unique solution to our iteration step. This sequence is hence constant and therefore trivially convergent to the solution
u
of our boundary value problem. Our iterative scheme can hence yield every possible solution
u
by correctly choosing
a
and
f
.

(ii)

The freedom to have
a
not be zero is interesting. In fact, if
D_{P}
is not invertible, then at least
D_{P}-a
will be invertible for
a\not\in\spec(D_{P})
since our spectrum was discrete thanks to our assumptions on
(D,P)
. We may thus apply the above iterative scheme and hope to find a solution to our boundary value problem. Unfortunately, the terms involving
|\lambda_{1}(D_{P}-a)|^{-1}
or
a
cannot be properly bounded, which creates difficulties when trying to apply the theorem based solely on checking the parameter conditions. Nevertheless, in some cases, one does not need to worry about these complicated convergence conditions. For example, as discussed in remark
(\text{i})
above, no such conditions are necessary: the mere possibility of choosing
a\notin\spec(D_{P})
already suffices to construct a solution to the partial differential equation via our special iterative scheme.

(5)

If
Pg\equiv 0
, then our theorem could a priori be useless, as we cannot be sure whether our iteration scheme converged to the trivial solution
u\equiv 0
. In contrast, if
Pg\not\equiv 0
, we can be sure that
u\not\equiv 0
since otherwise
Pu=0\not=Pg
. Unfortunately, the case of
Pg\equiv 0
does not seem to admit an easy argument to show that the limit
u
of the iterative scheme is nontrivial. Such a result would be of interest, as the boundary value
Pg\equiv 0
arises in the standard spinorial Yamabe equation which is equivalent to the Euler-Lagrange equation for the conformal invariant
\lambda_{\text{min}}^{\pm}(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
.

One could hope that different choices of
a
and
f
could lead to different solutions from the trivial one. Furthermore, a uniform lower bound on the norm of
u_{k}
could prove non triviality of the limit, but the iterative scheme does not allow an easy uniform lower bound since the eigenvalues of
D_{P}^{-1}
accumulate at
0
(same for
(D_{P}-a)^{-1}
and
a\not\in\spec(D_{P})
).

(6)

If the manifold doesn’t have a boundary and therefore no boundary operator, then we can look at the iterative scheme
u_{0}=g
,
Du_{k+1}=\lambda|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}
. If
D
is regular, self-adjoint and invertible as an unbounded operator
L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)
and the parameters are small, this scheme converges, but we can not yield any clear results either, as there is no easy way to tell if our limiting solution is already the trivial zero function.

5.
Regularity via Bootstrapping and A Priori Estimates

Our goal is to employ a bootstrapping argument in order to deduce higher regularity for solutions
u\in H_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
to the equation

Du=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u,

with the boundary condition
Pu=Pg
. To successfully carry out this procedure, we require a suitable
a priori
estimate on
u
.

Such an
a priori
estimate always holds in the interior of the domain (see the upcoming remark
5.2
). However, if we wish to achieve higher regularity on the whole compact set
\Omega
including its boundary, we need this estimate to be valid up to the boundary. This is, in general, only possible for a suitable class of boundary operators
P
, as we will soon specify.

More precisely, we require that for every
q\in(1,\infty)
there exists a constant
c_{q}>0
such that the
a priori
estimate

(3)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{W^{1,q}}^{2}\leq c_{q}\Big(\|\psi\|_{L^{q}}^{2}+\|D\psi\|_{L^{q}}^{2}\Big)

holds for all
\psi\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
with
D\psi\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
satisfying
P\psi=0
. Here the boundary operator is defined by

P\colon W^{1,q}(\Sigma\Omega)\to W^{1-1/q,q}\big({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}}\big).

A crucial sufficient condition for the validity of (
3
) is that the pair
(D,P)
defines an elliptic boundary value problem in the sense of Shapiro–Lopatinski (in particular,
P
needs to be local). Namely if this ellipticity condition is satisfied, then (
3
) holds for all
q\in(1,\infty)
(see
[
Sch95
, Thm. 1.6.2]
).

Theorem 5.1

(Regularity)
.

Let
(D,P)
be elliptic in the sense of Shapiro-Lopatinski, then any solution
u\in H^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
of
Du=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u
,
Pu=Pg
with
p=\frac{2n}{n-1}
and
g\in W^{1,\infty}
lies in
C^{1,\alpha}(\Sigma\Omega)
and is smooth on the complement of
u^{-1}(\{0\})
.

Remark 5.2
.

If one does not have the
a priori
estimate for a specific boundary condition (we assumed Shapiro-Lopatinski to get it), we can still use the interior version of the estimate, which does not need any requirements on the boundary condition. In
[
Amm03b
, Thm. 3.2.1]
, the author shows for compact
K\subset\text{int}(\Omega)
, that

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{W^{1,q}(\Sigma K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c(K,\Omega,p)\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}+\|D\psi\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)}^{2}\right)

for all
\psi\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
with
D\psi\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
, assuming
\Omega
is simply connected. This suffices to show that the solution
u
lies in
W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma K)
and has a
C^{1,\alpha}(\Sigma K)
representative that is smooth on the complement of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptu\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{K}}^{-1}(0)
.

Proof:

We want to prove higher regularity of our solution
u\in H^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)
:

Recall that the inclusion
L^{s}(\Sigma\Omega)\subset L^{t}(\Sigma\Omega)
is bounded for all
s>t
using Hölder’s inequality
\|f\|_{L^{t}}\leq\operatorname{vol}_{h}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{s}}\|f\|_{L^{s}}
and the compactness of
\Omega
. This further implies a bounded inclusion
W^{1,s}(\Sigma\Omega)\subset W^{1,t}(\Sigma\Omega)
of Sobolev spaces for all
s>t
.

Let
r=1<n
, and remark that
u\in H^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)\subset W^{1,r}(\Sigma\Omega)
by the above. Then we may apply the Sobolev embedding theorem (Thm. (
2.6
) (i)) to see that
u\in L^{l}(\Sigma\Omega)
, where
l
is the solution of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l}=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{n},

namely
l=\frac{rn}{n-r}=\frac{n}{n-1}
.

Since
L^{l}=L^{l}
, we get
u\in L^{l}(\Sigma\Omega)
.
Our partial differential equation then implies
Du\in L^{\frac{l}{p-1}}(\Sigma\Omega)=L^{r^{\prime}}(\Sigma\Omega)
by setting
r^{\prime}:=\frac{l}{p-1}
. We hence have
u\in L^{r^{\prime}}
by the inclusion property from above and
r^{\prime}<l
.

Since we assumed
(D,P)
to be elliptic in the sense of Shapiro-Lopatinski, we may apply the
L^{r^{\prime}}
-
a priori
estimate (
3
) to
u-g\in\dom(D_{P})

\displaystyle\|u-g\|_{W^{1,r^{\prime}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{r^{\prime}}\left(\|u-g\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}^{2}+\|Du-Dg\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}^{2}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle\leq c_{r^{\prime}}\left((\|u\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}+\|g\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}})^{2}+(\|Du\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}+\|Dg\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}})^{2}\right).

Since
g\in W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma\Omega)\subset W^{1,r^{\prime}}(\Sigma\Omega)
, we get
Dg\in L^{r^{\prime}}
by (
11
). This means that every term on the right-hand side of

\|u\|_{W^{1,r^{\prime}}}\leq\|g\|_{W^{1,r^{\prime}}}+\sqrt{c_{r^{\prime}}}\left((\|u\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}+\|g\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}})^{2}+(\|Du\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}+\|Dg\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

has an upper bound, implying
u\in W^{1,r^{\prime}}(\Sigma\Omega)
.

Assume
r^{\prime}<n
, then
u\in L^{l^{\prime}}
with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{p-1}{l}-\frac{1}{n}.

By the partial differential equation and the norm equivalence, we get
Du\in L^{\frac{l^{\prime}}{p-1}}(\Sigma\Omega)=L^{r^{\prime\prime}}(\Sigma\Omega)
by setting
r^{\prime\prime}:=\frac{l^{\prime}}{p-1}
.
We can characterize
l^{\prime\prime}
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{\prime\prime}}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{p-1}{l^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{l}-\frac{1+(p-1)}{n}.

We get by the
L^{r^{\prime\prime}}
-
a priori
estimate (
3
) and the regularity of
g\in W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma\Omega)\subset W^{1,r^{\prime\prime}}(\Sigma\Omega)
just like in (
4
), that
u\in W^{1,r^{\prime\prime}}(\Sigma\Omega)
.

The
M
-th step of this process assumes
Du\in L^{r^{(M-1)}}(\Sigma\Omega)
and
u\in L^{l^{(M-1)}}(\Sigma\Omega)
with
r^{(M-1)}<n
. The partial differential equation and the norm equivalence imply
Du\in L^{r^{(M)}}(\Sigma\Omega)
with
r^{(M)}:=\frac{l^{(M-1)}}{p-1}
, resulting in the regularity
u\in W^{1,r^{(M)}}(\Sigma\Omega)
by reasoning as in (
4
) through the
L^{r^{(M)}}
-
a priori
estimate (
3
) and the regularity of
g\in W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma\Omega)\subset W^{1,r^{(M)}}(\Sigma\Omega)
. The Sobolev embedding then implies, that
u\in L^{l^{(M)}}(\Sigma\Omega)
with

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l^{(M)}}=\frac{1}{r^{(M)}}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{p-1}{l^{(M-1)}}-\frac{1}{n}.

This recursional equation for
\frac{1}{l^{(M)}}
can explicitly be calculated by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l^{(M)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)^{M}}{l}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(p-1)^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)^{M}}{l}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{(p-1)-(p-1)^{M}}{1-(p-1)}

(6)

\displaystyle=(p-1)^{M}\bigg(\frac{1}{l}-\frac{1}{n(p-2)}\bigg)+\frac{1}{n}\frac{(p-1)}{p-2}.

Since
\frac{1}{l}-\frac{1}{n(p-2)}=\frac{n-2}{2n}-\frac{n-1}{2n}=-\frac{1}{n}<0
, the last line (
6
) would become negative for some sufficiently large
M
(and let
M
be the smallest such integer), which means by (
5
) that the assumption
r^{(M-1)}<n
did not hold any longer. We can still conclude that
Du\in L^{r^{(M-1)}}(\Sigma\Omega)
with
r^{(M-1)}\geq n
. This implies that we already have
Du\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
for all
q\leq n
.

We now aim to improve the regularity further. By doing one iteration of the Sobolev embedding theorem with
\hat{r}=n-\epsilon
for arbitrarily small
\epsilon
, we now get
u\in L^{\hat{l}}(\Sigma\Omega)
for arbitrarily large
\hat{l}:=\frac{\hat{r}n}{n-\hat{r}}
. By our partial differential equation, this then implies
Du\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
for
q:=\frac{\hat{l}}{p-1}
. With
Du\in L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
for arbitrarily large
q\geq 1
, the
a priori
estimate (
3
) and the regularity of
g\in W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma\Omega)\subset W^{1,q}(\Sigma\Omega)
imply
u\in W^{1,q}(\Sigma\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
. By Morrey’s inequality (Thm. (
2.6
) (ii)), this implies
u\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Sigma\Omega)
for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Furthermore, this implies the existence of a classical trace
{\left.\kern-1.2ptu\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}}\in C^{0,\alpha}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\Sigma\Omega\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial\Omega}})
.
By Schauder estimates for Dirac operators
[
Amm03b
, Cor. 3.1.14]
, the
C^{0,\alpha}(\Sigma\Omega)
regularity of
u
in the equation
Du=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u
implies
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\Sigma\Omega)
for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Since
v\mapsto|v|^{p-2}v
is smooth on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}\setminus\{0\}
, iterating higher Schauder estimates
[
Amm03b
, Thm. 3.1.16]
away from the closed set
u^{-1}(\{0\})
yields smoothness of
u
. The regularity on
u^{-1}(\{0\})
is a priori only
C^{1,\alpha}(\Sigma\Omega)
and can not be improved by Schauder estimates.

∎

6.
Appendix: The Euler-Lagrange Equation

We will show that the Euler-Lagrange equation defining
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
is equivalent to the spinorial Yamabe equation (
2
).

The Variational Characterization

The reason why specifically the chiral bag boundary conditions
B^{\pm}
appear in the definition of the conformal invariant
\lambda_{\text{min}}^{\pm}
is not only because the Dirac operator becomes self-adjoint under the corresponding elliptic boundary condition, but mainly because chirality operators behave nicely under conformal change of metric
h

[
Rau09a
]
.
Using theses statements, S. Raulot found the variational characterization

\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega):=\inf_{h^{\prime}\in[h]}|\lambda_{1}(D_{B}^{h^{\prime}})|\,\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega,h^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{n}}=\inf_{\varphi\in C\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}|D\varphi|^{q}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}{\left|\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Re}\langle D\varphi,\varphi\rangle\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h}\right|},

with
q=\frac{2n}{n+1}
, where
C
denotes the orthogonal complement of
\ker(D_{B})\subset\dom(D_{B}):=\{\psi\in H^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)\;|\;B\psi=0\}
.
For simplicity, we assume that
0\notin\spec(D_{B})
, so that
C=\dom(D_{B})
.

Taking the argument of the infimum on the right-hand side as our new functional
\mathcal{F}\in L^{p}(\dom(D_{B})\setminus\{0\},\mathbb{R})
, we observe that it is Fréchet differentiable, and the vanishing of its derivative is equivalent—using the shorthand

A[\varphi]=2\left(\int_{\Omega}|D\varphi|^{q}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h}\right)^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Re}\langle D\varphi,\varphi\rangle\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h},

to the condition that for all
\psi\in\dom(D_{B})
,

\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Re}\left\langle A[\varphi]|D\varphi(x)|^{q-2}D\varphi(x)-2\varphi(x),\,D\psi(x)\right\rangle\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h}(x)=0.

Using the self-adjointness of
D_{B}

[
FS98
]
,

\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Re}\bigg\langle D\bigg(A[\varphi]|D\varphi(x)|^{q-2}D\varphi(x)-2\varphi(x)\bigg),\,\psi(x)\bigg\rangle\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h}(x)=0.

for all
\psi\in\dom(D_{B})
.
Using that
\dom(D_{B})\subset L^{2}(\Sigma\Omega)
is dense, the Euler–Lagrange equation hence becomes

D\left(\frac{1}{2}A[\varphi]|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)=D\varphi,\quad D\varphi\in L^{p}(\Sigma\Omega).

Since the functional
\mathcal{F}
is positively
0
-homogeneous, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}(r\psi)=\mathcal{F}(\psi)\quad\text{for all }r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

we can restrict our analysis to functions
\varphi\in\dom(D_{B})
satisfying a normalization condition. A natural choice is to fix the
L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)
-norm of
D\varphi
, say

\|D\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)}=1.

In this case, the expression
\tfrac{1}{2}A[\varphi]
in the Euler-Lagrange equation can be interpreted by a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
enforcing the constraint. We thus obtain the Euler–Lagrange equation

D(|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi)=\lambda D\varphi,\quad\|D\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma\Omega)}=1,\quad B\varphi=0.

This Euler–Lagrange equation is equivalent to the spinorial Yamabe equation (
2
) in the following way:

Given a solution
\varphi
of (
6
), define the transformed spinor

\Psi:=|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi.

Then (
6
) becomes

D\Psi=\lambda|\Psi|^{p-2}\Psi,\quad\|\Psi\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma\Omega)}=1,\quad B\Psi=0,

with
p=\frac{2n}{n-1}
. The main subtlety lies in confirming the boundary condition:
The Dirac operator with domain
\dom(D_{B}):=\{\psi\in H^{1}(\Sigma\Omega)\;|\;B\psi=0\}
is self-adjoint since we are using a chiral bag boundary condition. Since
\varphi\in\dom(D_{B})
solves the Euler–Lagrange equation (
6
) in the weak sense, we have

\left\langle D\left(|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\right),\eta\right\rangle=\lambda\langle D\varphi,\eta\rangle.

With
\Psi=|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi
, this becomes by self-adjointness and since
\varphi\in\dom(D_{B})

\langle D\Psi,\eta\rangle=\lambda\langle D\varphi,\eta\rangle=\lambda\langle\varphi,D\eta\rangle,

for all
\eta\in\dom(D_{B})
. In particular,
\Psi\in\dom(D_{B}^{*})
, the domain of the adjoint of
D_{B}
. Since
D_{B}
is self-adjoint, this means that
\Psi\in\dom(D_{B})
and we are done.

Conversely, starting with a solution
\Psi
of (
6
), the function
\varphi:=\Psi
solves (
6
). The two transformations we used to transfer solutions from one to the other equation are not inverses of each other, but they show how to translate the one equation into the other one. Most importantly, we can now see why our spinorial Yamabe equation with boundary is relevant: It is equivalent to the Euler-Lagrange equation for the variational characterization of the conformal invariant
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
introduced by Raulot
[
Rau09a
]
.
For an arbitrary local boundary operator
P
with self-adjoint
D_{P}
, the same proof about the Euler-Lagrange equation and the spinorial Yamabe equation holds, as long as one chooses to define
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
by the variational characterization

\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\Omega,\partial\Omega):=\inf_{\varphi\in C\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}|D\varphi|^{q}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}{\left|\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Re}\langle D\varphi,\varphi\rangle\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h}\right|}.

MiR-19a-3p: a potential biomarker for predicting cardiac risk in anderson-fabry disease
Authors: Yiming Zhang, Hongqiang Yang, Shuping Wang, Chengcheng Yi, Yongxiang Wang, Ming Bai, Zheng Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMicroRNAs(miRNAs) have been shown to suppress the onset and progression of heart disease and have recently been widely proposed as biomarkers. However, few literatures have been reported on whether they protect against Anderson-Fabry-induced myocardial damage.ObjectiveThe aim was to explore whether miR-19a-3p could be used as a biomarker for the diagnosis and risk assessment of Anderson-Fabry disease (AFD), distinguishing AFD from healthy controls and myocardial damage caused by AFD.Methods60 AFD patients and an equal number of healthy controls were recruited to analyze miR-19a-3p expression. ROC analysis evaluated their diagnostic ability, and statistical analysis demonstrated the association of clinical indicators of cardiac damage with miR-19a-3p.Results53.3% of AFD patients exhibited cardiac damage, with notable structural changes in cardiac characteristics. MiR-19a-3p expression level was downregulated in AFD and AFD cardiomyopathy patients and showed high diagnostic accuracy for identifying both AFD and associated cardiac injury. It negatively correlated with severity of cardiac injury. Multivariate analysis identified miR-19a-3p as a significant independent protective factor in cardiac damage in AFD.ConclusionThe study identified downregulated miR-19a-3p as a potent diagnostic biomarker for AFD and its associated cardiomyopathy. MiR-19a-3p was established as an independent protective factor in cardiac damage. Thus, it was proposed as a promising candidate biomarker for AFD cardiomyopathy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04254-y.Keywords: MiR-19a-3p, AFD, Cardiac damage, Biomarker

Introduction

Anderson-Fabry disease (AFD) arises from mutations in the X-chromosomal GLA gene, which encodes α-galactosidase A (α-Gal A). The resulting enzyme deficiency or complete loss causes the accumulation of globotriaosylceramide (Gb3) and its derivative globotriaosylsphingosine (Lyso-Gb3) in various tissues, including the nerves, heart, kidneys, and skin, thereby triggering progressive multi-organ damage and life-threatening pathologyTuttolomondo, Pecoraro [1]. AFD was divided into classic type and late type according to clinical manifestations. Male patients are mostly classic type, and most of them start in childhood, with periodic severe pain in the extremities, vascular skin lesions [2]. Heart and kidney dysfunction, their complications and nervous system lesions usually occur in adulthood. Heart disease occurs in middle age, mainly manifested as left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), cardiomyopathy, heart failure, chest pain and arrhythmia [3], and its renal function gradually deteriorates to end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) [4]. Late onset is more common in women. Patients usually onset in adult age or later. The main manifestations include heart disease and central nervous system damage [5]. Literature reported that although the course of the late onset phenotype was milder, the incidence rate was 10 times higher than that of the classic type, and the degree of cardiac involvement was more serious [6]. Most of them manifested LVH, accompanied by heart failure, non-sustained ventricular tachycardia and cardiac conduction disorders [7]. And its incidence and severity increase with age [8, 9]. Heart disease continues to be the predominant cause of death in patients with AFD to this day [10].

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have recently been widely proposed as biomarkers that were involved in multiple physiological and pathological processes by regulating gene expression [11], such as cancer cardiovascular disease and renal dysfunction [12–14]. Most miRNAs have been shown to contribute to the development and progression of heart diseases [15–18]. In particular, several studies have reported dysregulated miRNA profiles in AFD, suggesting their potential as biomarkers for this condition [11] MiR-19a-3p has been shown to have tissue protective effects in disease models [19], such as myocardial hypertrophy, acute myocardial infarction, and myocardial ischemia [20–22]. However, to the best of our knowledge, whether it is independently associated with a lower risk of myocardial damage caused by AFD has not been directly reported. Moreover, the diagnosis rate of AFD is low, so early screening and timely diagnosis are extremely important for patients with AFD to delay disease progression and improve prognosis. Therefore, in this study, we investigated the relationship between circulating levels of miR-19a-3p, and myocardial damage caused by AFD, hoping to provide predictive value for clinical diagnosis of AFD.

Methods

Subjects

The healthy control group was retrospectively selected by 1:1 individual matching to the AFD patients based on age and sex. All healthy controls had no history of cardiovascular disease and underwent the same comprehensive transthoracic echocardiographic examination as the patients, showing no evidence of left ventricular hypertrophy (maximum wall thickness < 13 mm), preserved ejection fraction (≥ 50%), no significant valvular disease, and no regional wall motion abnormalities. In addition, controls exhibited normal α-Gal A enzyme activity (5.72±1.23 µmol/L/h). Exclusion criteria for controls included estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 60 mL/min/1.73 m², active malignancy, severe liver disease (Child‑Pugh B/C), or chronic inflammatory disease requiring immunosuppressive therapy. The First Hospital of Lanzhou University Ethics Committee endorsed the study protocol (No. LDYYLL2022-260), and informed consent was acquired from all participants. The research adhered to the principles set forth in the World Medical Association’s Declaration of Helsinki (2000).

Clinical data collection

This study focused on cardiac damage, which was characterized by left ventricular (LV) mass, ventricular volumes, and serum cardiac troponin I (cTnI). Key echocardiographic parameters assessed included interventricular septal thickness (IVS), left ventricular posterior wall thickness (PW), left ventricular mass index (LVMi), end-diastolic volume (EDV), and end-systolic volume (ESV). Subjects were classified with cardiac damage using the criterion of elevated left ventricular mass indexed to height2.7 (LVMh), with cut-offs of ≥ 50 g/m2.7 for men and ≥ 47 g/m2.7for women [23, 24]. All other echocardiographic parameters (including LVH defined as maximum wall thickness ≥ 13 mm, LAVi ≥ 34 mL/m², sPAP, EF, and cTnI levels) were used only for descriptive purposes and secondary analyses. They did not form part of the primary classification of cardiac damage. Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) was not performed in this study, and therefore fibrosis was not assessed. Additional echocardiographic standard were defined as follows: LVH was a maximum LV wall thickness ≥ 13 mm at end-diastole, excluding other causes of LVH; maximum wall thickness (MWT) was the greatest thickness from end-diastole and in short-axis views; LVMi was indexed to body surface area via the Devereux formula; systolic pulmonary arterial pressure (sPAP) was estimated by applying the modified Bernoulli equation to the peak tricuspid regurgitant jet velocity obtained with continuous-wave Doppler; ejection fraction (EF) was calculated by the modified Simpson’s biplane method; pulsed Doppler of mitral flow pool was used to extract early-to-late diastolic flow ratio and deceleration time [25]. Left atrial volume index for body surface area (LAVi ≥ 34 mL/m²) was obtained by the Simpson’s biplane method [26]. cTnI levels were measured using a high-sensitivity immunoassay.

Serum sample acquisition

5 mL blood samples were collected in vacuum tubes without anticoagulant. Prior to extracting RNA, we tested the serum samples to confirm the absence of hemolysis. We measured the optical density (OD) of 414 nm free blood hemoglobin to perform the test and excluded samples with an OD414 value greater than 0.2, as the presence of small RNA in the cells might compromise the experiment’s results. Whole blood was centrifuged at 3000×g for 10 min at 4 ℃ and serum was aliquoted into the enzyme-free cryovial. 1 mL serum for RNA extraction and the remaining serum samples were stored at -80 ℃ immediately. The total RNA extracted from the serum was determined for its concentration and purity using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer. The sample was considered qualified if the A260/A280 ratio was between 1.8 and 2.0 and the A260/A230 ratio was greater than 1.8. All plasma samples underwent no more than 2 freeze-thaw cycles to minimize miRNA degradation.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was separated from the collected 1 ml serum by Trizol Reagent, and then 500 ng total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit. RNA and cDNA were aliquoted immediately after quality inspection and stored at -80 ℃. MiR-19a-3p levels were relatively quantified by RT-qPCR with the miScript SYBR Green kit. MiR-19a-3p was amplified using its specific forward primer (5’-CTGGAGTGTGCAAATCTATGC-3’) and a universal reverse primer in kit, with U6 as the internal reference. The geNorm and NormFinder algorithms were used to assess the stability of the reference genes, and U6 had the lowest geNorm M value (M = 0.35), indicating that it is the most stable; furthermore, using U6 as a reference gene is similar to the approach used in the previous study of myocardial injury using microRNA biomarkers [20] The relative expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method and normalized to U6. The sample processing, RNA extraction and fluorescence quantitative PCR detection were all conducted under a blinded condition. The operators were unaware of the sample grouping information to minimize operational bias.

α-Gal A activity assay

The activity of α-galactosidase A (α-Gal A) was determined by a fluorescence assay using 4-methylumbelliferyl-α-D-galactopyranoside (4-MU-α-Gal) as the substrate: briefly, samples were incubated with the substrate in a citrate-phosphate buffer (pH 4.6–4.7) at 37 °C in the dark, the enzymatic hydrolysis reaction released 4-methylumbelliferone (4-MU), and the reaction was terminated by adding alkaline stop buffer; the fluorescence intensity was measured at an excitation wavelength of 360 nm and an emission wavelength of 445 nm, and the α-Gal A activity was calculated according to the standard curve constructed with 4-MU standards.

Statistical analysis

Categorical data were presented as proportions, while continuous variables were reported as mean ± SD. The normality of continuous variables was first examined using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and Levene’s test was applied to assess homogeneity of variances. Student’s t-test was used for between-group comparisons when the variables followed a normal distribution and exhibited homogeneous variances. Otherwise, the Mann-Whitney U test was adopted for variables that deviated from normality. Chi-square tests were used for categorical variables. In the validation cohort, the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was utilized to assess the ability of miR-19a-3p to not only distinguish patients with AFD from healthy control but also to identify the presence of cardiac injury among AFD patients. The 95% confidence interval (CI) of the AUC was estimated using the DeLong method. The optimal cut-off value for the biomarke model was determined using the Youden index (maximizing sensitivity + specificity – 1) We also used Pearson correlation and Spearman’s rank correlation analysis to evaluate the relationship between miR-19a-3p expression levels and indicators of myocardial injury. Collinearity diagnosis is performed using the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF), with a VIF > 5 serving as the threshold for severe multicollinearity. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify independent risk factors for AFD-induced cardiac injury. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 27.0) and GraphPad Prism (version 9.3).

Results

Clinical and echocardiographic profile of cardiac involvement in AFD

Table 1 summarized the clinical characteristics of the 60 AFD patients, among whom 32 (53.3%) were diagnosed with cardiac damage. Comparative analysis revealed that patients with cardiac damage were considerably older than those without (p = 0.004). However, no obvious differences were observed in gender, body surface area, BMI, blood pressure, and heart rate. Echocardiographic parameters demonstrated pronounced structural and functional alterations in the cardiac damage group. Key indicators of LVH, including interventricular septal thickness (IVS) and left ventricular posterior wall thickness (PW), were greater magnitude. Consequently, both left ventricular mass indices, LVMi and LVMh, were markedly elevated in patients with cardiac damage. This was accompanied by significantly larger cardiac volumes (EDV and ESV), increased LAVi, and higher systolic pulmonary arterial pressure (sPAP). Although ejection fraction tended to be higher in the cardiac damage group, the difference was not statistically significant. Furthermore, these structural alterations were associated with a marked rise in serum cTnI levels.

Potentiality of miR-19a-3p as an AFD biomarker

The RNA extracted from all serum samples met the quality control requirements. RT-qPCR analysis revealed a marked reduction in miR-19a-3p expression in AFD patients compared to healthy controls (Fig. 1A). ROC curve analysis affirmed its diagnostic utility for AFD. The analysis showed an AUC of 0.9092 (95% CI: 0.8591–0.9592, DeLong method). Using the Youden index, the optimal cut-off was determined as 0.7750, yielding a sensitivity of 83.33% and a specificity of 85.00% (Fig. 1B). We subsequently investigated its link to cardiac pathology. Cardiac involvement in AFD patients was associated with a statistical reduction in miR-19a-3p expression (Fig. 1C). The ROC analysis revealed an AUC of 0.906 3(95% CI: 0.7141–0.8768, DeLong method). Using the Youden index, the optimal cut-off was determined as 0.600, yielding a sensitivity of 84.38% and a specificity of 85.71% (Fig. 1D). Collectively, the data indicated that miR-19a-3p could be a powerful biomarker for both AFD and associated cardiac injury.

Negative correlation between miR-19a-3p and cardiac injury indicators

In AFD patients with myocardial injury, the correlation between miR-19a-3p and various myocardial injury parameters was analyzed. As summarized in Table 2, consistent with expectations, the expression of miR-19a-3p demonstrated a statistically significant inverse correlation with a spectrum of key structural and biochemical cardiac markers. Notably, the strongest negative correlations were observed with IVS (r = -0.752, p < 0.001), LVMi (r = -0.744, p < 0.001), and cardiac cTnI (r = -0.729, p < 0.001). Furthermore, the inverse relationship with miR-19a-3p extended to other remodeling parameters (PW, ESV, LVMh). Age didn’t affect miR-19a-3p expression, although aging was more prone to cardiac damage. In short, these results indicated that lower circulating levels of miR-19a-3p were closely associated with more severe cardiac involvement and structural remodeling in Fabry-induced cardiomyopathy.

MiR-19a-3p as an independent predictor of cardiac injury

The collinearity diagnosis results showed that the variance inflation factors of each variable in the regression model of AFD patients were all less than 5, suggesting the absence of severe multicollinearity (Supplementary Table 1). Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to assess whether miR-19a-3p was an independent risk factor for cardiac damage, alongside other clinical parameters including cardiac cTnI, IVS, age, and gender. The results summarized in Fig. 1E identified miR-19a-3p expression as a significant independent predictor, showing a strong negative association with cardiac injury (OR = 0.080, 95% CI: 0.017–0.376, p = 0.001, for low expression vs. high expression; detailed in Table 3). For clinical variables, odds ratios were scaled as follows: age (OR = 2.124, 95% CI: 0.481–9.376, p = 0.320, above-mean vs. below-mean), IVS (OR = 2.336, 95% CI: 0.484–11.273, p = 0.291, above-mean vs. below-mean), and cTnI (OR = 3.977, 95% CI: 0.874–18.102, p = 0.074, above-mean vs. below-mean). Gender (OR = 4.156, 95% CI: 0.961–17.975, p = 0.057, male vs. female). All p-values for age, sex, IVS, and cTnI exceeded 0.05, indicating no independent statistical associations between these variables and cardiac injury. This analysis underscores that miR-19a-3p is a powerful and independent protective factor against myocardial injury in AFD, outperforming established clinical indicators.

Discussion

AFD is a progressive multisystemic disease caused by the loss or inactivation of the lysosomal enzyme galactosidase A, which causes cells to fail to break down glycosphingolipids with terminal D-galactosyl residues. These deposits build up in nearly all organs over time, eventually giving rise to a progressive multisystem disease. In adulthood, most of the symptoms manifest as heart and kidney involvement, and myocardial involvement becomes more and more serious with age. Among them, Fabry cardiomyopathy starts late and usually develops after middle age. In recent years, there has been growing evidence that microRNAs played a key role in cardiovascular development diseases [19]. In functional studies of microRNAs in heart disease, global knockout of miR-17-92 gene cluster in mice led to lung hypoplasia, ventricular septal defects, and postnatal lethality [27]. Conversely, overexpression of miR-17-92 promoted cardiomyocyte proliferation in embryonic, neonatal, and adult mouse hearts, with miR-19 identified as essential for this proliferative effect in vitro [28]. Further research revealed that miR-19a/19b enhanced cardiomyocyte proliferation and promoted cardiac repair following injury. Adult mice overexpressing miR-17-92 exhibited improved cardiac function and reduced scar size. Intracardiac injection of miR-19a/19b mimics protected mice from myocardial infarction and stimulated cardiomyocyte proliferation by attenuating interactions with the pro-apoptotic protein Bcl-2. Additionally, another study demonstrated that miR-19a/19b protected cardiomyocytes from stress and suppressed cardiac hypertrophy through the regulation of pro-apoptotic and anti-hypertrophic genes [28–30]. MiR-19a-3p, an important constituent of the miR-17-92 gene cluster, has been functionally implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple cardiovascular conditions, including cardiac hypertrophy, heart failure, post-myocardial infarction remodeling, and vascular remodeling [21, 31–33], and our results also supported that miR-19a-3p could be an effective protective factor in the pathophysiological landscape of AFD.

The results showed that miR-19a-3p was significantly down-regulated in AFD patients, indicating its potential diagnostic utility with an AUC of 0.9092. More importantly, our research findings revealed stratification in the AFD cohort; patients with overt cardiac damage appear to exhibit even lower levels of miR-19a-3p compared to those without, a distinction captured by a second ROC curve. These implied that miR-19a-3p has dual functions of distinguishing patients from healthy individuals and identifying patients with cardiac involvement. In clinical examinations patients with confirmed cardiac injury were characterized by a significant increase in left ventricular mass, wall thickness, and ventricular volume, as well as biochemical evidence of injury (elevated cTnI). The expression of miR-19a-3p was closely related to the severity of heart injury, indicating that its downregulation was associated with more serious disease state. In study of myocardial hypertrophy, miR-19a-3p expression level was also found to change dynamically with pathological state [20]. Crucially, multivariate logistic regression analysis confirmed that miR-19a-3p was an independent predictor of cardiac injury, which was superior to established clinical indicators such as IVS, cTnI, age and gender. Low OR value meant strong independent protection; On the contrary, the higher level of miR-19a-3p was closely related to the reduction of the possibility of cardiac damage. Studies have shown that miR-19a-3p overexpression could alleviate cardiac hypertrophy [20]; in addition, the in vivo delivery of miR-19a-3p to a mouse model of myocardial infarction helped to better preserve the ejection fraction and maintain ventricular contractility [22]. Based on these findings, miR-19a-3p in serum was considered as a candidate for cardiac risk diagnosis of AFD.

A limitation of this study is its cross‑sectional design, which precludes causal inference. Thus, the inverse association between miR‑19a‑3p and cardiac injury (OR < 1) should not be directly interpreted as a protective effect, as reverse causality or unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded. Nevertheless, this is the first report linking miR‑19a‑3p to AFD‑relate potential biomarker ed cardiac injury, and its independent association suggests potential as an exploratory biomarker. Additionally, the modest number of cardiac injury events reduced model precision, though the direction of ORs for age, sex, IVS, and cTnI aligned with clinical knowledge. Our definition of cardiac damage relied solely on LVMh cutoffs, which may misclassify non‑hypertrophic involvement. Overall, these findings warrant validation in larger prospective cohorts.

In summary, our research results indicated that the utility of circulating miR-19a-3p extended beyond initial diagnosis and could be further applied to the stratification of cardiac involvement risk. It also showed potential to become a clinically meaningful biomarker for AFD in the future. Although the identification of such biomarkers requires comprehensive in vitro and in vivo validation, along with elucidation of the network mechanisms through which miR-19a-3p regulates cardiac damage. Our findings provided a certain data foundation for future mechanistic research and offered new insights into the diagnosis and treatment of Fabry cardiomyopathy.

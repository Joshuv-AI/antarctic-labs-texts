Nonlinear asymptotic stability of 2D Taylor-Couette flow in the exterior disk
Te Li, Ping Zhang, Yibin Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the asymptotic stability of the 2D Taylor-Couette flow in the exterior disk, with a small kinematic viscosity ν ≪ 1 \nu\ll 1 and a large rotation coefficient | B | |B| . Due to the degeneracy of the Taylor-Couette flow at infinity, we cannot expect the solution to decay exponentially in a space-time decoupled manner. As stated in previous work [ 20 ] , even space-time coupled exponential decay can not be expected, and at most, we can obtain space-time coupled polynomial decay. To handle the space-time coupled decay multiplier, the previous time-independent resolvent estimate methods no longer work. Therefore, this paper introduces time-dependent resolvent estimates to deal with the space-time coupled decay multiplier Λ k \Lambda_{k} . We remark that the choice of Λ k \Lambda_{k} is not unique, here we just provide one way to construct it. Finally, as an application, we derive a transition threshold bound of 1 2 \frac{1}{2} , which is the same as that for the Taylor-Couette flow in the bounded region.



1.
Introduction

We consider 2D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in the exterior disk
\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\bigl\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ |x|\geq 1\bigr\}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}V-\nu\Delta V+V\cdot\nabla V+\nabla P=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega,\\
&\text{div }V=0,\\
&V(0,x)=V_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

where
V\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the fluid velocity and
P
the scalar pressure function, which guarantees
the divergence free condition of the velocity field,
\nu
designates the kinematic viscosity of the fluid.

In this paper, we study the stability of a two-dimensional rotating flow in the exterior region
\Omega
, which is a steady solution to (
1.1
). The corresponding velocity, vorticity, and scalar pressure are given as follows:

\displaystyle V^{\star}(r,\theta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\sin\theta\\
\cos\theta\end{array}\right)\Big(Ar+\frac{B}{r}\Big){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v^{\star,1}\\
v^{\star,2}\end{array}\right)},\quad W^{\star}(r){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\partial_{1}v^{\star,2}-\partial_{2}v^{\star,1}=2A,

(1.2e)

\displaystyle P^{\star}(r,\theta)=P^{\star}(r)\quad\text{with}\quad\partial_{r}P^{\star}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\Big(Ar+\frac{B}{r}\Big)^{2},

where
A,B
are two constants and we assume that
\nu\ll|B|
throughout this paper. The steady flow of the form (
1.2
) is well-known as Taylor-Couette flow.

As a classic example of rotating flow, Taylor-Couette (TC) flow has been extensively studied in both the physical and mathematical contexts. In physical experiments, TC flow always refers to the fluid flow between two concentric cylinders, where the inner cylinder rotates while the outer cylinder can be either stationary or rotating. This flow configuration was first studied by G. I. Taylor in the 1920s
[
25
]
. Despite its simple structure, TC flow exhibits complex and rich stability properties, and has become a classic problem in fluid dynamics due to its rich flow patterns, as referenced in such review books as
[
12
,
10
,
23
,
24
,
18
]
. These corresponding flow patterns are controlled by the Reynolds number and the radius ratio of the cylinders. For example, the TC apparatus is also an excellent prototype for studying the transport properties of most astrophysical or geophysical rotating shear flows
[
14
]
. Depending on the rotational speed of each cylinder, different flow states can be achieved, corresponding to increasing or decreasing angular velocity and angular momentum. Likewise, regarding the issue of asymptotic stability at high Reynolds numbers
[
6
]
, TC flow, as a typical rotating shear flow, also exhibits rich decay properties.

This paper primarily investigates the fully nonlinear long-time asymptotic stability behavior near the TC flow in the exterior disk. Earlier, the well-posedness and stability of the two-dimensional stationary Navier-Stokes equations in exterior regions
[
5
,
16
]
were widely studied and discussed. The following results are related to the fully nonlinear stability of the time-dependent Navier-Stokes equations near the 2D TC flow.

•

Under the condition
|B|\ll 1
and with the domain being the exterior disk,
[
21
,
22
]
used perturbation theory to study the
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimate for the semigroup generated by the linearized operator, and further addressed the global well-posedness and nonlinear asymptotic stability for small initial data in the
L^{2}
space.

•

In
[
2
]
, nonlinear enhanced dissipation and transition thresholds near the TC flow were obtained in the bounded annular region
(r,\theta)\in[1,R]\times\mathbb{T}
. Since the domain is compact, the shear effect of the TC flow is non-degenerate, i.e.,
(A+\frac{B}{r^{2}})^{\prime}
is non-vanishing.

In the case when
\nu
is sufficiently small and
|B|
is large enough, that is, when the Reynolds number is high and the rotational effects are strong, the fully nonlinear long-time asymptotic stability problem of the 2D TC flow in the exterior disk is both interesting and challenging.

1.1.
Derivation of the perturbation equation

Let
(V^{\star},P^{\star})
be determined by (
1.2
). We denote
U{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}V-V^{\star}
,
p{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}P-P^{\star}
, and consider the following perturbation equations of
the system (
1.1
) near the 2D TC flow:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}U-\nu\Delta U+V^{\star}\cdot\nabla U+U\cdot\nabla V^{\star}+U\cdot\nabla U+\nabla p=0,\\
&\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits U=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega,\\
&U(t=0)=U(0).\end{aligned}\right.

We shall present a local well-posedness result concerning the system (
1.3
)
in Appendix
A
.

Since this paper aims at investigating the impact of degeneracy, which is caused by the external region,
on the nonlinear stability of TC flow, we impose the impermeable and Navier-slip boundary conditions
for the velocity field:

(1.4)

U\cdot\mathbf{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\bigl(\partial_{1}U^{2}-\partial_{2}U^{1}\bigr)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Let
W\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits U{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\partial_{1}U^{2}-\partial_{2}U^{1}
be the vorticity of
U
, and we rewrite (
1.3
-
1.4
) in the equivalent vorticity-stream formulation:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}W-\nu\Delta W+V^{\star}\cdot\nabla W+U\cdot\nabla W=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega,\\
&\Delta\varPhi=W,\quad U=\nabla^{\perp}\varPhi{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\begin{pmatrix}-\partial_{2}\varPhi,\partial_{1}\varPhi\end{pmatrix}^{T},\\
&W|_{\partial\Omega}=\varPhi|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad W(t=0)=W(0).\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\mathbf{e_{r}}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
and
\mathbf{e_{\theta}}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta).

In polar coordinates, (
1.5
) is equivalent to

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}W-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\Big)W+\Big(A+\frac{B}{r^{2}}\Big)\partial_{\theta}W+\frac{1}{r}(\partial_{r}\varPhi\partial_{\theta}W-\partial_{\theta}\varPhi\partial_{r}W)=0,\\
&\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\Big)\varPhi=W,\quad(t,r,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{T},\\
&U=U^{r}\mathbf{e_{r}}+U^{\theta}\mathbf{e_{\theta}}\quad\hbox{with}\quad U^{r}=-\frac{\partial_{\theta}\varPhi}{r}\quad\hbox{and}\quad U^{\theta}=\partial_{r}\varPhi,\\
&W(t=0)=W(0).\end{aligned}\right.

By expanding the functions
(W,\varPhi,U^{r},U^{\theta})
in Fourier series, we write

(1.7)

\begin{split}&W(t,r,\theta)=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ik\theta}\widehat{W}_{k}(t,r),\quad\varPhi(t,r,\theta)=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ik\theta}\widehat{\varPhi}_{k}(t,r),\quad\\
&U^{r}(t,r,\theta)=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ik\theta}\widehat{U}^{r}_{k}(t,r),\quad U^{\theta}(t,r,\theta)=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ik\theta}\widehat{U}^{\theta}_{k}(t,r).\end{split}

Then by substituting (
1.7
) into (
1.6
) and comparing the Fourier coefficients of the resulting equations, we obtain

(1.8)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\widehat{W}_{k}-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)\widehat{W}_{k}+\Big(A+\frac{B}{r^{2}}\Big)ik\widehat{W}_{k}\\
&\qquad+\frac{i}{r}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\big((k-\ell)\partial_{r}\widehat{\varPhi}_{\ell}\widehat{W}_{k-\ell}-\ell\widehat{\varPhi}_{\ell}\partial_{r}\widehat{W}_{k-\ell}\big)=0,\quad(t,r,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}\\
&\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)\widehat{\varPhi}_{k}=\widehat{W}_{k},\quad\widehat{U}^{r}_{k}=-\frac{ik}{r}\widehat{\varPhi}_{k},\quad\widehat{U}^{\theta}_{k}=\partial_{r}\widehat{\varPhi}_{k},\\
&\widehat{W}_{k}(t=0)=\widehat{W}_{k}(0).\end{aligned}\right.

To get rid of the terms involving
\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}
and
A
in (
1.8
), we introduce

(1.9)

\displaystyle w_{k}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{ikAt}\widehat{W}_{k},\quad\varphi_{k}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{ikAt}\widehat{\varPhi}_{k},\quad u^{r}_{k}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{ikAt}\widehat{U}^{r}_{k},\quad u^{\theta}_{k}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{ikAt}\widehat{U}^{\theta}_{k}.

It is easy to observe that

\displaystyle\|W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\approx\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\widehat{W}_{k}\|_{L^{2}((1,\infty);rdr)}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}((1,\infty);dr)}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\approx\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\partial_{r}\widehat{W}_{k}\|_{L^{2}((1,\infty);rdr)}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{k})\|_{L^{2}((1,\infty);dr)}^{2},

which will be frequently used throughout this paper. Moreover, we deduce from (
1.8
) that

(1.10a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{0}+\mathcal{T}_{0}w_{0}=r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}i\ell\partial_{r}\big(r^{-1}\varphi_{\ell}w_{-\ell}\big),\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times[1,+\infty),

(1.10b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{k}+\mathcal{T}_{k}w_{k}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}i\bigl[r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ell\partial_{r}\big(r^{-1}\varphi_{\ell}w_{k-\ell}\big)-kr^{-1}\partial_{r}(r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{\ell})w_{k-\ell}\bigr]-ikr^{-\frac{3}{2}}u^{\theta}_{0}w_{k},

(1.10c)

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)\varphi_{k}=w_{k},

(1.10d)

\displaystyle u^{r}_{k}=-i\frac{k}{r}\varphi_{k},\quad u^{\theta}_{k}=r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{k})=\partial_{r}\varphi_{k}-\frac{1}{2r}\varphi_{k},

(1.10e)

\displaystyle w_{k}|_{r=1}=\varphi_{k}|_{r=1}=0,\qquad\textrm{for any}\ k\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is defined as

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)+i\frac{kB}{r^{2}},\quad D(\mathcal{T}_{k}){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}H^{2}\bigl((1,\infty);dr\bigr)\cap H^{1}_{0}\bigl((1,\infty);dr\bigr).

So far, we have transformed the original problem into exploring the dynamic and long-time behavior of the system (
1.10
).

1.2.
Key ideas

It’s well known that, for transport-diffusion equations, the enhanced dissipation scale is closely related to the degree of degeneracy of the velocity field, see for instance
[
1
,
4
]
. However, as pointed out in
[
20
]
, due to the infinite degeneracy of TC flow in
the exterior disk (i.e.,
\partial_{r}^{n}(r^{-2})
vanishes at infinity for any
n\geq 0
), the previous results are no longer applicable to
e^{-\mathcal{T}_{k}t}
, which motivates us to investigate the mapping properties of
e^{-\mathcal{T}_{k}t}
in some space-time weighted spaces in
[
20
]
. In particular, we observe from Theorem 1.2 of
[
20
]
that due to the degeneracy at infinity, the non-homogeneous equation (
1.10b
) cannot exhibit exponential decay. Specifically,

Lemma 1.1

(Theorem 1.2,
[
20
]
)
.

For any positive continuous functions
a_{1}(r)
,
a_{2}(r)
and strictly decreasing function
\phi(r)
, there exists a sequence of functions,

\{(w_{n},f_{n})\}
, that solves

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{n}+\mathcal{T}_{k}w_{n}=f_{n},\quad w_{n}|_{t=0}=0,\quad w_{n}|_{r=1,\infty}=0,

and satisfies for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{1}(r)w_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};{L^{2}_{r}})}+\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{2}(r)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};{L^{2}_{r}})}<+\infty.

However, there holds

\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{1}(r)w_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};{L^{2}_{r}})}}{\|e^{t\phi(r)}a_{2}(r)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};{L^{2}_{r}})}}=+\infty.

Lemma
1.1
indicates that even if employing space-time coupling to mitigate the adverse effects of degeneracy at infinity in the Taylor-Couette flow, one still cannot obtain exponential nonlinear enhanced dissipation decay as in the Couette flow. Consequently, for the long-time behavior near the Taylor-Couette flow in the exterior region, even with space-time coupling, at most polynomial nonlinear decay can be expected.

In fact, Theorem 1.1 of
[
20
]
establishes that the solution to the linear homogeneous equation exhibits polynomial space-time coupled decay.
Precisely:

Lemma 1.2

(Theorem 1.1,
[
20
]
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
\frac{\nu}{|kB|}\ll(1+q)^{-3}
and let
w
be determined by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+\mathcal{T}_{k}w=0,\quad w|_{t=0}=w(0),\quad w|_{r=1,\infty}=0.

There exists constant
C=C(q)>0
independent of
\nu,k,B
and
w_{k}(0)
, so that

\bigl\|(1+\kappa_{k}t/r^{2})^{q}w\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};{L^{2}_{r}})}+\kappa_{k}^{1/2}\bigl\|(1+\kappa_{k}t/r^{2})^{q}r^{-1}w\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};{L^{2}_{r}})}\leq C\|w(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}.

The reasons for exploring the stability of the TC flow in the exterior disk list as follows:

(1)

Firstly, due to the degeneracy of the Taylor-Couette flow at infinity, we cannot expect the solution to decay exponentially in a space-time decoupled manner. One may check
[
11
,
7
]
for details.

(2)

Lemma
1.1
tells us that even the coupled space-time exponential decay is not valid.

(3)

Lemma
1.1
and Lemma
1.2
tell us that for the fully nonlinear equation (
1.10b
), one can at most expect polynomial space-time coupling decay.

At this point, a new issue arises: to close the
a priori
estimates for the nonlinear system (
1.6
), we need space-time coupling multipliers. The previous resolvent estimate methods
[
26
,
9
,
13
,
2
]
are all aimed at space-time decoupled cases (essentially performing a Fourier transform in the time direction). Therefore, here we introduce time-dependent resolvent estimate in Section
4
.

In the case of polar coordinates for the exterior disk, there is a critical issue: the weight of the stream function with respect to
r
differs from
w
by
1/r^{2}
. A simple basic fact is that the derivatives of
1/r^{2}
are non-vanishing in
[1,R]
yet they
may converge to zero at infinity. Therefore, for technical convenience, we shall choose
\Lambda_{k}
as the long-time space-time decay weight. The basic properties of
\Lambda_{k}
will be provided with a detailed explanation after we present the main theorem.

1.3.
Main theorems

The following result is the main result of this paper, which concerns global well-posedness and the asymptotic behavior of the solution to the
system (
1.10
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<2
and
\hat{C}
be a large constant, we denote
\Lambda_{k}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\log\big(\hat{C}r^{2}+\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t\big)
to be the enhanced dissipation weight. Then there exist constants
C_{\varepsilon},C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},\hat{C}_{\varepsilon}
depending only on
\varepsilon
, such that if
C_{\varepsilon}\geq\hat{C}_{\varepsilon}
and

(1.12)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(0)&\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\|rw_{0}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\|u^{\theta}_{0}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}M_{k}(0)\leq C_{\varepsilon}|\nu B|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\hbox{with}\quad\\
M_{k}(0)&{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\|r^{\varepsilon+2}w_{k}(0)\Lambda_{k}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\|r^{\varepsilon+3}\partial_{r}w_{k}(0)\Lambda_{k}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{r}},\quad\text{for}\ k\neq 0,\end{split}

the system (
1.10
) has a unique global solution
(w_{0},w_{k},\varphi_{k},u_{k}^{r},u_{k}^{\theta})
which satisfies

(1.13)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}&E_{0}(T)+100\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}E_{k}(T)\leq C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{M}(0),\quad\text{for any}\ T>0\quad\hbox{with}\quad\\
E_{0}(T){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}&\|rw_{0}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\|u^{\theta}_{0}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{0}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})},\\
E_{k}(T){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}&\|r^{\varepsilon+1}w_{k}\Lambda_{k}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\mu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\varepsilon}w_{k}\Lambda_{k}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\\
&+|kB|^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\|r^{\varepsilon-1}\partial_{r}\varphi_{k}\Lambda_{k}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+|k|\|r^{\varepsilon-2}\varphi_{k}\Lambda_{k}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\Bigr),\quad\text{for }\ k\neq 0.\end{split}

It is easy to observe from Lemma
B.3
and (
1.10d
) that

(1.14)

|kB|^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|\Big\|r^{\varepsilon-\frac{3}{2}}\varphi_{k}\Lambda_{k}\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{\infty}_{r}})}\lesssim E_{k}(T).

As a corollary of Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the pointwise logarithmic-type nonlinear enhanced dissipation estimate.

Theorem 1.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, we have

(1.15)

\|r^{1+\varepsilon}w_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\log\big(\hat{C}+\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t\big)}\mathcal{M}(0),\quad\text{for}\ k\neq 0,t>0,

and

(1.16)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{0}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}=0.

We have the following remarks in order concerning Theorem
1.3
:

(a)
The Choice of
\Lambda_{k}
:

In fact, throughout this paper, we only require the following properties for
\Lambda_{k}
:

A.

\mu_{k}^{-1}r^{2}\left|\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\right|+r^{2}\left|\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\right|+r\left|\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\right|\ll 1,
where
\mu_{k}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\max\bigl(\nu k^{2},\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}\bigr);

B.

\Lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda_{\ell}\Lambda_{k-\ell}
for
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
;

C.

\frac{\Lambda_{k}(t,r)}{\Lambda_{k}(t,s)}\lesssim\max\left(\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\varepsilon_{1}},\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\varepsilon_{2}}\right)
for some
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\ll 1
.

Rather than using the explicit expression for
\Lambda_{k}
,

(1)

Property A will be used to absorb terms arising from commutators
[\partial_{t},\Lambda_{k}]
and
[\partial_{r}^{2},\Lambda_{k}]
.

(2)

Property B is designed to deal with the frequency interactions in the nonlinear terms.

(3)

Property C will be used to recover the elliptic estimate (see Lemma
C.1
and the estimation of terms involving
\widetilde{K}_{k}
).

•

Thus, if one prefers polynomially enhanced dissipation for
w
, we can

\displaystyle\text{replace
}\log\left(\hat{C}r^{2}+\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t\right)\text{ by
}\left(e+\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}{r^{2}}\right)^{\delta},

where
\delta\ll_{\varepsilon}1
. However, for simplicity, this paper considers only the logarithmic-type enhanced dissipation.

•

Furthermore, we can replace
\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}
by
\mu_{k}
to provide better dissipation for high frequencies (
|k|\geq\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|B|^{\frac{1}{2}}
), and the proof is the same. We retain
\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}
in
\Lambda_{k}
to emphasize the dissipation scale
\mathcal{O}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}|B|^{-\frac{2}{3}})
.

(b)
The Criticality of Theorem
1.3
:

(1)

If we impose additional spatial weights
r^{\beta}
(
\beta>0
) on
w_{0}
, beyond those in
E_{0}(T)
, similar results as Theorem
1.3
hold only for
t\lesssim\nu^{-1}
. In this case, we cannot expect global stability results for general data, since Proposition
2.6
indicates that
e^{-\mathcal{T}_{0}t}
is linearly unstable in these weighted spaces. On the other hand, if we remove some spatial weights
r^{\beta}
from
E_{0}(T)
, we cannot close the energy estimate for
E_{k}(T)
. Therefore, up to this point,
E_{0}(T)
is critical.

(2)

In the linear theory of TC flow in the exterior domain (see
[
20
]
), the linear evolution
e^{-\mathcal{T}_{k}t}
can exhibit polynomial dissipation
(1+\kappa_{k}t)^{-\delta}
when
\frac{\nu}{|kB|}\ll(1+\delta)^{-3}
, at the cost of losing equivalent spatial weights. For nonlinear evolution, we can only deal with
\delta\ll_{\varepsilon}1
, because the elliptic estimate

\|r^{-2}\Lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq C\|\Lambda_{k}w_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\quad\text{for some $C$ depending only on $\delta$},

fails for
\Lambda_{k}=\Big(e+\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}{r^{2}}\Big)^{\delta}
and large
|\delta|
. Therefore, up to this point, the enhanced dissipation weight
\left(e+\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}{r^{2}}\right)^{\delta}
with
\delta\ll_{\varepsilon}1
may be optimal for the nonlinear problem.

(c)
Transition threshold:

The mathematic version of Transition threshold
was formulated by Bedrossian-Gremain-Masmoudi
[
3
]
as follows:

Given a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, find a
\beta=\beta(X)
so that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;\nu^{\beta}\,\,\Rightarrow\text{ stability},

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\gg\;\nu^{\beta}\,\,\Rightarrow\text{ instability},

where the exponent

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
W
be a smooth enough solution of (
1.5
) on

Then, for any

, one has

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|W(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\nu\|\nabla W\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}(\Omega)})}^{2}=\|W(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Proof.

Due to
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits U=0
and (
1.4
), we get, by taking
{L^{2}(\Omega)}
inner product of the equation (
1.5
) with
W
and using integration by parts, that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|W(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(V^{\star}+U)\cdot\mathbf{n}|W|^{2}dS-\nu\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla W\cdot\mathbf{n}WdS

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|W(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

By integrating the above inequality over

).
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the assumption that

,

, and

satisfies the boundary condition (

(2.2)

-\int_{\Omega}\Delta U\cdot Udx=\|W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{n}=(\mathbf{n}^{1},\mathbf{n}^{2})
be the outward normal vector to the domain
\Omega.
We get, by
using integration by parts, that

\begin{split}\|W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}&=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}U^{2}-\partial_{2}U^{1})^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}U^{2})^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{2}U^{1})^{2}dx-2\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}U^{2}\partial_{2}U^{1}dx\\
&=\int_{\partial\Omega}U^{2}\mathbf{n}^{1}\partial_{1}U^{2}dS-\int_{\Omega}U^{2}\partial_{1}^{2}U^{2}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}U^{1}\mathbf{n}^{2}\partial_{2}U^{1}dS-\int_{\Omega}U^{1}\partial_{2}^{2}U^{1}dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\partial\Omega}U^{2}\mathbf{n}^{1}\partial_{2}U^{1}dS-\int_{\partial\Omega}U^{1}\mathbf{n}^{2}\partial_{1}U^{2}dS+\int_{\Omega}U^{2}\partial_{2}\partial_{1}U^{1}+U^{1}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}U^{2}dx,\end{split}

from which,
\partial_{1}U^{1}+\partial_{2}U^{2}=0
and
(\partial_{1}U^{2}-\partial_{2}U^{1})|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, we infer

\displaystyle\|W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}(U^{2}\mathbf{n}^{1}-U^{1}\mathbf{n}^{2})(\partial_{1}U^{2}-\partial_{2}U^{1})dS-\int_{\Omega}\Delta U\cdot Udx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\Delta U\cdot Udx.

This leads to (
2.2
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
U
be a smooth enough solution of (

Then for any

,

\begin{split}&\|U(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\nu\|W\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}(\Omega)})}^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\|U(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+8\pi|B|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}|k|\big\|\frac{\varphi_{k}}{r^{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}\Big\|\frac{\partial_{r}\varphi_{-k}}{r}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{t}({L^{2}_{r}})}.\end{split}

Proof.

Thanks to (
1.4

and (
2.2
),
we get,
by taking

(2.4)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|U\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\nu\|W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=-\int_{\Omega}(U\cdot\nabla)V^{\star}\cdot Udx.\end{split}

Yet by virtue of (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(U\cdot\nabla)V^{\star}\cdot Udx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\big(U^{r}\partial_{r}+U^{\theta}\frac{\partial_{\theta}}{r}\big)\big(Ar\mathbf{e_{\theta}}+\frac{B}{r}\mathbf{e_{\theta}}\big)\cdot(U^{r}\mathbf{e_{r}}+U^{\theta}\mathbf{e_{\theta}})dx

Proposition 2.5

Proposition 2.6

There exists

and

, there holds

(3.6)

\nu\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|w/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\leq C\bigl(\big|\braket{F,w}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\big|\langle F,\tilde{\rho}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\mathbbm{1}_{(0,1)}(\lambda)\bigr).

For arbitrary
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we observe from (
3.1
) that
\eta{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}r^{\alpha}w
and
G{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}r^{\alpha}F
satisfy

(3.7)

-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)\eta+ikB\big(r^{-2}-\lambda\big)\eta=G+\nu(\alpha+\alpha^{2})r^{-2}\eta-2\nu\alpha r^{-1}\partial_{r}\eta.

Then we get, by applying (
3.6
) and setting
\tilde{\alpha}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}1+|\alpha|
, that

\begin{split}\nu\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|\eta/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}&\leq C\bigl(\big|\braket{G,\eta}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\big|\langle G,\tilde{\rho}\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\mathbbm{1}_{(0,1)}(\lambda)\bigr)\\
&\quad+C\nu\big|\braket{(\alpha+\alpha^{2})r^{-2}\eta-2\alpha r^{-1}\partial_{r}\eta,\eta}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\\
&\quad+C\nu\big|\langle(\alpha+\alpha^{2})r^{-2}\eta-2\alpha r^{-1}\partial_{r}\eta,\tilde{\rho}\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\mathbbm{1}_{(0,1)}(\lambda)\\
&\leq C\bigl(\big|\braket{G,\eta}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\big|\langle G,\tilde{\rho}\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\mathbbm{1}_{(0,1)}(\lambda)\bigr)+C\nu\tilde{\alpha}^{2}\|\eta/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nu\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}.\end{split}

Notice that
\nu\tilde{\alpha}^{2}\ll\mu_{k}
if
\frac{\nu}{|kB|}\ll\tilde{\alpha}^{-3}
, so that we deduce from the above inequality that

\nu\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|\eta/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\leq C\bigl(\big|\braket{F,r^{2\alpha}w}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\big|\langle F,r^{2\alpha}\tilde{\rho}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\mathbbm{1}_{(0,1)}(\lambda)\bigr),

which together with the fact:

\nu\|r^{\alpha}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\approx\nu\|r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\approx\nu\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|\eta/r\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}

ensures (
3.4
).

Next let us turn to the proof of (
3.5a
) and (
3.5b
). We first observe from (
3.3
) that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\big|\langle F,r^{2\alpha}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\leq\min\big(\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}},\ \|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}\|r^{\alpha}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}\big),

and for
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\big|\langle F,r^{2\alpha}\tilde{\rho}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\min\bigl(\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}\tilde{\rho}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}},\ \|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}\|r^{\alpha}\tilde{\rho}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\bigl(\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}},\ \|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}(\|r^{\alpha}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}+\|\partial_{r}\tilde{\rho}r^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}})\bigr).

Due to
\frac{\nu}{|kB|}\ll(1+|\alpha|)^{-3}
,
\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{3}}
is sufficiently small, so that one has

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}\tilde{\rho}r^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{3}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{1}{3}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}},

as a result, it comes out

(3.9)

\begin{split}\big|\langle F,r^{2\alpha}\tilde{\rho}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\lesssim\min\Big(&\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}},\\
&\quad\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}\big(\|r^{\alpha}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}+\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{1}{3}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\big)\Big).\end{split}

Then we deduce from (
3.4
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\nu\|r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq C\mu_{k}^{-1}\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\nu\|

\displaystyle r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}\bigl(\|r^{\alpha}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}+\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{1}{3}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigl(\nu^{-1}+\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}\mu_{k}^{-1}\bigr)\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bigl({\nu}\|r^{\alpha}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\bigr),

from which and the fact that
|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}\mu_{k}^{-1}\leq\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}},
we infer

(3.10a)

\displaystyle\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\leq C\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}},

(3.10b)

\displaystyle\nu\|r^{\alpha}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq C\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}.

Thanks to (
3.10
),
it suffices to estimate
(r^{-2}-\lambda)w
in order to complete the proof of (
3.5
). Notice that

\displaystyle\|kBr^{\alpha}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}+\nu\Big\|r^{\alpha}\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)w\Big\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}+\nu\Big(1+|\frac{\alpha}{k}|\Big)\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\nu|k|\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(1+|\frac{\alpha}{k}|+\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|}{\mu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}\lesssim(1+|\alpha/k|)\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}},

from which and (
3.10b
), we obtain (
3.5b
).

On the other hand, we get, by using integration by parts, that

\displaystyle\langle F

\displaystyle,r^{2\alpha+2}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle-\nu\big(\partial_{r}^{2}-(k^{2}-1/4)r^{-2}\big)w+ikB\big(r^{-2}-\lambda\big)w,r^{2\alpha+2}\big(r^{-2}-\lambda\big)w\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\big\langle w^{\prime},\big(r^{2\alpha+2}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\big)^{\prime}\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}+\nu(k^{2}-1/4)\big\langle w,r^{2\alpha}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}+ikB\|r^{\alpha+1}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\big\langle w^{\prime},r^{2\alpha+2}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w^{\prime}\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}+\nu\big\langle w^{\prime},\big(2\alpha r^{2\alpha-1}-\lambda(2\alpha+2)r^{2\alpha+1}\big)w\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle+\nu(k^{2}-1/4)\big\langle w,r^{2\alpha}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}+ikB\|r^{\alpha+1}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}.

By taking imaginary part of the above inequality and rewriting

2\alpha r^{2\alpha-1}-\lambda(2\alpha+2)r^{2\alpha+1}=2(1+\alpha)r^{2\alpha+1}(r^{-2}-\lambda)-2r^{2\alpha-1},

we obtain

\displaystyle|kB|\|r^{\alpha+1}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big|\langle F,r^{2\alpha+2}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+2\nu\tilde{\alpha}\big|\langle w^{\prime},r^{2\alpha+1}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+2\nu\big|\braket{w^{\prime},r^{2\alpha-1}w}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+2\nu\tilde{\alpha}\|r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\bigr)\|r^{\alpha+1}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+2\nu\|r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.

Observing from (
3.5a
) that

\displaystyle\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{1}{3}}\|r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}\leq C\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}.

Then
we get, by applying Young’s inequality and
(\frac{\nu}{|kB|})^{\frac{1}{3}}\ll\tilde{\alpha}^{-1},
that

\displaystyle|kB|\|r^{\alpha+1}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|kB|}\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\frac{\nu^{2}\tilde{\alpha}^{2}}{|kB|}\|r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\nu\|r^{\alpha}w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{|kB|}+\frac{\nu^{2}\tilde{\alpha}^{2}}{|kB|}\nu^{-\frac{4}{3}}|kB|^{-\frac{2}{3}}\Big)\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{|kB|}\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2},

which together with (
3.10b
) ensures (
3.5a
).
This completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Below we present the resolvent estimate of
\varphi
:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},\varepsilon\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
independent of
\nu,k,B,\lambda
, so that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\Big)\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.

Proof.

If
\lambda\leq 0
, it’s easy to observe from Proposition
3.1
that for
|\alpha|\leq 3

\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq\|r^{\alpha+1}(r^{-2}-\lambda)w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq\frac{C}{|kB|}\|r^{\alpha+1}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}},

which together with Lemma
C.1
ensures

\begin{split}\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-1}\|r^{\varepsilon}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-1}\min\big(|kB|^{-1},\mu_{k}^{-1}\big)\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\mu_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{-1}\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.\end{split}

If
\lambda>0
and
\nu k^{2}\geq|B|
, we deduce from Lemma
C.1
that

\begin{split}\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-1}\|r^{\varepsilon}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-1}\mu_{k}^{-1}\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\mu_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\nu k^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{-1}\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\mu_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.\end{split}

Therefore it remains to consider (
3.11
) for the case:
\lambda>0
and
\nu k^{2}\leq|B|
(i.e.,
\mu_{k}=\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}
). Let us denote
\tilde{\delta}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}(\frac{\nu}{|kB|})^{\frac{1}{3}}\ll 1
, and

(3.12)

\begin{split}&E{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\big\{r\geq 1:\ |1-\sqrt{\lambda}r|\leq\tilde{\delta}\big\},\\
&E_{m}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\big\{r\geq 1:\ \tilde{\delta}\leq|1-\sqrt{\lambda}r|\leq\frac{1}{2}\big\},\\
&E_{o}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\big\{r\geq 1:\ \frac{1}{2}\leq|1-\sqrt{\lambda}r|\big\},\end{split}

and split
w=w(\mathbbm{1}_{E}+\mathbbm{1}_{E_{m}}+\mathbbm{1}_{E_{o}})
.
Then we get, by applying Lemma
C.1
that

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|r^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}w\mathbbm{1}_{E}\|_{L^{1}_{r}}+\|r^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}w\mathbbm{1}_{E_{m}}\|_{L^{1}_{r}}\big)+|k|^{-1}\|r^{\varepsilon}w\mathbbm{1}_{E_{o}}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.

It follows from Lemmas
B.4
and
B.5
that

\displaystyle\|r^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}w\mathbbm{1}_{E}\|_{L^{1}_{r}}+\|r^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}w\mathbbm{1}_{E_{m}}\|_{L^{1}_{r}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\varepsilon}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\Big\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{E_{m}}}{1-\lambda r^{2}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\varepsilon}(1-\lambda r^{2})w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\varepsilon}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\tilde{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\varepsilon}(1-\lambda r^{2})w\|_{L^{2}_{r}},

from which and Proposition

3.1
, we infer

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\big(\frac{\nu}{|kB|}\big)^{\frac{1}{6}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{-\frac{2}{3}}+\big(\frac{\nu}{|kB|}\big)^{-\frac{1}{6}}|kB|^{-1}+|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{-1}\Bigr)\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{6}}|kB|^{-\frac{5}{6}}\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}=|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{2+\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.11
.
∎

To proceed further, we define sublinear operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}
, which will be frequently used in this paper, via

(3.13)

{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f](r){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\int_{1}^{\infty}(rs)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\min\Big(\big(\frac{r}{s}\big)^{a},\big(\frac{s}{r}\big)^{b}\Big)|f|(s)ds.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f]
be defined by (
3.13
). Then we have

(3.14a)

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}\Big)\|f\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\quad\mbox{if}\ a,b>0,

(3.14b)

\displaystyle\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2a+1}+\frac{1}{2b-1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{1}_{r}}\quad\mbox{if}\ a>-\frac{1}{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad b>\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

In view of (
3.13
), we get, by using changes of variables:

\tilde{r}=\log r
,
\tilde{s}=\log s
, that

\begin{split}e^{\frac{\tilde{r}}{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f](e^{\tilde{r}})=&\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min\big(e^{a(\tilde{r}-\tilde{s})},e^{b(\tilde{s}-\tilde{r})}\big)e^{\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}|f|(e^{\tilde{s}})\,d\tilde{s},\\
e^{\tilde{r}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f](e^{\tilde{r}})=&\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min\big(e^{(a+\frac{1}{2})(\tilde{r}-\tilde{s})},e^{(b-\frac{1}{2})(\tilde{s}-\tilde{r})}\big)e^{\tilde{s}}|f|(e^{\tilde{s}})\,d\tilde{s}.\end{split}

Applying Young Inequality gives rise to

\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}=\|e^{\frac{\tilde{r}}{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f](e^{\tilde{r}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};d\tilde{r})}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}\Big)\|e^{\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}|f|(e^{\tilde{s}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};d\tilde{s})}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}\Big)\|f\|_{L^{2}_{r}},

and

\begin{split}\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}&=\|e^{\tilde{r}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{a,b}[f](e^{\tilde{r}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};d\tilde{r})}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2a+1}+\frac{1}{2b-1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e^{\tilde{s}}|f|(e^{\tilde{s}})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};d\tilde{s})}\\
&\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2a+1}+\frac{1}{2b-1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{1}_{r}}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of (
3.14
).
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},\varepsilon\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
independent of
\nu,k,B,\lambda
, so that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\Bigr)\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|r^{1+\varepsilon}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}.

Proof.

In the case of
\lambda\leq 0
, it’s easy to observe from (
3.1
) that for
|\alpha|\leq 3
,

\displaystyle|kB|\big|\langle(r^{-2}-\lambda)w,r^{2\alpha}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|\leq\big|\Im\langle F,r^{2\alpha}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\big|\Im\langle\partial_{r}w,\partial_{r}(r^{2\alpha})w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|,

which implies in this case that

\displaystyle|kB|\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\lesssim\big|\langle F,r^{2\alpha}w\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.

Applying Young’s inequality and Proposition
3.1
gives

\begin{split}|kB|\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}&\lesssim\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}\|r^{\alpha}w\|_{H^{1}_{k}}+\nu\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\\
&\lesssim(\nu^{-1}+\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}^{2}\lesssim\nu^{-1}\|r^{\alpha}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}^{2},\end{split}

from which and Lemma
C.1
, we infer

(3.16)

\begin{split}\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-1}\|r^{\varepsilon}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-1}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{1+\varepsilon}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}.\end{split}

In the case when
\lambda>0
and
\nu k^{2}\geq|B|
, we deduce from Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
C.1
that

(3.17)

\begin{split}\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-1}\|r^{\varepsilon}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-1}\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|r^{1+\varepsilon}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{-\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{1+\varepsilon}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}.\end{split}

We claim that if
\lambda>0
and
\nu k^{2}\leq|B|,
then

(3.18a)

\displaystyle\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big\|\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|r^{1+\varepsilon}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}},

(3.18b)

\displaystyle\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}|kB|^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|r^{1+\varepsilon}F\|_{H^{-1}_{k}}.

By summarizing (
3.16
), (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we complete the proof of (
3.15
).
∎

It remains to prove (
3.18
).

Proof of (
3.18
).

In the case when
\lambda>0
and
\nu k^{2}\leq|B|,

we denote
r_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
\tilde{\delta}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}(\frac{\nu}{|kB|})^{\frac{1}{3}}\ll 1
and

\delta\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\tilde{\delta}r_{0}.
Let
E,E_{m},E_{o}
be defined as in (
3.12
). Since
\nu k^{2}\leq|B|
, we have
|k|{\tilde{\delta}}\leq 1
. Let
\eta(r)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be supported in
(-2,2)
and equal to
1
for
s\in[-1,1],
and we denote
\eta(\frac{r-r_{0}}{\delta})
by
\eta_{\delta}.

Then we write

(3.19)

\begin{split}-\frac{\varphi}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}&=\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-2+\varepsilon}K_{k}(r,s)w(s)ds\\
&=\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-2+\varepsilon}K_{k}(r,s)\frac{1-\eta_{\delta}(s)}{s^{-2}-\lambda}(s^{-2}-\lambda)w(s)ds\\
&\quad+\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-2+\varepsilon}K_{k}(r,s)w(s)\eta_{\delta}(s)ds\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}J_{1}+J_{2},\end{split}

and

(3.20)

4.
Resolvent estimates with enhanced dissipation weight

In this section,
we establish pointwise enhanced dissipation for the following linearized equations with
k\neq 0
:

(4.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{k}+\mathcal{T}_{k}w_{k}=F_{k},\quad w_{k}(t=0)=w_{k}(0),\quad w_{k}|_{r=1,\infty}=0,

(4.1b)

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)\varphi_{k}=w_{k},\quad\varphi_{k}|_{r=1,\infty}=0.

Since we shall deal with
L^{2}
type estimates, for simplicity,
we always set
w_{k}(0)=0
and assume that
\varphi_{k},w_{k},F\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}([0,\infty)\times[1,\infty)).

Recall that

\kappa_{k}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}
and
\Lambda_{k}(t,r){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\log\big(\hat{C}r^{2}+\kappa_{k}t\big),

we denote

(4.2)

w_{\star}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\Lambda_{k}(t,r)w_{k},\quad F_{\star}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\Lambda_{k}(t,r)F_{k},\quad\varphi_{\star}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\Lambda_{k}(t,r)\varphi_{k}.

Then in view of (
4.1
), we find

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}w_{\star}-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)w_{\star}+\frac{ikB}{r^{2}}w_{\star}\\
&\qquad=F_{\star}+\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}w_{\star}-\nu\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}w_{\star}+2\nu\Big(\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\Big)^{2}w_{\star}-2\nu\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\partial_{r}w_{\star}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}F_{\star}+R^{w_{\star}},\\
&\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)\varphi_{\star}=w_{\star}+\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\varphi_{\star}-2\Big(\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\Big)^{2}\varphi_{\star}+2\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\partial_{r}\varphi_{\star}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}w_{\star}+R^{\varphi_{\star}},\\
&w_{\star}(0)=0,\quad w_{\star}|_{r=1,+\infty}=\varphi_{\star}|_{r=1,+\infty}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

The key idea to handle the equations (
4.3
) is to view
R^{w_{\star}}
and
R^{\varphi_{\star}}
as small perturbations and using the
resolvent estimates we obtained in Section
3
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
\alpha\in[0,4]
,
T>0
, there exist constants
C>0
and
\hat{C}
sufficiently large, which are independent of
\nu,k,B,\alpha,T
, so that

(4.4)

\begin{split}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w_{\star}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha-1}w_{\star}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\leq C\|r^{\alpha+1}F_{\star}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}.\end{split}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we set
T=\infty
and define

\hat{f}(\lambda){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\lambda}f(t)dt.

We may extend
f(t)=0
for
t\leq 0
in case
f(0)=0.

Then we get, by taking Fourier transformation to the
w_{\star}
equation in (
4.3
) with respect to the time variable, that

(4.5)

\begin{split}&-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)\widehat{w}_{\star}(kB\lambda,r)+ikB(r^{-2}-\lambda)\widehat{w}_{\star}(kB\lambda,r)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\hat{f}_{\star}(kB\lambda,r)+\widehat{R^{w_{\star}}}(kB\lambda,r),\end{split}

from which and Proposition
3.1
, we infer

\displaystyle\nu\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}\widehat{w}_{\star}(kB\lambda,r)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\mu_{k}^{2}\|r^{\alpha-1}\widehat{w}_{\star}(kB\lambda,r)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq C\Big(\|r^{\alpha+1}\widehat{F}_{\star}(kB\lambda,r)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\|r^{\alpha+1}\widehat{R^{w_{\star}}}(kB\lambda,r)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\Big).

By integrating the above inequality over
\mathbb{R}
with respect to
\lambda
variable, and then using Plancherel’s equality, we obtain

(4.6)

\displaystyle\nu\mu_{k}\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w_{\star}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\mu_{k}^{2}\|r^{\alpha-1}w_{\star}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq C\Bigl(\|r^{\alpha+1}F_{\star}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\|r^{\alpha+1}R^{w_{\star}}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\Bigr).

Yet it follows from Lemma
C.2
that

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\|r^{\alpha+1}R^{w_{\star}}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\\
&\leq C\Bigl(\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}}}{\hat{C}\log\hat{C}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w_{\star}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\frac{\nu}{\log\hat{C}}\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w_{\star}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\frac{\nu}{\log\hat{C}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w_{\star}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}\Bigr)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\log^{2}\hat{C}}\Bigl(\nu^{2}\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w_{\star}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+(\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}|kB|^{\frac{2}{3}})^{2}\|r^{\alpha-1}w_{\star}\|_{L^{2}_{T}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\Bigr).\end{split}

By inserting (
4.7
) into (
4.6
), and taking
\hat{C}
to be large enough, we arrive at (
4.4
).
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
\alpha\in[0,4]
,
T>0
, there exist constants
C>0
and
\hat{C}
sufficiently large, which are independent of
\nu,k,B,\alpha,T
, so that

(4.8)

\begin{split}\nu\|r^{\alpha}w_{\star}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({H^{1}_{k}})}&+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\alpha-1}w_{\star}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\leq C\|r^{\alpha}F_{\star}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({H^{-1}_{k}})}.\end{split}

Proof.

Thanks to (

\begin{split}&\big\|r^{\alpha}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu\big\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\mu_{k}\big\|r^{\alpha-1}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\\
&+\kappa_{k}\Big\|\frac{\kappa_{k}t}{r^{3-\alpha}}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq C\|r^{\alpha}w_{k}(0)\Lambda_{k}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

For the brevity of notations, we only present the proof of (
5.10
) for the case
\alpha=1
, and we divide our proof into the following six steps:

Step 1.
The change of unknown:
\eta{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}
.

Let
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}.
Then in view of (
5.7
), we find

(5.11)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\eta-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}+\Theta^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)\eta+\nu\Big|\frac{2kBt}{r^{3}}\Big|^{2}\eta=R_{1}^{\eta}+R_{2}^{\eta},\\
&\eta|_{t=0}=\eta(0)=w_{k}(0)\Lambda_{k}(0),\quad\eta|_{r=1,+\infty}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where we denote
R_{1}^{\eta}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}-2\nu\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\partial_{r}\eta+\big(2\nu|\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}|^{2}-\nu\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda_{k}}{{{\Lambda_{k}}}}\big)\eta
and
R_{2}^{\eta}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}{{\eta}}
.

It is easy to observe from Lemma
C.2
that

\displaystyle\|r\partial_{r}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\|w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\approx\|r\partial_{r}\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}.

So that in order prove (
5.10
), it remains to prove

(5.12)

\begin{split}&\|r\eta\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu\|r\partial_{r}\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\kappa_{k}\Big\|\frac{\kappa_{k}t}{r^{2}}\eta\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\mu_{k}\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq C\|r\eta(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}.\end{split}

Step 2.
Dyadic decomposition.

We first fix some function
\chi(r)\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{c}[\frac{2}{3},2]
(see the construction in
[
20
]
) and denote
\chi_{j}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\chi(\frac{r}{2^{j}})
so that

(5.13)

\begin{cases}0\leq\chi_{j}\leq 1,\quad\chi_{0}(1)=1,\quad\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\chi_{j}(r)=1,\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\chi_{j}^{2}(r)\leq 1,\ \forall\ r\geq 1,\\
\operatorname{supp}_{r}\chi_{j}\subset[2^{j+1}/3,2^{j+1}],\quad\chi_{j}\chi_{\ell}=0\ \forall\ |j-\ell|\geq 2,\quad\|\chi\|_{C^{2}}\leq 4\cdot 10^{6}.\end{cases}

Then it is obvious to observe that

\|r^{\alpha}\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\approx\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2\alpha j}\|\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2},

where
\eta_{j}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\chi_{j}\eta
, which satisfies

(5.14)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\eta_{j}-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}+\Theta^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)\eta_{j}+\nu\Big|\frac{2kBt}{r^{3}}\Big|^{2}\eta_{j}=\chi_{j}(R_{1}^{\eta}+R_{2}^{\eta})-\nu(2^{-2j}\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\eta+2^{1-j}\chi_{j}^{\prime}\partial_{r}\eta),\\
&\eta_{j}|_{t=0}=\eta_{j}(0),\quad\eta_{j}|_{r=\max(1,\frac{2^{j+1}}{3})}=\eta_{j}|_{r=2^{j+1}}=0,\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times[\max(1,2^{j+1}/3),2^{j+1}],\end{aligned}\right.

here and in what follows, we always denote
\chi_{j}^{\prime}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\chi^{\prime}(\frac{r}{2^{j}})
and
\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\chi^{\prime\prime}(\frac{r}{2^{j}})
.
Noticing that
\eta(r)=\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 0}\eta_{\ell}(r)
for
r\geq 1
and
\chi_{j}\chi_{\ell}=0
if
|j-\ell|\geq 2
, we also have

\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\eta=\sum\limits_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\eta_{\ell},\qquad\chi_{j}^{\prime}\partial_{r}\eta=\sum\limits_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\chi_{j}^{\prime}\partial_{r}\eta_{\ell}.

Next we introduce the following energy functional:

(5.15)

\begin{split}\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq 0}\|r\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{T}}^{2},\quad\hbox{with}\quad\\
\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{T}}^{2}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\frac{1}{2}\|r\eta_{j}(T)\|^{2}&+\nu\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+4\kappa_{k}^{3}\|\frac{\tau}{r^{2}}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}.\end{split}

Step 3.
The control of
\kappa_{k}\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}
by
\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}
.

Let

(1)

D_{1}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\bigl\{(\tau,r)\in(0,T)\times(1,+\infty):r^{2}\leq\kappa_{k}\tau\ \bigr\}
;

(2)

D_{2}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}}\bigl\{(\tau,r)\in(0,T)\times(1,+\infty):0\leq\kappa_{k}\tau\leq r^{2}\ \bigr\}
.

Here
D_{1}
is the region where enhanced dissipation occurs. Then we decompose

\begin{split}\kappa_{k}\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}=\kappa_{k}\Big(\iint_{(\tau,r)\in D_{1}}+\iint_{(\tau,r)\in D_{2}}\Big)|\eta|^{2}drd\tau\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}.\end{split}

Since
\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\chi_{j}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, one has

\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq\kappa_{k}\Big\|\frac{\kappa_{k}\tau}{r^{2}}\eta\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq 2\kappa_{k}\sum_{j\geq 0}\Big\|\frac{\kappa_{k}\tau}{r^{2}}\eta_{j}\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}.

Whereas it follows from a direct computation that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa_{k}\sum_{j\geq 0}\iint_{(\tau,r)\in D_{2}}|\eta_{j}|^{2}drd\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa_{k}\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{\min(T,2^{2j+2}/\kappa_{k})}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{9}{2^{2j+2}}|r\eta_{j}|^{2}drd\tau

\displaystyle\leq 18\sum_{j\geq 0}\|r\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq 36\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}.

As a result, it comes out

(5.16)

\kappa_{k}\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}.

Step 4.
The estimation of
\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}
.

We get, by first taking the real part of
{L^{2}_{r}}
inner product of (
5.14
) with
r^{2}\eta_{j}
and then using integration by parts and (
2
), that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta_{j}(t)\|_{X_{t}}^{2}&\leq\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{1}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{2}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\|\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\\
&\quad+\nu\sum_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}(2^{-2j}\|r^{2}\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+2^{1-j}\|r\chi_{j}^{\prime}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{r}}}\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}})\|\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}.\end{split}

Observing that
\text{supp}_{r}\chi_{j}\in[2^{j+1}/3,2^{j+1}]
and
\|\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\chi_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 4\cdot 10^{6},
we find

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta_{j}(t)\|_{X_{t}}^{2}\leq&\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{1}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{2}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|+\nu\|\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\\
&+16\cdot 10^{6}\nu\sum_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}(\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}+\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}})\|\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}},\\
\end{split}

Then by integrating the above inequality over
[0,T]
and using the definition of
X_{T}
given by (
5.15
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\|r\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\leq 2\times\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\|r\eta_{j}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{1}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|d\tau\\
&\ +\int_{0}^{T}\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{2}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|d\tau+\frac{1}{\Theta^{2}}\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{T}}^{2}+16\cdot 10^{6}(1/\Theta+1/\Theta^{2})\|\eta_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\sum_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{X_{T}}\Bigr).\\
\end{split}

By summarizing the above inequalities for
j\geq 0
and using Cauchy Inequality, we achieve

(5.17)

\begin{split}\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}&\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\|r\eta_{j}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\geq 0}\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{1}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|d\tau\\
&\quad+2\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\geq 0}\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{2}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|d\tau\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{\Theta^{2}}\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}+16\cdot 10^{6}(1/\Theta+1/\Theta^{2})\big(\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}+3\sum_{j\geq 0}\sum_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\big)\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 0}\|r\eta_{j}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\big(2/\Theta^{2}+16\cdot 10^{7}(1/\Theta+1/\Theta^{2})\big)\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}\\
&\quad+2\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\geq 0}\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{1}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|d\tau+2\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\geq 0}\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{2}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|d\tau.\end{split}

Step 5.
The estimation of terms in (
5.17
) involving
R_{i}^{\eta}
.

We first observe that

(5.18)

\nu\|r\partial_{r}\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}.

While it follows from Lemma
C.2
that

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\geq 0}\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{i}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|d\tau\\
&\lesssim\frac{\nu}{\log\hat{C}}(\|r\partial_{r}\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})})\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+\frac{\kappa_{k}}{\hat{C}\log\hat{C}}\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\frac{\nu}{\log\hat{C}}\|r\partial_{r}\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\frac{\nu+\kappa_{k}}{\log\hat{C}}\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}.\end{split}

So that by taking
\hat{C}\gg 1
and using (
5.16
) and (
5.18
), we obtain

(5.19)

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\geq 0}\big|\braket{r^{2}\chi_{j}R_{i}^{\eta},\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}\big|d\tau\leq\frac{1}{8}\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}.\end{split}

Step 6.
The end of the proof.

Due to
\Theta\geq 10^{9}
,
by inserting (
5.19
) into (
5.17
), we achieve

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\|r\eta_{j}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\big(1/4+2/\Theta^{2}+16\cdot 10^{7}(1/\Theta+1/\Theta^{2})\big)\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|r\eta(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2},

which together with (
5.16
) and (
5.18
) ensures (
5.10
).
We thus complete the proof of Proposition
5.4
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
\alpha\in[1,5]
,
\hat{C}\gg 1
and
w_{k}^{(1)}
solve (
5.7
). Then there exists constant
C>0
independent of
\nu,k,B,\hat{C},T,\alpha
, so that

(5.20)

\begin{split}&\big\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\big\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu\big\|r^{\alpha}\partial^{2}_{r}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\mu_{k}\big\|r^{\alpha-1}\partial_{r}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\\
&+\kappa_{k}\Big\|\frac{\kappa_{k}t}{r^{3-\alpha}}\partial_{r}w_{k}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\leq C\big(\|r^{\alpha}\partial_{r}w_{k}(0)\Lambda_{k}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\|r^{\alpha-1}w_{k}(0)\Lambda_{k}(0)\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}\big).\end{split}

Proof.

We inherit the notations used in the proof of Proposition
5.4
, and only present
the detailed proof for the case
\alpha=2.
We first get, by taking the real part of the
{L^{2}_{r}}
inner product of (
5.14
)
with
-\partial_{r}(r^{4}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}),
that

(5.21)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|r^{2}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}^{2}+\nu\Re\Big\langle-\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}+\Theta^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)\eta_{j}+\frac{4|kB|^{2}t^{2}}{r^{6}}\eta_{j},-\partial_{r}(r^{4}\partial_{r}\eta_{j})\Big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad=\Re\braket{\chi_{j}(R^{\eta}_{1}+R^{\eta}_{2})-\nu(2^{-2j}\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\eta+2^{1-j}\chi_{j}^{\prime}\partial_{r}\eta),-\partial_{r}(r^{4}\partial_{r}\eta_{j})}_{L^{2}_{r}}.\end{split}

By using integration by parts, one has

\displaystyle\Re\Big\langle-\nu\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}+\Theta^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)\eta_{j}+\nu\frac{4|kB|^{2}t^{2}}{r^{6}}\eta_{j},\ -\partial_{r}(r^{4}\partial_{r}\eta_{j})\Big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle=\nu\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\nu\Re\braket{\partial_{r}^{2}\eta_{j},4r^{3}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}-2\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\Re\braket{r\eta_{j},\partial_{r}\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\quad+4\nu|kBt|^{2}\|r^{-1}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}-24\nu|kBt|^{2}\Re\braket{r^{-2}\eta_{j},r^{-1}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}}_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\nu}{2}\bigl(\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\bigr)+2\nu|kBt|^{2}\|r^{-1}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-C\bigl(\nu\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\nu|kBt|^{2}\|r^{-2}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\bigr).

By using the fact:
|\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}|+r|\partial_{r}\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}|\lesssim\frac{\kappa_{k}}{\log\hat{C}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Re\braket{\chi_{j}R^{\eta}_{2},-\partial_{r}(r^{4}\partial_{r}\eta_{j})}_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\eta_{j},-\partial_{r}(r^{4}\partial_{r}\eta_{j})\Big\rangle_{L^{2}_{r}}=\bigg\|\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}}r^{2}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\Big\langle\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\Big)\eta_{j},r^{4}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\kappa_{k}}{\log\hat{C}}\bigl(\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\|\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\bigr).

It follows from Lemma
C.2
that

\displaystyle\Re\braket{\chi_{j}R^{\eta}_{1}-\nu(2^{-2j}\chi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\eta+2^{1-j}\chi_{j}^{\prime}\partial_{r}\eta),-\partial_{r}(r^{4}\partial_{r}\eta_{j})}_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl(\|\chi_{j}r^{2}R^{\eta}_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\nu\sum\limits_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}(\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{r}})\bigr)\times(\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}})

\displaystyle\leq C\nu\sum\limits_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\big(\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\big)+\frac{\nu}{4}\big(\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}\big).

By substituting the above estimates into (
5.21
) and integrating the resulting inequality over
[0,T]
, we find

\displaystyle\|r^{2}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{\infty}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+4\kappa_{k}\Big\|\frac{\kappa_{k}t}{r}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|r^{2}\partial_{r}\eta_{j}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{k}}{\log\hat{C}}\big(\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\|\eta_{j}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{|\ell-j|\leq 1,\ell\geq 0}\Big(\nu\|r\partial_{r}\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\nu(k^{2}+\Theta^{2})\|\eta_{\ell}\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\kappa_{k}\Big\|\frac{\kappa_{k}t}{r^{2}}\eta_{\ell}\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\Big).

By summarizing the above inequalities for
j\geq 0
, and using (
5.16
) and (

), we achieve

\displaystyle\|r\partial_{r}\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|r^{2}\partial_{r}\eta(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{k}}{\log\hat{C}}\big(\|r\partial_{r}\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}+\|\eta\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}^{2}\big)+\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|r^{2}\partial_{r}\eta(0)\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\log\hat{C}}\|r\partial_{r}\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}+\|\eta\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}.

So that by choosing
\hat{C}\gg 1
and then applying Proposition
5.4
, we obtain
(
5.20
). This completes the proof of Propsition
5.5
.
∎

The next Proposition concerns the space-time estimates for the stream function
\varphi_{k,H}
, which is also referred to as the inviscid damping estimate.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
and
\varphi_{k,H}
solve the homogeneous equation (
5.1
), we denote

\varphi_{k,H}^{(1)}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}e^{-i\frac{kB}{r^{2}}t}\varphi_{k,H}.
Then there exist constants
C_{\varepsilon}>0,\hat{C}_{0}\gg 1
depending only on
\varepsilon
, so that for
\hat{C}\geq\hat{C}_{0}
,

(5.22)

\begin{split}&|kB|^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\Big\|r^{-1+\varepsilon}\partial_{r}\varphi_{k,H}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}+|k|\Big\|r^{-2+\varepsilon}\varphi_{k,H}^{(1)}\Lambda_{k}\Big\|_{{L^{2}_{T}}({L^{2}_{r}})}\Big)\leq C_{\varepsilon}M_{k}(0).\end{split}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
w_{k}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}([1,\infty))
.
Observing that
\frac{d}{ds}e^{-i\frac{kB}{s^{2}}t}=\frac{2ikBt}{s^{3}}e^{-i\frac{kB}{s^{2}}t},

we get, by using integration by parts, that

(5.23)

\begin{split}-r^{-2+\varepsilon}\Lambda_{k}(t,r)\varphi_{k,H}^{(1)}&=\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-2+\varepsilon}\Lambda_{k}(t,r)K_{k}(r,s)e^{-i\frac{kB}{s^{2}}t}w_{k}^{(1)}ds\\
&=\frac{1}{2ikBt}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-2+\varepsilon}\Lambda_{k}(t,r)K_{k}(r,s)s^{3}w_{k}^{(1)}d\big(e^{-i\frac{kB}{s^{2}}t}\big)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2ikBt}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-2+\varepsilon}\Lambda_{k}(t,r)\partial_{s}\bigl(K_{k}(r,s)s^{3}w_{k}^{(1)}\bigr)e^{-i\frac{kB}{s^{2}}t}ds,\end{split}

and by (
1.18a

Lemma B.1

Lemma B.2

For any

\begin{split}&\|A(r){\mathcal{K}}_{k}^{\prime}[w]\|_{L^{\infty}_{r}}\lesssim\|A(r)r^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{K}}_{k}^{\prime\prime}[w]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A(r)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{K}}_{k}^{\prime}[w]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|A(r)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{K}}_{k}^{\prime}[w]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\|A(r)r^{\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}+|k|^{2}\|A(r)r^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\mathcal{K}}_{k}[w]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A(r)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{K}}_{k}^{\prime}[w]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|A(r)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{K}}_{k}^{\prime}[w]\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|A(r)r^{\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}_{r}}.\end{split}

The estimates of fourth term in (
C.1
) and (
C.3
) follow along the same lines.

Finally, for (
C.2
) and (
C.4
), we only present the estimate to
typical term:
|k|^{2}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{K}[w]}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\right\|_{{L^{2}_{r}}}
. We first observe that

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{{\mathcal{K}}_{k}[w]}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big|&\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{\varepsilon-2}s^{-\varepsilon+\frac{1}{2}}|K_{k}(r,s)|s^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}|w|(s)ds\\
&\lesssim|k|^{-1}r^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{\varepsilon-\frac{5}{2}}s^{-\varepsilon+\frac{1}{2}}(rs)^{\frac{1}{2}}\min\big(\frac{r}{s},\frac{s}{r}\big)^{|k|}s^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}|w|(s)ds\\
&=|k|^{-1}r^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}(rs)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{s}{r}\big)^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}\min\big(\frac{r}{s},\frac{s}{r}\big)^{|k|}s^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}|w|(s)ds\\
&=|k|^{-1}r^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}(rs)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\min\Big(\big(\frac{r}{s}\big)^{|k|-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon},\big(\frac{s}{r}\big)^{|k|+\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}\Big)s^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}|w(s)|ds\\
&=|k|^{-1}r^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{|k|-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon,|k|+\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}[r^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}w],\end{split}

which together with (
3.14b
) ensures that

\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{K}}_{k}[w]}{r^{2-\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq\frac{C}{|k|}\Big(\frac{1}{2(|k|+\varepsilon-\frac{3}{2})+1}+\frac{1}{2(|k|-\varepsilon+\frac{3}{2})-1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{L^{1}_{r}}\leq\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{|k|^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|r^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}w\|_{L^{1}_{r}}.

We thus complete the proof of Proposition
C.1
.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\geq 1
,
\hat{C}\geq e
and
\Lambda_{k}=\log\bigl(\hat{C}r^{2}+\kappa_{k}t\bigr),

we have

(C.8a)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\Big|=\frac{\kappa_{k}}{(\hat{C}r^{2}+\kappa_{k}t)\log(\hat{C}r^{2}+\kappa_{k}t)}\leq\frac{\kappa_{k}}{\hat{C}\log\hat{C}}\frac{1}{r^{2}},

(C.8b)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\Big|\leq\frac{2}{\log\hat{C}}\frac{1}{r},\qquad\Big|\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda_{k}}{\Lambda_{k}}\Big|\leq\frac{4}{\log\hat{C}}\frac{1}{r^{2}},

(C.8c)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{r}(r^{2\beta}\Lambda_{k}^{2})}{r^{2\beta-1}\Lambda_{k}^{2}}\leq 2|\beta|+\frac{4}{\log\hat{C}},\quad\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}(r^{2\beta}\Lambda_{k}^{2})}{r^{2\beta-2}\Lambda_{k}^{2}}\leq 2\beta(2\beta-1)+\frac{1}{\log\hat{C}}(16\beta+8)+\frac{8}{\log^{2}\hat{C}}.

Proof.

We first observe that

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\Lambda_{k}=\frac{\kappa_{k}}{\hat{C}r^{2}+\kappa_{k}t},\quad\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}=\frac{2\hat{C}r}{\hat{C}r^{2}+\kappa_{k}t},\quad\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda_{k}=\frac{2\hat{C}\kappa_{k}t-2\hat{C}^{2}r^{2}}{(\hat{C}r^{2}+\kappa_{k}t)^{2}},\end{split}

which leads to (
C.8a
) and (
C.8b
).

Whereas observing that

\begin{split}\partial_{r}(r^{2\beta}\Lambda_{k}^{2})=2\beta r^{2\beta-1}\Lambda_{k}^{2}+2r^{2\beta}\Lambda_{k}\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k},\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}(r^{2\beta}\Lambda_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}^{2}(r^{2\beta})\Lambda_{k}^{2}+2\partial_{r}r^{2\beta}\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}^{2}+r^{2\beta}\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\beta(2\beta-1)r^{2\beta-2}\Lambda_{k}^{2}+8\beta r^{2\beta-1}\Lambda_{k}\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}+2r^{2\beta}(\Lambda_{k}\partial_{r}^{2}\Lambda_{k}+|\partial_{r}\Lambda_{k}|^{2}),

we deduce (
C.8c
) from (
C.8b
).
∎

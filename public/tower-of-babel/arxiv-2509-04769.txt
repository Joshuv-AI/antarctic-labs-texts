Ferrodark soliton collisions: breather formation, pair reproduction and spin-mass separation
Yixiu Bai, Jiangnan Biguo, Xiaoquan Yu

Abstract
We study collisions between a ferrodark soliton (FDS) and an antiFDS ( Z 2 Z_{2} kinks in the spin order) in the easy-plane phase of spin-1 Bose-Einstein condensates (BEC). For a type-I pair (type-I FDS-antiFDS pair) at low incoming velocities, the pair annihilates followed by the formation of an extremely long-lived dissipative breather on a stable background, a spatially localized wave packet with out-of-phase oscillating magnetization and number densities. Periodic emissions of spin and density waves cause breather energy dissipation and we find that the breather energy decays logarithmically in time. When the incoming velocity is larger than a critical velocity at which a stationary FDS-anti FDS pair forms, a pair with finite separating velocity is reproduced. When approaching the critical velocity from below, we find that the lifetime of the stationary type-I pair shows a power-law divergence, resembling a critical behavior. In contrast, a type-II pair (type-II FDS-antiFDS pair)never annihilates and only exhibits reflection. For collisions of a mixed type FDS-antiFDS pair, as Z 2 Z_{2} kinks in the spin order, reflection occurs in the topological structure of the magnetization while the mass superfluid density profiles pass through each other, manifesting spin-mass separation.



Introduction—

Kinks and domain walls are topological solitons, i.e., solitons with topological characteristics, which exist in many condensed matter systems
Chaikin et al. 1995
;
Nelson 2002
and are expected to play important roles in the evolution of early universe
Vachaspati 2007
. Collisions among topological solitons reveal the interaction between them and are crucial in determining the universality of relevant non-equilibrium processes
Damski and Zurek 2007
;
Fujimoto et al. 2019
.

Solitons in classical wave systems described by the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation
Korteweg and de Vries and 1895
;
Drazin and Johnson 1989
exhibit shape-preserving collision dynamics, a property regarded as a defining property of solitons and a feature of integrable systems. Similar properties also hold for kinks in integrable models. The representative examples are sine-Gordon kinks and they pass through each other without energy dissipation
Vachaspati 2023
;
Sickotra 2021
;
Drazin and Johnson 1989
.
Collisions of kinks in non-integrable systems show more complex characters
Kivshar and Malomed 1989
;
Vachaspati 2023
. For
Z_{2}
kinks in the
\phi^{4}
model
Vachaspati 2023
;
Campbell et al. 1983
, the outgoing state of head-on collisions of a kink and antikink pair depends on their initial velocities. At low initial velocities, the pair forms a long-lived oscillating bound state
Campbell et al. 1983
and the bound state can again decay to a kink-antikink pair or persist. Similar rich collision dynamics of kinks has not been well explored in superfluids, partly because there the propagating kinks are rare in such systems. Recently discovered ferrodark solitons (FDSs) in spin-1 BECs are propagating
Z_{2}
kinks in the spin order
Yu and Blakie 2021
;
Yu and Blakie 2022a
;
Yu and Blakie 2022b
;
Yu and Blakie 2024
;
Biguo and Yu 2025
and provide an example to explore rich dynamics of kink collisions in superfluids.

In this Letter, we numerically study collisions of FDS-antiFDS pairs in a uniform ferromagnetic spin-1 BEC. At low initial velocities, a type-I pair annihilates accompanied by the formation of a breather characterized by spatially localized periodic motion of magnetization and number density. Such a breather is extremely long-lived and persists in our longest time numerical simulations. Moreover, we find that the formed breather through the scattering process dissipates energy via emitting density and spin waves periodically and the breather energy exhibits logarithmic decay. When the incoming velocity is high enough, a type-I pair with finite separating velocity is reproduced, effectively exhibiting a reflection. Then there exists a critical velocity, at which a stationary type-I pair is reproduced. We find that when approaching the critical velocity from below, the stationary type-I pair has power-law divergent lifetime.
In contrast, a type-II pair only exhibits reflection and no annihilation occurs. For collisions between a type-I FDS and a type-II antiFDS, they reflect in the spin order as
Z_{2}
kinks however they pass through each other in the mass superfluid density, showing a phenomenon of spin-mass separation. Moreover, for the pair positioned symmetrically with respect to the origin, the collision center is shifted to the side of the type-I FDS, caused by the in-elasticity of the collision and the distinct dispersion relations of the type-I and type-II FDSs.

Collisions of a type-I pair: formation of dissipative breathers and pair reproduction—

Let us consider collisions between a FDS and a counterpropagating antiFDS of the same type in a uniform system. Note that two
Z_{2}
kinks can not be adjacent to each other and hence only kink-antikink scattering is possible
Vachaspati 2023
.
We prepare a largely-separated type-I pair with opposite velocities. The pair firstly annihilates, accompanied by the destruction of the kink structure in
F_{\perp}
[Fig.
S2
(a2)(a3)(a4)] and the out-going state depends on the incoming velocity. At low incoming velocities, the energy loss (via emitting density and spin waves) is large after the first collision and hence the reproduction of a new type-I pair is forbidden. Strikingly, the type-I pair does not annihilate completely into linear waves but forms a long-lived spatially localized nonlinear wave packet for moderate values of
\tilde{q}
—a monochromatic breather [Fig.
1
(a)], characterized by oscillations of the magnetization
F_{\perp}
and the total density
n
[Fig.
2
(a)(b)] with a frequency weakly depending on the quadratic Zeeman energy and the incoming velocity [Fig.
2
(d)]. The formed breather is weakly dissipative via periodically radiating density and spin waves and its energy decays logarithmically [Fig.
2
(c)]

, ensuring its extremely long lifetime. We should emphasize the observed weakly dissipative breather is on a stable background and is clearly distinct from well-known
breathers such as Akhmediev and Kuznetsov-Ma breathers which are solutions of an integrable system and on an unstable background
Akhmediev and Korneev 1986
;
Kuznetsov 1977
;
Ma 1979

Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, the only known models which support the phenomenon of fusion of a kink-antikink pair into a breather are the
\phi^{4}

;
Campbell et al. 1983

At sufficiently high initial velocities, a new type-I pair can be recreated with a finite separating velocity [Fig.
1
(c)], which can be effectively viewed as reflection.
The conspicuous feature here is that a type-I pair always annihilates firstly when colliding. If the remaining energy of the localized nonlinear wave packet is less than the energy of a stationary type-I pair, a reproduction can not occur, which is the case for low incoming velocities (low initial energies). For a type-I pair with high incoming velocities (high initial energies), the remaining energy is sufficiently large and recreating a separating type-I pair is possible [Fig.
1
(c)], where the separating velocity is lower than the incoming velocity since the type-I FDS energy increases as increasing the propagating velocity
Yu and Blakie 2022a
.

It is useful to summarize the similarities and the differences between collisions of
Z_{2}
kinks in the
\phi^{4}
model and collisions of type-I pairs. At very low incoming velocities, the
Z_{2}
kinks annihilate followed by the formation of a breather-like excitation (or a bound state of the kink-antikink pair). A breather also forms for a type-I pair while the breather has richer structure characterized by out-of-phase oscillations of magnetization and number densities. At very high incoming velocities, reflection takes place and here the reflection is an effective description of reproduction of a separating pair. For the
\phi^{4}
model, at intermediate velocities, there exists bands of incoming velocity at which annihilation and reflection happen alternatively
Vachaspati 2023
;
Campbell et al. 1983
. Rather differently, for a type-I pair the change from annihilation to reflection happens at one critical value of the incoming velocity. The formation of the stationary pair
at the critical incoming velocity and the power-law divergence of its lifetime near the critical incoming velocity were not observed in the
\phi^{4}
model.

Collisions of a type-II pair and a mixed type pair: reflection and spin-mass separation—

In contrast to type-I pairs, during the collision of a type-II pair [Fig.
1
(d)] the kink structure is preserved and the pair does not involve annihilation (kink structure destruction) [Fig.
S2
(a1’)-(a5’)]. The energy loss is still present (however is much weaker in comparison with collisions of a type-I pair), yielding that the reflected velocity is higher than the incoming velocity since the type-II FDS energy decreases as increasing the propagating velocity
Yu and Blakie 2022a
.

We now consider a FDS and an antiFDS of a different type with opposite velocities and initial positions symmetric with respect to the origin.
The most conspicuous phenomenon is that, as
Z_{2}
kinks in the spin order, the type-I FDS and the type-II antiFDS reflect. This is a generic feature of
Z_{2}
kinks
Campbell et al. 1983
since if passing through happens, the region between the kink and the antikink is at some value
F_{0}
different from the ground state value, the energy of this configuration is proportional to
L
[inset in Fig.

L
can not approach infinity. However in viewing of the mass superfluid density the type-I FDS and the type-II antiFDS pass through each other [Fig.
4
(b1)-(b5)]. This phenomenon manifests spin-mass separation, a novel feature in kink collisions due to the presence of two orders in the spinor superfluid and a related phenomenon called spin-charge separation is normally expected to occur in strongly correlated systems
Giamarchi 2003
. Another notable feature is that the collision position is not at the origin but is shifted towards the incoming side of the type-I FDS [Fig.
1
(e), Fig.
4

For the inelastic collision process, the energy loss is unavoidable and therefore the out-going velocity of the type-I FDS decreases while the out-going velocity of the type-II antiFDS increases
Yu and Blakie 2022a
, yielding the shift of the collision position. No annihilation appears in this collision process [Fig.

(a3)].

Conclusion—
We numerically investigate inelastic collisions of FDS-antiFDS pairs in ferromagnetic spin-1 BECs. We find that a type-I pair annihilates (destruction of the topological structure of kinks in the spin order) at low incoming velocities followed by the formation of an extremely long lived dissipative breather featured by the oscillations of spatially localized magnetization and number densities. The breather periodically emits spin and density waves, causing a logarithmic energy decay. When approaching the critical incoming velocity, the lifetime of a formed stationary pair after the annihilation shows a power-law divergence. At velocities higher than the critical incoming velocity, the outgoing state is a type-I pair with a finite separating velocity. For type-II pairs, annihilation does not happen and only reflection occurs. A mixed type-I type-II pair exhibits spin-mass separation, i.e., reflection in the spin order (as a
Z_{2}
kink) and passing through in the mass superfluid density. Note that transitions between type-I FDSs and type-II FDSs may occur during the scattering process for very small values of the quadratic Zeeman energy and the relevant phenomena will be discussed elsewhere. This work explores the new regime of kink collision dynamics and will also motivate experimental investigations
Chomaz et al. 2015
;
Gauthier et al. 2016
;
Semeghini et al. 2018
;
Higbie et al. 2005
;
Huh et al. 2020
;
Farolfi et al. 2020
;
Chai et al. 2020
;
Prüfer et al. 2022
.
